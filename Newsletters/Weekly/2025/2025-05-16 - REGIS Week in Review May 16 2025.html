REGIS REPORT

16 May 2025

1Q25 earnings season comes to a close this week with investors taking most of their cues from the last wave of reports. Aggregate PCOMP earnings are so far trailing market expectations of low double-digit growth for FY25, after clocking mere low single-digit growth in 1Q25. While around half of the corporates on our watchlist reported numbers that were in-line with forecasts, earnings misses vastly outnumbered the few beats, making a rather weak 1Q25 showing overall.

 

Still, the PCOMP managed to eke out a fifth straight weekly advance, finishing slightly higher by 0.1% or 7.33 pts WoW to 6,465.53. Gains early in the week from optimism over the US-China trade truce were only nearly wiped out as the index pulled back in the latter half with a pick-up in foreign selling.

 

Banks BDO (-3.4% WoW) and CBC (-7.8%) were the largest drag on the index, with BDO weighed down by foreign outflows while CBC corrected from near-peak levels after botched hopes of its inclusion at the upcoming May 2025 MSCI rebalancing.

 

Meanwhile, de-escalating US-China trade tensions continued to lift ICT (+6.5% WoW) which remained the biggest positive mover for the PCOMP. AEV (+5.8% WoW) was also among the top index gainers thanks to the announcement of its addition to the MSCI Small Cap Index at the month-end rebalancing.

 

Taking cues from 1Q25 results, investors bought up shares of GTCAP (+8.7% WoW) -- the PCOMP's top gainer for the week -- after earnings beat forecasts mostly on the strong performance of Toyota. On the other hand, MONDE (-13.2% WoW) was down the most among index names despite earnings coming largely within expectations, with some softness in its core branded business and given the continued drag from Quorn. AC (-4.8% WoW) also succumbed to foreign selling after 1Q results disappointed, dampened by the misses in GLO (-6.8% WoW) and ACEN (-2.6% WoW).

 

On macro, the BSP's published notes from its April Monetary Board meeting revealed its expectations for inflation to settle within the lower half of its 2-4% target range, well into 4Q25 thanks to lower food and oil prices. The central bank also expects GDP growth to meet the low end of the government's 6-8% target for 2025-27. The BSP noted current monetary policy settings remain relatively tight.

 

Foreigners reverted to net sellers for the week by $4m while ADTO continued to improve to $137m.

 

The peso slightly weakened by 0.3% WoW to 55.645:$1, snapping a four-week rally that had the currency last week hit its strongest level since September 2024. Yields relatively steady WoW, with the PH10Y +2.9bps to 6.1732%.

Philippine Strategy - Midterm election results -- A fractured and fractious Senate
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

A preview of the next three years?

The midterm Senatorial election delivered a blow to the administration of President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr, with his allies likely to take just five of the 12 contested seats (out of a total of 24). The result reflects the sharp drop in Marcos, Jr’s popularity, which we reported last month. Meanwhile, the allies of Vice President Sara Duterte are also tracking to gain five seats. Arguably the biggest surprise, however, was the entry of two Senate candidates not allied with either camp. This leaves the incoming Senate both fractured – with Marcos allies not holding a clear majority – and likely more fractious than ever. Prospects for constitutional changes as well the removal of Sara Duterte from office through a Senate impeachment trial are now much diminished. We could also see more populist policies over the coming years as the administration looks to regain voter approval with a view to the 2028 Presidential elections.

 

A surprise setback – Marcos administration will need to compromise

The midterms held on 12 May was a surprise setback for President Marcos, Jr, whose ticket won just 5 out of the 12 available Senate seats (see Fig 1). As recently as February, surveys had his slate taking at least 8 spots. Two previous Marcos allies who defected to the Duterte camp, including Marcos’ sister, made it to the Senate. The two biggest surprise winners are Paolo “Bam” Aquino (of the long-time family rivals of the Marcoses), and Francis Pangilinan. The two are members of the Liberal Party that opposed the short-lived Marcos-Duterte alliance. Both are in the top five of votes received, even though opinion polls as recent as three weeks ago predicted neither candidate was likely to win.

A fractured Senate – from likely majority to uneasy alliances

Assuming the count holds, the midterms would come out five for the Marcos camp, five for Duterte allies, and two from the Liberal Party. As it stands, the 24-member chamber will be fractured at nine for the administration, seven for Duterte allies, and eight members not openly aligned with either camp (Figs 2 and 3). Philippine politics are fluid in the best of times, but it seems the current situation will make for especially uneasy alliances. Actions that need a two-thirds Senate supermajority (constitutional change, conviction of impeached officials) will now be very difficult.

 

A fractious Senate – a noisy(er) next three years

We expect a more fractious Senate floor, especially given the impeachment trial of VP Sara Duterte, scheduled for July. And with popularity rankings clearly so fickle, we could see more time spent on jockeying for the 2028 Presidential elections than on passing meaningful legislation.

 

Even more populist policies to come?

The midterm results could also tempt the Marcos, Jr administration to push for more a populist agenda. We recently reported government plans to offer rice subsidies, as well as a new food vouchers program. While these and potential handouts would support our Consumer recovery theme for 2025, they could come at the expense of the fiscal deficit (5.6% of GDP as of 2024). A more divided Senate, however, could actually make such more difficult.

 
PLDT - 1Q25: Mobile weakness ... wireline strength?
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income dips 4% on revenue growth of 2%

PLDT reported headline income of P9.01bn for 1Q, down 8% from a year as group revenues rose 2% to P53.4bn. Adjusting for one-offs and others, we estimate core income for the quarter was P8.59bn, down 4% YoY and likely to be slightly lower than Street expectations. Among the notable operational highlights were a 2% decline in Mobile revenue and, curiously, a 14% increase in Fixed Line voice revenue. Meanwhile, its fintech unit Maya finally turned a quarterly profit.

 

Mobile revenue dips; EBITDA margin steady

Mobile revenue dipped 2% YoY and QoQ, just very slightly better than Globe. The numbers imply that was very little QoQ change in the relative market shares of the two. It’s quite apparent that there’s been a contraction in industry-wide mobile spend which we suspect is due to the exodus of parties engaged in the POGO industry and similar activities. 

PLDT also showed a 14% rise in Fixed Line Voice revenue, which we found rather curious. That, combined with a modest rise in Home Broadband, helped to bring overall revenue to positive growth territory (+2%). The company managed to keep costs steady in the period. Cash opex dipped 1% despite increased repairs and maintenance costs. The belt-tightening helped maintain EBITDA margins at 52%.

 

Maya in the black

Maya delivered its first quarterly profit since transitioning into its current business, contributing P143m in profit. We are hopeful that the current profit trajectory will continue.

 

No P&L guidance

While PLDT tweaked its FY25 capex guidance to P68-70bn (from P68-73bn), it still offered no guidance for both the top and the bottom lines. This is a bit unusual and reflects the uncertain markets it faces.

 
Globe - 1Q25: Weak operating results; Mynt profit soars
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income down 22%

Globe’s core income for 1Q dropped 22% to P4.5bn as revenue was surprisingly soft across all operating segments, falling 3%. Mobile revenue dropped 3% while broadband fell 5%. Even corporate data, which had been growing better than the rest, saw a 2% drop YoY. Headline reported income for 1Q25 was P6.98bn, inflated by gains relating to the dilution of its stake in Mynt.

 

Mobile revenue declines 3%; wireline broadband net adds at 132k

Mobile service revenue fell 3% YoY in the quarter and the drop appears to have been from the prepaid segment. The prepaid business is, by its nature, most vulnerable to pricing changes in the market. Management said, however, that competitive intensity did not worsen in the quarter. Home broadband revenue was down 5% YoY and 1% QoQ, dashing hopes for a recovery. The fixed wireless subscriber base continued to shrink, falling 40k (-9%) QoQ.

The good news is that Globe added 132k (+10%) new wireline subscribers, which is the most it has ever added in a single quarter. EBITDA managed to dip only 3% despite the revenue decline as the firm reduced cash opex by 4% YoY.

 

Mynt profit soars

Profits at Mynt jumped to P5.1bn, up 88%, and Globe’s share was P1.8bn. Mynt accounted for almost 40% of Globe’s 1Q core income. Note Mynt’s profit was larger than Globe’s core income for this period. Simplistically annualizing 1Q profit would imply that the recently achieved US$5bn valuation is equivalent to <14x PER for FY25E.

 

Meeting guidance would be a challenge

Management affirmed its guidance “low to mid-single digit” growth in revenue this year, which we reckon will be a challenge to meet. Capex for 1Q amounted to only P8.5bn (21% of revenue) which is on track to be down >30% this year.

 
Jollibee Foods - 1Q25: Robust domestic business mitigates weak international ops
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Operating profit +18% YoY—in line

JFC’s 1Q25 net profit to common (after dividends to prefs) declined 8% YoY to P2.3bn, which is trending a tad behind expectations. The miss was mainly due to higher interest expenses and a higher tax rate. Core operations were otherwise broadly in line with estimates, as consolidated revenues are up 15% YoY to P70.2bn and operating profit increased 18% YoY to P4.8bn. Consolidated SSSG was a healthy 5.5% in 1Q25, not far from the 5.7% posted in FY24.

 

Philippine business sustains strong growth momentum; int’l continues to lag

The domestic business saw systemwide sales (SWS) rising 11.9% to P62.2bn on the back of robust SSSG of +8.5%. The strong SSSG was driven by transaction count, as ticket sizes remained stable. Management says it is reluctant to raise prices and would prefer to see SSS improving on customer traffic. The international segment reported SWS growing 29.5% to P44.6bn, with an extra YoY boost from the inclusion of Compose Coffee. The older businesses saw mixed results with strong numbers from the Jollibee brand internationally (SWS up 15%), while Smashburger and the China business deteriorated further (SWS down -10.6% and -6.9%). For the quarter, the domestic business contributed 126% (P2.9bn) of net income. This implies that the international businesses in aggregate posted a loss of ~P600mn.

China and Smashburger turnaround still a work in progress

Smashburger, which saw another management change in 1Q, reported a further decline in SSS (-8.0% in 1Q25). The China business saw its fifth consecutive quarter of negative SSSG at -8.3% given weak performance from its Yonghe King (-10.1%) and Hong Zhuang Yuan (-19.1%) brands. Management highlighted improvements being made at Smashburger that may have an impact by 2Q. Meanwhile, a revamped store model for some China stores is being implemented which the company hopes will turn operations around. We will need to wait a few quarters to see if these efforts actually bear fruit.

 

FY25 guidance on track; strong PH business set to continue

The company is looking on track to meet its 10-15% operating income growth guidance in 2025. Management also says it is sticking to its guidance of 15% profit growth for this year. The positive trends for the domestic business have not slowed down in April/May relative to 1Q. However, the success of turnaround efforts for Smashburger and China is still far from certain. A rebound in the international segment, of which Smashburger and China are important components, is key for JFC to hit its triple net income target (to P24bn) by 2028.

 
Universal Robina - 1Q25: Modest bounce for core Domestic BCF
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1Q25 recurring profit dips 2% YoY

URC’s recurring profit in 1Q25 dipped 2% YoY to P4.1bn, which appears to be trending ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. Consolidated EBIT (+1% YoY to P5.5bn) also looks ahead of expectations. Revenues look in line with expectations at P45.3bn, up 7% YoY. The downbeat 2H24 results as well as the modest guidance from management appear to have kept expectations for this year rather subdued (and thus easier to hurdle).

 

Domestic BCF top line up 4% on volume rebound

Domestic BCF saw a +4% YoY improvement in revenue, driven by volume. The increase was led by double-digit volume growth in RTD beverages, snacks, and confectionary. Snacks, affected by downtrading during when inflation soared last year, saw recovery due to value-based pricing and promotional activities. Apart from snacks, the company prioritized A&P spend for coffee and noodles as those categories are still facing competitive challenges. Market shares across categories were generally stable, but 10-20bp decreases were observed in snacks, biscuits, and noodles (within the margin of error).

 

International BCF has upward momentum

Growth at the international BCF business continues to be faster than the domestic business, with revenues up 10% YoY to P9.6bn. Vietnam was the primary growth driver posting double digit growth with market share gains.

Malaysia and Indonesia also performed well despite Ramadan season in March. These positive trends offset the headwinds in Thailand and Cambodia as they face ongoing retailer negotiations and border distribution issues, respectively. EBIT margin for international expanded significantly (+120bp YoY to 14.1%). Management intends to reinvest gains in the international business as the priority will continue to be volume and top line growth.

 

Higher output and sales boost Non-BCF sales

Non-BCF (Agro-Industrial and Commodities) sales were up 8% YoY during the quarter as both refined sugar and flour delivered higher volumes. The SURE (sugar and renewables) segment benefited from strong refining output and higher external sales. EBIT margins normalized (-3.4ppt YoY) from last year’s high-margin base and sugar price volatility.

 

Execution will be key

Management reiterated its previous guidance, which is for full-year revenue growth to be in the high single-digit range, while profit growth is expected to be in the mid-single-digits. The lower profit growth vs revenue growth is a consequence of management’s plans to increase spending this year. We share management’s view that increased efforts (spending), whether in brand-building A&P or product development, are an imperative in order to lay the foundation for future gains. Whether the execution will live up to the plans, we should see in the next few quarters (or year).

 
Monde Nissin - 1Q25: APAC BFB segment steady despite noodle volume drop
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Revenues rise +2.8%, profit up +1.5%

Monde reported 1Q results showing revenue and core EBITDA both growing 3%, while core profit (which excludes marked-to-market gains/losses on its guaranty asset, among others) inched up +1.5% to P2.92bn. The APAC BFB segment delivered 4% top line growth while the Meat Alternative segment shrank 4% YoY (-6% on constant currency). Overall, we’d characterize the underlying results as being in line with expectations.

 

Biscuit volume jump, noodles fall

Within APAC BFB, it was the biscuit business that saw a remarkable 13% YoY expansion in revenue, almost entirely attributable to volume improvement. The instant noodle business, on the other hand, shrank 4.5% on revenue. Soup noodles (29% of noodle sales) saw a disconcerting 13% volume drop. Management sounded uncertain about the reasons behind it and has commissioned a deeper investigation given the importance of this category. Rising household incomes generally mean declines in noodle sales, but a drop of this magnitude seems unusual. Meanwhile, products under the “Others” umbrella, which include beverages, packaged cakes, et al, showed a +9% increase in revenue, driven also by volume.

Cost management helps Quorn deliver positive EBITDA

Despite a 6% drop in revenue, the Meat Alternative business (Quorn) managed to deliver positive EBITDA of ~GBP2m, a turnaround from the EBITDA loss in 1Q24. Management believes that Quorn is on track to meet the key targets this year that underpin its latest valuation (which would mean Monde will not need to take an impairment charge in FY25).

 

Outlook for FY25 maintained

Monde has managed to lock in the cost of key inputs for the year at what it deems are favorable prices. This, combined with the relatively weak USD in recent weeks, the existence of its USD cash stockpile as well as other ongoing cost management initiatives, gives management confidence that its APAC BFB margins can remain stable for the rest of the year. Management is thus maintaining its guidance that margins for APAC BFB will remain broadly the same as last year even as it delivers “mid-single digit growth”. For Quorn, the company is still aiming to produce positive core EBITDA for FY25 despite expectations of a drop in revenue.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Impact of lower 1Q25 volumes at SEC cushioned by strong win rates
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A return to profitability in 1Q25

Bloomberry’s 1Q25 headline profit rose 26% YoY to P3.3bn. However, excluding the P2.9bn non-recurring gain from refinancing a P40bn loan facility, recurring profit would have fallen 83% YoY to P446mn. This, however, is still an improvement from the P215mn net loss in 4Q24. Bloomberry’s 1Q25 EBITDA fell 11% YoY (+10% QoQ) to P4.4bn due to lower volumes at Solaire Entertainment City (SEC). Although EBITDA appears to be tracking ahead of our forecast, the outperformance was due to strong win rates both VIP and mass tables, which are unlikely to be sustained. Furthermore, VIP rolling chip will likely fall further since two major junket operators at SEC closed in 2Q25.

 

Looking ahead, management said that the prospects for SEC are unlikely to improve, particularly in light of the closure of the aforementioned junkets. In contrast, the company expects Solaire North (SN) to continue to ramp up until it reaches maturity in 2026 or 2027. The company also expressed its belief that its online business has significant potential, given the growth of the industry so far.

 

Reduction in SEC GGR cushioned by high VIP and mass table win rates

Although SEC’s GGR fell 3% QoQ and 18% YoY (to P12.4bn), we expected a more severe drop, given the POGO ban, which became effective in January 2025.

While VIP and mass table volumes indeed fell to their lowest levels since 1Q22 (see Figure 6), the GGR figures were cushioned by above-average win rates (see Figure 5). The VIP win rate of 3.2% is above the theoretical level of 2.9%. Moreover, the mass table win rate of 57.7% (+10.8ppt QoQ and 16.5ppt YoY) is well above the seven-year average of c. 40% and reached what appeared to be a record high.

 

Solaire North continues to ramp up

Solaire North’s GGR increased 29% QoQ to P4.6bn, with every category showing remarkable growth (see Figure 7). VIP rolling chip doubled QoQ to P8.9bn thanks to foreign VIP players, while slot coin-in rose 18% QoQ (see Figure 9).

 

BLOOM to spend P2bn-P4bn in marketing expenses for online business

The company has applied to make its app available on the Apple app store and Google play store but the official launch of the app will be in 3Q25. BLOOM intends to spend P2bn-P4bn in marketing for this business in 2H25. We reckon this could bring the company back into a net loss in 2H25 but would likely be worthwhile if the online business can grow substantially in the medium-to-long term.

1Q25 core profit falls 7% to P5.0bn – In line with Regis but behind Street FY25E

Property: Reservation sales improve 47% QoQ; Revenue bookings up 48%

Earnings recovery at nickel unit

The nickel unit staged a turnaround, reporting a P409mn profit in 1Q25, after shipments rose 34% YoY and ASPs rebounded 73%. Management aims to increase production to 2-3mn MT this year from only 1.5mn MT last year.

 

Construction, cement units remain challenged

Contributions from construction fell 49% due to project delays. The cement unit recorded a net loss of P546mn, after regulatory issues hampered production. Management however noted that this issue has now been resolved.

 
Manila Water - 1Q25 profit up 14% but East zone spending lags
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q25 profit rises 14% to P3.56bn – In line with Street estimates

Manila Water reported its net income rose 14% YoY to P3.56bn. After removing the share of participating prefs, we estimate profits to common stood at P3.12bn (+14% YoY), trending well ahead of Regis forecasts but appears in line with consensus full-year forecast of P11.2bn.

 

East zone EBITDA up 12% YoY; Spending trails

The East zone’s standalone profit rose 16% to P3.23bn while EBITDA jumped 12% to P5.7bn. Revenues grew 9% to P7.6bn largely on the implementation tariff adjustments. Billed volumes slipped 2% YoY, which management attributed to (a) base effects from last year’s leap year and (b) lower cross-border sales to Maynilad due to better water levels at Angat.

 

Cash costs rose 1% YoY to P1.9bn while capex fell 5% YoY to P4bn. Both metrics are trending below regulatory commitments. We are not too concerned about slower billed volume growth rates but are more concerned about the underspending. 

Recall that MWC earns a 12% fixed return on its cumulative net spending (i.e. revenues less cash opex and capex) and the positive free cash flow in 1Q25 suggests a reduction in that metric.

 

Non-East zone units perform better on tariff hikes; contributions from International up after East Water write-off

Profit contributions from its non-EZ units in the Philippines improved 33% YoY to P329 due to tariff hikes across each operating unit, as well as the revival of its bulk water contract in Cebu in 2Q24.

 

Combined contributions from its international operations rose 46% YoY to P124mn, as the full impairment of associate East Water meant MWC ceased to recognize its share of East Water’s losses. Contributions from its bulk water projects in Vietnam fell but these were partially offset by higher profits from its NRW management contract in Saudi Arabia.

JG Summit considering exit from petrochemicals

JG Summit Holdings Inc. (JGS PM) is considering to exit and divest from its petrochemicals business (JG Summit Olefins Corporation [JGSOC]). This follows after the company announced the prolonged shutdown for at least two years which was approved by JG Summit's Board yesterday. Rationale for the shutdown stems from the dampened demand for petrochemicals given current global overcapacity which in turn led to China (JGSOC's main export country) being oversupplied from previously being undersupplied. These factors compressed margins to unsustainable levels where continuing operations were not feasible. In an interview, JGS President and CEO Lance Gokongwei stated that they are weighing out various options for the business segment including: a full sale of the business, or a joint venture, or retaining the business for at least two years in hopes for a turnaround. Gokongwei noted that a deal for a full divestment could take years to negotiate, "if ever". He also elaborated that they recognize that JGSOC may not be a natural fit to their portfolio given that their core expertise is in consumer-related business, while the petrochemical segment is a highly industrial business-to-business operation. Note that for the FY24, JGSOC posted a net loss of P17.3bn.

Management attributed the slowdown to a shift in demand from domestic to international due to more expensive domestic travel costs (e.g. hotels) vis-à-vis international. We highly suspect that the government’s move to remove operations of turboprops from the Manila airport last March 30 may have dampened demand. Note that domestic seat load factors (SLF) slid 2.6ppts YoY to 85.6% while international SLF improved 2.7ppts to 79% for the month of April. As of 4M25, pax volumes are up 24% YoY to 9.24mn while SLF is up 40bps YoY to 84.6%.

BSP sees 2025 inflation below 3%, GDP at lower end of 6-8% target

The BSP published key highlights from its Monetary Board meeting held last April 10 where it cut rates by 25bp to 5.50%. Inflation expectations remain within target for 2025-2027. 2025 inflation is projected to stay below the midpoint (3%) of the target range (2-4%) until the fourth quarter due to lower food and oil prices. Upside risks stem from higher transport costs, meat prices, and electricity rates but may be offset by lower rice prices. 2025-2027 GDP growth is seen to settle around the low end (~6%) of the government’s growth target for 2025-2027 (6-8%). Domestic demand will be bolstered by low inflation, improvements in real wages and labor market conditions, as well as the gradual easing of monetary policy. Ongoing global macro uncertainties and a challenging external environment continue to pose risks that may negatively impact domestic economic activity. The BSP noted that current monetary policy settings "still appear to be relatively tight." The central bank also guided that the timing and magnitude of rate cuts will be decided on a meeting-by-meeting basis. Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be at 2.4% and projects 75bp total rate cuts this year to 5.00%. Regis expectations for full-year inflation are at 3.0% while our policy rate expectations are 50bp worth of cuts to 5.25%. The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of 6 this year) is scheduled for June 19.

OFW Remittances up 2.6% YoY in March

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.6% YoY to US$2.81bn, a slight deceleration from the 2.7% growth seen in February. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their percentage contribution to the total and significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth slowed to 1.0% YoY from 1.2% in February. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 5.5%.

Commercial banks' loan growth moderates to 11.8% in March; M3 growth slows to 6.1%

Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.8% YoY in March, a tad slower than the 12.2% rate in February.
