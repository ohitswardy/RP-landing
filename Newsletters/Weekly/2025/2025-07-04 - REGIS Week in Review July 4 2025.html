REGIS REPORT

04 July 2025

The PSEi ended virtually unchanged, -0.2% WoW at 6395, after rallying through much of the week. The market strength was sparked by rising expectations that the Fed would be embarking on more than just two rate cuts for 2H25. On the ground, the BSP also hinted on the possibility of up to two more cuts this year which will bring the benchmark rate to 4.75% from 5.25% currently. Officials cited the benign inflation throughout the year thus far - and the subsequent CPI data that came out on Friday reinforced this as inflation remained subdued at 1.4% in June. This is even slightly below the 1.5% consensus forecast and crucially remained below the BSP’s policy range of 2%-4% for the 4th month in a row now. That being said, a strong turnout in US June payrolls (147k vs 106k forecast), which put into question the expectations of more Fed cuts, ultimately led to some profit taking on the ground – hence a flattish WoW close for the local benchmark after hitting a 1.5 month high of 6468 during the week.

Another major development on the ground was the lowering of sales transaction tax from 60bps to 10bps that took effect at the start of July (Tuesday). This dramatically lowered transaction costs on trading Philippine shares – roundabout costs are now sub-1%. This appears to have helped boost liquidity on the ground as market turnover this week improved +30% WoW to a daily average of $134m.

In terms of sectors, views of more rate easing helped boost the property sector which has long been trading below historical valuations – Ayala Land (+5.4%) and Robinsons Land (+6.6%) saw renewed foreign interest; while SM Prime  (2.2%) likewise firmed up.

And amid the general positive sentiment on the market due to views of lower rates and lower transaction costs, conglomerates aka market proxies followed suit. GT Capital saw a strong 10% run – it was also helped by USD weakness (and hence relative Peso strength) which should be positive for the Toyota’s margins. Ayala Corporation rallied 5.6%, playing catch up to Ayala Land’s two-week advance. SM Investments (+0.2%) eked out a modest gain.

On the opposite end, Banks under-performed yet again. Apart from being crowded trade amongst foreigners and domestic instos, the key risk remains to be the margin impact of lower policy rates in the coming quarters. BPI fell 6.4%, followed by China Bank (-3.2%). Metrobank (-2.2%) and BDO Unibank (-0.6%) likewise saw some pressure.

Gaming related names were also in the spotlight. This is as Senators sought either ban e-gaming outright or at the very least introduce measures that would make access via e-wallets more prohibitive for the masses. Separately, the BSP is likewise coming up with a rule seeking to do the same. Digiplus practically halved for the week due to this and it was the worst performing stock in the entire market this week. Bloomberry (which has just recently launched its own challenger in the space) lost 17.2%.

Net foreign flow meanwhile was skewed to buy all through the week and netted nearly $50m in inflows – seeing foreign interest going into ALI, conglos broadly (GTCAP, AC, SM), and select names like URC and ICT. In contrast, banking names continued to face pressure from foreign selling.

Manila Water to Acquire Wawa Dam Operator

Manila Water (MWC) announced it will acquire 100% of WawaJVCo, Inc., the operator of a 712 million-liter-per-day (MLD) bulk water supply facility in Rizal. The facility has a 30-year take-or-pay agreement for 518 MLD with Manila Water’s East Zone concession. While the deal initially appears dilutive based on earnings multiples, its potential for value creation depends on MWC’s ability to sell the excess water capacity—194 MLD—to third parties at higher rates.

MWC is acquiring the company for P37.8 billion, to be paid in installments. This suggests a price-to-earnings ratio of over 30x based on the 518 MLD contract, far above MWC’s 2025 consensus PER of 7x. However, if the company sells the excess 194 MLD at P65 per cubic meter (as in Cebu’s bulk water agreement), the deal’s implied PER could fall to 10x.

Funding remains a challenge. With only P7 billion in cash as of March-end, MWC may need to raise capital. Assuming WawaJVCo has no existing debt, the acquisition could push MWC’s net debt from P104 billion to P142 billion, raising its net debt-to-equity ratio to 1.86x. However, if MWC taps its treasury shares—worth about P13 billion—gearing may rise more moderately to 1.44x.

The transaction is a related-party deal, as most shares of WawaJVCo will be acquired from its parent firm, Prime Infrastructure. While the deal includes the right to sell undrawn water to third parties, regulatory ambiguity remains regarding who benefits from the “excess revenue.” Because the take-or-pay payments are part of East Zone’s regulated cash outflows, it's likely the Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) will treat the excess revenue as within the regulatory framework.

Meralco energy sales flat in 1H25

In a media chat, Meralco Chief Revenue Officer Ferdinand Geluz said that energy sales may have risen by 0.5% YoY to 26,952 GWH in 1H25. For the month of June alone, energy sales fell 1% YoY. The data suggests that MER’s energy sales in 2Q25 alone may have dropped by 1.3% YoY (+15.7% QoQ) to 14,459 GWH. Recall that MER’s energy sales in 1Q25 rose 2% YoY to 12,493 GWH.

MER cited cooler weather caused by the onset of rainy season, dampening household demand while demand from commercial and industrial sectors remained lackluster due to the exit of POGOs. Meralco still expects a rebound in power demand in 2H, as it anticipates various data centers to come online and construction activity to rebound. It also expects base effects from the exit of POGOs to diminish. The company will revisit its energy sales growth target of 4.5% for this year.

First Gen, AP submit bids for CBK hydroelectric power plant

State-run Power Sector Assets and Liabilities Management Corp. (PSALM) said that it has received bids from two consortiums led by First Gen and Aboitiz Power: 1) FWKG Consortium, composed of First Gen Prime Energy Corp. and Korea Water Resources, and 2) Thunder Consortium, composed of Aboitiz Renewables, Inc., Sumitomo Corp., and Electric Power Development Co.

The opening of financial documents is set to take place “at a date, time, and venue to be determined by PSALM’s bids and awards committee. PSALM is bidding out the 796.64 MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydroelectric power plant (HEPP) complex in Laguna.

The government previously said that it aims to generate US$200-600mn from the CBK privatization, which puts the asking price at ~US$250-750k per MW. The price is below FGEN’s valuation for the privatization of the 165MW Casecnan hydro plant last year (US$526mn or >US$3mn per MW) due to absence of contracts. CBK’s power purchase agreement is set to expire in Feb 2026.

Ayala Land sells AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 12mn AREIT shares at P40.78/share on Thursday - this is a 3% discount to the previous closing price of P42/share. The sale was made for AREIT to meet public float requirements in anticipation of a share-for-property swap with Ayala Land. We estimate that the Ayala Group’s ownership in AREIT fell from 61.7% to 61.3%.

AREIT will acquire P21bn worth of assets from Ayala Land in exchange for 505.9mn new AREIT shares. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 18% and its shares outstanding by 16%. The mall, office, and hotel assets are located in Cebu, Davao, and Cagayan de Oro.

Aboitiz Power plans to build a 351MW + BESS solar facility

Northern Sun Power, Inc. (NSPI) is eyeing the development of a 351 MW-dc + 338MWh battery energy storage system (BESS) in Currimao, Ilocos Norte. The project is estimated to cost P18.6bn and is targeted for commercial operations by 1Q29. Construction is expected to commence in 2Q27 and commissioning is expected by 3Q28.

Aboitiz Power (AP PM) is currently operating an attributable 4,636 MW of capacity, of which 503 MW are renewables. It has a pipeline of 421MW of renewable energy projects that are under construction. The company is targeting an expansion of its total attributable net sellable capacity to 9.2 GW by 2030, with a 50:50 balance between renewable and thermal energy sources.

DigiPlus to launch Brazil e-games operations in September

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is set to launch its Brazil e-games platform this coming September. The offering will include a fresh lineup of livestreamed games, slots, table games, and exclusive digital entertainment developed in-house. The company cited a report by LCA Economic Consulting, which estimated the Brazilian iGaming market at US$4.6 billion. For comparison, the Philippine e-games gross gaming revenue (GGR), including bingo, from the second quarter of 2024 to the first quarter of 2025 was P179 billion (approximately US$3.2 billion).

Currently, around 70 companies are licensed to operate e-games in Brazil. DigiPlus had previously earmarked P660 million for its initial foray into the Brazilian market. This allocation covers license fees, minimum capitalization, and three months’ worth of operational expenses.

Cariaso appointed as new RCBC president and CEO

Rizal Commercial Banking Corporation (RCB PM) has appointed Reginaldo Anthony B. Cariaso as its new president and CEO, succeeding Eugene S. Acevedo, who will remain on the bank’s board of directors. Cariaso previously served as Executive Vice President and Head of the Operations Group, and he stepped into the role of deputy CEO after Acevedo’s retirement in January.

Prior to his tenure at RCBC, Cariaso held senior leadership roles at Bank of the Philippine Islands and JPMorgan Hong Kong. As the new head of RCBC, Cariaso intends to continue the bank’s push toward digital innovation and reinforce its commitment to modernizing its services.

Ayala Land Acquires New World Hotel Makati

Ayala Land has acquired the New World Hotel in Makati for an undisclosed amount. The company already owned the land on which the hotel stands and has now acquired the building itself. This transaction adds 598 rooms to Ayala Land’s hotel portfolio, bringing its total capacity to 4,862 rooms—a 14% increase. Despite this expansion, the hotel business contributes only around 2% to the company's overall operating profit. As of now, Ayala Land is trading at a 48% discount to its net asset value (NAV), with a 1.3 times FY25E price-to-book ratio and 13.2 times FY25E price-to-earnings ratio.

Arthaland Acquires Midland Mansion in Makati

Arthaland Corporation (ALCO PM) has acquired condominium units and land interest in Midland Mansion along Arnaiz Avenue in Makati’s central business district from ISOC Land for P1.49 billion. ISOC Land is owned by Michael Cosiquien, a co-founder of Megawide. The transaction covers a 1,061-square-meter parcel, implying a price of P1.4 million per sqm.

For comparison, Ayala Land’s Makati CBD land bank, located in more central areas, is valued at around P1.5 million per sqm. Meanwhile, another nearby parcel along Arnaiz Avenue was sold for P1.12 million per sqm in 2023, suggesting that land values in the area have appreciated by about 25% over the last two years.

Federal Land-Nomura JV Sells Out Cavite Commercial Lots

Federal Land NRE Global Inc. (FNG), a joint venture between Federal Land and Japan’s Nomura Real Estate, has sold out all commercial lots in its Riverpark North development in General Trias, Cavite. The parcels, ranging from 1,000 to 2,000 square meters, are intended for office, retail, and mixed-use development.

Due to strong demand, the second phase of Riverpark North is scheduled for launch by the second quarter of 2026, in time for the completion of the nearby CALAX interchange. GT Capital, which holds a 66% stake in FNG, reported that its property unit recorded reservation sales of P5.9 billion in Q1 2025—up 28% quarter-on-quarter and 51% year-on-year—mainly driven by these commercial lot sales.

ACEN, UPC May Divest Stake in India Solar Project

According to a Bloomberg report, ACEN and UPC are planning to divest a significant stake—possibly up to 74%—in their 1GW solar power project in India. The potential transaction could value the entire project at US$600 million, implying an equity value of around US$200 million. A full sale at that price would imply an enterprise value per megawatt (EV/MW) of US$600,000, well above India’s replacement cost of US$300,000–400,000 per MW for solar projects.

If realized, the sale could generate notable gains for ACEN and enhance its net asset value (NAV). ACEN currently has 1,300 MW of standalone solar projects in India, including 630 MW in operation, and holds an 80% economic stake in each project. The high valuation relative to investment costs could significantly improve ACEN's capital efficiency.

Century Pacific Rolls Back Sardine Prices by P1

Century Pacific Food, Inc. (CNPF PM) announced a P1 price rollback per can for its Ligo and 555 sardine brands, effective July 1. This translates to a 4–5% reduction, lowering the suggested retail price of a 155g can to around P21. The company stated that improved efficiencies across its value chain have allowed it to pass on cost savings to customers. With hundreds of millions of sardine cans sold annually, the rollback is expected to have a broad consumer impact.

In FY24, CNPF’s branded business—including marine, meat, and milk segments—generated P59.3 billion in revenue, growing 7% year-on-year and comprising around 80% of the company’s total revenues. However, the marine segment’s market share slightly declined to 82% as of 1Q25, down 120 basis points from December 2024.

Hotel 101 to List on NASDAQ

Hotel 101, 70% owned by DoubleDragon (DD PM), is set to debut on the NASDAQ under the ticker “HBNB” on July 1. The hospitality chain currently operates 1,100 rooms across the Philippines and is expanding internationally with a 680-room hotel under construction in Spain and a 480-room project underway in Japan. The listing is expected to enhance the company’s global visibility and attract broader investor interest.

DITO Tel Targets 1 Million Fixed Wireless Users by 2026

DITO Telecommunity Corporation is aiming to reach at least one million subscribers for its 5G-powered, plug-and-play fixed wireless access (FWA) service by 2026. According to DITO President and CEO Eric Alberto, the company’s existing infrastructure can accommodate up to 3.6 million FWA users. He also pointed out that the Philippine home broadband market remains underpenetrated, with only 8–9 million out of 24 million households currently connected.

Since launching its "DITO Home WOWFi" service in October 2024, the telco has amassed 250,000 subscribers as of end-June 2025. It now aims to increase that number to 300,000 by the end of the year.

BSP sees the possibility of two more cuts this year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) stated that there could be up to two more rate cuts this year (to 4.75% from 5.25% currently) given benign inflation and slower-than-expected growth. Inflation YT-May stands at 1.9%, lower than last year's 3.5% for the same period. Meanwhile 1Q25 GDP growth was at 5.4%, lower than consensus expectations of 5.7%.

BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona, Jr. noted that they will remain data dependent prior to deciding if more rate cuts are needed to support economic growth. The next Monetary Board meeting (4th out of 6 this year) will be on Aug 28. For 2025, consensus and Regis expects 75bp total worth of cuts to 5.00%. The Philippine gov't's GDP growth target for 2025 is 5.5-6.5% (lowered from 6-8%). Consensus and Regis expects 2025 GDP to be at 5.5%.

BSP to issue circular restricting access to online gambling

The BSP said it would issue a circular that would require BSP-regulated institutions, such banks and electronic money issuers, to better protect users of their digital platforms from risks associated with online gambling. Protection may come in the form of various limits to gaming access. Recall that in 1Q25, the BSP issued draft regulations that would prohibit online marketplaces from offering or presenting gambling. Separately, the Department of Finance (DOF) said that it is proposing a tax on online gambling and studying potential policies to restrict access to gambling.

Online gaming firms currently pay 38% of their e-casino and e-bingo GGR to the government. They pay 24.25% of sports betting GGR to the government. DigiPlus Interactive is the market leader in e-games, with market share of about 45%.

Senator Seeks Tighter Online Gambling Rules

Senator Sherwin Gatchalian has filed a bill that proposes stricter regulation of online gambling. Key features of the bill include raising the minimum cash-in amount to P10,000—compared to the current P25 on some platforms—and prohibiting the linking of e-wallets to gambling sites. The proposed law would also impose advertising restrictions on online gambling. In contrast, Senator Joel Villanueva has filed a separate bill that seeks to ban all forms of online gambling outright.

Earlier this year, Senators Alan Peter Cayetano and Francis Escudero also called for a thorough review of the online gambling sector. Despite regulatory scrutiny, the industry remains strong: the gross gaming revenue (GGR) for e-games (including bingo) reached P51 billion in the first quarter of 2025—an 88% increase year-on-year, though flat compared to the previous quarter. DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) remains the market leader with an estimated 45% market share and is currently trading at 12.6 times FY25E price-to-earnings, based on consensus forecasts.

NCR Wage Board Approves P50 Minimum Wage Hike

The National Capital Region (NCR) Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) has approved a P50 increase in the daily minimum wage, raising it to P695—an 8% hike. This adjustment follows the 19th Congress's adjournment in June without passing a broader P100–200 across-the-board wage hike bill. The P50 increase represents the highest percentage hike approved by the NCR wage board in the past decade.

The wage adjustment is expected to directly benefit 1.2 million minimum wage earners, while an additional 1.7 million full-time wage workers may benefit indirectly through the correction of wage distortions. The new rate will take effect on July 18, the day after the anniversary of the previous P35 wage hike granted on July 17, 2024.

Lawmakers Begin Filing Priority Bills for the 20th Congress

Several senators and congressmen have begun filing their personal priority bills and resolutions ahead of the 20th Congress, which is scheduled to convene on July 28. Among the notable bills already submitted are proposals for an across-the-board wage hike, with suggested increases ranging from P150 to matching the “estimated average family living wage” of P1,200. Other key proposals include expanding the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps), which currently provides conditional cash transfers to low-income households; stricter regulations or a complete ban on online gambling; and amendments to the Rice Tariffication Law along with the restoration of the National Food Authority’s (NFA) role in maintaining rice affordability.

It’s important to note that filing a bill is only the first step in the legislative process. A bill must pass both houses of Congress, undergo deliberations and reconciliation through a bicameral conference committee, and receive the President’s approval before it can become law.

Government debt inches up to P16.92tn in end-May

The government's total debt stock went up by 1% MoM to P16.92tn as of end-May. This was due to additional borrowings which was partially offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P56.25 end-Apr to P55.70 end-May). Domestic debt rose by 1.6% (P190bn) MoM to P11.78tn due to the impact of net borrowings. External debt decreased by 0.5% (P24bn) MoM to P5.14tn given a decrease in the peso value of foreign debt due to local currency appreciation and net repayments. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.6% and 30.4% being external borrowings.

The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn. This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%.

BSP Revises BOP and Current Account Deficit Projections

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has revised its 2025 balance of payments (BOP) deficit forecast to -1.3% of GDP, wider than its previous projection of -0.8%. For 2026, however, the BOP deficit is now expected to narrow to -0.5% of GDP from -0.8%. The wider 2025 projection reflects continued current account (CA) shortfalls and weaker financial flows, despite an improving trade deficit.

Although the CA deficit is expected to narrow—to -3.3% of GDP in 2025 and -2.5% in 2026 from -3.9% in 2024—this is offset by a softer financial account, driven by global trade uncertainty, geopolitical risks, and subdued investor sentiment. Export growth is expected to weaken in 2025, with goods exports seen contracting by 1% (from +1%) and services export growth slowing to 6% (from 8%).

Nonetheless, business process outsourcing (BPO) revenues and overseas Filipino worker (OFW) remittances remain resilient, providing a cushion to the external position. As of Q1 2025, the CA deficit stands at -3.7% of GDP, compared to -3.8% for full-year 2024.

SEC plans to increase investment options and revise REIT regulations

SEC Chair Francis Lim said he is looking at revising REIT regulations to “better meet market demand.” The SEC will also create a roadmap for alternative investment products, such as options, futures, and commodities.

New customs chief eyes catch up for 2025, raises 2026 collection target

Ariel Nepomuceno has been appointed as the new Commissioner of the Bureau of Customs (BOC), replacing Bienvenido Rubio. His appointment is part of President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s broader effort to revamp the BOC, with five other key appointments announced alongside his. This leadership change follows a disappointing revenue collection performance through May 2025, which came in flat year-on-year (+0.2%) at P382 billion—far short of the government's 8% annual growth target.

The shortfall is largely attributed to reduced tariffs on rice and electric vehicles. In response, Nepomuceno aims to meet the revised 2025 revenue target of P990 billion (+8% YoY), down from the original P1.06 trillion (+16% YoY). For 2026, however, the target has been raised to P1.1 trillion (+11% YoY), which, if achieved, would mark the first time the BOC breaches the P1 trillion threshold. Nepomuceno emphasized that ramping up digitalization should enhance efficiency and transparency, ultimately boosting revenue collection. The national government targets P4.5 trillion in total revenue this year (+16% YoY), with a goal of narrowing the fiscal deficit to 5.5% of GDP. As of Q1 2025, the fiscal deficit stands at 6.8% of GDP.

Election Ban Dampens April Spending

Government infrastructure spending, which includes expenditures by local government units (LGUs) and government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs), grew by 2.4% year-on-year to P419 billion in the first four months of 2025. However, spending for the month of April alone dropped sharply—falling 29% year-on-year and 24% month-on-month to P102 billion. The decline is attributed to frontloaded disbursements in previous months, done in anticipation of the election spending ban that was in effect from March 28 to May 11.

The national government has set a target of P1.54 trillion in infrastructure spending for the year, slightly lower by 0.5% compared to 2024. This figure represents 5.4% of the country’s GDP.

Lower Stock Transaction Tax Now Effective

Effective July 1, Republic Act No. 12214—or the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA)—has reduced the stock transaction tax (STT) from 0.6% to 0.1%. This aligns the Philippines with other ASEAN markets such as Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, and Malaysia, where transaction taxes also average around 0.1%.

This reform is expected to boost trading activity and encourage more participation in the local stock market by lowering trading costs for investors.

DOF Slashes 2025 Privatization Goal by 95%

The Department of Finance (DOF) has drastically lowered its privatization goal for 2025 to just P5 billion, down 95% from its original target of P101 billion. The reduction is attributed to legal issues that have delayed the sale of various government properties. Most of these assets are now expected to be sold in 2026 instead, prompting the DOF to raise its 2026 privatization goal to P100 billion from the previous P1 billion.

The adjustment comes as the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) revised the fiscal deficit targets for 2025 to 2028, largely due to downward revisions in revenue projections. For 2025 alone, revenue estimates have been lowered by P124 billion (-3%) to P4.52 trillion.

Trade Deficit Narrows to US$3.3 Billion in May

The Philippines’ trade deficit narrowed to US$3.3 billion in May 2025, down 30% year-on-year due to a sharp increase in exports and a decline in imports. This brings the five-month trade deficit to US$19.7 billion, which is 5% narrower compared to the same period last year.

Imports fell 4% year-on-year to US$10.6 billion, driven mainly by lower purchases of fuel and raw/intermediate goods. Meanwhile, exports surged 15% year-on-year to US$7.3 billion—the fifth straight month of export growth. Electronics exports rebounded, growing 8% to US$3.8 billion after contracting 5% in April. Electronics made up 53% of total exports. Non-electronics exports also saw strong growth, rising 24% year-on-year to US$3.4 billion, led by other manufactured goods, gold, copper concentrates, machinery, and transport equipment.

BSP Consumer Survey Shows Diverging Short-Term and Long-Term Sentiment

The latest Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) revealed that current consumer sentiment remains negative, with the consumer index (CI) dropping to -14.0% in 2Q25 from -13.0% the previous quarter. Respondents cited rising prices, shrinking wages, fewer job opportunities, and increasing "political noise" as primary concerns behind their pessimism. A negative CI indicates that more respondents are pessimistic than optimistic.

However, consumer expectations for the next quarter turned slightly positive at 0.6%, up from -0.5% in the previous survey. Over a 12-month horizon, consumer sentiment remains optimistic, with the CI at 11.8%, only slightly down from 12.4% in the previous quarter. The optimism is driven by anticipated improvements in inflation, job opportunities, and wage conditions. These survey results are among the data points considered by the BSP in its monetary policy decisions.
