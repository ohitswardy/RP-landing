REGIS REPORT

10 October 2025

The PCOMP pulled back though remained in a tight range as investors weighed September inflation and the BSP's rate decision this week. Though consumer prices rose at a slower clip than expected, the central bank's 25-bp rate cut surprised the market. Still, psychological support at the 6,000-level held, with the index down 1.2% or 71.07 pts WoW to 6,037.79.

September inflation inched up to 1.7% from 1.5% in August, beating consensus forecast of 1.9% and coming within the BSP's forecast range of 1.5-2.3%. This is also the seventh straight month that the CPI print has fallen below the BSP's 2-4% target range, and takes YTD average inflation to 1.7%.

This briefly lifted market sentiment early in the week, before the BSP's unexpected move to cut the key rate by 25bps to 4.75% pulled share prices back. Investors seemed to focus on BSP Gov. Remolona's comments that the slowing domestic growth outlook has prompted continued easing. The central bank chief added that the "sweet spot" for rates has moved lower to around 4.00% (from 5.00% prev.) considering favorable inflation and moderating domestic demand.

Index losers outnumbered the winners 4 to 1 this week. URC (+5.6% WoW) led gainers while MONDE (+2.6% WoW) also managed to buck the downtrend with softer inflation reinforcing views of a healthy 3Q showing for consumer companies. In contrast though, PGOLD (-6.5% WoW) succumbed to profit taking from concerns over increased competition following news of Alfamart launching its franchising program.

Also among the gainers were ICT (+2.7% WoW) which continued to rally to fresh ATHs amid strong foreign inflows, and MER (+2.2% WoW) as investors continued to look to defensives.

Meanwhile, biggest drags on the index were the large banks -- BPI (-5.8% WoW) and BDO (-2.3% WoW) -- with NIMs seen coming under pressure from continued monetary easing. Still, lower rates did nothing to contain the sell-off in ALI (-3.9% WoW) as the outlook on residential remains broadly downbeat.

The peso weakened anew, down 0.7% WoW to 58.269:$1. Foreigners were net sellers for the second straight week, by $16m.

Philippine Property - Light at the end of the tunnel
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Expecting a turnaround next year

The trajectory of the office and residential property sectors should see a positive turn over the next 12 months as the market works through the current surplus. The mall segment, on the other hand, remains healthy and pandemic woes are a distant memory. While a robust operating rebound is not imminent, we believe the worst is behind us. Valuations are near all-time lows and interest rates (which have historically been negatively correlated to property stock prices) will likely decline further. We maintain our Buy ratings on the four major developers.

 

Office rental reversions to start turning positive next year

After a five-year period when rental reversions were negative, we expect office rental reversions to finally turn positive in Makati and BGC by next year. Most other key office property locations should also see the same positive upturn in rental reversions beginning 2027E.

 

Residential reservation sales should begin recovery in 2026E

We forecast reservation sales falling in 2025E, before rising annually in 2026E–2027E, thanks to enticing promotions on ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units, strong provincial sales, and resilient demand for high-end lots. Although the Metro Manila mid-income condominium segment remains challenging, with condominium vacancy and unsold inventory levels remaining elevated, the rationalization of residential property supply should help keep conditions from worsening. 

Meanwhile, demand for provincial projects should remain strong, as infrastructure projects improve access.

 

RLC offers the best risk-reward; prefer SMPH among the large caps

RLC offers, in our view, the most attractive risk-reward balance. The value of its stake in RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) is c. 20% higher than its current market cap, which underscores how undervalued RLC is (5.1% div. yield, 69% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY26E P/B, and 5.0X FY26E P/E).

 

Among the large caps, we prefer SMPH, which we expect to continue hitting record level profits. Its plan to launch high-end residential and commercial lots should also lead to better reservation sales starting next year. Furthermore, we reckon the current share price does not place any value on the company’s coastal development project, which comprises 22% of NAV.

 

Revising target prices

We increase our NAV estimates by 2%-7%, as we roll over to 2026E. Correspondingly, we raise our target prices on ALI, MEG, and RLC by 2%-7%, as we maintain our target discounts (which are already wider than historical averages). However, we lower our target price on SMPH by 7% as we widen our target discount to NAV from (the historical average of) 10% to 20%. See pages 9-10 for changes to our estimates as well as our recommendation table. Key risks: interest rates, regulations, and REIT listings.

Security Bank - Mr Victor Lee appointed as new CEO; opportunities and challenges
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Leadership change – new CFO, and new CEO

SECB announced that Mr Victor Lee will replace Mr Sanjiv Vohra as its next CEO effective in Jan 2026. Mr Lee was most recently CEO of CIMB Singapore, where, according to the SECB disclosure, he doubled the firm’s revenues, and took ROE close to 20%. In August, the bank appointed Mr Allen Reyes as its new CFO. While the bank has rapidly grown its consumer/MSME book to 36% of total loans, it has struggled in 2024/25 with its credit costs, keeping ROEs below 10%. The ongoing cleanup of the credit card book should set a fresh slate for the new leadership. At 0.34x P/book and 3.7x PER on our new 2026E, the bar is not set particularly high. Maintain Buy with a revised P150/sh TP.

 

New CEO and CFO bring deep and broad banking, consumer experience

Mr Victor Lee Meng Tek was announced as SECB’s new CEO effective January 2026, giving time for his “gardening leave”, BSP regulatory approvals. Apart from his stints at CIMB Singapore and Malaysia, Mr Lee also has had exposure to the microfinance, retail, and SME sectors while at Fullerton Financial. Meanwhile, Mr Allen Reyes, as the new CFO, brings his broad experience in the banking, insurance, and consumer sectors from Deutsche Bank, Singlife, Diageo, etc.

Opportunities and challenges – a fresh-ish slate?

The entry of Mr Lee and Mr Reyes comes at a pivotal time for SECB. On the one hand, the bank has rapidly grown its consumer/MSME loans to 36% of total, among the highest in the sector. On the other, asset quality issues have plagued the credit card portfolio, with chunky write-offs dragging NPAT and ROEs in 2024 and into 1H25. To be fair, management has cleaned up and re-focused the consumer book, which should give new leadership a fresh start.

 

Low bar; lots of upside? Estimates adjusted, TP reduced to P150; Buy

We adjust our estimates down 22% for 2025E and 12% for 2026E to reflect higher credit costs. Despite this, the stock is on depressed valuations of 0.34x P/bk and 3.7x PER on our FY26E. With the bar set quite low for the new team, a steeper ROE increase than we are forecasting could see the stock re-rate. Our lowered target price of P150/sh (-6.3%), while providing 107% potential total return, would only put the stock on 0.7x P/bk and 7.4x PER. Maintain Buy.

ICTSI's Mexico port volumes jump 23% YoY in September

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), stood at 148k TEUs, up 23% YoY and the fastest pace in 11 months. CMSA accounted for all the 9% YoY increase in total port volumes at Manzanillo for the month, as competitor volumes slipped 1% YoY. Brings 9M25 volumes at CMSA up 7% YoY to 1.14mn TEUs, lagging our full-year forecast of 1.63mn TEUs (+12% YoY). To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year will need to average 165k TEUs per month. Despite the shortfall, we remain upbeat on the port’s medium-term outlook, supported by the anticipated completion of its ~20% capacity expansion by year-end. CMSA’s capacity will increase to “over 2mn TEUs” (~170k TEUs/month) by year-end from 1.7mn TEUs (~140k per month). We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues and 24% of EBITDA. ICTSI trades at 10x FY26E EV/EBITDA, below peer median of 11x, peer average of 12x and historical average of 11x.

ICTSI secures contract extension for Subic ports

Subic Bay International Terminals Corp. (SBITC) and ICTSI Subic Inc. (ISI) have been granted an extension of their respective concessions until 2058. SBITC and ISI, 90.5%-owned units of ICTSI (ICT PM), operate NCT-1 and NCT-2 terminals at the port of Subic. ICTSI is set to invest US$130mn to increase the annual capacity of its ports in Subic to 1mn TEUs from 600k TEUs. Our forecasts do not assume an expansion (i.e. DCF ends in 2032). We anticipate tariffs may be adjusted upwards to support investment recovery. We estimate the two Subic ports collectively contribute ~2% of revenues and 1% of NAV.

Maynilad secures more cornerstone investors

Maynilad has secured six more international cornerstone investors and four domestic investors for its IPO. Other foreign investors include: abrdn Malaysia Sdn. Bhd., Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (acting through its Mobilising Capital through Listed Products (MOBILIST)), Maven Investment Partners Ltd.-Hong Kong Branch, Maybank Asset Management Singapore Pte. Ltd., Robeco Switzerland Ltd. and QRT Master Fund-Torus Fund SP. Domestic cornerstone investors include BDO Capital, BPI Asset Management, Metrobank, Security Bank Trust. Based on the latest prospectus submitted to the Securities and Exchange Commission. IFC and ADB will be the lead cornerstone investors, after committing to invest US$100mn and US$145mn respectively. The offer period for the IPO is scheduled from Oct. 23 to 29, 2025, with the listing date set for Nov. 7, 2025. Assuming full subscription of the IPO shares (1.9bn primary and 355mn secondary) at the revised max. offer price of P15/sh, Maynilad’s market capitalization could reach P113bn, with a public float of 30%.

Maynilad eyes expansion opportunities in Bulacan, Cavite, Pasay's Harbor City

Maynilad is evaluating adjacent areas of its West Zone concession – Bulacan, Cavite, and reclamation areas in Manila Bay – for possible expansions. It plans to partner with local water districts in Bulacan and Cavite to leverage on its existing West Zone infrastructure. New reclamation activities along the coast of Manila Bay (i.e. Navotas and Pasay) could also be tapped given proximity to the West Zone’s infrastructure. The West Zone covers 11 cities in Metro Manila and three cities and three municipalities in Cavite Province, including Bacoor, Imus, Kawit, Rosario, Noveleta, and Cavite City.

Alfamart launches franchising program

Alfamart Philippines has launched its franchising program to ramp up its nationwide expansion with the MSME-led (micro, small and medium enterprise) franchising. The official launch of franchising follows the pilot program of two franchise-owned stores in Laguna with the first having opened back in Oct 2024 and the second opened in Aug this year. Alfamart has also engaged in strategic partnerships with local entrepreneurs such as the co-locating of laundromat stores as well as food and beverage brands Coco Fresh Tea & Juice and Potato Corner located inside select Alfamart stores to drive foot traffic. The SM Food Retail segment posted SSSG of +4.7% in the 1H25, with Alfamart SSSG flat for the same period. As of end-Sep, Alfamart has 2,337 stores nationwide, having opened 245 stores so far this year. This is in line with the company's target to open around 300 stores in 2025. Alfamart, part of the SM Group’s (SM PM) retail food segment, operates as a joint venture with Indonesia-based PT Sumber Alfaria Trijaya Tbk (AMRT IJ).

DOE clears Meralco's 200-MW power supply CSP

Energy Undersecretary Mario C. Marasigan said the department had issued a certificate of conformity (CoC) for Meralco’s proposed 200MW competitive selection process (CSP). The 200MW CSP will cover renewable baseload capacity requirements from Jan 26, 2026 to Jan 25, 2030. It is intended to replace ACEN Corp’s terminated baseload contract with Meralco. The issuance of a CoC by the DoE is a regulatory requirement before a CSP can begin. Bidding forms part of Meralco’s plan to secure over 2,100 MW of capacity under its long-term supply procurement plan covering 2026 to 2046. The Department of Energy has yet to issue COCs for Meralco’s proposed CSPs: 450MW mid-merit (Feb 2028 to Feb 2029), 600MW baseload (Feb 26, 2028), and 900MW baseload (15 years from Feb 26, 2030).

Aboitiz, partners get ERC nod for wind farm grid access

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has given Aboitiz-backed Lihangin Wind Energy Corp. (LWEC) the green light to build transmission facilities for its 206MW San Isidro Wind project in Samar. The project’s interim grid connection will be through the Calbayog substation of NGCP, pending completion of the proposed 138-kilovolt San Isidro substation for its permanent link. LWEC is a joint venture of Aboitiz Renewables Inc., Vivant Energy Corp. and Singapore-based Vena Energy. In case the subject assets shall be required for competitive purposes, the ownership of the same shall be transferred to NGCP using the fair market price of the said facilities, subject to optimization.

DigiPlus partners with Bayad for OTC cash-in

DigiPlus signed an agreement with Bayad, a bill payment service unit of Meralco. Customers of DigiPlus can now make deposits (cash-in) to their accounts through Bayad Centers. There are over 800 Bayad Centers nationwide. Customers can also make deposits in BingoPlus stores. There are over 130 BingoPlus stores nationwide. We expect only minor impact from the partnership with Bayad as we expect most customers to continue making deposits/cash-in through e-wallets.

Del Monte eyes to raise up to US$600mn through private placement

Del Monte Pacific Ltd. (DELM PM) intends to raise US$500-600mn through private placement at Del Monte Philippines Inc. (DMPI) to address the capital deficit following the recent deconsolidation of its US business. Del Monte seeks to reduce financing costs by replacing higher-interest debt with equity that carries a lower dividend yield. Recall that US subsidiary Del Monte Foods Holdings Ltd. initiated voluntary Chapter 11 proceedings in the US Bankruptcy Court last July 1. Management added that the placement will also ensure that uncertainties in the US business will not affect the company's continuing core operations.

BSP cuts rate by 25bp to 4.75%; expects GDP growth to slow

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.75%, the lowest since Sep 2022. This is the fourth consecutive 25bp cut this year (100bp total) since previously cutting rates in Apr, Jun, and Aug. The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 175bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024. The BSP stated that the outlook on GDP growth has softened in the near term, highlighting dampened business sentiment from "governance concerns" on public infra spending and moderating demand due to external uncertainties. The BSP kept its GDP growth forecast for 2025 and 2026 at 5.5% and 6.0%, respectively. These are at the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% and 6-7% growth targets for the same periods. 3Q25 GDP data will be released on Nov 7. BSP Governor Eli Remolona noted that the "sweet spot" has moved closer to 4% now vs the initial assessment of 5% especially with the favorable inflation outlook and moderating domestic demand. Another cut this year was said to be a possibility as "the Monetary Board sees scope for a more accommodative monetary policy stance." The inflation outlook was noted to be benign and well within the target range with expectations well-anchored. BSP still expects inflation to settle at 1.7% for 2025 but revised its inflation forecasts for 2026 and 2027 to 3.1% and 2.8% from 3.3% and 3.4%, respectively. Primary risks were said to be potential electricity rate adjustments and increased rice tariffs. Remolona also guided that peso defense will be done if depreciation is sharp and large enough to be inflationary. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75% and inflation to average 1.8%.

Inflation rate inches up to 1.7% in September

Headline inflation picked up to 1.7% in Sept from 1.5% in the previous month of Aug. This is lower than the consensus forecast of 1.9% but is within the BSP's forecast range of 1.5-2.3% for Sept. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past seven months. Primary drivers for the faster pace in inflation in Sept was food up 1.0% YoY, a tad higher from 0.9% YoY in Aug as well as transport which was up 1.0% YoY reversing from a 0.3% decline the previous month. Food was driven by a sharp 19.4% YoY increase in vegetable prices given adverse weather while transport saw higher diesel prices. Notably, rice prices are on their fifteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Oct 4), prices for local and imported well-milled rice range from P38-52/kg and P38-46/kg, respectively vs. P42-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 2. Core inflation inched down to 2.6% in Sept from 2.7% in Aug, while core non-food inflation climbed to 2.4% from 2.1% the previous month. This brings the YT-Sep headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting (5th out of 6 this year) will be on Oct 9. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%.

Gov't extends rice import ban till April 2026; lawmakers push for higher rice import tariffs

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has approved The Dept. of Agriculture's (DA) plan to extend the current three-month rice import ban to the end-April 2026 with a one-month rice import window in Jan 2026. Recall that a suspension of rice imports from Sep 15 to end-Nov was done to boost farmgate prices given the current peak harvest season of palay (unmilled rice). DA Secretary Tiu Laurel said rice imports should have totaled only 2.7 MMT as of 9M25, but the actual volume has already exceeded the target by 800,000 tons, reaching 3.5 MMT by end-Sept. He emphasized that the DA continues to push for the reversion to a higher rice import tariff but notes that it is still under review. The original duty on imported rice was 35% but reduced to 15% until 2028 back in Jul 2024 to reduce the retail prices of rice. House Speaker Faustino Dy III stated that one million farmers will receive P7,000 in cash aid under the proposed 2026 budget to mitigate losses from low palay prices. Dy noted that long-term reforms are being sought with the RICE Act (Rice Industry and Consumer Empowerment), which is a priority bill. The bill seeks to restore the National Food Authority's (NFA) power to buy palay during harvest season, raise the rice import tariff to 35%, set a P25/kg palay floor price, and allocate 75% of rice tariff revenue to farmers.

DOF priority tax reforms expected to generate P170bn in the next five years

The Department of Finance (DOF) expects to raise P170.9bn in the next five years from its priority tax reforms, according to the DBCC’s 2025 Mid-Year Report. About P3.8bn is expected to be collected this year. The reforms include: VAT on Digital Services (RA 12023) – estimated to generate P126.8bn from 2025–2030; requires both local and foreign platforms to remit VAT on local transactions. CREATE MORE Act (RA 12066) – projected to reduce revenues by P13.1bn from 2025–2030 due to lower corporate income tax (25% from 30%) and expanded deductions on power and trade fair expenses. Capital Market Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA, RA 2214) – expected to raise P35bn from 2025–2030 by rationalizing capital market taxes and adding an excise tax on pickup trucks. Mining Fiscal Regime (RA 12253) – will take effect January 2026, projected to generate P32.1bn in incremental revenues. The revenue-to-GDP ratio is projected to rise from 15.9% in 2025 to 16.8% by 2030. Overall tax revenues are seen to grow 10.3% annually until 2030, reaching P7.13tn by then (from P4.52tn in 2025).

Customs' revenues up 8% in September

Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P82bn in revenues for the month of Sept, up 7% vs the same month last year. Customs Assistant Commissioner Vincent Maronilla previously guided a recovery in collections during the '-ber' months as previous months saw lackluster volumes. Confiscated smuggled goods from illicit trade is expected to amount to P60bn this year or just 40% of the P150bn seized last year. Maronilla attributed the lower haul due to the suspension of their enforcement operations outside Customs premises. Total customs collections as of 9M25 amounted to P704bn, 2% higher YoY. The figure is 73% of the BOC's P959bn (+4.5% YoY) revised full-year collection target. The BOC's initial target set for this year was P1.06trn (+16% YoY).

PH dollar reserves at $108.8bn end-Sep

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Sep increased 1.6% MoM to US$108.8bn from US$107.1bn at end-Aug. The increase primarily reflected the surge in global gold prices which offset the drop in foreign exchange holdings due to the BSP's intervention in the forex market. The peso depreciated against the US dollar from P57.04 on Sep 1 to P58.04 by end-Sep. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.6x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$105bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn.

SEC to expand definition of income-generating assets for REIT listings

The SEC is drafting amendments to REIT regulations to expand and clarify what qualifies as income-generating assets. SEC Chairperson Francisco Lim believes that certain power, infrastructure, and telecommunication assets should qualify. He enumerated a few examples: electric towers, power plants, toll roads, and [cellular] towers.

BSP eyes tighter rules on digital/cash transfers to curb fund misuse, protect banks

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is deliberating on new thresholds not just for large cash withdrawals but also for digital and other fund transfers in a continued effort to prevent the misuse of public funds and strengthen detection of suspicious transactions. For now, the proposed thresholds will likely apply to government transactions, especially transfers from the state to private individuals or contractors. BSP Governor Eli Remolona noted that banks were uneasy about releasing funds from gov't agencies that are suspicious but could not refuse since the money came from the Treasury. The central bank plans to clearly define when banks can refuse transactions suspected of corruption. Uniform rules are sought to be applied across institutions to prevent depositors from simply moving funds elsewhere. Recall that the BSP required financial institutions to cap large-value cash transactions at P500,000 per banking day unless the amount is processed through traceable channels.

Government says IRR for KPA Law out next week

Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) aims to publish the IRR for the Konektadong Pinoy Law next week, according to DICT Secretary Henry Rhoel Aguda. DICT completed the final round of consultations with telecommunications companies. Aguda stated that publication will take ~15 days which implies that the law will be in effect by end-Oct. DICT previously aimed to release the IRR in the first week of October.

Draft Konektadong Pinoy IRR seeks stronger cybersecurity and competitive safeguards

The Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) eyes to tighten the cybersecurity requirements of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) ahead of the potential entry of new players. The draft implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the KPA exhibit a tighter due diligence process to reinforce cybersecurity protocols and to encourage fair competition. Some of the draft rules and regulations are as follows: The DICT will formulate guidelines which will detail the risk profile per data transmission industry participant (DTIP) segment with considerations for market share, network coverage, among others. DTIPs must follow DICT cybersecurity rules under the National Cybersecurity Plan, including a response team, secure SDLC, and zero-trust framework. DTIPs need to adopt global standards for internet security, submit risk plans, vulnerability tests, and incident reports. DTIPs are required to obtain a third-party issued cybersecurity certification and certificate of DICT compliance within two years from registration. Non-compliance will lead to suspension, license revocation, and permanent ban from PH operations.

Digital payments +45% YoY in August; InstaPay crosses P1trn +65% YoY

Digital payments over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) clocked in at P2,086bn in August, up 44.8% YoY, but edging down slightly -2.3% MoM from July. This took the year-to-August figure up to P15,348bn, up 40.9% YoY, faster than the 35.4% YoY pace in FY2024. The growth was led by InstaPay – for smaller-ticket payments of up to P50,000 – which crossed the P1trn level for the first time to P1,027bn, up 64.6%, the fastest monthly YoY growth rate since November 2021. For 8M25, InstaPay transactions were up 54.6% YoY to P6,898bn, just a hair’s breadth away from the P7,347bn in FY2024. Meanwhile, the volume of transactions continued to explode, up 255% YoY, indicating continued broadening of adoption for everyday payments.

LPC: Net office demand declines in 3Q25; residential take-up increases

During its 3Q25 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Metro Manila office vacancy was broadly flat QoQ at 18%. We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Net take-up hit 49,000sqm nationwide in 3Q25 – from 182,000sqm in 2Q25. BPO take-up seems to have declined in 3Q25. Meanwhile, LPC estimates that residential sales grew 16% QoQ (+12% YoY) to 7,713 units, the highest level in 7 quarters. Unsold condominium units in Metro Manila fell 4% QoQ to 79,400. We estimate that unsold inventory represents 38 months of take-up (vs 41 months in 2Q25).

Justice Secretary Remulla appointed as Ombudsman

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has appointed Dept. of Justice (DOJ) Secretary Jesus Crispin "Boying" Remulla as the new Ombudsman, replacing Samuel Martires whose term ended July. The Office of the Ombudsman is responsible for investigating and prosecuting government officials accused of wrongdoing, particularly graft and corruption. Remulla stated that "the people can expect transparency and a lot of action" and emphasized that strict access to the Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth (SALN) of gov’t officials will be lifted. Prior to being appointed as Ombudsman, Remulla was assigned as the DOJ Secretary back in June 2022. He previously served as Cavite Representative for three terms from 2004 to 2022 as well as Cavite Governor from 2016 to 2019. The President in a statement said that: "There will be no sacred cows, no exemptions, and no excuses." He emphasized that those who betray public trust will be held accountable. DOJ Undersecretary Fredderick Vida will serve as acting Justice Secretary, stepping in as OIC. Vida was appointed as an assistant secretary in 2023. As assistant secretary, he handled the DOJ's finance, administration, and personnel cluster.
