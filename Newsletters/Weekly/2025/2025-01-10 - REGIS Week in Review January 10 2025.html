REGIS REPORT

10 January 2025

The PCOMP pulled back this week, down 1.6% or 107.49 pts WoW to 6,496.32 as local shares were dragged by foreign outflows. This also came with slightly improved trading activity, though ADTO remained well below $100m at $73m vs last week's $61m. 

WLCON (-25.1% WoW) was the PCOMP's largest decliner for the week amid growing bets for its deletion at the February PSE Index rebalancing. In contrast, AREIT (+6.3% WoW) -- which is widely predicted to replace WLCON in the index -- outperformed. Still, large caps SM (-4.2% WoW) and SMPH (-4.4% WoW) were the biggest drag to the index this week, resp. accounting for over half and a third of the PCOMP's WoW decline. 

Meanwhile, GLO (+6.3% WoW) was the biggest advancer in the index as investors grew optimistic over the planned Mynt (GCash) IPO. Wires have reported that the PSE is weighing its exemption from the 20% minimum float requirement due to a potential record listing size of $1.7bn -- note this would also imply Mynt could fetch an $8-bn valuation which is a 60% premium over the $5-bn valuation at the last funding round in August 2024. Reports also said Mynt has already engaged several banks to run the potential deal, to which GLO only responded that they currently do not have any material information to disclose at this time.

All sector indices save for Mining/Oil closed the week lower, with Holdings (-2.4% WoW) and Property (-2.1%) down the most as investors continued to weigh global uncertainties and domestic macro headwinds, and their impact to the peso and rates outlook.

On macro, December inflation accelerated to 2.9% from 2.5% in November -- also printing worse than Street's 2.6% estimate. The culprit seemed to be the faster increase in housing & utilities (+2.9% from +1.9% previously). Food inflation was steady at +3.4%, with hotter price increases in meat (+4.9% from 3.9%) and vegetables (+14.2% from 5.9%) offset by softer rice inflation (+0.8% from +5.1%). On monetary policy, BSP Gov. Remolona again stated that the central bank still has room to ease, following 75bps of rate cuts last year that brought the key rate to 5.75%. However, he cautioned that despite 2024 inflation of 3.2% falling within the government's 2-4% target band, there are still uncertainties looming with Trump's trade policies that post upside risks to inflation. BSP Monetary Board will have its first policy review on February 20.

The peso depreciated by another 0.3% WoW to 58.356, taking the currency's YTD decline to 0.88%. The PH10Y yield was relatively steady, just down 0.8 bps to 6.1485%.

Robinsons Land - RLC appoints new President and CEO
 
 
by Carl Sy +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Lance Gokongwei to step down as President and CEO

Robinsons Land announced that Ms. Ma. Socorro Isabelle “Mybelle” V. Aragon-Gobio will replace Mr. Lance Gokongwei as President and CEO beginning 1 February 2025. Mr. Gokongwei will remain as Chairman of RLC.

Why is this happening?

The appointment of Ms. Aragon-Gobio is in line with the promotion of (non-family) professional management to the top position across much of the JG Summit Group over the past few years. It has taken place in Universal Robina (URC PM), Cebu Air (CEB PM), and more recently, in Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM).

Furthermore, Mr. Gokongwei, who has several positions across the JG Summit Group, is likely too busy to perform the functions of RLC President and CEO adequately given his other responsibilities. He only took over as President and CEO in January 2024, after the previous President and CEO, Frederick Go, stepped down to join the Marcos Administration.

What is the background of Ms. Aragon-Gobio?

Ms. Aragon-Gobio is a veteran of Robinsons Land and has served numerous roles over her 31 years in the company. She is currently the head of the Logistics and Industrial (warehousing) division as well as Robinsons Destination Estates (townships). She was also previously the head of RLC’s residential and office businesses.

Nickel Asia - Coral Bay HPAL divestment plans
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Negotiating sale of Coral Bay HPAL to Sumitomo

NIKL disclosed that it is in discussions with its shareholder and HPAL joint venture partner, Japan-based Sumitomo Metal Mining (5713 JP), to sell its entire 15.625% stake in Coral Bay Nickel Corp. (CBNC), the entity that owns the 20k Ni-MT high-pressure acid leach (HPAL) plant in Palawan. Negotiations for the exit valuation are ongoing. We view this latest development positively, given the formidable operational challenges of its HPAL facilities. Beyond this divestment, however, the viability of the two domestic HPAL plants that buy almost 40% of NIKL’s ore may be an issue.

Exit will not disrupt nickel ore offtake contract

CBNC accounted for 14% (1.9mn WMT) of NIKL’s 9M24 nickel ore sales volumes (13.6mn WMT) and we understand from management that the sale will not materially affect its offtake agreement with CBNC. It also helps that there are no other nickel ore miners near the HPAL plant, apart from NIKL’s own mines: Rio Tuba and Bulanjao.

CBNC to be reclassified as available-for-sale starting Jan 2025

NIKL noted that its investment in CBNC will be reclassified as an available-for-sale security; hence, it will no longer recognize any associate income/losses from this asset on its P&L beginning January 2025. Note that CBNC reported a net loss of P3.2bn and NIKL’s share of this loss was P503mn, or 20% of its P2.6bn profit in 9M24.  
Recall that NIKL holds a 10% stake in another HPAL plant (Taganito HPAL) in Surigao, which is also loss-making, and we suspect the company may also sell this eventually. Given depressed LME nickel prices (spot at US$15,180), these plants will unlikely turn a profit anytime soon.

Exit valuation under negotiation

NIKL will engage a third party to conduct a valuation of its shares in CBNC. As of end-September 2024, CBNC was carried at P1.89bn in NIKL’s books. Meanwhile, our valuation puts NIKL’s share of CBNC at P4.6bn (P0.33/sh).

PSE weighing exemptions for GCash IPO's $8bn valuation target

The Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE), with the help of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), is exploring the possibility of granting an exemption by lowering the 20% float requirement for the IPO of Globe Telecom's (GLO PM) mobile financial services app GCash. The rationale behind this would be because of the large size, with an estimated IPO valuation of P465bn (US$8bn), implying P90bn needs to be raised—much larger than the current record holder, Monde Nissin, which raised approximately P56bn in 2021. The P465bn potential valuation would make the company bigger than the market caps of Globe (GLO PM) P316bn and Ayala Corp. (AC PM) P377bn. Comparatively, it would also be bigger than Metrobank (MBT PM) P339bn. PSE CEO Ramon Monzon stated that Ayala has inquired him about possibly reducing the required float of 20%, and he mentioned that he is not too dogmatic about it. Based on current rules, IPO candidates need to sell at least 20% of shares when listing and must also maintain a public float of at least 10%. On IPOs raising over P5bn, they are allowed to sell just 15% to the public and then raise the remaining 5% in the next 2-3 years. An SEC exemption could also allow selling below 15% while meeting the 10% minimum float requirement, without mandating a follow-on share offering. Previous reports stated that GCash plans to list in 2H25 in an exclusive PSE listing instead of a dual PH-US-IPO. Although the deal timing has yet to be finalized, Monzon states that it may happen this year.

Jollibee's Milksha to buy 70% stake in Taiwan's Moon Moon Food

Milkshop International Co., Ltd. (Milksha), a 51%-owned subsidiary of Jollibee Foods Corporation (JFC PM), signed an agreement to buy a 70% stake in Tien Hsia Sheng Co., Ltd. (Moon Moon Food). Yung-Cheng Lai, Moon Moon Food Founder and CEO, will retain ownership of the 30% minority interest. Once the transaction is completed, Moon Moon Food will be consolidated into Milksha's portfolio and financial reports. While JFC will take on 51% of any acquisition impact to Milksha. The deal will be made through a share sale and purchase agreement with Milksha acquiring 980,000 Moon Moon Food shares at NT$105.92 (P187.80) per share, with the total price at NT$103.8mn, equivalent to US$3.15mn or P183.9mn. The purchase price was based on a multiples-based valuation of Moon Moon Food's FY23 NPAT. Moon Moon Food is currently operating a profitable business with 13 restaurants in Taiwan and has recently opened its first international location in Singapore in 2024. The restaurant is a popular brand in Taiwan specializing in Chinese wellness soups and has received the Michelin Bib Gourmand award for seven consecutive years from 2018 to present. As of September 2024, JFC has 9,598 stores in its network globally—3,340 in the Philippines and 6,258 internationally.

Meralco's PacificLight to build hydrogen-ready gas power plant in Singapore

PacificLight Power Pte Ltd. was awarded the right to build, own, and operate a hydrogen-ready Combined Cycle Gas Turbine (CCGT) facility in Jurong Island by the Energy Market Authority of Singapore. The project will be the largest single H-class CCGT plant in Singapore, with a capacity of at least 600MW. The site can accommodate a second CCGT unit. The plant is scheduled for commercial operations in January 2029. Recall that MGen Gas Energy Hldgs. Inc. President and CEO Yari Miralao previously said that the company plans to invest US$900mn if it wins the project. PacificLight is a 58%-owned unit of Meralco’s (MER PM) PowerGen Corp. (MGen), while the balance is owned by First Pacific. It currently operates an 830MW LNG plant in Jurong Island. Other greenfield projects of PacificLight include a 100MW hydrogen-ready gas turbine, with commercial operations slated to begin by 2Q25, and a 600MW solar farm in Bulan Island in Indonesia (granted conditional approval by the Singaporean government).

ERC to review Aboitiz-Meralco-San Miguel US$3.3bn LNG deal

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) said it will review the joint acquisition of Meralco/Aboitiz (Chromite) of two gas-fired power plants and an LNG terminal of San Miguel Global Power (SMGP) to ensure compliance with market share limitations. According to ERC Chairperson and CEO Monalisa Dimalanta, under the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) of 2001, no company or related group can own, operate, or control more than 30% of the installed generation capacity of a grid and 25% on a national scale. Recall that the Philippine Competition Commission approved the deal last month, but subject to certain conditions, including PCC oversight of the Competitive Selection Process (CSP). Under the US$3.3bn deal, Meralco and Aboitiz will jointly invest in SMGP’s 1,278-megawatt (MW) Ilijan power plant, its new 1,320-MW combined cycle power facility (Excellent Energy), and its LNG import and regasification terminal. MER will own a 40% stake in the gas-fired plants and LNG terminal; SMC’s stake will be diluted to 33% (from 100%) while AP will own 27%. The transaction will be funded through a leveraged buyout, wherein 50% of the funds will come from debt that will be injected into the operating companies.

House receives new bill for ABS-CBN franchise

The bill (House Bill No. 11252) filed at the House of Representatives last Tuesday seeks to provide media company ABS-CBN Corp. (ABS PM) with a new 25-year franchise. Four bills seeking to provide ABS a new franchise have been filed since 2022, and the proposals remain pending at the House legislative franchises committee. For the 9M24, ABS-CBN saw a net loss of P2.59bn, on the back of lower cable TV and broadband revenues.

Commission on Audit tells MWSS to collect P5.1bn concession fees from Maynilad

The Commission on Audit has required the Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) to provide proof of its efforts to collect concession fees worth P5.071bn from water concessionaire Maynilad. DITO slashes capex budget to focus on efficiency

Inflation rate up to 2.9% in December

Headline inflation rose to 2.9% in December from 2.5% in November. This is slightly higher than consensus forecast of 2.6% and came in at the higher end of BSP's forecast range of 2.3-3.1%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose 0.6%, primarily driven by higher food prices which increased by 0.8% MoM. The increase was mainly due to price upticks in vegetables and meat due to the several typhoons that hit the country in October and November. Rice CPI sequentially dipped for the sixth consecutive month by 0.7% bringing the cumulative decline to 3.7%. Outside of food, increases in the price of rentals and LPG contributed the higher inflation print, rising 2.4% and 7.8% respectively. Core inflation increased slightly to 2.8% in December from 2.5% in November. While core non-food inflation inched up to 2.8% from 2.7% for the same periods. With this, the country's average inflation rate in 2024 is at 3.2% in-line with BSP expectations and lower than the 6% average for 2023.

BSP chief states the central bank still has room to ease

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona states that "we're still in restrictive territory compared to what we think the Goldilocks rate is. So there's still some room to ease." Recall that the Monetary Board brought the target reverse repurchase rate to 5.75% as it delivered a total of 75 bps of rate cuts last year. Although the FY24 average inflation rate of 3.2% was within the gov't target band of 2-4%, the BSP Governor states the uncertainties looming from US President elect Donald Trump's trade policies poses risks to the upside. Key factors that could impact the rate cut expectations would be Trump's immigration policy as well as the proposed 10% universal tariff and 60% tariff on Chinese goods. Remolona however believes that the Philippines may be in better shape than other countries due to its significant reliance on services trade of BPO revenues and remittances. The BSP Governor also highlighted that the Fed has become more cautious than before as the market expects rate cuts of 25-50 bps this year vs. 100 bps previously. Despite this, Remolona emphasized data-driven monetary policy decision making that is not too reliant on the Fed's decisions. We expect the BSP to cut its policy rate by 75bps in 2025. Jefferies expects the Fed to cut rates by another 75bps for 2025. The BSP Monetary Board will have its first policy review on Feb. 20.

Trade deficit narrows to $4.76bn in November

The November trade deficit narrowed down to US$4.76bn, the smallest in three months, down both 0.04% YoY and 17.5% MoM from $5.8bn last Oct. Despite the faster contraction of exports this month, the slower but bigger decline in terms of the value of imports narrowed the deficit for the period. Imports declined 5% YoY to US$10.45bn. This decline is the first after four months of increases due to a big drop in fuel imports by 24% as well as decreases in capital and raw/intermediate goods imports at 4% and 2% respectively. Exports continue to fall at 9% to US$5.69bn, marking the third consecutive month of contraction. Led by the decline in electronics exports which continued to decrease down 21% YoY to US$2.78bn, electronics accounted for 49% of total exports during the period. Bringing the YT-November trade deficit to US$49.96bn, +3% YoY.

Dollar reserves drop 1.5% at end-December

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-December saw a 1.5% MoM decrease to $106.8bn from $108.5bn at the end-November. Primarily due to the BSP's net foreign exchange operations, payments for foreign debt obligations, downward valuation adjustments on gold holdings. The level of dollar reserves by end-Dec was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The BSP expects the country's GIR to settle at $106bn by end-2024. Reserve position in the IMF increased 1.1% MoM to $676.2mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $11bn, slightly down 0.2% MoM. Foreign investments declined by 1.3% MoM to $90bn. The end-December figure is in-line with the BSP's end-2024 target of $106bn. While the BSP expects $110bn by end-2025.

Government debt inches up to P16.09tn in end-November

The government’s total debt stock rose by 0.4% (P71bn) MoM to P16.09tn as of end-November. Cited depreciation of the PHP against the USD. Domestic debt rose by 0.3% (P31.8bn) MoM to P10.9tn due to the net issuance of domestic securities and the impact of the depreciation PHP on retail dollar bonds. External debt rose by 0.8% (P39bn) MoM to P5.17tn on the back of the PHP’s depreciation and net foreign loan additions. The government is targeting its outstanding debt to end at P16.06tn in 2024 (60.6% of GDP) and P17.35tn (60.4% of GDP) in 2025 (see here). As of end-September 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 61.3%. The ratio is expected to decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); By the end of the Marcos admin’s term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%.

Rice imports reach record 4.7mn MT in 2024

The country’s rice imports in 2024 reached 4.68mn MT, nearly 30% higher than the 3.61mn MT recorded in 2023. According to the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI). Vietnam remained as the country’s top rice supplier at 3.56mn MT (76%), followed by Thailand (598k MT) and Pakistan (284k MT).

Regional rice prices collapsing

Rice export prices out of Vietnam are in freefall. As of Wed night (early morning US time), the price of the grain declined 4% to $444.50/MT, compared to last week's $461.75. That's down 28% from the high of $619.50 in December 2023. This is the lowest level since March 2023. VN and TH rice prices started to fall sharply after India and Pakistan lifted export bans in October 2024, due to large oversupplies as a result of the ban. Since then, both countries have been competing aggressively to supply other countries, most recently with Indonesia. In December, both nations started negotiations with the Philippines for a combined 2mn MT of rice (1mn MT each). Last week, the Department of Agriculture (DA) said it expects to sign a deal with Pakistan by June. For perspective, the Philippines last year imported a record 4.68mn MT of rice.

Maharlika plans first investment in 1Q25

The Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) is expected to make its first investment this quarter, most likely in the energy sector. According to MIC President and CEO, Rafael D. Consing, Jr. Its plan will be up for board approval on January 9. Mr. Consing said he believes that investments in the energy sector, particularly transmission lines, would be “most impactful.” MIC would like to buy into the national grid, if it’s able to, or execute on its signed MOUs with SPUGS (small power utilities group) in Mindoro and Palawan. State-owned, National Power Corp. is currently doing an inventory of all its assets in Mindoro and MIC “will have to sit down and determine which part of those assets it would need as part of the expansion of these lines.” MIC previously said that it will invest in energy, food security, healthcare, and resource development, particularly mining. If its due diligence on the healthcare sector works out well, it could invest in 1H.

FX loans out of banks' FCDU flat in 3Q24

FX loans out of banks’ Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDU) were broadly flat in 3Q24 at $15.75bn, up just 1.6% YoY and 0.7% QoQ. FX loans accounted for just 6.1% of total loans, a new low (was 6.6% in 2Q24). This was partly due to the PHP strength in 3Q; in PHP terms, FX loans were up just 0.6% YoY, and down 3.7% QoQ. FX deposits, grew 10.9% YoY and 4.2% QoQ to $57.5bn. The 3Q24 YoY growth was a further slowdown from the 12.6% pace in 2Q24 and 20.3% in 1Q24, suggesting that the “flight to USD” is easing somewhat. It also helped that the USD weakened against the PHP in 3Q (from P58.61 in June to P56.05 in Sept). Give the USD’s strength in 4Q, it will be interesting to see if FX depos re-accelerates. FX deposits accounted for 16.8% of total, down from a recent high of 17.5% in 1Q24. This takes FX LDR down to 27.4%. This suggests that the PHP LDR is up to 82.8%, up from 79.4% in 2Q24, and much higher than the 73.6% of total loans.

BSP considering subscription fee on digital transactions

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said the central bank is considering a subscription fee model for digital transactions. This fee will be in lieu of person-to-person electronic fund transfers fees.

Tax exemption on secondary trades of asset-backed securities takes effect

The Bureau of Internal Revenue said that the secondary trade and transfer of asset-backed securities (ABS) is now exempt from all taxes. The original issuance of ABS and other securities related solely to such securitization transaction shall be subject to DST but shall be exempt from other taxes. Income gained from ABS will be subject to 20% final withholding tax, but yields from low-cost or socialized-housing related ABS will be exempt from income tax. However, the income to be exempted will have to come from the securitization of mortgage and housing-related receivables of government housing agencies or must be certified by the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development and the Department of Finance.

LRT-1 operator petitions to raise fares by 36%

The Department of Transportation held its first public hearing on the Light Rail Manila Corp.’s (LRMC) petition to hike fares for the LRT-1 rail line. LRMC is seeking to raise the boarding fee to P18.15 (+37% from current P13.29) and per kilometer fee to P1.65 (+36% from P1.21). This would increase the single journey end-to-end trip of a passenger to P60 from P45 today. Government has a maximum of 30 days from the public hearing to evaluate the petition and new tariffs could be implemented by April. LRMC president and CEO Enrico Benipayo justified the concessionaire’s fare hike appeal by presenting several improvements in the LRT1. Added five new stations (+6.5km) last November and added more trains. Since LRMC took over the concession, there has only been one fare increase approved in 2023 and the deferral of previous tariff petitions has resulted in a P2.17bn deficit as of Nov 2024. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (35.8%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19.2%), and Macquarie Infra (10%).
