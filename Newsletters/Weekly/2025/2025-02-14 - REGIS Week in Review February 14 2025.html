REGIS REPORT

14 February 2025

The PCOMP ended the week in the red, -1.5% or -93.66 pts WoW to 6,061.33 as the higher-for-longer view continued to weigh down sentiment. After the hotter-than-expected US CPI report released mid-week pushed Fed rate cut expectations further down to December (from September previously), the BSP surprised the market with a rate pause at its first policy-setting meeting for the year.

 

This sent developers further down at the week's close, with SMPH (-10.3% WoW) and ALI (-8.3% WoW) whipsawing between sharp gains and losses these last three weeks. Thus the Property sector index was the week's worst performer, down 6.4% WoW.

 

Index rebalancing predictions also continued to drive outsized moves in the market. Top gainer JFC (+9.7% WoW) rose 10% on the day it announced that the PSE removed its foreign ownership limit, which led to speculations of a potential upweight in regional indices given the expected increase in its float available to foreigners. On the other hand, largest decliner EMI (-14.9% WoW) sold off on chatter of its potential removal from another regional index.

 

Still on index matters, MSCI rebalancing results released mid-week confirmed the widely-speculated deletion of JGS (-5.3% WoW) and URC (+0.6% WoW) from the Standard Index. Both names will be migrated to the Small Cap Index, along with the addition of MONDE (+3.95% WoW).

 

Other corporate stories in focus this week included GLO's (-0.8% WoW) 4Q earnings that saw a one-time clean-up of assets drag Mynt/GCash earnings, which management assured was not related to the lending business. Meanwhile, ICT (-3.7% WoW) fell to lows last seen in June 2024 as investors weighed the most recent Trump tariff pronouncements.

 

On macro, the BSP keeping the key policy rate at 5.75% at the February 13th Monetary Board meeting went against Street’s forecast for a 25-bp cut to 5.50%. Nonetheless, BSP Gov. Remolona maintained that the central bank remains in an easing cycle. He reiterated (i) his previous guidance for 50bps in rate cuts this year, now potentially as early as the April meeting, as well as (ii) BSP’s plans to slash the RRR by as much as 200bps to 5.0%, which can happen sooner than mid-2025.

 

Market breadth was negative for the week with twice as many decliners in the index as there were advancers. ADTO weakened to sub-$100m anew while net foreign outflow reached $43m for the week. Despite rate concerns, the peso strengthened by 0.3% to 57.838:$1 while the PH10Y yield was flat at 6.1313%.

Globe Telecom - On course despite 4Q disappointment
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income rises 13% in FY24 but 4Q down 34% QoQ

Globe reported FY24 core profit of P21.5bn (+13%), putting it below Regis estimates. 4Q core income (P3.9bn) was down -34% QoQ, -28% YoY. There was visible softness in 4Q mobile revenue but a clean-up at fintech Mynt was also a reason for the income drop. Despite the disappointment, we remain comfortable about underlying prospects for both its core and non-telco segments.

Mobile revenue weakens

4Q mobile service revenue dipped 1% QoQ – a surprise given the seasonality. What’s arguably even more surprising was that mobile data traffic fell (-5% QoQ) for the 2nd straight quarter, which has never happened before. Management blamed the unusual typhoon disruptions during the periods, and we are inclined to take their word for it.

Solid 4Q broadband net adds due to prepaid service

Wireline broadband subscribers rose +79k in the quarter, driven by +114k jump in fiber prepaid subscribers (which thus implies a drop in postpaid subs).

The home broadband business seems to have finally turned, with revenues up 2% QoQ despite the continued contraction of the fixed wireless segment.

Mynt clean-up leads to QoQ profit decline

Profit contributions from Mynt dropped `74% QoQ to ~P338m. Management gave assurances the drop was a result of a one-time clean-up of assets (not from its lending operations) and that the underlying operating trajectory was otherwise undimmed. Costs related to the clean-up were not excluded from core income.

Dividend maintained; FCF to turn positive on further capex decline

Globe declared a dividend of P25/share for this quarter, unchanged from last year. Management says it expects free cash flow (before dividends) to finally turn positive, aided by a ~20% cut in capex from the P56bn in FY24

AREIT - [Initiation] The Largest Philippine REIT
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Initiating with a Hold; target price P38.80/share

AREIT is the largest Philippine REIT in terms of leasable space, assets under management, and market capitalization. It has superior occupancy and a track record of consistent DPS growth, though its portfolio is still heavily concentrated (75%) in the office segment, where the outlook is lackluster, in our view. Furthermore, we forecast AREIT’s DPS CAGR to decelerate to 5% in 2024-2026E. Thus, we initiate coverage of AREIT with a Hold rating and a TP of P38.80/share.

Well-managed portfolio enhanced by DPS-accretive acquisitions

AREIT has been able to consistently grow (or at least maintain) its dividends per share (DPS) every quarter. We credit this largely to: 1) strong portfolio occupancy; and 2) DPS-accretive acquisitions. Its portfolio’s occupancy has been consistently high, with current occupancy at 99% and office occupancy at 95%, much higher than the Metro Manila average of 82%. Meanwhile, AREIT’s sponsor, Ayala Land (ALI PM), has been injecting assets at DPS-accretive valuations.

Still concentrated in the office segment; DPS CAGR to slow down

Although AREIT has a history of consistently growing its DPS, we expect growth to slow down over the next few years. We forecast DPS CAGR to decelerate to 5% in 2024-2026E, from 10% in 2021-2024. This is predominantly because of two reasons: 1) 75% of AREIT’s AUM are in office property, a segment whose current outlook is uninspiring; and 2) as AREIT grows larger, it will become increasingly difficult to inject assets that will be meaningfully DPS accretive.

Initiating with a Hold, TP of P38.80/sh; key risks

We are initiating coverage of AREIT with a Hold rating and a target price of P38.80/share. Our TP is derived using a discounted cash flow and implies 15.6X FY25E P/AFFO and dividend yield of 6.2% (at the target price), lower than the yields of other Philippine REITs (based on consensus forecast). At the current price, AREIT offers a 2025E dividend yield of 6.0%, lower than the 7.3%-8.3% yields of other Philippine REITs.

Key risks relate to office vacancy and interest rates.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PSE removes Jollibee foreign ownership limit

The PSE has approved the request of Jollibee to amend its foreign ownership limit from 40% to “no limit.” This is due to the amendments to Jollibee’s articles of incorporation, particularly the removal of the company’s ability to “own, acquire, mortgage, pledge, or encumber land and/or any interest therein.” This means that JFC can now be 100% foreign-owned. In practice, the entire public float of JFC (45.5%) can be held by foreign investors. While this will not affect the weight in the PSE Index, it will have implications for JFC’s weights in other indexes, such as MSCI. As of December 2024, foreigners held 23.9% of JFC’s shares outstanding.

GCash IPO public float requirement concerns

GCash Chairman Ernest Cu, fintech platform of Globe Telecom (GLO PM), called for the PSE and SEC to ease the minimum public float requirement of 20% for its potential IPO. Cu also commented that their focus remains on a local listing and is not currently looking at other venues to list outside the PH. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, who also oversees the SEC, commented that they are open to the request to lower public float but also states that it may not be needed. He cited that a feasible option would be for GCash to conduct a follow-on offering in order to reach 20% float. The GCash IPO is expected to take place in the 2H25 or 1H26.

Pangilinan group looking to increase stake in Maya

PLDT chairman and president Manuel V. Pangilinan said his group is in discussions with KKR to increase its stake in fintech Maya. “I’m meeting them (global investment company KKR) next week. I’ve gotten some rumors about values, but I don’t know. We’re participating in the purchase of the total of 80%. We’ll probably pick 30%,” he said. “If we agree on the price and the process, because I don’t know who the bidders are. We’re picking up some noises, some names. Hopefully, middle of the year.” It was previously reported that KKR is eyeing a valuation of US$2bn for 100% equity of Maya, implying a value of US$400m for KKR’s ~20% interest. PLDT is the single biggest largest shareholder of Maya, with a ~37% stake. The last widely announced funding round (Apr 2022) valued Maya at US$1.4bn and raised US$210m. Maya also raised more funding early last year (2024) but the valuations for that round were not publicly disclosed.

ICTSI's Manila port volumes up 5% YoY in FY24; 4Q24 volumes up 9%

In a PR, the Philippine Ports Authority revealed that container throughput at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) flagship port in Manila (MICT) grew by 5% YoY to 2.95mn TEUs in FY24, bang in line with Regis FY24E. Competitor, South Harbor of Asian Terminal (ATI PM), grew volumes by 6.9% YoY to 1.29mn TEUs in FY24. It implies MICT’s volumes rose by 9% YoY to 816k TEUs in 4Q24 alone, accelerating from the 6% pace in 3Q24 and 4% in 9M24. South Harbor’s volumes rose by 8% YoY to 345k TEUs in 4Q24 (vs. +7% YoY in 9M24).

Meralco eyes P215bn capex for 2026-2029

Based on its filing with the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), Meralco plans to invest P215bn for RY 2026-2029. P34bn will be spent in P2026, P60bn in 2027, P58bn in 2028, and P65bn in 2029. MER is also proposing an annual revenue requirement (ARR) of P393bn, of which P82bn will be generated in 2026 and will rise to P115bn by 2029. MER said that its proposed ARR is partly based on a proposed WACC of 14.6%. This would translate to a maximum average price per kwh of P1.687/kwh for 2026, P1.6899/kwh for 2027, P1.6894/kwh for 2028, and P1.6872/kwh for 2029. The above tariffs would be ~25% above the current tariff of P1.352/kwh. Meralco confirmed its application filed with the ERC but clarified that the application has not yet been formally docketed and remains subject to approval.

Bloomberry refinances P40bn of debt

Bloomberry refinanced P40bn worth of debt. This refinancing is separate from the P72bn of debt that was refinanced in 4Q24. The interest rate on the previous P40bn loan was around 8%. The new syndicated refinancing facility will have an interest rate of PHP BVAL + 1 percentage point. This should mean annual savings of approximately P700mn (equivalent to 8% of FY23 net profit). BLOOM will also have the option to fix the interest rate within the next 12 months. If the loans had not been refinanced, BLOOM would have been required to pay P40bn over the next five years (by 2029). The new syndicated refinancing facility has a term of 10 years (until February 2035) and the principal payments will be back-ended.

Ayala Land to expand further in Malaysia

Ayala Land Senior Vice President Robert Lao said that the company would expand further in Malaysia through its 66% owned subsidiary, Avaland (AVALAND MK). He said that the real estate business in Malaysia is more stable. Avaland caters to the middle-income segment and its landbank is predominantly in Klang Valley. In 9M24, Avaland comprised 4% of Ayala Land’s operating profit.

Court of Appeals orders Megaworld to add elevators to condominium building

In a decision on 27 January 2025, The Court of Appeals (CA) found Megaworld (MEG PM) guilty of unsound real estate business practice because it only provided two parking elevators in Paseo Parkview Suites. The original plan approved by the Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board had four parking elevators. The CA ordered Megaworld to add operational elevators. The CA decision reversed a previous ruling that absolved Megaworld of liability. Should this ruling become widespread in the media, then Megaworld’s reputation might be damaged, making it more difficult to sell residential units. Paseo Parkview was completed in 2002. MEG is trading at a 72% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 3.0X FY25E P/E.

Metro Pacific's mWell acquires Ayala Corp's KonsultaMD

Metro Pacific Investments Corp.’s (MPIC) digital healthcare arm, mWell, is acquiring KonsultaMD from the Ayala Group for an undisclosed amount. mWell’s acquisition of KonsultaMD will integrate with its existing portfolio of services that already serves 3.1mn users and has 90,000 users globally. The valuation for the transaction was not disclosed, and it is unclear if mWell is buying 100% of KonsultaMD. KonsultaMD is owned between AC Health, 917Ventures of Globe, and Salud Interactiva. KonsultaMD was launched in 2015 as a health hotline under Globe’s 917Ventures and has evolved into a telehealth ‘super app’ providing 24/7 doctor consultations, diagnostics, medicine delivery, and homecare services. The platform currently serves 2.7mn users and a broad network of partner doctors in mobile-based healthcare solutions. The integration of KonsultaMD into mWell’s digital health ecosystem will allow MPIC to expand its digital health offerings while leveraging its hospital infrastructure to improve patient access to medical services.

Phoenix Petroleum still unable to payout dividends to preferred shares

Phoenix Petroleum (PNX PM) stated that it has no unappropriated retained earnings and is focusing on settling debt obligations restructuring—thus, the company is still unable to pay out any cash dividends for any class of shares. This was in response to the PSE's query for an update on the company's payment of dividends on its preferred shares (these of which are cumulative). Recall that PNX responded last year to the same query for its inability to pay its dividends to PNX3 and PNX4 prefs.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP keeps rates steady

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) held interest rates unchanged, leaving the target reverse repurchase rate at 5.75%, which was against consensus expectations of a 25 bps cut. This marks the first pause since having cut rates by 25 bps at each of the last three meetings since Aug 2024. BSP Governor Remolona stated that uncertainties on the outlook of inflation and growth were the main rationale for keeping rates steady. "Normally, we would have cut further…" but uncertainty over global economic policies and their impact on the domestic economy needs to be assessed in more detail. Remolona emphasized that the central bank remains in the easing cycle and that it is a matter of when to cut in the remaining five meetings. He also added that the central bank will likely continue reducing rates by 25 bps at a time and may cut by up to 50 bps in total for the year. A rate cut is still "on the table" for the next Monetary Board meeting on April 3. Notably, the central bank slightly raised its risk-adjusted inflation forecast this year from 3.4% to 3.5% but kept its 2026 projection at 3.7%. Bloomberg median consensus currently expects 75 bps worth of rate cuts this year.

PH Dollar reserves down 3.0% at end-January

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Jan saw a 3.0% MoM drop to $103bn from $106.3bn at the end-Dec, primarily reflecting the BSP's net foreign exchange operations and drawdowns used in payments for foreign debt obligations. The level of dollar reserves by end-Jan was enough to cover about 3.6x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The reserve position in the IMF increased 0.6% MoM to $671mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $11.8bn, which jumped 6.8% MoM. Foreign investments declined by 3.7% MoM to $86.1bn. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025.

Commercial banks' loan growth picks up to 12.2% YoY in December

Preliminary data showed the outstanding commercial bank loans rose by 12.2% YoY in December, faster than November's 11.1% rate. Loans to productive activities rose 10.8% in Dec from 9.8% the previous month, due to faster loan growth in retail, motor vehicle repair, electricity/gas/steam supply industries, and construction. Consumer loans growth accelerated to 25.0% in Dec from 23.3% as growth momentum was driven by credit card, salary loans, and motor vehicle loans. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth was unchanged at 7.7% in Dec relative to Nov.

Banks' asset quality improves further in December

Using preliminary figures from the BSP, the NPL ratio of Philippine banks fell to 3.27% in December from 3.54% in November. In nominal terms, NPLs fell 3.9% MoM to P500.3bn. Total gross loans rose 4.1% MoM to P15.3tn; On a YoY basis, gross loans accelerated slightly to 10.6% YoY in December from 10.4% YoY in November. The NPL coverage ratio rose to 96.08% in December from 93.21% in November. Past due loans fell by 4.8% MoM to P604.9bn, bringing the past due ratio down to 3.95% in December from 4.32% in November. Restructured loans accounted for 2.03% of the industry’s loan portfolio, a tad higher than the 2% in November. Loan loss reserves inched up by 0.9% to P481bn in December from P485bn in November, bringing the loan reserve ratio to 3.14%.

Digital payments up 38% in December, up 35% for FY2024

Payments made over the National Retail Payments System – via InstaPay or PesoNet – totaled P1,799bn in December, up 38% YoY, slightly slower than the 41% pace in November. This took the full-year 2024 figure to P17,423bn, up 35% YoY, faster than the 29% recorded in 2023. Note that these datasets only capture payments made between platforms (e.g., BDO-to-BPI, GCash-to-MBT, Maya-to-GCash, etc.), and not those done within a platform, and thus severely under-estimates the size of total digital transfers. Growth was again driven by InstaPay – real-time, smaller-ticket payments – which continued to surge 47% YoY in December to P810bn, though this was a sharper slowdown from the 54% YoY clip in November. For FY24, InstaPay recorded a total of P7,347bn, up 46% YoY. The use case for InstaPay continues to broaden, with the volume of transactions jumping 65% YoY in December, and 68% for FY24. Meanwhile, payments over PesoNet – larger, batched transactions – came in at P989bn, up 31% YoY in December, a shade slower than the 32% clocked in November. This brought the 2024 total to P10,077bn, up 29% YoY, faster than the 22% growth in 2024.

Third Green Energy Auction (GEA-3) proceeds, contract awards by June 6

At the GEA-3 auction on Feb 11, the Department of Energy (DOE) received offers totaling 7,500 MW, exceeding its goal of 4,650 MW. It received offers of 6,700 MW for pumped-storage hydropower projects in Luzon (vs. target of 4,000 MW) and 250MW worth of pumped storage hydro in the Visayas region (in line with target). It attracted 550MW worth of bids for impounding hydro projects (vs. target of 300MW). Geothermal attracted bids for 30.9MW, well below the 100MW target. Overall, the auction received offers for 14 projects, with delivery periods between 2025-2035. There were 12 shortlisted bidders, including listed companies First Gen (FGEN PM) and San Miguel (SMC PM). However, DOE said only five of the shortlisted bidders participated in the auction. DOE is hoping to award contracts to the winning bidders starting June 6. The GEA program aims to promote investments in renewable energy sources. GEA allows RE gencos to sell energy to the WESM at their winning (guaranteed) bid prices and any difference between the bid price and the WESM price will be settled through the Feed-in-Tariff system, which is a full pass-through to end users.

Country adds 794MW of new RE capacity in 2024

The Department of Energy (DOE) announced that a total of 794MW of RE capacity was added in 2024, exceeding the total 760MW installed RE capacity in 2021-2023. However, this fell short of the initial 2,167MW RE target at the start of 2024. Based on the DOE’s latest list of committed power projects, a total of 4,957 MW of RE capacity is expected to come online in 2025.

Maharlika signs MOU with Thai-based CP Group for US$1bn multi-sectoral private equity fund

The Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) and Thailand-based Charoen Pokphand Group (CP Group) signed an MOU to establish a private equity fund with a target of up to US$1bn. The fund will invest in agriculture, food production, digital innovation, and renewable energy. Funding for an undisclosed initial capital is expected to close within nine to 12 months.

15 projects join PPP pipeline led by Davao airport

The government added 15 new projects to the Public-Private Partnership (PPP) pipeline, bringing the total to 176 projects worth P2.47tn. Four projects are national-level, while 11 are local. Six of the 15 projects are solicited. Key additions include the Francisco Bangoy International Airport Rehabilitation in Davao (P12.9 billion), the third busiest airport in the country.

VP Sara Duterte impeachment trial to commence after SONA

Senate President Francis Escudero confirmed in a press conference that the actual impeachment trial will be held after President Marcos' State of the Nation Address (SONA). Escudero stated that this year's SONA will likely be held on July 21, so the trial will begin the day after the 21st. Recall that the House is currently on break for the midterm elections where half of the 24-member Senate and all 316 members of the Lower House will be elected during election day on May 12.

Latest Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences sees 10 from the Marcos-admin slate

Based on the Pulse Asia survey conducted end-January, 14 out of 66 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of winning any of the 12 seats that are available for contention this midterm election, which will be held on the 12th of May. Erwin Tulfo secured solo first place with 62.8%, followed by Bong Go (50.4%), Tito Sotto (50.2%), and Ben Tulfo (46.2%) in the top ranks (2nd-8th place). Pia Cayetano (46.1%), Bong Revilla (46%), Imee Marcos (43.3%), and Ping Lacson (42.4%) ranked between 4th and 12th. Willie Revillame (41.9%), Bato Dela Rosa (41.2%), Abby Binay (41.1%), and Manny Pacquiao (40.6%) placed 7th to 14th. Camille Villar (38.4%) and Lito Lapid (37.7%) rounded out the list, ranking between 9th and 14th place. Notably, most of the projected top 14 contenders except four are not part of the Marcos-admin endorsed slate—reelectionist senators Go and De la Rosa are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo and Willie Revillame are independent candidates. This was Pulse Asia's second senatorial preferences survey after the filing of certificates of candidacy last October.

Marcos appoints Vince Dizon as Transportation Secretary

President Marcos Jr. has appointed Mr. Vivencio “Vince” Dizon as the Secretary of the Department of Transportation (DoTr), effective Feb 21. Prior to this appointment, Mr. Dizon was the Chief Regulatory Officer of Enrique Razon’s Prime Infrastructure. He previously served as Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) President and CEO and Presidential Adviser for COVID-19 Response under the Duterte administration. Mr. Dizon will replace DoTr Secretary Jaime Bautista, who resigned due to health reasons.
