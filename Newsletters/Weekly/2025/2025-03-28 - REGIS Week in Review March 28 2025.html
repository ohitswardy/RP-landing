REGIS REPORT

28 March 2025

Philippine equities pulled back for another week, taking the PCOMP lower by 1.9% or 119.31 pts WoW to 6,147.44. The local index was down the most in Asean after relatively outperforming in the recent weeks, as global trade uncertainties weighed down sentiment and kept investors away from the market. Foreign flows reverted to net sell, snapping three straight weeks of net inflows, while ADTO fell to a measly $77m.

 

Connection issues at the exchange caused a delay in Monday trading, with the market only opening in the last hour of the morning session. The PCOMP dropped 1.2% to below 6,200 on the day and has since failed to reclaim the level. Still, for much of the week, the PCOMP hovered close to its 50-D MA and managed to hold at around the 6,150 support level.

 

Index breadth was negative for the week with only 20% of the PCOMP ending in the green. BLOOM (-11.0% WoW) was yet again the worst performer, breaking below P3.00 to close at its lowest level since 2009 as progress in Thailand's casino legalization plans hounded the outlook for local integrated resorts. SCC (-6.9% WoW) also among the biggest decliners due to disappointment from shrinking dividends.

 

Consumer names rounded up the PCOMP's bottom five this week, with (i) CNPF (-8.1% WoW) pulling back amid rising skipjack tuna prices; (ii) URC (-7.2% WoW) succumbing to profit-taking after its recent rally; and (iii) MONDE (-6.8% WoW) falling after 4Q results, though in-line with forecasts, showed larger impairment charges on Quorn amounting to P9.4bn nearly wiped out its net earnings for the year.

 

Also worth noting the slump in conglos, with the sector index down the most, by 2.7% WoW, and the declines in AC (-4.3% WoW) and SM (-2.4% WoW) accounting for a quarter of the PCOMP's weekly drop.

 

The last few 4Q reports continue to trickle in. GTCAP (-1.6% WoW) saw earnings miss Street forecasts dragged by lower contributions from infra unit Metro Pacific as well as its property business, which offset decent results from banks and auto. CEB (+0.6% WoW) also missed estimates due to the surprise acceleration of investments in 2H24, which masked strong operational results that further improved into 4Q.

 

On the macro front, the BSP is now projecting a weaker balance of payments (BOP) position for the Philippines in 2025-26, hinged on slower global trade and hampered investor confidence given global macro uncertainties. On inflation drivers, the Department of Agriculture announced it is further reducing the maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice to P45/kg effective next week, March 31st, from P58/kg currently.

 

Foreign outflow for the week hit $34m following three straight weeks of net foreign buying. ADTO slumped to $77m from $136m the previous week.

 

Currency and rates little changed for the week -- peso -0.1% WoW to 57.382 and PH10Y yield -2.3bps WoW to 6.219%.

GT Capital - 4Q24: Auto gross margins up
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY24 core profit at P27.8bn (+11%) – Above Regis FY24E but below Street

Headline FY24 profit after pref divs slipped by 2% YoY to P28.4bn. After adjusting for lot sales and other one-offs, core profit rose 11% to P27.8bn, below Street forecasts but above ours. Derived 4Q24 recurring profit of P6.6bn was higher by 22% YoY due to better margins at the auto segment and a tax reversal at Metrobank, despite lower contributions from its infrastructure and property units.

 

Auto GP margins up sequentially but profits lower on higher promotions

Toyota Motor Phils (TMP) grew profit by 15% to P15.9bn in FY24, implying 4Q24 profits alone rose 28% YoY to P3.79bn. Sequentially, profits in 4Q were actually down 18% due to higher promotional activities for the launch of the new Tamaraw. Despite the depreciation of the PHP against the USD, GPM expanded to 15% in 4Q24 from 14.5% in 3Q24, buoyed by higher-margin Japan-sourced CBUs. After growing sales by 9% to 218k units in FY24, TMP is targeting to grow sales by 8% to 235k units this year. So far, 2M25 sales are up by 11% YoY. 

Metrobank: Loan growth accelerates to 17% in 4Q24

Metrobank grew its loan book by 17% in 4Q24, accelerating from the 16% pace in 3Q24. However, higher opex drove pre-provisioning operating profit lower by 11% YoY and 27% QoQ. Net profit rose 19% YoY (+2% QoQ), flattered by a tax reversal.

 

4Q profits drop at Metro Pacific; Lower reservation sales at property unit

Contributions from associate and infrastructure arm, MPI, fell 63% QoQ and 9% YoY to P553mn in 4Q24, dragged lower by large provisions at the tollroad unit and higher non-cash and financing charges at the rail unit.  The property unit also did not do too well, with reservation sales down 10% YoY (+7% QoQ) to P4.6bn, taking FY24 sales to P16.6bn, lower by 28% YoY. Management noted that  it is currently sitting on three years’ worth of inventory and does not plan to launch until inventory levels normalize (~2 years).

 
Monde Nissin - FY24: A tale of two opposites
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Solid 4Q marred by more impairment charges

Monde reported that its FY24 core net income rose 29% to P9.8bn on the back of a P83.1bn of revenue (+3.7%), in line with Regis estimates and also the Street’s. EBITDA for the year rose 18% to P16.0bn as margins expanded due to better input costs. Headline profit, however, was only P450m as the company took more impairment charges related to its Meat Alternative business.

 

Improving 4Q top line for APAC BFB

Revenue growth for APAC BFB in 4Q was +9% YoY, an acceleration from the 4% in 9M24, with core EBITDA up +7%. The top line growth was driven principally by volumes lifted not only by the broad macro environment but also market share gains. Noodle volumes rose 7% in 4Q while biscuit volumes were up 8%.

 

Despite EBITDA improvement, daunting outlook for Quorn

The Meat Alternative business (“Quorn”) managed to break even at the EBITDA level for the year, although its bottom line was still in the red (P804m loss). Revenues contracted by 5% for the year and 11% for the last quarter, suggesting the business is still facing significant intrinsic challenges despite improved cash flow. Management said it is contemplating injecting funds into Quorn to help the latter in paying down debt and reduce its financing costs.

Twin impairment charges

A reexamination of the business resulted in another impairment charge of P6.8bn (GBP86m). Related to this, Monde also took a P2.6bn impairment charge on the value of the guarantee provided by some of its principals on the valuation of the Meat Alternative operation. The latter was apparently due to the decline in the value of the underlying collateral for the guarantee (Monde shares).

 

At current market price, the value of the collateral (2.16bn Monde shares) is already below the reduction in the value of Quorn over 2023-24 (GBP267m). We also note that Quorn’s latest valuation (which prompted the impairment charge) is premised on medium-term revenue CAGR of 1.8%, which might be a challenge to achieve.

 

Guidance for APAC BFB and Quorn

Management provided guidance of “mid single-digit growth” for the top line of the APAC BFB business, with margins broadly in line with FY24. We view this as being realistic, if just slightly conservative. For Quorn, management is guiding for a smaller decline in its top line and positive core EBITDA for the year. Reversing the fall in revenue is crucial. Being able to do that would not only demonstrate the viability of the business, it would also avoid another chunky impairment on its remaining GBP115m carrying value.

 
Cebu Air - Strong operating results despite 4Q profit drop
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY24 core profit falls 50% to P3.25bn, below Regis and consensus estimates

Headline FY24 profit of P5.4bn (-32%) included P2.2bn worth of net gains from leaseback transactions and FX adj. Removing these, core profit stood at P3.25bn, down 50% YoY. The results disappointed due to the surprise acceleration of investments in 2H24. Despite the miss, we are encouraged by the strong operational performance in 4Q24, as revenues jumped 32% QoQ and 28% YoY while EBIT expanded 17x QoQ and 43% YoY. However, profits fell 20% YoY to P1.79bn due to the large tax benefit booked in the previous year.

 

Pax volumes up 31% YoY but new routes trim fares by 6% YoY

CEB flew 7mn passengers in 4Q24, higher by 17% QoQ and 32% YoY, as the company expanded into new routes and hubs. CEB grew its market share in both domestic and international markets, making it the leading carrier in both markets for the first time in its history. However, the route expansion came at the expense of fares, which fell 6% YoY.

EBITDA margin slips 2ppts YoY to 25.9%

Despite the 28% YoY revenue improvement in 4Q24, EBITDA rose by just 19% YoY to P7.9bn, as cash costs surged largely due to higher airport fees, unfavorable FX, and higher fleet-related costs. CEB added net 13 aircraft in FY24 of which seven (7) were added in 4Q24 alone. As a result, EBITDA margin slipped by 2ppts YoY to 25.9% in 4Q.  

 

Outlook: Strong topline momentum continues with cost tailwinds

CEB is off to a blistering start in 2025, recording a 30% YoY increase in pax volumes with seat load factors expanding 150bps YoY to 86.7%. More notably, seat load factors expanded sequentially, suggesting that the market has fully absorbed the incremental capacity introduced in Sep 2024, boding well for fares. The recent drop in jet kero prices, as well as a favorable PHP:USD exchange rate should also boost margins near term. Capex peaked at P65bn in 2024 and should fall to P30-35bn annually. While CEB still needs surpass a regulatory hurdle before it can declare dividends, current fundamentals support this goal.

SEC releases statement on minimum public float requirement for IPOs

In a press release, the Securities and Exchange Commission reiterates its firm stance on the 20% minimum public float requirement for companies seeking to list. The SEC highlighted the float requirement's importance in enhancing market liquidity, improving price discovery, reducing the likelihood of price manipulation, and reducing ownership concentration. It was also stated that after discussions with the PSE, "the SEC has allowed, by way of exemptive relief, an initial public float of 15%, subject to strict criteria." This clarifies SEC Commissioner McJill Bryant Fernandez and PSE President Ramon Monzon's previous comments last week on allowing 15% public float over the 20% requirement for large IPOs. Companies may apply for this relief to IPO at 15% public float but are required to reach the 20% standard "within less than 24 months from the listing date and only as deemed necessary by the Commission." The exemptive relief covers listing applications that have already been filed by the SEC and PSE. As of March 25, the Commission and the Exchange have not received any application for regulatory relief from any potential IPO applicant.

BSP adjusts BOP forecasts amidst heightened uncertainties

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in a press release, projected a weaker balance of payments (BOP) position for the country in 2025-2026. This is hinged on slower global trade and hampered investor confidence given higher uncertainty in global macroeconomic conditions (i.e., trade policy, geopolitical issues). However, external risks could be offset by the domestic economy's sustained growth, easing inflation, and less restrictive monetary policy. BSP projections for the overall BOP position and current account (both as % of GDP) for 2025 were revised from 0.4% and -2.4% to -0.8% and -3.9%, respectively. While the GIR is set to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025. In summary for the rest of the BSP projections: Merchandise exports, after consecutive declines in 2023 and 2024, are set to recover with modest growth of 1-2%, with semiconductor exports to remain unchanged given global inventory shifts. Services exports are expected to see 8% growth but slower than initially expected, given local talent shortages and US reshoring efforts. Tourism activity is expected to return to pre-pandemic levels, driven by visitors from Korea and Japan. Overseas Filipino remittances are projected to be below the long-term trend as countries that are the top sources of remittances (Saudi Arabia, Qatar, US) have been advocating for localization of their workforces. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) is expected to be flat, as continued elevated rates hamper capital flows to emerging economies.

MSRP of imported rice to be cut to P45/kg

The Department of Agriculture (DA) stated that it will reduce the maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice to P45/kg effective March 31. This is due to the continued decline in global rice prices, which have been on a downtrend since India lifted its year-long export ban of non-basmati white rice. For context, Vietnam is one of the primary overseas rice suppliers for the country, and prices for rice with 5% broken-grain since December have gone down $200 to $490/MT. Recall that the DA first implemented an MSRP on imported rice of P58/kg back in January 20 and staggered the reductions to the upcoming P45/kg MSRP. An MSRP was implemented due to stubbornly high retail prices as much as P64/kg "despite rice prices softening, tariff reductions, and a stronger peso." According to the DA, well-milled rice prices as of March 25 for local and imported commercial rice are at P40-54/kg and P43-46/kg, respectively, versus P40-55/kg and P40-52/kg back in January 25.

BIR confident on hitting 2025 collection target of P3.23tn

The Bureau of Internal Revenue is confident in meeting its collection goal of P3.23tn. 2025's target of P3.23tn represents a 13% YoY increase, which is the same as the 13% YoY growth seen from the P2.85tn in collections for 2024. The breakdown of this year's collections is that 53% or P1.71tn is set to come from net income taxes, followed by value-added tax (VAT) of P710bn, excise taxes (P343bn), other taxes (P298bn), and percentage taxes (P178bn). BIR Assistant Commissioner Jethro Sabariaga stated that a downside risk would be the lower collections seen from the CREATE MORE law (Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy). As for registered business enterprises (RBEs) specifically, the new law lowers the corporate income tax to 20% from 25%, and it also expands their VAT incentives coverage. Recall that the CREATE MORE Act IRR was signed last February. However, the BIR also sees a boost from next measures which could generate P22bn more in revenues from VAT on digital services, windfall tax and royalty on mining, and single-use tax plastics.

Digital payments up 34% in Feb; transaction volumes surge

The value of payments over InstaPay (real-time, small-tickets) and PesoNet (larger, batched payments) grew 33.7% YoY in February to P1,646bn. The figure is down 8.7% MoM, though that’s rather typical following the holiday season surge. The headline growth is also slower than the 38.2% growth in January, and slower than the 40.6% posted in February 2024. That said, it is important to note that 2024 was a leap year (adding 3.3ppts), which likely skewed the growth figures. For 2M25, digital payments were up 36.0% YoY to P3,449bn, still slightly faster than the 35.4% in FY2024 despite the lost day.

PAGCOR to lower fees on bingo operators and e-games from integrated resorts

PAGCOR lowered the fees it charges on e-casino games offered by integrated resorts. For integrated resorts that have spent/committed at least US$200mn, they will only be charged 22% of e-casino GGR by PAGCOR (exclusive of 5% franchise tax and audit fees). This is lower than the 25% currently (exclusive of 5% franchise tax and audit fees), and lower than the 30% charged on PIGO firms (exclusive of 5% franchise tax audit fees), such as DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM). Despite the 170% growth in FY24 GGR of e-games (to P155bn), Okada Manila’s e-games GGR fell 31% to P292mn. E-casino operations currently comprise only about 5% of Bloomberry’s GGR. Bloomberry is targeting to launch an enhanced online platform in 2H25. PAGCOR will also lower the fees it charges on Traditional Bingo, eBingo, and sports-betting beginning July 2025. About 80% of DigiPlus Interactive’s revenue is from e-casino games, which will still be charged 30% of GGR. The remaining 20% of revenue might be charged 2.5ppt to 10ppt less by PAGCOR. This could raise PLUS’s net profit by 5%-10%, holding all other things constant.

SWS survey for senatorial preferences see 9 from the Marcos-admin slate

Based on the Social Weather Stations (SWS) conducted in March 15-20, 13 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 available seats in the upcoming midterm elections on May 12, 2025. This is the first survey conducted and published after the March 11 arrest of Former President Rodrigo Duterte and turnover to the International Criminal Court (ICC) in Hague, Netherlands. Bong Go (42%) and Erwin Tulfo (42%) secured the top spots, ranking 1st-2nd. Ben Tulfo (34%), Tito Sotto (34%), and Lito Lapid (33%) followed closely, ranking 3rd-4th. Bong Revilla (32%) and Pia Cayetano (31%) placed 6th-7th. Ping Lacson (31%), Bato Dela Rosa (30%), and Willie Revillame (28%) ranked between 7th-10th. Abby Binay (27%), Manny Pacquiao (27%), and Camille Villar (27%) secured places 11th-13th. Only four of the probable 13 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go and Dela Rosa are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo and Revillame are independent. Notably, comparing the March survey vs. the one conducted in February (before Duterte’s arrest), the composition of the top 13 remains unchanged but exhibited some changes in rankings such as Bong Go moving to the top spot and Camille Villar moving to 11-13th from 13th previously.
