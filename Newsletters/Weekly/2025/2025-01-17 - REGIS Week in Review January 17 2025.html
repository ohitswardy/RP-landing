REGIS REPORT

17 January 2025

The PCOMP extended losses to another week, underperforming most of the region with a 2.2% or 144.20-pt drop WoW to 6,352.12 as the higher-for-longer narrative took hold anew. The blowout US jobs report out late Friday the previous week dragged local shares when the market reopened, after traders slashed Fed rate cut forecasts to now just 30-40bps for 2025. The PH10Y yield continued to inch up (+18bps WoW to 6.33%) while risk-off mood persisted in equities. This took the PCOMP down past the 6,300-level to its lowest close in 29 weeks, before bargain hunters prevailed at the week's close to help trim the Wow decline.

Index breadth was negative with 20 out of the 30 PCOMP names closing in the red. Food manufacturers URC (-12.9% WoW) and MONDE (-13.4% Wow) were the worst performers this week. URC fell as predictions for its deletion from the MSCI Standard Index in the February rebalancing prompted the sell-down, which ended up also dragging closest peer comparable MONDE. 

Some of the week's outperformers included names that gave good previews for 4Q24/2025 earnings -- SCC (+2.73% WoW) said it achieved record high coal shipments in 2024, while BPI (+0.50% WoW) management said it expects stronger revenue performance in 2025 and targets double-digit growth for the year. And despite tempered monetary easing expectations, SMPH (+2.07% WoW) managed to rebound from the previous week's slump. SMPH management also commented this week that the company will likely no longer pursue a mall REIT IPO as cash flows remain strong.

ADTO improved to $93m from $73m last week with chunky MOC baskets going through this week. Foreigners remained net sellers by $57m, which took YTD net outflow to $81m.

The peso continued to weaken to 58.615, down 0.4% for the week and now losing 1.3% YTD.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Prime might not pursue mall REIT

Hans Sy, Director of SM Prime, said that the company is unlikely to list a mall REIT this year. Furthermore, due to the company’s strong cash flows, it might never pursue a mall REIT IPO.

BPI sees robust growth this year

Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI PM) is expecting stronger revenue performance in 2025 as it targets double-digit growth mainly driven by robust economic prospects and strategic investments. BPI president and CEO Jose Teodoro Limcaoco stated the bank targets higher growth vs. last year and that the company remains optimistic about the country's economy, highlighting growth in key sectors such as logistics, domestic tourism, trade and imports, and consumer lending. He also noted to look out for the impact of US trade policies and Fed decisions which could impact currency stability and inflation. The company will continue its commitment to digitization as it allocates approximately 10% of revenues to technology investments. Recall BPI's net income for the 9M24 increased 24% YoY to a record P48bn, while revenues rose 25% YoY to P125.8bn for the same period.

ICTSI's Mexico 4Q24 port volume growth accelerates to 16% YoY

Data from Mexico’s port regulator showed that volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), grew by 4% YoY to 117k TEUs in December. On a MoM basis, CMSA’s volumes dropped 11%, signaling the start of the de-stocking phase. CMSA lost a bit of market share in December, the first time it ceded share since it completed its expansion in October. Industry-wide volumes grew by 6% YoY in December (ex-CMSA, volumes at other competing ports in Manzanillo rose 7% YoY). It is worth noting that import volumes fell across all ports in Manzanillo, which again suggests de-stocking. This brings 4Q24 volumes at CMSA up by 16% YoY, accelerating from the 2% pace in 3Q24 and 13% in 9M24. For FY24, CMSA’s volumes grew by 14% YoY to 1.45mn TEUs, broadly in line with Regis FY24E of 1.47mn TEUs. For FY25E, we’ve built in a 15% YoY increase in CMSA’s volumes. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of FY25E consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 26% of EBITDA. ICT trades at 7.9x FY25E EV/EBITDA, lower than the peer average of 11x and historical average of 11x.

Thailand's Cabinet approves draft casino bill

Thailand’s Cabinet approved the draft “Entertainment Complex Business Act.” The draft will proceed to the House of Representatives. The draft bill would allow casinos to operate within a complex that includes a hotel, convention center, mall, or theme park. People below 20 years old will not be allowed to enter the casinos. Foreigners can enter for free, but Thai citizens must pay THB5,000 (US$148) to enter. Should there be Integrated Resorts in Thailand in the future, they could compete with Philippine casinos for foreign players, including VIPs. Foreigners comprise approximately 55% of the GGR of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM). VIPs comprise approximately 25% of BLOOM’s GGR.

Semirara 2024 sales volumes reach 16.5mn MT

In a press release, Semirara said it achieved record high coal shipments of 16.5mn MT in 2024, up 4% YoY and surpassing our forecast of 16mn MT. Exports rose by 3% YoY to 8.4mn MT, with sales to China rising by 46% YoY to 7.6mn MT. Domestic sales volumes grew by 4% to 8mn MT, driven by higher sales to its own power plants and cement plants. The company hit its maximum annual production limit of 16mn MT for the third consecutive year. SCC’s coal mining unit accounts for around 70% of profits. For 2025, we expect Semirara to generate profits of P18bn (vs. FY24E: P20bn), underpinned by coal mining sales volumes of 15.5mn MT. SCC trades at 4.3x FY25E PER. We forecast the company could pay dividends of P4.5/sh this year (~13% yield), lower than last year’s P6/sh.

Vena Energy, MGreen finalize funding for solar project

Vena Energy and MGen Renewable Energy, Inc. (MGreen) have reached financial close on the 550MW Bugallon Solar Power Project in Pangasinan. The project is slated for completion by 4Q25. The project is financed partly through an P18.3bn green loan facility with Security Bank Corp., Rizal Commercial Banking Corp., and Philippine National Bank serving as lenders.

Cebu Air posts 32% YoY growth in 4Q24 pax volumes, SLF stable

CEB reported that it carried 2.6mn pax in December 2024, a 31% YoY increase, resulting in a mild improvement in seat load factor (SLF) to 85.2% from 84.8%. This brings 4Q24 pax volumes higher by 32% YoY to 7.0mn, accelerating sharply from the 13% growth rate in 9M24, with SLF broadly unchanged QoQ, YoY at 83.1%. For FY24, pax volumes grew by 18% YoY to 24.5mn, within Regis’ FY24E of 24.6mn. Seat load factor slightly improved to 84.4% from 84% in FY23. Domestic pax volumes rose by 16% to 18.5mn while international pax volumes grew 25% to 6.0mn. To the downside, the airline continued to invest heavily in 4Q, ending 2024 with a fleet of 98, up from 91 in end-Sep 2024 and 85 in end-Dec 2023 (vs. 74 pre-Covid). This suggests costs may have remained elevated in 4Q. Management is guiding a net fleet addition of one aircraft in 2025 and zero in 2026/2027, given the end of the company’s investment phase.

Maynilad prepares for US$1bn IPO

Maynilad Water aims to launch a US$1bn IPO within the year or latest by 2026 to meet the regulatory requirement of listing by January 2027. Maynilad plans to file for an IPO in Q1 2025 and target launch dates in April or July, according to Maynilad Chairman Manuel V. Pangilinan. Under its franchise, Maynilad is required to list at least 30% of its outstanding capital stock within five years from the grant of its franchise in January 2022. Maynilad plans to fund its 2025 capex of P40bn through additional debt and internal funds. Maynilad is a 25%-owned affiliate of DMCI (DMC PM) and accounts for 23% of its NAV; DMC trades at a 19% discount to its market-based NAV of P13.41/sh. Other shareholders of Maynilad include Metro Pacific (53%) and Marubeni (20%).

D&L sees faster growth in net earnings this year

D&L Industries Inc. (DNL PM) is positive on its outlook for this year as the company expects faster earnings growth vs. last year. The company president, Alvin Lao, stated that the company's profitability in the last two years has been hampered by tight consumer wallets. But this year may be better due to easing inflation and interest rates, alongside higher spending with the election season. He expects a strong 4Q24 which will help the company surpass its 2023 net profit of P2.3bn. Lao highlighted strong demand in its biodiesel business as it has been operating at a higher capacity from 40% to above 60%. This is as the DOE (Department of Energy) has mandated downstream oil industry participants to implement a higher percentage of coco methyl ester (CME) blend in all diesel fuel sold nationwide. The mandated increase will be done gradually from 2% to 3% in October 2024, to 4% by October 2025, and to 5% by October 2026. The Biofuels Act of 2006 mandates that all liquid fuels for motors and engines sold in the Philippines be blended with biofuels. The current ethanol blend for gasoline has remained at 10% since 2012, while the CME blending last increased to 2% in 2007.

Indonesia-based Pertamina NRE takes 20% stake in CREC

Pertamina New & Renewable Energy (NRE) is subscribing to 2.23mn unissued common shares of CREC for P3/sh. Total buy-in valuation for a 20% stake in CREC will be P6.7bn (US$115mn). Pertamina NRE is a subsidiary of Indonesia’s state-owned enterprise PT Pertamina Power Indonesia. Proceeds will be used to fund CREC’s renewable energy project pipeline. Upon deal completion, CREC’s total shares outstanding will rise by 25% to 11.16bn from 8.93bn. Parent Citicore Power’s stake in CREC will be diluted to 63% from 79%, while its public float will be reduced to 17% from 21%. “The partnership with Pertamina NRE presents limitless opportunities for Indonesia and the Philippines to collaborate on innovative technologies and practices in renewable energy,” according to CREC President and CEO Oliver Tan. CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 285MW. The company is constructing 1,583 MW of solar projects over the next 12 months. CREC has a project pipeline of 6,716MW (gross) of solar and 813MW (gross) of onshore wind, and 26MW of hydro plants, which will take overall gross installed capacity to 7,570MW by 2028.

PremiumLands to conduct tender offer for Asiabest

PremiumLands will conduct a tender offer for the 33% of Asiabest Group (ABG PM) that it does not currently own. PremiumLands is owned by Francis Lloyd Chua of Leyte. In 4Q24, PremiumLands purchased a 67% stake in ABG for P510mn (P2.55/share). The tender offer price will not be below P2.55/share. The last traded price was P26.20/share.

Remittances rise 3.3% YoY in November

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.3% YoY to US$2.81bn but dropped 8.8% MoM—marking the lowest monthly inflow in the past six months. The acceleration in land-based remittances from Saudi Arabia, which was up 26.2% YoY and 2.0% MoM, was offset by the slower remittances growth from the USA, only up 0.6% YoY and down 18.6% MoM. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 1.0% from 0.6% in October. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 8.5%, marking the second-fastest increase of the year and surpassing the 3.6% growth recorded in October. Bringing YT-November remittances up 3.0% YoY to US$31.1bn, broadly in line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year.

Tourism arrivals up 9% YoY to 5.95mn but fell short of arrival target in 2024

Data from the Department of Tourism (DoT) exhibited that tourist arrivals for 2024 have increased by 9% YoY to 5.95mn from 5.5mn in 2023. However, the 2024 figure fell short of its arrival target of 7.7mn for the year. Although tourist arrivals from the top sources of tourism for the country all improved from 2023—relative to 2019 levels, the full-year figure was about 72% of 2019 levels. Tourism revenues have surpassed pre-pandemic levels at 126% of 2019 at approximately P756bn, while arrivals were behind at 72%. The DoT has not yet set its tourist arrival targets for 2025.

Commercial banks' loan growth picks up to 11.1% YoY in November

Preliminary data showed the outstanding commercial bank loans rose by 11.1% YoY in November, a tad faster than October's 10.6% rate. Loans to productive activities rose 9.8% in Nov from 9.1% the previous month due to faster loan growth in retail, motor vehicle repair, and electricity/gas/steam supply industries. Consumer loans growth decelerated slightly to 23.3% in Nov from 23.6% as growth momentum continued by credit card and vehicle loans. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth remained steady at 7.7% in November.

Banking sector NPL ratio falls in November

The NPL ratio of the banking sector fell to 3.54% in November, from 3.6% in October. It stood at 3.41% in November 2023. Nonperforming loans fell 0.7% MoM to P520.55bn in November (from 524.31bn in October), and NPL rose 14.6% YoY (from P454.28bn). The total loan portfolio rose 1.2% MoM to P14.72trn (from P14.55trn in October), growing 10.4% YoY (from P13.34trn in November 2023). Past due loans declined 0.8% YoY to P635.6bn in November (from P640.9bn in October), though it increased 12.8% YoY (from P563.4bn in November 2023). As a result, the past due ratio fell to 4.32% in November, from 4.4% in October. It stood at 4.22% in November 2023. Restructured loans rose 0.3% MoM to P293.7bn, but this represents a 4% decline YoY. NPL coverage rose to 93.2% in November, from 92.3% in October. However, this is lower than the 101.5% figure in November 2023.

Vehicle sales rise 7% YoY in December; FY24 sales up 8.7%

Industry car sales grew 7.4% YoY, 2.8% MoM to 42,044 units in December, according to the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Assoc. (TMA). This brings FY24 vehicle sales to 467,252 units, up 8.7% YoY and broadly in line with CAMPI’s target of 468,300 units. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 20,263 units in December, up by 3.6% YoY and 4.8% MoM. This brings FY24 retail sales higher by 9% YoY to 218,019 units, above management’s target of 215k units (+7%) and Regis’ forecast of 204k units (+3%). TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses. GTCAP is trading at a 54% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,243/sh and 5x FY25E PE.

Department of Agriculture plans to declare food security emergency

The National Price Coordinating Council (NPCC) has approved a resolution urging the Department of Agriculture (DA) to declare a “food security emergency for rice” as prices remain high. The declaration would allow the National Food Authority (NFA) to release buffer stock rice to stabilize local prices. The NFA would replace the released stocks with locally-produced rice to support Filipino farmers. Agriculture Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. welcomed the resolution, as it will complement the DA’s ongoing efforts. The DA will implement a maximum suggested retail price (SRP) of P58 per kilo for imported rice, effective January 20. Rice prices remain elevated despite reduced import tariffs. As of January 15, imported rice prices in Metro Manila ranged from P40 to P65 per kilogram, depending on the type.

Department of Finance pushing for key tax reform measures this year

The Department of Finance is hoping several tax reform measures will be approved by Congress this year despite the upcoming midterm elections. The 19th Congress resumes session on Jan 14 but will adjourn on Feb 8 for the start of the campaign period; elections are scheduled for May 12. Lawmakers will resume session on June 2 and will adjourn sine die on June 13. The 20th Congress will open on July 28. Priority measures include: Government Revenues Optimization through Wealth Tax Harmonization (GROWTH) bill, which is the new Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act (PIFITA); excise tax on single-use plastics (SUPs); and amendments to the Tax Incentives Management and Transparency Act (TIMTA).

President Marcos signs natural gas development bill into law

President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. has signed into law Republic Act 12120 or Philippine Natural Gas Industry Development Act on Jan. 8. The law seeks to establish the Philippine Downstream Natural Gas Industry (PDNGI) and increase the share of natural gas in the country’s energy mix. Salient provisions include: 12% VAT exemption on the sale and purchase of indigenous natural gas, aggregated gas, and electricity from gas-fired power generation facilities; Certified PDNGI facilities will be entitled to fiscal incentives under CREATE (e.g., income tax holiday); and power produced from indigenous natural gas shall have priority over other conventional energy sources. The law will become effective once the Department of Energy issues its implementing rules and regulations (IRR) within six months.

Energy Secretary sees 2025 peak demand rising by 5% vs. capacity increase of +30% for Luzon

Secretary of Energy Raphael Lotilla sees power demand rising by 5.4% to 14,769 megawatts (MW) in Luzon, 16% to 3,111 MW in the Visayas, and 8.2% to 2,789 MW in Mindanao in 2025. He believes that the country will be in a much better situation this year than last year, given new projects that have come online. A total of 5,743 MW (+30%) of new power plant projects are expected to come online in 2025 in the Luzon grid, of which 3,923 MW are renewable energy projects, 1,320 MW natural gas, and 500 MW of coal. Given that projects are typically delayed, most of these projects could be pushed back to 2026. Meanwhile, the privatization of the 796.64 MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydroelectric power plant complex could push through in April. Last year, Finance chief Ralph Recto said the state-run organization expects to generate P50bn to P100bn. Privatization was deferred due to the delay in the Energy Regulatory Commission’s issuance of pricing scheme for the third green energy auction.

House freezes work on bills seeking legislated wage hikes

Wage hike bills seeking to impose a legislated national wage hike have become secondary at the House of Representatives, according to Rizal Rep. Juan Fidel Felipe F. Nograles, the House Labor Committee Chairman. Recall, bills seeking to increase wages of private sector workers by between P150 and P750 were filed at the Lower House last year. The Senate approved a bill increasing the daily minimum wage in the private sector by P100. The wage hike bill will be revisited with the 20th Congress, which will open on July 28, to provide the opportunity to review existing wage orders before holding the wage hike hearing.

Bill reorganizing NEDA into a department gets final House nod

The Lower House unanimously voted to approve on third reading House Bill 11199 or the proposed Economy, Planning and Development Act. The bill seeks to reorganize and institutionalize NEDA into the Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDEV), expanding its functions and capacity. The measure’s counterpart bill in the Senate was approved on third and final reading last month.

SWS survey shows drop in Marcos, Duterte satisfaction ratings in December

President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating stood at +19 for December 2024, or 13ppts lower than his net satisfaction rating of +32 in September 2024. By area, Marcos’ rating dropped the most in Mindanao: -34 points to -18 from +16. In Metro Manila, it dropped by 16 points to +20 from +36. In Visayas, his rating dropped by 13 points to +18 from +31. In the rest of Luzon, his rating was hardly changed to +38 from +39. VP Sara Duterte’s net satisfaction rating dipped to +21, or 6 points lower than in September 2024. By area, Duterte’s rating dipped in all areas in the country, except Visayas. In Metro Manila, it dipped 18pts to -3 in December from +15 in September. In Visayas, her rating rose by 9pts to +32 in December from +23 in September. In Mindanao, it decreased by 4pts to +67 in December from +71 in September. In the rest of Luzon, it dropped 10pts to +1 in December from +11 in September. The survey was conducted from December 12 to 18, involving face-to-face interviews with 2,160 respondents. It has a ±2% margin of error.
