REGIS REPORT

21 November 2025

Philippine equities staged a strong rebound this week to just shy of 6,000, snapping a four-week slump that took the index to its weakest close since May 2020 to below the 5,600 handle. This was the strongest weekly rally since May 2021, with the PCOMP surging by 7.4% or 412.78 pts WoW to 5,997.13, powered by aggressive local bottom-fishing after valuations hit all-time lows. The PCOMP's forward PER had fallen to 8.2x -- cheaper than during any crisis period in the past 30 years -- which helped draw back domestic investors. Index breadth was overwhelmingly positive, with 29 out of 30 names finishing higher as the market embraced a broad risk-on tone.

 

Heavyweight banks and property names led the charge with double-digit gains -- SMPH (+18.2% WoW), BPI (+14.1% WoW), BDO (+10.5%), and ALI (+11.7% WoW) -- providing most of the index’s lift as buying accelerated through the week. Defensives in telcos and utilities, while also ending in the green, notably underperformed in a market that favored high-beta recovery plays. These included GLO (+0.3% WoW), MER (+1.9% WoW), as well as market darling ICT (+2.2% WoW).

 

The Philippines emerged as the best-performing market in the region, finishing strong on Friday (+1.1% DoD) despite weakness across global equities as investors grew more cautious about the durability of the AI-driven rally and the likelihood of continued Fed easing in December.

 

On the domestic macro front, BSP Governor Eli Remolona reiterated that another 25bp rate cut is “possible” in December, which would mark the fifth consecutive reduction since April and bring cumulative easing to 175bps since the cycle began in August 2024.

 

Though sentiment was generally positive for the week, investors still kept a close eye on local political developments. Pres. Marcos Jr. announced a fresh Cabinet reorganization following the resignations of Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman and Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin, after their departments were cited in the ongoing flood control corruption scandal. This marks the second major reshuffle since the administration’s “bold reset” in May. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto has been appointed the new Executive Secretary, while economic czar Frederick Go has been named the new Finance Secretary. Also, the Department of Public Works and Highways and the Independent Commission for Infrastructure urged the filing of plunder and graft charges against former House Speaker Martin Romualdez and ex-lawmaker Zaldy Co for their alleged involvement in the same scandal.

 

Foreigners were net buyers by $51m, due mostly to a large block in PLUS (+11.1% WoW), without which net foreign outflow would reach $92m for the week. The peso strengthened by 0.4% WoW to 58.845:$1.

Jollibee - 3Q25: Domestic disruptions; international looking better
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

9M25 profit +2%, top line +14%

JFC’s 9M25 profit was up 2% to P8.6bn, as higher financing charges tempered otherwise strong EBIT and topline growth of 15% and 14%, respectively. We consider these results in line with estimates. For 3Q25 alone, EBIT rose 7% to P5.2bn on top line growth of 13% to P76.4bn. Profit for the quarter increased 8% to P3.0bn, lifted by ‘Other income’, which benefited from gains from lease pretermination (P309m) and forex translation (P211m).

 

Weather disrupts domestic business; International sees improvement

Consolidated SSS in the quarter grew a modest 3.1% vs 5.5% in 2Q as the tempered domestic performance was lifted by the int’l business.

 

The domestic business was hampered by stronger-than-usual rains slowing systemwide sales (SWS) growth and SSSG to +5.5% and +1.2% in 3Q. The company noted that the Philippines saw significantly more typhoons (and with greater severity) in 3Q25 (14) compared to 3Q24 (9).

 

The int’l segment SSSG (+6.2%) was led by the Jollibee Int’l brand (US +13.4%, EMEA +6.0%, Vietnam +25.5%). The ramp up of franchising initiatives in the US and robust local consumer-driven growth in Vietnam (+40.7% SWS) should drive things forward.

China sustains improvement, Smashburger profitability by 2026 eyed

China saw the second straight quarter of positive SSSG. 3Q SSSG accelerated to +8.0% from +3.9% in 2Q, driven by a more delivery and value-oriented strategy. Meanwhile, Smashburger still saw its SSS decline by 4.7% (-9.9% in 2Q). Management is aiming for Smashburger to reach positive net operating income by end-2026. The company hopes to achieve this turnaround principally by refranchising ~100 corporate stores.

 

Volume-led strategy

Consolidated gross margin in 3Q contracted 70bp YoY to 18.4% with the company favoring volume-driven growth. Improving transaction count will still be the focus, so price increases will likely be restrained.

 

Domestic business normalizing in Oct

Management highlighted that the company remains on track to hit its full-year guidance this year (4-6% SSSG, 10-15% EBIT growth). The company sounded more upbeat on the domestic business for 4Q as Oct was said to have rebounded -- looking similar to the strong performance seen in the first half. We remain comfortable about the trajectory of the domestic business as it relates to JFC’s goal of trebling income by 2028. Of greater concern is whether the international businesses can contribute positively (and meaningfully) to that goal.

 
Puregold - 3Q25: Puregold moderates, S&R lifts sales
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

9M25 profit +6%, top line +11%

PGOLD’s 9M25 net profit grew 6% YoY to P7.3bn on top line growth of 11% to P168.1bn. We view the interim profit as trailing behind our estimates, even though top line is within expectations. For 3Q25 alone, profit increased by only 2% despite EBIT and net sales rising, 13% and 9%, respectively. “Other expenses” relating to store expansion weighed on the bottom. 3Q consolidated gross margins expanded 120bp benefitting from less discounting in Puregold as well as strategic price hikes and supply chain efficiencies in S&R.

 

S&R drives top line growth

Consolidated sales in the quarter rose 9% to P58.2bn primarily driven by continued momentum from the S&R store brand as it offset the deceleration seen in the Puregold brand. S&R sales for 3Q jumped +16% while the Puregold segment grew only 5%. Consolidated SSSG for 3Q moderated +3.5% with Puregold segment dipping +2.0% from +6.7% in 2Q, while S&R SSSG quickened to +6.7% from +5.4% in 2Q. Stronger-than-usual rains, along with a higher base (+6.7% SSSG 3Q24), dampened Puregold’s YoY growth for the quarter. For the 9M25 period, consolidated SSSG was +5.0%.

 

Private label is the next great opportunity

The Puregold brand eyes to relaunch a few hundred “house brand” SKUs by Dec.

Currently, private label contribution to total revenues is <1% for Puregold and we believe this is significant upside opportunity as it would allow to compete more aggressively on price as hard discounters grow. Private label products account for <10% for S&R.

 

Store rollout to accelerate next year

In 3Q alone, the group opened seven new Puregold stores—bringing the store count to 673 Puregold stores (including 153 Puremart stores), 31 S&R warehouses, and 67 S&R QSRs. On Puregold specifically, the company did not disclose the number of new stores planned to open for next year. However, it was guided that the amount will be more than this year’s target of 30 new Puregold stores. The expansion of the small-format Puremart is “on the table” for next year. The company emphasized its current focus of optimizing the supply chain despite tracking a robust ~40% growth in sales for this year. Puremart currently contributes <1% of sales.

 

Management optimistic for the rest of the year

The company retained its guidance of consolidated top line growth of 7-9%, which, frankly, looks conservative. For Puregold, Oct was said to be a soft month as heavy rains persisted and led to lower foot traffic, but Nov numbers have rebounded, with the balance of the year expected to be good. Meanwhile, S&R was said to be maintaining its current sales growth trend.

 
Wilcon Depot - 3Q25: Turning point?
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Growth returns

After many quarters of declining same-store sales, WLCN finally saw SSSG turn positive in 3Q25. SSSG for the quarter was +4.8% which we see as a good outcome even though a poor 3Q24 provided a flattering base of comparison. Overall revenue for 3Q25 rose 9% YoY, while EBIT jumped 11%. Net profit for 3Q increased 16% to P703m, bringing 9M to P1.87bn, -12% YoY. We view the results as being mildly better than expected.

 

3Q SSSG turns positive

3Q25 SSSG was +4.8%, the first such positive print after an extended period of declining SSS. While one can argue that this is in part due to a low 3Q24 base (SSSG of -8.5%), implying that SSS is still below the comparable 2023 period, we still see this as a positive outcome. Absolute daily sales across its network has picked up, as evidenced by the +6% QoQ improvement in top line.

Opex management keeps EBIT margins up

Gross margins for the period were narrower (-1.0ppt vs 2Q25), a decline seen across both its in-house and third-party brands. The company, however, managed to keep opex growth in check (+3% YoY), with measures to control rent and logistics expenses. Note “Other Income” growth was high (+61%) because costs related to the fire last year were booked in 3Q24. As a result, 3Q EBIT grew 11% to P988m and profit rose +16% to P703m.

 

Sounding upbeat

The company has 103 operating stores as of Sept 2025 and will open only one more before year-end after opting to defer a couple of store openings. Management sounded upbeat when asked about near-term prospects, citing good numbers going into 4Q25.

 
GT Capital - Shift in mix, excise tax squeeze auto margins in 3Q25
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M25 core profit at P25.7bn (+22%), In line with Street but Ahead of Regis

GTCAP reported its 3Q25 core income (before pref divs) amounted to P7.8bn, up 4% YoY, buoyed by modest profit growth at Metrobank (see results comment) and Metro Pacific (MPI), as well as higher ownership stakes in both. These gains helped offset softer sales and margin pressure at Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), and losses at the property unit. 9M25 core profit (after pref divs) reached P26bn, up 22% YoY. The results appear in line with consensus forecast but are ahead of our estimates.

 

Auto margins contract 280bps QoQ on volume mix

Derived 3Q25 profit for TMP dropped 30% QoQ, 7% YoY to P4.3bn, after sales volumes slipped 4% QoQ, 2% YoY to 54k units and GPM contracted 280bps QoQ (+18bps YoY) to 14.7%. Although the PHP saw a modest softening against the USD during the quarter, margin pressure was driven more significantly by the excise tax on pickup trucks and the normalization of higher-margin Japan-sourced unit sales. Opex rose 12% QoQ and accounted for 6% of revenues in 3Q, which we would consider in line with historical seasonality.

TMP’s HEV sales grew 11% YoY in 3Q25, moderating sharply from the 42% pace in 1H, but could rebound with the launch of new HEV models in 4Q (e.g. Ativ).

 

MPI: Power, water units offset higher financing costs at tollroad unit

MPI reported a core profit of P23.6bn in 9M25, up 14% YoY and implying that 3Q25 profit only grew by a mere 4%. The profit growth slowdown was largely due to higher financing costs at the tollroad unit, which almost negated substantial gains at the power and water units.

 

Property reservation sales rise 23% QoQ (-14% YoY)

The property unit’s reservation sales improved 23% QoQ (-14% YoY) to P3.7bn, even without new launches. 9M25 sales rose 5% to P12.6bn, driven by RFO sales, pre-selling of high-end and horizontal projects, as well as commercial lots. Booked revenues however fell 22% YoY in 3Q due to a combination of slower completions and higher cancellations, pushing its property unit to a loss in 3Q.

9M25 core profit rises 15% to P10.2bn – Ahead of Street estimates

East Zone spending dips 3% in 3Q25

Its core East Zone (EZ) unit’s capex declined 1% YoY to P14.9bn in 9M25, implying that capex spend in 3Q25 alone dropped 3% to P5.6bn. Philippine Gaming - 3Q25 recap: a reversal of fortune
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A reversal of fortune

The strong positive momentum of the online gaming segment in 2Q25 reversed in 3Q25, as the BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms (beginning August 17). As a result, after growing 23% QoQ (+78% YoY) in 2Q25, GGR of bingo and e-games fell 28% QoQ (up 14% YoY) to P45.7bn in 3Q25. On the other hand, despite concerns that the flood control corruption scandal would hurt the VIP and premium mass segments of physical casinos, 3Q25 GGR of licensed casinos inched up 3% QoQ (down 10% YoY) to P45.6bn, after declining 11% QoQ in 2Q25.

 

Online gaming GGR could fall further in 4Q25

While online gaming GGR already saw a meaningful decline in 3Q25, we believe GGR could still fall further in 4Q25. GGR of bingo + e-games in the month of September was only P12.2bn, a 45% drop from the June figure. If the September GGR level were to be maintained in 4Q25, then GGR for the quarter would amount to P36.6bn, another 20% lower than the 3Q25 level. While one cannot totally discount a recovery, 4Q25 GGR is likely to decline further.

 

PLUS gains market share despite revenue decline

Despite the 23% QoQ decline (flat YoY) of the 3Q25 GGR of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), its market share in the online gaming segment increased 2ppt QoQ to 41%.

Meanwhile, PLUS’ 3Q25 net profit fell by a larger magnitude (59% QoQ and 51% YoY) due to operating leverage.

 

BLOOM maintains GGR market share but loses share of EBITDA

3Q25 GGR of Bloomberry Resorts grew 4% QoQ (down 10% YoY), maintaining its GGR market share QoQ at 39%, still the highest among Metro Manila licensed casinos. However, higher cash costs led to a sharp decline in EBITDA (down 24% QoQ and 53% YoY). As a result, its market share of EBITDA fell 8ppt QoQ and 17ppt YoY to 24%. BLOOM’s EBITDA ranking also fell to third, from first.

 

Both segments face headwinds

Both the onsite and online segments are still facing headwinds over the next 12 months. Demand for onsite gambling is already weak due to the POGO ban as well as lower tourist arrivals from China and Korea. Furthermore, Alliance Global (AGI PM) plans to open a new integrated resort in 3Q26 which will add c. 20% to the table and gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City. Meanwhile, tighter regulatory restrictions are looming for the online business. Some restrictions have already been imposed, such as a ban on physical advertisements (such as billboards) as well as removal of links on e-wallets that redirect to online gambling platforms. Other restrictions proposed include: raising minimum bets, raising the minimum “cash-in”, and increasing taxes on online gambling.

SECB 9M25 NPAT P9.1bn -- strong PPOP +24%; have credit costs peaked?

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 9M25 NPAT of P9.1bn, up 7% YoY, slightly ahead of our FY25E of P11.9bn (consensus P12.1bn), with 3Q25 alone at P3.2bn. Notably, the 3Q25 performance came despite a massive P3.55bn provision charge (or >200bps credit cost). SECB management have been guiding that 3Q25 would be peak credit cost as part of the ongoing credit card cleanup. If it happens, then the potential profit uplift could be significant. PPOP surged 24% in 9M25, with 3Q25 PPOP ROE at c20%. Loan growth slowed sharply to 8% YoY, from 16% in 2Q25. This is in line with management guidance to end the year in high-single-digits to low-double-digits to conserve capital. That said, consumer loans still surged 24% YoY, driven by autos (+42%). NIMs climbed 12bps QoQ to 4.76%, driven by rising loan yields (+13bps QoQ) given continuing improvement in loan mix. Overall asset yields were up 16bps QoQ while funding costs were flat (+3bps QoQ) helped by falling policy interest rates and RRR cuts.

DigiPlus Interactive subscribes to convertible notes of integrated resort operator

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) signed a convertible notes agreement to acquire a controlling stake in International Entertainment Corporation (1009 HK). IEC, through its subsidiaries, owns and operates New Coast Hotel Manila, an integrated hotel and casino complex. New Coast Hotel Manila currently offers 203 guest rooms and operates 96 gaming tables, and 495 slot machines. The complex is currently under renovation – the casino will be completed in January 2026 and the hotly renovation will finish in 3Q26. The subscription to HK$1.6bn (c. P12bn) convertible notes will be issued in two tranches. PLUS has the option to convert the notes and could hold c. 53.9% of IEC’s issued capital stock, based on an agreed initial conversion price of HK$1.00/share. The notes carry an interest rate of 3% per annum. If PLUS will not convert, the notes will be redeemable at 108% at the end of their five-year maturity. For the year ending June 2025, IEC reported a net loss of HK$282mn (c. P2.1bn). Integrated resort operators are charged c. 9ppt less on their e-casino GGR than pure online operators. If this were to be applied to DigiPlus Interactive’s trailing 12 months operations (including 54% of the net loss of IEC), we estimate that net profit would have been about P19.4bn (c. 40% higher than the actual trailing 12m profit of P14.0bn).

DigiPlus plans soft launch in South Africa in early 2027

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) plans to soft launch its operations in South Africa in early 2027. It hopes to secure a gaming license in South Africa within eight months. The draft circular is open for public comments until December 3. Sports betting gross gaming revenue (GGR) in South Africa reached approximately US$1.9bn in 2024. Online sports betting contributed about US$1.6bn. In comparison, the Philippines’ electronic games GGR (online casino + online sports betting) totaled US$2.3bn (P136bn) in 2024. South Africa currently does not allow online casino games but there is Remote Gambling Bill in South Africa’s parliament which could expand the market by legalizing additional formats including online casino gaming.

PLDT revives REIT listing plan for ePLDT

PLDT Chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan has put a REIT listing back on the table to reduce debt and spur liquidity for PLDT. The REIT listing is an alternative given plans to finalize the sale of a minority stake in ePLDT to Japan's Nippon Telegraph and Telephone Corp. (NTT) has failed to progress. The company, however, remains in talks with a multinational group for a possible acquisition of stake in PLDT's data center arm—ePLDT Inc. ePLDT is currently negotiating with several companies to sell 49% of its data center business, valued at US$1bn.

Prime Infra, FGEN natgas deal reaches financial close

Razon-led Prime Infrastructure Capital Inc. (Prime Infra) has reached financial close of its acquisition of a 60% stake in First Gen’s (FGEN PM) gas assets. Acquisition price is P50bn, subject to adjustments and additional earnout amounts that are payable based on the fulfillment of certain conditions. Prime Infra now owns a 60% controlling stake in FGEN’s 2,047 MW natgas power plants and the offshore LNG terminal. FGEN will retain a 40% stake in the natgas plants and a 20% stake in the LNG terminal.

Manila Water appoints new CEO amid parent’s reorganization

Manila Water appointed Mr. Roberto Jose Locsin as president and CEO effective Jan 1, 2026. Mr. Locsin has been the company’s Chief Administrative Officer and COO for International Business since 2021. Prior to MWC, Mr. Locsin served as the chief executive officer of Philippine growth terminals of ICTSI (ICT PM). DMCI, Japanese partner bag P22bn subway package

The Department of Transportation (DOTR) awarded Contract Package (CP) 105 of the Metro Manila Subway to the joint venture of DMCI and Japan-based Nishimatsu Construction. Contract amounts to P21.7bn (ex-VAT). CP 105 covers a 0.66-kilometer tunnel, a 242.2-meter station in Kalayaan and a 436.05-meter station in Bonifacio Global City. DMCI said that it may take at least 67 months to complete the construction. DMCI’s order book stood at P32.8bn in end-September. The Metro Manila Subway spans 33km and will have 17 stations from Valenzuela City to the Ninoy Aquino International Airport. It was planned to partially operate by 2028 but due to right-of-way delays, the DOTR delayed the completion to 2032.

BSP Governor: rate cut possible in December

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that another 25bp rate cut is 'possible' in Dec. Another cut in Dec would mark the fifth consecutive cut this year since Apr. Rates have been lowered by a total of 175bp since the start of the easing cycle back in Aug 2024. Meanwhile, a cut in the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) from 5.% was said to still being deliberated. Remolona also commented that the BSP is not worried about the peso's recent movement but noted that minimal intervention has been done. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.5%. For 2026, consensus expects a 25bp cut to 4.25%, while Regis expects 50bp cuts total to 4.00%.

Finance Secretary rules out off-cycle rate reduction, expects 25bp cut in Dec

Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto dismissed an off-cycle move on monetary policy to reduce rates despite weaker-than-expected 3Q GDP growth of +4.0%, but he notes of a high chance of a rate cut at the BSP's next meeting in Dec. BSP Deputy Governor Zeno Abenoja stated that they did not discuss any possible off-cycle monetary policy easing and elaborated that it was "probably just rumors". Recall that BSP Governor Eli Remolona previously guided the possibility of another 25bp cut in Dec and further cuts in 2026 to stimulate the economy amid a slowdown from the flood control corruption scandal. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.5%. For 2026, consensus expects a 25bp cut to 4.25%, while Regis expects 50bp cut total to 4.00%. 2025 GDP growth expectations for consensus and Regis are at 5.2% and 4.8%, respectively. 2026 GDP growth expectations are both at 5.5%.

Economy Secretary maintains 6-7% GDP growth target for 2026-2028

Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that the medium-term GDP growth target will remain at 6-7%. He stated that 6-7% is "doable" assuming that the gov't will address current issues. This is higher than former Finance Secretary Ralph Recto's recently estimated guidance of 5-6% GDP growth for 2026. Balisacan cited that "the economy's overall trajectory remains firm" despite short-term fluctuations and headwinds. He highlighted that the economy is anchored on "sustained growth, easing inflation, health labor market, manageable fiscal deficit and public debt, broadly stable currency, external position, and a robust banking system". For 2025, current gov't GDP growth projections are ~4.7-4.8%, lower than the initial 5.5-6.5% target. 2025 GDP growth expectations for consensus and Regis are at 5.0% and 4.8%, respectively. 2026 GDP growth expectations are both at 5.5%.

DOF projects 2025 GDP growth to settle below 5.0%

The Dept. of Finance (DOF) projects GDP growth to be 4.7-4.8% for 2025, well-below the 5.5-6.5% gov't target range. This implies that 4Q GDP is expected to grow ~3.8-4.2%, below the consensus estimate of 4.8% for the quarter. The 4.7-4.8% FY25 growth will be the lowest since 2011 (ex-pandemic), where the economy grew 3.9% due to underspending during the Aquino administration's anticorruption campaign. For 2026, the DOF projects GDP growth to be around 5-6%, lower than the 6-8% gov't target set till 2028. 2025 GDP growth expectations for consensus and Regis are at 5.1% and 4.8%, respectively. 2026 GDP growth expectations are both at 5.5%.

Customs collections moderate in October

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) stated that collections in Oct were only up ~2% YoY, a slowdown from the 8% growth in Sep. Collections were said to have been hampered by a 3% drop in import volumes and fewer working days due to poor weather. Consumption-driven imports (which usually accounts for a large share of dutiable goods) fell sharply by 20%. Despite the challenging month, Customs Commissioner Ariel Nepomuceno stated that they are retaining their P959bn (+4.5% YoY) revised revenue goal for 2025. The BOC's initial target set for this year was P1.06trn (+16% YoY). Customs also seeks to fully digitalize its operations through PPP in an effort to mitigate corruption and streamline collections.

BOP posts US$706mn surplus in Oct; GIR settles at US$110.2bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Oct posted a surplus of US$706mn. Higher than the US$82mn surplus in Sep and a reversal from the $724mn deficit in Oct 2024. The higher surplus in Oct reflected the seasonal rise in remittances and strong earnings from BPO firms as well as softer import growth. This brings the YT-Oct deficit at US$4.6bn, reversing from the US$4.4bn surplus in the same period last year. The reversal to a deficit stems from the timing of gov’t debt issuances and the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Oct stood at US$110.2bn, up from the US$109.1bn in end-Sep. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. SEC issues draft revisions on REIT regulations

The SEC issued a draft memorandum circular proposing revisions to REIT regulations. The draft circular is open for public comments until December 3. Among the proposals is to broaden the definition of “income-generating real estate”. The SEC proposes to allow power, infrastructure, and telecom assets to qualify as REIT-able assets. Reinvestment of REIT proceeds must be completed within two years (instead of one year). The proposed amendments would also require detailed disclosures on leases, related-party transactions, and fees. We do not expect the proposed revisions to significantly affect the existing REITs.

InstaPay surges +66% in Oct, leads digital payments +41% in Oct

Digital payments continue to power away, up 41.4% YoY in October to P2,291bn (roughly $39bn). Year-to-date, that’s P19,749bn, also up 41% YoY. That’s an acceleration over the 35% posted in FY2024 Again, remember that these only count transactions between platforms, and do NOT include payments done on the same system (eg, GCash to GCash, BDO to BDO, etc). Transactions over InstaPay – real-time, up to P50,000 – surged to P1,123bn, up 66.1% YoY, the fastest monthly YoY growth since late 2021. The October figure is also up an impressive 10% MoM over September. For 10M25, InstaPay payments were up 55.8% YoY to P9,069bn. The 10M growth is much faster than the already-quick 46% pace set in FY24. More notable, though, is that the volume of InstaPay transactions continued to explode, up 327% YoY in October, bringing the average ticket size down to P1,983 (under $34), crossing below P2,000 for the first time, and further reinforcing the broadening of use cases for the function. For larger, batched payments over PesoNet, October came in at P1,167bn, up 23.7% YoY, slowing from the 36% pace set in September. For the first ten months, PesoNet transactions totaled P10,680bn, up 30.7% YoY, a shade faster than the 28.5% growth in FY24.
