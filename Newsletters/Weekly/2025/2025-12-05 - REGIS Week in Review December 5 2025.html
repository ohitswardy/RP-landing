REGIS REPORT

05 December 2025

Philippine equities began the last month of the year on a weak note, taking the PCOMP lower by 1.2% or 73.02 pts WoW to 5,949.22 as foreign outflows accelerated and overall market activity slowed amid a lack of fresh domestic leads.

 

That said, Friday's macro release offered a mild positive surprise, with November CPI printing at 1.5%, below the expected 1.7% and slowing further from 1.7% in the previous month. This helped spur some local buying to trim the market's weekly loss from down 2.2% at its low.

 

For the week though, market breadth was deeply negative with losers outnumbering gainers 9:1. Holdings (-4.2% WoW) led sector decliners followed by Financials (-3.0% WoW), as BSP Gov. Remolona reiterated the central bank's stance to cut the key rate at its December meeting on the 11th, again citing slowing domestic growth which continued to temper the broad macro outlook.

 

BDO (-6.3% WoW) bore the brunt of foreign selling for the week and was the biggest drag on the index. Proxies SM (-4.1% WoW) and AC (-5.7% WoW) were also large drags, together with SMPH (-2.8% WoW) and JFC (-5.0% WoW) which are normally regarded as barometers for private consumption.

 

Meanwhile, ICT (+8.2% WoW) massively outperformed, up 6.6% on Friday alone to a fresh high of P587 following news that the company has filed for renewal of its contract to operate the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT), seeking an early extension beyond 2038. All else equal, a 25-year extension under current tariff structures would add P80/sh to Regis' target NAV for ICT.

 

ADTO slowed to $104m for the week while net foreign outflow accelerated to $83m. Peso weaker by 0.5% WoW to 58.935:$1.

ICTSI - Initiates contract renewal process for Manila port, MICT
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Renewal of MICT contract highly probable but terms uncertain

ICTSI applied to renew its contract to operate the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) beyond 2038. Although the concession expires in 2038, ICT is seeking an early extension likely to support additional expansions. We estimate a 25-year extension under current fee/tariff structures and a 3.8mn TEU volume cap could potentially add P80/sh to the valuation of ICT, all else being equal. That said, this estimate could change once we have more visibility on the renewal terms.

 

Contract renewals beyond fifty (50) years are now legal

The Philippine Constitution previously capped PPP contracts for public utilities to a maximum term of fifty (50) years but Congress “relaxed” this limit when it passed Republic Act 11659 or the Public Service Act in 2022. Under the rules of the Philippine Ports Authority (PPA), concessionaires may apply for a renewal after completing half of their contract term, which ICT crossed last November. Recall that MICT’s 25-year contract was originally granted in May 1988 and this was renewed for another 25 years in May 2013.

Renegotiated contract terms still up in the air

The PPA can commence negotiations on the terms of the renewal next year and any revision should be mutually agreed by both parties. With MICT’s ongoing expansion expected to be fully utilized by 2028, ICT is likely to raise the prospect of expanding capacity beyond Berth 8, which would entail capital intensive reclamation works. Conversely, the PPA may (again) leverage on this opportunity and negotiate for higher concession fee payments. Note that the formula for MICT’s tariffs does not include a capital recovery fee and tariff hikes are linked to inflation.

 

Contract renewal could be worth P80/sh?

Without clarity on the scope of capacity expansion, the associated capex, and any prospective changes to the contract terms, it’s difficult to assess the valuation impact at this stage. Under a scenario where (a) MICT secures a 25-year contract extension, (b) current concession fees and tariff mechanisms are maintained, and (c) long-run volumes do not exceed 3.8mn TEUs, we estimate it could potentially add P80/sh to the valuation of ICT, all else being equal.

 
Philippine Banks/Finance - Interest rate caps coming? It’s complicated…
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

SEC proposes new rate caps for NBFCs; unlikely for banks

The SEC has proposed more stringent rules on small-ticket, short-term, unsecured loans for non-bank fincos (NBFCs). The SEC aims to reduce the “effective interest rate” (EIR) cap to 10% monthly from 15% currently, and to broaden the universe of loans covered by the caps. While the proposed cap still seems high, credit costs of NBFCs, digibanks, neo-banks we track average in the 20-25% range, due in part to the lack of a fully functioning credit bureau. Rate caps could limit the growth of the formal credit market, and push borrowers to informal lenders where rates are even higher, and collection practices are unregulated. We believe a compromise is likely. For banks, the BSP only caps rates/fees on credit cards today; we believe it will not expand this to other products. But that could cause other complications, too…

New interest rate caps proposed for NBFCs

On 31 October, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) released a draft circular proposing changes to the interest rate caps for loans issued by non-bank finance corps (NBFCs). Nominal interest rate cap retained at 6.0% per month, or 0.2% a day. Effective interest rate (EIR) of 10.0% a month, equal to nominal rate, plus fees (processing, service, etc), but excluding late and nonpayment fees. This is lower than the current EIR cap of 15.0%. The proposed changes aim to greatly expand the universe of loans covered by the rate caps, by increasing the loan amounts covered to P20,000 (about $340), double the previous P10,000, and including loans with initial maturity of up to 6 months, compared to 4 months previously.

 
Philippine Mining - Uncovering treasure
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

From zero to hero?

The Philippines holds substantial undeveloped gold, copper, and nickel resources, which we estimate could be valued at over US$1.7tn at today’s prices. We believe it is conceivable for these estimates to rise further pending ongoing exploration programs of various miners. Realizing this wealth could be crucial for economic growth, the current government has implemented policy moves to pave the way. In this report, we take an introductory look at the state of the mining industry and the potential revival that might just ensue in the coming years.

 

Gradual steps for sustained growth

The Marcos administration has been gradually restoring investor confidence by streamlining mining application procedures and resolving over a decade of fiscal uncertainty with the passage of Republic Act (RA) 12253 or the Enhanced Mining Fiscal Regime Law in September. Crucially, RA 12253 aims to strengthen the incentive structure for local governments by ensuring the prompt release of mining royalty allocations to them.

Renewed mining interest

Even before the recent surge in metal prices, listed miners have been steadily deploying capital to expand production, develop new sites, and resume exploration programs. Drawing from the stated targets of listed miners, we estimate that mineral exports could expand by 16% in 2026, underpinned by higher gold prices and output. Although these efforts are unlikely to yield widespread economic benefits near-term, we believe the sector’s trajectory is positive and the operating environment increasingly supportive.

 

Hoping for an investment boom

After years of muted capital spending in the sector, recent momentum in exploration activities point to the possibility of a significant acceleration in investments likely by 2027. Our estimates suggest over US$9bn in potential investments could pour into the mining sector should greenfield projects of Sagittarrius Mines (a.k.a Tampakan) and St. Augustine (a.k.a. Kingking mine) push through, and Philex Mining accelerates its expansion. Developments in the industry could become more exciting in the next few years, as studies confirm attractive prospects that have long been overlooked.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI files renewal application for MICT contract

ICTSI applied for the renewal of its contract with the Philippine Ports Authority to operate Manila International Container Terminal beyond 2038. Request for early contract renewal will ensure the port operator’s investments in equipment and infrastructure in MICT are viable. According to ICTSI Executive VP Christian Gonzales. MICT accounts for ~20% of EBITDA.

MGEN, Saudi-based ACWA Power to develop 125MW solar farm in Iloilo

Meralco Powergen (MGEN) and Saudi Arabia-based ACWA Power are moving forward with its first joint venture project, a 125MW solar plant in Concepcion, Iloilo. Project will rise on a 120-hectare site and MGEN is considering joining the government’s green energy auction to secure offtake. JV ownership structure was not disclosed. MGEN currently has 5,068MW (2,558 MW attributable) of operational generation assets and 6,327 MW (2,460 MW attributable) projects under construction.

Aboitiz-led consortium secures PCC approval for CBK hydro acquisition

The Philippine Competition Commission approved Thunder Consortium’s acquisition of the 797-MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydro complex. Thunder Consortium comprises Aboitiz Power (AP PM) unit, Aboitiz Renewables, Sumitomo Corp., and Electric Power Development Corp. Recall that the consortium offered P36bn for the facility. PSALM is targeting turnover of the power plant in February 2026. AP will have a 64% stake in the consortium; Plant is deemed to be immediately earnings accretive upon turnover. According to AP Chief Financial Officer Juan Alejandro “Sandro” A. Aboitiz. AP is currently operating an attributable 4,636 MW of capacity, of which ~3,500 are from thermal plants. It has a pipeline of 421MW of renewable energy projects that are under construction. Apart from CBK, AP is also acquiring a 25% stake in a 1,320MW coal-fired plant in Vietnam for US$220mn. The company set a target capacity of 9.2GW by 2030, with a 50:50 balance between renewable and thermal energy sources. As of end-September 2025, AP’s net debt stood at P237bn and net debt-to-equity stood at 1.08x. Note that AP’s is still carrying debt (~P50bn) from the acquisition of San Miguel’s LNG plants (i.e. Chromite). There is a plan to push down debt related to Chromite to the operating units.

RRHI completes P146mn acquisition of Premiumbikes

Following recent approval from the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) last month, RRHI has completed its acquisition of 100% of Premiumbikes Corp. The deal, executed through wholly owned subsidiary Robinson's Supermarket Corp., closed on Dec 1. The P146mn price tag implied a 1x P/B. Premiumbikes was previously owned by Lance Gokongwei. In 2024, it had a store network of 214 stores on P4.2bn (+15% YoY) of sales. RRHI's total sales in 2024 was P200.5bn (+4% YoY).

SEC approves increase in MREIT's authorized capital stock

The SEC approved the increase in MREIT’s authorized capital stock from P5bn to P8bn (at P1/share). MREIT previously announced that it would acquire 200,000sqm sqm of office buildings from Megaworld in a share-for-property swap. 165,000sqm in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio. 35,000sqm in Eastwood, Quezon City. This will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 41% to 680,000sqm. MREIT will issue up to 1.36bn new shares to Megaworld at a minimum price of P14.39/share. This implies a value of c. P19.6bn. The issuance will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 37%. The minimum price is 3% higher than the last closing price. If MREIT will issue 1.36bn new MREIT shares, then we estimate that the AGI Group would need to sell c. 100mn MREIT shares in order for MREIT to meet minimum public ownership requirements. MEG currently offers 4.5% dividend yield and is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.2X FY26E P/E. MREIT currently offers 7.6% FY26E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The 10-year government bond yield is currently 5.9%.

DigiPlus targeting 50mn registered users in 2026

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is targeting to have 50mn registered users “…sometime within 2026…”. It currently has over 40mn registered users. DigiPlus said that in 3Q25, it had an average of 6mn monthly active users. This is lower than the 8.5mn monthly active users recorded in 2Q25.

DigiPlus still in legal dispute with Zaldy Co firm

According to the Philippine Daily Inquirer, DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) remains in a legal dispute with a firm owned by former Congressman Elizaldy “Zaldy” Co. DigiPlus owns 51% of Hotel Enterprises of the Philippines, which owns Midas Hotel. The JV partner is Eco Leisure and Hospitality Holdings, which is reportedly owned by Zaldy Co. DigiPlus was in talks to sell its stake to a third party for P2.5bn but Eco Leisure invoked its right of first refusal. However, Eco Leisure was only willing to pay P1bn. The two parties went to court and DigiPlus won the case. Eco Leisure then filed an appeal before the RTC Pasig in 2022.

BSP says rate cut next week more likely given slower growth outlook

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the current slower growth outlook implies a higher chance for another rate cut during next week's Monetary Board meeting on Dec 11. Remolona added that GDP growth likely slowed to 4-5% for 2025, lower than the gov't target range of 5.5-6.5% for the year. For 2025 and 2026, consensus expects GDP growth to be 4.9% and 5.4%, respectively. Regis expects GDP growth to be 4.8% and 5.5%. On rate cut expectations, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.50% in 2025. For next year, consensus expects 25bp cut to 4.25%, while Regis expects 50bp cuts total to 4.00%.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.8bn in Oct

The Oct trade deficit narrowed to US$3.8bn, contracting 34% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports the robust growth of exports. This brings the 10M25 trade deficit to US$41.3bn, 9% narrower YoY. Imports for the month fell by 7% YoY to US$11.2bn, a reversal from the 2% increase in Sep. Primarily driven by the significant decrease in consumer goods (-12%) and fuel imports (-20%). Exports climbed by 19% YoY to US$7.4bn, the tenth consecutive month of growth and accelerating from the 16% increase in Sep. Electronics exports surged by 44% YoY to $4.2bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 3% to US$3.2bn.

Government debt inches up to P17.56trn in end-Oct

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.6% MoM to P17.56trn as of end-Oct. The increase was driven by net issuances of domestic and external liabilities alongside the depreciation of the peso against the US dollar. The peso averaged P58.3 per US dollar in Oct vs P57.3 in Sep. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.6% and 31.4% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 0.6% MoM to P12.1trn. External debt rose by 0.6% MoM to P5.5trn. The P17.56trn figure exceeds the P17.36-trn end-2025 projection by 1.2%. Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury stated that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down". The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 3Q25 stood at 63.1% of GDP unchanged vs 2Q25 but up from 60.7% in 4Q24. The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028.

Senate passes 2026 budget on 2nd reading

The Senate passed on second reading the proposed P6.79trn 2026 national budget. Some key amendments made relative to the House of Representatives' version of the budget were: Increased funding for social services and a reversal of the P52.1bn reduction in infra to avoid project delays. Dept. of Education (+P78.5bn to P992.7bn), Dept. of Interior and Local Gov't (+P22.6bn to P308.2bn). 4Ps Program (+P11.2bn). Dept. of Public Works & Highways (+P52.1bn). Budget cuts in certain line items and agencies. Unprogrammed appropriations (-P68.5bn to P174.5bn). Dept. of Health., Dept. of Social Welfare, and Dept. of Agriculture budgets were reduced by ~P20-30bn each. Senator Gatchalian stated that more details including line by line amendments will be disclosed on Dec 6, prior to approval on third reading of the General Appropriation Bill (GAB). The current schedule for remaining budget deliberations is as follows: Third and final reading on Dec 9, then finalization of the bicameral report by Dec 16 and ratification of the budget by Dec 17. Tentative signing of the General Appropriations Act (GAA) by the President on Dec 29. The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than 2025's P6.33trn (+10% YoY) budget.

President Marcos announces base pay hike for military, uniformed personnel

President Marcos signed Executive Order No. 107, which hikes the base pay of military and uniformed personnel (MUP) by 15% over three years, beginning Jan 1, 2026. He also ordered an increase in MUP’s Subsistence Allowance to P350 per day from P150 starting on Jan 1, 2026. Move will also consequently increase the pay of regular and survivorship MUP pensioners, as pensions are indexed to the salary of active personnel. More than 470k active military personnel will reportedly benefit from the hike. Funds for the increase in the base pay and subsistence allowance will come from available appropriations in the 2026 national budget and other allowable sources. Future increases will form part of the national budget. Under EO 107, an Inter-Agency Technical Working Group (lATWG) will also be created to conduct a comprehensive review of the current MUP pension system. Recall that former President Duterte raised salaries for MUPs by an average of 59% in 2018 and 72% in 2019.

Commercial banks' loan growth inches down to 10.3% in Oct

Preliminary data showed that loans of commercial banks rose 10.3% in Oct, slightly moderating from the 10.5% pace in Sep. Loans to productive activities were unchanged relative to the previous month at 9.1%. Consumer loans still grew at a heady pace of 23.1%, though a tad slower than Sep’s 23.5% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated further to 8.3% in Oct from 7.6% in Sep.

DICT aims to cut “internet” prices by 40%

In a press briefing, Dept of Information Communication Technology (DICT) Secretary Henry Aguda says he is aiming for a 40% cut in “internet costs” as early as next year. Press briefing was held following the release of the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA). Aguda’s statements imply he’s referring to reductions in broadband prices. Aguda claims the Philippines is #6 in ASEAN in terms of affordability of internet. A reduction of at least 40% would be needed for PH to reach #1 or #2. Broadband accounts for 84% of Converge’s (CNVRG PM) revenue; 26% of PLDT’s (TEL PM) and 15% of Globe (GLO PM).

CAMPI lowers 2025 sales target to 486k units (+4%)

The Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines Inc. (CAMPI) reduced its 2025 sales target to 486k units (+4%) from a previous target of 500k units (+7%). According to CAMPI President Rommel Gutierrez. Even though CAMPI’s 10M25 sales only reached 383k units (-0.2% YoY), Mr. Gutierrez anticipates the target remains achievable once all manufacturers submit results by year-end. We reckon CAMPI can achieve its target only with the inclusion of BYD’s sales in the count. We estimate BYD accounted for ~5% of 9M25 industry sales.

Second edition of anti-corruption rallies held in Quezon City and Manila

The second rally of the “Trillion Peso March” at the People Power Monument in Quezon City, along with the “Baha sa Luneta 2.0” protest at Luneta Park in Manila, was held on November 30, with both demonstrations calling for an end to corruption and greater accountability. It was reported that a small portion of left-leaning groups called for the resignation of President Marcos and impeachment of VP Sara Duterte given the allegations of both being involved in corruption. A "Manifesto of Unwavering Support and Solidarity" from retired and active generals as well as other officials of the Armed Forces of the Philippines, Philippine National Police, and other enforcement agencies was released. The statement iterated their stance "to condemn and reject any call for the AFP to engage in unconstitutional acts or military adventurism". The Quezon City Police District reported no violent or chaotic incidents throughout the event as rallyists dispersed peacefully by end of day. The crowd estimates at Quezon City ranged from 33,000-55,000 protestors throughout the day while the crowd at Manila peaked at around 5,000 people. This is lower than the 100,000 estimated participants in the first anti-corruption rally held last Sep 21.

Rogelio Singson resigns as ICI commissioner

Rogelio Singson has resigned as a member of the Independent Commission for Infrastructure (ICI), the agency tasked to investigate irregularities in flood control projects. ICI Chairperson Andres Reyes said that Singson described the work at the ICI as “very intense and stressful,” noting that it has taken a toll on his aging body. Resignation takes effect on Dec 15 but may be extended until year-end. Singson was the former secretary of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways during the late President Noynoy Aquino's administration from 2010-2016. He also oversaw the Good Governance and Anti-Corruption Program at the time.

SEC proposes tiered minimum public ownership requirement for IPOs

The SEC proposed a tiering system for minimum public ownership requirement of IPOs. Tier 1 – market cap below P500mn – at least 33%. Tier 2 – market cap from P500mn to P1bn – at least 25% public float. Tier 3 – market cap from P1bn to P50bn – at least 20% public float. Tier 4 – market cap from P50bn to P150bn – at least 15% public float. Tier 5 – market cap above P150bn – at least 12% public float.
