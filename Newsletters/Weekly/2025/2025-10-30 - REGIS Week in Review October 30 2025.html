REGIS REPORT

30 October 2025

This shortened trading week saw local equities log a second straight weekly loss, taking the PCOMP down 1.0% or 58.34 pts WoW to 5,929.68. The Philippine peso plunged to a historic low this week, breaching the 59:$1 level for the first time ever. The sharp depreciation came amid renewed dollar strength following hawkish remarks from the US Fed, and onshore, continued foreign outflows amid a deteriorating growth outlook. The record-low peso weighed heavily on sentiment, fueling concerns over detrimental effects on inflation. Note the BSP maintained they are not inclined to intervene in the fx market to temper volatility in the near term.

 

ALI (-6.4% WoW) continued to lag, breaking below the key P20 support level as foreign investors extended their selling streak. Sentiment on the property sector remained weak, with the residential segment facing expectations of tepid demand exacerbated by fallout from the flood control corruption scandal that is widely seen impacting appetite for high-end property.

 

Banks traded mixed, with BPI (+4.9% WoW) rebounding from its slump the previous week, while BDO (-1.8% WoW) pared its weekly losses, bouncing from its 52W low after a sharp sell-down early in the week. Recently released 3Q results actually came in-line for both banks marked by resilient loan growth, stable asset quality, and strong returns steadily at mid-teens.

 

Over to macro, the BSP signaled the likelihood of another 25-bp rate cut in December, considering the steady easing in inflation and amid headwinds to domestic growth. The guidance reinforced expectations of a more accommodative policy stance heading into 2026, though the peso’s sharp depreciation may complicate the timing and extent of further easing.

 

Overall, the market seemed to adopt a wait-and-see stance ahead of a busy stretch of 3Q earnings releases over the next two weeks. Investors also await the 3Q GDP report set for release late next week, which could influence growth expectations for the quarters ahead. Adding to next week’s key events, Maynilad’s (ticker: MYNLD) upcoming P34.3-bn ($582-m) IPO is seen drawing market interest, potentially serving as a fresh test of investor appetite for new listings in a challenging environment.

 

Foreigners remained net sellers for the fifth straight week. The peso finished lower by 0.4% WoW, though rebounded modestly from its all-time-low, ending the week at 58.885:$1.

BDO Unibank - 9M25 NPAT in line; stock falls below 1x P/book… Buy
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT of P63.1bn, +4% YoY, in line despite cost pressures

BDO’s 9M25 NPAT of P63.1bn (+4% YoY) was broadly in line with our FY25E forecast of P86.7bn (consensus P86.6bn). Loans grew 14% YoY, driven by consumers (+18%), though NIMs inched down QoQ on deposit competition. Strong fee incomes (+15%) were offset by opex growth (also +15%). Credit costs moderated, despite a “technical” increase in mortgage NPLs. While ROEs improved to 14.5% in 3Q25, the stock slipped under 1x P/book on our FY2026E forecasts. Our TP of P200/sh now represents total potential return of 56%. Buy.

 

Consumer loans +18%, but funding costs crimp NIMs; Opex still elevated

Loans expanded 14% YoY in 3Q25, maintaining the pace from 2Q (Fig 1). Consumers grew 18%, and now account for more than 25% of total loans. While this supported loan yields (Fig 3), NIMs inched down 4bps QoQ (Fig 2) as deposit competition intensified (Fig 4), despite policy rate cuts and RRR cuts. Fee incomes remained strong, up 15% YoY. Opex, though, stayed elevated, up 15%. That said, PPOP accelerated to +6% YoY in 3Q25, from +2% in 1H25.

Credit costs ease to 45bps; NPL cover down on “technical” mortgage NPLs

In 3Q, credit costs moderated to 45bps (Fig 6) as management keeps guidance at 40-45bps for FY25E. NPLs were steady at 1.77% while NPL Cover dipped to 134% (Fig 5). “Technical” issues saw mortgage NPLs jump 80bps ytd to 6.5%. One developer, whose buyers are in delinquency, has seen delays in titling of its completed units. BDO is thus technically unable to foreclose on, and dispose of, the properties. Management expects this to be resolved in due time.

 

Stock at new 52-wk low, now below 1x P/bk; Maintaining P200/sh TP; Buy

BDO shares hit an 18-month low of P131/sh on 27 Oct, and now trades at 0.98x P/book and 7.4x PER on our FY2026E numbers. Our target price of P200/sh – based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.0%, COE of 12.0% and growth of 8.0% – translates to a total potential return of 56%, including a 3% dividend yield. We maintain our Buy recommendation on the stock.

Robinsons Retail - 3Q25: SSSG moderates; buyback lifts EPS
 
 
 

9M25 sales up 5%, EBIT +4%

Robinsons Retail’s (RRHI) 9M25 net profit amounted to P3.12bn, which is down 60% YoY due to the P4.9bn one-time gain from the BPI-RBC merger last year. Note reported quarterly net income could see large swings due to the timing of dividends from its 6.5% holding in BPI. Core operations, however, remain steady with sales and EBIT for the period up 5% and 4% to P149.3bn and P6.6bn, respectively, in line with expectations. For 3Q25 alone, net sales rose 4% to P50.8bn, while EBIT was up 3% to P2.3bn. Blended SSSG for 3Q slowed to 1.6% from 4.8% the previous quarter. The slower SSSG was primarily due to a moderation in the growth of the Food and Drugstore (staples) segments as well as the -15% drop in SSS for Department Stores.

 

Staples growth moderates; department stores weaker

Revenues from the Food segment increased 4.5% YoY in 3Q25 to P31bn driven by +2.8% SSSG, new stores in 3Q (+14 net). Within the Food segment, convenience stores saw a 5th consecutive quarterly contraction in SSS (-1.0%). 

Drugstores’ net sales were up 10% YoY on +5.1% SSSG and store additions. The Food and Drugstore segments accounted for 80% of revenue in 3Q25. The Department Store segment saw revenue dip 12% with -15% SSSG following changes in the school calendar. Management expects a rebound for the segment in 4Q with key store renovations set to finish. The DIY business, meanwhile, saw positive SSSG (+1%) for the first time since 1Q23.

 

Better EPS despite additional financing costs

Recall that RRHI paid P15.7bn to buy back 22% of its own shares from DFI in June. This resulted in higher non-operating expenses (financing) in 3Q but also better EPS (+13% YoY) due to a lower share count.

 

Company retains 2025 guidance

Management says it expects its full-year SSSG figure to be around the midpoint of its 2-4% guidance. Very early indications suggest a continuation of current trends for the staple segments. The company is hopeful, however, that recent store renovations can help the discretionary formats.

Meralco core profit up 14% in 9M25 on strong generation and provision reversals

Meralco reported 9M25 core profit (excluding gains and FX adj) of P40bn, on trend to beat consensus forecast of P49bn and management’s guidance of P50bn. Derived 3Q25 core profit of P14.5bn improved 22% YoY, lifted by (a) the accretive contribution of the Chromite LNG deal, (b) provision reversals at the distribution unit following real property tax settlements, and (c) higher income from Global Business Power’s participation in the Visayas reserve market. These helped counter soft distribution volumes and reduced RES trading gains. Electricity demand contracted by 2% YoY in 3Q25, led by a 6% decline in residential sales, which management attributed to cooler temperatures and more frequent typhoons. Note that even commercial were unusually weak (-1%). With similar trends persisting early into 4Q, management expects full-year volume growth (-0.5% to -0.8%) to track close to the 9M25 decline of 0.4%.

Aboitiz Power posts 12% EBITDA recovery in 3Q25

In a press release, Aboitiz Power reported its core income (excluding FX and derivative gains) reached P23.1bn in 9M25, lower by 15% YoY. This is in line with the Street’s downgraded forecast of P30.6bn. Headline 9M25 profit was also lower by 15% YoY at P23.3bn. Implied 3Q25 core profit stood at P10.4bn, up 29% QoQ and 3% YoY. Beneficial EBITDA in 3Q25 rebounded by 12% YoY to P22.2bn, marking a recovery after two quarters of contraction. Generation/RES unit EBITDA jumped 12% YoY to P20bn, owing to (a) better hydro output and (b) reduced spot exposure. EBITDA for the distribution unit (DU) improved 5% YoY to P2.5bn, on the back of a 6% improvement in volumes.

Bloomberry to sell Korean casino business

Bloomberry Resorts (through a subsidiary) signed a share purchase agreement to spin off its casino business in Jeju and then sell the new company to Gangwon Blue Mountain. Gangwon Blue Mountain has made a down payment of KRW500mn (c. P20.5mn). Bloomberry will continue to operate the hotel business in Jeju. In 1H25, BLOOM’s Jeju operations recorded a net loss of P146mn. Bloomberry’s 1H25 recurring net loss was c. P960mn. Since BLOOM’s Korean gaming license has been fully impaired, the company could recognize a gain on the sale of its Korean gaming business.

Ayala Land and AREIT announce share-for-property swap

AREIT will acquire P19.5bn worth of assets from Ayala Land in exchange for 441mn new AREIT shares. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 15% to P158bn. AREIT’s shares outstanding will increase by 12% to 4.16bn shares. This implies that the AREIT shares will be priced at P44.15/share, which is 3% higher than the last closing price of P43.00/share. AREIT will acquire two malls with a combined GLA of 375,000sqm—Ayala Center Cebu and Ayala Malls Feliz. The companies aim to complete the transaction in 2H26. We estimate that the Ayala group would need to sell c. 140mn AREIT shares in order for the latter to meet minimum public ownership requirements. At the last closing price of P43.00/share, this would be equivalent to c. P6.0bn. ALI is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.9X FY26E P/B, and 9.4X FY26E P/E. AREIT currently offers 5.8% FY26E dividend yield. The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.0%.

MREIT to acquire 200k sqm of offices from MEG

MREIT will acquire 200,000sqm of offices from its sponsor, Megaworld, via share-for-property swap. This includes 165,000sqm of offices in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio, and 35,000sqm of offices in Eastwood, Quezon City. The transaction will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 42% to 680,000sqm. About half of MREIT’s portfolio is currently located in Fort Bonifacio. After the acquisition, we estimate that Fort Bonifacio offices will comprise 60% of MREIT’s portfolio. MREIT will issue 1.36bn new shares to Megaworld at a minimum price of P14.39/share. This is 3% higher than the last closing price of MREIT. Total transaction value of at least P19.5bn. This will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 37%. We estimate that the AGI Group will need to sell c. 100mn MREIT shares in order for MREIT to meet minimum public ownership requirements. MEG currently offers 4.6% dividend yield and is trading at 70% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 3.2X FY25E P/E. MREIT currently offers 7.3% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The current 10-year government bond yield is 6.0%.

PCC clears First Gen, Prime Infra natgas deal

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) approved Razon-led’s Prime Infrastructure’s purchase of a 60% equity stake in First Gen’s (FGEN PM) natural gas subsidiaries. FGEN and Prime Infra will continue to work towards satisfaction of all other conditions precedent. Recall that in early June, FGEN announced that it is selling 60% of its natgas assets to Razon-led Prime Infrastructure for P50bn, subject to adjustments plus additional earnout amounts if certain targets are met. Headline price of P50bn implies an equity value (100%) of P83bn and an enterprise value of US$1.8bn. Assigning the P83bn valuation to our FGEN NAV estimate would yield a NAV of P40/share (vs. current share price of P15/sh). Deal aligns interest of the two parties; deal provides FGEN with additional capital to use to (a) pay down debt, (b) undertake RE expansions and/or (c) acquire more shares of geothermal unit, EDC.

URC ethanol distillery halts operations due to waste spill

Universal Robina Corporation (URC PM) has halted the operations of its Bais Distillery Plant after the collapse of its wastewater lagoon wall due to damages from the recent earthquake and heavy rains, which resulted in the spillage of wastewater to the Tañon Strait (between Negros Island and Cebu). The distillery is located near the shoreline of Bais City, Negros Oriental and produces high-grade anhydrous ethanol for fuel blending using the molasses by-product sourced from the nearby URC sugar milling plant. The company is coordinating with affected communities to extend assistance and mitigate the impact of the spill. The Dept. of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) ordered URC to fast-track repairs to halt further contamination. Bais City Mayor Lugi Goñi said operations of the plant will only resume when URC gives "a more concrete, reliable, and long-term solution to this problem" as it has been recurring for several years already. Previously reported incidents include "intentional" waste discharge as well as reports of fish kill which led to the temporary closure of the plant back in 2015. The Bais distillery is one of two ethanol distillery plants part of the renewables segment of URC's SURE (Sugar and Renewables) business; the renewables business alone was ~4% of consolidated topline and EBIT in 2024.

Home Credit, Puregold to launch credit card for store owners

Puregold Price Club, Inc. (PGOLD PM) and Home Credit Philippines jointly launched a Visa-powered credit card that mainly caters for sari-sari store owners but is also offered to regular Puregold customers. Applications can be made in participating Puregold branches with "immediate approval". Home Credit stated that several of their 12mn customers (as of Sep 2025) used their Home Credit-issued credit card to buy groceries, which led them to seek the partnership with Puregold. Puregold VP for Operations Antonio delos Santos stated that there are close to 1mn Aling Puring members (Puregold's loyalty and membership program for business owners). The card will provide a credit limit of P20,000 to P100,000 with up to 45 days of interest-free purchases on a P200 monthly fee (waived for the first three months). Perks include a 5% rebate on purchases (up to P500 within first 30 days), card protection insurance for 120% of the outstanding balance, P500 daily hospital cash benefit, and 24/7 telemedicine services. Home Credit has 12mn customers as of Sep 2025.

Po family injects capital through parent holding firm of Century Pacific Food, Shakey's Pizza

The Po family injected capital into its holding company, Century Pacific Group Inc., through Zephryn Holdings which is reported to be owned by the Po family. The company elaborated that the exercise was an "internal restructuring at the holdings level." Zephryn Holdings, an entity under the Po family, secured indirect ownership in Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM) and Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM) at 29.9% and 29.15%, respectively, through a 45.7% stake acquired via a new redeemable preferred share issuance in the Po family’s holding company, Century Pacific Group Inc (CPGI). The transaction made Zephryn Holdings, a Po family vehicle, a new beneficial owner of CNPF—no listed shares were sold, and the family’s overall ownership remained unchanged following the internal restructuring.

Monetary Board member expects one more 25bp cut in December

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board member Benjamin Diokno stated in an interview that he expects another 25 bps cut [to 4.50%] by the next meeting in Dec with more rate cuts possible "maybe sometime next year." Diokno highlighted that the economy may "slow down a bit" given the corruption controversy. The gov't's GDP growth target this year is 5.5-6.5%. 1H25 GDP growth was at 5.4% with 3Q GDP to be released on Nov 7. Regis expects GDP growth to be 5.5% this year. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.5%.

BSP releases statement on recent peso depreciation

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) stated that they will "allow the exchange rate to be determined by market forces" as the USD-PHP rate closed at a record low, P59.13 by end of day, breaching the P59 level for the first time. The central bank also emphasized that intervention will be done "to dampen inflationary swings" in the exchange rate over time and not to address day-to-day volatility. Recent peso depreciation was said to reflect market concerns over dampened economic growth prospects due to the infra spending scandal as well as expectations of possible further easing by the BSP. Recall that the BSP against expectations cut earlier than expected in Oct and guided for another cut 25bp in Dec, with further cuts possible in 2026.

Colliers: Office and residential net take-up improve in 3Q25

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy remained flat QoQ at 20% in 3Q25. Makati CBD vacancy was flat QoQ at 8.2%. BGC vacancy fell c. 2ppt QoQ to 12.4%. Net office take-up rose 75% QoQ to 105,000sqm. This was largely due to higher take-up from traditional tenants as well as fewer POGO closures. Office rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ. Residential vacancy in Metro Manila was also relatively flat QoQ at 25%. Net take-up of Metro Manila condominium units doubled QoQ and YoY to 5,800 units, the highest level in eight quarters. The improvement in net take-up was driven by stronger take-up of mid-income units as well as lower cancellations. Colliers said that aggressive promotions (such rent-to-own schemes of up to 10 years) and cash discounts of up to 60% on select ready-for-occupancy units continued to entice buyers. On the other hand, Colliers Head of Research Joey Bondoc said that “…the flood control mess has had some impact on the more expensive condo segments – luxury and ultra luxury…”

US Senator introduces bill to penalize international outsourcing

In September 2025, US Senator Bernie Moreno (Republican from Ohio) filed the Halting International Relocation of Employment (HIRE) Bill. The bill would 1) prohibit outsourcing payments from being tax-deductive, and 2) impose a 25% excise tax on outsourcing payments. The IT-BPO Association previously estimated that outsourcing work to the Philippines (from the US) could result in cost savings of 70%-80%. If the HIRE Bill were to become law, the cost savings could drop to 60%-70%. About 70% of the Philippine BPO industry caters to North America. About 70% of BPO revenue is from voice operations. BPO firms account for 60%-80% of the occupied office space of the developers we cover.

President Marcos signs Anti-POGO Act of 2025

President Marcos signed the Anti-POGO Act of 2025. Offshore gaming operations are prohibited while work permits and visas issued to people involved in such activities are revoked. President Marcos announced a POGO ban in July 2024 and signed an Executive Order pertaining to this in November 2024. The POGO ban became effective in January 2025. Thus, there should be no impact from the signing of this law. Future administrations will not be able to legalize offshore gaming.
