REGIS REPORT

25 July 2025

The PSEi rallied this week, +1.74% WoW to 6413, as local shares tracked gains across the broader region, helped by a sense of relief from the announcement of trade agreements between US and several countries including the Philippines. In his meeting with President Marcos earlier in the week, US President Trump announced that tariffs on imports from the Philippines will be slightly reduced from 20% to 19% (although still higher than the initial 17% when tariffs were first announced). While not a huge change per se, it does appear to have helped sentiment on the ground, especially after last week’s SM/SMPH placement-driven selloff, insofar as it removes any uncertainty over trade moving forward.

Amid the relief on global trade, index heavyweight ICT touched a new all-time high during the week and ended +5.02% WoW. There was also news about the company’s acquisition of an additional stake in a shipyard in Brazil to support its expansion plans in the country, which in our view may improve margins of its Rio port. With the recent rise, note that at current levels ICT’s market cap is now less than 8% away from the coveted one trillion Peso mark.

The best performing stock in the benchmark this week was PGOLD +12.18% WoW, outperforming the broader market for the second week in a row and extending YTD gains to +32.9% - now best performing in the benchmark. Sentiment on the name appears to be boosted by optimism over upcoming 2Q results, especially considering the typical boost on consumer demand during election period. The rest of the consumer space was mostly higher as well with URC +2.61% (and its parent JGS +6.72%), JFC +2.19% and CNPF +0.54% - although one visible laggard was MONDE -0.13%.

Meanwhile, outside the main index PLUS +19.47% WoW partially recovered from last week’s hefty slump (-40%) and was once again the most actively traded stock on the board outside of blocks. Investors continue to anticipate the upcoming state of the nation address by President Marcos on Monday, July 28, where he is expected to make an announcement amid the growing criticism of the online gambling industry. However, another important consideration about PLUS is that it remains a candidate for a potential inclusion in the PSEi in the upcoming August rebalancing. Note that there is no fixed date for PSEi review announcement and effectivity of changes but there is a growing sense that any decision by the exchange may be somehow be influenced by what’s announced by President Marcos during his SONA and as such will likely come after the president’s address on Monday.  

Meanwhile BLOOM, which has mostly followed the direction of PLUS over the recent weeks amid the aforementioned hot topic of potential restrictions or even a complete ban on online gambling, notably trailed behind this time, -0.22% WoW. This is potentially due to investors’ attention now shifting towards the upcoming PSE review where BLOOM remains a top candidate for an imminent deletion. Another headwind for the company however is that the recent PAGCOR data on gaming revenues suggests continued weakness on physical casinos in 2Q. Using 1H data from PAGCOR, we derived that 2Q GGR of licensed casinos fell by -11% QoQ to P44.1b in 2Q (1H down 6% YoY to P93.6b) – which could be negative signal for BLOOM’s upcoming quarterly results. In contrast, 2Q GGR of bingo and e-games further surged +25% QoQ to P63b.

Average daily activity this week eased to $120m, -30% WoW, but note that this is coming from a high base last week due to the almost $220m combined placements on SM and SMPH – both stocks managed to recover this week, +3.11% and +0.65%, respectively. There was however an interesting block reflected in the market this week: MER $68m worth at 90/share vs its current levels of 533.5/share. Note that this reflects SMC’s acquisition of a 3.8% stake in MER which goes back to as far as 2008 but encountered legal issues when the seller, state-owned Landbank, supposedly rescinded the deal. The subsequent court case was ruled in favor of San Miguel only last year. MER was one of the few laggards this week -1.93% WoW as investors contemplate on what SMC might do with their stake moving forward (note that it’s not enough for a board seat), while SMC -1.47%.

Bank of the Philippine Islands - Credit costs jump to c75bps in 2Q25, blunting strong loans, NIMs
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Adjusting estimates, maintaining TP and Buy call

NPAT for 1H25 of P33.0bn grew 8% YoY, lagging our estimates, but in[1]line with consensus. Loans accelerated to 14% YoY, with consumer and MSMEs surging 27% YoY, driving NIMs to expand 18bps QoQ and 34bps YoY by the bank’s estimate. PPOP was up 16% in 1H25, in line with our forecasts. However, the consumer/MSME push also saw credit costs jump close to 75bps in 2Q25, above management’s previous guidance of 55-60bps for FY25E. We reduce our FY25-27E NPAT estimates by 1-3.5% to adjust for higher provisions. However, our target price of P160/sh, and our Buy recommendation on the stock remain.

Consumer/MSME loans surge 27%, NIMs up 34bps YoY; PPOP +19% in 2Q25

BPI grew gross loans 14.1% YoY in 2Q25, faster than the 13% clocked in 1Q25, driven by a continued 26.9% surge in its consumer/MSME book, which now stands at 30% of total loans (was 27% at end-2024). Institutional loans grew a decent 9.4% YoY. The improved loan mix – together with the 450bps of cumulative RRR cuts since 4Q24 – pushed NIMs up by 18bps QoQ and 34bps YoY despite the continued policy interest rate cuts. Opex was kept in check (+11% YoY), taking PPOP up 19% YoY in 2Q25, in line with our estimates.

Credit costs of c75bps in 2Q25 exceed previous guidance

However, 2Q provision expense nearly tripled to P4.25bn from an admittedly low base in 2024. However, we estimate credit cost at c75bps in 2Q alone - vs the bank’s previous guidance of 55-60bps for FY25E – coming from the “business banking” (SME), personal loan, and microfinance portfolios. Management now expects credit costs to stay elevated, though it believes its aggressive push into consumer/MSMEs will still result in positive risk-adjusted returns.

Estimates adjusted down slightly; keeping P160/sh target and Buy call

We reduce our FY25-27E forecasts by 1-3.5%, assuming slightly higher NIMs, but much higher provision expense. We maintain our P160/sh target price, based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g), with long-term ROE of 14.75%, COE of 12.0% and growth of 8.25%. Our target price for BPI translates to a 29% potential total return. Buy maintained. Key downside risks include higher NPLs from increasing unsecured lending, while intense competition could squeeze NIMs.

 
Philippine Autos - EV transition finally on its way
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

A narrowing field

We reiterate our BUY ratings on Ayala Corp (AC) and GT Capital (GTCAP), the two firms whose auto businesses are likely to become (or stay) dominant over the medium term. EV (electrified vehicles, including hybrids) adoption is already approaching its tipping point, and we expect it to accelerate further. The auto businesses of AC and GTCAP are, in our view, in the strongest position to lead the industry over the medium-term. With shares of both trading at deep discounts to their underlying assets and at single-digit PERs on our refreshed numbers, AC and GTCAP offer, in our view, attractive value.

Hitting the EV inflection point, thanks to more hybrids

The transition to EVs in the country has picked up steam this year, with EVs likely accounting for at least 10% of new car sales in 2Q25, up from only 5% in 2024 and just 1% in 2022. Hybrids are leading the charge, with this year’s substantial growth propelled in large part by AC Mobility, a subsidiary of Ayala Corp and the exclusive distributor of BYD in the Philippines.

Government action aiding EV adoption

EV adoption has been aided by regulatory changes from a government that’s eager to promote the EV transition. Chief among these changes are lower duties and excise taxes, and an exemption of EVs from the number coding scheme. The latter is a scheme which restricts vehicles from the road one day per week across key cities, including Metro Manila.

Ayala Corp: Strong position

AC subsidiary AC Mobility is, in our view, best placed to gain significant market share over the next three years, given its portfolio of brands that are well positioned for the EV shift. Meanwhile, AC’s initiatives to rationalize / divest underperforming assets, while not quite over, are visibly progressing with losses set to narrow.

GT Capital: Near-term margin headwinds at TMP more than priced in

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), majority owned by GT Capital, is well-armed to compete with its wide array of hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) and the breadth of its full-service dealer network. There could be a profit hiccup due to the imposition of new taxes for pickup trucks (13% of sales) this month, but we expect Toyota to remain the leading auto brand over the medium term.

Valuation, risks

At current levels, AC and GTCAP respectively trade at 29% and 42% discounts to market-based NAVs, much wider than historical levels. Key downside risks relate to macroeconomic, regulatory/political risks, competition, and value-dilutive investments.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI takes control of empty shipyard in Rio, Brazil for expansion

ICTSI acquired an additional 26% stake in FII Inahuma, a company that has perpetual rights in a 32-ha inactive shipyard adjacent to its port in Rio, Brazil. The valuation of the deal was not disclosed but it raises ICTSI’s ownership to 73%, after acquiring a 47% stake in April. This will support the company’s planned expansion in Rio.

We understand that ICTSI has been renting the property prior to this acquisition so it may also improve profit margins of its port in Rio. Regis estimates that ICTSI Rio accounts for 2% of consolidated volumes and 3% of revenues of the company.

San Miguel acquires 3.8% of Meralco after court win

San Miguel’s wholly-owned unit, SMC Global Power Hldgs. Corp. (SMGP), acquired a 3.8% stake in Meralco for P3.9bn. SMC purchased 43.23mn MER shares from state-owned Land Bank of the Philippines for P90/sh. The acquisition stemmed from a purchase agreement signed in Dec 2008 by both parties, involving 46.6mn MER shares for P90/sh – where Land Bank rescinded, which prompted SMGP to sue for damages. Resolutions from the Sandiganbayan in June 2024 and the Supreme Court in April 2024 favoring SMGP paved the way for the completion of the deal. At current level, SMC’s stake in MER is worth P23.6bn

Maya maintains positive profits in 1H25; online gaming at 10% of enterprise revenues

Maya chairman Manuel Pangilinan said that the company was “doing very well” and showed “good profitability” in 1H25. Recall that Maya recorded a profit of P300mn in 1Q25, a turnaround from a net loss of P400mn in the same period a year ago. Mr. Pangilinan previously said that the company’s good start may enable it to exceed P1bn in earnings by end of year. The company clarified that ~10% of enterprise revenue is from online gambling. PLDT owns 37.7% of Maya Innovations.

First Gen looking to invest more in geothermal facilities next year

First Gen is looking to invest more in its geothermal portfolio next year to upgrade existing older facilities and constructing newer facilities, according to First Gen President and Chief Operating Officer Francis Giles B. Puno. Over the past two years or so, FGEN has been investing heavily in drilling activities.

For context, FGEN, through Energy Development Corp. (EDC), currently owns and operates 13 geothermal plans with a combined capacity of 1,200 MW. First Gen is aiming to expand its renewable energy portfolio to 13 gigawatts (GW) by 2030. As of end-2024, FGEN’s installed capacity of 3,692 MW.

First Gen secures P15bn loan for Casecnan

Fresh River Lakes Corp. (FRLC), the operator of the 165-megawatt (MW) Casecnan hydroelectric power plant (HEPP) and a wholly-owned unit of First Gen (FGEN PM), has secured a P15bn loan with BDO, BPI, and RCBC. The loan will be used to pay off the P10bn loan acquired by the parent to finance the Casecnan acquisition and for general corporate purposes. As of end-March 2025, FGEN’s consolidated net debt stood at US$1.4bn, with net debt-to-equity at 0.40x.

SEC approves IPO of Clark integrated resort operator Hann Resorts

The SEC approved the IPO of Hann Holdings, which operates Hann Resorts, an integrated casino resort in Clark: 500mn primary shares at up to P23.60/share (primary offer of P11.8bn) plus an overallotment option of 50mn shares for a maximum offer size of P13bn. Including the overallotment option – the IPO shares would represent 22% of expanded shares outstanding. At the maximum price, the market cap would be P59bn. The company recorded net profit of P1.7bn 2024 which implies 34.7X FY24 P/E at the maximum offer price.

Hann Resorts, which is located in Clark, has 240 gaming tables and 1,230 slots/ETG – for context, Solaire Entertainment City has about 350 gaming tables and 2,700 slots/ETG. The IR includes hotel brands such as Swissotel and Clark Marriott. The company is developing Hann Reserve, a 450-hectare mountain resort in New Clark City which will feature three PGA affiliated championship golf courses, luxury hotels, convention facilities, luxury residences, and an international school.

In 1Q25, Hann generated P3.4bn in GGR, representing 48% of Clark’s gaming market. For context, 1Q25 GGR of the NCR integrated resorts is as follows:

Solaire Entertainment City – P12.1bn
Newport Resorts – P7.9bn
Okada Manila – P7.8bn
City of Dreams Manila – P6.3bn
Solaire North – P4.6bn

GGR of licensed casinos falls 11% QoQ in 2Q25, e-games likely grew over 20% QoQ

PAGCOR reported that GGR of licenses casinos hit P93.36bn in 1H25 (down 6% YoY). This implies that 2Q25 GGR fell 11% QoQ and YoY to P44.1bn.

2Q25 GGR of Okada Manila fell 9% QoQ and 20% YoY to P7.1bn. 2Q25 gaming revenue share of Belle Corp (BEL PM) fell 22% QoQ and 37% YoY to P339mn.

PAGCOR also said that total industry GGR in 1H25 stood at P215bn (up 25% YoY). From this, Regis  estimate that 2Q25 GGR of bingo and e-games likely grew about 25% QoQ and 75% YoY to c. P63bn.

PLUS is the market leader in e-games, with market share of c. 45% as of 1Q25.

Government posts P242bn budget deficit in June; P766bn deficit as of 1H25

The government recorded a budget deficit of P242bn in June, which is wider both by 16% YoY and 67% MoM. This brings the 6M25 deficit to P766bn, 25% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was stated to have "remained relatively within target" at P766bn as the gov't continues to ramp up public spending to support economic growth. The wider deficit results from the weaker than expected revenues especially as customs collections continue to lag behind the full-year target.

Revenue collections in June increased by 4% YoY, with tax revenues up 12% but offset by a significant decline in non-tax revenues of 43%. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections climbed 16% YoY mainly due to higher corporate income tax collections as well as increased personal income tax and VAT collections.

Customs collections rose by a modest 3% given the impact of reduced tariffs (i.e., rice and EVs). Non-tax revenues dipped by 43% given the high base effect of one-off remittances in 2024. Expenditures grew 9% YoY in June on higher primary spending and interest payments.

Primary spending for the month was higher by 9%. The spending growth can be attributed to a ramp up in expenses following the election ban on public spending which ended early May after the midterm elections. Interest payments inched up 3% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt.

The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

Finance Secretary sees two more rate cuts this year

Department of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto guided that he expects 50bp more worth of cuts this year. This implies 100bp total rate cuts for 2025, bringing the key policy rate to 4.75% by year-end from the current 5.25%. For context, both Bloomberg median consensus and REGIS expect 75bp total worth of cuts to 5.00% this year.

Recto stated that even if the Fed remains steady with rates, it is still possible to proceed with monetary easing but it would depend on external developments. He added that the next cut could happen as early as next meeting. Note that the next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled for Aug 28 (third out of six this year).

Trump cuts tariffs on PH imports to 19%; PH removes tariffs on US-made cars

US President Donald Trump, Jr. reduced tariffs on imports from the Philippines to 19% from 20%. In exchange, President Marcos, Jr. will remove tariffs on certain imports sourced from the US, such as automobiles. Currently, imports of US-made automobiles are charged a tariff of 30% (up to 10-seater) and 20% (buses and trucks). However, PH imports of US car brands (e.g., Ford) are already sourced from ASEAN nations, which already enjoy zero duty. Imports of electric vehicles from the US (BEV and HEV) also enjoy zero tariffs today. President Marcos also mentioned that the Philippines agreed to buy more products (e.g., soy, wheat, and medicine) from the US, but details will still need to be worked out.

DepDev Secretary sees 2Q GDP growth boosted by consumption

Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev) Secretary Arsenio M. Balisacan stated that 2Q economic growth may have benefited from the lag effects of interest rate cuts and benign inflation. He elaborated that the impact of easing and slower inflation likely boosted domestic demand despite continued uncertainties (i.e., tariffs) in the global economy. Balisacan also highlighted the gov't's continued efforts on pushing reforms to improve the country's competitiveness. Consensus and REGIS expectations for 2025 GDP growth are at 5.5%.

SEC mulls tiered public float for IPOs

The Securities and Exchange Commission is considering using a tiered approach for the minimum public float requirement of companies that eye to list on the PSE. SEC Chairperson Francisco Ed. Lim stated that the current 20% minimum public float requirement is a "one-size-fits-all" approach and does not take into account differing market capitalizations. Lim suggests using tiers depending on the company's market capitalization to decide the minimum float requirement, "the higher the market cap, the lower the percentage of free float." Previously, the SEC has allowed a lower initial public float of 15% through exemptive relief with conditions. Lim however stated he is not in favor of exemptive relief given that it is granted on a case-to-case basis while the tiered approach is more flexible and applicable to all.

The PSE targets six IPOs this year - only one has listed so far, Top Line Business Development Corp. (TOP PM). Key future listings slated to take place in 2025 include GCash, Maynilad Water Services, Inc., and Hann Holdings, Inc.

PH banking system total deposits rise 5% YoY in 1Q25; digital bank deposits surge 33% to surpass P100bn mark

The Philippine banking system's total deposits increased by 5% YoY to P20.2trn as of 1Q25 from P19.1trn in the same period last year. Universal and commercial banks held P18.9trn in deposits as of end-March, up 5% YoY from P17.9trn. Deposits from thrift banks rose 5% YoY to P840bn. Rural and cooperative banks' deposits jumped by 19% to P358bn. Total deposits of digital banks surged 33% YoY to P102.3bn from P76.8bn, breaching the P100bn level for the first time. Latest central bank data states that 65% of the Filipino adult population have a bank account as of 2022.

Digital payments rise 52% YoY in June

Payments made digitally over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) rose by 52% YoY to P2trn in June, accelerating from the 38% pace in May and bringing the 6M25 transaction value to P11.1trn, up 39% YoY. Payments through InstaPay, which handles real-time payments of up to P50k, rose 58% YoY to P916.6bn in June. By volume, InstaPay payments surged 227% YoY, even faster than the 217% pace in May.

Large-ticket payment transactions that go through PESONet, climbed 47% YoY to P1.1trn in May, accelerating from the 27% growth seen in May. In terms of volume, PESONet transactions increased by 21% YoY to 9.6mn, faster than the 14% growth in May.

President Marcos says that online gambling “destroys” many families

In his vlog, President Marcos said that many Filipino families are being destroyed by online gambling. Meanwhile, Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin said the government is still studying the implications of banning online gambling. Separately, Senator Loren Legarda filed a bill to ban online gambling. Recall that Senators Raffy Tulfo, Joel Villanueva, Alan Peter Cayetano, Pia Cayetano, Bato dela Rosa, Bong Go, and Miguel Zubiri previously filed their own bills banning online gambling; while Senators Risa Hontiveros and Sherwin Gatchalian filed bills to restrict online gambling.

BOP shifts to US$226mn surplus in June; GIR settles at US$106.0bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$226mn, which marks a turnaround from both the US$155bn deficit in June 2024 and US$298mn deficit in May 2025. The surplus reflected the national gov't's foreign currency deposits with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) and income from BSP investments. This brings the YT-June deficit at US$5.6bn, reversing from the US$1.4bn surplus in the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade gap, higher net outflows in the financial account, and frontloaded external debt repayments

The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (or -1.3% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments.

Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-June stood at US$106.0bn, up from the $105.2bn in end-May. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025.

New ERC chair lists five focus areas for agency

In a radio interview, newly appointed Chairman of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), Francis Saturnino Juan, listed his top priorities once he assumes office on Aug 8:

Re-review of the performance-based rate-setting methodology for private distribution utilities, such as Meralco (MER PM)

Revisit the approval process of Power Supply Agreements that pass through the Competitive Selection Process to fast track

Review the current yardstick regulation used for setting the on-grid electric cooperatives’ (ECs) rates and allow individual rate applications to help distressed ECs earn sufficient revenues

Consider allowing utilities, such as the National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) to start implementing their proposed capex, after three months from submission

Consider the possibility of deeming as approved, all certificates of compliance (COC) applications after 30 days from filing

Mr. Juan aims to address the backlog at ERC by streamlining processes and simplifying requirements.
