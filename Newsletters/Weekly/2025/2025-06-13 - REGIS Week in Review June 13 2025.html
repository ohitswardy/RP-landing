REGIS REPORT

13 June 2025

The market rose 0.3% week on week, buoyed by follow through buying post a favorable April inflation print.   This has increased the chances of a June rate cut by the BSP / Monetary Board, which has been helped too be a weak USD / strong Peso.

That said, saw mixed performance within sectors and within the market as a whole.  This reflects  a bit of wait and see on few things, such as the July 9 tariff deadline by the US on most trading counterparties.  There likewise has been some tentativeness ahead of the FTSE rebal on 20 June; large domestic investors in particular are very conscious of such rebalancing events and tend to trade on the actual liquidity event.

The action meantime was more pronounced on small and mid cap names with specific stories.   BLOOM rose another 22% this week on optimism over its new online e-games/casino division that finally launched this week.  The stock has more than doubled from its April low on the back of this.    Punters pushed the stock further as a potential new PSE rule on index composition could see BLOOM being retained in the PSEi / PCOMP.  The base case is that it will be removed by August, whereas it fails on a couple of areas using the unchanged PSE index composition criteria.

Another big gainers was AGI ( +14.7%) as it extended its on-going buyback program by another P2bn

Otherwise, stock performance within specific sectors was quite mixed and highlights further the lack of strong conviction overall.  For instance,  ALI rose 1.9% within the property sector but SMPH fell 1.7%.    Within the banks,  BDO rose 2.1% but BPI fell 0.9% alongside MBT.   Conglos saw the same story:  SM rose 2.8%, but AC was flat , GTCAP was off 0.3%

Consumer plays were generally flat to down:  JFC was flat, MONDE -0.3% CNPF fell 0.9%.   URC though was the stand-out, falling 5.9% on worries that a still worsening pest infestation in a key sugar producing province will eventually impact its sugar business. The latter is significant at roughly 15%-25% of EBIT depending on the year.

There were other big movers, owing to index changes.  JGS fell 7.2% ahead of a big FTSE rebal on 20 June that will result in big outflow.   CBC fell 9% as it is likely to be excluded from the PSEi / PCOMP if indeed the PSE adopts a proposed new methodology by August.

Meantime, the PHP was generally just trading at the P55.80 area but saw a weakening to P56.21 (down 1% wow) late in the week as oil rose over 6% following the Israel strike  versus Iran.   The Philippines is heavily dependent on oil and energy from overseas and a spike on the prices of those will put pressure once again on the trade accounts.

Philippine Strategy - Massive wage hike looms? Assessing the likelihood and potential impact
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Lower House passes P200/day wage hike – 31% to 63% on current levels
On 5 June, the Lower House passed House Bill 11376, calling for a P200/day hike in minimum wages across the country. Typically, wages are set by the 18 regional wage boards, taking into account local costs of living etc. Current minim wages range from a high of P645 (c$11.50) in Metro Manila (aka National Capital Region,
or NCR), to as low as P316 (c$5.65) in parts of Mindanao. 

A P200/day hike would raise minimum wages 31% to 63%, with a simple average of about 44%. Such a  uniform increase would thus have a disproportionately
larger impact on poorer, less developed areas, for good or ill.

The magnitude of the proposed increase is also near-unprecedented. The last nationwide wage hike was granted in 1989. From 1989-2000, the NCR minimum
wage rose by an average of 10%. In the last 10 years, the CAGR has slowed to 3.3%. Wages have tended to track inflation, with a lag.

Good for consumption spend, hard to gauge inflation impact
Notionally, the pay rises would be positive for  consumption spending, but bad for inflation. However, the impact is difficult to quantify. The tax bureau reported 23.6mn individual “compensation income” taxpayers in 2024. Meanwhile, the statistics agency claims 48.7mn employed individuals (as of April
2025), but that only 5.4mn were formally employed in 2022. There’s little reliable data on how many are paid the legal minimum and what median wages are.

Consumer companies could be hit hard; Banks a surprise second
Labor costs – direct employee compensation, plus contracted services – vary widely across companies we cover and follow closely.

Unsurprisingly, consumer companies are more labor intensive; we calculate that in 2024, labor costs accounted for 50-70% of EBITDA, with JFC an outlier at 175%. Banks are high on the scale, too, with labor costs ranging from 30-55% of PPOP. The impact to pre-tax profit is even more variable, as is to be expected. While any minimum wage hike will not affect a company’s entire employee pool, the
analysis shows a firm’s sensitivity to overall wage pressure.

Will it pass? Posturing, or a serious populist push?
It is tempting to dismiss the Lower House move as mere political theater. The current 19th Congress ends on 13 June. The bill still needs to go to the bicameral committee to be resolved against a similar 2024 Senate bill calling for a smaller P100/day wage hike. If they don’t make the 13 June deadline, both bills are dead.

We believe the risk is non-zero, though. A lot can happen  in 3-4 days. And, even should the bills miss the deadline, they could still be revived in the 20th Congress. As we previously reported, the popularity of President Marcos, Jr has collapsed, with his coalition faring poorly in the May midterms. The government has already launched populist policies such as rice subsidies and food vouchers.  According to a PulseAsia survey, the two most pressing concerns for Filipinos are a) controlling inflation, and b) higher worker pay.

The survey results will not be lost on those with an eye to the 2028 Presidential elections. During the midterm campaign, nearly every one of the 12 incoming
Senators expressed support for some version of a wage hike policy.

JFC acquires South Korean fried chicken chain, Norang Tongdak

Jollibee Foods Corp has been named as the preferred buyer of Norang Tongdak, in a deal estimated at KRW140bn (US$100mn or ~P5.7bn).

Norang Tongdak is a South Korean fried chicken chain that was founded back in 2009. Its signature dish is Norang Chicken which is made with a special batter mix using turmeric. JFC signed an MOU last Tuesday with Q Capital Partners and Corstone Asia to acquire the company from the two Korean private equity firms. Corstone and Q Capital acquired Norang Tongdak for KRW70bn back in 2020.

In 2024, the company posted revenue of KRW106.7bn (+10% YoY), operating profit of KRW12.7bn (+10% YoY), and net profit of KRW10.3bn. Norang Tongdak’s revenue and EBIT (for FY24) are ~2% and ~3% of JFC’s.

The KRW140bn acquisition price implies a PE ratio of 13.6x on Norang's 2024 net profit. Fried chicken chain Kyochon is on 8x 2025E PER; Korean fast-food chains trade at a median of 6.7x.

Norang Tongdak has over 700 stores, up from 400 back in 2020. Some notable competitors would be bb.q chicken which has 3,500 stores, BHC Chicken 2,200, and Kyochon 1,400.

JFC's current footprint in Korea is 2,700 Compose Coffee stores and around 200 CBTL stores.

Bloomberry launches new online gaming platform

Bloomberry soft-launched the browser version of MegaFUNalo (www.megafunalo.com) on 8 June 2025, offering users a mix of casino and arcade games. The mobile app is scheduled to launch in the coming weeks.

MegaFUNalo is not yet available on the GCash app. This could limit MegaFUNalo’s customer base (for now) as DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) said that about 80% of its clients access its platform through Gcash. Accounts require a minimum deposit of P40, compared to the P20 minimum of DigiPlus. Users can fund their accounts via UnionBank, BPI, QRPh, Maya, GCash, and GrabPay. MegaFUNalo is running several promotional offers to entice users, including free bets on signup, daily login rewards, and a 100% match on the first deposit.

Bloomberry plans to spend P2bn-P4bn this year on advertising and promotions. This includes the aforementioned rebates, daily login rewards, etc.

In 2024, DigiPlus spent P13bn on advertising and promotions, equivalent to 17% of revenue. Development of Bloomberry’s platform was outsourced to a third-party vendor under a revenue-sharing agreement.

1Q25 GGR of electronic games (and Bingo operations) was flat QoQ at P51.4bn. 1Q25 GGR of DigiPlus was similarly flat QoQ at P23bn. Bloomberry’s 1Q25 GGR grew 4% QoQ to P16.8bn. Alliance Global and Hann Holdings are set to launch their own online gaming platforms later this year. Hann Holdings plans to partner with a foreign operator for its online platform.

Emperador management shuffle; eyes whisky rebound in 2026

Emperador announced that Andrew Tan will be the chairman emeritus as current president and CEO Winston Co is set to be the new chairman and president.  Glenn Manlapaz, head of Emperador's international division will be replacing Co as the CEO.

For 2025, the company is expecting a global slowdown in demand for whisky, while it also faces more pressure from competition in key whisky markets in North America, China, and Europe. 

The implementation of the blanket 10% tariff by the US is expected to hamper demand given that the United States represents 10-12% of EMI's whisky business. Co elaborated that they are facing challenges on supplier and distributor negotiations on keeping the shelf price of their products as it is. However, the company believes that the whisky segment will eventually return to growth with a rebound starting 2026.  This is expected to be primarily driven by the attractive long-term prospects of Scotch whisky and single malt whisky as well as the expansion of its Dalmore and Invergordon distilleries in Scotland.

P4bn in capex will be allocated this year, with most of the amount to fund the expansion which is expected to significantly increase both facilities' production capacity.

DENR approves Semirara’s ECC, increases annual production limit to 20mn MT

The DENR has granted Semirara’s request to amend its environmental compliance certificate (ECC), which includes the following:

    • Increase in annual production limit to 20mn MT from 16mn MT from 2025-2027
    • Expanded coverage area of 5,222 hectares (from 4,370 hectares), allowing Semirara to operate the new Acacia pit once reserves in the Narra pit are depleted

Regis estimates the increase in production limit to 20mn MT could increase SCC’s FY25E profits and FCF by 27% and 16%, respectively, holding other things constant.

Alliance Global upsizes share buyback program

Alliance Global increased the size of its buyback program by an additional P2bn. The company has spent P8.68bn of its existing P9bn buyback program. The program was also extended by one year to 31 December 2026. P2bn represents 2.7% of AGI’s current market cap.

DITO eyeing positive profits by 2028

DITO CME President and COO Donald Lim said that the company is targeting to reach positive profitability by 2028, citing that the company already has more than 14mn regular subscribers and has been hitting commitments in terms of penetration and towers built. 19th Congress adjourns without passing minimum wage hike bill

Both the House and Senate adjourned on Wednesday, ending the third regular session without either chamber adopting the other's bill or presenting a bicameral conference committee report after not being able to agree on the amount of increase.

Rizal Rep. Juan Fidel Nograles, chair of the House committee on labor and employment, pushed for a bicameral conference committee for transparent discussions on the minimum wage hike. However, Sen. Joel Villanueva urged the House to adopt the Senate's P100 wage increase version, which would bypass the need for a bicameral conference, so it can be signed by the President. Villanueva expressed "strong reservations" against the House's proposed P200 hike, citing potential "adverse economic and social impacts" like increased unemployment and higher consumer prices. Admin’s economic managers also warned against the bill stating that a P200 increase in wages could raise inflation by about 2%, while a P100 may add 0.7%.

Under the Congress’ rules, when there are different provisions in similar bills approved by the Senate and the House, a bicameral conference committee shall be convened, which will come up with a final version of the proposed measure. The final version, in a bicameral conference committee report, must be separately ratified by the House and Senate before being sent to the President for approval.

Bills last only until the end of the congress they are filed in, but the bills that are not passed into law can be refiled after the next congress convenes.

Senate sends VP Duterte impeachment case back to House

The Senate voted 18-5 with no abstentions, to remand the articles of impeachment against Vice President Sara Duterte back to the House of Representatives. This followed a failed proposal to outright dismiss the articles of impeachment, which was proposed by Senator Dela Rosa and backed by other Duterte allied senators. The only five who voted against the motion to remand were Senators Pimentel, Hontiveros, Binay, Poe, and Gatchalian.

Senator Alan Peter Cayetano moved to amend Senator Dela Rosa's motion—to instead return the articles of impeachment to the House, without dismissing or terminating the case until a) the House can certify its non-violation of the Constitution; b) the House of the 20th Congress communicates to the Senate that it is willing and ready to pursue the impeachment complaint against the VP. The 20th Congress will convene in July. At least 16 votes (two thirds majority) are needed to impeach VP Sara Duterte, should the trial still proceed.

PH Dollar reserves at $105.5bn end-May

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-May inched up 0.1% MoM to US$105.5bn from US$105.3bn at end-Apr. The increase primarily reflected the upward valuations in BSP's gold holdings, net income from BSP's investments abroad, and net foreign exchange deposits of the national gov't with the BSP. The level of dollar reserves by end-May was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and is also equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments.  Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$110bn by end-2025.

IT-BPO Association expects sector revenue to hit US$40bn this year

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it would hit its US$40bn revenue target this year. This represents c. 5% growth from US$38bn in 2024. It is also projecting the sector to employ 1.9mn Filipinos by the end of the year. The sector had 1.82mn employees at the end of 2024.

BPO firms account for 60%-80% of the occupied office space of the developers under Regis coverage.

Sugar pest outbreak hits 842-hecatres in Negros; threatening 65% of PH sugar output

According to the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA), the red-striped soft scale insects (RSSI) infestation in Negros reached 842 ha as of June 6, nearly a tenfold rise from 87 ha on May 22, threatening plantations that supply 65% of national sugar output.

The Department of Agriculture has released P10 billion for pesticide procurement to support containment efforts. The SRA will bar unapproved movement of planting materials to or from Negros requiring farmers and research facilities to obtain clearance via the Bureau of Plant Industry. Researchers have identified a natural parasite for long-term control, but the SRA is prioritizing chemical spraying as the immediate response. RSSI can slash crop yields by up to 50%.

Asset quality broadly flat to slightly worse MoM in April

NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) inched up 1bp to 3.56%, with gross NPLs up 0.6% MoM to P519bn. Past Due (PD) ratio increased 4bps MoM to 4.49% from 4.45% in March, as the Peso value of PDs climbed 1.1% MoM to P653bn. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio, meanwhile, improved 1bp MoM to 2.14%, as nominal RLs were up just 0.1% MoM. NPL Cover was steady, up 5bps to 95.10% from 95.05% in March. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) slowed to 11.2% YoY, slower than the 12.2% pace in March, and posting the slowest YoY figure since October 2024. Loans were up just 0.3% MoM. Loan to deposit ratio (LDR) jumped to 73.7% from 72.0% in March, and much higher than the 68.9% recorded in April 2024.

 

The MoM deterioration was driven largely by the Digibanks, which saw NPL ratio jump 40bps MoM to 10.16%, and PD ratios surge 174bps MoM to 19.07%. That said, both ratios are already significantly lower than where they were in April 2024 (see table below). For Thrift Banks (TBs), NPL ratio was down 10bps MoM, while PD rates jumped 9bps. This suggests to us that there may have been some write-offs in April.

SEC chief eyes GOCCs to list in the PSE

Francisco Ed. Lim, new chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), hopes to encourage Philippine gov't-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs) to list on the stock exchange to spur investor activity. Currently there are no GOCCs listed on the PSE. 

Lim, who was PSE president from 2004-2010, stated that he will check the implementation of laws that require the public listing of companies that are availing of gov't incentives.  Republic Act 11534 or the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act mandates that in order for certain incentives to be granted, companies must either list on the PSE or have committed to a specified timeframe to do so. Lim said that this provision has not been fully implemented as of late.

The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) previously mentioned in a report that state-run banks such as Land Bank of the Philippines and Development Bank of the Philippines as potential candidates for listing.

 

Congress approves Bill allowing 99-year land leases to foreigners

The Senate and House of Representatives approved a bicameral conference committee report extending the land lease term to foreigners to a maximum of 99 years. The maximum of 99 years is in line with Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia. Currently, the Foreign Investors’ Long Term Lease Act allows foreigners to lease land for up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years (total of 75 years).

Foreigners will be allowed to sublease properties unless their contracts prohibit them from doing so. The Bill should now be transmitted to the Office of the President for signing (into law).

Department of Energy awards over 6,600 MW worth of RE projects under GEA-3

The Department of Energy has issued notices of awards to 12 projects that will supply ~6,600 MW of hydro and 30MW of geothermal capacities. Committed capacities are scheduled for installation between 2028 and 2035.

 The winning bidders under Green Energy Auction (GEA-3) are:

    • Pan Pacific Renewable Power Phils. Corp. – 2,000MW pumped-storage hydro project at P3.50/kwh and 300MW of impounding hydro at P4.50-P4.75/kwh
    • San Miguel’s (SMC PM) San Roque Hydropower – 1,850 MW of pumped-storage hydro projects, with tariffs ranging from P3.25/kwh to P3.73/kwh
    • Prime Infra’s Ahunan Power – 1,400 MW pumped storage hydro project at P5.46/kwh
    • Olympia Violago Water Power – 600 MW pumped storage hydro at P5.36/kwh
    • Coheco Badeo Corp. – 500 MW pumped storage hydro at P2.58/kwh
    • First Gen’s (FGEN PM) EDC – 9.3MW geothermal capacity at P5.11/kwh to P7.64/kwh and 21.6MW at P7.64/kwh

The GEA program aims to promote investments in renewable energy sources. GEA allows RE gencos to sell energy to the WESM at their winning (guaranteed) bid prices. Any difference between the bid price and the WESM price will be settled through the Feed-in-Tariff system, which is a full pass-through to end users.
