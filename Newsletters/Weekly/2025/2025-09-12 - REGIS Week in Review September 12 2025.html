REGIS REPORT

12 September 2025

The Philippines fell 0.65% this week to close at 6109.21, with no new catalysts on the ground. US and Asia stocks rallied as Fed cut speculation intensified, but the Phils market wavered to close out its fifth straight week of declines. Larger North Asia markets rose 3%-5% for the week.

Meanwhile, the ongoing controversy with regard to corruption at the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) involving a wide range of executives and legislators has drawn outrage. With the heat still on, it is expected that public spending will temporarily slow in the coming months and impact other parts of the economy.

Foreigners were sellers for US$1.7m, taking YTD outflows to US$727.8m.

Large cap market proxies SM, down-2.2%, and AC, down 4.6% ,weighed on the market, along with BPI which fell 3.2% this week as sentiment remained weak.

The steepest decliner in the index however was Converge, which stumbled 20.9%, as investors grappled with the potential impact of the new Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA). This new law lowers barriers to entry and could result in increased competition for the incumbents. Further concerns mounted that Starlink's potentially cheaper product offering (across the region) will accelerate the process.

Globe (-3.1%) and PLDT (-1.1%) fell as well though with milder declines. These two have already sunk in prior weeks on fears that KPA may attract new players too in the already mature and crowded mobile segment.

ICTSI (-1.3) consolidated this week. Mexico is imposing a tariff on China and other Asian countries, likely bowing to pressure from the US. ICT’s Manzanillo terminal services mainly Asia-related cargo and is of significant importance to ICTSI, contributing 25% of EBITDA. Mexico is ICTSI’s biggest contributor apart from Manila, which contributes about a quarter of EBITDA as well.

Consumer names corrected this week, led by URC (-3.6%) Jollibee (-3.2%) and Monde (-0.7%). Recent results in 2Q showed a recovery, albeit disappointing given the supposed boost coming from elections. Century Pacific (+8.1%) rallied hard however, on early indications that 3Q revenues are recovering better-than-thought.

Meanwhile, the top index mover was BDO, rising 3.45% this week, somewhat recovering after three straight weeks of drops.

Outside the benchmark, mining names sharply rallied once again with the sub-index up by another 3.7% this week, bringing the YTD gain to over 46%. Leading the way were gold exposures Apex Mining (+11.3%), OceanaGold (+7.3%) and Philex (+3.2%) as gold extended its record rally, rising by another 1.5% to a fresh high this week (and now nearly 39% YTD).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land’s latest high-end Makati project is now 30% sold

Ayala Land’s has presold P8bn worth of units in its high-end Makati residential project, Laurean (total value of units is P26bn). The units are priced at about P650,000/sqm. Note that the project has only been offered to select Ayala Land Premier clients and will be available for sale to the general public in October.

In 2Q25, Laurean accounted for P6bn of presales. Recall that residential reservation sales rose 11% QoQ (down 1% YoY) to P34.5bn in 2Q25 as premium sales fell 4% QoQ (down 9% YoY) to P19.9bn while core sales grew 39% QoQ (up 11% YoY) to P14.6bn.

SEC approves property-for-share swap between RLC and RCR

The SEC approved the property-for-share swap agreement between RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) and its sponsor, Robinsons Land. RCR will acquire 324,000sqm of malls from RLC in exchange for 3.83bn new RCR shares. 8 malls are in provincial areas while 1 mall is in Metro Manila: Dasmarinas, Starmills (Pampanga), General Trias, Cybergate Cebu, Tacloban, Malolos, Santiago, Tuguegarao, Magnolia.

RLC will subscribe to 3.83bn new RCR shares at P8/share (total of P30.67bn), while RCR’s shares outstanding will expand by 24%. The acquisition would expand RCR’s portfolio by 39% to 1.15mn sqm, 600k sqm or 52% is comprised of malls while the rest are offices. AUM will also grow by 27% to P145bn. We estimate that RLC’s ownership in RCR will increase from 57% to 66%.

Mexico plans to raise import tariffs on China, others to up to 50%

Mexico’s Economic Minister Marcelo Ebrard is proposing to raise tariffs on a multiple of goods (e.g. autos, textiles, steel) to up to 50% from 20% today. Tariffs will affect countries that do not have trade treaties with Mexico, which includes China, South Korea, India, Indonesia, Russia, Thailand, and Turkey. Congress, which is controlled by the current administration, will have to approve the proposal. ICTSI’s port in Mexico is estimated to generate ~30% of throughput from imports of Chinese goods (i.e. 48% of total throughput are imports, of which ~60% originate from China). We estimate the port in Mexico accounts for ~11% of volumes and ~25% of EBITDA.

Home Credit expects loan portfolio to hit P100bn this year

Home Credit Philippines (HCPH) Chief Marketing Officer Sheila Paul stated that they hope to hit P100bn (+35% YoY) in loan receivables within the year—driven by a growing customer base, expanding partner stores, manageable NPLs, and a boost from the Christmas season.

Loan receivables as of 6M25 amounted to P85bn, well above end-2024's P74bn. For one, smartphone financing is projected to reach P20bn this year, double from last year's P10bn. While other top-selling products are televisions, air conditioners, refrigerators, e-bikes, motorcycles, cellphone insurance.

The company had 12mn customers as of July, already exceeding the 11mn from October last year. Home Credit has 18,000 partner stores alongside 10,000 sales associate network which is expected to grow further.

Home Credit's NPL ratio remains below 10%, with Ms Paul noting that "… as long as we're able to manage it within that threshold, I think we are fine." Security Bank (SECB PM) has a 25% stake in Home Credit Philippines.

SM Prime to open new China mall in October

SM Prime will open its 9th mall in China next month: a 60,000sqm mall in Xiamen, Fujian Province. Then it will open its 10th China mall in 2027 which will be located in Fuzhou, Fujian Province. Steven Tan, who heads SM Prime’s mall division, said that the company would focus its China efforts on Fujian province. SM Prime currently has 88 malls in the Philippines and 8 malls in China. It has 9.5mn sqm of mall GFA in the Philippine and 1.7mn sqm in China.

Manila Water divests entire stake in Eastern Water

Manila Water disclosed that it sold its entire 18.72% stake in Thai-based Eastern Water (EASTW TB) to Mr. Bhavin Kunanderekwong for a total consideration of THB623mn (~P1.1bn). Note that the divestment effective price of THB0.37/sh is a large discount to EASTW’s market price of THB2.56/sh prior to the announcement of the transaction.

EASTW is one of MWC’s legacy assets (initial investment was THB5.3bn in 2018 or P8.8bn). Recall that MWC wrote down its investment to zero in 2024 after a major regulatory setback removed the company’s exclusive rights to supply water to Thailand’s eastern region. We thus anticipate MWC will book a paper gain from this sale.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Inflation rate accelerates to 1.5% in August

Headline inflation picked up to 1.5% in August from 0.9% in the previous month of July, higher than the consensus forecast of 1.2% but within the BSP's forecast range of 1.0-1.8% for August. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past six months.

The primary driver for the faster pace in inflation in Aug was food up 0.9% YoY, reversing from being down 0.2% YoY in July. Food was driven by sharp YoY price increases in vegetables and fish which were up 10% and 9.5%, respectively (vs -4.7% and 6.3% in July). But notably, rice prices are on their fourteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Sep 4), prices for local and imported well-milled rice range from P37-52/kg and P40-50/kg, respectively vs. P42-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 2.

Core inflation accelerated to 2.7% in August from 2.3% in Jul, driven by the surge in core food inflation to 3.9% from 2.8%; while core non-food inflation inched up to 2.1% from 2.0%.

This brings the YT-Aug headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting (5th out of 6 this year) will be on Oct 9. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%.

Government debt inches up to P17.56trn in end-July; exceeds year-end debt level projection

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.7% MoM to P17.56trn as of end-July. Domestic debt rose by 1.3% MoM to P12.11trn, while external debt increased by 2.6% MoM to P5.46trn. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.9% and 31.1% being external borrowings. The P17.56trn figure already exceeds the P17.36-trn full-year projection for 2025 by 1.15%. Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury said that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down."

The rising debt figure was driven by sustained borrowing needs given the 22% wider YoY fiscal deficit of P784bn as of 7M25. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 1H25 stood at 63.1% of GDP up from 60.7% in 4Q24. The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028.

PH Dollar reserves at $105.9bn end-August

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Aug increased 0.5% MoM to US$105.9bn from US$105.4bn at end-Jul. The increase primarily reflected the surge in global gold prices and income from BSP investments. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.4x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$104bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn.

Asset quality deteriorates in July - PDs up 10bps MoM

Banking sector asset quality ratios worsened across the board in July. (Note that we use loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP) which tend to distort the monthly numbers.) The ratios were hurt slightly by the mild 0.3% MoM increase in total loans (es-IBL/RRP). That said, all bad loan data was up even more MoM in nominal terms.

Most notably, Past Due (PD) ratios blipped up 10bps MoM to 4.60% from 4.50% in June, marking the highest level since October 2024 (4.66%). In PHP terms, PD loans jumped 2.5% MoM to P687.6bn. NPL ratio was up a much milder 2bps MoM to 3.58% from 3.56% the previous month, with nominal NPLs up 1.0% MoM to P535.4bn. The drop in NPL ratio could partly be attributed to a jump in Restructured Loans (RLs) ratio, up 11bps MoM to 2.20% from 2.09% in June. The PHP value of RLs surged 5.6% MoM to P329.6bn, or a P17.6bn increase over the June level.

Notable, too, was that the MoM asset quality slip was driven by the larger Universal and Commercial banks (UKBs). While Thrift Banks (TBs) saw slightly higher MoM increases, the Digibanks actually saw a sharp decline in PD and NPL ratios.

The data do not bode well for credit costs heading into 3Q25. Recall that provision expense for all banks we cover – and for many that we don’t – surprised to the upside in 2Q25

On a positive note, loan growth stayed elevated, up 12.1% YoY (ex-IBL/RRP), sustaining the pace from June. This is despite the MoM drop, as July historically tends to be a soft month.

Commercial banks’ loan growth 11.8% in July; M3 growth at 6.2%

Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.8% YoY in July, moderating from the 12.1% rate in June. Loans to productive activities increased 10.8% YoY, slowing down from the 11.1% in June due to decreased lending in key industries such as Manufacturing (-6.3% from -4.0%), trade (+8.5% from +9.7%), and information, communication (+8.5% from +9.6%). Consumer loans grew 23.6%, slightly slower than the 24.0% pace in June. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up to 6.2% in July, accelerating from the 5.9% revised growth in June.

Consumer loans jump 21% YoY in 2Q25; NPLs ease, but…

Consumer Loans for the Philippine banking system jumped 21.2% YoY in 2Q25 to P3.4trn, accelerating from the 18.1% YoY pace in 1Q25. That’s the fastest quarterly YoY growth rate clocked since 1Q 2015. Industry-wide, consumer loans now account for 22.3% of total loans, a new all-time high. Consumer NPL ratio stood at 5.33%, down 9bps QoQ from 5.42% in 1Q25, and down 49bps YoY from the 5.82% in 2Q24. However, we note that the NPL ratios for mortgages and auto loans ticked up QoQ.

 Mortgages grew 10.9% YoY to P1,152bn, slightly faster than the 10.8% pace in 1Q. Mortgage NPL ratio edged up 8bps QoQ, but was down 29bps YoY.

Motor Vehicle (MV) loans expanded 17.6% YoY to P656bn, slowing from the 18.5% in 1Q25. Of this, Auto loans were up 17.9% YoY (vs 19.4% in 1Q) and Motorcycle loans were up 13.8% (accelerating sharply from +3.7% in 1Q). MV NPLs were up 6bps QoQ to 4.99% though were down 73bps YoY.

Credit Card (CC) loans continued to surge 29.9% YoY to P1,036bn, marking the 12th consecutive quarter of >25% growth. This has put CC loans just shy of total mortgages as the largest category of consumer loans in the banking system. NPL ratio for CCs dipped 9bps QoQ to 4.51%, and down 5bps YoY.

Salary Loans jumped 37.0% YoY to P501bn, accelerating sharply from +15% in 1Q, with NPL ratio improving dramatically to 4.65% from 5.23% in 1Q25, and from 5.94% in 2Q24.

 The continued surge in consumer loans explains both a) the higher NIMs, and b) higher credit costs experienced by the banking system in recent quarters. While the credit costs / provision expense seem worrying, it can be argued that the significantly higher loan yields more than compensate, and result in higher risk-adjusted returns. Only time will tell if the banks – collectively and individually – are able to balance the risk/reward calculus.

August tourist arrivals -3% YoY, 8M25 -2% YoY to 3.96mn

Data from the Department of Tourism (DOT) exhibited that tourist arrivals for August dropped 3% YoY to 457,438—bringing the 8M25 down by 2% YoY to 3.96mn. The 3.96mn figure is ~67% of the FY24 arrivals. 2024 saw 5.9mn tourist arrivals for the year, short of the gov't target of 7.7mn. The DOT has yet to provide its tourist arrivals target for 2025.

YTD, Tourist arrivals from the USA and Japan were up 8% YoY and 19% YoY, respectively, but these were not enough to offset the decline in arrivals from Korea and China (-19% and -24%). Tourism Secretary Christina Frasco noted that the drop in South Korean tourist arrivals was also seen in other Southeast Asian destinations such as Singapore (-2%), Thailand (-17%), Cambodia (-7%), and Vietnam (-2%)

Frasco highlighted difficulties with funding as the DOT's branding and promotions budget steadily declined over the years from ~P1bn before the pandemic, P1.2bn in 2023, P200mn in 2024, and P100mn in 2025

Tourism's share to GDP in 2024 was 8.9% (P2.35trn, +10.3% YoY) (Tourism Direct Gross Value Added [TDGVA]) – this was the highest in five years but still lower than 2019's 12.9%. The DOT expects TDGVA to amount to P2.7trn (+15% YoY) this year.

Marcos forms independent probe commission to investigate corruption in public works

 

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has implemented Executive Order (EO) No. 94 which establishes a three-member Independent Commission for Infrastructure (ICI), they are tasked with investigating "anomalies, irregularities, and misuse of funds" concerning gov't flood control and other infrastructure projects nationwide. The EO states that the investigation will "prioritize" flood control and other infrastructure projects "within the last 10 years."

Marcos stated that the House and Senate can continue its inquiries as the ICI investigation will not impede with the ongoing investigations by the legislative branch. Some of the key functions and powers of the ICI mentioned in the EO are as follows:

>The ICI can act on its own initiative, and on information against all gov't officials and employees as well as private individuals, who may be involved

>Criminal, civil, and administrative charges can be held against those found liable for violating various laws

>All departments, bureaus, agencies, and offices in the executive branch are ordered to assist the ICI

>The commission also has the power to obtain documents such as contracts and bank statements from the BSP and BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue)

>The ICI will continue its work until it has been accomplished or it is dissolved by the president

A few high-profile personalities have reportedly been tapped to be part of the ICI, including former Senior Associate Justices Antonio Carpio and Estela Perla-Bernabe, and Baguio Mayor Benjamin Magalong.
