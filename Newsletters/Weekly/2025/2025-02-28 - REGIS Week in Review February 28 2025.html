REGIS REPORT

28 February 2025

The PCOMP ended the week in the red, -1.64% WoW at 5997, as shares came off sharply at the closing auction on Friday (-2.06% on the session), dragged down by a sharp surge in net foreign selling amid the MSCI rebalancing which saw the demotion of URC and JGS out of the standard cap index and into the small cap benchmark. Prior to rebal day, the benchmark basically fluctuated steadily around the 6100-handle while trading on fairly light volumes, as investors digested another busy round of 4Q/FY2024 corporate results on the ground and assessed the rhetoric about tariffs overseas – ahead of the March 4 deadline when US is supposed to effect its 25% tariffs on Canada and Mexico. Investors also had a chance to react for the first time to the BSP’s earlier than expected announcement of a 200bp RRR cut late last week, which is expected to free up about P350b of liquidity into the system and enhance annual earnings of lenders under Regis coverage by ~3%.

Corporate results released during the week looked generally inline. One stand-out was MER +0.62% WoW which announced that its FY24 core profit surged +22% YoY to P25b, ahead of Street estimates, lifted by healthy volume growth from its distribution business (sales of distribution utility +6% YoY) and higher contributions from its power generation business as well (+28% YoY and now accounting for 38% of core income). Results from other utilities TEL -3.55% and MWC (unchanged) released during the week meanwhile looked inline with forecasts.

In other news, following SM’s earnings announcement which on the surface looked better than Regis and Street forecasts (FY24 net income P82.6b vs Regis P79b, StreetP81.1b), the company also unveiled a $1b buyback program, citing the significant undervaluation” of its shares. SM shares however still tracked the widespread weakness across the broader market this week, -1.92% WoW.

In terms of movers, MSCI standard cap deletions JGS -13.9% WoW and URC -11.08% were the worst performing stocks in the benchmark during the week. Note that these 2 names are also coming off their sharp gains of +20.2% and +18.3%, respectively, through the prior week. With the headwind from MSCI rebalancing now out of the way, investors will be anticipating URC’s 4Q/FY2024 results due within the next 2 weeks. Note that numbers from consumer companies that have already reported (RRHI and MONDE) showed improving 4Q trends. URC's parent JGS will likely announce shortly after (as of writing no specific results briefing date yet for both).

On the opposite end, leading the index members this week was BDO +5.63% WoW, which managed to outperform peers BPI -2.97% and MBT -1.65% this time around. The company also announced results earlier in the week, which looked inline with Regis and Street forecasts. Outlook on banks in general this year remains positive still, not least due to the aforementioned 200bp RRR cut that is set to take effect on end-March.

Average daily turnover of the market stood at $144m this week, +58% WoW, skewed higher by the hefty $355m session on Friday due to the active MSCI rebalancing. Net foreign selling ballooned to $83m and again bulk of this was on Friday ($59m). With this week’s hefty outflows, YTD outflow now stands at -$259m just 2 months into the year – compared to -$408m net outflow of FY24.

PLDT - FY24 results: Mobile bounce
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income at P34.1bn, inclusive of Maya losses

PLDT reported telco core income of P35bn for FY24, up +2%. Adjusting for its share of losses at Maya, we estimate core income at P34.1bn, up +6% YoY. We’d characterize the results as broadly within expectations. There was no big outperformance, but we see a notable pick-up in 4Q mobile revenue. Broadband net adds, however, were a little disappointing relative to the competition.

Mobile gains in 4Q

There was visible 4Q improvement for mobile, with revenue bouncing a surprising +5% QoQ. Recall that, in contrast, Globe’s mobile revenue dipped 1%. As a result, we estimate PLDT gained ~1.5pt of market share on a two-player basis. We may need to revisit our views on the mobile market following these numbers, and those from the third operator when they become available.

Broadband net adds slow

PLDT saw a net increase of +37k in wireline broadband subscribers in 4Q. While a creditable performance, it could be seen as a disappointment relative to the competition. This is a reflection, in our view, of PLDT’s reluctance to go deeper into the prepaid market.

Maya reaches breakeven

PLDT’s attributable net loss from Maya for FY24 amounted to P932m, which implies an attributable 4Q net loss of P14m. While no explicit guidance for Maya was provided, PLDT is hopeful that Maya will contribute positively this year.

Increased stake in Maya being considered

Management expressed its preference for PLDT and biggest shareholder First Pacific to increase their combined stake (now just under 40%) to a majority, but only at an acceptable valuation. Such an investment could be funded, at least in part, by the sale of under-utilized real estate and obsolete copper assets.

 
Manila Water - East zone total spending drops in 4Q24
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY24 core profit up 46% YoY to P12.3bn – in line with consensus estimates

Headline FY24 profit of P10.5bn (+88% YoY) included another large impairment charge of its investment in Thailand (P3.6bn), and other write-offs/gains. Reported core profit rose 46% YoY to P14.1bn. Removing the share of participating prefs (PPS), we estimate recurring profit to common stood at P12.3bn, well above Regis estimates but broadly in line with the consensus forecast.

MWC continued to spend below its committed cash operating expenditures for the East zone (EZ), which has boosted near-term profits. However, we reiterate that the under-spending should not be viewed positively, as it negatively affects the concession value (i.e. Opening Cash Position, or OCP). The OCP ultimately determines the value of the EZ concession.

EZ overall spending dips 5% YoY in 4Q24

Derived 4Q24 EBITDA for the EZ unit rose by 11% YoY to P5.0bn. Revenues rose 19% but cash opex jumped 44% YoY. However, capex for the quarter fell 12% YoY to P8.5bn, taking overall spending (i.e. cash opex + capex) lower by 5% YoY to 10.5bn in 4Q24.

For full-year 2024, total spending rose 20% YoY to P31.7bn but is still trending below our forecast of P34bn. We estimate cumulative spending for the EZ has reached only P58bn in FY23-FY24 (P29bn/year), lagging its regulatory commitment of P175bn for RY2023-2027 (P35bn/year).

Non-EZ units account for 13% of FY24 profits

On a recurring basis, combined profits from the non-East zone domestic and international units rose more than two-fold to P1.8bn in FY24, accounting for 13% of profits. This was above our forecast of P1.6bn due to higher tariffs at the domestic units.

Payout ratio rises to ~40%

MWC declared a total dividend of P1.841/sh (ex-div date on March 7), equivalent to a yield of ~6%. This dividend is equivalent to 39% of FY24 core profit to common, an increase from last year’s payout ratio of 35%.

 
AREIT - P21bn share-for-property swap to increase AUM by 18%
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY24 net profit rises 49%, thanks in part to accounting adjustments

AREIT’s FY24 net profit rose 49% to P7.4bn. While this is notionally above REGIS and consensus forecasts, the outperformance came from 4Q24 accounting adjustments on contracts under straight-line revenue recognition. If not for these adjustments, revenue would only have risen 3% QoQ (instead of 9%) and results would have been in line. The adjustments did not have any cash impact.

Office occupancy improves QoQ

AREIT’s office occupancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 95%. The company also said that rental rates on contract renewals in FY24 were “flattish”. Meanwhile, occupancy of the entire portfolio was unchanged at 99%.

Limited lease expiries in FY25

Only a limited amount of AREIT’s office space will experience lease expiries this year but expiries will pick up in FY26. There will be 42k sqm of lease expiries in FY25 and 115k sqm in FY26. These represent 7% and 18% of office space respectively.

New P21bn share-for-property swap with Ayala Land

AREIT will acquire 306k sqm of mall, office, and hotel assets (located in Cebu, Davao, and Cagayan de Oro) from Ayala Land in exchange for 506mn new AREIT shares. The total transaction value of P21bn implies that the shares will be issued at a price of P41.50/share. After the acquisitions, AREIT’s shares outstanding and AUM will increase by 16% and 18% respectively.

AREIT said that the acquisitions were priced at c. 6.5% cap rate (higher than the current AREIT dividend yield of 6.1%) and would raise DPS by c. 3%.

In order to meet minimum float requirements, the Ayala Group will need to sell 12.5mn AREIT shares (c. P500mn) ahead of the effectivity of the acquisition.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land to purchase ABS-CBN property

Ayala Land (ALI PM) signed an MOA to acquire 30,000sqm of land from ABS-CBN (ABS PM). The parcel will be acquired for P6.24bn (inclusive of VAT) to be paid over 10 years. This implies a transaction value of P208,000/sqm, lower than a transaction done at c. P250k/sqm in the 2-3 years ago. The value of the transaction represents c. 1% of ALI’s NAV.

The property currently houses several buildings, including production studios. ABS will consolidate its facilities in the remaining 14,000sqm it holds (adjacent to the 30,000sqm). ALI is trading at 58% discount to NAV, 1.1X FY25E P/B, and 10.6X FY25E P/E.

ABS engaged in this deal in an effort to repay its debts which the company has been struggling to do since losing its legislative franchise back in 2020. As of 9M24, ABS reported a net loss of P2.59bn while its total consolidated bank loans stood at P16.3bn. Banks mentioned in the loan section include BDO, BPI, UBP, and SECB. Of the loans, parent ABS was P11.8bn, while SkyCable's was P4.5bn.

ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rise 11% YoY in January

Data from Mexico’s port regulator showed that volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), grew by 11% YoY to 137k TEUs in January 2025. This was faster than the 4% YoY growth rate in Dec 2024. CMSA’s market share in January stood at 42% (up from a 40% share in Jan 2024 and 37% in FY24), making it the largest port by volume in Manzanillo. Volumes across the four ports in Manzanillo grew by 6% YoY to 329k TEUs. Industry growth was virtually unchanged from Dec 2024/FY24 (+6% pace), given that the port is already congested. Recall that CMSA increased its capacity in October 2024 by 250k TEUs (+15%) to 1.7mn TEUs. For FY25E, we’ve built in a 16% YoY increase in CMSA’s volumes to 1.69mn TEUs. To reach our forecast, CMSA’s volumes should average 141k TEUs for the rest of the year. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of FY25E consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of EBITDA. ICT trades at 7.2x Regis’ FY25E EV/EBITDA (consensus: 7.8x), lower than peer average of (11x) and historical average (11x).

Meralco FY24 core profit up 22% to P45bn

Core income is up 22% to P45bn. Meralco reported FY24 core income of P45.1bn, up 22% YoY and ahead of Street estimates. The earnings were lifted by stronger contributions from its generation assets as well as healthy volumes from the core distribution utility (DU) business.

DU unit sales grew 6%. Unit sales were up 6.4% for the year, with residential volumes leading the way (+9%). Note that the pace of growth normalized to +4.4% in 4Q after a blistering 1H24. The industrial segment still managed to show positive YoY growth (+0.9%) in the last quarter. The DU business delivered 62% of core income for the year. The company is guiding for 4% to 4.5% DU volume growth this FY25.

Lift from generation. The unregulated business, the largest component of which is power generation, contributed P17bn, 38% of core income, and up ~28%. We note 4Q contributions saw a nice jump from GBP (now “MThermal”) and MGreen. The former began selling ancillary power services last year. The other unregulated businesses, though, seem to have fared poorly.

For FY25, the acquisition of the LNG assets (“Chromite”) from San Miguel (SMC PM) is likely to power earnings even higher. Management expects contributions of P5-6bn from the Chromite acquisition on an annualized basis. Meralco’s economic ownership of Chromite is now 40.2% following the completion of the deal a few weeks ago.

Franchise extension almost done. The extension of Meralco’s congressional franchise by another 25 years appears practically done. Meralco says a reconciled House-Senate bill is already set to be enrolled before it is sent to Malacañang for the President’s signature.

Rate reset imminent? The ERC appears to be rushing to complete Meralco’s long-delayed reset for its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) by June. Meralco’s application seeks a ~25% increase in its tariff (P1.69/kwh vs P1.35/kwh) premised on a regulatory return (WACC) of 14.6% and a chunky P246bn (US$4.2bn) capex program over four years. We reckon there’s a lot of room for that applied tariff to be cut.

Dividends of P13.736/sh. Meralco declared dividends of P13.736/sh, equivalent to a 2.8% yield. This final dividend, combined with the one declared in 3Q24 (=P10.295/sh), is equivalent to ~60% of the prior year’s income. Meralco typically declares dividends twice a year.

BDO FY24 net profit rises 12%, in line

BDO’s FY24 earnings rose 12% to P82bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. 4Q24 net profit grew 10% to P21.4bn. FY24 pre-provisioning operating profit rose 4% to P117.7bn, in line with REGIS forecast. 4Q24 fell 6% YoY, up 3% QoQ. FY24 net interest income rose 8% to P187bn. 4Q24 net interest income grew 7% YoY (+3% QoQ).

FY24 net interest margin fell to 4.35%, from 4.37% in FY23. 4Q24 net interest margin rose to 4.31%, from 4.23% in 3Q24. Gross customer loans grew 13% YoY (to P13.2trn), the same pace as 3Q24. Consumer loans were up 16%, middle-market loans up 12%, and corporate loans up 13%. This is slightly higher than the 12.2% loan growth of the industry.

Deposits rose 6% YoY to P3.8trn, slower than the 10% YoY growth in 3Q24. CASA ratio stood at 71%. Strong asset quality allowed the bank to lower provisions—FY24 down 14%, 4Q24 fell 31% YoY. The NPL ratio was flat QoQ at 1.83% (1.82% in 3Q24), lower than the industry’s NPL ratio of 3.27%. NPL cover was also stable QoQ at 145% (144% in 3Q24). Restructured loans fell to 1.3% of total loans, from 1.9% in 2023.

The CET1 ratio was 14.1%. Parent CET1 was at 13.7%. Return on common equity hit 15.1% for FY24. In 2025, BDO is targeting low-teens loan growth, with provincial areas and consumer loans likely to grow more quickly (REGIS FY25E: +12%). The bank expects a stable NIM, as lower RRR and rate cuts are expected to offset each other, though the lending environment remains competitive (REGIS expects a 10-basis point reduction in 2025 NIM).

Mid-teens growth in fee income is targeted (REGIS FY25E: +8%). A deceleration in opex growth is expected (REGIS FY25E: opex +7%). Capital ratios are expected to be maintained.

Megaworld FY24 net profit rises 12%

In a press release, Megaworld said that its FY24 net profit (before minority interest) rose 12% to P21.7bn. A corresponding 12% increase in net profit to parent equity holders would be equivalent to P19.4bn, above REGIS and consensus forecasts. Revenue of every segment outperformed our expectations. FY24 residential revenue grew 19% to P51bn, a record level. In 9M24, MEG sold about P40bn of RFO units, which can be recognized as revenue quickly. This trend might have continued in 4Q24. FY24 office revenue rose 7% to P13.4bn. 4Q24 office revenue increased 13% QoQ and 19% YoY, as new office buildings began contributing to revenue. FY24 mall revenue rose 19% YoY to P6.3bn, just slightly above the pre-COVID level. 4Q24 mall revenue climbed 19% QoQ and 27% YoY. FY24 hotel revenue grew 34% to P5.1bn, thanks to the completion of the 1,530-room Grand Westside Hotel, the largest hotel in the Philippines. MEG is trading at a 72% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 3.0X FY25E P/E. The company plans to hold a briefing next week.

NIKL: Profits rebound in 4Q

Headline FY24 profit of P1.52bn included a P1.55bn provision for its geothermal investment in Mindoro. Reported core profit stood at P3.07bn (-18% YoY), beating consensus forecast of P2.4bn but fell short of our estimate of P4.2bn. FY24 EBITDA stood at P8.8bn (-20% YoY) fell a tad short of our forecast (P8.9bn) but was above consensus forecast (P8.6bn). This suggests that the key swing factor to the miss/beat was the loss of the HPAL plants.

Implied 4Q24 core profit rose five-fold YoY to P518mn, primarily driven by lower equitized losses at its HPAL facilities. On a sequential basis, earnings fell 64% due to monsoon rains, which typically hit Palawan/Surigao in 1Q-4Q. EBITDA marginally improved (+2% YoY) to P2.1bn in 4Q24 on the back of a mild improvement in ore prices, which we suspect was offset by higher operating costs.

NIKL declared a dividend of P0.15/sh (ex-div: Mar 12), equivalent to a yield of 6%. The dividend is equivalent to 68% of NIKL’s core income. Management is holding a briefing on March 5, 2pm.

ACEN starts construction of 400MWh BESS project in Australia

ACEN will begin construction on the 400MWh battery energy storage system (BESS), which will complement its 936MW dc (720MWac) New England Solar Farm (NESF) project. BESS will provide 200MWh of energy over a two-hour period, when required – day or night. BESS project is expected to be completed within the year. The first stage of NESF project (521MWdc) was completed in 2023. The second stage of NESF, 416MWdc (320MWac), will begin construction in 2026. We estimate the total cost of the NESF + BESS project could be ~US$800mn (or ~US$900k/MW). ACEN owns 100% of the NESF. As of Oct 31, 2025, ACEN’s total attributable RE capacity stood at 6,754 MW. Its operating RE capacity stood at 3,051 MW, of which 1,067MW is in the Philippines. It is currently constructing 2,302 MW, of which 1,330MW is in the Philippines. Another 1,401 MW of new capacity is “committed,” which likely includes the second stage 416MWdc NESF project.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP cuts reserve requirement ratio

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) will reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for commercial banks by 200 bps to 5% starting March 28. Reserve requirements for digital and thrift banks will also be cut by 150 bps to 2.5% and 100 bps to 0.0%, respectively. The cut is expected to inject at least P300bn worth of liquidity into the financial system.

This move follows the BSP’s decision to keep rates steady during its last monetary board meeting, where BSP Governor Remolona had already guided that an RRR cut would happen this year, with the timing expected within 1H25.

Gov't records budget deficit of P330bn in December (-17.8% YoY); P1.51tn for FY24 (-0.4% YoY)

The government recorded a budget deficit of P330bn in Dec, narrower than Dec 2023's deficit of P401bn, bringing the FY24 deficit to 1.51tn, which narrowed by P5.7bn (0.4% YoY). The FY deficit is equivalent to 5.7% of GDP—much lower than the 6.2% seen in 2023 but short of the 5.6% of GDP target set by the gov't.

Revenue collections in Dec jumped 21.0% YoY to P315.bn, primarily driven by non-tax revenues which surged 333% YoY as tax revenues were flattish only up 2% YoY. The full-year revenue figure rose 15.6% YoY to P4.419tn as BIR collections were on target alongside better-than-expected non-tax revenues but was offset by Customs' missed FY24 goal. Non-tax revenues surged due to windfall collections from Public-Private Partnership (PPP) concession fee (P30bn) and fund balance transfers (P167bn) from the Philippine Health Insurance Corp. (PhilHealth) and Philippine Deposit Insurance Corp. (PDIC). The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) also saw higher dividend remittances and PAGCOR profits.

Customs were short P23bn of their P940bn target given the implemented tariffs reductions on rice, meat products, and electric vehicles. Expenditures went down slightly by 2.6% in Dec as both primary expenses and interest payments were down for the month. Full-year disbursements however were up 11% YoY to P5.93tn and 3% higher than the FY24 program of P5.75tn due to higher-than-expected primary spending. Primary spending for the FY24 rose 9.6% YoY to P5.16tn.

The increase in primary spending was attributed to the (a) infrastructure/other capital outlays of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) (b) maintenance and other opex for health and social protection programs, (c) salary adjustments of qualified gov't employees. Interest payments went up 21.5% given elevated interest rates alongside less favorable forex fluctuations. The gov’t targets the deficit to GDP ratio to narrow further to 5.3% by year-end 2025.

Tax collections rise 12% YoY in January

In a Facebook post, the Department of Finance reported that preliminary data showed a 12% YoY increase in tax collections to P429.9bn for January. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections rose by 13.7% YoY to P350.6bn, while the Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P79.3bn, up 8.1% YoY.

For 2025, the BIR and BOC are tasked with collecting P4.33tn, equivalent to 15% of GDP. Year-to-November 2024 tax collections amounted to P3.55tn, up 11.5% YoY, and broadly in line with the year-end target of P3.82bn (+12% YoY).

Philippines removed from FATF's grey list

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) has removed the Philippines from its increased monitoring list for "dirty money." The country had been on the FATF's grey list for over three years since June 2021. This decision follows BSP Governor Remolona’s meetings with the FATF Plenary last week.

The FATF noted that the Philippines has made positive progress in addressing anti-money laundering (AML), countering the financing of terrorism (CFT), and proliferation financing (CPF). In July 2023, an executive order mandated all government offices to adopt the National AML, CTF, and CPF strategy for 2023-2027. The next FATF assessment will be in 2027 to verify whether the Philippines has sustained its current measures in addressing AML/CFT concerns.

Exiting the grey list is expected to result in faster and lower-cost cross-border transactions, reduce compliance barriers, and encourage more foreign banks and entities to do business in the country. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto also stated that this supports the government’s push to achieve an A-credit rating.

January digital payments jump 38% to P1.8tn

Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped 38.2% YoY in January, keeping pace from the 38% growth seen in Dec, and 41% in Nov. Combined PesoNet and InstaPay payments totaled to P1.8tn (roughly $30.8bn), much higher than P1.3tn last Jan 2024 but unchanged relative to the previous month of Dec. By volume, consolidated volume of transactions through InstaPay and PesoNet surged 58.4% YoY to 160.2mn from 101.2mn. It is of note that these datasets only reflect inter-platform transactions (e.g., BDO-to-BPI, GCash-to-MBT, Maya-to-GCash, etc.) and do not include transfers within the same platform. As a result, they significantly underestimate the total volume of digital transfers. Real-time, small-ticket transactions over InstaPay amounted to P751bn, up 47.8% YoY but down 7.3% MoM given seasonality from the holiday season last Dec. In terms of number of transactions, InstaPay volumes grew 62% YoY—slightly slower than Dec's 65% growth. For larger payments over PesoNet, the January figure increased 32% YoY and 6.2% MoM to P1.05tn, aligned with the 31% growth in Dec and 32% in Nov. While PesoNet volumes are up 21% to 9.6mn. The BSP targets online payments to comprise 60-70% of the country's total retail transaction volume by 2028. Bringing the fee to zero (from as high as P25 transfer fees per transaction) would encourage even lower-ticket payments and more transactions to be done digitally.

PAGCOR says Philippine GGR grew 24% in 2024

PAGCOR said that the country’s gross gaming revenue (GGR) rose 24% to P410bn in 2024. It expects GGR to grow to P450bn-P480bn in 2025. PAGCOR expects the growth to be led by e-games. It expects licensed casinos to comprise 50% of 2025 GGR and e-games to account for 45%. 49% of GGR came from licensed casinos. This would imply FY24 GGR of P200bn (-3% YoY) and 4Q24 GGR of P51bn (flattish to mildly up QoQ and YoY). 38% of GGR came from e-games. This would imply FY24 GGR of P155bn (+170% YoY) and 4Q24 GGR of P52bn (+30% QoQ).
