REGIS REPORT

20 June 2025

The PCOMP turned lower this week, -0.87% WoW at 6339, as investors weighed the BSP’s 25bp rate cut on Friday, which had been widely expected amid the sub-2% CPI reading for the past 3 months, against the rising inflationary threat amid the brewing conflict in the Middle East – which has resulted to oil prices spiking over 20% so far this June. This is crucial area of concern given Philippines is a net importer of oil. But despite this local shares managed to fare much better than our closer peers in the region: Thailand -4.86% WoW and Indonesia -3.61%. That said, the PHP did post a pronounced decline, -1.74% WoW to the worst level since early April. Another important event during the week is the FTSE rebalancing on Friday which had outflow estimates ranging from $80-$100m – but note that the benchmark did close almost unchanged, -0.27% during the session, and net outflow on Friday stood at $15m.

In terms of sectors, the consumer space was broadly higher with PGOLD +4.72% WoW, URC +4.3%, CNPF +1.9%, and MONDE +0.56% - potentially as investors are sticking to bets with defensive earnings prospects relative to other sectors.

Another notable winner was index heavyweight ICT +2.65% after the latest shipping data in Mexico showed that ICT’s CMSA managed to further gain market share Manzanillo port. CMSA’s volumes grew by +16% YoY in May, faster than +14% in April, despite a -10% YoY drop in volumes for the entire Manzanillo port (which was attributed to a workers strike that affected operations for a week). One factor cited behind ICT’s market share gain is its proximity to the rail used by shippers. As CMSA accounts for 12% of ICT's consolidated vols, 21% of revenues and 25% of EBITDA, this preliminary data should bode well for ICT’s 2Q numbers (following an upbeat 1Q) notwithstanding the ongoing trade uncertainty due to US’ tariff threats.

On the downside, gaming names PLUS -8.67% and BLOOM -13.59% (which is the worst performer in the benchmark this week) both sharply turned lower primarily on the back of news that Senator Cayetano is pushing for a Senate investigation into online gambling in the country. Outside of this news however, there were some notable flow considerations about PLUS - it had some outflows related to the FTSE rebalancing on Friday (estimates ranged $8-10m in net ouflow), but its imminent inclusion in the PSEi in August remain top of mind among investors (and specifically bargain hunters). Note that regardless of any rule changes in the PSE’s index policy, Regis expects PLUS, which at this point already has a market cap of over P250b or $4.4b, to get into the PSEi in the upcoming review (to replace BLOOM). Our prelimary inflow estimate for PLUS is ~$50m - for context, in the previous PSEi rebal on Jan 31, CBC, which surged 40% on rebal day, had an inflow estimate of $100m (but granted it had less liquidity and was much lesser held compared to PLUS).


Despite the eventful week and the FTSE rebalancing on Friday, market turnover was slightly lower at a daily average of $129m, -8% WoW. Net foreign flow ended skewed to sell by a hefty $61.9m – but note that a huge chunk of this was due to FPH which had a $45m outflow block on Monday related to the company’s buyback program.

ICTSI's CMSA grows volumes by 16% in May; Sustains market share gains

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), rose by 16% YoY to 121k TEUs in May. Growth accelerated further from +14% YoY in April, as exports (46% of total) grew at a faster rate of +33% YoY in May from +29% in April. CMSA took market share, with volumes of ports across Manzanillo falling by 10% YoY in May.

Moreover, CMSA implemented tariff hikes of 5% (e.g. yard, storage) to 12% (stevedoring) in May. These tariff increases are broadly in line with Regis forecast.

The latest print brings YT-May volumes at CMSA to 630k TEUs, up 10% YoY. This is trending below Regis forecast of 1.69mn TEUs (+16% YoY). Note that Regis assumes the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) treaty will be cancelled only by July 2026. Every 5% decrease (increase) in CMSA’s FY25E volumes would shave off (increase) EBITDA by 1.6% and profit by 2.4%.

Regis estimates CMSA accounts for 12% of consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of group EBITDA. 

Bloomberry aspires to be market leader in online gaming

Cyrus Sherafat, Head of Bloomberry’s gaming operations, said that the company is targeting 20% market share in online gaming in two years.

Regis estimates this would be equivalent to about P40bn in GGR. This could increase FY27E GGR forecast by about 45% and FY27E EBITDA forecast by about 30% (to c. P26bn). This could also raise Regis FY27E net profit forecast by about 110% (to P11bn).  BLOOM’s FY24 GGR was P62bn.

The company aspires to eventually be the market leader in online gaming. DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is currently the market leader, with c. 45% market share.

Bloomberry also appointed Gregory Francis Hawkins as President and COO. He had been Acting President and COO Since December 2024, when his predecessor, Thomas Arasi, retired. Mr. Hawkins joined Bloomberry in November 2023 as head of Solaire North.  He was previously Chief Casino Officer with the Star Entertainment Group, CEO of Crown Melbourne, President of City of Dreams Macau, and CEO of Altira Hotel Macau.

The ERC has issued the final ceiling prices for the fourth round of Green Energy Auction (GEA-4), covering 10,653 MW of solar and wind capacities.

The green energy auction reserve (GEAR) price for ground-mounted solar (3,940 MW) was set at P5.68/kwh:
GEAR for rooftop solar (48 MW) at P4.4832/kwh

  • GEAR for floating solar (3,175 MW) at P6.5258/kwh
  • GEAR for onshore wind (2,390MW) at P6.0859/kwh

LWUA chief says overwhelmed with consumer complaints over Prime Water

The Local Water Utilities Administration (LWUA) has been overwhelmed with complaints received over the poor service of Prime Water, according to LWUA Administrator Jose Salonga.

LWUA wants Prime Water to address the water problems immediately. When asked if LWUA is considering a takeover if issues are not address, Mr. Salonga said “we’ll cross the bridge if we get there”.

Prime Water has joint ventures with local water districts in Pampanga, Bulacan, Cavite, Leyte, Bacolod City and Malaybalay, Bukidnon.

Cebu Air (CEB PM) pax volumes jump 22% in May

CEB flew 2.4m passengers in May, up by 22% YoY. Domestic pax volumes grew 20% YoY to 1.8mn with seat load factors (SLF) up 22bps YoY to 91%. International pax volumes rose 28% YoY to 600k on seat growth of 26% (SLF up 99bps to 75%). This brings YT-May pax volumes up 24% YoY to 11.7m, in line with our FY25E of 28.5m (+16%).

Maya expects continued profitability

Maya’s wallet, merchant acquiring, and banking segments all reported positive EBITDA and EBIT through May, extending overall profitability from its first profitable month last December 2024. The platform generated P311 million in net income for Q1 2025 and remained profitable in April and May, supported by positive cash flows over the first five months.

Management projects Maya will contribute about P1 billion in profit for 2025. PLDT chairman Manuel Pangilinan said there are no immediate plans to raise PLDT’s equity stake.

Total lending to date has reached P120 billion after Maya disbursed P28 billion in new loans in 1Q25. Deposits reached P43.6 billion as of March, roughly twice UnionDigital’s P22 billion and equivalent to a 61% share of the total deposits held by the country’s six licensed digital banks.

PLDT owns 37.7% of Maya Innovations, which holds Maya Bank.

Wilcon expects conservative growth this year

Wilcon Depot Inc. (WLCON PM) management guided for conservative growth this year—eyeing a bit higher than mid-single digit top line growth and flattish to very low growth in earnings on the back of a second half recovery.

COO Rosemarie B. Ong elaborated that the slowness of the overall market can be felt in Metro Manila but some bright spots such as the Cavite-Laguna area and other areas outside of Metro Manila are seeing better growth. Management noted ongoing initiatives such as store rightsizing to streamline inventory, space, and optimize expenses.

The company targets eight new store openings to end this year with 108 . stores. Capex budget for 2025 is P3.2bn, with P652mn spent as of the first quarter. This is higher than the P2.2bn capex spent last year. P2.2bn will be allocated for the construction of new stores and warehouses, P568mn for renovations, P327mn for store and transportation equipment, P137mn for IT infrastructure. 

Century Pacific renews long-term partnership deal with Vita Coco

Century Pacific Food Inc. (CNPF PM) has renewed its supply agreement with The Vita Coco Company Inc. (COCO US), in a deal valued at around P14bn. The five-year contract extension will be effective from January 2026 onwards.

The deal ensures continued production to supply Vita Coco's base coconut water requirements at CNPF's coconut manufacturing facilities in Gen. Santos City, Mindanao. The renewed partnership implies continuing their 2024 incremental long-term agreement which covered Vita Coco's additional volume requirements of approximately 90mn liters of coconut water over five years. To meet these additional requirements, Century Pacific back in Sept 2024 invested US$40mn in acquiring Coco Harvest., a coconut processing facility located in Misamis Occidental, Mindanao.

CNPF's OEM business in 2024 posted P16.2bn (+36% YoY) in revenues, contributing 21% to the company's P75.5bn total. This was driven by favorable commodity prices and strong demand.

PLDT cuts price for prepaid fiber

PLDT (TEL PM)  cut the price of its 30-day prepaid service by 30% to P699 (from P999). This matches the 30-day price of GFiber Prepaid of Globe (GLO PM) and Surf2Sawa of Converge (CNVRG PM).

PLDT’s net adds for wireline broadband have been lagging because it has avoided competing in prepaid fiber, possibly because it has a higher cost structure.

ACEN sells diesel assets to parent

ACEN Corp. is selling its three diesel plants with a combined capacity of 189MW to its parent, AC Energy and Infrastructure Corp. (ACEIC). Total consideration is less than 10% of ACEN’s assets. Divestment is subject to the satisfaction of agreed conditions precedent and the execution of definitive documentation. The company cited that the divestment would help it achieve its goal of reaching 100% RE generation by end-2025.

ACEIC is a 100%-owned unit of Ayala Corp. (AC PM) and owns 58% of ACEN. Similar to its “exit” from the 246MW coal-fired plant SLTEC, we expect ACEN to maintain the contracts of diesel plants so that revenue/income streams will still accrue to ACEN.

Senator wants further probe of online gambling

Senator Alan Peter Cayetano urged the incoming 20th Congress to continue investigating online gambling in the country. He lamented that while an anti-POGO Bill has been passed, Philippine Inland Gaming Operators (PIGO) have grown.

Earlier this year, Senate President Francis Escudero called for a review of Philippine Inland Gaming Operators, which offer e-games to Filipinos.

Gross Gaming Revenue of e-games (including bingo) grew 88% YoY (flat QoQ) to P51bn in 1Q25. DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is the market leader, with 45% market share. 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) dip in May; Toyota sales up 6%

CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales fell 1.2% YoY to 39,775 units in May. On a MoM basis, vehicle sales were up 18% due to the low base effects from Easter break. Note that CAMPI/TMA data does not capture BYD’s sales

For 5M25, sales are up 1.7% YoY to 190,429 units; If we include BYD, we estimate industry car sales would have grown over 5%.

Sales of electrified vehicles (ex-BYD) hit 3,613 units in May, up 139% MoM. EVs accounted for 9.1% of new car sales in May. This took 5M25 EV sales to 10,433 units, accounting for 5.5% of the market.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 19,725 units in May, up 5.6% YoY and 20% MoM. TMP’s 5M25 sales volumes to 91,652 units (+6.3% YoY). TMP is targeting to sell 235k units this year (+8% YoY). To reach this target, sales should average 20.5k units per month for the rest of the year.

TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses. 

Digital payments rise 38% YoY in May

Payments made digitally over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) rose by 38% YoY to P1.98tn in May, accelerating from the 31% pace in April. This took the 5M25 transaction value to P9.1tn, up 37% YoY.

Payments through InstaPay, which handles real-time payments of up to P50k, rose 54% YoY to P930.6bn in May. By volume, InstaPay payments surged 217% YoY, even faster than the 190% pace in April.

Large-ticket payment transactions that go through PesoNet, rose 27% YoY to P1.05tn in May, faster than the 18% growth seen in April. 

Government formalizes contract extensions of Manila Water, Maynilad

The Economy and Development (ED) Council, formerly known as the NEDA Board, has approved the 10-year extension of the two water concessionaires in Metro Manila. The extension to Jan 21, 2047 (from July 31, 2037) aligns the contracts with the legislative franchises of both concessionaires. The water regulator, MWSS, already assumed the extension during the 2022 rate rebasing exercise. The auditors of both water companies have also assumed the extension in the amortization of concession assets.

RCR to acquire 324,000sqm of malls from RLC via share-for-property swap

RCR will acquire 324,000sqm of malls from RLC in exchange for 3.83bn new RCR shares. Eight malls are in provincial areas while 1 mall is in Metro Manila: Dasmarinas, Starmills (Pampanga), General Trias, Cybergate Cebu, Tacloban, Malolos, Santiago, Tuguegarao, Magnolia (Metro Manila).

RLC will subscribe to 3.83bn new RCR shares at P8/share (total of P30.67bn). RCR’s shares outstanding will expand by 24%. At the current RCR price of P7.06/share, the transaction is valued at P27bn, slightly higher than our own estimate of P26bn for the value of the assets. The acquisition would expand RCR’s portfolio by 39% to 1.15mn sqm. 600k sqm or 52% is comprised of malls while the rest are offices. AUM will also grow by 27% to P145bn.

Regis estimates suggest that the transaction would increase RCR’s DPS by c. 2%. Regis estimates as well that RLC’s ownership in RCR will increase from 57% to 66%.

DigiPlus appoints new CEO

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) appointed Hu Jianguo (Tommy Hu) as CEO. Tsui Kin Ming (Andy Tsui) will remain President of DigiPlus. The CEO title did not previously exist in DigiPlus.

Mr. Hu joined DigiPlus in 2021 and was COO just prior to his appointment as CEO. He has over 20 years of experience in platform innovation, gaming operations, and enterprise-scale technology leadership. He helped launch and scale BingoPlus into the country’s number one digital bingo platform.

PH current account deficit balloons to $4.2bn in 1Q25

The widening deficit was driven by the Philippines' wider merchandise trade gap and slower services trade. The trade-in-goods deficit was wider 15% YoY to US$16.8bn, given the faster growth in imports (+8%) vs. exports (+1.2%). Net receipts from services trade fell 9% YoY to US$3.3bn, as imports grew 1.7% while exports slowed 1.5%.

The decline in services receipts was primarily due to lower earnings from transport services (from US$1.1bn to US$755mn) and technical, trade-related, other business services (from US$5.5bn to US$5.4bn). This was offset by the growth in BPO export revenues which grew 1.3% to US$7.2bn from US$7.1bn last year.

For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to hit US$19.8bn or -3.9% of GDP. This was revised earlier in the year from the previous US$12.1bn CA deficit (2.4% of GDP) projection back in 4Q24. Recall that in 2024, the country posted its second largest CA deficit at US$17.5bn (3.8% of GDP)—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of US$12.4bn (2.8% of GDP).

OFW Remittances up 4.0% YoY in April

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 4.0% YoY to US$2.66bn, accelerating from the 2.6% growth seen in March. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 3.8%.

Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth accelerated to 3.8% YoY from 1.0% in Mar.

P1 jeepney fare hike likely next week—LTFRB

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) chairman Teofilo Guadiz III stated there may be a P1 increase across the board for jeepney fares given rising fuel prices. He stated that it will not be a per-kilometer hike but only an increase to the flat rate. The fare hike is much lower than the P2 petitioned fare increase early this year.

The minimum fare for traditional jeepneys is P13 while P15 for modern jeepneys. Recall that the government hiked minimum jeepney fares by P1 in October 2023. Passenger road transport has a weight of 3.5% in the CPI basket.

BSP trims policy rate by 25bp, signals one more cut and easing inflation ahead

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board cut key policy rates by 25bp to 5.25%. BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that they will probably cut one more time this year if things "stay on track" and depending the data. The BSP policy rate has been reduced by a total of 125bp from 6.50% since the easing cycle started back in Aug 2024. 

Adjustments were also made to the BSP's inflation forecasts, with 2025 inflation projected to average 1.6% from 2.4% previously. Upward revisions (+10bp) were made for 2026 and 2027 inflation projections to 3.4% and 3.3%, respectively. The revisions were made considering heightened risks to inflation from geopolitical tensions and US trade policy uncertainty.

Remolona elaborated that the BSP may need to intervene more seriously if the peso depreciation continues further given the possible impact on inflation. The USD-PHP exchange rate has slumped to P57.4 since P55.7 at end-May.

The next Monetary Board meeting (4th out of 6 this year) will be on Aug 28. For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps total worth of cuts to 5.00%.

BOP posts US$298mn deficit in May; May GIR settles at US$105.2bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$298mn. A reversal from the US$2.0bn surplus in May 2024 but narrower than Apr 2025's US$2.6bn deficit.

The deficit reflected the national gov't's drawdowns on the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits for external debt obligations. This brings the YT-May deficit at US$5.8bn, reversing from the US$1.6bn surplus for the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade gap, higher net outflows in the financial account, and front-loaded external debt repayments.

The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$4bn deficit (-0.8% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments.

Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-May stood at US$105.2bn, down slightly from the US$105.3bn in end-Apr. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.1 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025.
