REGIS REPORT

14 March 2025

Philippine equities joined the weakness in regional markets this week, though the PCOMP ended lower by only 0.1% or 4.18pts WoW to 6,294.11. This was a better performance vs bigger pullbacks seen in Asean benchmarks as jitters over global trade were offset by strong foreign buying, with the banks taking in much of the foreign inflow this week.

 

The biggest macro story for the week was former Pres. Duterte's widely-covered arrest and extradition to The Hague by the International Criminal Court (ICC), which had served him a warrant for "crimes against humanity" over his "war on drugs". Despite elevated market chatter on implications of the current political climate on the domestic macro and corporate outlook, local shares seemed little impacted by the developing news.

 

BSP Gov. Remolona's comments earlier in the week about eyeing further cuts to the key policy rate as well as banks' RRR helped spur further optimism on the sector. Gains in BDO (+4.8% WoW) and BPI (+3.5% WoW) provided the biggest lift to the index for the week. Conversely, despite the view on monetary easing, investors continued to stay away from the property sector. Developers corrected from the previous week's sharp gains, with ALI (-4.9% WoW) and SMPH (-2.3% WoW) dragging the index the most, along with SM (-1.6% WoW). Meanwhile, bargain hunters picked up BLOOM (+10.9% WoW) which was the PCOMP's top gainer for the week.

 

On the latest round of 4Q earnings... ACEN (-1.0% WoW) fell 5% on the day it released results that came well below Street forecasts due to typhoon disruptions, higher pre-development costs, and depreciation of newly operational plants. RLC (+0.5% WoW) also missed expectations as the residential segment underperformed. FLI (unch. WoW) came ahead of expectations as margins expanded due to some accounting changes as well as higher contribution from RFO units. AP (+1.3% WoW) earnings were in-line with double-digit EBITDA growth partly offset by depreciation and financing charges from GNPD now being recognized. AEV (-2.7% WoW) was in-line, though profits dropped from asset impairment on its investment in Republic Cement. PLUS (-0.1% WoW) earnings beat forecasts as gaming revenues and EBITDA continued to double YoY in 4Q. IMI (+2.0% WoW) losses were halved to a still hefty $50m due to persistently declining revenues and one-off costs from the rationalization of its facilities globally.

 

Net foreign buying continued for a second straight week and reached its highest weekly level since early October last year, logging $32m. Note Bloomberg data shows Philippines as the only market in Asia that has seen positive foreign equity inflows MTD. ADTO however was softer WoW to $112m.

 

The peso slightly depreciated, -0.1% WoW to 57.258:$1. The PH10Y yield was flattish at 6.2301%.

Philippine Strategy - Rice is life (5) - Inflation drop paves way for rate cut
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

­Rate cuts back on?

CPI inflation slowed sharply to 2.1% in February (from 2.9% in January). As we have been predicting for a while now, the collapse in rice prices (-4.9%) was a major driver of the Feb deceleration, and should continue to drag CPI lower in the near-term. The Monetary Board chose not to reduce policy rates in February, for fear of a feedback loop from potential PHP weaknesses. However, with the DXY falling out of bed recently, and with US Fed cut expectations rising again, we do expect the BSP to cut by 25bps at its next meeting in April… or maybe even earlier. Property stocks will likely benefit, despite fundamental oversupply concerns. We remain bullish on the consumer sector, on falling inflation, minimum wage cuts, and election spending. Dividend yield plays will likely also be in focus.

Inflation surprises (but maybe shouldn’t have) to the downside

CPI inflation dropped sharply in February to 2.1% YoY, from 2.9% in January. The reading was well below consensus of 2.6% and was even below the low-end of the BSP’s own forecast range of 2.2-3.0%. 

 

We, however, were not surprised – if anything, we were more disappointed that inflation hadn’t dropped lower, sooner. As we have repeatedly highlighted, rice prices have been a major driver of inflation in the last 24 months or so. And with the collapse in regional rice prices, we were already anticipating significantly slower CPI prints from 4Q24. While the domestic price of rice has been slower to react, the Department of Agriculture has recently guided traders to lower prices further in February and March.

 
Philippine Strategy - Duterte arrested - The noise we (really) don't need
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

­Former President arrested, flown to The Hague

On 11 March, former President Rodrigo Duterte was arrested upon his arrival from Hong Kong, was taken into police custody, and was extradited to The Hague. President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr says that his administration was following the order of Interpol, which in turn was serving a warrant from the International Criminal Court (ICC) for “crimes against humanity” for Duterte’s “war on drugs”.

 

Marcos-Duterte feud – from bad to worse

While President Marcos ran under a "unity" ticket with Vice President Sara Duterte, the relationship between the two families rapidly deteriorated. With this latest turn of events, the Marcos-Duterte conflict appears to have reached a major confrontation point. The question now is: how this will all play out? Mr Duterte was extremely popular up to the end of his term. 

Protests expected, but likely contained; Impact on mid-term elections?

We expect protests, but we see a low likelihood of these gaining critical mass on a national level. It is far from clear how the arrest might make a difference in the outcome of any electoral contests in the midterm elections set for 12 May. Noise will continue to ramp up after the elections, with the impeachment trial of VP Sara Duterte scheduled for July.

 

The noise we really don’t need

As we wrote last week, the sharp drop in inflation in February likely paves the way for a policy rate cut in April. Falling food (rice), and fuel prices also bode well for consumption, especially for low-income households.

 

The increase in political risk is, at best, an unwelcome distraction.

 
Universal Robina - In transition; reducing our estimates and target price
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Reducing our estimates and target price

We adjust our numbers for URC ahead of the upcoming results release, cutting our EPS estimates by 12%/15% for FY24/FY25 and our target price by 31% to P100/share. Despite setbacks over the past year, we are optimistic about the near-term outlook due to prospects for a broader consumption rebound. Longer-term, much will hinge on whether the recent management re-shuffle will deliver the changes needed to restart growth.

 

Rising tide

The core domestic business has been struggling recently, squeezed by more frugal consumers and market share losses. However, we expect a rebound this year (FY25) due to broad macro factors. The easing of inflation pressures combined with election-related spending should give a lift to consumption and consumer company revenues.

 

An opportunity to refresh, or to change course

Margins for its BCF business widened in FY24 with the fall in commodity costs but top line growth has been anemic. The recent management re-shuffle (Jan 2025) is, in our view, not a bad thing.  

It provides URC an opportunity for new perspectives and fresh views. While the changes are no assurance that things will turn for the better, we view this as a chance to try new ideas that may (or may not) be successful. FY25 is thus likely to be a transition year with the fruits of such changes, if any, likely to be visible only after a few quarters.

 

Non-branded businesses should “normalize”

The non-branded businesses (agro-industrial and commodity) suffered a surprise 3Q setback due to the plunge in domestic sugar prices, following a period when they were abnormally high. We believe profits for this segment should settle at more “normal” levels this year. These businesses are in highly protected and concentrated industries and will therefore be subject to risks attendant to such.

 

Target price P100

We peg our revised target price (TP) at P100/share (-31%), which still offers 39% upside from current levels. Our revised TP approximates the sum of our estimated valuation of the parts of URC’s various operations. The branded businesses are valued using DCF (COE of 12%) and we assign 7x PER multiple for its more commoditized operations.

 
ACEN - Higher non-cash, pre-development costs drag improving 4Q24 operations
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY24 core profit up 39% YoY to P6.56bn – Below Street forecasts

ACEN reported FY24 profit before pref divs rose 27% YoY to P9.36bn, inclusive of P2.8bn of value realization gains. Stripping out the gain, core profit rose 39% YoY to P6.56bn, well below consensus and Regis estimates.

 

Implied 4Q24 core income of P804m was lower by 1% QoQ, 3% YoY, which management attributed to higher pre-development costs as well as depreciation of new plants now declared operational. We also note that 4Q output was also affected at two RE farms (230MW) from Nov due to typhoon damage and the disruption was carried over into 1Q25.

 

4Q24 core attributable EBITDA up 34% QoQ, 12% YoY to P4.97bn

Derived 4Q24 core attributable EBITDA rose 34% QoQ, 12% YoY to P4.97bn, buoyed by higher prices in Australia and higher output from its solar plants in India and Vietnam.

Its Philippine assets generated P2.1bn of attributable EBITDA in 4Q, up 24% YoY, as higher RE output more than compensated for (a) lower output from its fossil fuel plants and (b) lower spot prices.

 

Capex set to rise further

ACEN spent P44bn (US$0.8bn) in capex in 2024, and the budget for 2025 is P70bn (US$1.2bn). The firm aims to complete ~700MW of attributable RE capacity abroad within 2025, adding to its current operating capacity of 3.3GW.  Another 2GW of capacity could start construction within the next 18 months.

 

Decision on capital-raising soon

Given its capex plans and debt load (attributable net debt is P173bn, with gearing now 1.2x), ACEN needs to raise capital soon. Management expects to announce a decision on this well-flagged exercise as early as next month (April). We believe there are a few capital-raising options, including a rights issue as well as the entry of a strategic investor. These options need not be mutually exclusive.

 
Robinsons Land - Residential segment still struggling in 4Q24
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY24 operating profit rises 2%

Robinsons Land’s FY24 operating profit rose 2% to P17.6bn, behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. With the exception of the residential business, the operating profit of all segments outperformed our expectations. RLC’s standalone (excluding JV) residential business saw a 90% drop in EBIT for FY24.  

 

Operating loss of residential segment widens in 4Q24

Although revenue of the standalone residential segment grew 90% QoQ, its operating loss widened to P434mn, from P210mn in 3Q24. The company blamed this on lumpy items such as: higher marketing expenses, pre-operating expenses on new sales offices, as well as the purchase of new software.

The company expects better residential profitability in 2025, since some of the opex items should come down this year. Furthermore, revenue recognition should pick up in 2H25 thanks to timing of construction and buyer payments.

 

Unsold residential inventory remains high

After net cancellations of P3bn in 3Q24, presales reversed to a net positive of P4.2bn (+3% YoY). Unsold inventory of standalone projects amounts to P48.7bn, c. 7X the full-year presales in FY24 (P7.3bn). Even if we were to assume that the P4.2bn in 4Q24 represents the typical quarter, unsold inventory would still be equivalent to 3 years’ worth of presales.

Meanwhile, for JV projects, 4Q24 presales fell 33% QoQ and 68% YoY to P2.2bn. We believe the company is finding it difficult to sell the remaining inventory since most of the high-end JV projects are over 80% sold. As of FY24, unsold inventory of JV projects amounted to P18bn, about 17 months’ worth of presales.   

 

Demand for middle-income condominiums in Metro Manila remains weak

Management acknowledges that demand for middle-income condominiums in Metro Manila remains weak. However, the company sees better prospects for high-end projects as well as provincial projects. Thus, it plans to launch more provincial projects (particularly horizontal ones) as well as a new tower of its JV project with Shang Properties (SHNG PM), which caters to high-end buyers.  

 

Mall revenue growth slows; office occupancy stays flat QoQ

4Q24 mall revenue grew 8% YoY, slower than the 12% pace in 9M24. Nevertheless, the company said that 4Q24 same-mall revenue growth was 6%, within the typical range of 4%-8%.

For the full-year, mall EBIT grew 22%, as revenue grew 11% and EBIT margin expanded 380 basis points (to 39.9%). In addition to the higher revenue, the margin expansion was largely attributed to lower power costs. 

Meanwhile, office occupancy was flat QoQ at 86%.

 
Filinvest Land - Office revenue improves in 4Q24 but...
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY24 net profit rises 11%

FLI’s FY24 net profit rose 11% to P4.2bn, better than REGIS and consensus forecasts, as margins expanded. Half of the 8ppt improvement in the residential GPM was due to accounting changes, while the other half was largely due to the contribution of ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units, which have higher margins.     

    

Residential sales pick up in provincial areas  

4Q24 presales grew 12% QoQ (-13% YoY) to P3.5bn. As a result, FY24 reservation sales rose 1% to P19.4bn, equivalent to the 2019 level.  Sales in provincial areas grew faster and accounted for 65% of FY24 presales (from 58% in 9M24, 55% in 2023). FLI also placed greater emphasis on liquidating RFO units. Thanks to these efforts, RFO units comprised 40% of FY24 presales. However, unsold inventory (of P64bn) remains high and is equivalent to 40 months of presales.

 

Office revenue rises in 4Q but will likely fall in 1Q25

Office occupancy improved 3ppt QoQ to 61%. 

Although office revenue rose 8% QoQ (+9% YoY) thanks to the lapse of rent-free periods of new tenants, Filinvest will likely see a drop in 1Q25, due to the POGO ban. POGO firms occupied 3% of FLI’s office space and were still paying rent in 4Q24.  

 

Share-for-property swap with Filinvest REIT will be a sale-and-leaseback

It was previously disclosed that Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) will acquire Festival Mall from FLI by issuing 1.626bn new FILRT shares to FLI, increasing FILRT’s shares outstanding by 33% and raising FLI’s ownership by 12ppt to 63%. The 121,000sqm mall in Alabang will increase FILRT’s leasable area by 37%.

 

Upon completion of the sale, FLI will lease the mall back from FILRT under a 30-year contract and sublease to third parties. FLI will pay a fixed rental rate with an annual escalation rate of 5%. FILRT said that the deal could increase its DPS by c. 5%. For FLI, the transaction could dilute NAV/share as it values the mall at only P5bn (based on FILRT’s latest close), lower than our P7.6bn estimate.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Aboitiz Power FY24 core income up +5%

Core profits up 5% to P33.7bn with attributable EBITDA growing 13% to P73bn. Profit grew less than EBITDA as AP started to recognize depreciation and interest cost of GNPD. Headline income was up just 2% to P33.9bn. Energy sales for the generation business was flat YoY. For the distribution utility, residential volumes grew 13% while commercial/industrial volumes were up 5%. Implied overall volumes rose +8%. 

AEV FY24 headline profit falls 23% YoY to P18.1bn on asset impairment

Headline FY24 profit fell to P18.1bn from P23.5bn after the company wrote off P7.4bn of its investment in Republic Cement. Core profit was up 15% YoY to P25.5bn, equivalent to P4.59/sh, broadly in line with consensus estimate of P4.54/sh. Power unit accounted for 59% of FY24 core profit, followed by financial services (20%) and food & beverage (20%) units. Real estate and infrastructure units accounted for 3% and -2%, respectively. The company incurred a headline net loss of P645mn in 4Q24 alone due to the impairment charge. 4Q24 core profit was up 39% YoY to P4.8bn.

DigiPlus profit triples in FY24

FY24 net profit of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) grew 208% to P12.6bn, ahead of consensus forecast. 4Q24 net profit rose 10% QoQ and 95% YoY to P3.9bn. FY24 revenue grew 176% YoY to P75.2bn – implying 45%-50% market share for the full-year. 4Q24 revenue rose 23% QoQ and 110% YoY to P23.6bn – implying 40%-45% market share in 4Q. PAGCOR fees on e-casino games, which comprise majority of PLUS’ revenue, fell from 41.25% in August 2023 to 35% in April 2024. They dropped further to 30% in January 2025 – this will likely cause online operators to increase spending for marketing, driving up revenue further. DigiPlus introduced several new games on its platform in 2024, including Pinoy Drop Ball in September 2024.

IMI suffers another big loss

IMI reported a net loss of US$49.8m for FY24, albeit down from US$105.6m for FY23. Revenues declined by 17% to US$1.1bn. Excluding one-off costs relating to the rationalization of facilities globally, “core” net loss would have been US$24.6m. For 4Q alone, reported loss was US$40.6m. Ayala Corp (AC) owns 52% of IMI through subsidiary AC Industrials. Implied share of AC in the net loss of IMI would be P1.2bn for 4Q alone. AC management recently said it is no longer divesting IMI.

Metro Pacific selling at least 20% stake in tollways unit soon; Maynilad IPO likely in next two quarters

Metro Pacific is in talks with several parties to sell a minimum 20% stake in Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC). MPI owns ~93.4% of MPTC. Share sale will allow MPTC to repay debt. Meanwhile, its 53%-owned water unit, Maynilad, is preparing to conduct an IPO in the next two quarters. Its franchise requires it to sell 30% of its shares to the public by Jan 2027. Maynilad is also a 25%-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM).

ERC orders Meralco to refund overcollections of P20bn

In its 30-page order released on Thursday evening, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) said Meralco must refund a total of P19.96bn over a period of 36 months. Meralco's refund accounts for the difference between its actual weighted average tariff and most recent approved distribution rate of P1.35/kWh from Jul 2022 to Dec 2024. Refund amounts to P0.1189/kwh for all customer classes. Residential customers were granted a refund of P0.2024/kwh. Household rate in Feb 2025 stood at P12.026/kwh. Majority of the commissioners (i.e. Alexis Lumbatan, Floresinda Digal, and Marko Romeo Fuentes) resolved to grant provisional authority. ERC chairperson Monalisa Dimalanta and commissioner Catherine Maceda dissented from the resolution. Meralco has provisioned for the refund so this development will not have a direct P&L impact.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Philippines sends ex-President Duterte to ICC over 'drugs war' killings

Former Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte was arrested in the Manila airport after arriving from Hong Kong, and was flown to the Netherlands (RP-C5219) to face crimes against humanity charges at the International Criminal Court (ICC). The ICC issued an Interpol arrest warrant accusing Duterte of crimes linked to deadly anti-drug crackdowns during his presidency. Vice President Sara Duterte criticized the Marcos administration for handing over her father, arguing the ICC has no jurisdiction, while President Marcos defended the arrest as lawful and not politically motivated, since the Philippines is a member of Interpol.

BSP Governor gives guidance on possible rate cut and further RRR cuts

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Remolona stated that a rate cut by the next Monetary Board meeting (April 10) is “on the table” but will still consider March inflation data prior to making a decision. Remolona stated that the BSP prefers to cut gradually by 25bps each meeting as long as CPI remains on track as well as emphasized the importance of monitoring peso volatility and its impact on inflation. But a 50bps cut or more remains an option should economic conditions deteriorate significantly. Note that the Monetary Board meeting was moved from April 3 to April 10 as March inflation data releases on April 5. He also commented that the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) is still “too high” at 5% even after the recent 200 bps cut that will take effect by March 28. Recall that the BSP held rates steady at 5.75% during its last February meeting due to continued global macro uncertainties. Bloomberg median consensus estimates the BSP to cut by 75 bps to 5.00% this year. We estimate the BSP to cut by 50 bps to 5.25% this year.

PH Dollar reserves up 3.3% at end-February

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-January saw a 3.3% MoM drop to $106.7bn from $103.3bn at the end-January. Primarily reflecting the BSP's net foreign exchange operations and proceeds from its recent global bond sale worth $3.3bn. The level of dollar reserves by end-Feb was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and is equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Reserve position in the IMF decreased 0.2% MoM to $670mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $12.1bn, which was up 2.5% MoM. Foreign investments increased by 3.5% MoM to $89.4bn. Foreign currency deposits went up 5.1% MoM to $787mn. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025.

FDI inches up to US$8.9bn in 2024

Foreign direct investments (FDI) increased by 0.1% YoY to US$8.93bn in 2024, but missed the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas’ (BSP) target of US$9bn. December inflows plummeted 85.1% YoY to US$110mn from US$743mn, marking the lowest monthly level since December 2013 of inflows at US$102.16mn. BSP attributes the decline in December to increased debt repayments by resident corporations to their nonresidential direct investors. For FY24, investments in debt instruments decreased by 4.7% YoY to US$6.23bn and reinvestment of earnings also declined by 11.2% YoY to US$1.17bn. Net equity capital placements, excluding reinvested earnings, jumped by 42.4% YoY to US$1.54bn, driven by a 4.3% increase in total equity placements to US$2.17bn, while withdrawals fell to US$628mn. The sources of capital infusion for 2024 were from Japan, the United Kingdom, the United States, and Singapore, with investments primarily directed to manufacturing, real estate, and information and communication sectors. The BSP expects FDI net inflows to rebound this year, projecting a rise to US$10bn.

DOF to expedite sale of state-owned assets

Finance Undersecretary Catherine Fong stated that the DOF holds a list of assets that may yield around P20bn in revenues this year. Note that this is about 20% of the privatization target as the DOF is expecting unsolicited proposals to bolster its progress towards the 2025 P100bn privatization target, more than double the P42bn target in 2024. She also highlighted that the revised privatization rules should ramp up the process. The key revision being allowing unsolicited bids on top of public auctions—as prior to this, there was no way to directly sell state-owned assets without going through a long process despite buyers expressing interest. Any interested party can submit an unsolicited proposal with the property description, price offer, payment terms, among others. Part of the payment will be the need to submit a bid security of at least 10% of the asset's market value. Some of the state assets for disposal this year include: Food Terminal Inc., Philippine National Construction Corp., Financial Center Area, Ecology Villages, Mile Long Complex, National Housing Authority-Caloocan property, Fil-Eastern Woods Industries Inc., Pioneer Glass Manufacturing Corp., and Mindanao Progress Corp. As well as 28,000 real estate titles in varying sizes also up for sale.

Commercial banks' loan growth accelerates to 12.8% in January; M3 growth decelerates to 6.8%

Commercial bank loans rose by 12.8% YoY in January, mildly accelerating from 12.2% in December. Loans to productive activities rose by 11.8% (from 10.8%), led by utilities (+24%), trade (+14%), real estate (+10%), and transport/storage (+21%). Consumer loans rose by 24% YoY, mildly decelerating from +25%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 6.8% YoY, decelerating from the 7.7% pace in December.

Banking sector NPL rises MoM in January

Preliminary data from the BSP showed that the banking sector’s NPL ratio rose to 3.38% in January, from 3.27% in December 2024. However, this is lower than the 3.44% figure in January 2024. The value of NPL rose 2.5% MoM (+11% YoY) to P512.8bn. NPL coverage fell to 95.3%, from 96.0% in December 2024. Total loans of the banking sector fell 1% MoM but grew 13% YoY to P13.4trn. The past due ratio also increased to 4.17% in January, from 3.95% in December 2024. However, this is lower than the 4.27% figure in January 2024. The value of past due loans rose 5% MoM and 11% YoY to P571.6bn. The ratio of restructured loans also increased to 2.05%, from 2.03% in December 2024. It was 2.26% in January 2024.

Department of Energy launches GEA-4, seeks bids for 10.5GW of RE capacity

The Department of Energy released the terms of reference for the fourth Green Energy Auction (GEA-4), which will involve 10,478 MW of solar, onshore wind, and integrated energy storage technologies nationwide. 8,088 MW of will come from ground-mounted solar, roof-mounted solar, and floating solar, of which Luzon will account for 5,553 MW. 2,390 MW will come from onshore wind projects, of which 1,670 MW will be in Luzon. Delivery of projects will be between 2026-2029, with a supply contract of 20 years from commercial operations. Recall that GEA-3, covering 4,650 MW of mostly hydro capacity, is currently ongoing with results likely out by June.
