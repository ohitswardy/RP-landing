REGIS REPORT

02 May 2025

The PCOMP advanced for a third straight week, rising 2.3% or 143.11 pts WoW to 6,411.86 as global equities continued to recover buoyed by easing trade tensions between China and the US. Foreign flows reverted to net buying, to its highest weekly level since mid-March, which brought the index back above its 100-D MA for the first time since November last year.

 

Beyond the global outlook, investors took cues from the set of 1Q25 corporate earnings that were released this week. SMPH (+6.1% WoW) was the third largest advancer in the index as the market seemed encouraged by the sequential improvement in its residential pre-sales; earnings were just in-line. BPI (+6.1% WoW) and MBT (+5.1% WoW) were the fourth and fifth top gainers in the PCOMP also thanks to upbeat earnings, whereas BDO (-0.6% WoW) underperformed as results missed forecasts. Overall, earnings of the Big 3 banks were marked by still solid double-digit loan growth across the board but also divergent NIM performance.

 

The top performer in the index was BLOOM (+8.1% WoW) as bargain hunting continued from the previous week and kept the share price afloat. The stock is now up 24% from its recent bottom though also still down 24% YTD. Elsewhere in gaming, PLUS (+12.9% WoW) notably climbed to a fresh ATH, coinciding with news that PAGCOR revenues from online gaming surged another 48% YoY in 1Q25. AC (+7.7% WoW) was the second biggest advancer in the PCOMP, catching up to gains in SM (+3.9% WoW) which extended its rebound rally from last week.

 

On macro news, the BSP forecasts inflation to head further down in April, settling between 1.3% to 2.1%, after March inflation clocked at 1.8%. The central bank cited lower prices of rice, fruits, and vegetables from improving domestic supply conditions alongside lower oil prices and peso appreciation. As for other inflation drivers, the Department of Labor and Employment is set to start the review of minimum wage rates this May, amid calls from labor groups for a P200 wage hike which represents a 25% increase from the current national average daily minimum wage of P465. The Department of Agriculture also said it will defer the full implementation of its recently announced P20/kg rice program until after the May 12 mid-term elections.

 

Foreigners were net buyers this week by $38m, the highest level since mid-March, while ADTO also held at a relatively improved $115m.

 

The peso appreciated by another +1.2% WoW to 55.590:$1. Yields declined slightly with the PH10Y -8bps to 6.2603%.

Philippine Strategy - Rice is Life (and Politics) (7) - Rice subsidies and food vouchers announced
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Here come the subsidies – good for consumers, not so much for fiscal deficit

Over the past two days, the government announced 1) heavily subsidized rice at P20/kg, and 2) a P75bn (US$1.3bn) food stamps program. Both programs are supportive of our strategic theme of a recovery in consumer spending, particularly for lower-income families. As we noted last week, the move could also help the popularity of President Marcos, Jr and his coalition though it could be a tad late with the mid-term elections less than three weeks away (12 May). For the “bottom 30%” of households, food is 55% of CPI, with rice alone at 18% (Fig 5), which would make the programs resonate with the poorer households (voters). But the subsidies could also weaken the fiscal position which could have longer-term negative consequences.

 

Rice at P20/kg – fulfilling Marcos Jr’s 2022 campaign promise

Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel, Jr announced a program to sell rice to lower-income households at P20/kg well below market prices (P44-45/kg in Metro Manila), limited to 40kg per month per qualified family. The “P20 rice” was a campaign promise of President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. when he ran in 2022, but was made untenable by the spike in rice prices in 2023 (Figs 1&2). Secretary Tiu Laurel said his initial plan was to offer P20 rice until end-2025, but the President wants to sustain the program until the end of the administration’s term in 2028.

 

“Enhanced” food stamps program worth P74.6bn

The government also approved a project called the Reducing Food Insecurity and Undernutrition with Electronic Vouchers (REFUEL), an “enhanced” version of the existing food stamp program.

It will target 750,000 “food-insecure” families, and is expected to cost P74.6bn (about US$1.3bn) from July 2025 to July 2028.

 

Supports our bullish calls on Consumer stocks

We have consistently called for a recovery in consumer sentiment, particularly for lower-income households, premised on a) sharply lower inflation led by a collapse in regional rice prices, b) minimum wage hikes in 2H24, and c) election spending relating to the midterms on 12 May. The rice subsidies and food stamps programs should lend further support to that theme.

 

Could help bolster Marcos ratings; but watch out for fiscal impact

The announcement could also help bolster the popularity of President Marcos, Jr. and his coalition allies ahead of the 12 May midterm elections. As we reported last week, the President’s approval ratings collapsed in March (Figs 3&4), with those of his rival VP Sara Duterte and her allies rising. It is difficult to estimate how the two programs will affect the fiscal deficit (5.6% of GDP in 2024, Fig 6). The details of the rice subsidy are not yet final. Meanwhile, it is unclear if REFUEL was already part of the 2025 budget.

 

Could the administration adopt/announce even more populist policies? Much will depend, in our view, on how President Marcos’s popularity evolves, and on how his coalition’s candidates perform at the midterms.

 
BDO Unibank - Soft headline 1Q25, but ROE guidance held at 15%
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1Q25 NPAT behind, on front-loaded opex

BDO reported net profit of P19.7bn pre-prefs for 1Q25, up 6% YoY, slightly behind our and Street forecasts. Loan growth slowed a tad to 12% YoY as corporates held back due to global/local uncertainties. PPOP grew just 1% due to an unexpected 16% jump in opex. The bank claims, though, that some expenses were front-loaded, and should moderate in coming quarters. Management was confident of hitting 15% ROE in FY2025E, despite the 13.5% showing in 1Q. We refresh our forecasts in line with BDO’s new accounting (NPAT broadly unchanged), maintaining our P200/sh target price and our Buy rating on the stock.

 

Loans slow to +11.7% YoY, NIMs steady QoQ

1Q25 customer loan growth slowed to 11.7% YoY, from 13.1% in 4Q24, due largely to the corporate segment, which decelerated sharply to 8.7% YoY (from 12.6%) as uncertainties around the US’s tariff wars had companies pause new borrowings. Consumer loans, in contrast, grew 17.3%, faster than the 16.1% pace in 4Q24. The improved loan mix, and the RRR cuts in October helped keep NIMs flat QoQ at 4.31% despite the policy rate cuts last year.

Opex jumps 16%, on “front-loaded” costs; Lower credit costs support NPAT

The bank’s opex unexpectedly jumped 16% YoY, taking cost-to-income up to 60%, and dragging PPOP growth to just 1% YoY. Management explained, though, that some expenses – such as business taxes and IT costs – were front-loaded, and should moderate through the year. NPAT was supported by lower provisions as NPL ratio improved to 1.77%.

 

Introducing 2027E numbers; target price of P200/sh and Buy maintained

We refresh our 2025/26E numbers using the new accounting introduced in 3Q24; the net effect at the NPAT level is negligible. We also introduce 2027E estimates. We maintain our target price of P200/sh, based on our 2025E estimates, using (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.5%, COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%. This represents a total potential return of 31% from current levels. This includes a 2.8% dividend yield, using the bank’s increased quarterly payout from P1.00/sh to P1.10/sh per. Maintain Buy. Key risks include downward pressure on margins from further rate cuts, and intensifying competition.

 
Metrobank - 1Q25 loans +16%; profit in line
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

In line profits; refreshing numbers, maintaining Buy

Metrobank’s 1Q25 came in at P12.3bn, up 2.1% YoY, in line with our estimates for FY25E. Loan growth was 16.1%, a tad slower QoQ but much faster than the 12-13% of its peer banks. NIMs were up 5bps QoQ on lower funding costs as the bank let go of expensive TDs. NPLs blipped up due in part to delayed credit card write-offs. We make minor adjustments to our 2025/26E and add 2027E forecasts. Maintain Buy.

 

Loans +16%, faster than peers; NIMs up 5bps QoQ

Loan growth (gross) slowed to 16.1% (from 17.4%),. Corporate and commercial demand eased from 18% in 4Q to 16% in 1Q25. In contrast, consumer loan growth accelerated to 16%, compared to 14% YoY the previous quarter. Within the consumer segment, auto loans jumped 21% while credit cards were up 18%. NIMs improved 5bps QoQ; the YoY numbers were less comparable given the cross-currency swaps in 2024. While asset yields dipped, funding costs fell faster, as the bank let go of expensive time deposits. Fees were up 11% YoY.

Opex under control; credit costs normalize to 57bps
Metrobank managed to moderate opex growth (+7% YoY), bringing cost-toincome to a low 50.6%. Meanwhile, NPL ratio inched up to 1.6% (from 1.5% in 4Q), though management claims this was due to delayed write-offs of credit card delinquencies. Credit cost normalized to 57bps, compared to the unusually low base of 14bps in 1Q24.

 

Refreshing our numbers; Buy maintained with P100/sh target price

We update our numbers making minor changes to our net profit forecasts for 2025/26E, and introduce new 2027E estimates. We maintain our target price of P100/sh which represents a total potential return of 37%, including a dividend yield of 6.5%. We value the stock on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 12.25%, COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%. We retain our Buy recommendation on the stock. Key risks include margin compression from policy rate cuts, and from intensifying competition.

 
SM Prime - 1Q25 reservation sales improve sequentially; strategic shifts announced
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q25 net profit rises 11%

SM Prime’s 1Q25 net profit rose 11% to P11.7bn as operating profit grew 13% to P16.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. More than the results, however, what we found more noteworthy was the sequential improvement in reservation sales as well as the strategic shifts announced by the company.

 

Presales fall YoY but improve sequentially; reducing unsold inventory levels

1Q25 residential reservation sales rose 45% QoQ (down 57% YoY) to P11.6bn. Furthermore, the company is targeting c. 10% growth in FY25E “core” sales (to P60bn), implying that presales should improve further in the coming quarters.

 

Management is clearly cautious about the Metro Manila mid-end condominium segment and is seeking to reduce its inventory level.

The company did not launch any residential projects in 1Q25 and only plans to launch one “core” project this year (worth P7bn), which will be a provincial development.

 

Deeper foray into high-end residential segment

SMPH is more optimistic about the prospects of the high-end residential segment and plans to increase its exposure. It will launch one high-end vertical (condominium) project and one high-end horizontal (lot-only) project this year.

 

Commercial lots to be sold

Another strategic change for SM Prime is the plan to start selling commercial lots in its new townships. This will generate cash and accelerate the development of its master-planned communities.

 

Mall revenue grows 7% YoY

Systemwide mall revenue grew 7% YoY, similar to 4Q24 (and FY24). This likely means that Philippine same-mall revenue growth was 5%-6%, within the historical range of 4%-8%.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Excessive underperformance; reducing target price but maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Sell-off overdone; Maintain Buy

We consider the decline of BLOOM shares to be excessive and view the current weakness as a buying opportunity. BLOOM has fallen 70% YoY, underperforming the PCOMP by 62% over the period. The stock now trades at just 6.7X FY25E EV/EBITDA, offering 38% total return to our revised target price of P4.40/share. Thus, we maintain our Buy rating. 

 

Expanding its physical and online presence

We expect Solaire Entertainment City (SEC) to face challenges near-term, but Bloomberry’s second site, Solaire North (SN), as well as its enhanced online platform, should help compensate, leading to net profit CAGR of 110% from 2025E-2027E for Bloomberry.

 

As the only integrated resort in the most populous city in Metro Manila, SN is forecast to grow GGR to P28bn in FY27E, from only P8.4bn in FY24. Similarly, we expect the company’s online business to grow to about 10% of total GGR in 2027E, from only c. 5% in FY24. This is partly due to the cost advantage of integrated resorts, which are only charged 29% of e-casino GGR by the government (vs 38% of GGR for pure online operators).

Twin challenges on its home turf

On the other hand, SEC is set to face challenges on both demand and supply. Demand from its two largest foreign markets, China and Korea, are likely to weaken. Meanwhile, Suntrust (SUN PM) will open a new resort which will add 20% to table capacity and 13% to slot capacity in Entertainment City (in 4Q25E).

 

Revising estimates; profit to grow at 110% CAGR from 2025E-2027E

We revise our estimates in light of the challenges facing SEC, the launch of the enhanced online platform (in June 2025E), and the expected ramp-up of SN. As a result, we lower our FY25E/26E EBITDA forecasts by 29%-31%.

 

Lowering target price

We reduce our target price by 58% to P4.40/share. This new target is based on DCF and represents 7.5X FY25E EV/EBITDA, lower than the current 7.8X of Macau gaming firms, and much lower than BLOOM’s pre-COVID average of 11X.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco 1Q25 profit rises 11% YoY

Meralco reported 1Q25 net income of P10.5bn (+9% YoY) and core income of P11.2bn (+11% YoY). The sustained strength in the generation segment helped drive overall profitability. Distribution volume growth saw a marked deceleration, rising by just 2% YoY in 1Q25 due partly to high base effects from last year’s warmer temperatures, as well as the adverse impact of POGO closures. The generation business grew 1Q25 profits by 25% YoY to P3.4bn, boosted by (a) better plant availability across all its fossil fuel-based plants, as well as (b) lower fuel costs and higher contracted prices at Pacific Light. “Chromite”, its newly-acquired LNG unit, contributed P660mn before financing expenses, which includes a little more than a month’s worth of profits from the two operating units of Excellent Energy (EERI).

RRHI 1Q25 headline profit down 85% YoY

Robinsons Retail's (RRHI) 1Q25 headline net profit dipped 85% YoY to P760mn due to a P4.4bn one-time gain from the BPI-RBC merger back in 1Q24. Excluding the one-time gain, 1Q recurring net profit is up 9% to P760mn, broadly in line with Regis and consensus expectations. Both revenue and EBIT are trending within expectations at P47.8bn (+4%) and P1.9bn (+3%), respectively. 1Q consolidated SSSG improved to +3.0% versus +0.9% the same period last year on the back of the improved performances from the Food (+3.0% vs +1.7%) as well as the recovery of the Department (+5.0% vs -2.1%) and Specialty store (+5.1% vs -7.1%) segments.

Aboitiz Power 1Q25 core profit drops 40%

In a press release, Aboitiz Power reported 1Q25 net income stood at P4.6bn, lower than P7.9bn last year. Excluding FX and derivative gains, core profit was 40% YoY lower at P4.7bn. Both headline and core profits are trending behind consensus forecast of P35.3bn. Beneficial EBITDA fell 8% YoY to P15bn, which management blamed on lower spot prices and scheduled plant outages. Generation and RES EBITDA was 10% YoY lower at P13.1bn, on energy sales of 8,650 GWH (-2% YoY). Distribution EBITDA was 4% YoY lower at P2.0bn, due to non-recurring refund of regulatory reset fees (energy sales were not disclosed).

ACEN sees robust growth in 2026, 2027, despite slower growth expectations this year

ACEN Corp. expects its earnings growth in 2025 to be “a little bit slower” but should accelerate sharply in 2026 and 2027 as it completes new projects, according to ACEN CFO Jonathan Back. ACEN expects some moderation in its 2025 earnings growth, as it does not have as much new capacity coming online. Recall that ACEN’s core profit before pref divs jumped 39% YoY to P6.6bn in 2024, after completing 1,632 MW worth of attributable RE capacity. ACEN president and CEO Eric Francia said the proceeds of its planned rights issue would be used to bankroll the company’s projects and finance its maturing obligations. ACEN is currently constructing 2,315 MW and has committed to build another 1,379 MW of capacity. Its operational capacity currently stands at 3,284 MW.

Belle's share in COD Manila gaming revenue rises 8% YoY

Belle Corp. (BEL PM) 1Q25 share of gaming revenue from City of Dreams Manila rose 8% YoY (down 45% QoQ) to P433mn. Belle has an EBITDA sharing agreement with Melco Resorts (200 HK) for the operations of City of Dreams Manila.

Belle hopes to get license for Clark casino this year

Belle Corp (BEL PM) hopes to get a license for casino in Clark by the end of the year. The company will spend US$200mn to US$300mn for the Clark casino. It has already obtained a Letter of No Objection.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP guides for April inflation to settle within 1.3 to 2.1%

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) forecasts April inflation to remain low and be between 1.3% to 2.1%. The prior month ahead inflation forecast range was 1.7% to 2.5%, with March inflation at 1.8%. Note that the inflation rate has continued to be on a downtrend since 2.9% in Jan to 2.1% in Feb, and to 1.8% in March. Easing prices of rice, fruits, and vegetables from improving domestic supply conditions alongside lower oil prices and peso appreciation contributed to downward price pressures. But this may be offset by higher electricity rates and LRT-1 fares. The gov't targets to keep inflation within 2-4% in 2025. Bloomberg median consensus for the full year average is at 2.8%. We expect inflation to average 3.0%. The Philippine Statistics Authority will announce March inflation figures on May 6, Tuesday. The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of six this year) will be on June 19.

NEDA: Inflation downtrend likely continued in April

The National Economic and Development Authority's (NEDA) projected inflation to continue easing in April. Inflation declined for two consecutive months from 2.9% in Jan to 2.1% in Feb, and to 1.8% in Mar. This brings the 1Q25 inflation at 2.3%, lower than 2.6% the previous quarter. The downtrend was primarily driven by slower increases in food and transportation costs. NEDA Chief Arsenio M. Balisacan stated that easing inflation should support household consumption, while recent interest rate cuts by the BSP can stimulate both consumption and investments. However, he cautioned about the lingering global macroeconomic uncertainties that may negatively impact the domestic economy.

March budget deficit at P376bn; 1Q budget deficit widens 76% YoY to P479bn

The government recorded a budget deficit of P374.6bn which is wider both 91.8% YoY and 119.2% MoM. This brings the 1Q25 deficit to P478.8bn, 75.6% wider YoY. Revenue collections in Mar inched down 3.0% YoY to P279.3bn, as non-tax revenues dipped 71.0% YoY. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections jumped 20.9% to P175.7bn mainly due to ongoing revenue enhancement measures such as the crackdown on fake receipts, illicit trade, as well as improvements in digitalization and tax payment facilities. Customs saw higher growth, up 7.3% to P80.4bn in collections from higher VAT and excise collections. Non-tax revenues dropped by 71.0% to P19.6bn due to the earlier timing of remittances from Government Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs) last year. The gov't expects this to improve in the succeeding months as dividends from various GOCCs are set to be remitted to the National Treasury starting May. Expenditures surged 35.4% YoY in Mar to P655bn mainly driven by higher primary spending. Primary spending for the month jumped 36.3% YoY to P652.9bn, likely due to accelerated spendings prior to the midterm elections. The increase in primary spending was attributed to the (a) capital expenditures of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), (b) health and social protection programs. The Bureau of Treasury commented that with the upcoming dividend remittances and other non-tax revenues expected in the coming quarters, and spending continuing to be on track, the 2025 fiscal deficit is seen to stay within the P1.5-tn target. The gov't for 2025 targets the budget deficit to narrow to 5.3% of GDP from 5.7% in 2024, and the government aims to further reduce this to 3.7% in 2028.

Trade deficit widens to $4.1bn in Mar

The March trade deficit widened to US$4.1bn, up 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in imports than exports. This brings the 1Q25 trade deficit to P12.7bn, 13% up YoY. Imports for the month grew 12% YoY to US$10.7bn, a reversal from the 2% decline in Feb. Primarily driven by the growth in imports across all categories except fuel. Exports grew 6% YoY to US$6.59bn, the third month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction. Electronics exports were flat at $3.64bn, a turnaround from the 3% YoY increase in Feb. These made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 13% YoY to US$2.95bn.

Minimum wage rates review to start this May

The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) is set to start the review of minimum wage rates in some regions and National Capital Region (NCR) in the month of May. Labor consultation and employer consultation will be conducted in May, while a public hearing will be held in June. The result of the wage deliberations is expected to be announced in July. Numerous labor groups have been urging the President to certify the P200 legislated wage hike bill for private sector workers as urgent before the 19th Congress ends on June 30, 2025. The P200 wage hike bill (House Bill 11376) was approved on second reading last February and is currently on the third and final reading in The House of Representatives. While The Senate's version of the bill (Senate Bill No. 2534) which seeks a P100 daily minimum wage hike has been approved on third and final reading last February. Note that a bill becomes law only when it is passed by both houses of Congress, then processed through a bicameral conference committee, and approved by the President. Recall that last July 2024, the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved the P35 hike to P645 for the daily minimum wage of workers in NCR. To note, 16 regions have also implemented wage hikes and 28 wage orders have been approved since June 2024. The last legislated (nationwide) wage hike that was approved was back in 1989 which created the RTWPBs and set a P25 daily wage increase. President Marcos in his Labor Day speech mentioned that they are listening to the call for better wages as the regional wage boards are reviewing current pay rates. But he also emphasized the importance of considering the effect wage hikes will have on the growth of businesses, jobs, and the economy.

P20 rice rollout postponed to May 13

The Department of Agriculture (DA) will defer the full implementation of the P20/kg rice program until after the May 12 midterm elections. This comes after the Commission on Elections' (Comelec) recommendation to defer the program until after the ongoing 10-day ban on the distribution of all forms of "ayuda" (welfare and social assistance) as LGUs (local gov't units) partly subsidize the program implementation.

P20/kg rice pilot run expands to additional LGUs

The Department of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Fransico P. Tiu Laurel Jr. announced that the P20/kg rice will be sold at KADIWA centers (DA backed stores) and participating LGUs starting May 2. This comes after the program was initially launched in Visayas but has now expanded to select locations such as San Juan City, San Jose del Monte in Bulacan, Camarines Sur, and Mati City in Davao Oriental. Sale of P20 rice at KADIWA centers will only be available to vulnerable consumers (indigents, senior citizens, solo parents, PWDs); while LGUs can make the cheaper rice available for all regardless of vulnerability status. Based on the current program budget of P4.5bn, the program is expected to last until Dec 2025. The P4.5bn project cost will be shouldered by the contingency fund of the Office of the President. The DA Secretary noted that a full-scale subsidy for all Filipinos is not feasible, but he is finalizing a recommendation to the President for the nationwide implementation of the P20 rice to be available to 50% of the population.

Department of Finance withdraws proposal to hike capital gains tax

Finance Secretary Ralph G. Recto has withdrawn his department’s proposal to increase capital gains tax, donor’s tax and estate tax, citing higher revenue collections in the first quarter. These tax increases were previously sought to be included in the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA). Secretary Recto cited these amendments in a letter to House Ways and Means Committee Chairperson and Albay Rep. Jose Ma. Clemente S. Salceda. The DoF’s withdrawal of its proposed tax hikes is considered moot since the CMEPA was already ratified by Congress last February. The bill is set to be transmitted to Malacañang for the President’s signature. Among the CMEPA bill’s key measures include (a) a reduction of stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1% and (b) a reduction in the dividend tax on foreign entities from 25% to 10%.

BPO sector to hit US$40bn in revenue this year

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it would hit its US$40bn revenue target this year. This represents c. 5% growth from US$38bn in 2024. It is also projecting the sector to employ 2mn Filipinos in 12 to 18 months. The sector had 1.82mn employees at the end of 2024.

Digital payments re-surge in March, up 45% YoY, partly on low-base

Payments made digitally over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped sharply to P1,843bn in March, a new record-high month, up 44.5% YoY, compared to the 33.7% pace in February. Year-to-date, the 1Q25 total stood at P5,292bn, up 38.8% YoY, faster than the 35.4% booked in FY2024. For PesoNet – large-ticket, batched transactions – March transactions came in at P1,017bn, also a new record, up 38.3%, accelerating from the 26.9% the previous month. This brought the 3M24 figure up to P3,003bn, up 32.4%, compared to the 28.5% for FY24. Meanwhile, InstaPay – real-time payments of up to P50,000 – saw payments explode +52.9% YoY to P825.7bn, also a new all-time high, and growing much faster than the 43.6% clocked in February. Year-to-date, P2,289bn went through InstaPay, up 48.3% YoY, accelerating from the 46.3% growth for the whole of 2024.

Colliers: Office vacancy stays flat in 1Q25

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy remained flat at 19.7% in 1Q25. Makati CBD vacancy fell 1ppt to 7.2%. Vacancy in the Manila Bay area rose to 41% in 1Q25, from 35% in 4Q24. Net office take-up hit 36,000sqm 1Q25, from net closures of 185,000sqm in 4Q24. This is despite 82,000sqm in POGO closures. Office rental rates in most areas fell c. 1% QoQ. Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 22% by end-2025. While it expects net take-up to hit 150,000sqm, it expects about 600,000sqm in new supply. Residential vacancy was also relatively flat QoQ at 24.3% (from 23.9% in 4Q24).

PAGCOR revenue from gaming operations rises 14% in 1Q25

PAGCOR’s 1Q25 revenue from gaming operations rose 14% YoY to P25.5bn. Revenue from electronic games grew 48% YoY to P14.32bn. Revenue from licensed casinos increased 3% YoY to P8.32bn. Revenue from PAGCOR-operated casinos fell 22% YoY to P2.88bn.
