REGIS REPORT

26 December 2025

Philippine shares closed the shortened trading week on a firmer note, with the index rebounding to back above the 6,000 level once again, despite thin liquidity and muted participation. Average daily turnover this three-day week slipped to $89m, while Friday trading was particularly subdued at sub-$50m with many traders already away for the Christmas and New Year holidays. With one final trading session remaining for 2025, the PCOMP finished higher by 2.4% or 144.77 pts WoW to 6,065.64, providing a modest year-end lift to sentiment.

Foreigners extended their net selling streak into a fifth consecutive week WoW, although the pressure was largely absorbed by local buying amid the light volumes. 

All sectors finished higher WoW, led by Mining at +8.4% WoW as gold continued to chase historic highs. Cyclical sectors notably drove much of the broader rally, with Property up +3.6% WoW and Financials gaining +3.4% WoW. The two biggest banks BDO (+5.5% WoW) and BPI (+3.1% WoW) and the two largest developers SMPH (+6.3% WoW) and ALI (+5.4% WoW) were four of the five top positive movers for the index. Meanwhile, YTD-top gainer ICT (+3.7% WoW) extended its run, while bottom-fishing activity emerged in some of the year’s laggards such as URC (+3.9% WoW) and AEV (+7.4% WoW).

The peso was slightly weaker for the week, -0.1% WoW to 58.765:$1, and has so far depreciated by 1.3% YTD which is the second-worst performing currency in Asean after the Indonesian rupiah (-3.7% YTD). The Philippine benchmark is still down by 7.1% so far YTD, the second-worst performer in Asean after Thailand (-10.1% YTD) and bucking a generally green region for 2025.

ICTSI’s Manila port, MICT, throughput reaches 3mn TEUs in 2025; Implies 4Q25 volume contraction?

Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) handled a record 3mn TEUs as of December 22. This suggests that 4Q25 volumes may reach 778k TEUs (assuming full quarter adjustment), down 2% YoY. Recall that 9M25 volumes at MICT rose 8% YoY to 2.3mn TEUs. We estimate MICT’s full-year 2025 volumes may have reached 3.08mn TEUs, +5% higher than the port’s 2024 throughput of 2.9mn TEUs but mildly short our full-year forecast of 3.1mn TEUs (+6%). We estimate MICT accounts for ~20% of EBITDA and 23% of NAV. Note that consensus and REGIS’ FY25E EPS growth rates of +22% YoY and +21% YoY, respectively, imply a degree of softness in 4Q, given 9M25 actual EPS growth of +24%. ICTSI trades at 10x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 17x PER, below peer average (12.8x/18x) and historical average (11x/21x).

Megaworld sells MREIT shares

Megaworld sold 98mn MREIT shares at P13.50/share, with total proceeds of P1.3bn. This is a 4% discount to the previous closing price. At that price, RCR offers an FY26E dividend yield of 7.9% (based on consensus forecast). We estimate that MEG ownership in MREIT fell from 56.6% to 54.0%. MEG is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.2X FY26E P/E. MREIT currently offers 7.6% FY26E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.0%.

Rockwell Land acquires 75% stake in Alabang Town Center

Rockwell Land (ROCK PM) acquired a 74.8% stake in Alabang Town Center (ATC) from the Madrigal Group. ATC has a GLA of 137,000sqm and sits on a 17-hectare parcel of land. The acquisition will expand Rockwell Land’s leasing portfolio by 58%. ROCK will pay P21.6 in three equal tranches on 22 December 2025, 15 December 2026, and 15 December 2027. The Madrigal Group acquired a 50% stake in ATC from Ayala Land earlier this month and will pay P13.5bn in three tranches. Rockwell Land’s acquisition price is 7% higher than the recent purchase price of the Madrigal Group. Rockwell Land Chairman and CEO Nestor Padilla said that the property has long-term potential and Rockwell is planning its redevelopment.

Sumitomo Mitsui increases stake in RCBC's to 24.5%

Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation (SMBC) acquired an additional 4.46% equity stake in the Rizal Commercial Banking Corp. (RCBC PM) for P6.4bn—raising total ownership to 24.46%. In 2023, SMBC previously raised its stake in RCBC to 20% from its initial 5% stake in 2021. The disclosure did not state whether SMBC purchased primary or secondary shares. We estimate that if SMBC purchased secondary shares, then it paid c. P59.30/share, equivalent to 0.97X 3Q25 P/B and 137% higher than the last closing price. SMBC is the primary banking arm of Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group Inc. (8316 JP) which is the holding company for the bank and other subsidiaries and affiliates (i.e., SMBC Trust Bank, SMBC Nikko Securities, etc.). SMBC has been expanding its presence in other emerging markets such as India, Indonesia, and Vietnam.

Government posts P158bn budget deficit in Nov; P1.26trn deficit as of 11M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P158bn in Nov, 26% narrower YoY as gov't spending continued to decline. This brings the 11M25 deficit to P1.26trn, 7% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was said to be in line with the P1.56trn full-year program and the gov't's "commit to prudent fiscal management and consolidation efforts." The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues. Revenue collections in Nov were flattish, only up 0.7% YoY, given the modest 3% growth tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were up by 3%. Recall that BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza stated that the slowdown in consumer and gov't spending led to lower-than-expected collections this year. Customs collections likewise rose 3%. Recall that the Bureau of Customs (BOC) stated that foregone revenues due to illicit trade are estimated to be around P150bn in 2025 (vs. just P30-40bn last year). Non-tax revenues dipped 42% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024. Expenditures continued to decline by 10% YoY in Nov due to the decrease in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 13%. Similar to the previous four months, the continued gov't spending decline YoY in Nov was primarily caused by the slowdown in infra spending due to the flood control scandal. Interest payments rose 16% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

BOP posts US$225mn deficit in Nov; GIR settles at US$111.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Nov posted a deficit of US$225mn, a reversal from the US$706mn surplus in Oct but narrower than the $2.3bn deficit in Nov 2024. The narrower deficit YoY in Nov reflected the seasonal rise in remittances which offset higher imports and softer exports. This brings the YT-Nov deficit at US$4.8bn, reversing from the US$2.1bn surplus in the same period last year. The reversal to a YTD deficit stems from earlier front-loaded imports of capital goods and energy as well as the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects the BSP's expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Nov stood at US$111.3bn, up from the US$110.2bn in end-Oct. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to slightly decrease from $106.3bn in 2024 to $106.0bn in 2025.

Dept. of Agriculture targets to expand P20/kg rice program for mid-income homes

The Dept of Agriculture (DA) is preparing to expand the gov't's P20/kg subsidized rice program to also include lower-middle-income households and other population groups. The program is currently also open to indigent sectors (e.g., solo parents, PWDs, minimum wage earners, etc.). The broader goal is to reach 15mn Filipino households by 2026 with the initial launch to begin in eight provinces in Jan-Feb next year. The purchase limit is set at P30/kg a month for senior citizens and P10/kg a month for other beneficiaries. The subsidized rice program budget is set to be P23bn with P4bn from the President's contingency fund, P9bn from the National Food Authority (NFA) and P10bn from the general appropriations fund. DA Secretary Fransico Tiu Laurel stated that eligible households are to register in the digital platform for the P20/kg rice program for proper documentation and smoother collection of allocated rice.

Consumer loans +21.3% in 3Q25; Credit card NPLs blip up

Consumer loans in the Philippine banking system grew 21.3% YoY in 3Q25 to P3,537bn, just slightly faster than the 21.1% in 2Q25, but still setting a new fastest YoY pace since 2015. Consumers now make up 22.7% of total loans, up from 22.3% in 2Q, to a new record high. Growth was driven by credit cards, up 29% YoY, and salary loans, up 37.3% YoY. As a group, unsecured loans (including “others”) were up 31.6% YoY, and now account for 47.5% of consumer loans. For perspective, that was just under 30% at end-2019 pre-Covid. Meanwhile, consumer loan NPL ratio stood at 5.38%, inching up from the 5.33% in 2Q, driven largely by a 31bps QoQ jump in credit card NPLs ratio. Mortgages grew 10.9% YoY to P1,178bn, maintaining the pace set in 1Q/2Q, while NPL ratio was 6.38% improving 5bps QoQ and 40bps YoY. This runs slightly counter to the narrative of a slowing residential property sector. It does indicate that the secondary market is still active, compared to the primary / pre-sale market. Motor Vehicle (MV) loans were up 17.3% YoY, slowing slightly from the 17.6% in 2Q, with autos up 17.2% and motorcycles up 20.6%. MV NPL ratio fell 9bps QoQ and 46bps YoY to 4.90%, as auto NPLs were down 8bps to 4.25% and two-wheel NPLs improved 34bps QoQ to 16.14%. The data here, too, seems inconsistent with the CAMPI car loan data that shows weak new car sales figures. Credit card receivables continued to surge 29.5% YoY to P1,094bn, nearing the levels of mortgages. To the downside, credit card NPL ratio blipped 4.82% up 31bps QoQ and 47bps YoY. Salary loans jumped 37.3% YoY, while NPLs improved to 4.57%, down 8bps QoQ and a massive114bps YoY.

Digital payments breach P2.3trn in Nov, +41% YoY

The National Retail Payments System (NRPS) recorded a new record-high P2,305bn worth of digital transactions in November 2025, up 41.0% YoY, This puts the 11M25 figure at P22,054bn, up 41.2% YoY, faster than the 35% clip in FY2024. As always, recall that the NRPS data only counts payments made between platforms (ie Bank A to Bank B, or Wallet A to Wallet B, etc), and does not include payments made within an institution (ie BDO-to-BDO or GCash-to-GCash, etc). Larger, batched transactions over PesoNet grew 26.8% YoY to P1,165bn, faster than the 23.7% pace set in October. The PHP figure though, was a shade lower MoM compared to October. Year-to-date, PesoNet payments totaled P11,845bn, up 30% YoY just a tad faster than the 29% YoY recorded in FY24. For InstaPay, which is for real-time transfers up to P50,000 (about $852), November payments amounted to P1,140bn, up 59.2% YoY, slower than the 66.1% posted in October. That said, the 11M25 growth rate of 56% YoY is still much faster than the 46% pace set in FY24. Perhaps more importantly, the volume of InstaPay transactions continues to surge, up 320% YoY for the month. This indicates further broadening out of digital adoption for ever-widening use case for the service, for smaller and smaller value transactions.

Trust AUMs hit P8.2trn in 3Q25, up 14% YoY

Trust AUMs of entities regulated by the BSP grew 13.7% YoY to P8,198bn (about $140bn). While the YoY growth decelerated from the +20.4% clip in 2Q25, the AUMs were still up 3.4% QoQ. Growth was pretty even between the banks (+13% YoY) and the non-banks (+15%), though we note that an increasing number of banks – BPI, RCBC, and UBP – have spun off their trust operations into “stand-alone trusts” which are technically classified as non-banks. BDO (BDO PM) remained the dominant player, with consolidated AUMs of P2,461bn, up 12.3% YoY, and accounting for a market share of 30.0%. That said, the other banks are catching up. BPI (BPI PM) grew its AUMs 13.8% YoY to P1,731bn, enough for 21.6% of the market. Most notable was Metrobank (MBT PM) whose AUMs exploded 54.1% YoY to P844bn, with market share crossing 10%. Together, the Big Three banks account for 61.4% of total AUMS.

Department of Finance issues mining IRR

The Department of Finance (DOF) issued the Implementing Rules and Regulations of RA 12253 or the Enhanced Fiscal Regime for Large-Scale Metallic Mining Act. The DOF expects P6.3bn in additional revenues annually. RA 12253 streamlines the taxation/royalties on mining (see below). The filing and payment of royalties on locally produced or extracted metallic minerals or mineral products shall be made within 60 days after the end of the calendar quarter. The share of each LGU shall be released directly and immediately to the provincial, city, municipal, or barangay treasurer without the need for further action and without being subject to any lien or holdback that may be imposed by the national government. The DOF, Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall jointly issue the necessary rules and regulations for a streamlined disbursement scheme.

Government releases implementing rules on 99-year land leases

The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI ) and the Board of Investments (BOI) announced the signing of the implementing rules and regulations for Republic Act No. 12252, which allows foreigners to lease land for up to 99 years. The maximum of 99 years is in line with Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia. Previously, foreigners were only permitted to lease land for up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years (total of 75 years). Under the IRR, landowners are required to have leased contracts annotated on the land title. The DTI said this ensures that lease agreements are binding to the public and strengthens legal protection for both landowners and lessees. Foreigners will be allowed to sublease properties unless their contracts prohibit them from doing so. The lease contract can be terminated if the foreign investor fails to commence the project within three years of signing. The law allows long-term land lease for “the establishment of industrial estates, factories, assembly or processing plants, agro-industrial enterprises, land development for industrial or commercial use, tourism, agriculture, agro-forestry, ecological conservation and other similar priority productive endeavors.”
