REGIS REPORT

16 April 2025

With Philippine markets shutting for Holy Week holidays, we end the shortened trading week with the PCOMP finally snapping a five-week losing streak, +0.9% or 52.18 pts WoW to 6,134.62. However, this was on very light trading activity with ADTO plummeting to $73m from $174m the previous week as local participation remained soft ahead of the break.

 

Top index gainer CNVRG (+8.7% WoW) has bounced to clock a fresh 52-week high after the recent correction. But the biggest positive index movers were MER (+5.6% WoW) which continued to hit new highs amid investors' search for yields, as well as recent underperformer JFC (+8.3% WoW) which finally saw some relief after four straight weekly declines. This week, JFC management had downplayed the impact of Trump tariffs on its business, mainly on the sourcing aspect, but acknowledged that any effect would be 'indirect'.

 

On the macro front, latest BSP comments will see the central bank review its 2-4% inflation target range, with Gov. Remolona saying 3% as a midpoint looks high and citing the continued decline in key inflation items such as rice, vegetables, and oil. Meanwhile, NEDA says hitting the higher end of its 6-8% GDP target range looks unlikely due to heightened global uncertainties. Sec. Balisacan adds that 1Q GDP could still have hit the lower end of the range, considering falling inflation and support from domestic consumption.

 

Elsewhere, the local politics scene is also heating up. The latest Pulse Asia survey has revealed Senatorial candidates that are allied with former Pres. Duterte have moved up the polls, though the top 8 continue to be from the Marcos administration slate. Note the survey was taken after Duterte's arrest and extradition to The Hague by the ICC.

 

Foreigners continued to dominate market activity with 62% participation. Net foreign flow also reverted to positive for the week, at $6.0m.

 

The peso strengthened by another 0.4% WoW to 56.746:$1.

Puregold - FY24 results: A 4Q rebound
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Profits rise +12% on 4Q jump in revenue and profits

Puregold (PGOLD) reported FY24 results yesterday showing revenues rising +10% to P219bn. Profits, meanwhile, jumped +12% to P10.4bn, meaningfully better than Street expectations. 4Q numbers were particularly strong, reversing what the Street might have viewed as a disappointing 3Q. Revenues for 4Q alone were up +12%, while gross profits grew +20%.

 

Revenue growth picks up in 4Q

Revenues for 4Q alone were up 12% YoY, with Puregold stores growing 11% and S&R delivering 15%. The top line numbers were driven by the continued growth in SSS carried over from 3Q. Puregold saw SSSG of 7% while S&R had an even faster 12% for the final quarter.

 

Overall gross margins improved to 15.5% in 4Q, as the company saw less need to price as aggressively as it did in 3Q. We view this as a small sign that the environment for the consumer as well as on competition are improving.  Note 4Q net profit growth (+77% YoY) was magnified by a ~P600m expense relating to its retirement plan booked the previous year.

 

Bounce likely to be carried over into 1Q25

Management indicated that the healthy 4Q operating results are being carried over into 1Q25.  

So far, SSSG for Puregold has been in the “5-7%” range while SSSG for S&R is “positive”, albeit lower than 2024.

 

Modest guidance

The company is guiding for revenue growth of 6-8% and for gross margins to be broadly the same as 2024. We find guidance to be on the conservative side, particularly for the top line. Note PGOLD is aiming to increase store count for Puregold by 6% (30 stores) and for S&R by 10% (3 stores), and SSSG is likely to be positive for the year.

 

Puremart likely to be an important part of future strategy

Puremart, which PGOLD acquired for P568m, had 153 stores with an average store size of 150sqm. This purchase provides PGOLD with a jump-start entry into what is the fastest growing area of grocery retail: the small-format “proximity” shopping segment.

 

Dividend yield of 7%

PGOLD declared dividends payable in two tranches: one this May (P1.09/share) and the other in Sept (P0.72/share). The dividends are the highest the company has ever paid and represent approximately half of profits for FY24. The combined cash payouts are equivalent to 7% dividend yield on yesterday’s closing price.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Philippine Seven FY24 net profit P3.8bn (+9% YoY)

SEVN FY24 net profit P3.8bn (+9% YoY), behind consensus estimates. For 4Q alone, profits were unchanged at P3.8bn (+1% YoY) and revenues were only up 7% YoY to P23.8bn, which is a significant slowdown from the 16% YoY growth seen in the 9M24. The earnings miss stems from the rather soft second half of the year, with SSS actually contracting in 4Q. 4Q24 SSSG dropped to -2.3%, the first such quarterly decline since the pandemic. The unusual level of disruption from the 4Q typhoons along with system outages from a key e-wallet partner negatively impacted foot traffic. Operating margins in the 4Q were up 1.9 ppts QoQ as supplier rebates usually kick-in during year-end, while YoY, operating margins were only up 30 bps as operating leverage was offset by higher utility and labor costs as well operational efficiency investments. Net margins for the FY were down 30 bps YoY due to a higher effective tax rate.

Emperador FY24 net income down 27% to 6.32bn

EMI's 4Q24 net income came in at P1.45bn which improved QoQ +43% but was down 22% YoY. Profit from brandy dipped 51% YoY into negative territory (-P62bn) on lower revenues and continued expenses, this offset the net income from whisky (P1.21bn +16% YoY) as revenues were more resilient. 4Q Brandy segment revenues were down 7% YoY despite the boost from holiday spending. Management commented that downtrading to bottom-shelf products was persistent which likely tempered any further gains from the seasonally strong quarter. Gross margin was down -3ppts to 17.9% due to elevated input costs and peso weakness. Whisky revenues in the 4Q were up 11% YoY as demand in the category was more resilient, while margins followed the positive topline momentum. The segment now contributes to 71% of EMI's profit in 2024 vs. 58% the previous year.

Jollibee comments that Trump tariff impact will be 'indirect'

Jollibee Foods Corp. (JFC PM) downplayed the impact of Trump's global tariffs on its business as the company stated that any effect would be 'indirect'. JFC stated that most of the raw materials for its US products were sourced from North America but did not specify which raw materials were sourced locally. Although the tariffs were put on a 90 day pause, the company continues to assess the potential impact of the reciprocal tariffs on the business. The company stated that they are prepared to adapt their sourcing strategies as needed to bolster supply chain stability and operational efficiency. JFC out of its 9,766 global store network has 369 stores total in North America which include the Jollibee, Red Ribbon, Chowking, and Smashburger brands. These four contribute 11.9% of JFC's SWS in 2024 which is down from 11.9% the previous year due to weakness in Smashburger. 6.5% is from Jollibee, 4.0% from Smashburger, 0.9% from Red Ribbon, and 0.5% from Chowking. For 2025, management guided for 8-21% SWS growth, 4-6% SSSG, 700-800 (+4-8%) new stores.

Meralco franchise renewal signed into law

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. on April 11 signed into law the Meralco franchise extension. With the extension, the company will have the authority to distribute power to Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Rizal, and select areas in Batangas, Laguna, Quezon, and Pampanga until 2053. Recall that, the House version was approved back in Nov, while the Senate approved its version in Feb. In a statement, Meralco Chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan stated that Meralco will engage in modernization initiatives to ensure resilience from climate-related disruptions and achieve energy independence.

Meralco partners with French firm for nuclear energy development study

Meralco signed a two-year memorandum of cooperation with state-controlled French multinational electric utility company Electricite de France SA (EDF). Cooperation involves the feasibility study of developing nuclear energy source. Under the Philippine nuclear energy roadmap, the country is targeting to have at least 1,200 megawatts (MW) of nuclear energy capacity by 2032, scaling this up to 2,400 MW by 2040 and 4,800 MW by 2050.

Cebu Air pax volume growth decelerates to 19% in March on timing of Easter break; 1Q25 pax volumes rise 26%

Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported its pax volumes stood at 2.2mn in March, up 18.9% YoY, as seat capacity grew 19.2% YoY. Note that pax volume growth in March of 19% sharply moderated from the 30% pace in 2M25 due to high base effects from the timing of Easter break, which fell in March last year. Domestic pax volumes rose +20% YoY while international pax volumes grew 15% in March. Seat load factors dipped 20bps YoY to 81.4% due to a 500bps YoY drop in international load factors to 74.3%; Domestic load factors inched up 170bps YoY to 84.1%. Brings 1Q25 pax volumes to 6.95mn, up 26% YoY.

Okada Manila GGR falls in 1Q25

Okada Manila’s 1Q25 GGR fell 13% QoQ and 12% YoY to P7.8bn, the lowest level since 1Q22. The decline was led by VIP, which saw a 44% QoQ and 31% YoY decline to P1.8bn, the lowest level since 2Q21. Gaming machine GGR also fell 8% QoQ and 7% YoY to P3.0bn, the lowest level since 2Q22. GGR from gaming machines and mass online also fell on a QoQ and YoY basis. Volumes from VIP, gaming machines, and mass online also fell on a YoY and QoQ basis. The POGO ban likely contributed to the decline in volumes. Furthermore, 1Q25 tourist arrivals from the two largest foreign gaming markets, China and Korea, fell 34% and 14% respectively, to 73k and 395k.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP to review 2-4% inflation target

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the current 2-4% inflation target may be revised next year. Remolona stated that the midpoint [3%] of the range might be "a bit high" and that 2.5% may be a "better" midpoint for the BSP's target band. He cited that the possible revision stems from the reduced need for a wide scope of relative price adjustments, as prices of key inflation items [i.e., rice, vegetables, oil] have continued to ease.

NEDA confident on at least 6% GDP growth for 2025

Secretary Arsenio Balisacan of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) stated that the upper end of the 6-8% gov't GDP target range may be unlikely especially given heightened global uncertainties. But he also noted that the Marcos administration is still "confident" on hitting the lower end of the target band of 6%. Balisacan stated that the boost from the midterm elections will be positive for growth but may not be strong enough to offset the drag from significant uncertainties. The NEDA Secretary stated that he would like to see 6% growth in the first quarter, and he added that it is not too far-fetched considering easing inflation and support from domestic consumption. 1Q24 GDP was 5.9%. 1Q25 GDP data will be released on May 8. Consensus expects GDP to be 5.7% for 1Q25 and 5.9% for the full-year. We expect GDP to be 6.0% for the year.

OFW Remittances up 2.7% YoY in February

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.7% YoY to US$2.72bn, decelerating from the 2.9% growth seen in Jan. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 1.2% YoY from 0.9% in Jan. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 6.9%, slower relative to the previous three months given 4Q's seasonal strength.

BIR collections rise 13% YoY to P669bn in 1Q

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) saw a 13% YoY increase in tax collections to P669bn for 1Q25, higher than the P592bn in 1Q24. This was a combination of higher net income taxes, excise, VAT, percentage taxes of the regional offices, and the large taxpayer service. For 2025, the BIR has a full-year tax goal of P3.23trn; the 1Q tax collection of P669bn is ~20.6% of the full-year target. BIR Commissioner Romeo Lumagui is confident on continuing the good collection performance in the following months.

Vehicle sales rise 8% YoY, 3% MoM in March; Toyota sales up 14% YoY, 2% MoM

CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales of 40,306 units, up 7.6% YoY and 2.9% MoM. Brings 1Q25 vehicle sales up 6.8% YoY to 117,074 units. Note that CAMPI/TMA data does not include BYD. CAMPI is targeting sales of 500k units for 2025 (+7% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 18,907 units in March, up by 13.9% YoY and 2.1% MoM. Brings 1Q25 retail sales up 11.8% YoY to 55,513 units. TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year; To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 19.9k units per month. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses. GTCAP is trading at a 59% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,279/sh and 4.1x FY25E PER.

Department of Energy (DoE) flags delay of EERI 1,320 MW capacity

In a virtual briefing, Energy Assistant Secretary Mario C. Marasigan said not all units of the gas-fired power plant of Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. (EERI) are in full commercial operation. All three 440MW units are still under testing and commissioning, which means energy output remains inconsistent. Note that EERI initially targeted to declare commercial operations last Dec 2024. DoE said that the completion of Linseed Field Corp’s LNG storage Unit 1 may be pushed back to month-end, which could delay commercial operations target date of May 15. EERI is jointly owned by Meralco (40%), San Miguel (33%) and Aboitiz Power (27%). EERI is the single largest new baseload capacity to be added to the Luzon grid this year and its timely completion is crucial to avoiding tight supply during the ongoing peak summer months. We have been skeptical about the timely completion of EERI. Peak demand in the Luzon grid stood at 14,011 MW in 2024; While headline dependable capacity in Luzon is at almost 20GW, actual supply stood at only 14,600 MW from March 31-Apr 6.

Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences sees 8 from the Marcos-admin slate

Based on the latest Pulse Asia survey, 16 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 seats in the upcoming May 12, 2025 midterm elections. Re-electionist Bong Go leads the race with a voter preference of 61.9%. He is followed by ACT-CIS Rep. Erwin Tulfo (51.1%) and Sen. Ronald “Bato” dela Rosa (48.7%), ranking 2nd to 3rd. Former Senate President Vicente Sotto III is in 4th with 44.2%. Sen. Pia Cayetano (37.5%), Sen. Bong Revilla (36.9%), former Sen. Panfilo Lacson (36.0%), Willie Revillame (35.7%), Ben Tulfo (35.4%), and Makati Mayor Abby Binay (35.3%) are in the 5th to 12th range. Sen. Lito Lapid (33.3%) follows at 6th to 13th, while former Sen. Manny Pacquiao (32.0%) places 8th to 16th. Actor Phillip Salvador (30.0%), Rep. Camille Villar (29.0%), former Sen. Bam Aquino (28.6%), and Rep. Rodante Marcoleta (28.3%) round out the group with rankings between 11th and 18th. Six of the probable 16 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go, Dela Rosa, Salvador, Marcoleta are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo, and Revillame are independent. Notably, Salvador and Marcoleta now have a probable chance of winning after trailing in the previous surveys.
