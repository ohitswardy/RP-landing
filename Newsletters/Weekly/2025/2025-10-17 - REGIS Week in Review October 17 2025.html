REGIS REPORT

17 October 2025

The Philippine market staged a modest rebound, seeing the PCOMP climb 0.9% or 51.74 pts WoW to 6,089.53 with individual corporate stories of index heavyweights lifting the benchmark. The mid-week rebalancing of a closely-tracked equity index also added to market volatility.
 

The biggest positive mover was ICT (+3.1% WoW), which dominated headlines earlier in the week after a South African court ruling upheld its right to operate the Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2). This pushed ICT shares to fresh highs anew, which in turn lifted the PCOMP where it is now the top-weighted stock at 15.7% of the index.

 

Meanwhile, BPI (+2.6% WoW) kicked off the 3Q earnings season with results that slightly beat consensus forecasts, marked by upbeat (though moderating QoQ) loan growth and continued NIM expansion. Positive read-through for sector earnings likewise lifted BDO (+3.6% WoW).

 

Mid-week, the Wisdom Tree rebalancing added to volatility with large inflows seen in added names MONDE (-1.3% WoW) and URC (-2.9% WoW), while deleted names SCC (-2.7% WoW), MWC (-8.8% WoW), and PGOLD (-2.0% WoW) saw outflows.

 

Among sector indices, Property (-1.1% WoW) also continued to lag, with ALI (-3.0% WoW) weighed down by heavy foreign outflows while SMPH (-0.9% WoW) pulled back to a smaller extent.

 

Mining (+4.3% WoW) sustained its outperformance with gold miners OGP (+12.5% WoW) and APX (+10.2% WoW), with gold hitting a fresh record past $4,300/oz this week.

 

On domestic macro, Finance Sec. Ralph Recto said the ongoing flood control corruption scandal could weigh on Philippine GDP growth until early 2026 due to slower public spending, though he expects reforms to eventually offset the drag. This comes ahead of the 3Q GDP report due in three weeks' time.

 

The peso finished the week only slightly stronger WoW, up 0.1% WoW to 58.182:$1. Foreigners remained net sellers for the third straight week.

RESEARCH
ICTSI - Wins legal challenge for Durban port
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Durban High Court upholds ICT’s right to operate Durban port

Judge Mahendra Chetty of the Durban High Court dismissed APM Terminal’s petition to prevent state-owned Transnet from granting ICTSI the 25-year concession to operate the Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2). Barring a possible but unlikely court appeal, the ruling should clear the way for ICTSI’s takeover of DCT-2’s operations – a move that we estimate could add P30/sh to ICT’s value.

 

Claims of “irregularities” rejected

Judge Chetty found that ICTSI was transparent about its calculation of solvency ratio and that it had provided ample credible evidence of its financial capacity. The judge also ruled that APM’s legal challenge was filed with undue delay, providing further grounds for its dismissal.

 

Dilutive at the onset but value creation opportunities could be meaningful

ICTSI’s winning bid of ZAR 11.1bn (~US$634mn) implies a US$1.26bn enterprise value for the DCT-2 concession. We estimate DCT-2 could have generated around US$225mn in revenues and US$78mn in EBITDA last year. Based on these estimates, we estimate the deal translates to trailing valuations of roughly 16x EV/EBITDA—above ICT’s own 13x trailing multiple. 

Note that ICT will own a 50% stake less 1 share in the concession. Although the deal may not deliver material earnings accretion immediately, ICTSI’s track record in operational turnarounds and tariff optimization suggests ample potential to create value over the 25-year term, which we estimate could translate to an incremental value of at least P30/sh.

 

Pre-funded upfront fee

With most of the US$634mn upfront payment already pre-funded, we reckon only limited additional borrowing may be required to finance the acquisition.

 

Room to optimize existing port capacity

Transnet estimates the port’s annual capacity at only 2.1mn TEUs, based on the current average productivity of 15 gross crane moves per hour across its 15 ship-to-shore cranes. Note that the Manila International Container Terminal’s (MICT), which has a capacity of 3.5mn TEUs, achieves an average of 27 crane moves per hour using 18 ship-to-shore cranes.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Court clears ICTSI to run Durban container terminal

 

Durban High Court Judge Mahendra Chetty rejected APM Terminal’s petition seeking to block state-owned Transnet from awarding ICTSI the 25-year concession to operate Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2). Judge Chetty found no irregularities in the bidding process, saying that ICTSI was transparent about its calculation of solvency ratio and that it has provided ample evidence of financial capacity. Maersk said “We recognise the importance of operational improvements and infrastructure development in Durban proceeding without further delay.” This development should pave the way for the turnover of DCT-2’s operations to ICTSI. ICTSI reportedly offered an upfront payment of ZAR11.1bn (~US$630mn) to secure a 50% less one share in DCT-2’s concession. We estimate ICT’s bid implies ~17x EV/EBITDA, above ICT’s current multiple of 10x; it also implies a valuation of ~US$500 per TEU (using 2.9mn TEU capacity), which is broadly in line with replacement cost.

Ayala Land to redevelop more malls

Ayala Malls COO Paul Birkett said that ALI would redevelop an additional five malls over the next five years. In 2024, Ayala Land announced that it would spend P13bn to redevelop its four flagship malls – Glorietta, Greenbelt, TriNoma, and Ayala Center Cebu. Mr. Birkett said that the redevelopment of Glorietta, TriNoma, and Ayala Center Cebu will be completed this year. Earlier this year, the company said it would spend P4.5bn to redevelop Ayala Malls Abreeza, Ayala Malls Cloverleaf, Ayala Malls Fairview Terraces, and MarQuee Mall.

DigiPlus pauses launch of Brazil platform

DigiPlus announced a strategic pause on its first Brazilian brand, “GamePlus,” following the soft launch on September 22. The company is preparing for a full-scale relaunch in early 2026. It intends to provide a “digital entertainment offering that is not only locally relevant but also resonates deeply with Brazilian culture and entertainment habits.”

CNPF acquires Mindanao coconut process facility for US$45mn

Century Pacific Food Inc. (CNPF PM) with an investment of US$45mn or P2.6bn is set to acquire and upgrade a coconut processing facility in South Cotabato, Mindanao from Roxas and Company, Inc.'s (RCI PM) agriventure subsidiary. The plant is CNPF's third coconut hub alongside its other Mindanao facilities in General Santos and Misamis Occidental. The new acquisitions aim to expand product lines to boost production for domestic and export markets. For the OEM business, tuna and coconut OEM exports contributed 21% of total revenue in FY24 with coconut contributing approximately 30% of the 21%. Coco Mama, CNPF’s fresh coconut cream product, is part of the Milk & Others segment, which accounted for 29% of total revenue in FY24.

Cebu Air's pax volume growth muted at 3% YoY in September amid lean season

Passenger volumes grew at a modest +3% YoY to 1.8mn in September. Seat load factors stood at 81.1%, slightly lower than 82.6% a year earlier, following a 4% seat expansion. This brings YT-September pax volumes up 14% YoY to 19.95mn, trending a tad behind our full-year forecast (28.5mn, +16%). To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year should average 2.9mn per month.

Okada Manila 3Q25 GGR falls QoQ and YoY

Okada Manila’s 3Q25 GGR fell 2% QoQ and 15% YoY to P6.98bn, the lowest level since 1Q22. Although mass GGR improved QoQ, these gains were eroded by the decline in VIP GGR. Volumes of mass tables and slot machines rose 14% QoQ but VIP rolling chip fell 22% QoQ. The decline in VIP volumes was likely due to lower tourist arrivals from China and Korea. On a YoY basis, volumes fell across all categories.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Finance Secretary expects faster GDP growth in next 3 years

The Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto expects GDP growth to pick up in the next three years to an average of 5.9%. This is below the gov't's target growth rate 6-7% for 2026-2028. 1H25 and FY24 GDP growth was at 5.4% and 5.7%, respectively. Recto highlighted that the acceleration in growth would be driven by "the gov't addressing the leakages" exposed by the recent flood control scandal through "stronger institutions and better governance." He also emphasized other growth drivers such as low inflation, easing policy rates, strong consumer spending, and a robust labor market. The Finance Secretary also guided a debt-to-GDP ratio of 58% by 2030.

Finance Secretary says corruption scandal may slow GDP growth until 1Q26; hopeful for another rate cut

Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that the Philippine economy may experience a slowdown in growth until early 2026 given the flood control corruption scandal. Recall that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. flagged irregularities in flood control projects with investigations initiated by the Senate, the House, and the recently formed Independent Commission for Infrastructure. Recto guided that there will be a slowdown on public spending but highlighted that there will also be upside from reforms moving forward. He said that if the budget was utilized properly, GDP growth may have grown 6.0-6.2% as well as tax and customs collections would have been higher. Recto is optimistic that the economy will hit the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% GDP growth target this year. 1H25 GDP growth was at 5.4%. Regis expects GDP growth to be 5.5% this year. The Finance Secretary said that there will "hopefully, [be] another rate cut" but it will be hinged on inflation staying within target (2-4%). Inflation averaged 1.7% for 9M25. Regis expects inflation to average 1.8% for 2025. The BSP has reduced rates by a total of 100bp to 4.75% so far, with one more meeting by Dec 11. Regis expects a total of 100bp cuts this year to 4.75% and 25bp to 4.50% for next year.

BIR expects lower tax collection due to corruption scandal; Finance Secretary still expects to hit revenue target

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Commissioner Romeo Lumagui Jr. stated that its tax collections have declined likely due to the lingering issues regarding the flood control corruption scandal. Lumagui guided that BIR collections may decline in September. The BIR has collected P2.14trn as of 8M25, 11% higher YoY. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that the gov't remains on track to meet its revenue target of P4.52trn (+16% YoY) this year. Recto stated that he expects non-tax revenues to cover the lower-than-expected tax and customs collections.

House approves P6.79trn budget on final reading

The House of Representatives passed the proposed P6.79trn national budget for 2026 on final reading, with 287 voted for, twelve voted against, and two opted to abstain. The 2026 national budget is 7.4% higher YoY. The House deliberated on the bill for 62 days with numerous revisions made given the recent corruption scandal involving flood control projects. P255bn was initially allocated for flood control infrastructure for 2026. P201bn was redirected to the agricultural, health, and other social sectors. After the budget bill has finished deliberations through the House plenary level, it will move to the Senate, the bicam, and finally to President Marcos Jr. for approval.

Gov't to impose sugar import ban until 2Q26

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Fransico Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that the gov't will not import sugar until mid-2026 given the low prices exhibited in the first sugar auction in Negros earlier this month. Future imports were said to only be classified as reserve sugar and will not be released into the domestic market. Sugar output according to the DA has hit 2.01 MMT, surpassing the 1.92 MMT in the previous crop year. This is the highest in four years, on track to approach the 2.14 MMT in 2020-2021. Note that in FY24, URC's net sales and operating income totaled P162bn and P20bn, respectively—with the SURE (Sugar and Renewables) segment contributing 18% of total revenues and 17% of EBIT.

Dept. of Agriculture eyes 84% rice self-sufficiency by 2026

Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Assistant Secretary U-Nichols Manalo stated that 84% level rice sufficiency is achievable by end-2026 if its P216bn 2026 budget will be approved. The proposed budget includes funding for initiatives to boost rice production, support farmers, and improve farm-to-market logistics. Currently, rice self-sufficiency is estimated to be at 77%. The Marcos administration targets around 90% self-sufficiency by 2028. Rice production as of 1H25 hit 9.08 MMT, 6% higher YoY and 45% of the 20.35 MMT FY25 target. This is on track to reach the 20.3 MMT full-year target, assuming the 1H continues to account for ~45% of annual output, which has typically been the case in past years.

Marcos expands e-voucher use to end hunger in the PH by 2027

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. stated that he will increase the use of electronic food vouchers in part of his plan to eradicate hunger among the poorest families in the country by 2027. The Reducing Food Insecurity and Undernutrition with Electronic Vouchers (REFUEL) project was recently launched in Manila City where each recipient will get P3,000 in monthly electronic food credits through "benefit transfer cards" which are usable at accredited partner retailers. The gov't plans to link electronic benefit transfer cards to mobile payment platforms like GCash to enhance transaction efficiency and minimize administrative expenses. Program participants are required to participate in productivity enhancement sessions which eye to prepare them for livelihood opportunities. The P74-billion REFUEL program is supported by the Asian Development Bank, Agence Française de Développement and the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) Fund.

August NPLs tick up 8bps MoM to 3.66%

Asset quality deteriorated MoM in August, with Philippine banks’ NPL ratio climbing 8bps to 3.66% from 3.58% in July. That said, the figure is 15bps lower YoY vs the 3.81% in August 2024. (Note that we compute these ratios excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs). The reported BSP data was up 11bps MoM to 3.59% because the IBL+RRP total was down MoM.) In PHP terms, NPLs stood at P550bn, up 2.7% MoM and 7.3% YoY.

Philippines seeks exemption from “Keep Call Centers in America” Bill

Philippine ambassador to the US Jose Manuel Romualdez said that he is working “…with our friends in the US Congress to exempt the Philippines” from the “Keep Call Centers in America” Bill. He also asked US companies and industry associations to lobby for less stringent restrictions on BPO firms already established outside the US. In July 2025, US Senators Ruben Gallego and Jim Justice filed a bill that seeks to penalize firms that outsource call centers operations. The Secretary of Labor will be required to maintain a publicly available list of all employers that relocate call center or contract center work overseas. Such companies will not be eligible for Federal grants or guaranteed loans. The head of an agency, when awarding a Federal contract, shall give preference to a United States employer that does not appear on the list. Customer service agents must inform the customer of his/her physical location and the customer may request to be immediately transferred to a customer service agent who is physically located in the US. The IT-BPO Association previously estimated that outsourcing work to the Philippines (from the US) could result in cost savings of about 80%. About 70% of the Philippine BPO industry caters to North America. About 70% of BPO revenue is from voice operations.

SEC proposes new rules requiring companies to disclose beneficial owners

The SEC has released for public comment a draft memorandum circular that would require corporations, partnerships, foreign companies, and one-person corporations to provide detailed information about the persons who ultimately own or control them. The proposed guidelines categorize beneficial owners based on various modes of control or ownership. These include direct ownership of at least 20% of shares, contractual control, influence over board elections, dominant influence over corporate policies, nominee arrangements, and senior management control when no natural person is otherwise identifiable. Entities will also be required to maintain accurate and up-to-date records of beneficial ownership, including names, addresses, tax identification numbers, nationalities, and ownership percentages.
