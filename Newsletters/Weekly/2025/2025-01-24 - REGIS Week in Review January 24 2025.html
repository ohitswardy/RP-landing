REGIS REPORT

24 January 2025

The PCOMP settled another week the red, -0.88% WoW at 6296.2, marking its 3rd consecutive week of decline and bringing the YTD loss to -3.56%. Shares mostly fluctuated near its worst levels in 6 months while trading on listless turnover throughout the week. Trading volumes eased -23% WoW to a daily average of only $72m, reflecting investors’ cautious sentiment as they assess Trump’s executive orders and plans announced during his first week in office. Along with the easing in activity however came a slowdown in net foreign selling to $19m vs $57m last week, although the current daily outflow streak remains unbroken at 14 days.

On the policy front, in focus were comments from Finance Secretary Recto who stated that he expects 50bps of interest rate cuts this year. This follows the 75bps of cuts last year and is inline with Regis’ reduced forecast. Note that the Fed is set to hold its first policy meeting of the year towards the end of the month and futures are currently pricing in no movement in the benchmark rate (<1% chance of a 25bp cut). BSP will follow with its own policy meeting 2 weeks later on Feb 13.

In terms of movers, index heavyweight ICT -3.46% WoW after Trump announced his plan to slap a 25% tariff on imports from Mexico starting February 1. Regis estimates that ICT’s port in Mexico, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), accounts for 26% of EBITDA.

Biggest laggard in the benchmark during the week was JGS -6.72% WoW which marked a fresh low since 2011. Note JGS and its sub URC -2.48%, which also marked fresh lows since 2012 during the week, now have the lowest weightings among the remaining 13 Philippine names in the MSCI standard cap index. The next MSCI rebal is scheduled to take place on end-Feb and changes are expected to be announced on Feb 11.

Before that however will be the upcoming PCOMP/PSEi rebalancing. Changes were announced after the closing bell on Friday. There will be multiple changes to main benchmark: CBC -0.43% WoW and AREIT +0.25% to be included, NIKL -1.58% and WLCON -5.46% to be removed. Rebal day is on Friday, January 31.

ICTSI - Trump to slap 25% tariff on Mexico by Feb 1
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Worst case for Mexico materializing soon?

Shortly after his inauguration, US President Donald Trump said that he plans to impose a 25% tariff on imports from Mexico and Canada by Feb. 1, following through on his previous campaign threats. We previously wrote (see here) that US protectionist policies could hurt ICTSI’s operations in Mexico, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), which we estimate accounts for 26% of FY25E EBITDA. On a positive note, President Trump seems to have tempered his threats on imposing universal tariffs, as well as, increasing tariffs on China.

Minimal impact on CMSA?

We await further detail on this announcement, particularly clarity on coverage and timing (i.e. phased implementation?), and even the possibility that the threat might not materialize. Retaliatory tariffs could also exacerbate the impact on the Mexican economy, which could have secondary effects on the country’s import purchasing power.

Management believes the impact of tariffs on CMSA will be minimal (i.e. 2-5% hit on volumes), which suggests market dynamics may not be greatly interrupted and container charges can be maintained. However, we reckon yield per TEU could still soften, given the possibility of a decline in the volume of higher-yielding imports.

Taking management’s volume prediction, we estimate that CMSA’s revenues could decline by <10%, which in turn could lead to a 4% reduction in ICT’s profit on an annualized basis, an outcome that appears less disastrous than what we have built into our model. One, however, cannot discount the risk that the trade conflict could get worse and lengthy, inflicting prolonged damage on the Mexican economy. What’s more likely, though, is that a deal can be quickly hatched to resolve the tensions.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

GCash to spend IPO proceeds on lending, global expansion

GCash will spend the proceeds from its initial public offering (IPO) on expanding the lending business and speeding up international growth, according to GCash president and CEO Martha Sazon in an interview with Bloomberg at the World Economic Forum. However, it remains unclear when GCash will list its shares to become public, as the e-wallet waits for the perfect timing to file its IPO. Previous reports stated that GCash plans to list in 2H25, with an estimated IPO valuation of US$8bn. Ms. Sazon also said that bonds will become available as investment products on GCash at denominations as low as P500, lower than the P5,000 minimum for government retail treasury bonds.

KKR selling stake in Maya

Private equity firm KKR has reportedly engaged an advisor for the sale of its stake in Maya Innovations (formerly Voyager Innovations). KKR owns 20% of Maya Innovations which is the holding firm for the Maya e-wallet and Maya Bank. Maya has been losing money in recent years and was aiming to reach breakeven by 4Q24.

KKR is reportedly eyeing a valuation of US$2bn for 100% equity, implying a value of US$400m for KKR’s interest. PLDT is the single biggest largest shareholder of Maya, with a ~37% stake. It’s unclear if PLDT is willing to buy KKR’s stake at the said valuation. The last widely announced funding round (Apr 2022) valued Maya at US$1.4bn and raised US$210m. Maya also raised more funding early last year (2024) but the valuations for that round were not publicly disclosed.

Meralco seeks P75bn of loans to fund Chromite deal

According to Meralco CFO Betty C. Siy-Yap, Meralco plans to borrow P75bn from BDO, BPI, and Metrobank to finance its planned acquisition of San Miguel’s LNG facilities (a.k.a. Project Chromite). The loan is payable over 12 years and will be drawn down within the week.

Recall that Meralco plans to jointly invest in San Miguel’s 1,278-megawatt (MW) Ilijan power plant, its new 1,320-MW combined cycle power facility (Excellent Energy), and its LNG import and regasification terminal. MER will own a 40% stake in Chromite; SMC’s stake will be diluted to 33% (from 100%) while AP will own 27%.

New management of Cemex (CHP PM) targets profitability in three years

CHP President and CEO Herbert Consunji said that they are targeting to regain profitability within three years. Management is looking to implement operational efficiencies to go above water through the removal of various expenses, royalties, and other cost-cutting initiatives. CHP incurred an EBITDA loss of P316mn and net loss of P2.87bn as of 9M24. As of end-September, CHP's gross debt stood at P21.1bn while cash stood at P1.1bn.

DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) has a 51% stake in CHP, while Semirara (SCC PM) and Dacon Corporation own 10% and approximately 29%, respectively. Recall that the DMCI group acquired CHP for an enterprise value (100%) of US$660mn and equity value of US$272mn.

Semirara sets 2025 capex at P6.9bn

Semirara expects to spend P6.9bn in capex for 2025, higher than its 2024 capex budget of P6.5bn. Bulk of capex (P5.8bn) is allocated for the coal mining division’s re-fleeting initiatives and acquisition of other mining equipment. As of 9M24, Semirara has spent P3.8bn of its P6.5bn 2024 budget; In 2023, the company spent only P4bn out of its P7bn capex budget.

Regis expects SCC to defer spending until it secures the renewal of its coal operating contract (expiring in July 2027). We expect dividends of P4.5/sh for 2025 (~13% yield).

DMCI’s nickel mining arm doubling production in 2025

DMCI Mining is targeting a combined annual production from three mines of 3mn MT this year, up from its FY24 production guidance of ~1.5mn MT. It plans to fully operate a new mine in Zambales, which has a 1mn MT annual production limit, by 1Q25. It is also in the final stage of securing a Mineral Production Sharing Agreement (MPSA) for its Long Point mine in Palawan within the year. Regis believes that the production increase is timely, given the strength in nickel ore prices following the Indonesian government’s announced production cuts. Note that export prices of nickel ore with 1.4% grade are currently at US$32-35 per WMT. For context, DMCI Mining’s ASP (ave. nickel grade of 1.35%) stood at US$25 per WMT while cash costs per MT stood at US$20/WMT in 9M24.

DMCI Mining is a wholly-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM). It incurred a net loss of P35mn in 9M24 due to lower prices and sales volumes. The latter was a consequence of permitting delays.

Ayala Corp. initiates CFO succession plan, appoints Syquia as Deputy CFO

Ayala Corporation (AC PM) President and CEO Cezar P. Consing announced the appointment of Mr. Juan Carlos L. Syquia as Deputy CFO effective May 1, 2025. Mr. Syquia is currently the Executive Vice President and Head of Institutional Banking at the BPI. Syquia will support Ayala Corporation CFO Alberto M. de Larrazabal before succeeding him on January 1, 2026 after the latter’s retirement.

URC appoints two new executives

URC appointed two executives to senior roles within the group effective Feb 1: Francisco “Pancho” del Mundo was appointed Chief Finance and Strategy Officer, Anna Milagros David was made EVP and Group President of Branded Consumer Foods Philippine. Del Mundo is currently Chief Strategy Officer while David is Chief Marketing Officer. Current CFO of URC, Evelyn Ng, will be leaving the firm.

CREC to issue new shares to the public

Citicore Renewable Energy Corp (CREC PM) said it would issue 346mn new shares to the public in order to comply with minimum public ownership requirements. Pertamina New & Renewable Energy (NRE) will subscribe to 2.23bn unissued common shares of CREC at P3/sh. Total buy-in valuation for a 20% stake in CREC will be P6.7bn (US$115mn).

Upon completion of all the transactions, CREC’s total shares outstanding will rise by 25% to 11.16bn from 8.93bn. Parent Citicore Power’s stake in CREC will be diluted from 78.8% to 59.9%. Public float will fall from 21.2% to 20.1%.

Pertamina NRE is a subsidiary of Indonesia’s state-owned enterprise PT Pertamina Power Indonesia. Proceeds will be used to fund CREC’s renewable energy project pipeline.

CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 285MW. The company is constructing 1,583 MW of solar projects over the next 12 months. CREC has a project pipeline of 6,716MW (gross) of solar and 813MW (gross) of onshore wind, and 26MW of hydro plants, which will take overall gross installed capacity of 7,570MW by 2028.

DigiPlus expects slower customer growth in 2025

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) expects its registered users to grow double digits in 2025, after doubling to 40mn in 2024. PLUS Chairman Eusebio Tanco cited the high customer base as well as entry of new competitors as reasons for the slower pace of growth in 2025. The company’s 3Q24 revenue grew 175% YoY (+2% QoQ) to P19.3bn, while 3Q24 net profit grew 246% YoY (+9% QoQ) to P3.5bn.

Okada Manila volumes fall in 4Q24

Okada Manila’s 4Q24 GGR rose 9% QoQ (-4% YoY) to P9.0bn due to an improvement in win rate, particularly in VIP. Volumes of every category fell QoQ – in fact 4Q24 VIP rolling chip of P76.9bn was the lowest since 2Q21. FY24 GGR fell 22% to P35bn, as volumes of VIP, mass online, and slots all declined. The POGO ban, which became effective at the start of this year, will likely cause further weakness for casinos in Entertainment City.

Davao Light seeks franchise extension

Aboitiz Power (AP) subsidiary Davao Light (DLP) is seeking to extend its franchise, which is expiring this year. The company is the third largest distribution utility (DU) in the country. Recto expects 50 basis points cut from BSP in 2025

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that the government expects another 50bps reduction in the policy rate this year: “Interest rates are still high…we expect maybe another 50 basis points in 2025…that would help us grow 6-6.5%.” This is in line with REGIS’ expectations. The BSP has rescheduled the first meeting of the Monetary Board to Feb 13 from Feb 20 due to BSP Governor’s planned attendance at the Financial Action Task Force Plenary and Working Group Meetings in Paris, France from 17-20 Feb. Note that the FOMC’s first meeting is scheduled on Jan 29 (EST).

Recto: Government might have missed 2024 growth target

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that economic growth in 2024 likely fell below 6%, missing the Marcos administration’s target of 6% to 6.5%. Late-season typhoons disrupted growth despite the usual holiday-season economic surge. Third-quarter GDP growth was 5.2%, the weakest in over a year due to storm-related disruptions to government spending and agricultural damage caused by the typhoons.

Average GDP growth for the first nine months of 2024 was 5.8%, requiring 6.5% growth in Q4 to meet the low-end target. The gov’t aims for 6% to 8% GDP growth in 2025, which Secretary Recto believes is achievable despite fiscal constraints. Public sector disbursements may weaken in early 2025 due to the election-related spending ban and conditional implementation of P757 billion in congressional items, which requires agencies to justify fund releases.

Note that REGIS forecasts 2024 GDP growth of 5.9% and 2025 GDP growth of 6.0%. Consensus forecasts meanwhile: 2024 GDP growth of 5.9%, 4Q24 GDP growth of 6.1% YoY, and 2025 GDP growth of 6.0%.

Philippines launches global bond offering; sells $3.3bn of USD, EUR bonds

The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) has launched a dual-tranche (10-year and 25-year) US dollar-denominated bond offering as well as a euro sustainability bond. The government raised $2.25bn USD for the dual-tranche USD bond and $1.04bn USD for the 7-year EUR bond. In terms of yield, the 10-year bond will yield around 90 basis points above comparable US Treasuries, down from initial guidance of about 120 basis points. The 25-year sustainability note will yield 5.9%, versus guidance of around 6.1%. While the 7-year euro bond priced at mid-swaps plus 125 basis points, narrower than guidance of about 160 basis points.

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto previously stated that they plan to raise $3.5bn USD this year in global bond sales. To note, the Philippines has about $1.5bn in USD bonds that will be maturing in March and €650mn in EUR bonds that will be due in April this year. The government plans to focus more on domestic borrowing, with a financing mix of 80% domestic and 20% foreign this year, to minimize FX exposure. Recall that the government raised over US$5bn from global bond sales in 2024. The national government’s debt stock amounts to P14.51tn in end-November, of which 69% is domestic debt and 31% is external debt.

BOP posts US$1.5bn deficit in December 2024; Full-Year 2024 BOP surplus at US$609mn; End-December GIR settles at US$106.3bn

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$1.5bn in December 2024, according to preliminary data. This is a reversal of the US$642mn BOP surplus recorded in Dec 2023 but lower than the US$2.3bn deficit in November 2024. The BOP deficit in December 2024 reflected the Bangko Sentral nf Pilipinas’ (BSP) net foreign exchange operations and drawdown on the National Government's (NG) deposits. This brings the cumulative BOP position to a surplus of US$609mn in 2024. This is lower than the US$3.7bn surplus recorded in 2023 and the US$3.5bn BOP surplus 2024 target of the BSP.

Preliminary data illustrates the decline in cumulative BOP surplus was due to higher trade in goods deficit, and lower net receipts from trade in services and net foreign borrowings by the National Government. The BOP position reflects a decrease in final gross international reserves (GIR) level to US$106.3bn as of end-December 2024 from US$108.5bn as of end November 2024. The latest GIR level represents an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income.

Finance Department admits difficulties in customs’ 2025 target collections

Department of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto expects the Bureau of Customs to face difficulties in reaching the P1.06tn collection target for 2025. This is 13% YoY higher than the preliminary P917bn collections (+4% YoY) of the customs bureau in FY24. In the event of a shortfall, the national government is looking at additional non-tax revenues and other privatization proceeds. Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Recto expressed optimism about the BIR’s ability to achieve its 2025 collection target of P3.2tn, 12% YoY higher.

Department of Agriculture targets partnerships with major retail chains and seeks to enforce imported price rice cap in efforts to lower prices

The Department of Agriculture (DA) is eyeing partnerships with major retail chains like SM, Robinsons, Puregold, 7-Eleven, and MerryMart to expand availability and stabilize rice prices. The initiative is considering 3,200 outlets nationwide, piloted in Metro Manila where rice prices remain high. The DA will monitor the Metro Manila pilot for several months to assess its potential for expansion to other regions. Food Terminal Inc. (FTI), a government owned and controlled corporation, will manage supply and packaging for rice distributed through retail outlets. Programs include the Kadiwa Ng Pangulo’s P29-per-kilo rice for vulnerable sectors and the Rice-for-All (RFA) program offering various grades priced between P36 and P45 per kilo.

The DA is also enforcing a maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) of P58 per kilo for imported rice, effective January 20, 2025. Initial inspections showed partial compliance, as some retailers continued selling rice at P60–P65 per kilo due to branded or local varieties exempt from the MSRP. Retailers are being given time to negotiate prices with suppliers, but penalties for violations (fines of up to P1 million) will take effect in February. Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that stricter price controls may be imposed if the MSRP fails to lower rice prices to reasonable levels.

BPO sector achieves 2024 targets

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it hit US$38bn in revenue and 1.82mn employees in 2024. These represent 7% growth from 2023: US$35.5bn in revenue and 1.7mn employees. IBPAP previously announced that it was targeting US$59bn in revenue and 2.5mn employees by 2028. However, IBPAP President Jack Madrid said that there will be a recalibration of its targets in light of “macro environment that affects global demand.” BPO firms account for 60%-80% of the occupied office space of the developers we cover.

InstaPay and PESONet transactions surge in 2024

Digital transactions through the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) saw faster growth overall for the FY24. The combined PesoNet and InstaPay value of transactions figure surged 35.4% YoY in 2024 and amounted to P17.42tn from P12.86tn the year before. This was faster than the 29.4% YoY growth seen in 2023 vs. 2022. In terms of combined volume of transactions, it hit 1.5bn, rising 62.2% YoY from 2023, quicker than the 46.7% volume growth seen in FY23.

Real-time, small-ticket payments over InstaPay totaled to P7.35tn, up 46.3% YoY to P7.35tn, which was faster than FY23's 42% growth rate. On volume of transactions, InstaPay volumes grew to 1.4bn, up 67.8% YoY.

For larger transactions over PesoNet, the 2024 figure rose 28.5% YoY to P10.08tn—higher than the 22% growth in FY23. By transaction volume, it increased by 10.7% YoY to 100.9mn.

Recall that the BSP hit their 2023 target of at least 50% of the volume and value of retail transactions to be done online. While their 2028 target would be online payments comprising of 60-70% of total retail transaction volume.

LTFRB reviewing 15% jeepney fare hike

The Land Transportation and Franchising Regulatory Board (LTFRB) is reviewing the petition of various transport groups for a P15 minimum fare for public utility jeepneys. The nationwide minimum fare today stands at P13. Recall that the government last hiked minimum jeepney fares by P1 in October 2023. Note that passenger road transport has a weight of 3.5% in the CPI basket.

PAGCOR implements the previously announced lowering of fees on electronic games

PAGCOR lowered the fees it charges on electronic casino games operators from 35% to 30% as of 1 January 2025. Recall that the lowering of fees was first announced in 2023. This is the third time in two years that PAGCOR lowered fees on electronic games: In August 2023, PAGCOR lowered the fees from 58% to 41.25%, then in April 2024, it lowered the fees from 41.25% to 35%. GGR of E-games is likely to grow further due to this reduction. In 3Q24 GGR of E-games grew 16% QoQ and 464% YoY to P35.7bn. DigiPlus is a key beneficiary of the lowering of E-games fees.
