REGIS REPORT

22 August 2025

The PSEi inched lower this week to close at 6281.58, down 0.5%.  Market activity was lackluster, in the wake of an uninspired 2Q corporate reporting season and a lack of domestic catalysts. Meanwhile, traders were also tentative,  in anticipation of the Fed’s Jackson Hole address this week. Average daily volumes hit a mere US$113.5m.

Risk aversion was apparent, with market proxies SM (-2.5%) and Ayala Corp (-4.3%) among the top laggards this week. Other holdcos JG Summit (-4.0%) and GT Capital (-2.8%) also ended the week lower. Banks also weighed on the index, with BPI (-3.5%) and BDO (-1. 7%) dropping this week, amidst expectations of lower interest rates crimping NIMs, coupled with concerns on rising credit costs.

Meanwhile, the top index mover was Jollibee (+7.2%). The stock cemented last week’s 2.8% gain, after 2Q topline and EBIT grew an impressive 15% and 19%, respectively. Other consumer names also managed gains. URC saw some respite rising 0.8% this week, arresting three straight weeks of declines. CNPF, with a  1.5% gain this week, likewise halted a three-week decline.

Ayala Land (+2.8%) closed higher, bouncing from its 3.7% drop in the previous week, as well speculation of a September Fed rate cut.

Also helping to offset index losses this week was ICTSI (+1.0%) as well as San Miguel (+14.3%) which posted a recovery from its 14.5% decline last week. 

Retail favorite PLUS (+3.5%) likewise bounced post its decline in the previous week. PAGCOR said online gambling transactions fell by half in the first days following the de-linking of e-wallets with gambling sites and apps effective Sunday (Aug 17).

Foreigners were net sellers for US$27m, of which ACEN accounted for US$12m in outflows. Outflows were also seen in Converge (unch), SM and BPI.

ICTSI - Not too late to join the party; Raising TP to P560/sh, Upgrade to Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

De-escalation

We raise our TP for ICTSI to P560/sh (+40%) as we turn optimistic on the potential outcome of negotiations over a free trade agreement between the USA and Mexico. We believe the risk-reward is favorable, with only minimal downside from current levels should more protectionist US policies prevail. Even though ICT’s shares have outperformed the PCOMP by 28% year-to-date, its valuation at 9x FY26E EV/EBITDA remains below peer median and historical average of 11x. We upgrade our rating to Buy from Hold.

Mexico benefits from nearshoring but remains vulnerable

President Trump exempted Mexico from the sweeping “Liberation Day tariffs”, a positive outcome for Mexico that supports the country’s nearshoring boom. While the renegotiation of the USMCA trade treaty, due to expire in July 2026, is still up in the air, we take comfort that the level-headed responses of the Mexican government to the US government’s position on tariffs could yield a favorable outcome.

Profit estimates revised; SOTP NAV-based TP raised to P560/sh

We trim our FY25E profit by 9% after adjusting for actuals but upgrade our FY27E profit by 9%, after assuming a 2025E-2027E revenue CAGR of +14% for its port in Mexico alone, a reverse of our previous CAGR assumption of -12%. We also raise our TP to P560/sh, after upgrading our valuation for ICT’s port in Mexico, adding the recent acquisition in Batam island, Indonesia, rolling over our estimates, changing our PHP:USD exchange rate assumption, as well as incorporating its brownfield expansions. These adjustments offset the reduced revenue assumptions for its ports in Manila (MICT) and Melbourne.

Favorable risk-reward

ICT’s risk-reward profile is favorable, in our view, with a potential 18% share price upside on our baseline TP of P560/sh and limited downside of 1% under a bear case outcome. We believe ICT can deliver strong earnings growth in the near-to medium-term and advise investors to accumulate. Downside risks: macroeconomic/political risks, acquisitions, and labor issues.

Jollibee Foods Corp. - 2Q25: Core brand leads growth; China improves, Smashburger deteriorates
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 operating profit up 18% YoY to P10.8bn

JFC’s 1H25 net profit was slightly down 1% YoY to P5.6bn, which appears in line with expectations. 1H consolidated revenues rose 15% to P148bn, while EBIT grew 18% YoY to P10.8bn. Despite growth there, increased financing costs and taxes kept the company’s bottom line flattish. For 2Q25 alone, top line grew 15% YoY to P77.6bn and EBIT increased 19% YoY to P6.0bn. Income for the quarter rose 6% to P3.2bn. Group SSSG was steady at 5.5% in 2Q25, unchanged from the previous quarter.

Jollibee brand continues to drive the overall business

The domestic segment’s 2Q systemwide sales (SWS) grew +11.3% to P65.2bn, boosted by new store openings in the quarter as SSSG decelerated mildly to +6.4% (+8.5% in 1Q25). The Jollibee brand (+7.0%) and Mang Inasal (+12.0%) were the primary drivers of SSSG in 2Q, while SSSG of Chowking slowed to 2.3% from +6.2% in 1Q.

The international business saw 2Q SWS climb 32.6% to P49.3bn with a YoY boost from Compose Coffee (6.8% of total SWS). SSSG improved to 4.1% from 0.7%, lifted by a reversal from China. We also note encouraging SSS numbers from the Jollibee brand internationally (+9.4% in North America, +10.0% in Other Asia).

China improves, Smashburger deteriorates

China saw an improvement in 2Q after five consecutive quarters of SSS decline. SSS rose 3.9%, following a shift to a value-oriented strategy. In contrast, Smashburger continued to struggle, with SSS falling 9.9%—a deeper drop than the 8.0% decline in 2Q. Still, management remains hopeful 2H will be better for the brand as it leans into “value-led offerings” alongside other menu changes introduced in late July.

Increasing leverage to lower cost of capital

JFC signaled that it intends to further reduce its average cost of capital by increasing leverage (debt). We understand this will be undertaken by financing new expenditures primarily (90%) with debt. Frankly, we don’t think leverage is particularly low today. Net gearing (including capitalized leases) as of 2Q dipped slightly to 1.7x from 1.8x as of end-2024, on our estimates. Excluding capitalized leases, we estimate net gearing is now 1.05x.

On track to meet “guidance”

Management expressed confidence on delivering the higher end of the range for its FY25 guidance (4-6% SSSG, 10-15% EBIT growth), as they expect sustained momentum from the 1H. The next few quarters for China and Smashburger will be pivotal to whether there will be a meaningful turnaround. Better performance in the international segment is essential for JFC to reach its goal of tripling net income (to P24bn) by 2028.

Philippine Gaming - 2Q25 recap: Bricks vs clicks
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A tale of two segments

There was a large divergence in the performances of the onsite and online gaming businesses in 2Q25. Gross gaming revenue (GGR) of licensed casinos fell 11% QoQ and YoY to P44.1bn while GGR of Bingo and e-games grew 23% QoQ (+78% YoY) to P63.4bn, equivalent to 0.9% of GDP. However, both segments are likely to encounter headwinds in the next 12-18 months, with tighter regulatory restrictions for online gaming on the horizon and a new integrated resort opening in PAGCOR Entertainment City.

Market leaders of both segments lost share

Interestingly, the market leaders of both segments lost share in 2Q25. GGR of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) grew 7% QoQ (+31% YoY to P24.4bn), slower than the 23% QoQ (+78% YoY) increase of the sector. This brought its market share down to 39% in 2Q25, from 44% in 1Q25.

Meanwhile, Bloomberry Resorts saw the biggest decline among the Metro Manila licensed casinos, with GGR falling 16% QoQ to P14.1bn. Volumes of every category fell on a sequential basis, with the VIP segment showing the biggest decline, due to the closure of two junket operators in Solaire Entertainment City in 2Q25. As a result, Bloomberry’s GGR share of Metro Manila licensed casinos fell from 42% in 1Q25 to 39% in 2Q25.

Both segments face headwinds

Both the onsite and online segments are facing headwinds over the next 12-18 months. The onsite business is already encountering demand issues and is expected to see more supply next year. Meanwhile, tighter regulatory restrictions are looming for the online business.

As we have previously written, online gambling is facing tremendous scrutiny from various branches of government (see report: “E-gaming – (literally) eating our lunch”). Some regulatory restrictions have been imposed, such as a ban on physical advertisements (such as billboards) as well as removal of links on e[1]wallets that redirect to online gambling platforms. Other restrictions proposed include: raising minimum bets, raising the minimum “cash-in”, and increasing taxes on online gambling (see Figure 7). This would be a reversal from the experience over the past two years, when PAGCOR lowered the fees it charges on online gambling, paving the way for online operators to spend more on advertising and promotions.

Meanwhile, demand for onsite gambling is already weak due to the POGO ban as well as lower tourist arrivals from its largest foreign markets, China and Korea. Furthermore, Suntrust Resort Holdings (SUN PM) plans to open a new integrated resort in 2H26 which will add 15%-20% to the table and slot capacity in Entertainment City.

Philippine Seven - 2Q25: SSSG remains negative
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 net profit +1% YoY

Philippine Seven reported 1H25 profit of P1.8bn (+1% YoY). EBIT increased 8% to P2.8bn on revenue growth of 7% to P46.5bn. Results for 1H are, overall, broadly within REGIS expectations. For 2Q25 alone, profit was up 5% YoY to P1.2bn. EBIT rose 8% YoY to P1.8bn, while top line grew 9% to P24.8bn. EBIT and net margin dipped 10bp and 20bp YoY, respectively, on store expansion-related expenses from higher logistics costs and accretion of interest costs on lease liabilities.

SSSG sees mild improvement but remains negative

Same store sales contracted by 0.5%, marking the third consecutive quarter of negative SSSG. While this appears to be a slight improvement from -1.2% in 1Q, it remained down YoY despite the usual boost from the Holy Week timing this year (Apr 25’ vs Mar 24’) and election spending. Management noted that consumers cut back on discretionary spend (e.g., chips, instant noodles, etc.) and focused on essentials such as canned goods.

New payment system switch completed; store network expansion on track

The switch to the new payment system was said to have been completed mid-July, enabling better eliability of e-wallet payments and cash-ins. The company eyes further improvements with the implementation of card payment transactions in 1,000 stores by 4Q. SEVN is still aiming to open 500 new stores this year, with about 70% located in Visayas and Mindanao, where sales growth has been utpacing that of Metro Manila and Luzon. In 1H, the company has opened 218 stores so far, ending the period with 4,268 stores, 9% higher vs 2Q25.

Rough start to 3Q but optimistic for the rest of 2025

Management noted that July was a tough month due to strong monsoon rains causing flooding around Luzon including Metro Manila, while August at the very least was said to see some recovery. The company is optimistic for an improved 2H, but one should recall that the YoY comparisons will look flattering simply because of a more depressed base during 2H24. Improvement in store economics, especially SSS, will depend on execution (SKU assortment, new product and service offerings, store improvements) as well as a rebound in overall consumer spend.

AREIT - Office contracts renewed at lower rental rates in 1H25
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H25 net profit rises 45%, thanks in part to accounting adjustments

1H25 net profit rose 45% to P4.3bn. While this is notionally above REGIS and consensus forecasts, the outperformance came from accounting adjustments on contracts under straight-line revenue recognition. These adjustments did not have any cash impact and will not affect our dividend forecasts.

Office contracts renewed at lower rental rates

On average, expiring office contracts (which comprised c. 2% of total floor area) were renewed at rental rates that were 10%-15% lower than the previous contracts because (for the most part) they were previously signed in 1H20, when rental rates were at their peak. We do not expect material changes to our dividend per share forecasts.

Since contracts to be renewed next year were mostly signed in 2021, when rents had already fallen, we would expect any negative rental reversions to be far more modest. In fact, given the lower office vacancy rates in Makati CBD, where nearly half of AREIT’s office space is located, we believe positive rental reversions are likely in the area.

Lease expiries to increase in 2026-2027

Only a limited amount of AREIT’s office space is due for contract renewal this year but lease expiries will pick up over the next two years. There are 42k, 115k, and 119k sqm of lease expiries in 2025, 2026, and 2027 respectively. These represent 4%, 11%, and 12% of AREIT’s building floor area respectively.

New President appointed

AREIT appointed Mr. Alberto M. de Larrazabal, who is concurrently CFO of Ayala Corporation (until end-2025), as President and CEO. He will replace Mr. Jose Eduardo A. Quimpo II, who has been President for a year. We suspect this means Mr. Quimpo will take on more responsibility at AREIT’s sponsor, Ayala Land.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Container volumes in Mexico drop in July amid customs delays

Data from the Mexican port authority showed that container volumes across Mexico’s ports fell 5% YoY in July, after growing 3% in 1H25. Based on a report, cargo dwell time has worsened across Mexico’s international ports due to longer customs processes and other bottlenecks in port operations. Recall that last week, we reported that ICTSI’s port in Mexico (CMSA) saw volumes drop 12% YoY in July, extending the 14% decline in June and reversing the +10% growth rate in 5M25. Management noted that the onboarding of new customs officials (previous officials were replaced after strike in May) has prolonged processing times at the port of Manzanillo. We anticipate the volume weakness was likely mitigated by higher storage revenues.

SMPH REIT IPO to be conducted after 2026

During the PSE STAR Investor Day, SM Prime CFO John Ong said that SM Prime’s REIT IPO will take place after 2026. He said that the company has to take market conditions into account. Mr. Ong also said that strong cash flows give the company flexibility on funding. We estimate that a mall REIT IPO could raise SM Prime’s NAV by up to 20%. In other matters, the company will unveil the master plan for its 360-hectare reclamation project, called Pasay 360, within the year.

Ayala Land appoints new CFO

Ayala Land appointed Mr. Jose Eduardo A. Quimpo II as Chief Finance Officer (CFO) and Treasurer, in addition to his roles as Head of Corporate Finance and Chief Risk Officer. Mr. Quimpo joined Ayala Land in May 2024 and served as President of AREIT from August 2024 until last week. Prior to joining Ayala Land, he had been in various roles in corporate finance, capital markets, and risk management in Philippine banks for over 20 years. He will replace Mr. Augusto D. Bengzon, who has been with Ayala Land for 21 years and served as CFO for 8 years. Mr. Bengzon will remain a member of Ayala Land’s management committee and a director of various subsidiaries, including AyalaLand Logistics Holdings Corp (ALLHC PM) and Avaland Berhad (AVALAND MK).

Cebu Air July passenger volumes flat YoY

Cebu Air reported its passenger volumes stood at 2.1mn in July, unchanged from the same period last year. Seat capacity rose 4% YoY in July, resulting in seat load factors (SLF) falling by 320bps to 85.6%. Cited the earlier start of the academic calendar (June from July last year)

This brings 7M25 pax volumes to 16mn, up 18% YoY. We’ve baked in a +16% YoY increase in 25E pax volumes to 28.5mn. The seasonal adjustment is not a cause for concern, as all airlines similarly scale back capacity during the lean period. We note that SLF in July is still higher than the load factors in 3Q24, suggesting fares/yields may have gone up.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in July

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 10bps MoM to 18.8% (in July) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 8bps MoM to 23.9%. Note that high foreign-owned stocks such as MONDE, TEL, and ICT outperformed the market in July. Net foreign selling fell to US$29mn, from net selling of US$72mn in June.

Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 12 saw increases in foreign ownership while 17 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

GTCAP – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership. Foreign ownership rose 1.20 percentage points MoM to 23.0%, as net foreign buying hit US$23.6mn, the second highest for the month.

BLOOM – foreign ownership climbed 1.03 percentage points MoM to 12.3%, as it experienced net foreign buying of US$6.7mn. It previously saw the biggest declines in foreign ownership for three consecutive months.  

AREIT – foreign ownership declined 3.72 percentage points MoM to 4.4%. We suspect that the 4.1% percentage point increase in June was an error.

PGOLD – foreign ownership fell 66bps MoM to 17.6%.

BOP shifts to US$167mn deficit in July; GIR settles at US$105.4bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$167mn, marking a reversal from both the US$62bn surplus in July 2024 and June 2025's US$226mn surplus. The deficit reflected the national gov't's drawdowns on its foreign currency deposits with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) to service debt obligations. This brings the YT-July deficit at US$5.8bn, reversing from the US$1.5bn surplus in the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade in goods gap and external debt repayments.

The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (-1.3% of GDP). This reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments.

Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-July stood at US$105.4bn, down from the $106bn in end-June. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025.

Infrastructure spending picks up in June, reversing slump during election ban

Overall infrastructure spending (incl. infra components of subsidy and equity to GOCCs and LGU transfers) rose 17% MoM and 3% YoY to P162.5bn in June. This was driven by a rebound in public works spending after declining in April/May or during the election ban. Officials also cited base effects from sizable releases for the Dept. of National Defense’s (DND) modernization program in June last year (bulk of DND’s big-ticket releases are expected in 2H25). This brings 1H25 infra spend to P720bn, flat YoY and broadly in line with the 1H government’s target. The government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP).

OFW Remittances up 3.7% YoY in June

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.7% YoY to US$3.0bn, accelerating from the 2.9% growth seen in May. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, and Saudi Arabia were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 3.5% YoY from 3.1% in May. In PHP terms, remittances declined by 0.5% YoY given the appreciation of the peso.

July Digital payments jump 44% YoY, 7% MoM, a new record

Transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) hit P2,135bn ($37.2bn) in July, setting yet another record high, rising 44.4% YoY and 6.7% MoM. This put the 7-month total at P13,261bn, up 40% YoY, faster than the 35% growth pace set in FY2024.

Payments over PesoNet – larger, batched transactions – stood at P1,151bn, up 32.7% YoY.  While this was a deceleration from the 47% YoY clip in June, the July figure was still up 6% MoM. For 7M25, PesoNet payments of P7,300bn were up 31% YoY, compared to the 29% recorded in 2024.

Meanwhile, real-time, smaller-ticket transactions over InstaPay continued to surge, up 60.9% YoY and 7% MoM to P983bn, and seem to be fast closing in on the size of PesoNet. The July growth was also fastest YoY pace since late 2021, when adoption exploded during the Covid pandemic. Year-to-date, InstaPay payments were P5,961bn, up 53% YoY, still accelerating from the 46% growth in 2024.

The continued heady pace of growth is both surprising and encouraging, showing that adoption of digital payments, particularly at the retail consumer level, still has lots of headroom. As we pointed out in our report last month, the BSP is constantly pushing for innovations in the fintech space to further improve financial inclusion.

DICT Secretary expects that the Konektadong Pinoy bill will be signed into law

Dept. of Information and Communication Technology (DICT) Secretary Henry Aguda stated that his department remains confident that President Marcos Jr. will sign the proposed bill into law. He highlights that the President supports it and emphasizes that the bill is "not against telcos, it is pro-consumer." Aguda also added that the telcos have the right to legally challenge the measure if it is enacted or lapses into law. Recall that the Konektadong Pinoy bill will remove the Congressional franchise requirement for data providers, lowering the barrier to entry with the aim to expand internet access in the country

The listed telco players have stated their varying opinions on the bill:

PLDT, Inc. (TEL PM) and Converge ICT Solutions, Inc. (CNVRG PM) stated that they are willing to give their inputs on the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the Konektadong Pinoy bill, if it were to lapse into law

DITO Telecommunity Corp., subsidiary of DITO CME (DITO PM), supports the call for a level playing field, that entrants should follow the same rules as the incumbents

Globe Telecom, Inc. (GLO PM) maintains its stance that the bill should be vetoed and sent back to Congress, stating that "we cannot support the bill in its current state or form"

PAGCOR: online gambling transactions fall 50%

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that online gambling transactions on regulated sites have dropped by 50% since the BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms. This is based on data from August 17 to 19. However, he said that illegal operators continue to thrive – these are predominantly based in Russia, Dubai, Cambodia, and Singapore. PAGCOR tracks about 12,000 illegal sites and taken down 8,000 of them. Meanwhile there are 77 licensed online gaming operators. Mr. Tengco said that about 60% of the online gaming market is illegal.

PAGCOR expects revenue to grow c. 5% in 2025-2026

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco expects the agency’s revenue to rise 4% in 2025 (to P117bn) and to grow another 6% in 2026 (to P124bn). PAGCOR previously said it expected the country’s GGR to hit P450bn-P480bn this year, a 20%-30% increase from P372bn in 2024. Note that 1H25 GGR grew 26% to P215bn. Recall that in 2024, PAGCOR lowered the fees it charges on online gaming operators by c. 12ppt to 38%.

Senator wants owners and operators of online gambling firms to be made public

Senator Juan Miguel “Migz” Zubiri wants the next senate hearings on online gambling to focus on individuals who own and operate the online gambling firms. He also wants to know their income and the number of users on the platforms. He said they would share the results of the hearing to the media.

We estimate that DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is owned by: Mr. Eusebio Tanco – 26%, Mr. Alfredo Benitez – 11%, Foreign group – 18%

Separately, The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) said that beneficiaries of cash assistance programs who gamble could be removed from the program. Note that there are about 4mn families are beneficiaries of the DSWD’s 4Ps program.
