REGIS REPORT

21 March 2025

The PCOMP held in a narrow range, ending 0.4% or 27.36 pts lower WoW to 6,266.75 as investors digested another round of 4Q earnings reports along with latest cues from the central bank on monetary policy and the macro outlook. The market also remained relatively supported by foreign inflows that have persisted throughout much of the month.

 

We ended the week with the FTSE March rebalancing that saw the inclusion of PLUS (-1.1% WoW) and RCR (-2.6% WoW), though both names ended lower for the week as investors used the liquidity event to take profit following the stocks' recent strength.

 

PCOMP breadth was quite balanced with 16 advancers against 14 decliners. CBC (+7.7% WoW) led the positive index movers amid speculation of potential index inclusions in regional benchmarks. 4Q earnings results also dictated the direction for some large movers. CNVRG (+13.8% WoW) broke out of its range to a high last seen in Jan 2023 after results came in-line, with market-leading net adds in 4Q and a decent dividend payout equivalent to 4% yield at the time of the announcement. URC (+7.0% WoW) was also in-line, with continued market share losses in the domestic branded business made up for by lower costs that drove double-digit growth in the segment's operating income. JFC (-8.5% WoW) was the biggest drag on the index this week after missing 4Q earnings expectations, with a mild disappointment in operations exacerbated by a one-time loss related to its acquisition of Titan, and deteriorating performance of Smashburger and its China business.

 

Other earnings releases in focus this week included AC (-2.8% WoW) that also disappointed due to the GLO (+4.4% WoW) and ACEN (-0.7% WoW) earnings misses as well as continuing losses in its smaller, non-listed subsidiaries. DMC (+3.0% WoW) likewise missed forecasts dragged by SCC (+4.7% WoW), construction delays, and property revenue reversals.

 

The IPO space has also been heating up. Maynilad (which is 25%-owned by DMC) this week filed for its P49.2-bn ($850m) IPO with the SEC, targeting a July 10 listing. Meanwhile, leadership of GLO's GCash earlier in the week reiterated its intent to list with 10-15% public float for its target valuation of $8bn, given that the 20% minimum level required by the SEC would entail a sizable $1.6-bn IPO. By mid-week, the SEC had approved the PSE's proposal to relax the minimum float requirement to 15% for large IPOs offering at least P5bn ($87m).

 

On macro, the BSP signaled the high likelihood of a 25-bp rate cut at the April 10 meeting, especially if March inflation turns out better than expected. BSP Gov. Remolona guided a base case of 50bps in rate cuts for the year, with a possibility for 75bps should economic growth fail to meet expectations. The BSP in its latest Monetary Policy Report also warned of a possible breach of its 2-4% inflation target range later in the year, and a consequent slowdown in economic activity with 2025-26 growth seen settling near the lower bound of the Marcos administration's 6-8% target range.

 

Foreigners remained net buyers for a third straight week, by $40m. Philippines remains the only Asian market that is seeing positive foreign equity inflows MTD according to Bloomberg data. ADTO higher at $136m due to the FTSE rebalancing.

 

Peso slightly down WoW, by 0.1% to 57.330:$1. PH10Y yield still flattish at 6.2425%.

Ayala Corp - FY24 income up 10% to P42bn; losses at smaller units not fully over
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Profits up 10% to P42bn

Ayala Corp reported headline income of P42bn, up 10% YoY.  Its core income, as reported by AC, was also 10% higher at P45bn. We believe this is below Street expectations. Beyond the profit underperformances at Globe and ACEN, losses at smaller and unlisted subs are not quite behind us. The company also took large impairment losses for its stake in the LRT-1 operation as well as for the last mile logistics provider Entrego.

 

AC Industrials still in the red as IMI losses balloon

IMI reported a net loss of US$50m for FY24, implying a loss of US$40m for 4Q alone. The losses were partly due to clean-up and turnaround initiatives, including impairment charges as well as the costs of plant shutdown/s and rationalization.  For the year, the Industrials segment (of which IMI is the biggest part) posted a loss of P2.4bn. AC management expects IMI to return to profitability this year (on “core” operations) with a full turnaround in 2-3 years, after which, we believe, a divestment may finally be on the cards again.

 

Reason to be optimistic at AC Mobility

Vehicle sales under AC Mobility rose 46% to 23.5k but investors should be focusing on its BYD business.  From virtually nothing in FY23, the number of BYD units sold hit 5,106 (which is ~1% market share).  We believe market share can exceed 3% this year, if not more.  

AC estimates it had 82% of the “NEV” market (EVs and hybrids) already.  To boost adoption, it plans to have 700 charging points by end-2025 (from 215 at end-2024). AC Mobility, however, still reported a P465m loss in FY24.

 

AC Health losses widen

Losses at AC Health widened to P610m (vs P233m), partly due to impairment charges for Konsulta MD (KMD), the online health app that was eventually sold to the Metro Pacific group. AC Health’s target to reach EBITDA of P1b and profit breakeven has now been pushed back by one year to 2025.

 

AC Logistics secures partner

AC Logistics (ACL) saw losses rising to P2.2bn (vs P1.8bn).  ACL and AC signed an agreement for Copenhagen-based AP Möller Capital (APMC) to take up to 40% interest in AC Logistics. This will hopefully provide expertise for ACL to finally turn things around.

 

Parent capex at P31bn, bulk for ACEN

For FY25, AC earmarked P31bn for capex at the holdco level of which more than half (P15.9bn) will be for ACEN.  ACEN’s intention to raise capital this year is well-flagged and this info from AC should give an idea of how much may raise. Much of the rest of the AC parent capex will be for AC Mobility and ACL.

 
Jollibee - FY24: Domestic business still carrying the day; Smashburger and China struggle
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Eyes on the prize

Jollibee (JFC) delivered net income of P10.3bn for FY24, 18% higher YoY.  While it can be said that the bottom line figure is within expectations, there was a wide variance in the performance across the various segments. The domestic business remains robust but the same cannot be said of Smashburger (SB) and its China operation. Management is resolute about reaching its target of trebling profit (to P24bn) by 2028. Reaching this target will depend on a turnaround of the underperforming units, which will be quite challenging.

 

Domestic operations carried the business even more

For FY24, JFC’s domestic business produced systemwide sales (SWS) of P232bn and revenue of P164bn, up +10% and +9% YoY. respectively. SSSG stayed a sold 7.5% for the year and 4Q.  EBIT for the year, on the other hand, grew +5.5% to P14.7bn, with 4Q EBIT affected by wage hikes and seasonal A&P expenditures. As reported by JFC, FY24 net income for the domestic business amounted to P11.4bn. This figure implies that the international businesses as a group posted a net loss.

 

Revenue challenges for China and Smashburger

The challenges facing the China business look quite daunting as SSS fell 11.2% for the year, which was blamed on the broad consumer environment in that market.

Meanwhile, for SB, after getting into the black, EBITDA slipped back into the red in FY24. Revenue also remains the key challenge as SSS dropped 2.7%. After another management change, JFC will again try to revamp SB’s menu.

 

Tim Ho Wan takeover completed

The purchase and transfer of the Tim Ho Wan (THW) operation from the private equity fund to directly under JFC resulted in a one-off adjustment that cut profit by close to P600m in 4Q. The company is guiding for THW to broadly break even this year.

 

Near-term guidance and medium-term targets

Management is sticking to its medium target of reaching P24bn of net profit by 2028. Simplistically, this translates to profit CAGR of ~24% from FY24. This is premised on both China and SB reaching breakeven by 2026 and contributing positively by 2027 -- a tall order, in our view. For this year (2025), JFC is providing guidance of group-wide SSSG of 4-6%, SWS growth of 8-12% and operating income growth of 10-15%.

 
Universal Robina - FY24: Results as expected; branded business still under pressure
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Net profit dips 5%

URC reported profit to shareholders of P11.4bn, 5% lower YoY on revenue growth of +3%. Excluding the losses from its discontinued operations (in China), profit would have been up down 2% to P11.8bn. We view the results as within our expectations. Over the last few quarters, the company’s core branded business (BCF) benefitted from more favorable input costs, in the absence of top line growth. We, however, do expect some improvement this year.

 

EBIT growth there …

Consolidated EBIT dipped 4%, as the period of supernormal profits at its sugar business ended, and rather abruptly. Excluding the sugar business, however, all of URC’s other operations delivered what looks like a solid performance: EBIT growth of 20%. Much of that was a result of improved costs as revenues were only modestly up.

 

Top line for Domestic BCF remains under pressure

Revenue for Domestic BCF was down 1% for the year and 3% for 4Q alone. The softness in top line can be traced in part to the overall macro consumption environment. This has been evident across many of the consumer names over the last few quarters. Part of the weakness in revenue, however, is also directly attributable to market share losses across key categories (snacks, noodles, biscuits, coffee etc.).

International BCF a bright spot

The international BCF business was the key bright spot. Revenues grew 8% even as EBIT margins expanded more than 300bp over the course of the year.

 

Organizational adjustments

As we noted in a previous report, URC undertook some organization changes over the last couple of months, which include the appointment of a president for the Domestic BCF segment as well as a new CFO. The fruits of these changes, if any, are likely to be visible only after a few quarters.

 

Guarded optimism for FY25

Management sounded, to use the cliché, cautiously optimistic for this year. Against the backdrop of an improving consumer environment (lower food prices, higher wages), it is guiding for revenue growth in the “high single digit range” and profit growth in “mid-single digits”. The lower growth in profits (vs revenue) is a consequence of higher A&P spend programmed to boost revenue. We welcome management’s focus on revenue and regaining market share, rather than on near-term margins.

 
DMCI - 4Q24: Property, construction, cement units face challenges
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY24 profits fall 21% to P19bn, below consensus estimates

DMCI’s core profit (ex-FX adj) fell 11% YoY to P4.0bn in 4Q24, pulled down by Semirara (see note), construction delays, and property revenue reversals. For the full year, core profit dropped 21% YoY to P18.97bn, below consensus forecast but in line with ours. 

 

Property: Unsold inventory climbs; FY25 project launches on pause

Profit contributions from the property unit fell 35% YoY in FY24 and 34% in 4Q24, as booked revenues dropped due to revenue reversals. Underlying demand was particularly soft in 4Q24, with reservation sales in the quarter falling to a three-year low of P4.9bn, despite the launch of its first project in Cebu. As a result, (a) FY24 sales of P33bn (-7% YoY) came in below guidance (P35bn) and (b) unsold inventory climbed to P92bn, equivalent to almost three years’ worth of sales. Launches are on pause this year, as management focuses on selling its inventory, 33% of which are RFO units. One bright spot is that net debt levels dropped 23% YoY to P25bn by end-2024.

 

Construction unit incurs loss in 4Q

The construction unit incurred a P220mn net loss in 4Q, as management recognized input costs for revenues that they believe can still be claimed in the future. The order book dwindled to P40.6bn, after booked revenues outpaced project awards. The investment environment remains challenging, but management is hopeful more projects will be bid out in 2H.   

 

 

Water: FY24 spending trails regulatory commitment

Maynilad’s standalone profits rose 40% to P12.8bn in FY24, lifted by tariff hikes. However, combined spending on cash opex and capex was only up 1% to P36.5bn in FY24, trailing its 2023-2027 regulatory commitment of P43bn annually. Management plans to spend P42bn on capex in 2025, which should more than make up for last year’s spending shortfall. DMCI’s management also revealed that incumbent shareholders could likely be diluted pari-passu if Maynilad’s IPO pushes through.

 

Strong 4Q24 rebound for nickel mining

Profits from nickel mining rebounded sharply in 4Q, after ASPs surged and offset lower shipments. The company is guiding for its annual production to double this year from 1.5mn in 2025 and reach 5-6mn WMT by 2026, once it secures permits for its mine in Palawan by mid-year. Management said that most of its mining tenements will unlikely be affected by the proposed 50-year mining permit moratorium of the Palawan provincial council.

 

Cement: Local government suspends limestone quarry

Shortly after taking over the operations of Cemex (CHP PM) in December, the local government of Cebu issued a cease-and-desist order against its limestone supplier, which has affected production of its APO plant (70% of capacity). It is unclear at this stage when the order will be lifted.

Converge profit rises 19% to P10.8bn for FY24

Converge ICT reported profit for FY24 of P10.8bn (+19%), which appears in line with the Street. Revenues grew a healthy 15%, led by the Enterprise segment (+22%). Residential revenues grew 14%. Profit for 4Q alone, however, was P2.6bn -- down 11% QoQ and 5% YoY. There was a large jump in G&A expenses (+76% or + P1.2bn). Management says it was due to a rise in the cost of managed services. Converge added a net +104k of subscribers for the last quarter, roughly the same as 3Q. The company is maintaining lead in wireline broadband sub base, with roughly half of all new subscribers. The company declared a dividend of P0.43/share, equivalent to ~30% of previous year’s earnings. This is CNVRG’s second dividend (after P0.18/sh in Sept 2024). Management is aiming for net subscriber gains of 100k per quarter and offered guidance of revenue growth of 14-16%. Capex for FY22 is expected to reach P20-25bn (vs P9bn in FY24), with P12bn for special projects, including the investment in two international cable systems and two data centers (30MW capacity).

Aboitiz Power core profit up 5% for the year

FY24 core profit rose 5% YoY to P33.7bn, with beneficial EBITDA up 13% YoY to P73bn. Derived 4Q24 EBITDA was P17.2bn while up 20% YoY, was actually down 13% QoQ due to seasonal factors. AP also booked ~P1bn of income in 4Q related to the suspended reserve market collections in 1Q. Note income growth is lower than EBITDA growth in FY24 principally because of the depreciation and financing charges from GNPD, which became fully operational in 2Q24. Despite management’s subdued view on WESM prices and its spot exposure of ~30%, management believes it can maintain generation margins this year, given (a) lower Newcastle thermal coal and (b) the recently signed emergency power contracts. The company aims to lower its spot exposure to 10% by year-end. AP started operating 500MW of RE capacity in 2024, taking its total RE capacity to 1.4GW, accounting for ~28% of its 5GW genco portfolio. Another 388 MW of solar plants could be added this year, with the company targeting to reach 4.6GW of RE capacity by 2030. Note AP’s purchase, through the SPV “Chromite,” of an effective 26% stake (40% of 66%) in the 2,500MW gas plants of San Miguel (SMC PM) was also completed last month. Given these expansions, the company is earmarking P78bn (attributable is >P50bn) for capex this year, up from last year’s P27bn. Energy sales from its distribution arm rose by 7% YoY in 4Q24, a notch lower than the 8% rate in 9M24. Residential demand slowed to 9% in 4Q while commercial/industrial was steady at +5%. However, demand has moderated in 1Q25 so far, specifically in the commercial/industrial segments in Cebu and Davao. The slowdown was also apparent in its RES clients in the cement and steel sectors.

Cebu Air - Feb pax volumes up 26% YoY, driven by domestic +29% YoY

Pax volumes rose 26% YoY to 2.2mn in February 2025, with domestic volumes growing by 29% and international up 20%. Growth moderated from the 33% pace in January due to the timing of Lunar New Year. Seat load factors rose by 154bps YoY and 30bps MoM to 86.8%, as the market continues to fully absorb its capacity growth, which in Feb 2025 expanded by 24% YoY. The sequential improvement in load factors suggests that the market has absorbed the incremental capacity that was introduced in Sep 2024. This brings 2M25 pax volumes up 30% YoY to 4.7mn, trending ahead of our FY25 pax volume forecast of 27.7mn (+13% YoY).

BSP to cut its policy rate by 50-75 bps this year, hinged on inflation and growth

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Remolona stated the high likelihood that the Monetary Board (MB) will cut on April 10, especially if inflation beats expectations on April 4. Recall that February inflation posted at 2.1%, bringing the YTD average to 2.5%—within the BSP's target range of 2-4%. Consensus expects 75 bps worth of rate cuts to 5.00% for 2025. We expect 50 bps worth of cuts.Remolona guided a base case of up to 50 bps worth of rate cuts but also hinted on the possibility of up to 75 bps should the economy output turn out to be weaker than expected. This is as 2024 GDP disappointed at 5.6% below the 6-8% gov't target, and with 2025-2026 GDP expected to settle within the lower end of the range given elevated global macro uncertainties (i.e., commodity prices, trade outlooks, etc.). 1Q25 GDP is set to be released on May 8 with consensus expecting 5.7% growth for the first quarter. While consensus and Regis expectations for FY25 GDP is 6.00%. The BSP Governor also commented that there will be negative spillover effects of the tariff war on the Philippines. As multiple countries raising tariffs will slow down growth and heighten inflation, he emphasized that the mere uncertainty of how much negative impact is the bigger factor in itself. However, he noted that the hit on the Philippine economy will only be "modest." In another article, a BSP MB member cited that the country's information technology and business process management (IT-BPM) sector may be at risk, given that 70% of its clients originate from the United States.

OFW Remittances up 2.9% YoY in January

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.9% YoY to US$2.92bn, but down 13.7% MoM given December's seasonal strength. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances, posting significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 0.9% YoY from 0.6% in December. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 7.9%, slower relative to the months of November and December but faster than the growth seen in September and October. This year's BSP's remittances target growth rate has yet to be disclosed as of late. Recall that last year's growth of 3.0% was in-line with the BSP target of 3.0%.

PH current account deficit widens to $17.5bn in 2024, missing BSP target

The country posted its second-largest CA deficit at $17.5bn, equivalent to 3.8% of GDP—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of $12.4bn or 2.8% of GDP. For context, 2022's CA deficit was the record largest at $18.3 or 4.5% of GDP. The 2024 deficit breached the BSP's forecast of a $10.4bn (2.2% of GDP) CA deficit for the year. The larger-than-expected deficit was driven by the Philippines' heavy import bill and slower services trade. Recall that the country imported $68.7bn more goods than what was exported in 2024, primarily due to the FY24 seeing higher consumer goods imports, which were up 6% YoY to $25.8bn, and lower electronics exports, down 7% to $38.1bn. There was also smaller net earnings posted from services trade to $14.6bn from $18.2bn in 2023. Service exports were only up 7.5% to $52bn relative to the 24% jump in service imports to $37bn. These led to more dollar outflows, which added pressure on peso volatility. For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to be $12.1bn or 2.4% of GDP.

January gov't budget surplus down 22.3% YoY to P68bn, -22.3% YoY

The government recorded a budget surplus of P68bn, which is down 22.3% from January 2024's surplus of P88bn but a reversal from the deficit of P330bn in December. Revenue collections in January grew 10.8% YoY to P467bn, primarily driven by growth in tax revenues, which is up 13.6% YoY. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 15.1% to P355bn, mainly due to an increase in VAT and income taxes as well as stronger collection efforts, crackdown on illicit trade, and digital transformation projects. Customs saw 8% growth to P79bn in collections from higher VAT and excise collections. Non-tax revenues dipped 26% to P30bn, given the one-time gain recognized in January 2024. Expenditures jumped 19.5% YoY in January to P399bn as both primary expenses and interest payments were up 13.4% and 40.7%, respectively. Primary spending for the month rose 13.4% YoY to P294bn. The increase in primary spending was attributed to the (a) infrastructure/other capital outlays of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), (b) health and social protection programs, (c) higher National Tax Allotments (NTA) releases to gov't corporations, and (d) Commission on Elections (COMELEC) expenses for the 2025 National and Local Elections preparatory activities. Interest payments surged 40.7% given shifts in coupon payment timing with the re-issuance of treasury bonds that were issued in January last year alongside an earlier coupon payment for a Global Bond.

BOP surplus at US$3.1bn in February 2025; GIR rises to US$107.4bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$3.1bn, a significant turnaround from both January 2025's $4.1bn deficit and the $196mn deficit in February 2024. The surplus reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits, which include ROP Global Bonds proceeds and foreign investments' net income. This brings the YTD deficit to $992mn as of February 2025, slightly higher than the $936mn deficit a year ago. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-February 2025 stood at US$107.4bn, up from the US$103.3bn in end-January 2025. GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income.

BSP wary of possible inflation target breach and slower economic growth

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its latest Monetary Policy Report stated that inflation could exceed the target range of 2-4% in the latter part of 2025, given risks of supply chain disruptions, volatile commodity prices, and forex fluctuations. Specific key risk factors that will lead to a target breach cited by the BSP were geopolitical tensions/production cuts pushing Dubai crude oil prices to average $100/pb in 2025, food supply disruptions (i.e., African swine fever), depreciation of the peso, potential wage hikes as well as transport and utility rate adjustments. The continued decline in rice prices, however, should alleviate risks to guide inflation lower. The baseline inflation forecast for 2025 was revised to 3.5% from 3.3% last December but maintained its 3.5% 2026 projection. While the risk-adjusted inflation forecast was also raised to 3.5% from 3.4%, it kept unchanged its 3.7% 2026 projection. Considering inflation risks, the BSP guided that economic activity may be negatively impacted—with 2025-2026 growth projected to be "near the lower bound" of the Marcos administration's target range of 6-8%.

Lower float requirement for large IPOs approved by the SEC

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) approved the PSE's proposal to grant an exemption to large IPOs of P5bn or more to initially offer 15% with a commitment to conduct a follow-on offering or private placement within two to three years to be compliant with the minimum 20% public float requirement. PSE President Ramon Monzon stated that GCash's planned listing, given its size that is "too big for the market to absorb," may not be required to do a follow-on but may have to do so anyway given the index's strict 20% minimum public float. Monzon also elaborated that the policy is not permanent and will be in place while liquidity problems persist, as the policy will be reassessed for its effectiveness in the future. This policy comes to light with GCash's upcoming IPO (tentatively by end-2025) as Globe Telecom's (GLO PM) President and CEO Ernest Cu cited that they are eyeing an $8bn valuation. Assuming they offer 15%, it implies an IPO of $1.2bn, which in peso terms would be larger than the P48.6bn raised by Monde Nissin Corp. (MONDE PM) with their IPO back in 2021.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in February

We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 7bps MoM to 19.3% (in February), while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 44bps MoM to 24.4%. The reduction in foreign ownership of the PSE Index was partly due to the index rebalancing – CBC and AREIT have lower ownership than NIKL and WLCON, which they replaced in the PSEi. Net foreign selling increased to US$145mn, from US$113mn in January. Of the 28 PSEi constituents that remained, 10 saw increases in foreign ownership, while 18 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were: MBT – foreign ownership fell 53bps MoM to 20.8%. It also saw the third-highest net foreign selling for the month, at US$28.4mn; BLOOM – foreign ownership dropped 36bps MoM to 14.9%; ALI – foreign ownership declined 31bps MoM to 26.0%, as it experienced $38.5mn of net foreign outflow, the highest in the market; AC – foreign ownership increased 33bps MoM to 27.1%; GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 23bps MoM to 20.6%; and BPI – foreign ownership increased 22bps MoM to 18.8%. It also saw the highest foreign buying for the month, at US$12.9mn.

Vehicle sales rise 2.9% YoY in February; Toyota sales up 9%

CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales of 39,164 units, up 2.9% YoY and 4.1% MoM. This brings 2M25 vehicle sales to 76,768 units, up 6.4% YoY. Sales of electric vehicles reached 3,416 units in 2M25, with hybrid EVs at 3,034 units, followed by battery EV at 335 units and plug-in hybrid EV at 47 units. Note that CAMPI/TMA data does not include BYD. CAMPI is targeting sales of 500k units for 2025 (+7% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 18,528 units in February, higher by 9.1% YoY and 2.5% MoM. This brings 2M25 retail sales up 10.7% YoY to 36,606 units. TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year. To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 19.8k units per month. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses. GTCAP is trading at a 59% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,260/sh and 4.5x FY25E PER.

Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences sees 8 from the Marcos-admin slate

Based on the Pulse Asia survey conducted in late February, 13 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 available seats in the upcoming midterm elections on May 12, 2025. Bong Go (58.1%) and Erwin Tulfo (56.6%) secured the top spots, ranking 1st-2nd. Tito Sotto (49.0%) and Bong Revilla (46.1%) followed closely, ranking 3rd-6th. Bato Dela Rosa (44.3%), Willie Revillame (42.3%), and Ben Tulfo (40.7%) placed 4th-11th. Manny Pacquiao (39.9%), Lito Lapid (39.4%), Abby Binay (37.6%), and Pia Cayetano (37.5%) ranked between 6th-13th. Camille Villar (36.6%) and Ping Lacson (35.8%) secured places 8th-13th. Notably, only four of the probable 13 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go and Dela Rosa are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo and Revillame are independent. This was Pulse Asia's latest senatorial preferences survey after the filing of certificates of candidacy last October.
