REGIS REPORT

23 May 2025

The PCOMP broke a five-week winning streak, pulling back by 0.8% or 52.43 pts WoW to 6,413.10 as local shares succumbed to foreign selling that hit its highest weekly level since mid-April.

 

Early in the week, the index had retreated below 6,400 following weakness in global markets after Moody's downgraded the US' credit rating. Later, the PCOMP further dropped to its lowest level since late-April as investors weighed Pres. Marcos' call for courtesy resignations across his entire cabinet. A reversal in foreign flows to net buying come Friday helped the index climb back above 6,400 to end the week.

 

CBC (-12.7% WoW) was the biggest drag on the index as selling from the previous week persisted after it failed to meet inclusion expectations at a major rebalancing event for end-May. SMPH (-5.1% WoW) was also a big drag, falling on heavy foreign outflows.

 

Amid risk-off mood for much of the week, banks reigned supreme once more with BDO (+2.9% WoW) and BPI (+2.9% WoW) the top positive movers for the index for the week. Meanwhile, JGS (+10.0% WoW) was the index's biggest outperformer as bargain hunters turned more confident on prospects following the company's announcement of an extended plant shutdown for its loss-making petrochemical business.

 

On domestic macro, the Philippines' balance of payments (BoP) deficit widened to $2.6bn in April, bringing the YTD-April deficit to $5.5bn as against the $401-m deficit in the same period last year. The central bank projects FY25 BoP deficit to settle at $4bn, or -0.8% of GDP, reflecting expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given global uncertainties around trade and geopolitics.

 

By Friday afternoon, the administration had already received 52 official resignations from 52 cabinet members and government heads, with Pres. Marcos declining the courtesy recognitions of the ff. officials handling critical posts related to managing the country's economic affairs: (i) Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin, (ii) Trade Secretary Cristina Roque, (iii) Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, (iv) National Economic and Development Authority Secretary Arsenio Balisacan, (v) Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman, and (vi) Special Assistant to the President for Investment & Economic Affairs Frederick Go.

 

Foreign outflows for the week accelerated to $32m while ADTO softened WoW to $119m.

 

The peso bounced, stronger by 0.7% WoW to 55.265:$1. Yields continued to inch higher with the PH10Y +9.6bps to 6.2693%.

GT Capital - A very good 1Q25 outcome
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q25 core profit up 27% to P8.6bn – Ahead of Regis and Street estimates Headline 1Q25 net income rose 29% YoY to P9.14bn. Stripping out one-offs at associate Metro Pacific (MPI) and removing pref dividends, core income stood at P8.61bn, up 27% YoY and on trend to beat Regis and Street forecasts. While Metrobank (here) was within expectations, contributions from Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) and MPI came in stronger than expected.

 

Auto: Retail sales up 12%, GP margins jump to near decade high of 16.8% Standalone profits from TMP rose 67% QoQ and 57% YoY to P6.33bn in 1Q25, boosted by a 12% improvement in retail sales, which mildly outpaced the industry’s growth of 10%. GPM likewise rose to its highest level since 1Q16 at 16.8% due to favorable currency movements and models mix. We agree with management that this margin level is unlikely to be sustained and should normalize back to 12-13%, once pent-up demand of higher-margin vehicles wanes and PHP weakens against the USD. Meanwhile, TMP estimates that industry-wide sales of electric vehicles (including BYD) grew 129% YoY to 9.3k units in 1Q25. 

Combined EV sales of Toyota and Lexus rose by 36% YoY, lagging the growth of other new energy vehicles (NEV) following the market introduction of PHEVs, which mostly came from BYD.

 

Meralco, Maynilad lift MPI’s 1Q25 profits up 18% Infrastructure arm, Metro Pacific, grew its standalone core income by 18% to P6.6bn, owing to the robust growth of Meralco’s (MER PM) generation business and tariff hikes at Maynilad.

 

Property: Reservation sales up 28% QoQ, 51% YoY Reservation sales rose 28% QoQ, 51% YoY to P5.9bn driven by commercial lot sales in Cavite and sales of residual units at its projects in BGC. Inventory levels are down to a year’s worth of sales.

 

Parent net debt declines further Net debt (incl. prefs) at the parent fell further to P60bn in end-March from P64bn in end-2024 and the peak of P88bn at end-2018. It does not plan to raise dividends further for now as it continues to look for acquisitions.

 
ACEN Corp - Waiting for clouds to clear
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Downgrade to Hold

The growth in ACEN’s expansion pipeline is moving more quickly than either management or Regis have anticipated, which has consequently led to an accelerated need for capital. Project execution and day-to-day operations, however, have not been proceeding as smoothly. We cut our rating to Hold, given the near-term overhang from the announced capital raise and pending evidence that 2H targets can be met.

 

Extended repairs cause 16% YoY drop in 1Q25 profit

ACEN reported its 1Q25 profit fell 16% YoY to P1.95bn due to the prolonged repair of its wind farms in north Luzon, lower solar output due, and lower spot prices. Profitability was also weighed down by the timing of recognition of non-cash and interest expenses of new plants. Management conveyed optimism that it would be on much better footing in 2H, given the expected completion of repairs and the energization of ~800MW of new capacity across its international markets.

 

Estimates recalibrated, Pricing in SRO

We cut our FY25/FY26 EPS forecasts by 63%/39% after incorporating (1) 1Q25 actuals, (2) revised projections on financing costs and project completions, as well as (3) the impact of its planned stock rights offer.

Our new numbers assume ACEN will raise the maximum amount of P30bn at the minimum price of P2.30/sh by July 2025, increasing share count by 33%.

 

Strong growth outlook but near-term issues abound

ACEN’s operating capacity could double to 6GW by 2028 from just 3GW in 2024, which we estimate could translate to an EBITDA CAGR of 21% and EPS CAGR of 28% over this period. Execution risks remain high, delays are not uncommon, and the multitude of projects (17 and counting) makes their management more difficult. Moreover, ACEN’s business has a high degree of operating and financial leverage, which increases earnings volatility.

 

Revising TP to P3.20/sh (-55%)

Our revised TP of P3.20/sh is based on a sum-of-the-parts valuation, using discounted cash flow analysis of each project. At the current share price, ACEN trades at 24x PER, which is above peer average. Multiples, however, should decline as more projects come online. Risks relate to execution, merchant exposure, technological developments, and regulation.

AGI core income rises 18% YoY

AGI’s attributable net income jumped 100% YoY to P8.4bn due to a non-recurring gain from the deconsolidation of Golden Arches. Removing this gain, recurring profit grew 18% QoQ and YoY to P5bn. GGR of Travellers rose 5% YoY (+4% QoQ) to P7.9bn thanks to a better VIP hold rate. VIP volume rose slightly QoQ but fell slightly YoY. This is better than the performance of other integrated resorts in Entertainment City, which saw GGR fall on a QoQ and YoY basis. Travellers applied for an e-games license and plans to expand its online business. Megaworld’s 1Q25 profit rose 16% YoY to P5.1bn. Reservation sales fell 10% QoQ and 26% YoY to P26.9bn. Office occupancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 87%. EMI 1Q25 net income increased 5% YoY to P1.8bn. Recovery in brandy sales (+10% YoY to P8.6bn) mitigated the weaker performance of whisky (-13% YoY to P4.5bn). Brandy gross margins were lower at 23% vs 24% in 1Q24 due to product mix. Whisky gross margins deteriorated to 37% vs. 44% last year given moderate single malts' sales in the product mix. Volume based market share in spirits for EMI dropped to 12.7% in Mar 2025 from 14.9% in Dec 2024. GADC 1Q25 net profit dipped 42% YoY to P157mn. Sales by company-operated QSRs declined 25% QoQ and 10% YoY to P9.3bn. Note that the company did not discuss GADC's operations in detail during the briefing and 1Q25 data only comprises of GADC segment operations till prior to deconsolidation last March 17. AGI deconsolidated GADC due to stricter franchise terms from McDonald’s USA during renewal, which conflicted with AGI’s obligations as a publicly listed firm (i.e., timely disclosure releases). There's no change in AGI’s equity stake (49%)—GADC will be recognized as an associate moving forward and will no longer be consolidated line by line from March 17 onwards.

DITO CME posts net loss of P41bn for FY24

DITO net loss for FY24 (inclusive of minority interests) was P41bn, while net loss for DITO CME (the listed entity) was P14.7bn. The larger figure (P41bn) should be closer to what the net loss of Dito Telecommunity was for FY24. Note that Dito Tel is consolidated into DITO’s financials, and accounts for virtually all of the P&L and Balance Sheet. DITO has been reducing its stake in the intermediate holding companies that own Dito Tel and its economic stake is only 35.6% despite it consolidating Dito Tel’s accounts (due to voting control). Recall a firm called “Summit Telco” is presently seeking to acquire voting control of Dito Tel. We estimate 4Q25 market share of mobile revenue was 7.4% which is just marginally higher than the 7.3% in 3Q. This suggests Dito Tel is not making much headway in the industry. We estimate DITO managed to post a positive EBITDA of ~P400m for the year FY24, which is really just above breakeven. However, given that lease expenses were capitalized (and amortized), one can argue that DITO is really still below EBITDA breakeven. DITO’s consolidated debt was P184bn, roughly US$3.2bn, and leases would add another P24bn (US$420m). This still does not include P32bn (US$560m) of payables to vendors. The company has very little cash left (P1bn). Consolidated capital deficiency (i.e. negative equity) has now ballooned to P73bn (US$1.3bn).

ICTSI's Mexico port volumes jump 14% YoY in April

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), grew volumes by 14% YoY to 122.5k TEUs in April. This is faster than the 8% YoY rate in 1Q25. Meanwhile, combined TEU volumes of ports across Mexico (excluding CMSA) grew by 4% YoY in April, also outpacing 1Q25 growth rate of 2%. Brings CMSA’s 4M25 TEU volumes to 509k TEUs, up 9% YoY and trending below our full-year forecast of 1.69mn TEUs (+16%). Note that our forecasts assume the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) treaty will be cancelled only by July 2026. Every 5% decrease (increase) in CMSA’s FY25E volumes will shave off (increase) EBITDA by 1.6% and profit by 2.4%. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of group EBITDA.

URC P10bn Malvar plant set to be operational before end of year

Universal Robina Corp.'s (URC PM) P10bn production plant in Malvar, Batangas is expected to be operational before the end of the year. Recall that the company broke ground for construction of this plant last Feb 2024. The plant's expansion will span for 10-15 years and once completed, will total to 30.7 hectares with ~3,000 workers directly and indirectly employed. Manufacturing lines to be installed in the plant were guided to streamline operations by reducing material resource inputs, product wastage, labor efforts, energy and water consumption. Lance Gokongwei in an interview stated that the company is focused on "reigniting growth" in the Philippine branded business especially as inflation continues to ease and consumer confidence is coming back. He also elaborated that the number one priority is to bring back growth to the domestic business, highlighting that they are taking some active measures on marketing, distribution, and innovation.

Court orders release of P3.5bn worth of BDO shares from escrow

The Sandiganbayan has ordered the release of 6.3mn (~P3.5bn worth) PCIBank shares to the late Benjamin Romualdez. Note that the bank shares had changed hands due to mergers and are now part of BDO Unibank Inc. (BDO PM). The case stems from Civil Case 0035, filed over 30 years ago, which accused Romualdez of illegally acquiring the shares through dummy buyers. These shares which were held by Romualdez' firm, Trans Middle East (Phils.) Equities, Inc. (TMEPEI), were sequestered by the Presidential Commission on Good Government (PCGG) decades ago. In July 2022, the Supreme Court ruled that the Sandiganbayan (a special court that deals with civil cases over graft and corruption) acted with grave abuse of discretion for not returning the shares after voiding the original sequestration order, but instead putting the shares in the Land Bank of the Philippines in escrow. This Supreme Court decision became final and executory back in October 2023. In light of the decision, the Sandiganbayan can no longer retain legal custody over the previously sequestered assets and has tasked the PCGG and BDO to return the shares to TMEPEI. PCGG and BDO were asked to report their actions on the matter within 15 days of the May 16, 2025 resolution.

BOP widens to US$2.6bn deficit in April; Final GIR settles at US$106.7bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$2.6bn, wider than both Feb 2025's US$2.0bn deficit and the US$639mn deficit in Apr 2024. The deficit reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits drawdowns for external debt obligations as well as its net foreign exchange operations. External debt obligations that matured in April includes a EUR$650mn 5-year bond. This brings the YT-Apr deficit at US$5.5bn, much higher than the US$401mn deficit for the same period last year. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$4bn deficit (-0.8% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Apr stood at US$105.3bn, down from the US$106.7bn in end-Mar. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease slightly from US$106bn in 2024 to US$105bn in 2025.

OFW Remittances up 2.6% DA proposes P10bn budget for continuation of P20/kg rice in 2026oY in March

The Department of Agriculture (DA) is seeking for a P10bn budget in order to sustain the P20/kg rice program next year. The P10bn will be needed to subsidize next year's operation of Food Terminal Inc. (FTI) which currently purchases the aging rice stocks of the National Food Authority (NFA) that are sold in the program. Current inventory of the NFA is equivalent to around 8mn 50/kg bags of milled rice or around 400,000 metric tons. This proposed P10bn budget for 2026 is double the P5bn currently allocated for the pilot run this year (which started early this month). The P5bn was sourced from the Office of the President's contingency fund—with P4.5bn to be used as rice subsidy, and the remaining for logistics. Nationwide rollout of the subsidized rice program is set to continue throughout the year to reach around 14mn Filipinos or 3.3mn households by Sept. Initial target areas will be provinces with high poverty incidence and/or typhoon prone locations (i.e., Zamboanga del Norte, Maguindanao, Sorsogon, Catanduanes). The pilot run of P20/kg rice set till December is only available to vulnerable sectors such as indigents, senior citizens, solo parents, and PWDs who can purchase up to 30/kg a month. For local gov't units (LGUs) that will share the subsidy, the LGU can determine eligibility for the program.

Emerging pest infestation threatens Philippine sugar output

Sugar farms in Negros Occidental, which accounts for about half (~54%) of the country's sugar production, have reported an infestation of red-striped soft scale insects (RSSI). The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) reported that RSSI can potentially reduce sugar content in cane by roughly 50%. Pest infestation may have been brought to Negros (Western Visayas) from Pampanga and/or Batangas (Luzon), where RSSI infestations are still present in some farms. The pest was first observed in Bacolor, Pampanga in June 2022, then Balayan, Batangas in Aug 2023. Notably, two fields in Balayan, Batangas were observed, with one field was moderately damaged, while the other was 20-40% heavily infested. The quantified impact of the previous RSSI infestations on sugar output was not stated, but based on PSA data, contribution of Batangas to total sugar output fell to 3.6% in 2023 from 4.3-4.8% in 2020-2022. SRA Administrator Pablo Luis S. Azcona highlights the need for urgency given that some farmers have already started to plant cane for next crop year. Sugar production for crop year 2024-2025 could total 1.84 million metric tons (MMT), lower than the output of 1.92 MMT in the previous crop year. The PH imported ~200,000 MMT of sugar in 2024. The National Crop Protection Center has previously tested insecticides that counter RSSI infestations which have been deemed effective. However, official use outside of testing will require an emergency-use permit from the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority, as said insecticides have not been registered for sugarcane yet.

March asset quality improvements much milder than headlines suggest

Headline NPL ratios improved 8bps MoM to 3.30%, and headline past due (PD) ratios dropped 6bps MoM. However, this was only due to a jump in RRPs to the tune of nearly P400bn MoM, equivalent to about 2.7% of total loans. We suspect this was due to the 200bps RRR cut from 7% to 5%, which became effective on 28 March 2025. Reported loan growth (including IBL/RRP) was 14.2%, when ex-IBL/RRP the “real” gross loans were up just 12.2% YoY. Banking sector asset quality was flat-ish MoM in March. NPL ratio was 3.55% down 3bps MoM and down 4bps YoY. In nominal terms, NPLs were up 0.5% MoM and 11.1% YoY. Past Due (PD) ratio was flat MoM at 4.45%, but 10bps lower YoY, as the Peso value of PDs grew 1.3% MoM and 9.8% YoY. Restructured Loan (RL) ratio improved to 2.15%, down 2bps MoM, and down 13bps YoY, with nominal RLs up just 0.1% MoM to P311.bn. NPL Cover, meanwhile was 95.1%, slightly lower than the 95.4% in Feb, but more than 5%age points lower than the 100.7% in March 2024. As mentioned, loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) in March was 12.2% YoY, slowing down from the 12.5% pace in February. System-wide LDR was 72.0%. By bank type, the universal/commercial banks (UKBs) and thrift banks (TBs) saw improving PD and NPL trends. However, Digibanks saw NPL ratio jump 12bps MoM to 9.76%, while PD ratio jumped 42bps MoM to 17.33%.

Vehicle sales fall 10% YoY in April; Toyota sales drop 8%

CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales fell 10% YoY, 17% MoM to 33,580 units in April. Cited seasonal factors, economic conditions, and evolving consumer demands. Adjusting for Easter break, combined sales for the months of March and April rose 1% YoY, moderating from the 6% pace in 2M25. Note that CAMPI/TMA’s data does not include sales of BYD. Brings 4M25 industry car sales up 2.5% YoY to 150,654 units. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 16,414 units in April, down 8% YoY and 13% MoM. Brings TMP’s 4M25 industry car sales up 6% YoY to 71,927 units. TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year; To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 20.4k units per month.

Government eyes at least US$200mn from privatization of 797MW CBK hydro plant

The Department of Finance is looking to generate US$200-600mn or higher from the privatization of the 797MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan hydroelectric complex in Laguna. Proceeds will depend on the offtake price the Energy Regulatory Commission will approve, according to Dept. of Finance Undersecretary Catherine L. Fong. The re-bid of the CBK complex is currently ongoing; Bid submission is set for June 16. However, the government has not set a minimum bid price yet. Contact Center industry to grow 5%-7% this year

The Contact Center Association of the Philippines (CCAP) is projecting 5%-7% growth in 2025. In 2024, it generated US$31.5bn in revenue and employed 1.62mn Filipinos. This implies that it comprised 82% of BPO revenue and 90% of employees.

US House Republicans to impose 5% tax on remittance transfers of non-US citizens

House Republicans have included in President Donald Trump’s big priority bill a 5% excise tax on remittance transfers that would cover more than 40mn people. Tax will cover green card holders and nonimmigrant visa holders but will exempt US citizens. Remittances from the US account for 34% of the Philippines’ cash remittances (ex-seafarers). The US is home to over 4mn Filipinos.

Cabinet officials resign after Marcos recalibrates gov't

Over 30 Cabinet members have submitted their courtesy resignations as of late after President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr.'s called for their resignations yesterday. The officials will continue to perform their duties and responsibilities until their resignations are accepted, or new appointments have been made by the President. The President can also choose to reject the courtesy resignation if he believes the official remains qualified and performs their duties effectively. Palace Press Officer Clarissa A. Castro stated that the President was frustrated with the performance of some of his Cabinet members, but no names were specified. She also states that the President ensures that all pending and ongoing projects will not be affected during the transition. A performance review will be conducted for all those who submitted their resignations. There was no indicated timeline but it was noted that it will be done with utmost urgency.

Zeno Abenoja named as new BSP Deputy Governor

Zeno Ronald Abenoja has been appointed deputy governor of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)'s Monetary and Economic Sector (MES). The MES is responsible for monetary policy formulation, implementation, and assessment. BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. named Abenoja to succeed Fransico Dakila Jr., who previously held the post for six years since 2019. Remolona highlighted Abenoja's seasoned experience in economic policy and deep understanding of monetary economics. Abenoja rejoined the central bank last year as assistant governor after serving as chief economist at the Department of Finance (DoF) during Benjamin Diokno’s term as Finance Secretary from 2022 to 2024. Before his assignment at the DoF, he was the managing director of the BSP’s Department of Economic Research.
