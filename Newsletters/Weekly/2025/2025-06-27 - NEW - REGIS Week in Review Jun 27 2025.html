REGIS REPORT

27 June 2025

The Philippines recovered last week’s losses, rising 1.1% this week to end at 6,408. This was prompted by a sharp drop in oil prices (-13% for the week) after the US’ bombing of Iran’s nuclear facilities and a subsequent US-brokered ceasefire between Israel and Iran, which ultimately led to the easing in tensions in the Middle East. At roughly USD 65 per barrel, oil is now back at pre-war levels.

The market’s ascent was likewise driven by more aggressive Fed cut expectations for 2H25, with the market now anticipating 2 to 3 cuts. Ten year US yields compressed back to 4.26%, after rising to as high as 4.51% in early June. The USD likewise weakened (DXY fell 1.5% for the week) - that is generally positive of course for EM assets like the Philippines.

Amidst that macro backdrop, the interest rate sensitive property sector led the market higher this week with heavy weights Ayala Land  (+4.7%) and SM Prime (+2.7%) paving the way. Market proxies / conglos saw interest as well led by Ayala Corp (+2.7%), JG Summit (+1.6%), GT Capital (+1.5%) and SM Investments (+1.2%).

Conversely, banks lagged the market on the general assumption that falling rates could pose a risk to margins later on. BPI (-0.7%), BDO (+0.7%) and Metrobank (flat) failed to keep up with the market at large.

Consumer stocks were generally stronger too as the quick fall in oil prices debunked earlier fears that elevated energy prices would snag the nascent consumer recovery here. Universal Robina (+2.5%) rose, as did MONDE (+1.4%) and Puregold (+1.4%).

In terms of on the ground developments,  two key items have come out of Congress. First, the Konektadong Pinoy Bill (approved by the Senate and Lower House) has been sent to the President’s office for his signature. If signed, the Bill will lower a key barrier to entry in the telco sector by allowing new players in without having to secure a Congressional franchise (which has been a long-standing requirement for decades). Incumbent telcos are naturally lobbying against this. PLDT (+0.5%) and Globe (1.6%) managed to hold up well despite the potential risk that this becomes law.

The second topic coming out of Congress are moves to curtail the growth of e-games (online casinos), that have become readily accessible to the masses on their mobile devices. This industry has grown quite a bit (GGR is expected to reach P200bn this year, roughly 0.7% of GDP), and concerns are mounting  with regard to the social ills that will be brought about now that one third of the population are registered users of e-gaming platforms. One method being discussed is to possibly ban the click-through links (to these e-gaming operators’ sites) found on most e-wallets today. The BSP (the defacto overseer of e-wallets) is likewise drafting guidelines apparently along the same lines. Digiplus (the leading player in e-games, with roughly 50% share of the market)  shed 2.1% on the back of this.

Market turnover was lighter at a daily average of $103m, down -20% vs last week. Net foreign flow remained skewed to sell by $20m, but did notice a trend of inflows into property names like Ayala Land.

Philippine Strategy - E-gaming – (literally) eating our lunch
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Carl Sy, CFA Research Analyst +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Tepid consumer rebound, due to surge in e-casino spend (now 1% of GDP)?
The recovery in consumer demand that we have been predicting has been quite disappointing. We believe that e-gambling has – almost literally – eaten into the consumer pie. E-gaming GGRs have skyrocketed from P8.1bn in 2022 to P136bn in 2024, with annualized 1Q25 closing in on P200bn, or about 0.7% of GDP. For perspective, the 1Q25 e-game GGR was just a bit lower than domestic branded revenues of URC, MONDE and CNPF combined. The accessibility of the apps, and the low price of bets – as little as P0.40, or under $0.01 – mean e-games are played by even lower-income Filipinos. Put another way, the P200bn GGR represents money lost by gamers that could otherwise have gone to buying burgers, snacks, noodles, or canned food. The success of e-game firms such as DigiPlus (PLUS PM) has  attracted traditional casino operators like Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) to launch their own e-game apps. Could this further
dampen consumer spend?

Consumer recovery in 4Q24 / 1Q25 disappointed
We have been anticipating a strong rebound in consumption spending on a) sharply lower inflation thanks to sharply lower rice prices, b) minimum wage hikes in 2H24, and c) election spending. However, the recovery has been quite
muted. GDP growth in 1Q25 came in at 5.4%, well below consensus of 5.7%, with PCE spending also falling short, breaking with historical patterns. Consumer sector 1Q25 results, too, were mixed.

E-gambling has exploded, GGRs now c0.7% of GDP …
We believe the culprit is something we completely did not expect — e-gambling. E-gaming has seen explosive growth since our first report on the sector. From P8.1bn in 2022, the gross gaming revenue (GGR) of e-games — e-casinos mostly, but also bingo and sports betting —skyrocketed to P134bn in 2024. In 1Q25 alone, industry GGR stood at P47bn, growing another 110% YoY. To help put that into better perspective, consider the following:

By our estimate, as of 2024, the Philippines is among the highest gaming and e-gaming GGR as % of GDP in the world (Fig 1) beating out Australia. If we annualize 1Q25, the P200bn e-gaming GGR will rise to about 0.7% of nominal GDP for 2025E.

• In 1Q25, e-gaming GGRs (including bingo) of P51.4bn exceeded the P49.3bn total GGR of licensed physical casinos , some of whom have invested over $1bn in their integrated resorts. 

• E-gaming GGRs in 1Q25 (including casinos) were just a shade below the combined domestic branded revenues of consumer giants Universal Robina (URC PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), and Century Pacific (CNPF PM), which we estimated at P52.7bn in 1Q25. 

• DigiPlus (PLUS PM) alone booked GGR of P74bn in 2024, larger than BLOOM’s P53bn for its Solaire Entertainment City, P35bn for Okada Manila, and P32bn for RW Manila. PLUS’s 1Q25 GGRwas larger than each of the domestic branded revenues of URC, of MONDE and CNPF.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Pricing in higher coal production
 
 
by Klyne Resullar Research Analyst +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA Research Analyst +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Surprise production increase but maintaining Hold
We raise our FY25E/FY26E profit forecasts by 7%/21% after assuming higher coal mining sales volumes, higher power plant output, and an
extension of the coal unit’s tax holiday. We nonetheless maintain our Hold rating at a TP of P22/sh, as uncertainty over its request to extend
the term of its Coal Operating Contract (COC) remains.

Higher sales volumes beginning this year
The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) has amended SCC’s Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC), allowing the company to expand its annual coal mining production limit by 25% to 20mn MT. Our revised estimates assume SCC’s coal mining unit will sell 19mn MT this year and 20mn MT in 2026, higher than last year’s actual sales volume of 16.5mn MT. Despite lower ASPs, our revised estimates mean EPS will be mildly lower (-2%) this year.

Coal Operating Contract (COC) term adjustment still in limbo
The Marcos administration’s recent cabinet reset has left SCC’s main regulator, the Department of Energy (DOE), without a permanent Secretary. 

While an Officer-in-Charge (OIC) has been appointed for the DOE, her powers may be limited to the day-to-day operations of the Department. There is thus even greater uncertainty now over SCC’s request for a term adjustment of its COC, which is due to expire by July 2027.


Binary outcome
After incorporating higher sales volumes and rolling over our DCF to 2026, we peg our TP at P22/sh, which assumes the worst-case scenario that its COC will end by July 2027. Conversely, securing a 15-year term adjustment could raise our estimate value to P41/sh.

Attractive dividend yield
Despite a higher FY25E EPS, we retain our FY25E DPS at P4.50/sh, as the production hike will likely entail higher capex. At today’s price, our dividend forecast translates to an attractive yield of 13%. Risks relate to commodity prices, plant availability, and regulatory risks.

GT Capital continues to search for acquisition opportunities

GT Capital CFO George Uy-Tioco, Jr. said that that the company has declined some potential opportunities as these did not meet the company’s criteria in terms of sector, returns, and technical partnerships. The company continues to look for opportunities in new sectors such as healthcare, renewable energy, education, and data centers. GT Capital has earmarked P9-14bn for investments/acquisitions, as well as property-related capex in 2025. 

Its parent level net debt (incl. prefs) stood at P59bn in end-March 2025, down from a peak of P88bn in 2018. Net debt-to-equity at 40%.

Maynilad pushes back IPO to October

Maynilad has updated its proposed IPO timetable in response to robust potential demand from strategic cornerstone investors. Investors conveyed strong and sustained interest in participating in the offering, but requested additional time to complete their internal approval processes. Maynilad’s target listing on the Main Board of the PSE will be no later than end of October 2025 (vs. July 17 previously). 

Megaworld to enter “ultra high-end” residential market

Megaworld will introduce the Megaworld SIGNATURE COLLECTION later this year. It will target ultra-high net worth individuals and only one to two such projects will be launched annually. Units will be priced above P100mn. For reference, the average price of an Ayala Land Premier unit launched in 2024 was c. P35mn.

ACEN signs deal to acquire a 25% stake in a 1GW offshore wind project

ACEN has signed a deed that will allow the company to acquire a 25% stake in CI GMF II Camarines Offshore Wind Energy Corp. for P2.95mn. The shares were acquired from Danish firm CI NMF Philippines CV.

CI GMF II is developing an offshore wind project that could potentially have an installed capacity of up to 1GW. The project will be in San Miguel Bay in Camarines Sur and was previously estimated to cost US$3bn. The project is currently in its pre-development stage in anticipation of the Department of Energy’s 5th round of the Green Energy Auction (GEA-5) and will be subject to relevant regulatory approvals

Leviste unloading SPNEC shares, majority of which will be acquired by Meralco

Solar Philippines stated that its founder, Congressman-elect Leandro Leviste, has agreed to sell another 5.01bn (equivalent to 10%) SPNEC common shares to Meralco for P6.26bn. This is on top of previous agreements with Meralco for the sale of 5.82bn shares (12%) for P7.5bn. Both transactions suggest a price of P1.27/sh, slightly higher than SPNEC’s closing price of P1.21/sh. Meralco has fully paid P13.76bn, while the transfer of the 10.8bn shares will come after.

As of end-March 2025, Meralco owned 17.87bn common shares (~35.7% economic) and 37.3bn voting shares (54% voting interest) of SPNEC. These transactions would raise MER’s stake to 57.3% of SPNEC’s commons and 69% of voting shares. The last share transfer of SPNEC shares done by Meralco was in Jan 2024.

Mr. Leviste will be left with 8.16bn common shares (16%) of SPNEC. Leviste is currently considering options for other private placements and financing on the remaining SPNEC shares.

Suntrust integrated resort opening delayed to 3Q26

The opening of Suntrust’s integrated resort will be in 3Q26. It was previously scheduled for completion in 4Q25.

The integrated resort will have 280 gaming tables and 1,260 gaming machines. These will add about 20% to table capacity and 15% to gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City. Suntrust will use the gaming license of Travellers under a revenue sharing agreement.

Government posts P145bn budget deficit in May

The government recorded a budget deficit of P145.2bn in May, which is narrower by 17% YoY and a reversal from the P67bn surplus in April. The lower deficit was due to higher growth (+13% YoY) in revenue collections and slower expenditure growth (+4% YoY) given the election ban.

Revenue collections in May increased 13% YoY to P433.1bn, driven by higher tax receipts from the BIR. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 11% YoY to P242.7bn mainly due to higher corporate income tax collections as well as increased personal income tax and VAT collections on the back of the enhancement of digital services.Customs collections fell 7% to P75.7bn given the impact of reduced tariffs (i.e., rice and EVs) and lower import volumes given global trade uncertainties. Non-tax revenues surged 41% to P110.2bn due to higher dividend remittances from Government Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs).

Expenditures grew a modest 4% YoY in May to P578.2bn driven by tepid growth in primary spending. Primary spending for the month inched up 2% YoY to P508.3bn.

The slower primary spending growth can likely be attributed to the election ban on public spending which started late March till election day on May 12. Interest payments were up 14% to P70.0bn from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt.

This brings the 5M25 deficit to P523.9bn, wider by 29% YoY. The 5M25 deficit appears on trend to exceed the full-year deficit target of P1.54tn due lower-than-expected tax revenue collections.

Government tempers GDP growth targets; raises fiscal deficit targets on lower projected revenues

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) trimmed its economic growth target for 2025 to a range of 5.5-6.5% narrower than the previous 6-8% goal.  The DBCC cited lower-than-expected 1Q25 GDP print of 5.4%, dampened global sentiment due to Trump's tariffs, and heightened risk from the Israel-Iran conflict. To meet the lower end of the GDP growth target range (5.5%), GDP growth needs to average ~5.5% for the next three quarters. To hit the mid-point (6.0%) and high end (6.5%) of the range, GDP growth needs to average ~6.2% and ~6.8% for the rest of the year, respectively.

The inflation target band was also trimmed to 2-3% from 2-4%. This was hinged on the assumption that inflation will continue to be manageable and benign over the near term. Inflation has averaged 1.9% so far for the first five months of the year. It also raised its fiscal deficit-to-GDP targets due to lower revenue collection assumptions.

Government proposes budget of P6.793tn for 2026

The government is proposing a national budget of P6.793tn for 2026, equivalent to 22% of GDP and a 7.4% increase from the 2025 GAA of P6.326tn. Details of the budget will be revealed once the Department of Budget and Management submits the proposal to the 20th Congress after July 28. The national budget prioritizes human capital development by prioritizing investments in quality education, healthcare, and workforce upskilling.

DOTr to privatize LRT-2 via PPP

President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. has approved the public private partnership mode of privatization for LRT-2. The bidding is set to start before year-end with the International Finance Corp. as the project advisor. Line expansions in the pipeline, rising ridership, as well as hours-long unplanned shutdowns underscore the need for PPP to improve reliability and overall quality of operations.  Despite private operation, train fares will remain regulated by the Dept. of Transportation (DOTr), mitigating concerns over sudden fare hikes.

PCTO warns against the Konektadong Pinoy Act

The Philippine Chamber of Telecommunication Operations (PCTO) called on President Marcos to think twice before signing the Konektadong Pinoy Act into law. The law allegedly favors the technology of satellite providers, which are mostly foreign companies, given the cost of deploying and managing space-based assets.

The country’s biggest telcos, who’ve largely invested in terrestrial assets, warned that the Philippines would be the only country that permits satellite providers to come in without regulation. New foreign operators, under the bill, will not be required to build infrastructure. They will also not be required to service or subsidize underserved rural areas, nor support government in times of national emergency.

The Konektadong Pinoy bill eliminates barriers to entry in the telecommunications sector, removing the need for legislative franchises for connectivity services in the hopes of attracting more foreign investment in digital infrastructure.

Multiple groups call for signing of Konektadong Pinoy bill

Thirty-five organizations called for President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. to sign the "Konektadong Pinoy" bill despite aggressive lobbying for it to be vetoed. The multiple groups in favor of the bill include various ICT industry groups, tech organizations, foreign chambers of commerce, among others.

The Open Access in Data Transmission measure or Konektadong Pinoy bill will remove the Congressional franchise requirement for data providers. The multiple organizations in a statement cited that the issues raised against the bill are "unfounded". Endangerment of national security was said to not be an issue given that the bill asks network providers to comply with internationally recognized standards on cybersecurity. The bill mandates a cybersecurity performance audit as a requirement for continuing operation and license renewal. It also disallows foreign gov't-controlled and state-owned enterprises from operating data transmission networks.

The thirty-five groups emphasized that the bill would slash internet costs and improve accessibility to internet connectivity. 19,000 barangays (45.5% of all barangays nationwide) were cited to still lack internet access

Dept. of Agriculture drafting bill to restore NFA powers

The Department of Agriculture (DA) is drafting a bill to restore some of the powers of the National Food Authority (NFA) such as selling rice in the commercial market and the implementation of a floor price on palay (unmilled rice). DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that the proposed bill will also amend other key provisions of the Rice Tariffication Law (RTL)—i.e., removing the restriction on the NFA's ability to import rice for better response to supply shortages and price volatility. Other proposed key measures would be restoring the NFA's authority to sell non-aging stocks of rice commercially and doubling their palay procurement budget to P18bn in 2026. Currently, the NFA can only sell two months and one-day old rice stocks (one month before the aging process begins).

House Speaker Martin Romualdez sought the help of the DA to draft a bill for next Congress to help the gov't's food security agenda, specifically President Ferdinand Marcos' Jr.'s P20/kg rice program for vulnerable sectors through 2028. The prices for local and imported well-milled rice as of June 19 both range from P38-48/kg, compared to P40-55/kg [local] and P44-50/kg [imported] back in Jan 19.

DTI aiming to roll out electric vehicle incentive program soon

The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) has completed the EV incentive framework called “Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy” (EVIS). The Fiscal Incentives Review Board will deliberate and finalize EVIS next month. Once the review is over, the DTI is expected to start the rollout of fiscal incentives.

The DTI aims to implement tax breaks, duty exemptions & investment support under EVIS:

  • 0% excise tax on fully built-up EVs imported as CBUs (Completely Built Unit) through 2028 
  • 15% excise tax on hybrids versus 50% under the TRAIN law
  • Duty-free importation of CKD EV units and EV parts upon BOI approval and Zero-tariff rates on batteries, motors, and charging-station equipment
  • Up to seven-year Income Tax Holiday for new EV manufacturing/assembly Post-ITH option to pay a 5% special corporate income tax on gross income in lieu of all national and local taxes
  • Zero-rating of VAT on EVs, parts, and batteries sold to end-users with and exemption from motor vehicle user’s charge and other local levies during the incentive period
  • One-stop-shop BOI processing for permits, environmental clearances, and import licenses 
  • Priority LTO registration and issuance of special EV license plates 

The DTI expects EVIS to create 680,000 jobs and spur P120bn in investments. Program is expected to produce P11.4 T in output and P400bn in additional tax revenues. The DTI aims to produce 9mn EVs from 2028-2040 under this program and mandates the installation of 400k charging stations.

Lowering of imported rice MSRP delayed

The Department of Agriculture (DA) is postponing the plan to lower the maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) of imported rice on July 1 by one or two months. This is due to heightened geopolitical risk given the conflicts in the Middle East which drove the recent surge in oil prices. DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that the delayed rollout of lower MSRP will enable them to get a clearer picture on the movement of global rice prices.

The initial plan was to lower the MSRP of imported rice from the current P45/kg to P43/kg starting July 1. The MSRP was initially P58/kg at end-Jan, which was lowered to P49/kg by end-Feb, and P45/kg by end-March. The prices for local and imported well-milled rice as of June 21 range from P38-52/kg and P38-48/kg, respectively, compared to P40-55/kg and P40-52/kg on Jan 21.

Some members of Congress want online gambling to be inaccessible on e-wallets

Some members of the House of Representatives are drafting a bill that seeks to remove gambling apps from e-wallet platforms. Representative Jonathan Keith Flores said that e-wallets have become “…channels of addiction”. Meanwhile, Representative Rolando Valeriano believes that the government should prohibit all online gambling.

Wage hike deliberations by NCR board to start this week

The National Capital Region (NCR) wage board is set to meet this week to deliberate on possible wage adjustments. This follows after the 19th Congress adjourned earlier this month without passing the proposed P100-200 across-the-board wage hike bill. With no law passed before the end of the legislative cycle, wage adjustments will primarily be decided by the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Boards (RTWPBs).  

Labor Secretary Bienvenido E. Laguesma stated that the last wage board order increase of P35 (from P610 to P645) took effect on July 17 last year, so the NCR board is within schedule to issue a new wage order. Laguesma stated that a new wage order for NCR workers can be expected—if any—by next month.  

The current minimum wage in NCR stands at P645, while the average minimum wage nationwide is P474
