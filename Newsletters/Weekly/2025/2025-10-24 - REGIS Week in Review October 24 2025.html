REGIS REPORT

24 October 2025

The PCOMP finally retreated below 6,000 to end a listless week of trading, closing at 5,988.02, down 1.7% or 101.51 pts WoW.

 

Light on cues from local news and macro releases, investors seemed to consider mixed indications on the upcoming 3Q earnings releases. BPI (-8.0% WoW) was the biggest drag on the index this week, with shares falling despite 3Q results released last week coming slightly ahead of consensus forecasts. BDO (-2.7% WoW) also pulled back ahead of its earnings disclosure early next week. 

 

ALI (-5.8% WoW) was a loss leader once again amid heavy foreign outflow and unrelenting bearish views on the real estate sector. At the Jefferies & Regis Partners Philippines Conference the previous week, management had guided for lower residential launches of P60bn (from P80bn) and cut its profit growth target to 6-8% (from 10-12%). 

 

Meanwhile, PLUS (-12.8% WoW) dropped the most among PCOMP names following continued negative press on online gaming. Other than the de-linking of online gaming platforms from e-wallets, PAGCOR said that its recent implementation of an initial minimum deposit of P500 for new users also largely contributed to the slump in the regulator's online gambling revenues.

 

Miners also pulled back following the correction in gold prices, which brought the Mining sector index down 11.7% WoW. While metal prices remain elevated, the continued push for sectoral reform in the local mining industry is also adding to potential tailwinds. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said it is drafting an executive order (EO) that will extend mineral processing permits to a fixed 25-year period (from 5 years renewable every 5 years up to 25 years, at present). The DENR also reportedly plans to launch the bidding of some 50 idle mining assets by November.

 

On the flipside, investors seemed to prefer defensives with MER (+4.0% WoW) bucking the market weakness along with telcos CNVRG (+5.1% WoW) and TEL (+1.2% WoW).

 

Elsewhere, the IPO of Maynilad Water (ticker: MYNLD) was priced at P15/sh early in the week. At a market cap of P113bn ($1.9bn), this implies forward valuations of 1.1x EV/OCP, 6.4x EV/EBITDA, and 8x PER, with dividend yield of just under 6%. In comparison, shares of East Zone water concessionaire MWC (-4.7% WoW) were trading at 5x EV/OCP, 7.6x EV/EBITDA, and 9x PER at the time of the announcement, before the stock declined later in the week. MYNLD makes its PSE debut on November 7th. 

 

Net foreign flows turned just barely positive following three straight weeks of outflows. The Philippine peso depreciated anew, weaker by 0.8% WoW to 58.632:$1.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Maynilad IPO price set at P15/sh

Maynilad has set its IPO price at P15/sh, which would put its market capitalization at P113bn assuming full subscription of offer shares. The price is lower than the initial ceiling of P20/sh. Media sources say the IPO is nearly two times oversubscribed. The retail sale period will be from Oct 23-29, with the listing date on Nov 7.

Semirara declares P1.25/sh special dividend

Semirara declared a special dividend of P1.25/sh, payable to shareholders of record as of November 4 (ex-date on Nov 3). The dividend fell short of consensus’ median estimate of P1.35/sh and Regis’ estimate of P2/sh. This dividend tranche is equivalent to a yield of 4%. Payment date is on Nov 20. Together with the P2/sh dividend declared early this year, SCC has paid a total of P3.25/sh this year, equivalent to a yield of ~10%. Cumulative 2025 dividend of P3.25/sh is equivalent to a payout ratio of 70%, down from last year’s payout ratio of 91% and the lowest since 2020. Note that the company guided for capex of P6.9bn this year, up from P5.3bn in 2024 for re-fleeting and equipment acquisitions. In the event of a non-extension of its coal operating contract, Semirara will retain ownership of all its mining equipment, port facilities, and other infrastructure in the island.

Converge open to asset co-sharing with Konektadong Pinoy entrants

Converge ICT Solutions Inc. (CNVRG PM) expects to increase its fiber utilization by sharing the usage of its fiber infrastructure network with the new data transmission industry participants (DTIPs) after the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) takes effect. Converge CEO Dennis Anthony Uy stated that the initiative can generate additional cash for the company. The law requires existing providers to co-share their infrastructure network with DTIPs, and this provision can boost utilization of Converge's underutilized fiber network. As of June, Converge manages 9.05mn ports across its fiber network nationwide but is only currently serving 2.82mn customers. The KPA lapsed into law back in August and its IRR is pending finalization.

Converge eyes to build 100 MW data center in Visayas or Mindanao

Converge ICT Solutions Inc. (CNVRG PM) CEO and Co-founder Dennis Anthony Uy states that they seek to build a 100 MW data center either in Visayas or Mindanao for better internet reliability and speeds for subscribers in the VisMin region. The facility would generate an initial capacity of 20 MW but can be raised to as high as 100 MW, depending on demand. This would be its largest data center aside from the data centers in Pampanga (10 MW) and Caloocan City (3 MW). The plan to build a data center follows the recent linkage of the Bifrost Cable System to Davao which enables faster data transmission between Singapore and the US as well as for other countries in close proximity.

Martirez resigns as Smart COO

Anastacio Martirez has stepped down as Chief Operating Officer of Smart Communications following employee complaints over the working environment and culture. Martirez rejoined the company as COO in Sep 2024, returning after two decades since leaving the company back in 2005. An internal investigation was launched after he was suspended last month as complaints of a "toxic" workplace arose internally and in social media. Lloyd Manaloto and Majorie Garrovillo were appointed as OICs of Smart's wireless business since Sep 8. Manaloto is the first VP for strategy and corporate brand while Garrovillo is the first VP for marketing as well as the sales and development head. The OICs were said to have been tasked to continue Martirez's initiatives such as the revival of Smart Money and the Gen Z-focused mobile brand KiQ.

DMCI to discuss leadership succession

DMCI Chairman, President, and CEO Isidro Consunji hinted at plans to step back from active management in DMCI and focus on the family’s agriculture ventures. Mr. Consunji, 77, has served as the head of the corporation since 1995. DMCI’s Board of Directors is expected to deliberate on its succession planning toward the end of next year.

Megaworld to subscribe to MREIT shares at P14.39/share

MREIT seeks SEC approval to increase its authorized capital stock from P5bn to P8bn (at P1/share). Its sponsor, Megaworld, will subscribe to 25% of this increase (750mn shares) at P14.39/share – P10.8bn. This is higher than the 30-day VWAP and 3% higher than the last closing price of P14.00/share. The share issuance will increase MREIT’s share outstanding by 20%. We estimate that the AGI Group’s ownership in MREIT will increase from 58% to 65%. This will likely be used for a share-for-property swap with its sponsor, Megaworld. MREIT is targeting to grow its leasable area to 600k sqm by end-2025 and to 1mn sqm by 2027. It currently has a leasable portfolio of 480k sqm.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Government posts P248bn budget deficit in Sep; P1.17trn deficit as of 9M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P248bn in Sep, 9% narrower YoY on slower government spending. This brings the 9M25 deficit to P1.17trn, 15% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "expected to remain within the P1.56trn full-year program." The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues. Revenue collections in Sep declined by 6% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 5% mainly due to higher revenues from corporate and personal income tax. Customs collections were higher by 5%, reversing from the 1% decline in Aug. Non-tax revenues dipped 66% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024. Expenditures fell 7% YoY in Sep due to the decrease in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 10%. Similar to Jul and Aug, the spending slowdown in Sep was due to the "lower spending performance of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH)." Interest payments rose 11% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The government eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

BOP posts US$82mn surplus in Sep; GIR settles at US$109.1bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$82mn, narrower than both the US$3.5bn surplus in Sep 2024 and Aug 2025's US$359mn surplus. The smaller surplus in Sep reflected lower forex inflows and a still-wide trade gap. This brings the YT-Sep deficit at US$5.3bn, reversing from the US$5.1bn surplus in the same period last year. The reversal to a deficit stems from the timing of government debt issuances and the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Sep stood at US$109.1bn, up from the $107.1bn in end-Aug. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106.3bn in 2024 to $105.0bn in 2025.

Government infrastructure spending extends decline in August

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) fell 16% YoY, 8% MoM to P105bn in August, citing suspension of payments for public works amid ongoing validation and audit related to corruption concerns. This brings YT-August infra spend to P940bn, down 5% YoY and lagging the government’s infra spend target of P1.51tn (-2% YoY).

PAGCOR requires initial minimum deposit of P500 for online gambling

During a congressional hearing, PAGCOR AVP Jessa Fernandez said the agency's revenues have dropped by as much as 49 percent in September. Ms. Fernandez said that the drop might be due to the prohibition on e-wallets to redirect to online gaming platforms as well as the implementation of an initial minimum deposit of P500. The initial minimum deposit of P500 will likely deter low-income Filipinos from starting to gamble online. During the hearing, the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) also said that it supports either tighter regulation or a complete prohibition of online gambling in the Philippines. Socioeconomic Planning Director Desiree Joy O. Narvaez said “Given its minimal contribution to the economy and vis-à-vis the significant social cost, we do support either its complete prohibition or stricter regulation.” The BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms (effective August 17). There have been several other proposals to restrict or ban online gambling. DigiPlus Interactive is the market leader in online gambling, with c. 40% market share as of 2Q25.

DICT eyes Konektadong Pinoy IRR to be published soon

The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) is set to finalize the IRR for the Konektadong Pinoy Act as DICT Secretary Henry Aguda stated that it is "ready for signing" with a target to publish it no later than Monday, Oct 20. Recall that the DICT previously set Oct 2 as the target date to finalize the IRR. After the IRR is signed, it will be transmitted to the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC), the Department of Education (DepEd), and other relevant agencies. Aguda highlighted that the new reforms will encourage more players to enter the data transmission space, which may drive down prices for better affordability.

Big-ticket gold/copper mining projects to commence 2026-2028

Philex Mining said that its Silangan project is on course to produce copper cathode and gold doré by 1Q26, with or without new investors. According to Philex Mining president and CEO Eulalio Austin Jr., it is targeting to produce 2k MT per day initially, but volume will be gradually ramped up over time to the mine’s full capacity of 12k MT/day by the 12th year. He said that Philex was in discussions with several local and foreign firms to invest in the project, although no agreement has been reached. Meanwhile, Sagitarius Mines is seeking a strategic partner to advance the Tampakan copper-gold project in 2028. The production target was pushed back from the original target of late-2026. The Chamber of Mines of the Philippines (COMP) expects the local mining industry to become a major driver of the national economy once again. COMP Chairman Michael Toledo cited the lifting of the moratorium on new projects, the removal of the open-pit ban, and the ongoing efforts to simplify the permitting process. He is confident that mining can once again reach the heights it once reached during the 1980s, when the industry accounted for over 20% of the country’s export earnings (from only <10% last year).

Government drafting rules to extend mining processing permits to a fixed 25-year period

Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) Undersecretary Carlos Primo David said that they are drafting an executive order (EO) that will extend mineral processing permits to a fixed 25-year period. Currently, processing permits carry an initial validity of five (5) years and are renewable every five years, up to a maximum period of 25 years. The draft EO is meant to protect mining operations from abrupt policy changes including retroactive tax adjustments and new local ordinances that could restrict or halt activities. The EO would also tighten enforcement of the “use it or lose it” policy for mining tenements, allowing the government to take back idle mineral assets and open them for redevelopment. The draft EO is still awaiting approval from the Executive Secretary and must also undergo stakeholder consultation before issuance, which the DENR is aiming to complete next year. The DENR plans to launch the bidding of about 50 idle mining assets by November.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 3.8% YoY in September; Toyota sales flat

CAMPI reported car sales fell 3.8% YoY (+5.1% MoM) to 38,029 units in September. Note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales. This brings YT-September vehicle sales to 343,410 units (-0.3% YoY). Assuming BYD’s market share was at ~5%, we estimate industry car sales may have risen by ~5% YTD. EV sales (ex-BYD) reached 20,662 units in 9M25, accounting for 6% of total sales. This implies that for the month of September alone, sales of EVs stood at 2,223 units, down 1% MoM and accounting for 5.8% of overall sales. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 164,797 units in 9M25, up 3.6% YoY and lagging TMP’s and REGIS’ full-year sales targets. Derived TMP sales for September of 18,440 units indicate a flat YoY trend, breaking the downward trend seen in the prior two months. To reach TMP’s sales target of 235k units (+8%), sales should average 23.4k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach REGIS’ sales forecast of 231k units (+6%), sales should average 22.2k units per month for the rest of the year. We’ve baked in narrower margins for TMP, projecting GP margins/net margins of 10.8%/2.5% in 2H25 from 17.1%/9.2% in 1H25 to reflect expectations of a weaker PHP (vs. USD) and higher promotional activities. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV (24% of gross asset value) and 35% of profit.

ERC announces plan to reset power rates for private distribution utilities

The Energy Regulatory Commission’s (ERC) Rationalized Rules for Setting Distribution Wheeling Rates (RRDWR) aims to streamline the rate setting process of private distribution utilities. RRDWR addresses past delays in resetting the distribution rates and the ERC shall render only one final decision covering the reset and rate translation. This group includes the Manila Electric Company (MER PM), Dagupan Electric Corporation (DECORP), Cagayan Electric Power and Light Company (CEPALCO), and Cotabato Light and Power Company (CLPC). The 1st RP (previously known as 5RP) for this group covers the period from July 1, 2026, to June 30, 2030. Meralco withdrew its 5RP application last August 5 and will re-apply in November. MER sought a tariff of P1.6884/kwh, higher than the current tariff of P1.35/kwh, underpinned by a P246bn 4-year capex proposal and 14.6% WACC. The capex program involves the installation of a 1,500-circuit-km underground cable system within its franchise area. At present, about 97% of Meralco’s cable network is still composed of overhead lines.

ERC approves 74% increase in FIT-All tariff to P0.2073/kwh

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has increased the Feed-in Tariff Allowance (FIT-All) effective November 2025, to PHP0.2073 per kilowatt-hour (kWh). The current rate under the Feed-in Tariff program is PHP0.1189/kWh. This new rate covers outstanding payments and maintains a small buffer fund to prevent delays in future payments to RE producers. It considers the required funds to settle a PHP19.06bn FIT differential and build a PHP3.74bn working capital allowance, which serves as a buffer to guarantee timely payments to the RE producers.

Digital payments +47% in Sept to new high; InstaPay surges 63%

Digital payments continued to surge in September, up 47.3% to P2,170bn, a new monthly record-high, up 5.5% MoM. (Note though that the July/Aug data was revised down slightly, though the Sept figure would still be higher than the previously reported August data.) For the 9M25 period, the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) posted total transactions worth P17,458bn, up 41% YoY and already surpassing the P17,423bn recorded for all of 2024.

PH to resume eVisa program for Chinese tourists

The government expects a rebound in tourist arrivals with the resumption of the eVisa program for Chinese tourists in November. The Department of Tourism (DOT) is also working with Chinese industry partners to increase the frequency and number of charter flights into the country. Tourist arrivals to the Philippines as of 9M25 are at 4.3mn, 2% lower YoY—dragged by a slowdown in Korean (-20% YoY) and Chinese (-22% YoY) tourist arrivals. Pre-pandemic, China was the second biggest source of tourists to the country with 1.7mn visitors in 2019.

DTI approves P14 per 40-kg bag safeguard duty on imported cement

The Department of Trade and Industry imposed a safeguard duty amounting to P14 per 40-kilogram bag or P349 per metric ton of imported cement for a period of three years. We estimate the tariff is equivalent to ~11% (~US$6 per MT) of the landed cost of imported bagged cement of US$54/MT. Prior to this ruling, the temporary safeguard duty was set at P400/MT. This should provide some relief to local manufacturers, as imports have grown to represent more than 30% of market share. Concreat Holdings Phils (CHP PM) is 51%-owned by DMCI (DMC PM) and 10%-owned by Semirara (SCC PM). Aboitiz Equity (AEV PM) owns 45% of CRH Aboitiz (a.k.a. Republic Cement).

Marcos' approval rating dips 9ppt to 33% in Sep

In the latest Pulse Asia Research Survey, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s trust rating deteriorated 9ppt to 33% from 42% in Jun. The drop in approval rating follows Marcos' order to investigate alleged corruption in flood control projects during his 2025 State of the Nation Address (SONA) on July 28 as well as the nationwide protests against flood control corruption that took place on Sep 21. Notably, this is still much lower than the 82% approval rating at the start of his term and a reversal from the 10ppt gain to 42% in Jun from 32% in May. Marcos' approval rating in Luzon stands at 50% in Jun unchanged from May, Visayas at 23% falling sharply from 41%, while his approval rating in Mindanao nosedived to 4% from 25%. Vice President Sara Duterte's approval rating saw a 4ppt decrease to 55% in Sep from 59% in Jun. VP Duterte's approval rating in Luzon was at 36% from 41%, Visayas at 56% from 58%, Mindanao at 97% from 96%.

PSE to change index methodology in 2026

PSE President and CEO Ramon Monzon said that the Exchange plans to revise its index policy. The effectivity will likely be in June 2026 [for the August rebalancing]. The PSE received comments on some of its proposed revisions. Among the proposed changes is a lower public float requirement (of 15%) for companies with market capitalizations above P120bn. The minimum public float requirement for PSE Index constituents is currently 20%. Mr. Monzon wants the PSE’s index policy to be “…benchmarked against the best: MSCI, FTSE, S&P Global…”

Power firms given more time to comply with public offering rules

The ERC has revised the rules requiring power generation firms and distribution utilities to offer 15% of their common shares to the public. They were previously required to offer shares to the public five years after the ERC authorized the company to begin operations. Now, the five-year window begins after the firm has met the prerequisites for a share offering. The PSE has certain prerequisites before listing on the SME Board: at least three years of operations prior to listing, and cumulative EBITDA, excluding non-recurring items, of at least Php15 million for the three (3) fiscal years immediately preceding the application; OR cumulative operating revenues of at least Php150 million for the last three (3) fiscal years immediately preceding the filing of the listing application. Only 40 of 264 generation companies have complied with the POR. Non-compliant firms represent nearly 14,000 megawatts of generation capacity.
