REGIS REPORT

07 November 2025

The Philippine equity market struggled for most of the week, weighed down by hugely disappointing 3Q GDP results and mixed corporate earnings. The PCOMP retreated below the key 5,800 level, ending the week lower by 2.9% or 170.31 pts WoW to a fresh 52W low of 5,759.37.

 

Philippine 3Q GDP came in at 4.0%, well below the expected 5.2%, reinforcing concerns over slowing economic growth in the medium-term and contributing to cautious trading throughout the week .This was the lowest quarterly growth rate since 3Q11's +3.0%, and obviously was a big miss vs the government’s 5.5-6.5% target range. The improvement in net exports (+1.0% from -0.1% prev.) was offset by deteriorating metrics across private consumption (+4.1% from 5.3%), gov't spending (+5.8% from +8.7%), and capital formation (-2.8% from +1.2%). Notably, if we exclude the pandemic YoY declines from 2020-2021, private consumption growth at +4.1% was the lowest since 3Q10's +2.6%. Private investments were negative for the first time at -2.8% since 3Q19's -0.7%.

 

Consumer names were among the biggest losers as the weakening domestic consumption outlook as well as lackluster 3Q earnings pressured stocks. MONDE (-13.2% WoW) was the biggest loser in the index after 3Q results missed expectations as noodles continued to drag, while JFC (-12.3% WoW) also sold off on poor sentiment over the sector. However, CNPF (+7.0% WoW) outperformed following solid 3Q results that slightly beat estimates, while management also upped its earnings guidance.

 

Property (-4.1% WoW) was also a big underperformer dragged by SMPH (-7.6% WoW) as well as ALI (-1.0% WoW) ahead of their earnings releases early next week. Financials (-1.9% WoW) was a broadly negative space too despite 3Q results largely meeting forecasts, reflecting fragile sentiment over broad local macro.

 

What kept the index from a larger fall this week was the outperformance in heavyweight ICT (+1.9% WoW) -- already at over 16% weight in the PCOMP -- which again report strong earnings with volumes accelerating and margins improving, while the medium-term outlook continues to improve on big-ticket port acquisitions and auctions underway.

 

The market saw a busy Friday outside of the GDP release. The PCOMP quarterly rebalancing resulted in added volatility, with PLUS (+20.8% WoW) ending as the week's biggest index gainer following an unexpected upweight -- the resulting inflows from which helped offset disappointing 3Q earnings -- while AREIT (-3.7% WoW) faced outflows. Elsewhere, MYNLD (-0.1% WoW) inched lower on its market debut also last Friday.

 

Philippine equities snapped five straight weeks of net foreign selling, posting net inflows of $77m. Still, the dimming macro outlook saw the peso depreciate by 0.3% WoW to 59.040:$1.

BPI -- Consumer/MSME growth paying off - 9M25 PPOP +16%, credit costs stabilizing
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

9M25 NPAT P50.5bn, in line, as consumer/MSME loans drive PPOP +16%

BPI booked NPAT of P50.5bn for 9M25, up 5% YoY, in line with Regis’s FY25E estimates, and slightly ahead of the Street. Loans grew 13% YoY, with “noninstitutional” loans jumping 27% YoY. The improved loan mix, pushed up NIMs, driving PPOP up 16% YoY. Meanwhile, credit costs have stabilized at c75bps as NPL ratio and NPL cover have levelled off. Despite solid results, the stock is near two-year lows, and now trades at around 1.0x P/bk, with a 4.8% dividend yield on our 2026E forecasts. Maintain Buy and TP of P160/sh.

 

“Non-institutional” loans +27%, now >30% of total, driving yields, NIMs up

Loan growth eased slightly to 13.3% YoY from 14.4% in 2Q (Fig 1). Consumer and MSME loans continued to surge 27.2% YoY, and now account for 30.4% of the portfolio. The higher loan yields helped offset rising funding costs as the bank saw faster growth in its TD and wholesale funding (Fig 2). Higher NIMs drove NII up 16% YoY in 9M and 3Q. Fee incomes were disappointing though, up just 6% YoY in 9M, due largely to price competition in payments and remittances. That said, PPOP was still up 16% YoY in 9M25.

Credit costs normalizing at c75bps as NPL and cover ratios level off

Asset quality was steady QoQ with NPL ratio at 2.3% from 2.25% in 2Q (Fig 5), and NPL cover at 97% (Fig 6). Credit costs normalized at c75bps in 3Q25 (Fig 4) without the “noise” of the banks running down of over-provisioning in 2023/24. At the briefing, management took pains to detail how the shift in the loan mix delivered superior risk-adjusted returns – ie that the higher yields from consumer, MSME and micro-loans exceeded the increase in credit costs.

 

Stock down to 1.0x P/bk, 7.4x PE, and 4.8% yield; Buy with TP of P160

The stock is not far off its 22-month low set last week. The current price puts BPI on 1.0x P/bk on our 2026E estimates, nearing Covid-lows (see righthand column). Including our forecast 4.8% dividend yield, our target price of P160/sh – derived using (ROE-g)/(COE-g) on long-term ROE of 14.5%, COE of 12% and growth of 8% - provides 60% potential total return. Buy maintained.

Metrobank -- 9M25 in line - loan growth slows, shifting assets back to treasury
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT P37.3bn in 9M25, still in line; but taking cautious stance

MBT reported 9M25 NPAT of P37.3bn, up 4% and in line with Regis (FY25E P50.9bn) and the Street (P50.2bn). Loan growth slowed to 11% YoY, though an improving loan mix and shifting to longer-dated treasury assets pushed 3Q NIMs up 5bps QoQ. Fee incomes were soft, but opex was kept under control, and credit costs moderated. CET-1 improved 70bps to 16.3%, suggesting the dividend (7.5% yield) can be sustained into 2026E. Maintain Buy and P100/sh target price.

 

Loan growth moderates to 10.8%, NIMs up 5bps on a shift to treasury assets

Gross loan growth slowed again to 10.8% YoY, from 13.3% in 2Q and from 17.0% at end-2024 (Fig 1). Consumer loans, though, grew 15.8%, slightly faster than 2Q. MBT had been planning for stronger loan demand, and instead shifted funds to securities, which now account for 40% of total asset (Fig 2). NIMs expanded 5bps QoQ despite upward pressure on funding costs (Fig 3).

Credit costs ease despite NPL bump; could see ECL review in 1Q26E

Fee incomes were soft, up just 1% YoY, but trading/fx gains picked up the slack, up 18% YoY. Opex increased just 1.7% YoY, helping 9M PPOP grow 12% YoY. NPL ratio blipped up to 1.7% from 1.5% in 2Q25 (Fig 5), as consumer loan NPLs jumped 60bps QoQ to 3.7%. Despite this, credit costs moderated to 60bps in 3Q25 (Fig 4) as the bank reduced NPL cover to 147% (Fig 6). Management is reviewing their ECL models which could see further wind down the over-provisioning in 2026E.

 

CAR jumps to 16.3%; special dividend sustainable?

The bank again paid P5/sh in dividends this year (P3.00 regular and P2.00 special), for a 7.5% yield. The slower-than-expected loan growth pushed consolidated CET-1 to 16.3, up 70bps QoQ. Unless loan demand dramatically picks up, we could see the dividend sustained in 2026/27E. The stock currently trades on 0.66x P/bk, and 5.5x PER, on our 2026E forecasts. We maintain our P100/sh target price, based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 12.25%, COE of 12% and growth of 8%. Buy.

ICTSI -- Ancillary revenues from Mexico port buoy 3Q25 profits up 27%
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

3Q25 profit up 27% to US$264mn

Headline 9M25 profit rose 19% to US$752mn, but adjusting for last year’s oneoffs and perpetual distributions, profit increased 24% YoY. In 3Q alone, profit grew 27% to US$264mn, partly supported by a temporary lift from storage revenues amid the congestion in Mexico. Overall, we view the results as broadly within Regis and Street expectations.

 

Temporary tailwinds drive revenue, volume acceleration in 3Q

Organic volume growth grew at a faster pace of +11% in 3Q25, as the impact of the May customs strike in Mexico subsided. Interestingly, the same disruption led to 3Q25 revenues rising 8% QoQ and 20% YoY to US$828mn, an acceleration from the +14% pace in 1H, as the appointment and retraining of new customs officials led to processing delays that boosted storage revenues. Management anticipates yield per TEU to normalize to above US$215 in 4Q, after rising to US$219 in 3Q.

 

EBITDA margin expands >100bps QoQ/YoY to 66.8%

EBITDA rose 22% YoY to US$553mn in 3Q25, taking 9M25 EBITDA up 17% to US$1.54bn.

Margins expanded by 130bps QoQ and 150bps YoY to 66.8% but this is expected to normalize in the coming quarter.

 

Big-ticket port acquisitions/auctions underway; leverage set to rise

While Maersk still has until Nov 11 to file an appeal, ICTSI is now in the final stage of completing the requirements for Transnet’s confirmation of the Durban Container Terminal 2 (DCT-2) award, a process that could be finalized by yearend. Management hopes to take over operations of the port within 1H26. The company is also keen to participate in the bidding of Tecon 10 in Santos, Brazil, which reportedly has a capacity of over 3mn TEUs.

 

Net debt-to-equity (inclusive of lease/concession liabilities and perps) stood at 2.3x in end-September, down from 3.0x in end-3Q24. Management plans to use some of its cash (US$873mn) to pay for DCT-2 and draw down ~US$750mn in credit lines to fund other expansion projects, pointing to a rise in leverage.

Century Pacific -- 3Q25: Top line growth bounces back; guidance downgrade reversed
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

9M25 net profit +10% to P5.8bn, top line +8% to P61.8bn

CNPF reported net profit of P5.8bn (+10% YoY) for 9M25 on the back of 8% top line growth to P61.8bn. The results look in line with REGIS estimates but slightly ahead of consensus estimates. For 3Q25 alone, profit and revenues both climbed 15% YoY to P1.9bn and P22.1bn, respectively. Top line was propelled by an acceleration in the branded business (+18% YoY from +6% YoY in 2Q) and a reversal in the OEM business (+4% YoY from -17% YoY in 2Q). 3Q gross margins contracted (-2.6ppt) as input costs normalized (milk and tuna) but this was offset by lower opex spend as EBITDA margin expanded (+50bp).

 

Branded volumes increase across segments

Branded segment sales climbed 18% YoY to P17.4bn in 3Q driven by volume—lifting YTD branded top line growth to +12% from +9% in 1H. Volume growth was seen across segments (marine, meat, milk). With the exception of marine, CNPF saw market share gains across the key businesses. Management said that the market share loss was most evident in its value-oriented tuna brand (-230bp YTD). Volume growth was surprisingly robust, and it’s possible that this was supported by demand for relief goods amidst this year’s unusually harsh weather.

OEM sales rebound

Top line for the OEM segment (21% of revenue in 3Q25) rose 4% YoY to P4.7bn. This marked a recovery to growth from two consecutive quarters of decline despite the high base (9M24 OEM sales +45%). The company elaborated that the previous uncertainties surrounding tariffs and global trade have begun to ease.

 

Opex savings offset gross margin pressure

EBITDA margin during the quarter expanded 50bp YoY to 13.2% despite a gross margin contraction (-2.6ppt YoY to 25.1%), as CNPF trimmed opex spend. However, management guided that opex will rise in 4Q due to increased advertising and promotions aimed to sustain and accelerate sales further.

 

Company upgrades guidance after prior downgrade

Management returned to its previous guidance of double-digit growth for both top and bottom lines, after previously reducing it to “high single to low doubledigit growth” last quarter. Management turned more optimistic as branded business growth rebounded, while concerns for the OEM business due to tariff uncertainties have eased.

Universal Robina -- 3Q25: Coffee costs temper EBIT growth; Int’l BCF headwinds persist
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

9M25 core profit +1% YoY to P9bn, top line +5% to P124.6bn

URC’s 9M25 core profit (ex-exceptional items) inched up 1% YoY to P9bn. Top line rose 5% to P124.6bn, in line with expectations, while EBIT lagged, up only 1% to P12.4bn. For 3Q25 alone, top line rose slightly +2% YoY as the +5% growth in domestic sales was dragged by the 4% and 1% decline in int’l and non-branded revenues, respectively. EBIT in 3Q increased a modest 3% YoY to P3bn as a rebound from poor non-branded (e.g. sugar) margins (+6.5ppt) was cushioned by the contraction in domestic EBIT margin due to coffee (-3.4ppt YoY). Expowdered beverages, management says 3Q EBIT growth would have been 16% higher YoY.

 

Domestic BCF still dragged by coffee

3Q revenues for the domestic business increased 5% YoY to P19.4bn primarily driven by volume growth. Snacks and RTD tea led with high single-digit growth but were offset by the still challenged coffee and noodles segments. Although the company noted that the noodle segment, despite being a declining category, has seen volume growth on the back of market share gains in both pouch and cups. URC appears to have been gaining traction in the category through aggressive A&P and a value-focused strategy.

Int’l BCF headwinds continue

Int’l BCF in 3Q saw revenue growth turn negative (-4% YoY) from +3% in 2Q as the previously mentioned macro and geopolitical problems in Vietnam, Thailand, and Indonesia persisted in the third quarter. Management noted that these have abated as of late, especially with Vietnam already showing a major lift in sales.

 

Margin expansion capped by coffee

Consolidated EBIT margin in 3Q was relatively unchanged (+10bp) at 7.6% as the expansion from the normalization of sugar price and supply was muted by stubbornly high coffee prices (2x higher than two years ago). The company guided that high-cost inventory will still linger until 1Q26, with improvements to start in 2Q26. Pricing interventions were noted to not have been sufficient to cover coffee input cost increases.

 

Guidance further downgraded

Management revised its guidance once again. Full-year top line growth is now projected to be “mid-single digit” from “mid to high single-digit”. It also expects “2025 EBIT weighed down by coffee” with acceleration next year from “low to mid-single-digit growth”. The back-to-back guidance highlight the multiple challenges facing the company. We should see by next year whether current brand-building efforts will be fruitful by next year.

Monde Nissin -- 3Q25: Noodle business remains under pressure; Quorn breaks even
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

9M25 top line up 3.0%, core profit down 4%

MONDE reported a 4% YoY drop in 9M25 core net profit to P7.2bn (which excludes gains/losses on its guaranty asset, among others but includes FX impact of USD cash), which would be a bit behind our expectations. 9M net sales was in line with estimates at P63.3bn (+3%), while margins were a little weaker due to elevated input costs for edible oils (coconut and palm oil). For 3Q25 alone, core net profit rose 5% YoY to P2.5bn with revenues up 4% YoY to P21.8bn. Consolidated gross margins improved QoQ (+1.6ppt) but are still down YoY (-2.1ppt).

 

Biscuits and “Others” continue to drive growth

3Q25 APAC BFB revenues grew 4% YoY to P18.4bn, primarily driven by volume growth from biscuits (+7% YoY) and “others” (+14%) but weighed down by noodles which saw overall volumes drop 1% in the quarter. The noodles business was still dragged by wet pouch category challenges with flattish volume growth (+0.3%). Overall, noodles saw a sequential market share drop given heightened competition from local and int’l brands. Within biscuits, crackers and graham had strong growth momentum, while wafers and cookies saw slower growth relative to competition.

 

Quorn breaks even on cost cuts; impairment charge may not happen

The meat alternative business saw 3Q sales dip 1% YoY on a constant currency basis but was still able to post its 5th consecutive quarter of positive “core” EBITDA and its first positive “core” profit in a while.

Gross margin jumped 5.5ppt to 26.7% in 3Q, behind its “transformation program”. Management says it expects to just around break even on an EBITDA basis in 4Q as it plans to reinvest into the brand to revitalize sales growth. At the current run rate, the company guided that an impairment charge will not be likely this year.

 

Dividends of P0.16/share declared

The company declared a dividend of P0.16/sh for shareholder as of Dec 4 (albeit payable in Jan 2026). This brings dividends declared this year to P0.31/sh. This level, if maintained, would be equivalent to 5% yield on current share price.

 

Margin and capex guidance revised; strong October for APAC BFB

Guidance for the top line was maintained with APAC BFB expected to deliver mid-single-digit growth even as top line challenges for Quorn are expected to “taper”. Management is now a little more upbeat on its margin outlook for APAC BFB as it is now expecting “sequential gross margin improvement from 4Q25 to 2Q26.” 2025 capex was likewise revised from P4bn to P4.5bn given construction progress in their biscuits plant. Management noted that October was a record all-time high month in terms of sales for APAC BFB, driven in part by calamity-related purchases. While this sounds like a good sign, we’d caution against extrapolating from one month.

Semirara -- 9M25 profit in line; contract extension muddles outlook
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

3Q25: Power unit beat offsets mining unit’s underperformance

Semirara’s 3Q25 profit (ex-FX) fell 53% YoY to P1.47bn, taking 9M25 profit down 38% YoY to P9.76bn. While the power segment delivered better-than-expected results, the mining unit appeared to underperform, mainly due to elevated noncash expenses.

 

Coal mining: Adverse weather prompts reduction in sales volume guidance

Coal mining earnings dropped sharply in 3Q25, down 80% YoY to P335mn, weighed by a 61% rise in non-cash costs. EBITDA contracted 22% YoY to P2.0bn, reflecting softer ASPs (–20% YoY to P2,249 – In line). Although 9M25 volumes increased 5% YoY to 12.9mn MT, management lowered its full-year target to 15.5–16mn MT from a previous target of 16–17mn MT, citing weather disruptions. Even with regional coal prices (ICI4 spot at US$46/MT) rebounding 13% from 3Q lows, the lower volume guidance could mean the company may not meet our full-year sales estimate.

Better plant availability mitigates ASP drop

The power unit’s 3Q25 profits fell 17% YoY to P1.2bn while EBITDA declined by 8% YoY to P2.2bn. Sales volumes rose 9% YoY on improved plant availability factor of 82% (vs. last year’s 71%), but this was offset by a 13% drop in ASPs to P4.4/kwh. Management expects spot prices to weaken further to P3/kwh next year (vs. 9M25: P3.70/kwh) and targets a lower spot exposure of 30% (vs. 60% in 9M25).

 

Higher sales volume target hinges on coal contract extension

With the coal operating contract set to expire in July 2027 and the extension still pending government approval, management is holding off on expanding capacity beyond the current 16mn MT. While volumes could fall short of our 19mn MT FY26E, capex (under review) could be much lower than our estimate of P5.5bn. Management expects to begin producing higher-grade coal at the new Acacia mine by 1H26, which, together with lower operating costs, could help mitigate coal price weakness. It also reiterated its commitment to optimizing dividend payouts despite the uncertain environment.

Converge ICT -- 3Q25: Top-line slowdown
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q revenue growth slows to +7%

Converge reported net income for 9M25 of P8.90bn, +8% YoY, on the back of revenue of P33bn, +10% YoY. For 3Q alone, revenue growth was +7% while net income was up +1% to P2.95bn. While it’s still conceivable for CNVRG to reach our FY25 profit estimate (P12bn), we’d describe the results as being short of expectations. Top line growth has decelerated and profit growth even more so.

 

Stable subscriber net adds but ARPUs dipping further

Converge added a net 107k new broadband subscribers in 3Q, taking it to 2.93m. The firm has managed to keep net adds at over >100k per quarter consistently. Revenue growth, though, is not keeping pace as ARPUs dip since the company is now mining more of the lower-income subscriber market.

 

Opex jumps; EBITDA and profit boosted by FX gain

The firm saw a chunky rise (+27%) in G&A to P2.80bn in 3Q, leading to a decline in operating profit. 

A large “Other income” gain of P409m, which Converge treats as part of EBIT and EBITDA helped boost those two items (as well as profit). Management explained that bulk of the gain was due to the appreciation of the USD (vs PHP) on its USD cash holdings. This suggests that, without the FX gain, net profit would have actually declined ~6% for 3Q.

 

Bifrost operation imminent

Converge’s big investment in subsea cables, Bifrost, is expected to begin operations soon. This could have implications on its reported profit, as Converge could need to begin amortizing the cost of its attributable capacity there.

 

Konektadong Pinoy Act: Boon or bane?

Management expressed its view that the soon-to-be implemented law, Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) will likely boost revenues for its enterprise segment as new operators lease Converge’s capacity. We note, however, that these new operators would only be leasing its capacity to compete against Converge’s residential broadband business (85% of revenue).

On the demand side, the improvement in net exports (+1.0% in 3Q from -0.1% in 2Q) was offset by deteriorating metrics across the expenditure accounts—private consumption (+4.1% from 5.3%), gov't consumption (+5.8% from +8.7%), and capital formation (-2.8% from +1.2%). If we exclude the pandemic YoY declines from 2020-2021, private consumption growth at +4.1% was the lowest since 3Q10's +2.6%. Private investments were negative for the first time at -2.8% since 3Q19's -0.7%. The decline was likely caused by the slump in construction (-0.5%) given the corruption scandal involving infrastructure projects. Gov't construction declined -26.2% in 3Q, slowing further from the 8.2% growth in 1Q and offset sustained double-digit growth in private construction (+16.7% from +14.6%). Exports grew for the fourth straight quarter (+7.0%), while imports tempered to 2.6% from 3.5% in 2Q. On the supply side, there was a broad deceleration in growth across the sectors. The service sector (67% of GDP) exhibited a below 6% growth rate at 5.5% for the first time since 4Q14 (+5.7%). The agri sector slowed to 2.8% from 7.0% given harsher-than-usual rains in the quarter. The industry sector slowed to 0.7% from 2.1% as manufacturing decelerated to +1.2% from +2.5%. This brings 9M25 GDP growth to 5.0%, below the lower end of the government's target of 5.5-6.5% and consensus and Regis expectations of 5.5% GDP growth for 2025. GDP for 4Q25 needs to grow by at least 6.9% to hit the minimum full-year target. Economic Secretary Arsenio Balisacan commented that current and next year macroeconomic forecasts are under review.

Inflation holds steady at 1.7% in October

Headline inflation remained unchanged at 1.7% in Oct relative to the previous month of Sep. This is in line with the consensus forecast of 1.8% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.4-2.2% for Oct. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past eight months. Inflation held steady in Oct as price upticks in fish and utilities (electricity, gas, and fuel) were balanced by slower increases in meat and vegetable prices. Food overall was up 0.5% YoY, slower than the 1.0% pace in Oct. Food saw faster price upticks in fish but slower price increases in meat and vegetables. Notably, rice prices are on their sixteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P42.6/kg and P42.9/kg as of Oct vs. P46.6/kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan, respectively. Electricity, gas, and fuel saw an acceleration to 2.5% YoY from the 0.9% increase the previous month likely driven by the recent depreciation in the peso. The peso depreciated to P59 per US dollar by end-Oct from P58.15 in the beginning of the month. Core inflation inched down to 2.5% in Oct from 2.6% in Sep, core non-food inflation likewise went down to 2.3% from 2.4% the previous month. This brings the YT-Oct headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. For 2025, the BSP expects inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8% in 2025. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.5%.

BSP projects inflation to accelerate until 1H26

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has projected inflation to accelerate until 1H26 but within the 2-4% target. BSP Deputy Governor Zeno Abenoja stated that the increase in 4Q25 and 1H26 "could be mainly driven by statistical effects (low base)" as well as anticipated rate adjustments for electricity. He added that by 2H26, global oil prices are projected to stabilize which will help keep inflation pressures under control. Abenoja added that even if headline inflation may pick up due to some supply-side shocks (bad weather), the underlying pressures have cooled down, which has been "one of the factors that was considered in shifting to a more accommodative stance."

Finance Secretary expects lower 2025 GDP growth due to gov't underspending

Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that gov't underspending in 3Q due to the corruption crackdown will likely drag overall GDP growth this year. The gov't disbursed P1.46trn in 3Q, P141.7bn (9%) less than the 1.6trn program for the period primarily due to lower spending in the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH). Recto, however, stated that he expects faster growth in the medium term given that they are "plugging those leaks (inefficient capital expenditures) and reallocating funds to high-impact investments" such as education, healthcare, agriculture, and digitalization. The gov't is targeting 5.5-6.5% GDP growth in 2025. Economic Secretary Arsenio Balisacan and Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman previously said that the 2025 GDP growth target is still attainable. 1H25 GDP growth was 5.4%. 3Q GDP data will be released by the PSA on Nov 7 (Fri). For 2026-2028, the gov't's target growth rate is 6-7%. Consensus and Regis expect GDP growth in 2026 to be 5.7% and 5.8%, respectively.

Government issues IRR for Konektadong Pinoy Act

The Dept. of Information & Communications Technology (DICT) has released the final implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA). The publication of the IRR paves the way for the implementation of the KPA. The IRR takes effect 15 days from publication (Nov 5). Among the highlights of the KPA: new operators no longer need to secure a franchise (license) from Congress to offer services; carriers will be required to share their infrastructure with other operators at non-discriminatory rates; government will establish a “Spectrum Management Policy Framework” to govern management, issuance and recall of spectrum; and it provides for consumer rights and expectations on performance standards. We expect implementation of the KPA to usher in greater competition, particularly for the wireline broadband sector. DICT claims that “three” foreign operators are planning to enter the market because of the KPA.

Commercial banks’ loan growth slows to 10.5% in September

Preliminary data showed that loans of commercial banks rose 10.5% in September, moderating further from the 11.2% pace in August. Loans to productive activities decelerated to 9.1% from 9.9% the previous month, driven by a steeper 10% contraction in manufacturing loans (vs. -5.5% YoY in Aug) and a sustained slowdown in real estate loan growth (+9.2% in Sep from +11% in Aug). Consumer loans still grew at a heady pace of 23.5%, though a tad slower than August’s 23.9% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up further to 7.3% in September from 6.6% in August.

PAGCOR 3Q25 revenue falls YoY and QoQ

PAGCOR revealed that its 9M25 revenue rose 6% YoY to P84bn. From this, we estimate that 3Q25 revenue fell 19% QoQ and 10% YoY. The BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gaming platforms beginning August 17. PAGCOR previously said that online gaming revenue fell c. 50% after this BSP directive.
