REGIS REPORT

11 April 2025

The PCOMP closed flat after wild moves in global markets this week, inching lower by a measly 1.75 pts WoW to 6,082.44. Volatility increased amid a surge in trading activity with ADTO jumping to US$168m (from US$121m), largely driven by foreign outflows with net foreign selling accelerating to US$65m for the week and foreign participation jumping to 63% (from 40%).

 

Large crosses in BDO (+2.8% WoW) and SM (+2.6% WoW) in the latter half of the week easily comprised over a third of market volume, reflecting investors' concentrated interest in Philippine equities. Locals were notably quiet given the shortened work week and upcoming Holy Week holidays.

 

The index printed a fresh 52-week low Monday, plunging 4.3% to the 5,800 level as foreigners dumped shares amid tariff woes. Bargain hunters emerged in the following days and helped lift the index back above 6,000, following some relief from Trump's mid-week announcement of a 90-day tariff pause for all countries except China.

 

Global port operator ICT (+1.0% WoW) continued to see big swings but managed to end higher for the week as investors weighed the evolving outlook on global trade. This follows the previous week's 9.0% drop.

 

On local macro, the BSP resumed its easing cycle with a 25-bp rate cut to 5.50% at Thursday's Monetary Board meeting, though this did little to inspire broad buying into equities as the move was already widely anticipated by the market. Notably, the central bank's inflation forecast for 2025 has been revised sharply down to 2.3% (from 3.5% as of the February meeting), largely due to falling rice prices and the anticipated impact from weaker global demand. BSP Gov. Remolona guided for more 25-bp cuts later in the year.

 

Among sector indices, Mining (+1.3% WoW) yet again outperformed while Property (-2.0%) slumped, pulling back after decent gains the previous week.

 

PGOLD (+7.2% WoW) was the week's top gainer thanks to strong 4Q earnings that beat market expectations, along with a dividend declaration that offered a high yield of 7.0%.

 

The peso slightly weakened but remained below the 57:$1-mark, down by 0.3% WoW to 56.978, while the PH10Y yield moved higher by 15.6bps to 6.2576%.

Philippine Strategy - Rice (and oil) is Life (6) -- April rate cut near certain as CPI falls below 2%
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

March CPI slows to 1.8%; rice CPI -7.7%

Inflation decelerated yet again in March, with CPI coming in at 1.8% YoY, from 2.1% in February, and at the bottom end of the BSP’s forecast. Rice (-7.7% YoY) continued to drive CPI down. Meanwhile, the “reciprocal tariffs” announced by the US last week sent oil prices tanking, putting even more downward pressure on CPI. The threat of a trade war has also led to a spike in Fed rate cut expectations, while the USD has weakened further.

 

All of this points to a nearcertainty of a policy rate cut by the Monetary Board at its meeting on 10 April. Our FY forecast of 50bps now seems overly cautions. We reiterate our bullish call on consumer names, on slowing inflation, minimum wage hikes, and election spending. High dividend yield stocks should continue to do well, too.

 

Rice, oil prices could keep inflation subdued for longer

Headline inflation slowed further in March to 1.8% YoY from February’s 2.1% print, and came in at the low-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 1.7-2.5%. Unsurprisingly, rice was again the main driver declining 7.7% YoY.

Meanwhile, world crude prices have plunged over fears of sharply slower GDP across the globe, partly as a result of the reciprocal tariffs announced by US President Donald Trump last week. Since the Philippines imports nearly all of its petroleum needs, lower oil prices should keep domestic inflation low too

 

Even more rate cuts possible, but…

The main constraints to more aggressive BSP cuts have been a) a continued US Fed pause, and b) the PHP’s depreciation against the USD as a result, which could feed back into higher import prices. But last week’s “Liberation Day” announcement has stoked fears of a recession in the US, and slower growth globally. Expectations of Fed rate cuts for 2025 jumped to over 120bps from barely over 25bps at the start of the year. Meanwhile, the USD continues to weaken against the world’s major currencies.

 

Still bullish on consumers; Div yield plays offer good defensive returns

We reiterate our bullish stance on consumer stocks, as demand rebounds from slower inflation, minimum wage hikes in 2H2024, and election spending. Lower interest rates should keep higher dividend-paying stocks in the spotlight.

 
Century Pacific Food - 4Q24: Branded segment revenue growth quickens
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Profit rises 14% on revenue growth of 12%

Century Pacific (CNPF) released FY24 results showing revenues rising 12% to P75.5bn and profit growing 14% to P6.3bn. While the bottom line is a slight miss, we are positive about the overall results. Branded revenue growth of 7% for the year looks better than the rest of the sector, and 4Q expansion bodes well for FY25.

 

OEM business leads top line expansion

Consolidated revenue for CNPF rose 12% but the top line growth was led by the OEM business (21% of revenue), which saw sales jump 36%. Note that the OEM segment is largely a cost-plus business and the large rise in revenue is a result of higher input costs.

 

Branded revenue growth accelerates in 4Q

The more important branded business (79% of revenue), on the other hand, saw sales rising a respectable 7%. Management noted that growth was driven entirely by volumes as, like many other consumer firms, no price increases were implemented during the year. We also highlight that revenue growth for the branded business accelerated to +10% for 4Q, compared to +6% for 9M24.

Gross margin gains reinvested

There was a notable drop in margins (gross and EBITDA) for 4Q, which is not particularly unusual for the firm. As in the past, management said that the gains from lower input costs were reinvested in A&P in order to sustain longer-term brand-building efforts. As a result, full-year EBITDA margins were just a tad lower (-30bp) despite gross margins being 210bp wider.

 

Guidance of “low to mid-teens” growth maintained

The company is guiding for growth of “low to mid-teens” for both top and bottom lines, consistent with past guidance. The Milk category is expected to be a key driver given its success in capturing market share. The entry into new products/categories (coconut products, plant-based protein, condiments) should also add a few points. Capex guidance for FY25 is P3-4bn, also similar to FY24 (P3.2bn).

 

Management also offered a glimpse into 1Q25, saying that revenue growth for the period would be in the high single digit (i.e., below 10%). Unlike in FY24, it’s the branded segment now leading the growth with that segment showing growth in the “low double digits”. Overall. CNPF offered a cautiously optimistic view for this year, given improved inflation and the likely consumption boost from election spending.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP resumes easing cycle and guides for more cuts this year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas cut the policy rate by 25 bps to 5.50%. The move can be attributed to the improved inflation outlook and heightened external risks which enabled the shift to a more accommodative monetary policy. Notably, the inflation forecasts have been revised from the previous Feb policy meeting. The risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2025 is down to 2.3% from 3.5%, while the 2026 forecast is now 3.3% from 3.7%. While the 2027 risk-adjusted inflation forecast remains at 3.2%. The BSP notes that risks to the inflation outlook have eased and is broadly balanced from 2025 to 2027. Upside pressures stem from possible higher transport costs, meat prices, utility prices while downside risks are from easing rice prices and the impact from weaker global demand. The Monetary Board however cautioned a more challenging external environment, which may hamper global GDP growth and can negatively impact domestic economic activity. BSP Governor Remolona guided for more rate cuts this year which will be done 25 bps at a time. Although he added that it's unlikely that all four remaining Monetary Board meetings will result in a cut. He also noted that the easing cycle may likely conclude this year but remains cautious for the possible risk of a hard landing. The next Monetary Board meeting will be on June 19. Bloomberg median consensus for the year expects 75 bps worth of rate cuts to 5.00%.

DTI open to lowering tariffs on US goods

The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) Secretary Cristina Roque stated that they are "definitely" looking at reducing tariffs on US goods but will discuss the extent and terms of the offer as they are set to meet [with the US] soon. Recall that US President Donald Trump announced sweeping reciprocal tariffs which will take effect on April 9, which includes a 17% tariff on Philippine goods. Roque also cited that the gov't is considering a collective response with other ASEAN member-countries to address the higher US tariffs. This comes after Malaysia Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's call for a united ASEAN response to the US tariffs, stating that the country, as ASEAN chair, will be ready to lead efforts to be heard on the int'l stage. Cambodia is facing a 49% tariff, followed by Laos (48%), Vietnam (46%), Myanmar (45%), Thailand (37%), Indonesia (32%), Malaysia (24%), Brunei (24%), Philippines (17%), and Singapore (10%). In 2024, the Philippines exported $12.14 bn worth of goods to the US while importing $8.17 bn, resulting in a trade surplus of $3.97 bn.

PH Dollar reserves down 1.1% at end-March

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Mar saw a 1.1% MoM decrease to $106.2bn from $107.4bn at end-Feb. This primarily reflected the national gov'ts drawdowns on foreign currency deposits with the BSP to meet external debt obligations as well as the BSP's net foreign exchange operations. The level of dollar reserves by end-Mar was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Reserve position in the IMF decreased 2.6% MoM to $653mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $12.8bn, which jumped 5.9% MoM. Foreign investments decreased by 1.7% MoM to $88.6bn. Foreign currency deposits sharply dropped 38.2% MoM to $498mn. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025.

Loan growth slows to 12.5% in February

Asset quality metrics were broadly stable in February, despite the slight 0.1% MoM drop in loans. (These numbers are adjusted using loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP) to reduce “noise” from those items.) Total loans (ex-IBL and RRP) increased 12.5% YoY, slowing from the 13.5% YoY pace in January.

Banks' asset quality remains steady in February

Using preliminary figures from the BSP, the NPL ratio of Philippine banks remain unchanged at 3.38% in Feb relative to Jan. This was slightly lower than the 3.44% NPL ratio in Feb 2024. In nominal terms, NPLs were flat MoM at P513.3bn but was higher by 10% YoY (vs. Feb 2023: P466.1bn). Total gross loans likewise were unchanged at P15.2tn; On a YoY basis, gross loans decelerated slightly to 12.1% YoY in Feb from 13.4% YoY in Jan. NPL coverage ratio was steady at 95.36% in Feb from 95.25% in Jan (vs. 100.06% in Feb 2024). Past due loans inched up slightly 0.8% MoM to P637.8bn, bringing the past due ratio to 4.20% in Feb from 4.17% in Jan and 4.31% in Feb 2024. Restructured loans accounted for 2.05% of the industry’s loan portfolio, unchanged from Jan but lower than the 2.16% in Feb 2024. Loan loss reserves were stable MoM at P489.6bn in Feb. This brings the loan reserve ratio to 3.23% in Feb 2024 from 3.22% in Jan and 3.44% in Feb 2024.

LPC: BPO office demand rebounds in 1Q25; residential unsold inventory days rises

During its 1Q25 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy fell 1ppt QoQ to 17%. Metro Manila office vacancy fell 2ppt QoQ to 16%. We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Net take-up hit 78,000sqm nationwide in 1Q25 – from 100,000sqm of net closures in 4Q24. BPO net office take-up rebounded to 136,000sqm, from 1,000sqm in 4Q24 (66,000sqm in 1Q24). Net office take-up from traditional tenants (including government agencies) fell 15% QoQ (flat YoY) to 83,000sqm. Meanwhile, POGO firms experienced net closures of 141,000sqm, from net closures of 200,000sqm in 4Q24. LPC expects office rental rates in Makati and BGC to rise this year but also projects rental rates in other parts of Metro Manila to fall. Meanwhile, the residential unsold inventory level rose from 34 months of sales in 4Q24 to 38 months in 4Q24. Residential sales fell 6% YoY (up 49% QoQ) to 6,508 units. Unsold inventory rose 28% YoY and 9% QoQ to 81,400 units.

Trust AUMs jump 19% YoY to P7.4trn in 2024

Trust Assets Under Management (AUMs) closed 2024 at P7,372bn – just under $130bn – up 18.6% YoY and up 2.3% QoQ. The distribution of the growth, though, was somewhat complicated. AUMs directly within the banks were up just 6% to P4,451bn, while “non-bank” AUMs surged 44% YoY to P2,921bn. This was partly due to two banks – RCBC and Union Bank – that spun off their AUMs into “stand-alone trust” vehicles. Those two alone combined for about P300bn, or a third of the growth in non-bank AUMs. We also saw roughly P200bn growth in AUMs of SunLife, as the new rules allowed the assets of the insurance company to be managed by the Trust entity. (And to recall, BPI’s AUMs are also considered part of the “non-bank” trust group, having spun off its Trust department a while back.)
