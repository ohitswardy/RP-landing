REGIS REPORT

30 May 2025

The PCOMP headed lower a second straight week, retreating below the 6,400-level anew and erasing gains for the month of May dragged mostly by foreign selling at the month-end MSCI rebalancing. The index closed lower by 1.1% or 71.57 pts WoW to 6,341.53.

 

Net foreign outflow accelerated to $267m for the week, largely due to Friday's $283-m block sale of shares of RRHI (+1.7% WoW) held by DFI Retail Group, which were reacquired by the company. Excluding this transaction, foreign flows this week would have netted +$16m.

 

Continued rotation into laggards lifted shares of AGI (+18.6% WoW) and MONDE (+13.1% WoW) -- the top gainers in the index for the week. On the other hand, JFC (-8.2% WoW) was the largest decliner as sell flows placed to take advantage of the increased liquidity from its MSCI upweight overwhelmed against the expected inflow. This played out similarly for MSCI Small-cap Index addition AEV (-3.5% WoW) which was also among the week's bigger underperformers. Meanwhile, large caps SM (-5.0% WoW) and SMPH (-4.0% WoW) were the biggest drags on the index, succumbing to heavy foreign outflows.

 

On domestic macro, the BSP signaled two more rate cuts for the year after its first 25-bp rate cut in April, which will take the key rate down to 5.00% by YE-25, citing the slowdown in inflation. However, RRR cuts are likely done for the year but may resume next year, after the last 200-bp cut to 5.00% in March.

 

Elsewhere, traders kept watch of key developments in the Marcos cabinet, with the president's call for resignations now extended to heads and board members of government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCC) such as pension fund administrators SSS and GSIS, government banks DBP and Landbank, and gaming regulation agency PAGCOR, among others. Recall that so far, Pres. Marcos has decided to retain most of the cabinet secretaries in his administration's economic management team.

 

The peso slipped anew, down by 0.9% WoW to 55.772:$1. Yields fairly steady with the PH10Y -1.2bps WoW to 6.2569%.

Philippine Property - 1Q25 recap: Residential presales improve QoQ; BPO office demand rebounds
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A tale of three segments

The three major property segments showed varying performances in 1Q25: BPO office demand showed a remarkable rebound; residential presales showed a more modest sequential improvement; while mall revenue growth decelerated. Meanwhile, the POGO ban caused an increase in both residential and office vacancy in the Manila Bay area.

 

BPO office demand rebounds

Despite further POGO closures, we estimate Metro Manila office vacancy stayed flat QoQ (at 18%), thanks to the rebound in BPO office demand (see Figure 4). This is a very encouraging signal after the weak 4Q24. Furthermore, consultants and developers said that BPO firms continue to look for space, suggesting that artificial intelligence has not led to a net reduction in BPO employment yet.

 

Residential reservation sales improve sequentially but…

After staying flat QoQ in 4Q24, aggregate residential reservation sales rose 5% QoQ (down 26% YoY). Meanwhile, unsold inventory levels showed modest declines, thanks to the reduced launch plans of the developers. Although we consider these to be positive signs, we also refrain from being overly bullish. 

While developers generally said that provincial and high-end projects performed well, selling Metro Manila mid-income units remains challenging. Weak demand for such units led developers to offer discounts and rent-to-own schemes for ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units. As a result, residential receivables receivable days lengthened for some developers (see Figures 6 to 11).

 

Mall revenue growth slows to 6%

Mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators slowed to 6% YoY, from 8% in 4Q24. We estimate that same-mall revenue growth was c. 4%, at the low end of the historic range (of 4%-8%). The landlords said this was due to weak cinema operations, as tenant sales generally grew about 7% YoY.

 

POGO ban raises vacancies in Manila Bay area

The POGO ban, which became effective in January 2025, has been particularly detrimental to residential and office properties in the Manila Bay area. Colliers estimates that office vacancy there rose to 41%, 6ppt above the 4Q24 level, and 11ppt higher than 3Q24, when the POGO ban was first announced. Furthermore, the departure of foreign POGO employees brought residential vacancy in the district to 53%, from 27% in 3Q24.

 
Cebu Air - Cleared for takeoff, despite 1Q25 miss
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Still poised for strong earnings growth

Cebu Air’s 1Q25 results demonstrate how management’s decision to accelerate investments in 2H24 are affecting short-term profits but with an eye to magnifying them longer term. Despite the disappointing 1Q bottom line, encouraging revenue trends, as well as macro tailwinds from lower jet kero prices and a weak USD, provide a case for strong earnings recovery. We reduce our FY25E profit forecast by 55% but maintain our Buy rating at a reduced TP of P48/sh. Note that our forecasts/valuation assume full dilution of the convertibles.

 

1Q25 results: Strong topline negated by surge in fixed costs

Headline profit fell 79% to P466mn. Adjusting for one-offs and FX adjustments, we estimate core profit dropped 74% QoQ/YoY to P506mn, despite a 20% improvement in revenues. While fares fell 6% YoY due to base effects from the timing of Easter break, they have been trending higher sequentially alongside seat load factors, proving that new routes introduced in 3Q are increasingly becoming less of a drag.

Fleet-related expenses such as maintenance and financing costs, as well as airport costs, continued to surge, but should normalize by 4Q. We cut our 25E profit estimates by 55% to incorporate 1Q25 actuals and introduce our 26E/27E profit forecasts.

 

Short-term pain for (hopefully) long-term gain

Management accelerated investments on aircraft and engines in 2H24 as a response to the ongoing global aviation supply chain issue and the impact of this action only started to show up in CEB’s P&L in 4Q24. Nevertheless, macro tailwinds on jet kero and the PHP:USD exchange rate should help accelerate earnings recovery. Crucially, CEB’s market share gains are visible and sustainable, in our view, and favorable market dynamics should help propel earnings growth over the next 6 to 18 months.

 

TP of P48/sh offers 42% total potential upside

We peg our TP at P48/sh, based on 6x FY25E EV/EBITDA. At current levels, CEB trades at 5.5x FY25E EV/EBITDA, below global peers of 7x. Risks: higher jet kero prices, weak PHP:USD, lower demand. Every 10% drop in our average jet kero forecast of US$81/bbl (vs. spot: US$78) could lift EBITDA/profit by 11%/62%.

SEC approves Ayala Corp's P20bn pref share offering

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has approved Ayala Corp’s preferred share offering of up to P20bn. Offering consists of the re-issuance of 5mn preferred B shares, with an overallotment option of up to 5mn shares, at a price of P2,000/sh. Proceeds of up to P19.86bn will be used to repay short-term bank loans, general corporate purposes, and capex. AC’s outstanding pref shares amount to P28bn with average dividend rate of 5.5%. AC’s parent level net debt (inclusive of prefs) stood at P193.5bn in end-March 2025. Capex guidance (parent level) at P31bn for 2025, of which P15.9bn is earmarked for ACEN. AC is trading at a 27% discount to its market-based NAV of P805/sh.

Meralco seeking DOE nod to proceed with 800MW CSP bidding

Meralco is awaiting the issuance of certifications from the Department of Energy (DOE) to proceed with the competitive selection process (CSP) to procure 800MW of electricity. 200MW RE baseload starting Jan 26, 2026. 600MW baseload starting Feb 26, 2028. These two proposed CSPs are part of Meralco’s 2025 power supply procurement plan to secure over 2.1 GW of capacity spanning 2026-2028. Apart from the two CSPs, Meralco also plans to conduct CSPs for 450-MW mid-merit and 900-MW baseload within the year. It also received an unsolicited proposal for the supply of 1.43-MW RE baseload power.

Jollibee sells stake in C-Joy Realty

JFC in a disclosure, stated that it will sell its 30% stake in C-Joy Poultry Realty Inc. to Agrotex Commodities Inc. for P33.9mn. The chicken production plant is a partnership between JFC and US-based food company Cargill Inc. The facility supplies chicken to Jollibee Group restaurants. JFC said it will keep its 30% stake in C-Joy Poultry Meats Production. Rationale for the divestment is the company's “strategic shift towards an asset-light business model, enabling greater capital efficiency and sharper focus on scalable, high-return investments."

Globe targets single-digit growth in subscribers and revenues

Globe Telecom Inc. (GLO PM) is targeting single-digit growth in prepaid subscribers and revenues for 2025. Tanbauco noted that the rise of new content and apps besides the already popular Facebook, TikTok, and Mobile Legends will help drive and stimulate usage.

ACEN acquiring a 25% stake in first large-scale offshore wind project in Camarines Sur

ACEN signed definitive agreements with Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners’ (CIP) Growth Markets Fund II to acquire a 25% minority stake in CIP’s proposed offshore wind project. Further information regarding the transaction and the project will be disclosed in due course. ACEN’s RE portfolio stands at 6.98GW, of which 3.6GW are operating and 2.6GW are under construction. Of which, 2.4GW are in the Philippines, of which 1.3GW are operating.

BSP expects at least two more rate cuts this year, RRR cut next year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees to cut rates at least twice more in 2025 after the first 25 bps cut last April. This would bring the current benchmark rate from 5.5% to 5.0% by year-end. BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. elaborated that there is more leeway for easing and that a rate cut is on the table for the next Monetary Board policy meeting given the slowdown in inflation. Inflation cooled to 1.4% in April from 1.8% in March, YT-Apr inflation currently stands at 2.0%. Remolona also stated that the BSP is looking to shift to a point target for inflation (2%), from the current 2–4% target band. Implementation for the change was said to not be anytime soon but next year at the latest. The BSP is currently working with the IMF on studying the shift to an inflation point target. Expectations for reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cuts were said to be unlikely for the rest of the year but more likely to be done next year. Last March, the RRR for universal and commercial banks was reduced by 200bp to 5%, while the ratio for digital banks was cut by 150bp to 2.5%, and the RRR for thrift lenders was cut 100bp to 0%. The next Monetary Board meeting (third out of six this year) is on June 19.

Gov't posts P67bn budget surplus in April

The government recorded a budget surplus of P67.3bn in April which is higher 57% YoY and a reversal from the P376bn deficit in March. This brings the 4M25 deficit to P411.5bn, wider 79% YoY. The Bureau of Treasury stated the higher cumulative deficit was due to "faster expansion in public spending to fuel economic activity and support priority programs of the Marcos administration". Revenue collections in Apr inched down 2.8% YoY to P522.1bn, as non-tax revenues dipped 69% YoY. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 11% to P420.5bn mainly due to higher corporate income tax collections due to tax filing deadlines in Apr as well as increased personal income tax and VAT collections on the back of the enhancement of digital services. Customs collections fell 7% to P74.7bn given fewer working days due to Holy Week and lower import volumes given global trade uncertainties. Non-tax revenues dropped by 69% to P24.1bn due to the earlier timing of remittances from Government Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs) last year. The gov't expects this to improve in the succeeding months as dividends from various GOCCs are set to be remitted to the National Treasury soon. Expenditures slowed 8% YoY in Apr to P454.8bn mainly driven by lower primary spending and interest payments. Primary spending for the month was down 4% YoY to P408.3bn. The decrease in primary spending can likely be attributed to the election ban on public spending which started late March till election day on May 12. Interest payments dipped 31% to P46.4bn as lower debt repayments in the month was due to the shift of payment timings due to the Lenten holidays. The gov't targets for the 2025 budget deficit to narrow to 5.3% (1.5trn) of GDP from 5.7% in 2024, and the government aims to further reduce this to 3.7% in 2028.

DA backs 15% rice tariff retention; gradual hike under review

The Department of Agriculture (DA) will recommend retaining the 15% rice import tariff through 2Q25 to prevent price surges during the lean season (July–September) when domestic supply is typically at its lowest. The 15% tariff, reduced from 35% in July 2024 under EO 62, is subject to review every four months. The next review is in June 2025, a year after the cut was enacted. Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stressed that any tariff hike should be gradual and aligned with harvest cycles of exporting countries to avoid disruptive supply shocks. Laurel cautioned that abruptly reverting to the previous 35% rate could destabilize markets and instead proposed a "slight, phased adjustment" to avoid sudden price swings. DA officials warned that a tariff increase during lean months, when local output declines by 20–30%, could drive retail rice prices significantly higher. DepDev (Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Dev't) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan supported exploring a seasonal tariff scheme, where import duties would rise during times of low global prices and fall during price spikes, to help stabilize farmer incomes without fueling inflation. Vietnamese rice prices rose to US$396/MT in Apr 2025, a 6% increase from Feb levels and just 5% below Jan's high of US$416/MT. The recent uptick follows the end of Vietnam’s harvest and reflects tightening global supply. However, this is still notably lower than 2023's peak of US$619/MT. Vietnam remains the Philippines’ largest rice source, accounting for over 40% of imports. Retail rice prices for local and imported well-milled rice as of May 22 range from P38–50/kg and P40–48/kg, respectively vs. the start of the year (Jan 22), P40–55/kg and P40–52/kg.

Gov't expects higher domestic rice output; mulls floor price for rice

The Department of Agriculture (DA) stated that they expect the domestic output of palay (unmilled rice) to hit their target of 20.4 MMT (million metric tons), 7% higher than last year, barring any harsher than usual weather disturbances. This would yield approximately 12.65 MMT of rice. Note that the Philippines imported a record 4.68 MMT of rice in 2024, this filled the shortfall between domestic production and the estimated total consumption of 16.5 MMT that year. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in a dialogue with farmers said that the gov't is studying the implementation of a floor price for rice. A floor price is being considered in order to protect farmers from losses and ensure stable supply. Marcos also eyes to return the role of the National Food Authority (NFA) as a rice buyer and price stabilizer. Recall that the Rice Tariffication Law in 2019 rescinded the NFA's ability to import rice and sell it directly to the public—effectively liberalizing the importation of rice. Currently the NFA can directly buy rice from local farmers but is limited to maintaining a buffer stock of around 555,000 MT or 15 days' worth of rice. The NFA at present, buys fresh palay (unhusked rice) for P19/kg and P24/kg for dried. Marcos states that they are trying to determine whether the current P19 and P24 prices are enough.

Digital payments rise 31% YoY in April

Payments made digitally over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) rose by 31% to P1.85tn in April, moderating from the 44.5% pace in March. Adjusting for the timing of Easter break, combining digital payment transactions for the months of March and April alone rose 37% YoY, mildly accelerating from the +36% growth rate in 2M25. Brings 4M25 transaction value to P7.15tn, up 37% YoY. Payments through InstaPay, which handles real-time payments of up to P50k, rose 51% YoY to P841bn in April. By volume, InstaPay payments surged 190% YoY, even faster than the blistering 97% pace in March. Large-ticket payment transactions that go through PesoNet, rose 18% YoY to P1.011tn in April, slower than the 38% pace in March.

Air pax volumes up 11% YoY in 1Q25

The Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) reported that air passenger volumes rose 10.9% YoY to 15.98mn in 1Q25. Growth rate has normalized after the Covid rebound. Cebu Air (CEB PM) grew its market share to 40% in 1Q25 from 38% in FY24 and 34% in FY19. Cebu Air’s market share gains have been at the expense of PAL (market share at 25% in 1Q25 vs. 27% pre-Covid) and Air Asia Philippines (9% in 1Q25 from 13% pre-Covid). Note that CAB data does not capture ticketed passengers that do not show up for their flights (i.e. no-show). Domestic pax volumes rose 14% YoY to 8.5mn while international pax volumes grew 7% YoY to 7.5mn.

Supreme Court rejects challenge to LRT-1 concession

The Supreme Court denied a petition by Bagong Alyansang Makabayan, Train Riders Network, and others, contesting the validity of the Concession Agreement of Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC). Petitioners claimed the periodic tariff adjustments provided in the concession agreement violate due process due to the absence of a procedure for giving notice and conducting hearings. Plaintiffs also challenged the government’s assumption of real property tax liabilities for rail project asset. The court found that the Concession Agreement does not violate due process rules of the Public Service Act and the Administrative Code of 1987, which require notice and hearings for rate-fixing. Concession agreement provides a mechanism for fare increase applications, statutory notice and hearing requirements. Court upheld the government’s assumption of real property taxes to entice private participation. Note that since taking over the concession in 2015, LRMC was only granted tariff adjustments in 2023 (+21%) and 2025 (+21%). LRMC is entitled to fare hikes of 10.25% every two years, which is aligned with an annual 5% rate adjustment that was initially due in 2016. LRMC is the operator of LRT-1 and is owned by Metro Pacific (35.8%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19.2%), and Macquarie Infra (10%).

Marcos retains economic team but makes changes in other departments

After President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s call to resign last Thursday, fifty-two (52) Executive Branch officials submitted their courtesy resignation. Those who will remain in the Cabinet are Trade Secretary Cristina Roque, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, DepDev (Department of Economy, Planning, and Dev't) Secretary Arsenio Baliscan, Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman, and SAPIEA (Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs) Frederick Go. The five economic team members were retained given their importance in keeping the country's "economic health" amidst current domestic and international headwinds. Meanwhile, Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla was reassigned to be the new head of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), replacing Maria Antonia Yulo-Loyzaga. DOE undersecretary Sharon Garin will serve as the Officer-in-Charge for the DOE. The ongoing performance review for the rest of the fifty-two officials will be completed in the coming days. Results of said review and any further "calibrations" to the cabinet will be announced in the following days as well.

GOCC officials also called to resign by Marcos

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has extended the call for courtesy resignations to gov't-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs), more than a week after calling the resignation of his Cabinet secretaries. State-run firms' non ex-officio chairpersons, chief executive officers (CEOs), as well as all appointive directors/trustees/members of their respective GOCCs' governing boards were instructed to immediately submit their courtesy resignations to the President. Notable GOCCs include, among others, Social Security System (SSS), Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), Land Bank of the Philippines, Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP), Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA), Philippine Reclamation Authority (PRA), Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR), National Power Corporation (NPC), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth). Similar to the "bold reset" of Marcos' Cabinet, the President will have the final say on the performance review, the acceptance, reassignment, or replacement of GOCC heads. So far, Commission on Higher Education (CHEd) Chairman Prospero De Vera III has been replaced by the CHEd commissioner Shirley Agrupis. Darlene Berberabe will be the new solicitor general in place of Menardo Guevarra. Heads of PAGCOR, PRA, Landbank have submitted their courtesy resignations but no changes have been made regarding their positions as of late.

Marcos' trust rating inches up 3ppt to 32% in May

In the latest Pulse Asia Research Survey, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s trust rating improved slightly to 32% from 29% in April. Notably, this is still much lower than the 82% trust rating at the start of his term as well as his 42% trust rating in Feb prior to dropping to 25% in March following the arrest of Former President Rodrigo Duterte. Marcos' trust rating in Luzon stands at 41% in May from 34% in April, Visayas at 39% from 38%, Mindanao at 3% from 10%. Vice President Sara Duterte's trust rating saw a 7ppt dip to 50% in May from 57% in April. Duterte's trust rating in Luzon was at 29% from 37%, Visayas at 46% from 70%, Mindanao at 97% from 95%.
