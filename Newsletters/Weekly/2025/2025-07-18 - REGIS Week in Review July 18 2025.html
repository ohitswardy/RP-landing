REGIS REPORT

18 July 2025

The PSEi plummeted this week to around its lowest levels in nearly 3 weeks, -2.42% WoW at 6303.72, trailing behind a mostly higher region this time. The local benchmark initially started firm and drifted around the psychological 6500 handle in the earlier part of the week before being weighed down by the sizable placements on index heavyweights SM and SMPH (which collectively account for almost 20% of benchmark in terms of weighting) at discounted levels by a foreign seller. Sizes and prices of the placements were as follows: SM $140m worth at 830/share and SMPH $77m worth at 23.18/share. SM capped the week as worst performer in the index, -7.87% at 819.5/share, while SMPH likewise tumbled sharply, -6.09% at 23.15. Outside of those two, the placements appeared to drive some selling into other Philippine names as well as subscribers raised funds for their allocation – hence the negative breadth of the market this week with 23 laggards (13 of which by over -2%) vs only 7 gainers in the PSEi.

Another key name in focus was online gambling name PLUS which capped the week -40.21% WoW. The stock was highly volatile throughout the week and is now down by -65% from its all-time high recorded just a little over a month ago – market cap is now just a little over P100b from nearly P300b at its peak in early June. Excluding the placements mentioned above, note that PLUS was the most actively traded stock in the market all week. Along with PLUS, BLOOM which recently made an entry into the internet gambling space as well, also fell by -6.32%. Discussions over tighter restrictions or potentially even a full-out ban on online gambling remained a top subject in the mainstream media throughout the week. Investors are also anticipating the nearing State of the Nation Adress by President Marcos on July 28 where he is expected to comment on the matter – whether that is an announcement of new restrictions/taxes or a full-out ban (similar to what the Marcos admin did to POGOs) remains to be seen.

Another key development this week is the start of the 2Q results season kicked off by BPI – its net income grew by +8% YoY to P33b in 1H, inline with Street consensus (of P66b FY2025E) but behind Regis’ P70.7b FY2025E. Of note was a sizable +142% spike in provisions and a decline in NPL cover to 97% from 100% in 1Q, which suggests a jump in write-offs, likely in the consumer/SME segment. More details in the upcoming briefing on Tuesday at 4pm. In terms of stock performance, the three biggest lenders fared relatively better than the broader market this time after visibly lagging behind over the past several weeks: BPI -0.81% WoW, MBT -0.21% and BDO +0.13%.

Among the few brights spot this week was TEL +2.12% WoW after its div yield nearly hit 8% last week. TEL stood out as an outperformer vs peers CNVRG -4.95% and GLO -0.96%. Power names MER +0.65% and SCC +0.15% which are more defensive in nature also managed to resist the broad selloff. Meanwhile in the consumer space, one of our top picks PGOLD +1.53% managed to outperform as well while the broader sector turned lower: URC -5.05%, JFC -3.44%, MONDE -2.68% and CNPF -1.84%.

ICTSI’s Mexico port throughput drops in June, as aftermath of customs strike lingers

International Container Terminal Services Inc. (ICTSI) saw a decline in container throughput at its Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) port in Mexico, with volumes dropping 14% year-on-year to 111,000 TEUs in June, according to data from Mexico’s port authority. This decline mirrored a broader slowdown across all Manzanillo ports, which posted an 11% drop in container volumes. The decrease follows a four-day customs labor strike from May 12–15, which disrupted logistics operations across Manzanillo for over a month. Given that tariffs for import and export containers are the same at CMSA, the disruption may have also contributed to reduced yield.

For the second quarter of 2025, CMSA recorded volumes of 354,000 TEUs, up 4% year-on-year but slower than the 8% growth in Q1. In the first half of the year, cumulative volumes reached 741,000 TEUs, a 6% increase from the same period last year, although still trailing ICTSI’s forecast of 1.69 million TEUs (+16% YoY). To meet that target, monthly throughput for the remainder of the year must average 157,000 TEUs. The port’s current capacity is 1.7 million TEUs (around 142,000 TEUs/month), with plans to expand to over 2 million TEUs (168,000–175,000 TEUs/month) once a new berth is completed by August or September 2025.

Note that Regis’ forecasts assume that the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) treaty will not be canceled until July 2026. A 5% change in CMSA’s FY25E volumes could impact EBITDA by 1.6% and net profit by 2.4%. CMSA currently accounts for 12% of ICTSI’s consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of group EBITDA.

Ayala Land to add 700,000sqm of mall space over five years

Ayala Land said it would add 700,000sqm of mall space over the next five years. This would expand its mall floor area by 32% over 5 years.  The company also increased its mall redevelopment budget by P4.5bn to P17.5bn. More malls will be redeveloped over the next few years; the original P13bn only covered four flagship malls.

Philippine Seven targets 5,000 stores by 2026; appoints new chairman and president

Philippine Seven Corp. [PSC] (SEVN PM) in its Annual Stockholders' Meeting announced its plans to have at least 5,000 stores nationwide in 2026. The company had 4,200 stores as of 1Q25 with a target to have around 4,600 stores by end 2025 on a capex budget of P5.5bn. More than half of new store openings will be in Visayas and Mindanao given lower property prices and less pressure from competition. Former President and CEO Jose Victor P. Paterno was officially appointed as Chairman of PSC, while Richard Lee, previously COO, was named president of PSC.

FGEN signs agreement to sell 60% of natgas assets to Prime Infrastructure

First Gen (FGEN PM) and Prime Infrastructure signed a Share Purchase Agreement for the sale of a 60% stake in First Gen’s natgas subsidiaries to Prime Infrastructure. Equity stake is valued at P50bn, subject to adjustments and modifications as may be agreed by the parties. FGEN will likewise be entitled to receive additional earnout amounts, subject to the fulfillment of certain conditions. The deal is subject to the approval of the Philippine Competition Commission and certain conditions precedent.

A valuation of P50bn for 60% would imply a valuation of P83bn for 100%. This compares with  Regis  valuation of US$1,779mn (P99.6bn). At P83bn for 100% of its gas assets and assuming our valuation for all other assets, Regis estimate a NAV of P143bn or P40/share. Prime Infrastructure is the operator of the Malampaya natural gas field, which is a major source of gas for FGEN’s plants.

GCash to increase “friction” on gambling “clickable links”

GCash is introducing new standards for gambling-related advertisements to “increase friction” in user interactions beginning July 16. According to a representative from GCash speaking to InsiderPH, these gambling ads will remain clickable, but enhanced security measures and reminders will be added. The new guidelines apply to various ad formats including pop-ups, hero banners, category banners, and headers.

Okada Manila volumes fall QoQ and YoY in 2Q25

Okada Manila reported a decline in volumes for the second quarter of 2025, both year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter, across all major categories. Gross gaming revenue (GGR) for the period fell 9% QoQ and 20% YoY to ₱7.1 billion. Mass table drop plummeted 29% QoQ and 31% YoY to ₱7.5 billion, while mass table GGR dropped 33% to ₱2.0 billion. Gaming machine handle also declined by 4% QoQ and 17% YoY to ₱46.6 billion, with corresponding GGR falling 7% QoQ and 6% YoY to ₱2.8 billion. VIP rolling chip volumes dipped slightly by 1% QoQ but sank 33% YoY to ₱52.4 billion. Interestingly, VIP GGR rose 29% QoQ to ₱2.3 billion despite being down 21% YoY, largely due to a higher-than-usual win rate of 4.4%.

Cebu Air grows pax volumes by 8% YoY in June; Load factors dip but remain high

Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported that systemwide pax volumes rose 8% YoY to 2.2mn in June. The growth was markedly slower than the 24% pace in 5M25 due to onset of the leaner season. Seat capacity rose 9% YoY and load factors dipped 73bps YoY to 87.5% due to the earlier start of school calendars (from July to June). Domestic pax volumes rose 7% YoY to 1.7mn with load factors up 42bps to 92.1%. International pax volumes grew 10% YoY to 558k with load factors down by ~3ppts to 76.0%.

As of 1H25, pax volumes are up 21% YoY to 13.9mn, trending mildly behind Regis full-year forecast of 28.5mn (+16%). To reach this, pax volumes for the rest of the year would need to average 2.4mn per month. Every 1% reduction in pax volumes could reduce EBITDA/profit forecasts by 3%/17%, holding other things constant. We are encouraged by robust load factors, which spells strength in fares.

SM Prime will not list REIT in the near term

SM Prime Holdings (SMPH) has no immediate plans to list a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT), according to director Hans Sy. While the company has not ruled out the possibility, it clarified that a mall REIT IPO is not in its near-term agenda.

Malampaya’s $893mn MP4 drilling project is on track for completion in 2025

The $893mn Malampaya Phase 4 (MP4) drilling project remains on track for completion by November 2025. The initiative aims to extend the operational life of the Malampaya deep-water gas-to-power project and secure a new gas supply by 2026. It includes drilling two deep-water wells—Camago-3, which is expected to be completed by late July or early August, and Malampaya East-1—alongside a new exploratory well named Bagong Pag-asa-1. The Malampaya gas field, located offshore Palawan, supplies around 20% of Luzon’s electricity and is the country's only indigenous gas source. It operates under Service Contract 38, which was recently extended to February 2039. The project is led by Razon-owned Prime Energy Resources Development, together with UC38 LLC, PNOC Exploration Corp., and Prime Oil and Gas Inc.

Finance secretary expects below 6% GDP growth this year

Finance Secretary Ralph G. Recto stated that the Philippines’ second quarter GDP growth in 2025 is expected to improve from the first quarter’s 5.4%, but full-year growth may still fall below 6% due to mounting uncertainties surrounding U.S. tariffs. The first quarter figure came in below consensus expectations of 5.7%, and also slower than the 5.9% posted in the same period in 2024. Recto highlighted that government spending and household consumption—together accounting for over 70% of the economy—will remain the primary growth drivers. He guided full-year growth to settle around 5.7–5.8%, compared to 5.6% in 2024. The government recently revised its 2025 GDP growth target down to 5.5–6.5% from the previous 6–8% range, citing global economic headwinds. Consensus forecasts peg 2025 growth at 5.5%, while Regis expects a slightly higher 5.8%.

Marcos appoints three new ERC commissioners

President Marcos appointed three new commissioners to the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC). The three include the new chair, Francis Saturnino Juan, who replaces Monalisa Dimalanta who resigned only a few days ago. The two other commissioners are Amante Liberato and Paris Real. The appointments complete the line-up at the ERC with all five seats now occupied. Juan is currently president of IEMOP, which runs the electricity spot market. He was long associated with the ERC and previously served as Executive Director, the most senior position below the commissioners. Liberato and Real are both lawyers. Liberato was previously with the Commission on Audit. Real has been engaged as a lawyer in proceedings with the ERC.

Finance Secretary is against complete ban on online gambling

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto believes that stricter regulations and higher taxes on online gambling would be more effective in managing the industry than a complete ban. He proposed a 10ppt increase in taxes (on revenue). Online gaming firms currently pay 38% of their e-casino and e-bingo GGR to the government. They pay 24.25% of sports betting GGR to the government. Secretary Recto is also considering the mandatory listing of online gambling firms on the PSE. There are currently about 70 online gaming firms, including integrated resort operators that have online operations.

DOF downplays 1% remittance tax impact

The Department of Finance (DOF) addressed concerns over the impact of a new 1% remittance tax imposed by the U.S. as part of the “One Big Beautiful Bill,” signed into law by President Donald Trump on July 4. The tax, which will take effect on January 1, 2026, will apply to remittances sent through nonbank channels by U.S. citizens, green card holders, visa workers, international students, and other legal migrants. Despite the policy, the DOF expects minimal economic impact.

The BSP projects 2026 remittances to grow 3% to US$36.5 billion, with roughly US$14.8 billion—or 40%—originating from the U.S. However, this figure may be overstated, as some remittances recorded as U.S.-origin may actually be routed through American financial institutions. DOF Chief Economist Domini SD. Velasquez estimates that only 12.8% (US$1.9 billion) of the total may fall under the scope of the tax, and as such the remittance tax will affect only 0.009% of GDP in 2026. She emphasized that the overall economic effect would be negligible and that OFWs could compensate for the taxed amount.

MSRP of imported rice to be cut to P43/kg

Department of Agriculture (DA) announced a reduction in the maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) of imported rice to ₱43 per kilogram, effective July 16. This is down from the ₱45/kg price set on March 31, as global rice prices continue to decline. The DA first introduced an MSRP of ₱58/kg in January 20 and has implemented gradual reductions since. The measure addresses persistent high retail prices that had reached up to ₱64/kg, despite favorable conditions such as lower global prices, reduced tariffs, and a stronger peso. As of July 14, well-milled rice prices ranged from ₱37–₱50/kg for local rice and ₱38–₱45/kg for imported rice, compared to ₱40–₱53/kg and ₱40–₱54/kg, respectively, in January.

President Marcos, Jr. approves privatization of North-South Commuter Railway O&M contract

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has also approved the privatization of the North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR) operations and maintenance (O&M) contract under a public-private partnership (PPP) arrangement. The O&M contract is valued at ₱229 billion. The ongoing construction of the 147-kilometer railway project will connect Central Luzon, Metro Manila, and CALABARZON, and is expected to serve 800,000 passengers daily in its first year. Pre-operations for the project will begin in March 2026 until July 2027. The concession period for the partial operations of Phase 1, which stretches from Clark International Airport (CIA) to Valenzuela (13 stations), will commence in December 2027 and continue until September 2028. Asset quality steady in May

Philippine banks maintained broadly stable asset quality in May. When excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP)—which often distort the data—the non-performing loan (NPL) ratio edged up slightly by around 2 basis points (closer to 1.5bps) to 3.58% from 3.56% in April. In nominal terms, NPLs rose 1.6% month-on-month to ₱527 billion, outpacing the 1.2% growth in total loans. Meanwhile, the Past Due (PD) ratio fell by 2bps to 4.47%, despite PD loans increasing 0.9% MoM to ₱659 billion. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio ticked down by 1bp to 2.13%. The NPL coverage ratio dipped slightly to 94.6% in May from 95.1% in April. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) re-accelerated to 11.7% YoY, up from 11.2% in April, though slightly behind the reported 12.4% due to a sharper rise in IBL/RRPs (+27% YoY). This growth aligns with the industry’s full-year forecast of 12%. By bank classification, universal and commercial banks saw a 2bps increase in NPLs, while digibanks posted the most improvement, with NPL ratios down 38bps to 9.79% and PD ratios down 79bps to 18.28%. Overall, the steadiness in asset quality is reassuring, particularly given the relatively strong loan growth.

Power rates rise in July due to higher generation costs and weak peso

Power rates in the Philippines to rise July due to increased generation charges and a weaker peso. Household rates increased by ₱0.49 per kilowatt-hour (kWh), or about 4%, reversing a two-month rollback. For a typical household consuming 200 kWh per month, this translates to an added ₱98 on their bill, as the effective rate climbs to ₱12.64/kWh. The hike offsets a prior ₱0.86/kWh drop seen in May and June. Generation charges alone rose by ₱0.34/kWh as global fuel prices climbed, driven in part by tensions such as the 12-day conflict between Israel and Iran. Transmission and tax charges each added ₱0.07/kWh. Higher costs from both Power Supply Agreements (PSAs) and Independent Power Producers (IPPs) contributed to the increase, with PSA and IPP rates rising by ₱0.45 and ₱0.50/kWh, respectively. However, a decline in spot market prices helped temper the hike, as Luzon’s electricity demand dropped by 770 MW in June—more than offsetting 490 MW lost to plant outages.

ERC chief Dimalanta files ‘irrevocable resignation’

Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) Chairperson and CEO Monalisa Dimalanta has submitted her irrevocable resignation, as confirmed by Malacañang Press Officer Clarissa A. Castro. Dimalanta was originally appointed by President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in 2022. She was previously suspended by the Ombudsman in August 2024 but reinstated by the President two months later. Following her resignation and the retirement of two commissioners on July 10, the ERC is now left with only two sitting commissioners.

Marcos' net trust rating improves by 19ppt to 18% in June

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s net trust rating improved significantly in June, climbing to 18% from -1% in May, according to the latest Social Weather Stations Survey. Net trust ratings are derived by subtracting the percentage of respondents who express "little trust" from those who say they have "much trust." Marcos' "much trust" rating rose to 48% in June from 38% in May, while the "little trust" rating dropped to 30% from 39%. Despite this improvement, the current net trust rating remains considerably lower than the 63% he enjoyed at the start of his term. The recent uptick follows a decline to -3% in March 2025 after the arrest of former President Rodrigo Duterte.

Meanwhile, Vice President Sara Duterte’s net trust rating inched up by 1 percentage point to 38% in June from 37% in May. Her "much trust" rating slightly increased to 61%, while the "little trust" figure held steady at 23%. Her current rating, though improved from the 23% seen during her February 2025 impeachment by the Lower House, still pales in comparison to the 73% net trust rating she held at the start of her term.

Marcos approves P6.8tn 2026 budget

President Ferdinand Marcos. Jr. approved the proposed P6.8tn national budget for 2026 which will be submitted to Congress later this month. The P6.8tn budget is equivalent to 22% of GDP and 7% higher than 2025's P6.33 budget. Priority allocations are the education, agriculture, and transportation sectors, which will see increases in their respective budgets for next year.

Marcos-Trump meeting set later this month to discuss tariffs and security

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. is scheduled to meet U.S. President Donald Trump later this month to discuss the recently imposed 20% tariff on Philippine imports to the United States and other geopolitical issues. The meeting will take place during Marcos' visit to the U.S. from July 20 to 22, following the deployment of a Philippine economic and trade delegation earlier in the week to negotiate tariff revisions. The new tariff rate is an increase from the previous 17%. Other key topics expected to be addressed include China’s increasingly assertive activities in the South China Sea.

BSP issues draft regulations on online gambling payment services

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has issued draft regulations governing online gambling payment services. Under the proposed rules, individuals who wish to participate in online gambling will be required to create a designated account called an Online Gambling Transaction Account (OGTA). Transfers to this account will be capped daily at 20% of the average daily balance of the user’s main transaction account. Additionally, the OGTA will only be operational during a six-hour window each day. Once created, all lending services offered on the same digital platform will be disabled, and users will have the option to further limit daily transfers or to disable the OGTA altogether. Separately, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that a new law to tax online gambling could be passed within the year. At present, online gaming operators pay 38% of their gross gaming revenue (GGR) from e-casinos and e-bingo to the government, and 24.25% from sports betting GGR.

Gambling ads to be regulated and pre-screened

PAGCOR signed an MOU with the Ads Standard Council (ASC) to pre-screen and regulate all gambling-related advertisements across media platforms. Gambling ads are now part of the ASC’s must-screen categories, which include alcoholic beverages, over-the-counter medicines, food supplements, and breast milk substitutes. Last week, PAGCOR ordered the removal of billboard and other out-of-home advertisements related to gambling activities by August 15.
