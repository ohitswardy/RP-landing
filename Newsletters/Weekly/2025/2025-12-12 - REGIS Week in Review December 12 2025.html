REGIS REPORT

12 December 2025

Philippine shares rebounded this week, taking the index to reclaim the 6,000 level after last week’s slump. Local investors continued to provide support, while foreigners remained net sellers for the third straight week, trimming positions mainly in banks, property, and select consumer names. The PCOMP finished the week higher by 1.5% or 87.50 pts WoW to 6,036.72.

Providing some macro support to the market was the BSP delivering a widely expected 25-bp rate cut to 4.50%, with Gov. Remolona signaling that the central bank is at or near the end of its easing cycle. The move followed benign November inflation (out Friday last week) of 1.5%, down from 1.7% in October and also softer vs the consensus estimate of 1.7%. Recall this brought YTD-November average inflation to 1.6%, below the 2-4% target range of the BSP and Street forecast of 1.7%.

ICT (+3.9% WoW) remained a standout performer and the index’s biggest positive contributor after officially securing the long-awaited concession to operate the Durban Container Terminal in South Africa, which lifted the stock to fresh all-time highs anew. BDO (+3.6% WoW) and ALI (+6.4% WoW) were also outperformers and big positive movers for the index this week.

But the biggest gainer on the index was ACEN (+10.9% WoW), rallying on local bargain-hunting that helped trim its YTD loss to 33.8%,  though it remains the index's second largest decliner for 2025. The PCOMP's worst performer for the year thus far has become PLUS (-22.2% WoW), which this week suffered deep losses amid market chatter of insiders unloading shares amid mounting regulatory risk on online gaming in the last recent months.

Elsewhere, in politics, Pres. Marcos has pushed for the passage of four anti-corruption measures, including the high-profile anti-dynasty bill, in an effort to tackle corruption and help repair public trust in the government. The flood control corruption issue remains largely unresolved and although arrest warrants have been issued, the suspected "bigger fish" behind the scandal remain at large.

The Philippine peso was weaker by 0.3% WoW to 59.087:$1, while the PH10Y yield climbed above 6% for the first time in five weeks.

RESEARCH
Philippine Seven - Moving beyond a challenging year
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Trimming our estimates, maintaining Buy

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) shares have fallen 40% YTD, following disappointing results. Operational issues and intensified competition have put pressure on top line and SSSG. We reduce our profit forecasts for FY25/26E by 10/6% but maintain our TP as we roll over estimates. Despite near-term headwinds, we believe that the resolution of in-store issues alongside the CVS+ rollout and continued expansion in newer areas can revitalize growth. We remain optimistic on SEVN’s long-term prospects. At 14x FY26E PER with a 3-year EPS CAGR of 16%, we are still upbeat about SEVN and reiterate our BUY rating.

 

Challenging year so far for small format retailers…

Excluding the new and hyper-aggressive hard discount chains, SSS for the small format retailers, including SEVN, has been weak this year. We believe (1) the departure of POGOs, (2) the unusually bad weather in 3Q, and (4) the entry of hard discounters all combined with company-specific operational issues (e.g., ewallet downtime for SEVN) to produce the weak top line.

Getting crowded in Luzon but not so in VisMin

The competitive environment for small format retailers is unlikely to ease up, especially in Metro Manila and Luzon. Industry-wide store additions are likely to remain brisk, with Puregold signaling intent to join the race. We don’t think this will be the case for VisMin, however, as the region remains underpenetrated and SEVN is the only player that has significant presence (~1,200 stores) and largescale expansion capability.

 

Profit dip for FY25; EPS CAGR of 16% beyond this year

We expect profits this year to dip YoY but forecast 3-year EPS CAGR of 16% on 13% revenue CAGR from thereon. The new systems to allow wider acceptance of non-cash payments, the further rollout of CVS+, and continued network expansion with its first-mover advantage in VisMin, are key factors that should help drive growth.

 

Readjusting estimates; maintaining Buy rating and TP

We adjust our forecasts for the latest results but maintain our target price at P60/sh as we roll over estimates. The TP implies FY26E PER multiple of 22x, still below historical average. The stock also provides a modest 2% yield.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI, Transnet sign concession agreement for Durban port

ICTSI and South Africa’s state-owned Transnet signed a 25-year partnership agreement for Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2). Agreement will take effect by January 1, 2026, when ICTSI will take over operations of the port. ICTSI will own a 50% less one (1) share interest in the joint venture entity. Earlier reports indicated that ICTSI would make an upfront payment of ZAR11bn (~US$650mn) to Transnet. DCT-2 is expected to increase its capacity from 2mn TEUs to 2.8mn TEUs and improve Gross Crane Moves per Hour (GCH) from 18 to 28 as well as Ship Working Hour (SWH) from 60 to 120. We previously estimated DCT-2 could add P30/sh (6%) to ICTSI’s valuation. ICTSI trades at 10.7x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 18x PER vs. peer average (12.6x EV/EBITDA / 18x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA / 20x PER).

PPA approves 20% hike in cargo, passenger charges for ICTSI's 50%-owned Manila North Harbour

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) approved a 20% hike in cargo-handling tariff and passenger terminal fee at Manila North Harbour (a.k.a “North Port”. Cargo-handling tariff will be raised in two equal tranches: Jan 1, 2026 and July 1, 2026. PPA also approved 10 new tariff items and raised tariffs on other charges (e.g. porterage, parking and storage). Approved port charges were broadly the same as North Port’s petition (+20.32%). We have modeled a 20% tariff hike over a two-year period. We estimate North Port accounts for 9% of consolidated volumes, 4% of revenues, and 5% of EBITDA. ICTSI owns 50% of North Port.

ICTSI Mexico port volumes drop 6% YoY in November but expansion prospects still firmly positive

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), fell 6% YoY to 124k TEUs. In contrast, volumes in other ports in Manzanillo rose 23% YoY, indicating market share loss. Combined volumes across all ports in Manzanillo rose 10% YoY to 324k TEUs. Brings YT-November volumes at CMSA to 1.4mn TEUs, up 4% YoY. Despite the volume softness in 4Q25 so far, we remain positive on CMSA’s 12-month outlook, supported by the anticipated completion of its ~20% capacity expansion by year-end. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA. ICTSI trades at 10.7x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 18x PER vs. peer average (12.6x EV/EBITDA / 18x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA / 20x PER).

AREIT shareholders approve share-for-property swap

AREIT shareholders approved a previously announced share-for-property swap. AREIT will acquire P19.5bn worth of assets from Ayala Land in exchange for 441mn new AREIT shares. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 15% to P158bn. AREIT’s shares outstanding will increase by 12% to 4.16bn shares. This implies that the AREIT shares will be priced at P44.15/share. This is 4% higher than the last closing price of P42.50/share. AREIT will acquire two malls with a combined GLA of 375,000sqm. Ayala Center Cebu and Ayala Malls Feliz. The companies aim to complete the transaction in 2H26. We estimate that the Ayala Group would still need to sell another c. 50mn AREIT shares in order for the latter to meet minimum public ownership requirements. ALI is trading at 61% discount to NAV, 0.9X FY26E P/B, and 10.0X FY26E P/E. AREIT currently offers 5.8% FY26E dividend yield. The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.0%.

Megaworld to infuse malls into MREIT in 2H26

Alliance Global President Kevin Tan said that Megaworld would infuse about 70,000sqm of malls into MREIT in 2H26. MREIT previously announced that it would acquire 200,000sqm sqm of office buildings from Megaworld in a share-for-property swap. 165,000sqm in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio. 35,000sqm in Eastwood, Quezon City. This will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 41% to 680,000sqm. MREIT will issue up to 1.36bn new shares to Megaworld at a minimum price of P14.39/share. This implies a value of c. P19.6bn. The issuance will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 37%. The minimum price is 3% higher than the last closing price. If MREIT will issue 1.36bn new MREIT shares, then we estimate that the AGI Group would need to sell c. 100mn MREIT shares in order for MREIT to meet minimum public ownership requirements. MEG currently offers 4.4% dividend yield and is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.2X FY26E P/E. MREIT currently offers 7.6% FY26E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The 10-year government bond yield is currently 5.9%.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP cuts rates further to 4.50%; PH economy expected to recover in 2H26

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.50%, the lowest since Sep 2022. This is the fifth consecutive 25bp cut this year (125bp total) since the decision to resume easing back in Apr 2025. The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 200bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024. BSP Governor Eli Remolona emphasized that the possibility of further cuts next year will remain hinged on the data and would be more likely "when things are worse than we thought" (e.g., 3Q GDP growth worse-than-expected). On the economic growth outlook, it was noted to have weakened further as business sentiment remains negative given the lingering impact of the corruption scandal. Remolona also commented that "the new data suggests that even 4Q will see weak growth". Domestic demand is expected to rebound slowly with the lag effect of easing alongside improvement in the "pace and quality of public spending". The recovery was noted to likely take place in 2H26 vs 1H26 as previously expected. The inflation outlook was said to still be benign with expectations well-anchored. The BSP still expects below target inflation for this year (1.6%) but slightly revised its inflation forecasts for 2026 and 2027 to 3.2% and 3.0% from 3.1% and 2.8%, respectively. The first out of the six Monetary Board meetings in 2026 will be likely be held during the 2nd week of Feb. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.50% and inflation to average 1.7% and 1.8%, respectively. For 2026, consensus expectations are one 25bp cut to 4.25% and inflation at 2.6%, while Regis expects two 25bp cuts to 4.00% and inflation to settle at 3.0%.

Inflation inches down to 1.5% in November

The headline inflation rate in Nov eased to 1.5% from Oct's 1.7%. This was a tad below with consensus expectations of 1.7% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.1-1.9% for Nov. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past nine months. Inflation in Nov decelerated slightly given slower YoY increases in meat and vegetable prices. Food overall was only up 0.1% YoY, moderating from the 0.5% pace in Oct. Notably, rice prices were up MoM (+0.4%)—the first MoM price increase since Jun 2024. Rice prices are down 15.4% YoY in Nov, marking the eleventh consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P42.7/kg and P43.5/kg as of Nov vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan, respectively. Core inflation inched down to 2.4% in Nov from 2.5% in Oct, core non-food inflation was unchanged at 2.3% relative to the previous month. This brings the YT-Nov headline inflation rate to 1.6%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target. For 2025, the BSP expects inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.7% and 1.8%, respectively. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.50%. For 2026, consensus expects one 25bp cut, while Regis expects 50bp cuts total.

PH dollar reserves at $111.1bn end-Nov

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Nov increased 0.8% MoM to US$111.1bn, a 13-month high. The increase primarily reflected the surge in global gold prices which offset the drop in foreign exchange holdings due to the BSP's intervention in the forex market. The peso depreciated against the US dollar from an average of P58.3 in Oct to P58.9 in Nov. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.4x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$105bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn.

Tentative signing of the General Appropriations Act (GAA) by the President on Dec 29. The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than 2025's P6.33trn (+10% YoY) budget. SEC sets monthly interest rate cap on loans up to P10,000

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), through a memorandum circular, has issued an order limiting the interest rate that financing and lending companies can charge on loans of up to P10,000 and loan tenor of up to four months. The circular will take effect on April 1, 2026. Banks, investment houses, savings and loans associations, insurance companies, cooperatives, and other credit institutions already regulated by law are not subject to the new circular. The prescribed ceilings are as follows: The nominal interest rate is capped at 6% per month or 0.2% per day. The effective interest rate is limited to 12% per month or 0.4% per day. Effective interest rate is the total nominal interest paid plus other fees and charges but excluding penalty and late payment fees. Penalties for late or non-payment is capped at 5% per month on the outstanding scheduled amount due. A total cost cap of 100% of the total amount borrowed (applying to all interest, other fees and charges, and penalties), regardless of the time the loan has been outstanding. Non-compliance with the prescribed ceilings will be subject to a fine of P50,000 on first offense, a larger monetary penalty plus suspension of financing and lending activities for 60 days for the second offense, and a revocation of Certificate of Authority and Certificate of Incorporation by the third offense. Any circumvention or attempt to circumvent the ceilings shall also constitute a violation.

Asset quality mixed in Oct - NPLs improve, but PDs up MoM

October banking sector asset quality indicators were mixed. Excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs), system-wide NPL ratio improved to 3.53%, down 3bps MoM from 3.56% in September, and down 29bps YoY from 3.81% in Oct 2024. It is also the lowest NPL ratio so far in 2025. The ex-IBL/RRP runs counter to the headline report NPL ratio (3.33% up 2bps MoM) to the sharp drop in IBP/RRPs in October. In nominal terms, NPLs were down 0.4% MoM to P536.8bn. However, Past Due (PD) ratio was up 4bps MoM to 4.52%, though this was down 14bps YoY. In Peso terms, PDs increased 1.5% MoM and 7.3% YoY. Restructured Loans (RL) were flat, inching down 1bp MoM to 2.19%, but up around 6bps YoY. NPL Cover dipped slightly to 94.6% from 95.1% the previous month. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP), meanwhile, decelerated 10.8% YoY, compared to the 11.1% pace in September. That’s the slowest monthly YoY growth since September 2024. By bank type, it was the large universal/commercial banks (UKBs) that saw a large PD spike, up 6bps MoM, while the smaller thrift banks (TBs) and digibanks (DGBs) posted big MoM improvements of 21bps and 57bps MoM respectively. The reverse was true for NPLs, though, with UKBs improving 2bps MoM, while the TBs and DGBs reporting 20bps increases in NPL ratio MoM.

PAGCOR believes industry GGR could hit low P400bn level in 2026

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that industry GGR could hit the low P400bn level in 2026. Mr. Tengco’s statement suggests that he expects a recovery from the 3Q25 level. 9M25 industry GGR grew 16% to P309bn. 3Q25 GGR fell 15% QoQ (flat YoY) to P94.5bn. 3Q25 GGR of licensed casinos grew 3% QoQ (-10% YoY) to P45.6bn. 3Q25 GGR of bingo + e-games fell 28% QoQ (+14% YoY) to P45.7bn. September GGR of bingo + e-games fell 45% (from June) to P12.2bn. Mr. Tengco also said that the sector has only recovered 10%-15% of the revenues that were lost since the e-wallets were ordered to delink from gaming platforms.

Marcos urges Congress to prioritize four anti-corruption-related bills

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. during a Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting has directed Congress to prioritize the passage of four proposed legislative measures in an effort to tackle corruption and promote good governance. Anti-Dynasty bill. To prevent the consolidation of power given more than half of incumbent public officials are from dynasties. Sen. Hontiveros, author of the bill, described political dynasties as relatives up to the fourth degree of consanguinity or affinity who run at the same time for national or local posts, including party-list seats. Party-list System Reform Act bill. To ensure the genuine representation of marginalized sectors and to prevent the use of the party-list system as a backdoor for political dynasties or large corporations. Independent People's Commission (IPC) Act bill. The creation of a law-created oversight body to replace the Independent Infrastructure Commission (ICI). Citizens Access and Disclosure of Expenditures for National Accountability (CADENA) Act bill. Publication and digitalization of all public budget data given scrutiny over the budget and the flood control project scandal. Opposition lawmakers, however, are pressing for Marcos to certify the measures as "urgent" in order to further fast track the passage of these bills. When the President certifies a bill as urgent, Congress can pass it on second and third reading on the same day, bypassing the required three-day gap.
