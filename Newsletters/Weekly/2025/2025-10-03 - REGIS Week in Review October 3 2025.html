REGIS REPORT

03 October 2025

Philippine equities saw a choppy week with sentiment remaining cautious following the corruption scandal at the public works department while foreign outflows persisted. The PCOMP was down 1.2% by mid-week due to quarter-end redemptions, but managed a modest rebound to end the week decently higher, by 1.4% or 81.74 pts to 6,108.86.

 

Biggest positive mover for the PCOMP was ICT (+3.6% WoW) which raked in over a third of the index’s weekly gains in points, as strong foreign inflows lifted the stock +5.4% on Friday alone. Yield plays DMC (+5.3% WoW), MER (+5.0% WoW) and GLO (+4.4% WoW) also outperformed.

 

Consumer names were generally positive, seeing JFC (+4.4% WoW), URC (+3.2% WoW), MONDE (+2.9% WoW), and PGOLD (+2.9% WoW) all outpace index gains. Note September inflation is out next week and expectations are for an accelerated but still soft CPI print, with the BSP forecasting a 1.5-2.3% range and Street median estimate settling at 1.9%. Also, news of a proposal to hike excise taxes on sugary drinks were largely shrugged off.

 

All sector indices finished higher save for Property (-1.3% WoW). ALI (-4.7% WoW) sold off, reflecting an overall weak outlook on the residential segment, particularly the luxury market which is seen to slow as “conspicuous consumption” cools while the political temperature is high.

 

Certain corporate stories were also in focus. Investors seemed to cheer the appointment of Victor Lee Meng Teck, former CEO of CIMB Singapore, as new president of SECB (+5.5% WoW). MWC (+8.8% WoW) announced the completion of its acquisition of WawaJVCo from Prime Infra. Meanwhile, books open next week for the $500m+ Maynilad IPO, with IFC and ADB reported to be considering bids of $100m and $145m, resp., at P15/sh.

 

The peso strengthened by 0.4% WoW to 57.881:$1. ADTO weakened to $104m, a testament to weak market appetite amid the political uncertainty. Foreign outflows accelerated to $74m for the week.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Security Bank names Singaporean banker as next CEO

Security Bank Corp. (SECB PM) has appointed Victor Lee Meng Teck as its new President and CEO effective early January 2026, replacing Sanjiv Vohra who was president for six years. Vohra will remain as senior advisor to the board once he steps down next year. Lee has over 30 years of leadership experience throughout Asia, having been the former CEO of CIMB Singapore and CEO of Growth Markets for CIMB Bank Berhad. Security Bank in its disclosure highlighted Lee's contribution to the growth of CIMB Singapore, doubling the bank's revenue and raising its ROE to nearly 20%.

Ayala Land sees subdued sales

Ayala Land’s Augusto Bengzon said that property investors are on “wait and see” in light of the flood control corruption scandal. “The investment appetite is a little bit subdued now.”

SEC approves share-for-property swap between Ayala Land and AREIT

The SEC approved a previously announced share-for-property swap transaction between Ayala Land and AREIT. AREIT will acquire P21bn worth of assets from Ayala Land in exchange for 505.9mn new AREIT shares. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 18% and its shares outstanding by 16%. The mall, office, and hotel assets are located in Cebu, Davao, and Cagayan de Oro.

Ayala inks partnership with Spinneys for Philippine stores

Ayala Corp (AC PM) signed an agreement with Spinneys (SPINNEYS UH) for the operation of its premium fresh food supermarkets. AC will own 60% of the partnership while Spinneys will hold 40%. Spinneys will initially spearhead the operations prior to handing day-to-day management to the new entity. Spinneys is a UAE-based premium grocer which has 80 stores in UAE, Oman, and Saudi Arabia with this partnership marking its first venture outside the Gulf area. Sunil Kumar, CEO of Spinneys, highlighted the Philippines' long-term growth potential, strong economic fundamentals, and growing affluent population as key factors driving the company’s expansion beyond the Gulf region. The group intends to open its first store by 4Q next year (2026). The deal is indicative of Ayala Corp's continued retail push as it follows the recent deal with CP Axtra to bring warehouse club Makro to the country. Another notable recent entry into retail for AC was Anko, where it holds a 50% stake.

ACMobility exits Honda dealerships

Ayala Corp. announced the transfer of ACMobility’s operations of Honda dealerships to “new dealer principals” by Jan 1, 2026. Transition aims to streamline portfolio and focus on new growth areas in the electric vehicle space. ACMobility, through Iconic Dealership Inc., operates nine (9) Honda dealerships in the country. AC first partnered with Honda in 1990. We understand that AC anticipates to book asset write-offs and employee separation costs but the final amount will be determined before year-end. This move follows recent exits from the Volkswagen and Maxus distribution business. By Jan 1, ACMobility will only carry the BYD, Kia, and Isuzu automobile brands.

Ayala-led IMI consolidates manufacturing operations in China

Integrated Micro-Electronics, Inc. (IMI) has completed the consolidation of its Kuichong operations into its Pingshan facility in Shenzhen, China. Move expected to streamline its presence in the country, improve efficiency, and increase capacity utilization.

Manila Water completes acquisition of Wawa bulk water project

Manila Water said the closing conditions of the former’s acquisition of WawaJVCo from Prime Infrastructure have been met on September 30. WawaJVCo is the owner of the soon-to-be 712MLD bulk water supply facility in Rizal, which has a 518MLD, 30-year take-or-pay agreement with the MWC’s East zone concession. Recall that MWC committed to acquire full ownership of WawaJVco for P37.8bn, to be settled in two tranches: P6.3bn will be paid by Dec 15. The balance will be paid by Jan 30, 2026, subject to adjustments to balance sheet items.

MREIT aims to double its portfolio by 2027

MREIT is targeting to double its portfolio to 1mn sqm by 2027, from 480,000sqm of offices currently. It intends to acquire mall assets in order to achieve its 2027 target. MREIT currently offers 7.5% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast).

RLC’s new office building is over 50% leased

Robinsons Land said that its new 60,000sqm office building in Bridgetowne, GBF Center 2, is over 50% leased. GBF Center 2 was opened on September 29. Although Metro Manila office occupancy is only 82%, some tenants have been preleasing uncompleted buildings in order to be in their preferred locations. In 2Q25, RLC’s 793,000sqm office portfolio was 87% occupied.

ePLDT launches first locally hosted AI platform -- Pilipinas AI

ePLDT, Inc., ICT service provider and data center subsidiary of PLDT (TEL PM), has launched Pilipinas AI, which is the country's first "sovereign" artificial intelligence (AI) platform that allows enterprises to build and deploy AI models within the country using locally hosted infrastructure and data. The platform was developed with Dell Technologies and Katonic AI, hosted at ePLDT’s 50-MW VITRO Sta. Rosa data center in Laguna. This expands ePLDT's GPU-as-a-Service offering, an on-demand computing model which gives businesses access to advanced graphics processing units (GPUs) for AI applications. ePLDT President and CEO Victor Genuino stated that Pilipinas AI will provide enterprises access to computing power and AI tools without heavy infrastructure investment. He stated that the AI platform is expected to support multiple industries such as banking, BPO, healthcare, public services, and academia with potential use cases such as fraud detection, weather forecasting, customer service, medical diagnostics.

Maynilad says IFC, ADB may subscribe to IPO shares at up to P15/sh

International Finance Corp. (IFC) and the Asian Development Bank (ADB) plan to invest up to US$245mn in Maynilad Water’s IPO. IFC agreed to subscribe to US$100mn worth of shares in Maynilad at a subscription price of up to P15/sh. ADB is also considering participating as a cornerstone investor for up to US$145mn or up to P15/sh. According to Maynilad’s latest prospectus filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission. We estimate IFC and ADB could end up owning ~5% and 7%, respectively, of Maynilad. ADB is in the process of obtaining approval from its Board of Directors. At P15/sh and assuming full subscription of the IPO shares (1.9bn primary and 355mn secondary), Maynilad’s market capitalization could reach P113bn, with a public float of 30%. The offer period for the IPO is scheduled from Oct. 23 to 31, 2025, with the listing date set for Nov. 7, 2025.

MGEN eyes backdoor listing of RE unit, MGreen, next year

Meralco PowerGen (MGEN) is studying the possibility of listing its renewable energy unit, MGen Renewable Energy Inc. (MGreen) via SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM) next year, according to MGEN president and CEO, Emmanuel Rubio. MGEN will end up owning 60.86% of SPNEC once both (P5.0bn, P5.8bn) exchangeable notes are exchanged into common equity shares (vs. 35.7% today). Mr. Rubio said that it is currently in the process of acquiring land for Terra Solar 2, which would require 2,200 hectares of land for a 1,500 MW of capacity. The 3,500 MW MTerra Solar project is 54% complete as of end-June (Phase 1 of 2,500 MW is expected to be operational by 1Q2026 and the balance will be operational by 1Q2027).

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP expects higher inflation in September

The BSP projects September 2025 inflation to settle within the range of 1.5% to 2.3%. Cited higher prices of rice and fish, elevated domestic fuel costs. Bloomberg consensus expects September inflation to tick up to 1.9% from 1.5% in August. For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect full-year inflation to average 1.8%. The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release September CPI on October 7.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in Aug

The Aug trade deficit narrowed to US$3.5bn, contracting 19% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports alongside the growth of exports. This brings the 8M25 trade deficit to US$32bn, 6% narrower YoY. Imports for the month fell by 5% YoY to US$11.4bn, a reversal from the 2% increase in July. Primarily driven by the significant decrease in fuel imports by 34%. Exports grew by 5% YoY to US$7.1bn, the eighth consecutive month of growth and moderating from the 17% increase in July. Electronics exports climbed by 9% YoY to $3.9bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were flat % at US$3.2bn.

Government debt inches down to P17.47trn in end-Aug

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.5% MoM to P17.47trn as of end-Aug. Domestic debt decreased by 0.5% MoM to P12.1trn. External debt fell by 1.4% MoM to P5.4trn. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.2% and 30.8% being external borrowings. The P17.47trn figure already exceeds the P17.36-trn full-year projection for 2025. Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury said that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down". The rising debt figure was driven by sustained borrowing needs given the 25% wider YoY fiscal deficit of P869bn as of 8M25. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 1H25 stood at 63.1% of GDP up from 60.7% in 4Q24. The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028.

BSP raises BOP/current account deficit forecasts

The BSP made upward adjustments to its current account deficit forecasts, largely to reflect lower tourism receipts. 2025/2026 current account deficits revised to US$16.4bn/US$15.5bn from US$16.3bn/US$13.6bn. It made modest downward revisions to 25E/26E trade deficit forecasts to US$69.6bn/US$70.2bn from US$70.5bn/US$71.9bn. It maintained BPO revenue and OFW remittances forecasts at US$33.5bn and US$35.5bn, respectively. 25E/26E Tourism receipts were downgraded by 12%/19% to US$9.4bn/US$9.7bn. It raised its BOP deficit targets to US$6.9bn/US$3.4bn in 2025/2026 due to higher current account deficit forecasts and restrained capital inflows.

Current consumer confidence and next 12 months outlook improves -- BSP survey

The latest Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) results exhibited that current sentiment or the overall consumer index (CI) was less downbeat at -9.8% in 3Q25 from -14.0% the previous quarter. A negative CI implies more respondents are pessimistic than optimistic. Less pessimistic sentiment in 3Q stemmed from expectations of new income sources, higher income, better employment conditions and/or opportunities. For the next quarter, consumer sentiment jumped to 6.9% from 0.6% the previous quarter. For the upcoming 12 months, consumers expect economic prospects to improve, with a positive CI at 14.1%, up from 11.8% the previous quarter. The positive consumer outlook can be attributed to the expected improvement of wages, employment, and price stability. The CES results are taken into account in the BSP's decision-making for monetary policy. The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%.

Gov't extends rice import ban for 30 days

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) stated that the current two-month rice import ban was extended for at least 30 days till end-Nov. Recall that a 60-day suspension of rice imports from Sep 1 to Oct 30 was approved by the President to boost farmgate prices given the current peak harvest season of palay (unmilled rice). DA Secretary Tiu Laurel noted that palay farmgate prices have dropped back to P8-10/kg after having previously improved to P13-17/kg. A significant number of rice farmers remain unprofitable given the P14/kg production cost of palay and difficulties in drying their harvest amid heavy rains. Tiu Laurel guided that the ban could last until end of the year depending on market conditions. He also stated that a floor price for palay and a higher rice import tariff may be implemented soon or by the end of the import ban. The original duty on imported rice was 35% but reduced to 15% after EO No. 62 in Jul 2024 to reduce retail prices of rice.

Tax hike on sugar-sweetened beverages filed by lawmakers

House Bill (HB) 5003 seeks to amend the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) law by significantly raising the excise tax on sugar sweetened beverage (SSBs) in an effort to protect the health of Filipinos given rising obesity rates. The excise tax on drinks (e.g., soda, sweetened juice) using caloric or noncaloric sweeteners will be raised to P20 per liter from the current P6 and to P40 per liter from P12 for beverages containing high fructose corn syrup. A P6 per liter tax on flavored milk and non-dairy beverages as well as all sweetened coffee products was also proposed. The Excise tax rates will be hiked 6% annually starting in 2026. White plain milk, infant formula, roasted ground coffee, beverages sweetened with coconut sap sugar or steviol glycosides remain exempt. The proposal was estimated to raise about P55bn annually with 40% of proceeds to be allocated to PhilHealth, 10% to the Dept. of Health, and 50% to the Dept. of Interior and Local Gov't. Rep. Leila de Lima, principal author alongside co-authors Rep. Cielo Lagman and Rep. Arlene Bag-Ao is pitching the bill as a public health initiative rather than an added levy on consumption.

Accenture cuts 22,000 jobs globally in six months

During its quarterly earnings call, Accenture reported that it had 779,273 employees globally as of August 2025. May 2025 – 790,692. February 2025 – 801,099. August 2024 – 774,303. Accenture CFO Angie Park said that the reduction in headcount was due to “talent rotation” related to its AI push as well as the divestiture of two acquisitions “that are no longer aligned with our strategic priorities”. The company expects headcount to grow in 2026. In March 2025, Accenture reported that it lost contracts with the US government. Accenture is one of the largest BPO firms in the Philippines.

Draft amendments on REIT regulations to be released in October

SEC Commissioner McJill Bryant Fernandez told the Philippine Daily Inquirer that the SEC was working on amendments to the REIT law’s provision on tax income deductibility. Commissioner Fernandez did not elaborate further. The draft regulations will be released for public comments in October. REITs are currently required to pay out at least 90% of their distributable income as dividends. REITs only need to pay income tax on the portion of distributable income that was not paid out as dividends. SEC Chair Francis Lim previously said that the changes being considered included: Allowing funds raised from REIT issuances to be reinvested within three years (from one year currently). Allowing REIT proceeds to be used for debt repayment.

44 priority bills to be pushed in the 20th Congress

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and Congress leaders in a Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting identified 44 priority bills under the Common Legislative Agenda of the 20th Congress. The bills are said to be focused on improving the investment climate, modernizing governance as well as the agri sector, expanding social services, and ensuring energy security. Some key measures in the list are as follows: Proposed amendments to the Bank Deposits Secrecy Law and Anti-Money Laundering Act. A progressive budgeting bill and proposals which require civil servants to waive bank secrecy protections. Masterplan for Infrastructure and National Development. Amendments to a number of social programs such as the 4Ps, Universal Health Care Act, gov't assistance to students and teachers, free college education, among others. Digital Payments Act and legislation on online gambling which seek to secure online transactions and ensure responsible use of digital platforms. National Land Use Act which seeks to create a national level framework for land allocation to enforce sustainable land use to prevent environmental degradation, overdevelopment, conversion of farmland to non-agricultural uses, etc. EPIRA Amendments which seeks to strengthen the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC). The establishment of a Dept. of Water Resources.

Commercial banks’ loan growth 11.2% in Aug; M3 growth at 6.6%

Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.2% YoY in Aug, moderating from the 11.8% growth in July. This marks the slowest growth rate in nine months since Nov 2024's +11.1%. Loans to productive activities slowed to 9.9% YoY, slowing down from the 10.8% in July due to decreased lending in key industries such as Elec, gas, steam (+28.1% from +30.3%), trade (+8.1% from +8.5%), and finance, insurance (+6.9% from +13.1%). Loans to manufacturers declined for the fourth consecutive month, -5.5% in Aug from a -6.3% in July. Consumer loans grew 23.9%, inching up from the 23.6% pace in July. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up to 6.6% in Aug, accelerating from the 6.2% growth in July.

Chinese firm submits lowest bid for land segment of Bataan-Cavite bridge

China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC) offered to build the land segment (i.e. CP1) of the Bataan-Cavite Interlink bridge (BCIB) for P4.87bn. CHEC submitted the lowest bids among the eight (8) qualified bidders, including the construction arm of DMCI (DMC PM, P7.8bn) and EEI (EEI PM, P7.19bn). CP1 involves the construction of the 5-km Bataan Land Approach, covering 6.89km of ramps. The DPWH said all financial proposals are still subject for further evaluation. The US$3.9bn BCIB project will be divided into seven contract packages, with the construction to begin in the two on-land packages. The BCIB project will span 32km and will provide a permanent road link between the provinces of Bataan and Cavite. Once the bridge is completed, travel time between the provinces of Bataan and Cavite will be reduced from 5 1⁄2 hours to about 45 minutes. In 2023, the Asian Development Bank (ADB), which is co-financing the project, approved a US$2.11-billion loan for the project.

President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating improves in June SWS survey

President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating improved by 20 points to +10 in June from the -10 rating in April. His gross satisfaction rating improved to 46% in June from 38%. Gross dissatisfaction declined to 36% from April’s 48%. Gross undecided rose to 19% from 14% in April. SWS noted the increase in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas: Rating jumped by 35 points in Mindanao (from “bad” -44 to “neutral” -9). +21 points in Balance Luzon (from “neutral” +7 to “good” +28). +9 points in the Visayas (from “poor” -11 to “neutral” -2), and +7 points in Metro Manila (from “neutral” -6 to “neutral” +1). Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,200 adults from June 25-29, 2025.
