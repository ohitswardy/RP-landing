REGIS REPORT

19 December 2025

Philippine equities ended the week under pressure, with the PCOMP retreating as global risk-off sentiment, heavy foreign selling, and FTSE rebal-related flows overwhelmed intermittent local support. While the market started the week on firmer footing, gains proved difficult to sustain as profit-taking and external headwinds drove the index lower into the latter part of the week. Overall price action was volatile, with the index repeatedly closing near intraday lows, reflecting fragile sentiment and crowded positioning.

 

With Friday's sharp 1.8% sell-down taking the index back below the 6,000 handle, the PCOMP finished the week lower by 1.9% or 115.85 pts WoW to 5,920.87.

 

Banks stood out with the Financials sector index bucking broad market weakness, up 1.6%, lifted by CBC (+9.6% WoW) and MBT (+2.1% WoW) while BPI (+0.7% WoW) and BDO (+0.6% WoW) also managed to end higher for the week. This was after BSP Gov. Remolona early in the week expressed openness to further cuts to the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) down to 2.0% in 2026 from currently 5.0%, which reinforced expectations of a more accommodative policy backdrop as we enter the new year. Regis estimates that a 300-bp reduction in the RRR could lift big banks' profits by c.5%.

 

Meanwhile, dragging the index was ICT (-9.9% WoW) which continued to succumb to profit-taking from its record highs. YTD though, it remains the PCOMP's top gainer, up 42.4%. Another big YTD outperformer, MER (-2.6% WoW) was likewise among the larger drags to the index for the week. Top developers SMPH (-2.4% WoW) and ALI (-1.8% WoW) were also big negative movers for the PCOMP. Shares were actually strong to start the week despite news that 4Q residential take-up fell QoQ and YoY, but both counters succumbed to heavy selling in the latter half of the week.

 

Foreigners remained net sellers for the fourth consecutive week, by some $33m. Peso appreciated by 0.6% to 58.732:$1.

RESEARCH
Philippine Banks/Finance - SEC sets 12% monthly rate cap for NBFCs; BSP unlikely to follow for banks
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

EIR capped at 12%/month, for loans up to P10,000

The SEC on 10 Dec issued a circular capping the effective interest rate (EIR) charged by non-bank finance cos (NBFCs) at 12% a month (6% headline interest plus all fees), covering loans up to P10,000 (c$170) and with tenor of up to 4 months. This is considerably less stringent than the initial proposal of 10% EIR cap for loans up to 6mos / P20,000 we had reported earlier this month. We expect the impact on the major NBFCs to be manageable, given the relatively limited scope of loans covered. However, we maintain that rate caps are a crude tool for “consumer protection”, and instead look forward to further improvements in the national credit bureau and National ID systems. We do not expect the BSP to impose new rate/fee caps on banks’ products and services.

 

New SEC rate cap seems high, but much lower than some (legal) lenders’ rates

The SEC exercised its authority under the Financial Product and Services Consumer Protection Act to impose limits on EIR, fees, late payment penalties, etc in response to numerous complaints of high rates, hidden fees, and abusive collection practices. Indeed, a casual search revealed that many online lenders (of which there are 111 registered in total) charge EIRs or APRs far in excess of 12% a month, with at least one charging as much as 1.99% per day, or 726% per annum. That said, a good number fell within the 12-15%/month range.

Could push borrowers to illegal/informal lenders

We grant that 12%/month is still high – and 726% pa is egregious. However, as we pointed out in our 02 Dec report, credit costs are extremely high as well, averaging in the 20-30% range. Needless to say, some cohorts will have higher default rates. In theory, NBFCs can skirt the cap by lending either a) more than P10,000 or b) longer than 4 months. However, some of the loan products are much smaller ticket, and for much shorter periods (14 days) just to tide borrowers over. And without a fully functioning National ID and national credit bureau, fincos need higher yields to compensate for the risks. Capping EIRs will likely force NBFCs / fintechs to limit their risk appetites for new customers, which in turn could push borrowers to the illegal, unregulated lenders.

 

BSP unlikely to cap bank rates – an arbitrage opportunity?

This marks the first time the SEC sets interest rates independent of the BSP – though we understand they were consulted. The BSP has historically been averse to rate/fee setting, and we believe it unlikely that they will impose similar caps. If so, this could open up arbitrage opportunities for digibanks / rural banks / neo-bank. It could also encourage larger fintechs to acquire banking licenses.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land sells stake in Alabang Town Center mall

Ayala Land signed a share purchase agreement for the sale of its 50% stake in Alabang Town Center mall to its JV partner. ALI received an unsolicited offer from its JV partner, the Madrigal Group. According to Bilyonaryo.com, the Madrigal Group is in talks with Rockwell Land (ROCK PM) to redevelop and operate Alabang Town Center. The transaction values the 50% stake at P13.5bn. We currently value Ayala Land’s 50% stake in Alabang Town Center at P5bn. Ayala Land said that proceeds from the sale will fuel the growth of its leasing portfolio. ALI President Meean Dy said that the sale reflects Ayala Land’s strategy of developing assets, stabilizing operations, and monetizing them at optimal valuations to boost shareholder returns. We estimate that (100% of) Alabang Town Center currently comprises about 5% of Ayala Land’s mall GLA and revenue. The sale of Ayala Land’s stake could raise our ALI NAV estimate by c. 1%.

ICTSI investing US$175mn to boost Rio port’s capacity by 70%

ICTSI is investing a total of BRL948mn (US$175mn) to raise the capacity of its port in Rio, Brazil by 70% to 750k TEUs by 2029. Our estimates and valuation already assume this expansion. We estimate the port in Rio, Brazil accounts for 3% of EBITDA and 7% of NAV.

ICTSI expands Croatia port

ICTSI unveiled new super post-panamax quay cranes at its port in Croatia, Adriatic Gate Container Terminal (AGCT). New cranes will allow it to service large container vessels but will not add to port’s capacity of 600k TEUs. We estimate AGCT accounts for 1% of group revenues and EBITDA. Note that APM Terminals, a subsiariary of Maersk, opened its port in Croatia this month. Prior to APM’s entry, AGCT was one of three ports in Rijeka, Croatia and had a market share of 18%.

Energy Secretary says decision on Semirara's coal contract extension by 1Q26?

The Department of Energy is awaiting the Department of Justice’s opinion before deciding whether to amend Semirara’s contract or open it for bidding, according to Energy Secretary Sharon Garin. “I’m hoping (for the decision) within this year, but probably that will be in the first quarter of next year,”. Recall that Semirara’s coal operating contract is set to expire on July 2027. The outcome is binary; We estimate a 15-year extension puts SCC’s valuation at P37/sh while a no extension scenario puts its valuation at P21/sh (vs. current share price of P28.10/sh). In the event of a non-extension of its coal operating contract, Semirara will retain ownership of all its mining equipment, port facilities, and other infrastructure in the island.

Jollibee on track to hit year-end targets

Jollibee (JFC PM) reported “encouraging developments” in its operating performance, noting that preliminary indicators point to sustained momentum in fourth quarter sales. JFC guided for 8-12% systemwide sales growth in 2025, on the back of 4-6% growth in SSS and 4-8% store network growth.

Lucio Co's Crystal Bridges acquires Villar’s PrimeWater

The Villar group said Crystal Bridges Holding Corporation of the Lucio Co Group has entered into definitive agreements for the acquisition of 100% of PrimeWater Infrastructure Corp. PrimeWater did not disclose the valuation for the transaction. PrimeWater serves over 1.7mn households and supplies about 500MLD across more than 100 partnered water districts nationwide. Its operations span multiple provinces, including Bulacan, Batangas, and Laguna. Recall that state-run Local Water Utilities Administration is investigating PrimeWater’s 73 joint venture agreements for service failures. Crystal Bridges Holdings Corp. does not seem to be part of any of Lucio Co’s listed units – Cosco Holdings (COSCO PM), Puregold (PGOLD PM), The Keepers Hldgs (KEEPR PM). Crystal Bridges is owned by Co’s children, with Vincent Co as chairman and president, according to the company’s 2025 general information sheet.

MREIT to acquire office assets from Megaworld

MREIT will acquire office assets from its sponsor, Megaworld, in a share-for-property swap. MREIT will issue 997mn new shares to Megaworld in exchange for 165,000sqm of offices in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio and P187.5mn in cash. The shares will be issued at P16.27/share. This represents a 16% premium to the last closing price and a 15% premium to the 30-day VWAP. MREIT’s portfolio will grow by 34% 645,000sqm. MREIT’s shares outstanding will expand by 27%. Megaworld’s ownership in MREIT will increase from 56.6% to 65.8%.

Federal Land divests stake in Crown Central Properties

GT Capital's (GTCAP) property arm Federal Land is selling its entire 52% stake in Crown Central Properties Corp. for P73.5mn. Stake will be sold to Crown Equities, Inc. Crown Central Properties develops residential and commercial projects.

Visa eyes to expand to 1,000 7-11 convenience stores by year-end and to 3,000 stores by 1Q26

Visa Philippines said that it is now available as a payment option in 700 7-11 branches from only 11 branches in November, according to Country Manager Jeffrey Navarro. It aims to be available to 1,000 stores by year-end and to 3,000 stores by 1Q26. This is part of Philippine Seven’s (SEVN PM) initiative to expand non-cash payments. At the start of the year, only a handful of stores accepted credit cards. As of end-September, SEVN had 4,366 stores nationwide.

Cebu Air pax volumes drop 9% YoY in November; 11M25 pax volumes up 10%

Cebu Air flew 2.14mn passengers in November, down 9% YoY. The decline was underpinned by a 7% YoY reduction in seat capacity and the impact of severe weather events. Moderating capacity growth during the lean season was intended to ensure operational resilience ahead of the peak travel season (Dec/Jan). This brings 11M25 pax volumes up 10% YoY to 24mn, lagging our full-year forecast of 28.5mn (+16%). During its 3Q briefing, management indicated that stronger fares would help offset softer volumes and hinted at the potential resumption of common-share dividend payments by March/April.

PAGCOR revokes provisional gaming license of PH Resorts in Cebu

PAGCOR revoked the provisional gaming license for the Cebu casino project of PH Resorts (PHR PM). PHR previously intended to build and operate a US$300mn integrated resort project in Cebu. Earlier this year, China Bank (CBC PM) foreclosed the 12-hectare land parcel on which the casino was supposed to be built. PAGCOR also revoked the provisional gaming license for PHR’s Clark casino in 2024.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Current account deficit drops by 39% YoY in 3Q25; YTD CA deficit lower by 6% YoY to US$12.5bn

The current account deficit plunged 39% YoY, 37% QoQ to US$3.2bn (equivalent to 2.8% of GDP). This was driven by a narrower trade-in-goods deficit, higher net receipts from trade in services and sustained modest increase in remittances. The trade deficit declined by 5% YoY to US$17.5bn due to strength in electronic exports (+32%) and mineral exports (+73%). Total estimated BPO export revenues, including computer and other business services, reached US$9.1bn in 3Q25, a 7.8% increase from US$8.4bn in 3Q24. This brings 9M25 current account to a narrower (-6% YoY) deficit of US$12.5bn, equivalent to 3.6% of GDP. This appears broadly in line with the BSP’s year-end CA deficit target of US$16bn (3.3% of GDP). The improvement is due to (a) higher electronic exports (+7%) and mineral exports (+52%).

Bicam approves 2026 P6.79tn budget; Presidential sign off by Dec 29

The bicameral conference committee reconciled provisions of the P6.79tn (+7% YoY) General Appropriations Bill. The Department of Public Works secured a budget of P530bn, 40% (~P350bn) lower than its initial proposal of P881bn and 49% YoY lower than its 2025 budget due to removal of locally funded flood control projects. The reduction in public works was re-allocated to education, agriculture, healthcare (including PhilHealth, which received an additional P130bn), and social spending. Unprogrammed funds, intended for use if excess revenues or new collections arise, now total P243bn, including increased incentives for vehicle manufacturing to P4.3bn from P333mn. Recall that GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) Toyota Motor Phils (TMP) still has P4.5bn worth of un-credited tax incentives under the CARS program, equivalent to ~30% of TMP’s FY26E profit. The bicam report is expected to be signed on Dec. 28, ratified by both chambers on Dec. 29, and signed into law by the President on the same day.

Remittances rise by 3% in October

Cash remittances amounted US$3.17bn in October, 3% higher YoY. This brings 10M25 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$29.2bn. Weak remittance growth from North America (e.g. USA: -0.2%) has been partially offset by stronger contributions from the rest of the world.

Potential RRR cut to 2.0% in 2026?

Bloomberg reported that BSP Governor Eli Remolona said he would be “happy” to see the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) on banks’ PHP deposits down to 2.0% in 2026. Gov Remolona has consistently advocated for a move to zero RRR, at least eventually, citing the inefficiency of RRR in fine-tuning monetary policy. The BSP, too, has long shared this view. Following its new charter in 2018 – which gave it a broader set of monetary tools – the BSP steadily reduced the RRR from as high as 21.0% in 2005, to 5.0% in its most recent cut in March 2025.

LPC: 4Q25 residential take-up falls YoY and QoQ

Leechiu Property Consultants reported that condominium take-up in Metro Manila fell 4% to 24,732 units in 2025. This implies that 4Q25 take-up fell 50% QoQ and 12% YoY to 3,868 units. Unsold condominium units in Metro Manila inched up 1% QoQ to 80,300 units. We estimate that unsold inventory represents 39 months of take-up (vs 38 months in 3Q25).

LPC: 4Q25 net office take-up rises YoY and QoQ

Leechiu Property Consultants reported Metro Manila office vacancy was flat QoQ and YoY at 18%. Nationwide net office take-up increased to 169,000sqm in 4Q25, from net take-up of 21,000sqm in 3Q25 (net incremental vacancy of 100,000sqm in 4Q24). BPO net office take-up expanded to 85,000sqm, from net take-up of 6,000sqm in 3Q25 (1,000sqm net take-up in 4Q24). Net take-up from traditional tenants hit 84,000sqm, from 23,000sqm in 3Q25 (down from 98,000sqm in 4Q24).

Metro Manila water utilities to implement fourth tranche of tariff hike in January

The water regulator, MWSS, approved the tariff increases of +7.64% for Manila Water (MWC PM) and +2.85% for Maynilad (MYNLD PM) beginning January 1, 2026. These adjustments cover the (a) fourth tranche of the approved “R” tariff, which assumes the construction of a new water source, Kaliwa dam, as well as (b) CPI adjustment (+0.68%). MWSS also approved the increase in MWC’s environmental surcharge to 30% from 25%.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 9% YoY in November; Toyota sales up 5%

CAMPI reported industry car sales fell 8.7% YoY (-6.7% MoM) to 37,352 units in November. CAMPI does not capture sales of BYD. This brings 11M25 sales to 420,776 units, down 1% YoY. Assuming BYD’s market share stood at 5%, we estimate vehicle sales are up ~4-5% YTD. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 20,351 units, up 5.3% YoY (flat MoM). This brings TMP’s 11M25 sales up 3.9% YoY to 205,552 units and lagging our full-year forecast of 231k units (+6%). TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV and 35% of profit. GTCAP is trading at a 49% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,123/sh and 4x PER.

PAGCOR to privatize Casino Filipino in late 2026 or early 2027

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco is targeting to privatize the PAGCOR-operated casinos by late 2026 or early 2026. He hopes to raise P30bn-P50bn from the sale. PAGCOR-operated casinos generated P3.2bn of GGR in 3Q25. They generated P13.2bn in GGR from 4Q24 to 3Q25.

DPWH seeks charges vs Romualdez, 86 others in flood control scandal

The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) has recommended the filing of criminal charges against 87 people over irregularities in flood control projects. The recommendations include charges of plunder, malversation, graft, and bribery. The list includes Leyte Rep. and former House Speaker Martin G. Romualdez and resigned Party-list Rep. Elizady S. Co.
