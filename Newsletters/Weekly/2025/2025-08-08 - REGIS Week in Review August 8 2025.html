REGIS REPORT

08 August 2025

The PCOMP ended a busy week of results with a moderate gain of +0.5% at 6339, partially recovering from last week’s slump, as local shares piggybacked the sharp rally across the region which was primarily driven renewed bets of rate cuts by the FOMC following a disappointing jobs report that included substantial downward revisions for the 2 prior months over the weekend. Futures are now again pricing in at least two 25bp cuts by the Fed by the end of the year with a few pundits even raising the possibility of a 50bp cut in the upcoming mid-September meeting. And this dovish backdrop, the peso swiftly reversed its sharp losses from last week, strengthening by nearly +1.9% to 57.1.

On the macro front, data showed that CPI further eased to 0.9% in July, lower than 1.1% forecast, which bolstered bets of more rate cuts at home. 2Q GDP meanwhile came in at 5.5%, near the 5.4% consensus forecast and 5.4% pace in 1Q.

The pace of gains in local shares however visibly lagged the rest of the region as the busy round of corporate results this week painted a mixed picture. With half of index components having reported so far, our tally of 2Q/1H results shows that preliminary 2Q earnings growth looks soft at only +2%, while the 1H is at +4%.

Best gainer in the benchmark this week was ALI +8.5%, notwithstanding slightly softer than expected results, as rotation into the property sector at large persisted after 2Q results from developers almost unanimously showed sequential improvement in residential sales, which further bolstered views that the real estate market is probably past its worst point. Other property stocks outperformed the broader market as well with SMPH +3% and non-PCOMP members RLC +3% and MEG +4.6%. Meanwhile in the REIT space, RCR +5.4% after the announcement of its inclusion in the MSCI small cap benchmark in the upcoming rebalancing later this August.

Another notable outperformer this week was index heavyweight ICT +7.3% after it reported another positive set of results, notwithstanding all the noise related to tariffs. In contrast, index heavyweight SM plummeted -4.7%, notwithstanding its positive results as well – where SM Retail in particular looked good, growing topline and SSSG by nearly 7%. Note that SM has generally been on a downward trend over the past two weeks, potentially as some of the subscribers from the sizable private placement in mid-July are now starting to reduce holdings. Rest of the conglo sector meanwhile looked mixed with GTCAP +5.7% amid the recovery in peso, and JGS +4.1%, while in contrast SMC -7.1%, AEV -6.5% and AGI -5.8%. With the diverging share price direction this week, note ICT has now slightly surpassed SM in terms of market cap: P985b vs P976b.

Market activity was virtually same as last week at a daily average of $109m. As opposed to last week however, most actively traded stock in the market this time was ICT – following a month of PLUS -1.5% being the most actively traded stock on the board everyday. Along this came an improvement in net foreign participation as well to 44% from 41% last week. That said, note that after close the PSE finally announced changes for the mid-August rebalancing: PLUS included, BLOOM removed.

Meanwhile, net foreign flow this week was essentially neutral as inflows into ICT +7.3% were offset by consistent outflows on BDO (unchanged) - which has consistently been among the top foreign sold stocks in the market since reporting worse-than-expected results nearly two weeks ago.

ICTSI - Solid growth in 2Q25 despite deceleration
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H25 profit of US$476mn (+22%) - Ahead of Street expectations

Headline 1H25 net income rose 15% to US$483mn. Excluding last year's one-offs, recurring profit grew 20% YoY, on the back of a 14% revenue improvement to US$1.51bn. While revenues are broadly in line with our estimates, 1H EBITDA of US$991mn (+15%) disappointed due to higher-than-expected cash costs. However, the results are trending above Street estimates.

Short-lived disruptions slightly dampen revenue growth to 12% in 2Q25

Volumes grew 9% in 2Q25, a tad slower than the 12% pace in 1Q, following prolonged labor strikes at its ports in Mexico and DRC. These disruptions, however, did not materially boost storage revenues, with yield per TEU up by 3% in 2Q25, mildly slower than the 4% rate in 1Q25. As operations normalize at these ports in 3Q25 and a new berth in Mexico comes online in September, it is highly plausible for growth to re-accelerate in 2H.

EBITDA margin dips 20bps QoQ to 65.5% in 2Q25

EBITDA margin dipped 20bps to 65.5% in 2Q25, after manpower costs jumped following the conclusion of CBA negotiations in Manila. As a result, EBITDA rose 11% to US$501mn in 2Q25. Management indicated that the acquisition of a property in Rio, Brazil in April should generate cost savings from 3Q25 onwards, given that it previously leased the said property at higher rates than its tariff.

Indonesia port likely to add ~4% to volumes and 1% to revenues

ICT secured a 30-year license to run Batu Ampar Container Terminal (BACT) in Batam island in Indonesia, which is a special economic zone and where cargo throughput is rising at a multiple of GDP growth. ICT will have a 75% interest in BACT, and the upfront cost will be US$85mn, which reflects the low yield environment in the country. We estimate the port should add 4% to consolidated volumes and 1% to revenues upon takeover sometime in 4Q.

Awaiting judge’s decision on Durban container terminal

Hearings were concluded in March and management expects the judge to render a decision on the case before the end of 3Q.

 
SM Prime - 2Q25 reservation sales improve sequentially
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H25 net profit grows 11%, ahead

SM Prime’s 1H25 net profit grew 11% to P24.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. While revenue growth was tempered (at 5%), margins improved due to the company’s cost control efforts. The Philippine mall segment remained healthy, while revenue of the office, residential, and China mall businesses grew at slower paces.

Residential reservation sales improve sequentially

2Q25 reservation sales grew 23% QoQ (-19% YoY) to P14.3bn. While this was partly due to stronger sales of provincial projects, the company also sold more ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units in Metro Manila. It appears that buyers have been enticed by rent-to-own schemes and cash discounts on RFO units. Management said that sales remained healthy in July.

High-end residential projects to be launched

SM Prime recently unveiled its high-end residential brand, Signature. The company plans to launch a lot-only project in Muntinlupa, Metro Manila by the end of 2025 and a condominium project in Makati in 2027.

Philippine malls remain healthy

Operating results for the Philippine malls remained good in 2Q25, with revenue growing 8% YoY, despite Holy Week falling in April this year (vs. March in 2024). We estimate that 2Q25/1H25 same-mall revenue growth was c. 6%, which is within the historical range (of 4%-8%).

Presidential Proclamation for reclamation project likely to be delayed

The Presidential Proclamation relating to SMPH’s reclamation project, which is a prerequisite for preselling, could be delayed further. The new Environment Secretary was only appointed in May and will likely need more time to review the project. However, SMPH believes it can still presell commercial lots next year.

 
Ayala Land - 2Q25 reservation sales improve sequentially, thanks to "core" rebound
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H25 earnings rise 8%

Ayala Land’s 1H25 net profit rose 8% to P14.2bn. While 2H is seasonally stronger, we would still consider this slightly behind REGIS and consensus full-year forecasts. 2Q25 development revenue was weak, with both residential and commercial lot revenue falling on a YoY and QoQ basis. However, management

said that it expects stronger revenue recognition in the coming periods.

Reservation sales of core projects rebound

2Q25 residential reservation sales rose 11% QoQ (-2% YoY) to P34.5bn, thanks to the 39% QoQ (+11% YoY) rebound in sales of core (mid-income) projects. Management said that promotions on ready-for-occupancy units in Metro Manila, such as 30% discounts, spurred buyers and sales remain strong in July. We estimate that the company has about P20bn of unsold RFO units, most of which are in the core segment.

Sales of premium projects decline

In contrast to the core segment, 2Q25 sales of premium projects fell 4% QoQ and 9% YoY (to P19.9bn). The decline largely came from provincial lot-only projects, which the company attributes to the lack of launches in 1H25. Furthermore, Laurean, a P28bn project in Makati, was only launched in June and only registered in P6bn in sales in 2Q. Management said that Laurean and new provincial lot-only launches should contribute to 2H25 sales.

Reservation sales guidance unlikely to be met

We believe Ayala Land is unlikely to meet its presales growth target of 12% for the full year (which includes commercial lots). Although 1H25 commercial lot sales grew 18% (to P8bn), residential presales fell 4% (to P65.7bn), bringing total 1H25 presales down 2% to P73.7bn.

Mall redevelopments to finish sooner

Mall revenue grew 5% YoY (in 2Q and 1H) despite the redevelopment of ALI’s four flagship malls. Excluding the malls under renovation, 1H25 revenue rose 9%. The company plans to complete two of the redevelopments in 2H25 and the other two by 1H26, six months ahead of the original plan. The early indications from some the renovated spaces are promising, with rental rates rising more than the 20% that the company expected.

Office occupancy improves 1ppt QoQ

Office revenue rose 1% QoQ (+5% YoY) to P3.0bn, as occupancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 91%. This is much higher than the industry average of 82%.

1H25 hotel revenue stays flat despite closures

1H25 hotel revenue was flat despite the closure of four hotels (under renovation), which comprised c. 20% of rooms. Excluding these hotels, revenue rose 14%.

 
Megaworld - 2Q25: All segments outperform
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Revenue of every segment exceeds expectations

Megaworld’s 1H25 headline net profit grew 25% to P10.7bn. Stripping off forex gains in 1H25 (and forex losses in 1H24), we estimate that 1H25 recurring profit rose c. 10% to P10.4bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Revenue of every segment was higher than our expectations, as was the residential GPM.

Ultra high-end residential project to be launched by end-2025

2Q25 residential reservation sales rose 3% QoQ (-13% YoY) to P27.8bn. We estimate that 62% of 2Q sales came from Metro Manila (vs 52% in 1Q25), thanks to new launches in Fort Bonifacio and Newport. While the company continues to sell ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units, it only offers 10% cash discount now. This is lower than the 25% it offered last year and lower than the 30%-50% offered by some of the other developers.

Although 1H25 reservation sales only hit P54.7bn (-20% YoY), the company is maintaining its P130bn target for the year (flat YoY) because it expects strong sales from its maiden ultra high-end project, which will be launched in 2H25.

Office occupancy remains flat QoQ

2Q25 office revenue grew 1% QoQ (+18% YoY) as occupancy remained flat at 87%, much higher than the industry average of 82%. MEG intends to add 100k sqm of office space over the next 18 months (from 1.5mn sqm currently).

Mall revenue increases thanks to stronger tenant sales

Megaworld did not add any new mall space in the past year, implying that the 9% YoY increase in 2Q25 mall revenue was primarily driven by higher tenant sales. Revenue should continue growing in the medium-term as the company is targeting to expand its mall portfolio by 29% (150k sqm) over the next 18 months.

Monde Nissin - 2Q25: Higher input costs weigh on margins
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 revenues increase +3.0%, core profit down -7%

MONDE’s 1H core net profit (which excludes various items including marked-to-market gains/losses on its guaranty asset) dipped 7% YoY to P4.7bn. 1H25 net sales of P41.5bn (+3% YoY) was in line with expectations, but margins were not. In 2Q25 alone, net sales grew 4% YoY to P20.6bn driven by APAC BFB (+5%) which offset the decline in Quorn sales (-2% YoY). Consolidated margins for the quarter contracted both YoY and QoQ due to higher prices of coconut and palm oil. The margin contraction was the main cause of the profit disappointment.

Biscuits and “Others” drive growth, noodles flat

2Q25 APAC BFB revenues increased 5% YoY to P17.3bn on the back of strong growth in sales of biscuits as volumes for the category grew 10% YoY.

Noodles, however, were a mixed bag as it saw a 160bp YoY market share gain to 69% across its three sub-segments. Despite this, overall volumes still dropped 1% YoY. Volumes for soup noodles declined 2% in the quarter, an improvement from the 13% decline in 1Q25 but still not a great result. A commissioned study claims that lower income households are cutting back on spending, thus affecting its noodle business.

The "Others" category (beverages, culinary, etc.), which was 22% of 1H25 sales also did well, growing 11% for 2Q and 10% for 1H.

Quorn sees sales dip but positive EBITDA

Quorn revenue in 2Q25 posted another quarter of declining sales (-5% YoY, constant currency basis) but was able to post its 4th straight quarter of positive core EBITDA. Management says July was the first month to see revenue growth in years. Maintenance of the sales momentum will be key to avoid a possible impairment charge, as the valuation of the meat alternatives business is premised on a medium-term revenue CAGR of +1.8%.

Margins contract YoY and QoQ on higher commodity prices

Consolidated gross margin narrowed by 240bp YoY due to higher coconut oil and palm oil prices. Coconut prices alone were up 10% YoY in 2Q25. The contraction in gross margin drove the decline in EBITDA margin (-200bp) as operating expenses were steady relative to last year. To mitigate further margin compression, management stated that it was able to lock in better prices for palm oil moving forward and will adjust prices for SKUs directly affected.

Topline guidance on track, margin and capex guidance revised

Management stated that its guidance for the topline is on track with mid-single-digit growth for APAC BFB and moderating top line challenges for Quorn. However, guidance for APAC BFB margins was revised from "stable" to "100bp to 200bp lower" vs last year. 2Q25 capex was also revised to P4bn (from P7.5bn); completion for its biscuit plants in Central Luzon will be delayed to 2Q26.

Cebu Air - Robust 2Q25 results; Declares pref dividends
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H25 recurring profit up 34% to P4.23bn – Ahead of consensus, Regis estimates

Headline 2Q25 net income of P8.50bn (+551% YoY) included exceptional income of P4.9bn from Pratt & Whitney as compensation for aircraft-on-ground (AOG) issues. Stripping out extraordinaries and FX, we estimate recurring profit jumped 214% YoY to P3.72bn in 2Q25, taking 1H25 profit up 34% YoY to P4.23bn. The results were a strong beat on consensus estimates but were just a notch better than our estimates. CEB also surprised the market, after it declared  P9.12/sh (~19% yield) of dividends for convertible pref shareholders (CEBCP PM), representing cumulative unpaid dividends since 2021.

2Q25 revenue tailwinds

Revenues jumped 26% YoY to P32.9bn, as pax volumes grew 16%. Average fares rose a solid 12% YoY, along with the 16% improvement in ancillary yield. While the revenue gains were strong during the quarter, it was partly driven by tailwinds from the timing of Easter break (April vs. March last year) and changes in the start of school calendar (June vs. July last year).

EBITDA up 61% YoY in 2Q25 due to favorable fuel, FX, and operating leverage

EBITDA jumped 61% YoY to P10.74bn and EBITDA margin improved 720bps to 32.6%. Cost tailwinds from lower jet kero prices and a stronger PHP:USD exchange rate boosted margins but operating leverage also played a large role in the improvements.

Outlook: Strong forward bookings, fares/ancillary yields up YoY but…

Management conveyed cautious optimism heading into the seasonally weaker third quarter, as it cut back on seat capacity growth to 8-10% for the quarter (vs. 21% in 1H25). Forward booking trends are improving and fares/ancillary yields are holding up relative to last year.

That said, AOG challenges remain, with management scaling back its FY25E seat growth guidance to 15% from 15-20% the previous quarter. It also flagged that capacity growth could remain muted at just 5-10% next year. The silver lining is that its peers also have capacity limitations.

by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Profit drops 34% to P8.29bn in 1H25 – Behind estimates

After considering management’s guidance, we would describe the results as disappointing on both REGIS and Street estimates. Apart from lower-than-expected coal mining ASPs, management’s sales volume guidance of 16.8mn MT (+2% YoY) is also lower than our expectation of 19mn MT (+15%). The company’s net cash balance of P5.3bn (P1.25/sh) as of end-June reassures that it can still meet or even exceed consensus 2H25E DPS of P1.75/sh, equivalent to a yield of 5%.

Coal mining: Rising inventory of non-commercial grade coal

The coal mining unit saw 2Q25 standalone profits drop 19% QoQ, 42% YoY to P2.19bn, after ASPs fell 10% QoQ and 20% YoY to P2,223/MT. Even though coal production rose 12% YoY to 11.3mn MT in 1H, sales volumes dipped 1% to 9.3mn MT, as the company could not sell its non-commercial grade coal. Its stockpile of non-commercial grade coal has jumped to 1.9mn MT as of end-June, up 58% QoQ/YoY. Management said that this issue may only be resolved once it starts production from the Acacia mine, w/c has higher quality reserves, by mid-2026.

 

Lower WESM prices drag power unit’s 2Q25 performance

Profits from the power unit rose 20% QoQ to P1.9bn in 2Q25 but this figure was lower by 16% YoY after spot prices plunged 36% YoY to P4/kwh. Overall ASPs declined 19% to P4.51/kwh and pulled down EBITDA 15% YoY lower to P3.1bn. Given that ~60% of its capacity will undergo scheduled maintenance in various times within 2H25, profitability for the segment will also be sequentially lower.
Update on COC term adjustment

Management recently met the newly-appointed head of the Department of Energy to discuss the company’s request for a term adjustment on their Coal Operating Contract (COC), which is due to expire in July 2027. The Secretary was unable to give her decision at this meeting but management remains hopeful a favorable conclusion can be achieved within the year or latest by 1Q26.

Cutting profit estimates by 20-31% and TP by 5%

We downgrade our 25E-26E profit forecasts by 20-31% to incorporate lower coal mining ASPs and management’s coal sales volume guidance (see Fig 2). We also cut our DCF-based TP to P21/sh (-5%). We maintain our Hold rating, as securing a 15-year term adjustment could lift our valuation to P37/sh.

 
ACEN Corp. - Same issues linger in 2Q25; Another chunky impairment charge
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H core profit drops 30% to P2.79bn, in line with Regis estimates

ACEN’s 1H25 profit collapsed 88% to P763mn, after it booked a chunky impairment charge of P3bn in 2Q25, which relates to its investment in the Lac Hoa and Hoa Dong wind projects (48MW att. capacity) in Vietnam. Stripping this out and removing pref dividends, recurring profit fell 30% to P2.79bn, trending well below Street estimates but looks broadly in line with our forecasts.

Recurring profit falls 10% QoQ, 38% YoY to P1.32bn in 2Q25

2Q25 core income after pref dividends of P1.32bn was lower by 10% QoQ and 38% YoY, a result of (a) lower spot prices in the Philippines, (b) lower irradiance affecting its solar farms in the Philippines and Australia, as well as (c) extended repairs of its wind facilities in northern Luzon. New contributions from Stubbo (Australia) and Stockyard (USA) help mitigate the decline.

Setbacks in Vietnam prompt further impairments

Following a review of assumptions for the Lac Hoa and Hoa Dong wind farm, management took an additional impairment provision of US$50mn (~P2.8bn) in 2Q25, taking cumulative impairment charges since 2022 to US$119mn. This project has been hounded by various issues, such as project delays, lower capacity, higher costs, and a low provisional tariff of VND793/kwh (<P2/kwh). These have led to the asset write downs, which has taken the carrying value of these projects down to only US$77m (vs 60MW capacity).

Management, however, has since been able to secure a final tariff of VND1,587/kwh (~P3.50/kwh), which will be applied retroactively to the start of operations in early 2024 and should boost profits in succeeding periods.

Upcoming capacity completions, repair of wind facilities on track

Management said that it is on track with its goal of energizing ~800MW of new capacity this year. The 520MW Stubbo solar facility in Australia has begun commissioning in 2Q while the 600MW (attributable 146MW) Monsoon Wind project is expected to follow in 2H. Management is also optimistic it can complete the repairs of the north Luzon wind plants in time for the peak wind season in 4Q.

 
Metropolitan Bank & Trust Co - 1H25 NPAT P24.8bn, refreshingly in line
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Boring can be good; 1H25 profit broadly in line

Metrobank reported 1H25 NPAT of P24.8bn, up 5% YoY on track to meeting our P50.9bn forecast for FY25E (consensus P50.2bn). Loan growth slowed to 13.5%, but NIMs expanded on lower funding costs. PPOP jumped 16% YoY as opex growth (+6%) was kept in check. Asset quality was steady; provision expense jumped off a (very) low base YoY, but waswell within management guidance. The in-line results were refreshing, following the misses from other banks that have reported so far. Maintain Buy with TP of P100.

Loan growth slows to 13.5%, consumers +15%; NIMs jump on lower funding cost

Gross loans slowed to 13.2% (net +13.5%), from 16.1% in 1Q24, due largely to the corporate and commercial book (+13% vs +16%). Consumer loan growth was steady at +15% YoY, driven by auto and credit cards (both +18%). NIMs expanded 17 bps QoQ, on lower funding costs (-17bps QoQ) as the bank let go of wholesale funds in favor of cheaper deposits. Trading/fx gains were strong, though the 1H24 base included losses on interest rate swaps which were unwound in 3Q24.

Opex, asset quality in check – mindful of potential upticks in 2H25

Opex was up just 6% YoY, tracking behind our forecasts. Management, though, guided for increased spending in 2H25 on IT, and on marketing for its consumer loan push. Asset quality was steady, with NPL ratio at 1.5% (vs 1.6% in 1Q) and NPL cover at 154% (from 151%). Credit cost increased to 71bps in 2Q25 from 57bps in 1Q, though the 1H was well within the 50-60bps guidance for FY25E. Management says it is currently reviewing its ECL models.

CAR jumps to 16.3%; dividends sustainable?

The bank is on track to maintain its P5.00/sh dividend payout for FY25, for a 7% dividend yield. Capital ratios jumped 90bps QoQ, with consolidated CAR at 16.3% and CET-1 at 15.6%, as ROEs were sustained at 13.1%. Management has yet to guide for 2026E dividends, but hopes to give firmer feedback depending on their loan growth outlook for 2026E and beyond.

2Q25 GDP grows 5.5%—in line with expectations

GDP grew at 5.5% in 1Q, a tad higher than the 5.4% growth in 1Q25 but much lower than the 6.5% growth rate back in 2Q24. The 5.5% GDP growth is in line with consensus expectations of 5.5%. The print is at the lower end of the government’s 5.5–6.5% target range (was previously 6-7%).

On the demand side, the slight acceleration in growth from private consumption (+5.5%) was offset by the slowdown in gov't consumption (+8.7% from +18.7%) and capital formation (+0.6% from +4.8%). Private consumption saw improvement to 5.5% in 2Q, slightly better than the 5.3% growth seen in the previous quarter. Notably, the 5.5% growth was the highest since 1Q23 (+6.3%).

Private investments dipped to 0.6% in 2Q from 4.8% in 1Q, the sharp decline was led by the fall in construction to 0.6% from 7.0% for the same period. The significant contraction was likely due to the frontloading of construction in 1Q prior to the election ban leading up to the midterm elections last early May. Gov't construction declined -7.7% in 2Q, a reversal from 9.1% growth in 1Q and offset sustained double-digit growth in private construction (+16.7% from +14.6%)

Exports grew for the third straight quarter (+4.4%), while imports tempered to 2.9% from 10.3% in 1Q. Merchandise exports grew 13.6% from 8.1% seeing a boost from frontloading prior to the effectivity of higher US tariffs

On the supply side, growth was led by the services sector at 6.3% but was slower than the 7.0% expansion in 1Q24. The agri sector gained momentum accelerating to 7.0% from 2.2% in the quarter. The industry sector slowed to to 2.1% from 4.6% in 1Q as all industry subsectors saw decelerations in growth rates. Services sector saw faster growth at 6.9% from 6.2% in 1Q.

The reading brings 1H25 GDP growth to 5.4%, right at the lower end of the government's target of 5.5-6.5% and in line with consensus and REGIS expectations of 5.5% GDP growth for 2025. GDP growth for 2H25 needs to average 5.6% to hit the minimum full-year target.

Inflation rate dips to 0.9% in July

Headline inflation fell to 0.9% in July from 1.4% in the previous month of June. This is slightly ahead of the consensus forecast of 1.1% and stands well within the BSP's forecast range of 0.5-1.3%. This is also the lowest inflation print since Oct 2019 (0.6%). Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past five months.

Primary drivers for the continued benign inflation were YoY price declines in rice (-15.9%) and transport (-2.0%) as well as the slower price acceleration in electricity, gas, fuel (+1.5% from +5.5% in June), which offset rising prices of meat (+8.8%) and fish (+6.3%). Notably, rice prices are on their thirteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Aug 2), prices for local and imported well-milled rice range from P37-52/kg and P38-50/kg, respectively vs. P42-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 2.

This brings the YT-Jun headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.0%, while Regis expects the full year average to be 1.8%.

Rate cut likely this month; BSP Governor sees room for more cuts beyond 2025

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the central bank has more leeway to continue the easing cycle next year after two more 25bp cuts for the rest of 2025. Remolona guided for a possible rate cut later this month and stated that another cut in 4Q is now "more likely" given the six-year low inflation print in July. This implies 100bp total rate cuts for 2025, bringing the key policy rate to 4.75% by year-end from the current 5.25%.

He also added that the BSP does not necessarily follow US rate moves given the peso's relative strength despite not closely matching the Fed's rate path. The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled for Aug 28 (fourth out of six this year). For 2025, Bloomberg median consensus and REGIS expects 75bp total worth of cuts to 5.00%.

Rice imports to be suspended but no decision yet on imported rice tariff hike

President Marcos Jr. ordered the suspension of all rice importation for 60 days (effective Sept 1, 2025) to protect local farmers from low palay (unmilled rice) prices. Recall that the Dept. of Agriculture (DA) recently proposed for an import freeze on rice and a gradual increase of the imported rice duties back to 35% from the current level of 15%. No decision has been made yet regarding the tariff increases on imported rice as Marcos and the DA is looking to "strike the right balance" in protecting both local famers and consumers. Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that if the import ban brings higher farmgate prices and income for farmers, they may no longer need to raise the tariff.

Debt-to-GDP ratio grows to 63.1% as of end-June

Data from the Bureau of Treasury exhibited that the gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of end-June increased to 63.1% of GDP, up from 62% the prior quarter and 60.7% at end-2024. Outstanding debt as of end-June stood at P17.27trn (+12% YoY, +2.1% MoM). The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 60.4% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in his podcast stated that the country's debt, which is P17trn and still growing, would eventually go down to a sustainable level. “We will have enough budget to fund these projects. So long as the country's money is being used properly."

PH Dollar reserves at $105.7bn end-July

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Jul slightly decreased 0.3% MoM to US$105.7bn from US$106bn at end-Jun. The decline primarily reflected lower gold prices and drawdowns on foreign exchange deposits by the national gov't with the BSP. Notably, foreign exchange deposits were down 34% MoM. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.4x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$104bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn.

Commercial banks’ loan growth 12.1% in June; M3 growth at 6.3%

Loans of commercial banks rose by 12.1% YoY in June, an acceleration from the 11.3% rate in May. Loans to productive activities increased 11.1% YoY, growing faster than the 10.2% in May due to increased lending in key industries such as: Electricity, gas, steam, air conditioning supply (+29.2% from +25.6%), transpo, storage (+15.9% from +14.0%), finance, insurance (+12.0% from +9.6%), and real estate (+9.9% from +8.7%). Consumer loans grew 24.0%, slightly faster than the 23.7% pace in May. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up to 6.3% in June, accelerating from the 5.5% growth in May.

Government to craft policy on online gambling

President Marcos said the government will convene a conference in order to craft a comprehensive policy on online gambling. The conference will include religious groups, parents, etc. The primary focus will be the effective regulation of online gambling.

President Marcos believes that banning online gambling will push the industry underground, then the government will have no control over it: “The problem is not online gambling. The problem(s) are the social effects on our children and those who are addicted to gambling.” The President said a comprehensive policy will address the social problems associated with online gambling, such as addiction, debt, and exposing young persons to gambling.

BSP finalizing rules related to online gambling

The BSP is finalizing rules that will require banks, e-wallets, and other financial service providers to adopt stronger safeguards against gambling-related harm. The upcoming measures will include:

Strict identity verification, including biometric checks, such as facial recognition, to ensure only eligible individuals can use their funds for online gambling

Daily limits on gambling-related transfers to reduce excessive financial losses

Time-based restrictions on gambling payments to help curb impulsive behavior

User tools for setting personal spending caps, taking voluntary breaks, or self-excluding from gambling transactions
