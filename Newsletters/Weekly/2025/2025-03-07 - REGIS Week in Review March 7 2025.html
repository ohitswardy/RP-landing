REGIS REPORT

07 March 2025

The PCOMP clocked a 5-day winning streak this week, ending higher by 5.0% or 300.32 pts WoW to 6,298.29 lifted by optimism over falling domestic inflation and the weaker US dollar. This is the benchmark's highest close in a month after a mostly sluggish February.

 

Philippine inflation dropped to 2.1% in February, way better vs Street's 2.6% estimate and a large deceleration from January's 2.9% print. Slower food inflation (+2.6% from +3.8%) was the main culprit, along with falling CPI in housing & utilities (+1.6% from +2.2%) and transport (-0.2% from +1.1%). Encouraged by the latest figures, Finance chief Ralph Recto commented that slower inflation is creating more room for potential monetary easing. The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled on April 3rd.

 

This helped improve sentiment on the property sector this week, leading SMPH (+9.2% WoW) and ALI (+8.1% WoW) to outperform much of the market.

 

Another outperformer was ICT (+10.1% WoW) which was the biggest contributor to the PCOMP's weekly gain, after Trump backpedaled on tariff plans for Mexico and Canada that were initially slated to take effect earlier this week. The company also posted a decent 4Q result that came within market expectations, showing organic volume growth accelerated in the quarter, though higher cash costs slightly offset yield improvement and softened EBITDA margin for the period.

 

4Q earnings season remains in full swing. SCC (+0.9% WoW) missed consensus forecasts as mining and power ASPs disappointed, as did higher non-cash costs. BLOOM (+3.4% WoW) also missed estimates as 4Q losses widened from a one-time tax charge related to debt refinancing. Core gaming operations remained soft with 4Q volumes down QoQ and YoY. SECB (+2.7% WoW) was within expectations, with loans growing double vs industry growth while NIMs contracted. MEG (+0.6% WoW) earnings were in-line, though reservation sales were down QoQ in 4Q, and management's outlook on the residential segment likewise remains cautious. NIKL (+3.4% WoW) beat consensus forecasts, seeing 4Q core earnings jump fivefold on lower equitized losses from HPAL. DNL (+0.9% WoW) earnings only marginally higher, but the Batangas plant booked a significant turnaround in 4Q to return positive income for the FY.

 

Also among the week's largest advancers were JGS (+14.2% WoW) and URC (+8.5% WoW), which rebounded from last week's pullback after their demotion to the MSCI Small Cap Index (from Standard).

 

The peso appreciated by 1.4% WoW to 57.191:$1 as the US dollar continued to weaken. It is now the third best-performing currency in the Asia basket over the last month, up by 1.5% MoM and only trailing the JPY (+2.7% MoM) and the SGD (+1.9% MoM).

 

Foreigners turned net buyers by $7m following three weeks straight of net outflows. ADTO softer WoW to $130m.

SM Investments - 4Q24: Retail rebound; buyback announced
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY24 profits up 6% to P82.6bn

SM Investment reported net income for FY24 reached P82.6bn, +6% over the prior year, which is within Street expectations. Banking remained the biggest profit contributor. We note that the retail operations saw a nice bounce in 4Q. The company’s board also announced a P60bn buyback program, the first in its history.

 

Reservation sales of residential property grow sequentially

Real estate subsidiary SM Prime’s FY24 profit grew at a healthy pace of 14% (to P45.6bn), as mall revenue increased 8%. While full-year reservation sales fell 46% (to P55bn), the fourth quarter showed a sequential improvement, growing 18% QoQ to P8bn. Furthermore, the company highlighted that cancellation rates fell from 22% in FY23 to 13% in FY24, thanks to tighter payment schemes.

 

Retail sees 4Q bounce; Alfamart slows down expansion

Retail revenues were +5% in FY24 and +7% in 4Q alone. 4Q profits saw a strong bounce, rising ~31% YoY to P8bn. We estimate groupwide SSSG for 4Q alone was over +4% while Food Retail’s was +5%. An improvement in consumer spending seems evident.

 

Majority-owned Alfamart Philippines added over 400 stores, ending the year with 2,092 stores. The store expansion program will slow down to 200-250 stores this year. SSSG for this operation was “flat” in 4Q.

 

Buyback would be accretive

The board of SM approved a P60bn buyback program, the first in its history. The amount represents ~6% of SM’s market cap. Given the prevailing cost of debt and our estimate of SM’s NAV, we reckon a buyback at current levels would be accretive for both EPS and NAV/sh.

 
ICTSI - Profit growth a tad softer in 4Q; Business as usual
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY24 profit up 28% YoY to US$830mn, in line with Street but below Regis FY24E

FY24 core profits after perps distributions rose 28% YoY to US$830mn while EBITDA grew 18% to US$1.78bn. The results were in line with Street forecasts but were below Regis estimates. Implied 4Q24 profits went up by 22% YoY to US$213mn, slightly moderating from the 26% pace in 9M, as cash costs kept pace with the 16% revenue growth during the quarter.

 

4Q organic revenue growth steady at 15% as volume growth quickened to 5%

Organic volumes grew 5% YoY in 4Q, slightly accelerating from the 4% pace in 3Q, after ICT (a) completed the Phase 3A capacity expansion in Mexico and (b) gained back market share in Ecuador in December. Meanwhile, yield per TEU rose 10% YoY to US$204 (flat QoQ) in 4Q, as the tariff hike in Manila and favorable volume mix in EMEA were partly weighed down by (a) unfavorable currency movements and (b) the recovery of volumes in the low-yielding port of Ecuador.

 

4Q24 EBITDA margin softens sequentially to 63.8%

EBITDA margin slipped further to 63.8% in 4Q24 from 65.3% in 3Q and 65.4% in 1H24, after cash costs rose 10% QoQ and 15% YoY.

Management blamed the lower margin to (a) the port fee structure in Manila, as well as (b) additional manpower needed in Brazil and Poland.

 

Capex jumps to US$517mn in FY24; 2025 capex guidance at US$580mn

Capex soared to US$517mn in FY24, higher than guidance of US$450mn and prior year’s capex of US$336mn. Management accelerated spending for Phase 3B expansion in Mexico and the greenfield port in Batangas. The company also guided for capex of US$580mn in 2025 for the ongoing expansions in Mexico, Batangas, and Manila, as well as upgrade of equipment at other ports.

 

Sees limited impact from Trump’s tariffs

Management reiterated its view that its volumes will be largely unaffected by Trump’s tariffs. It estimates only 1% of its total volumes (6% of Mexico volumes) are Chinese-origin intermediate goods imports into Mexico and are re-exported to the US. In addition, only 2.5% of its global volumes are exports to the USA. Demand and profitability continued to be strong so far in 2025, without any front-loading activity in anticipation of higher tariffs.

 
Semirara Mining & Power Corp - 4Q24 profit decline as expected; 2025 subdued pricing guidance
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY24 profit drops 29% to P19.77bn - In line with Regis, Below consensus FY24E

SCC’s profit (ex-FX adj) fell 26% YoY to P4.1bn in 4Q24, bringing FY24 profit to P19.77bn (-29%), broadly in line with Regis’ FY24E (P20bn) but was behind consensus estimate (P20.7bn). Mining and power ASPs disappointed, as did higher non-cash costs, but these negatives were made up for by lower taxes, higher mining sales volumes, and better plant output.

 

Management’s pricing guidance for 2025 is lower than our estimates but we are inclined to keep our forecasts for the time being. There were no material updates on the renewal of its coal operating contract, with management still waiting for the Department of Energy’s final decision.

 

Soft outlook for coal prices

Standalone mining profits fell 36% YoY to P13.9bn in FY24, after ASPs dropped 25% to ~P2,800 per MT (~US$50) and more than offset the 5% YoY improvement in sales volumes to 16.5mn MT.

Management expects Newcastle/ICI4 thermal coal prices to decline by 15%/9% YoY to US$115/US$49 per MT. However, management believes that (a) Indonesia’s moves to use Harga Batubara Acuan (HBA)-based pricing for exports and (b) increasing the proportion of sales to higher-margin domestic buyers could temper the rate of decline of SCC’s ASPs. While there is downside risk to our ASP forecast (US$48/MT, -2% YoY), we reckon the extension of the income tax holiday (ITH) and higher sales volume guidance of 16mn MT (vs. Regis FY25E: 15.5mn) could mitigate the drop in ASP. All else held equal, ASPs would likely need to drop to US$42/MT to wipe out the gains from the ITH extension.

 

Higher costs hit 4Q performance of power unit; CHP contributes P11mn profit

Profits from the power unit fell 42% QoQ and 16% YoY to P446mn in 4Q24, after heavy rains limited the company’s ability to optimize fuel blending.  Management is likewise wary about the influx of new capacity this year, which could lower WESM prices to P4/kwh from P5.14/kwh in 2024. Lastly, SCC said that its cement manufacturing associate now renamed Concreat Holdings (CHP PM), contributed P11mn to its bottom line since Dec 2.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - 4Q24 volumes at Solaire Entertainment City fall across the board
 
 
by Carl Sy +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Another net loss in 4Q24

Bloomberry’s FY24 headline profit fell 73% to P2.6bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. 4Q24 was hit by a P706mn charge related to the refinancing of P72bn in debt. Stripping this out, the 4Q24 net loss would have been P215mn (instead of P920mn), and FY24 profit would have been P3.3bn (-66% YoY). FY24 EBITDA fell 14% to P16.6bn, in line with consensus but behind REGIS forecast.

 

Solaire Entertainment City (SEC) 4Q24 volume falls QoQ and YoY

Volumes at SEC fell 7% QoQ, the second consecutive quarter of decline. The reduction was seen across all categories both on a QoQ and YoY basis. The declines cannot be fully explained by cannibalization from Solaire North (SN), since they are greater than the QoQ increases of SN’s volume (see tables 3, 6, and 9). Despite the reduction in volume, SEC GGR’s was flat QoQ (down 9% YoY) thanks to the improvement in the win rate, particularly for mass table games.

 

For FY24, SEC’s GGR fell 9% (to P53bn) while its EBITDA declined 14%. Although management believes that the brunt of the POGO ban has been felt (given the GGR declines in the past two quarters), we expect further GGR reduction (to P45bn) in FY25E. Of SEC’s GGR (for 4Q/FY24), 28% were from players from Greater China and we believe many of them had ties to the POGO sector. 

Solaire North GGR flat despite VIP contribution

4Q24 volume increased 8% QoQ to P41.8bn, as VIP rolling chip grew 86% QoQ (to P4.2bn). However, mass table and slot volumes barely increased (+3%) QoQ. Meanwhile, 4Q24 GGR of SN fell 2% QoQ (due to lower win rates) while EBITDA declined 47% to P350mn.

 

Management expects SN to continue ramping up and for the property to reach maturity in FY26. At that point, BLOOM expects SN’s GGR to be about half of SEC’s. Our assumptions seem to be in the same ballpark. We forecast SN’s GGR to rise to P25bn (about half of SEC’s FY24 GGR) in FY26E, but for SEC’s GGR to fall to P42bn (due to the opening of a new integrated resort in its vicinity).   

 

Marketing expenses to rise ahead of launch of new online platform

Management said that marketing expenses will ramp up ahead of the 3Q25 launch of its new online platform. The company acknowledges that it needs to spend aggressively for this since there are about 60 online operators. 

 

Debt refinancing to save P1.2bn annually

While the additional P40bn in debt refinancing will lead to another non-recurring charge of P250mn in 1Q25, BLOOM estimates that it will save P1.2bn in interest cost annually from the combined P112bn refinancing in 4Q24/1Q25, assuming that interest rates stay where they are.

 
Megaworld - 4Q24 presales fall 11% QoQ; management remains cautious on residential
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY24 earnings rise 8%

Megaworld’s FY24 earnings rose 8% to P18.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. While operating revenue of every segment outperformed our estimates, this was largely offset by forex losses on US$ debt.

 

FY24 reservation sales fall 5%, unsold inventory drops 10% YoY

FY24 reservation sales fell 5% P131.9bn, 88% of the 2019 level. On a more positive note, the value of its unsold inventory fell 10% YoY (down 1% QoQ) to P133bn. Moreover, we estimate that the company sold over P40bn of ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units in FY24. These RFO sales, which can be recognized as revenue more quickly, are likely to have been a key factor in the 19% increase in residential revenue to P51bn, a record level. 

 

4Q24 reservation sales fell 11% QoQ (flat YoY) to P29.9bn, as the company saw faster declines from international buyers and from Metro Manila projects.

International buyers comprised 20% of FY24 sales, from 24% in 9M24 (25% in FY23). Meanwhile, Metro Manila projects accounted for 56% of FY24 sales, from 60% in 9M24 (55% in FY23).

 

Residential outlook remains cautious

Management’s presales and launch guidance reflect its cautious stance on the residential segment. It is targeting flat presales (P130bn in FY25). However, only P20bn worth of new project launches (much lower than FY24 presales) are currently being programmed. The latter suggests a more serious level of concern over the residential markets.     

 

Mall revenue improves; office occupancy declines

4Q24 mall revenue grew 19% QoQ and 27% YoY, as the opening of new concepts (such as Morton’s and Tesla) helped increase foot traffic and sales.

Meanwhile, office occupancy declined 1ppt QoQ to 86% due to POGO closures.

Adding +370MW of attributable solar capacity by 2026

The company is adding 370 MW of attributable solar capacity by end-2026, which will take its total att. RE capacity to over 500MW from 147MW today.

Dividend of P0.11/sh, equivalent to a yield of 5%

NIKL declared a dividends of P0.11/sh (ex-div: Mar 12), of which P0.044/sh was the special portion, slightly lower than our forecast of P0.12/sh The dividend is equivalent to a payout ratio of ~50%, just higher than the previous year’s payout ratio of 48%. AGI clarifies ownership discrepancy in EMI, cites human error

In a response to the PSE's request for Alliance Global Group Inc. (AGI PM) to explain the discrepancy between its reported ownership in Emperador Inc. (EMI PM), AGI clarified that human error was the cause for the misstatement in a disclosure. AGI stated that EMI's Public Ownership Reports (POR) show the exact ownership percentages to two decimal points, calculated by issued shares minus treasury shares. While AGI's quarterly and annual reports exhibit consolidated, rounded-off ownership percentages which reflected AGI's effective ownership, including 65.48mn shares held by its subsidiary, Emperador International Limited (EIL)—treating EMI and its subsidiaries as one unit and thus AGI counted EIL's shares in EMI as part of EMI's own treasury shares. Prior to AGI's 3Q21 report, there were no issues from the difference in calculation, but by the release of the quarterly report, the discrepancy arose, which recorded its effective ownership in EMI at 86% instead of 85%, and 81% instead of 80% in year-end 2022 — a mistake carried over in subsequent reports. The company attributed the error to oversight due to an unintentional mistake of a rounding error or computational error. AGI and EMI reaffirmed their intent to comply with PSE regulations and expressed their commitment to expanding their public float to increase share tradability.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Inflation rate eases to 2.1% in February

Headline inflation eased to 2.1% in February from 2.9% in the previous month, marking the lowest print since Sept 2024. This is lower than consensus forecast of 2.6% and came in below the BSP's forecast range of 2.2-3.0%. On a MoM basis, CPI dropped 0.2%, primarily driven by lower food and diesel prices. Food & non-alcoholic beverages saw a drop in prices 0.9% MoM for the first time since Sept 2024. This is as vegetables dipped 12.4% MoM with prices starting to normalize after the onslaught of typhoons last 4Q24. Rice also saw its eighth consecutive month of price decline (-1.6% MoM), taking the cumulative decline to 6.2%. Core inflation dipped slightly to 2.4% in Feb from 2.6% in Jan, with core non-food inflation falling to 2.2% from 2.5% for the same periods. Notably, inflation for the bottom 30% income group dipped to 1.5%, down from 2.4% in Jan. This brings YT-Feb headline inflation rate to 2.5%. The gov't targets to keep inflation within 2-4% for this year. Bloomberg median consensus is for the full year average is 3.0%. We expect inflation to average 3.5%.

DOF: Slower inflation gives more leeway for rate cut

The Department of Finance in a statement said that the slower inflation figure in Feb is "creating more room for potential monetary policy rate easing." The DOF also emphasized the gov't's commitment to mitigating price pressures and long-term price stability. Recall that the BSP last Monetary Board meeting left rates unchanged at 5.75% citing continued global macro uncertainty. The next MB meeting (second out of six this year) is scheduled on April 3. Bloomberg median consensus currently expects 75 bps worth of rate cuts this year. We expect the BSP to cut its policy rate by 50 bps to 5.25%.

Trade deficit widens to $5.09bn in January

The January trade deficit widened to US$5.09bn, up 17% YoY and 23% MoM, reflecting a larger gap between imports and exports. Imports for the month rose 11% YoY to US$11.45bn. The increases came from all major types of goods but primarily driven by the 12% YoY increase in raw and intermediate goods as well as the 14% YoY increase in consumer goods. Exports grew 6% YoY to US$6.36bn, the first month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction. Electronics exports declined by 3% YoY but recovered +20% MoM to US$3.37bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 53% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports surged 19% YoY to US$2.99bn.

Budget deficit seen falling within 2025 target

Finance Secretary Ralph G. Recto has stated that the national gov't should be able to narrow the budget deficit below the P1.54tn ceiling and the 5.3% of GDP target for this year. This comes after the budget deficit for 2024 was relatively unchanged at P1.51tn (but narrower at 5.7% of GDP vs. 6.1% in 2023) and narrowly missing the P1.48tn ceiling (5.6% of GDP) target. Recto emphasized that hitting the deficit to GDP target is part of the gov't's macro fiscal framework to ensure macro stability, progress towards a credit rating upgrade, exhibit a seal of good housekeeping and macro stability for a flourishing business environment. He also commented that the DBCC is on track with its targets and hence, does not see the need to revise in its upcoming March meeting. Budget Undersecretary and Principal Economist Joselito R. Basilio cited lower interest rates and improved price conditions which will be the primary factors in meeting the target. He also cited fiscal discipline and better revenue performance through better tax collection in the BIR and Customs (digitalizing the tax system, stricter compliance, illicit trade crackdown) and new tax measures such as the proposed excise tax on single-use plastics (pending in the Senate), rationalization of the mining fiscal regime (due for bicam after election break).

Government debt inches up to P16.31tn in end-Jan

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.6% MoM (P261.5bn) to P16.31tn as of end-Jan. This was due to the net incurrence of new domestic and external debt as well as the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of November. Domestic debt rose by 1.4% (P153.7bn) MoM to P11.08tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P152.2bn). External debt increased by 2.1% (P107.8bn) MoM to P5.23tn given net foreign loan additions (P59.3bn) and unfavorable currency movements. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn. This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); By the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%.

Finance Secretary prefers tighter rules on online gaming

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that the Philippines should tighten rules and enforcement on online gaming rather than have a complete ban. The Bicameral Conference Committee ratified the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotions bill. A copy of the bicameral conference committee report of the measure was not immediately available. Key features of the bill: lowering the stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1% to encourage stock market activity; the documentary stamp tax (DST) on the original issue of shares of stock will be lowered from 1% to 0.75% to reduce transaction costs for investors; the DST on unit investment trust funds (UITFs) will also be removed; a 50% tax deduction will be introduced for employers' contributions to their employees’ personal equity and retirement accounts to encourage businesses to help employees save for retirement; the authorized capital stock of DBP will be increased from P35 billion to P300 billion to strengthen its financial capacity; the National Government will be required to maintain at least 70% ownership of DBP at all times. PAGCOR says that new BSP guidelines could curtail growth of e-games

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the BSP draft guidelines could curtail the growth of the e-casino sector. The BSP’s draft “Guidelines on the Adoption of a Digital Marketplace Model” seeks to prohibit online marketplaces (banks and electronic money issuers) from offering or presenting gambling. In addition to access, advertising of gaming platforms will likely be prohibited in online marketplaces. About 80% of the players of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) access its platform through GCash. The draft guidelines would suggest that players will no longer be able to access the PLUS platform through GCash but will still be able to transfer funds via GCash or banks. Existing players would likely continue to play on the PLUS platform. However, PLUS and other online gaming operators might experience more difficulty growing their current user bases.

DTI imposes P16 per 40-kg bag safeguard duty on imported cement

The Department of Trade and Industry issued an order imposing a provisional safeguard duty of P400/MT or P16 per 40kg bag for 200 days. PDIC deposit insurance coverage doubled to P1mn

On Friday, 28 February, the Philippine Deposit Insurance Corp (PDIC) announced that the maximum insurance coverage on banks’ PHP deposits would be doubled from the current P500,000 to P1mn (roughly USD17,300) effective 15 March. The last time the maximum coverage was adjusted was in 2009 (from P250,000) in the wake of the Global Financial Crisis. According to the PDIC’s press release, the increased coverage will NOT result in higher fees charged to the banks. The number of accounts fully insured will only rise by 100bps, from 97.6% previously, to 98.6%. However, that 100bps will increase the total value insured by 34% to P4.8trn, equivalent to 24.5% of total banking sector deposits (up from 18.3% previously). According to our estimates, banks currently pay premiums to PDIC equivalent to approximately 20-25bps of deposits. This – along with other friction costs such as documentary stamp taxes (stamp duties) on time deposits (TDs), and reserve requirements (RRR) – is why banks at times choose to tap the bond markets instead of paying up for expensive TDs.
