REGIS REPORT

01 August 2025

The PCOMP gave up last week’s gains, falling -1.7% to 6306, while most markets in the region likewise turned lower. In focus was the sharp strength in the USD – and consequently the notable weakness in the PHP which fell by a sizable -1.8% to 58.17, around its weakest levels since February – as views of at least 2 rate cuts by the Fed before year-end were broadly trimmed after the central bank decided to keep rates steady in its meeting during the week, after the latest US inflation gauge remained above target and 2Q GDP stayed robust at +3% QoQ. As such, investors are re-assessing our own monetary policy outlook at home and judging whether the BSP will still be able to trim rates further before year-end amid a falling peso. Additionally, sentiment across risk assets was also weighed down by Trump’s announcement of a slew of additional tariffs on several countries later in the week – although as of writing there is no change on Philippines’ 19% rate.

On the ground, investors digested more earnings results, particularly from lenders which generally showed strong growth in loans - but this was unanimously accompanied by increased provisions and credit costs as well. In terms of bottomline, BDO’s numbers missed forecasts as it was particularly dragged by a surge in operation expenses related to IT expansion. In contrast, MBT’s numbers looked generally solid overall and appear to be inline with consensus. In terms of share price performance, BDO -6% stood out as a laggard this week, while the bigger lenders BPI -1.9% and MBT -0.6% both turned lower as well; but in contrast the smaller lenders like CHIB +1.5%, SECB +4.4% and EW +5.6% gained.

Another important aspect of the week was that volumes were once again highly concentrated on PLUS which ended the week with a slight +1.1% gain - but after posting wild swings in either direction throughout the week (closed at limit down of -30% on Thursday). The stock was consistently the most actively traded in the entire market, accounting for 20%-30% of daily trades for a few weeks now. Investors also continue to anticipate the PSE’s August index review announcement where PLUS remains a candidate for inclusion – in place of expected deletion BLOOM which in contrast capped the week -7.7% and was the worst performer in the index this week.

Market activity stood at a daily average of $109m, -9% lighter compared to last week. Foreign participation improved but was still lighter than usual at only 41% as bulk of trades on most actively traded PLUS came mostly from retail investors. Net foreign flow turned skewed to sell by a light $8m – bulk of the outflows this week went into BDO post results, while in contrast ICT -1.1% saw consistent inflows.

BDO Unibank - Strong loans, fees; but 2Q NPAT dragged by opex, provisions
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1H25 NPAT behind, as opex stays high, provisions jump QoQ

BDO’s 1H25 NPAT of P40.6bn was up 3% YoY, behind Regis and Street forecasts, with 2Q25 profits flat YoY. Loan growth accelerated to 14% YoY (vs 12% in 1Q), and fee incomes were strong, up 17% in 1H. However, opex stayed elevated (+15% YoY in 1H/2Q), which saw PPOP up just 2% YoY in 1H. Provisions jumped 36% YoY in 2Q25, further dragging profits. Management, though, guided for a) moderating opex growth in 2H25, b) credit cost in the “low-40s”, and c) ROEs at or near 15% for FY25E. We have taken a slightly more conservative view, and adjust our FY25-27E estimates down by c4%. Our Buy call, and P200 target price remain.

Loans re-accelerate to 14% YoY, NIMs flat; Fee incomes strong +17%

Customer loans grew 14% YoY -- accelerating from the 11.6% pace in 1Q25 – driven by consumers +17.6% and “mid-market” corporates +13.3%. NIMs were up 5bps QoQ as lower policy rates and competition saw asset yields dip QoQ, while RRR cuts lowered funding costs. Fee incomes, meanwhile, remained strong, up 17% YoY, offsetting weak trading/fx gains. This took revenues up 10% YoY, broadly in line with our forecasts.

Opex stays elevated 15%, credit costs jump QoQ

Opex remained high, up 15% YoY. While manpower cost growth slowed in 2Q (+12% YoY vs +17% in 1Q), investments in branches and IT infrastructure grew. Management expects opex growth to moderate into 2H25, but noted that IT spend would stay elevated until 2028E. PPOP grew just 2% YoY in 1H25. Provision expense jumped 36% YoY in 2Q25 (+13% in 1H25), with credit costs at c49bps (vs 37bps in 1Q25), further dragging NPAT. NPLs were flat (1.75% vs 1.77% in 1Q) as was NPL cover (140% vs 143%), suggesting write-offs in the consumer book.

Adjusting forecasts, but keeping TP, Buy call

We reduce our 2025-27E forecasts by about 4%, assuming slightly higher opex, and higher credit cost than guided. We maintain our target price of P200/sh, based on our 2025E estimates, using (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.5%, COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%, and maintain our Buy call. Key downside risks include NIM pressure from rate cuts and competition, and higher credit costs.

 
Robinsons Retail - 2Q25: SSSG improves; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 sales and EBIT rise 5%

Robinsons Retail’s (RRHI) reported 1H25 headline net profit of P2.3bn, down 67% YoY with the absence of a P4.9bn one-time gain from the BPI-RBC merger in 1Q24. For 1H25, sales and EBIT rose 5% YoY to P98.5bn and P4.3bn, respectively, a tad short of expectations. For 2Q25 alone, net sales increased by 6% to P50.7bn while EBIT was up 7% to P2.4bn. Group SSSG for 2Q25 improved to +4.8% from +3.0% the previous quarter. The SSSG improvement was visible across each of the segments except specialty stores.

Food segment revenues up +4.0%

Food segment revenues were up 4.0% in 2Q25 to P30.1bn on SSSG of +4.2%, as basket sizes expanded. Within the food segment, convenience stores saw its 4th consecutive quarterly contraction in SSS (-3.5% in 2Q25). Drug stores saw 12% revenue growth with strong SSSG (+8.2%) and a boost from new store openings. The Food and Drugstore segments comprise 78% of revenue in 2Q25.

Department store revenue grew 12.9%, with SSSG (+8.1%) lifted by changes in the school calendar. The DIY business, meanwhile, continues to languish with sales virtually unchanged YoY.

O!Save still growing briskly

O!Save sales in 2Q25 surged ~140% YoY to US$118mn. Store expansion continues to accelerate, and the firm ended the period with 560 stores, implying another 89 stores were opened in 2Q25. It aims to reach 800 stores by year-end. O!Save management commented that its results are tracking better than their expectations with 1H25 EBITDA margin at -0.23%.

Treasury share retirement excludes Dairy Farm shares

RRHI announced that it is retiring 158mn shares held in treasury, all of which were previously repurchased under the company’s buyback program. The share retirement does not include the 315mn shares bought back from the Dairy Farm group at P50/sh (P15.7bn). We note that the transaction will be accretive to EPS despite an increase in finance costs due to the reduction in share count. For more details on the DFI buyback see our note here.

Revising forecasts; maintaining Buy rating and TP

We maintain our Buy rating on the stock but revise our estimates to factor in the (1) latest operating results (2) buyback of 22% of shares from DFI (3) acquisition of Premiumbikes. Our profit estimates have been trimmed but our EPS estimates have been raised due to the lower share count (see Fig 2). We also roll over our valuation to 2026E and maintain our TP of P83/sh. Our target price implies 2026E multiples of 14x PE and 7x EV/EBITDA. While the current consumer recovery is less robust than anticipated, we stay positive on RRHI’s long-term growth prospects. Key downside risks relate to competition, consumer sentiment, and execution risk.

 
Philippine Banking/Finance - Fintech in PH – takeaways from webinar with BSP payments head
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Adoption still surging, but still early days

We recently hosted a webinar on the state of fintech in the Philippines, with the BSP’s head of payments policy Atty Bridget Mesina-Romero, and close to 100 local and foreign institutional clients. Digital finance continues to surge, with about 57.4% of all retail transactions in 2024 done digitally. The BSP wants to broaden the base further, by a) reducing fees for small-ticket transactions, b) encouraging MSMEs to digitize, and c) offering new services to improve efficiency and inter-operability. Other plans include seamless cross-border remittances, and a Peso CBDC to facilitate trade. Atty Mesina-Romero also addressed investor concerns over tax evasion, cybersecurity, competition, and online gambling. We came away very bullish on the prospects of fintech, digibanks, and for incumbent banks that embrace digitalization.

Digital payments adoption continues to surge, encouraged by BSP

The country has had mobile wallets since the early 2000s (in SMS form), run by the two major telcos. However, the share of digital payments to total languished at just about 1% until 2013. This was because the wallets had to negotiate bilaterally with each bank or financial institution.

Unquestionably, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has been a key enabler in the adoption of digital payments in the Philippines. In 2017, Congress passed Republic Act 11127, giving the BSP the power to create, oversee and supervise the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) providing a secure and reliable payment platform. The NRPS allows “non-discriminatory” access for all financial institutions to facilitate transfers to all banks, non-banks, and e-wallets (aka Electronic Money Issuers , or EMIs) on the system.

The timing could not have been more perfect, as the Covid-19 pandemic saw demand for contactless payments explode in 2020-2021.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Chinabank - 2Q NPAT - massive provisions vs massive "other income"

Chinabank (CBC PM) reported in a press release that 1H25 NPAT came in at P13bn up 14% YoY, and in line with consensus forecast of P27bn for FY25E. However, the innards of the disclosure were… odd. Notably, provision expense exploded to P6.25bn in 2Q25 alone, equivalent to about 0.7% of total loans, or an annualized credit cost of 260bps. This contrasts with management’s guidance of 40bps (and 1Q25’s 12bp credit cost). This was offset by a massive swing in non-interest income to P4.8bn in 2Q25. No details were given re: the nature of the gains. In previous briefings and conversations, management cited prior periods’ low credit costs were due to write-backs / write-ups of NPLs that became current. Unsure whether a) these loans became NPL again, or b) if this is due to new accounts/s, or c) this is, as they said, “proactive” provisioning.

Elsewhere, operations seemed to chug along. Loan growth was 18%, sustaining the 18-19% level over the previous two quarters. Management estimates NIMs were up 13bps YoY, though we take this with a healthy helping of salt, considering the size of the interest rate swaps. In previous briefings, CBC’s CFO noted that the impact of the swaps would be to reduce NIMs by c100bps. Opex was steady QoQ, still up sharply YoY due to heavy IT and manpower investments.

Meralco signs MOU with Vietnam-based EV charging firm

Meralco signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) with V-Green Global Charging Station Development Corp. and ride-hailing company Green and Smart Mobility Joint Stock Co. The partnership aims to identify and evaluate sites for EV charging stations and electric taxi hubs, as well as explore other opportunities to scale up EV adoption. In Vietnam, V-Green serves as the exclusive charging infrastructure partner for VinFast electric vehicles. Green GSM, meanwhile, is an innovative electric ride-hailing platform that operates a fleet powered entirely by VinFast EVs. As of July, the Philippines had a total of 622 AC/DC charging points, up 14% MoM from 544 in end-June. The government is targeting 7,300 EV charging stations by end-2028.

DigiPlus applies for gaming license in South Africa

DigiPlus plans to enter South Africa’s online sports betting and fixed odds market (note that South Africa currently does not allow online casino games.) The company will apply for three licenses with the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB): National Manufacturer License, Bookmaker License, and Bookmaker Premises License and the process is expected to take at least six months from filing.

Sports betting gross gaming revenue (GGR) in South Africa reached approximately US$1.9bn in 2024. Online sports betting contributed about US$1.6bn and is projected to grow at around 5% YoY. In comparison, the Philippines’ electronic games GGR (online casino + online sports betting) totaled US$2.3bn (P136bn) in 2024. There is Remote Gambling Bill in South Africa’s parliament which could expand the market by legalizing additional formats including online casino gaming.

South Africa’s total GGR hit US$3.3bn in 2024, a 25% jump YoY. For context, the Philippines gaming industry GGR was US$6.4bn (P373bn). There are currently 350 licensed gaming operators in South Africa (land-based and online combined).

GCash chief says company unlikely to IPO this year

GCash President and CEO Martha Sazon stated that the company's IPO will probably not happen anymore this year after previously hinting to list in 2H25. Sazon elaborated the reason behind the delay was for more preparation in order "…to do it right" and that "…we're making sure that when we finally list, we are ready." Apart from Sazon's comments, other possible reasons for the delay may be due to waiting for clearer policy and regulatory direction on the gov't's stance on online gambling.

Robinsons Retail acquires motorcycle retailing business

Robinsons Retail Holdings Inc. (RRHI PM), through subsidiary Robinsons Supermarket Corp. (RSC), has signed a share purchase agreement to acquire 100% of Premiumbikes Corp., an affiliate of JG Summit Holdings (JGS PM) for P146mn. Premiumbikes is a motorcycle retailer that currently operates 214 stores nationwide and was founded back in 2009 by John Gokongwei. In 2024, Premiumbikes' revenues climbed by 15% YoY to P4.2bn. RRHI President and CEO Stanley Co stated that the acquisition is in line with its strategy to diversify revenue by entering "a new and fast-growing category that is also profitable." Premiumbikes General Manager Joselito Pojol also highlighted that the Philippines has low motorcycle penetration vs. other Southeast Asian markets.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Government debt inches up to P17.27trn in end-June

The government's total debt stock went up by 2.1% MoM to P17.27trn as of end-June. Domestic debt rose by 1.4% MoM to P11.95trn due to the impact of the additional issuance of gov't securities, while external debt increased by 3.5% MoM to P5.32trn. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.2% and 30.8% being external borrowings. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 1Q25 stood at 62% of GDP up from 60.7% in 4Q24. Note that the gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 60.4% by end-2025. The Dept. of Finance stated that "the gov't is on track to reduce the national gov't debt-to-GDP ratio to within 60% by end of the President's term."

BSP projects July inflation to settle within 0.5-1.3%

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects July inflation to fall within 0.5-1.3%. Inflation has averaged 1.8% so far for the first six months of the year; inflation in June was 1.4%. Upward price pressures are higher prices of meat and vegetables, elevated fuel and electricity prices as well as the depreciation of the peso. But these may have been partially offset by lower prices of rice.

Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.0%. Regis expects the full year average to be 1.8%. July inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on Aug 5 (Tuesday).

Dept. of Agriculture expects record rice output this year

The Department of Agriculture is "cautiously optimistic" on hitting a record-high harvest of palay (unmilled rice) this year despite the recent typhoons. Unmilled rice production in 1H25 is up 6% YoY to 9.1mn MT, currently on track to beat the 20.5mn MT full-year target, assuming 1H continues to account for over 40% of annual output, which has typically been the case in past years. If production at least hits the 20.5mn MT target this year, it will be 7% higher than the 19.1mn MT output last year and 2% higher than the record 20.06mn MT record output in 2023.

Trade deficit narrows to $4bn in June

The June trade deficit narrowed to US$4bn, contracting 9% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in exports over imports. This brings the 6M25 trade deficit to US$24bn, 4% narrower YoY. Imports for the month increased by 11% YoY to US$11.0bn, a reversal from the 4% decrease in May. Primarily driven by the increase in capital and consumer goods imports.

Exports surged by 26% YoY to US$7.0bn, the sixth consecutive month of growth and the fastest since posting 28% growth back in April 2024. Exports to the US jumped by 35% to US$1.2bn in the month of June, the highest rate of growth in recent years. Electronics exports climbed by 30% YoY to $3.8bn, an acceleration from the 8% increase in May - they made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total.

Meanwhile, non-electronics exports jumped by 22% YoY to US$3.1bn, a tad slower than the 24% growth in May. Growth in non-electronics exports was led by mineral products as well as machinery and transport equipment.

Colliers: Office and residential net take-up improve in 2Q25

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy remained flat QoQ at 20% in 2Q25 - Makati CBD vacancy rose 1ppt QoQ to 8.1%, BGC fell 1.5ppt QoQ to 14.4%, while Manila Bay area rose to 45% in 2Q25, from 41% in 1Q25. Net office take-up rose 67% QoQ to 60,000sqm largely due to fewer POGO closures but office rental rates in Makati CBD and BGC were generally flat QoQ. Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 21% by end-2025. While it expects net take-up to hit 150,000sqm, it also expects about 277,000sqm in new supply

Residential vacancy was also relatively flat QoQ at 24.5%. Residential net take-up jumped to 2,866 units in 2Q25, from only 175 units in 1Q25. Colliers said that aggressive promotions (such rent-to-own schemes of up to 10 years) and cash discounts of up to 50% on select ready-for-occupancy units “appear to be working.” It also said that residential cancellations fell sequentially.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) rise 4% YoY in June; Toyota unit sales up 9% YoY

CAMPI reported vehicle sales grew 3.6% YoY, 1.8% MoM to 40,483 units in June (Note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD’s sales). This brings 1H25 sales up 2% YoY to 230,912 units - assuming BYD’s market share was at least 5%, we estimate industry car sales would have likely grown over 7% YoY. Sales of electrified vehicles (ex-BYD) fell 15% MoM to 3,057 units, accounting for 7.6% of overall new car sales. Cumulative 1H25 EV sales reached 13,490 units, accounting for 5.84% share of overall sales.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 19,624 units in June, up 8.5% YoY (-0.51% MoM) which brings TMP’s 1H25 sales volumes to 111,276 units, up 6.6% YoY. TMP is targeting to sell 235k units this year (+8% YoY). To reach this target, sales should average 20.6k units per month for the rest of the year. Regis is forecasting unit sales of 231k units (+6% YoY). TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV (24% of gross asset value) and 28% of net profit.

Highlights from Marcos' fourth State of the Nation Address

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. delivered his fourth State of the Nation Address (SONA) yesterday, discussing the administration's accomplishments and progress for ongoing initiatives in key areas but controversial topics such as online gambling, the 19% US tariffs on PH goods, and any mention of the Vice President were not brought up.

Marcos also talked about additional social welfare programs such as expanding PhilHealth coverage, higher financial assistance limits in public hospitals, more funding for free tertiary education programs, free bus transportation in certain cities, among others. These were likely in response to public dissatisfaction that showed in the administration slate's poor performance in the recent midterm elections last May.

Economy - President Marcos acknowledged the lackluster delivery of gov't services and improvements in the quality of life of the everyday Filipino despite better macroeconomic indicators of slowing inflation, falling unemployment, improving business confidence

National budget - Marcos emphasized that he would veto any 2026 budget proposal that is not aligned with the admin's economic roadmap, stating that he would do so even if it leads to a reenacted budget

Infrastructure - The "Build Better More" program will continue to be pushed with core projects such as railways, expressways, ports, and flood control projects. Marcos ordered the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) to audit all flood control projects started or completed over the past three years and make the list public.

Power - Marcos pledged full electrification by 2028. 2.5mn households since 2022 have gained access to power, with ~200 new power plants under development

Agriculture - The P20/kg rice program was stated to have been a success as he highlighted that is feasible without harming earnings of farmers. Rice traders that continue to engage in price manipulation were warned to be charged with economic sabotage.

Online gambling - No clarity was given for the gov't's stance on online gambling as the issue was not discussed. Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin a few days ago cited that Marcos has not yet reached a final decision as they are "still looking into it."

Senate committee to tackle online gambling next week

The Senate committee on games and amusement will probe the online gambling sector next week. Panel Head Senator Erwin Tulfo said that the committee will invite all stakeholders: PAGCOR, Department of Finance, youth organizations, parent organizations, etc. He said that the panel intends to form a strong position before asking the President to issue an Executive Order banning online gambling.

Supreme Court rules VP Sara Duterte impeachment as unconstitutional; Lower House to appeal SC ruling

The Supreme Court (SC) ruled that the impeachment of Vice President Sara Duterte was unconstitutional, halting the trial that was supposed to take place early July. Four impeachment complaints were filed against Duterte, with the final one that was lodged back in Feb 2025 being the only one approved by the Lower House. The SC argued that failing to properly process the first three filings effectively dismissed these prior complaints. The high court argues that the dismissal of these cases, triggered the Constitutional rule of the one-year ban on filing new cases. This implies that another complaint must be refiled by Feb 2026, should the Lower House pursue the impeachment of VP Duterte. Senate impeachment court spokesperson Regie Tongol said the SC ruling affirms the Senate's decision to act in line with the Constitution and uphold the rule of law, emphasizing their duty to respect the finality of high court decisions. Princess Abante, the Lower House spokesperson, stated that they will file a motion of reconsideration on the impeachment after it was declared unconstitutional by the SC.
