REGIS REPORT

14 November 2025

A sharp bout of risk aversion pulled the PCOMP below the 5,600 handle – its weakest close since May 2020 – as political noise, lingering macro concerns, and mixed corporate results weighed heavily on sentiment. The sharp sell-off on Friday (-2.5% DoD) came after former Congressman Zaldy Co alleged that Pres. Marcos and former House Speaker Martin Romualdez were among the principal orchestrators of the controversial flood control budget scheme, rekindling political uncertainty and prompting investors, especially locals, to aggressively cut exposure. This widened the index's weekly loss to 3.0% or 175.02 pts WoW, to 5,584.35.

 

Market sentiment remained fragile throughout the week as participants also digested the last batch of 3Q earnings, which drove stock-specific moves. In particular, amid relatively soft Consumer earnings, names that bested peers on earnings growth CNPF (+4.1% WoW) and PGOLD (+3.5% WoW) were among the week's outperformers.

 

Another major driver was speculation of an off-cycle BSP rate cut, which fueled a rotation into yield plays later in the week. TEL (+11.5% WoW), GLO (+9.3% WoW), and MER (+7.0% WoW) were the three biggest index gainers for the week. The rumored cut, however, did not materialize. Despite this, the market continues to expect the BSP to ease at its December policy meeting. Against this backdrop, Financials (-6.0% WoW) lagged the most for the week. Big three banks BDO (-9.8% WoW), MBT (-6.0% WoW), and BPI (-4.8% WoW) were all major laggards.

 

Flows were choppy, with foreign investors caught between selective bottom-fishing and reducing positions. Local institutions appeared persistently defensive with capital likely continuing to shift toward fixed income amid rising rate-cut expectations.

 

The peso hit a fresh low mid-week at 59.185:$1, though closed flat for the week at 59.055:$1.

Philippine Strategy -- GDP slumps on "flood control" fallout; Dec rate cut looking likely
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

3Q25 GDP growth 4.0%, slowest since 2011 (ex-Covid); policy support likely

GDP growth in 3Q25 slumped to 4.0% YoY, well behind consensus (+5.2%), and the weakest print since 2011, not counting Covid. We had warned of a slowdown due to the “flood control” corruption scandal. However, the severity of the “miss” was unexpected, and not quite in sync with other indicators, especially on consumption. The government had already said it intends to increase noninfra “social spending”. The weak GDP print, and the slower-than-expected inflation, also raise the prospect of a 25 (or 50?) bp rate cut on 11 Dec.

 

GDP misses as government construction plunges 26% on corruption scandal…

GDP growth came in at 4.0% YoY for 3Q25 (Fig 1), a sharp deceleration from the 5.5% in 2Q25, and far below the consensus estimate of 5.2%. The print – the slowest since 2011 – was due largely to a drop in government spending in light of the ongoing corruption scandals. We had warned of infrastructure projects grinding to a halt, as they did in 2013/14 around a similar (but smaller) scandal. Government Consumption slowed to 5.8% (vs 8.7% in 2Q). Capital Formation contracted 2.8% YoY in 3Q (Fig 2), driven by a 26% YoY collapse in government construction. Private construction still grew 13-14% YoY.

 

… but the slowdown in Consumption seems inconsistent with other data

What we had not expected was the sharp deceleration in Private Consumption to 4.1% (Fig 3). This was counter-intuitive, given a) much slower inflation (averaging 1.4% vs 3.2% in 3Q24), and b) increased government handouts and food subsidies since 2Q25.

Of the consumer companies we cover that have reported 3Q25 results, aggregate domestic topline growth has broadly been steady relative to 2Q25. Most striking, the official GDP report attributed the slowdown to a 7.5% contraction in the alcohol and tobacco sub-segments. However, the 3Q25 disclosures of two of the three largest liquor producers – Emperador Inc (EMI PM), and Ginebra San Miguel (GSMI) – reported revenue growth of “double digits” and 7%, respectively.

 

More “social spending” to offset infrastructure drag?

As we noted in our previous “flood control” report, it is reasonable to expect government-led infrastructure spending to continue to drag over the next 4-5 quarters, as the corruption investigations continue. However, we do expect freed up funds to be re-allocated to social programs to support the agriculture sector, education, healthcare, etc. This could help boost consumption.

 

BSP to the rescue (again?) – policy rate cut in December?

The weak GDP print could give the BSP reason to cut its policy rate by at least another 25bps at its final Monetary Board meeting of the year on 11 December. Technically, the BSP’s mandate is purely inflation-targeting. But inflation has been quite tame, coming in at 1.7% in October (Fig 4), flat MoM. Despite the 175bps worth of cuts since mid-2024 – the latest cut was in September, to 4.75% – real interest rates remain high (Fig 5). Before the GDP print, Regis and consensus had the BSP pausing in December. However, former BSP Governor and current MB member Benjamin Diokno has said he expects another rate cut in December, with potential further cuts in 2026.

 
SM Prime -- 3Q25 reservation sales rise QoQ and YoY; GDP growth slowdown not felt
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

3Q25 net profit rises 8% YoY

SM Prime’s 3Q25 net profit rose 8% YoY (flat QoQ) to P12.8bn, faster than revenue growth of 1% YoY, thanks to margin expansion of the mall business. As a result, 9M25 net profit rose 10% to P37.2bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Management said that it has not felt any negative impact from the flood control corruption scandal because that is more likely to affect high-end property, which is only a small portion of SM Prime’s market. However, SMPH acknowledged that the impact might be felt in subsequent quarters.

 

Mall revenue growth steady at 6% YoY

Although GDP growth slowed in 3Q25, SM Prime’s mall revenue still grew 6% YoY, the same pace as 2Q25. The company expanded its mall EBIT margin in 3Q25/9M25 (see Figure 2). Management attributes this to being able to rein in certain expenses, such as utilities (thanks to more solar panels on mall rooftops) and contracted services (e.g. janitorial, security, etc.)

 

Reservation sales rise QoQ and YoY

Reservation sales grew 4% QoQ and 79% YoY to P14.8bn (about 65% of the 2019 quarterly average), thanks to strong demand for ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units. Our channel checks suggest that if a buyer is willing to pay a 5% spot down payment, he/she can get a discount of about 15% and can move into the unit in about 3 months. 

This is likely why management said it was generally easier to sell RFO units and wants to accelerate completion of projects. Although unsold residential inventory fell 3% QoQ to P177bn, we estimate it still represents about four years of reservation sales (by value). This is likely why the company does not intend to launch any new mid-income condominium projects in Metro Manila. However, it plans to launch provincial projects as well as a luxury Metro Manila condominium building next year. Meanwhile, residential revenue declined 8% YoY to P11.7bn. The residential EBIT margin also fell for the second consecutive quarter, dropping 1.9ppt QoQ and 4.8ppt YoY to 37.8%.

 

Office revenue falls QoQ and YoY despite increase in occupancy

Office revenue fell 2% QoQ and YoY in 3Q25 (down 1% in 9M25) despite a modest improvement in occupancy (1ppt QoQ and 2ppt YoY to 80%). We suspect that some tenants are renewing contracts at rental rates that are lower than their previous contracts. This is likely to be most prevalent in the Manila Bay area.

 

Trading near record low valuations

SMPH is trading at 54% discount to NAV, 1.3X FY25E P/B, and 12.4X FY25E P/E, all of which are close to record lows.

 
Ayala Land -- 3Q25 profit falls 10% YoY; management lowers guidance
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M25 earnings stay flat

Ayala Land’s 3Q25 net profit fell 10% YoY to P7.2bn, due to a decline in development revenue. This brough 9M25 earnings to P21.4bn (-1%), slightly behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. While management expects 4Q growth to be better, it still reduced its FY25 earnings growth guidance to a minimum of 5%, from a minimum of 10% previously. The company also lowered some of its residential targets.

 

Residential reservation sales inch up QoQ despite corruption scandal

Despite the eruption of the flood control corruption scandal in 3Q25, reservation sales of both the core and premium residential segments rose 1%-2% QoQ. On a YoY basis, sales of the premium segment (which we believe are more likely to be negatively affected by the corruption scandal) fell 6%, while sales of the core segment climbed 40%, thanks to promotions on ready-for-occupancy units.

 

Managing unsold inventory levels

We estimate that unsold inventory fell c. 5% QoQ to P189bn. Furthermore, readyfor-occupancy (RFO) units now comprise only 8% of unsold inventory, from 9% in 2Q25 (12% in 1Q25). Management estimates that its inventory is now equivalent to about 20 months of reservation sales (from 23 months in 2Q25) and is targeting to bring this down to about 18 months by the end of the year.

Reservation sales and launch guidance lowered

The company lowered its targets for reservation sales and launches, due to a more subdued outlook on Metro Manila condominiums. It reduced its launch target from to P65bn-P68bn, from P100bn earlier this year. It also lowered its total reservation sales target (including commercial lots) to P135bn-P145bn, from c. P155bn previously (FY24A of P142bn).

 

Mall revenue growth to accelerate next year

9M25 mall revenue grew 4% (+3% YoY in 3Q) despite the redevelopment of its four flagship malls. Excluding the malls under renovation, 9M25 revenue rose 7%. With the redevelopment of two malls to be completed this year, ALI expects systemwide mall revenue to grow over 10% in 2026.

 

Office revenue grows 5% QoQ

Despite a slight (1ppt) uptick of vacancy QoQ (to 10%), office revenue grew 5% QoQ to P3.1bn. We suspect this is due to a combination of rental escalations as well as the lapse of rent-free periods for some tenants.

 

Hotel revenue rises QoQ and YoY

3Q25 hotel revenue rose 8% QoQ (+16% YoY) to P2.5bn, thanks to the acquisition of a new hotel as well as the reopening of some redeveloped hotels.

Robinsons Land -- 3Q25: mall segment outperforms but residential demand remains weak
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M25 recurring profit grows 10%

Robinsons Land’s 3Q25 net profit rose 19% to P3.3bn. As a result, 9M25 recurring profit grew 10% to P10.2bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. The mall segment outperformed our expectations, thanks to margin expansion. However, demand for RLC’s residential projects remains weak.

 

Mall revenue and margins expand

3Q25 mall revenue rose 7% QoQ (+14% YoY) despite no additional mall space and occupancy remaining flat at 94%. Management attributed this to stronger tenant sales, replacement of tenants that were not performing well, as well as conversion of some cinemas to retail spaces. Same-mall revenue growth stood at 7% in 3Q/9M, slightly above the midpoint of the historical average (of 4%-8%). RLC’s 3Q25 mall EBIT margin also expanded 1.6ppt QoQ and 3.8ppt YoY to 41.2%, largely thanks to managing utility costs.

 

Reservation sales remain weak

Demand for RLC’s standalone and JV residential projects remains weak. Reservation sales of standalone projects fell 63% QoQ to P860mn, only 17% of the 2019 average. The company is cautious about the Metro Manila mid-income condominium market and is managing its inventory level. 

The value of unsold residential inventory of its standalone projects is P45.3bn (-2% QoQ), which we estimate is equivalent to 5.5 years of reservation sales. Meanwhile, 3Q25 presales of JV projects climbed from P524mn in 2Q25 to P1.7bn (down 48% YoY). While this is a large improvement QoQ, it is only 35% of the 2019 average. Management said that buyers of high-end units (such as RLC’s JV projects) prefer large units but the unsold JV inventory (worth P16bn) is mostly comprised of smaller units (such as 1-bedroom units). Thus, the company is offering promotions on these smaller units to stimulate sales.

 

Office occupancy rises 1ppt QoQ

3Q25 office revenue rose 2% QoQ (+7% YoY) as occupancy increased 1ppt to 88%, higher than the Metro Manila average of 82%. However, we estimate the occupancy could fall to c. 85% by the end of the year, since the offices due for completion in 4Q25, which will add 13% to floor area, are only 60% preleased.

 

Trading at attractive valuations

RLC is currently trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY25E P/B, 5.6X FY25E P/E, and offers 5.1% dividend yield. Furthermore, the value of its stake in RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) is about 20% higher than RLC’s market cap.

9M25 net profit -8%, revenue +7%

Philippine Seven’s 9M25 profit fell 8% to P2.4bn. Revenues for the same period increased 7% to P69.1bn, while EBIT was only up 1% to P3.8bn. Overall results are behind REGIS expectations. In 3Q alone, profit fell 26% YoY to P600mn as EBIT declined 14% to P1.1bn. Revenue rose 5% to P22.6bn as systemwide sales were up a disappointing 2% to P23.4bn, despite +9% increase in store count. SSS declines further

3Q same-store sales continued to decline (-3.9%) (-0.5% in 2Q) amid reduced foot traffic due to weather disruptions as office work and schools faced suspensions. Consequently, 3Q revenues grew just 5% YoY to P22.6bn despite a 9% increase in store count and the benefit of a lower base in 2H24.Management also cited continued importation challenges involving a major vape brand that continued to hamper sales growth.

Meanwhile, demand for RTE (ready-to-eat) offerings picked up slightly in the quarter, which the company attributed to less downtrading by consumers.

 

Margins contract on expansion related expenses

EBIT and net margins both narrowed by ~1ppt in the quarter to 4.7% and 2.7%, respectively, as opex was higher primarily due to store rollout costs. The store rollout has yet to result in a corresponding increase in revenue. Margins also saw pressure from system development costs related to the new payment system recently launched as well as higher utilities and third-party service expenses.

 

Praying for better weather

Management has a more positive outlook for 4Q, banking on the expected better weather conditions and holiday spending boost. It should be noted that 4Q25 is still lapping a low YoY base of comparison, so the YoY growth % will appear more flattering.

9M25 core profit rebounds 117% YoY to P4.42bn – Behind estimates

CEB recorded a 3Q profit of P499mn in 3Q25, with an estimated core profit (i.e. excluding FX, one-offs) of P190mn. Profits were boosted by a tax benefit, as EBIT reversed to a loss of P122mn. Revenue growth decelerated sharply to 5% in 3Q from 23% in 1H due to the longer lean season. Although achieving our full-year forecast remains within reach, CEB’s 9M25 core profit of P4.4bn (+117% YoY) was softer than anticipated.

 

3Q25: Ancillary, wet lease, cargo lift revenues up 5%

Pax volumes barely grew in 3Q (+1%) due to the change in school calendars (started in mid-June this year vs. Aug in 2024), resulting in a mild dip (-1%) in average fares. Nevertheless, revenues rose 5% due to the 14%/24% increases in ancillary/cargo revenues. Ancillary yields jumped 16% YoY, underpinned by the 8% YoY improvement in international pax volumes and to a smaller extent, the wet lease agreement with Saudi-based Flyadeal.

EBITDA rises 11% YoY to P4.8bn in 3Q

Given lower fuel costs, EBITDA improved 11% YoY to P4.8bn, even as airport, fleet, and maintenance costs rose. However, management noted that the low base effect of higher airport costs should start fading in 4Q.

 

AOG issues worse than expected

To the downside, management expressed dismay over persistent AOG issues and has scaled back its seat growth target further to 10-12% (vs. initial 15-20%). It however believes favorable market dynamics should enable them to offset slower seat growth through higher fares.

 

Strong 4Q outlook; Dividends to commons seen returning by 1Q26

Management noted that its revenue per available seat kilometer (RASK) is up 5% YoY so far in 4Q, stronger than the 1% decline in 9M25. Likewise, average fares are up 11% YoY so far. Management conveyed optimism in resuming dividend payments to common shareholders once FY25 results are released (usually late March or early April), assuming operating conditions remain stable.

ICTSI's Mexico port volumes dip 3% YoY in October but prospects for expansion still positive

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), saw its throughput slip 3% YoY, 8% MoM to 136k TEUs in October. CMSA shed a bit of market share, as combined volumes at its three competitors in Manzanillo rose 3% YoY. Brings YT-October volumes at CMSA to 1.27mn TEUs, up 5% YoY and lagging our full-year forecast of 1.6mn TEUs (+12%). To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year will need to average 179k TEUs per month. Despite the volume shortfall, we remain positive on CMSA’s 12-month outlook, supported by the anticipated completion of its ~20% capacity expansion by year-end. Note that Mexico’s nationwide port volumes are up 3% YoY in 9M25, despite Trump’s tariffs; ICT’s port is well positioned to capture incremental volumes, given capacity limits of its competitors. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA.

Puregold 3Q 9M25 profit +6%, top line +11%

PGOLD’s 9M25 net profit grew 6% YoY to P7.3bn on top line growth of 11% to P168.1bn—profit is trailing behind forecasts, while top line is within expectations. In 3Q25 alone, profit increased by only 2% despite EBIT and net sales rising, 13% and 9%, respectively. Consolidated sales in the quarter rose 9% to P58.2bn primarily driven by continued momentum from the S&R store brand as it offset the deceleration seen in the Puregold format. 3Q SSSG for Puregold moderated to +1.6% from +6.7% in 2Q, while S&R SSSG quickened to +6.8% from +5.4% in 2Q. Despite the moderation in Puregold SSSG, its YTD growth of +4.8% in same-store sales as of 9M25 is still higher than the full-year SSSG figures seen for the format in the past three years.

DMCI: 3Q25 profits down across segments

9M25 recurring profit of P11.5bn (-23%) underperforms – Headline 9M25 profit fell 22% to P11.8bn. Excluding FX and one-offs, we estimate recurring profit stood at P11.5bn, down 23% YoY and lagging consensus’ FY25E of P18bn. Profits for 3Q25 alone fell 38% YoY/QoQ to P2.49bn, pulled down by lower thermal coal and spot prices at Semirara, as well as broad-based weakness across other key segments. Note that DMCI did not report contributions from its associate, Maynilad (MYNLD PM), which released results separately. Key performance of its businesses: (i) Property reservation sales rise 23% QoQ on RFO demand; (ii) Construction margins dip, despite 20% revenue growth; (iii) Nickel unit swings to loss in 3Q as weather disrupts shipments; (iv) Cement unit posts positive EBITDA in 3Q.

Maynilad's 9M25 capex rises 33% but trails commitment; Interim OCP rises to P165bn

Maynilad reported its 9M25 net income rose 18% to P11.4bn. In 3Q25 alone, profits jumped 16% YoY to P3.95bn, on the back of tariff hikes. Billed volumes slipped 1% YoY while NRW improved 440bps YoY to end at 35.9%. We estimate overall cash spending (capex, cash opex, con fees) rose 6% YoY to P9.1bn in 3Q25 and 23% YoY to P27.3bn in 9M25. Spending remains short of its regulatory commitment of ~P43bn per year. Cash opex surged 27% in 3Q25, taking 9M25 cash opex up 3% YoY. Capex (incl. con fees) rose 6% to P9.1bn in 3Q25 and 33% YoY to P27.3bn in 9M25. Maynilad estimates its interim OCP has risen to P165bn as of end-September 2025 from P145bn in end-2024 and P100bn in end-2022. The OCP increase largely reflects its 12% regulatory return (or Appropriate Discount Rate, ADR), as free cash flow generation in 9M25 was only a very small negative P189mn.

Aboitiz Power: 3Q EBITDA rebounds on hydro strength, lower WESM exposure

9M25 core profit drops 15% to P23.1bn, within consensus estimates. Aboitiz Power reported its 9M25 core income amounted to P23.1bn, down 15% YoY and appearing broadly within consensus full-year forecast of P30.6bn. Even though beneficial EBITDA jumped 12% YoY in 3Q25, derived 3Q25 profit rose a mere 3% to P10.4bn, as higher non-cash and interest charges from GNPD still weighed. EBITDA for its generation/RES unit jumped 12% YoY (+16% QoQ) to P20.1bn, ending a two-quarter downtrend, boosted by higher contributions from its hydro facilities and increased contracting levels (~90% in 3Q25 vs. 87% in end-June 2025 and 70% in 2024). EBITDA from its distribution unit (DU) rose 5% YoY to P2.5bn in 3Q25 and 4% YoY to P7bn in 9M25. Sales volumes grew at a faster pace of 6% in 3Q from 4% in 1H, thanks to new mall openings and a rebound in the steel sector. Residential volumes also rose by 5% in 3Q25, slightly accelerating from the 2% pace in 2Q25.

AGI 3Q25 profit falls QoQ and YoY

Alliance Global said that its 9M25 core attributable profit grew 8% to P13.9bn, in line with. We estimate that 3Q25 core attributable profit fell c. 40% QoQ and 20% YoY, largely due to Emperador. Megaworld’s 3Q25 operating profit rose 9% YoY (-2% QoQ) to P7.4bn, largely thanks to the increase in rental revenue. Travellers’ 3Q25 operating profit grew 25% YoY (flat QoQ) to P1.6bn, thanks to largely to lower cash costs as well as higher mass GGR. 3Q25 GGR rose 7% QoQ, thanks to the 10% increase in mass GGR. Emperador's 3Q25 EBITDA fell 18% YoY (-40% QoQ) to P1.8bn, primarily due to declining topline and lower gross margins for both the brand and whisky segments. Both segments saw a drop in sales in the quarter with brandy sales down 6% YoY to P7.8bn while whisky revenues fell 17% YoY to P5.2bn. Gross margins for both segments contracted mainly due to product mix with brandy's continued value brands push and whisky's continued softness in single-malt whiskeys. The company said that proceeds from the 2.2bn warrants (P0.50/warrant) as well as the potential issuance of new shares (at P12/share exercise price) would be used to fund capex and reduce debt. Total proceeds could reach P27.5bn if all the warrants are exercised. AGI is exploring new businesses and is also looking to infuse equity into its existing subsidiaries.

Nickel Asia: Strong nickel ore prices lift 9M25 core profit up 72%

NIKL reported 9M25 profits doubled to P5.2bn. Removing the FX adjustment relating to the divestment of Coral Bay HPAL in 1Q, we estimate recurring profit stood at P4.4bn, up 72% YoY and trending well ahead of our forecast of P4.7bn and exceeding consensus full-year estimate of P4.3bn. In 3Q25 alone, core profit more than doubled to P3.1bn, driven solely by a 48% YoY improvement in mining revenues. EBITDA jumped 71% YoY to P6.3bn, a new quarter high. The mining unit maintained its recovery momentum after weather-related disruptions in 1Q, with sales volumes up 12% YoY in 3Q25, reversing the 4% drop in 1H. ASPs also strengthened by 12% QoQ, 33% YoY to US$31/WMT, bringing 9M25 ASPs up 38% to US$27/WMT. EBITDA for the power segment (including non-RE facilities) fell 39% YoY to P110mn in 3Q25, bringing 9M25 EBITDA down 20% YoY to P518mn.

JGS Writing Off Petrochem Business

JG Summit plans to write off JGS Olefins Corp (aka JG Petrochem), after more than two decades of losses. The write-off will mean an estimated impairment loss of P114.bn (see below). This is equivalent to 30% of JGS’ book value per share so the impairment will reduce JGS’ book value per share from ~P50/share to around P35/share. The positive take on this is that JG Summit will significantly reduce the cash losses from the business until it is able to sell or completely shut it down. We understand JGS will still need to spend P100-P150m/month just to keep the plants in such a condition for it to be eventually sold.

Marcos seeks to ramp up gov't spending in 4Q

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has stated that "public spending will now be increased" to ensure that spending levels are aligned with the original programmed P1.31trn disbursement allocated for 4Q. The President seeks to recover from the disappointing 3Q GDP growth of 4% through accelerating infrastructure and social sector disbursements in the final quarter of the year. Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vince Dizon stated that multiple projects will resume before year-end as construction will ramp up "but with safeguards". P9.5bn was said to be allocated for conditional cash transfers.

Budget committee to revise debt-to-GDP and GDP growth projections; retains fiscal deficit target

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC)—the gov't committee in charge of macro, revenue, budget projections—for 2025, has retained its fiscal deficit projection of 5.5% but revised the debt-to-GDP target of 61.3% to 63%. The current debt-to-GDP ratio is 63.1% as of 3Q25, unchanged from the previous quarter but higher than the 60.1% in 3Q25. Senator Sherwin Gatchalian, head of the Senate finance committee, notes that the figure is slightly better than ASEAN peers (~70% debt-to-GDP). Gatchalian highlighted that the DBCC is targeting to restore the debt to GDP ratio to its pre-pandemic levels especially given the current trajectory which was exacerbated by the disappointing 3Q GDP print of 4.0%. The DBCC is also set to deliberate possible revisions on its current growth targets of 5.5-6.5% for 2025 and 6-7% for 2026-2028. GDP growth expectations for consensus are currently at 5.4% and 5.7% for 2025 and 2026, respectively. Regis expectations are at 4.8% and 5.5% for 2025 and 2026.

Tax bureau likely to miss 2025 collection target

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Commissioner Romeo Lumagui guided that the P3.22trn (+13% YoY) collection target this year will likely not be met. Lumagui stated that there are ongoing discussions to reduce the BIR's collection target for the year. Overall BIR collections as of 9M25 rose 11% to P2.32trn which is 2.5% behind the P2.38trn expected collections for the period. He stated that "overall performance is low" dragged by sluggish gov't spending which weighed on overall tax receipts. Collections across the categories (e.g., VAT, income, excise taxes) YTD were lagging full-year targets. Excise tax collections were at P251bn as of 9M25, 1% lower than the P254 goal for the period. There has been improvements, however, YoY with cigarette excise taxes up 26% to P106 while e-cigarette taxes surged to P2.1bn from P449mn.

Marcos implements flexible rice tariff starting next year

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. Signed Executive Order (EO 105), modifying the import duty on rice depending on global rice prices. Starting Jan 1, 2026, Most Favored Nation (MFN) rice tariffs are to be adjusted based on global rice prices. The duty will increase by 5ppt for every 5ppt drop in the int'l rice price or decrease 5ppt for every 5ppt rise—the MFN tariff cannot fall below 15% or exceed 35%. The current 15% base tariff (which has been lowered from 35% originally back in June 2024) will be maintained when imports resume in Jan, with a newly established Inter-Agency Group on Rice Tariff Adjustment to set guidelines on future tariff changes. Agriculture groups commented that they were not supportive of the new EO as they argue that the tariff rate should be based on local not int'l rice prices, they also highlighted the possibility that tariff adjustments could encourage more speculative behavior and smuggling.

BSP eyes to implement 24/7 payment system by 2026

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Deputy Governor Mamerto Tangonan stated that the central bank plans to expand the Philippine Payment and Settlement System (PhilPaSS) Plus operations to run 24/7. The new 24/7 real-time gross settlement system will be able to settle more transactions in real-time which may include (remittances, cross-border e-commerce flows, bond trading, etc.). The PhilPaSS Plus system, owned and operated by the BSP, is currently only accessible from 9:00am to 5:45pm during weekdays.

E-games GGR falls 28% QoQ; GGR of licensed casinos rises 3% QoQ

PAGCOR reported that 3Q25 GGR of the e-games (including bingo) sector fell 28% QoQ (up 14% YoY) to P45.7bn. The BSP prohibited from redirecting to online gaming platforms beginning August 17. E-games GGR (including bingo) for the month of September stood at P12.2bn. Meanwhile, GGR of licensed casinos rose 3% QoQ to P45.6bn.

New BIR chief appointed by Marcos

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. appointed Finance Undersecretary Charlito Mendoza as the new commissioner of the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), replacing Romeo Lumagui. Mendoza, prior to his current appointment, heads the Dept. of Finance's supervisory group that oversees the BIR and Bureau of Customs (BOC). He also served as customs district collector for Cebu and was a practicing lawyer prior to joining the gov't. Lumagui was appointed as BIR Chief back in Nov 2022 by President Marcos. A day prior to being replaced, he had ordered a "major personnel overhaul" across the BIR's regional offices. Last month, the BIR under Marcos' orders had begun conducting lifestyle checks on gov't officials.
