REGIS REPORT

28 November 2025

The PCOMP took a breather after last week’s sharp surge, but still managed to end modestly higher by 0.4% or 25.11 pts to 6,022.24, repeatedly testing the 6,000 and finally finishing the week above the psychological handle. This is also its highest weekly close in six weeks. Local buying remained the steady source of support throughout the week.

 

The MSCI rebalancing at the start of the week brought elevated volumes, with traders seen taking advantage of the expected heavy outflows expected on BPI (+3.1% WoW) to accumulate the stock. Despite its downweight, BPI was still among the biggest positive movers for the PCOMP this week.

 

Mid-week action centered on the AREIT (-1.2% WoW) $70-m placement which involved ALI (-3.1% WoW) selling 100m AREIT shs at P41.90/sh. Activity thinned significantly with daily turnover slipping below $100m later in the week, as cautious sentiment prevailed while domestic catalysts were lacking. Even so, the market’s pullbacks were contained as locals continued to buy dips in banks, property, and select consumer names.

 

Sectors ended mixed, with Holdings (+3.4% WoW) outperforming after financials and property names drove most of the market’s advance the previous week. AC (+8.4% WoW) and SM (+1.6% WoW) were also among the larger contributors to the index's weekly gain, while AEV (+7.7% WoW) and GTCAP (+6.1% WoW) also led advancers.

 

Banks (+0.6% WoW) and Property (+0.3% WoW) ended only slightly higher, while consumer performance was mixed, with URC (-6.0% WoW) and JFC (-5.4% WoW) notable laggards, whereas PGOLD (+4.5% WoW) and CNPF (+4.1% WoW) outperformed.

 

In the macro space, news flow remained relatively quiet. Investors weighed comments from newly appointed Finance Secretary Frederick Go about potential revisions to revenue targets and budget adjustments -- fueling concerns about near- to medium-term growth -- alongside BSP Governor Eli Remolona reiterating that the central bank is considering a 25-bp rate cut in December -- which in turn would support a more constructive backdrop for risk assets.

 

Foreigners were net sellers by $55m. Peso stronger by 0.4% to 58.635:$1.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property - 3Q25 recap: Residential reservation sales improve despite corruption scandal
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A mixed bag in 3Q25

There were a few noteworthy items during the property sector’s 3Q25 results season. First, despite concerns that the flood control corruption scandal would weaken residential demand, reservation sales still grew on a QoQ and YoY basis. Second, contrary to the slowdown in GDP growth (including personal consumption), mall revenue grew at the same pace. On the other hand, net office take-up fell due to weaker demand from both BPO firms and traditional tenants.

 

Residential reservation sales improve for 3rd quarter in a row

Aggregate reservation sales of the top four developers grew 8% QoQ (+20% YoY) to P85bn (c. 85% of the 2019 average), marking the third consecutive quarter of improvement. Despite concerns that the flood control corruption scandal would be particularly detrimental to high-end residential demand, sales of premium projects still grew QoQ. Meanwhile, similar to 2Q25, multiple developers cited strong take-up of ready-for-occupancy units, thanks to promotions such as cash discounts of up to 50% as well as rent-to-own schemes of up to 10 years. While we consider the sale of RFO units to be a positive sign, we refrain from being overly bullish since unsold inventory levels remain high. Based on our estimates, the unsold inventory levels of some developers represent over four years of reservation sales (based on cumulative sales over the past 12 months).

Office take-up declines

Although office vacancy remained flat QoQ (at 18%), BPO firms and traditional tenants both took less incremental space in 3Q25 (see Figures 5 to 8). While there are concerns that the “Keep Call Centers in America” and the “Halting International Relocation of Employment (HIRE)” bills will significantly curtail outsourcing to the Philippines, our channel checks suggest that they are not major concerns for BPO firms and the decline in net BPO office take-up in 3Q25 is coincidental. In fact, multiple BPO firms continue to inquire about office space in 4Q25. Spot rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ but remain 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. However, rental rates in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio inched up QoQ, since vacancy rates in those areas are lower. Furthermore, we would expect most contracts in those areas to be renewed at higher rental rates, in contrast to many other districts, where rental rates are still being renewed at lower rental rates.

 

Mall revenue growth maintains pace despite weak GDP growth

Although real GDP growth (including personal consumption) slowed in 3Q25, Philippine mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators still grew 8%, the same pace as 2Q25. This implies that same-mall-revenue growth was likely still around 6%, the mid-point of the historical range (of 4%-8%).

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land sells AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 100mn AREIT shares at P41.90/share. This is 4% lower than the previous closing price (of P43.65/share). At this price, we estimate that AREIT would offer FY26E dividend yield of c.6.0%. Total of P4.19bn. We estimate that AREIT’s public ownership will rise from 33% to 36%. We estimate that the Ayala Group would need to sell another c. 50mn AREIT shares (over the next 12 months) in order for AREIT to meet minimum public ownership requirements. At the last closing price, this would be equivalent to c. P2.1bn. AREIT will acquire P19.5bn worth of assets from Ayala Land in exchange for 441mn new AREIT shares. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 15% to P158bn. AREIT’s shares outstanding will increase by 12% to 4.16bn shares. This implies that the AREIT shares will be priced at P44.15/share. This is 1% higher than the last closing price of P43.65/share. AREIT will acquire two malls with a combined GLA of 375,000sqm. Ayala Center Cebu and Ayala Malls Feliz. The companies aim to complete the transaction in 2H26.

ICTSI to sign Durban deal next month; Takeover in April

Transnet said that it plans to sign the concession agreement for the Durban Container Terminal 2 (DCT-2) with ICTSI next month. According to Transnet Chairman Andile Sangqu. ICTSI will be “onboarded: at the facility from April 2026. We have not incorporated the DCT-2 in our estimates and valuation. However, our preliminary calculations indicate a potential P30/sh uplift to our NAV of P560/sh (here). ICTSI trades at 10x FY26E EV/EBITDA, below peer average (12x) and historical average of 11x.

ICTSI's port in Melbourne raises landside port charges; domestic port in Manila adds new quay cranes

ICTSI is raising landside port charges (including storage) at its port in Melbourne, Australia (“Victoria International Container Terminal” or VICT) by ~10%. New charges will take effect by January 1, 2026. In 2026, VICT will embark on Phase 3B expansion (capacity increase of over 20%) with an investment of >AUD$100mn. Meanwhile, its domestic port, Northport, has acquired two new harbor cranes as part of its capacity expansion. Recall that Northport has applied for a 20% tariff increase with the Philippine Ports Authority in August. We estimate VICT and Northport account for 7% and 5%, respectively, of EBITDA. Tariff hikes and capacity expansions are baked into our estimates and valuation.

ICTSI Batam begins integration of operations with state-run port

ICTSI’s Batu Ampar Container Terminal (BACT) will officially merge operations with state-run Batam Terminal Petikemas starting Dec 1. We estimate the integration will allow BACT to increase market share in Batam to 70% from 45% when it took over operations last Sep.

BPI expects consumer loan growth to ease in 2026

The Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI PM) is expecting consumer lending growth to slow next year as a weaker macroeconomic backdrop and geopolitical risks temper demand. For 2026, BPI expects its consumer loan book to grow by 10-15%, a slowdown from the 28% growth so far this year. As of 9M25, BPI's total noninstitutional loan portfolio is at P750bn. The bank plans to keep its loan mix at 30% for this segment next year. BPI Consumer Banking head Maria Cristina Go stated that growth could have been stronger but noted that the bank is taking a cautiously optimistic stance after the softer-than-expected 3Q GDP print of 4.0%. She said that the BSP's rate cut cycle could spur loan activity but she also noted that consumer confidence will be critical which will impact their appetite to spend (e.g., travel, new house/car, credit card spending).

SM Prime to launch luxury residential project

Despite concerns regarding the flood control corruption scandal, SM Prime said that it would still launch its luxury residential project in Susana Heights. It will be the maiden project under the luxury line, The Signature Series. Lots will start at P100mn with no parcel smaller than 750sqm. This implies a price of c. P133,000/sqm. The company will spend P25bn to develop the initial phase of the 284-hectare property. SM Prime’s 9M25 reservation sales fell 23% to P40.7bn.

Meralco eyes backdoor listing of MGreen, SPNEC upon TerraSolar completion or in 2027

Meralco and SPNEC Chairman, President and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan said the backdoor listing may have to wait until 2027. Re-IPO is contingent on the completion of the Terra Solar project, whose first phase will likely be energized by Aug–Nov 2026. Meralco is currently eyeing to transfer its renewable energy projects into SPNEC, which may happen in 2026.

Meralco hikes distribution unit capex to P26-30bn in 2026

Meralco is set to spend P26-30bn for its distribution business next year. According to Meralco executive VP and COO Ronnie Aperocho in an ambush interview. Meralco plans to spend P25bn for the distribution segment in 2025 and as of 9M25, it has spent P19.8bn. Around P8bn of the 2026 capex program relates to the roll-out of advanced metering infrastructure.

DigiPlus sees gradual recovery

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) said that its customer service team has been reaching out to its high value players to try to bring them back to PLUS’ platform. 3Q25 revenue of DigiPlus fell 23% QoQ (flat YoY) to P19.1bn, while net profit declined 59% QoQ (down 51% YoY) to P1.7bn. The BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gaming platforms (beginning August 17). DigiPlus President Andy Tsui said that the company was “…seeing a gradual recovery from the last two months”. Industry data from PAGCOR showed that September GGR of Bingo + e-games was P12.2bn, a 45% drop from the June figure. If the September GGR level were to be maintained in 4Q25, then GGR for the quarter would amount to P36.6bn, another 20% lower than the 3Q25 level.

DigiPlus partners with Pay&Go

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) announced a “strategic partnership” with Pay&Go, one of the leading providers of self-service payment kiosks in the country. Users of DigiPlus platforms will be able to fund their wallets in the 3,500 self-service kiosks. A cashout feature is being eyed for 2026. Pay&Go is a self-service payment kiosk network operated by BTI Payment Philippines, a subsidiary of Australia-based The Banktech Group.

Cebu Air pax volumes, load factors slide in October

Cebu Air reported its pax volumes dipped by 1% YoY to 2.1mn in October. Seat load factors also declined 7.4ppts YoY to 71.4%. Management cited ongoing capacity challenges related to Pratt & Whitney, as well as weather-related disruptions. It also said that moderating capacity growth during the lean season was intended to ensure operational resilience ahead of the peak travel season (Dec/Jan). Brings YT-October pax volumes up 12% to 22mn, lagging our full-year forecast of 28.5mn (+16%). During its 3Q briefing, management indicated that stronger fares would help offset softer volumes and hinted at the potential resumption of common-share dividend payments by March/April.

FGEN weighs delisting

First Gen CEO and Chairman Federico Lopez said that the group was open to privatizing FGEN if the opportunity arose. However, it is not in a rush to delist FGEN. FGEN has a public float of 12%.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP considering 25bp cut in Dec; RRR cut not urgent

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the central bank will consider another 25bp rate cut in Dec given that "demand is affected, just like investor sentiment." He noted that a jumbo 50bp cut is unlikely. Remolona also elaborated that a reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cut is "on the table… but there's no urgency in adjusting it." The RRR is currently at 5.0%. The ratio was 9.5% at the start of Remolona's term in 2023. On forex intervention, the BSP Governor stated that the central bank lets the market determine the exchange rate as it does not have a specific target peso level for it to intervene. Intervention was guided to only be done for "large swings in the peso that could lead to inflation." The peso depreciated from P58.15/USD on Oct 1 to P58.98/USD as of Nov 24. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.50%. For 2026, consensus expects a 25bp cut to 4.25%, while Regis expects 50bp cuts total to 4.00%.

Finance Secretary expects gov't revenue target revisions and budget adjustments

Dept. of Finance Secretary Frederick Go said revenue target revisions and budget adjustments need to be made, as the ongoing corruption probe has weighed on economic activity and put collection goals at risk. Recall that the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and Bureau of Customs (BOC) have been guided to likely to miss their targets due to lower remittances from the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and slower import growth. Go emphasized that corrective actions need to be made (i.e., optimized spending, identifying areas where savings can be achieved) to address current gov't fiscal challenges. Executive Secretary Ralph Recto stated that the Budget Committee will review its macroeconomic targets and assumptions in Dec. As of 9M25, revenue collections are up 2% to P3.37trn (74.5% of the gov't's P4.52 trn goal for 2025).

Government posts P11bn budget surplus in Oct; P1.11trn deficit as of 10M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget surplus of P11bn in Oct, 76% wider YoY on sluggish revenue growth and slower gov't spending. This brings the 10M25 deficit to P1.11trn, 15% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was said to be in line with the P1.56trn full-year program. The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues. Revenue collections in Oct declined 7% YoY, dragged by poor tax and customs collections. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were only up by 1%. Recall that former BIR commissioner Romeo Lumagui previously stated that there was a slowdown in revenue collection given the ongoing probe on the Dept. of Public Works and Highways' (DPWH) flood control issues. Customs collections fell 5%, reversing from the 2% increase in Sep due to lower import volumes and fewer working days in the month. Non-tax revenues dipped 53% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024. Expenditures decreased by 8% YoY in Oct due to the decline in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 9%. Similar to the previous three months, the gov't spending decline in Oct was primarily caused by the ongoing flood control probe. Interest payments rose 4% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

S&P reaffirms Philippines' investment grade credit rating

S&P Global Ratings affirmed the Philippines’ investment grade credit rating of BBB+ with a “positive” outlook as growth prospects are noted to remain strong despite the corruption scandal weighing on the economy this year. BBB+ is a notch below “A”-level grade and the positive outlook implies the Philippines’ credit rating can be raised within 24 months if improvements are sustained.

Infrastructure spending slump persists in September

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) fell 9% YoY to P1.04tn in 9M25. This implies that infrastructure spending contracted by 35% YoY and 5% MoM to P100bn for the month of September alone. Cited suspension of payments for public works amid ongoing validation and audit related to corruption concerns. DPWH Secretary Vince Dizon lifted the suspension of procurement last September 16. Cumulative 9M25 spending of P1.04tn lags the full-year target of P1.5tn (-2%).

Philippine ports' foreign container volume growth moderates to 7% in 3Q25 on slower exports

Data from the Philippine Ports Authority showed that nationwide foreign container volumes grew by 7% in 3Q25, moderating from the 12% pace in 1H25. Growth decelerated largely due to moderating export volume growth of +8% in 3Q25 from +14% in 1H25. Import volumes rose 7% in 3Q25, mildly accelerating from the 6% rate in 2Q25. This brings 9M25 nationwide foreign container volumes up 11% YoY. ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) also saw volume growth decelerate to 4% in 3Q25 from 6% in 2Q25 and 10% in 1H25. MICT’s market share loss persisted, after South Harbour of Asian Terminals Inc (ATI PM) saw volumes increase by +9% in 3Q25 (vs. +21% in 1H25). Note that ATI expanded South Harbour’s capacity by over 40% to 2mn TEUs in May. We believe ATI’s market share gains will be short-lived, as MICT’s Berth 8 Phase 2 expansion should be completed in 2027. MICT’s cumulative 9M25 volumes are up 8% YoY to 2.3mn TEUs, broadly in line with our FY25E of 3.1mn TEUs (+6%).

Philippine sugar output projected to remain flat next year

The US Dept. of Agriculture (USDA) estimated that local raw sugar production for the 2025-2026 crop year would remain unchanged from the 2.09mn metric tons (MT) output in current crop year 2024-2025. The flat projection stemmed from the negative impact of the excess rainfall and ongoing red-striped soft scale insect (RSSI) infestation affecting sugar plantations. According to the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA), the estimated area affected by the RSSI infestation is less than 2% of the total area planted to sugarcane. Total sugarcane area in the country is expected to grow 2% to 400,000 hectares from 392,000 hectares this year. Sugar consumption was reported to likely remain steady at 2.2mn MT. The USDA is expecting zero sugar exports and no raw sugar importation as the gov't is set to prioritize and protect the local industry.

Semiconductor exports expected to grow 5-7% this year

The Semiconductor and Electronics Industries in the Philippines Foundation Inc. (SEIPI) expects electronic exports to grow 5-7% this year. The 5-7% growth rate was revised from flat growth as the threat of US semiconductor tariffs has subsided. Reuters reported that Trump's semiconductor tariff plan was likely delayed, with no set date for implementation. SEIPI President Danilo Lachica stated that semiconductors are still exempt from tariffs under the US Trade Expansion Act of 1962. As of 9M25, semiconductor exports amounted to P25.1bn (+8% YoY). Semiconductor exports declined 8% and 6% in 2023 and 2024, respectively. In 2024, total PH exports to the US were $12.1bn. Semiconductors and electronics make up around 53% of Philippine exports to the US. Most locally made semiconductors are manufactured by US companies and exported.

Recto to resign from Monetary Board by end-Dec; Go named as successor

Executive Secretary Ralph Recto indicated that he will step down from the Monetary Board (MB) by end-Dec and will recommend Finance Secretary Frederick Go as his replacement. To ensure a smooth transition, Recto said he will participate in the final MB meeting this year, which will take place on Dec 11.

OSAPIEA to disband after Go's appointment to the DOF

President Marcos will disband the Office of the Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (OSAPIEA) following the reassignment of Secretary Frederick Go to the Dept. of Finance (DOF). Palace Press Officer Claire Castro elaborated that the dissolvement of the OSAPIEA is to remove redundancies as the office was created to enhance inter-agency coordination and ensure effective integration and implementation of the gov't's investment and economic policies and programs. Go's transition as the Finance Secretary was said to still put him in a position to work closely with the Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) and the Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI).

Two new directors elected to the PSE board

The Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE) has elected Cecile Ang and Jaime Bautista as nonbroker director and independent director, respectively, following the recent resignation of two directors. Cecile Ang, VP of New Naia Infrastructure Corp., replaces Ferdinand Constantino, former SMC CFO who retired earlier this year.
