REGIS REPORT

09 May 2025

We close out a data-heavy week that had investors mull over the 1Q25 GDP disappointment, the April inflation beat, and a mixed bag of 1Q25 corporate earnings reports. The PCOMP managed to close higher by 0.7% or 46.34 pts WoW to 6,458.20, extending its rally to a fourth week.

 

ICT (+7.4% WoW) was the biggest positive mover for the index, climbing back past the P380-mark to levels last seen before Trump's 'Liberation Day' tariff pronouncements in April. 1Q earnings bore resilient results with double-digit organic volume growth, yield improvement, and a strong bounce in EBITDA margin. Investors also seemed comforted by management's comments that no demand pull-forward was noted in 1Q and into 2Q so far, and their confidence over how US-China trade conflicts could benefit some of its ports as supply chains reshuffle.

 

BLOOM (+18.7% WoW) was the week's top gainer as optimism over the anticipated launch of its enhanced online gaming platform pushed shares sharply higher another week. BLOOM has already gained 47% in a four-week rally from its recent April low. Second biggest gainer URC (+8.9% WoW) also kept on a three-week winning streak, boosted by a strong 1Q earnings beat that still saw a slight YoY contraction in net income for the period. Similarly, URC is now up 50% from its January low. Consumer names PGOLD (+5.8% WoW) and CNPF (+5.4% WoW) also rallied on the back of solid 1Q results.

 

Beyond cues from earnings, investors bought up consumer stocks as April inflation continued to slow to 1.4% -- a strong beat vs Street's 1.8% estimate and also a big deceleration from the 1.8% print in March. This took YTD-April inflation to 2.0% which tracks lower vs the FY25 consensus forecast of 2.8%. Following the inflation release, BSP Gov. Remolona stated that he is open to 75 bps more in rate cuts for 2025, against Street expectations of 50 bps in rate cuts this year.

 

On the other hand, 1Q GDP missed forecasts, clocking at 5.4% vs the median estimate of 5.7%. Strong growth in private and public consumption was offset by the deterioration in net imports and soft investment growth. Still, Finance Sec. Recto said government remains confident in hitting its 6-7% target range, which would imply growth needs to hit at least 6.2% for the rest of the year.

 

Philippine markets are closed for mid-term elections on Monday, May 12th, which will determine half of the Senate along with many national and local government posts.

 

Foreigners remained net buyers this week by $31m. ADTO continued to improve to $123m.

 

The peso appreciated by another +0.2% WoW to 55.480:$1. Yields declined slightly with the PH10Y -11.7bps to 6.1438%.

ICTSI - 1Q25 profits off to a good start despite trade conflicts
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q25 core profit up 28% YoY to US$236mn – In line with Street estimates

ICT’s profits after perps distributions rose 16% YoY to US$236mn in 1Q25. Adjusting for last year’s one-offs, profit growth was a robust 28% on the back of revenue growth of 17%. The results look on trend to meet Street estimates but fell a tad short of our forecasts due to higher-than-expected cash costs. Management provided early 2Q indications and reiterated views that trade war/s will have minimal impact on operations.

 

Organic volume gr accelerates to 12% on strong demand, market share gains

Organic volume growth accelerated to 12% in 1Q25 from the ~5% pace in previous quarters, following (a) strong underlying demand at its ports in the EMEA region and the Philippines, as well as (b) market share gains in Ecuador, which added 2ppts to group volume growth in the quarter. Management reiterated that it has not seen any evidence of demand pullforward at any of its ports in 1Q25 and even in 2Q25 so far and continues to see “balanced” growth across its portfolio, despite Trump’s “Liberation Day” tariffs kicking in April. 

Further, management believes the trade war between the USA and China could reconfigure supply chains, which may benefit its ports in the medium-to long-term.

 

Yields improve 4% YoY to US$209

Yield per TEU improved 2% QoQ and 4% YoY to US$209 boosted by the implementation of tariff hikes in Manila and volume improvements in the PH and PNG. These factors more than compensated for unfavorable FX movements of the BRL/MXP against the USD and slightly lower storage revenues.

 

EBITDA margin rebounds to 65.7%; EBITDA jumps 18% to US$490mn

After two quarters of declining trend, 1Q25 EBITDA margin bounced 190bps QoQ and 80bps YoY to 65.7%. This led to an 18% YoY improvement in EBITDA to US$490mn, which can be partly attributed to the volume recovery at the port of Ecuador, which has high operating leverage. Meanwhile, the shipyard acquisition in Rio, Brazil announced this quarter should also boost near-term margins and provide the company with ample space for expansions.

 
Ayala Land - Commercial lots come to the rescue in 1Q25
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q25 net profit rises 10% YoY

Ayala Land’s 1Q25 net profit rose 10% YoY to P6.9bn. While this only represents 22% of our FY25E profit forecast, we would consider this to be in line since earnings are seasonally stronger in 2H. ALI saw some weakness in both residential revenue and presales but these were largely offset by the strong commercial lot segment. Although 1Q25 residential revenue growth was muted (at 3% YoY), the bottom line was lifted by commercial lot revenue, which doubled YoY.

 

Premium horizontal projects see better presales

Residential presales fell 6% YoY but was up 17% QoQ to P31.2bn, also in line with our forecast. The YoY drop came from (“core”) mid-income Metro Manila vertical projects while premium horizontal projects performed better on a QoQ and YoY basis (see Figure 3). We estimate that the company’s P200bn in unsold inventory represents c. 2 years’ worth of presales.

 

Management more optimistic on commercial lots

Commercial lot presales grew 233% YoY to P5bn. This brought the total reservation sales up 4% YoY (+10% QoQ) to P36.2bn. ALI will have to grow total reservation sales more rapidly in order to achieve its FY target of 12% growth. In addition to management’s tone, its launch plans reveal that it is more optimistic about the commercial lot segment over the residential one. 

It intends to launch only P80bn worth of residential projects (same as FY24), lower than the FY24 reservation sales of P127bn. This suggests that management is cautious on the residential segment and aims to reduce unsold inventory levels. On the other hand, Ayala Land plans to launch P20bn worth of commercial lots this year, higher than the P15bn of sales in FY24.

 

Mall revenue grows 4% YoY despite redevelopments

Mall revenue grew 4% YoY despite the redevelopment of ALI’s flagship malls, which dragged the 1Q25 performance. The malls under redevelopment saw a 5% decline in revenue while the rest of the portfolio saw an 11% increase. The company plans to finish the redevelopments of Trinoma and Ayala Center Cebu by 3Q25, about six months ahead of the previous plan.

 

Office occupancy falls 1ppt QoQ

Office occupancy fell 1ppt QoQ to 90%, which is still much better than the c. 80% of the industry. The decline was due to new buildings and a POGO closure.

 

Hotel revenue rises 10% YoY despite some hotel closures

Hotel revenue rose 10% YoY despite the closure of three hotels (due to renovations). Excluding these hotels, revenue rose 25% YoY.

 
Century Pacific Food - 1Q25: Branded segment lifts top line
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1Q25 net profit up 11% YoY

CNPF’s profit in 1Q25 amounted to P1.9bn (+11% YoY), in line with both Regis and consensus expectations. 1Q25 net sales were up 10% YoY to P19.9bn for the quarter, driven by the branded segment (+13% YoY). Both gross and EBITDA margins were stable only moving +10 bps and -20 bps, respectively, with no big movements in input costs for the quarter.

 

Branded sales quicken in 1Q

The branded business saw good growth momentum as it grew +7% QoQ and +13% YoY. The increase in revenues was seen across all categories and driven by volume growth, with the core Marine segment leading the way. Management noted that easing inflation and effective promotions boosted sales. Market share gains in Mar ’25 vs. Dec ’24 were seen in Dairy (+70 bps) and Meat (+10 bps), while Tuna (-120 bps) saw a decline from its value brands.

 

OEM topline growth normalizes

The OEM business (19% of revenue in 1Q25) saw a slight dip in revenues, -2% YoY to P3.8bn. Although there was sustained demand for OEM products, revenue dipped given the high base as sales spiked in the first quarter last year. 

Note that 1Q24 OEM revenue was up as much as 49% YoY.

 

Margins hold in 1Q but pressure looms

1Q gross margin remained relatively unchanged (+10 bps) at 26.2% as favorable input costs carried over from the prior year. EBITDA margin for the quarter dipped slightly YoY by 20 bps to 14.2% given forex movements.

 

Management, however, is flagging margin pressures (100-200 bps) that are likely to be visible from 2Q given the recent rise in input costs (i.e., milk, tuna, and coconut). Tuna and milk prices have been normalizing (higher) from lows last year, while coconut raw materials jumped on high global demand and constrained supply from the strong El Niño last year. The company, however, indicated it will manage opex to keep EBITDA margins stable.

 

Company reaffirms low to mid-teens guidance

The company reiterated its guidance of “low to mid-teens” growth for both top and bottom lines. The dairy segment, which saw headwinds last year due to elevated inflation, is back on track in capturing market share (+70 bps). While the emerging categories of coconut products, plant-based protein, and pet food are scaling and seeing strong growth momentum.

 
Puregold - 1Q25: Healthy top line expansion
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1Q profits increase 6% on top line growth of 11%

Puregold (PGOLD) net profit for 1Q25 rose 6.5% to P2.6bn (+6% YoY), in line with Regis and consensus expectations. Revenue growth for the retailer was a rather healthy 11%, taking 1Q25 sales to P52.4bn. The company was able to keep gross margins unchanged at 19.6%. Operating margins saw a slight decline to 7.6% (-20bp), due to pre-operating expenses for new stores.


Double-digit revenue growth

Group sales were up 11% YoY for the quarter with Puregold stores posting +9% and S&R delivering 13% growth. Puregold and S&R both posted notable YoY improvements in SSSG in 1Q25, accelerating to 5.9% from 1.5% and to 4.0% from 1.2%, respectively. Foot traffic was the primary driver for top line growth in the quarter as Puregold saw 9.7% growth in footfall while S&R foot traffic saw a 24.8% jump. The higher visitor count offset the -0.2% and -8.7% drop in average basket size for Puregold and S&R.

 

For the quarter, S&R accounted for 34% of revenue while the rest was from the Puregold stores. We note that PGOLD’s top line growth in 1Q appears to be outpacing a number of its larger and more directly comparable competitors in the grocery retail space.

EBIT margin dips slightly on new store opex

Gross margins overall were unchanged at 19.6% as vendor support alongside operating leverage from volume growth offset compression from competitive pricing initiatives. Operating margin, however, saw a minimal decrease (-20bp) as pre-operating expenses from store expansion in the quarter kicked in.

 

Store expansion

In 1Q25, the company in opened three new stores for the Puregold brand, one new store for S&R, and 3 S&R QSRs. This brings the total store count to 757 as of 1Q25 (vs. 575 in 1Q24; note 153 Puremart stores were added to the portfolio in 1Q25). Management says it does not plan to grow the Puremart store count this year and will instead focus on improving current operations.

 

Company maintains 2025 guidance

The company maintained its guidance of 6-8% consolidated revenue growth and for gross margins to be broadly similar to 2024. We still find the guidance to be on the conservative side, specifically for the top line. Management also noted that the trends for April were similar to 1Q for both Puregold and S&R.

 
Semirara - 1Q25: Mining ASPs decline more than anticipated
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q25 core profit falls 34% to P4.27bn – Behind Regis, Street forecasts

Headline 1Q25 recurring profit (ex-FX adj) fell 34% YoY to P4.27bn, trending slightly behind Regis and consensus estimates. Even though mining ASPs fell more than we had anticipated, the surprise extension of the coal mining unit’s tax holiday helped cushion the blow.

 

Mining profits contract 22% QoQ, 45% YoY

Standalone profits from coal mining contracted by 22% QoQ and 45% YoY to P2.7bn in 1Q25, after ASPs slumped 12% QoQ, 17% YoY to P2,481/MT due to (a) 5% QoQ, 14% YoY decline in ICI4 benchmark price, as well as (b) higher sales of low-grade and non-commercial grade coal. Sales volumes reached 4.7mn MT (9% QoQ, -2% YoY), on track to reach management’s target of 16mn MT. Despite a 16% YoY surge in production to 5.7mn MT, cash costs (ex-royalties) rose by a mere 3% YoY on the back of lower fuel prices.

Better plant output, higher contracted prices offset lower spot prices

Even though spot prices fell 20% YoY, the power unit still managed to grow profits by 6% to P1.6bn. The completion of a major repair last year boosted energy output by 11% YoY to 1.4GWH. ASPs managed to avoid a collapse, declining by just 1% YoY to P4.42/kwh, after the company secured more favorable contracts.

 

Recent updates and outlook

The sequential softness in ICI4 (2Q-to-date: -1% QoQ, -10% YoY) suggests sustained profit weakness for the coal mining unit heading into 2Q but the impact should be partially cushioned by the decline in fuel prices. While the new “Acacia” mine, deemed to have higher quality coal reserves, could resolve its coal quality issues, management reckons it can only start production from this mine in 2H26 should it secure the environmental permit by 2Q/3Q. Management also continues to wait for the Department of Energy’s approval of its request for a term adjustment on its Coal Operating Contract due to expire by July 2027.

 
Wilcon Depot - 1Q25: Better but not good; reducing estimates and TP
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Revising our estimates and target price

We slash our estimates for FY25/26 by 44/45% and our target price by 55% for Wilcon (WLCON) to P10/sh following the latest results. The business environment for WLCON remains challenging and a hoped-for 2H rebound is by no means assured. However, we remain optimistic about the long-term trajectory of the firm and retain our BUY rating.

 

1Q24 net profit -28% YoY to P536mn—behind

WLCON reported net profit of P536mn in 1Q25, falling 28% YoY. This is below Street expectations due to worse-than-expected margins and same store sales (SSS). Net sales rose 1% as SSS contracted 3.6%. The silver lining here is SSS is down less than last year (-6% for FY24). The 1Q operating margin saw a 4.2 ppt drop YoY to 9.6% given higher opex from new store openings and an increase in lease liabilities interest expense.

 

1Q topline shows minimal improvement as demand remains tepid

Revenue was up slightly (+1% YoY) to P8.4bn in 1Q, better than the -1% YoY decline in FY24. SSSG in 1Q was -3.6%, an improvement from the -7.3% in 1Q24 and the -7.0% in 4Q24. However, note that the timing of this year’s Holy Week (a slow period for sales which was in 2Q this year vs 1Q last year) was likely a factor. Transaction count turned positive, growing 1% YoY in 1Q after declining -2.8% in FY24, but average basket size shrank further (1Q: -4.5% YoY, FY24: -3.5% YoY).

Continued topline push and higher opex from new stores compress margins

Gross margin contracted 110 bps YoY due to the continued push of “best deals” promotions and “price refreshes.” Operating margin saw a significant YoY decline of 4.2 ppts to 9.6% given new store openings opex and temporary accounting adjustments that led to the frontloading of lease liabilities interest expense. WLCON opened 2 new stores in 1Q25 (1 Depot/1 Do-It-Wilcon) out of the 8 stores (of which 7 Depots) planned this year.

 

Management hopeful for a turnaround in 2H

Management said that average daily sales was encouraging in March and after Holy Week in April compared to Jan and Feb. If this positive momentum continues, they are hopeful for a reversal of the profit decline later in the year. However, any signs of a turnaround will only be visible in late 2Q and will depend on the success of strategies/promotions and market recovery.

 

Revising 25-26E EPS estimates and target price

We adjust our forecasts to account for the latest results as we expect the company to see near-term topline weakness and margin pressure amidst subdued demand. We also trim our DCF-based (COE of 12% and terminal growth of 3.5%) target price for WLCON to P10/share, which implies 17x PER for this year. At current levels, the stock is on 11x PER with yield of 5%. Key downside risks arise from competition and softness in end-user demand.

 
Robinsons Land - Residential segment disappoints in 1Q25; P25bn profit target in 2030
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q25 recurring profit rises 4%

Robinsons Land’s headline net profit fell 14% YoY to P3.5bn. Stripping off non-recurring gains in 1Q24 (from the dilution in GoTyme), recurring profit grew 4% to P3.5bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Both the JV and standalone residential businesses saw weak revenue and presales. However, the unusually strong mall margins cushioned the impact on the bottom line.

 

JV and standalone residential businesses disappoint

Both the JV and standalone residential businesses saw weak revenue and presales. Revenue of the standalone residential business fell 16% QoQ and YoY (to P453mn) while equitized earnings from JV projects saw an even bigger drop (47% QoQ and 31% YoY). Meanwhile, presales of the standalone business fell 80% QoQ to P846mn while JV presales declined 99% QoQ and YoY to just P47mn.

 

RLC said that the weak performances were partly due to cancellations, particularly for the JV projects. However, the weak revenue (of both segments) was largely due to timing and this should improve in 2H25.

 

Unsold inventory value of the JV business was P18bn (equivalent to 2 years of presales). For the standalone business, it was P49bn, which represents 6.5 years of presales.

 

 

Mall EBIT margin jumps  

Mall revenue grew 6% YoY (down 2% QoQ), as occupancy remained stable at 93%. Mall EBIT rose 11% YoY and 17% QoQ due to the substantial jump in the mall EBIT margin (+7ppt QoQ, +2ppt YoY) to 45%, an unusually high level. However, RLC said this will decline in the coming quarters, similar to the FY24 experience.  

 

Office occupancy stays flat QoQ

Office revenue grew 6% YoY thanks to the 7% expansion in floor area. On a QoQ basis, revenue remained flat, as did occupancy, at 86%. However, BPO firms have recently signed preleasing contracts for RLC’s upcoming office buildings.

 

Industrial leasing to experience the fastest growth

Industrial leasing revenue climbed 40% YoY (flat QoQ) thanks to the 30% expansion in floor area. The company plans to grow this segment faster than the other businesses. It is targeting to expand floor area from 295k sqm in FY24 to 329k sqm this year (12% growth), and 635k sqm in 2030, representing 14% CAGR.

 

2030 profit target of P25bn

RLC announced a 2030 net profit target of P25bn, which represents 12% recurring profit CAGR from 2024. While the company intends to grow its rental portfolio (see Figure 3), we believe the profit target will also require a substantial recovery of the residential businesses.

 
Megaworld - Unsold inventory falls in 1Q25 but presales also drop
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q25 net profit rises 16% YoY

Megaworld’s 1Q25 headline profit rose 16% YoY to P5.1bn, slightly ahead of REGIS forecast (in line with consensus). Operating revenue of every segment was higher than our expectations, as was the residential GPM.       

 

Unsold inventory falls but presales also drop

Residential presales fell 10% QoQ and 26% YoY to P26.9bn. Projects in Metro Manila were weaker and now comprise only 52% of presales, from 55% in 1Q24 (56% in FY24). While presales were down, we consider the 8% QoQ drop in unsold inventory (to P122bn) to be a silver lining. We estimate it is equivalent to c. 18 months of net presales, lower than the c. 3 years of some developers.

 

Office occupancy increases 1ppt QoQ

Office occupancy increased 1ppt QoQ to 87%, higher than the c. 80% of the industry. While a POGO tenant vacated in Pasay, Megaworld signed up new tenants in Eastwood and Fort Bonifacio.

Office revenue rose 17% YoY even though no new buildings were completed in the past year. This was largely due to the lapse of rent-free periods for some tenants as well as the renewal of some contracts at higher rental rates. Reported revenue was further enhanced by the application of a straight-line revenue recognition policy.

 

Mall revenue increases thanks to stronger tenant sales

Megaworld did not add any mall space in the past year, implying that the 12% increase in mall revenue was primarily driven by higher tenant sales. This should rise further as recently signed tenants open their stores in the next few months.

 

Hotel revenue climbs 26% YoY

Hotel revenue climbed 26% YoY to P1.4bn. While this is partly due to the c. 15% increase in number of rooms, new partnerships with online travel platforms also helped to boost the average daily room rate.

URC 1Q25 profit -2% YoY to P4.1bn

Chinabank earnings up 10% YoY to P6.5bn

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

1Q25 GDP grows 5.4%; falls short of expectations

GDP grew at 5.4% in 1Q, lower than the 5.9% growth in 1Q24 but a tad higher than the 5.3% growth rate in 4Q24. The 5.4% GDP growth is below consensus expectations of 5.7%. The print fell short of the government’s 6-8% target range, implying that growth of at least 6.2% for the rest of the year is needed to meet the 6% full-year goal. Main drags stem from slower capital formation and the higher differential between exports and imports. On the demand side, the strong growth in private and gov't consumption was offset by the deterioration of net imports to -2.1% which contracted sharply from -0.1% the previous quarter. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated the gov't remains confident in hitting its 6-8% full-year growth target despite global and domestic headwinds. He also highlighted the resilience of the Philippine economy amidst heightened global uncertainty and noted that the presence of clear signals of accelerating demand should bolster growth. The Department Budget Coordinating Committee (DBCC) will meet next week to review the country's economic targets as a prerequisite for the 2026 national budget. The Bloomberg consensus median forecast for 2025 is 5.9%.

Inflation rate decelerates further to 1.4% in April

Headline inflation eased to 1.4% in April from 1.8% in the previous month of Mar, marking the lowest print since Nov 2019 (1.2%). This is lower than the consensus forecast of 1.8% and stands at the lower-end of the BSP's forecast range of 1.3-2.1%. Inflation has continued to be on a downtrend since the start of the year, falling from 2.9% in Jan to 1.4% as of Apr. Primary drivers for easing inflation were MoM price declines in key food staples—rice -10.9%, meat -0.3%, and vegetables -5.4%. Notably, rice prices are on their tenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. Core inflation was unchanged at 2.2% in Apr vs. Mar. While core non-food inflation in Apr inched up slightly to 2.1% from 2.0% in Mar. This brings the YT-Apr inflation rate to 2.0%, at the lower-end of the 2-4% gov't target for this year. Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of 6 this year) is at Jun 19, Thursday. For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps total worth of cuts to 5.00%.

BSP sees more space to adjust rates given easing inflation

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli M. Remolona, Jr. noted that April's inflation which is expected to be below 2% (BSP projection: 1.3-2.1%) gives them more room to adjust rates. He also noted that the 1Q25 GDP result which could be near or below the gov't's 6-8% target will be a key factor in the Monetary Board's next meeting on June 19 (3rd out of 6 this year). Inflation for April will be reported today by the PSA while the 1Q25 GDP report will be on May 8 (Thurs). Bloomberg median consensus for rate cuts this year is 75 bps to 5.00%.

Government debt inches up to P16.68tn in end-Mar

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.31% MoM to P16.68tn as of end-Mar. This was due to the net issuance of new domestic and external debt which was offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P57.897 end-Feb to P57.382 end-Mar) as well as net repayments. Domestic debt rose by 1.4% (+P155.8bn) MoM to P11.38tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P157.9bn). External debt decreased by 1.9% (+P103.9bn) MoM to P5.3tn given a decrease in the peso equivalent of USD debt (P66.2bn) due to local currency appreciation, and net repayments (P60.8bn). The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.2% and 31.8% being external borrowings. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn (see here). This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%.

PH Dollar reserves down 1.9% MoM at end-April

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Apr saw a 1.9% MoM decrease to $104.6bn from $106.7bn at end-Feb. The decrease primarily reflected the national government's drawdowns on foreign currency deposits with the BSP to meet external debt obligations as well as the BSP's net foreign exchange operations. The level of dollar reserves by end-Apr was enough to cover about 3.6x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Reserve position in the IMF jumped 13.6% MoM to $742mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $13.3bn, which rose 4.5% MoM as gold continues hit new all-time highs in April. Foreign investments decreased by 3.2% MoM to $86.1bn. Foreign currency deposits surged 23.5% MoM to $649mn. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025.

Infrastructure spending rises by 19% YoY in the first two months of 2025

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 19% YoY to P182.9bn in 2M25. For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP. Recall that the gov't spent 1.55tn (+9% YoY) or 5.8% of GDP back in 2024. The DBM (Department of Budget and Management) noted that while disbursements likely accelerated before the March 28 election ban on public funds, spending may temporarily slow in April but is expected to pick up again from late May to June once the ban is lifted.

PH gov't approves more infrastructure flagship projects

The Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev) stated that the gov't has approved more infrastructure flagship projects (IFP) to 207 total projects that amount to US$178bn (~P9.86tn). This is an increase from 186 projects worth P9.6tn that were initially slated late last year. Majority of the IFPs are related to physical connectivity/transportation at 139 projects. These are followed by 32 projects in water resources, nine in agriculture, six in digital connectivity, five in health, three in power and energy, two in housing, and two in education, among other sectors. The gov't targets to complete 13 IFPs this year. Seven IFPs were completed last year. For 2025, total infrastructure spending is expected to be at least 5.4% of GDP. The Marcos administration aims to spend 5-6% of GDP on infrastructure annually.

DENR to tighten ECC rules following Manila Bay assessment

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said it would tighten its guidelines on the issuance of Environmental Compliance Certificates (ECC) after its Manila Bay Cumulative Impact Assessment revealed severe environmental threats. This could include ECCs that have been issued. The assessment revealed that the 21 proposed and ongoing reclamation projects in Greater Metro Manila could threaten marine ecosystems and fisherman’s livelihoods, and worsen flooding in urban areas. Environment Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo Loyzaga said that Manila Bay will be part of a continuing review. “There is in the ECC a section that will allow us to modify any approval that has been granted, and we take that section very seriously in light of the results of the cumulative impact assessment phase one.” Part of the review process will be a consultation with the Philippine Reclamation Authority, proponent local government units, and stakeholders to determine whether the ECCs need to be revised or amended.

Okada Manila’s 1Q25 GGR fell 13% QoQ and 12% YoY to P7.8bn.
