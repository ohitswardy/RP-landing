REGIS REPORT

07 February 2025

PCOMP capped another busy week on a positive note, +4.99% WoW at 6154, as shares recovered their hefty losses from the volatile PCOMP/PSEi rebalancing of the prior week. The benchmark was initially steady at 5800 at the onset as Trumps’ announcement of new tariffs over the weekend evidently sparked continued risk aversion on the ground. Upon his temporary suspension of said tariffs on Mexico and Canada however, local shares managed to find relief and bargain hunting ensued, leading the benchmark to post its best back-to-back sessions since 2021 (up nearly 7% through Tuesday and Wednesday.)

In the latter part of the week, focus then shifted to the start of 4Q/FY2024 reporting season which was kicked off by generally inline (if not slightly behind) numbers by BPI +12.99% WoW. Weak results from other banks in the region like Indo’s Mandiri (which was dragged by expenses) also appears to have lifted perception of BPI’s numbers. In the consumer space, RRHI +4.53% missed bottomline FY24 consensus but 4Q trends did show some signs of recovery – SSSG during the quarter accelerated to +3.4% compared to just +0.7% in 9M24, which lifted the FY24 to +1.5%.

Another big area of focus in the latter part of the week was the 4Q property report by Colliers which yet again showed further deterioration in the real estate market. On the residential side, while unsold residential units did fall to 74k units in 4Q (-1% QoQ and -4% YoY), take ups slowed to just 9k (from 13k in 3Q and 23k a year ago), resulting to an increase in the unsold inventory ratio to 8.2 years from 5.8 years in 3Q. On the office leasing side, Colliers reported that the vacancy rate in Metro Manila increased to 19.8% in 4Q from 18.5% in 3Q, as vacancy in Manila Bay area alone worsened to 35% from 30%. Furthermore, office transactions in 4Q were a net negative of -185k sqm, largely attributed to the POGO ban. That said, property names ALI +12.11% WoW and SMPH +10.8% still ended sharply higher this week – but note that a key nuance is that ALI basically recovered from its -11.5% drop through the prior week, while SMPH is coming off only a -2.9% loss during the same period.

On the macro front, investors digested the latest CPI reading which showed that inflation remained unchanged at 2.9% in January, same pace as December and just a tick above the consensus forecast of 2.8%. Main driver still was food inflation which accelerated to 3.8% from 3.4% – but this appears to be attributed more towards supply-side effects on meats and vegetables caused by the several typhoons that hit the country in the latter months of last year, rather than a demand-side issue. As such, this keeps alive hopes for a potential 25bp rate cut next week (Feb 13), especially considering the slower-than-expected 4Q GDP reading from the prior week (5.2% vs 5.5% consensus.) Regis expects a total of 50bps cuts this year – the first one potentially next week, then the other in 2H.

Breadth of the market was positive with 90% of index members in the green – biggest gainers were the 2 biggest laggards of the PSEi rebal, AGI +22.83% WoW and SMC +22.7%, which both posted -20% declines during the rebal session alone. On the opposite end, among the few decliners were the latest index inclusions CBC -6.13% and AREIT -5.83%, as expected, but note the drop in the former appears fairly contained considering it surged by almost +40% during the rebalancing. Faring worse than the 2 however was EMI -10.72%.

RRHI: 4Q24 SSSG rebounds

FY24 recurring profit up 30% to P5.61bn – in line with REGIS but behind consensus; EBIT of P9.5bn (+9%) in line

RRHI posted 4Q24 net income of P2.7bn (+62% YoY), bringing FY24 net income to P10.3bn, up 1.5x YoY due to the one-off gain from the BPI-RBC merger of P4.66bn recognized in 1Q24. Recurring income as reported by the company grew 12% YoY to P6.3bn in FY24. We estimate recurring profit (i.e. including FX adj, interest expenses and dividend income related to BPI, equitized losses from associates) grew by 30% YoY to P5.61bn, broadly in line with REGIS (P5.8bn) but behind consensus estimates (P7.7bn) EBIT rose by +9% YoY to P9.8bn for the year, within Regis (P9.9bn) and consensus (P9.4bn) forecasts.  

For 4Q24 alone, recurring net profit increased 5% YoY to P2.3bn while EBIT improved to P2.3bn +22% YoY underpinned by better category mix and the rationalization of underperforming stores.

4Q SSSG recovers across most segments - Blended SSSG improved to 3.4% in 4Q24 from +0.7% for 9M, primarily due to faster growth rates in key segments, such as food (+4.1% in 4Q24 from 1.5% in 9M24) and specialty stores (+1.7% in 4Q24 from -8.6% in 3Q24). The DIY segment was still hampered by stiff competition, with SSSG dropping by 2% in 4Q, marking a sustained seven-quarter period of decline.

4Q operating margins improve - The company's consolidated EBIT margin for the 4Q expanded 130 bps QoQ and 80 bps YoY, with the food and specialty stores segments showing QoQ margin improvement of 30 bps and 340 bps, respectively. The food segment saw better margins on the back of higher demand of imported products while specialty store margins were boosted by the aforementioned optimized category mix and vendor support.

Less inflation worries, discretionary spending recovery as drivers for 2025 growth - Management is guiding for 2-4% consolidated SSSG in 2025 (Regis FY25E: +3.6%). Although expectations for primary growth drivers are still hinged on food and drug stores, with the bulk of net store additions in 2025 (c.90% of total)—less pressure on the consumer wallet as inflation continues to ease will increase the propensity for discretionary spending.

Senate approves Meralco franchise bill on final reading

The Senate approved on the final reading the proposed measure to extend Manila Electric Co.'s (MER PM) franchise for another 25 years. House Bill No. 10926, allows the power provider "to continue constructing, operating, and maintaining its electric distribution systems in areas such as Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, and Rizal." The bill also states that at least 30% of Meralco's outstanding capital stock must be owned by Filipinos and that the company must submit an annual report of its finances by April 30 after the measure takes effect. By law, all public utilities are required to be 60% Filipino owned and Meralco is classified as one.

ERC to assess Meralco's P19bn refund proposal by March

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is set to decide on Meralco's proposed P19bn refund to its customers as announced by ERC Chairperson Mona Dimalanta. Meralco's refund accounts for the difference between its actual weighted average tariff and most recent approved distribution rate of P1.35/kWh from Jul 2022 to Dec 2024, which results in a refund of P0.19/kWh for residential customers over three years, based on Meralco's prevailing end-user rate of P11.74/kWh as of Jan 2025. ERC's technical and legal teams are currently evaluating the proposal on whether the refund is sufficient or should be higher given the overcollection and will make a decision on the application by March 1.

The second tranche (+6%) of the cargo-handling tariff hike at Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) and South Harbor takes effect on Feb 6. Inflation rate unchanged at 2.9% in January

Headline inflation was unchanged at 2.9% in January vs. the previous month. This is slightly higher than consensus forecast of 2.8% and came in at the midpoint of BSP's forecast range of 2.5-3.3%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose 0.5%, primarily driven by higher food prices which increased by 1.1% MoM. The increase was mainly due to price upticks in vegetables and meat due to the lingering effects of the several typhoons that hit the country in October and November.

Inflation was primarily driven by food—specifically meat, fish, and vegetables which rose 2.0%, 3.6%, and 3.9% respectively. The big uptick in vegetable prices was due to the recent spike in the price of tomatoes which ranged as high as P250-350/kg at the start of Jan according to the Department of Agriculture's price tracker. Rice CPI MoM sequentially dipped for the seventh consecutive month by 0.9% bringing the cumulative decline to 4.6%. Notably, rice prices on a YoY basis decreased for the first time since Dec 2021.

Core inflation dipped slightly to 2.6% in Jan from 2.8% in Dec. While core non-food inflation also dropped slightly to 2.5% from 2.8% for the same periods.

The gov't targets to keep inflation within 2-4% for this year. Bloomberg median consensus is for the full year average is 3.0%. Regis expects inflation to average 3.5%.

Lower House impeaches VP Sara Duterte, Senate trial to be held

215 of the 306 lawmakers have endorsed an impeachment compliant against Vice President Sara Duterte. The grounds cited include: culpable violation of the Constitution, betrayal of public trust, graft and corruption, other high crimes. This implies that with the signatures of at least 1/3rd of all House members, the petition was fast-tracked directly to the Senate with no need for a deliberation by the House committee on justice. The impeachment bill will first undergo review by Senate Secretary Renato Bantug Jr., who will then report to the plenary which is a prerequisite for the complaint to be proceed. Given that there was no plenary action on the bill prior to Senate's adjournment of its final session, it will likely be tackled after elections as Congress will go on break till resuming on June 2. Senate Minority Leader Koko Pimentel III however stated that there is a possibility for the Senate to work on the impeach complaint while on break if there is a 100 percent unanimity or zero objections on proceeding.

In the Senate trial, a two-thirds vote of the 24 senators is needed for impeachment to take place which will also result in a lifetime disqualification from holding office. If impeached, the President is granted authority by the Constitution to nominate a new Vice President from Congress, which needs confirmation from both the Lower House and the Senate. The trial date has yet to be finalized.

Senate to tackle VP Sara Duterte's impeachment post-elections

Senate President Francis Escudero stated that the Senate will not tackle the impeachment complaint against Vice President Sara Duterte during the break. He stated that for the impeachment case to be taken up in the plenary, a process must be followed including a review of the Senate rules of impeachment. Escudero also cited that past impeachment cases had at least a month between filing and the impeachment trial commencing.

Bicam approves capital markets reform bill, new DBP chapter

Congress ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee report, which reconciled the Senate and Lower House’s versions of the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotions bill. The key measures are as follows:

>Reduction in the tax on stock transactions to 0.1% from 0.6%

>Cut in the documentary stamp tax (DST) on original issue of shares of stock to 0.75% from 1% of the par value of the shares of stock

>Exemption of DST on the original issuance, redemption or other disposition of shares or units of participation in a unit investment trust fund

>Introduction of an allowable deduction of 50% of an employer’s contribution to their employees’ personal equity and retirement accounts

A copy of the bicameral conference committee report of the measure was not immediately available. The Philippine Stock Exchange previously forecasted that lowering the stock transaction tax to 0.1% would boost the stock market’s trading volume to P4.9tn by 2029.

Meanwhile, the Senate also ratified the Bicam report on the new Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) charter. The measure mandates that the National Government will always own 70% of the DBP’S capital stock, with P32bn or 10.67% being fully subscribed to and paid for by the state. It will also allow the state-run lender to conduct an initial public offering.

Government debt goes down to P16.05tn in end-December

The government's total debt stock declined by 0.2% MoM to P16.05tn as of end-December, given the appreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of November. Domestic debt rose by 0.1% (P8.8bn) MoM to P10.9tn due to the impact of net borrowings. External debt decreased by 0.9% (P48.4bn) MoM as the peso appreciated and reduced the level of foreign denominated debt. The year-end figure is in-line with the P16.06 outstanding debt target.

The debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 60.7%, higher than end-2023's 60.1% and is slightly above the target of 60.6%. This was mainly due to the lower-than-expected FY24 GDP growth of 5.6%. Note that the gov't target level of debt for 2025 is 60.4% of GDP. The government expects it to further decline to 58.4% by 2027 and to 56.3% by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028.

Colliers: residential demand falls in 4Q24

In its 4Q24 briefing, Colliers said that the number of condominium units sold in Metro Manila in FY24 fell to 9,000 units, from 23,000 units in 2023. In 3Q24, trailing 12M take-up stood at 13,000 units. The number of unsold residential units fell 1% QoQ and 4% YoY to 74,000 units. As a result, the level of unsold residential inventory in Metro Manila now represents 8.2 years of reservation sales, from 5.8 years in 3Q24, 3.3 years in 4Q23.

Colliers said that pre-selling condominium units in Makati CBD were priced at an average of P400,000/sqm, 50% higher than the prices of ready-for-occupancy units in the area. During the pre-COVID period, prices of preselling projects in Makati were about 20%-30% higher than prices of RFO units.

Colliers: Office vacancy rises in 4Q24

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy rose to 19.8% in 4Q24 from 18.5% in 3Q24, 19.3% in 4Q23. Makati CBD vacancy stayed flat at 8%, while Manila Bay area vacancy rose to 35% in 4Q24, from 30% in 3Q24. Office transactions were negative in 4Q24, with net closures of 185,000sqm, largely due to the POGO ban. This is coming from net closures of 33,000sqm in 3Q24 and net take-up of 130,000sqm in 4Q23. Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 22% by end-2025 – as, while it expects net take-up to hit 150,000sqm, it expects about 600,000sqm in new supply. Regis estimates that there are still about 200,000sqm of offices occupied by POGO firms.

Food security emergency declared and ceiling set on imported rice

The Department of Agriculture (DA) will impose a P55 per kilogram maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice starting February 5; that is P3 lower than the level set on January 20. With MSRP in place, the DA hopes to stabilize rice prices and ease inflationary pressures on basic commodities.

The move follows Monday’s declaration of a food security emergency that allows the government to release rice buffer stock held by the National Food Authority (NFA) to bring down prices that have remained high in markets despite a cut in tariffs on imported rice. According to Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel, the MSRP could be further reduced in the coming weeks to below P50 per kilogram to reflect the lower global prices of rice and the reduced tariff. “After this reduction, we plan to lower it further to P52 by mid-February and then P49 per kilo two weeks after.”

In the gov't plan, the NFA would release 150,000 metric tons (MT) of its rice stock at P33 per kilogram, leading to an estimated loss of P12 to P15 per kilogram, based on the agency’s average buying price in 2024. Ultimately adding up to a loss of P1.8 billion to P2.25 billion. Government agencies, government-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs) and local government units (LGUs) will sell rice to the public at a suggested retail price of P25 per kilogram. The Agri Sec. also stated that this will speed up the release of rice stocks in their warehouses to make way for unmilled rice procurement as the harvest season starts this month.

Food emergency declaration approved to address high rice prices

The National Price Coordinating Council (NPCC) has approved the Department of Agriculture's (DA) proposal to declare a food security emergency. Recall that the DA is authorized to propose food emergency declarations under the recently amended Rice Tariffication Law, in response to a shortage or extraordinary prices increases. DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated last Friday the need to declare a food emergency on Feb. 4, Tuesday in order to bring down elevated rice prices.

Upon declaration, the gov't will release around 300,000 MT of rice stocks within six months from the National Food Authority (NFA) which will be sold to local gov't units (LGUs) at a subsidized price of P36/kg to be passed on to consumers at P38/kg. The NFA will also be allowed to purchase rice from farmers this coming harvest season. This initiative is part of the push by the gov't as the DA Secretary reiterates its targets of reducing the MSRP of rice which is currently at P58/kg to be lowered to P55 on February 5, then to P52 on February 15, and P49 by March 1.

Senate passes mining tax regime measure

The Senate approved on the third reading the bill setting up a margin-based royalty and windfall profit system for the mining industry with expectations that it will raise the gov't's share of mining profits. The Lower House approved its version of the bill last Sep 2024 so a reconciled bicameral version will be needed. Senate Bill No. 2826 sets a five-tier margin-based royalty tiers ranging from 1 to 5%, while the five-tier windfall profit tax system ranges from 1 to 10%. It also requires large-scale miners to pay the gov't 5% of their gross output.

Mining companies will be barred from exporting raw ore five years after the measure takes effect. The rationale behind the limitation is to encourage mining firms to establish processing plants for more value-adding processing vs. simply exporting the ore. The Chamber of Mines of the Philippines stated their support for the bill but disapproves of the raw ore export ban as it can lead to job losses and given the low probability of finishing building plants within five years and disruptions caused in mineral trading. The Department of Finance estimates P6.26bn in additional revenue.

Department of Energy prepares for GEA-4 auction

The Department of Energy said it will release the terms of reference for the fourth Green Energy Auction (GEA-4) this month. GEA-4 will be limited to solar power plants with Battery Energy Storage Systems. Recall that the bidding for GEA-3 is scheduled on Feb 11. GEA-3 will involve 100 MW of geothermal, 300 MW of impounding hydro, and 4,250 MW of pumped-storage hydro, with targeted deliveries in 2028-2030 for hydro and 2024-2030 for geothermal. Listed companies that have been shortlisted are: First Gen (FGEN PM) and San Miguel (SMC PM). The Green Energy Auction program aims to promote investments in renewable energy sources. GEA allows RE gencos to sell energy to the WESM at their winning (guaranteed) bid prices and any difference between the bid price and the WESM price will be settled through the Feed-in-Tariff system, which is a full pass-through to end users.
