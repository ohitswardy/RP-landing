REGIS REPORT

11 July 2025

The PCOMP ended the week on a positive note, +1.01% WoW at 6459.88. Among the key areas of focus in the market this week were the ongoing tariff negotiations in the US and, on the ground, another busy round of news flow regarding gambling restrictions as more lawmakers come out with proposals to either ban or impose tighter regulations.

Biggest mover of the benchmark this week was ICT +5.01% WoW as investors digested the latest tariff developments out of the US where crucially the July 9 deadline was extended to August 1 amid ongoing negotiations. For the Philippines, tariffs were set at 20% which is not too far from the initial rate of 17% announced a month ago. Also helping sentiment on ICT is the lack of any negative newsflow regarding US tariffs on Mexico, which is still technically covered by the USMCA agreement - note that ICT’s Manzanillo port in Mexico accounts for about a fourth of the company’s EBITDA. Furthermore, there also appears to be some optimism ahead of the 2Q results season which is set to kick off later this July. Note that shipping data out of Mexico showed that ICT’s Manzanillo grew volumes by +14% in April and +16% in May, which is even faster than +8% growth in 1Q.

Leading index gainers were consumer names MONDE +7.68% WoW and URC +7.66%. Seeing the former start to catch up to the latter after being a relative laggard through much of the year. Note that despite the rally this week MONDE is still -8.72% YTD, while in contrast URC now extends its YTD gain to +22.78%. That said, the biggest laggard in the index was another consumer manufacturer CNPF -3.8% which suggests rotation within the sector as it has enjoyed a higher multiple compared to the two over the past year (but a bit closer now due to the share price movement this week.)

Another bright spot were gaming names, which recovered after the drastic selloff of the prior week, as investors appear to be pricing in tighter restrictions on online gambling rather than a complete ban after the Palace commented this week that President Marcos will not oppose proposals to impose higher taxes and restrictions to curb online gambling. Any official announcement is likely to come during the President’s upcoming state of the nation address on July 28. As such, online gaming market leader PLUS surged +28.14% WoW, partially recovering from its nearly -50% slump through the prior week. Meanwhile BLOOM +10.72% and was the top gainer in the benchmark. Note that there was also news out of Thailand that the government is set to formally withdraw the proposed casino legalization bill which, through way of less competition, is a positive for BLOOM’s physical casino operations, particularly the VIP segment.

Property stocks were mixed with SMPH +4.67% WoW outperforming peers ALI (unchanged) and RLC -0.69% this time after being a relative laggard to the two over the past couple of weeks. Investors digested the latest report from property consultant Leechiu which showed that nationwide office take-ups improved sequentially to 182k sqm in 2Q from 88k in 1Q. While on the residential side, sales also saw a sequential improvement, albeit much more modestly, +2% QoQ (but still -10% YoY). Both points fit into the view that perhaps the cyclical bottom of the property sector is now out of the way (which some argue is probably the best time to buy?) – although granted, it’s hard to envision any sharp recovery anytime soon to replace the forgone demand due to exit of POGOs.

Another mixed space was telco with CNVRG +4.4% WoW outperforming GLO -2.29% and TEL -1.2% this time. Similar to the sector above there appears to be a reversal of past week’s trend after CNVRG fell by more than 10% last week. While concerns over the Konektadong Pinoy bill, which essentially seeks to remove barriers to entry in the telecom industry, remains a headwind to general sentiment – note that at these levels GLO is already around its lowest in 5 years (since pandemic lows) and div yield is now at 6%. Even better however is TEL whose div yield is now close to 8%.

On the downside, a generally weak space in the market once again were banks with BDO -2.71% WoW, BPI -1.36%, and CBC -1.49% (although MBT managed to outperform +2.67%) as the outlook of lower rates, at least on a thematic point of view, appears to drive preference towards other sectors instead. Add to that the fact that lenders (particularly BDO and BPI), along with ICT, are perhaps the most widely foreign-held names in the Philippines.

Market activity this week improved to a daily average of $147m, +10% WoW, as foreign participation in the market picked up by 8% to 51%. Net foreign flow this week turned negative (by a moderate $15m) and among the consistently top sold throughout the week were banks.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Prime launches premium residential brand

SM Prime Holdings (SMPH) has launched a new premium residential line called the Signature Series. Its inaugural project will be an “ultra-luxury village” located in Susana Heights, Muntinlupa. According to Signature Series Group Head Jose Juan Jugo, lot sizes will range from 750 to 1,000 square meters, with an entry price of ₱100 million—implying a price of approximately ₱130,000 per square meter. SMPH is also pursuing a high-end partnership project in Makati.

SM Prime unveils house and lot brand

SM Prime Holdings has launched a new residential brand called Symphony Homes, signaling the company’s move into the house-and-lot market and expanding into suburban and rural locations. At present, about 95% of SM Prime’s residential sales come from condominiums. In the first quarter of 2025, the company reported a 57% year-on-year decline in reservation sales to ₱11.6 billion, although this was up 45% quarter-on-quarter.

DigiPlus announces share buyback program

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) has announced a ₱6 billion share buyback program, equivalent to approximately 4.6% of its current market capitalization. This move follows a period of increased legislative scrutiny of online gambling. PLUS is currently trading at 7.4x FY25E price-to-earnings, based on consensus estimates.

URC appoints new chief marketing officer

Universal Robina Corp. (URC) has appointed Karen Ong as its new Chief Marketing Officer, succeeding Anna Milagros "Mian" David, who assumed the role of President for the Branded Consumer Foods (BCF) segment earlier this year. Ong brings more than 20 years of experience from Colgate-Palmolive, Johnson & Johnson, and Fonterra. She is expected to drive regional expansion and reinforce local brand equity. Notably, as Senior Marketing Director for UR Munchy’s Malaysia, she helped URC capture 25.4% market share in the Malaysian biscuit segment by end-2024, up from 23.2% at end-2023.

Aboitiz Power plans to build a 351MW + BESS solar facility

Northern Sun Power, Inc. (NSPI) is eyeing the development of a 351 MW-dc + 338MWh battery energy storage system (BESS) in Currimao, Ilocos Norte. The project is estimated to cost P18.6bn and is targeted for commercial operations by 1Q29. Construction is expected to commence in 2Q27 and commissioning is expected by 3Q28.

Aboitiz Power (AP PM) is currently operating an attributable 4,636 MW of capacity, of which 503 MW are renewables. It has a pipeline of 421MW of renewable energy projects that are under construction. The company is targeting an expansion of its total attributable net sellable capacity to 9.2 GW by 2030, with a 50:50 balance between renewable and thermal energy sources.

Meralco’s 3,500MW MTerra Solar project on track, with 778MW panels installed

Meralco PowerGen Corp. (MGEN) reported the installation of 778MW of solar photovoltaic panels as part of its 3,500MW MTerra Solar project. As of end-June, Phase 1—which targets 2,500MW—has reached 54% completion, with full completion expected in the first quarter of 2026. Phase 2, which accounts for the remaining 1,000MW, is scheduled for completion by 2027. MTerra Solar is 60% owned by SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC), in which MGEN currently holds a 35.7% economic stake and 54% voting rights. This stake will rise to 57.3% economic and 69% voting once SPNEC issues common shares as payment for exchangeable notes from Leviste-owned Solar Philippines.

Aboitiz Power-led consortium bags 797MW CBK hydro plant complex

The Thunder consortium, led by Aboitiz Power, has emerged as the highest bidder for the 797MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydroelectric power plant complex, offering ₱36.27 billion. This bid was 85% higher than the ₱19.6 billion submitted by the consortium of First Gen (FGEN PM) and Korea Water Resources Corp., and exceeded the Asian Development Bank’s upper estimate of approximately US$600 million or ₱33.8 billion. The Thunder consortium includes Aboitiz Power’s wholly-owned unit Aboitiz Renewables, Sumitomo Corporation, and Electric Power Development Co. The offer implies an enterprise value per megawatt (EV/MW) of around US$800,000—significantly below the valuation of over US$3 million per MW seen in FGEN’s purchase of the 165MW Casecnan hydro plant last year. Notably, CBK’s existing power supply contract is set to expire in February 2026. The complex includes the 39.37MW Caliraya HEPP in Lumban, 22.91MW Botocan HEPP in Majayjay, and the Kalayaan I (366MW) and Kalayaan II (368.36MW) pumped-storage plants. State-run PSALM is expected to issue the notice of award by mid-July.

DITO expects 2025 revenue to be over P20bn

DITO Telecommunity Corp. forecasts revenue of over ₱20 billion in 2025, a 22% increase from ₱16.4 billion in 2024. According to President and CEO Eric Alberto, the growth will be fueled by continued expansion in data services. This year, the company has allocated ₱10–15 billion in capital expenditures—down from ₱20 billion in 2024—with most of the funds going toward 5G network development. For the first quarter of 2025, DITO reported gross revenues of ₱4.7 billion, reflecting 24% year-on-year growth.

AirAsia Philippines plans to receive 20 new aircraft over the next five years

AirAsia Philippines (AAP) plans to receive 20 new aircraft over the next five years, according to parent company CEO Tony Fernandes. These deliveries are expected to primarily replace aging aircraft in its current fleet. AAP aims to expand its active fleet to 21 planes by the end of 2025, up from 15 at present. Notably, AAP has been flagging this planned expansion since 2023.

Inflation rate inches up to 1.4% in June

Headline inflation in the Philippines inched up to 1.4% in June from 1.3% in May, in line with the consensus forecast of 1.5% and within the BSP’s target range of 1.1% to 1.9%. Inflation has declined notably from 2.9% in January and has remained below 1.5% for three straight months. The continued deceleration was mainly driven by year-on-year price declines in rice (-14.3%) and transport (-1.6%), which offset rising costs of meat (+9.1%) and housing/utilities (+3.2%). Rice prices, notably, have now dropped for twelve consecutive months. As of July 2, the Department of Agriculture reported that local well-milled rice costs between ₱37–48/kg, while imported well-milled rice is priced at ₱38–48/kg—down from ₱42–52/kg and ₱40–54/kg, respectively, in early January.

Core inflation was steady at 2.2% in June, with non-food core inflation unchanged at 2.0%. This brings the year-to-date (YT-June) headline inflation rate to 1.8%, which is below the BSP’s 2–4% target. Consensus forecasts peg 2025 inflation at 2.1%, while Regis expects full-year inflation to average 1.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting, the fourth of six scheduled for the year, will take place on August 28. Both consensus and Regis forecast a total of 75 basis points in policy rate cuts this year, bringing the policy rate to 5.00%.

Meralco sees higher electricity rates in July

Meralco expects electricity rates to rise in July, driven by potentially higher generation and transmission charges. According to spokesperson Joe Zaldarriaga, the depreciation of the Philippine peso versus the U.S. dollar—given the dollar-denominated nature of power supplier contracts—could contribute to upward price pressure. Additionally, reserve market costs may push transmission rates higher. Meralco hopes that lower charges in the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) may help mitigate the overall rate hike. Generation charges have been elevated in recent months, particularly due to high-cost power from LNG plants such as the 1,300MW Excellent Energy facility, in which Meralco has a stake.

LPC: Net office demand improves in 2Q25; residential rents decline

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC), in its second-quarter 2025 briefing, reported that Metro Manila office vacancy remained broadly flat quarter-on-quarter at 18%. Rental rates in most areas of Metro Manila are still estimated to be 10%–50% below pre-COVID levels, though Makati and Fort Bonifacio enjoy lower vacancy rates of 10%–15%. Nationwide net office take-up reached 182,000 sqm in 2Q25, rising from 88,000 sqm in the previous quarter. However, business process outsourcing (BPO) net office take-up declined 21% QoQ but rose 3% YoY to 107,000 sqm. Net take-up from traditional tenants, including government agencies, increased 10% QoQ but fell 38% YoY to 91,000 sqm. Meanwhile, Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) firms experienced net closures of 16,000 sqm, an improvement from net closures of 132,000 sqm in 1Q25.

On the residential side, LPC estimates condominium rental rates fell 0%–6% QoQ. While Bonifacio rental rates are about 5% above pre-pandemic levels, rates in other Metro Manila areas remain 15%–50% below pre-COVID levels. Residential unsold inventory rose from 39 months of sales in 1Q25 to 41 months in 2Q25. Residential sales dropped 10% YoY (but increased 2% QoQ) to 6,643 units, while unsold inventory grew 31% YoY and 2% QoQ to 82,800 units.

Commercial banks’ loan growth 11.3% in May; M3 growth at 5.5%

Loan growth of Philippine commercial banks remained stable in May 2025, with an 11.3% year-on-year increase—slightly higher than April’s 11.2%. Loans to productive activities grew 10.2%, marginally slower than April’s 10.3%, weighed down by a 3.0% decline in manufacturing and slower agricultural growth at 6.7% from 9.5%. Consumer loans continued to show strength, growing 23.7%, just slightly below April’s 24.0%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 5.5% in May, easing from 5.8% in April.

Digital payments make up 57% of PH retail transactions in 2024

Digital payments continued to gain momentum in the Philippines in 2024, accounting for 57.4% of retail transaction volume and 59.0% of value, surpassing the BSP’s targets of 52–54% and 55–57%, respectively. Merchant payments led digital volume with a 66.4% share, followed by person-to-person transfers at 20.6% and business-to-business transactions at 6.2%. QR Ph merchant adoption surged by 148.7% year-on-year, while InstaPay transactions grew by 67.8% in volume and 46.3% in value from 2023. Government disbursements were almost fully digital, with 97.2% of both volume and value processed electronically. The BSP is also pursuing key digitalization initiatives, including pilot testing of a central bank digital currency and expanding services such as request-to-pay and direct debit.

President Marcos will not oppose proposal to tax online gambling if it is well studied

President Marcos has expressed openness to the Department of Finance’s proposal to tax online gambling, provided that it is supported by sufficient studies. Communications Undersecretary Claire Castro stated that the President would also consider placing restrictions on online gambling to curb addiction, emphasizing the government's intent to limit gambling activities and reduce the number of individuals negatively affected. In parallel, members of the Akbayan party-list have filed a bill in the House of Representatives to impose strict regulations on online gambling. The proposed bill seeks to ban advertising and prohibit e-wallets and super apps from linking to online gambling platforms. It would also require e-games operators to assess a player's source of funds and spending patterns to ensure financial sustainability. Additionally, the bill mandates PAGCOR to set daily, weekly, and monthly betting and loss limits and proposes a 10% tax on online gambling to support addiction rehabilitation, education, enforcement, and research. Currently, online gaming firms remit 38% of their e-casino and e-bingo gross gaming revenue (GGR) to the government and 24.25% of sports betting GGR.

PAGCOR orders removal of gambling billboard advertisements; DEPDev Secretary proposes higher tax

PAGCOR has ordered the removal of billboard and other out-of-home advertisements related to gambling activities by August 15. This includes advertisements on vehicles such as trains and buses. Separately, Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan proposed taxing online gambling through e-wallets. Currently, online gaming firms pay 38% of their e-casino and e-bingo gross gaming revenue (GGR) and 24.25% of their sports betting GGR to the government. In the first quarter of 2025, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) issued draft regulations that would prohibit e-wallets from offering or presenting gambling content.

Thai government withdraws bill to legalize casinos

Thailand’s chief government whip, Visuth Chainaroon, announced plans to formally withdraw the proposed casino legalization bill from the legislative agenda, following strong opposition. Legalizing casinos in Thailand would potentially make the country a new competitor in the region for VIP gaming clientele, particularly if it develops integrated resorts. In the Philippines, VIP clients made up 20% of Bloomberry Resorts’ GGR in the first quarter of 2025.

New Dept. of Energy secretary appointed; two ERC Commissioners retire

Malacañang has appointed Sharon Garin as the new Secretary of the Department of Energy (DOE), as confirmed by Palace Press Officer Claire Castro. Garin was previously the DOE’s officer-in-charge (OIC) following the reassignment of former Secretary Raphael Lotilla to the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), and had served as undersecretary under Lotilla. A lawyer and certified public accountant, Garin has over a decade of legislative experience as a representative of Iloilo. Meanwhile, two of the five commissioners of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), Alexis Lumbatan and Catherine Maceda—both appointed by former President Duterte—retired on July 10 after completing their seven-year terms. Despite the retirements, the ERC can continue to function, as decisions may still be made with the remaining three commissioners.

PH gov't hikes 2026 budget for education, agriculture, and transportation

The Philippine government plans to increase its 2026 national budget to ₱6.79 trillion, which is 7% higher than the 2025 budget of ₱6.33 trillion and equivalent to 22% of GDP. Based on the draft 2026 National Expenditure Program (NEP), allocations for education, agriculture, and transportation will be raised. The education sector is set to receive the highest allocation at ₱1.1 trillion, a 16% increase from this year’s ₱965 billion. Within this, the Department of Education (DepEd) will see a 20% budget increase to ₱782 billion, primarily for salary adjustments and the creation of teaching and non-teaching positions. The Department of Agriculture (DA) is proposed to receive ₱154 billion—a 23% increase—covering programs such as affordable rice and the development of agricultural food hubs. Meanwhile, the Department of Transportation (DOTr) is expected to double its budget to ₱196 billion from ₱88 billion, to support projects including the North-South Commuter Railway, the Metro Manila Subway Project, the New Cebu International Container Port, and upgrades to provincial airports. The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) is proposed to receive the second highest allocation at ₱854 billion, a 22% decrease from ₱1.1 trillion in 2025, due to reduced funding for access roads, multipurpose buildings, and flood mitigation structures. The NEP will be presented to the Cabinet next week and will be submitted to Congress two weeks after the SONA (July 28), with the constitutional deadline for the budget set at the end of August.

PH delegation to be sent to US to negotiate tariffs, possible FTA

A Philippine delegation led by Frederick Go, Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (SAPIEA), will travel to Washington D.C. next week to negotiate improved tariff terms with the United States. The delegation, which includes Trade Secretary Cristina Roque and Undersecretaries Perry Rodolfo and Allan Gepty, had already scheduled meetings from July 14–18 even before the US announced a new unilateral tariff rate increase to 20% (from 17%). The Philippine side also aims to pursue a free trade agreement (FTA) or a bilateral comprehensive economic partnership to secure specific tariff concessions. Go emphasized that semiconductors and electronics—key Philippine exports—remain exempt from tariffs due to US exemptions in place since April, which also cover pharmaceuticals and critical minerals. Electronic products accounted for 53% of the country’s total exports in 2024.
