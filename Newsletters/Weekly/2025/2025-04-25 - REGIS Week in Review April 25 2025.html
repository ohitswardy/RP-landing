REGIS REPORT

25 April 2025

After lackluster trading for the better part of the week, Philippine equities mirrored the relief rally in global equities come Friday amid cooling trade tensions and hopes for earlier Fed rate cuts. The PCOMP extended gains to a second week, climbing 2.2% or 134.13 pts WoW to 6,268.75 lifted by large caps SM (+6.2% WoW), ALI (+6.8% WoW), and ICT (+2.9% WoW) which accounted for nearly two-thirds of the index's weekly advance.

 

Comparably, key regional benchmarks closed higher by sub-1% to just over 3% WoW. The dollar logged its first weekly gain in over a month, rebounding past the 99-mark while gold eased to the $3,300-level.

 

On local outperformers, some consumer stocks jumped double-digits on general optimism over the sector which tracked positive sentiment in the region on domestic consumption themes that can weather the global trade uncertainties. URC (+14.4% WoW), PGOLD (+11.5% WoW), and CNPF (+10.0% WoW) were three of the four best-performers in the index this week, along with BLOOM (+13.4% WoW) that surged 18.9% on Friday alone following news that its online app will be launched soon and ahead of schedule.

 

1Q25 earnings season kicked off with solid BPI (+1.7% WoW) results that slightly beat Street forecasts. Strong loan growth was sustained at 13.2% and NIMs rose 30bps, taking net income +9% to P16.6bn for the quarter — a notable feat considering provisions already doubled as BPI brought NPL cover down to 100%. Meanwhile, BDO (unch. WoW) trailed market estimates with earnings +6% to P19.7bn on the back of still solid 12% loan growth, though limited details have been provided thus far.

 

Macro highlights this week revealed potential further drivers for consumption, in (i) the Department of Agriculture’s new program to sell P20/kg rice starting in Visayas; and (ii) the National Economic and Development Authority’s (NEDA) enhanced food stamp program, dubbed the ‘Reducing Food Insecurity and Undernutrition with Electronic Vouchers’ or ‘REFUEL’ Project. While the programs should help bring down inflation and increase consumer spending, these would come at a significant fiscal cost — at P3.5-4.5bn (up to c.$80m) for the DA’s rice project and at P74.6bn (c.$1.3bn) for REFUEL.

 

Market volumes recovered as traders returned from the Holy Week holidays, taking ADTO to $108m from $76m last week. Foreign participation finally normalized to 54%.

 

The peso continued to appreciate, +0.9% WoW to 56.255:$1. Yields rose slightly with the PH10Y +3.9bps to 6.3404%.

Philippine Strategy - Politics - Blowback from Duterte arrest?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Marcos, Jr approval ratings fall sharply in March; VP Duterte and allies up

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr’s approval ratings collapsed in surveys done after the arrest and extradition of former President Rodrigo Duterte in March. In contrast, VP Sara Duterte’s numbers improved. The polls for the Senate race in the May mid-terms show a similar trend, with Duterte-allied candidates gaining. Meanwhile, some on Marcos’s ticket (including his sister) have sought VP Duterte’s endorsement. That said, based on polling, Marcos’s coalition appears likely retain control of the Lower House. But allegiances in the Senate could shift unpredictably. If these ratings persist, President Marcos may decide to adopt more populist policies to bolster his standing.

 

Majority disapprove of Marcos, Jr; ratings fall across geographies, incomes…

A report from survey firm PulseAsia showed President Marcos’s approval rating fall 17pts to 25% in March, from 42% in February. More notably, his disapproval ratings jumped to 53% nationwide, from 32% the prior month. The trends were consistent across geographies and income classes, with the greatest swings seen in Metro Manila (or NCR). The results are similar to those from smaller survey firm Publicus earlier this month.

 

…while VP Sara Duterte’s numbers improve – Blowback?

In contrast, VP Duterte’s ratings improved across the board, with her approval rising to 59% and disapproval down to 16%. While not explicit in either survey, we note that the only major event between each poll’s dates was the arrest and extradition of former President Duterte on 11 March.

Cracks showing? Senate race got interesting, as Marcos’s sister breaks ranks

The shifts appear to be affecting the Senate race, with 12 seats (out of 24) up for grabs in the mid-term elections on 12 May. A separate PulseAsia survey showed Duterte’s closest allies Christopher “Bong” Go in the top spot and Ronald “Bato” dela Rosa at #3. Two more – actor Philip Salvador, and Congressman Marcoleta – are just outside the “Magic 12”. Meanwhile, Imee Marcos – the President’s sister – criticized the extradition of the elder Duterte, broke from the party, and received VP Duterte’s endorsement. Another member of President Marcos’s ticket Camille Villar has also reportedly sought VP Duterte’s endorsement.

 

Does it matter? Or is it just (more) noise we don’t need?

While President Marcos will likely retain control of the Lower House, per current polling, his coalition’s hold in the Senate seems less firm, given fast-shifting allegiances. This could also upend the Senate’s impeachment trial of the VP in July, complicating any consolidation of the President’s power.

 

Increased risk of populist policies?

This isn’t to say the President can’t bounce back. As they say, in politics, one month is a long time; more so three years till the next Presidential election. From a macro/market perspective, though, Marcos, Jr may decide to pursue more populist policies. The same PulseAsia survey showed inflation and higherwages as the top two concerns of voters. Handouts, subsidies, or aggressive minimum wage hikes, among others, could boost consumer (and voter) sentiment, but could come at longer-term costs to fiscal policy and/or inflation.

 
BPI - Solid 1Q25 results; updating estimates
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1Q25 NPAT up 9% YoY, in line; Maintaining TP and Buy rating

In a press release, BPI’s reported 1Q25 NPAT of P16.6bn, up 9% YoY, in line with our forecasts, but slightly ahead of the Street’s. Loan growth was 13.2%, sustaining the pace in 2024 (ex-RBank merger), while NIMs increased 30bps YoY by the bank’s estimate. The performance was notable, given the doubling of provision expense, as NPL Cover came down to 100%. We update our numbers for the 2024A, make modest changes to our 2025/26E, and introduce 2027E estimates. We maintain our target price of P160/sh, and our Buy recommendation on the stock.

 

Loan growth sustained at 13% YoY, NIMs jump 30bps YoY

Gross loans increased by 13.2% YoY in 1Q25, sustaining the 13% pace exRobinsons Bank (vs headline +19%) in 2024. Growth was driven by the “noninstitutional” book of consumer, MSME and micro loans. The bank estimated NIMs expanded 30bps YoY to 4.49%, pushing NII up 15% YoY, on improving loan mix, as well as the 250 bps RRR cuts in 4Q24. Strong fee income was offset by lower trading/fx gains, taking PPOP up 13% YoY.

Provisions double YoY, as credit costs normalize (as guided)

The bank’s NPL ratio increased 13bps QoQ to 2.26%, as 4Q tends to be flattered by write-offs at year-end. NPL Cover fell from 106% to 100%, as the bank completed its run-down of Covid-era over-provisioning. Provision expense doubled to P3bn, with credit cost at c54bps, in linewith management’s guidance. The higher provisions makes the 1Q25 bottomline more remarkable, in our view.

 

Forecasts tweaked; P160/sh target price and Buy call retained

We make minor adjustments to our 2025/26E earnings estimates, which are 2- 5% above the Street. We also introduce new 2027E forecasts. We maintain our P160/sh target price, based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g), with long-term ROE of 14.75%, COE of 12.0% and growth of 8.25% The stock has outperformed, up 11% ytd against the PSEi Index’s 8% decline. Despite that, our target price for BPI still provides 22% potential total return. Buy maintained. Key downside risks include higher NPLs from global uncertainties, and from increasing unsecured lending, while intense competition could squeeze NIMs.

 
Jollibee Foods - Refreshing our estimates
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Tweaking our estimates

We trim our EPS estimates for FY25/26 by -8/-3% and update our target price for Jollibee (JFC) to P250/sh (-17%) following the latest results, which show continued resilience for its domestic business but deterioration at two of the bigger parts of its international operations.

 

Still aiming to treble profit by 2028

JFC management kept its target of reaching P24bn of profit by 2028, roughly 3x the level in 2023, implying CAGR of ~24% for headline profit from last year. This is, in our view, a properly ambitious target but not a wholly unrealistic one. Achieving it, however, will still not be easy.

 

Domestic business a steady compounder

We believe the domestic business can sustainably deliver profit CAGR of >10% over the medium-term given its dominant brand position, diversified portfolio and a market potential that has not been fully tapped. Steady growth in income per capita should be supportive of this outlook.

 

International operations: Modest wins, big challenges

The international business in aggregate delivered a P1.1bn loss in FY24 and some sort of turnaround will be necessary for JFC to have a chance of reaching its profit goal, even if the likelihood of the segment producing half of JFC’s P24bn profit target by FY28 is very slim.

 

JFC completed the acquisition of Compose Coffee in 3Q24, and that acquisition has been able to deliver so far (EBITDA of P1bn for FY24). The expansion of the Jollibee brand in North America has also been gaining traction. However, operations at two of JFC’s larger overseas businesses (China + Smashburger) took a turn for the worse in FY24. Management appears to be set on sticking it out on those two despite the fumbles.

 

Estimates and target price trimmed

We adjust our estimates for the latest results, factoring in new businesses as well as the weaker turn at some international operations. We also trim our DCF-based (COE of 12%, terminal growth of 3.5%) target price for JFC to P250/share, which implies 25x PER for FY25E. Key downside risks: deterioration of consumer demand, downturn in overseas markets and dilutive acquisitions.

 
ACEN Corp - Planning rights issue of up to P30bn
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Raising up to P30bn through a rights issue

ACEN today announced that it is looking to raise up to P30bn via a stock rights offering at a minimum indicative offer price of P2.30/sh.  The final terms of the offer will be determined once relevant regulatory approvals are obtained and market conditions are deemed suitable. The disclosure also stated that its principal shareholders, Ayala Corp. (AC PM) and Arran Investments, who own a combined 75.1% stake in ACEN, have committed to subscribe to their entitlements subject to finalization of terms.

 

Proceeds enough for 3GW of RE projects

We expect ACEN to use the proceeds to fund its capacity expansions. Assuming a debt-to-total capital ratio of 70%, we estimate this exercise could allow the company to undertake as much as P100bn worth of projects. Note that as of March, ACEN had 3.7GW of additional RE capacity that are either under construction or have been “committed”.  This is on top of 3.3GW that is currently operational.

 

On our estimates, this capital-raising exercise should be sufficient to fund close to 3GW of solar capacity. 

Given that over 2GW of projects undergoing construction have already been pre-funded, we estimate this exercise could allow ACEN to grow its operational RE capacity to over 8GW over the next three years. 

 

Substantial EPS dilution near-term but…

Raising P30bn at somewhere near the minimum price (P2.30/share) is likely to lead to meaningful short-term EPS dilution. The capital raised, however, will allow ACEN to complete ~3GW of projects, and possibly more than double its FY24 core income when the projects are fully operational.

 

Gearing should drop

Simplistically, raising P30bn of new equity could lower ACEN’s net debt to P144bn from P174bn and its gearing ratio down to 0.91x from 1.36x.

 

It is unclear if ACEN’s two principal shareholders intend to purchase any unsold rights shares.  If participation from minorities in the rights offer is absent and the two shareholders take up the entire rights issue, the company’s public float (officially 24.4%) might fall to just around 20%. Membership in the main local stock index (PSEi) requires a minimum public float of 20%.

BOP swings to US$2.0bn deficit in March 2025; Final GIR settles at US$106.7bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$2.0bn. This is a reversal from both Feb 2025's US$3.1bn surplus and the US$1.2bn surplus in Mar 2024. The deficit reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits drawdowns for external debt obligations as well as its net foreign exchange operations. External debt obligations that matured in March includes a US$2.0bn 25-year bond. This brings the 1Q25 deficit at $3.0bn, reversing from the $238mn surplus for the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Mar 2025 stood at US$106.7bn, down from the US$107.4bn in end-Feb 2025. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is -0.8% of GDP. GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025.

NEDA approves enhanced P74.6bn e-voucher food stamp program

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), chaired by President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., has approved the Reducing Food Insecurity and Undernutrition with Electronic Vouchers (REFUEL) Project. The program will be implemented from July 2025 to July 2028 and costs an estimated P74.6bn, to be funded by the Asian Development Bank, Agence Française de Développement, and the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries Fund for International Development. The REFUEL program is an enhancement of the pilot Philippine Food Strategic Transfer and Alternative Measures Program (STAMP) of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), rolled out late last year. REFUEL targets to benefit around 750,000 food-insecure households, which is higher than STAMP's 300,000 household beneficiaries last year. These food-insecure households will be given an electronic benefit transfer (EBT) card with monthly food credits worth P3,000 that can be used to buy high-nutrition food items from DSWD's partner retailers. STAMP's beneficiaries had to fulfill two conditions to receive the food vouchers, 1) attend nutrition education sessions once a month and 2) participate in employment promotion activities at gov't job fairs or classes conducted by TESDA (Technical Education and Skills Development Authority).

PH targets beneficial tariff deal with the US

Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (SAPIEA) Secretary Frederick Go will lead a delegation to Washington to discuss the tariffs on Philippine goods. The PH gov't stated that it is confident on securing a "mutually advantageous" arrangement with the US ahead of scheduled trade talks with US counterparts in the first week of May. United Portusers Confederation of the Philippines President Nelson Mendoza elaborated on the current situation as a lot of orders for exports, although have not been canceled, were put on hold. He added that orders are being held due to the tariff announcements given the uncertainty of what will happen after the 90-day pause. Recall that US President Donald Trump introduced 10% blanket tariffs on all its trading partners as he paused the initial plan to impose higher reciprocal countries for 90 days. Philippine exports to the US face a 17% tariff, second lowest among ASEAN after Singapore's 10%.

Colliers: 54% of firms adopt full return-to-office

Colliers said that based on its survey: 54% of firms have adopted a full return-to-office (RTO) set-up, 41% are operating under a hybrid scheme, and 5% have a full work-from-home set-up. It said that the percentage of companies adopting full RTO has been increasing.

BSP to set new pricing scheme for lower fund transfer fees

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is set to release a draft this week on a new pricing mechanism targeted at lowering fees for electronic fund transfers. BSP Deputy Governor Mamerto Tangonan did not disclose full details but stated that the pricing mechanism will apply to all transaction sizes and is to be straightforward as well as easy to implement. Tangonan said fees will be low but may not be zero, as the rates will hinge on "competitive forces". Current InstaPay fees hovers around P25 per transaction. He emphasized that lowering fees will be key in converting non-users to expand the country's digital payments base. The central bank has a goal for online payments to make up 60-70% of the country's total retail transaction volume by 2028. Note that the share of digital payment transactions to total monthly retail payments in the country grew from 42% in 2022 to 53% percent in 2023 (from a BSP report dated July 2024). The value of electronic fund transfers through PESONet and InstaPay surged 39% YoY to P5.29trn as of end-Mar 2025.

AMLC assesses risks related to online gambling

BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr said that the Anti money laundering council (AMLC) is assessing potential risks and vulnerabilities related to online gambling that criminals could exploit to facilitate illegal flow of funds. DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is the market leader in online gambling, with about 50% market share. The BSP previously published draft guidelines that would prohibit access of online gambling via digital marketplaces (which would include GCash).
