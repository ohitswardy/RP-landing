REGIS REPORT

05 September 2025

The Phils had a virtually flat week, closing 6 points lower vs last week. The 0.1% decline this week, extends three straight weeks of declines for the Phils market.

Even as markets continued to anticipate rate cuts by the Fed, a lack of inspiration domestically kept trading lackluster. Foreigners continued to sell the market, with this week’s net outflow reaching US$28m. Foreign net selling YTD has reached US$726m.

The inflation print for August came in at 1.5%, an acceleration from the 0.9% reading in July. While this came in above consensus expectations of 1.2%, it is still well within BSP targets. Meanwhile, the PHP improved 0.4% this week to close at P56.917/US4 even as the USD reflected expectations of a September Fed rate cut. US 10 yr bond yields were lower.

The view on interest rates was reflected in the banks this week – as China Banking Corp (-6.0%), BDO (-1.3%), and BPI (-1.2%) emerged as the top drags to the index.

On the flipside, property name Ayala Land was the top index mover, with its 3.2% helping to offset the market’s losses this week.

ICTSI (+1.0%) as well as SM Investments (+1.1%) recovered from last week’s declines and likewise muted the PSEi’s drop.

Globe (+4.5%) also gained, recovering from its 7.7% drop in the previous week. Converge (+1.1%) also managed to close higher this week, following recent sharp drops as investors discounted the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) which is expected to result in increased competition for the incumbents.

The consumer space was mixed, with the gainers this week – Puregold (+3.4%) and Jollibee (+0.3%) – reporting solid operations for 2Q. On the other hand, CNPF (-4.2%) and URC (-1.25%) fell, with companies tempering FY growth guidance.

News this week was dominated by pronouncements by the government. These include a proposal to lower the VAT from the current 12% to 10%, as well as a proposal from the Department of Energy (DOE) proposal to shut down all coal mining and coal-fired power plants. The Finance Department is pushing for the passage of new taxes, including a tax on single-use plastics, a tax amnesty program, and minimum global tax. Meanwhile, President Marcos signed into law a reform of the tax regime governing large-scale metallic miners.  All this, amidst widespread public outrage over corruption uncovered in the Dept of Public Works (DPWH) relating to flood control projects.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Maynilad sets October listing date for IPO

In its updated preliminary prospectus, Maynilad said that its IPO will be priced on Oct 13. Offer period will run from Oct 16 to 22 and listing is targeted on Oct 30. Maynilad is offering 2.289bn (1.9bn primary and 355mn secondary) shares to the public at a maximum offer price of P20/sh. At the max. offer price and full subscription of offer shares, Maynilad’s market cap could be P151bn, and public float will be 30%. Under its franchise, Maynilad is required to offer at least 30% of its outstanding capital stock to Filipinos by Jan 21, 2027.

Metro Pacific Water reportedly eyeing Prime Water assets?

A media source hinted at Metro Pacific Water’s interest in taking full control of Villar-led Prime Water Infrastructure Corp. The source indicated that Metro Pacific is currently in talks for a potential buyout and that the company could take the lead if the acquisition goes through. Another source also said that several other groups have expressed interest.

Metro Pacific Water is the holding company which owns an effective 53% stake in West Zone operator Maynilad. PrimeWater currently holds 75 joint venture agreements with local water districts and LGUs in Pampanga, Bulacan, Cavite, Leyte, Bacolod City, and Bukidnon.

SM Prime to complete 12 new malls over the next five years

SM Prime said it would spend P150bn over the next five years to redevelop 16 of its existing malls and complete 12 new malls in the Philippines. Among the malls to be redeveloped are SM North EDSA and SM Megamall, two of its three largest malls. Note that the redevelopment of Mall of Asia was already finished recently. The company currently has 88 Philippine malls. We expect minimal changes to our forecasts/estimates.

Alliance Global gains control of Manila Bay integrated resort project

In its disclosure to the PSE, Suntrust Resort Holdings (SUN PM) said it will pursue an agreement with Alliance Global. In the agreement, Suntrust Resort, which is controlled by the LET Group, will effectively hold a 20% indirect interest in Entertainment City Resorts Corporation (ECRC), a subsidiary of Alliance Global. ECRC will assume all rights and obligations related to the Westside Integrated Resorts Project, which consists of a 5-star hotel, a casino complex, a mall, and a theater complex. Suntrust Resort has spent c. US$700mn out of the US$1.25bn construction cost for the casino complex. The completion of the IR was previously moved from 4Q25 to 3Q26. It will have 280 gaming tables and 1,260 gaming machines – and these will add 20% to table capacity and 13% to gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City.

ERC extends Meralco, First Gen power purchase agreement

The Energy Regulatory Commission has granted a five-month extension to Meralco and First Gas Power Corp.’s (FGPC) power purchase agreement (PPA). This extension to Jan 31, 2026 was implemented to avoid potential power outages. The PPA, covering 1,000 MW of FGPC’s Santa Rita power plant’s capacity, was originally due to expire on Aug 28.

Among the conditions set by the ERC are a minimum dispatch of the Santa Rita power plant and the use of the previously approved rate, which is equivalent to or computed at an 83% plant capacity factor. FGPC is a wholly-owned unit of First Gen (FGEN PM). Last June, FGEN agreed to sell 60% of its natgas assets to Razon-led Prime Infrastructure.

ACEN completes divestment of oil assets

ACEN Corp executed a deed of absolute sale, transferring ownership of a total 189MW oil-fired plants to its parent, AC Energy Infrastructure Corp. (ACEIC). The sale covers diesel plants: 52MW in Bulacan, 116MW in Subic, 21MW in Luzon. ACEIC is a wholly-owned unit of Ayala Corp. (AC PM) and owns 58% of ACEN. Similar to its exit from the 246MW coal-fired plant SLTEC, we expect ACEN to maintain the contracts of these diesel plants so that revenue/income streams will still accrue to ACEN.

ACEN secures approval for 900MW US$3bn wind project in Australia

ACEN said that its 900MW Robbins Island Wind project in Tasmania has obtained regulatory approval from the Federal Government, according to ACEN Australia’s managing director David Pollington. Project is estimated to cost US$3bn; ACEN did not provide a completion timetable but noted that the facility may start generating electricity by 2030. ACEN previously said that it is planning to build another ~2GW of renewable energy project in Australia.

ACEN currently has over 1,000 MW of operating plants in Australia, which we estimate contributes 9% to core att. EBITDA. ACEN has 7,053 GW of RE capacity, of which 3,598 MW are operating, 2,941 MW are under construction, and 514MW are committed.

AC Health finalizes entry of new investor

Temasek-backed fund, ABC Impact, completed its capital infusion into AC Health, allowing it to own an initial ~16% economic stake in AC Health. ABC Impact subscribed to 121mn common shares and 208mn redeemable preferred shares in AC Health. AC Health plans to use the funds to expand its portfolio to at least 10 hospitals (vs. 6 today), 300 clinics (vs. 270), and 1,150 pharmacies (vs. 879) by 2027. The transaction value was not disclosed, however, during AC’s 2Q briefing, management mentioned that the deal valued AC Health at a post-money EV of US$500mn, which is equivalent to “high-teens to low-20s” forward multiples. AC Health generated EBITDA of P586mn in 1H25 (+144% YoY) and trimmed net losses to P100mn.

Metro Pacific Tollways appoints former PAL executive as new CEO

Metro Pacific Tollways (MPTC) appointed Rogelio Santa Maria as its new President and CEO effective Sep 1. He will succeed Mr. Jose Maria Lim, who served as interim president and will remain on the board as a director. Mr. Santa Maria previously served as Philippine Airlines’ (PAL) president and CEO during the pandemic and headed its restructuring efforts. MPTC has undergone several leadership changes since 2023. MPTC is 93%-owned by Metro Pacific and 6.6% by Japan-based Mitsui.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Dept. of Finance expects to hit lower end of GDP goal

Finance Undersecretary and Chief Economist Domini Velasquez stated that they expect GDP growth to be faster in 2H25 to be able to meet the lower end of the 5.5%-6.5% growth target for 2025. Recall that 2Q GDP growth was 5.5%, slightly faster than 1Q's 5.4%. GDP growth in the 2H needs to average 5.6% in order to meet the minimum 5.5% growth target. Velasquez expects that household consumption (>70% of the economy) will continue to be the main growth driver alongside benign inflation and lower interest rates. She also noted a lower base for 2H25 given that GDP growth was 5.3% in 2H24 vs. 6.2% in 1H24.

For context, the gov't targets 5.5-6.5% growth for 2025 and 6.0-7.0% for 2026-2028. Consensus and Regis expect 2025 GDP growth to be 5.5%.

DPWH faces probe and revamp following corruption scandal

Newly appointed Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vince Dizon has asked for the resignations of all executives at the department in what appears to be a sweeping revamp. The call follows an explosive corruption scandal over a multitude of projects meant to address perennial flooding in many areas. Dizon noted that the flood control projects also need to be “corrected” and re-bid as soon as possible given that "the people [especially in flood-prone communities] need the projects." Allocations for such projects rose from under P50 billion a year in 2010 to over P350 billion by 2025, amounting to a total of P1.4 trillion over the past 15 years. In the proposed 2026 budget, ~P275bn is being allotted for flood control

A number of contractors, even those not involved in the ongoing investigation into flagged flood control projects, were said to have stopped or partially halted their operations and progress in gov't contracts awarded by the DPWH. Some contractors noted that receivables from the DPWH have been steadily accumulating and that they are unsure on when they will be paid by the gov't. Certain banks have also been said to pause on new loan releases connected to flood control projects until the situation stabilizes

Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto commented that "maybe if the money had been spent better, we could have grown by 6%." The country has failed to hit its growth targets in the recent years: 5.7% in 2024 (vs. 6.0-6.5% target) and 5.6% in 2023 (vs. 6.0-7.0% target). Recto noted that the anomalous flood control projects by the DPWH have cost the economy an estimated P42bn-P118bn from 2023 to 2025 or around 95,000-266,000 jobs.

1H25 infra spend amounted to P720bn, flat YoY and broadly in line with the 1H government’s target. Government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP).

Dept. of Finance pushes for Congress to prioritize key tax reforms

The Dept. of Finance (DOF) wants the 20th Congress to support the gov't's fiscal consolidation effort by prioritizing several tax measures. Finance Undersecretary Karlo Adriano stated that they want to tap new revenue streams to ease the limited fiscal space as the debt-to-GDP ratio currently stands at 63% as of 1H25. He emphasizes the need for a fiscal buffer amidst heightened global uncertainties and in case of a crisis. Some key tax measures being pushed by the DOF are as follows:

Single-use plastics excise tax: P150/kg on single-use plastics with an annual 4% increase; Estimated to generate ~P12bn annually for the first five years of implementation.

Tax amnesty: Proposes a general tax amnesty on various taxes, at 2% of total assets (as of Dec 2024) or 5% of net worth, whichever is higher. Estate tax amnesty, which expired in June 2025, is sought to be extended to June 2028. The gov't collected P2.7bn in estate tax amnesty in 2024 and P4.2bn in 7M25.

Global minimum tax (GMT). 15% domestic top-up tax for multinational companies with a global revenue exceeding EUR€750mn (~P50bn) in two of the four preceding fiscal years. The Philippines, as part of the OECD’s Base Erosion and Profit Shifting program, risks losing revenues without a legislated 15% GMT. The absence of a GMT framework has already cost an estimated P163bn annually since 2021, as taxes may be paid in other jurisdictions.

Registered electric vehicles (EVs) up 5% to 29,715 units in end-July 2025 from end-2024

The number of registered EVs has reached 29,715 units in end-July, up from 24,286 units in end-Dec 2024. Note that the data covers 4W/2W EVs but 2W is likely <5% of total EVs. As of end-2024, there are a total of 14.6mn registered vehicles, of which ~7mn were 4W. Electric Vehicle Association of the Philippines (EVAP) expects the count to reach 35,000 units by year-end.

Proposed lower 10% VAT under review by House panel

The "VAT Reduction Act of 2025" or House Bill 4302 proposes to reduce the current value-added tax (VAT) rate by two percentage points from 12% to 10%. The bill, if passed, is estimated to reduce annual VAT collections by ~P200bn, which is equivalent to ~P7,000 savings per household each year.

Congressman Leandro Leviste said he authored and filed the bill to further address inflation and provide relief to millions of Filipino households. He highlighted that increased consumption and improved tax compliance could offset the revenue shortfall. The bill includes a clause that allows the President, upon recommendation of the Dept. of Finance (DOF) and Budget Committee, to temporarily restore the VAT rate to 12% in case the gov't's deficit exceeds target projections

House Committee on Ways and Means Chairman Romiro Quimbo stated that the panel will give it serious consideration as "any measure that eases the tax burden [or makes our tax system more progressive] is very welcome." DOF Undersecretary Karlo Adriano, however, warns of several billions of lost revenue if VAT is reduced with no new taxes in place. Leviste added that he may push for a "wealth" tax to offset possible revenue losses.

The Philippines imposes the highest VAT rate in Southeast Asia at 12%. For context, Vietnam and Cambodia impose a 10% VAT; Indonesia, 11%; Singapore, 9%; Thailand, 7%; Malaysia, Laos, and Myanmar 5-7%. Recall that the VAT rate was raised to 12% from 10% back in 2005. VAT collections have grown from P157bn in 2005 to P1.2trn in 2024.

Government mulls shutdown of coal mines, power plants

The Department of Energy (DOE) plans to conduct a study to evaluate the impact of a permanent closure of coal mines and coal-fired power plants in the country, according to DOE director Michael Sinocruz on the sidelines of the 2025 Philippine Energy Transition Dialogue. The study may start next year and will focus on the possible impact on workers and host communities. For context: Coal-fired plants account for 45% of nationwide dependable capacity and 62% of generation, while renewables account for 31% of nationwide dependable capacity and 22% of generation. The government aims to raise the share of renewables to 35% of overall generation by 2030.
