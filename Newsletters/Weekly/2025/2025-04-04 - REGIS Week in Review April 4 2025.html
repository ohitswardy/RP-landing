REGIS REPORT

04 April 2025

Philippine equities were not spared from the rout in global markets that followed Trump's "Liberation Day" tariff announcements. The PCOMP dropped 1.0% or 63.25 pts WoW to 6,084.19, erasing modest gains from earlier in the week after Trump's April 2nd declaration bore a 17% tariff on goods imported by the US from the Philippines.

 

This marks the fourth straight weekly decline for the PCOMP, although the tariff-driven sell-off was less severe vs more export-oriented markets within Asean and in the broader Asia region.

 

Global port operator ICT (-9.0% WoW) was the biggest drag on the index despite Mexico and Canada being exempt from this round of sweeping tariffs, as the broader outlook on global trade has sharply deteriorated. Consumer names also continued to underperform, with CNPF (-8.9% WoW) and JFC (-3.9% WoW) among the PCOMP's largest decliners while investors assess their exposure from US exports and US operations.

 

On domestic macro, the market shrugged off better-than-expected March inflation that dropped to 1.8% from 2.1% previously, and vs the 2.0% median Street estimate. This is the lowest level in nearly five years, since the 1.6% print in May 2020, thanks largely to the continued fall in food inflation.

 

The central bank maintained it is keeping a "measured approach" to monetary policy easing while uncertainty over global economic policies and their impact on the domestic economy has "increased significantly." Consensus forecast is for a 25-bp BSP rate cut to 5.50% at the April 10 policy-setting meeting.

 

Developers showed relative strength with ALI (+6.3% WoW) the largest positive mover for the PCOMP and Property (+1.6% WoW) the best-performing sector index for the week as investors weighed the evolving rate outlook.

 

The peso strengthened by 1.1% WoW to 56.778 -- breaking below the 57:$1 mark for the first time since October 2024.

 

ADTO improved to $121m in the shortened trading week, lifted by a $110-m placement on RCR Friday. Net foreign outflow slowed to $15m.

Philippine Strategy - Trump tariffs - Less negative for PH; Unintended positive consequences?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

It’s all relative – lower tariff rates, and PH less reliant on exports

The US says it will impose new “reciprocal tariffs” of 17%* on imports from the Philippines, much lower than those on other Asian countries (ex-Singapore). Within the region, the Philippines is much less exposed, with exports to the US accounting for just 3% of GDP, and with 1/3rd of which (semiconductors) likely to be exempt from the new duties. Also, few major listed companies have significant direct exports to the US.

 

Domestic demand-driven economy; Much less reliant on exports

Unlike much of the region, the Philippines is a domestic, consumer-driven economy. In 2024, private consumption accounted for 77% of GDP. On the production side, GDP is 63% services, with only 28% from industry.

But watch out for Current Account Deficit: 3.8% of GDP in 2024

That said, we would still be wary over the impact of the new tariff on Philippine exports. The BSP last week reported that the Current Account Deficit (CAD) for 2024 came in at $17.5bn, equivalent to 3.8% of GDP, wider than the 2.8% of GDP recorded in 2023.

 

Is there a silver lining?

There could, however, be unlooked-for positive consequences. The much lower tariff on US-bound goods from the Philippines could, encourage a shift in manufacturing here. The possibility of lower consumer prices as exporting countries look to sell excess production may also be an unanticipated benefit. Also, with significantly lower tariffs compared to our Asian / ASEAN neighbors, the Philippines could benefit from some manufacturing / re-export capacity moving here.

 
ICTSI - Global disruption
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Initial thoughts on impact of US tariffs

The global tariffs announced by the US government, if actually implemented, should have a significant disruptive impact on trade. For ICTSI, we believe the direct adverse impact on its port operations is likely to be minimal, but the greater risk is from a broad economic downturn it may spark. We’ve baked in a 15% topline increase this year and we estimate every 1% drop in our revenue forecasts could shave off 2% of EBITDA and 4% of profit.

 

Trump slaps blanket tariffs globally

President Trump ordered to impose a blanket 10% tariff on all imports by April 5 and implement country-specific tariffs on April 9. The order exempts some goods including copper, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, lumber articles, certain critical minerals, and energy and energy products. ICT operates ports in 19 countries globally and import levies on these countries range from 10% (Honduras, Brazil, Australia, Colombia, and Argentina) to as high as 47% for Madagascar. The Philippines, where ICT derived 31% of its revenues last year, will face a tariff of 18% but our Philippines Strategist believes the impact on the economy could be mildly negative (see here).

 

US exempts Mexico from across-the-board tariffs but…

Surprisingly, Mexico avoided the worst-case scenario, as goods compliant under the US, Mexico, Canada (USMCA) trade agreement were exempted from the blanket tariff.

However, President Trump already unilaterally reneged on the USMCA after he began imposing 25% tariffs on automobiles on April 3, as well as on aluminum and steel last Mar 12. On May 3, the US administration will begin implementing a 25% tariff on automotive parts from Mexico that are not compliant with USMCA. Note that automobile and auto parts each account for 18% and 14%, respectively, of Mexico’s exports to the US while Mexico’s exports of steel and aluminum to the US are minimal.

 

Negative for global trade but direct Impact on ICT’s ports likely minimal

How the affected emerging market countries react to the recent announcement will be closely watched but we anticipate that many will find a diplomatic approach with the US to limit the damage.

 

Global trade will take a hit from these protectionist policies. Note that management estimates only 3% of its global volumes are direct exports to the US and a large share of the exports of ICT’s host countries to the US comprise staples (e.g. bananas, coffee, vegetables, vanilla, dates) and commodities (e.g. nickel, cobalt) that the US cannot produce. We reckon more discretionary items such as automobiles and garments, which are main exports of Mexico and Honduras, respectively, could be adversely affected. Overall, we believe the direct impact to ICT’s volumes could be minimal, with the caveat that a possible resulting recession in many of the countries could exacerbate that.

WLCON 4Q24 net profit drops 27%

Wilcon (WLCON PM) posted a FY24 profit of P2.6bn, 27% lower YoY—behind consensus expectations. For the 4Q alone, net profit dipped even lower by 46% YoY to P2.5bn as operating profit fell by 41% to P410mn. Revenue growth in 4Q was down 2% YoY, still weak relative to the 1% decline in 9M24 as demand remained soft in the quarter. CNPF targets mid-teen growth in 2025

Century Pacific (CNPF PM) Chief Operating Officer Gregory Banzon guided for mid-teens growth for both top line and bottom line. He highlighted that the company had consistently posted ~13% sales and income growth and thus seeks to be consistent. The COO also gave guidance on capex, allotting about P3bn mainly for boosting production capacity for the tuna, coconut, and pet food segments. This was similar to 2024's capex spend of P3-4bn. Banzon also noted that the company cut prices for their canned meat, tuna, and sardines products as commodity prices corrected to ease the burden of the consumer. CNPF has yet to report its FY24 earnings but as of 9M24 posted a net income of P5.3bn (+14% YoY) with revenues up 13% to P56.9bn on the back of better exports and branded segments.

DTI secretary sees potential opportunities from Trump's tariff push

Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) Secretary Cristina Roque commented that US President Trump's sweeping tariffs could present an opportunity for the Philippines as it was hit with a reciprocal levy of 17%, which is within the lower end relative to its neighbors. She noted that significantly higher tariffs were put on Vietnam (46%), Thailand (36%), and Taiwan (32%). Roque highlighted that the US remains a "crucial export market" as the US is the top destination for PH exports, comprising a ~16% share of the country's total exports in 2024. The DTI Secretary also stated that they are prepared to discuss "enhanced market access" for key US export interests, which include automobiles, dairy products, frozen meat, and dairy products. She mentioned that she has already reached out to her US counterpart to set up talks.

DOF, BSP cautions elevated risks to PH growth from US tariffs

The Department of Finance in a statement noted that the 17% reciprocal tariff on PH exports may hamper PH growth as the expected decline in international trade and global growth slowdown may bring about risks of supply chain disruptions, accelerated inflation, and elevated interest rates. Despite the headwinds, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto highlighted that the consumption-driven economy provides resiliency against trade wars and can withstand economic shocks. He also noted potential upside on opportunities from global trade development, with the PH positioned to possibly be a hub for global value chains in industries such as electronics, textiles, food, and automobiles. The country's exports for coconut-based products and garments could also increase in market share as major competitors face higher tariffs. In an effort to diversify export markets, the government continues to pursue new and expand free trade agreements with economies such as the UAE, EU, Chile, and Canada. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) also commented that there may be further pressure on the peso-dollar exchange rate from the reciprocal tariffs. But Remolona noted that the analysis is still being done on how the US Tariffs may impact consumer prices and the peso.

March CPI slows to 1.8% YoY from 2.1% in Feb

Philippines March CPI rose 1.8% YoY, below the 2% consensus forecast. This was also near the lower-end of the BSP's 1.7-2.5% forecast range, and brings 1Q25 average CPI to 2.2%. March CPI fell 0.2% MoM vs the estimated +0.1%. Core inflation for March at +2.2% YoY. Overall inflation eased, primarily due to the slower annual increment in the index of food and nonalcoholic beverages in March. Note that rice prices fell 7.7% YoY in March.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.15bn in Feb

The February trade deficit narrowed to US$3.16bn, down 11% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports and growth recovery in exports. This is the lowest deficit in four years. Imports for the month inched down 2% YoY to US$9.41bn, a significant slowdown from the 11% increase in Jan. Primarily driven by the decline in fuel imports, which was down 23% YoY to US$1.3bn, negating the increases in other major goods categories. Exports grew 4% YoY to US$6.25bn, the second month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction. Electronics exports grew by 3% YoY to $3.52bn, a turnaround from the 3% YoY decline in Jan. These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 6% YoY to US$2.73bn.

February gov't budget deficit widens 4.1% YoY to P171.4bn

The government recorded a budget deficit of P171.4bn, which is wider by 4.1% YoY but a reversal from the surplus of P68.4bn in Jan 2025. Revenue collections in Feb grew 12.4% YoY to P251.8bn, primarily driven by growth in tax revenues, which is up 10.9% YoY. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 15.7% to P159.7bn mainly due to an increase in collections of corporate and personal income taxes, value-added tax, excise tax on tobacco and alcohol, and documentary stamp tax. Customs saw modest growth, up 1.7% to P71.8bn in collections from higher VAT and excise collections. Non-tax revenues increased by 12.6% to P17.4bn from higher treasury income, PAGCOR profit, and privatization proceeds. Expenditures are up 8.9% YoY in Feb to P423.2bn as both primary expenses and interest payments were up 1.3% and 9.9%, respectively. Primary spending for the month rose 9.9% YoY to P374.8bn. The increase in primary spending was attributed to the capital expenditures of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), health, and social protection programs. Interest payments increased slightly by 1.3% from higher coupon payments on domestic securities but offset by lower foreign payments. The gov't for 2025 targets the budget deficit to narrow to 5.3% of GDP from 5.7% in 2024, and the government aims to further reduce this to 3.7% in 2028.

Government debt inches up to P16.63tn in end-Feb

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.96% MoM (+P319.3bn) to P16.63tn as of end-Feb. This was due to the net issuance of new domestic and external debt, which was slightly offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P58.375 end-Jan to P57.990 end-Feb). Domestic debt rose by 1.26% (+P139.6bn) MoM to P11.22tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P140.7bn). External debt increased by 3.44% (+P179.64bn) MoM to P5.41tn, given net foreign debt additions (P193.7bn). This includes the previous global bond offering of $2.25bn USD for the 10- and 25- USD bonds as well as the $1.08bn USD for the 25-year EUR bonds. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 67.5% and 32.5% being external borrowings. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn, which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn. This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%.

Commercial banks’ loan growth decelerates to 12.2% YoY in February; Domestic liquidity (M3) slows to 6.3%

Loans of commercial banks expanded by 12.2% YoY in February, moderating from 12.8% pace in January. March WESM prices spike to highest in 7 months

Nationwide power spot prices soared by 95.5% MoM to P5.34/kwh in March from P2.73/kwh in February. Cited that nationwide supply slipped by 4% MoM to 19,611 MW while demand rose 6% MoM to 13,670 MW. Arjon Valencia, IEMOP corporate planning and communications manager, attributed the increase in demand to the “extreme heat” during the first week of March. For the Luzon grid, spot prices soared by 103% MoM to P5.50/kwh after supply fell 6.5% MoM to 13,530 MW while demand rose 7.1% MoM to 9,713 MW. Assuming the power prices for March, we estimate spot prices in 1Q25 may have averaged P3.75/kwh, down 12% QoQ and 25% YoY. Semirara (SCC PM) has the largest exposure to the spot market (~70% of capacity). Lower spot prices could mean lower profits for its power unit (~30% of profits).

SEC relaxes investment limits for equity and multi-asset funds

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in a memorandum circular issued on March 28, eased the single business group (SBG) investment limit for equity funds, balanced funds, and multi-asset funds exposed to equity securities. This was put into place given the requests from various fund managers to be exempted from the SBG limit. An SBG is defined as a company and its related entities, including subsidiaries and parent firms. Under the current limit, an investment company is prohibited "from investing, in aggregate, more than 20% of its net assets in transferrable securities, money market securities, deposits, and OTC financial derivatives issued by any SBG. Additionally, investments in OTC financial derivatives that are non-investment grade or with an unrated counterparty should not exceed 5% of the net assets of the investment company. The new circular exempts certain funds—those without actual investment in financial derivatives such as equity funds, balanced funds, and multi-asset funds (that have actual exposure to equity securities) will not be subject to the SBG limit. Instead, they will be subject to the single entity or issuer limitation from the IRR (implementing rules and regulations) of the Investment Company Act. "The investment company must not invest, in aggregate, more than 20% of its net assets in transferrable securities, money market securities, deposits, and financial derivatives issued by a single entity or issuer." The SEC stated that it will not impose fines/penalties for any breach of the SBG limit committed for covered funds from May 15, 2020, until March 27, 2025. But breaches for the single-issuer cap will be subject to penalties under relevant regulations.

Batangas LNG plants to attain full capacity by May — DoE

The Department of Energy (DoE) expects sufficient power supply during the summer, as two gas-fired power plants in Batangas are set to generate a full combined capacity of 2,500 megawatts (MW) by mid-May. According to Energy Undersecretary Rowena Cristina L. Guevara, instead of the 1,350 MW produced from the two plants, there will now be 2,500 MW. South Premiere Power Corp. (SPPC) and Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. (EERI) implemented a three-day temporary shutdown of their gas-fired power plants in Batangas, and operations have since resumed, increasing output to 1,350 MW from the previous 1,300 MW. Major energy firms, including Meralco PowerGen, Aboitiz Power, and San Miguel Global Power, have invested $3.3 billion to launch the country’s first LNG facility. Under the agreement, MGen and Therma jointly invested in two of SMGP’s gas-fired power plants: the 1,278 MW Ilijan power plant and the new 1,320 MW combined-cycle power facility. According to Energy Assistant Secretary Mario C. Marasignan, while electricity supply is adequate, there is still a possibility of yellow or red alerts due to reserve shortages. The DoE urges consumers to adopt energy-saving measures, as peak demand is projected at 14,769 MW for Luzon, 3,329 MW for Visayas, and 2,552 MW for Mindanao.
