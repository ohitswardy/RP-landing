REGIS REPORT

26 September 2025

The Philippine market erased the previous week's gains with a five-day losing streak, and is now re-testing the 6,000 handle once again -- a level last seen in April when global markets roiled over the April 9 Trump tariffs. The PCOMP ended lower by 3.8% or 237.37 pts WoW to 6,027.12. The peso also notably fell 1.7% WoW to 58.115:$1 -- the worst performer among Asian currencies.

 

Domestic investor confidence weakened anew, as ongoing hearings on the corruption scandals at the national public works department now point to even wider institutionalized corruption involving not just officers of the agency but select Senators and Congressmen as well.

 

Widely held names were sold off across banks, conglos, property.

 

The big underperformer was the property sector with ALI (-5.8% WoW) and SMPH (4.2% WoW) weighing down on the market heavily. This was partly due to less dovish comments by the BSP that interest rates have hit a "sweet spot," alluding to rates for now having less propensity to fall.

 

More importantly, pre-sales of high end property is expected to slow down as part of the fallout from the ongoing corruption investigation.

 

Likewise, government spending is also expected to slow (at least temporarily) as officials become more circumspect and careful with disbursements. This in turn could have implications on overall growth, which already had been tepid through 1H25 despite the supposed boost from election related spending.

 

No surprise then that market proxies and widely held names tumbled anew: SM (-4.2% WoW), AC (-4.6% WoW), BDO (-8.2% WoW).

 

GTCAP (-10.1% WoW) stood out also, with investors disenchanted with its purchase of 113m shares of MBT (-2.9% WoW) (equivalent to 2.5% interest in the bank) which the market suspects were from a hold co controlled by the family. While the purchase valuation was not outrageous (0.7x PBR, 7% yield, 6x PER), the deal was frowned on and highlights the current fragile overall sentiment.

Philippine Strategy - "Flood control" fallout limited (for now) -- some potential positives?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Policy reactions bad for infra, but could benefit consumption, digitalization

Large crowds gathered across the country over the weekend to protest the PHP1 trillion Pesos (c$17.5bn) allegedly lost to corruption in flood control projects. Unlike other similar protests around Asia, though, ours were broadly peaceful, and the political fallout has so far been fairly limited. We expect the initial policy response to be bad for infra spend, with officials wary of blowback. Some spending on infrastructure in the 2026 budget is already being diverted to social spending instead, which could boost consumption. Meanwhile. the BSP imposition of a daily cash withdrawal limit to combat cashbased, untraceable payoffs could accelerate digitalization of payments. Beyond the above, we should also expect spending on conspicuous, high-end goods, cars, property, etc. to slow.

 

Peaceful protests vs flood control scandal

Public indignation has risen in recent weeks with an alleged P1 trillion (US$18bn) lost to corruption around ‘flood control’ projects that were either overpriced, substandard or never built at all (aka “ghost projects”). News media have been flooded (pun intended) with legislative hearings on the matter as well as videos of contractors and their families flaunting luxury goods, cars, homes, etc. Over the weekend, rallies were staged nationwide, drawing a reported 100,000 in crowds. Unlike recent protests in other Asian countries, though, ours were broadly peaceful.

 

Limited political fallout (for now); Marcos forms independent commission

Despite the outrage, there has been little political fallout, at least so far. The Speaker of the House, Mr Martin Romualdez – President Marcos, Jr’s cousin – has resigned his post, though he remains a congressman.

President Marcos, Jr has ordered the creation of an independent commission to investigate, and to file appropriate charges. The business community, meanwhile, is “calling for reform, not the resignation or overthrow of the duly elected government.”


Infra, other spending could fall sharply – looking back to the 2013 PDAF scam

Newly appointed Public Works Secretary Vince Dizon has cut projects worth P255bn (US$4.5bn) from the department’s proposed 2026E budget, equivalent to c1% of GDP. Apart from this, other government spending could be in peril, as officials become wary of increased scrutiny. In the wake of the PDAF pork barrel scandal in 2013 – with similar allegations of “ghost projects” – government spend slowed sharply, dragging overall GDP growth down for 5-6 quarters (Fig 1). Conspicuous consumption – luxury goods, casino visits, high-end property – could also be hard hit while the political temperature is high.

 

Policy responses could boost consumption, accelerate digital adoption

Secretary Dizon has proposed re-allocating the budget to social programs such as agriculture, education, health care, social welfare, etc, which could help boost consumption near-term. Meanwhile, the Bangko Sentral has imposed a daily cash withdrawal limit of P500,000 to make untraceable cash-based payoffs more difficult. While digital payments adoption has surged in recent years, businesses have lagged significantly ((Figs 2-5). The new rule could have the unintended benefit of accelerating digitalization of (legal) payments.

GTCAP raises stake in Metrobank to 39.8%

GTCAP disclosed it bought 113mn shares (~2.5%) of Metrobank (MBT PM) for P72.52/sh. Acquisition price is above MBT’s closing price of P68/sh. At the P72.52/sh price, MBT is valued at 6.4x FY25E PE and 0.8x FY25E P/B. GTCAP’s stake in MBT will rise to 39.84%.

Robinsons Land sells RCR shares

Robinsons Land sold 1bn RCR shares at P7.75/share. Total proceeds of P7.75bn. This is a 5% discount to the previous closing price. At that price, RCR offers an FY25E dividend yield of 5.7% (based on consensus forecast). We estimate that RLC’s ownership in RCR fell from 65.6% to 60.5%. RLC currently offers 4.9% dividend yield and is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY25E P/B, and 5.3X FY25E P/E. The value of RLC’s stake in RCR is P97bn, c. 30% higher than its current market cap of P73bn. RCR currently offers 5.7% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). Ayala Corp, through wholly owned subsidiary ACX, signed an agreement with CP Axtra for the operation of Makro stores. ACX will own 49.9% of the partnership while CP Axtra will hold 50.1%. CP Axtra is part of the Charoen Pokphand group, the controlling shareholder behind CP All, among others. CP All owns 60% of CP Axtra. CP Axtra operates both wholesale and retail stores in as many as ten countries across Asia. Wholesale stores are under the Makro brand. The group intends to open its first store next year (2026). AC used to hold a minority interest in the local Makro operation before it was sold to SM and eventually shut. A notable recent entry into retail for AC was Anko, where it holds a 50% stake.

Ayala Corp.’s Automobile Central Enterprise, Inc. (ACEI) and Volkswagen AG have agreed to terminate the distribution of Volkswagen vehicles by ACEI in the country. It became the official importer and distributor of VW cars in October 2012, and relaunched the VW brand in 2013. ACMobility carries the Honda, Isuzu, Kia, Maxus, VW and BYD automobile brands. As of the end of 2024, ACMobility managed a network of 27 BYD dealerships, 40 Kia dealerships, and 4 Volkswagen dealerships nationwide. ACMobility plans to expand its BYD dealerships to 77 by year-end.

DigiPlus Interactive applied for three licenses with the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB): National Manufacturer License, Bookmaker License, and Bookmaker Premises License. BSP open to Oct cut if growth slows

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that a fourth consecutive 25bp rate cut to 4.75% by Oct is a possibility if output slows down because of the lack of demand. Remolona also noted that two 25bp cuts in Oct and Dec are "possible but not likely". He emphasized that the BSP is inclined to hold rates steady as long as inflation and growth forecasts remain intact. For 2025, the BSP expects GDP growth to settle at the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% goal, and inflation to be at 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect GDP growth and inflation to be 5.5% and 1.8%, respectively. Recall that BSP Governor Remolona during the last Monetary Board meeting in Aug mentioned that the current rate of 5.00% is the "sweet spot" given recent data. The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%.

BOP shifts to US$359mn surplus in Aug; GIR settles at US$107.1bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$359mn, wider than the US$88mn surplus in Aug 2024 but a turnaround from July 2025's US$167mn deficit. The August surplus reflected the BSP's net income from its investments abroad. This brings the YT-Aug deficit at US$5.4bn, reversing from the US$1.6bn surplus in the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (-1.3% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Aug stood at US$107.1bn, down from the $105.4bn in end-Jul. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025.

Government posts P85bn budget deficit in July; P869bn deficit as of 8M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P85bn in Aug, which is wider both by 56% YoY and 348% MoM. This brings the 8M25 deficit to P869bn, 25% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "well-managed and on track with the P1.56trn full-year program". The wider YTD deficit results from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues as customs collections continues to lag behind the full-year target. Revenue collections in Aug increased by a modest 3% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues and lackluster customs collections. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 5% YoY mainly due to higher revenues from corporate and personal income tax. Customs collections declined 1% as the BOC noted that Aug is a seasonally slow month alongside lower global oil prices and adverse weather. Non-tax revenues dipped 68% as remittances from the treasury and other gov't offices fell. Expenditures inched down 1% YoY in Aug as primary spending decreased. Primary spending for the month was lower by 3%. Similar to July, the spending slowdown in Aug was likely driven by the delayed timing of big-ticket disbursements of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways, Dept. of Social Welfare and Development, and Dept. of National Defense. Interest payments jumped 20% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

Inflation risks tilted to the downside—BSP

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees risks to inflation to be weighted towards the downside ahead of the next Monetary Board meeting on Oct 9 (5th out of 6 this year). Director of BSP's Monetary Policy Research Group Lara Ganapin emphasized that inflation needs to stay within target for the BSP to consider easing further. Ganapin highlighted that "the risk will clearly be tilted to the downside" as expecations are "well-contained and well-anchored". However, she notes of some risk from geopolitical, global trade policy uncertainty, and adverse weather driven supply shocks. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75% and inflation to average 1.8%.

Dept. of Agriculture seeks rice import ban extension

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) stated that it will likely recommend extending the ongoing rice import ban by 15-30 days. Recall that a 60-day suspension of rice imports from Sep 1 to Oct 30 was approved by the President to boost farmgate prices given the current peak harvest season of palay (unmilled rice). DA Secretary Tiu Laurel noted that palay farmgate prices improved to P13-17/kg from P8-10/kg but noted that farmers in certain provinces remain unprofitable given the P14/kg production cost of palay. The Dept. will submit its recommendation to extend or lift the import ban on Sept. 30. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto and other economic officials are also set to meet to discuss the extension proposal's impact on rice prices and inflation expectations.

House committee approves 2026 national budget bill; starts plenary deliberations

The House Committee on Appropriations approved House Bill No. 4058 or the General Appropriations bill on a vote of 54 for, 6 against, 4 abstaining—moving the budget bill to plenary level deliberations. This comes after 37 days of committee-level vetting and the recent finalization of budget amendments after the reallocation of the P255bn flood control budget from the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) to the education, health, and agricultural sectors. After passing the committee level, the budget bill will be deliberated through the House plenary level, then to the Senate, the bicam, and finally to President Marcos Jr. for approval. The House targets to approve the budget bill on second reading before Oct 10, and by final reading after sessions resume a month after in Nov. The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than this year's P6.33trn budget.

Dept. of Finance open to talks on billionaire wealth tax

Rep. Bella Suansing and Finance Secretary Recto stated that the Dept. of Finance (DOF) is "very open to discussions relating to the wealth tax" to bolster the revenue program of the national gov't. Recto was noted to support any tax law passed by Congress moving forward after previously stating that he ruled out new tax proposals. House Deputy Minority Leader Antonio Tinio previously stated plans to file a 3% wealth tax proposal on billionaires estimating potential collections of P98bn annually. Infrastructure spending dips in July given slow DPWH disbursements

Overall infrastructure spending (incl. infra components of subsidy and equity to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell 29% MoM and 20% YoY to P114.7bn in July due to slow Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) disbursements given project schedules, delays in procurement, incomplete submission of progress billings and required documents by contractors. Also cited the new tax clearance requirement of the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) for the release of final payments. Clearance will only be given when a contractor has no outstanding tax liabilities. Brings 7M25 infra spend to P835bn, -3% YoY and behind the 1H government’s target (here). Government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP).

Budget secretary expects slower gov't spending given corruption crackdown

Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman states that gov’t spending may slow due to the ongoing investigations into the alleged corruption in flood control projects. The investigations by lawmakers and the independent commission for infrastructure (ICI) as well as a number of suspensions on multiple DPWH projects were cited to temper infrastructure outlays. The probes was also cited to possibly impact other agencies’ budget utilizations. Pangandaman however, noted that the spending slowdown is unlikely to significantly affect GDP, as gov’t spending is less than 20% of the nation's economic output.

BPO Association expects industry to grow in 2026

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) expects the industry to achieve revenue of US$42bn and employ 1.97mn Filipinos by the end of 2026. These represent 4%-5% growth from its 2025 targets of US$40bn in revenue and 1.9mn employees. IBPAP President Jonathan Madrid said that the industry’s growth will still be driven by core segments – financial services and healthcare. He also said that Global Capability Centers (GCC) are growing faster. These are are offshore units established by multinational corporations to provide specialized services such as finance, IT and customer support to their global operations.

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) expects to submit its proposed bidding scheme for the privatization of LRT-2 to the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) soon. The feasibility study will soon be completed, which should pave the way for the DEPDev’s approval of the project’s auction. ADB still willing to fund flood control projects

Asian Development Bank (ADB) Country Director for the Philippines Andrew Jeffries stated that the flood control corruption scandal will not deter it from financing work in the Philippines given continued "close oversight" of its investments. Jeffries emphasized that the recent corruption scandal "doesn't stop our work, but makes the [due diligence] processes we have in place all the more important". Marcos vows accountability amid anti-corruption protests, says he is confident of finishing his term

Palace Press Officer Claire Castro stated that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. is not concerned about any destabilization attempts as the anti-corruption protests are not targeted towards him. Castro stated that "the Filipinos are not mad at the President or at his administration, but to all the people who are involved in the anomalous flood control projects". Rally organizers were noted to not have formally called for President Marcos' resignation but for accountability of gov't officials involved in the anomalous flood control projects. Castro emphasized the President's promise that "no one will be spared; those who must be held accountable will face the consequences ". The number of participants in the anti-corruption rallies in Luneta Park, Manila and EDSA People Power Monument in Quezon City were estimated to be around 50,000-80,000 people, marking one of the biggest protests in years. Protests were also held in other regions nationwide such as Bulacan, Davao City, and Baguio City.

President Marcos willing to submit himself to ICI investigation

President Marcos Jr. said he is willing to face investigation by the Independent Commission for Infrastructure (ICI) over alleged campaign fund irregularities. A Philippine Center for Investigative Journalism (PCIJ) report alleged he received ₱20 million from contractor Rodulfo Hilot Jr. during the 2022 elections. Such donations are barred under the Omnibus Election Code (no contributions from government contractors). Palace said the Comelec will also investigate as part of its mandate. Marcos created the ICI in response to flood control project anomalies and pledged transparency.

ICC prosecutors charge Former President Duterte with 3 counts of crimes against humanity

International Criminal Court (ICC) prosecutors charged former President Rodrigo Duterte with three counts of crimes against humanity tied to at least 76 killings in the “war on drugs". The charges cover murder and attempted murder during his time as Davao City mayor (2013-2016) and President (2016-2022). The confirmation of the charges hearing was originally set for Sept. 23 but was postponed as Duterte’s defense requested an indefinite adjournment, citing he is “not fit to stand trial”. The ICC granted a "limited postponement" of the proceedings.
