REGIS REPORT

21 February 2025

The PCOMP traded in a narrow range this week, eventually closing higher by 0.6% or 36.71 pts WoW to 6,098.04 with investors mostly taking cues from the last round of corporate earnings releases. Philippine equities also joined a broadly green region that looked past a widening tariff war and hawkish Fed minutes from the January policy meeting, and was instead encouraged by the softer dollar and lifted by inflows into the Chinese tech space.

 

Property earnings were in focus, leading to increased volatility in ALI (-0.6% WoW) and SMPH (+3.0% WoW), with lingering concerns on the residential segment sending the stocks down sharply on their respective earnings release dates (ALI -8.1%; SMPH -5.0%). Although earnings for both were in-line with market expectations, the 17% QoQ slump in ALI's pre-sales in 4Q along with the 40% YoY drop in SMPH pre-sales for FY24 cemented a gloomy near-term outlook for property.

 

MBT (+0.2% WoW) results were also in-line, but the numbers were flattered by a sizable tax reversal in 4Q. Otherwise, pre-provisioning operating profit was actually down 11% YoY / 27% QoQ due to increased opex. Meanwhile, MONDE (+1.9% WoW) released earnings guidance ahead of its 4Q report due in March, which showed the core APAC branded business accelerated in 4Q and Quorn managed to hit EBITDA-breakeven for the FY. However, MONDE is taking another GBP 80-100m impairment charge on Quorn.

 

Elsewhere, this week's top advancers in the benchmark were JGS (+20.2% WoW) and URC (+18.3% WoW) that bounced back strongly from last week's confirmation of their widely-speculated deletion from the MSCI Standard Index. Among local sector indices, Mining was a big outperformer, up 7.1% WoW as copper and gold miner PX (+25.7 WoW) surged on the back of still-rising gold prices.

 

Domestic macro headlines did little to direct sentiment. Investors remained glued to global developments, weighing how Trump's reciprocal tariff plans could potentially impact Philippine exports, among others. Traders also mulled over the January Fed minutes that showed officials are inclined to keep US monetary policy on pause until they see further improvement in inflation.

 

ADTO further weakened to $91m (from $99m last week) while net foreign outflows persisted for a second straight week, amounting to -$47m. Despite the weaker dollar, the peso was slightly down by -0.2% to 57.948:$1. PH10Y yield was again flat WoW at 6.13%.

SM Prime - Residential segment improves sequentially in 4Q24
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY24 profit rises 14%

SM Prime’s 4Q24 net profit grew 19% to P11.7bn. As a result, FY24 earnings rose 14% to P45.6bn, in line with consensus but ahead of REGIS forecast, as revenue of the residential, hotel, and office segments outperformed our estimates.

The disclosure to the PSE provided limited information but we hope to get more details during the SM Investments briefing on February 28.

4Q24 reservation sales improve, residential revenue hits record level

SM Prime’s residential revenue and presales went in opposite directions in FY24. Residential revenue rose c. 11% to P47.7bn, a record level. On the other hand, full-year reservation sales were P60bn, a drop of approximately 40% from 2023.

The full-year figure also implies that reservation sales were c. P13bn in 4Q, which would be up around 90% QoQ (+2% YoY). Meanwhile, we estimate that residential revenue grew 27% QoQ (+23% YoY) to about P15bn, which would be a record quarter.

We suspect that the record-level residential revenue in 4Q24 was partly thanks to stronger sales of ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units (earlier in the year), which are recognized as revenue in the income statement quickly.

From our channel checks, we understand that SM Prime now offers discounts coupled with rent-to-own schemes on RFO units. A buyer willing to pay 5% of the unit value upfront can get a 10%-15% discount on an RFO unit and move in within 3-4 months.

Mall revenue grows 7%

Systemwide mall revenue grew 7% in 4Q24 and FY24, from 6% in 3Q24 (8% in 9M24). This likely means that Philippine same-mall revenue growth was c. 5%, at the lower-end of the historical range (of 4%-8%).

Offices and hotels show stronger growth

The office and hotel businesses showed stronger growth than the larger segments. Combined revenue increased 18% in FY24 (+27% YoY in 4Q24).

Ayala Land - 4Q24 residential presales fall; management remains cautious in 2025
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY24 net profit rises 15%, in line

Ayala Land’s FY24 net profit rose 15% to P28.2bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. While revenue of every segment outperformed to varying degrees, lower margins (see table on right-hand side) and higher net interest expense dampened the outperformance. Looking ahead, ALI guided for 12% presales and earnings growth this year, also broadly in line with our forecasts.

4Q24 residential reservation sales fall, some positive signs in 1Q25

4Q24 reservation sales fell 17% QoQ and 5% YoY. While management blamed this on late 4Q launches and subdued demand from Filipino Americans demand due to the US elections, we believe that the market for (“core”) mid-income condominiums remains generally weak. For the full-year, presales rose 12% to P127bn (88% of the pre-COVID level), as premium sales grew 24% (to P80.8bn) while core sales fell 5% (to P46.3bn).

ALI highlighted that it sold P4.7bn worth of Ayala Land Premier (ALP) projects in the first week of February 2025. We would consider P5bn/month in ALP sales to be a strong performance but we also believe that would be difficult to sustain.

Commercial lot presales grow substantially

Commercial lot presales were strong, growing 52% in FY24 (130% YoY and 265% QoQ in 4Q), thanks to robust demand across the country, particularly in Nuvali. 

Cautious on residential, optimistic on commercial lots

The company’s launch plans reveal its outlook for the residential and commercial lot segments. It plans to launch only P80bn worth of residential projects (same as FY24), lower than the FY24 reservation sales of P127bn. This suggests that management is cautious on the residential segment and intends to reduce unsold inventory levels. We believe the company will have sufficient projects to sell, as launches in 4Q24 increased the unsold inventory level to 22 months’ worth of presales (from 18 months in 3Q24).

On the other hand, Ayala Land plans to launch P20bn worth of commercial lots this year, higher than the P15bn of sales in FY24. We believe this signals management’s more optimistic outlook on the segment.  

Mall revenue growth accelerates, office occupancy improves

4Q24 mall revenue growth accelerated to 16% YoY (from 7% in 9M24), as occupancy of two (previously) underperforming malls improved. For the full-year, we estimate that same-mall revenue growth was about 7%, at the high-end of the historical range (of 4%-8%). Meanwhile, office occupancy improved 2ppt QoQ to 91%, better than the average occupancy of 82% across the industry.

Ayala Land is trading at trough valuations on nearly every metric. It currently trades at 57% discount to NAV, 1.1X FY25E P/B, and 10.8X FY25E P/E.

Monde Nissin - The good, the bad ...
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Management gives a peek into FY24 results

Monde issued a press release today, providing ‘guidance’ to the market ahead of its official FY24 results announcement in March. The good news is that operating results for its core APAC BFB segment look healthy, with revenue growth accelerating in 4Q. The bad news is that Monde will take another GBP80m to 100m impairment charge on Quorn. Coupled with the drop in Monde’s share price, this would mean the “backstop” (guarantee) provided by some of Monde’s principals on the value of Quorn has now been largely depleted.

Consolidated sales growth above 3%, APAC BFB up 8% YoY and 4% QoQ

Management provided advance guidance on FY24 results, including on sales and margins. The numbers quoted suggest a relatively solid quarter for the APAC BFB business. The company also noted market share gains in its noodle business of 1.4pt and 1.5pt on value and volume for 4Q.

Additionally, MONDE also said it expects FY24 “core” income to grow by over 25%. Given that MONDE includes (among other things) unrealized FX profits on its USD cash as part of “core” income, we’d rather not put too much emphasis on this.

EBITDA breakeven for Quorn in FY24; another large impairment charge

Management says the Meat Alternative business (Quorn) was EBITDA breakeven for FY24, implying 4Q was slightly positive. However, MONDE revealed that it will take another GBP80-100m impairment charge on this business. Using end-2024 FX rates, that’s as much as P7.6bn (excluding any possible tax or FX adjustments).

“Backstop” now largely depleted.

Recall that some of MONDE’s principals provided a “backstop” for the value of Quorn, effectively insuring the valuation of the latter against further losses (from end-FY22). Recall as well that the maximum amount of this “insurance” is equivalent to the value of 2.156bn MONDE shares. Because of MONDE’s drop in the last months, those 2.156bn MONDE shares are now worth only ~P18.5bn. On the other hand, we estimate the cumulative losses (impairment) from when the guarantee was provided may have reached at least GBP220m (P17bn) by end-2024.

OFW Remittances up 3.0% YoY in December

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.0% YoY to US$3.38bn, a 20.3% MoM jump as Nov was the lowest monthly inflow in the past six months. Land-based remittances from the USA, Saudi Arabia, and Singapore were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances posting significant YoY and MoM growth. Sea-based remittances growth decelerated to 0.6% YoY from 1.0% in Nov. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 8.8%, marking the second-fastest increase of the year and surpassing the 8.5% growth recorded in Nov. Bringing FY24 remittances up 3.0% YoY to US$34.5bn. This is in-line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year.

BOP deficit at US$4.1bn in January 2025; GIR drops to US$103bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$4.1bn. This is higher than Dec 2024's $1.51bn deficit and the $740mn deficit in Jan 2024. It was also the widest monthly BOP deficit in 11 years, primarily reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign exchange operations and drawdown on the national government's deposits. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Jan 2025 stood at US$103.3bn, down from the US$106.3bn in end-Dec 2024. GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income.

Prices of rice and vegetables on the decline

After conducting a recent inspection at the Commonwealth Public Market in Quezon City, Department of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. observed that the prices of rice and vegetables have gone down. Looking into the DA's price monitoring data, the prices of local well-milled rice remain sticky but prices of imported well-milled rice saw significant decline, with the price range for local well-milled rice narrowing slightly from P40-54/kg (last Dec 18) to P40-52/kg (as of Feb 15). While prices for imported well-milled rice saw a bigger decline from P52-60/kg (last Dec 18) to P44-46/kg (as of Feb 15). Notably, prices of tomatoes have also decreased from as much as P160-260/kg (last Dec 18) to P35-70/kg (as of Feb 15). The DA is positive that rice prices have room to go down further to around P35/kg in the near term. Recall that since last month, the DA has set a price cap for imported rice from P58/kg on Jan. 20, P55/kg on Feb. 5, and P52/kg on Feb. 15.

NFA releases rice buffer stocks

The National Food Authority (NFA) started to release rice buffer stocks to local gov't units (LGUs) who will sell the rice at P33-P35/kg. This is in part of the Department of Agriculture's (DA) initiatives to lower rice prices as it also placed staggered price caps on rice since last month. The current imported rice price cap is at P52/kg since Feb. 15, set to go down to P49/kg by March. 25,000MT of rice per month from the NFA's total stock of 300,000MT will be released, which will last up to a year, but total volume may be increased if deemed necessary, given that P9bn was allocated for NFA's rice procurement. From the DA's latest price monitoring as of Feb 17, the price for local well-milled rice is at P40-54/kg while imported well-milled rice is priced at P44-45/kg.

BPO firms can continue to enjoy incentives with 50% WFH

The implementing rules and regulations of the CREATE MORE Act was released. PEZA-accredited BPO firms can now implement work-from-home policies (up to 50% WFH) and still enjoy all the fiscal incentives. Prior to CREATE MORE, BPO firms were only allowed 30% WFH. Most PEZA-accredited BPO firms are likely instituting c. 30% WFH. BPO firms availing of BOI incentives can enjoy all the said incentives even if they implement 100% WFH.

Government eyes new car manufacturing program

The Department of Trade and Industry plans to roll out the Revitalizing the Automotive Industry for Competitiveness Enhancement (RACE) program. According to the Office of the Special Assistant to the President for Investments and Economic Affairs (OSAPIEA) Secretary Frederick Go, the program is a re-designed version of the Comprehensive Automotive Resurgence Strategy (CARS) program. The RACE program aims to provide incentives to more participants than CARS, which only allotted three slots for participating models (only two slots were availed – Toyota’s Vios and Mitsubishi’s Mirage). Unlike the CARS Program, RACE will be a department initiative and would not need an executive order for implementation since the new program has a budget allocation in the 2025 national budget (P250mn).

Government eyes incentives for four-wheeled ICE vehicle production

The government expects to complete a joint administrative order (JAO) for the implementation of the Revitalizing the Automotive Industry for Competitiveness Enhancement (RACE) program. Details of the JAO are expected to be released by mid-March, and applications could be opened by April or May. A program briefer from Board of Investments (BOI) executive director Corazon Halili-Dichosa showed that the government would provide P9bn in fiscal support for the program. This will cover the production of three models of four-wheeled internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles. Each participating car maker can get fiscal support worth up to P3bn for tooling and equipment, as well as fixed investment support (FIS), which will cover up to 40% of capex. Each participant will have to invest in vehicle manufacturing, produce 100k units, and introduce the enrolled model to the market within two years. The fiscal support will be provided through the tax payment certificate scheme. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) plans to enroll the “Tamaraw” in the RACE program. TMP recently invested P5.5bn to produce the Tamaraw to produce about 20k units annually.

Government approves 21% fare hike for LRT-1

The Department of Transportation approved a 21% fare adjustment for LRT-1 operator, Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC), effective April 2. The boarding fee will rise to P16.25 from P13.29, while the increment per km fare will rise to P1.47 from P1.21 currently. Single end-to-end journey fares will rise to P52 from P45. The fare hike is lower than LRMC’s petition of 36%. LRMC said the newly approved fair scheme is only equal to its initial petition in 2022, as provided for in the concession agreement (CA). Under its CA, LRMC is entitled to fare hikes of 10.25% every two years, which is aligned with an annual 5% rate adjustment that was initially due in 2016. Since LRMC took over the concession in 2015, there has only been one fare increase approved in 2023, after the government deferred fare adjustments in 2018, 2020, and 2022. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (35.8%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19.2%), and Macquarie Infra (10%).

Philippine container ports handled 1.52mn TEUs of foreign containers in 4Q24, up by 8% YoY and accelerating from the 7% pace in 3Q24.
