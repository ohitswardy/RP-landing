REGIS REPORT

15 August 2025

The PSEi inched down 0.4% this week to 6315.93. Even as global markets were preoccupied with speculation on Fed easing, domestic drivers for the market included a spate of uninspiring 2Q corporate results as well as the PSEi rebalancing.

All index constitutions save for JG Summit (+7.8%), Jollibee (+2.8%), and San Miguel (-14.5%) saw downweights for the PSEi rebal. Meanwhile, the well-anticipated deletion of Bloomberry attracted punters and saw the stock rising 9% on rebal day.  The stock however ended the week 3.7% lower as its 2Q swung to a P1.4bn loss from recurring profit of P460m in 1Q as volumes dropped at Solaire Entertainment City.

Taking Bloomberry’s place in the index was Digiplus (-3.35%). The stock faltered this week despite its inclusion, as it was once again at the center of news headlines. The central bank on Thursday ordered banks and e-wallets to remove links that redirect users to online gambling platforms within 48 hours. This came amidst hearings on online gambling at the Senate, which commenced this week.  The company also reported a slight miss in its 1H25 net profit (P8.4bn which is 61% higher YoY) as it lost market share in 2Q25.

Converge (-16.6%) was the top index decliner for the week. The stock was sold down after it reduced its guidance for FY25 revenue growth to 10-12% (from 14-6% previously), despite reporting inline earnings for 1H. PLDT (-1.98%) also dropped, likewise reporting inline earnings. Overall softness in the core mobile business in the country has weighed on sentiment for the telcos.

Guidance reduction seemed to be a theme. Universal Robina (-6.9%) which also tempered its guidance for FY25, also ended the week with a sharp decline.  Full-year top line growth was softened to “mid to high single-digit” (from high single-digit) and EBIT growth to “low to mid-single-digit” (from mid-single-digit).  Century Pacific (-5.6%) suffered a similar fate this week as it likewise trimmed its full-year guidance to “high single to low double-digit” top line growth (vs. 10-15% growth previously).

On the other hand, Puregold (+3.7%) and Jollibee (+2.8%) gained with both reporting solid operations. Puregold reported strong top line numbers, underpinned by strong SSSG of 6.3% for 2Q. Meanwhile,  Jollibee’s operating profit came in up 18%, with its domestic business at +6.4% SSSG and the international business side improving to +4.1% from 0.7% in the previous quarter.

Meanwhile, the property sector consolidated in the wake of strong gains in the previous week. Ayala Land (-3.7% ) and SMPH (-1.0%) corrected after gaining 8.5% and 3% in the previously.

Banks were mostly lower as investors have become wary of rising credit costs for the sector, as seen in this reporting season. Security Bank (-7.1%) dropped the most, with 1H25 profit (P5.9bn, up 8% YoY) falling behind forecasts, dragged by a 5.% rise in provision expense.

GT Capital (+7.2%) ended with a notable move, as 1H25 core profit (+31%) came in ahead of forecasts. .  Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) delivered robust numbers, with 1H25 profit up 66% YoY to P12.5bn.

Foreigners were net buyers this week for US$21m, with flows going into ICTSI (-0.6%), Ayala Corp (+2.0%) and GT Capital.

The PHP was stable this week (-0.03%), settling at PHP57.077/US$. The DXY however shed to 97.9 (from 98.18) as expectations of a Fed rate cut remain despite the PPI spike.

In the meantime, BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that an August rate cut is "quite likely" and that two more cuts this year remain "more likely" than just one, depending on economic data. This implies 125bp total rate cuts for 2025, bringing the key policy rate to 4.75% by year-end from the current 5.25%.

Ayala Corp. - 2Q25: Positive momentum continues for portfolio investments
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H25 core profit dips 2% to P23.7bn, within Street and REGIS forecasts

Headline profit of P23.4bn (+5%) included net one-off losses mostly from the impairment of ACEN’s Vietnam investments offset (ACEN) by gains from AC Ventures-MUFG transaction and others. Removing one-offs, recurring profit of P23.7bn (-2%) is broadly in line with both Regis and Street forecasts. Weakness at GLO and ACEN were partly mitigated by profit gains at ALI and BPI, as well as a profit turnaround at its portfolio investments. The latter was driven by the strong performance of AC Mobility, AC Health and others.

AC Mobility unit sales surge >2x

Sales of NEV (largely of BYD vehicles) propelled the two-fold increase in AC Mobility’s 1H25 unit sales to 20k (vs. 9.1k units in 1H24). The 1H25 figures imply that sales volumes in 2Q25 jumped 17% QoQ, 122% YoY to 10.8k units. While 1H25 profit of P122mn (+408%) included P277mn in income from dividends, we are encouraged by the solid sales momentum. Its EV charging network has expanded to 239 as of end-June from 120 as of end-2024, with management aiming to end the year with 700 charging points.

AC Health: Solid topline momentum boosts EBITDA and trims losses

AC Health’s revenues jumped 35% to P6bn in 1H25, which implies that revenues rose 19% QoQ and 46% YoY to P3.3bn, driven by strong patient count and higher bed occupancy. EBITDA rose 144% YoY to P586mn in 1H25, further trimming losses to P100mn.

Management said that the planned equity infusion from a Temasek-backed fund, which will give the fund a 16% stake, values AC Health at a post-money EV of US$500mn, equivalent to “high-teens to low-20s” forward multiples. AC Health plans to use the funds to expand its portfolio to 10 hospitals (vs. 6), 300 clinics (vs. 270), and 1,150 pharmacies (vs. 879) by 2027.

GT Capital - 2Q25: Toyota delivers record GP margins
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H25 profit of P17.9bn (+31%) outperforms REGIS, Street estimates

1H25 core profit, net of preferred dividends and excluding one-off gains at Metro Pacific (MPI), rose 31% YoY to P17.9bn, on pace to exceed REGIS and consensus estimates. 2Q25 core profit improved 8% QoQ and 35% YoY to P9.3bn, underpinned by the robust performance of Metrobank (here), Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), and MPI.

Auto: Favorable currency moves drive GP expansion in 2Q25

TMP’s 2Q25 profit dipped 3% QoQ to P6.15bn, even though favorable FX drove GP margin up 70bps QoQ to a new high of 17.5% in 2Q25. The high margins allowed for intensified promotional activities, which drove sales (+2% YoY in 2Q25 due to timing of Easter break; +7% in 1H25) and preserved market share at ~46%. TMP’s EV sales jumped 31% QoQ and 47% YoY to 5.2k units in 2Q25, representing 9% of overall sales. Management is pleased by the recent, relative stability of PHP/USD but believes margins are unlikely to be sustainable.

MPI sustains growth momentum

Contributions from MPI climbed 31% YoY to P1.65bn in 2Q25, buoyed by Meralco’s generation business and provision reversals, as well as higher tariffs and lower opex at Maynilad. The tollroad unit also saw profits improve 8% YoY in 2Q25 on better PH traffic volumes +4%. Profits from its healthcare unit doubled as topline improved 32%.

Lower reservation sales at property unit

The property unit reported a 59% YoY decline in 2Q25 profits due to the absence of lot sales this year. Removing lot sales, 2Q25 profit came in at P200mn, reversing last year’s loss, driven by higher occupancy rates and associate income. Reservation sales softened, falling 49% QoQ and 21% YoY, after selling out commercial lots in May. Management noted that it is currently sitting on three years’ worth of inventory.

Universal Robina - 2Q25: Modest gains; international BCF slows
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 core profit +3% YoY to P6.9bn

URC’s 1H25 core profit, which excludes exceptional items, increased 3% YoY to P6.9bn. Consolidated 1H EBIT was flat at P9.4bn, while revenues were up 6% to P85.9bn. Although 1H results still appear to be trending ahead of REGIS expectations, 2Q looks soft, and management offered cautious guidance. For 2Q25 alone, core profit was up 1% YoY to P2.8bn. Top line for 2Q was +6% YoY to P40.6bn, lifted by the commodity segments (+10%), particularly sugar and renewables (SURE, +32%). Revenue for Domestic BCF grew a modest +5% YoY, while International BCF slowed to +3% YoY growth from +10% YoY in 1Q25.

More hits than misses for Domestic BCF; int’l BCF growth slows

2Q25 Domestic BCF sales rose 5% YoY to P19.5bn driven by strength and share gains in snacks and RTD beverages. Management said that overall volume growth for Domestic BCF was just above revenue growth, implying business-wide ASPs were, simplistically, just about stable. Coffee and noodles continued to struggle due to competition as well as macro conditions. Overall, URC increased market share across most of the categories where it is present. Longer-term, however, we think it needs to move beyond its current portfolio to reignite growth.

International BCF in 2Q saw top-line growth slow to +3% YoY due to weakness in Vietnam and Thailand. The reasons for the slowdown appear to be macro-related and management is guiding for softness to persist into 2H25.

Overall EBIT margin for 2Q25 dipped 70bp to 9.7%, weighed down by the continued push for top line growth and high-cost coffee inventories. Domestic BCF margin dipped 60bp to 12.1%. Management expects margin pressure to continue into 2H on elevated A&P spend and high-cost coffee inventories. Int’l BCF margin expanded 140bp, propelled by favorable input costs and better operational efficiencies.

Mixed results from non-branded businesses

Non-BCF (Agro-Industrial and Commodities) 2Q revenues were up 10% YoY to P12.5bn as strong volumes at SURE offset the decline in flour and animal feeds. SURE saw revenue surging 32% YoY on higher volumes. Revenues for flour and animal feed both declined 9% YoY.

“Soft” downgrade to guidance?

Management tweaked its guidance to what appears to be a more conservative range. It’s now guiding for full-year top line growth to be “mid to high single-digit” (from high single-digit) and EBIT growth to be “low to mid-single-digit” (from mid-single-digit). While management said that July was the best month for domestic BCF in the year so far, it remains too early to draw any conclusions.

Puregold - 2Q25: Revenue growth remains robust but opex crimps margins
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 profit +7% YoY on top line growth of 12%

PGOLD’s 1H25 net profit increased 7% YoY to P5.30bn, which we view as being slightly below REGIS and consensus expectations. The top line figures, however, remain robust as revenues were up +12 % to P110bn. Opex growth, though, was faster (+16%) which the company attributed to pre-operating expenses for new stores. For 2Q alone, revenues also grew 12%, while profits rose 8% to P2.66bn.

Group-wide same-store sales growth (SSSG) for 1H was +5.2% and for 2Q alone it was a tad faster at +6.3%.

Outperforming the competition

Consolidated sales rose +12% YoY in 2Q25 with Puregold stores growing +10% and S&R seeing +16% growth. SSSG for Puregold was faster at +6.9% (estimated) compared to the +5.4% for S&R. Bigger basket sizes lifted Puregold SSS while it was foot traffic that boosted S&R’s.

The above numbers suggest the group is outperforming the other big grocery retailers and is continuing to gain share.

Opex growth accelerates

Opex rose 16% YoY to P15.0bn in 1H, which the company attributed to pre[1]operating expenses for new stores. The derived 2Q opex would have been P7.8bn, an increase of +19% YoY for the quarter. However, the company appeared to suggest that opex will normalize at lower levels (as a share of sales) in future periods as stores mature.

Beneficiary of lower tariffs on US imports?

Management declined to speculate on the impact of a possible tariff reduction on goods imported from the US. Around 30% of the merchandise of S&R is directly imported from the U.S. Management did say, however, that, as a normal practice, it passes on cost savings to consumers.

Revenue guidance tweaked upwards

The company revised its revenue guidance to growth of 7% to 9%, which is a minor upward adjustment to its previous guidance of 6% to 8%. The previous revenue guidance was unduly conservative in our view, and the new guidance is just slightly less so.

Century Pacific Food - 2Q25: Top line growth moderates; guidance trimmed
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1H25 net profit up 7% YoY to P3.9bn

CNPF’s profit in 1H25 grew 7% YoY to P3.9bn as revenues rose 9% to P32.3bn. The results look slightly behind REGIS estimates. For 2Q25 alone, revenues were only up 1% YoY to P19.8bn as the branded business saw moderated growth (+6% YoY from +13% YoY in 1Q) and the OEM business saw sales further decline (-17% YoY from -2% YoY in 1Q). Consolidated gross margin narrowed by 80bp on higher input costs, but this was more than offset by lower opex spend as EBITDA margin expanded 110bp.

Branded sales moderate in 2Q, dragged by dairy and branded exports

Revenues of the branded business grew +6% YoY to P16.2bn, slowing down from the +13% YoY growth in 1Q25. The deceleration in growth was attributed to the softness in the dairy segment and branded exports products. Dairy sales were affected by a price increase, while branded exports experienced operational issues in the quarter.

OEM top line contracts on tariff and trade uncertainties

OEM revenues, which comprised 18% of revenue in 2Q25, were 17% lower YoY to P3.6bn. The high base (1H24 OEM sales +42%) alongside weak demand in the quarter led to the dip in sales. Management said that uncertainties surrounding tariffs and global trade put OEM buyers on a wait-and-see mode.

Lower opex offsets gross margin contraction

2Q gross margin narrowed by 80bp to 25.2% as input prices for milk, tuna, and coconut were higher vs last year. The contraction in gross margin was offset by lower opex spend as EBITDA margin expanded 110bp to 15.4%. Management, however, is guiding for an increase in opex in 2H as the company plans to ramp up A&P spend to boost sales to meet its full-year targets.

Company trims guidance but is optimistic for a better 2H

The company revised its guidance for both top and bottom lines to “high single to low double-digit growth”, compared to “10-15% growth” previously. Management is more optimistic on 2H due to (1) a strong start in 3Q for the domestic business, (2) expectations that operational issues on its branded will be resolved soon, and (3) improvement in OEM tuna exports.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - BLOOM returns to net loss in 2Q25; SEC volumes fall across the board
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Back to a net loss in 2Q25

Bloomberry recorded a net loss of P1.4bn in 2Q25, a reversal from the recurring profit of P450mn in 1Q25 (P1.3bn profit in 2Q24). 2Q25 EBITDA fell 42% QoQ and 30% YoY to P2.5bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The decline was led by Solaire Entertainment City (SEC), which saw volumes fall across all categories.

Looking ahead, management is hopeful that Solaire North (SN) will continue to ramp up and SEC’s VIP business will recover after the replacement of two junket operators that closed in May. It also plans to fully launch its new online gaming platform, MegaFUNalo, in 1Q26 (after its soft launch in June 2025).

Solaire Entertainment City volumes fall

2Q25 GGR of SEC fell 20% and 27% YoY to 9.8bn, as VIP and mass table volumes fell to their lowest levels since 4Q21/1Q22. VIP volume fell 27% QoQ (down 46% YoY), largely due to the closure of two junket operators in May. Fortunately, the company has since replaced these junkets and is hopeful that these new operators can ramp up beginning 4Q25.

Meanwhile, mass table volume fell 8% QoQ (down 23% YoY) due to lower premium mass activity, particularly from Chinese and Koreans. Unless tourist arrivals from the two countries rebound sharply, we would consider a strong recovery in foreign mass activity to be unlikely.

We reckon that the unusually low VIP win rate (of 2.2%) and the unusually high mass table win rate (of 49%) in 2Q25 largely offset each other. We estimate that normalizing the win rates of both segments would have brought SEC’s 2Q GGR down by just another 3%.

Solaire North (SN) mass volumes improve sequentially

Solaire North’s 2Q25 GGR fell 7% QoQ to P4.3bn, largely due to the decline in VIP volume. Management said that the 1Q25 VIP level was unusually high because the company held a launch event for its VIP business then. On the other hand, slot coin-in rose 8% QoQ to P40.1bn (49% of SEC’s 2Q25 level) while mass table volume grew 9% QoQ to P7.0bn (89% of SEC’s figure).

Marketing spending for online gaming to be deferred

BLOOM soft-launched its new online gaming platform, MegaFUNalo, in June 2025 and spent P460mn in marketing expenses (which was recorded as opex of SEC). However, in light of what is likely to be an evolving regulatory landscape on online gaming, the company will hold back on marketing spending for this segment until the full launch of MegaFUNalo in 1Q26.

by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q25 profit rises 16% as East Zone opex drops

2Q25 profit after the share of participating prefs rose 16% YoY to P3.86bn, bringing cumulative 1H25 profit up 15% YoY to P7.0bn. The results are in line with consensus estimates but significantly lag ours. As was the case in past quarters, MWC’s profit outperformance has largely been driven by the disappointing pace of spending at the East Zone (EZ). In 2Q25 alone, consolidated cash opex fell 6% YoY due to lower power costs at the EZ.

EZ spending flat

The EZ spent 4% YoY more on capex to P5.3bn in 2Q25, which combined with the decline in opex caused overall spending to remain flat at P7.3bn in the quarter and only reach P13bn (flat) in 1H25. With 1H25 revenues rising by 11%, free cash flow generation at the East Zone was positive. Given the under-spending since 2023, we estimate MWC’s will need to spend P39bn annually from 2025-2027 to meet its spending commitment of P175bn for RY2023-2027. Recall that MWC receives a 12% return on its net spending (revenues less opex+capex) and the under-spending, if not addressed, heightens the risk of a tariff clawback in the next rebasing.

Non-EZ contributions flat in 2Q25

Contributions from its non-East Zone assets were flat in 2Q25 due to weak sales from its semiconductor clients in Laguna and persistent operational issues at its international ventures.

Update on Wawa acquisition, other potential M&A

Management is eyeing the takeover of WawaJV Co., its affiliate bulk water provider, by the end of September and will pay the P37.8bn acquisition price on a staggered basis. Management also hinted at its interest in acquiring some of the 77 joint venture water contracts held by Villar-led Prime Water, should the government decide to give these assets to another operator.

 
PLDT - 2Q25: Mobile revenues remain weak
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income up 1% in 1H despite weak mobile revenues

PLDT reported net income of P18.1bn for 1H25, down 2% YoY. Adjusting for exceptional items, core income was P17.6bn, up 1% YoY. Despite the decline in mobile revenue, core income growth was still slightly positive (+1%) due to a turnaround in its fintech / digibank associate Maya and a 3% reduction in cash expenses (and accelerated depreciation of assets in prior year/s). While the core profit figure appears to be in line with Street estimates, the core telecom business does look soft.

Mobile revenues still down

Mobile revenues dipped 2% both QoQ and YoY in a continuation of the softness seen in the prior quarter. This weakness is atypical considering that mid-term elections were held in 2Q25 and such an event historically produces a spending bounce. We estimate PLDT likely ceded ~80bp of revenue share in the quarter.

The highlight for the broadband segment was the +66k net adds for wireline broadband in 2Q25, which took PLDT to 3.57m subs. This is only around half of Globe’s and is likely reflection of PLDT’s initial reluctance to enter the prepaid fiber market.

Maya contributions rise further

The contributions of Maya to PLDT’s core income reached P406m for 1H25 (P143m in 1Q25), implying a sequential QoQ improvement in profits for the former. Considering PLDT’s ~38% stake in Maya would imply Maya produced >P1bn in profit for the half.

Capex guidance reduced but still no profit guidance

PLDT reduced guidance for FY25 capex further to P63bn (previously P68 – 73bn) after spending only P27bn in 1H (-22%) YoY. Management remained reluctant to offer guidance for core income but expressed hope that it will exceed last year’s (P34bn by our reckoning).

 
Robinsons Land - 2Q25: Residential segment improves sequentially but still underperforms
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H25 recurring profit grows 5%

Robinsons Land’s 1H25 headline profit fell 5% to P6.9bn. Removing non[1]recurring gains in 1Q24, recurring profit grew 5% to P6.9bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus. The mall and office segments are trending ahead of our forecasts but the residential segment is trailing behind, despite the sequential improvement in 2Q25.

Residential businesses improve sequentially but still underperform

Although reservation sales of the JV and standalone residential businesses improved sequentially, we would still consider both to be weak. Presales of standalone projects rose 178% QoQ (-57% YoY) to P2.4bn, only 50% of the 2019 average. We estimate that ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units comprised over 70% of the 2Q25 sales. The company said that rent-to-own schemes where the buyer pays for the unit over 10 years were particularly popular.

Meanwhile, 2Q25 presales of JV projects climbed from P47mn in 1Q25 to P524mn (-85% YoY). While this is a large improvement QoQ, it is only 10% of the 2019 average. Management said that buyers of high-end units (such as RLC’s JV projects) prefer larger units but the unsold JV inventory (worth P17.4bn) is mostly comprised of smaller units (such as 1-bedroom units).

Mall revenue growth accelerates

Despite Holy Week falling in April this year (vs March in 2024), 2Q25 mall revenue growth accelerated to 11% YoY, from 6% in 1Q25, as mall occupancy improved 1ppt QoQ (and YoY) to 94%. Same-mall revenue growth stood at 7%, slightly above the midpoint of the historical average (of 4%-8%).

Office occupancy improves QoQ

Office occupancy improved 1ppt QoQ (and YoY) to 87%, higher than the industry average of 82%.

Share-for-property swap with RCR could be catalyst for RLC

We believe the recently announced share-for-property swap between RLC and RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) will be a positive catalyst for RLC. RCR will acquire 324,000sqm of malls from RLC. In turn, RLC will subscribe to 3.8bn new RCR shares, expanding shares outstanding by 24% and increasing RLC’s ownership from 57% to 66%.

RLC is currently trading at 67% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY25E P/B, 5.2X FY25E P/E, and offers 4% dividend yield. Furthermore, its market cap is only about the same value as its current stake in RCR and the upcoming share-for-property swap could raise the value of its stake by over 40% by the end of the year.

JFC 1H25 profit down 1% YoY to P5.6bn—in line

JFC’s 1H25 net profit was slightly down 1% YoY to P5.6bn, which is in line with REGIS and consensus expectations. Core operations appear broadly in line as 1H consolidated revenues rose 15% while operating profit was 18% higher YoY. For 2Q25 alone, top line grew 15% YoY to P77.6bn and operating profit increased 19% YoY to P6.0bn. Consolidated SSSG was steady at 5.5% in 2Q25, unchanged from the previous quarter.

The domestic segment’s 2Q systemwide sales (SWS) grew 11.3% to P65.2bn, boosted by new store openings in the quarter as SSSG mildly decelerated to +6.4% from +8.5%. The Jollibee brand (+7.0%) and Mang Inasal (+12.0%) were the primary drivers of growth in SSS in 2Q. The slight deceleration was due to Chowking SSSG slowing to 2.3% from 6.2% in 1Q.

The international segment in 2Q saw SWS climb 32.6% to P49.3bn given a YoY boost from Compose Coffee, which contributed to 6.8% of total SWS. In terms of SSSG, the int'l segment improved to 4.1% from 0.7%, driven by a reversal from China. The segment continued to see good performance from the Jollibee brand internationally, which posted strong SSSG (e.g., Jollibee NA +9.4%, Jollibee Other Asia +10.0%).

China saw a turnaround in 2Q, arresting five consecutive quarters of SSS decline with SSSG up 3.9%. However, Smashburger remains challenged seeing SSS decline 9.9%, even worse than the 8.0% drop in the previous quarter.

Volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port down 12% YoY in July as logistics disruptions persist

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) fell 12% YoY to 110k units in July/ Decline is still due to the prolonged effect of the labor strike in May, which disrupted operations across Manzanillo. Ex-CMSA, combined volumes at other ports in Manzanillo dropped by 1% in July. Note that revenues would have likely been steady due to higher storage revenues.

This brings CMSA’s cumulative 7M25 volumes to 851k TEUs, up 3% YoY and trailing our forecast of 1.69mn TEUs (+16% YoY). To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year would have to average 167k TEUs per month. The port’s capacity today is 1.7mn TEUs (~142k TEUs per month) and this capacity will increase to “over 2mn TEUs” (~168-175k TEUs per month) by year-end (new berth will be completed by Aug/Sep 2025).

SECB 1H25 NPAT P5.9bn, strong PPOP but credit cost still high

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1H25 NPAT of P5.9bn, up 8% YoY, tracking behind our forecast (P15.5bn for FY25) and consensus (P13.3bn). Revenues (+23%) and POPP (+19%), both broadly in line with our expectations. As with the previous quarter, profits were dragged by provision expense of P5.1bn (+53%) as the bank cleans up its credit card book. Management had previously guided that credit costs (161bps in 2Q) would peak in 2/3Q25. Meanwhile, 2Q25 already included the 25% stake in Home Credit Phils (HCPH). It is unclear how much of the associate income P211mn in 2Q was attributable to HCPH, but it does look like it would be in line with our previous estimates of a PE of around 10x. More after the briefing on Monday, 18 August at 3pm.

Loan growth slowed to 16% YoY (from 19% in 2Q and 26% in 4Q), in line with the bank’s guidance to end the year at 10-12% to conserve capital. Consumer/MSME loans were still up 32%, driven by credit cards +43%, auto +54%, and MSME +46%. Mortgages were up 17%. Consumer/MSME loans are 36% of total , steady QoQ

Opex remained elevated, up 26% YoY on increased staff and IT costs. Despite that, PPOP was up 19% in 1H25

This was flagged by management in 1Q25 as the bank continues to clean up its problematic credit card loans. The bank had guided that the cleanup would be complete by 2025, and that credit costs could peak in 2Q/3Q.

Aboitiz Power: 2Q25 EBITDA declines 4% YoY on lower spot prices

1H25 profit of P12.7bn (-26% YoY) lags consensus estimates. AP’s 1H25 net income fell 26% YoY to P12.7bn, weighed down by (a) the timing of planned outages (largely in 1Q25), (b) lower spot prices, (c) the recognition of non-cash and financing costs for GNPD, and (d) higher financing expenses arising from the acquisition of Chromite (i.e. natgas assets).  The profit decline was partly cushioned by contributions from Chromite, as well as new solar plants (377 MWp). Generation 2Q25 EBITDA down 4% YoY due to steep drop in spot prices. Asset quality metrics steady in June, but...

The Philippine banking sector’s asset quality seemed stable in June. (As a reminder, we use loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP) to remove the “noise” in the data, as the IBL/RRP balances fluctuate widely on a monthly basis.)

NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) came in at 3.56%, about 2bps lower MoM from the 3.58% in May. In nominal terms, NPLs were up 0.5% MoM and 5.5% YoY to P530.1bn. Past Due (PD) ratio though was up 3bps MoM to 4.50% from 4.47% in May, as the PHP value of PDs increased 1.7% MoM. Restructured Loans (RLs) improved to 2.09%, 4bps lower MoM. Meanwhile, NPL Cover increased slightly to 76.4% from 75.8% in May.

Note, though, that the NPL and PD figures are period-end, and do not take into account write-offs during the period. This masks the new NPL formation, which is more relevant. This was in full display in 2Q, where nearly all banks that have reported thus far showed sharply higher credit costs / provision expense in 2Q5 compared to 1Q25 and to 2Q24. That said, the increase was due largely to the shift towards higher-risk / higher-margin consumer loans. Despite the higher provisions, bank earnings were still up YoY, suggesting better risk-adjusted returns.

By bank type, larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs) saw NPLs down 3bps MoM, but PDs inched up 2bps MoM. Thrift banks (TBs) posted huge improvements in both ratios, with NPL ratios down 47bps MoM and PD ratios down 85bps. Meanwhile, Digibanks’ ratios deteriorated MoM, as NPLs jumped 130bps to 11.1% and PDs up 128bps.

Apple Pay / Google Pay to operate in PH soon?

Last week, at a “press chat”, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Deputy Governor Mamerto Tangonan said that the BSP had cleared the way for Apple Pay and Google Pay to operate in the Philippines. To date, NFC-based phone-tap payment options are not available in the Philippines. (I, for example, cannot upload my Philippine credit cards onto Apple Pay, even for use abroad.)

The major bone of contention was this: are Apple Pay and Google Pay considered “operating payment systems” (OPSs)? Essentially, are they like e-wallets, that hold users’ / customers’ money? According to DG Tangonan, the BSP has determined that they are NOT OPSs, as they merely process / execute payment credentials. Thus, they do not need to register separately with the BSP as regulator.

Allowing Apple Pay / Google Pay NFC could make payments much more convenient, and could further encourage broader digital adoption of payments.

For Filipinos, credit card penetration is quite low. But if wallets like GCash, Maya, Shopee Pay, Grab Pay, etc are allowed on the phone makers’ respective OSs, it would vastly improve speed of payments, without having to open the apps. This is crucial especially as the BSP and Department of Transportation (DOTr) are working on inter-operable payment system for all modes of public transportation. At the moment, paying via the apps can be a bit time consuming, even with QR adoption.

For tourists, the convenience advantage would be more obvious, with the ability to use their phones to pay without having to whip out their credit cards, or cash.

According to the BSP, it is now up to the individual financial service providers (banks, wallets, etc) to negotiate with Apple and Google.
