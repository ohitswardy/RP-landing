05 June 2025

The PCOMP rebounded from a two-week slump, returning a modest 0.6% or 35.26 pts WoW to 6,376.79 as foreign selling eased following the MSCI May rebalancing at the close of the previous week.

Laggard plays continued to lead advancers for the week with BLOOM (+19.6% WoW), AGI (+11.8% WoW), and GTCAP (+10.3% WoW) up the most among index names, though modest gains in heavy-weight SM (+1.8% WoW) and a rebound in property names ALI (+5.4% WoW) and SMPH (+2.9% WoW) did the most to lift the PCOMP.

All sector indices finished higher save for Financials (-1.9% WoW), dragged by CBC (-13.1% WoW) that continued to sell off on index speculation as well as BDO (-3.2% WoW) that slumped on persistent foreign outflows.

On macro, inflation continued to head lower in May, down to 1.3% from 1.4% in April and coming in-line with consensus forecast. Rice prices were again down MoM (-2.4%) as were energy-linked costs such as electricity and transportation. This took YTD-May inflation down to 1.9%, already below the BSP's 2-4% target range and tracking lower vs Street estimate of 2.2% for FY25.

Optimism over falling inflation likewise helped lift consumer names this week, with solid gains in PGOLD (+9.4% WoW), JFC (+3.3% WoW), URC (+3.0% WoW), and CNPF (+2.6% WoW). Notably, WLCON (+18.7% WoW) was the big outperformer as sell flows may have been exhausted from its deletion at the MSCI rebalancing last week.

Trading activity moderated to $115m ADTO while foreign flows reversed to net buy for the week by $10m.

Even with rate cuts nearing, yields remain elevated with the PH10Y at 6.2997%, inching up by 4.3 bps WoW. Peso slightly stronger by 0.2% WoW to 55.640:$1.

Philippine Strategy - Stock sales tax cut to 0.1% (from 0.6%), to spur market liquidity?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Lower STT, interest rate cuts to encourage equity trading
The Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA), or Republic Ac 12214, which aims to harmonize tax rates across financial products, was signed into law on 29 May. Among other things, the law includes the long-awaited reduction in the stock transaction tax (STT) to 10bps of the selling price. The current STT of 60bps has been cited as one reason for the stock market’s low liquidity (Fig 1), as it discourages active portfolio trading / churn. Local funds’ Trust AUMs have risen sharply, up 23% in 1Q25 to P7,867bn ($140bn), double the 2019 levels. Policy interest rate cuts, together with the lower STT, could encourage a shift back into domestic equities. Increased market liquidity might also see a return of foreign investors, and bring foreign ownership up from near record lows.

STT slashed from 60bps to 10bps, as part of capital markets reform
President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr signed RA 12214 into law on 29 May. The CMEPA is meant to streamline capital markets by narrowing tax arbitrage opportunities between financial products. For example, it standardizes tax on interest income at 20% (from the current 0-20% depending on the product), and standardizes stamp duties (or documentary stamp taxes, or DST) on bonds, debentures, and stocks issued in any foreign country at 0.75%.

Most crucially for the stock market, though, the Act slashes the STT on the sale of listed equities to 10bps, from the current 60bps (still in lieu of capital gains tax). The high STT today has been cited as a major hindrance to active trading in the local stock market.

After broker commissions, VAT, and other fees, the “round-trip” cost of buying and selling a stock can be 100bps or higher, especially for retail investors. The STT cut could thus cut total friction cost to investors by around 50%, or even more for institutions able to get lower comm rates.

Domestic AUMs surging; could STT, rate cuts spur market liquidity?
As of 1Q25, Trust assets under management (AUMs) of domestic banks and non-bank institutions regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) stood at P7,867bn (about $140bn), jumping 23% YoY, and nearly double the levels at end-2019 (Fig 2). However, fund flows into domestic equities has been tepid at best, with allocations primarily going to fixed income portfolios. With policy interest rates already down 100bps from the 6% peak, funds are anecdotally seeing increased flows into (relatively) high dividend yield stocks. With further rate cuts likely, we could see domestic asset allocation shift more rapidly into the stock market. The lower STT could also encourage more active portfolio management (vs index-hugging).

Virtuous cycle? Attracting foreign buying in the broader market
Should stock market liquidity improve, foreign interest could also rise and broaden out. While valuations are cheap, foreign investors have been turned off by low stock turnover, forcing many to only focus on the 5-10 largest, most liquid names. The most recent data shows foreign ownership of listed stocks was just 19% – near record lows – with ownership at a higher 24% for stocks in the main PSEi Index (Fig 3).

 
Robinsons Retail - Buys back 22% stake from Dairy Farm Group
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

RRHI buys back 22% stake at P50/share
RRHI disclosed that it has bought back 22.2% of its own shares from the Dairy Farm Group for P50/share or P15.7bn in total. We estimate that the transaction will be meaningfully accretive to EPS (>10% on an annualized basis) even if the deal was struck at a 32% premium to Friday’s close. The deal helps to optimize RRHI’s capital structure and further highlights how undervalued the shares are.

Buying back shares from strategic shareholder
RRHI announced that that it has reacquired 315mn of its common shares (22.2% of RRHI’s total shares) at P50/sh from GCH Investments Pte. Ltd., a subsidiary of the DFI Retail Group through a block sale worth P15.7bn. In one stroke, the company bought back a large chunk of its owns shares at what we believe is an attractive valuation and without having to affect market liquidity.

Following the deal, we estimate net gearing for RRHI would rise to from what some may view as sub-optimal 12% to 36%.

Value accretive immediately
The transaction price is a 32% premium to last Friday's (May 30) closing price of P37.8/sh but a 46% discount to when DFI bought its first stake in RRHI at P94/sh back in 2018. At the deal price, the shares would be on 11x PER for 2025E and less than 0.2x EV/sales after adjusting for the value of RRHI’s interest in BPI. We expect the transaction to be immediately accretive to EPS (>10% annualized), as the financing cost from the additional debt incurred will be more than offset by a 22% reduction in share count.

In addition, the stake of DFI is not considered “public float” and would thus not affect the already meager share liquidity. Public float (%) will increase to 35.7% from 27.8% given the smaller outstanding share base.

Commercial arrangements to be maintained
The arrangement between RRHI and DFI will continue despite the strategic exit as the former will continue to be the exclusive distributor of the latter’s private label brands (Meadows and Guardian).

 
Philippine Seven - Speed bump; reducing TP but maintaining Buy
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Revisiting our estimates and target price
The shares of Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) are down 34% YTD, in part because of the disappointing 1Q results. The looming leadership change is, in our view, also a factor as it introduces a measure of uncertainty. We reduce our profit estimates for FY25/26 by 16/20% and our target price by 14% following the latest, disappointing results. Despite the near-term speed bump and the management reshuffle, we are cautiously optimistic about SEVN’s long-term prospects and retain our BUY rating.

Soft start for the year
SEVN reported net profit of P601mn in 1Q25, down 6% YoY. Both revenues and systemwide sales grew a rather soft +5% as same-store sales unexpectedly contracted 1.2%. While there were short-term seasonal issues (e.g. Holy Week timing and the leap year base of comparison), the key underlying factor was, in our view, weaker demand attributable to the exodus of POGOs. The latter was aggravated by operational issues that should be resolved by 2H.

Impending leadership shuffle
The company disclosed in April that its president, Victor Paterno, will assume the role of non-executive chairman in July and the current COO, Lee Ying-Jung, will take over as president. Paterno has been in his role for two decades and it’s unclear how the reshuffle might alter the company’s strategy and outlook.

Small format stores taking the lead
The overall industry outlook remains bright, in our view. Growth in modern grocery retailing in recent periods has been led by small format stores with both consumers and retailers alike adopting and adapting to “proximity shopping.” SEVN has been a key participant in this trend, and we expect this to remain so.

Revisiting FY25-26E EPS estimates and target price
We adjust our forecasts for the latest results as the company experiences near-term demand softness. We also cut our DCF-based (COE of 12% and terminal growth of 4.0%) target price for SEVN to P60/share, which implies 23x PER for this year sliding to 21x for FY26E. Key downside risks are mainly competition and execution.

BPI P10 InstaPay transfer fee made permanent

Bank of Philippine Islands (BPI) permanently reduced its InstaPay transfer fee (inter-bank fund transfers made online) to P10 from P25 originally. Previously, the lower P10 fee was a temporary promo (March 7 to May 31). Head of consumer banking Maria Cristina Go noted that there was strong demand with increased transactions throughout online and mobile banking channels throughout the promo period. She highlighted that the lowering of the fee is part of BPI's commitment for more affordable banking. For other banks, BDO and UnionBank charge P10 per InstaPay transaction, while Security Bank and Metrobank charge P25.

Monde continues Quorn overhaul but keeps options open

In an interview with Bilyornaryo, Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) CFO Jesse Teo stated that the company is focused on revitalizing its meat alternatives business segment, Quorn Foods. Teo highlighted that there has been improvements made in the segment with losses in Quorn down 73% to P58mn, gross profit up 11% to P759mn in 1Q25, and Quorn's improved cash generation to repay a GBP£12mn loan. Net sales however, was still in a decline, down 4% YoY to P3.3bn in 1Q. He said that the turnaround will take time as it has only been two years but also mentioned that "…If it really doesn't work out, we can always sell." Teo reiterated management's commitment as the Ang family and CEO Henry Soesanto pledged up to 2.15bn of their Monde shares to protect the company from further impairment losses in the meat alts business for the next 10 years from Dec 2023. Recall that Monde acquired Quorn back in 2015, but post-pandemic the segment has been posting losses given elevated input costs and tighter consumer wallets. In FY24, the meat alternatives segment posted P13.6bn (-4% YoY) in sales, gross profit of P2.9bn (-3%), hit EBITDA breakeven, and a core net loss of -P804bn.

May inflation down further to 1.3%

CPI inflation for May dipped further to 1.3% from the 1.4% print for April. The lower headline inflation figure is being led declining commodity prices. House approves P200 wage hike after third reading

House Bill No. 11376 or the Wage Hike For Minimum Wage Workers Act was approved during its third and final reading last Tuesday. Under the bill, the daily rate of minimum wage workers in the private sector will be increased by P200 per day. This includes those in contractual and sub-contractual arrangements, whether agricultural or nonagricultural. The bill also warned that the wage hike must not lead to the reduction of existing allowances and benefits. Not adhering to the law will result in fines ranging from P100K to P500K and the imprisonment of responsible officers. Small businesses that may be affected by the wage hikes can avail of incentives from the Department of Labor and Employment. BMBEs (Barangay Micro Business Enterprises) are exempted from implementing the wage hike once it turns into law. Business enterprises engaged in production, processing, or manufacturing of products, as well as trading and services with total assets of not more than P3mn. The proposed P200 per day wage hike is higher than the Senate's version of the bill (SB 2534), which proposes a P100-daily minimum wage increase. Note that the bill has been passed by both houses of Congress, but will only become law after being processed through a bicameral conference committee, and lastly approved by the President. The Congress will adjourn for a final time on June 13.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in April

The March trade deficit widened to US$4.1bn, up 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in exports and the decline in imports. This brings the 4M25 trade deficit to US$12.7bn,13% up YoY. Imports for the month declined 7% YoY to US$10.2bn, a reversal from the 12% increase in Mar. Primarily driven by the decrease in fuel and raw & intermediate goods imports. Exports grew 7% YoY to US$6.7bn, the fourth consecutive month of growth. Electronics exports fell 5% to $3.4bn, a reversal from the 1% YoY increase in Mar. These made up the bulk of exports at 50% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports jumped 22% YoY to US$3.4bn. Growth in non-electronics exports was led by other manufactured goods, coconut oil, machinery and transport equipment.

Government debt inches up to P16.75trn in end-April

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.41% MoM to P16.75tn as of end-April. This was due to additional borrowings which was offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P57.38 end-Mar to P56.25 end-Apr). Domestic debt rose by 1.8% (P211bn) MoM to P11.59trn due to the impact of net borrowings. External debt decreased by 2.7% (P142.3bn) MoM to P5.16trn given a decrease in the peso equivalent of USD debt (P124.7bn) due to local currency appreciation, and net repayments (P58.3bn). The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.2% and 30.8% being external borrowings. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35trn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn (see here). This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%.

Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act sees vetoed changes; law to take effect July 1

President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. signed Republic Act No. 12214 or the Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act (CMEPA) last May 30. Some key features of the law would be the reduction of stock transaction tax 0.6% to 0.1%, documentary stamp tax (DST) on original share issues lowered to 0.75% from 1%, and DST exemption for mutual fund and UITF share deals (original issuance, redemption, or other disposition of shares). It is to note that President Marcos vetoed certain provisions prior to signing. Marcos vetoed the proposed removal of the tax exemption of foreign investors' income from their transactions with foreign currency deposit units (FDCUs). The removal of such tax exemption would have implied higher costs for dollar-denominated borrowings. The President also vetoed the provision imposing DST liability on betters of the Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) games. This was done to avoid negative impacts on revenues from legal gaming activities which are a funding source for official gov't programs. According to the Dept. of Finance, CMEPA was projected to raise P41.9bn in gov't revenues by 2030 but after adjusting for the vetoed items, the law is expected to generate P25bn by 2030. The law will take effect on July 1, following publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one newspaper of general circulation.
