REGIS REPORT

19 September 2025

The market finally saw a rebound after falling for 5 straight weeks, rising +2.5% to end at 6265, as the Fed’s much anticipated 25bp cut appears to have sparked interest in our lagging market.

In terms of sectors, a big rally in Banks carried the market. Recall that the sector had been sold down for weeks due to concerns on asset quality (coming from the fast-growing consumer loans segment) as well the potential margin pressure that may come from a lower interest rate environment. The market rally was led by BDO +10.2%, BPI +6.0%, MBT +4.3% which all came from oversold levels. Note that BDO and MBT have now recovered their losses quarter, although BPI remains the laggard (down 12% for the quarter still).

Driving the index as well was heavyweight ICT which advanced +5.7% to a new all-time high, as it was consistently among the top foreign-bought names in the market during the week. A rebound in its key Mexico port volumes for August lent confidence to investors that indeed the slowdown seen in prior months was due to a temporary disruption by a customs strike rather than weak underlying demand. Likewise, prior worries over the effects of a tariff war looks to have dissipated – note the stock is up by more than 60% from its April lows during the height of US’ tariff rhetoric. Also, given its sizable market cap and high liquidity, it’s become a proxy of sorts for the Philippine market, notwithstanding its global operations. Note that its market cap exceeded the coveted 1 trillion peso mark this week.

Meantime, property stocks corrected despite the cut in the Fed rate. That was well anticipated after all, akin to a sell-on-news phenomenon. Moreover, investors fretted over the BSP's repeated comments that domestic rates have hit a "sweet spot". ALI -6.8% led the sector lower, followed by SMPH -0.6%.

Average daily activity stood at $154m, 38% more than last week. This was boosted by the active, $250m session on Friday due to the FTSE rebal where there were notable inflows that went into AP -0.2%, MWC (unch) and SECB +1.9%, and outflow on FGEN -7.3%.

RESEARCH
Philippine Telecoms - A new playing field?
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

A more subdued outlook

Telecom industry revenue growth has slowed to a crawl and new legislation could introduce further competition risks. We refresh our numbers for the telecom operators under coverage and turn more cautious. However, we remain of the view that the telcos offer attractive value, especially in light of the significant underperformance of their shares. We adjust our target prices for PLDT (to P1,300), Globe (P2,100) and Converge (P18.0) but retain our BUY ratings.

Mobile revenue stalling; broadband sub growth rebounding

Industry-wide mobile revenue growth slowed to a stop in 1H25, with both incumbent operators already reporting YoY declines in revenue. Meanwhile, growth in wireline broadband subscribers is re-accelerating. However, revenue growth is lagging with ARPUs under pressure. Longer-term, we expect broadband ARPUs to decline further given affordability constraints.

New legislation likely to raise competition

New legislation (“Konektadong Pinoy Act” or KPA) was recently passed that would ease the entry of new operators in the telecom industry. 

Incumbent broadband operators are, in our view, more vulnerable than the mobile operators. New entrants in broadband can start small (and local) and would thus need less initial capital than a new mobile operator. Furthermore, broadband pricing in the country is still considerably higher than in the region.

 

Devil is in the details

The real-world impact of the new law will depend much on the final wording of its implementing rules and regulations (“IRR”), which will still be crafted by the DICT. It is still possible that the IRR would water down the changes outlined in the KPA, making the law less than effective relative to its aims.

Attractive value and yields

We re-affirm our BUY ratings on the three Philippine telecom plays, notwithstanding the tempered outlook for the industry. The larger telcos, PLDT and Globe, provide attractive yields of close to 9% and 7%, respectively, even if the outlook for them isn’t exciting (revenue growth <5%). For Converge, we reckon its shares offer good value, especially after the recent battering they suffered. The stock is now on 7x PER and 4x EBITDA, with 3-year earnings CAGR of 9%. Key risks: competition and adverse government regulatory action.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Corp. expands P20bn buyback program to include listed subsidiaries

Ayala Corp.’s board approved the widening of coverage of the company’s share buyback program to include its purchase of shares in publicly listed subsidiaries. Recall that AC’s buyback program of P10bn was approved in Dec 2019 and was further expanded to P20bn in 2021. Since Dec 2019, we estimate AC has spent P7.9bn for its buyback program. AC has also spent P6.5bn since Dec 2019 buying shares of ALI, GLO, and BPI.

ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rebound in August, as congestion eases

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), rose 12% YoY to 139k TEUs in August, reversing declines in the past two months. We attribute the recovery to easing customs delays. This brings CMSA’s 8M25 volumes to 987k TEUs, up 4% YoY and trending below our full-year forecast of 1.63mn TEUs (+12% YoY). To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year will need to average 160k TEUs per month.

CMSA’s capacity today is at 1.7mn TEUs (~140k per month) and this will increase to “over 2mn TEUs” (~170k TEUs/month) by year-end (new berth will be completed by Sep 2025). We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues and 24% of EBITDA.

Megaworld sells MREIT shares

Megaworld sold 168.6mn MREIT shares at P13.28/share or a total value of P2.24bn. At this price, MREIT offers 7.6% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). MREIT seeks shareholder approval for the issuance of 1.36bn primary shares, which will likely be used for a share-for-property swap with its sponsor, Megaworld. It will hold its stockholders’ meeting on September 29. MREIT is targeting to grow its leasable area to 600k sqm by end-2025 and to 1mn sqm by 2027. It currently has a leasable portfolio of 480k sqm.

The share issuance will raise MREIT’s shares outstanding by 37%. We estimate that the sale of shares brought the AGI group’s ownership from 62.2% to 57.7% of MREIT. If all of the 1.36bn new shares will be issued to the AGI group, then we estimate it will need to sell another c. 150mn MREIT shares in order for the latter to meet minimum public ownership requirements. At the last closing price, this would be equivalent to c. P2.0bn. MREIT currently offers 7.5% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast).

DigiPlus reportedly in talks to acquire COD Manila operations

According to Bilyonaryo.com, DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is in talks to acquire Melco’s contract for City of Dream’s Manila. But the report did note that talks have been “complicated by PLUS’s share price slide…” Recall that Bilyonaryo.com previously reported that Melco wanted US$1bn for its contract with Belle Corp (BEL PM) to operate COD Manila; the two parties have a revenue sharing agreement. 

For context, integrated resort operators (that have spent minimum capex of US$200mn) are charged only 29.2% of online gross gaming revenue vs 38% of GGR for pure online firms. We estimate that if the lower fees/taxes were applied to 1H25, then the net profit of PLUS would have been c. 40% higher – c. P11.8bn vs the actual 1H25 net profit of P8.4bn.

City of Dreams Manila generated US$58mn (c. P3.3bn) of EBITDA in 1H25. Melco’s share of this was US$27mn (c. P1.54bn).

DigiPlus to launch Brazil operations on September 22

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) said it would soft-launch its Brazil online platform on September 22: “GamePlus”, its first international brand. DigiPlus’ Brazil country manager Graham Tidey said: “GamePlus will launch with a catalogue featuring over 150 of Brazil’s top games – both in free-to-play and real-money versions – and will soon add exclusive content inspired by local folklore, casual games, and sports, ensuring a culturally resonant experience.” Then DigiPlus plans to launch “BingoPlus” as its second platform in Brazil in 2026.

The Brazil market generated US$3.2bn in GGR in 1H25. For context, 1H25 Philippine GGR grew 26% to US$3.8bn, US$2.0bn of which came from e-games.

AGI to begin construction of Mactan and Boracay casinos next year

AGI’s gaming subsidiary, Travellers International, will begin construction of the US$400mn Mactan World Resorts and the US$300mn Boracay World Resorts next year. The upcoming integrated resorts will be in existing Megaworld/GERI townships (Mactan Newtown and Boracay Newcoast). Travellers is prioritizing the completion of the US$1.25bn Westside Integrated Resort, which is slated to open in 3Q26.

Cebu Air: August pax volumes flat, but load factors higher

Cebu Air reported that its systemwide pax volumes dipped 0.4% YoY to 2.1mn in August, underpinned by a 3.2% reduction in seat capacity due to unscheduled engine removals, removal of two aircraft under wet-lease, and scheduled maintenance activities. Note that 3Q is also typically a lean season, while seat load factors rose 240bps YoY to 83.7%, which suggests stronger fares.

This brings 8M25 pax volumes to 18.1mn, up 15% YoY and trending slightly below our full-year forecast of 28.5mn (+16%). To reach our forecast, pax volumes will need to average 2.6mn per month for the rest of the year.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in August

We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 26bps MoM to 18.5% (in August) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index declined 3bps MoM to 23.9%. The more muted drop in the foreign ownership of PSE Index was partly due to the removal of BLOOM (which has foreign ownership of 12.5%) and the addition of PLUS (which has foreign ownership of 25.2%). Net foreign selling increased to US$74mn, from US$29mn in July. Of the 29 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 16 saw increases in foreign ownership while 12 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

o          GTCAP – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership for the second consecutive month. Foreign ownership rose 39bps MoM to 23.4%, as net foreign buying hit US$12.5mn, the second highest for the month.

o          ICT – foreign ownership climbed 34bps MoM to 34.9%, as it experienced net foreign buying of US$18.9mn, the highest for the month.  

o          AGI – foreign ownership increased 33bps MoM to 16.6%, as foreign buying amounted to US$4mn.

o          ACEN – foreign ownership fell 1.03 percentage points MoM to 23.0%.

o          CNVRG – foreign ownership declined 37bps MoM to 15.6%. It saw net foreign selling of US$10.6mn, the fourth highest for the month.

Finance Secretary expects one more rate cut this year

Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that there is room for "at least one more cut" but noted that the US Federal Reserve's stance and the Philippines' inflation trajectory are the key decision-making factors. Recall that BSP Governor Remolona during the last Monetary Board meeting in Aug mentioned that the current rate of 5.00% is the "sweet spot" given recent data but floated the possibility of one more cut this year depending on external factors. The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect another 25bp cut to 4.75% (or total 100bps for the year).

DPWH cuts P255bn worth of flood control projects in 2026 budget proposal, resumes bidding of infra projects with stricter requirements

Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vince Dizon recommended to the House of Representatives a revised budget of P626bn which is 30% (P255bn) lower than the initially proposed P881bn and 42% lower than this year's DPWH budget of P1.1trn. The main revision follows President Marcos' directive for a "sweeping review" of the DPWH's budget and the removal of the P255bn allocation for flood control projects for 2026. The P255bn was said to likely be reallocated to other priority sectors such as education, agri, health programs, etc.

Infrastructure spend for 2026 was initially projected to be P1.56trn (+3% YoY, 5.1% of GDP). Accounting for the P255bn cut in spending for flood control projects, infra spend would amount to P1.3trn (-13% YoY, 4.3% of GDP). For 2025, projected infra spend is P1.51tn (-3% YoY, 5.3% of GDP). The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than this year's P6.33trn budget.

The gov't has lifted the temporary suspension on infrastructure project bidding but prospective bidders for public works now face stricter requirements such as: Live-streaming of all bidding activities; Geotagged photos that will be monitored by relevant offices; Net financial contracting capacity (NFCC) to be assessed based on tax filings through the BIR's electronic system with backed audited financial statements; Sworn disclosures of beneficial ownership, including individuals who ultimately own or control the entity.

Current account deficit narrows to $5.0bn in 2Q25

The country's CA deficit narrowed to US$5.0bn (4.0% of GDP) in 2Q25 from the US$5.9bn (5.2% of GDP) deficit in 2Q24. The decline in the deficit was driven by the Philippines' narrower trade in goods gap - the trade-in-goods deficit was narrower by 5% YoY to US$12.6bn, given the faster growth in exports (+7%) vs. imports (+4%). Meanwhile net receipts from services trade fell 12% YoY to US$2.4bn, as imports grew 2% (to US$9.8bn) while exports fell 1% (to US$12.2bn). The decline in services receipts was primarily due to higher travel imports given increased outbound travels as well as lower export receipts from a drop in tourist arrivals which was slightly offset by robust revenues from BPO activities.

This brings the 1H25 CA deficit to 3.9% of GDP, wider than last year's 3.6%. For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to hit US$16.3bn or 3.3% of GDP. Recall that in 2024, the country posted its second largest CA deficit at US$17.5bn (3.8% of GDP)—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of US$12.4bn (2.8% of GDP).

OFW Remittances up 3.0% YoY in July

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.0% YoY to US$3.2bn in July, decelerating from the 3.7% growth seen in June. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, and Saudi Arabia were cited to be the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total. Sea-based remittances growth moderated to 3.1% YoY from 3.5% the previous month. In PHP terms, remittances were unchanged YoY.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) slump again in August

CAMPI reported vehicle sales fell 7.6% YoY, 5.5% MoM to 36,174 units in August, marking the second month of weakness (note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales). This bring YT-August vehicle sales to 305,381 units, +0.2% YoY. Assuming BYD’s market share of ~5%, we estimate industry car sales may be up ~5% YoY YTD.

EV sales totaled 2,244 units in August, down 17% MoM and accounting for 6.2% of overall sales. 8M25 EV sales reached 18,439 units, accounting for 6.04% of sales.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,023 units, down 5% YoY, 5.7% MoM. This brings TMP’s 8M25 unit sales to 146,357, up 4.1% YoY. To reach TMP’s sales target of 235k units (+8%), sales should average 22k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach REGIS’ sales forecast of 231k units (+6%), sales should average 21k units for the rest of the year.

Anti-Money Laundering Council to station personnel in casinos

The Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) said it might station personnel in casinos in order to prevent money laundering. Senate Committee on Games and Amusement Chairman Erwin Tulfo believes that casinos were aware that government officials gambled in their premises. He plans to plan to file a resolution to investigate casinos which are turning a blind eye on questionable transactions and refusing to report such to the AMLC.
