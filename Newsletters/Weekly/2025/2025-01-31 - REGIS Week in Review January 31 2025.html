REGIS REPORT

31 January 2025

Philippine equities have officially entered bear market territory, as the PCOMP's 6.9% or 433.61-pt WoW drop to 5,862.59 brought the index down 22.4% from the recent October 2024 peak. The index fell below the 6,000 handle for the first time since November 2023 and closed at its lowest level since October 2022. 

Funds adjusting for the PSE Index February rebalancing changes dragged local shares this week as large caps sold off to make room for the latest constituent additions in the benchmark. At Friday's close, CBC PM and AREIT PM joined the PCOMP, resulting in significant passive outflows for the existing index members which pulled the index down 4.0% on Friday alone.

On rebal day, CBC PM closed limit up (+38.9% DoD / +33.8% WoW) and AREIT PM likewise outperformed (+7.7% DoD / +4.7% WoW), while deleted NIKL PM (-17.8% DoD / -30.2% WoW) slumped. The impact on the other deleted name WLCON (-1.6% DoD / -10.2% WoW) was much less as bargain hunters finally emerged to pick up one of last year's biggest underperformers. Meanwhile, outflows for the sizable downweights in PCOMP heavyweights SM PM (-7.7% WoW) and ICT (-9.1% WoW) caused the biggest drag on the index, with the drop in both names accounting for nearly a third of the PCOMP's WoW decline. 

What further dragged market sentiment was the disappointing 4Q GDP report that clocked economic growth of just 5.2% for the quarter vs consensus' 5.5% estimate. This took FY24 GDP to just 5.6% against consensus' 5.8% forecast -- also trailing government's 6.0-6.5% target. The miss came from softer-than-expected private consumption (+4.7% in 4Q) and capital formation (+4.1% in 4Q), which was partly attributed to unusually disruptive weather during the period.

ADTO more than doubled WoW to US$157m due to PSE rebalancing flows, while the market logged modest foreign inflow of US$1m for the week. Peso and treasury yields were relatively flat WoW, at 58.372:$1 and 6.2288% for the PH10Y.

Filinvest REIT to acquire Festival Mall from Filinvest Land

Filinvest REIT will acquire Festival Mall from Filinvest Land via share-for-property swap. Festival Mall is a 122,000sqm mall in Alabang, Muntinlupa. The transaction will not include the expansion wing of Festival Mall. The acquisition will increase FILRT’s GLA by 37% to 452,000sqm. Filinvest REIT will issue 1.626bn new FILRT shares at P3.85/share – P6.26bn. This represents a 30% premium to the 30-day VWAP. This will increase FILRT’s shares outstanding by 35%. At the current price of P3.14/share, the total cost is effectively P5.1bn. Upon completion of the transactions, FLI’s ownership in FILRT will rise from 51% to 63%.

Meralco, Aboitiz Power completes acquisition of San Miguel's LNG facilities

Meralco PowerGen and Aboitiz Power-owned unit, Therma NatGas Power Inc., reached financial close of the US$3.3bn LNG deal (aka “Chromite”) with San Miguel Global Power (SMGP). Under the deal, Meralco and AP will jointly invest in SMGP’s 1,278-megawatt (MW) Ilijan power plant, its new 1,320-MW combined cycle power facility (Excellent Energy or EERI), and its LNG import and regasification terminal. MER will hold 60% of the joint venture with AP, which will take a 67% in the LNG facilities. MER will thus hold a 40% economic interest in the gas-fired plants and LNG terminal; SMC’s stake will be diluted to 33% (from 100%) while AP will hold 27%. The three units (440MW each) of the new LNG power plant under EERI are now mechanically complete; two of those began testing a few weeks ago.

Meralco 2024 energy sales rise 6.4% YoY in FY24

Meralco said that energy sales reached 54,325 GWH in 2024, up 6.4% YoY and surpassing its target of 53,473 GWH. The growth was attributed to El Niño-induced warmer temperatures and sustained customer energizations. Note that MER’s energy sales grew by 7.1% in 9M24 and 4.1% YoY in 3Q24. This implies that 4Q24 energy sales grew by ~4% YoY. Residential energy sales volume grew by 9.4% (vs. 10.4% in 9M24). Over 215,000 new customers were added, bringing the total to eight million as of October 2024. The commercial segment saw a 7.3% increase in energy sales volume, decelerating from the 8.1% YoY pace in 9M24. Growth was driven by strong performance in the hotel, restaurant, retail trade, education, and real estate sectors. The industrial sector had a modest 1.5% growth (vs 1.6% YoY in 9M24). The semiconductor, electronics, food and beverage, cement, and plastic products sectors contributed to growth, offsetting the decline in the steel sector.

ADB, Ayala Sign $100 Million Financing Deal to Support Electric Mobility in the Philippines

Asian Development Bank (ADB) has signed a financing package of up to $100 million to support Ayala Corporation's efforts to develop an EV ecosystem in the Philippines. The package includes a concessional loan from the Canadian Climate and Nature Fund for the Private Sector in Asia (CANPA), a trust fund managed by ADB and supported by a commitment of Can$360 million from the Government of Canada. The blended financing features an innovative pricing structure aimed at accelerating the deployment of EV Charging Station (EVCS) infrastructure; and a portion of the ADB financing will be allocated to procure EV’s from manufacturers across the Philippines. Ayala Corporation’s subsidiary AC Mobility Holdings, Inc. (AMHI) will be implementing the EVCS project, which would involve the installation of up to 1,700 EV Charging Stations around the country. According to ADB, EV development is still nascent in the Philippines with high barriers to adoption- including high initial costs, limited charging infrastructure, and evolving technologies. However, the Philippines EV Industry Development Act and various tax incentives are helping promote the growth of the EV sector. As of Dec 2024, data from the Department of Energy showed that the country has 833 charging points = 329 AC + 41 DC + 463 battery swapping stations.

Ayala Land aims to grow earnings at double the pace of GDP growth

Ayala Land CFO Augusto Bengzon said that the company aims to grow net profit at double the pace of GDP growth over the next five years. He did not specify if he was referring to real GDP growth or nominal GDP growth. The company previously said that it plans to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023). This implies 15% CAGR.

IMI to shut down Chengdu facility

Integrated Micro-Electronics, Inc. (IMI PM), the electronics manufacturing arm of the Ayala Group, is strategically closing its facility in Chengdu, China. Production at the site concluded last Dec 2024 and after finalizing transfer of assets and equipment will return the facility to its landlord by Feb 2025. This was done in an effort to streamline operations as IMI will continue serving its global client base from other manufacturing sites.

Pacific Online to acquire 37.5% stake in e-casino operator

Pacific Online Systems Corporation (LOTO PM) entered into an agreement with HHR Philippines and the latter’s principal shareholders. LOTO will acquire 37.5% of the expanded outstanding capital (after LOTO’s investment). The proceeds will be used by HHR to fund its expansion program. HHR Philippines has an online gaming (e-casino) license from PAGCOR and operates under the brand “Buenas.”

Tanduay eyes France, Italy, Latin America in global expansion push

Tanduay Distillers Inc., the alcoholic beverage arm of the LT Group (LT PM), aims to sustain its growth trajectory by entering into both emerging and established markets. The company plans to introduce its products to France, Italy, Spain, Denmark, the Nordic region, and several Latin American markets, on top of its offerings in 26 countries. As of late, Tanduay is the world's number one rum brand having sold 23.4mn case liters in 2023. Tanduay’s Bacolod-based plant produces up to 55,000 cases of rum daily, made with molasses sourced from the sugarcane farms of Negros Occidental.

PSE Index rebalancing

The PSE announced that China Banking Corp (CBC PM) and AREIT (AREIT PM) will be added to the PSE Index, replacing Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM) and Nickel Asia (NIKL PM). The rebalancing takes effect at the opening of February 3.

4Q24 GDP grows 5.2% but misses 2024 target given weak consumption

GDP grew at 5.2% in 4Q24, flat vs. the 5.2% growth rate in 3Q. This is below consensus expectations of 5.5%. On the demand side, the lackluster growth stems from the slowdown in private consumption (4.7% from 5.2% in 3Q) and a slowdown in private investments (4.1% from 13.7%). Private consumption may have been dampened by the six typhoons that hit the country from Oct to Nov as well as the lingering effects of elevated inflation. The slowdown in private investments was mainly due to durable equipment growth coming off from a high base due to significant aircraft orders in 2023. Net imports narrowed to -0.3% from -3.0%, but still weighs down on overall GDP. On the supply side, the agri sector exhibited a decline for the third month in a row, declining by -1.8%, but is still an improvement from -2.7% the previous quarter. Growth in the service sector also improved and was faster at 6.7% from 6.3% but the industry sector slowed to 4.4% from 5.1%. Bringing FY24 GDP growth to 5.6%, below the government's target of 6-6.5% and the Bloomberg consensus median forecast of 5.8%. Recall that the gov't revised its GDP growth expectations for this year last Dec 2024. The 2025 GDP growth target is set at 6.0-8.0% a wider band than the previous 6.5-7.5% range given "evolving domestic and global uncertainties."

BSP chief: Reserve requirement ratio may go down to 5% this year

In an interview, BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that the country's current RRR of 7% is "still too high" especially when comparing to peers in the region. He states that the RRR cut may happen "possibly this year." Recall that the BSP previously cut the RRR by 250 bps to 7% last Oct 2024.

Trade deficit further narrows to $4.14bn in December

The December trade deficit narrowed down further to US$4.14bn, the smallest since Mar 2024, down 1.0% YoY and 14.8% MoM from $4.85bn last Nov. Driven by the decline in the value of imports which narrowed the deficit for the period. Imports for the month declined 2% YoY to US$9.79bn. The drop was led by fuel as it continued to decrease YoY by 3%, a six month streak of declines in fuel imports. Exports continue to fall at 2.2% YoY to US$5.66bn, marking the fourth consecutive month of contraction. Led by the decline in electronics exports which continued to drop by 17% YoY to US$2.80bn, electronics accounted for 50% of total exports during the period. Bringing the FY24 trade deficit to US$54.21bn, +3% YoY. Although wider than last year's deficit, this latest figure is lower than the recently largest trade gap of US$57.65bn in 2022.

Price cap on imported rice to go as low as P49 in March

The maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice, currently set at P58 per kilo, will be gradually reduced starting February 2025, with a potential drop to P49 by March if global prices remain stable, according to Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel. The MSRP will be lowered to P55 on February 5, then to P52 on February 15. This reduction is contingent on global prices staying within $530–$550 per metric ton for 5% broken rice. To note, recent prices from Asian exporters are notably lower, with Vietnam offering 5% broken rice at $413 per metric ton and 25% broken rice at $387 per metric ton. The gradual price reduction aims to give rice traders and importers time to adjust and to avoid destabilizing the industry. Japanese black rice, red rice, basmati, imported glutinous rice, and locally produced rice are exempt from the MSRP. The initiative is an additional measure to tackle persistently high domestic rice prices despite falling global prices alongside President Marcos' earlier rice tariff cut from 35% to 15% last June 2024. Recall the DA implemented the MSRP on January 20 to keep rice affordable for consumers while supporting the rice industry's profitability. The DA is closely monitoring compliance with the MSRP in public markets. Strict enforcement will begin once official guidelines are issued in February as those caught with violations could result in penalties.

Senate approves bill for lowering tax on stock transactions

Senate Bill No. 2865 or the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act was approved on final reading. Rationale for the bill was to make the country's capital market more competitive relative to its regional peers as well as to encourage more Filipinos to invest in the stock market. A forecast by the PSE, states that a lowered 0.1% stock transaction tax would boost stock trading to P4.9tn by 2029. Key measures in the bill are as follows: Reduction of stock transaction tax; Tax on stock transactions from 0.1% to 0.6%; Taxes on dividends; Tax on dividends for non-resident investors to be lowered from 25% currently to 10%, unified with the current 10% tax rate on dividends for residents; Minimum corporate income tax for foreign entities; Requires resident foreign corporations and their regional operating headquarters to pay a minimum corporate income tax of 10% on taxable income from 2% previously. While tax rates on capital gains and interest income remain unchanged at 15% and 20%, respectively. The bill shall proceed to a bicameral conference committee with the lower house' version before the President signs the final version into law. The effectivity date of the bill was stated to be on or after January 1, 2025, as of the latest version of the reading but may likely be adjusted until after it is signed into law.

House Committee approves P200 minimum wage hike

The House Committee on Labor and Employment approved a substitute bill, proposing for a P200 across-the-board daily wage increase. The approval follows Speaker Martin Romualdez's announced support for the passage of the wage hike. Recall that the Senate approved a bill increasing the daily minimum wage in the private sector by P100. Note that the 19th Congress will adjourn on Feb 8 for the mid-term elections and will resume on June 2 to June 13. A new set of lawmakers (20th Congress) will be appointed by July 28. Minimum wages in NCR today are between P608-645 while the region with the lowest min. wages (P316-361) is BARMM.

Lower House approves bill to lower tax rates on cigarettes, sought to improve collection

The House of Representatives approved the bill which will lower the excise tax increases for cigarettes, tobacco, vapor (e-cig/e-vape) products as to mitigate the decline in tax collection from the goods. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) was P52bn short of its P185bn tobacco excise tax target last year. The new bill seeks to lower the increase to 2% every even-numbered year effective Jan 2026 and maintain 4% every odd-numbered year effective Jan 2027 till Dec 2035. Current law has the annual increase on excise tax for tobacco products at 4%. A projection from the House ways and means committee says the measure can net the gov't P66bn from 2026 to 2030. The bill also gives the President the option to increase the tax rate up to 5% if the national gov't deficit of the previous year exceeds the program deficit by an equivalent of 2% of GDP of the previous year.

Maharlika takes 20% stake in Synergy Grid

Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) signed a binding agreement with Synergy Grid & Development Phils., Inc. (SGP PM) for an undisclosed amount. MIC will subscribe to preferred shares offered by SGP and will be entitled to two (2) seats in SGP’s expanded nine-member board (currently 7 board seats). At the NGCP level, MIC will also have two board seats, once total seats are increased to 15 from 10. MIC President and CEO Rafael D. Consing said that this flagship investment will “safeguard the nation’s power supply from external threats and disruptions, citing the need for the government to have a say in NGCP decisions.” A news outlet wrote that the MIC has initially purchased 1.3bn preferred shares of SGP at P15/sh (P19.5bn). MIC has the option to convert the preferred shares to common shares at an additional P7.50/sh (P9.75bn). SGP last traded at P10.98/sh (total authorized and issued common shares at 5.266bn). SGP indirectly controls 60% of National Grid Corp’s (NGCP) voting capital stock and has an effective equity interest of 45.726%.

Department of Energy pre-qualifies 12 companies in GEAP-3

The Department of Energy said that 12 companies have been shortlisted for the third green energy auction round (GEA-3). First Gen’s Energy Development Corp., Bac-Man Geothermal Inc., and First Gen Hydro Power Corp. Razon-led Ahunan Power Inc. and Olympia Violago Water and Power Inc. SMC’s San Roque Hydropower. Yuchengco-led Maibarara Geothermal Inc. Dexter Tiu-led Repower Energy Development Corp. Pan Pacific Renewable Power Phils. Corp. Pulangi Hydro Power Corp. United Hydro Power Builders Corp. COHECO Badeo Corp. For GEA-3, the government is offering impounding hydro, pumped storage hydro and geothermal contracts with a combined capacity of 4,650MW. Auction will consist of 100 MW of geothermal, 300 MW of impounding hydro, and 4,250 MW of pumped-storage hydro. Auction is scheduled on Feb 11 and the notice of award is targeted in early May. The DOE previously said that the target start of deliveries is 2028-2030 for hydro while 2024-2030 for geothermal.

PAGCOR revenue grows 41% in 2024

Revenue of the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, grew 41% to P112bn in 2024. Revenue from e-games (including bingo) climbed 125% to P48.8bn. Revenue from licensed casinos was flat at P33.1bn.
