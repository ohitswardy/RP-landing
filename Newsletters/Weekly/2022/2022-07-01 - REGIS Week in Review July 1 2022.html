REGIS REPORT

01 July 2022

The PSEi inched down 0.8% this week, closing lower for the fourth straight week on continued foreign outflows.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$41m this week – with net outflows now stretching to 20 straight sessions. Inflation fears amidst rising rates and weakening demand continue to weigh on sentiment globally. Biggest outflows were seen in banks, property, and telcos.

Large cap conglos, banks, and property weighed heavily on the market this week as risk-off sentiment prevailed.  SM Investments (-3.9% WoW) and BDO (-4.8% WoW) pushed the index lower.  AEV (-5.7% WoW), Ayala Land (-3.2% WoW), Metrobank (-4.3% WoW), and BPI (-23% WoW) dragged down the index as well.  Losers overwhelmed gainers 16 to 12 this week.

The consumer sector  meanwhile outperformed this week. Investor interest was prompted by some soft commodities coming off sharply from recent peaks, helping to provide some relief for the likes of URC (+14.4% WoW) and Monde (+1.6% WoW) which have been sold down significantly due to cost input inflation. Improved sentiment was enjoyed by other consumer names Jollibee (+2.5% WoW), Wilcon (+2.1% WoW), and Puregold (+1.3% WoW) which closed higher this week.

Converge (+13.2% WoW) and AC Energy (+7.7% WoW) also gained markedly, bouncing from previous sharp declines.

Bloomberg reported that Globe (+1.4% WoW) is in “advanced talks” to sell part of its telecom tower network. Among the potential bidders are Aboitiz Group (-5.8% WoW),  Meralco (-1.1% WoW) as well as private equity firm KKR.  A sale involving half of Globe’s 12,000 towers would be very value-accretive for Globe, assuming a similar transaction and valuations to PLDT’s tower sale. Regis estimates value enhancement worth over P450/share (vs last price of P2180) on an after-tax basis. Such a deal would also significantly de-lever Globe’s balance sheet.

Metro Pacific (-4.2% WoW) disclosed it was acquiring 51% of dairy producer, Carmen’s Best Group. The deal is worth P198m. The Carmen’s Best Group is an integrated dairy firm producing and selling premium ice cream and milk products. The transaction is part of the company’s plan to diversifying out of highly regulated industries.

Megawide (+41% WoW) surged after the anti-dummy case against officers was dismissed by a local court. The court cited the recent passage of the Amended Public Service Act. The newly-enacted law lifted foreign ownership limits on airport operations (among others). MWIDE and its partner GMR were being accused of circumventing these restrictions in the anti-dummy case.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ING exiting retail banking in PH

Dutch financial giant ING Bank is exiting the retail banking market in the Philippines, the second foreign bank to leave the country’s consumer banking segment after Citi sold its business to Union Bank of the Philippines. In a statement, ING said it would leave the retail banking market in the Philippines before the end of the year, but would further invest in wholesale banking and global shared services. ING ventured into retail banking in 2018 and currently serves more than 380l customers with savings accounts, current accounts and consumer lending.

 

LPC: POGOs start taking up office space again

Property Consultant David Leechiu said that POGOs recently took up c. 20,000sqm of office space. This is the first (take-up) transaction since 1Q20. In 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4mn sqm of office space in Metro Manila but have since vacated about 600,000sqm. Metro Manila office vacancy rose from 4% in 2019 to 19% currently.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:

57% of Filinvest Land’s NAV

47% of Megaworld’s NAV

32% of Robinsons Land’s NAV

MPIC enters strategic partnership with Carmen’s Best Group

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) bought a 51% stake in The Laguna Creamery (TLC) for P198mn. The transaction is a combination of primary and secondary shares. The shares acquired from Carmen’s Best Group, the group behind premium ice cream brand “Carmen’s Best” and locally pasteurized and homogenized fresh milk, “Holly’s milk”. Under the partnership, TLC will be consolidating the assets and business of the Carmen’s Best Group. The transaction is conditional upon customary regulatory confirmation and approvals. This latest development is part of MPI’s plans to diversify out of highly regulated businesses. Last April, MPI revived its ambitions into real estate/hospital sector, after taking full ownership of niche property developer, Landco.

 

UnionBank to infuse an additional P2bn to its digital bank

UnionBank will infuse as much as an additional P2bn of capital to its digital bank, UnionDigital Bank. UnionDigital will be launched in 2H22.

Court dismisses anti-dummy law case against Megawide

The Regional Trial Court of Lapu-Lapu City dismissed the criminal case against the directors and officers of Megawide (MWIDE PM) for the alleged violation of the anti-dummy law. The directors accused include MWIDE CEO and President Edgar Saavedra and three others. The Omnibus Order cited the enactment of RA 11659 or “Amended Public Service Act” last March 2022, which lifted foreign ownership limits of airports. Regis estimates the airport unit accounts for c. 53% (P3.9/sh) of MWIDE’s NAV of P7.2/sh (vs. current share price of P3.17/sh). Pre-pandemic, the airport unit accounted for c. 50% of MWIDE’s EBITDA and c. 60% of profit.

SEC clears Philex’s P2.65-B share sale

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has conditionally approved a P2.65-billion stock rights offering (SRO) planned by Philex Mining (PX PM) to raise funds for its Silangan copper-gold project in Surigao del Norte. Under this SRO, which will run from July 14 to July 25, existing shareholders are entitled to buy one share for every 5.8674 common shares owned.

 

 

LPC: 2Q22 net office take-up rises to 85k sqm

Leechiu Property Consultants said that nationwide net office take-up reached 85,000sqm in 2Q22 from 20,000sqm in 1Q22. The Gross take-up reached 255,000sqm (mostly from BPO) while closures amounted to 170,000sqm (mostly from POGOs). Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 19% as of 1Q22. While net office take-up has been positive, it has been far below pre-COVID levels. In 2019, BPO and traditional tenants took up a combined 750,000sqm in Metro Manila. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:

57% of Filinvest Land’s NAV

47% of Megaworld’s NAV

32% of Robinsons Land’s NAV

PSE Index August 2022 rebalancing prediction: SCC (+), SECB (-)

Regis expects SCC to be added to the PSE Index during the August 2022 rebalancing. This will lead to the removal of SECB from the index. The PSEi rebalancing in August will be based on the 12 months from July 2021 to June 2022. Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap. Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when 1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35. Based on Regis estimates, SCC has reached the top 25 and based on Regis preliminary estimates, total inflow will reach US$34mn. SCC will see an inflow of US$28mn while CNVRG will experience inflow of US$6mn (due to higher free float). SECB will see an outflow of US$31mn while the other PSEi constituents experience a combined outflow of US$3mn. Note: among brokers who make PSE Index predictions, the removal of SECB appears to be a unanimous prediction but brokers are split on whether SCC or UBP will be added to the index. Regis estimates that UBP was only among the 25% of most liquid stocks in 8 of the past 12 months, lower than the requirement of 9 months.

 

Dividend rate on Ayala Land voting prefs increases to 6.33%

The dividend rate on Ayala Land’s voting preferred shares was increased to 6.32862% from 4.74786% which was based on 90% of the 10-year BVAL as of 29 June 2022. In line with the original terms, the dividend rate was repriced on the 10th anniversary of the issuance. Regis estimates that this will reduce net profit by c. P15mn annually (c. 0.1%). Ayala Land redeemed 623mn voting preferred shares – 12.4Bn shares remain outstanding. Regis estimates that Ayala Corp owns c. 98% of the remaining voting preferred shares.

The voting preferred shares are now convertible to ALI common shares at the higher of:

1) the average closing price of the preceding 30 trading days less the par value of P0.10/share or

2) the closing price preceding the conversion date less the par value of P0.10/share    

Thus, there is no advantage in converting the voting preferred shares into common shares

ALI is currently trading at 54% discount to NAV, 1.6X FY22E P/B, and 20.0X FY22E P/E

U/KB loan growth accelerates to 10.8% in May

Based on preliminary data, bank lending of U/KBs grew 10.8% in May, faster than in April (10.1%). The loans to the manufacturing, real estate, and consumer sectors drove loans higher. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector jumped by 6.9% in April. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Overall banking sector loan growth also likely accelerated further in May. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) slowed further to 6.9% in May from 7.2% (revised from 7.3%) in April.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

SolGen Calida is COA chair under Marcos admin; PNB exec appointed to GSIS

President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has appointed outgoing Solicitor General Jose Calida as chairperson of the Commission on Audit under his administration, incoming press secretary Trixie Angeles announced Wednesday. Marcos’ camp also confirmed the appointment of Jose Arnulfo "Wick" Veloso as president and general manager of the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS). Veloso is the president and CEO of the Philippine National Bank, and also served as president of the Bankers Association of the Philippines. He was president of HSBC Philippines from 2012 to 2018.

LTFRB approves P2 hike in jeepney fares nationwide

The LTFRB approved a provisional P2 fare (+22%) increase in the minimum fare of public utility jeepneys (PUJs) nationwide beginning July 1. The minimum fares for traditional PUJs will now be P11 for the first four kilometers. In early June, the LTFRB approved a provisional fare hike to P10 from P9 in select regions such as Metro Manila, Central Luzon, Calabarzon, and Mimaropa. The LTFRB did not increase fares for succeeding kilometers. Transport fares have a weight of 4.54% in the CPI basket.

New BSP Governor open to big hikes to aid peso

Incoming BSP Governor Felipe Medalla will not shy away from larger interest rate increases if the exchange rate overshoots and selling foreign exchange wont resolve the problem. He will not rule out rate hikes of more than 25bps. The aggressive hikes will depend on the exchange rate “overshooting” and stoking imported inflation. As it stands, there was no need to match the Fed’s hikes like-for-like. Policy makers will balance managing inflation and helping the nation’s “nascent” economic recovery.

Gov’t hopes to contain rising poultry prices by easing import bans

The Department of Agriculture (DA) said it is relaxing import bans, allowing vaccine imports, and exploring new types of feed to help contain the rising prices of poultry products, including eggs. The Bureau of Animal Industry Director Reildrin G. Morales said producers are holding back production to minimize losses and are experiencing increased competition as hog raisers shifted to poultry and egg production after their herds were reduced by African Swine Fever. The DA will also soon allow the import of poultry from non-infected parts of Spain, Denmark and the Czech Republic to bolster the breeding stocks.

Wholesale prices of building materials in Metro Manila rise by 8.3%, fastest since 2011

Wholesale prices of building materials in Metro Manila rose by 8.3% in May vs +6.9% in April and +2% in May 2021. This is the fastest pace of growth since the 8.6% figure in November 2011. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) said that supply chain issues also delayed the delivery of materials from abroad, which pushed up costs onshore. The minimum wages have risen 6% in Metro Manila and c. 15% in most parts of the Philippines.

 

Treasury rejects all bids for 7-year bonds

The Bureau of Treasury rejected bids for reissued 7-year T-bonds. Had the offer been fully awarded, yield would have reached 6.947%, 52bps higher from the previous auction in May (6.428%). The yield would have also been above the 6.627% for 7-year BVAL and 45bps higher than its 6.5% coupon. The total bids reached P62bn, higher than the P35bn program. National Treasurer Rosalia V. de Leon said that bids were rejected bids as investors asked for too high a risk premium, given anticipated aggressive rate hikes from the Fed and BSP. For July, the Bureau of Treasury plans to raise P200bn, of which P60bn will be via T-bills and P140bn via T-bonds. It is set to offer longer bond tenors next month, with maturities as long as 14 years.

Metro Manila, most areas stay under Alert Level 1 until July 15

Alert Level 1 will remain in Metro Manila from July 1-15. Around 37 areas from across the Philippines “shall be placed” under the alert system’s level 2. The Gov’t also redefined alert level classifications to be based “on the revised cross tabulation of total beds utilization rate and average daily attack rate”. The new classification removes the two-week growth rate in determining case-risk classification. Recently, the nationwide cases (7-day average) are now at 711 (up from 479 the previous week), with NCR at 342 (vs 238 the previous week). The nationwide weekly covid bed occupancy rates are now at 14.9% (ICU) from 14.6% the previous week.

 

Peso breaches P55 vs dollar

The Philippine peso breached the P55-level against the US dollar on Monday but managed to temper losses at the end of the trading. The local currency finished at P54.78 against the USD from an intra-day low of P55.15. The last time the peso sank below P55 was in October 2005. The more aggressive tightening by the US Federal Reserve was a stark contrast to the dovish strategy taken by central banks in the region including the BSP. The peso is down 7.4% ytd, the worst performer among ASEAN currencies. THB is down 5.7% ytd, IDR is down 3.9% ytd, VND is down 1.9% ytd and MYR is down 6.3% ytd.

Retail prices of building materials in Metro Manila rise by 4.8%, fastest in 10 years

Retail prices of building materials in Metro Manila rose by 4.8% in March vs +3.3% in February and +1.2% in March 2021. This is the fastest pace of growth since the 5.4% figure in January 2012. Minimum wages have risen 6% in Metro Manila and c. 15% in most parts of the Philippines.
