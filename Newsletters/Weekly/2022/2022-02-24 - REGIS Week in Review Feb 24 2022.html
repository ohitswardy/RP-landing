REGIS REPORT

24 February 2022

The PSEi lost last week’s gain and then some as concerns about the escalating situation in Ukraine grew. The PSEi dropped 2.8% week on week with the weakness seen across the board. The Ukraine situation dampened the optimism in the Philippines which was starting to gain ground on lower COVID cases, re-opening plays and improving earnings by listed corporations.

However, foreigners were buyers for US$31.5mn this week and have been buyers for seven consecutive sessions. Biggest inflows were in seen in ICTSI ($10.8m), SMPH ($8.8m), SM ($8m). On the flip side the biggest outflows were seen in BDO (-$7.2m), URC (-$5.4m) and MONDE (-$4m).

The top index loser this week was MONDE (-7.9%). Regis hosted Jefferies US consumer analyst Rob Dickerson recently. During the call it was mentioned that valuations of alternative meat firms have crashed in recent weeks. The weakness of these alternative meat firms globally could have added concerns on the expansion plans of MONDE into this space.

Other top index losers this week were AC Energy (-7.2%), Ayala Land (-6.2%) and Aboitiz Power (-5.8%). The index gainers this week were Wilcon Depot (+1.5%), PLDT (+1.0%), San Miguel (+0.7%) and JG Summit (+0.5%).

SM investments (-5.4%) was sold off earlier this week but recovered some ground as the week progressed. It was announced that it would be removed from the FTSE GEIS Asia Pacific Index. SM failed one of the liquidity tests, which requires that a certain percentage of shares trade in a month. Thus, the higher the market cap of a company, the higher the required daily turnover (in US$ terms).

Earlier this week, Credit rating agency Fitch affirmed the investment grade rating of the Philippines (BBB) with a negative outlook. This follows the deterioration of the country’s fiscal ratios because of the pandemic. Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez will be proposing a new set of taxes to the incoming administration (whoever they might end to be). Among the new revenue measures are a carbon tax, a tax on cryptocurrencies, a removal of all VAT exemptions, and an increase in sin taxes. It is unlikely, however, that any new administration will choose to begin its term with the imposition of new taxes.

SM Prime - Mall performance surges in 4Q21
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Magnitude of mall improvement surprises

SM Prime’s 4Q21 net profit surged 71% YoY to P6.2bn, thanks to a 57% jump in Philippine mall revenue. While we expected an improvement in mall performance in 4Q21, the magnitude was a positive surprise. As a result, FY21 earnings rose 21% to P21.8bn, ahead of our forecast (in line with consensus).

4Q21 Philippine mall revenue already 53% of 4Q19 level despite concessions

Mobility restrictions were progressively loosened in 4Q21 as Covid-19 cases dropped, leading to a 64% QoQ jump in Philippine mall revenue (to P8.3bn). It is remarkable that 4Q21 Philippine mall revenue already reached 53% of the 4Q19 figure. As of December, fixed-rent tenants still received discounts of 30%- 50% while percentage-of-sales based tenants paid only c. 30% of pre-COVID minimum rent (on top of percentage-of-sales). Rental concessions are currently at levels similar to December 2021, but SM Prime plans to gradually reduce these throughout the year. Exceeding our FY22E Philippine mall revenue forecast is looking more likely now as our estimates only assume it will reach 60% of the 2019 level this year (from 42% in 2021).

4Q21 residential revenue rebounds, cancellations likely contained

Residential revenue rebounded 81% QoQ (+4% YoY) to a record P13.8bn. This suggests that the company is no longer plagued by the same level of cancellations as in 3Q21, when revenue fell 40% QoQ. Meanwhile, reservation sales rose 7% QoQ (-30% YoY) to P22.6bn. The company’s residential sales have been in the range of P21bn to P23bn over the past three quarters, from c. P32bn in 4Q20-1Q21. Nevertheless, FY21 reservation sales of P98.9bn is in line with our forecast.

 
Philippine Power - Flying too close to the sun
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Ambitious solar pipeline neglects near-term challenges

Amidst the mania, we examine the growth of renewable energy (RE) in recent years: the early movers and emerging players, the near-term pipeline and the likely implications on power supply. Power investments have now shifted towards solar farms, as investors and lenders shun coal or even gas, despite a dearth of baseload options. The government’s full backing of RE (and a ban on coal) has led to what appears to be an “overabundance” in the RE pipeline. However, we believe this abrupt transition and lack of planning will actually lead to potential shortages over the next 3-5 years.

Companies attempt to capitalize on the RE frenzy

Following the successful transformation of AC Energy (ACEN PM) over the past year, a barrage of companies now aim to mimic its trajectory. The promise of easy funding, generous valuations and strong investor interest have seen small independent companies all the way to the largest gencos express their intentions to build solar farms. The claimed pipeline has ballooned to well over 20,000MW of solar and wind projects from 2022-2030.

No oversupply yet, but dispatch among solar developers could be an issue

Government-backed enabling mechanisms are in place to encourage investment in this space. The Renewable Portfolio Standards (RPS), for instance, mandate DUs and other industry participants to achieve a 35% renewable energy mix by 2030. On paper, the said pipeline could boost the RE mix to over 30% by 2030, all else equal, indicating no oversupply per se, given the much lower availabilities of intermittent RE. However, constraints specific to solar (low capacity factor and peak availability) could lead to a situation where we have an oversupply among solar operators, but still be short on power overall.

Traditional gencos getting in on the action

Traditional operators who remain heavily invested in thermal have started to incorporate RE into their pipeline. Without efficient battery technology, RE for now makes the most sense within a portfolio of other technologies to remain flexible when fulfilling contracts. The announcements, however, could make investing in the traditional gencos more palatable again, after years of fossil[1]fuel-associated de-rating.

Multi-year positive for gencos; AP is our top pick

All told, the excitement surrounding solar and RE can do little to change the supply-demand situation in the medium term. We expect upward pressure on electricity prices to persist over the next few years. All gencos should benefit, but we reiterate our preference for Aboitiz Power (AP PM) due to the immediate impact of a substantial capacity addition and the highest exposure to RES contracts that reprice more frequently.

 
Philippine Consumer - Alternative Proteins: Reality vs Hype
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Reality vs hype

There has been much optimism about the long term growth prospects of the alternative protein industry with the growing trend of flexitarianism and environmental sustainability. While the runway for growth is long, it has also attracted many players globally from large FMCGs to food tech startups. Philippine-based companies Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) and Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM) have also forayed into the space with their respective brands Quorn and unMEAT. In recent quarters, however, growth in the US has plateaued because of intense competition and the wide price gap vs animal protein. We offer our key takeaways from a recent fireside chat with Jefferies’ US consumer analyst Rob Dickerson, who shed some light on the US alternative meat market, industry dynamics, and key growth drivers.

Early stages, long runway for growth

The global alternative protein market is expected to grow >30% annually over the next 5-10 years fueled by the increasing trend of flexitarianism and growing concern for environmental sustainability. It is a small category in the US (household penetration is estimated at <10%) and is still in its very early stages, leaving much room for growth, consolidation, and innovation. At this point, players are putting out as much product as they can across various formats (chilled, frozen, etc), using a wide range of raw materials (plant-based, labgrown meat, etc). Rising tide lifts all boats? The potential size of the market has attracted significant investment over the past few years, driving up competitive pressure. This has made it challenging for players to accelerate growth in a market where supply appears to be outpacing demand (for now). While retail distribution coverage is relatively wide (estimated at c60% of retail stores), the velocity (i.e. buying rate) of these products has decelerated. Because of this, household penetration has been stagnant over the past two years. The price gap of alternative meats vs animal meats is still relatively wide (20%), and the pricing premium has been magnified by recent inflationary pressures in the US. As the category is still in its infancy, the question is – will demand accelerate if prices go down? It is unclear at this point.

Foodservice: the next battleground

Foodservice is the next battleground for alternative meats in the US as retail shelf space is limited and already crowded. Securing contracts with foodservice chains could give players a competitive advantage by boxing out the competition (e.g. exclusivity for a certain menu item), and even accelerate growth on a global scale if they partner with a major global QSR chain.

AllDay 4Q21 foot traffic jumps 28% YoY

Foot traffic of the Villar Group’s AllDay Supermarket climbed 28% YoY in 4Q21. The 9M21 sales was up 22% while 9M21 SSSG was up 3%. Regis estimates that AllDay occupies c. 10% of Vista Land’s mall space

Megaworld to extend share buyback program

Megaworld’s share buyback program will be extended by 12 months to end-February 2023. Regis estimates that P3.8bn (out of the original P5bn allotted) is still unused and is equivalent to 3.7% of market cap. MEG is currently trading at 60% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 7.8X FY22E P/E.

PLDT to outdo rivals with 100-MW data hub, biggest in PH yet

PLDT is building a 100-megawatt data center estate in Laguna, marking its largest expansion to date given explosive demand for internet and cloud services. The series of facilities would rise on a five-hectare property in Sta. Rosa, Laguna. PLDT’s 100-MW project would exceed all data center capacity in the Philippines, which is currently estimated at 94MW. It would also dwarf the combined 48-MW capacity of ePLDT’s 10 existing data centers. Alibaba Cloud announced it would build its first data center in the Philippines. Singapore’s SpaceDC said it would put up a $700mn data center in Cainta, Rizal. Beeinfotech PH announced the opening of a $100mn data center in Pasig, Metro Manila.

 

PSE to return to 5-hour trading schedule beginning March 1

Beginning March 1, the PSE will return to a 5-hour trading schedule of 9AM to 3PM with a market recess from 12noon to 1PM.

Cebu Air's (CEB PM) domestic market share improves in FY21/4Q21

CEB domestic market share improves to 53% in 2021.  The airline regulator, Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB), released domestic passenger traffic data for 2021, which shows CEB’s market share rose to 53% from 51% in 2019.  Domestic pax volumes in 2021 reached 5.5mn or about 19% of pre-pandemic levels. 

 

This implies 4Q21 domestic market share was at 56%, the highest since 2017 and looks better than forecast.  In 4Q21 alone, Regis estimates domestic pax volumes for the whole industry grew 1.3x QoQ to 2.5mn or about 33% of pre-pandemic levels from 14% in 3Q21. CEB grew at a faster rate of 1.6x QoQ, suggesting that its domestic pax volumes were already close to 37% of pre-pandemic levels, better than our forecast of 32% for the quarter.  Regis estimates CEB’s market share improved to 56% in 4Q21, the highest level achieved since 2017.

 

RRHI acquires minority stake of Ministop Japan in CVS joint venture

RRHI has acquired the 40% stake of Ministop Japan in its CVS JV, increasing the former’s stake to 100%. The total consideration is P200mn cash for the 40% stake, which Regis estimates is equivalent to 0.5x P/B. RRHI will continue to operate the stores under the Ministop brand within a transition period; the new brand of the stores has yet to be disclosed. The transaction will have minimal impact to our 2022E EPS and SOTP NAV of P78/sh. CVS accounts for <4% of RRHI’s consolidated sales and is EBIT breakeven. RRHI’s 60% stake in Ministop accounts for 2% of our NAV.

Fitch affirms PH investment grade rating but outlook still negative

Fitch Ratings affirmed the Philippines' investment grade rating of ‘BBB’ with a negative outlook. The negative outlook could mean current rating could be downgraded. Fitch cited weak government revenue mobilization compared with peers and government debt-to-GDP ratio is forecast to stay close to the ‘BBB’ median over the next few years. According to the government’s own data, its debt-to-GDP stood at 60.5% in 2021 from 39.6% in 2019; Forecasts for 2022/2023 are 60.8%/60.7%. Fitch is forecasting general debt-to-GDP ratio to reach 54.5% in 2022 and 53.1% in 2023 from an estimated 54% in 2021 and 48% in 2020. Fitch forecasting Philippine GDP to grow by 6.9% in 2022 and 7% in 2023.

Finance Secretary to recommend new taxes to service debts

Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III is ready to brief Presidential candidates and their economic teams a fiscal consolidation package.

Among the new taxes being eyed are:

Carbon tax

Levy on cryptocurrencies

Removal of all exemptions from 12% VAT

Further hikes in excise taxes on cigarettes, e-cigarettes, alcoholic drinks, and sugary beverages. No other details disclosed

Balance of payments reverts to deficit in January

Based on preliminary data, the country’s balance of payments (BOP) position stood at a US$102mn deficit in January 2022. This is a reversal of the surpluses in the past three months but lower than the US$752mn deficit in January 2021. The government cited payment of maturing national government foreign debt. For 2022, the BSP expects a smaller BOP surplus of US$700mn from a US$1.35bn surplus in 2021.

BSP has room to keep policy rates low

BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said there is room to maneuver on monetary policy amid manageable inflation and the improving economy. He wants to see 4-6 quarters of economic growth before looking into a possible rate hike. The economy has improved in the last three quarters. While the BSP has maintained policy rates low, it has started unwinding support for the National Government. The BSP’s participation in government securities market is now down to 0.25% of total volumes, from 4% in 2021 and 22% in 2020. Governor Diokno is also not worried about the PHP’s weakness against the USD and is looking at the PHP settling between P48-53 until 2024.

Metro Manila mayors lobby for Alert Level 1

The Metro Manila council voted to ease restrictions in the region to the most relaxed level of Alert 1 amid decreasing COVID infections. The council will recommend to the IATF, who will make the final decision this weekend. Metro Manila is currently under Alert Level 2 until Feb 28. Under Alert Level 1, businesses and schools can operate at full capacity (vs. c. 50-70% under Alert Level 2). Daily cases in NCR (7dma) is down to 344 (from 520 the previous week) vs 1400 for the rest of the country (vs 2700 the previous week). The hospital utilization rates are also low (<30%).
