REGIS REPORT

04 February 2022

The PSEi surged 2.8% this week,  gaining three out of four trading sessions. Falling covid cases boosted sentiment. The government also lowered the Covid-19 Alert level for Metro Manila (NCR) and seven other provinces to Level Two, starting Tuesday this week. It likewise announced a relaxation of rules for inbound travelers.

Reopening optimism took the market higher, even as foreigners turned net buyers for US$25m this week. Biggest inflows were seen in cyclical sectors banks and property.

This week’s gain was broadbased, with 20 of the 30 index members closing the week with gains.

The top movers were Ayala Land (+8.5% WoW), SM Investments (+4.0% WoW), and SM Prime (+5.7% WoW).  The three names contributed 125 index points to the PSEi’s 205-point ascent this week.

Security Bank (+8.9% WoW) gained significantly, as did power names (MER +6.8% WoW, AP +6.5% WoW). Property was also strong, with RLC (+6.5% WoW) and Megaworld (+5.2% WoW) also gaining apart from their larger cap counterparts.

Other banks were up as well.  Metrobank (+3.3% WoW) and BDO (+1.9% WoW) advanced. BPI (+0.7% WoW), which kicked off the 4Q21 reporting season, also gained. FY2021 profits were in line with consensus. Of note, loan growth accelerated to 4.9% YoY and asset quality improved.

ICT (+2.3% WoW) gained.  Container volumes at the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) were up 14% YoY the last quarter of 2021. MICT accounts for 30% of ICT’s EBITDA.  This would take full year FY21 volume growth to +21%, in line with Regis estimates and just above pre-pandemic (FY19) levels.

AC Energy (+0.3% WoW) announced it is buying a 49% stake in the Vietnam solar platform of Thai-listed Super Energy Corp Plc for US$165m. The purchase price for the Vietnam solar plants implies a valuation of roughly US$800-900k/MW. The deal will raise ACEN’s total attributable capacity to a little over 4,700MW. Vietnam will now account for ACEN’s largest exposure outside the Philippines at 21% of capacity.

DITO (+3.9% WoW) the controlling shareholder (54%) of third player Dito Telecommunity (Dito Tel), announced it was “deferring” its P8bn rights issue days after the offer period already lapsed. Management blamed the “less than ideal market conditions” for the deferment. This is despite the fact the deal supposedly had a “firm” underwriting commitment from its sole underwriter  and an undertaking from its main shareholder to buy all shares left unsubscribed in the rights issue.  While China Telecom could easily fund more equity at the operating company level,  they are hampered by the current law that limits them to 40% max.  

Meanwhile, Converge (-8.05% WoW) continued to correct, while Wilcon (-2.2% WoW), SMC (-1.9% WoW), and Globe (-1.85% WoW) also declined.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ACEN to buy 49% stake in Vietnam solar energy platform

A unit of AC Energy Corp (ACEN PM) is acquiring a 49% interest in the Vietnamese solar energy platform of Thailand’s Super Energy Corp Plc. Co. Ltd to form a strategic partnership to develop, own and operate renewable energy projects across ASEAN. The share purchase agreement to acquire 49% interest in Solar NT is for US$165mn. Post-restructuring, Solar NT will own and operate nine solar plants across Vietnam with a total capacity of 837MW. The latest investment will expand its international portfolio to more than 1,900MW, of which more than 1,000 are in Vietnam. ACEN has more than 4,300MW of attributable capacity in the Philippines, Vietnam, Indonesia, India and Australia.

Another Macau junket boss arrested

Tak Chun chairman Levo Chan Weng Lin is suspected of being a leader in a triad criminal group and has been detained since Friday. Macau’s Public Prosecutions Office stated in its note that Mr. Chan’s arrest sprung from further investigation linked to the detention in November of Suncity’s Alvin Chau. Mr. Chan is suspected of being a triad leader, money laundering, illegal running of gambling operations and illegal running of gambling operations in a casino. Tak Chun is widely considered to be the 2nd largest junket operator in Macau after Suncity Group. Mr. Chan is also the chief executive of HK-listed Macau Legend, operator of three casinos under a service agreement with SJM Holdings and owner of the Fishermans Wharf.

BPI FY21 NPAT P23.9bn, +12% YoY; loans accelerate to +4.9%, NPLs drop to 2.49%

In a press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported FY21 NPAT of P23.88bn , up 12% and in line with consensus. The results were also in line with our numbers on a PPOP basis. Loan growth accelerated from +0.3% in 3Q21 to +4.9% at year-end. Regis estimates fee incomes surged in 4Q21, up 52% YoY, and up 29% QoQ from an admittedly soft 3Q21. Asset quality also improved, with NPL ratio down to 2.49% and NPL cover up to 136.1%. All in all, a pretty strong set of results.

 

Some highlights:

Loan growth accelerated to 4.9% YoY, from just +0.3% in 3Q21. The press release said the growth was driven by mortgages, credit card, and microfinance loans. Regis estimates that loans were up 6.5% QoQ. Deposits were up 13.9% YoY; TDs jumped 28.2% as customers elected for high(er) yield deposits after BPI’s bonds matured in 3Q21. CASA ratio, though, fell to 77% from a peak of 83% in 2Q21.

Regis estimate NIMs were flat to slightly higher QoQ in 4Q21. NII was up 3% YoY and 6% QoQ.

 

Fee income was up 23% for FY21. BPI says most business lines are already above 2019 levels. Fees rebounded sharply in 4Q21 from a soft 3Q. Total non-interest income, though, was tempered by a sharp 63% YoY drop in trading/forex gains, as interest rates increased.

 

Provision expense dropped 53% YoY for the full year, as asset quality improved considerably. NPL ratio was reported at 2.49%, down 24bps from the 2.73% reported in 3Q21. Meanwhile, NPL coverage improved slightly to 136.1% from 130.7% the previous quarter.

 

The accelerating loan growth and improving NPLs augur well for BPI in 2022E. Recently management has been guiding for a sharp drop in provisions. This will allow for a c20% increase in opex, which Regis estimates to be driven largely by a near-doubling of IT spend. The bank aims to accelerate its digitalization strategy, with a view to double or triple its customer count from the current c8.5mn over the near term.

Dito CME “defers” stock rights issue

Dito CME (DITO PM) announced it was deferring its P8bn stock rights issue the subscription period already lapsed. This is despite the rights issue supposedly having received a firm underwriting commitment from its underwriter. DITO’s main shareholder Udenna also supposedly issued an undertaking that it would subscribe to all rights shares not taken up by minorities. DITO’s main shareholder Udenna previously said it was not participating, ostensibly to give minority shareholders an opportunity to raise their stake. Management blamed the “less than ideal market conditions” for what it describes as a “deferment”. The company could face sanctions on its decision to cancel this exercise. DITO’s main assets is a 54% indirect stake in Dito Telecommunity, which is a new mobile operator 40% owned by China Telecom. Udenna’s apparent unwillingness or inability to fund this rights offer could be interpreted as a warning sign about Dito Telecommunity. Without foreign ownership restrictions being lifted or circumvented, it appears Dito Tel cannot raise funding from its majority shareholder.

Federal Land partners with Japan-based Nomura Real Estate

Federal Land forged a strategic partnership with Nomura Real Estate Dev’t Co. Ltd. The JV will start in April 2022. Nomura will be investing US$324mn (P16bn), representing 34% of the total capital investment of the new company of P48bn. Regis understands Federal Land will be infusing cash and land for its 66% share. The initial project will incorporate four areas of land development with a total area of 250 hectares in Metro Manila, Cavite, and Cebu. The initial pipeline will be for residential, office, commercial, and industrial facilities. Federal Land is a wholly-owned unit of GTCAP and accounts for c. 15% of 2022E profit.

MICT TEU volumes up by 14% YoY, 4% QoQ in 4Q21

Data from the Philippine Ports Authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s MICT port rose 14% YoY and 4% QoQ in 4Q21. This brings FY21 volumes up 20% YoY to 2.1mn TEUs, exceeding 2019 by 2% and is in line with our estimates for the port. However, overall volumes from the two foreign container terminal operators in Manila rose by just +7% YoY in 4Q21 and +14% FY21. FY21 combined volumes of the two foreign port operators were 4% below pre-Covid (4Q21 alone was just 1% below 2019). Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbor saw its volumes drop 8% YoY in 4Q21 alone, bringing its FY21 volumes up by +3% YoY and 18% below 2019 levels. Regis believes MICT will sustain market share gains, given its competitor’s capacity limitations and estimates MICT accounts for c. 20% of group-wide volumes, 30% of revenues/EBITDA, and 52% of NAV. ICTSI is trading at 9.5x FY22E EV/EBITDA, a discount to historical valuations (11x).

More domestic destinations ease travel requirements

Cebu Pacific said 18 domestic destinations re-opened to leisure travelers. Travelers with vaccine cards will no longer require COVID tests. Following the surge in COVID cases in early January, local governments restricted leisure travel. In December, 30 domestic destinations were open to leisure travelers. CEB is currently flying to 35 domestic destinations (vs 37 pre-Covid). Based on Jefferies’ latest airline capacity survey, CEB flew at 31% of pre-Covid seats in the week of January 24. This is down from 44% in December but better than January 2021, which was at 17% of pre-Covid.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Metro Manila, 7 other provinces to be under Alert Level 2 starting Feb. 1

Metro Manila and 7 other provinces will be placed under Alert Level 2 from Feb 1 to 15. The provinces include Batanes, Bulacan, Cavite, Rizal, Biliran, Southern Leyte, and Basilan.

‘Hot money’ continued to exit in 2021

Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. hot money) saw a net outflow of US$574.46mn in 2021. This is the third straight year of net outflows but the 2021 outflow was significantly lower than the US$4.24bn outflow in 2020. In December 2021 alone, hot money posted a US$4.38mn net outflow. This is lower than the US$524mn outflow in December 2020 but was a reversal from the US$110mn net inflow in November 2021.

U/KB loans jump 4.6% YoY, 2.6% MoM in December; Domestic liquidity expands 7.7%

Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase placements with the BSP, grew at a faster rate of 4.6% YoY in December. Growth accelerating from 4.0% in November and improving by 2.6% MoM. Lending to real estate activities (+9%), ICT (+27%), manufacturing (+9%), finance (+10%), and transport (+9%) accelerated. Consumer loans declined at a softer rate of -6% (from -7% in November), with credit card loans up +4.7% (+2.6% previously) and auto loans flattening. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 7.7% in December to P15.3tn, slowing from the +8.3% expansion in November.

Pace of vaccinations falls further

As of January 30, the 7-day average vaccinations figure fell to 443,000/day from 632,000/day the previous week. Around 54% of the Philippine population (58.8mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 217mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 126mn doses (as of January 30). The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22.

Customs beats January collection target

The Bureau of Customs collected P58.16bn in January, up 23% YoY and beating its target by 12%. The agency is tasked to collect P679bn this year, up from 2021 collections of P646bn (+5% YoY).

Law on registration of SIM cards and social media also ratified

Congress also approved a new law requiring the registration of all SIM cards. All public telecommunications entities must require registration prior to activation and sale of SIM cards. Additionally, existing SIM card subscribers will have 180 days to register with the telcos. According to a media report, the law also requires an individual’s real name and phone number upon the creation of a social media account. Troll accounts spreading fake news have been employed effectively by a number politicians in order to influence elections and public opinion.

 

Congress approves amendments to Public Service Act, with limits on foreign state-owned enterprises’

Congress ratified a reconciled version of the Public Services Act, effectively lifting the 40% foreign ownership limits on a number of industries. Among the industries that are no longer considered “public utilities” : telecoms, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports. These are thus no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit. Those still considered public utilities are: distribution and transmission of electricity; petroleum pipeline transmission systems; water distribution systems; seaports and public utility vehicles. Additionally, foreign state-owned entities will not be allowed to own more than 50% of companies that own “:critical infrastructure” unless there is reciprocity. This will be a constraint on China Telecom taking over Dito Telecommunity fully.

Bill protecting financial consumers now awaits President’s signature

The Senate passed on third and final reading Senate Bill No. 2488 or the proposed Financial Consumers Protection Act (FCPA). The House of Representatives also adopted the Senate’s version of the bill. The measure will strengthen regulators’ ability to enforce sanctions on erring entities. Additionally, the measure includes a provision allowing regulators to order refunds to victim. BSP Governor Diokno cited that more than half of the 23,275 complaints received by the BSP in 2020 were resolved in favor of consumers, while a third of complaints were acted upon by concerned financial institutions.

Senators delay decision on RCEP

The Senate adjourned sessions for the election break, without giving its concurrence to the ratification of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP). RCEP is a trade deal bet. Australia, China, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand and 10 members of ASEAN. The RCEP was signed by member nations in November 2020 and has been in force in 11 countries since Jan 1. Senators cited they want all voices to be heard before they concur the ratification. President Duterte ratified RCEP on Sept 2. The Senate will resume its session on May 23. The Department of Trade and Industry estimates RCEP will drive GDP expansion above 6%, FDI growth to 49%, and export growth up to 15%. The RCEP has largely been opposed by agricultural groups.
