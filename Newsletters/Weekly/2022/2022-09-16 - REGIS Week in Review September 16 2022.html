REGIS REPORT

16 September 2022

This market extended its losing streak to 4 weeks, following a 0.9% drop to close at 6548.77. The Phils was soft alongside regional and global markets with expectations of another Fed hike after the August CPI print.

Foreigners ended net buyers this week by US$19.7m. This week's inflows were driven primarily by San Miguel crosses. Exclude San Miguel, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$9.1m. Biggest outflows were seen in FTSE rebal names SMPH (-1.8% WoW)  and MONDE (-13.8% WoW) while inflows were in SMC (-3.55% WoW), EMI (-1.0% WoW), and SM (+0.7% WoW). The FTSE rebal on Friday saw large activity in downweight SM Prime.  EMI was an addition while Monde saw an upweight. Both stocks fell on Friday, however.

Consumer stocks led the laggards, with Monde, Puregold (-6.8% WoW), and Universal Robina (-5.6% MoM) the top index decliners this week.  A new junk food tax and additional taxes on sugary beverages being pushed by the Health Department could be negative for consumer companies. The “junk food” tax could be difficult to pass, however.

BDO (-4.4% WoW), AGI (-3.7% WoW), and SMC (-3.55% WoW) also declined.  Index losers outnumbered gainers 3:2.

On the other hand, telcos were strong. PLDT (+5.8% WoW) topped the PSEi gainers, flowed by Globe (+3.0% WoW), whose shares went ex-rights today. Ayala Land (+2.7% WoW), Ayala Corp (+2.0% WoW) and Jollibee (+1.6% WoW) also gained.

The country’s trade deficit hit a new all-time high in July, expanding 75% YoY to $5.9bn. This took the 7M22 figure to $35.7bn (+67% YoY). The continued deterioration in the trade data should continue to put pressure on the PHP, which dropped another 1% this week to PHP57.415/US$.

Fernando Zobel de Ayala resigns from all positions in Ayala Group

Fernando Zobel de Ayala officially resigned from his various positions in the Ayala Group in order to focus on his health.

The positions were:

Ayala Corp – Vice Chairman, President and CEO

Ayala Land – Chairman

BPI – Vice Chairman

Globe – Co-Vice Chairman

ACEN – Chairman

Integrated Micro Electronics – Director

Mr. Zobel de Ayala took a leave of absence from his various positions beginning August 15. Cezar Consing has been acting President and CEO of Ayala Corp since August 15.

 

Metro Pacific Tollway plans US$500mn IPO in 2023/2024

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp (MPTC) could raise US$500mn from a planned IPO, according to Reuter’s sources. MPTC’s President and CEO Rodrigo Franco declined to comment on the financial details but said the aim was to list the company in late 2023 or 2024. MPTC is a 99.9%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and accounts for c. 25% of gross asset value/profit. MPI is trading at a 45% discount to its market-based NAV of P7/sh.

Manila Water signs P15bn loan for capex projects

Manila Water signed a P15bn loan facility with a 10-year term to fund its upcoming projects in the East zone. The facility was signed with China Banking Corp. As at end-June, MWC’s gross debt stood at P76bn and cash at P8.6bn, with net debt-to-equity at 0.96x.

Toyota sales jump 88% YoY, 6% MoM in August

Toyota Motor Philippines sold 14,720 units in August, up 88% YoY, 6% MoM. Its market share dipped slightly to 48.8% in August from 50.1% in July. The YT-August sales reached 108,746 units, +32% YoY. Sales are tracking well ahead of company guidance (162k units, +25% YoY) and Regis estimates (158k units, +22% YoY). Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c.20% of NAV and profits. GTCAP is trading at a 44% discount to its market-based NAV of P914/sh.

Consistent application sought for key ERC ruling

ACEN Corp has joined participants in the energy sector that have called on the regulator to decide on a joint plea by the country’s dominant power distributor and energy supplier to hike their previously approved electricity rate to reflect unforeseen events. The call came days after the ERC heard the joint petitions filed by Meralco and a unit of San Miguel Corp. “Whatever the decision will be, we hope that our regulator as well as Meralco, will treat all suppliers fairly and consistently,” ACEN President and CEO Eric Francia said in a statement. “We have the same contract and similar set of circumstances, so if there is a change in circumstance in one supplier, the same should apply to others”. Last week, Meralco presented various scenarios to ERC arguing to allow San Miguel its change in circumstance in claim. This would result in an increase of P1.57/kWh vs its original rate over a six month period. If the CIC claims are approved, the rate impact to Meralco’s generation charge is P0.28-0.29/kWh against the generation charge of around P10/kWh.

GCash: Gross transaction value surpassed P3trn in 1H

GCash said that its gross transaction value surpassed P3trn in 1H22, nearly matching the P3.8trn it posted in FY21. The mobile wallet is expected to end 2022 with a P6trn gross transaction value. GCash said its user base has expanded to 66mn. Merchant and social seller base also reached 5.2mn. Cash-in and cash-out outlets grew to 339,000, up 5.7x from last year.

Keepers Holdings (KEEPR PM) completes acquisition of 50% stake in Alfonso toll manufacturer

KEEPR disclosed that it completed the acquisition of a 50% stake in Bodegas Williams & Humbert SA (W&H) for a total cash consideration of EUR88.75mn (approx. P5bn), which implies ~15x 12m trailing P/E. W&H is the toll manufacturer for KEEPR’s Alfonso brandy, which accounts for c60% of the latter’s revenues. W&H also produces a variety of alcoholic beverages, including brandy, sherry wine, vermouth, and gin. Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) holds a 78% stake in KEEPR. COSCO currently trades at a 65% discount to our market-based NAV estimate of P12.45/sh.

 
 
Macro News
 
 
 
 

Entire Philippine sugar output now for domestic use

The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) issued Sugar Order No. 1, requiring 100% of sugar production in the coming crop year to be classified as “B” or domestic sugar supply. The crop year is from 1 September 2022 to 31 August 2023. The SRA said that total sugar production in the crop year is estimated at 1.88mn MT, while demand is expected to reach 2.03mn MT. As of September 2, the average retail price of refined sugar in wet markets was P97.43/kg, (+85%) vs P52.71/kg a year ago. Raw sugar in wet markets is now priced at P72.43/kg (+60% YoY).

Concerns remain over draft rules for BOT Law

The government plans to remove some provisions under the draft IRR of the Build-Operate-Transfer Law according to Public-Private Partnership Center Deputy Executive Director Jeffrey Manalo. Provision on “acts and decisions of regulators shall not be subject to arbitration” will likely be removed, as this absolves the government of liability. There were also concerns on “availability payments” and “viability gap funding”, as these can be construed as direct government subsidies. The Marcos administration is looking to attract more investments in infrastructure through PPPs amid the fiscal challenges it is facing.

Gov’t raises P45bn from 10-year treasury bonds

The government raised a total of P45bn from an original issue of 10-year T-bonds. T-bonds carry a coupon of 6.75% but fetched a yield of 6.703% (9.4bps higher than the previous auction; BVAL: 6.6%). The Bureau of the Treasury fully awarded P35bn through a regular auction and, subsequently, offered an additional P10bn through a tap facility. Investors tendered almost 3x the volume offered. The street expects the Fed to raise policy rates by another 50bps on its Sep 21 meeting; BSP’s Monetary Board also expected to raise 50bps on Sep 22.

Brownouts hit Luzon as 7 power plants shut down

Power interruptions were experienced in Nueva Ecija, Aurora, Pampanga and Zambales after the National Grid of the Philippines (NGCP) placed the Luzon grid under red alert on Monday afternoon. Heightening the yellow alert status declared in the morning. The yellow alert is issued when the excess power is insufficient to meet the transmission grid’s regulating and contingency requirements. Meanwhile, red alert is placed under a grid area when the electricity supply is insufficient to meet demand. As of 10am Monday, the Luzon grid’s available capacity stood at 10,727MW while peak demand was 10,585MW. At least 3,627MW were lost from the grid after seven power plants went on forced outage. These include units of GN Power, Calaca, Masinloc, Sual, Quezon Power and Avion. WESM has been averaging >P9/kWh in recent days.

FDI net inflows slip to 13-month low in June

FDI net inflows dropped 52% YoY to US$471mn in June. This is the lowest monthly FDI net inflow since the US$455mn seen in May 2021. MoM, FDI net inflows dropped 36.5% from US$742mn in May and cited higher payments of debt instruments, offsetting net investments in equity capital and reinvestment earnings. The 1H22 net FDI inflows are up by 3.1% YoY to US$4.641bn. The BSP is targeting net FDI inflows to reach US$11bn this year, lower than the US$12.41bn in 2021. The government is projecting the balance of payments to be in a US$6.3bn deficit by year-end (1.5% of GDP). Due to a projected wider current account deficit of US$19.1bn (4.6% of GDP) vs. net financial account inflow of only US$11.8bn.

 

Gov’t partially awards T-bills as rates rise

The Bureau of Treasury raised only P5bn from the 182-day T-bills it auctioned recently. This is lower than its planned P15bn program, even as bids reached P19.481bn. The 182-day T-bill fetched a rate of 3.634% (14.9bps higher from the previous auction; BVAL: 3.3671%). The government rejected all bids for the 91-day and 364-day T-bills. Had it been awarded, rates for the 91-day T-bill would have risen by 103.1 bps to 3.349% (vs. BVAL: 2.469%). Had it been awarded, rates for the 364-day T-bill would have risen by 61bps to 4.392% (vs BVAL: 3.9969%). Markets are on wait-and-see mode ahead of the release of US consumer price index (CPI) data for August but remain cautious, with continuous hawkish Fed statements according to National Treasurer Rosalia V. de Leon.

DOH wants additional taxes on junk food, sweetened beverages

The Department of Health (DOH) is pushing for additional taxes on junk food and sweetened beverages to address obesity and boost revenues for the universal healthcare program. The DOH has yet to provide a specific proposal on taxes but Vergeire said the additional revenue will be used to finance the Universal Healthcare which enrolls all Filipino citizens under the National Health Insurance Program. The TRAIN Law mandates a P6/liter excise tax on beverages using caloric and non-caloric sweeteners and a P12/liter tax on beverages using high fructose corn syrup.

FIRB to decide on BPO work-from-home policy on September 15

The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) maintained the status regarding work-from-home policies of BPO firms (enjoying PEZA incentives) until its next Board meeting (on September 15). PEZA allowed 30% of BPO employees to work from home until March 2023. Previously, the 30% cap would lapse on September 12, after which only 10% of employees would be allowed to work from home. Regis expects only minor impact from this extension (or non-extension). For the most part, Regis believes BPO firms have decided if they will expand office footprint or if they will no longer avail of PEZA incentives. Most BPO firms are expanding via Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have a requirement regarding WFH policies.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) comprise:

55% of FLI’s NAV

41% of MEG’s NAV

30% of RLC’s NAV

Trade deficit wider in July, as exports slump

The country’s trade deficit widened to US$5.93bn in July. Higher than the revised trade deficit of US$5.87bn in June 2022 and US$3.51bn deficit in July 2021. Imports rose at a slower rate of 22% YoY in July (vs. 26% in June), likely largely reflecting sequential drops in commodity prices (e.g. oil). Imports of consumer goods remained strong, up 26% YoY. Meanwhile, capital goods imports still posting lackluster recovery, up by just 7% YoY while exports dropped by 4% YoY, with electronic exports down 8% YoY. The YT-July trade deficit is now at US$35.75bn, up 67% YoY. Additionally, the FY21 trade deficit revised down slightly to US$42.2bn (vs. US$43.1bn previously), equivalent to 11% of GDP.

Remittances rise to 7-month high in July

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.3% YoY, 5.9% MoM to US$2.92bn in July which is the highest level so far this year. The three-month average growth in remittances stood at +2.8% in July, slightly slower than in June (+3.4%). In PHP terms, remittances rose by 14% YoY in July. Land-based remittances rose by 6% MoM, driven by USA (+8%), Singapore (+27%), Japan (+8%), Saudi Arabia (+31%), and UAE (+15%). Remittances from Europe sustained softness, with UK down -30% MoM. Sea-based remittances also improved by 7% MoM. The YT-July remittances are up by 2.8% YoY to US$18.3bn. To reach the government’s FY22E target of +4% YoY, remittances would have to average US$2.88bn per month for the rest of the year.

BPO firms allowed to work from home

The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) allowed PEZA-registered firms to transfer their registration to the Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have restrictions on work-from-home. The BPO firms under PEZA were also permitted to implement 30% WFH policies until December. This development is positive for the BPO industry but will likely cause a larger proportion of BPO employees to work-from-home, potentially reducing office demand further.

41% of MEG’s NAV
