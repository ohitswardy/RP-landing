REGIS REPORT

12 August 2022

The PSEi took its winning streak to a fourth week, rising 4.6% this week to close at 6699.66.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$201m this week after a large foreign seller was seen unloading Ayala Land  to parent Ayala Corp. If these ALI blocks were excluded, foreigners would have been net buyers for US$9.5m. Biggest inflows were seen in Ayala Corp, Jollibee, SM Prime and Ayala Land (outside the large blocks).

GDP expanded 7.4% YoY in 2Q22, down from the 8.2% pace in 1Q22.  Oddly, personal Consumption not only decelerated (8.6% in 2Q vs 10.0% in 1Q), but also contracted 2.1% QoQ. This was at odds with high frequency industry data such as loan growth, electricity sales, and loan growth which showed strong YoY and QoQ growth. 1H22 GDP growth of 7.8% was solid, nonetheless. 

Meanwhile, the inflation print in July was at 6.4% -- an acceleration for 6.1% the previous month.  With solid GDP numbers and the latest CPI figure, it is looking likely the BSP is raising rates on the next policy meeting on Aug 18.

Gains were broad-based; 27 of the 30 index stocks closed up for the week. Top market movers were large caps SM (+8.1% WoW), Ayala Land (+10.0% WoW), and SM Prime (+4.8% WoW). These names contributed 55% or 162 index points to the PSEI’s overall 294-index point advance for the week.

Globe (+11.4% WoW) led the advancers this week, advancing the sale of its 7000 towers (over half of its 12k total towers). The initial 5,700 towers will be sold for P71bn, which would imply ~P12.4m per tower, only slightly lower than PLDT’s tower deal (P13m per tower). The sale is estimated to reduce net gearing to 1.2x from the >2x today.

Another incumbent, the PLDT (+6.3% WoW) group, made an announcement. The group entered into a series of transactions that would effectively give it ownership of Sky Cable in exchange for half of TV5. PLDT’s Cignal (PLDT owns 64% of economic rights) will initially be acquiring 39% of Sky Cable (for P2.862bn)  with another 61% eventually through an Exchangeable instrument. The Lopez group, through ABS-CBN (+30.6% WoW), will then take a 35% interest in TV5 (for P2.16bn), which could eventually rise to just below 50%. The transactions would add value to PLDT’s pay TV business and increase broadband subscriber count.  Meanwhile,  ABS-CBN will finally be able to air and monetize its content again, after the previous administration denied its franchise extension.

Converge (-4.1%  WoW) fell, with its 2Q performance falling behind street forecasts despite strong 42% EBITDA growth (36% revenue growth).  Management  guided for revenue growth of 25-30% for the year, down from 50% previously. Net adds guidance for the year was also tempered to ~300k from 700k previously.

Other top gainers were GT Capital (+10.9% WoW), Wilcon (+10.7% WoW).

Robinsons Land (+10.6% WoW) also led, as a good set of 2Q results propelled the stock.  The estimate-beating results were helped by China revenue and a bounce in mall revenue, and  lower residential cancelations. Megaworld (+8.4% WoW) came in ahead as margins expanded contrary to expectations and it appears there were no meaningful sales cancellations as residential revenue for the quarter rose YoY and QoQ. Ayala Land (+10.0% WoW), however,  appeared slightly behind forecasts.  2Q22 residential revenue fell 12% YoY due to cancellations from the middle-income and affordable brands.

Jollibee (+8.0% WoW) also gained significantly after a strong 2Q performance, with operating income doubling YoY.  Same-store sales grew across all geographies (ex-China), led by the Philippines, which saw a 52% jump in SSS as mobility (and dine-in restrictions) were lifted. The rest of the consumer sector was mixed, with Monde (+3.5% WoW) and PIZZA’s (+13.6% WoW) 2Q falling behind expectations on margins, Emperador (+0.2% WoW) falling within estimates and DNL (+2.9% WoW) coming in ahead.

The reopening/recovery theme was also in play with Bloomberry (+12.6% WoW), which reported that 2Q GGRs are back to nearly pre-pandemic levels even without Chinese VIPs flying in to the country. Meanwhile, Cebu Air’s (+6.5% WoW) 2Q EBITDA however turned positive for the first time since the start of the pandemic on a strong rebound in revs tempered by high fuel costs. DMC’s (+6.2% WoW) 1H profit surged 142%, as 2Q22 profit alone more than doubled. Apart from robust numbers from SCC (+0.1% WoW), almost all key segments posted strong recoveries.

Ayala Land - 2Q22: residential revenue disappoints due to cancellations
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Focus on residential cancellations

2Q22 net profit jumped 51% YoY (+55% QoQ) to P4.9bn as the economic reopening boosted rental revenue and expanded margins. While 1H22 earnings of P8.1bn (+34%) only appear slightly behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, we believe investors should focus on the drop in residential revenue brought about by sales cancellations.

Cancellations hit middle-income and affordable brands; likely to remain high

2Q22 residential revenue fell 12% YoY as cancellations hit the middle-income (Avida) and affordable (Amaia) brands. We note, however, that cancellations in 2Q22 were lower than in 1Q22, which allowed residential revenue to rise 12% QoQ. Moreover, commercial lot sales soared 164% YoY (+42% QoQ). (See Figure 2) While management did not cite specific numbers, it expects 2022 residential cancellations to be 10-12% higher than 2019 levels. This likely implies that defaults will remain high for the remainder of the year.

Reservation sales grow on OFWs, foreigners

2Q22 reservation sales grew 28% YoY (+5% QoQ) thanks to large jump in sales to OFWs and foreigners (mostly Americans). As a result, they comprised 22% and 15% of residential sales respectively (see Figure 3). Management said that OFWs mostly purchased Avida units while Alveo was popular with foreigners. While reservation sales only inched up 2% in 1H22, they could pick-up later this year as the company launches more horizontal projects, which have generally sold more briskly in recent years.

Mall and hotel revenues surge on economic reopening

The mall and hotel segments clearly benefitted from the economic reopening. Mall tenant sales reached 82% of pre-COVID levels (from 61% in 1Q22, 43% in 2Q21). This helped revenue jump 168% YoY (+34% QoQ) to P3.9bn, already 75% of the 2Q19 figure. Mall revenue should improve further since Ayala Land removed all concessions to its mall tenants in July. Meanwhile, hotel revenue surged 160% YoY on strong domestic tourism.

Office vacancy improves

Office vacancy declined from 19% in 1Q22 to 16% in 2Q22 as BPO and traditional tenants took up space. BPO demand likely increased due to the government mandate that requires at least 70% of BPO employees to work from the office (beginning April 1).

 
Monde Nissin - Cost inflation, competition weigh on 2Q margins
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H22 NPAT -4% YoY as cost inflation, competition weigh on 2Q performance

MONDE’s 1H22 NPAT came in at P4.1bn, down 4% YoY. The figure is tracking behind Regis estimates but slightly ahead of consensus. Competition and cost inflation weighed on margins, leading to the profit underperformance. 2Q22 NPAT rose +9% YoY mainly because of a lower effective tax rate and interest expense. EBITDA was practically flat YoY as margin pressure persisted. 1H22 revenues (+10% YoY to P37.2bn) are tracking broadly within expectations, as 2Q22 revenues (+13% YoY to P18.9bn) were boosted by price increases across both segments. However, management is guiding for a softer 3Q following the impact of the recent product recall in the EU on domestic instant noodle sales.

APAC BFB: Price adjustments boost top line, but underlying trends soft

Segment sales grew 18% YoY, boosted by the +10% average price increase across the categories. Volume growth was relatively healthy at +7%, but MONDE lost market share QoQ in major categories which collectively account for ~80%of segment sales: instant noodles (-130bps QoQ to 66.6%) and biscuits (- 100bps QoQ to 28.6%). The company admitted that it was facing competitive pressure in both categories as consumers become more price conscious amidst rising inflation.

Quorn: Competition pressures 2Q performance

Quorn revenue declined 4% YoY in 2Q, even after price increases. On the one hand, Quorn gained market share during the quarter (~300bps YoY), as volumes (-8% YoY) did not contract as much as the rest of the category (-13% YoY). The price adjustments were not enough to mitigate cost pressures, leading to a sharp contraction (>500bps YoY / >200bps QoQ) in gross margin. Notably, EBITDA margin was largely flat QoQ, implying that opex has stabilized (at least for now).

Pointing to a softer 3Q on EU recall, cost inflation

Management is pointing to a softer 3Q as domestic instant noodle sales declined 15% YoY in July following news of the product recall in the EU. At the same time, they are hoping to take back lost market share in noodles and biscuits through new product launches, which started in 2Q and will continue throughout the quarter. While prices of key inputs (wheat, plam oil) have peaked, the company locked in prices closer to the 2Q highs for the balance of the year, which suggests margin relief will not likely come through until early-2023.

 
Shakey’s Pizza - 2Q22 margins lag but there’s room to catch up in 2H
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Profit turnaround in 2Q22; margins lag but momentum is strong

PIZZA reported 2Q22 net profit of P174mn, a turnaround from the P15mn net loss in the previous year. This brings the cumulative 1H22 figure to P250mn (vs near breakeven in 1H21), which appears to be tracking behind our FY22 estimates. We believe, however, that there is room for margins and profitability to catch up in 2H22 as SSSG could be further boosted by (1) the resumption of onsite classes and (2) the seasonally strong 4Q.

SSSG accelerates in 2Q as dine-in restrictions are lifted

SSSG jumped to 41% in 2Q22 as dine-in restrictions were lifted. We estimate on[1]premise channels contributed ~55% of sales during the quarter, inching closer to its pre-pandemic share of 60%. Notably, off-premise channels continued to grow QoQ (around mid-single digit) even as mobility restrictions eased. On a same-store basis, we believe sales are already tracking very close (>90%) to pre-pandemic levels.

Operating leverage overshadows cost inflation

EBITDA surged over 2.5x YoY in 2Q lifted by operating leverage from the SSSG recovery. Despite the surge in prices of key raw materials (cheese, chicken, wheat), price adjustments were negligible and cost inflation was more than offset by proportionately lower opex. Some of the opex savings were a result of cost-cutting measures implemented during the pandemic’s peak, and should be ‘permanent’.

Room to catch up in 2H22

While 1H22 results appear to be tracking behind our FY22 estimates, we believe the company can still meet our full-year forecasts as we move into the seasonally strong 4Q, which is typically boosted by holiday celebrations. Moreover, the resumption of onsite classes in 3Q could provide a further boost to foot traffic. Pre-pandemic, 4Q typically accounts for ~30% of FY sales, and 35-37% of FY profit. As SSSG continues to improve sequentially, we believe the impact of operating leverage on the bottom line will become more pronounced.

 
Robinsons Land - 2Q22: Mall revenue jumps, residential cancellations decline
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Major segments perform well in 2Q22

Robinsons Land’s major segments generally performed well in 2Q22: 1) mall revenue jumped thanks to the economic reopening; 2) residential cancellations declined; and 3) office vacancy remained well below the industry average. RLC’s 2Q22 EBIT jumped 28% YoY (+96% QoQ) as it recognized revenue from the final phases of its China residential project. 1H22 EBIT grew 6% to P6.1bn, in line with consensus estimates. While Philippine operating profit was broadly in line with our expectations, China revenue was booked more quickly than we projected. We expected full completion only by next year and thus forecasted some of the revenue to be recognized then.

Mall revenue jumps but margins fall on power costs

Thanks to the economic reopening, mall foot traffic improved from 75% of pre[1]COVID levels in 1Q22 to 85% in 2Q. As a result, 2Q22 mall revenue jumped 57% YoY (+14% QoQ) to P3.0bn, c. 75% of the 2019 average. Unfortunately, the mall EBIT margin fell 220 basis points QoQ (to 18.4%) as the company has not fully passed on higher power costs to tenants. Nevertheless, mall operations should improve further since footfall rose further to 90% of pre-COVID levels in July, prompting the company to remove headline rental concessions. Fully passing on higher utility costs, which is allowed by its contracts, might be done later this year.

Residential cancellations decline

2Q22 Philippine residential revenue grew 4% YoY (+97% QoQ) as residential cancellations declined for the second straight quarter. Moreover, the company expects even less cancellations in 2H22. Reservation sales of RLC’s standalone projects grew a modest 12% YoY (+7% QoQ) to P3.1bn, only 62% of the 2019 average. However, sales of its JV projects jumped at a remarkable pace of 70% YoY (and QoQ), led by high-end projects in partnership with Shang Properties and HK Land.

Office vacancy below the industry average

Office vacancy remained flat QoQ at 7%, much lower than the industry average of 18%. 1H22 office EBIT grew 21% to P2.6bn, tracking ahead of our forecast.

China project completed

1H22 revenue from the China residential project jumped 20% to P12.7bn, much higher than our forecast of P7bn for the full year. The company completed the construction of all the phases and has thus recognized revenue on all the sold units (96% sold). Since RLC will no longer pursue other projects in China, it has repatriated capital to the Philippines.

Philippine Strategy - GDP slows to +7.4% in 2Q22, defying bottom-up data
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Growth dragged by Net Exports, and “Statistical Discrepancy”

GDP expanded 7.4% YoY in 2Q22, down from the 8.2% pace in 1Q22. Strangely, in our view, Personal Consumption not only decelerated (8.6% in 2Q vs 10.0% in 1Q), but also shrank 2.1% QoQ. This is at odds with sector data, Google Mobility, and even early corporate earnings reports. We note that 2Q22 was operating off low bases due to hard lockdowns in April/May 2021, and some mobility restrictions in January 2022. Further, 2Q22 included the Presidential elections in May. Net Exports was an expected drag, knocking 400bps of 2Q growth. What was not expected was a 140bp growth drag from the “Statistical Discrepancy” line. We maintain our FY22E forecast of 6.0% GDP growth for now.

Consumption slows, defying bottom-up data

The sharp YoY slowdown and QoQ contraction in Personal Consumption contradicts high-frequency sector data – such as auto sales, electricity sales, toll road traffic, domestic air travel, loan growth, and Google Mobility (see charts on page 2). Even early results from consumer companies posted strong YoY and QoQ growth trends. Note, too, that 2Q22 included the May Presidential elections, which typically boosts spending However, we concede the possibility that inflation hurt the lower-end consumers more, and that this was under[1]represented in the data we collect.

Gov’t and Investments surge; Drags from Net Exports, Statistical Discrepancy

Government spending jumped 11.1% YoY and 34% QoQ in 2Q22, likely in for the May 2022 elections, but also due to fuel subsidies for public transport operators. Capital Formation surged 21% YoY, as inventories were built up (vs drawdowns during the pandemic). “Domestic demand” was up 12.8% YoY. As expected, Net Exports (ie trade deficit) grew 40% YoY in 2Q22, taking a full 400bps off 2Q GDP growth. However, there was a surprise drag from the “Statistical Discrepancy” line, which knocked off fully 140bps off 2Q growth.

Glass still half full? Keeping growth targets for now

Despite the robust 1H22 GDP growth of 7.8%, we maintain our FY22E forecast of 6.0%, wary of spillover inflation pressure and higher base effects in 2H.

 
Wilcon Depot - Solidifying its lead; upgrading to Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Coming out on top

We are upgrading Wilcon Depot (WLCON) from Hold to Buy following the strong 1H22 results, which highlight the strength of its operations. WLCON’s unparalleled scale has allowed it to navigate the challenging external operating environment (and coming out on top), and in our view, has further raised the barriers to entry for the home improvement retail segment. We forecast +19% 2022-24E EPS CAGR to be driven by (1) the continued recovery of private home construction, (2) new store openings, and (3) operating leverage as the business further scales up. We lift our target price to P34/sh (+21%) as we raise 2022-24E EPS estimates by 5-16% and roll over our DCF to 2023E. Key downside risks relate to supply chain pressures, forex risk, and weakening of consumer demand.

Unmatched scale has been a key advantage

WLCON’s unparalleled scale has allowed it to navigate the current operating environment, which has been challenged by supply chain pressures, input cost inflation, and temporary store closures due to lockdowns. Gross margins have been expanding sequentially since 4Q20 as the company continues to scale up its in-house brands and comfortably passes on any input cost inflation through price adjustments. WLCON’s lead over smaller competitors has widened, in our view, as the latter have been unable to keep up with the sudden surge in demand amid supply chain constraints.

Store expansion, SSSG to drive growth as margin expansion moderates

We are forecasting +19% EPS CAGR for 2022-24E to be largely driven by (1) the continued recovery of private home construction, which will drive SSSG, (2) store expansion as the company achieves its 100-store target by 2025, and (3) operating leverage as the business further scales up. We believe gross margins are likely to have peaked already at ~40% in 2Q22, with higher cost inventory coming through in subsequent periods. Over the next 1-2 years, gross margin upside will likely have to come from a rising share of in-house brands, which currently stands at ~51% (Regis: 52% by 2024E)

Raising estimates, TP; upgrading to Buy

We are raising our 2022-24E net profit estimates by 5-16% largely on the back of higher margin assumptions vs previous estimates. Our upgraded forecasts coupled with the rollover of our DCF to 2023E result in a +21% increase in our TP to P34/sh. Our TP implies 2023E multiples of 31x PE and 15x EV/EBITDA, a premium to peers, which we believe is justified given faster EPS growth and ROE vs its peers. Key downside risks primarily relate to intensifying supply chain pressures, forex risks, and weakening consumer demand.

 
ACEN Corp - 2Q rebounds on net-sell position to spot, higher plant availability
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H22 income P2.183bn (-19%)

ACEN reported that its 2Q22 income jumped to P1.778bn (339%QoQ, 25%YoY) after higher plant availability allowed it to reverse its short position in the power market. It also booked fresh contributions from new plants, particularly from Vietnam wind farms and India solar plants, which drove attributable output by 11% in the first half. Despite the recovery, 1H22 earnings (P2.183bn, -19%YoY) are tracking behind both Regis and consensus estimates for the full year.

On ACEN’s spot and coal exposure

After buying replacement power from WESM in 1Q, ACEN turned to a net seller in 2Q, with a baseload supply of about 450MW matching about the same level in peak demand from customers (Meralco, RES) and >260MW in attributable solar capacity selling to the spot. However, planned outages in 2H will revert it to being a “modest net buyer” again of expensive replacement power, should spot prices remain elevated. Management also discussed the potential risks from its coal exposure mismatch, which at current prices could translate to an estimated hit of about $10mn on an annualized basis.

A cosmetic divestment?

We get more detail on the “divestment” of ACEN from SLTEC. About P7.2bn will be raised by ACEN through a capital redemption (P3.5bn, debt funded) and the sale of shares to ETM Phils (EPHI) (P3.7bn). On paper, ACEN will no longer own SLTEC, but in reality, it will still retain bulk of the economics. The complicated transactions to effect the nominal divestment of SLTEC will, in our estimates, reduce the recurring income accruing to ACEN by >30% in exchange for the “capital release”. The resulting arrangement appears similar to the IPP Administrator (IPPA) structures that the Philippine government formed as a means of privatizing BOT assets, where the IPPA bears operating risk without legal ownership. The workaround is meant, in our view, to address concerns from ESG-inclined investors about ACEN’s coal exposure.

20GW RE target by 2030

Fresh off the heels of hitting close to 4GW in capacity (of which 3.5GW are renewable), management unveiled its 2030 target of hitting 20GW in RE in 8 years time. They cited an 18GW pipeline, bulk of which are in Phils and Australia. The ambitious plan necessitates a balance sheet transformation that will be predominantly debt driven – an exercise that would have been more generously regarded in last year’s low interest rate environment.

Although revenues saw some recovery (25%YoY in 2Q), GP margins fell to 25.9% from 45% in 2Q21 (and mid 30s historical average). Management cited higher cost of distillation, glass and transport for the collapse, and hinted that these could persist for most of the year. Opex and A&P expenses have also been creeping up along with the reopening, dragging 2Q22 EBITDA to P2.2bn (-32%YoY) and 1H22 to P4.1bn ( - 21%).

Whisky: underlying business up 20% despite logistics constraints

Profits from Whyte & Mackay, meanwhile jumped to P1.4bn (58%QoQ, 178%YoY) in 2Q22. Excluding the one-time charges relating to taxes last year, we estimate growth would have been closer to 20%YoY for both 2Q/1H. Sales nevertheless remained buoyant, as 2Q/1H revenues grew 8%/13% on the back of sustained growth in Asia and North America. In contrast to the brandy segment, margins were actually stable (GP margin 36.5%, EBITDA margin 35.3%). Management indicated that logistics issues continue to temper their ability to service demand in Asia and US. Current shipments are thus reflective of this restriction, which is expected to remain an issue throughout the second half. The contribution from international whisky was 35%/45% to revenue/profit in the latest period. Given the faster trajectory of the international business, the company’s goal of hitting 50% of sales and profits from international is likely to happen in the not-so-distant future.

 
Cebu Air - Nascent recovery in 2Q22
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

2Q22 core loss shrinks QoQ to P2.95bn

Headline net loss in 2Q22 stood at P1.89bn. Removing gains from MTM adj on its convertible bond (CB), FX losses, other one-offs, and adding back interest expenses on the CBs, CEB’s core loss shrunk by 40% QoQ, 50% YoY to P2.95bn in 2Q. EBITDA turned positive in 2Q, the first time since the pandemic began, at P453mn. While 1H results fell short of expectations, 2H trends are encouraging, with management guiding a return to profitability in 4Q.

Domestic business still the key driver in 2Q; Forward bookings strong

Revenues rebounded QoQ/YoY, as travel restrictions eased within the country. CEB’s domestic capacity exceeded pre-pandemic levels, bringing systemwide capacity to 85% of pre-Covid levels. Seat load factors improved by 7ppts to 77%, with pax volumes reverting to 75% of pre-Covid levels. Pax fares likewise improved (+30% QoQ) on favorable network mix but were still only about 70% of pre-Covid levels, with key international markets (i.e. North Asia) still closed. Management indicated domestic fares were very close to pre-Covid levels in 2Q and continued to rise. Forward bookings are also improving sequentially, despite higher fares. While 3Q is the leanest quarter for the domestic market, the addition of more international routes could offset this.

CASKs (ex-fuel) on a downward trend

Cost per available seat kilometer (CASK) dropped by 26% QoQ/53% YoY, with CASK (ex-fuel) also dropping 41% QoQ/67% YoY to P2.4. Even though this number is still 50% higher than it was pre-Covid (P1.6), management believes it is on track to reach this level by year-end. With fuel prices tailing off, margins are likely to improve in succeeding periods.

Generating cash for the first time since the pandemic began

CEB generated cash of P5.5bn (i.e. cash from operations less debt service) in 2Q22, after burning cash since 2Q20. This was partly aided by strong forward bookings generating P2.8bn in cash during the quarter, up from only P1.9bn in 1Q.

 
 
Corporate News
 
 
 
 

Globe 1H income up marginally to P11bn

Globe’s 1H core income came in at P11.0bn (+3%), looks within expectations. Mobile service revenue for 2Q was up +1% QoQ and +3% YoY. Globe added 29k net wired subscribers in 2Q, and continued to see decline in fixed wireless subscribers. Broadband revenue is still shrinking and Globe plans to launch prepaid fiber. The Core telco business appears mildly disappointing but non-telco businesses better than expected. Affiliates contributed ~P900m in profit in 1H with Mynt contributing P594m. Vega also no longer losing money.

Globe to sell over 7,000 towers

Globe disclosed it is selling over 7,000 of its telecom towers for P71bn. It will be a sale to and lease-back from three separate parties. MIESCOR Infrastructure Development will acquire 2,180 towers for P26bn. Frontier Towers Asia will acquire 3,529 towers for P45bn. While another unnamed firm will acquire 1,350 towers. The towers to be sold (~7,000) comprise over half of Globe’s total. The first two batches will raise P71bn for 5,709 towers or P12.4m/tower, just slightly less than PLDT’s sale. The proceeds will be used to fund capex and for debt service.

Converge ICT 2Q highlights

Revenue and income growth slow down. Revenue for 2Q reached P8.3bn (+33%) while income for the quarter was up +16% to P1.98bn. 1H revenue growth was +36% (to P16.1bn) while income growth was +21% (to P3.95bn). The slowdown is evident in (and the result of) the steep drop in net adds to only 15k for 2Q.

 

Management reduced guidance for revenue growth to 25-30%.  This was +50% at the start of the year, which consensus cut to ~40% after 1Q results.   Guidance for year-end subscribers is now only 2m (vs 2.4m), which implies net adds of 308k for the entire FY22 or 183k for the rest of the year. This would mean +18% growth in subscriber base in FY22 over FY21.

 

Gross adds for 2Q were 150k, and churn was 135k (2.2% monthly).  Gross adds were the lowest in the last two years and churn the highest (ever). Management claims churn was high because it attracted sub-optimal subscribers after deciding to stop collecting upfront fees a couple of quarters ago.  It has since reinstated upfront fees (in June).  Imposition of upfront fees, however, will normally reduce gross adds.

 

While net adds were down, ARPUs are up slightly (+3% QoQ) to P1,278.  Management didn’t say the reason for this but it may have been lifted by the upfront fees (which are amortized and form part of monthly ARPU).  Management guidance for ARPU is for it to remain stable.

Soft launch of prepaid fiber.  Converge said it “soft launched” a prepaid service in July.  For this service to have a meaningful uptake and impact, Regis believes monthly revenue (ARPU) has to be significantly lower than current levels, which contradicts the guidance for stable ARPUs. 

 

Management expects the prepaid service to be launched within the year.

Consensus profit estimate for this year is P9.76bn (before 2Q results were released) and Regis thinks Converge is likely to reach just slightly above P8bn. The Street will thus likely have to cut estimates by over 15%.  Assuming ~P8bn profit for FY22, CNVRG would be trading at 18x PER.

 

The decline in net adds has been attributed to higher churn (disconnections). This is reflective of lower quality subscribers who simply are unable to pay for wired broadband on a continuing basis at current prices (P1,500/month).

PLDT group’s Cignal investing in ABS-CBN’s Sky Cable; ABS-CBN to invest in TV5

Cignal Cable Corp is acquiring 38.8% of Sky Cable for P2.862bn from the Lopez group through Sky Vision Corp, ABS-CBN, and Lopez Inc. It is also investing in an Exchangeable instrument (face value of P4.388bn) to allow it to acquire the remaining 61.1% of Sky Cable. A small (P250m) convertible instrument of Sky Cable is also being purchased. Sky Cable is the second largest pay TV operator behind Cignal and the 4th largest broadband operator. It has 300k cable TV subscribers and 350k broadband subscribers.

 

Cignal is effectively controlled by PLDT through Mediaquest, a holding company under its employee pension fund. PLDT has depositary instruments giving it 64% economic rights in Cignal. PLDT and the Lopez group intend to close the deal within this month. In order to avoid having to secure approval from the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC). The Bayanihan 2 Act lifted the requirement for PCC review for transactions below P50bn until Sept 2022. The PCC will, however, still have the right to review the transaction post-closing.

 

Regis believes there is high risk that the PCC may impose post-closing conditions (or worse). The deal will have competition implications not just on pay TV but also on broadband. In a separate transaction, ABS-CBN announced that it is acquiring 34.99% of TV5 for P2.16bn. TV5 is ultimately owned by the PLDT Beneficial Trust Fund through MediaQuest. ABS-CBN is also investing in a convertible note (face value of P1.84bn). This will allow ABS-CBN to increase its stake in TV5 to a maximum of 49.92% within the next 8 years, pending regulatory approvals. The deal appears to be beneficial for both parties given ABS’ extensive content library and TV5’s free-to-air franchise.

Globe seeks penalties for Dito Tel on international calls fees

Globe asked the regulator, NTC, to impose P622m (US$11m) in penalties on Dito. This is relating to international voice calls that are being masked as local calls, and thus not paying the appropriate termination fees. International calls terminating with local carriers have to pay interconnection/termination fees while domestic mobile calls don’t pay interconnection fees. Globe’s complaint comes after Dito Tel filed an anti-competition complaint with the Philippine Competition Commission. Dito Tel’s complaint arose from the difficulty in completing voice calls on the networks of Globe and PLDT.

Dito files competition complaint against PLDT, Globe

New mobile operator Dito Telecommunity filed complaints against PLDT and Globe for “anti-competitive” behavior. The complaints relate to voice interconnection. Dito claims only 30% of calls from its network get through to PLDT and Globe. Regular voice calls are becoming less frequent and important already as calls are increasingly being done through the data network. Voice revenue represents a decreasing share of total mobile revenue (~15%). The government-mandated removal of mobile interconnect/termination fees also results in little incentive to improve voice interconnection.

ACEN 2Q profit recovers to P1.778bn

ACEN reported that its 2Q22 income amounted to P1.778bn, more than 4x the level in 1Q and up 25%YoY. The press release attributed to recovery to fresh contributions from new Philippine and International plants, as well as easing of curtailment issues in Visayas. Improvements in plant availability also allowed ACEN some excess capacity, enabling the company to benefit from strong spot prices. This brings 1H22 net income to P2.183bn, down 19%YoY. The attributable output grew 11% to 2,482GWh with increased operating capacity from Vietnam wind farms and India solar plants. ACEN trades at PE 44x for FY22E and attributable EV/EBITDA of 22.2x.

Macro News
 
 
 
 

NG debt rises to P12.79tn in end-June

The national government’s outstanding debt reached P12.79tn in end-June. This is P296bn (2.4%) higher MoM and P1.063tn (9.1%) higher than in end-December 2021. Of the total debt stock, 31.5% was sourced externally while 68.5% were domestic borrowings. The government is targeting to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to below 60% only by 2025.

End-July GIR level drops 2% MoM to US$98.8bn

The country’s GIR level, based on preliminary data, dropped by US$2.0bn (-2%) MoM to US$98.8bn in July. This is down from US$108.8bn in end- 2021 and US$110.1bn in end-2020. However, this is still above end-2019 GIR of US$87.8bn (~7.7 import cover). July level represents 8.3 months’ worth of imports.

 

Inflation accelerates further to 6.4% in July

Headline inflation continued its uptrend, rising to 6.4% in July, from 6.1% in June. This is the fastest recorded inflation rate since October 2018. This came at the upper-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 5.6-6.4% and above consensus (6.1%). Almost all categories saw an uptick in inflation, except for moderation in utilities, operation of transport equipment, and health. Food inflation driven by fish, meat, sugar; offset by lower inflation of vegetables. Prices of transport services jumped by 7%, faster than June’s 3%, due to nationwide increase in jeepney fares. The YT-July inflation now at 4.7%, above the government’s average inflation target of 2-4%. Monetary Board meeting is set for Aug 18.

Employment improves in June

The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that the number of employed improved MoM/YoY by 508k/1.5mn. The MoM improvement due to agriculture sector, which created 1.3mn jobs in June vs. May. This brings employment rate to 94%, higher than in June 2021 but still below pre-Covid levels (94.7%). The number of unemployed rose slightly MoM but was significantly lower than in June 2021. The June unemployment rate at 6.0%, down from last year’s 7.7% but still above pre-Covid levels of ~5%.

Trade deficit widens further in June

Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit widened further to US$5.8bn. This is higher than the revised US$5.6bn deficit (previously US$5.7bn) in May 2022 and the US$3.33bn deficit in June 2021. Imports rose 26% YoY, 5% MoM in June due to a sustained surge in fuel imports (+125% YoY) and a strong rebound in consumer goods imports (+30% YoY). Capital goods imports rose just 3% YoY. Exports barely improved, up 1% YoY, as electronic imports dropped. The 1H22 trade deficit now at US$29.8bn, up 66% YoY.

Asset quality, loan growth continue to improve in June

Asset quality continued to improve in June, not only in ratio terms, but also in absolute PHP amounts. NPL ratio (ex-interbank), dropped 16bps MoM to 3.69% (vs 3.85% in May), and is the lowest NPL print since September 2020. The PHP value fell to P421bn, down 1.8% MoM and down 12.8% YoY. The Past Due (PD) ratio dropped even faster, down 25bps MoM to 4.19% in June vs 4.44% in May, with PD loans down 3.5% MoM. Restructured Loans (RLs) edged down another 5bps to 2.89% (vs 2.94% in May), though the nominal amount crept up 0.7% MoM. NPL cover jumped to 97.1% from 94.8% in May, due largely to the drop in NPLs; loan loss reserves were up marginally MoM. Loan growth (ex-interbank), meanwhile, accelerated again to 9.1% YoY in June, compared to 7.7% in May. This is now the 15th consecutive month of faster YoY growth rates. Loans were up 2.5% MoM. LDRs were slightly up to 71.0% from 70.7% in May.

The data remains consistent with Regis’ thesis of the banking sector as a play on the economic reopening.

 

2Q22 GDP growth slows to 7.4%, led by an odd 2% QoQ drop in consumption

Philippine real GDP growth clocked in at 7.4% YoY in 2Q22, slowing from the (revised) 8.2% pace in 1Q22. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data, Personal Consumption Expenditure (PCE) slowed sharply to 8.6% YoY from 10.0% in 1Q22; more oddly, the PCE figure for 2Q22 was down 2.1% QoQ. This was despite relatively lower base effects in 2Q21 given the tight lockdowns in April/May 2021, and lighter lockdowns in January this year. The slowdown was at odds with bottom-up sector data, Google Mobility data, and early corporate results, which showed an acceleration in demand.

 

Government consumption grew 11.1% YoY and 34.3% QoQ likely in part due to spending related to the May 2022 elections. Capital Formation surged 20.5% YoY and 34.66% QoQ. The growth was driven largely by Changes in Stock, ie huge inventory buildup (vs drawdown in 2Q21), and by Construction, which was up 15.7% YoY and 67.9% QoQ. Regis estimates Domestic Demand grew 12.8% YoY, compared to 12.1% in 1Q22. Net Exports was a major drag, with the deficit ballooning 39.6% YoY in real terms, which Regis estimates pulled GDP down by roughly 400bps. Exports grew 4.3% YoY, while Imports were up 13.6%. Annoyingly, the “Statistical Discrepancy” line accounted for a 140bp reduction to the YoY growth in 2Q22.

 

On the Supply side, Industry slowed sharply to 6.3% YoY from 10.5% in 1Q22, with Manufacturing growing just 2.1% YoY vs 9.8% in 1Q22. This negated a surge in Construction, up 19% YoY. The Services sector accelerated to 9.1% YoY (and up 10.5% QoQ), driven by Transportation (+27.1% YoY) and Accommodation/Food Service (+29.9% YoY) as mobility restrictions were eased.

 

Agriculture dragged, growing just 0.2% YoY, and contracting 4.1% QoQ. GDP growth in 1H22 was 7.8% YoY, significantly faster than Regis’ 6.0% forecast for FY 2022E. While it is tempting to upgrade, Regis retain their numbers for now, wary of a) higher base effect from 2H21, b) higher inflation in 3Q, and c) potential normalization after the “revenge spending” in 1H22.
