REGIS REPORT

11 March 2022

The Phils dropped 3.1% this week with geopolitical tensions sparking aggressive selldowns.  The PSEI saw its worst day since June 2020 on Tuesday, falling 4.3% to below the 7000 index level.  The market managed to make up some of the earlier losses, closing above the 7100 level for the week.  

The decline was widespread, with index losers overwhelming gainers 9 to 1.

The government is studying measures to blunt the impact of the Ukraine-Russia crisis. The proposals include increasing fuel subsidies, suspending the biofuels law and boosting food imports. However, it is looking likely for inflation to breach the central bank’s target (2% to 4%) as the surge in prices feeds through to consumer prices.

Cost inflation concerns led to steep drops in the consumer space. Emperador (-23.5% WoW) fell markedly as did  URC (-10.0% WoW) and Century Pacific (-14.3% WoW). Large cap property and conglos SM (-2.5% WoW), Ayala Corp (-5.6% WoW), SM Prime (-3.1% WoW), Ayala Land (-3.2% WoW), and JG Summit (-4.8% WoW)  weighed on the index.  Most sectors were down save for power – with Aboitiz Power (+1.9% WoW), AC Energy (+1.8% WoW), and Meralco (+1.5% WoW) the only index gainers for the week with sentiment lifted by the tight supply situation and elevated power prices.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$47.5m this week as investors were uneasy over tensions in Ukraine. Foreigners were seen selling banks and conglomerates while buying ports and property.

 On corporate side, this week featured another slew of 4Q21 results, detailed below.

Universal Robina - Margin squeeze in 4Q21
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY21 boosted by one-offs; core operations behind

URC reported headline net profit of P23.3bn, boosted by non-recurring gains relating to sales of land assets (2Q21) and its Oceania business (4Q21). Excluding these one-offs, we estimate recurring income was P10bn, behind expectations. Headline revenues of P135.8bn (+2% YoY) were broadly within consensus forecasts, even after accounting for the exclusion of the Oceania business, which was de-consolidated as of 29 Oct. FY21 headline EBIT contracted 22% YoY to P14.8bn (behind), but was down 8% YoY to P12.7bn ex-Oceania.

Branded consumer food (BCF) top line recovers across key markets in 4Q21

Excluding Oceania, total BCF sales grew 5% YoY in 4Q21, rebounding from the 4% YoY decline in the previous quarter. Sales across most of the key markets improved on a QoQ and YoY basis as mobility restrictions eased. It was the Int’l BCF segment (ex-Oceania) that saw faster QoQ growth in sales (+15%) vs the Phils BCF segment (+4% QoQ).

Input cost inflation drags margins

EBIT (ex-Oceania) fell 15% YoY to P2.9bn in 4Q21. Margins contracted across the board due to input cost inflation. Initial pricing adjustments were made in 2Q and 3Q, and another round of adjustments was implemented in Nov-Dec. This brought the cumulative weighted price increase to +4% for FY21, which offset only half of the impact of the increase in raw material cost.

Cost environment remains challenged

Management noted that the cost environment remains challenged, notwithstanding the positive top line trends in early 2022. For FY22, URC is guiding organic (ex-Oceania, Munchy’s) top line growth of high-single digit to low teens, plus another mid-single digit contribution from the consolidation of Munchy’s. Margins, however, could be volatile in the near term. While the company has contracted c90% of its requirements for 1Q22, key raw materials (wheat/flour, palm oil, packaging cost, coffee) have jumped by as much as +65% YTD, giving management limited visibility on margins beyond 1Q. Further pricing adjustments will be made in the coming months, though they may not be enough to fully mitigate the cost pressures near term.

 
Globe Telecom - Back to basics
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Return to a new normal

The threat from the third operator now appears to be less serious than many feared as the Philippines shuffles towards a “new normal” of living with Covid-19. We expect mobile revenue growth to return, gingerly, as mobility recovers. Globe has spent a surprisingly large amount for capex, partly to meet such a rebound, and partly in anticipation of competition from the third operator. The resulting leverage ratios have made an equity issue likely this year. We tweak our numbers for Globe, lift our TP to P2,700 and reduce our rating to Hold.

Improving operating outlook but record capex to hold back earnings

We expect growth to resume in 2022 after a period of restrained revenue. As personal mobility improves, so should mobile usage and, eventually, mobile revenue. A recovery in personal incomes should also help as employment rebounds. We also believe the market will be less worried about the threat from the new operator. Nevertheless, earnings recovery will be tempered by the record capex numbers due to higher depreciation and financing charges.

Balance sheet is stretched

Globe spent a record amount of capex in 2021 (~60% of revenue) and expects to spend almost the same amount again this year. We believe Globe’s balance sheet is now stretched, and financial leverage high, notwithstanding the accounting treatment of the recent perps as equity. There is a good chance, in our view, that Globe will raise equity this year.

Target price 2,700

We revise our target price to P2,700/share, taking into account its core telecom operations (using DCF), and the value of its stakes in Mynt as well as its data center JV. Key risks to the downside relate to a deterioration in the macro conditions while upside risk could come from reduced competition.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Mass segments jump in 4Q; capacity limits lifted
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

FY21 EBITDA ahead; net loss in line, dragged by Jeju impairment

BLOOM’s 4Q EBITDA doubled to P1.851bn in 4Q21, bringing the FY21 EBITDA to P5.214bn, ahead of both Regis and consensus estimates for the year. GGR continued to trend higher, this time driven by the local mass segment, as Metro Manila eased restrictions twice in the quarter. Alongside the operational outperformance, the company booked an P820mn impairment charge relating to its Jeju property, resulting in a FY21 net loss of P4.220bn, in line with estimates. BLOOM trades at 10.5x EV/EBITDA for FY22E, lower than Macau median of 19.4x.

GGR up 13%QoQ; local mass makes up for VIP softness

4Q21 GGR grew 13%QoQ to P7.979bn, with mass and slots offsetting a weak VIP segment due to the closure of certain junket rooms. Underlying volumes for mass mimicked the revenue trends, while a sequential improvement in VIP win rate helped soften its underperformance. The continued pressure by the Chinese government on Macau junkets could mean a more prolonged recovery for VIP in the Philippines, but recent trends show that pent-up demand from locals should be a focal point for recovery in the coming quarters.

March foot traffic at 60% of pre-Covid; no more capacity limits for property

In the call, management cited foot traffic hitting 12k/day in early March, about 60% of pre-pandemic levels. That number has steadily climbed from 6.6k/day in September, save for a blip in January because of the Omicron surge. The current alert level which was imposed at the start of the month means that there are no more capacity limits on gaming and non-gaming offerings. The property also just started accepting hotel room bookings from the public in February and occupancy levels are at a over 20% and climbing.

Focus shifts back to physical property; planned capex at >P20bn in 2022

Its online gaming initiatives contributed P2.1bn of total GGR last year (c.8%). Management, however, believes that the focus should revert to live play as Manila reopens. Target capex for Solaire North is P20bn this year and another P15bn in 2023 to meet a 4Q23/1Q24 opening.

 
Robinsons Land - Residential revenue plunges in 4Q21 as cancellations stay high
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Weak residential revenue drags operating profit

Robinsons Land’s FY21 headline profit rose 61% to P8.5bn. However, this was flattered by a P600mn tax gain in 4Q21. FY21 EBIT only rose 14% to P9.7bn, behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts, as residential revenue fell 47%. While operating profit disappointed in 2021, we expect the mall and residential segments to improve substantially in 2022E. In addition, 2021 office revenue already surpassed our 2022E forecast.

4Q21 residential revenue drops another 51% QoQ; reservation sales jump

After dropping 60% QoQ in 3Q21, residential revenue fell another 51% QoQ. Even if we were to add back cancellations (P1bn in 4Q, P1.8bn in 3Q), 4Q21 revenue would still have fallen 47% QoQ (down 35% YoY). Due to payment moratoriums and restructurings, some accounts had not yet reached the 10% payment threshold at which revenue recognition begins. However, revenue recognition should accelerate in the coming years since unbooked revenue (which will be recognized over the next four years) grew 7% YoY to P32bn. Furthermore, despite the cancellations, 4Q21 net presales grew 23% QoQ (+117% YoY) and FY21 reservation sales rose 48% to P10.8bn.

Mall revenue improves in 4Q as foot traffic climbs

4Q21 mall revenue rose 24% QoQ and 22% YoY as foot traffic improved to 65% of pre-COVID levels, from only 40% in the five previous quarters. This resulted in FY21 mall revenue of P8.3bn (-3%), c. 45% of the 2019 figure.

Office revenue already exceeds our 2022E forecast

FY21 office revenue grew 8% to P6.5bn, ahead of our forecast (and even exceeding our 2022E estimate of P6.1bn). Due to the higher vacancy of the completed buildings in 4Q21, RLC’s aggregate vacancy increased 1ppt QoQ to 7%. Nevertheless, this is still much better than the industry average of 18%.

by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q21 core profit up 24% YoY, above Regis forecast

Headline FY21 income stood at P10.1bn (+113%). Tollroad traffic vols match pre-pandemic levels in 4Q and already above YTD

The tollroad unit grew 4Q profits sharply YoY/QoQ, as traffic volumes positively surprised, reverting to pre-pandemic levels. Management noted that volumes continue to improve YTD and are above pre-pandemic levels. Domestic travel has been robust so far even with the recent surge in oil prices. However, the oil price shock has dashed the company’s hopes of securing a tariff hike near term.

Water contributions boosted by opex reductions, lower tax rate in 4Q

Despite a 4% reduction in revenues, profit from its water unit jumped 47% YoY due to lower opex and tax rate. Management is preparing its rate rebasing proposal, which will involve a catch[1]up plan from tariff freezes and capex delays the past two years. Rail volumes recover; Acquisitions in non-regulated sectors

It is however committed to maintain dividends and continue its buyback.

 
Aboitiz Power - 4Q: Claims soften impact of higher steam costs, lower dist volumes
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY21 core profit +68% - in line; 4Q21 -5%QoQ on lower distribution income

AP’s 4Q core income dipped to P5.342bn (-5%QoQ, -12%YoY), dragged by higher steam costs from the generation side and the impact of Typhoon Odette on its distribution areas. The profit nevertheless included P1bn in claims related to delays in GNPD, which brought the FY21 core profit to P21.1bn – in line with Regis estimates but ahead of consensus (P18.7bn). Although energy sales of distribution utilities fell 4%QoQ, sales of its own generation business jumped 14%QoQ and 25%YoY.

Generation: Better volumes, selling prices and a boost from insurance

Core income from generation/RES came in at P5.888bn (-2%QoQ, -4%YoY). Gross margin dipped 1%QoQ, affected by higher rates of purchased power and higher steam costs at APRI (which were indexed to coal). 4Q21 energy sales nevertheless rose to 7,590 (+14%QoQ, +25%YoY), bringing FY21 volumes 14% higher. Selling prices to the spot market averaged P5.7/kWh (from P5.36 in 9M21) and more than double last year’s. The generation business booked >P4.2bn in claims for the year, including P1bn in 4Q21 in liquidated damages from the GNPD Unit 2 delay.

Distribution: 4Q sales fell 4%QoQ

Distribution income fell 33%QoQ to P669mn, dragged by lower volumes and additional opex brought about by Typhoon Odette. Margins also declined due to timing differences between higher power prices and pass-thru recognition, which should reverse in succeeding period/s. Total volume across its distribution utilities grew 4% for FY21, implying 4Q21 volumes were up 1%YoY but down 4%QoQ. Residential fell 5.1%QoQ.

Coal update

Management stressed that while coal prices have risen, its generation contracts are either a) covered by fuel-pass through or b) hedged for the duration of a fixed price contract. However, there is now an aversion to signing long term fixed price contracts, given the prohibitive hedging cost. Management also noted that while Newcastle is above $400/MT, current indices for low calorific coal used by GMEC/GNPD are well below this price, such that its fuel cost would still come in under P3/kWh.

DMCI achieves record profits in 4Q21; Better 2022 prospects

DMCI’s headline profit for FY21 stood at P18.4bn. Excluding one-offs and FX adjustments, recurring profit jumped 166% YoY to P17.1bn, in line with street estimates. Core profit for 4Q21 alone surged YoY/QoQ to a record of P5.03bn, boosted by (a) higher thermal coal and power prices at Semirara, (b) higher margins and non-operating income at its property unit, (c) cost reductions at Maynilad, and (d) higher power volumes for its off-grid plants. These more than offset (1) the poor performance of the construction unit and (2) higher costs at its nickel mining unit. 

 

For property the company is guiding +30% increase in FY22 reservation sales. Even though revenue bookings dipped YoY, 4Q21 profit for DMCI Homes jumped to P1.2bn on (a) margin improvements and (b) higher income from sales cancellations. Prospects for 2022 were unexciting, with management expecting revenues and EBITDA margins to be flat/slightly lower amid the decline in reservation sales and rising input costs.

 

Reservation sales remained weak in 4Q, declining by 26% YoY but better QoQ. Management is targeting sales to improve by 30% this year as it plans to launch 8 projects from only 1-2 the previous two years. It has seen sales improve sequentially so far in 2022 as mobility restrictions were further eased. 

 

The construction incurred a loss in 4Q after it conservatively adjusted revenues downwards for claims that remained uncollected. Management also gave a very somber outlook for 2022, as it expects construction activity to slow down markedly this year from both public and private sectors. Its order book is now even lower at P49n from P59bn the previous year. The company is participating in 24 bids worth P128bn, of which biddings for ODA-funded rail projects are likely to push through in 2Q despite the election ban.

The good news is that the company secured tree cutting permits for its mine in Zambales and is also starting exploration activities for its potentially new mines in Palawan. Current conditions have forced traders to hold off buying at these elevated prices and management noted further that freight costs have doubled.

 

Outlook and valuations.  We believe DMCI’s 2022 profit will be much better than last year, considering elevated thermal coal prices. Assuming Regis revised forecasts for Semirara, we estimate DMC’s 2022 earnings could rise to P21bn, equivalent to a PER of 5.6x, lower than its 5-year average PER of 10x.

 

Even though the stock has outperformed the PCOMP by 22% YTD, we believe it is inexpensive, trading at an 18% discount to its market NAV of P11/sh, wider than its historical discount of 11%.  Its dividend yield of at least 10% is also attractive. Buy.

SECB FY21 NPAT P6.9bn; loans +2.2% YoY, NPLs

Loans grew 2.2% YoY (4.2% QoQ), posting its first positive YoY print since 2Q20. Inflation steady in Feb as food prices ease

Headline inflation was steady at 3% in February, below street forecasts (3.2%) due to deceleration in food inflation to 1.2%, driven by slower inflation rates for meat and fish. Meanwhile, non-food inflation accelerated due largely to rising oil prices. The government will use available resources to provide targeted subsidies to affected sectors and to tame inflation in the next few months, given rising commodity prices amid the Ukraine-Russia conflict. BSP Governor Diokno said 2022 inflation rate could average 4.4%-4.7% in worst case. The BSP’s base case inflation forecast is 2-4% said it was prepared to deploy liquidity tools if needed.

GIR level at US$107.98bn in February

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) edged slightly higher in end-February to US$107.98bn. This is up from US$107.69bn in January and US$105.2bn in Feb 2021. The rise was largely due to the increase in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings after the rise in gold prices. The reserves are equivalent to 10.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The BSP is projecting the GIR to rise to US$112bn by year-end.

Gov’t explores ways to cushion impact of Ukraine-Russia war

Economic managers approved the implementation of several measures to cushion the impact of higher energy and food prices to the economy. They have requested President Duterte to issue executive orders to implement the following:

 

Double fuel subsidy to the public transport sector to P5bn (US$96mn)

Suspend biofuels law

Boost coal supply and reduce prices by removing the most favored nation tariff rate of 7% under the ASEAN India Free Trade Agreement until December

Boosting rice imports if local production falters

Extending lower tariffs on rice and pork imports

Boosting imports of fish, given a supply gap of 200k MT

Increasing buffer stock of petroleum to 45 days from 30 days and liquefied petroleum gas to 15 days from 7 days

Allowing foreign ownership in micro-grid, solar, wind, and tidal energy

 

Some of these proposals will require certain changes in existing laws and a special session in Congress should the situation escalate

Pressure to raise jeepney fares grows

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) is facing pressure to restore the minimum jeepney fare of P10 vs. P9 currently. The LTFRB decided to “temporarily” reduce the minimum jeepney fare to P9 as diesel pump prices fell to P38 per liter in December 2018. The current price of diesel is at P63-63 per liter. There is also a proposal to raise the minimum fare to P14-15 but the LTFRB has deferred public hearings on this to March 22. Transport fares account for c. 4.5% of the CPI basket

BPO workers to return to offices beginning April 1

The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) rejected the request of the BPO industry to extend the current policy allowing 90% of workers (in PEZA zones) to work from home. Finance Secretary Dominguez said this was meant to be a temporary policy and would lapse on April 1. Thus, 90% of BPO employees (whose offices are in PEZA zones/buildings) must return to the office beginning April. This could soon lead to more BPO firms signing up for office space

Office leasing (including their REIT stakes) comprises:

48% of MEG’s NAV

32% of RLC’s NAV
