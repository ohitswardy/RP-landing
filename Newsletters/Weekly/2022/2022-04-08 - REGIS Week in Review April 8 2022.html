REGIS REPORT

08 April 2022

The Phils pulled back 1.9% this week as global markets anticipated more rate hikes by the Fed. Meanwhile, continued geopolitical tensions and elevated commodity prices weighed. 

Market Selling was broad-based – with 20 of the 30 index member dropping this week. Foreigners were sellers for $18.3m this week. Biggest outflows were seen in SM, DNL, Ayala Land, and Metrobank. 

Large cap SM (-3.7% WoW) fell. This week, the company announced that it will be acquiring 81% of geothermal company Philippine Geothermal Production Company  that operates the Tiwi and Makban geothermal fields in exchange for 17.4m new shares (1.4% of capital).  The  deal takes their stake to 100%. The notional value of the deal is  P15.72 bn (US$ 300m).  The transaction is likely to be EPS accretive, and 2022 earnings for PGPC should be far higher this year as steam contracts are benchmarked to coal prices (which are up 2x vs 2021). One other motivation for the  injection of PGPC was made for ESG reasons (as it will provide some carbon offsets for the listed company).

General risk of sentiment led to selldowns of other market heavy weights.  Ayala Corp (-4.9% WoW), JG Summit (-5.8% WoW), SM Prime (-2.2% WoW), and Aboitiz Equity (-5.1% WoW) weighed heavily on the market. 

Wilcon (-7.2% WoW) dropped markedly, while Monde (-3.5% WoW) was also weak. 

Meanwhile, Puregold (+3.6% WoW) continued to play catch up this week, with its relatively low valuations (~10x FY22 PER) attracting buy interest. 

PLDT  (+2.5% WoW) likewise gained. PLDT has picked two bidders to buy half of its telecom tower portfolio in deals valued at US$1.5bn. The deal is expected to be significantly accretive.  Converge (- 4.5% WoW) and Globe (-1.6% WoW) did not fare as well.

Globe announced the completion of its deal to sell its data center operations for which it will reportedly book a pretax gain of P10.5bn (US$202m). Globe earlier disclosed that it will form a joint venture for its data center business with ST Telemedia and Ayala Corp. Globe will hold 50% in the venture while ST Telemedia and AC will hold 40% and 10% respectively. The post-money valuation of US$350m and the US$100m of proceeds that Globe will receive post-deal implies that Globe’s data center business was being valued at US$275m.

MPI (+2.4% WoW) and unit Meralco (+0.05% WoW) outperformed this week.  Meralco is seeking a distribution tariff of P1.57/kwh for its next regulatory period (Fifth Regulatory Period or 5RP), based on documents released by the regulator. This tariff represents an increase of ~14% from the tariff it currently collects from consumers (P1.38/kwh) and is actually much higher than the rate it recognizes in its P&L (likely closer to P1.1/kwh). While the regulator is likely to slash this number, any tariff over current assumptions of just around P1.1/kwh would impact forecasts positively.  MPI trades at around 50% discount to NAV; its stake in Meralco is already well above its own market cap.

CPI inflation came in at 4.0% for March, higher than 3% in Feb and overshooting expectations CPI is nowhere from peaking as the spike in prices of wheat-based products has yet to be seen in the CPI. Transport fares have not been raised either despite record retail pump prices.

Meanwhile, in a survey conducted by Pulse Asia March 17-24  former Senator Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr continued to show an overwhelming lead. Marcos was the preferred candidate for 56% of respondents, down 4ppt from the last poll in Feb. Vice President Leonor Robredo’s numbers rose by  +9ppt but she is far behind Marcos at 24%. The elections are schedule for 9 May.

Universal Robina - Reopening to outweigh volatile cost environment
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

‘Keep calm and carry on’

URC’s President and CEO Irwin Lee was cautiously optimistic during our CEO Roundtable Series, where much of the discussion focused on how he intends to steer the company through the volatile cost environment. There are strategies in place to help stabilize margins, but he recognizes that it will take some time before these materialize. At the same time, he believes that the economy’s reopening (and election spending boost) will outweigh the effects of inflation on consumer wallets. Moreover, he expects the company’s free cash flow generation should remain healthy despite the expected margin volatility.

Reopening story outweighs inflation

URC is seeing double-digit top line growth in 1Q22 thus far. Mr Lee believes the reopening story is outweighing the impact of inflation on consumer wallets, and URC is not seeing significant downtrading, as was the case during previous periods of high inflation. All players are exposed to the same cost pressures and will thus have to adjust prices. Market leaders like URC have advantages that provide more flexibility in adjusting prices ahead of expected cost increases.

Margin stabilization amidst volatile cost environment

The cost environment is highly volatile, and Mr Lee believes the best course of action is to roll with the punches and stabilize margins as best as they can. URC’s raw material requirements are locked in for the next 3-4 months, though Mr Lee hesitates to take longer positions unless there are opportunities for more attractive pricing. URC also has a partial hedge on commodity prices through its flour and sugar milling businesses URC raised prices by +4% (cumulative weighted average) in 2021 and has just concluded another round of price adjustments. Further price increases are likely to be implemented over the next few quarters and margin relief may not be felt until 2H22.

Healthy cash flow generation despite short term margin squeeze

Despite the short-term margin squeeze, Mr Lee is confident that the company will continue to generate healthy free cash flow, as it has historically done so through various cycles. The company intends to pursue organic and inorganic opportunities while maximizing shareholder return (including through dividend payouts).

 
Robinsons Retail - Well-positioned in an uncertain environment; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Recovery to drive growth

In our CEO Roundtable Series, RRHI’s President and CEO Robina Gokongwei-Pe said she believes the company is poised to see strong performance in 2022 as sales recover from Covid-era lows and receive a boost from inflation and election spending. Even amidst rising inflation, RRHI’s core market (middle-income consumers) is seeing a resurgence in spending after holding back during various Covid lockdowns over the past two years. As the economy reopens, store expansion will also accelerate, though the expansion will be focused in areas that are underserved areas and de-emphasize formats that have yet to see a meaningful recovery in a post-Covid environment.

Comfortable position amidst ‘unprecedented’ inflation

Supermarkets and drug stores are the formats most exposed to commodity price movements, though historically, margins for these formats have been stable even during periods of high inflation, according to Ms Gokongwei-Pe. When suppliers/vendors raise prices, RRHI adjusts selling prices in their stores accordingly while keeping gross margins intact. In some cases, RRHI gets a margin windfall as suppliers provide opportunities to buy ahead of planned price increases. For supermarkets, price inflation has been “unprecedented” with suppliers raising prices by as much as 20% YTD.

‘Covid has taken a step back’

Ms Gokongwei-Pe noted that SSSG across the group has been strong thus far (+4.6% YT-Feb), which she attributes to easing mobility restrictions even despite the Omicron-driven surge in January. She believes Covid has taken a step back from consumers’ minds, and pent-up demand is driving most of the growth despite rising prices of oil/transport and other basic commodities. A further boost could be expected in 2H22 as face-to-face classes and onsite work fully resumes.

Store expansion still focused on essentials, underserved areas

Bulk of the 140-150 new stores planned for 2022 will be accounted for by supermarkets (30-50 stores) and drug stores (80-90 stores, ex-TGP), and most of the new sites will be in underserved areas (e.g. outside Metro Manila). Outside of the essential formats (e.g. department stores, convenience stores), new store expansion will be largely opportunistic until the economy fully reopens RRHI remains our top pick in the sector, and our recent conversation with Ms Gokongwei-Pe reinforces our views that 1) top line growth will be lifted by the economy’s reopening and 2) the business is largely insulated by inflationary pressures. The stock trades at 18x 2022E PE, below historical average and the current average for that of its peers (25x). We adjust our forecasts to account for FY21 results and tweak our TP to P76/sh (-3%). Maintaining Buy.

 
Century Pacific Food - 4Q21 margins contract on input cost surge; margin pressure to persist
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY21 net profit +20% YoY within expectations, cost inflation hits 4Q21 margins

CNPF’s net profit in FY21 rose 20% YoY to P4.7bn, in line with Regis and consensus estimates. Revenues (+13% YoY to P54.7bn) and EBITDA (+10% YoY to P7.1bn) were likewise in line. Higher input cost led to margin compression in 4Q21 even as revenue growth (+19% YoY) was healthy.

Branded revenues resilient in 4Q21, OEM growth slows

Branded revenues in 4Q21 (81% of total) grew 21% YoY. While this was partly coming from a low base, the demand for CNPF’s core branded products remained healthy, and new innovations provided a boost. The OEM business, meanwhile, saw growth decelerate to +8% YoY as exports were challenged by the sharp jump in freight cost.

Margins contract on input cost inflation

EBITDA margin contracted by c100bps YoY during the quarter, mainly due to higher input costs, particularly freight, packaging (tin plates), meats, and milk. CNPF adjusted prices by c3% cumulatively in FY21, though most of the pricing adjustments were done in 4Q21.

Further margin compression seen as impact of price adjustments delayed

Management expects double-digit top line growth for its branded business in 2022. Even amidst an inflationary environment, demand for its core branded products is likely to be resilient, while categories that have lagged (e.g. milk) should see a recovery as the economy reopens.

However, margin compression in the short term is expected given the recent surge in commodity prices. Requirements for major raw materials (skipjack tuna, meats, milk) have been secured until 2Q/3Q22, but freight cost continues to be prohibitive, particularly for the OEM business. Pricing adjustments across its product portfolio are being implemented, but the planned increases for the year (mid- to high-single digit cumulatively) will not be enough to fully offset the expected increases in input prices.

 
AC Energy Corp - Notes from the REGIS CEO Roundtable
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

RE momentum poses challenges of its own

Mr. Eric Francia, President and CEO of ACEN, shared his take on the outlook for the power sector, the supply-demand situation, and RE in a recent CEO roundtable series Regis hosted. Among our key takeaways are: 1) power prices are likely to remain elevated, 2) medium-term supply is looking increasingly insufficient, and 3) RE has become more competitive vs coal & gas but will take more time before it could be dependable as baseload. Against this backdrop, ACEN continues to build on its 18,000MW pipeline, announcing a new partnership with German developer ib vogt for at least 1,000MW of solar projects in APAC.

Demand-supply will remain tight over medium term

Echoing the views brought up by other power companies, Mr. Francia believes that the demand-supply balance will continue to get tighter over the coming years, owing to strong post-Covid demand recovery and a confluence of factors affecting supply. While the supply outlook appears adequate on paper, he reiterated there is no substantial baseload capacity, apart from GNPD-2, expected to come up over the next three years and there is a real risk that planned capacity for 2025/26 does not get built on time. Thus, the overhang from a) declining output from Malampaya and b) the frequent breakdown of older coal plants will be key variables that will affect adequacy of supply.

Power prices rising, ACEN to reverse over-contracted position by 2H

Mr. Francia is of the view that power prices will continue to be elevated throughout the year. Spot prices are already averaging over P6/kWh YTD, from around P5/kWh in 2021 (the second highest annual average in the last decade). The contracting strategy of ACEN has thus shifted to becoming net sellers in the Philippines by the second half of this year. Until then, ACEN will have to brace itself for high power prices due to its over-contracted position.

Fuel prices have gone up, but so have RE costs

Despite the high prices of coal and gas today, Mr. Francia lamented that RE (solar or wind) plus storage still has some way to go before becoming competitive as a baseload solution. There is upward pressure on costs (panels, batteries), and new projects are likely to cost at least 10% more (per MW) vs last year. There is an expectation that upward pressure on raw materials could push back the potential baseload use of solar/wind by another 5-10 years.

Pipeline buildup continues

ACEN recently announced a JV with German developer ib vogt to build at least 1,000MW of solar projects in APAC, on top of the 18,000MW in pipeline that ACEN is considering. Locally, there appears to be an abundant pipeline of solar projects over the next decade. Despite this, Mr Francia believe that between the (1) likelihood that many of these are not built and (2) possibility of an oversupply situation emerging, the balance of risk is in favor of the former.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM to acquire geothermal energy firm from principals

SM Investments will acquire 81% of Allfirst, holding company of Philippine Geothermal Production Company (PGPC). SM will buy it from its controlling shareholders, the Sy family.  SM already owns 19% of Allfirst. PGPC is the entity that operates the Tiwi and Makban geothermal fields. Its steam output is sold entirely to Aboitiz Power which converts it into ~300MW of electricity. The transaction will generate carbon offsets under the Task Force on Climate-Related Disclosures (TFCD) for the company. From a real-world perspective, it will not have practical impact on reducing emissions since it is only being transferred. SM will issue 17.4m of its own shares (valued at P903.88/share) to acquire the 81% stake in Allfirst. The valuation was based on cash flow of the existing geothermal fields and does not include the other concession areas PGPC holds. Management expects the deal to be accretive on the basis on 2021 earnings. This implies that Allfirst made at least P700m in earnings in 2021. PGPC sells its geothermal output at tariffs which are based on coal prices so earnings are likely to be much higher this year. The shares SM are currently trading at par with NAV.

MPI revives real estate ambitions in Landco buyout

MPI announced it will be converting P429mn worth of shareholder advances in Landco into equity, allowing it to own 100% of Landco (from 38%). The previous owner of Landco was Alfred Xerez-Burgos. Landco is an upscale developer of the 88-hectare Peninsula de Punta Fuego in Nasugbu, Batangas in 1995. Landco has a landbank of 15 hectares (largely in San Juan, Batangas). The takeover revives MPI’s ambitions in the real estate/hospitality sectors, in line with its plans to diversify out of highly regulated businesses. MPI currently has c. 330 hectares of raw land in San Juan, Batangas, which Regis never accounted for in their NAV, as this was inherited from Metro Pacific Corp. and had titling issues. MPI estimates the market value of its land in Batangas at P5.6bn, or <P2,000 per sqm. MPI is trading at a 53% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.93/sh.

Semirara, DMC declare first tranche of cash dividends for 2022

Semirara (SCC PM) declared a dividend of P1.50/sh (+20% higher vs. 1H21 div of P1.25/sh), equivalent to a yield of 5%. Ex-div is on April 11. The divs were lower than what Regis expected for 1H (P2-2.5/sh), given estimated cash levels. Regis believes the company could declare a chunkier dividend in 2H22, as Regis estimates free cash flow yield could be at least 16% this year.

 

DMCI (DMC PM) declared a dividend of P0.48/sh (unch vs. 1H21 div), equivalent to a yield of 5%. Ex-div is on April 12. In 2021, DMCI declared record divs of P0.96/sh. DMCI is trading at a 16% discount to its market-based NAV of P10.7/sh.

MREIT to acquire more office buildings from Megaworld

MREIT will acquire 44,567sqm of PEZA-accredited offices from Megaworld via share-for-property swap. The properties are Iloilo – Festive Walk 1B and Two Global Center; McKinley West (Fort Bonifacio) – One West Campus and Five West Campus. The average occupancy rate of 96%. The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 16% to 325,000 and the properties are valued at P5.3bn. MREIT will issue 263.7mn new shares at P20/share which was a premium to last closing price of P18.40/share when the news came out. MEG is trading at 64% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, 7.0X FY22E P/E. MREIT currently offers 5.4% dividend yield.

ACEN, ib vogt plan 1,000MW solar projects in Asia

ACEN Corporation has partnered with German solar developer ib vogt to build at least 1,000MW of renewable energy projects in Asia Pacific. The JV will focus on late-stage, shovel-ready projects in Indonesia, Vietnam, Malaysia, Laos, Bangladesh and other countries in the region. Under the deal, ACEN expects to invest up to $200mn equity investment in addition to debt funding. Most of the projects will be derived from ib vogt’s pre-existing projects from its Asia development pipeline that of 5,000MW. ACEN aims to have 5,000MW attributable renewable capacity by 2025. It now has more than 3,800MW of total attributable capacity of which 3,300 is renewable. ACEN is trading at 47.4x PE and 23.5x EV/EBITDA for FY22E.

PLDT (TEL PM) selects two bidders for its tower sale

PLDT has picked two bidders in relation to the process to sell half (6,000) of its telecom towers. Edgepoint was selected to acquire ~3,000 towers from PLDT in the Greater Manila area. edotco Group Sdn was picked to acquire another ~3,000 tower outside Manila. Edgepoint is a newly formed firm backed by DigitalBridge (a US-listed firm) and Abu Dhabi Investment Authority. edotco is a subsidiary of Malaysia telecom firm Axiata. The deals are expected to raise US$1.5bn for PLDT under a sale-and-leaseback arrangement. Regis believes the deals will be significantly value-accretive for PLDT.

 

Meralco applies for 14% increase in distribution tariffs for next regulatory period

Meralco has filed for an average tariff of ~P1.57/kwh for the 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) based on an order disclosed by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC). The tariff would represent an increase of 14% from the rate currently being collected (P1.38/kwh). The 5RP covers the period July 2022 to June 2026. The ERC has called for hearings on April 26 and 28 on this application. The tariff applied for by Meralco would likely result in earnings rising by ~70% from current levels. The proposed regulatory return (WACC) was not explicitly disclosed but we estimate it >13% based on the Meralco application. Part of Meralco’s application includes capex of P38bn per year, roughly double what it has been in recent years.

Inflation accelerates to 4.0% in March

Headline inflation rate rose to 4.0% in March from 3.0% the previous month. The street forecast ranged between +3.3% to +4.4% (median at +3.7%). BSP forecast ranged between +3.3% to +4.1%. Food inflation rate accelerated to +2.6%, with notable increases in corn (+31.3%, unch MoM), flour/bread (+3.5% from +3.1%), meat (+2.9% from +1.4%), and fish (+4.3% from +2.9%). Non-food inflation rate jumped to 5.0%, largely due to higher oil prices. Prices for utilities jumped +17% from +13% in Feb. The 1Q22 average inflation rate at 3.4%, within the BSP’s 2-4% target range. Policy interventions to manage supply and prices include:

Extension of lower tariffs on pork until Dec 2022

Accelerating release of imported port and cold storages

Fuel subsidies of P3k each to 158k corn farmers and fisherfolk / P6.5k fuel subsidy for 115k public utility drivers

Cash transfers (P500 per month) to poorest 50% of households

Metro Manila wage board junks P470 wage hike petition

The National Capital Region’s Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB-NCR) junked the petition of a labor group for a P470 hike in wages. Labor group, Trade Union Congress of the Philippines (TUCP), petitioned to raise the minimum wage in Metro Manila to P1,007 from P537. RTWPB reasoned that granting an across-the-board minimum wage hike does not fall within its jurisdiction, citing the Employers Confederation of the Philippines (ECOP) contention against the hike. RTWPB cited that its mandate is limited only to minimum wage fixing and determination in the region. TUCP also sought wage hikes in Central Visayas (+P430) and Davao regions (+P423). Minimum wages in NCR/Cebu/Davao regions were last adjusted in Nov 2018/Jan 2020/Feb 2019.

BIR says 5pct franchise tax due from PAGCOR licensees

The Philippines Bureau of Internal Revenue filed on Wednesday a clarification that it expects licensees and contractees of the country’s casino regulator, the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) to pay 5 percent franchise tax to the BIR on their income from operations. BIR said the clarification on the casino industry’s liabilities regarding franchise tax, income tax, and VAT was based on current laws and recent jurisprudence. The circular added that “the regulatory fees paid by contractees to PAGCOR are different and distinct from the 5 percent franchise tax payable to the BIR”. In 2014, Bloomberry petitioned the Supreme Court to nullify the provision of a 2013 BIR Memorandum that imposed corporate income tax on casinos. The High Court affirmed that Bloomberry, as a contracting party of PAGCOR was subject only to the 5% franchise tax on gross gaming revenue, in lieu of all taxes, as provided under the PAGCOR Charter. Bloomberry then said that the decision allowed PAGCOR and BRHI to revert to the original license fee structure of 15% for junket and 25% for mass licensee fee (inclusive of the 5% franchise tax).

Hot money inflows surge to US$274mn in February

Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. hot money) recorded a net inflow of US$274mn in February, up from a US$15mn in net inflow in Jan 2022 and a reversal of the US$40mn net outflow in Feb 2021. The US$945mn registered investments for February 2022 reflected an increase of 29% MoM (or by US$213mn) compared to the US$731mn recorded in January 2022, largely in PSE-listed securities. This brings YT-Feb foreign portfolio investments yielded net inflows of US$289mn, larger than the US$57mn net inflows in the same period last year.

 

Marcos keeps lead, Robredo gains in March 2022 survey

Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. kept his lead in the 2022 Presidential race while closest rival, Leni Robredo, gained 9points in the latest Pulse Asia survey. The survey had a sample of 2,400 respondents and was held from March 17-21. Robredo gained points in all locations, except NCR, where she lost 1pt; Gain attributed to ground campaigns. Marcos dropped 4pts from the Feb 2022 poll; While he kept his lead in Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao, Marcos dropped points in NCR (-2), Luzon ex-NCR (-4), Visayas (-5) and Mindanao (-6). Moreno (-2pts) and Pacquiao (-2pts) also dropped points. Robredo gained points in all locations, except NCR, where she lost 1pt; Gain attributed to ground campaigns. In the VP race, Sara Duterte strengthened her lead, gaining +3pts.

NPL ratio hits 3-month high in February

The NPL ratio of the banking industry rose to 4.24% in February coming from 4.14% in January 2022 and 4.08% in February 2021. Bad loans grew 2.4% MoM (9.6% YoY) to P472.664bn. The gross loan portfolio was flat MoM (+5.4% YoY) at P11.15trn. Past due loans rose 1.2% YoY to P558.0bn. The past due ratio was 5% of total loans (from 5.2% a year ago) . 

GIR rises in March

Based on preliminary data, the country’s gross international reserves (GIR) rose to US$108.54bn in end-March. This is up by US$740mn (+0.7%) MoM from a revised end-Feb GIR level of US$107.8bn (previously US$107.9bn). This is equivalent to 9.6 months’ worth of imports. The increase reflected proceeds of ROP Global bonds.

Gov’t revises 2020/2021 GDP growth numbers

The government revised annual 2021/2020 GDP estimates by industry and expenditure. 4Q21 GDP growth rate was revised upwards to 7.8% from 7.7% previously. Consequently, 2021 annual GDP growth rate was revised upwards to 5.7% from 5.6% previously.

Labor force data shows further improvements in February

The labor force participation rate increased to 63.8% in Feb, leading to a net employment creation of 2.5mn MoM. The unemployment rate was steady at 6.4% MoM but remained high relative to pre-pandemic levels (5.3%). The quality of jobs also improved as seen in the decline in the underemployment rate to 14.0% from 14.9% the previous month. The country eased restrictions in February, with NCR placed under Alert Level 2. As of April 1, 2022, 79% of the economy has been placed under alert level 1; However, face-to-face classes have not resumed.
