REGIS REPORT

10 June 2022

The PSEi dropped a sharp 3.1% WoW to close at 6530.04 index points.

The market was largely directionless all week amid a lack of positive catalysts. Sentiment continued to be hampered by global inflation and growth concerns. Heightened risk off sentiment at the close of the week sent the market significantly lower amidst a lack of liquidity/bids. Trading was lackluster – as daily average volumes reached only an anemic US$104m.

Foreigners were sellers for US$48.3m this week, with almost half of the week's outflows coming through on Friday. Multiple foreign baskets were seen selling across the board with biggest outflows spread across conglos, ports, and consumers.

Majority of the index names ended the week lower, with only five of the 30 index members recording gains. Top laggards were SM Investments (-5.2% WoW), ICTSI (-6.05% WoW), Ayala Corp (-9.9% WoW), and SM Prime (+8.5% WoW).  These names accounted for over half (53.4%) of the market's overall decline this week.

CPI inflation for May came in at 5.4%,  accelerating from 4.9% in April. Food and transport costs were the main contributors to the higher inflation print.

The CPI doesn’t seem to have peaked, however. Aside from high commodity prices fully catching up, a majority of regions (14 out of 17) have approved minimum wage increases, pointing to higher inflation going forward. The latest minimum wage increases outside Metro Manila are at around 12-16%, while for Metro Manila it is around +6%. Meanwhile, the  government has approved a P1.00 (+11%) increase in the minimum fares for jeepneys in Metro Manila and the surrounding regions. This should also lead to higher inflation in the coming months. 

Monetary authorities are already signaling more rate increases soon. Incoming BSP Governor Felipe Medalla says 25bp hikes are to be expected later June and in August. These would take the policy rate to 2.75%. Relatively low PHP interest rates should put pressure on the currency.

Adding to this pressure is the trade deficit. The trade deficit for April reached US$4.8bn for the month, up 54%. YT-April, the trade deficit was US$18.7bn (~US$4.7bn/month average), +64% higher YoY. The trade deficit is on pace to exceed last year’s record deficit of US$43bn last year, and looks to be headed to around ~15% of GDP.

Nonetheless, the recovery story is intact.  The numbers from Google show that mobility are now firmly above pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated again in April, up 6.9% YoY, marking the 13th consecutive month of YoY improvement. This was the fastest pace of YoY expansion since June 2020. Consumer loans turned the corner in 1Q22, growing 0.7% YoY, the first positive print since 4Q20.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Toyota prices increase across the board

Toyota implemented across-the-board price increases of c. 1-2% on June 1, to cushion impact of higher raw material costs. Toyota sources raw materials from ASEAN (in USD – 54%), Japan (in JPY – 10%), and local suppliers.

Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP trades at a 22% discount to its market-based NAV of P638/sh.

PH Banks - Consumer loans - 1Q22 back in +ve growth territory; NPLs improving

Consumer loans continue to recover in 1Q22, posting 0.7% YoY growth, back in positive territory for the first time since end-2020. This was despite a surprise deceleration in mortgages, which grew 5.3% YoY, down from 7.9% in 4Q21. In fact, mortgages were down 1.5% QoQ, the first QoQ decline since mid-2015. Regis suspects there may have been a bank that missed the deadline for submission.

Consumer NPL ratio also improved o 8.99%, down 32bps QoQ from 9.31% in 4Q21, and down from the peak of 9.90% in 3Q21.

Growth in 1Q22 was led by credit card (CC) receivables, which jumped 11.5% YoY, following four quarters of decline. In absolute PHP terms, the P446bn balance was just a shade below the all-time high posted in 1Q20 just before the pandemic hit. Credit card NPLs also improved to 6.54%, down 24bps from 6.78% in 4Q21 (the peak was 8.85% in 4Q20).

As mentioned, mortgages grew 5.3% YoY, though balances were down 1.5% QoQ. NPL ratios here were flat at 9.37%, from 9.36% the quarter prior.

Motor vehicle (MV) loans were still down 15.8% YoY and down 2.0% QoQ. While banks are anecdotally loosening credit standards, the balances are still shrinking due to the faster roll-off of existing loans. Banks also noted the lack of supply of vehicles, owing to the global supply bottlenecks. To the upside, NPL ratios improved to 11.42% from 12.01% in 4Q21.

Based on banks’ earnings calls, the industry seems to be ready to re-grow its consumer loan portfolio. This could boost NIMs, given the higher yields on consumer loans compared to corporate / commercial / mid-market loans. 

Malampaya supply contract for Ilijan’s 1,200MW expires

The 1,200MW Ilijan plant operated by KEPCO is no longer getting gas from Malampaya beginning June 6 after the gas-offtake agreement expired.

There was reportedly no keen interest on the part of PSALM or San Miguel to renew the gas contract with Malampaya.

The plant’s build-operate-transfer (BOT) contract is also supposed to lapse this month, and turnover talks are reportedly being sorted with SMC Global Power’s South Premier Power Corporation which is its IPPA

Under PSALM’s privatization terms for supply contracts, the ownership of the plant has to be turned over to the IPPA at zero value upon expiration of the BOT provided all outstanding obligations are mutually resolved

On the part of San Miguel group, it is already looking at a timeline of bringing in imported LNG by August this year, which should fuel the gas-fired facilities including Ilijan

The expiration of Ilijan’s contract means First Gen get full allocation of gas again, even with the declining output from Malampaya. Its San Gabriel power plant has largely been derated (210MW from 420MW) over the last few months because of gas restrictions, weighing on FGEN’s earnings over the last two quarters

Ilijan’s lack of gas supply over the next few months could reduce the grid’s capacity by 800MW or put pressure on prices as it sources replacement power

 

AC Energy repeals swap deal with unit

AC Energy Corp called off a deal that involves swapping some of its assets for shares in its oil and gas exploration unit.

ACEN disclosed to the PSE that its board of directors decided during a special meeting to cancel the property-for-shares swap and the deed of assignment it had entered into with ACE Enexor. The deal would have entailed ACE Enexor issuing 339mn shares to its parent firm for P10/share or a total of P3.4bn in exchange for ACEN’s five power assets (Palawan55 Exploration, Bulacan Power Generation Corp, CIP II Power Corp, Ingrid3 Power Corp, ne Subic Power Corp).

The agreement was meant to be part of ACEN’s goal of spinning off its thermal assets.

“ACEN will continue to look into options to optimize value for the assets, including selling these assets to an appropriate buyer no later than 2025”

Government turns over Ilijan plant to SMC Global Power

On June 3, the government turned over the 1,200 MW Ilijan plant to South Premiere Power Corp, a wholly-owned subsidiary of SMC Global Power Holdings Corp. 

This is in line with the 2010 administration agreement between South Premiere and PSALM. 

Before the turnover, the Ilijan facility was operated by Korea Electric Power Corp under a BOT contract which expired on June 4. PSALM supplied natural gas to the Ilijan power plant from Malampaya under a gas supply agreement with Shell Exploration BV which also expired June 4.  SMC is planning to source fuel from an LNG terminal that is being built by AG&P

BPO sector headcount grew 9% in 2021

The number of BPO employees grew 9% to 1.44mn in 2021. This is already higher than the target of 1.43mn employees in 2022. The industry added 120,000 employees in 2021.

BPO revenue expanded 11% to US$29.5bn in 2021. However, the industry only took up c. 100,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila in 2021 (net of space given up). The annual pre-COVID take-up of the BPO industry was 400,000-450,000sqm

Regis’ channel checks suggest that a number of BPO firms are looking to expand their office footprint, particularly since the government mandate requiring at least 70% of BPO employees to work in the office. However, demand does not appear to be at pre-COVID levels.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for: 57% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 47% of Megaworld’s NAV, and 32% of Robinsons Land’s NAV.

 

Prime Infra unit plans world’s largest solar farm

Razon-led Prime Infrastructure Hldgs plans to build a solar farm with a capacity of up to 3,500 MW plus a battery energy storage that can hold up to 4,500 MWH, according to president and CEO Guillaume Lucci.

The project will be under Terra Solar Philippines Inc, a unit of Terra Renewables. Terra Renewables partnered with Solar Philippines Power Project Hldgs. Terra Solar will supply 850 MW to Meralco from power generated by the facility, of which 600 MW will be available by 2026 and the rest will be delivered by 2027.

Jefferies coal price upgrades

Jefferies’ global commodities analyst, Chris Lafemina, published a very bullish report on thermal coal. He upgraded seaborne thermal coal price forecasts for 22E/23E/24E by 36%/89%/78% to US$320/255/178 per MT. He also upgraded his long-term coal price forecast by 25% to US$100/MT. These are lower than the current spot price of US$412/MT.

Assuming his revisions to Semirara (SCC PM), Regis DCF-based TP will go up to P41/sh (+64%) while our 2022E/2023E profit forecasts could more than double to P53bn/P36bn, holding other things constant.

At SCC’s current price and assuming higher coal price estimates, SCC’s FCF for 22F/23F could be as high at P11/sh (=32% yield) and P8/sh (23% yield).

Cement manufacturers seek extension of safeguard duty

Cement Manufacturers Association of the Philippines (CeMAP) is seeking to extend the safeguard duty on imported cement, which will expire in October. The safeguard duty is currently at P200 per MT (or P8 per 40-kg bag).

Regis estimates the safeguard duty currently accounts for 5% (US$4/MT) of an importer’s total cost (US$77/MT).

The safeguard duty is also c. 5% of the ASP of local manufacturers, such as Cemex Philippines (CHP PM).

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Diokno sees no need for spending cuts

Incoming Secretary of Finance and current BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno will not reduce spending to address the ballooning deficit. He will focus instead on raising enough taxes; He is confident of the tax system that the Duterte admin will leave. He believes recent tax reforms would provide the next administration with enough room to generate enough revenues.

The Bureau of the Treasury earlier estimated the government needs to raise P249bn every year in incremental revenues to avoid new borrowings. Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominiguez III has proposed a fiscal consolidation plan that involves imposing new tax measures, repealing some tax exemptions, and deferring the personal income tax reductions

Inflation jumps to 5.4% in May

Headline inflation jumped to 5.4% in May, up from 4.9% the previous month, broadly within consensus expectations (5.4%) and the BSP’s target band of 5-5.8%. This is the fastest pace since November 2018 (+6.1%).

Food inflation accelerated (+4.9% vs 3.8% the previous month) due to faster price increases in flour, meat, vegetables, fish, and oils/fat. Rice inflation dipped slightly to 1.5%; government yesterday announced an extension of lower rice tariffs to end-2022. The government also cut tariffs for corn (5% from 15% previously) and pork (15%-25% from 30-40% previously).

Transport inflation inched up further (+15% from +13%) due to higher gasoline and diesel prices. Inflation of electricity and LPG slowed to 19% from 20% the previous month. Prices of sin products (cigarettes, spirits, liquor, and beer) also jumped.

The latest print brings 5M22 inflation average to 4.1%, above the BSP’s 2-4% target band. The Monetary Board will meet on June 23.

New BSP head signals at least two more rate hikes; may cut reserve ratio

Incoming BSP Governor and current Monetary Board member Felipe Medalla signaled to raise policy rates to 2.75% from 2.25%. Around 25bps rate hike each will be implemented on June 23  and August 18.

Increases beyond this will be data-dependent. The BSP says it need not move in lockstep with the Federal Reserve. The Fed has hiked policy rates by 75bps so far this year and analysts expect 100bps hike in June and July meetings.

Mr. Medalla also plans to lower banks’ reserve ratio to 10% from 12% currently to counter the impact of stimulus withdrawal. A cut will likely be done a month or two before a rule that allowed lending to small businesses be counted as compliance to the requirement expires in December.

 

GIR level dips to US$103.5bn in May

The country’s GIR level, based on preliminary data, dipped by 1.8% (US$1.87bn) MoM to US$103.5bn in end-May, due to the national government’s foreign currency debt payments and various expenditures, as well as lower gold prices. The GIR is equivalent to 9.1 months’ worth of imports.

Mobility well above pre-Covid, focus on Workplace data

Mobility data is firmly above pre-pandemic levels, at least for the national figures, whether it be for retail/recreation, for grocery/pharmacy, for transit stations, or for the workplace. As before, Regis believes Metro Manila remains below pre-Covid levels because a) more companies are still on WFH or hybrid work mode, particularly for BPOs, b) adoption of e-commerce (online shopping or food delivery)  is higher in the capital, and c) there is still greater residual wariness over Covid in Metro Manila compared to the provinces.

Workplace data likely tells us that employment is pretty much back to 2019 levels. This would also explain why the consumption figures are so strong despite the inflationary environment. Filipinos are used to periods of high inflation – 2011 and 2018 are the most recent episodes (inflation ran up to 6.7% in 2018).

 

Government cuts tariffs on food, coal

The government issued an executive order modifying the tariff schedule for imported rice, pork, corn, and coal from countries outside Southeast Asia.

Tariffs on rice imports were reduced to 35% (from 50%) until Dec 31, 2022. Tariff rates on pork imports were reduced to 10-15% from 30-40% until Dec 31, 2022. Meanwhile, Corn tariffs were decreased to 5-15% from 35-50% until Dec 31, 2022. Tariffs for coal (7%) were removed until Dec 31, 2022.

In early May, the government also extended reduced tariffs on the same commodities from within southeast asia to end-2022

LTFRB approves P1 jeepney fare provisional hike in NCR, Central Luzon, and Calabarzon

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board approved a P1 provisional fare increase on minimum fares for public utility jeepneys (PUJs).

Minimum fares for the first four kilometers will rise to P10 beginning June 9. There’s no increase in fares for succeeding kilometers. Fare increase will cover PUJs in NCR, Region III, and Region IV.

Transport fares have a weight of 4.54% in the CPI basket

Trade deficit stays elevated in April

The country’s trade deficit stayed elevated at US$4.8bn in April vs US$5.0bn in March. This brough YT-April trade deficit to US$18.7bn, up +63% YoY

Imports grew at its slowest pace in 13 months. Imports rose 23% YoY in April from 28% YoY in March. This was due to slower growth in capital goods imports (+3% YoY) from the previous month (+10% YoY). Consumer goods import growth also slowed to +7% YoY from +9% due lower imports of non-durable goods (i.e. rice and fruits). Imports of fuel and raw material sustained strong increases.

Exports rose +6% YoY, broadly unchanged MoM.
