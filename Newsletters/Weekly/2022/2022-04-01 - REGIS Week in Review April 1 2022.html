REGIS REPORT

01 April 2022

The PSEi inched up 0.4% this week to close at 7152.88.   Volumes were quiet as geopolitical uncertainty continued amidst a lack of domestic catalysts.

Foreigners were sellers for US$10.4m this week. Biggest outflows were seen in Ayala Corp, Ayala Land, Metrobank, and Globe.  Inflows were in recent FTSE upweight Converge.

Gainers outnumbers losers 19:10. Consumer names continued to see respite from recent selldowns, with URC (+5.0% WoW) and Monde (+0.7% WoW) closing the week with gains. Retailer Puregold (+8.3% WoW), Wilcon (+3.0% WoW) , and Robinsons Retail (+0.5% WoW) also gained.

Among the biggest advances were in AC Energy (+4.8% WoW) and Converge (+5.3% WoW), both bolstered by foreign inflows.

GT Capital (+6.0% WoW) also led, despite FY21 profit falling short of estimates.  Management conveyed optimism about a full recovery of auto sales this year. Toyota is targeting to revert to pre-pandemic unit sales of 162k this yr (from ~130k units last year). 

Among the top decliners were telcos Globe (-4.5% WoW) and PLDT (-3.0% WoW) after surging +17.0%  and 9.7% respectively in the previous week. The incumbents reacted positively to the newly signed Public Service Act  which places constraints on third player Dito Telecommunity from receiving additional capital from China Telecom.

Emperador (-4.1% WoW) and parent AGI (-3.9% WoW) also fell. Also down were property names Ayala Land (-2.9% WoW) and Megaworld (-2.3% WoW).

D&L (-2.8% WoW) consolidated post its 12.3% WoW advance last week.  D&L's FY21 result came in slightly below consensus estimates as margins dragged. 

Union Bank (+6.2% WoW ) is proceeding with its stock rights offering, with plans to raise about P40bn to fund its  acquisition of Citigroup’s Philippine consumer banking business (for c. P55bn). 

Cebu Air (+6.7% WoW) advanced despite  CEO Lance Gokongwei still expecting losses for the airline this year.  Of note, domestic capacity is already at 96% of pre-pandemic levels.

Filinvest Land’s (-0.9% WoW) FY21 came in behind street forecasts. Notably, its reservation sales of P2.0bn for 4Q21, the lowest level for a single quarter in at least the past ten years. Meanwhile, the office segment’s vacancy rate (ex-REIT) is in the vicinity of 30%.

Vista Land (unch) is looking to list its own REIT, with a plan to spin of ten malls and two office buildings (leasable area of 256,000 sqm or close to 25% of VLL’s rental portfolio).

GT Capital - Auto, bank segments rebound in 4Q21
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

4Q21 profit falls YoY on absence of lot sales

GTCAP reported headline and core profit of P10.9bn in FY21, behind street estimates. 4Q21 core profit fell YoY due to the absence of lot sales but profits from its banking and auto units were sharply improved QoQ/YoY, the latter due to the receipt of tax credits. Management conveyed optimism about a full recovery of auto sales and stronger credit growth this year.

4Q21 profit lifted by tax incentives

The auto segment’s 4Q21 profit jumped strongly QoQ/YoY due largely to a P1bn tax credit booked in November. Unit sales recovered QoQ as mobility restrictions eased. However, GP/operating profit margins dove to lows of 9%/3% due to (a) unfavorable model mix, (b) a weaker PHP vs USD, and (c) higher promotional/advertising costs. Management noted that Toyota Motor Phils (TMP) has been raising prices to protect margins due to the unfavorable currency outlook. It is also targeting to revert to pre-pandemic unit sales of 162k this year.

Lower provisions drive strong 4Q21 profit for the bank

Its banking unit doubled profits in 4Q due to a sharp drop in provision expense. Provisions seemed to have normalized, despite a slight uptick in NPL ratio to 2.2% (vs 2.1% in 3Q). NPL cover remains quite high at 175% as of 4Q21. While loans still declined by 1% YoY in 4Q21, this improved from the 4% decline in 3Q21. Management expects loans to grow this year, in line with GDP growth.

Property reservation sales bounce in 4Q

Implied 4Q21 profit for Federal Land declined QoQ, even with a slight uptick in revenues. Reservation sales were up 92% QoQ and declined by just 7% YoY. For 2022, the company is looking to sustain the recovery of reservation sales with two project launches this year, which will include portions of its JV project with the SM Group. Meanwhile, the company revealed details of its JV with Japan[1]based Nomura Real Estate. Federal Land’s injection of cash and land into the JV will give it a 66% stake. The JV’s first projects will be launched in Metro Manila and Cavite in mid-2023 and 4Q23.

 
Wilcon Depot - Limited margin upside amidst volatile cost environment; maintain Hold
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Cost inflation poses risk to margins

WLCON’s dominance in the home improvement retail space continues to be a competitive advantage amidst a highly inflationary environment. During our CEO Roundtable Series, WLCON’s President and CEO Lorraine Belo-Cincochan shared her thoughts on how she expects their business to operate under the current environment. Historically, WLCON’s customers have been able to absorb price adjustments and keep margins largely intact. However, current supply chain bottlenecks could drive up working capital requirements in the near term.

Historical margins have been neutral to cost inflation

According to Ms Belo-Cincochan, WLCON’s business has historically not been significantly affected by inflationary pressures. Any price adjustments implemented by suppliers are passed on to consumers, who are typically able to absorb these price adjustments. Unlike fast-moving consumer goods, WLCON’s products are considered essentials (e.g. toilets, sinks) and purchases of larger ticket items are usually planned months in advance. Demand is relatively inelastic for these products.

Working capital requirements to remain elevated amidst supply chain bottlenecks

Ms Belo-Cincochan recognized that there is upward pressure on expenses given rising fuel cost and logistics. Moreover, supply chain bottlenecks, particularly in China (where majority of merchandise is sourced), will continue to drive up funds tied up in working capital. Management indicated that inventory levels could remain elevated at >200 days even as turnover improves.

Strong 2022 top line, market leadership a key advantage

We believe WLCON should see strong top line growth in 2022 as mobility improves and smaller construction projects resume. Ms Belo-Cincochan noted that they are seeing sales of hard lines picking up in early 1Q, with independent contractors starting to return. The competitive environment remains fairly benign, even with the launch of IKEA Philippines – which Ms Belo-Cincochan largely views as a complement to their business rather than a competitor. We believe smaller hardware stores could feel even more pressure amidst the high inflation environment. The outlook for 2022 earnings remains largely positive, but the stock looks fully valued at 33x 2022E PE. In our view, there is limited upside to near-term forecasts given the volatile cost environment. We refresh our forecasts for FY21 results and tweak our TP to P28/sh (-3%). Maintaining Hold.

 
Philippine Retail - Philippine Seven Corp. (SEVN PM): The road to recovery
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

The road to recovery

Philippine Seven is recovering from Covid lows, even if the pace of recovery has been uneven across its store network. In our latest CEO Roundtable, SEVN’s CEO Victor Paterno shared how his business has adapted to the changing landscape and lingering effects of the pandemic. He believes the company is in a comfortable position as competition has been benign, even with the rapid expansion of Alfamart, which he believes does not directly compete with 7-Eleven. The company’s digital strategy will continue, though the focus will be on monetizing existing assets.

Store expansion resumes in 2022

After holding back on store expansion over the last two years, SEVN is resuming growth with 300 new stores in 2022. Mr. Paterno believes that while consumer mobility has improved, the pandemic has some lingering effects, and that these pandemic-induced behaviors will drive the direction of store expansion moving forward. Expansion will be less focused on CBD areas where recovery has lagged, and would favor faster-growing underpenetrated areas, where stores can be more profitable. This will mean larger stores (~40% larger vs current average footprint of ~100sqm) + wider assortment.

Corporate News
 
 
 
 

Cebu Pacific expects lower losses this year

Cebu Pacific expects to reduce losses this year, as domestic travel strongly recovers according to President and CEO Lance Gokongwei. While the company expects revenue to revert to 60-70% of 2019 levels, higher fuel prices will likely mean the airline will continue to post losses but at a reduced rate. Regis assumes CEB’s 2022E revenues will be 66% of 2019. Additionally, Regis expects a profit of P5bn for 2022E based on an average jet kero price assumption of US$80/bbl (vs spot US$130/bbl). Every 10% increase in jet kero prices will reduce CEB’s EBITDA and net profit by 4%/12%. Currently, CEB is operating at 96% of its pre-pandemic domestic capacity and is looking to restore more than 100% in April. They increased flights to Boracay and Cebu; Flights to these locations have exceeded 2019 frequencies. CEB is trading at 5.4x FY22 EV/EBITDA, lower than historical average (7x) and peers (13x).

Filinvest Land 4Q21 reservation sales drops to lowest level in over 10 years

Filinvest Land’s 4Q21 reservation sales fell 71% QoQ and 39% YoY to P2.04bn. This is the lowest quarterly presales level in over 10 years. FY21 reservation sales increased 5% to P16.04bn, below REGIS forecast. Despite missing Regis FY21 residential revenue forecast, net profit of P3.8bn (+2% YoY) was in line with estimates (but behind consensus). This could be due to non-operating items (such as a tax benefit). Filinvest might have suffered from residential cancellations in 4Q21. The company will hold a briefing next week. FLI is trading at 64% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 6.5X FY22E P/E.

Megawide officers file motion to quash anti-dummy case

During the arraignment, Megawide’s four (4) directors and officers entered a plea of "not guilty" to the alleged violation of the Anti-Dummy Law. The directors/officers include Edgar Saavedra, President and CEO of Megawide. .Respondents also filed a Motion to Quash, stating that the Information does not allege an offense, given that the signing into law of Republic Act No. 11659, otherwise known as the Amended Public Service Act. The new law removed foreign ownership limits of airports and has therefore rendered the legal issue at hand moot and academic. Megawide owns a 60% stake in the Mactan Cebu Airport; Its partner, India-based GMR Infrastructure, owns 40%, Regis estimates the airport is worth P13bn (=100% equity value) or an attributable value of P7.8bn or P3.87/sh. The hearing for the Motion to Quash is set on April 25, 2022. Megawide is trading at 12.9x FY22E EV/EBITDA, below historical average (16x).

Vista Land to raise up to P9.2bn from REIT listing

Vista Land will spin off 10 malls and two PEZA-registered offices into a REIT with a total GLA of 256,000sqm. The deal would have around 3.33bn secondary shares with an overallotment option of 333.75mn shares. The maximum offer price was set at P2.50/share and implies a maximum market cap of P25bn. The malls are located in: Las Pinas, Bacoor, General Trias, Imus, Tanza, Antipolo, San Jose Del Monte, San Fernando, and Talisay in Cebu. The office buildings are in Taguig and Bacoor. As of 3Q21, Vista Land had total debt of P167bn and net debt of P104bn and a net debt/equity of 93%. At the maximum offer price, Vista’s NAV could rise by 5%-10%. VLL is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, 4.3X FY22E P/E.

 

UnionBank to conduct stock rights offering

UnionBank could raise P51.6bn to P59.0bn through a stock rights offering. The bank previously said it would raise up to P40bn in its rights offering. It will list up to 800mn new common shares. The right offering will be 1 rights share for every 1.9 UBP shares held. The offer price range will be P64.55/share to P73.78/share. UBP’s last traded price was P97.80/share when the news came out. Ex-Date date will be April 6 and the offer period will be  from April 25 to May 6. The tentative listing date has been set to May 16. The proceeds will be used to fund UnionBank’s acquisition of Citigroup’s Philippine consumer banking business. UBP is trading at 1.3X FY21 P/BV (of P73.07/share).

U/KBs loan growth accelerates to 8.8% in Feb

Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, grew at a faster rate of 8.8% YoY in February. The BSP cited easing COVID-19 restrictions drive the improvement in mobility and market demand. Driven by faster credit growth for wholesale and retail trade, as well as credit card loans. Auto loans posted a smaller drop of 5.2% (vs 5.6% the previous month). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth was steady at 8.5% YoY in February.

 
 
Macro News
 
 
 
 

Infra spend rises by 1.5% YoY in December

The national government spent P134.7bn for infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in December 2021, up by P2bn/1.5% YoY. The gov’t cited releases for the AFP Modernization Program and the construction of smart campuses in Marawi. The govt also attributed to high base effects from the timing of payments for DOTr ODA-funded rail projects in Dec 2020, which amounted to P12.5bn (vs. only P2.5bn in Dec 2021). For FY21, overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) jumped by 29% YoY to P1.124tn, equivalent to 5.8% of GDP. For FY22, the government is aiming to spend P1.27tn (+13% YoY) on infrastructure, or 5.9% of GDP.

Phivolcs declares Alert Level 3 on Taal Volcano

The Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (Phivolcs) raised the alert status of Taal Volcano from Alert Level 2 (increasing unrest) to Alert Level 3 (magmatic unrest). Some nearby communities have been evacuated. Taal Volcano is about 60km south of Metro Manila.

Metro Manila under Alert Level 1 until April 15

Metro Manila will remain under Alert Level 1 until April 15. More than 60 areas on the main Luzon island, more than 80 areas in Visayas, and 50 areas in Mindanao would also be under Alert Level 1. Daily coronavirus infections in the Philippines were among the lowest among southeast Asian nations. The daily average infections was at 389 on March 21 to 27, 24% lower than a week earlier.

BSP chief: Inflation may breach March target

Headline inflation could accelerate beyond the central bank’s target of 3.3-4.1% (point inflation target at 3.7%) in March. February inflation rate stood at 3.0%. Street forecasts at +3.6% for March. The BSP cited oil price hikes along with high electricity rates in Meralco (MER PM) service areas, higher meat prices, and the peso depreciation are the primary sources of inflationary pressures during the month. The PSA will release inflation data on April 5. BSP Governor Diokno has said they are looking to start increasing interest rates by the second half of the year (to 2.5%), noting the key policy rate could reach 2.75% by 2023.

Budget deficit narrows in February

The National government’s budget deficit narrowed to P106bn in Feb, down 9% YoY. Tax revenues dropped 3% YoY due to lower collections from the BIR (-11% YoY). Expenses also fell 5% YoY on high base effects from the stimulus under Bayanihan II. Excluding last year’s stimulus, expenses would have grown by 9.6% YoY due to higher national tax allotments to LGUs; health and education programs; and funds for the COMELEC. This brings YT-Feb budget deficit to P129bn, lower YoY.

National government debt at P12tn in end-Feb

The national government’s outstanding debt hit a record of P12.09tn in February. This is higher by P63.83bn (+0.5% MoM) from January 2022 due to currency fluctuations, and net financing from both local and foreign sources. Around 70% of debt stock are from domestic lenders while 30% are from external sources. In March 2022, the government raised US$2.25bn from its triple tranche USD-denominated bond offering, as well as P458bn of retail treasury bonds. The government’s debt stock reached a record of P11.7tn in end-2021, equivalent to 60.5% of GDP.

 

Power prices increase as demand inches up

Average electricity spot market prices rose in March as margins thinned with power demand breaching pre-pandemic levels, according to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines. In a virtual briefing, the IEMOP said average wholesale electricity spot market prices increased from P6.19/kWh last February to P6.97/kWh in March. The average margin for March decreased by 4.04% due to increasing demand. Peak demand was recorded on March 23 at 13,749MW, surpassing the peak demand in February at 11,985MW. Luzon peak demand reached 11,617MW.
