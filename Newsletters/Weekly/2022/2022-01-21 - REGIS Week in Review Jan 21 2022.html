REGIS REPORT

21 January 2022

The Phils had a lackluster week, inching up marginally (0.4%) after last week’s 3.5% surge, as foreign outflows weighed.

Foreigners were sellers for US$25m as investors continued to take gains off the table; selling was widespread with largest outflows spread across consumer/property/banks.

The 7day moving average (33k) of new COVID cases has come off from the 35k peak and case counts in Metro Manila have dropped over the last few days. While even the government admits the numbers are likely to be understated, hospital occupancy (ICE beds) has remained manageable at 50% nonetheless.

Ayala Corp (+1.4% MoM) and Ayala Land (-4.3% MoM) announced that ALI will be acquiring certain assets from AC valued at P17.3bn. ALI will be paying for the assets with new shares to be issued to AC at a price of P55.80/share – a premium of over 50% from ALI’s latest price.  The swap streamlines the ownership of the assets, placing them within ALI and simplifying the corporate structure. AC’s stake in ALI will rise to 47.2% (from 46.1%) as a result of this transaction.

The market was mixed, with 16 gainers and 13 losers for the week.

Power names were strong this week – led by AP (+7.6% WoW), which pulled parent AEV (+4.9% WoW) up as well. The power grid operator National Grid Corp (NGCP) warned that the Luzon grid could experience power outages during the peak of the dry season this year.  This highlights the thin power reserves within the sector and tight power supply over the next few years, from which generators like  AP should stand to benefit.

Also among the top gainers were Meralco (+2.7% WoW), on expectations of a decision on tariffs for the lapsed regulatory period (from July 2015) coming imminently, thus removing an overhang on the stock. A tariff reduction retroactively applied to July 2015 is not likely to impact Meralco negatively as it has already provisioned as if a tariff cut was implemented in 2015. Should there be no retroactive calculation for its rates, Meralco would keep the P1.38/kwh it has been charging and could see a huge windfall (US$1bn, pretax) from a reversal of provisions.

RE play AC Energy (+1.6% WoW) also gained, as did recent listing Solar Philippines Nueva Ecija (+14.6% WoW).  SPNEC says it is eyeing the injection of “over 10,000MW” worth of planned projects in exchange for the issuance of ~24bn new shares (current shares at 8.1bn).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

CEB seats drop below 40% of pre-Covid in week of Jan 10

The latest airline capacity survey from Jefferies showed Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) weekly seat capacity dropped to 45k or about 38% of pre-Covid levels in the week beginning January 10.  This came off from 57k (c. 50% of pre-Covid) in the first week of the 2022 and the December 2021 average of 52k weekly seats (or 44% of pre-Covid levels).  The drop reflects tighter travel restrictions following the surge in cases in Metro Manila and the subsequent downgrade of mobility to Alert Level 3. Regis suspects capacity will drop further in subsequent weeks, as Metro Manila remains under Alert Level 3 until January 31 and cases rise in other areas.  However Regis believes it is still premature to be making downward adjustments to our forecasts. Cases in Metro Manila have dropped for four straight days and hospitalization rates seem to have plateaued.  To recap, our forecasts assume capacity will revert to an average of 70% of pre-Covid levels this year, with 1Q22 at 40%, gradually rising to 90% by year-end.  CEB’s shares have dropped 16% since its recent peak in early November, underperforming the PCOMP by 13% over the period. It currently trades at 5.2x EV/EBITDA, below peers (16x).

 

Iberdrola enters Philippines market

Spanish clean energy developer Iberdrola has signed an agreement with Stream Invest Holding AG and Triconti ECC Renewables – largest independent wind developer, allowing the company to secure an option to enter five offshore wind projects. The projects have wind energy service contracts awarded to Triconti by the DOE. These project sites, located in shallow waters off the coast of areas in Luzon and Visayas are the first offshore wind service concessions awarded in the country. Iberdrola operates 34.9GW of renewables globally and now adds the Philippines to its pipeline of over 81.5GW over renewable projects under development.

Solar PH eyes swap of 20 projects with SPNEC shares

Solar Philippines Power Projects Holdings is open to swapping its solar portfolio with over 10,000MW of developments for about 24bn shares of SPNEC.  SPNEC’s current outstanding shares stands at 8.124bn. SPNEC has not yet approved any asset-for-share swap deal which is subject to a third-party valuation.  SPNEC has also set a stockholders’ meeting on March 7 to seek approval for the increase of its authorized capital stock to 50bn shares from 10bn shares.  

Ayala Land Board approves property-for-share swap with AC and Mermac

Ayala Land will issue 312mn new ALI shares to parent Ayala Corp and Mermac at P55.80/share (310mn ALI shares to Ayala Corp, 2mn shares to Mermac). This will increase Ayala Land’s shares outstanding by 2%. 

AC and Mermac will exchange five assets worth P17.4bn for the ALI shares. 

The largest portion (P13.2bn) is for a 50% stake in the ALP project Park Central Towers and the land on which Manila Peninsula is built. 

Since the ALI shares to be issued will be priced close to our P57.50/share NAV, the transaction should be mildly NAV accretive (less than 1%) for Ayala Land. ALI is currently trading at 41% discount to NAV, 1.9X FY22E P/B, 18.8X FY22E P/E.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Remittances up 5% in November

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 5.1% YoY to US$2.5bn in November. By region, remittances from the Middle East, Asia, USA remained robust while sea-based remittances were up 1% YoY while in PHP terms, November remittances were up +9.7% YoY. This bring the 3-month moving average growth rate steady at +4.2%. The YT-November remittances are up 5.3% YoY to US$28.4bn, below the BSP’s forecast (+6% YoY). For 2022, the BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +4% YoY (vs. 2010-2020 CAGR of +5%).

Pace of vaccinations rises further

As of January 16, the 7-day average vaccinations figure climbed to 797,265/day from 644,363/day the previous week. Around 51% of the Philippine population (55.2mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 214mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 119mn doses (as of January 16). The Philippines will start administering booster shots in pharmacies beginning January 21. This will first be implemented in Metro Manila and then rolled out to other areas. The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22.

Daily case count drops in NCR for third straight day

Moody’s downgrades PH GDP growth rate to 5.6% due to Omicron

Moody’s Analytics expects the Philippine economy to grow by 5.6% this year, below Regis and consensus median forecast of +6.5%. This is also lower than government’s target range of +7-9%.  This is lower than its previous forecast of +6.4% released in October 2021. Moody’s cited the Omicron-driven surge in COVID cases and the return of movement restrictions.
