REGIS REPORT

09 September 2022

The Phils dropped for a third week, as prospects of continued tightening by the Fed influenced sentiment across the globe.  The PSEi slid another 1.3% this week to close at 6606, taking YTD losses to 7.25%.

Losses were across the board; 19 of the 30- index members closed the week in the red.

The list of laggards was populated by various sectors. Topping the decliners was ACEN (-7.9% WoW), followed by JG Summit (-7.5% WoW) and Puregold (-5.6% WoW).  Megaworld (-4.8% WoW) also fell as did other property names Ayala Land (-4.3% WoW), Robinsons Land (-1.3%. WoW), and SM Prime (-0.3% WoW)

Telcos  Converge (-4.4% WoW), PLDT (-0.9% WoW) and Globe (-4.7% WoW) also closed lower. Globe released details for its upcoming rights issue. From the initial plan of raising up to a maximum of P32bn, Globe will now be raising P17bn (US$300m) instead with a ratio of 1 share for every 13.2366 at an offer price of 1680/sh. Given the terms, Globe will be issuing a maximum of approximately 10.1m new shares, an increase in its share count by ~7.7%. Given the price discount of the rights shares, the theoretical ex-rights is 2061, -1.4% vs the latest Globe price (P2090).

Other conglos also fell, with Ayala Corp (-3.0% WoW), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-2.7% WoW), San Miguel Corp (-1.3% WoW), and Alliance Global (-2.6% WoW) closing lower.  

Meanwhile, banks Metrobank (+1.1% WoW) gained as did BPI (+0.1% WoW) and Union Bank (+1.2% WoW). Consumer stocks were among the gainers; URC (+2.5% WoW), Wilcon (+0.3% WoW), and Jollibee (+0.1% WoW) closed higher.

Foreigners were net buyers for US$20 this week, with inflows seen mostly in San Miguel. Large inflows were also seen in Phinma (+2.3% WoW). Meanwhile, outflows were seen in property names Ayala Land and SM Prime.

As the dollar trended higher, the PHP slid for its sixth straight week (-0.1%) to close at PHP56.827/US$, afer closing as low as PHP57.15/US$ on Wednesday.  Despite CPI in August slightly dipping in August (to 6.3%),  the weakening PHP could prompt another hike from the Monetary Board as they meet on 22 Sept for its policy meeting.

Megawide - Sells entire stake in airport unit to Aboitiz group
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Divests airport unit

MWIDE announced that it is divesting its entire 60% stake in GMR[1]Megawide Cebu Airport Corporation (GMCAC), the holder of the 25-year concession for the Mactan Cebu airport. MWIDE’s partner, GMR Infrastructure, will also divest its 40% stake in GMCAC. The shares will be sold to Aboitiz InfraCapital, Inc, a unit of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM). The deal will, on our estimates, be significantly accretive to MWIDE and should ease concerns about its balance sheet.

Value crystallized

Aboitiz is buying the shares at a 100% equity value of P25bn, broken down as (1) P9.5bn in cash for 33.33% in GMCAC and (2) P15.5bn in exchangeable notes that will mature on October 30, 2024 and are exchangeable for 66.67% in GMCAC. The transaction is subject to certain conditions, which are expected to be fulfilled after approximately 105 days from today. Considering the net debt of GMCAC, we estimate the deal is valued at an enterprise value of P51bn or 22x on “normalized” EV/EBITDA. Note that the airport’s unit’s EBITDA as of 1H22 was only P166mn, still far from its 2019A EBITDA of P2.3bn. The divestment valuation of P25bn is also higher than our estimated valuation of P13bn for the airport, which is equivalent to 17x EV/EBITDA.

Potential NAV accretion of c. P2.8/sh (+39%)

After removing friction costs, we estimate MWIDE’s net proceeds will be c. P13.5bn. Given that the transaction price is higher than our current valuation for the airport, we estimate potential NAV accretion of cP2.80/sh (+39%).

Eases balance sheet concerns

Liquidity risks have been an issue for MWIDE since the pandemic began. This deal will allow the company to reduce its parent net debt (incl. prefs) to P16.5bn from P30bn, net debt-to-equity to 2.0x from 3.7x, and net debt-to-EBITDA (22E) to 4.4x from 8x. Even though the post-deal leverage ratios still appear high, liquidity risks are now more manageable, which should ease a key overhang on the share price.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Building a winning streak; raising TP to P11.70 (+15%)
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Back to basics; reiterate BUY

We assess Bloomberry’s post-pandemic prospects after a stellar 2Q saw revenues near peak levels despite the absence of Chinese tourists. Over the course of the pandemic, BLOOM has consolidated its hold on licenses and sites that would make them the only operator in some of the most attractive areas in the Philippines. This deliberate shift back to the domestic market not only sets up the harvest of low hanging fruit; it also enhances its product offering to foreigners when they eventually return. At 7.6x FY23E EV/EBITDA on just Solaire’s earnings, we reiterate our BUY rating with a target price of P11.70 (+15%).

Recovery well under way despite surge in Covid cases

Despite the recent surge in cases, indications suggest that the market is well beyond the point of rising Covid cases and new variants causing drops in visitation. We expect a return to pre pandemic levels by 1Q23, despite the virtual (and rather literal) absence of Chinese VIPs.

Playing the long game

Amidst the lockdowns, BLOOM has been leading the consolidation and expansion of the sector ahead of a re-opening. It was the first to get a license for online casino operations and is in serious discussions to acquire two casino projects of Dennis Uy’s PH Resorts. It also acquired a beachfront property just outside Manila for development into another integrated/golf resort. Its Solaire North project, located in the most populous city of Metro Manila, is slated for opening in 2023 and will increase its capacity by more than 50%.

Target of P11.70/share at 10x Solaire’s FY23 EBITDA; risks

We tweak our target price by 15% (to P11.70) as we roll forward to 2023. We ascribe a target multiple of 10x EV/EBITDA to Solaire’s FY23E EBITDA and add the value of unused landbank. At our TP, BLOOM would trade at 13.5x consolidated EBITDA for FY22E and 10.2x for FY23E – in line with historical multiples and below Macau (record low GGR and at risk for gaming concession revamp). Key risks include regulation, prolonged restrictions on travel, competition and new lockdowns.

 
Corporate News
 
 
 
 

Toyota hikes prices anew

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) has implemented its fourth price hike this year on its various models. The latest round of price hike of between 1-2% on selected models took effect last Sep 1. The latest price hikes resulted in higher prices for the Raize, Avanza, and Veloz models at an average of P15,000 to P20,000 or 1-2%. Other models affected are: Vios, Innova, HiAce Cargo, Coaster, Alphard, Commuter Deluxe. TMP implemented price hikes in January, June, and July. Over 50% of TMP’s COGS is USD-linked while around 10% is JPY-linked. As a result, every 5% depreciation/appreciation of PHP vs USD is 40% reduction/increase in profit, ceteris paribus. Regis estimates that every 5% depreciation of PHP vs JPY is 8% reduction/increase in profit, ceteris paribus. Regis forecast assumes GPMs will drop to 8.7% for the rest of the year, after averaging 10.1% in 1H22. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/gross asset value. GTCAP is trading at a 44% discount to its market-based NAV of P914/sh and 7.6x FY22E PER.

 

Megawide to foray into data centers

Megawide plans to embark into data centers according to chief business development officer Jaime Raphael Feliciano. Megawide has started negotiating with tech giants looking for hubs where they can locate their data requirements. Regulatory filings will be made soon to reveal Megawide’s foreign partner in putting up the digital facilities. PLDT (TEL PM) leads the market with 10 data centers in its portfolio, as it awaits the completion of its 11th and largest facility in Laguna.

ERC considers rate hike bid of Meralco, SMC power units

The temporary rate hike petitions that were jointly filed by Meralco and SMC Global Power units are now up for deliberation at the ERC. Meralco presented various scenarios – all of which would lead to higher electricity rates. The first scenario presented by Meralco is the one where the CIC claim by SMC Global Power is approved, which would result in an increase of P1.57/kWh vs the original rate over a six month period. All other scenarios presented by Meralco (emergency PSAs, sourcing from WESM, etc) would result in higher rates than the CIC claim. “Meralco believes that it can continue supplying power to its customers at the least cost by preserving the PSAs while addressing the financial concern of power suppliers through recognition of the CIC claim”.

Shell refuses to deliver banked Malampaya gas to San Miguel

The Malampaya consortium and its operator SPEx continues to drag its feet on the delivery of the banked natural gas to San Miguel Corp. The consortium claimed that delivery could affect the pressure of the gas reservoir at the gas field. PNOC earlier said SPEx had failed to comply with the directives of DOE requiring it to immediately resume supply to the Ilijan plant.

Implications of P420bn RTBs on banks

The banking industry’s total deposit base is about P16.5trn, of which P13.9bn is in PHP. The P420bn worth of RTBs is equivalent to roughly 2.5% of total deposits, and just over 3.0% of PHP deposits. Assuming all of the RTBs will be funded by transfers out of deposits (not perfect, but assuming), then the RTBs could be a significant liquidity reduction to the system.

The total LDR of the banking system was about 69% as of June 2022. The problem is that only less than 8% of total loans is in FX (largely USD). Regis estimates that the FX LDR is only about 33%. This means that the PHP LDR is closer to 76%. In theory, the PHP LDRs could jump to over 78% as a result of the RTB. (It’s actually not as simple that, since the government will be using that money for operations, which eventually goes back into the economy.

But again, just simplistically…) No need for panic yet, though. The RRR is just 12% for universal/commercial banks (UKBs) and much lower for thrift banks and rural banks. This means that the theoretical limit for PHP LDR is 88% (or higher). But as discussed, a large fiscal deficit means the government needs to borrow more. Over time, government could effectively “crowd out” the private sector for funds.

PAGCOR issues CDO against Kazuo Okada board

PAGCOR has issued a cease and desist order on September 2 against Kazuo Okada for allegedly usurping the board of Tiger Resort and Entertainment (TRLEI), operator of Okada Manila from the group (Tiger Resort Asia Limited or TRAL) led by his estranged son Tomohiro Okada. In its opinion, the Justice department said that the Status Quo Ante order merely required the return of Kazuo as a stockholder, director, chairman and CEO but did not empower Kazuo to form a new TRLEI board and make himself sole representative. The previous PAGCOR board sanctioned the installation of Kazuo Okada as TRLEI Chairman and his representatives (Cojuangco, Espeleta, etc) to various positions in the board. PAGCOR ordered Okada Manila management to allow nominees of TRAL access to Okada Manila premises and assume operation and management.

 

 
 
Macro News
 
 
 
 

Inflation at 6.3% in August

Headline inflation was recorded at 6.3% in August. The median forecast: +6.4%, BSP: 5.9%-6.7%. The print is mildly moderating from 6.4% in July due to slower growth in food and transport prices. Food inflation slowed to 6.2% from 6.9% (due to meat, fish, vegetables). Operation of transport equipment grew by 35% YoY in August from 48% YoY in July (regional gasoline/diesel prices rose 28%/71% YoY in August from 57%/95% YoY in July). MoM, CPI rose by 0.4%, slowest since May due to drops in fuel and meat prices. The regional gasoline/diesel prices dropped by 17%/12% MoM in August. Meanwhile, core inflation (i.e. ex-energy, food) climbed to 4.6% in August from 3.9% in July, depicting underlying demand-side pressures. The YT-August inflation at 4.9%, above the BSP’s target band of 2-4%. The next monetary board meeting is on Sep 22; policy rate currently at 3.75% (+175bps from end-2021). The Fed meeting is set on Sep 21; US Fed funds rate at 2.25%-2.5% (+225bps from end-2021).

GIR settles at US$98.98bn in August

The country’s gross international reserves stood at US$98.98bn in August. US$863mn (-0.9%) lower MoM and is down from US$109bn in end-2021. However, this is still above end-2019 GIR level of US$87.8bn (~7.7 import cover). The gov’t cited payments of the government’s foreign currency debt obligations and downward adjustments in BSP’s gold holdings due to lower gold prices. August GIR level represents 8.3 months’ worth of imports and 7.1x the country’s short-term external debt.

Gov’t raises P420bn from local-currency retail bond

The Philippine government raised P420bn from local-currency retail bonds. The bonds are due in Mar 2028 and pay a coupon of 5.75%. Of the total sold, P108.5bn pesos were switch subscriptions which were a record high. This also accounted for 17% of the government planned borrowings this year. As at end-July, the national government debt stood at P12.89tn, 0.8% up MoM and 9.9% higher than in end-Dec 2021. Domestic debt accounted for 68.5% while external debt accounted for 31.5%.
