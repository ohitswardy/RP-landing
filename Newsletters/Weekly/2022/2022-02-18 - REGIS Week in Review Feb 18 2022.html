REGIS REPORT

18 February 2022

The PSEi gained 2.0% this week, bouncing from last week’s PSEi rebal related decline.  While investors grappled with geopolitical uncertainty and Fed moves, waning new Covid case counts and recovering mobility boosted reopening optimism.

Gains were widespread with gainers outnumbering losers 4:1.

Leading the charge was JG Summit (+8.8% WoW), recovering from last week’s 7.6% drop.  Property names Robinsons Land (+8.3% WoW) and Ayala Land (+6.7% WoW) were strong.  Aboitiz Power (+7.2% WoW) continued to be strong, pulling up parent Aboitiz Equity (+6.4% WoW) as well.  ICTSI (+5.3% WoW) and Security  Bank (+5.2% WoW) also gained.

Foreigners ended the week sellers for US$27.7m with bulk of the outflows in PSEi recent adds/upweights Monde (-11.6% WoW) and Converge (-4.0% WoW). Other telcos Globe (+0.4% WoW) and PLDT (-2.6% WoW) also saw heavy outflows. Meanwhile, foreigners were seen flocking into property large caps Ayala Land and SM Prime (+1.1%  WoW).

Jollibee Foods (2.9% WoW) turned in a solid performance this week, on follow through buying post FY21 results.  The company is looking to achieve 2018 levels in terms of operating profit (P9.3bn) and net profit (P8.2bn) in 2022, largely driven by a double-digit growth (vs 2021) in revenues, and a further improvement in margins.

On the macro front, the BSP, as expected, kept policy rates unchanged. BSP has maintained the record low 2% policy rate since last year after cutting during the early part of the pandemic.

Bongbong Marcos widened his lead in Presidential surveys. According to Pulse Asia, 60% of its respondents chose Marcos as their candidate in the survey conducted 19-24 January. This figure is up from the 53% in Pulse Asia’s December 2021 survey. Meanwhile, current VP Leni Robredo’s numbers fell to 16% in January, from 20% in December, though she is still in 2nd place.

Philippine Property - Positioning for a reopening
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Turning positive

The PPROP has underperformed the broad market by 15ppt since the start of 2020. As a result, valuations are now attractive, if not compelling, with shares of most developers trading at one standard deviation below the mean on both P/B and discount to NAV. Land prices have been resilient and office REIT valuations have been remarkably strong. Now, mall operations, in particular, appear poised for a sharp rebound and potential mall REIT listings should provide further NAV enhancement. We turn positive on property stocks and, within the sector, prefer exposure to the mall segment.

Mall operations to rebound; mall REITs could raise NAVs by up to 20%

We expect a strong bounce in mall operations as the economy reopens. For the most part, we forecast same-mall revenue to jump 70% in 2022E and to climb another 50% in 2023E. Furthermore, mall REIT spin-offs could lead to NAV increases of up to 20% for some mall operators (see page 7).

Prefer high-end and provincial residential, PEZA-accredited offices

We project a longer bottoming out process for the residential and office segments but believe the worst is behind us. Within the residential segment, we expect high-end and provincial projects to continue outperforming condominiums in Metro Manila. On office property, we expect new take-up from BPOs to return to pre-COVID levels and prefer PEZA-accredited offices.

RLC is our top pick; ALI, MEG, and SMPH also Buys

Robinsons Land is our top pick in the sector. Not only are its mall operations poised to rebound, we reckon it would be the biggest beneficiary of a mall REIT spin-off. Among the large caps, we prefer SM Prime due to its mall exposure and because we expect it to show earnings rebound of 51% CAGR over the next two years, the fastest in the sector. See pages 8-9 for changes to our estimates/ratings. Key upside and downside risks: COVID, POGOs, interest rates, and REIT listings.

 
ICTSI - The EM port operator to own; Maintain Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Upgrading TP/earnings

An improved outlook due to (a) market share gains, (b) tariff hikes and (c) the prospect of economic re-opening lead us to raise FY21E-FY23E earnings estimates by 11-22% and our TP by 25% to P250/sh. Even after outperforming the PCOMP by 68% over the past year, we believe the shares remain attractive, trading at 9x/8x on FY22E/FY23E EV/EBITDA, below historical average (11x). We maintain our Buy rating.

2022: A story of re-opening and return to growth path

2021 was a year when ICTSI improved market dominance in key locations, including at its largest port in Manila, whilst countries hosting most of its ports were still mired in frequent pandemic-related lockdowns. Tariff hikes were implemented in its ports in Manila and Melbourne, helping boost yields. We expect 2022 to be a return to the growth path, as (1) vaccination levels improve in most of the areas in its portfolio, which we believe will propel their respective economic and trade recoveries and (2) yields stay healthy due to anticipated tariff adjustments.

Above-trend volume/EBITDA growth; Street upgrades will likely follow

With this backdrop, we believe ICT can deliver above trend volume/EBITDA/EPS CAGR of 7%/13%/29% from 2021-2023. Our revised earnings forecasts are now the highest on the Street and we believe Street upgrades will follow.

Revised TP of P250/sh offers 17% total potential return

We raise our TP by 25% after incorporating (a) stronger volume growth assumptions (+7% vs. c. +5% previously), (b) higher yields, (c) a tax holiday extension in DRC, and (d) the concession extension in Madagascar. Our revised TP implies 11x FY22E EV/EBITDA and offers 17% total potential return. We believe the stock should re-rate, given its relatively more diversified EM portfolio and strong earnings growth. Downside risks relate to trade, dilutive acquisitions, and labor issues.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Key takeaways from the 4Q21 results briefing with CFO Mr Ysmael Baysa

Margins are sustainable despite near term headwinds. Mr Baysa believes margin levels in 4Q21 are sustainable and should even improve over time, despite the near term inflationary headwinds. JFC believes the margin improvement is structural in nature given: (1) Significant reduction in fixed cost due to Business Transformation in 2020. (2) Closure of unprofitable Smashburger and CBTL stores, which has driven the latter to profitability in 4Q21. (3) Increased contribution from international business, which generates higher margin vs Philippines.

 

Philippines: new store expansion to ramp up as economy reopens. The Philippine business generated op profit levels similar to 2019 despite sales still tracking c20% below pre-Covid. Mr Baysa is optimistic that profitability should continue to improve – and even exceed – 2018 levels once the economy fully reopens. JFC is cautious, however, in terms of new store expansion and is planning to open only c.100 stores in the Phils in 2022. Once the economy fully reopens, JFC expects to return to its previous run rate of 200-300 new stores per year, of which c70% will be franchised. Currently, around 66% of stores in the Phils are franchised.

 

Smashburger operations continue to improve. Smashburger’s loss in 4Q21 was 80% lower than the loss reported in 4Q19, implying a loss of c.P200mn in 4Q21. Mr Baysa remains optimistic that Smashburger is on the path to profitability given that 1) same-store sales continue to be strong (+15-18%) and 2) established stores are already profitable. On the whole, losses are still being dragged by pre-operating expenses for new stores and cost inflation (which will be mitigated by price increases). Assuming the trends continue, JFC expects Smashburger to be profitable by the end of 2022.

 

CBTL swings to profitability in 4Q21. CBTL swung to profit in 4Q21 (>P100mn by our estimate), bringing FY21 to just slightly above breakeven. This was attributed to the closure of non-performing stores, reduction in head office expenses, and the continued improvement in SSSG. As of 4Q21, CBTL’s same-store sales levels were already above pre-pandemic.

2022 targets. Mr Baysa did not provide specific provide guidance, but provided some indication on the direction for 2022 profitability. The company is looking to achieve 2018 levels in terms of operating profit (P9.3bn) and net profit (P8.2bn) in 2022.This will be largely driven by a double-digit growth (vs 2021) in revenues, and a continued improvement in margins

MPI revives share buyback

MPI’s Board of Directors approved a P5bn share buyback program to enhance and improve shareholder value. The buyback will commence on Feb 17 and will be equivalent to 4.6% of market cap. MPI began its buyback program in Feb 2020 and has since bought back P5.7bn worth of shares (average price of P3.81/sh).

Cebu Air back to 48% of pre-pandemic capacity in first two weeks of Feb

Jefferies’ airline capacity survey showed Cebu Pacific’s capacity bounced back to 48% of pre-pandemic levels in the first two weeks of February; Up from c. 34% during the Omicron surge in January 2022. Cebu Pacific Chief Executive Adviser Michael Szucs indicated the company is on track to reach 60-65% of pre-pandemic domestic capacity in February. He expects this to climb to 100% by April as restrictions are further rolled back. Regis estimates assume domestic capacity to reach 90% of pre-pandemic levels and for overall capacity to reach c. 70% of pre-pandemic levels for the year. Cebu Air is trading at 5.4x FY22E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers (18x).

Converge to lease fiber, earth station to SpaceX

Converge ICT’s Dennis Uy said they have reached an agreement to lease fiber lines and other ground support equipment to SpaceX. SpaceX is forming a constellation of thousands of low-orbit broadband satellites that will beam high-speed internet to far-flung areas. The project, known as Starlink, will launch commercially in the Philippines in 2023 – a delay from its original target sometime this year. It was not immediately clear if Converge and SpaceX would expand their tie-up beyond the leasing agreement. Uy said satellite providers would not displace more affordable fiber services where it was present.

CHP swings to a loss in 4Q21; Expansion capex hiked/pushed back to 2024

Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) swung to a net loss of P172mn in 4Q21, after sales volumes dropped and the impact of higher fuel costs finally took hold. The loss in 4Q took FY21 profit down 26% YoY to P726mn. Sales volumes fell 2% YoY and 16% QoQ in 4Q21, dropping further from what was already a low base in 4Q20.  Management attributed the drop to adverse weather conditions, including Typhoon Odette.  Its FY21 volumes rose +7% YoY but Regis estimates this was still 5% below 2019. Management however estimates that the industry exceeded pre-pandemic sales volumes in 2021. For 2022, management is guiding its volumes to rise by “high single-digits” but admitted the target is flattered by low base effects in the latter part of the year. Meanwhile, competitive pressures remained intense with 4Q21 ASPs just flat QoQ and only up +3% YoY from a low base in the same period the year prior. Management noted that government intervention remained insufficient to curb cement imports, as (a) the safeguard duty is now down to P8 per 40-kg bag beginning Dec 2021 (from P9.80 per bag previously) and (b) the provisional anti-dumping duty on Vietnamese imports were significantly less than the difference between domestic cement ex-factory prices and landed costs.

 

EBITDA fell 48% QoQ/41% YoY to P522mn in 4Q21, with EBITDA margin down to a record low of just 11%.  After benefitting from favorable coal supply contracts in 9M21, the adverse impact of elevated coal prices began to put downward pressure on the company’s margins. With coal prices at record levels, Regis believes margins will continue to drop in succeeding periods. Cemex secured a new contractor, AG&P and Betonbau Phil, to complete its 1.5mm MT expansion in Rizal, after terminating its previous Chinese contractor. The plant is now estimated to be operational by April 2024 (from June 2022 previously) and is estimated to cost more at US$323mn (from US$235mn previously). In the meantime, the company plans to fund the expansion by incurring more debt from its parent. The company continues to negotiate with its lender, BDO, to restructure its debt and management believes they will come to a favorable agreement shortly. While its debt covenant sculpted out debt related to the expansion, the previous agreement had a large portion of maturities expiring within 2022. 

Market foreign ownership declines in January

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership fell 31bps MoM to 21.4% while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 54bps MoM to 26.0% (in January). The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

CNVRG – foreign ownership fell 5.1ppt to 26.5%. Regis suspects that the share sale by strategic investor Coherent Cloud Investments (in November 2021) was only recorded in the foreign ownership data in January. If CNVRG’s foreign ownership had stayed flat MoM, then the PSEi foreign ownership would have fallen only 42bps MoM to 26.2%.

LTG – foreign ownership dropped by 2.3ppt to 13.4%. This was also likely a delay in the recording of transactions. Despite net foreign selling in 2H21, the reported LTG foreign ownership data was completely flat from June 2021 to December 2021.

GTCAP – foreign ownership declined 51 basis points to 21.2%. It had the third highest net foreign selling for the month (US$8.1mn).

ACEN – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents. Foreign ownership rose 18 basis points to 24.1%.

Raslag Corp plans P805mn IPO

Raslag Corp is planning to list on the stock market next month with an P805mn IPO to fund its solar projects. The company is planning to sell 350mn primary common shares for up to P2/share and an overallotment option of 52.5mn secondary common shares owned by J Ten Equities, Inc. The company may raise up to P700mn from the sale of primary shares. The net proceeds will be used to fund the balance of its Pamapanga Solar Power Project Phase 4 (35MW). It currently operates 2 solar farms with 23MW of capacity in Pampanga with 53MW under construction and another 60MW being planned. The market capitalization at the offer price is P3bn and the founded of the company is Peter Nepomuceno of Pampanga in 2013.

Vehicle sales slump in January 2022 due to Omicron surge

CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales dropped 11% YoY/25% MoM to 20,765 units in January 2022. The group cited tighter restrictions imposed in January 2022 due to the surge in COVID cases, as well as seasonality. Toyota’s market share stood at 47.57%, implying it sold 9,878 units (-9% YoY, -27% MoM). Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 16% discount to its market-based NAV (P684/sh).

Metro Manila to stay under Alert Level 2 until Feb 28

Metro Manila will remain under COVID-19 Alert Level 2 until February 28. However, seven areas were placed under Alert Level 3 from Feb 16-28. These areas were Davao deo Oro, Davao Occidental, Guimaras, Iloilo City, Iloilo Province, South Cotabato, Zamboanga City. Daily cases (7dma) are now down to 3.6k, of which Metro Manila new cases are now at just 560.

 

Vaccine hesitancy falls sharply in December

Filipinos are increasingly willing to get vaccinated against Covid-19. According to a December 2021 survey by the Social Weather Stations (SWS), only 8% of Filipino adults refuse to be inoculated (7% “surely not” and 1% “probably not”), down from 18% in September. Meanwhile, 87% are either willing to be jabbed, including 51% who have reported already receiving at least one dose. Vaccine hesitancy is now extremely low in Metro Manila (or NCR) at just 4% of adults, down from 7% in September. The figures are also very low in Luzon (ex-Metro Manila) and Mindanao, both at 8%. The anti-vaccine sentiment seems stubbornly high in the Visayas, with 16% unwilling to get the jab. That said, vaccine hesitancy fell in every geographic area between September and December. With so many Filipinos open to being vaccinated, the slow progress in the nation-wide vaccine rollout lies squarely on the government’s inability to deliver the doses outside the major city centers. As of last count, Regis estimates that only 55% of Filipinos have been jabbed, compared to 80% in Metro Manila, and roughly 70% in Cebu and Davao.

The pace of vaccinations has dropped steadily in recent weeks, down to roughly 325,000/day, compared to close to 800,000/day at the start of 2022. This is despite the government having roughly 90mn doses in inventory (by our estimate), compared to 130 doses administered so far. Most recently, the government announced it would target 6mn jabs in a 3rd “national vaccine drive” on 10-11 February. Regis reckons they administered just 1.3mn on those two days combined. From a macro/market standpoint, having high vaccination rates in the major economic centers is enough for us to be positive on the economic reopening/recovery in 2022.

Over 7,000 visitor arrivals logged since Feb. 10 border reopening

7,051 international visitors landed in the Philippines since the borders reopened on Feb. 10 to fully vaccinated travelers according to Tourism Secretary Bernadette Romulo-Puyat. Many visitors are returning Filipinos, though many foreigners are on business or are reuniting with spouses. On Feb 10, the government allowed quarantine-free entry of fully vaccinated foreign nationals who do not require a visa to enter the Philippines. Other requirements include a negative RT-PCR test 48 hours before departure, outbound ticket, and travel insurance with coverage payout of a minimum of US$35k. The country has also been admitting returning Filipinos quarantine-free since Feb 1. Pre-pandemic, tourist arrivals stood at 8.3mn annually (or c. 23k monthly); In 2020, tourist arrivals plunged to 1.5mn (or c. 4k daily).

Remittances rises to a record US$2.99bn in December

Remittances rose to the highest monthly level of US$2.99bn in December (+3% YoY, +19% MoM). Land-based remittances rose +3%, pushed higher by remittances from Asia (+6%) and USA (+5%) but dragged lower by the Middle East (-2%). In PHP terms, remittances rose +8% YoY and the 3mma growth rate slightly moderated to +3.5% (from +4.2%). This brings FY21 remittances to US$31.42bn, up +5% YoY but slightly short of the BSP’s +6% YoY target. For FY22, the BSP projects remittances to grow by +4%.

 

Mobility continues to improve (updated for 13 Feb data)

Mobility across key segments continues to rebound. The country’s Covid case count drops to under 3,000/day, with Metro Manila cases creeping down to the P500/day mark. Alert Levels across the country are being relaxed. Retail/recreation destinations jumped back up to pre-pandemic levels, or close to it (though one wonders how much of that was the Valentine’s weekend). Transit stations stats are also rising quickly as restrictions fall off. Workplace data is basically back to December levels (Covid-era highs). While the numbers seem to be so much lower than December, they are significantly better – as in 20-30 percentage points higher – than they were in Jan/Feb 2021. And that’s considering that there was no Covid surge in Jan/Feb 2021. The data supports our continued broad reopening theme, but also highlights our recent bullish report on Philippine Property

 

Monetary Board maintains policy rates

For a 10th straight meeting, the BSP maintained its overnight reverse repurchase rate at a historic low of 2% which was within market expectations. The BSP cited a manageable inflation environment and emerging uncertainty surrounding domestic and global growth prospects. However, BSP Governor Diokno hinted at an “eventual normalization” of policy once recovery is sustained or inflation risks rise. They see the economy reaching pre-pandemic GDP levels by 3Q22. The risks to the inflation outlook continue to lean slightly towards the upside for 2022 but remain broadly balanced for 2023. The upside risks are linked mainly to the continued shortage in domestic pork and fish supply and the possible impact of higher oil prices on transport fares. The BSP raised its average inflation forecast for 2022 to 3.7% (from 3.4%) and for 2023 to 3.3% (from 3.2%). Inflation is seen to decelerate in early 2022 due to base effects. But inflation could accelerate towards the upper end of the target band (2-4%) in the second quarter on elevated oil and nonoil prices. By 2H22, inflation is expected to move back to within target range.
