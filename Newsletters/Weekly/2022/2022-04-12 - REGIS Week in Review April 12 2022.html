REGIS REPORT

12 April 2022

The Philippines inched down 0.5% this week as investors braced for quicker tapering by the Fed. The market only traded three sessions this week as Phils goes on Easter break starting Thursday.

Declines were broad-based amidst very thin trading. Average daily volumes reached only US$81m. Index gainers (11) were outnumbered by decliners (18).

Among the top gainers were Emperador (+21.3% WTD) and Wilcon (+5.4% WTD), with the latter bouncing from its 7.2% decline in the previous week. Converge (+4.5% WTD) likewise recovered from its 4.5% drop last week.

Meantime, large caps BDO (-3.0% WTD), Ayala Land (-2.7% WTD), ICTSI (-2.0% WTD) and Universal Robina (-2.8% WTD) weighed on the index.

PLDT's (-0.4% WTD) fintech arm Voyager raised US$210m in new funding. The latest funding round was completed at a valuation of US$1.4bn. Voyager is the holding company for e-wallet operator PayMaya and digital bank Maya Bank. Leading the new round of investors is SIG Venture Capital, with two other investors EDBI and First Pacific (142 HK) also coming in. As a result of this transaction, PLDT’s stake in Voyager will be diluted to 36.8% (from 38.5%).

Shakey's Pizza (+5.6% WTD) rose after EBITDA margins in 4Q surprised to the upside as efforts to rationalize costs have begun to bear fruit.

The Bangko Sentral is expecting inflation to breach its 2% to 4% band in 2H. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno is in no hurry raise rates, with a near term preference to address elevated inflation thru "non-monetary" measures. Nonetheless, Governor Diokno previously signaled that an increase in policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% as being “reasonable”.

Meanwhile, the trade deficit for the month of February came in at US$3.5bn. This is down from US$4.7bn in January but is still 30% higher YoY. The cumulative trade deficit for the first two months is now US$8.2bn, 48% higher YoY.

Foreigners were sellers everyday this shortened week, with outflows amounting to US$24m. Biggest outflows were seen in big-caps Ayala Corp, Ayala Land, BPI, and Universal Robina.

RESEARCH
Filinvest Land - Reservation sales drop to 10-year low; office vacancy remains at 30%
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Two largest segments perform poorly
Filinvest Land’s FY21 net profit rose 2% to P3.8bn (behind consensus). While earnings were in line with our forecast, we believe investors should focus on two key items. First, 4Q21 reservation sales fell to the lowest level in over 10 years.
Second, vacancy of its office portfolio remains at 30%, the highest among developers we cover.

Reservation sales fall to 10-year low
4Q21 reservation sales fell 71% QoQ (down 39% YoY) to P2.0bn. Management blamed this on the delta variant spreading in provincial areas of the Philippines and said that sales improved in 1Q22. Nevertheless, FLI’s 4Q21 performance was worse than the rest of our coverage since the other developers we cover showed QoQ or YoY improvements.

FY21 reservation sales increased 5% to P16.0bn, thanks to stronger demand for house and lot projects. House and lot units now comprise 53% of sales, from only 32% in FY19. Meanwhile, cancellation rates have only inched up modestly to 3.4%, from 1.9% in 2019.

Office vacancy still at 30%
FLI’s office vacancy has been flat at 30% for three consecutive quarters, worse than the industry average of 18%. While the Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) portfolio’s vacancy is only 11%, we estimate the non-REIT portfolio’s vacancy to be about 55%.

Keep an eye on debt levels too
While 97% of FLI’s debt is fixed rate, P12bn out of its P68bn in debt will mature this year and will likely be refinanced at higher rates. Thus, management expects the cost of debt to rise from the current 4.9%. FLI’s net debt of P58.8bn is
equivalent to 7.4X our FY22E EBITDA forecast.

PIZZA FY21 results – turnaround in 4Q21

PIZZA swings to profit in 4Q21. PIZZA reported 4Q21 net profit of P156mn, a turnaround from breakeven levels in the same period last year. This brought FY21 profit to P121mn, ahead of consensus estimates. Net sales of P5.5bn were broadly within expectations.

Operating leverage drives 4Q margins. Revenues rose 17% YoY to P1.7bn in 4Q on the back of 5% SSSG and a 12% YoY increase in the number of stores. We estimate the contribution of dine-in sales jumped to c50% from 20-30% in 9M21 as mobility restrictions eased. Operating leverage from the strong growth in dine-in sales led to a significant improvement in EBITDA (over 2x YoY).

4Q21 EBITDA margin of 27.8% is significantly higher vs pre-pandemic levels (4Q19 EBITDA margin = 25.3%). Management believes EBITDA levels are sustainable give the significant permanent cost-reduction measures (e.g. reduced manpower per store, head office expenses) implemented over the past two years.

Strong recovery seen in 2022, Potato Corner to provide a boost. Management noted that on-premise sales continue to recover amidst the further loosening of restrictions. SSSG for the first few months of 2022 is in the low-teens, tracking c90% of 2019 levels. On an organic basis, management expects to hit 2019 systemwide levels (P10.4bn) this year, while the consolidation of Potato Corner (PC) beginning March 2022 is expected to provide a boost. In FY21, PC reported systemwide sales of c.P2bn. For FY22, PIZZA is planning to open 42 new stores for its existing brands, plus another 140 new Potato Corner stores.

Recovery to overshadow input cost pressure. Management expects to achieve pre-pandemic profitability levels by 2023 and recognizes that there are multiple headwinds being faced this year, particularly with respect to raw material cost inflation. Prices of cheese and potatoes (collectively 20-25% of total raw mats) have been locked in at 2021-lows for majority of 2022, providing some cushion against the increases in the prices of other inputs. The company is planning a price increase of low- to mid-single digits in 2022 (implemented on a staggered basis), but ultimately we believe the operating leverage from the continued improvement in SSS will drive 2022 profitability.

30% work-from-home arrangements are allowed for BPOs

PEZA said that BPOs (which avail of PEZA incentives) can have 30% work-from-home arrangements. The government previously required at least 90% of BPO employees to return to the office beginning April 1.

Our channel checks suggest that there are a number of BPOs looking to expand their office footprint. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for: 57% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 47% of Megaworld’s NAV, and 32% of Robinsons Land’s NAV. 

Power rates up in April as generation charge rises

In a virtual briefing on Monday, Meralco officials announced that the overall rate for residential users inched up by P0.5364 to P10.1830/kWh from P9.6467/kWh in March. Meralco said higher charges from independent power producers (IPPs) and the electricity spot market prompted an increase in the generation charge.

As a component of the month’s price increase, the generation charge accounted for P0.3987/kWh. Other charges are transmission at P0.0071m taxes P0.0915 and others P0.0390. The generation charge in April rose to P5.8724/kWh from P5.4737 the previous month. The distribution charge stayed unchanged.

New York court dismisses Bangladesh Bank complaint against Bloomberry

On Monday, the New York courted granted Bloomberry Resort’s motion to dismiss the complaint filed by Bangladesh Bank against its subsidiary Bloombery Resorts and Hotels Inc (BRHI) for lack of jurisdiction.

The complaint was for the conversion, theft, misappropriation etc and return of money received. The case stemmed from a 2016 incident where Bangladesh Bank lost $81m through an online heist involving an RCBC branch.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Trade deficit balloons to US$3.5bn in Feb

The country’s trade in goods balance stood at a US$3.5bn deficit in February, up 30% YoY but down from US$4.7bn in January. The YoY increase was due to higher imports of fuel (+132%) and sustained recoveries in consumer goods, capital goods, and raw/intermediate goods imports. Total trade (exports + imports) rose +18% YoY, slightly slower than in January (+20% YoY). This brings YT-Feb trade deficit to US$8.25bn, up 48% YoY.

For 2022, the BSP is forecasting trade in goods imports to rise by +15% YoY and exports to rise by +7% YoY.

FDI drops in January

Foreign direct investment inflows declined by 16% YoY and 23% MoM to US$819mn.  This is the first annual decline in eight months or since May 2021. The decline is attributed to the Omicron case surge and the re-imposition of stricter quarantine measures during the month.  Only debt instruments recorded an inflow for the month of US$634mn (+18% YoY) while equity and investment fund shares dropped 58% YoY to US$184mn.  The BSP is targeting FDI inflows to reach US$11bn in 2022, after hitting a record US$10.5bn in 2021.

BSP sees inflation breaching target in 2H

Inflation could breach the BSP’s 2-4% target in the 2H due to the surge in global oil prices but does not see this breaching 5%.  It expects inflation to gradually decelerate back to within the target range in the 1H23, according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. 

Near-term, BSP Governor Diokno prefers to address elevated inflation in the coming months thru “non-monetary” measures. The BSP has not seenclear signs of second-round effects. The economy is expected to operate below capacity near term even as the recovery gains traction.

Given the potential broadening of price pressures over the near term, the BSP stands ready to deploy timely and appropriate monetary policy measures, in line with its price and financial stability mandate. Governor Diokno previously signaled that an increase in policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% as being “reasonable”. BSP’s next policy meeting is on May 19.
