REGIS REPORT

16 December 2022

The market corrected for a second straight week (-1.3% WoW) to close at 6,496.50.

ADV jumped to US$508.8m, although this figure is skewed by multiple corporate events. First off, the acquisition of Eagle Cement by San Miguel Corporation was reflected in a block sale worth Php 110b last Wednesday alongside a tender offer. Second, Premiere Island Power REIT likewise listed shares worth Php 2.42b during its IPO.

Excluding these, ADV dropped to US$104.4M (vs. US$215.2M last week) while foreign outflows registered at US$25m (excluding foreign outflows from the EAGLE block of US$10m). While there was a FTSE rebalancing event late in the week, this exercise had little impact on volumes as turnover on rebalancing day was only $128.6m  given minor changes in weightings.

Overall, index names saw large swings given the lack of liquidity normally seen this time of the year.

Telcos PLDT (-14.6% WoW), CNVRG (-14.1% WoW), and Globe (-4.2% WoW) dropped sharply. PLDT disclosed an estimated budget overrun of around P48bn over 3 years, equivalent to 13% of what had been programmed for the period.  Disclosures on these came after the market closed on Friday, and hence it is not conclusive that the fall in the share price was truly due to this. Investigations are currently ongoing although there has been no fraud or loss of assets that has not been uncovered so far. A management reorganization is in process and will be disclosed once implemented.

Banks outperformed after the Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised its policy rate by 50bp. The BSP policy rate is now 5.5%, the highest in 14 years and ~125bp higher than the Fed’s. BPI (+1.47% WoW) and Metrobank (+1.00% WoW) both higher for the week. Market expectations are for the Fed raising rates by another 50-75bp next year (to just over 5%), but BSP Governor Medalla pointed out he sees less “urgency” to match the Fed. With the PHP now under less pressure, there is not much need to raise rates. The rise in inflation has largely been a result of higher import prices, driven by conditions elsewhere and not under the BSP’s control.

Conversely, real estate names underperformed given that a period of generally higher interest rates would present headwinds to demand.

Lastly, power and utility names ACEN (+2.07% WoW) and MER (+4.86% WoW) trekked higher as expensive power prices are likely to stay as supply remains tight on the back of the extended outage of the 1200 MW Ilijan plant and lack of baseload additions.

RESEARCH
Philippine Power - Hanging in the Balance
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Bracing for the inevitable; gencos well-positioned

Well into the post-Covid recovery, the Philippines is fast heading into the shortage we’ve been flagging as early as 2020. Peak demand grew 4% this year, and is expected to grow by 5% p.a. from 2023 to 2024. Compounding the already tight situation is the 1,200MW Ilijan plant which has been out since June for a myriad of reasons. The baseload pipeline is thin over the next two years, and we are likely to see more of the same in 2023 – high power prices, low reserves and intermittent power interruptions. We reiterate our BUY call on the gencos.

Demand to grow 5% p.a.; headline supply is up but key plant missing

We estimate peak demand will grow 5% in 23E (+600MW), led by the brisk recovery of the commercial sector. On the supply side, we expect some 750MW in capacity, mostly from RE developers, but notably without baseload from coal or gas. The difference between peak demand and actual available capacity at any given time is going to be dangerously small, despite what the headline numbers suggest.

Expensive power here to stay

WESM prices have remained in the P8-9/kWh range over the last few months, more than 4x the lows of 2020, and double the previous expectations of what its long-term average should be. While the demand-supply equation is to be little changed next year, the complications arising from the stranded 1,200MW Ilijan plant have mostly offset the capacity addition from the last big plant that opened (1,300MW GNP Dinginin).

Has RE delivered? No RE oversupply, viability improving

The first big wave of new RE plants will be coming online over the next 12-18 months, with the biggest additions coming from ACEN Corp (ACEN PM). The headline capacities appear impressive, but the effective addition falls well under 200MW if one takes availability into account. Longer-term, however, we believe that the sharp jump in fuel costs has improved the viability of RE.

Multi-year positive for all gencos; BUYS on ACEN, AP and FGEN

AP is our top pick, as its earnings should benefit from the last big capacity addition. A third of output is on short-term contracts that reprice more frequently, while a generous dividend enhances potential return. We have also upgraded ACEN recently to a BUY on the merits of a more favorable risk-reward, after a 40% drop from its peak. Meanwhile, we like FGEN on valuations, but fear that near-term growth and limited liquidity could hamper fair price discovery.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Is a China reopening in the numbers?
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Reiterate BUY on BLOOM

We tweak our estimates and reiterate our positive view and BUY rating on Bloomberry on the back of a recovery in its gaming business. While we expect the rebound to be led by the local market this time, this could be further accelerated by an eventual China reopening. Relaxation of Covid controls in China ahead of an eventual end to travel restrictions bodes well for Macau, and the benefits of this reopening should eventually make their way to other gaming jurisdictions in the region. Its 7.8x EBITDA multiple for FY23E is at an unwarranted discount to Macau gaming firms, especially in light of the relative earnings visibility.

Locals and non-Chinese foreigners are now the key markets

The GGR contribution from Chinese tourists is now down to c.13% of BLOOM’s GGR, far from the 40% contribution at the peak. GGR from locals (both VIP and mass) has exceeded pre-pandemic numbers, both by % contribution and absolute Peso terms and will continue to drive the 22% GGR CAGR up from FY22E to FY24E.

Ample capacity if tourists return in a big way

Despite the strong growth registered in 2Q-3Q, capacity utilization remains low. Foot traffic has held steady at around 13k/day while hotel occupancy levels have plateaued at 60%. Much of the jump has been generated from a higher share of premium (within mass). The property has ample capacity to service fly-in businesses and could provide another leg for growth.

Target of P11.70/share at 10x Solaire’s FY22 EBITDA; risks

At 7.8x EV/EBITDA for FY23E, its multiple is at a steep discount especially against growth (21% EBITDA CAGR until FY24E). We think a discount to Macau operators is no longer warranted under the current conditions, with BLOOM now demonstrating better earnings visibility and more sustainable growth. At our target, BLOOM would only trade at its historical multiples and still below Macau operators. Key risks include prolonged restrictions on travel, competition, and regulation.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco asks SPPC to pay for cost of TRO

Manila Electric Co (Meralco) has issued a notice of claim against San Miguel’s South Premiere Power Corp (SPPC) to cover the additional costs it has been incurring in relation to a 60-day stay order on their Ilijan power supply agreement (PSA). Meralco said it had told SPPC to pay the difference between their contract price and the price at the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM), to which the utility is exposed amid the TRO on their contract. Aside from the additional cost of sourcing power from WESM, Meralco said it was also asking SPPC to pay fines, penalties and liquidated damages under the PSA.

 

Consumer loans jump 11.7% YoY in 3Q22 led by salary loans, credit cards, and mortgages; Asset quality improves across the board

Consumer loan growth in the banking sector accelerated to 11.7% YoY in 3Q22, up from the 6.4% YoY pace in 2Q22, and the fastest YoY growth since the pandemic. This is notable, as motor vehicle (MV) loans are still -8%, though the pace of decline is slowing sharply. Growth was led by salary loans (+52%), credit cards (+26%), and mortgages (+9%). Meanwhile, consumer NPLs dropped 92bps QoQ to 7.19% from 8.11% in 2Q22 (peaked at 9.86% in 2Q21). Asset quality improved across the board, with ratios lower across all major segments, and with even nominal PHP NPLs down QoQ for most segments.

 

The growth in higher-margin consumer loans bodes well for bank NIMs, and further supports our positive view on the banking sector as a beneficiary of the reopening / recovery story.

 

Mortgages grew 8.6% YoY, faster than the 8.1% clip in 2Q22. On a QoQ basis, mortgages were up 1.4%. Mortgage NPL ratio eased to 7.92%, down 71bps QoQ from the 8.63% in 2Q22 (peak was 9.85% in 3Q22). In PHP terms, mortgage NPLs fell 12% YoY and 7% QoQ. Despite rising interest rates, anecdotes from banks suggest continued acceleration in mortgage demand heading into 4Q22/2023.

 

Motor Vehicle (MV) loans contracted 8.2% YoY. Notably, though, this was a vast improvement over the -12% YoY in 2Q22 and -16% YoY at end-2021. On a QoQ basis, MV loans were already flat (-0.4%) QoQ in 3Q22. Anecdotally, we should start to see MV loans in positive QoQ territory in 4Q22, and positive YoY by 1Q23. MV NPL ratio improved to 9.31%, own 129bps QoQ from the 10.60% in 2Q22 (peak was 12.7% in 3Q21).

 

Credit card loans surged 25.5% YoY, accelerating from 18.9% in 2Q22. On a QoQ basis, credit card receivables were up 6.1% vs 2Q22. NPLs for credit cards improved to 5.02%, compared to 5.80% in 2Q22. Even on a nominal PHP basis, credit card NPLs fell 8% QoQ. The acceleration is likely supported by continued relaxation of international travel restrictions, which is a huge driver of credit card spend.

 

Salary loans spiked 52.4% YoY and 20.8% QoQ. We suspect this is due in part to the start of operations of the digibanks, for whom salary loans are the main product. While they alone won’t account for the P44bn QoQ increase, it is likely that the incumbent banks are starting to compete in what should be a relatively lucrative segment. NPL ratios here fell to 4.69%, from 5.17% in 2Q22. However, that makes sense given the sharp increase in the denominator. In absolute PHP terms, salary loan NPLs were up c10% QoQ.

October asset quality improves, in both ratio and PHP terms; loan growth moderates a smidge

Asset quality (all banks) improved across all major ratios in October, showing even nominal PHP declines MoM. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) came in at 3.49%, down 3bps MoM; that’s the lowest since August 2020, when the first Bayanihan period expired. In PHP terms, NPLs fell to P411bn, from P414bn in September. Past Due (PD) loans improved to 4.03%, down 1bp MoM, with the nominal value at P487bn down from P489bn the month prior. Restructured Loans (RLs) resumed their downtrend, down 5bps MoM to 2.71%, after a small blip up in September. In PHP terms, RLs fell to P327bn, from P334bn in September. NPL cover inched up to 104% from 103% the previous month.

 

Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) unexpectedly slowed a tad to 10.9% YoY, from 11.2% in September. This was in stark contrast to the UKB (universal and commercial bank) data that showed loan growth accelerating to 13.9% in October from 13.4% in September. Even the MoM data was odd, as the All Banks data showed a MoM decline of 0.4%, while the UKB data was up 0.7% MoM.

 

The improving data – particularly the PD data – is encouraging, as it shows no deterioration in asset quality despite the sharply rising interest rates. Of course, it’s early days, and loan rates may not have repriced yet as of October. Regis will be watching this data closely over then coming months. The loan data is a bit of a head-scratcher. Regis would not be surprised to see some revisions (either to the All Banks, or to the UKB numbers) next month.

Malampaya consortium denies it is refusing to deliver banked gas to SMC Global Power

In a statement, the Malampaya consortium said that it strongly refutes South Premiere Power Corp’s  (SPPC) claim that is refusing to sell to Ilijan because “there are no legal means by which the sale of gas to the plant could be made”. Trade deficit shrinks to US$3.3bn in October, as exports rebound

The country’s trade balance recorded a deficit of US$3.31bn in October. This is 32% lower than the trade deficit recorded in September 2022 (US$4.8bn) and 13% lower than the trade deficit in Oct 2021 (US$3.82bn). This is the lowest trade deficit in 13 months. Imports growth slowed sharply to 7% YoY in October due to moderating growth in fuel & raw material imports, and a 3% YoY drop in capital goods imports. Implementing rules for SIM Registration Law released

The implementing rules and regulation (IRR) for the SIM Registration Act (RA 11934) were released by the NTC. The Law provides for the mandatory registration of all SIMs. Individuals will have to provide personal details and an ID.  “Juridical entities” will have to be provide business names and authorized signatories. Foreign tourists will have to provide passport details and addresses in the country. All new SIMs will have to be registered before activation. Existing SIMs will have 180 days to register or risk deactivation; deadline can be extended by another 120 days.

 

SIMs registered to tourists will be automatically deactivated after 30 days, but this can extended. Penalties are imposed for registering false information or selling stolen SIMs. Telcos are required to deactivate SIMs used for fraudulent calls/text upon investigation. Regis expects the implementation of this to result in a large decrease in active SIMs / subscribers. Many SIMs likely to be deactivated by the deadline. Revenues are also likely to be temporarily affected but ARPUs likely to rise (subscriber count decreased more than revenue). Longer term, the practice of throwaway or burner SIMs will be reduced. The other key impact will be on micro-finance / fintech firms as SIMs can now be identified with individuals. This will also allow lending through mobile apps to be accomplished with greater security.

BSP raises rates by 50 bps, signals more tightening

The BSP raised its benchmark interest rate by 50bps to 5.5% within consensus expectations. The move followed the 50bp rate hike of the Fed. Overnight borrowing rate of 5.5% at the highest since November 2008. The BSP cited the further uptick in headline and the sharp rise in core inflation in November amid pent-up demand. Recall headline and core inflation accelerated to 8% and 6.5%, respectively, in November. The BSP raised its 2023 average inflation target to 4.5% from 4.3%, up from 5.8% in 2022 (unch). This is due to faster-than-expected Nov 2022 inflation and the looming impact of approved water rate hikes beginning Jan 2023. The latest forecast point to inflation peaking in December. The BSP expects inflation to return to the 2-4% target band by 2H23. Additionally, it lowered its 2024 average inflation target to 2.8% from 3.1% previously. The BSP Governor Medalla expects the pace of rate increases to slow down next year, depending on the data. He sees less urgency to match the US Federal Reserve’s monetary tightening next year as bringing inflation back to the 2-4% target remains their top priority.

Cash remittances rise 3.5% YoY in October

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.5% YoY to US$2.9bn in October. In PHP terms, remittances are up 20% YoY and the 3-month average growth was rate at +3.9% YoY. This was driven by higher inflows from Europe, Middle East, HK, Singapore. MoM, Oct remittances were higher by 2.5%. This brought YT-October remittances to US$26.7bn, up 3.1% YoY and was driven by inflows from USA, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Qatar. To reach the BSP’s 22E of +4% YoY, remittances for the balance of the year should average US$2.97bn per month.

Lower House approves Maharlika Investment Fund bill

The House of Representatives approved on third and final reading the bill creating the controversial Maharlika Investment Fund. Voting 279-6, the House of Representatives overwhelmingly approved House Bill (HB) No. 6608, which was just filed two weeks ago. President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. on Wednesday certified the MIF bill as urgent. A mix of public funds and dividends from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) will be used as seed money for the MIF, based on the current iteration. Initial funding will come from the Land Bank of the Philippines (P50bn), Development Bank of the Philippines (P25bn) and BSP. The BSP will initially put in the MIF 100% of its dividends to be declared as income. Subsequent annual contributions will come from BSP dividends, and part of the gaming revenues of the PAGCOR. and other state-owned gaming operators, among others. According to a BSP official, the BSP’s projected dividends for 2022 is P30-35bn, which is equivalent to 50% of its income. Recall that the original bill involved public funds of P275bn, which included contributions from SSS (P50bn) and GSIS (P125bn).
