REGIS REPORT

17 June 2022

The Phils dropped 3.0% this week, as recession fears took hold and whipped global markets. The Fed raised rates by 75 bps this week -- the biggest single hike in three decades -- and signaled continued aggressive tightening to fight soaring inflation. The PSEi’s back to back weekly 3% fall has taken its YTD loss to 11%. 

Foreigners were sellers for US$69m on heightened risk-off sentiment. Biggest outflows were in ICTSI (-3.5% WoW), Universal Robina (-4.3% WoW), and Ayala Land (-7.3% WoW). Meanwhile, decent inflows were seen in SM Prime (+2.0% WoW) after seeing an upweight in FTSE.

The index was mostly down this week, with 24 of the 30 index members ending the week lower. Large caps SM (-2.3% WoW) and Ayala Land moved the index lower, accounting for some 35% of the overall decline.

Top declines were seen in Wilcon (-15.3% WoW), Converge (-13.5% WoW), Megaworld (-13.1% WoW), and Alliance Global (-9.3% WoW).   Consumer names Monde (-7.9% WoW), Puregold (-4.6% WoW), Jollibee (-4.5% WoW), and Universal Robina (-4.3% WoW) were also weak. 

Incoming Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla appears to be hinting at possibly more  policy rate increases than the market expects. According to a media report, Medalla has mentioned there could be four or five hikes this year that would take the policy rate to 3.25% to 3.5% (vs expectations of 2.75-3.00%). However, the local debt markets have already moved well ahead. The 7-year and 10-year Philippine Treasury bond yields have already climbed ~220bp and ~170bp YTD.

The Dept of Health has warned of the possibility that Metro Manila (NCR) can revert back to pandemic Alert Level 2 given the rise in Covid-19 cases. This seems to be meant as a preemptive warning to the public, as the average daily attack rate (ADAR) in NCR is less than one, and the government considers ADAR above 6 to be “moderate risk”.

Meanwhile, on the corporate side, Globe (-6.1% WoW) is considering the sale of part of its telecom tower network. PLDT (+1.5% WoW) managed to secure gross proceeds of P77bn for half of its tower network. A similar transaction for Globe, could be worth ~P600 per Globe share on a gross basis (~P450 after taxes and fees). This would also preempt the need for a capital call, amidst record capax and net debt/EBITDA over 3x.

Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Stronger for longer; Upgrade to Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Strong capital returns

We upgrade our recommendation to Buy from Hold, after raising our earnings forecasts by 59-147% and lifting our price target by 52% to P38/sh. The unprecedented strength in seaborne thermal coal prices should allow Semirara to generate outsized returns over the next 12 months, in our view. We expect SCC will continue to prioritize paying dividends of at least P6.50/sh (c. 19% yield), thus making it an attractive yield play, while it awaits developments on its diversification plans. Trading at 3.9x/4.2x FY22E/FY23E PER, SCC is attractively valued relative to its peers and historical average.

Higher coal prices for longer

Our earnings revisions reflect coal ASP assumptions of US$108/MT and US$92/MT in 2022 and 2023, respectively. These are higher than our previous estimates by 65%/99% but are also not too far from what SCC already achieved in 1Q22 (US$99/MT). Given that coal-fired plants account for half of the Luzon grid’s power capacity, higher thermal coal prices can also push up power rates and benefit SCC, as it is selling 60% of its power output to the spot market.

Growth opportunities mostly hinge on renewal of coal contract

Even though management is exploring expansion opportunities outside of coal (e.g. cement and nickel), we believe execution of these plans could take some time, as these require government approvals, particularly the renewal of its coal operating contract. We thus believe SCC’s FCF generation will remain high over the next 12-18 months and allow it to pay at least a dividend of P6.50/sh in 22F/23F. At a net cash position of P4.7bn in end-March 2022, SCC’s balance sheet is very healthy and has room to fund these investments with leverage.

DCF-based TP of P38/sh offers 29% total shareholder return

We raise our 12-month DCF-based TP by 51% to P38/sh after putting through coal and power ASP upgrades, as well as rolling over our valuation to 2023. These upgrades more than offset higher crude oil assumptions. Our TP offers 29% total shareholder return. Downside risks: collapse in coal prices, execution risks; an undiversified power portfolio; and mine accidents.

 
Philippine Banks - Ready or not, here they come
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Ready for reopening. Ready for digital competition?

Banks are arguably the ideal “reopening play”. Loan growth is back, asset quality concerns have peaked, and NIMs are poised to expand. Banks are also largely unaffected – at least not directly – by inflation and supply chain issues. We upgrade our earnings, target prices, and ratings across our coverage. However, a new threat has emerged – e-wallets, fintech, and digibanks. Each bank we cover has a different view and strategy for digitization. While BPI seems to have an early lead, it is far too early to tell which strategy will prevail.

Reopening play – NPLs easing, loan growth accelerating; NIMs set to rise

The sector is ready for the ongoing economic reopening. Sector loan growth accelerated to 6.9% in April. System-wide NPL ratios are down sharply from their peak in 3Q21, and high provisioning levels in 2020/21 mean credit costs are falling rapidly. NIMs have stabilized, and should turn upward as loan demand continues to recover and as the BSP starts hiking short-term interest rates. As a “reopening play”, banks do not have the (direct) downside from rising inflation; in fact, the upward pressure on interest rates could see upside risk to NIMs.

Ready for digital competition? E-wallets, fintech, and digital banks

The BSP has championed technology in improving financial inclusion. The Covid[1]19 pandemic lent urgency to digital adoption. While only about 30% of Filipino adults have formal bank accounts, more than 80% have e-wallets. In 2021, the BSP awarded 6 Digital Bank licenses. The incumbent banks seem to have different strategies for digital competition. Of those we cover, BPI seems to have most embraced IT to rapidly grow its customer base. BDO sees it more to strengthen its hold on its existing clients. MBT will use tech to lower cost-to[1]serve. SECB sees IT as crucial to its survival as mid-sized bank.

Buys all around; but mindful of macro risks

We refresh our forecasts and target prices (based on ROE-g/COE-g), bringing all banks we cover to Buy territory. At our target prices, all four would still be below long-term P/bk averages. Key downside risks relate to macro factors, as surging inflation could blunt the consumption-driven economy. This, coupled with sharply higher interest rates could lead to a relapse in sector NPLs. Digital competition could also intensify, and put pressure on rates and fees.

 
Manila Water - Better days ahead?
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 Klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Maintain Hold

The rate rebasing process has begun but delays in the issuance of a letter of undertaking by the Department of Finance are making it more likely that the revised concession agreement may never come into force. We revise our valuation and TP to P19/share (from P13/share) to incorporate our expectations of the ongoing rate rebasing process. We maintain our Hold rating, and highlight the high degree of uncertainty over the rebasing and the wide range of outcomes it could produce for MWC.

Water franchise law and delays in effectivity of the new contract

The government approved the water franchise law early this year, which will allow the extension of the concession contracts to 2047 and make it more difficult for the concessions to be terminated early. At the same time, the government also has yet to issue the letter of undertaking (LOU) for the new concession contracts. This means the previous, superior agreement remains valid until such time the LOU is issued, if at all.

Rate rebasing outcome: Higher but how much higher?

MWC’s capex has been rising and is likely to go up further as management and creditor confidence improve and the regulator seeks more spending to meet higher water and wastewater standards. This is a prelude to an increase in the value of its recoverable investments (or Opening Cash Position, OCP) and ultimately the value of its concession.

TP revised to P19/sh

We revise our TP to P19/sh (+46%) after incorporating (a) higher capex (+50% higher from previous assumptions and +70% higher from the previous approved capex), (b) contract extension to 2047, and (c) higher OCP of P31bn. Risks: regulation, water supply, and acquisitions.

 
 
Corporate News
 
 
 
 

Market foreign ownership stays flat in May

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership rose 2bps MoM to 21.3% while PSEi foreign ownership increased 8bps MoM to 25.7% (in May). Although net foreign selling amounted to US$350mn in May, the foreign ownership percentage was boosted by the outperformance of high foreign ownership stocks such as MONDE (+14% MoM) and GLO (+7% MoM). Around 22 of the 30 PSEi constituents saw declines in their foreign ownership levels. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

CNVRG – foreign ownership fell 454 basis points to 21.4% as private equity firm Warburg Pincus reduced its stake. The stock had the fourth highest net foreign selling in May (US$19.5mn)

SECB – foreign ownership dropped by 55 basis points to 37.4%.

ACEN – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents. Regis estimates that foreign ownership rose 266 basis points to 26.6%.

MONDE – foreign ownership increased 40 basis points to 64.3%. It experienced the largest net foreign buying in May (at US$28mn) due to an upweight in the MSCI Index.

 

PSE Index August 2022 rebalancing prediction: SCC (+), SECB (-)

Regis expects SCC to be added to the PSE Index during the August 2022 rebalancing. This will lead to the removal of SECB from the index. The PSEi rebalancing in August will be based on the 12 months from July 2021 to June 2022. Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap. Based on Regis estimates, SCC has reached the top 25 and is unlikely to be dislodged by end-June.Based on our preliminary estimates, total inflow will reach US$33mn. SCC will see an inflow of US$27mn while CNVRG will experience inflow of US$6mn (due to higher free float). SECB will see an outflow of US$30mn while the other PSEi constituents experience a combined outflow of US$3mn. Note: among brokers who make PSE Index predictions, the removal of SECB appears to be a unanimous prediction but brokers are split on whether SCC or UBP will be added to the index. Regis estimates that UBP was only among the 25% of most liquid stocks in 8 of the past 12 months (including June), lower than the requirement of 9 months. In determining the total number of stocks to consider, Regis excludes stocks that are ineligible to be part of the PSE index, such as preferred shares, ETFs, stocks listed on the SME board, foreign companies whose shares are also listed in exchanges overseas.

Completion of FGEN LNG terminal pushed back to 2Q/3Q 2023

First Gen disclosed that its LNG subsidiary requested the Department of Energy to extend the validity of its permit to construct, expand, rehabilitate and modify for its interim offshore LNG terminal from September 23, 2022 to March 23, 2023. The request was made due to a projected delay in the completion of the project. FGEN LNG and BW FSRU have separately agreed to move the delivery of the floating storage regasification unit BW Paris from 1Q 2023 to end of 2Q or early 3Q 2023. The expiration of Ilijan’s contract with Malampaya this month resulted in FGEN’s gas plants getting their full gas allocation.

 
 
Macro News
 
 
 
 

Remittances rise 3.9% YoY in April

Remittances coursed through banks rose 3.9% YoY in April to US$2.4bn. However, this was lower by 8% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances in April rose 1.9% YoY to P125bn, which is lower than the monthly averages in 3M22 (P133bn) and FY21 (130bn). The 3-month average growth rate at +2.8%. Land-based remittances jumped 4.7% YoY, driven by USA (+9%) and Middle East (+7%). This brings YT-April remittances to US$10.2bn, up 2.7% YoY. To reach the BSP’s FY22 target (+4% YoY), remittances would have to average US$2.8bn per month for the rest of the year.

Vehicle sales improve further in May

Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, rose 20% YoY, 5% MoM to 26,370 units in May. This is about 85% of pre-pandemic sales (vs. 2019 average) and brings 5M22 vehicle sales to 126,273 units (+15% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 14,723 units in May (+36% YoY, +7% MoM). Toyota’s market share improved to 55.8% from 48% in end-December. Sales were strong due to pull-forward sales ahead of blanket price hike in June. This brings 5M22 sales to 65,668 units, up 25% YoY. To reach Toyota’s target of 162k (+25% YoY) units this year, Toyota needs to sell 13,800 units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 22% discount to its market-based NAV of P617/sh and 7.2x FY22E consensus PER.

Metro Manila may be put under Alert Level 2 due to uptick in COVID-19 cases

The Department of Health raised the possibility of escalating the Covid alert system to Level 2 amid an uptick in covid cases in NCR. Metro Manila is currently under Alert level 2. Metro Manila should first be classified under “moderate risk” before being escalated to Alert Level 2. Moderate risk should have a positive two-week growth rate (TWGR) and average daily attack rate (ADAR) of at least 6 average cases per day per 100k population. As of June 10, Metro Manila was showing a positive TWGR but its ADAR continues to be below 6 at less than 1 case per 100,000 population. Covid-19 bed utilization in Metro Manila remains at low risk. Nationwide new cases (7-day ave) are up 48% WoW to 270. NCR new cases (7-day ave) are up 53% WoW to 131.

BSP targets 1-digit bank reserve ratio

The BSP will pursue the reduction of the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) of big banks to within single digit in the fourth quarter according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. Governor Diokno previously said that the BSP was targeting to bring the RRR to single digits only by 2023. In 2020, the BSP lowered the RRR by 200 basis points to 12%.

BSP signals gradual tightening

BSP will likely raise rates in its policy meeting in June/July; depending on the data, it could raise policy rates further in succeeding periods according to incoming BSP Governor and current member of the Monetary Board, Felipe Medalla. Mr. Medalla personally does not like raising rates by 50bps. BSP also sees that they have the luxury of time and large reserves. The BSP has raised policy rates by 25bps in May. In 2020, BSP reduced its policy rates by 200bps to 2%.
