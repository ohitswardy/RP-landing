REGIS REPORT

30 September 2022

Risk off sentiment took the PSEi a whopping 8.3% lower this week, as global markets reacted to further tightening by the Fed. The PSEi closed at 5741.07 this week, with the drop taking YTD losses to 19.4%.   

Index losers overwhelmed gainers, with 27 index stocks closing down for the week. Pushing the index lower were well held, large cap names SM (-10.3% WoW), SM Prime (-11.6% WoW), and ICTSI (-13.9% WoW).   Also weighing on the index were Ayala Corp (-11.5%), Ayala Land (-8.6% Wo), and BDO (-6.9% WoW).  Steep declines were seen as well in GT Capital (-14.1% WoW) and Converge (-13.5% WoW), and Meralco (-12.4% MoM). 

The PHP fell to a record low against the USD this week (PHP58.996/US$ close) but managed to recover to PHP58.65/US$ (-0.3% WoW) after the BSP said it tightened its reporting guidelines for forex transactions. BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said earlier that an off-cycle rate increase was not necessary at this point.

Global/macro factors dominated sentiment this week. There was some corporate news flow, however.  

BPI (-4.0% WoW) and Robinsons Bank (RBC), a small bank currently controlled by the Gokongwei group, are in discussions on a potential collaboration. According to news reports, the merger will be done through a share swap, wherein BPI is the surviving entity and the Gokongwei Group would end up with a collective 6% stake in BPI. 

Robinsons Bank is roughly 7% the size of BPI and acquiring it would lift BPI slightly above Metrobank (-6.5% WoW) in terms of asset size. RBC is 60% owned by JG Summit (-11.1% WoW), and 40% by Robinsons Retail (-4.0% WoW). 

Cezar Consing,  previously CEO of Bank of the Philippine Islands, has been officially appointed as President and CEO of Ayala Corp. He replaces Fernando Zobel who has resigned his post. Consing become President in an acting capacity when Zobel took a medical leave a number of weeks back.

Globe Telecom (-6.6% WoW) disclosed that it signed an agreement to sell its third tranche of towers numbering 1,350.  This batch is being sold to Phil-Towers, a consortium led by an Australian investment bank, for P20bn. In total, Globe will receive P91bn for all three tranches (total of 7,059 towers). This translates roughly to a compelling price of  P12.9m per tower, similar to PLDT’s own tower sale. The transaction will mean large cash inflows for Globe amounting to over P80bn, after tax, or  over P550/share.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$230.5m this week. Outflows however were bloated by a HOUSE (-0.6% WoW) block. Excluding this, foreigners would have been net sellers for $36m , with the largest outflows seen in SM Investments, SM Prime,  Ayala Land, Ayala Corp, BDO, and ICT.

Philippine Property - POGO firms could exit Metro Manila, possibly the country
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Government could change POGO regulations

Due to the recent spate of crimes linked to offshore gambling, several lawmakers and members of the administration have called for a reassessment of the country’s stance on Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs). While some have recommended to transfer them to POGO-specific zones in order to better maintain law and order, others have proposed to ban them outright. Either of these would be detrimental to both the residential and office segments in Metro Manila, where most POGOs operate. The competing voices will be many, and maintenance of the status quo is by no means assured.

Why has there been greater backlash on POGOs recently?

There has been a recent uptick in crimes associated with offshore gambling, particularly kidnapping. In fact, the Philippine National Police (PNP) said that 15 of 29 kidnapping cases in 2022 were linked to offshore gambling.

What does the government plan to do?

Due to the increase in crimes, several lawmakers have called for the abolition of POGOs. Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno and Senator Grace Poe have both said that the government revenue collections (c. P4bn in 2021) do not justify the social costs to the country. Furthermore, Senate Majority Leader Joel Villanueva has filed a bill seeking to ban online gambling in the country.

On the other hand, House Ways and Means Committee Chairman Joey Salceda said that a blanket ban on the sector due to some bad actors would only lead to the industry going underground. Instead, he proposed to limit POGOs to specific zones. New licensees would be required to set up in such zones while existing licensees would be permitted to finish their existing rental contracts before moving to the said zones. We expect such PEZA-specific zones to be in provincial areas, where property prices and rents are lower.

Detrimental for Metro Manila property; negative office rental reversions likely

Even if POGOs will merely be transferred to provincial areas, this would be detrimental to Metro Manila property rents/prices. We estimate that POGOs still occupy c. 750,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila (c. 5% of office stock) and likely employ at least 75,000 people, mostly in Makati and the Manila Bay area.

Coupled with the government decision that effectively allows BPO firms to implement WFH policies indefinitely, a POGO exodus would likely cause Metro Manila office vacancy to rise above our 21%-22% forecast in 2023E-2024E (2Q22 office vacancy: 18%). Since rental rates are largely 20%-40% below 2019 levels (see Figure 2), and most contracts expiring over the next two years were signed in 2018-2019, negative rental reversions will become increasingly likely. CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco expects 6% growth in 3Q energy sales

Meralco FVP and chief commercial officer Ferdinand Geluz said the company’s energy sales during July, August and September recorded growth ranging from 6-8% per month. <eralco mentioned said “Commercial is growing double digits. There are months showing growth of 15 percent, 19 percent”. Additionally, for the industrial sector, Geluz said Meralco is seeing a steady three to four percent growth in the third quarter.

Globe announces sale of 3rd tranche of towers

Globe signed the sale and lease-back of 1,350 towers with the Phil-Tower consortium. The transaction price was P20bn. The sale brings the number of towers sold to 7,059, with gross proceeds of P91bn (P12.9m or US$220k per tower). The Phil-Tower consortium is led by Australian investment bank Macquarie. This tranche of towers is located primarily in Visayas and Mindanao. The pre-tax gain from this tranche is estimated to be P5.2bn, bringing total gain from the sales to P31bn (pretax). Based on the latest disclosure, Regis estimates net proceeds of ~P83bn from the tower sales (approximately P570 per Globe share).

JFC announces additional investment in Tim Ho Wan operator

JFC, through its wholly-owned subsidiary, Jollibee Worldwide Pte Ltd announced that it is making an additional SDG90mn (PHP3.7bn) investment in Titan Dining LP. In conjunction with the increase in the fund size of Titan to SGD350mn from the current SGD250mn. JFC currently has a 90% stake in Titan. Titan is the private equity fund that operates the Tim Ho Wan brand and company-owned stores. Total 67 outlets in Asia (mostly franchised), of which 11 are in Mainland China. JFC is targeting to hit 100 Tim Ho Wan stores in Mainland China in the next four years. Titan also has other investments in F&B (Tiong Bahru Bakery and Common Man Coffee Roasters) and non-F&B (Strip, Browhaus).

Metro Pacific’s Cebu tollroad’s daily revenue at P1mn

Cebu-Cordova Link Expressway (CCLEX) is currently generating revenues of c. P1mn per day from the passage of at least 12.5k motorists according to Renato Ticzon, vice president and marketing consultant at MPT Mobility, a unit of Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. The company expects traffic volumes to surge to 20k by December as a result of the holiday influx and for revenues to hit P2mn a day. To boost traffic, MPTC plans to build an P800mn Drive&Dine complex to boost traffic. CCLEX was opened to the public last April 30. Note that Regis forecasts assume daily traffic and revenues of 36k and P4mn, respectively, in 2023; Regis expects CCLEX to incur a net loss of P1.4bn next year. CCLEX is a 99%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and accounts for c. 1% of gross asset value. MPI trades at a 48% discount to its market-based NAV of P6.45/sh.

SMC files registration for P60bn bonds

Ang-led San Miguel Corp (SMC) filed the registration statement and preliminary prospectus for its P60bn fixed rate bonds to be used for redemption of preferred shares and bonds, and debt repayments. The base offer amounts to P40bn with an oversubscription of up to P20bn and will be issued at 100% face value. The prospectus stated the net proceeds for the offer will be used for the optional redemption of the company’s series 2H preferred shares and up to P13.15bn for final redemption and payment of the company’s series E bonds. Up to P17bn will be used to repay company’s $300mn facility with MUFG Bank set to mature in 2Q23. In July, SMC subsidiary SMC Global Power Holdings raised P40bn from the issuance of bonds.

Cezar Consing officially appointed as Ayala Corp president, CEO

Ayala Corp announced the official appointment of Cezar “Bong” Consing as its president and chief executive officer, officially taking over the post vacated by Fernando Zobel de Ayala. Consing was first appointed as interim chief on August 15 after Zobel de Ayala took a medical leave of absence. Zobel de Ayala has since resigned from his position in the conglomerate and other companies under the group. Prior to his appointment, Consing served as president and CEO of the Bank of the Philippine Islands from 2013 to 2021. He also worked with JP Morgan in Hong Kong and Singapore from 1985 to 2004 and headed the firm’s investment banking business in Asia Pac from 1997 to 2004. Consing also served as partner at the Rohatyn Group from 2004 to 2013.

Ayalas, Gokongweis to merge bank units

A possible merger is in the works between Ayala-led BPI and Robinsons Bank Corp. (RBC) of the Gokongwei Group to form the country’s second largest lender in terms of assets. BPI confirmed it was in talks with Robinsons Bank over a potential collaboration and it will make further disclosures at the appropriate time. Merger will reportedly be done through a share swap, wherein the Gokongwei group would end up with a 6% stake in BPI.  BPI would emerge as the surviving entity, with BPI becoming the second largest bank in terms of assets. BPI is the third largest private bank in the country in terms of assets, with P2.35tn, and second in terms of capital, with P297.37bn as of end-March. RBC is 17th in terms of assets, with P175.9bn, and 15th in terms of capital, with P16.7bn. BPI is reportedly after the digital bank license of GoTyme Bank according to a source of the Philippine Star. The Gokongweis own a 60% stake in GoTyme while Singapore-based digital banking group has the remaining 40%.

Supreme Court issues ruling regarding taxes on POGOs

The Supreme Court declared a provision of the Bayanihan 2 Act unconstitutional because it was unrelated to the subject of the law (this type of provision is called a “rider”). Bayanihan 2 had a provision taxing POGOs – 5% of gross bets. This ruling is arguably moot since a subsequent law, RA 11590 “An Act Taxing Philippine Offshore Gaming Operations” supersedes that provision and declared that POGOs would be taxed based on 5% of gross gaming revenue. MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

More hot money flees in August

Foreign portfolio investments recorded a net outflow of US$86mn in August 2022. This was lower than the US$103mn net outflow recorded in July 2022 but was a reversal of the US$12mn net inflow in August 2021. This is the fourth straight month of hot money outflows. Note that the PSE recorded foreign net outflows of US$152mn in August, up from US$80mn foreign net outflow in July. Gross inflows in August rose 16% MoM to US$792mn, of which 76% came from PSE-listed securities. The gross outflows in August rose 12% MoM to US$878mn. The YT-August foreign portfolio investments stood at a net inflow of US$539mn (vs. net outflow of US$434mn in the same period last year).

BSP may relax single-borrower limit to promote ESG

The BSP said it may relax the single borrower’s limit and rules for directors, officers, stakeholders and related interests for ESG lending according to the BSP’s Twitter account, citing Governor Felipe Medalla. There is currently a cap on the loans, credit accommodations and guarantees that may be extended by a bank to any individual or corporation at 25% of its net worth.

Bicam approves SIM card registration bill

The bicameral conference committee gave its nod to the subscriber identity module (SIM) registration bill. The bicameral’s approval happened on the same day the Senate passed its own bill on its third and final reading. The Bill seeks to eradicate crimes aided by SIM, such as scam text messages, by requiring end-users to submit their personal information. The Lower House’s version approved fines ranging from P5,000 to P1mn on telcos, their authorized SIM card sellers, and even government workers who fail to comply with the law. Both the Senate and Lower House’s versions removed the social media provision. Recall that former President Duterte vetoed the bill in April citing the inclusion of a provision that required social media accounts to be registered. The Bill will likely make headline subscriber count lower and ARPUs higher; It could also pave the way for accelerated adoption of credit processes via mobile.

Treasury partially awards 182-day T-bill; Rejects bids for 91-, 364-day T-bills

The Bureau of Treasury only raised P3.35bn out of its total P5bn goal for the 182-day T-bill. The 182-day T-bill yields rose by 14.8bps to 3.958%; The yield was 25.4bps higher than the 3.704% prevailing rate at the secondary market. Meanwhile, the Treasury rejected bids for the 91-day and 364-day T-bills, as rates would have risen by more than 200bps. Had it fully accepted the tenders for the 91-day T-bill, the yield would have surged by 207.9 bps to average 4.397%, up from 2.318% in the previous award last Sept. 5. Similarly, a full award would have raised the rate on the 364-day debt paper by 110.6 bps to an average of 4.888% from 3.782% last Aug. 22.

IMF sees Philippine economy cooling in 2023

The IMF revised its GDP growth projections for the Philippines, as it sees the economy slowing to 5% in 2023 from 6.5% this year. IMF previously forecasted 2022/2023 GDP growth rates at 6.7%/6.3% and cited the confluence of global shocks to weigh on the economy in the coming months. Bloomberg consensus forecast at 6.8% for 2022 and 5.8% for 2023; Regis FY22E/FY23E at 6%/6.5%.
