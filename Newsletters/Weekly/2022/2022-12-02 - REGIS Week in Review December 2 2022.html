REGIS REPORT

02 December 2022

The market ended the week lower, closing at 6,489.65 (-1.8% WoW) after three straight weeks of gains. Selling pressure accelerated mainly due to the MSCI rebalancing that occurred last Tuesday as Converge (-4.37% WoW), MPI (-1.13% WoW), GT Capital (+1.77% WoW) were deleted from the index. Additionally, profit taking ensued among both local and foreign investors after the market has ran-up sharply (+18% since September lows). Foreigners are back as net sellers for $44.6m after being buyers for the last three weeks.

Consequently, turnover improved to US$217.7m (double of the previous week’s ADV). Among the top gainers for the week were conglomerate laggards AGI (+3.65% WoW) and JGS (+3.56% WoW) while consumer names fell across the board with JFC (-6.69% WoW), WLCON (-5.79% WoW), and MONDE (-5.56% WoW).

On corporate news, ACEN (+3.52% WoW) was an outperformer this week after it disclosed its acquisition of a 60MW solar plant in Pangasinan while property names RLC (-2.91% WoW), SMPH (-2.71% WoW), and ALI (-1.02% WoW) remain depressed as the continued implementation of work-from-home policies keep office demand below pre-COVID levels, thus prolonging the sector’s recovery. SMC (-1.86% WoW) and MER (-1.66% WoW) continues to trade lower having finally secured a restraining order against the ERC.

Lastly, the peso also continues to gain strength at 55.765 vs the dollar likely as remittances grew +3.1% to US$23.6bn YT-Sept (+13% in PHP terms).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

San Miguel secures restraining order against ERC; Marcos hopes CA will reconsider

San Miguel Global Power (SMGPL) secured a 60-day temporary restraining order against the Energy Regulatory Commission. The ERC previously denied the petition of SMGPL and Meralco (MER PM) to amend its power supply agreements (PSA) in order to raise tariffs for its fixed price contracts. The petition was ostensibly filed in order to avoid even higher prices should SMGPL choose to terminate the PSAs. Notably, SMGPL did not file a motion of reconsideration (MR) with the ERC. The TRO suspends its power supply agreement (PSA) with Meralco and allows SMPL to sell at higher prices via the spot market. Meralco will be forced to buy at higher prices too, which will lead to higher prices for consumers. It is curious how the CA can justify the TRO when SMGPL did not exhaust its legal remedies through an MR with ERC. The ERC rejected the changes to the PSAs, so it is also unusual that the CA can issue TRO suspending it as SMGPL did not seek to legally seek to terminate / suspend it with the ERC. SMGPL is incurring large losses selling at fixed price under the PSAs, given where fuel prices are SMGPL did not seek to hedge or buy forward its fuel requirements but is instead asking consumers to pay more. Meanwhile, President Marcos issued a statement saying he hopes the CA will reconsider its order. The statement will raise pressure for the CA to retract its TRO.

Puregold announces purchase of PSMT Philippines

The board of directors of Kareila Management Corp. greenlit the purchase of 100% capital stock of the Co-led PSMT Philippines for P112.5mn on November 25. Kareila Management Corp. is the owner and operator of S&R membership shopping warehouses.

DMCI eyeing more subway packages

The construction arm of DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) wants to bid for three more subway contracts together with its foreign partners. The company is waiting for the government to open the bidding likely by January 2023. Meanwhile, the company is aiming to start work on Subway package 102 in 2Q23, after right-of-way issues are cleared . The subway package 102 is valued at P21bn and DMCI’s attributable order book portion is P6.8bn, which excludes DMCI’s allied services (project support). The construction is estimated to be over a 5-year period. DMCI’s order book stood at P45bn in end-Sep 2022.

ACEN to acquire developer of 60MW solar plant in Pangasinan

ACEN Corp said that it has signed a share purchase agreement with Sungrow Power Renewables Corp and Havilah AAA holdings for the acquisition of Sinocalan Solar Power Corp (SSPC). SPPC, the developer of a 60 MW solar power plant in Pangasinan will become a wholly-owned subsidiary upon the completion of the acquisition scheduled on December 15. ACEN currently has a generation portfolio of 3,704MW. Around 58% are operating while the balance is under construction. Subsidiary ACE Enexor, meanwhile, informed the stock exchange of its name change to ENEX Energy Corp effective on December 9.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Bank lending rises 13.9% YoY in October

U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) accelerated to 13.9% YoY in October from 13.4% in September. This is the 15th straight month of loan growth. On a MoM basis, loans rose +0.6%, buoyed by consumer loans (+2.2%). Recall that loan growth for the entire banking system rose by +10.7% in September. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated slightly to +5.4% in October from +5.2% in September.

T-bond yield drops, first time in 3 months

The Bureau of Treasury partially awarded P22.97bn worth of re-issued 20-year T-bonds on offer with a remaining life of four years and nine months. The 5-year T-bonds fetched an average rate of 6.568%, 6.3bps below BVAL (6.631%). The yield is 56bps lower than the 7.131% yield during the last 5-year T-bond on offer on Nov 7. The yield is also lower than the 8.625% rate when the T-bond was first issued in 2007. Yields went from a low of 6.475% and a high of 6.625%. National Treasurer Rosalia de Leon said that they took advantage to compress rates closer to secondary market levels. Markets also took a signal from the slower pace of rate hikes by the US Fed and the BSP. Total bids reached P60.514bn, oversubscribing the auction by 1.87 times, and 45% higher than the previous five-year offer that fetched P41.6bn.

IT-BPM sector on track to meet 2022 targets

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it was on track to reach 1.56mn employees (+8% from 2021) and US$32bn in revenue (+8% from 2021). These are in line with the association’s 2022 targets. The BPO sector is one of the major drivers of office demand. However, Regis expects continued implementation of work-from-home policies to keep office demand below pre-COVID levels.

Gov’t raises P35.5bn from T-bill sales in November

The government raised P9.62bn in T-bills, lower than its intended amount of P15bn. Rates went up from secondary markets, prompting the Treasury to only partially accept offers. The 91-day T-bill yielded 4.205%, lower by 2.8bps from the previous auction but above the secondary rate of 4.177%. The 182-day T-bill yielded 4.92%, lower by 0.04bps from the previous auction (BVAL: 4.8236%). Meanwhile, the 364-day T-bill yielded 5.15%, higher by 6.9bps (BVAL: 5.13%). The auction was 2.4x oversubscribed, with bids reaching P35.8bn. The latest auction marked the 15th straight week of T-bills’ offer that the Treasury failed to raise its intended amount. The government raised a total of P35.5bn in November, short of its P75bn target.

Budget deficit widens in October

The National Government’s budget deficit ballooned to P99.1bn in October from P64bn in October 2021. On a MoM basis, the budget deficit was lower by 45% due to a sharp drop in expenditures. Tax revenues jumped 19% YoY, bringing 10M22 tax revenues up 18% YoY (trending ahead of the government’s target of +13.5% YoY). Expenses in October rose 22% YoY, as primary expenditures (i.e. excluding interest payments) jumped 24% YoY. The 10M22 budget deficit at P1.11tn, down by 7.6% YoY and trending lower than the government’s target. For 2023, the government expects the deficit to GDP ratio to be lower at 6.1% from 7.6% in FY22 and 8.6% in 2021.

World Bank sees slower growth in remittances in 2023

The World Bank expects remittances to the Philippines to grow by +2% in 2023 from +3.6% in 2022. The BSP is projecting remittances to grow by +4% in 2022/2023. The World Bank cited a global slowdown could slash migrants’ wage gains in host countries. In YT-Sep, remittances grew by +3.1% to US$23.8bn (in PHP terms, remittances are up by +13%).
