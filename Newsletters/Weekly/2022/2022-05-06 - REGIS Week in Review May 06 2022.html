REGIS REPORT

06 May 2022

The PSEi was marginally higher this week (+0.4% WoW), post last week’s 3.8% drop. Volumes were light as investors stayed on the sidelines ahead of the elections next Monday (May 9).  In the latest major opinion poll, Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr maintained his commanding lead with over 50% of preferences.

Gainers outnumbered losers 3:2 this week.  Leading the gainers were Globe (+8.55% WoW) and URC (+6.3%. WoW) with sentiment helped by 1Q results. The results-filled week actually clearly demonstrated the impact of reopening.

Both URC (+6.3% WoW) and CNPF (-3.3% WoW) reported robust revenue growth with the branded businesses enjoying strong demand.  Both companies managed to beat expectations despite input cost inflation. Also reporting beats were San Miguel Food and Beverage (-2.5% WoW) with a bigger-than-expected rebound from food (1Q22 profits 82x QoQ) – and D&L (+2.1% W0W) which saw a strong volume and margin jump from its oleochemical segment.

Telcos PLDT (+1.3% WoW) and Globe (+8.55% WoW) also managed to beat expectations. For PLDT, broadband did well but mobile was soft. Losses at Voyager climbed further ahead of the launch of its digital bank.  Meanwhile, Globe’s fintech unit Mynt drove the outperformance, swinging to profitability from a loss the prior year.  On operations, broadband fell short but wireless performed better.

ICTSI (+2.1% WoW) also beat expectations.  Profits for 1Q jumped 80% YoY as yields surprised to a record US$183 (+14% YoY).  EBITDA margins also reached record levels. Management reiterated its optimism on the sustainability of its yields, as it plans to implement more tariff hikes across its portfolio in succeeding periods. The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) will implement an 8% tariff increase beginning May 14 for the ports in Manila. This should lift revenues for ICT”s Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) which accounts for 28% of ICTSI’s revenue. 

Another beat was delivered by Metrobank (+3.4% WoW). 1Q numbers came in well ahead of full year estimates as provisions were lower than expected.  Metrobank’s loan book grew 5.2% YoY, the first positive YoY print since the start of the pandemic in 1Q20.

Monde (+3.1% WoW) also gained after an increase to its weighting in the PSEi  in the upcoming rebalancing next week.

Meanwhile, declines were seen in Alliance Global(-6.9% WoW), JG Summit (-3.4% WoW), Wilcon (-3.4% WoW), and Meralco (-2.4% WoW).  Meralco has been ordered by the regulator to refund P7.8bn to consumers in relation to a tariff revision retroactive to 2011. The ERC decision stems from review of the distribution rates during the 3rd Regulatory Period (“3RP”), which was from July 2011 to June 2015. This order  suggests that the regulator is undertaking a process of re-calculating tariffs for the lapsed period (which would have been 4RP) and not simply allowing the interim tariff of P1.38/kwh to stand. Meralco has set aside over P50bn (our estimate) in provisions relating to possible refund.

On macro news, The CPI print for April was +4.9%, up from 4.0% in March and above Street expectations (4.6%).

Foreign outflows continued, with  US$69m exiting this week. Large cap conglos SM and Ayala Corp saw outflow of US$6.5m and US$2.7m, respectively. Meanwhile, ICT (US$1.7m) and Globe (US$1.5m) saw inflows.

Universal Robina - Reopening drives 1Q22 outperformance
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q22 net profit jumps 16% YoY; strong revenues drive outperformance

URC reported 1Q22 net profit of P3.5bn, up 16% YoY, trending ahead of Regis and consensus estimates. The outperformance was largely driven by strong revenue growth across the board (+22% YoY), even after considering Munchy’s, which added c5% to the consolidated top line. The strong revenues overshadowed EBIT margin compression (180bps YoY) from the surge in input cost.

Solid top line recovery across branded consumer food (BCF) segments

Total BCF revenues grew +22% YoY, boosted by the consolidation of Munchy’s. In the Philippines, nearly all categories saw >20% growth as mobility restrictions were lifted. Most notable improvements were in RTD tea (+90%) and cup noodles (+52%) – categories that are typically consumed on-the-go. Meanwhile, key international markets saw double-digit growth as economies likewise started to reopen.

Input cost pressures persist but pricing adjustments provide some relief

Total BCF EBIT contracted >200bps YoY as the surge in key raw materials (wheat, palm oil, packaging) weighed on margins. The impact of price adjustments provided some relief. URC raised prices by an average of 5% across the board in January, bringing the cumulative price increase to c8% since 2Q21 by our estimate. Another round of price increases will be implemented in May, and further in 2H22.

Recovery to continue but expect near term volatility; maintain Buy

Management is guiding for low- to mid-teens top line growth in 2022 (Regis FY22E: +17%) as the momentum will likely be sustained as economies reopen. However, growth trends and margins over the next few quarters could be volatile given 1) base effects resulting from various lockdowns across markets in 2Q/3Q21 and 2) the delayed impact of price adjustments. Near-term performance is likely to be volatile, but underlying trends suggest that URC’s scale and market leadership are key advantages in navigating the difficult cost environment. We update our forecasts for FY21 actuals, and tweak our 2022-23E EPS estimates (+5-8%) and TP (+4% to P128/sh). On our revised forecasts, the stock trades at 18x 2022E PE, a discount to its peers and its historical average. Maintain Buy.

 
ICTSI - 1Q22: Another positive yield surprise
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q22 profit up 80% YoY to US$132mn – Ahead of estimates

1Q22 profit to common jumped 80% YoY to US$132mn, trending above Regis and street estimates. Yields and income from associates outperformed, which more than made up for the volume shortfall. 1Q22 EBITDA improved further QoQ/YoY to US$338mn. Despite short-term disruptions to global trade due to the lingering effects of the pandemic and the war, management reiterated its optimism on its yield outlook.

Volumes soft in 1Q, up 5% YoY; Yield per TEU likely to stay above US$180

Global supply chain issues and the war weighed on container volumes in the Americas (-5% YoY) and EMEA (+3%) regions. Despite a strong volume rebound in Asia (+12%), group volumes just rose by +5% YoY in 1Q22. Volume growth accelerated in April following the resolution of various issues across a number of locations. However, management is wary that the lockdowns in China could affect this. Yield per TEU rose sharply to US$183 (+14% YoY/+8% QoQ) on better volume mix, tariff hikes at some ports, and higher storage revenues. With the tariff hike in Manila approved this month, as well as tariff hikes in Mexico and Melbourne, management is confident yields will hold up in succeeding periods.

EBITDA margin improves further; input cost inflation largely controlled

EBITDA margin reached a record 64% in 1Q22, up 210bps QoQ and 320bps YoY. Yield increases have so far more than offset the 11% YoY increase in cash opex. On an organic basis, cash opex rose by +9% YoY, reflecting increases in manpower and fuel costs.

Focus still on de-leveraging, but remains on the lookout for acquisitions

We estimate net debt-to-EBITDA (i.e. treating perps, concession liab, and lease liabilities as debt) was lower at 3.5x in end-March from 3.77x in end-2021. We believe the company’s leverage ratio will decline further in succeeding periods, as we estimate it can generate sufficient free cash flow to pay down debt (company paid US$216mn worth of perps today) without sacrificing dividends. Management is however continuing to look for ports to acquire globally (e.g. Indonesia, Turkey, Mumbai, Africa, and Central America).

PLDT, Inc - Broadband saves 1Q from weakness in mobile
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income lifted by broadband segment

PLDT reported net income for 1Q22 at P9.08bn, which included two rather lumpy items: P4.8bn of manpower reduction costs and a P7.8bn gain from the extinguishing of redemption liabilities on its prefs. Excluding those and other exceptional items, we estimate core income at P7.47bn (+3%), inclusive of losses at Voyager. We reckon this is mildly ahead of Street expectations as broadband delivered solid numbers, compensating for the surprise weakness in the mobile business.

Broadband revenue rises 22%

Revenue from home broadband continued to grow at a fast pace, rising 22% YoY. This segment is now 25% of service revenue from <20% in FY20. Net adds for wired broadband, however, slowed down a bit to 167k in 1Q22, from 198k in 4Q.

Weak quarter for mobile

Notwithstanding the relative “re-opening” of the economy by Feb, the mobile business delivered surprisingly weak numbers. Revenue was down 5% YoY and QoQ. The softness wasn’t from lack of usage as mobile data traffic rose 15% QoQ. Data yields, however, dropped 10% QoQ, due to what we believe were aggressive promotions offering “unlimited” data. Between itself and Globe, we estimate PLDT ceded 2ppt of revenue market share this period.

Losses at Voyager rise, higher capex and profit guidance

Losses from Voyager amounted to P729m in 1Q, up +42%, as the firm invested in start-up costs for its digital bank, among other things. With the re-branding of its e-wallet and the launch of its bank in 2Q, losses are likely to remain elevated. Following the deal to sell half its telco towers for P77bn, PLDT also lifted its guidance for capex to P85bn and “telco core income” to P33bn for the year. We are reviewing our estimates, target price and rating following recent developments, which include the latest results and the sale of its tower assets.

 
Century Pacific Food - 1Q22: Opex control cushions input cost inflation
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q22 net profit +10% YoY as lower opex cushions input cost inflation

CNPF reported 1Q22 net profit of P1.4bn, up 10% YoY, trending ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. Total revenues grew 10% YoY, driven mostly by the branded segment (82% of revenues). While input cost inflation weighed on gross margin, the impact to EBITDA margin was cushioned by proportionately lower opex.

Branded business: demand resilient amidst price increases

Revenues from CNPF’s branded segments (+17% YoY) were the driver the of top line as OEM revenues (-14% YoY) were dragged by supply chain issues. Growth was broad-based across the branded portfolio (marine, meat, and milk) and volumes remained healthy despite price increases. CNPF implemented an average price increase of +4-5% during the quarter, bringing the cumulative price increase to at least 6% since 4Q21 by our estimate.

Opex control cushions input cost inflation

The price adjustments were not enough to fully cover the increase in raw material prices, resulting in a 30-bps YoY contraction in gross margin during the quarter. However, EBITDA margin managed to expand 70bps YoY due to tight cost control measures. Among these cost control measures include scaling back on new product launches and being more conservative on A&P/trade spending.

Top line momentum to be sustained; further price adjustments planned

Top line momentum for the branded segments has been sustained into early 2Q and management believes it is on track to achieve double-digit revenue growth in 2022 (Regis FY22E: +16%). Prices of key inputs (skipjack tuna, packaging, milk) have been locked in for most of 2022 but we expect near-term margins to stay subdued as the full impact of price adjustments will not be felt until late this year.

Philippine Property - Work-from-home and the long-term demand for office space
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Work-from-home vs. return-to-office

Concentrix, which has 100,000 employees across the country, is opting to continue implementing its work-from-home (WFH) policies even at the risk of losing its fiscal incentives. This development could prompt other BPO companies to follow suit, lest they lose in the battle to attract talent. Ultimately, this could have far-reaching implications on the entire office property sector.

Why would BPO companies lose incentives because of WFH?

Most BPOs are registered with the Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA), which requires at least 70% of employees to work in the PEZA zone/building. When COVID struck, the government waived the requirement for BPO employees to be physically in the office. However, earlier this year, the government announced that, starting April, 70% of employees of BPOs enjoying PEZA incentives must once again report to the office on the pain of losing tax perks. After tasting the WFH life, the resistance to return to the office was understandable. In particular, several BPO employees returned to their home provinces during the pandemic, and many threatened to resign if they were forced to return to the office.

Expansion projects can get incentives through BOI

BPOs cannot transfer existing operations from PEZA to other incentive-granting agencies. However, our channel checks suggest that most BPOs are expanding through the Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have a requirement regarding the proportion of employees in the office. This means that expansion projects can still receive fiscal incentives even if BPO firms implement WFH.

The rise of WFH will have implications on future office space demand

Very few tenants have expressed intention to reduce office space so far, and many even signed new office (expansion) contracts this year. Nevertheless, this development, if it becomes a trend, could have major repercussions on future demand for office space. The required office space per employee will likely come down, particularly for expansions.

Metro Manila office vacancy at 19%; expect a long bottoming out process

Despite positive net office take-up, Metro Manila office vacancy has still been rising and is now at 19% (1Q22) from 18% (4Q21), due to new supply. Rental rates also remain 20%-30% below pre-pandemic levels. While we believe the worst is behind us, continued implementation of WFH could affect the pace and magnitude of near-term recovery and lead to a longer bottoming-out process for the office segment.

Filinvest Land and Megaworld are the most exposed to office leasing

Filinvest Land and Megaworld are the most exposed to office leasing, as this business comprises over 45% of their NAV (see figure 3 on page 4).

 
Ayala Corp. - Spreading the gains
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Ayala Corp distributing part of its ACEN stake to shareholders

Ayala Corp (AC) disclosed that it will distribute part of its stake in its clean energy arm ACEN (ACEN PM) to its shareholders through a property dividend. The exercise will not only provide a modest value boost to AC, whose shares are trading at considerable discount to NAV. It will also raise ACEN’s public float to above 20%.

Three ACEN shares for each AC share held

Ayala Corp declared a property dividend in relation to 1.86bn of the shares in ACEN it owned and will be distributing those shares to AC shareholders at ratio of three ACEN shares for each AC share held. After the exercise, AC will still own 22.9bm shares of ACEN, equivalent to 58% of the latter’s capital.

Value enhancement to AC shareholders

ACEN has been one of the most rewarding investments of AC in recent years as current market values are multiples above what AC invested in the business. The property dividend will provide shareholders part of that value while giving them the option to either keep the ACEN shares or sell them for cash. AC shareholders will receive the ACEN shares at full value (before taxes, if applicable) when AC shares are trading at 31% discount to NAV. At last Friday’s close, this distribution is equivalent to 3% dividend yield.

Raises ACEN’s public float to above 20%

ACEN’s current public float is below the 20% required for it to stay a component member of the PSE’s main benchmark index, the PSEi (“PCOMP”). This distribution will raise the float to 21% after taking into account the transactions that are currently pending. Remaining part of the PSEi is generally expected to improve trading activity and valuations.

MBT 1Q22 NPAT P8.0bn, well ahead on lower provisions; loans +5%, first +ve since 1Q20

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported NPAT of P8.0bn for 1Q22, up just 3% YoY but tracking well ahead of REGIS (P27.2bn) and the Street (P27.6bn). The beat was driven largely by much lower provisions than what Regis was expecting, as NPLs and cover continue to improve. Loans were up 5% YoY, the first positive YoY growth print since 1Q2020. NIMs were slightly lower QoQ though, as the bank built up its treasury/placement book, funding it through TDs and borrowings. MBT also booked surprisingly strong trading/fx gains and “miscellaneous” income.

 

Some highlights:

Loans grew 5.2% YoY, the bank’s first positive print since 1Q2020, just before the pandemic. Growth was driven by corporate loans (+10%) and credit cards (+8%).

NIMs dipped 4bps QoQ, despite higher loans yields and flat deposit costs. This was due to a surge in time deposits (+28% QoQ) and bills payable (+40% QoQ), for an incremental P160bn QoQ increase. The additional liquidity went largely into placements with the BSP and other banks. Regis suspects the bank will either be paying down loans, or will be gapping out its treasury book soon as yields have risen considerably at longer tenors. LDR dropped below 70% in 1Q22.

Fee incomes were up 8% YoY to P3.6bn, normalizing well above pre-Covid levels. The 19% YoY jump in non-interest income was driven by trading/fx gains, as well as a 39% surge in “miscellaneous” income.

Opex was up just 1% YoY with compensation expenses actually down 1.6% YoY.

Provision expense fell 18% YoY to P2bn, as NPL ratio stood at 2.16% (was 2.2% at end-2021) and NPL cover at 179% (vs 175%). Overall a pretty strong set of results.

Cemex Philippines’ (CHP PM) 1Q22 profit jumps 27% YoY, EBITDA up 2%

CHP reported 1Q21 profit of P261mn, up 27% YoY and tracking ahead of consensus (P931mn) and Regis forecasts. EBITDA stood at P1.05bn, up 2% YoY and was in line with consensus but above Regis forecasts. Regis attributes the beat to lower-than-expected power & distribution costs, non-cash, and financial expenses, which Regis believes is not sustainable. Revenues grew 1% (in line), after ASPs jumped +7% YoY (+3% QoQ) to offset input cost inflation. Based on data from the Bureau of Customs, Regis estimates that the landed cost of imports rose to US$67/MT in 1Q22, up 6% YoY and flat QoQ. Volumes however dropped 6% YoY due to lingering effects of Covid and typhoon Odette. Management noted that volumes of importers grew +10-12% YoY during the quarter, suggesting CHP lost share. The company is guiding volumes to grow by +7% YoY this year, with most of the growth pushed back to 2H22 on low base effects. Cash expenses were flat YoY and up 3% QoQ as input costs were contained, as Regis estimates power and distribution costs per MT were lower by 9% YoY and 18% YoY, respectively. Management noted that it has secured long-term contracts for power and made “arrangements” for fuel (thermal coal and diesel) requirements for the year, suggesting that EBITDA margins could be maintained. Non-cash expenses dropped 14% YoY due to timing of interest capitalization for its new clinker line. CHP is trading at 4.3x FY22E (consensus) EV/EBITDA, lower than historical average (7x).

Globe results

Core income P5.1bn, ahead of expectations.  Globe’s headline profit in 1Q of P13.7bn (+86% YoY) included P10.5bn of gains from the partial sale of its data center business.  Stripping that and other exceptional items out, core income was P5.1bn (+3%), which is trending ahead of estimates. Contributions from Mynt were the main factor for the positive variance, with costs also being another factor. Cash costs were steady YoY, which helped magnify the 4% growth in revenue down the P&L.

 

Wireless business up, broadband business down.  In an exact reversal of PLDT’s own 1Q, the wireless business did well. Revenue for the quarter was up 3% QoQ, 4% YoY.  Regis estimates GLO’s revenue share rose by 2ppt in the quarter but it may have been skewed a bit by how the impact of the typhoon was accounted for.  Broadband, meanwhile, surprised to the downside again.  Revenue for this segment was down -4% YoY with the loss of more fixed wireless subscribers.  Wired broadband subscribers crossed the 1m mark, rising 65k to 1.03m.

Mynt contributes P327m to profit.  Globe’s share of Mynt’s profit in 1Q was P327m, a sharp turnaround from the P282m loss the prior year.  It’s also further improvement from the P76m profit in 4Q.

Bulk prices surge to over 10-year high

Nationwide bulk prices of general goods rose to their highest level in more than a decade in February due to higher fuel costs, preliminary data from PSA showed. The country’s general wholesale price index (GWPI) jumped 5.6% in February, from 4.6% in January and 2.5% in February 2021. This was the highest reading in more than 10 years or since the 5.8% growth recorded in December 2011. YTD, the national GWPI averaged 5.1%, faster than the 2.3% in the same period in 2021. The GWPI is u sed to monitor the wholesale trade sector and serves as a basis for price adjustments in business contracts and projects. The heavily weighted food index, which contributed 36.8% in the theoretical wholesale basket of goods, went up 4.9%YoY in February from 3.9% the previous months. Manufactured goods classified by materials increased 6.6% from 6.4% the previous month. Minerals fuels, lubricants, and related materials surged 37.7% in February, from 30.4% the previous month and 2.1% in February 2021. Crude materials, inedible except fuels, also jumped 26.7% from 16.3% in January. Luzon outpaced the national level’s GWPI with 5.9% in February, faster than the 4.7% in January and 2.8% in Feb 2021.

Inflation surges to 3-year high in April

Inflation surged to 4.9% in April, the highest level recorded since December 2018 and higher than in March 2022. This is higher than the BSP’s target range for the year (2-4%) and near the upper bound of its 4.2%-5% forecast range for the month and also higher than street estimates (median: +4.6%). Prices of food jumped by 3.8%, faster than the previous month while electricity, gas, fuel inflation increased further by 20% from 17% in March. Transport inflation also picked up to 13% from 10% the previous month. This brings 4M22 average inflation rate to 3.7%. BSP Governor Diokno previously signaled the Monetary Board could raise the policy rates in June. The decision will depend on 1Q22 GDP growth data (if GDP grows by 6-7%, it may consider a rate hike in June). The government will release 1Q22 GDP figures on May 12 (street estimate: +6%). Regis is forecasting key policy rates to rise to 2.5% by year-end from 2% currently.

 

National debt soars to record P12.68tn in March

The outstanding debt of the Philippines soared to P12.68tn in March. This is higher than in end-2021 (P11.7tn) and pre-pandemic (P7.7tn). Assuming the government’s nominal GDP target for FY22 (P21.5tn), the national debt is equivalent to 58.8% of GDP. Around 69.9% of debt is domestic while 30% is foreign. By maturity, 5% is short term, 29% is medium term, and 66% is long term.
