REGIS REPORT

20 May 2022

PSEi finally gets a “good” week gaining +5.8% to end the week at 6,746. It would be interesting to see what happens next week as the 6,800 level looks like a resistance which we hope eventually gets broken but let’s just wait and see. Foreigners were 47% of the activity this week and slightly net sellers as we saw a net foreign outflow of around -$10m. This brings total net outflow for the year to -$376.4m.

The top index gainers for the week were Globe Telecom +13.9%, AC Energy +13.3% and Monde Nissin +11.3% while the only index losers were Alliance Global -5.7% and Emperador -3.2%.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised policy rates by 25bp to 2.25% this week, the first increase in RRP since 2018 and the first after sharp reductions to cushion the impact of the pandemic. While yesterday’s rate hike was already widely anticipated, that was not the case until very recently. The BSP had been hinting at a possible hike only in June before the latest GDP and inflation data. The rate action is one of the first steps towards a normalization of monetary policy. Policy rates are still 175bp below what they were immediately before the monetary stimulus response to the pandemic. The BSP’s balance sheet also expanded eight-fold to unprecedented levels over the period, in the Philippines’ version of quantitative easing. This QE will ultimately have to be unwound too, with the re-payment of the loan to the Treasury (P540bn in this chart) paid down fully today, a first step.

Earnings continue to trickle in. GT Capital (+9.8% WoW) disclosed during their briefing that (1) Toyota intends to raise prices across all models to keep GPMs above 10%; (2) its 162k (+25% YoY) unit sales target for the year can be easily achieved, given improved access to bank financing and minimal risks from global supply chain issues; and (3) GTCAP could book a gain on the transfer of its landbank this year and next year. Overall, Regis believes earnings upgrades are likely. The shares trade at an 18% discount to its market-based NAV of P627/sh.

Security Bank (+6.2% WoW) earnings were in line. 1Q22 NPAT of P2.7bn was up 66% YoY, in line with Regis (FY22E of P10.9bn) but well ahead of consensus (P8.7bn). However, this was due mostly to a sharp drop in provision expense to just P80mn in the quarter. While this can be partly explained by NPLs falling 29bps QoQ to 3.65%, note that NPL cover fell to 90% from 93% at end-2021. Loans grew 8% YoY, driven by corporates (+11%) while consumer loans were still -4% YoY.

Ayala Corp (+5.5% WoW) reported a P7.8bn profit (+45% YoY) for 1Q looks which were within expectations, note it was lifted by exceptional gains at Globe. Without those extraordinary items, income would have been P5.8bn (below Street expectations) as contributions from property and power fell short.

A week after announcing an investment into Udenna’s casino projects, Bloomberry disclosed that it was acquiring roughly 280hectares of beachfront land about 60km from Manila for P7.55bn ($144mn) to develop into an integrated resort with mixed-use development. The property includes a golf resort and country club built in the 80s but is largely abandoned today. The recent announcements would push BLOOM’s portfolio to five properties in the Philippines.  It also raises the possibility that an equity raise could be on the cards to fund future development.

In other news, banks’ asset quality improved across the board in March, with NPL ratio (ex-interbank) down 16bps MoM to 4.20% from 4.36% in Feb. Notably, the nominal value of NPLs fell 2.6% MoM to P460bn from P471bn in February. Similar trends were seen in Past Due, and Restructured Loans with MoM improvements both in ratio and in PHP terms. NPL cover also increased to 88.4% from 86.1% the previous month. The improving ratio was helped by a further acceleration in loan growth to 5.8% YoY in March, from 5.3% in Feb. This marks the 12th consecutive month of accelerating YoY prints. Faster loan growth, falling NPLs, and higher BSP policy rates all support our overweight call for the banking sector.

This week the wage boards of Metro Manila and Western Visayas have approved an increase in minimum wage for workers in various sectors in the regions. For Metro Manila, workers were granted a P33 (+6%) wage hike to P570. Wages were previously hiked in November 2018 for about +5%. For Western Visayas (Region 6), the minimum wage has been increased by P55-110 (14-35%) to P420-450. Wages were previously hiked in Jan 2020 by around +13%. Additionally, three more regions will be implementing minimum wage increases over the next few weeks, following the lead in Metro Manila (NCR).  The magnitude of these new hikes is frankly a bit surprising, being in the range of 14% to 32%. With the rise in prices of basic goods, especially energy, the pressure for higher wages will be difficult to resist. This rise in wages will mean higher input costs for nearly all firms, as the initial inflation spark spreads its 2nd and 3rd order effects. From a macro perspective, CPI inflation figures are likely to stay higher for longer. Many businesses will thus face further cost pressures in the coming months, even if their workers are not being paid minimum wages. The positive effect of this, on the other hand, is that consumption spending at the base of the pyramid will likely be spurred.

Bloomberry Resorts: EBITDA doubles on continued GGR recovery, posts first income since 1Q20
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1Q22 marks first profit since 1Q20
BLOOM’s 1Q22 EBITDA doubled to P2.857bn, lifted by strong performances from its mass table and VIP segments. Total GGR continued to trend upward, supported by a strong recovery in volumes for VIP and a high win-rate for mass tables. Volumes were sequentially higher across the board, despite the Omicron surge in January, sustaining the reopening trend alongside loosening restrictions. BLOOM booked a profit of P687mn, its first time in the black since 1Q20. The numbers are on track to meet estimates, as we expect numbers to continue ramping up in succeeding quarters.

GGR up 12%QoQ despite Omicron; VIP volumes recover on new junkets
1Q22 GGR jumped to P8.927bn (12%QoQ, 30%YoY), with mass tables and VIP offsetting a soft contribution from slots. Underlying volumes for mass tables and slots both inched up sequentially despite the surge in case counts at the start of the year, while divergent win rate trends led to the performance disparity. Meanwhile, VIP volumes unexpectedly jumped 45%QoQ after dropping in 4Q due to the closure of junket rooms by big operators.

Foot traffic hitting 65% of pre-pandemic; hotel occupancy hitting >50%
Management said that foot traffic averaged close to 10k in the quarter (50% of pre-pandemic) and has been hitting 12-13k (65% of pre-pandemic) as of May, Since March, all restrictions on capacity have been lifted. Hotel room bookings have slowly climbed from mid 20s in March to over 50% in the most recent weeks. 

Focus shifts back to physical properties
With the economy largely free from any restrictions, the focus has shifted to live play (vs online initiatives). Apart from the >P20bn it plans to spend this year on Solaire North, the recently disclosed deal to buy into Udenna’s Cebu and Clark casino projects will mean substantial capex geared at capturing the recovery in casino visitation locally. Management clarified that the numbers involved for the
initial purchase are not big enough to require a financing plan. The construction costs will require one, however, but this will come after Solaire North is completed.

 
Ayala Corp: 1Q power and property results disappoint
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Globe’s divestment gain lifts 1Q earnings
Ayala Corp reported a net profit of P7.8bn for 1Q, +45% from the previous year. Its income, however, was boosted by its share of the gains (P2bn) from Globe’s partial divestment of its data center business. Without that, 1Q income would have been P5.8bn, which is tracking behind Street expectations. We saw disappointments in the numbers from Ayala Land (ALI PM) and power subsidiary ACEN (ACEN PM).

Earnings rebound at BPI
Earnings at BPI climbed 60% to P8.0bn with loan growth  recovering to +7% and loan loss provisions fell 31% to P2.5bn. The YoY growth in reported profit also appeared higher due to the write-down of deferred tax assets in the prior year.

Ayala Land and power segment disappoint
Contributions from both ALI and ACEN disappointed. ALI saw a rebound in mall revenue but sales cancellations at its residential segment led to a drop in revenue there. Earnings there were still up +14% YoY, however.

ACEN’s income, on the other hand, fell 68% to P405m in 1Q as it had to pay high prices for power in the spot market to fulfill its contract obligations. The company also took an upfront charge after buying out a customer contract to cap its losses there. AC’s other, non-listed, power subsidiary, meanwhile, saw contributions drop 28% to P0.6bn after the partial divestment of its coal  facility last year.

Outside its core investments, AC’s smaller holdings contributed an aggregate net loss of P0.4bn.

Digital initiatives of Globe show growth
Globe’s reported income of P13.7bn for 1Q included a lumpy gain from the partial sale of its data center operation. Core income would have otherwise been P5.1bn, flattish YoY. Mobile revenue starting to recover but broadband was a disappointment.

Arguably the key highlight from Globe’s 1Q was the rising profitability of its fintech business Mynt. Globe’s share of Mynt amounted to P327m in the quarter, a ~P600m turnaround from last year’s loss. Its e-wallet now has 60m registered users with GTV in March at P500bn (just under US$10bn).

 
LT Group: Bank profits make up for softness in tobacco; provision reversals continue
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph | Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q22 flat, but on track to meet estimates
LTG’s reported 1Q22 income was flat at P6.5bn (+1%). While this is tracking in line with estimates for FY22E, the quality of earnings is low. The contribution from PNB outperformed because of the reversal of loan loss provisions, offsetting the
softness from tobacco which stemmed from the delayed price hikes. We nevertheless expect tobacco’s contribution to catch up in succeeding periods to reflect the better margins from the late March price increase.

Tobacco profits -16%YoY on late price hike
Its share of profits from the tobacco unit fell 16%YoY to P4.2bn due to the delayed price increase, which was only implemented late March. Cigarette sales, on the other hand, improved 18%YoY to 9.6bn sticks, which was presumably influenced
by the timing of the price increase. Unit market share climbed slightly to 61.3%, after dipping in 4Q21. We believe volumes are likely to drop in 2Q, but wider margins could nevertheless lead to profit improvement.

PNB continues reversals of loan loss provisions
PNB’s contribution of P1.6bn was 58% higher than last year. The bank incurred trading losses in 1Q22 (as opposed to a P1.5bn gain last year) but chose to reverse P394mn worth of loan loss provisions (vs. a P2.1bn net provision in 1Q21). NPL coverage of 61% is largely unchanged from 4Q21 and 18ppts higher vs. 1Q21, still one of the lowest among major banks. Gross NPL ratio worsened to 10.86% (from 10.07% in 4Q21, 10.74% in 1Q21), and gross loans declined 7%YoY to P584bn.

Management added that while there were no ROPA sold in the quarter, they are expecting some P3.5bn in gains from a sale that was executed in April.

Tanduay volumes, margins up, while ABI under pressure from input costs
Income from Tanduay improved to P330mn (+38%QoQ, +42%YoY), with volumes growing 2% to 5.145mn cases. Gross margin improved by 1ppt, helped by a price hike that exceeded the excise tax increase. Meanwhile, ABI’s profits improved sequentially but was lower by 62%YoY to just P80mn on input cost pressures. Gross margin for ABI fell 7ppts vs the same period last year

 
Shakey’s Pizza: 1Q22 recovery continues but margins pressured by cost inflation
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q22 net profit surges to P76mn; EBITDA behind on margin compression
PIZZA’s 1Q22 net profit surged 2.7x to P76mn from the near-breakeven level in 1Q21. Revenues rose 26% YoY to P1.6bn, boosted by the consolidation of Potato Corner beginning March 2022. Ex-Potato Corner, top line growth was reportedly in the mid-teens, tracking broadly within our FY22 estimates. The profit underperformance was primarily due to margin compression resulting from input cost inflation. While cost pressures are likely to persist, we believe margins should improve sequentially given the proportionately larger contribution of Potato Corner in succeeding quarters.

SSSG +5% in 1Q22; channel mix shifts as dine-in recovers
SSSG during the quarter was +5%, and we estimate SSS levels are tracking >80% of pre-pandemic levels (vs ~70% in 4Q21). While the year started slow because of the Omicron surge in January, SSS saw a strong upward trend from Feb onwards. The recovery was driven by the resurgence in dine-in sales, which now accounts for 40% of SWS (vs 60% in 1Q19). As of March, 100% dine-in capacity was allowed in Metro Manila

Cost inflation crimps margins
EBITDA margin contracted 50bps YoY due mainly to input cost inflation as well as higher freight and logistics cost. Prices of key inputs (potatoes, cheese) have been contracted for most of the year, but supply chain disruptions have caused delays. Management has outlined several cost management strategies, such as menu optimization and SKU mix. We understand that the company did not adjust prices meaningfully in 1Q.

Potato Corner, operating leverage to drive margins sequentially
While cost pressures are likely to persist, management believes this can be mitigated by (1) the full-quarter consolidation of Potato Corner, which has higher margins on a stand-alone basis, and (2) operating leverage from the continued improvement in dine-in sales. 2Q top line trends continue to improve, with sales
reportedly tracking >90% of pre-pandemic levels.

 
Megaworld: Office occupancy and foot traffic improve in 1Q22
 
 
 

Higher residential margins boost operating profit
1Q22 net profit rose 30% YoY to P3.1bn. EBIT jumped 53% YoY to P4.7bn, ahead of REGIS forecast. Residential GPM climbed nearly 5ppt QoQ (c. 2ppt YoY) due to the higher proportion of revenue from lots. However, we expect margins to normalize at lower levels in the coming quarters due to higher steel prices.

Megaworld’s office segment is performing better than the industry and strong inquiries suggest that occupancy will improve further.

Office occupancy and foot traffic improve
Megaworld’s office vacancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 9%. This is much better than the industry’s 19% vacancy. While work-from-home has been in the news recently, management does not consider this a major risk. Even before the requirement for BPOs to return to the office by April, office foot traffic in MEG’s
major townships improved, with McKinley Hill (in Fort Bonifacio) office foot traffic quadrupling from a year ago.

Furthermore, MEG’s tenants have not signaled any intention to reduce office footprint. On the contrary, office inquiries increased and management is confident that there will be more contracts signed in 2H22.

Horizontal projects continue to drive residential sales
Reservations sales rose 12% YoY to P23.2bn, in line with REGIS forecast and management guidance. Provincial lots continue to sell well. As a result, horizontal projects now comprise 35% of residential sales, from only 9% in 2019 (32% in FY21).

Mall revenue grows 40%
Mall revenue grew 40% to P717mn, 47% of the 1Q19 figure. Although tenant sales are nearly 95% of the pre-COVID level, mall revenue has not caught up due to rental concessions, which should be gradually reduced throughout the year.

 
Cebu Air: International ops worse off in 1Q22, eclipsing domestic market gains
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Core net loss wider QoQ – Behind expectations
CEB reported 1Q22 headline net loss of P7.6bn. Excluding MTM losses on its convertibles and FX, we estimate its core net loss was wider QoQ at P5.1bn, trending behind Regis and street forecasts. EBITDA losses were also wider QoQ
at P2.2bn, as pax fares dropped, following the Omicron-induced travel restrictions locally and globally. This negated operational improvements in the domestic market, which only largely began in March. Despite the disappointing results, we remain upbeat on the recovery of CEB’s  operations.

Pax vols improve, QoQ drop in fares from limited int’l travel
Pax vols and seat load factors positively surprised, improving QoQ even after a slow start in Jan/Feb from Covid-related mobility restrictions. However, international travel was limited, particularly for key markets in Dubai, HK, and Korea, driving overall fares lower QoQ. This negated sustained QoQ improvements in domestic fares.

Cash opex jump broadly in line; Monthly cash burn rises to P1bn
Cash opex rose 19% QoQ due to higher jet kero prices and elevated maintenance costs. Jet kero is sharply higher in 2Q so far (US$133/bbl) relative to 1Q (US$107). This suggests margins will likely be squeezed in 2Q, as fares have only been rising gradually. As a result of the above issues, CEB’s cash burn per month was also larger in 1Q22 (P1bn) than in 4Q21 (P800mn).

Medium-term outlook remains positive
With travel restrictions slowly lifted in Feb for the domestic market and in 2Q for the international market, CEB saw its systemwide seat capacity revert to 58%/73%/92% of pre-Covid levels in March/April/May, trending better than forecast. Its domestic capacity alone is now at 110% of pre-Covid levels while its market share has risen further to 63% in May from only 53% pre-Covid. Management noted that fares are improving sequentially to help offset higher fuel costs. Meanwhile, talks with regulators on the upward adjustment of fuel surcharge continue.

SECB 1Q21 NPAT P2.7bn beats consensus, as provisions drop 80%

In a press release, Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1Q22 net profit grew 66% to P2.7bn, in line with Regis (FY22E P10.9bn), but well ahead of consensus (P8.7bn). However, the outperformance was due nearly entirely to a plunge in provision expense to just P80mn. This was likely due to a 29bps QoQ drop in NPLs to 3.65%. Loans grew 8% YoY, driven by corporate loans (+11%). The bank will hold a briefing on Wednesday, 18 May at 5pm.

Ahead of the briefing, some highlights from the press release:

Loans grew 8% YoY, surpassing 2019 levels. Growth was driven by corporate loans (+11%). Consumer loans were still down 4% YoY; despite looser credit underwriting, new loans were unable to overcome maturing loans. In past calls, management expects consumer loan balances to show YoY growth from 2H22. Deposits were up 2% YoY, driven by a 20% surge in CASA, which now accounts for 61% of total deposits.

NII was up 5% YoY; management estimates NIMs inched down 2bps YoY, likely driven by loan mix in favor of corporates.

Fee incomes continued to jump 22% YoY, now consistently at or above pre-Covid levels. Based on the press release, it looks as though trading/fx gains broke even (small gain or small loss).

Opex was up 8% YoY, likely due to catch-up investments in IT spend

Provision expense plunged 80% to just P80mn. To be fair, NPL ratio fell 29bps to 3.65% from 3.94% at end-2021. However, the low provision meant that NPL Cover dipped again to 90% from 93% in 4Q. In previous calls, management said they expect NPL Cover to get back above 100% by 2H22.

While loan and fee data are encouraging, we are slightly disappointed with the low provision and low NPL Cover. We await further details from the earnings call.

NPLs back down in March to 4.20%; Loan growth accelerates to 5.8% YoY

Asset quality improved across the board MoM in March 2022, reversing two months of deterioration in Jan/Feb, as mobility restrictions were loosened after the Omicron wave in Jan/Feb. NPL ratio improve to 4.20% (ex-interbank loans), down from 4.36% in Feb, though still higher than the 4.12% at end-21. Even in PHP terms, NPLs dipped to P460.5bn, down 2.6% MoM from the P472.7bn in Feb. Importantly, Past Due (PD) ratios improved too, down to 4.83% from 5.00% in Feb. As with NPLs, the nominal value also fell to P544.6bn, improving 2.4% MoM from P558.0bn in Feb. Restructured loans (RL) also improved to 3.03% of loans, from 3.09% in Feb. Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated to 5.8% YoY in March, compared to 5.3% in Feb. This is now the 12th consecutive month of accelerating YoY growth. (Note – we reiterate that this is lower than the 8.9% growth reported for commercial banks, due to the merger of BPI with its savings bank subsidiary Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the Thrift Bank before Jan 2022.). Banks continue to be the preferred play on the economic recovery, as the sector does not suffer from rising input costs / inflation. If anything, the recent 25bps BSP rate hike signals potential widening of NIMs, adding a boost to profitability. The downside risk clearly is if runaway inflation causes new stress in the economy.

BPI declares P1.06/sh 1H dividend; shifts to payout ratio

BPI’s board approved a shift in dividend policy from a fixed Peso (previously P1.80/sh, paid P0.90 per semester) to one based on a payout ratio equivalent to 35-50% of prior year’s profit. The ratio is dependent on BPI meeting regulatory limits on CET1/CAR, management’s internal assessment of liquidity, and on the bank’s growth targets, etc. As far as we can tell, this makes BPI the first bank to implement a payout based div policy. In line with this, the bank declared a cash div of P1.06/sh for the first semester. Assuming the same in 2H, the total P2.12/sh would be equivalent to about 40% payout of the EPS of P5.35/sh in 2021, and equivalent to a dividend yield of 2.2%.

Wage hikes approved in Metro Manila, Western Visayas

The wage boards of Metro Manila and Western Visayas have approved an increase in minimum wage for workers in various sectors in the regions. For Metro Manila, workers are granted a P33 (+6%) wage hike to P570. Wages were previously hiked in November 2018 (+5%). For Western Visayas (Region 6), the minimum wage has been increased by P55-110 (14-35%) to P420-450. Wages were previously hiked in Jan 2020 (+13%).

OFW remittances rise 3.2% in March

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.2% YoY to US$2.59bn in March. In PHP terms, remittances jumped 11% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate was at 2.3% in March. Land-based remittances jumped 3.7% YoY while sea-based remittances rose 1.3% YoY. This brings 1Q22 remittances to US$7.77bn, up 2.3% YoY. This is tracking behind the BSP’s FY22E target of +4% YoY.

BSP set to begin tightening cycle

The space for maintaining an accommodative policy stance has considerably narrowed, according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. A higher inflation rate in April, better-than-expected GDP growth rate in 1Q22, and ongoing downside risks “strengthen the case for a withdrawal of monetary policy accommodation”. The Monetary Board is set to meet Thursday (consensus expects 25bps hike).

Government approves wage hikes in 3 more regions

The wage boards of Region I, II, XIII issued orders to raise minimum wages in their areas: Region I will increase wages by 18-32%, Region II will increase wages by 14-16%, and Region XIII will increase wages by 15%.

 

Meanwhile, recently approved wage hikes in NCR and Western Visayas shall take effect on June 4 and 5, respectively. There were 10 wage hike petitions filed with six regional wage boards involving the NCR, Regions 3, 4A, 6, 7, and 8.

BSP hikes policy rates by 25bps

The Monetary Board raised the benchmark rate by 25bps to 2.25%, within street expectations. The BSP cited elevated inflation pressures could persist over the horizon. The BSP is projecting inflation to average 4.6% this year and settle down to 3.9% in 2023. Inflation averaged 3.7% in 4M22, with the latest print in April at 4.9%. The balance of risks to the inflation outlook now leans toward the upside for both 2022 and 2023. The BSP will have its next policy review on June 23.

Balance of payments reverses to a deficit in April

The country recorded a US$415mn balance of payments (BOP) deficit in April. This is reversing from the US$754mn surplus in March 2022 and US$2.6bn surplus in April 2021 and was due to the national government’s payments of foreign currency debt. Additionally, this brings 4M22 BOP surplus to US$79mn, a reversal from the US$231mn deficit in the same period last year.
