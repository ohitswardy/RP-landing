REGIS REPORT

19 August 2022

The Philippines climbed for a fifth consecutive week, with another +2.5% WoW ascent as foreign buying continued to provide an uplift. Foreigners were net buyers for US$57m, with biggest inflows seen across banks, property, and consumer names. Ayala Land, BDO, and Jollibee in particular were top bought. 

The BSP hiked its policy rate by 50bp during its meeting yesterday to 3.75%. The hike was in line with consensus estimates.  Nonetheless, the 10-year BVAL is already down >100bps from the June high, indicating lower inflation expectations.  The current 3.25% policy rate is still 75bps away from the 4.0% just before the pandemic hit.

The 2Q earnings season finally came to an end this week and the final tranche of results were consistent with earlier releases. Security Bank (+5.1% WoW) continued the trend of positive surprises from the banks, with its 1H22 NPAT doubling and coming in well ahead of estimates.  Max’s Group (+3.8% WoW) and Phils Seven (+9.8% WoW) reported sharp revenue jumps. Meanwhile,  property companies Vista Land (+7.1% WoW) and Filinvest Land (unch) came in behind expectations, with the residential segment raising concern. Holdcos Ayala Corp (+5.0% WoW), LT Group (+5.0% WoW), and Alliance Global (+10.7% WoW) all came in ahead.  More details and company results below.

Overall 2Q results for the market reflected the strength of economic reopening.  Topline was strong despite high inflation, and earnings beats were many despite margin pressure. Property lagged, as cancellations of previous sales weighed on revenues. Aggregate earnings of the companies we cover amounted to P181bn in 2Q22, already surpassing the peak levels in 2Q19 pre-Covid.

The positive 2Q showing helped market sentiment. Gains were broad based; 24 of the 30 index members closed up for the week. The top movers were large caps SM (+4.7% WoW), BDO (+9.3% WoW), Ayala Corp (+5.05% WoW), and Ayala Land (+3.7% WoW).  These four names contributed over 118 points to the PSEi’s 164-point gain for the week (72%).

Globe Telecom - 2Q: Growth stalls; tower sale to crystallize underappreciated value
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income within expectations despite soft telco revenue

Globe reported 1H22 core income of P11.0bn, +3% YoY, excluding one-off items but before distributions to perps. 2Q core profit was P5.9bn (+2% YoY), which is in line with our estimates. While the core telecom business seems a tad soft, the small non-telco operations are picking up. Apart from 1H results, the other development revealed today was Globe’s divestment of 7,000 of its towers, which should be significantly value-accretive.

Slowdown of core telecom business

Service revenue for 1H was up +6% YoY, lifted by Corporate Data (+21%) and non[1]telco revenues (+118%). Revenue for the core mobile business as well as broadband are looking soft. 1H mobile revenue was up only +3% over a particularly weak period last year. Broadband revenue, on the other hand, dropped as subscribers of its fixed wireless product were churned out.

Broadband race enters new phase

Globe began offering a prepaid “fiber Wi-Fi” product targeting lower income consumers unable to afford the current fiber plans. This new product allows consumers access something akin to a “neighborhood Wi-Fi”, which users can pay for and use in increments as short as one hour. The introduction of this service suggests that the market for the usual, more expensive postpaid broadband is nearing saturation, especially at the current price points. However, the jury is still out whether this is the best way to go deeper into the lower income segments.

Tower sale should be significantly value-enhancing

Globe announced it was divesting (and leasing back) ~7,000 of its ~12,000 towers, of which the first 5,709 will be sold for P71bn. Like the deal/s of PLDT, these tower sales should be significantly value-accretive for Globe. Beyond that, these divestments will also reduce the need for Globe to do an equity issue.

 
Manila Water - 2Q22 profit jumps 27% YoY; Back to the old agreement?
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Profits rebound in 2Q due to FX loss adjustments, economic rebound

MWC reported a 27% YoY jump in 2Q22 profit to P1.82bn, lifted by a P625mn adjustment for FX losses in prior periods (“FCDA”). The government failed to submit the Letter of Undertaking (LOU) for the revised concession agreement last June 30, prompting the company to revert to the terms of the old agreement. Outside of this, underlying operations outside the East zone are on a path to recovery, with Manila Water Philippine Ventures breaking even in 2Q.

East zone: 1H22 capex up 89% YoY, on track to beat Regis FY22E

Billed volumes were flat YoY in 2Q22 and 1H22, as commercial/industrial volumes dragged. EBITDA went up by 4% YoY, on lower cash costs. Despite larger non[1]cash and financing costs, profit jumped 13% YoY in 2Q due to the FCDA reversal. As of end-June, the company’s deferred FCDA amounted to P2.6bn. 1H22 capex surged 89% YoY to P5.3bn, accounting for 56% of our FY22E.

Rate rebasing updates

Management indicated that rate rebasing negotiations are progressing well and both sides are pushing hard to meet the year-end deadline. Given that the Department of Finance failed to submit the LOU for the new concession agreement within the required period, the company and the MWSS regulatory office are now reverting to the old concession agreement. Nonetheless, finality on this matter (original vs revised concession agreement) could take a long while to resolve. The MWSS Board of Trustees will want to have a say and President Marcos, Jr. has yet to appoint new members of the MWSS Board. Management also indicated that it will likely follow the returns embedded in its congressional franchise. It is worth noting, however, that the law granting the franchise did not specify explicitly a return for the water concessionaires.

 
Alliance Global - 2Q22: GGR recovery accelerates, GADC helped by price increases
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1H22 profit -3% to P8.2bn – mildly ahead

AGI reported a 2Q22 profit of P4.3bn (+11%QoQ), which rose on the back of a bigger-than-expected jump in GGR from Travellers and stable margins from Golden Arches. Earnings from liquor and property were broadly in line with expectations. Emperador’s weak brandy margins were offset by a 20% growth in whisky and other income booked in the period. Megaworld, meanwhile, saw residential and office revenue grow 17%YoY and 13%YoY, respectively. AGI’s 1H22 profit of P8.2bn (-3%YoY) is tracking slightly ahead of estimates for the full year.

Casino back in the black; GGR outperforms on substantial jump in Mass GGR

Travellers GGR increased 25%QoQ to P8.2bn, driven by a 75% jump in mass (P3.1bn). VIP GGR also grew 6 % to P5.0bn, helped by the increase in contributions from new non-China junkets that opened recently. Without citing details, management said that win rates were close to theoretical levels, implying that the growth in revenues was largely volume-driven. Travellers turned a profit of P100mn, its first time to be profitable without one-off gains since the pandemic started.

 

GADC overcomes cost pressures; sales grew despite 4 price increases

Golden Arches continued to grow profits (P300mn, 91%QoQ) despite the cost pressures felt across the industry. Sales by company-owned restaurants grew 14%QoQ, helped by continued loosening of restrictions, while SSSG in 2Q was 28.5% (from 22.2% in 1Q22). Alcohol, property segments in line

Emperador’s profits in 2Q were buoyed by P1.0bn in other income booked in the quarter relating to sale of non-core assets. This, coupled with the 20%YoY growth in whisky profits, offset the collapse in brandy GPM (25.9% in 2Q22 from 45.0% in 2Q21). Meanwhile, Megaworld’s profit was supported by the growth in residential revenue (17%YoY) and office revenue (13%YoY). Office vacancy remained at 9%, lower than the industry average of 18%.

 
LT Group - Provision reversal, sale of ROPA boost PNB; tobacco still soft
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q22 profit jumps on bank profits

LTG reported that its 1H22 reached P15.4bn (+313%YoY), ahead of estimates, on the back of a sharp rise in contributions from PNB. We, however, believe the quality of the beat is low, given that profits of the bank were largely driven by a reversal of loan loss provisions totalling P3.7bn and a large gain on the sale of ROPA. This offset the weak performance from tobacco which saw profits fall 16%QoQ. In 2Q alone, LTG’s income jumped 36%QoQ to P8.875bn, largely on the jump in PNB earnings.

Tobacco profits -16%QoQ despite price hikes announced in March

LTG’s share of profits from tobacco fell 16%QoQo to P3.5bn. Cigarette sales fell to 7.1bn sticks (-27%QoQ, -18%YoY) following a price hike implemented in March. Unit market share was nevertheless stable at 61.6%, implying that the industry fell at a similar pace. We had anticipated margins to improve following the price hike announcement, but the profit trend suggests that the effective hikes were likely to have been implemented later in the quarter. We nevertheless think that profits will improve in succeeding periods.

PNB continues reversals of loan loss provisions

PNB’s contribution jumped to P4.675 (191%QoQ), which was again driven by a substantial P3.7bn reversal of loan loss provisions in the quarter. The bank incurred more trading losses in the quarter (P107mn in 2Q22 vs P58mn in 1Q22) and its loan portfolio was still down by 7%YoY at the half. NPL ratio (6.5% in 2Q22 vs 10.8% in 1Q22) and NPL coverage (79.8% in 2Q22 vs 61% in 1Q22) showed marked improvements, as some loans were reclassified back to performing status. Management claims these loans have completed a curing period where accounts have been paying over the past 6 months. There was also a P5.2bn gain on the sale of ROPA relating to a Manila North Harbour property transaction in April.

Tanduay volumes up 22%QoQ; non-alcohol volumes also higher on reopening

Income from Tanduay fell 31%QoQ to P231mn. Despite volumes growing to 6.3mn cases (22%QoQ, 17%YoY), we estimate GPM fell 3.7ppt to just 9.3% in 2Q on higher raw material costs. Meanwhile, ABI’s profits improved sequentially by 68%QoQ to P214mn on higher sales. GPM was, however, still 5-6ppts lower than last year’s levels.

 
First Gen - 2Q22 improves on higher fuel availability
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q22 up 19%QoQ

FGEN saw a marked improvement in 2Q earnings ($67.1mn, 19%QoQ) on the back of higher fuel availability for its natural gas plants. We estimate that this brings 1H22 core income (after preferred dividends) to $124mn, down 14%, and in line with Regis estimates for the full year. FGEN has been getting its full allocation of gas from Malampaya since June, which should be positive for profits in the near term.

Nat gas recovering with lifting of fuel supply constraints

Earnings from natural gas were lower by 14% to $93mn in 1H. This was dragged primarily by the derated capacity of San Gabriel and an outage at Avion in 1Q22. The result implies 2Q earnings from natural gas already improved 16%QoQ to $50mn, despite a short outage of San Gabriel in May. Gas restrictions have been resolved since June, following the expiration of Ilijan’s contract with Malampaya. The full allocation of gas should mean an improvement in earnings in the near term due to higher capacity fees and without the negative impact of buying expensive replacement power. However, there is a possibility that the sale of banked gas to San Miguel Group could again cut supplies to FGEN in the future.

EDC still weighed down by plant maintenance, lower wind contribution

FGEN’s attributable income from EDC was also lower by -20% at $37mn in 1H22, implying a slight improvement in the quarterly contribution ($20mn, +4%QoQ). The mild recovery was due to the depressed 1Q base (Odette constraints) but this was tempered by the continued need to buy replacement power because of maintenance activities. Contribution from wind was also markedly lower (- 70%YoY) because of lower output.

 
Ayala Corp. - 1H: Divestments boost earnings
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Visible improvement in operating environment

Ayala Corp (AC PM) reported net income of P16.3bn for 1H22, up +56% YoY. Earnings for 2Q alone were P8.5bn, +68% YoY, which is also a further improvement from 1Q22 (+8%). There’s a recovery evident across operating units in 2Q (vs 1Q22) for banking, property and even power. While AC’s 1H profits appear to be trending above Street estimates (P30bn for FY22E), the outperformance is mainly due to divestments at two of its key operating companies. BPI booked a large gain from the sale of property where one of its branches used to be located (2Q22), while Globe sold half of its data center business (in 1Q22).

BPI leading 2Q improvement; Ayala Land mixed

Among AC’s main operating units, the recovery was most unambiguous with BPI (even excluding the P5bn pretax gain on a property sale), with loan growth rising, interest margins widening and asset quality improving. Ayala Land’s (ALI) results were not as clear cut despite the improvement in the leasing business (especially malls). ALI’s residential business is seeing a rise in back-outs (cancellations), raising concern on the health of residential property market which accounts for >50% of ALI’s earnings.

Improvements in ACEN; Divestments to lift Globe FY22 profit

ACEN saw a marked profit improvement in 2Q due to improved plant availability, but not quite enough to offset the disappointing 2Q. The firm unveiled an ambitious plant to have 20,000MW of renewable capacity by 2030, 6x the current level. Globe’s results, on the other hand, were also mixed, with service revenue for its core telco business appearing a tad soft. The divestment of half of its data center business boosted 1Q/1H profit, and the just-announced sale of a large part of telecom tower portfolio should lift 2H.

Digital initiatives of Globe show growth

Not a lot of detail on the unlisted businesses was provided. The company, however, did say that AC Health is now above breakeven (in addition to being EBITDA positive since last year) and is expected to report its first full year profit this year. AC Motors, which had been posting losses consistently over recent years, was also in the black in 1H. It claims to be now outperforming the rest of the industry in terms of sales growth after taking over the Kia distributorship. The logistics arm, meanwhile, is still waiting to complete its purchase of Air21, possibly by next month.

Raising stake in ALI back up above 50%

Ayala Corp also disclosed that it acquired another 468.7m shares (3.2%) of Ayala Land last week, at a price of P25.2/share (P11.82bn or ~US$210m). Together with the impact of the impending property-for-share swap with ALI, we estimate AC’s stake in ALI will rise from 46.3% to 50.5%.

Vista Land - 2Q22 residential revenue falls; weakness to continue in 2H22
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Margin expansion unlikely to be sustained

Despite an 18% YoY drop in residential revenue (3% decline in total revenue), 2Q22 EBIT rose 27% to P2.6bn. Residential GPM for 2Q expanded 530 basis points YoY to 60.6% (the highest level in over 10 years), and opex for the quarter saw a 5% drop (1H22 opex is down 9%). While VLL has initiated cost-saving initiatives, we believe both of these trends are unlikely to persist in the coming quarters.

2Q22 residential revenue falls; weakness to continue in 2H22

2Q22 residential revenue fell 18% YoY and QoQ. While management attributed the drop mainly to extended payment terms (which meant revenue takes longer to recognize), it also acknowledged that cancellations were partly to blame. The need to extend payment terms as well as the rise in cancellations both suggest that demand is weaker. Moreover, management said that residential revenue would likely fall c. 15% YoY in 2H22, implying a 40% drop HoH. This would result in FY22 residential revenue of only P14.2bn, 35% lower than our forecast.

High-end projects sell more briskly

2Q22 reservation sales increased 12% YoY, bringing 1H21 sales up 8% YoY to P31.8bn. Similar to the experience of other developers, Vista’s high-end brand, Brittany, saw the fastest growth.

Mall foot traffic at 80% of pre-COVID levels

1H22 rental revenue grew 36% to P4.9bn, about 130% of the 2019 average. However, rental receivables of P16.8bn are equivalent to 575 days of rental revenue, arguably implying that revenue is overstated relative to actual cash rent. Vista’s same-mall cash rent is likely closer to 80% of pre-COVID levels, similar to its foot traffic levels (which are comparable to the levels of other developers).

SECB NPAT doubles YoY, on provision plunge; PPOP slightly ahead

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT of P6.25bn, double YoY, and well ahead of Regis (8.24bn) and consensus (P9.08bn) for the full year. Regis notes though that the beat was driven largely by a collapse in provision expense; while NPLs improved, NPL cover was still sub-100%. Otherwise, the results were broadly in line, and in keeping with Regis’ thesis of a) accelerating loan growth (15% YoY), improving NIMs (+9bps QoQ), and improving asset quality. Notably, consumer loans turned positive (+6% YoY).

 

Loan growth accelerated to 15% YoY from 8% in 1Q22. Corporate/mid-market loans led the way again, jumping 18% (was +11% in 1Q). Notably, though, consumer loans grew 6% YoY, turning from a 4% contraction in 1Q22. Consumer loans account for 23% of total loans, still among the highest in the sector. Deposits grew 9% YoY.

 

NIMs expanded 9bps QoQ and 17bps YoY in 2Q22. Loan yields widened 6bps QoQ, matched by a 6bps increase in deposit costs. Fee incomes grew 22% YoY in both 2Q and 1H, staying well above pre-Covid levels. Trading/forex gains were down 51% YoY in 1H as expected, given the high base in 2021 and the rising interest rates in 2Q22. PPOP was up 6% YoY in 1H22, trending slightly ahead of Regis’ expectations. Opex was up just 4% in 1H, but was up 8% in 2Q alone.

 

Provision expense collapsed -83% YoY to just P408mn. NPL ratio dropped 37bps QoQ to 3.28% from 3.65% in 1Q. However, with the drop in provisions, NPL cover just edged up to 92% from 90% in 1Q. Of the stocks Regis covers, only SECB is still under 100% NPL cover.

 

SECB’s core operations seem solid, trending slightly ahead of Regis’ forecasts. Regis is disappointed that the bank did not take the opportunity to significantly increase NPL cover this quarter.

Domestic sugar prices jump to 5-year high

Domestic sugar prices continue to climb, breaching P3,000/50kg bag since the end of July. This is 75% higher vs year-ago and end-2021 levels. In USD terms, it is nearly 3x global sugar prices.The recent surge in prices has been attributed to poor supply resulting from Typhoon Odette last year, coupled with the recovery in demand. Consumer companies that are exposed to beverages, bakery, and confectionery will be the most affected by an increase in sugar prices. MONDE’s biscuits account for ~30% of APAC BFB’s revenues. Sugar is a major raw material for its APAC BFB business, accounting to ~10% of the segment’s raw material cost.  URC’s beverages (coffee, tea), confectionery, and baked goods account for c70% of the domestic BCF segment. Sugar accounts for 8-10% of the segment’s total raw material cost. Note, however, that URC is a net seller of sugar through its Sugar and Renewables (SURE) segment.

10-year government bond yield drops

The Philippine treasury sold P35bn worth of 10-year bond at a yield 5.813%. There were P128.8bn worth of bids. The yield at the previous auction (on July 19) was 6.865%. Bloomberg 10-year BVAL - 5.8423%.

Market foreign ownership falls in July

Regis estimate that market foreign ownership fell 36bps MoM to 20.3% (an all-time low) while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 6bps MoM to 24.8% (in July). The market net foreign selling amounted to US$80mn in July (from US$207mn in June). Although the PSEi foreign ownership declined, only 14 of the 30 PSEi constituents saw reductions in their foreign ownership levels. Fifteen PSEi constituents experienced increases in foreign ownership while 1 was unchanged. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

SECB – foreign ownership increased 55 basis points MoM to 38.1%. The stock saw net foreign buying of US$3mn, the fifth highest in July. 

PGOLD – foreign ownership fell 37 basis points MoM to 18.9% as net foreign selling reached US$4.2mn

WLCON – foreign ownership dropped 30 basis points MoM to 17.1%.

Remittances rise 4% YoY, 14% MoM in June

Cash remittances coursed through banks reached US$2.76bn in June, up 4.4% YoY and 13.6% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances rose 18% YoY in June. The 3-month average growth rate rises slightly to +3.4% in June from +3.0% in May. Land-based remittances jumped 5% YoY, led by USA, Asia, and Middle East, offsetting decline in Europe. Sea-based remittances jumped 2.6% YoY but were still below pre-Covid levels. The YT-June remittances are higher by 2.9% YoY to US$15.3bn, trending behind the government’s target (+4%).

Vehicle sales jump 29% YoY in July

Vehicle sales jumped by 29% YoY in July to 27,813 units, though sales dipped by 3% MoM according to data from CAMPI/TMA. This brings YT-July sales to 182,687 units (+18% YoY), trending behind the industry group’s target of +25%. Toyota sold 94,026 units in 7M22, up 26% YoY. This implies sales in July alone rose 29% YoY to 13,936 units (-3% MoM). Toyota’s market share dipped to 51.5% from 51.7% in June but remained high relative to end-2019 (47%). The YT-July sales are tracking ahead of Regis FY22E (158k, +22%); Toyota needs to sell 12.8k units per month for the rest of the year to reach target. YT-July sales are tracking within Toyota’s year-end target of 162k units (+25% YoY); Toyota needs to sell 13.6k units per month for the rest of the year to reach its target. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 24%/20% of the latter’s profit/gross asset value. GTCAP is trading at a 44% discount to its market-based NAV of P915/sh.

 

BSP hikes key policy rates by 50bps

The BSP delivered another 50bps rate hike in yesterday’s Monetary Board meeting. Rates on the overnight reverse repurchase facility is now at 3.75%. The BSP has so far raised policy rates by 175bps so far this year. Pre-covid, the policy rate stood at 4%. The Fed has raised its policy rates by 225bps so far this year. The BSP also raised its 2022 inflation forecast to 5.4% (from 5% previously). It lowered inflation projections for 2023/2024 to 4%/3.2% from 4.2%/3.3%. Inflation averaged 4.7% in 7M22, staying above the BSP’s 2-4% target range, after quickening to 6.4% in July from 6.1% in June.
