REGIS REPORT

26 August 2022

After five straight weeks of gains, the PSEi succumbed to profit taking amidst weakness in overnight and regional markets.  The Phils fell 1.6% this week, with losses across the index. 

Foreigners reverted to selling the market, with US$16.5m in net outflows this week. Biggest outflows were seen in property names SM Prime and Ayala Land. Meanwhile, some inflows were seen in banks BDO and Security Bank.

Share price drops were widespread.  Losses were led by ACEN (-7.4% WoW), following its overnight secondary share placement done this week.

Also dropping significantly were holdcos  GTCAP (-6.6% WoW), and Ayala Corp (-6.5% WoW).

Telcos Globe (-4.8%% WoW) and Converge (-4.8% WoW) also fell.  Globe announced it has rolled out its prepaid fiber WiFI service, called “TMBayan”, to around 200 sites with partner establishments, fell this week. The service allows users to buy access to the WiFi for a specified period of time through a retail hub. Denominations go as low as P5 for one hour and as high as P500 for 30 days.

PLDT (-2.2% WoW) also ended lower. PLDT’s TV5 and ABS-CBN (ABS -4.2% WoW) have deferred the closing of the deal in which ABS-CBN will take a 35% stake in TV5 initially. 

Only three of the 30 index stocks managed to close with gains. 

Jollibee (+3.9% WoW) was the top gainer.  Sentiment for the consumer sector was lifted by expectations of a boost in demand owing to the resumption of face-to-face classes across the country (after a long pandemic-induced pause). URC (-0.6% WoW) and Puregold (-0.7% WoW) outperformed the market.  

Banks rounded up the other index gainers – with large caps BDO (+1.7% WoW) and BPI (+1.2% WoW) closing up for the week. Metrobank (-0.1% WoW) was not far behind, closing flat. Security Bank (+1.5% WoW) likewise gained.

Philippine Property - 2Q22 recap: malls rebound; residential cancellations bite
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Malls outperformed and should continue to lead

While majority of our coverage firms beat profit expectations in 2Q22, the property sector was a clear laggard. Mall revenue showed a substantial rebound, in line with the strong performance from the consumer companies. However, things were less rosy for the other segments as cancellations plagued the residential business while office rental rates continued to decline. We expect malls to continue recovering in 2H22 even as the residential and office segments face headwinds.

Mall revenue jumps; operations to improve even further

Thanks to higher foot traffic coupled with lower rental concessions, mall revenue of the top 3 operators jumped 44% QoQ (+138% YoY) to about 80% of pre-COVID levels. Revenue should improve even further since mall operators reduced concessions further in July, with some developers already removing them completely.

Residential cancellations continued in 2Q22, will remain elevated in 2H22

It is apparent that some residential buyers are still experiencing financial difficulties. Despite the economic reopening, aggregate residential revenue (of the developers we cover) fell 4% QoQ and 10% YoY due to revenue reversals brought about by sales cancellations. Moreover, most developers said that cancellations would remain elevated in 2H22.

Aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover rose 2% QoQ (+33% YoY). For most developers, 2Q22 sales were 60%-80% of 2019 levels. Similar to previous quarters, high-end lots in provincial areas sold briskly. There was also a pick-up in demand for high-end condominiums in Metro Manila, particularly for JV projects with foreign developers.

Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) were generally flat QoQ. This means that prices of most units are still 10%-25% below 2019 levels.

Office vacancy improves slightly but rents continue to fall

Leechiu Property Consultants said that Metro Manila office vacancy dropped slightly to 18% in 2Q22 (from 19% in 1Q22), as office take-up remained net positive for the second consecutive quarter. However, Colliers estimates that rental rates still fell another c. 3% QoQ in most areas, implying that they are now 20%-40% lower than 2019 levels.

 
Philippine Strategy - Glass (more than) half full? Reopening drives 2Q beats, despite inflation
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

A blockbuster quarter – 60% of stocks beat expectations

Aggregate profits for 2Q22 surged 53% YoY for Regis-covered stocks. More notably, total NPAT already surpassed 2Q19 levels. Roughly 60% of companies exceeded Regis and Street expectations. The beats were led by the power, consumer, and banking sectors, as the economic reopening drove sales and/or profit to (or past) pre-Covid levels, despite inflation and margin squeezes. While we are wary of fading “revenge spending”, early indicators point to continued momentum in 3Q, with schools finally reopening. Easing global commodity prices could give consumer demand a boost, and bring down companies’ input costs. We’ve begun our forecast upgrades, and expect more to come.

2Q22 NPAT + 49% YoY, surpassing 2019 peak; reopening trumps inflation

Aggregate NPAT for stocks we cover surged 53% YoY, taking 1H22 profit to +42%. Notably, total profits already surpassed their 2Q19 peaks. Fully 56% (24 of 43) of companies beat our expectations, while only 26% disappointed. Revenues were strong nearly across the board, as companies were surprised by the resilience of demand, despite high inflation. The results underscore the strength of the economic reopening story, and are more consistent with our accelerating bottom-up indicators than the slowing 2Q22 GDP print.

Power, consumers, banks lead; property dragged by residential cancelations

Improving mobility, loan growth, electricity sales, all led to strong revenue numbers for consumer/retail, banks, and power companies. Aggregate profits for these sectors were already at or above their 2019 quarterly highs. Consumers were most surprising, as the beats came despite margin squeeze from commodity input prices. Property companies’ mall revenues were strong, as Covid-era rental concessions were withdrawn. However, high condo vacancy rates led to cancellations of residential pre-sales, dragging profits.

Glass more than half full? Reopening continues + moderating inflation

We remain wary of a potential fading of the “revenge spending” and low base effects boosting 1H22 numbers. There are also the top-down concerns over the country’s twin fiscal and current account deficits. However, early indications are that demand remains robust at least into 3Q22. Reopening continues with face[1]to-face schooling for the first time in 2 years. Consumer sentiment could stay buoyant as global oil prices have dropped considerably. Falling commodity prices could also see corporate margin pressures ease.

Earnings upgrades coming?

Aggregate 1H22 earnings accounted for c53% of our FY22E estimates (pre[1]results). Given seasonally strong demand in 2H, especially 4Q, we expect a round of upward revisions to both our and the Street’s forecasts.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Semirara willing to construct additional 700MW when grid issues are resolved

Semirara Mining and Power said that it would push through with the construction of 2X350 MW units in Calaca, Batangas once grid issues are resolved. Management said that the “present grid is maxed out” and new lines have not been added as NGCP has been experiencing right-of-way issues.

San Miguel power unit buys PNOC banked gas for $1.2bn

South Premiere Power Corp (SPPC), a unit of SMC Global Power Holdings, purchased the remaining banked gas of the Philippine National Oil Co (PNOC) for $1.2bn, as confirmed by SMC President Ramon Ang in an interview. Based on a filing, SPPC entered into a gas supply agreement for 70.26 petajoules of banked gas with PNOC at a daily volume dispatch sufficient to run the Ilijan Power Plant at 45-75% plant factor. Adequate to supply Ilijan’s fuel requirements until Feb 2024. PNOC confirmed that it sold the remaining uncontracted banked gas to SPPC under a gas sale and purchase agreement on June 23. The delivery has not commenced as the SC38 Consortium have yet to comply with the series of directives by the DOE. The agency is requiring them to immediately resume supply and maintain allocation of natural gas minimum daily contract quantity to Ilijan pursuant to the GSPA. The resumption of gas supply to the 1,200MW Ilijan would be good for overall industry supply but would again lead to First Gen’s plants running at derated capacities in the near term.

Filinvest Land expects office vacancy to jump to 45% by end-2022

Filinvest Land’s office vacancy rose from 30% in 1Q21 to 32% in 2Q22. The company’s office portfolio is 520,000sqm. The vacancy of FILRT’s 300,000sqm portfolio was flat at 12%. The company will complete 200,000sqm of office space in 2H22 and with the additional space, Filinvest Land expects its office vacancy to rise to 45%.

Consortium to sign contract for Sangley airport in Sept

The SPIA Development Consortium is set to be awarded the contract to develop the Sangley Point International Airport (SPIA) on September 14 according to Cavite Governor Juanito Victor “Jonvic” C. Remulla, Jr. The SPIA consortium is composed of Philippine, European and South Korean companies:

Virata-led Cavitex Holdings; Yuchengo-led House of Investments, Inc.; MacroAsia Corp.

Samsung C&T Corp., a South Korean construction and engineering company

Munich Airport International, a wholly owned subsidiary of Flughafen München GmbH

Arup, a UK-based design company

 

Cavite government did not receive any competing bids during the Swiss challenge. To recall, MacroAsia and its partner China Communications Construction Co. Ltd. had negotiated with Cavite for the project in 2020 but the province canceled the project in Jan 2021 due to lack of requirements. The project is estimated to cost US$11bn and will expand the airport’s annual capacity to 25mn passengers initially, with plans to create a second runway that could expand capacity further to 75mn

SMC warns of power price surge starting October after issuing notices of PSE termination to Meralco

SMC Global Power Holdings warned on Tuesday that electricity prices in Metro Manila and nearby provinces could go up as much as 30% in October if the energy regulator fails to act on its petition for a temporary rate hike. The petition was jointly filed with the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) by San Miguel Corp and Meralco covering the companies’ two power supply agreements. If the ERC approved the petition for temporary relief (filed in May), SMC Global Power said electricity prices in Luzon would rise by 30 centavos per kWh over a period of six months. Without it and with a termination of the PSA, SMC Global Power said energy prices would rise by P0.80-1.30/kWh for the next three to four months  as “it will need to find alternative sources of energy.. including the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market”. San Miguel units SPPC and SMEC, administrators of the Ilijan and Sual plants, had already issued notices of termination of their PSAs to Meralco. They blamed the “change in circumstance” including global surge of fuel prices. They claim the termination is effective starting October 4 if no relief is given. SMC President and CEO Ramon Ang is urging the ERC for a “fair and objective” assessment of the joint petition.

ABS-CBN investment in TV5 on “pause”

ABS-CBN disclosed that it has deferred the closing of its purchase of a 35% interest in TV5 has been put on “pause”. This is in order to address issues raised by a number of politicians opposed to the deal. Among those issues are the supposed legality / constitutionality of the purchase. ABS-CBN is supposed to buy 35% of TV5 for P2.16bn and subscribe to a convertible worth P1.84bn that would allow it to raise its stake to 49.92%. The related transaction for PLDT group firm Cignal Cable to buy Sky Cable will proceed. Sky Cable has 300k pay TV subscribers and 350k broadband subscribers. The parties apparently wish to avoid PCC review of the Cignal-Sky day, thus an urgency to close deal before lapse of the PCC review suspension. The ABS-CBN investment in TV5 would not require PCC review.

Globe rolls out TMbayan Fiber Wifi to 200 sites

That is still a modest number but I can imagine it becoming tens of thousands in a few months (depending on consumer acceptance). This prepaid fiber Wifi product is in many ways a substitute for Globe’s fixed wireless broadband (FW). It’s installed with business partners (sari-sari stores, etc) in various neighborhoods as an alternative to hooking up to mobile data.  It’s prepaid and allows Globe to tap users who can’t afford more. Users also don’t need to purchase any equipment (modem, etc). One benefit for Globe is that this service will not strain its mobile network (unlike FW which runs on it), as it is WiFi connected to fiber.  If users gradually switch to TMbayan from its FW service, more capacity for mobile data will be freed up.

Hot money exit continues in July

Data released by the BSP showed that total foreign investments registered through authorized agent banks logged a net outflow amounting to US$103.14mn in July. However, this is lower than the US$342mn outflow in June 2022 and US$340mn outflow in July 2021. Gross inflows amounted to US$681mn in July, lower than the US$1.0bn recorded in June 2022. Majority of investments (or 64.8%) registered were in PSE-listed securities. The gross outflows amounted to US$784mn in July, lower than the US$1.4bn outflow in June 2022. The YT-July hot money remains at a net inflow of US$625mn, a reversal from the US$446mn net outflow in the same period last year.

Customs exceeds collection target in latest data for August

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P60.52bn from August 1 to Aug 23. This is already 2.8% above its original target of P58.85bn for the month. This is the eighth consecutive month that the BOC exceeded its monthly targets. This brings cumulative collections at P541.66bn for the January to Aug.23 period accounting for 74% of its full-year target. Customs has been attributing its success in exceeding its target goals to the improved valuation of the bureau, as well as its digitized and modernized systems.

Malampaya deal to be resolved this year

The Department of Energy has given its assurance that a decision on the Malampaya stake sale will be made within the year. Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla said that PNOC Exploration Corp is considering giving its consent to the sale of Shell Philippines Exploration BV’s stake in Malampaya to MEXP Pte. Ltd, a company previously owned by Udenna Corp that was sold to Enrique’s Prime Infrastructure Capital Inc. PNOC EC, with a 10% stake in the project, will have to give consent on the deal. MEXP previously inked an agreement with Shell Petroleum NV for the 45% stake of SPEX in the Malampaya project. Another Udenna subsidiary, UC38, acquired the shares of Chevron Malampaya which holds the other 45% in the project.

Lower House committee approves removal of tax exemption of pick-up trucks

The Ways and Means Committee of the House of Representatives approved the Dept. of Finance’s proposal to remove the excise tax exemption on pick-up trucks. The removal of tax exemption was included in the bill formerly called “Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act (Pifita)”. Previously, Pifita was pitched as a revenue-neutral measure however, with the removal of tax exemption, the bill is now expected to generate incremental revenues of P18bn in 2023, P7.9bn in 2024 before collections taper off from 2025 onwards. Pick-up trucks (i.e. Hilux) accounted for 15% of Toyota’s sales volumes in 2021. Regis forecasts assume Toyota’s sales volumes will reach 158k units in 2022 (+22% YoY) and 166k units in 2023 (+5% YoY). Toyota accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit and gross asset value. GTCAP trades at a 46% discount to its market-based NAV of P914/sh.

Dept. of Finance proposes new fiscal regime for mining

Department of Finance (DOF) bared its proposal to reform the mining fiscal regime to achieve simplification, fair share, value-adding and good governance. DOF wants to impose a royalty rate of 5% for all large-scale mining operations and provide incremental revenues of at least P5bn yearly. This would effectively change the current set-up where only those located inside a mineral reservation are subject to royalty payment. A royalty payment for non-metallic minerals extracted within or outside mineral reservations is also being proposed. The DOF is also pushing for a rationalized and single fiscal regime applicable to all large-scale metallic mines, regardless of location. In terms of value-adding, the DOF is proposing a 10% export tax on the gross value of mineral ore to encourage downstream and proper valuation of minerals. Currently, there is no duty on unprocessed export of minerals. The current mining tax policies in the country include a 25% corporate tax, 4% excise tax of gross value, and 5% mineral royalty inside mineral reservation areas, as well as 50:50 mode of sharing between government and mining proponents under an FTAA. The proposal is estimated to result in total revenue of P37.52bn per year for the government.

PEZA allows 30% WFH for BPOs until March 2023

PEZA allowed 30% of BPO employees to work from home until March 2023. Previously, the 30% cap would lapse on September 12, after which only 10% of employees would be allowed to work from home. Regis expects only minor impact from this extension. For the most part, we believe BPO firms have decided if they will expand office footprint or if they will no longer avail of PEZA incentives. Most BPO firms are expanding via Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have a requirement regarding WFH policies

Businessman Al Tengco appointed PAGCOR head

Al Tengco will replace Andrea Domingo as chairman and CEO of the gaming regulator. He was a former vice mayor of Malolos, Bulacan and was special assistant of Tonyboy Cojuangco. He also owns Nationstar Development Corp, a building contractor.

 

Budget department submits P5.268tn budget for 2023 to Congress

The Department of Budget and Management submitted the proposed P5.268tn budget for 2023 to Congress. The proposed 2023 budget is a 4.9% increase from the 2022 budget. The 2023 budget is estimated to account for 22% of GDP. Education, infrastructure, food security, healthcare and clean energy got top priority in the 2023 budget. On infrastructure, the government expects to spend P1.180tn on infrastructure in 2023 (5.0% of GDP), 2% lower than its 2022 target of P1.1995tn (5.5% of GDP). Congress is expected to approve the budget by October and Marcos is expected to sign it into law before year’s end. The government is targeting a lower fiscal deficit of P1.45tn (6.1% of GDP) in 2023. The 2021A deficit stood at P1.67tn (8.6% of GDP) and the 2022E deficit projected at P1.65tn (7.6% of GDP). The budget deficit is expected to return to the pre-pandemic levels of 3.2% of GDP beginning 2027, before closing at 3% of GDP by 2028.

PH to open SY22-23 with face-to-face classes

After two years without face-to-face classes, the Department of Energy formally opens the School Year 2022-2023 on Monday, August 22. Public schools nationwide will be opening their doors to all students for face-to-face classes. As of August 20, DepEd said there are 27,691,191 enrolled students for this year school year.
