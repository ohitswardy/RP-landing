REGIS REPORT

04 March 2022

The PSEi managed a gain of 1.8% this week, amidst the Russia-Ukraine conflict and soaring old prices.  Providing some optimism on the ground were expectations of increased mobility with the pandemic Alert classification for Metro Manila and 38 other areas dropping to Level One effective March 1.  Alert Level One is the lowest restriction level and allows nearly all businesses to operate at full capacity.

Bulk of the gains were driven by two stocks – SM and ICT.

SM (+7.0% WoW) staged a recovery after last week’s selldown that came after the announcement that it would be removed from the FTSE GEIS Asia Pacific Index (after failing one of the liquidity tests). 

ICTSI (+7.7% WoW) likewise moved up, after reporting a strong set of results that beat Regis and consensus forecasts and signaling forthcoming upgrades.  Early 2022 indications point to robust growth rates and management reiterated its continued positive yield and EBITDA margin guidance.

Reopening optimism boosted by improving mobility data benefitted SMPH () which closed higher this week. Other property names closed higher, including Robinsons Land (+4.7% WoW) and Ayala Land (+2.7% WoW). Ayala Land reflected recovery in operations, with a surge in 4Q mall and commercial lot revenue. 

PLDT (+2.8% WoW) also ended higher, with FY21 EBITDA coming in in line with expectations.  PLDT revealed that it was selling half of its telco tower network at valuation “north of US$1bn” in a deal that appears value-accretive.

Other telcos however did not perform as well.  Converge (-3.3% WoW) continued to correct, while Globe (-4.7% WoW) also declined.  Globe is increasing its authorized capital (common shares by 20m from 148.9m to 168.9m), signaling a likely equity issuance given where debt levels are and its near-record capex guidance (P89bn) for FY22. 

Globe, along with Ayala Corp (-0.1% WoW)  also announced that they have signed definitive agreements with regard to a joint venture with ST Telemedia for AC and STT to take minority stakes in Globe’s data center operation. The data center operation will be held under a firm called KarmanEdge, which will be owned 50% by Globe, 40% by STT and 10% by AC. A post-money valuation of US$350m for KarmanEdge was cited, implying a valuation of US$275m for the data center business of Globe (US$100m in proceeds + 50% of post-money valuation). This is equivalent to around +3% of Globe’s market cap. 

Banks were mixed, with Metrobank (-2.3% WoW) and BDO (-0.1% WoW) soft despite both slightly beating FY21 forecasts. Both saw profits boosted by a drop in provisions though BDO’s loans accelerated to 6.5% while MBT’s loans were still down 1.3% YoY.

Soaring coal prices led to a huge rise for Semirara (+27.6% WoW) this week. The company also reported a 4Q21 profit surge (to P5.8bn) on higher coal ASPs, lower coal mining costs, and  better plant availability factors. 

Consumer names were soft, with Emperador (-8.0% WoW) leading the decliners.  Puregold (-1.4% WoW), URC (-1.3% WoW), and Monde (-0.3% WoW) also fell.

RRHI (-1.0% WoW) also decline despite managing an earnings beat in 4Q21 driven by non-essential formats.  Wilcon’s (-0.7% WoW) results meanwhile were in line with expectations as SSSG and margins stabilized in 4Q21.

Foreigners were slight buyers for US$2.9m this week as investors digested a slew of earnings and the situation in Ukraine. Biggest inflows were seen in SMPH and ICT while outflows were in banks BDO and BPI.

Ayala Land - Mall revenue doubles in 4Q21
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Malls and commercial lots provide a boost in 4Q21

4Q21 net profit jumped 54% YoY (+43% QoQ) to P3.6bn, thanks to the surge in mall revenue and commercial lots (which have high margins). As a result, FY21 net profit climbed 40% to P12.2bn, ahead of REGIS forecast (in line with consensus). Earnings are still far below pre-COVID levels, but we expect net profit to jump c. 60% in 2022, led by the mall and residential segments.

Mall revenue doubles in 4Q21 and we expect further recovery this year

4Q21 mall revenue doubled (QoQ and YoY) as tenant sales reached 78% of the pre-COVID level, from 44% in 3Q21 (50% in FY20). December foot traffic also climbed to 85% of the pre-COVID level, from 34% in FY20. Ayala Land’s mall recovery has lagged SM Prime’s, likely because 70% of ALI’s space is in Metro Manila, which has been harder hit by the pandemic. However, this could also lead to a sharper rebound with the reopening of the economy.

High-end lots continue to drive residential sales

4Q21 reservation sales grew 5% (+1% QoQ) to P22.1bn, thanks to high-end lots. This brought FY21 presales up 13% to P92.2bn. Horizontal projects have sold well and have risen to 47% of FY21 sales, from 40% in 2020 (26% in 2019). The company plans to launch P100bn of residential projects this year (+33%), about half of which will be horizontal projects in provincial areas.

Residential margins could remain under pressure

Residential margins have fallen (see table on right-hand side) and management believes that they could remain under pressure as geopolitical tensions keep commodity prices high. On the other hand, margins on commercial lots have risen, thanks to higher selling prices in Pampanga and Cavite.

 
Manila Water - Capital expenditures accelerate in 4Q21
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY21 profits disappoint but capex ramp up encouraging

MWC reported profit of P3.67bn before the share of participating prefs in FY21, an 18% YoY drop. Implied 4Q21 profit dropped QoQ/YoY, as billed volumes remained soft while cash opex and interest costs jumped. Notably, group capex soared by 40% YoY to a record of P17bn, suggesting that the company spent P8bn (+60%YoY) in 4Q21 alone, majority of which was for the East zone. Even though results disappointed, we are encouraged by the ramp up in capex, which should boost its recoverable investments (i.e. Opening Cash Position, OCP), as it heads into a very important rate review this year.

East zone: Billed volumes down 2% YoY in 4Q21

Billed volumes for the East zone dropped less in 4Q21 (-2% YoY) that in 9M21 (- 4%), with commercial clients still a drag. Meanwhile, capex for FY21 stood at P14bn for the East zone, a stark improvement from only P9bn annually from 2018-2020, albeit still below our forecast (P17bn). Management attributed the faster pace of project execution to better coordination with both local and national government.

Rate rebasing updates

The company is currently drafting its business plan, which it is targeting to submit to the regulator by March 31. Given that the regulator has hired technical/financial consultants, management does not expect any undue delays in the rate rebasing process. With tariff adjustments on hold since Jan 2020, management hopes discussions with the regulator will be “fair".

Investments outside East zone also soft in 4Q21

Its other water investments in the Philippines, collectively known as Manila Water Philippine Ventures (MWPV), was still loss-making and even had a significant drop in volumes in 4Q21. Meanwhile, profit contributions from its regional investments also seemed to have declined QoQ and YoY.

 
Robinsons Retail - Non-essentials drive 4Q21 SSSG; momentum to continue in 2022
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY21 profit outperformance lifted by lower tax rate; operating profit in line

RRHI reported FY21 net profit of P4.5bn (+39% YoY), ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. The profit outperformance was largely driven by a lower effective tax rate; net sales of P153.3bn (flat YoY) were broadly in line and EBIT of P6.1bn (+5% YoY) came in ahead of forecasts. For 4Q21 alone, net profit jumped over 2x to P1.8bn vs the same period last year.

Non-essentials SSS recovers in 4Q21

Consolidated SSSG improved sequentially to +2.3% during the quarter, driven mostly by non-essential formats (c30% of sales) as mobility restrictions (particularly in Metro Manila) were partly lifted in Nov-Dec. Meanwhile, supermarkets and drug stores SSSG normalized given the higher YoY base. Across RRHI’s retail portfolio, we estimate SSS levels of department stores and convenience stores (c12% of sales combined) were still <70% of pre-Covid as of 4Q21.

Margins improve on operating leverage

Operating margin expanded both QoQ and YoY to 5.1%, similar to 4Q19. The improvement was attributed to 1) operating leverage from SSSG and 2) the continued improvement in Rose Pharmacy’s margins. Rose Pharmacy posted EBITDA margin of 7.5% in FY21, a 400-bp improvement vs the previous year, but still below the margins for South Star Drug (c9%).

SSSG to further improve as mobility restrictions lifted

On a consolidated basis, management is targeting to achieve 5-10% SSSG in 2022 (Regis FY22E: +11%), coming from a low base of comparison in 2021. The lifting of mobility restrictions bodes well for store traffic, particularly for mall[1]based formats that have yet to see a meaningful recovery in same-store sales (e.g. DIY stores). As a result, consolidated margins are also expected to improve due to operating leverage.

 
ICTSI - Solid 4Q21 EBITDA margin; Upbeat 2022 outlook
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Another quarter of earnings beat

Headline FY21 net income before perps came in at US$429mn. Excluding charges for debt prepayments and others, and also perps distributions, core profit to common stood at US$384mn (+74% YoY). Derived 4Q21 profit of US$117mn (+10% QoQ, +43% YoY) was a beat due to lower financing costs and equitized losses. EBITDA improved 33% YoY to US$310mn in 4Q21 and was broadly in line with expectations.

4Q21 volume growth slows to +5% YoY, but likely temporary

Throughput volumes rose 10% YoY to 11.2mn TEUs in FY21, implying that 4Q21 volume growth moderated to +5% from +7% in 3Q21. The slowdown was attributed to Covid-related disruptions in some key ports. Management is seeing very strong re-opening growth rates across its portfolio so far in 2022, with some ports (e.g. Iraq/Ecuador) benefitting from the commodity upcycle. Management is however cognizant of the uncertainties brought by the recent events in Ukraine. Some of its ports in Eastern Europe trade with Ukraine/Russia but revenue contributions are small.

Solid 4Q21 yield, Positive 2022 yield outlook

Yield per TEU strengthened further in 4Q21 to US$169 (+17% YoY), bringing FY21 yields up 12% YoY to US$165. Management is guiding a sustained positive yield outlook in 2022, with an anticipated tariff hike in Manila in 2Q22 alone likely to lift overall yields by another US$3 (+2%) this year. Management believes its dominant position in most markets will allow them to raise tariffs.

EBITDA margin solid at 62% in 4Q21; Acquisition pipeline healthy

EBITDA margin was solid at 62% in 4Q21. Management set a 2022 capex guidance of US$330mn for existing port expansions, with spillover effects from deferred capex in the previous year. Management believes these expansions will not erode near-term margins once completed, given strong demand.

Declares P6/sh dividend, below forecast

The company declared a regular dividend of P5.56/sh and a special div of P0.44/sh, which is below our forecast for the year (P6.67/sh) and is equivalent to 26% of FY21 operating income. Moving forward, management intends to pay a similar proportion as its regular dividend.

 
Wilcon Depot - Margins stabilize in 4Q21; inflationary pressures loom
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

4Q21 net profit +23% YoY; FY21 net profit in line at P2.6bn

WLCON’s 4Q21 net profit rose 23% YoY to P691mn, bringing the FY21 figure to P2.6bn (+77% YoY), in line with expectations. Net sales during the FY21 period rose 22% YoY to P27.5bn, mildly behind our forecasts. Margins, however, came in above expectations.

SSSG decelerates as base normalizes

4Q SSS was flattish YoY as with the more normalized base in the prior year. Mobility restrictions in Metro Manila continued to pressure same-store traffic growth in October but improved as restrictions were lifted in Nov-Dec. Management noted that November was their best month in terms of sales, though private construction activity in Metro Manila has yet to recover to pre[1]pandemic levels.

Gross margin stabilizes, new stores push opex up

Gross margin was unchanged QoQ at 38% though the contribution of higher[1]margin in-house brands increased to 50.1% (49.1% in 3Q21). The company was able to pass on higher supply chain costs through price adjustments, but newly opened stores pushed opex higher, resulting in a 30-bp QoQ contraction in operating margin.

Inflationary pressures loom

Management expects the top line momentum to be sustained in 2022 as the economy reopens and private construction activity continues to recover. Supply chain issues, however, continue to be felt and are not expected to ease until possibly the latter part of 2022. Management intends to maintain its strategy of paying its suppliers in advance in exchange for better prices and security of supply. This will keep inventory levels high (>200 days), which will mean increased working capital requirements in the next few quarters.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Strong coal prices, better plant availability drive 4Q21 outperformance
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

20-fold YoY jump in 4Q21 profit; Raising FY22E-FY23E estimates

Core profit (ex-FX adj) rose almost 20-fold to P5.76bn in 4Q21, bringing FY21 profit above Regis and consensus forecasts at P15.8bn. We attribute the 4Q outperformance to (a) higher coal ASPs, (b) lower coal mining cash costs and (c) better availability factors. The recent strength in seaborne thermal coal prices and power prices prompt us to lift our FY22E/FY23E profit forecasts by 40%/16% and TP by 20%. We nonetheless maintain our Hold rating, as we anticipate thermal coal prices peaking in 1H22 and drop thereafter.

Coal mining: Record high ASPs more than offset drop in volumes

Following record high seaborne thermal coal prices in Oct/Nov, the coal mining units saw profits soar QoQ/YoY, which more than offset the anticipated drop in sales volumes. Even though crude oil prices rose QoQ, cash costs (ex-royalties) dropped on favorable weather and controlled water seepage. Management is guiding for higher capex for 2022 of P8.8bn (up from c. P4bn in 2021) largely due to the planned construction of a new 30MW coal-fired plant in Semirara island.

Better plant availability boost 4Q profits

Profit contributions from power improved massively as plant availability factors rose to 78% in 4Q21 from 59% in 9M21, allowing SCC to take advantage of elevated power spot prices. SCC is currently selling over 60% of its operating capacity to the spot market and management indicated it is not in a rush to sign new contracts. Management expects to complete the rehab of Unit 2 by 2Q22 and, so far, its planned outages are significantly fewer than our estimates.

Dividend forecasts upgraded

We estimate free cash flow will remain above P4/sh, on our upgraded coal and power ASP assumptions (see Figures 2 and 3 in page 2 for details), allowing the company to pay a dividend of at least P3/sh (11% yield) this year.

Globe Increasing Authorized Capital; Equity Issue Looking Likely

Globe is increasing its authorized capital, raising its authorized common shares from 148.9m to 168.9m (a +20m increase).  Only the authorized common shares is being increased. There is no change for the preferred shares being planned. This move suggests that Globe is planning to do an equity issue, possibly a rights offer. Given where leverage ratios are, an equity raise is a prudent course of action. Globe’s reported net debt to equity was 1.6x (63:37 debt-equity) and net debt/EBITDA was 2.35x as of Dec 2021.  However, this treats the recent US$600m perpetual as equity. Treating those as debt would push net gearing to 2.6x (that’s 72:28 debt-equity) and net debt/EBITDA to 2.7x respectively.  And this is before the planned capex spend this year of US$1.7bn (P89bn). Regis believes an equity issue of close to US$1bn is looking probable.

MBT FY/4Q profits surge as provision normalize

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY21 NPAT of P22.16bn, up 60% YoY and slightly ahead of consensus (Bberg P21.5bn). For 4Q21 alone, profits more than doubled to P6.0bn. Both the FY and 4Q bottomline were boosted largely by a sharp drop in provision expense. The bank seems to be on its way to normalizing its provisioning, allowing its NPL cover to fall from 191% in 3Q21 to 175% in 4Q21 despite a slight uptick in NPL ratio to 2.2% (vs 2.1% in 3Q). Regis expects provision expense to continue to drive profit growth in 2022E.

 

The bank announced an increase in their regular dividend policy from P1.00/sh per year to P1.60/sh (in two tranches of P0.80). It also will pay out a special dividend of P1.40/sh, bringing total cash dividends this year to P3.00/sh, or roughly a 5% dividend yield. The bank’s capital ratios remain high, with CAR at 20.1%, and CET-1 of 19.3% at end-2021.

 

Some highlights from the financials:

 

Loans ended FY21 at -1.3% YoY, though improving from the -4.2% in 3Q21. On a sequential basis, loans were up 5.3% QoQ, driven by corporate loans and by credit cards. Its end-2021 loan book was roughly 17% lower than pre-Covid levels.

Big resurgence in “trade loans” which presumably will be the SME traders. However, auto loans were still down 23% YoY, and down 35% from the 2019 levels.

 

NIMs were 3.17% by Regis estimates, holding steady at these sort of levels since 2Q21. As loan growth recovers, and as interest rates rise, NIMs should see improving trends into 2022E. Fees (incl trust) were up 15% YoY in FY21, and roughly +20% YoY in 4Q21 alone, closing in on pre-Covid levels. There was a surge in “miscellaneous income” to P3.0bn in FY21 from just P669mn in 2020. This was driven by ROPA foreclosures – ie excess of assessed value vs loan value –  worth P813mn vs P127mn in 2020; and by “recovery on charged-off assets” worth P1.4bn vs P692mn in 2020. Trading/fx income fell 72% YoY, as expected, given the extremely high base in 2020. Opex was flat, -1% YoY. Compensation expense was up just 1.5% YoY while occupancy expense was down 6%. Taxes and licenses were down 20% YoY, in line with the drop in revenues. PPOP was down 32% YoY for FY21, but was rising nicely in 4Q, up 12% YoY and 13% QoQ. Provision expense fell 71% in FY21 to P11.8bn, given the extremely high base in 2020. In 4Q alone, provision expense dropped to P1.8bn, down 66% YoY and down 39% QoQ. This was despite a slight uptick in headline NPL ratio to 2.2%, up from 2.1% in 3Q21. NPL cover also fell to 174.7% at end-2021, from 190.7% in 3Q. It would seem that MBT management is beginning to normalize its provisioning policy, and is willing to run down its excess allowances. This should continue to be a significant earnings driver in 2022E

 

Notes from BDO earnings call

Management painted a very bullish picture heading into 2022. Loan growth is accelerating (+6% in 2021), guiding for 8-12% for 2022. NIMs positive, as balance sheet is positively geared towards rate hikes (c25bps for every 100bps increase in BSP rate). BDO is looking at strong fee growth and normalizing credit costs as asset quality improves. BDO posted ROE of 10.5% in 2021, and is targeting mid-teens ROE in the medium term. On the digital side, BDO noted that digital fees only accounted for c1% of total revenues (c7% of total fees). So, while they acknowledge that digital is the future, they will continue to focus on the “traditional” parts of the business. For now, management believes that digitalization has higher profit potential from improving efficiency / productivity.

Notes from MBT earnings call

MBT CEO Fabian Dee kicked off the call on a optimistic note. The rest of management echoed this with expectations of higher GDP growth (+6.8% in 2022), stronger loan growth, improving NIMs, and falling provisions. However, the line people were still cautious in their guidance.

 

Loans are still only expected to track GDP growth. They cited risks including supply-chain disruptions (for their trader clients), and high cash levels (ie run down before borrowing). On the consumer side, they are much more aggressive in new loan “production”, but the impact to the balances will be delayed given the paydowns of existing loans.

 

Credit cost guidance of 50-70bps, compared to 92bps in 2021. While they noted that NPL cover was still high at 174%, they were loathe to talk about potential write-backs. More positive on NIMs, given the large Treasury book – either gap out as the yield curve has steepened, and/or use for lending (c10bps for every 25bps rate hike). Digital is still a 2023 story, as the bank is in the midst of their big IT upgrades.

Meralco FY21 Results Comments

FY21 core income P24.6bn. Meralco reported headline profit of P23.5bn (+44% YoY).  Core income, as reported by the company grew 13% to P24.6bn, which is better than our estimate (P22.4bn) and consensus (P23.2bn). The “outperformance” is largely a consequence of differences in the treatment of tax assets and, to some extent, contributions from generation, particularly Pacific Light. Core distribution operations were otherwise within expectations. Volumes up 6% in FY21, with growth of 4% in early 1Q22. Electricity sales for MER were up 5.7% in FY21 and 5.8% in 4Q alone, which is just a tad below our assumption of 6%.  Sales for Jan were only up around 2% due to the Omicron-fueled surge but Feb numbers were up around 6%.  Management expects volume growth of 5-6% in FY22.

 

Dividends of P10.226/share declared, on track for 4% yield. Meralco declared dividends of P10.226/share today.  With a likely dividend of at least P5/share later this year, this calendar year’s dividend would be over P15/share.  This is higher than what Regis expected and equivalent to 4.2% yield on recent price.  Meralco ended the  year with ~P110bn in cash equivalents (P55bn) and investments, putting net cash at ~P15bn, inclusive of debt at recently acquired GBP.

Tariff review update.  Meralco will file its tariff review application for its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) by March 11 with the regulator expecting a Final Determination by July 2022.  Should there be delays (likely in our opinion), the decision/s will still be retroactively applied to July 2022.

 

Also important is a decision on Meralco’s tariffs for the “lapsed period” (i.e. from July 2015 to present), which would affect the level of refunds it may give to customers.  Management expects a decision on the lapsed period to be released before one on 5RP can proceed. Meralco is seeking a decision from the ERC that not result in a retroactive application of tariffs which could result in a reversal of over P50bn in cumulative provisions.

 

Work continues for Atimonan but now aiming for 2026.  Meralco is still continuing site  development work on the 1200MW Atimonan project but is now hoping to secure a contract under Meralco’s power supply auction (“CSP”) process to supply the DU’s needs by 2026. Meralco is also targeting the development of 1,500MW of RE projects in the next 5 to 7 years.

SM Investments 4Q/FY21 results comment

Net income P38.5bn (+65%).  SM Investments net income for FY21 amounted to P38.5bn, +65% YoY and above the Street (P34.8bn).  This implies income of P11.3bn for 4Q alone, up ~40% YoY and ~60% QoQ, The eased restrictions and improved personal mobility of the population helped boost 4Q profits. The earnings split for FY21 was 51% from banking, 25% from property, 17% from retail. The company cited, for the first time, the contribution of “portfolio investments” -- 7% to income for the year. 

 

Retail operations see 4Q bounce. Income from SM Retail amounted to P9.6bn for FY21 (+133% YoY) boosted by a rebound in 4Q21. Income for 4Q21 alone was 2.5x the previous year.  Revenue across the three main segments all saw sharp increases QoQ with the rebound more pronounced in the more discretionary formats (Department store and Specialty retail). Income from the Specialty store segment was unusually high in 4Q.

 

Food retail same store sales down 6.7% for FY21. Overall, the same store sales (SSS) for SM Retail were supposedly 1.1% lower YoY.  It was +10.9% for the Department Stores and +7.3% for the Specialty segment. However, Regis reckons only the SSS number for Food retail segment is actually useful and meaningful since those stores were open for practically the entire period since the pandemic broke out. SSS for Food retail was down 6.7% in FY21 but that figure implies that 4Q SSS would have been down only around 1% -- a significant improvement from 9M21.

 

Update on portfolio investments. The investments of SM not grouped under banking, property or retail are lumped under “portfolio investments”. These include its investments in mining, shipping/logistics, office property (ex- those under SM Prime), bakeries, dorms and gaming. The company increased its stakes in a number of these investee companies: 2GO (now 53%), Goldilocks (74%) and MyTown (71.3%).  Among these investments, the biggest contributors to profits this past year were Atlas (AT PM), a copper mining firm, and the Neo portfolio of office buildings.

SunAsia Energy Inc has formed a consortium of solar power developers to challenge Terra Solar’s unsolicited bid to supply 850MW of mid-merit renewable energy to Meralco. AGI to infuse P7.8bn into Travellers

AGI disclosed that it has agreed to infuse P7.8bn into Travellers International’s paid-up capital. The infusion of fresh capital “will afford AGI’s entertainment and hotel business the opportunity to keep current with its obligations and catch the window of business recovery”. AGI said that it has already informed Genting to infuse their share of the capital increase and awaiting their response or the liquidators’. Regis estimates AGI’s market-based NAV is P34.2/share, with the share price currently at 61% discount. Travellers accounts for c.7% of its NAV.

Globe, ST Telemedia and Ayala Corp form data center venture

Globe is spinning off its data center operation into a company called “KarmanEdge” where STT and Ayala Corp (AC) will take a 40% and 10% stake respectively.  Globe will remain the biggest shareholder with 50%. STT and AC are both infusing cash into the venture. The deal will be completed in 1Q22.

 

KarmanEdge will have the “potential to expand up to 100MW” in the medium- to long-term but it’s not clear what the current capacity is. KarmanEdge will have a post-money valuation of “over US$350m” based on the transaction/s.  Note that of the funds coming from STT and/or AC, US$100m will accrue to Globe.  This means Globe’s existing data center business is being valued at US$275m (US$100m in cash + US$175m valuation for the 50% post-money stake).

 

The above valuation is 3% of GLO’s market cap so it will move the needle slightly.  However, a comparable valuation for PLDT’s data center will probably be more meaningful. Regis believes Globe’s data center capacity is currently much smaller than PLDT’s (48MW) and PLDT is also in the process of adding another 100MW. Data centers are becoming a growth area in the Philippines as tech giants diversify away from geopolitical hotspots.

PLDT 4Q/FY21 Highlights

The bottom line: Results were decent (not great) with a mild impact from Typhoon Odette.  The impending sale of half of its telco tower network could be meaningfully value-accretive (>10%)

 

Core earnings P28bn (+3%), ex- Voyager P30.2bn (+8%). Despite some mild disappointment at the revenue line, EBITDA of P96bn (+11%) came within expectations. While core income came in short due to non-cash charges as well as losses at Voyager, Regis characterizes FY21 results as only a mild miss. The top line fell a little short due to the impact of Typhoon Rai (Odette) across the Visayas region.

 

Wireless revenue market share largely unchanged. Wireless revenue in 4Q was practically unchanged QoQ but down YoY. Even broadband revenue was a mild disappointment. Both were blamed on the impact of the typhoon which affected telecom facilities across large parts of the country. Regis estimates PLDT’s wireless revenue market share vs. Globe was 48.6%, almost unchanged QoQ.

 

A key metric Regis also keeps track of is mobile data yield (revenue/GB) and notes PLDT’s mobile data yield rose to P22/GB in 4Q, and is now ~10% higher than Globe’s. This suggests Globe has been more aggressive with its pricing.

 

Wired broadband net adds slow down slightly.  PLDT added 198k subscribers for its wired broadband service in 4Q, a slight dip from the 230k added in 3Q.  PLDT again blamed the typhoon, in part, for this slowdown. It ended the year with 2.97m subscribers. For perspective, Globe added 58k wired subscribers in 4Q and ended 2021 with 965k subscribers.

 

Telco tower sale will be value accretive.  PLDT claims to have received offers from parties to buy 6,000 of its telco towers (half of its total) at a valuation “north of US$1bn”. This should be viewed in the context of the leaseback arrangement that PLDT is stipulating with the sale: i.e. that PLDT will pay a lease broadly equivalent to the current opex (rent, security, power, etc) of these towers. Under those terms, the apparent value accretion to PLDT would be the net, after tax proceeds of such a sale.  This is likely to be significant compared to its current market cap of US$7.3bn.

 

Voyager losses rise in 4Q. PLDT’s share of Voyager’s losses was almost P800m in 4Q (P2.2bn for FY21), implying losses of ~P2.3bn at the Voyager level. Regis reckons Voyager has been spending aggressively in recent months to catch up to Gcash and in preparation for the launch of Mayabank early this year.

 

Dip in capex for FY22, with aim to be FCF positive; special dividend eyed. PLDT is guiding for capex of P76-80bn in FY22, and is aiming to be free cash flow (FCF) positive this year after 2021’s record capex of P89bn. The company is eyeing a special dividend from the proceeds of the telco tower sale. Management is guiding for mid-single digit growth in revenue, with EBITDA of over P100bn and telco core income of P32-33bn for FY22.

MWSS removes VAT on water bills

The MWSS announced it will be removing the 12% VAT beginning this month, which will result in lower water bills. The VAT will be replaced by a 2% national franchise tax and the actual rate of local franchise tax, which can range bet. 2-2.85%. This comes after Manila Water and Maynilad were granted new legislative franchises. This will take effect starting March 21. Regis reckons there will be a short term negative impact on the profitability of Manila Water and Maynilad since input VAT will no longer be offset against output VAT. The inability to benefit from input VAT will raise costs but these will be included in the calculation of OCP and should have no material impact on the value of their concessions.

Budget deficit expands to P1.67tn in FY21, P338bn in December 2021

The National Government recorded a budget deficit of P338bn in December 2021 due to a contraction in revenues (-3% YoY) and higher expenses (+5%). This brings FY21 budget deficit to P1.67tn, equivalent to 8.61% of GDP, broadly in line with the government’s target (P1.61tn, or 8.2% of GDP). Tax effort of 14.13% in 2021 rose from last year’s 13.96% while total expenditures was 24.1% of GDP, slightly higher than in 2020 (23.57%). For 2022, the government is targeting a deficit-to-GDP ratio of P1.65tn (7.7% of GDP).

Duterte signs EO to revive nuclear power

President Duterte signed an executive order (EO) allowing the country to tap nuclear power as an alternative energy source. The EO directed the Department of Energy to conduct a pre-feasibility study on the viability of introducing nuclear power. The Nuclear Energy Program Inter-Agency Committee (NEP-IAC) was also tasked to make recommendations on the use and viability of the mothballed Bataan Nuclear Power Plant. Energy Undersecretary Gerardo D. Erguiza, Jr. said the government needs to come up with a regulatory framework for the nuclear energy program, which will need legislation. Nuclear power plants with small modular reactors, which are being eyed in off-grid areas, can be built as soon as the regulatory framework is already. In 2020, the Philippines’ power mix consisted of 57% coal, 21% renewable energy, 19% natural gas, and 2% oil-based.

Review of oil deregulation law sought

President Duterte urged Congress to review the country’s oil deregulation law in order to intervene in the event of oil spikes according to Cabinet Secretary Karlo Nograles. He wants to review provisions on: price unbundling, inclusion of the minimum inventory requirements, and government intervention during oil spikes. The Downstream Oil Deregulation Act of 1998 removed government control on the pricing, exportation, and importation of petroleum products. Congress is currently on break for the May 9 elections and Malacanang did not indicate if it would call for a special session on the topic. President Duterte approved the release of P3bn fuel subsidies and discounts for public utility vehicle drivers and agricultural workers. Fuel vouchers worth an average of P6.5k will be given to 377k qualified drivers. P500mn will be given as fuel discount vouchers to farmers and fisherfolk.
