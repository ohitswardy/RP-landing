REGIS REPORT

02 June 2022

This Philippines continued to consolidate between the 6800-6700 level to close at 6741.40, up just +0.23% WoW. Liquidity was skewed to the early part of the week due to MSCi rebalancing flows but went dry towards the end of the week. Market turnover last Tuesday (MSCi Rebalancing) was US$680m but wasa mere US$88m on Friday.  

Index gainers numbered 17, while losers this week were 12. Foreigners were sellers for US$20m this week amid global hyper inflation concerns. MSCI rebalancing also added to the outflows where US$5m exited on rebalancing day. Biggest outflows were seen in across telcos/banks/property.

Holdcos pushed up the index, with SM Investments (+3.2% WoW) and JG Summit (+5.1% WoW) were among the top movers for the week. Recent underperformers Ayala Land (+4.0% WoW) and parent Ayala Corp (+2.3%) also helped push the index higher. Both have recently underperformed partly on the back of MSCI rebal downweights. Other strong gainers include SMC (+3.6% WoW) and utilities Aboitiz Power (+3.3% WoW), Meralco (+2.5% WoW), and AC Energy (+2.3% WoW). 

Monde (-13.1% WoW) was the main drag to the index, giving up its accumulated gains coming from its MSCI inclusion. 

Telcos found themselves among the laggards list this week. Globe (-7.8% WoW) declined markedly, after recent strength.  

Meanwhile, competition is intensifying in the broadband space.  MSCI rebal addition Converge (-4.9% WoW) announced that it was increasing speeds for all subscribers of its entry level plan (FiberX 1500) from 50mbps to 100mbps. The new speeds are twice as fast as the closes packages for Globe and PLDT (-0.7% WoW). 

ICT (-3.5% WoW), Jollibee (-3.1% WoW), and banks Metrobank (-1.5% WoW) and BPI (-0.6% WoW) also declined.

Wages are on the rise, raising inflation pressure. Several more regions in the country approved  increases in their minimum wages, with the average increase in the ~10% range. Eleven out of 17 regions have approved wage hikes recently. These are expect to create short-term cost pressures for many companies though this could also help improve consumption. 

Newsflow continued from the incoming administration. It appears the Marcos goverment will return to a public-private partnership (PPP) model to undertake its infrastructure initiatives instead of teh ODA-funded proects that the Duterte governemnt favored. The return of PPPs should create infra investment opportunities for many corporates. 

Earlier this week, President-elect Ferdinand Marcos, Jr revealed a few more names joining the Cabinet. One of these was the which was the announcement of Bangko Sentral (BSP) Assistant Governor Amenah Pangandaman as Budget Secretary. She will join the economic team with current BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno has been tapped as the next Secretary of Finance.

Philippine Strategy - Marcos Cabinet shaping up – Diokno moves from BSP to Dept of Finance
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Economic team headed by economists

Ferdinand R Marcos, Jr – officially proclaimed as the country’s next President – has announced nominations for key Cabinet positions. Current BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno has been tapped as the next Secretary of Finance, with Monetary Board member Mr Felipe Medalla taking over as BSP chief. Mr Arsenio Balisacan will head the NEDA, a post he held under President Aquino. All three are career economists, and all have extensive government experience. These three signal continuity of current fiscal and monetary policy, and are in line with our base case of economic orthodoxy. However, we need to see other key nominations – such as Agriculture, Energy, and Environment – for a more complete view of Mr Marcos’s economic program. A strong Cabinet should help reassure the business and investment community, particularly given domestic and global macro challenges.

Growth-focused, but fiscally responsible

Mr Diokno has, over his academic and government career, consistently advocated for robust fiscal spending in support of domestic growth, and that poverty reduction is best served through provincial infrastructure development. However, he has also in the past cautioned against agriculture subsidies, and against cutting oil taxes. He, together with Mr Medalla and Mr Balisacan were contemporaries as professors at the University of the Philippines (UP) School of Economics, and likely share these views. Perhaps coincidentally, President[1]elect Marcos has recently walked back his campaign promises of bringing down rice prices to P20/kg, and of suspending the fuel excise taxes.

Other Cabinet picks seem credible, too; still

Mr Marcos’s Cabinet is by no means complete, but those that he has nominated are largely credible and qualified. Mr Alfredo Pascual, nominated to the Department of Trade and Industry, is currently the head of the Management Association of the Philippines (MAP). The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) will be headed by Mr Manuel Bonoan, currently CEO of San Miguel Tollways. Meanwhile, Vice President-elect Sara Duterte-Carpio will be Secretary of Education. (See page 2 for more details of Cabinet nominees so far.)

A few more pieces to complete the picture

Mr Marcos has yet to communicate his overall economic program, which should be clearer once the Cabinet is complete. We are closely watching the pick for Department of Energy, given our projections of tight supply in the Luzon grid. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources head will dictate policy for the mining sector. Meanwhile, the incoming Agriculture Secretary will need to map out a strategy for food security given soaring soft commodity prices globally.

 
Philippine Strategy - Minimum wage hiked 6% in Metro Manila, much higher in provinces
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Wage hikes ease consumers’ pain; but further squeeze margins

In response to rising inflation, the mandated minimum wages are being raised for the first time since 2018/19. For Metro Manila (or National Capital Region or NCR), the hike was P33/day, or 6.1-6.6% from the current P500-537/day level. To date, 9 other regions have increased wages by an average of about 12-16% (but ranging from 8-36%); we expect the remaining 7 regions to hike soon. The wage adjustments will bring relief to employed consumers. However, second-round effects could make inflation stickier, posing challenges to monetary policy. The wage hikes will also squeeze profits, particularly for companies already dealing with soaring commodity input cost.

Minimum wage raised 6-7% for NCR, 12-16% for other regions (so far)

On 22 May, the Regional Tripartite Wage Board (RTWB) for NCR approved a P33/day hike, raising the minimum wage by 6.1-6.6% from the current P500- 537/day to P533-570/day. Since then, 9 other regions (17 total) have granted wage hikes, averaging 12-16%. Western Visayas was by far the highest, raising minimum wages there by P55-110/day – a whopping 14-35% (see Figure 1 on page 2). These will be the first increases across the country since 2018/2019.

A salve for consumers, but a challenge for the BSP

The wage increases will be a welcome relief for consumers, particularly given the rapidly rising inflation (4.9% in April), though the hike doesn’t even cover inflation from 2019-2021 (Figure 2). However, it might pose an additional challenge for the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), as wage inflation is “stickier”, and could keep CPI elevated even if global commodity prices moderate. The BSP raised policy rates by 25bps in May, for the first time since the pandemic. BSP Governor Diokno had guided for 50-75bps for the year, though the risk to this seems weighted to the upside, in our view.

Downside risk to corporate profits, on top of input cost squeeze

While not all employees are on minimum wage, regular salaries tend to keep pace, more so for heavily unionized industries. We analyzed our coverage for profit sensitivity to higher domestic labor costs, holding all else equal (understanding, of course that this may be overly simplistic). As expected, restaurant operators (MAXS, JFC and PIZZA) are among the worst hit (Figure 3). Surprisingly, manpower is also quite high as a share of profit for telcos and banks (see page 2). Food manufacturers (CNPF, MONDE, URC) are typically not that affected by wage increases. However, the current margin squeeze from elevated commodity input prices could make labor costs another unwelcome pressure point.

 
Vista Land - 1Q22 mall operations start to recover; REIT listing to raise P4.8bn
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Earnings flattered by accounting rules

Vista Land’s 1Q22 net profit rose 11% YoY to P2.2bn. While mall operations are recovering well, we believe that rental revenue is overstated relative to actual cash rent and that earnings would have disappointed otherwise.

Reported mall revenue is up sharply but overstates the recovery

1Q rental revenue, as reported, grew 29% YoY to P2.6bn, already 150% of the 2019 average. Rental receivables of P17.4bn, meanwhile, represent 630 days of rent. VLL employs straight-line rental accounting for its long-term rental contracts. This results in reported rental revenue being much higher than actual rents collected during initial years. Same-mall cash rent is, we estimate, actually closer to 70% of pre-COVID levels, just slightly better than other mall operators (which are showing 45%-65% of pre-COVID levels).

Growth in reservation sales driven by condominium projects

Presales grew 5% YoY (14% QoQ) to P16.8bn, 85% of the 2019 average. In contrast to other developers, growth was driven by condominiums rather than horizontal projects. Thus, VLL plans to launch more vertical projects later this year. Meanwhile, after three consecutive quarters of declines, residential revenue grew 64% QoQ to P4.8bn, which is still 40% below the 2019 average.

To raise P4.8bn via REIT listing

VLL will spin off 256,000sqm of rental properties (10 malls and 2 offices) into Vista REIT (VREIT). It aims to raise as much as P4.8bn from the sale of 2.75bn secondary VREIT shares at P1.75/share. Based on management projections (in the prospectus), VREIT will offer a 2023 dividend yield of 9%, higher than the 5.5%-7.5% of existing Philippine REITs.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PLDT completes sale of initial batch of telecom towers

PLDT completed the sale of 3,012 telecom towers of the 5,907 towers subject of the sale agreement earlier. PLDT received P39bn in cash for the tower portfolio, out of the total P77bn consideration. The sale of the remainder will be completed by 4Q. PLDT is expected to record a pretax gain of P55bn once the entire sale is completed. Part of the gain will be deferred over the life of the 10-year lease of the towers. The savings on financing cost for PLDT will exceed P2bn on an annual basis.

EW - Moncupa retiring; New CEO and new President announced

Tony Moncupa is retiring as CEO/President of East West Bank (EW PM). He will retire as President on 30 June. The post will be taken by Ms Jacqueline Fernandez, who currently is the bank’s Chief Lending Officer. Mr Moncupa will stay on as CEO until end-2022, after which he will be replaced by Mr Jerry Ngo, who is currently a strategy consultant for EW’s parent co Filinvest Development Corp (FDC PM). Mr Moncupa will remain Vice Chairman of EW.

Converge raises speeds for entry level plan again.

Converge increased speeds for subscribers (existing and new) for its entry level plan (FiberX 1500) from 50mbps to 100mbps. It previously increased speeds only for new subscribers. The entry level plan costs P1,500 / month. The installation fees (up to P2,500) have also been waived. The speed for its entry level plan is now 2x those of its close competitors. The move is a reflection that industry is now moving to a new phase, from being land grab to a situation of rising competition on price and service. Regis expects the other providers to follow suit, even if they don’t match the new speed/s exactly.

Vista Land 1Q22 net profit rises 11% YoY

Vista Land’s 1Q22 net profit rose 11% to P2.2bn. While this appears ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, Regis would argue that this is due to overstated rental revenue (relative to cash rent). Residential revenue fell 23% YoY to P4.8bn, c. 60% of the 2019 average and trailing behind Regis forecast. Rental revenue grew 29% YoY to P2.6bn, 150% of the 2019 average. Other mall operators only achieved 45%-65% of the 2019 average. Regis estimates that rental receivables stood at P17.4bn, 175% of trailing 12-month rental revenue. Additionally, Regis estimates that cash rent is only c. 50% of the rental revenue figure. Net debt stood at P107bn while gross debt of P173bn. Net debt/equity is at 93%.

Razon firm to acquire controlling stake in Malampaya

Razon-led Prime Infrastructure Holdings said on Thursday that it is “poised to acquire a controlling stake” in the Malampaya gas-to-power project. The acquisition has “kicked off” and is subject to the consent of the Department of Energy (DOE) and Philippine National Oil Co Exploration Corp (PNOC EC). Prime Infra Chairman Enrique K. Razon, jr. said the project “will allow us to contribute more to the country’s transition from expensive and volatile coal and imported fuel prices to affordable cleaner and domestic energy resources like natural gas”. Prime Infra referred to the seller as Dennis A. Uy, whose business unit acquired the 45% stake in the deepwater project held by Chevron Malamapaya. The deal announced in October 2019 was approved by the DOE in March 2021. Another Udenna unit has a pending deal to acquire the 45% stake of Shell Philippines Exploration B.V. (SPEx) but is waiting for the consent of PNOC EC. PNOC EC holds the remaining 10%. The Service Contract covering the Malampaya gas field will expire in December 2024. The Razon group is also in talks to gain control of Udenna’s casino projects in Cebu and Clark.

2022 budget = P5.024trn

Loan growth accelerates to 10.1% YoY in April

U/KBs bank lending grew 10.1% YoY in April, faster than in March (+8.9%) according to preliminary data from the BSP. The acceleration was driven by consumer loans and loans to manufacturing sector. Recall that loan growth for Philippine banks jumped by +5.8% in March. U/KBs loans figure distorted by the merger of BPI with Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) slowed to 7.3% in April from 7.7% in March.

Marcos eyes more PPP projects

The incoming admin will work to entice investors in the government’s infrastructure program through PPP according to the next head of the Dept of Public Works (DPWH) and currently SMC Tollways President/CEO Manuel Bonoan. In contrast to outgoing President Duterte, who steered clear of PPPs. DPWH will focus on food production and tourism programs and will reprioritize some projects in the pipeline to favor high-impact infra projects that will contribute immediately to the economic recovery.

More regions approve wage hikes

The government approved wage hikes for five (5) more regions over the past week. MIMAROPA (IV-B) wages hiked by 10-12% (to be implemented in two tranches). Bicol (V) wages hiked by 18% (to be implemented in two tranches). Central Visayas (VII) hiked by 8-9%. Northern Mindanao (X) hiked by 12-13% (to be implemented in two tranches). SOCCKSARGEN (XII) hiked by 10% (to be implemented in two tranches). These will add to recently approved wage hikes in NCR, Ilocos, Cagayan valley, and Caraga.

Hot money reversed to net inflows in April

BSP-registered foreign portfolio investments (i.e. hot money) in April 2022 yielded a net inflow of US$1.4bn. This is a reversal from the US$305mn net outflow in March 2022. Gross inflows for the month amounted to US$2.2bn, up 71% MoM and 92% of which were investments in PSE-listed securities. Gross outflows of US$823mn were lower by 48% MoM. This brings YT-April hot money to a net inflow of US$1.34bn, a reversal from the US$857mn net outflow in the same period last year.

Gov’t posts budget surplus in April

The national government reverted to a P4.9bn budget surplus in April. This is a reversal from the P44bn deficit in the same period last year and P187bn deficit in March 2022. Revenues jumped 19% YoY, with tax collections up 13% YoY. The primary spending dropped 2% YoY on base effects from last year’s one-off financial assistance to LGUs under ECQ (NCR plus) and timing of GOCC subsidy releases. Excluding last year’s one-offs, primary spending would have risen by 9.4% YoY. This brings YT-April budget deficit to P312bn, down 15% YoY. The government is targeting a budget deficit of P1.65tn by year-end, equivalent to 7.7% of GDP.
