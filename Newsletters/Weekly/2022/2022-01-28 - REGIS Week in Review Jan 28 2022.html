REGIS REPORT

28 January 2022

The Phils closed 0.6% lower this week, amidst global markets’ preoccupation with the Fed’s next moves.

On the ground, the Covid-19 situation showed daily new case counts declining to ~24k (7 day ma) vs ~33k, with new cases in Metro Manila falling to ~5400 (7 day ma) from around 13k in the previous week. The country has fully vaccinated 53% of the population and the commencement of vaccination for children ages 5-11 next week should bring this ratio higher.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$28m this week amid concerns over Fed tightening coming in March. Biggest outflows were in consumers and property.

Index losers outnumbered gainers 19:11 this week.  Index heavyweights pushed the market lower with declines in holdcos SM (-2.1% WoW), JG summit (-3.6% WoW) and Ayala Corp (-1.8% WoW), pushing the index down over 44 index points.  The PSEi dropped a total of 42 points this week.  Also weighing on the index property names SMPH (-1.4% WoW), Ayala Land (-1.3% WoW).

Banks on the other hand outperformed this week – with BPI (+4.9% WoW) and BDO (+3.5% WoW) leading.  

Consumer names were mixed -- Wilcon (+7.4% WoW) gained sharply and Jollibee (+2.7%) gained while Puregold (-2.5% WoW), URC (-0.8% WoW), and Monde (-1.45 %WoW) dropped.

Robinsons Retail  (+0.6% WoW) closed slightly up. After it disclosed its purchase of the 40% stake of Robinson Convenience Stores held by Ministop (Japan) and the rebranding of its convenience store business, RRHI's e-commerce arm Growsari raised US$45m from KKR. 

On the macro side, the country’s 4Q21 GDP expanded 7.7% as private consumption accelerated. FY21 GDP growth stood at 5.6%, beating consensus (5.1%) and government 5.0%-5.5%) forecasts. Meanwhile, the 2021 trade deficit to doubled to a record US$43.2bn, underscoring expectaions of peso deprecation moving ahead.

RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - 2022 Outlook – Glass still half full?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Recovery vs inflation, the PHP, and the Presidential elections

We remain positive on the Philippine recovery story, forecasting GDP growth of 6.5% in 2022E. With Metro Manila and key cities 70-80% fully vaccinated, the current Covid-19 wave seems to be abating quickly. A rebound in the battered domestic tourism could provide upside surprise. Inflation is a concern, however, in a consumption-driven economy. We also expect the PHP to weaken on a growing trade deficit. The Presidential elections in May could raise uncertainty, though we expect the new government’s economic policy to be broadly orthodox. We would overweight the banking, property, power, and transport sectors, and select consumer names.

Bullish on economic recovery, despite Covid surge

GDP growth in 4Q21 was 7.7%, roundly beating consensus, taking FY21 to +5.6%, in line with our 5.5% estimate. The print was consistent with indicators such as mobility, electricity sales, and loan growth which were strong in 4Q21. While the new Covid-19 surge saw mobility crash, the case count is falling steeply in Metro Manila, where c80% of residents are fully vaccinated. We expect mobility to rebound quickly, and forecast GDP growth of 6.5% in 2022E. “Revenge travel” could provide upside, given the country’s high reliance on domestic tourism.

 

Inflation, interest rates and PHP

Inflation eased in 4Q21, thanks to high base effects in late 2020. However, stubbornly high global food and energy prices could blunt consumer demand, as well as corporate margins. The BSP will likely keep short-term rates low for most of the year. The long end of the curve, though, has already steepened ahead of the US tightening cycle. Meanwhile, we expect the PHP to weaken this year, as the trade deficit balloons on resurgent demand and high import prices.

Do the Presidential elections matter?

The Presidential elections are set for May this year, and so far, surveys show Ferdinand Marcos, Jr leading the pack. Whoever wins, we expect economic policy to be broadly orthodox. The next administration will need to build investor confidence – domestic and foreign – to continue the economy’s growth momentum. The key challenge, though, will be getting the fiscal deficit (c9% of GDP) and government debt (c60% of GDP) back under control.

How to play it? Sector and stock picks

We are overweight on the banking, property, power, and transportation sectors as recovery plays. While the consumer sector is rebounding, input cost inflation could squeeze margins this year, making us more selective. We are underweight the telecom sector; we believe the massive outperformance last year will be hard to match. Our top picks include Metrobank, Robinsons Land, Aboitiz Power, ICTSI, Cebu Air, GT Capital, Ayala Corp, Century Pacific, and Robinsons Retail.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

MWSS probes water output gaps in Maynilad’s concession area

The water regulator, MWSS, is investigating to determine whether it needs to impose penalties on Maynilad. Water production at Maynilad’s Putatan water treatment plant remains below its 300 MLD capacity. Maynilad has argued that the water turbidity in Laguna Lake was beyond the design parameters of the plant. Maynilad is currently building a third treatment plant in Muntinlupa that will add another 150 MLD upon its completion in 2023. Around 220k customers are on rotational schedule out of Maynilad’s 1.5mn customers/connections but all have water within a 24 hour period. In 2019, MWSS imposed a regulatory penalty of P534mn on Manila Water (MWC), after severe water service interruptions to its entire concession area. Maynilad is jointly owned by Metro Pacific (MPI PM – 53%) and DMCI (DMC PM – 25%). Maynilad accounts for c. 16% of MPI’s NAV and 12% of DMCI’s NAV. MPI is trading at a 44% discount to its market NAV of P6.80/sh. DMC is trading at a 14% discount to its market NAV of P9.50/sh.

8990 Holdings to conduct follow-on offering

Up to 1.25bn existing HOUSE shares will be sold by major shareholders: TPG Rafter Holdings, Pasir Salak Investments (SPV of Khazanah Nasional Berhad), and IHoldings. This represents 23% of current shares outstanding. The plan also includes an overallotment option of 150mn shares (from IHoldings). At the current HOUSE price of P11.40/share, the offering could be as large as P14.25bn. Up to 450mn new common shares will be issued to IHoldings. This assumes that the overallotment option was exercised, and this would increase shares outstanding by 8%. At P11.40/share, 8990 Holdings could raise as much as P5.13bn. IHoldings is currently the largest shareholder of 8990 Holdings. The funds from the primary share issuance will likely be used to pay down debt. As of 3Q21, 8990 had net debt of P28.9bn. 

Additional notes from RRHI disclosure re Ministop

RRHI’s disclosure did not provide much more detail. They are unable to disclose price and valuation at this point as the transaction has not yet closed. Expected close is end-Feb. As early as now, RRHI will start rebranding the Ministop stores, though they couldn’t reveal yet what the new brand is. It will retain the CVS format and continue to focus on ready-to-eat (RTE) offerings. RTE currently accounts for 40% of Ministop’s sales. Accdg to RRHI, Ministop Japan has been less active in running the Phils operations over the last couple of years, so there should be no material impact in terms on business operations. The current General Manager of Ministop is an appointee of RRHI and was previously with 7-Eleven Singapore.

DM Wenceslao sells another land parcel in the Manila Bay area

DM Wenceslao and Associates (DMW PM) sold a land parcel in January 2022. The size and price of the parcel were similar to the December 2021 transaction, which was 1,790sqm at roughly P425,000/sqm-P450,000/sqm. The two parcels are adjacent to each other and were sold to the same buyer. The price of a land transaction in 2019 was P425,000/sqm. A Colliers estimate in 2020 valued the company’s properties at P204bn (c. P60/share) with the useable land was valued at P350,000/sqm. This would imply that DMW is trading at 89% discount to NAV.

Dito aims for 12m subscribers in 2022

Dito Telecommunity is aiming to reach 12m subscribers within this year. The new operator claimed to have reached 5m subscribers by end-2021. That figure would suggest a subscriber market share of ~7%. Regis estimates Dito was able to take ~1% revenue market share in 2H21. The telco Dito Telecommunity is 54% owned by Dito CME (DITO PM).

Villar-linked firm secures ABS-CBN frequencies

The National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) confirmed that Villar’s Advanced Media Broadcasting System (AMBS) was awarded provisional authority to operate Channel 16, ABS-CBN’s digital TV frequency. The NTC also issued AMBS a temporary permit “for simulcast purposes” over the analog Channel 2, effective January 6 until the analog shutoff in 2023. NTC cited that it sought the legal opinion of the Department of Justice and the concurrence of the Office of the Executive Secretary. Villar’s daughter-in-law (wife of Mark Villar) Emmeline Aglipay-Villar is a justice undersecretary. NTC cited that AMBS was the first applicant for digital TV in Metro Manila; application was filed over 15 years ago/Oct 5, 2006.

Ayala Land Board approves property-for-share swap with AC and Mermac

Ayala Land will issue 312mn new ALI shares to parent Ayala Corp and Mermac at P55.80/share. The breakdown would be 310mn ALI shares to Ayala Corp and 2mn shares to Mermac. This will increase Ayala Land’s shares outstanding by 2%. AC and Mermac will exchange five assets worth P17.4bn for the ALI shares. The largest portion (P13.2bn) is for a 50% stake in the ALP project Park Central Towers and the land on which Manila Peninsula is built. If we were to assume zero value for Park Central, the Manila Peninsula land would be valued at c. P1.3mn/sqm, which seems like a fair price. Thus, this portion (which includes a 50% stake in Park Central) appears slightly cheap. Manila Peninsula has a long-term land lease so this parcel cannot be developed earlier than 2030. Since the ALI shares to be issued will be priced close to our P57.50/share NAV, the transaction should be mildly NAV accretive (less than 1%) for Ayala Land. ALI is currently trading at 41% discount to NAV, 1.9X FY22E P/B, 18.8X FY22E P/E.

MREIT to acquire P20bn worth of offices this year

MREIT will acquire P20bn worth of offices this year, raising its portfolio value by 34% to P78.5bn. The acquisitions will be funded primarily through equity and potentially some debt. Megaworld currently owns 62.5% of MREIT. In December 2021, MREIT 55,700sqm of office space (from MEG) for P9.1bn in cash. This expanded MREIT’s portfolio by 25% to 280,000sqm. MREIT is targeting to have a portfolio of 500,000sqm by 2024. MREIT currently offers a dividend yield of 4.5% based on management guidance of P1/share. The 10-year government bond yield is 5.0%. MEG is currently trading at 59% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 6.9X FY22E P/E.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

NCR cases/hospitalizations on a downtrend

The country’s cases (7dma) dropped 12% WoW to 30k, with NCR cases down 41% WoW to 10k. Hospitalization rates are now at 46%-52% range in NCR, down from recent peaks of 58%-69%. Metro Manila was placed under a stricter mobility “Alert Level 3” in early January. To be classified under looser “Alert Level 2” status, covid transmission rates should be at a low/decreasing rate and bed utilization rates should be decreasing (<60%). However, nationwide hospitalization rates are still on an uptrend (51-52%).

BOP posts US$991mn surplus in December

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$991mn in December 2021. This is a reversal from the US$123mn BOP deficit in November 2021. This is likely due to inflows from JERA’s purchase of a 25% stake in Aboitiz Power (AP PM). Additionally, this brings 2021 BOP surplus at US$1.345bn, lower than the US$16bn surplus is 2020.

Mobility recovers in Metro Manila as Covid cases drop

The latest data from Google (as of Saturday, 22 Jan) showed mobility data improving in Metro Manila, while the national numbers have been sideways over the previous week. Regis expects the momentum to continue into Feb.

Gov’t revises down 3Q21 GDP to 6.9%

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) revised 3Q21 GDP downwards to 6.9% from 7.1% previously and cited revisions to financial/insurance activities, professional/business services, and real estate and ownership dwellings.

DoE solicits green energy bids for 2,000MW

The Department of Energy (DoE) said it is inviting companies to supply 2,000MW of renewable energy (RE) in its first round of its Green Energy Auction Program (GEAP). In the Notice of Auction, the Doe Said the power will be supplied to Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao at the following volumes: 1,400MW, 400MW and 200MW, respectively. For Luzon bidders, the total capacity for auction is 900MW of solar, 360MW of wind, 80MW hydro and 60MW biomass. For Visayas, the breakdown is 120MW biomass, 260MW solar and 20MW wind. For Mindanao, the targets are 100MW solar, 50MW biomass and 50MW hydro. The GEAP aims to boost RE as a source of energy, with RE generators participating in an auction for their output. The GEAP features the Green Energy Tariff, which influences the commercial value of power generated from qualified RE facilities and sets a benchmark price for DUs under an opt-in mechanism. DU can procure power from the GEAP pool of winning bidders to reduce Feed-in Tariff-All charges to the end-users.

Pace of vaccinations falls

As of January 23, the 7-day average vaccinations figure climbed to 632,000/day from 797,000/day the previous week. Around 53% of the Philippine population (57.3mn Filipinos) have been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 216mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 123mn doses (as of January 23). The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22. “Vaccine Czar” Secretary Carlito Galvez said that vaccine hesitancy fell to only 8% (from 30% in May 2021).

GDP expands 7.7% in 4Q21 / 5.6% in FY21

The country’s GDP expanded by +7.7% in 4Q21 and +3.1% QoQ on a seasonally adjusted basis beating analyst expectations of +6.3%. By expenditure, private consumption accelerated to +7.5%, as the government shifted to an Alert Level system with granular lockdowns. Both industry and services sector sustained improvements. For FY21, GDP expanded by +5.6%, beating consensus estimates (+5.1%) and came in above the government’s target range of +5.0-5.5% a reversal from the 9.6% contraction in 2020. In nominal terms, 2021 GDP stood at P19.39tn, just a shade below 2019 (P19.52tn). For FY22, the government remains optimistic GDP growth could range +7 to +9%; Consensus/Regis at +6.5%.

Trade deficit balloons in 2021; worrying monthly trend

The country’s trade deficit ballooned, with FY2021 at an all-time high $43.22bn, almost double YoY. This came as imports surged 30.4% YoY, far outpacing export growth of 15%. The import explosion was due to a combination of a) economic recovery, ie demand for imported goods, and b) higher import prices. The monthly picture looks even more worrisome, with the deficit worsening almost every month last year. If the deficit holds even at the 4Q21 average, we could be looking at a 2022 full year trade deficit of $55bn. Needless to say, this won’t be good for the PHP.

COVID vaccination of children ages 5 to 11 to begin on Feb 4

The country is scheduled to receive 2.6mn doses of the pediatric formulation of Pfizer’s COVID-19 vaccine next week. Vaccination in Metro Manila will begin on February 4. The program will be rolled out nationwide in the middle of February. The DOH estimates that there are 13.5mn Filipinos aged 5 to 11. Around 53% of the Philippine population (58.1mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 216mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 125mn doses (as of January 26). The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22.
