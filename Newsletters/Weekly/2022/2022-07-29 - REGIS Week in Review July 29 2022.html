REGIS REPORT

29 July 2022

The Phils gained +0.8% this week, consolidating around the 6200-6300 index level amidst a lack of domestic catalysts.  Volumes continued to be thin while foreign flows continued to move outward.

Index gainers outnumbered losers 16 to 13. Wilcon (+20.2% WoW) led this week, surging significantly after 2Q results beat consensus expectations.  Profit for 2Q jumped 56%, driven by gross margin expansion.

BPI (+4.8% WoW) also helped to push the index higher, with its strong 2Q print from last week leading to spillover interest. Metrobank (+4.1% WoW) also gained, though BDO (-1.4% WoW) was soft. .  A consortium led by BDO initiated  foreclosure proceedings against a  subsidiary of Udenna Corp, the holding company of Davao-based businessman Dennis Uy. The news report claimed that the subsidiary, Global Gateway Development Corp (GGDC), was behind in about $4m lease payments to Clark International Airport Corp (CIAC).  Udenna has since made the P274m lease payment, allowing it to  avoid to avoid a default.   

ICTSI (+6.3% WoW) also gained after recently underperforming. ICTSI is acquiring another port in Indonesia (for a price of US$46.5m with a size of 5m MT annually, bulk). The acquisition takes its number of ports across the globe to 36.

Large caps Aboitiz Equity (+8.9% WoW),SM Prime (+1.7% WoW), Ayala Corp (+2.6% WoW), and Ayala Land (+1.4% WoW) also pushed the  market higher.

Aboitiz Power (+2.7% WoW) also gained after it reported a strong bounce for its 2Q profits (59% YoY, +111% QoQ), post disappointing 1Q results, thanks to fresh contributions from the newly available capacity of GN Power Dinginin (GNPD).

JG Summit (unch) sold 36m shares of Meralco (-3.95% WoW) in an overnight placement at P344/sh (6% discount to Thursday’s close). JG Summit still holds around 297m shares in Meralco post the transaction.

Of the total shares sold, Metro Pacific (-1.3% WoW) acquired 22.54m shares (~2% of Meralco), increasing its indirect and direct stake in Meralco to 47.5%.

There was no let-up in foreign selling. While headline foreign flows for the week showed inflows of US$9.8m, this was skewed by the Meralco placement which registered US$47m in inflows. Excluding this, foreigners were net sellers for US$37m.

Manila Electric Company - 2Q lifted again by overseas genco; lapsed period overhang finally resolved?
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1H core income rises 15%

Meralco reported another solid quarter earnings-wise. Core income for 1H22 came in at P13.1bn (+15% YoY), implying 2Q core income of P7.46bn (+19% YoY). Sales volumes for its power distribution business grew 5.8% for both 1H and 2Q. The generation business made the difference this year, with Singapore subsidiary Pacific Light largely responsible for the outperformance.

Electricity volumes rise 6%, Pacific Light delivers another strong quarter

Electricity sales for its core distribution business were up 5.8% in 2Q, with commercial volumes leading the way (+17%). 2Q residential volumes, however, were down 1% YoY. The rather unusual YoY changes across the segments reflect the hard lockdown/s in place last year (2Q21). Meralco’s 58%-owned Pacific Light posted core income of P5.7bn for 1H (zero in 1H21), implying 2Q core income of P2.6bn. Global Business Power (GBP), on the other hand, continued to hemorrhage, losing P1.2bn for 1H.

Regulator sets tariff for lapsed period but no provision reversals yet

Early this month, the regulator (“ERC”) set a tariff of P1.3522/kwh for the period beginning July 2015 to June 2022. This is a period where the regulator was previously unable to undertake a proper tariff review (thus “lapsed period”). The ERC decision also ordered Meralco to refund customers more, taking cumulative refunds to P48.3bn. Meralco, however, has set aside more than enough provisions to cover the refunds over the last seven years. In fact, we estimate it likely has close to P40bn of “excess” provisions that may be reversed. The timing of this, however, is uncertain given the sensitive nature of the issue. Beyond just the refund, the ERC decision fixing the tariff at P1.3522/kwh (even though provisional) is also likely to mean changes in how Meralco reports its earnings, possibly through a reduction in continuing provision expenses. We may see earnings rise by over P2bn per quarter as a result.

 
Philippine Property - Standing on one leg
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Malls are the bright spot; RLC is our top pick

Despite optimism that the economic reopening would revive the property sector while malls are seeing a sharp rebound, the outlook for the residential and office segments has worsened. Thus, we expect malls to be the key bright spot in the sector as the country climbs back from two years of lockdown. Tenant sales are approaching pre-COVID levels, and mall revenue should follow suit as rental concessions are reduced. Moreover, the likely spin-off of malls into REITs could lead to NAV increase of up to 15% for some developers.

RLC is our top pick in the sector. Beyond just the mall recovery, RLC would also see, on our estimates, the biggest uplift in NAV from a mall REIT spin off. Among the large caps, we prefer Ayala Land due to its earnings growth (3-year CAGR of 38%, the highest in our coverage) and the NAV enhancement we expect from infrastructure projects adjacent to its land bank. Furthermore, current valuations for ALI are already compelling, in our view.

Stronger headwinds for office and residential segments

While the outlook for the malls has become rosier, the reverse is true for the residential and office segments. Office vacancy has already risen to 19% and we expect it to climb to 21%-22% as work-from-home crimps demand while new supply is added. Meanwhile, the outlook on the residential segment is also bleaker as interest rates are rising at a time when construction costs are jumping and the mortgage NPL ratio is close to a 15-year-high.

Slashing target prices but maintaining ratings

Despite rolling over our estimates to 2023E, we slash our target prices by up to 35%, in light of higher cap rates as well as lower profit forecasts. See pages 8-9 for changes to our estimates. Key upside and downside risks: COVID, POGOs, interest rates, and REIT listings.

 
Wilcon Depot - Solid 2Q22 results as margin expansion continues
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Margin expansion drives 2Q22 net profit

WLCON’s 2Q22 net profit jumped 56% YoY to P1bn, driving 1H22 net profit +49% YoY to P1.86bn. The YTD figure is tracking ahead of Regis and consensus FY22E forecasts. The profit outperformance was largely driven by better-than[1]expected margins even as net sales (+19% YoY) were broadly in line. Margins have been expanding sequentially since 1Q21 despite supply chain pressures, highlighting the strength of WLCON’s scale.

Operating leverage, pricing power drive 2Q22 margin expansion

Gross margins expanded 200bps YoY and 60bps QoQ. While this was partly attributed to product mix and the higher contribution on in-house brands (51% vs 49% in 2Q21), it was more due to reduced competition, as smaller competitors were (and have been) unable to keep up with demand. This has allowed WLCON to adjust prices and pass on the higher cost of merchandise. Moreover, operating leverage from the recovery in store sales further drove operating margins to expand another >100bps YoY on top of the GPM expansion.

Same-store sales already above pre-pandemic levels

2Q22 SSSG was +15%, accelerating from the +9% clip in 1Q22. While the YoY growth figure may have been flattered by a low base (lockdown in Apr 2021), SSS levels are already comfortably above (~10%) pre-pandemic levels.

Top line trends remain healthy but margin expansion could taper off

The top line growth momentum is expected to be sustained as the economy’s reopening continues, though YoY growth trends may taper as the base normalizes. Margins are expected to remain healthy even as supply chain pressures persist, but we believe that gross margins could stabilize at current levels over the near term as WLCON’s average inventory cost increases. Longer term margin expansion will be driven by the continued increase in the contribution of its in-house brands, which management is targeting to reach ~52% in the near term and ~55% longer term.

Cemex PH refinances debt with BDO

Cemex (CHP PM) signed a facility agreement with BDO for a 5-year senior unsecured term loan for a principal amount of P6.69bn. The proceeds will be used to repay P6.68bn of outstanding debt with BDO. Prior to this exercise, CHP had maturing loans worth P3.4bn/P4.5bn due in 2022/2023. The refinancing exercise has pushed out maturities to 2024. Approx. P2.3bn from the 2017 loan facility will be due in 2024. The principal payments on the new P6.69bn loan will start accruing in November 2024, with the final maturity due in Aug 2027. The interest on the new loan will accrue at a floating rate (vs. 70% fixed: 30% floating previously). Regis estimates CHP’s cost of debt under the original loan facility was 5.5%. As at end-March, CHP gross debt and cash stood at P10.9bn and P4.6bn, respectively. It still needs to fund approx. P13bn of capex over the next 3 years to complete its 1.5mn MT clinker expansion, due to be completed in March 2024.

Marcos vetoes act expanding Davao Light Franchise Area

President Marcos Jr. has vetoed House Bill (HB 10554 or An Act Expanding the Franchise Area of Davao Light and Power Company Inc (DLPC). According to PHILRECA, a letter addressed to the Senate and House of Representatives stated the President recognizes the intent to improve access to electricity but “constrained to veto the bill due to susceptibility of the proposed expansion of the franchise area of DLPC to legal and/or constitutional challenge due to the apparent overlap and possible infringement into the subsisting franchise, permits and contracts previously granted to North Davao Electric Cooperative Inc. (NORDECO)”. DLPC is the third largest privately owned electric utility in the Philippines and is owned by Aboitiz Power (AP PM). It accounts for 7% of AP’s NAV.

Colliers: 2Q22 office rents fall; residential prices stay flat

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy rose to 17.7% in 2Q22, from 17.3% in 1Q22. The 2Q22 net office take-up was 45,100 sqm (vs. 26,400sqm in 1Q22). As a result, it estimates that office rental rates fell c. 3% QoQ. Office rental rates are 15%-40% lower than pre-COVID levels. Colliers is forecasting 2022E office vacancy to stay at 18% and office rental rates to fall 7% for the full-year. These are comparable to REGIS forecasts: 19% office vacancy, rental rates -5% in 2022E.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:

55% of Filinvest Land’s NAV – FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY22E P/B, and 7.2X FY22E P/E

41% of Megaworld’s NAV – MEG is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/E, and 5.2X FY22E P/E

30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV – RLC is trading at 54% discount to NAV, 0.7X FY22E P/B, and 11.8X FY22E P/E

Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums inched up 1% QoQ

Metro Manila condominium prices are generally 10%-25% lower than pre-COVID levels

Colliers expects Metro Manila condominium prices to rise 3% in 2022.

ACEN divests shares in coal plant to SPV

ACEN Corp is divesting its shares in the remaining coal fired plant in its portfolio in a deal valued at P3.7bn. The shares will be acquired by ETM Philippines Holdings, Inc. (EPHI) and The Insular Life Assurance Co., Ltd. (InLife). ACEN described EPHI as a special purpose vehicle that allows financial investors to invest in energy transition by accelerating the retirement of coal-fired power plants and to fund the development of new clean energy. It added that the transaction will serve as a pioneering local energy transition financing, taking off from the principles of the energy transition mechanism piloted by the Asian Development Bank. ACEN said it had approved the provision of a bridge financing to EPHI to facilitate the latters’ investment in SLTEC. SLTEC (244MW, 100% owned) is the single largest NAV component (14%) of ACEN at earnings driver (25%). It accounts for 6% of the known portfolio (3,800MW attributable). ACEN trades at 44x PER and 22.2x attributable EV/EBITDA for FY22E.

AP 2Q22 core income jumps 111%QoQ, 59%YoY to P6.2b buoyed by fresh contributions from GNPD.

AP 2Q22 core income hit P6.2bn (+ 59%YoY, +111%QoQ), on fresh contributions from GNPD Units 1 & 2, higher availability of GMEC and higher water inflows. Including the impact of gains on hedging and appreciation of the USD, net income for 1Q22 amounted to P7.1bn (+79%YoY, +146%QoQ). As of the 1H22, AP’s core net income was P9.2bn, down 9%YoY. Although this is just 39% of our FY22E, we think that it could still be on track to hit our FY estimate. Generation and RES EBITDA grew 13%YoY to P23.1bn driven by fresh contributions from GNPD. The capacity sold in 1H increased by 5% to 3,785 MW. Energy sold grew 17% to 13,762GWh in 1. The 1H figure implies energy sold in 2Q jumped 36%YoY and 27%QoQ. The Distribution segment recorded an EBITDA of P3.0bn in 1H22, down 27%YoY, dragged by delayed pass through of higher generation charges. Meanwhile, energy sales increased 1% to 2,778GWh in 1H. The 1H figure implies energy volumes recovered to 1,480GWh in 2Q (+14%QoQ, +3%YoY) after a soft 1Q (Typhoon Odette).

ICTSI to take majority stake in Indonesian bulk port

ICTSI is acquiring a 66.7% stake in PT East Java Devt for US$46.5mn. PT East Java Dev’t is the owner of a multi-purpose port in Lamongan Regency, East Java, Indonesia, which has 47 remaining years in its concession. The port is a brownfield and has a capacity of 5mn MT annually. Meanwhile, the shares will be acquired from Indo Port Hldgs and Eastlog Hldg. Pte. Management believes the acquisition will be immediately earnings accretive. The port also has the potential to be a container terminal in the future. As at end-March 2022, ICTSI’s gross debt (incl. perps, concession liab, leases) and cash stood at US$5.1bn and US$602mn, respectively.

BSP Governor rules out another off-cycle, 75bps rate hike

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla guided that any further changes to key policy rates will not be made off-cycle again. Further, he signaled a potential 25-50bp rate hike on Aug 18. “If you are to bet on four numbers -- zero, 25, 50 and 75 -- you can rule out the lowest and the highest”. Medalla said the decline in oil prices to less than USD100 per barrel “signals that there is less need for rate hikes.”. The overnight reverse repurchase rate is now at 3.25%. The policy rate has been increased by 150bps since the start of the year (vs YTD Fed: +150bps). The current policy rate remains below pre-pandemic levels (4%).

Budget deficit widens in June

The government’s budget deficit widened YoY, MoM to P216bn in June. Tax revenues jumped 17% YoY but dropped 12% MoM, owing to strong tax collections from the Bureau of Customs; BIR collections lagged. Non-tax revenues were lifted by higher dividend remittance and interest income from NG deposits. Expenditures rose 28% YoY, 12% MoM due to higher capital outlay disbursements for public works and transport projects, as well as the release of P10bn coco levy fund. This brings 1H22 deficit to P674bn, 6% YoY lower 6. While revenue collections are in line with the government’s target, expenditures are tracking behind due to slower-than-expected capex during the election ban and the timing of release of LGU funds to July. The government is targeting deficit of P1.65tn in FY22, equivalent to 7.6% of GDP (vs. 2021 deficit of P1.67tn, 8.6% of GDP).
