REGIS REPORT

13 May 2022

It was a rough week for global markets, buckling under the weight of inflation, tightening, geopolitical risk, COVID lockdowns in China. The Philippines dropped alongside, with a sharp 5.6% decline for the week.

Reflecting heightened risk aversion, foreigners were heavy sellers this week with US$79m (net) flowing out. MSCI announced multiple downweights as well which added to the pressure. Biggest outflows were seen across big caps Ayala Corp, Ayala Land, SM, and SM Prime.

The Philippines held Presidential elections help on May 9.  Based on unofficial results from the Commission on Elections, Ferdinand R Marcos, Jr (aka “Bongbong” or “BBM”) will be the 17th President of the Philippines in a landslide victory.

GDP growth in 1Q came in at +8.3% , falling within Regis expectations ( and ahead of Street). The 1Q showing compares with a 3.8% contraction the prior year and +7.8% expansion in 4Q. 1Q22 economic output is already above 1Q19’s. The rebound in growth was broad-based across most sectors. Net exports remained a drag. The BSP Governor has indicated that the BSP could raise rates next month if the GDP print showed recovery was underway.

The market’s decline was broad based, with only three of 30 index names clinging on to gains this week.  Monde (+7.3% WoW) gained, after an upweight in today’s PSEI rebal. The stock gained despite cost inflation weighing on 1Q results.

Other earnings misses came from AC Energy (-18.0% WoW) – the top decliner this week – as it had to purchase expensive replacement power due to prolonged outages at its coal plant.

Other earnings disappointments came from Ayala Land (-15.4% WoW) as cancellations dampened residential revenue. Robinsons Land (-7.7% WoW ) likewise saw sales cancellations muting residential revenue. Cebu Air (-0.4% WoW) meantime,  saw losses widen due to the lockdown in January.

Nevertheless, the recovery story continued to play out as evidenced by Jollibee’s (-3.4% WoW) 24% YoY jump in revenues.  SSSG (consolidated) of +16.5% driven mostly by the Philippine operations (+22.9%) and CBTL (+23.3%).  Earnings were ahead of expectations. Robinsons Retail’s (-0.9% WoW) operating profit jumped 37% YoY as SSSG improved to +5% in 1Q. Most formats have recovered to pre-pandemic levels. 

SM Prime’s (-0.3% WoW) Philippine mall revenue recovered 40%, and residential sales take-up is trending well above 2019 levels. Parent SM Investment’s (-6.7% WoW) net income for 1Q expanded 27%, already surpassing pre-pandemic level (1Q19).

Meanwhile, Puregold’s (-5.8% WoW) net sales were flattish (+2% YoY) during the quarter as SSS levels continued to normalise from the high base.

Semirara (-0.2% WoW) reported an exceptional P15bn in profits in 1Q22, up 552% YoY, almost approximating its full year profit last year, as the rise in global thermal coal prices lifted profits.

Bloomberry (+1.4% WoW) announced it was investing in the casino properties of the Udenna group.  Neither the transaction value nor the exact stake to be taken was disclosed.  However, we expect BLOOM to take at least majority control of the Udenna gaming assets, which include a beachfront casino being built in Cebu as well as a license for a casino in Clark, Pampanga. BLOOM’s 1Q22 profit of P687mn marks a return to profitability after seven quarters of pandemic-related losses.

Globe Telecom - 1Q: Mobile bouncing back; positive surprise from Mynt
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Headline profit lifted by data center sale, core income ahead of expectations

Globe reported 1Q22 net income of P13.7bn, which includes a chunky P10.5bn gain from the partial sale of its data center operation. Stripping that and other exceptional items out, core income was P5.1bn, which we estimate was up +3% YoY and trending above our FY22 estimate. The main driver of the positive variance was the contribution of Mynt.

Broadband disappoints

Much of Globe’s broadband subscriber base is still accounted for by fixed wireless subscribers so the erosion of that segment led to another quarter of revenue decline (-4%). Management is guiding for fixed wireless subscribers to drop further to 1.3m by year, which implies another 60% will be purged. On the positive side, Globe added 65k wired broadband subscribers in the quarter, breaking above 1m subs.

Clawing back mobile revenue share

Mobile revenue was up a surprising +3% QoQ. There were base effects because of the impact of the super typhoon in 4Q but we found the QoQ hike a positive surprise. It stands in contrast to PLDT’s 5% contraction. As a result, we estimate Globe regained 2ppt of revenue market share.

Mynt starts the year strong

36%-owned Mynt, the holding company for e-wallet firm GCash, contributed income of P327m for the quarter vs a loss of over P200m the prior year. GCash now has 60m registered users and claims GTV of P500bn for March (vs ~P310bn/month in FY21). It expanded its suite of credit products with the introduction of BNPL last Dec and is about to launch new services with BPI.

Lifting revenue guidance

Management is now guiding for “mid single digit” growth in revenue from “low single digit”. Capex guidance of P89bn (>55% of revenue) was unchanged.

 
Robinsons Retail - Operating leverage drives 1Q22 performance
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Operating leverage drives 1Q22 net profit +25% as SSS recovery continues

RRHI’s 1Q22 net profit jumped 25% YoY to P1.2bn, broadly in line with Regis estimates but tracking ahead of consensus expectations. Net sales rose 11% YoY, trending mildly behind our FY estimate, but we expect growth to accelerate in the succeeding quarters. The impact of operating leverage from the SSS recovery led to a 37% YoY jump in operating profit.

SSS recovery continues in 1Q22 despite January surge

Consolidated SSSG for the quarter improved to +5%. January SSS was dragged by the Omicron surge, but management noted that it quickly rebounded in Feb[1]Mar. Non-essential retail formats department stores (+22%) and specialty stores (+21%) saw strong SSSG, albeit coming from a low base. Meanwhile, SSSG for drug stores (+15%) was lifted by high demand during the January surge. By our estimate, most of RRHI’s formats have recovered to pre-pandemic SSS levels as of 1Q, except for department stores and convenience stores.

Operating profit jumps 37% as non-essentials recover

Operating profit grew sharply during the quarter, led by the improvement in profitability of the non-essential formats – particularly department stores, convenience stores, and specialty stores –which posted losses or just broke even in the same period last year. The drug store segment’s EBITDA margin expanded 180bps YoY as margins for Rose Pharmacy continued to improve.

SSS recovery to continue; P2/sh dividend declared

Management maintained its SSSG guidance of 5-10% for the year (Regis FY22E: +10%) given the easing of mobility restrictions and impact of inflation. SSSG is reportedly tracking mid-teens also partly due to the low base last year. The continued improvement in SSS should allow RRHI to reap the benefits of operating leverage in the coming quarters. The company also declared a P2/sh cash dividend, equivalent to c68% of FY21 earnings and a 3.7% yield as of last closing price.

 
SM Prime - Robust 1Q22 mall revenue and residential sales
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Strong mall revenue and residential sales take-up to start the year

SM Prime’s 1Q22 net profit rose 15% to P7.4bn. While this appears ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, we believe investors should focus on operating profit, which rose 17% (to P10.1bn) and which we consider in line with REGIS and Street estimates. Domestic mall revenue is trending ahead of our expectations but this was offset by weak residential revenue (likely due to slow construction). Two items caught our attention in SM Prime’s 1Q22 results press release. 1) Mall revenue recovered remarkably despite an omicron-related lockdown in January. 2) Residential reservation sales are well above 2019 levels.

Philippine mall revenue at 60% of 1Q19, should improve further

Philippine mall revenue jumped 40% YoY to P8.2bn, already 60% of the 1Q19 figure. We consider this a strong performance since mobility restrictions were especially tight in January due to an omicron-related COVID surge. We expected 1Q22 same-mall revenue to achieve only c. 40% of the 2019 average and then improve to c. 80% by 4Q22 (averaging at c. 60% for FY22E).

During our REGIS Roundtable Series, SMPH President Jeffrey Lim said that weekday tenant sales (in March/April) were already 80%-85% of pre-COVID levels. While mall rent was only 60%-65% of pre-pandemic levels, this should improve over time as the company reduces rental concessions.

Residential sales take-up also trending ahead

Reservation sales climbed 38% QoQ to P31.1bn. While the figure is down 4% YoY due to a high base, it is a robust performance as well. It is 38% higher than the 2019 average quarterly presales of P22.5bn (average of P24bn from 1Q19-4Q21). SMPH is trading at 27% discount to NAV and 2.9X FY22E P/B.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - All-time high coal production, ASP boost 1Q22 profit
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Record 1Q22 profit of P14.85bn – Ahead of estimates

1Q22 profit (ex-FX) surged to a record high of P14.85bn, buoyed by a sharp jump in coal production volumes, as well as unprecedented global thermal coal prices. The 1Q figure almost matched the company’s full-year 2021 profit and is tracking well ahead of Regis and street forecasts. While global coal prices and power prices are higher QoQ so far in 2Q, lower coal production and normalized coal mining costs would mean lower profits in succeeding quarters.

Coal mining benefits from low strip ratio, record global coal prices in 1Q

The coal mining unit posted its highest quarterly profit level, as it benefitted from (a) elevated global thermal coal prices, (b) higher production, and (c) lower cash costs. It produced and sold 7mn/5mn MT in 1Q but management is maintaining its FY22 target of 15mn MT. There was also reduced stripping, with the strip ratio at 5.6 (vs. full-year guidance of 10), leading to abnormally low cash costs. Moreover, coal ASPs surged to P5,100/MT, in line with global prices. Management indicated some of its clients are already locking in prices for the rest of the year, given uncertainties in the global coal market.

Power profits up 98% YoY on better prices

Its power unit saw profits surge, as power ASPs rose 51% YoY. The company was able to take advantage of power supply tightness, selling c. 60% of its power generation output to the spot market. Management noted that power spot prices so far in April/May are higher than the previous quarter. Meanwhile, the rehab of Unit 2 was again pushed back to August from its initial guidance of 2Q22.

Wait-and-see mode on expansion plans; More divs likely in 2H

The company’s plans to (1) enter the cement market, (2) acquire renewable power assets, (3) acquire new mining concessions remain on hold, as it awaits clarity on regulatory policy under the new administration. Given that there is no immediate need for cash (est. at P12bn, after div payment), management hinted about the possibility of declaring another special dividend after the rainy season.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp - 1Q22: Opex savings cushion input cost pressure
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q22 net profit boosted by one-offs; operating profit mildly behind estimates

JFC’s headline net profit in 1Q22 was P2.3bn. After accounting for coupons to perps and dividends to prefs, we estimate net profit to common was P2bn, a turnaround from the net loss in 1Q21. The figure appears to be tracking ahead of Regis and consensus FY22 expectations, but the outperformance was largely driven by a P1.8bn one-off gain relating to the transfer of land assets to CentralHub, a subsidiary of Double Dragon (DD PM). Revenues grew 24% YoY to P42.9bn, which is within expectations. Operating profit jumped 34% YoY but margins are trending mildly behind our FY22E.

SSSG jumps 16.5% led by Philippines, CBTL

Group SSSG jumped 16.5% led by the Philippines and CBTL, both of which posted SSSG of +23%. The easing of dine-in and mobility restrictions across these markets (ex-US for CBTL) was the main driver of SSSG. On the flipside, a new round of lockdowns in China led to a 9% SSS decline there during the quarter. We estimate that the Philippine revenue is now tracking >90% of pre-pandemic on a same-store basis (in line with Regis expectations).

Opex savings, operating leverage cushion higher input cost

Gross margin contracted by 100bps YoY and >200bps QoQ during 1Q22 due to input cost pressures. Despite this, operating leverage from improved sales and proportionately lower opex kept operating margins afloat. In the Philippines, JFC raised prices by ~1% during the quarter, bringing the cumulative price increase since 1Q21 to just under +4%. We understand that another round of price adjustments was implemented in April but given the heightened prices of key inputs (beef, chicken, oils) and cost of freight, we reckon that further price hikes could follow. Ayala Land - 1Q22: Strong rental segments not enough to offset weak residential business
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Strong mall and office; weak residential segment

Ayala Land’s headline profit grew 14% YoY to P3.2bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Stripping out the sale of Qualimed and tax asset write[1]downs from the CREATE law from 1Q21 numbers, we estimate that net profit would have increased c. 35%. While the mall and office segments outperformed our expectations, they could not compensate for the weak residential business.

Residential revenue falls, partly due to cancellations

Despite the strong sales of commercial and industrial lots, total development revenue fell 2% YoY (down 35% QoQ). Management said there were cancellations in an Ayala Land Premier condominium project (though figures were not provided). Buyers were not in financial distress but chose to cancel their purchases when Ayala Land made changes to the project. While this might be project-specific (and thus an infrequent item), the magnitude of the disappointment leads us to believe that there might have been cancellations in some of the mid-to-low end projects as well.

Reservation sales also disappoint

Reservation sales fell 15% YoY to P24.1bn, 66% of the 2019 quarterly average. While this is c. 5% better than the 2021 average, we were expecting a stronger recovery. On a positive note, sales improved every month in 1Q22 (see table on right hand side).

Mall revenue jumps as concessions are reduced

Mall revenue jumped 49% YoY to P2.9bn, already 57% of the 1Q19 figure. Furthermore, thanks to the reduction in rental concessions, revenue was flat QoQ despite the drop in tenant sales (brought about by a seasonally strong 4Q and the hard lockdown in January). Revenue should continue to improve as the company reduces rental concessions further.

1Q22 profit drops 16% YoY – Behind estimates

Robinsons Land - Residential cancellations weigh on 1Q22 profit but guidance is more optimistic
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential sales cancellations hamper 1Q22 results

1Q22 headline net profit fell 40% YoY to P1.74bn while operating profit fell 21% YoY. The underperformance was due to weak Philippine residential revenue (brought about by sales cancellations). Nevertheless, malls are recovering nicely and the office segment is trending ahead of our estimates. Moreover, to our surprise, management is guiding for a 40% jump in net presales this year. Although the 1Q22 results were disappointing, the headline YoY drop is exaggerated due to 1) tax gains from the CREATE law in 1Q21 and 2) lumpy China residential revenue in 1Q21.

Guiding for a 40% jump in FY22E net presales

Despite 1Q22 net presales being flat YoY and QoQ at P2.9bn, management is guiding for a 40% jump this year to P15bn. This is remarkable for a number of reasons: 1) it’s the first time that management has given presales guidance, 2) it’s c. 20% higher than our forecast of P12.7bn, and 3) it is also 75% of the 2019 figure, which was boosted by sales to foreigners (c. 40% of 2019 sales).

Mall revenue grows 19%; c. 50% of 2019 level; should rise further

Mall revenue grew 19% YoY (and QoQ) to P2.7bn, about 50% of the 2019 average and broadly in line with our forecast. The mall business should improve further since 1Q22 was affected by a tight lockdown in January and foot traffic has been improving, from 65% (of pre-COVID levels) in December to 75% in March (c. 85% in April). Furthermore, the company plans to continue lowering rental concessions.

Office tenants not reducing footprint

Office revenue grew 12% YoY (trending ahead) as leasable area grew by the same amount. Furthermore, the company’s tenants have not signaled any intention to reduce their office footprint due to work-from-home/hybrid policies.

Monde Nissin - 1Q22: Cost inflation continues to weigh on margins
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q22 core net profit -13% YoY; Quorn underperforms

MONDE reported 1Q22 core net profit of P2.1bn, down 13% YoY, tracking behind Regis but broadly in line with consensus expectations. The underperformance was largely due to Quorn, which saw a 43% YoY drop in EBITDA. Meanwhile, results of the APAC BFB segment are tracking within expectations even as input cost pressure continued to weigh on margins.

APAC BFB: Price increases provide some QoQ margin relief

APAC BFB segment sales rose 9% YoY largely driven by the 5% (cumulative) increase in pricing. The price increases stabilized gross margin on a sequential basis but were not enough to fully mitigate the impact of higher wheat and palm oil prices vs 1Q21. Volumes for key categories (e.g. instant noodles, biscuits) were healthy, particularly for biscuits, which has recovered to pre-pandemic levels. MONDE maintained market leadership in instant noodles and biscuits, but competition led to some market share erosion vs year-ago levels.

Quorn: Margins crimped by cost inflation, brand-building investments

In local currency terms, Quorn’s sales during the quarter were flattish YoY as UK retail sales remained challenged. The foodservice segment – now ~15% of Quorn’s sales – surged 124% YoY, cushioning the decline in retail. EBITDA margin contracted over 400bps YoY during the quarter due to input cost inflation and further investments in brand building. Quorn likewise raised prices by ~5% during the quarter, but it wasn’t enough to fully offset the input cost pressure.

Margin pressure likely to persist; price hikes forthcoming

Further price adjustments have been planned across both key segments given the continued surge in prices of key inputs (e.g. wheat and palm oil). For the APAC BFB segment alone, the company raised prices by ~3% in January, and another ~6% in April, which should provide some margin relief on a sequential basis. MONDE has locked in inventory for most of 2H22 at prices below spot, but the price hikes already implemented may not be the last.

Semirara’s 1Q22 profit surges to record P15bn

In a press release, Semirara reported its 1Q22 profit stood at P15bn, up 552% YoY and the highest quarterly figure on record. 1Q22 profit alone almost reached its full-year 2021 profit of P16bn. This is tracking way ahead of Regis (P23.8bn) and street full-year estimates (P18.7bn). Regis reckons the outperformance largely came from the coal mining unit’s higher ASPs and volumes. Management cautioned against annualizing 1Q due to market volatility and unfavorable weather conditions typically in 2H and also cited record-high coal production and shipments, soaring global thermal coal prices, and higher spot electricity sales at elevated prices. On coal mining, the company sold 5.1mn MT (104% QoQ, 31% YoY) in 1Q21 at an average price of P5,125/MT (15% QoQ/180% YoY). Both are tracking ahead of our estimates on full-year volumes (15.2mn MT) and ASP (P3,190/MT). Note that Newcastle thermal coal prices rose 44% QoQ / 203% YoY in 1Q22 to US$265/MT. Its power unit sold 908 GWH (+14% YoY), bulk of which was to the spot market (57%). Power ASPs improved by 17% QoQ / 50% YoY to P5.29/kwh, tracking below forecast.

Megaworld sees better office demand in 1Q22

During the MREIT briefing, management said that office demand improved in 1Q22. MREIT’s office occupancy was maintained at 96% but new inquiries suggest that new contracts will be signed later this year. Megaworld signed new office contracts in 1Q22. LRMC files arbitration case against Republic of the Philippines

Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC) filed a Request for Arbitration with the International Chamber of Commerce in respect of its disputes with the Republic of the Philippines. The request pertains to the adjustment of the approved fare for the years 2016, 2018 and 2020 and LRMC’s claims for compensation for:

(1) the difference between the stipulated fare and the approved fare based on the schedule provided in the concession agreement

(2) the losses, costs and expenses incurred by LRMC for the government’s failure to deliver the required number of light rail vehicles that meet the stipulated technical requirements and the structural defects on the existing LRT 1 system.

 

As of March 31, 2022, LRMC’s monetary claims for the abovementioned matters amounted to approximately P2.67bn, before interest, in addition to its application for the implementation of the long-overdue fare adjustments for 2016, 2018, and 2020. LRMC holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 railroad. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), Macquarie Infra (10%). MPI is trading at a 48% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.34/sh. AC is trading at a 33% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,083/sh.

DMCI profit surges to all-time high in 1Q22

DMCI (DMC PM) reported a core profit of P11.2bn in 1Q22, up 177% YoY. This accounts for 58% of street forecasts and about 66% of its full-year 2021 profit. Apart from Semirara’s outperformance, results were also better than expected for its property, nickel mining, and off-grid power units. The property unit’s profit dropped 7% YoY, as revenues dropped on lower downpayment recognition and higher sales cancellations. The good news is that reservation sales bounced back strongly (+86% QoQ) to P8.1bn (vs. c. P9bn per quarter pre-pandemic). Its construction unit also recorded a 7% YoY drop in profit due to high base effects. While revenues rose, gross margins are still dropping but this was offset by the absence of vaccine-related costs. The order book dropped further to P46.7bn on the absence of new contracts. Its nickel mining unit’s profit rose 20% YoY on higher shipments and higher ASPs. Its off-grid power unit benefitted from higher electricity demand.

ACEN’s 1Q22 profit falls 68% to P405mn - behind

ACEN’s 1Q22 profit fell 68% YoY to P405mn, tracking behind Regis (P7.416bn) and consensus (P8.255bn) estimates for FY22E. Management attributed the decline to a) higher cost of purchased power from spot during SLTEC outage, b) a one-time buyout expense related to a customer contract and c) curtailment in Visayas due to a transmission line damage caused by Typhoon Odette. Management estimates that these items collectively depressed profit by around P1.6bn. Excluding these, profit would have been 23% higher YoY.

 

Extended outages at SLTEC resulted in much lower availability for the period (40%), which prompted expensive replacement power from WESM to supply its contracts. The company estimates a negative impact of about P550mn to net income as result. It also bought out a fixed-price customer contract that had an upsizing option to reduce risk moving forward, which resulted in a one-time expense of P450mn. The balance of c.P150mn (of P1.6bn) relates to profits that could have been booked without the curtailment in Visayas.

Largely shielded from coal spikes. Management reiterated that they have “physical” fixed price, long-term agreements with a coal supplier that shields them from spikes, save for some 20MW worth that sources coal from a third party supplier (partly hedged, not long-term). According to management, the bigger impact/risk would be from when plants are not running and they have to buy replacement power.

Back to net-buyer by 3Q. As it stands, ACEN is currently a net seller to the spot market, with a baseload supply of about 450MW matching about the same in peak demand from customers (Meralco and RES customers). It has an estimated 266MW (attributable) of solar selling to the spot market plus 250MW of diesel elected for ancillary. However, planned outages in 3Q22 would return ACEN to a net buyer status for at least a month.

 

AC’s property dividend of ACEN shares would reduce AC’s stake in ACEN to 57.8% from 62.5% and improve public float to at least 21%. This takes into account the shares (1.332bn) still to be issued to UPC for their stakes in Australia, etc which will increase total outstanding shares to 39,647.84mn. The exercise will allow ACEN to meet the minimum float required to be a component of the PSEi.

Philippine wholesale prices highest in 10 years

According to the PSA data, the general wholesale price index (GWPI) in the Philippines jumped to 7.6% in March, faster than the 5.6% print in February and 2.8% in March 2021. This is the highest recorded GWPI in over 10 years since the 7.4% print in November 2011. The index of mineral fuels, lubricants and related materials registered the highest increase at 45.3% in March from 37.7% in February. This was followed by the index of crude materials (29.2% from 26.7% in February) and food (8.2% from 4.9% in February). The GWPI is used to monitor the wholesale trade sector and serves as a basis for price adjustments in business contracts and projects.

End-April 2022 GIR settles at US$106.76bn

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR), based on preliminary data, settled at US$106.76bn in end-April. This is down from end-March level of US$107.31bn. This also reflects the government’s payments of foreign currency debt and the decrease in the price of gold. This is equivalent to 9.4 months’ worth of imports. The BSP projects the GIR to reach US$108bn by the end of 2022.

Trade deficit expands further in March

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit widened further to US$5bn in March. This is up from a revised US$4.2bn deficit in Feb 2022 and US$2.8bn deficit in March 2021. The trade deficit was due to a steady growth in imports (+28% YoY) and a deceleration of export growth (+6% YoY from +16% YoY in Feb 2022). This brought 1Q22 trade deficit to US$13.89bn.

GDP expands by 8.3% in 1Q22 on strong domestic demand recovery

GDP grew +8.3% YoY in 1Q22, reversing the 3.8% decline in 1Q21 and was faster than 4Q21 (+7.8%). This was above consensus expectations (+6.8%) but broadly in line with Regis expectations (+8.5%). It also surpassed pre-pandemic GDP level. Mobility improved after the government eased restrictions in February. Growth was broad-based as most sectors rebounded strongly, except for agriculture (due to swine flu). On the demand side, consumption and capital formation grew robustly at +10%/ 20%, respectively, reversing last year’s decline. Gov’t spending slowed, with public construction contracting by 5% YoY due to the election ban and the Mandanas ruling. Negative net exports expanded +32%, reversing last year’s 4.8% drop. The government is maintaining its +7-9% GDP growth target for the year. Resumption of face-to-face classes is still missing. Regis is forecast 2022 GDP growth of +6%.
