REGIS REPORT

11 February 2022

The market pulled back 2.5% this week, in the wake of its 2.8% advance in the prior week.  Gains earlier in the week had taken the market above the 7500 level – the highest since the pandemic began. Profit taking soon ensued however, even as US inflation surged and suggesting urgency for rates to rise. Losers overwhelmed gainers, with drops in 23 out of 30  index stocks.

The PSEi rebal at today’s close added to the drop.  Except for Converge (+3.4% WoW) which saw an upweight, all  index names saw downweights to make room for inclusions Monde (+13.1% WoW) and Emperador (-7.4% WoW). Index deletions Bloomberry (-3.6% WoW) and Robinsons Retail (-1.4% WoW) fell.

On the ground, the rapid decline in new COVID cases in the country has boosted reopening optimism, with NCR and surrounding areas gearing up for another round of easing. Already, Google mobility data as shown a sharp rebound by early February and this data should see a further rise on further lowering of Alert Levels.

Foreigners were buyers for US$111m this week with biggest inflows seen in PSEi rebalancing additions Emperador and Monde. Most of the inflows were seen on Friday amid the rebalancing as facilitation books were likely more active than usual. Big inflows were also seen in Ayala Land (+0.1% WoW), BPI (-1.7% WoW), and BDO (-1.2% WoW) while decent outflows were in Globe (-15.4% WoW) and RRHI.

Globe fell markedly this week after a 4Q/FY21 miss, with the company blaming the drop in broadband revenue to Typhoon Odette. Capex also surprised  negatively, hitting P92.8bn or 61% of service revenue.  The capex guidance for FY22 is only slightly lower at P89bn.  Some of the e-wallet euphoria that led to the surge in Globe’s share price last year has also waned.

Other large declines were in previous strong performers AC Energy (-8.1% WoW) and Wilcon (-7.9% WoW).  Large index names AEV (-8.1% WoW) and JG Summit (-7.6% WoW) dropped as well.  Also closing the week down were Aboitiz Power (-4.7% WoW), SM (-4.6% WoW) and Robinsons Land (-3.0% WoW).

Meanwhile, apart from Converge, gains were seen in SM Prime (+4.6% WoW), ICTSI (+3.4% WoW), and Jollibee (+1.0% WoW).  Jollibee’s FY21 headline profit and EBIT came in ahead of Street expectations. Results were supported by a strong +22% jump in revenues for the 4Q.  Operating leverage as a result of the 2020's Business Transformation initiative has helped boost operating profit in 4Q.

POGOs looking to lease offices again

Leechiu Property Consultants said that it was close to signing an office lease contract for a POGO. That would be the first POGO transaction in two years. SMPH is trading at 26.6X FY22E P/E, 2.9X FY22E P/B and 10% discount to NAV.

UBP FY21 - NPAT +9% to P12.6bn; 4Q soft on provisions; digital transformation accelerates

UBP FY21 - NPAT +9% to P12.6bn; 4Q soft on provisions; digital transformation accelerates

Union Bank (UBP PM) reported headline NPAT of P12.6bn for 2021, up 9% YoY. The bank’s 4Q12 though was quite soft, at just P1.9bn, down 38% YoY as provisions jumped. The bank’s press release highlighted the advances in their digital initiatives, claiming they added 2.5mn new accounts in 2021, of which close to 1mn came from the digital-only account opening service. It wasn’t clear how this was directly affecting profitability, though the bank’s fee incomes were accelerating sharply in 3Q21/9M21. Regis expects this, and the Citi portfolio acquisition, to be the focus of the analyst briefing on 9 March at 4pm, together with the rest of the AEV group.

 

Some highlights:

Loans grew 6% YoY, accelerating from -4% YoY in 3Q.  Note, though that 4Q20 was a very low base (-14%). Net loans were still 8.5% lower than FY2019 levels. NIMs were flat QoQ, though the bank estimates FY margins were up 9bp YoY, largely on lower funding costs. No breakdown yet on Non-Interest Income. However, Regis expects bulk of the 14% YoY growth to have been driven by Fees, which were accelerating sharply, up 42.5% in 9M21 and up 78% YoY in 3Q21 alone.

 

Provision expense was down 31%YoY for the full-year. However, provisions jumped 82% YoY and 58% QoQ in 4Q21 alone. This seems disproportionate to the slight deterioration in headline NPL ratio of 5.0% (from 4.9% in 9M). Regis suspects the bank did some write-offs in 4Q21. The press release did not mention NPL cover, but the bank has kept the ratio in the high-60% range all year.

 

ROE for the full year came in at c12%, though this was dragged up by the huge trading gains in 1Q21. The bank remains highly reliant on its Treasury operations. As of 9M21, its loan book accounted for just 43% of total assets. This has resulted in extremely volatile quarterly profits/ROEs even pre-pandemic. Regis expects the market to keenly watch the bank’s digital efforts, the Citi acquisition, and the rights issue to determine the profit/ROE trajectory moving forward.

8990 Holdings to conduct follow-on offering of up to P26.6bn

8990 Holdings said that it would conduct its follow-on offering at a maximum price of P18.99/share. This is higher than the last price of P11.88/share. The deal will have up to 1.25bn existing HOUSE shares sold by major shareholders: TPG Rafter Holdings, Pasir Salak Investments (SPV of Khazanah Nasional Berhad), and IHoldings. This represents 23% of current shares outstanding. The deal also includes an overallotment option of 150mn shares (from IHoldings).

At the current HOUSE price of P11.88/share, the offering could be as large as P16.6bn. Up to 450mn new common shares will be issued to IHoldings. This assumes that the overallotment option was exercised. This would increase shares outstanding by 8%. At P11.88/share, 8990 Holdings could raise as much as P5.3bn. IHoldings is currently the largest shareholder of 8990 Holdings. The funds from the primary share issuance will likely be used to pay down debt. As of 3Q21, 8990 had net debt of P28.9bn.

 

 

Globe 4Q/FY21 initial comment

4Q income distorted by one-off gains and typhoon costs. Globe said Typhoon Odette, which had inflicted serious damage on various parts of central Philippines in Dec, caused P2.2bn worth of impact on the firm’s operations in 4Q. This came in the form of rebates given to broadband customers as well as network-related damage.  On the flip side, Globe recorded a lumpy gain in 4Q, which we suspect is attributable to the investment of Warburg Pincus into Mynt / GCash.

Service revenue was practically flat QoQ (+2% YoY). While mobile service revenue was slightly up QoQ (+1%), home broadband revenue dropped a surprising 11% QoQ. The drop was blamed on the typhoon, which led to lower top-ups for its fixed wireless service as well as rebates for others.

Broadband disappoints. After a strong pick-up in 3Q, broadband revenue, surprisingly, dropped 11% QoQ. We thought the period of the period of broadband underperformance was over but it doesn’t appear so. Globe added 58k wired broadband subscribers in 4Q, taking it to 965k subs. The company added a net 77k subs in 3Q.

Capex surprise. Another surprise was capex --- Globe spent a surprising P92.8bn (US$1.82b) in capex over 2021, higher than previous guidance (P76bn) and likely higher than PLDT (whose guidance is P88bn – P92bn). That figure implies Globe spent P27bn in 4Q alone. For 2022, Globe is guiding for P89bn in capex, a slight dip from last year.

Leverage ratios higher than they appear. Globe issued US$600m worth of perpetuals which are treated as equity for accounting purposes. The fact that they are treated as equity for accounting reasons does not change their effect on financial leverage from a common shareholders’ perspective. Treating them as debt, we estimate net debt to EBITDA is now at 2.7x and net gearing would now be 2.6x.

GCash’s GTV reaches P3.8trn for 2021. The value of transactions that went through GCash reached P3.8trn, which is more than 3x the level in 2020 (P1.2trn).

JFC FY21 headline net profit ahead of estimates at P5.9bn; operating profit in line at P6.3bn

JFC reported FY21 headline net profit of P5.9bn, coming in ahead of Regis (P3.7bn) and consensus (P3.1bn) expectations. Excluding coupons to perps and divs to prefs, estimated recurring net profit to common was P4.8bn.

The profit outperformance was largely driven by non-operating income and a lower effective tax rate; FY21 operating profit of P6.3bn is in line with Regis expectations. 

For 4Q21 alone, recurring net profit to common was P3bn. Revenues in 4Q21 jumped 22% YoY. SSSG in the Philippines was +24%, implying that SSS was tracking 80% of pre-Covid levels. 

4Q21 operating profit was P2.5bn, bringing margins to 5.6% (back to pre-Covid levels). The improvement was attributed to Cost savings from the 2020 Business Transformation Program, which allowed the Phils to generate similar operating profit vs 4Q19, a reduction in Smashburger’s losses (we estimate the 4Q21 loss was P100-120m vs P250m in 3Q21), and CBTL’s turn to profitability during the quarter.  

Colliers: residential prices have bottomed, office rents will fall further

During its 4Q21 briefing, Colliers said that prices of Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) fell 7% YoY in 4Q21 (down 1% QoQ). Metro Manila condominium prices are c. 15%-25% lower than 2019 levels. It forecasts prices to rise 2% in 2022. Colliers’ condominium price estimates are generally in line with Regis’ forecasts. 

Meanwhile, Metro Manila office rents fell 12% YoY (down 3% QoQ). Metro Manila office rental rates are c. 20%-30% below 2019 levels. Colliers expects rental rates to fall another 10% in 2022 as office vacancy rises. In contrast, Regis forecasts rental rates to stay approximately flat in 2022E as vacancy declines slightly.  Regis expects BPO office take-up to return to pre-COVID levels but Colliers likely expects work-from-home policies to curtail office demand.

Tonik gets $131mn fresh equity for expansion

Digital bank operator Tonik Financial Pte Ltd. has raised $131mn from a new equity funding round led by Japanese banking giant Mizuho Bank. Other significant new investors in the “series B” funding round included Prosus Ventures (formerly Naspers Ventures), the cofounder of DST Partners Rahul Mehta, as well as Singapore investment fund Sixteenth Street Capital and Indonesian family office Nuri Group.

The digibank’s previous venture capital investors – Sequoia India, Point72 Ventures, Insignia, iGlobe, Alpha JWC, Citius, Blauwpark and Kraft – also participated in the funding round.

Launched in March 2021, Tonik has set new bank growth records in the Philippines by reaching $20m and $100mn in consumer deposits within the first and eight months of operations.

PMFTC volumes -3%QoQ; overall industry softened in 4Q

Philip Morris International disclosed overnight that cigarette volumes in the Philippines (PMFTC) for 4Q21 was 8.7bn sticks, -9%YoY and -3%QoQ.

Industry volumes hit 14.2bn sticks (-6%YoY, -2%QoQ). Unit market shared dipped again to 61.1% from 61.8% in 3Q21 after appearing to have stabilized in 2Q/3Q

For FY21, PMFTC’s cigarette volumes hit 34.2bn sticks, down 18%YoY. This compares to a 11% decline for the industry.

The lower market share is largely due to lower volumes of Fortune, Jackpot and More, partly offset by Chesterfield.  

Regis’ PMFTC’s FY21 profit estimate is  P37.4bn (+12%YoY). 

LTG is trading at 4x FY22E PE and a 68% discount to market NAV.  Total dividends paid last year amounted to P1.08/share, exceeding Regis dividend estimate of P0.82/share for FY21. Assuming this level is sustained, dividend yield could be in the range of 10-11%. 

PEZA proposes to extend BPO WFH schemes until September 2022

PEZA proposed to extend work-from-home (WFH) schemes until 12 September 2022. This is still subject to the approval of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB)

Only 10% of BPO workers (in PEZA zones/buildings) are currently required to work onsite. This scheme is scheduled to end on 31 March 2022.

Should FIRB approve the new proposal, BPOs might delay their return to office as well as their decisions to sign up for new office space.

Gov’t infrastructure spending rises in November

Government infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) stood at P60bn in November. This is up 49% YoY but was slightly down (-1%) MoM. The YT-November overall infra spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P967bn, up 33% YoY. This implies November infra spend at P83bn, up 52% YoY and 8% MoM. The YT-November infra spend is ahead of the government’s FY21 target of P1.095tn (+26% YoY). For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn (+16% YoY).

Inflation slows to 3.0% in January

Headline inflation slowed down to 3.0% in January 2022 from 3.2% in December 2021 and 3.7% in January 2021. The Philippine Statistics Authority re-based the CPI series to 2018. Inflation settled at the midpoint of the government’s target range of 2-4%. The slowdown was due to lower electricity prices and LPG prices, as well as easing inflation for alcoholic beverages and tobacco. Restaurants and accommodation services inflation also moderated. Using the re-based series, the annual headline inflation for 2021 stood at 3.9% (vs 4.5% for the 2012-based CPI).

Pace of vaccinations falls further

As of February 4, the 7-day average vaccinations figure fell to 325,000/day from 474,000/day the previous week. The government plans to hold another major vaccination drive on Feb 10-11. The country administered about 2.5mn doses per day during the first vaccination drive (held on November 29 to December 1). The Philippines began the vaccination of children aged 5 to 11 on Feb 7. Around 55% of the Philippine population (59.8mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 217mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 128mn doses (as of February 4). The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22.

Metro Manila mayors ready to shift to Alert Level 1

Metro Manila mayors are ready to open more industries and sectors once restrictions are further eased. Metro Manila is under Alert Level 2 from Feb 1 to Feb 15 after declining case numbers; cases are expected to decline to 1k by month-end. New cases in NCR (7dma) are down to c. 1k from 2.6k the previous week. On Feb 8, NCR case count stood at 689. Under Alert Level 1, establishments and activities are allowed to be undertaken at full capacity. However, face-to-face classes for basic education level shall be subject to prior approval of the Office of the President.

Mobility recovers quickly, back to Nov '21 levels

Filipinos are back on the move. While the Covid-19 omicron surge drove people back into their homes in early January, the current sharp drop in Covid cases saw an equally sharp rebound in mobility. As of the last weekend, Google mobility numbers are back to where they were in November, right before the Christmas rush. Perhaps more importantly, the Jan/Feb mobility numbers are 10-20 percentage points higher than they were in Jan/Feb 2021.

This is consistent with Regis' “Glass still half full” view, that the economic reopening/recovery momentum is picking up nicely. Regis expects mobility data to improve further as Metro Manila mayors push for a further easing of restrictions down to Alert Level 1. While no timetable was given, Regis expects this in the coming weeks as Covid-19 cases plunge in Metro Manila.

Unemployment rate inches up to 6.6% in Dec

The unemployment rate inched up to 6.6% in Dec from 6.5% in the previous month. Labor force participation rate also improved to 65.1%.

According to NEDA Sec. Karl Kendrick Chua, some 3.7m new jobs were created, already above pre-pandemic levels. He also expects unemployment figures to slightly deteriorate in Jan 2022 due to the Omicron surge.

GIR falls slightly in January

The level of gross international reserves fell to US$108.5bn in January (from US$108.8bn in December). This is equivalent to 10.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and 8.6X short-term debt based on original maturity and 5.9X based on residual maturity.

The MoM reduction was due to payments of the national government’s foreign currency debt obligations and the lower price of gold.

NPLs drop sharply to 4.12% in December, PDs fall 50bps MoM

Asset quality improved significantly in December 2021. Total banking sector NPL ratio (ex-interbank) fell to 4.12%, down 36bps MoM, and the lowest since Feb’21. In nominal terms, NPLs fell to P454bn, down 5.7% MoM (about P27bn MoM) . Regis believes  there were chunky write-offs done in December, as loan loss reserves (LLR) also fell by P22bn MoM. Perhaps more encouraging was the even sharper drop in Past Due (PD) ratio to 4.66%, down 46bps MoM, with the peso value down 6.4% MoM to P531bn (-P36bn). Restructured Loan (RL) ratio was up slightly to 3.13% (vs 3.11% in Nov), with RLs up 3.3% MoM in peso terms.

NPL cover was flat MoM at 87.4% (vs 87.1% in Nov), and LLR ratio fell to 3.49%, down 30bps MoM, both due to our presumed write-offs in the month.

Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated again to end the year at +4.2% YoY, marking the 9th consecutive month of improving growth prints. Loans were up 2.7% MoM, as December is typically a strong month for corporate borrowing.

The asset quality gains are likely to continue into 2022 as the economic recovery takes hold. Falling provision expense will be a major earnings driver for banks this year.

Comelec’s First Division dismisses Marcos DQ cases for lack of merit

The Commission on Elections’ (Comelec) First Division has dismissed the consolidated disqualification cases against presidential candidate Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr for lack of merit.

The resolution covered the cases filed by martial law survivors through Bonifacio Ilagan, Akbayan party-list group, and the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos Commissioner Abubakar Mangelen.

The groups sought to disqualify Marcos because of his conviction for failing to file income tax returns from 1982-85. The First Division’s resolution read that perpetual disqualification by reason of failure to file income tax returns was not provided for under the original 1977 NIRC. 

Another disqualification case against Marcos is pending before the Comelec’s Second Division.

The latest presidential surveys show Marcos leading at 47-53%.
