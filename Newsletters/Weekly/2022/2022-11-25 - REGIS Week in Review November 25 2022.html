REGIS REPORT

25 November 2022

The PSEi ended the week strong, breaking above the 6600 levels for the first time since September. The index gained another +2.6%, marking the 6th straight week of gains mainly lifted by strong foreign buying.

Foreign flows have been consistently flowing into the market for a month now, seeing another US$41.8M this week – both from passive and active funds. On the passive end, buying is widespread across the board especially towards the close while active funds seem to reallocate into large caps such as SM, SMPH, AC, ALI, and BPI. Top bought names for the week were SM (US$11.7M), JFC (US$11.6M), BDO (US$10.9M), and MBT (US$10.5M).

Evidently, outperformers for the week were SM (+5.94% WoW), SMPH (+5.74% WoW), AC (+4.43% WoW), BPI (+4.72%). MBT (+8.7% WoW) also a top gainer for the week after lagging behind its peers the previous weeks. ADV likewise improved to US$107.1M (vs US$80M ADV at the start of the month), which could suggest renewed interest in the market and a risk-on appetite for investors.

MSCI names up for rebalancing next week all ended higher despite the deletions. Converge (+8.7% WoW) continued its sharp run due to strong foreign demand on attractive valuations coupled with possible short coverings. Metro Pacific and GT Capital likewise ended the week strong, up 5.0% and 5.8% respectively.

On the flipside, Meralco (-7.05% WoW) and SMC (-1.22% WoW) underperformed after the ERC denied its request for temporary relief. At present, SMC Global Power is asking the Court of Appeals to allow it to disregard its current fixed price contracts and charge higher rates.

On the macro front, dollar has been weakening as the peso is now trading at 56.7. The 10-yr BVAL also dropped -22.7bps to 7.28% (vs 7.5% last week) which could help renew appetite for equities. BSP Governor Medalla also ruled out the possibility of another jumbo rate hike earlier this week as he expects the Fed to slow the pace of tightening in December. Lastly, the Senate approved the budget bill for 2023 of P5.27tn (+5% higher than 2022).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

3Q22 Domestic passenger volumes drop 3% QoQ

The Civil Aeronautics Board reported domestic passenger volumes in 3Q22 reached 6.24mn, down by 3% QoQ but almost 6x higher YoY. Regis estimates domestic pax volumes in 3Q22 were at 85% of 2019 average (vs 87% in 2Q22). Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) market share dropped to 57% in 3Q22 from 60% in 2Q22 as Air Asia Philippines took market share in 3Q22. Management blamed the lean season for the 7% QoQ drop in its domestic pax volumes. In 9M22, domestic pax volumes stood at 15.93mn (vs. full-year 2019 pax volumes of 29.54mn).

PSE approves P3.2bn IPO of Villar-backed Premiere REIT

The Philippine Stock Exchange approved Premiere Island Power REIT Corp’s listing application. The offer period of its P3.2bn IPO is set on Nov 28-Dec 5 and pricing was set to Nov 23 (stock code: PREIT). Premiere REIT appears to be a firm leasing land to a solar farm, effectively a solar farm disguised as leasing company. The IPO involves the sale of up to 1.4bn common shares priced at P2/sh plus an option to sell another 210mn shares. The shares are entirely secondary. The proceeds would be used by sponsors to acquire and develop real estate that would be leased to affiliate PAVI Green Renewable Energy Inc. for its various projects. The initial portfolio will consist of land and power plant assets in Cebu and Siquijor, which have a total combined capacity of 21.2MW. The company is projecting a “base case” dividend payout of 6.9% in 2022 and 7.2% in 2023. This will be the 9th IPO for the year and the 7th publicly-listed firm of the Villar group.

SMC power unit files suit against ERC decision

SMC Global Power filed a petition for certiorari with the Court of Appeals to reverse an Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) decision denying its request for temporary relief. SMC Global Power also asked the appellate court to issue a Temporary Restraining Order so the ERC order will not be implemented. Last Sep 29, the ERC denied SMC’s petition to adjust its tariffs temporarily to allow it to recover fuel costs. According to ERC, the PSA provides no room for price adjustment and only a guaranteed supply of energy to Meralco consumers at a fixed price. SMC Global Power is effectively asking the Court of Appeals to allow it to disregard its current fixed price contracts and charge higher rates.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Gov’t raises P93bn from sale of T-bonds

The Bureau of Treasury partially awarded P27.597bn worth of original issue of 20-year T-bonds on offer during the last auction for November. Effectively bringing total borrowings for T-bonds to P93.3bn, about 67% of the P140bn program for the month. In the last auction, the 20-year T-bond fetched an average rate of 8.012%, 20bps above the 7.81% BVAL reference rate. The yield was lower than the 8.168% last week. The demand for the securities attracted P70.36bn, oversubscribing the auction by 2.01x.

BSP Governor rules out jumbo rate hikes

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla ruled out the possibility of another jumbo rate hike, as he expects the US Federal Reserve to slow the pace of tightening in December. If the Fed will hike by 50 bps, Mr. Medalla said the BSP cannot afford to keep rates unchanged. The Monetary Board will be meeting on December 15; Fed meeting on Dec 14. The US central bank raised rates by 375 bps since March while the BSP has raised rates by 300bps.

Senate approves 2023 budget bill

The Senate voted unanimously to approve the 2023 proposed budget of P5.27tn (+5% higher than the 2022 budget). Senators realigned P170mn worth of agencies’ intelligence funds. Outside of this, it appears that the bills passed in Senate and the Lower House are not too different from the Department of Budget and Management’s proposal. The ratified version of the reconciled bill be up for the President’s approval by mid-December or “before Christmas”.

163 BPO firms endorsed by PEZA to BOI

PEZA has endorsed 163 BPO firms for registration to the Board of Investments (BOI). The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) allowed PEZA-registered firms to transfer their registration to the Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have restrictions on work-from-home (WFH). There are about 1,300 BPO firms registered with PEZA. PEZA-registered firms have until 31 December 2022 to transfer their registration from PEZA to BOI. So far, it seems that only a minority of BPO firms will be able to transfer their registration to BOI.

Hot money registers net inflow of US$83mn in October

Total foreign investments registered through authorized agent banks (“hot money”) was a net inflow of US$83.4mn in October. This is a reversal of the net outflow of US$367mn in September 2022 and US$221mn in October 2021. This is also ends net outflows since May 2022. Note that the Philippine Stock Exchange recorded foreign net outflows of US$11.6mn in October. The gross inflows amounted to US$645mn, 28% lower MoM, while gross outflows were down 55% MoM to US$561mn. The 10M22 foreign portfolio investments yielded net inflows of US$305mn, a turnaround from the US$680mn net outflow in the same period last year.

NEDA Board endorses new EO reducing tariffs on e-vehicles

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) Board approved an executive order (EO) that will modify the tariffs imposed on electric vehicles (EV). The EO will temporarily reduce the Most Favoured Nation (MFN) tariff rates to zero for five years on CBUs of certain EVs. Tariffs on parts and components of EVs will be reduced to 1% from 5% for five years. The EO will not cover hybrid-type EVs and is up for President Marcos Jr’s approval. Tariff modifications will be reviewed after a year of implementation to assess the development of the EV industry.
