REGIS REPORT

18 November 2022

The PSEi ended the week higher, helped again by foreign buying. A strong 3Q earnings season on the ground served to lift risk appetite. The index gained 2.4% this week, marking the fifth straight week of gains.

Foreigners were net buyers for a third consecutive week with another US$29.7m flowing through. Inflows would have been larger had it not been for a BPI block that registered US$32.9m in outflows. Excluding BPI, foreigners would have been net buyers for US$51.4m. Top buys were seen across Ayala Land, BDO, Jollibee, and Universal Robina.

Gainers overwhelmed losers 11:4.

After a long bout of underperformance (down 61% YTD as of last week), Converge (+20.0% WoW) led the gainers this week.  Gains likely came on the back of covering of positions prior to MSCI’s rebal announcement last week.  Converge will be deleted from the MSCI Index on rebal day, with an estimated US$37m in outflows on Nov 30. 

Also advancing markedly was ACEN (+10.8% WoW).  Likewise gaining this week were property names  Ayala Land (+10.4% WoW) as did Megaworld (+9.7% WoW) which saw reservation sales jump 102% YoY (+24% QoQ) in 3Q thanks to a jump in OFW sales.

Large cap SM (+4.1% WoW) also helped prop the index. GT Capital (+8.8% WoW) also gained, despite a sharp contraction in Toyota’s GP margins which negated strong sales volumes. LT Group (+5.15% WoW) likewise rose, with 9M profits tracking ahead of estimates.

Consumer names fared well. Monde (+7.6% WoW) and URC (+6.9% WoW) gained, as did Puregold (+4.9% WoW).  The spate of results from the sector continue to show strong revenue and profit growth albeit margin pressure was a constant feature. Jollibee (+2.9% WoW) continues to reap benefits from its business transformation, while Shakey’s (+3.5% WoW) enjoyed strong topline growth in 3Q. Max’s (+0.2% WoW) saw EBITDA more than double with SSSG growth   estimated in the ~50% range, while Philippine Seven (+2.6% WoW) was back in the black after losses in the same period last year.

FB’s (+2.6% WoW) profit  in 3Q fell sequentially however as profits from the food segment contracted by more than 50%.

Banks were mixed. BPI (+3.8% WoW) managed a rise while BDO (+3.4% WoW) and Metrobank (-0.7% WoW) fell. Security Bank (+5.7% WoW) meanwhile rose. Its 9M22 profit trended well ahead of FY22 forecasts coming in largely due to a large drop in provisioning.  Loan growth slowed during the  quarter (to 12%) and NIMs fell QoQ.

Globe (-3.7% WoW) also fell, despite in-line results. Mobile revenue was largely expected, up +3% YoY, while broadband revenue fell 9% with the loss of the Fixed Wireless (FW) subscribers. 

Higher raw material and selling costs at Emperador (-2.7% WoW) and lower win rates at Travellers weighed down Alliance Global’s (+4.4% WoW) 3Q. Cost pressures also caught up to Golden Arches (GADC).

Ayala Corp (-1.4% WoW) slid, though 9M profits (+23% to P24bn) are tracking ahead as its main listed units did better than expected, particularly BPI. The non-listed operations are still losing money, most notably AC Industrials, however.

Manila Water (+10.2% WoW) surged on positive results from the water regulator’s rate review for the water concessionaires (including Maynilad). The approved rate adjustments look surprisingly large (+28% for MWC and +9% for Maynilad in January, excluding the CPI-linked adjustment) and will have a huge impact on earnings next year.

On the macro side, the Bangko Sentral raised policy rates by 75bp (to 5.0%). The rate hike was not surprising, as it has been telegraphed by the BSP, and in line with the latest action by the Fed. Policy rates are now higher than where they were immediately before the pandemic (4.0%).

Globe Telecom - 3Q: Corporate data segment a bright spot; wired broadband net adds pick up
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Results up modestly YoY, trending within expectations

Globe reported core income of P16.0bn, +3% YoY, for 9M22. Revenue was P118bn (+4% YoY) while EBITDA came in at P60.3bn (+6%). The YoY numbers for the three metrics in 3Q were no better than in 1H. Nevertheless, we’d characterize the results as being within expectations.

Core revenue lifted by corporate data

Mobile revenue seems under a little bit of pressure, despite the +3% YoY growth in 3Q. We estimate mobile data yields declined 7% QoQ to only US$0.30/GB despite only modest gains in data usage. Corporate data was one bright spot, as revenue for this segment was up +20% for 3Q and +21% for 9M22.

Wired broadband net adds rise; prepaid fiber to be launched

Home broadband revenue contracted again, down 15% in 3Q and 9% in 9M, with the continued fall in fixed wireless (FW) subscribers. There’s a silver lining, however, as Globe added 71k subscribers for the wired segment in 3Q, more than PLDT and Converge combined.

 

The company revealed it is set to launch a prepaid fiber broadband service soon in order to access lower income households. This is distinct from the “prepaid fiber WiFi” service called “TMbayan” announced a few months ago.

Mynt contribution drops

Profit contribution from Mynt in 3Q was P55m, a sharp drop from 1H (P595m), as the firm spent more on new initiatives. Operationally, the trajectory appears to still be strong. Its e-wallet GCash now has 71m users and P4.8trn of GTV YTD.

Capex to drop next year

Without providing exact guidance, Globe says it expects capex to begin declining next year from US$1.7bn this year. Capex should drop to around US$1bn by 2024, which would be 1/3rd of service revenue from around half this year.

 

 

 
Metropolitan Bank & Trust Co - Wider NIMs, lower opex spur upgrades; but wary of NPL blip
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT estimates raised 20-25%; TP upped 17% to P76/sh

Metrobank 9M22 NPAT surged 45% YoY, with 3Q alone up 77%, beating our expectations. NIMs jumped 22bps QoQ, as rising interest rates meant upward loan repricing and better yields, as the bank gapped out its treasury book. Opex was surprisingly flat YoY, adding to the beat, though expenses should start climbing from 4Q22. We raise our 2022- 24E NPAT forecasts by 20-25%, largely on higher NIM assumptions. We are wary, though, of asset quality, though, as the bank’s NPLs blipped up 20bps QoQ in 3Q22. We raise our target price by 17% to P76/sh, and maintain our Buy.

NIMs expand, as investment book gaps out

Metrobank’s 3Q22 NIMs increased by 20bps QoQ. Loan growth accelerated to 13.3% YoY in 3Q, from 10.2% in 2Q, and loan yields expanded by 30bps QoQ, as short-term loans were repriced up. Its high CASA ratio saw deposit costs up at a much slower 18bps QoQ. The bank also added close to P80bn (+9%) QoQ to its Treasury book, likely gapping out to longer tenors. We increase our NIM assumptions by 4-12bps across our forecast range.

NPLs rise 20bps QoQ from Covid-hit accounts

The bank’s asset quality unexpectedly deteriorated in 3Q22, with NPL ratio blipping up 20bps QoQ to 2.11%. Management explained that these accounts were already on the watchlist coming from industries hit hard by Covid-19. Lower provision expense took NPL cover down to 172% from 196% in 2Q22. While cover is still high, we are wary of higher NPLs due to high inflation and a weak PHP.

Raising forecasts and TP; maintain Buy

We raise our 2022-24E NPAT forecasts by 20-25%, driven largely by higher NIM estimates. We also increase our target price by 17%to P76.00/sh, representing a 49% potential total return. At our new TP, the stock would be trading at around 1x Price-to-book on our 2023E numbers, given our long-term ROE estimate of 12.0% and COE of 12.0%. Maintain Buy. Downside risks include a) competition for loans and deposits squeezing NIMs, and b) further deterioration in asset quality.

9M22 core profit rises 43% YoY to P10.89bn – In line

Core profit contributions for the auto unit dropped QoQ/YoY in 3Q22, after GP margins contracted further to a low of 7.9%. Sequential improvement in property sales reservations

The property unit’s profits dropped in 3Q due to higher cancellations largely at its projects in Pasay and slower completions, despite higher equitized earnings from its JV projects in BGC and Makati. Reservation sales improved by 26% QoQ to P5.3bn, bringing 9M22 reservation sales up 74% YoY to P13.7bn. After only launching one (1) project since the pandemic, it plans to launch two (2) more projects in Marikina and Pasay soon.

 
Megaworld - 3Q22 reservation sales double YoY thanks to international buyers
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Forex losses shave profits amidst strong residential margins

Megaworld’s 9M22 operating profit of P15.1bn (+30%) is tracking ahead of our forecast, thanks in large part to the 3ppt improvement in residential GPM (which was brought about by a higher proportion of lot sales). However, headline profit is up only 3% to P8.4bn, in line with REGIS forecast, due to forex losses on the company’s US$ debt.

3Q22 reservation sales double YoY due to international buyers

3Q22 reservation sales soared 102% YoY (+24% QoQ) to P34.6bn, already 93% of the 2019 average. This is despite the absence of mainland Chinese buyers, who comprised 10% of 2019 presales. Sales to international buyers (about 90% of whom are OFWs) comprised 20% of 9M22 reservation sales, a significant increase from 14% as of 1H22. Similar to the experience of most of the other major developers, the US was the largest market for international buyers. Management said that presales have not slowed down in early 4Q22.

 

Office vacancy improves

Office vacancy improved to 8% (from 9% in 2Q22). The company’s vacancy rate should remain below the industry average (of 18%) since it will not have any large office completions until 2024. Furthermore, the company’s 2024 Fort Bonifacio office pipeline is already 50% preleased. Meanwhile, 9M22 office revenue of P9.1bn (+12%) is tracking ahead of our forecast.

Mall rental concessions to be fully removed in 4Q22

Mall revenue was flat QoQ (+73% YoY) to P780mn, only about half of the 3Q19 figure. Megaworld expects a strong 4Q22 as the holiday season coincides with the full removal of mall rental concessions.

 
First Gen - 3Q22 dips again on San Gabriel derating
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q22 -5% to $63.8mn; 9M22 broadly in line

FGEN’s 3Q22 core income (after preferred dividends) dipped 5%QoQ to $63.8mn, dragged by lower earnings from San Gabriel. The company cited having to buy replacement power for San Gabriel owing to the return of gas supply restrictions during the period. This overshadowed the recovery from EDC which saw the return of Unified Leyte and higher revenues from repriced contracts and WESM sales. This brings 9M22 core income to $187.3mn (-8%), and broadly in line with Regis and consensus estimates for the year.

Nat gas dips again on San Gabriel derating, even without Ilijan

Earnings from natural gas unexpectedly dipped to $40mn (-20%QoQ, -22%YoY) in 3Q22 on our estimates. The disclosure cited San Gabriel power plant having to buy replacement power again in 3Q and thinner margins for Avion’s merchant sales. The result brings income from natural gas to $133mn (-16%YoY) in 9M22. San Gabriel started getting its full allocation of gas from Malampaya following the expiration of Ilijan’s contract in early June. Management noted that supply restrictions have become an issue once again, with San Gabriel’s output derated by about 210MW (out of 420MW) for a good portion of September due to inadequate gas supply. This affirms what the Malampaya operator cites as “technical issues” for its inability to deliver any of the banked gas supposedly sold to San Miguel a few months ago.

EDC recovers on higher WESM sales, repriced contracts

Attributable income from EDC jumped 44%QoQ and 18%YoY to $28mn. Management credited the recovery to the return of Unified Leyte (coming from an outage) and higher revenues from WESM and upward repricing of contracts). More recent contracts for RES customers are supposedly being signed >P5/kWh from around P4/kWh previously.

3Q23 target for commercial ops of LNG terminal

FGEN is in the process of negotiating two LNG supply contracts with short-listed suppliers. Although LNG spot prices for Asia delivery remain quite elevated, medium-term/contracted prices may be substantially lower. Should LNG prices remain elevated well into next year, management thinks that it can lower its total fuel cost by blending with liquid fuel ($20/mmbtu) and/or Malampaya gas ($12/mmbtu). They are targeting to finish construction by 2Q23 and commence operations by 3Q23.

Watch out for office vacancy and debt refinancing

3Q22 net profit jumped 52% QoQ and 40% YoY to P799mn. However, while profits have certainly risen, we have lingering concerns on the company: 1) office vacancy of 32% is poised to climb even higher and 2) 30% of FLI’s debt will mature next year and will likely be refinanced at much higher interest rates. Office vacancy at 32%; upcoming office building has no preleasing

Office vacancy was flat QoQ at 32%, much higher than industry average of 18%. While vacancy of Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) was a respectable 12%, the non[1]REIT portfolio’s vacancy was 60%.

FLI said that the vacancy rate will rise even further because an office building due for completion in 4Q22 has no preleased tenants yet. If there are no signed tenants by end-4Q22, we estimate that FLI’s office vacancy will rise to 37%.

Interest cost likely to rise

Filinvest has P22bn of debt maturing in 2023. We estimate that this debt has an average interest cost of c. 4.5%, lower than the current 5-year government bond yield of 7.0%. Thus, a 2ppt increase (or higher) in average interest rate on the refinancing would be likely, suggesting that interest cost could rise by P440mn, 9% of our 2023E pretax profit forecast.

Reservation sales close to 2019 levels but…

While 3Q22 presales of P5.7bn (+26% QoQ) are already 95% of the 2019 average, we refrain from being too positive since FLI’s payment schemes are currently much looser than in 2019.

Mall revenue climbs 10% QoQ

Mall revenue climbed 10% QoQ (+88% YoY) to P442mn, 90% of the 3Q19 figure. We estimate that FLI still offers rental concessions for 20% of its retail space. Thus, there should be further rebound as these are fully removed.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - 3Q22: Robust recovery despite margin pressure
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M22 NPAT surges >3x YoY, in line with expectations

JFC’s NPAT to common (ex-perps and prefs) surged over 3x YoY to P6.4bn, tracking broadly in line with Regis FY22E but ahead of consensus. Revenues (+38% YoY to P150bn) are trending ahead, but margins are slightly lagging our FY22 expectations. For 3Q22 alone, operating profit surged 3x to P3bn as revenues grew 49% YoY. NPAT growth was lower at +47% because of a higher tax expense (vs tax credit in 3Q21).

Recovery continues to drive 3Q SSSG Consolidated

SSSG during the quarter was quite strong at 31%, led by the Philippines (+49%), albeit moderating from 2Q. SSSG was led by the surge in transaction count: +78% for the Philippines alone. Average checks in the Philippines grew 22%, boosted by price increases. Except for China and Superfoods Vietnam, we estimate SSS levels for all geographies are well above pre-pandemic levels.

QoQ margins moderate as JFC shares burden of inflation

On a QoQ basis, operating profit was flattish due to gross margin contraction. GPM contracted 80bps YoY, driven mostly by the Philippines, which saw GPM contraction of >200bps QoQ. While this was partly due to seasonality, management also mentioned that it has been a deliberate strategy to not fully pass on cost inflation to squeeze out competitors. JFC had raised prices cumulatively by +7-8% on average as of 1H22, and we believe the price adjustments in 3Q, while not disclosed,were more modest. Raw material costs continue to be managed actively, and the company has already locked in prices and supply of key inputs until 1H23.

Momentum continues into early-4Q

Management says it expects to sustain +35-40% systemwide sales growth for the year (Regis FY22E: +25%), while operating profit levels are expected to be at par with or even exceed 2018 (2018A: P9.3bn / Regis 2022E: P10.7bn).

Foreign ownership inches up in October

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership rose 8bps MoM to 21.0% while PSEi foreign ownership also increased 8bps MoM to 25.2% (in October). Market net foreign selling dropped to US$12mn in October (from US$216mn in September).

 

The increase in market foreign ownership appears to be due to the outperformance of some high foreign-owned stocks such as: GLO (+14.4% MoM), ICT (+10.5% MoM), TEL (+8.4% MoM), SM (+13.1% MoM). 21 PSEi constituents saw declines in foreign ownership while 7 experienced increases.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

SMC –saw the highest increase in foreign ownership. Its foreign ownership rose 1.16 percentage points MoM to 5.6%. It also experienced the highest net foreign buying for the second consecutive month.

AC – saw the largest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 1.02 percentage points MoM to 25.2%. It also experienced the second highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$7.9mn)

PGOLD – foreign ownership declined 56 basis points to 18.9%. Foreign selling amounted to US$3.7mn

Results of 6th Rate Rebasing period

Conclusion: While the headline tariff hikes are quite large for both water companies, the regulatory framework used for the rate rebasing appears to follow the “less attractive” Revised Concession Agreement (RCA). Nevertheless, the results of Maynilad’s rebasing are much better than what Regis had anticipated.  There is less information on Manila Water’s (MWC PM) rate rebasing results outside of the tariffs, for now.  However, the tariff hikes for MWC are much larger than Maynilad’s, which suggest the underlying metrics are also favorable. 

 

MWSS Board approves water rate hikes. 

The Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) Board of Trustees approved the tariff petitions of its two water concessionaires, Manila Water (MWC PM) and Maynilad yesterday. The table below details the approved “R” tariff hikes, which will be implemented on a staggered basis and will depend on whether the new water sources (i.e. Wawa dam for MWC and Kaliwa dam for Maynilad) will be completed.  Note that the current basic water tariff for MWC is P28.5/cum while Maynilad is at P35.1/cum. 

 

Environmental surcharges hiked.  The MWSS also approved an increase in the environmental surcharge of MWC to 25% by Jan 2023 and 30% by 2026 from 20% today.  Meanwhile, the MWSS also raised Maynilad’s environmental surcharge to 25% by Jan 2025 but subject to the company achieving sewer coverage of 25% by end-2024.

 

CPI adjustment.  For both water utilities, the MWSS Board of Trustees approved a CPI adjustment of +3.7% (+P1.31/cum), which is equivalent to 2/3 of the projected 5.6% CPI for 2022.  It is worth noting that this adjustment reflects the commercial terms of the Revised Concession Agreement (RCA).  The Original Concession Agreement (OCA) allowed for a full CPI adjustment. 

 

Maynilad Rate Rebasing Results.  So far, Regis has only received more pertinent details of Maynilad’s rate rebasing results.  The key highlights of Maynilad’s RR decision are:

Business plan reflects RCA’s regulatory return of 12%.  Regis understands that Maynilad’s Business Plan is more akin to the terms of the RCA, with the regulatory return being set at 12%. This is, however, in stark contrast to what the MWSS Regulatory Office has been communicating, which is for a reversion to the original concession agreement.    

 

Higher OCP.  The regulator approved an Opening Cash Position (OCP) of P101.3bn, 13% higher than our estimate and much higher than the P77bn OCP approved in the 2018 rate rebasing. 

Higher committed expenditures.  The committed expenditures approved for 2023-2047 amounted to P889bn, 60% higher than our estimate. 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Monetary Board raises key rates by 75bps

The BSP raised the interest rate on the overnight reverse repurchase facility by 75bps to 5.0%. This brings total YTD rate hike to 300bps and within street expectations. The move followed the 75-bp hike of the Federal Reserve at its meeting early this month, which took its policy rate to 3.75%. The BSP tweaked its average inflation target for 2022 to 5.8% from 5.7% previously. The average inflation rate YT-October is at 5.4%. This implies that inflation for Nov/Dec will rise by an average of +8.1% YoY. It also raised its average inflation targets for 2023/2024 to 4.3%/3.1% from 4.1%/3%.

BSP keeps credit card rate cap; next review in Jan 2023

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) reported that the Monetary Board decided to keep the existing rate/fee caps on credit card transactions. The “reasonableness” of the caps will be reviewed again in January. Recall that the rate caps were set for the first time in 4Q2020. The most important of the caps was the rate on unpaid balances, set at 2.0% a month, or 24% per annum. Before the cap, banks were charging 3.0-3.5% a month on revolvers. Prior to 2020, the BSP did not (explicitly) regulate ANY rates/fees/charges that banks levied on its customers. That the BSP is reviewing the caps is potentially positive for banks. However, as we remarked back in 2020, the fact that the BSP imposed the cap at all was a slippery slope. Note that the BSP is reviewing the cap, but is not giving up its authority to regulate the caps at all. Regis remains wary that the BSP will use the precedent to set rate/fee caps on other bank products/services in the future.

SIM Registration ACT effective on December 27

The SIM Registration is expected to be made effective on December 27. According to Senator Grace Po during a hearing, the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) is expected to issue the implementing rules and regulations for the SIM Registration Act on December 12. By then, new SIM cards will have to be registered before they are activated. Existing SIM users will have to register within 180 days (with possible 120 days extension).
