REGIS REPORT

11 November 2022

The Phils gained 1.6% this week to close at 6286.77.  This marked the fourth straight weeks of gains for the PSEi, helped by a turn in foreign flow.

Foreigners were buyers every day this week with net inflows amounting to US$36.4m. Buying was a mix of both active and passive activity, with significant inflows seen at the close of each session. Biggest inflows were seen across banks BDO and BPI while outflows were in Emperador and Semirara.

Gainers outnumbered losers 17 to 12 this week, with a generally upbeat earnings season boosting sentiment on the ground. Strongest gains were seen in Puregold (+9.1% WoW), SM Prime (+8.4% WoW), Ayala Corp (+7.8% WoW), and Ayala Land (+6.1% WoW). Meanwhile, SCC (-10.1% WoW) declined on tempered coal prices.

Metro Pacific (-7.4% WoW) also fell. Along with GT Capital (-2.6% WoW) and Converge (+4.0%  WoW), MPI is set to be deleted from the main MSCI index on the next MSCI rebal effective Nov 30.

GDP in 3Q grew 7.6%, beating consensus expectations. Private consumption helped drive growth. This theme was recurring in the flurry of corporate results that came out this week and helping to result in numerous beats.

Consumer names have enjoyed strong topline momentum, notwithstanding the inflationary environment and margin pressure. URC (-2.5% WoW) saw 3Q22 consolidated revenues grew 32% YoY, with domestic BCF top line momentum sustained with growth accelerating to +27%. Robust consumer demand was also seen in Jollibee’s  (+4.5% WoW) 3Q, with SSSG at 15%, led by domestic top line growth of +49% for the quarter.  

Retail also saw strong jumps in profitability. Puregold saw SSSG for both its brands accelerate during the quarter, taking consolidated SSSG to +10.4% YoY.  Meanwhile, nearly all formats of Robinsons Retail (+6.5% WoW) have recovered to pre-pandemic SSS levels.

Monde (+2.8% WoW) was the exception within the consumer space, as its 3Q performance was hit by a product recall in the EU, which indirectly affected its core Philippine instant noodle business. Consolidated sales were flat YoY as domestic instant noodle sales contracted 24% YoY.

Meanwhile, strong recovery post the long pandemic restrictions was also apparent for malls. SM Prime  saw an almost doubling in profits YoY, as mall revenue continued to improve with the full removal of rental concessions in July 2022.  Parent SM (-1.2% WoW) also saw a big jump in profits.  SM Retail’s revenue and income for 3Q have now both exceeded the comparable pre-pandemic levels (2019). 

Ayala Land (+6.1% WoW) and Robinsons Land (+4.4% WoW) also saw revenues bounce, thanks largely to the removal of mall rental concessions.

Majority of other results this week painted a positive  picture. Beats were delivered by MPI and DMCI (-9.0% WoW) on strength of their operating units. ICTSI (+0.2% WoW) delivered strong profits despite concerns on global trade. Bloomberry (-2.2% WoW) likewise showed strength in underlying volumes even after the big reopening jump in 2Q. Metrobank (-0.3% WoW) saw its profits jump, as loans accelerated (+13.3% YoY) and NIMs expanded.

Some misses did come in. Cebu Air’s (+1.8% WoW) losses widened on cost pressures, while EMI’s (+5.2% WoW) revenue slowed (-7.0% QoQ) with at home consumption slowing post the pandemic. 

Meanwhile, as ACEN (+3.1% WoW) and Converge (+3.65% WoW) results also looked less compelling. ACEN saw EBITDA declining in 3Q  due to its net-buyer status as SLTEC went on outage. Meanwhile, adjusting for non-operating items, EBIDTA of CNVRG came in line. Net subscriber additions were also disappointing.

Aboitiz Power - 3Q22: Income jumps on GNPD contribution, arb strategy
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q profit +51%QoQ; AP delivers expected boost in 2H

AP reported a 3Q22 core profit of P9.247bn (51%QoQ, 65%YoY), equaling the income it posted in 1H22, on increasing contribution from GNPD and better plant availability. Energy sales of generation jumped 22%YoY while distribution volumes grew 4%YoY, a manifestation of both the continued growth in post[1]pandemic demand as well as additional generating capacity. The result brings AP’s 9M22 core profit to P18.347bn (17%YoY), now tracking slightly ahead of Regis (P23.6bn) and well above consensus (P19.3bn) estimates, after a weak start to the year.

Generation: GNPD, better performance from hydros and overall higher prices

Generation & RES contributed P9.449bn (38%QoQ, 57%YoY) in 3Q, on the back of improved contributions from GNPD units and better performance from hydros. Management estimates that GNPD contributed P2.3bn in 3Q and P5.6bn year-to[1]date, with the 2nd unit largely selling to the spot market. As expected, average selling prices to the spot market (P7.18/kWh, +34%) and bilateral contracts (P6.52/kWh, +39%) were pressured higher by the prevailing supply tightness and higher indices. Absolute gross margin increased another 23%QoQ, helped by favorable trading and arb opportunities. Management reiterated that its hedging strategies for commodities have played a big part in the performance to date, keeping them whole despite having fixed price contracts amidst surging fuel/coal prices.

Distribution: Volumes recover but profit still weighed down by cost pass-through

Income from distribution improved 15%QoQ to P908mn, although still lower by 10%YoY. The timing difference between pass-through cost and recovery continued to weigh on margins, which were still c.13% off from normal levels. When power prices stabilize, we expect profits to catch up to around P1.1bn/quarter. Meanwhile, underlying volumes across its distribution utilities were driven by commercial and industrial segments (+4%QoQ, +6%YoY).

Secondary price caps in WESM increasingly being hit

Secondary price caps were hit at an average of 60% of intervals in September, from just 20% in the first two quarters of the year. Affected plants (oil, Therma Luzon) have applied for cost recovery in cases where cost of fuel exceeded the cap, but have added challenges to managing working capital.

 
Universal Robina - Margins stabilize in 3Q22 but cost pressures persist
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

3Q22 recurring NPAT +37% YoY led by strong revenue growth

URC reported 9M22 net profit of P9.3bn, up 31% YoY on a recurring basis (ex[1]one-offs in 9M21). This is tracking broadly in line with Regis FY22E (P12.3bn) but ahead of consensus (P11bn). 9M22 EBIT of P10.8bn (+10% YoY) is slightly lagging our FY22 forecasts, but revenues (+26% YoY to P107.8bn) are ahead. For 3Q22 alone, net profit jumped 37% YoY to P3.1bn. While the bottom line was boosted by forex gains, EBIT also grew strongly (+35% YoY).

Margins stabilize as impact of price adjustments come through

Consolidated EBIT margin stabilized at 9.2% during the quarter, though the segmental trends were mixed. Int’l branded consumer food (BCF) and non-BCF margins saw some improvement, while domestic BCF margins continued to be pressured by elevated input cost. URC implemented another 3-4% price increase in Sep, bringing the cumulative increase to ~12% as of 9M22. Management estimates that the price adjustments thus far have only been enough to cover ~80% of the absolute cost inflation and has signaled another round of pricing action (+2-4%) in November.

Domestic BCF: top line momentum sustained in 3Q22

3Q22 consolidated revenues grew 32% YoY, partly lifted by the Munchy’s consolidation. Domestic BCF top line momentum was sustained in 3Q with growth accelerating to +27%. Nearly all categories (ex-coffee) saw YoY growth of >20%. So while price adjustments came through, domestic volumes still grew by around mid-teens.

A balancing act as cost pressures persist

Management believes it can sustain the ~20% top line growth rate for the rest of the year (Regis FY22E +17%) as they continue to see demand pick up following the reopening of face-to-face classes and the beginning of the holiday season. The outlook for margins, however, is a bit more uncertain as inflationary pressures persist. While prices of some commodities (e.g. wheat, palm oil) have come off the Feb-Mar peaks, prices of most commodities are still significantly higher vs 2021 levels. Moreover, URC imports majority of its raw materials, thus the strengthening USD is tempering any potential margin relief from easing input prices.

 
BDO Unibank - Upgrades, as NIMs surge, fueling record profits in 3Q22
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Forecasts upgraded 15-19%; TP raised to P175/sh

NPAT surged to P40.0bn in 9M22, roundly beating Regis and Street expectations (P49bn for the full year). 3Q22 alone came in at an all-time high P16.1bn, up 46% YoY, driven by trading/fx gains and improving asset quality. Our forecast upgrades, though, are due largely to NIMs, which expanded 24bps QoQ in 3Q, faster than we anticipated. As loan repricing catches up with policy rate hikes, we expect NIM momentum to carry into 4Q22 and 2023. We raise our 2022-24E NPAT estimates by 15-19%, increase our target price by 17% to P175/sh. We maintain our Buy rating on the stock, while keeping an eye on asset quality.

3Q22 NPAT surprises to a record-high at P16bn

BDO reported 3Q22 of P16.1bn, up 48% YoY, and by far the highest quarterly profit the bank ever reported. On the face of it, the “beat” on the revenue side was driven by surprisingly strong fx gains. Meanwhile, asset quality improved considerably with NPL ratio falling 16bps QoQ, and Restructured Loans dropping around 120bps QoQ. This allowed BDO to reduce 9M provision expense by 17% YoY, but still have NPL coverage increase to 152% from 138% in 2Q.

Rapid NIM expansion, operating leverage drive chunky forecast upgrades

The main surprise, though, was the speed with which NIMs expanded, up 24bps QoQ. We expect further margin expansion, given a) continued BSP policy rate hikes, and b) lagged re-pricing of loans, and increase our NIM forecasts by around 20bps. BDO’s relatively high cost-income ratio means extreme operating leverage, taking our NPAT estimates up by 15-19% across 2022-24E.

Target price hiked 16.7% to P175/sh; Buy retained

We hike our target price to P175/sh (+16.7%), representing a potential total return of 39%, and maintain our Buy call on BDO. We value the stock on (ROE[1]g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.0%, COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%, equivalent to a target P/bk of 1.6x on our 2023E book value estimates. Key risks include a) a deterioration in asset quality due to high inflation, rising interest rates, and a weak PHP; and b) intensifying competition for both loans and deposits, which could limit NIM expansion.

 
SM Prime - 3Q Philippine mall revenue near pre-covid levels as rental concessions removed
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Mall operations and residential cancellations improve

SM Prime’s 3Q22 net profit rose 18% QoQ (+98% YoY) to P7.9bn as mall revenue continued to improve with the full removal of rental concessions. Meanwhile, residential revenue also rebounded, due to a likely drop in cancellations. As a result, 9M22 earnings jumped 41% to P22.0bn, slightly ahead of our forecast (in line with consensus). These results could indicate continued improvement of both mall operations and residential cancellations for the rest of the sector.

Philippine mall revenue reaches 95% of pre-COVID level

Although 3Q is seasonally weaker than 2Q, 3Q22 Philippine mall revenue rose 7% QoQ (+163% YoY) to P13.3bn, already 95% of the 3Q19 figure. This was thanks to the removal of all rental concessions beginning July 2022. Philippine mall rent grew 8% QoQ (+143% YoY) to P11.8bn, already 8% above the 3Q19 figure. Since the mall portfolio is about 5% larger than in 3Q19, same-mall rent arguably exceeds the 3Q19 level. However, inclusive of cinema and event sales, same-mall revenue is still c. 10% below the 3Q19 level.

Residential revenue rebounds, cancellations likely dropped

Residential revenue jumped 63% QoQ (+32% YoY) to P10.1bn. While this is lower than our expectations, it also suggests that cancellations have dropped substantially relative to 2Q22. Given the gradual reopening of the economy, we believe that cancellations peaked in 2Q22. Although reservation sales dropped 13% QoQ (+16% YoY) to P24.5bn, this is still 9% above the 2019 average, implying that demand remains strong. Sales in provincial areas likely increased since the company has been launching more projects outside Metro Manila.

 
ICTSI - 3Q22: Yields healthy, costs controlled
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M22 profit rises 64% YoY US$439mn – Ahead of estimates

Reported 3Q22 profit less perps distributions jumped 14% QoQ and 55% YoY to US$163mn while 3Q22 EBITDA further improved to US$366mn (+9% QoQ, +23% YoY). Cumulative 9M22 profit rose 64% YoY to US$439mn, accounting for 77% of Regis FY22E and 88% of consensus forecast. Given that 4Q is seasonally the strongest quarter, we believe ICT is on track to beat our recently upgraded, Street-high FY22E (see here) due to (a) higher organic yields, (b) lower cash costs, (c) lower financing expenses, and (d) a lower tax rate.

3Q22 organic volume growth steady at +6% YoY

The consolidation of MNHPI in Sept helped lift volume growth to 11% YoY in 3Q. Organic volume growth was, however, still healthy at +6% YoY in 3Q22, with Asia leading (+15% YoY) and more than offsetting the volume declines in Pakistan and Poland. Management noted that volume trends for October are similar to 9M22 (+6% YoY), but given global macroeconomic challenges, management is guiding volume growth to moderate to “low single digits” in 2023. They are banking on (a) economic recoveries of its markets and (b) market share gains to offset the likely downward trajectory of global trade volumes.

Organic yield remains robust at US$183/TEU in 3Q22

Organic yield per TEU strengthened further to US$183 in 3Q22 from US$181 in 1H22, on the back of tariff hikes and more favorable container mix. The consolidation of MNHPI should dilute group-wide average yields slightly (to US$181), but this would remain above forecast (US$176). Management remains “constructive” on its yield outlook even for 2023, with several tariff hikes and new tariffs likely to be implemented over the next year or so.

EBITDA margin at 63% in 3Q

EBITDA margin improved by 90bps QoQ, 200bps YoY to 63.4%, despite a sustained increase in cash costs (+5% QoQ / +13% YoY). The company is operating more efficiently than we had anticipated, as 9M22 cash cost is only up by 12% YoY, better than our full-year forecast of +15% YoY. While management sees upward cost pressures continuing into 1H23, they remain confident about their ability to raise yields to offset these pressures.

 
Ayala Land - 3Q22: residential cancellations moderate; sales to OFWs hit record
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Operations heading in the right direction

Ayala Land’s 3Q22 net profit rose 107% YoY (+7% QoQ) to P5.3bn. Although residential revenue is trailing our forecast, operations of most of the major segments are heading in the right direction: 1) reservation sales improved thanks to strong demand from OFWs and foreigners; 2) development revenue grew as cancellations moderated; and 3) mall and hotel revenue recovered close to pre-COVID levels.

Reservation sales rise on international buyers

3Q22 reservation sales jumped 27% YoY (+10% QoQ) thanks to the expansion in sales to OFWs and foreigners (see Figure 3). Sales to OFWs surged 68% YoY (+16% QoQ) to P6.3bn, a record level. Avida and Amaia condominiums in Metro Manila were popular with OFWs based in the Middle East. Meanwhile, the strong US$ appears to have boosted demand from foreigners (particularly from Americans). In 9M22, sales to foreigners jumped 50% (+59% YoY in 3Q22). Sales to Americans in particular, rose 69%, and comprised 63% of sales to foreigners. The company said that it has been able to maintain residential margins since its average price increase of 7% matches the rise in construction costs.

3Q22 development revenue climbs as cancellations drop

3Q22 development revenue grew 21% YoY and 15% QoQ (see Figure 2) as residential cancellations moderated sequentially. Management has been seeing improvements and believes that we’ve seen the worst with respect to cancellations. Mall and hotel revenue both reach 92% of pre-COVID levels Although 3Q is seasonally weaker than 2Q, mall revenue rose 10% QoQ to P4.3bn, already 92% of the 3Q19 figure. This was thanks to the full removal of rental concessions in July 2022. Meanwhile, hotel revenue jumped 151% YoY (+6% QoQ) on strong domestic tourism. 9M22 hotel occupancy improved 6ppt (to 56%) while resort occupancy rose 15ppt (to 28%).

BPO office inquiries remain strong

Office vacancy was flat QoQ at 16%. Nevertheless, despite new regulations that effectively allow BPOs to fully implement work-from-home policies, office inquiries remain strong. This has led management to expect occupancy of its office portfolio to improve by end-4Q22.

3Q22 profit softens QoQ, but underlying volumes grow across the board

BLOOM posted a net profit of P1.560bn in 3Q22, a reversal of last year’s P1.0bn loss but slightly lower vs 2Q22 (-7%QoQ). Hold rates across all segments dipped from the unusually high levels in 2Q22, while promo allowances ticked upward due to more points redemptions. More importantly, volumes trended upward across the board, extending the streak to five straight quarters of growth. The result brings 9M22 net profit to P4.052bn (77%YoY), ahead of estimates, although 9M22 EBITDA (P10.313bn) is tracking slightly behind.

GGRs steady as win rates correct; slot volumes close to +30% vs 3Q19

Lower win rates tempered QoQ growth of GGR to just 2%, with the drop in slot hold rate from 6.0% to 5.0% dragging segment revenue -7% lower QoQ. The win rates overshadowed the strength in underlying volumes – VIP rolling chip (13%QoQ, 23%YoY), mass table drop (14%QoQ, 77%YoY), slot machine handle (10%QoQ, 118%YoY). Volumes for slots are now 27% higher than pre-pandemic levels, while mass tables (-22%) and VIP (-33%) are still down.

Premium mass growing; foot traffic hovering at <70%

Foot traffic and hotel occupancy levels have held steady at 13k/day and 60%, with fly-in Chinese VIPs still largely absent. They suggest that the local business is still growing organically even after the large reopening jump in 2Q. Within mass market, the growing share of premium mass (now 50% of total mass vs c.30% of total mass pre pandemic) means they are attracting a higher income demographic who are less affected by macro volatility.

Jeju Sun reopens in October

Jeju Sun reopened last October, putting an end to government subsidies that paid for employee salaries etc. Although largely closed throughout the pandemic, it had a limited impact to P&L because of government aid. The opening could mean a return to >P100mn in losses per quarter.

Monde Nissin - 3Q22 hit by product recall; Quorn restructuring announced
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M22 NPAT -21% YoY as product recall hits core instant noodle business

MONDE reported core net profit of P5.6bn, down 21% YoY, behind Regis FY22E but tracking broadly in line with consensus. Consolidated net sales rose 7% YoY to P54.9bn, but EBITDA dropped 21% YoY to P8.8bn. 3Q22 NPAT fell 46% YoY to P1.5bn. Consolidated sales were flat YoY and margin pressure was evident across both segments.

APAC BFB: The perfect storm

Segment sales were flattish in 3Q22 as the company was hit by a product recall in the EU with respect to its core instant noodle products. While export sales account for a small proportion (<5%) of the business, news of the recall indirectly impacted its larger, more profitable domestic business. Instant noodle sales (~50% of sales) saw a 24% YoY decline during the quarter, leading to a ~700bps YoY (-300bps QoQ) drop in market share. APAC BFB EBITDA fell 42% YoY as a result, exacerbated by input cost inflation and proportionately higher A&P to address the drop in instant noodle sales.

Quorn: Industry remains challenged; restructuring undertaken

In constant currency terms, Quorn revenues rose 7% YoY in 3Q22, led by the UK (+8% YoY). Quorn continued to gain market share in retail, but the overall category remains challenged. The US market also continues to be difficult, with Quorn sales declining 15% in the US.

Worst is likely over, but recovery could take a while

Instant noodle sales are on the path to recovery, and management noted that average daily sales in October are already at par with the June average (pre[1]recall). This should, in our view, lead to a sequential improvement in segment profitability in 4Q22. However, margin pressure from input cost inflation could persist as the company will need until 2Q23 to work through high-cost inventory (mainly wheat and palm oil). Moreover, the strengthening USD is tempering the landed cost of raw materials, which are mostly imported.

Meanwhile, management announced that it is undertaking a restructuring at Quorn, resulting in ~P800mn one-off costs (excluded from core income). These one-offs relate to a writedown in some production assets, as well as some personnel and overhead rationalization. These are expected to result in P300- 400mn worth of cost savings in 2023, but top line pressure could persist given the industry dynamics.

9M22 core operations in line; headline NPAT boosted by other income

Operating leverage drives 3Q22 EBITDA

Holiday season to fuel 4Q22

Disappointing net adds
