REGIS REPORT

14 January 2022

The PSEi rose 3.6% this week, recovering from a three-week string of declines.

Citing hospital occupancy data, the government kept the current alert level in terms of restrictions (Alert Level 3) until the end of the month. Sentiment improved on the ground as hospitalizations remained manageable despite record case counts.  The COVID-19 daily case count hit 34,021 on Thursday – the highest day of new cases since the pandemic began. The positivity rate of 48% suggests undercounting of cases however, a notion strongly supported by anecdotal evidence.

As of January 9, the 7-day average vaccinations figure climbed to 644,363/day.  Some 48% of the Philippine population (54.2m) has been fully vaccinated against COVID; The proportion for Metro Manila is higher at  78%. The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77m Filipinos by end of this quarter, and reach 90m Filipinos by end of the following quarter.

Gains were widespread with only two of 30 index names ending the week down.

Banks led this week – with Metrobank (+8.0% WoW) and Security Bank (+7.6% WoW) gaining markedly.  BPI (+2.6% WoW) and BDO  (+5.3% WoW) ended higher as well. BDO president Nestor Tan was quoted as saying the bank is expecting loans to bounce back this year, growing 8-12% YoY, led by the top corporate segment.

GT Cap’s (+7.3% WoW) gained. Its auto unit Toyota Motor Phils sold 129,667 units in 2021 – hitting its 30% YoY growth target this year (130k unit).  Toyota’s market share for the year hit 46%, an improvement from pre-pandemic levels (40-44%), partly aided by gains of its financing arm Toyota Financial Services (TFS).  Meanwhile, Toyota is adding  another small SUV (“Raize”) to its vehicle lineup in the coming weeks.

Other conglos JG Summit (+6.7% WoW) and Aboitiz Equity (+5.8% WoW) also gained.   AEV’s power subsidiary AP (+5.5% WoW) closed higher, even as banking unit Union Bank (+12.2% WoW) surged.  

In the property space Ayala Land (+6.9% WoW), Robinsons Land (+6.2% WoW), and SM Prime (+3.6% WoW) ended higher.

Robinsons Retail (+6.9% WoW) bounced from recent declines, while Jollibee (+6.5% WoW) ended higher as well.

Foreigners were light buyers this week w/ market seeing net inflows of US$1.2m. Biggest inflows were seen in banks BDO/MBT as well as property names while saw outflows in consumer names.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Asset quality ratios better again in Nov, as loan growth accelerates further

Major asset quality ratios improved MoM in November, while absolute values were flat-ish. Only Restructured Loans (RLs) deteriorated, both in Peso and ratio terms. The ratios were helped by a continued acceleration in loans. The data suggests that the NPL cycle has peaked, which could provide significant upside for bank earnings in 2022. The NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dropped to 4.48% down 8bps MoM compared to 4.56% in October. In nominal terms, NPLs were down slightly (-0.4% MoM) to P481bn. Past Due (PD) ratio improved to 5.12% from 5.16% the previous month. The PHP value edged up a bit, but less so than loans. Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio deteriorated slightly to 3.11% from 3.08% in October. In PHP terms, RLs jumped 2.1% MoM to P345bn. The NPL cover edged up to 87% from 84% the previous month. Loan loss reserve ratio (LLR) also improved by a smidge. Loan growth accelerated to 3.9% YoY in November, from 3.2% in October, marking the 7th consecutive month of improvement. Loans (ex-interbank) were up 1.2% MoM. Loans typically jump 2.5-3.5% MoM in December (even in 2020), partly on trade stocking, and partly on window-dressing. If the trend holds, we could see the loan growth close the year at or about 5%. Regis could conceivably see a deterioration in ratios in January from the Covid surge, and drop in mobility. However, Regis suspects that households and businesses have amply adapted to the sporadic opening and closing of the economy. Additionally, Regis notes that asset quality did not suffer materially during the Aug/Sept lockdowns.

DM Wenceslao sells land parcel in Manila Bay area

Towards the end of December 2021, DM Wenceslao and Associates (DMW PM) sold a 1,790sqm land parcel in Aseana, which is in the Manila Bay area. Regis sources said that it was priced at P425,000/sqm-P450,000/sqm. The price of the previous transaction (in 2019) was P425,000/sqm . A Colliers estimate in 2020 valued the company’s properties at P204bn (c. P60/share) with the useable land was valued at P350,000/sqm. This would imply that DMW is trading at 89% discount to NAV. To arrive at our P39.68/share SMPH NAV estimate, Regis assigns P260,000/sqm on unused Mall of Asia (MOA) complex land and P170,000/sqm on the reclamation projects. The reclamation projects currently account for 31% of SM Prime’s NAV. Every P10,000/sqm change in the value of the reclamation projects adds 2.8% to our SMPH NAV. Every P10,000/sqm change in the value of existing unused MOA land would raise our SMPH NAV by less than 0.1%. Assuming P400,000/sqm for unused Mall of Asia complex land and P260,000/sqm for the reclamation projects, our SMPH NAV estimate would rise 26% to P49.98/share. SMPH is currently trading at 2.8X FY22E P/B, 25.7X FY22E P/E, and 13% discount to NAV (of P39.68/share).

Robinsons Land to redevelop Forum mall

Bilyonaryo.com said that Robinsons Land informed its Forum Robinsons tenants that the last day of mall operations would be on 30 April 2022. Forum Robinsons is along EDSA (corner Pioneer Street). The property will be redeveloped into a high-end mall with four LEED Gold certified buildings on top. The redevelopment will likely take about six years. Forum Robinsons comprises 2% of RLC’s mall floor area and it likely accounts for c. 1% of total EBIT. RLC is trading at 55% discount to NAV, 0.8X FY22E P/B, and 10.2X FY22E P/E.

 

Fire hits Starmall Alabang

Five people were hurt during a fire in Starmall Alabang. The fire alarm was at 3:40AM on January 8 and was declared under control by 7PM. Starmall Alabang accounts for about 5% of Vista Land’s mall floor area. Vista’s malls are insured against damages from fire. VLL is trading at 61% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY22E P/B, and 4.8X FY22E P/E.

Duterte signs into law new franchises for Manila Water, Maynilad

President Duterte signed the franchise laws on December 10 last year, labeled Republic Act No. 11600 and Republic Act No. 11601. Two laws grant water concessionaires, Manila Water and Maynilad franchises, allowing them to extend their concessions to 2046 (from 2037 previously)

Vehicle sales rise 20% YoY in 2021; Toyota meets year-end target

The Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines Inc. (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA) sold 268,488 units last year, up 20% YoY. For 2021 car sales were 27% below 2019 and came in short of CAMPI’s 294k target. Additionally, in December alone, industry car sales rose 1% YoY, 5% MoM to 27,846 units (highest since March 2020). Toyota sold 129,667 units in 2021, up 30% YoY and came within its year-end target (130k). Toyota’s 2021 sales were 20% below 2019. In December, Toyota sold 13,502 units, down 2% YoY but up 6% MoM. Toyota ended 2021 with a market share of 48%, up from 44% in 2019. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 19% discount to its market-based NAV (P680/sh).

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Unemployment rate falls in November, but job quality worsens

The unemployment rate fell to 6.5% in November from 7.4% the previous month. This is also the lowest jobless rate since the pandemic started and was due to more relaxed mobility restrictions. However, the quality of available jobs (ie. underemployment rate) remained high at 16.7%, highest in four months. Around 7.6mn employed are looking for additional work or longer working hours.

PH logs highest daily tally of 28,707 new Covid-19 cases

The DOH reported a record 28,707 new Covid-19 cases on Sunday, the highest daily tally since the start of the pandemic. 2 out every 5 tested were positive, translating to a record positivity rate of 44%. Covid-19 intensive care beds in NCR were now 52% full, half of the isolation beds occupied and 65% of ward beds in use.

Indonesia eases coal export ban after 10 days

Indonesia allowed 14 coal vessels to depart, after seeing a much better supply condition according to Coordinating Minister of Maritime and Investment Affairs Luhut Pandjaitan. The government will conduct a review on removing the ban gradually. Last week, Indonesia’ Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources imposed a ban on coal exports from Jan 1-31. Indonesia is the largest supplier of seaborne thermal coal, accounting for c. 44% (c. 400mn MT) of annual supply. Coal price futures rose to US$197/MT yesterday from US$158/MT on Jan 3.

Pace of vaccinations rises

As of January 9, the 7-day average vaccinations figure climbed to 644,363/day from 491,545/day the previous week. Around 48% of the Philippine population (52.4mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 213mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 113mn doses (as of January 9). The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22.

Metro Manila to remain under Alert Level 3 despite COVID spike

Despite the alarming surge in COVID-19 cases nationwide, the 17 mayors in Metro Manila still see no need to impose stricter rules to restrict mobility as they cited the high vaccination rate and manageable health care utilization rate which was below the 70% threshold. Meanwhile, Hospital beds and ventilators remained relatively available; most cases were mild to moderate according to Metropolitan Manila Development Authority Chair Benhur Abalos

PH total trade growth accelerates to 24% in November; Trade deficit expands to US$4.7bn

The country’s total external trade rose 24% YoY in November, fastest since August due to imports jumping +37% YoY (fastest since June) on sustained growth of fuel imports, raw & intermediate goods, and consumer goods. Meanwhile, exports also rose +7% YoY. This brings the country’s trade deficit to US$4.7bn for the month, up 120% YoY and +17% MoM. The YT-November total trade is up 24% YoY to US$175bn and trade deficit at US$38bn – both above pre-pandemic levels.
