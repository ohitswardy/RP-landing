REGIS REPORT

22 April 2022

The Philippines snapped a two week losing streak, managing a marginal gain of 0.2% this week. The market closed just below the 7000 level.

Foreign selling remained elevated amid anemic volumes, with US$15.6m (net) exiting this week. Uncertainty over the elections, and the lack of any short term catalysts continue to prompt investors to take profit. Biggest outflows  were spread across banks (BDO and Metrobank), ports (ICTSI), and property names (SM Prime and Ayala Land).

Losers actually overtook gainers this week at 17:13.  Large caps BDO (+5.1% WoW) and Ayala Land (+2.4% WoW) helped the market higher while defensives took a backseat. ICTSI (2.1% WoW) and PLDT (+3.7% WoW) likewise ended higher. 

PLDT gained after announcing the sale of its towers in a value accretive deal.  The telco is selling close to 6,000 of its telecom towers to two separate groups for P77bn (US$1.5bn). The deals will come with leaseback arrangements allowing PLDT to continue using those assets. Regis estimates value accretion (after tax) of as much as P300 per share for PLDT from this deal.

Power was weak, with declines seen from Meralco (-6.1% WoW), Aboitiz Power (-4.2% WoW), and AC Energy (-3.5% WoW).  Consumer names URC (-5.2% WoW) and Monde (-1.7% WoW) as well as Puregold (-2.2% WoW) registered declines as well.

Nonetheless, reopening continued to be a theme domestically, with GT Cap’s (+0.6% WoW) Toyota Motor Phils reporting a 64% surge in vehicle sales for March.  Meawnhile, capacity at Cebu Air (-2.7% WoW) is now at >70% of pre-pandemic levels as demand for air travel continues to rebound. Results of consumer companies Philippine Seven (-0.7% WoW) and Max’s (+0.5% WoW) for 4Q also reflect a rebound, both returning to profitability post the height of the pandemic.

Banks profits reflected recovery as well. BPI (+0.5% WoW) reported 1Q21 net profit that was up an impressive 60% YoY (beating estimates of P30bn for the full year) as NII was drive by accelerating loan growth at 7.1% YoY and NIMs. Provisions also fell 31%. Meanwhile, BDO reported loans growing at a similar clip (+7.5%), driven by the mid-market segment. However, provisions were up 28%, despite an improvement in NPL ratio as the bank appears to be building its NPL coverage amidst strong operations.

RESEARCH
Shakey’s Pizza - Back on track
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Back on track; reiterating Buy
Over the past two years, restrictions on on-premise dining have led to a protracted recovery in Shakey’s Pizza’s same-store sales. While off- premise revenues (delivery, takeaway) grew sharply, the growth was not enough to make-up for the lost dine-in channel, which previously accounted for c60% of PIZZA’s sales. With dine-in restrictions now mostly withdrawn, we believe PIZZA should see a marked improvement in SSS and profitability as these recover from pandemic-lows, notwithstanding the recent surge in prices of raw materials. We are forecasting 105% EPS CAGR for 2022-24E as we raise our profit estimates by 11-22% and our TP to P12/sh. Reiterating Buy.

Reopening to overshadow input cost pressures
Prices of key raw materials (e.g. chicken, cheese, and potatoes) have surged. Despite this, we believe a profit rebound magnified by operating leverage and lifted further by cost reductions implemented during the pandemic, will overshadow any margin pressure from elevated input costs. PIZZA has locked in prices for key raw materials for majority of 2022E, which should provide some cushion to margins.

EPS CAGR of +105% over 2022-24E; Potato Corner to provide an added boost. 
We are estimating +105% EPS CAGR for 2022-24E to be largely driven by the improvement in same-store sales as the economy reopens. Relative to 2019 levels, we are estimating SSS to be c90% in 2022E and close to 100% by 2023E. Potato Corner, which will be consolidated beginning 2Q22, is expected to add c.30% to systemwide sales on a FY basis.

Raising estimates, TP; reiterating Buy.
We are raising our 2022-24E net profit estimates by 11-22%, though the upgrades to EPS over the forecast period are slightly lower (+1-12%) due to the expansion of PIZZA’s capital base following the buy-in of the Gokongwei Group (JE Holdings) in 2021. We roll over our DCF to 2022E and raise our TP to P12/sh (+20%). Our TP implies 25x 2022E PE, a premium to PIZZA’s historical average but broadly in line with peers. Key downside risks relate to a protracted recovery in consumption and raw material cost inflation.

Meralco power sales climb 6% in 1Q

Electricity sales of Manila Electric Co rose nearly 6%YoY in 1Q as mobility restrictions eased. Initial numbers indicate close to 6% growth in 1Q compared to the same period last year, Meralco FP and Chief Commercial Officier Ferdinand Geluz said over the weekend. All sectors grew with strong energization performance specially for project covered applications, recovery of commercial segment and steady growth of industrial segment after the January Omicron surge. He said that once more commercial establishments resume operations, the 6% sales volume growth in 2021 may be surpassed this year.

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) swings to profit in 4Q21

SEVN reported P123mn net profit in 4Q21, a turnaround from net losses in previous quarters. The figure, however, is 26% lower than the loss in 4Q20 mainly due to adjustments on tax relating to CREATE. Revenues grew strongly in 4Q21 (+33% QoQ / +14% YoY). SSSG accelerated to 8.5% as mobility restrictions eased. Operating profit during the quarter rose 3% YoY to P558mn. While revenues grew sharply, other income was proportionately lower as a % of revenues (7.5% in 4Q21 vs 10.8% in 4Q20).

Emperador secures conditional listing eligibility in Singapore

Emperador disclosed that it obtained a conditional eligibility-to-list (ETL) for its proposed secondary listing on the Singapore Exchange from SGX-ST. The ETL is subject to fulfillment of certain conditions, including the submission of confirmations and undertakings by the company to the SGX-ST, with the receipt of the ETL being one of the requirements needed to proceed with the secondary listing. PLDT signs deal to sell half of its telecom towers for P77bn

The deal raises P77bn (US$1.5bn). PLDT signed a deal to sell 5,907 telecom towers and related passive infrastructure for P77bn (US$1.5bn) yesterday.  The transactions would see the transfer of half of its towers to the winning bidders, subsidiaries of Edgepoint and edotco Group and PLDT subsequently leasing the towers back for the next ten years.

 

The company will post a gain of ~P41bn (US$800m) from these deals, of which P28bn will be recognized when they close and the rest over the life of the leaseback arrangement.

 

The transaction price is estimated to be at a whopping 20x EBITDA and the ~P7bn in annual lease payments that PLDT expects to pay will approximate the opex (cash and non-cash) it current has for the towers.  As a result, the deal will be EBIT neutral (excluding the impact of the recognition of the transaction gains) even if it will reduce EBITDA by less than 4%. After accounting for the impact of savings on financing costs, Regis estimates an earnings enhancement of over P2bn annually (+6% to 7%), excluding the recognition of deferred gains on the tower sale.

 

PLDT will pay around P100,000 (US$2,000) per month to lease each tower, which appears high (half that in Indonesia) but the payments will cover capex for power (batteries, generators, etc) and PLDT will benefit from co-location discounts should the towercos secure other tenants.  PLDT is also protected from future inflation with annual lease escalation effectively capped at less than 1%.

 

Management has earmarked P52bn (US$1bn) to reduce PLDT’s debt (partly outright debt prepayment) and up to P9bn to pay a special dividend.  The P9bn would be approximately P42 per PLDT share.  Regis expects any special dividend to be paid only in early FY23 given that the transactions will closed on a staggered basis up to 4Q22.

 

With PLDT trading at 6x EBITDA, a sale of its passive tower assets at 20x EBITDA would add value. Regis estimates the transaction will allow PLDT to realize/capture incremental value of close to P300/share (16% of the share price) given the terms of the deal (sale price and leaseback arrangements) and the fact that minimal value was likely to have been attached to the towers in the first place. 

 

There should be anticipation that Globe can conclude a similar deal with regard to its own tower assets.  Such a deal would not only enhance value for Globe, it would also obviate the need to raise new capital (something which Regis were raising).  Even without such a sale, there would also be benefits to Globe as ~6,000 towers would be become available for it to lease too. This could reduce future capex needs.

Cebu Air restores over 70% of pre-pandemic capacity

Jefferies’ latest airline capacity survey showed Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) overall capacity (domestic+international) now at over 70% of pre-pandemic level from March 28 to April 17. This is up from c. 49% in 1Q22 and 20% in 2Q21. At this level, we estimate CEB’s EBITDA is now in positive territory and cash burn is nil. Management previously noted it is targeting to exceed pre-pandemic level domestic capacity in April.

PMFTC volumes rebound in 1Q22 to 9.6bn sticks (+18%YoY, +11%QoQ).

Philip Morris International disclosed overnight that cigarette volumes in the Philippines in 1Q22 came in at 9.6bn sticks, +18%YoY and +11%QoQ. Industry volumes hit 15.6bn sticks (+19%YoY, +10%QoQ). PMFTC’s unit market share climbed slightly to 61.3%, after having lost market share in 4Q21.

 

The recovery in volume was driven by Marlboro and Chesterfield brands, offsetting the decline of mid-priced Fortune. Prices were increased by 17-21% for high, low end brands last March 21. 

BPI 1Q22 surges 60% to P8bn, ahead; but mind future opex growth

In a press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported headline net profit of P8.0bn, up 60% YoY and ahead of consensus and Regis estimates of P30bn for FY2022. It is also ahead of management’s (soft) guidance of P28bn for the full year. Profit was driven by NII, which grew 13% YoY on the back of accelerating loan growth (7.1% YoY) and expanding NIMs (+11bps YoY to 3.42%, by BPI’s estimate). Provisions also fell 31% to P2.5bn, as NPLs improved to 2.38% (vs 2.49% in 4Q21). Trading gains and fees were down YoY, as both were coming off high bases (including IPOs in 1Q21). NPAT got a big boost from lower tax expense, as the bank took a large hit in 1Q21 as they wrote off deferred tax assets (DTA) as a result of CREATE. 

 

Some other highlights:

  • Loan growth accelerated to 7.1% YoY from 4.9% in 4Q21. The 1Q22 growth was driven by credit cards, which was up 12.2% YoY, followed by corporates (+7.7%) and mortgages (+6.6%). Deposits were up 13.1% YoY, slowing slightly from 13.9% the previous quarter. CASA ratio improved QoQ to 81% from 77% in 4Q21.
  • NIMs improved 11bps YoY to 3.42% by BPI’s estimates (different computation from ours), as a result of improving loan growth, loan mix, and deposit mix. Net interest income (NII) jumped 13% YoY.
  • Non-interest income was down 15% YoY, due to very high base of trading gains in 1Q21. Fees were also coming off a high base given the strong IPO/FOO pipeline in 1H21.
  • Opex was up just 6% YoY. We suspect this will jump sharply in coming quarters, as the bank has guided for a 20% increase in full-year 2022 opex as the bank ramps up its IT spend.
  • Tax expense also plunged; we estimate tax+minorities were down some 40% YoY. Recall that BPI took a big one-time hit in 1Q21, writing down deferred tax assets (DTA) as a result of the CREATE law, which also lowered the statutory corporate tax rate from 30% to 25%.

Regis believes the future quarters could be softer NPAT wise, as the bank’s opex ramps up on its planned IT spend. We hope to hear more during the analyst briefing, though this won’t be until 04 May.

Vehicle sales post strong rebound in March

Industry car sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, jumped +43% YoY, +22% MoM to 29,685 units. This brings 1Q22 car sales to 74,754 units, up 6% YoY. March performance has reached pre-pandemic level of sales; it is the highest monthly sales recorded since the start of the pandemic. Toyota sold 15,110 units (+64% YoY, +23% MoM) and their market share gained to 50.9% from 48% in Dec. Additionally, this brings 1Q22 sales to 37,231 units, up 12% YoY. To reach Toyota’s year-end target of 162k units (+25% YoY), Toyota would need to sell 13,900 units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 21% discount to its market-based NAV of P650/sh and 7.4x FY22E consensus PER.

Remittances hit 3-month low in February

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 1.3% YoY in February to US$2.5bn. This is the lowest level of absolute remittances since November. The 3month moving average growth rate went down to +2.4% from 3.6% the previous month. In Peso terms, remittances in Feb grew +2.8% YoY. The government cited reimposition of restrictions in host countries and the Philippines amid a resurgence in COVID cases across the globe. Most land-based remittances sources posted declines, except for the USA which grew +4% YoY. This brings YT-Feb remittances to US$5.2bn, up 1.9% YoY. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +4% YoY, suggesting that monthly remittances should average US$2.75bn for the rest of the year.

Gov’t seeking bids for 20-year Davao Sasa port management deal

The Philippine Ports Authority is seeking bids for the contract to manage the Davao port for 20 years with a minimum concession fee of P8.7bn (exclusive of all taxes). The operator will also build the port’s physical landside infrastructure, which will cost P9.9bn. The deadline for bid submission is on May 5. The Davao Sasa port is currently being operated by ICTSI (ICT PM)

Contract technically expired in 2016 but has been renewed annually due to the delayed takeover of Chelsea Logistics. To recall, Dennis A. Uy-led Chelsea was awarded the original proponent status in 2019 for its unsolicited offer to modernize Davao’s Sasa port but this was eventually dropped last year. The port has a rated capacity of 500k TEUs and accounts for c. 1% of ICT’s volumes.

BOP reverses to a surplus in March

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$754mn in March. This reversed the deficits in Jan/Feb 2022 (c. US$130mn per month) and in March 2021 (US$73mn). The BSP cited inflows from the government’s US$2.25bn triple tranche US dollar-denominated bond offering. This brings 1Q22 BOP to a surplus of US$495mn, a turnaround from the US$2.84mn deficit in 1Q21. The BSP is targeting a BOP deficit of US$4.3bn by year-end, reversing from a US$1.345bn surplus in 2021.

 

Duterte vetoes bill requiring SIM card, social media account registration

President Rodrigo R. Duterte vetoed a bill that seeks to mandate the registration of all subscriber identity module (SIM) cards and social media accounts. President was “constrained to disagree” with the inclusion of social media in the measure. The inclusion of social media measure “needs a more thorough study”.

NCR, over 60 areas under Alert Level 1 until April 30

NCR will remain under Alert Level 1 from April 16 to April 30. Over 60 areas will also be under Alert Level 1. Under Alert Level 1, all establishments, persons, activities are allowed to operate at full capacity. Meanwhile, 44 areas are under Alert Level 2 until April 30. Under Alert Level 2, all establishments, persons, activities are allowed to operate at 50% capacity indoors, 70% outdoors.

Infrastructure spending drops in 2M22

Government disbursements on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) declined by 16% YoY to P90bn in 2M22. The drop was attributed to timing of payables for completed projects of the following agencies: agriculture, public works, transportation, and defense. The government did not provide similar figures for overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies). The Department of Budget and Management expects overall expenditures to improve in March but expects a slowdown in 2Q22 due to election ban. For FY22, the government is aiming to spend P1.27tn (+13% YoY) on overall infrastructure (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), or 5.9% of GDP.

CAB maintains fuel surcharge level for May 2022, pending review

Due to the pending review and evaluation, the Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) said the current passenger fuel surcharge will be maintained for May 2022. The current fuel surcharge (i.e. Level 4) reflects average jet kero prices (c. US$88/bbl) for December 2021/January 2022. This has been in place since March. Transportation Secretary Arthur Tugade, who chairs the CAB, previously said that he was opposed to the fuel surcharge. Fuel accounts for c. 40% of CEB’s cash opex and 30% of opex. Every 10% increase in our jet kero forecast (average of US$80/bbl) could reduce CEB’s EBITDA/profit by 4%/12%, holding other things constant. Regis expects CEB’s near-term margins to be squeezed, given the recent sharp spikes in fuel prices.

 

Government lowers vaccination targets.

The government lowered its end-June vaccination target to 77mn-80mn Filipinos from previous target of 90mn. The government also previously intended to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22. 


As of April 19, 67.1mn Filipinos (61% of the population) have been fully vaccinated against COVID while 12.7mn have received booster doses. The Philippines only administered 6.7mn doses in March (8.7mn doses were administered in February, 18.2mn jabs in January)
