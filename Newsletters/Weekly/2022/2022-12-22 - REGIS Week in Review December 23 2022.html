REGIS REPORT

22 December 2022

The Phils ended the week slightly higher at 6,541.03 (+0.7% WoW) on thin volumes. Average daily volume for the week dropped sharply to just $148.4m (-70% WoW) as most investors stayed on the sidelines ahead of the holidays. Trading was volatile amid lower liquidity.

Throughout the week, market activity was concentrated in PLDT (-16.78% WoW), with the stock taking up 15% of the week’s turnover. PLDT Management hosted a call last Wednesday to discuss issues surrounding its capex overspending. While they reiterated that there was no fraud, with the overspend confined over-ordering equipment/services, PLDT continued to tumble on both foreign and local selling. 

Meanwhile, Globe (+5.99% WoW) announced that it had just completed the closing of its tower sale to MIESCOR, taking in P1.9bn in cash.

Outside of telcos, the market was mixed. The top gainer was AGI (+9.35%) as the company aggressively bought back its shares, based on disclosures. As of today, the company has only repurchased Php5.7bn worth of shares out of the Php7b appropriated for the buy-back program.

Local funds likewise appeared to position ahead of next year’s PSEi rebalancing. DMC (+7.69% WoW) and UBP (+2.00% WoW) were up sharply, with both being the top candidates for inclusion. Conversely, Robinsons Land -3.47% WoW) and Megaworld (-0.47% WoW) saw selling pressure as they can potentially be deletions. 

On the macro front, BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said the regulator may consider cutting the banks’ deposit regulatory reserve requirement (RRR) by June 2023, although would like to see inflation to stabilize first.

Foreigners were sellers for US$25m this week. Top sold names were TEL (-US$33.6m), MONDE (-US$4.9m), and GTCAP (-US$4.6m).

Philippine Banks - BSP to resume RRR cuts in 2H23?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Important, not urgent, but definitely good for banks

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla says the regulator may consider resuming cuts to banks’ regulatory reserve requirement (RRR) by mid-2023. While the BSP has previously committed to bringing commercial banks’ RRR from 12% to under 10%, there doesn’t seem to be much urgency. The BSP is still in monetary tightening mode, and is unlikely to cut RRR until inflation cools materially. Also, banks are still quite flush, with system-wide LDRs at c72%. When it does happen, though, we estimate that every 100bps RRR cut could add roughly 1% to our earnings forecasts on a full-year basis.

BSP to consider RRR cuts, but not yet

Governor Medalla said he may consider resuming RRR cuts by June 2023. However, he also said the BSP will continue raising interest rates, and does not want to muddle the messaging with simultaneous tightening and loosening of monetary policy. RRR cuts will only likely happen once inflation begins cooling significantly and sustainably.

“Free money” for banks

For universal and commercial banks (UKBs), the 12% of PHP deposits held in reserve with the BSP does not earn any interest. Any reduction in the RRR will clearly be positive for banks’ profitability. Assuming simplistically that banks place the freed-up cash in fixed income instruments earning around policy rate (5.5% today), then we estimate that every 100bps cut in the RRR would result in about a 1% increase in full-year pre-tax profits for banks we cover.

 
PLDT - Budget blowout
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Capex budget overruns of as much as P48bn

After weeks of market speculation, PLDT released a press statement that it uncovered budget overruns of as much as P48bn in relation to capex over the last four years. The admission is a blow to the credibility of the company’s financial controls even if, as PLDT asserts, there’s no fraud involved. A management reorganization now looms. We may need to reassess our numbers and views.

How big is the issue?

The P48bn figure is 13% of PLDT’s capex over the last four years and represents 15% of its market cap on Friday. We expect some of the overspend to already be on PLDT’s books, though part of is still to be reflected as the acquired assets have yet to turned over.

Was there fraud?

PLDT asserts there’s no evidence of fraud, so far. Nonetheless, it appears there was a material failure of controls, leading to PLDT contracting equipment, materials and/or services beyond the aggregate amount approved by its board. Each individual project on a stand-alone basis appears to have been undertaken after approval from the concerned parties. It also appears, however, that the monitoring and tallying was not accomplished properly in aggregate, leading to the sum being greater than what the Board previously approved.

Will there be retroactive adjustments to financial statements?

There is no finding of missing assets and or hidden liabilities so far. Unless that changes, we believe there are likely to be only minimal retroactive adjustments (write-downs). Among the first activities of any forensic analysis would have involved a process of seeing if the assets paid for are actually there. The investigation is supposed to have begun in October. So far, there has been no evidence that PLDT paid for assets that were not delivered. PLDT is now undertaking a reconciliation of accounts with vendors and negotiating with them to reduce or defer spending.

What happens to earnings in future periods?

Management earnings guidance now appears to have been based on faulty estimates of its capex and PPE, going by the press release. Thus, earnings guidance will have to be lowered to reflect depreciation on the incremental P48bn in capex that does not seem to have been previously considered. Assuming a 9- to 10-year depreciation period, that means incremental depreciation of cP5bn a year, which is around P3.7bn after tax. PLDT may decide to offset the higher non-cash charges against the additional gains it is expected to report from the sale of its towers. The small consolation here is that this will not affect EBITDA.

 
BDO Unibank - Adjusting for 20% stock dividend
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Target price adjusted to P146/sh, maintain Buy

BDO issued a 20% stock dividend (ie bonus share issue), which went ex[1]div on 12 December. We adjust our share-based ratios (EPS, BVPS) accordingly, while forecasts (earnings, NIMs, loan growth, etc) remain unchanged. Our target price has been adjusted to P146/sh to reflect the new share count, with no change to our (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assumptions (ROE 14.0%, COE 12.0%, growth 8.0%). At our target price, the stock would trade at 1.5x P/bk and 11.7x PER based on our 2023E estimates. We maintain our Buy call on BDO, given the stock’s 36% potential total return.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco asks court to lift TRO on power supply agreement

 

Meralco has asked the Court of Appeals to junk an application of SMC Global Power unit South Premiere Power Corp (SPPC) for a writ of preliminary injunction. Meralco cited “the disruption of basic and essential services being rendered by SPPC…to the detriment of millions of Filipinos”. In November, the Court of Appeals granted a TRO in favor of SMC Global Power, which led to the cessation of a power supply agreement. SPPC stopped supplying to Meralco on December 7. Meralco has since been sourcing power from WESM.

Globe completes sale of another batch of telco towers

 

GLO disclosed that it received P1.9bn for the second closing of 159 towers to be acquired by MIESCOR Infrastructure Development Corp (MIDC). With the closing, 860 of 2,180 towers have been turned over to MIDC. Cumulatively, GLO has completed 34% out of the previously earmarked 7,059 towers to be sold, raising P30bn.

ICTSI signs new 30-year lease for container port in Poland

ICTSI and the Port Authority of Gdynia SA signed a new 30-year lease that will run until 2053. Our valuation assumes contract will end in 2043. Baltic Container Terminal (BCT) has an estimated handling capacity of 1.2mn TEUs. Regis estimates the port accounts for c. 1% of revenues/EBITDA. ICTSI trades at 12.3x FY23E PER and 7.6x FY23E EV/EBITDA, below historical valuations (22x PER/11x EV/EBITDA) and peer average (14x/12x).

PGOLD raises dividend payout policy

PGOLD raised its stated dividend policy to at least 30% of prior year’s net income from at least 15% previously. In the past five years, PGOLD has paid out 17-19% of prior year’s net income. The Board declared a dividend of P0.86/sh (payable on Jan 20, 2023). This is equivalent to ~25% of Regis FY22E net profit. The dividend implies a yield of 2.6% as of last closing price. PGOLD currently trades at 9x FY23E PE.

DMC and UBP could enter PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing

Based on our estimates, DMC is highly likely to enter the PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing. Regis expects this to lead to the removal of MEG. Meanwhile, we consider the likelihood of UBP entering the PSE Index to be moderate. This should lead to the removal of RLC from the index. The PSEi rebalancing in February will be based on the 12 months from January 2022 to December 2022. Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap.

 

Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when

1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or

2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 

 

Based on our estimates, DMC and UBP have reached the top 25 (as of 11M22)

In addition to DMC and UBP, we also expect relatively large inflow to SMC and EMI due to increases in their public float. In addition to the PSEi constituents to be removed, we also expect relatively large outflow from ALI due to the reduction in its public float. Regis estimates US$114mn in total flows (US$57mn in each direction).

 

Notes:

EMI disclosed that its public float increased to 20%. The SSS stake in UBP is about 19%. Regis assumes that the SSS stake in excess of two board seats (3.5%) will be treated as public float.

 

Philippine market foreign ownership inches up in November

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership rose 9bps MoM to 21.1% while PSEi foreign ownership also increased 4bps MoM to 25.3% (in November). The increase in foreign ownership was despite the 5.2-percentage point drop in EMI foreign ownership. If the EMI foreign ownership had remained unchanged, Regis estimates that both PSEi and total market foreign ownership would have risen by 21 basis points instead. Market net foreign buying reached US$99mn in November (from US$12mn of net foreign selling in October). 16 PSEi constituents saw increases in foreign ownership while 14 experienced declines.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

EMI – saw the biggest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 5.21 percentage points MoM to 23.2%. However, this appears inconsistent with trading data. We suspect that the 17.2 percentage point increase MoM in August 2022 was overstated.  

BPI – foreign ownership declined 42 basis points to 18.8%

BDO – saw the largest increase in foreign ownership, which rose 69 basis points MoM to 24.4%. It also experienced the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$68.7mn)

PGOLD – foreign ownership rose 53 basis points to 19.4%. Net foreign buying amounted to US$9.1mn.

 

 

 
 
MACROS NEWS
 
 
 
 

President Marcos extends tariff cuts on some commodities by one year

President Marcos approved the recommendation of NEDA to extend tariff cuts on pork, corn, rice, and coal until 31 December 2023.

In-quota imports of pork – 15% (from 30% previously)

Out-quota imports of pork – 25% (from 40% previously)

In-quota imports of rice – 35%

Out-quota imports of rice – 50%

In-quota imports of corn – 5% (from 35% previously)

Out-quota imports of corn – 15% (from 50% previously)

The zero tariff on coal will be applied beyond 2023

GIR revised up to US$95.1bn in end-Nov; BOP posts US$756mn deficit in November

The BSP revised the end-November 2022 GIR level to US$95.1bn from a previously reported figure of US$93.95bn. GIR rose by 1.1% MoM (c. +US$1.1bn) but down from end-2021 GIR level of US$108.79bn. This represents adequate external liquidity buffer of 7.2 months’ worth of imports and 5.8x short-term external debt. The balance of payments recorded a deficit of US$756mn in November, reversing the BOP surplus of US$711mn in October 2022. On a YoY basis, deficit was higher than the US$123mn deficit recorded in Nov 2021. This brings YT-November BOP deficit to US$7.9bn (BSP FY22E target at US$11.2bn, equivalent to 2.8% of GDP).

BSP to cut reserve ratio at end June 2023

The BSP plans to further reduce banks’ reserve requirement ratio before the end of the first half. The cut will coincide with monetary authority’s plan to wind down from tightening. It has pledged to cut the reserve requirement ratio to below 10% by 2023 from 12% currently. The BSP will keep raising interest rates in the next two meetings according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla. The BSP has raised key policy rates by 350bps so far this year to catch up with the Fed’s 425bps rate hike.

Budget deficit narrows to P124bn in November

The government’s budget deficit shrunk by 4% YoY to P124bn in November. Revenues grew 17% YoY, outpacing growth in expenditures of 10% YoY. Tax revenues jumped 16% YoY, 19% MoM, well ahead of target due to higher collections from the Bureau of Customs. Expenses rose 10% YoY, 17% MoM as primary spending accelerated, rising by 12% YoY, 21% MoM. Interest expenses dropped 16% YoY and 21% MoM. The YT-November budget deficit at P1.23tn, down by 7% YoY and tracking below the government’s target (P1.5tn deficit, 6.9% of GDP).
