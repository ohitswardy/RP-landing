REGIS REPORT

07 January 2022

It was a subdued opening week for 2022 as Omicron fears took over. The PSEi slid 1.6% WoW to close at the week at 7011.11.

The daily new COVID case counts reached 17k as of Thursday, up exponentially from less than 200 a few weeks ago and triggering fears of another round of tightening. The government had already raised the Alert Level at the capital to Level Three beginning Monday, effectively limiting capacity for establishments to 30% (from 50% under Level Two). It is also restricting movement for those who are unvaccinated within the capital. The government has subsequently raised restrictions to Alert Level Three in areas outside NCR.

Regardless of the lockdown level in place, Google Mobility data has shown a plunge in movement already, sparking renewed fears of the economic impact of further tightening.

The PSEi’s decline was broad-based, with 24 of the 30 index names ending the week lower.  Robinsons Retail (-16.7% WoW) fell on expectations of a potential deletion from the PSEi on the next rebal in February. AC Energy (-15.2% WoW) dropped 17%, with high growth stocks taking a breather of late. Banks Security Bank (-12.5% WoW) and Metrobank (-4.0% WoW) fell as well as property names Ayala Land (-7.8% WoW) and Robinsons Land (-6.5% WoW).

Index gainers meanwhile were just a handful, led by index names JG Summit (+6.0% WoW) and Aboitiz Equity (+4.1% WoW).  Defensives Meralco (+3.7% WoW) and PLDT (+2.1% WoW) rose as did large banks BDO (+1.9% WoW) and BPI (+0.4% WoW).

In the meantime, Semirara (10.8% WoW) rose sharply after the Indonesian government halted coal exports for the entire month of January. The move is expected to bolster demand and support coal prices regionally, benefiting coal miners like Semirara.

The announcement by Beyond Meat that it will supply its meatless chicken alternative to KFC in the US dampened sentiment on Monde’s (-1.2% WoW) alternative meat business Quorn, as investors have been hopeful for the latter to secure a similar deal.

On macro news, CPI inflation came  in at 3.6% for December, sharply down from 4.2% in November. While the full-year average of 4.5% for FY21 is above the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target range of 2% to 4%, the Dec print could embolden the BSP to keep rates lower for longer.

Foreigners started the year off sellers for US$6.9m. Biggest outflows were spread across Ayala names AC Energy, Ayala Land, and Ayala Corp (-0.3% WoW).

Average turnover was light  US$111m this week.  The  market traded only four sessions out of five as technical issues on Tuesday – with at least a third of PSE members unable to connect to the Exchange’s trading systems -- led to the cancellation of trading for the day.

Cebu Pacific cancels flights as Metro Manila shifts to Alert Level 3

Cebu Pacific announced the cancellation of 46 flights from January 4 to January 10, which includes flights to Boracay, CDO, Cebu, Davao, Puerto Princesa, and Tacloban. The company cited the intensified Alert Level 3 status of Metro Manila from Jan 3-Jan 15. As a preemptive measure, the airline is consolidating a number of its flights and has cancelled some flights. CEB operated 7,271 flights in 3Q21. CEB is trading at 5.2x FY22E EV/EBITDA.

PSE Index February 2022 REGIS Rebal Forecast: MONDE (+), EMP (+), BLOOM (-), RRHI (-)

Regis forecast's the addition of Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) and Emperador (EMP PM) into the PSE Index during the mid-Feb 2022 rebalancing. Among eligible stocks, MONDE and EMP are among the top 25 in terms of full market (based on 12-month VWAP). In turn, we expect the removal of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) and Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM). Among the existing PSEi constituents, BLOOM and RRHI have the lowest market cap (based on 12-month VWAP). Regis preliminary estimates suggest that the rebalancing will result in US$68mn of inflow to MONDE and US$37mn to EMP. Meanwhile, Regis estimates the rebalancing will result in outflows for all the existing PSEi constituents, particularly for RRHI (US$25mn) and BLOOM (US$18mn). Official announcements for the rebalancing results will be made by the PSE a few days trading days prior to implementation.

AC to acquire stakes in Singapore, Myanmar firms for $237.5mn

Ayala Corporation announced plans to acquire a 20% stake in Singapore-based Yoma Strategic Holdings Inc (YSH) and a 20% stake in First Myanmar Investment Public Co Ltd. (FMI). The total investment in these amounts to $237.5mn. Around $108.6mn of the $155mn in YSH was completed on December 2, 2019. The balance will be completed once approved by the Singapore Exchange. The investment in FMI of $82.5m through a convertible loan disbursed on Jan. 23, 2020. YSH and FMI will serve as Ayala’s main platform for strategic investments in Myanmar.

Shakey's Pizza - highlights from briefing re Potato Corner acquisition

Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM) management hosted a briefing to discuss its recent acquisition of Potato Corner (PC). Below are some highlights:

 

Margin accretive, fast-growing franchise model. PC has consistently been among the top 3 players in the chained kiosks segment for the past 5 years. Pre-2020, its store network was growing at 20% CAGR, primarily driven by its franchise model. Of the roughly 1k stores in the Phils currently, 80% are franchised. PC currently has close to 300 stores in 11 countries overseas, with a growing presence in Indo (>100 stores) and Thailand (70 stores).

 

Systemwide sales in 2021 was roughly USD40mn (P2bn), equivalent to c30% of PIZZA’s SWS. Pre-pandemic, PC’s EBITDA margin was in the mid-20s range vs PIZZA’s low-20s.

Acquisition valued at single-digit 2019 EV/EBITDA. While the acquisition price was not disclosed (due to NDAs), the transaction appears accretive. PC reported revenues of USD20mn (PHP1bn) in 2021, though this is still at 50-60% below pre-pandemic levels. PC was reportedly valued at single-digit 2019 EV/EBITDA, and below forward multiples for PIZZA (2022 consensus: 15x EV/EBITDA / 2.5x P/sales). This implies an EV of cP3bn for PC.

 

PIZZA plans to use a mix of internal cash and debt to fund the acquisition. The transaction is expected to close within 1Q22. New growth pillar + cost synergies. PC’s brand equity – among customers and franchisees – was among the key factors in PIZZA’s decision to acquire the brand. The brand provides PIZZA an opportunity to grow within another segment of the foodservice market, and management believes the brand is complementary to PIZZA’s current portfolio. Over the next 5 years, PIZZA is looking to grow PC’s revenues by 15-20% CAGR and expand EBITDA margin by over 400bps. Growth will come from store expansion (both here and abroad), scale improvements, and cost synergies (e.g. business development, R&D, franchise management, supply chain, and other shared services).

Typhoon, Omicron threat adversely affects CEB’s recovery in latter part of December

December rebound underwhelming.  Jefferies’ latest airline capacity survey showed Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) capacity reverted to just 44% of pre-Covid levels in December.  While capacity jumped to as high as 58k seats (c. 50% of pre-Covid levels) in the week of December 13, which is the week prior to Typhoon Odette (Rai), it significantly dropped to 51k/45k in subsequent weeks.  The typhoon disrupted airport operations in Visayas and northern Mindanao, including the country’s second largest airport, Cebu.  The threat of Omicron could have also adversely affected the recovery in the latter part of the month.  

 

In 4Q21, CEB’s capacity only reached 37% of pre-Covid levels, which looks below forecast of c. 40%.  Regis previously estimated that CEB would need to reach c. 40% of pre-Covid levels to break even on EBITDA, holding other things constant. A recovery in load factors and air fares will also be key to reach our forecast. 

       

Latest wave of Covid infections could derail recovery in the short term.  Stricter movement restrictions in Metro Manila due to the post-holiday exponential increase in Covid cases have led to flight cancellations this week and understandably heightened risks to our 2022 estimates.  Note that our estimates assume CEB’s capacity to average at 39% of pre-Covid levels in 1Q and gradually rise to 93% by year-end.  Regis believes it is critical for the latest wave of infections to subside by February so that they do not miss out on the peak summer holidays.

 

Regis maintains their Buy rating on a price target of P65/sh.  The stock is trading at 5.2x FY22E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers. 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

NCR under Alert Level 3 from Jan 3 to Jan 15

The Inter-Agency Task Force for the Management of Emerging Infectious Diseases (IATF-EID) decided to shift to a more stringent alert level status (Level 3) from Jan 3-15 due to rising Covid cases in Metro Manila. Under Level 3, establishments or activities shall be allowed to operate or be undertaken at a maximum of 30% indoor venue capacity for fully vaccinated individuals only and 50% outdoor venue capacity. Those below 18 years of age can go out for essential activities only; Individual outdoor exercises allowed for all ages regardless of comorbidities or vaccination status. Train capacity to remain at 70% but the government will undertake random antigen tests. Meanwhile, intrazonal and interzonal movement are still allowed, however, reasonable restrictions may be imposed by the LGUs. The daily case counts in Metro Manila (7-dma) surged to 960 from <100 a week prior while the nationwide case counts (7-dma) surged to 2061 from c.300 a week prior. Nationwide positivity rate up to 11% (7-dma) from 1% a week prior; Latest positivity rate at 21%.

Indonesia bans coal exports from Jan 1-31

The government of Indonesia banned coal exports in January due to concerns that low supplies at domestic power plants could lead to widespread blackouts. The widespread export ban may disrupt monthly coal production volumes of around 38-40 mn MT. Indonesia is the largest exporter of thermal coal in the world, accounting for c. 44% of seaborne thermal coal supply (or c. 400mn MT annually). Coal price futures slipped to US$158/MT on Jan 3 from US$170/MT the previous week but could rise again in the next few days/weeks.

Customs 2021 collection exceeds pre-pandemic levels

Based on preliminary data, the Bureau of Customs’ (BOC) revenue collection monitoring group showed end-2021 collections reached P645.8bn (+20% YoY). This is exceeding the agency’s year-end target of P616.8bn and was c. 2% better than 2019 collections of P630bn. The government cited 13 of 17 ports overshot its 2021 targets, including ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal. In December alone, the BOC collected P62.5bn, up 38% YoY and the highest level of collections for the year. This is also a significant improvement over its 2019 monthly average collection of P53bn. For 2022, the agency is confident to meet its collection target of P671bn (+4% YoY), as it expects imports to rise by +10% YoY.

Pace of vaccinations drops

As of January 2, the 7-day average vaccinations figure dropped to 491,545/day from 643,573/day the previous week. Around 46% of the Philippine population (49.9mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 69% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) had been fully vaccinated. Additionally, as of November 25, 77% of the Metro Manila population (10.4mn people) had received one dose. In Metro Manila, individuals who are not fully vaccinated are not permitted to leave their homes, except when purchasing essential goods. The Philippines has received a total of 210mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 109mn doses (as of January 2). The government is targeting to inoculate

77mn by end-1Q22 and 90mn by end-2Q22.

 

More areas placed under Alert Level 3

The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) has placed more areas under the heightened Alert Level 3 until Jan 15. Baguio City, Dagupan City, City of Santiago, Cagayan, Angeles City, Bataan, Olongapo City, Pampanga, Zambales, Batangas, Lucena, Naga City, Iloilo City, Lapu-Lapu City. Other locations that were escalated to heightened restrictions were Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Rizal, and Laguna. Under Level 3, establishments or activities shall be allowed to operate or be undertaken at a maximum of 30% indoor venue capacity for fully vaccinated individuals only and 50% outdoor venue capacity. Those below 18 years of age can go out for essential activities only; Individual outdoor exercises allowed for all ages regardless of comorbidities or vaccination status. Meanwhile, train capacity to remain at 70% but the government will undertake random antigen tests. Additionally, intrazonal and interzonal movement are still allowed, however, reasonable restrictions may be imposed by the LGUs. Fatalities have yet to rise, although its early days. ICU hospitalization rates remain low at 43% in NCR and 31% nationwide (vs peaks of 88%/79%).

Timing of unwinding of monetary policy remains uncertain

BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that the timing of the BSP’s exit from the current loose monetary policy will depend on economic outcomes rather than calendar dates. He said that COVID-19 continues to pose downside risk to both growth and inflation. The BSP’s strategy includes: returning to normal operations, unwinding liquidity provision, reducing monetary accommodation, and preparing for the next crisis.

Inflation eases to 3.6% in December

The country’s inflation rate eased further to 3.6% in December from 4.2% in November

Due to decelerating food prices (3.1% from 3.9%), specifically rice, vegetables, and fish.  Transport inflation also slowed to 6.1% from 8.8% on lower crude oil prices. This brings 2021 average inflation to 4.5%, within the government’s recently upgraded target range of 4.3%-4.5%. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno, however, expects inflation to accelerate over the near-term due to supply disruptions and agricultural damages from Typhoon Odette. The BSP expects inflation to ease close to its midpoint target range of 2-4% in 2022 and 2023.
