REGIS REPORT

27 May 2022

The PSEi consolidated this week, inching down 0.3% to close at 6726.14. Investors weighed the next Fed moves as well as China-US tensions.  On the ground however, all eyes were fixed on the new administration’s economic team appointments, which President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr bared this week.

The appointments have not been controversial, with the market buoyant amidst the announcements. The incoming Finance Secretary will be BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno.  Having served as Budget Secretary under the Duterte administration, he is expected provide some measure of continuity for current economic policies. Felipe Medalla, former NEDA Director General under the Estrada administration, will be appointed Governor of the BSP to take over Diokno’s current post.  Alfredo Pascual, a former president of UP who spent years in finance in the private sector, will be appointed Trade Secretary.  Manuel Bonoan, CEO of San Miguel’s infrastructure arm, will  be appointed Secretary of Public Works. Arsenio Balisacan will serve as Economic Planning Secretary (i.e. head of NEDA).  Balisacan is head of the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) and was also head of NEDA under the Aquino government.

Losers overwhelmed gainers 19:10. Conglos and banks dropped, with JG Summit (-4.55% WoW), SM (-0.8% WoW), BDO (-1.2% WoW) and BPI (-1.4% WoW) weighing on the market heavily. 

Converge (-5.5% WoW) was the top index loser this week. Private equity firm Warburg Pincus placed 3.8% (289m shares) of its remaining 10.2% stake in Converge to public investors.  It will also simultaneously sold 3.4% (260m shares) to the company itself. The residual 3.0% will be “distributed” to “certain parties as part of existing arrangements” within 30 days. The series of transactions will allow Converge to comply with the 30% minimum public float mandated by June 2022 under its franchise. 

Consumer stocks were also weak across the board though Monde (-4.4% WoW) was one of the top index gainers this week, on pre-positioning ahead of its MSCI upweight next week. Other gainers were AEV (+6.25% WoW), ICT (-6.0% WoW), and Ayala Corp (+3.1% WoW).

Foreigners were sellers for US$191m this week. Outflows though were bloated by the Converge placement which registered US$164m in outflows. Excluding the Converge transaction, foreigners would have been sellers for US$27m with the largest outflows in Converge, SM, Ayala Land, and Ayala Corp. Meanwhile, top foreign bought this week were Monde and SM Prime.

Megaworld - Confusion over tax bureau’s closure threat
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Confusion surrounding Megaworld’s tax issues

Congressman Jose Salceda said that the House Ways and Means Committee would hold a special meeting regarding the confusion over Megaworld’s recent tax issue. Neither the company nor the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has stated the amount of supposed tax liability or the land parcels of concern. However, it appears to involve Fort Bonifacio, the location of the most valuable portion of Megaworld’s land bank. While Megaworld insists that it has no outstanding tax liabilities, this issue could be an overhang for the stock until it is resolved with finality.

What’s going on?

Here is the sequence of events:

  • On the afternoon of May 17, The Manila Times website showed a document from the BIR’s Revenue Region No. 8B-South NCR (BIR RR-South NCR), inviting members of the press to Uptown Parade mall (in Megaworld’s Bonifacio Uptown Township) at 7:30AM the following day (May 18) for “Issuance of Closure Order Against Megaworld Corporation”.
  • Later that afternoon, the BIR released another media advisory that the scheduled activity on May 18 would be “held in abeyance until further notice” as representatives of Megaworld “manifested their full cooperation…”
  • On May 18, Megaworld’s disclosure to the PSE said that it is regularly audited by the Large Taxpayers Service of the BIR head office and has “no outstanding or unpaid past tax liabilities…”. The disclosure further stated that “…the matter has been clarified and resolved yesterday.”
  • On May 20, BIR RR-South NCR issued another press release, stating that it would still audit Megaworld and that the issue pertains to taxes related the sale/transfer of property by Megaworld in Taguig City, where Megaworld’s most valuable properties are located.
  • Later on May 20, the Secretary of Finance directed the BIR to suspend until further notice all audit-related activities of three task forces, including the one for real estate developers
  • Also on May 20, during an interview with CNN Philippines, Congressman Jose Salceda said that the House Ways and Means Committee would hold a special meeting on May 23 regarding the matter. His statements during the interview suggest that his main concern is the lack of coordination within the BIR.
 
Philippine Property - 1Q22 recap: malls on stronger footing; residential disappoints on cancellations
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Malls benefit from economic reopening

While economic reopening benefits the property sector in general, the pace of recovery of the three major segments is varied. Malls clearly benefitted from the economic reopening in 1Q22 and this segment should improve even further. However, things are not as rosy for the residential and office segments as office vacancy continued to climb while residential cancellations remained elevated.

Malls deliver; revenues to improve even further

Thanks to the reduction in rental concessions, aggregate mall revenue inched up 3% QoQ (+37% YoY), with mall operators generally hitting 50%-60% of pre[1]COVID levels. This is remarkable since (1) 4Q is seasonally stronger and, (2) 1Q22 was weighed by the hard lockdown in January. In April, tenant sales generally reached 80%-85% of pre-COVID levels. Thus, we expect mall revenue to continue improving throughout the year as rental concessions are gradually reduced further.

Residential cancellations continue; NPL ratio still near 15-year high

It is apparent that some residential buyers are still experiencing financial difficulties. Residential revenue of all developers underperformed our expectations as cancellations remained high in 1Q22. Meanwhile, the banking industry’s mortgage NPL ratio improved 20bps to 9.4% in 4Q21, still close to the 15-year high.

Aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover rose 14% QoQ (-2% YoY). For most developers, 1Q22 sales were 55%-70% of 2019 levels. Similar to previous quarters, the shift in demand in favor of horizontal projects (house and lot or lot only) in provincial areas (vs. Metro Manila condos) is still evident. Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) were generally flat QoQ. This means that prices of most units are still 10%-25% below 2019 levels.

Office vacancy climbs to 19%, rents fall further; WFH could delay recovery

Leechiu Property Consultants said that Metro Manila office vacancy climbed to 19% in 1Q22 (from 18% in 4Q21), largely driven by new supply. Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that rental rates fell another c. 3% QoQ and rents in most areas are now 20%-30% lower than 2019 levels. While the government directive mandating 70% of BPO employees return to the office has certainly increased foot traffic in offices, some BPOs have resisted. In fact, Concentrix, the country’s largest BPO firm, said it was willing to lose tax incentives in order to continue implementing work-from-home (WFH)/hybrid. (See: Work-from-home and the long-term demand for office space). Should most BPOs continue to implement WFH, then there will be a longer bottoming out process for the office property segment.

 
Ayala Corp - Notes from Regis CEO Series
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Peering ahead

Mr. Fernando Zobel, Ayala Corp CEO, was our guest again at our Regis CEO Roundtable, where he discussed the latest on the execution of the group’s strategic agenda as well as his upbeat outlook for the group while the country is getting ready for a transition to a new administration.

Carrying out the plan

Execution of the “Strategic Agenda” unveiled last year is underway. Regarding its value realization initiatives, Ayala Corp has raised over US$600m (out of its target US$1bn) from divestments so far, much of which has been deployed to buying back its own shares and Ayala Land’s. This, and the increased budget for capital investments group-wide, is reflective of Ayala’s outlook for the business environment.

Ready for regime shift

The looming change in the country’s leadership is at the top of the minds of many investors. Based on our discussion, the Ayala group appears comfortable with the impending transfer of power to a new Marcos administration. The incoming government has apparently held consultations with the business community, including with the Ayala group itself, to prepare for the transition.

Charting the growth path with new initiatives

The growth path of two of its newer businesses is being laid out. The Health Care business is poised to become profitable this year while the Logistics group is still bulking up with investments in new and related operations. Mr. Zobel seemed particularly excited about the potential stars in the making under 917Ventures, held by Globe and run by the same people behind GCash, the now dominant e-wallet business in the country.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Megaworld will still be audited by the BIR

Finance Assistant Secretary Dakila Napao said that the BIR’s Large Taxpayers Service would still audit Megaworld. BIR RR 8B-South NCR, which filed the complaint against Megaworld, said there were discrepancies regarding its unincorporated JV with BCDA. Regis suspects this refers to Bonifacio Uptown, which comprises c. 20% of MEG’s NAV (of P8.20/share). MEG is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY22E P/B, and 6.5X FY22E P/E.

 

Upson to raise up to P4.8bn in IPO

Upson Technology Corp, a retailer of computers and technology products, will raise as much as P4.9bn in an IPO and sell up to 888mn shares. 789mn primary shares and 99mn secondary shares. The offer price was set to be up to P5.50/share. UIC has outlet brands such as Octagon Computer Superstore, Micro Valley, and Gadget King. It will open 250 new stores over the next three to five years and currently has 183 branches. The 2021 net profit 95% to P404mn.

MWIDE 1Q22 losses widen YoY – Behind expectations

Megawide reported unreviewed 1Q22 net loss to common of P222mn, wider YoY and trending behind Regis (+P85mn) and street (+P622mn) FY22E estimates. The company will hold a briefing in the first week of June. Regis suspects the drag came from lower booked revenues/margins at the construction unit and lower revenues for the landport. The airport unit’s EBITDA improved 72% QoQ to P48mn, as pax volumes reverted to 21% of pre-pandemic levels (i.e. 2019 average), up slightly from 19% in 4Q21. Domestic pax volumes jumped 17% QoQ and reverted to 28% of pre-pandemic levels. International pax volumes dropped 14% QoQ and were about 6% of pre-pandemic levels (down from 7% in 4Q21). 1Q22 airport operations were likely badly hit by the Omicron surge. Receivable days increased further to over 380 days, with operating cash flow at a negative P2bn and end-1Q22 cash level depleted to P3.8bn. Net debt (inc. prefs) to equity rose to 5.2x from 4.8x in end-2021.

Converge buys back shares as Warburg Pincus exits

Warburg Pincus launched a placement for 289m shares (3.8%) of Converge ICT at P25/share. The investor also sold ~260m Converge shares (3.4%) to Converge itself at the same price. Warburg Pincus held 10% of Converge immediately prior to this sale of shares. The remaining Converge shares it holds will be distributed so it will no longer own shares in Converge. This series of transactions will allow Converge to comply with the minimum public float of 30% by June 2022 required by its franchise. Initially, float will rise to 31% and subsequently 34% after Warburg Pincus transfers the rest of its shares. The buyback of 260m shares will reduce Converge’s outstanding shares and market cap by 3.4%. Regis expects the buyback to be broadly EPS-neutral or marginally accretive on the first year.

Ayala Land and Federal Land also said to be under BIR audit

BIR RR 8B-South NCR’s Regional Director Eduardo Pagulayan Jr. said that in addition to Megaworld, two other property developers were being audited. The names of the two companies were not disclosed but Mr. Pagulayan said they had links to the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA). According to Bilyonaryo.com, these two developers are Ayala Land and Federal Land, which have agreements with BCDA for properties in Fort Bonifacio. Fort Bonifacio comprises c. 5% of Ayala Land’s NAV (of P55.88/share) and 17% of GTCAP’s market-based NAV. ALI is trading at 50% discount to NAV, 1.7X FY22E P/B, and 21.8X FY22E P/E. GTCAP is trading at an 18% discount to its market-based NAV (of P618/share).

 

AGI 1Q profit +52%YoY to P3.9bn

AGI reported a 1Q22 profit of P3.9bn (-20%QoQ, +52%YoY) – tracking behind estimates (vs P17.7bn consensus). The income in the quarter is 52% higher YoY, largely on account of higher contributions from MEG and Golden Arches and a smaller loss from Travellers. Travellers incurred a smaller loss of P207mn (vs -P1.1bn in 1Q21) as GGR increased 43%YoY to P6.5bn. Non-gaming revenues also jumped 46%YoY due to higher hotel occupancy rates. Meanwhile, GADC’s income jumped to P258mn (+253%YoY) on revenues of P7.2bn (+27%YoY). GPM for the quarter was slightly lower at 6.3% (vs 6.9%), but net margin was markedly higher at 3.6% (vs 1.3%). AGI is trading at 5.7x FY22E PE and a 68% discount to market-based NAV.

 

 

EMP 1Q22 profit hit P2.1bn (flat YoY, -23%QoQ) - behind

Emperador said in a limited press release that its net profit to owners amounted to P2.1bn in 1Q22. The figure is flat vs 1Q21 and down 23%QoQ – behind estimates. Profit appears to be tracking behind estimates for the year (cons P11bn). Revenues reached P12.3bn (+2%YoY, -30%QoQ), with the press release citing “challenges posed by global logistics and the Omicron variant”. The brandy segment’s profit contribution fell 12%YoY to P1.2bn, dragged by a 6.5% decline in revenues and 2.7ppt contraction in GPM. Without citing volumes, the disclosure logistics constraints and lockdown restrictions due to the Omicron wave. Recall in the previous briefing, management warned of cost pressures and logistics issues that have resulted in a number of SKUs not being available. Profit from whisky meanwhile grew 25% to P893mn. This was driven by a 20% jump in revenues. GP also grew 18% as margins were stable at c.40.3%.

Single malt sales continued to grow in “double digits” on back of “strong sales in Europe, Americas and APAC”. EMP is trading at 26x FY22E PE and EV/EBITDA of 19x.

Vista Land to raise as much P4.8bn from REIT IPO

Vista Land will spin off 10 malls and two PEZA-registered offices into a REIT with a GLA of 256,000sqm. The sale will involve up to 2.75bn secondary shares, which includes an overallotment option of 250mn shares. The offer price was set at P1.75/share which implies a 2023E dividend yield of 9.1% (based on prospectus) and implies a market cap of P13.1bn. The previous plan was to raise up to P9.2bn with up to 3.67bn shares and a price up to P2.50/share. The malls are located in: Las Pinas, Bacoor, General Trias, Imus, Tanza, Antipolo, San Jose Del Monte, San Fernando, and Talisay in Cebu. While the office buildings are in Taguig and Bacoor. As of 4Q21, Vista Land had total debt of P169bn and net debt of P107bn and a net debt/equity of 95%. Vista’s entire rental portfolio has a GLA of about 1mn sqm. Vista’s 2021 rental revenue jumped 29% YoY to P9.3bn, 20% higher than the 2019 figure. This is a contrast to other mall operators, whose mall revenues are about 60% lower than 2019 levels. Rental receivables grew 50% YoY to P19.4bn, 200% of trailing 12-month rental revenue. VLL is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, 4.1X FY22E P/E.

 

Airline regulator raises fuel surcharge to highest level

The Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) raised the passenger fuel surcharge to Level 7 for the month of June. This is up from Level 4 for the months of March to May. Under Level 7, fuel prices range from P39-42 per liter (vs. current jet kero spot of US$139/bbl or P46 per liter). Cebu Air (CEB PM) has been raising base fares to offset rising jet kero prices. Fuel accounts for c. 40% of CEB’s cash opex and 30% of opex. Every 10% increase in our jet kero forecast (average of US$105/bbl) could reduce CEB’s EBITDA/profit by 7%/12%, holding other things constant.

Marcos names more economic managers

President-elect Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr. bares his economic team:

Secretary of Finance – Current BSP Gov. Benjamin Diokno

BSP Governor – Felipe Medalla (will serve the rest of Diokno’s unexpired term that is scheduled to end in July 2023)

Dept. of Trade & Industry – Alfredo Pascual (former UP President and current independent director of SM, MWIDE, and CIC)

Dept. of Public Works – Manuel M. Bonoan (CEO of San Miguel Tollways Corp.)

BSP chief hints at another rate hike

The Monetary Board is likely to pursue a gradual tightening of the country’s monetary policy stance next month according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. The BSP raised policy rates by 25bps to 2.25% last May 19 and the next meeting is set on June 23.

Infra spend drops 3% in 1Q22

Infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 14% YoY to P100bn in March. This reverses the 16% YoY drop in 2M22. The government cited payments of completed projects of Dept. of Public Works, Dept. of National Defense, and Education. This brings 1Q22 infra spend down 3% YoY to P190bn. Overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in 1Q22 amounted to P253bn, up 4% YoY. The government is targeting to spend P1.27tn on infra this year (+13% YoY).

DoF urges next government to impose new taxes

Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III said in a press conference that the government could cover existing debt by borrowing more ore reducing spending by P249bn each year

Instead, he said the government has to raise revenues, improve tax administration and cut unnecessary spending. The DoF proposed three tax reform packages for 2023-2025.

Some of the items were:

Thee-year deferment of reduction of personal income tax under TRAIN, which will have an estimated average revenue impact of P97.7bn.

Expansion of VAT base and possible reduction in VAT rate.

Intensified implementation of income tax on social media influencers.

Mandatory casino admission charge at P3,500 and 5% tax on gross gaming revenues of electronic betting machines, which is expected to contribute P13.1bn annually.

Health tax reform that involves increasing taxes on cigarettes, e-cigarettes, alcopops and sweetened beverages estimated to bring in P91.4bn a year.

Increasing petroleum tax by P1/liter for a minimum of three years.

Excise tax on domestic coal and imported coal.

Tax on cryptocurrencies.

Tax on carbon emissions.

In total, the projected revenue impact of all packages combined is seen to be an average of P349bn a year between 2023 and 2027.

DBCC lowers growth target for 2022; Proposed 2023 budget at P5.268tn (+5% YoY)

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) revised its macroeconomic/fiscal program to account for domestic and external developments. It lowered the upper-end of its 2022 GDP growth target to +8% from +9% previously. It raised its 2022E inflation rate target range but kept 2023E/2024E. The fiscal deficit targets for 2023/2024 were also tweaked. Changes reflect 1Q22 GDP (+8.3%) and heightened external risks. The revised estimates assume 2022E Dubai crude oil prices of US$90-110 per barrel (from US$68-70 per barrel previously) and 2023E Dubai oil price of US$80-100 per barrel. The DBCC said next year’s proposed national budget is now at P5.268tn, up 5% from last year’s approved budget of P5.024tn. The 2023 budget accounts for 23% of GDP.

Rates for 10-yr bonds rise to 6.89%

Treasury rates for 10-year bonds jumped to almost 7% yesterday after the BoT issued all of the P35bn in reissued 10-year Treasury bonds with a remaining term of 9 years and 8 months. The auction marked the first time the Treasury made a full award since the May 9 elections. The 10-year T-bonds fetched a rate of 6.894%, up by 58.1bps from the last time they were offered in April. Bids for the securities ranged from a low of 6.625% to 6.95%. The yield also exceeded by 48.7bps the market pricing of 6.407% for the tenor. Last week, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas delivered its first rate hike, pushing the benchmark rate to 2.25% from a record low of 2.0%.

Marcos selects Arsenio Balisacan as NEDA chief

Presumptive President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. has selected Arsenio Balisacan as head of the National Economic Development Authority (NEDA). Balisacan was also NEDA chief during the administration of President Benigno Aquino III. Marcos’ chief-of-staff Atty. Vic Rodriguez confirmed that Balisacan has accepted the offer.
