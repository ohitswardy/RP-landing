REGIS REPORT

09 December 2022

The index ended higher, bouncing off its 6500 psychological support to close slightly above at 6,580.12 (+1.4% WoW). Local funds seem to be accumulating on dips and window dressing prior to the yearend may have helped lift the market in the past sessions. Foreign investors turned net sellers for the second straight week for -US$27.7M.

Foreign participation slightly declined to 50% of the market vs 58% the previous week (skewed by the MSCI rebalancing done by foreigners back then). Liquidity remained healthy though, with an ADV of US$215.2M (vs US$217.7M last week).

Outperformers for the week were large caps SM Investments (+3.16% WoW) and SMPH (+1.91% WoW). ICT (+4.91% WoW) gained heftily despite being one of the top sold names by foreign investors, registering an outflow of -US$12.5M.

November inflation figures came out at 8% (vs. est. 7.8%) driven by higher food prices due to tropical storm Paeng. Higher LPG prices likewise contributed to the print.

Monde Nissin (-5.55% WoW) dropped sharply as the company continues to lose market share and Quorn, its supposed growth driver, is seeing a stagnant meat alternatives market in the near-term.

Jollibee (-0.08% WoW) remained resilient as they are currently in talks to sell a partial stake (10%-15%) in coffee chain Highlands Coffee, at a valuation of USD 800mn (100%).  Highlands Coffee is seen to be doing an IPO in the future. It is not clear yet if the above partial sale will be in lieu of an IPO, or if it a sale to a private equity group that eventually will exit come IPO time (something that has been common in recent deals such as CNVRG, MONDE).

Semirara (+1.07% WoW) was strong too after the company confirms report that 2022 profit is likely over Php30bn amid high coal prices.

On the macro front, the Peso continues to strengthen (55.36 vs the dollar), helped by the seasonal influx of worker remittances and a USD that has been weakening as of late. Additionally, government debt is up 0.9% MoM to P13.64tn in end-October. The government targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and eventually decline to 51% by 2028.

ACEN Corp - Starting over; raising TP to P8.8 (+10%) and upgrading to BUY
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Risks factored in, valuations reflect growth

ACEN Corp (ACEN PM), along with a few other “concept” plays, bore the brunt of the market sell-off following the global increase in interest rates. While we believe valuations were once daunting, buoyed by the mania surrounding renewable energy, we think the risks that underpinned our previously cautious call on the name have now been discounted by the market. Expectations have been reset, and the pressure to execute perfectly has dissipated. We upgrade ACEN to Buy with a revised target price of P8.8 (+10%). The stock has corrected by more than 40% from its all-time peak, and we believe the risk-reward has now turned favorable.

Transformation is moderating

The transformation of ACEN continues, albeit at a more gradual pace following the infusion of its parent company’s international assets last year. Over the past twelve months, it upped its stakes in Northwind, NLREC, Pagudpud and New England Solar. It has signed agreements with multiple foreign RE developers to explore projects worth as much as 18GW as part of its long-term pipeline. As we incorporate projects that are about to be completed in our forecasts, we adjust our 23-24 EPS estimates by 14-59%. Our estimates now imply a CAGR of 49% over FY22 to FY24E.

Downside from fixed price PSA, contracted capacity mismatch minimized

In the short term, ACEN is not necessarily out of the woods just yet. Over the next three quarters, SLTEC could remain exposed to a mismatch between 1) high fuel cost and a fixed rate PSA, and 2) an over-contracted position. However, ACEN has already taken steps to cap the downside by securing both fuel and replacement power contracts.

Target of P8.8/share at 18x FY23E EBITDA; risks

We adjust our target price higher by 10% (to P8.8), setting our target multiple at 18x EV/EBITDA (mean of international comps), and applying it to ACEN’s FY23E EBITDA. At P8.8/sh, ACEN would trade at 40x PE and an implied EV per MW of $2.1mn/MW. Key risks relate to the successful execution of plans, merchant exposure, developments in technology and regulation.

 
Monde Nissin - A rough patch
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Maintaining Hold

We reduce our target price (-7% to P13/share) but maintain our Hold rating for MONDE after cutting our 2022-23E estimates by as much as 20%. While MONDE is likely to see profits rebound in 2023E (+18% EPS gr) on lower input prices (e.g. wheat, palm oil), it should continue to face competitive pressure on multiple fronts over the near term. In the Philippines, MONDE has been losing market share in major categories (instant noodles, biscuits), a situation aggravated by food safety issues on instant noodles this year. Meanwhile, Quorn continues to navigate rough waters in hopes of gaining a bigger slice of what now appears to be a shrinking pie. We forecast modest +7% EPS CAGR (2022-24E) to be largely driven by a margin recovery next year but expect more muted EPS growth beyond that. The stock currently trades at 23x 2023E PE, with minimal upside risk to earnings, in our view.

APAC BFB: Competitive pressures abound even as cost headwinds temper

We believe MONDE’s APAC BFB is likely to see some much-needed margin relief in 2023E as prices of key inputs (wheat, palm oil) recede from the 1H22 highs. However, we believe there is limited scope for faster earnings growth beyond 2023E given the shifting competitive dynamics within the instant noodle category, where MONDE has been losing market share to Universal Robina (URC PM) over the past few years. This market share decline was exacerbated by the food safety issue in 3Q22, which MONDE is just recovering from.

Quorn: Light at the end of the tunnel?

The meat alternatives market has stagnated after seeing explosive growth over the past few years, as the industry faces lower-than-expected conversion rates, with the situation compounded by intensifying competition. Given the challenging environment, management has signaled plans to rationalize the organization and shift its strategy in the US to plug losses and improve profitability. While we see this as a positive move, we remain cautious on the medium-term growth outlook for the industry itself.

Cutting estimates and TP; maintaining Hold

We cut our 2022-23E estimates by as much as 20% mainly on lower margin assumptions and we consequently reduce our SOTP-based TP to P13/sh (-7%). Our SOTP uses a DCF valuation for the APAC BFB business, implying 24x 2023E PE, broadly in line with its peers. We also ascribe a 0.5x 2023E EV/Sales multiple for Quorn, which is a discount to the current EV/Sales multiple of 4x for Beyond Meat (BYND) based on Bloomberg. Key upside/downside risks relate to commodity price inflation, forex, and competition.

 
Philippine Property - 3Q22 recap: residential sales rebound; malls almost back to pre-COVID levels
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment provides a pleasant surprise

The rebound in residential reservation sales was eye-catching. Nearly every listed developer showed remarkable jumps, both QoQ and YoY. Moreover, other positive trends were evident within the segment, such as stabilizing condominium prices and lower cancellations/nonperforming mortgages. Meanwhile, office rental rates dropped further while mall revenue climbed with the removal of rental concessions.

Residential sales rebound; condo prices stabilize; mortgage NPL ratio drops

There were a number of positive trends in the residential segment in 3Q22. Most notably, reservation sales jumped, thanks to international sales. In addition, condominium prices were stable while NPLs and cancellations dropped. Reservation sales of nearly every developer showed both QoQ and YoY increases in 3Q22. Aggregate reservation sales climbed 35% YoY (+9% QoQ), representing 90% of 2019 levels. Sales to OFWs and foreigners, particularly Americans, jumped in 3Q22, likely supported by the weak peso. Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums in the secondary market were broadly flat for the fourth consecutive quarter. This means that prices of most units are still 10%-25% below 2019 levels.

Meanwhile, the banking sector’s mortgage NPL ratio has also begun moving in the right direction, dropping to 8.6% in 2Q22 (from 9.4% in 1Q22). Despite the 4% QoQ increase in total mortgages, nonperforming mortgages fell 4%. Furthermore, the 10% QoQ (+15% YoY) increase in residential revenue suggests that cancellations have likely come down. Given the gradual reopening of the economy, we believe that cancellations peaked in 2Q22.

Mall rental concessions removed; rental revenue back to pre-COVID levels

Following the removal of rental concessions, mall revenue of the top 3 operators climbed 8% QoQ (+153% YoY) to about 90% of pre-COVID levels. Although rental revenue from tenants is back to pre-COVID levels, cinema operations remain weak due to a lack of blockbuster movies.

Office vacancy flat but rents continue to fall

Leechiu Property Consultants estimates that Metro Manila office vacancy was flat QoQ at 18%, even as office take-up remained net positive for the third consecutive quarter. However, Colliers estimates that rental rates (on new contracts) still fell another c. 2% QoQ in most areas and are now about 20%-50% lower than 2019 levels (40%-50% below 2019 levels in the Manila Bay area).

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Semirara sets higher 2023 capex of P5.6bn

Semirara is setting aside P5.6bn for its capex projects in 2023, up by 8% from P5.1bn (+31% YoY) in 2022. P4.1bn will be allocated for mining equipment refleeting  and P1.5bn will be for maintenance activities for its power plants. This is in line with Regis estimates (P5.6bn). As of end September, SCC has spent P3.6bn, flat YoY.

D&L moves start of commercial ops of Batangas plant to mid-2023

D&L Industries disclosed that it has moved the start of commercial operations of its new Batangas plant to mid-2023 from early 2023 due to delayed permits and shipments. The company cited delays in processing permits and certifications, as well as in the arrival of shipments from overseas suppliers. PEZA has granted an extension until the end of 2023, providing allowances in case of fore majeure. The plant will sit on a 26-hectare property in First Industry Township – Special Economic Zone in Batangas. The first phase will cover 16 hectares of the PEZA area.

 

 

 

SMC plants stop supplying Meralco

San Miguel Corp is walking away from its contract to supply electricity to Manila Electric Co (Meralco) under its Ilijan power supply agreement, citing mounting losses caused by the surge in fuel costs that it could not recover through power rates. SMC Global Power Holdings Corp notified Meralco that it would no longer supply Meralco the 670MW under the Ilijan PSA effective December 7, 2022. The ERC meanwhile has not received any notice from Meralco yet. At the same time, there are reports that Meralco is attempting to woo First Gen to the divert the use of its natural gas allocation from Malampaya to the 1,200MW Ilijan gas-fired power plant of San Miguel Coro. Under the offer, Meralco will be the one to procure the fuel for the gas-fired power plant. Meralco first vice president and head of regulatory management Jose Valles said that Energy Sec. Lotilla wants the 1,200MW to remain operational in the grid “so the only way to do it is to source fuel for it”. The mechanics of the proposal would have to be agreed upon first by Meralco, SMC and First Gen, and approved by the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).

Cebu Air to expand NEO fleet in 2023; announces leadership changes

Cebu Air (CEB PM) will take delivery of 11 NEO aircraft in 2023 to replace 10-11 CEOs/ATR exits. The NEOs will account for 56% of CEB’s fleet in 2023 from 43% in 2022. Fleet guidance is flattish, with projected 73 aircraft in 2023. As of end-September 2022, CEB’s fleet size stood at 77. CEB committed capex of P53bn in 2023, up from P33bn in 2022. Net capex (i.e. less proceeds from leasebacks) will be c. P20bn, up from P4bn in 2022. Regis estimates net capex of P32bn in 2023 from P4bn in 2022. CEB’s net debt to equity stood at 5.02x in end-September 2022, assuming conversion of CBs. CEB has restored 92% of its pre-pandemic capacity to date, with > 100% of domestic capacity reinstated but only 68% of international capacity. It expects to break even in 4Q22 and to reach profitability in 2023.

CEB also announced succession planning:

Lance Gokongwei will be Chairman (previously President & CEO)

Michael Szucs will be CEO (previously Chief Executive Adviser)

Alexander Lao will be President (from Chief Commercial Officer)

Appointments will become effective by Jan 1, 2023

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

GIR dips to US$93.95bn in end-November

Gross international reserves (GIR) stood at US$93.95bn in end-November, down 0.08% MoM (US$73.9mn). Reflected mainly the government’s payments of its foreign currency debt obligations and the BSP’s net foreign exchange operations. This is equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods.

Government debt up 0.9% MoM to P13.64tn in end-October

The National Government’s outstanding debt rose by 0.9% MoM (+P124bn) to P13.64tn in end-October. This is 16% higher than in end-Dec 2021 (P11.7tn) and equivalent to 62.5% of GDP (Nominal GDP 2022E: P21.8tn). Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% of total debt and was up +0.6% MoM and 15% higher than in end-Dec 2021. Meanwhile, external debt accounted for 31.4% of total debt and was up +1.6% MoM and +20% higher than in end-Dec 2021. In USD terms, external loans stood at US$73.8bn (+2.7% MoM and +5.8% higher than in end-Dec 2021). The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually go down to 51% by 2028.

Proposed Philippine sovereign wealth fund will not receive capital from pension funds

Representative Stella Quimbo, senior vice chair of the House appropriations panel, said that the proposed Maharlika Wealth Fund will not receive capital from Social Security System (SSS) or Government Service Insurance System (GSIS). The original proposal was for GSIS and SSS to contribute a combined P175bn out of the Maharlika Wealth Fund’s P275bn. Instead, Representative Quimbo said that funds will be sourced from the profits of BSP.

 

Inflation quickens to 8.0% in November

Headline inflation rose by 8.0% YoY, 0.9% MoM in November. Consensus was expecting 7.8% YoY and the BSP had a range of 7.4%-8.2% YoY. Prices of food (i.e. rice, fish, fruits, vegetables, and sugar) picked up due to the severe tropical storm Paeng. The full-month effect of the transport fare hike, as well as higher LPG prices, contributed to the higher inflation print. Fuel prices (diesel and gasoline) also rose 0.8% MoM. In PHP terms, regional prices of gasoline/diesel dropped 2%/8% MoM but impact on pump prices may have lagged. Core inflation (ex- food and fuel) accelerated to 6.5% in November from 5.9% in October. The YT-November inflation average at 5.6%. Assuming MoM trends slow to 0.5% in December, inflation for December 2022 will likely be slightly higher at +8.1%. Jefferies expects the Fed to raise policy rates by another 50bps this month (Dec 14). Regis expects the same rate hike in the next BSP policy meeting (Dec 15).

Full award of T-bond as rates drop

The Bureau of Treasury made a full award of P35bn worth of re-issued 25-year T-bonds, with a remaining life of 11 years and 11 months. T-bonds fetched an average rate of 7.189%, lower than the 8.168% yield when it was reissued in November and lower than the original coupon rate of 9.25% set in Nov 2009. The yield is slightly higher than BVAL reference rate of 7.179%. The  gov’t cited expectations of inflation peaking and BSP’s slowing pace of rate hikes. Auction attracted total tenders of P79.4bn, more than 3x oversubscribed. The National Treasurer Rosalia de Leon said the government may prefer longer tenors in the borrowing program in the future if the market pricing is good.

 

Government trims 2023 growth target

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) downgraded its 2023 GDP growth target to 6-7% from 6.5%-8% previously. The gov’t cited headwinds such as slowdown in major advanced economies, amidst sustained strength in domestic demand. GDP is expected to moderate from this year’s target growth rates of 6.5%-7.5% (9M22A: +7.7%). Regis expects 2022/2023 GDP of 6%/6% while Bloomberg consensus expects GDP to grow by 6.9% in 2022 and 5.5% in 2023. For 2024-2028, DBCC kept its growth targets of 6.5%-8% to reflect impact of government strategies and the Philippine Dev’t Plan.

Congress ratifies 2023 budget

The Bicameral Conference Committee ratified the proposed P5.268tn national budget for 2023

Under the consolidated version, the Bicam restored the P150mn confidential and intelligence funds of the Department of Education, among other things

Key changes were:

Education sector budget raised to P900.9bn, up from the Senate’s proposal of P884.6bn

Infrastructure budgets for the Department of Justice and Department of Interior and Local Government  were also raised (but were not disclosed)

Budget for the Energy Regulatory Commission was increased by P453mn to support its capital outlay

An additional P10bn was given to the Department of Public Works and Highways to address delays in project implementation and to repair school buildings and bridges

Budget could be signed into law before Congress goes on Christmas break on Dec 17
