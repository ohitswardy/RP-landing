REGIS REPORT

24 June 2022

The PSEi dropped for a third week as US recession fears pushed markets lower.  The Phils fell 1.8% to close at 6217.56. This took its YTD drop to 12.7%, lagging its ASEAN neighbors (which are -7% to +10% YTD). Turnover was extremely thin, averaging a mere US$80m daily. 

Losses were across the board, with 21 of the 30 index members closing lower this week. 

Telcos were also weak, with PLDT (-8.8% WoW) and  Converge (-8.2% WoW) among the week’s top losers. Globe (-3.15% WoW) also fell.

Globe is planning to raise up to P32bn in a rights issue. At the maximum amount to be raised (P32bn), Globe’s net gearing is estimated to drop to 2.0x, from 3.0x (on Regis FY22E estimates). Given the amount to be raised, actual dilution to EPS and valuation is not likely to be large.

Meanwhile, Globe is employing a new pricing strategy, effectively lower the entry level price points for its broadband service plans (P899/month as well as a P1,299/month fiber package) . This is likely to usher in lower pricing from competitors as well.  

Financials were also lower, with BPI (-7.2% WoW), SM Prime (-3.7% WoW), Ayala Land (-3.4% WoW), BDO (-2.6% WoW), Security Bank (-2.4% WoW) also down.

Consumer names Monde (-3.9% WoW) and URC (-2.4% WoW) were also weak. ICTSI (-5.1% WoW) likewise fell.

Emperador (+10.1% WoW) led the gainers this week.  The company received an update on its application (eligibility-to-list (ETL) letter) and is taking steps to comply with the requirements in order to have a secondary listing in Singapore.

Other gainers were AEV (+5.9% WoW), Jollibee (+4.2%  WoW) and SM (+2.1% WoW).

Wilcon (+0.9% WoW) also gained despite news of what could be a longer term threat to the competitive landscape.  Cosco Capital (+0.2% WoW) announced a joint venture with Thai firm Siam Global (GLOBAL TB), a leading retailer of home improvement and building materials in Thailand. The JV will begin with 3-5 stores.

Coming as no surprise, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised its policy rate (overnight RRP) by 25bp to 2.50% at  the  Monetary Board (MB) meeting held this week. YTD, the policy rate in the country has been raised by +50bp, at a pace slower than the rate hikes of +150bp done by the Fed. This has led to PHP weakness, with the local currency falling 7% YTD, and 2% this week alone. Pressure on the PHP should persist, as the BSP expects a current account deficit of US$19.1bn (4.6% of GDP) for the year. 

Foreigners continued to be net sellers in the Phils with another US$36.5m exiting this week. Biggest outflows were in big cap banks and property.

RESEARCH
Philippine Telecoms - Unlocked value
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Upgrading Globe to Buy and raising our target price for PLDT

We have turned more positive about the outlook of the two incumbent telcos. The sale of half of PLDT’s tower network not only unlocked previously overlooked value for itself but will likely spur Globe to do the same. At the same time, the diminished threat from the third mobile operator allows us to be more upbeat about the incumbents’ prospects. We lift our target prices for both Globe and PLDT and raise our rating for the former to Buy.

Threat from third mobile operator recedes

The threat from the third mobile operator has diminished, in our view. While there is a new law lifting foreign ownership limits on telecom firms, it also explicitly disallows foreign state-owned entities from investing any more capital. With funding from China Telecom likely to be curtailed, the ability of the third operator to compete will be in doubt.

Tower sale unlocks overlooked value

The announced sale of half of PLDT’s ~12,000 towers for a princely P77bn (>20% of market cap at the time) unlocked a lot of value. This is especially so given the 10-year leaseback arrangement which kept PLDT whole on an EBIT basis. Globe has, unsurprisingly, made plans to undertake a similar transaction.

Broadband will still be growth engine

We expect growth for wired broadband to continue industrywide. The easy wins from the “land grab” phase of this business may be over, but subscriber and revenue growth can continue as the operators aim to capture the lower income families.

Valuations lifted

We raise our target price for PLDT to P2,300 (+35%), driven by the tower sale as well as improved prospects for its core business. Our TP for Globe has been raised to P2,800 (+4%) but this has yet to include upside from any tower sale, which could add over P400/share if done at identical valuations as PLDT’s. We reiterate our Buy rating for PLDT and upgrade Globe to Buy.

 
GT Capital - NAV discount at its widest; Maintain Buy
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Earnings recovery in-full-swing

GTCAP’s key businesses (auto, banking and property) are highly geared to a recovering economy. We expect profitability to improve, despite headwinds from higher interest rates and a stronger USD. Its shares have underperformed the PCOMP by 12% over the past year and are now trading at its widest discount to market NAV of 47%. Furthermore, it's now on a mere 7x PER, which we reckon is unjustifiably low given the quality of its businesses. We adjust our TP to P890 (+18%) and reiterate our BUY rating.

Proactive expansion over the pandemic

Its auto unit’s market share improved over the course of the pandemic, aided in part by support from its financing arm Toyota Financial Services (TFS) as regular banks cut back on consumer lending. Its commanding c. 50% market share today (vs. 40% in 2019) has so far been sustained and has also allowed the company to raise prices gradually to address margin pressures. Meanwhile, its property unit is poised to benefit from the impending completion of critical infrastructure around areas where its land bank is located.

2021-2024E EPS CAGR of +24%

We refresh our profit forecasts to reflect better-than-expected auto sales. Our revised estimates suggest 3-year EPS CAGR of +24%, faster than its pre[1]pandemic annual growth rate (+12%). We are also optimistic about the company’s initiatives in the used car market, which are still in the early stages of development but could potentially be huge long term.

Raising SOTP NAV to P1,046/sh (+32%)

We raise our SOTP NAV by 32% to P1,046/sh after (1) upgrading vehicle sales forecasts, (2) raising estimated land values, and (3) incorporating lower tax rates. We also roll forward our valuation to 2022. Our revised TP of P890/sh assumes a larger holding company discount of 15% (from 5% previously). Key risks relate to interest rates and exchange rates, credit risks for its bank, and changes to its auto JV.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Globe introduces new, lower priced broadband plans

Globe introduced new broadband service plans that cut entry level pricing. The lowest new plan now costs P899 (US$17) per month but with a data cap of 300GB. The next lowest one costs P1,299, for 50Mbps with no data cap. The speeds were also increased for higher priced package. This is a departure from previous tweaks that only raised speeds but did not lower monthly pricing. Regis believes the industry will need to reduce entry level price in order to tap lower income subscribers. The result is that monthly pricing will likely decline across the industry.

Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) announces JV with Thai-based home improvement retailer

In a disclosure, COSCO announced it has signed a JV with Thai-based Siam Global House (GLOBAL TB). Siam Global is a leading distributor/retailer of building materials and home improvement in Thailand with 76 stores. The JV will be 55-45 in favor of Siam Global; COSCO will own 45%. Initial investment for the JV company will be P500mn to P1bn. They are looking to open 3-5 stores within the next 12-18 months. The stores will be initially located in NCR, CALABRZON and Region 3. There is no indication yet on what the capex per store will be, but merchandise will focus on building materials including steel bars (but not gravel and sand). So this likely means that the store footprint will be big (i.e. depot-type). It will likely be a direct competitor to Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM). Overall, the JV’s plans don’t appear to be very aggressive (for now) at just 3-5 stores initially. For reference, WLCON is opening 8 Depots in 2022 on a base of 74 stores as of end-2021. COSCO trades at a 64% discount to our market-based NAV estimate of P11.91/sh.

ERC fines AP unit for prolonged downtime

The ERC fined Hedcor Bukidnon, a unit of Aboitiz Power, P8.38mn for exceeding the maximum number of days of unplanned outage. It went on unplanned outage for 102 days in 2021 (vs the allowed 6.8 days).

Razon’s Prime Infra files for P28bn IPO

Razon-led Prime Infrastructure will sell 1.76bn common shares at a price of P14.60/sh. This plus an overallotment option of 175.6mn shares will allow the company to raise P28bn in net proceeds. The proceeds will largely go to its energy business (P23bn) while P3.5bn will go to its water investments; Remaining P1.1bn will be spent on waste and sustainable fuels. The IPO is targeted to happen from October 3-7; Listing target date “on or about” October 12. Prime Infra is developing 140 MW solar power plant in Tanauan, Batangas. The first 70 MW of Batangas solar plant is expected to be completed in the 1H24, with the second phase comprising 70MW is expected in 2H24.

Airline regulator expands fuel surcharge matrix; CEB restores close to 80% of systemwide seat capacity in June.

The Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) expanded the passenger fuel surcharge matrix to up to Level 20 from the previous maximum Level 7. CAB also shortened its evaluation period to 1 month from two months previously. CAB also raised the fuel surcharge to Level 11 from Level 7 for the month of July. Fuel surcharge for domestic market will range P355-P1,038 from P219-739 previously. Regis estimates the current fuel surcharge level reflects jet kero spot price of c. US$160/bbl and PHP:USD spot rates. Fuel surcharge has increased more than 3x since the start of the year (i.e. we started at Level 4). Fuel surcharges accounted for 13% (or P201 per pax) of Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) overall pax fares (P1,543 per pax) in 1Q22. Meanwhile, Jefferies’ airline capacity data showed that CEB has restored c. 80% of its pre-pandemic systemwide seat capacity in the first two weeks of June, up from 77% in May.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Marcos names new BIR chief

President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has tapped BSP Assistant Governor and former Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Deputy Commissioner Lillia Guillermo to head BIR. Ms. Guillermo heads the BSP Technology and Digital Innovation Office and is in charge of managing the BSP’s IT modernization roadmap. She was also credited with implementing the Philippines tax computerization project at the BIR and BOC.

Luzon placed under red alert causing power outages

The National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) raised a red alert in Luzon on Saturday due to insufficient power supply. They cited a decrease of an approx. 1,200 MW in Meralco’s load affecting 1.6mn customers. This was due to tripping of Hermosa-Limay 230kv lines, disconnecting two Bataan power plants from the grid. The red alert was triggered at around 5pm but alert levels were eased to :yellow” at 11pm. It is unclear at this stage if the issue was resolved.

Sugar prices jump

Based on data from the Sugar Regulatory Admin, wholesale prices of raw sugar have jumped (+48% YTD) to ~P2700/50kg bag as of the week ending June 17, surpassing previous highs of P2100-2200/50kg bag in mid-2018. Consumer companies that are exposed to beverages, bakery, and confectionery will be the most affected by an increase in sugar prices. For MONDE, biscuits account for ~30% of APAC BFB’s revenues. Sugar is a major raw material for its APAC BFB business, accounting to ~10% of the segment’s raw material cost. Meanwhile for URC, beverages (coffee, tea), confectionery, and baked goods account for c70% of the domestic BCF segment. Sugar accounts for 8-10% of the segment’s total raw material cost. Note, however, that URC is a net seller of sugar through its Sugar and Renewables (SURE) segment. Historically, the SURE segment outperforms when sugar prices are high. This supports consolidated margins by cushioning compression at the BCF segment. This segment accounted for 16% of URC’s consolidated revenue and 36% of EBIT in 1Q22.

Rates for 10-year T-bonds hit 7.1%

The Bureau of Treasury raised P34.9bn through 10-year bonds and fetched a coupon rate of 7.25% and a rate of 7.145% (vs. BVAL: 6.944%). Demand reached P67.3bn, oversubscribing the offer by almost 2x.

BOP posts US$1.6bn deficit in May 2022

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$1.61bn in May 2022. This is larger than the deficits recorded in April 2022 (US$415mn) and in May 2021 (US$1.4bn). The government cited outflows from NG debt payments. This brings YT-May BOP deficit to US$1.53bn, lower than the US$1.63bn deficit in the same period last year.

Infra spend drops MoM in April

Infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) reached P63.8bn in April, down 36% MoM but up 9.6% YoY. Infra spend in April largely towards Department of Agriculture’s farm equipment and machineries and the Dept. of Education’s basic education facilities. The MoM drop was due to the election ban. This brings 4M22 infra spend relatively unchanged YoY at P254bn. Overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in 4M22 amounted to P332bn, up 3% YoY. This implies infra spend in April only rose by 1% YoY to P79bn. The government is targeting to spend P1.27tn on infrastructure this year (+13% YoY).

Marcos names himself agriculture chief

President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr. appointed himself agriculture secretary “at least for now”. He wants to review or restructure the Department of Agriculture; many sub-agencies have changed their function over the years. The incoming leader has pledged to increase farm output through loans and modern machinery. He also said that he wants to invest more for farmers by using funds collected from the rice tariffication law. Other presidents that assumed concurrent roles in the cabinet were Gloria Arroyo (Defense), Joseph Estrada (Interior), Ferdinand Marcos (Defense). Marcos Jr. also indicated that the Philippines must export processed metals instead of raw ore. He reiterated that mining should be “environmentally neutral”. Additionally, he favors targeted subsidies to sectors affected by fuel price hikes, instead of suspending oil excise tax. Marcos is yet to name his health, foreign affairs, transportation, energy, and environment secretaries.

Monetary Board hikes policy rates by 25bps; Raises inflation forecasts

The Monetary Board decided to raise the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight reverse repurchase facility by 25bps to 2.5%. This is within street expectations. The policy rate has risen by 50bps so far this year (vs. YTD for Fed: +150bps). The policy rate is still 150bps lower than it was prior to the pandemic (4%). BSP Governor Diokno said that inflation expectations continue to rise. It raised its 2023 inflation rate forecast to 4.2% from 3.9% previously. The 2022 inflation forecast kept at 5%; BSP forecasting inflation to average 5.6% in 2H22. Incoming BSP Governor and current Monetary Board member Felipe Medalla previously signaled another 25bps hike in August. Further rate hikes beyond this will be data-dependent.

 

Marcos appoints ex-PAL president Jaime Bautista as Secretary of Transportation

President-elect Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. picked former Philippine Airlines (PAL) president Jaime Bautista to lead the Department of Transportation (DoTr). Mr. Bautista spent four decades in various executive capacity in the Lucio Tan group. He spent 26 years in PAL; He retired in 2019. Other appointments under the DoTr include: Philippine Ports Authority General Manager: Christopher Pastrana (businessman, owner of Archipelago Philippine Ferries Corp). Undersecretary of railways: Cesar Chavez (career has been mostly in DoTr rail unit). LTFRB head: Cheloy Garavil (lawyer, currently serving as service director at the House committee on rules).

 

Budget deficit narrows in May, on soft primary spending

The national government posted a budget deficit of P147bn in May, narrower than the deficit in May 2021. The MoM, budget reversed from April’s surplus, which was due to timing of annual tax collections. Revenues in May rose 19% YoY, with tax collections up 22% YoY and already over 20% of pre-pandemic levels. Expenditures, however, dipped due to lower primary spending. This brings YT-May budget deficit to P459bn, down by 19% YoY. This is tracking below target, as revenues are trending ahead of the government’s target while primary spending trails.
