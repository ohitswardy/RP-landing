REGIS REPORT

25 March 2022

The PSEi made up for its 1.5% WoW drop last week, gaining 1.7% in the last 5 sessions. The market snapped a two-week decline amidst the Ukraine-Russia crisis and soaring commodity prices. 

Gains were widespread, with index gainers outnumbering losers 11:4.

Telcos were the big outperformers, after leading losses in the previous week.  Globe (+17.0% WoW), Converge (+13.1% WoW), and PLDT (+9.7% WoW) were the top three index gainers this week.

The newly signed Public Service Act disallows foreign state-owned entities from acquiring any additional capital in companies involved in “critical infrastructure”, which includes telecoms. This suggests third Dito Telecommunity  would be constrained from receiving any more capital from its 40% shareholder China Telecom.

On the flipside, Monde (-5.9% WoW) led the decliners  following FY21 earnings disappointment.  Monde’s  FY21 core net profit (-5% YoY to P8.2bn) fell short of Regis and consensus expectations as margins of both APAC BFB and Quorn took a hit in 4Q21 amidst input cost pressure. Prices of large inputs  wheat and palm oil (~20% of COGS) jumped in 4Q and continue to surge YTD.

Other consumer names took a breather from previous declines, as sentiment for the sector has been marred by spikes in commodity prices and input costs.  Gainers this week include Emperador (+5.2% WoW), URC (+4.5% WoW), and PGOLD (+2.3% WoW). 

Banks fared well, with gains see in BDO (+3.7% WoW), Metrobank (+3.3% WoW), and BPI (+2.15% WoW).

Continued in selling in SM (-0.6% WoW) after its deletion from the FTSE index last Friday saw the stock decline. Other conglos GT Capital (-1.65% WoW) and  LT Group (-1.05% WoW) dropped while property names Megaworld (-3.2% WoW) and SM Prime (-2.5% WoW) also fell. 

Foreigners remained sellers this week with another US$60m exiting the market amid geopolitical concerns and higher commodity prices. Biggest outflows were seen in SM even after FTSE deletion last week. Big outflows were also seen in consumer names URC and MONDE.

Philippine Power - Inconvenient truth, uncomfortable consequences
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

A greener future is not without cost

There is much optimism about the long-term prospects of renewable energy, alongside the global movement towards sustainability. The past couple of years have been characterized by an aggressive shift towards renewable energy plants, to the extent that the Philippine government has imposed a moratorium on new coal power facilities. This transition, coming amidst global energy supply shocks, will likely push energy security to the fore. Against this backdrop, we hosted the CEOs of some of the top power sector firms in the country – Aboitiz Power, First Gen Corp and Manila Electric – to share their thoughts on power supply[1]demand, striking the right balance between cost and the Earth, and the potential revival of nuclear energy in the country.

Power tariffs heading up

At around P7/kWh, spot prices are now more than 2x the level recorded last year, in large part driven by a post-pandemic demand recovery and plant outages that resulted in thin reserves. With energy commodities soaring (coal, oil), the power companies acknowledge that power rates for end-users (consumers, industrials, et al) are headed even higher in the coming months. Malampaya is bound to complicate things further, as 3,000MW of Luzon capacity remain dependent on this depleting resource. While plans are in place to transition to another fuel source (LNG) this year, the risks to supply and the cost of available alternatives to make up for the shortage could only mean one thing: higher tariffs for consumers.

Demand-supply situation and outlook

There is now a consensus among key industry players that the supply-demand situation will get tighter over the coming years before the next big plants come online in 2025/26. Among the plants planned, the power companies noted the key hurdles: off-take contracts, financing, and rising costs to operate, particularly for non-RE. New coal plants have become extremely unlikely, limiting the baseload option to LNG.

The views on RE (specifically solar)

Amidst the recent spike in energy commodities, power companies agreed that RE, particularly solar, has definitely become more competitive. The cost of storage systems will, however, need to come off even more for solar to become a dependable source of power. Amidst the growing pipeline of solar farms, constraints posed by land availability and grid connection may temper the number of solar farms that actually get built.

 
Cebu Air - 4Q21 EBITDA loss contracts on pax vol rebound
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

4Q21 net loss shrinks QoQ, YoY

Excluding FX, non-cash MTM losses on its convertible bond, and one-offs, CEB’s core net loss in FY21 reached P23.8bn, broadly in line with Regis forecast but short relative to consensus estimates. 4Q21 EBITDA/net losses shrunk to P576mn/P3.5bn, after CEB restored 34% of pre-Covid seat capacity and pax volumes reverted to 27% of pre-Covid levels. The volume and EBITDA recoveries, however, are admittedly slower than we would have liked, as cash costs were also larger than estimated. We are encouraged by recent strong booking trends and better initiatives to lift travel restrictions, but are also cognizant of the short-term adverse impact of recent spikes in oil prices to CEB.

Pax volumes, SLFs rebound strongly; Cargo revs surprise to the upside

Pax volumes more than doubled QoQ, with seat load factors (SLF) also up to 68% (vs. 58% in 3Q21). SLFs for domestic alone were at a healthy 80%, very close to pre-pandemic levels. Fares dropped YoY, given stricter international travel restrictions. However, cargo revs were surprisingly strong and more than offset the disappointment in passenger vols/fares.

 

CEB has restored 60% of its domestic seats in March alone, which is better than in December. Some local government units are still regulating flight frequencies until today but this should hopefully change in the coming weeks, as the government completely removes restrictions. The company is looking at fully restoring its domestic capacity in 2Q, with forward bookings already breaching pre-Covid levels. Its domestic market share further improved to 57% YTD from 56% in 4Q21 and 51% in 2019. They are targeting a market share of 62% by April.

Cash costs jump on higher fuel; Fleet guidance revised

Cash costs jumped 31% QoQ/16% YoY, driven by higher fuel costs. Note that fuel costs per ASK rose 39% YoY but this was more than offset by a 50% increase in pax revenue per ASK. Following the conclusion of negotiations with lessors and suppliers, CEB is reducing its fleet size to 69-73 in 2022 (from 74 in end[1]2021/2020), depending on the outcome of sales of 4 ATRs.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Group to sell LRMC stake

Ayala Corp. is looking to sell its stake in Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC) as part of the realignment of its asset portfolio according to AC president and CEO Fernando Zobel de Ayala. LRMC holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 railroad. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), Macquarie Infra (10%). MPI sold a 19% stake in LRMC to Sumitomo in 2019 for P3bn. AC is currently trading at a 32% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,167/sh (vs historical discount of 13%).

FGEN recurring profit of $252, flat - behind Regis/consensus; 4Q21 37% lower QOQ

FGEN’s FY21 recurring income hit $252mn, flat YoY and behind Regis ($279mn) and consensus ($287mn) estimates for the full-year.  The full year figure implies 4Q fell to $40mn, down 37%QoQ from $64mn in 3Q. After pref dividends, Regis estimates FY21 core income was $240mn, lower than Regis FY21E of $259mn.

 

Decline in 4Q was largely caused by need to buy replacement power for plant outages that exceeded outage allowance. Without citing details, management attributed the contraction to buying expensive replacement power for San Gabriel and certain plants of EDC. EDC also booked a lot of expenses in 4Q relating to steamfield maintenance, etc. 2021 was generally a catch-up year for a lot of maintenance related opex,and picked up towards the latter part of the year as restrictions lifted.

 

Nat gas earnings grew 6% to $198mn, largely driven by higher electricity sales from Avion in the early part of the year. This implies 4Q of nat gas was $40mn, down 38%QoQ. Meanwhile the lower contribution from 420MW San Gabriel due to outages and replacement power. EDC’s attributable income for FY21 of $88mn was lower by 18%YoY and implied 4Q was $18m which declined 25%QoQ. The press release cited higher plant and steamfield maintenance expenses. Earnings from hydro almost tripled to $4mn for the year. FGEN is trading at 7.1x FY22E and a discount to Regis TP of P34/share.

DENR grants MPSA to Semirara’s limestone project

The DENR granted the Mine Production Sharing Agreement (MPSA) to Semirara’s (SCC PM) Himalian limestone project last March 10. Semirara applied for the MPSA in 2015. The said MPSA allows SMPC the exclusive right to immediately undertake mining operations within the contract for a period of 25 years, renewable for like period. The MPSA brings the company closer to its plans to expand into cement. The company previously planned to build a 2.5mn MT greenfield cement plant in Semirara island at an estimated cost of US$600mn. Once the cement plant is built, Regis believes Semirara will likely have the lowest production cost in the country. However, even with the MPSA, Regis believes Semirara will need clarity on the renewal of its coal operating contract, which is due to expire in 2027, before it can proceed with its cement expansion. Regis believes this development will unlikely reduce Regis 2022 forecast dividend of at least P3/sh (9.6% yield). SCC is trading at 5.6x/9.4x on Regis’ 22F/23F PER.

Ayala Corp to sell Muntinlupa-Cavite Expressway Concession to Villar group

Ayala Corp will sell MCX Project Company Inc. (MCXPCI) to the Villar group’s privately held company, Prime Asset Ventures Inc., for P3.8bn. MCXCPI holds the concession assets, rights, and obligations under the concession agreement for the Muntinlupa-Cavite Expressway (MCX) Project. MCX is a 4km toll road which connects Muntinlupa and Cavite. The Villar group has large tracts of land in Cavite, Muntinlupa, and Las Pinas, which is adjacent to Muninlupa

These are held privately as well as through Vista Land (VLL PM). Regis estimates that c. 50% of VLL’s GAV is comprise of land in the said provinces/cities. VLL is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 4.5X FY22E P/E.

LTG 4Q21 rebounds on bank write-back; FY21 profit -4% - behind

LTG’s FY21 came in at P20.3bn, -3.7% YoY. This is behind Regis (P22.6bn) but ahead of consensus (P16.8bn). While the bank appears to have outperformed estimates for the year, PNB largely cause the group’s underperformance, netting out the impact of the P34bn reval gain in 2Q. Tobacco also underperformed, but its profits nevertheless grew 4%YoY despite the volume pressure.

 

Tobacco profits dip 3%QoQ on soft volumes, no price hike. Tobacco profits dipped 3%QoQ to P4.167bn in 4Q as volumes declined 3%QoQ to 8.7bn sticks. Unlike last year, there were no price hikes that helped margins in 4Q. The FY profit nevertheless increased 4%, despite volumes coming in 18% lower YoY.

 

PNB writes back P7.6bn. Bank profits jumped to P5.4bn in 4Q, buoyed by a P7.6bn reversal in loan loss provisions. Management said a small portion of this amount had been transferred to “performing status”, while a large chunk remained in NPL but there was an improvement in the expected recovery from the account. Management added that there could be more reversals in NPL when the PAL debt-to-equity conversion is executed in 1H22.

 

Beverage units soft, margin pressure adds to woes. Liquor volumes ended the year down 1% to 23.7mn cases, despite 4Q showing a decent 11% growth QoQ. Profits from both spirits and beverage units contracted QoQ arising from pressure from higher production costs. Tanduay’s GP margin, in particular fell to mid single-digit in the quarter, from low-teens in 9M21.

At P9.30/share, LTG trades at <4x FY22 PE and a 70% discount to market NAV.

 

Ayala Land’s Lio resort to cease operations by mid-April

The National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) issued a cease-and-desist order against Ayala Land’s 325-hectare Lio Airport and Tourism Estate. NCIP Mimaropa said that Ayala Land had “not submitted itself to Free Prior and Informed Consent processes despite the series of communications and notices from NCIP”. Ayala Land said that “all its operating properties have the necessary permits, titles, and licenses”. ALI’s legal team is addressing the issue. Lio comprises 1% of Ayala Land’s revenue. ALI is trading at 37% discount to NAV, 2.2X FY22E P/B, and 27.8X FY22E P/E.

BLOOM petition to dismiss GGAM complaint denied in NY

Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) disclosed that a New York Court did not grant its motion to dismiss the case file by GGAM against Sureste Properties (SPI), Bloomberry Resorts and Hotels Inc (BRHI) and Mr. Enrique K. Razon, Jr. SPI and BRHI are subsidiaries of BLOOM. It did, however, dismissed the cases file against real estate and oil and gas companies in the U.S. associated with Mr. Razon. This means that GGAM, SP, BRHI and Mr. Razon will have to present their evidence to prove their respective claims/defense for the enforcement of an Arbitral Award in the New York Court. In March 2021, Global Gaming Asset Management LLC (GGAM) sued Enrique K. Razon, Jr and affiliated companies in a federal court in New York to confirm a $297mn arbitration award by a Singapore court. BLOOM maintains that the arbitration award can only be enforced by a Philippine court. The Final Award issued in September 2019 ordered Bloomberry to pay GGAM about US$100mn in damages due to the termination of the management services agreement. It also ordered BLOOM to buy GGAM’s 921mn shares in BLOOM for P10.17bn@P11/sh (or allow the sale of the shares and make up the difference between market sale and P10.17bn).

MONDE FY21 – Inflationary pressures hit 4Q21 margins

FY21 core profit falls short of expectations as cost inflation hits 4Q margins

MONDE’s core profit in FY21 was P8.2bn, down 5% YoY, below Regis and consensus expectations. While sales (+2% YoY to P69bn) were in line with forecasts, EBITDA (-15% YoY to P13.2bn) was behind. 4Q21 saw a sharp decline in EBITDA (-28% YoY) due inflationary pressures across the group. Higher wheat and palm oil prices weighed on APAC BFB EBITDA margin (-170bps YoY), while higher A&P and R&D costs dragged Quorn’s EBITDA down to just breakeven levels (P26mn). Regis believes margin volatility could persist over the near term amidst the highly inflationary environment.

 

APAC BFB: Typhoon impact, higher input prices hit 4Q21 margins

Sales of the APAC BFB segment were flattish in 4Q21, mainly due to supply chain constraints following Typhoon Odette, which affected its Cebu plant. Outside of this, volumes for the core instant noodles business remained healthy, while biscuits saw an uptick in volumes (+3%) as consumer mobility improved. Meanwhile, the cumulative +3% price increase as of 4Q21 was not enough to mitigate the surge in wheat and palm oil cost during the period. 4Q21 GP margins contracted by  >300bps YoY  >400bps QoQ as a result.

 

Quorn: Challenging environment weighs on growth

Quorn reported sales of P4.1bn in 4Q21, up 3% YoY in PHP terms, but flattish in local currency terms. The meat alternatives grocery retail market in the UK declined by ~5% in 4Q21 as consumers increased frequency of dining outside of the home. Higher cost relating to raw materials (wheat/glucose), A&P, and R&D led to a significant reduction in EBITDA.

 

Cost management initiatives for FY22

Amidst a highly volatile and inflationary environment, management has outlined several cost management initiatives for 2022. For APAC BFB, prices of wheat and palm oil have been locked in until 2Q/3Q22 – at higher levels on a YoY basis but below current spot prices. A +3% price increase was implemented in late-Jan, but Regis believes further price adjustments could be made in the coming quarters.

 

Meanwhile, Quorn has locked in prices of key raw materials (wheat/glucose, egg albumin, packaging) for most of 2022. However, the geopolitical situation in Europe has heightened the risk of supply issues for these raw materials. Moreover, management is anticipating an increase in utilities and logistics costs, further putting pressure on margins. While the near-term outlook is challenging, management remains positive on the longer-term growth of meat alternatives, and will continue to invest in brand-building and R&D.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Size of labor force dips in January 2022

The country’s labor force participation rate dropped to 60.5% in January from 64% in December. In absolute terms, 2.7mn were removed from the workforce MoM due likely to the Omicron surge. The number of employed persons dropped by 2.5mn MoM, led by the agriculture sector

BOP posts US$157mn deficit in February; BSP revises 2022 BOP outlook

The country’s overall balance of payments position posted a deficit of US$157mn in February 2022. This is lower than the US$2.02bn deficit in Feb 2021 but larger than the US$102mn deficit in January 2022 and the govt cited national government foreign currency debt payments. This brings YT-Feb BOP to a cumulative deficit of US$259mn, lower than the US$2.77bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP revised its 2022 BOP outlook.  They are now looking at a BOP deficit of US$4.3bn (from a mild surplus in its previous forecast), from a US$1.3bn surplus in 2021 due to a wider trade gap and current account deficit.

Largest labor group seeks P430 wage hike in Central Visayas

The Trade Union Congress of the Philippines (TUCP) petitioned to increase the minimum wage in Central Visayas (Region 7) by P430 from P404 currently. Central Visayas region comprises provinces of Cebu, Negros Occidental, Bohol, and Siquijor. The Regional Wage Board raised the minimum wage in Central Visayas by 4.5% or P18 in January 2020. TUCP also called to increase the minimum wage in NCR to P1007 from P537 last week. The wages in NCR have not changed since Nov. 2018. President Rodrigo R. Duterte is set to announce his decision on the proposals soon.

Fuel retailers cut pump prices

Fuel retailers implemented a rollback of prices of gasoline, diesel, and kerosene by P5.45 (c. -7%), P11.45 (c. -15%), and P8.55 (c. -10%) per liter, respectively. This ends 11 straight weeks of price hikes, which has totaled P20.35 (+30%), P30.65 (+60%), and P24.90 per liter (+45%), respectively, thus far. Energy Secretary Cusi cited the drop in the average price of gasoline to US$120/bbl last week, down from US$138/bbl the previous week. The average price of diesel slipped to US$125/bbl from US$157/bbl the previous week. Despite the decline in fuel prices, the government will still double fuel subsidies to P6.1bn from P3bn.

Duterte signs law amending Public Service Act, raising foreign ownership limits on various sectors

Duterte signed the bill amending the Public Services Act into law (Republic Act# 11647), paving the way for the lifting of the 40% foreign ownership limits on a number of industries. Among the industries that are no longer considered “public utilities” : telecoms, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports. These are thus no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit. Those still considered public utilities are: distribution and transmission of electricity; petroleum pipeline transmission systems; water distribution systems; seaports and public utility vehicles. Foreign state-owned entities will not be allowed to own shares in public utilities or companies that own/operate “critical infrastructure”. Those who already own the shares are allowed to keep their shares but will not be allowed to add more. This will be a constraint on China Telecom taking over Dito Telecommunity fully.

Taxi operators seek P20 hike in flag-down rate in NCR

The Philippine National Taxi Operators Association (PNTOA) indicated intentions to file a petition for a P20 increase in the current P40 flag-down rate. The petition will be filed within the week at the LTFRB. The number of active taxi units plunged to 28k from 50k pre-pandemic. The government currently has a P2.5bn fuel subsidy program for public utility vehicle operators and drivers. The program aims to support 377,443 beneficiaries; As of Tuesday, the LTFRB reported it has distributed P6,500 worth of fuel subsidy to 108k beneficiaries. Another P2.5bn is being prepared for the program. The LTFRB recently denied a petition to restore the minimum jeepney fare to P10 from P9. Passenger transport services has a weight of 4.54% in the CPI basket.

 

Gov’t raises US$2.25bn via offshore bonds

The Philippine government raised US$2.25bn from its triple tranche US dollar-denominated bond offering. The US$1bn was from a 25-year green bond offer, priced at 4.2% or 50bps tighter than the initial guidance of 4.7%. Additionally, US$500mn was from 5-year bonds, priced at 90bps over treasuries or a coupon of 3.229% while US$750mn was from 10.5-year bonds, priced at 125bps over treasuries or a coupon of 3.556%. In 2021, the government raised (a) US$3bn from a dual tranche global bond offering in June; (b) EUR2.1bn triple tranche Global Bonds in April and (c) JPY 55bn Samurai bond offering in March. The government plans to raise a total of P2.2tn (US$42bn) this year, of which 75% will be sourced from the domestic market. As at end-January 2022, the government’s debt stock stood at P12.029tn, of which 70% is domestic and 30% is external.

BSP maintains key rates

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) maintained the interest rate for the overnight reverse repurchase facility at an all-time low of 2%. The BSP revised its 2022 inflation rate forecast to 4.3% from 3.7% previously. It also raised its 2023 inflation rate forecast to 3.6% from 3.3% previously. The BSP cited uncertainties in the crude market brought about by Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. Oil price forecast at US$102/bbl in 2022 (from US$83 previously) and US$88/bbl in 2023 (from US$76/bbl previously). BSP Governor Diokno said they are considering to cut the reserve requirement ratio for banks in 2H22. The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled on May 19.

Consumer loan growth recovering, NPLs falling - past the hump?

Consumer loans showed signs of early recovery in 4Q21. Overall, consumer loans were still down 0.6% YoY, but significantly better than the -2.6% in 3Q21. Furthermore, consumer loans were already up 2.7% QoQ. Asset quality also improved in 4Q21, with NPLs falling nearly 60bps QoQ to 9.31% from the 9.90% in the previous quarter. Granted, the level is still uncomfortably high.

 

The growth was most notable in Mortgages, where loans were up 7.9% YoY (was 5.6% in 3Q), and up 4.3% QoQ. NPL ratio also fell to 9.36%, down 22bps QoQ. However, the trends don’t seem to have turned yet for Motor Vehicle (MV) loans, which were still down 16.5% YoY, and down another 4.6% QoQ. According to the banks Regis covers, new loan approvals are up sharply. However, with very little new lending in the last two years, and given the relatively short duration, the loans are maturing faster than the banks can write new loans. Offhand, Regis expects outstanding loan balances to flatten out in 2Q/3Q22 (on a QoQ basis). To the upside, MV NPLs fell sharply QoQ to 12.01% from 12.70% the previous quarter. More encouragingly, MV NPLs in absolute terms were down nearly 10% QoQ, though Regis suspects this is partly on more aggressive foreclosures.

 

For Credit Cards (CC), loan balances contracted 4.6% YoY and 0.9% QoQ. This seems counter-intuitive, given the sharp jump in mobility/spending in 4Q21. This is likely due to aggressive write-offs by banks. This is partly evidenced by a sharp 118bp QoQ drop in NPL ratio from 7.96% to 6.78% in 4Q. The nominal value of CC NPLs fell 15.6% QoQ. Based on Regis recent conversations with bank CEOs, the consensus seems to be that the industry is ready to re-grow the consumer loan portfolio, as the economic reopening gives management confidence in loosening credit standards. This would be positive not only the banks (higher NIMs etc), but also for residential real estate, and especially for auto sales, where demand is boosted by availability of credit.

Mobility jumps to new Covid highs

Mobility data hit new highs last weekend, with most indicators now at or above pre-Covid levels, at least at the national level. Interestingly, there was nothing special about last weekend – no holidays, etc. Furthermore, the improving data came despite the sharp increases in petrol prices at the pump (10-20%) last week.

 

Some insights:

Visits to Retail / Recreation establishments held steady for most of March, but popped up last weekend. Transit station data surged, beating even the Christmas rush. Again, this was surprising considering the jump in petrol prices. Workplace data continued to grind upwards too, and was above pre-pandemic levels for the first time at the national level. Metro Manila data continued to lag the national average. This is ironic, considering the capital is already down to Alert Level 1, while other provinces are at higher alert levels. One has to wonder if the rapid digitalization has meant a) more online shopping, hence less need to go to shops/restaurants, and b) more work-from-home arrangements, hence fewer trips to the office. Regis thinks it could see another bump up in mobility, particularly with the order for BPO workers to return to full on-site work from 01 April, or else the BPOs risk losing their tax incentives. The mobility data supports Regis’ thesis (“Shaken, not spilled”) that the reopening story is potentially more powerful than the inflation/PHP concerns with high global commodity prices.

Duterte signs EO on economic recovery

President Rodrigo Duterte signed an executive order aimed at accelerating the economy’s recovery from the pandemic. Restrictions on the use of COVID-19 vaccines by the private sector will be reduced. Expansion of public transport capacity, and resumption of face-to-face learning. Streamline requirements for domestic travel, limiting these to vaccination cards or negative RT-PCR test results for unvaccinated individuals, and a QR code that can be scanned by various contact-tracing apps. Further loosen the requirements for international travel, such as providing quarantine exemptions for vaccinated foreign tourists. Passage of laws to accelerate digital transformation.
