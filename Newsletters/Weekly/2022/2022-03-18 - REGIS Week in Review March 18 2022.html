REGIS REPORT

18 March 2022

The Phils had a choppy, volatile week, going down over 4% on Monday on escalating Ukraine tensions as well as a ballooning trade deficit on the ground.  The market gained back earlier losses  by Thursday but heavy FTSE related outflows on Friday took the market lower.  The Phils closed the week down 1.5% to 7007.63.

The country’s trade deficit looks to be widening sharply this year, with the January figure already at US$4.7bn (up from US$2.9bn the previous year ) even before the conflict in Ukraine and the succeeding dramatic spikes in commodity prices. Even putting more pressure on the PHP is possible inaction from the BSP – with BSP Governor Diokno saying he sees no need to move monetary policy in line with the Fed.  The PHP has deteriorated 2.6% against the dollar since the start of the year.

The market was mixed, with 18 losers vs 12  gainers this week.

Among the top decliners this week were the telcos Globe (-10.5% WoW), and PLDT (-5.7% WoW).  Converge (+3.5% WoW) gained, after reporting a 114% jump in FY21 net profits.  Revenues grew 69% while EBITDA expanded 80%. The company is guiding for 50% growth in revenue. 

Consumer names also declined.  Jollibee (-6.1% WoW) dropped as did Wilcon (-2.9% Wo), Monde (-2.45% WoW),  Puregold (-2.4% WoW). Conglos also fell with AEV (-4.4% WoW), AGI (-3.9% WoW) dropping. 

Meanwhile gains were seen in San Miguel (+5.05% WoW), Metro Pacific (-4.7% WoW), Aboitiz Power (-4.4% WoW), and URC (-4.0% WoW).

Ayala Corp (-1.6% WoW) reported net income of P27.8bn for FY21, which is up 62% YoY. Ayala Land (-3.8% WoW) and BPI (+0.8% WoW) did better than expected while Globe and ACEN (-1.0% WoW) fell short. Earnings estimates for these will have to be adjusted following FY21 results but we reckon the medium-term outlook for all of them is improved. This is notwithstanding the short-term earnings hiccups some (GLO and ACEN) may have to face.

Meanwhile, significantly, there is clear progress on the execution of its strategic agenda, particularly for the hardest part: divestments. To date, AC has announced transactions that will yield P27bn (US$515m) of proceeds and looks likely to meet its goal of US$1bn by next year.

AC’s share price has not moved much since last year but the share prices of the two core investments lifted by over-exuberance last year (GLO and ACEN) have now deflated quite a bit (down over 30% from their peaks). Despite the latter, the discount at which AC trades vs its NAV has not narrowed. At ~33%, the discount is still close to its widest historically, and offers attractive value.

Foreigners were sellers for $96m with bulk going through on Friday due to FTSE rebalancing. Biggest outflows were seen in SM (-0.7% WoW) after being deleted from the index. Meanwhile, MER (-3.2% WoW), ALI,  JFC, GLO, AC also saw large outflows.

RESEARCH
Philippine Consumer - Balancing act
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Navigating inflationary headwinds

We believe the sector could face near-term pressure as Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), and Universal Robina (URC PM) navigate volatile commodity prices, its potential impact on margins and underlying consumption. For these companies, 2022E will be a balancing act between margins vs growth as consumers become more price sensitive. Despite the muted outlook, we believe CNPF, MONDE, and URC are likely to weather the storm better given their scale and market leadership and will continue to generate healthy returns despite near term volatility. We cut our target prices by 17-24% but maintain Buy ratings on CNPF and URC, while we downgrade MONDE to Hold on valuation.

Inflationary headwinds dampen recovery

We believe inflation will see an upward trend in the coming quarters given the surge in oil and commodity prices, which will eventually feed into prices of local goods and services (e.g. food, transport prices, etc). While top-line growth across the sector should see a recovery vs 2021 levels, we believe pricing will also be a key driver of consumption trends as input cost inflation is passed on.

Further consolidation amidst soaring input costs

The near-term cost environment will be challenging, in our view, but CNPF, MONDE, and URC are well-positioned to weather the storm. They (a) are market leaders with strong brands in their respective categories, (b) have a high degree of pricing power and (c) have greater scale relative to competition. Rapidly rising input costs will exert greater pressure on smaller firms and will thus likely result in further consolidation in industries that are already seeing such due to the pandemic.

Adjusting forecasts and target prices

Broadly, we have cut our 2022-23E EPS estimates by as much as 18% on lower margin assumptions, and we are expecting average EPS CAGR of 6% for 2021- 22E. Our SOTP-based target prices are likewise adjusted based on our new forecasts, with the branded segments valued at 23-25x 2022E PE, broadly in line with peers. We are maintaining our Buy ratings for CNPF and URC, and downgrading MONDE to a Hold on valuation. Key downside risk for the sector is a continued increase in commodity prices, which could put further pressure on margins and consumption.

 
Philippine Strategy - Shaken, not spilled – Glass still half full?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Will global commodity prices derail the recovery story?

The PSEi lost as much as 7% last week, knocking it off its perch as one of the best markets in the region. In our recent strategy report, we noted that the two major macro downside risks were inflation and the PHP. The conflict in Ukraine has sent prices soaring for many commodities that the Philippines imports, including oil, coal, wheat, corn, palm oil, etc. While inflation has been tame (3.0% in Feb), we estimate it could move north of 6% by 2Q22. The rising import bill would put increasing pressure on the Current Account and the PHP. Our base case of a reopening-led economic recovery remains, especially if the conflict is brief. However, a protracted period of elevated commodity prices could rein in the country’s consumption-driven economy. The markets will likely discount the worst though, at least in the near-term.

Philippines – an import-dependent economy

The country imported $118bn worth of goods in 2021. Of this, $14.6bn (12.4%) was fuels including coal and petroleum. Another $5.3bn was wheat, corn, feeds, and edible oils, while a further $9.0bn was food. The trade deficit swelled to $43.2bn, nearly double YoY, and equivalent to around 11% of GDP. Thanks to service exports such as OFW remittances, and BPO revenues the Current Account Deficit (CAD) was closer to 1% of GDP.

Inflation likely to pop in 2Q22, wage hikes proposed

Philippine inflation has historically been strongly influenced by commodity prices, particularly food and fuel, which make up 37.8% and 6.7% of the CPI respectively (ex-second round effects). Inflation has been low so far, at 3.0% YoY in Jan/Feb, due to a high 1Q21 base, and a CPI re-basing. Given the price moves, we reckon inflation could jump to over 6% in 2Q22. Fuel subsidies for public transport could ease the transmission to the public. A minimum wage hike has also been proposed for the first time since 2018.

Current Account, PHP vulnerable

The trade deficit ballooned through 2021, ending the year at $5.2bn in December, and stayed high in January. If commodities stay where they are for the rest of 2022, the CAD could jump to 5% of GDP or higher, levels not seen since the early 1990s. We have revised our year-end target to P54:$1 for the PHP. To the upside, the BSP has close to $110bn in FX reserves, equivalent to 10 months of imports.

Glass still half full? Shaken, not spilled… yet

The recovery of the Philippine domestic economy seems to be playing out, as mobility continues to improve. We have, however, adjusted our macro forecasts (at right) to account for the current situation. That said, commodity prices have rolled over in recent days, with crude oil down c20% from its 8 March peak. While the markets have been shaken, the water hasn’t spilled out of the glass just yet.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Corp FY21 net income P27.8bn (+62%)

AC reported net profit of P27.8bn, up +62% (vs P17.1bn). While this appears ahead of consensus estimates (P23.2bn), it is inclusive of various items that are arguably one-off/exceptional, including gains from the GNP Kausawagan sale as well as remeasurement gains from its residual stake in Manila Water (MWC PM) and Mynt.

 

Among its core investments, BPI (BPI PM) and Ayala Land (ALI PM) did better than expected while Globe (GLO PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM) disappointed. AC Health reported positive EBITDA for the year while AC Industrials lost another P1.5bn.

 

As of March 2022, AC executed P26.9bn (US$515m) of transactions as part of its efforts in its “value realization initiatives”. Among the transactions are its sale of ACEN and MWC shares, as well as assets to ALI. AC has a stated goal of achieving US$1bn by next year.

Sale of its investments in roads and rail are progressing.  AC has stakes in MCX toll road as well as LRT1.

 

AC has also deployed P18bn for its buyback and to increase its stake in ALI.  The company spent P10.8bn to increase its stake in ALI by 2ppt to 47.4%.

There’s a wide range for capex guidance at the AC parent level (P8bn to P24bn) as it will depend on buyback and investment opportunities.

 

PMFTC raises prices by +17-21% for high and low-end brands effective March 21, retains pricing for mid-priced segment

LT Group said that its unit PMFTC raised cigarette prices by around P20/pack for its high (Marlboro) and low-end (Jackpot) brands effective March 21. However, it did not raise prices for mid-priced brand Fortune. The price hikes for the high/low-end are above Regis estimates for the year (+P10/pack) while the low-end prices the mid-segment below Regis estimates  – all coming in later in the year than anticipated. The higher price hike but later implementation means the average effective price for FY22 of about P109 will be slightly higher (+1%) than Regis estimates of average price/pack for the year.

 

Truck prices higher by as much as 21% per pack except for Fortune. The truck price of Marlboro has been raised to P138/pack (+17%) while the truck price of Jackpot has been raised to P109/pack (+21%). Meanwhile, it held off any increase for its mid-priced segment Fortune, which retained pricing at P109/pack. Regis forecasts have the price increase at P10/pack across the board beginning Jan 1. On a per stick basis, the increases translate to a +14-20% for Marlboro and Jackpot, but none for Fortune.

 

Average price for FY22E of P109/pack very close to Regis estimates. The late implementation and different hikes across brands mean the effective price increase for the year will be very similar to Regis estimates. Regis estimates that PMFTC’s average price for FY22E will be around P109/pack vs Regis estimates of P108/pack. Assuming that volumes hold (c.35bn sticks), Regis thinks PMFTC’s annual profit can come in around P38bn. Of this amount, LTG’s share is about P19bn.

 

LTG can comfortably maintain dividend at >P1/share. LTG’s prospective share in PMFTC’s profits  is still well above the dividends LTG paid out last year (P1.08/share ~ P11.7bn total), which gives us confidence that the dividend yield can be sustained at >10%.

LTG is trading at an estimated 3.5x PE for FY22E and a discount of 71% to market-NAV.

 

Cebu Air update - Capacity + Oil

CEB reducing capacity? According to Jefferies’ airline capacity survey, CEB’s weekly seats (domestic + international) dipped back to 51% of pre-Covid levels in the week starting March 7 from 53% the previous week and 55% in end-Feb. Regis reckons the decline could be reflecting (a) normalization after the Feb 25 holiday and/or (b) a reaction to the surge in crude oil prices in the first two weeks of March. It is worth noting that the survey is based on estimated seat counts of flights for the upcoming week and can still change once actual data is captured and reflected after 1-2 weeks. However, Regis conversations with CEB management suggest capacity has not been reduced at all and they have been mounting flights in preparation for the upcoming summer peak season. They are, however, closely monitoring seat load factors, fares, and fuel cost increases on a per route level, given the recent spike in oil prices.

 

Year-to-date, CEB’s capacity seems to have reverted to 46% of pre-Covid levels. To reach Regis forecast for the year (70% of pre-Covid levels), CEB’s capacity would need to average c. 90% for the rest of the year.  Note that Regis forecast is broadly in line with management’s announced guidance. A reduction in capacity due to surging oil prices raises downside risks to Regis forecasts. Every 1% reduction in pax vols/capacity could reduce CEB’s EBITDA/profit by 4%/15%, holding other things constant.

 

Jet kero prices drop but still elevated. Since Russia invaded Ukraine on Feb 24, jet kero prices have swung wildly to as high as US$151/bl on March 9 but has since declined to US$118/bbl.  CEB has zero outstanding fuel hedges this year and Regis does not have visibility on the hedging strategy of Philippine Airlines and Air Asia Philippines.  The most hedged player (if there’s one) will be able to compete more aggressively in the market.

 

The fuel surcharge currently in place provides protection from rising crude prices and currency depreciation. It is, however, delayed by approximately 3 months. Ultimately, demand and supply will dictate the direction of CEB’s profitability.  Prior to the recent fuel spike, CEB has been able to raise fares over and above the fuel surcharge, with its competitors following its lead. 

 

However, the recent fast and steep increase in jet kero prices has made fare hikes more tricky, likely leading to margin compression, near term.  Every 10% increase in Regis jet kero forecast (average of US$80/bbl) could reduce CEB’s EBITDA/profit by 4%/12%, holding other things constant.

 

CEB inexpensive relative to peers but elevated fuel prices an overhang.  Trading at 5.2x FY22E EV/EBITDAR, CEB is inexpensive relative to LCC peers (Capital A – 20x; Spring Airlines – 18x, Vietjet – 40x) and regional airlines (36x).  However, given the volatility in oil prices, investors may not be in a rush to buy it.

Remittances climb by 2.5% in January

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.5% YoY to US$2.67bn in January. Remittances were slightly above (+0.8%) January 2020 remittances of US$2.65bn. In PHP terms, remittances were up 9.3% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate broadly steady at +3.6% YoY. Meanwhile, land-based remittances jumped +2.9% YoY, on the back of strong remittances from Asia, USA, Saudi Arabia, and Europe. For 2022, the BSP is looking at remittances growing by +4% YoY to US$36bn.

 

Duterte nixes suspension of fuel excise tax; Looking at other options

President Duterte thumbed down a proposal to suspend excise taxes of fuel products. The government expects to collect P147bn in taxes from fuel products this year, of which P131bn is from excise taxes. The government is instead looking at direct subsidies equivalent to P200 per month to the bottom 50% of Filipino households or an estimated 74.7mn Filipinos. The subsidy will amount to P33bn and will be sourced from VAT collections on oil imports. Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua is proposing a four-day workweek to cut the costs of businesses and workers. A similar four-day workweek was implemented in 2008 when fuel prices were also high. Workers will still work 40 hours per week, which will involve working 10 hours per day (vs. 8 hours) for 4 days. Labor Assistant Secretary Tutay also proposed a three-month wage subsidy worth P24bn, benefitting 1 million workers. If approved, subsidy will be distributed from April to June. These proposals are in lieu of calls to increase minimum wage amid the rising costs of petroleum products. NEDA chief Chua estimates a P39 increase in the daily minimum wage will add 1ppt to inflation. Raising jeepney fares by P1.25 will add 0.4ppts to inflation. Labor groups are calling to raise the minimum wage to P1007 from P537 currently; There are also calls to raise jeepney fares by P6-7 from P9 currently. Ukraine-Russia war could shave off 0.4% to the government’s 2022 GDP target of +7-9%

NCR to remain under Level 1, more areas ease restrictions

The National Capital Region will remain under Alert Level 1 while 47 areas have also shifted to the lowest COVID-19 alert level until the end of March, Malacanang announced Tuesday. Areas not under Alert Level 1 shall be under Alert Level 2 beginning March 16 until March 31. Under Alert Level 1, most health restrictions have been removed. This will allow 100% capacity in government offices and public transport. Nationwide cases averaged 590/day from March 7 to 13, 35% lower than previous week.

House panel approves bill amending oil deregulation law

The House committee on energy approved the bill amending the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act. The bill seeks to require a minimum inventory level (of 30 days) and unbundle the cost of petroleum. Marikina City Representative Stella Luz Quimbo said that the retail price should not be based on the weekly changes in the Mean of Platts Singapore. Rep. Quimbo also proposed to add a provision in the bill giving the President the power to suspend or lower the excise tax rates on fuel products when the price of Dubai crude oil reaches US$80/barrel.

Public Service Act amendments

Here’s a key section of the final (bicameral) proposed law amending the Public Service Act.

The proposed law would prohibit foreign state-owned enterprises from owning any capital in a service classified as “public utility” or “critical infrastructure”.  This would cover electricity utilities (transmission) as well as telecom (classified as “critical infrastructure”). There is a provision allowing such foreign state-owned enterprises to keep their existing stakes (acquired prior to the proposed law) but they would be “prohibited from investing in additional capital”.  As it reads, State Grid (China) and China Telecom cannot infuse any more capital into National Grid (NGCP) and Dito, respectively.  NGCP arguably would not need new capital but that certainly is not the case for Dito. Foreign sovereign wealth funds and independent pension funds may own up to 30% of such public services.  This is the loophole for SingTel on Globe, as Singtel is controlled by Temasek (a SWF). Singtel owns 46.9% of Globe’s common shares but only 21% of its voting shares. In theory, Duterte can veto the law (but only in its entirety). If that happens, everything is back at square one (i.e. China Telecom can’t increase its stake in Dito beyond 40%).

 

Marcos Jr, Duterte keep leads in February 2022 Pulse Asia survey

The latest Pulse Asia survey showed Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr and running mate, Sara Duterte maintaining their leads. The tandem saw huge jumps in Metro Manila, with Marcos rising to 66% preference from 57% in January. They dominated in all subnational divisions (NCR, Balance Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao) and were also the top choice across all socio-economic classes. VP Leni Robredo was a distant second for the Presidential race, with a score of 15%, down from 16% in January. Survey conducted from Feb 18-23 and involved 2,400 registered voters.

Market foreign ownership declines in February

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership fell 38bps MoM to 21.9% while PSEi foreign ownership rose 13bps MoM to 26.2% (in February). In addition to declines in foreign ownership of some index constituents, the reduction in market foreign ownership was exacerbated by the 17.4% MoM share price drop of Globe, which has among the highest levels of foreign ownership (at 57.7%). The increase in the PSEi’s foreign ownership was largely due to the index rebalancing in mid-February. MONDE, which Regis estimates has foreign ownership of 64%, was added to the PSEi.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

AC – foreign ownership fell 2.2ppt to 26.4%. This is inconsistent with trading data, which show that AC did not even turn over 1% of shares outstanding in February.

ALI – foreign ownership dropped by 2.1ppt to 30.9%. This is inconsistent with trading data, which show that ALI experienced net foreign buying in February.

GTCAP – foreign ownership declined 1.6ppt to 19.6%.

SECB – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents. Foreign ownership rose 26 basis points to 38.0%.

Trade deficit widens YoY but dips MoM in January

The country’s trade deficit widened to US$4.7bn in January from US$2.9bn in the same period last year. However, it dipped from US$5.3bn in December 2021. Imports jumped 27% YoY, on the back of increases in fuel imports (+97%) and raw/intermediate goods imports (+30%). Petroleum crude oil imports rose 129% YoY while imports of wheat/corn/unmilled cereals rose 70%/879%/1022% YoY. Imports of consumer goods rose 11% YoY and were 1% above pre-pandemic levels. Exports rose 9% YoY, faster than in December 2021 (+7%) and January 2021 (-4%); It was also above pre-pandemic levels by c. 2%.

BSP: No pressure to act in tandem with US Fed

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees no need to move in tandem with the US Federal Reserve according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. BSP calibrates its monetary policy settings in response to external developments only to the extent that they influence the outlook on growth and inflation. BSP said various tools, such as the flexible exchange rate system, strong external buffers, macro prudential policy framework as well as liquidity enhancing and management tools will deal with any short-term volatility from monetary tightening. Additionally, Diokno said he prefers targeted government interventions to address supply-driven inflation. The next monetary policy meeting is set on March 24.

Budget deficit widens to P23bn in January

The National Government’s budget deficit widened to P23bn in January on faster disbursements. Revenues rose 7%, driven by an 11% YoY jump in tax revenues but was tempered by a 23% contraction in non-tax revenues due to high base effects from dividend remittances from Transco and PAGCOR. Expenditures rose 9.7% due to higher national tax allocation releases and higher interest payments. The government is targeting a budget deficit of P1.65tn for 2022, equivalent to 7.7% of GDP. In 2021, the budget deficit stood at a record P1.67tn (8.6% of GDP).
