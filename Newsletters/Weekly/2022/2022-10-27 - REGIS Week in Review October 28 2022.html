REGIS REPORT

27 October 2022

The Philippines climbed 2.8% this week, cementing its 1.3% gain in the previous week, as marginal foreign inflows propped the index higher.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$6.4m this week. Excluding San Miguel blocks amounting to US$9.24m, foreigners were slight net buyers for US$2.8m. Biggest inflows this week were seen in BDO (+7.3% WoW)  and SM (+6.5% WoW) while largest outflows were in property names SM Prime (-1.6% WoW)  and Ayala Land (-1.7% WoW).

SM  and BDO were in fact the top index movers, with both names chipping in 54 and 31 index points respectively, of half of the PSEI’s 170-index point overall gain  for the week.

Delivering strong gains were PLDT (+9.7% WoW), URC (+9.1% WoW), and BDO (+7.3% WoW).

Wilcon (+6.5% WoW) also gained.  The company delivered better-than-expected 3Q numbers, with net profit surging 77% YoY. SSSG accelerated to +23.5%, and margin expansion also boosted the result.

Megaworld (+5.6% WoW), BPI (+5.3% WoW), and Monde (+5.2% WoW) were also higher.

Index gainers outnumbered losers 11:4. 

Semirara (-7.1% WoW) declined. Sector wise, the property sector lagged,  as rising office vacancy and interest rates conintued to weigh on the sector.  Robinsons Land (-2.4% WoW), Ayala Land (-1.7% WoW), and SM Prime (-1.6% WoW) fell.

Telcos Converge (-0.5% WoW) and Globe (-0.3% WoW) were also weak. Meantime, telco player DITO (+30.4% WoW) surged on speculation that it was in talks with potential investors.

Meanwhile, Eagle cement (+9.1% WoW)jumped on news that San Miguel’s (+0.7% WoW) buyout of EAGLE does not need to go thru review by the Philippine competition Commission. SMC is  buying 88.5% of EAGLE from Ramon Ang at P22/sh.

President Marcos issued an Executive Order (EO) making the indoor use of face masks voluntary.  Masks will still be required in public transportation and medical facilities. The easing of masking rules should pave the way for the further normalization of economic activity.

Converge ICT: Beyond the land grab
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Initiating coverage with a Buy rating and target price of P16.50
Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) is a pure play broadband service provider in a market that remains under-penetrated. It has managed to capture 28% of the wired broadband market in a short amount of time, banking on its nimbleness and cost efficiency, which have allowed it to roll out speedily, while producing value-accretive returns on capital. The current share price now provides attractive value, following a sharp correction in line with the adjustment of what used to be frothy growth expectations. We initiate coverage of Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) with a Buy rating and target price of P16.50/share.

Right place, right time
Converge ICT came into the market at the perfect time to take advantage of an under-served market at the cusp of explosive, pandemic-fueled growth. With an estimated 28% of the wired broadband subscriber market, it is already the second largest player, coming from nowhere just six years ago.

Penetration has room to grow but prices will need to declinePenetration for wired broadband, now at 1/4th of Philippine households, has room to rise further, in our view, but pricing will have to come down to adapt to affordability levels for the market. The key neighboring markets with higher penetration levels have either (1) higher GDP per capita, (2) lower entry level prices or (3) both.

Low-cost operations lift ROIC
Among the operators, Converge has the widest EBITDA margins and highest ROICs. We attribute this to the company not being saddled with legacy costs. The fact that it’s run by hands-on entrepreneurs is, in our view, a key factor.

Target price at P16.50/share, Risks
We set our target price at P16.50/share, derived using DCF analysis employing a COE of 12% and terminal growth of 1%. Our TP implies PER of 12.2x and EV/EBITDA of 6.5x on FY23 estimates. Key risks include “key man” risk and those relating to the macro environment, competition, and regulation.

 
Wilcon Depot - 3Q22: Margins continue to expand
 
 
 

9M22 NPAT +58% YoY to P2.96bn – ahead
WLCON reported 9M22 net profit of P2.96bn, up 58% YoY, tracking well ahead of Regis and consensus FY22E. Net sales (+23% YoY to P24.7bn) was broadly in line with expectations, and the profit outperformance continued to be driven by strong margins. For 3Q22 alone, net profit surged 77% YoY to P1.1bn.

Same-store sales jumps from low base but stabilizes QoQ
SSSG accelerated to +23.5% during the quarter, albeit coming from a low base given the hard lockdown in Aug 2021. The legacy stores – mostly in Metro Manila – drove much of the growth, but these stores were also the hardest-hit during the lockdowns last year. We estimate, however, that SSS has stabilized on an absolute basis, and YoY growth rates should likewise normalize in the coming quarters.

Pricing power drives continued gross margin expansion
Gross margin in 3Q22 breached 39%, expanding another 50bps QoQ even as the contribution of in-house brands to sales was stable at ~51%. The continued margin expansion illustrates WLCON’s pricing power even amidst inflationary pressures and supply chain bottlenecks.

Top line momentum to continue into 2023; further margin expansion seen
Management remains optimistic on sequential top line growth trends going into 4Q22/early-2023 as the pipeline for private construction projects remains healthy (project sales are up 2-3x) and WLCON’s key customer base (mid- to upper-income consumers) are generally more insulated from inflationary pressures. Many of these projects were delayed or paused during the pandemic and only resumed in 2Q/3Q22. The company also believes that margins could expand further as the challenging cost environment largely worked in WLCON’s favor by keeping competitors on the sidelines.

AC’s property dividends consisting of common shares of ACEN Corp has been issued the Certificate of Filing the Notice of Property Dividend Declaration by the Securities and Exchange Commission last October 24, 2022. The payment date will be determined soon as the Bureau of Internal Revenues issues Certificates Authorizing Registration for the transfer of the Property Dividends to the qualified stockholders. ACEN board clears power supply deal with Dinginin plant

ACEN Corp said that its board approved the execution of an agreement with GNPower Dinginin Ltd Co for the supply of 43MW of capacity. The power purchase and supply agreement will start on October 26, 2022

GNPower Dinginin owns a 1,336MW coal fired plant in Mariveles, Bataan. ACEIC owns 62.43% of ACEN and has a 20% economic interest in GNPower Dinginin. 

 

Supreme Court upholds Meralco settlement with National Power Corp

The Supreme Court upheld the validity of the P27.5bn settlement between Meralco and National Power Corp (Napocor). The settlement covers disputes over the failure of both to meet their contractual obligations under a decades-old power supply agreement. Among other things, this covers Meralco’s failure to buy the agreed-upon amounts of electricity from Napocor, among other thing.  The settlement between Meralco and Napocor was agreed upon but was subsequently opposed by the Office of the Solicitor General. The settlement includes pass-through elements that will ultimately have to be decided upon by the regulator (ERC).

Supreme Court affirms ruling vs. MWSS, Maynilad, Manila Water, but lowers fees

The Supreme Court (SC) has unanimously affirmed its 2019 ruling on Metropolitan Water Works and Sewerage System (MWSS) and water concessionaires, Maynilad and Manila Water. SC ruled that they should held liable for violating Republic Act 9275 or the Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004. The SC however reduced the fine of each of the water concessionaires to P202mn from P921mn previously for the period of May 7, 2009 to August 6, 2019. Both Manila Water and Maynilad provisioned for the original fine in 2019.  

Apart from the above fine, the 2019 SC decision also lowered the daily fine of each concessionaire to P30k per day (from P322k per day), subject to a 10% increase every two years until full compliance of the Clean Water Act. This has accumulated to P202mn from May 7, 2009 to January 21, 2022.  Costs related to this will likely form part of the water concessionaires’ future opex, which will be fully recovered. 

 

 

Colliers: 3Q22 office rents continue to fall; residential prices inch up

Colliers reported that 3Q22 Metro Manila office vacancy stayed flat QoQ at 17.7%. It estimates that office rental rates fell c. 2% QoQ. Office rental rates are generally 20%-50% lower than pre-COVID levels.

Colliers is forecasting Metro Manila office vacancy to jump to 20% in 2022E (21% in 2023E) and office rental rates to fall 10% for the full-year. REGIS forecasts: 19% office vacancy in 2022E (22% in 2023E), rental rates -5% in 2022E (-5% in 2023E).

Due to WFH policies and increasing BPO penetration in the provincial areas, Colliers expects BPOs to take up only 200,000sqm-250,000sqm annually (in Metro Manila) going forward, compared to c. 400,000sqm annually pre-COVID.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for: 55% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 41% of Megaworld’s NAV, and 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV.

Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 1% QoQ. Metro Manila condominium prices are generally 10%-25% lower than pre-COVID levels.

Colliers expects Metro Manila condominium prices to rise 3% in 2022.  REGIS forecasts condominium prices to stay flat this year.

 

Meralco signs 850MW power supply agreement with Terra Solar

Meralco signed 850MW worth of power supply agreements (PSA) with Terra Solar. Terra Solar is part of Prime Infrastructure of Enrique Razon.

The PSAs involve two separate blocks of mid-merit capacity: 600MW of capacity by Feb 2026 and  250MW by Feb 2027.  This will involve not just solar capacity but also battery storage.

The agreement will be at a levelized cost of P5.80/kwh based on the assumptions at the time of the unsolicited proposal. 

Terra Solar first offered an unsolicited proposal to supply Meralco with 850MW of mid-merit power for 20-years at headline rate and levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) of P6.08/kWh back in January. Meralco then called for bids to challenge Terra Solar’s proposal through a competitive selection process (CSP). Meralco declared a failed bidding due to the lack of competing bids in April.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Budget deficit slightly lower YoY

 

The government’s fiscal deficit stood at P179.8bn in September, 0.6% lower YoY. On a MoM basis, the budget deficit was wider than the P72bn deficit in August. Revenues dropped MoM amidst higher expenses, specifically on interest payments. 

Tax revenues rose by 18.6% YoY but dropped 17.9% MoM; MoM drop is consistent with pre-pandemic trends. Non-tax revenues surged by 83% YoY, 48% MoM due to higher PAGCOR profit, BTr managed funds and interest on NG deposits

Primary spending gradually rose by 12% YoY and 9% MoM, indicating a gradual catch-up of agency disbursements. Interest payments surged 25% YoY and 95% MoM. 

YT-September budget deficit stood at P1.013tn, down 11% YoY and trending slightly lower than the government’s target.

 

 

Infrastructure spending still lackluster in August

The government spent P73.7bn on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in August (+4% YoY, -4% MoM). In terms of overall infra spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), the government spent P91.1bn (-1% YoY, -7% MoM).  

Overall infra spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) YT-August amounted to P789bn (+10% YoY). To reach the government’s full-year infra spend target of P1.2tn (+7% YoY), infra spend should average P103bn per month for the rest of the year.

Philippines to make indoor face mask use voluntary

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. is set to issue an executive order making the wearing of face masks indoor areas voluntary, according to Tourism Secretary Christina Frasco. Frasco said the decision was reached during a Cabinet meeting early Tuesday.  Masks will still be required in public transportation, medical transportation and medical facilities. 

According to Frasco, the direction of the Marcos administration is to lift the remainder of COVID-related travel restrictions. She added that easing measures will allow the country to be at part with ASEAN neighbors that have since “liberalized their mask mandates”.

BSP may match US Fed rate hikes

According to BSP Gov. Felipe Medalla, policy rates could be hiked by as much as 100bps before the year ends, in line with the large hikes expected from the US Federal Reserve. Gov. Medalla says that if the Fed hikes rates by 75bps in Nov, the BSP will “have to match it”. The BSP has raised policy rates by 225bps so far this year, among the steepest moves in the region. The next Monetary Board meeting is set for 17 November. 

 

Gov’t partially awards T-bills, yields rise

The government raised P6.6bn out of its P15bn goal from T-bills.  The auction was more than 1.4 times oversubscribed, attracting P20.9bn in total tenders.  The government made a partial award amid a pickup in demand for the T-bills due to banks needing to place their funds in debt instruments, according to National Treasurer Rosalia V. de Leon. 

The yield on the 91-day T-bill rose by 41bps to 4.22% (BVAL: 3.7036%)

The yield on the 182-day T-bill rose by 23.5bps to 4.65% (BVAL: 4.4823%)

The yield on the 364-day T-bill went up by 109.3bps to 4.875% (BVAL: 4.8598%)

Finance chief vows to keep peso from breaching P60

 

 

The Philippine government will act aggressively to prevent the peso from weakening to P60 per dollar. It is prepared to deploy billions of dollars more in reserves, according to the Secretary of Finance and concurrently member of the BSP Monetary Board, Benjamin Diokno. He has assured President Marcos that the peso slump would have eased by year-end to “where we want it to be” at P55, citing another US$15.8bn of inflows from overseas Filipinos and BPOs from now until year-end, of which US$10bn will be used to defend the peso. The government also plans to sell USD-denominated bonds targeting overseas Filipinos in December, to capture at least 10% of remittances or at least US$3bn.

The BSP Monetary Board is also considering an additional 100bps of policy rate hikes in its last two meetings for 2022, taking the policy rate to 5.25% by year-end. The BSP has raised policy rates by 225bps so far this year, among the steepest moves in the region. 

 

 

Hot money outflows widen to US$367mn in September

Gross inflows amounted to US$892mn in September, +12.7% MoM. Gross outflows were higher by 43.4% MoM to US$1.3bn. The latest print brings YT-September foreign portfolio investments to a net inflow of US$221.6mn, a reversal from the US$494mn net outflow in the same period last year.
