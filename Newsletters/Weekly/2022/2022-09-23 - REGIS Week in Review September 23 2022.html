REGIS REPORT

23 September 2022

The PSEi dropped sharply this week, falling 4.4% WoW to close at 6259.54, and marking five straight weeks of declines.  The market fell on accelerated foreign selling, as investors reacted to this week’s Fed hike as well as forthcoming tightening. YTD, the PSEi is down 12%.

Turnover was light for most of the week, with average daily turnover at a mere US$89m,  as investors stayed sidelined ahead of the Monetary Board meeting on Thursday. Foreigners accounted for 45% of turnover and ended the week net sellers for -US$30.4m. Excluding SMC crosses (inflow +US$25.8m), net outflow should have been larger at US$56.2m.

Foreign selling was across the board, with property names ALI and SMPH both top sold at -US$13.6m and -US$7.5m respectively. Index heavy weights SM (-US$4.7m) and Globe (-US$4m) also saw significant outflow. Outside of SMC, foreigners bought consumer names Jollibee  (+US$7.6m), and Wilcon (+US$1m). Banks meanwhile saw late foreign demand after BSP’s rate hike.

The BSP raised policy rates by +50bp to 4.25%, in line with consensus expectations. The +50bp increase however is still short of the Fed’s  +75bp hike this week. The current interest rate differential (BSP policy rate at 4.25% vs the Fed Funds Rate at 3.00% to 3.25%) is still much narrower than it has been.  The gap between the two has averaged ~300bp over the past decade.  This points towards continued pressure on the PHP, which this week depreciated 1.85% to PHP58.495/US$.

Declines were widespread – with 25 of the 30 index names ending lower for the week.

Converge (-12.5% WoW) was the top decliner, dropping steeply on talks of possible deletion from the MSCI.  Incidentally, the board of Converge approved a P1.5bn share buyback program. The size of the program is equivalent to ~100m shares or 1.4% of market capitalization.

Leading the decliners as well were interest rate sensitive property stocks Ayala Land (-12.4% WoW) and Robinsons Land (-10.1% WoW).  Losses were across sectors, with ACEN (-8.6% WoW), PLDT (-7.4% WoW), and SM (-7.2% WoW) also dropping.

Globe (+2.6% WoW) meanwhile managed a gain, on news that a unit of Australian investment bank Macquarie is finalizing a deal to buy 1,350 of Globe’s telecom towers for around US$400m. The total tower sale would be estimated at over P90bn (including the first two tranches) and would lower Globe’s net gearing to 1.0x and would produce significant incremental value for Globe of around P500/sh. Meanwhile, Globe’s e-wallet GCash is launching a stock trading feature on its app by next month, adding to its features and functionalities. 

Also gaining this week was SMC (+2.8% WoW). SMC has issued notices of termination of their PSAs effective Oct 4 – if ERC denies its appeal for temporary relief under its fixed price contracts with Meralco (-3.2% WoW). Meralco has apparently signed emergency supply agreements to replace the supply SMC is threatening to withhold. Either would mean an increase in power prices of at least 3% or more to the residential consumers.

Emperador (+2.0% WoW), Aboitiz Equity (+1.1% WoW),  and Wilcon (+0.5% WoW) also closed higher.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property - Government allows BPO firms to retain incentives while maintaining WFH
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

BPO industry can retain incentives while maintaining WFH

The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) paved the way for BPO companies to keep their current level of fiscal incentives while maintaining policies allowing work-from-home (WFH). While this development strengthens the long-term viability of the Philippine BPO sector, we believe it will be negative for office demand in the near-term.

Why would BPO companies lose incentives because of WFH?

As mentioned in our previous report (“Work-from-home and the long-term demand for office space”), most BPO firms are registered with the Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) , which requires at least 90% of employees to work inside the PEZA zone/building. This requirement was relaxed because of the pandemic but was set to be reinstated beginning 12 September 2022. Understandably, most BPO employees wanted WFH policies maintained. The industry thus lobbied to retain their fiscal incentives while maintaining WFH policies.

What must BPO firms do to retain incentives?

The FIRB allowed PEZA-registered firms to transfer their registration to the Board of Investments (BOI), an incentive-granting agency that does not have a cap on employees working from home. Until December, PEZA-registered firms will follow status quo, wherein up to 30% of employees can work from home. This should be sufficient time to transfer their registration to BOI.

Good for the BPO sector but office vacancy will likely worsen

This development will give BPO firms the flexibility to fully implement relaxed WFH policies without the risk of losing incentives. Thus, the attractiveness of the Philippines as a BPO destination will be preserved. However, we suspect that most BPO companies will implement policies that allow employees to work from home for 1-2 days/week. This could mean that in the worst case (for office landlords), the BPO industry will give up 20%-40% of its office footprint. Although the BPO Association projects annual employee growth of c. 8%, this development could cause Metro Manila office vacancy to rise above our 21%-22% forecast in 2023E-2024E (2Q22 office vacancy: 18%).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Macquarie said to be buyer for Globe’s 1,350 telecom towers

A unit of the Australian investment bank Macquarie is said to be the buyer of the 3rd tranche of Globe’s telecom towers. Globe previously disclosed the sale of two batches of towers numbering ~5,700. It also said it was finalizing the sale of a third batch numbering 1,350. The price was reported to be around USD$400m which would be around P23bn. That would equate to a price of P16m per tower and would push total gross proceeds from the three batches to over P90bn. Regis estimates the incremental value to Globe from the tower sales would be around P550/share based on the post-rights shares.

Stock trading via GCash set for November soft launch

The Philippine Stock Exchange formalized a milestone partnership between GCash and stockbrokerage AB Capital Sec paving the way for the entry of millions of first-time stock market investors. The venture, which will be known as GStocks, will hold its soft launch by November this year while the nationwide rollout was targeted by early 2023, according to PSE president Ramon Monzon. Monzon had previously said the partnership was targeting the entry of about nine million new investors in five years. Last year, the PSE reached 1.6mn participants due to the surge in online trading accounts.

Villar power company to conduct REIT IPO

Premiere Island Power REIT Corp, a firm led by Manuel Paolo Villar, plans to raise up to P3.2bn in a REIT IPO at the maximum offer price of P2/share for 1.4bn secondary shares plus overallotment option of 210mn shares. Premiere’s portfolio consists of land, land rights, power plant assets, and other ancillary infrastructure leased to the sponsor, Prime Asset Venture Inc (PAVI). The weighted average lease expiry is 9.24 years and the total generating capacity was said to be at 21.27MW.

Converge ICT announces P1.5bn buyback program

Converge ICT disclosed that its board approved a P1.5bn program yesterday (Sept 22). The amount of P1.5bn would be equivalent to ~1.3% of current market capitalization. The stock plunged 11% yesterday supposedly due to speculation over its deletion from global benchmark indices. The stock is now down 66% from its 52-week high. The company already has 260m shares in Treasury after it purchased stock from pre-IPO investor Warburg. CNVRG’s leverage ratios are comfortable with net gearing at 0.5x.

 

ERC to decide on SMC rate increase petition by Oct. 4

The Energy Regulatory Commission assured that it would issue a fair decision on or before Oct. 4 on the temporary relief sought by San Miguel Corp and Manila Electric (MER). “We are mindful of the timeline in SMC’s letter to Meralco”. In SMC’s letter to Meralco, South Premiere Power Corp and San Miguel Energy Corp had issued notices of termination of their PSAs, effective Oct. 4, if the regulatory body denied its appeal for relief. Meanwhile, Meralco previously said that it had already secured emergency power supply deals with “four to five” generators in case the termination of the PSAs will push through. It claimed that preserving the existing PSAs with SMC is the least cost option for consumers. “Weighted average offers from the different suppliers were between P7 and P8/kWh for one year supply”.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Congress not keen on carbon tax

Lawmakers in the Lower House may likely be cold to any measure that will impose a carbon tax according to Ways and Means Committee chair Rep. Joey Salceda. A carbon tax would make electricity more expensive, especially with global prices of coal at US$400 per metric ton. Coal accounts for 55% of power generation. Last month, the Department of Finance commenced the study on the viability of a carbon tax.

BSP hikes policy rate by 50bps

The Monetary Board increased the overnight borrowing rate by 50bps to 4.25% within street expectations (50bps). “Price pressures continue to broaden. Second-round effects continue to manifest, with inflation expectations remaining elevated in Sep due min wage hikes and transport fare hikes”. The BSP is targeting inflation to average 5.6% this year, up from previous target of 5.4%. The YT-August ave inflation rate at 4.9%, with latest print in August at +6.3%. Inflation may start to decelerate in 4Q22 and ease to within the BSP’s 2-4% target range by the second half of next year. It raised its inflation forecast for 2023 slightly to 4.1% (from 4%) but expects inflation to ease to 3% in 2024. The BSP has raised its policy rates by 225bps year-to-date (vs. Fed: +300bps).

POGO shut down after rescue of 43 Chinese nationals

Authorities rescued 43 Chinese nationals who claim they were abducted and forced to work for Lucky South 99 Outsourcing, a licensed POGO company. Some lawmakers and members of the Marcos administration have recently called for the abolition of POGOs due to their relation to crime.

Senator files bill to ban online gambling

In light of the spate of news linking kidnapping cases to online gambling firms, Senate Majority Leader Joel Villanueva filed Senate Bill 1281 or the anti-online gambling act. The bill seeks to prohibit online gambling and the placing of wagers or bets online. PAGCOR said that there are only 26 active POGO firms in the country. At the end of 2019, there were 60 licensed POGO firms and occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. POGO firms took up about 20,000sqm of office space in 2Q22. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-2Q22. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock. The Entertainment City casinos were able to secure online casino permits from PAGCOR during the pandemic to help augment lost revenue from mobility restrictions.  The revenue contribution from the online casino of BLOOM PM is less than 5%.

Congressman proposes to limit POGO operations within specific zones

Albay Representative Joey Salceda, who is the chairman of the House Committee on Appropriations, has opposed the call to ban POGOs, saying that it will only create an underground sector. Instead, he proposes to limit POGO operations within POGO-specific zones. The new licensees will be restricted to these zones while existing licensees will be allowed to finish their current leases. Regis believes that these POGO-specific zones will likely be in provincial areas. Thus, this proposal would still lead to higher office (and residential) vacancy in Metro Manila.

August BOP deficit at US$572mn

The country’s balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$572mn in August, based on preliminary data. This is a reversal of the US$1bn BOP surplus in August 2021. However, August’s BOP deficit was the lowest since April 2022. The government cited outflows from the national government’s foreign currency debt payments and wider trade deficit. This brings 8M22 BOP deficit to US$5.5bn, higher than the US$253mn deficit in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the GIR dropped to a revised figure of US$97.4bn (vs. US$98.97bn based on preliminary data) in August. This is down from US$99.8bn in July 2022 and US$108.8bn in end-2021. The August GIR represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.8 months’ worth of imports and is also is also about 7x the country’s short-term external debt.

Gov’t sees sugar prices stabilizing in Nov as supply normalizes

The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) expects retail prices of sugar to start easing by November as imports arrive and domestic supply normalizes. Earlier this month, the SRA authorized the importation of 150k MT of refined sugar, which are expected to arrive by 15th Nov. The milling season also started on 1st Sep, and mills are expected to be ramping up capacity until Nov. The SRA is targeting to lower the average retail price of refined sugar to P70-80/kg. Approx 17-28% lower than the avg retail price of ~P97/kg (+87% YoY) as of Sep 9.

BOP/Current account deficits balloon in 1H22; BSP raises deficit forecasts

The government’s balance of payments position stood at a deficit of US$3.1bn in 1H22, wider than the US$1.9bn deficit in 1H21. The current account deficit was almost 9x larger YoY at US$12.03bn in 1H22 vs US$1.35bn in 1H21 due to a higher trade deficit. The financial account reversed to a net inflow of US$7.2bn in 1H22 from US$1.4bn net outflows in 1H21 due to lower net outflows of portfolio investments and higher net inflows of other investments. The government raised its BOP deficit projections for 2022/2023 to US$8.4bn/US$2.5bn from US$6.3bn/US$2.6bn previously. It now expects larger current account deficits of US$20.6bn/20.1bn in 2022/2023 from US$19.1bn/US$20.5bn previously.

LTFRB approves fare hikes across public transportation

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) has approved fare hikes for several modes of transportation, effective by October 4. This is the first blanket fare increase implemented this year. Prior to this, the government only allowed fare increases for jeepneys (implemented in June). The approved fare increases were lower than petitions. Transport services account for 4.5% of the CPI basket.
