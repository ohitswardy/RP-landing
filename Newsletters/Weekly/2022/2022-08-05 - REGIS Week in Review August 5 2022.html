REGIS REPORT

05 August 2022

The Philippines gained this week, advancing 1.4% amidst a generally strong 2Q earnings season and as foreign selling paused. Foreigners were net buyers for US7.7m , with largest inflows in Universal Robina as well as Ayala Corp.  Semirara (-1.2% WoW) which was an addition in today’s PSEi rebal saw inflows as well.  

Semirara replaced Security Bank (-9.4% WoW) in the PSEi index.  The rebal also led to increased weightings for Ayala Corp (+12.4% WoW), ACEN (+5.9% WoW), Converge (+5.3% WoW) and Monde (+11.0% WoW). These upweights surged this week, pushing the index higher.  Semirara meanwhile had risen markedly in the run-up to the announcement.

Investors found themselves in the thick of reporting season hich yielded more than a few positive surprises. Overall earnings reflect solid economic recovery.

Banks profits were either in line or ahead of forecasts but a consistent theme was NIM expansion, improving asset quality,  and loan growth acceleration which reached 9% for both BDO (-2.2% WoW) and Metrobank (-1.9% WoW).  

The rebound in consumer spending was quite evident in consumer results this week, despite where inflation levels have been. Revenue for SM’s (+0.6% WoW) SM Retail is now almost at pre-pandemic levels (2Q19) while RRHI (+10.0% WoW)  saw consolidated SSSG accelerate to +15% in 2Q.  For Puregold (+4.8% WoW), net sales grew 14% YoY as SSSG during the quarter accelerated for both brands.

Meanwhile, volumes for URC (+10.5% WoW) remained healthy despite price adjustments the company has had to make (~12% cumulatively since 3Q21) to help mitigate the impact of input cost inflation.  The same can be said for CNPF (+6.4% WoW) which had healthy volume growth (>10%) despite the mid-single digit price increase.

The consumption rebound was reflected as well in SM Prime’s (+0.3% WoW) numbers, with its forecast-beating result boosted by a 51% QoQ jump in Philippine mall revenue, already 85% of 2Q19 revenue. On the other hand, residential revenue fell 50% QoQ and YoY due to sales cancellations.  An increase in cancellations also led to a 12% drop in Ayala Land’s (+2.6% WoW) residential revenue.  ALI’s rental segment performed well however, with mall revenue in 2Q surging 170% YoY.

Stronger-than-expected revenues from MPI’s (-4.3% WoW) tollroad units, apart from better-than-expected results from Merlaco (-7.1% WoW) also helped MPI beat expectations in 2Q.  Organic daily traffic volumes for the tollroad unit rebounded by 26% YoY, already exceeding pre-pandemic levels.

Meanwhile, ICTSI’s (-4.9% WoW) 2Q trended ahead of estimates as operations remained robust.  Yield per TEU remained high at US$179 in 2Q and throughput volumes rose +6% YoY, slightly accelerating from 1Q (+5%). 

Semirara also delivered a big beat, with 2Q22 core profit was surprisingly strong at P10.07bn, buoyed by lower-than-expected cash and non-cash costs at the coal mining unit.

AP’s (-5.1% WoW)  2Q22 core income rebounded +111%QoQ and  59%YoY), boosted by income contributions from both units of GNPower Dinginin (2x668MW).

In the meantime, PLDT’s (+3.3% WoW)  1H core income came in ahead of estimates despite softness on the revenue side as the wireless business contracted.

Universal Robina - 2Q22 margins near bottom; adjusting TP, maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

2Q22 EBIT flat YoY as margin squeeze tempers strong top line growth
URC reported 1H22 net profit of P6.2bn, up 28% YoY on a recurring basis. EBIT for the period inched +2% YoY to P7.4bn. Both NPAT and EBIT are broadly in line with Regis estimates, but trending above consensus expectations. For 2Q22 alone, recurring net profit jumped 48% YoY. EBIT (P3.3bn), however, was practically flat YoY as margins contracted across the board tempering the strong top line growth (+24% YoY).

We believe margins have likely bottomed in 2Q, though a full recovery is not likely until late-2022, when the full impact of price increases and declining inventory costs comes through. We are rolling over our valuation to 2023E and raising our TP to P140/sh (+9%); maintaining Buy.

Domestic BCF: strong volumes despite price increases
Domestic BCF sales grew 24% YoY, partly boosted by price increases. We estimate the impact of the cumulative price increases since 3Q21 is ~12%. Despite the sizeable price increase, volumes grew double-digit (>10%) across the board, by our estimate.

Margins contract QoQ as input costs peakEBIT margins contracted nearly 200bps on a QoQ basis across segments as URC bore the brunt of more expensive inputs (eg wheat, palm oil, among others), aggravated by higher logistics cost. The more significant price adjustments for the BCF segment were implemented in Apr/May, so the full impact will not be felt until 2H22.

Margins have likely bottomed, but recovery could be protractedWe believe margins may have bottomed in 2Q22 as commodity prices come off recent peaks. However, we believe margins could still see some near term volatility given the depreciation of the PHP (and most other currencies against the USD), as well as supply chain issues. Management is ready to respond with further pricing action, but is also wary of the potential impact of inflation on demand as the pent up demand normalizes.

 
Aboitiz Power - 2Q22: Income rebounds on GNPD contribution
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph| Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q22 profit +111%QoQ; 1H -9% but 2H should be better
AP’s 2Q22 core profit jumped to P6.231bn (111%QoQ, 59%YoY) largely on the back of a rising contribution from GNPD and better plant availability. Energy sold by its generation business jumped to 7.707GWh (27%QoQ, 36%YoY), while distribution volumes also recovered in 2Q (14%QoQ, 3%YoY) post-Typhoon Odette. The result brings 1H22 core income to P9.187bn (-9%), which could still be on track to hit our FY22E estimates on the continued ramp-up of GNPD and a recovery in distribution margins in the second half.

Generation: higher plant availability, both units of GNPD drive sales
Core income from generation/RES amounted to P6.924bn (112%QoQ, 80%YoY), helped by a growing contribution from GNPD and a higher availability of plants. Management estimates GNPD Units 1 and 2 (1,336MW) contributed P2.5bn in income to AP in 2Q22 and P3.4bn in 1H22. Unit 1 is serving contracts while Unit 2 is selling to the spot. Availability of plants also improved, with GMEC’s 1Q issues largely resolved, and TVI and Pagbilao plants back online in 2Q. Absolute gross margin also jumped 29%QoQ. Average selling prices to spot (P6.62/kWh +16%) and bilateral contracts (BCQ) (P5.95/kWh +31%) continued to climb.

Distribution: Volumes recover but profit still weighed down by cost pass- through.
Distribution income amounted to P793mn (7%QoQ, -35%YoY), still largely weighed down by timing differences relating to the recovery of rising pass- through costs. Reported margins were P1.37/kWh in 1H22, well below the 1H21 average of P1.64/kWh. We expect this to reverse in succeeding periods when prices stabilize. Total volumes across its utilities amounted to 1,480GWh (14%QoQ, 3%YoY), recovering from a soft 1Q (from the effects of Typhoon Odette).

Outlook
Management expects earnings to catch up in succeeding periods as 1) spot prices continue to be elevated, 2) both units of GNPD increase in profit contribution and 3) margin recovery in distribution. They also estimate there could be another P1bn in claims in 2H relating to prior year’s plant outages.

 
BDO Unibank - 1H22 NPAT in line; rapid asset quality improvement (despite Udenna exposure)
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Loans accelerate, NIMs widen, NPLs fall
BDO reported 1H22 NPAT of P23.94bn, bang in line with our full-year forecast of P47.9bn (consensus P47.7bn). The themes are broadly in line with our recentsector report – loan growth accelerated to 9% YoY (from 7% in 1Q); and NIMs expanded 8bps QoQ. Most notable, in our view, was the rapid improvement in asset quality, with NPLs down 33bps QoQ to 2.39% and NPL cover of 138%. During the briefing, management addressed recent (and very public) issues with the Udenna group, and downplayed the impact of potential defaults.

Loans accelerate; NIMs expand; Fees above pre-Covid levels
Customer loans grew 9.2% YoY, faster than the 7.4% in 1Q22, and the fastest YoY pace since the start of the Covid-19 pandemic (Fig 1). Growth was driven by large corporates, up 10% YoY, while consumers were up 7%. NIMs expanded 8bps QoQ to 3.99% (Figs 2-4). Margins should continue to widen in coming quarters, as the central bank recently hiked policy rates. BDO also has P40bn worth of bonds (4.4% yield) maturing in 2H22 which should lower cost of funds. Fee incomes grew 15% in 1H22 and are now consistently above pre-Covid levels.

NPLs fall to 2.39%; provision expense kept high to boost cover to 138%
Asset quality improved across the board (Figs 5 and 6). NPL ratio fell to 2.39%, down 33bps QoQ. Despite this, provisions were up 21%, taking NPL cover to 138%. Management wants to rebuild coverage to 150-170% to provide a buffer for future crises. Restructured Loans also improved to 4.9% from 5.2% in 1Q22.

A word (or two) on Udenna group, and other sources of new NPLs
Management addressed recent issues surrounding the Udenna group. BDO has P36bn in loans to the group, roughly c1.5% of total loans. The bank estimates that existing provisions plus collateral value are equivalent to 130-160% of the loans; a potential default will not have a material impact on BDO’s financials. More broadly, management opined that NPL risks from inflation and the weak PHP would likely be more sector and company specific, rather than a systemic issue.

 

 
SM Prime - 2Q22: Mall revenue surges, residential cancellations bite
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Robust malls compensate for residential cancellations
SM Prime’s 2Q22 net profit rose 29% YoY to P6.7bn. Two things stood out: 1) on the positive side, mall revenue surged thanks to the economic reopening; 2) on the other hand, residential revenue halved due to sales cancellations.

As a result, 1H22 net profit climbed 21% to P14.1bn, slightly ahead of our forecast (in line with consensus). These results could foreshadow both strong mall operations and residential sales cancellations for the rest of the sector.

Philippine mall revenue already 85% of pre-COVID levels
Mall operations clearly benefited from the reopening of the economy and the subsequent revenge spending. 2Q22 Philippine mall revenue jumped 51% QoQ (+156% YoY) to P12.4bn, already 85% of 2Q19 revenue. This is particularly remarkable since some tenants still received rental concessions during the period. Mall operations should improve even further since the company intends to remove all concessions by end-3Q22.

Steven Tan, head of the mall division, said that most tenants already exceeded pre-COVID sales levels in June and our recent mall visits suggest that foot traffic remained robust in July.

Residential revenue drops 50% as cancellations bite
Despite the reopening of the economy, some residential buyers are still experiencing financial difficulties. Thus, due to cancellations, 2Q22 residential revenue fell about 50% QoQ and YoY to P6.2bn, the lowest level since 2018.

Despite the cancellations, 2Q22 reservation sales were still strong, growing 25% YoY to P28.3bn, 125% of the 2019 average. We suspect that the share of provincial projects increased, as the average price/unit fell to P4.6mn, from P5.1mn in 1Q22 (P7.1mn in 2Q21).

 
Metropolitan Bank & Trust Co - 1H22: industry-best asset quality fuels earnings beat
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT well ahead on lower provisions, surprise trading gains
Metrobank reported 1H22 NPAT of P15.6bn, up 33% and tracking well ahead of Regis FY22E estimate of P27.2bn (consensus P28.4bn). Core operations were in line with our expectation — net loan growth sped up to 10%, NIMs widened 9bps QoQ, and fees were strong (+18% YoY). The PPOP beat was driven primarily by surprise trading/forex gains, at least through the P&L. The main feature though was a 46% YoY drop in provisions, backed up by industry-leading NPL ratio of 1.91% and NPL cover of 196%.

Loans jump 10% YoY, NIMs +9% QoQ; Trading/fx gains surprise, but...
MBT’s core operations were broadly in line with our sector thesis. Loan growth accelerated to 10.2% YoY from 5.2% in 1Q22 (Fig 1), driven by corporate loans (+12%) and credit cards (+16%). NIMs expanded by 9bps QoQ, as loan yields gained 11bps QoQ and deposit costs were up a milder 5bps QoQ (Figs 2-4). We expect even wider margins in 2H22 given the 75bp policy rates hike in July. Trading/forex gains were strong at P3.4bn (-15% YoY) despite rising interest rates. However, we note that mark-to-market losses on the FVOCI/AFS book were significantly larger at P17.0bn in 1H22 and P7.9bn in 2Q alone. To be fair, if the bank holds to maturity, the losses will revert to gains closer to maturity.

Industry-best asset quality allows for lower provisions
Provision expense in 1H22 was P3.8bn, down 46%, with 2Q alone at just P1.78bn, down 61% YoY. MBT’s NPL ratio dropped 25bps QoQ to 1.91%, about half thesector average (3.85% in May). Despite the drop in provisions, NPL cover jumped to 196% from 179% in 1Q. Both ratios are the best among banks we cover.

Maintaining forecasts, for now
Despite the 1H22 beat, we are inclined to retain our FY22E forecasts. The large FVOCI losses underscore the volatility of trading/forex gains. And while thebank’s asset quality is impressive, we are wary that inflation and the weak PHP could see a small rebound in credit costs in 2H22. 

 
SM Investments - 1H income rises 27% as consumers return
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Net profit for 1H jumps 27% to P25.5bn
SM Investments reported another strong quarterly result as the return of consumer spending lifted revenues for its retail-linked segments, and banking benefited from a resumption of credit growth and widening interest margins. Net income for the half rose 27% to P25.5bn, which implied 2Q income was also up 27% to P13.5bn. These numbers appear on track to beat Street estimates for the year (P38bn). Beyond the improving core businesses, the acquisition of geothermal assets (to be closed this month) will also boost SM’s income.

Positive near-term prospects
Banking again accounted for almost half (47%) of group earnings in 2Q. Near- term prospects are looking good with loan growth accelerating and NIMs expanding. Meanwhile, SM Prime’s mall business is approaching (85%) pre- pandemic normal and is likely to get there before year-end.

Retail: Record profits helped by rent concessions
Revenue for SM Retail rose 31% YoY to P89bn in 2Q, with growth from the Non- Food segments more pronounced as those were affected more by the lockdowns last year. 2Q revenue for the retail business is now only 2% below the same quarter in 2019 (pre-pandemic).

Overall, income for the entire Retail segment amounted to P4.37bn (+116% YoY), the highest profit ever tallied for the same quarter, despite revenues not quite (but almost) at their peaks. We believe rental concessions from sister firm SM Prime played a big part in the lower costs cited by management. These rental concessions are likely to cease within 3Q.

Beyond the big P&L items, we also note that the grocery business (“Food Retail”) reported revenue growth of +15% YoY on SSSG of +4.7% for 2Q22. It’d be interesting to see how this compares with other supermarket operators.

 
Century Pacific Food - 2Q22: better-than-expected margins despite input cost pressure; raising TP
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

2Q22 margins crimped by higher cost; adjusting estimates and TP
CNPF’s net profit for 2Q22 rose 8% YoY to P1.5bn, bringing the 1H figure to P2.95bn (+9% YoY). The YTD figure is trending ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. The outperformance was largely because CNPF is weathering the current environment of rising input costs better than we thought it would.

We are adjusting our 2022-23E EPS estimates by +6-10%, rolling forward our valuation to 2023E, and raising our TP to P31/sh (+15%). We believe demand for CNPF’s core branded products will continue to be resilient, while margins should stabilize as the impact of price increases comes through. We expect margin expansion to come through by late-2022, barring any further cost inflation from current levels. Maintaining Buy.

Branded business: demand resilient despite price increases
CNPF saw strong growth across its two main segments during the quarter. The core branded business (78% of revenues) grew 19% YoY, partly boosted by a mid-single digit (YTD) price increase. The implied volume growth was 

strong at >10%, driven by (1) the economy’s reopening and (2) the defensive nature of its core canned fish and meat products even as consumers tightened their belts. We likewise estimate that the emerging categories (e.g. pet food, coconut milk, alternative meats) and new products contributed another ~100bps of growth. Meanwhile, the OEM business jumped +25% YoY / +41% QoQ, recovering from previous quarters.

Input, freight cost inflation pressures margins
EBITDA margin contracted YoY and QoQ as price increase were not enough to fully offset the increase in raw material and freight cost. Prices of key commodities, such as skipjack tuna, meat, and skimmed milk peaked around Mar-Apr.

Margins likely approaching the bottom; further pricing action planned
We believe that margins are likely approaching the bottom as prices of CNPF’s key raw materials (particularly skipjack tuna, meat, and tin plate) come off recent peaks. Management, however, expects margin expansion to take time as it winds down high-cost inventory and implements a few more rounds of price adjustments (+2-4% for the balance of year). Meanwhile, demand for CNPF’s core branded products will likely be resilient even amidst an inflationary environment.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Reducing rating to Hold after share price outperformance
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph | Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Investor aversion to coal tempers attractive valuation
SCC’s share price has risen by 19% since mid June, outperforming the PCOMP by 18% and the Newcastle thermal coal price index by 10% over the same period. We last wrote about SCC here: last report on June 15. The current share price, in our view, reflects the balance between attractive valuations (5x PER, 16% yield) and investor aversion to coal exposure. We tweak our near-term profit estimates to incorporate 1H results but maintain our TP of P38/sh and downgrade our rating to Hold from Buy.

2Q22: Another stellar performance, unlikely to recur in 2H
SCC’s 2Q22 profit came in better than expected at P10.07bn, thanks in large part to lower-than-expected costs (both cash and non-cash) at the coal mining unit. We do not expect 1H22 profit of P24.9bn to recur in the 2H, as (1) non-cash expenses are likely to catch up, as capex is expected to rise HoH and (2) production will also be lower. While global thermal coal prices in July-to-date are higher than in 2Q22, further ASP improvements will likely be capped since management has already locked in its prices for the domestic market.

Potential upside risks unlikely to be resolved soon
President Marcos Jr. appointed Raphael Lotilla as the Secretary of the Department of Energy (DOE), the main government agency regulating SCC’soperations. It is unclear at this stage whether Mr. Lotilla will act on the renewal of SCC’s coal operating contract expiring in July 2027, which is necessary before the company can pursue its expansion plans. The contract renewal and a subsequent expansion into cement, all else being equal, could raise our valuation by c. P6/sh (+15%). We believe these potential upside scenarios may take a while to be resolved.

Balance of risks look even for now
We value SCC’s coal mining and power units separately using DCF analysis, based on contract life and plant life, respectively. The key risk to our forecasts/valuation are coal prices and we estimate every 10% increase/(decrease) in our benchmark coal price assumptions could raise/(lower) our profit estimates by 12% and our TP by 6%.

 
PLDT - 2Q: Earnings appear ahead of estimates amidst soft top line
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H core income rises +9%, ahead of expectations despite soft top line
PLDT reported net income of P16.7bn for 1H22, up +30% YoY. Stripping out one- off items but including losses at Voyager, we estimate core income for 1H at P15.5bn, +9% YoY. This is trending ahead of our estimates despite revenues being on the soft side. Service revenue for 1H rose +6% YoY to P97.1bn but the wireless business saw another quarter of YoY contraction. Proportionately lower costs helped lift core earnings.

Home broadband net adds disappoint; mobile reports another weak quarter
Home broadband revenue for 2Q was still up a healthy +21% YoY (+6% QoQ), behind subscriber gains over the past year. Net adds, though, were a disappointment as PLDT added only a net 67k wired subscribers in 2Q, the lowest since 1Q20 as churn rose sharply.

Weakness in the mobile business persisted, with revenues down 2% YoY for the quarter and 4% for 1H. Management indicated July figures remain soft and seemed wary about the outlook for demand over the near future.

Reducing future depreciation
PLDT reported a chunky P16.5bn gain from the partial completion of its tower sales and offset this with the accelerated depreciation of its fixed assets for a similar amount. This accounting move will cosmetically lift pre-tax earnings by over P1bn/quarter for the next few periods, allowing PLDT to comfortably meet its earnings guidance for this year (“telco core income” of P33bn).

Dividends of P75/share declared
As of Aug, PLDT has completed the sale of 68% of the tower portfolio it agreed to sell for P77bn. It has thus declared a special dividend of P28/share, 2/3rds of what it earmarked as dividends arising from the tower sale. This is on top of the P47/share in regular dividends also declared on the same day. 

 

 
ICTSI - The beat goes on in 2Q22
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

2Q22 profit of US$144mn outperforms Regis and street forecasts
ICTSI recorded 2Q profit after perps distributions of US$144mn, an improvement of 9% QoQ and 62% YoY. This brought 1H22 profit up 70% to US$276mn, trending ahead of Regis and street forecasts on better-than-expected yield per TEU and a lower tax rate. 1H22 EBITDA of US$672mn (+26%) also appears on track to beat consensus estimates (US$1.27bn) and ours (US$1.31bn).

Volumes mildly better, up +6% YoY in 2Q
Volume growth in 2Q22 mildly accelerated to 6% from 5% in 1Q, driven by a rebound in volumes in Mexico and Iraq. Management also noted that group volumes in July were better MoM and breached 1mn TEUs, which was a record. Management is “cautiously optimistic” about its operating outlook, expectingglobal trade growth this year to moderate to 3-4%, a pace which management aims to beat.

Yield per TEU still elevated, beating forecasts
Yield per TEU softened QoQ to US$179, but was still higher by 10% YoY. The company implemented tariff hikes at its ports in Manila (May) and in Mexico. It recently implemented another hefty tariff hike in Melbourne in July and expects to implement more in PNG, Brazil, and Mexico in 2H. Yield per TEU in 1H22 reached US$181 (+13% YoY), well above our forecast of US$169.

EBITDA margin at 63% in 2Q, 1H
EBITDA in 2Q22 contracted slightly (-1%) QoQ to US$334mn, likely due to the uneven timing of fuel-related cost increases and tariff hikes. EBITDA margin stood at 63% in 2Q and 1H, in line with forecasts. Meanwhile, the planned consolidation of 50%-owned unit, Manila North Harbour, will optically lift EBITDA/EBIT but will not have an impact on earnings.

Other updates: Sudan, potential acquisitions, contract renewal in Poland
The company is close to reaching a re-payment arrangement with the Minister of Finance of Sudan, bolstering its hopes of recovering the remaining Euro188mn worth of receivables. Apart from its latest acquisition in Indonesia, management is also in active discussions with several private parties for other ports. To our surprise, the company also managed to secure a 30-year contract renewal in Poland, on very attractive terms.

1H22 operating profit in line as lower-than-expected tax rate flatters NPAT

While the August figure will likely be flattered by a low YoY base (lockdown in Aug 2021), we expect SSSG to taper as the YoY base normalizes. We are expecting FY22 SSSG to average +10% YoY, which is at the upper range of management’s FY22 guidance (+5-10%).

 
Puregold - SSSG accelerates in 2Q22 but margins remain stable
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H22 net profit +5% YoY to P4.2bn – broadly within expectations
PGOLD reported 1H22 net profit of P4.2bn, up 5% YoY. This is trending broadly within Regis expectations but mildly ahead of consensus. For 2Q22 alone, net profit rose 4% YoY despite the 17% jump in operating income due to a tax adjustment in 2Q21 (relating to CREATE). Net sales grew 14% YoY as SSSG during the quarter accelerated for both brands. However, this did not translate to a meaningful improvement in margins. EBIT margin in 2Q22 (7.6%) was largely flat on a YoY basis.

Average tickets diluted by the return of walk-in customers
The core Puregold brand reported SSSG of +5.6% in 2Q22, a turnaround from the -4.5% clip in the previous quarter. Same-store tickets declined by ~6% during the quarter, but same-store traffic growth accelerated to around +10% as foot traffic from Puregold’s walk-in customers continue to recover.

The traffic recovery of walk-in customers, however, is also diluting average tickets. Walk-in customers account for 70-75% of Puregold’s total store traffic, but generate significantly lower tickets (P700-800/ticket on average) vs institutional customers (i.e. Aling Puring members at P3000-4000/ticket). We understand that average tickets on a per-segment basis continue to grow QoQ and YoY.

Margins largely stable YoY but contract QoQ
Margins were largely unchanged YoY, but contracted on a QoQ basis. While the disclosures did not provide further detail, we believe the sequential contraction could be driven by (1) the timing of the recognition of supplier/trade support (2) the depreciation of the PHP against the USD (especially for S&R), and/or (3) additional discounting from the Aling Puring convention, which resumed in May.

It is unclear at this stage whether Mr. Lotilla will act on the renewal of SCC’s coal operating contract expiring in July 2027, which is necessary before the company can pursue its expansion plans. The contract renewal and a subsequent expansion into cement, all else being equal, could raise our valuation by c. P6/sh (+15%). We believe these potential upside scenarios may take a while to be resolved.

Balance of risks look even for now
We value SCC’s coal mining and power units separately using DCF analysis, based on contract life and plant life, respectively. The key risk to our forecasts/valuation are coal prices and we estimate every 10% increase/(decrease) in our benchmark coal price assumptions could raise/(lower) our profit estimates by 12% and our TP by 6%.

SM Prime has started charging full rent on most mall tenants

Steven Tan, President of SM Supermalls, said that in June, majority of mall tenants had exceeded pre-COVID sales levels. As a result, the company has started charging full rent on most mall tenants.

In February 2022 fixed-rent tenants received 40%-50% discounts on rent. Percentage-of-sales based tenants paid  % of sales + 30% of pre-COVID minimum rent. 

The mall segment appears to be recovering slightly faster than we expected. Regis expects Philippine same-mall revenue to reach 65% of 2019 levels this year (95% of 2019 levels in 2023). 

JG Summit trims stake in Meralco

JG Summit (JGS PM) sold 36mn Meralco (MER PM) shares (c. 3.2%) for P344/sh in an overnight placement. Price was a 6% discount to the last closing price on July 28. 

The company raised P12.4bn from the sale and trimmed its ownership in MER to c. 26.4% from 29.56% previously. Proceeds will be used to beef up its balance sheet as the economy faces headwinds. MER accounts for 28% of JGS’ NAV; JGS trades at a 5% discount to its market-based NAV of P54.13/sh.

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) was one of the buyers, purchasing 22.54mn shares (2%). This raised MPI’s ownership in MER to 47.5% from 45.5% previously. MER accounts for 65% of MPI’s NAV; MPI is trading at a 50% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.40/sh

Cemex PH: 2Q volumes drop 11% YoY; FY22 volume guidance cut

2Q22 EBITDA drops YoY, posts net loss.  Cemex (CHP PM) swung to a net loss of P528mn in 2Q22, after sales volumes dropped 11% YoY. This brings CHP’s 1H22 cumulative net loss to P266mn. 1H22 EBITDA stood at P2.12bn, down 9% YoY and trending BEHIND street expectations (FY22E: P4.6bn) but in line with ours (P3.6bn).  The near-term outlook remains uninspiring, with management signaling upward cost pressures on fuel, power, and distribution costs, as well as the possible non-extension of the safeguard duty.

Volumes drop 11% in 2Q, 8% in 1H; FY22 guidance revised down to “flat to low single-digit percentage decrease”.  CHP’s sales volumes fell 11% YoY and were up just slightly (+1%) QoQ.  Management blamed the election ban and bad weather, and consequently downgraded its FY22 volume growth guidance to “flat to low single-digit percentage decrease” from “mid-single digit percentage increase”.  With volumes in 1H down by 8% YoY, management expects volumes to recover strongly in 2H.

2Q ASPs up 9% YoY, 3% QoQ, broadly in line with FX movements and the slight uptick in landed costs.  We estimate the landed cost of bagged cement were flat YoY and were up 2% QoQ to US$68/MT while import volumes of third party cement traders dropped 31% YoY.  We believe competitive dynamics neither improved nor deteriorated during the quarter. Management still awaits the government’s decision on the extension of the safeguard duty, which accounts for c. 5% of import prices.

EBITDA margin likely to fall further in 2H.  EBITDA fell 17% YoY to P1.08bn in 2Q22, with EBITDA margin dropping 3ppts to 20%. Even though fuel costs per MT jumped 89% YoY, CHP’s power costs and distribution costs were broadly unchanged, thanks to favorable contracts. Management however noted that its power suppliers are negotiating to change its prices upwards, while the impact of higher diesel prices will start kicking in 2H. Costs will also increase in 2H, in line with its planned maintenance activities for its two plants.

San Miguel’s power unit says losses hit P15bn

SMC Global Power Hldgs. Corp. said that its two plants had suffered combined losses of P15bn from 2021 to date, citing skyrocketing coal prices and unilateral natural gas supply restrictions from Malampaya. This is according to San Miguel Corp. President and CEO Ramon Ang.

To cover the losses incurred from January to May 2022, the company had sought a rate increase on its contract capacity under the power supply agreements (PSAs) to be paid for a period of six months. This will allow them to recover P5.2bn of losses for January to May 2022. It is seeking to raise tariffs by P0.80/kwh to P5.10/kwh from P4.30 per kWh for its 670MW of contracted baseload capacity from the Ilijan plant. For the Sual plant, the company is seeking an average increase of P4.00/kwh to P8.30 per kWh from P4.30 per kWh for its 330-MW contracted baseload capacity. The net rate impact to Meralco is just P0.28/kwh over a period of six months

SM Investments receives SEC approval to acquire Philippine Geothermal Production Company

SM Investments received SEC approval to acquire the 81% stake of related parties in Allfirst Equity Holdings in exchange for shares of SM. Allfirst is the holding company for Philippine Geothermal Production Company (PGPC). 

PGPC operates geothermal fields in Tiwi, Albay and Makban (Makiling-Banahaw), Laguna. The geothermal output is sold to Aboitiz Power’s power plants which produce electricity from it. Tariffs are linked to coal prices. 

The transaction will bring SM’s ownership in PGPC to 100%. 

SM Investments will issue 21.5mn SM shares. The acquisition will increase SM’s shares outstanding by 17.4mn shares (or 1.4%).

MICT throughput volumes rise 9% YoY in 2Q22

Volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port in Manila, MICT, rose by 9% YoY to 685k TEUs in 2Q22. 

MICT’s 2Q volumes exceeded 2019 average by 12%. This brings 1H22 volumes at MICT up 12% YoY to 1.36mn TEUs, broadly in line with Regis FY22E (2.67mn TEUs, up 7% YoY). 

Regis estimates MICT accounts for 24% of ICT’s consolidated volumes. Recall that ICT’s consolidated volumes grew by 5% YoY in 1Q22, while MICT grew volumes by 16% YoY in 1Q22. 

Volumes for the two foreign container ports in Manila rose by 6% YoY to 960k TEUs in 2Q22, 2% higher than 2019 average. 1H22 volumes at the two ports in Manila are up by 8% YoY, broadly in line with 2016-2019 trends (+7%).

T-bill rates mixed

The government raised P15bn from T-bill auction, with demand reaching P43bn. This is the first time in three weeks that the Bureau of Treasury made a full award. Demand was heavily concentrated on the 91-day tenor. 

Rates were mixed: 91-day T-bill dropped by 18.3bps to 2.09% (BVAL: 2.2683%), 182-day T-bill rose by 4.6bps to 3.188% (BVAL: 2.8857%), and the 364-day T-bill rose by 12.4bps to 3.48% (BVAL: 3.2775%).
