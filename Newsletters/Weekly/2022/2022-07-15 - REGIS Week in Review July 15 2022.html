REGIS REPORT

15 July 2022

It was a rough week for the Phils, dropping 2.6% as funds continued to flow out amidst anemic liquidity levels.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) announced a surprise increase in policy rates, in the wake of the 9.1% print in US inflation. The off-cycle hike as well as the magnitude of the hike (75bp) surprised the market. After a series of hikes (two +25bp and +75bp), the BSP policy rate now stands at 3.25%, still well below pre-pandemic levels of 4.0%.  No mention was made to address the recent moves of the PHP but the move seemed to be aimed as well at the PHP’s steep decline.

Losers overwhelmed the gainers with 18 of the 30 components ending the week with losses.

The interest rate sensitive property sector bore the brunt, with Ayala Land (-12.9% MoM) dropping steeply this week on continued foreign selling. Other property counters fell as well, with the rise in interest rates seen to further impede sector recovery.  Robinsons Land (-7.9% MoM), Megaworld (-5.6% MoM), ended the week lower as well.

Macro proxies closed down. SM Investments (-7.6% WoW) and Ayala Corp (-10.5% WoW) fell significantly while GT Capital (-10.3% WoW) also fell.

Following recent strength, consumer names consolidated as well. Universal Robina (-5.1% WoW) dropped, as did Jollibee (-0.5% WoW). Puregold (-10.6% Wow) and Wilcon (-5.1% WoW) were weak. However, Monde (+8.1% WoW) was strong on eased investor worries over the news reports on a recall involving batches of its main product last week.

With banks seeing as beneficiaries of rising interest rates, banks BPI (+1.8% WoW) and BDO (+1.6% WoW) gained this week.

The power sector outperformed, as sentiment was helped  by the sharp spike in electricity spot prices (to P9/kwh in June, from P6.4/kwh in May).  Semirara (+3.7% WoW) has the largest spot exposure, AP (+2.8% WoW) should also benefit. Meralco (+3.7% WoW) also closed up.

Foreign selling amounted to US$46m this week. Biggest outflows were seen across large caps Ayala Land, SM Investments, Metrobank (-5.0% WoW). Meanwhile, marginal inflows were seen in Globe (-1.05% WoW).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ERC urged to junk motion of 3 firms to get out of power deal

The United Filipino Consumers and Commuters (UFCC) called on the Energy Regulatory Commission to dismiss the join motion filed by three leading power corporations to relieve them from the power supply agreements that set a fixed cost of electricity. Meralco, South Premier Power Corp and San Miguel Electric Corp filed the motion in a hearing conducted by the ERC on June 9. South Premier (670MW) and San Miguel Energy Corp (330MW) are contracted to Meralco at fixed prices (P4.93/kwh) from Dec 2019 to Dec 2029.

MPI tollroad unit yet to feel impact of high fuel prices; exceeds pre-pandemic traffic in June

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) has not felt the impact of fuel price increase according to MPTC CFO Daniel Lizo. Across its network, they are above 2019 traffic volumes by 5-6% in June. However, it has seen traffic drop by 6% in the first five days of July compared with June due to seasonality. MPTC accounts for c. 20% of MPI’s profit and NAV. MPI is trading at a 51% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.48/sh.

Emperador to list in Singapore on July 14

Emperador disclosed that it expects to be listed and commence trading on the main board of the Singapore Exchange Securities Trading Limited (SGX-ST) on July 14. It said it expects to satisfy the conditions set out in the eligibility-to-list letter. This includes release of an SGXNET announcement disclosing the latest share price of the company on the PSE and the Singapore dollar equivalent prior to the listing of the company on the SGX-ST. Emperador will maintain its primary listing on the PSE and the stock is anticipated to trade on both exchanges concurrently. Investment banks have been mandated to explore ways to introduce liquidity to the proposed secondary listing, subject to market conditions and satisfaction of the ETL conditions

Market foreign ownership falls in June

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership fell 63bps MoM to 20.7% (an all-time low) while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 79bps MoM to 24.9% (in June). Market net foreign selling amounted to US$207mn in June and the headline foreign ownership figures fell even further because of a reclassification of BPI shares from foreign-held to local. Regis estimates that if the reclassification of BPI shares had not occurred, market foreign ownership would have fallen 36 basis points to 21.0% while PSEi foreign ownership would have declined 40 basis points to 25.3%. 23 of the 30 PSEi constituents saw declines in their foreign ownership levels

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

BPI – foreign ownership fell 9 percentage points MoM to 18.8%. While the stock had the fourth highest net foreign selling in June (US$18.9mn), we estimate that 8.6 percentage points of the drop was due to a reclassification of shares held by an AC subsidiary from foreign to local. 

JFC – foreign ownership dropped by 64 basis points to 21.9% as net foreign selling amounted to US$13.5mn.

CNVRG – foreign ownership fell 62 basis points to 20.8% as net foreign selling reached US$12.5mn

SMPH – foreign ownership increased 15 basis points MoM to 23.6%. Thanks to an upweight in the FTSE Index, it also experienced the strongest net foreign buying (US$22.4mn).

June WESM prices rise to P9.01/kWh

The Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) said the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) price averaged P9.01/kWh in June, up from P6.42/kWh in May. IEMOP said that in May 26 to June 25 period, average supply was 15,214MW vs average demand of 11,325MW. Month on month, the average supply dropped by 192MW while supply margin decreased 258MW. The upward adjustment of market price was affected by the offer price of generators which are dependent on fuel costs.

Vehicle sales sustain strong recovery in June

Vehicle sales rose 27% YoY, 9% MoM to 28,601 units in June according to data from CAMPI/TMA. June industry sales have reverted to 93% of pre-pandemic levels from 86% the previous month. This brings 1H22 industry unit sales to 154,874, up 17% YoY and tracking slightly behind its 336k units (+25% YoY) year-end target. Toyota sold 80,909 units (+27% YoY) in 1H22, suggesting that unit sales in June alone jumped 36% YoY, 4% MoM to 15,241 units. Recall that Toyota implemented blanket price hikes of 1-2% in June. Market share dipped slightly to 53.3% in June from 55.8% in May. Toyota’s 1H22 sales are tracking ahead of management’s 2022 target (162k, +25% YoY) and Regis’ FY22E (158k, +22% YoY). Toyota accounts for 25% of GTCAP profit and 30% of NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 50% discount to its market-based NAV of P882/sh.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Trade deficit widens to US$5.7bn in May

The country’s trade deficit widened to US$5.68bn in May, based on preliminary data. This is higher than in US$5.35bn in April 2022 (revised up from prelim figure of US$4.77bn) and also higher than in May 2021 (US$3.18bn). This is the largest monthly trade deficit figure. Imports jumped 31% YoY to US$12bn, led by fuel (+129% YoY). Note that regional prices of refined oil products were up sharply in May, with gasoline up 74% YoY and diesel up 122% YoY. Imports of raw materials and consumer goods rose 24% YoY and 7% YoY. The capital goods imports also jumped by 22% YoY. Exports rose 6% YoY, 3% MoM to US$6.3bn. The cumulative 5M22 trade deficit now at US$24.9bn, up 70% YoY.

Marcos Jr. nominates Toni Yulo-Loyzaga as environment chief

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has nominated Ma. Antonia “Toni” Yulo-Loyzaga as secretary of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR). The nomination is still subject to the fulfillment of required documents. Once done, this will be her first political appointment

Ms. Loyzaga is a former executive director of Manila Observatory & current president of National Resilience Council. Her work largely focuses on disaster science and risk management. She also served as technical adviser of the Philippine Disaster Resilience Foundation and was a part of the Senior Advisory Board of the Armed Forces of the Philippines’ Command and General Staff College. President Marcos has yet to announce his selections for Dept. of Health, Science and Tech, and Human Settlements and Urban Dev’t.

 

FDI net inflows rises by 48% in April

Net inflows of foreign direct investments (FDI) grew by 48% YoY to US$989mn in April 2022. Net investments in equity and investment fund shares rose by 69% YoY to US$305mn. Equity other than reinvestment of earnings jumped by 128% YoY to US$206mn. Reinvestment earnings increased by 10% YoY to US$99mn. Net investments in debt instruments jumped by 41% YoY to US$684mn. This brings YT-April net FDI to US$3.4bn up 12% YoY. The BSP expects net FDI to reach US$11bn in 2022 from US$10.5bn in 2021.

Lotilla nominated DOE Secretary

President Bongbong Marcos has nominated Raphael Lotilla as next energy secretary according to Press Sec Trixie Cruz Angeles. Angeles however stressed that is right now a nomination pending clarification of his employment status. Lotilla is currently an independent director of Aboitiz Power and Enexor. Lotilla was energy secretary of GMA from 2005-2007. He was also previously president of PSALM and deputy director general of NEDA.

Marcos admin open to digital tax, tax on plastics

The government supports imposing 12% VAT on online purchases. The Bill will be tackled by the Senate in the upcoming 19th Congress. The estimated to generate P13bn in incremental revenues per year. It also plans to impose a levy on single-use plastic bags, which would raise P1bn per year in additional tax revenues. However, the Marcos admin is cold to the previous admin’s proposal to to slap new or higher taxes slapped on consumption plus a three-year deferral of scheduled personal income tax cuts. It is also not keen on slashing public spending on non-priority projects. Instead, Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said they will focus on improving tax administration. This targets to raise tax effort by 0.3-0.4% annually. Tax revenues to GDP stood at 14.1% in 2021, down from the peak of 14.5% in 2019.

BSP surprises with off-cycle 75bps rate hike

The BSP delivered a huge 75bps interest rate hike during a surprise off-cycle meeting. The overnight reverse repurchase rate is now at 3.25%. This is the biggest one-time rate hike delivered since year 2000. The policy rate has been increased by 125bps since the start of the year (vs YTD Fed: +150bps). The current policy rate remains below pre-pandemic levels (4%). The Monetary Board noted that favorable conditions arising from the strong rebound in growth thus far in the year suggest that the domestic economy can accommodate a further tightening. Monetary Board aims to anchor inflation expectations further and temper mounting risks to the inflation outlook. The economic team raised its inflation targets to 4.5%-5.5% last Friday, from 3.7%-4.7% previously. The 1H22 average inflation rate is at 4.4%, after rising by 6.1% in June.
