REGIS REPORT

07 October 2022

The Phils gained 3.3% this week, snapping a six-week losing streak in which the PSEI dropped over a thousand points.  The index’s gain this week took to it to just below the 6,000 level at 5932.19.  A rally on Tuesday in line with strong overnights boosted the market to +4.3% WTD, though subsequent declines pared the overall  advance.Turnover was uninspired, with average daily volumes hitting only US$74m.

Corporate news this week was focused on the San Miguel group. 

San Miguel Corp  (+3.1% WoW) agreed to buy 88.5% of Eagle Cement (+28.7% WoW)at P22.02/sh. SMC will also conduct a mandatory tender offer for the remaining 11.5% of Eagle. The price values Eagle at about P110bn, or an EV of about $277/MT of clinker capacity, significantly higher than replacement cost (c$150/MT). San Miguel says it will pay cash for the acquisition of Eagle Cement. Both SMC and EAGLE are controlled by Ramon S Ang. He is the Chairman of SMC, while the 88.5% shares of Eagle are owned directly and indirectly by Mr Ang and his family members.

Meanwhile, San Miguel and Meralco’s (+11.2% WoW)  joint application to adjust tariffs under their price power supply agreements (PSA) was rejected by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).  SMC had been asking for an adjustment in order to cope with rising fuel costs for  some 1000MW in  contracts expiring in Dec 2029.

SMC had been publicly saying that it would terminate the PSAs today (Oct 4) if the adjustments were not granted. SMC says it will continue to provide power to Meralco for 60 days until the termination of its fixed price power supply agreement (PSA) takes effect. 

Inflation jumped to 6.9% in September, accelerating sharply from the 6.3% in August. Putting upward pressure moving ahead are transport fares, which have been allowed to rise by as much another 9-20% starting October, which would take the YTD hikes to 15-30%. Nonetheless, the YoY base effects could start to fade as the CPI base was already going up as of 4Q21.

Gainers overtook losers 11 to 4.

The leading movers this week were last week’s top laggards SM (+4.8% WoW), ICTSI (+13.4% WoW), and SM Prime (+4.5% WoW). The three accounted for close to a quarter of the PSEi’s 191-point rise.  Other gainers Other strong gains were seen in Globe (+6.4% WoW), AEV (+5.5% WoW), JG Summit (+4.4% WOW), and BPI (+3.9% WoW).\

Meanwhile, Wilcon (+11.4% WoW) gave up the most this week, after holding the top performer spot last week.  Puregold (-4.4% WoW) was weak.

GTCAP (-3.75% WoW) fell as well, on speculation of MSCI deletion at the next rebal. Converge (-0.8%) was down for the same reason.

Foreigners ended the week slight net sellers for US$1m. Headline flows were skewed by multiple San Miguel crosses. Excluding these, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$24m. Biggest outflows were in ICT, BDO, and SMPH.

RESEARCH
Bank of the Philippine Islands - Merger with Robinsons Bank – less for scale, more for customer access
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

BPI-RBC merger – access to JG Group ecosystem

BPI confirmed negotiations for a merger with Robinsons Bank (RBC). The deal could increase BPI’s assets by 6-7%, and maybe sneak it past Metrobank (MBT PM) for the #2 spot among domestic non-SOE banks. However, we believe BPI was less interested in the size –loans, deposits, or branches – of RBC itself. Instead, in our view, the value of taking the JG Summit Group on as shareholders (6% of the merged entity) is in giving BPI potential access to the vast ecosystem of customers, suppliers, and tenants of Cebu Air (CEB PM), Universal Robina (URC PM), Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM), Robinsons Land (RLC PM), etc. While we reckon the synergies are likely to be earnings accretive over time, we maintain our earnings forecasts for now, and maintain our Buy call on the stock.

Does size matter? Merger to increase assets 6-7%, enough for the #2 spot

RBC is the country’s 12th largest domestic non-SOE bank by assets as of 1H22, roughly 6-7% the size of BPI (Figure 1, below). This could put BPI a shade behind MBT in assets, but ahead by loans and deposits. By branches and ATMs, though, RBC punched above its class, at 15-20% the size of BPI. However, we believe scale wasn’t BPI’s goal in the merger. In the last 10 years, BPI has organically grown its assets by a CAGR of about 11%. The bank has also been shrinking its branch network, in favor of digital (online/app) delivery of products and services.

Extracting synergies with JG Summit group is the main attraction

The JG Summit group will end up owning roughly 6% of the merged entity. We believe the main attraction of the merger is that BPI could potentially offer more banking products (credit cards, BNPL, for example) to millions of the JG group’s airline and retail loyalty programs, and extend loans to the group’s supply chain.

Likely accretive long-term; maintain Buy

Given the relatively small size of RBC, we expect minimal near-term EPS dilution from the merger. While more difficult to quantify, we believe the synergies with the JG ecosystem will be earnings accretive to BPI over time. We retain our estimates, and our Buy rating on BPI.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Aboitiz InfraCapital partners with EdgeConnex to develop data center platform

Aboitiz InfraCapital said on Wednesday that it partnered with EdgeConneX to develop a data center in NCR. It said the partnership is meant to address the rising data usage in the country. It described EdgeConnex as a global provider of full-range sustainable data center solutions. After paring down its stake in power, AEV has been redeploying capital to other industries (banking, infrastructure).

Ayala Land subsidiary to enter data center business

AyalaLand Logistics Holdings Corp (ALLHC PM) entered a JV partnership with FLOW Digital Infrastructure to develop and operate carrier-neutral data centers across the Philippines. The first data center facility will be in a hyperscale campus, which will eventually have a capacity of 36MW. The initial roll-out of 6MW will be ready by end-2024. ALLHC is 71% owned by Ayala Land.

DMCI, Japanese partner bag subway station project

A joint venture between D.M. Consunji, Inc. and Japan’s Nishimatsu Construction Co. Ltd. bagged Contract Package 102 of the subway project. The contract is valued at P21bn and will involve the construction of Quezon Avenue and East Avenue stations. Assuming a 40% share in the JV project, we estimate the project will add P8bn to DMCI’s orderbook of P43bn. Regis also estimates earnings accretion of c. P200mn annually (vs. P30bn 23E profit of DMCI Holdings). DM Consunji Inc. is a wholly-owned unit of DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) and accounts for c. 5% of profit/NAV. DMCI is trading at a 21% discount to its market-based NAV of P13/sh.

San Miguel takes over Eagle Cement

San Miguel (SMC PM) is buying an 88.5% stake in Eagle Cement (EAGLE PM) for P22.02/sh. The price is 43% higher than Eagle’s last closing price of P15.40/sh. Regis estimates the buy-in is equivalent to an EV/MT of US$277, higher than replacement cost of c. US$150/MT. SMC will also launch a mandatory tender offer to Eagle’s minority shareholders. The transaction involves related parties, as Ramon Ang is currently the president and COO of SMC but he is also concurrently the chairman of Eagle. Eagle is led by Ramon Ang’s son, John Paul Ang. Regis estimates Eagle is the second-largest cement manufacturer/distributor in the country, accounting for 18% of the country’s consumption. As at end-June, Eagle had a net cash of P11.96bn.

 

ERC rejects SMC-Meralco joint petition for rate hike

ERC denied the joint motion for price adjustment filed by Meralco and South Premiere Power Corp (SPPC), a subsidiary of listed conglomerate SMC. According to ERC, the PSA provides no room for price adjustment and only a guaranteed supply of energy to Meralco consumers at a fixed price. “The fact that the PSA is financial in nature provided Meralco and its consumers the assurance that any fuel supply risk was of no moment and was assumed in full by the supplier” “The commission, therefore, cannot afford relief to any party for its miscalculations, imprudence or inadvertence, at the cost of the consumers,” ERC added. The ERC then reminded the applications of the joint motion “to observe not only their reciprocal obligations, but more importantly, the obligation to supply electricity consumers in the least cost manner. Meralco presented various scenarios to ERC arguing to allow San Miguel its change in circumstance claim would be the lowest cost option for consumers. Meralco claimed that the termination of the PSA would lead to an increase in energy price of P0.80-1.30/kWh for the next three to four months as it will need to find alternative sources of Energy vs +P0.30/kWh if ERC approved SMC’s claim. SMC had issued notices of termination of their PSAs effective October 4 if the regulatory body denied its appeal for relief. Meralco also previously said it had already secured emergency power supply deals with four to five generators in case the PSA termination pushes through.

Lower power rates from NGCP review

Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian, vice chairman of Senate Committee on energy said a reset of power transmission rates by the ERC, which has been delayed for 10 years, could provide some respite to consumers, as it is likely to lead to lower transmission rates. While the current power transmission rates take into account a weighted average cost of capital for NGCP at 15.04%, a 2018 study by the NTC said that WACC for NGCP should be at 6.4%. Recently, the ERC promulgated Resolution No. 8 amending the rules for setting transmission wheeling rates, which triggers the process of resetting transmission rates of the NGCP. A new RTWR means that current WACC of the NGCP will be revised after more than 10 years. ERC Chair Monalisa Dimalanta, in a briefing last Monday, said that initial figures point to a lower transmission rate applicable for the fourth regulatory period. The Commission is targeting to complete the review for the fourth regulatory period covering 2016-2022 within the year, while hearings and review for the fifth regulatory period will be conducted next year.

SMC to acquire Eagle Cement in cash transaction, to launch tender offer for minorities

SMC told the stock exchange that as a result of the planned acquisition of 88.5% of Eagle Cement, the buyer is required to conduct a tender offer of the 11.5% shares held by the target company’s minority shareholders. SMC said the acquisition of the Eagle Cement shares will be paid in cash. It added that the closing of the transaction is subject to approval by the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC). It intends to file a request for exemption with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) to allow the tender offer to start after the PCC approval.

SMC will keep supplying Meralco power under current PSA for next 60 days

San Miguel confirmed that it had received the ERC order dated September 29 denying its petition for a temporary rate increase. It said that its subsidiaries will keep supplying power to Meralco for next 60 days “at a cost higher than the contracted price under the PSAs”. Once the termination of the PSAs take effect, SPPC and SMEC will have to sell its power to WESM and enter into bilateral contracts with off-takers. SPPC and SMEC will continue to explore other legal remedies relating to the ERC orders.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Budget gap in August trimmed

The budget deficit shrunk YoY, MoM to P72bn in August. Revenues improved YoY, MoM on sustained improvements in tax collections. Additionally, growth in primary spending slowed further to 4.9% YoY. The YT-August budget gap at P833bn, down 13% YoY and tracking lower than the government’s full-year target of P1.65tn (7.6% of GDP) due to higher-than-expected revenue collections and slower spending.

Government debt breaches P13tn in August

The National Government’s outstanding debt rose to a record-high P13.02tn in August. This is up by 1% MoM from P12.887tn in July 2022 and 11% higher than in end-Dec 2021 (P11.7tn). Domestic debt accounted for 69% of total at P8.94tn (+1.3% MoM and +9.5% higher than in end-Dec 2021). Meanwhile, external debt accounted for 31% of total at P4.08tn (+0.6% MoM and +14.6% higher than  in end-Dec 2021). In USD terms, external loans stood at US$72.6bn (-1% MoM and +4% higher than in end-Dec 2021. The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually go down to 51% by 2028.

Loan growth accelerates in August

U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) accelerated slightly to 12.2% YoY in August from 12.0% in July. MoM, loans rose by 1.1% (faster than the +0.2% MoM improvement in July). The notable improvements were seen in manufacturing, trade, utilities, and consumer loans. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector rose by 9.2% in July. Note, the U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity growth (M3) decelerated further to 6.8% in August (from 7.0% in July).

Headline inflation jumps to 6.9% in September

Headline CPI for the month of September rose by 6.9% YoY, faster than in August 2022 (6.3% YoY) which came within street expectations (6.9%) and the BSP’s target range of 6.6%-7.4%. Food was the biggest driver, after last year’s low base for prices of meat, fish, vegetables, sugar, and oils/fats. Electricity prices also grew at a faster rate of 16.7% from 15.7% in August. Transport decelerated slightly to 14.5% from 14.6% in August, as the YoY growth in regional prices of diesel/gasoline (in PHP terms) decelerated to 78%/31% from 100%/47% in August. On a MoM basis, headline CPI increased by 0.4%, broadly unchanged from August (+0.4%). This was due largely to higher prices of food (i.e. flour, vegetables, and sugar) and electricity prices (+1.7% MoM) and semi offset by lower fuel prices; regional prices of diesel/gasoline (in PHP terms) dropped by 6%/9% MoM. Regis estimates headline CPI will rise >0.4% MoM in October, given that transport fares (i.e. jeepneys) were raised by another 9% beginning Oct 3. The Core CPI (ex-food and fuel) rose by 4.5% YoY in September, slightly moderating from 4.6% in August. On a MoM basis, core CPI was flat for the first time since December 2021. This brings 9M22 average inflation rate to 5.1% (vs BSP’s target of +5.6% for 2022). The next monetary board meeting is on November 17; policy rate currently at 4.25% (+225bps since end-2021). The Fed meeting on Nov 3; US Fed funds rate at 3%-3.25% (+300bps since end-2021).

Gov’t raises US$2bn in fixed rate global offering

The Republic of the Philippines raised US$2bn in a three-tranche fixed rate global bond offering. This follows previous fund raising exercises of JPY70bn Samurai bond offered in April 2022 and the US$2.25bn bond offering in March 2022. The US$500mn, 5-year tranche was priced at US Treasury plus 120bps with a coupon of 5.17%, 194bps higher than March offering. The US$750mn, 10.5 year tranche was priced at US Treasury plus 185bps with a coupon of 5.609%, 205bps higher than March and the last US$750mn, 25-year Sustainability tranche was priced at 6.1% with a coupon of 5.95%, 190bps higher than March. The 25-year Global Bonds marks the country’s third ESG G3 bond offering. The government’s debt as of end-August stood at P13.02tn, of which 69% was domestic and 31% was external. The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% this year and gradually decline to 51% by 2028.
