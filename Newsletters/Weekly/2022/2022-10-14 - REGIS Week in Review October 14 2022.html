REGIS REPORT

14 October 2022

The Philippines fell 0.5% WoW, consolidating after its 3.3% rise in the previous week. The PSEi stayed below the 6000 level, closing at 5904.75 this week, as investors continued to digest CPI data and tightening by the Fed. 

Trading lacked conviction on a lack of positive catalysts on the ground. Volumes plummeted to an average of US$63.5m daily, with Monday clocking in a mere US$39m – the quietest trading day so far this year.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$5.4m but this was skewed by SM Prime blocks on Friday. Excluding these, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$12.7m. Biggest outflows were spread across conglos and property.

Index gainers and losers were almost evenly split. Steepest falls were seen in Monde (-8.1% WoW), JG Summit (-6.5% WoW), PLDT (-5.3%  WoW), and ICTSI (-4.4% WoW).  On the opposite end were BDO (+6.8% WoW) and Robinsons Land (+5.0% WoW) with strong gains for the week. Also closing up were Puregold (+4.1% WoW), Aboitiz Equity (+2.3% WoW), and ACEN (+2.1% WoW). 

With US inflation data out and expectations of another sharp rise in rates during the next Fed meeting, BSP Governor Felipe Medalla signaled an increase in policy rates of 50-75bp at the next Monetary Board meeting (Nov 17). This would help ease pressure on the peso, which this week was flat against the US$. Pressure is coming as well from the ballooning trade deficit, which  hit another record high of $6bn in August. The 8M trade deficit stands at US$41.8bn (+69% YoY), already approximating last year’s full-year trade deficit of US$42bn.

The BSP has admitted to intervening in the FX market,  to help moderate PHP weakness. This is reflected by the latest GIR print which showed another US$2.4bn drop in September to US$95bn.  Reserves have already fallen 12% from the start of the year.

Bloomberry (-3.4% WoW) closed lower, with the gaming sector in the spotlight this week. Senator Miguel Zubiri said that the Philippines was on China’s “blacklist” due to the continued presence of POGOs. However, the Chinese embassy in the Philippines soon affter denied that the country was on China’s  tourism blacklist.

Globe (+1.1% WoW) has completed its P17bn rights offering, with 10.1m new shares issued (taking outstanding shares to 144m).  Combined with its recent tower sale, the capital raise would improve Globe’s financial position. Regis estimates gearing to fall just below 1x.

RESEARCH
Philippine Banks - Credit card rate caps under review; could be positive for banks
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

BSP looking to adjust credit card rate/fee caps

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla says that the regulator is reviewing the cap set on interest rates and fees charged by banks on credit card transactions. In 4Q2020, the cap was set at 24% per annum (2% a month), much lower than the prevailing rate of 36-42% (3-3.5%/month) at the time to help consumers deal with the Covid-19 pandemic. We are wary of pushback, though, considering the strain of inflation on consumers. Either way, it does mean that credit card rates/fees remain regulated. Credit card receivables account for 4-7% of banks’ loan books, but have an outsized impact on profit due to the high rates. We estimate every 25bps/month change in the cap translates to about 2-3%change in our NPAT forecasts.

Adjusting the 24% p.a. finance charge; first review in 2 years

The BSP is reviewing the caps on rates and fees that banks can charge on credit card transactions. The most crucial of these is the finance charge on unpaid balances, where the maximum is set at 24% per annum, or 2.0% a month. The limits were set in November 2020. Since then, the BSP’s policy rates have been hiked 225bps, and the 10-year Treasury rate has risen some 400bps. According to Gov Medalla, “given the higher interest rates it’s obvious that we have to adjust the cap.”

Still on the slippery slope; could there be a pushback?

The rate caps were implemented ostensibly to help consumers deal with the Covid-19 pandemic. At that time, credit card finance charges ranged from 2.75% to 3.5% a month (33-42% per annum). However, as we wrote then, the rate cap was not a temporary measure, but instead a fundamental change in the BSP’s remit. Prior to this, the BSP explicitly did not prescribe any rate or fee for any bank service. While the banks will welcome the review and potential adjustment, it is clear to us that the BSP’s control over credit card rates/fees will remain. We are also wary of consumer pushback regarding the timing of the rate cap adjustments. Covid-19 may be largely behind us. But consumers are now dealing with inflation. While we may understand the financial / regulatory rationale for lifting the limits, it may not play well with the average Filipino.

Staying positive on the banks

Credit card receivables account for just 4-7% of the loan portfolios of banks we cover (see table at right). However, they have an outsized impact on banks’ margins and bottomline. In 2021, the average loan yield on total loans of banks we cover ranged from just 5% to 6%. We estimate that every 25bps/month change in the rate cap will result in a 2-3% change in our pre-tax forecasts. Our positive stance on the sector was not predicated on upward adjustments to the credit card rate cap.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PLDT demands P430mn payment from DITO

PLDT disclosed last Friday that it served a notice of material breach and demand for payment on DITO Telecommunity for contracted services delivered that the third telco refused to pay. The demand for payment was for over P429mn. PLDT said it has fully performed and delivered relating to the building and provisioning of transmission facilities that DITO required and is using for delivery of telecommunication services to subscribers. DITO responded that “the information we believe is misleading”. It said that Smart’s adamant refusal to augment DITO’s capacity to interconnect with Smart subscribers prompted the third telco to inform Smart the said facilities are no longer needed. DITO earlier filed a complaint with the PCC against Globe Telecom and PLDT citing “abuse of dominant position” as it claimed subscribers were having difficulty calling numbers from rival telcos. Globe on the other hand has requested the NTC to compel DITO to pay a P622mn fine for allegedly violating their interconnection agreement.

LPC: Metro Manila 3Q22 office vacancy stays flat QoQ at 18%

Leechiu Property Consultants said that Metro Manila 3Q22 office vacancy stayed flat QoQ at 18%. On a positive note, nationwide BPO net office take-up rose to 146,000sqm in 3Q22. This is comparable to the pre-COVID annual take-up of 500,000sqm to 650,000sqm. However, nationwide net office take-up of 209,000sqm is still far from the 2018-2019 annual pace of 1.6mn to 1.8mn sqm. Regis forecasts Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 19% in 2022E, 22% in 2023E due to new office completions. In September 2022, the government effectively allowed BPO firms to maintain WFH policies indefinitely.

Vehicle sales jump strongly in September

Vehicle sales jumped 17% MoM, 64% YoY to 35,282 units in September according to CAMPI/TMA. This brings 9M22 sales up by 29% YoY to 246,815 units. Toyota grew sales by 10% MoM, 63% YoY to 16,138 units in September alone, despite the blanket price hike early in the month. Its market share slipped by 530bps MoM to 45.7%. Toyota’s 9M22 sales reached 124,884 units, up 35% YoY, well ahead of the company’s target (162k, 25% YoY) and Regis estimates (158k, +22% YoY). Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP) and accounts for c. 20% of the latter’s NAV/profit. GTCAP is trading at a 55% discount to its market-based NAV of P873/sh.

Gov’t consent to Razon’s Malampaya buy-in conditional

The Department of Energy clarified that the approval of Prime Infrastructure’s buy-in of the Malampaya gas project was premised on its commitment to maximize the development of Malampaya’s existing resources, with a view to developing the nearby fields as well according to Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla. The DoE cleared the sale last month after Prime Infra was found to be technically, financially and legally qualified to take over as new operator of Malampaya. Prime Infra will assume full ownership and control of the project upon completion by Nov. 1, 2022 of the process for safe and seamless turnover of operations from Shell. Next on the agenda of the Malampaya consortium— composed of Prime Infra, UC38 LLC and PNOC Exploration Corp. (PNOC EC)—is the extension of the service contract that will expire in 2024.

BSP to adjust rate cap on credit card transactions

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said the regulator is reviewing the interest rate cap on credit transactions currently pegged at 2% per month of 24% per year. The central bank imposed a cap on the interest rate on all credit card transactions to ease the burden of Filipino consumers impacted by the pandemic. A separate interest rate ceiling was also prescribed for credit card installment loans.No other charges or fees could be imposed or collected on credit card cash advanced except for a maximum processing fee of P200. Prior to the imposition of the cap two years ago, the annualized interest rate on credit card receivables averaged 36%.

Globe completes rights issue

Globe Telecom announced the completion of its rights offering. The P17bn rights was fully subscribed. A total of 10.1m new shares were issued at P1,680/share, increasing the numbers of 144.1m. The listing of the new shares will be “on or about Oct 28”, which is when they become tradeable. The rights issue and the recently announced tower sale/s should reduce Globe’s net gearing to just below 1.0x.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

New BOT Law IRR in effect

The revised implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) Law took effect this week. The amendments aim to promote transparency and accountability in delivering infrastructure projects. The amendments push for “appropriate risk sharing” between the government and the private sector. The definition of Material Adverse Government Action (MAGA) was revised to cover all government actions, not just the Executive Branch. The PPP Center said there are currently 74 projects worth P2.25tn in the pipeline as of end-August. These include 53 national projects and 21 local projects.

Retail prices of sugar hit all-time high

Retail prices of refined sugar in Metro Manila have risen to as much P134/kg as of Oct 7, according to data from the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA). The average retail price is now at P97.54/kg (+82% YoY). The SRA previously said that it expects retail prices of sugar to start easing by November as imports arrive and domestic supply stabilizes. The 150k MT of refined sugar imports are expected to arrive by 15th Nov. The ramping up of sugar milling capacity by Nov following the start of the milling season in Sep. The SRA is targeting to lower the average retail price of refined sugar to P70-80/kg.

Trade deficit at US$6.0bn in August

The country’s trade deficit ballooned to US$6.003bn in August. This is wider by 81% YoY and broadly unchanged from July 2022 (US$5.989bn). Import growth accelerated to 26% YoY in August from 22% YoY in July 2022. This is despite fuel imports decelerating to 76% YoY in August from 87% YoY in July 2022. Consumer goods imports soared by 46% YoY while capital goods import grew at a faster rate of 15% YoY. Meanwhile, eports dropped for the second straight month. The YT-August trade deficit is 69% YoY wider at US$41.8bn, almost matching full-year 2021’s trade deficit of US$42bn.

T-bill results mixed

The Bureau of Treasury partially awarded 91-day and 182-day T-bills while rejecting bids for the 364-day T-bill. The yield for the 91-day T-bill averaged at 3.819%, 150bps higher than previous auction (BVAL: 3.3335%). The yield for the 182-day T-bill averaged at 4.415%, 45.7bps higher than previous auction (BVAL: 3.8845%). The yield for the 364-day T-bill reached 5.401%, had it been awarded (BVAL: 3.9273%). The government raised P4bn of its P15bn programmed offering. The auction was slightly oversubscribed, attracting P16.3bn in total tenders.

FDI sinks to 14-month low

Foreign direct investment net inflows dropped for a third straight month to US$460mn in July (down 64.4% YoY). This is the lowest level recorded in 14 months. The MoM, FDI net inflows dropped by 2.3% from US$471mn. The government cited lower non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments of their local affiliates (-81% YoY to US$213mn). This decrease more than offset the growth in their net investments in equity capital (+25% YoY to US$248mn). The YT-July FDI net inflows are at US$5.101bn, down 12% YoY. The BSP is targeting FDI net inflows to reach US$10.5bn this year (-15% YoY).

GIR drops to US$95bn in September

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) dropped by US$2.4bn MoM to US$95.014bn in September. This is down from US$108.79bn in end-Dec 2021 but remains above end-2019 at US$87.84bn. The MoM drop reflects national government foreign currency debt obligations and downward adjustment in BSP’s gold holdings. Additionally, this represents 7.6 months’ worth of imports and 6.8x the country’s short-term external debt. The BSP is targeting an end-2022 GIR level of US$99bn.

Tariff Commission recommends against extension of tariffs on imported cement

The Tariff Commission recommended against the extension of tariffs on imported cement. The Commission found no evidence that cheaper imports were killing the local industry. The safeguard duty is currently at P200 per MT (or P8 per 40-kg bag). Regis estimates the safeguard duty currently accounts for 5% (US$4/MT) of an importer’s total cost (US$77/MT). The safeguard duty is also c. 5% of the ASP of local manufacturers, such as Cemex Philippines (CHP PM).
