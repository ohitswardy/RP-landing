REGIS REPORT

04 November 2022

The PSEi inched up this week, even as a Hawkish Fed continued to influence market sentiment. Foreign inflow helped prop up the market.

Foreigners ended net buyers for US$13.2m with largest inflows seen across banks. consumers, and property. BDO was top bought after reporting record 3Q numbers. Meanwhile, Ayala Land and Universal Robina also saw among the highest inflows. ICTSI on the other hand was top sold this week, with foreign sellers providing liquidity.

On the ground, rates are also set to move up. According to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla, the Monetary Board will raise the BSP’s policy rate by 75bps when they meet on Nov 17, in a bid to maintain the gap in interest rates and to cushion the currency impact of the most recent Fed hike. The increase would bring the BSP’s policy rate to 5.0%.

The PSEi closed up 0.5% this week to close at 6185.53.  Gainers outnumbered losers 17:13.

Large caps SM (+1.8% WoW), and SM Prime (+2.5 % WoW) accounted for a large chunk of the PSEi’s ascent this week. Also moving the index higher were ICTSI (+2.8% WoW) and Universal Robina (+3.8% WoW), which saw 3Q profits jump 37% with robust domestic branded foods revenue growth for the quarter.  Revenues for Century Pacific (+1.5% WoW) were also strong, rising 13% YoY.  For both however, margins continue to be under pressure.

Meralco (+2.9% WoW) was also among the top movers, with profits coming in ahead on healthy volume growth (+7% YoY) during the quarter and also strong contribution from the generation segment (Pacific Light). 

Within the sector, Aboitiz Power (+2.2% WoW)   reported robust 3Q numbers, helped by the favorable supply situation in the power sector. The same was the case for Semirara (-2.3% WoW) but coal prices also helped keep profits elevated.

Banking was a busy sector this week. BDO’s (-0.2% WoW) 3Q clearly came in ahead of both Regis and Street estimates.  NPAT for 9M22 was up +23% to P40bn, with P16b for 3Q alone (+46% YoY). The bank reported an impressive +24bps QoQ NIM increase.

Metrobank’s (+0.9% WoW) also beat forecasts, as 3Q NP surged 77%.  Loan growth accelerated to 13% while NIMs expanded 22bps QoQ. NPLs however rose to 2.1% from 1.9%.

Meanwhile, Union Bank’s (+2.6% WoW) board approved a P20bn (US$345m) equity raising, to be done via a rights and/or private placement, after completing a P40bn (US$690m) rights issue in May. The additional capital raise is likely to be partially aimed at improving float.

Finally on banks, Sumitomo Mitsui Bank (SMBC) will take an additional 15% stake in Rizal Commercial Bank (10.1% WoW).  SMCB will purchase 382m shares at P71/sh, taking their stake from 4.99% to 20%. This will take the bank’s CET-1 ratio from 11.9% to 15.9%.

Meanwhile, telcos were weak as overall telecom spend, based on PLDT’s (-1.2% WoW)  3Q results, is looking lackluster. While PLDT’s profit number came in slightly ahead, mobile revenue remains soft with consumers seeming to spend more on other essentials amidst high inflation. Net adds for wired broadband business (+42k in 3Q)  slowed further from 2Q. Converge (-2.4% WoW) and Globe (-0.9% WoW) were also down this week.

Manila Electric Company - 3Q: Earnings lifted by generation business yet again
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

9M core income up 9%

Meralco reported net profit of P19.8bn for 9M22, which is up 20% YoY. Excluding non-core items (as reckoned by the company), core profit amounted to P19.6b, +9% YoY. This puts it on pace to exceed our FY22E estimate of P25.3bn. The main source of outperformance in 3Q continues to be the generation business, particularly Pacific Light. Despite the lack of clarity on tariffs from 3Q22 onwards, the core distribution business appears robust as evidenced by the growth in power volumes.

Distribution volumes rise 7% in 3Q

Electricity sales for its distribution business rose 7.2% in 3Q, taking YT-Sept volume growth to +6.3%. While overall power demand is already above pre[1]pandemic levels (3Q19), we are not yet past the distortions inflicted by the pandemic. Demand for the commercial segment (offices and shopping centers) remains 6% below 3Q19, while residential demand is already 18% above it.

Provisions still masking true financial health

Meralco does not appear to have reversed (yet) the provisions relating to the “lapse period” amounting to P40bn or more. Doing so may lead to a large gain that may attract public scrutiny. Furthermore, Meralco appears to be still taking continuing provisions on current tariffs (operations) given that its rate review (for period beginning July 2022) will take some more time to be resolved.

Contrasting fortunes for generation subsidiaries; telco tower business begins

Pacific Light produced core income of P8.97bn for 9M, implying 3Q core income of P3,3bn. From virtually nothing last year, its core profit contribution is now 27% of Meralco’s in 9M22. On the other hand, the losses at its domestic generation firm, Global Business Power (GBP), continued into 3Q. Subsidiary MIESCOR holds 51% of a partnership which agreed to buy 2,180 telecom towers from Globe for P26.2bn. The partnership took possession of the initial 701 in 3Q22. We are apprehensive about the value of this new venture.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Strong 3Q rebound for power unit; Coal mining costs remain subdued
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q22 profit remains elevated, better than forecast

SCC’s 3Q22 profit (ex-FX adj) surprised to the upside, dropping by just 7% QoQ to P9.38bn. Cumulative 9M22 profit is up more than 3x at P34.29bn, representing 93% of Regis FY22E and 81% of consensus estimates. We attribute the outperformance to (a) lower than expected costs (cash + non-cash) for the coal mining unit and (b) better power generation capacity, leading to higher spot sales volumes. These factors more than compensated for the mild shortfall in coal mining ASPs.

Coal mining volumes, ASPs drop QoQ

Profits from the coal unit were 17% lower QoQ at P7.59bn, due to lower sales volumes (-14% QoQ to 3.2mn MT), and lower ASPs (-4% QoQ to P5,173/MT). Given intense competition from Russian coal in China, regional prices for low-grade coal (i.e. ICI4) dropped 10% QoQ, forcing SCC to strategically reduce exports by 39% QoQ. With ICI4 prices now back to peak prices in March/April, management is confident it can reach its sales volume guidance of 15mn MT by year-end. Meanwhile, cash costs per MT (ex-royalties) fell 2% QoQ to P1,400 on lower fuel costs, bringing 9M22 cash costs per MT to P1,200, trending well below forecast.

Resumption of Unit 2 boosts 3Q22

Its power unit saw 3Q22 profits rising by 28% QoQ to P1.5bn. Average capacity improved to 695 MW in 3Q22 alone from 515 MW in 1H, as Unit 2 began commissioning. This allowed the company to sell a larger proportion of sales volumes to the spot market. However, Unit 2’s rehab is far from completed, as it is only operating at 200 MW vs. its “normal” 300 MW capacity. Management indicated that the repair of Unit 2 is expected to be completed next year.

Massive net cash position, even after record special div; Building a war chest

Management expects to remain in a massive net cash position of >P20bn (P5/sh) by year-end, even after paying record special divs. Management wants to build a war chest, should an acquisition opportunity arise. However, they did not discount the possibility of another chunky dividend next year.

 
ICTSI - Weathering (another) storm
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Maintain Buy

Fears of a global macroeconomic slowdown have led to ICT’s share price dropping by 30% from its peak on March 10, underperforming the PCOMP by 20% over the same period. We believe the underperformance is unwarranted, given the defensive characteristics of ICT’s O&D port model and the relatively modest risks to EM trade volumes. At 9.3x 23E PER and 6.3x 23E EV/EBITDA, we believe the shares are undervalued. We advise investors to accumulate during the current period of price volatility and weakness.

Trade volumes likely to be more resilient

The IMF is predicting global trade growth to decelerate to 2% in 2023 from a projected 2.9% this year and 10.8% in 2021. While the global macroeconomic situation is not ideal, it is nonetheless predicted to be better than in previous downcycles, particularly in EM economies where ICT operates.

Sound long-term fundamentals, despite near-term cyclical issues

We raise our 22E EPS by 9% but largely maintain our EPS estimate for FY23E, in anticipation of a global slowdown next year. Earnings growth should re[1]accelerate to +18% in 24E, as global macroeconomic conditions settle. Our revised earnings forecasts are the highest on the Street and we believe risks to earnings remain to the upside.

12-month TP of P250/sh offers 59% total potential return

We reduce our USD-based NAV by 14% to incorporate higher WACC assumptions, driven by a higher US risk-free rate assumption. We nonetheless kept our TP unchanged at P250/sh, as we also revised our PHP:USD conversion assumption to P59 from P54. Our revised TP implies 8x FY23E EV/EBITDA and offers 59% total potential return. Downside risks relate to trade, dilutive acquisitions, and labor issues.

 
Century Pacific Food - Margin pressures persist in 3Q22
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

3Q22 net profit flat as margins compress

CNPF’s net profit in 3Q22 was flat YoY at P1.27bn, bringing 9M22 net profit to P4.2bn (+6% YoY). The cumulative figure is tracking broadly in line with FY22 expectations, considering the 4Q seasonality. 3Q22 continued to be pressured by elevated input costs, even as revenue growth (+13% YoY) remained healthy.

Branded segment leads 3Q top line growth

3Q revenues were up 13% YoY, led by the branded segment (77% of total), which grew 15% YoY. Growth was boosted by a cumulative price adjustment of ~9% (weighted average) as CNPF implemented another +2-3% price increase during the quarter. We estimate that volume growth has moderated to +6-8% vs the low-teens growth seen in the previous two quarters. This was attributed to trade inventory levels normalizing as larger retailers loaded inventory ahead of price increases.

Input cost inflation continues to pressure margins

Cost inflation continued to pressure margins with GPM contracting another >100bps QoQ during the quarter. While CNPF raised prices, the adjustments thus far have only been enough to cover ~60% of the cost inflation. On top of this, higher A&P spending to support brand-building efforts also weighed on EBITDA.

Uncertainties abound but momentum sustained

Management maintained its guidance for top line to grow by mid-teens (Regis FY22E: +13%), though they continue to be wary about the impact of inflation on demand, noting that downtrading is still evident across the portfolio. The outlook for input cost inflation is still “murky” in management’s opinion, and margins may continue to see volatility into 1H23. CNPF has another round of price increases programmed in 4Q22 but management believes that margin relief may not be felt until mid-2023.

 
PLDT - 3Q: Softness persists in mobile
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M core income up +7% on service revenue growth of +5%

PLDT reported net income of P27.4bn for 9M22, up +45% YoY. Note that the bottom-line figure includes a number of exceptional items, including gains from the sale of its telecom towers, prescription of its liabilities from its prefs, as well as the impact of accelerated depreciation of some fixed assets, among other things. Excluding the exceptional items, but keeping the attributable losses from Voyager, we estimate 9M core income at P23.4bn, +7% YoY, putting it on track to slightly exceed our FY22 forecast. Note that depreciation charges going forward are lower because of the accelerated depreciation undertaken in 2Q.

Mobile revenue still down YoY

Mobile revenue remains down YoY (-3%), though the pace of the decline has moderated. Mobile spend is one item that appears to have suffered cuts in consumer budgets amidst inflation pressures even as pandemic restrictions are loosened. We highlight that promotions for mobile appear to have turned particularly aggressive in 3Q, with PLDT’s ave revenue/GB declining 14% QoQ.

Home broadband lifting top line but net adds disappoint

Home broadband revenue was up +13% YoY in 3Q, a still respectable performance. But a further deceleration in revenue growth is imminent given the drop in net subscriber additions. PLDT added only 42k wired subs in 3Q taking the total to 3.24m. This is down from +67k in 2Q.

Fintech losses continue as digital bank begins to operate

The group’s digital bank has so far generated P10bn in deposits after an aggressive push, which included offering an above-market deposit rate of 6%. The start-up costs contributed to equity-accounted losses of P2.0bn from Voyager for PLDT’s share for 9M22. This last figure implies that Voyager’s losses YTD are over P5.5bn (~US$95m). Voyager raised US$210m in 2Q22, which means it will not likely need to raise new money until late 2023 (if not later).

 
Bank of the Philippine Islands - Big beat in 3Q22; raising estimates and target price
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT beats again; wider NIMs push our 2023/24E up 14-16%

BPI’s 3Q22 NPAT of P10.1bn surged 79% YoY, and blew past our and the Street’s expectations. The proximate causes of the beat were higher trading/fx gains and continued delays in IT spend. More important, though, was the sharp NIM expansion – up 15bps QoQ in 3Q – on higher policy rates. While 9M22 NII seemed in line, we expect NIM momentum to carry into 4Q22 and into 2023, given the lag of loan repricing. Meanwhile, BPI recently launched its e-wallet called VYBE, the newest initiative in its digital strategy. We raise our NPAT forecasts by 13-16%, and increase our target price by 14% to P126/sh. Maintain Buy.

Steep NIM trajectory prompts upward revisions to our forecasts

NII in 3Q22 jumped 29% YoY, as loans grew 15%, and NIMs widened 38bps YoY (+15bps QoQ) to 3.66%, by management estimates. However, NIMs in September alone were close to 10bps higher than the 3Q average, as the BSP only aggressively hiked policy rates in June/July. With half the loan book repricing every 3 months, NIM expansion should accelerate into 4Q and 2023E. We raise our NIM forecasts by 10bps in 2022E, 28bps in 2023E, and 21bps in 2024E. This translates to a 13-16% increase in our NPAT estimates.

Digital update – VYBE launched, IT spend to rise in 4Q

Opex grew just 10% in 9M22, far short of management’s 20% guidance. The delayed rollout of IT projects meant progress-based payments were also pushed back. BPI recently launched VYBE – its own e-wallet, payments, and rewards app – initially to existing customers. As this and other projects roll out, we expect IT spend to accelerate heading into 4Q22 (though still below our previous estimates).

Raising TP to P126.00 (+14.5%); Buy, but mindful of NPL risks

We lift our target price by 14.5% to P126.00/sh, for a potential total return of 34%, and keep our Buy rating. Our target is based on ROE-g/COE-g, (long-term ROE of 14.5%, COE of 12%, growth of 8%), which translates to 1.7x P/bk on our 2023E estimates. Key downside risks include new NPL formation due to high inflation and the rapidly depreciating PHP. Our forecasts also do not yet impute the proposed Robinsons Bank merger, which isn’t due until end-2023E.

BDO 9M22 P40bn +23% well AHEAD on NIMs, fees

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported 9M22 NPAT of P40bn, up 23% YoY. For 3Q22 alone, NPAT was P16bn, surging 46% YoY. The results are WELL AHEAD of Regis (P48bn) and Street (P49bn) forecasts for FY22. On the surface, the key beats came from fee incomes, and from FX gains which overwhelmed the trading losses. While NII looked in line, the momentum is clearly building on a QoQ basis, and will likely also surpass our FY estimates. Asset quality also improved with better NPLs and NPL cover. Most remarkable, in our view, is that the Restructured Loans (RLs) have dropped to about 3.7%, down from 4.9% in 2Q22, and from a peak of 5.75% in 2Q21.

 

Some other highlights:

“Customer loans” grew 10% YoY, up from the 9.2% recorded in June. The press release did not say which segments led the growth. Deposits grew 11%, but was due largely to TDs, which were up 27% YoY and 23% QoQ as wealthier customers demanded higher rates. CASA ratio dipped to 82% from 85% in 2Q.

 

NIMs expanded 24bps QoQ to 4.23%, which saw NII surge 17% YoY in 3Q22 alone (+11% for 9M). Loan yields jumped 38bps QoQ to 5.67%, far outpacing the 18bps increase in average deposit costs to 0.42%, which is still an industry low. As benchmark interest rates continue to rise, Regis expects the NIM expansion trend to continue into 4Q22 and into 2023.

 

Fee incomes grew 22% YoY in 3Q22, and 18% in 9M. This was driven primarily by service charges, which surged 37% YoY in 3Q and 30% in 9M. Trust fees were up 7% in 9M, while insurance premiums grew 6.7% in the same period. BDO had a monster P3bn in FX gains in 3Q alone, dwarfing the P1.1bn trading loss.

 

Opex accelerated to 12.2% in 3Q22, driven by a 20% YoY surge in manpower costs for the quarter. Provision expense stood at P12.2bn in 9M22, up 16.5%, equivalent to a credit cost of about 64bps. Asset quality improved across the board. NPL ratio eased to 2.23%, from 2.39% in 2Q22, and NPL cover improved to 152% from 138% in 2Q. Most impressively, RL ratio dropped sharply to 3.7% from around 4.9% in 2Q. In nominal terms, RLs dropped to P92bn from P118bn in 2Q22, and from a high of P143bn in 2Q21.

 

Overall a solid set of results.

 

Cemex Philippines’ 3Q22 EBITDA plunges 33% YoY, 38% QoQ

EBITDA collapses in 3Q.  Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) reported a wider net loss of P552mn in 3Q22, taking 9M22 net losses to P819mn.  3Q22 EBIT plunged 63% YoY, 66% QoQ to P208mn while EBITDA collapsed 33% YoY, 38% QoQ to P671mn.  The results are trending well below estimates due to lower than expected sales volumes and higher cash costs. Sales volumes drop 16% YoY in 3Q.  CHP’s sales volumes dropped by another 16% YoY in 3Q22, which management attributed to both weak demand and intense competition. The company revised its FY22 guidance yet again to “mid-single digit percentage decrease” from “flat to low single digit decrease” in July and from “low single digit increase” at the start of the year. Management estimates industry volumes rose slightly in 3Q, as third party importers gained share.

 

Sumitomo to infuse P27bn of equity into RCBC

Sumitomo Mitsui will purchase 382mn shares of Rizal Commercial Banking Corp (RCB PM) at P71/share; 213mn treasury shares and 168mn new shares. RCB’s last closing price was P23.95/share before the announcement. The equity infusion will increase Sumitomo’s ownership in RCB from 4.99% to 20%. The parent bank expects its CET-1 ratio to increase from 11.9% to 15.9%. Regis estimates that BVPS will increase from P47.9/share to P51.50/share. RCB will seek value-add from Sumitomo’s expertise in Corporate banking, sustainable finance, and project finance, Leasing, Consumer lending, Branch banking (digital technology and data-based sales strategies), Wealth management and trust, Digital.

UnionBank to raise up to P20bn in equity

Unionbank’s Board of Directors approved a capital raising activity of up to P20bn. This would represent 12% of the current market capitalization (P171bn). The capital raising may take the form of a stock rights offering or a private placement.

 

AP 3Q22 core income jumps to P9.2bn (65%YoY, 48%QoQ); 9M22 core of P18.3bn (17%YoY) tracking ahead

AP’s 3Q core income was P9.2bn (65%YoY, 48%QoQ), again driven by contributions from both units of GNPD, higher water inflows and gains from commodity hedges. Including non-recurring gains, net income for 3Q22 amounted to P9.6bn (72%YoY, 35%QoQ). The result brings AP’s core net income for 9M22 to P18.3bn (17%YoY) – AHEAD of Regis (P23.6bn FY22E) and well ahead of consensus (P19.3bn FY22E). Generation and RES EBITDA grew 21% to P39bn in 9M22. The 3Q Gen & RES EBITDA was P15.9bn (+11%QoQ). Meanwhile, capacity sold in 9M22 increased 8% to 3,940MW. Energy sold grew 19% to 21,892GWh for 9M22. This implies energy sold in 3Q reached 8,130GWh (+5%QoQ, +22%YoY). The distribution EBITDA amounted to P4.8bn in 9M22, down 22%YoY, still dragged by the delayed pass through of generation charges. Energy sales increased 2% to 4,294GWH for 9M22 and implies 3Q was 1,516GWh (+2%QoQ, +4%YoY). AP trades at 9.9x FY22E PE and 5.6x FY22E EV/EBITDA.

DMC likely to enter PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing

DMC will likely enter the PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing. Regis expects this to lead to the removal of either EMI or MEG. If EMI does not increase its public float to 20% (from 16% as of end-September), then it will be removed from the PSEi. If EMI increases its public float to 20% by end-December, then MEG will be removed from the PSEi. The PSEi rebalancing in February will be based on the 12 months from January 2022 to December 2022. Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap.

 

Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when

1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or

2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 

 

Based on our estimates, DMC has reached the top 25 (as of 10M22). In addition to DMC, Regis also expects relatively large inflow to SMC due to the increase in its public float. In addition to the PSEi constituent to be removed, Regis also expects relatively large outflow from ALI due to the reduction in its public float. Note: since SSS has a board seat in UBP, Regis expects the PSE to treat the SSS stake as non-public. Thus, Regis estimates that UBP will not meet the 20% public ownership requirement (to enter the PSEi).

 

Bank lending growth picks up to 13.4% YoY in September

Outstanding loans by U/KBs, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, rose by 13.4% YoY in September. This is faster than August’s 12.2% and fastest pace in 27 months. The growth was fueled by loans to the following sectors: real estate (+16%), manufacturing (+16%), trade (+11%). Consumer loans sustained momentum, rising by 21% YoY, with both credit card (+26%) and auto loans (+4%) accelerating. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector jumped by 9.6% in August. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity growth (M3) decelerated 5.0% in September (from 6.7% in August).

Government’s debt stock rises to P13.517tn in September

The National Government’s outstanding debt rose further to P13.517tn in September. This is P496bn (+3.8%) higher MoM and P1.79tn (+15.2%) higher than in end-Dec 2021. This is also the fastest MoM increase this year due to the peso’s depreciation against the USD and the net issuance of govt securities to support the budget. Domestic debt accounted for 68.8% of total at P9.3tn (+4% MoM, +13.8% higher than in end-Dec 2021). The government cited net issuance of govt securities (+P352.09bn MoM) and the impact of the local currency depreciation against the USD (+5.18bn). The external debt stood at P4.216tn (+3.4% MoM, +18.5% higher than in end-Dec 2021) due to the P180bn impact of the local currency depreciation against the USD, which was partially offset by the P31bn effect of third-currency depreciation against the USD and net repayment of P10.8bn. In USD terms, external loans stood at US$71.896bn, lower by 1% MoM). The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually fall to 51% by 2028.

BSP to raise policy rate by 75bps on November 17

The Monetary Board will raise the policy rate of Bangko Sentral ng PIlipinas by 0.75 percentage points to bring it to 5% when they meet on November 17, BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said on Thursday. Medalla said this six hours after the US Fed announced that their target range for the federal funds rate was raised to 3.75-4% from 3-3.25% that was in place since September 21. “The BSP deems it necessary to maintain the interest rate differential prevailing before the most recent Fed rate hike, in line with its price stability mandate and the need to temper any impact on the country’s exchange rate of the most recent Fed rate hike”. Since May, the MB has raised the BSP’s overnight borrowing rate by a total of 2.25 percentage points from a record low of 2%
