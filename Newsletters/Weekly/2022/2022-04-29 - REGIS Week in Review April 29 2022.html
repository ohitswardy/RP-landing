REGIS REPORT

29 April 2022

The PSEi got punished this week dropping -3.8% week on week to close at 6,731 and crucial closing below the 6,800 support level. Headline net foreign outflows stood at -$28.0mn this week despite an overnight placement of AREIT which was a net foreign inflow transaction of  $55.8m. If we exclude the AREIT inflow, foreigners would have been net sellers for $83.8m.

There we only two index gainers this week in Emperador (+6.3% WoW) and Meralco (+3.7% WoW) while the top index losers this week were AC Energy (-12% WoW), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-11% WoW), Puregold (-8.6% WoW), Universal Robina (-8.0% WoW) and Ayala Land (-7.9% WoW).

This week, Meralco (+3.7% WoW) and Aboitiz Power (-5.4% WoW) reported 1Q results. Meralco disclosed headline profit of P5.56bn (+28% YoY), with core income of P5.62bn (+10%) on the back of +5.7% growth in electricity sales. The core profit is within our expectations despite the losses posted by Global Business Power (GBP) whose operations were affected by Typhoon Odette in the Visayas. At its briefing, the company provided an update on its tariff reset application (which we previously reported). Meralco is seeking a tariff of P1.57/kwh for the next regulatory period (“5RP”), 14% more than the current tariff which set in 2011. Even if we don’t think Meralco will receive anything close to P1.57/kwh, a final tariff that’s anywhere close to the current one (P1.38/kwh) could provide significant upside to current earnings (>25%).

Meanwhile, Aboitiz Power’s 1Q22 earnings came in lower at P2.9bn (-44%QoQ, -53%YoY) but the YoY/QoQ declines looked worse than the actual underlying operations because of insurance claims booked last year. Income from the generation and distribution segment declined due to outages caused by Typhoon Odette in the early part of 1Q.  The recovery from Odette and the growing contribution from GNPD would mean profits have potential to catch up to our estimate over the succeeding periods.

Also this week a lot of news came out from the Ayala Group of companies this week. First was on ACEN, the company said they would be venturing across the Pacific with its plan to enter the U.S. wind power market. This venture will be with its frequent partner UPC and will add to its pipeline of projects. ACEN looks set to top its near-term goal of achieving 5,000MW of attributable capacity.

Secondly, Ayala Corp (-2.5% WoW) said it would be distributing part of its stake in ACEN as a property dividend. Holders of each AC share will receive 3 shares of ACEN. So a total of 1.86bn ACEN shares will be distributed. This would be equivalent to around 4.9% of ACEN's outstanding shares. Additionally, this will also boost ACEN's float and allow it to meet PSEi requirements.

The last one was on Ayala Land. The company sold 87.4mn shares of AREIT (AREIT PM) at P39.70/share (total of P3.5bn). Recall that Ayala Land will receive 252mn new AREIT shares in exchange for six Cebu office buildings. Thus, Ayala Land is selling AREIT shares ahead of the property-for-share swap in order to meet minimum public ownership requirements.

Megawide - All segments disappoint in 4Q21
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

4Q21 net loss widens – Behind Regis and street estimates

MWIDE reported unaudited FY21 net loss after minorities of P343mn. After removing pref dividends, we estimate the net loss was P864mn, wider than Regis and street forecasts. The implied loss in 4Q21 was wider QoQ at P404mn, after the construction unit booked a large impairment charge and revenues for the landport unit plunged. On the bright side, losses at the airport unit shrunk in 4Q21 as pax volumes reverted to 18% of pre-pandemic levels. To fund increasing working capital requirements, the company is taking in more short-term debt and plans to raise P3bn worth of bonds this year.

Construction: Receivables surge

The construction unit incurred a loss in 4Q after it impaired c. P180mn worth of receivables from a client and recognized cost overruns from higher input costs. Management indicated it has reached a settlement with the client in question. However, issues with some clients remain unresolved, leading to delays in collection and a rise in receivables (+15% YoY). Its order book stood at P60bn in end-2021, flat QoQ but down 12% YoY. Management expressed optimism on securing several big-ticket railway projects this year.

Airport losses shrink; Turns break-even on EBITDA in 4Q

4Q21 was a decent quarter for the airport unit, as EBITDA turned positive and losses shrunk. Management noted it is nearing break-even levels already this month. Meanwhile, a hearing is already set on the dismissal of the anti-dummy case on April 25.

Landport unit revenues drop QoQ again; incurs a loss in 4Q

The landport unit incurred a loss in 4Q, the first time since the pandemic began. Revenues and EBITDA dropped further QoQ, which may have been due to adjustments in lease rates.

 
Cebu Air - Riding out the turbulence; Maintain Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Medium-term outlook intact

After a false start, the anticipated recovery in the domestic market is well underway. While there are headwinds (oil prices, interest rates, as well as a weaker PHP/USD rate), we remain upbeat about the medium[1]term outlook as demand bounces back. We believe the company is well positioned to sustain market share gains and raise profitability despite high fuel prices. We reiterate our BUY rating on a revised 12-month TP of P60/sh.

Domestic recovery shining through

With nearly all its domestic destinations (36 out of 37 pre-Covid) largely open for tourists since March, CEB announced it is aiming to deploy more than 100% of its pre-pandemic domestic capacity this month. Its competitors have shrunk capacity more than we had anticipated (see Figure 3 inside) and we believe CEB is poised to sustain the market share gains it has achieved so far. Jefferies’ airline capacity survey estimates CEB restoring over 70% of its pre-pandemic seats on both domestic and international routes as of April, up from <50% in 1Q22. This suggest the airline is already matching pre-pandemic levels of operating capacity on domestic routes, in line with management’s plan.

FY22E adjusted for higher input costs

We cut our FY22E EBITDA forecasts to incorporate the anticipated margin compression in the short term from jet kero price spikes. Despite the three[1]month lag between the jet kero price index and the fuel surcharge, we believe airlines will be able to fully recoup higher fuel costs. We also believe domestic passenger demand will be robust enough to withstand higher ticket prices. Our FY23E EBITDA (unch) is forecasted to exceed 2019, premised on sustained growth in the domestic market and a rebound in international travel.

12-month TP of P60/sh offers 27% shareholder return

We downgrade our TP to P60, which is based on 6.5x EV/EBITDA on the average of FY22E/FY23E EBITDA, compared to its historical average (pre-pandemic) of 7.1x. Risks: higher jet kero prices, weak PHP:USD, lower demand.

 
Century Pacific Food - Speed bumps ahead
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Runway for growth is long despite short term speed bumps

CNPF’s Executive Chairman Chris Po, at our recent CEO Roundtable, remained optimistic on the company’s longer term growth prospects despite near-term speed bumps. After two years of consecutive >20% earnings growth, Mr Po sees growth this year to be more modest due to headwinds. Longer term, Mr Po believes that the new categories and segments (e.g. pet food, alternative proteins/milk) that they have invested in over the last couple of years should boost growth and profitability as these businesses mature.

Core products historically outperform during high inflation periods

CNPF’s core branded canned food products are considered pantry staples and are among the cheapest sources of protein. During periods of high inflation, volumes are typically driven by CNPF’s value brands as consumers become more price-sensitive and trade down. For 2022 specifically, the reopening of the economy (i.e. higher employment) is likely to give consumers more capacity to absorb the price increases. Amidst this backdrop, Mr Po believes that double[1]digit revenue growth for 2022 (Regis FY22E +16% YoY) is achievable.

Muted earnings growth but margins actively being managed

The company plans to raise prices by high single-digit in 2022, but Mr Po cautioned that these price adjustments may not be enough to fully cover input cost increases. Prices of key inputs (e.g. packaging, oils, skimmed milk powder) remain elevated, and a depreciating peso is aggravating pressure on cost and margins. Mr Po expects these cost pressures to result in more muted earnings growth in 2022 (low single-digit) vs the past two years. Regis is forecasting a mild decline in earnings (-3% YoY) in FY22E.

 

Long term growth runway remains long

Despite the immediate headwinds, Mr Po remains positive on the company’s growth prospects over the longer term. CNPF has made several investments (both organic and inorganic) over the past few years, namely pet food, alternative proteins, and refrigerated meats. These categories are expected to see significant structural growth over the next 5-10 years as GDP per capita in the country expands. Mr Po’s longer-term ambition is for CNPF to grow by 2x GDP annually (+10-15%).

Healthy returns, free cash flow

Being a market leader in its more established categories (branded canned marine and meat), we believe CNPF has a high degree of pricing power and should be able to pass on input cost inflation, albeit gradually. Notwithstanding the near-term pressure on earnings, we believe CNPF’s cash flow generation should remain healthy – its ROE and FCF yield are among the highest within our consumer sector coverage. At 17x FY22E, CNPF trades at a discount to peers.

 
Robinsons Land - Rosier rental segments
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Notes from the REGIS CEO Roundtable Series

At our REGIS CEO Roundtable Series, Frederick Go, Robinsons Land’s President, as well as other members of the management team, provided an update on current operations as well as their views on the company’s outlook. The rental segments appear to be recovering nicely and Mr. Go remains optimistic about their long-term prospects. However, the residential business is currently facing challenges in the form of cancellations and higher input costs.

Malls – tenant sales back to 80%-85% of pre-COVID levels

Since mobility restrictions were further loosened in March, mall tenant sales have reached 80%-85% of pre-COVID levels. However, mall revenue has not yet recovered as much since RLC continues to grant rental concessions to tenants. Mr. Go believes that at the earliest, malls will only return to pre-COVID profitability in late 2022. Thus, malls will not be spun off into REITs before 2023.

Offices – New contracts signed

Despite continued implementation of work-from-home/hybrid work by many companies, Mr. Go remains positive on the prospects of the office segment. None of RLC’s office tenants have signaled an intention to reduce office space. In fact, there have been new contracts from both BPOs and traditional tenants in RLC’s new office buildings (in Ortigas Center and Iloilo). Management’s long-term bullishness on the office segment is exemplified by the redevelopment of Robinsons Forum (an old RLC mall) into four office buildings on top of a retail podium.

Residential – cancellations continue; steel prices jump

Headwinds remain for the residential segment. Residential cancellations remain high and Mr. Go believes that the cleanup of residential accounts will likely take the entire 2022. In addition to the cancellation issue, input costs have risen. In particular, steel prices have jumped more than 50% over the past two years. To our surprise, however, Mr. Go appeared unperturbed, saying that price increases of RLC’s condominiums would likely be sufficient to maintain margins.

2022 profit to exceed 2019 levels

Mr. Go expressed his confidence that RLC’s 2022 net profit would exceed the 2019 level. This would imply that earnings will reach at least P8.7bn, much higher than the REGIS forecast of P7.3bn and consensus estimate of P7.9bn, which likely means that there are sizeable lot sales in the works. We consider RLC a key beneficiary of economic reopening as well as a mall REIT spin-off. We also share management’s view that RLC shares are undervalued. The stock currently trades at 49% discount to NAV and 0.8X FY22E P/B.

 
Aboitiz Power - 1Q22: Income dips on outages, GNPD starting to contribute
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q22 profit -44%QoQ, -53%YoY - behind

AP’s 1Q22 dipped to P2.908bn (-44%QoQ, -53%Yoy). Apart from the high base from insurance claims/liquidated damages in prior periods, the result also reflected the impact of plant outages due to Odette (unplanned) and maintenance outages ahead of elections (planned). Energy sales for both generation and distribution were down 1%YoY. The results appears behind Regis and consensus estimates for FY22, although GNPD is expected to rise in contribution over the coming quarters.

Generation: income declines on plant outages, high comps due to claims last year

Core income from generation/RES amounted to P3.241bn (-45%QoQ, -46%YoY). Excluding the impact of insurance claims, profit would have been flat QoQ and - 14% YoY due to plant outages and booking of dep’n & interest for GNPD Unit 1. Management estimates the outages of TVI (due to Typhoon Odette) and Pagbilao (earlier maintenance ahead of elections) shaved off about P820mn from core income. Absolute gross margins fell 13% QoQ on higher volume/rates of purchased power. On a more positive note, GNPD’s unit 1 contributed P1.6bn in beneficial EBITDA in the period.

Distribution: 1Q sales down 1%YoY, margins fall due to pass-through timing difference

Distribution income amounted to P742mn (11%QoQ, -32% YoY), bouncing slightly from 4Q which saw additional opex relating to Typhoon Odette. Margins fell to P1.34/kWh, well below the 2021 average of P1.6/kWh due to timing differences between higher power prices and pass-thru recognition. This should reverse in succeeding periods. Total volumes across its utilities dipped 1%YoY (residential -5%YoY), in large part due to the impact of Odette on its biggest utility VECO felt until early February.

Outlook

Management expects earnings to catch up in succeeding periods as 1) spot prices are expected to peak in 2Q-3Q and unit 2 of GNPD begins contributing to earnings. They also estimate they could book insurance claims of around P1.4bn sometime this year relating to prior plant outages.

 
Wilcon Depot - Margin outperformance in 1Q22; momentum to be sustained
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q22 net profit jumps 41% on strong margins

WLCON’s 1Q22 net profit jumped 41% YoY to P851mn, tracking slightly ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. The outperformance was mainly due to strong margins during the quarter. 1Q22 net sales grew 15% YoY to P7.7bn, but this is likely to accelerate in the coming quarters given the low base in 2Q/3Q.

SSSG accelerates as economy reopens

SSSG accelerated to +8.6%, driving same-store sales levels to c5% above pre[1]Covid levels. While January was soft because of the Omicron surge, SSS quickly recovered in Feb-Mar. There was a +13% in average ticket size during the quarter, though transaction counts declined c4%. Management noted that consumers visited the stores less frequently but purchased larger baskets per visit.

Operating leverage drives 1Q margin

Operating margin jumped c250bps QoQ and YoY due to operating leverage from the SSS improvement. Gross margin was largely unchanged on a QoQ basis at 38.3% even as the contribution of higher margin in-house brands inched higher – now at >51% from 50% in 4Q21. We believe there is still room for margins to expand slightly as the contribution of in-house brands increases.

Top line momentum to be sustained as economy continues to reopen

Management noted that the strong top line momentum has been sustained and will likely continue for the rest of the year as the economy continues to reopen. Supply chain issues are expected to persist, but pricing adjustment have been implemented to cover the additional costs. Inventory levels remain elevated (>200 days) and management plans to keep them high due to anticipated delays in shipping and logistics issues.

 
Manila Electric Company - 1Q22: Recovery underway but tariff reset remains key issue
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1Q core income up 10%

Meralco reported net income of P5.56bn (+28%) and core income of P5.62bn (+10%) for 1Q22, broadly on trend to meet our full-year estimate. Electricity sales for the quarter were up 5.7% YoY, which is just a tad below our assumed +7% for the year. The quarter was marked by the contrasting fortunes of its two largest generation subsidiaries: one produced surprisingly strong profits while the other dipped into a net loss.

Electricity sales up 5.7%

Electricity sales for 1Q were +5.7% higher compared to the previous year with the key segments all growing at roughly the same pace. Overall volumes are now 7% above the pre-pandemic 1Q19, we note that sales to the commercial segment (i.e. offices, malls, et al) are still 8% below.

Contrasting fortunes

Meralco’s two biggest generation subsidiaries posted contrasting results. GBP contributed a P517m loss for the quarter as Typhoon Odette affected operations. In contrast, 58%-owned subsidiary Pacific Light recorded a surprisingly large profit of P3.1bn as spot prices in Singapore climbed.

Rate reset application seeks 14% tariff hike

Last month, Meralco filed its rate reset application for the Fifth Regulatory Period (5RP), which will run for four years from July 2022. Based on the filing, Meralco is seeking an average tariff of P1.57/kwh for 5RP, premised on a regulatory WACC (return) of 13.2% and average annual capex of P38bn. This is 14% higher than what Meralco is currently collecting (P1.38/kwh) and >40% more than what we estimate it is recognizing in its P&L (~P1.1/kwh). While a P1.57/kwh tariff is very unlikely, anything above P1.1/kwh would likely lead to upgrades across the Street.

BDO 1Q22 NPAT up 13% to P11.7bn, ahead on revenue recovery, cost controls

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported NPAT of P11.7bn, up 13%, ahead of Regis (P40.3bn) but slightly behind consensus (P48bn). The outperformance (vs our forecasts) were largely due to opex, which came in lower than what Regis expected. Overall revenues were up 7% YoY, while opex was up just 3% YoY, taking PPOP up 15% YoY. Loan growth accelerated slightly to 7.5% YoY from 6.5% in 4Q, though NIMs were flat/down slightly QoQ. Asset quality improved, with NPLs down a tad to 2.72%; BDO used strong operating results to rebuild its NPL cover to 121% (from 111% in 4Q).

 

Some highlights:

Loan growth was 7.5% YoY, driven by the mid-market segment. Deposits were also up 7.1% YoY, but CASA grew faster at 11%, taking CASA ratio to a new high of 86%.

NIMs, though were down slightly (-5bps QoQ) to 3.91%, driven by lower loan yields (down 8bps QoQ), while deposit costs were broadly flat, -2bps QoQ from already extremely low levels (was 0.21% in 1Q22).

Fees grew 6% YoY, sustaining levels that are well above pre-Covid levels. The growth was driven by service charges, which surged 18% YoY. Trading/fx gains were up 55% YoY from a low base.

Opex was kept surprisingly benign, up just 2.5% YoY. Compensation was up just 4% YoY, with insurance policy reserves and insurance benefits down 6%.

Provisions were up 28% to 3.7bn, despite an improvement in NPL ratio to 2.72% from 2.81% in 4Q21.

 

The bank seems to be using strong operations to rebuild its NPL coverage, which improved to 121% from 111% in the previous quarter. Regis also estimated that Restructured Loans (RL) improved to 5.2% of loans, down from 5.7% at end-2021. Overall a solid set of results. The bank will not be holding a briefing for 1Q22.

RCBC to remove foreign ownership limit

RCBC (RCB PM) disclosed that its board of directors have approved the deletion of the provision limiting foreign ownership to 40% from its articles of incorporation. The change requires ratification by the shareholders at its AGM scheduled for 27 June. As of March 2022, RCBC’s foreign ownership was at 33.67%. Of this, Cathay Life was 22.19%. In June 2021, Japan’s Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corp (SMBC) acquired a 4.99% stake in the bank. It is unclear at this stage if RCBC is making room for either Cathay or SMBC (or both?), or indeed for yet another foreign partner.

 

In any case, a stronger, better capitalized RCBC could pose a new competitive threat in the industry. As of end-2021, RCBC was the country’s 6th largest non-government bank, with total assets of P959bn (roughly $18.5bn). However, that’s less half the size of the Big Three, at between P2.5-3.7trn. As of end-2021, RCBC’s CET-1 was 12.16% at the consolidated level, and 11.81% at the “solo” or parent bank level, not far from the regulatory minimum of 11.0%. (Though the BSP suspended the capital conservation buffer for Covid, it will probably reinstate it at some stage.)

 

To the downside, though, having foreigners own more than 40% of the bank will constrain it from owning land assets. The bank will likely sell/transfer these into different entities to comply with the Constitutional limit of 60% Filipino ownership of land. However, the inability to own land will hinder the bank’s ability to do mortgage lending, or indeed to lend while taking any land assets as collateral. In theory, the bank can set up an SPV that will take the asset at foreclosure. Apart from the requirement to sell within 5 years, the SPV will make foreclosing more complicated and/or expensive. Historically, foreign banks have tried to enter mortgage lending, but this requirement forced them to eventually retreat.

Beyond RCBC though, one has to wonder if either Cathay or Sumitomo (or both) might also be interested in the family’s insurance businesses. Malayan Insurance is the country’s largest non-life insurance company by premiums written, and second-largest by assets. The family also controls 51% of Sun Life Grepa Financial, with Sun Life of Canada taking the 49% stake.

At current prices (P20.10/sh), the bank’s market cap is just under P40bn (c$780mn), or about 0.35x Price-to-book, based on its end-2021 book value (P111bn, or P54.50/sh). SMBC’s purchase of 4.99% in June 2021 was priced at about P44/sh.

Colliers: 1Q22 office rents fall; residential prices stay flat

BSP sanctions BDO, UBP on fraud incident

The BSP said it has imposed sanctions on BDO Unibank (BDO PM) and Union Bank (UBP PM) for the hacking incident in December 2021. Recall that hackers were able to access accounts of BDO depositors, and transferred funds to accounts in UBP. Some reports say the funds were converted into crypto and transferred out. The transactions were in lots of up to P50,000, with around 700 accounts affected. While no figures were verified, some estimates ranged from P5mn to P7mn (about $100-140k) in terms of total amount stolen. BDO claims to have reimbursed clients for the losses. In January, police arrested two Nigerian nationals and three Filipinos that allegedly carried out the scheme. The nature of the sanctions was not disclosed. However, our sources say that these are mostly commitments to improve processes within a given time frame. There was also an increase in capital charge for operational risk. As far as we can tell, there was no monetary penalty for either bank.

Domestic pax volumes in NAIA reverts to 78% of pre-pandemic levels in March

Domestic passenger traffic at NAIA jumped sharply to 1.5mn in March, reverting to 78% of pre-pandemic levels from 49% in Feb 2022 and 20% in the same period last year. This brings 1Q22 domestic pax volumes up 215% YoY to 3.17mn or 55% of pre-Covid pax vols. Meanwhile, international pax volumes rose 55% MoM and 186% YoY to 489k, or about 23% of pre-pandemic levels. 1Q22  international pax volumes doubled YoY to 1.03mn pax or 16% of pre-pandemic levels.

 

 

Budget deficit narrows in March, as revenues pick up pace

Data from the Bureau of Treasury showed the budget deficit shrink by 2% YoY to P187.7bn in March. Revenues picked up pace, with tax revenues up 29% YoY while non-tax revenues surged 88% YoY due to higher dividends from state-run firms. Primary spending also reversed the slight decline in 2M22, rising by 18% YoY in March. The government cited release of funds for scholarship programs and capital outlay projects of the Dept. of Public Works and Dept. of National Defense. This brings 1Q22 budget deficit to P317bn, down 1.4% YoY.

Hot money exits in March

Data released by the BSP showed the Philippines booked its biggest hot money outflow in eight months amounting to US$305mn in March. This is 44% lower than the US$541mn outflow in the same month last year and reversed net inflows in Jan/Feb 2022. The inflows jumped to US$1.28bn, of which 86.7% went to listed securities in the PSE and remaining 13.3% went to peso government securities while outflows grew to US$1.58bn. This brings 1Q22 net outflow of foreign portfolio investments to US$16.44mn, down 97% YoY.
