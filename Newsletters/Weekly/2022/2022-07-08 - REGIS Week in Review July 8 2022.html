REGIS REPORT

08 July 2022

The Phils snapped a four-week losing streak with a 3.2% advance this week. Stocks rose as investors discounted softening in commodity prices, even as recession worries remained.  Brent fell to below US$100 – a first since April.  

Gains were broadbased, with gainers overtaking losers 11:4.

Large caps pushed the market higher, with SM (+9.0% WoW) and BDO (+6.0% WoW) pushing the market higher.  URC (+9.8% WoW) also led, seeing notable net inflows this week.

The rest of the market though continued to see funds flow out, though outflows were lighter at US$26.2m this week. Biggest outflows were in ALI, ICT, TEL while URC saw net inflows.

Taiwan, along with several other EU countries have ordered a recall of several batches of MONDE’s (unch)  flagship brand Lucky Me! instant noodles after traces of ethylene oxide were found. The immediate financial impact will not be large as exports account for <2% of group sales though concerns on its longer -term impact caused some  market jitters. The shares dropped markedly on Thursday when the news broke (-7%). Instant noodles account for ~35% of MONDE’s consolidated sales, and an even larger proportion of profit. Lucky Me! dominates the instant noodle market with 70% market share.

Meanwhile, Meralco (-0.85% WoW) received a long awaited decision relating to its distribution tariffs for the “lapsed period”  (July 2015 to June 2022)  where no proper tariff review was conducted. The regulator affixed a tariff of P1.3522/kwh for Meralco for the lapsed period, lower than the P1.38/kwh previously approved.  The regulator issued an order directing Meralco to refund P21.8bn to consumers.

The refund order is much smaller than what Meralco has provisioned for. In fact, Regis estimates Meralco would have P50bn in “excess” provisions that it can reverse. The after-tax impact of a P50bn provision reversal would be ~P38bn, equivalent to almost 10% of Meralco’s market capitalization. Looking ahead, it is also worth asking if Meralco will cease setting aside provisions for its tariff beginning this month (July 2022). Looking ahead, a decision on its rate review for the 5th Regulatory Period points to significant earnings uplift should rates be kept at P1.35/kwh.

The CPI inflation print for June came in at 6.1%, a further acceleration from the 5.4% in May but within Street expectations. Food and Transport were the big drivers for inflation. Food inflation climbed to 6.0% while Transport soared to 17%. The CPI figure has not yet hit its peak it seems, as the impact of a 22% (nationwide) hike in jeepney fares will only be felt this month, and the second and third order impact will be felt in subsequent months.  The anticipated rise in the CPI prints is giving the BSP greater push to raise policy rates sooner and by bigger steps. The new BSP Governor Felipe Medalla is now guiding for policy rates to reach 3.50% by year-end. This implies a  +100bp increase from the current 2.50%.

Meanwhile, the PHP declined weakened for the 7th straight week. The PHP depreciated 1.7% this week to PHP56.034/US$.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ERC orders P21bn refund for Meralco’s lapsed period, large provision reversal looms

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) ordered Meralco to refund P21bn after setting Meralco’s tariff for the lapsed regulatory period. The lapsed regulatory period covers July 2015 up to June 2022, during which time the regulator did not undertake a review of Meralco’s tariffs. The refund will be over a 12-month period to June 2023; residential customers will receive a refund of P0.86/kwh which will reduce end-user rates by 8%. Meralco has set aside provisions for possible refunds since July 2015 resulting in significantly understated earnings over that period. Meralco accounts for 60% of MPI’s gross asset value. Regis estimates MPI’s target price NAV could rise by 7% to P7.95/sh. MPI is trading at a 53% discount to its market based NAV of P7.66/sh.

Supreme Court upholds 2013 ERC order allowing Meralco’s staggered power rate hike

The Supreme Court has upheld the 2013 order of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) which has allowed the Manila Electric Company (Meralco) to implement a staggered power rate hike amounting to P22.64bn. Six justices voted to affirm the 2013 ERC order, while five dissented. Two magistrates inhibited from the voting. In 2013, the ERC approved a staggered increase of P7.67 per kilowatt hour (kWh) for the December 2013 billing and ordered an additional P1.00/kWh increase in the February 2014 billing. This was due to the shutdown of Malampaya’s operations and scheduled maintenance of other power generation plants, causing WESM prices to spike. Meralco has neither collected the amount from end users nor paid the gencos.

Wawa JV to start delivery of 80MLD water to Manila Water

WaWa JVCo said Phase 1 of the WaWa Bulk Water Supply project filled up its reservoir, signaling completion ahead of its schedule in October. It can now deliver 80 MLD to offtaker Manila Water (MWC PM). The phase 2 of the project (518 MLD) will be completed in 2025. Once fully complete, MWC’s total water supply will rise to 2100 MLD from 1600 currently. The entire project cost is estimated at P27.6bn. Wawa JVCo is an 82%-owned unit of Razon-led Prime Infrastructure while 18% is owned by San Lorenzo Ruiz Builders & Development Group Inc.

MPI tollroad unit buys 40% stake in Indonesian tollroad

PT Margautama Nusantara (MUN), a 72%-owned unit of Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC), is acquiring a 40% stake in Jasa Marga Jalanlayang Cikampek (JJC). JJC holds the concession for the 38-km elevated tollroad called Jakarta-Cikampek Elevated (“Japex”). Japex serves as an entry/exit gate from Jakarta to West, Central and East Java; Began operations on Dec 2019. JJC is an 80%-owned unit of Jasa Marga (JSMR IJ). The transaction is subject to certain conditions. The payment will be in tranches: 18% (July 31, 2022), 73% (Dec 20, 2022), 9% (Dec 31, 2024). MUN is buying the 40% stake for IDR4.4tn (P16.2bn). The valuation implies 0.7x PBV, which is lower than valuation of JSMR IJ (1x). The acquisition will be funded with short-term debt initially at MUN level. MUN will effectively be funding the acquisition with cash through a combination of sales of its tollroad assets and additional cash infusion from MPTC/MPI. As at end-March 2022, MPI’s parent-level gross cash stood at P17bn and net debt stood at P66bn. MPI is trading at a 54% discount to its market NAV of P7.58/sh.

Nickel Asia ties up with Shell to develop 1GW of renewable energy

Nickel Asia Corporation disclosed that its renewable energy unit Emerging Power Inc (EPI) signed a joint venture with Shell Overseas Investments B.V> to develop 1GW of renewable energy projects in the Philippines by 2028. The JV between Emerging Power Inc would “potentially increase” to 3GW. The venture will focus on utility-scale solar photovoltaic but will also consider onshore wind and energy storage systems. NIKL owns 87% of Emerging Power Inc. EPI has 68MW in operating capacity.

Taiwan, EU order recall of Monde Nissin’s (MONDE PM) Lucky Me! products

Taiwan, along with several other EU countries have ordered a recall of several batches of MONDE’s flagship brand Lucky Me! instant noodles. This was after traces of ethylene oxide (a commonly used pesticide) were detected in the products. MONDE maintains that its products are safe to consume and comply with local and US FDA standards for ethylene oxide. Branded exports account for <5% of MONDE’s consolidated sales, while instant noodles account for ~35% (50% of APAC BFB sales). MONDE currently trades at 25x PE and 15x EV/EBITDA for FY22E.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government debt dips in May

The government’s debt stock decreased by P267bn (-2%) MoM to P12.5tn in May and cited settlement of P300bn in provisional advances from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas. Compared with end-2021, the national government’s debt was P768bn (+6%) higher. Of the P12.5tn debt stock, 69% were domestic borrowings while 31% were sourced externally. The government expects its debt-to-GDP ratio will peak in 2023 at 67% before falling to 66% in 2026. Former Finance Secretary Dominguez previously said that the country’s debt-to-GDP ratio may not return to pre-pandemic levels (40%) for at least 10 years. To reach this ratio by 2031, a median annual primary balance (revenues minus non-interest expenditures) increase of 2.42% of GDP would be needed. Based on the most optimistic scenario that assumes a GDP growth rate of 7% and a real interest rate of 2%.

T-bill rates up across the board

The government raised P15bn from the issuance of 91-, 182-, 364-day T-bills. The auction was 2.2x oversubscribed, with total bids reaching P32.8bn. The 91-day T-bill fetched a yield of 1.908%, +53bps from previous auction (BVAL: 1.8363%). The 182-day T-bill fetched a yield of 2.608%, +20bps from previous auction (BVAL: 2.474%). The 364-day T-bill fetched a yield of 2.811%, +18bps from previous auction (BVAL: 2.7006%). The higher rates due to higher inflation expectations. The BSP expects inflation for June to range bet 5.7%-6.5%; Consensus: 6%. The Bureau of Treasury plans to raise P200bn this month, of which P60bn will be via T-bills and P140bn via T-bonds.

NGCP places Luzon power grid on yellow alert

The Luzon power grid was placed on “yellow alert” on Tuesday after four power plants experienced four outages according to the national Grid of the Philippines

The yellow alert was in effect from 10am to 11am and would continue from 1pm to 4pm

NGCP said the following plants were on forced outage –

GNPD 1 – 668MW

GMEC 2 – 316MW

Calaca 2 – 240MW

SLPGC 3 & 4 – 50MW

Masinloc 1 in Zambales was derated by 165MW

Luzon had 1,439 unplanned unavailable energy with a net operating margin of only 236MW

Gov’t eyes 100% resumption of face-to-face classes in November

President Marcos Jr. announced that his government is targeting to fully implement in-person classes nationwide by November. Gov’t will start phased schooling by September.

 

BSP chief signals rate hike path to 3.5%

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that he won’t rule out at least 100bps more rate hikes this year, which would take the policy rate to 3.5% by year-end. A 25bps rate hike in the August meeting is “all but guaranteed”. The Monetary Board members may even vote for a 50bps point move, but no one wants a 75bps rate hike. The BSP has so far raised its policy rates by +50bps this year (vs FED: +150bps). The BSP is closely monitoring MoM consumer price trends. The board will have to mount a bigger response if the numbers are bad. The pre-pandemic, BSP’s policy rate was at 4%.

Inflation soars to four-year high in June at 6.1%

Headline inflation rate accelerated to 6.1% in June from 5.4% in May. This is broadly within street expectations (6%) and the BSP’s forecast range (5.7%-6.5%) and  the highest recorded inflation data since October 2018. The MoM, inflation jumped 0.9% biggest jump since January 2022. The inflation was driven by food, particularly rice, meat, fish, fruits, vegetables, and sugar. Prices of electricity and fuel used for transport services also rose by 18% and 54%, respectively. The average inflation for 1H22 stood at 4.4%. The gov’t plans to continue and to expand the fuel subsidy for the transport sector to include tricycles.

 

Marcos Jr. admin tempers 2022 economic growth target; unveils six-year fiscal framework

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said they are now aiming for GDP to grow by 6.5%-7.5% this year. This is slightly lower than the 7-8% target set by the Devt Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) in May but will still be the highest GDP growth rate among ASEAN+3. The DBCC will meet on Friday to firm up projections. Diokno said the economy likely grew at a double-digit rate pace (or above 10%) in 2Q, faster than the 8.3% print in 1Q22. The 2Q GDP data is set to be released on August 9

 

Its 6-year fiscal framework is aimed to:

+6.5-8% GDP growth rates from 2023-2028 (faster than Duterte admin’s 6-7% goal)

Reduce poverty incidence to its lowest-ever of 9% by 2028 from 17-18% pre-pandemic

Revert budget deficit to pre-pandemic levels equivalent to 3% of GDP starting 2026

Ease public debt ratio to 60% of GDP by 2025 from 63% currently

Infrastructure spend-to-GDP to be limited at 5-6% in 2023-2028; Will welcome PPP projects for national and local levels

Achieve an “upper-middle income status” or US$4,046 per capita income by 2028

More details will be released during President Marcos Jr’s first SONA on July 25. The Marcos admin will also push for the passage of Packages 3 and 4 of the Duterte-era Comprehensive Tax Reform Program (CTRP). Package 3 looks to build an “equitable and efficient” real property valuation system (currently at the Senate committee since end-2021). Package 4 (PIFITA) seeks to make passive income and financial intermediary taxes “fairer and more competitive”. The will reduce the number of tax rates from 80 to 36 and harmonize the tax rates on interest, dividends and capital gains, and business taxes on financial intermediaries. These packages will be “revenue-neutral” but will simplify the tax structure

Bad loan ratio falls to 16-month low in May

Gross NPL ratio for the banking sector stood at 3.75% in May. This is the lowest since 3.72% in January 2021 and down from 3.93% in April. Bad loans dropped by 4% MoM and 11% YoY to P429bn in May. The gross loan portfolio expanded by 7% YoY and 0.4% MoM to P11.44tn. NPL coverage ratio improved to 94.76% from 79.96% a year ago. The past due loans fell by 14% YoY to P508.5bn, bringing the ratio down to 4.44% from 5.56% a year ago. Meanwhile, restructured loans rose by 27.78% YoY to P337bn, accounting for 2.94% of banks’ loan portfolio in May. Loan loss reserves were higher at P407bn from P383bn a year ago, bringing the ratio down to 3.55% vs. 3.59% a year ago.

Unemployment rate improves YoY but still worse than pre-pandemic levels

The unemployment rate improved to 6% in May from 7.7% in the same period last year. However, this is still worse than pre-pandemic levels (c. 5%). The higher number of labor force outpaced job creation. The employment rate in May stood at 94%, better YoY and is also close to pre-pandemic levels (94.7%). Around 46mn are employed, with net employment creation at 3.5mn compared with pre-pandemic levels. Significant employment gains were recorded in the services sector as tourism and business outlook improved with the further easing of restrictions. Underemployment rate remained high at 14.5%.

GIR drops to US$102bn in June

The country’s gross international reserves dropped by US$1.7bn (1.6%) MoM to US$102bn. This is down from the peak of US$110bn in December 2020. The gov’t cited national government’s foreign currency debt payments and decrease in value of gold holdings. The GIR is equivalent to 8.5 months’ worth of imports of goods.
