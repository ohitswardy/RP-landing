REGIS REPORT

19 January 2024

The market finally corrected following its outperformance to start the year. The index fell -2.1% WoW to 6,503.5 mostly on the back of locals taking profit. Investors were generally on the sidelines on concerns that the cut in rates may be delayed.

Headline flows also turned negative at US$8.9m following two straight weeks of inflows.

All sectors were generally weak as the selling was broad-based. Biggest drags to the index were the large caps SM (-3.6% WoW), SM Prime (-2.4% WoW), and Ayala Land (-5.0% WoW).

ICT (-2.7% WoW) likewise dragged the index.  Unsuccessful bidders for the 25-year contract to operate and manage South Africa’s largest gateway port, Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2), are reportedly demanding to know the reason why the government selected ICTSI (ICT PM) to be its private partner. While this development seemingly reduces ICTSI’s prospects for an April 2024 turnover, it is nonetheless a common occurrence of high-profile biddings. It is also worth noting that a foiled takeover will not have an impact on our valuation, as our estimates do not include this acquisition and ICT itself has not shelled out money to secure the contract.

Only 10 names ended in the green.

Jollibee (+0.8% WoW) posted modest gains, although URC (-5.4% WoW) fell on heavy foreign selling. URC saw the most outflows for the week at US$6.9m.

Bloomberry (+1.8% WoW) outperformed after gaming agency PAGCOR said that it expects 2024 gaming revenues for the industry to reach P336bn in 2024, a growth of 17% from a record-setting 2023 performance. GGR jumped 33% in 2023 on the back of strong contributions from integrated resorts (P207bn) and, more notably, electronic games (P58bn). The regulator’s outlook for 2024 aligns with our revenue estimates of integrated resorts this year (+15-20%), and Bloomberry is expected to extend its revenue streak on the back of increased play from existing players, the return of tourists and the new property.

Globe (+1.1% WoW) also rose earlier in the week as there are talks again that GCash (Mynt) could list by 2H24.

Meanwhile, GTCAP (-1.0% WoW) reported that vehicle sales rose 5% YoY in December to 39,153 units, bringing full-year industry sales up 22% YoY to 429,807 units. Toyota’s sales were solid, jumping by 13% YoY to 19,551 units in December. For the full year, Toyota sold 200k units, up 15% YoY and exceeding our forecast of 192k units. Last year’s sales outperformance also suggests that our FY24E sales forecast of 206k units looks very achievable.

Foreign inflows were concentrated in the banks BDO and BPI for US$6.9m and US$3.7m respectively. However, foreign demand still failed to lift the sector. BDO still ended in negative territory for -1.6% WoW while BPI -0.8% WoW.

Philippine Strategy - 2024 Outlook – Dragon vs Groundhog
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Finally, the year of the Philippines?

The PSEi roared early into 2024, ushering in the Year of the Dragon. But it’s hard to shake the feeling of Groundhog Day, with the market starting the past few years strong, only to end the year flat-to-down. We stayed bullish through the headwinds of 2022 and 2023, and hold the course into 2024. The Philippine macro and market stand to gain in the consensus narrative of soft US/China growth, easing inflation, falling interest rates, and weak USD. After years of de-rating, Philippine equities now offer exceedingly good value, with decades-low PERs, and high div yields. The PSEi could be the high-beta play on EM, as inflows from foreign and local funds squeeze into a relatively illiquid market.

Profits up 24% in 9M23, to grow another 9-12% in 2024E

Earnings stayed buoyant in 9M23, up 24% YoY in aggregate for stocks we cover, topping the record-high profit in 2022. This was despite challenges from inflation and government underspending. Domestic demand remained broadly resilient in 2023, though there were clear signs of down-trading in 2H23. We forecast 2024E NPAT to grow another 9% (up 12% ex-SCC/DMC)

Cheap valuations, high dividend yields, and insider buying

The PSEi Index, however, fell another 2% YoY. After years of derating, the index is now at 11x forward PER, the lowest in over a decade. More than half the stocks we cover trade at single-digit PERs, with many paying dividend yields of 4-5% or higher. Insiders seem to agree; we estimate that companies have bought back close to $2bn worth of stock in the last 5 years.

All about the flow; rate cuts bullish EM, Phils as a beta play

As our partner Jefferies’s Chris Wood writes, “the Fed switch is clearly… USD bearish and therefore positive for Asia and EM equities.” Back home, December inflation eased to 3.9%, giving the BSP room to begin rate cuts by 2Q2024. Foreigners are back as net buyers from November, with over $70mn in inflow in the first two weeks of 2024 alone. Local AUMs, meanwhile, could see rotation away from fixed income and FX asset, back into PHP equities.

Staying bullish, but mindful of El Niňo, CAD

Thematically, we Overweight the property and consumer sectors. We’d expect conglomerates to attract investors looking for (relatively) liquid market proxies. We move banks to Neutral, though valuations remain undemanding. We raise telcos to Neutral, and Underweight the power sector. Our top picks would include Ayala Corp, Ayala Land, GT Capital, Jollibee, ICTSI, and Globe. For the brave, we’d look at Figures 12 and 13 for deep value, potential multi-baggers. The country’s Current Account deficit remains a long-term concern, especially with the threat of AI to the outsourcing industry. The El Niňo dry weather could persist, and keep inflation elevated.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - What’s in store for ‘24
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Base building; reiterate BUY

We reiterate our BUY rating on Bloomberry as we look towards 2024 and beyond. Despite the added overhead this year as the new property ramps up, the opening of Solaire North in 2Q24 provides it a competitive advantage, with a new avenue to pursue its domestic initiatives over the medium term. We tweak our estimates slightly to reflect recent developments but maintain the 3-yr CAGR outlook of 18% for GGR, 21% for EBITDA and 36% for profit. Our target price is unchanged at P15.0/share (FY24E) which presents a 50% upside from current levels.

First FY impact of Solaire North could drive EBITDA 26% higher than 23E

While we expect a marginal net addition to EBITDA in FY24E (+8%YoY), net profit is forecast to go down by 19%YoY on pre-opening expenses, depreciation and interest expense as the new property ramps up through 2H24. After the initial overhang, however, we expect EBITDA and income on its first full year of operations to be 26%/23% than peak profitability in FY23E.

Expanded footprint – 38% more tables, 112% more machines

BLOOM’s expanded footprint arising from the opening of Solaire North implies a 38-112% growth in total gaming capacity. We assume table yields to be half and slot machine yields to be the same as Solaire’s in its first full year of operations (FY25E), which we believe are reasonable given the target demographic. On a fully-ramped basis, we think the property could add c.P10bn in EBITDA and c.P5bn in profit, which is likely to be 3-4 years out, in our view.

Estimates tweaked on higher 23E base, expanded common shares

We tweak our estimates to account for developments including the most recent volume and win rate trends and share placement. Our revised EBITDA and profit estimates are higher by 5% and 10% for FY23E, and marginally higher for FY24E[1]FY25E. Our EPS estimates are nevertheless lower by 1-3% due to an increase in number of shares due to the placement.

Target price at P15.0/sh on 10x EV/EBITDA

We keep our target price unchanged at P15.0/sh which is based on a target multiple of 10x EV/EBITDA to Solaire’s FY24E plus the market value of the unused landbank. At our TP, BLOOM would trade at 10.1x consolidated EBITDA for FY24E– in line with historical multiples and Macau. Key risks include regulation, competition, tourism and the arbitration award.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Commercial bank loan growth slows to 7.0% in November; M3 growth decelerates to 7.0%

Preliminary data showed commercial bank loans grew 7.0% YoY in November, mildly slowing down from 7.1% in October. Loans for productive activities grew 5.7% YoY, decelerating from 5.9% in October. Meanwhile, loans to manufacturing sector fell at a slower rate of 0.1% YoY from -3.6% in Oct. Real estate loan growth accelerated to 11.9% YoY from 8.3% YoY the previous month. Consumer loans rose 23.6% YoY, faster than the 22.8% YoY growth in October driven by the faster increase in credit card loans, motor vehicle loans, and salary-based general purpose consumption loans. Regis forecasts loan growth of 12% across the period 2023-25E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew at a slower rate of 7.0% in November from 8.1% in October.

Losing bidders for South Africa’s Durban port demand basis for selection of ICTSI

Companies that lost out to ICTSI (ICT PM) for the right to operate DCT-2 Durban port in South Africa have demanded to know why it won the deal according to state-owned Transnet SOC Ltd. Transnet is in the process of responding to any requests for information. Transnet previously said that it shortlisted 10 bidders in Aug 2022 and there were six bidders at the final stage. Last July, Transnet selected ICT to be its equity partner to run and expand Durban Container Terminal Pier 2.

 

Under the agreement, ICTSI will pay an undisclosed amount to own 50%-1 share of a newly formed JV company while its partner, Transnet will own 50%+1 share. Assets at the terminal are currently worth R2.5bn (~US$130mn) and at the end of the contract, Transnet will buy the shares back from the partner. The transaction is expected to be completed by April 2024. Regis forecasts and valuation do not assume the acquisition of Durban; ICTSI also has not signed the concession agreement, nor has it paid for its equity stake. ICTSI trades at 7.8x FY24E EV/EBITDA, well below peers (11x) and historical average (11x).

 

GCash could IPO in 2H24

Oscar Reyes Jr., President and CEO of G-Xchange, the operator of GCash, said that the company is preparing internally to that it can be ready for an IPO by 2H24. Mynt, the parent of GCash, contributed P648mn (13%) to Globe’s 3Q23 net profit.

 

Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in December

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 18bps MoM (-61bps YoY) to 20.4%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index also increased 9bps MoM (-43bps YoY) to 24.6%. Net foreign buying stood at US$18mn, flat from November. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 18 saw declines in foreign ownership while 12 experienced increases.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

ICT – foreign ownership rose 61bps MoM to 33.5%. It also experienced the second highest net foreign buying for the month (at US$25.6mn)

ALI – foreign ownership increased 42bps MoM to 28.2%. Net foreign buying was US$37.9mn, the highest for the month.

CNPF – foreign ownership dropped 54bps MoM to 25.3%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$3.1mn

PGOLD – foreign ownership declined 32bps MoM to 18.0%. Net foreign selling hit US$4.4mn

URC – foreign ownership fell 25bps MoM to 24.4%. It also experienced the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$14.9mn)

PLDT eyeing new president and CEO within the year

PLDT Chairman, president, and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan said that the company recently began its search for the next president and CEO. Since the retirement of Mr. Panlilio due to health concerns, Mr. Pangilinan has assumed the role since January 1, 2024. Capex is expected to be lower in 2024 as the company will focus on positive free cashflow.

PLDT trades at 8.3x FY24E PER and 4.6x EV/EBITDA

 

Megawide real estate unit mulls IPO in 2025

Real estate firm PH1 World Developers Inc. is considering an initial public offering as early as 2025 according to Megawide (MWIDE PM) Chairman, President, and CEO Edgar Saavedra. He sees PH1’s earnings to be larger than MWIDE’s construction business in three years’ time

PH1 offers affordable and mid-income housing. Recall that MWIDE bought its 100% stake in PH1 last July 2023 for P5.2bn from its parent, Citicore Holdings.

AREIT acquires hotel from Ayala Land

AREIT purchased the 153-room Seda Lio in El Nido, Palawan from Ayala Land. This transaction was previously announced in 4Q23 and was acquired for P1.2bn in cash. The property will increase AREIT’s AUM by c. 1% to P88bn. Ayala Land will lease Seda Lio from AREIT for 25 years with a fixed rent with 2.5% rental escalation beginning January 2024. The contract will be reviewed every five years. AREIT currently offers 2024E dividend yield of 6.8% (based on consensus forecast). The 10-year government bond yield is 6.2%.

SEC approves Citicore Renewable Energy Corp's planned P12.94bn IPO

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has approved the planned initial public offering (IPO) of Citicore Renewable Energy Corporation's (CREC) primary and secondary shares for as much as P12.94bn. Citicore will offer to the public up to 2.9bn common shares at a maximum price of P3.88/sh. The offer will include additional 435mn outstanding common shares (secondary) for overallotment. The offer shares will account for 28.9% of Citicore’s outstanding capital and up to 33.2% if the overallotment option is exercised. The IPO will run from March 4 to 8.

 

The company targets to list its shares on the PSE main board on March 15. Net proceeds amounting to P10.76bn will be allotted for capital expenditures and development of solar energy plants in 2024 and 2025, as well as for capex and development of battery energy storage systems (BESS) next year. The company currently has ten operating solar assets with a combined gross installed capacity of 285MW. It has a development pipeline of 6,446MW in solar power generation and 812.5MW in onshore wind power.

PCC approves PLDT, Sky Cable deal?

Media reports say that the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) has reportedly approved PLDT’s 100% buyout of ABS-CBN’s Sky Cable. The P6.8bn deal has been under scrutiny over the last five months as the PCC assessed whether the merger of major players would result in a significant reduction of competition.

Civil Aeronautics Board lowers fuel surcharge

The airline regulator, Civil Aeronautics Board lowered the fuel surcharge level to Level 5 from the previous Level 6. New fuel surcharges will be implemented in February. Despite lower fuel surcharges, Regis believes overall fares will continue to rise this year, given favorable supply-demand dynamics. As of 3Q23, overall fares are already 21% above 2019 levels (above forecast: 13%).

BIR raises VAT-free ceiling on housing

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has raised the VAT-free ceiling on housing units to P3.6mn, from P3.2mn previously. Regis expects this to be mildly positive for Filinvest Land and Vista Land. This development should be neutral for other developers Regis covers.

Remittances rise by 2.8% YoY in November

Remittances in November grew by 2.8% YoY to US$2.72bn. Remittances were down 9.3% MoM due to seasonality and In PHP terms, remittances dipped by 0.3% YoY. Land-based remittances rose 2.9%, lifted by higher remittances from Saudi Arabia (5.6%) and UAE (9.3%); Sluggish growth from the USA (+1%) persisted. Sea-based remittances continued to gain pace, rising by 2.6% YoY in Nov, fastest growth rate since April 2023’s +2.7%. This brings YT-November remittances are up by 2.8% YoY to US$30.2bn. The BSP is forecasting remittances to rise by 3% in 2023/2024.

Finance chief Recto calls for study of carbon pricing instruments

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto to impose carbon tax and the study of carbon pricing instruments. He specifically identified the development of a carbon tax and emissions trading system (ETS) as crucial steps towards achieving a low-carbon economy. The DOF will lead technical group working on carbon pricing, with government agencies, multilateral banks, and development partners.

Department of Transportation to launch Swiss Challenge for AEV’s Bohol airport proposal in 1Q

The Department of Transportation is eyeing the conduct of a Swiss challenge of Aboitiz Infracapital’s P4.53bn unsolicited proposal to upgrade, operate, and maintain the Bohol international airport in 1Q23. Negotiations with the proponent are at the tail end already so the Swiss challenge could be launched this quarter. Aboitiz Infracapital is a wholly-owned unit of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM).

 

Earlier this month, the department also announced that it plans to launch the Swiss challenge for AEV’s P12.75bn unsolicited proposal for the Laguindingan airport. The Bohol airport project will add to the government’s PPP pipeline of “live bids”, which currently includes big-ticket projects such as P171bn NAIA airport PPP and a Swiss challenge for San Miguel’s (SMC PM) P27.5bn TPLEX extension project.

 

House panel endorses CREATE MORE

The House Ways and Means Committee endorsed to the members the CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy). The Bill seeks to amend Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act, reconciling “disparities between the CREATE Act and its implementing rules”. A committee report will be taken up at the Ways and Means panel next week before it is debated at the plenary.

 

Proposed amendments include:

*Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%

*Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports

*Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board

*Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises

*IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements
