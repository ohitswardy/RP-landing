REGIS REPORT

12 July 2024

The PSEi extended its uptrend this week as investors increasingly priced in an imminent 25bp rate reduction by the BSP in August. Yields on the ground reflected as much with 10-year rate falling by roughly 25bps WoW to 6.2%. Risk assets across the globe likewise mostly advanced in anticipation of the US CPI that was released late in the week. The print came in better than expected and further reinforced bets of a dovish turn in US monetary policy. The local benchmark gained through most of the week and broke past 6600 for the first time since late May on Thursday. It ultimately settled the week at 6648 +2.4% WoW, closing above the technical 100-day moving average for the first time since end-April. 

Breadth of the market was positive with ~3/4 of index members in the green. Top gainer this week was JGS +7.78% WoW, which finally rebounded from YTD lows after hitting oversold levels in June. The stock also appears to be benefitting from the rally in its subsidiary URC which gained +6.06% WoW to its best levels in almost 5 months. We continued to see evident rotation into the broader consumer space with CNPF +7.08% and PGOLD +2.22% posting substantial gains as well, as investors position for a low-CPI environment and hence stronger consumer demand this 2H. Banks were a bright spot as well with BDO +3.53%, BPI +3.11% and MBT +4.46% all rising this week after their sharp selloff in June. Our sense is that we are likely to see continued strong results in the sector in the upcoming 2Q reporting season.

On the opposite end, JFC -2.82% WoW trailed behind its consumer peers, which potentially signals a mixed reception by investors to the company’s latest acquisition of South Korean coffee chain Compose Coffee. While the deal is immediately accretive on paper, some investors have voiced concern over the company’s management spreading itself too thin by making yet again another acquisition. Other notable laggards this week, were ACEN -4.94% and BLOOM -4.12%.

Another source of optimism this week is that along with the general rise in share prices, market activity on the ground also improved to a daily average of $101.9m, +50% WoW compared to last week’s sub-$70m pace. Furthermore, we continued to see pockets of net foreign buying, particularly into BDO +3.53% WoW, TEL +6.46%, SM +3.53%, URC +6.06%, SMPH +2.27%. Top sold names meanwhile were ALI +1.2% and JFC -2.82%. Net foreign buying for the week stood at a modest $4m, but note that this marked the third consecutive week of inflows.

RESEARCH
Jollibee Foods Corp. - More on Compose Coffee acquisition
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

We hosted JFC’s CFO, Mr Richard Shin, to provide more detail on the company’s announced acquisition of South Korean coffee chain Compose Coffee. Here are our key takeaways:

Margin and value accretive

JFC will acquire its effective ~75% stake (70% direct, 5% through 90%-held Titan Dining II) for approximately P15bn, which will be financed through a combination of debt (47%) and cash (53%). Compose Coffee is expected to add 12% to JFC’s 2024 EBIT on an annualized basis, which suggests it is immediately earnings accretive (+6-7%). At ~7x forward EV/EBITDA, it is also value accretive to JFC (9.5x).

Strategic fit, capital-light business model

Compose Coffee appears to be a good strategic fit under one of JFC’s four pillars – Coffee & Tea. While JFC has multiple brands in its coffee & tea portfolio (Coffee Bean and Tea Leaf, Highlands Coffee, Milksha, Common Man Coffee Roasters), Compose Coffee targets a different price point (value segment) and has a slightly different business model (franchise model) from its other brands.

Compose Coffee has over 2,600 stores as of Jun 2024 and ranks 2nd in the Korean value coffee segment in terms of number of franchisees. It operates a 100% franchise model, making it asset-light. Stores are around 33sqm on average and generate a payback of one year (to the franchisee). At the core of the business is its proprietary in-house coffee roasting plant in Korea, allowing it to be the lowest-cost producer relative to the competition.

Growth amidst a highly competitive market

South Korea is the world’s 3rd largest consumer of coffee per capita, but consumer preferences are constantly evolving. The Korean coffee market has been growing 5-10% CAGR over the past 10 years, but the value segment (now ~28% of the market) has been growing in the mid-teens. Major players in the value coffee segment include Compose Coffee, Mega Coffee, and Paik’s Coffee. Compose Coffee is estimated to have one-third market share in the value segment (~8% of overall coffee industry).

JFC’s near-term priority is to continue expanding Compose Coffee stores, having identified 1,000 potential new locations. Apart from store expansion over the next few years, management plans to ramp up SSSG by extending product offerings and optimizing Compose Coffee’s menu while still maintaining value price points. JFC currently has no plans to expand stores outside South Korea, given that the brand is still relatively young (founded in 2014) and unknown outside of its home market.

ICTSI (ICT PM) Update - Port in Mexico rebounds to new high in June

After a brief interruption in May to make way for the arrival of new cranes, volumes at ICTSI’s port in Mexico, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), rebounded by 14% YoY and 23% MoM to a new monthly record of 128k TEUs in June.  CMSA’s market share surged to 39% for the month of June, higher than the typical 34%-ish level for the port in the past five years.

While CMSA’s 2Q24 volume growth sharply decelerated to 7% YoY from the 37% pace in 1Q24, we note that this was only temporary and was largely due to a drop in lower-yielding transshipment volumes.  We reckon volume growth should re-accelerate once the company fully completes its expansion in August. CMSA’s volumes in 1H24 grew 20% YoY to 698k TEUs, on track to reach or even exceed our full-year volume forecast of 1.43mn TEUs (+12% YoY).  The latest data suggests that monthly volumes for the rest of the year should only average 122k TEUs to reach our forecast.  We estimate CMSA to be ICT’s second largest port, accounting for 11% of ICT’s consolidated volumes and 18% of consolidated revenues.

We believe the cycle of earnings upgrades is far from over and current valuations of 9x/8x FY24E/FY25E EV/EBITDA and 15x/12x FY24E/FY25E PER remain attractive relative to history and peers. We reiterate our BUY call.

Meralco sees generation charge hike for July

Meralco is expecting an increase in the generation charge this month as it begins to collect the previously deferred estimated payment of P0.77 per kilowatt-hour (kWh). This follows a reduction in June due to an Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) directive, with charges from the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) expected to normalize. In June, Meralco reduced its rate to P9.4516 per kWh from P11.4139 per kWh. The generation charged decreased by P1.8308 per kWh despite the previous announcement of an increase by P0.3466 per kWh. Furthermore, the collection of P500mn in generation costs were deferred to mitigate the impact of higher pass-through charges.

This amount will be recovered over the billing cycle from July-September. There will also be an impact from the amortization of deferred charges, estimated at P0.77 per kWh. Note that WESM charges were affected by red alerts on the Luzon grid early in the June supply, but it is expected that this impact will be mitigated by the reduced in demand during the rainy season. In June, the average WESM price system-wide declined by 25.2% to P6.15 per kWh. Note that electricity has a weight of 4.6% in the CPI basket.

SM Investments Corp. (SM PM) has secured the approval to increase the number of board seats to 9 from 8. Last April, SM’s stockholders voted to elect Lily Gruba as an independent director which will be effective on the approval date of the SEC. She has also been the director of the Philippine Economic Zone Authority, director of Overseas Workers Welfare Administration and undersecretary of the Department of Finance. First Gen defers LNG purchase; EDC completes capacity expansion in Albay

First Gen (FGEN) has postponed the delivery of its fifth LNG cargo due to sufficient supply and uncertainty over the ERC’s decision to allow full cost recovery over its LNG cost.

Meanwhile, its renewable energy subsidiary, Energy Development Corp. turned on its 28.9 MW geothermal plant in Manito, Albay, which increased the capacity of its existing 140 MW Bacon-Manito (BacMan) facility. The P7bn project was synchronized to the grid in January and is one of the four geothermal projects to be operational this year. EDC’s expansion projects totaled P28bn and includes other projects such as the 28MW Mahanagdong Binary in Leyte, 20MW Tanawon Binary in BacMan, and 5.6MW Binary in Negros Occidental.

DALI sells KULINA banana ketchup, Raja Puro vinegar, and soy sauce - these products are packaged similarly to Datu Puti and UFC products which are manufactured and popularized by Nutri-Asia.

PLDT completes biggest data center

VITRO Inc. (PLDT’s data center arm) said it has completed the construction of the US$350mn VITRO Sta. Rosa in Laguna. The project covers about 5 hectares of land and will more than double PLDT’s data center capacity by adding 36MW to the network (28mw existing). VITRO president and CEO said the facility is certified Rated 3 and looking to be Rated 4 in terms of energy efficiency. PLDT previously said it is looking to divest a 49% share in its data centers, valued at more than USD 1bn, to a foreign company. Proceeds from the sale will be used to settle expiring debts and improve its financial health.

Philex eyes fundraising for second phase of Silangan project

Philex Mining Corp. (PX PM) may raise another US$400mn to finance the second phase of its forthcoming Silangan Mine project in Mindanao. The fundraising would “most likely” be a mix of debt and equity with a ratio of 70:30 in favor of debt. Company is considering a follow-on offer in late 2025 or in 2026 to fund phase 2.

The company said that once the Bayugo ore body is mined it could increase annual production to 6.5mn tons.

Digital payments up 33% in May, to a new record

Payments over the National Retail Payments System digital channels climbed 33% YoY in May to P1,433bn, a new record high (up 1% from April). On the surface, the pace of growth seems to have moderated from the +51% in April; recall though that the Easter holidays distorted the March/April data. For the 5-month total, digital payments were up 37% YoY to P6,658bn.

Growth was again driven by InstaPay – smaller tickets up to P50,000 – which saw payments up 46.1% YoY to P605bn in May. This took the year-to-date figure to P2,706bn, up 45.4% YoY. As before, the volume of transactions continues to explode, up 72.5% YoY, further evidence of broader acceptance of digital payments at the retail / personal level.

Payments over PesoNet (large, batched transactions) were up 25% YoY in May to P829bn, bringing the 5-month total to P3,952bn, up 31% YoY.

We reiterate that the figures only capture transactions between platforms (bank-to-bank, bank-to-wallet, wallet-to-wallet, etc), and do not reflect digital payments made within one platform (eg BDO-to-BDO, GCash-to-GCash). Digital adoption is even more pervasive than the data suggests.

Globe (GLO PM) is our preferred play here, given the dominance of its affiliate GCash/Mynt in the e-wallet space.

Port regulator sees cargo volume rebound in 2H; eyes 10-13% growth for 2024

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) sees cargo volumes rising by 10-13% YoY this year. While January to May 2024 cargo volume growth was flat, PPA expects a rebound by the 2H due to the holiday season. Foreign cargo volumes are currently experiencing delays due to congestion at regional transshipment hubs arising from the conflict at the Red Sea. Recall that container volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) was flat in 1Q24 (664k TEUs). We forecast MICT’s volumes will grow by 5% YoY in 2024 to 2.9mn TEUs and we estimate MICT accounts for 23% of ICT’s consolidated volumes.

Government revenue collections up 15% YoY to P2.13tn in 1H24

Based on preliminary data, Finance Secretary announced that the government collected P2.13tn in revenues in 1H24. This would be 15% higher than the P1.86tn in revenue collections in 1H23 and is equivalent to 49% of its FY24 revenue target of P4.27tn (+11.7% YoY). This implies that overall collections for the month of June alone rose 4% YoY to P277bn. Tax revenues improved by 8.8% to P1.81tn, with the BIR collecting P1.34tn (+10% YoY) and the BOC collecting P454bn (+4% YoY). Officials note that shortfalls in tax revenues that were made up for by non-tax revenues, which rose by 65% YoY to P317bn. This implies that tax revenues for June 2024 alone was 6% lower YoY at P224bn, with BIR collections falling 9% YoY to P151bn and BOC collections dipping 1% YoY to P73bn.

GIR settles at US$104.7bn in end-June

Preliminary data showed the country’s GIR settled at US$104.7bn in end-June, down by 0.3% MoM (-US$315mn), which represents 7.7 months’ worth of imports. The MoM decline is attributed to national government’s foreign currency payments and lower gold prices.

Government considering tapping Samurai, dollar bonds this year

The government is looking to issue Japanese yen-denominated and US-dollar denominated bonds this year. Recall that the government last issued Samurai bonds in April 2022. The timing of bond issuance will also depend on the US Federal Reserve’s easing path.

For 2024, the government plans to borrow US$5bn, of which US$2bn was raised from the issuance of global bonds in May. As of end-May, the government’s total outstanding debt reached P15.35tn. For 2024, the government aims for a debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.6%, slightly higher than 2023’s 60.2%. After 2024, the government aims to gradually lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 56% by 2028.

Bulacan province bans POGO firms

The Bulacan Provincial Board passed an ordinance banning POGO firms in the province. Note that in Metro Manila, Pasig City and Valenzuela City have passed similar ordinances. However, there appears to be limited POGO operations in Bulacan and POGO firms are predominantly in the Manila Bay area (Pasay City and Paranaque City).

PAGCOR to ban POGO hubs

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that PAGCOR will come out with new guidelines that prohibit POGO hubs. Instead, POGO operations will be “site specific” and “building specific.” It was shown that POGO hubs in Tarlac and Pampanga provinces were involved in illegal activities. Furthermore, PAGCOR will form teams that monitor POGO firms 24/7. The banning of POGO hubs could cause POGO firms to lease space in office buildings. Regis estimates that offshore gaming firms, now called Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL), occupy about 900,000sqm of Metro Manila office space (about 6% of Metro Manila office stock).

Lower energy prices drive June inflation lower to 3.7%

Headline inflation eased to 3.7% in June from 3.9% in May, lower than Street estimates of 3.9% and at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.4%-4.2%. On a MoM basis, CPI was flat, moderating slightly after rising +0.1% in May. Electricity CPI down by 13% YoY, 6.3% MoM due to implementation of staggered payment scheme to temper impact of higher generation costs in May. While operation of transport equipment eased to 3.5% YoY from 5.2% YoY on lower gasoline prices. Food inflation however rose to 6.1% from 5.8% due to faster increases in vegetable and meat prices, while rice inflation remained elevated at 22.5% YoY (+0.1% MoM).

Core inflation was steady at 3.1%, with non-food “core” CPI also steady at 3.3%. This brings 1H24 average inflation rate to 3.6%. Note that the BSP’s baseline inflation forecast for 2024 at 3.3%. BSP Governor Remolona said that even if inflation for the upcoming month of July breaches its target band of 2-4%, it won’t derail the monetary board’s planned rate cut on Aug 15. Governor Remolona previously said that the BSP could cut rates by 50bps this year, 25bps in August and another 25bps in 4Q. The next monetary board meeting on August 15.

Trade deficit widens by 5% YoY to US$4.6bn in May

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit widened to US$4.60bn in May, up 5% YoY. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit was slightly lower than the US$4.73bn deficit in April. Imports were flat at US$10.93bn - despite a 21% YoY increase in fuel-related imports, imports of consumer goods and raw/intermediate goods were flattish (0%/1%) while capital goods imports fell 12%. Exports declined by 3% YoY to US$6.3bn due to lower electronic exports (-5%). This brings 5M24 trade deficit to US$20.59bn, down 13% YoY.

FDI drops 37% YoY to US$556mn in April

Foreign direct investments (FDI) net inflows amounted to US$556mn in April, down 37% YoY. Equity capital placements in April came largely from Japan (47% of total), USA (21%), Malaysia (11%), and Singapore (9%). These were channeled primarily to manufacturing (36% of total), real estate (26%), trade (13%), and financial sectors (10%).

This brings YT-April FDI to US$3.5bn, up 18.7% YoY. Top sources of equity capital placement were Netherlands (63%), Japan (22%), and USA (6%). These were channeled to financial/insurance (67%), manufacturing (18%), and real estate (7%). The BSP expects FDI inflows to reach US$9.5bn in 2024, up from US$8.9bn in 2023.

Ex-BDO exec appointed to Monetary Board

President Marcos Jr. appointed banker Walter C. Wassmer to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas’ (BSP) Monetary Board. The announcement comes weeks after the resignation of 2 Monetary Board members. Wassmer served as a consultant and non-executive director of BDO Unibank Inc. from 2022 to the present. He was also the Senior EVP and Institutional Banking Group Head of BDO from 1997 until 2022. He served various positions in Far East Bank and Trust Co. and Union Bank, among others, throughout his career. With Wassmer’s appointment, one seat remains vacant on the Monetary Board.

LPC: Office vacancy drops slightly in 2Q24; high-end residential prices rise

During its 2Q24 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy fell 1ppt QoQ to 16%. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels.

Nationwide net office take-up rose 47% QoQ (32% YoY) to 270,000sqm. BPO net office take-up increased 58% QoQ (+13% YoY) to 104,000sqm. Net office take-up from traditional tenants (largely government agencies) climbed 74% QoQ (+284% YoY) to 146,000sqm. Meanwhile, POGO net office take-up fell 41% QoQ (down 73% YoY) to 20,000sqm.

LPC also said that secondary market prices of high-end Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio condominium prices generally rose 5-10% YoY. It estimates that sales of Metro Manila condominiums in the primary market rose 7% QoQ (-16% YoY) to 7,377 units.

SEC requires public firms to post meeting minutes

The SEC has mandated all publicly listed companies to upload the minutes of their regular and special stockholders’ on their respective company’s websites within 5 business days from the date of the meeting. Both the draft and approved minutes must remain on the company’s website for 5 years. This mandate would take effect on July 15, 2024. Companies which have conducted their 2024 annual stockholders meeting prior to July 15 have 5 business days to upload the minutes of the meeting in their websites.
