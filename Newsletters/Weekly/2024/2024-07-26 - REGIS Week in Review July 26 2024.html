REGIS REPORT

26 July 2024

The PSEi posted its first weekly drop in 5 weeks, ending -0.97% WoW at 6726, as local shares tracked the widespread weakness across the broader region and followed suit of the AI-driven correction in the US. On the ground, investors digested BBM’s surprise announcement of a ban on POGO/IGLs in the country, who will be given until the end of the year to cease operations. Chart-wise, the benchmark also appears to have hit a snag at the crucial 6800 resistance level which it again tested unsuccessfully on Monday, before it fluctuated around 6700 through the rest of the week. The benchmark however ultimately capped the week off lows as it managed to rise by over +0.8% in Friday’s session, while the rest of the region likewise managed to find some signs of support after a mostly downbeat week.

On the ground, sentiment, particularly on the property sector, was weighed down by the President’s move to ban POGOs. Regis estimates that the sector occupies 6% of the Metro Manila office space and a disproportionate 30% of the Manila Bay area office space. As such, the ban may lead to office vacancy rate in Metro Manila to rise to 22% from 16% currently. Taking into account this this negative impact on the office leasing business, Carl estimates that the bottomline hit to developers under our coverage will be within 5%. That said, he notes that SMPH may take a meaningful hit in its NAV as well if property prices in the Manila Bay area were to fall significantly (see more in his note below.) With that, the property sector turned broadly lower this week with SMPH -1.65% WoW, ALI -1.1%, and non-PSEi RLC -2.61%, MEG -3.73% and DD -6.88%, which has a particularly significant exposure in the Manila Bay area. Losses spilled over to the REITs as well which corrected from their uptrend in the prior weeks with DDMPR -8.62%, MREIT -2.11%, RCR -1.93% and AREIT -1.48%.

Another sector that we deem may take a hit from the POGO ban are the casinos. Carl notes that about 20% of BLOOM’s GGR comes from Chinese citizens living in the Philippines, bulk of whom are likely employed in the POGO sector. That said, BLOOM shares managed to find signs of support in the latter half of the week after hitting new 52-week lows and falling by over 20% over the past 2 months. It capped the week with a slight +0.23% WoW gain.

Breadth of the market was skewed to the negative with over 2/3 of index members ending the in the red. Other notable laggards in the main index were NIKL -6.63% WoW, ICT -4%, MONDE -3.03%, URC -2.66% and AEV -2.33%. On the opposite end, a handful of the few gainers this week were mostly utilities and conglos: ACEN +1.83%, GLO +1.63%, CNPF +1.19%, DMC +1.09% and SM +0.93%. Market activity was moderate at a daily average of $87m, nearly same pace as last week, as the heavy volumes at the earlier part of the week were offset by the slump in activity in the latter half. Net foreign activity remained skewed to buy for the 5th week in a row by $23m, bulk of which was in the earlier part of the week. Their top bought names were SM +0.93% WoW, BPI -0.63%, BDO -1.88%, URC -2.66%, MBT -0.64%, MER –0.26% and JGS +0.54%, while top sold ICT -4%, BLOOM +0.23%, ACEN +1.83% and AEV -2.33%.

Up ahead, earnings season to come in full swing next week with scheduled briefings by BDO, MER, RRHI, WLCON, SCC and URC.

Philippine Property - President Marcos bans POGO sector
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Negative for offices and Manila Bay properties

In his State of the Nation Address, President Marcos said that he would ban the Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) sector immediately. He instructed the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, to wind down operations of POGO firms by the end of the year. We expect this ban to be detrimental to the Metro Manila office market and to all forms of property in the Manila Bay area.

Why is the POGO sector being banned?

While some members of the government continue to support the sector, criminal activities associated with the POGO sector (such as human trafficking, kidnapping, etc.) have increasingly been in the spotlight recently. This is likely what led President Marcos to ban offshore gaming.

Most negative for Manila Bay area

The POGO sector occupies a significant amount of office space, particularly in the Manila Bay area. We estimate that the sector occupies 6% (900,000sqm) of Metro Manila office stock and 30% of Manila Bay area office space. Thus, a ban on the sector could result in Metro Manila office vacancy rising to 22% (16% currently) and Manila Bay area office vacancy climbing to about 50%. In such a scenario, it would be reasonable for office rental rates to fall c. 10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area.

The POGO sector also employs over 100,000 foreign nationals (based on our estimates), a large portion of whom reside in the Manila Bay area. Thus, their departure could lead to significant drops in Manila Bay area property prices.

What is the impact to the listed developers?

Based on our initial estimates, the annual profit hit to the developers we cover should be manageable at less than 5% due to their relatively diversified operations. The office segment comprises less than 40% of EBIT and POGO firms occupy less than 5% of the office space of the developers we cover (see Figure 1).

Counterintuitively, SM Prime will likely take a larger cut to its NAV than its office exposure (7% of EBIT) might suggest. If Manila Bay area property prices fall 20%, we estimate that SMPH’s NAV will drop by 5%-10%, with the drop largely coming from the Pasay Coastal Development project, which accounts for 27% of NAV.

In addition to the property companies, casinos in PAGCOR Entertainment City (which is in the Manila Bay area) will likely be negatively affected by a POGO ban. About 20% of the GGR of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) comes from Chinese citizens living in the Philippines, many of whom are likely POGO employees. This proportion, however, was already likely to drop with the recent opening of Solaire Resort North.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Deal or no deal
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Mining contract renewal priced in

Despite lingering uncertainties on the renewal of its coal operating contract beyond July 2027, SCC’s shares have outperformed the PCOMP by 38% over the past year on a total return basis. We believe the current share price more closely reflects the optimistic outcome of securing a mining contract renewal (P37/sh) than the downside risk of a non[1]renewal. On our upgraded coal price assumptions, we believe SCC can pay dividends totaling P6.50/sh over the next 16 months, a welcome compensation whilst waiting for the Department of Energy’s final decision.

Dividend play

We raise our FY24E/FY25E EPS by 19%/15% to incorporate 28%/33% higher coal mining ASP forecasts of US$53/49 per MT. On our revised estimates, we estimate SCC can pay another dividend of P2/sh in 2H24 and P4.50/sh in FY25, equivalent to yields of 5% and 13%, respectively.

Binary outcome

The Department of Energy is still reviewing SCC’s application for a renewal of its coal operating contract covering the neighboring islands of Semirara, Caluya, and Sibay in the Visayas region. Under a worst-case scenario of non-renewal, SCC will need to (a) cease its coal mining operations by July 2027 and (b) import more expensive coal to run its power plants. In that situation, SCC could be worth only P22/sh. On the other hand, our DCF valuation would rise to P37/sh should a renewal until 2040 be secured. Both scenarios assume a long-term coal ASP of US$40/MT, 20% lower than current ICI4 spot of US$52/MT.

SCC trades at par with coal mining peers but offers superior yield

We value SCC’s coal mining and power units separately using DCF analysis, based on contract life (2027) and plant lives (2035/2040), respectively. At current levels, SCC trades at 7.9x FY25E PER and 4.5x EV/EBITDA, broadly in line with global coal mining stocks. However, SCC offers superior dividend yields than its peers. Coal prices are one of the key risks to our forecasts, as every 10% increase/(decrease) in our coal ASP assumptions could raise/(lower) our FY25E EBITDA/profit forecasts by 9%/10%.

Converge to partner with Sky Cable

A partnership between Converge and Sky Cable was announced on July 22. This entails a “commercial arrangement” that will enable Sky to upgrade its network and improve its services. The terms and nature of the “agreement” are vague, but we understand that Sky will pay Converge for the use of some of its assets. In theory, use of the fiber assets of Converge will allow Sky to become more competitive, without necessitating the rollout of its own network. From the perspective of Converge, the deal should help generate incremental revenue from under-utilized assets.

Note that Sky Cable’s net loss stands at P653mn as of 2023 and it currently owes P4.53bn to its lenders. Sky Cable has also lost its right to offer direct-to-home services when ABS-CBN failed to land a fresh franchise in 2020. Prior to this, PLDT Inc. was supposed to buy out Sky Cable for P6.75bn, but the deal was called off in February. An earlier report by InsiderPH said the agreement would allow Sky Cable to ride on Converge’s fiber network, converting it from cable TV to digital TV. The report also included that CNVRG will extend P2bn to Sky Cable to repay some of its liabilities.

Cebu Air reportedly in talks to acquire AirSWIFT

As of end-2023, AirSwift flew 355k passengers, or c. 1% of industry-wide domestic pax volumes.

Note that Insiderph.com also previously reported that Tiger is looking to raise US$500-US$750mn through an IPO in 2025. For context, Okada Manila reported gross gaming revenue of P45bn in 2023 vs Solaire Entertainment City P58bn, Newport Resorts P34bn and City of Dreams Manila P30bn.

Highlights from Pres. Marcos' 3rd SONA

President Marcos delivered his State of the Nation Address (SONA) on Monday in front of the government’s assembled officialdom, which included the Cabinet and members of both Houses of Congress. The SONA, the Philippine equivalent of the State of the Union Address of the US, is part legislative / policy agenda (akin to the King’s Speech in the UK) but still 100% political theater.

Among the key priorities Marcos outlined were (1) ongoing agricultural programs to reduce food prices, (2) proposed changes to the power sector law (“EPIRA”), (3) healthcare programs and (4) “continuous’ implementation of infrastructure projects.  The proposed changes to EPIRA could, in our view, pose some risk to some of the power companies, including Meralco (MER PM).

He reserved the latter (last) part for two topics which elicited by far, the biggest reactions, most likely be design. It’s notable that this administration’s policies on these particular issues are directly contradictory to those promoted by the previous government:

•           He reiterated his administration’s stance on the South China Sea (West Philippine Sea), expressing the aim of pursuing a peaceful solution to the competing claims without giving up the country’s rights. The previous government pursued a policy that can arguably be described as appeasement. Frankly, it looks like the new government policy aims will be difficult if not impossible under the current environment.

•           Marcos announced he was banning POGOs, the offshore gaming firms that have mushroomed in the country, and whose operations have dominated recent headlines because of the criminal activities associated with them. The previous government conferred official recognition on POGOs in exchange for a share of revenue of their gaming activities. Marcos’ policy reversal would have significant repercussions on the presence of foreign nationals (mostly from China) and demand for property, particularly in certain locations.

President Marcos to ban offshore gaming

During his State of the Nation Address, President Marcos said he would ban offshore gaming. Existing POGO firms have until the end of the year to cease operations. We reckon that a ban on offshore gaming would be detrimental to the Metro Manila office market and to all forms of property in the Manila Bay area. We estimate that POGO firms occupy c. 900,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila (about 6% of office stock) and that POGO firms employ over 100,000 foreign nationals who will soon vacate the Metro Manila business districts. However, POGO firms are concentrated in the Manila Bay area and we estimate they occupy over 30% of office stock in that vicinity. Thus, a ban on offshore gaming could raise Metro Manila office vacancy to 22% (from 16% currently) and Manila Bay area office vacancy to c. 50% (from c. 20% currently). We estimate that office rental rates could fall c. 10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area. Prices of other Manila Bay area properties (such as land) are likely to fall 10%-20%. Based on our initial estimates, the annual profit hit for the developers we cover should be within 5% over the next few years. While POGO firms should vacate their offices quickly, negative rental reversions from contract renewals of other tenants will be staggered. Megaworld would likely take the biggest hit to profit from a drop in office earnings. Unintuitively, SM Prime might take a larger hit to its NAV than its office exposure might suggest - the Manila Bay reclamation comprises 27% of our SMPH NAV estimate; thus, a 20% drop in Manila Bay land prices would result in a 5%-10% reduction in SM Prime’s NAV.

PAGCOR clarifies POGO ban covers IGLs

PAGCOR chair Al Tengco says President Marcos’ ban on online gambling firms called “POGOs” covers internet gaming licensees (IGL). Clarification was being made due to supposed ambiguity of Marcos’ directive during the SONA. The online gaming firms targeting offshore gamblers are now called IGLs. The clarification should remove doubt that all licensed POGOs (IGLs) will be wound down within the year.

20,000 foreign POGO workers told to leave Philippines in 60 days

The Bureau of Immigration Commissioner, Norman Tansingco, said that the Bureau ordered 20,000 foreign POGO employees to leave the Philippines within 60 days. Most foreign POGO workers likely reside in the Manila Bay area.

Digital payments - growth moderates to 26% in June due to sharp PesoNet slowdown

Digital transactions done over the National Retail Payments System grew 26% YoY to P1,317bn. This was slower than the 33% YoY growth in May, and the 37% average over the previous 5 months. The June figure was also down 8% MoM; while June is typically a slow month, the drop was larger than usual. Still, the 1H2024 figure of P7,976bn was up a respectable 35% YoY.

The June deceleration was driven nearly entirely by PesoNet (large, batched transactions),  where transactions only grew by 14% YoY to P738bn, sharply slower than the 25% in May. On a MoM basis, PesoNet payments were down 11% compared to May. This takes the 1H24 total to P4,690, up 28% YoY.

To the upside, payments over InstaPay (up to P50,000 per transaction) continued to surge, up 45% YoY to P579bn (was +46% in May). The June print was down 4% MoM, in line with previous years’ seasonality. For the first half of the year, InstaPay clocked P3,286bn, up 45% YoY.

We view the continued adoption of digital payments, particularly at the smaller-ticket level, as a positive long theme.

Typhoon Cariña damages schools, agriculture, and infrastructure: latest tally at almost P500mn

The latest data from various government agencies showed that at least P500mn worth of agriculture and infrastructure were damaged and over 880k people were affected by typhoon Cariña. The Department of Education (DepEd) reported an estimated damage of P308.5mn in school infrastructure. This amount is equivalent to the funds needed for the reconstruction and rehabilitation of 45 damaged schools. Meanwhile, the Department of Agriculture estimates the agri sector has incurred damage and losses of more than P156mn. Infrastructure damage was reported at P700k, mostly in Luzon and Visayas regions.

Budget deficit narrows in June; 1H24 deficit widens by 11% YoY to P614bn

The national government recorded a lower budget deficit of P209bn in June, down 7% YoY. Revenues grew 11% YoY due to 74% YoY increase in non-tax revenues while expenditures rose by 3% YoY. Tax revenues grew 3.5% YoY, with BIR collections up 4.7% YoY and Customs collections up 0.7% YoY. The surge in non-tax revenues were due to undisclosed items unrelated to GOCCs dividends (-31% YoY) and privatization proceeds. Primary spending (excluding interest payments) grew 2.3% YoY. In 2Q24 alone, the budget deficit grew 22% YoY due to 18% YoY jump in spending (primary spending up 16% YoY). Prelude to 2Q24 GDP; favorable base effects have likely boosted GDP for the quarter. Brings 1H24 budget deficit up 11% YoY to P614bn; Primary deficit in 1H down 12% YoY.

BOP posts US$155mn deficit in June; End-June GIR rises to US$105bn

The country’s balance overall balance of payments position posted a deficit of US$155mn in June. This was a reversal of the US$1.997bn BOP surplus in May 2024 but lower than the US$606mn BOP deficit in June 2023. Officials cited government debt payments of foreign currency loans. This brings 1H24 BOP surplus to US$1.4bn, lower than the US$2.3bn surplus in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final end-June GIR level rose to US$105.2bn in end-June from the US$105bn in end-May, which represents 7.7 months’ worth of imports.

Lawmakers back bill on natural gas development

Lawmakers have vowed to support the Philippine Downstream Natural Gas Industry Development Act. Sen. Pia Cayetano stressed that the government has tagged natural gas, including those coming from the Malampaya field off Palawan province, as a transition fuel to full renewable energy. She said that the government must have policies that will support more exploration of natural gas. The Malampaya deepwater gas-to-power project ran by Filipinos is the country’s first and only indigenous gas resource supplying 20% of Luzon’s electricity needs. The project has been significantly contributing to the nation’s energy independence since 2001. Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian backed Prime Energy’s (Malampaya’s operator) commitment to further explore Philippine natural gas resources.

27 airports being considered for PPP

The Department of Transportation (DoTr) said 27 other airports are undergoing public-private partnerships (PPP) screening by the Asian Development Bank (ADB) and International Finance Corp. It is looking to revive previous plans to privatize the Davao airport (4th largest airport in the country). It is also seeking PPP-led upgrades for Iloilo, Bohol, Puerto Princesa, and Siargao. The government has so far bid out the country’s largest gateway, NAIA. It is currently in the process of bidding out, under a Swiss challenge, the Laguindingan airport and Bohol-Panglao Airport. Aboitiz InfraCapital, Inc., a wholly-owned unit of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM), is the original proponent for the two projects and has the right to match the best bids. Negotiations are also ongoing with Prime Asset Ventures, Inc. which secured OPS for the upgrade, operation and maintenance of Iloilo International Airport.

Government to implement pre-border inspection of imports

The government plans to implement the pre-border technical verification and cross-border electronic invoicing of all imported commodities to expedite customs processes. A technical working group will review and finalize the draft joint administrative order. The cross-border electronic invoicing system will be on a single electronic platform that will be shared by different government agencies. The government will implement the order in three phases: First phase will cover agricultural goods; Second phase will cover non-agri goods with health and safety issues; and Third phase covers other goods with misdeclaration to avoid duties and taxes. The system is targeted to become effective within two years. This measure could potentially be incrementally negative for port operators, such as ICTSI (ICT PM) due to lower storage revenues.

DTI certifies P3tn projects for green lane processing

1090 projects with investments amounting to P2.73tn have been approved by the Board of Investments (BOI) and Philippines Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) from July 2022 until May 2024. Most projects approved by the BOI are in the renewable energy (RE) sector while most projects approved by PEZA involve manufacturing. The approved investments by the BOI and PEZA are expected to generate a total of 182,000 jobs. There are 210 projects with a total cost of USD60.9bn in various stages as a result of the presidential visits, excluding public-private partnerships. 20 projects worth USD1.26bn are in Category 6 or already started operations. 23 projects worth USD1.6bn are in Category 5 or have registered with the investment promotion agency (IPA). 30 projects worth USD16bn are in Category 4 or those in the process of registering with the IPA.

Marcos OKs new laws to enhance procurement, fight online scams

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed into law the New Government Procurement Act (NGPA) and the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA). The NGPA law updates the Government Procurement Reform Act (RA No. 1984), streamlining the procurement process from 3 months to just 60 days by standardizing procurement forms and institutionalizing electronic procurement. The NGPA features 11 new procurement modalities "that will afford greater flexibility for government agencies in choosing the manner of acquiring the best goods and services.” The NGPA also ensures best prices for Filipinos through the “Most Economically Advantageous Responsive Bid.”

The AFASA aims to combat the rising tide of online financial scams. The law mandates financial institutions to implement stronger safeguards to protect Filipino financial accounts. The AFASA also criminalizes money muling and social engineering schemes, and grants the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) authority to probe financial accounts involved in illegal activities.

Data from the Philippine National Police recorded 19,000 cybercrimes in 2023 (68.98% increase YoY). Majority of these cases were online scams, totaling 14,030 incidents (an increase of 7,208 cases or 94.64% YoY).
