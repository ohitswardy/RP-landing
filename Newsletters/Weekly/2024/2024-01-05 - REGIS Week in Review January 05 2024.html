REGIS REPORT

05 January 2024

The market rallied 2.8% to 6,629.64 during the first trading week of the year, outperforming regional markets on the back of strong foreign demand.

Headline inflows stood at US$24.7m.

Sentiment improved as BSP Governor Eli Remolona seems to have slightly softened his stance on monetary policy, saying the BSP was in a “neutral territory at this point.” This is a slight departure from his statements as recently as 21 December when he said the BSP was still “hawkish”.

Inflation figures for the month of December likewise decelerated to 3.9%, from the 4.1% pace in November. The turnout was lower compared to consensus estimates of 4.0% and at the lower end of the BSP’s estimated range of 3.6-4.4%. The drop was due largely to an easing in food inflation (38% of the basket), which slowed to 5.4% YoY from 5.7% in November. This is the third straight month of slowing inflation, from the 6.1% recorded in September, when food inflation spiked to 9.7% driven by surging rice prices.

The rally was broad-based as 28 out of the 30 index names ended in the green.

Conglomerates led the market as SM (+2.8% WoW), AC (+4.5% WoW), JGS (+12.5% WoW), and AEV (+11.9% WoW) all outperformed the index.

Banks followed suit. BPI, BDO, and MBT all gained 0.7%, 2.0% WoW, 6.1% respectively. To start the year, the BPI and Robinsons bank (RBC) merger became effective last 01 Jan after it secured regulatory approvals in late December. To recall, BPI issued 314mn new shares – c6% of the merged entity – to the JG Summit (JGS PM) Group, which will end up with a 10% stake in the bank.

Consumer names saw a modest boost following the better-than-expected inflation print. URC gained 4.4%, while JFC +2.6%. The latter also disclosed that they are infusing more money into the vehicle that holds the international operations of Tim Ho Wan, the dimsum chain founded in HK. The fund size of the vehicle, Titan Dining, will be raised to SGD450m from SGD350m and JFC’s stake will rise to 92%. JFC aims to have 100 Tim Ho Wan stores in China, from the current 19.

The defensive names lagged, with Meralco just ending flat for the week. AP (+1.2% WoW) also underperformed despite being bullish on electricity demand, expecting consumption to increase 6.6% in 2024E (higher than the 5% growth in peak demand in 2023).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Aboitiz Power expects nationwide power demand to rise by 6.6% this year

In a press release, Aboitiz Power’s President and CEO Emmanuel Rubio expressed confidence in the company’s prospects in 2024. He cited that its diverse fleet of generation assets will continue to address “tight market conditions”. The company expects demand to rise by 6.6% this year, requiring 600-700MW of new or freed electricity stock. AP has close to 1,000MW of renewable energy projects in the pipeline en route to building at least 4,600 MW over the next decade. For 2024, AP anticipates the launch of the 17-MW Tiwi Binary Geothermal plant in Albay and the 159-MW peak (MWp) Laoag solar project in Pangasinan. A number of projects are also estimated to come online by the end of 2024 to early 2025.

44-MWp Solar Plant in Armenia, Tarlac

85-MWp Solar Plant in San Manuel, Pangasinan

206-MW wind project in San Isidro, Northern Samar in partnership with Vena Energy and Vivant Energy

173-MWp solar power project in Calatrava, Negros Occidental

Manila Water terminates bulk water contract with Provincial Government of Pangasinan

Manila Water terminated its 25-year concession for a bulk water project within the Province of Pangasinan last Dec 31. The company cited “non-fulfillment of conditions”. Under the canceled contract signed in Jan 2022, the project had an estimated capital expenditure program of P8bn to deliver 200MLD to 1.4mn Pangasinan residents by the 25th year.

BPI-Robinsons merger takes effect

BPI (BPI PM) disclosed, having received the BSP and SEC approvals in December, the merger with Robinsons Bank (RBC) became effective on 01 January 2024. BPI will issue 314.04mn new shares, equivalent to 6.35% of the previous number of shares (just under 6.0% of expanded shares). From a balance sheet standpoint, the merged entity will be just shy of Metrobank (MBT PM) in terms of assets and deposits, pro-forma based on 3Q2023. However, BPI’s lead in terms of loans will widen further, putting it at 25-30% larger than MBT’s.

 

Regis expects EPS dilution in the near-term. Based on RBC’s annual reports, its NPAT from 2020-2022 was 3.5% to 5.1% that of BPI’s (it was even less pre-Covid at 1.5-2.5%). That, together with integration costs could see low- to mid-single-digit dilution in EPS. (see tables, below)

 

Upside from RBC’s high NIMs, cost reduction. There is, however, clear upside potential. Most notably, RBC’s NIMs were 90-120bps higher than BPI’s, due to its higher-yielding loan book (including two-wheel finance). The drag on RBC’s ROEs came largely from a) higher funding costs (40-50bps higher than BPI’s), and high cost-to-income ratio (15-20%age points higher). RBC also has quite a large branch network at 189, more than 1/4 that of BPI.

 

Access to JG Summit group? The holy grail for BPI would be getting access to the eco-system of the entire JG Summit group. This could include suppliers, tenants, and customers of Universal Robina (URC PM), Cebu Air (CEB PM), Robinsons Land (RLC PM), Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM), etc. Extending credit cards to CEB frequent fliers, or RRHI loyalty card holders would be an obvious easy step.

 

Jollibee to expand Tim Ho Wan store network in China

Jollibee will increase its stake in Titan Dining from 90% to 92%. It will purchase the 2% stake of another partner for S$7.7mn. Furthermore, the fund size of Titan will increase from the current S$350mn to S$450mn in order to fund the store expansion and working capital requirements of Tim Ho Wan and the completion of other projects. Jollibee has joint ventures with Titan for Tim Ho Wan in China, and for operations of Tiong Bahru Bakery and Common Man Coffee Roasters in the Philippines. Jollibee is targeting to have 100 Tim Ho Wan stores in mainland China in the medium term. There are currently 19 stores in mainland China, most of which are in Shanghai. At the end of Nov 2023, Jollibee’s store network stood at 6,805 stores worldwide.

Globe optimistic for 2024

Globe President Ernest Cu said he was “very confident” about the country’s business climate next year and remains optimistic for 2024. He believes Globe will be able to sustain “a lot of what has happened” in its mobile and fixed-line businesses in 2023. Globe’s data center venture, STT GDC, will “respond to the growing demand for hyperscale quality locations in the Philippines”. The company will also scale up Gogoro in the Philippines. Globe offers battery-swapping technology for Gogoro’s “smartscooters”.

SPNEC breaks ground for world’s largest solar project

SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC) has broken ground for the Terra Solar development in Nueva Ecija and Bulacan. Work has commenced on clearing the site, which will eventually cover approximately 3,500 hectares. Target delivery of the first phase of the project is by the first quarter of 2026. Terra Solar project will consist of 3,500 megawatts of solar panels and 4,000 megawatt-hours (MWh) of battery storage, with an estimated cost of P200bn. The project is expected to produce over five billion kilowatt-hours annually, or an estimated 5% of the total volume of the Philippine grid and 12% of its total demand.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

FY23 tourist arrivals reach 5.45mn (66% of FY19)

The Department of Tourism reported that there were 5.45mn international visitor arrivals in 2023, which is 66% of 2019 levels. Tourist arrivals in 2023 more than doubled from the 2.6mn reported in 2022. South Korea remained the top source (26% of total) of tourist arrivals at 1.44mn, reverting to 72% of 2019 levels. USA came in second at 903k (17% of total and about 85% of pre-Covid levels). Chinese tourists only reached 264k, or 15% of pre-Covid levels. Implied December 2023 tourist arrivals at 565k, 82% of pre-Covid levels. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY and about 93% of 2019 levels.

FX deposits jump, loans fall in 3Q23

The Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDU) of banks showed a divergence in loans and deposits, given the sharp depreciation of the PHP in recent years, particularly in 2022. As of 3Q2023, FX deposits jumped to $51.8bn, up 13.2% YoY and 5.7% QoQ. This is the fastest YoY growth since the taper tantrum in 2014. In PHP terms, FX deposits grew a milder 9.3% as the PHP was particularly weak in 3Q2022. As a percentage of total deposits, FX deposits took up a 16.3% share, up more than 100bps since end-2022, and the highest level since 2018.

 

In contrast, FX loans contracted 1% YoY in USD terms to $15.5bn; in PHP terms, foreign currency loans fell a steeper 4.5% YoY. FX loans now account for just 6.9% of total loans, the first time it’s fallen below 7% since 2007. FX LDRs stood at 29.9% dipping below 30% for the first time since 2011. (Banks invest a much larger portion of FX funds in sovereigns, mostly USD-denominated Philippine bonds.) With total LDRs at 69.8%, Regis estimates that PHP LDRs were at 77.5%. Given the reserve requirement (RRR) on PHP deposits was cut to 9.5%, banks have lots of capacity for loan growth, in either currency.

Government debt stock reaches P14.51tn in November

The national government’s debt stock reached P14.51tn in end-November, up 0.19% MoM. It’s a slight MoM uptick due to net issuance of domestic securities. Domestic debt rose 1.23% MoM to P10.02tn, accounting for 69.1% of total debt. Meanwhile, external debt fell 2.06% MoM to P4.48tn due to the net repayment of foreign loans and favorable exchange rate movements. The government is targeting the debt stock to reach P14.62tn by year-end, equivalent to a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61%. By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028.

 

Government eyes US$5bn from global bonds

National Treasurer Sharon Almanza said the programmed global bond issuance for 2024 is equivalent to US$5bn, almost the same level as in 2022 (US$5.26bn). However, the Treasury could not say for now what would be the first issuance for the year.

BSP to limit its intervention in forex market; Monetary policy on “neutral territory”

The BSP plans to implement a new framework regarding intervention in the foreign exchange market later this year. BSP Governor Remolona believes that the BSP has been “intervening a bit too much" and since intervention should only been done during times of stress, it should be done infrequently. Meanwhile, expectations that inflation could have returned to within its 2-4% target band for December is giving Governor Remolona scope to refrain from further rate hikes

 

“The BSP is no longer behind the curve in taming prices” and describes its policy rate on “neutral territory at this point”. Gov Remolona has repeatedly signaled that the BSP is unlikely to pivot to policy easing soon as price risks persist, including El Nino’s effects on food and energy costs. The BSP’s key interest rate is at a 16-year high of 6.5%. Its first monetary policy meeting for 2024 is on Feb 15.
