REGIS REPORT

10 May 2024

The PSEi ended the busy week of corporate earnings and macro data on a lower note, -1.57% WoW at 6511.93, weighed down by continued outflows. The benchmark initially alternated between gains and losses through the earlier part of the week, then notably suffered its worst daily drop in 3 weeks on Thursday (-1.75% to 6,542, its lowest close since), dragged by a surge in foreign outflows which coincided with the 1Q GDP announcement, then proceeded to extend losses on the final day of the week. Data released on Thursday 10am, coinciding with the intraday peak of the benchmark, showed that 1Q GDP grew by 5.7% YoY, below consensus forecast of 5.9%. The breakdown showed that private consumption growth slowed to 4.6% as inflation limited spending, but even more disappointing was government spending which only grew by 1.7%. Another highlight this week was the latest CPI reading which came out on Tuesday. Data showed that inflation ticked higher to 3.8% in April from 3.7% in March, but was notably below the 4.1% consensus forecast. Food inflation remained elevated at 6% but we note that rice prices climbed by only 0.4% MoM, which is the slowest pace since October 2023. Furthermore, the Agriculture Secretary is optimistic that rice prices will start to decline by June, citing the direction in international rice prices and the changing in weather conditions at home after El Nino largely affected 1Q palay production.

In terms of index movers, top gainer this week was WLCON +10.54% WoW, notwithstanding the company’s weak results (details below). The stock is up for the 2nd week in a row after hitting a 3-year low in late April, supported by apparent bargain hunting. Another big gainer was last week’s leader ACEN +9.73% WoW, now +45% from its mid-April bottom, as investors digested the company’s positive results. 1Q income rose by 34% to P2.72b and looks en route to beat both Regis and consensus FY24 forecasts. Additionally, our sense is that 2Q may be a particularly strong quarter given the company’s newly onboarded capacities and its net seller exposure to the spot electricity market (see note by our research team below). Another bright spot was URC +8.91% as it reported that its 1Q net income grew by 21% to P4.1b, inline with Regis’ forecast. Other consumer plays like PGOLD +6.14%, MONDE +3.1% and JFC +1.37% were also in the green, indicating some optimism on the mostly battered consumer sector as inflationary pressures are expected to start improving within the next 2 months.

On the opposite end however, among the worst performing names this week was BDO -6.41% as focus seems to be shifting away from lenders in general as the recent CPI and GDP prints now imply perhaps a less hawkish monetary policy outlook for the year compared to the prior weeks. Its parent SM -6.58% was the most battered stock in the index this week as it was also the top foreign sold name, while its peers MBT -4.05% and BPI -3.09% also moved in the same direction. The property sector meanwhile was mixed as the busy round of results from developers this week painted a similarly mixed picture. Looking at reservation sales, ALI +1.3% WoW was the bright spot, growing by 20% YoY and 19% QoQ to P33.1b. Meanwhile for SMPH it was down -26% YoY but up 109% QoQ to P26.5b, MEG down -8% YoY but up 24% QoQ to P36.5b, and on the other end, RLC plummeted -88% YoY and -83% QoQ to P684m, the lowest level in 15 years (see notes by our research team below). That being said, RLC fared the best among those 4 names, rising +4.08% this week, while SMPH -2.37% and MEG -0.56%.

Market activity this week stood at a daily average of $111.5m, down by -33.2% WoW but this was mostly due to the high-base effect from last week’s outsize ~$290m BLOOM block. Foreign investors were net sellers throughout the week, extending the outflow streak to 8 days and netting a weekly outflow of $53m. Their top sold stocks were SM -6.58%, ALI +1.3%, SMPH -2.37%, AC -1.51%, and BDO -6.41%; while their top bought were URC +8.91%, TEL +4.85%, ACEN +9.73% and MWC +5.48%.

Universal Robina - 1Q24: BCF margins recover sharply
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q24 net profit +21% YoY – in line

URC’s net profit in 1Q24 grew 21% YoY to P4.1bn, broadly in line with Regis but slightly ahead of consensus expectations. While the bottomline was boosted by forex gains (~P400mn), consolidated EBIT (+16% YoY to P5.4bn) also came in mildly above expectations. Revenues were in line at P42.6bn, up 7% YoY.

Top line trends mixed amidst downtrading, competition

PH branded consumer food (BCF) sales grew 6% YoY, largely driven by volume growth. Category trends were mixed amidst persistent downtrading, though key categories such as snacks (+8-17%), biscuits (+15-21%) and RTD beverages (+22%) were up sharply YoY, albeit off a relatively low base from the ERP transition in 1Q23. Meanwhile, coffee and noodles continued to be challenged amidst competition and shifting consumer trends.

On the international side, revenues were flattish on a PHP basis but grew 4% in local currency terms. This was driven by the continued recovery in Thailand and Indonesia, which grew 5% during the period.

 

BCF margins recover sharply

EBIT margins for the domestic and international BCF segments expanded 100bps and 170bps YoY, respectively during the quarter. This was mainly driven by more favorable input cost (particularly sugar and wheat), further boosted by cost management strategies implemented. At nearly 13% (see Fig. 2), the EBIT margin is the highest quarterly margin achieved by both segments since 1Q22. This is also the highest quarterly margin achieved by the int’l BCF segment since the Munchy’s acquisition in 2021.

Margin improvement to be sustained despite volatile demand, cost environment

Management is optimistic that margins should continue to improve, notwithstanding seasonality in the coming quarters (e.g. Holy Week, Ramadan holidays). Top line growth will likely continue to face challenges given soft demand amidst inflationary pressures to consumers. The company, however, is investing in A&P and implementing other strategies to drive demand and recover market shares, especially in key domestic categories (e.g. coffee, noodles). Meanwhile, prices of certain key commodities (e.g. cocoa and coffee) have soared, but management believes the recent margin improvement is sustainable given more structural operating cost improvements.

 
ACEN Corp - New capacities drive 1Q24 profit jump
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Increased output drives earnings growth

ACEN reported net income of P2.72bn for 1Q24, +34% higher YoY. The figure includes a P316m impairment charge as well as a P389m valuation realization gain, which largely offset each other. Thus, even after stripping those out, the 1Q24 bottom line looks on trend to beat our FY24 estimates as well as the Street’s (the latter handily), given seasonality and the gradual entry of new capacities over the course of the year. Some of the new capacities already helped to deliver more GWH output, driving earnings growth in 1Q24. Core “attributable” EBITDA rose 32% to P5.25bn.

New capacities lift output

Over 500MW of new capacities went operational in 1Q24, lifting generation output up 49% YoY and 19% QoQ to 1,597 GWH. In the Philippines, ACEN sold 231GWH (net) into the spot market (“WESM”), 17% of its total electricity sales there. We expect the proportion of WESM sales to rise in the near-term. Recently completed projects overseas (Australia and India) also contributed more GWH.

 

2Q looking better for PH operations

The new capacities and net seller exposure to WESM mean that ACEN’s Philippine operations are in a good position to deliver particularly strong numbers for 2Q. The tight supply situation (as evidenced by the grid alerts) point to exceptionally high spot prices in 2Q just as solar farm output is at its peak.

Management expressed a view that WESM prices might soften next year due to the scheduled completion of a large power plant in Luzon. Given the history of power projects domestically, we believe the possibility of completion delays is high.

Massive capex ahead

ACEN plans to spend ~P72bn (US$1.3bn) for capex this year, possibly more. We believe the pace of spending is unlikely to abate in FY25 (and beyond) given its goal of reach 20,000MW capacity by 2030. This, in our view, means ACEN will likely need to raise equity soon.

 
Ayala Land - 1Q24 reservation sales grow 20%, led by premium segment
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q24 net profit rises 39%, led by development revenue

Ayala Land’s 1Q24 net profit rose 39% YoY to P6.3bn, as development revenue grew 47%. Since 1Q is typically the weakest period for ALI earnings, we consider the results to be broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts.

Reservation sales grow 20%, led by premium segment

Ayala Land’s reservation sales rose 20% YoY and 19% QoQ to P33.3bn, 91% of the 2019 average and on track to meet management’s 15% growth target for the full[1]year. ALI credits this to the c. 25% increase in the number of sales agents for the premium segment (in 2023). This in turn led to a 34% jump in premium segment reservation sales.

We estimate that about P4bn of sales came from Ayala Land Premier’s “ultra high-end” project in Makati CBD, Park Villas, which is now 40% presold.

The improvement in 1Q24 sales led to a reduction in unsold inventory levels to 21 months (from 24 months in 4Q23). The cancellation rate for residential projects has also been relatively contained (at 7%).

Mall redevelopment begins

Mall revenue grew 9% YoY to P5.5bn, 7% higher than the 1Q19 level. Although the company is redeveloping the flagship malls (which comprise 30% of space) in phases, we would expect some disruption to operations going forward. Management, however, has said that mall revenue will not drop due to the mall reinvention.

Office vacancy rises 2ppt QoQ

Ayala Land’s office vacancy rose 2ppt QoQ to 10%, still much lower than the industry average of 18%. Half of the increase was due to a BPO tenant not renewing all of its space. However, vacancy might decline again soon as Ayala Land is in talks with potential tenants for 40,000sqm (3% of the total portfolio).

Usage of land bank on track

The company has a target of consuming 800 hectares annually over the next five years (total land bank of 11,200 hectares in 2023). Management believes it is on track to meet the target this year.

Share buybacks to continue

The company bought back P2.6bn worth of shares YTD and management previously said that it would continue buying back shares as long as the share price was well below its fair value. The company still has P14bn left in its buyback program, equivalent to about 4% of market cap.

ALI is trading at 46% discount to NAV, 1.4X FY24E P/B, and 15.3X FY24E P/E.

 
SM Prime - 1Q24 presales rebound QoQ; mall revenue growth normalizes
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q24 net profit rises 11%

SM Prime’s 1Q24 net profit rose 11% to P10.5bn while operating profit grew 6% to P14.7bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The underperformance appears to be due to lower-than-expected residential revenue.

The disclosure to the PSE provided limited information but we hope to get more details during the SM Investments briefing on May 8 (registration link).

Mall revenue growth normalizes to 7% YoY

Mall revenue (of Philippines and China combined) grew 7% YoY (-6% QoQ) to P18.2bn, in line with our forecast. Mall rental revenue grew 8% YoY while other revenue (including cinema and event ticket sales) was flat.

After exceeding 2019 levels last year, we expect Philippine same-mall revenue growth to return to the historical range of 5%-8% (from 13% YoY growth in 4Q23).

 

Presales rebound QoQ

1Q24 residential reservation sales reached P26.5bn. While this is down 26% YoY (up 109% QoQ), we do not consider this alarming, given the base in 1Q23. The 1Q24 figure is actually slightly higher than the 2023 average. This latest data point suggests the sharp drop in 4Q23 presales may have been a blip.

On the other hand, 1Q24 residential revenue of P9.0bn (+6% YoY, -28% QoQ) is trending behind our estimates.

Revenue of other businesses grows c. 10%

Hotel revenue growth also slowed to 10% YoY (vs. 45% in FY23, 12% in 4Q23) as the low base effect for travel has dissipated. Meanwhile, office revenue grew 9% YoY, similar to the floor area expansion of c. 7%.

 
SM Investments - 1Q24: Profits rise +6%; Retail contributions lag
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Profits rise 6% to P18.4bn

SM Investment announced 1Q24 results, with profits rising 6% to P18.4bn. While this is within our expectations, it is likely to be well short of the Street’s. The property segment was only mildly off but the shortfall (vs. Street expectations) is likely to have come from the unlisted units (Retail and “portfolio investments”).

Mall revenue up 7%, with PH mall rents up 9%

Overall mall revenue for SM Prime grew 7%, led by rental revenue from Philippine malls (+9%). Management noted that revenue would have been higher if not for the timing of this year’s Holy (Easter) Week, which meant less days that the malls were open.

Reservation sales decline

Management attributed the 26% drop (to P26.5bn) in 1Q24 residential reservation sales to a couple of factors. First, the firm raised the upfront fees on reservations to weed out the less serious buyers. Management also cited changes in tax regulations that led buyers to temporarily delay purchases.

Retail segment disappoints with 23% drop in income

1Q revenue for SM Retail rose a rather soft 3%, not very different from other listed retailers that have reported so far. Sales for SM Store and for the specialty stores declined 4% and 1% YoY, as inflation hurt consumer wallets. Profits for both were down much more sharply (70% and 27%). The Food group (groceries) fared better, as revenues rose 6%, lifted by its Alfamart operation.

Alfamart accelerating expansion

After adding 280 stores (+19%) last year, majority-owned Alfamart Philippines is looking likely to comfortably exceed its target of 400-store openings this year. As of 1Q24, it already added 134, ending the quarter with 1,820 stores.

 
Robinsons Land - 1Q24 residential reservation sales drop sharply
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q24 recurring profit rises 21%; presales drop sharply

Robinsons Land’s 1Q24 headline net profit jumped 53% to P4.1bn. Excluding non[1]recurring gains from the “de-equitization” of the affiliate digital bank, GoTyme, recurring profit rose 21% to P3.3bn, in line with consensus and slightly ahead of REGIS forecast. With RLC’s ownership in GoTyme diluted from 20% to 19%, GoTyme is now treated as a financial investment rather than an associate. As a result, the losses that were previously recorded were reversed.

More than the earnings, however, what caught our attention was the sharp drop in residential reservation sales.

Reservation sales of standalone residential projects drop to 15-year low

Reservation sales of RLC’s standalone residential projects fell 88% YoY (83% QoQ) to P684mn, the lowest level in over 15 years. Management attributes this largely to: 1) the increase in reservation fee from P25k-P50k to P50k-P80k, and 2) an internal restructuring that involved the merger of RLC’s condominium and house & lot divisions.

RLC CEO Lance Gokongwei expects 2H24 sales to be more similar to 2023 levels and there are already some signs of recovery. A newly launched project in April 2024 has been 40% presold, implying that sales from this project alone have amounted to P1.8bn.

Although RLC did not tighten the payment schemes of its JV projects, presales of this segment also fell 24% YoY and 45% QoQ to P3.8bn. While this might seem alarming, we also refrain from being overly bearish on this segment for now. FY23 presales of JV projects hit P19.5bn (equivalent to the 2019 level) even though the 2Q23 and 3Q23 figures were similar to 1Q24 (~P3.8bn).

Consumer spending in RLC malls remains healthy

Consumer spending in RLC malls remained healthy, with mall revenue staying flat QoQ (from the seasonally strong 4Q). Furthermore, mall revenue grew 14% YoY to P4.5bn, c. 110% of the 2019 level, thanks in part of a 2ppt YoY improvement in occupancy (to 93%).

However, we suspect that the unusually high mall EBIT margin of 42.8% (+6ppt YoY and 11ppt QoQ) will normalize to the typical 32%-38% in the coming quarters.

Office occupancy drops due to completion of new building

Although office revenue grew 3% YoY (+4% QoQ), office EBIT fell 8% YoY (down 9% QoQ) due to higher operating expenses from the newly completed GBF Tower. We estimate that the new building is only c. 40% occupied. As a result, occupancy of the entire portfolio fell from 87% in 4Q23 to 84% in 1Q24.

by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q24 profit rises 8%, forex losses drag

Megaworld’s 1Q24 headline net profit rose 8% YoY to P4.4bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Operating profit grew 28% YoY (ahead) on strong residential revenue and margin expansion. PLDT - 1Q24: Mobile revenue bounce continues; broadband stagnates
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core earnings rise 13% for 1Q24

PLDT reported net profit of P9.8bn for 1Q24, 9% higher YoY. We estimate core income (inclusive of losses at Maya) to be P8.9bn, +13% YoY, which would be broadly within Regis expectations but ahead of the Street’s. Service revenues rose 5% YoY, driven by a rebound (+6%) in mobile revenue.

Mobile revenue continues rebound

Mobile revenue rose 6% YoY, and was, notably, practically unchanged from 4Q23. This reinforces our view that the wireless segment will lead growth this year. Growth for mobile began to pick up noticeably beginning mid-2023. Sustaining this will require resistance against the temptation to compete purely on price.

Broadband stagnates

Broadband revenue was virtually unchanged YoY and PLDT saw a slight loss (14k) in wired subscribers. Management highlighted that fiber broadband revenues alone rose 7% but we don’t think the fiber business should be viewed separately from the legacy DSL operation. One is a substitute for the other, and migration from copper (DSL) to fiber is a natural, expected progression.

Free cash flow positive after dividends next year?

Management aims to be free cash flow positive even after dividend payments next year. This would require a meaningful reduction in capex, in our view.

Strategic options for data center business

PLDT is weighing options for its data center business (P5-6bn of annual revenue). NTT has apparently offered to buy a majority stake in the business for a valuation “north of US$1bn.” Other parties are wiling to buy a minority stake but at lower valuations. Spinning the business off as a REIT is another option.

Maya losses narrow

Losses from fintech Maya halved to P376m, suggesting it’s on the road to break even by late this year. Management touted that it is the leader in the processing of payments for QRPH, the mandated QR standard for digital payments.

 
Monde Nissin - 1Q24: Margin recovery sustained
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q24 net profit jumps 53% YoY on strong APAC BFB margins

MONDE reported core net profit of P2.9bn in 1Q24, up 53% YoY, ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. While consolidated net sales were broadly in line (+2% YoY to P20.3bn), EBITDA is tracking ahead at P4.5bn (+31% YoY). By segment, APAC BFB EBITDA grew 29% YoY, while Quorn registered a narrower EBITDA loss (P60mn) vs 1Q23 (P116mn).

APAC BFB: margin recovery continues

APAC BFB sales growth moderated to 2% YoY, on flattish volumes, due to the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23) which resulted in fewer selling days. Management expects the YoY impact to normalize in 2Q24, with April sales +13%. Margins continued to improve QoQ and YoY due to favorable input cost for wheat and palm oil. Gross margin expanded 740bps YoY while EBITDA margin expanded almost 600bps YoY to levels last seen in 2019-2020.

Quorn: Tight opex control tempers EBITDA loss

Quorn sales grew 2% YoY as the market remained difficult. Gross margin took a hit, contracting almost 400bps YoY, but tight opex control led to a narrower EBITDA loss vs 1Q23.

Margins likely to be maintained

Management expects margins for the APAC BFB segment to be maintained as it has locked in prices of wheat and palm oil (~50% of total segment raw material cost) until 3Q24. Amidst intensifying competition, particularly in instant noodles, some of the gross margin gains may be reinvested into A&P, which is expected to ramp up to c5% of sales for the full-year from the current level of ~3%.

 
DMCI Holdings - 1Q24: Profit declines in key segments; More on cement acquisition
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q24 core profit falls 31% to P5.38bn - Above estimates

Recurring profit (ex-FX adj) of P5.38bn was 31% lower YoY but is nonetheless on trend to beat our full-year estimate due to the better-than-expected profits at Semirara (see results comment), Maynilad, and the off-grid power units. Its property, construction, and nickel mining arms, however, underperformed. Management also provided details on its cement acquisition financing plans.

Reservation sales mildly up; Unsold inventory also up

The property unit’s reservation sales rose 2% YoY, 53% QoQ to P11.35bn in 1Q24, above its 2019 quarterly average of P9.1bn. Management said that cancellation rates are broadly the same as in 2023 and in 2019, as most recent project launches have catered to leisure/high-income markets. Unsold inventory stood at P77bn, up 17% YoY, 25% QoQ, and equivalent to 26 months’ worth of sales. However, profit fell 31% to P684mn, after booked revenues dropped 37%.

Construction profits fall on project delays

Revenues declined 18% YoY leading to profits falling 64% to P98mn. The slow pace of revenue recognition was blamed on delays in both building and infrastructure projects. The order book fell by 8% YoY, 7% QoQ to P39bn as contract awards have also been delayed.

Maynilad: Profit up 26% YoY; Capex dips by 4%

Maynilad’s standalone profit rose 26% to P2.7bn, on the back of 6% billed volume growth and the second tranche of its tariff hikes. Capex, however, dipped by 4% YoY to P4.0bn but management is maintaining its commitment to spend P31bn (+57%) this year.

Nickel mining suffers from permitting delays and lower ASPs

The nickel mining unit booked a P22mn net loss in 1Q24 due to reduced production and lower ASPs. Delays in securing permits from the regulator prompted management to lower its volume guidance to 1.6mn MT from 2mn MT.

Off-grid power: Electricity sales up 11%

The off-grid power unit grew profit by 97% YoY underpinned by an 11% increase in systemwide sales volumes and new capacity. Management also indicated that it is seeing unprecedented sales growth so far in April due to El Niño.

Cement acquisition will not affect dividends

Reducing DMCI’s reliance on Semirara was a key motivation behind its planned acquisition of Cemex Philippines (CHP PM). Management plans to lever up and fund its entire P9bn portion of the acquisition by issuing long-term bonds or preferreds to be able to continue paying dividends. It aims to turn around the cement operations by 2025, after which the dividends it extracts from CHP can be used to pay down its borrowings. The group also does not intend to de-list CHP, as it would have six months to re-issue secondary shares if the tender offer results trigger a de-listing. DMCI’s parent company, Dacon, may also sell down its stake CHP either through a third party or to the public in the future.

ICTSI: 1Q24 profit hits new record high

ICT's headline 1Q24 profit of US$210mn (+36%) included a one-off US$20.5mn income from the settlement with the labor union in Portland, Oregon and a US$1.6mn loss from the sale of PT OJA in Indonesia. Excluding these one-offs and after perps distributions, recurring profit was 25% higher YoY and, remarkably, up 5% QoQ at US$184mn. The results are trending ahead of Regis and consensus estimates on better-than-expected revenues, lower financing costs, as well as taxes.

Organic volumes up 5%, yields up 9%. Excluding the terminated contracts in Pakistan and Indonesia, organic volumes rose 5% YoY, with Asia, Americas, and EMEA volumes up 7%, 3%, 6%, respectively. Volumes were strong at most ports, except for the Philippines, particularly MICT, and Ecuador. While volumes have started to recover at MICT in 2Q so far, management noted that volumes at its port in Ecuador remain weak.

Yield per TEU rose to a new high of US$201, up 8.9% YoY on a like-for-like basis. Yields were boosted by tariff hikes and favorable container mix, particularly for its ports in Melbourne, Mexico, Honduras, and Brazil, and have so far been sustained in 2Q.  Management added a caveat, however, that yields could swing in both directions, depending on volume and/or jurisdiction mix.

EBITDA margin rose further to 64.9%. EBITDA climbed 4% QoQ, 17% YoY to US$414mn, as cost increases were kept at a minimum. In particular, higher manpower and admin costs were partly offset by lower power/fuel costs and rent expenses. These efforts resulted in EBITDA margin further improving to 64.9%.

An update on acquisitions (Durban, et. al) - Management believes that it can sign the concession agreement for DCT-2 in Durban, South Africa within 2Q24 or early 3Q24, notwithstanding the ongoing court case lodged by Maersk. The company is also actively looking for acquisition opportunities and has a couple of targets in the Americas and West African regions that are in advanced stages.

Wilcon Depot 1Q24 dragged by weak demand, high opex

WLCON’s net profit fell 23% YoY to P740mn in 1Q24, trending behind Regis and consensus expectations. Revenues are tracking behind forecasts at P8.3bn, down 3% YoY. Meanwhile, operating profit contracted 20% YoY as expansion-related expenses continued to weigh on cost.

Same store sales were down 7.3% as transactions contracted ~8%. This was partly attributed to the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23), which is typically a slow period for sales. Underlying SSS, however, appeared to be soft, with SSS down ~3% in Jan-Feb alone. While SSS has recovered in Apr, YT-Apr SSS is still tracking -3%, according to management.

Operating margin contracted 300bps YoY despite a mild improvement in GP margin (+50bps). The latter was driven by the increase in the contribution of in-house brands to 52.6% vs 50.4% in 1Q23. Meanwhile, pre-operating expenses for new stores continued to weigh on cost. In 1Q24 WLCON opened 3 new stores (1 Depot / 2 Do-It-Wilcon) out of the 10 stores (of which 8 Depots) programmed for the year.

1Q24 GDP accelerates to 5.7% on export rebound; Domestic demand slows

GDP grew 5.7% in 1Q24, below consensus forecast of 5.9%. On the demand side, domestic demand growth moderated to 4.0% but was offset by a rebound in net exports to 9.5%. Private consumption grew 4.6%, the slowest pace since the lows of the pandemic as inflation limited spending. Meanwhile, government spending was up by just 1.7%, which the government attributed to the timing of large expenditures to April this year from March last year. Capital formation was still sluggish due to high interest rates, rising by just 1.3%, with construction moderating to 6.8% and investments in durable equipment contracting for the first time since 1Q21.

On the supply side, service sector remained robust, rising by 6.9% and contributed 4.2ppts to GDP. Industry sector grew at a faster rate of 5.1% due to faster growth in manufacturing and higher output of food, electronics, and chemical products. Agri sector grew by 0.4%

To reach Regis’ FY24E GDP growth forecast of 6.0%, GDP for the succeeding periods should average 6.1%. To reach consensus’ FY24E GDP growth target of 5.8%, GDP for succeeding periods should average 5.8%.

Inflation mildly accelerates to 3.8% in April

Headline inflation for April was mildly up at 3.8% from 3.7% the previous month. This is below the consensus forecast of 4.1% and at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.5%-4.3%. The mild uptick driven by food inflation (+6% from +5.6%) and higher fuel prices (+3.4% from +0.8%). Rice CPI rose by 0.4% MoM, the slowest pace since Oct 2023. Note that international rice prices have fallen c. 10% YTD But curiously, electricity CPI (4.6% of CPI basket) dropped 11% YoY and 3% MoM, despite tightness in the electricity market – as such, Regis’ sense that this could re-accelerate in the succeeding month.

Core inflation slowed further to 3.2% from 3.4%. Headline inflation rate this year has averaged 3.4%, trending below the BSP’s target of 3.8%. Note that the BSP’s next policy rate meeting is on May 16.

DA sees lower rice prices starting June

Department of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said rice prices will likely decline starting next month, citing the decline in international rice prices for the past weeks. Note that rice accounts for 8.9% of CPI basket. Note that companies catering to lower-income buyers fared relatively poorly in 2023, and Regis’ sense is that they should benefit from rebounding demand in 2H24E (CNPF, MONDE, PGOLD, URC.)

House to pass Rice Tariffication Law amendments before May 25

According to House Speaker Martin Romualdez, who is also the cousin of President Marcos, Jr., the House of Representatives will prioritize the passage of the proposed amendments to the Rice Tariffication Law (RA 11203) by May 25.

The proposed changes are: The restoration of the National Food Authority’s (NFA) ability to import rice through the authority of the DA secretary; Extend the Rice Competitiveness Enhancement Fund (RCEF) until 2030; Increase RCEF’s total annual appropriation from P10bn to P15bn; Creation of the Rice Industry Development-Program Management Office that will serve as the secretariat of the RCEF; The law, signed in 2019, stripped the NFA of its regulatory and import licensing functions and allowed the private sector to import freely, subject to a tariff system; The collected tariffs would then be used to support farmers through various programs, including mechanization.

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.2bn in March

Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit shrunk 37% YoY, 13% MoM to US$3.18bn in March due to a 20% drop in imports to US$9.3bn, which is the sharpest drop since July 2020. Raw material imports dropped 25% and capital goods imports dropped 15%. Consumer goods imports fell 19% and fuel imports dropped 18%.

Meanwhile exports fell 7% to US$6.13bn due to decline in mineral products (electronics exports up +1%). Exports to the US and HK rose 9% and 60%, respectively. This brings 1Q24 trade deficit down by 22% to US$11.25bn.

Rules tightened for Chinese tourist visas

The Department of Foreign Affairs will implement stricter rules for the issuance of tourist visas to Chinese nationals, citing cases of fraudulently acquired visas and crimes related to POGO firms. New visa requirement includes social security documents, on top of usual government IDs, proof of financial capacity, employment certificate, and bank statements. The DFA will also increase the minimum number for group applicants from three to ten. Unlike group applicants, group applicants don’t have to personally appear before a Philippine consular office in China.

Note that Chinese tourists rose 105% YoY to 141k as of April 2024, which is about 24% of pre-Covid levels.
