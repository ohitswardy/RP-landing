REGIS REPORT

20 December 2024

The PCOMP ended a second straight week in the red, -3.2% or -210.13 pts WoW to 6,406.38 mostly as investors weighed scaled Fed easing expectations for 2025. The PCOMP only snapped a seven-day losing streak Friday, though much of the day's gains were erased at the close due to the FTSE rebal that had Phils' country weight slightly downweighted.

Phil equities joined the sell-off in global markets after the Fed dialed back its rate cut projections for 2025. This pushed the dollar to fresh highs and lifted long-term US treasury yields which added to risk-off sentiment in the local bourse. Consequently, the PH10Y yield has closed above 6.00% anew, while the peso depreciated 0.6% WoW to close at 58.80 after nearly hitting the 59.00-mark mid-week. Adding to concerns on the currency, the Phils' current account deficit ballooned 10% QoQ to $5.7bn in 3Q, equivalent to 5.2% of GDP. This took the YT-Sep deficit to 3.9% of GDP, which is behind the BSP's target to narrow the gap to 1.0% from 2.6% last year. 

The sell-off on large cap names continued with BPI (-8.0% WoW), ALI (-6.8% WoW), and SMPH (-5.3% WoW) making up nearly half of the index's drop. Among sector indices, Property (-4.9% WoW) and Financials (-4.6% WoW) fell the most as investors reduced exposure on cyclical issues. However, BLOOM (-8.7% WoW) was the index's largest decliner for the second week in a row as sentiment on the stock further soured upon the sudden retirement of its President and COO. 

Market ADTO improved WoW though remained soft at $76m. This week's $70-m net foreign outflow took MTD to $107m and QTD to $434m, already nearly reversing the $558-m inflow in 3Q.

BLOOM President retires
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

In a disclosure to the PSE, Bloomberry reported that Mr. Thomas Arasi, 66, who has been President and COO since 2013, retired due to personal reasons. His retirement seems rather abrupt, as the company has not revealed his replacement. We would expect the Chairman, Mr. Enrique K. Razon, Jr. to fill this role until a new President and COO is announced.

How might this affect the company?

Mr. Arasi’s retirement will likely cause a minor disruption to operations in the near-term, largely because it appears unexpected.

While Mr. Razon can perform the functions of President and COO, we would expect him to begin the search for Mr. Arasi’s replacement soon.

Looking ahead, we expect the company will place incrementally more emphasis on the online business, which it plans to revamp by 2H25. Industry gross gaming revenue (GGR) from electronic games grew from P6bn in 3Q23 to P36bn in 3Q24. In contrast, GGR of licensed (physical) casinos fell 2% YoY to P51bn in 3Q24.

Bloomberry appoints Solaire North chief to replace recently-retired President and COO Thomas Arasi

Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) appointed Mr. Gregory Francis Hawkins, who currently heads Solaire North, as Acting COO. While he only joined the company in November 2023, he is a veteran casino executive. Prior to joining BLOOM, he was Chief Casino Officer at Star Entertainment Group (SGR AU). He was also previously CEO of City of Dreams and was part of the founding executive team at Crown Melbourne. Mr. Hawkins’ appointment comes after the rather sudden retirement of Thomas Arasi as President and COO on December 17.

Jollibee to redeem US$600mn perpetual securities

Jollibee Foods Corp. (JFC PM) is set to fully redeem its US$600mn (P30.6bn) guaranteed senior perpetual capital on Jan 23, 2025. The perps had a coupon of 3.9%, payable semi-annually, noncallable for five years after the issuance back in Jan 2020. As of Sep 2024, the remaining balance amounted to US$396.5mn or P20.3bn after previous repayments back in 2021. When JFC redeems the securities, the company will pay back the principal amount, include any unpaid interest or distributions owed to investors, and cancel and delist the securities from the Singapore Exchange. Recall that the securities were issued on Jan 23, 2020 through its subsidiary Jollibee Worldwide Pte. Ltd. (JWPL) and were listed in the Singapore Exchange Securities Trading Limited (SGX-ST). The securities are treated as equity as part of non-controlling interests. The proceeds from the issuance were used to repay debt from acquiring The Coffee Bean & Tea Leaf (CBTL) which the company bought for $350mn back in Sept 2019.

South African court denies ICTSI appeal on Durban port ruling, prefers to proceed with hearings in March

The KwaZulu-Natal High Court denied ICTS’s bid to appeal the interdict ruling against Transnet’s selection of ICTSI as the concessionaire for DCT-2 port in Durban, South Africa. Court said that it is “not in the interests of justice to permit an appeal,” adding that hearing of the case is just three months away in March 2025. It also cited that the first respondent [Transnet], which is the party interdicted and restrained by the order, has not itself sought leave to appeal. Our forecasts/valuation for ICTSI do not include DCT-2. We estimate the acquisition of DCT-2 would have added US$211mn (6%) in revenues and US$74mn in EBITDA (3%) to ICTSI’s consolidated numbers on its first full-year of operations.

ICTSI Mexico port volumes up 20% YoY in November

Volumes at ICTSI’s port in Mexico (CMSA) grew by 20% YoY to 132k TEUs in November. On a MoM basis, CMSA’s volumes dipped by 5% MoM. ICTSI continued to take market share, as volumes at competing ports in Manzanillo fell 7% YoY in November. Brings YT-November volumes at CMSA up by 15% YoY to 1.33mn TEUs. This is in line with our FY24E of 1.47mn TEUs (+15%). We estimate CSMA accounts for 12% FY25E consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, 26% of EBITDA. ICT trades at 7.9x FY25E EV/EBITDA, lower than peer average of (11x) and historical average of (11x).

ICTSI’s port in Argentina receives certification to handle neo-Panamax vessels

ICTSI’s Tecplata port in Argentina received certification from the Argentine Naval Prefecture to handle docking maneuvers for neo-Panamax vessels. Accreditation positions TecPlata as the first terminal in Buenos Aires—and in the entire country—capable of accommodating these large ships. Tecplata only handled 8k TEUs in 2023 against Buenos Aires’ total throughput of 634k TEUs. Our valuation assumes a negative equity value of P8/sh for Tecplata over its remaining 14-year concession life.

ACEN wins bid for a 936MW greenfield wind project in Australia

ACEN won the capacity investment scheme agreement (CISA) from the Australian government to build a 936MW wind farm (“Valley of the Winds”) in New South Wales. Valley of the Winds is 95%-owned by ACEN and is expected to be completed by 2030. According to ACEN President and Chief Executive Officer Eric T. Francia, the government bid out 3.6GW worth of new wind projects under the CISA program, which is similar to the Philippines’ Green Energy Auction Program. If completed, this project will be the single largest project of ACEN to date and will add to the company’s attributable RE portfolio of 6,745MW (3,051MW operational, 2,302 MW under construction, 1,401MW “committed”). At an estimated replacement cost of US$1.6mn per MW, we estimate the project could cost US$1.5bn. ACEN’s international RE portfolio (attributable) stands at 4,357 MW, of which 2,004 MW (46%) is operational, ~22% is under construction, and ~32% is “committed.”

ACEN targets to add 1.2GW of new capacity by end-2025

ACEN expects to complete the construction of a total of 1.2 GW of new RE capacity within and outside the Philippines by end-2025. According to ACEN President and CEO Eric Francia, among these projects are: 520 MW Stubbo Solar in New South Wales, Australia, 146MW Monsoon Wind project in Laos, 123MW hybrid-solar/wind in India, 109MW Stockyard wind project in Texas, USA, 160MW Pagudpud and 57MW Capa Wind projects in Ilocos Norte, Philippines, and 60MW Pangasinan solar project in the Philippines. ACEN has 6,754MW of attributable RE capacity, of which 3,051MW is operational. In the Philippines alone, ACEN has a total of 2,397MW of capacity, of which 1,330 MW are under construction. Its international att. RE capacity totals 4,357 MW, of which c. 2,400 MW are under construction.

Globe on track to complete first phase of data center in 2Q25

NuBank invests $150mn in Tyme SG - to fund GoTyme expansion

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Monetary board cuts policy rate 25bps as expected

The BSP's Monetary board reduced its policy rate by 25bps to 5.75%, in-line with consensus expectations. BSP also reduced its risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2025 slightly higher to 3.4% from 3.3%, while 2026 remains unchanged at 3.7%. This is based on the inflation outlook continuing to lean to the upside due to potential upward adjustments in transport fares and electricity but may be offset by the impact of lower import tariffs on rice. Given the within-target inflation outlook, Governor Remolona states that further cuts are expected next year but with 100 bps being "too much" and 0 bps being "too little." He also stated that barring any big surprises, the BSP will continue its easing cycle as any small surprises will not warrant a change in current policy direction.

Remittances rise 2.7% YoY in October

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.7% YoY to US$3.08bn. This was primarily driven by the acceleration in land-based remittances from Singapore up 10.4% YoY and 17.1% MoM. Sea-based remittances growth remain slow, only up 0.6% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances rose 3.6% for the month, an increase from September (+2.0%) but still the second slowest for the year. Bringing YT-October remittances up 3% YoY to US$28.3bn. Broadly in line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year.

Current account deficit widens to US$5.7bn in 3Q24

The BSP reported that the country’s current account deficit widened to US$5.67bn in 3Q24, up 10% QoQ/154% YoY and accounted for 5.2% of GDP. This was due to a 10% QoQ, YoY expansion in the trade deficit to US$18.5bn, on the back of lower exports and higher imports. Electronic exports fell 27% YoY to US$4.6bn, which the BSP attributed this to a shift in international demand for new electronic products (e.g., AI, EVs, solar panels, batteries, and high-end semiconductors). Higher imports were driven by the 31% YoY expansion in iron and steel imports to US$1.4bn, as well as miscellaneous manufactures, such as magnetic tapes suitable for computer use, which rose by 15.1% to US$1.5bn. Bringing YT-September current account deficit to US$12.93bn, up 77% YoY and equivalent to 3.9% of GDP. This is trending higher than the BSP’s FY24 projection of a US$4.7bn current account deficit (1.0% of GDP).

November balance of payments deficit hits US$2.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$2.3bn, higher than both of the deficits of US$724mn in Oct 2024 and the US$216mn deficit in Nov 2023. Bringing YT-November registered a surplus of US$2.1bn, lower than the US$3.0bn surplus recorded the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-November stood at US$108.5bn, a slight drop from the US$111.1bn in end-October. The GIR level is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$106bn in end-2024.

Marcos postpones 2025 budget signing for further review

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. postponed the signing of the proposed P6.35tn General Appropriations Act (GAA) which was originally scheduled for December 20. Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin stated that the President is personally leading the review and is consulting with major government departments. He also confirmed that Marcos will veto some items but did not disclose which items specifically. The rationale behind the items to be vetoed would be "in the interest of public welfare, to conform with the fiscal program, and in compliance with laws." Legislative leaders remain optimistic that the GAA will be signed before the year ends. It is to note that the GAA must be enacted before year-end to avoid a reenacted budget, which would limit the implementation of the government's new programs and initiatives.

International arrivals reach 5.31mn as of end-November, behind target

Data from the Department of Tourism (DoT) exhibited that tourist arrivals for the month of November declined slightly 2% YoY and MoM to 432k. This was 63% of 2019 average. Korean tourists remain as the top source of visitor arrivals but visitors from Korea was flat both YoY and MoM. Chinese tourists declined 19% YoY, marking five consecutive months of YoY decreases. The DoT stated that visa liberalization delays and geopolitical issues may have impacted projections, such as the DoT anticipated 2mn Chinese visitors with the e-visa system implementation which was suspended by the DFA. Bringing YT-November tourist arrivals to 5.3mn tourist arrivals, up 9% YoY and 71% of 2019 average. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, which is up 41% YoY or 93% of 2019 levels. 2023 tourist arrivals hit 5.5mn or 66% of pre-covid.

BIR confident in hitting downgraded P2.85tn collection target this year

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) stated it is on track to meet its downgraded P2.85tn collection target (+13% YoY). The target had been adjusted to account for unpassed legislation and weaker-than-expected GDP growth. Laws that were expected to be passed this year were estimated to have generated P107bn. These included taxes on single use plastics, pre-mixed alcoholic beverages, sweetened beverages and junk food, as well as the mining fiscal regime rationalization. For YT-November, the BIR collected P2.67tn (+14% YoY). Income tax collections rose 9% to P1.41tn. VAT collections are up 37% to P622.2bn. Excise taxes totaled P284bn, an increase of 9%. With alcoholic beverages and tobacco accounting for P103bn and P131bn respectively, other taxes category amounted to P214.7bn, posting 11% growth.

ERC denies early start for Meralco, San Miguel’s Masinloc power supply deal

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has denied the request of Meralco and San Miguel-led Masinloc Power Co. Ltd. (MPCL) to begin their Power Supply Agreement (PSA) ahead of the original Aug 26, 2025 schedule. ERC determined that awarding provisional authority for their PSA is “premature,” despite the successful competitive selection process (CSP). Recall that MPCL secured 500 MW of the 600 MW 15-year baseload power requirement that Meralco offered in a bidding last August. MPCL offered P5.6015 per kilowatt hour (kWh). MPCL owns the 659-MW coal-fired thermal power plant in Zambales.
