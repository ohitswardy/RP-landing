REGIS REPORT

12 January 2024

The index closed just slightly higher for the week at 6,643.18 (+0.20% WoW). Sentiment continued to be buoyed by expectations that the Fed would start lowering rates this year.

Inflation figures declined to 3.9% in Dec (vs 4.1% in Nov), falling to within the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target band of 2-4% as the prices of most commodity groups saw a drop in CPI inflation, except for rice.  Rice inflation for Dec rose to 19.6% from 13.5% as regional export prices continued to go up (MoM and YoY).  If it were not for rice, the headline CPI inflation number would be almost 2%, near the bottom of the BSP’s target range. Barring anything unusual, Regis estimates that CPI inflation is likely to dip further next month (Jan 2024) on base effects alone.

For the week, losers actually outnumbered gainers 18:13, but the strength in the banks carried the index to positive territory.

As sentiment improved, foreign investors continued to buy into the market. This week alone, the market saw US$47.2m worth of inflows – the highest weekly inflow since July last year.

Most of the inflows were skewed into the banks. BDO (+4.4% WoW), BPI (+2.4% WoW), and MBT (+5.4% WoW) saw US$19.9m, US$2.2m, and US$7.0m respectively.

Property names ended mixed. Ayala Land (-0.7% WoW) corrected following its recent rally while its peers SM Prime (+1.4% WoW) and Robinsons Land (+2.1% WoW) played catch up. Ayala Land President Anna Ma. Margarita “Meean” Dy reiterated her target to double net profit by 2028 (vs 2023), representing a 15% earnings CAGR (consensus FY23 net profit at P24.5bn).

Large cap Ayala Corp (-4.4% WoW) was the biggest drag to the market. Last Wednesday, Mitsubishi Corp sold US$8.6m shares of Ayala Corp at Php634.00/sh. This is a 9% discount to last Tuesday’s closing price.

Daily turnover improved to US$116.2m and foreign participation was higher than usual at 58%, mostly due to the AC placement.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Aboitiz Power, San Miguel submit lowest bids for Meralco 1,800MW power supply

Meralco announced that three bidders, out of six, submitted the lowest bids to supply it 1,800MW of power from Dec 2024. The three are GNPower Dinginin (300MW), Mariveles Power Generation (300MW) and Excellent Energy (1,200MW). GNPower Dinginin is a subsidiary of Aboitiz Power (AP PM), while the other two are subsidiaries of San Miguel Corp (SMC PM).

Banks’ NPL ratio eases in November

Banks’ NPL ratio stood at 3.41% in November, easing from the 3.44% clip in the previous month. In absolute terms, NPLs inched 1.1% MoM / 11.3% YoY to P454.28bn. Loans of the entire banking system (ex-interbank) grew 9.3% YoY in November. Past due loans rose 14.3% YoY, accounting for 4.22% of total loans (4.04% in Nov 2022). Meanwhile, restructured loans fell by 6.2%YoY, bringing the ratio to 2.29% of banks’ gross loan portfolio. NPL coverage ratio — which shows the allowance for potential losses due to bad loans — was lower at 101.47% (105.73% in Nov 2022).

Labor unions demand no job cuts once ICTSI takes over Durban port

The United National Transport Union and South African Transport and Allied Workers Union are demanding that ICTSI agree to no job cuts for the duration of its 25-year contract with state-run Transnet. Labor unions submitted their demands to ICTSI and Transnet before December but they have yet to get a response. Transnet said it has held numerous consultations with the two unions “primarily focused on the treatment of employees,” and said workers won’t lose their jobs as they cross over to the joint venture.

 

Last July, Transnet selected ICT to be its equity partner to run and expand Durban Container Terminal Pier 2. Under the agreement, ICTSI will pay an undisclosed amount to own 50%-1 share of the a newly formed JV company while its partner, Transnet will own 50%+1 share. Assets at the terminal are currently worth R2.5bn (~US$130mn) and at the end of the contract, Transnet will buy the shares back from the partner. The transaction is expected to be competed by April 2024.

ICTSI submits bid for management of Iloilo Commercial Port Complex

ICTSI confirmed that it submitted a bid for the 25-year concession of the Iloilo Commercial Port Complex (ICPC) last Jan 11. It is unclear, however, if the regulator, Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) also received bids from other companies. Recall that the PPA decided to re-bid the contract last month, after the lone bid ICTSI submitted was declared “failed” due to the lack of documents showing proof of authorization. If awarded, ICPC will be its first foray in the Visayas region and will add to its current global portfolio of 32 ports.

DITO, Converge ink deal for facilities sharing

Converge and DITO Telecommunity signed a Materials Facilities Provisioning Agreement, which serves as a framework for collaboration on the sharing of submarine fiber optic cable assets. “Our collaboration will allow us to reach more customers and deliver a better service with increased resiliency.” Converge CEO Dennis Anthony Uy, however, did not identify the areas or assets included in the collaboration. In 2023, total fiber assets of Converge has reached a span of  682,000 fiber kilometers with its household coverage covering 62.3% in the Philippines. DITO Telecommunity said it has more than 7,000 sites in over 850 cities and municipalities in the country.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

GIR drops MoM to US$102.45bn in December

GIR hit US$102.45bn in December. This is down from US$102.72bn in November due to payment of maturing foreign loans but up from US$96.15bn in 2022. This is equivalent to 7.7 months of imports.

Inflation cools further to 3.9% in December; 2023 average inflation rate at 6.0%

Headline inflation slowed to 3.9% YoY in December from 4.1% in November. This is slightly better than consensus: 4.0% and came in near the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.6-4.4%. Inflation of food and non-food items decelerated to 5.4%/2.6% in December from 5.7%/2.9%. Headline inflation averaged at 6.0% in 2023, higher than 5.8% in 2022. On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.2% in December, broadly the same rate as in November. Rice CPI grew at a faster MoM rate of 3.5% in December from 2.7% in November and accounted for bulk of the acceleration in food items (+0.7% MoM in Dec vs +0.2% in Nov).

 

Non-food items dipped -0.2% MoM, slowing further from +0.1% in Nov thanks to lower electricity prices and fuel prices. The average MoM change for the whole of 2023 was +0.3%, slower than +0.6% in 2022. Core inflation (excluding volatile food and fuel items) decelerated to 4.4% from 4.7%. Regis anticipates the BSP will begin cutting rates in 2Q24 (50bps), with the rest of the cuts (100bps) by 2H24.

DOF expects new taxes to contribute to 2024 revenue

Finance Secretary Diokno expects the following tax laws to be passed in 1H24 (and thus contribute to 2024 revenue).

*Tax Package 4 – which will lower stock transaction tax, make taxes on interest and dividends uniform (at 15%)

*Value added tax on digital service providers

*Excise tax on single-use plastic bags

*Excise tax on sweetened beverages and junk food

 

He said that these have already gone through the Lower House but are still pending with the Senate. The DOF is also pushing for the passing of the following tax laws. Tax Package 3 or Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform and VAT refund for non-resident tourists.

Finance Secretary says BSP benchmark rates could drop 1ppt in 2024

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno estimates that the BSP can cut its benchmark rates by up to 100bps this year. The BSP RRP rate is currently 6.5%. He said that “a 75 basis point cut by the Fed this year could actually be matched by the  central bank or it could be even 100 basis points”. Meanwhile, Regis expects the benchmark rate to drop by 150 basis points in 2024.

 

Oath-taking of Ralph Recto and Frederick Go to be held.

The Presidential Communication Office announced that Batangas Rep. Ralph Recto will take his oath as Finance Secretary. Mr. Recto is a House Deputy speaker, a former three-term senator, and former NEDA Secretary. Mr. Frederick Go will also take his oath as Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs. Mr. Go was President of Robinsons Land (RLC PM) for 17 years.
