REGIS REPORT

09 August 2024

The PSEi posted its first weekly gain in 3 weeks, outperforming a mostly lower region, as shares managed to find support after a turbulent start to the week, with investors taking reprieve from an as-expected GDP reading, but at the same time kept hopes for a potential BSP rate cut next week as data showed that private consumption stood below pre-pandemic levels. Stocks initially tracked the widespread bloodbath across risk assets earlier in the week with the index posting a steep -2.58% slump on Monday, its 2nd biggest 1-day this year, as risk sentiment across the globe was bogged down by concerns over a slowing US economy. Local shares then managed to find better footing from there as investors digested a busy batch of macro data and corporate results on the ground, and the benchmark ultimately ended the week higher by +0.64% WoW at 6,647.

On the macro front, CPI data released on Tuesday showed that inflation accelerated to 9-month high of 4.4% in July, higher than the 4.1% consensus forecast. The key drivers behind this were electricity, food, and transport costs. Following the release of the data, BSP Governor Remolona stated that the expected August rate cut is now “a little bit less likely,” which marks a contrast from his previous stance where he indicated a potential of up to 50bps of rate cuts before the year ends. The governor however noted that an off-cycle cut is a possibility – note that the next BSP policy meeting after next week’s (August 15) is as far back as October 17. Later in the week, 2Q GDP growth came in as expected at 6.3%, accelerating from 1Q’s 5.8% and matching the best pace since 4Q2022. This was primarily driven by government spending which grew by 10.7%. But on the other end, private consumption grew by just 4.6%, below pre-pandemic levels, which also mirrors the weakness in 2Q results of most consumer companies. The weakness in consumption however presents a motivation for the BSP to cut rates on Thursday. Note that despite the recent comments from the BSP Governor, consensus forecast currently sees a 25bp cut in the forthcoming meeting (9 cuts vs 6 holds in Bloomberg survey).

On the results front, similar from last week’s trend we continued to see weakness across topline growth numbers of most consumer companies. URC, which was the most battered stock this week -12.96% WoW, and MONDE +1.91% both recorded 1H topline growths of just +3% YoY. URC’s recurring profit grew by +8% YoY, trailing Regis but inline with consensus, while MONDE’s +46% core profit jump was ahead of both Regis and consensus. Meanwhile CNPF +1.49% recorded a much healthier 1H topline growth of +13% YoY, but its 1H net profit growth of +14% YoY still mildly trailed behind Regis forecast but inline with street consensus.

In the property space, most developers recorded a decline in pre-sales with SMPH +1.03% WoW posting a steep -58% YoY and -48% QoQ contraction in its 2Q reservation sales, marking a reversal from the rebound recorded in 1Q, while MEG’s -2.75% dipped by -12% YoY and QoQ, extending its decline from 1Q. One standout however was ALI +3.81% which managed to grow its reservation sales by +15% YoY and 5% QoQ in 2Q.

Another bright spot in this week’s busy batch of reporters was GLO +3.52% WoW which recorded a +18% YoY surge in its 1H core profit to P11.7b, inline with Regis forecast. Mobile revenue growth in 2Q looked healthy at +6% YoY, while contributions from Mynt/GCash in 1H stood at P2.14b, offsetting the weakness in its home broadband business which recorded a revenue slump of -5% YoY in 2Q. More details about this week’s busy round of corporate results in our analysts’ notes below.

Lastly, another key event this week was the PSEi rebalancing on Friday. As previously announced, there were no new additions or deletions in the main index, but there some slight weight reductions in BPI and BLOOM due to the reductions in their public float levels. Carl estimated the outflow for BPI at $7.5m or roughly 2 days worth of turnover, while BLOOM $5m or 4 days. Both stocks ended the week on a lower note BPI -3.04% WoW and BLOOM -3.07%.

Market activity this week was moderate at a daily average of $90m +10% WoW. The rise from last week appears to be local investor driven as foreign participation eased to just 44% vs 55% in the previous week. Net foreign flow remained skewed to sell for the 2nd week in a row by nearly $30m. Bulk of this however was in the earlier part of the week, and foreign investors have turned net buyers for the past 2 sessions, albeit very lightly. Top outflow stocks for the week were URC -12.96%, ICT +2.23%, SMPH +1.03%, AEV -8.04% and lenders MBT -1.24% and BDO +2.27%; while top inflows were GLO +3.52%, CNPF +1.49% and BPI -3.04%.

Ayala Land - 2Q24 reservation sales grow 15% YoY, led by premium segment
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H24 earnings rise 15%, in line

Ayala Land’s 2Q24 net profit rose 9% QoQ (-1% YoY) to P6.8bn. This brought 1H24 earnings up 15% to P13.1bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Reservation sales were healthy and management remains optimistic on the outlook ofthe premium residential segment. However, the company also warned that the lower margin levels in 1H24 could persist since some margin compression was necessary to achieve its aim of improving product quality.

Reservation sales led by premium segment

Despite the relatively high base in 2Q23, 2Q24 reservation sales grew 15% YoY (+5% QoQ) to P35.1bn, just slightly below the pre-COVID level. For the first half, reservation sales rose 17%, led by the premium segment, which grew 23% (core segment sales increased 9%). Management said that premium vertical projects performed particularly well.

Unsold inventory level improves

The unsold residential inventory level dropped to 19 months (21 months in 1Q24) and management plans to reduce this to 16-18 months. To achieve this, the company trimmed its FY24 launch target to P85bn (from P115bn previously).

Mall revenue growth likely to slow down

1H24 mall revenue grew 8% (+7% in 2Q24) on the back of 7% same-mall revenue growth. However, revenue growth will likely slow down going forward since the company will grant 1-2 months of rental concessions to tenants affected by mall redevelopments.

Office occupancy much better than industry average

1H24 office revenue grew 6% YoY. While office occupancy will likely drop by 1ppt due to the POGO ban, the company’s occupancy (currently at 91%) should remain well above the industry average of 84%.

Lower margins could persist

We estimate that operating margin fell c. 2ppt YoY due to discounts on selected residential units and quality improvement initiatives in the leasing businesses. The company said these margin levels would likely persist as it spends more to improve the quality of its offerings (such as redevelopment of malls/hotels).

Direct impact from POGO ban will be limited

Management expects limited impact from the recently announced POGO ban because only 1% of its office space is occupied by POGO firms and only 1% of its residential sales are to mainland Chinese. Furthermore, less than 5% of its residential units are occupied by POGO employees. While there will also be indirect repercussions from the ban, we expect Ayala Land to be among the least affected developers since it has limited exposure to the Manila Bay area.

 
SM Prime - 2Q24 mall revenue growth accelerates but reservation sales drop sharply
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Earnings are in line but presales fall sharply

SM Prime’s 2Q24 net profit rose 16% YoY (+11% QoQ) to P11.6bn. This brought 1H24 earnings up 13% to P22.1bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. While the company’s mall revenue growth accelerated in 2Q24, the most noteworthy portion of the press release was the sharp drop in reservation sales.

Reservation sales drop again

After a substantial QoQ rebound in 1Q24, 2Q24 reservation sales fell 48% QoQ (down 58% YoY) to P13.7bn. The disclosure to the PSE provided limited information but we hope to get more details during the SM Investments briefing on August 7. We suspect that the weakness largely came from Metro Manila condominiums.

Mall revenue growth accelerates, partly due to timing of holidays

2Q24 mall revenue growth (of Philippines and China combined) accelerated to 10% YoY (from 7% in 1Q24), partly due to the timing of the Easter holidays (2Q in 2023, 1Q in 2024). Nevertheless, 1H24 mall revenue growth of 8% is stronger than the revenue growth of many consumer companies.

Office and hotel revenues rise

2Q24 hotel revenue grew 18% YoY, equivalent to the growth in the number of hotel rooms. Meanwhile, office revenue rose 5% QoQ (+18% YoY), possibly indicating an improvement in occupancy (from 85% in 1Q24).

POGO ban to be negative for Pasay Coastal Development

We expect the recent ban on the POGO sector to be negative for property in the Manila Bay area, including SM Prime’s Coastal Development Project, which comprises 27% (P11.48/share) of our SMPH NAV estimate. If selling prices of the project fall 20%, then SM Prime’s NAV (of P42.33/share) could drop by 5%-10%.

1H profits rise 10% to P40bn

1H revenue for SM Retail rose 4% to P197bn, implying 2Q revenue growth of 6%. Net income for 2Q alone was P4.6bn, a tad higher (+2%) YoY. Globe Telecom - 2Q24: Mobile segment still healthy; Mynt delivers 20% of core income
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core profit up 18% to P11.7bn for 1H

Globe reported net income of P14.5bn for 1H24, up slightly (+1%) from a year ago. Core income (ex- exceptional items) came in at P11.7bn, +18% YoY. Mobile revenue remains healthy, but the broadband business has been unable to reverse its decline. EBITDA margin for 1H was 52% (vs 50%), which was likely driven in part by the sale-and-leaseback of its telecom towers. Also noteworthy is that the profit contribution of fintech unit Mynt is now 20% of 2Q core income.

Mobile revenues rise 6% in 2Q, but broadband is still contracting

The core mobile segment reported revenue of P29.3bn in 2Q, up a respectable 6% YoY. 1H mobile revenue was up 7% YoY. Management noted that the competitive environment remains benign with pricing moves “rational”.

The home broadband business, though, is still under pressure with the fixed wireless segment still contracting. Revenues were down 2% QoQ and 5% YoY. One silver lining is the 23k net adds for wired broadband in 2Q (likely dominated by prepaid), an improvement over 1Q (+19k).

2Q contributions from Mynt at P1.2bn; transactions validate US$5bn valuation

Mynt, which operates GCash, contributed P1.18bn to associate income in 2Q (+22% QoQ), taking 1H contributions to P2.14bn. This implies Mynt’s profit for the half was P6bn, based on 36% ownership as of June. Profit is still rising sharply.

The recently announced transactions by Ayala Corp (AC) and MUFJ to buy 8% each of Mynt will, however, dilute Globe’s ownership to 34.3% at a valuation of US$5bn (approximating our valuation estimate). Globe will recognize a P2.7bn gain from the ownership dilution.

Capex down 25% in 1H, will be below US$1bn next year

For 1H24, capex amounted to P28.3bn, down 25%. This puts Globe on track to meet its FY24 capex guidance of US$1bn (P58bn). Globe is guiding for capex to be below US$1bn in FY25.

 
Universal Robina - 2Q24: Top line challenged amidst persistent downtrading
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H24 recurring net profit +8% YoY - behind

URC’s recurring net profit for 1H rose 8% YoY to P7.2bn, behind Regis but in line with consensus forecasts. Revenues (+3% YoY) are trending slightly behind expectations, though consolidated operating margin of 11.7% is in line. Top line softness persisted in 2Q24 with consolidated revenues practically flat YoY as PH branded consumer food (BCF) succumbed to downtrading, while unfavorable prices dragged non-BCF sales. EBIT grew mildly (2% YoY) driven by strong BCF margins (+200bps YoY).

BCF: Top line challenged amidst downtrading

Total BCF sales were flattish YoY as weak consumer sentiment persisted across key markets, particularly in the Philippines. PH BCF sales contracted 4% YoY due to unfavorable pricing and mix as consumers continued to down-trade. While volumes grew 5% YoY on a relatively high base (trade restocking in 2Q23 following 1Q23 SAP transition), growth was driven by value products across majority of its categories. Favorable input prices and cost management strategies supported EBIT margins for the segment (>100bps YoY for PH BCF / >400bps YoY for int’l BCF).

Non-BCF dragged by unfavorable prices

Non-BCF sales declined 2% YoY during the quarter due to lower prices even as volumes continued to grow. The SURE (sugar and renewables) segment was negatively impacted by lower prices – segment revenues contracted 2% YoY while EBIT dropped 48% YoY during the quarter.

Softer top line seen amidst protracted demand recovery

Management expects top line challenges to persist even if inflationary pressures are easing, as consumers are likely to remain cost conscious in the near term. Amidst this environment, management downgraded its FY24 top line growth guidance to low- to mid-single digit (mid- to high-single digit previously). The bottom line (ex-SURE), however, is still expected to grow double digit as input costs remain favorable.

 
Monde Nissin - 2Q24: Instant noodles volumes recover; margin expansion tempered
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H24 core net profit +46% YoY – ahead

MONDE’s core net profit jumped 46% YoY, ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. 1H24 net sales of P40.1bn (+3% YoY) is in line with expectations, but margins are tracking ahead. For 2Q24 alone, core net profit grew 37% YoY. Net sales grew 4% on strong APAC BFB sales while Quorn sales contracted 3% YoY. Consolidated margins continued to expand YoY but contracted QoQ due to unfavorable forex and higher A&P spending for both APAC BFB and Quorn.

2Q24 sales driven by strong noodle volumes

APAC BFB sales rose 6% YoY in 2Q24 driven mainly by strong instant noodle sales volumes (+10% YoY). This was partly due to the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23) which results in fewer selling days. MONDE’s market share inched higher (+170bps YoY / +50bps QoQ) as it continued to gain traction with its value SKUs (Kasalo pack). Meanwhile, biscuit sales contracted 2% YoY due to an unscheduled shutdown of one of its lines. This has been resolved and volumes are expected to recover in the coming quarters.

Margins contract QoQ due to higher A&P spending, unfavorable forex

APAC BFB gross margin were wider YoY in 2Q24 (+390bps) due to favorable wheat and palm oil prices. EBITDA margins, however, expanded less as the company reinvested GPM gains into higher A&P. Unfavorable forex also hit margins QoQ as majority of raw materials are imported.

Top line momentum to be sustained but EBITDA margins should ease

Management expects to sustain mid-single digit growth for noodles and biscuits in the Philippines for the balance of the year. Meanwhile, gross margins will continue to be supported by favorable input cost for wheat and palm oil with majority of requirements for the year already locked in. The reinvestment of gross margin gains into A&P, however, should temper EBITDA margin improvement.

1H24 profit at P12.59bn (-35% YoY) – In line

Profit in 2Q dropped 39% YoY, 5% QoQ to P6.13bn, bringing 1H24 profit down 35% YoY to P12.59bn. We would describe the results to be broadly in line with Regis and consensus estimates. However, management’s surprise pronouncement regarding the potential 2-year extension of the mining unit’s tax holiday, as well as the better-than-expected power plant availability guidance, are material upside risks to our near- to medium-term earnings and dividend forecasts.

Mining unit profit declines further in 2Q on lower ASPs

The coal mining unit saw 2Q profits drop 22% QoQ/51% YoY, as coal mining ASPs fell 7% QoQ/33% YoY to P2,780/MT. Sales volumes were 18% YoY higher at 9.4mn MT in 1H. The biggest surprise to us, however, was management’s pronouncement that it is confident it can secure a 2-year tax holiday (ITH) extension soon. We did not assume SCC would continue to enjoy tax holidays beyond July 2024. Thus, a positive outcome could lift our FY24E-25E profit forecasts by 8%-20%, ceteris paribus.

Power unit posts record quarterly profit in 2Q on higher ASPs

The power unit had a blockbuster quarter, as profits rose QoQ/YoY to a quarterly record of P2.3bn. Even though SCPC 2 was under rehab for most of the quarter, EBITDA still managed to rise by 3% YoY and 44% QoQ to P3.67bn, thanks to elevated spot prices. After posting 87% availability factor, management guided for an availability factor of 83% for the year, well above our forecast of 64%. Every 10ppt improvement in our availability estimate could raise EPS by 5%.

Waiting game not over on COC renewal but management very positive

Management is still waiting for the final decision of the Department of Energy regarding its application to renew its Coal Operating Contract (COC). Management reiterated its confidence in a positive outcome soon. Meanwhile, they also conveyed confidence on a special dividend announcement in 2H.

1H24 profit falls 31% to P11bn – In line with Street

Profit contributions from DMCI Homes fell 41% YoY to P737mn in 2Q24, after booked revenues dropped 41%. While 2Q24 reservation sales fell 14% QoQ to P9.76bn, 1H24 reservation sales are up 28% YoY to P21bn, on trend to match or exceed pre-Covid levels (P37bn). Inventory was flat QoQ at P70bn, equivalent to 1.9 years’ worth of sales.

Construction unit still suffering from soft market

The construction unit’s profit improvement of 73% YoY in 2Q24 was largely driven by lower non-cash and non-operating items. Booked revenues fell 14% YoY while EBITDA dropped 7% due to general slowdown in construction activity from the private sector (i.e. buildings and private sector-led infra projects). The order book marginally improved to P41bn.

Maynilad’s standalone profit rose 32% YoY in 2Q24, on the back of higher tariffs and billed volumes. However, we are concerned about the 14% YoY drop in capex during 2Q, which took 1H24 capex down 3% YoY to P8.2bn, lagging its annual commitment of P21bn. We suspect this could just be a timing issue, but it bears watching.

Permitting delays, weak market hit nickel unit but 2025 prospects strong

Net losses at the nickel unit persisted in 2Q, after shipments and ASPs fell 34% YoY and 53%, respectively. The segment suffered from the permitting delays, but management is confident it can secure all the necessary permits for its mines in Zambales and Palawan within the year so it can sell at least 3.5mn WMT annually by 2025. This would be up >2x from the 1.7mn WMT it sold in 2023.

Favorable weather, improved plant availability boost off-grid unit

The off-grid power plants posted its strongest quarterly profit of P354mn in 2Q24, up 34% QoQ and 53% YoY. Management attributed this to the warm weather and improved availability.

Issuing convertible prefs to fund cement acquisition

The DMCI group is in the process of securing PCC approval for its planned purchase of Cemex Phils (CHP PM). Management hopes to secure the approval within the 3rd week of Aug and start the process for the planned mandatory tender offer thereafter. Management has abandoned its original plan of issuing long-term bonds to fund the acquisition and is now planning a private placement of convertible prefs instead, given the latter’s tax advantages and speedier process. Management reiterated its intention of maintaining dividend payments despite the large acquisition.

by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H24 EBITDA of P13bn ahead of estimates but profit disappoints

Excluding FX and other one-offs, core profit rose 4% YoY to P3.17bn in 1H, trailing REGIS estimates. EBITDA of P13.3bn (+39% YoY), however, is well ahead of forecast. Higher financing costs related to additional aircraft and spare engines weighed on 2Q profit, which fell 40% QoQ, 30% YoY to P1.19bn. 2Q24 pax volumes up 9% QoQ, fares drop 8% on mix

Stronger 25E seat growth guidance of +20%, lower capex

Meanwhile, management guided for seat capacity of +20% in 2025, higher than our forecast of +11%, as it plans to unground aircraft and improve fleet utilization. Given the heavy buildup of aircraft and engines since last year, management also reckons 25E capex should drop substantially from this year’s peak of >P60bn but a firmer guidance will be provided in the next briefings. Lastly, the company clarified that while it has ordered 152 aircraft, only 70 of these are “firm”, giving CEB the flexibility to adjust fleet growth beyond 2029.

Power unit outperforms

The power segment saw a massive turnaround in 2Q, with profit contributions up >4x QoQ to P279mn. Management attributed the stellar performance to (1) the completion of the 72MW solar expansion, which brought capacity to 172MW and (2) higher spot prices. Management plans to start the construction of another 145MW solar plant next month, which it hopes to complete by 4Q25.

HPAL plants still loss-making

Despite the (temporary) spike in LME nickel prices in 2Q, equity losses from Taganito (THNC) and Coral Bay (CBNC) were flat QoQ at P194mn. Management hopes that the opening of the Bulanjao mine in 4Q, which has higher grade limonite, as well as the prospect of better pricing with Sumitomo, could provide some relief in succeeding periods.

2Q24 GDP accelerates to 6.3%

GDP expanded by 6.3% in 2Q24, accelerating from the 5.8% (revised from 5.7%) pace in 1Q24 and 4.3% in 2Q23, in line with the consensus forecast of 6.3%. This brings 1H24 GDP to 6.0% (vs. Regis FY24E: 6% and government’s 6-7%).

On the demand side, domestic demand grew 7.9%, the fastest pace since 1Q23 following a strong rebound in government spending (+10.7%) and capital formation (+11.5%). Private consumption grew at a steady pace of 4.6%, but remained below pre-pandemic trends (5.4%). National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) Secretary Arsenio M. Balisacan described household consumption growth of 4.6% as “anemic.” Government spending rebounded by 10.7% in 2Q24, after declining by 7.1% in 2Q23. Capital formation went up by 11.5%, after construction activity rebounded (+16% YoY in 2Q24 vs 6.9% in 1Q24 and 2.5% in 2Q23). Public construction grew 22% in 2Q24 from 12% in 2Q23; Private construction rose 10% from 5%; Commercial construction grew 14%. Strong fixed capital formation was also aided by slower growth in inventory withdrawals. Net imports turned positive, reversing last year’s net exports and weighing on overall GDP, albeit mildly (-0.9ppts).

On the supply side, the industry sector grew at a faster rate of 7.7%, boosted by strong construction growth while service sector grew a solid 6.8%. Agriculture sector declined by 2.3%, primarily driven by the decline of Palay by 9.5%.

Headline inflation accelerates to 4.4% in July

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported that headline CPI rose 4.4% in July, accelerating from 3.7% in June. This was above consensus estimate of 4.1% but came in the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.0%-4.8%. Key drivers were electricity prices (-5.4% YoY in July vs. -13.7% in June), food prices (+6.4% YoY vs. 6.1%), and fuel prices for transport (+6.4% YoY vs. 3.8%). While rice inflation moderated to 20.9% in July from 22.5% in June, it remained elevated; inflation of other food items quickened (meat, fruits, vegetables, and corn). However, unfavorable base effects on rice CPI should start fading by August due to falling Vietnam rice prices and the rice tariff cut last July 5.

As of Aug 6, the Department of Agriculture indicated that prices of imported well-milled rice ranged P51-53/kg and regular milled rice ranged P46-48/kg in NCR. The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that NCR retail prices of well-milled rice averaged at P53.1/kg while regular milled rice averaged P45.88/kg in July 2024. In Aug 2023: NCR retail prices averaged P47.06/kg (well-milled) and P42.70/kg (regular).

Core inflation eased to 2.9% in July from 3.1% in June, with non-food “core” inflation steady at 3.3%. The average 7M24 headline inflation rate stood at 3.7%, within the BSP’s 2-4% target band. The BSP said that the latest inflation outturn is consistent with the BSP’s projections that inflation will temporarily rise above target in July but could follow a general downtrend beginning in August. Monetary Board will consider the latest inflation outturn and 2Q24 GDP (to be released on Aug 8) in its policy meeting on Aug 15. However, BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said the BSP "is a little bit less likely [to cut rates] kasi mataas ng inflation (because inflation is high).” His statements were made at the sidelines of the official launch of the Financial Services Cyber Resilience Plan. BSP is also "always open" to the possibility of an off-cycle easing, particularly if (GDP) growth is unexpectedly weak.

Remolona says: BSP can hold rates ‘for the time being’

During a budget hearing, BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said that “evolving inflation conditions show the BSP can hold its policy settings steady for the time being.” If price pressures continue to ease, it will be possible for the BSP to consider a less restrictive monetary policy stance. Previously, Governor Remolona said the BSP may cut rates by a total of 50bps this year, with 25bps set on Aug 15 and another 25bps in 4Q.

Government workers get salary hike

President Marcos approved Executive Order 64, which sets the four-tranche (2024-2027) salary increases of government employees under the Salary Standardization Law. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) earmarked P70bn in the proposed national budget for 2025 to cover the additional cost requirements for the first 2 tranches (i.e. 2024 and 2025). Some P36bn have already been set aside for the first tranche under the 2024 General Appropriations Act.

DBM will finalize the guidelines but salary adjustments including the differential and back pay would be received by government workers at the “soonest” possible time. A typical government employee under Salary Grade 15, as an example, will have a salary increase of ~4% this year to P41,296; EO 64 mandates that increase is retroactive to Jan 1, 2024. By January 1, 2027, the fourth and last tranche of the initiative could increase the Salary Grade 15 employee’s maximum salary by P7,805 to P47,172 compared to the 2023 rates (~4% CAGR).

DBM said the salary increase for government workers is expected to benefit 165,007 sub-professionals; 1,170,647 professionals, such as teachers and lawyers; and 22,640 personnel in executive functions.
