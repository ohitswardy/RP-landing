REGIS REPORT

22 March 2024

The PSEi capped the week on a higher note, +0.87% WoW, recovering from the steep drop that it suffered during the FTSE rebalancing last week as investors took relief from the FOMC's guidance for 3 rate cuts this year. The benchmark started the week off on a positive note, bouncing up by +0.45% on Monday after its steep -2.09% selloff last Friday. It then settled into a flattish trend within the 6800-level through the next 2 sessions as investors awaited Fed policy announcement. Upon the FOMC's guidance for rate cuts this year, local shares then tracked the widespread uptick across the region and rallied +1.55% to a 5-day high of 6963. Shares however failed to hold the momentum today and ultimately ended the week below the 6900-level.

Best performing stock in the benchmark this week was EMI +11.38% WoW with the headwind from its FTSE deletion now out of the way. Other notable winners were DMC +10.38%, after its sub SCC +3.86% declared a sizable 3.5/share dividend, and CNVRG +8.72% after announcing results earlier in the week. We note however that while the +22% rise in net profits appear to be better than street estimates, underlying operating results do not look as good as headlines suggest (research comments below). On the opposite end, among the worst performers were BLOOM -4.5% as the company officially announced the settlement of its long-standing case with GGAM which requires BLOOM, through its subsidiary Sureste, to buy 921.2m shares from the latter for $300m, which translates to a price per share of about ~18, a massive premium from its current price of 11.04.

Average daily turnover this week stood at $118m, -25% WoW after last week's rebal-boosted activity. Net foreign flow turned skewed to buy by $8.5m vs last week's sharp outflow of $55.5m. Top foreign bought stocks were lenders BPI +2.62% WoW and MBT +5.7%, followed ICT +1.08% and SM -0.1%. Top sold meanwhile were this week's laggards ALI -2.47%, URC -3.56% and AC 4.46%.

Jollibee Foods Corp. - Int’l business underperforms in 4Q23; long term targets set
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY23 recurring net profit to common P7.4bn - behind

JFC’s FY23 recurring net profit to common was P7.4bn (+288% YoY), behind expectations. Consolidated revenues of P244.1bn (+15% YoY) were broadly in line, but operating profit of P14.4bn (+36% YoY on a recurring basis) came in slightly below estimates. 4Q23 revenues grew 8% YoY while operating profit was flat YoY on a recurring basis. The Philippine business continued to perform strongly in 4Q (EBIT +12% YoY), but the international segmentreverted to an EBIT loss due to softness at CBTL and Highlands Coffee.

Philippines 4Q23 SSSG slows; A&P spending catches up

SSSG in the Philippines slowed to +5.4% in 4Q (+13% in 3Q). “Champion” brands (Jollibee, Chowking, Mang Inasal) continued to grow strongly (+7% SSSG) but overall performance was dragged by competitive pressures at Red Ribbon (-10% SSSG). Segment operating margins expanded only 30bps despite more favorable input cost as JFC reinvested these gains into A&P. FY23 segment EBIT came in at P14bn, in line with expectations.

Int’l business dragged by CBTL, Highlands Coffee

The international segment reverted to an EBIT loss of P992mn in 4Q23, but 18% smaller vs the EBIT loss in 4Q22. Segment EBIT settled at P447mn for FY23, behind our estimates. The 4Q23 performance was a mixed bag: China continued to grow strongly (+22% SSSG) on the reopening momentum, while Smashburger’s EBITDA (P236mn in 4Q23) continued to improve. On the flipside, CBTL faced competitive pressures in North America, while Highlands Coffee was dragged by macro headwinds in Vietnam.

Long term target: NPAT 3x by 2028 (+25% CAGR)

For 2024, JFC is looking to achieve 10-14% systemwide sales growth (Regis FY24E: +9%) on the back of continued store expansion and 5-7% SSSG (Regis FY24E: +4.8%). Management is expecting operating profit to grow 10-15% as a result, which translates to P15.9-16.5bn (Regis FY24E: P16.9bn). JFC also plans to open 700-750 stores in 2024. Longer term, management expects to grow net profit 3x by 2028 (P24bn). This implies 25% profit CAGR over the next five years.

 
Aboitiz Power - Shutdowns hit 4Q; massive capex looms
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q core income disappoints; big jump in capex

Aboitiz Power’s core income reached P32bn, up 27% YoY, implying 4Q core income of P5.2bn (-20% YoY) – a disappointment. 4Q23 core income was the lowest figure since 1Q22. As we suspected, shutdowns at its biggest units were a major factor in the profit decline for the quarter. For 2024, the company is earmarking a huge increase in capex in line with its ambitious RE rollout plan.

Shutdowns at its coal plants

There were shutdowns at one unit each at GMEC (unplanned) and GNPD (scheduled) in Dec. The affected units had a combined attributable capacity of >700MW. “Beneficial” EBITDA for its generation/RES segment fell 13% QoQ to P13.9bn as a result. The affected units are now back online.

Huge jump in capex to pursue RE targets

To support its aim of adding 3,600MW of RE capacity by 2030, AP is budgeting P73bn (US$1.3bn) of consolidated capex this year, with AP’s ‘beneficial’ share at P56bn. Seventy-two percent of the capex will be for RE projects. For perspective, AP’s capex was P18bn in 2022 and P26bn in 2023. Note that the P73bn capex has yet to include the investment in the LNG complex (power plants + terminal) with Meralco. We estimate the equity component of that acquisition will cost US$260m.

 

Margins steady; power price outlook

FY23 margins for its generation business were virtually unchanged (at P1.87/kwh) despite headline selling prices falling ~20%. This suggests the drop in power prices was mostly fuel-price-driven. Management expects power prices to soften in the near term but to remain high relative to history. This view seems to be a result of the expected entry of the 1,200MW+ LNG facility of Excellent Energy (part of the LNG complex AP is investing in) late this year. We have penciled in Excellent Energy’s capacity to be fully available only in 2026. Note Luzon’s peak demand last year was 12,300MW. Growth of 5% p.a. would imply that over 600MW of new capacity is needed every year.

DU volumes up 6%

Electricity sales at its distribution utilities were up 6% for FY23. Management is budgeting volume growth accelerating to 6.6% this year. This is probably driven by expectations of improved industrial demand as well as expectations of higher atmospheric temperatures due to El Nino.

Dividend outlook

AP declared a dividend of P2.30/share, equivalent to ~50% of prior year’s income. Despite the record capex budget this year, AP does not foresee a reduction in dividends. Net debt as of end-2023 was virtually unchanged YoY at P178bn, while gearing declined to 0.9x (vs 1.0x).

 
Bloomberry Resorts - BLOOM settles with GGAM; will buy back 921mn shares for US$300mn
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

BLOOM to effectively buy back shares at P18.32/share

Bloomberry has reached a settlement with Global Gaming Philippines (GGAM) wherein the company (through a wholly-owned subsidiary) will effectively purchase 921mn BLOOM shares (8% of current shares outstanding) from GGAM at P18.32/shares (P16.9bn). The settlement would have a meaningful impact on BLOOM’s financials, particularly its gearing level (see below) but ends a decade-long overhang.

How did the dispute between BLOOM and GGAM come about?

GGAM had a management service agreement (MSA) to operate Solaire Entertainment City. In addition to a profit-sharing agreement, GGAM also had an option to purchase 921mn BLOOM shares (at P1.67/share), which it exercised.

However, in September 2013, Bloomberry terminated GGAM’s contract due to “material breach” of the MSA (which GGAM contested). The two parties submitted their dispute to a 3-member arbitral tribunal in Singapore. In 2019, the arbitral tribunal awarded c. US$290mn (+6% annual interest) to GGAM. This was broken down as: c. US$190mn to acquire the 921mn BLOOM shares (at P11.04/share) held by GGAM and c. US$100mn for other damages.

GGAM did not file the required petition to enforce the arbitral award in the Philippines. However, it sued BLOOM and its Chairman, Mr. Enrique K. Razon, Jr. (and US companies associated with Mr. Razon), in the US District Court in Southern District of New York (in 2021) to enforce the arbitral award. BLOOM and GGAM have finally agreed to settle their dispute, with BLOOM agreeing to purchase GGAM’s 921mn shares at P18.32/share (through one of its subsidiaries).

What is the impact to BLOOM?

The settlement between BLOOM and GGAM will raise net debt (by P16.9bn), reduce equity (by P16.9bn), and (effectively) lower shares outstanding (by 921mn shares or 8%). As a result:

  • Gearing - We estimate that FY23 consolidated net debt/equity will rise from 1.2X to 2.0X. We also estimate that FY23 net debt/EBITDA will rise from 2.9X to 3.7X.
  • EPS – If BLOOM were to raise P16.9bn in debt at an interest rate of 7%, we estimate that 2025E EPS would only drop by c. 1% (due to the reduction in shares outstanding).
  • Target price – Our initial estimates suggest that if we were to maintain our target EV/EBITDA multiple of 10.1X (as well as account for the higher net debt and lower shares outstanding), then our target price would drop by c. 3% to P14.60/share.

Initial estimates on financial impact of BLOOM settlement

First Gen may shut down Pantabangan plant sooner than expected

First Gen may need to close its 132MW Pantabangan-Masiway hydroelectric plant earlier than expected due to decreasing water levels caused by El Niño according to Richard P. Difuntorum, complex head of the Pantabangan-Masiway and Casecnan hydro plants. The shutdown typically starts in third week of April. Data from the National Irrigation Administration (NIA) showed that the water elevation at the Pantabangan Dam is projected to reach 177.80 meters by March 27. The current water elevation at the dam was at 179.9 meters, below the 207 meters required for the plant to operate at its full capacity. Currently, the Pantabangan plant produces 40 MW, which is c. 30% of its installed capacity. Data from the National Grid Corp. of the Philippines showed that the Luzon grid has an available generating capacity of 14,709 MW and a system peak demand of 11,284 MW.

Arguably a silver lining in the results was CNVRG’s announcement that it added 80k subscribers (net) in 4Q, of which 47k were postpaid. These are, frankly, good numbers, in the context of the numbers previously reported by PLDT and Globe. However , we need to be convinced that these numbers will stick and will translate to sustainable revenue.

Guidance for FY24.  For FY24, management is guiding for 7-8% growth in revenue with EBITDA margins of 58-59% and ROIC of 15-16%. The guidance figures imply 2-5% growth in FY24 EBITDA.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government budget surplus widens to P88bn in January

The government recorded a budget surplus of P88bn in January, up 92% YoY. Revenues grew 21% YoY, as tax collections rose 25% YoY. BIR collections jumped 31% due to the shift in VAT remittance from monthly to quarterly, pushing the crediting of Q4 2023 collections over to January 2024. Meanwhile, BOC collections were up 4%. Non-tax revenues declined due to reduced collections from the national government’s share from Malampaya proceeds. Disbursements rose 10% YoY due to a 58% jump in interest payments. Primary spending was up just 2% YoY. The government is targeting to lower the budget deficit by 10% YoY to P1.36tn in 2024, equivalent to ~5% of GDP.

Remittances rise 2.7% YoY in January

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.7% YoY to US$2.84bn in January. In PHP terms, remittances rose 4.5% YoY. Land-based remittances from USA (+2.7%), Saudi Arabia (+5.7%), UAE (+7.7%), and Singapore (+4.2%) contributed the most to growth this month. The BSP is projecting remittances to grow by 3% to US$34.5bn in 2024.

BSP sees lower current account deficit in 2024

The BSP downgraded its 2024 current account deficit target to US$6.1bn (1.3% of GDP) from US$9.5bn (2.0%) previously. The 2024 target is also lower than the preliminary 2023 current account deficit of US$11.2bn (2.6% of GDP). The downgrade reflects lower projected trade deficit of US$69bn (vs. US$74bn in previous forecast). This change brings the BOP surplus forecast to US$700mn, up from US$400mn previously. For 2025, the BSP is forecasting the BOP to reverse to a deficit of US$500mn due to higher trade deficit of US$74bn (due to imports of goods for infrastructure) and lower capital inflows (due to elections in US and UK).

House passes CREATE MORE legislation on final reading

The Lower House approved House Bill No. 9794 or CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy)

The proposed amendments include:

-Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%

-Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports

-Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board

-Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises

-IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements

 

Senate ratifies bicameral report on property valuation reform

The Senate ratified a bicameral report on the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA). The Bill seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units. Assessed valued used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. The measure sets a two-year amnesty on interests and penalties for taxpayers with unpaid real property tax. Regis estimates that real property taxes account for 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers Regis covers. This law would likely have a large impact on developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land, Filinvest Land, and Vista Land.

PAGCOR expects industry GGR to hit P450bn-P500bn by 2027

PAGCOR, the gaming regulator, expects Philippine GGR to hit P450-P500bn by 2027, from P285bn in 2023. This would represent 13%-16% CAGR and would be due to opening of about six new casinos over the next few years – in Manila, Clark, Cebu, Boracay, and other areas. It also expects online gaming to grow during the period.

PAGCOR to lower fees on e-games operators

PAGCOR will lower the fees that it charges online gaming operators by 2.5ppt to 7.5ppt depending on the game. Regis estimates that revenue of DigiPlus would likely increase c. 10%. Holding all other things constant, EBITDA could increase up to 40%. However, the company will likely increase spending on advertising and promotions substantially.

BSP sees inflation accelerating closer to 4% in March

The BSP sees inflation accelerating closer to 4% (or 3.9%) in March due to positive base effects according to BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona. Inflation in February was at 3.4% and the BSP sees inflation averaging 3.6% this year. The Philippine Statistics Authority will release March CPI data on April 5. The Monetary Board’s next policy review is scheduled for April 4.

February BoP deficit eases to US$196mn

Data showed that the balance of payments narrowed sharply to US$196mn in February, lower than the US$895mn recorded in Feb 2023. This reversed surpluses of US$2.2bn in Jan-Feb 2023. The reflected outflows primarily from the National Government's payments of foreign currency debt obligations. The GIR dipped 1.26% MoM to US$102bn in February.
