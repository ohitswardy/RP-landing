REGIS REPORT

02 August 2024

The PSEi tumbled this week, posting its 2nd consecutive weekly drop and swinging along a mostly lower region, as a disappointing set of US economic data and tech results, and the BOJ’s expected policy tightening all weighed heavily on risk sentiment through the latter part of the week. This is notwithstanding the increased expectations of a dovish turn by the Fed through the remainder of the year. Note that compared to last week when futures were pricing in 2 rate cuts with 100% certainty, futures by the end of this week are now pricing in 3 rate cuts in the remaining Fed meetings this year (September, November, and December.) On the ground, the local benchmark hit a resistance near the crucial 6800-line right at the onset, with the benchmark posting a steep drop during Monday’s closing auction after trading steadily with gains and near 6800 through majority of the day. The index did see some signs of support mid-week but suffered another bout of selling on Friday, and ultimately settled the week with a -1.79% WoW slide to 6605, our biggest weekly drop since June 21.

In focus was busy round of 2Q earnings which showed mostly inline numbers by BDO -3.56% WoW and MBT -1.73% (BPI -4.17% already reported previously, was also inline), notwithstanding higher NPL from the former. BDO’s 1H NPAT grew +12% YoY, while MBT’s +13%, both inline with Regis and Street. Loan growths for both remained healthy at a mid-teens pace but BDO’s NPL ratio did blip up 18bps QoQ to 2.06%, while in contrast MBT’s improved 4bps QoQ to 1.66% (see more in Rafa’s research updates below.) In contrast, retailers RRHI -0.55% and WLCON -5.44%, the first 2 names to report in the consumer space, both posted results that were behind Regis and Street: RRHI’s 1H recurring net profit +34% YoY while net sales +3% YoY, and WLCON’s 1H -17% YoY while 2Q SSS -2% YoY (see more in Carissa’s notes below.) URC -1.79% meanwhile just came out with its own results on Friday which showed that 1H24 NPAT looks flat YoY, inline with consensus and slightly below Regis forecast. One standout outperformer in the busy batch of results this week was MER +0.52% which recorded a +21% YoY surge in 1H core income to P23.2b, well ahead of both Regis and Street. Electricity volume growth remained healthy at +8.5%, which was unsurprising given the heatwave through most of the quarter. What was surprising was its blockbuster profits in its unregulated businesses (generation and RES) which delivered as much as P6.2b in profits for 2Q alone.

Another significant corporate news this week Friday’s announcement that Mitsubishi UFJ purchased an 8% stake in GLO’s Mynt/GCash in a transaction that values 100% of Mynt at $5b, which validates Regis’ own numbers. AC (-0.51% WoW) also increased its stake in Mynt by 8% to 13%. Reports indicate that the transaction involved mostly secondary shares, which means that some of the private holders have likely trimmed their stakes. As such, this will lessen the clamor for Mynt to conduct an IPO over the near-term. With this, GLO shares outperformed the broad market selloff on Friday and ended the week as the best performing stock in the benchmark, +4.43% WoW to a new 52-week high.

One notable weak spot this week was the Property sector amid losses in main plays ALI -7.81% WoW, which was the worst performer in the benchmark and was also the top foreign sold name, and SMPH -1.85%. It appears that outlook on incoming 2Q results and the imminent POGO ban could be clouding sentiment over the sector, and is offsetting the expected benefit from the imminent reduction in interest rates, which could start with a potential 25bp cut by the BSP on the 15th. Another notable laggard was BLOOM -7.18%, carrying its downward momentum from last week and settling at a new 52-week low, as outlook on incoming results appear tempered as well following PAGCOR’s report weeks ago that GGR of licenses casinos were flat in QoQ. Furthermore, outlook on the longer term could also be weighed down by the expected increase in competition with new casinos looking to open shop within the next 1-2 years, and not to mention the likely negative impact of POGO ban on the casino customer base as well.

Market activity this week was moderate at a daily average of $81.6m, -6% WoW. Net foreign flow ended skewed to buy by $23m, virtually the same pace as last week, which also suggests that at least some part of the market’s weakness this week was local selling driven. Top inflows were MBT -1.73% WoW, CNPF -1.76%, MER +0.52%, RLC -1.34%, and GLO +4.43%, while top outflows were ALI -7.81%, BDO -3.56%, SMPH -1.85%, BLOOM -7.18%, and GTCAP +0.17%.

BDO Unibank - Strong 2Q24 sequential growth, on high fees, flat opex, and lower provisions
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1H24 NPAT +12% to P39.4bn, in line; provisions fall despite higher NPLs

BDO’s NPAT for 1H24 clocked in at P39.4bn, up 12% YoY, in line with Regis and the Street. For 2Q24 alone, profits were P20.9bn, up 12% YoY, and up 13% QoQ, driven by a notable rebound in fee incomes (+18% YoY), and easing opex growth. Loan growth (+13%) and NIMs were steady QoQ. Bottom-line was boosted by falling provisions (-6% YoY in 1H24) despite a slight uptick in NPLs. We are encouraged by the sequential gains and maintain our Buy call on the stock.

Consumer loans jump 16%, supporting NIMs despite rising funding costs

Loan growth was sustained at 13.0% YoY, similar to 1Q24’s 13.3% pace. Loan mix improved, though, with consumers accelerating to 15.7% YoY (was 13.5% in 1Q). Mid-market loans, too, sped up to 10.5% (from 6.7% in 1Q), while corporate loans slowed to 12.9% (from 16.6%). This helped loan yields climb 20bps QoQ, balancing out the 12bp QoQ increase in deposit costs. As a result, NIMs were steady, up 2bps QoQ.

Fees recover sharply in 2Q; Treasury book buildup to support NIMs, trading

PPOP was up sharply, +10% QoQ, driven by a strong recovery in non-interest income. Fees jumped 17% YoY in 2Q24, from just 5% in 1Q. On a QoQ basis, 2Q24 fees were up 18%. Opex, too, was up just 1% QoQ. Meanwhile, BDO built up its Treasury book to just over P1trn, up 8% QoQ and 19% YoY, and now account for 22% of total assets. About 58% is in HTC, which should help sustain NIMs as rates fall. The 37% in FVOCI and 5% in FVPL could boost trading gains.

Provisions fall, despite NPL increase; NPL cover still high at 169%

Provision expense in 2Q24 fell 16% YoY, for a credit cost of around 40bps. This was despite an 18bps QoQ blip up in NPL ratio to 2.06%. The bank noted that the increase was due to one or two large-ish accounts, and not a broad deterioration in the book. The higher share of consumer loans also played a part. That said, NPL cover stood at a very healthy 169%. Management expects NPL ratio to stabilize or improve in coming quarters.

 
Metropolitan Bank & Trust Co - Loans jump 15% in 1H24; NPAT in line, on lower provisions, NPLs
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1H24 NPAT P23.6bn +13%; lower provisions make up for trading/fx losses

Metrobank net profit for 1H24 clocked in at P23.6bn, up 13% YoY, in line with Regis and consensus estimates. Net interest income was slightly ahead, and fees rebounded in 2Q, but revenues were dragged by trading/fx losses in 2Q24. This was more than made up for by a 77% drop in provision expense to just P1bn for the semester. That said, NPL ratio improved, down 4bps QoQ to 1.66bps, in contrast to peers that saw NPLs rise. We maintain our Buy call on the stock, trading at 6.6x PER, 0.81x P/bk, and 7.3% yield on our 2024E estimates.

Loan growth accelerates to 15%, NIMs steady(ish) despite rising funding costs

Net interest income was up 15% YoY in 1H24 tracking ahead of our forecasts, as gross loans jumped 14.9% YoY, faster than the 12% pace in 1Q, and faster than our forecast of 12% for FY24E. Loans were again driven by strong corporate demand (+15.2% YoY). Consumer loans expanded a respectable 13.7%, due to credit cards (+21.4%) and auto loans (+16.6%). NIMs edged down 4bps QoQ, despite loan yields rising 14bps, and flat deposit costs (+1bp QoQ). Overall funding costs climbed 9bps QoQ as the bank likely refinanced bonds at higher rates.

Fee incomes rebound, though revenues dragged by trading/fx losses

Service charges and fees rebounded sharply, up 8.4% YoY in 2Q24 alone, reversing the 5% YoY contraction in 1Q. However, the bank booked P1.05bn worth of trading/fx losses in 2Q, dragging revenues down. Opex growth was kept in check, up 8% YoY for 1H24 (+10% in 2Q alone).

NPLs down to 1.66%, provisions fall 77% as MBT winds down over-provisioning

Similar to its peers, MBT’s profits were boosted by low provision expense, which fell 78% to just P472mn for 2Q24 equivalent to a credit cost of 11bps. In contrast, though, MBT’s NPL ratios improved QoQ to 1.66bps (-4bps QoQ), while BDO and BPI saw asset quality deteriorate. NPL cover fell from 174% in 1Q24 to a still[1]conservative 163% in 2Q. The bank will host a briefing tomorrow, Friday, 02 August at 4pm Manila time.

 
Wilcon Depot - 2Q24: SSS decline slows but no clear sign of turn yet
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H24 net profit -17% YoY - behind

WLCON reported net profit of P770mn in 2Q24, down 10% YoY. This brings the cumulative 1H24 net profit to P1.5bn, down 17% YoY, tracking behind Regis and consensus expectations, even after excluding the impact of a P12m write-off on one of its stores that was damaged by a fire during the quarter. While 1H24 net sales (flat YoY) are trending only slightly below estimates, operating profit (-14% YoY) is behind as expansion-related expenses continued to weigh on margins in 2Q24.

SSS improves but underlying demand remains soft

SSS declined 2% YoY in 2Q24, an improvement from the -7% decline in 1Q24. This was partly driven by the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23), when stores have limited operating hours. So while SSS was up in April, it declined again in succeeding months. Average baskets contracted 2% YoY during the quarter while transaction count was flat.

Expansion-related expenses continue to drag margins

Gross margin was stable QoQ and YoY at 39.7%. Operating margin, however, contracted 160bps YoY as expansion-related expenses for new stores and newly opened stores continued to put upward pressure on opex. WLCON opened two new Depots during the quarter, bringing store count to 95 as of end-June.

Market challenged but outlook remains positive

Management noted that SSS improved in the first few weeks of July but the momentum was interrupted by the typhoon which hit Luzon in the last week of the month. Demand stayed under pressure with customers not undertaking large scale projects, but management believes that sentiment in the coming quarters should improve as inflationary pressures ease. There is also an expected uptick in demand leading up to the mid-term elections in May 2025. In the meantime, store expansion will continue, and the company expects to reach its 100-store milestone by the end of 2024.

 
Robinsons Retail - Top line remains soft in 2Q24; refreshing forecasts and TP
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H24 recurring net profit in line

RRHI’s reported net profit rose 3.8x YoY to P6.8bn. Excluding the one-off gain from the BPI-RBC merger of P4.4bn recognized in 1Q24, we estimate 1H24 recurring income grew 34% YoY to P2.4bn, trending behind Regis and consensus estimates. Net sales (+3% YoY to P93.7bn) and EBIT (+5% YoY to P4.1bn), however, are trending within consensus expectations. For 2Q24 alone, recurring net profit grew 36% YoY to P1.7bn due to lower interest expense and a lower effective tax rate. EBIT, however, grew just 7% YoY as top line weakness persisted into 2Q24.

Top line weakness persists in 2Q24

Consolidated SSSG decelerated to +0.5% YoY in 2Q24 from +0.9% YoY in the previous quarter despite the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23), which resulted in the partial closure of some discretionary formats in 1Q. Supermarkets (57% of consolidated sales) saw slower SSSG (+0.6% in 2Q24) as continued downtrading led to a steeper decline in average baskets (-12% in 2Q24 vs -9% in 1Q24).

While the company is optimistic that top line trends should improve heading into 4Q24, it downgraded its SSSG guidance for the year to +2-4% from +3-5% previously given the weakness seen in 1H24.

Management changes announced

The company announced that effective January 2025, Ms. Robina Gokongwei[1]Pe will retire as President and CEO and assume the role of Chairman of RRHI. Mr. Lance Gokongwei will step down as Chairman and assume the role of Board Adviser. Meanwhile, Mr. Stanley Co will take over as President and CEO of RRHI. Mr. Co has been with the company for over 20 years, having served in various parts of the business. We do not expect material changes to operations and strategy as a result of these movements.

Adjusting forecasts and TP, maintaining Buy

We are maintaining our Buy rating on the stock as we refresh our forecasts to account for recent results and reduce our 2024-25E EPS forecasts by 9-11%. We also roll over our valuation to 2025E and slightly adjust our TP to P83/sh (+1%). We continue to be positive on RRHI’s longer term growth runway as the 2nd largest multi-format retailer in the country, who should benefit from the continued structural growth of the sector. Our new TP implies 19x 2025E PE, which is a discount to its historical average (21x). Key downside risks relate to competition and weak consumer demand.

Ayala Corp and Japan’s MUFG invest in Mynt, valuation at US$5bn

Japan’s Mitsubishi UFJ Financial (MUFG) has bought an 8% stake in Mynt. Ayala Corp. (AC PM) also increased its stake in Mynt by 8% to ~13%. Both transaction values Mynt at US$5bn, more than double its US$2bn valuation in the last funding round in 2021. Regis currently values 100% of Mynt at US$5bn. The proposed investment is subject to the execution of definitive transaction documents and the satisfaction of customary closing conditions. News reports indicate that transaction involved a mix of primary and secondary shares, with some private equity investors selling down their stakes to make way for the new shareholder. Globe (GLO PM) will be retaining its 36% stake in Mynt.

Meralco 1H24 results comment

Core income rose 21% in 1H to P23bn.  Meralco reported net income of P22.4bn for 1H, up 26%. Stripping out non-core items, “core” income was P23.2bn, +21% YoY.  This is well ahead of expectations, not just of Regis but also the Street.  Surprisingly, the company’s unregulated businesses delivered exceptional numbers in 2Q after the relative slowdown in 1Q.

Volumes stayed strong in 2Q.  Volumes for 2Q rose 8.5%, still strong historically but a tad lower than 1Q24.  As a result, 1H volumes were 8.7% up.  For 1H, residential volumes rose 12.6% while commercial volumes grew 9.9%.  Industrial volumes rose 2.4%, with management noting that demand from semiconductor firms rose 3%.

Blockbuster profits from Generation and RES.  Beyond the decent (and not unexpected) numbers from the core distribution business, the “unregulated businesses” delivered exceptional profits (see yellow highlight in table above).  We estimate the “unregulated businesses” (generation, retail supply and others) delivered P6.2bn of profit in 2Q alone. This would be the largest ever contribution from the non-DU segments ever in a single quarter.  We believe Meralco managed to take advantage of the volatility and high prices in the spot market in the latest quarter.

ERC rejects plan to withdraw old tariff application. The regulator rejected Meralco’s proposal to withdraw its tariff application (under 5RP or 5th Regulatory Period) and fix the tariff for 2022-2024 at current rates (P1.35/kwh).  Simply put, the ERC wants Meralco to continue undergoing a tariff review that will cover a period (2022-2026) that is already half over.

Dividends and guidance.  Meralco declared a dividend of P10.295 per share (record date Aug 28) with respect to its 1H profits.  Combined with its dividend earlier this year, Meralco is paying a total of P21.52/sh this FY24.  This is 5.6% of the current price (and >6% of the price earlier this year).  Meralco also provided an upgraded its guidance for core earnings to P43bn (from P40bn).  This implies profits will be lower in 2H vs 1H.

Taguig court issues TRO vs Meralco’s power supply bidding; Resolution filed at Senate

The Regional Trial Court of Taguig granted the petition of the members of the consortium operating the Malampaya gas field to issue a 72-hour temporary restraining order (TRO) against Meralco’s conduct of competitive selection process for its 600 MW and 400 MW power supply requirements. Plaintiffs were: Prime Energy, Prime Oil and Gas Inc, UC38 LLC, and the Philippine National Oil Exploration Corp (PNOC-EC). Meralco earlier invited bids for a contract capacity of 600 MW (bid deadline on Aug 2), which is set to take effect in September 2025. It also invited bids for an additional contract capacity of 400 MW, effective September 2025.

In a previous petition, the Malampaya consortium stressed that pursuing the bidding can put indigenous natural gas at a disadvantage, “violat[ing] the preference given” by existing laws. “Increased reliance on imported sources of fuel threatens the country’s energy security and energy sovereignty because these are greatly susceptible to a volatile market,” the previous petition said. It also said Meralco’s bidding done through the Competitive Selection Process (CSP) was “flawed, skewed or supplier-driven and grossly violative of existing laws, rules and regulations.” It said that awarding PSAs only to suppliers using imported fuel will also harm the exploration and development of indigenous resources, and will affect the Malampaya project, which has been reliably and securely supplying natural gas to power plants that provide 30 to 40 percent of Luzon’s electricity requirements.

Meanwhile, Senator Alan Peter Cayetano filed Resolution 1090 on July 29, to call on Meralco to defer its CSP “until its terms of reference (TOR) are reviewed. Senator Cayetano said the TOR remain unclear and may put certain bidders at an advantage at the expense of power suppliers that use indigenous natural gas.

UBP 2Q24 - massive PPOP improvement, blunted by (still) huge provisions

Union Bank (UBP PM) reported net profit of P5.07bn for 1H24, down 20% YoY, and tracking behind Bloomberg consensus of P13bn. To the upside, 2Q24 showed dramatic recovery, up 56% QoQ to P3.09bn, driven by falling opex on the end of the Transition Service Agreement (TSA) on the Citi assets. PPOP was strong, up 25% in 1H24 and up 28% YoY in 2Q24 alone. However, profits were again dragged down by massive provision expense of P9.0bn in 1H24 (P4.36bn in 2Q24 alone) as the bank continues to clean up its books, partly due to huge delinquencies at UnionDigital (NPL was 42% in 1Q24).

Some highlights from the PR and 17C:

Loans contracted 1.4% YoY (vs +6.3% in 1Q). No segmental discussion, but we suspect there were significant write-downs of the UnionDigital portfolio. UBP claims 59% of their loans are in consumer loans. Deposits were down 3.9% YoY.

We estimate NIMs jumped 27bps QoQ to 5.56%, though the quarterly NIMs have been oddly volatile in recent quarters. We are uncertain whether the bank engages in the deposit swap products that distort NIMs and trading/fx accounts.

Opex fell 1% in 1H24; for 2Q alone, opex was down 11% YoY and 5% QoQ. The bank’s TSA ended in March with the completion of the migration of all Citi clients to UBP.

Provision expense stayed extremely high, at P8.99bn for 1H24 (+80% YoY) and P4.36bn for 2Q24 (+53% YoY), with credit cost remaining well above 300bps. It is unclear how much of the provisions were due to UD, and how much to the parent bank. However, we do understand that the UD went through a big cleanup as NPLs hit 42% in 1Q24. Management has indicated that the systems at UD have been adjusted, and they expect much more moderate growth, but also lower defaults/provisions going forward. No NPL / NPL cover data was disclosed yet. Waiting for full FS and SOC. ROE improved to 6.85% in 2Q, from 4.54% in 1Q.

The improvement in revenues and opex are encouraging. And if the asset cleanup is completed soon, ROEs could bounce quite quickly.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP predicts July inflation likely breached upper 4% target

The BSP expects headline inflation for the month of July to settle between 4-4.8%. This would be higher than June inflation of 3.7% and would be the first time in seven months that inflation would be above 4%. Officials cited higher electricity rates and domestic oil prices, as well as higher food prices (vegetables, meat, and fruits). This is expected to be offset by lower rice prices and the appreciation of the PHP against the USD. BSP Governor Remolona previously flagged that they expect inflation to breach its target band of 2-4% in July before returning to its band by 3Q. Despite a possible uptick in July, the BSP said it is already considering cu its policy rate by 25bps on Aug 15, and possibly another 25bps in 4Q. The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release July CPI data on Aug 6 (consensus: 4.0%).

Rice imports slow down as traders still cautious

Data from the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI) showed that rice imports levied with 15% tariff rate reached 56,073 metric tons as of July 20 (vs monthly average of 400,000mt in 1H24). Note that the reduction in rice tariffs took effect on July 5. Department of Agriculture (DA) said it is possible that rice importers and traders are still unloading earlier stocks imported at the 35% tariff before bringing in new stocks. Some rice traders and importers were hesitant to import pending the decision of the Supreme Court on Executive Order (EO) 62.

Nevertheless, the DA expects import arrivals to pick up in the coming weeks. Substantial volume is said to have a “significant” effect on pulling down retail rice prices. Prevailing rice prices were said to fall below P50/kg once imported rice stocks with lower tariff rates start to be sold in the market. The retail price of imported well-milled rice in Metro Manila ranges from P51 to P53/kg while regular-milled rice is P47-48/kg.

Colliers estimates 1H24 condominiums sales fell 58% YoY

In its 2Q24 report, Colliers reported that 1H24 Metro Manila condominium sales fell 58% YoY to 6,200 units. As a result, unsold condominium inventory in Metro Manila represents 4.9 years’ worth of take-up. Meanwhile, it also reported that Metro Manila office vacancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 18%, thanks in large part to office transactions by government agencies in the Manila Bay area. However, it estimates that the POGO ban could lift the Metro Manila vacancy rate to 22%. We estimate that the POGO ban could also lift the Manila Bay area vacancy rate to 50%.

PAGCOR sees complications regarding POGO ban

During a Senate hearing, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the ban on offshore gaming includes Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL). Recall that in 2023, all POGO firms were deemed suspended and were required to apply for an IGL. However, he sees complications in implementing the ban with respect to the Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA), which can issue its own gaming licenses as it is not under the jurisdiction of PAGCOR. Prior to the issuance of POGO licenses in 2016, offshore gaming firms operated in Metro Manila through the CEZA license. If existing offshore gaming firms are permitted to apply for CEZA licenses, then the POGO ban will likely have minimal impact.

Government debt rises to P15.48tn in June

The National Government’s (NG) outstanding debt inched up by 0.9% MoM to P15.48tn from P15.35tn. The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) said that such rise was caused by the net issuance of both domestic and external debt and the effect of peso depreciation. Domestic debt edged up by 1.2% MoM to P10.57tn from P10.44tn due to the P129.89bn net issuance of government securities. External debt (31.71% of total) inched up by 0.1% MoM to P4.91tn from P4.9tn. The NG’s debt as a share of GDP stood at 60.2% at the end of the first quarter, while the government aims for a 60.3% debt-to-GDP ratio by year-end. Government aims to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 51% by 2028 (vs. trough of 39.6% in 2019).

Budget Department submits 2025 P6.35tn budget proposal to Congress

The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) submitted its proposed P6.35tn 2025 budget to the Lower House, which is 10% higher than the approved 2024 budget and is equivalent to ~22% of GDP. Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively, while allocation for debt payments up 27% YoY and to local government units up 17% YoY.

Under its proposal, the government debt stock is expected to increase by 8.08% to P17.35tn from the projected P16.06tn debt at the end of 2024. The bulk comes from the outstanding domestic debt which is expected to rise by 9.64% to P11.98tn by end-2025 from P10.92tn by end-2024. The outstanding external debt is expected to rise by 4.76% to P5.38tn by end-2025 from P5.13tn by end-2024.

Note that the NG’s debt as a share of GDP stood at 60.2% at the end of the first quarter, while the government aims for a 60.3% debt-to-GDP ratio by year-end. This suggests that the 2025 NG debt will be roughly 60% of GDP. The government aims to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 51% by 2028 (vs. trough of 39.6% in 2019).

Government to hike debt expenses by 25% YoY in 2025 budget

The government reportedly plans to increase the share of debt expenses in the proposed P6.352tn 2025 budget, which would mean that the allocation for debt expenses will be P876.7bn or 13.8% of 2025 budget. Under the 2024 budget (i.e. GAA), the allocation for debt expenses was P699.2bn, or 12.1% of total GAA. Interest payments under the 2025 budget will amount to P848bn (+26.5% YoY) while net lending will be flat at P28.7bn. Note that principal payments are not included in the calculation of debt expenses. The Department of Budget and Management will submit the complete Budget of Expenditures and Sources of Financing (BESF) for 2025 to Congress today.

Gov’t allots P1.28tn for infrastructure in 2025, up 3%

For 2025, infrastructure and other capital outlays (excluding subsidies to government-owned corporations and infrastructure transfers to local government units) are expected to jump by 3.04% to P1.28tn. The overall infrastructure outlay (i.e. including subsidies to government-owned corporations and transfers to LGUs) will decline slightly to P1.506tn in 2025 from P1.51tn in 2024. Infrastructure outlay is equivalent to c. 5-6% of GDP.

Senate ratifies bicameral report on 12% VAT on foreign digital service providers

The Senate adopted the bill imposing the 12% value-added tax (VAT) on digital service providers without a physical presence in the Philippines (e.g. Amazon, Shein, Taobao). The reconciled version of the bill reinforces the Bureau of Internal Revenue’s (BIR) power to “impose and collect VAT on digital services and transactions.” The Department of Finance (DoF) set withholding tax rates for companies falling under the P3mn VAT threshold under the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) law to protect small online businesses. Nonresident digital service providers and electronic marketplaces must register with the BIR for the remittance of VAT on their services. Under the bill, 5% of the revenues from digital service VAT (around P900mn) will be used to support the digital creative industry.

Trade Secretary Alfredo Pascual resigns

Trade Secretary Alfredo Pascual has resigned from his post, effective August 2. The search for Pascual’s successor will begin immediately to ensure a seamless transition and continuity in the Department of Trade and Industry’s initiatives.

US government to give Philippines USD500mn in defense funding

U.S. Secretary of State Antony Blinken announced the the US will provide USD500mn in military funding to the Philippines to boost ties with the Philippines amid its maritime tensions with Beijing.
