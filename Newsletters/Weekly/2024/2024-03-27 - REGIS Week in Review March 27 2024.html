REGIS REPORT

27 March 2024

The PSEi ended the shortened week little changed at 6903.53 +0.31% WoW as shares managed to find support after testing crucial technical levels mid-week. The benchmark basically continued its broad consolidation trend within the 6800-6900 line this week. It tested the 6800-line and the 50-day moving average during the continuous session on Tuesday, before staging a sharp reversal and spiking towards the 6900-line at the closing auction, boosted by some heavy foreign buying MOC baskets. The benchmark then found relatively more stable footing during the final session of the week, consolidating around the 6900-line throughout the day and ultimately ending the week practically unchanged and 1Q with a +7.03% QoQ gain, but not without some sharp moves at the closing auction in a few select names like CNPF which spiked by +8.11% at the close alone, boosted by apparent window dressing, and on the opposite end DMC which came off by -5.65%.

Despite the shortened trading days, headline market activity was heavier than usual at a daily average of $141m, +20% WoW. Note however that this was largely inflated by blocks into non-PSEi names, such as the $89m local-local block in CREIT (+3.33 WoW) on Wednesday to reflect SM's 28.79% acquisition in the company, and a $24m outflow block in PLUS (+1.63%) on Tuesday. With the sizable outflow in PLUS, net foreign flow for the market ended skewed to sell by $41.7m this vs an inflow of $8.5m last week. 

Best performing stock in the benchmark this week was CNPF +13.31% WoW, largely due to the aforementioned late spike during Wednesday's closing auction. Among the winners were also other consumer stocks like MONDE +6.02%, PGOLD +3.28% and URC 2.23%. However on the losing end, worst performer was JFC -4.39%. Moving forward, likely to see the sector remain in focus in the coming week with the latest CPI data due on Friday, expected to show an inflation reading of 3.8% in March vs 3.4% in February.

GT Capital - Robust 4Q23 profit growth
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY23 core profit up 64% to P25.1bn - Ahead of forecast, in line with consensus

Headline FY23 profit of P28.7bn (+57%) included gains from lot sales. Stripping out one-offs and pref dividends, GTCAP’s core profit rose 64% to P25.1bn, broadly in line with consensus but above Regis forecasts. 4Q23 profit rose 24% to P5.5bn, as its key operating segments posted strong growth.

Profits from auto flattish QoQ; Tough 1Q24 comps not a concern

The auto unit grew 4Q23 profits by 3% QoQ as sales volumes rose 10% and compensated for the typical heavy seasonal promotional activities, subsidies, and taxes that happen during 4Q. Management was also not too concerned about facing tough comps in 1Q24, as sales are robust and FX rates are better than anticipated. Moreover, TMP still has P2.8bn worth of unutilized tax credits that could be booked in succeeding periods.

Metrobank (In line): Loan growth accelerates in 4Q; Stable NIMs, asset quality

Metrobank (MBT PM) grew its loan book by 7.6% in 4Q from 7.1% in 3Q, driven by commercial loans. NIMs were up 30bps YoY and flat QoQ to 3.8%. Asset quality remained healthy, with NPL ratio flat QoQ at 1.7% and NPL cover still at an above[1]industry 180% (vs 187% in 3Q23).

Property: 4Q reservation sales drop 37% QoQ

Federal Land’s FY23 core profit was up 36% to P1.7bn, lifted by higher bookings from high-end JV projects. However, after matching pre-Covid level reservation sales in 9M23, 4Q23 sales fell 37% QoQ to P5.1bn or 84% of pre-Covid levels. With limited land available for high-end projects in Manila, management is counting on the completion of a tollroad project in Cavite in mid-2025 to boost sales.

Prefers growth than paying more dividends

With record dividends from its banking and auto units, as well as a positive 2024 outlook, management deems it best to use its excess funds to fund potential acquisitions in healthcare, renewable energy, and data centers.

 
Wilcon Depot - Revenue weakness persists in 4Q23
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY23 net profit -9% to P3.5bn - behind

WLCON reported FY23 net profit of P3.5bn, down 9% YoY, behind Regis and consensus estimates. Revenues (+3% YoY to P34.6bn) fell short of expectations while pre-operating expenses for new stores dragged EBIT 7% lower YoY. 4Q23 net profit declined 15% YoY as soft demand continued to drag same-store sales.

SSS weakness persists into 4Q23

SSS decline persisted into 4Q, contracting 7% during the quarter. Management attributed the weakness partly due to a high YoY base from the reopening momentum in 4Q22 as well as soft demand from construction/renovation projects. To some degree, we believe there was also some cannibalization from WLCON’s new stores, particularly in more competitive areas like Metro Manila.

Pre-operating expenses continue to drag margins

EBIT declined 12% YoY as a result of the weak top line, exacerbated by the continued drag from pre-operating expenses for new stores. The continued increase in the share of in-house brands, 52% in 4Q23 from ~51% in 9M23, helped keep gross margin flattish (QoQ and YoY).

Store expansion to continue amidst a challenged top line

The company continues to expand stores aggressively in 2024, even as the top line will continue to be challenged in the near term. Management noted that it is still seeing weakness in private construction activity as inflationary pressures weigh on consumer sentiment. Management, however, is more optimistic about the latter half of 2024 and expects WLCON to see the benefits of operating leverage due to the ramp-up of new stores. WLCON plans to open 9 Depots and 2 Do-It-Wilcon stores and hit its 100-store target by the end of 2024.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Investments reported to acquire 29% of CREIT for P5bn

As per Inquirer's industry sources, SM Investments Corp. (SMIC) will be closing a deal to buy close to a 29% stake in Citicore Energy REIT Corp. (CREIT) for P5bn. This implies SMIC will buy shares at around P2.63/sh, a little less than its market price of P2.70/sh and capitalization of P18.39bn. The deal has been arranged by BDO Capital Investment Corp. and will be finalized soon. CREIT is the first renewable energy REIT and the largest renewable energy landlord with a gross leasable area of 5.96mn sqm and currently leases its land to parent company and pure renewable energy developer, Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. (CREC), which is seeking to IPO this 2Q24. Based on consensus forecasts, CREIT will offer a 7.3% dividend yield for FY24.

Globe generates P5bn from sale of towers

Globe Telecom said that it raised P5bn in 1Q24 from the sale of towers to MIESCOR. Infrastructure Development Corp, Frontier Tower Associates Philippines, PhilTower Consortium, and Unity Digital Infrastructure. Globe will reduce FY24 capex by 22% YoY to P55bn.

Megaworld to guarantee debts of subsidiaries and affiliates

Megaworld’s Board of Directors approved amendments to its articles of incorporation. Due to this Megaworld will be able to act as a guarantor for the debts/obligations of its subsidiaries and affiliates. Megaworld’s authorized capital stock will rise from P40.2bn to P45.7bn. The current is  P40.14bn common shares (40.14bn common shares at P1/share par value) and P60mn voting preferred shares. In the amended version there will be P45.64 common shares (45.64bn common shares at P1/share par value) and P60mn voting preferred shares. The amendments could signal that Megaworld will raise equity in order to guarantee the debt of an affiliate/subsidiary. Megaworld’s FY23 consolidated debt/equity stood at 28%. There are currently 31.2bn common shares outstanding.

Meralco tracking 9% volume growth in 1Q24

Meralco is currently tracking 9% growth in electricity sales in 1Q24. Residential sales are higher due to El Nino. It also benefitted from an additional billing day brought about by the leap year. Management also added “Industrial is also showing signs of recovery though a bit modest”.

SMC and MPIC to finalize toll road JV in the coming months

San Miguel Corp. (SMC) and Metro Pacific Investments Corp. (MPIC) have been in discussions regarding a possible JV for a toll road business. The finalization is pending SMC's evaluation of MPIC's Indonesian tollway assets. MPIC Chairman Pangilinan hopes to complete and publicly list the planned JV with SMC within the year.

Finance Secretary believes 6% GDP growth is a reasonable target

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto considers 6% to be a realistic GDP growth target but “we will endeavor to hit 6.5%”. This is lower than the 6.5%-7.5% target set by the Development Budget Coordination Committee. Regis has FY24E GDP growth forecast at 6.0% and 2023 GDP growth at 5.6%. Secretary Recto also said the Marcos administration will focus on improve tax collection and would not introduce new taxes, except as a last resort. He said that the government’s fiscal position was “not that bad” as to impose new taxes. However, he wants to extend the corporate life of PSALM (Power Sector Assets and Liabilities Management Corp.) by another 25 years in order to help reduce the government’s deficit position. PSALM is scheduled to end its corporate life in June 2026. PSALM is currently prioritizing the sale of the 796MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan hydroelectric plant in Laguna. This could generate P50bn to P100bn (awarding in 2025). The rehabilitation of the Agus-Pulangi Hydropower complex in Mindanao is also ongoing.

 

Potential yellow alerts for Luzon grid in April, May due to El Niño

Luzon grid may face yellow alerts in April and May due to El Niño's impact, leaving hydroelectric power plants running below capacity level, per the Department of Energy (DOE). Yellow alerts are declared when the available energy supply to the grid falls below a set safety margin. Possible yellow alerts are based on the scenario that there will be a "70% reduction" or "zero" capacity from hydropower plants. As of end-November 2023, there are 44 existing hydroelectric power plants with an installed capacity of 2,548MW. Demand forecasts for 2024 under an El Niño scenario remain at a 12% increase from 2023.

Finance Secretary expects 50 basis points in rate cuts this year

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, who is part of the Monetary Board, said that he expects two rate cuts totaling 50 basis points this year. However, he does not expect a rate cut by the next MB meeting (in April). REGIS is expecting 100 basis points in rate cuts this year.

 

Digital payments (surprisingly) re-accelerate in February

Amazingly, digital transactions made over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) accelerated even further in February, surging to P1,231bn 41% YoY, compared to 25% in December, and 37% in January. More surprisingly, payments over both InstaPay (smaller-ticket, real-time) and PesoNet (larger, batched) accelerated. We had thought that PesoNet was maturing, with growth slowing in 2023. Growth was still faster at InstaPay, up an astounding 49% YoY to P496bn. Even more impressive was the number of InstaPay transactions, which skyrocketed 85% YoY (vs an already heady 73% in December). PesoNet payments came in at P735bn, re-accelerating 35% YoY in February, compared to the 15% YoY posted in December.

 

For the first two months of 2024, the combined payments were up 38% YoY to P2.5trn, faster than the 29% clip for all of 2023. InstaPay transactions were up 47% YoY to P1.0trn (vs 42% in 2023), while PesoNet was up 33% YoY to P1.5trn (vs 22% in 2023).

 

The resurgence reinforces our views set out in our recent report (Digital Finance – who wins?) detailing the rapid adoption of digital payments. For now, it seems that the best way to play the theme is through Globe (GLO PM) given the dominance of its 36%-owned Mynt/GCash affiliate. Among the banks we cover, BPI (BPI PM) seems to be making the biggest push into digitalization, while mid-sized banks like Union Bank (UBP PM) and Rizal Commercial Bank (RCB PM) have made big moves too. However, it remains unclear how (or when) the investments will result in better profits and ROEs.

Electricity spot prices rise in March

The average electricity spot price for the month jumped by 32% MoM to P5.26/kWh. In Visayas, prices rose from P4.58/kWh to P6.26/kWh meanwhile in Mindanao, they rose from P3.71/kWh to P4.20/kWh.
