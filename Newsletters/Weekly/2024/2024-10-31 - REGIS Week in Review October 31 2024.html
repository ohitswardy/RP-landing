REGIS REPORT

31 October 2024

The PSEi fell for the 2nd week in a row, -2.34% WoW to 7,142.96, as shares were weighed down by an acceleration in net foreign selling ahead of the upcoming US presidential elections where betting odds are currently placing a ~60% chance of a Trump win. Net foreign selling hit nearly $50m which halved our YTD inflows down to $44m (vs peak of $99m earlier in October). Bulk of the outflows came during the month-end session (and at the closing auction) when the PSEi posted its biggest slide in a month (-1.89% on Thursday) to settle at its lowest close in 33 sessions. The PSEi trailed behind peers in the region and crucially closed below the 50-day moving average which, before this week, it had last touched when it was still at the 6500-level back in August.

On the ground, in focus is the busy round of earnings releases during the shortened trading week, highlighted primarily by lenders. Results across the biggest lenders looked generally positive and with a healthy mid-teens pace of loan growth. One area to note however is the margin compression which appears to be happening across the board and possibly suggests heightened competition in the lending space. That said, with the exception of BDO who chose not to do so yet, most lenders booked notable trading gains which provided further support their results, hence mostly inline or ahead of forecasts bottomlines (see Rafa’s notes below). That said, lenders tracked the widespread weakness in the market this week with BDO -4.32% WoW, BPI -1.92% and MBT -1.3%.

In the consumer space, results by early reporters RRHI -1.02% WoW and WLCON -0.74% both came in below forecasts and hence possibly suggest another soft quarter for consumers. However, prospects moving forward still looks intact as recent inflation trend (with a September CPI print of sub-2%) and 4Q being a seasonally strong quarter, not to mention the upcoming mid-term elections further down the line, should provide catalysts to consumption moving forward.

Another bit of negative news this week is property consultant Colliers’ 3Q update which showed a deterioration in both the office leasing and residential sales sides of the business, ahead of results announcements by major developers. Office take-ups turned negative (-33k sqm according to their numbers) as POGOs vacated -57ksqm ahead of the expected POGO ban by year-end. Meanwhile unsold residential inventory further grew to 70 months from 60 months in 2Q. While looking worse compared to Leechiu’s numbers, note that both are directionally showing the same deteriorating trend. Top laggard in the benchmark this week was ALI -6.03% WoW, which is set to hold its briefing on Wednesday, while its peer SMPH managed to resist the selloff, +0.16% (parent SM briefing the following week).

Another name in focus was ICT after 3Q Philippines ports data showed that its Manila port MICT’s 3Q volumes grew at a slower pace of +6% YoY vs +7% in 2Q, as it appeared to have lost market share to ATI’s South Harbour which in contrast saw a steep +17% volume growth during the quarter. Note that ICT is set to report results on Tuesday. Klyne anticipates that 3Q volume growth could be softer compared to 2Q, considering this Manila port headline and the congestion affecting its Mexico port – ICT’s 2 biggest ports which collectively account for about half of revenues. That said, tariff hikes in several ports should provide enough boost that revenues will likely outpace 2Q’s +17% growth still. ICT shares were sharply sold down on Thursday and capped the week -4.07% WoW.

Market activity this week improved to a daily average of $91m +25% WoW. Breadth of the market was skewed to the negative over 2/3 of index members in the red. Net foreign selling stood at $49.6m which was spread out across many names. Top net sold were ALI -6.03% WoW, BDO -4.32%, SM +1.89%, ICT -4.07%, GLO -5.63%, MBT -1.3%, TEL -2.39%, SMPH +0.16% and CNVRG -4.17%, while top bought were BPI -1.92% and GTCAP +0.77%.

BDO Unibank - Solid 9M24, building buffers; raising TP to P200
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Record 3Q24 NPAT, all core; TP raised 11%

BDO booked solid 9M24 NPAT of P60.6bn, up 12% YoY, with 3Q at a record P21.2bn. Notably, the results were achieved without out-sized trading gains, or a drawdown of provisions (NPL cover climbed to 178%). Management reckons this gives the bank’s profit and capital adequate support against potential NIM compression, or asset quality “black swans”. We make minor adjustments to our NPAT forecasts, and raise our target price by 11.1% to P200/sh as we roll over to our 2025E estimates, for a total potential return of 27%. Maintain Buy.

Steady as she goes – loan growth 13%, consumers +16%

Customer loan growth was steady at 12.8% YoY (was 13.0% in 2Q), with consumers also maintaining the same 15.7% YoY clip. NIMs took a hit, down 12bps QoQ, as the bank refinanced maturing sustainability bonds at higher rates. Management noted heightened competition in loan pricing, though this should be offset by the recent 250bps RRR cut. Fee incomes were solid, up 12% YoY.

Building buffers in Treasury book, NPL cover

The record 3Q24 profit was achieved without significant trading/fx gains (which were actually down sharply YoY and QoQ). Management reckons it was too early to monetize the book, with further policy rate cuts still forthcoming. Nor did the bank draw down its provisions, with NPL cover rising to 178% (from 169% in 2Q). As NIM pressures increase, both provide the bank adequate buffers for its bottom-line.

Target price hiked 11.1% to P200/sh; Buy retained

We make minor adjustments to our NPAT forecasts (0-3%) for 2024-26E, but lift our target price from P180/sh to P200/sh as we roll over to our 2025E estimates. Our new TP represents a total potential return of 27%. We value the stock on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.5%, COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%, equivalent to a target P/bk of 1.63x on our 2025E book value estimates. Key risks include intensifying competition for both loans and deposits, which could put further downward pressure on margins.

 
Bank of the Philippine Islands - Big beat in 3Q24; lifting estimates and TP
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Strong NIMs and trading gains prompt TP upgrade to P160/sh

BPI’s 9M24 NPAT of P48bn roundly beat Regis and Street estimates. Loan growth of 19% was driven by high-yielding consumer loans pushing NIMs up 22bps YoY. Profits also received a boost from a tripling of trading gains. The consumer push, though, has hurt asset quality, with NPL cover down to 111% (from 156% at end-2023). We increase our 2024-26E earnings forecasts by 4-9%, and up our target price by 18.5% to P160/sh, rolling over to our 2025E estimates. Maintain Buy.

9M24 beats, on trading and fees; NIMs still expanding on consumer push

BPI posted record-high NPAT of P17.4bn in 3Q24, up 30% YoY and 14% QoQ. The main driver of the beat was trading gains, at over P2bn in the quarter alone (vs just P171mn in 3Q23). Fee income was strong, too, up 26% YoY in 3Q (+28% in 9M24). NII was slightly ahead of our expectations, driven largely by higher NIMs on explosive growth in personal loans (+129%), “business banking” (+88%), and microfinance (+67%). These should help cushion the impact of lower interest rates heading into 2025E.

Credit costs low, but likely to rebound in 2025/26E

Asset quality deteriorated, though this was to be expected given the rapid consumer loan growth. Quarter-end NPL ratio was up 10bps QoQ to 2.30%, but NPL cover fell sharply to 111%, allowing credit cost to stay low (30-35bps in 9M). However, with cover closing in on 100%, we expect credit costs to jump to 50-60bps from 2025E onwards. Despite this, we believe the risk-adjusted returns of the consumer portfolio are still accretive.

Raising forecasts 4-9% and lifting TP to P160.00 (+18.5%); Maintain Buy

We increase our net profit forecasts by 8-9% for 2024/25E, and 4% for 2026E. We also raise our target price by 18.5% to P160.00/sh, as we roll over to our 2025E estimates. Our target is based on ROE-g/COE-g, assuming long-term ROE of 14.75% (from 14.5%), COE of 12%, and growth of 8.25%, which translates to 1.73x P/bk. At our target, the stock offers potential total return of 15%; maintain Buy. Key downside risks include new NPL formation from the fast-growing consumer book, and intensifying competition on loan pricing.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Metrobank 9M24 - strong trading gains make up for NIM collapse

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 9M24 NPAT of P35.7bn, up 12% YoY, slightly ahead of Regis (FY 24E P46.9bn) and consensus (P47.9bn). However, the “quality” of the earnings leaves something to be desired. While loan growth remained robust (15.6% YoY gross, 16.6% net), margins were crushed (-27bps QoQ) by sharply higher funding costs as the bank shifted from TDs to wholesale funding. Fee incomes were soft, too, down 19% YoY. These were offset by extremely strong trading/fx gains of P6bn in 3Q alone (P5.6bn for 9M). To the upside, asset quality improved, with NPL ratio down 7bps QoQ to 1.59% and NPL cover steady at 162%.

Highlights:

Gros loans grew 15.6% YoY accelerating a tad from the 14.9% in 2Q24 (net loans +16.6% vs 16.3%). Corporate loans led the growth, up 16.6% YoY, while consumer loans were up 12.3%. Within consumers, credit cards were up 16.6% and auto loans were up 15.7%. Loan yields were broadly flat QoQ

Deposits fell 2.7% YoY and 6.0% QoQ, as the bank let go of about P160bn worth of TDs QoQ. These were replaced with wholesale funds (short-term bills/bonds) which nearly doubled QoQ. Funding costs surged 25bps QoQ as a result. That said, the move MAY have been justified. Time deposits have lots of “hidden costs” including a 75bps stamp duty (DST), c25bps deposit insurance, and the RRR (recently reduced from 9.5% to 7.0%. Hence even if wholesale funds are more expensive on a headline basis, the net cost could be lower than TDs. Bigger picture, though, it does highlight that MBT is far more reliant on expensive funding compared to BDO/BPI.

NIMs collapsed 27bps QoQ and 33bps YoY in 3Q24 owing largely to the funding issues.

Fee incomes remained soft, up 3% YoY. There was also a 17% drop in miscellaneous income in 9M24 (-49% YoY in 3Q).

The bank booked monster trading/fx gains of P6bn in 3Q24 alone, making up for the softness of other revenues.

Opex grew 11% in 9M24. However, for 3Q24 alone, opex jumped 17% YoY, with compensation expenses surging 21% YoY and 15% QoQ.

Provisions fell 48% in 9M24m but were up 17% in 3Q alone. Asset quality improved QoQ, with NPL ratio down 7bps to 1.59%, while NPL cover was broadly stable at 162% (vs 163% in 2Q).

Meralco 9M core profit rises 17%

Core income up 17% for 9M24. Meralco reported net income of P33.8bn (+19% YoY) for 9M. Adjusting for one-off items, core income was P35.1bn (+17%). Derived 3Q core income was P11.9bn (+10% YoY) which we believe is ahead of Street estimates.  The core distribution business is growing steadily while the non-regulated businesses continue to do well, even if the profits are now well off their peaks.

3Q volume growth moderates to +4%. Volume growth for 3Q slowed to 4.1%, bringing 9M unit growth to +7.1%. Most segments saw a slowdown in growth but did not contract in 3Q. Residential volumes still grew 6.5%, which management said was largely due to higher temperatures. Industrial volumes grew only 0.1% in the quarter.

Regulatory decision on current regulatory period (“5RP”). Management brought up media reports quoting the ERC chair, who said that a vote within the ERC has been made regarding Meralco’s 5th Regulatory Period (5RP). The vote apparently favored Meralco’s application to treat 5RP as a lapsed period and thus fix its tariff to what it was for 4RP (= P1.35/kwh). Such a decision, if indeed promulgated, would have a substantial impact on Meralco’s immediate profit as well as for the entire period up to Jun 2026. We estimate the annualized impact would be in the range of P6bn (post-tax), excluding the impact of provision reversals.

Chromite deal unlikely to be closed by year-end, greenfield plant now delayed. We reckon the likelihood of the Chromite deal being completed by year-end is becoming slimmer by the day.  “Chromite” is the name of the deal for Meralco to buy 40% of the LNG complex (~2,500MW of LNG plants + LNG terminal) controlled by San Miguel.  With two of its key conditions still pending (approval by competition authorities and regulatory approval of a key power supply agreement), we believe deal completion is unlikely by year-end.

Furthermore, management also indicated that only one unit (437MW) of the greenfield Excellent Energy (EERI) LNG plant, part of the Chromite deal, could be exporting power by Dec. We assume this means only one unit (out of three) could be in the commissioning phase by Dec. The next two units are now supposed to be undergoing the same by Jan and Feb. The entire ~1,300MW EERI facility was supposed to be in commercial operation by Dec 2024, in time for its contractual commitments to Meralco.  We believe further delays cannot be discounted.

Guidance: core income “north of P43bn”. Management provided updated guidance for FY24 core income, saying it will be “north of P43bn”. This appears to be too conservative a guidance to be particularly meaningful.

Meralco refunds of P16bn once ERC releases 5RP ruling?

Meralco head of regulatory affairs Atty Ronald Valles claims Meralco may give consumers a refund of as much as P16bn. Valles claims this could arise once the ERC decision on its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) is released. Regis thinks the claims of refund are misleading - Meralco is, in fact, asking the regulator to fix the tariff for its 5RP is be fixed (@ P1.35/kwh) by considering 5RP as a “lapsed” period. This would imply Meralco would NOT be issuing refunds. P1.35/kwh is the current tariff charged and is the approved tariff for 4RP.

Wilcon 9M24 net profit falls 22%; behind

Wilcon’s 9M24 net profit fell 22% to P2.1bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. 3Q24 earnings declined 33% YoY and 21% QoQ on both lower revenue and margins. 3Q24 net sales fell 3% YoY and 4% QoQ. SSSG worsened to -8.5%, from -2.0% in 2Q24. Transaction count fell 3.8% YoY while basket size dropped 4.8% YoY (vs flat transaction count and a 2% contraction in basket size in 2Q24). GPM fell 90bps QoQ and 80bps YoY (to 38.8%). The company is discounting certain SKUs in order to liquidate old inventory. EBIT margin fell 440bps YoY, partly due to higher expansion-related expenses on new stores. The company opened three new stores in 3Q24, bringing the total number to 98. Management expects weakness to persist due to the oversupply of condominiums in Metro Manila. New building permits also remain low.

Robinsons Retail core income up 8% to P4.1bn; EBIT up 4%

RRHI reported net income of P7.81bn (+202% YoY) for 9M24, inclusive of gains from the BPI-Robinsons Bank merger. Core income, as reported by RRHI, was P4.07bn (+8% YoY) for 9M24. Core income was P1.43bn for 3Q, practically unchanged YoY (+0.1%). The operating results are trending behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Net sales rose +3% for 3Q and 9M while EBIT was up 4% in 9M and +0.6% in 3Q alone. Groupwide SSSG dipped slightly in 3Q (+0.5%) vs 1H (+0.7%). The Food segment (i.e. groceries) saw a modest uptick - Revenues for 3Q grew 4.8% (3.7% in 1H24), while SSSG was +2% (+1.5% in 1H24). Drug store segment reported sales growth of 9% in 3Q (vs 8% in 1H); SSSG was 3.8% in 9M. The other segments (department stores, DIY and specialty) are seeing YoY drops.

Tanduay Distillers sells entire stake in ethanol unit to affiliate

Tanduay Distillers sold its 94.74% stake in Asian Alcohol Corp. (AAC) to Prior Holdings Corp. (PHC) which is a privately-held company of Lucio Tan. AAC owns the second largest distillery in the country located in Negros Occidental. Distillery has a rated capacity of 210k liters of ethyl alcohol daily that are sold to Tanduay, representing 70% of its requirement. AAC ceased operations in late 2022 “to stop incurring further losses due to high operating cost.” LT Group was close to selling Asian Alcohol to Victorias Milling in 2022 for P2.21bn but this was mutually called off.

San Miguel unit takes over Sual coal-fired plant in Pangasinan

Sual Power (SPI) has taken over the 1,200-megawatt (MW) Sual Coal-Fired Thermal Power Plant in Pangasinan province after the contract between TeaM Energy Corp. and the government expired. In a statement at the weekend, TeaM Energy said it handed over the power plant to the government through the Power Sector Assets and Liabilities Management Corp. (PSALM), and National Power Corp. (Napocor) on Oct. 25. Mitsuhiro Kojima, officer-in-charge at TeaM Energy stated that there has been close coordination between Napocor, PSALM, and SPI to ensure a seamless transition process. He also stated that the plant has been turned over in excellent condition, ready to continue generating electricity for the Luzon grid. SPI, a unit of San Miguel Global Power Holdings Corp. (SMGP) and the independent power producer administrator (IPPA) of the Sual facility, received the power plant from PSALM. The power plant, the largest coal-fired power plant in the Philippines in terms of installed capacity, has been providing power to the Luzon grid since 1999 under a 25-year build-operate-transfer scheme that ended this year.

Philippine ports’ container throughput up 7% YoY in 3Q24; MICT volumes up 6% YoY

Philippine ports handled 1.43mn TEUs in 3Q24, higher by 7% YoY and accelerating from the 5% pace in 2Q24. This brings nationwide foreign container volumes up 4% YoY to 4.1mn TEUs in 9M24.

In 3Q24 alone, ICTSI’s MICT grew volumes by 6% YoY to 731k TEUs, mildly decelerating from the +7% rate in 2Q24. MICT lost market share during the quarter, with Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbor posting a +17% growth rate during the quarter. This brings MICT’s 9M24 volumes up 4% YoY to 2.13mn TEUs. To reach Regis’ forecast of 2.99mn TEUs (+7% YoY) for this year, volumes in 4Q24 would need to reach 857k TEUs, up 14% YoY and 17% QoQ; This appears difficult to achieve. Regis estimates MICT accounts for 23% of consolidated volumes and 22% of revenues for ICTSI (ICT PM).

ICT will be reporting 3Q results on November 5 (Briefing at 5pm). Regis anticipates 3Q organic volume growth could be a tad softer than the 6% growth rate in 2Q. Nevertheless, organic revenue growth could be much faster than the 17% YoY pace in 2Q, given the tariff hikes in MICT (+10%), Subic (+15%), and Melbourne (+10%) in 3Q.

Colliers: net office take-up turns negative in 3Q24, unsold residential inventory level rises

During its 3Q24 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila 3Q24 net office take-up turned negative for the first time since 4Q21. Net office take-up was -33,000sqm, from +98,000sqm in 2Q24 (+17,000sqm in 3Q23). 57,000sqm of office space vacated by POGOs. As a result, Metro Manila office vacancy rose 0.2ppt to 18.5%.

Due to the POGO ban, Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 20.5% by end-2024. POGO firms still occupy c. 400,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila. Rental rates in most Metro Manila business districts fell c. 1% QoQ. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels.

Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that the level of unsold residential inventory represents c. 70 months’ worth of presales (from c. 60 months as of 2Q24). This appears to be due to an increase in unsold inventory level and a reduction in residential take-up. 3Q24 residential take-up fell c. 20% YoY to 3,100 units (flat QoQ).

PAGCOR 3Q24 revenue from e-games falls 31% QoQ

PAGCOR reported that its revenue from e-games (such as e-Bingo and e-casino) fell 31% QoQ to P7.56bn (in 3Q24). This could imply weakness in the GGR of DigiPlus (PLUS PM), which had a 50% market share as of 2Q24. Meanwhile, PAGCOR revenue from licensed casinos grew 5% QoQ to P8.44bn. Since GGR of Okada Manila fell 8% QoQ in 3Q24, the GGR of other licensed casinos should show stronger improvement. Solaire North likely showed stronger GGR in 3Q24, since it only contributed for one month in 2Q24.

Government infrastructure spending drops in August

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 12.9% YoY to P989.4bn in 8M24. Growth moderated sharply from the 17% pace in 7M24. This implies infrastructure spending for August alone fell by 8% YoY to P125bn. Officials cited project delays of major infrastructure projects, timing issues on encashment of payments to contractors, and adverse weather conditions.

To reach the government’s FY24E target of P1.47tn (+4% YoY), spending for the rest of the year could fall by 11% YoY. For 2025, the government plans to spend P1.53tn, up 4% YoY and equivalent to 5.4% of GDP. The government aims to spend 5-6% of GDP annually for infrastructure through 2028.

Government debt rises 2% MoM to P15.89tn in end-September

The national government’s outstanding debt rose to P15.89tn in end-September, 2.2% (+P343bn) higher than the end-Aug level of P15.55tn due to net issuances of domestic and external debt. Domestic debt rose by 1.3% (+P145bn) MoM to P10.94tn due to net issuance of new government securities. External debt rose by 4.2% (+198bn) MoM to P4.96tn partly due to the issuance of US$2.5bn global bonds. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of ~60% by year-end and is aiming to reduce the ratio further to 51% by 2028. As of end-June 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 60.9%.
