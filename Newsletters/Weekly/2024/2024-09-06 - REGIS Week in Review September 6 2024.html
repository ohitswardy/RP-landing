REGIS REPORT

06 September 2024

The PSEi bounced back this week, ending +0.56% WoW to 6936, as shares continued to be supported by consistent, albeit lighter, foreign inflows which persisted for the fourth straight week in a row. The benchmark initially drifted flattish around the 6900-line earlier in the week, before finding support in the latter amid a better than expected CPI turnout. Data released on Thursday morning showed that CPI eased to 3.3% in August, substantially lower than the 3.6% consensus forecast and the 4.4% prior reading in July. This reading brought the 8M24 average inflation to 3.6%, which may further come down as price pressures, particularly on rice, are expected to further ease in the latter part of the year. The latest print helped bolster hopes for further policy easing in the coming months. Note that the BSP Governor previously signaled 1 more possible rate cut before the year ends. The next BSP policy decision is on October 17.

Breadth of index members was virtually neutral with an even split between gainers and losers (14 gainers, 14 losers, and 2 unchanged). Leading the way this time was GTCAP +8.97% WoW as its sub Toyota Philippines is expected to benefit from the recent strength in PHP, which broke below the 56-level this week – first time since March. Also adding to the optimism over the conglomerate is the recent rally in MBT over the past weeks, which also added another +2.18% gain this week. Consumers were a generally bright spot as well with CNPF +8.32% and PGOLD +8.11% posting hefty gains; its peers JFC -1.85% and URC -1.04% however trailed behind this time. On the other end, worst laggard in the benchmark was MER -4.75%. Note that there was news this week of the Ombudsman suspending Energy Regulatory Commission Chair Dimalanta due to alleged neglect of duty in connection with a complaint filed by a consumer group, which relates to the rate calculation of Meralco.

Market activity was much lighter this week at a daily average of just $95m, -33% WoW, coming from a high base due to the active MSCI rebalancing in the previous week. This figure however is roughly inline with the YTD average. Foreign participation eased to 47% vs 56% last week. Net foreign buying stood at $22m, marking the fourth consecutive weekly inflow streak, albeit the lightest of that period so far.

RESEARCH
Security Bank Corp - Turning a corner (2) – Deep value turnaround story
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

ROE turnaround under-appreciated, at 0.33x P/bk

SECB’s underwhelming headline 2Q24 NPAT masked a continued turnaround in ROEs. Management is now guiding for sustained sequential gains, crossing into double-digits by 2025E, with a medium term goal of getting ROE back to the teens. Consumer/SME loan growth of 37% YoY in 2Q (now 32% of total) should allow for NIM expansion even as interest rates fall. Its investment in a new IT stack could help it scale up top-line efficiently, and control credit risks. Our revised forecasts for 2025/26E (+10%/18%) are conservative relative to guidance but are 20-25% above the Street. Despite a recent rally, the stock still offers deep value at 0.33x P/bk and 3.4x PER on our FY25E numbers. We raise our TP to P160/sh, for 136% potential return.

NIMs to benefit from lower rates; consumer/MSME loans surge 37%

Consumer/MSME loans jumped 37% YoY in 2Q24, accounting for 32% of total loans, the highest among banks we cover. Management reckon NIMs could expand from falling interest rates given the high yield and/or fixed-rate nature of the portfolio. The bank’s relatively low CASA could see funding costs fall as deposits reprice downward.

Monetizing IT investments, managing credit costs

The bank has invested heavily in its new IT stack, and opex growth should moderate from here. As it rolls out its new apps, SECB hopes to monetize the digitalization, allowing it to scale up its customer acquisition/retention, and improve cross-selling. Better credit scoring could also help control asset quality, as the bank re-focuses on “quality” high net worth and mass-affluent customers. We are wary, though, and forecast credit costs to stay in the 90-100bps range.

Deep value, compelling risk-reward

SECB stock has massively de-rated, falling to a decade-low of P60 earlier this week. Despite a 15% rally from the trough, the stock is still in deep value, trading on 0.33x P/bk, 3.4x PER and paying a 4.35% div yield on our FY25E estimates. While our (ROE-g)/(COE-g) derived TP of P160/sh seems aggressive (potential 136% return), we note that the stock would only be at 0.77x P/bk and 7.9x PER even at our TP. Key risks include intense competition and rising credit costs.

Meralco (MER PM) is increasing its stake in SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM) to 47.3% from 35.7%. DMCI will be issuing P10bn worth of perpetual convertible prefs (coupon of 4%) to Dacon to fund its portion of the deal (conversion price is not fixed).

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Inflation eases to 3.3% in August

Headline inflation eased to 3.3% in August from 4.4% in July. This was below consensus forecast of 3.6% and came in at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.2%-4.0%. Slowdown due largely to (a) favorable base effects on rice CPI, (b) slower price increases/negative base effects in other food items such as meat, fish, vegetables, and sugar, and (c) lower fuel prices, which drove transport inflation lower. These items more than compensated for the upticks in electricity prices. Core inflation also eased to 2.6% in August from 2.9% in July, with the “non-food” core inflation moderating to 3.0% from 3.3%. Brings 8M24 average headline inflation rate to 3.6%, within the BSP’s range of 2-4%. BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. previously signaled for another 25bps cut in the policy rate this year, after implementing a 25bps cut last August. Monetary Board’s next meeting is schedule on Oct 17.

Consumer loans jump 20% YoY in 2Q24; at a record-high 22% of total loans

Consumer loans in 2Q24 surged 19.7% YoY to P2,807bn, accelerating from the already-heady 17.6% pace in the previous quarter, and the fastest YoY clip since 4Q2016. On a QoQ basis, consumer loans expanded 4.2% vs 1Q24. The growth in consumer loans far exceeded the 10.3% YoY pace of total banking sector loans, suggesting that corporate loans grew 7.9% YoY in 2Q24. As a result, consumer loans now account for 21.8% of total, a new all-time high.

Asset quality deteriorated slightly, with NPL ratio up 2bps QoQ to 5.82%, but still down 43bps YoY. Interestingly, the NPL slip was much more benign than overall loans, which saw NPL ratio jump 19bps QoQ to 3.69%. We estimate that non-consumer loan NPLs were up 23bps QoQ to 3.10%. In nominal terms, consumer NPLs grew 4.6% QoQ, while non-consumer loans jumped 10.4% QoQ.

Mortgages grew 13.5% YoY to P1,039bn, accelerating sharply from the 7.9% pace in 1Q24, and posting the fastest growth since 3Q2018. Meanwhile, mortgage NPL ratio improved further to 6.72%, down 22bps QoQ and 90bps YoY. In PHP terms, NPLs fell 1.1% QoQ to P69.8bn

Motor Vehicle (MV) loans also sped up, growing 18.7% YoY to P557bn (was +17.6% in 1Q24, and fastest since 1Q2018). With MVs, auto loans surged 20.3% YoY to P526bn, while motorcycle loans contracted 3.9% YoY to P31bn. Asset quality improved dramatically with NPL ratio down 27bps QoQ to 5.72%. The drop was driven by auto loans (5.08% vs 5.36% in 1Q), while motorcycle NPLs blipped (16.57% vs 16.10% in 1Q).

Credit card receivable growth slowed slightly, up 28.8% to P797bn (vs 30.0% in 1Q). To the downside, NPL ratio jumped 43bps QoQ to 4.56%. To be fair, some banks (notably BPI) have consciously eased their risk metrics, contending that credit card yields are high enough to warrant higher credit cost.

Salary loans grew 27.2% YoY to P366bn, though this was a slowdown from the 29.3% in 1Q24 and 54.5% pace in 2Q23. NPL ratios jumped 55bps QoQ to 5.94%.

The continued acceleration of consumer loans should help banks defend NIM compression as benchmark interest rates fall. We are slightly wary though of rising NPLs, particularly in the credit card and salary loan categories, as these could indicate increased stress among households.

Of the banks we cover, Security Bank (SECB PM) has the highest proportion of consumer/MSME loans at 32% to total, which is reflected in higher average loan yields compared to the Big Three.

Digital payments re-surge in July, up 35% YoY, to a new record high

Digital adoption re-accelerated in July, as payments over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped 35.2% YoY to P1,479bn (c$26bn) – a new all-time high – compared to the 25.7% YoY pace recorded in June. This took the 7M24 figure to P9,454bn (c$167bn), up 34.7%, faster than the 29% clip in FY2023.

The July rebound was driven by PesoNet – the rail for batched, high-value transactions – which grew 30.1% YoY to P868bn, compared to the 14.0% increase in June and 25% in May. Year-to-date, PesoNet transactions were up 28% YoY to P5,557bn, up from the 22% growth set in FY2023.

Meanwhile, InstaPay – the platform for smaller-ticket, real-time payments – sustained its growth trajectory, up 43% YoY to P611bn in July (was 44.5% in June), taking the 7M24 figure to P3,897bn, up 44.9% YoY (was 42.0% in FY2023).

In a further sign of broadening out of digital acceptance, the number of transactions over InstaPay continued to explode, growing 62% YoY in July (+65% in 7M24), bringing the average ticket size down to P5,233 (about $92). This to us indicates increased usage of the platform for everyday purchases.

Of the banks we cover, BPI (BPI PM) has made digitalization central to its strategy to vastly increase its customer base. However, Globe Telecom’s (GLO PM) affiliate GCash/Mynt continues to dominate the consumer digital payments space.

Commercial bank loans expand by 10.4% in July; M3 growth accelerates to 7.4%

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 10.4% YoY in July, mildly faster than the 10.1% pace in June. This was largely due to big rebounds in professional/scientific sectors and sustained growth in other key sectors. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose by 7.2% in July from 6.6% in June and the fastest pace since Oct 2023.

Government debt rises to P15.69tn in July

The national government’s outstanding debt rose by 1.3% MoM (P206bn) to P15.69tn as of end-July. Domestic debt rose 1.7% (P180bn) MoM to P10.75tn due to net issuances of government securities, while external debt rose by 0.5% (P26.5bn) MoM to P4.94tn. The increase due to net availment of project loans (P5bn) and third-currency upward revaluation (P35bn), offset by the impact of PHP’s appreciation (P14bn). Note that the government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of ~60% by year-end. As of end-June 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 62.4%. Government aims to lower the ratio further to 51% by 2028.

Jose Querubin to take remaining Monetary Board seat

BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said that Jose Querubin will take the last remaining seat at the Monetary Board, which has seven members. Mr. Querubin will replace Anita Linda, Aquino, who resigned after being linked to the ghost employees scandal. He was previously the President of United Coconut Planters Bank and Treasurer of Solid Bank.

Ombudsman suspends ERC chair Dimalanta for 6 months

The Office of the Ombudsman ordered the six-month preventive suspension of Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) Chair Monalisa Dimalanta. Due to alleged neglect of duty in connection with a complaint filed by consumer group, National Association of Electricity Consumers for Reforms, Inc. (Nasecore). Nasecore complained about the ERC’s failure “to recalculate the rate of Meralco that protects the interest of the public.” Nasecore cited the ERC’s postponement of Meralco’s presentation of evidence for its 5RP capex due to the lack of a pre-trial order, which Ms. Dimalanta allegedly had sole authority to issue.

Department of Energy plans to endorse EV incentives program to President Marcos by year-end

The Department of Energy hopes to endorse its proposed executive order (EO) on electric vehicle incentive strategy to President Marcos by the end of the year, according to Patrick T. Aquino, director of the Energy Utilization Management Bureau at the Department of Energy (DoE). The Incentive Strategy includes direct rebates or discounts for purchases; tax credits; value-added tax exemption; fuel cost savings through special electricity rates of EV charging; trade in bonus for vehicle owners; and incentives to install EV charging infrastructure at their home.

The Incentive Strategy will be like the Comprehensive Automotive Resurgence Strategy (CARS) program, which will be supported by funding. To further drive EV adoption, the DoE is looking at mandating gasoline stations to put up charging stations. Note that sales of battery EVs reached 811 while hybrids reached 261 in the first four months of 2024, while battery EV sales were 1359 and hybrids were 6156 in 2023.

Raw sugar output dropping to 25-year low

The country’s raw sugar production could fall below 1.8mn metric tons (MT) in the new 2024-2025 crop year (vs. 1.9mn in previous year), the lowest level in 25 years, according to the Sugar Regulatory Administration. Officials cited effects of El Nino. Note that sugar accounts for 1.05% of CPI basket.
