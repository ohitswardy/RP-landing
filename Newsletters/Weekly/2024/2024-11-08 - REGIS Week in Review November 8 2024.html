REGIS REPORT

08 November 2024

The PSEi extended its downturn to 3 consecutive weeks and crucially broke below the psychological 7000-level for the first time in nearly 2 months, -2.32% WoW to 6977.18. This brought the benchmark’s decline from its October 7 peak to -7.6% and its YTD gain down to +8.17%.

After a quiet start to the week, foreign selling in the market accelerated as it became apparent that Donald Trump will be the next president of the United States. Note that Regis research team released a note leading into the elections which highlighted the potential negative impact of a Trump win on Philippines – in particular, Trump’s protectionist stance posing a threat on the business process outsourcing (BPO) industry and his push for deportation’s potential detrimental impact on remittances, among a multitude of other issues. As such, the most widely foreign-held stocks in the Philippines, particularly those in the Property and Banking sectors, suffered steep declines this week.

While the overwhelming focus among investors was the US elections, note it was also a very busy week of macro data and corporate earnings on the ground. On the macro front, 3Q GDP failed to help provide relief to sentiment as it came in half a percentage below forecast, growing by only +5.2% YoY vs +5.7% consensus and +6.4% in 2Q. This was primarily dragged by a slowdown in government spending which only grew by +5% YoY in 3Q vs +11.9% in 2Q. On the opposite end, private consumption continued to show signs of improvement, +5.1% YoY in 3Q vs +4.7% in 2Q. Note that at this pace, 4Q GDP growth will have to reach at least +6.5% in order for the FY2024 figure to meet Regis’ +6% forecast (which is still attainable given 4Q seasonality effects.) Meanwhile the October inflation data came in at 2.3%, slightly accelerating from September’s 1.9% and inline with the Street consensus. These 2 data points should support another 25bp rate cut in the BSP’s next policy decision in December.

On the results front, ALI’s 9M net profit came in inline with both Regis and Street forecasts with its residential sales was once again supported by its high-end, premium projects. However, its mid-income segment saw a -20% YoY contraction in reservation sales to P32.1b in 3Q, reflecting the weakness in the residential market as indicated by the prior data from property consultants Colliers and Leechiu. Meanwhile, RLC’s net profit came in slightly behind Regis and Street estimates as 9M presales plummeted by -82% YoY P3.2b. Note that for 3Q the company reported net cancellations amounting to P3b but this was primarily due to the company’s changes in credit policy. If it were not for this change, 3Q pre-sales would have hit P5b, -10% QoQ and +3% YoY, but it does imply that presales for prior periods were overstated. Property names were among the worst performers this week with SMPH -6.69% WoW, ALI -5.2%, and outside of the benchmark RLC -3.51% and MEG -9.09%.

Meanwhile results from consumers MONDE and CNPF both showed mid-single digit revenue growth and an improvement in gross margins, showing some early signs of recovery as we head to 4Q which is seasonally strong quarter for the sector. Consumer names fared mixed this week with URC +0.82% WoW (results on Tuesday), CNPF unch, PGOLD -1.84%, JFC -1.96% and MONDE -2.94%.

Another stock in focus this week was ICT, which is arguably the most widely held stock in the market among foreign investors. It was the heaviest foreign sold name as presents optically a negative outlook for the shipping industry moving forward. Net foreign selling on the name exceeded $40m this week – a considerable chunk of the $133m net outflow for the broader market. Despite that however the stock fared much better than the index, down by just -0.89% WoW. The company disclosed that it bought back over 4m shares on Thursday – when the stock ended +1.2% on the day after earlier falling by 7%, which had been inline with the widespread post-US election selloff in the local market. Other top foreign sold names were BDO -3.8%, ALI -5.2%, SMPH -6.69%, BPI -3.63%, SM -2.66%, MBT +0.66% and JGS -1.23%.

The continued selloff in the market this week was backed by an uptick in turnover – average daily activity stood at $103.6m, +14% WoW, with a nearly neutral split between local-foreign investor participation. The $133m net foreign selling this week has wiped out the headline YTD inflow of the market which now stands at a net outflow of $89m. That said, note that this number includes the nearly $300m outflow in BLOOM related to the settlement the company’s dispute with GGAM from earlier this year. As such, “real” YTD flow in the market is still positive.

Philippine Strategy - Reverberations from across the Pacific
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6636 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Status quo vs change

The U.S. presidential elections are only a couple of days away, and the outcome should have important implications for the Philippines. A Harris presidency will likely mean an extension of the status quo. A Trump return, however, could lead to major changes in the bilateral relationship. We offer our views on how some of these changes might affect the Philippines but think, broadly, that a Trump administration would be incrementally negative for the country over the medium-term.

Tariffs and trade

Mr. Trump has vowed to impose universal tariffs on imports into the U.S. This will have an impact on PH exports to the U.S., which amounted to US$11.4bn in FY23 (Fig 1&2). If we extend Trump’s protectionist leanings further, a threat to the outsourcing industry cannot be discounted. Outsourcing (BPOs) contributed US$35bn of revenue, around 8% of Philippine GDP. The property sector would be under threat because of how much office space the BPOs occupy (Fig 5&6). The potential loss of jobs would also have grave implications for consumer / retail firms. That said, offshoring reportedly increased in Trump’s first presidency.

Immigration, deportation and remittances

With Trump’s promise to deport “millions” of immigrants, the implications for the Philippines’ overseas remittances cannot be ignored. The US is home to over 4mn Filipinos (almost 1mn “temporary” or undocumented) and accounts for 34% of remittances (ex- seafarers), around US$14bn (Fig 3&4). A decline would have a major impact on the country’s current account and consumer spending.

Fossil fuels and renewable energy

Policies favoring fossil fuel extraction could be enacted under a Trump presidency while those favoring renewable energy (RE) could be reduced or revoked. Lower fossil fuel prices (oil and coal) would be a likely consequence. Ironically, coal firm Semirara (SCC PM) may be affected negatively as would RE firms like ACEN (ACEN PM). Consumers of fossil fuels like Cebu Air (CEB PM) would be better off, as would power generation companies.

FX and interest rates

Trump’s statements on Fed independence could affect its monetary policy, thus influencing short-term USD interest rates. Trump has also espoused views favoring a weaker USD, which would be positive for PH importers and those whose costs are in USD. Lower interest rates and a weak USD would also boost Philippine financial markets.

Geopolitics and the South China Sea

Because of its location, the Philippines received new-found importance with the Biden administration following rising tensions between China and the U.S. This has allowed the country to take a more assertive stance on issues relating to the South China Sea. The transactional inclinations of Trump could change that.

 
Ayala Land - 3Q24 premium sales rise 50% YoY while mid-income sales fall 19%
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M24 earnings in line

Ayala Land’s 9M24 net profit rose 15% to P21.2bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. More than the earnings, however, what caught our attention was 1) the strong residential sales of premium projects and 2) management’s rather optimistic outlook on the core (mid-income) residential segment despite the weak 3Q24 reservation sales of that market.

3Q24 reservation sales grow 16% YoY, led by premium segment

3Q24 reservation sales grew 16% YoY (-9% QoQ) to P32.1bn. However, there was a divergence in the performances of the two main segments. Sales of premium projects rose 50% YoY (flat QoQ) to P21.6bn, while sales of core projects fell 20% YoY and QoQ to P10.6bn (see Figure 3). For 9M24, reservation sales reached P100.5bn, up 17% YoY and tracking ahead of our forecast.


More core projects to be launched

Despite the high levels of unsold inventory in Metro Manila industry-wide (over 30 months’ worth of presales) and the company’s weak 3Q24 reservation sales of core projects, Ayala Land intends to increase launches of this segment in 4Q24 (and in 2025). Management explained that it is now comfortable with its unsold inventory level for the core segment, which stands at 16 months of presales (vs 19 months for premium, 18 months overall).

Furthermore, management intends to focus launches on certain areas, such as Cavite and Laguna, where the company’s (and the industry’s) inventory levels are low. Lastly, management is counting on a drop in mortgage rates, in light of the recent policy rate cuts by the BSP.

3Q24 mall revenue growth slows

3Q24 mall revenue growth slowed to 4% YoY from 7% in 2Q24. This is due to the redevelopment of the company’s four flagship malls, which has led to about 5% of floor area being “in transition” (not generating revenue).

Office vacancy increases 2ppt QoQ on new completions

3Q24 office revenue grew 5% QoQ and 9% YoY, thanks to the completion of two new office buildings, which added 3% to floor area. While the new buildings caused a 2ppt QoQ increase in Ayala Land’s office vacancy (to 11%), its vacancy is still well below the industry’s 16% level.

 
ICTSI - FX, operational disruptions cause 3Q24 miss but story is intact
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

3Q24 profit up 28% to US$208mn, behind Regis but in line with Street forecasts

ICT’s positive profit momentum was sustained in 3Q24, reporting a 28% YoY increase in profit to common of US$208mn. The results, however, disappointed relative to our expectations (but in line with Street), as the large sequential improvements we had anticipated did not pan out. Organic volume growth decelerated to 4% and yields, as well as EBITDA margins, were sequentially softer. To the upside, management sounded more upbeat about how 4Q is shaping up, which suggests that ICT’s growth story remains intact.


Organic volume growth decelerates to 4% in 3Q

Organic volumes grew 4% in 3Q24, moderating from the 6% pace in 2Q. Management said that growth is “normalizing” but we reckon the slowdown can be attributed partly to volume softness at its ports in Manila, Mexico, and Rio,which we believe were temporary. Management also cited operational disruptions, such as bad weather in the Americas, as well as rail and truck strikes which affected some of its ports during the quarter.

Sequential dip in yield per TEU blamed on unfavorable FX

Yield per TEU surprisingly dropped to US$204 in 3Q24 from US$206 in 2Q, despite the implementation of several tariff hikes, including the 10% tariff hike at MICT in August. Management attributed the sequential decline largely on unfavorable FX and lower storage revenues. We reckon the weather disruptions also contributed to the yield softness. As a result, EBITDA for the quarter barely improved sequentially at US$452mn, with EBITDA margin slipping by 70bps QoQ to 65.3%.

Durban, other potential M&As, expansion updates

Volume growth should re-accelerate in 4Q, after the company secured long-term contracts with new shipping lines in Ecuador and after the yard expansion in Mexico was completed in late September. The latter will allow them to grow volumes again there this month. While management is awaiting to schedule the second set of hearings for the legal challenge in Durban, South Africa (likely by 1Q25), they are actively participating in other M&A opportunities in other countries.

 
Robinsons Land - 3Q24: residential segment reports a loss; presales turn negative
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M24 recurring profit rises 4%, slightly behind

Robinsons Land’s 9M24 headline profit rose 13% to P10.0bn, slightly behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Meanwhile, recurring profit in 9M24 increased 4% to P9.3bn, also slightly behind, as the residential segment delivered two negative surprises in 3Q24: 1) net presales of RLC standalone projects turned negative, and 2) the residential segment (excluding contributions from JV projects) reported an operating loss.

3Q24 net presales turn negative

9M24 net presales (of standalone projects) fell 82% to P3.2bn. For 3Q24 alone, the company reported net cancellations of P3.0bn, from P5.5bn of positive net presales in 2Q24. The company cancelled P8bn of presales due to a tightening of its credit standards. Thus, this quarter’s cancellations are not necessarilyindicative of a drastic change in demand conditions (or buyer balance sheets) in 3Q24. It does, arguably, imply that presales for prior periods were overstated.

If not for the change in credit policy, 3Q24 presales would have hit P5bn, down 10% QoQ and up 3% YoY. Management expects 4Q24 net presales to remain in the P4bn-P5bn range, slightly weaker than the 2023 average (of P5.3bn). JV projects fared better, though presales of such projects also dipped in 3Q24 (down 6% QoQ and 12% YoY) as well as 9M24 (down 15%).

Residential segment reports an operating loss

After falling 49% QoQ in 2Q24, residential revenue (of standalone projects) fell another 26% QoQ (to P897mn) in 3Q24. Management previously guided that residential revenue would be weak this year (due to timing issues) but would recover next year, as presales from 2021 (when reservation sales grew 48%) begin revenue recognition. As a result of the revenue decline, the segment reported an operating loss of P210mn in 3Q24 and 9M24 EBIT fell 60% to P610mn.

Management guided that 4Q24 residential revenue would hit c. P1bn. While this would be an increase of over 10% QoQ, it would also represent a decline of 60% YoY. It would also mean that FY24 residential revenue would be 40% lower than the 2023 figure.

New mall openings boost revenue

3Q24 mall revenue grew 4% QoQ thanks to newly opened malls, which added 3% to floor area. 3Q24 mall revenue grew 11% YoY, which we estimate implies c. 6% same-mall revenue growth, within the historical range of 5%-8%.

Office vacancy remains flat QoQ

Office revenue was flat QoQ, as was vacancy (at 14%). However, this could increase by 1ppt in 4Q24 as POGO firms vacate by the end of the year, unless other tenants take more space.

 
Monde Nissin - 3Q: Wide margins still propping up earnings
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M core profit up 31% on margin recovery

Monde posted core profit of P7.45bn for 9M, up 31% as margins expanded on lower input costs. For 3Q alone, revenues rose 3% while EBITDA jumped 14% YoY. Core income for 3Q was up only +8% due to FX losses on its USD cash holdings (management treats them as “core”). We view these results as being a tad better than Regis expectations as well as the Street’s.

APAC BFB revenue up 5% in 3Q

Revenue growth for its core APAC BFB operations grew 5% YoY in 3Q, roughly the same pace as 2Q. Top line growth was driven almost entirely by volumes / mix (i.e. not by price). We note a marked pick-up in biscuit volumes for 3Q with as production bottlenecks were addressed. Capacity issues held back volumes for the noodles segment in 3Q, but the resolution of these should also lift sales in 4Q.

Meanwhile, 3Q gross margins stayed high (38%) due to favorable input costs and productivity gains. Management says the gross margin gains are being partly reinvested in marketing spend (and partly retained for shareholders).

EBITDA breakeven for alternative meat segment

Monde’s alternative meat business was able to break even on EBITDA but still reported a P169m net loss for the quarter. Impairment charges (non-cash) were taken on some fixed assets. Cost cuts are helping to trim losses, but the more important problem of declining revenue faces no easy resolution. Revenue was down 5% YoY in 3Q and 2% in 9M.

Buyer’s remorse for Figaro?

Management expressed what we would describe as “buyer’s remorse” regarding its investment in restaurant operator Figaro (FCG PM). Monde invested P820m to buy 15% of Figaro last year, with hopes of being able extract product synergies. Those hopes appear to have been dashed.

Near-term outlook improving, aiming to pay more than 60% of profits

Management said that October numbers seem to have picked up, with revenues hitting a monthly record. This bodes well for 4Q, even if management continues to express caution about domestic economic conditions. The company is aiming to pay more than its target 60% of profits next year, depending on how cash flows shape up.

 
Century Pacific Food - 3Q: Steady amidst a challenging environment
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Continuing the 1H pace

9M net profit rose 14% to P5.28bn with 3Q profit up 15% to P1.6bn, both within expectations. While there is a sequential, seasonal dip for 3Q, the YoY growth pace for the quarter was a virtual mirror image of 1H for both for the top and bottom lines. Note, however, that top line growth was lifted by the non-branded OEM business (+44% YoY) as the branded business expanded by only 6%.

Branded business still affected by tight consumer purse-strings

Growth for the branded business (+6%) remained sub-par, especially relative to the firm’s aim of +10-15% medium-term growth. CNPF believes consumers are still struggling with higher prices of goods. Management estimates its prices are up ~20% over the last few years but consumer incomes have not kept up.

Gross margins widened more in 3Q

Gross profits grew +27% in 3Q as margins widened to almost 28%. This was a result of tamer input costs and the impact of cumulative price hikes over prior periods. EBITDA growth was much slower (+13% in 3Q), due to a deliberate effort to invest more in A&P while gross margins were high. We believe 4Q margins (gross and EBITDA) are unlikely to stray much from current levels given that the company has locked in inventory costs, and also intends to spend more on A&P.

We also expect EBITDA margins to stay at similar levels until 1H25. Managementhas room to resist price increases (given where current inventories and margins are) and/or adjust A&P spend if the situation warrants.

Early signs of improvement?

Based on CNPF’s sales and retailer feedback in Sept-Oct, consumer sentiment seems to be improving, and management is hopeful that it can benefit. For FY24, CNPF is sticking to its 10-15% profit growth guidance (Regis: +17%) and is also guiding for the same pace next year (Regis: +15%).

 
Metropolitan Bank & Trust Co - Sifting through the noise – target price raised to P100
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Solid numbers (ignoring the noise); TP raised 14%

MBT reported solid 9M24 NPAT of P35.7bn, up 12% YoY. The numbers were distorted, though, by the unwinding of cross-currency swap products. Looking past these, the bank posted impressive loan growth of 15.6%, and improving asset quality. MBT also posted P6bn worth of trading/fx gains in 3Q24, and should benefit from falling interest rates given its large treasury book. We raise our target price to P100/sh, for a potential total return of 38%. Maintain Buy.

Loans surge 15.6%, gaining share in corporates; NIMs muddled by swaps

Gross loan growth accelerated to 15.6% (from 14.9%), as the bank pushed hard to gain share in corporate loans (+18% YoY). Headline NIMs collapsed 27bps QoQ. However, this was due to the unwinding of deposit products that included cross-currency swaps, previously booking the swap costs as trading/fx losses. While these muddled YoY comparisons, the net impact to bottom line should be much less severe as trading/fx gains/losses normalize, too.

Improving asset quality provides buffers

Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio at 1.59%, down 7bps QoQ to the lowest level since before the Covid pandemic. NPL cover remains high at 162% providing buffers for new NPL formation. Meanwhile, the bank’s IT investments should allow the bank to ramp up consumer loans and fee incomes, at lower incremental expense growth.

TP increased to P100/sh, for 38% total return; Buy

We refresh our numbers, resulting in minor changes (3-4%) to our 2024-26E NPAT forecasts. We also adjust our target price up 14% from P88/sh to P100/sh as we roll over to our 2025E estimates. This represents a total potential return of 38%, as we believe the current dividend yield can be sustained, making up for the sluggish near-term EPS growth. We value the stock on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 12.25% (from 12.0%), COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%. This translates to a target P/bk of 1.06x on our 2025E book value estimates. Key risks include more aggressive competition for loan and deposit pricing, and volatility in the bank’s trading book.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Sell-off overdone; reducing target price but maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Sell-off overdone; Maintain Buy

We consider the decline of BLOOM shares to be excessive and view the current weakness to be a buying opportunity. BLOOM has fallen 22% YoY, underperforming the PCOMP by 35%. As a result, the stock now trades at just 6.8X FY25E EV/EBITDA and offers 38% total return to our revised target price of P10.40/share. While we expect Bloomberry’s first casino, Solaire Entertainment City (SEC), to face challenges near-term, its second site, Solaire North (SN), should help compensate, leading to net profit CAGR of 17% from 2024E[1]2026E (for the entire company). Thus, we maintain our Buy rating.

Expanding its footprint

In May 2024, Bloomberry opened Solaire North, doubling the company’s slot machines (2,700) and increasing its table capacity by 40% (160 tables). SN should ramp up over the coming quarters and we expect it to comprise 43% of BLOOM’s 2026E EBITDA.

Twin challenges on its home turf

SEC is set to face challenges on both demand and supply (competition). We expect demand to be affected by the POGO ban, since about 25% of GGR came from mainland Chinese, many of whom are likely involved in the POGO sector. Meanwhile, Suntrust (SUN PM) will open a new resort which will add 20% to table capacity and 13% to slot capacity in Entertainment City (in 2026E).

Revising estimates; profit to grow at 17% CAGR

We revise our estimates on BLOOM in light of the 1H24 performance, the supply and demand challenges at SEC, and our expected ramp-up of SN. As a result, we lowered our 2024E/2025E EBITDA forecasts by 8%-9%. While we expect net profit to fall 18% in 2024E, we forecast 17% profit CAGR from 2024E-2026E.

Lowering target price

We reduce our target price by 31% to P10.40/share. This new target is based on 8.0X FY25E EV/EBITDA, similar to the current valuations of Macau gaming firms, and much lower than BLOOM’s pre-COVID average of 11X.

 
Semirara Mining & Power Corp. - Higher power output lifts 3Q24 profit up 5%
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M24 profit falls 30% YoY to P15.7bn, in line with REGIS but behind Street FY24E

SCC reported that its core profit for 3Q24 was 8% lower YoY at P3.1bn. Excluding last year’s one-time income from wharfage fees, we estimate recurring profit for the quarter grew 5% YoY, after the power unit generated higher profits and more than compensated for the coal mining unit’s weakness. Cumulative 9M24 recurring profit was 30% lower YoY at P15.7bn, on trend to meet Regis’ FY24E of P20bn but looks behind consensus forecast of P21.8bn.

Coal mining: Unfavorable coal mix drags 3Q/9M ASPs lower

Mining profits fell 16% YoY to P1.67bn in 3Q24. Despite securing an income tax holiday extension, operations came in softer than we had anticipated, after ASPs for the quarter fell 17% YoY to P2,811 per MT. The ASP decline was larger than the 1% dip in its main benchmark index, ICI4, and the price was lower than our FY24E ASP of P3,000. Management attributed the softness to the higher proportion of non-commercial grade coal sales, which will only be resolved once it starts production in the higher-grade Acacia mine by 2H25. Note that our FY25E forecast assumes an ASP of P2,792 and ICI4 continues to hold at ~US$52 per MT, suggesting that our FY25E forecast remains intact.

Better plant availability leads to higher profits for power unit

The power unit contributed P1.4bn in 3Q24, up 43% YoY on the back of a 10% improvement in output. Plant availability factor stood at 83% in 9M24, with management guiding for an availability factor of 79% for FY24E and 91% for FY25E, the latter much higher than our forecast of 64%. The availability factor alone more than offset lower 9M24 ASPs of P4.95/kwh. Management anticipates WESM prices will soften to ~P4/kwh in 4Q24 and in 2025 due to the anticipated arrival of new capacity. Only 33% of SCC’s capacity is contracted.

Still waiting for approval of COC renewal

Management is closely monitoring updates from the Department of Energy (DOE) regarding its application to renew its Coal Operating Contract (COC). Management believes that it has fulfilled all the requirements of the DOE and is just waiting for the final approval.

3Q24 GDP slows to 5.2% on weak government spending, lower exports

GDP grew at 5.2% in 3Q24, moderating from the 6.4% growth rate in 2Q. This came in below consensus forecast of 5.7%.

On the demand side, the slowdown can be largely attributed to the softness in government spending (+5.0%) and lower exports (-1.0%). Private consumption grew at a faster rate of 5.1% in 3Q from 4.7% in 2Q. Private investments likewise accelerated to 13.1% in 3Q24 from 11.6% in 2Q and rebounded from the anemic -0.3% growth rate in 3Q23 on the back of stronger durable equipment and inventory re-stocking. Construction slowed to 8.9%, after public construction moderated to 3.7% (from 22% in 2Q) and offset sustained growth in private construction (11.9% from 10.3%). Net imports grew, after goods exports fell 3%, weighing on overall GDP.

On the supply side, the agri sector continued to fare poorly, declining by 2.8% from 2.3% the previous quarter. Industry and services sectors also slowed to 5.0% and 6.3%, respectively

This brings 9M24 GDP growth to 5.8%, below the government’s target of 6-7% but in line with Bloomberg consensus median forecast of 5.8%. To reach Regis’ FY24E of 6.0%, 4Q GDP should pick up to 6.5%.

Inflation picks up to 2.3% in October

Headline inflation rose to 2.3% in October from 1.9% in September. This is in line with consensus forecast of 2.3% and at the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 2.0-2.8%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose by 0.2%, largely due to higher food prices, which rose by 0.5% MoM. This increase was largely driven by upticks in the prices of vegetables, corn, fruits/nuts, and meat, which were partly attributed to weather disturbances.

Rice CPI sequentially dipped for the fourth consecutive month by 0.5%, bringing the cumulative decline to 1.4%. Low base effects from the implementation of price ceilings on rice in Oct 2023 pushed up the YoY growth rate to 9.6% in October from 5.7% in September. Outside of food, inflation was tempered by lower electricity prices (-0.7% MoM) and petrol prices (-0.2%); but admittedly, these two items could pick up again in succeeding periods.

Core inflation remained stable at 2.4%, the same as in September, with core non-food inflation easing to 2.6% from 2.8%

Brings average YT-October inflation rate to 3.3%, on track to meet the BSP’s risk-adjusted inflation forecast of 3.1% – in line with Regis’ FY24E of 3.1%. Regis does not expect this to derail our expectations of a policy rate reduction (25bps) by the Bangko Sentral (BSP) in Dec.

Operation of renewable energy certificate market eyed by Dec

The Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) is aiming to launch commercial operations for trading of renewable energy certificates (REC). RECs are supposed to be certificates that serve as evidence of RE generation. RECs are similar in concept to “carbon credits” and can be traded. Firms who generate RE are entitled to RECs as verified by government, which can be sold. The REC market will allow distribution utilities to (indirectly) comply with government mandates to increase RE use. The mandate is under the Renewable Portfolio Standards (RPS). The market will provide transparent pricing for the value of RECs.

Trade deficit widens to US$5.1bn in September

The country’s trade deficit widened to US$5.09bn in September. This is the highest level since March 2023 and was wider by 16% MoM and 43% YoY. Imports rose 10% YoY, 2% MoM to US$11.3bn. Strong rebounds on imports of consumer goods (+21% YoY) and raw materials (+20%). These more than offset the 11% drop in fuel imports and the slack in capital goods imports (+1%). Exports dropped 8% YoY, 7% MoM to US$6.3bn. Electronics exports fell 23% YoY to US$3.15bn; This was broadly the same level in the past 9 months. Brings YT-September trade deficit to US$39.4bn, flat YoY.
