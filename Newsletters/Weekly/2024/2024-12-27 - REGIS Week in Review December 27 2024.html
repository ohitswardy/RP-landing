REGIS REPORT

27 December 2024

With very light trading activity amid the Christmas holidays, the PCOMP managed to move higher for the week, up 1.9% or 122.41 pts WoW to 6,528.79. At the very least, the market managed to snap a four-year losing streak to close out 2024 just slightly up by 1.2% YoY. The peso strengthened by 1.7% WoW to 57.84, though it depreciated by 4.2% YoY from last year's close of 55.39.

Limited news flow this shortened and quiet trading week provided little direction for investors, though bargain hunting saw foreign flows notch a modest inflow of $4m for the week. Despite this, we saw chunky foreign outflows for the 'Big 3' banks BDO (-$2.7m), MBT (-$1.9m), and BPI (-$0.9m), as well as ALI (-$1.4m), which recorded the top net outflows for the week. Note that for the year, net foreign outflow hit $408m.

Some of the year's worst performers were bought up this week, with NIKL (+11.5% WoW) and WLCON (+10.0% WoW) among the week's top gainers... although both still lost over 30% for the year. Accounting for nearly half of the PCOMP's upward move this week were SM (+1.8% WoW), MONDE (+10.8% WoW), and ALI (+5.6% WoW).

The few big corporate stories for the week were mostly on the Power sector, with ACEN announcing a larger P70-bn capex for 2025 and PCC approving a $3.3-bn LNG deal between San Miguel (SMC PM) and Chromite Gas Holdings -- a jv between Meralco (MER PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM). On macro, the BSP guided for more rate cuts in 2025 and as early as the first Monetary Board meeting that is usually held in February. All that said, traders seemed to have barely taken cues from these stories.

Market ADTO slumped to just $46m from $76m last week. For the year, ADTO was a measly $73m.

Security Bank - Leaning into the consumer segment with Home Credit PH purchase
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

­Deepening consumer penetration

SECB has agreed to buy 25% of Home Credit Philippines (HCPH) – one of the country’s largest consumer finance companies – for P11bn. The purchase deepens the bank’s consumer push, as HCPH targets a lower-income segment through its cash loans, and point-of-sale loans for appliances, mobile phones, furniture, etc. While details are scant, we calculate SECB paid c2.3x P/bk (on 2024E), which seems fair given HCPH’s 20-25% ROE in 2022/23. However, the deal could cut SECB’s CET-1 ratio by about 100bps (was 13.1% at 3Q24). We maintain our P160/sh target price, and our Buy recommendation on the stock.

To buy 25% of HCPH for P11bn, broadening consumer footprint

SECB will buy 25% of HCPH from the MUFG Group, for which it expects to pay P11bn upon closing in 1Q25. The bank is leaning into the consumer lending segment – already 32% of its loan book – focused on the more affluent customer segments. In contrast, HCPH caters to the broader mass market for unsecured consumer finance, complementing SECB’s 50%-owned SB Finance. Krungsri of Thailand will retain its 75% stake in HCPH, and its 50% stake in SB Finance.

Paying for high growth, high margins, and high ROEs; but capital drag

HCPH is one of the country’s leading non-bank fincos. Its regulatory filings showed gross loans grew 39% YoY in 2022 and 43% in 2023 to P64.5bn, nearly as large as SECB’s consumer book (ex-mortgages). Unsurprisingly, its NIMs were extremely high, ranging from 29-34% in 2021-23. Despite credit costs of 10-12%, HCPH printed ROEs of 20-25% in 2022/23. Assuming ROEs held at 20% into 2024, we estimate SECB’s deal values HCPH at 2.34x P/bk in 2024E. However, the deal will shave CET-1 by 100bps, and could limit SECB’s future growth.

Estimates adjusted, P160 TP maintained

We update our numbers for the 9M24 results, and for the HCPH acquisition. The net impact is minor adjustments to our NPAT forecasts. Despite the stock’s recent run, it still offers deep value, trading on 0.41x P/bk, 4.2x PER on our FY25E estimates, and still presents significant upside potential to our TP of P160/sh. Key risks include intensifying competition, rising credit costs, and capital constraints on medium-term growth.

Bloomberry - BLOOM appoints Acting COO
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Bloomberry Resorts appointed Mr. Gregory Francis Hawkins, who currently heads Solaire North, as Acting COO. Mr. Hawkins’ appointment comes after the rather sudden retirement of Mr. Thomas Arasi as President and COO on December 17.

What is his gaming background?

While Mr. Hawkins only joined Bloomberry in November 2023, he is a veteran casino executive whose career spans multiple jurisdictions and properties.

He was previously Chief Casino Officer with the Star Entertainment Group, CEO of Crown Melbourne, President of City of Dreams Macau, and CEO of Altira Hotel Macau.

How might this affect the company?

The appointment of a gaming industry veteran from within Bloomberry’s ranks as the new COO (albeit in an interim capacity) should allay some concerns regarding operational disruptions arising from the abrupt retirement of Mr. Arasi.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ACEN to spend at least P70bn for capex in 2025

ACEN is looking to spend roughly P70bn on capex next year, according to ACEN President and CEO Eric T. Francia. About P50bn was allotted for capex this year. ACEN expects to start operating 962MW of new attributable RE capacity next year. These include Stubbo Solar in Australia (520 MW), Monsoon Wind in Laos (150 MW), Stockyard Wind in Australia (110 MW), Pangasinan Solar (60 MW), and Maharashtra Hybrid in India (122 MW). ACEN’s attributable RE portfolio stands at 6,745MW (3,051MW operational, 2,302 MW under construction, 1,401MW “committed”).

PCC approves Aboitiz-Meralco-San Miguel US$3.3bn LNG deal

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) approved Chromite Gas Holdings Inc. (Chromite) acquisition of two gas-fired power plants and an LNG terminal of San Miguel Global Power Holdings Corp. (SMGP). Chromite is a joint venture of Meralco (MER PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM). Approval is subject to certain conditions, including PCC oversight of the Competitive Selection Process (CSP). Other conditions were the appointment of a competition compliance officer, submission of reports on unplanned outages and Competitive Retail Electricity Market (CREM) reports to the PCC, and the election of independent members on boards of directors. These conditions will be in place for five years. The US$3.3bn deal will allow Meralco to own a 40% stake in the gas-fired plants and LNG terminal; SMC’s stake will be diluted to 33% (from 100%) while AP will own 27%. The transaction will be funded through a leveraged buyout, with 50% of the funds coming from debt that will be injected into the operating companies.

Meralco's power sales increased by 6.6% in 11 months

Power sales rose to 49,953 GWh, a 6.6% increase YoY, driven by high demand primarily from the residential and commercial sectors. Residential YTD sales hit 17,962 GWh, a 9.8% increase YoY, driven by consistent energization of new accounts and organic growth. Commercial sales reached 18,719 GWh, a 7.6% increase YoY, due to expansion in retail, real estate, and hotel accounts. Industrial sales were at 13,138 GWh, a 1.2% increase YoY, slightly up from increased demand in semiconductor, F&B, and plastics. Meralco’s volumes grew by 7.1% in 9M24 (4.1% in 3Q24). This implies that volumes grew by about 4.3% in October/November. SVP and Chief Revenue Officer Ferdinand Geluz guided for around 53,350 GWh for the full year, representing a 6% growth from 2023.

Cosco and Siam Global terminate JV agreement

Cosco Capital and Siam Global mutually agreed to terminate their joint venture (JV) agreement to operate stores “…for building and construction-related materials, home and garden decorative goods, home improvement goods, construction equipment, and tools in the Philippines…” The JV agreement was first announced in June 2022 but did not open any stores.

BSP remains open to more rate cuts in 2025

BSP Governor Remolona stated that they are open to delivering policy rate cuts as early as its first meeting in 2025. The BSP has finalized its calendar for 2025, with six monetary policy meetings scheduled, but exact dates are yet to be disclosed. The first monetary policy meeting is usually held in the third week of February. Remolona emphasized the BSP's reliance on data and its plan to continue monitoring inflation, which is expected to ease in 2025 (3.4%) with a slight pickup in 2026 (3.7%) based on the BSP's risk-adjusted inflation forecasts. Remolona also stated that the BSP does not directly track US policy rate changes but instead assesses the broader implication of Fed decisions on exchange rates and inflationary pressures. He commented that the Fed is less dovish than before but added that the BSP is on the same trajectory, neither more nor less dovish. We expect the BSP to cut its policy rate by 75bps in 2025, while Jefferies expects the Fed to cut rates by another 100bps in 2025.

Government records budget deficit of P213bn in November

The government recorded a budget deficit of P213bn in November, 130% higher than the deficit of P93bn in November 2023. Revenue collections in November were flat YoY (-0.6%), with tax revenues up 13% YoY, offset by a significant decline in non-tax revenues of 68%. BIR collections rose 18% YoY in November, accelerating from the 14% pace in 10M24 due to higher collections of VAT, personal/corporate income taxes, documentary stamp tax, and excise taxes. Customs collections dropped slightly by 2% YoY in November, compared to the 5% growth in 10M24. Expenditures accelerated by 27% in November, as both significant primary expenses and interest payments were incurred in the month. Primary spending rose 26% YoY, faster than the 10% pace in 10M24. The continued increase in primary spending was attributed to (a) larger tax allotments disbursed to local government units (LGUs), (b) higher spending on road, defense infrastructure, and social/educational projects, and (c) personnel services requirements. Interest payments were up 37%, as coupon payments were made to domestic debt and global bond issuances, alongside forex fluctuations. This brought the YTD November budget deficit to P1.2tn, higher by 6% YoY. The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. As of 9M24, the budget deficit-to-GDP ratio stood at 5.14%.

BIR and BOC target P4.26bn revenue collection target for 2025 amid tax reforms

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and Bureau of Customs (BOC) are aiming for double-digit increases in their revenue targets for 2025. The BIR's revenue goal increased to P3.23tn from P2.85tn in 2024 (+13% YoY), while for the BOC, it increased to P1.06tn from P930.60bn (+13% YoY). For 2024, the BIR is on track to achieve its target of P2.85bn (+13% YoY), while the BOC is running slightly behind its P940bn (+6% YoY) target. The revenue targets will be supported by several key tax measures, such as excise taxes on single-use plastics, mining fiscal regime rationalization, and Motor Vehicle Users' Charge reforms.

Marcos to sign P6.35tn 2025 budget on December 30

The P6.35tn national budget is set for enactment on December 30 after undergoing "rigorous" examination by President Marcos and key officials. This comes after Marcos chose to postpone the budget signing, which was previously scheduled for December 20, due to public backlash on some issues such as DepEd's budget being reduced by P10bn to P737bn, which is lower than the P1.11tn DPWH budget. The budget also includes P26bn allocated for the Ayuda para sa Kapos and Kita Program (AKAP), as well as zero subsidy for PhilHealth. Specific details on how the budget was revised or which items were vetoed have yet to be disclosed.

Infrastructure spending rises 11% YoY in 10M24

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 11% YoY to P1.28tn in 10M24. The government raised its target to P1.54tn, up 8.5% YoY (from P1.47tn, up 4%) and equivalent to 5.8% of GDP. This growth was driven by the accelerated implementation of infrastructure activities, including both ongoing and carryover projects of the DPWH and foreign-assisted railway projects of the DOTr, financed from prior year’s unprogrammed appropriations. For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP.

Vehicle sales rise 8.5% YoY in November

CAMPI reported that vehicle sales rose by 8.5% YoY and 2.2% MoM in November, bringing YTD retail sales to 425,208 units, up 8.8% YoY. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 197,756 units in 11M24, up 9.6% YoY, trending above TMP’s target of 215k units (+7%) and Regis’ FY24E of 206k units (+3%). The implied retail sales for November alone of 19,321 units was higher by 5.9% YoY but lower by 0.14% MoM. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses). GTCAP is trading at a 52% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,257/sh and 5.4x FY25E.

Marcos, Sara Duterte ratings continue to fall in latest Pulse Asia survey

The approval ratings of President Marcos saw a decline by 2 percentage points to 48% in November from 50% in September. The decrease mainly stems from the Visayas (48% from 52%) and Mindanao (14% from 26%), while his approval ratings in Luzon and NCR stayed relatively stable at 65% (from 61%) and 51% (from 52%), respectively. In terms of approval ratings by socioeconomic classes, Class E declined the most to 35% (from 47%), Class D slightly to 51% (from 52%), while Class ABC rose to 39% (from 35%). Vice President Sara Duterte's approval ratings slumped 10 percentage points to 50% from 60%. The significant drop was driven by all three regions, with Visayas down to 51% (from 71%), Mindanao to 80% (from 93%), Luzon to 40% (from 46%), and NCR to 34% (from 36%).

Pulse Asia senatorial pre-election preference polls see Tulfo, Sotto at the top

Based on the survey conducted in late November, 14 Senate candidates have a statistical chance of winning any of the 12 available seats. Erwin Tulfo led with 62.2%, followed by Sotto at 59.2%, and Bong Go and Ben Tulfo with 54.7% and 52.7%, respectively, taking the top four spots. Pia Cayetano (46.5%), Manny Pacquiao (45%), and Panfilo Lacson (44.1%) ranked fifth to tenth. Abby Binay (40.1%), Lito Lapid (39.2%), Imee Marcos (37.5%), Bato Dela Rosa (36.9%), and Camille Villar (36.5%) were positioned between seventh and 15th places. Notably, only one of Duterte's allies, Senator Bong Go, holds a winning ranking range of rank 3-4, while Senator Bato Dela Rosa is ranked 10-15 and may fall outside the top 12. The other four PDP Laban Senate candidates are out of contention with low rankings. This was Pulse Asia's first non-commissioned survey after the filing of certificates of candidacy in October. The 2025 Philippine Midterm Elections will be held on May 12, 2025.
