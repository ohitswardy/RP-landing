REGIS REPORT

12 April 2024

Philippine shares came off for the 2nd week in a row as investor sentiment continued to be weighed down by the prospects of delayed rate cuts this year, especially after the BSP’s hawkish tone post its decision to keep rates steady on Monday. The local benchmark initially closed flat on Monday as stocks managed to find support through the afternoon session ahead of the BSP announcement and a 2-day break. But upon resumption from the holidays, the PSEi extended its downtrend and ultimately settled at -1.3% WoW, near its 100-day moving average.

Rate-sensitive Property sector once again fared the worst, -3.7% WoW, adding to its -5.1% slump in the prior week. The key stock in focus was ALI (9.0% WoW) notably broke below its 200-day moving average and closed at its lowest since November. That said, its peer SMPH saw a shallower drop, ending the week -2.0% WoW.

Most conglos also dragged the market lower, led by JG Summit (-8.1% WoW), Aboitiz Equity (-6.9% WoW), GT Capital (-3.6% WoW), and Ayala Corp (-3.1% WoW).

On the other end, Banking sector (+0.7% WoW) was the  bright spot amid gains by BPI (+3.8% WoW) and Metrobank (+2.9% MoM).

The top index gainer was Wilcon, with its +4.4% WoW gain this week ending seven straight weeks of declines. Jollibee (+2.4% WoW) likewise gained, recovering from a sharp 9.0% drop in the previous week.

Market turnover during the shortened trading week stood at a daily average of US$105m, WoW, while net foreign flow ended skewed to sell by US$30.5m.

On the macro front, the BSP held rates steady on Monday, as widely expected. In the subsequent statement, the BSP Governor struck a hawkish tone and indicated a potential rate cut may be as far back as post-3Q.  The PHP managed to end the week flat though at PHP56.54/US$ (-0.06% WoW).

RESEARCH
Monde Nissin - 4Q23: APAC BFB sustains margin recovery
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Margin recovery drives 4Q23

MONDE 4Q23 core net profit surged 2x YoY to P1.9bn, bringing the FY23 figure to P7.6bn – in line with expectations. The company recognized a P10.1bn impairment charge at Quorn, which was partly offset by a P1.3bn gain on the shareholder guaranty asset. These non-cash items are excluded from “core” net profit.

Consolidated revenues for 4Q23 grew 5% YoY driven primarily by the APAC BFB business (+8% YoY), while EBITDA jumped 50% YoY to P3.5bn as margins continued to recover. For FY23, revenues rose 9% YoY to P80.2bn, while EBITDA grew 21% YoY to P13.5bn, both also within expectations.

APAC BFB: strong finish to 2023

APAC BFB sales grew 8% YoY in 4Q23 driven by strong volumes of instant noodles (+9%). Segment EBITDA jumped 56% on the back of a 400-bp YoY improvement in gross margin on more favorable costs for wheat and palm oil. Despite downtrading among consumers, market shares in key categories continued to improve QoQ and YoY. Management noted, however, that competitors are responding.

Quorn: Cost control amidst challenging environment

Revenues for Quorn declined 4% YoY as the category continued to face challenges. Margins during the quarter took a hit relative to 3Q23 due to higher inventory costs that management is looking to deplete in the coming months. Despite this, tight opex control, coupled with restructuring efforts earlier in the year, resulted in a sequential improvement in EBITDA.

Margin recovery to continue in 2024

For the APAC BFB business, management expects growth to be aided by a more meaningful recovery in gross margin given favorable input costs for wheat and palm oil, which have been locked in until 3Q. Top line growth could moderate as price increase are likely to be tempered, and some of the gross margin gains could be reinvested in A&P to help further drive volumes and defend market share amidst heightened competition. Meanwhile, management will continue implementing cost control measures at Quorn to maintain breakeven EBITDA while the environment remains challenged.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

APM Terminals challenges Transnet’s choice of ICTSI for DCT Pier 2 upgrade

APM Terminals has filed a case at the Durban High Court, challenging Transnet’s decision to select ICTSI (ICT PM) to operate and upgrade the DCT Pier 2 in Durban. APM Terminals claims that it received final confirmation that Transnet would proceed with ICTSI as the preferred bidder on 1 March 2024. “This challenge will not delay the process in any real sense and will rather ensure that the process is lawful”, APM Terminals said. Regis forecasts and valuation do not assume the acquisition of DCT Pier 2. ICTSI trades at 15.8x FY24E PER and 8.1x FY24E EV/EBITDA, lower than peer average (17x/11x) and historical valuation (22x/11x).

CNPF FY23 net profit +12% YoY to P5.6bn – behind Regis, in line with consensus

CNPF reported FY23 net profit of P5.6bn, up 12% YoY, behind Regis estimates but in line with consensus. Consolidated revenues rose 8% YoY to P67bn (in line), while EBITDA grew 15% YoY to P8.7bn (behind) on higher-than-expected opex. For 4Q23 alone, net profit grew 25% YoY to P948mn. Margins recovered sharply during the quarter, driving EBITDA +47% YoY.

4Q23 revenues rose 9% YoY to P16.7bn as the OEM segment recovered, growing 17% YoY during the quarter. The branded segment slowed to +7% YoY (+10% in 9M23), driven mainly by double-digit growth in milk (29% of revenues). While downtrading persisted, volumes across all the branded categories continued to be healthy (+mid single digit). CNPF maintained market share in the core marine (85% market share), meat (52%) and milk (28%) categories.

Gross profit expanded nearly 500bps YoY in 4Q23 due to favorable input cost, particularly for skipjack tuna and milk. The gross margin gains were partly offset by higher A&P and trade spending to help drive volumes. Management is guiding to grow top line and net profit by low double digits in 2024 (Regis FY24E: +14%) driven by the continued recovery in OEM and growth of the branded business. Input prices of most raw materials continue to move favorably and CNPF has locked in requirements for these until 1H24, which should buoy margins in the coming quarters.

DALI targets up to 950 stores by year-end

DALI Everyday Grocery plans to open at least 300 new branches in Luzon this year, expanding its total stores to 900-950 by year-end according to Anja Grote Westrick, Director of Strategic Supply Chain of Hard Discount Philippines Inc., operator of DALI. DALI currently has around 630 stores in Luzon; it closed 2022 with 250 stores. The expansion focus will be in Luzon for now. DALI operates small format groceries (180-300sqm) typically in residential areas. Stores have roughly 400 SKUs, of which 60-70% are private label.

SM Prime aims to have 100 Philippine malls by 2027

SM Prime is targeting to have a portfolio of 100 Philippine malls by 2027 from 85 currently. This represents 4% CAGR in the number of malls, similar to our 3% floor area expansion forecast over the next two years.

DoubleDragon’s Hotel 101 to be valued at US$2.3bn

JVSPAC acquisition Corporation (JVSPAC) and DoubleDragon’s 70% owned subsidiary, Hotel101 Global, have entered into a binding definitive merger agreement. Hotel 101 will have an equity value of US$2.3bn following the merger completion, which is expected to be completed in 2H24. The combined entity is expected to be publicly listed on the US NASDAQ under the ticker symbol “HBNB”. Hotel 101 currently has 1,100 rooms in the Philippines. It is currently constructing hotels in Cebu, Davao, Boracay, Palawan, Libis, Cagayan de Oro, and Zamboanga. It is also constructing a 680-room hotel in Spain and a 480-room hotel in Japan.

Robinsons Land sells 1.7bn RCR shares

Robinsons Land sold 1.7bn RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) shares in a placement (equivalent to 16% of shares outstanding). The shares were sold at a price of P4.92/share. This is 5% lower than the previous closing price and was able to raise P8.5bn. This transaction reduced RLC’s ownership in RCR from 66% to 50%. RLC plans to infuse P25bn worth of assets into RCR and grow the REIT’s floor area by 60%. It also intends to infuse assets such as malls, hotels, and warehouses. RCR currently has a portfolio of 480,000sqm, all of which are comprised of offices. RCR currently offers 8.1% dividend yield. RLC is trading at 61% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 6.5X FY24E P/E

 

More on PGOLD 4Q23 results

PGOLD’s 4Q23 net profit fell 29% YoY to P2bn, bringing the FY23 figure to P8.6bn, behind Regis and consensus forecasts. The company recognized a P600mn non-cash accrual relating to retirement benefit expense. An adjustment that was recognized only at the end of the FY23. It is expected to be a recurring annual expense. Underlying 4Q23 results, however, still appeared soft. Much like the rest of the sector, SSS slowed further in 4Q23: it was flattish for the Puregold brand and -6% for S&R. The company partly attributes the weakness to a relatively high YoY base.

Consolidated gross margin contracted 80bps YoY during the quarter but this was mostly driven by the core Puregold brand. Gross margin for Puregold contracted 100bps YoY to 14.5% as the company adopted a more aggressive pricing strategy in response to hard discount retailers (e.g. Dali and O!Save). Puregold is matching hard discounters’ pricing for its top 150 SKUs.The company will host a briefing on Apr 18. We hope to get more detail then.

CREC shrinks IPO size and plans May 31 listing

Based on its prospectus updated last April 3, Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. (CREC) trimmed its IPO size from P12.9bn down to P7.97, following the P5bn sale of Citicore Renewable Energy REIT (CREIT) shares to SM Investments Corp. (SMIC). CREC will offer up to 1.76bn, originally 2.9bn, common shares for up to P3.88/sh with an overallotment option of up to 267.86mn shares, previously 435mn. The offer period will run from May 20 - 24 and will be listed on the PSE on May 31. The proceeds will be used to fund capex for pipeline development of solar and BESS projects. CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 175.5MW. The 8 solar projects with a combined gross installed capacity of 1,583MW are planned for commercial operations this year as the company is expecting capex of P35bn for 2024. The company earmarked approximately P220bn across 53 projects between 2024 and 2030.

AC indirect subsidiary VIA optronics to delist

VIA optronics is seeking to voluntarily delist from the NYSE. VIA is a “supplier of interactive display solutions” and VIA is a 50.3% subsidiary of Integrated Microelectronics (IMI PM). IMI, in turn, is 52% owned by Ayala Corp (AC PM). The company said, in essence, that the cost of maintaining the listing is no longer justified. It is unclear whether IMI would undertake a tender offer to buy out minorities in VIA and at what price. VIA’s market capitalization at previous close was only US$14m.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Monetary Board keeps policy settings unchanged; Governor sees RRR cut unlikely by 3Q

The Monetary Board kept its key policy rate unchanged at 6.5%. This is widely expected by the market. The BSP raised its inflation forecast for 2024 to 3.8% from 3.6% previously. Meanwhile, BSP Governor Remolona laid out two conditions for a potential rate cut in 3Q: softer inflation and a weak economic growth. BSP Governor Remolona said that it is unlikely that the BSP will reduce the reserve requirement by 3Q24. The BSP is trying to figure out the right timing to reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR).

FDI net inflows rise 90% YoY in January

Preliminary BSP data showed that FDI net inflows surged by 89.9% YoY to US$907mn in January. This is also higher by 9.8% from US$826mn recorded in December. The gov’t said that this is due to nonresidents’ net investments in debt instruments surging 173.2% YoY to US$820mn. The BSP expects to record FDI net inflows of US$9bn at end-2024, slightly higher than US$8.9bn FDI net inflows in 2023.

Inflation accelerates to 3.7% in March

Headline CPI rose 3.7% YoY and 0.1% MoM in March. This is mildly better than consensus (3.8%) and within the BSP’s forecast range of 3.4%-4.2% but faster than 3.4% in February. Food inflation rose to 5.6% in March, its fastest print in four months. Rice prices sustained uptick (+24% YoY, +1% MoM) and contributed 1.8ppts to headline inflation. Meat inflation accelerated to 2% in March from 0.7% in Feb. Higher fuel prices and tricycle fares pushed transport inflation to rise at a faster rate of 2.1% from 1.2% in Feb. Meanwhile, core inflation further eased to 3.4% in March from 3.6% in February and 8% a year ago. The 1Q24 average inflation rate stood at 3.3%. The BSP expects inflation to average 3.6% this year while REGIS expects 4.6%.

GIR rises to US$104bn in March

Preliminary data showed the country’s gross international reserves (GIR) rose by 2% MoM to US$104.03bn in March from US$101.99bn in end-February. The gov’t cited national government’s net foreign currency deposits with the BSP, upward valuation adjustments in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings from higher gold prices, and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. This is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. Additionally, the BSP projects the GIR level to settle at US$103bn.

Bill to ban cross-ownership in power industry filed

A bill to forbid cross-ownership between the generation and distribution sub-sectors in the power industry has been filed.

Under the proposed law:

Distribution utilities will not be allowed to control generation companies, and vice versa. The prohibition would extend to firms that control generation companies / Dus. The Bill was filed by two congressman connected with the SAGIP party-list. One of a number of parties part of the majority coalition within the House of Representatives. Such a ban on would affect many of the main power company groups (e.g. Meralco, Aboitiz Power).

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.6bn in February

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit narrowed further to US$3.65bn in February.This is down from US$4.39bn in January 2024 and US$3.88bn in Feb 2023 underpinned by a sustained recovery in exports. Imports rose 6.3% YoY in February from -6% in January due to higher imports of (a) fuel (+8%), (b) raw materials (+12%), and (c) consumer goods (+9%). Meanwhile, the decline in capital goods imports persisted (-3% YoY). Exports grew at a faster rate of +16% in February as Electronic exports rose 27% YoY. Exports to top trading partners jumped: USA (+23%), HK (+46%), and China (+14%). This brought YT-Feb trade deficit to US$8.0bn, down 15% YoY.

Commercial banks’ loan growth accelerates to 8.6% in February; M3 growth slows to 5.0%

Preliminary data showed commercial bank loans expanded by 8.6% YoY in February from 7.8% in January. This is consistent with all banks’ loan growth of 10% in February, up from 8.8% in January. This was due to faster lending to real estate activities (11.6%), electricity, gas, steam, and airconditioning supply (11.2%); transportation and storage (21.1%); and manufacturing (5.9%). Meanwhile, consumer loan growth was steady at 25% on sustained increases in credit card and auto loans. Lastly, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 5.0% YoY in February from 6.0% in January

Philippines, US, Japan, to invest in “Luzon corridor”

The Philippines, US, and Japan have agreed to accelerate high-impact projects in Luzon. The Luzon corridor will be part of the United States’ Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment and will connect Subic Bay, Clark, Manila, and Batangas. It will include investments in ports, rail, clean energy, semiconductors, supply chains, and other forms of connectivity. Philippine Ambassador to the United States Jose Manuel Romualdez said that Tokyo and Washington were expected to invest at least US$100bn in the Philippines over the next 5 to 10 years.
