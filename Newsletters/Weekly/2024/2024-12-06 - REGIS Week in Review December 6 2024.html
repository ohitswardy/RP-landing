REGIS REPORT

06 December 2024

The PCOMP ended the week the green, +1.74% WoW at 6,729.14, partially recovering from last week’s steep -2.45% slump, as local shares tracked the widespread uptick across the region. Broader term however the index appears to be in a holding pattern with support at the 6600 level and resistance around 6800, where the 200-day moving average lies.

Amid a fairly light week of material corporate news, in focus during the week was the latest CPI data which showed that inflation inched up to 2.5% in November from 2.3% in October. The uptick was primarily due to an increase in the prices of vegetables and fish which was attributed to the several typhoons that hit the country during the month. Ultimately the CPI reading was inline with the Street forecast and at the mid-point of the BSP’s target range, and brings the YTD inflation rate to 3.2%, which is on track to meet the BSP’s FY forecast of 3.1% and Regis’ forecast of 3.1% as well. On the policy front, the relatively subdued inflation print should help the case for potentially another rate cut by the BSP in their upcoming December 19 meeting. Note however that a big deciding factor for the BSP will be the Fed's policy decision which will come out on the 18th. Jerome Powell’s latest comments indicated that FOMC officials will be considering either a cut or a pause, depending on how macro data in the coming days look. At the moment, futures are pricing in a 70% chance of a cut in the December 18 meeting. Amid this, the PHP sharply strengthened this week, +1.5% WoW to 57.747, its strongest level since mid-October, as the currency finally managed to find some support after weeks of fluctuating around the crucial 59 support level.   

In terms of market movers, biggest drivers of the benchmark were ICT +5.68% WoW, which bounced from 3-month lows, and large cap conglos AC +5.88% and SM +4.57% as they were all among the top foreign bought names during the week. Gains in the conglo space spilled over to the mid-cap plays as well with DMC +4.88%, AGI +4.67%, GTCAP +2.14%, and AEV +2.06%. A standout laggard in the sector however was JGS -3.85% as it tracked the continued selloff in its consumer sub URC -3.55% which extended its downtrend to ~13-year lows, dragged down by heavy foreign selling. The only other name that fared worse than the 2 was another consumer name MONDE -5.4% which broke down to its lowest level since February. Banks meanwhile were mixed with BPI +1.09%, BDO -1.88% and MBT -0.33%. Continued to see foreign investors switch among the names as BPI was the top foreign sold name during the week, while BDO was again among the top buys, and furthermore the 2 names were among the most actively traded stocks this week. That said, despite the outflows BPI +1.09% managed to outperform its peers, finally finding signs of support after falling ~14% from October peaks and hitting its lowest levels since September.

Market activity was moderate at a daily average of $105m, +4% WoW. A huge chunk of the volumes during the week however were blocks, particularly in the aformentioned widely-held names BPI and BDO. Foreign participation in the market improved to 60%, but their net flow remained skewed to sell, resulting to the 7th consecutive week of weekly net foreign selling. The pace of outflows however showed some signs of easing at $32.6m this week, halved from the prior week.

RESEARCH
ICTSI - Trump 2.0 overhang; Downgrade to Hold
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Muddy waters

The recent election of Donald Trump threatens to cloud the medium[1]term outlook of ICT. We examine these concerns in this report and conclude that the downside is already priced in. We cut our SOTP NAV-based TP by 15% to P400/sh and our FY24E/FY26E profit estimates by 4%/9%, after reducing our estimates for its port in Mexico. The shares have retraced by 16% from its peak on Oct 8 but questions over trade policies under Trump 2.0 are likely to keep the stock range-bound until there’s greater clarity. We downgrade our rating from Buy to Hold.

Mexico under threat (again)

US President-elect Trump’s threat of tariffs, if implemented, could directly impact Mexican exports the most. Given that the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) free trade agreement is up for review / renewal in July 2026, there is understandable trepidation about the long-term prospects of ICT’s port in Mexico (CMSA), which we estimate accounts for 25% of EBITDA. Management estimates only <10% of CMSA’s volumes could be at risk from Trump’s tariffs.

Earnings forecasts assume bear case scenario

We reduce our FY26E profit forecast by 9% after assuming the half-year impact of a 30% reduction in CMSA’s revenues. Given the competitive advantages of CMSA and the gradual adjustment of trade patterns, we believe our assumptions could later prove to be the worst-case outcome.

SOTP NAV-based TP of P400/sh assumes impact from Trump 2.0

Our revised TP of P400/sh is 15% lower than our previous TP, after cutting our valuation for CMSA by 40%. While the shares look attractively valued, at 8x FY25E EV/EBITDA (vs peers and history of 11x), the unpredictability of Trump’s trade policies could mean that investors could remain on the sidelines for the time being. Upside/Downside risks: macroeconomic/political risks, acquisitions, and labor issues.

SECB acquires 25% of Home Credit Philippines

Security Bank (SECB PM) disclosed that it has agreed to buy a 25% stake in Home Credit Philippines (HCPH) from MUFG Bank for about P11bn. HCPH is one of the biggest non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) in the country, focusing on buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) installment loans, largely for personal acquisitions of appliances and mobile phones. The company also offers unsecured cash loans, and insurance services. (See website www.homecredit.ph/). According to the disclosure, HCPH has issued more than P350bn (roughly $6bn) to some 11mn customers in the company’s 11yrs of operation.

The acquisition boosts SECB’s thrust to further increase its exposure in the higher-margin consumer/MSME market. As of 9M24, the consumer/MSME segments accounted for 32% of SECB’s loan book.

The disclosures give almost no other information to derive any meaningful financial valuation. However, SECB’s purchase price values the whole HCPH at P44bn, which seems quite high on first blush. In November 2022 (deal closed in May/June 2023), 100% of HCPH was acquired by MUFG (25%) and Krungsri/ Bank of Ayudhya of Thailand (75%) for a total price of P24.4bn. (Links here and here.) The price SECB is paying is an 80% premium over MUFG’s acquisition cost, after just over 2 years. That said, we do not know how HCPH has performed since the 2022/23 acquisition. We also do not know if MUFG/Krungsri infused any additional capital into HCPH in the intervening period.

The deal is also a bit quirky. MUFG currently owns 20% of SECB. MUFG is also the largest shareholder of Krungsri (76.88). Meanwhile, SECB and Krungsri are 50-50 partners in SB Finance, which offers loans for cars and motorcycles, as well as unsecured personal loans. Krungsri bought 50% of SB Finance in 2019 (closed in 2020). It is not clear why SECB and/or SB Finance were not involved in the HCPH acquisition in 2022.

We await further details from the bank. Meanwhile, we maintain our Buy rating on SECB stock.

DMCI group completes acquisition of Cemex Phils

The DMCI group completed the acquisition of Cemex Phils (CHP PM), after acquiring 100% of CHP’s parent, Cemex Asian South East Corp. (CASEC) for an equity value of US$272mn. The purchase price was lower than the initial purchase price of US$306mn (P1.42/sh) after adjusting for debt, cash, and working capital at the completion date on Dec 2. Note that those who participated in the CHP tender offer received P1.42/sh; Tender offer documents indicated that the bidder will not claim a refund if there’s a downward adjustment in the final price. It is unclear at this stage if the initial Enterprise Value guidance of US$660mn has also changed. At the financial close, DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) secured a 51% effective stake in CHP, while Semirara (SCC PM) and Dacon Corporation accounted for 10% and approximately 29%, respectively.

DMC Board Advisor, Executive Vice President and Chief Finance Officer, Herbert M. Consunji, has been appointed as President and Chief Executive Officer of CHP. He will lead turnaround efforts to further streamline operations and unlock synergies. Company plans to enhance CHP’s logistics network, optimize product mix, manage production and operating costs, and leverage on potential operating synergies within the DMCI ecosystem. As of end-September, CHP's gross debt stood at P21.1bn while cash stood at P1.1bn. CHP incurred an EBITDA loss of P316mn and net loss of P2.87bn as of 9M24. We estimate CHP’s cement-equivalent clinker capacity is at 5.0mn MT. This will rise to 6.5mn MT after it completes its 1.5mn MT expansion by early 2025.

ACEN plans to replace 246MW coal plant with US$1.5bn solar facility by 2030

ACEN is looking to build a mid-merit 1,400-megawatt peak (MWp) solar plant + 1,600 megawatt-hour (MWh) battery energy storage system to replace the 246MW SLTEC coal plant. The company expects to shut down the plant by 2030, ten years earlier than scheduled. The solar facility is expected to cost US$1.5bn. It expects to fund the project through revenues generated from selling transition credits and additional funds from its partnership with Temasek and Singapore’s Keppel Ltd. Plans are still subject to final Board and relevant regulatory approvals.

SPNEC signs EPC contract with Power China for a portion of 3,500MW Terra Solar

SPNEC signed an engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contract with Power Construction Corp. of China and its affiliates (“Power China) to build the east section of its 3,500 MW Terra Solar project. The East section will cover 1,050MW encompassing roughly 1505 hectares in Nueva Ecija. Power China will be handling the procurement, design, permitting, manufacturing, logistics, and long-term operational protocols for that specified section. There has been no further disclosure as to whether Power China will continue to develop the rest of Terra Solar or if Meralco will contract with another firm.

Power China, is a global leader in energy infrastructure, and has been active in the Philippines, building coal-fired power plant and transmission projects. Some of its completed projects in the Philippines include the 4x135MW Kauswagan and 2x660MW Dinginin (Mariveles) coal fired plants. They have also constructed the transmission line for the NGCP’s Mindanao-Visayas Interconnection Project (MVIP).

Terra Solar is set to be commercially operational by 2027, with 2500MW expected to be operational by Feb 2026. The 3,500 MW solar + 4500MWh BESS facility is estimated to cost P200bn. Terra Solar is 60%-owned by SPNEC; SPNEC is 50.5%-owned by Meralco (MER PM), through its MGen stake of 47.3% and Metro Pacific stake of 3.2%.

SM Prime to open three malls in 2025

SM Prime said it would open three new Philippine malls in 2025. It currently has 87 malls in the Philippines. The company will also redevelop 10 Philippine malls next year. It also plans to complete around five malls annually in 2026 and 2027. This will bring the total number of Philippine malls to 100. These plans are broadly within our expectations.

First Gen lowers 2025 capex to P35bn

First Gen Corp. is allocating a lower capex budget for next year as it will be focusing on geothermal activities. The lower 2025 budget stems from the company having already completed the capex program in gas as well as in hydro. Hence, 90% of the budget will be allocated for geothermal activities particularly in drilling new wells (approx. 16 new wells). The drilling programs alongside the completion of the power plants (83 MW) will be operational by next year. For comparison, FGEN's 2024 capex budget is P74.4bn or $1.27bn. $560mn was for the hydro platform (including the cost of Casecnan Hydroelectric Power Plant). $670mn was allotted for its renewable energy subsidiary Energy Development Corp. (EDC). And the remainder will be allotted for natural gas projects and the LNG Terminal Project. FGEN has a total of 3,668 MW of installed capacity from its portfolio of plants that run on geothermal, wind, hydro, solar, and natural gas.

Aboitiz Power sees strong 2024 finish

Aboitiz Power expects the strong positive earnings momentum to continue through the end of 2024, according to AP President and CEO Danel Aboitiz. Cited better plant availability, new capacity additions, strong growth in distribution business, and infrastructure improvements. Management believes it will easily exceed consensus full-year expectations (P32bn). As of 9M24, AP’s core income grew by 2% YoY to P27.2bn, with 3Q24 core profit alone up 14% to P10.1bn. On consensus’ FY24E, AP trades at 8.4x PER. The company is adding 3,700 MW of new renewable energy capacity, en route to 4,600 by 2030. AP currently has 3,962MW of attributable net sellable capacity.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Inflation inches up to 2.5% in November

Headline inflation inched up to 2.5% in November from 2.3% in October. This is in-line with consensus forecast of 2.5% and came in at the midpoint of the BSP's forecast range of 2.2-3.0%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose 0.4%, primarily due to higher food prices which increased by 0.7% MoM. The increase was mainly driven by price upticks in vegetables and fish as several typhoons hit the country in the month of November. Rice CPI sequentially dipped for the fifth consecutive month by 1.5%, which brings the cumulative decline to 3.0%. Prices of well-milled rice stood at P42-52/kg for local and P45-54 for imported on Dec 3 (vs. P43-54 for local and P45-55 for imported rice on Oct 31) based on the Dept of Agriculture’s data. Outside of food, petrol prices which saw a slight decrease of 0.2% last month—saw an increase of 1.2% in November due to the weaker peso and global oil supply concerns. Core inflation and core non-food inflation both remained relatively stable at 2.5% and 2.7% respectively, as both saw a small increase of 0.1% from last month.

Bringing average YT-November inflation rate to 3.2%, on track to meet the BSP's risk adjusted inflation forecast of 3.1%—in-line with Regis' FY24E of 3.1%. This latest print increases the likelihood that the Bangko Sentral (BSP) will cut policy rates again two weeks from now. Although what may derail such an action would be events overseas (e.g. the Fed). To note, the next FOMC meeting will be on Dec 18 (EST), while the next BSP Monetary Board meeting is on Dec. 19.

Electricity prices set to rise in January after ERC approves WESM charges worth P3.1bn

The Energy Regulatory Commission approved the collection of the remaining reserve trading amounts in the WESM. Un-collected amount of P3.05bn will be charged to consumers over a three to six-month period. Deferred payments stemmed from the moratorium imposed on March 26, which suspended the billing for the WESM’s co-optimized energy and reserve due to concerns with the ERC-approved Price Determination Methodology. On May 9, the ERC allowed energy providers to collect only a portion of the P8.8bn during the moratorium. Consumers in Luzon and Mindanao will see an additional P0.124/kwh increase in rates for three months. Consumers in Visayas will see an increase of P0.033/kwh for six months. Electricity accounts for 4.6% of the CPI basket.

DOE resets Green Energy Auction to early 2025

The Department of Energy (DOE) is rescheduling the third green energy auction (GEA-3) for geothermal and hydro technologies to January 2025, according to DOE Undersecretary Felix William Fuentabella at the DOE Investment Forum last week. It is currently waiting for the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) to release the green energy auction reserve (GEAR) prices, which could be released within the next two weeks. GEA-3 will cover non- feed-in-tariff (FIT) technologies: 399 MW of impounding hydro, 3,120MW of pumped storage hydro and 380 MW of geothermal, as well as FIT-eligible 200 MW run-of-river hydro. Target start of deliveries is 2028-2030 for hydro while 2024-2030 for geothermal.

Department of Energy (DOE) sees 4,000-6,000 MW of new coal-fired power plants may push through

Development of 4,000-6,000 MW coal-fired power plants could be pursued over the next few years, according to DOE Undersecretary Rowena Guevarra. These projects are among the 20,000 MW coal-fired projects that are not covered by the coal moratorium. Other coal-fired projects may no longer push through due to financing issues. This includes the following projects: Meralco’s (MER PM) 1,200MW ultra-super critical coal-fired power plant in Atimonan, Quezon, as well as Global Business Power’s 80 MW coal plant; San Miguel’s (SMC PM) Masinloc 700 MW and Mariveles 600 MW greenfield plants; Coal-fired plants (11,730 MW) account for 45% of the country’s dependable capacity (25,876 MW) today. In the Luzon grid alone, coal plants total 8,457 MW, accounting for 46% of the grid’s total capacity of 18,559 MW.

Survey shows majority of office occupiers plan to expand in 3-5 years

Based on a survey by property consultancy firm Santos Knight Frank, 64% of respondents plan to expand their office space over the next 3-5 years. We suspect that very few (if any) POGO firms were part of the survey. Based on our estimates, POGO firms still occupy about 4% of Metro Manila office stock (equivalent to 600,000sqm). Thus, Metro Manila office vacancy is likely to increase to 20% (from the current 16%) once the POGO ban becomes effective (at the end of the year).

Government debt rises 0.8% MoM to P16.02 trillion as of end-October

The national government's outstanding debt rose to P16.02tn in end-October, 0.8% (+P127bn) higher than the end-September level of P15.89tn. Primarily driven by valuation impact of peso depreciation against the USD from P56.01/USD at end-Sept to P58.19/USD end-Oct. Domestic debt declined slightly by 0.4% (-P46bn) MoM to P10.89tn due to the net redemption of government securities. External debt rose 3.5% (+P173bn) MoM to P5.13tn due to net foreign loan availments of P20.5bn as well as foreign exchange movements. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.6% by year-end and is seeking to reduce the ratio further to 56.3% by 2028. As of end-Sep 2024, the government's debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 61.3% vs. 60.9% end-Jun 2024.

DBCC tweaks GDP growth targets

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) trimmed the economic growth target for this year to a range of 6-6.5% narrower than the previous 6-7% goal. For 2025 until 2028, the target band was widened to 6-8%, due to "evolving domestic and global uncertainties." Budget Secretary Amenah F. Pangandaman, who chairs the DBCC, stays optimistic that despite the domestic challenges the country can still attain the growth target for the year of 6-6.5%. This assumption is hinged on the anticipated holiday spending, continued disaster recovery efforts, low inflation, and a robust labor market.

The government also revised to its macroeconomic assumptions for this year and the medium term (2025–2028), aiming to reduce the fiscal deficit gradually while enhancing long-term investments for job creation, income growth, and poverty reduction. The revenue forecast for 2024 was raised to P4.383 trillion, while expenditures are expected to average 21% of GDP through 2028, reaching P7.621 trillion in 2028. Revenue growth will be supported by new measures like VAT on digital services and digital tax administration reforms, expected to raise annual collections significantly.

Inflation is projected to average 3.1-3.3% in 2024 which is narrower than the previous assumption of 3-4%, with prices for crude oil at $78-81/barrel from $70-85/barrel previously, then $60-80/barrel for 2025 to 2028 from $65-85/barrel. The DBCC sees the Philippine peso averaging P57-57.50/USD for this year, P56-58/USD for 2025, and P55-58/USD 2026-2028.

Commercial banks’ loan growth moderates to 10.6% YoY in October

Preliminary data showed the outstanding commercial bank loans rose by 10.6% YoY in October, a tad slower than September’s 11% rate. Loans to productive activities rose 9.1% in Oct from 9.8% the previous month. This was due to slower loan growth in manufacturing, retail, and real estate sectors. Consumer loans growth accelerated slightly to 23.6% in Oct from 23.4% due to faster growth in salary loans (18.4% in Oct from 15.2% in Sep). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth remained steady at 5.5% in October.

Impeachment complaint filed against VP Sara Duterte

A coalition of various organizations filed an impeachment complaint against VP Sara Duterte with the Lower House. This is over allegations of “betrayal of public trust, culpable violation of the Constitution, and other high crimes.” The impeachment process faces time constraints due to the legislative calendar and upcoming 2025 elections. The impeachment complaint has to be sponsored by members constituting 1/3rd of the House for it to proceed to a Senate trial. If impeached by the House, she will not be immediately ousted but first be tried in the Senate. President Marcos has urged lawmakers to focus on economic reforms and other legislative priorities.
