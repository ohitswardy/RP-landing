REGIS REPORT

22 November 2024

Philippine shares found better footing this week with the PCOMP posting its first gain in 5 weeks, +1.55% WoW to 6,780.13, supported by evident bargain hunting following the heavy bloodbath post-US elections. Note that prior to this week, the index tumbled -10% through the previous 4 weeks and touched the 6500-level which is the lowest in 3 months; furthermore, technical indicators had also suggested an oversold market. Note that bulk of the support this week appears to have come from local investors as foreign flow continued to be skewed to sell for the 5th week in a row by another $50m (albeit lighter than the past 2 weeks’ ~$90m average). This has brought the YTD net outflow to $240m (but note that this includes the ~$290m inflow into BLOOM related to the GGAM settlement).

On the ground, 3Q/9M earnings season concluded on a mixed note. Regis’ tally of results showed that while aggregate earnings growth looked inline with forecasts at the surface, a granular look into sectors revealed a largely mixed picture. Out of 40 companies under Regis coverage, 40% missed estimates and similarly another ~40% beat forecasts. Moving forward, recent results suggests that Banks look en route to continue post good results over the next few quarters as they set to benefit from the massive RRR cut that took effect on October 25. Meanwhile most consumer names started to show signs of recovery in 3Q and look poised to post sequential improvements in 4Q not only due to seasonality effects but amid easing inflation. That being said, one area of concern is the Property space as reservation sales in 3Q fell by an alarming -19% QoQ. Furthermore, note that the ban on POGOs in the country is set to take effect by year-end.

On the policy front, BSP Governor Remolona indicated that a 25bp cut is still on the table at the final policy meeting of the year on December 19. As such, the yield on the 10-year Treasury slipped 5bps and returned below 6% at 5.996%. The PHP meanwhile weakened by another -0.29% WoW and for the past few sessions has been knocking at the 59-level, which was previously tested back in October 2022.

As the selloff in the market halted and with earnings season now out of the way, activity on the ground eased to a daily average of $88m, -16% WoW. Furthermore, turnover on the final day of the week stood at just $63m, which is the lightest since mid-June.

Best performing stock in the benchmark this week was GTCAP +7.97% WoW, supported by better-than-expected results. Banks were another generally bright spot with BDO +7.12%, which is the top foreign bought name this week, and MBT +5.08%. That said, there appears to be a switching over from BPI which in contrast suffered a -4.07% drop as it was among the top foreign sold stocks during the week. Worst laggard in the benchmark was WLCON -11.56% as its own outlook remains tepid amid the worsening real estate market.

Philippine Strategy - 3Q24 Scorecard – fortunes turning in 4Q24/1H25?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

NPAT up 9% YoY in 3Q24 despite unexpected losses

Aggregate earnings for 40 stocks under REGIS coverage were up 9% in 3Q24 and 8% for 9M24. While this seems to lag our 11% FY24E, we note that 4Q23 was quite a low base, putting the 9M figures broadly in line. Meanwhile, total NPAT for the PSEi Index increased 16% YoY in 3Q24 and 9% YoY in 9M24, though the 3Q figure was pushed up by massive P25bn forex gain at San Miguel (SMC PM, not rated).

The spread was highly polarized, with relatively few (9 of 40) “in line” results, and with the 15 “beats” nearly matching and negating the 16 “misses”. This was all the more impressive as some of the misses were quite severe, including unexpected losses from Cebu Air (CEB PM) and Bloomberry (BLOOM PM).

Banks, utilities, telcos deliver strong results

Utilities surprised to the upside, led by Meralco (MER PM) and Manila Water (MWC PM), though MWC’s under-spending is bad news longer-term. Banks posted accelerating loan growth, while bottom line was propped up by trading gains. For Telcos, we witnessed a slowdown in mobile revenue growth (+4% in 3Q vs +6% in 2Q) but a sharp jump in new broadband subscriptions (up 1.5x QoQ).

Property reported NPAT was ahead, but only because the P&L was recognizing past sales. Reservation sales were pretty poor, falling another 19% QoQ in 3Q24, though cancelation rates seem to have peaked.

The Consumer sector was mixed, though skewed to the “miss” end of the spectrum, as inflation took its toll. What was encouraging, though, was the near consensus among companies that 3Q24 had marked the bottom, with demand seeing a rebound due to a) slower inflation, b) minimum wage hikes, and c) early election spending. As mentioned above, the Transportation and Gaming sectors were dragged by surprise losses from CEB and BLOOM.

Overweight Banks and Consumers heading into 2025E

We remain overweight Banks, as recent RRR cuts could help mitigate NIM compression. Consumers are already seeing early signs of a recovery in demand, and we expect their fortunes to continue turning upward into 4Q24/1H25.

 
Philippine Property - 3Q24 recap: residential presales fall, inventory days rise
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment weakens further

The 3Q numbers for the residential segment showed further deterioration, with reservation sales falling and inventory days rising. Meanwhile, office vacancy remained flat while mall revenue growth decelerated.

Residential reservation sales fall again

After falling 10% QoQ in 2Q24, aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover dropped another 19% QoQ (down 16% YoY) to P91bn, c. 20% below the 2023 average (and about 30% below pre-COVID levels). Demand for mid-income condominiums in Metro Manila had already been under pressure and the

announcement of the POGO ban in July likely caused further weakness. Colliers estimates that the level of unsold condominium inventory in Metro Manila increased to c. 5.5 years of reservation sales, from c. 5.0 years in 2Q24. The deterioration appears to be due to a reduction in trailing 12M sales, rather than an increase in the number of available units for sale.

On a more positive note, aggregate residential revenue (of the developers we cover) grew 18% YoY to P57bn. The strong growth over the past four quarters (+23% YoY) likely indicates that cancellation rates have peaked (for units where the buyer has paid at least 10% of the unit value).

Office vacancy stays flat but POGO exodus looms

Office vacancy remained flat QoQ at 16%, while rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila declined slightly. Office transactions from every major tenant category fell QoQ, with POGO net take-up turning negative in 3Q24.

We expect the POGO ban to be negative for the office segment, particularly in the Manila Bay area. We estimate that the sector occupies 4% (600,000sqm) of Metro Manila office stock and c. 20% of Manila Bay area office space. Thus, a ban on the sector could result in Metro Manila office vacancy rising to 20% and Manila Bay area office vacancy climbing to about 50%. In such a scenario, it would be reasonable for office rental rates to fall 5%-10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area.

The POGO sector employs over 100,000 foreign nationals (based on our estimates), a large portion of whom reside in the Manila Bay area. Thus, their departure could lead to significant drops in Manila Bay area property prices.

Mall revenue growth slows down

Mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators slowed to 7% in 3Q24 (from 9% in 2Q24). This implies that same-mall-revenue growth is at the low-end of the historical range of 4%-8%.

 
Philippine Seven - 9M profits up 14%; SSSG decelerates
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Profits rise 14% on revenue growth of 16% for 9M24

Philippine Seven Corp (SEVN) reported revenues rising +16% to P64.9bn for 9M24, lifting profits to P2.58bn (+14%). System-wide sales expanded 17% to P68.8bn. For 3Q alone, revenues rose 12% to P21.6bn while profits grew +13%and system-wide sales expanded 13%.

Some deceleration was visible in 3Q, as SSSG slowed to +2.7%, the lowest since the middle of the pandemic. We view the 3Q results as being slightly below expectations.

SSSG dips

SSSG for the latest quarter was +2.7%, a slowdown from the previous quarter/s (see Fig 1) and the lowest since 2Q21, when the country and the world were one year into the Covid pandemic. The company cited unfavorable weather as a factor, specifically a significant increase in rainfall. The country has been beset by a string of typhoons of unusual ferocity over the past few months. Given the relatively higher proportion of discretionary / impulse products, SEVN would be more vulnerable to such swings.

We understand that SSSG remains soft in the early part of 4Q, and weather is likely to have remained a factor.

Store count surpasses 4,000

The company reported gross additions of 101 stores with five closures in 3Q. This brought store count to 4,022, implying net additions of 254 stores YT-Sept. In contrast, Alfamart added 327 stores over the period, taking the latter to 2,019. SEVN is aiming to add `100 stores in 4Q and open another 500 next year.

4Q bounce could fall short

We can expect a 4Q bounce for both revenue and margins – the former due to holiday spending and the latter due to supplier rebates. Meeting our FY24 profit expectation (P4.08bn) looks like a tall order, however. Nevertheless, we remain upbeat about the longer-term trajectory of the sector and the company.

 
GT Capital - Strong profits at key units compensate for weakness in property unit
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M24 core profit up 7% YoY to P20.9bn – Ahead of Regis but in line with Street

3Q24 profit to common rose 11% QoQ, 15% YoY to P7.46bn, taking 9M24 profit up 7% to P20.9bn. The results are trending ahead of Regis estimates but appears broadly in line with Street estimates. Profit contributions from Metrobank (see results comment here), as well as Toyota and Metro Pacific (MPI) were robust but the property unit disappointed.

Auto profits rebound in 3Q24, but likely temporary

Standalone profits from its 51%-owned unit, Toyota Motor Phils (TMP), stood at P4.6bn in 3Q24, up 32% QoQ (+62% YoY), as the PHP strengthened sequentially against the USD and lifted GPM to 14.5%. Wholesale volumes likewise jumped 10% QoQ, boosting TMP’s revenues, after dealer inventory levels were depleted in end-June. Retail volumes were flat QoQ (+8% YoY) but should rebound as the peak holiday season approaches. Given that the PHP weakened against the USD starting in October, we reckon 4Q margins and profits could soften again.

MPI sustains strong momentum, with 9M24 core profit up 34%

Profit contributions from its infrastructure unit, MPI, rose 34% YoY to P3.8bn in 9M24, with profits for 3Q24 alone up by 33% YoY to P1.5bn. Its power and water distribution units, as well as the tollroad unit, delivered strong results, underpinned by tariff hikes, robust volume growth, and lower costs. After  Japan-based Mitsui swapped its stake in MPI for a stake in MPI’s tollroad unit in October, GTCAP’s effective stake in MPI rose to 19.6% from 18.2%.

Property: Cancellations lead to wider losses

Excluding lot sales, we estimate the property unit incurred a net loss of P110mn in 9M24, of which P123mn was booked in 3Q24 alone. The company attributed the weak performance to higher sales cancellations. Reservation sales fell 33% YoY to P12bn in 9M24, which the company blamed on (a) lower launches (1 in 9M24 vs. 4 last year) and (b) the depletion of its BGC inventory. Cognizant of tough property market conditions, the company will focus on launching horizontal projects outside Metro Manila.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI’s Manila port posts record high volumes in October

The Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) saw record high monthly volume in October of 275,028 TEUs, according to a PR from the Philippine Ports Authority. This suggests volumes grew by 10% YoY, accelerating from the anemic 4% pace in 9M24. Import volumes alone rose to a record 147,935 TEUs, up by 9% YoY, faster than the 4% growth rate in 9M24. MICT accounts for over half of the country’s foreign container volumes

Brings YT-October volumes up 5% YoY to 2.41mn TEUs, trending just slightly behind Regis FY24E of 2.9mn TEUs (+7%). Regis estimates MICT accounts for 23% of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) volumes, 22% of revenues and 20% of EBITDA.

ICTSI Mexico port volumes rebound in October on new capacity

Volumes at ICTSI’s port in Mexico (CMSA) rose by 25% YoY and 16% MoM to 139k TEUs in October. This follows the completion of Phase 3A expansion, which added 17% to the port’s capacity to ~1.7mn TEUs. ICT took market share, as industry volumes grew by 6% YoY and 10% MoM to 340k TEUs.

Brings YT-October volumes up by 15% YoY to 1.2mn TEUs, on track to exceed our FY24E forecast of 1.43mn TEUs (+12%), given that volumes only need to average 112k TEUs per month for Nov/Dec. Regis estimates CMSA accounts for 11% of ICTSI’s volumes, 20% of revenues, 25% of EBITDA.

BDO CEO expects net profit growth to be sustained in 2025

BDO President Nestor Tan said he expects the bank to sustain its profit growth in 2025 because earnings have been driven by the core businesses, rather than extraordinary items. Regis forecasts FY25E net profit to hit P90.2bn, from P82.4bn in FY24E; note that 9M24 net profit rose 12% to P60.6bn.

BDO vs BPI - historical P/bk

Putting the dynamics between BDO and BPI valuations into context…

Looking at the 20-year charts, BPI was for a VERY long time THE premium bank. In fact, until 2013/14, BDO was trading closer to MBT’s valuation levels. But from 2013-2016, it was a massive re-rating for BDO, where it really pulled away from MBT. That period also coincided with BPI’s steady de-rating. So much so that from 2016 to 2019, BDO started to trade at a PREMIUM to BPI.

Then during Covid (and just after), the two converged.

It was really only this year when BPI’s P/bk pulled away from BDO. As of this week, BPI’s P/bk is now a 23% premium to that of BDO (trailing P/bk)

Not surprisingly, the valuation premium used to be completely explain-able by the ROE premium of BPI vs BDO. However, I think the current BPI premium is getting a bit stretched. It assumes that BPI’s ROE will sustainably be higher than that of BDO. And that’s not in our (or the Street’s) forecasts

Meralco allots P25bn for capex in 2025

Meralco is allotting “a minimum of P25bn” for its distribution unit’s capex in 2025. This is higher than this year’s capex budget of P22bn. The capex earmarked for storm hardening and grid resilience, according to Meralco Executive Vice President and Chief Operating Officer Ronnie L. Aperocho.

Meanwhile, it expects capex for its generation unit to be “much higher” than this year due to SP New Energy Corp.’s (SPNEC PM) Terra Solar project. Meralco allotted P40bn of capex in 2024, up from P30bn in 2023.

ACEN spending P26bn for integrated solar project

ACEN is spending P26bn to build a 420 MWp solar plant and 1,238.5MW hours battery energy storage system (BESS) in Palauig, Zambales. Project is under its wholly-owned subsidiary, Giga Ace 8. It has seeking clearance from the Department of Environment and Natural Resources to increase the capacity and land area of its solar project to 370 has from 275 has. Project originally had a target capacity of 346-megawatt peak (MWp). ACEN will start the project in early 2026 with completion set in 2030.

ACEN has 6,754MW of attributable RE capacity, of which 3,051MW is operational. In the Philippines alone, ACEN has a total of 2,397MW of capacity, of which 1,330 MW are under construction. Its international att. RE capacity totals 4,357 MW, of which c. 2,400 MW are under construction.

ADB extends US$100m for Ayala Corp’s EV charging network

ADB has approved loans of up to US$100mn (P5.9bn) for Ayala Corp. subsidiary, AC Mobility Hldgs, to support its initiative in building an electric mobility system in the country. AC Mobility sought an US$85mn ordinary-capital-resources loan and a US$15mn concessional loan from the ADB. This is to fund (1) the installation of up to 1,700 electric vehicle charging stations (EVCS) and (2) procurement of EVs for distribution operation over the next 5-7 years.

AC Mobility has 111 EVCS today available in 50 locations nationwide. As of November 2024, the country has 833 EVCS. The government is targeting 7,300 EVCS by 2028 and 2.5mn by 2040. EV sales (BEV, HEV, PHEVs) rose by 133% YoY to 14,178 units in 9M24, accounting for c. 4% of new vehicle sales.

Jollibee to fold in Tim Ho Wan by January 2025

Jollibee is folding Tim Ho Wan (THW) into JFC by Jan 2025 to improve JFC ROIC. However, it will not be consolidating Tim Ho Wan into JFC’s balance sheet. Jollibee confirmed that it has booked an P810mn windfall in the form of a revaluation gain from selling its stake in Tim Ho Wan to the parent company, according to JFC CFO, Richard Shin. Recall that earlier this month, Jollibee announced it is taking full control of THW by buying out the other investors in Titan Dining. Prior to this, JFC was only an investor (albeit 92% ownership) in the fund that owned the business so did not manage it. Tim Ho Wan is a Chinese-style dimsum restaurant chain with over 60 branches globally.

SM Prime to launch more horizontal residential projects

SM Prime said that over the next five years, 85% of its residential projects will be horizontal developments. The company will offer a range of products, from economic to premium and leisure developments. The premium line will offer units over P25mn. SM Prime’s residential segment comprises c. 30% of operating profit.

The company also said that it would add over 500,000sqm of mall GFA in 2025, all of the which will be in provincial areas. This will bring SM Prime’s Philippine mall portfolio to 10mn sqm by end 2025 (9.4mn sqm currently).

Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) to accelerate store openings

RRHI plans to accelerate its store openings over the next three to five years to increase topline, according to RRHI Vice President for Corporate Planning Gina R. Dipaling during the Philippine Stock Exchange’s STAR Investor Day. Hard-discount grocery retail store, O!Save, plans to put up 200-300 stores next year from 100 stores this year. By end-2024, store count will be 400 (318 as of Sept 2024). RRHI has a 23% interest in O!Save.

AGI 9M24 net profit remains flat at P13.0bn

AGI’s 3Q24 net profit rose 14% YoY (down 8% QoQ) to P4.2bn. Three of its subsidiaries saw a worsening of profits in 3Q24. Emperador’s 3Q24 attributable profit fell 42% QoQ and 44% YoY to P1.0bn as revenue fell c. 10% YoY and 5% QoQ to P14.6bn. The brandy segment saw weakness in Spain, Mexico, and the Philippines, where consumers shifted to value brands. The whisky segment experienced weakness in Asia Pacific and North America.

Travellers showed a loss of P107mn in 3Q24 (vs profit of P254mn in 2Q24 and a loss of P13mn in 3Q23). Travellers’ 3Q24 GGR fell c. 50% YoY and 60% QoQ to P4.2bn. For the 9M period, Travellers said that it experienced higher volume but suffered from a lower win rate.

Golden Arches’ attributable profit dropped 68% QoQ and 63% YoY to P99mn.

Megaworld’s attributable profit grew 37% QoQ and 34% YoY to P4.1bn. Residential revenue grew 30% YoY in 3Q and 9M, thanks in part to faster revenue recognition of sales from ready-for-occupancy units.

Golden Arches 3Q24 SSSG falls to -2%

Alliance Global revealed some details about its operating subsidiaries during its 3Q24 briefing: Golden Arches’ 3Q24 SSSG fell to -2%, from +5% in 2Q24. Revenue fell due to typhoons during the period, while margins fell due to higher input costs, higher A&P, and higher minimum wages.

As for AGI’s other subsidiaries - Emperador’s 3Q24 brandy and whisky sales fell 9%-10% YoY (down 6%-7% QoQ). Brandy GPM fell due to higher cost of liquid and higher packaging cost. The brandy segment saw weakness in Spain, Mexico, and the Philippines, where consumers shifted to value brands. The whisky segment experienced weakness in Asia Pacific and North America.

Travellers’ VIP GGR fell 8% YoY and 20% QoQ as volume increased but the company suffered from a lower win rate.

DigiPlus Interactive reportedly in talks to buy CasinoPlus

According to Bilyonaryo.com, DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is in talks to purchase CasinoPlus. The combined entity will reportedly have quarterly revenue of P30bn. Recall that PLUS reported 3Q24 revenue of P19bn.

DigiPlus is in the final stages of the licensing process to operate in Brazil

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) has passed the qualification stage for a federal license with Brazil’s Ministry of Finance’s Secretariat of Awards and Bets (SPA). The final stage requires PLUS to fulfill post-qualification regulatory requirements within 30 days, including platform certification and license fee payments. Upon completing these requirements, the SPA will release the final list of operators authorized to operate starting 1 January 2025. Once finalized, this will enable PLUS to offer online sports betting and other iGaming services in Brazil.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in October

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 60bps MoM to 20.1% (in October) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index was flat MoM at 25.3%. The reduction in foreign ownership was largely due to the P329bn increase in the market cap of Golden MV Holdings (HVN PM), which has zero foreign ownership. We estimate that if the market cap of HVN had stayed flat MoM, then foreign ownership of the market would only have fallen 14bps MoM to 20.6%.

Net foreign buying declined to US$22mn, from US$346mn in September. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 19 saw increases in foreign ownership while 11 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

AC – foreign ownership rose 137bps MoM to 27.4%. Although AC’s saw the highest net foreign buying for the month, the 137bps MoM increase in foreign ownership looks large relative to the net foreign buying of US$24.5mn.  

URC – foreign ownership increased 83bps MoM to 23.2%.

PGOLD – foreign ownership expanded 51bps MoM to 19.0%, while net foreign buying reached US$8.0mn, the fifth highest for the month.

WLCOM – we estimate that foreign ownership fell 30bps MoM to 15.7%. Net foreign selling hit US$3.9mn, the fourth highest for the month.

BSP Governor signals easing on course

BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said that either a 25bps cut or a pause is on the table at the Monetary Board’s final policy meeting on Dec 19, saying “We are still in the easing cycle. Either we cut in Dec or we cut in the next meeting. But gradual cuts only.

He also said that the BSP is intervening in the foreign exchange market, focusing on preventing excessive volatility. Regis anticipates the BSP to cut its policy rate by another 25bps in December, in line with Jefferies’ expectation of a 25bps cut at the Fed’s Dec meeting

Government keen on retaining rice tariff cut

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) is recommending keeping the present rice tariff rate of 15% for now, according to Senator Cynthia Villar during plenary deliberations on the proposed 2025 budget of the Department of Agriculture. Officials cited foreign exchange rate concerns, which would impact rice prices.

Under Executive Order 62, the tariff rate shall be subjected to a review every four months, with the next review set in February 2025. Agriculture Secretary Tiu Larel Jr. said that “a lot bought “palay” (rice paddy) at a high price of P35 per kilo in July-Aug” and they will need about six months to sell to avoid incurring huge losses, which explains the gradual decrease in the retail price of rice.

BOP posts US$724mn deficit in October; End-October GIR settles at US$111bn

The BSP reported that the country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$724mn in October. This was a reversal of the US$3.5bn surplus in Sep 2024 and the US$1.51bn surplus in October 2023. This brings YT-October BOP position to a US$4.4bn surplus, higher than the US$3.2bn in the same period last year. BSP is forecasting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn in FY24, equivalent to 0.3% of GDP. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-October stood at US$111.1bn, slightly lower than the US$112.7bn in end-September. GIR level is equivalent to 8.0 months’ worth of imports. The BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$104bn in end-2024.

Air pax volumes rise 21% YoY in 9M24 but still a shade below pre-Covid levels

Data from the Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) showed that systemwide passenger volumes grew by 21% YoY to 44.1mn in 9M24. This is still a tad below 9M19 levels of 44.9mn. This implies that in 3Q24 alone, systemwide pax volumes rose 13% YoY to 14.6mn pax.

Cebu Air (CEB PM) sustained market share gains, with pax volumes up 23% YoY to 5.6mn, exceeding 3Q19 levels by 12%.

Remittances rise 3.3% YoY in September

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.3% YoY to US$3.01bn in September due to acceleration in land-based remittances from the USA (+4.3% in Sep vs 2.5%% in 8M24). Sea-based remittances growth remain sluggish at 1.5%. In PHP terms, remittances rose by 2%, slowest paces in ten months. This brings YT-September remittances up by 3% YoY to US$25.2bn. This is broadly in line with the BSP’s target growth rate of +3% for the year.

Vehicle sales pick up in October, rising by 4.9% YoY, 1.2% MoM

CAMPI/TMA reported industry-wide vehicle sales grew to 40,003 units in October, up 4.9% YoY, 1.2% MoM. Note that the Philippine International Motor Show was held during the month. This brings YT-October sales up by 8.9% YoY to 384,310 units.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) maintained its market share at 46.4% in YT-October, suggesting that it sold 178,435 units during the period, up by 10% YoY. Implied unit sales for October alone at 19,347 units, up by 7.5% YoY, +5% MoM. TMP appears on track to beat its target sales of 215k units (+7%), as it only need to sell 18.3k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales for the rest of the year only need to average 13.7k units per month.

TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).
