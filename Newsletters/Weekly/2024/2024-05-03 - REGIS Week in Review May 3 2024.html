REGIS REPORT

03 May 2024

The PSEi ended the week little changed after signs of support in the beginning of the week faded through the subsequent sessions. The index initially posted its best daily gain so far this year on Monday, +2.13% to a three-week high of 6769, before mostly stepping lower through the rest of the week and ultimately settling flattish at 6615 -0.12% WoW as investors parsed through a busy round of earnings reports.

Best performing stock in the benchmark this week was ACEN +8.99% WoW, extending its momentum from last week amid the continuing power shortage alerts in the country. The stock is now +32.58% from its 52-week low that it hit on mid-April but remains a laggard YTD -6.16% (and before that -42.52% YoY in 2023, the 2nd worst in the benchmark of that year.) Other names with positive exposure to the spot market are AP +0.13% and SCC +0.77%. Another highlight was ICT which surged +5.64% WoW and marked a new 52-week high during the week. Volume preview for its ports in Mexico and Melbourne showed strong underlying demand, and as such our sense is that the company may post strong organic volume growth rates when it reports 1Q results on Monday.

On the earnings front, MBT’s 1Q NPAT stood well above forecasts (27% of Regis’ P44.8b FY24E) as provisions fell by -77% (see below). The stock extended gains this week, +2.81% WoW, as is still currently the best performing stock in the benchmark, now +39.57% YTD (+47.75% total return). On the other end, property plays ALI -5.78% and SMPH -3.35% remained weak. In Colliers’ 1Q24 briefing held this week, their data revealed that office vacancy stayed flat and condominium presold units dropped 50% during the quarter. Results by their peer RLC (unchanged WoW) however showed that1Q core net profit rose +21% to 3.34b, largely inline with street forecasts, while revenues +19% while EBIT +28%, both also inline. More details after their earnings call later.

The consumer sector meanwhile looked mixed with URC +4.46% rising back up the psychological 100-level, while JFC -7.45%, PGOLD -2.76% and MONDE -2.02% trailed behind. URC announced results today:1Q NPAT +21% to P4.1b, ahead of Regis and consensus, while EBIT +16% to P5.4b, also ahead of Regis and consensus. More details after their briefing later. One bigger gainer outside the benchmark however was SEVN +23.55% (new 52-week high), extending gains from the prior week after the company announced a sizable P9.6/share cash dividend. Note that our research team initiated a coverage on SEVN in the earlier part of April – we have a target price of 135/share, which at these levels represents a nearly 30% upside.

Average daily market activity this week stood at $167m, +116% WoW, but note that this was largely inflated by a $290m block in BLOOM -0.2% reflected on the board last Tuesday (BLOOM sub Sureste’s purchase of shares from GGAM). Net outflow this week was quite heavy at nearly P291m but that too was practically due to the BLOOM outflow block. Furthermore, there was also a smaller $10m outflow block in SMPH reflected on the board today (looks like an internal change of hands), and as such, excluding those one-off blocks, net foreign flow would be lightly skewed to buy this week, coming off a long 14-day outflow streak that ended last week. Top inflows were into lenders BDO +2.92% and MBT +2.81%, followed by ICT +5.64%, MER -0.92%, GTCAP +3.69% and ACEN +8.99%, while top outflows were developers SMPH -3.35% and ALI -5.78%, and JFC -7.45%.

Manila Electric Company - 1Q24: Power usage up sharply; generation profit dips on maintenance
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income rises 11%to P10bn

Meralco reported 1Q24 net income of P9.6bn (+19% YoY), with core income of P10.1bn (+11% YoY). The core profit figure appears well ahead of both Regis and Street estimates. While profits from generation may have declined, Meralco saw unusual growth in power usage for its core distribution (DU) business and benefited from a sharp jump in profits from its retail supply (RES) business.

Unit sales jump 9%

Electricity volumes for its DU business rose 9% in 1Q24. This unusually high number is a consequence of the high temperatures currently being experienced across the country. Residential volumes were up an exceptional 12% while commercial volumes rose 11%. Industrial usage was up a more modest 3%. Management says that usage for April is likely to be 8% higher YoY. The regulated DU business accounted for 58% of core profit in 1Q24 (vs 49% in 1Q23).

Generation profits subside; retail supply business takes up slack

Contributions from the generation business declined ~27% to P2.7bn, partly due to plant maintenance done in 1Q24, and partly because the exceptional margins at its Singapore subsidiary Pacific Light are dropping to more normal levels. The RES segment, meanwhile, saw profits jump sharply (>80%) to P1.5bn. Meralco’s RES unit unilaterally terminated supply contracts that it was losing money on over a year ago.

Big acquisitions in the works

After taking control of RE developer SPNEC, Meralco is aiming to conclude an even bigger purchase: the acquisition of San Miguel’s LNG facilities at a valuation of US$3.3bn. Meralco expects to have a 40% economic interest in the pooled LNG assets. The papers for the deal have yet to be filed with competition authorities. Approval of the deal could, in our view, be contentious.

Core profit guidance of P40bn for FY24

Management provided guidance for core profit of P40bn for the year, well above Regis and Street estimates. This considers the strong power usage being witnessed so far, as well as contributions from its ancillary investments.

 
Metropolitan Bank & Trust Co - 1Q24 NPAT beats, ROEs at 18-year highs as loans jump 12%, credit costs fall
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1Q24 NPAT P12bn, well above forecasts as provisions fall 77%

Metrobank’s 1Q24 NPAT increased 14% YoY to P12.0bn, handily beating our expectations (27% of our P44.8bn for FY24E) and consensus (P46.6bn). PPOP was broadly in line, up 7% YoY. The beat came mainly from provision expense, which fell 77%YoY as the bank unwinds its over-provisioning while asset quality remains benign. ROE for the quarter jumped to an 18-year high of 13.7%, helped by a special cash dividend that is part of the bank’s continuing plan to improve capital efficiency. We maintain our Buy rating on the stock.

Loan growth jumps to 12% on corporate capex; NIMs steady

Gross loans grew 12.1% YoY (net was up 13.1%), accelerating from the 8.4% clip in 4Q23. Corporate loans jumped 11.2%, doubling the 5.5% pace set the previous quarter as capex seems to be picking up. Consumer loan growth was steady at 15.3% YoY (15.9% in 4Q), driven by credit cards (25.5%) and auto loans (18.2%). NIMs were stable, down a smidgen (-2bps QoQ) as deposit and overall funding costs inched up 4-5bps QoQ.

PPOP +7%; profit boosted by provision drop to 14bps credit cost

Non-interest income was soft, down 19% YoY as trading/fx gains fell 68% YoY. Opex, was contained, though, propping up PPOP +7%, in line with expectations. Asset quality was benign, with NPL ratio steady at 1.7%, allowing the bank to cut provision expense 77% to P562mn, or a credit cost of 14.4bps (vs 57bps in 2023). Despite the cut, NPL cover remained extremely high at 174% (was 180% in 4Q).

Capital management – special cash div (53% payout) takes 1Q24 ROE to 13.7%

Metrobank declared a P2/sh special cash dividend in March, on top of the regular P3/sh (P1.50 in March and September), equivalent to 53% of 2023 NPAT and a 7% yield on current share prices. This helped propel 1Q24 ROE to 13.7% the highest quarterly level since 1Q2006. Despite the high dividend, consolidated CAR stayed high at 16.8%, with CET-1 at 16.0%.

Metro Pacific expects to bag tollway contract in Indonesia within May

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) hopes to secure the contract for its toll project bid in Indonesia by end of May according to MPTC CFO Christopher Lizo. MPTC was declared the preferred bidder and is now in the process of negotiating with Jasamarga, a state-owned enterprise and the largest toll road operator in Indonesia. The contract will allow MPTC to own a 35% stake in PT Jasamarga Transjawa Tol, a 676-km tollroad that services 900k vehicles per day. Regis understands that the contract is purely an O&M contract, given that Jasamarga has already spent for the buildout of the tollroad. For perspective, MPTC’s daily traffic volumes for all its tollroads in the Philippines stood at 660k in 2023. Previously, MPTC said it expects to invest US$600mn to secure its bid for a portion of the Trans-Java toll road in Indonesia. The company jointly bid for the project with Singapore’s GIC.

He is now the second largest shareholder of the company (after the Lopez family). He owns 75.88mn ABS shares through LL Holdings. He also holds 619,000 ABS-CBN Holdings Corp (ABSP PM) shares through Countryside Investments. These ABSP shares are being converted into ABS shares.

PLDT completes purchase of minority stake in Radius Telecom

PLDT completed its acquisition of 34.9% of Radius Telecom. The purchase price was previously announced as P2.12bn for that stake. Radius is a subsidiary of Meralco and offers broadband services under the Red Fiber brand and cloud services.

ICTSI (ICT PM) 1Q24 volume preview for Mexico / Melbourne

Strong underlying demand was evident at the ports of Manzanillo, Mexico and Melbourne in 1Q24. Regis is particularly encouraged by the strong volume recovery in Melbourne, which was a key drag to organic volume growth in 2023. Regis believes ICTSI (ICT PM) could post strong organic volume growth rates when it reports results on Monday, May 6

 

CMSA volumes up 36.5% YoY in 1Q24. Volumes at Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) rose 36.5% YoY, 6.8% QoQ to 358k TEUs in 1Q24, averaging 119k TEUs per month and trending well above our full-year forecast of 1.39mn TEUs (+9% YoY).  CMSA’s market share improved further to 37% in 1Q24 from ~34% in 2023. With the completion of CMSA’s 250k TEU expansion by mid-year, Regis anticipates the company could continue to capitalize on the strong trade activity in Mexico.  They also estimate CMSA accounts for 11% of consolidated volumes and 20% of revenues. 

 

Melbourne ports recover.  Volumes at the three container ports in Melbourne jumped 11% YoY, +5% QoQ to 727k TEUs in 1Q24, the first material increase since 3Q22.  Regis suspects volume growth at Victoria International Container Terminal (VICT) may have been much stronger than the industry figure of +11%, after a major client transferred all its ships at VICT beginning January.  With the volume recovery, Regis anticipates VICT’s yield per TEU could also rise by at least double-digits this year, as tariff hikes that were eroded in 2023 due to unfavorable volume mix should reverse this year.  Regis estimates VICT accounts for 7% of group-wide volumes and 8% of revenues. 

 

MICT volumes still likely soft in 1Q24.  The port authority has not released 1Q24 volume data yet but Regis reckons demand may have still been lackluster for ICT’s port in Manila (MICT).  Recall that container volumes in the Philippines rose by a mere 1% in 2023, with MICT’s volumes falling by 2%.  Weak demand, however, could be offset by the approval and implementation of a 17% tariff hike sometime in 3Q.

 

Valuations remain inexpensive relative to peers and historical average.   ICTSI has outperformed the PCOMP by 30% year-to-date but valuations remain inexpensive, in our view.  At 16x FY24E PER and 8x EV/EBITDA, ICT still trades below peers (17x/11x) and historical valuations (22x/11x).

The high provision expense continues to be a concern. The PR and limited FS did not disclose any NPL or NPL cover data, nor did it explain the high credit cost.

DMCI eyes quick CHP turnaround; unveils recovery roadmap

In a press release, DMCI identified its plans of turning around Cemex Holdings Philippines’ (CHP PM) operations by 2025. The company cited lowering power, fuel, and other production supplies costs, which represent 73% of CHP’s cost of sales, by sourcing from Semirara (SCC PM). It also plans to lower administrative and selling expenses, which accounted for 52% of prior-year operating expenses, by lowering talent and business process onshoring initiatives. Initiatives will also be aided by the completion of CHP’s 1.5mn MT production line by Sep 2024. Semirara plans to supply 500k MT of coal annually to CHP, up 227% from 2024 levels, and it will also supply 50 MW of electricity and fly ash. DMCI and DMCI Homes are estimated to source around 500k MT of cement from CHP initially, which can gradually ramp up once the outlook for its construction and property units gets better.

Robinsons Retail - 1Q24 SSS slows on soft demand, Holy Week timing

1Q24 recurring net profit +30% YoY – in line

Robinsons Retail’s headline net profit surged 9.5x YoY to P5.1bn due to a one-time gain resulting from the BPI-RBC merger. Excluding this one-time gain, Regis estimates recurring net profit grew 30% YoY to P696mn, broadly in line with Regis and consensus expectations. Both revenues (P45.9bn) and EBIT (P1.9bn) grew 3% YoY, trending within expectations. Consolidated SSSG slowed to +0.9% due in part to the timing of the Holy Week (March 2024 vs April 2023), which led to the partial closure of some stores. Among the retail formats, the food segment (supermarkets + CVS), drug stores, and department stores performed in line with expectations. Meanwhile, DIY and specialty stores are tracking behind.

 

Supermarkets continue to be weighed by downtrading, drug stores outperform

SSS for the supermarket segment grew 1.5% in 1Q24, slightly faster than the 4Q clip. Transactions grew 11%, while average baskets contracted 9% YoY, albeit better than the declines in previous quarters (-10%), as consumers continued to downtrade. Meanwhile, drug stores outperformed with SSS +6.5%, driven by strong demand for pharmaceuticals. The food and drug store segments collectively accounted for 80% of consolidated sales in 1Q24.

 

Discretionary formats underperform due to weak demand, seasonality

DIY stores and specialty stores were the underperformers, with segment SSS declining 6.5% and 7.1%, respectively. This was partly driven by seasonality, due to the timing of the Holy Week (March 2024 vs April 2023), which led to the partial closure of stores for two days. Underlying demand also continued to be weak given inflationary pressures to consumers.

 

SSSG guidance maintained; sequential recovery seen

Management is optimistic that overall demand should improve in the coming quarters as inflationary pressures ease. Despite the soft top line in 1Q24, the company believes SSS trends should likewise improve, particularly for discretionary formats. It is maintaining its FY SSSG guidance of +3-5% (Regis FY24E: +3.9%).

PAGCOR 1Q24 revenue jumps 43%

PAGCOR said that its 1Q24 revenue grew 43% to P25.2bn, largely due to the growth of e-games. Around P22.3bn came from gaming operations, P9.7bn from e-games and P8.0bn from “Licensed casinos and integrated resorts”. Regis estimates that GGR of e-games was close to the 4Q23 level of P25.8bn, 175% higher than the 1Q23 figure of P9.4bn. DigiPlus (PLUS PM) net profit hit P2bn in 4Q23.

Government raises target borrowings to P2.57tn this year

The government raised its 2024 borrowing program by 4% to P2.57tn this year. This will be 17% higher than the P2.19tn total borrowings in 2023. They cited recent adjustments in the budget deficit targets, which resulted in slightly lower tax revenue targets due to a lower nominal GDP forecast (-3% lower vs previous target). As of 1Q24, the government has borrowed P830bn, up 32% YoY.

Government hikes dividend rate of GOCCs

The Department of Finance has increased the mandated dividend remittance rate of government-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs) to at least 75% of net earnings from the minimum 50%. The idea is to improve revenue collection without imposing new taxes. Dividend collections from GOCCs reached P39.8bn as of April 24, up by 497.5% from the P8bn recorded in the same period in 2023. The government received P139bn from GOCCs in 2023, accounting for 3% of total revenues.

ERC to suspend WESM trading when Luzon and Visayas are under red alert

The ERC ordered the suspension of trading in the WESM during red alerts to prevent a spike in electricity prices. During the suspension, the administrative price, or the price imposed by the market operator to the trading participants during market suspension or market intervention, will be applied. If a dispatch interval is subject to both a price mitigation and the administered price, “the lower of the two prices shall apply in the settlement of transactions for such interval. Suspensions will be lifted “only if the regional available capacity, less actual regional demand, reaches above zero for 24 consecutive hours.”.

 

The ERC said the average spot prices per day increased by 11% in Luzon and by 53% in the Visayas when alert notices were issued due to the high heat index. The ERC’s move will reduce the supernormal profits that could accrue to generators during periods of tight supply (e.g. now). It would also have implications on the willingness of gencos to invest, especially in the absence of contracts. Among those who have significant power capacity available for sale to the spot market are: Aboitiz Power (AP PM), Semirara (SCC PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM).

Marcos calls for review of minimum wage rates

President Marcos Jr. called on the Regional Tripartite Wage Productivity Boards to review the minimum wage rates in their respective regions and to consider the impact of inflation, among others. The review will be done within 60 days prior to the anniversary of their latest wage order. Since 2022 and 2023, the government has implemented cumulative wage hikes of +20-22% across regions.

BSP sees headline inflation settling at 3.5%-4.3% in April

The BSP is forecasting headline inflation to settle within the range of 3.5%-4.3% in April. Consensus is at 4.1% for April while March inflation rate stood at 3.7%. The government cited sustained increases in food prices, particularly rice and meat, as well as higher gasoline prices and a weaker PHP. April inflation will be released on May 7.

Colliers: Office vacancy stays flat in 1Q24, condominium units presold drops 50%

During its 1Q24 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila office vacancy fell slightly to 19.0%, from 19.3% in 4Q23. Rental rates in most Metro Manila business districts rose 1% YoY and QoQ. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise slightly to 19.6% by end-2024. While it expects office take-up to increase this year, new supply will cause an increase in vacancy. It also expects office rental rates to rise 2.5% in 2024. Yellow alert raised over Luzon grid

The National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) placed the Luzon grid under yellow alert on Thursday (May 2) afternoon (3-4pm). Peak demand hit 13,818 megawatts (MW) against available capacity of 15,115 MW. The ERC commissioner Dimalanta urged distribution utilities to communicate with their suppliers about a staggered payment mechanism for the collection of generation charges, to provide temporary relief to customers.

Government debt dips to P14.93tn in March

The national government’s total outstanding debt dropped by 1.7% MoM to P14.93tn as of end-March. The  gov’t cited a net redemption of domestic government securities. Meanwhile, domestic debt fell by 2.8% MoM to P10.28tn while external debt rose by 1.0% MoM to P4.65tn. By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028.
