REGIS REPORT

08 February 2024

The index rallied 2.1% for the week and closed at 6850 on the back of a decelerating inflation print for the month of January .

The January 2024 CPI inflation was 2.8%, well below the Dec 2023 print (3.9%) and Street predictions (3.1%). The headline inflation figure was pulled down by food, which printed inflation of 3.5% from 5.4% in Dec, despite the continued rise in rice prices. The decline in vegetable costs deepened further in Jan (-21% from -9%). The decline in CPI inflation is not a surprise since the Bangko Sentral (BSP) has been guiding for a drop in CPI inflation for early 2024 due to the YoY base effects. The pace of the drop, however, appears to be faster than the market is anticipating.

The rally was broad-based. Only four index names ended in the red.

Foreign demand lifted the index. Headline inflows were strong at US$41.6m. BDO saw the most inflows at US$11.4m.

Top performer was ICT (+9.3% WoW) after announcing it reached a settlement with the labor union in Portland for US$20.5m. ICT will accept the amount as settlement for a lawsuit it filed against the union, which initiated a work stoppage at ICTSI’s Portland operations at that time, disrupting regular operations. ICT subsequently terminated its concession agreement in Portland in 2017 due to those labor issues.

Earlier this week, BPI (-2.3% WoW) reported headline profit for 2023 at Php51.7bn, up 31% YoY, in line with Regis (P51.55bn) and ahead of consensus (P50.33bn). Loan growth jumped to 10.5% YoY, compared to 8.8% in 3Q23, while NIMs expanded 50bps YoY. Higher opex was offset by strong trading gains. CFO Eric Luchangco says it expects “stronger” loan growth this year.  For 2024, Regis expects loans to grow ~17%, partly due to the consolidation of Robinsons Bank.

Globe (+0.8% WoW) also released their earnings results. Core income for FY23 came in at P19.1bn, down 1%, and below expectations. While the bottom line was a disappointment, mobile revenue for 4Q23 was up a surprising 8% YoY. Further, Mynt contributed P2.37bn (US$42m) of profit to Globe last year. The company also saw a 30% drop in full-year capex; and is able to maintain its dividend yield of 6% .

Monde (+5.6% WoW) was actively traded as well. Regis consumer analyst upgraded the stock to a BUY on an adjusted TP of Php12.00/sh. At the current share price, the implied valuation for its market-leading APAC BFB business is a ~15% discount to regional peers; and outlook for this segment’s profit is positive given market share recovery and easing input costs. Though the outlook for Quorn continues to be hazy, the downside risk should be limited by the backstop extended by MONDE’s principal shareholders.

Outside earnings, property services firm Colliers says office vacancy in Metro Manila inched up slightly to 19.3% as of end-2023 due to the addition of new office supply. Colliers also noted that there was net positive office take-up from POGOs. Demand from POGOs could be a major factor in the direction of office vacancies in the near term. Large cap property names Ayala Land (+4.2% WoW) and SM Prime (+3.1% WoW) saw a decent boost.

On the power space, Meralco’s (+1.1% WoW) preliminary indications show that Jan 2024 unit sales are going to be ~10% higher YoY. While the YoY comparison looks better because of soft base in Jan 2023, this latest figure is still a positive indicator for demand.

Despite the rally, URC (+0.4% WoW) continued to underperform. URC saw the most outflows for US$7.1m this week.

Daily turnover improved to US$103.9m.

Monde Nissin - Back on track; upgrading to Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Upgrading to Buy

We are upgrading Monde Nissin (MONDE) to a Buy (from Hold) on an adjusted TP of P12/sh (-8%). We believe MONDE’s APAC BFB is back on the growth track following market share recovery and easing input costs. While the outlook for Quorn continues to be hazy, we believe the downside risk is limited by the backstop extended by MONDE’s principal shareholders. The stock has underperformed the PCOMP by ~20% over the past 12 months, and we believe the risk-reward at current levels is now attractive. The current MONDE share price implies the value of its market-leading APAC BFB business is already at a discount (~15%) to its peers, despite similar earnings growth and without assigning any value for Quorn. Our revised 2024-25E net profit forecasts are 6-10% lower vs previous estimates, but the resulting numbers imply faster 2023-25E EPS CAGR (+13%). Key downside risks include a protracted recovery in consumer sentiment and further operational losses at Quorn.

APAC BFB: Back on track; resilient despite inflation

After a challenging 2023, we believe the APAC BFB business is back on track as market shares for instant noodles has recovered. Despite inflationary pressures to consumers, volumes continued to grow, even surpassing pandemic era volumes, which were boosted by pantry-stocking. While were expecting top line growth for the segment to normalize (+6%) in 2024, we believe margins have bottomed in 2023 and operating profit growth over the next few quarters could be more meaningful (+11%) given more favorable inventory costs for wheat and palm oil.

Quorn: Challenges continue, backstop limits downside risk

Challenges at Quorn are likely to continue in the near- to medium-term given unfavorable industry dynamics. While we have little confidence on when market conditions could improve, we believe the backstop extended by MONDE’s principal shareholders is an important step to reduce the downside risk for minority shareholders arising from Quorn.

Adjusting estimates and TP; upgrading to Buy

Given our more positive outlook on MONDE, we are upgrading our rating to Buy from Hold. We adjust our 2024-25E EPS estimates by -6-10% our TP to P12/sh (- 8%) to account for preliminary FY23 results. The APAC BFB business accounts for >90% of our SOTP valuation and is valued at 20x 2024E PE, broadly in line with peers. Meanwhile, Quorn is valued using the present value of the FY32 valuation guaranteed by its principal shareholders. Key risks relate to inflation, competition, and further operational losses at Quorn.

 
Globe Telecom - 4Q23: Unmistakable signs of “market repair”
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income below estimates but mobile revenue rebound evident

Globe reported FY23 core income, excluding one-offs, of P19.1bn for FY23, just a touch lower (-1%) YoY. For 4Q alone, core income was P4.29bn, down 12% sequentially. The bottom line figure is below our expectations, but appears within Street estimates. Despite the apparent shortfall, however, we view the 4Q results positively, as mobile revenue is showing that “market repair” is happening.

4Q mobile revenue jumps 8% YoY

Improvement in the mobile market is evident. Globe’s 4Q mobile revenue was a surprising 8% YoY. We view this as an unmistakable sign that the competitive situation is improving and that the operators have managed to push through revenue-enhancing tweaks (“price increases”) to their service packages. Our derived mobile data yield (revenue/GB) was flat QoQ, implying Globe was able to turn data usage growth to revenue growth.

Broadband net adds disappoint

The broadband business was a mild disappointment, with revenue not seeing a turn yet. Net adds for its wired broadband segment amounted to only 5k in 4Q23, well below the 23k in 3Q23. We had expected more adds following the launch of prepaid fiber mid-2023. Nevertheless, management sounded quite optimistic about the outlook for its prepaid fiber service during the earnings call.

Capex falling, profits from Mynt continue to rise

Capex for FY23 came in P71bn, down 30% YoY. Management is guiding for capex to fall further to P55bn this year and below that next year. This would put it on track to become free cash flow positive in FY25 and improve its leverage ratios. Meanwhile, contributions from its fintech arm Mynt continue to show positive QoQ trajectory. 4Q earnings rose another ~16% QoQ, taking FY23 contributions to P2.37bn, which is double the prior year and >10% of Globe’s core income.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

BPI FY23 NPAT P51.7bn in line; loan growth speeds up to 10.5%

BPI in a press release, reported headline FY23 NPAT of P51.7bn, up 31% YoY, bang in line with Regis estimates of P51.5bn, and slightly ahead of consensus. Loan growth accelerated to 10.5% (in line with Regis 10.3%), up from the 8.8% pace in 3Q23. Opex was slightly elevated in 4Q, but this was offset by higher trading gains, and lower provisions than Regis expected. Overall a pretty solid set of results.

 

Some highlights from the press release:

Loan growth sped up to 10.5% YoY, from 8.8% in 3Q23, with expansion seen across the board. Regis suspects the acceleration was led by late big-ticket corporate loans, while consumer stayed robust. Deposits grew 9.5%, driven by TDs, which took the bank’s CASA ratio down to 67%, from 69.3% in 3Q, and from 74% at end-2022. Full-year NIMs were up 50bps YoY, by the company’s estimates, in line with Regis forecast (51bps). Fee incomes were down 3% YoY on a headline basis. However, recall that FY22 included a P5bn sale of a property asset, without which fees would have been up 16.6% YoY.

 

Trading/fx gains also jumped strongly to P5.2bn. Opex was up 19% YoY for FY23, higher than Regis forecast. The 4Q23 was particularly surprising, up 19% QoQ. Regis suspects some of the jump was due to performance and/or milestone payments for the bank’s tech contractors. Provision expense was P4bn, down 40% YoY. NPL ratio improved to 1.84% (from 1.97% in 3Q) while NPL cover dipped to 154% (from 159% in 3Q). The bank has been guiding that they intended to bring down NPL coverage, as they (and their auditors) believed they were over-provided.

 

ICTSI reaches settlement with labor group in Portland, Oregon

ICTSI and International Longshore Warehouse Union (ILWU) reached a settlement on all of ICTSI’s claims against the latter. Pursuant to the settlement, ICTSI will receive a payment of US$20.5mn. The settlement was agreed on during ILWU’s chapter 11 bankruptcy case, which will be voluntarily dismissed as part of the terms of the settlement. Recall that ICTSI was forced to pre-terminate its contract in Portland due to the work stoppage of ILWU in 2017, even though it still had 20 years left on its contract. ICT paid US$11.5mn as a pre-termination fee to the Port of Portland in 2017. ICTSI will book a gain from this settlement. ICT trades at 8.1x FY24E EV/EBITDA and 12.7x PER, below peers (11x/15x) and historical valuations (11x/23x)

Meralco sees electricity sales +10% in January

According to preliminary data, Meralco says it recorded over 4,000GWh sales in Jan, around 10% higher vs Jan 2023. The growth comes off a relatively soft base in Jan 2023 but the company also noted that all segments are “performing better”. Meralco also noted that the country experienced warmer temperatures in Dec up until the 3rd week of January, which likely contributed to the growth. For 2024, the company is expecting +4.5% volume growth.

PCC clears FGEN acquisition of Casecnan Power Plant

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) cleared the acquisition of the Casecnan Hydroelectric Power Plant by FGEN, stating that it will not affect market competition. The 165MW plant is located in Nueva Ecija and is a run-of river hydroelectric power plant which generates electricity by diverting water from the Casecnan and Taan rivers through a 26km tunnel. The facility is expected to augment supply from the Pantabangan-Masiway hydro plant, which is running at reduced capacity due to lower water levels. Casecnan is also a source of irrigation for farmers in Nueva Ecija. First Gen, through its wholly owned subsidiary, Fresh River Lakes Corp, won the auction in May 2023 with a bid of US$526mn. The other bids ranged from US$258mn to US$298.9mn.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Headline inflation slows to 2.8% in January

Headline CPI grew 2.8% YoY in January, slowing from 3.9% in December. This came in better than consensus forecast of 3.1% and was at the lower-end of the BSP’s target range of 2.8%-3.6% for the month due to moderation in food items to 3.4% from 4.4% the previous month. Vegetable and meat prices continued to decline while inflation for fish slowed. These offset the sustained increases in rice prices (+22.6% from +19.6% in Dec). Lower commodity prices still aiding in the decline of transport and electricity inflation.

 

On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.6% MoM, slower than past seasonal trends (+0.9%). Core inflation moderated for the 10th consecutive month to 3.8%, with the core non-food category also declining further to 3.6%. The latest inflation print is consistent with the BSP’s outlook that inflation will likely moderate in Q1 2024 due largely to negative base effects and the easing of supply constraints affecting key commodities. Regis is forecasting inflation will moderate to 4.6% this year from 6.0% in 2023 (consensus FY24E: 3.7%). The first Monetary Board meeting for 2024 is scheduled on Feb 15.

GIR settles at US$103.4bn in January

Preliminary data showed the GIR level settled at US$103.4bn as of end-Jan 2024. This is lower by US$347mn (-0.3%) MoM from end-Dec 2023 level of US$103.8bn. The  gov’t cited debt payments and decrease in price of gold. The GIR is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to end at US$102bn this year.

Colliers: Metro Manila office vacancy inches up in 4Q23

During its 4Q23 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila office vacancy inched up to 19.3%, from 18.7% in 3Q23 due to an increase in office supply. Rental rates in Makati, Fort Bonifacio, and Ortigas Cener inched up in 4Q23 (+0.5% QoQ) . Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Although POGO office demand in the Manila Bay area picked-up, rental rates in the area were flat. Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise slightly to 19.6% by end-2024 due largely to new supply. It also expects office rental rates to rise 2.5% in 2024. Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 1% QoQ. Colliers estimates that they have risen 4% YoY. This is the ninth consecutive quarter that prices have inched up. Prices are generally 5%-20% below 2019 levels.

Customs collections rise 4% YoY to P73bn in January

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) said it collected P73bn in January, up 3.9% YoY. For 2024, the BOC is aiming to collect P959bn, up 8% YoY (vs. previously reported full-year 2023 figure of P884bn). BOC collections typically account for c. 23% of the national government’s revenues.
