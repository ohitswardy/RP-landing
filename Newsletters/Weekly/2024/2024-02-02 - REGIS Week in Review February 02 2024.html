REGIS REPORT

02 February 2024

The market ended slightly higher at 6,707 (+0.3% WOW) as the strong GDP print for 4Q23 lifted the index. The Philippine economy grew 5.6% in both 4Q23 and for full year 2023. Although this was a slowdown from the 3Q23 growth (+6.0%, revised), this is better than Street expectations (5.2%).

Trading was mixed however, as gainers and losers almost equal at 7:8.

ICT (+5.1% WoW) lifted the index. After a re-bid, ICTSI was awarded the concession for a port in the province of Iloilo in the western Visayas. This new, 25-year concession would add to its nine concessions in the Philippines and over 30 globally.  On a full year basis, Regis estimates the Iloilo port would add 1% to group volumes and revenues.

Banking names remained resilient on strong foreign demand. BDO (+2.6% WoW) and BPI (+2.1% WoW) saw USS$11.2m and US$4.3m respectively.

Consumer names ended mixed. MONDE (+11.7% WoW) rallied sharply. However, JFC (-5.4% WoW) corrected following its recent outperformance.

Meanwhile, Ayala Land (-1.5% WoW) disclosed that it sold 181m shares of AREIT (AREIT PM), equivalent to 7.6% of the latter’s outstanding shares. The sale appeared to be part of ALI’s efforts to maintain a 33.3% free float for AREIT (i.e., for the latter to continue enjoying its tax perks as a REIT). The planned injection of new assets by ALI into AREIT in exchange for AREIT shares would bring its float to below 1/3rd.

For conglos, the Philippine Competition Commission approved the acquisition by Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-2.3% WoW) and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners of 100% of the local Coca-Cola bottling operation. The reported valuation for the transaction was US$1.8bn.

On the telco space, TEL (-0.6% WoW) announced it was acquiring 35% of the broadband service provider Radius Telecom, a subsidiary of Meralco (MER PM). Radius has around 48,000 subscribers, but its fiber network and the potential for it to tap more enterprise customers is likely the key attraction. The deal would strengthen PLDT’s position in the consumer broadband and enterprise businesses. PLDT is adding to its lead in broadband by acquisition, with the just-approved purchase of Sky Cable expected to add over 300k broadband subscribers.

Last Friday, there was also a PSEi rebalancing. There were no additions or deletions to the components, only slight changes in weightings. BLOOM and AEV saw upweights while AEV and AGI saw downweights.

Average daily turnover for the week was slightly lower at US$91.5m (-22.6% WoW) following last week’s rally but headline inflows remained firm at US$11.5m.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Prime in no rush to launch REIT IPO

Hans Sy, SM Prime’s Executive Committee Chair, said that while he would not rule out a REIT IPO this year, the company is not in a rush. SM Prime plans to conduct a US$1bn mall REIT IPO. This implies a US$3bn market cap.

 

ICTSI awarded 25-year contract for Iloilo port

The Philippine Ports Authority awarded ICTSI (ICT PM) the 25-year cargo-handling contract for the Iloilo Commercial Port Complex (ICPC). ICPC will be renamed “Visayas Container Terminal” (VCT). ICTSI won the contract after it was rebidded last month due to lack of documents showing proof of authorization. VCT will be ICTSI’s first foray in the Visayas region. The port will handle domestic and foreign containers in the first 6 years of operations and will eventually handle foreign containers solely after. ICTSI will pay the PPA 20% of revenues on the first five years of operations but this will revert to a fixed P750mn concession fee payment from the 6th-10th year. Regis estimates the port could add c. 1% to consolidated volumes and revenues.

Manila Water to revive contract with Provincial Government of Pangasinan

In a press release, Manila Water said that it reached out to the Provincial Government of Pangasinan (PGP) to explore the revival of the botched concession agreement. Recall that MWC and PGP mutually terminated its 25-year contract for a bulk water project due to “non-fulfillment of conditions” last December. Under the canceled contract signed in Jan 2022, the project had an estimated capital expenditure program of P8bn to deliver 200MLD to 1.4mn Pangasinan residents by the 25th year.

Aboitiz group and partner receive approval for Coca-Cola PHL joint acquisition

Aboitiz Equity Ventures and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners receive go signal from PCC to acquire Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines, Inc. for US$1.8bn. Through CCEP Aboitiz Beverages Philippines, Inc., incorporated in December, Aboitiz will have a 40% beneficial ownership while CCEP will hold a 60% stake in Coca-Cola. AEV and CCEP will acquire 2.45mln shares of class A common stake with a par value of P2 and 1k shares of class B common stock with a par value of P1. All parties expect to close the transaction in late February 2024.

PLDT buys 35% of Radius Telecoms

PLDT disclosed it is subscribing to 34.9% of Radius Telecoms. Radius is a broadband service provider with a congressional franchise and is presently a 100% subsidiary of Meralco. The purchase price for the 35% interest was P2.116bn. This implies post-money valuation of P6.06bn and pre-money valuation of P3.95bn. Radius has a subscriber base of 48k.

MGreen signs EPC contracts with Chinese firm for 2 solar projects

MGEN Renewable Energy, Inc. (MGreen) has awarded engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contracts to China-based SUMEC Complete Equipment and Engineering Co. Ltd. SUMEC will construct its 49 megawatt-alternating current (MWac) solar plant in Isabela and 18.75MWac solar plant in Nueva Ecija. Plants targeted for commercial operation in the last quarter of 2024.

PSE approves Citicore Renewable Energy P12.9bn IPO

The PSE approved the initial listing of Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. (CREC) on its main board on March 22. The offer period will be from March 11-15. The company is selling up to 2.9bn common shares at up to P3.88/sh, with an over-allotment option of up to 435mn secondary shares. The estimated public floats with and without over-allotment are 33.2% and 22.9%, respectively. Net proceeds amounting to P10.76bn will be allotted for capex pipeline of 1.4GW of solar projects in the medium term. The company currently has 10 operating solar assets with a combined gross installed capacity of 285MW. It has a development pipeline of 6,446 MW in solar power generation and 812.5MW in onshore wind power.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

4Q23 and FY23 GDP up 5.6%

4Q23 GDP settled at +5.6% YoY, slowing down from +6.0% in 3Q23 and +7.1% in 4Q22. This was mildly better than consensus forecast of +5.2% for the quarter. Majority of the heavy lifting was done by private consumption and a strong rebound in capital formation. Private Consumption grew slightly to +5.3% in 4Q from +5.1% in 3Q. This was propelled by sustained strong growth in discretionary spending: restaurants/hotels (+16% in 4Q), transport (+12%), and recreation (+7%). However, food spending remained soft for the fourth straight quarter, up by +0.5% YoY (below historical trend: +5.1%). Capital formation rose sharply to +11.2%, after being lethargic for the past two quarters due to the (a) turnaround in durable equipment, which expanded 14.6% YoY in the quarter, as well as (b) inventory re-stocking.

 

Meanwhile, government Consumption, however, contracted to -1.8% from +6.7% in 3Q. Domestic demand was strong, accelerating to 6.1% in 4Q after posting below trend growth rates in 2Q/3Q. Net imports dragged overall GDP growth by 1.2ppts, largely due to a 12% YoY drop in goods exports. On the supply side, growth accelerated for the services and agriculture sectors, which more than compensated for the slowdown in the industrial sector. Industrial sector growth moderated to +3.2% from +5.6% in 3Q, as the manufacturing sector barely grew during the quarter. FY23 GDP growth averaged +5.6% YoY, falling below the government-set target of 6-7% but was broadly in line with consensus estimates (5.5%). For 2024, we expect GDP growth to accelerate to 6.0% (Street: 5.7%).

Government debt reaches P14.62tn in end-2023, equivalent to 60.2% of GDP

The national government’s debt reached P14.62tn in end-Dec, up by P108bn (+0.74%) MoM and by P1.2tn (+8.9%) from end-2022 levels. The Debt-to-GDP ratio mildly improved to 60.2% from 60.9% in end-2022. Domestic debt dropped by P6.5bn (-0.06%) MoM to P10.0tn. Meanwhile, external debt rose by P114bn (+2.54%) MoM to P4.6tn. By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028.

Government revises 3Q23 GDP to 6%

The Philippine Statistics Authority revised the real GDP growth rate for 3Q23 to 6.0% from 5.9%. This brings 9M23 GDP growth to 5.6%. The gov’t cited upward revision in private consumption (5.1% from 5%). On supply side, upward revisions came from manufacturing (+5.1% from 4.5%), financial and insurance (9.6% from 9.5%), and accommodation and food service activities (21% from 20%).

Philippines secures 5-year rice supply deal with Vietnam

During President Marcos' two-day visit to Vietnam, among the deals signed was a memorandum of understanding on Rice Trade Cooperation. The agreement entails a steady supply of 1.5 to 2mn metric tons of white rice at "a competitive and affordable price" from Vietnam's private sector to the Philippine private sector. The country sources 90% of its rice imports from Vietnam, importing 2.4mn tons of rice worth US$1.5bn in the nine months to September 2023.

DOF pushes for limited tax exemption for pickup trucks

In a Senate hearing, Finance Undersecretary Karlo Adriano said that the government wants to remove tax exemptions on pickup trucks, except for single-cab chassis pickup trucks. Other types of vehicles such as the Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, and Nissan Navara will no longer be granted special tax treatment. Proposal is under Tax Package 4 of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Program. The DOF estimates additional government revenues of up to P42.6bn should this push through. Pickup trucks comprised 16% of total industry sales in 2023. Regis estimates price hikes of c. 20% if the tax exemption is removed.

Trade deficit narrows to US$4.0bn in December; FY23 trade deficit down 9% YoY to US$52.4bn

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit fell 11% YoY, 15% MoM to US$4.0bn in December. This is better than the 29.5% YoY expansion in Nov 2023 and due to imports falling 5% YoY; Export growth was mildly negative (-1%). Imports fell 5% YoY to US$9.8bn due to a 27% YoY drop in fuel imports (note that Brent prices in Dec 2023 fell 5% YoY). Raw material imports posted a modest 6% YoY decline. Meanwhile, consumer goods imports rose 11% while capital goods imports were flat. Exports fell 0.5% YoY, after falling double digits in October and November. This brought FY23 trade deficit to US$52.4bn, down 9% YoY
