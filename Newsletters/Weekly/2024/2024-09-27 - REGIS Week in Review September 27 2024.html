REGIS REPORT

27 September 2024

Philippine shares had another upbeat week with the PSEi ending +2.43% WoW to 7428, around its best levels since early 2022. Gains were widespread across different sectors and were driven primarily once again by continued net foreign buying, which has now extended for 20 sessions in a row. Notably, inflows during the week have tipped the YTD net flow of the market to the positive (+$21m) vs bottom of -$534m in late June. If maintained through the end of the year, we could see our first annual net foreign buying in as long as 7 years.

A huge chunk of the inflows this week went into AC (nearly $80m) amid the sizable blocks reflected on Thursday. The company announced that it sold about 3m Treasury shares at a premium of 720/share (4% premium from Wednesday’s close). Note however that the total size of blocks reflected that day stood at ~7.2m shares ($92m). All the buying was tagged as foreign while the selling (outside of the ~3m Treasury shares) was a mix of both local and foreign. AC shares settled the week flattish +0.29% WoW at 692/share.

That said, foreign buying into the overall market did show signs of easing at the tailend of the week, as much of the attention in the region evidently towards China where shares surged by ~15% this week, propelled by a deluge of new stimulus measures by the PBOC.

On the ground, best performing stock in the benchmark this week was MER +9.09% WoW, supported by positive development regarding the electricity distributor’s franchise renewal, which is set to expire in 2028. The House of Representatives approved on second reading the company’s franchise renewal, which now paves the way for a third and final reading which could happen in early November once Congress reconvenes.  MER shares climbed to a new all-time high during the week and are now +14% YTD (while PCOMP +15.17%).

Lenders saw continued strength as well as investors further digested the clearly positive bottomline impact of the prior week’s sizable RRR cut announcement: MBT +4.45% WoW, BPI +3.4% and BDO +0.88%. Gains in the bid 3 lenders however continued to be outpaced by some of the smaller cap names such a PNB +13.67% and once again SECB +7.65% which is now +60.58% from its end-August lows.

Other notable gainers were consumer plays PGOLD +7.91% WoW (new 52-week high) and URC +3.47%, ahead of the latest CPI reading due next week which is expected to show inflation easing to 2.5% in September, coming from 3.3% in August. Note that an inflation print of 2.5% will mark the lowest reading since October 2020 and should help boost consumer demand heading into 4Q.

On the opposite end, one notable laggard was SCC -2.08% WoW. Note that there was a headline during the week that the government plans to unwind its ~3% stake in the company in order to boost revenues. Along with the SCC stake also came a long list of other government owned assets that could be up for privatization (news clip below).

Market activity this week was even heavier at a daily average of $171.5m, +14% WoW. This however was in significant part boosted by the aforementioned $91m block on AC. Excluding that, average daily turnover would be at pace with last week. What’s notable however was that foreign participation picked up to an unusual majority of 53% vs their normal share of 40%-45%.

Net foreign buying stood at a sizable $190m. Outside of AC +0.29% WoW which saw a sizable $79m worth of net buying this week ($66m from Thursday’s blocks), other top foreign bought names include ALI +2.48%, SM +2.68%, MBT +4.45%, URC +3.47%, BPI +3.4%, ICT +1.64%, JFC -0.37%, SMPH +3.57%, SECB +7.65% and GTCAP +5.64%. While top sold were AREIT -1.87%, SMC +1.46%, RLC +1.4% and BLOOM -1.3% but by a much lighter pace relative to the buys.

Up ahead, expecting an active session on Monday due to month- and quarter-end. For the broader week, in focus is the latest CPI reading due early Friday. As mentioned, CPI is expected to show inflation easing to 2.5% in September, which would be the lowest reading since October 2020, and following a 3.3% print in August. This should also help pave the way for further policy easing by the BSP, which is now expected by some to potentially cut twice more before the year ends. The central bank is scheduled to decide on policy in October and December.

ICTSI - Party's not over; Raising TP to P470/sh
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Prospects continue to improve

We raise our FY24E-FY26E profit forecasts by 7-14% and lift our 12-month TP by 16% to P470/sh, after considering (1) the company’s stronger-than-expected 1H results and (2) our improved confidence on its brownfield expansions. We believe the period of outperformance is not over, as valuations remain undemanding at 13.8x FY25E PER and 8.5x EV/EBITDA, below peers (16x/11x) and historical average (21x/11x). Maintain Buy.

Timely, strategic brownfield expansions will drive strong earnings growth

The completion of ICT’s brownfield expansions in Mexico, Melbourne, and Manila will be the key drivers of profitability in the near-to medium-term. ICT’s competitive advantages in these markets, primarily on location and land availability, are not well understood and appreciated, in our view. We believe these advantages will enable gains in market share and yields until 2026, resulting in 24E-26E profit CAGR of at least +17%.

Stronger 2H24 profit outlook; Street forecasts are at least 14% below Regis

After seeing its 1H24 core profit jump by 26%, we estimate ICT’s recurring earnings will rebound by 55% YoY in 2H24, driven by the implementation of 10% tariff hikes in Melbourne and Manila in July and August, as well as the completion of the expansion in Mexico by early 4Q and deleveraging. Our upgraded forecasts are now the highest on the Street and we believe Street upgrades will follow.

Revised TP of P470/sh offers 18% total potential shareholder return

Our profit upgrades are largely a result of uplifts in yield per TEU (+4-5%) and volume (+1-2%) assumptions (see page 2). We also upgraded our SOTP NAV[1]based TP to P470/sh, on the back of profit upgrades, changes to our target capital structure and PHP:USD assumptions. Our revised SOTP NAV-based TP of P470/sh offers 18% total potential return and implies a FY25E EV/EBITDA multiple of 9.5x, below historical average of 11x. Downside risks relate to trade restrictions, macroeconomic/political risks, dilutive acquisitions, and labor issues. Our forecasts and valuation do not include the potential acquisition of DCT-2 in Durban, South Africa due to the absence of sufficient information.

 
Filinvest Land - Board approves tender offer for 7.7% of FLI shares
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

0.32 FILRT shares for every FLI share

Filinvest Land’s Board of Directors approved a tender offer to purchase up to 1.866bn FLI shares, equivalent to 7.7% of shares outstanding (and 24% of float shares). Instead of paying in cash, however, the company will pay with Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) shares, at a ratio 0.32 FILRT shares for every FLI share tendered.

What is the implied tender offer price?

At FILRT’s share price of P3.11/share, the tender offer would value FLI at P1.00/share, 20% higher than the last closing price of P0.83/share. At first glance, this seems like an arbitrage opportunity. However, since only 24% of float shares (1.866bn out of 7.786bn shares) can be tendered, the opportunity is not so straightforward.

What will happen to public float?

Assuming that 1.866bn FLI shares are indeed tendered, the company will pay 597mn FILRT shares, raising FILRT’s public float from 35% to 47%. This could pave the way for another share-for-property asset injection in the future.

Meanwhile, Filinvest Land’s public float will fall from 32% to 24%, potentially reducing FLI’s meager liquidity further.

How will this affect EPS and NAV/share?

Based on our preliminary estimates, assuming full take-up, this transaction could result in a 1% increase in Filinvest Land’s FY25E EPS (to P0.15/share) and a 5% expansion in NAV/share (to P2.26/share).

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Lower House approves Meralco franchise renewal on 2nd reading

The Lower House approved the bill granting Meralco (MER PM) a 25-year renewal of its franchise on 2nd reading. Meralco's franchise is set to expire in 2028.

Meralco and its power suppliers ask ERC for approval of PSA

Meralco, as well as subsidiaries of San Miguel (SMC PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM), are seeking approval of their Power Supply Agreement (PSA) from the Energy Regulatory Commission. San Miguel’s (SMC PM) San Roque Hydropower Inc. (SRHI) offered the lowest total headline rate of P7.10 per kilowatt-hour (kWH) for 340 megawatts (MW) of the total requirement.     Two units of ACEN secured the lowest bid for the remaining 159MW requirement: Gigasol3 submitted a bid price of P8.1819 per kWh for 139 MW of the requirement, and Santa Cruz Solar Energy Inc. (SCSEI) took the remaining 21 MW requirement at a rate of P8.1998 per kWh. Competitive selection process (CSP) involves a 10-year PSA for Meralco’s 350 MW mid-merit requirement starting February 2025 which will increase by 150 MW in February 2026. To date, Meralco has contracted 1,880 MW of RE capacity, surpassing its initial target of 1,500 MW. RE is expected to account for 22% of the company’s supply portfolio over the next 6 years.

SMC Power unit plans US$100mn securities offering for projects

San Miguel Global Power Holdings (SMGP) will issue US$100mn senior perpetual capital securities for its solar and battery energy projects, which will be listed on the Singapore Exchange (SGX). The company issued US$492.11mn senior perpetual capital securities in November 2019, and another US$723.9mn in October 2020. Move was driven by “prevailing market conditions and as may be advantageous to the corporation.” Proceeds will fund solar energy projects and battery energy storage capital expenditures. As of June 2024, SMGP’s total capacity is 5,207 MW, spanning natural gas, coal, hydroelectric, and battery storage. Solar and battery projects are part of SMGP’s push toward renewable energy development.

Filinvest Land’s Board of Directors approved a tender offer for up to 1.866bn FLI shares

This represents 7.69% of FLI’s outstanding shares. FLI shares will be exchanged for FILRT shares at a ratio of 0.32 FILRT shares for every 1 FLI share tendered, as such 597mn FILRT shares will be exchanged for 1.866bn FLI shares – this is equivalent to 12.2% of FILRT shares outstanding. Based on the swap ratio, FLI should be worth P1.00/share (when FILRT is at P3.11/share). After the transaction: FLI’s public float will fall from 32.1% to 24.4%, while FILRT’s public float will rise from 34.5% to 46.7%.

Ayala Land sells AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 75mn AREIT shares at P36.20/share in an overnight placement on September 19. This represents a 4% discount to the previous closing price. At P36.20/share, AREIT offers a dividend yield of 6.4% (based on consensus forecast). After this transaction, we estimate that Ayala Land’s ownership in AREIT fell from 54% to 51%. After the previously announced property-for-share swap is executed, we estimate that Ayala Land’s ownership in AREIT will rise to 64%.

ICTSI’s Melbourne port hits 5mn TEU milestone

Victoria International Container Terminal (VICT) has reached the five million TEU mark after seven years of operations yesterday. This was achieved after VICT completed its capacity expansion last Dec 2023, which raised the port’s capacity by 30% to 1.25mn TEUs. This suggests that VICT’s volumes are trending better than our expectations, given that we only assume the port will hit the 5mn TEU mark by year-end. VICT is a wholly-owned unit of ICTSI (ICT PM); We estimate VICT accounts for 8% of ICT’s revenues. VICT is the only port in Melbourne that can handle super-Panamax vessels. We estimate its market share has risen to 34% today since it began operations in 2017. We anticipate shipping lines will continue to bid up for space at VICT and allow ICT to increase its market share further.

UnionDigital Bank gets additional P1.6bn capital infusion

Union Bank of the Philippines is infusing an additional P1.6bn in capital to support the ongoing business operations and sustain the expansion of UnionDigital Bank Inc. The capital infusion was approved by its board of directors last Sept. 23. The move will be subject to the applicable regulatory approvals. This is the fourth time that the bank injected additional capital to its digital banking arm. In November last year, Unionbank infused P1.8bn into UnionDigital to support its operations. In June 2023, Unionbank also beefed up the capitalization of its digital banking unit through the infusion of P900mn in additional equity. In June 2022, the bank also pumped P2bn in additional capital to the wholly owned subsidiary. Last year, the digital lender saw its revenue grow to over P5bn mainly driven by higher deposits and loans.

Telcos seek reductions in spectrum user fees

Telco industry players are asking for a reduction in cost on the spectrum user fees (SUF) that the government charges on telcos, which amount to P5.39bn a year. Philippine Chamber of Telecommunication Operators (PCTO) Vice President Roy Ibay said additional SUF costs make it harder for telcos to expand their networks. The PCTO hopes that the government can consider reducing the SUF so providers can allocate more capital for infrastructure buildup. The SUF funds the P2.5bn requirement of the Free Internet Access Program, which puts internet sites in public spaces, such as government offices and schools.

Axelum approves P500mn buyback program

The board of directors of Axelum Resources (AXLM PM) approved a P500mn share buyback program. This represents 6% of the current market cap. The buyback program will end on 24 March 2025.

Meralco taps MIESCOR to link Terra Solar Project (TSPI) to Luzon grid

Meralco subsidiary MIESCOR has been contracted to connect the P200bn Terra Solar project to the Luzon grid. The project involves 34.5/230/500-kilovolt (kV) main collector substation, two 34.5/230-kV solar photovoltaic satellite collector substations, and two double-circuit 230-kV transmission lines. This will ensure the delivery of electricity once the project becomes operational.

MIESCOR is the engineering, procurement, construction, and operation subsidiary of Meralco. TSPI is currently developing a 3500MW solar plant and 4000MWh energy storage system estimated to cost P212bn. Expected to generate over 5bn kWh of electricity annually. Phase 1 is to be completed by 2026, whilst phase 2 is to be targeted for 2027.

PSE approves Ayala Corp’s P15bn preferred share offering

The PSE approved Ayala Corp’s offering of P15bn worth of preferred shares. AC will reissue and sell up to 5mn preferred shares at P2,000 per share, with an overallotment option of an additional 2.5bn shares. Pref shares will have an initial dividend rate of 6.0538%, computed based on the 3-day simple average of 5-year BVAL plus 40bps. Offer period is from Oct 1 to Oct 7. Proceeds of the offering will be used by Ayala to redeem what is left of the original Class B preferred shares the company issued that is due to be called by November 29.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP Governor signals two more rate cuts this year

At the sidelines of an ADB event, BSP Governor Remolona Jr. stated that policy rates could be cut by up to 50bps “in principle.” This would bring benchmark rate down to 5.75% by end-2024, in line with REGIS expectations. Governor Remolona said there could be a risk of a hard landing if the BSP decides to cut by 50bps in one meeting. Governor Remolona adds that September inflation is estimated to be lower than August print of 3.3%. The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release September inflation data on October 4.

Lower House approves P6.35tn 2025 budget on third reading

House Bill (HB) No. 10800, which contains the P6.352tn proposed budget for 2025, was approved by the House of Representatives on the third reading. The budget is 10% higher than the approved 2024 budget and is equivalent to ~22% of GDP. It appears that the key change to the proposed budget is only the reduction in the budget of the office of the Vice President, which was cut by over half to P733mn from P2.04bn previously. The budget will now be transmitted to the Senate for its own deliberations. Key items of the budget are: Debt service (i.e. interest payments) will increase by 27% YoY; Primary spending (excluding debt service) will increase by 8% YoY, driven largely by the 17% increase in allocations to LGUs; Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively.

BSP revises BOP forecasts; sees wider current account deficits but larger BOP surpluses in 2024/2025

The BSP is projecting higher BOP surpluses to US$2.3bn in 2024 and US$1.7bn in 2025, higher than its previous projections of US$1.6bn and US$1.5bn, respectively. Officials cited higher-than-expected non-resident inflows or financial account in 1H24, driven by higher foreign portfolio investments, which emanated from higher withdrawals of placements in foreign-issued debt securities. This offset higher current account deficit projections of US$6.8bn in 2024 and US$5.5bn in 2025 due to lower projections in merchandise exports.

Budget deficit narrows to P54bn in August

The government’s budget deficit narrowed by 59% YoY to P54bn in August. The decline is due to 24% YoY jump in revenues, alongside a mild contraction in expenses. Revenues rose 24% YoY, driven by strong non-tax collections. Tax collections grew 10%, with BIR collections rising by 12% and Customs revenues by 5%. Non-tax revenues surged >3x to P70bn, on the back of PSALM’s P10bn settlement of guarantee fee arrears, along with higher PAGCOR income. Expenses declined by 0.7%, despite a 24% YoY increase in interest expenses. Primary spending (net of interest payments) fell 3% YoY. YT-August budget deficit now stands at P697bn, down by 5% YoY. Note that the government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

Government plans to unload stake in Semirara

The government is planning to unload its 145.6mn shares (3%) in Semirara Mining and Power Corp. (SCC PM) in a bid to boost its non-tax revenues through privatization, according to Finance Undersecretary and Chief Economist Domini Velasquez at the sidelines of an event. At current share price, the government’s stake sale could allow it to raise P4.9bn. The Department of Finance has a long list of assets eyed for privatization over the course of the Marcos administration, with P100bn privatization proceeds targeted for 2025 alone. These include: Food Terminal Inc., Financial Center Area, Ecology Village, Mile Long Complex National Housing Authority-Caloocan Property, FIl-Eastern Woods Industries Inc., Pioneer Glass Manufacturing Corp., Mindanao Progress Corp.

Government reportedly defers bidding for 797MW hydro plant

The government has reportedly postponed the privatization of the 797 MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan hydroelectric power plant complex indefinitely, according to the Philippine Star. Officials cited the plant’s ineligibility to take advantage of the third round of green energy auction (GEA-3), given that it has no expansion plans. Ineligibility to participate in GEA-3 will effectively lower the value of the asset. The government earlier expected to raise P50-100bn in proceeds from the privatization. There were reportedly nine pre-qualified bidders for the hydro plant: Semirara Mining & Power Corp. (SCC PM), Japan’s Marubeni, First Gen Corp. (FGEN PM), Prime Energy Corp., ACEN Corp’s (ACEN PM) Giga Ace 11, Thunder consortium, and Meralco’s (MER PM) PowerGen Corp. reportedly backed out of the bidding due to the same issue.

Senate ratifies PHL-South Korea FTA

The Senate ratified the free trade agreement (FTA) between the Philippines and South Korea. FTA will remove Philippines tariffs on 96.5% of goods from South Korea, while Seoul will lift tariffs on around 94.8% of Philippines products. FTA is still undergoing ratification process at the Korean National Assembly. One of the provisions of the FTA is the removal of 5% import duties on automobiles from South Korea while tariffs of EVs and hybrids will also be eliminated for five years. 30% tariffs of Philippine bananas will be removed within five years, while 36% tariffs of processed pineapples will be removed within seven years.

Korean vehicles (Hyundai and Kia) accounted for 3.4% of vehicle sales in 2023. Ayala Corp. (AC PM) is the main distributor of Kia in the country. The Philippines is also seen to increase banana and processed pineapple exports to Seoul. Philippines ran a trade deficit of US$4.96bn with South Korea in 2023.

Senate approves VAT Refund bill for foreign tourists, Lower House set to adopt Senate version

The Senate passed the VAT refund bill for foreign tourists on its third reading, with 19 senators voting in favor. Non-resident visitors can claim refunds for VAT on goods purchased worth at least P3,000 from accredited retailers. The Department of Finance expects the measure to boost tourism revenues by P10bn annually. The Department of Tourism estimates visitor receipt have reached P282bn in 1H24, up 33% YoY. In 2019, foreign visitor receipts reached P600bn, equivalent to 3.1% of GDP. The VAT refund system is projected to be fully implemented by 2025, contributing to the government’s target of 12mn foreign tourist arrivals by 2028.

DOJ Undersecretary Jesse Andres named OIC chairman and CEO of ERC

Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin appointed Department of Justice Undersecretary Jesse Hermogenes T. Andres as OIC Chairman and CEO of the ERC. The position will be in effect until a replacement is designated or until otherwise directed. This comes after the 6-month suspension of previous ERC Chairperson Monalisa Dimalanta last Sep 9. This move reportedly breaks from tradition, as typically the most senior commissioner of the ERC would be named OIC. Mr. Andres was previously the chief of staff of former Vice President Noli de Castro and was the previous head of the litigation department at Ponce Enrile Cayetano Bautista Reyes & Manalastas Law Offices (PECABAR) before establishing his own law firm.
