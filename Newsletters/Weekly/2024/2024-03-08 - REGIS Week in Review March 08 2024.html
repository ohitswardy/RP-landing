REGIS REPORT

08 March 2024

The PSEi consolidated this week on lackluster volumes which reached only US$92m (daily ave). The market inched up a mere 0.33% to close at 6942.21 index points.  

February CPI of 3.4% also came in worse than consensus expectations (3.0%), serving  as backdrop to sentiment. Rice prices continued to creep higher, even as transport costs rebounded. The latest inflation print muted hopes of earlier rate cuts by the BSP.

Despite managing to close higher (by 23 index points), the index saw losers outnumbering gainers 18 to1.  The top drags to the index this week were BPI (-3.2% WoW) and Ayala Land (-4.0% WoW), pushing the market lower by 18-index points apiece.  Consumer names were also weak, with large caps URC (-5.6% WoW), Jollibee (-2.8% WoW) and Wilcon (-8.4% WoW) also weighing on the index.

Meralco (-3.9% WoW) also declined. Along with Aboitiz Power (+2.3% WoW) and San Miguel (-1.0% WoW), the company will jointly own ~2,500MW of gas-fired plants as well as the LNG terminal that will supply fuel to them. The deal is valued US$3.3bn. The deal is aligned with Meralco’s goal of further expansion into power generation ang gives AP future access to the LNG terminal, which is likely to be the source of fuel for the LNG-fired plants it plans to build. Meanwhile it provides SMC greater security of offtake for its power plants.

ICTSI (+9.4% WoW) meanwhile was the top index gainer, after recurring profits for FY23 outperformed Street estimates.  Profits were supported by organic volume growth of 4% on top of sustained yield improvements.

SM (+4.5% MoM) surged on strong foreign inflow, while banks Metrobank (+5.65% WoW) and BDO (+4.95%) also closed higher. PLDT (+1.1% WoW) also gained. PLDT’s mobile revenue growth was at 3%, with decent sequential QoQ growth of +4%.

Similar to many developers, Filinvest Land (unch) saw soft presales.  Its presales fell for the 2nd consecutive quarter and unsold inventory stood at 28 months’ worth of presales.  Meantime, half of Megaworld’s (-0.5% WoW) P148bn in unsold inventory is now comprised of ready-for-occupancy units (vs. just 20% for most other developers).The company will cut launches by nearly 40% in order to manage inventory levels.

ICTSI - One-off masks 4Q23 operational improvements
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY23 recurring profit up 8% to US$648mn – Above Street forecasts

ICTSI’s FY23 headline profit before perps fell 17% YoY to US$512mn following the full impairment of the remaining US$155mn carrying value of the receivable from the Sudanese government in 4Q23 and another US$10mn impairment charge for the terminated contract in Pakistan in 2Q. Stripping out one-offs and removing perps distributions, recurring profit rose 8% YoY to US$648mn or US$0.318/sh, well above consensus forecast (US$0.298/sh) and a tad better than our estimate (US$0.312/sh). Implied 4Q23 profit grew 8% YoY to US$174mn, an improvement from 3Q23 (flattish YoY).

Derived 4Q23 organic volume growth at 4%; yields up 2%

Consolidated volumes dropped 2% YoY in 4Q23 largely due to the terminated contracts in Pakistan and Indonesia. We estimate organic volumes, excluding the impact of terminated concessions, grew 4% YoY in 4Q23, an improvement from the +1% YoY growth rate in 3Q23/9M23. 4Q23 volumes from the Americas and EMEA regions respectively rose 10% and 16%, more than compensating for the 3% drop in Asia. Derived organic yield growth of 2% YoY in 4Q was broadly similar to 3Q. Despite tariff hikes, we estimate 4Q23 yields in Asia and EMEA fell 5% and 2%, respectively, but these were offset by the 13% yield increase in the Americas.

EBITDA rises 7% YoY in 4Q23/FY23; EBITDA margin at 63.5%

4Q23 EBITDA rose 7% YoY to US$399mn, taking FY23 EBITDA up 7% to US$1.51bn, above consensus estimates (US$1.43bn) and ours (US$1.48bn). 4Q23 EBITDA margin improved 2ppts YoY to 63.5%, partly due to the removal of the lower[1]margin port in Pakistan. Sequentially, margins were unchanged as equipment (+10%) and admin costs (+13%) grew faster than revenue growth of +6%.

Encouraging 2024 start; Possible delay in takeover of Durban port

Management said they are “happy” with how volumes and revenues have trended so far in 2024, particularly for VICT (Melbourne), which was most responsible for the Asia weakness in 2023. The implemented and soon-to-be implemented tariff hikes across its portfolio will likely sustain yield improvements. Management however admitted that a 2Q takeover for the Durban port in South Africa remains uncertain, given the elections on May 29.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - 4Q23 volumes fall; Management confident about 2nd casino
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY23 earnings disappoint on interest and retirement-related expenses

Bloomberry’s FY23 net profit rose 85% to P9.5bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. GGR of P58bn (+16%) was in line with our forecast but EBITDA was dragged lower by a P670mn retirement-related expense in 4Q23. Adding this back, EBITDA would have risen 40% to P20.0bn, in line with our forecast. This report marks the transfer of coverage of Bloomberry Resorts.

Total 4Q23 volumes drop QoQ and YoY; offset by higher hold rates

Total 4Q23 volumes dipped 7% QoQ (and YoY) as VIP rolling chip fell 11% QoQ while mass table drop declined 14% QoQ. Management blamed the decline to patrons traveling abroad in 4Q, which we note had several three-day weekends. We do not view the reduction in mass table drop to be particularly alarming since the 4Q figure was just 7% below the 2019 average. However, the VIP rolling chip in 4Qwas noticeablyweaker (32% below the 2019 level). Management did not sound optimistic about the near-term prospects for foreign VIP.

Management expects 2nd casino to break even in one quarter

The company will open its 2nd casino, Solaire Resorts North (SRN), in Quezon City in late May 2024. The site will expand BLOOM’s table capacity by 38% (slot capacity by 112%). Remarkably, management expects SRN to reach EBITDA breakeven in one quarter, faster than our own forecast of six months.

The original Solaire (in Entertainment City) only took one quarter to reach EBITDA breakeven and management believes it should be even easier for SRN because: 1) Quezon City is a densely populated area, 2) as the only casino in Quezon City, SRN can capture clients of other resorts in Entertainment City or in Clark (who reside closer to SRN), 3) Solaire has a stronger brand now than when the original site was completed (in 2013).

 
Megaworld - FY23 earnings ahead but presales fall for third consecutive quarter
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Revenue of every segment outperforms

Megaworld’s FY23 net profit rose 29% to P17.3bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, as revenue from every segment outperformed our expectations. However, some cracks in residential demand seem to be appearing as presales have now fallen for three consecutive quarters.

Provincial sales drop in 4Q23

4Q23 presales fell 12% QoQ (11% YoY) to P29.5bn, due to a sharp drop in provincial sales. The share of reservation sales from provincial areas fell from 58% in 9M23 to just 45% in FY23.

Management is cautious on residential; some projects are struggling

Management seems cautious about the residential segment and is seeking to trim its unsold inventory. While its FY24 presales guidance of P145bn would mean a substantial rebound from 4Q23, it only represents 4% growth from FY23.

Furthermore, the company plans to cut FY24 launches by 38% to P40bn in order to manage its unsold inventory level. Half of Megaworld’s P148bn in unsold inventory is comprised of ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units, higher than the c. 20% of most other developers.

Even Fort Bonifacio projects are not immune from the weakness. Uptown Modern, priced at P430k/sqm, was launched in 3Q23 but is only 16% sold. This is despite the longer payment period of 7 years (vs the typical 5 years).

Office vacancy inches up Despite the completion of a fully preleased

Fort Bonifacio building in late December, office vacancy rose 1ppt QoQ to 12% due to closures of POGO and BPO tenants. About 60% of Megaworld’s office portfolio is in Fort Bonifacio, where vacancies are only c. 10%, lower than most parts of Metro Manila (average vacancy of 17%).

 
PLDT - 4Q23: Mobile market improving
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income up 7%; more one-offs in 4Q

PLDT reported income of P26.7bn for FY23, +154% YoY. Core income, inclusive of losses at Maya, amounted to P32.2bn, +7% YoY and in line with our estimates. We note that “one-offs” continue to mar reported profit (while also reducing amortization expenses in future periods). We are, however, encouraged by visible improvements in its mobile business which portend a better FY24.

Visible improvement in mobile revenue; reduction in wired broadband subs

While the YoY growth in mobile revenue was only 3% (vs Globe’s +8%), sequential QoQ growth was more than respectable at +4%. This, in fact, implies a market share gain (albeit modest) for PLDT in 4Q. We believe the pricing environment is improving for the telcos. On our numbers, mobile data yields rose 2% QoQ in 4Q.

On the broadband side, PLDT lost 11k wired subscribers, and 4Q revenue was practically unchanged QoQ. It revealed that trials for a prepaid fiber service are underway.

Another chunky write-off

In 4Q, PLDT wrote off another P13.9bn of capitalized assets related to the provision of services (CPE and installation costs) to subscribers that have already been disconnected. While this is a non-cash charge, the frequency of such items does not inspire confidence in its financial accounts.

Maya funding round imminent

Maya quietly raised another US$80m in Dec, enough to sustain operations until it reaches target breakeven by 4Q this year. We understand the infusion resulted in a small increase in PLDT’s ownership of Maya (now 36.9%).

Capex dropping further

Capex for FY23 was P85bn (42% capex/revenue), down from P97bn in FY22. PLDT is guiding for it to fall further to P75-78bn this year. The company’s focus will be to achieve positive free cash flow and reduce leverage in the near term.

 
DMCI Holdings - FY23 profit misses; Acquisition plans at early stage
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q23 core profit rebounds but still misses FY23 forecast

DMCI’s recurring profit (ex-FX adjustments) rebounded by 25% QoQ and 21% YoY to P4.78bn in 4Q23. However, this only brought FY23 profit to P24.7bn, down 19% YoY. The results disappointed due to shortfalls at Semirara (see SCC PM, 4Q23 misses on higher mining costs, lower power ASPs), construction, and nickel mining units, which more than outweighed the beats from property, water, and off-grid power units.

Management reckons its acquisition plans are still at the very early stages, implying that it can still pay at least a P0.72/sh dividend (6% yield) next month.

Property: FY23 reservations near 2019 levels but cancellations remain elevated

4Q23 reservation sales were up 14% YoY to P7.5bn due to the take-up of its newly launched affordable housing project, Anissa Heights, and leisure-oriented project, Solmera Coast. FY23 reservation sales of P35.9bn (+12%) almost matched 2019 levels (P37bn) and was well above forecast. However, management was cautious about its outlook for this year and guided for flat sales. Cancellation rates broadly stayed at ~13% through 2023, but still above normal levels (single digits).

Construction order book improves; Awaiting bid results of big-ticket projects

Project delays affected the construction unit’s FY23 performance, as revenues and profit contribution dropped 15% and 2% YoY, respectively. The order book mildly improved QoQ to P42bn in end-4Q and management hopes to secure more packages of the Subway project this year. However, given persistent right-of[1]way issues, management is still guiding for lower revenue bookings this year.

Water: Higher effective tariffs boost FY23; El Niño risks to be mitigated

The water unit’s 4Q23 profit contributions rose 15% YoY to P412mn, taking FY23 contributions up 37% to P2.0bn, outperforming our forecast due to better-than[1]expected effective tariffs. Capex rose 29% YoY to P19.7bn, in line with our forecast (P20bn). Management believes they are well-prepared for El Niño, after augmenting supply from its newly built water treatment plants.

Nickel FY23 profit misses but exciting near-to medium-term outlook

The nickel mining unit’s profit contribution fell c55% YoY in 4Q due to lower ASPs and a surge in costs. Management is targeting sales volumes to rise to 2-2.25mn MT in 2024 and 3.25-3.5mn MT in 2025 from 1.6mn MT in 2023, depending on whether it can secure mining permits for its asset in Palawan, where it has the bulk of its resources.

Off-grid power: New plant lifts 4Q23 profit up 69% YoY

The off-grid power unit’s profit rose 69% YoY in 4Q23 due to the contributions from its new plants in Masbate and Palawan. Management plans to add another 40MW in the next 12 months or so, which should boost profits further.

Meralco, Aboitiz Power and San Miguel sign deal for US$3.3bn gas complex

Meralco (through subsidiary MGen), Aboitiz Power and San Miguel (through San Miguel Global Power, SMGP) sign deal for the three to jointly control 2,500MW power generation capacity and an LNG terminal. The deal was valued at US$3.3bn (likely enterprise value). Under the deal, MGen and AboitizPower will jointly invest in two of SMGP’s gas-fired power plants: the 1,278-MW Ilijan power plant and a new 1,320-MW combined cycle power facility. The Ilijan power plant resumed its operations last year, while the new power facility under Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. is scheduled start operations by the end of 2024.

 

Together with SMGP, the companies will invest in “almost 100%” of the LNG import and regasification terminal owned by Linseed Field Corp., a local unit of Atlantic, Gulf, and Pacific Co. (AG&P). This will be used to receive, store and process LNG fuel for the two power plants, thus fully integrating the local energy sector into the global natural gas supply chain. The ownership breakdown between the three parties over the various asset/s was not disclosed. The two power plants combined will be larger than First Gen’s aggregation of gas-fired plants (~2,000MW). The deal will allow this group to exercise tremendous market power over the electricity market, principally because of Meralco’s natural monopoly on the last mile wires. Scrutiny from the competition authority can give rise to imposition of conditions.

SPNEC seeks investors for P200bn Terra Solar project

SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC) is looking to bring in partners, particularly foreign investors, to help finance the development of the P200bn according to Manuel Pangilinan. SPNEC, through subsidiary Terra Solar Philippines Inc., is developing the massive solar farm in Nueva Ecija and Bulacan which will consist of 3,500 megawatts (MW) of solar panels and 4,000 megawatt-hours of battery storage. SPNEC is willing to sell as much as 40% stake in Terra Solar. The project is set to be completed in two phases. The first phase will have a capacity of 2500 MW and is slated for completion by 2026 while the second phase will have a capacity of 1,000 MW and is slated to be completed by 2027.

RCBC considers appeal of NY Supreme Court decision

Rizal Commercial Banking Corp (RCB PM) received the Decision and Order from the Supreme Court of the State of New York regarding the US$81mn Bank of Bangladesh cyberheist incident in 2016. The Court dismissed three causes of action against the Bank and all RCBC defendants (conversion, aiding and abetting, and conspiracy to commit conversion). It also dismissed the case against four RCBC defendants for lack of personal jurisdiction. However, the Court ruled that the case can proceed against RCBC and the remaining defendants on the other causes of action. These are: fraud (against RCBC); aiding and abetting and conspiracy to commit fraud; conspiracy to commit trespass against chattels; unjust enrichment; and return of money received. RCBC is considering whether or not to appeal the decision by the Court. As of 3Q23, RCBC’s book value (excluding hybrid perpetual securities) stood at P132bn, representing BVPS of P54.60/share.

Manila Water signs US$110mn 3-year term loan facility for refinancing

In a disclosure to the PSE, Razon-led Manila Water said its wholly owned subsidiary, Manila Water Asia Pacific Pte. Ltd. (MWAP), signed a loan facility deal with Mizuho Bank - Singapore Branch and ING N.V. Singapore. Proceeds of the loan are to be used to refinance existing debt.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP to review 3% interest rate cap on credit card transactions

The BSP will review this month whether to keep or hike the 3%/month interest rate cap on credit card transactions. The BSP reviews the interest rate ceiling on credit card transactions every six months. It raised the cap from 2%/month to 3%/month in January 2023.

Inflation accelerates to 3.4% in February

Headline CPI rose 3.4% YoY in February from 2.8% in January. This was above consensus (3%) and came in at the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 2.8%-3.6%. The uptick was due to faster increases in food CPI (4.6% in Feb from 3.5% in Jan) and fuel-related transport CPI (+1.2% in Feb vs -0.4% in Jan). Rice price inflation persisted, with rice CPI climbing 1.0% MoM and 23.7% YoY in Feb (base effects could start to fade in Sep 2024). According to NEDA, international rice prices have started to ease and local supply is expected to increase with the dry season harvest beginning this month through April.

 

Meat prices climbed 0.7% YoY and 1% MoM in February after being fairly muted in previous months. The base effects resulted in lower vegetable prices (-11% YoY) but this will certainly fade in March. Meanwhile, fuel-related transport inflation rose 1.3% MoM and 1.2% YoY, accelerating from January (-0.4% YoY, flat MoM). Core CPI slowed to 3.6% in February from 3.6% in Jan. However, the “non-food” core items were broadly unchanged at +3.6% YoY for both months. BSP said that the risks to the inflation outlook have receded but remain tilted toward the upside. BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. earlier discounted the possibility of a rate cut within the first half of the year, saying such a policy move is “too soon.”. The BSP’s next policy meeting is on April 4.

Customs collections rise 12% YoY in February

Preliminary data from the Bureau of Customs (BoC) showed its February collection rose 12% YoY to P70.6bn. This also exceeded the agency’s target for the month of P66.2bn. This brings 2M24 collections to P144bn, up 8% YoY. The BoC is tasked to collect P959bn this year, up 9% YoY.

Dept. of Transportation proposes 67.5-km subway in Cebu

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) proposed to the Cebu provincial government a 67.5-km subway connecting Carcar City in southern Cebu to Danao City in the north. The project was named as Metro Cebu Urban Mass Rapid Transit (UMRT). The proposed project is open to PPP but no project cost/timetable has been disclosed.

 

Dept. of Finance proposes “compromise” to Lower House’s mining fiscal regime bill

The Department of Finance is proposing a simplified fiscal regime for the Philippine mining industry that is seen to generate as much as P10bn in additional revenues every year. The proposal is simpler than House Bill (HB) No. 8937 as it adopts fewer tiers and rates for easier compliance and implementation. HB 8937 was approved in the Lower House last Sep 2023. Based on its new proposal, the DoF expects the “compromise” mining fiscal regime to generate an average of P10.23 billion a year from 2025 to 2028. Chamber of Mines of the Philippines (CoMP) Chairman Michael T. Toledo said the proposed four-tier royalty rate will result in an increase in the annual effective tax rate to 60.6% from the 59.6% under HB 8937. This would be higher than those in Indonesia, Chile, Peru, South Africa, and Canada.

Senate approved Bill on property valuation reform

The Senate approved on second reading Senate Bill 2386, the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act. This seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units. The assessed valued used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. Regis estimates that real property taxes account for 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers Regis covers. This law would likely have a large impact on developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land, Filinvest Land, and Vista Land.
