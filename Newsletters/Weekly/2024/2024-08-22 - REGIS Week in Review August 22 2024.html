REGIS REPORT

22 August 2024

Philippine shares gained for the 3rd week in a row, buoyed by a resurgence in net foreign inflows amid a dovish monetary policy backdrop at home and in anticipation of up to 100bps of rate cuts in the US before the year ends. The PSEi advanced +1.67% WoW to 6961, our best close since early April, after earlier testing the crucial 7000-level (which we have yet to close above so far this year.) Note that among our closer peers in the region, the Philippines stands out as one of the first to cut rates as central banks in Indonesia and Thailand both decided to keep rates steady during the week. Furthermore, in terms of technical, the PSEi is on the cusp of forming a golden cross in its daily chart.

The market rally during the shortened week was supported by increased volumes with a daily average turnover of $123m, +24% WoW and +23% more than the YTD average. Interesting to note that local investors took up the larger slice of 55% of activity, albeit less than last week’s 61%. That said, net foreign buying was quite heavy at $112m. Note that this includes the $22m block inflow into OGP reflected in the market on Thursday. Outside of that, top foreign bought stocks this week include BDO by a sizable $24m, ALI $21m, ICT $13m, MBT $10m, followed by SM, CNVRG, JFC, TEL, BPI and CNPF by $5m or less. Meanwhile, outflows were quite light in comparison with top sold stocks URC by $4m, SECB $2m and EMI $1.4m.

Banking sector led the rally with the subindex surging +5.5% WoW, lifted by gains in the big 3. BDO +8.68% WoW was the top gainer in the PSEi this week, while MBT +6.86% and BPI +3.57%, supported primarily by heavy foreign buying. There was also news regarding the RRR which helped bolster sentiment on the sector. The BSP Governor Remolona commented that the current level of 9.5% is too high and should be reduced substantially. One laggard in the sector however was SECB -1.62%, dragged conversely by outflows, ahead of its removal from the MSCI small cap index in the forthcoming rebalancing next week, August 30.

Another notable gainer in the benchmark was ICT +5.62% WoW which similar to BDO is widely-held and highly-liquid name, and hence benefits as a sort of proxy for foreign investors wanting exposure in the Philippines, notwithstanding the company’s global exposure fundamentally. ICT settled the week at a new all-time high and is now +69% YTD vs PSEi's +8%. Another Razon stock, BLOOM +5.48%, likewise fared well, extending its rebound after marking fresh lows in the previous week. Consumer plays PGOLD +4.19% and URC +3.05% were also among the better performers this week, notwithstanding the sharp outflows on the latter.  

Telco names GLO -2.37% WoW and TEL -0.57%, which were among the better performers when the market was in a holding pattern in the 6400-6800 range, trailed behind this time as investors evidently rotate into other plays. CNVRG however managed to buck this trend, surging +4.9% to its best close since March 2023. It is now the best performing stock in the index YTD, +79%.

Despite the rate cut backdrop, property stocks actually trailed behind the broader market rally, likely weighed down by a tempered earnings outlook on the ground. Top laggard in the benchmark was SMPH -2.62% WoW, while ALI -1.06%, despite seeing over $20m in net inflows this week, also turned lower after its initial strength in the earlier part of the week failed to hold in the latter.

Philippine Property - 2Q24 recap: residential presales fall, unsold inventory level rises
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment weakens

The 2Q numbers for the residential segment show a deterioration with reservation sales falling and unsold inventory levels rising. Meanwhile, office vacancy stayed flat while mall revenue growth remained healthy.

Residential reservation sales fall

After rebounding in 1Q24, aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover fell 10% QoQ (down 14% YoY) to P112bn, slightly below the 2023 average (and about 10% below pre-COVID levels). Like the previous period, sales of high[1]end and provincial projects generally grew faster than sales of mid-income condominiums in Metro Manila.

Colliers estimates that the level of unsold condominium inventory in Metro Manila increased to 4.9 years of reservation sales, from 3.6 years in 1Q24 (3.1 years in 4Q23). The increase in unsold inventory level was largely caused by the reduction in trailing 12M sales, rather than an increase in the number of available units. While our estimates suggest that the nationwide unsold inventory levels of the major developers are in the 1.5-2 year range, the Colliers data suggest that the situation is more precarious in Metro Manila.

On a more positive note, aggregate residential revenue (of the developers we cover) grew 19% YoY to P58bn. The strong growth over the past three quarters (+24% YoY) likely indicates that cancellation rates have peaked.

Office vacancy improves but POGO ban looms

Office vacancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 16% while office rental rates inched up. Office transactions picked up QoQ, thanks to demand from BPO firms as well as government agencies (listed as “traditional” tenants). Meanwhile, POGO firms took up less office space QoQ. 

We expect the POGO ban to be negative for the office segment, particularly in the Manila Bay area (see: President Marcos bans POGO sector). We estimate that the POGO sector occupies about 5% (800,000sqm) of Metro Manila office stock and nearly 30% of the Manila Bay area office space. Thus, a ban on the sector could result in Metro Manila office vacancy rising to 21% and Manila Bay area office vacancy climbing to about 50%. In such a scenario, we believe it would be reasonable for office rental rates to fall c. 10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area.

The POGO sector employs over 100,000 foreign nationals (based on our estimates), a large portion of whom reside in the Manila Bay area. Thus, their departure could lead to significant drops in Manila Bay area property prices.

Mall revenue growth stays flat at 9%

Mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators remained at 9% in 2Q24 (from 9% in 1Q24). Now that revenue is already above pre-COVID levels, we expect same-mall revenue growth to stay within the historical range of 5%-8%.

 
Philippine Consumer - Shifting tides, part 2
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Demand recovery in 2H24

We expect consumption to recover in 2H24 as inflationary pressures on consumers ease. The tapering of food price increases, including the outright decline in some (notably rice), should, in our view, perk up consumer spending as the seasonally strong 4Q approaches. Margins across the sector have recovered but could improve further as volumes rebound. For the companies covered in this report – Century Pacific (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), and Universal Robina (URC PM) – we update our 2024-26E forecasts for recent results and refresh our target prices as we roll over to 2025E. All the above names are on mid[1]teens PER, below regional peers (19x). With consumer sentiment (and thus spending) likely to improve soon, we believe there is scope for positive forecast revisions and a re-rating of the sector as year-end approaches.

Easing inflationary pressures to boost consumer sentiment

Consumer sentiment in the Philippines is still weak compared to year-ago levels but has stabilized in recent quarters on expectations of easing inflation. The reduction of rice tariffs (from 35% to 15%) is an important catalyst, in our view, as it will spur price declines for a key component of the CPI and the average Filipino food basket. Lower income households, in particular, are still reeling from the impact of inflationary pressures, and would benefit greatly from lower rice prices.

Sector re-rating on more positive top line outlook

The margin recovery story from lower input costs has materialized but we believe there is room for margins to improve further as demand recovers. We are forecasting an average EPS CAGR of 10% for 2024-26E for the stocks covered in this report, driven by an average top line CAGR of 8%

Estimates, target prices refreshed; Buy CNPF, MONDE and URC

We maintain our Buy ratings on CNPF, MONDE, and URC given favorable risk reward, in our view. We are adjusting our 2024-25E EPS estimates, rolling over our valuations to 2025E, and refreshing our respective target prices for each stock. Our SOTP-based target prices value the respective branded segments using DCF, implying 2025E PE multiples of ~19x – broadly in line with peers. The key sector risk relates mainly to inflation and its impact on input costs and consumer sentiment.

Trust AUMs up 13% YoY to new record high P6.6trn

Trust AUMs of banks and non-banks regulated by the BSP grew 13% YoY and 3% QoQ to P6.61trn in 2Q24 (about $117bn), new all-time high. Bank AUMs grew 1% YoY to P4.1trn, while non-banks surged 39% YoY to P2.5trn. This discrepancy is partly due to RCBC (RCB PM), which spun off its trust department into a “stand alone trust” in 1Q24, thus classifying the AUM from bank to non-bank. BPI did the same thing in 2017.

Among banks we cover/follow, the big gainer seemed to be Philippine National Bank, whose AUMs grew 4.5% QoQ to P178.8bn, and Union Bank grew 3.4% QoQ. On the other end, Security Bank (SECB PM) lost 4.6% of AUMs QoQ to P103.8bn. Among the Big Three, BPI gained 2.5% QoQ to P,1.384bn, while BDO added 1.4% to P2,025.6bn. Metrobank AUMs, meanwhile, shed 1.1% to P497.7bn.

BDO remains by far the biggest at P2,026bn (close to $36bn), for a 30.6% market share, with BPI at 20.4%, and Metrobank at a far third at 7.5%.

As we’ve said in the past, local Trusts could prove a major driving force for the Philippine equities market. Anecdotally , only 10-12% of AUMs are in local equities, down from a peak of close to 25%. Falling interest rates could see a reallocation of funds away from fixed-income and into the PSE.

ICTSI files 'Request for Arbitration' against Republic of Honduras to retain investment protections

ICTSI and its wholly-owned unit in Honduras, Operadora Portuaria Centroamericana, S.A. de C.V., filed a “request for arbitration” before the World Bank’s International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID). This filing should (in theory) allow the company to retain its investment protections, even after the withdrawal of the government of Honduras’ from the ICSID takes effect on Aug 25. Note that the Republic of Honduras denounced the ICSID Convention last Feb 24 under President Xiomara Castro. The withdrawal from ICSID did not have legislative authorization and has been questioned at local courts. This development will have no impact on ICTSI’s operations in Honduras and fundamentals should remain intact. However, it is unclear if the government of Honduras will continue to adhere to international arbitration rules should there be contractual disputes in the future. Regis estimates that ICT’s port in Honduras accounts for 6% of volumes and 7% of revenues/EBITDA.

DMCI to issue P10bn convertible prefs to Dacon Corp.

DMCI plans to issue 10mn Class B preferred shares at an issue price of P1,000/sh to the Consunji family-run Dacon Corp. or a total value of P10bn via a private placement. The preferred shares will have an annual dividend rate of 4% to be paid out on a quarterly basis. The conversion price will be set at a 30% premium over the VWAP of common shares over 30 trading days immediately preceding the conversion date. Note that Dacon currently owns 50% of DMCI. The proceeds will be used to fund DMCI’s portion of its payment for acquiring 100% of Cemex Asian South East Corp. (CASEC), which in turn owns 89.9% of Cemex Philippines (CHP PM). DMCI currently trades at a 15% discount to its market-based NAV of P13/sh, in line with its historical average discount.

Court clears Meralco's power supply auctions

The Taguig Regional Trial Court junked the petition for injunction against Meralco’s competitive selection process (CSP) for 600-MW baseload and 400-MW mid-merit supply contracts. Meralco can now proceed with the competitive selection process. The 600 MW auction, which covers a 15-year power supply agreement (PSA) that was targeted to start on Aug. 26, 2025, has been moved to Aug 27. Meanwhile, Meralco has yet to provide the updated bidding schedule for 400 MW of mid-merit PSA for 15 years.

ACEN to complete floating solar projects by 2027

ACEN will spend P48bn to complete five floating solar projects in Laguna Lake, will have a combined capacity of 1,120 megawatts peak (MWp), by 2027. ACEN currently has a capacity of 4.8GW of attributable renewable energy (RE) capacity across the Philippines, Australia, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Vietnam. It has a target of quadrupling its capacity to 20 GW by 2030.

SM acquiring property from subsidiary

SM Investments will be acquiring 184 hectares of land in Muntinlupa City (Susana Heights). The property will be acquired from 96.75% subsidiary of SM called Intercontinental Development Corp (ICDC) and will be done through a property-for-share swap. The number of shares to be issued was not disclosed, however, since ICDC is almost wholly owned, there should be almost no change in the outstanding shares.

Solaire patrons to receive BLOOM shares

Bloomberry’s Board of Directors approved the purchase of 1.94mn BLOOM shares “through the Stock Market at market price.” This represents 0.02% of shares outstanding. These shares shall be given as a reward to Solaire’s loyal patrons and as part of Solaire’s marketing program

DigiPlus Interactive is applying online gaming operation license in Brazil

Following the recent legalisation of gaming and betting activities in Brazil in June 2024, PLUS’s wholly owned subsidiary, DigiPlus Brazil Interactive, applied for the operation license, Licença Para Loterias De Apostas De Quota Fixa. The results of the license application are expected to be released on or before the end of November 2024. 17 other competitors are also applying for the license.

Aboitiz Group is consolidating its food and agribusiness units

Pilmico (flour and feed manufacturer) and Gold Coin Management Holdings Inc. (animal feed manufacturer) are being consolidated into the unified corporate brand Aboitiz Foods. By combining expertise in trading, feed, specialty nutrition, and food production, and with facilities spanning across the region in the Philippines, China, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brunei, Aboitiz Foods said it is strategically positioned to become a dominant force in the region.

Vista Land 1H24 headline profit rises 10%, ahead of REGIS and consensus

Vista Land’s 1H24 headline profit rose 10% to P5.4bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Stripping off non-recurring gains (from insurance claims) in 1H23, 1H24 recurring profit climbed 55% to P5.4bn. A key reason for the outperformance was a 15% (P979mn) drop in 1H24 operating expenses (to P5.4bn). Impairment losses fell from P463mn in 1H23 to zero in 1H24. The company also spent less for advertising and promotions as well as repairs and maintenance.

1H24 reservation sales grew 10% to P39.2bn, approximately equivalent to the pre-COVID level. The company increased the launches of higher-end Brittany projects in Mega Manila and launched “resort-type” projects in Palawan, Boracay, and Tagaytay. 2Q24 reservation sales rose 8% YoY (-12% QoQ) to P18.4bn, slightly below pre-COVID levels. 2Q24 residential revenue climbed 25% YoY (-27% QoQ) to P4.1bn, the fourth consecutive quarter of YoY increase. This likely implies that cancellation rates have peaked; however, residential revenue is only about half of pre-COVID levels.

1H24 residential revenue increased 20% to P9.6bn. 1H24 rental revenue grew 7% to P8.5bn, more than double the pre-COVID level. VLL’s rental portfolio is about 15% larger than 2018. The combined revenue of ALLDY and HOME, which occupy over 50% of VLL’s mall space, fell 3% YoY in 1H24.

Topline aims to raise ₱3.1B from its initial public offering

Cebu-based fuel retailer Top Line Business Development Corp. (Topline) has filed a registration statement with the SEC for its planned IPO at the PSE in November. Topline will offer the public up to 3.68 billion primary common shares with an overallotment option of up to 368.31 million in secondary common shares. The indicative offer price is up to P0.78 per share and the company intends to raise P3.15 billion. The tentative offer period will be from October 28 through November.

Philippine Banks: Impact of potential RRR cuts

As we mentioned in last week’s comment on the policy rate cut, BSP Governor Eli Remolona was quoted saying, “I think we should reduce the reserve requirement quite substantially,” adding that the current level of 9.5% is “ridiculous.” Earlier this year, he said he would like to see the RRR down at 5%. However, Mr Remolona has not committed to any timetable for the reduction. He has consistently said, though, that it would likely have to wait for the BSP’s policy to move towards an easing stance. And here we are.

We’ve dusted off our old RRR sensitivity tables, but reach pretty much the same conclusion – RRR cuts are not a major driver for profits. We estimate that every 100bps cut in RRR will only increase pre-tax profits by 1.2-1.5% for banks we cover. This (generously?) assumes that banks will place the freed-up funds in PHP Treasuries at 6.50%.

Some may argue that they could get better yields if they lend it out. But banks’ LDRs are in the 70-80% range, which means they already have lots of capacity to lend without the RRR cut. But it should be easy enough to adjust the numbers below.

Bottom line – it’s positive. A RRR cut could also mitigate the impact of the reduction in policy interest rates on NIMs. Excess liquidity could also ease the competitive pressure on PHP deposits.

BSP has room for one more rate cut

After cutting interest rates by 25 bps last Thursday, the BSP said there is room for one more rate cut this year, according to BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. in an interview with CNBC. The next moves by the BSP will largely be driven by the inflation outlook. If inflation continues a downward path, the BSP can look to further remove the restrictiveness in the monetary stance to support a recovery in domestic demand and overall growth. BSP is scheduled to conduct two more policy reviews on October 17 and December 19. The GDP growth projection for the year is expected to be close to 6%, slightly higher than last year's growth rate of 5.6%.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market inches up in July

We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 2bps MoM to 20.1% (in July) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index expanded 15bps MoM to 24.9%. Net foreign buying amounted to US$60mn, a reversal from net foreign selling of US$104mn in June. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 17 saw increases in foreign ownership while 13 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

PGOLD – foreign ownership rose 28bps MoM to 18.5%.  

URC – foreign ownership increased 26bps MoM to 22.3%. Net foreign buying hit US$13.8mn (the fourth highest for the month).

WLCON – foreign ownership fell 70bps MoM to 15.8%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$3.1mn (the seventh highest for the month).

JFC - foreign ownership declined 32bps MoM to 23.2%. It also experienced the highest net foreign selling for the month (US$12.1mn).

Infrastructure spending rises 18% YoY to P721bn in 1H24

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 18.4% YoY to P720.5bn in 1H24. This exceeded the government’s 1H24 program of P671.3bn by 7.3% and is equivalent to 5.7% of GDP. Implies that infra spend for 2Q24 alone grew by 24% YoY to P454bn. The government aims to spend P1.47tn (+4% YoY) on infrastructure for FY24 (5.6% of GDP). However, this implies that infrastructure spending in 2H24 will drop by 7% YoY to P752bn. For 2025, the government plans to spend P1.5tn of infrastructure, up 4% YoY and equivalent to 5.4% of GDP. The government aims to spend 5-6% of GDP annually for infrastructure through 2028.

Balance of payments position swings to a US$62mn surplus in July; End-July GIR level rises to US$106.7bn

The country’s balance of payments registered a US$62mn surplus in July, a reversal of the US$53mn deficit recorded in July 2023 and the US$155mn deficit in June 2024. Officials cited inflows from the BSP’s investments abroad and the national government’s net foreign currency deposits with the BSP. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-July stood at US$106.7bn, up from US$105.2bn in end-June. This is equivalent to 7.9 months’ worth of imports of goods.

Tax collections grow by 11% YoY in 7M24; July up 15% YoY

Preliminary data from the Bureau of Internal Revenue showed YT-July tax collections reached P1.68tn, up 12.6% YoY. This implies that for July 2024 alone, BIR’s collections grew by 16% YoY. YT-July BIR collections, however, fell short of the government’s collection goal of P1.83tn for the period. Moreover, preliminary data from the Bureau of Customs showed 7M24 collections rose 6% YoY to P536bn. Implied July 2024 collections grew 11% YoY. Overall tax revenues grew by 11% YoY in 7M24, with tax revenues from the month of July alone rising by 15% YoY.

Air passenger volumes rises 26% YoY to 29.5mn in 1H24, 3% below pre-Covid levels

Data from the Civil Aeronautics Board showed that nationwide air pax volumes rose by 26% YoY to 29.5mn in 1H24. This was 3% below pre-Covid levels. Cebu Air (CEB PM) sustained market share gains, with pax volumes up 27% YoY to 10.9mn, exceeding 1H19 levels by 3%. International pax volumes grew 54% YoY to 13.8mn in 1H24, 90% of 1H19 levels. Domestic pax volumes grew 8% YoY to 15.8mn in 1H24, surpassing 1H19 levels by 4%.

Finance Department lowers revenue projections from mining fiscal reform bill

The Department of Finance lowered its projected annual revenue estimates to P6.3bn from P10.2bn previously from the mining fiscal reform bill, citing lower metal prices. Broken down: it expects P4.5bn in incremental revenue from royalty tax inside mineral reservations, P1.1bn from outside reservations and P730mn from windfall profit tax. The latest proposal imposes five-tiered margin-based royalty rate and windfall profit tax with five tiers (vs. four tiers previously) The DoF’s proposal also includes a provision treating mining contractors as separate taxable entities under law “with respect to each and every mineral agreement it holds or operates.”

Philippine ports foreign container throughput rises by 5% YoY in 2Q24

Philippine ports handled almost 1.4mn foreign container TEUs in 2Q24, up 5% YoY, which marks a rebound from -1% YoY in 1Q24. For the quarter, ICTSI’s MICT grew volumes by 7% YoY to 738k TEUs, after posting flat growth in 1Q24. This takes 1H24 volumes of MICT to 1.4mn TEUs, up 3% YoY. To reach our forecast of 2.9mn TEUs (+5% YoY) for 2024, volumes for the rest of the year need to grow by 6% YoY to 1.5mn TEUs.

Senate backs Natural Gas Industry Development bill

17 senators have supported the proposed Philippine Natural Gas Industry Development Act (Senate Bill No. 2793), which seeks to revitalize indigenous gas exploration and develop natural gas infrastructure that covers all aspects of the natural gas industry, including transportation, transmission, and storage. Prioritizing the use of indigenous gas over imported LNG, ensuring lesser dependence on foreign suppliers for this critical resource.
