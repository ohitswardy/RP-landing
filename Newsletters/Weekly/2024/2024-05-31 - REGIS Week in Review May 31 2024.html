REGIS REPORT

31 May 2024

Philippine shares tumbled sharply this week, sending the PSEi -2.82% WoW to 6433 (and now in the red YTD, -0.26%), as risk assets across the globe mostly tumbled amid a renewed spike in US Treasury yields, while on the ground investors assessed the BSP policy path moving forward after some comments from members of the monetary board and amid the continued weakness in the PHP. The local benchmark fell in nearly each of the trading sessions this week, first breaking below the technical 200-day moving average mid-week, then the key 6400 support line (which had notably held up during the mid-April selloff) to mark a new YTD low of 6371 on Thursday, dragged by continuous net foreign outflows. The benchmark then managed to find support at the closing auction of today’s month-end session, which is also the MSCI rebalancing day, after trading steadily around the flatline throughout the continuous session, to ultimately settle the week back above the key 6400 line. During today’s close, the benchmark spiked by over 1% as a third of index members jumped by 3%-6%. Biggest movers include MONDE +6.06% (swing at today’s close), PGOLD +5%, AGI +4.98%, TEL +4.96%, LTG +4.9% and ICT +4.59%. Bulk of the auction volumes however were in MSCI deletion AEV which swung down by -4.98% at the auction alone. AEV was today’s most actively traded stock, accounting for a fourth of the outsize $338.3m turnover which is the heaviest since August 15, 2023, and netting $51.8m in outflows.

On the policy front, investors digested mixed signals from officials after BSP Senior Assistant Governor Sicat commented earlier in the week that policy will remain restrictive as they deem that upside risks to inflation remain. Meanwhile Finance Secretary Recto, who also sits on the Monetary Board, stated that policymakers have room start cutting rates by 3Q and could reduce the benchmark rate by as much as 150bps through the next two years. Note that just this Friday morning the BSP announced that it will now be adopting a 7-day quiet period before each scheduled policy meeting where only the Governor can speak on policy, as the central bank seeks to project a single voice when communicating policy direction and intention to the public. The next scheduled policy meeting is on June 27.

Another important area of focus was the PHP, which hit as low as 58.625 on Thursday, marking its weakest level against the USD since November 2022, and ultimately capped the week at 58.526. The currency is now inching closer towards the crucial 59-level, which notably managed to hold as support during the late 2022 weakness in the local currency, and as such could potentially be a level that may trigger more intervention from the central bank. Note that the BSP has started to acknowledge the weak PHP’s potential impact on inflation this week when it announced its inflation expectation for the upcoming May CPI reading due on Wednesday. The central bank expects the latest reading to be between 3.7%-4.5%, while the consensus forecast is currently at 4%, which would mark an uptick from the 3.8% print in April.

Worst sectors again this week was Property -3.95% WoW, closely followed this time by Banks -3.65% despite the threat of higher-for-longer rates, as they both succumbed to heavy outflows. The two biggest decliners this week were MBT -10.07% WoW and ALI -9.01% - we note however that a stark difference between the two is that MBT remains one of the best gainers YTD, +20.96%, hence potentially succumbing to some profit taking this week, while ALI on the opposite end has already been quite battered this year, -23.8% YTD and the second worst performer in the benchmark. Another notable laggard this week was aforementioned AEV -7.03%, whose bulk of losses came during today’s closing auction amid its removal from the MSCI. Other key changes during this rebalancing were the weight cuts in AC -3.57% and SM -2.9%, and modest upweight in SMPH -2% in the standard cap index, and the inclusion of ACEN -4.19% and deletion of CEB -7.28% and SPNEC -9% in the small cap index. On the opposite end, notable outperformers in the PSEi this week were DMC +8.18%, TEL +4.23% and CNPF +4.02%.

Market activity this week was better than the last at a daily average of $149m, +36% WoW, largely helped by today’s outsize rebal-driven volumes. Foreign investors were better sellers throughout the entire week and netted an outflow of nearly -$140m vs last week’s under $10m. Top outflow stocks were AEV -7.03%, ALI -9.01%, SMPH -2%, BDO -4.2%, SM -2.9%, JFC -5.91% and BPI -2.28%.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property 1Q24 recap: residential presales rebound after three quarters of decline
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment recovers

After three consecutive quarters of declines, residential reservation sales grew on a sequential basis for nearly every developer we cover. Residential revenue also showed remarkable YoY growth in 1Q24. Meanwhile, office vacancy stayed flat while mall revenue growth normalized.

Residential reservation sales rebound QoQ

After falling for three consecutive quarters, aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover grew 27% QoQ (down 7% YoY) to P123.5bn, slightly higher than the 2023 average (and just slightly below pre-COVID levels). Similar to previous periods, sales of high-end and provincial projects generally grew faster than sales of mid-income condominiums in Metro Manila.

Aggregate residential revenue grew 28% YoY to P54.6bn, which is also slightly higher than the 2023 average. The 27% YoY revenue growth over the past two quarters could indicate that cancellations have peaked.

Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums in the secondary market inched up for the tenth consecutive quarter, up 3% YoY. Secondary market prices of CBD condominiums are 5%-15% below pre-COVID levels while prices of preselling condominiums are 10%-30% above 2019 levels. As a result, Colliers estimates that prices of condominium units under preselling are now 40%-80% higher than secondary market prices. This price gap could curtail demand for Metro Manila condominiums in the primary market.

Office vacancy stays flat

Office vacancy remained flat QoQ at 17% while office rental rates inched up (see figures 4 and 5). Office transactions picked up QoQ, thanks to demand from BPO firms as well as government agencies (listed under “traditional”). Meanwhile, POGO firms took up less office space QoQ (see figures 6 to 9). Our channel checks suggest that firms from all three categories are likely to see net expansions in demand for office space in 2Q24.

However, negative rental reversions are likely to worsen this year, since 1) spot office rental rates are currently 10%-50% below 2019 levels, and 2) many of the contracts up for renewal this year were previously signed in 2019, when rental rates were close to the peak.

Mall revenue growth normalizes

Mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators normalized to 9% YoY, from 13% YoY in 4Q23. Now that revenue is already above pre-COVID levels, we expect same-mall revenue growth to stay within the historical range of 5%-8%.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PLDT introduces lower priced broadband services

PLDT began introducing lower price wired (fiber) broadband services available in both postpaid and prepaid formats. The new postpaid plan of  “up to 35mbps” goes for P899/month, but requires a P3,500 upfront payment. For comparison, Converge’s Bida Fiber service is priced at P888/month. A new prepaid fiber plan was also introduced, with price points similar to Globe’s GFiber prepaid: P299 for a 7 day duration, P599 for 15, and P999 for 30.

Meralco eyes bid for 500MW of RE capacity

Meralco started the competitive selection process for 500MW of renewable energy (RE) to comply with the government’s renewable portfolio standards. The 10-year PSA will cover Meralco’s 350MW mid-merit requirement starting Feb 2025, which will increase by 150 MW a year later. The deadline to submit bids set for July 17. Note that Meralco has already contracted 1,880 MW of RE capacity from various suppliers, exceeding its initial target of 1,500 MW. Renewable energy is expected to account for 22% of Meralco’s supply portfolio by 2030. For context, the government requires distribution utilities to source at least 2.5% of its energy requirements from RE sources.

First Gen says 80MW geothermal projects set for 2024 launch

FGEN is targeting to complete 80MW of geothermal energy projects within the year. According to FGEN President and COO Francis Giles Puno. It recently completed a 29 MW binary power plant in Palawan. Last year, EDC said it was investing P60bn in the next three years to drill 40 additional wells in Leyte, Davao, Albay, and Sorsogon.

DigiPlus launches BingoPlus Poker and Tongits+

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) launched BingoPlus Poker on gamefun.ph, while Tongits+ is available for download on GooglePlay. Recall that PLUS recorded P13.4bn of GGR in 1Q24. PAGCOR lowered the fees it collects from E-games operators in April 2024 and will further reduce it in January 2025.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP to maintain "restrictive policy"; Finance Department sees 150bps rate cut over two years

The BSP will keep monetary policy settings “restrictive” as upside risks to inflation remain, according to BSP Senior Assistant Governor Iluminada T. Sicat. The BSP expects inflation to accelerate from May to July, but expects it to return within the 2-4% target band after. Recall that inflation accelerated to 3.8% in April, bringing YT-April average inflation rate to 3.4% (BSP FY24E baseline forecast at 3.5%). On the peso, BSP reiterated that it does not target any specific exchange rate level. Mr. Sicat thinks that the recent weakness of the PHP is temporary, given the Fed’s hawkish stance.

Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said the Monetary Board could reduce the policy rate by as much as 150bps in the next two years. Mr. Recto, who sits on the Monetary Board, said that BSP has room to reduce rates by 3Q.

Digital payments surge in April, up 51% YoY, a new all-time high

Digital transactions over InstaPay and PesoNet surged to P1,413bn, up 51% YoY in April, accelerating from the 23% posted in March. As we reported last month, we suspected that the March slowdown – and thus the sharp rebound in April – were due to the timing of the Easter holidays, which landed in March this year vs April last year. The variance was much more pronounced for PesoNet, since these are batched transactions that are only processed during business days. PesoNet payments came in at P855bn, jumping 54% YoY in April, up from just 13% in March. Meanwhile, smaller-ticket, real-time transfers over InstaPay also rebounded, growing 47.8% YoY to P558bn in April, faster than the 39% clip in March. Both PesoNet and InstaPay figures for April were record-highs.

For the 4 months of 2024, combined PesoNet and InstaPay transactions stood at P5,225bn, growing a healthy 37%, faster than the 29% posted in FY23. For PesoNet, 4M24 payments were up 33% YoY, compared to +22% in FY23, while InstaPay grew 45% YoY, up from 42% in FY23.

Equally important, in our view, is the volume of transactions, particularly over InstaPay. These were up a scorching 76% YoY in April, taking the average value per transaction to P5,426 (or about $93mn), about 16% lower YoY. This to us signifies continued broadening out of use cases for digital payments.

Regis reiterates that, as impressive as the numbers are, they still vastly understate the actual volume/value of digital payments. This is because InstaPay and PesoNet only records transactions made between different platforms (eg BPI to BDO, or MBT to GCash, or Maya to GCash, etc). It does not include transfers made within a platform (BDO to BDO, GCash to GCash, etc).

Finance Secretary backs lower rice tariffs

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said he is open to cutting the import tariff on rice to 17.5%-20% from 35% today. The DOF is looking at the power of the President to modify the tariff rates when Congress is not in session. Note that Congress adjourned its session on May 24 and the next session opens on July 21. The Tariff Commission (TC) is expected to begin reviewing the proposed rice tariff cut soon.

Government expects rice prices possibility falling 20% by September

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto expects the retail price of rice to drop by as much as 20% by September as effects of El Niño fade and all restrictions on exports by major producing countries are expected to have been lifted by then.

Department of Energy warns Luzon grid may experience red alerts until next week

The Luzon grid could face red alerts until next week if the power plants that experienced forced outage or derated capacities fail to resume normal operations. The 1,200MW Ilijan power plant went on forced outage when its floating storage unit had to be disconnected and relocated for safety reasons due to the typhoon, while Pagbilao Units 1,2,4 (1,184 MW) were also shut down. A red alert was raised in the Luzon grid from 1-5pm and 6-10pm while yellow alert was raised from 12-1pm, 5-6pm, and 10pm-12am on May 27. Typhoon Aghon caused substantial decrease in available power supply at a time when hydropower plants have not yet recovered from their low water supply.

The Luzon grid was again placed under red (1pm-4pm/6pm-10pm) and yellow (12pm-1pm, 4pm-6pm, 10pm-12am) alerts on May 29. A total of 16 power plants have been on forced outage, while six plants are running derated. 

Santos Knight Frank: Metro Manila prime property prices rise 27%

Santos Knight Frank said that prime property prices in Metro Manila rose 27% YoY in 2023. Data from Leechiu Property Consultants suggest that prices of gated communities in Metro Manila rose 15%-25% in 2023 while secondary market prices of high-end condominiums in Makati and Fort Bonifacio increased 5%-15%. Developers have said that high-end condominiums are selling faster than mid-income condominiums in Metro Manila.

Senator files Bill seeking to ban POGO operations

Senator Sherwin Gatchalian filed a Bill seeking to ban offshore gaming operations in the Philippines. Recall that earlier this year, the House Games and Amusements panel approved a bill banning Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators due to crimes linked to the sector. Note however that PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco previously said that he is in favor of allowing them to continue operating if they are properly monitored and regulated. Regis estimates that offshore gaming firms, now called Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL), occupy about 900,000sqm of Metro Manila office space (about 6% of Metro Manila office stock). Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:

49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV – POGOs occupy c. 5% of office space
43% of Megaworld’s NAV – POGOs occupy c. 5% of office space
28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV – POGOs occupy 1% of office space

NG debt rises to P15.02tn in April

The national government’s outstanding debt reached P15.02tn in end-April 2024. This was up 0.61% MoM due to net financing and the PHP depreciation against the USD. Domestic debt rose to P10.31tn (+0.3% MoM) due to net issuance of government securities, while external debt rose 1.3% MoM to P4.71tn due to PHP depreciation. This resulted in a P109bn MoM upward adjustment in translation of USD-denominated debt (82% of external debt are USD loans). Note that the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio target this year is set at 60.3% and it seeks to bring this down further to 55.9% by 2028

Vehicle sales rise 22% in April

CAMPI reported that car sales rose 22% to 37,314 units in April (but down 0.4% on a MoM basis). Balancing out the timing of Holy Week, industry car sales for the months of March and April rose 11%. This brings YT-April vehicle sales up 14.8%.

Toyota Philippines’ sales in 4M24 reached 67,580 units (+14% YoY), implying that in April alone, Toyota sold 17,913 units (+27% YoY, 7.9% MoM). To reach Toyota’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.4k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 17k units for the rest of the year. Note that Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).

Regulator approves higher toll rates for NLEX starting June 4

The Toll Regulatory Board approved the second tranche of NLEX’s 2018/2020 petitions, which were supposed to be implemented in 2019/2021. The increase in toll rates is as follows: Open system (including VAT) +P5 per entry (+7%) to P74, Closed system (including VAT) +P0.35 per km (+8%) to P4.37.

Note that NLEX is a 76%-owned unit of Metro Pacific, which is a 20%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM). Tollroads account for 5% of GTCAP’s profit (MPI accounts for 14% of GTCAP’s gross profit before parent expenses).

SBMA eyes US$879mn worth of port projects

The Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA) is eyeing various port projects worth a total of USD878.7mn in anticipation of future demand at the Subic Bay Freeport Zone: A 300k TEU container port (“New Container Terminal 3”, NCT3) worth US$359mn, which will add to the existing capacity of 600k available at NCTs 1&2 that are currently being operated by ICTSI (ICT PM), a US$162mn multipurpose terminal at the Redondo Peninsula, a US$182mn multipurpose terminal in Lower Mau for bulk and break-bulk industries, and a P10.2bn dedicated passenger cruise terminal.
