REGIS REPORT

21 June 2024

Philippine shares tumbled for the second week in a row, sending the PSE index to its lowest level so far this year, as sentiment on the ground continued to deteriorate amid the tight liquidity in the market and lingering uncertainty over the timing of policy easing. The benchmark was flattish around 6300’s and near YTD lows through majority of the week, then submitted to a sharp selloff during today’s FTSE rebalancing and marked its worst daily drop (-2.93%) since December 2022, notwithstanding the minimal outflow estimates ahead of the event. The local benchmark ultimately closed -3.53% WoW at 6158, its lowest close since late October. Note that on a YTD basis, the index is now down by -4.14% and in USD terms more than twice of that, -10.11%.

Breadth of the market was poor with half of the thirty index components tumbling down by over -3% WoW, bulk of those losses coming through the final session of the week. The worst among them was JGS -9.09% WoW which fell to its lowest level since 2012, followed by BDO -7.69% and ICT -6.95% which are potentially two of the most widely held names in the local market. JGS is now the worst performing stock in the PSEi so far this 2024, -33.16% YTD, while in contrast ICT is still among the best, +28.04%. Meanwhile the losses in BDO this week tipped the stock to the red by -2.45% YTD. On the opposite end, notable outperformers this week were SCC +2.99% WoW, WLCON +2.3% which had already fallen by -9.84% last week, AGI +2.3% as the company again bought back shares this week (over 2m shares on Tuesday), and SMC +2.05%.

The biggest event in an otherwise quiet week was today’s FTSE rebalancing. Note that it was initially expected to be a relatively quiet one due to a modest passive outflow estimate of less $10m for the entire Philippine market. Notable changes for constituents were upweights in ACEN -3.12% WoW and JFC -3.65% of less than $10m inflow each. That being said, the thin liquidity on the board and the downbeat sentiment on the ground appear to have resulted to an outsize slump in the market with the local index posting its biggest one-day drop since late 2022. Activity during today's rebal stood at $140m, which is actually relatively light compared to the usual rebal pace. For example, turnover in the previous end-May MSCI rebal stood at $388m. 

Among the key topics of discussion this week was the enactment of the Property Valuation Reform Law, which is set to (potentially) mostly increase real property taxes. Regis reiterates however that the impact of this law on developers will be relatively minor given that real property taxes accounted for only 2%-7% of the 2023 pre-tax profit of the companies under our coverage. Furthermore, that the increase in property taxes will be capped at 6% on the first year of implementation, and hence we estimate that the profit hit should be less than 0.5% on that first year. For the succeeding years, we expect the reduction in profits to be less than 5%. See more in Carl’s report below.

On the political front, the notable development this week was VP Sara Duterte’s resignation from her post as Secretary of Education. In our view, this marks a clean break in the political alliance between the Marcos administration and the Dutertes ahead of the elections and a possible plebiscite next year. While we don’t expect the departure of VP Duterte to have much of an impact on how the administration runs things, we reckon that the biggest impact, near-term, will be on the shape and outcome of the probable Charter change plebiscite next year.

Despite the generally risk-off mood this week however, one promising development that we want to highlight is President Marcos’ official signing of the rice tariff cut bill which, Regis reiterates, will help further ease inflation in the coming months and as such may help keep alive the chances of up to two rate cuts this year. Note that rice accounts for 9% of the CPI basket and the bill will cut tariffs from 35% to 15%, effective 15 days from now.

Market activity through majority of the week when the index was flattish at 6300 was fairly soft, then surged more than two-fold during today’s rebalancing session, bringing the average daily turnover this week to $82m, +34% WoW. Foreign investors continued to be net sellers throughout the week, extending the outflow streak to 8 consecutive days. Net outflow for the week stood at $47m, doubling last week’s $22m. Top foreign outflow names were BDO -7.69% WoW, SMPH -5.34%, GTCAP -4.48%, ALI -4.86%, JFC -3.65%, JGS -9.09%, BPI -5.34% and ICT -6.95%, while top inflows were ACEN -3.12% and SM +0.48%.

Philippine Strategy - Groundhog hat trick?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Or can the Dragon mount a 2H24 comeback?

The PSEi has given up all its gains from 1Q24, with the weak PHP pushing it to -6% ytd in USD terms. Will “Groundhog Day” rule for a 3rd year in a row? Frustratingly, our fundamental bull case hasn’t changed much. GDP (+5.7%) and corporate earnings (+6%) were broadly in line, with 2Q24 growth likely to benefit from a low YoY base. Inflation, meanwhile, is likely to slow sharply in 3Q24, as tariff cuts could push rice prices down 15-20%. Can lower food prices reignite consumer demand, spur BSP rate cuts, and reawaken the animal (Dragon) spirits?

Soft-ish 1Q24 GDP and earnings, thanks to inflation… and the Easter Bunny?

Elevated inflation — especially food / rice — took a toll on consumers. GDP growth in 1Q24 was 5.7%, with private consumption slowing to just 4.6%. Aggregate NPAT for the PSEi Index also seemed soft at just +6% YoY. However, the timing of the long Easter holiday (March this year vs April in 2023) could have played a role, too. High-frequency data show a healthy bounce in April. Growth is likely to bounce, as 2Q23 GDP was a low base due to government underspending.

Rice is life – falling prices could stoke consumption recovery…

We recently noted (here and here) that the export prices of rice out of Vietnam are down -11% ytd. Meanwhile, the Philippine government has proposed a cut in rice tariffs from 35% to 15%, which could quickly push down retail prices by 15%. Rice is 8.9% of CPI, likely much more for lower-income families. Lower prices should ease household budgets and spur a recovery in consumption from 3Q.

… and clear the way for BSP rate cuts (with or without the Fed?)

Even without tariff cuts, rice inflation should fall sharply, given the high base in 3Q2023, and could push overall CPI down closer to 2%. The BSP recently opened the door to possible policy rate cuts as early as August, with another towards year-end. We agree, at least fundamentally, as real interest rates could be back to historical highs by 3Q. The BSP should be wary of moving ahead of the Fed, though, as a weaker PHP could feed back into higher imported inflation.

Rooting for the Dragon – cheap valuations, high dividends

What has changed since the start of the year is investor expectations on Fed and local rate cuts. Fund flows have reversed. Foreigners have turned net sellers to the tune of $320mn since March, erasing the $243mn inflow from November to February. Local AUMs continue to rise (+16% YoY in 1Q24) but mostly into fixed income and FX assets.

Our fundamental bullish premises remain. We forecast aggregate earnings growth of 11% YoY this year, 15% excluding mining names Semirara, its parent DMCI, and Nickel Asia. Meanwhile, the market continues to present compelling value, now at 10.6x PER on a 12-month forward basis, with half our coverage trading at single-digit PERs and a similar proportion paying 4% or higher dividend yield. Can lower inflation and interest rate cuts reawaken the Dragon?

 
Philippine Property - Property Valuation Reform Law enacted; short-term impact minimal
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Higher taxes looming?

The Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act has become law after it was signed by President Marcos. This legislation will, among other things, transfer the responsibility of updating the “Schedule of Market Values” (from which real property taxes are based) from local governments to the national government.

Unlike the earlier versions, the enacted version of the law also places a 6% cap on the increase in real property taxes on the first year of implementation. Local governments will have a free hand to determine any increases thereafter. Thus, we expect only minor negative impact on the listed property firms from the new law in the near term.

What is the status quo?

All property owners pay real property taxes to city governments annually. There are three items to consider in determining the real property tax to be paid:

  • The Schedule of Market Values, which is supposed to be updated by the local governments every three years but, in practice, is not.
  • The assessed value, which is a percentage of market value
  • The percentage of the assessed value to be charged as real property tax. There is a legal cap of 3% (inclusive of “Special Education Fund”) but there is no floor.

Why is there a need for a new law?

While raising real property taxes would expand the coffers of a city government, it could also lead to the mayor (and other officials) losing the next election. As a result, local governments in many places are hesitant to regularly update their Schedule of Market Values. As a result, there are cities/municipalities that might not have updated it for c. 20 years. Congress believes that transferring the power to update the Schedule of Market Values to the national government will lead to more regular updates and higher tax collection.

How much will real property taxes of developers increase?

We estimate that real property taxes were equivalent to 2%-7% of the 2023 pretax profit of the developers we cover. Since the increase in real property taxes will be capped at 6% on the first year of implementation, the profit hit should be less than 0.5% on that year. While it is difficult to determine the exact impact in succeeding years, we expect the reduction in profit to be less than 5%.

How might this affect residential demand?

We expect (potentially) higher real property taxes to have only a minor impact on residential demand, though the impact on condominiums will likely be higher.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Alliance Global's Travellers appoints new CFO

Alliance Global’s gaming subsidiary, Travellers International, appointed Laurence Hawke as its new CFO. Mr. Hawke was previously CFO of Hoiana Resort & Gold in Da Nang, Vietnam. The previous CFO, Bernard Than, resigned on June 1. Recall that Travellers also announced a new CEO (Nilo Rodriguez) and COO (Lance Gautreaux) earlier this month.

Meralco launches bidding for 400MW power supply

Meralco is seeking bidders for 400MW of mid-merit 15-year power supply agreement (PSA) and they are open to both greenfield and brownfield power plant operators. PSA will start on Aug 26, 2025. The minimum offered contract capacity per bidder should be at least 150 MW. Bid submission deadlines are scheduled for July 11 and Aug 9.

Ayala Land to buy out Aboitiz in JV project

Ayala Land will purchase the 50% stake of AboitizLand in their JV project, the 17.5 hectare Gatewalk Central in Mandaue, Cebu for P1.8bn. AboitizLand is a subsidiary of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM). Regis expects minimal impact to Ayala Land as the project comprises less than 1% of NAV.

Suntrust Resort Holdings secures US$17mn loan for casino project

Suntrust Resort Holdings (SUN PM), the Philippine unit of Hong Kong’s LET Group, secured a 10-year US$17mn loan from LET Group Holdings. The proceeds will be used for the construction of a casino complex in Alliance Global’s section of PAGCOR Entertainment City. The Main Hotel Casino will have 475 rooms and a ballroom that can accommodate 550 people. It will also have a performing arts theatre and a grand opera house. Structural works and the construction of the tower facade up to roof-level has been completed. SUN is targeting to open the complex in 1Q25. At that time, the casino will have 281 gaming tables, 1,126 slot machines, and 134 electronic gaming tables. For comparison, Solaire Entertainment City has about 350 gaming tables and 2,700 slot machines. Recall that SUN borrowed US$38mn from LET Group in April 2024.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP official says restrictive policy stance appropriate

PH central bank will keep monetary policy settings restrictive to keep inflation in check as peso currency weakens in line with global conditions. The BSP remains optimistic that macroeconomic conditions would be favorable in 2024 and 2025 and that inflation would stay within the 2% to 4% target of BSP. The central bank’s key policy rate is at a 17-year high of 6.5% after a series of rate hikes last year to tame inflation which hit a 14-high peak of 8.7%.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market stays flat in May

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 3bps MoM to 20.4% (in May) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index also declined 3bps MoM to 25.0%. Net foreign selling slowed to US$174mn, from US$414mn (net selling) in April. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 13 saw increases in foreign ownership while 16 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

·                     BLOOM – foreign ownership fell 8.04 percentage points MoM to 17.2% as the company (through a wholly-owned subsidiary) purchased an 8% stake from its former partner, GGAM.  

·                     PGOLD – foreign ownership declined 69bps MoM to 18.0%.

·                     JFC – foreign ownership fell 65bps MoM to 23.8%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$31.4mn, the fourth highest for the month.

·                     GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 75bps MoM to 20.1%. Net foreign buying hit US$3.1mn

·                     URC – foreign ownership increased 36bps MoM to 21.9%. Net foreign buying amounted to US$6.4mn, the fourth highest for the month.

Current account deficit falls 61% YoY in 1Q24; BOP posts US$238mn surplus

The BSP reported that the country’s current account deficit fell 60.6% YoY to US$1.7bn in 1Q24, equivalent to 1.6% of GDP. Officials cited 15% YoY reduction in trade in goods deficit to US$14.7bn, which resulted from higher volume of exports and lower prices of imports. BPO export revenues amounted to US$7.1bn in 1Q24, up 2.1% YoY. This brings 1Q24 Balance of Payments (BOP) to a US$238mn surplus, lower than the US$3.5bn surplus in 1Q23. The BSP raised its 24E BOP surplus forecast to US$1.6bn from US$700mn previously due to a lower current account deficit forecast of US$4.7bn from US$6.1bn previously.

BOP reverses to US$2 billion surplus

The Philippine’s BOP position shifted to a surplus of US$2bn in May, reversing the US$439mn deficit recorded in May 2023. This marks a turnaround from the US$639mn shortfall in April and is the biggest surplus since January 2023 (US$3.08bn), reflecting inflows mainly from the national government’s net foreign currency deposits with BSP. This includes proceeds from ROP Global Bonds and net income from BSP’s foreign investments.

The cumulative BOP position has now reverted to a surplus of US$1.6bn. However, it is 44.3% lower than the surplus in the same period last year. Personal remittances rose to US$12.01bn from January to April (2.8% increase), while cash remittances increased by 2.8%. The BOP position reflects increase in GIR level to US$105bn, which is 6.1 times the country’s short term external debt and 3.8 times based on residual maturity. The foreign exchange buffer represents an external liquidity equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. Note that the BSP raised its BOP surplus forecast to US$1.6bn for this year.

Employers seek delay in minimum wage hike

In a public hearing, the Confederation of Wearable Exporters of the Philippines asked the NCR wage board to delay a proposed P100-750 wage hike. The group asked for a one-year moratorium on wage hikes to allow exporters to fully recover and preserve jobs until orders stabilize by 2025. The NCR wage board is set to release its decision on or before July 20, and is expected to set the tone for the wage increases in other regions. In 2022 and 2023, the government has implemented cumulative wage hikes of +20-22% across regions. In NCR alone, the cumulative wage hike was 13% over the same period. The cumulative headline inflation rate was 11.8% over the same period.

Remittances rise 3.1% YoY in April

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.1% YoY to US$2.56bn in April. In PHP terms, remittances grew 6%, fastest growth rate since May 2023. Land-based remittances rose 3.2%, driven by Singapore (+5.7%), Saudi Arabia (+6.8%), Qatar (+5.9% ), and USA (+2.8%). Meanwhile, sea-based remittances rose 2.8% YoY. This brings YT-April remittances up 2.8% to US$10.78bn. BSP sees remittances growing by 3% this year, suggesting remittances could reach US$34.5bn. To reach this, remittances for the rest of the year should average US$2.96bn per month.

IT-BPM industry likely to hit 7% goal

The Information Technology and Business Process Management (IT-BPM) industry is on track to achieve 6.5%-7.5% growth in revenue and headcount this year, according to IT and Business Process Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) President Jack Madrid. Under the IT-BPM industry roadmap 2028, the industry is projected to hit 1.84mn (+8% YoY) in headcount and US$40bn (+12% YoY) in revenues. Mr. Madrid admitted that achieving 7% growth means the industry will just be hitting the “baseline scenario” and not the aggressive targets. Based on 7% growth targets, the industry may end the year with 1.82mn in headcount and around US$38bn in revenues. In 2023, the industry employed 1.7mn individuals and generated US$35.5bn in export revenues. Under the roadmap, the industry is targeting to reach 2.5 million jobs and generate $59 billion in revenues by 2028. BPO firms occupy about 60%-80% of office space of the major listed developers. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 43% of Megaworld’s, and 28% of Robinsons Land’s.

DA chief seeks shorter period for rice tariff cut

Agriculture Secretary: rice tariff reduction should be between 6-12 months instead of the 5-year period saying it would affect rice farmers. Said tariff reduction must also have a regular review to review efficiency of the measure. Increase in domestic prices were said to be caused by external shocks (e.g. India’s ban on non-basmati rice exports) putting pressure on global rice supply. Agriculture Undersecretary proposed to revert rice tariffs to 50%, citing that eduction in tariff revenues could jeopardize enhancement projects for the rice industry. Members of Cabinet have not discussed a fiat aimed at cutting import duties on rice to 15% from the current 35%. Marcos has until July 21 to modify tariff rates through an EO. Recall that fnflation rose to 3.9% in May but food inflation slowed to 6.1%, with rice easing to 23%. Local regular milled rice is sold for P45-52 while well-milled rice is sold at P48-55, both higher than in the same period last year.

ERC allows staggered payment for WESM purchases

The ERC said purchases from the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) may now be settled via a four-month staggered payment, starting with bills payable this month of June to ease the impact of high generation costs. Meralco (MER PM) said that power rates for the month of June will be lowered due to the ruling. The average household tariff will be lowered to P9.4516/kwh from the original announced tariff for June 2024 of P12.0575/kwh. This also means that the tariff for June will be lower than the P11.4139/kwh the previous month.

Local LNG industry getting off the ground

Developments in liquefied natural gas (LNG) begin as private sector was given go-signal to proceed with construction of LNG facilities. The DOE has issued regulations for local LNG industry allowing it to contribute more to the country’s energy security. The department also issued regulations to support exploration and development of indigenous natural gas. As of now, there are 4 LNG companies that were issued permits, most of which projects involve floating storage and regasification units: FGEN LNG Corp., Linseed Field Corp., Luzon LNG Terminal Inc., Energy World Gas Operations Philippines Inc.

LNG development is essential in the exploration and development of RE resources in the PH. This should also foster more infrastructure development, technology transfer, job creation, and trade.

Nickel production to remain flat in 2024

The Philippine Nickel Industry Association (PNIA) expects the country’s nickel production to remain flat this year due to limited capacity in ore-supplying regions. Thus, the industry is pushing for the government to fast-track the approval of mining permits by establishing a “one-stop shop” application process. PNIA believes streamlining approvals will attract more investment in mining. About 470 applications are currently awaiting approval for exploration of copper, chromite, nickel, and cobalt, according to the Mines Geosciences Bureau. The phase 1 of the MGB’s priority list consists of metallic mines, with about 12 projects expected to start operations in the next six months. The projects include: magnetite sand (Iron) site in Region 2, nickel laterite in Region 3, gold in Region 5, four nickel, copper and gold sites in Region 11, and five nickel, copper and gold sites in Caraga. Regis estimates that Nickel Asia’s (NIKL PM) sales volumes will grow by 7% this year and 5% annually in succeeding years.

Government issues EO 59 guidelines to cut red tape in key infrastructure projects

The government signed and launched the implementing guidelines of Executive Order 59 (EO 59) that will streamline the permitting process for infrastructure flagship projects (IFPs). The salient features include:

·                     Reduction in the number of IFP-licensing agencies from 30 to just 12-18

·                     Mandating all national government agencies and local government units to remove burdensome requirements, limit signatories to max of three, and allow the use of e-signatures

·                     Establish a one-stop shop for IFPs that will serve as the single point of entry for all NEDA Board-approved IFPs

·                     Allow for simultaneous processing of applications through the submission of an Affidavit of Undertaking and mandate the automatic approval or renewal of documents if relevant agencies fail to act within 20 days

 

There are currently 185 IFPs with an indicative project cost of P9.5tn. Of the total, 31 projects worth P1.3tn have been approved by NEDA and are ready to be implemented, including:

·                     P181bn Laguna Lakeshore Network Dev’t Phase 1 (ODA-funded)

·                     P175bn PNR South Long Haul (ODA-funded)

·                     P219bn Bataan-Cavite Interlink Bridge (ODA-funded)

·                     P57bn MRT-4 (ODA-funded)

·                     P188bn Panay-Guimaras-Negros Inter-Island Link Bridge (ODA-funded)

VP Sara Duterte resigns from Cabinet post

Vice President Sara Duterte resigned from her post as a Member of the Cabinet, secretary of the Department of Education and vice chairperson of the National Task Force to End Local Communist Armed Conflict (NTF-ELCAC, effective July 19.
