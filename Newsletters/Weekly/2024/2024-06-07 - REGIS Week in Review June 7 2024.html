REGIS REPORT

07 June 2024

The PSEi found support this week after breaking below the crucial 6400-line and marking a new YTD low in the previous week. The index initially climbed to near 6500, then suffered some volatility and crumbled back below 6400 on Tuesday, dragged down by heavy outflows which exceeded P1b ($22m), the largest non-rebal outflow since May 9. Shares then managed to steadily recover from that point as investors digested the below-estimate CPI reading on the ground, and the beginning of rate cuts abroad (by Bank of Canada and ECB), which both support the case for a potential BSP rate cut in August. Flow-wise, foreign activity also turned skewed to buy over the last two sessions following an 8-day outflow streak. The benchmark ultimately capped the week at  6518 +1.33% WoW.

On the macro front, data released on Wednesday showed that inflation stood at 3.9% in May, which marks a a slight uptick from 3.8% in April but below the 4% consensus forecast and at the lower end of the BSP’s 3.7%-4.5% forecast range. Related to this, another key development this week was the government’s announcement of a tariff cut on rice imports from 35% to 15%, which will be effective until 2028. Our sense is that this should quickly result to a drop in rice prices of at least 15%, and as such provide a meaningful boost to consumer demand. We highlight consumer plays URC +0.47% WoW and PGOLD -1.19% as among the stocks that may benefit most out of this. Note that both names are still trading notably below historical average multiples (see our latest strategy piece below.)

Last week’s most battered sectors Financial +4.24% WoW and Property +2.99% led the recovery this time as the renewed optimism for rate cuts in 2H lifted overall market sentiment. The best performing stock in the benchmark was last week’s worst, MBT +12.01%, while other notable gainers that recovered from the previous selloff were BDO +7.62%, ALI +5.9% and SMPH +2.23%. Another name to note was AEV +9.84% with the headwind from its MSCI deletion now out of the way. Note that the company has also consistently bought back shares this week, totaling over 8m shares from Monday-Thursday disclosed so far (any buy-backs done today to be reported Monday.) That said, the broader Holding sector was in the red -1.62% WoW and was actually the worst performing sub-index this week, dragged by widespread losses: AGI -6.15%, DMC -5.04%, LTG -4.58%, JGS -3.23%, SMC -2.53%, and index heavyweight SM -3.45% which saw heightened volatility during the week, touching its lowest intraday level since October of P806/share on Wednesday.

Outside the main benchmark, another Aboitiz-held name was in focus - UBP +14.86% WoW, which started to rally following the completion of its P10b stock rights offering. Note that there’s some market chatter going around, speculating over its potential re-inclusion back into the PSEi but no official announcements have been made thus far. Note that its market cap currently stands at P128b, which already exceeds a handful of existing members in the benchmark. The next PSEi rebalancing is in early August, and changes for which will likely be announced in late July.

Market activity this week was quite soft given relative lack of leads outside of the May CPI print, and after last week’s rebal-inflated turnover. Average daily turnover stood at just $93m, -37.5% WoW, and mostly trended downwards throughout the week. Meanwhile net foreign flow remained skewed to sell but was much lighter this time at $36m vs last week’s $138m. Top foreign sold stocks were SM -3.45% WoW, AEV +9.84%, AC +0.25%, SMPH +2.23%, ALI +5.9%, GTCAP -0.33% and BPI -0.33%, while top bought were ICT +3.75%, MBT +12.01% and BDO +7.62%.

Philippine Strategy / Consumer - Rice is Life (2) - tariff cut to 15% from 35%, good for consumption, rates
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph | Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Rice prices could drop by 15% (or more) relatively quickly

The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA), with President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr acting as chair, approved a cut in rice tariffs from 35% currently, to 15%, effective until 2028. (Ironically, Marcos rejected the idea of tariff cuts in Sept 2023, at the start of the rice price surge.) The new tariff should result in a rapid 15% drop in the landed cost of imported rice prices. Meanwhile, the export price out of Vietnam – which supplies 80-90% of our rice imports – has fallen 11% YTD (-6% in PHP terms) and could push retail prices even lower.

Rice is life – at 8.9% of CPI, lower prices would offer relief to consumers…

The average retail price of rice in Metro Manila as of end-May has been flattish year-to-date. As a result, rice inflation has stayed stubbornly high, at around 24% YoY. Rice is 8.9% of the CPI basket – likely more for low-income households. A sharp drop in prices would be a welcome relief. In fact, rice inflation could turn negative in 3Q/4Q given the high base effect. 

BSP policy rate cuts – Will they or won’t they?

In early May, BSP Governor Remolona opened the door to possible policy rate cuts in 2H24, ahead of the US Fed. Since then, though, the PHP has weakened as  much as 3% against the USD. The BSP is mindful, though, of the potential  of a weak PHP feeding back into imported inflation.

How to play it? URC, PGOLD likely major beneficiaries

Consumer companies posted mixed results in 1Q24, as wider margins made up for soft volumes. Easing inflation could see a rebound in demand, boosting top-line. We continue to like Universal Robina (URC PM) as a beneficiary of looser consumer wallets. Its valuations are attractive, too, at a 2024E PER of 14x, compared to its historical average of 28x, and to peer average of 20x. Among the retailers, Puregold (PGOLD PM) has been hurt most by hard discount formats as consumers down-trade, a trend which could slow or reverse. The stock now trades at just 6x 2024E PER, compared to ASEAN peers averaging 20x.

 
ICTSI - Strong organic growth + PH expansions boost TP to P405/sh
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Maintain Buy

We upgrade our 12-month TP by 32% to P405/sh, after factoring in new expansions in the Philippines and better-than-expected yields at its ports in the Americas. Even though the stock has outperformed the PCOMP by 72% over the past year, valuations at 14x FY24E PER and 9x EV/EBITDA remain inexpensive relative to peers (17x/10x). Maintain Buy.

Pricing in new ports in the Philippines

The recently announced greenfield port expansion in Bauan, Batangas will become ICT’s second largest foreign container port upon full build-out of over 2mn TEUs. The port is well positioned to capitalize on the long-term limitations of incumbent ports in Manila, and to generate higher margins due to its more favorable cost structure. Meanwhile, the other recently acquired brownfield port in Iloilo requires minimal investment, but we believe the port has the potential to be a key gateway in the Visayas region long term.

Another round of forecast upgrades; Street likely to follow

We raise our FY24E-25E EPS forecasts by 11-12% after pricing in 7% higher yield per TEU estimates to US$205 for 2024E and US$217 for 2025E. Given that the yield improvement in 1Q24 to US$201 was largely driven by structural factors, we believe this level of yields can be sustained and may even improve further as more tariff hikes are implemented. Our forecasts remain the highest in the Street and we believe Street upgrades will follow.

Revised TP of P405/sh offers 27% total potential return

Our revised TP of P405/sh is 32% higher than our previous valuation after incorporating the accretive impact of its expansions in Bauan (+16%) and Iloilo (+2%), higher EBITDA forecasts (+8%), and a DCF roll-over to 2025 (+6%). Our TP offers 27% total potential return and implies 17x FY24E PER and 10x FY24E EV/EBITDA. We believe the stock should re-rate, given its strong growth profile and relatively low reinvestment rate. Downside risks relate to trade restrictions, macroeconomic/political risks, dilutive acquisitions, and labor issues. We note that our forecasts and valuation do not include the potential acquisition of DCT-2 in Durban, South Africa due to the absence of sufficient information.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

RCR to grow portfolio by 72% in property-for-share swap

RLC will acquire 279,000sqm of malls and 69,000sqm of offices from Robinsons Land via property-for-share swap. The 11 malls to be acquired will mostly be in provincial areas: Novaliches, Cainta, Luisita, Cabanatuan, Lipa, Sta. Rosa, Imus, Los Banos, Palawan, Ormoc, and Davao; while the offices to be acquired are: Giga Tower in Bridgetowne, Quezon City, and Cybergate Delta 2 in Davao City.

RLC will subscribe to 5bn primary RCR shares at P6.80/share (total of P33.9bn), which is 36% higher than the last closing price. RCR’s shares outstanding will expand by 47%. Regis estimates that the implied cap rate at the subscription price is about 7%. The acquisition would expand RCR’s portfolio size by 72% to 828,000sqm. After the acquisition, malls will comprise 34% of floor area while offices will account for the rest. AUM would also grow by 42% to P114bn. We estimate that the acquisition will be yield accretive for RCR. After the transaction, RLC’s ownership in RCR will rise from 50% to 66%.

Megaworld sells MREIT shares

Megaworld sold 79.7mn MREIT shares at P12.30/share, 3% of current shares outstanding and a 2% discount to the previous closing price. MREIT previously said it would acquire 156k sqm of office buildings via a property-for-share swap: McKinley West, Fort Bonifacio – 94,300sqm, Iloilo Business Park, Iloilo City– 36,200sqm, and Davao Park District, Davao City – 26,100sqm.MREIT will issue 926.16mn new shares to Megaworld at P14.20/share, bringing the total transaction value to P13.15bn. This will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 33% and its portfolio by 45% to 475,000sqm.

UnionBank completes P10bn stock rights offering

UnionBank completed its P10bn stock rights offering: 327mn rights shares at P30.57/share. 1 rights share for every 9.1382 existing common shares. Shares outstanding expanded 10% to 3.3bn. UBP’s principal shareholders – Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM), Social Security System, and Insular Life – fully participated in the rights offering. Proceeds will be used to fund the bank’s strategic growth initiatives.

ACEN eyes US$6bn equity raising for expansion

ACEN Corp. is looking at raising US$6bn in equity over the coming years to fund its US$15bn renewable energy expansion, according to CEO Eric Francia. The fund raising may include asset sales and issuance of new shares and the company is looking at a 40% equity and 60% debt (mostly bilateral loans) capital structure. The company could look at selling shares in the next 12-18 months.

ACEN is looking to quadruple its renewable power generation capacity to 20,000 MW by 2030. Its net attributable capacity stood at 4,852 MW in 1Q24, of which 3,177 MW were operational. In the Philippines alone, it has 1,147 MW of net attributable operating capacity and 765MW currently under construction or partially operational. As of end-March 2024, ACEN had P101bn of gross debt and P73bn of net debt (net debt-to-equity stood at 46%).

JFC to slow down China expansion

The company will open 100 stores in China this year, citing that it is cautious about increasing investments in China given macro challenges and low consumer confidence. Majority of the new stores will be located in tier 3 and 4 locations, where its brands are unrepresented, and will be primarily done through franchising. Note that JFC had 553 stores in China as of 1Q24.

Alliance Global President retires

Kingson Sian, President and COO of Alliance Global, retired on June 1. He will be succeeded as President by Kevin Tan, who is currently Vice chairman and CEO of AGI.      Mr. Sian also retired as CEO of Alliance Global’s subsidiary, Travellers International (operator of Newport Resorts). COO Hakan Dagtas and CFO Bernard Than also resigned on the same day. Recall that Mr. Dagtas and Mr. Than were previously executives of Star Cruises (of Genting Hong Kong). In 2022, Alliance Global (AGI PM) bought out the stake of Genting Hong Kong (678 HK) in Travellers.The former Chief Strategy Officer, Nilo Rodriguez, will take over as CEO, while the former Chief Casino Officer, Lance Gautreaux, will be the new COO.

Cebu Air targets for record 24mn pax volumes in 2024

Cebu Air is confident it can increase pax volumes by 15% YoY to an all-time high of 24mn this year, which will also be 7% above 2019 pax volumes of 22.5mn. This is mildly below our FY24E of 24.5mn. With better-than-expected 1Q24 pax fares of P3.2k, we reckon CEB could meet or even exceed Regis’ FY24 EBITDA of P27bn and the Street’s P24.1bn. CEB’s 2019 EBITDA stood at P28bn.

NexGen Energy IPO bid gets green light of SEC

SEC approves IPO of NexGen Energy Corp. (formerly known as Just Solar Corp.), a wind and solar RE developer: 300M common shares to be offered publicly at up to P1.68/share. Shares will be listed & traded on the small, medium, and emerging board of PSE. Selling shareholder Pure Energy Holdings Corp. will offer 45M shares for the same price.

NexGen expects to net P478.4M in proceeds which will be used to partially finance its RE projects in Zambales, Cavite, etc. China Bank Capital Corp. will be the sole issue manager and sole bookrunner for the offer, while Investment & Capital Corporation of the Philippines as joint lead underwriter. In 2023 NexGen recorded P21.6M profit (reversal from previous year’s loss of P22.79M). Electricity sales for 2023 fell by 15% (from P152.99M). Through subsidiary Solar Powered Agri-rural Communities Corp. or SPARC, NexGen currently operates 3 solar plants with an aggregate capacity of 13.859MWP.

Note that the PSE is targeting 5 IPOs, P175bn worth of capital to be raised this year. Another RE play CREC, which listed today, is second IPO for the year following OceanaGold (PH) Inc.’s P6.08B IPO last month.

Dito plans follow-on offering for up to P4.2bn

Dito disclosed that its board approved the sale of new shares equivalent to up to 10% of its capital stock (1.95bn shares). The offer price is up to P2.15/share, which implies the offer would raise a maximum of P4.2bn (<US$80m). The proceeds will be used for telecom business. Note that Dito had a capital deficit of P44bn (~US$800m) and interest-bearing debt of US$3bn as of 1Q. The interest expense for 1Q24 alone was P4.6bn, larger than the planned offering.

Inflation accelerates to 3.9% in May

Headline CPI rose 3.9% YoY in May, slightly faster than the 3.8% print in April, this was however a tad lower than consensus forecast of 4% and came in at the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.7%-4.5%. The mild uptick was due to faster transport inflation (+3.5% YoY in May vs. +2.6% in April) and a milder deflation in electricity CPI (-8.5% YoY in May vs -11% in April). Food inflation slowed to 5.8% from 6.0% as rice CPI dipped 0.2% MoM - the first MoM decrease in seven months, resulting in moderating rice inflation to +23.0% in May from 23.9% in April. Core inflation slowed further to 3.1% in May from 3.2% in April, with non-food “core” CPI moderating to 3.3% from 3.2%.

Headline inflation averaged at 3.5% in 5M24, trending lower than the BSP’s risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2024 is 3.8%. The BSP said the risks to the inflation outlook continue to lean toward the upside in the near-term due to the possible impact of adverse weather conditions on farm output. Nonetheless, the BSP expects full-year average inflation to settle within the target range of 2-4% for 2024 and 2025. Next BSP policy meeting is scheduled on June 27.

BSP Governor believes cutting rates by 150 basis points by end-2025 would be “too aggressive”

BSP Governor Remolona said that reducing rates by 150 basis points by end-2025 would be too aggressive based on the economy’s current trajectory. Recall that Finance Secretary Ralph Recto previously said that he expected the BSP to cut rates by 150 basis points by the end of next year.

Lower rice tariffs to bring down retail prices as early as July

Slashing tariffs on imported rice could bring avg retail prices to P6-P7/kilo as early as July. Note that current prices of imported rice: P52-P55 (well-milled), P49-P51 (regular milled). NEDA approved a medium-term plan to lower tariffs on agri and industrial products, which includes the reduction in rice tariffs from 35% to 15% until 2028. The DOF is willing to forego an estimated P10bn in tariff collections to lower rice prices; while the DA will be plugging potential funding gaps for the RCEF. The DA is seeking to extend RCEF and bring back regulatory powers to NFA to bring down rice prices.

Commercial bank loan growth mildly accelerates to 9.6% YoY in April

Preliminary data showed commercial bank loan growth accelerated to 9.6% YoY in April from 9.4% the previous month. Loans for production activities expanded by 7.8% in April from 7.7% in March. Due to faster loan growth to wholesale, retail trade (+7.6% from +6.6%) and transport (+22% from +14%). Consumer loan growth was steady at 25%, with credit cards up 29% and auto loans up +21%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) mildly decelerated to 5.6% in April from 5.7% in March.

Department of Energy eyeing P31tn clean energy investment by 2040

The country needs to invest P20-31tn in renewable energy investment by 2040 to achieve its targets. By 2040, the country wants to achieve 5% energy savings, 10% electric vehicle penetration and 50% RE share in the generation mix, according to Energy Undersecretary Rowena Guevara. Based on the demand, the target is to achieve a total capacity of over 52.8 gigawatts of RE, with 27 GW of solar, 16.6 GW of wind, six GW of hydro, 2.5 GW of geothermal and 360 megawatts of biomass. However, the country faces several challenges, including financing, permitting, and grid interconnection issues. Currently, our transition system is able to support about 28 GW of generation, but in order to move 52.8 GW of RE by 2040, we have to almost double our transmission system.

Gov't eyes PPP auction of big-ticket railway projects in 1Q25

The government is hoping to auction off the O&M contracts for the following railway projects by 1Q25: MRT-3 – No indicative amount given, Metro Manila Subway – P72bn, and North-South Commuter Railway – P164bn.
