REGIS REPORT

11 October 2024

The PSEi posted its first decline in 6 weeks, -2.13% WoW to 7308.74, with the market going into consolidation mode after its sharp +17% run from August lows, ahead of some key events on the ground - i.e. BSP policy decision next week and the start of 3Q earnings season later this month. The weakness in the market also comes as foreign investors’ long 27-day buying streak, which netted over $400m in inflows and tipped the YTD net flow to the positive ($78m currently), took a pause and turned lightly skewed to sell over the last 3 sessions.

Initially, the benchmark rallied past 7500 and marked its best level since January 2020 at the start of the week, before steadily falling through the subsequent sessions as investors assessed the interest rate outlook in the US where the latest strength in economic data and an above-estimate CPI put to question the certainty of a Fed cut in the next November meeting. Note that futures are currently pricing in an 85% chance of a 25bp cut on November 7 after a 50bp cut in the last meeting. With this, the peso sharply weakened -1.63% WoW to 57.22. Another outside headwind for the local market is foreign investors’ heightened focus on China which resumed trading on Tuesday after a week-long break.

On the ground, investors are awaiting the upcoming BSP policy decision due on Wednesday post-market. Officials are widely expected to cut the benchmark rate by another 25bps to 6.00% (10  out of 12 entries in Bloomberg as of writing; 1 forecast of a -50bp move). This is after a dovish CPI reading of 1.9% in September, which was the lowest print since April 2020 and more importantly was below the 2%-4% target range of the BSP. Beyond upcoming policy meeting, investors are also anticipating the 3Q earnings season set to begin in late October. However, data from property consultant Leechiu released during the week cast some concerns over the earnings outlook of real estate developers as it showed an increase in Metro Manila condo supply and a decline in office take-ups due to net closures of POGOs (see macro news clip below). With this, large caps ALI -4.46% WoW and SMPH -2.93% both tumbled this week.

The selling in the market was broad based 2/3 of index members in the red (and 40% down by over -3%). Other big laggards include NIKL -8.44% WoW which reversed last week’s gains, WLCON -7.32% amid a tempered outlook for upcoming results, JGS -4.36%, BPI -4.38% and MONDE -4.26%. That said, there were a few bright spots such as MER +3.24%, which marked a fresh all-time high during the week, and PGOLD +2.56%. Note that news on MER’s 9M2024 sales volumes, which grew by +7.1% YoY, implied a continued but slower growth +4% YoY in 3Q vs +8.7% in 1H.

Daily average turnover this week was virtually the same as last week at $111m. Note however that after a strong and active session on Monday, activity on the ground has visibly toned down. In fact, volumes in the last 3 sessions have stood at the sub-$100m level. Foreign investor participation for the week eased to 46% vs 53% in the prior week, potentially reflecting the shift in focus to China and uncertainty over the Fed’s next move. Net foreign flow remained skewed to buy but was relatively lighter at +$22m, as foreign investors turned better sellers (almost neutral) over the past few sessions.

RESEARCH
ICTSI - Durban acquisition setback
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Court issues injunction against deal

The Durban High Court has interdicted state-owned Transnet from taking any steps to implement its decision to award the 25-year concession for Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2) to ICTSI. The injunction will stay until the hearing of "Part B” takes place and the case is resolved. Transnet said that it is committed to the judicial process and is reviewing its options. Meanwhile, ICT said it will continue to pursue its legal rights.

Tender process “irregularities” on technical submission

A losing bidder, APM Terminals, a subsidiary of Maersk, complained that ICT was allowed to use its market capitalization instead of book equity in computing for its solvency ratio during the first stage of the bidding process. ICT would not have passed the minimum solvency ratio criteria of 0.4 if it had used its book equity. Transnet argued, however, that the solvency requirement was not mandatory and had been intended to simplify the proof of a financial capacity.

Superior financial bid

Court documents revealed that ICT was selected as the preferred bidder after it submitted a financial offer of ZAR11.1bn (~US$630mn), ZAR2bn (~20%) higher than APM’s offer. This offer would have allowed ICT to own a 50% stake less 1 share in the DCT-2 concession.

Dilutive at the onset but value creation opportunities could be meaningful

ICT’s bid suggests an enterprise value of US$1.26bn for the DCT-2 contract, which we estimate translates to an EV/EBITDA of ~17x, higher than ICT’s valuation 8.6x. We estimate the acquisition would have added US$211mn (6%) of revenues and US$74mn of EBITDA (3%) on a consolidated or 100% basis in the first full year of operation. Even though the deal is unlikely to be meaningfully accretive at the onset, we believe ICT has a lot of room to create value over the 25-year contract, particularly on efficiency and tariff improvements.

Durban not in forecasts/valuation

We have not factored in the acquisition of DCT-2 in our forecasts and valuation. We also note that ICT has not paid its financial offer yet and any future provisions from this setback could be negligible. It is possible that Transnet could just opt to re-bid the contract and set a minimum offer of ZAR11.1bn. A re-bid, however, could prolong the privatization process.

Maintain Buy

Despite this setback, we believe investors can take comfort from several likely catalysts: (a) expansion in congested market of Mexico, (b) second-tranche tariff hike (+6%) in MICT in Feb 2025, (c) revenue recovery in Ecuador by 4Q, and (d) consensus upgrades. The stock remains inexpensive at 8.6x FY25E EV/EBITDA, below peer average (11x) and historical average (11x).

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI acquires 27-hectare property for Batangas expansion

ICTSI purchased a 27-hectare property in San Roque and San Andres, Bauan, Batangas, which is adjacent to its Bauan Port property and will be used for its expansion plans. The purchase price was not disclosed. The acquisition forms part of the company’s US$800mn greenfield expansion for the Bauan International Port, Inc (BIPI), which will add over 2mn TEUs of capacity upon full build-out. The initial capex will be US$300mn to be spent between 2025-2027 to build an 850k-TEU port. Regis estimates that BIPI will account for 1% of EBITDA on its first full year of operation; Upon reaching 100% utilization of 2mn TEU capacity, we forecast BIPI will account for 11% of EBITDA (US$380mn of 2037E EBITDA of US$3.5bn). Regis believes this investment addresses the long-term capacity and concession limits of the two ports in Manila.

BSP okays BPI’s unloading of GoTyme shares

BPI was given the go signal by BSP to sell its 15% stake in Gokongwei-backed GoTyme Bank for P902.4mn. Notice of approval was given on Oct. 9. The transaction involves the sale of 752.06mn shares for P1.20 apiece to GoTyme Financial Pte Ltd. And Giga Investments Holdings Inc. The sale is intended to address any potential conflict of interest created by significant overlap in and similarly of product offerings of GoTyme Bank and BPI. The Go Tyme Shares were acquired by BPI pursuant to the merger between BPI and Robinsons Bank Corporation (“RBank”) with BPI as the surviving bank. GoTyme Financial is a subsidiary of JG Summit (JGS PM). JGS owns 3.58% of BPI.

Meralco electricity sales volumes rise 7.1% YoY in 9M24

Meralco’s electricity sales rose by 7.1% YoY to 40,331 GWH in 9M24. Residential segment grew volumes by 10%; Commercial segment grew 8%; Industrial segment rose by 2%. In September 2024 alone, electricity sales volumes grew by “over 3%” YoY to 4,553 GWH. This implies that in 3Q24 alone, MER’s sales volumes were up 4% YoY to ~13,900 GWH, after rising by 8.7% YoY in 1H24. Meralco is guiding for a core income of P43bn this year, with 1H24 “core” profit at P23.2bn (+21% YoY). At the current share price, guidance implies 12.2x PER. The company will be holding its 3Q24 briefing on October 28 at 3:30pm.

Ayala Corp. secures US$200mn loan from Metrobank

Ayala Corp. has secured a US$200mn senior long-term loan from Metrobank. To fund the expansion of the conglomerate’s investments in technology and payment, health, logistics, and mobility. This loan will support Ayala Corp efforts to sharpen their portfolio and allocate capital to clear business winners, says Alberto M. de Larrazabal, the CFO of Ayala Corp. As at end-June 2024, AC’s parent-level net debt stood at P171.6bn (inclusive of P25.2bn prefs).

MPTC- SMC Tollways merger to be finalized this month

The merger between Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) and San Miguel Corp. (SMC) is expected to be finalized this month, according to MPTC President Rogelio Singson. This is earlier than previous pronouncement of a 1Q25 completion due to the finalization of MPTC’s acquisition of a 35% stake in the Trans-Java toll road network in Indonesia. However, merger will need to go through regulatory approvals, including the approval of the Philippine Competition Commission. Ownership in the merged company will be equally split between MPTC and SMC. IPO plans for the merged entity have not been determined yet.

Cebu Air reportedly buys Ayala Land’s AirSWIFT for P1.75bn

Cebu Air (CEB PM) and Ayala Land’s ALI Capital are reportedly scheduled to sign a share purchase agreement to acquire all ALI’s shares in AirSWIFT for P1.75bn. The acquisition boosts CEB’s turboprop fleet to 20 from five, as well as opens a new market for CEB in El Nido, Palawan. AirSWIFT operates from Manila and Clark to El Nido, as well as provides inter-island connectivity to Boracay, Cebu, Coron, and Panglao from El Nido. ALI Capital will remain the owner and operator of the Lio Airport in El Nido AirSwift. Regis anticipates that CEB will rationalize AirSwift’s operations and remove duplicate route.

PSE approves listing of DigiPlus shares

The PSE approved the listing of 691.2mn DigiPlus shares. There will be no change to shares outstanding as these shares were already issued and outstanding as of 2023. The company approved the issuance of 691.2mn new shares in 2022 to the following: Clearspring Holdings Corp (330.6mn shares), Belvedere Skies Asset Holding OPC (330.6mn shares), and Tucket Holdings (30mn shares). The shares were purchased at P2.68/share.

DigiPlus receives approval to operate in Brazil until year-end

DigiPlus (PLUS PM) is one of the 93 companies to have met the regulatory compliance of Brazil and gained approval to operate in Brazil. These 93 companies will be authorized to operate in Brazil until December 31, 2024. At the end of this transition period (Now to December 31, 2024), the Ministry of Finance will release the final list of companies and websites that will be authorized to operate from January 1, 2025, when Brazil’s regulated betting market will commence.

Filinvest Land tender offer to begin on October 7

Filinvest Land provided the relevant dates for its tender offer: Tender offer period – October 7 to November 27, Cross date – December 9, and Settlement Date – December 11. Filinvest Land’s Board of Directors approved a tender offer for up to 1.866bn FLI shares, which represents 7.7% of FLI’s outstanding shares and 24% of float shares. FLI shares will be exchanged for FILRT shares at a ratio of 0.32 FILRT shares for every 1 FLI share tendered. 597mn FILRT shares will be exchanged for 1.866bn FLI shares – this is equivalent to 12.2% of FILRT shares outstanding.

Kevin Tan appointed as Executive Director of Megaworld

Katherine Tan stepped down as Director of Megaworld due to personal reasons. The Board of Directors subsequently appointed her son, Kevin Tan, as member of the Board and Executive Director. Kevin Tan is Executive Vice President and Chief Strategy Officer of Megaworld, President of MREIT, and Vice Chairman and President of Megaworld’s parent, Alliance Global. Regis does not expect material changes to Megaworld due to event.

Dennis Uy’s Comclark bids to operate the country’s air traffic control system

Dennis Uy’s Comclark network and Technology Corp. is courting the government to approve its P29.82bn bid to operate and maintain the country’s air navigation facilities. The Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines (CAAP) seems interested in the project and has decided in August to proceed with the detailed evaluation. Comclark, which is also the parent of Converge ICT Solutions Inc., wants to build new facilities for communications, navigation, surveillance, and air traffic management (CNS-ATM). The government has started considering the option of turning over the CNS-ATM to a private operator since 2023.

Jaime Alfonso Zobel de Ayala appointed to committee seeking to increase foreign investments

Mr. Jaime Alfonso Zobel de Ayala, who is a director of Globe Telecom and CEO of AC Mobility, has been appointed as Private Sector Representative from the National Capital Region of the Inter-Agency Investment Promotion Coordination Committee. The Committee is under the Department of Trade and Industry and is tasked with improving foreign investments.

SPNEC to inject P8.9bn into subsidiaries for solar expansion

Solar Philippines Nueva Ecija Corporation (SPNEC) is set to infuse P89bn into two of its subsidiaries to accelerate solar farm developments. Its board of directors approved a subscription of P6.03bn to Terra Solar whilst also an additional P2.9bn subscription towards Terra Nueva. The capital infusion of Terra Nueva will be used for ‘land and right-of-way acquisition and land conversion cost.’ The 3500 hectare Terra Solar farm will be owned by Terra Nueva, which will enter into a lease agreement with Terra Solar to cover the property. Last month, SPNEC sealed a US$600mn deal with UK investment house Actis, which is equivalent to a 40% stake in Terra Solar.

Leviste-led Solar Philippines faces risk of losing 21 government contracts

Half of the total 42 contracts the government awarded to Solar Philippines could get cancelled due to the lack of complying timelines. Solar Philippines, which was created in 2013, had committed itself to 10000MW of solar power, yet only 174.41MW, equivalent to 2% of its target have been operational. Some other solar developers only need two or three years two complete, whilst it is taking Solar Philippines longer just solely for the development phase, which excludes the construction timeline. New requirements are now posed onto Solar developers regarding constructing projects, such as a prerequisite for developers to have possessory rights of the land before service contracts can be offered. The government is still aiming to increase its share in renewable energy to 50% by 2040, from 22% as of now.

SEC clears P2.87bn Topline IPO

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has approved the planned P2.87bn initial public offering (IPO) of Cebu-based fuel retailer Top Line Business Development Corp. (Topline). The IPO will consist of up to 3.68 billion primary common shares at a maximum price of 78 centavos per share, and an overallotment option of 368.31 million secondary common shares. The company will not receive proceeds from the exercise of the overallotment option. The offer period will be from Nov. 6 to 12, with listing on the main board of the Philippine Stock Exchange by Nov. 22. Topline aims to generate up to P2.75bn in net proceeds from the sale of primary offer shares, which will fund the construction of fuel depots and service stations, acquisition of fuel tankers and tank trucks, working capital, as well as for general corporate purposes.

Inflation now on a ‘target-consistent path’ says Remolona

According to BSP Governor Eli M, Remolona, inflation is now on a “target-consistent path” which provides room for further easing. Governor Remolona stated that BSP now has room for a more calibrated shift to a less restrictive monetary policy stance. Recall that Governor Remolona earlier said that the Monetary Board can cut by up to 25 bps at each of its last two meetings this year, slated on Oct. 16 and Dec. 19. BSP kicked off its easing cycle in August with a 25bp rate cut, bringing back the benchmark rate to 6.25% from an over 17-year high of 6.5%. BSP chief noted that cutting ahead of the Fed did not have any significant impact on the financial system.

Metro Manila condominium supply is equivalent to 29 months of sales

Leechiu Property Consultants said that there are currently 67,600 condominium units available for sale in Metro Manila. This is equivalent to 29 months’ worth of sales. LPC said that the unsold inventory level is typically closer to 12 months’ worth of sales. Several developers have experienced weakness in Metro Manila condominium sales and have reduced launch plans. The major developers have (nationwide) unsold inventory levels equivalent to 18 to 24 months of reservation sales.

LPC: Office take-up declines in 3Q24

During its 3Q24 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy was flat QoQ (at 17%), while Metro Manila office vacancy was also flat QoQ (at 16%). Regis estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Nationwide net office take-up fell 75% QoQ (down 47% YoY) to 68,000sqm - BPO net office take-up fell 51% QoQ and YoY to 51,000sqm, take-up from traditional tenants (including government agencies) declined 52% QoQ (up 400% YoY) to 70,000sqm, while POGO demand reversed from net take-up of 20,000sqm in 2Q24 to net closures of 53,000sqm.

FX loans, deposits drop QoQ

Last week, the BSP released its latest data for banks’ foreign currency deposit units (FCDUs), which showed 2Q2024 FCDU loans at $15.6bn, up 1.6% YoY, slowing from the 4.0% pace in 1Q24. The figure was also down 2.7% QoQ from 1Q24 levels ($16.1bn).

More striking, in our view, was that FX deposits dropped 5.9% QoQ to $55.2bn. On a YoY basis, growth slowed sharply from 20.3% YoY in 1Q24 to just 12.6% YoY in 2Q24. We have been watching this closely as it could indicate sentiment towards the PHP assets. Recall that by mid-June, the talk of a Fed rate cut had begun to re-intensify, signaling the start of the “weak dollar” narrative. It also marked the start of the PSEi rally. We would be surprised if the FCDU deposit trend did not continue into 3Q, considering the USD’s softness.

As a result of the FCDU contraction, FX loans as a share of total loans dipped to 6.6% in 2Q24 from 6.8% in the previous quarter. FX deposits accounted for 16.9% of total, compared to 17.5% in 1Q24. On an LDR basis,  The drop would have been more dramatic, had it not been for the weak PHP which depreciated 4% QoQ in 2Q24.

FX LDRs climbed to 28.3% from 27.4% in 1Q24. This compares to overall LDR of 70.9%, suggesting that the PHP LDR was 79.4%.

Commercial banks’ loan growth accelerates to 10.7% in August

Loans of universal/commercial banks grew 10.7% YoY in August, slightly faster than the 10.4% pace in July. Due to faster growth in manufacturing, trade, utilities, and real estate. Consumer loan growth moderated to 23.7% from 24.3%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth slowed to 5.5% in August from 7.3% in July.

Banks’ bad loan ratio flat at 3.68% in August

Preliminary data showed that the NPL ratio of Philippine banks (net of interbank) was steady at 3.68% in August. NPLs (net of interbank) rose to P512bn in August, up 15.8% YoY and 0.9% MoM. Total loans (net of interbank) rose by 10.3% YoY, 0.8% MoM to P13.91tn in August (+10.3% pace in July). NPL coverage ratio was at 94.11% in August from 94.32% in July. Past due ratio climbed to 4.42% in August from 4.40% in July. Past due loans rose by 0.9% MoM to P531bn. Restructured loan ratio was steady at 2.05%, with restructured loans up 0.7% MoM to P293bn.

GIR climbs to new record of US$112bn in end-September

Preliminary data showed that the country’s GIR rose by US$4.1bn (+3.8%) MoM to US$112bn at the end of September. Prior to this, the GIR peaked at US$108.8bn in Dec 2021. Cited the rise in the national government’s foreign currency deposits, which included proceeds from the issuance of US$2.5bn global bonds in August. Increase was also due to upward valuation adjustments in gold holdings and higher net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. BSP also gained US$1.2bn MoM from FX trades. The GIR is sufficient to cover 8.1 months’ worth of imports.

Government’s infrastructure budget for 2025 down by 0.3% YoY

For 2025, the proposed budget for infrastructure is P1.507 trillion, down 0.3% from funding levels in 2024. Government is counting on PPPs to play a bigger role in funding. Note that the government projected expenses for debt service will rise by 27% YoY to P848bn. With interest rates falling, the government could potentially generate interest savings, which could be re-allocated without the need for Congress’ approval.

Trade deficit widens to US$4.4bn in August

Preliminary data showed that the country’s trade deficit widened by 7% YoY to US$4.38bn. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit in August was lower than the US$4.9bn deficit in July. Exports were flat YoY at US$6.75bn, as electronic exports fell 8% and offset the 12% improvement in non-electronic exports. Imports rose 3% YoY to US$11.1bn due to 10% YoY increase in capital goods imports and 5% higher raw material imports. Consumer goods imports were steady at US$2.24bn while fuel imports fell by 9%. Electronic imports rebounded, rising by 9% YoY and could lead to a rebound in exports in succeeding periods. This brings YT-August trade deficit to US$34.3bn, down 4% YoY.

Sari-sari stores continue to dominate business registrations in the country

Businesses engaged in wholesale and retail trade, many of which are small neighborhood shops or “sari-sari stores”, made up 55.43% of a total 923k business name registrations and renewals filed as of October 6, according to data from the Department of Trade and Industry. New business registrations and renewals are up 2% from the 903k recorded in 10M23. This was followed by accommodation and food service (14%), manufacturing (5%), and real estate (4%). Data also showed that Region IV-A continued to lead the highest number of registrations at 175k (18% of total), followed by NCR (16%).

New SEC guidelines expected to boost energy sector investments

The SEC is planning to launch early next year, a new set of guidelines to further help propel the country’s power sector; Proposed changes include: a 45-day period to complete the review of registration for power generation and distribution companies; to waive the public float requirement of 20% for listed companies, and now in favor towards a 15% minimum instead under EPIRA.

PPA reviewing processes to stop importers from using ports for storage

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) is coordinating with the BoC to streamline their processes to prevent ports from becoming storage areas. This came after the PPA said hundreds of rice containers, chicken meat, pork, and onions have been unclaimed and overstaying at Manila port. Importers have a 30-day window to remove their cargo once it has been cleared by the BoC; otherwise, the goods will be considered condemned. The PPA said it has increased its monitoring of pork, chicken, and onion inventories in preparation for the Christmas season.
