REGIS REPORT

20 September 2024

Philippine shares sharply advanced this week, sending the PSEi to its best close since early 2022, as investors digested the FOMC’s 50bp rate cut which evidently drove appetite for risk assets, and also solidifies the chances of another cut (or possibly even 2) by the BSP in its 2 remaining policy meetings before the year ends. On the ground, the BSP added further tailwind to the market by announcing a sizable 250bp cut in banks’ minimum reserve requirement ratio on Friday, shortly after BSP Governor Remolona reiterated his desire to “substantially” reduce the RRR (see more on this on Rafa’s note below.) Most markets in Asia likewise advanced but the PSEi stood out as one of the better performers, +3.27% WoW to 7252.32, our best close since March 2022. This now brings the benchmark’s nominal YTD gain to +12.44%. Alongside the rally in stocks, the PHP also sharply strengthened, +0.56% WoW to 55.678, back to the same levels at the start of the year and +5.42% from June lows. As such the PSEi in dollar terms is similarly up by about +12% YTD.

Breadth of the market was skewed to the positive with over 80% of index members ending the week in the green. Top gainer in the benchmark was NIKL +13.44% WoW, which showed signs of reversal from its over -30% decline YTD. The strength in the battered small/mid-cap name, and among a handful of other stocks, suggests that investors are scooping up value plays amid a decidedly risk-on mood across the entire market. Other notable gainers in the benchmark were larger cap AC +11.29%, which was among the top inflow names during the week, consumer plays URC +10.14%, JFC +6.3% and MONDE +6.17%, and lenders BPI +7.37% and MBT +4.86% amid the RRR cut announcement.

On the opposite end, SMC -5.03% WoW fared the worst of all PSEi members, dragged primarily by its deletion from the FTSE mid-cap index. Another notable laggard was ICT -1.85%, which is arguably one of the most favored names in the market through most of the year. Its weakness potentially also reflects investors’ rotation into more value-oriented plays. With this, note that a number of smaller cap stocks outside the benchmark posted sizable gains this week: SECB +15.71%, FNI +13.6%, CEB +12.17%, UBP +8.81%, RCB +8.55%, DNL +7.62%, among others.

Average daily turnover this week stood at a heavy $151m, +27% WoW and about +50% more than the YTD usual. This was however largely driven by the eventful $305m session on Friday amid the FTSE rebal and a $49m AREIT placement. Average daily turnover prior to Friday stood at $112m. Foreign buying continued all through the week, netting an inflow of nearly $85m ($11m of which from the AREIT placement) and bringing the inflow streak to 15 consecutive days. This cuts down our YTD net outflow to -$169m – note however that if excluding the nearly $300m outflow block in BLOOM related to the GGAM settlement, net foreign flow to our market would actually be in the positive territory already.

Philippine Strategy - Enter the Dragon (2) – Fed cuts by 50bps; BSP eyes “substantial” RRR cuts
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Doves (and Dragons) all around – staying bullish on Philippine equities

Our bullish thesis for 2024 is playing out. The US Fed delivered a long-awaited rate cut (50bps) overnight. The median “dots” suggest another 50bps in cuts by yearend, and a further 100bps in 2025E. This gives the BSP greater latitude in its own policy rate setting, as we see inflation slowing sharply in coming months. Adding to the dovish tone, the BSP said it could reduce reserve requirements (RRR) “substantially” this year, and into 2025E. Despite the PSEi Index’s 17% rally from the June lows, we believe the market still has legs, given solid earnings growth, modest valuations, and low foreign ownership.

BSP gets more elbow room – more rate cuts / “substantial” RRR cuts coming

The BSP’s 25bp rate cut last month (to 6.25%) narrowed the premium over the Fed funds rate to an all-time low <100bps, from the ten-year average gap of around 200bps. The current and expected Fed cuts give the BSP more room for further monetary easing. We also see inflation slowing sharply – likely below 3% in Sept/Oct – on lower rice and oil prices, and reduce our FY24E inflation to 3.4% (from 3.5%). We revise our forecast for the BSP’s policy rate to 5.75% (from 6.00%) by end-2024E and 5.00% (from 5.50%) by end-2025E. the bond markets are already discounting this, with the 10-year BVAL falling below 6.0% this week (Fig 2). Meanwhile, the BSP intends to reduce the RRR on banks’ PHP deposits “substantially” before yearend, with further cuts likely in 2025E.

Market valuations still modest, despite 17% rally

The PSEi has rallied hard, up 17% from the June lows, in anticipation of the Fed cuts (Fig 1). Despite the move, we estimate the 12-month forward PER at just 11.6x PER, not far off the multi-decade lows, and well below the long-term averages (Fig 3). Meanwhile, 1H24 earnings were in line with our FY24E forecasts of 11% EPS growth (c15% excluding mining stocks).

Foreign flows turn positive; but ownership still light; will locals feel the FOMO?

Foreigners turned net buyers to the tune of over $300mn since end-June (Fig 4). YTD we estimate net inflow of c$70mn, potentially the first full year of net buying since 2017. But positioning remains light, with foreign ownership of listed stocks still close to all-time lows, with the vast majority of flows going to large caps (Fig 5). Meanwhile, local AUMs have grown rapidly (Fig 6), but anecdotally have yet to shift allocation meaningfully from fixed income into equities.

Enter the Dragon (2) – How to play it?

Despite our fundamental concerns, property names Ayala Land (ALI PM) and SM Prime (SMPH PM) are the clear thematic. We remain overweight banks as RRR cuts and loan growth could offset NIM compression. Mid-cap banks like Security Bank (SECB PM) could benefit from lower rates, while Metrobank’s (MBT PM) treasury portfolio could see outsized trading gains. The consumer sector could see volumes recover in 2H24 and into 2025 as inflation fades; Jollibee (JFC PM) and Universal Robina (URC PM) are our top picks. While conglos have been out of favor, Ayala Corp (AC PM) and GT Capital (GTCAP PM) have significantly lagged their listed constituent parts. Among mid-caps, Robinsons Land (RLC PM), Wilcon (WLCON), and Puregold (PGOLD PM) could see valuation catch-ups.

 
Philippine Banks - RRR slashed 250bps, will help defend NIMs as interest rates fall
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

“Substantial”, indeed – could add c4% to our NPAT forecasts

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) will cut banks’ deposit reserve requirement (RRR) from 9.5% to 7.0%, effective 25 October. On a full-year basis, we estimate that the move could add 6-8bps to bank NIMs, and increase NPAT by around 4%, based on our current 2025E numbers. However, our forecasts do not yet incorporate potentially faster reduction in policy rates. On balance, we view the RRR cuts as positive, especially if the BSP reduces reserve requirements further in 2025.

RRR cut 250bps to 7.0%, just days after promising “substantial” reductions

The BSP cut the RRR for PHP deposits of universal and commercial banks (UKBs) by 250bps to 7.0%, from 25 October. The move comes just days after Governor Eli Remolona said he would “substantially” reduce the RRR this year, with further cuts likely in 2025. The regulator has for many years now expressed its desire to bring the ratio down (potentially to zero) over time. That said, the timing and magnitude of the current cut was still a bit of a surprise.

Could raise NIMs by 6-8bps, NPAT by c4%, but… it’s complicated

The 250bps cut will release more than P400bn (c$7bn) into the financial system. We assume simplistically that banks place the freed-up funds in PHP sovereigns, currently yielding just under 6%. We estimate that banks we cover will see NIMs expand by 6.3-7.7bps, and NPAT rise by 3.5-4.2%, based on our 2025E numbers, all else held equal.

The reality, though, could be more nuanced. Our current forecasts do not yet consider our expectation of larger cuts in policy interest rates. To the upside, lower rates and higher liquidity could boost loan demand, and reduce competition for deposits. On balance, the 250bp RRR – and potential future cuts – can only be viewed positively, bolstering our positive stance on the sector.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

RCR receives SEC approval for share-for-property swap

RCR received the Certificate of Approval of Valuation from the SEC for its previously announced share-for-property transaction. RLC will acquire 279,000sqm of malls and 69,000sqm of offices from Robinsons Land via property-for-share swap. The 11 malls to be acquired will mostly be in provincial areas: Novaliches, Cainta, Luisita, Cabanatuan, Lipa, Sta. Rosa, Imus, Los Banos, Palawan, Ormoc, and Davao. The offices to be acquired are: Giga Tower in Bridgetowne, Quezon City, and Cybergate Delta 2 in Davao City.

RLC will subscribe to 5.0bn primary RCR shares at P6.80/share (total of P33.9bn). RCR’s shares outstanding will expand by 47%. We estimate that the acquisition price would yield a cap rate of about 7%. At the current price of P5.76/share, RCR would have a market capitalization of P91bn, higher than RLC’s market cap of P77bn. The acquisition would expand RCR’s portfolio size by 72% to 828,000sqm. After the acquisition, malls will comprise 34% of floor area while offices will account for the rest. AUM would also grow by 42% to P114bn. After the transaction, RLC’s ownership in RCR will rise from 50% to 66%.

Metro Pacific reportedly seeking foreign partners to buy out KKR-led consortium in Metro Pacific Hospital Holdings

Metro Pacific is reportedly seeking partners, which are “preferably foreign”, to buy the 80% stake of the KKR-led consortium in Metro Pacific Hospital Holdings, Inch (MPHHI), according to media reports. Metro Pacific plans to increase its stake in MPHHI by up to 25%; It currently owns 20% of MPHHI. Metro Pacific reportedly wants to have the largest stake after the buyout. The KKR-led consortium was previously reported to be asking for a valuation of US$3bn for 100% enterprise value of MPHHI. Recall that the KKR consortium bought its 80% stake in MPHHI in 2019 at a 100% equity valuation of P77.8bn (US$1.5bn).

Meralco subsidiary combines telco tower assets into new firm

Meralco subsidiary MIESCOR is combining its telco tower operations under MIDC with those of the Philtower Consortium. MIDC is a partnership between MIESCOR and Stonepeak, an alternative investment firm focused on infrastructure. The Philtower Consortium is a partnership between Macquarie Capital and Global Network Inc. MIDC previously agreed to acquire 2,180 towers from Globe in 2022. The Philtower Consortium agreed to buy 1,350 towers from Globe in the same year. The combined entity will be the largest independent telecom tower firm with over 3,300 towers. MIESCOR will hold 25% interest in the combined telco tower entity.

ICTSI (ICT PM) Brazil volume update

Volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) two ports in Brazil, Tecon Suape and ICTSI Rio, continue to grow at a blistering pace in July.  These two ports have been benefiting from a combination of good macroeconomic conditions and market share gains. The latter was a result of management’s investments in several depots near industrial areas over the pandemic, which made it easier for trucks to transport containers directly to ICT’s ports. We estimate these ports combined account for 6% of group volumes and 7% of consolidated revenues.

Tecon Suape saw volumes jump 132% YoY, 12% MoM to a new record of 58.6k TEUs.  This brings 7M24 volumes up 84% YoY to 362k TEUs, well on track to beat our full-year forecast of 555k TEUs (+62%).

ICTSI Rio also saw record volumes in July of 27k TEUs, up 123% YoY and 44% MoM.  Cumulative 7M24 volumes are up by 60% to 156k TEUs, trending well ahead of our full-year forecast of 235k TEUs (+30%).

PLDT appoints new COO

The board of PLDT approved the appointment of Menardo Jimenez, Jr as EVP and COO. Jimenez has served in various positions within the PLDT group over more than 20 years, most recently as Network Head. His appointment to the COO role could mean reduced day-to-day involvement by CEO Manuel Pangilinan. Pangilinan has many other roles across his entire group (Meralco, Metro Pacific, First Pacific, etc).

Globe appoints new CFO

Globe announced the appointment of Juan Carlo Puno as CFO effective Oct 16, 2024. Puno is current Senior Vice President, Corporate Finance for Globe. Puno will take over from Rosemarie (Rizza) Maniego-Eala. Maniego-Eala is retiring to “pursue other personal and professional goals”, according to the Globe disclosure.

Alliance Global’s to open Boracay casino in 2025

Travellers, the gaming arm of Alliance Global (AGI PM), is looking to open a US$300mn casino in Boracay by the end of 2025. AGI President Kevin Tan declined to give exact figures but noted that it would be a small casino. Travellers previously said it would spend US$400mn to open a casino in Cebu by 2026. The company will also add gaming capacity in Entertainment City (through a partnership with Suntrust) by 2026. Travellers currently has c. 500 gaming tables and 2,500 slot machines. The new casino in Entertainment City will add 280 gaming tables and 1,300 slot machines.

JFC to operate and license Milksha stores in the US

JFC disclosed that its subsidiaries, Milkshop Japan Inc. and MKSA LLC (MKSA) have entered a license agreement whereby MKSA has been granted the right to open and operate Milksha branded stores in California, New York, and Texas in the United States. This allows franchising opportunities to be available for said locations through Milkshop Japan Inc. In November of 2021, JFC signed a deal to acquire 51% of Milkshop International Co. Ltd. for USD12.8mn. Since JFC’s acquisition of  Milksha, its store network, which is 93% franchised, has grown by 25% to 327 outlets as of July 2024. In 2023, Milksha generated USD96mn systemwide sales (30% increase from pre-acquisition sales). At the end of July 2024, the Jollibee Group’s store network increased by 4.8% compared to a year ago.

Laguindingan airport contract goes to Aboitiz InfraCapital

Aboitiz InfraCapital, Inc. will be awarded the contract to operate and maintain Lguindingan International Airport in Misamis Oriental. The contract for the Laguindigan Airport will run for a period of 35 years. The airport will have a capacity of 1.6mn passengers a year, which will increase to 3.9mn by the end of the first phase and to 6.1mn by the end of the second phase. DoTr stated that they had extended the deadline for counter proposals to Sept. 13, moving it from the deadline in August but there were no other proposals received. The infrastructure arm of the Aboitiz group has submitted unsolicited proposals for the operations, maintenance, and development of New Bohol-Panglao International Airport, Bicol International Airport, and Iloilo International Airport. The group has also secured the original proponent status for the New Bohol-Panglao International Airport, which Mr. Bautista said would undergo the Swiss challenge by November. Meanwhile, the Department of Transportation said it wants to auction to modernize and operate Davao International Airport to be a solicited process.

Semirara sees better 2H earnings

Semirara expects higher earnings in 2H24 due to enhanced plant efficiency and stable coal prices, according to Semirara President and COO Maria Cristina Gotianun. However, due to scheduled maintenance shutdowns of some plants, she pointed out that the contribution from energy sales will still hinge on the average price in WESM. We forecast SCC’s profits to reach P20bn in FY24, lower by 29% from than last year’s P27.9bn. As of 1H24, SCC’s profits reached P12.6bn, lower by 39% YoY. As we previously cited, the biggest source of upside risk to earnings is the extension of its coal mining unit’s tax holiday beyond July; Outcome on this will be determined this month.

Leandro Leviste to invest another P15bn in Batangas, Tarlac, and Nueva Ecija

Provincia Investments Corp., an affiliate of Leandro Leviste’s Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings (SPPPH), will invest P15bn in Batangas, Tarlac, and Nueva Ecija. SPPPH owns 29% of SP New Energy Corporation (SPNEC PM). SPPPH has raised a total of P15bn from the sale of SPNEC shares. Provincia, which is engaged in making investments in real estate and equities for industrial, commercial, energy, and infrastructure related developments, will collaborate with SPPPH to develop its landbank in the three provinces. Provincia recently acquired JJPNM Agro Industrial Corp, which owns a 60-hectare poultry farm in Nueva Ecija, the largest in the province. Mr. Leviste also invested P5bn in Batangas through a company called Countryside Investments Holdings Corporation.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP plans to cut RRR substantially this year

BSP Governor Eli Romolona said that the BSP will reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) substantially this year and there may be further reductions next year. In June 2023, the BSP lowered the RRR of universal and commercial banks (U/KB) from 12% to 9.5%, where it remains. A RRR cut could mitigate the impact of the reduction in policy interest rates on NIMs. Excess liquidity could also ease the competitive pressure on PHP deposits. We estimate that for every 100-basis point cut in the RRR, net profit of the banks we cover could rise 1.2%-1.4%.

Finance Secretary seeks 50bps rate cut

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said he will support a 50bps rate cut at the next Monetary Board meeting. Secretary Recto is part of the seven-member Monetary Board. The remaining BSP policy meetings in 2024 are set for Oct. 17 and Dec. 19.

BOP posts US$88mn surplus in August; End-August GIR revised up to US$107.9bn

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$88mn in August. This was a reversal of the US$57mn deficit a year ago and was higher than the US$66mn surplus in July 2024. Officials cited inflows from the net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. Recall that the government raised US$2.5bn from a three-tranche global bond offering in late August, which brought YT-August BOP surplus to US$1.6bn, lower than the US$2.1bn surplus recorded in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn this year.

Meanwhile, the revised end-August GIR level stood at US$107.9bn, higher than the US$106.7bn level in July 2024 and the previous preliminary figure of US$106.92bn. This is equivalent to 7.8 months’ worth of imports. BSP is forecasting an end-2024 GIR level of US$104bn.

Vehicle sales rise 6.6% in Aug

Vehicle sales grew by 6.6% to 39,155 units in August. On a MoM basis, this was lower by 0.4%. This brings YT-Aug retail sales to 304,765 units, up 10.3% YoY. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,924 units in August, up 7.7% YoY but down 2.5% MoM. This brings YT-Aug retail sales up 11% to 140,654 units. To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.6k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 16.3k units for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).

Government receives P30bn upfront payment from NAIA consortium

The Department of Finance said the remittance from the San Miguel-led NAIA consortium was cleared with the Bureau of Treasury on Sep 16. This follows the official turnover of NAIA’s operations last Sep 14. Apart from the upfront payment, the government expects to receive P2bn annually as concession fees and will get an estimated P36bn annually as its share of revenues over the 25-year term, assuming the 15-year contract is extended for another 10 years. The privatization of NAIA is the largest PPP project bid out under President Marcos, Jr.’s term and is estimated to involve capex of P144bn over the contract period. The consortium expects the increase the airport’s capacity to 62mn pax per year from 35mn and augment air traffic movements to 48 from 40 currently.

There are currently 164 PPP projects in the pipeline with a combined project cost of P3.2tn (US$64bn). Only P28.3bn of these are currently undergoing procurement, including the recently awarded P12.75bn Laguindingan airport unsolicited project of the Aboitiz group.

Remittances rise 3.1% YoY in July

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew by 3.1% YoY, 7% MoM to a 7-month high of US$3.09bn in July. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 9.8% YoY. The bump likely due to the start of school year, which varied between July and Aug. Land-based remittances rose 3.6% YoY, with remittances from the US rising by 3% and UAE by 12%, while sea-based remittances grew by 0.9% YoY. This brings YT-July remittances to US$19.3bn, up 2.9% YoY. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% this year, which suggests remittances could reach US$34.5bn.

Government hikes minimum wages in Regions IV-A, VII

The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved the increase in minimum wages in regions IV-A and VII. Region IV-A will be implementing minimum wage hikes of 6-17% for the non-agriculture sector and 4-18% for the agri sector of various municipalities by Sep 30 (last increase of 10-11% became effective on Sep 24, 2023.) Region VII will increase minimum wages by 7-8% by October 2 (last increase of 8-9% became effective last October 1, 2023.) Labor Secretary Bienvenido Laguesma said he expects regional wage boards across the country to finish their review before year-end.

Digital Payments +29% in August; driven by InstaPay +45%

Digital transactions done over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) grew 29% YoY in August to P1,441bn. Growth slowed from the 35% pace in July, and the August figure dipped slightly (-3%) MoM. Despite the slowdown, the 8-month total of P10,896bn, up 34% YoY, still faster than the 29% growth for FY2023.

The deceleration was due to PesoNet – used for larger transactions – which saw August values at P817bn, still up a respectable 20% YoY. However, this was slower than the 30% clip in July, and was down 6% MoM. Given the chunky nature of the payments, the monthly variability in growth rates has been quite wide. Year-to-date, though, payments over PesoNet were still up 27% YoY (vs 22% for FY23) to P6,374bn.

To the upsidde, smaller-ticket, real-time payments over InstaPay continued to power on, rising 45% YoY to P624bn. This was slightly faster than the 43% YoY recorded in July, and in line with the 8-month trajectory, up 45% to P4,521bn (vs 42% in 2023). Once again, the volume of transactions over InstaPay surged, growing 69% YoY in August, accelerating from the 62% in July (and +71% YoY in 8M24).

Digital adoption, particularly at the retail/individual level seems to be picking up steam. Among stocks we cover, Globe (GLO PM) still offers the most direct exposure via its affiliate GCash/Mynt. Meanwhile, banks continue to invest heavily in their IT infrastructure, with BPI (BPI PM) seemingly ahead of the curve among the banks we cover.

Current account (CA) deficit widens by 25% YoY in 2Q24; 1H24 CA deficit lower by 18% to US$7.1bn

The country’s current account deficit widened by 25% YoY to US$5.1bn in 2Q24, equivalent to 3.9% of GDP. Due to the 4.2% expansion in the trade deficit to US$16.8bn due to the 0.8% YoY drop in exports to US$13.8bn and the 1.9% YoY increase in imports to US$30.7bn. BPO export revenues, which fall under net services receipts, grew 4.9% YoY to US$7.4bn in 2Q24. Brings 1H24 current account deficit to US$7.08bn, lower by 18% YoY and equivalent to 4.1% of GDP. Due to the 5% YoY drop in trade in goods deficit of US$31.5bn. 1H24 BPO export revenues amounted to US$14.5bn, up 3.1% YoY. BSP forecasts the current account deficit will be lower by 58% YoY to US$4.7bn by year-end, equivalent to 1% of GDP.

OFW rose by 9.8% last year from 2022

There were 2.16 million Overseas Filipino Workers (OFW) last year compared to 1.96 million in 2022. Overseas contract workers accounted for the bulk of the total estimate at 98%. This is attributed to taking advantage of employment opportunities overseas to earn more and then sending the money home.
