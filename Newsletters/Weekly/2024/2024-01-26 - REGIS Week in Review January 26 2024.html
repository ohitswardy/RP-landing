REGIS REPORT

26 January 2024

The index continued to rally and gained another 2.8% for the week and closed at 6686 following the strength in global markets on hopes of rate cuts this coming year. Regis’s co-brand partner Jefferies made drastic changes to its US GDP and rate forecasts, now expecting a much milder recession, and for just 100bps in Fed rate cuts this year.

The local market continued its ascent despite the BSP seemingly taking a more relaxed stance. Governor Eli Remolona still expects to begin cutting policy rates in 2H24, but opened the door to the possibility of a move in late 1H (though he did still say it was not likely).

The strength was driven by foreign demand in large caps SM, BPI, and JFC. The index ended in the green despite losers outnumbering gainers 17:13.

Headline inflows were high at US$14.1m.

Foreign demand lifted BDO (+0.6% WoW), SM (+2.3% WoW), and BPI (+2.3% WoW) for US$10.4m, US$9.5m, and BPI $6.4m respectively.

Outside the large caps, trades were directionless.

Consumer names generally dragged.  URC (-2.0% WoW) continued to fall along with small cap consumer names Monde (-2.0% WoW) and Century (-2.9% WoW). JFC (+1.6% WoW) outperformed its peers on strong foreign demand worth US$4.4m. Earlier this week, Monde reported its operating performance for 2023. The core income of Monde implied in the statement would be ~P7.5bn for FY23, which is just a shade above Street estimates. The implied profit, though, has yet to include the impact of any valuation test on its alternative protein business, Quorn. URC saw heavy foreign outflows of US$8.5m.

Property names ended flat, with Ayala Land (-0.4% WoW) and SM Prime (+0.30% WoW). Ayala Land will be renovating its four most important shopping malls this year. The renovations will be done in phases to minimize disruption to operations, but revenue is likely to be affected short-term. ALI, however, does not expect a YoY decline in mall revenue to result. Later in the week, an AREIT (-6.2% WoW) placement went through in relation to its property-for-share swap transaction with Ayala Land. The deal size was 181m shares at Php31.10/sh.

Biggest drag to the market was ICT (-2.1% WoW) as the company is selling a small listed Indonesian subsidiary for US$2m to rationalize its portfolio, leaving it with just one operation in that country. Foreign investors let go of US$5.6m worth of ICT shares for the week.  

Meralco (-0.7% WoW) says it is expecting 4.5% growth in electricity sales this FY24, which would likely be just below real GDP growth (in line with historical trends) and not far from growth in FY23. The company is pinning expectations on a recovery in industrial usage to offset a moderation in commercial demand.

On the telco space, the competition authorities approved the purchase by PLDT (+0.5% WoW) of Sky Cable from the Lopez group for P6.75bn (US$120m). The deal adds over 300k subscribers to PLDT’s 3.3m wired broadband count. Even before the acquisition, PLDT had just around 50% of the wired broadband market.

RESEARCH
Philippine Power - A balancing act
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Reserves to remain tight through 2025
Slower demand growth early last year and the return of the 1,200MW
Ilijan plant in 2Q23 combined to avert a shortage in 2023. However, an
acceleration in 2H23 suggests growth is on track to return to 5%p.a.
beginning 2024. While an operational and much more expensive Ilijan
buys the grid time, increases in peak demand are seen to outpace
baseload additions in the next two years. This low reserve environment,
despite the abundant renewable energy pipeline, anchors our BUY call
on the gencos.


Demand to grow 5% p.a.; timing of baseload additions is key
Electricity demand grew stronger through 2H24, with the latest prints from
distributors hitting 6% in 3Q23 (as high as 9% for commercial segment). We thus
estimate peak demand will grow 5% (~600MW) per year in 24E and 25E. Of the
expected addition of 2,200MW over the next two years, only 1,000MW is viable
as baseload (coal + gas) and barely correspond to the increase in demand. Delays
to these thermal plants will mean a tighter variance between peak demand and
available capacity.

Spot prices off their highs but should remain elevated
We expect little change to the demand-supply balance over the next two years,
though the intermittent output from Malampaya presents risks to supply,
especially while the LNG terminals remain in commissioning phase. WESM
prices have since tapered off from the c.P9/kWh highs registered in 2Q23 but
have declined to the P5-6/kWh range through the cooler months, helped in part
by lower fuel costs. We expect prices to remain around these levels, which are
double the lows printed in 2020 and are higher than previous long-term
expectations.


Increasing scrutiny on transmission and grid operator
Companies like ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) have previously identified transmission as
a key bottleneck that needs to be addressed. The completion of the first big
wave of new RE plants from 4Q23-1H24 will put to the test the ability of the grid
to make this output available.


Theme intact; BUYS on ACEN, AP and FGEN
We like ACEN as a high risk, high reward bet in this space as sizeable plants that
just came online (or about to) are envisioned to drive earnings 26% this year. AP
also plays well into the theme as a third of output is on short-term contracts that
reprice more frequently, while a generous dividend enhances potential return.
Meanwhile, FGEN stands out on valuations, butwe acknowledge that the liquidity
could hamper fair price discovery

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Cuts in RRR, not just rates, possible – BSP

A cut in the policy rate is possible in 1H24 but not likely. According to BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona Jr. While the data on inflation and other economic indicators are “looking good” and that “we like the trend so far” he is not encouraging further talk of rate cuts anytime soon. The BSP’s 24E inflation forecast is 4-4.2%, citing supply shocks from El Nino as key risks. Remolona previously signaled that they will consider easing monetary policy only when the CPI is firmly within the mid-range of the target or around 3%. He also said that a cut in the RRR within the year is very possible. The first Monetary Board policy meeting for 2024 is scheduled on Feb. 15

Meralco expects 4.5% growth in power sales in FY24

Meralco says it expects electricity sales to rise 4.5% this year, broadly similar to 2023. Company expects recovery in industrial usage, moderation in commercial consumption. Spike in residential usage is expected due to El Nino. The volume expectation is largely in line with our own estimates.

Red Sea crisis brings higher freight to some PH shippers; but effects not yet fully felt

The Red Sea crisis has brought additional shipping charges to some Philippine shippers but its effects have yet to be fully felt by the general trading community. According to a Philippine-based forwarding company executive, who requested anonymity. It will make shipping costs 15% more expensive and add 10 days for the exchange of goods between Europe and Asia. According to Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) director general Tereso Panga. Based on our conversations with ICTSI (ICT PM), they have not seen any material impact from this issue so far. However, we reckon that if the issue spills over to their ports, volumes could be negatively affected near term, as the disruption could delay the arrival of containers at ICT’s ports but these containers will eventually arrive at their ports and possibly lead to congestions and higher yields.

PCC approves sale of Sky to PLDT

PLDT (TEL PM) and ABS-CBN (ABS PM) disclosed that the transaction for the former to buy Sky Cable from the Lopez group, including ABS-CBN, has been cleared by the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC). Sky Cable has a broadband business as well as pay TV (i.e., cable TV) business.The deal was first announced last year.  PLDT (TEL PM) agreed to buy 100% of Sky from ABS-CBN and affiliates for P6.75bn. The deal has apparently been approved by the competition authorities even if it would result in PLDT further increasing its market share (to over 50%) in wired broadband (as opposed to all “broadband” segments, including fixed wireless). Sky has around 300k to 350k broadband subscribers and a similar number of pay TV subs. PLDT had 3.28m broadband subs as of Sept 2023.

MONDE sees mid-teens growth in core net income in 2023

Based on unaudited preliminary results for FY23, MONDE expects core net income to grow by mid-teens in 2023, this implies core net income of c.P7.5bn. FY23 sales grew high single-digit YoY (Regis FY23: +4%), while gross margin is in line with 2022 (31.3%). The APAC BFB business continued to perform strongly in 4Q23. The segment posted all-time high volumes during the quarter, rising 5% YoY / 2% QoQ. Market share in instant noodles was 67.5% in 4Q23 up slightly vs 3Q23 (67.3%). GPM improved 400bps YoY due to favorable prices of wheat and palm oil, which have been locked in until 2Q24 and 3Q24, respectively. Meanwhile, Quorn’s top line continues to be challenged, but still EBITDA-neutral in 4Q23.

ICTSI fully divests stake in Indonesian unit

ICTSI agreed to sell its entire 80.19% stake in PT ICTSI Jasa Prima Tbk (KARW IJ) to PT Sarana Kelola Investa for US$2mn. KARW wholly owns PT PBM Olah Jasa Andal (OJA), which has concession with state-run PT Pelabuhan Indonesia II (Pelindo) at the port of Tanjung Priok in Jakarta, valid until 2028. Company cited rationalizing operations and redirect resources in Indonesia to focus on full concession contracts, where ICTSI controls all aspects of the business, including regulatory and commercial activities. ICT now only has one port in Indonesia, located in Lamongan Regency, East Java. Transaction is subject to approval of change of ownership application in the Indonesian Stock Exchange, among other conditions.

San Miguel unit advances in 1,200-MW supply bid for Meralco

South Premiere Power Corp. (SPPC) is advancing to the post-qualification stage after Meralco (MER PM) declared its bid to supply 1,200 MW as the most favorable. SPPC offered a total levelized cost of electricity rate at P7.0718 per kwh for the entire 1,200-MW requirement. SPPC is a unit of San Miguel (SMC PM) and is the administrator of the natural gas-fired power plant in Ilijan, Batangas. Other bidders that offered less favorable proposals were:

    • SMC units Limay Power and San Roque Hydropower offered P7.10/kwh for a capacity of 150MW
    • First Gen’s (FGEN PM) First NatGas Power Corp offered P8.4489/kwh
    • All other offers received were below the P7.1538/kwh reserve price set for the competitive selection process

All contracts resulting from this CSP will be subject to the regulatory proceedings of the Energy Regulatory Commission. The resulting 15-year power supply agreement (PSA) from this CSP will cover its future capacity requirements, “including the 1,000 MW (net) supply that was covered by its PSAs with change in circumstance cases that are pending resolution.

Four flagship Ayala malls to start renovation this year

Glorietta, Greenbelt, Trinoma, and Ayala Center Cebu will begin major renovation this year. The renovations will be completed in 2026. The renovations will be done in phases to reduce disruptions to operations. These major renovations were previously announced during the company’s 3Q23 briefing. The company does not expect the renovations to result in lower mall revenue. These 4 malls comprise about 35% of Ayala Land mall GLA.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

DOF expects P126bn revenue loss from PIFITA (Tax Package 4) over 5 years

During the hearing of the Senate Committee on Ways and Means, the DOF said that the Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act (PIFITA) could result in lower tax revenue of P126bn from 2025 to 2029 (average of P21bn annually). Many of the changes will be gradual (lowered by 1ppt annually or 0.1% annually in the case of the stock transaction tax).

New finance chief joins BSP’s monetary board

Newly appointed Finance Secretary Ralph Recto has formally assumed the role of the government’s representative to the seven-member Monetary Board. Recall that Mr. Recto was the former House deputy speaker before his appointment as the Secretary of the Department of Finance last Jan 12. He replaced former finance secretary Benjamin Diokno, who was appointed to a six-year term as a private sector representative in the Monetary Board.

DOF shelves plans to impose new taxes on junk food and sweetened beverages

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto culled the proposed junk food and sweetened beverage tax from the list of proposed new taxes. Citing that such taxes will continue to fuel inflation. On the other new taxes proposed, Sec. Recto is supportive of imposing taxes on single-use plastic and digital services. The proposed hike in taxes on motor vehicle users may also be tempered.
