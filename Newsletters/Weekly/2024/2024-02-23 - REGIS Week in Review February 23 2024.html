REGIS REPORT

23 February 2024

The market gained another 0.6% WoW to close at 6,913.21.

The rally was driven mostly by corporate earnings as large cap index names reported strong earnings for FY23.

Ayala Land (+8.6% WoW) led the index as FY23 earnings rose 32% to P24.5bn, in line with consensus and ahead of REGIS forecast. Despite a substantial increase in launches in 4Q, presales were virtually unchanged QoQ and YoY. As a result, FY23 presales were only up 9% to P114bn, 22% below the 2019 level. Looking ahead, the company also expressed optimism about the premium residential segment (ALP + Alveo brands) but sounded more cautious about the mid-to-affordable segments. Foreign demand for the stock was strong at US$17.8m.

Meanwhile, SM Prime (-2.4% WoW) full-year earnings grew 33% to P40bn driven by the predictably robust performance of its core mall business. Same-mall revenue growth is expected to normalize further this year to 6%, within the historical range of 5%-8%.

GT Capital (+5.0% WoW) likewise outperformed as key subsidiaries Metrobank and auto business Toyota posted remarkable results. FY23 profits for Metrobank rose 29% to P42.2bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts, as strong net interest income offset weak trading gains. Thus, Metrobank (+3.6% WoW) declared cash dividends of P5/share for the year, equivalent to 8.5% yield (and much higher than Regis expectations at Php3.00/share). Apart from Metrobank, auto sales (from CAMPI/TMA) for January rose 15.5% YoY to 34k units while sales of Toyota rose 20% YoY to 16k.  Given seasonality, the absolute number of Toyota units sold for the month should be mildly better than expected relative to our full-year FY24 assumption (206k).

Banking names BPI (+4.4% WoW) and BDO (-0.5% WoW) continued to see follow through buying after postings results last week. BPI saw US$9.2m worth of inflows, while US$7.1m for BDO.

Outside earnings, the rest of the index names actually ended in the red. Losers outnumbered gainers 7:3.

Biggest drags were consumer names. URC (-3.4% WoW), JFC (+2.0% WoW), and EMI (-4.4% WoW) saw heavy outflows for US$2.8m, US$2.3m, and US$1.6m respectively.

On corporate news, Aboitiz Power (+0.7% WoW) raised its stake in STEAG State to 85% (from just under 80%) for US$11m.  STEAG State runs a 210MW coal-fired power plant in northern Mindanao under a BOT contract with the government until 2031.

PLDT (-0.8% WoW) and ABS-CBN (-3.0% WoW) issued a very succinct press release announcing that PLDT’s purchase of Sky Cable from ABS-CBN will not proceed. The termination of the deal, after approval from the competition authority, comes after years of back-and-forth negotiations and three announcements about a transaction. Sky Cable had already announced it was ceasing operations of its pay TV business within the month. PLDT was acquiring Sky for its broadband business, which had around 300,000+ subscribers. Meanwhile, it was also disclosed that PLDT agreed to settle for US$3m the US class action lawsuit filed against it over the capex overrun issue in 2022. That hopefully puts that matter to rest.

Headline inflows registered at US$30.2m for the week while turnover was slightly lower at US$84.0m (-0.9% WoW) as the market consolidates.

ICTSI - Staying bullish
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Maintain Buy

We raise our FY24/FY25 profit estimates by 5%/6% and TP by 15% to P305/sh after incorporating anticipated market share gains at ICT’s ports in Manila, Mexico, and Melbourne. We believe investors can look forward to (a) the completion of brownfield expansions within 1H24 and (b) the implementation of higher container handling tariffs at MICT in September. Despite outperforming the PCOMP by 29% over the past year, valuations remain low relative to peers (16x/11x) and history (20x/11x), at 13.1x 24E PER and 8.2x 24E EV/EBITDA.

What to expect in 4Q23?

ICTSI is due to release 4Q23 results on March 1 and given recent channel checks, we anticipate operational trends will likely be almost identical to what transpired in 3Q23. We also assume a US$100mn hit to headline profits from the write down of the remaining receivable from Sudan. Despite the large write off, we assume ICT will pay FY24E DPS of US$0.196, equivalent to a yield of 4%.

Street upgrades likely

We forecast ICT will see EPS CAGR of 19% from 2023E to 2025E, driven by (a) 3% volume CAGR, (b) 7% yield CAGR, (c) 3ppts EBITDA margin expansion, and (d) lower overall financing costs on sustained de-levering. We are confident about potential market share gains at ICT’s ports in Manila, Mexico, and Melbourne, which should lead to volume and yield improvements. Our revised forecasts are now the highest on the Street and we believe Street upgrades will follow.

TP of P305/sh offers 17% potential return

We raise our SOTP NAV-based TP by 15% after (a) lifting our FY24E/FY25E EBITDA forecasts 5%/7% and (b) lowering our WACC assumptions by 20-400bps. Our revised TP implies 15x PER and 9x EV/EBITDA. We believe there is scope for upside surprise from acquisitions. Note that our forecasts and valuation do not incorporate the potential acquisition of DCT-2 port in Durban, South Africa. Downside risks relate to trade, dilutive acquisitions, regulatory, and labor issues.

 
SM Prime - Sharp drop in 4Q23 reservation sales
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Earnings and operating profit were in line but watch out for presales

SM Prime’s 4Q23 net profit rose 22% YoY (-8% QoQ) to P9.9bn. This brought FY23 earnings up 33% to P40bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Operating profit of P61.3bn (+24%), was also in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. The most noteworthy part of the press release, however, was the sharp drop in 4Q23 reservation sales.

Lowest quarterly presales figure since 2017

After falling 36% QoQ in 3Q23, 4Q23 presales fell another 39% QoQ (30% YoY) to P12.7bn, the lowest quarterly level since 2017. As a result, FY23 presales were flat at P102bn, which is still 13% above the FY19 level. We suspect that the drop in 4Q23 was partly due to a tightening of credit standards as the reservation fee to purchase a unit was raised from P10k to P25k in 2H23. On a more positive note, residential revenue rose 8% QoQ and 6% YoY, which likely means that cancellation rates have continued to moderate.

Philippine mall revenue growth normalizes

We estimate that Philippine mall revenue growth fell from 23% YoY in 3Q23 to 12% YoY in 4Q23, as the low-base effect dissipated (4Q22 was the first full quarter without rental concessions). 4Q23 Philippine mall rental growth appears to have normalized to 8% while other mall revenue (such as cinema sales) grew c. 35% YoY. We forecast 2024 same-mall revenue growth of 6%, within the historical range of 5%-8%.

Office and hotel revenue rise

Meanwhile, office revenue grew 9% QoQ (+13% YoY), signaling that occupancy improved from c. 85% in 3Q23. Hotel revenue also rose 5% QoQ (+12% YoY) due to the seasonally strong holiday period.

 
Ayala Land - Presales stay flat in 4Q23; management cautious on mid-income residential
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Earnings in line; land bank reduction has begun

Ayala Land’s FY23 net profit rose 32% to P24.5bn, in line with consensus and ahead of REGIS forecast. While development revenue rebounded in 4Q23, this was offset by a sharp QoQ drop in EBIT margin (from the unusually strong 3Q23).

The company has started delivering on its plan to reduce land bank, consuming 1,000 hectares in 2023. It plans to use another 800 hectares annually until 2028, which would reduce land bank by c. 40% over six years. This suggests that the proportion of horizontal/landed residential launches will stay high (52% in FY24).

Flat presales in 4Q23; cautious about mid-income segment

Despite launching an additional P40bn worth of projects in 4Q23 (from P36bn in the entire 9M23 period), 4Q23 presales were flat QoQ and YoY. As a result, FY23 presales only rose 9% to P113.9bn, 22% below the FY19 figure.

Management seems optimistic about the prospects for premium residential projects (ALP + Alveo brands) but it is more cautious about the mid-to-affordable income brands. Although only 57% of FY23 presales were from premium brands, a larger portion (80%) of ALI’s P115bn launches in FY24 will be from this segment. The company still has a large amount of unsold inventory of mid-to-affordable residential projects so it will focus on reducing such units.

ALI acknowledges that if demand for mid-end projects does not rebound in the next three years, then its target to double earnings by 2028 will be at risk.

4Q mall revenue uncharacteristically flat QoQ despite holiday season

Despite what should be a seasonally strong holiday season, mall revenue was only flat QoQ (+11% YoY). As a result, FY23 mall revenue grew 31% to P21.1bn, 4% below the 2019 level. Management said there were sales that did not meet the cut-off for revenue recognition in 4Q but would contribute in 1Q24 instead.

Ayala Land will spend P13bn over the next three years to redevelop four flagship malls. While they comprise about 40% of mall revenue, management does not expect systemwide mall revenue to drop during the period, since the redevelopment will be done in phases to reduce operational disruptions. After the redevelopments are completed, Ayala Land expects the tenant sales of those malls to improve by about 20%.

Office occupancy improves 3ppt QoQ

4Q23 office occupancy rose 3ppt QoQ to 92%, higher than the industry average of 83%, thanks to an increase in BPO take-up. Looking ahead, the company is not concerned about the additional 7% to its floor area this year (98k sqm) since the space to be completed will be in areas where occupancy is especially high.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PLDT and ABS-CBN terminate Sky Cable deal

PLDT and ABS-CBN announced that deal for PLDT to buy Sky Cable will no proceed. This announcement comes after approval from competition authorities was secured. PLDT had attempted to buy Sky multiple times in the past but previous attempts were derailed for various reasons. The attraction of Sky for PLDT is its broadband business. Sky has over 300k broadband subscribers, equivalent to around 5% of total wired broadband subscriber base

AboitizPower increases equity interest in coal-fired power plant

AP announced yesterday its purchase of an additional 15.6% equity stake in STEAG State Power, Inc. (SPI), bringing its equity interest to 85%. The company purchased 48.22mn common shares and 25.76mn preferred shares for a total $11mn. The remaining shares are held by STEAG Power GmbH and La Filipina Uy Gongco Corp. SPI operates a 210MW coal-fired thermal power plant in Mindanao, and with this investment, AP increases its attributable net sellable capacity. Based on their website, AP holds a total 3,962.25MW net sellable capacity.

MPI to pursue buyout of Ayala stake in LRT-1

MPI is exploring the possibility of acquiring Ayala Corp’s stake in LRT-1 according to MPI Chairman, President, and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan. MPI owns a 35.8% stake in LRT-1 while Ayala Corp owns 35%; Other shareholders are Sumitomo (19.2%) and Macquarie Infra (10%). The company cited that being a majority-owner will enable MPI to do what they want to do; It could also draw synergies if they win the O&M contract for the MRT-3 rail. The government previously announced that it plans to privatize the O&M contract for MRT-3 under a solicited bid in 2025.

MPI, SMC plan to list soon-to-be merged tollroad units

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) and San Miguel Corp’s (SMC PM) plan to list once its tollroad units are merged according to MPI Chairman, President, and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan. The two companies are currently addressing hurdles to their merger, starting with the concession agreements for their tollroads. Both companies will need to go to Congress to apply for a franchise. A merger would require approval from foreign shareholders of MPTC’s operations in Vietnam and Indonesia. Shortly after Metro Pacific de-listed last year, San Miguel’s President and CEO Ramon Ang made a personal investment in Metro Pacific and was also voted to the board of the company.

Cebu Air signs MOU with Pratt & Whitney for engine supply of 15 aircraft

Cebu Air (CEB PM) signed an MOU with Pratt & Whitney (P&W) to provide the company engines for its 15 narrowbody jets. Recall that in 2019, CEB ordered 10 A321 and 5 A320 NEOs from Airbus, which will be delivered in 2025. CEB expects to grow its fleet size to 92 by end-2024 from 79 in end-2023; Management has guided for 24E seat growth of 5-8%. Recall that CEB grappled with P&W’s global engine recall, forcing CEB to ground 10-20 aircraft in 2024-2025 and sign damp short-term leases to augment its capacity. CEB is currently negotiating with P&W for compensation. P&W’s engine recall affected engines delivered from 2015-2021. Note that the A320 Neo family aircraft are available with two engines from two manufacturers: P&W and Leap 1-A from CFM International; However, introducing a new engine on a single aircraft type could be costly.

Ayala nears deal to exit LRT1, other non-core assets

Ayala Corp. is closing in on the sale of its 35% stake in Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC), the concession holder for LRT-1 according to Alberto de Larrazabal, CFO of AC. The sale might be concluded within the next four to six months. Metro Pacific, which co-owns 35.8% of LRMC, is among those interested to buy their railway shares. AC also plans to gradually sell its shares in Manila Water (MWC PM) to Mr. Enrique Razon. Larrazabal said they are targeting to raise US$350mn to US$400mn, mainly from the sale of the LRT 1 stake and remaining 19.25% stake in Manila Water. This means the conglomerate would exceed the US$1bn fundraising target. AC is trading at a 25% discount to its market-based NAV of P942/sh.

BSP gives GCash the green light to enter Europe and the Middle East

GCash, operated by G-Xchange Inc., has received BSP's approval to offer financial services to overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) in Europe and the Middle East. The e-wallet can now operate in the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, South Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong, Spain and Germany. A beta version is currently accessible in Canada, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. New products and services will be tailored to the needs of OFWs as the e-wallet hopes to become an alternative remittance channel. As of FY23, around P118bn worth of loans have been disbursed to Filipinos. The company is still eyeing to go public as soon as market conditions allow it.

Citicore pushes back IPO to 2Q24

Citicore Renewable Energy Corporation (CREC) informed the PSE of its decision to adjust the indicative date of the Offer from March to 2Q24. Company is currently evaluating offers from institutions interested in participating in its IPO and will look to finalize them as soon as possible.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

First investment of Maharlika Investment Fund will be in power sector

Maharlika Investment Fund CEO Joel Consing said that the fund’s first investment will be in the power sector. President Marcos previously said that the government was studying a proposal to privatize National Grid Corp of the Philippines. Mr. Consing said that in three years, each sector should not comprise more than 15% of the fund.  

Senate approves P100/day wage increase on 3nd reading

The Senate approved on third reading a bill calling for a P100 across-the-board minimum wage increase for workers in the private sector. The Bill exempts businesses with less than 10 employees, establishments with less than P3mn capital, and barangay micro enterprises. If implemented, this could result in a 22-24% increase in minimum wages for the private sector across all regions. The current minimum wage in the National Capital Region is P573-610/day (~USD10-11). The House of Representatives is looking to pass a bill raising minimum wages by up to P400/day.

January BoP swings to deficit

Preliminary data showed the balance of payments swung to a US$740mn deficit in January. Reversed surpluses of US$3.08bn in Jan 2023 and US$642mn in December. It was also the largest deficit recorded in 11 months. The government cited outflows arising from the national government’s payments of foreign currency obligations. The GIR dipped 0.5% MoM to US$103.3bn in January.

DOE, DENR sign MOA to speed up offshore wind projects

The Department of Energy (DOE) and the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) signed a memorandum of agreement (MOA) that will simplify the issuance of an environmental compliance certificate (ECC) for offshore wind energy projects. MOA covers pre-development and exploration activities. Once DENR issues an interim ECC, developers can push through pre-development activities and will not need to undergo consultations with LGUs and communities yet. Other renewable technologies do not have interim ECC, and the ECC is only required during the development stage.

Maharlika fund is in talks of a potential JV for rural telco towers

Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) President Rafael Consing discussed the potential of a JV with foreign partners to build telecommunication towers in rural areas. No foreign companies have been named due to nondisclosure agreements but rural areas have already been identified for construction. Clients of the JV tower company will primarily be incumbent telco players. The project is said to be in the pipeline of investments for next year
