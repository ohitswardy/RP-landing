REGIS REPORT

29 November 2024

The PCOMP erased prior week’s gains and marked its 5th week of decline over the last 6 weeks, -2.45% WoW to 6613.85, as the lightly traded market was weighed down by continued net foreign selling, which at this point has erased the “real” inflow into the market (if excluding the ~$290m outflow block in BLOOM related to GGAM settlement from earlier this year). The benchmark’s nominal YTD gain is now down to +2.54% vs a peak of +17% 8 weeks ago.

A huge chunk of the foreign selling this week went into ICT amid heightened uncertainty over global trade following Trump’s tweets regarding his tariff plans once he takes office. As such, ICT was one of the worst laggards in the benchmark -6.8% WoW. Note that this steep decline comes despite some support from the company’s buyback program – the company disclosed that they bought nearly 1.3m shares or $9m worth on Thursday.

Worst performing stock in the benchmark was ACEN -9.55% WoW, which fell to its lowest levels since late April. Sentiment on the stock is potentially weighed down by expectations that Trump’s policies will favor fossil fuels and as such may result to renewables becoming out of favor. Another big laggard was BLOOM -5.68%, which extended its downtrend to new 52-week lows after reporting dismal 3Q/9M results earlier this month. Note that BLOOM is down by -44.31% YTD, only second worst among PCOMP constituents behind NIKL -45.8%, and its market cap is now down to $1.07b, substantially lower than e-games player PLUS $1.64b, which in contrast gained +8.19% this week.

Losses this week were widespread with over half of index members down by over -1% WoW and nearly a third by at least -4%, but there were still a few bright spots such as CNVRG +3.8%, DMC +3.29%, consumer plays PGOLD +2% and CNPF +1.82%, and BDO +1.64% which was among the few top foreign inflow names this week.

Average daily turnover this week improved to a daily average of $100m. Note however that this was skewed higher by the MSCI rebalancing on Monday when activity hit $169m. Compared to prior index reconstitutions however this recent one involved relatively minimal changes (small net outflow for overall market). After the rebalancing, activity on the ground turned visibly light at a daily average of only $83m daily, and net foreign selling further accelerated, especially in the latter part of the week. Cumulative net foreign selling for the week amounted to $65m, which marked the 6th consecutive week of outflows, during which period outflows have now netted $355m. YTD net foreign selling is now at $305m, coming from a peak net inflow of $100m 6 weeks ago, and with a month left to the year the Philippines looks en route for the 7th consecutive year of net outflow (last inflow year 2017).

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

GCash mulls raising up to US$1.5bn from IPO in 2H25

Mynt/GCash has reportedly invited banks to pitch for an IPO by 2H25, potentially selling US$1-1.5bn worth of shares. Deliberations are ongoing and details of the offering such as size and timeline could still change. The plan to list Gcash on two bourses (PSE and Nasdaq) was reportedly dropped, with the company opting to undertake an IPO in the Philippines instead.

Globe introduces new Prepaid Fiber plans

Globe introduced new Prepaid Fiber (GFiber Prepaid) plans that directly compete with Converge’s Surf2Sawa service. The pricing matches the prepaid product Surf2Sawa of Converge ICT and are ~30% lower than previous plans, with comparable speeds after Converge raised them earlier this month. The service requires a one-time fee of P999, which can be paid via e-wallet (e.g. GCash, Maya), similar to Surf2Sawa. Regis expects this to spur price competition, leading to increased fiber take-up and penetration. Recall that the industry added over 200k subs in 3Q24. Household penetration (currently <30%) should rise with lower prices.

DMCI group completes tender offer for Cemex Phils

The parent company of DMCI, Dacon Corp. completed the tender offer of Cemex Holdings Phils. (CHP PM). 12.9mn CHP shares were tendered, which brought CHP’s public float down to 10.005% from 10.1%. CHP remains compliance with the PSE’s minimum public ownership requirement of 10% and will remain listed. Recall that the DMCI group agreed to purchase 100% of CHP’s parent company, Cemex Asian South East Corp. (CASEC) for an enterprise value of US$660mn and an equity value of US$306mn. DMCI will own 56.75% of CASEC; Dacon Corp. will own 32.12%; Semirara (SCC PM) will own 11.13%. With the completion of the tender offer, DMCI will effectively own 51% of CHP while SCC will own 10%.

MGen’s PacificLight allocates US$900mn for 600MW Singapore plant bid

PacificLight Power. plans to invest US$900mn if it wins a public bidding for a 600MW natural gas greenfield power plant in Singapore, according to MGen Gas Energy Hldgs. Inc. President and CEO Yari Miralao. The Singapore’s Energy Market Authority requested for the private sector to build, own, and operate 2x600MW combined cycle gas turbine plants by 2029 and 2030. The government is set to announce the winning bidder by next month or early next year.

PacificLight is a 58%-owned unit of Meralco’s (MER PM) PowerGen Corp. (MGen) while the balance is owned by First Pacific. It currently operates an 830MW LNG plant in Jurong Island. Other greenfield projects of PacificLight are the following: 100MW hydrogen-ready gas turbine, with commercial operations slated to begin by 2Q25, and 600MW solar farm in Bulan Island in Indonesia (granted conditional approval by the Singaporean government).

ACEN acquires 49% stake in Vietnam-based BIM

ACEN signed a share purchase agreement with Huntington Renewable Investments Ltd to acquire a 49% stake in BIM Energy Holding Corp. (BIMEH) for US$70.5mn. Previously, ACEN had a 30% stake in BIMEH. The acquisition will allow ACEN to increase its pipeline projects in Vietnam. BIMEH owns the 405MW Ninh Thuan Solar and 88MW Ninh Thuan Wind projects. ACEN has 6,754MW of attributable RE capacity, of which 3,051MW are operational. In the Philippines alone, ACEN has a total of 2,397MW of capacity, of which 1,330 MW are under construction. Its international att. RE capacity totals 4,357 MW, of which c. 2,400 MW are under construction.

Aboitiz wins 30-year Bohol-Panglao airport contract

The Department of Transportation awarded Aboitiz InfraCapital the 30-year contract to construct and operate the P4.5bn Bohol-Panglao International Airport project. The award comes after no comparative proposals were received by the deadline for submission on November 11. The airport expansion project aims to increase the maximum passenger throughput from 2-2.5mn passengers per year at the project’s start and to at least 3.9mn by the end of the project. Bohol airport project adds to Aboitiz’ airport portfolio, which consists of the Cebu and Laguindingan airports.

Three other airport PPP projects are expected to be awarded next year: Iloilo, Kalibo, Puerto Princesa, and Davao airports: Iloilo airport (P21bn) – Proponent is Villa-led Prime Asset Ventures, Kalibo airport (P3.62bn) – Proponent is Mega 7 Construction, Puerto Princesa airport (P10.2bn) – Proponent is Prime Asset Ventures, and Davao airport – solicited project (no project cost).

DigiPlus allots P660mn for Brazil entry

The Board of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), approved the funding of P660mn for its initial entry into Brazil. The P660mn is intended for a three-month horizon - this includes funding for license fees, minimum capitalization, financial services, and other operational expenses.

PLUS passed the qualification stage for a federal license with Brazil’s Ministry of Finance’s Secretariat of Awards and Bets (SPA). The final stage requires PLUS to fulfill post-qualification regulatory requirements within 30 days, including platform certification and license fee payments. Upon completing these requirements, the SPA will release the final list of operators authorized to operate starting 1 January 2025. Once finalized, PLUS will be authorized to offer online sports betting and other iGaming services in Brazil.

S&P raises PH credit outlook to positive

S&P Global Ratings raised the Philippines' credit rating outlook to "positive" from "stable," citing the country's effective policymaking which delivered improvements in its credit metrics. S&P also kept the sovereign credit ratings at "BBB+' for long-term and "A-2" for short-term.

Senate approves P6.35tn 2025 Budget

The Senate approved on third reading the bill containing the P6.352tn proposed national budget for fiscal year 2025, which is 10% YoY higher than 2024 and is equivalent to 21.9% of GDP. Senate will consult with the Lower House during Bicam regarding (a) P39bn cash aid program (removed by Senate) and (b) reduction in the budget of the Office of the VP to P733mn from P2bn. Senate increased the budgets for the AFP Modernization, calamity fund, ASEAN Summit in 2026, air traffic management but no details on magnitude of increases were provided.

Some key items of the 2025 budget: Debt service (i.e. interest payments) will increase by 27% YoY; Primary spending (excluding debt service) will increase by 8% YoY, driven largely by the 17% increase in allocations to LGUs; Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively.

Government infrastructure spending up 12% YoY in 9M24

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 12% YoY to 1.43tn in 9M24. This was equivalent to 6.1% of GDP, better than the 5.9% outturn in the same period last year and the government’s year-end target of 5.6%. The government is targeting to spend P1.47tn this year, up 4% YoY. Higher-than-expected spending was attributed to accelerated infrastructure spending of the DPWH especially for carry-over and ongoing projects, and the direct payments made by development partners for foreign-assisted rail projects of the DOTr. For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP.

Government records budget surplus of P6bn in October as revenues rebound

The government recorded a budget surplus of P6.3bn in October, reversing the deficit of P34bn in Oct 2023. This was due to strong revenue collections. Revenue collections in October were 23% YoY higher, with tax revenues up 17% YoY and non-tax revenues up 82% YoY. BIR collections rose 19% in October, accelerating from the 13% pace in 9M24 due to higher collections of VAT, personal/corporate income taxes, documentary stamp tax, excise tax on tobacco and percentage taxes. Customs collections rose 11.5% YoY in Oct, much faster than the 5% pace in 9M24. Expenditures rose 11%, despite the 6% YoY decline in interest payments. The latter was attributed to “timing issues.” Primary spending rose 14% YoY, accelerating from the 5% pace in 3Q24 and 9% pace in 9M24. The rebound in primary spending was attributed to (a) the release of the first tranche of gov’t employee salary adjustments, the (b) release of FY2022 performance-based bonus for the Department of Education, as well as (c) higher spending on public works, transportation, and social/health projects. Brings YT-October budget deficit to P964bn, lower by 5% YoY. The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. As of 9M24, the budget deficit-to-GDP ratio stood at 5.14%.

October Digital payments surge 40% to P1.6trn a new record high

 

Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) surged 40.3% YoY in October, accelerating sharply from the 33% in September, and 29% in August. Combined PesoNet and InstaPay payments also jumped 10% MoM to P1,620bn (roughly $27.5bn), a new all-time high. Both platforms – Instapay and PesoNet – also hit record highs for the month. For the 10M24 period, digital transactions hit P13,989bn (c$218bn), growing 35% YoY, faster than the 29% pace in FY2023.

Real-time, small-ticket transactions over InstaPay amounted to P676bn, up 48.0% YoY and 9% MoM, posting the fastest monthly YoY growth since February this year. This took the year-to-date total to P5,820bn, up 45.3% YoY, and faster than FY23’s 42% growth . By number of transactions, InstaPay volumes grew 53% YoY. While still quite impressive, this was much slower than the 67% YoY pace the prior month.

For larger payments over PesoNet, the October figure surged 35.3% YoY and 11% MoM to P944bn. The pace of growth was markedly faster than the 26% in September, and 20% in August. The 10-month total stood at P8,168bn, up 27.9% YoY, compared to the 22% growth in FY23.

Adoption could improve further if the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) succeeds in eliminating all P2P (person-to-person) transaction costs. At present, banks and wallets charge as high as P25 (about $0.42) per transaction over InstaPay, or about 0.5% of the average InstaPay payment of about P5,250 in 10M24. Bringing the fee to zero would encourage even lower-ticket payments to be done digitally.

 

Trust AUMs jump 9% QoQ, 20% YoY to P7.2trn

Trust Assets of banks and non-banks regulated by the BSP stood at P7,209bn ($122bn), jumping 20.1% YoY and 9.1% QoQ. We reckon a significant portion of the growth came from mark-to-market gains, as both equity and fixed-income markets performed well QoQ. The PSEi gained 13% in between June and September, while the 10-year BVAL fell 54.4bps from 6.03% to 5.49% in the same period.

Of the bank-owned Trust departments, BDO and BPI still stand head-and-shoulders above the pack. BDO still leads with consolidated AUM of P2,190bn, up 15% YoY and 8.1% QoQ, holding a 30.4% market share. BPI, meanwhile, saw its AUM grow 25.6% YoY and 8.6% QoQ to P1,464bn, with market share at 21.6%. Metrobank was still a far third at P548bn, up 10% YoY and QoQ. Security Bank saw AUM surge 24.3% QoQ to P129bn. (We still don’t have full details per institution, particularly for the non-banks. Note that RCBC Trust was spun off in 1Q24, and is now just 40% owned, its AUMs are not disclosed in the quarterly PSE filings.)

ATRAM  Trust Corp was in the news lately. First, its parent company announced the acquisition of First Metro Asset Management Inc (FAMI) from Metrobank. Second, Union Bank disclosed that it would merge UBP Trust with ATRAM. Based on ATRAM’s 2Q24 data, and UBP’s 3Q24 data, the merged entity would have a combined AUM of P461bn. We do not have hard data on FAMI’s AUM. Either way, the merged entity would significantly close the gap between them and Metrobank.

We continue to hold that the local institutions could be a major driver for the local equities market. As benchmark interest rates fall, a shift of AUM weights from fixed income to equities would have a powerful impact, especially given the relatively thin trading liquidity. However, the potential of a slower rate-cutting pace could delay the re-allocation.

DOE sees over 17,000MW of committed power plant projects by 2030

Latest data from the Department of Energy (DOE) showed that 17,250MW of projects are expected to come online between 2024 to 2030. These projects have secured financial closure or are already in construction stage. 9,142 MW are renewable (RE) projects.

In the Luzon grid alone, 14,795MW of projects have been committed to be completed by 2030. For the remainder of 2024, 1,476MW will come online, of which 880 MW is nat gas, 150 MW is coal, and 446MW are renewable energy. For 2025, a total of 4,453MW are expected to come online, of which 350MW is coal and 440 MW is natural gas. In 2023, the Luzon grid’s dependable capacity stood at 17,478 MW (of which ~12,000MW is baseload), while peak demand stood at 12,550 MW. DOE is seeing power demand increasing by 6% annually until 2028.
