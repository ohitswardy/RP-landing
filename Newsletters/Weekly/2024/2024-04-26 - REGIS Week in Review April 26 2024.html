REGIS REPORT

26 April 2024

The PCOMP capped the week on a positive note, inline with the widespread uptick across the broader region, as net foreign selling finally abated after a 14-day outflow streak. The benchmark initially started flattish at the 6400-level on Monday, then reclaimed the psychological 6500-level and posted its best back-to-back gains so far this year through the next 2 sessions. It found further support on final session of the week and ultimately settled at 6628 +2.88% WoW, posting its first weekly gain in 4 weeks. Alongside the easing in outflows however came lightened turnover, which averaged only $77m daily.

Biggest gainers in the benchmark were JGS +21.5% WoW and ACEN +13.17%, which had been heavy laggards YTD and touched new 52-week lows during the previous week, signaling a turn into value plays. Furthermore, ACEN has also come into focus amid the frequency red and yellow power alerts all over the country recently. We deem that the stock is among the best ways to play the tight power supply theme given the company’s positive spot exposure and recently completed incremental solar capacity.

Big corporate headline this week was the official announcement of DMC +7.74% WoW, SCC +8.33% and privately held Dacon Corp’s acquisition of CHP. There was heightened volatility in CHP with the stock swinging within a wide Php1.22-2.3/share range this week, but it ultimately ended with a hefty -37.04% WoW slump to Php1.36 after the official announcement that came out post-market on Thursday showed that the acquisition price is at roughly ~Php1.4/share. The transaction will effectively give DMC a 51% stake in CHP while Dacon Corp. and SCC will each own 28.9% and 10%, respectively. As such, our sense is that DMC may be less likely to pay another sizable dividend in 2H, while SCC on the other hand may be more compelled to do so, precisely to help the former fund the deal.

Net foreign activity remained skewed to sell this week by another sizable $49m – note however that a large part of this was the one-off $30m outflow block in APX +9.71% WoW which was reflected on Wednesday. Furthermore, bulk of the selling outside of APX was through the earlier part of the week. Foreign investors finally ended their long 14-day net selling streak and have turned net buyers, albeit very lightly, over the past 2 sessions. Top inflow names this week were BPI +0.81%, ICT -0.61%, JGS +21.53%, MWC +11.95% and MBT +3.58%, while top outflow outside of APX were ALI +5.74%, BDO -1.03%, AC +0.17% and SMPH +3.85%.

BDO Unibank - 1Q24 loans jump 13%, but NIMs pinched; declares cash, property dividends
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1Q24 NPAT up 12% YoY, in line; ups cash dividend payout

BDO reported headline NPAT of P18.5bn, up 12% YoY, broadly in line with Regis (P79.6 bn for FY24E) and consensus (P80.6bn). Loan growth accelerated to 13% YoY, from the 9% pace in 4Q23. However, NIMs fell 12bps QoQ due to rising funding costs. Asset quality remained benign, with NPLs at 1.88% and NPL cover at 181%. Meanwhile, BDO will up its quarterly dividend to P1/sh (from P0.75), and distribute the SMKL-related Treasury shares as a property dividend. The bank will host a briefing on Monday 22 April.

Loan growth jumps 13%, fastest since 2018; but funding costs pinch NIMs

Customer loans grew 13% YoY, up from the 9.1% posted in 4Q23, and the fastest YoY growth since late 2018 (Fig 1). NIMs were squeezed, though, down 12bps QoQ (Fig 2). Loan yields were down 9bps QoQ (Fig 3), though management says this is partly due to distortion from “other receivables”. Overall asset yields were flat QoQ. Deposit costs continued to rise, up 8bps QoQ (Fig 4), pushing total funding costs up 13bps QoQ. CASA ratio dipped slightly to 70% (from 72% in 4Q).

Fee incomes moderate, asset quality stays benign

Other income grew 11%, driven by trading/fx gains, up 33% YoY, while fee income grew 5% YoY. Opex jumped 15% YoY due in large part to stamp duties from time deposits (TDs). Asset quality, meanwhile, was benign, with NPL ratio at 1.88% (was 1.85% in 4Q) and NPL cover at 181% (was 185%).

Raises regular cash div; to pay out Treasury shares as property dividend

BDO upped its regular quarterly cash dividend to P1/sh (from 0.75/sh) starting 2Q24, raising its annual total to P3.75/sh in 2024E and P4/sh in 2025E for a dividend yield of 2.5% and 2.7% respectively (vs our forecast of 2.0%). The bank also disclosed it will distribute Treasury shares – equivalent to c1% of total shares outstanding – arising from its merger with its 100%-owned subsidiary SM Keppel Land (disclosed in January) as a property dividend. We estimate the merger will increase CET1/CAR by about 50bps.

 
Philippine Seven - Operating leverage drives 4Q23 margins; store expansion strategies continue
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Interest income boosts 4Q23 net profit; operating profit in line

Philippine Seven’s net profit in FY23 jumped 69% YoY to P3.5bn, ahead of Regis estimates. The FY outperformance was primarily due to higher-than-expected interest income in 4Q, which jumped. In 4Q alone, net profit grew 64% YoY. For the full year, operations were generally in line, with revenues +25% YoY to P78bn and operating profit +43% YoY to P5bn. SEVN ended the year with 3,768 new stores, a net addition of 375 stores, 11% higher vs end-2022.

Higher foot traffic drives 1Q24 SSSG

4Q revenues rose 20% YoY on the back of +9.5% SSSG. While SSSG was slower vs previous quarters, the YoY base is also higher. SSSG continued to be driven by growth in customer traffic.

Favorable sales mix, operating leverage drives margins

Operating profit was higher by 36% YoY during the quarter. This was partly driven by better gross margins (+40bps) resulting from the availment of early payment discounts from suppliers. The GPM improvement was also a result of a more favorable sales mix on higher sales contribution from foodservice. Operating leverage from the new stores further boosted operating margin (+90bps YoY).

Store expansion, CVS+ strategies continue, dividends expected soon

For 2024, the company plans a net addition of at least 400 stores (Regis FY24E: 450 stores) as it continues to expand in underserved areas, particularly in the Visayas and Mindanao regions. These areas will account for bulk (>50%) of the new stores programmed for 2024-25. Management also continues to develop its CVS+ model as to further drive basket size growth and defend market share amidst growing competition from other small-format groceries. Meanwhile, management intends to declare dividends soon, pending regulatory approvals. SEVN was in a net cash position of ~P10bn as of end-2023.

 
Bank of the Philippine Islands - 1Q24 NPAT beats, as RBC merger boosts consumer push
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1Q24 NPAT up 26% YoY, loans jump 18.7% (+11.9% ex-RBC)

BPI reported NPAT of P15.3bn in 1Q24, up 26% YoY, and beating Regis forecasts (27% of FY24E) which are near the top of the Street. The figures incorporate the merger with Robinsons Bank (RBC) effective 1 Jan. Loans grew 18.7% YoY, with BPI estimating that even ex-RBC, loans grew 11.9% YoY, faster than the 10.5% pace in 4Q23. RBC boosted BPI’s consumer push, helping lift NIMs. Asset quality deteriorated, but due largely to loan mix. Profits continued to benefit from the bank’s effort to gradually reduce NPL over-provisioning.

NIMs boosted by consumer loans +38%, aided by RBC merger

Overall loans grew 18.7% YoY, helped by the merger with RBC. Consumer loans continued to surge, up 38% YoY, accelerating from 21% in 4Q23, with RBC contributing significantly to mortgage, auto, and personal loans (including teacher loans). Corporate loans were strong, too, up 13.2% YoY, up sharply from the 7.0% pace in 4Q23. BPI estimates 1Q24 NIMs were up 25bps YoY and 5bps QoQ. This was despite higher funding costs as CASA ratio dropped to 65% from 67% in 4Q23 and from 70% in 1Q23.

Fees surged 27% YoY as customers hit 12mn, opex in check post-merger

BPI added 1.3mn customers in 1Q24 alone, to 12.0mn up from 10.7mn at end[1]2023, with half the growth coming from digital channels. The count does not yet include RBC, as the bank is still assessing overlaps. Fee incomes surged 27% YoY as a result, driven by credit card fees (+28%), wealth management (+26%), and insurance (+184%), among others. Opex was up 20% YoY (in line) as the merger increased headcount by 11% and branches by 20%.

NPLs jump due to loan mix; NPAT helped by lower NPL cover

The aggressive growth in consumer loans pushed NPL ratios up 28bps QoQ, as the bank seems willing to take on more risk for higher margins. While provision expense jumped 50% to P1.5bn, this was far below our FY expectations. The bank continues to run down its NPL cover (136% in 1Q24 vs 156% in 4Q23), and guided for similar quarterly provisions through the year.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land to continue buying back shares

Ayala Land President Anna Maria Margarita Dy said that the company would continuing buying back shares as long as the share price was well below its fair value. The company has been buying back shares almost daily since late March and has spent about P2.5bn. It still has c. P15bn left in its buyback program, equivalent to about 4% of market cap. ALI is trading at 45% discount to NAV, 1.4X FY24E P/B, and 15.6X FY24E P/E.

OceanaGold PHL sets final IPO price at P13.33/sh

OceanaGold (Philippines) Inc. announced the final offer price for its upcoming IPO at P13.33/sh (vs max target or P17.28/sh). The IPO will consist of 456mn secondary shares with listing date set on May 13. The IPO price is equivalent to 20x FY23 PER and translates to a dividend yield of 12%.

Metro Pacific Tollways prefers term extension over toll hike

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) is leaning toward getting its concession agreements extended instead of securing tariff hikes. The company is discussing with relevant agencies how it can recover its capex for all of these efforts. The NLEX concession is set to expire in 2037 but periodic tariff hikes are set to be halved by 2027. Regis estimates NLEX accounts for over 80% of MPTC’s net income while MPTC accounts for 23% of Metro Pacific’s gross profits (i.e. before head office expenses). Metro Pacific is a 20%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 11% of NAV. GTCAP trades at a 53% discount to its market-based NAV of P1250/sh.

Premium Leisure revenue falls 40% in 1Q24

Total revenue of Premium Leisure Corp (PLC PM) fell 40%. Its gaming share revenue fell 44% YoY (32% QoQ) to P401mn. PLC holds the license for City of Dreams Manila and has an EBITDA sharing agreement with Melco Resorts Philippines.

SM Prime to delay REIT IPO

During a briefing after the company’s ASM, SMPH President Jeffrey Lim said he does not expect the company’s REIT IPO to push through this year, due to market conditions (the high interest rate environment). SM Prime’s Pasay reclamation project will still be completed in 2028 (as scheduled). The total reclamation cost for the 360-hectare reclamation will be US$2.5bn-US$3bn, slightly lower than our estimates. The company also said that its China malls were performing well. It will expand SM Xiamen next year and will complete one new mall annually in 2025 and 2026. Future China expansion will be predominantly in Fujian province. SMPH will complete four Philippine malls this year and will add c. 5% to Philippine mall floor area. It will also launch 8,000-10,000 affordable housing units, mostly in provincial areas. The company launched 7,300 units in 2023. The company will also complete 130,000sqm of offices in Pampanga, Bacolod, and Iloilo.

Ayala Corp’s St. Joseph Drug acquisition triggers intensive PCC review

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) found possible competition concerns in AC Health’s pharma arm, AHCHI Pharma Ventures (APV), proposed purchase of a 49% stake in St. Joseph Drug. Under its Phase 1 review, PCC pointed to competition concerns in 28 localities in the Ilocos and Cordillera Administrative regions. It will conduct a Phase 2 revies that requires more detailed and extensive assessment on whether the transaction may lead to a substantial lessening of competition in the relevant markets. APV operates the Generika Drugstore and is also engaged in the pharmaceutical importation and distribution via Medica and MedEthix. AC Health currently has 742 Generika stores; It is targeting to have 1,000 stores by 2025. St. Joseph Drug has more than 112 stores in North Luzon.

OceanaGold (Philippines) faces legal battle ahead of IPO

An advocacy group called Legal Rights and Natural Resources Center (LRC) filed a petition for certiorari last April 22 at the Bayombong Regional Trial Court seeking to reverse the renewed. FTAA issued to OceanaGold (Philippines) (OGPI). LRC claimed that the renewal of OGPI’s FTAA lacked consultation with the communities concerned and disregarded the autonomy of the local government unit. LRC also claimed that OGPI violated the provincial ordinance against open-pit mining. OGPI’s FTAA expired in 2019 but was renewed in 2021 for another 25 years. OGPI said that it complied with the requirement of community endorsements prior to the grant of permit to operate the mine. Henry Guay, the captain of Brgy. Didipio, said thousands of village residents support the operations of Didipio mine in their locality. OGPI has scheduled its market debut on May 13 as it seeks to raise as much as P7.88bn from its IPO.

BPI headline NPAT P15.3bn, AHEAD of Regis, consensus

In a (very) limited press release, BPI (BPI PM) disclosed 1Q24 NPAT of P15.3bn, up 26% YoY, ahead of Regis estimates (27% of our FY24E of P56.5bn) and of consensus (P55.9bn). The results already incorporate the merger with Robinsons Bank (RBC). While our forecasts already include the merger, the YoY numbers are a bit distorted by it.

 

EPS was up a slower 18.1% due to the dilution from the merger.

Loans jumped 18.7% YoY. However, even without the RBank merger, BPI estimates loan growth would have been 11.9% YoY, faster than the 10.5% registered in 4Q23. Deposits grew 12.8%, with CASA up 4%, taking CASA ratio down to 64.8% from 67% in 4Q23 and 70% in 1Q23.

 

BPI estimates NIMs jumped 25bps YoY due in large part to the high margin loan book of RBC, which helped BPI’s personal loan book surge +147.4% YoY. Opex was up 20%, driven by the merger, and by BPI’s continued investment in manpower and IT systems.

 

Asset quality metrics were also affected by the RBC merger, as the smaller bank had significantly higher NPL ratio (3.45% at end-2023 vs BPI’s 1.84) and NPL Cover (65% vs BPI’s 156%). As a result, the combined entity reported NPL ratio of 2.12% and NPL cover of 136.2%. The press release did not disclose provision expense for the quarter.

Suntrust Resort Holdings secures US$38mn loan for casino project

Suntrust Resort Holdings (SUN PM) secured a US$38mn loan from LET Group Holdings. SUN is the Philippine unit of Hong Kong’s LET Group. The loan will mature in 10 years. The proceeds will be used for the construction of a casino complex in Alliance Global’s section of PAGCOR Entertainment City. The Main Hotel Casino will have 475 rooms and ballroom that can accommodate 550 people. It will also have a performing arts theatre and a grand opera house. As of end 2023, structural works and the construction of the tower façade up to roof-level had been completed. SUN is targeting to open the complex in 1Q25. At that time, the casino will have 281 gaming tables, 1,126 slot machines, and 134 electronic gaming tables.

ICTSI, ATI ask for 17% cargo-handling tariff hike

Asian Terminals Inc. (ATI PM) and International Container Terminal Services, Inc. (ICT PM) has petitioned for a 17% hike in cargo-handling tariffs. The last cargo-handling tariff rate adjustment for the two ports was in 2021, when PPA granted a 10% rate increase implemented in two tranches—2% in 2021 and 8% in 2022. Regis has incorporated a 16% tariff hike to be implemented in two tranches of 8%, beginning Oct 2024, for the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT). MICT accounts for 23% of volumes and 27% of revenues. ICT trades at 15.7x FY24E PER and 8.1x FY24E EV/EBITDA, below peers (17x/11x) and historical average (22x/11x).

Megaworld sells 40mn MREIT shares

Megaworld sold 40.65mn MREIT shares in a block sale transaction. This represents 1.5% of shares outstanding. The shares were sold at P12.30/share which was a 4% discount from the previous close. After the transaction, Megaworld will still own 54% of MREIT. In 2023, MREIT signed an MOU for the acquisition of up to 150,000sqm of office space from Megaworld. This could expand MREIT’s portfolio by up to 46% 475,000sqm. MREIT currently offers FY24E dividend yield of 7.9% (based on consensus forecast).

UnionBank to conduct P10bn stock rights offering

UnionBank will conduct a stock rights offering at P33.73/share to P38.23/share. This is an 8%-19% discount from the last closing price before the news came out. The 296.5mn rights shares (provided that in no case shall the aggregate value of the offer exceed P10bn) represent 10% of current shares outstanding. Pricing Date is on May2 and Ex Date is on May 8. Meanwhile, offer period was set for May 16-24 with a tentative listing date of May 31.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BOI-approved investment commitments reach P607bn

According to the Board of Investments (BOI), approved investment commitments reached P607.22bn as of April 17 with a total of 117 projects, up 15% from P527.24bn in the same period from January to April 2023. Domestic investors accounted for 80% or P494.37bn of the investment pledges. Majority of the projects are in renewable energy with the biggest being a 1400MW hydropower and pumped storage hydroelectric plant from Ahunan Power Inc. which will cost P296.98bn. The BOI approved P1.26tn in investments last year with 66% or P763.22bn coming from foreign investments and is targeting to approve P1.3tn to P1.5tn in investments this year.

Balance of payments post US$1.2bn surplus in March

Preliminary data showed the country posted a US$1.17bn BOP surplus in March. This was slightly lower than the US$1.27bn surplus recorded in March 2023 but reversed the US$196mn BOP deficit in February 2024 due to inflows from the National Government’s net foreign currency deposits and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. This brings 1Q24 BOP level to US$238mn surplus, lower than the US$3.5bn surplus recorded in 1Q23. The BOP reflects an increase in the GIR level to US$104.1bn in March from US$102bn in end-Feb, equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports.

President Marcos issues order easing agri import process

President Marcos signed Administrative Order 20 to further streamline administrative procedures and policies on the importation of agricultural products and remove non-tariff barriers. Nontariff barriers include quotas, import licensing systems, regulations and red tape that restrict or delay the entry of imports. Processing of Sanitary and Phytosanitary Import Clearance (SPSIC) should be done within 15 days; otherwise applications with completed documents would be deemed approved. The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) was also ordered to streamline and standardize existing guidelines within 30 days from the order’s effectivity to allow more traders and SRA-registered industrial users to participate in the sugar import program. DA, DTI, Philippine Competition Commission, and law enforcement agencies are ordered to create a surveillance team tasked to ensure the implementation of the AO.

Infrastructure spending rises by 5.3% YoY in 2M24

Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 5.3% YoY to P153.4bn in 2M24. The gov’t cited disbursements for road projects and defense modernization program. The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

Luzon, Visayas grids issued yellow and red alerts again

NGCP placed Luzon and Visayas grids under yellow and red alerts yesterday, March 23

Luzon grid was placed on red alert from 3-4 PM and 6-10 PM and yellow alert from 4-6 PM and 10-12 MN, citing an available capacity of 12,832MW with a peak demand of 12,671MW as 18 plants were on forced outage with 2 running on derated capacities, making for a total 1,519MW unavailable to the grid. Visayas grid was placed on yellow alert from 1-8 PM following an available capacity of 2,742MW and 2,571MW peak demand, with 20 plants on forced outage while 9 were running on derated capacities, for a total 604MW unavailable to the grid. At 3:30 PM yesterday, NGCP monitored a system disturbance that affected 4 power plants and aggravated the already thin power supply which affected GNPower 1 (316MW), Limay Block B (60MW), SCPC 1 (150MW), and SCPC 3 (150MW). Automatic Load Dropping (ALD) was implemented in parts of La Union, Tarlac, and Camarines Sur for roughly 15 minutes, and Manual Load Droppign (MLD) was implemented in parts of Isabela, Sorsogon, and Pangasinan for 1 hour.

Budget deficit narrows in March

The government recorded a budget deficit of P196bn in March, down 6.8% YoY due to higher non-tax revenues and muted spending. Revenues rose 11% YoY to P288bn, even though tax revenues dipped 0.5% YoY. Meanwhile, BIR collections rose 3% YoY to P145bn while customs collections fell 7% YoY. Non-tax revenues rose 81% YoY due to higher dividend remittances, interest on advances from the National Irrigation Administration and share of PAGCOR income. Expenses rose 3.2% on the back of a 17% jump in interest payments. Additionally, primary spending grew by a mere 1% YoY, bringing the primary deficit to P125bn, down 16% YoY. Primary spending was weighed down by the lower subsidies to government corporations and transfers to local government units (LGUs). This brings 1Q24 budget deficit to P273bn, up 0.6% YoY. Primary deficit in 1Q24 stood at P79.7bn, down 38% YoY. The government has set a budget deficit ceiling of P1.48tn this year, down 1.8% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. It also aims to reduce the deficit-to-GDP ratio to 3.7% by 2028.

DOE firm on coal plant moratorium as additional supply is underway

In a virtual press briefing, Energy Secretary Raphael P.M. Lotilla said the Department of Energy (DOE) will maintain the moratorium on the development of greenfield coal-fired plants. The gov’t cited at least 4,165MW worth of power projects are expected to come online this year.
