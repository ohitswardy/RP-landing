REGIS REPORT

17 May 2024

It was an even busier week in the Philippines, highlighted by another batch of corporate results, BSP policy decision, and announcement of changes for the upcoming MSCI rebalancing. The local benchmark surged at the onset, reclaiming the 6600-level on Monday, as the prior week’s CPI and GDP prints suggested a potential dovish turn in monetary policy outlook, ahead of the BSP meeting which took place on Thursday post-market. Stocks then found renewed support and held on to the 6600-level through the latter part of the of the week after the BSP held rates steady as widely expected, and more crucially, indeed struck a dovish tone in their subsequent statement. BSP Governor Remolona indicated a potential 25bp cut as early as August and raised the possibility of up to two rate cuts through the 2nd half of the year - this is inline with Regis’ forecast of a cut each in 3Q and 4Q, respectively.  

On the results front, our sense is that the recently concluded 1Q reporting season looks generally positive. Our tally of results shows that there are more beats than misses for PSEi constituents, 40% against 27%. The nominal growth 1Q earnings growth for index members stood at 6%, which on the surface looks low compared to our 9% FY24 estimate, but note that this was primarily weighed down by SMC -2.64% WoW. Excluding SMC, we estimate that 1Q earnings growth of PSEi members would be at 12%.

Top gainer this week was GLO +11.73% WoW which closed at the 2000-level and marked a fresh 52-week high. The company reported a core profit of P5.8b in 1Q, +13% YoY and better than street expectations. One key highlight is that the contribution of 36%-owned Mynt (Gcash) rose 138% to nearly P1b and now accounts for 17% of the company’s post-tax profit. The broader telco sector was quite strong this week amid their generally positive results: TEL +3.63% and CNVRG +10.81%. Note that the latter reported net subscriber additions of 125k in 1Q24, the highest net adds since 3Q21. This to us is quite notable given that TEL saw a net subscriber reduction (14k) for its wired broadband business.

Among the top winners again for 4 weeks in a row now was ACEN +8.65% WoW, helped this time by the announcement of its inclusion in the MSCI small cap index in the upcoming rebalancing on month-end. Our sense is that while the inflow itself may be modest, this at least puts ACEN in the radar for a potential return to the main cap benchmark down the line after its removal back in February 2023, given the recent trend of index ‘upgrades’ from small/mid cap into the main cap benchmark, rather than from completely outside and directly into standard index. Another bright area this week were the property large caps SMPH +9.16% and ALI +8.09%, buoyed by hopes of a dovish policy outlook in 2H. Furthermore, in terms of technicals we point out that ALI looks to be forming a double bottom in its daily chart.

On the other end, biggest laggard in the benchmark this week was AEV -6.04% WoW, dragged down by the official announcement of its upcoming removal in the MSCI standard cap index. Outflow estimates for AEV range from $100m-$120m (vs its ADV of ~$1m.) Other notable changes for the upcoming rebalancing on month-end include: downweights in AC +5.62% and SM -0.57% (of under $40m estimated outflow each), and slight upweight in SMPH +9.16% in the standard cap index; and ACEN +8.65% inclusion, and CEB +0.34% and SPNEC -3.57% deletion in the small cap index.

Market activity this week stood at a daily average of $166.1m, +49% WoW, inflated by the $105m OGP IPO on Monday and the $224m MWC (+2.31% WoW) block on Friday (sold by AC and bought by Razon’s Trident). Net foreign flow for the week turned skewed to buy by $50m, but this was again primarily due to the $60.5m foreign-subscribed portion of the $105m OGP IPO (-5.03% WoW). That being said, while foreign investors would be net sellers through most of the week excluding OGP, they did turn slight net buyers at the end of the week following the BSP’s dovish pronouncements. Top foreign sold stocks for the week were JFC +1.8%, SM -0.57%, BDO -0.07%, AEV -6.04% and ALI +8.09%; while top bought were GLO +11.73%, SMPH +9.16%, ACEN +8.65%, GTCAP +1.61% and TEL +3.63%.

Globe Telecom - 1Q24: Continuing mobile rebound; Mynt profit jumps
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income rises 13%

Globe reported net income of P6.8bn for 1Q24, down 7% due to lower gains from tower sales. Core profit, on the other hand, rose 13% to P5.8bn, better than Street expectations. Overall service revenue grew only 3%, but this was due to a drop in non-telco revenue (primarily from the sale of a subsidiary). More importantly, the recovery in mobile growth continues. The other key highlight was the sharp jump in earnings from fintech unit Mynt.

Mobile revenue up 8%, home broadband down 6%

Globe’s core mobile business saw revenue growth of 8% YoY, a continuation of the rebound first seen in 3Q23. Within mobile, data revenue increased 10% YoY. We estimate the relative market shares of mobile revenue between the two main operators were almost unchanged from 4Q23. Home broadband revenue, however, was down 6% YoY due to the erosion of its fixed wireless subscriber base. We had been expecting the latter to have ceased dropping already. Wired broadband, on the other hand, saw subscriber base rising 19k to 1.15m. We believe most of new subscribers are likely to be on the prepaid service.

Mynt profits on track to top P10bn

Income contribution from 36%-owned Mynt rose 138% to P962m and is now 17% of post-tax profit. The growth in profit came from new services on the platform, and we believe lending is likely the biggest driver. Grossing up Mynt’s 1Q contribution would imply its 1Q income was P2.7bn. At the 1Q pace, Mynt could make more than P10bn this year (almost US$200m).

Capex down 22%

Capex for 1Q was P13.7bn, lower by 22% YoY. The company previously guided that it is aiming to reduce capex this year to around US$1bn (~P57bn). Globe is targeting to be free cash flow positive by next year.

 
Ayala Corp. - Divests Manila Water stake
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Sells residual stake in Manila Water

Ayala Corp disclosed it has, through its subsidiaries, sold 578m common shares of Manila Water (MWC PM), representing ~19.6% economic interest in MWC, at P22.361/share. AC also agreed to the sale of 872m preferred shares in MWC at a price of P1.844/share. The preferred shares will be paid for in tranches up to 2019. The buyer of both blocks is Trident Water, a holding company of Enrique Razon, the current controlling shareholder of MWC.

The deal would raise P14.5bn for AC and would bring the total proceeds of its “value realization” program to over P50bn, equivalent to USD1bn when the plan was first unveiled.

Slight discount to market, premium to previous transactions

The transaction price of P22.361/share is a 7% discount to its 30-day VWAP. It is, however, at a premium to Razon’s previous purchase prices (~P17/share) and the most recent Regis target price for MWC (P18/share). We expect AC to post a modest gain (<P1bn) from the sale/s.

Cutting clean

The final completion of these transactions would mark the full exit of AC from the water utility, an exit prompted by adverse government actions a few years ago. AC founded MWC in 1997 after it was awarded one of two concessions in what is still the largest water utility privatization in the world. It decided to sell a controlling interest in MWC to Razon in 2020.

On to the next one

We view the transaction as positive for AC as it raises cash better deployed elsewhere. Even though it has already met its “value realization” goal, we believe AC still has some assets that are better held by other owners.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - 1Q24: Strong PH performance offsets soft int'l
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Operating profit +14% YoY – in line

JFC’s 1Q24 net profit to common (after pref divs and coupon distributions to perps) grew 32% YoY to P2.3bn, trending slightly behind Regis expectations but in line with consensus. Core operations, however, appear to be broadly in line, with consolidated revenues +11% YoY and operating profit +14% YoY.

Philippines leads 1Q24 SSSG; international performance mixed

JFC posted SSSG of 5.5% in 1Q24 led by the strong performance of the Philippines, which registered SSSG of +6.9% on higher transaction count. The international business was a mixed bag with EMEAA growing 14.7%, but SSS declining at Highlands Coffee (-9%), China (-3.7%) and Smashburger (-4.1%). The apparent weakness in China and Vietnam (Highlands) was attributed to a high base and soft demand. Meanwhile, the SSS decline of Smashburger was a result of a store network cleanup.

Solid Philippine results cushion decline of international segments

Philippines was the main driver of operating profit (90% of consolidated) with segment operating profit +17% on the strong top line and favorable input cost. The international business saw an 8% YoY decline in operating profit due to the softness in China and Vietnam.

On track to meet FY24 guidance; robust PH performance to continue

The company is on track to meet its guidance to grow operating profit by 10-15% in 2024. Management is expecting a continuation of the strong top line performance of the Philippine business for the rest of the year, and a recovery in China and Smashburger within 2H24. A recovery in the top line should lift operating margins, especially as requirements for key inputs have been secured for the balance of the year.

 
Philippine Seven - 1Q24 margins crimped by timing of supplier subsidies; cash div declared
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Net profit rises 21% YoY in 1Q24

SEVN reported net profit of P639mn in 1Q24, up 21% YoY, trending broadly in line with Regis expectations. Revenues rose 19% YoY to P20.6bn while operating profit grew 9% YoY to P930mn; both appear to be tracking within expectations. SEVN added 61 stores in 1Q, ending the period with 3,829 stores.

1Q24 SSSG +8.8% on Holy Week timing

Same store sales grew 8.8% on higher customer traffic (+9%). This is partly driven by base effects due to the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23), which is seasonally strong for 7-Eleven stores. Excluding the impact of the Holy Week, SSSG is estimated to be +4-5%.

Margins crimped by timing of subsidies

Operating margin contracted 40bps YoY due to lower gross margin (-120bps). Gross margins were crimped by the timing effect of the collection of other income and price subsidies from cigarette manufacturers. The GPM contraction was cushioned by higher service income as a % of sales (+20bps YoY) and operating leverage. We expect margins to recover in succeeding quarters as the subsidies are collected and recognized.

P9.60/sh cash dividend declared

Earlier this month, the company declared a cash dividend of P9.60/sh (ex-date: 16 May / payment date: 4 Jun), surpassing our estimates (P4.21/sh). This is equivalent to a total dividend payment of P7.3bn and translates to a yield of ~9% based on the last closing price. SEVN had P14.6bn net cash (excluding lease liabilities) as of end-1Q24. Moving forward, the company expects to maintain a regular dividend payout of 40% of prior year’s net profit.

by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Higher profits from banking and infra units offset by lower profits at auto unit

Reported core income of P7.06bn was 7% higher and included a c. P400mn gain from the sale of a lot in Ortigas. Removing the gain and pref divs, we estimate core profit was marginally up (+2%) at P6.57bn. The results are on track to beat our estimates but appear below consensus forecasts. Auto profit down 11% from high base

Profits from the auto unit dropped 11% YoY to P4.0bn on base effects from the recognition of tax credits from the CARS program last year. Profit would have been down only slightly (-1%) if we adjust for this. Toyota’s GPM inched higher to 14.7% on favorable currency movements and models mix. The company implemented across-the-board price hikes in May and guided for normalizing GPMs of 11-12% for the rest of the year. Metro Pacific: Higher volumes, tariffs lift 1Q profit up 30%

Infrastructure arm, Metro Pacific, grew its standalone core income by 30% to P5.6bn, buoyed by higher profit contribution from power (+4% YoY), Maynilad (+29%), and its tollroad unit (+8%). These were largely driven by higher sales volumes and tariff hikes.

Property: Reservation sales drop

Reservation sales fell 36% YoY to P3.9bn on lower sales of high-end JV projects in BGC, which are almost fully sold. Standalone reservation sales were up 6%. Reported net income rose 2% to P291mn. Puregold - 1Q24: Margin pressures persist
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q24 net profit +3% YoY as margin pressures persist

PGOLD reported net profit of P2.5bn in 1Q24, up 3% YoY, trending behind Regis expectations but in line with consensus. Net sales rose 7% YoY to P47.3bn, while operating profit grew more slowly (+3% YoY) to P3.7bn on lower margins. Both are tracking behind our FY24 forecasts. Margins continued to be weighed by competition and higher opex, particularly for the core Puregold brand.

Puregold: Margins weighed by competition, higher opex

Core Puregold brand net sales grew 6% YoY on the back of +1.5% SSSG. Same[1]store average baskets declined ~1%, while same store traffic grew 2.6%. Segment EBITDA margin contracted over 100bps YoY due to (1) reduced gross margin resulting from price matching vs hard discounters, (2) higher labor cost from minimum wage increases in 3Q23, and (3) proportionately higher opex from stores that were opened in 2H23.

S&R: Margins recover on favorable forex, supply chain improvements

S&R’s net sales rose 9% YoY on higher store count. Same store average baskets continued to contract (-1.9%), dragging SSS down 1.2% during the quarter. EBITDA margin, however, improved 150bps YoY due to favorable forex, price increases, and benefits of supply chain improvements

Margin expectations subdued

For FY24, management is looking to achieve GP margins of 15.5-16.5% for Puregold (Regis FY24E: 15.5%) and 21.5-22.5% for S&R (Regis FY24E: 21.5%). We believe margins for the core Puregold brand could continue to trend downward in the coming quarters (beyond the usual seasonality) as it continues to implement its price matching strategy and other promotional activities to help drive sales. Meanwhile, we believe margins for S&R could settle at the higher end of the guidance range given the upward adjustments in selling prices.

 
Shakey's Pizza - 1Q24: SSSG slows on downtrading, promotions drag margins
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q24 net profit -15% YoY on increased promotional spending

PIZZA’s net profit in 1Q24 declined 15% YoY to P171mn, tracking behind Regis expectations. The company reinvested gross margin gains into higher A&P, leading to a reduction in operating profits. Revenues were broadly in line at P3.1bn (+6% YoY), while operating profit is behind at P248mn (-16% YoY). Management expects margins to recover in succeeding quarters as sales pick up.

SSSG slows further to +1%

SSSG slowed to +1% (vs +2% in 4Q23) partly due to a high base in 1Q23. Underlying consumer spending was under pressure from the inflationary environment. PIZZA’s full-service brands (Shakey’s, Peri-peri) succumbed to downtrading, while Potato Corner outperformed given its value-for-money positioning.

Gross margin gains reinvested in promotional spending

Gross margin expanded 160bps YoY on favorable input cost. Against the soft demand backdrop, the GPM gains were reinvested in promotions and other brand-building activities, resulting a 16% YoY decline in operating profit.

Investments to continue; 2H24 recovery seen

The consumer environment will likely continue to face challenges in the very near term, even as the company keeps on reinvesting gains from lower input cost into brand-building efforts. Accelerating sales arising from a rebound in consumer sentiment coupled with operating leverage should lead to a more meaningful margin recovery in 2H24.

 
Cebu Air - 1Q24 profit beats on robust demand, higher fares
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Core profit up 47% YoY to P1.98bn – Ahead of estimates

Operations were boosted by favorable market dynamics in 1Q24, leading to core profit rising by 47% YoY and EBITDA jumping 61% to P6.64bn. The results are trending ahead of both Regis and Street estimates on better-than-expected fares and lower cash costs.

Capitalizing on favorable market dynamics

Pax volumes grew by 3% QoQ, 14% YoY to 5.5mn while average fares rose 6% QoQ, 9% YoY to P3,240, thanks to the strength in both domestic and international markets. Load factors inched higher by 1ppt OoQ, YoY to 84%, just 2ppts below pre-Covid levels. CEB was the only airline in the domestic market, which is operating above pre-Covid seat capacity, while its competitors PAL and Air Asia Phils are still at 15% and 30% below pre-Covid levels, respectively. Even though inbound tourism in the Philippines still lags pre-Covid levels, management noted that outbound tourism has been strong.

Strong forward bookings

Forward bookings are looking better in 2Q24 than 1Q24. Leisure sectors have been robust in 2Q while trunk routes are also strong for June and July. Given robust demand, management remains comfortable about its very minimal fuel hedging strategy, as they believe they can continue to raise fares without eroding demand.

Quasi-reorganization soon but dividend may be pushed back to next year

The company plans to process the planned quasi-reorganization of its capital soon, which will allow it to eliminate its retained earnings deficit and be able to declare dividends. However, ongoing negotiations with Airbus and Boeing for its planned 100-150 aircraft orders (delivery from 2029 onwards) are creating some near-term uncertainties on any upfront capex required for the deal. Thus, management is erring on the conservative side and has deferred the possibility of a dividend declaration in 4Q24 to next year. Note that the company’s convertible pref shares (CEBCP PM) accrue an annual dividend of P2.28/sh, and the accumulated unpaid dividends now amount to P2.15bn or P6.9/sh. CEBCP last traded at P31.85, compared to the P29.80/sh of CEB commons.

 
Manila Water - 1Q24 profit outperforms on unsustainably low cash costs
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q24 profit to common rises 37% to P2.74bn

Net income rose 37% to P3.12bn in 1Q24. After removing the share of participating prefs, we estimate profits to common stood at P2.74bn, trending well ahead of Regis and Street forecasts due to lower-than-expected cash costs at the core East zone (EZ) unit. Profits from its non-EZ units grew 17% YoY and contributed 8% to overall profitability.

East zone EBITDA up 29% YoY; Spending trails

The East zone’s standalone profit rose 34% to P2.79bn while EBITDA jumped 21% to P6.99bn. Revenues grew 21% to P7.0bn, buoyed by (1) the implementation of the second tranche of tariff hikes and (2) a 4% YoY improvement in billed volumes to 129mcm, or 8% above 1Q18 levels. Management attributed the strong growth in billed volumes to the unusually hot weather, citing that demand from residential accounts are already 9% above pre-pandemic levels while commercial/industrial accounts are still 9% below pre-pandemic levels.

More importantly, cash costs grew by just 4% YoY to P1.89bn, trending well below its annual programmed opex target of P15bn. Capex rose 66% to P4.2bn, also on trend to fall below its regulatory commitment of P20bn annually. We reiterate that the persistent underspending is actually not desired and could result in the erosion of its recoverable investments (i.e. Opening Cash Position) and eventually, a tariff clawback.

Non-East zone PH units boosted by tariff hikes; Foreign units face issues

Profit contributions from its non-EZ PH units rose 76% YoY, underpinned by tariff hikes across each business segment. Profits from its foreign units, however, declined 91% YoY due to various regulatory issues.

Management admitted that there are only a handful of acquisition opportunities that will fit its strategy, as it prefers to acquire projects that will generate immediate cash returns and will not erode margins.

1Q24 profit of P202mn is trending below Regis, Consensus estimates

NIKL’s profit dropped 79% YoY to P202mn in 1Q24. The weak results can largely be attributed to the softness in global nickel prices. Even though LME nickel prices have rebounded QoQ in 2Q, we are reviewing our estimates. It is also looking more unlikely that special dividends will be declared in 2H, as NIKL wants to preserve its cash for possible M&A opportunities locally and abroad.

ASP drop more than offset mild improvement in sales volumes

The mining unit generated revenues of P2.09bn in 1Q24, lower by 41% YoY on the back of a 48% YoY decline in ASPs to US$13.8/MT. The two HPAL plants also did poorly. THNC barely broke even and the more inefficient plant of CBNC recorded a standalone net loss of P1.28bn. Despite the rebound in LME nickel price in 2Q (spot at US$19.5k/MT), it is probable that these plants remain in the red.

New plant, higher WESM prices drive 1Q24 profits for power unit

The power unit was the sole bright spot, as EBITDA rose 40% YoY to P210mn while profit rose 83% YoY to P67mn. They also benefited from high WESM prices in the quarter, as they had 10% of excess output that was sold to the spot market.

ICTSI Batangas greenfield expansion

Greenfield expansion in Batangas. ICTSI announced it is investing a total of US$800mn to convert its wholly-owned Bauan International Port, which largely services Ro-Ro vessels today, into an international container port, with a fully built-out capacity of over 2mn TEUs.  The investment will be in phases, with the construction of Phase 1 slated to start in 1Q25 and will be fully completed with a capacity of 800-900k TEUs by end-2027.  Meanwhile, investments for Phase 2 will be volume dependent.

New port will likely address capacity limits of Manila ports. Batangas is currently only being serviced by one gateway port, Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) Batangas Container Terminal (BCT), which is as an alternative port for shippers based in the Calabarzon (Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal, and Quezon). Once built, ICT’s port in Bauan will directly compete with ATI’s port.

Regis believes, however, that the key rationale for this investment is to address the long-term capacity limitations at the ports of Manila. Combined throughput volumes at the three foreign container ports servicing Metro Manila and southern Luzon reached 4.3mn TEUs in 2023 while combined capacities stood at 5.2mn TEUs. We anticipate overall capacity at these ports can only grow up to 5.7mn TEUs by 2027 largely coming from ICT’s ongoing Berth 8 expansion.  With incremental demand at these ports projected to grow by c. 200k-250k TEUs annually, we anticipate capacity bottlenecks at the three ports by 2028/2029 with ICT’s Bauan port likely to get future incremental volumes.  This assumes, however, that ATI will not expand the Batangas port and other announced, possible competing ports will not push through (i.e. Bataan port of India-based Adani Ports).

Similar yields as MICT, without port fees. Management envisions the Bauan port will have similar yields as MICT (>US$200 per TEU). Even though this guidance looks high relative to the tariffs of BCT, Regis believes such yields can only be achieved if capacity at existing ports will not be sufficient to meet demand.  Moreover, given that the port will be privately-owned and will not require a license from the government, ICT’s Bauan port will also not be paying any port fees. 

Funding for phase 1 is manageable. Management plans to spend US$300mn over the first three years for the construction of Phase 1.  ICT’s gross debt (inclusive of perps and lease/concession liabilities) amounted to US$5.3bn while net debt stood at US$4.2bn in end-March 2024. Even if the entire additional US$300mn of borrowings are assumed, Regis estimates its net debt-to-EBITDA will rise to just 2.9x from 2.7x in end-March.

Ayala Corp sells remaining Manila Water stake

Ayala Corp. (AC PM) announced it is selling its entire remaining 22.5% economic stake (23.5% voting) in Manila Water (MWC PM) to Trident Water Company, a subsidiary’s of Mr. Enrique Razon’s Prime Infrastructure, for ~P14.5bn. The transaction valued MWC at ~P22/sh, a 7% discount to its 30-day VWAP. AC said that proceeds from this sale and other previous asset sales under its US$1bn divestment plan have amounted to ~P51.5bn. Regis believes AC has other assets to sell such as its 35% stake in LRT-1 and other underperforming assets under AC Industrials.

Ayala Corp 1Q profit up 26% to P12bn

Ayala Corp core profit rose 26% to P12bn, which appears ahead of Street estimates. Headline income was P13.1bn, up 28%, as all major listed subsidiaries saw double-digit increases in core income: BPI income up 26%, ALI 39%, ACEN 34% and Globe 13%.

Bloomberry 1Q24 EBITDA falls 14% YoY, behind

Bloomberry’s 1Q24 net profit fell 11% to P2.6bn, as the unusually strong win rate and record slot volumes were not quite enough to offset declines in VIP rolling chip and mass table drop. Meanwhile, EBITDA fell 14% to P4.9bn - since Regis expects Solaire Resorts North to post EBITDA (and net profit) losses during its first six months of operations, we would consider this result as being behind. Pre-operating expenses from Solaire Resorts North amounted to P280mn - if these were added back, then EBITDA would have fallen 10% YoY (to P5.2bn) and net profit would have declined 3% YoY (to P2.9bn). VIP rolling chip fell for the third consecutive quarter to P107bn (-36% YoY, -18% QoQ), as volumes from junkets (across the region) were particularly weak. Management expects the VIP business to remain weak since there continue to be limitations on the movement of funds from China. Slot chip-in rose 17% YoY and 7% QoQ to P98bn, a record level and 50% higher than the 2019 average. The company noted that in 1Q24, some mass table players migrated to slots. Solaire Resorts North, which will open on May 25, will have about the same number of slot machines as Solaire Entertainment City.

Converge 1Q24 results – Strong subscriber additions

Subscriber additions rise to 125k, highest since 3Q21.  Converge reported net subscriber additions of 125k in 1Q24, of which 46k (37%) were prepaid.  This is the highest net adds figure since 3Q21 and is, in our view, the highlight of the quarter’s results. In contrast, PLDT saw a net subscriber reduction (14k) for its wired broadband business.

Reported earnings up 17%. Revenues rose 10% due to the increased subscriber count. Net income, however, jumped 17% to P2.55m (vs P2.17bn), with much of that increase due to the swing in “Other Income” (P381m, pretax), which came from lower provisions on modems, as well as FX gains and higher interest income.  Note that its self-reported EBITDA of P5.8bn, up +13%, includes “Other income”, which makes it not comparable with other telcos.

Capex deferred. CNVRG reduced capex guidance to P12-14bn for FY24 (vs. P17-19bn), but this is really a deferral of expenditures to FY25. Delays in its international sub-sea cable investment was the reason for this drop but FY25 will rise as a result.

Dividends in 2025. Management intends to set out its dividend policy by late this year and pay out its first dividend in 2025.

LTG results comment

Profits up slightly in 1Q24.  LTG reported net profit of P6.42bn for 1Q24, barely up (<1%) from the prior year.  Contributions from the bank rose but the tobacco segment continued to shrink. While the headline profit looks better than Street estimates, the business outlook is looking challenging, especially for tobacco.  However, the long-awaited distribution of PNB’s property assets could finally boost LTG’s underlying value.

Tobacco segment remains under pressure. Profits for the tobacco business dropped 13% to P2.66bn. Tobacco volumes dropped 18%, worse than the (legal) industry’s 11%, as PMFTC continued to lose ground.  PMFTC’s market share is now down to 53% vs 55% last year.  This situation is compounded by the continuing rise of illicit tobacco.

PNB income up 10% on lower provisions.  PNB’s income was up 10% to P5.31bn, despite lower PPOP. Loan growth was 4% up and net interest income rose 12%, but fee income and other income  were both lower. Provision expenses were reduced by P1bn to P620m.

Liquor volumes contract. Tanduay’s liquor volumes fell 13% and revenue dropped 5% due to price increases. Profits, however, managed to stay almost unchanged (-1%). The company claims it managed to increase market despite the fall in volumes. Based on estimates from third party surveys, market share of Tanduay was 31.6% in 1Q24, up from 29.1%.

Property dividend finally?  Almost  exactly three years ago, PNB announced the distribution of 51% of its 100% ownership of PNB Holdings (PHC) to its shareholders as a property dividend.  The bank says it has finally received the eCARs relating to the property dividend covering shareholders owning 97% of its shares. The distribution of the PHC shares (as property dividends) would begin in the next few months.

LTG, being owner of 56.5% of PNB, would receive 28.8% (=56.5% x 51%) of PHC as a dividend. Assuming PHC is worth P35bn (our estimate), the firm would receive ~P10bn worth of PHC shares.

SECB 1Q24 loans jump 12%; NIMs still muddied by interest rate swaps

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT for 1Q24 of P2.63bn, up 11% YoY, seemingly just a shade behind REGIS (22% of FY24F of P12.0bn) but well ahead of consensus (FY24F P10.8bn). That said there were one-off gains and expenses, though the bank claims these canceled each other out. The nature of the one-offs was not mentioned in the press release. Some highlights:

Loan growth accelerated to 12% YoY, from 7% in 4Q23. Consumer/MSME loans again took the lead, surging 32% YoY (from 29% in 4Q23). But corporate loans also posted 4% YoY growth, reversing the -0.5% print in 4Q23. On the consumer side, mortgages grew 18% YoY, while the bank saw explosive growth in auto loans (+46%), credit cards (+49%), and MSME loans (+74%). Consumer/MSME loans now account for 31% of total loans, up from 29% at end-2023, and a new record high by our reckoning.

NIMs were again muddled by the accounting of interest rate swaps, which showed artificially low deposit costs, but which booked the differential as a forex loss. On the upside, loan yields stayed flat QoQ, despite the increase in corporate loans. As before, we expect management to give their estimates of “pro-forma” NIMs assuming the swaps were treated as interest expense.

Other income was boosted by a chunky (not quite one-off) P1.8bn milestone fee from SECB’s bancassurance partner, without which fees would be flattish YoY.

Provision expense more than doubled YoY. This is consistent with management’s guidance in the FY23 briefing of one more quarter of cleanups relating to legacy credit card delinquencies. NPL ratio was flat QoQ at 3.4%, while NPL cover edged down to 81% from 83% in 4Q23.

It is targeting to grow its store network to 790 in 2024 (from 740 in 2023, 744 in 1Q24).

BSP maintains policy rate, signals possible rate cut in August

The BSP kept its policy rate unchanged at 6.5%, as expected. BSP Governor Remolona, Jr. however said “We are actually somewhat less hawkish than before, which means we could ease (or) cut rates by the third or fourth quarter this year.” They are looking at one or two rate cuts in 2H, possibly a 25bps cut at its Aug 15 meeting ahead of the Fed, but are not too worried about the potential impact on the peso. BSP also lowered its inflation forecast to 3.5% from 3.8% for this year, citing better-than-expected inflation in April of 3.8% (YT-April average inflation rate at 3.4%.) Furthermore, the BSP may also consider cutting banks’ reserve requirement ratios once the Monetary Board starts easing interest rates this year.

NPLs improve, -5bps MoM in March; big banks perform much better

Asset quality (all banks) saw slight improvement across all metrics in March, while loan growth inched up. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dropped 5bps MoM to 3.47% from 3.52% in February. More importantly, in our view, the absolute PHP value of NPLs fell 0.3% MoM. Past Due (PD) rates, meanwhile, edged down 1bp MoM to 4.30%, as did Restructured Loans (RL), to 2.15%. NPL Cover, also improved a hair, up to 100.7% from 100.1% in February. Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) inched up to 10.2% YoY in March, compared to 10.0% in February. On a MoM basis, loans were up 1.1%.

Intriguingly, the asset quality data was much better for the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs), which saw PD ratios down 7bps, and NPL ratios down 8bps MoM. For Thrift Banks (TBs), PD rates jumped 77bps and NPLs increased 38bps MoM. The biggest jump by far was for Digibanks, whose PDs spiked 588bps MoM while NPLs jumped 369bps MoM.

On the one hand, the stability / improvement in UKB asset quality is comforting for investors in the larger listed banks. On the other, the sharp deterioration in TB/Digibank data suggests heightened stress among smaller borrowers, likely consumers and/or SMEs. The BSP has yet to release consumer loan data for 1Q24.

Remittances rise 2.5% to US$2.74bn in March

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.5% YoY to US$2.74bn in March. In PHP terms, remittances rose 4.5% YoY. Land-based remittances rose 3%, driven by UAE (+4.6%), Qatar (+6.2%), Singapore (+3%), and USA (+2.8%), while growth in sea-based remittances slowed to 0.9%. This brings 1Q24 remittances to US$8.22bn, up 2.7%. The BSP projects remittances to rise by 3% YoY, implying FY24E of US$34.5bn. To reach this, remittances for the rest of the year should average US$2.9bn per month.

FDI net inflows rise to US$1.36bn in February

FDI net inflows rose 29% YoY to US$1.36bn in February, driven by a 927% YoY expansion in net investments in equity capital (other than reinvestment of earnings) to US$764mn but tempered by 42% YoY contraction in net investments in debt instruments and 3.8% drop in reinvestment of earnings. Bulk of the equity capital placements during the reference month came from the Netherlands with investments directed mostly to the financial and insurance industry. Senators buck revival of NFA import power

Senators Cynthia Villar and Imee Marcos are opposing the proposal of the Department of Agriculture to restore the power of the National Food Authority (NFA) to import rice. This is one of the amendments the Lower House hopes to pass under its revised Rice Tariffication Law bill, which passed second reading earlier this week. Senator Villar said that President Marcos must have the special power to import rice during emergency situations and not the NFA. She reiterated her call for the passage of the Anti-Agricultural Economic Sabotage Law to prevent the middlemen and traders from causing hardship to farmers and consumers. Recall that the Rice Tariffication Law was signed in 2019 and stripped the NFA of its regulatory and import licensing functions and allowed the private sector to import freely, subject to a tariff system.

DENR to shorten permit process for mining

The DENR plans to implement “parallel” processing of mining permits this year to shorten bureaucratic approval of prospective mining operations to just 1-3 years from 6 years. Mining permits that are not prerequisites for securing another government permit will be simultaneously processed by concerned government agencies.
