RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Groundhog trips the Dragon
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena | +632 8894 6644 | rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

PSEi gains wiped out by US Fed (no) rate cut expectations
In “Groundhog Day” fashion, the PSEi index closed at 6,405, putting it in negative territory (-0.7%) for the first time this year, and -3.5% ytd in USD terms, as foreigners turned aggressive net sellers. The sole culprit seems to be the view on US Fed rate cuts, with our partner Jefferies now expecting just one 25bp cut in late 2024. We adjust  our own BSP policy rate forecasts to -50bps for the year. However, our broader fundamental bull – or “Dragon” – case remains. Index earnings jumped 20% in 2023, and we forecast 10-15% growth in 2024E. This puts the PSEi at 10.9x forward PE, a new 14-year low. In the “higher for longer” narrative, we bring Banks back to Overweight together with Consumers, while we move Property to Underweight.

Dragon ruled in 1Q24, but undone by the Groundhog in 2 weeks
The “Year of the Dragon” theme worked well in 1Q24, with the PSEi index up 8.2% for the quarter – and 17% from the Oct-2023 lows. However, gains were wiped out in just over two weeks, with the index falling below end-2023 levels. Feels like “Groundhog Day”, as the pattern of the previous years repeats itself. We take some comfort in that the PSEi is about the middle-of-the-pack within the region. 

All about the Fed – and then there was one (rate cut)
Regis’s partner Jefferies recently revised its forecast for US Fed rate cuts to just one 25bps in 4Q24, given the strength of US economic data, and stubbornly high inflation (“It’s Still All or Nothing”, Thomas Simons, 12 April). We adjust our own BSP policy rate forecasts accordingly (table at right), and assume just 50bps in cuts, from 100bps previously. While Philippine inflation could moderate soon on lower rice prices, the BSP will not likely stray too far from the Fed for fear of a weak PHP feedback loop into inflation.

Demand drivers resilient into 1Q (so far), but definitely “K-shaped”
Despite the market’s weakness, domestic demand  indicators remain resilient. Car sales were up 23% YoY in February. Electricity sales jumped 9% in 1Q24. Loan growth stood at 10% in February, with NPLs stable. Tourist arrivals were up 19% in 1Q24. That said, lower-income households continue to feel the pinch from high food (rice) prices, though we expect pressures to ease by 2Q/3Q24.

Return of the Dragon… coming soon?
Aggregate earnings for the PSEi grew 20% YoY in 2023. For 2024E, we expect profits to increase 10%. Excluding coal-intensive SCC/DMC, growth rises to 15%. This takes the PSEi’s forward PE to 10.9x, a new 14-year low, more than 1SD lower than the long-term average, with many companies buying back stock, and paying higher dividends. We remain bullish. While waiting for the Dragon to make its comeback, we raise Banks back to Overweight on higher interest rates, and maintain our OW on Consumers. We downgrade Property to Underweight, given soft residential pre-sales and buildup of unsold inventory

BDO 1Q24 net income up 12% to P18.5bn

In a press release, BDO reported Q24 NPAT P18.5bn, up 12% YoY and broadly in line with Regis (23% of our FY24F P79.6bn) and consensus (P80.6bn). Loan growth accelerated to 13% YoY, vs 4Q23's 9%

NPL ratio was stable at 1.88% (was 1.85% in 4Q) while NPL cover slipped a tad to 181% (from 185%).

Ayala streamlines logistics business

 

Ayala Corp. is integrating Entrego’s business and operations with the AIR21 group to maximize asset utilization and create greater efficiencies. Since the start of the year, Entrego’s courier, express and parcel services were subcontracted by AIR21. Entrego, which began in 2018, officially stopped its services on April 1. AIR21 was acquired by AC Logistics in August 2022 initially for a 60% ownership stake; It raised its stake to 80% in March 2023.

ACEN, Rockefeller Foundation plans first CCCI pilot project

ACEN Corp. and the Rockefeller Foundation are collaborating on the first "Coal to Clean Credit Initiative (CCCI)" which involves replacing a coal plant with clean energy. The Pilot project aims to decommission the 246MW South Luzon Energy Corp. (SLTEC) coal plant in 2030, ahead of its supposed retirement by 2040, and replace it with clean power and battery storage.

ACEN is coordinating with SLTEC's owners for the early closure of the plant and aims to finalize the project design document in 2024, and is also finalizing buyer discussions for the CCCI.

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) FY23 net profit +69% YoY to P3.5bn

Philippine Seven’s net profit in FY23 jumped 69% YoY to P3.5bn, ahead of Regis estimates. The profit outperformance was primarily due to higher-than-expected interest income. Core operations are in line with revenues +25% YoY to P78bn and operating profit +43% YoY to P5bn. 4Q23 net profit grew 64% YoY to P1.2bn boosted by higher interest income. Revenues rose 20% YoY on the back of +9.5% SSSG. While SSSG was slower vs previous quarters, the YoY base is also higher. SSSG continued to be driven by growth in customer traffic. Operating profit was higher by 36% YoY during the quarter, driven by gross margin improvements and operating leverage from the ramp up of new stores. SEVN ended the year with 3,768 new stores, a net addition of 375 stores vs end-2022.

Manila Water acquires 70% stake in Equipacific

Manila Water Philippine Ventures is acquiring a 70% stake in Equipacific Holdco, Inc. (EHI) for P1.15bn.

EHI is the investment company that holds a 90% stake in Laguna Water District Aquatech Resources (LARC). LARC is a joint venture with the Laguna local government that operates water systems for five towns in Laguna (Los Baños, Bay, Calauan, Victoria, and Nagcarlan). Details on LARC’s operations were not disclosed.

Sellers are Equi-Parco Hldgs Corp. (30%), Metropac Water Investments Corp. (30%), and TwinPeak Hydro Resources Corp. (10%). P920mn will be paid upon closing and the balance will be paid within six months after the closing date. Customary conditions precedent for transactions of similar nature (e.g., submission of corporate approvals and other third-party consents).

The acquisition expands MWC’s presence in Laguna; it currently handles the water distribution for three towns (Sta Rosa, Biñan, Cabuyao).

Vista Land FY23 recurring profit rises 19%, ahead of REGIS

Vista Land’s headline profit grew 41% to P8.7bn. Stripping off gains from insurance claims, estimated recurring profit rose 19% to P7.3bn, ahead of REGIS forecast (in line with consensus).

  • FY23 reservation sales rose 10% to P71.9bn, ahead of our forecast
    • 4Q23 reservation sales increased 7% QoQ (10% YoY) to P18.8bn
    • Similar to previous years, OFWs comprised 55%-60% of residential sales
  • FY23 residential revenue rose 19% to P15.3bn, in line
    • This is 47% of the FY19 figure
    • FY23 gross profit margin expanded 320 basis points to 60%
    • Only 5% of residential receivables are over 90 days past due, from 37% in 2022
  • FY23 rental revenue grew 17% to P16.0bn
    • This is double the FY19 figure, even though the rental portfolio is only about 15% larger
    • Rental receivables amounted to P32.5bn, representing two years’ worth of rental revenue

Luzon, Visayas grids on yellow alert

National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) placed the Luzon and Visayas grids under yellow alert yesterday (Apr 17) as more than 30 power plans went offline. 18 power plants went on forced outage in Luzon while three others were running at de-rated capacities.The Luzon grid was also placed under yellow alert last Tuesday (Apr 16) due to outages.

SEC to remove minimum stockbroker commission

The SEC issued a Memorandum Circular removing the minimum amount of commission charged by stockbrokers to its customers. Stockbrokers currently charge a minimum commission rate of 0.25% to 0.05% depending on the value of the transaction.

The Circular shall take effect immediately upon completion of its publication in two newspapers of general circulation. The government aims to improve liquidity in the stock market by lower friction costs. A Bill in Congress also seeks to reduce the stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1%.

ICTSI gets green light to operate Iloilo port

ICTSI received the Notice to Proceed to operate the Visayas Container Terminal (VCT), following a review by the Philippine Ports Authority and the Office of the Government Corporate Counsel. VCT’s currently handles domestic throughput volumes of 100k TEUs and 2mn MT on non-containerized cargo annually. The contract  is valid for 25 years.

The port will handle domestic and foreign containers in the first 6 years of operations and will eventually handle foreign containers solely after. Regis estimates the port could add only ~1% to consolidated volumes and revenues in its first full year of operations of handling domestic cargo. However, contributions could be larger in future years once management successfully diverts foreign containers from the ports of Cebu and Manila directly to Iloilo. ICTSI plans to spend US$20mn in the first 18 months to improve operations and future capital investments will be volume-driven.

EMI FY23 net profit -14% YoY to P8.6bn – behind

EMI reported FY23 net profit of P8.6bn, down 14% YoY, behind Regis and consensus expectations. The underperformance was mainly due to lower-than-expected margins. Revenues were ahead of Regis expectations but broadly in line with consensus at P65.6bn, up 5% YoY. 4Q23 net profit declined 35% YoY to P1.9bn due to weak top line for the Brandy segment. Meanwhile, gross margins across segments were lower due to sales mix and higher A&P.

  • For the Whisky segment:
    • Revenues were flattish in 4Q23, which management attributes to the timing of the launches of its premium SKUs, which was earlier in the year
    • Volumes were reportedly “strong”, driven by Asia (mostly China), which saw vols +67% for the FY
    • Shift in sales mix, coupled with higher A&P led to lower gross margins during the quarter
    • Segment net income declined 8% YoY as a result
  • For the Brandy segment:
    • Revenues were down 12% YoY as the volumes continued to be dragged by downtrading among consumers
    • Low volumes, coupled with high A&P led to a 53% drop in segment net income

LPC: Office vacancy stays flat in 1Q24; high-end residential prices rise

During its 1Q24 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy stayed flat at 18%.

  • Nationwide net office take-up rose 17% QoQ (14% YoY) to 184,000sqm
    • BPO net office take-up increased 61% QoQ (-15% YoY) to 66,000sqm
    • Meanwhile POGO net office take-up fell 54% QoQ to 34,000sqm
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV
  • LPC expects office vacancy to rise to 19% in FY24 before falling to 17% in FY25 (14% in FY26)
  • It said that primary residential condominium units sold fell 29% QoQ and 42% YoY to 6,928 units
  • However, it also said that secondary market prices of high-end Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio condominium prices generally rose 5-10% YoY

Remittances rise 3% in February

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3% YoY to US$2.65bn in February. In PHP terms, remittances rose by 5.4% YoY. Top growth contributors for the month were Saudi Arabia (+4%), UAE (+3.5%), and Singapore (+3.8% YoY).

This brings YT-Feb remittances up 2.8% YoY to US$5.48bn. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 3% this year.

Department of Agriculture to propose P514bn infra-heavy budget for 2025

The Department of Agriculture (DA) said it will propose a P514bn budget for 2025. This would be more than double the agency’s budget for 2024 of P209bn. Agriculture Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel, Jr. said that the funds will support the construction of irrigation and postharvest facilities.

P514bn budget will be allocated to the following, which will broadly modernize the fisheries and farm sectors, and address food safety and anti-smuggling efforts:

  • P294bn for rice (largely for irrigation of 1.2mn hectares to boost production and reduce imports
  • P50.6bn for fisheries
  • P34.5bn for cross-cutting programs
  • P32bn for high value crops
  • P29bn for livestock,
  • P14bn for foreign-assisted projects
  • P11bn for corn
  • P3bn for credit programs
BSP says rate cut more likely in 2025

Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. said the window for reducing policy rates in 2H24 is narrowing, citing upside risks to inflation are becoming worse. Monetary easing will more likely begin in 1Q25 and the cuts will likely just take the policy rate to 6% from 6.5% currently.

Gross borrowings rise 22% in February

Data from the Bureau of the Treasury (BTr) shows the national government's gross borrowings jump 22% YoY to P419.97bn in February from P343.63bn last year, this is also up 107% MoM from P203.15bn in January 2024. Roughly 99% of the month's gross borrowings are attributed to domestic sources as gross domestic debt climbs 26.7% YoY to P415.23bn from P327.64bn last year; this consists of PHP341.41bn in retail T-bonds, PHP60bn in fixed-rate T-bonds, PHP13.82bn in T-bills. New external borrowings fell 70% YoY to P4.74bn from P15.98bn as external borrowings were composed of new project loans.
