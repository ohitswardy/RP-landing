REGIS REPORT

05 April 2024

Philippine shares were heavily sold off this week, coming off key support levels and slipping below the crucial 6800-7000 channel that is has maintained for nearly 2 months, as investors appear to be pricing in the possibility of less rate hikes this year vs earlier anticipated, owing to the recent signs of strength in the US economy and subsequent Fed rhetoric. The PSEi initially started the week off on a positive note, rallying to as high as 7070 intraday on Monday (and closing at 6979 or +1.1% on the day), supported by substantial net foreign buying. But this testing of the psychological 7000-mark appears to have flicked on a switch for profit taking, particularly among domestic investors initially, as the index then proceeded to sharply tumble through the rest of the week, first breaking below the 50-day moving average on Thursday and then the key 6800 support line today, to ultimately settle at 6745.46, -2.29% WoW and the lowest close since February 5.

Amid the uncertainty over interest rates, the most battered sector this week was Property -5.12% WoW, dragged by sharp declines in large caps SMPH -6.42% WoW and ALI -5.12%. On the opposite end, the best performing stock in the benchmark was DMC +7.04% as the company finally announced dividends totaling Php0.72/share (0.46 common, 0.26 special), same as its payout in October. Notably, there were a handful of names that hit new 52-week lows, including AEV -8.96% WoW, JGS -7.42%, MEG -3.65%, PGOLD -9.33% and this week’s worst PSEi member WLCON -10.01%. One bright spot outside the benchmark however was PLUS +20.18% which climbed to a new all-time high and added $175m to its market cap to now $1.05b. Average daily activity for the market stood at $124m, -12% WoW, while net foreign flow ended skewed to sell by $9m as foreign investors reversed their early inflow and turned better sellers through the latter part of the week.

On the macro front, key highlight for the week was the latest CPI reading released on Friday which showed that inflation ticked higher to 3.7% in March from 3.4% in February, but a nudge below the 3.8% consensus forecast. Breakdown of the data however showed that food inflation accelerated to a 4-month high of 5.6% from 4.6% in February. For the week ahead, key focus is the BSP policy decision due on Monday – note this comes before a 2-day break for the Philippine market in observance of Day of Valor on Tuesday and Feast of Ramadan on Wednesday.  The monetary board is expected to keep the key rate on hold for the 4th meeting in a row after raising it to 6.5% in October.

Cebu Air - 4Q23 EBITDA surprises; 24E seat growth guidance raised
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY23 core profit of P7.27bn beats estimates

FY23 core income (i.e. excluding one-off/MTM gains, FX adj, interest on CBs) of P7.27bn was a turnaround from the previous year’s P11.8bn net losses and exceeded our forecast of P4.1bn. Lower operating costs and a higher tax benefit boosted profits, even though revenues were a tad softer than forecast. Meanwhile, an improved fleet situation prompted management to raise its 24E seat growth guidance to +11-15% from +5-8% previously.

Lower maintenance costs lift 4Q23 EBITDA

The shift in school calendars reduced the seasonal difference between 3Q and 4Q, resulting in just a 2% revenue improvement between the two periods. Seat load factors were a bit softer at 83% in 4Q from 84% in 3Q and were still below pre-Covid levels due largely to a weak China market. Pax volumes reached 5.3mn in 4Q or 5% below pre-Covid levels while fares were at P3.1k, 11% above 2019 levels. EBITDA rose 17% QoQ to P6.6bn in 4Q, bringing EBITDA margin to 28%, 4ppts lower than the 2019 average margin of 32%. Cash costs fell 3% QoQ with fewer technical challenges with respect to AOG (aircraft on ground) and a reversal of provisions for asset retirement obligations with the conclusion of aircraft lease exits.

 

2024 seat growth guidance revised up to +11-15%

Management raised its 24E seat growth guidance to 11-15% YoY for 2024 from 5-8% previously, after successfully securing more spare engines and aircraft, as well as an improvement in Pratt & Whitney’s MRO capacity. The guidance implies seat capacity could rise to as much as 10% above pre-Covid levels this year, in line with our forecast. Forward bookings remain healthy, with seat capacity for 1Q24 already up 14% YoY and pax volumes also up by as much. Management will start the quasi-reorganization of its capital in 2Q to eliminate its retained earnings deficit and allow CEB to declare dividends.

 
Philippine Seven - Ready for takeoff
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Initiating with a Buy; target price P135/sh

Philippine Seven (SEVN) is the leading operator of convenience stores (CVS) in the Philippines, a fast-growing and underpenetrated market. While the segment is attracting new (and old) players, we believe the growth runway is long and the rising tide should lift all boats. Amidst rising competitive pressure, we believe SEVN should maintain market leadership given its first-mover advantage with regard to locations, franchising, and distribution. We initiate coverage of SEVN with a Buy rating and a TP of P135/sh, which implies 2024E multiples of 27x PE and 10x EBITDA on 18% EPS CAGR for 2024-26E. Key downside risks relate to competition and execution risk.

Rising tide lifts all boats

We estimate that the Philippines has just under 7,000 modern small format groceries (e.g. convenience stores, minimarts) as of end-2023 and low penetration (5 stores per 100k population) is attracting new (and old) players. Despite new entrants, we believe the market offers significant growth opportunities and potential. Even if all players doubled stores over the next 5 years, we estimate penetration levels in the Philippines would still be far from where Thailand and Indonesia are today, c.31 stores and c.15 stores per 100k population, respectively.

 

Ready for takeoff

SEVN is the market leader in the modern small format grocery segment with an estimated market share of ~60% (based on store count). Amidst rising competitive pressures and pandemic-induced changes in consumer behavior, we believe SEVN has two main competitive advantages arising from it being a first mover: (1) it has secured many key locations, and (2) it has greater economies of scale with respect to its distribution and franchise network.

18% net profit CAGR for 2024-26E

We are forecasting 18% EPS CAGR for 2024-26E on the back of 16% revenue CAGR. Store expansion should be the main driver of growth and we are assuming a net addition of 450-500 per year over the forecast period. Amidst the race for space, we expect some upward risk on rent/lease expenses, which will likely offset margin gains from economies of scale as the company opens more stores and ramps up franchising. We are expecting operating margins to be largely flat from 2024-26E.

Initiating with a Buy, TP of P135/sh; key downside risks

We are initiating with a Buy rating and a target price of P135/sh. Our TP is derived using a discounted cash flow and implies 2024E multiples of 27x PE and 10x EV/EBITDA. The implied multiples are broadly in line with regional peers but a premium to domestic consumer/retail peers. Key downside risks relate to competition, execution risk, and macroeconomic risk.

 
Philippine Strategy / Consumer
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Rice export prices easing, down 7-8% YTD, as El Niňo weakens

The Philippine weather agency PAGASA announced that the El Niňo phenomenon is weakening, and could return to “neutral” conditions from April, in time for the rainy season in 2H24. Rice is the country’s main staple, accounting 8.9% of the CPI basket, likely more the further down the income pyramid you go. With the El Niňo droughts, and with India banning exports in July 2023, export prices from Vietnam and Thailand – where the Philippines gets the majority of rice imports – shot up. As El Niňo fades, VN/TH export prices are down 7-8% YTD (Fig 1). On a YoY basis, export prices are “only” up c30%, from a peak of 55-60% (Fig 2).

Domestic prices to follow, taking pressure off low-income households

As of 18-22 March, the average retail price of rice in Metro Manila was still holding steady at close to peak prices (Fig 3). However, we would expect local prices to reflect VN/TH export price trajectories, with a bit of lag (eg shipping times). In February, headline CPI was 3.4% YoY, but rice inflation was an eye-popping 23.7% (Fig 4). An easing of rice prices would be a welcome relief to lower income households, whose wallets have been squeezed by higher rice prices.

How to play it? Buys on URC, PGOLD

Companies catering to lower-income buyers fared relatively poorly in 2023, and should benefit from rebounding demand in 2H24E. These would include Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), Puregold (PGOLD PM), and Universal Robina (URC PM). Of those, URC looks particularly interesting, after a recent sell-down has taken valuations down to 2024E PER of 14x, a huge discount to historical average of 28x, and to peer average of 20x. Meanwhile, PGOLD trades at 7x 2024E PER, compared to ASEAN peers averaging 20x.

Could the BSP cut rates in 2Q24?

The BSP has held its policy rate at 6.5% since 4Q23, despite the sharp drop in inflation (Fig 5). While officials say a rate cut is unlikely in April, could easing rice prices cap CPI risk, and open the door to a cut in May or June?

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

JFC to invest USD28mn in beverage tech startup

JFC is investing USD28mn (P1.5bn) for a 10% stake in US-based beverage tech startup, Botrista Inc. Botrista holds more than 100 patents globally for its proprietary dispense technology which provides automated solutions to serve cold specialty coffee and tea-based drinks with premium and all-natural ingredients.

DTI leases entire building from Filinvest Land

The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) signed an agreement to lease the entire Filinvest Buendia building from Filinvest Land. The building has about 10,700sqm of leasable area and is located in Makati. DTI will consolidate some of its operating units into the building. This transaction should increase FLI’s office occupancy from 62% to 64%.

JFC to invest in Titan Dining Partners anew

JFC is investing SGD90mn in Titan Dining II for a 90% interest. The fund will be used to fund strategic investments in F&B concepts around the APAC region. Titan Dining Partners is the same group that owns Tim Ho Wan, among other brands.

CREIT 2023 net profit rises 12%

Citicore REIT (CREIT PM) FY23 earnings rose 12% to P1.4bn, in line with consensus forecast

Revenue grew 31% to P1.8bn. Its leasable portfolio (comprised of land leased to renewable energy projects) tripled to 7.1mn sqm. CREIT is 29% owned by SM Investments. CREIT currently offers FY24E dividend yield of 6.9% (based on consensus forecast). The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.2%.

DMCI declares P0.72/sh dividend for 1H24

DMCI declared a regular dividend of P0.46/sh and a special dividend of P0.26/sh. Total cash dividend amounts to P0.72/sh, equal to dividends declared in 1H23. Total div is equivalent to a yield of 5.9%, based on April 4 closing price. Ex-dividend: April 19; Record date: April 22. Regis is forecasting cash dividends of P0.90/sh for FY24 (vs. FY23: P1.44/sh).

Megaworld’s ArcoVia township designated as an ecozone

Megaworld’s ArcoVia township was designated as a PEZA ecozone (IT Park). Arcovia is along E. Rodriguez, Jr. (C-5) in Pasig City. This means that BPO firms in ArcoVia can enjoy PEZA incentives. ArcoVia comprises 2% of MEG’s office floor area. MEG is trading at 70% discount to NAV, 3.4X FY24E P/B, and 0.2X FY24E P/E.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government debt rises to P15.2tn in February

The national government’s debt stock reached P15.2tn in end-February, up 2.6% MoM and 3.8% from end-2023 due to net issuances of domestic government securities, which was partially tempered by the effect of a stronger peso on foreign debt valuation. By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028.

Customs collections fall by 6% YoY to P75.4bn in March

The Bureau of Customs collected P75.4bn in March, lower by 6% YoY. This brings 1Q24 collections to P219.4bn, up 2.6% YoY. The agency is tasked to collect P959bn (+8.5% YoY) this year.

Tourist arrivals up 6% in March; +18% in 1Q24

Tourist arrivals rose 6% YoY to 506k in March or 73% of pre-Covid levels. This is however down from 582k arrivals (85% of pre-Covid) in February. This brings 1Q24 tourist arrivals to 1.66mn, up 18% YoY. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY or reverting to 93% of 2019 levels (vs 2023A: 5.5mn or 66% of pre-Covid). To reach this target, tourist arrivals for the rest of the year should average 671k per month.

BSP forecasts March inflation within 3.4%-4.2%

The BSP projects March inflation rate to settle within 3.4%-4.2%. This is higher than the February inflation rate of 3.4%. Meanwhile, consensus forecast is at 3.8%. The BSP cited sustained price hikes of rice and meat, as well as fuel and electricity prices to offset lower prices of fruits, vegetables, and fish. The Philippine Statistics Authority will release March inflation data on April 5.

Budget deficit widens to P165bn in February

The national government recorded a budget deficit of P165bn in February. This is wider than last year’s deficit of P106bn and a reversal of the previous month’s P88bn surplus due to a 22% YoY jump in expenditures. Revenues rose 6% YoY, with tax revenues up 9%. BIR revenues rose 7% while customs collections grew at a faster rate of 12%. Other revenue streams fell 21% YoY due to lower Malampaya proceeds. Meanwhile, expenditures rose 22%, with interest payments up 40%. Primary spending jumped 20% YoY and 31% MoM due to higher releases to LGUs, as well as public works disbursements. This brought YT-Feb budget deficit up 27% YoY to P77bn. The government is targeting a budget deficit of P1.36tn in 2024, down 10% YoY.

DOE expects additional 300MW of capacity this dry season

The Department of Energy (DOE) is expecting over 300MW of additional capacity from various facilities to enter operations this summer. 150MW will come from a coal-fired power plant and the rest will be supplied by various renewable energy facilities. The DOE is not expecting an outright shortage despite forecasting yellow alerts in April and May and sees the return of hydropower plants from preventive maintenance given signs of El Niño weakening.

Panel to address right-of-way issues hounding railway projects

President Marcos Jr. created the Inter-Agency Committee for Right-of-Way (RoW) Activities for National Railway Projects to streamline land acquisitions for railway projects. The Panel will ensure “effective completion” of programs related to land acquisition and other right-of-way activities such as livelihood programs, income restoration, and resettlement. The panel will be chaired by the secretary of the Department of Transportation (DoTr) and co-chaired by the secretary of the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development. The government’s infrastructure projects have been hampered by RoW issues that have delayed their completion. Around 45% of the P488bn Metro Manila Subway Phase 1 project’s RoW has not yet been secured, which threatens the project’s 2029 completion.

Auto sales rise 23% in February

Vehicle sales jumped 23% YoY, 12% MoM to 38,072 units in February. This brings YT-Feb sales to 72,132 units (+19% YoY). Toyota sold 33,070 units in YT-Feb (+17% YoY), with Feb sales alone at 16,977 units (+14% YoY, +5% MoM). 1Q is typically the time when other car brands ramp up promotions to catch up on fiscal year-end targets. To reach Toyota’s target of 215k unit sales (+7%), monthly sales for the rest of the year should average  ~18k units. To reach REGIS’ forecast of 206k units (+3%), sales should average ~17k units per month. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 28% of profit. GTCAP trades at a 42% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,222/sh and 6x PER.

DOE urges NGCP, DUs to prepare for ILP this dry season

In an advisory, the Department of Energy (DOE) requested the National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) and all distribution utilities (DUs) to fully prepare for the implementation of the Interruptible Load Program (ILP) amid the dry season. ILP will help mitigate energy supply deficiency in the Philippines as participating companies with stand-by generation capacities will be compensated for their output during instances of power supply deficit. Meralco earlier stated that 105 companies expressed commitment to participate in the program. Luzon is still expected to experience yellow alert in the coming two months whereas Visayas and Mindanao grids are expected to have "normal reserve levels".

Banks’ NPL ratio slightly dips in February; Loan growth re-accelerates to 10%

Philippine banks’ NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was slightly lower at 3.52% in February from 3.53% in January. Loan growth accelerated to 10% in February from 8.8% in January. Past due ratio went up to 4.31% in February from 4.27% in January while restructured loan ratio dropped to 2.16% in Feb from 2.26% in Jan. NPL coverage ratio fell to 100.06% in Feb from 100.29% in January.

Government lowers 2024 growth target to 6-7%; Raises budget deficit ceiling targets

The government downgraded its 2024 GDP target range to 6-7% from 6.5-7.5% previously according to National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) chief Arsenio Balisacan. It also lowered its 2025 target to 6.5% to 7.5%, down from the initial range of 6.5% to 8.0%. The Philippine Statistics Authority also revised 2023 GDP to 5.5% from the 5.6% it initially reported on Jan 31. 4Q23 GDP growth was also revised down to 5.5%  from 5.6% previously. Meanwhile, the government maintained its inflation targets at 2-4% but raised the budget deficit ceiling for the next five years. The 2024 budget deficit was raised to P1.48tn (5.6% of GDP) from P1.39tn (5.2% previously). Budget deficit to GDP ratio targets are higher due to (a) Mildly higher disbursements (+2-3%) and (b) lower nominal GDP targets (-3% to -4%). The deficit program is expected to gradually decline from 6.2% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2028.
