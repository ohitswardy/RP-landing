REGIS REPORT

13 September 2024

Philippine shares gained for the second week in a row and drove the PSEi to finally close above the 7000 level after several attempts since the BSP cut rates last month, while the rest of the broader region traded mixed. Shares were met with volatility in the earlier part of the week with the benchmark consistently being weighed down by selling baskets at the closing auction after repeatedly testing the psychological 7000-level. This trend persisted from Monday to Wednesday with Tuesday’s move being particularly notable as the benchmark had rallied sharply to the 7100 level during the continuous session, supported by heavy volumes, only to be dragged back down below 7k by close due to MOC sells that came mostly from local investors (-1.36% swing at auction). On Thursday however the late sellers appear to have taken a pause, which then allowed the benchmark to finally end a session atop 7000 for the first time since January 2023, albeit on notably lighter volumes. The index managed to hold the level during Friday’s session despite again facing some selling pressure at the closing auction, and ultimately settled the week at 7022.85 +1.25% WoW.

Notable names that supported the benchmark this week were heavyweights SM +3.47% WoW and ICT +2.75%, which both saw notable foreign inflows. Note that foreign investors were consistent net buyers of the Philippine market all throughout the week, but did show signs of easing towards the end with Friday’s net inflow cooling to just $3m vs $15m average through Monday-Tuesday. In terms of sectors, holding firms were a generally bright spot – in addition to SM, its peers AEV +8.36% WoW, JGS +5.49%, GTCAP +2.94% and AC +1.14% were in the green. The consumer space again looked mixed with last week’s outperformers CNPF -3.53% and PGOLD -3.33% trailing behind this time, while MONDE +5.65% and URC +1.83% outperformed. Banks meanwhile were mostly in the red with the big 3 all down (BPI -2.06%, MBT -1.46% and BDO -0.51%) but we did see continued flocking into deep value play SECB which gained by another +7.73% this week. Note that Rafa released a note on SECB late last week, highlighting its turnaround story (TP of P160 or +106% upside).

The best performer in the benchmark was CNVRG +10.47% WoW which marked a new 52-week high during the week and extended its YTD gain to +101.43% (vs PSEi +8.88%). Note that the company declared a P0.18/share cash dividend this week, a first in the company’s history. While this is a relatively small payout and only represents a ~1% yield (vs GLO’s ~4.5% and TEL ~6.5%), it is nonetheless a positive development as it comes earlier than the company’s indicated timeline of 2025. The company also revealed is new policy of paying out 25%-30% of prior year’s earnings, which could translate to a yield of 2%-3% for next year.

Another notable gainer was BLOOM +8.3% WoW, which comes in contrast with the sharp slump in PLUS -25.75%. Note that several lawmakers reiterated their calls to either put tighter regulation or ban online gaming in the country. Senators Cayetano and Villanueva proposed an anti-online gambling bill, which will adversely affect PLUS.

Market activity this week improved to a daily average of $118.5m +25% WoW but note that the heavy volumes in the earlier part of the week were pulled down slightly by an visible easing in the latter part. Turnover in the last 2 sessions (right when the PCOMP finally managed to conquer 7k) stood a tad below $100m. Foreign participation for the broader week eased to 41%, but again their net buying continued by a meaningful $47m. Top foreign bought stocks this week were SM +3.47%, BDO -0.51%, SECB +7.73%, ICT +2.75%, GTCAP +2.94%, ALI -0.71% and URC +1.83%; while top sold was GLO -0.36%.

Highlights of Terra Solar briefing

World’s largest integrated RE project. Terra Solar is building a 3,500MW solar farm with 4,500MWh of battery storage in Central Luzon across a couple of provinces. It will be the largest such integrated (solar + battery) project in the world once completed. Terra Solar will sell 850MW of power to Meralco under 20-year contracts beginning Feb 2026 (600MW) and Feb 2027 (250MW). The contracts are for mid-merit power, with a capacity factor of 50%, thus necessitating battery storage and much higher headline capacity vs the contract capacity.

Attractive returns, magnified. Terra Solar looks like a very attractive project with project IRR “in the teens”. With leverage and the entry of a new partner at a substantial premium, the equity returns for SP New Energy (SPNEC PM) are likely to be many times that (easily >50%).

Effective tariffs much higher than P5.7 per kwh. Meralco’s effective purchase price for power from Terra Solar (which it controls anyway) is likely to be close to P8/kwh, after “cost of capital” adjustments embedded in the agreement. This would be ~40% higher than the much-publicized P5.7/kwh in the press releases just two years ago. For perspective, ACEN’s winning bids for 160MW of power at similar terms were at just under P8.2/kwh.

On schedule so far. Conscious and keenly aware that project delays are common, SPNEC / Meralco management has been working early on issues that might delay completion. Work on transmission (grid connection) is supposedly ahead of schedule. Transmission is frequently a reason for delay. Most of the rest of the project work is within schedule so far for Phase 1. Phase 1 involves 2,500MW of the total 3,500MW solar capacity.  Delays, however, are always because of events that are unanticipated.

Actis injecting US$600m for 40%. New UK-based investor Actis will inject US$600m (~P35bn) into Terra Solar for a 40% stake. This means SPNEC will only need to pony up P15bn for its 60% share in the equity of Terra Solar.

P10bn contribution for SPNEC? Employing back-of-the envelope calculations, we reckon Terra Solar can contribute over P10bn in profit to SPNEC (for its 60% stake). Note SPNEC’s equity contribution is likely to be only around P15bn.  The profit figure also implies EPS of at least ~P0.20 based on outstanding shares of 50bn.  Meralco is expected to take ownership of just over 50% in SPNEC with the exchangeable instrument it acquired from SPNEC founder Leandro Leviste.

Other items:

Meralco claims that Excellent Energy, the San Miguel (SMC) subsidiary building the 1,300MW LNG plant in Batangas, will begin commissioning by next month (Oct 2024). The claimed target for commercial operation is Dec 2024. Commissioning, however, typically takes much longer than two months.

Meralco management reiterated its intention to revive the Atimonan project in its original coal-fired form (1,200MW). The claim is that Atimonan was granted its permits before the moratorium against coal-fired plants was put in place. Meralco management expressed its desire for the coal moratorium to be lifted.

The deal for Meralco to buy part of the LNG complex of SMC is not going to meet its Sept 2024 deadline (obviously).  Meralco signed a deal to buy effectively 40% of the LNG complex which includes Ilijan (1,200MW), Excellent Energy (EERI, 1,300MW) and the associated LNG terminal.  One of the conditions precedent is the approval (provisional) of the power supply agreement between EERI and Meralco by the regulator (ERC). We are unsure if the recent suspension of the ERC chair will speed things up or slow them down.

One other condition precedent is the clearance by the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC). The PCC has yet to approve the deal.

Meralco household rates inch up in September

Meralco’s overall household rate will climb by P0.1543/kwh (+1.3%) MoM to P11.7882/kwh. The company cited 50% higher transmission charges due to the doubling of ancillary service charges, which offset the 2.2% MoM drop in generation charges. On a YoY basis, the overall rate is up 3.4%, slightly slower than the 6.7% pace in August. Note that electricity prices account for 4.5% of the CPI basket. Recall that headline inflation decelerated to 3.3% in August from 4.4% in July, even as electricity CPI rose 3.1% YoY, 3.4% MoM in August due to slower increases in rice, other food, and fuel-related transport CPI.

ICTSI (ICT PM) Volume (Mexico, Melbourne) Update

The latest data from the ports of Manzanillo, Mexico and Melbourne, Australia show decelerating growth trends for 3Q24 thus far but we are not worried.  A closer look at the data suggests that higher-yielding cargo volumes are still growing robustly in Mexico while ICT’s market share gains in Melbourne should offset any demand weakness, near term.  We estimate ICT’s ports in Mexico and Melbourne respectively account for 18% and 8% of consolidated revenues.

We remain positive on the near-to medium-term prospects of ICTSI (ICT PM), with the bias of earnings revisions still heavily skewed to the upside. At 9x FY25E EV/EBITDA and 14x PER, lower than historical average multiples of 11x and 21x, respectively.

Volumes rise 3% YoY in Aug at CMSA on lower transshipment; Import volumes up 20%.  Throughput volumes at ICT’s second largest port, Contecon Manzanillo (i.e. CMSA), rose 3% YoY to 122k TEUs in August, decelerating from the 20% pace in 1H24 and 7% in 2Q24. As with previous months post-Feb, the deceleration in the “headline” growth rate was largely a consequence of a substantial drop in lower-yielding transshipment cargo.  The “core”, higher-yielding O&D cargo still grew at a robust 15% pace during the month, with import volumes alone rising by 20% YoY.  Note as well that the slowdown was evident for all the ports in Manzanillo, with industry-wide port volume up just 1% YoY in August, moderating from the 12% rate in 1H. We highly suspect that this was merely a function of the congestion at the port and not indicative of a material decline in demand.

 

Cumulative volumes at CMSA have reached 945k TEUs in the first eight months of the year, growing by 15% YoY and on trend to meet or even exceed our full-year forecast of 1.43mn TEUs (+12% YoY). It will be interesting to see how volume trends progress from September onwards, given that the port is due to complete its 250k TEUs expansion soon, which will bring capacity to 1.7mn TEUs from 1.45mn TEUs today. We reiterate that ICT is the only port in the already congested port of Manzanillo that has the available infrastructure to expand.

Ports of Melbourne: Container volume growth slows to 2% in July.  Container volumes at the three ports in Melbourne grew by 2% YoY in July, moderating from the 9% pace in 1H24. In 7M24, volumes at the port of Melbourne have grown by 8% YoY, trending mildly better than our full-year industry growth forecast of 5%.

It is worth noting, however, that ICT’s market share has improved to the “mid-30%” level this year from 26% in 2023 and it is now the largest port (by volume) in Melbourne, as the port’s location advantage continues to attract shipping lines who are looking to save on costs by using larger vessels.

Cavite segment of the Cavite-Laguna Expressway (CALAX) to partially open by 1Q25

MPCALA Holdings is set to open the Governor’s Drive Interchange of the CALAX project by 1Q25. The segment is currently at a 25% completion rate. It is anticipated to help traffic congestion in Aguinaldo Highway in Silang, Cavite and other major roads in Cavite (Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal, and Quezon). The segment spans 7.8km from Silang (Aguinaldo) Interchange to Governor’s drive, will be the longest operational section of CALAX.

To date, the overall completion rate of CALAX reached 64%, it said, adding that the expressway is now serving about 45,000 motorists per day. CALAX is a 45-kilometer four-lane expressway with eight interchanges. Once fully operational, it will serve at least 95,000 motorists daily. The entire expressway is set to be fully operational by 3Q25. Property companies (e.g. GTCAP, Ayala Land) with landbank in the surrounding areas of Cavite could see land values increase once CALAX is completed.

Globe eyes sustained improvements in positive free cash flow levels

Globe expects strong operating cash flows to continue through to next year, which will help sustain its positive free cash flow momentum, even without one-offs. Globe’s free cash flows after interest payments in 2Q turned positive, which included tower sales. It plans to finalize its tower sale and leaseback deal by year-end. Positive free cash flows should help reduce leverage beginning 2025.

DMCI Power wants SPUG power plants to be exempted from coal ban

DMCI wants the coal moratorium to exclude Small Power Utilities Group (SPUG) power plants operating in off-grid areas, according to DMCI Chairman and CEO Isidro Consunji. The company cited that new plants will be replacing diesel, which is the most expensive fuel. DMCI Power is a wholly-owned unit of DMCI and has 160 MW of installed capacity in off-grid areas (Palawan, Masbate, Oriental Mindoro). 55% of capacity is sourced from diesel, 19% from coal, and 26% from bunker. DMCI Power accounts for 6% of DMCI’s profits.

Alliance Global's Travellers gets approval to open casino in Cebu

Travellers, the gaming arm of Alliance Global (AGI PM), received a “no objection” certificate from the Local Government Unit of Lapu-Lapu City, Cebu. Travellers will open a P17bn casino resort in Megaworld’s Mactan Newtown. The company will also add gaming capacity in Entertainment City (through a partnership with Suntrust) and Boracay by 2026. Travellers currently has 500 gaming tables and 2,500 slot machines. The new casino in Entertainment City will add 280 gaming tables and 1,300 slot machines.

Robinsons Retail Holdings Inc. focuses on expanding into new market segments to drive growth

RRHI is actively seeking acquisitions to enhance its portfolio and is looking to expand outside its current portfolio. RRHI currently has an expansive network of retail food stores, drugstores, department stores, do-it-yourself stores, and specialty stores. RRHI ended the first half with a total of 2,401 stores consisting of 755 food stores, 1,082 drugstores, 49 department stores, 224 DIY stores, and 291 specialty stores. It also has over 2,100 franchised stores of TGP. Management stated that they could still open about 100 more stores before the year ends. Note that Stanley Co is set to take on Robina Gokongwei-Pe’s role as RRHI president and CEO starting next year (effective Jan. 1, 2025).

AREIT sells Alabang office space

AREIT sold 339sqm of office space in Ayala Life FGU Center Alabang for P42.7mn, net of fees and taxes, which is equivalent to a selling price/sqm of c. P126k/sqm. The office space was sold to Next AsiaLand. In 2021, AREIT acquired 551sqm of office space in Ayala Life FGU Center Alabang for P63mn at an acquisition price of c. P114k/sqm.

Vista Land sets dividend rate for preferred shares

Vista Land will offer up to P5bn worth of preferred shares: 30mn preferred shares with overallotment option of 20mn shares at P100/share. Series 2A will have a yield of 7.9892%, while Series 2B 8.4%. Offer period is on September 16 to 25. Recall that Vista Land recently issued US$350mn of bonds with an interest rate of 9.375%. As of 2Q24, VLL’s debt amounted to P170bn.

Dept of Energy rejects SPNEC’s application to abandon solar project

The Dept of Energy (DOE) rejected the application of SP New Energy (SPNEC PM) to no longer pursue a 280MW solar project. Recall that SPNEC secured the right (and obligation) to sell that amount of power to the grid at a fixed price through the Green Energy Auction Program. SPNEC was invoking force majeure to break the commitment, which was to deliver power by year-end. The company claims the absence of transmission connection as basis for its inability to build the solar capacity. SPNEC will now have to either build the solar farm elsewhere or forfeit its performance bond. Meralco (MER PM) is the controlling shareholder of SPNEC.

Bicam approves CREATE MORE bill

The Congressional Bicameral Conference Committee approved the CREATE MORE bill, which aims to draw in more foreign direct investments by enhancing tax incentives. Senate agreed to adopt provisions in the House version regarding fuel VAT refunds and local taxes on economic zone locators. Bicam version allows fuel suppliers serving tax-exempt entities to be eligible for tax refunds and allows local government units to set the local tax for registered business enterprises at up to 2% of gross income

Salient provisions of the CREATE MORE bill (Bicam report) are:

>Reduction of corporate income tax rates to 20% from 25% for (a) corporations with less than P5mn net taxable income and assets less that P100mn and (b) Registered Business Enterprises under the enhanced deductions regime.

>Introduction of the “Registered Business Enterprise (RBE) Local Tax”, which is capped at 2% of gross income and will replace all local taxes and fees.

>Allowing RBEs 100% deduction on power expenses (from 50% previously), which could lower power costs by P3/kwh for manufacturing.

>Transfer of the responsibility of processing VAT refund claims to the Department of Finance from the Bureau of Internal Revenue to cut delays.

>Allowing firms to maintain work-from-home setup for 50% of workforce to cut costs.

>Reinstate powers of the President to give fiscal and non-fiscal incentives to enterprises without the need for a recommendation from the Fiscal Incentives Review Board.

>Exemption of international carriers from paying VAT on imported fuel, goods, and supplies used for international shipping or air transport operations.

GIR climbs to US$106.9bn

Preliminary data showed the country’s gross international reserves (GIR) inched up to US$106.92bn in end-Aug from US$106.74bn in July. Officials cited higher net income from BSP’s investments abroad. The GIR level represents 7.9 months’ worth of imports. Note that BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$104bn by year-end.

Trade deficit widens to US$4.87bn in July

Preliminary data showed the country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.87bn in July. This is wider than the US$4.1bn deficit in July 2023 and US$4.3bn deficit in June. Exports were flat at US$6.25bn, following a 12% YoY drop in electronic exports. Imports rose 7% YoY to US$11.1bn due to higher imports of capital goods (+10% YoY), raw materials (+13%), and consumer goods (+3%) offset by lower fuel imports (-7%). Imports of electronic products rose 12% YoY, which could indicate a possible rebound in exports in succeeding months. This brings YT-July trade deficit to US$29.9bn, lower by 6% YoY.

FDI net inflows fall 29% YoY to four-year low in June

FDI net inflows fell 29% YoY, 28% MoM to US$394mn in June, the lowest level since April 2020. The decline resulted from lower net inflows across all major FDI components. Investments in debt instruments fell by 30% YoY to US$213mn, net investments in equity capital fell 33% to US$74mn, while reinvestment earnings fell 23% to US$107mn. By source, equity capital placements were mainly from Japan (47%), followed by the United States (15%), Sweden (14%) and Singapore (14%). These were mainly invested in the manufacturing (48%), real estate (18%), wholesale and retail trade (16%) and financial and insurance (11%) sectors. This brings 1H24 net inflows to US$4.4bn, up 7.9% YoY. Note that BSP expected FDI net inflows to reach US$9.5bn this year, up from US$8.9bn in 2023.

Asset quality worsens in July; loans unusually dip 1.3% MoM

Asset quality ratios of the banking sector deteriorated MoM in July, though the magnitude of the move was partly due to an unusual MoM drop in loans. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) increased 9bps MoM to 3.68%, the highest level since July 2022. In nominal terms, NPLs stood at P507.8bn, up 1.1% MoM and 15.5% YoY. Past Due (PD) ratio also blipped up 11bps MoM to 4.40%, with the PHP value up 1.9% MoM and 18.4% YoY. NPL cover dipped 110bps to 94.3% vs 95.4% in June. On a positive note, Restructured Loans (RL) were flat MoM at 2.05%, with the nominal value down 0.9% MoM and down 4.5% YoY.

As mentioned, the asset quality ratios were exacerbated by a 1.3% MoM contraction in loans. We find this odd, given that July is typically a strong/stable month. The last time we observed a MoM drop of this magnitude was way back in 2011 (-1.7% MoM). Compared to the universal/commercial (UKB) loan data, it would seem that the drop is due to reverse repos (RRPs), which is (weirdly) included in the NPL denominator. According the UKB data, loans including RRPs were down 1.2% MoM, while loans EX-RRPs were  up 0.5% MoM. Because of the RRP discrepancy, loan growth (ex-interbank) slowed sharply to 10.3% YoY, compared to 12.5% in June.

The increase in the PD and NPL numbers (and the ratios) are likely due at least in part to banks’ increasing exposure to consumer/MSME/micro loans. This is supported by the much larger jump in PD rates at the Thrift Banking (TB) sector, up 44bps MoM. Counter-intuitively, the ratios for the Digibank industry improved sharply – PD and NPL ratios down c400bps MoM. We think this could be the result of hefty write-downs, as the loan portfolio also shrank 4% MoM.

PAGCOR targets P50bn from privatization of casinos

PAGCOR is targeting P50bn from the privatization of its 45 casinos. It is currently upgrading its facilities ahead of the privatization, which will begin in early 2026. PAGCOR also said that it expects GGR of e-games to triple YoY to P100bn this year – 1H24 GGR of e-games grew 530% to P53.4bn. It also expects GGR of licensed casinos to grow 33% to P275bn this year – note that 1H24 GGR of licensed casinos fell 6% to P100bn.

NEDA sees faster GDP growth in 2H

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) sees GDP growth for 2H24 to be faster than the 6.0% print in 1H24, according to NEDA Secretary Arsenio M. Balisacan. The Secretary thinks the likelihood of the government achieving its 6-7% GDP growth goal this year is now very high, citing easing inflation. To reach the high-end of the government’s target range, GDP growth in 2H will need to average 7.9%.

Senators propose Bill banning online gambling

Senators Alan Peter Cayetano and Joel Villanueva proposed an anti-online gambling Bill. There are currently 45 licensed gaming companies offering e-games. The BIR expects to collect P15bn to P20bn in franchise fees from e-games this year (after collecting P13bn in 2023.) A ban on online gambling would be extremely detrimental to DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM).

Foreign ownership of Philippine market inches up in August

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 5bps MoM to 20.2% (in August) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index expanded 14bps MoM to 25.0%. Net foreign buying increased to US$143mn, from US$60mn in July. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 19 saw increases in foreign ownership while 11 experienced declines.

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

CNVRG – foreign ownership rose 63bps MoM to 17.9%. Net foreign buying hit US$15.3mn, the fifth highest for the month.  

CNPF – foreign ownership increased 26bps MoM to 24.6% while net foreign buying amounted to US$7.8mn.

URC – foreign ownership fell 28bps MoM to 22.0%.  Net foreign selling reached US$31.0mn, the highest for the month.

BLOOM - foreign ownership declined 26bps MoM to 17.0%. It also experienced the third highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$7.0mn).
