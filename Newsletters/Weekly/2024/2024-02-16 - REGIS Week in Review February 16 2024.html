REGIS REPORT

16 February 2024

The market began to consolidate for the week, closing at 6,873.23 (+0.3% WoW), following its recent rally. Year-to-date, the index has already gained 6.5%.

Sentiment remains positive though as the Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept its policy rate (RRP) unchanged at its most recent Monetary Board meeting. This is widely expected given the BSP’s own pronouncements and the US Fed’s moves to keep rates steady. Outside of the maintenance of the policy rate at 6.5%, the BSP also lowered its inflation forecast for this year.  Its baseline forecast was reduced to 3.6% from 3.7%.

Foreigners continued to be net buyers in the market. Headline inflows were strong at US$37.8m.

However, foreign demand was met by local sellers as local investors begin to take profit as the index nears the 7000 resistance.

Gainers still outnumbered losers 9:4, but sectors ended mixed.

Consumer names played catch up given its recent underperformance against the index. URC (+6.0% WoW) and Monde Nissin (+8.7% WoW) led as inflation is expected to taper off this year.

Meanwhile, conglos were lifted by strong foreign demand. GT Capital, JG Summit, and Ayala Corp gained 2.8%, 1.5%, and 1.3% WoW respectively, with GT Capital and Ayala Corp seeing US$6.4m and US$3.1m worth of inflows. Earlier this week, Ayala Corp also disclosed that its subsidiary Integrated Microelectronics (IMI PM) is electing a new CEO effective May. The new CEO, named Lou Hughes, will replace long-time CEO Arthur Tan. 

Property names lagged, SM Prime (-1.9% WoW) and Ayala land (-1.9% WoW). Ayala Land says it is spending P13bn (US$260m) to redevelop four of its flagship malls.  These four malls constitute 35% of ALI’s mall space and roughly half of its mall revenue. The redevelopment, to be done over three years, will not cause a reduction in overall mall revenue.  Malls are ~25% of ALI’s EBIT. Regis property analyst also notes that the POGO sector is once again growing after shrinking for three consecutive years.

Turnover was lower this week at US$84.8m (-18% WoW). Foreigners continued to be net buyers for US$37.8m.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property - POGO sector trickles back
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Demand drivers: POGO sector, infrastructure, and tax incentives

We turn incrementally more positive on the property sector given: 1) the trickling back of the POGO sector which will likely lead to improved office and residential demand in Metro Manila (particularly the Manila Bay area), 2) the imminent completion of a number of upcoming infrastructure projects which will enhance connectivity of adjacent provinces, and 3) the higher VAT-free ceiling on low-cost housing which should improve affordability in more remote areas.

Mall REIT IPOs likely soon; residential prices recovering; office negative rental reversions to peak

Now that mall profitability has returned to pre-COVID levels, the anticipated decline in interest rates could lead developers to spin-off mall assets into REITs, which in turn could result in NAV increases of up to 20% for some companies.

Residential prices continue to recover, and reservation sales have generally returned to pre-COVID levels. Furthermore, the residential segment should receive a boost from: more (foreign) POGO employees residing in Metro Manila, infrastructure projects improving connectivity, and the higher VAT-free ceiling improving affordability. However, we refrain from being overly bullish due to modestly rising credit risks brought about by looser payment schemes.

While we expect negative office rental reversions to peak this year, we forecast Metro Manila vacancy to drop by 2025E, as new supply will only add 5% to office stock over the next two years.

RLC is fundamentally most compelling; prefer ALI among the large caps

We forecast RLC to show the fastest earnings growth in the sector (relative to 2019) and, on our estimates, it would be the biggest beneficiary of a mall REIT spin-off. We also consider current valuations to be compelling (at 61% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 6.6X FY24E P/E).

Among the large caps, we prefer ALI due to our strong growth projections over the next two years (presales/earnings CAGR of 15%/16%) and the NAV enhancement we expect from infrastructure projects adjacent to its land bank.

We raise the ALI and SMPH target prices by 15%/13%

We made only minor tweaks to most of our target prices. However, we raise our target on ALI by 15% as we lower our target discount to NAV to 20% (from 30%) due to potential NAV enhancement from the completion of Cavite-Laguna Expressway in 2H25. We also raise our target price on SMPH by 13% as we lower our target discount from 10% to zero in anticipation of NAV enhancement from lower interest rates in 2025. Meanwhile, we upgrade FLI to Buy.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land to spend P13bn to redevelop four flagship malls

Ayala Land said it would spend P13bn to redevelop four flagship malls. The malls are said to be Glorietta, Greenbelt, Trinoma, and Ayala Center Cebu. The renovations will begin in 1Q24 and will be completed by end-2026. They will be done in phases to reduce disruptions to operations. The company does not expect the renovations to result in lower mall revenue. Ayala Land’s target to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023) will remain intact. The spending for the redevelopment as well as the resulting lower occupancy are already in Regis forecasts.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market stays flat in January

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 2bps MoM to 20.4%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index also dropped 2bps MoM to 24.5%. Net foreign buying rose to US$80mn, from US$18mn in December 2023. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 18 saw declines in foreign ownership while 12 experienced increases

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

BPI – foreign ownership fell 1.11 percentage points MoM to 18.5% due largely to the issuance of new shares to the Gokongwei group (brought about by the merger with Robinsons Savings Bank)

NIKL – foreign ownership dropped 87bps MoM to 35.5%.

URC – foreign ownership fell 84bps MoM to 23.6%. It was also the top foreign sold stock for the month (at US$14.9mn).

BDO – foreign ownership increased 51bps MoM to 28.7%. It was also the top foreign bought stock (at US$40.6mn)

San Miguel consortium submits highest bid for NAIA PPP

SMC SAP & Co. Consortium offered a bid of 82.2% for the government’s share of the airport’s revenues, excluding passenger service charge (i.e. revenues from terminal fees). The second best offer was GMR Airports Consortium – 33.3% while the third was the Manila International Airport Consortium – 25.9%. All bids will still need to be reviewed by the Department of Transportation. Winning bidder will be announced next week pending resolution of “disputes submitted by certain bidders vis a vis other certain bidders.”

Preliminary timetable:

March 15 – Signing of Concession Agreement

Sep 11 – Start date of concession

The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62mn annually from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years).

 

MGreen raises stake in SPNEC

MGen Renewable Energy Inc. (MGreen) bought 2.17bn preferred shares from SPNEC's parent company, Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings Inc., worth P2.50bn. The investment further raises the controlling stake of MGreen and its affiliates to 55.96% from 50.55%. MGreen and its affiliates have now made acquisitions amounting to P20.20bn with 19.473bn common shares, 38.89% of SPNEC's total outstanding common shares. MGreen is the largest shareholder followed by Solar Philippines now with less than a 30% stake. SPNEC is currently developing a 3.5GW solar farm in Nueva Ecija and Bulacan, the world's largest solar power project, for completion by 2027.

ERC says Meralco rate hike unduly burdens consumers

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) questioned Meralco’s (MER PM) higher generation charge in its February 2024 billing. ERC said it was premature for the utility firm to pass on to the consumers the cost related to higher gas prices. The ERC, in a letter to Meralco dated February 12, urged the distribution utility (DU) firm to complete the validation to justify the rate adjustment. Meralco raised electricity prices by P0.5738/kwh to P11.9168/kwh, primarily due to the higher cost fuel costs of Sta. Rita and San Lorenzo gas plants, which imported liquefied natural gas. Meralco, for its part, said it will comply with the directive of the ERC and reported that a refund of the gas component is in the offing.

SPNEC added to MSCI Philippines Small Cap Index

SP New Energy Corporation (SPNEC PM) was added to the MSCI Philippines Small Cap Index

SPNEC replaced MREIT. The Index has 20 constituents and represents 14% of the free-float adjusted market cap of the Philippine equity universe.

Ayala group names new CEO for IMI

The Ayala Group’s tech manufacturing arm Integrated Micro-electronics Inc. (IMI PM) announced that its Chief Executive Officer Arthur R. Tan will be replaced by Lou Hughes in May this year. Tan has been in the IMI leadership role for the past 22 years.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government pushes P839bn water infrastructure projects

Finance Undersecretary Catherine Fong said that the government wants to implement 44 infrastructure flagship projects worth P839bn aimed at boosting water resources. The largest is the P115bn Central Luzon-Pampanga River Floodway Control Project (funding through Official Development Assistance). The other big ticket item is the P102bn Paranaque Spillway Tunnel Project (funding through Japanese ODA loan). All projects are awaiting government approvals and are part of the Marcos administration’s Build Better More program of P8.78tn infrastructure projects.

Korean tourists jump to post-Covid high in January

Tourist arrivals jumped 24% YoY and 15% MoM to 574k in January 2024, sustaining the big recovery in December 2023. The latest data showed a reversion to 83% of pre-Covid, a new post-covid high due to a 38% YoY, 15% MoM recovery in Korean tourists, which at 174k was already 5% above 2019 average. Chinese tourists jumped 3.5x YoY and 76% MoM to 33k, reverting to 23% of pre-Covid levels. Improving tourism data should bode well for the current account and GDP.

Banks’ end-2023 NPL ratio drops to 3.23%

Preliminary data showed Philippine banks’ NPL ratio fell to 3.23% as of end-Dec from 3.41% in end-Nov. Gross NPLs stood at P447bn in end-Dec, up 12% YoY but down 1.6% MoM. Loans (net of interbank) amounted to P13.5tn, up 10.1% YoY and 3.8% MoM (faster than November loan growth of 9.3% YoY). NPL coverage ratio at 102.13% in end-Dec ’23, down from 107% in end-Dec ’22 but higher than 101.47% in end-Nov ’23. Past due loans rose 14% YoY to P547bn, bringing past due ratio to 3.95% from 4.22% in end-Nov ’23 and 3.79% in end-Dec 2022. Restructured loans dropped 8.3% YoY to P302bn, accounting for 2.19% of total loans (down from 2.61% in end-Dec ‘22 and 2.29% in end-Nov ’23).

Commercial banks’ loan growth steady at 7.0% in December; M3 growth moderates to 5.9%

Preliminary data showed commercial banks’ loans grew by 7.0% YoY in December, unchanged from Nov ’23 (7.0%). Outstanding loans for production activities rose by 5.5% in December from 5.7% in November due to slowdown in real estate loans (10.9% in Dec from 20.8% in Nov); wholesale and retail trade (7.2% from 9.6%), electricity (6.2% from 12.8%). Consumer loans increased by 23.5% in December from 23.6% in November. Credit card loans grew 29.6%, slowing from 31.0% in Nov. Auto loans up 16% YoY in Dec, broadly unchanged from 15.6% YoY in Nov. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3 growth) slowed further to 5.9% YoY in December from 7.0% in November.

DOE sets 4,399MW green energy auction program in August

The Department of Energy will conduct the Third Round of the Green Energy Auction (GEA-3) this August with 4,399MW of the following renewable energy up for bidding.

-699MW from impounding Hydro with a target Delivery Commencement Period (DCP) from 2028-2030

-3120MW from Pumped-Storage Hydro with a DCP from 2028-2030

-380MW from Geothermal with a DCP beginning 2026-2028

-200MW from Run-of-river Hydro with a DCP from 2026-2028

GEA-3 will cater to non-feed-in-tariff eligible renewable energy. No price caps will be set and developers will have to submit price offers pending the review of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).

E-games revenue hits P58bn in 2023

PAGCOR said that revenue from E-games grew over 92% to P58bn in 2023. This comprises e-games, e-bingo, specialty games, and sports betting (does not include POGO). In 2022, GGR from Integrated Resorts in PAGCOR Entertainment City hit P146bn.

House committee approves bill banning POGO

The House Games and Amusements panel approved a bill banning Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators due to crimes linked to the sector. However, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco is in favor of allowing them to continue operating if they are properly monitored and regulated. He said that since the number of POGO firms (now called Internet Gaming Licensee) has been reduced to 75 (from nearly 300 in 2019), the number of crimes has dropped substantially. He also said that licensees now come from more countries, such as Malaysia, Singapore, United States, and European countries. Separately, the Local Government Unit of Pasig shut down POGO, E-games, and E-bingo establishments in the city. A City Ordinance in 2022 gave the gambling establishments until the end of 2023 to wind down their operations in Pasig. Regis estimates that c. 5% of POGO office space is located in Pasig City.

FDI net inflows rise 28% YoY to US$1bn in November

Preliminary data showed foreign direct investments (FDI) posted net inflows of US$1.05bn in November, up 28% YoY and 60% MoM. This was due to the 58% YoY and 78% MoM jump in nonresidents’ net investments in debt instruments to US$897mn. Investments in equity capital (ex-reinvestment of earnings) fell 53% YoY to US$85mn while reinvestment of earnings dropped 8% YoY to US$66mn. Cumulative 11M23 FDI net inflows stood at US$7.6bn, lower by 13% YoY.

Senate approves P100 wage increase on 2nd reading

The Senate approved on second reading a bill calling for a P100 across-the-board minimum wage increase for workers in the private sector. Senate President Juan Miguel Zubiri expects the bill to be approved on third and final reading next week. If implemented, this could result in a 22-24% increase in minimum wages for the private sector across all regions. The current minimum wage in the National Capital Region is P573-610/day (~USD10-11).

Lower House looking at wage hike of as much as P400

The House of Representatives is seeking to pass a P400 across-the-board wage increase for workers in the private sector according to House Deputy Majority Leader and Iloilo Rep. Janette L. Garin, citing instructions from Speaker Ferdinand Martin G. Romualdez. Recall that last Feb 14, the Senate approved on second reading a bill calling for a P100 across-the-board minimum wage increase. Ms. Garin said the wage increase should be balanced by the capacity of companies to pay since some might be forced to lay off workers or even shut down.

Remittances rises 3.8% YoY in December; FY23 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$33.5bn

Remittances coursed through banks rose 3.8% YoY to US$3.28bn in December. This was the fastest pace since Dec 2022 and the absolute amount was also a new high. In PHP terms, remittances were up 3.7% YoY in Dec. Growth accelerated in almost all key remittances sources, except for Kuwait, Singapore, and UAE. Remittances from Kuwait fell for the 8-straight month. Meanwhile, remittances growth from Singapore and UAE slowed to 2.4% and 3.8% in Dec from 3.3% and 9.3% the previous month. These were more than offset by stronger growth rates in most countries. For FY23, remittances grew by 2.9% YoY to US$33.5bn, broadly in line with the BSP’s forecast growth rate of 3%. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% in 2024.

BSP maintains policy rate

The Monetary Board left the target reverse repurchase rate at 6.5% for the third straight meeting. Meanwhile the BSP adjusted its risk-adjusted inflation forecasts. The 2024 “risk-adjusted” forecast was lowered to 3.9% from 4.2% previously while “baseline” forecast was lowered to 3.6% from 3.7% while the 2025 “risk-adjusted” forecast was raised to 3.5% from 3.4% while “baseline” forecast was kept at 3.2%. BSP said there is “scope for a rate cut” this year as soon as they see a firmer inflation downtrend. Inflation might continue to ease this quarter due to base effects, but it might pick up above the 2-4% target in the second quarter. Inflation will go back to the target in the second half. They also stated that higher-than-expected minimum wage increases in the Philippines could reverse slowing inflation.
