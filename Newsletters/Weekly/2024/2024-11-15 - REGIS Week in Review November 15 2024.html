REGIS REPORT

15 November 2024

Phils sank deeper into the red, falling 4.3% on the week on heavy foreign selling. At its best, the PSEi gave a year-to-date return of 17% through early October. That has now been diminished to +3.5% for 2024, eroded by four straight weeks of declines. Foreigners were de-risking yet again and were net sellers for USD 86.7mn.

What prompted this was a continuation of the sell flows from the prior week, when Trump’s victory resulted in an even stronger USD (which was already rising from the bottom in early October) as well as rising US bond yields. This “Trump Trade” had actually been manifesting even in late October, when the perception of a Republican win gained traction with markets.

The risk aversion was prompted too by further losses on the Peso, which has slid back down to June lows after losing 0.8% for the week. It has now fallen a steep 4.8% since early October on concerns that the BSP is looking more dovish on monetary policy versus what the Fed is likely to do near term.

On the ground, what soured sentiment was the recent spate of earnings. In particular, the real estate sector did not paint good picture. Demand remains tepid in the mid-income housing segment, well beyond the pandemic lockdown’s effects. Consequently, pre-sales have been on a declining trend. Cancellations (a result of prior years of loose downpayment policies) continues to be sore point and has resulted in inventory hovering now at 18-24 months equivalent of sales.

Meantime, the office sector’s vacancy woes are set to worsen from today’s 17% to probably over 20% with the definitive expulsion of POGOs by end 2024. This likewise will have a knock-on effect on the condo market, as vacancies there are likely to rise as mainland Chinese tenants leave the country. It could put pressure capital values as well.

The property sector was a key drag, led by heavyweights SM Prime (-5.6% wow) and Ayala Land (-6.3% wow). Conglos being market proxies likewise underperformed led by Ayala Corporation (-11.3% wow),  GT Capital (-9.2%),  JG Summit (-7.5%) and SM Investments (-4.5%).

There were specific misses as well on well followed stocks that impacted sentiment. Universal Robina (-16.4% wow) missed estimates as the non-branded business (in particular sugar) saw a big contraction in margins. Moreover, the important domestic branded foods business saw another mild contraction in revenue which peers did not see. The more discretionary nature of its domestic offering took a back seat, at a time when the mass consumer was still reeling from the effects of inflation (particularly food).

Bloomberry (-20.5% wow) was the worst performing index member after booking a loss in 3Q24. While the new Solaire North casino did well on revenue, the main Solaire Entertainment City facility unfortunately saw a 5% decline in GGR as VIP and Slots shrank for the quarter. Both volumes and win rates moved unfavorably for those revenue segments.

Philippine Strategy - CREATE MORE Law – tax cuts aimed at new investments, exporters
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Tax breaks (not) for all

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr signed the CREATE MORE Act into law on 11 November. The law came as a disappointment to some that had expected an across-the-board reduction in corporate income tax from 25% currently to 20%. Instead, the tax cut was reserved for (very) small businesses, and for “Registered Business Enterprises” (RBEs) as approved by incentive-granting agencies. To the upside, CREATE MORE’s tax and non-tax perks should promote new investments, particularly for exporters. A provision supporting work-from-home for BPOs, though, could hurt the already-beleaguered office sector.

Targeted incentives to promote investments

The Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy (CREATE MORE) Act’s main feature is to lower the corporate income tax rate from the current 25% to 20%. However, this only applies to:

  • small businesses with taxable income of less than P5mn (roughly $85,300), and with total assets of under P100mn (or $1.7mn), and
  • RBEs, companies that are registered with government incentive[1]granting bodies such as the Board of Investments (BOI), Philippine Export Zone Authority (PEZA), etc.

The intention is clearly for the CREATE MORE Act to benefit a very targeted set of businesses. The government estimates cumulative lost tax revenue of just P5.9bn from 2025 to 2028.

Other perks to benefit FDIs, exporters…

Apart from lower income taxes, the new law also addresses some concerns of existing and potential FDIs, particularly export-oriented industries, including:

  • Larger tax deductions on power costs, which we estimate could reduce an RBE’s electricity bill by as much as 25%
  • Exporters are exempt from paying the 12% VAT on goods and services that are directly attributable to production
  • Streamlining the VAT refund process by bringing these up to the Department of Finance (DOF) from the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). Some foreign investors have complained of multi-year delays in claiming billions of Pesos worth of refunds.

… but work-from-home revisions could hurt offices

The new law also allows firms in special economic zones to have as many as 50% of employees working remotely – up from 30% – and retain their full incentives. While this is positive for the business process outsourcing (BPO) operators, it is potentially negative for office landlords. The office sector is already reeling from high vacancy rates of 16% in Metro Manila, with the recently formalized ban on POGOs potentially raising that to 20%.

 
Philippine Property - President Marcos formalizes POGO ban
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

President issues Executive Order banning offshore gaming

President Marcos issued Executive Order 74 (EO 74), which formalizes the ban on the offshore gaming sector (which President Marcos first announced during his State of the Nation Address in July 2024). Under EO 74, offshore gaming activities, including those granted licenses under the Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) or Internet Gaming Licensee (IGL) framework, must cease operations by 31 December 2024. We expect this ban to be detrimental to the Metro Manila office market and to all forms of property in the Manila Bay area.

Interestingly, EO 74 explicitly exempts offshore gaming “…conducted in PAGCOR-operated casinos, licensed casinos, or integrated resorts with junket agreements.” However, we suspect that licensed casinos will refrain from offshore gaming (except perhaps for VIP proxy betting) since President Marcos has already expressed his disapproval.

Most negative for Manila Bay area

The POGO sector occupies a significant amount of office space, particularly in the Manila Bay area. We estimate that the sector occupies 4% (600,000sqm) of Metro Manila office stock and c. 20% of Manila Bay area office space. Thus, a ban on the sector could result in Metro Manila office vacancy rising to 20% (16% currently) and Manila Bay area office vacancy climbing to about 50%. In such a scenario, it would be reasonable for office rental rates to fall 5%-10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area.

The POGO sector also employs over 100,000 foreign nationals (based on our estimates), a large portion of whom reside in the Manila Bay area. Thus, their departure could lead to significant drops in Manila Bay area property prices.

What is the impact to the listed developers?

Based on our estimates, the annual profit hit to the developers we cover should be manageable at less than 5% due to their relatively diversified operations. The office segment comprises less than 40% of EBIT and POGO firms occupy less than 5% of the office space of the developers we cover (see Figure 1).

Counterintuitively, SM Prime will likely take a larger cut to its NAV than its office exposure (7% of EBIT) might suggest. If Manila Bay area property prices fall 20%, we estimate that SMPH’s NAV will drop by 5%-10%, with the drop largely coming from the Pasay Coastal Development project, which accounts for 27% of NAV. However, since SMPH is trading at 31% discount to NAV, we would argue that the share price assigns little to no value to the Coastal Development project.

What about gaming firms?

Casinos in PAGCOR Entertainment City (which is in the Manila Bay area) will likely be negatively affected by the POGO ban as well. About 20% of the GGR of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) comes from Chinese citizens living in the Philippines, many of whom are likely POGO employees.

 
SM Prime - 3Q24 presales fall 50% QoQ and 67% YoY
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Presales fall sharply

SM Prime’s 9M24 net profit rose 12% to P33.9bn while its operating profit grew 7% to P47.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. However, the most noteworthy portion of its disclosure was the sharp drop in 3Q and 9M presales.

Reservation sales fall for second consecutive quarter

After falling 48% QoQ in 2Q24, presales dropped another 50% QoQ (67% YoY) to P6.8bn in 3Q24, the lowest quarterly figure in the past 10 years. As a result, 9M24 presales fell 47% YoY to P47bn. SM Prime isn’t alone, as a few other developers also showed substantial QoQ and YoY declines in 3Q24.

The disclosure to the PSE provided limited information but we hope to get more details during the SM Investments briefing on November 13 (Registration link).

Mall revenue growth slows

3Q24 systemwide (Philippines + China) total mall revenue growth slowed to 6%, from 8% in 1H24. This could mean that Philippine same-mall revenue growth was just 4%-5%, at the lower end of the historical range (of 4%-8%). Cinema sales seem to be the weak link, though rental revenue growth also appears to have slowed in 3Q.

Offices and hotels show stronger growth

SM Prime’s office and hotel segments, which typically comprise c. 10% of EBIT, grew faster than the larger businesses in 3Q24. Office revenue grew 5% QoQ (+13% YoY), possibly signaling an improvement in occupancy. Meanwhile, hotel revenue grew quite rapidly, at 16% QoQ and 28% YoY.

Universal Robina - 3Q: Plunge in commodity segment; BCF rebound not yet in sight
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q setback

URC reported a slight increase (+1%) in revenue for 9M to P119bn, which produced a modest drop (-3%) in EBIT to P12.3bn. The implied 3Q numbers, however, were rather poor as margins dove due to a plunge in the profitability of its non-BCF segment, particularly its sugar operation.

As a result, headline profit fell 18% to P8.0bn, with 3Q profit down 54% to P1.4bn (inclusive of P498m in FX losses).

Non-BCF margins dive

EBIT margins at its non-BCF segments collapsed to 3% as its sugar business was pushed to a loss because of a sharp decline in sugar prices. The windfall periods of 4Q23 and 1Q24 (when EBIT margins were as high as 19%) quickly turned into the current situation, underscoring the inherent volatility of the industry.

Rebound in its BCF not yet in sight

URC’s domestic BCF business is still struggling to return to positive top line growth, following continued price hikes (~25% cumulative) due to the surge in input prices from 2022, which sapped consumer wallets. 3Q revenues were still down 3% YoY. This is in contrast to the positive (albeit modest) growth figures posted by other listed consumer goods manufacturers.

We suspect the weak top line may be because URC’s products are more discretionary than the others. “Premiumization” initiatives over the past years have come to naught (for now) as consumers are going back to “value" due to high food prices.

The international BCF is one bright spot, with revenues firmly on the rebound (+5% YoY). Revenue growth would have been higher if not for the relative strength of the PHP.

Near-term outlook

Management believes the worse is over for the sugar business and profitability there should normalize over the succeeding months. The domestic sugar industry is tightly regulated and protected so profitability will remain highly subject to government action, including decisions on allowing imports.

For the full year FY24, management is guiding for low single digit top line growth and for EBIT to decline slightly (“low single digits”). For next year (FY25), the company is guiding for an improvement in top line growth to “mid to high single digit”.

Puregold - 3Q: Margin sacrifice
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M earnings rise 4.5% on revenue growth of 9%

Puregold (PGOLD) reported net income of P6.9b for 9M, +4.5% YoY, with revenues growing +9.1%. For 3Q alone, profit was down 11% to P1.96bn, on top line growth of +11%, as gross margins dropped.

While the 9M profit figure means PGOLD is unlikely to meet our FY23 estimates, we are not too alarmed. In fact, we are positive about what recent developments imply about PGOLD’s shift in tactics and strategy. The company has chosen to drop prices to address the rise of hard discounters and has now plunged into the small format “proximity shopping” segment of the grocery market.

Margins narrow, sales rise

The decline in consolidated gross margins to 16.7% in 3Q (-180bp QoQ and -140bpYoY) was a result of more aggressive price moves in response to the increased presence of hard discounters (e.g. Dali).

The positive impact those price moves had on customer purchases is evident. Sales rose +11% YoY in 3Q, compared to +8% in 2Q. SSSG for the Puregold brand accelerated to 6.7% (vs 2.0% in 2Q) while S&R’s rose to 7.6% (vs 6.0%). We agree with management’s view that its value proposition to consumers should be maintained (even at the cost of lower short-term margins).

Entering new segment with Puremart acquisition

The company recently disclosed the acquisition of the Puremart chain of stores from PGOLD’s principal shareholder for P568m (US$10m). Puremart currently has around 120-130 stores with an average size of ~150sqm, smaller than PGOLD’s minimart format (>250sqm). We figure it is not making a profit today but is likely to add ~1% to PGOLD’s revenue.

The fact that it is being acquired from a related party could be an issue for some, but we are not too concerned. The absolute price is small, and the price per store also looks reasonable if not cheap.

Puremart likely to boost long-term growth

Management did not disclose near-term plans for Puremart, apart from the expected integration into the Puregold system. However, we believe entry into small format stores is necessary for PGOLD to retain its edge. We note that Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) now has 4,000 outlets while Alfamart has 2,000. Dali has ~600 stores and O!Save has over 300.

 
Cebu Air - Higher fleet, weak demand push CEB to a 3Q loss but outlook is upbeat
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M24 profit drops 54% YoY to P2.04bn – Behind estimates

CEB incurred a core net loss of P1.13bn in 3Q24, as sustained heavy investments coincided with seasonally weak demand. This trimmed its 9M24 profit by 54% YoY to P2.04bn, falling short of our FY24E of P7.5bn. Despite the disappointing results, management is confident that the worst is behind them and things are looking up.

3Q24 topline down 1%, as fares soften on weak demand

CEB grew its seat capacity by 13% YoY and pax volumes by 14% in 3Q, with load factors marginally (+50bps YoY) higher at 84%. However, average fares fell 15% YoY due to (a) shift in school summer vacation (from July/Aug in 2023 to June/July in 2024) and (b) general weakness in consumer spending.

Fixed costs surged on accelerated investments

Even though jet kero prices fell 19% YoY during the quarter, other (mostly fixed) costs surged (e.g. non-cash costs, aircraft maintenance, airport/ground handling). Financing costs also climbed by 44% YoY, after the company took on additional borrowings to grow its fleet to 91 by end-September from 85 last year. The company deemed these investments as necessary considering the ongoing global aircraft supply and P&W engine issues.

Guiding for lower capex and net debt, capacity growth of 20-25%, a share

buyback

Management further raised its 24E seat capacity growth guidance to 18-20%, suggesting >35% capacity increase for 4Q24. Given that over half of these capacity increases will be allocated at the congested airport of Manila, management expressed confidence on a corresponding rebound in unit revenues. For 2025, it emphasized that its seat growth guidance of 20-25% will only involve a net fleet addition of +1 aircraft, as it expects a lower number of grounded aircraft and higher utilization. As a result, it expects (a) its 2025 capex could be “halved” from this year’s peak of over P60bn and (b) net debt levels to start tapering down from October 2024 onwards. To manifest its confidence, it has initiated a P2bn buyback program of CEB and CEBCP shares. The program is equivalent to 6% of the combined market capitalization of these shares.

The QoQ output drop was also evident in India (but not Australia and Vietnam).

Apart from the low RE output, a key reason for the sharp QoQ profit drop was a 1+½ month maintenance outage at SLTEC in 3Q. While the coal-fired SLTEC is no longer considered part of ACEN’s portfolio, it remains the single largest source of kWh for ACEN. ACEN still buys the output of SLTEC under a long-term contract, which it sells to other parties (e.g. Meralco). Management mentioned that another outage at SLTEC is expected in Dec.

Adding to pipeline

ACEN added 1,973MW of (attributable) new capacity to its total “pipeline” in 3Q. Of that figure, 553MW was in the Philippines and the rest (1420MW) overseas. On ACEN’s reckoning, it has 6,754MW of capacity (operational, under construction and committed), of which around 3,051MW is currently operational.

Equity-raising in 2H25?

Given its aggressive project pipeline, it is apparent ACEN will need to raise capital soon. Management now says it may raise at least US$500m of equity by the latter half of next year.

RE certificate trading by Dec?

The Philippine government has indicated that the trading of renewable energy certificates (REC) could commence next month. This could unlock a windfall for ACEN, which has ample RECs that it could potentially monetize.

Banks’ Asset quality (Sept) improves... but not really

Based on headline reported BSP data, asset quality of Philippine banks improved dramatically in September with Past Due (PD), and NPL ratios falling 17bps and 12bps MoM respectively. However, the BSP data is pretty “dirty”, due mostly to the “wrong” denominator that is used when calculating asset quality ratios. While asset quality ratios did improve MoM, it was much, much milder than the reported numbers suggested.

When the BSP reports loans data for their “loan growth” press release, they net out interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs, both with the BSP and with other banks). However, when it comes to the asset quality data, the “total loan portfolio” denominator INCLUDES both IBL and RRP. And this is a problem, because both IBL and RRP can move up and down quite aggressively on a monthly basis. For example, for September alone, the RRP base for all banks increased from P528bn in August to P995bn in September. That growth of P466bn is equivalent to 3% of the “total loan portfolio” base!

From here on out, I will present the reported ratios, alongside the more relevant data (excluding IBL and RRP), which I will refer to as “adjusted”.

As reported, NPL ratio dropped 12bps MoM to 3.47%. However, for the adjusted NPL ratio, the improvement was much milder, down 1bp MoM to 3.80%. In nominal terms, gross NPLs were P517.5bn, up 0.9% MoM. The sector’s Past Due (PD) ratio saw bigger improvements, with the reported PD down 17bps MoM to 4.25%, while the adjusted PD ratio dipped 4bps MoM to 4.65%. The Peso value of PDs crept up just 0.2% MoM to P632.9bn. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio fell 7bps MoM to 1.98% as reported, with the adjusted RL ratio down 2bps MoM to 2.16%. The absolute level of RLs was up 0.5% MoM.

It would appear that at least some of the improvement in asset quality came from write-offs at both the Thrift Bank (TB) and Digibank (DGB) levels. The Peso value of NPLs fell 1.5% MoM for TBs and a whopping 17.5% MoM for DGBs. Both segments also saw big MoM drops in their loan loss reserves (LLR), supporting our theory. The LLR dip was milder in the All Banks data. But the LLR do tend to dip every quarter-end – even more dramatically at year-end – coinciding with earnings reporting.

Loan growth, ex-IBL/RRP, was steady at 11.0%, up slightly from the 10.9% posted in August. This was milder than the reported data that showed a 14.1% YoY growth, due to the massive jump in RRPs, as discussed above. Excluding IBL/RRPs, system-wide LDRs were flat at 69.6%, from 70.3% in August, indicating significant room for loan growth.
