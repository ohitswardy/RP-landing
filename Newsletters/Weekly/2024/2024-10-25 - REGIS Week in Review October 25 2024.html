REGIS REPORT

25 October 2024

The PSEi fell -1.37% WoW to 7314.23, virtually erasing last week’s gains, as the lightly traded market was weighed down by pause in net foreign buying. Foreign investors turned better sellers for the week, albeit lightly at under $4m, which put an end to their 10 weekly inflow streak. Activity on the ground thinned down to a daily average turnover of just $73m, -28% WoW and more than -30% below the YTD average. Note that government work was suspended from Wednesday through Friday due to heavy storm. And while the stock market was open all week, FX trading was shut for 2 days. Note that upon resumption on Friday the PHP sharply weakened and capped the week down by another -1.4% WoW to 58.333, marking its 5th week of decline and settling around its weakest levels since start of August. Meanwhile most equity indices across the broader region were likewise lower or flattish ahead of the forthcoming US elections which is less than 2 weeks away.

On the ground, investors are awaiting the bulk of the 3Q earnings season. BPI +2.03% WoW, which announced results during the prior week, held their briefing during the week (see Rafa’s note below), but there were no new reporters among the PSE benchmark constituents. Outside the main index, AP -0.26% announced that their 9M core earnings edged up +2% YoY to P27b, trending below Regis estimates but ahead of the Street. Meanwhile RRHI -1.5% announced that its 3Q net income surged +29% YoY to P1b, while 9M core +8% to P4b. Announcement of more corporate results is expected to accelerate in the coming week with scheduled briefings by BDO, MER, WLCON on Monday, followed by MBT on Tuesday.

Breadth of the local market was skewed to the negative with half of index members down by more than -2% WoW. Biggest laggards this week were GLO -6.03%, ACEN -5.41%, SMPH -4.67%, WLCON -4.59%, MBT -4.11% and NIKL -4.01%. On the opposite end, some of the few bright issues were consumer plays MONDE +4.17% WoW and URC +1.02%, along with GTCAP +2.36% and BPI +2.03%. In terms of foreign flow, top sold this week were MBT -4.11%, MONDE +4.17% and URC +1.02%; while top inflows were BPI +2.03%, AC +0.14%, GTCAP +2.36% and BDO -2.62%.

Up ahead, we have a 4-day trading week with the Philippine market shut on Friday, November 1, for All Saints.

RESEARCH
Bank of the Philippine Islands - Big beat in 3Q24; lifting estimates and TP
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Strong NIMs and trading gains prompt TP upgrade to P160/sh

BPI’s 9M24 NPAT of P48bn roundly beat Regis and Street estimates. Loan growth of 19% was driven by high-yielding consumer loans pushing NIMs up 22bps YoY. Profits also received a boost from a tripling of trading gains. The consumer push, though, has hurt asset quality, with NPL cover down to 111% (from 156% at end-2023). We increase our 2024-26E earnings forecasts by 4-9%, and up our target price by 18.5% to P160/sh, rolling over to our 2025E estimates. Maintain Buy.

9M24 beats, on trading and fees; NIMs still expanding on consumer push

BPI posted record-high NPAT of P17.4bn in 3Q24, up 30% YoY and 14% QoQ. The main driver of the beat was trading gains, at over P2bn in the quarter alone (vs just P171mn in 3Q23). Fee income was strong, too, up 26% YoY in 3Q (+28% in 9M24). NII was slightly ahead of our expectations, driven largely by higher NIMs on explosive growth in personal loans (+129%), “business banking” (+88%), and microfinance (+67%). These should help cushion the impact of lower interest rates heading into 2025E.

Credit costs low, but likely to rebound in 2025/26E

Asset quality deteriorated, though this was to be expected given the rapid consumer loan growth. Quarter-end NPL ratio was up 10bps QoQ to 2.30%, but NPL cover fell sharply to 111%, allowing credit cost to stay low (30-35bps in 9M). However, with cover closing in on 100%, we expect credit costs to jump to 50-60bps from 2025E onwards. Despite this, we believe the risk-adjusted returns of the consumer portfolio are still accretive.

Raising forecasts 4-9% and lifting TP to P160.00 (+18.5%); Maintain Buy

We increase our net profit forecasts by 8-9% for 2024/25E, and 4% for 2026E. We also raise our target price by 18.5% to P160.00/sh, as we roll over to our 2025E estimates. Our target is based on ROE-g/COE-g, assuming long-term ROE of 14.75% (from 14.5%), COE of 12%, and growth of 8.25%, which translates to 1.73x P/bk. At our target, the stock offers potential total return of 15%; maintain Buy. Key downside risks include new NPL formation from the fast-growing consumer book, and intensifying competition on loan pricing.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Aboitiz Power 9M core income up 2% to P27bn

AP’s core income (ex- one-offs) for 9M24 rose 2% to P27.2bn from P26.7bn, trending below Regis estimates but ahead of Street’s. Headline income for 9M was P27.3bn and “beneficial” EBITDA rose 12% to P56bn. Generation business saw unit sales rise by 2% with the addition of two solar farms, while volumes for distribution business grew 8%.

Globe confirms appointment of new CEO

Globe confirmed that it is appointing Carl Raymond Cruz as CEO. Cruz will assume the CEO post after the stockholders’ meeting next year. He will replace long-time CEO Ernest Cu who is retiring after 15 years. Cruz will first become Deputy CEO in Jan 2025 before he assumes the CEO post later in the year. He has been CEO of the Nigerian operations of Airtel less than two years. Prior to that, Cruz was with Unilever.

Roxas Holdings (ROX PM) debt default; BPI, BDO have minimal exposure

Roxas Holdings said that it defaulted on its loans with BDO and BPI, as well as state-owned Land Bank of the Philippines, in March. The default came shortly after ROX shut down permanently the refinery operations of Central Azucarera Don Pedro which was founded in 1927. ROX did not meet it covenant, specifically the DSCR requirement of at least 1.1x and debt-to-equity ratio of not more than 2.33. ROX’ had P4.29bn worth of loans as of September 2023. BPI and Land Bank granted an extension on payments but BDO declined. BDO’s exposure is about P1bn, 70% of which have been provided for, while BPI’s exposure was P1.8bn. ROX’s property, plant, and equipment was valued at P8.36bn, which were mortgaged to secure the loans.

Malampaya Phase 4 development certified as project of national significance

Prime Energy Resources Development B.V. has secured national significance certification for Phase 4 of the Malampaya deep-water gas-to-power project worth US$600mn. The project entails the drilling of the Camago and Malampaya East production wells and the drilling of the Bagong Pag-asa exploration well. The wells are expected to be drilled next year as the Malampaya consortium aims to deliver new gas supply by 2026. Service Contract 38 operated by Prime Energy and consortium partners UC38 LLC, PNOC Exploration Corp. as well as Prime Oil and Gas Inc. will govern the project.

Sangley Airport upgrade moves closer to implementation after securing PCC approval

The Philippine Competition Commission approved the proposed US$11bn Sangley Airport upgrade project in Cavite. Project is a JV between the Cavite provincial government and the SPIA consortium that includes Virata-let Cavitex Holdings, the Yuchengco’s House of Investments (HI PM), and MacroAsia (MAC PM). Samsung C&T Corp., Munich Airport International GmbH, and Ove Arup & Partners Hong Kong Ltd. are also involved in the project likely as technical consultants. However, Transportation Undersecretary for Aviation and Airports, Roberto Lim, said that the project is still at its very early stage. Consortium still needs to submit their proposal, which includes the detailed engineering design; It also need to secure an environmental compliance certificate (ECC).

GIC, Macquarie divesting stake in Energy Development Corp.

GIC and Macquarie are looking at selling their stake of roughly 30% in Energy Development Corp. (EDC). The deal could reportedly fetch US$2bn. Recall that GIC and Macquarie invested in EDC in 2017.

Metro Pacific unloads stake in jet fuel terminal

Metro Pacific will divest its 50% stake in Philippine Coastal Storage and Pipeline Corp. (PCSCP) to Coral Terminal, an affiliate of I Squared Capital (ISQ). Singapore-listed Keppel Infrastructure Trust (KIT), which owns the remaining half of PCSPC, will likewise sell its stake to ISQ. The deal values PCSCP at US$510mn EV and US$296mn equity value on a 100% basis. Regis suspects MPI will book a gain from this sale. The transaction will need to secure approval from the Philippine Competition Commission.

Manila Water completes sale of Bulacan assets to San Miguel for P1bn

Manila Water completed the sale of its 90% stakes in Bulakan Water Company, Inc. (BWCI) and Obando Water Company, Inc. (OWCI) to San Miguel (SMC PM). BWCI is a joint venture corporation with Bulakan Water District to operate the concession for the provision of water and sanitation services in the Municipality of Bulakan. OWCI is a joint venture corporation with Obando Water District to operate the concession in the Municipality of Obando, both in the Province of Bulacan. Both transactions amounted to P1.024bn.

MGen’s renewable energy focus could lead to MGreen IPO

MERALCO PowerGen Corp. (MGen) President and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) Emmanuel V. Rubio is bullish on the company’s future, banking on its renewable energy (RE) projects to establish MGen as a powerhouse in the Philippines. MGen is also seeing a growing market appetite for green companies, Mr. Rubio said, noting that its unit, MGen Renewable Energy (MGreen) might potentially list its unit MGreen on the local bourse. An IPO for MGreen could happen sooner than five years. MGreen is the entity the holds the group’s controlling stake in SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM), which is said to be the biggest renewable energy company, with projects consisting of a 3,500-megawatt solar power plant and a 4,000-megawatt-hour energy storage system. It is expected to generate more than five billion kilowatt-hours of electricity per year. The project is expected to complete its first phase by 2026 and its second phase by 2027.

SPNEC defers start of Nueva Ecija solar farm project to 2025-2027

SPNEC announced that it is targeting to start commercial operations of the Phase 1A 50 MW solar farm by 4Q2025. The project has been installed but was not yet operational due to delays in the construction of the connection asset due to right-of-way challenges. It began construction of Phase 1A in December 2021 and originally targeted to complete by mid-2024. Project is part of its 500MW Sta. Rosa Nueva Ecija 2 Solar Power project (NE 2). The first phase will have a capacity of 225 MW. We suspect Phase 1B (175 MW), originally targeted for 4Q2025, could also be delayed. The second phase involves a 275-MW solar power plant.

CREC, Levanta partner for 375 MW of wind investments

Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. (CREC) is forming a joint venture with Levanta Renewables to develop an onshore wind portfolio in the Philippines. Levanta Renewables is backed by Actis, who have acquired a 40% stake in SPNEC’s 3500MW Terra Solar project. The partnership will cover development, financing, construction, and operations and maintenance of CREC’s four onshore wind projects in Luzon and Visayas. Wind projects total 375MW and have secured offtake via the Department of Energy’s Green Energy Auction Program in July 2023. CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 285MW. The company is constructing 1,583 MW of solar projects over the next 12 months. CREC has a project pipeline of 6,716MW (gross) of solar and 813MW (gross) of onshore wind, and 26MW of hydro plants, which will take overall gross installed capacity of 7,570MW by 2028.

AllHome, AllDay Marts appoint new acting presidents

Villar-led companies AllHome Corp. and AllDay Marts, Inc. have elected Frances Rosalie T. Coloma as the new acting president for both companies effective Oct. 21, replacing Benjamarie Therese N. Serrano, who died on Sept. 5, the company said in a regulatory filing on Tuesday. Ms. Coloma also serves as a director in other Villar-led companies, Vista Land & Lifescapes, Inc. and Golden MV Holdings, Inc. She previously served as the president and chief executive officer of AllDay Marts. Consequently, Ms. Coloma resigned as AllHome’s treasurer and was replaced by Louella M. Fernandez.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Budget deficit widens to P273bn in September

The government ran a budget deficit of P273bn in September, 8.9% YoY wider, due to a rebound in expenditures. Revenues grew 17.3% YoY, with tax collections up 8.5% and non-tax revenues doubling. BIR collections rose 15% due to the higher personal income taxes from government workers’ salary hike and higher collections from documentary stamp tax. BOC collections fell 3%, which was attributed to the double-digit negative growth in import duties partly due to tariff reduction of some commodities, as well as smuggling. Non-tax revenues doubled to P49bn due to the upfront fee from the new concessionaire of NAIA. Expenditures rose 13% YoY, as primary spending rose 15% YoY. Interest expenses rose by just 3% YoY. This brings YT-September deficit to P970bn, down by 1.3% YoY. The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

BOP posts US$3.5bn surplus in September

The country’s overall balance of payments posted a surplus of US$3.5bn in September, which was a reversal of the US$414mn deficit in Sep 2023 and higher than the US$88mn surplus recorded in August 2024, primarily due to the US$2.5bn global bond issuance. This brings YT-September BOP surplus to US$5.1bn, higher than the US$1.7bn surplus in the same period last year. The BSP is targeting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn this year, lower than the 2023 BOP surplus of US$3.7bn.

Ilocos Region wage board raises minimum wages by 8%

The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) of the Ilocos Region granted a P33 (~8%) daily minimum wage hike across all sectors. Minimum wages for non-agri sector was raised to P468 daily from P435 previously. For the agri sector, the daily minimum wage was raised to P435 from P402. New wages will take effect on November 7. To date, the government has raised minimum wages in seven (7) regions out of 17 regions; Increases have ranged between 7-8%. This should help boost consumption, particularly for the low-end. Regis’ top picks are: URC, MONDE, CNPF, PGOLD.

Department of Energy preparing to terminate 105 RE projects

The Department of Energy is working on canceling at least 105 renewable energy (RE) projects due to failure to meet project timelines. 88 are either delayed in the predevelopment stage or making no progress. These include 53 solar, 17 hydropower, 10 wind, 5 geothermal, and 3 biomass projects. Most of the contracts were awarded in 2017 and 2019, and delays were caused by failure to secure rights or system impact studies indicating an inability to connect to the grid. At least 1,435 service contracts were awarded as of June, the renewable energy industry is poised to introduce more than 156,700 megawatts (MW) of capacity. About 6,100 MW has been installed. The government hopes to increase the share of RE in the generation mix to 35% by 2030 and 50% by 2040.

NEDA approves P37bn Mindanao transport project

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) has approved the P37bn Mindanao Transport Connectivity Project (MTCIP). Consisting of five major components, the MTCIP involves upgrading the Cagayan de Oro-Davao-General Santos Corridor: Regions 10. 11, and 12. The project will sum up to 277.4km from Cagayan de Oro to Davao and 150.8k from Davao to General Santos. It will also involve the upgrade of three local roads adding up to 129.86km. Expected completion is in 2030. It will be financed through an official development assistance loan from the World Bank.
