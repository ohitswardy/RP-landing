REGIS REPORT

28 June 2024

The PSEi recovered from last week’s steep selloff and reclaimed the 6400 handle, lifted initially by evident bargain hunting after last week’s sharp selloff, then in the latter part of the week, fueled by optimism over the policy setting outlook in 2H after the BSP’s dovish comments in its post-decision statement on Thursday. Stocks gained in all 5 sessions of the week with the index posting a sharp +1.85% gain at the outset as shares quickly bounced from the nearly -3% drop during last Friday’s FTSE rebalancing that brought the index down to its lowest level since mid-November. Shares then posted steady gains in the next 2 sessions, before surging by another +1.23% on Thursday ahead of the BSP policy meeting. It then ticked above the 6400-line during today’s session, albeit closing off the day highs, supported by month/quarter-end buying. The PSEi ultimately capped the week +4.12% WoW at 6411.91, its best closing level in 12 sessions.

On the policy front, the BSP kept the benchmark rate steady at 6.5% for the 6th meeting in a row, as widely expected. Perhaps what was a pleasant surprise however was the BSP’s notable downward revision of its inflation forecasts and the Governor’s affirmation for a potential 25bps rate cut in 3Q (August) and a total of 50bps cut for this year, notwithstanding the Fed’s policy direction. The monetary board lowered their inflation projection this year to 3.3% from 3.5%, and next year to 3.1% from 3.3%. Note that YTD-May average inflation rate stands at 3.5%, and as such, the updated forecasts imply a meaningful slowdown in their inflation view for the latter half of the year. This aligns with Regis’ view that inflation may even hit the 2%-level by August/September, helped not least by an imminent easing in rice prices following the official enactment of the rice tariff cut. Our analysts discuss this in more detail in our latest strategy piece below. Notwithstanding this dovish tone, another thing we want to point out is that the PHP actually strengthened slightly post-BSP meeting, which if sustained will likely further solidify the chances of an August cut.

In corporate developments, we highlight SMPH’s Pasay coastal development project. Regis hosted a call with management this week and our key takeaway is that the project may be even more lucrative than initially estimated. On the cost side, SMPH looks to spend Php40k-Php45k per sqm, lower than our own estimate of Php50k. More importantly however, on the revenue side, while the company has not decided how much of the land to sell, management noted that selling 44 hectares will be sufficient to fully fund the project. As such, based on our calculations this implies a selling price of Php325k-Php370k per sqm, which is much higher than our assumption of Php250k (see our research note below.) Note that SMPH was one of the best performing stocks this week, +10.12% WoW.

Breadth of the market this week was quite positive with half of index members gaining by over +2% WoW. Top gainers include GTCAP +11.91% WoW whose bulk of gains came during today’s quarter-end closing auction, ICT +10.7% and aforementioned SMPH +10.12%. On the losing end, some of the notable laggards include MONDE -6% which now sits at a crucial support level at its 200-day moving average, and BLOOM -5.84% (which did manage to close off the lows). Market activity was better this week at a daily average of $90m, +10% WoW notwithstanding last week’s FTSE rebalancing. Foreign flow ended virtually neutral at under +$150k net inflow, as the today’s $6.7m quarter-end inflow marked the biggest daily net buying since mid-May. Top bought stocks this week were ICT +10.7%, GLO +8.92%, BPI +8.47%, ALI +7.95%, TEL +6.44% and URC +7.44%; while top sold were BDO +0.71%, WLCON +0.56%, SM -0.66%, AEV unch, MEG -3.31%, and JFC +7.11%.

Philippine Strategy / Consumer - Rice is Life (3) - It's official: tariff cut to 15%; can the BSP cut before the Fed?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph | Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

EO 62 signed, lower rice tariff takes effect in early July

On 20 June, President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr signed an Executive Order (EO 62) that, among other things, officially cut the import duty on rice from 35% to 15%. The new rate will take effect 15 days from publication (around 6/7 July) and will stay in place until end-2028, but will be subject to review every 4 months. We earlier reported that the move should quickly result in a c15% reduction in the retail price of the staple. A cut of that magnitude would be a welcome relief, especially for lower-income consumers. It also has the potential to push overall CPI inflation to below 2% by Aug/Sept. This could more than justify a cut in the BSP’s policy rate of 6.5%, and give the market a boost... but will they?

Rice import tariff finally formalized, despite late opposition

The tariff cut was not a foregone conclusion. In early June, the National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) – chaired by the President – announced the reduction in the import duty on rice from 35% to 15%. However, in the intervening two-and-a-half weeks, there was expected protests from agricultural groups. What was unexpected, though, were comments from the Department of Agriculture (DA) with one Undersecretary claiming he had recommended an increase in the duty to 50%, and the Secretary himself wanting a much shorter period of 6-12 months for the cut’s effectivity.

Poorer households to benefit – rice is 18% of their basket

Food and non-alcoholic beverages make up roughly 38% of the headline CPI basket, of which 8.9% is rice. However, the Philippine Statistics Authority estimates that for the bottom 30% of households, food/bev is 55% of their CPI, with rice alone at a staggering 17.9%. Rice is life, indeed. Put another way, rice is about 1/3rd of a low-income family’s monthly food budget. Falling rice prices should free up spending for higher-value protein, or for discretionary food items. We reiterate our Buy calls on Universal Robina (URC PM) and Puregold (PGOLD PM) as the most likely beneficiaries here.

Can / will the BSP cut policy rates ahead of the Fed?

BSP Governor Remolona and members of the Monetary Board have guided that policy rate cuts could come as early as August, possibly ahead of the US Fed. Fundamentally, we agree. Even without a tariff reduction, rice inflation could be near-zero by Aug/Sept, lapping the high base in 2023, and take headline CPI to the bottom of the BSP’s target 2-4% range. A 15% drop in rice prices could push CPI inflation below 2% in 3Q24 (Fig 3). Without an interest rate cut, real interest rates could shoot up to an extremely tight 450bps or higher (Figs 4 and 5).

However, even if the BSP does start to ease monetary policy, it likely cannot cut too much until the Fed does, too. The BSP is surely wary of attacks on the PHP. Year-to-date, the currency has depreciated 5.8% against the USD, half of which came after Gov Remolona’s comments in May. Further weakness in the PHP would feed back into prices of rice, and other imported goods such as other food items, as well as oil, coal, etc. A weak PHP could also turn away foreigner portfolio investors who measure returns in USD.

 
SM Prime - Pasay coastal development could be even more lucrative than estimated
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

More profitable than our projections

We hosted SM Prime management to discuss the Pasay coastal development project in more detail. Although the project already accounts for 25% of our (P40.80/share) SMPH NAV estimate, the company’s assumptions could make the project even more profitable than we thought. If management’s projections hold, then our NAV estimate could rise by 10%-20%.

What are the terms of SM Prime’s Pasay coastal development project?

The 360-hectare Pasay coastal development is a joint project between the Pasay City government and SM Prime. The company is targeting completion of the reclamation by 2028.

SM Prime will shoulder all the costs and will own 49% (176.4 hectares) of the land. The company’s share of the land will all be developable/sellable, while the roads and other public areas will be part of Pasay City’s 51% share.

A community focusing on healthcare and education

The project aims to create a mixed-use community with residential, commercial, and other developments. Management shared that its vision is for the area to be the “Healthcare Capital of Asia” and the “Cradle of Filipino Excellence for Learning”, aspirations that are indicative of what its plans are. Furthermore, the company highlighted some of the planned green features, such as bike lanes and a central park.

How much will it cost and how much might it be worth?

The company’s projections for the coastal development project would make the development more profitable than our own estimates. It expects both lower costs and higher land values than we do. SM Prime will spend P40k/sqm to P45k/sqm, lower than our own estimate of P50k/sqm. This implies a total project cost of P144bn to P162bn, of which P40bn has been spent.

While the company has not decided how much of the land to sell, it believes that selling 44 hectares will be sufficient to fully fund the project. This implies a selling price of P325k/sqm to P370k/sqm, higher than our own assumption of P250k/sqm. SM Prime believes this is feasible since zonal land values in Mall of Asia Complex (MOA) are currently P290k/sqm for residential and P413k/sqm for commercial. Furthermore, some portions of the project will have floor-to-area ratios of up to 14, more than double the FAR of 6 of MOA complex.

Preselling could begin in 2026, after SM Prime receives titles for some parcels.

Minimal risk of more supply in the area

Management believes there is minimal risk of more land supply from other reclamation projects in the area because other proponents either did not get the necessary permits or do not have sufficient funding.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Port authority approves 16% tariff hike for MICT, South Harbor

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) approved the 16% increase in cargo-handling tariff at Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) and Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbor. Implementation will be in two tranches: 10% by Aug 5 and 6% after six months after effectivity of first tranche. Our forecasts assume MICT will raise tariffs by 8% by October 2024 and 8% by June 2025. Recall that MICT petitioned for a 17% tariff hike while ATI petitioned for 16.55%. We estimate MICT accounts for 23% of ICT’s consolidated revenues.

NGCP seeks recovery of P88bn in project costs

The National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) wants to collect P87.7bn in overdue recoveries from two projects completed this year, namely: Full energization of the P20.9bn Mariveles-Hermosa-San Jose 500 kilovolt facility and P67bn Cebu-Negros-Panay (CNP) 230-kilovolt backbone project. The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) only allowed the recovery of less than 1% of both project costs (P201.78mn). NGCP included the unrecovered amounts in its fourth regulatory period (4RP) reset filling, which are still under evaluation. The 4RP and 5RP reset processes cover 2016-2020 and 2021-2025, respectively. Recall that the last transmission reset was for the five-year RP covering 2010-2015.

 

MREIT to acquire mall assets this year

MREIT President and CEO Kevin Tan said that the REIT will acquire mall assets starting this year. Note that currently MREIT’s portfolio is comprised of only offices. MREIT previously said it would acquire 156k sqm of office buildings via a property-for-share swap: McKinley West, Fort Bonifacio (94,300sqm), Iloilo Business Park, Iloilo City (36,200sqm), and Davao Park District, Davao City (26,100sqm). The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 45% to 475,000sqm. Given MREIT’s target portfolio size of 500,000sqm by end-2024, it will likely acquire c. 25,000sqm of malls this year. Thus, malls will comprise c. 5% of floor area by the end of the year. MREIT currently offers 8.0% FY24E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The current 10-year government bond yield is 6.7%.

Andrew Tan steps down as President of Megaworld

Andrew Tan will step down as President of Megaworld but he will remain Chairman of the company. The current COO Lourdes Gutierrez-Alfonso will replace Mr. Tan as President. She has been with the company since 1990.

Emperador sets P6.5bn capex to boost whiskey business

Emperador (EMI PM) said it allocated P6.5bn for its 2024E capex budget, bulk of which will be allotted for the expansion of the Dalmore distillery, which will double capacity and is slated for completion in the 2H24. Recall that in 2023, capex was P7bn. In 1Q24, EMI’s revenues fell 16% YoY as both the Whiskey and Brandy segments saw weaker revenues attributed to downtrading across markets.

Junket operator Dowinn remains closed

Contrary to its previous announcement, junket operator Dowinn did not resume operations on June 24. It had operations in Okada, Solaire Entertainment City, City of Dreams Manila, D’ Heights, and Royce. Bloomberry expects minimal impact from its closure, implying that it is not a large contributor to BLOOM revenue.

Globe nears completion of tower sales to Phil-Tower Consortium

Globe said it is close to finalizing the sale of another 48 towers for P710mn to Phil-Tower Consortium, Inc.. This will bring total sales to Phil-Tower to 1,148 towers, or 85% of Globe’s planned 1,350 towers (aggregate amount of ~P20bn).

Metro Pacific Tollways seeks foreign partner for Cebu tollroad

Metro Pacific Tollways (MPTC) plans to sell a 45% stake in the Cebu-Cordova Link Expressway (CCLEX) to a foreign investor for P7bn. The share sale will consist entirely of primary shares, as the proceeds will be used to fund CCLEX’s expansion, which will involve extending the tollroad to improve connectivity to key city roads. CCLEX is an 8.9-km elevated tollroad that opened in April 2022. Daily traffic volumes are currently at 17k vehicles, significantly lower than its initial traffic projection of 60k.

SMC to build P323bn of new Luzon tollways

SMC to start construction of over P300bn in tollways within the next 12 months. The TRB expects SMC to start the civil works for the Northern Access Link Expressway (NALEX) this year. SMC has laid the groundwork for the Balintawak section of NALEX and can proceed with the toll road construction once the engineering design is approved. NALEX will cost SMC at least P148.3bn and stretches for 136.4 kilometers.

SMC is also building the NMIA (New Manila International Airport) in Bulacan, which is set to be the largest gateway in the Philippines. Furthermore, SMC is tasked to complete the final engineering design for Southern Access Link Expressway (SALEX) within June - SALEX requires P152.39bn and covers 40.65 kilometers. Lastly, SMC is scheduled to push through with the first phase of the SLEX TR5 - initial phase spans almost 60 kilometers and will cost P22.6bn. The entire SLEX T5 will cover 417 kilometers.

GT Capital seeks new businesses

TY Group to embark on a new era of growth over next decade for its listed holding company GT Capital Holdings Inc as it plans expansion of its diversified portfolio through entering new business spaces. GT Capital has set its sights on healthcare, renewable energy, education, and data centers as potential new businesses as it seeks to address increasing demand for healthcare services and the lack of government investments in said sector and boosts country’s power requirements.

GT Capital can provide the structure needed on data centers and potential involvement in actual operations. Its expansion will not only be limited to new sectors but also ramping up value chain of its existing businesses.

PSE approves listing of NexGen Energy IPO shares

PSE has approved NexGen’s planned P580mn IPO - primary offer of 300mn primary common shares, with an over-allotment option of up to 45mn secondary shares at offer price of P1.68/sh. Listing scheduled for July 16, offer period from July 1 to 8. NexGen expects to generate P478.4mn worth of net proceeds at the maximum offer price of P1.68/share. Proceeds to be used to finance existing RE projects. NexGen will be the third IPO this year (following OceanaGold and Citicore).

JFC statement on reported cybersecurity incident

The company has notified the National Privacy Commission on a cybersecurity incident involving a reported personal data breach on its e-commerce platforms. They said that the platforms are unaffected and remain operational. It is addressing the incident and has implemented its response protocols and deployed enhanced security measures to further data against threats. It has also launched an investigation on the matter.

NBI leases 24k sqm of office space from Filinvest Land

The National Bureau of Investigation will lease 23,600sqm of office space in Filinvest Cyberzone Bay City in Pasay, which comprises 4% of FLI’s office space. As such, Filinvest Land’s occupancy rate could rise from c. 60% in 1Q24 to c. 65% in 2Q24.

Ayala Corp to raise P15bn from preferred shares issue

Ayala Corp’s board approved the issuance of preferred shares with a base amount of P10bn and an oversubscription of up to P5bn. The terms of issuance will still be finalized. Recall that the Ayala Group has earmarked P284bn in capital expenditures this year, up 14% YoY, with its Property and energy units getting higher expenditures to support their expansion. The company on the parent level plans to raise up to P29bn to finance maturing obligations this year.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Infrastructure spending jumps 18% in 4M24

Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 18% YoY to P410bn in 4M24. This implies that in April alone, infrastructure spending rose 39% YoY, 27% MoM to P143.4bn, amid the implementation of public works and transport sector projects. The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

LEDAC eyes approval of 28 priority bills by June 2025

National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) said the Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) agreed to prioritize the passage of 28 bills before the end of the 19th Congress in June 2025. 18 bills are considered “top priority”, the key ones among them:

·                     Reforms in Philippine capital markets, which seeks to facilitate capital raising for companies through the stock market and boost the value of pension funds by reducing taxes on stock transactions and equalizing dividend tax

·                     Bill amending the “Right-of-Way” Act to hasten the implementation of infrastructure projects

·                     Rationalized mining fiscal regime, which seeks to impose margin-based royalties and a windfall profit tax on mining companies

·                     CREATE MORE bill, which seeks to further lower taxes on domestic and foreign companies

Traders commit to lowering rice prices starting July

Rice retailers have committed to selling rice at P45 per kilo starting in July after the issuance of the EO 62 on June 20 formally reduced tariffs to 15% from 35% until 2028. The effects of the tariff cuts would be felt as soon as possible, ideally before the third State of the Nation Address happening on July 22. Before EO 62 takes effect on July 6, a farmers’ group plans to file a temporary restraining order before the Supreme Court. They cite that 500,000 farmers would be displaced amid flooding of imported rice. But unless the high court issues a TRO, the DA will abide by the EO 62. Meanwhile, the NFA will continue procuring palay from farmers at higher farmgate prices (P23-P30). P80bn in government revenue losses is projected with the reduced tariff on imported rice.

Department of Agriculture to recommend raising rice tariffs if retail prices drop to P42-45/kg

Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel, Jr. floated the idea of raising the rice import tariff if retail prices go down to as much a P42-P45 per kg in the next four months. Currently, the prices of local well-milled rice range between P48-55 per kg while imported well-milled rice are at P52-55 per kg. EO 62 reduced import tariffs on rice to 15% from 35% by July 6 until 2028, with a provision for a periodic review every four months. Traders recently indicated that the tariff cut could lower retail prices to P45-46/kg by July.

Labor chief says wage hike likely but magnitude still uncertain

Labor Secretary Bienvenido Laguesma said that minimum wage adjustments will likely be based on historical data, but he declined to give an estimate. A formal petition seeking a P500 increase in daily wage was filed before the NCR wage board. Note that the prevailing daily minimum wage in NCR is at P610. The wage board in NCR is expected to be released on or before July 16. Recall that the government implemented a cumulative wage hike of 13% in NCR during 2022 and 2023, during which period cumulative headline inflation rate was 11.8%. Regis expects a more reasonable increase to be the outcome, possibly in the ~5% range.

Indonesia cuts access to gambling websites from the Philippines

Bloomberg reported that Indonesia will cut access to online gambling sites from Cambodia and the Philippines. Note that online gambling is banned in Indonesia. Indonesia and other countries might put pressure on the Philippine government to ban offshore gaming. Note that POGO firms occupy about 6% of Metro Manila office stock. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for: 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 43% of Megaworld, and 28% of Robinsons Land.

Higher airport fees opposed

Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) is proposing to raise charges for NAINA passenger service charges: P200 to P390 for domestic travelers, and P550 to P950 for international guests. Consumer group opposes, says adjusting the PSC may discourage Filipinos and foreigners from traveling to the Philippines. They believe that the New NAIA Infrastructure Corp. (NNIC) must first upgrade airport and its facilities before changing rates.

Kuwait lifts travel, OFW deployment ban

The government of Kuwait has decided to lift the ban on the entry of Filipino travelers and workers to the Arab state after more than one year of diplomatic and labor impasse. Kuwait accounts for 2% of overall remittances; Remittances from the country has been on a declining trend since May 2023.

BSP maintains policy rate; signals rate cut in Aug

The BSP kept its policy rate unchanged at 6.5%. The central bank also downgraded its inflation outlook citing the imminent reduction in rice tariffs and its second-round effects. It is now looking at a baseline inflation rate of 3.3% (from 3.5%) for 2024 and 3.1% (from 3.3%) for 2025, and a risk-adjusted inflation rate of 3.1% for 2024 (vs 3.8% previously) and 3.1% for 2025 (vs. 3.7% previously). Note that YT-May average inflation rate is at 3.5%, with inflation for May alone at 3.9%

BSP Governor Remolona said the they are now “less hawkish” and that the monetary board is “on track” to cut rates when it meets on August 15. The BSP could cut rates by 25bps in 3Q and another 25bps in 4Q. The central bank is “occasionally” intervening in the FX market, as it does not want the PHP to depreciate “too sharply.” Mr. Remolona thinks the peso’s effect on inflation is “not very large”, citing the estimated “pass-through” is only 0.36% per 1% depreciation of the peso.

Budget deficit widens to P175bn in May on accelerated spending

The national government’s budget deficit widened by 43% YoY P174.9bn in May. Revenues grew 15% YoY due to a surge in non-tax revenues. BIR collections rose 2.8% YoY while customs collections grew 4.3% YoY. Non-tax revenues surged 93% YoY due to higher collections from interest on advances from GOCCs, guarantee fees, and the NG share from PAGCOR income. Recall that GOCCs raised its dividend payout to 75% this year from 50%. Disbursements rose 22% YoY to P557bn, of which interest payments rose 48% YoY. Primary spending grew 20% YoY due to implantation of capital outlay projects on public works, defense, social welfare, and health. Note that 2Q23 primary spending was a low base. This brings YT-May budget deficit to P405bn, up 24% YoY.

Palace proposes P6.352 trillion 2025 budget

Marcos administration is proposing another record budget of P6.352tn for 2025, up 10.1% from the 2024 budget of P5.768tn. 2025 proposed budget is equivalent to 22% of GDP. Budget will largely focus on social sector (education, health, social protection) and agriculture. This also builds in a higher budget allocation for LGUs, which will be higher by 18.7% YoY to P1.03tn due to increased tax collections in 2022. There will also be an expansion in the conditional cash transfer program of the government, as well as the higher allowance of P10,000 for teachers.

The economic team is set to present the NEP to the Cabinet on July 2 and submit it to Congress on July 29 (a week after SONA). It was emphasized that the budget will build on the gains from the first two years of the Marcos administration. The team is less optimistic on the inflation trend (hiking assumption from 3% to 4% from 2-4% in April).

Agri secretary warns of rice shortage if Supreme Court issues TRO on EO 62

Agri Secretary Tiu Laurel warned of a rice shortage if Supreme Court issues a 60-day temporary restraining order against EO 62. Said if TRO is issues, no importers will import and instead will only use remaining stocks. Hence, the buffer stock of rice will be sold out by the time TRO is lifted. This early, traders are panicking to unload their rice stocks amid the expected lower price of imported grains once the 15 percent tariff becomes effective. If TRO is granted, they will start hoarding their stocks, hurting consumers. The Agri Secretary thinks one year is enough with evaluation every 4 months.
