REGIS REPORT

30 August 2024

The PSEi ended a 3 consecutive weekly winning streak as shares submitted to some selling pressure after testing but failing convincingly to break past the crucial 7000-line through the earlier part of the week. Continuing the trend from late last week, the benchmark again touched above 7000 intraday during Tuesday and Wednesday’s sessions, but in both instances closed a few points below the line. As such, we have yet to see the PSEi settle a session past 7000 since January 2023. The benchmark ultimately capped the week at 6897.54, -0.93% WoW, ending a 3-week winning streak that saw the index gain by more than +5% and bring the YTD gain to about +7%.

Market activity during the shortened trading week was even higher at a daily average of $143m, +16% WoW and about +40% more than the YTD average. This was primarily boosted by an outsize $237m session during Friday’s MSCI rebalancing, notwithstanding the minimal changes for the index review. As previously announced, there were no new additions or deletions in the standard cap index, while for the small cap PLUS +7.39% WoW was included and SECB removed -0.99%. Friday’s turnover was the highest since the end-May MSCI rebalancing. With the rebalancing, foreign participation for the broader week spiked to 56% from to last week’s 45%.

Net foreign flow was again mostly skewed to buy during the week, netting an inflow of nearly $40m (vs last week’s $112m). Note however that the inflow streak was ended at 14 days as foreign investors turned better sellers on the day of the rebalancing, albeit moderate at $5.5m. Top bought stocks for the week were ALI +7.79% WoW, the best performing stock in the index this week, BDO -0.78%, BPI -0.08%, JFC +2.28%, SMPH +4.21%, MBT -1.74%, and PLUS; while top sold were SM -5.35%, SECB -0.99%, which marked its lowest close since 2011, URC -5.45% and BLOOM +2.21%.

Except for the inflow driven property sector (ALI +7.79% WoW and SMPH +4.21%) and the recovery in ACEN +4.62% (after last week's -10.5% slump), interesting to note that other notable gainers in the benchmark this week were the relatively smaller/mid cap stocks such as NIKL +5.97%, DMC +5.41%, WLCON +4.56% and SCC +3.67%. This comes in contrast with the pullback in larger cap plays like ICT -5.03% and SM -5.35%.

On the ground, it was a fairly quiet week in terms of news following the past few weeks heightened focus on easing interest rates. The PHP however did continue to strengthen and is now at its strongest vs the USD since March at 56.138, which at least supports the case for less inflationary pressure and potentially more policy easing moving forward. For the week ahead, investors will be turning in to the August CPI reading due on Thursday morning, expected to show inflation easing to 3.6% vs 4.4% in July.

Six firms eye Meralco's 400-MW contract

Six companies participated in the pre-bid conference for the 400MW mid-merit power supply contract: First Gas Power Corp. (subsidiary of First Gen Corp.), First Natgas Power Corp. (subsidiary of First Gen Corp.), Masinloc Power Co. Ltd. (the power arm of San Miguel Corp.), Sual Power, Inc. (the power arm of San Miguel Corp.), GNPower Dinginin Ltd. Co. (privates limited partnership that includes AC Energy & Infrastructure Corp., and Power Partners Ltd. Co.), and FDC Misamis Power Corp. (subsidiary of FDC Utilities, Inc.). Meralco signs agreement with Vena Energy

Meralco’s wholly owned subsidiary, MGen Renewable Energy, signed an agreement with Singapre-based Vena Energy for the development of the 450MW alternating current (MWac) Bugallon solar power project in Pangasinan. The project will start construction in 3Q24 and will begin commercial operations in 4Q25. Based on a recent filing with the DENR’s Environmental Management Bureau, the project will have a capacity of 650.1 MWpeak or 530.4 MWac and will cost P31.9bn. The major components of the project are solar PV panels, battery energy storage system, HV transformer, buildings, and other structures.

ACEN, Indonesian firm partner to develop wind renewable energy projects across Indonesia

ACEN Corp. is tying up with Jakarta-based PT Barito Renewables Energy to develop wind energy projects in the provinces of South Sulawesi, Sukabumi, and Lombok. Details about the partnership were not revealed. However, recall that ACEN acquired three late-stage wind development assets with a combined potential capacity of 320MW in the same provinces seven months ago. ACEN currently has a portfolio of 99MW of geothermal assets (operating and under construction) in Indonesia. In aggregate, ACEN’s current total attributable RE capacity stands at 4.8GW across the Philippines, Australia, Vietnam, Indonesia, and India. ACEN has a target of quadrupling its renewable energy capacity to 20 gigawatts (GW) by 2030.

San Miguel, Aboitiz submit lowest bids for Meralco's 600MW CSP

San Miguel (SMC PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM) submitted the lowest bids for Meralco’s (MER PM) competitive selection process (CSP) of 600MW baseload power supply agreement (PSA). SMC’s Masinloc Power Co. Ltd. submitted the lowest bid of P5.6015/kWh for the 500 MW PSA. AP’s GNPower Dinginin Power Ltd. Co. submitted the lowest bid of P5.7392/kwh for the 100 MW PSA. Other bidders were: SMC’s Mariveles Power Generation Corporation – P6.4017/kwh, EGCO group’s Quezon Power – P6.5487/kwh, Semirara’s (SCC PM) Southwest Luzon Power Generation Corp. – P7.7303/kwh, and AP’s Therma Luzon Inc.- P8.3388/kwh. Winning offers had been lower than the reserve price set by Meralco –with a headline rate of P6.9537/kwh; and a levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) at P7.2609/kwh. The PSAs to be sealed for the winning offers will have a term of 15 years – with a delivery period commencing on August 26, 2025, or upon the approval of the contract by the Energy Regulatory Commission. The next CSP round will be for MER’s 400MW mid-merit capacity requirement, with bid submissions targeted by October 1.

San Miguel Global Power Holdings Corp eyes US$300mn offshore bond issuance to finance solar energy projects

San Miguel Global Power Holdings Corp.’s board of directors approved the issuance of US$300mn worth of senior perpetual capital securities, which will be listed on the Singapore Exchange, SGX. Proceeds will be used to finance solar energy projects. The firm has yet to disclose the timeline for the planned offer.

Converge is opening two data centers by 2025 with a combined capacity of 13 MW

In a partnership with US-based Super Micro Computer, Inc., Converge’s two new data centres, Pampanga and Caloocan, will have a capacity of 10 MV with 1200 racks and 3 MW with 290 racks respectively. These two data centres are developed to be energy efficient to help reduce costs. The data centres are expected to cost up to P5 billion and take three years to finish construction.

Hann Resorts delays IPO to 2025, hoping for a better macroeconomic condition

Hann Resorts is pushing back its listing to mid-2025 in hopes of better macroeconomic conditions worldwide when the FED cuts interest rates, which should help increase foreign demand. Hann Resorts operates casinos located in Clark Freeport Zone in Pampanga. While data from PAGCOR showed that GGR from All Licensed Casino has dropped by 6% in the 1H24 compared to 1H23, GGR from Licensed Casinos in Clark has rose by 12% in the 1H24 compared to 1H23.

"PSE is developing the derivatives market for FY26 launch" according to InsiderPH

PSE and Taiwan Stock Exchange have signed a memorandum of understanding to develop a derivatives trading market. The agreement will allow the two parties to create a framework for how to proceed not only with the development of the actual infrastructure of the derivatives market but also with the regulations and best practices around its operation and governance.

Congressman says Lower House will not adopt Senate version of CREATE MORE bill

According to Ways and Means Committee Chairman and Albay Rep. Jose Maria Clemente “Joey” S. Salceda, the Lower House would unlikely adopt the Senate version of the CREATE MORE bill, given the following:

(1) Senate version does not resolve issues on cross-border doctrine, as it deleted the House’s definition of separate Customs territories, repealing them from the special ecozone laws that established them

(2) It still allows tariff- and VAT-free importation of petroleum for international carriers, allowing such products to not be fuel-marked, which could result in confusion in law enforcement

(3) Senate version allows local governments to apply their respective registered business tax rates, in case these are lower than the proposed 2% under the House version

These items will be discussed at the Bicameral Conference Committee. The Senate is expected to approve the CREATE MORE bill on second and third reading next week. The CREATE MORE bill is among the priority legislative measures identified by the Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council for passage before the 19th Congress ends in June 2025. Recall that among the more important provisions of the CREATE More bill are the following:

(1) a reduction in powers of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board

(2) allowing the IT and BPO sectors to enjoy incentives while implementing full work-from-home

(3) a streamlined tax refund system

If passed, the bill could have important implications on many sectors, including the BPO industry (and thus the property sector).

Budget deficit narrows to P29bn in July; Primary surplus up >3x to P51bn

The national government recorded a budget deficit of P28.8bn in July, lower by 40% YoY. Revenues grew by 11% YoY to P457bn, buoyed by a 16% YoY jump in tax revenues to P403bn. Disbursements/expenses rose 6% YoY to P486bn, following a 25% YoY jump in interest expenses to P79bn. Primary spending (i.e. excluding interest expenses) rose by 3% YoY in July, resulting in a primary surplus of P51bn, up 222% YoY. This brings YT-July budget deficit to P643bn, higher by 7% YoY. The government appears to be broadly on track to hit its fiscal deficit target of P1.48tn by year-end, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

Government raising US$2.5bn in three-tranche bond offering

The government returned to the international bond market for the second time this year via a three-tranche sustainable bond offering: US$500mn 5.5-year bond offer was priced at +75bps over US treasuries or a coupon of 4.375% (vs. initial guidance of T+110bps). US$1.1bn 10.5-year bond offer was priced at +95bps over US treasuries or a coupon of 4.75% (vs. initial guidance of T+125bps). US$900mn 25-year bond offer was priced at +103.4bps over US treasuries or a coupon of 5.175%(vs. initial guidance of 5.5%).

Proceeds will be used for general purposes including budgetary support, of which US$900mn 25-year bond will be used to finance/refinance assets in line with the Republic’s sustainable finance framework. The government plans to borrow US$5bn this year, of which US$2bn was raised from the issuance of global bonds last May. The National Government’s (NG) outstanding debt stood at P15.48tn as of end-June, equivalent to 62.4% of GDP. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of ~60% by year-end; It aims to lower the ratio further to 51% by 2028.

Rice importers blame Vietnam exporters for elevated rice prices

A group called Philippine Rice Industry Stakeholders Movement (PRISM) said that contracts signed by local importers are being canceled by Vietnamese suppliers, who are seeking higher prices. They cited that Vietnamese rice export prices have steadily increased in recent months due to higher demand in neighboring countries like Philippines and Indonesia. PRISM co-founder Orly Manuntag claims that rice is now being priced at US$680 per MT from US$600 per MT previously. PRISM blames the slowdown in imports due to contract cancellations of Vietnamese exporters. As a results, rice importers are having a hard time bringing down local rice prices to its estimate of P42-46/kg, following the reduction on rice tariffs to 15% from 30% previously last July 5.

Zero tariffs drive demand for EVs, but sales are expected to be less than 10% of total vehicle sales this year

Auto industry group, CAMPI, said the expansion of Executive Order (EO) No. 12 to include hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) and plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) in the tariff exemption will drive demand for EVs. As of 1H24, sales of HEVs and PHEVs accounted for 4% of total new car sales. However, CAMPI said that sales of EVs is unlikely to reach 10% of new car sales this year, as demand for ICEs is also still increasing.
