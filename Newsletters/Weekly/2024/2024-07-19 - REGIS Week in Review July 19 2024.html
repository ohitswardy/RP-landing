REGIS REPORT

19 July 2024

Philippine shares extended their uptrend this week, sending the benchmark higher for the 4th week in a row to its best close since early April, driven primarily by a surge in net foreign buying as rate cuts are now firmly back into play. The local benchmark initially drifted sideways below 6700 through majority of the week, before breaking into 6800 during Friday’s session. It ultimately settled the week just a few points below the crucial line, which had been the key support level through most of February-April, and closed at 6791 +2.16% WoW. Notably, the local market outperformed a mostly lower region, and broader risk assets overall, whose weakness in the latter part of the week appeared to be triggered by the initial knee-jerk selloff in mega-cap tech stocks after news that the US may impose tighter restrictions on chip sales to China.

Note that in his latest Greed & Fear piece, Chris Wood highlighted the Philippines (along with India) as among the best ways to play a potential Fed easing, citing our market’s low valuation vs historical and a still robust earnings growth. Furthermore, the 2Q reporting season on the ground was kicked off on a positive note by BPI +4.16% WoW, which reported +22% YoY growth in its 1H2024, tracking ahead of Rafa’s forecast (51% of Regis FY2024E) and of consensus. In 2Q alone, the lender reported +18% YoY growth in earnings. More details in our research clip below. With that, the broader banking sector was a bright spot with BDO +3.55% and MBT +3.09%.

Best gainer in the PSEi this week was ALI +7.09% WoW, which in our sense tends to be the usual rate cut play in our market, particularly for foreign investors. The strength was spread out across the broader property sector with RLC +7.19% and SMPH +3.24%. REITs also continued to rise with RCR +4.01%, which marked a new 52-week high during the week, and FILRT +3.06%. Another notable gainer was ACEN +6.49% WoW, bouncing from its weakness in the prior weeks. Note that the NGCP declared a power alert during the week, which marked the first of such alerts in over a month. Our sense is that the company may report even better results in 2Q given its net seller exposure to WESM and due to its new capacities. As with the past weeks, consumer space continued to be a bright spot, with URC +4.07% and PGOLD +3.56%, which are 2 of Regis’s top picks. Our sense is that lower inflation in 2H will result to improved consumer demand, and these 2 names are perhaps the most attractive plays in the sector from a valuation point of view.

On the opposite end, top laggard was BLOOM -5.81% WoW which settled the week at a new 52-week low and is now -10.98% YTD (vs PSEi’s +5.3%). Note that there were many casino-related headlines that came out during the week, particularly the latest data from PAGCOR showing that GGR of licensed casinos was flat QoQ (-4% YoY) in 2Q. Okada Manila’s GGR in the same period was likewise virtually flat, which in our sense suggests a potentially soft number as well for BLOOM. Furthermore, there were also a number of headlines about new casinos looking to start operations over the next 2 years, which would present more competition in the sector. In contrast, PAGCOR data also showed that GGR of bingo and electronic games surged by 31% QoQ. With that, PLUS, which in our sense has about a 50% share of the e-games market, jumped +5.47% WoW and closed at a fresh record high (now +97.5% YTD). Another weak spot this week was the telco space with TEL -4.86%, CNVRG -2.1% and GLO -1.79%, trailing behind the broader market this time after their outperformance over the past few weeks, as investors favor other sectors instead with imminent rate cuts now being the overarching theme.  

Market activity this week was moderate at a daily average of $90.1m, -12% WoW. After seeing pockets of inflows in the prior weeks, foreign buying this week was more consistent and significant this time around at a net figure of $48m. Note that buying in Friday’s session hit nearly $25m, which is the largest daily inflow since the OGP IPO in mid-May. Top foreign bought stocks this week were ALI +7.09% WoW, SM +3.52%, BPI +4.16%, BDO +3.55%, URC +4.07%, MBT +3.09%, PLUS +5.47%, SMPH +3.24%, ICT +0.49%, MER +1.04%, RLC +7.19% and AC +2.23%; while top sold were JFC +0.88%, BLOOM -5.81%, TEL -4.86%, MEG +2.17%, MONDE +0.21%, AEV +0.69% and DMC -0.54%.

Bank of the Philippine Islands - 1H24 beats again; consumer loans push up NIMs, but also NPLs
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1H24 NPAT up 22% YoY, ahead of Regis, Street

In a press release, BPI disclosed 1H24 NPAT of P30.6bn, up 22% YoY, tracking ahead of Regis forecasts (51% of FY24E) and of consensus. Earnings in 2Q24 alone were up 18% YoY and flat QoQ. Loan growth of 18% YoY was driven by surging personal, SME, and microfinance loans, helping NIMs expand to 4.26%, but also saw NPLs climb to 2.2%. Bottomline was boosted by slower opex growth compared to our forecast, and by relatively low credit costs as the bank reduces over-provisioning. BPI will host an earnings call on 26 July.

Loans up 18% YoY, NIMs boosted by surge in consumer loans

The bank’s loans expanded 18% YoY (including the Robinsons Bank portfolio), with growth seen across all major segments. BPI noted much faster growth in higher-yielding personal loans (+129%), “business banking” (+88%), and microfinance (+67%). This pushed NIMs up to 4.26%, by the bank’s estimate, compared to 4.21% for 1Q24 alone. Meanwhile, deposits grew 14% YoY, with CASA ratio stabilizing at the 65% level. Competition for TDs seems to be easing, helping support margins.

Fees jump 30% YoY in 2Q; Opex growth still under control

Non-interest income grew 29% YoY in 2Q24, with trading/forex gains up 24%. Fee incomes jumped 30% YoY, driven by transaction fees, bancassurance, and credit card fees. Meanwhile, opex growth remained under control, up 22% in 1H24 (+24% YoY in 2Q alone), despite IT and merger costs, resulting in PPOP growth of 25%.

NPLs continued to rise due to loan mix; still running down NPL cover

The increase in personal/SME/micro loans also pushed NPL ratio up to 2.20% from 2.12% in 1Q24. Management, though, considers this acceptable given the much higher yields on those portfolios. BPI kept credit cost relatively low at c30bps, consistent with management’s guidance to bring NPL cover to more normal levels. Coverage stood at 128% in 2Q, from 136% in 1Q, and down from its peak of 180% in 4Q22.

 
Philippine Property - Residential challenges in Metro Manila
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Turning more cautious on residential segment

We turn incrementally more cautious on the Metro Manila mid-income (primary) residential segment given: 1) unsold condominium inventory is high at about three years of presales, potentially leading to balance sheet issues (if the units remain unsold); and 2) prices of preselling condominiums are up to 80% more expensive than units available in the secondary market, which may dissuade buyers from the primary market (crimping presales). As a result, we widen our target discounts to NAV by 10ppt across the board to account for higher risks in the sector.

However, we refrain from being overly bearish since cancellation rates have been moderating, high-end and provincial sales have generally been robust, and valuations of most developers are compelling.

More mall assets to be spun off into REITs

Now that mall profitability is above pre-COVID levels, developers have begun to spin off their malls into REITs and the anticipated decline in interest rates should accelerate this process, potentially leading to NAV increases of up to 20%.

Office negative rental reversions to peak

While we expect negative office rental reversions to peak this year, we forecast Metro Manila vacancy to drop beginning next year, as new supply will only add 4% to office stock in 2025E-2026E RLC is fundamentally most compelling; prefer SMPH among the large caps RLC is our top pick. We forecast it to show the fastest earnings growth in the sector (relative to 2019). Moreover, we expect asset injections into RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) to enhance NAV and further highlight how undervalued RLC is (66% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY25E P/B, and 5.3X FY25E P/E).

Among the large caps, we prefer SMPH, which we expect to continue hitting record level profits. A mall REIT IPO could be a catalyst for the stock and the sale of lots in its Pasay coastal development could raise NAV by 10%-20%. Based on our estimates, the current SMPH share price does not place any value on the company’s coastal development project, which represents 27% of NAV.

We lower target prices by 7%-25%

Despite rolling over to 2025E, our wider target discounts to NAV resulted in 7%-25% drops in our target prices. However, we maintain our Buy ratings on ALI, MEG, RLC, and SMPH, due to valuations. We also maintain our Hold rating on VLL and downgrade FLI to Hold, in light of deteriorating credit ratios.

 
Wilcon Depot - Recovery on the horizon; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Maintaining buy on revised TP of P22/sh (-35%)

A perfect storm of weak demand, heightened competition, and expansion-related costs battered WLCON over the past few quarters, leading us to cut our 2024-25E EPS estimates by 28-30% and our TP by 35%. We believe, however, that the worst is behind us as pressure on its top line and its margins should subside as inflation-related pressures ease and store expansion moderates. WLCON’s scale, first[1]mover in key locations, and portfolio of in-house brands are key advantages that will be difficult to overcome and should, in our view, allow it to maintain market leadership longer-term. The stock has underperformed the PCOMP by ~25% over the past 12 months, pushing valuations to near all-time lows. We believe the risk-reward trade-off at current valuations (18x PE / 8x EV/EBITDA) is attractive given the longer-term growth runway. We are forecasting +11% EPS CAGR for 2024-26E on continued store expansion, SSSG recovery and margin improvement beyond 2024E. Our TP translates to a PER of 21x 2025E, and with our projected dividend, offers a total return of 24%. Maintain Buy.

Worst is behind us

Weak consumer sentiment amidst the inflationary environment and rising competition have weighed on WLCON’s top line for the few quarters. We believe, however, that the worst is likely over as inflationary pressures to consumers should ease over the next 6-12 months. This could, in our view, spur discretionary spending (e.g. home improvement) beyond 2024. While competition is stepping up, we believe WLCON has key advantages relating to its scale, first-mover in key locations, and portfolio of in-house brands.

Top line recovery seen in 2025E

We expect earnings growth accelerating to 14% in 2025E as SSSG recovers (+3% in 2025E from -1% in 2024E). We likewise expect the impact of expansion[1]related costs to subside as the rate of store expansion normalizes (+6% 2024-26E CAGR) and new stores built over the last 12-24 months mature ramp up. We expect operating margins to settle at 15-16%, below the 2022 peak of ~17%, but still above the pre-pandemic average of ~10%.

Adjusting estimates and cutting TP by 35% but maintaining Buy

We are cutting our EPS estimates by 28-30% to account for slower SSSG and lower operating margins. Consequently, our DCF-based TP is reduced to P22/sh (-35%) but maintain our Buy rating on the stock given its relative underperformance (-25% vs PCOMP). Our TP implies 2025E valuations of 21x PE and 10x EV/EBITDA, broadly in line with regional peers. Key downside risks relate to competition, consumer sentiment, and forex risk.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco rates up by P2 per kWh in July as power costs 'normalize'

Meralco’s overall rate will increase by P2.1496 per kilowatt-hour (kWh) to P11.6012 per kWh in July (from P9.4516 per kWh in June), with management citing “normalizing power costs following artificially low rates last month.” The release of Meralco bills will again be delayed as the utility firm “sought guidance from the ERC on the implementation of this month’s rate adjustment.” Note that electricity has a weight of 4.6% in the CPI basket.

SM's geothermal unit starts exploration for new sources

The Philippine Geothermal Production Co., Inc. (PGPC) said it has started the exploration and development of new geothermal energy sources in various parts of Luzon. It aims to establish fully operational geothermal fields capable of generating between 250-400 MW of additional renewable baseload capacity. The projects are slated for completion within the next five to seven years.

PGPC currently operates the Tiwi field in Albay and the Makiling-Banahaw (Mak-Ban) steam field spanning Laguna and Batangas. PGPC is a wholly-owned unit of SM Investments (SM PM) and accounts for 5% of its market-based NAV of P945/sh.

Cebu Air initiates process for equity restructuring

Cebu Air’s Board of Directors approved a restructuring plan to eliminate the company’s retained earnings deficit, which stood at P16.3bn in end-2023. This will be offset from APIC of P20.7bn. The equity restructuring plan will go through SEC approval and once approved, we reckon CEB should be able to declare dividends by 1Q25. Convertible pref shares (CEBCP PM) accrue an annual dividend of P2.3/sh and accumulated unpaid dividends have reached P6.4/sh as of end-2023.

Solaire Resort North sees strong foot traffic

Joy Wassmer, Director of Public Relations of Bloomberry, said that Solaire Resort North (SRN) is seeing strong foot traffic. For now, the visitors mostly enjoy the restaurants but SRN hopes to see more activity in the gaming area. The current foot traffic mainly consists of Quezon City residents. SRN would also like to target residents of northern provinces such as Pampanga and Baguio. SRN is located in Quezon City and is the only integrated casino resort serving northern Metro Manila. There are currently 200 rooms and this will be increased to 526.

Okada Manila 2Q24 GGR flat QoQ, down 22% YoY

Okada Manila’s 2Q24 hit P8.8bn, flat QoQ but down 22% YoY. Volume and GGR of every category fell on a YoY basis. The slight QoQ increase in mass table and slot machine volumes suggest that the opening of Solaire Resorts North has not caused any weakness for Okada Manila. PAGCOR reported that the GGR of licensed casinos (including integrated resorts) was flat QoQ (down 4% YoY).

Villar Group to open casino in 2025

Manuel Villar Jr. said his group is on track to open an integrated casino resort as early as mid-2025. The planned investment is approximately US$1bn. It will open on the redeveloped 18,000sqm Vista Mall Global South along C5 extension in Las Pinas. Mr. Villar expects it to perform well since it is closer to NAIA Terminal 1 than the existing integrated casino resorts. The number of hotel rooms and gaming tables has not been decided.

Belle Corp applies for license to operate casino in Clark

BusinessWorld reported that SM Investments’ subsidiary, Belle Corp (BEL PM), applied for a license to build and operate a casino in Clark. Belle will invest at least US$300mn for the integrated casino resort, which will include a convention center and a hotel.

Hann Resorts reportedly looking at US$200mn IPO this next year

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that Hann Resorts, which operates and integrated casino resort in Clark, is looking to raise P15bn to P20bn in an IPO next year. Hann has 150 gaming tables, 870 slot machines, and two five-star hotels. This compares to Solaire Entertainment City’s 350 gaming tables, and 2,700 slot machines. The company will use the proceeds to fund expansion plans, including three championship golf courses and five-star hotels.

In 1Q24, GGR of licensed casinos in Clark stood at P7.8bn, while that of licensed casinos in Metro Manila amounted to P39.1bn. Bloomberry’s 1Q24 GGR stood at P14.8bn.

Maynilad plans IPO in 2025

Maynilad plans to conduct an IPO as soon as 2025, according to CEO Ramoncito Fernandez. The company recently issued P15bn worth of blue “sustainability” bonds due in 2029 and 2034 with interest rates of 6.7092% and 7.0931%, respectively, which will help finance its capex of P30bn this year. As of end-2023, Maynilad’s net debt stood at P57bn, translating to a net debt-to-equity of 83% and net-debt-to-trailing EBITDA of 3.46x.

Under its water franchise, Maynilad is required to issue at least 20% of its shares to the public on or before January 2027. Maynilad is 53%-owned by Metro Pacific, 25% by DMCI (DMC PM), and 20% by Japan-based Marubeni. Maynilad accounts for 24% of DMCI’s market-based NAV of P13.20/sh.

SM issues US$500mn 5-year note

SM Investments raised US$500mn from a 5-year note, the first tranche of its planned US$3bn multi-issuer Euro Medium-Term Notes (EMTN) program. The issuance was 3.2x oversubscribed with final demand reaching US$1.6bn, which marked SM’ largest offshore bond issuance since 2014. The notes were priced at a yield of 5.466% (135 bps above US treasury benchmark). Note that as of end-March 2024, SM’s parent level net debt-to-equity stood at 35% and its average cost of debt stood at 4.99% (peso-foreign currency mix at 62:38).

The 5-year Notes were distributed to high-quality global accounts: 87% to Asia, 13% to Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA); 83% to Fund Managers / Asset Managers, 11% to Banks / Financial Institutions, and 6% to Private Banks / Others. Net proceeds from the EMTN issue will be allocated for general corporate purposes.

SMC, ACEN units best bids for Meralco RE supply deal

San Miguel’s (SMC PM) San Roque Hydropower Inc. (SRHI) offered the lowest total headline rate of P7.10 per kilowatt-hour (kWH) for 340 megawatts (MW) of the total requirement. While two units of ACEN secured the lowest bid for the remaining 159MW requirement: Gigasol3 submitted a bid price of P8.1819 per kWh for 139 MW of the requirement, while Santa Cruz Solar Energy Inc. (SCSEI) took the remaining 21 MW requirement at a rate of P8.1998 per kWh.

The competitive selection process (CSP) involves a 10-year PSA for Meralco’s 350 MW mid-merit requirement starting February 2025 which will increase by 150 MW in February 2026. To date, Meralco has contracted 1,880 MW of RE capacity, surpassing its initial target of 1,500 MW. RE is expected to account for 22% of the company’s supply portfolio over the next 6 years.

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) declares 100% stock dividend

SEVN declared a 100% stock dividend, equivalent to 756.4m common shares; record date on August 2, payment date August 15.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in June

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 42bps MoM to 20.1% (in June) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index declined 22bps MoM to 24.7%. Net foreign selling slowed to US$104mn, from US$174mn (net selling) in May. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 8 saw increases in foreign ownership while 22 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

AEV – foreign ownership fell 2.36 percentage points MoM to 3.2%. It was removed from the MSCI Index at the end of May, but it appears the trades related to this were only reflected in June. AEV saw net foreign selling of US$71mn in May (the highest for the month) and US$20mn in June (the second highest for the month).

WLCON – we estimate that foreign ownership declined 70bps MoM to 16.0%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$10.4mn (the fifth highest for the month).

ACEN – foreign ownership increased 24bps MoM to 25.2%. Net foreign buying reached US$5mn, the fourth highest for the month.

Remittances rise 3.6% YoY in May

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.6% YoY to US$2.58bn in May. In PHP terms, remittances expanded by 7.4% YoY. This was driven by higher remittances from Singapore (+5%), USA (+5%), Saudi Arabia (+4%), and UAE (+5%). This brings 5M24 remittances up 3% YoY to US$13.37bn. Note that the BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 3% YoY this year (suggests FY24E: US$34.5bn).

Commercial banks' loan growth accelerates to 10.1% YoY in May; M3 growth accelerates to 6.5%

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 10.1% YoY in May, accelerating from 9.6% YoY in April. Loans for production activities grew by 8.4% in May from 7.8% in April due to faster growth in loans for manufacturing (+10%), retail (+11%), transport (27%), and real estate (+13%). Meanwhile consumer loan grew at a steady pace of 25.6% YoY, with credit card loans up 29% YoY and auto loans up 21% YoY. Domestic liquidity (M3) growth accelerated to 6.5% YoY in May from 5.6% YoY in April and was the fastest growth rate since November 2023’s 7.0%.

New vehicle sales rise 7.6% YoY in June; Toyota sales up 10.5% YoY

CAMPI reported industry-wide car sales rose 7.6% YoY to 39,088 units in June, mildly accelerating from May’s 5.5% YoY pace. On a MoM basis, industry car sales were down 2.9%. This brings 1H24 industry car sales to 226,279 units, up 11.8% YoY.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) cornered 46.1% of the market, selling 104,350 units in 1H24, up 11.5% YoY. For the month of June alone, TMP sold 18,093 units, up 10.5% YoY but down 3.1% MoM. To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.4k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 17k units for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).

GGR of electronic games grows 230% YoY

PAGCOR reported that gross gaming revenue (GGR) in the Philippines grew 9% QoQ (+33% YoY) to P89.4bn. GGR of bingo and electronic games grew 31% QoQ and 231% YoY to P35.7bn, while GGR of integrated resorts was flat QoQ (-4% YoY) at P49.5bn and GGR of PAGCOR operated casinos fell 11% QoQ and 15% YoY to P4.2bn.

Pulse Asia survey shows 76% of Filipinos disapprove of government's performance on inflation

76% of Filipinos (from 71% in 1Q24 survey) disapproved of how the government handled inflation. Only 5% of respondents gave their approval, while 19% were undecided. The same poll revealed that the government’s attempts at reducing poverty had the second-highest disapproval percentage (51%, from 1Q24’s 42%). Inflation levels are the only concern that majority (57%) of Filipinos want to hear from the President during his SONA on July 22. Inflation levels are also the issue that 72% of Filipinos find most urgent. Other issues are territorial defense (5%) and charter change (1%).

Marcos Jr. approval rating down 2ppt, Sara Duterte up 2ppt

In June, the approval and trust ratings of President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. declined by 2ppts and 5 ppts, respectively. This came from an approval and trust rating of 55% and 57%, respectively, in March 2024. The biggest drop in approval rating was from Luzon at 9ppts, while he received an increase of 14ppts from the National Capital Region. The approval rating also dropped by 13ppts among Class ABC.

Meanwhile, the approval rating of Vice President Sara Duterte went up by 2ppts to 69% in June. This came from an approval rating of 67% in March 2024. Duterte’s approval rating went up by 12ppts and 1ppt in NCR and Visayas, respectively. The approval rating increased by 17ppts in Class E. The survey was conducted from June 17 to 24, 2024 among 2,400 respondents, and has a margin of error of ± 2%.

NGCP declares yellow alert over Luzon grid

The National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) declared a yellow alert over the Luzon grid yesterday (July 17), the first such declaration in over a month. This was after the San Gabriel gas-fired power plant in Batangas which has a capacity of 417.4 megawatts (MW) tripped. Available capacity was 13,198 MW while peak demand was estimated at 12,028 MW. A total of 1,652.7 MW was unavailable to the grid, including the output of 15 power plants on forced outage and 6 running below their rated capacity.

Recall that the Luzon grid was last placed on yellow alert on June 5 while red alert on June 2. To date, the grid was under red and yellow alerts for 11 and 32 days, respectively. Manila Electric Co. (Meralco) said it has advised the participants of its Interruptible Load Program to be on standby “in case the situation escalates to red alert.” Meralco and Department of Energy also urge the public to implement energy conservation and efficiency practices to help manage overall demand. Energy Undersecretary Rowena Cristina L. Guevara said that the department expects the power situation to improve with the addition of new capacity. Note that the Luzon grid’s LWAP stood at P8.15/kwh on July 16, up from the July1-7 weekly average of P6.35/kwh.
