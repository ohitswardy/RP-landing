REGIS REPORT

16 August 2024

The PSEi rallied past the crucial 6800 line with bulk of the gains coming through the final day of the week as investors rejoiced the BSP’s decision to cut rates following some mixed signals from BSP Governor Remolona leading into the event. The benchmark fluctuated within the 6600-6700 range through majority of the week, before surging +2.31% during Friday’s session, to ultimately cap the week at 6847 +3% WoW, marking its 2nd consecutive weekly gain and its best close since early April.

Focal story this week was the BSP’s announcement after the closing bell on Thursday to trim rates by 25bps to 6.25%, which marked the central bank’s first rate reduction since November 2020. The Street consensus forecasted the 25bp cut but taking a closer look at the individual entries shows that economists were split on the matter. Bloomberg’s survey showed that 13 expected a cut while 10 expected a hold. This may be due to the BSP Governor’s mixed signals leading into the event, where he stated that an August cut is a “little less likely” following last week’s hotter-than-expected CPI reading and an as-expected GDP print, which grew at the fastest pace in 4 quarters. After the decision, the BSP Governor struck a more dovish tone and indicated that Monetary Board may cut rates by another 25bps either in October or December.

Outside of policy easing, investors also digested another busy batch of corporate results (see notes from our Research team below). One highlight beat was ICT which reported a 2Q core profit growth of 26% YoY to $206m. This brought its 1H +26% YoY to $389m, well above Regis and Street forecast. With that and a bullish market overall due to the rate cut, ICT was one of the best performing stocks this week +10.4% WoW to a new all-time high of P395/share. The only name that managed to better ICT in the benchmark was CNVRG, which surged +19% WoW to its best close since March 2023. The company also reported strong results with its 1H profit +24% YoY to P5.3b, ahead of Street. Note that for 2Q alone, profit was up +30% YoY to P2.74b, while net subscriber adds stood at 99k, lower than 125k in 1Q but still a healthy number considering that it accounts for 63% of industry net adds.

Other notable gainers this week include property giant ALI +10.33% WoW, which appears to be a go-to rate cut play among foreign investors, and JFC +9.18% WoW, which also reported 2Q/1H results this week. JFC’s 1H net profit climbed +29% YoY to P5.7b, ahead of Street but slightly behind Regis expectations. Notably, 2Q SSSG looked healthy at +7.4%, driven primarily by Philippines which grew by +9%.

On the opposite end, one notable earnings miss came from BLOOM which reported a 2Q net profit slide of -49% QoQ and -61% QoQ, behind both Regis and Street forecasts. Furthermore, outlook remains a concern amid the imminent POGO ban which is expected to take effect by the end of the year. Note that players from Greater China, a good chunk of which are likely employed the POGO sector, accounted for 25% of 1H GGR. BLOOM was the worst laggard in the benchmark this week, -7.59% WoW and closed near 52-week lows.

Market activity improved this week with a daily average of $99m, +10% WoW. Note however that this appeared to be driven primarily by local investors as foreign participation for the week stood at a lighter-than-usual 39%. Foreign net flow however turned supportive at a net buy of $25m, nearly half of which came through Friday alone.

ICTSI - 2Q24: Another solid set of results
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

2H24 core profit up 26% YoY to US$206 – Ahead of estimates

Headline 2Q24 profit rose 32% YoY to US$211mn. Removing one-offs and perps distributions, recurring profit grew 26% YoY to US$206mn during the quarter, bringing 1H24 profit up 26% to US$389mn. The results are trending well above Regis and Street forecasts. Management sounded upbeat about the company’s prospects, despite noise on a possible global trade slowdown.

Volume growth mildly better in 2Q; Yields up 14% YoY to new record of US$206

Consolidated volumes grew 2% YoY in 2Q24, with organic volume growth accelerating to 5.9% in 2Q from 5.3% in 1Q. Volumes at its ports in the Philippines rebounded in 2Q and more than compensated for the volume softness in Ecuador and Honduras.

Meanwhile, yield per TEU rose 3% QoQ, 14% YoY to a new high of US$206, thanks to the tariff hike at CMSA, higher storage revenues, and favorable volume mix across its ports. With the implementation of the 10% tariff hike at MICT last week and given the “conducive”, elevated environment of global freight rates, management is confident it can sustain or improve yields further in succeeding periods. This suggests further upside risk to our FY24E yield per TEU of US$205.

2Q24 EBITDA margin up to new record of 66%

EBITDA improved further by 9% QoQ, 20% YoY to US$451mn in 2Q, with EBITDA margin expanding by 110bps QoQ/280bps YoY to a new record of 66%. Management conveyed confidence about sustaining margins at these levels. 1H EBITDA grew 19% YoY to US$865mn, on trend to exceed forecasts.

Expansions to augment profits further; South Africa takeover deferred to 4Q

The company is set to complete the first phase of its expansion in CMSA (+250k TEUs) by next month, with all phases expected to be completed by end-2025 (+250k TEUs). Management reiterated its view that its brownfield expansions will result in immediate earnings accretion. Meanwhile, management deferred its guidance for the takeover of DCT-2 in Durban, South Africa to 4Q, given that it must hurdle Maersk’s legal challenge first. The first set of court hearings is scheduled for September.

 
Philippine Seven - 2Q24: Margins dragged by frontloaded opex; adjusting forecasts and TP
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H24 net profit +14% YoY; tweaking estimates and maintaining Buy

Philippine Seven saw net profit grow 14% YoY in 1H24, tracking broadly in line with expectations. Revenues (+18% YoY to P43.3bn) are trending slightly ahead of forecasts, but operating profit (+10% YoY to P2.6bn) appears in line. For 2Q24 alone, net profit rose 10% YoY to P1.1bn. SEVN ended the period with 3,926 stores, 11% higher vs 2Q23.

We are tweaking our EPS 2024-25E estimates (+3-7%) for recent results, rolling over our DCF (6% pretax cost of debt, 12% cost of equity, 4% terminal growth) to 2025E, and adjusting for the 100% stock div (ex-date 1 Aug). We are maintaining our Buy rating on a revised TP of P77/sh (+14%), which implies 2025E multiples of 25x PE and 10x EV/EBITDA. Key downside risks relate to competition and execution risk.

SSSG moderates in 2Q24 due to Holy Week timing

Same store sales growth moderated to +7.4% in 2Q24 from +8.8% in the previous quarter. This is partly driven by base effects due to the timing of the Holy Week (Mar ’24 vs Apr ’23), which is seasonally strong for 7-Eleven stores. For May and June alone, SSSG was 9-10% on sustained customer traffic growth of ~8%. The company noted that SSSG slowed in July due to heavy rains in the latter part of the month but had recovered in early-Aug.

Operating margin crimped by lower gross margins, frontloaded expenses

Operating margin contracted 50bps YoY, driven by lower gross margin (-20bps YoY) due to slower sales of alcoholic beverages and cup drinks (coffee). The company also incurred additional IT development costs relating to the CVS+ project, which will be rolled out next year. We expect the impact of these frontloaded expenses to moderate in the coming quarters as sales pick up and new stores are opened. The company says it is on track to open 450 stores (gross) this year.

 
ACEN Corp - 2Q: Seasonally weak output
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Weak output trims profit

ACEN reported net income of P6.3bn for 1H24, up 49% YoY. Excluding the net value realization gains (P1.4bn) as well as impairment charges (P0.57bn) for the period, we estimate ‘core’ income to be P5.5bn (+22%). This implies 2Q ‘core’ income was P2.8bn, which would be a tad higher than 1Q24. The 2Q “core income” is lower than what we expected for the quarter but likely to be in line with Street estimates.

2Q attributable EBITDA flat QoQ

“Core attributable EBITDA” was P10.6bn for 1H, which means 1Q and 2Q numbers were broadly similar (P5.3bn per quarter).  Overall output in 2Q (1.42GWH) was down 11% from 1Q (1.60GWH) due to seasonally lower wind output exacerbated by El Nino and some grid issues in Australia. Output from wind farms dropped 45% QoQ to 271GWH. The sale of Sidrap in Indonesia also reduced GWH output marginally in 2Q.

Better yields from PH spot exposure help to cushion the volume drop. Around 25% of PH sales volumes was done in the spot market in 1H (29% in 2Q).

Equity raise next year?

While management said that ACEN was unlikely to raise equity in the “next six months”, it did say that the ideal timing would be sometime middle of next year (2025). Management said a capital raise could be done at various levels, not just at the listed holding company (i.e. ACEN), but maybe at the project level.

A sell-down or dilution of ACEN’s ownership in its project companies would set ACEN back on its path to reach 20,000MW of attributable capacity (its target by 2030). Consequently, an equity issue at the ACEN level is, in our view, more likely. ACEN’s controlling shareholder Ayala Corp (AC PM) has publicly expressed willingness to help fund ACEN.

Guiding for soft 2H

Management also took pains to say that 3Q is also seasonally weak for its RE portfolio, as this is the period when output from its wind and solar farms is relatively low. We interpret this guidance as a signal that 2H earnings are likely to be lower than 1H. New capacity (~1,500MW) is on its way but bulk of that will only be operational from 2025 onwards. The company also pointed out that depreciation charges for some of its new capacities are only about to start.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - 2Q24 GGR falls despite contribution of new casino
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q24 GGR falls 2% QoQ and 4% YoY

Bloomberry’s 2Q24 net profit fell 49% QoQ and 61% YoY to P1.34bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Despite the contribution of Solaire Resort North (SRN) for 37 days, 2Q24 GGR was lower by 2% QoQ and 4% YoY due to lower foreign VIP and premium mass revenue at Solaire Entertainment City (SEC).

2Q24 EBITDA (down 26% QoQ and 33% YoY) was further dragged by P764mn of pre-operating expenses from SRN. Excluding the impact of these expenses, consolidated EBITDA would have fallen 20% YoY.

Solaire Resort North (SRN) performance improves in July

SRN contributed P1.1bn of GGR and P250mn of EBITDA (excluding pre-operatingexpenses) in 2Q24. This could improve further in 2H as SRN already saw a significant increase in July. Management expects the “full ramp up” after 24 months, after which it believes SRN should hit half of the GGR and EBITDA levels of SEC.

POGO ban could have negative impact

The POGO ban could have a negative effect since some of the players from Greater China (who accounted for c. 25% of 1H GGR) likely have ties to the sector.

Improving its online platform

The company has witnessed the substantial rise in GGR of the e-casino industry and wants a bigger piece of this pie. While BLOOM’s online GGR is currently less than 5% of total GGR, the company has taken steps to grow this business.

Cash dividend policy established

BLOOM’s Board of Directors approved a dividend policy that will pay 35% of prior year’s earnings as cash dividends. While these are subject to certain financial conditions (such as meeting debt covenants), management expects to pay out 35% of 2024 earnings next year.

 
PLDT - 2Q: Mobile segment disappoints
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core profit up +6% for 1H24

PLDT announced 2Q/1H results today, with headline profit of P18.4bn for the half, +2% YoY. Core profits (inclusive losses at Maya) amounted to P17.4bn. +6% YoY on our estimates. The bottom line would be, at first glance, broadly within Regis expectations. However, underlying operations were, in our view, softer than they appear. Lower depreciation charges as well as smaller losses at Maya helped.

Mobile revenue rises less than expected; data yield drops

Mobile revenue for 2Q rose +3% YoY to P23.7bn, which is actually down 2% QoQ. We reckon PLDT lost close to 1pt of revenue market share in the quarter, likely to the third operator. We also estimate that its mobile data yields tumbled more sharply in 2Q than they did since 4Q22.

Home broadband revenue flat

PLDT added 35k subscribers to its wired base, but overall revenue remains virtually unchanged QoQ (the case for the past five quarters, see table). The decline in fixed wireless and DSL just about offset any growth in fiber. The company launched new fiber broadband products: one a lower-priced postpaid service priced at P899/month and the other a prepaid service. Both services, however, seem fairly lackluster.

Maya losses narrow

Losses from its fintech unit Maya halved to P635m for the half and management says it is on track to break even in 4Q. Its digital bank now has ~P7bn in loan receivables and deposits of ~P32bn.

Partial sale of data center business nears

PLDT has agreed with a potential investor on a valuation for its data center business (“Vitro”). Some details are still being ironed out, including what proportion of the buy-in will accrue to PLDT (through a secondary share sale).

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - 2Q24: PH SSSG resilient, int’l business improves; reiterating Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

International business drives 1H operating profit

JFC reported headline net profit of P5.7bn in 1H24, up 29% YoY. This is tracking slightly behind Regis expectations but ahead of consensus. Stripping out coupons to perps and pref dividends, we estimate net profit to common was P5bn, up 34% YoY. 1H24 revenues grew 11% YoY, broadly within expectations. Meanwhile, operating profit is trending ahead of forecasts, up 21% YoY to P9.2bn. By geography, it was the international business that outperformed, posting operating profit of P1.3bn, nearly 2x YoY.

Philippines leads top line growth in 2Q24; Jollibee int’l outperforms

2Q24 SSSG for the group was 7.4%, led by the Philippines, which posted solid SSSG of +9% amidst a tepid consumer environment. CBTL (+11%) and Jollibee in N. America (+11%) and EMEAA (+17%) outperformed on strong transaction count  growth. Meanwhile, China (-13%) continued to be challenged. Smashburger SSS declined 3%, though average daily sales (ADS) continued to improve QoQ and YoY.

International business sees improved profitability

2Q24 operating profit jumped 27% YoY on margin improvement, due to operating leverage from the strong SS performance of CBTL and Jollibee int’l. Operating margin for the int’l segment alone surged to 3.4% from breakeven last year - the highest since the company started disclosing this in 2021.

Management guidance upgraded

Given the strong 1H operating results, management upgraded its FY24 operating profit growth guidance to +18-20% (from +10-15% previously), which does not yet take into account the Compose Coffee acquisition. We believe JFC is likely to exceed its own guidance for the year given strong top line growth expectations for 2H, especially in the Philippines.

Adjusting estimates; maintaining Buy rating and TP

We are rolling over our valuation to 2025E and adjusting our 2024-25E estimates to account for the results, though we have not imputed Compose Coffee pending the closing of the transaction. Our 2024-25E EPS estimates are -5-6% vs previous due to a reduction in other income, but our operating profit estimates are raised by 7-9%. We are reiterating our Buy rating on JFC on our TP of P300/sh (unchanged), which implies 2025E multiples of 28x PE and 11x EV/EBITDA. Key downside risks relate to consumer sentiment, input cost inflation, execution risk, and dilutive acquisitions.

1H24 net profit -14% YoY - behind

Shakey’s Pizza reported 1H24 net profit of P421mn, down 14% YoY, tracking behind expectations. Revenue (+8% YoY) was broadly in line, but operating profit (-14% YoY) was behind due to higher-than-expected opex. For 2Q24 alone, revenues rose 9% YoY but operating profit fell 13% YoY.

SSSG accelerates in 2Q24

Filinvest Land - Reservation sales improve further in 2Q24
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H24 profit rises 11%, in line

FLI’s 1H24 net profit rose 11% to P1.5bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. (Note that FLI’s earnings are seasonally stronger in the second half of  the year.) The company’s reservation sales showed remarkable improvement in 2Q24. However, its office portfolio is likely to see higher vacancies as new buildings are completed in 2H24.

2Q24 reservation sales improve further

After rising 44% QoQ in 1Q24, reservation sales grew another 25% QoQ (+21% YoY) to P7.1bn in 2Q24. As a result, 1H24 reservation sales expanded 16% to P12.8bn. Management attributes the improvements to several factors: a strong push to liquidate ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units, new sales offices in more countries, and the government raising the VAT-free ceiling (from P3.2mn to P3.6mn). The strong RFO sales also helped 1H24 residential revenue rise 22%.

2Q24 office occupancy improves but…

2Q24 office occupancy improved c. 5ppt QoQ (to 63%) as BPO firms and a government agency took up space. However, occupancy is likely to drop in 2H24.

Although a different government agency signed up for c. 24k sqm in FLI’s Manila Bay area buildings in July (equivalent to 4% of FLI’s office portfolio), this will largely be offset by the anticipated closure of the company’s POGO tenants, which occupy 3% of the company’s office space. Furthermore, the new buildings to be completed in 2H24, which will expand FLI’s office portfolio by 12%, are still largely vacant.

POGO ban to have negative impact

We expect Filinvest Land to be among the most negatively affected by the POGO ban since about 10% of the company office space is in the Manila Bay area, over half of which is still vacant.

 
GT Capital - Auto margins slide in 2Q24
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H24 core profit up 1% YoY to P13.27bn – Ahead of Regis estimates

Reported 1H24 core profit of P13.85bn (-17% YoY) included gains from lot sales. Stripping this out and after deducting pref dividends, we estimate core profit just narrowly improved to P13.3bn. There were significant gains from Metrobank and its infrastructure arm, Metro Pacific. Contributions from its auto and property units were lower and there appear to have been higher parent-level costs. Overall, however, the results are tracking ahead of Regis FY24E but appears mildly below consensus estimates.

Auto profits flat in 2Q despite robust sales

Notwithstanding the 9% YoY improvement in wholesale volumes, which in turn was achieved due to a solid 13% improvement in retail sales, standalone 2Q24 profit of Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) was flat at P3.5bn. GPM compressed 110bps QoQ and 80bps YoY to 13.6% during the quarter, as the PHP depreciated sharply against the USD during the quarter. Given the recent appreciation of the PHP and the sustained strength in vehicle sales in July (+11% YoY), we reckon the profit weakness in 2Q could soon be reversed.

Management also shared that industry-wide electrified vehicle (including hybrids) sales grew by 183% YoY to 9,238 units in 1H, accounting for 4% of overall new car sales. TMP alone has captured ~70% of this market.

MPI: 1H24 core profit rises by 27% YoY; 2Q24 up 24%

Standalone core profits at MPI rose 27% YoY to P12.54bn, with 2Q24 profit up 24% YoY to P6.9bn. Very strong profits at Meralco, as well as tariff hikes at its water and tollroad units, were the biggest drivers of profitability.

Property: Reservation sales drop 21% YoY in 1H on absence of new launches

Federal Land’s reservation sales dipped 3% QoQ to P3.8bn (+6% YoY), taking 1H24 sales lower by 21% YoY to P7.7bn. Management blamed this on the absence of new project launches in 1H and the depletion of its BGC inventory. Meanwhile, implied 2Q24 profit fell 8% YoY to P484mn, despite a mild 3% improvement in booked revenues due to higher opex.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Corp 1H core income up 18% to P24.3bn

Core income up 18% to P24bn in 1H. Ayala Corp reported net income of P22.3bn for 1H, +21% YoY.  Excluding what AC considers one-off items, core income for the half was P24.3bn, +18% YoY.  We believe the results are mildly ahead of Street estimates. 2Q core earnings were an improvement over 1Q with key subsidiaries turning in results that were in line with expectations (mostly).

Nonetheless, AC’s profit continues to be significantly affected by large provisions/charges, which, fortunately for AC, were largely offset by one-off gains this quarter.

More impairments at unlisted units. AC took a P4bn charge for its holdings in IMI in 2Q and another P2.2bn for the closure of Entrego (last mile logistics). Both IMI and AC Logistics recently hired new management to help turn around the businesses. We view recent actions here (clean-up and new management) positively but the ultimate proof of the pudding is in the eating.

Losses still at AC Health but there’s light at the end of the tunnel.  AC Health posted a loss of P327m for 1H with a modest positive EBITDA. Management expressed confidence that it can reach the P1bn EBITDA target this year.

US$400m to increase stake in Mynt.  AC invested P22.9bn (~US$400m) to increase its stake in Mynt to 13% (from 5%) at a US$5bn valuation. At the same time, Japanese banking group MUFG also bought 8%.  We estimate Mynt raised >US$200m in new money from either of the two transactions, which will allow it to become even more aggressive in funding its lending activities.

Converge 1H profits up 24% on revenue growth of 12%

Converge reported P5.3bn profit for 1H up 24% YoY. Converge’s reported profit of P5.3bn for 1H (+24%) is likely ahead of consensus (~P9.4bn for FY24).  For 2Q alone, profit for was P2.74bn (+30% YoY). While results appear to be better than Street estimates, the extent of the beat was exaggerated by FX gains on its USD cash holdings (P207m in 1Q, P517m in 1H). Note the FX gains form part of EBITDA (and other return metrics) as management reports it.

Revenue growth accelerated to +14% in 2Q but gross profit was up only 7%. We are encouraged by the acceleration in revenue growth to 14% in 2Q (vs 10% in 1Q), led by Enterprise. Gross profits, however, grew only 7%. This suggests cost pressures are squeezing profitability.

Net subscriber adds dip.  Net subscriber additions for 2Q was 99k, taking total subscribers to 2.35m.  This is a reduction from the 125k of 1Q but is still a commendable number, accounting for 63% of industry net adds. We suspect the balancing act between churn management vis-à-vis gross subscriber additions will be the key growth issue – not so different from the trade-off between NPLs and loan growth for banks. Just like NPLs, any spike in churn due to more lax standards may not emerge until months/quarters later.

Guidance on revenue growth and capex. The company is guiding for revenue growth of 12-14% for the year, which means growth is likely to accelerate in 2H. Management said the guidance does not consider the potential impact of its deal with Sky Cable, which agreed to pay for the use of Converge’s network in the provision of broadband to consumers.

After lowering it previously (to P12-14bn), Converge now expects capex this year to rise to P15-17bn, as spending on international cable capacity was moved up again. For FY25, capex is likely to remain high (~P15bn) due to further spending on international cable capacity as well as a new 10MW data center.  Despite the capex, management signaled its commitment to pay its first dividend next year.

SECB 1H24 misses - strong loans, revenues, but dragged by still-high provisions

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT of P5.45bn, up 11% YoY, tracking behind Regis (FY24 P12.0bn) but in line with consensus (P10.6bn). Core operations were surprisingly strong, with loans up 19% (consumer +37%, corporate +14%), and PPOP up 31%, already accounting for 55% of our FY24 forecast. However, profits were dragged by provision expense, which more than doubled YoY. Credit cost stayed high at 124bps in 2Q24, much higher than management guidance (60-70bps). It would seem that there continued to be large write-offs, as NPL ratio improved 10bps QoQ, and NPL cover was flat QoQ.

Loan growth accelerated to 19% YoY, from 12% in 1Q24. This was far higher than the sector average 12.5%. Consumer loans continued to surge, up 37% YoY, with mortgages up 21% YoY, auto loans up 50%, credit cards up an astounding 60%, and MSME loans up 68%. Consumer/MSMEs now account for 32% of total loans, a new high for the bank. Deposits grew 29% YoY, though most of the growth came from TDs, up 64% YoY, with CASA up just 9%.

Headline NIMs dipped 13bps QoQ to 5.01%. However, it is difficult to compare, given the interest rate swaps from previous quarters. We hope management will give similar “pro-forma” or adjusted NIM data during the briefing. Loan yields, though, jumped 37bps QoQ to 8.29%, presumably as a result of the much-improved loan mix.

Fee incomes were up 19% YoY in 2Q24. Note that the 1Q24 figures included chunky milestone payments from the bank’s bancassurance partner.

Opex was up 20% YoY in 1H, as the bank continued to invest in IT and manpower to build up capacity. This took PPOP up 31% to P10.7bn, ahead of our forecasts.

Provision expense doubled to P3.3bn in 1H24. Frustratingly, credit cost stayed high, rising to 124bps in 2Q24 alone (from 112bps in 1Q24). This was despite management guidance of credit cost normalizing to the 60-70bps level after the cleanup of the credit card portfolio in 4Q23 and 1Q24. We suspect there was another round of write-offs, as NPL ratio improved to 3.31% (from 3.41% in 1Q) and NPL cover was flat at 81%.

Puregold 1H24 net profit +12% YoY to P4.9bn

In a limited press release, PGOLD reported net profit of P4.9bn in 1H24, up 12% YoY. This is trending behind Regis estimates but broadly in line with consensus. Net sales rose 8% YoY to P98.5bn (behind) while operating profit grew 11% YoY to P7.4bn. Implied 2Q24 net profit is P2.5bn, up 24% YoY on operating margin improvement (+60bps YoY). SSSG for 1H24 was 1.9% for the core Puregold brand and 2.4% for S&R. This suggests SSSG accelerated in 2Q24. Note that in 1Q24: +1.5% Puregold / -1.2% S&R.

DigiPlus Interactive 2Q24 net profit quintuples

2Q24 net profit of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) quintupled to P3.2bn. This brought its 1H24 earnings +383% to P5.2bn, ahead of consensus forecast. 2Q24 revenue quadrupled to P18.9bn, higher than BLOOM’s record quarterly GGR (P17bn in 3Q19). 2Q24 Operating profit rose 363% to P3.3bn. The operating margin expanded 230 basis points QoQ (+260 basis points YoY). Note that in April 2024, PAGCOR lowered the fees that it collects from e-games operators and it is expected to reduce fees further in January 2025.

AGI 1H24 net profit fall 6%, in line with consensus forecast

Alliance Global’s 2Q24 net profit rose 11% QoQ (flat YoY) to P4.6bn. 1H24 earnings fell 6% to P8.8bn, due to lower contributions from Emperador and Travellers. 2Q24 profit contributions from Emperador (EMI PM), Travellers, and Golden Arches rose QoQ. BSP cuts policy rate by 25bps to 6.25%

The BSP monetary board decided to reduce its policy rate (target reverse repurchase rate) by 25bps to 6.25%, in line with consensus estimates. Officials cited weak consumption in 2Q24 GDP, as well as expectations of inflation declining in the coming months. Headline inflation is projected to trend downward to within the government’s 2–4% target range despite the uptick in July. BSP Governor Eli Remolona said the cut may only be felt next year, given lag effects. BSP said that the balance of risks to the inflation outlook continues to lean toward the downside for 2024 and 2025 with a modest tilt to the upside for 2026. Moving forward, the BSP Governor Remolona signaled another 25-bp cut for the remainder of the year. Note the Monetary Board will next meet on Oct. 17 and Dec. 19.

Remittances rise 2.5% YoY in June

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.5% YoY to US$2.88bn in June. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 7.6% YoY. Strong growth rates seen from land-based remittances coming from Singapore (+4.6%), Saudi Arabia (+5.2%), UAE (+5.4%), Qatar (+6%), UK (+3.3%), and Canada (+3.3%), while sea-based remittances rose 2.1% YoY. This brings 1H24 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$16.25bn, broadly in line with the BSP’s target of +3% YoY.

Asset quality improves in June; loan growth accelerates to 12.5% YoY

Asset quality ratios for the Philippine banking system improved nearly across the board in June. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dipped 7bps MoM to 3.59% from 3.66% in May. In PHP terms, NPLs were up 1.4% MoM to P502bn. Past Due (PD) ratio fell 9bps MoM to 4.29%, with the nominal amounts up 1.0% to P614bn. Restructured Loans (RL) improved to 2.05%, with absolute RLs down 0.8% MoM to P294bn. NPL cover was flat-ish MoM, with June coming in at 95.41%, 40bps lower than the 95.81% in May.

The better ratios were seen across all the different banking classifications. NPL ratios for universal/commercial banks (UKBs) were down 6bps MoM to 3.29%. For Thrift Banks (TBs), NPL ratio dropped 24bps MoM to 6.79%. Most notably, Digibank NPLs plummeted 245bps MoM, though this was still quite high at 18.19% (we also suspect there may have been large write-offs).

The ratios were partly helped by strong loan (ex-interbank) data, growing 3.4% MoM. On a YoY basis, loan growth accelerated sharply, up 12.5% YoY, the fastest pace since February 2019 (12.7%).
