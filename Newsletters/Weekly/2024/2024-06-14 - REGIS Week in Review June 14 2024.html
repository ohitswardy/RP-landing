REGIS REPORT

14 June 2024

Philippine shares were downbeat this week with the index falling in all 4 trading sessions (holiday Wednesday) while trading on lackluster volumes, signaling low conviction on the ground amid the lack of strong leads and lingering uncertainty over the monetary policy outlook. The benchmark initially came off both the psychological 6500-line and technical 200-day moving average on Monday, then continued to fall through the rest of the week. Losses did start to ease in the latter part of the week after the index broke below 6400 and neared YTD lows. Investors also took some reprieve from the Fed’s indication of a single rate cut this year, and the subsequent data showing an unexpected contraction in US May PPI which supports the case for policy easing. The debate on the ground however is whether the BSP will be able to cut rates ahead of the Fed. Finance Secretary Recto offered his comments this week and said that he does not see the BSP cutting ahead of the Fed. Note that this follows his comments just last week that he sees the BSP cutting rates by a total of 150bps by end-2025. The PSEi ultimately settled at 6383.7, -2.07% WoW, reversing the prior week’s +1.33% uptick. Broader term, the benchmark looks to be in a holding pattern within a range between its YTD low (6371.75) and the psychological 6500-level, since first marking a new YTD low in late May.

Trading volumes were notably soft this week, averaging a daily turnover of only $61m, -34% WoW. In fact, turnover (including blocks) on Monday was the lowest YTD, and three sessions this week were among the four lightest so far this 2024. That said, there were some holidays that likely affected activity as well – HK being out on Monday for Dragon Boat Festival, and the mid-week break at home for Independence Day. Furthermore, we, along with most of ASEAN, also have a long weekend coming up - Eid Al-fitr holiday on Monday - which may have affected activity to some extent at the end of the week. Meanwhile, net foreign flow was skewed to sell all throughout the week but by a relatively moderate amount. Net selling figure for the broader week stood at $22m, which is the lightest in 3 weeks.

Breadth of the market was quite poor with only 3/30 index members resisting the slump: MER +3.58% WoW, AGI +0.11% which started buying back shares again for the first time since early May, and LTG (unchanged). On the other end, over a third of index components posted declines of more than -3%. Biggest laggards were WLCON -9.84%, JGS -6.5%, consumer plays JFC -4.78% and CNPF -4.37%, and Razon stocks ICT -4.18% and BLOOM -4.17%. For ICT, we note that the weakness could be due to several factors, the most notable among them particularly concerns its Mexico port: the sharp weakness in the Mexican peso post-elections amid a general uncertainty over the ruling party, and the latest port volume data in Mexico which showed that industry volumes rose +7% YoY in May but ICT’s CMSA port actually saw a -4% decline (see our detailed clip below). That said, we do believe that CMSA’s market share loss will not persist given that it is set to complete its 250k TEU expansion this year, and hence will be well-positioned to capture any incremental demand in the already congested port of Manzanillo.

Globe Telecom - Core business on the mend; Mynt profits prompt reevaluation
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Reaping what’s been sown

We adjust our target price for Globe to P2,750, the highest on the Street. Beyond just the oft-cited value of Mynt/GCash, we believe the drag on its core business is now behind us. Mobile revenue growth is bouncing back, capex is falling, and free cash flow is set to turn positive. All this while fintech unit Mynt’s profitability is soaring. At 12x PER with yield of 5%, we believe there is strong valuation foundation for our Buy rating.

Mobile industry on the mend

Globe saw mobile revenue rising 8% in 1Q24, confirming our view that the rebound in mobile market is seeing “repair”. Both main carriers are seeking to finally reap the benefits of unprecedented capex levels over the last few years. Coupled with the fact that the third operator appears to be on the ropes, we believe the benign competitive environment will hold.

Profit jump justifies higher Mynt valuations

Globe’s share of Mynt’s earnings implies the latter produced P2.7bn in profits for 1Q24, 1.4x the prior year. On that pace, Mynt could make close to US$200m in profit for 2024. Consequently, a US$5bn valuation is, in our view, justifiable if not conservative.

Improving cash flow

After peaking in 2022 at US$1.9bn, capex should fall to US$1bn this year. We forecast Globe becoming modestly free cash flow positive by next year. Coupled with the sale of half its telecom tower portfolio, we expect Globe’s leverage ratio to begin moderating.

Target price raised to P2,750

We adjust our target price to P2,750/share. Our valuation/TP was derived using (1) DCF analysis for Globe’s core operations, and (2) assumed valuations for some of its affiliates, most importantly Mynt (US$5bn). Key risks to the downside relate to a deterioration in the macro conditions while upside risk could come from reduced competition.

 
Philippine Banks - Stronger for longer?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Defending ROE gains

The Big Three banks are looking to sustain decade/s-high ROEs, while SECB seems to have turned a corner. Loan growth accelerated in 1Q24, historically a slow quarter. Policy rate cuts have been pushed back, supporting “stronger for longer” ROEs. However, competition in loan and deposit pricing seems to have intensified, tempering NIMs. Asset quality has stayed benign allowing for low credit costs in 2024. Meanwhile, the Big Three are upping dividends to improve capital efficiency. We maintain Buys on the four banks under our coverage.

Loan growth surprises, but NIMs tempered

Banks’ 1Q24 profits were strong, either meeting or beating our expectations. Loan growth unexpectedly accelerated to 11-13% YoY, driven by a surprise spurt in corporate demand, while consumers remained robust (+15-30% YoY). NIMs were tempered in part by lower-yielding corporate loans. But increasing competition for loan and deposit pricing seem to also be limiting margin gains.

ROEs to get a boost from lower credit costs, higher dividends

Asset quality has stayed benign, and all four banks have guided for low credit costs for 2024E. The Big Three are arguably over-provisioned with NPL cover at 136-181%. SECB completed a cleanup of its credit card base in 1Q24, guiding for provisions to normalize from here. Meanwhile, the Big Three announced higher dividend payouts to improve capital efficiency. MBT paid a special dividend, taking its payout ratio to over 50%, and div yield to over 7%.

Staying bullish, for now…

The sector continues to outperform, up 15% ytd versus a flat PSEi Index. MBT has been the rising star, returning 42% so far this year including cash divs, while BDO may be suffering for being a market proxy. Meanwhile, SECB stock seems to have found a floor, though its size could mean delays in realizing its deep value. We increase our 2024/25E earnings estimates by 3-4%, and retain our Buy calls on the four banks we cover. Key sector risks include further intensifying competition, and deteriorating asset quality.

 
Robinsons Land - RCR to grow portfolio by 72% via share-for-property swap with RLC
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

RCR announces major property acquisition

RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) announced that it would acquire 279,000sqm of malls and 69,000sqm of offices from its sponsor, Robinsons Land. In turn, RLC will subscribe to 5bn new RCR shares, expanding shares outstanding by 47%. Surprisingly, RLC will subscribe to the new shares at P6.80/share, a ~ 30% premium to RCR’s last closing price.

RCR will have the highest mall exposure among Philippine REITs

RCR will acquire 11 malls (10 of which are in provincial areas) and two offices from RLC. While RCR currently has no mall exposure, after the acquisition, 1/3 of RCR’s 828,000sqm portfolio will be malls, giving it the highest mall exposure among Philippine REITs. We would consider this a differentiating factor for RCR, since we have a more optimistic outlook on the mall business than the office segment, which is the main exposure of most Philippine REITs.

Yield accretive for RCR

We expect the acquisition to be yield accretive for RCR. We estimate that the transaction would yield c. 7% at P6.80/share. However, at RCR’s current price of P5.21/share, we estimate that the assets would yield c. 9%, higher than RCR’s FY24E dividend yield of 7.8% (based on consensus forecast). Thus, the dividend yield should rise to over 8% after the property acquisition. After the transaction RLC’s ownership in RCR will rise from 50% to 66%.

Fundamentally neutral for RLC but should be a positive catalyst

Although we expect the transaction to be fundamentally neutral for RLC, we also expect it to be a positive catalyst for the stock. RLC will only infuse buildings and will still receive land lease payments from RCR. Coupled with lower taxes on REIT assets, RLC’s EPS and NAV should be practically unchanged. However, we would still expect the transaction to be positive for RLC’s share price. Once the transaction is completed, RCR’s market capitalization will be higher than RLC’s current market cap, highlighting how undervalued RLC shares are, particularly since the company still has 1.3mn sqm of malls (and 200,000sqm of offices) that can be infused into RCR in the future.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI (ICT PM) Update

There could be two credible reasons for ICT’s share price weakness. One issue relates to its port in Mexico, which we estimate accounts for 21% of ICT’s revenues, while the other issue could affect the port of Manila, which accounts for 26% of group revenues.

1. Mexican Peso’s weakness post-elections.  The Mexican peso has depreciated by 11% since end-May or since the ruling Morena party won the elections, raising fears that the constitutional reforms the party is pursuing will undermine Mexico’s checks and balances.

While the P&L impact of a weak MXP to ICT is minimal (-0.9% impact to profit for every 5% depreciation of MXP against USD), holding other things constant, a massive and instantaneous depreciation such as this will likely adversely affect the country’s purchasing power to import and could affect ICT’s volumes.  

The latest volume data for ICT’s port in Mexico came out this morning.  Industry volumes in May rose 7% YoY but ICT’s port, CMSA, actually saw volumes fall 4% YoY. Sequentially, volumes at CMSA have also been on a declining trend, in contrast to industry volumes, which has been rising MoM.

We believe CMSA’s market share loss will not persist, given that it is set to complete its 250k TEU expansion this year. Note that the port of Manzanillo, where CMSA is located, is already congested today and CMSA is well-positioned to capture any incremental demand. As of 5M24, CMSA’s volumes are up 22% YoY to 570k TEUs, still on trend to meet our forecast of 1.43mn TEUs (+12% YoY) given typical demand seasonality. However, we do acknowledge that the recent currency weakness pose risks to demand so we will continue to keep a watchful eye on this.

2. Port congestion in Singapore, which could potentially mean short-term weakness in volumes at ICT’s ports, particularly Manila.  Two weeks ago (see here), reports came out about a severe congestion at PSA Singapore, the largest transshipment hub globally.  The congestion could have knock-on effects on the arrival of containers to their “end destinations”.

ICT’s management has not seen any significant delays in the arrival of containers at their ports in the Philippines so far and they do not expect this issue to have a material impact on their operations, given that shipping lines have other transshipment hub options in the region (e.g. Malaysia, Vietnam, HK, Chinese ports).

However, we will acknowledge that the effects could just be delayed so we will be monitoring this space closely.  It is worth noting that while there could potentially be weakness in volumes near-term, these containers will eventually arrive at the end destination ports, such as Manila.  What is harder to predict, however, is whether these containers will arrive in droves, resulting in congestions, and higher storage revenues.

 

PLDT agrees to valuation of over US$1bn for data center business with partner

Relating to the entry of a foreign partner, CEO Manuel Pangilinan said that PLDT has agreed to a valuation of over US$1bn for its data center business. For perspective, PLDT has a market capitalization of P312bn (US$5.3bn). The prospective foreign entity will take a 49% stake in the DC operation. Recall that Pangilinan previously said Japanese telco NTT was agreeable to a valuation of over US$1bn but wanted a majority interest. PLDT was supposed to be considering other bids to take less than 50% but at lower valuations. Note that a possible REIT listing for the assets was also considered.

The data center business today contributes less than 2% of revenue. PLDT is in the process of building its 11th data center which will be bigger than all its existing DCs combined, with the first expected to be completed next month. Proceeds from a secondary sale will be used to reduce debt at PLDT.

Trust AUMs grow 16% YoY 1Q24, with BPI the big gainer

Trust assets under management (AUMs) of banks and non-banks regulated by the BSP stood at P6,403bn (roughly $110bn) as of 1Q24, up 16% YoY, sustaining the pace set in 4Q23. AUMs were up 3% QoQ. The composition of AUMs changed dramatically, though, as both Rizal Commercial Bank (RCB PM) and Union Bank (UBP PM) spun off their trust departments into “Stand-Alone Trusts”, similar to BPI’s, effectively reclassifying their AUMs as “non-banks”.

BPI (BPI PM) was the big winner in 1Q24, with AUMs surging 38% YoY and 8% QoQ to P1,315bn, bringing market share up to 20.5% from 19.7% in 4Q23. In 1Q24, BPI accounted for just under 50% of incremental AUMs of the whole sector. This further narrowed the gap to BDO (BDO PM) Parent, which saw AUMs dip 1% QoQ to P1,385bn. On a consolidated basis (including BDO Private Bank), BDO was flat QoQ but continues to hold a commanding market share lead at 31.2%. Metrobank (MBT PM) was a far third at P503.5bn, up 1% QoQ.

The Big Three account for roughly 60% of total AUMs, with BDO and BPI already more than half the industry at a combined 51.8% market share. Of the smaller banks, RCBC and East West (EW PM) took market share, posting QoQ growth of 5% and 6% respectively.

ACMobility opening 22 BYD dealerships this year

AC PM’s ACMobility is aiming to open 22 BYD dealerships within the year, according to Antonio “Toti” Zara III, head of automotive retail and distribution at ACMobility. ACMobility currently has 4 operating BYD dealerships in Metro Manila and Cebu and is currently constructing 7 new dealerships. It has secured 11 sites in major cities and provinces such as Alabang, Cagayan De Oro, Cavite, Dagupan, Davao, Iloilo, Lipa, Marikina, Naga, Pampanga and Sta. Rosa.

UK fund buys into CREC, invests US$12.5mn

The UK government’s Mobilist Programme invested US$12.5mn into CREC’s IPO, which marks their investment in the PH. Mobilist Programme aims to support infrastructure development and green transition in SEA. Its investment covered a “significant portion” of the company’s P5.3bn IPO. Note that currently, CREC has 285.1 megawatts of solar, hydro, and wind projects; and it aims to grow this by 5,000 MW in the next 5 years, mostly through solar projects.

JFC expects Smashburger to turn profitable by 2025

According to CFO Richard Shin, the company expects Smashburger to record positive net operating income by the end of 2025, citing MoM improvements in average daily sales. Recall that Smashburger posted EBITDA of P145mn in 1Q24 and ended the period with 232 stores.

Meralco to bid out 600MW baseload supply agreement

Meralco launched its Competitive Selection Process for 600MW of baseload supply, which will involve a 15-year Power Supply Agreement that will begin in August 2025. The important dates to note: June 25 – deadline to submit Expression of Interest, July 24 – pre-bid conference, and August 2 – Meralco will close bid submissions.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

President Marcos signs Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform into law

President Marcos signed the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform into law. The reforms will take effect within two years. It seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units. The national government will be required to update the Schedule of Market Values every three years. Note that the values used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. Although Local Government Units will have to follow the Schedule of Market Values, they will have the power to reduce the tax rate on the said values. In the first year of implementation, the increase in real property taxes will be capped at 6%.

Regis estimates that real property taxes account for 2%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover. As such, this law would likely have a bigger impact on developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land, Filinvest Land, and Vista Land.

Consumer loans jump 18% in 1Q24

Consumer loans for all banks grew 17.6% YoY in 1Q24, accelerating from the 17.0% pace in 4Q23. This is the fastest YoY print since 1Q20, just before the Covid pandemic started. Consumer loans also expanded at a significantly faster pace than total loans (+7.4%). As a result, consumers in 1Q24 accounted for 21.4% of total loans, an all-time high.

Growth was again driven by credit cards, up 30.0% YoY in 1Q24. This is the sixth consecutive quarter of more than 25% expansion YoY. Motor vehicle loans zoomed 17.6% YoY, the 9th consecutive quarter of accelerating YoY growth. Mortgage growth sped up to 7.9% YoY, up from 7.0% at end-2023. Meanwhile, Salary loans decelerated to +23.5% YoY, understandably, as it lapped the heady 62% growth posted in 1Q23.

NPL ratio for the consumer segment ticked up slightly ln 1Q24 to 5.80% up 3bps QoQ. This is not particularly worrisome, as 4Q is typically when most banks do significant write-offs. On the secured side, Mortgage NPLs edged up 1bp QoQ to 6.94%, while motor vehicle NPLs fell sharply to 5.36% from 5.62% in 4Q23. For unsecured consumer loans, NPLs increased for credit cards (+16bps QoQ to 4.13%) and for salary loans (+29bps QoQ to 5.41%). The increase in unsecured delinquencies could be a sign of increased stress on household cashflows given due to inflation.

Among the banks we cover, Security Bank (SECB PM) has the highest exposure to consumer loans at 31% of loans. Of the Big Three, its BPI (BPI PM) at 26%.

The growth in motor vehicle loans is positive for GT Capital (GTCAP PM), which part owns the Toyota franchise in the Philippines.

April NPL, Past Due rates blip up; loans accelerate to new high since 2019

First, the good news. Loan growth (ex-interbank) in April shot up to 11.6% YoY (was 10.2% in March). That’s the fastest YoY growth rate since April 2019 (12.2%).

Oddly, that makes the bad news even bad-der. NPL and Past Due (PD) rates both blipped up, despite the larger loan denominator. Even bad-der-er, the deterioration was driven by the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs). Asset quality actually improved MoM at the Thrift Bank (TB) and Digibank segments.

System-wide NPL (ex-interbank) ratios were up 6bps MoM to 3.53% (from 3.47% in March). In nominal terms, NPLs jumped 3.4% MoM to P480bn, nearly all of which (>97%) came from UKBs. NPLs for the UKBs were up 8bps MoM to 3.15%.  Surprisingly, NPLs for Digibanks dropped sharply to 17.69% in April, from 25.33% in March (though to be fair there could have been large write-offs…)

Past Due (PD) ratios blipped up 13bps MoM to 4.43%, with the PHP value spiking 5.0% MoM to P618bn. This time, UKBs accounted for 101% of the sector’s PD increase MoM. Worryingly, the PD ratio is now at a near-2yr-high (since May 2022, at 4.44%)

To the upside, Restructured Loans (RLs) continued to improve, down 7bps MoM to 2.08%, with the nominal value down 1.4% MoM.

It is possible that the big MoM jumps in NPL and PD rates were (bizarrely) due to the timing of the Easter holidays this year (28-31 March), causing late recognition of delinquencies. However, the ending balances would not have been affected, and both NPL and PD ratios in April are higher than Feb’s.

While the loan growth numbers are welcome, we would keep a very close eye on asset quality, particularly the PD data.

FDI net inflows up 23% YoY in March, 42% YoY in 1Q24

Foreign direct investments (FDI) posted a net inflow of US$686mn in March, up 23% YoY as non-residents’ net investments into debt instruments increased 19% YoY to US$465mn, while net investments in equity capital (ex-reinvestment) rose 67% to US$157mn.

Equity capital placements were primarily from Japan, Singapore, and USA and these were largely invested in manufacturing, financial/insurance, and real estate industries. This brings 1Q24 FDI to US$3bn, up 42% YoY. Note that the BSP is targeting FDI net inflows of US$9.0bn this year, slightly higher than 2023’s FDI of US$8.9bn.

April trade deficit mildly lower YoY at US$4.76bn

The country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.76bn in April, slightly (-1%) down YoY but was much larger than the US$3.44bn deficit recorded in March. We suspect the sequential rebound in the trade deficit figure was largely due to timing of Holy Week. Note that while most ports are generally open year-round, customs offices are often shut during the holidays.

Imports grew 13% YoY and 15% MoM to US$11bn - most major type of goods accelerated: raw materials (+14%), capital goods (+10%), consumer goods (+16%), and fuel (+8%). Electronic imports also rose by 9% YoY. Meanwhile, exports also grew by 26% YoY and 1.4% MoM to US$6.2bn. This brings YT-April trade deficit to US$16.3bn, lower by 16% YoY.

Vehicle sales rise 5.5% YoY, 7.9% MoM in May

CAMPI reported car sales rose 5.5% YoY, 7.9% MoM to 40,271 units in May, which brings 5M24 car sales up 13% YoY to 187,191 units. CAMPI targets industry car sales to reach 468,300 units (+8% YoY) but also expressed optimism that car sales could reach 500k units this year (+16% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 86,257 units in 5M24, up 12% YoY. This implies that for the month of May alone, TMP sold 18,677 units (+4.5% YoY, +4.3% MoM). To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.4k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 17k units for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).

IMF cuts Philippine growth outlook

IMF now sees PH GDP expanding by 6% (still within the government’s 6%-7% target), lower than their 6.2% forecast in its World Economic Outlook (WEO) in April. They expect growth to then rebound to 6.2% in 2025 thru stronger consumption demand, higher public and private investment, and a recovery in exports. IMF sees PH inflation settling at 3.4% this year (lower than earlier forecast of 3.6%, also lower than BSP’s 3.5%) as they expect that food price inflation will come down faster due to recent tariff amendments. The broader inflation figure is projected to decline towards the target of 3% in the second half of the year.

Rate cut probably after Fed — Recto

The BSP will probably cut its policy rate after US Federal Reserve, signaling to ease monetary policy in December. Finance Secretary Recto says he does not think BSP will cut rates ahead of Fed, and says that it’s “highly probable” that BSP would begin easing once US central bank cuts rates. In contrast, recall that BSP Governor Remolona said that the earliest the central bank can begin cutting rates is August, and sees up to 25-50bps cuts this year.
