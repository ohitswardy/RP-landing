REGIS REPORT

05 July 2024

The PSEi ended the first week of 3Q on a positive note, adding to last week’s +4.12% rally, as general sentiment was supported by optimism for 50bps rate cuts this year, starting with a potential 25bp reduction next month (August 15 BSP meeting). In the US, futures are also currently pricing in up to 2 cuts this year, starting with a first move in September (80% priced in), which should bode well for our own chances of rate cuts at home. Local shares initially drifted through the earlier part of the week while trading on listless volumes, before starting to find support mid-week primarily from net foreign buying, which took the benchmark to the 6,500 level for the first time in a month. However it failed to hold the level through the end of the week as shares succumbed to some late selling during today’s session after trading in the green all day, notwithstanding a dovish CPI print this morning. The PSEi ultimately settled the week a few points below the psychological line at 6,492.75 +1.26% WoW.

On the macro front, in focus was the better-than-expected CPI reading. Data released this morning showed that inflation eased to 3.7% in June, lower than 3.9% forecast and 3.9% prior, helped by a MoM easing in utilities and transport costs. We note that inflation would have been even lower if not for the further increase in food prices, which climbed +6.1% YoY and +0.6% MoM in June. In our view, CPI will likely further ease to the 2% level by August/September, helped by the imminent reduction in rice tariff imports which is supposed to take effect over the weekend. That said, one key risk and a potential factor behind today’s late selling pressure is several farmer groups’ filing of a temporary restraining order (TRO) request against the tariff reduction before the Supreme Court. If granted, this will likely cause a quick rebound in rice prices.

In corporate news, we highlight JFC’s acquisition of a 70% stake in South Korean coffee chain Compose Coffee announced at the earlier part of the week. The deal is expected to be immediately earnings-accretive (by ~5%) and, at 8x EBITDA, should also improve JFC’s multiple which, at a FY2024 basis, is currently at 9x. However, it does raise some concerns about whether JFC management is spreading itself thin, with many things going on at the same time, such as its restructuring activities for some new and underperforming brands. JFC shares capped the week +3.54% WoW, inline with the widespread rally across most consumer names (MONDE +5.11%, PGOLD +2.27% and CNPF +1.09% but URC -1.98%), which appears to be lifted by optimism that the imminent easing in inflation in the latter half of the year will result to increased consumer demand.

Top gainer this week was BDO +8.5% WoW, as it was also the top inflow name, notwithstanding the latest data showing that the NPL ratio in May rose to a 2-year high of 3.57%. Another notable gainer outside of the consumer space was ICT +2.92%, which was the 2nd top inflow stock. Note that BDO and ICT are among the most widely held names in the market, and as such are potentially benefiting from a Philippine proxy angle, as foreign investors start to position with a dovish monetary policy backdrop for the remainder of the year in mind. On the other end, the biggest laggard this week was AEV -4.64%, notwithstanding the company’s continued buy-backs this week, as it succumbed to some profit taking after its sharp +10.7% MoM rise in June. This was followed weakness in other Holding companies LTG -4.62% and GTCAP -4.52%, which marks a continuation in the weakness of the sector since June (-3.5% MoM).

Market activity showed some signs of improvement this week with a daily average turnover of $68m, +25% WoW, but was still significantly lighter than the $96m YTD average. Net foreign flow ended skewed to buy, albeit minimal at $4m. Top bought stocks were BDO +8.27% MoM, ICT +2.92%, SMPH +1.24%, GLO +0.48%, URC +1.98%, SM +2.35% and BLOOM +2%; while top sold were MBT -3.77%, ALI +2.63%, GTCAP -4.52%, AC -2.41% and AEV -4.64%.

Philippine Strategy - Politics - The noise we don't need
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

VP Duterte resigns from Cabinet, setting up mid-term showdown

Vice President Sara Duterte resigned as Secretary of Education on 19 June, formalizing her split from President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. This sets up a battle in the mid-term elections in May 2025, in which former President Rodrigo Duterte is likely to run for Senate, possibly alongside his two sons. We do not believe a potential win by one or more of the Duterte family will have significant impact on government policy, infrastructure plans, etc, much less on company profits. It would, however, give the former President a national platform, with coverage from local and international press. And given the elder Duterte’s penchant for colorful language, the noise could become a distraction that the market doesn’t really need.

What just happened?

Ms Sara Duterte resigned as DepEd Secretary. She stays on as VP, but with no executive function. She also said that her father and her two brothers Sebastian and Paolo could run for Senate in the 2025 mid-terms. While Congress, Governors and Mayors are elected locally, the Senate is a national vote. There are 12 Senate seats (out of 24 total) up for grabs. The number of Duterte family members (and their allies) would be a test of the family’s “brand”, and could be a preview of the 2028 Presidential race. Sara Duterte suggested that her youngest brother Paolo might run for President, while she could return to Davao as Mayor.

No direct impact on policy / macro / earnings

The mid-terms tend to be much less consequential, as the incumbent President will likely retain control of the Lower House, which handles the budget and taxes. As such, we do not expect the outcome to have material impact on economic and monetary policy, or crucially the infrastructure spending program. The outcome is even less likely to affect company earnings outlook.

Noise and more noise – constitution change, treaties, privileged speech

The Duterte family could shift the balance in the Senate, which could derail the current administration’s moves to amend the Constitution. While Congress has the “power of the purse”, the Senate has the power to enter new treaties, or to repudiate existing ones. In 2019, former President Duterte withdrew the Philippines from the International Criminal Court (ICC) in 2019 after it launched an investigation into allegations of killings during Duterte’s drug war. In late 2023, President Marcos, Jr suggested a return to the ICC. The Philippines also has a long-standing Mutual Defense Treaty with the United States. President Duterte was known for his pro-China and anti-US stance.

Members of the Senate, like the Lower House, are immune from arrest for anything said during a privileged speech or as part of debate in the chamber. This would give any winning Duterte family member a national platform that will be covered by the local and potentially the international press. While he was President, Rodrigo Duterte loudly criticized opposition members, as well as US President Obama and even the Pope. A ratcheting up of political noise could be an unwelcome distraction to investors.

JFC to acquire majority stake in Compose Coffee

JFC is acquiring a 70%-stake in South Korean coffee chain Compose Coffee. The remaining stake will be acquired by private equity group Elevation (25%) and Titan Dining II (5%). JFC has a 90% participating interest in the latter. The total consideration is USD340mn (~P20bn), valuing Compose Coffee at 8x trailing EV/EBITDA on a debt-free basis. JFC plans to finance the acquisition through a combination of cash and debt.

Fast-growing leading Korean coffee chain; likely earnings accretive

Compose Coffee was founded in 2014 in Busan, South Korea. It has 2,470 stores today, all of which are franchised. Various news articles report it is among the fastest growing and leading coffee chains in Korea based on store count.

In 2023, the company posted sales of KRW89bn (~P4bn) and net profit of KRW11.5bn (~P500mn). Operating margin was ~40%. Upon consolidation, JFC expects Compose Coffee to add 2% to its revenue and 12% to its EBIT. We estimate it is likely to be earnings accretive (~5%) immediately.

Strategic fit, but some concerns remain

The acquisition further expands JFC’s footprint in South Korea, where it currently operates ~200 CBTL stores. The brand is a fit into one of JFC’s four strategic pillars (coffee and tea), which the company is looking to build on as it aims to grow net profit 3x by 2028. The acquisition, however, does raise some concerns about whether JFC is stretching itself thin management-wise amidst restructuring activities for some recently acquired and underperforming brands.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PSE Index Aug 2024 rebalancing predictions - no additions/deletions

Regis expects no additions/deletions during the August 2024 PSE Index rebalancing. The PSE Index rebalancing in August 2024 will be based on the 12 months from July 2023 to June 2024. Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap. Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when: 1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25, or 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35. Based on our estimates, neither of the two criteria has been met. Based on our preliminary estimates, there will be US$6mn of outflow from BLOOM and US$12mn of inflow to BPI. BLOOM’s weighting will decrease due to a reduction in public float (brought about by the purchase of GGAM’s 8% stake). We forecast BPI’s assigned public float to increase due to the merger with Robinsons Savings Bank.

Converge taps US firm for data center sustainability

Converge ICT Solutions, Inc. announced a partnership with US-based Super Micro Computer, Inc. (Supermicro) to develop energy-efficient data centers aimed at reducing costs and environmental impact. Supermicro’s servers are said to provide exceptional AI computing capabilities and intensive deep learning tasks. With this tie-up, all of Converge’s data centers will be developed in line with Supermicro’s design and process. Converged announced in January its plan to allocate up to P5bn over the next 3 years to build data centers hosting planned digital platforms and store applications and information, located in Pampanga, Laguna, and Caloocan. The two companies recently signed a memorandum of understanding for the development of an AI-powered, “green” data center in the country. Supermicro is an IT solutions provider specializing in data centers and designing energy-saving servers, storage systems, and software.

Meralco-Batelec JV gains traction

Batangas Mayors passed a resolution supporting the partnership between Meralco and Batangas Electric Cooperative Inc (Batelec) 1 and 2. The partnership aims to address the province’s prolonged power outrages and recurring brownouts. The joint venture is expected to improve consumer services and ensure reliable and affordable electricity. Batelec 1 covers one city and 11 municipalities in the western part of Batangas. Batelec 2 provides service to 2 cities and 15 municipalities in the eastern part of Batangas. Batelec 1 and 2 have more than 500,000 consumer members and around 1,671 gigawatt-hours in energy sales.

MGen appoints Emmanuel Rubio as President, CEO

Meralco PowerGen Corp. (MGen) has appointed Emmanuel “Manny” V. Rubio as its president and chief executive officer (CEO), effective July 1. MGen is the wholly-owned power generation unit of Meralco (MER PM). Mr. Rubio’s leadership will help achieve MGen’s goal of expanding its renewable energy capacity by 1,500MW and help the company realize its significant investments including the planned integrated liquefied natural gas (LNG) facility that is currently in the pipeline. Mr. Rubio was the former president and CEO of Aboitiz Power Corp. (AP PM).

Edwin Bautista to step down as UnionBank President on Jan 1

Edwin Bautista will step down as President of UnionBank (UBP PM) on 1 January 2025. He will be replaced by Ana Maria Aboitiz Delgado. She joined UBP as a management trainee in November 2003. Over the course of her career, she led the business development of SME Banking and Retail Consumer Finance, which included credit cards and loans. She led UnionBank’s digital retail banking innovations such as the digital bank branch model and UnionBank Online. In July 2023, SEVP Ana Delgado expanded her role to head the Bank’s Institutional Banking business on top of heading Customer Experience and Digital Channels.

Cebu Air signs MOU with Airbus for 152 aircraft orders

Cebu Air signed an MOU with Airbus for firm orders of 102 A321neo, plus 50 A320neo family purchase rights. Based on list prices, the order will amount to US$24bn (P1.4tn), which will be the largest aircraft order in the history of Philippine aviation. The purchase agreement will be finalized in 3Q24 and the aircraft will be delivered from 2029 onwards.

CEB guided for capex of P48bn this year, which did not include the pre-delivery payments for this order. We await further detail on revised guidance on capex and dividend outlook once the purchase agreement is finalized. As of end-March 2024, CEB had a fleet of 87 aircraft, higher than pre-pandemic level of 74. Company is targeting to fly 24mn passengers this year, higher than pre-pandemic level of 22.5mn.

Century Pacific Food increases capex to P5bn for coconut business

Century Pacific Food’s (CNPF) 2024 capex budget is around P4-5bn, higher than 2023’s P3.5bn. About half will be allotted to increasing their coconut processing capacity by roughly 25%, while the other half will go to maintenance and cost improvement capex. CEO Po said that the company is already in the mid-stages of completing the expansion of its coconut processing and that they have been getting more demand from their principals in the US and SEA. CNPF is currently in the first stage of the expansion, and if market trends continue, they expect to be double their current capacity in 5-7 years from now.

CNPF previously committed USD40mn to expand its coconut processing capacity as it aims to serve both its original equipment manufacturer and domestic coconut business. The commitment is part of an expanded agreement with US-based beverage firm The Vita Coco Company, Inc., which requires approximately 90mn liters of coconut water over the next 5 years.

MPTC, Singapore’s GIC forge US$1bn deal for Indonesian toll stake

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) announced a “strategic investment cooperation” with its subsidiaries and its partner Singapore’s GIC Pte. Ltd. to acquire a 35% stake in Jasamarga Transjawa Tol (JTT) for a valuation of US$1bn. JTT is Indonesia’s state-owned toll road operator consisting of a network of 13 toll roads in West Java, Central Java, and east Java. It currently owns a 676-km tollroad that services 900k vehicles per day (vs. MPTC’s Philippine tollroads, which collectively received 660k daily traffic in 2023). The addition of JTT toll roads will boost MPTC’s portfolio to 1,130km connecting various economic zones in the Philippine and Indonesia.

DALI Everyday Grocery operator widens net loss

Hard Discount Philippines, Inc. (HDPI), the corporate entity behind the DALI discount grocery chain, reported a net loss of P1.88bn in 2023, a wider loss from 2022’s net loss of P894.68mn. HDPI still achieved a revenue of P22.31bn in 2023, a 141% growth YoY (from P9.27bn). Operating expenses jumped by 255% YoY to P3.01bn in 2023 (from P850.39mn), mainly attributed to increased expenditures on salaries, wages, and benefits. As of end-2023, HDPI accumulated losses totaling P3.26bn, resulting in a capital deficit of P1.29bn for the same period. HDPI also disclosed that P4.67bn worth of additional capital was infused by stockholders as of end-2023.

The company forecasts that profit margins will improve over the next 5 years resulting from the implementation of cost efficiency measures. The planned increase in equity, commitment of and continue financial support from parent company, and projected improvement in net margin is seen to generate sufficient cash flows from operations to meet its obligations as and when they fall due. In March, Singapore-based growth equity firm Venturi Partners announced a USD25mn investment to fund the expansion of DALI. DALI seeks to have up to 950 stores across the Philippines by end-2024.

Okada operator no longer interested in acquiring PH Resorts’ Cebu project

Tiger Resort Leisure and Entertainment, which manages the Okada Manila resort, will no longer pursue the Cebu assets of PH Resorts Group Holdings (PHR PM) - the assets pertain to properties as well as gaming license of the upcoming Emerald Bay Resort and Casino. PHR is 77% owned by Udenna Corporation of Davao-based Dennis Uy. Recall that in December 2023, the two parties signed a Term Sheet under which Tiger would acquire the Cebu assets by July 2024. Prior to the agreement with Tiger, PHR previously signed agreements with other potential buyers, namely Bloomberry Resorts in 2022 and Cebu-based developer AppleOne in 2023. A new casino resort in Cebu may not materially impact the competitive landscape (primarily Entertainment City operators) given the much smaller market and niche product offering of the location.

CREC, SMC power arm to boost solar capacity with 153.5-MW plant in Bataan

Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. (CREC) formed a 50:50 joint venture with SMC Global Light and Power Corp. (SGLP), the power arm of San Miguel Corp. (SMC). The JV aims to build a 153.5MW solar power plant in Mariveles, Bataan, which will add 76.75MW to CREC’s attributable solar energy capacity.     CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 285MW. It has a project pipeline of 6,446MW (gross) of solar and 813MW (gross) of onshore wind, and 26MW of hydro plants, which will take overall gross installed capacity of 7,570MW by 2028. The company has allotted approximately P220bn in capex, of which P35bn will be spent in 2024 alone.

DoubleDragon: Ongoing Madrid hotel project tops $10M in sales

DoubleDragon Corp. (DD) said its ongoing hotel project in Madrid, Spain, has achieved sales exceeding USD10mn. According to DD, majority of these sales were recorded in 2Q24. DD anticipates securing over USD100mn (~P5.8bn) in contracted sales over the next 12 months driven by the momentum of its international projects. Hotel101-Madrid commenced construction in March and is set to become one of Madrid’s largest hotels, featuring 680 rooms on a 6,593-square meter property along Avenida Fuerzas Armadas, Valdebebas. Ferrovial Construcción, one of Spain’s largest construction firms, is executing the project, with completion scheduled for 4Q25. The property is surrounded by major landmark buildings and rests nearby train stations and notable establishments including the Madrid Barajas International Airport. Hotel101 is eyeing to have presence in 25 countries by 2026.

DoubleDragon returns to bond market with P10bn offer

DoubleDragon Corp. received the approval from the SEC to sell its 3.5-year retail bond offering with a coupon rate of 8.008%. The offer period will run until July 10 and the listing is set on July 16. DoubleDragon has completed over 1.3mn square meters of diversified asset portfolio consisting of provincial community malls, office buildings, hotels, and warehouse complexes. Its total equity is set to exceed P100bn for the first time this year.

Villar gears up for the next phase of development for Villar City

The 3,500-hectare Villar City was launched in August 2023 and spans 15 cities and municipalities in Metro Manila and Cavite. The development will feature modern districts including a central business district, a tech valley, university town, a premier lifestyle hub, including leisure and recreational facilities. At present, 900 hectares of Villar City have been developed by Vista Land. Last year, the Villar Group opened the 10km, 10-lane Villar Ave. which connects Las Piñas, Bacoor, and the university district in Dasmariñas, Cavite. Note that some portions of Villar City are owned by the Villar family through private companies.

Major energy players eyeing Meralco’s 600-MW supply contract

The government requires distribution utilities to select the cheapest electric supply through a competitive bid. Last month, Meralco initiated the bidding process for a 15-year power supply agreement to fulfill its requirements starting August 26, 2025. 8 companies have expressed interest and participated in the pre-bid conference for the baseload supply on Thursday. The 8 companies include the subsidiaries of First Gen Corp., San Miguel Global Power Holdings Corp., Aboitiz Power Corp., Semirara Mining and Power Corp., and Southwest Luzon Power Generation Corp., and Quezon Power (Philippines) Limited Co. Meralco has also reported a 63% increase in stolen electric meters, totaling to 1,131 incidents from January to June.

Tourist arrivals rise 2% YoY in June; 1H24 arrivals up 12% YoY

Data from the Department of Tourism showed that tourist arrivals for the month of June grew 2% YoY, 3% MoM to 459k, 67% of 2019 average. Korean tourist remained the top source of tourists but saw arrivals dip by 5% YoY in June. Chinese tourists grew 4% YoY, sharply slowing down after rising 94% YoY in 5M24 (likely due to the recent PH imposition of stricter visa requirements and tensions in the West Philippine Sea). This brings 1H24 tourist arrivals to 3.03mn, up 12% YoY or 73% of 2019 average. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY or 93% of 2019 levels. In 2023, tourist arrivals reached 5.5mn or 66% of pre-Covid level.

NCR wage board approves P35 minimum wage hike

The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved a P35 hike in the daily minimum wage of workers in the National Capital Region (NCR). The new minimum wage for non-agri sector in NCR will rise by 5.7% to P645 (from P610), and for agri sector workers by 6.1% to P608 (from P573), effective July 17. This hike was much lower than wage petitions from labor groups, which ranged from P597 to P750. Recall that the government implemented a cumulative wage hike of 13% in NCR in 2022/2023 (vs. cumulative headline inflation of 12%). Gov’t estimates the wage order will directly impact 988,243 minimum wage earners in NCR. We note that the NCR wage hike will likely set the tone for possible wage hikes in other regions in the coming months.

House Speaker favors strict regulation of POGO rather than total ban

House Speaker Martin Romualdez said that a total ban of the POGO industry would only lead to operators going underground and as such he instead prefers strict regulation of the industry. These sentiments were also previously expressed by PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco. Earlier this year, the House Games and Amusements panel approved a bill banning Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators due to crimes linked to the sector. Regis estimate that offshore gaming firms, now called Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL), occupy about 900,000sqm or about 6% of Metro Manila office space.

Delays stall Maharlika’s organizational structure

State-run Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) still has not received approval for its organizational committee, according to MIC president and CEO Rafael Consing. This means that the company still has no manpower to jumpstart its investment portfolio. Mr. Consing initially targeted to complete its organizational structure in 1Q but he hopes the matter to be resolve within four weeks. The Philippine Star reported that the delay is coming from the Department of Finance and the Governance Commission for GOCCs (GCG) - an approval from the GCG and Office of the President is necessary for the paygrade and benefits, among others.

Palace accepts resignations of two Monetary Board members

The resignations of Monetary Board members Anita Linda Aquino and Bruce Tolentino, who were embroiled in a ghost employee scandal, took effective on June 30, 2024. The ghost employees, often political appointees, were included in the BSP payroll despite not performing substantive work. Investigations by the BSP found that there were 4 employees involved and 2 supervisors who had vouched for them. Malacañang has yet to issue a statement regarding the resignations or potential replacements. The Central Bank said the policymaking body would still be able to fulfill its function with the current 5 members.

Bulacan special ecozone bill lapses into law

Republic Act 11999 (RA 11999) or the Bulacan Special Economic Zone and Freeport bill lapsed into law on June 13. This bill creates the Bulacan EcoZone (BuZ) that covers the airport city project of San Miguel Aerocity (subsidiary of San Miguel Holdings) in Bulacan. The Bulacan Special Economic Zone and Freeport Authority (BEZA) will manage the BuZ. The newly-enacted law is seen to create US$200 billion in exports, according to Rep. Joey Salceda, chairman of the House ways and means committee. The project “will create massive revenue gains in income taxes and property taxes, so the forgone revenue from tax incentives will get offset.” The country’s domestic product could be boosted by no less than P130bn annually, land value gains will be over P226bn through the RA. Recall that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. had vetoed the measure creating BuZ in July 2022, citing defects such as the low tax base and lack of coherence with existing laws, etc. Salceda filed a new version in the 19th Congress to correct the deficiencies.

Senator Angara named education chief

Senator Juan Edgardo Angara will take over as the new secretary of Department of Education (DepEd) after VP Sara Duterte’s resignation from her post takes effect on July 19. According to the Office of the President, Senator Angara pushed “significant educational reforms” since joining the Senate in 2013, which includes a law that added 2 more years to secondary education in a bid to make Filipino students “globally competitive” (i.e. K-12 program). Filipino students were still among the world’s weakest in math, reading and science, ranking 77th out of 81 countries and performing worse than the global average in all categories. 9 in 10 Filipino students aged 10 can’t read basic text. Angara promised a comprehensive review of the K-12 program amid public pressure to either revise or scrap it.

CPI eases in June

CPI inflation figure for the June was 3.7%, down from 3.9% in May and is lower than Street predictions. Lower energy costs helped trim the YoY CPI inflation with MoM declines in utilities costs as well as transport costs. CPI inflation would have been lower if not for a further rise in food prices. The imminent reduction in rice import tariffs (and thus rice prices) should help drive the headline inflation number even lower in the next couple of months.

Banks’ NPL ratio soars to near two-year high

The banking industry’s nonperforming loan (NPL) ratio rose to 3.57% in May from 3.45% in April and 3.46% in 5M23, matching 7M22 ratio of 3.57%. May’s ratio is also the highest in 23 months (3.6% in 6M22). NPLs rose to P481bn +13/7% YoY / +3.1% MoM in May, while loan portfolio +9.3% / -0.3% MoM as of end-May to P13.9tn.

Past due loans dropped 1.6% MoM in May (from P618.05bn to P608.07bn). It YoY rise was 15.7% (from P525.51bn). Past due ratio in May was 4.37%, lower than 4.43% in April but higher than the 4.17% ratio in 5M23.

Restructured loans stood at P295.89bn, up 1.9% MoM from P290.37bn but down 4.6% YoY from P310.3bn. Higher than April’s 2.08%, but lower than 2.46% 5M23. These borrowings accounted for 2.13% of the industry’s total loan portfolio.

Banks’ loan reserves inched up by 0.7% MoM in May (from P471.35bn to P474.88bn). Its YoY rise was 6.9% (from P444.03bn). Loan loss reserve ratio was up at 3.42% from April’s 3.37%, but lower than the 5M23 3.52%. Lenders’ NPL coverage ratio slipped to 95.81% from 98.07% in April, and 101.81% in 5M23.

Government debt rises to P15.35tn in May

The national government’s outstanding debt reached P15.35tn in end-May, up 2.2% MoM (+P330bn). The increase is largely due new debt issuances and the impact of local currency depreciation on FX debt. Domestic debt increased by 1.3% MoM (+P134bn) to P10.44tn largely due to the net issuance of government securities. While external debt rose to P4.9tn, up 4.2% MoM (+P196bn) due to net foreign loan issuances and the upward revaluation of USD debt. For 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio is expected to settle at 60.6%, slightly higher than 2023’s 60.2%. After 2024, the government aims to gradually lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 56% by 2028.
