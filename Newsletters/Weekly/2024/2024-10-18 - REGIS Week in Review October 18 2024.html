REGIS REPORT

18 October 2024

The PSEi bounced back this week, +1.44% WoW to 7,415.73, posting its 6th weekly gain in the last 7 weeks as local shares were supported by renewed foreign buying amid the BSP’s decision to trim rates for the 2nd meeting in a row and a positive start to the earnings season, kicked off by BPI +3.25% WoW. Furthermore, we also had the Wisdom Tree rebalancing this week, which involved mostly inflows into mid-caps like RLC +2.9% and PGOLD +3.13%. Despite these notable events on the ground however, market activity further eased to a daily average of $102m, -8% WoW. Note that volumes during Monday’s session was particularly light at only $67m, which marked the slowest session in 2 months.

On the policy front, the Monetary Board trimmed the benchmark rate by another 25bps to 6%, inline with expectations. The decision came despite the growing uncertainty over the rate outlook in the US where futures are now pricing in a 43bp cumulative reduction in the benchmark rate by the end of the year, which suggests some level of uncertainty over the Fed ability to cut in each of their remaining policy meetings this year, as more economic data during the week signaled strength in the US economy. As such, with the rate cut at home the PHP to weakened for the 4th week in a row, -0.55% WoW to 57.525, albeit off its intraweek low of 57.85. Meanwhile, the yield on the 10-year Treasury bonds was virtually steady, -2.3bps WoW to about 5.74%.

Breadth of the market was positive with over a third of index members posting gains of above 2%. Leading the way this time were the smaller/mid caps such as WLCON +8.28% WoW which recovered from its sharp -7.32% slump last week, CNPF +6.03% which hit a fresh record high, and CNVRG +5.43%. Among the larger caps, lenders were a bright spot with BDO +4.79%, BPI +3.25% and MBT +2.29% after BPI kicked off the 3Q/9M reporting season with stellar numbers. BPI’s 9M net income surged +24% WoW to P48b, ahead of both Regis and Street forecasts (already 81% of Regis FY24 forecast.) Furthermore, outlook on lenders for 4Q continues to be positive, not least as that the September-announced 250bp RRR cut is set to officially take effect next week (October 25). Another notable gainer this week was MER +4.26% which hit a fresh record high during the week.

On the opposite end, biggest laggard in the benchmark was NIKL -5.42% WoW, extending losses after last week’s -8% slump. Another notable laggard was URC -2.96% which trailed behind the widespread strength among its consumer peers, dragged down by net foreign selling.

RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy / Consumer - Rice is Life (4) – Local prices finally falling; consumption boost into 2025E
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph | Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Lower prices; rate cuts, wage hikes to boost consumption in 4Q24/1H25

Prices of locally-grown rice have fallen 5-6% in the past two weeks, finally following regional trends, but not yet fully reflecting recent tariff cuts. As we had predicted, this helped drive CPI inflation below 2% in September, giving the Bangko Sentral (BSP) more than enough room for further policy rate cuts in its Oct/Dec meetings. Meanwhile, minimum wages are being raised 6-13%. All of this bodes well for a much-needed rebound in consumer demand, particularly among lower-income households. Momentum should pick up from the 4Q24 holiday season, and carry into 1H25 with mid-term election spending.

Local rice prices down 6%, more downside to come?

Data from the Department of Agriculture shows that retail market prices of locally grown rice fell by as much as 6% as of 10 Oct (a low of P44/kg), compared to end-Sept, and are now down some 10-12% from the peak in Jan. This is broadly consistent with the 11-12% ytd drop in export prices out of Vietnam and Thailand. However, this suggests that rice traders are not yet passing on the July cut in tariffs from 35% to 15%. We estimate that, if both the export prices and the tariff cut were fully passed onto consumers, the price of rice should be another c10-15% lower than where it is today.

BSP policy rates – room for further cuts next week, and in December

Even at current rice prices, we see further downward pressure on inflation, which came in at 1.9% in Sept, already below the BSP’s target range of 2-4%. Falling prices, and near-record-high real interest rates should give significant elbow room for further easing.

Minimum wage hikes, election spending to further boost for consumption

In July, the minimum wage in Metro Manila was increased by 5.7% to P645/day. Since then, wage hikes of between 6% and 13% were granted in Central Luzon (just north of Manila), Calabarzon (areas just south and east of Manila), and Central Visayas (including Cebu). The Department of Labor and Employment expects the rest of the country to grant increases by year-end.

Both the drop in rice prices and the wage hikes are especially beneficial for lower-income households. While rice is 8.9% of the CPI basket, the staple accounts for 17.9% of CPI for the “bottom 30%” of the population.

We reiterate our bullishness on a recovery in consumption spending, having cited rice prices as a catalyst since early this year. The timing is ideal, as we head into the traditionally strong holiday season in 4Q24E. Momentum could carry well into 2025E, what with election spending for the mid-terms in May. The YoY numbers could look particularly good given the low base year-to-date. While overall consumption should see a boost, we’d expect companies like Universal Robina (URC PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), Century Pacific (CNPF PM), and Puregold (PGOLD PM) to benefit more based on the demographic they serve.

BPI (BPI PM) 9M24 NPAT jumped 24% to P48.0bn

BPI (BPI PM) reported in a press release that 9M24 NPAT jumped 24% to P48.0bn, handily beating Regis (81% of FY24E P59.4bn) and consensus (P61.2bn) estimates. The main source of the outperformance was a tripling YoY of trading gains to P3bn. But performance was solid nearly across the board. Loans grew 18.9% YoY (c12% ex-Robinsons Bank), driven by high margin products like personal loans (+129%), “business banking” (+88%), and microfinance (+67%). BPI reckons NIMs expanded 22bps YoY for 9M24 to 4.29%. We estimate this translates to around 4.35% for 3Q24 alone. Opex was kept in check (+22%), leading to a 27% increase in PPOP.

Asset quality deteriorated slightly, consistent with the bank’s push into consumer loans. NPL ratio was up 10bps QoQ to 2.30% while NPL coverage dropped 17ppts to 111% from 128% in 2Q24. Provision expense jumped 60% to P4.8bn, with 3Q24 alone at P1.8bn, compared to P1.5bn per quarter in 1Q and 2Q.

Overall a solid set of results. We expect more detail from the earnings call, scheduled for Tuesday, 22 October at 4pm.

Congestion in Manzanillo, Mexico causes 3Q24 volume slowdown in ICTSI’s Mexico port

Data from the government of Mexico showed that volumes in ICTSI’s Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) port rose by 3% YoY to 120k TEUs in September on lower transshipment volumes. Industry volumes in Manzanillo fell 6% YoY during the month of September due to lower transshipment volumes. This brings 3Q24 volumes in CMSA up by 2% YoY, 8% QoQ to 367k TEUS; growth rate slowed sharply from +20% YoY pace in 1H24. Industry-wide volumes fell 2% YoY and was flat QoQ to 976k TEUs.

Note that ICTSI opened its new container yard in Mexico in end-September. This should prompt a rebound in CMSA’s volume growth in 4Q, given that the new container yard boosts the port’s annual capacity by 17% to 1.7mn TEUs from 1.45mn TEUs. At full build-out by end-2025, ICT estimates the port’s capacity could increase by 40-45% from 1.45mn TEUs in September 2024. Note that ICT is the only port in Manzanillo that has available land for expansion.

9M24 volumes in CMSA is now up by 13% YoY to 1.065mn TEUs. Regis forecasts CMSA’s volumes will grow by 12% YoY to 1.43mn TEUs in 2024, suggesting that volumes for the rest of the year only need to average 120k per month to reach our forecast. Regis estimates CMSA to be ICT’s largest port today, accounting for 11% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA.

Okada 3Q24 GGR falls 8% QoQ and 34% YoY

The 3Q24 gross gaming revenue (GGR) of Tiger Resorts, the operator of Okada Manila, fell 8% QoQ and 34% YoY to P8.2bn. Although VIP rolling, mass table drop, and gaming machine handle all increased QoQ, the VIP win rate and the mass table hold rate fell to unusually low levels. Volumes of every category fell YoY.

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) sets P6bn capex for 2025, eyes 500 store openings

SEVN is looking to open another 500 stores next year, allocating P5bn-P6bn in capex. 200 branches will be in Visayas and Mindanao while 150 will be in Metro Manila and the remainder will be in other parts of Luzon. SEVN opened its 4,000th store on Wednesday and hopes to end the year with 4,100 outlets. Currently, 52% of stores are corporate-owned and 48% are franchise-operated. SEVN is targeting to change the mix to 60% franchise-owned in the medium term. By 2026, SEVN wants to reach 5,000 branches. Regis is forecasting 450-500 net store additions per year.

DMCI declares special dividend of P0.48/sh

DMC declared a special cash dividend of P0.48/sh, better than REGIS’ forecast of P0.18/sh. Ex-dividend date is on Oct 29, record date Oct 30, and payment date Nov 15. This brings cumulative dividends for 2024 to P1.20/sh, equivalent to a yield of ~10%, but lower than the P1.44/sh dividend paid in 2023.

Bloomberry refinances P72bn of debt

Bloomberry refinanced P72bn worth of debt. The previous loans had an interest rate of c. 7.5%. The new syndicated refinancing facility will be priced at a spread that is 75 basis points lower than the previous facilities. This should mean annual savings of c. P540mn (equivalent to 5% of FY23 net profit). BLOOM will also have the option to fix the interest rate within the next 12 months.   If the loans had not been refinanced, BLOOM would have been required to pay P72bn over the next four years (2028). The new syndicated refinancing facility has a term of 10 years (until October 2034) and the principal payments will be back-ended. This should slightly increase the likelihood of BLOOM paying dividends. The company’s recently announced dividend policy is to pay 35% of previous year’s earnings as dividends.

Semirara clarifies planned P291bn coal mining investment

Semirara indicated that the estimated project cost of P291bn covers capex, production costs, and operating expenses over a five-year period. Estimates are subject to change, depending on various factor, including operational efficiencies and approval from the Department of Energy. The expansion is based on the discovery of additional coal resource and reserve of 90mn MT and 72mn MT, respectively, from the Acacia pit. Acacia is located northwest and part of the upthrown block relative to Molave pit. SCC expects to start coal mining operations at the Acacia prospect in late 2025. Currently, SCC is operating the Narra mine, whose coal reserves (45mn MT) are expected to be depleted by 2026.

Puregold plans expansion in 5 provinces

Puregold (PGOLD PM) said it is planning to expand its presence in the Philippines to at least five provinces. The prospective areas include Iloilo, Davao, Cavite, Bulacan, and Metro Manila. Puregold is aiming to strengthen its presence in key growth regions, paving the way for a broader market share and investment opportunities. Puregold stated that its concerted focus on regional markets, particularly in Visayas and Mindanao is part of a larger strategy to drive sustainable growth. As of the 2Q24, Puregold has a total of 581 stores nationwide. This includes 498 Puregold stores, 28 S&R membership shopping warehouses, and 55 S&R New York-style QSRs. Regis is forecasting 30 new stores every year.

Airlines seeks approval to implement new fee to cover higher NAIA fees

Domestic carriers, Philippine Airlines (PAL), Cebu Air (CEB PM), and Air Asia Philippines (AAP) are asking the Civil Aeronautics Board to approve the collection of a “terminal enhancement fee” to help recover the increasing cost of flying in NAIA. Domestic flights are sought to have a terminal enhancement fee of P75 per way. PAL and CEB sought a P300 fee for foreign flights while AAP sought for a P275 fee. The new private operator of NAIA has doubled, if not tripled, airport costs since Oct 1; Landing and takeoff fees alone have increased by 400%. CEB said that the hike is costing the company P9.3mn per day or P3.4bn a year, equivalent to 43% of its 2023 profit. Proposed terminal enhancement fee only covers <50% of costs, as it does not have plans to pass on the entire cost to sustain travel demand. CAB is scheduled to hold a board meeting toward the end this month to discuss the proposals for a terminal enhancement fee.

Meralco signs MOU with Samsung C&T for nuclear energy development

Meralco signed the MOU with Samsung C&T Corporation who will share design capabilities for nuclear technology. The agreement includes a pilot demonstration project for nuclear power. Note that nuclear power provides over 20% of South Korea’s electricity. Meralco has shown interest in pursuing nuclear energy projects. Regis believes that any such nuclear power projects are likely to be commercially available only in the next decade (if ever).

EDC eyes P25bn expansion of Negros steam field project

Energy Development Corp. (EDC) plans to allocate P25bn for the expansion of its 282.5-megawatt (MW) Southern Negros Geothermal Project (SNGP) in Negros Oriental. This covers the cost of drilling new wells, the expansion of existing pads, and the construction of new pads, road networks, pipeline routes and other support structures, as necessary. The estimated amount also includes the budget for emerging technologies.

SNGP currently has a total dependable capacity of 221.8 MW due to steam availability constraints of one unit (Nasuji geothermal plant). The company is currently applying for an Environment Compliance Certificate amendment for the steamfields of the SNGP. It is proposing a change in the boundary/shape of the development block and a reduction from 5,163 hectares to 4,027.59 hectares. At present, EDC has an installed capacity of 1,480.19 MW, accounting for about 20% of the country’s total installed renewable energy capacity.

SEC approves MREIT-MEG property-for-share swap

The SEC approved the property-for-share swap agreement between Megaworld and MREIT. MREIT will acquire six Megaworld office buildings via a property-for-share swap. MREIT will issue 926.16mn new shares to Megaworld at P14.20/share for a total transaction value of P13.15bn. This will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 33%. Megaworld’s stake in MREIT will rise from 51.3% to 63.4%. The buildings have a combined leasable area of 156,600sqm and have an aggregate occupancy rate of 94% (similar to the 95% occupancy of MREIT’s existing portfolio): McKinley West, Fort Bonifacio – 94,300sqm, Iloilo Business Park, Iloilo City– 36,200sqm, Davao Park District, Davao City – 26,100sqm. The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 48% to 480,000sqm.

SMC, Ayala Land breaks ground on Ayala Greenfield Interchange project

San Miguel and Ayala Land, through Ayala Greenfield Development Corp. will commence construction of the Ayala Greenfield Interchange project next month. The project will connect South Luzon Expressway (SLEx) Toll Road 3 (TR3) to local roads while enhancing access to both SLEx and the STAR Tollway, and is expected to be completed in about a year. Ayala Greenfield Development is a joint venture between Ayala Land and the Greenfield Group, the developer of the 550-hectare premier residential Greenfield Estates in Laguna. SMC is currently conducting the expansion of SLEx (SLEX Toll Road 4), which hit 70% completion as of August and is expected to be finished by the end of the year. TR4 is expected to cut travel time between Sto. Tomas Batangas and Lucena to 45mins from the usual three hours.

Toll Regulatory Board approves implementation of toll hike for NLEX Connector Road

The Toll Regulatory Board has authorized NLEX Corp. to start collecting higher toll fees for the P23bn Connector Road project starting on October 15. This follows the completion of Section 2 (España to Magsaysay Boulevard) last October 2023. Toll fees for Class 1 vehicles (regular cars and SUVs) will increase to P119 from P86 (+38%). NLEX is a 76%-owned unit of Metro Pacific, which is a 20%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM). Tollroads account for 5% of GTCAP’s profit (MPI accounts for 14% of GTCAP’s gross profit before parent expenses).

Philex Mining seeks permit for nickel exploration in Pangasinan

Philex Mining Corp. is awaiting a permit to explore a potential nickel mining site in Pangasinan as part of its strategic goal to diversify its mineral extraction portfolio, the mining company’s president said. Nickel is a crucial mineral for the production of electric vehicle batteries and stainless steel. The Philippines is the world’s second-largest producer of nickel ore, following Indonesia. The President and CEO, Eulalio Austin Jr., said the company remains on track to commence commercial operations at its Silangan gold and copper mine by early 2025. Philex Mining primarily engages in the large-scale exploration, development, and utilization of mineral resources. The company operates the Padcal gold and copper mine in Tuba, Benguet Province, and the Silangan Project in Surigao del Norte.

Monetary Board delivers widely expected policy rate cut of 25bps

The BSP’s Monetary Board reduced its polity rate by 25bps to 6.0%, in line with consensus expectations. BSP reduced its risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2024 to 3.1% (from 3.3% previously). It mildly raised its risk-adjusted inflation forecasts for 2025 and 2026 to 3.3% and 3.7%, citing potentially higher electricity prices and impact of higher minimum wages.

Given the better-than-expected inflation forecast for the year, Governor Remolona said another 25bps reduction during its last meeting in December is possible. Furthermore, Governor Remolona did not rule out a 100bps easing for 2025 but admitted that such magnitude is on the dovish side. Regis expects the BSP to cut its policy rate by another 25bps in December and 75bps in 2025. Jefferies expects the FED to reduce policy rates by 25bps in November and 25bps in December. For 2025, JEFF expects the Fed to cut rates by another 100bps.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in September

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 15bps MoM to 20.4% (in September) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index expanded 21bps MoM to 25.2%. Net foreign buying increased to US$346mn, from US$143mn in August. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 18 saw increases in foreign ownership while 11 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

ALI – foreign ownership rose 51bps MoM to 28.1%. Net foreign buying hit US$35.3mn, the third highest for the month.  

MBT – foreign ownership increased 48bps MoM to 22.1% while net foreign buying amounted to US$27.7mn, the fifth highest for the month.

JFC – foreign ownership expanded 47bps MoM to 23.8%.  Net foreign selling reached US$18.6mn, the eighth highest for the month.

SMC - foreign ownership fell 42bps MoM to 4.9%. It also experienced the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$16.1mn), due to its deletion from the FTSE mid-cap index.

Remittances rise 3.2% YoY in August

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 3.2% YoY to US$2.89bn in August, mildly accelerating from 3.1% rate in July. In PHP terms, remittances rose by 5% YoY, moderating from the 6.5% pace in YT-July due to PHP appreciation. The change in school calendar (start of school year varied between July/August this year vs Aug/Sep last year) could have still distorted the growth rate. This brings YT-August remittances up by 2.9% YoY to US$22.2bn. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% YoY in 2024 (to ~US$34bn).

Growth in vehicle sales moderates to 2.4% YoY in September; Toyota sales rise 5.7%

Vehicle sales rose by 2.4% YoY, 1.0% MoM to 39,542 units in September, according to CAMPI/TMA. CAMPI attributed the slowdown to absence of new vehicle models, as auto companies are likely preparing for the upcoming Philippine International Motor Show in October. This brings 9M24 vehicle sales up 9.4% YoY to 344,307 units.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) recorded unit sales of 159,088 units in 9M24, up 10.3% YoY, which implies sales volumes for September alone stood at 18,434 units, up 5.7% YoY and 2.9% MoM. To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), sales should average 18.6k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ FY24E target of 206k units (+3%), sales should average 15.6k units per month for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (i.e. before parent expenses).

MGB to submit draft EO to revamp mining laws

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources’ (DENR) Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) is preparing a draft Executive Order (EO) which aims to address some of the conflicting interpretation of the law on mining royalty and complexities with LGUs and indigenous peoples and indigenous cultural communities, according to DENR Undersecretary for Integrated Sciences Carlos Primo David. The EO will be setting a ceiling for royalties, as the current law only sets a minimum royalty.

The Chairman of the Chamber of Mines of the Philippines, Mike Toledo, urges the government to implement the following policies: Clarify the role of national and local government units and specify the approach to indigenous community management; simplify and expedite the approval processes for mineral agreements; and establish a stable and predictable mining fiscal regime including adding a “financial stability clause” in all mineral agreements to guarantee continuity of mining operations and to assure the sanctity of contracts, regardless of material adverse change in government policies that would prevent mining contractors from performing their obligations.
