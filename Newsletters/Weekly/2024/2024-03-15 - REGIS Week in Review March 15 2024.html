REGIS REPORT

15 March 2024

The market fell 1.7% WoW to 6,822 as investors started to take profit following the recent rally.  

Losers outnumbered gainers 2:1.

Most index names fell on the back of disappointing earnings, along with the FTSE rebalancing last Friday. Estimated outflows were at US$176m as EMI (-9.1% WoW), SMC +1.9% WoW), AGI (+2.2% WoW), SECB (2.6% WoW), and MWC (2.8% WoW) were deleted from the index.

Large cap AC dragged the market as FY23 income of P38bn, +38% YoY fell behind expectations. The results were affected by more write-offs (impairment charges, et al) in 4Q, this time on Manila Water (MWC PM), Yoma and Zalora. Fortunately, management is finally taking steps to clean up / divest the nonprofitable assets.  This will hopefully set the stage for management’s challenging target of reach P65bn in ‘core income’ by 2026.

Consumer names also saw weakness across the sector. JFC (-2.5% WoW) and URC (-4.3% WoW) fell as profits came in weak. Jollibee Foods reported headline net profit of P8.8bn (+16% YoY), behind Regis estimates but in line with consensus expectations. SSSG decelerated in 4Q23 across most markets, except China and CBTL. This may have contributed to the contraction in operating margins during the quarter, despite gross margin expansion. Meanwhile, URC (-4.3% WoW) results likewise came in slow, as soft demand persisted, resulting in flat Philippines branded consumer food (BCF) sales in 4Q23. A notable improvement in margins, particularly for international BCF, helped lift 4Q EBIT (+7% YoY) despite the soft top line. Management is guiding for mid-high single digit top line growth in FY24 and continued margin improvement.

On the power sector, ACEN (-7.4% WoW) reported P7.4bn profit which was well below forecasts. Income for 4Q23 alone, which was devoid of any non-operational gains (one-off items), came in P830m. While the operating numbers are disappointing because of delays on new projects, the risk still lies on the company’s ability to execute its project pipeline (and also operate them) according to plan. The company has attributable capacity of 2,608MW at the start of the year and should be able to add another ~1,700MW by year’s end, an increment of over 65% based on their goals.  On the other hand, Nickel Asia (-2.8% WW) FY23 net profit fell 53% YoY to P3.7bn, also behind both Regis and consensus estimates. This was after (a) mining ASPs disappointed and (b) the HPAL plants recorded larger-than-expected equitized losses of P1bn. Derived 4Q23 profit was only P54mn, with the HPAL plants incurring P620mn worth of equitized losses during the quarter. 

Top index gainers for the week were ICT (+5.4% WoW) and GT Capital (-0.3% WoW). ICT posted stellar results as FY23 core profit before perps rose 8% to $648m, ABOVE consensus and even Regis’ street high forecasts. Notable that organic volume growth accelerated from just 0.7% in 9M to 4.3% in 4Q while yields grew another 2% last quarter. The company also declared P11/sh div (P9.35 regular + P1.65 special), implying 3.8% yield. GT Capital also announced dividends totaling P8.00/share, P5.00 of which will paid next month (April 12).

On corporate news, Belle Corp (+44.5% WoW) made a tender offer for all outstanding shares of its 80%-owned subsidiary, Premium Leisure Corp (+21.9% WoW). PLC holds the license for City of Dream Manila and has an EBITDA sharing arrangement with Melco Resorts Philippines.

Headline outflows registered at US$55.5m.

Philippine Property - 4Q23 recap: residential presales decline for 3rd consecutive quarter
 
 
by Carl Sy, Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential presales decline for 3rd consecutive quarter
The decline in 4Q23 residential presales of nearly every developer was eye-catching, and the companies under our coverage were almost unanimously cautious on the segment. On the other hand, mall revenue continued climbing on a QoQ and YoY basis while the POGO sector
provided some support to the office business.

Residential presales fall 14% QoQ; gap between preselling and RFO widens
Aggregate reservation sales of the  developers we cover fell 14% QoQ and 6% YoY. After exceeding pre-COVID peaks for two consecutive quarters, reservation sales are now back down to just 75% of the 2019 average. Unsurprisingly, the property firms sounded cautious on the segment, with most developers planning
to manage unsold inventory levels by launching less projects in 2024.

Colliers estimates that the gap between preselling and secondary market prices has widened over the past few years (see Figure 4). After falling during the early part of the pandemic, secondary market prices of CBD condominiums have recovered and are now just 5%-15% below pre-COVID levels. However, prices of pre-selling condominiums have climbed to 10%-30% above 2019 levels. As a result, Colliers estimates that prices of condominium units under preselling are 40%-80% higher than secondary market prices. 

The mortgage NPL ratio fell 17 basis points QoQ to 6.9%. The ratio has fallen 240 basis points over the past two years while the absolute value of nonperforming mortgages has declined 17% over the period.

POGO office demand returns; work-from-home Bill a risk to the sector
Office vacancy fell 1ppt QoQ to 17%, thanks in part to POGO office demand. While office take-up from the POGO sector fizzled in 3Q, we saw a bit of a rebound in 4Q. POGO office demand was largely concentrated in the Manila Bay area and likely contributed to the 20% land price  appreciation in the vicinity in 2023.

Over the year, some office tenants renewed contracts at lower rental rates. We expect more such cases in 2024,  since 1) spot office rental rates are currently 10%-50% below 2019 levels, and 2) many of the contracts up for renewal this year were previously signed in 2019, when rental rates were close to the peak.

A key risk to the office segment is the proposed legislation seeking to allow BPO firms to avail of tax incentives even if employees are fully work-from-home. The impact to the sector could be large since BPO companies occupy 50%-80% of the office space of the developers we cover.

Mall revenue still grows QoQ and YoY
Mall revenue of the top 3 mall operators grew another 8% QoQ (+13% YoY) in 4Q23 to P27.7bn, 6% higher than the 4Q19 level. However, now that revenue is above pre-COVID levels, we expect YoY growth figures to slow down. We forecast 2024 same-mall revenue growth of 6%, within the historical range of 5%-8%.

 
Robinsons Land - FY23 profit ahead; presales of standalone projects fall for 2nd quarter
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA | Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY23 net profit rises 24%, ahead of REGIS and consensus
Robinsons Land’s FY23 net profit rose 24% to P12.1bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Mall EBIT (+94% YoY) was in line with our forecast but all other segments outperformed. We note that both RLC’s FY23 EBIT and net profit were already slightly ahead of our FY24 estimates.

Presales of standalone projects fall for second consecutive quarter
Similar to the experience of many other developers, presales of RLC’s standalone residential projects fell for a second consecutive quarter. 4Q23 presales fell 17% QoQ (down 37% YoY) to P4.0bn. For the full-year, presales grew 26% to P21.3bn, slightly above the FY19 level.

Management appears slightly cautious on the residential segment. When asked about FY24 launches, management declined to provide a number but said that the earliest launch would be in late 2Q. Since RLC has about 16 months’ worth of inventory (5% of which is RFO), it will still have sufficient units to sell until 2Q.

JV residential projects perform well
4Q23 presales of JV residential projects (which are higher-end than RLC’s standalone projects) jumped 83% QoQ (235% YoY) to P6.9bn. As a result, FY23 JV presales climbed 117% to P19.5bn, similar to the FY19 level. We estimate that unsold residential inventory of the JV projects represents 18 months’ worth of presales. The unsold inventory, coupled with another upcoming JV project in Bridgetowne (likely in 2025), should provide sufficient time for the company to finalize its plans for its new Fort Bonifacio property.

Fort Bonifacio land acquired via JV
RLC said that it will jointly develop a 33-hectare land parcel in Fort Bonifacio with SM Prime. This property would allow RLC to continue launching high-end residential JV projects (with Shang Properties and HK Land) after the current projects are nearly sold out.

Mall profitability above pre-COVID levels
FY23 mall EBIT jumped 94% to P5.85bn, in line with our forecast and 13% above the FY19 level. Profitability of the mall business improved due to lower power costs and the removal of tenant rental concessions in 4Q22. As a result, FY23 mall EBIT margin expanded 13ppt to 36%.

Office vacancy stays flat QoQ
Office vacancy was flat QoQ at 13%, lower than the industry average of 17%. However, RLC’s upcoming office buildings, which will expand floor area by 11%, are only 19% preleased. Thus, the company’s vacancy rate could rise to as high as 20% later this year if no new tenants are signed.

 

ACEN FY23 profit of P7.4bn below expectations
  • ACEN reported net income of P7.4bn for FY23, down 43% YoY and below estimates.  This is well below both Regis and Street estimates.  The profit decline from P13.1bn was due to the large one-off (P8.9bn) recorded the prior year relating to the partial sale of Indonesian geothermal assets and the accompanying remeasurement gain. Nonetheless, the underlying results for FY23 do appear weak relative to expectations.  Derived 4Q23 income was only P830m.  While this was up more than 2x on a recurring basis from the prior year, it looks poor in comparison to 9M23.
  • Attributable capacity now at 2,608MW, with another 2,245MW on the way. ACEN claims attributable capacity of over 4,800MW, of which 2,608MW is already operational.  Given the frequent experience of delays, we are rather hesitant to count the chickens that have yet to hatch.  In addition, employing the 4,800MW figure to calculate EV/capacity ratios without an accurate projection of debt to complete the projects in the future would lead to misleading conclusions. Management does claim, however, that ~1,700MW of the new capacity will be fully online within this year.
  • ACEN has a professed target to have 5,000MW of attributable capacity (both under construction and operational) by 2025.  This aim should not be hard to beat given where they are today.
ACEN strikes deal with Australian firm for clean electricity

ACEN Corp.'s wholly-owned subsidiary in Australia has signed an eight-year power purchase agreement (PPA) with SmartestEnergy Australia. Offtake will be 25% output of ACEN Australia's 400MW New England Solar project, which is currently still in development and is planned for 720MW of total capacity. SmartestEnergy Australia said the renewable energy purchased from ACEN will be made available to commercial and industrial customers throughout Australia. 

SEC clears OceanaGold (Philippines) IPO

The SEC has cleared the P7.88bn IPO of OceanaGold (Philippines) Inc. Up to 456mn common shares owned by selling shareholder OceanaGold (Philippines) Holdings Inc. will be offered to the public at a price of up to P17.28 per share.

OceanaGold is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Canada-listed OceanaGold Corp. The company operates the Didipio gold and copper mine in the Nueva Vizcaya province. The mine has reserves of 1.1mn ounces of gold and 140k MT of copper as of end-2023 (estimated mine life of 10 years). The ongoing underground “optimization work” at the mine has the potential to increase production of larger volumes of higher-grade ore and an increase in the mine life.

The IPO is targeted to run from April 22 to 26, with listing on the PSE scheduled for May 7.

SC backs CA ruling on PLDT worker regularization

The Supreme Court (SC) upheld a decision of the Court of Appeals (CA), which had found that the Labor department acted improperly in ordering the regularization of PLDT Inc. support staff. The CA ruled that regularizing contractual workers should be the discretion of management (i.e. janitorial, clerical, information technology, back-office support, call center, sales, and other such services).

Former Labor Secretary Silvestre H. Bello III had ordered PLDT to grant regular status to 7,344 workers. The SC affirmed the CA finding that Mr. Bello had committed grave abuse of discretion in issuing his resolutions. The SC held that labor contracting is not illegal per se, as management enjoys some leeway in deciding which functions can be performed by regular workers.

Robinsons Land FY23 net profit rises 24%, above

Robinsons Land’s FY23 earnings rose 24% to P12.1bn, above REGIS and consensus forecasts. FY23 EBIT and earnings are slightly above REGIS FY24E estimates. FY23 mall EBIT rose 94% to P5.85bn, in line with our forecast and 13% above the FY19 level. Operating profit of every other segment outperformed our expectations.

  • 4Q23 mall revenue rose 11% QoQ (+17% YoY).
  • Similar to many other developers, reservation sales of RLC’s standalone projects fell for the second consecutive quarter. 4Q23 presales of standalone projects fell 17% QoQ and 37% YoY to P4.0bn. However, 4Q23 presales of JV projects jumped 83% QoQ and 235% to P6.9bn.
  • FY23 presales of standalone projects rose 26% to P21.bn while presales of JV projects climbed 117% to P19.5bn. These are both at 2019 levels (or slightly higher)

Office vacancy was flat QoQ at 13%

NPLs, PD ratios blip up in January -- partly cosmetic?
  • Asset quality seems to have taken a turn for the worse in January 2024. However, this is suspected to be partly due to sizeable write-offs done in December 2023, particularly in the credit card portfolio, which saw big MoM improvements in asset quality ratios. Evidence for this can be gleaned from the sharp drop in loan loss reserves (LLR) ratio from 3.46% in November, to 3.30% in December, only to bounce back to 3.45% in January. When compared to November 2023, the asset quality data for January is flattish.
  • NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was 3.53% in January, up 21bps MoM, while Past Due (PD) ratio jumped 31bps MoM to 4.27%. But when compared to November, the NPL ratio was up just 4bps, while the PD ratio was up 5bps. Restructured Loan (RL) ratio, was up 8bps MoM to 2.26%, but was actually down 3bps versus the November level. NPL cover dipped to 100.3%, down from 101.7% in December.
  • Meanwhile, loan growth slowed to 8.8% YoY from the 10.3% pace in December. This is fairly typical, as the December figure tends to be boosted by year-end window-dressing. We expect loan growth to steadily pick up as the year progresses.
ACEN partners with BrightNight for RE portfolio development

Meralco declares failed bid for 260MW power supply

Manila Electricty Co. (Meralco) fails to secure bids for 260MW peak requirement in preparation for the dry months. Meralco may have to resort to the spot market for power sources after failing to receive any interest in the second round of competitive bidding.

Belle Corp makes tender offer for Premium Leisure

Belle Corp (BEL PM) disclosed that it would make a tender offer for all outstanding shares of Premium Leisure Corp (PLC PM). BEL currently owns 80% of PLC.

The tender offer price will be based on a Fairness Opinion by an independent appraiser (First Metro Investments Corp). At the last price of P0.74/share, PLC traded at 10.6X FY23 P/E.

At the PLC market cap of P23.1bn, BEL would have to spend P4.6bn to acquire the PLC shares held by minority shareholders.

PLC holds the license for City of Dreams Manila. It has an EBITDA sharing arrangement with Melco Resorts Philippines. PLC’s FY23 gaming revenue share grew 50% to P2.3bn. BEL is trading at 9.1X annualized 9M23 P/E

Jollibee Foods FY23 headline net profit +16% YoY to P8.8bn – behind

JFC reported FY23 headline NPAT of P8.8bn, up 16% YoY, behind Regis but broadly in line with consensus expectations. After deducting perp coupons and pref dividends, we estimate NPAT to common was P7.5bn, 4x vs P1.9bn in FY22.

FY23 revenues of P244bn (+15% YoY) were in line while operating profit of P14.4bn (+36% YoY) fell short of estimates. For 4Q23 alone, we estimate recurring operating profit was flat at P2.5bn. Recall that the company incurred a one-time provision expense in 4Q22 relating to the closure of Pho24 stores in the Philippines.

Revenues grew 8% YoY during the quarter while consolidated SSSG slowed to 5.4%. SSSG slowed across all formats except for China (4Q SSS +22% vs +2% in 3Q) and CBTL (+2.4% vs -1.5%). Philippines SSS was at +5.4% from +13% in 3Q. North America SSS was flat vs +6% in 3Q

Operating margin in 4Q23 contracted QoQ and YoY despite GPM expansion due to proportionately higher opex.  The company also provided FY24 guidance:

  • +10-14% systemwide sales growth and +10-15% operating profit growth
  • 700-750 new stores with capex of P20-23bn
  • Regis FY24 operating profit (P16.9bn) is within the upper end of the guidance range
Jollibee to raise up to P8bn from preferred share issuance

Century Pacific forges new supply contract with Vita Coco

CNPF entered into a new contract with The Vita Coco Company. Vita Coco is CNPF’s largest customer for its coconut OEM business. The agreement is an addition to the previous agreement signed in 2020, which is up for renewal in 2025.

The new contract covers additional volume requirements by Vita Coco of approximately 90m liters of coconut water over the next five years. The coconut OEM segment contributes 8-10% of CNPF’s annual revenue and approximately 8-12% of operating profit (estimated).

Meralco to complete Atimonan gas conversion project by 2029

Meralco expects to complete conversion of its coal-fired power plant in Quezon province into a gas-fired plant by 2029. Meralco Powergen Corp. (MGen), through its subsidiary Atimonan One Energy, Inc. (A1E), is developing a 2,400MW natural gas-fired combined cycle gas turbine power plant.

About P175bn is being invested into the project which is targeted for commercial operations by 2029. The p;roject includes building an LNG storage unit, LNG unloading facility, and an LNG re-gasification system.

Trade deficit narrows to US$4.2bn in January

Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit stood at US$4.22bn in January, down 24% YoY and flat MoM. Imports fell 8% YoY to US$10.2bn in January on lower commodity prices. Imports of fuel fell 35% YoY while raw materials dropped 5% YoY. Consumer goods imports jumped 16% but capital goods imports still posted a decline of 6% YoY.

Exports rose 9% YoY due to base effects (Jan-Apr 2023 were the weakest months for exports in 2023). Electronics exports rose 16% YoY, due to a strong recovery in exports to the USA (+23%) and HK (+79%).

Tourist arrivals sustain recovery in Feb

Tourist arrivals rose 23% YoY, 1% MoM to 582k, equivalent to 85% of 2019 average. After arriving in droves in January, Korean tourists fell 11% MoM to 156k (+23% YoY) or 94% of pre-Covid levels. Visitors from the USA rose 7% YoY to 92k or 4% above pre-Covid levels. Chinese tourists extended gains, jumping by 47% YoY to 49k but were still about 34% of pre-Covid levels.

This Brings YT-Feb tourist arrivals to 1.16mn, up 24% YoY. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY or reverting to 93% of 2019 levels (vs 2023A: 5.5mn or 66% of pre-Covid).

FDI net inflows rise 30% YoY in December; FY23 inflows dip 7% YoY to US$8.9bn

Foreign direct investments posted a net inflow of US$826mn in December, up 30% YoY, due to an 86% YoY jump in nonresidents’ investments in debt instruments to US$527m. Reinvestment of earnings rose 4% to US$91m while investments in equity capital dropped 22% YoY to US$208m.

This brings FY23 FDI net inflows to US$8.9bn, down 6.6% YoY and equivalent to 2.0% of GDP. This is above the BSP’s target of US$8bn for the year.

Marcos wants more US semiconductor firms to expand in PHL.

President Marcos is calling for US companies to set up wafer fabrication facilities in the country; upskilling of workforce would help the Philippines expand its role in the semiconductor industry beyond assembly and packaging.

US Trade Secretary Gina Raimondo reiterated a plan to crank out more than 120k engineers and process technicians by 2028 to boost output.

The Philippines is one of seven countries that the US is partnering with to diversify its semiconductor supply chain under the CHIPS and Science Act. The Law provides US$52.7bn in federal subsidies to support chip manufacturing and persuade chipmakers with operations in China to relocate to the US or other friendly countries.

Senate approves Bill on property valuation reform

The Senate approved on third reading Senate Bill 2386, the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act. The law will take effect two years after it is passed.

This seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units. Assessed valued used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. Regis estimates that real property taxes account for 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover. This law would likely have a large impact on developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land, Filinvest Land, and Vista Land.

Nickel Asia - A very soft end to 2023 and a bearish 2024 management outlook
 
 
by Klyne Resullar | klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Lower ASPs, large losses from HPAL plants weigh on 4Q23
NIKL reported its FY23 profit dropped by 53% YoY to P3.7bn, well below REGIS and consensus estimates. Implied 4Q23 profit collapsed by 95% YoY to a mere P54mn, after nickel ASPs fell 17% YoY and equitized losses from its two HPAL plants ballooned. Management conveyed a bearish near-term outlook on global 
nickel prices and a tempered view on FY24 production. Nevertheless, they tried to sound optimistic about a possible tightness in nickel supply, but only by 2028.

Mining EBITDA up in 4Q23 on lower costs
Even though we estimate mining revenues fell 22% YoY in 4Q23 due to lower  ASPs, we estimate mining EBITDA in the quarter rose 43% YoY to P3.09bn. Cash costs fell 55% YoY, which we suspect could be partly due to lower fuel costs and gov’t taxes. We hope to get more clarity on this volatility.

A disastrous quarter for HPAL plants
Equity losses from Taganito THPAL (THPAL) and Coral Bay (CBNC) amounted to P215mn and P822mn, respectively in FY23, a reversal of the respective P752mn and P190mn profit contributions in FY22. The losses from the HPAL plants appear to have been booked (incurred) mostly in 4Q23, with implied losses 

in the last quarter at P278mn for THPAL and P381mn for CBNC. Management blamed the drop in nickel LME prices and cobalt prices, as well as expenses related to the repair of the tailings dam at CBNC.

While its solar plant in Subic grew its standalone EBITDA by 14% YoY to P595mn, the power segment still incurred a net loss from higher financing costs. Another 70 MW came online in February, raising its gross renewable capacity to 174 MW.

Somber 2024 outlook
Management reckons that the recent bounce in LME nickel prices could be short lived, following the completion of Indonesia’s Presidential elections. They are also guiding for low-single digit production growth for FY24, which is lower compared to our +7% forecast.

 
Universal Robina - 4Q23: Int’l BCF margin recovery amidst soft top line
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat | carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY23 net profit +11% to P12.2bn - behind
URC reported FY23 net profit of P12.2bn, up 6% YoY on a recurring basis, behind expectations. FY23 revenues (+6% YoY to P158.4bn) were slightly behind. At the bottom line, the FY23 underperformance was exacerbated by higher-than-expected interest expenses, associate losses, and forex losses.

4Q23 revenues were softer than expected as branded consumer food (BCF)
sales, domestic and international, were practically unchanged YoY. A notable
improvement in margins, particularly for int’l BCF, helped lift 4Q EBIT (+7% YoY) despite the soft top line.

PH BCF: A&P investments amidst soft demand
PH BCF sales were flattish YoY in 4Q23 as soft demand persisted. Performance
by category continued to be mixed amidst inflation concerns to consumers –
snacks and RTD beverages performed well, while confectioneries succumbed to downtrading. Segment EBIT margin was flat YoY but contracted 90bps QoQ as gains from lower input cost were reinvested into A&P.

Int’l BCF: Key markets recover
Int’l BCF sales were flat YoY in 4Q23 in PHP terms, but grew 6% YoY on a constant  currency basis. Thailand and Indonesia grew 9% YoY during the quarter, recovering from previous quarters, which were weighed by inventory
corrections and price adjustments. The strong top line, coupled with lower input
cost, drove segment operating margins over 300bps YoY.

Margin recovery to continue; guiding for mid-high single digit top line growth in 2024
Management remains optimistic on the margin outlook for 2024. While they
continue to see pressure on certain inputs (e.g. cocoa beans, coffee), prices for
other inputs (e.g. potato, palm oil, sugar) are moving favorably. The company is guiding for mid-high single digit top line growth in 2024 (Regis FY24E: +5%),
which we believe will likely be driven by volume growth as price adjustments taper and consumer sentiment picks up. Management indicated that early 1Q24 is off to a “good” start, albeit coming from a low base last year (e.g. ERP transition).

 
Philippine Banking/Finance: Digital finance – who wins? Banks vs Digibanks vs E-wallets
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena | rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Digital payments surge; but lending has been a challenge
While it remains difficult to gauge the overall digital economy, data
indicate rapidly broadening adoption of digital payments, particularly
by individuals. Smaller-ticket transfers over InstaPay surged 42% YoY
in 2023 in PHP terms, and 53% in number of payments. The BSP
estimates P2X payments – personal transfers to individuals or
institutions – were already 63% digitalized by value and 56% by volume
in 2022. Meanwhile, the BSP issued Digibank licenses in 2021. Deposits
have surged, but lending (and collecting) have been a challenge. Can
digital finance be profitable? Globe’s Mynt/GCash already seems to be…

InstaPay volumes up 53% in 2023, surging 73% YoY in December alone
Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) grew
another 29% YoY in 2023 to P12.9trn (about $230bn). Of these, smaller-ticket, real time payments through InstaPay surged 42% to P5.0trn. Most notably, the volume of transactions soared 53%, with December alone skyrocketing 73% YoY. This suggests that digital payments adoption is rapidly broadening out, gaining acceptance a) by more users, and b) for more and smaller transactions.

Individual (and government) adoption is far outstripping business
The BSP aims to have 50% of all transactions done digitally by 2023E, up from 42% in 2022. It estimates that personal payments (P2X) were already at 56% of total in 2022, up sharply from 36% in 2021. Given the InstaPay data, we would expect an even higher proportion in 2023E. Curiously, though, businesses have lagged far behind, with only 10% of B2X payments done digitally.

Digibanks – strong deposits, but struggling (to collect) on loans
The BSP handed out 6 Digital Bank (digibank) licenses in 2021, with most starting
operation in 2H22. The segment has quickly grown deposits. However, the
sector has been struggling with lending. Digibank loans peaked at P20bn in
August 2020, falling back down to P17.0bn as of January 2024. Meanwhile NPL
ratio ballooned to 13.5% and past due ratio to around 30%. We believe the lack
of a fully-functioning National ID and a credit bureau is a significant challenge.

Who wins – Banks? For now, maybe it’s GCash/Mynt…
Traditional Philippine banks have adopted very different digital strategies. Of
the Big Three, BPI seems to have leaned most heavily into digital banking, while
BDO and MBT have been more measured. Mid-sized banks like UBP and RCBC
have also made significant inroads. However, the jury is still out on the impact
on profits/ROE, at least in the near-term. Meanwhile, Globe Telecom’s (GLO PM)
Mynt/GCash e-wallet and finance platform is already profitable. Given its
dominance and ubiquity – and the massive user data it generates – can Mynt’s
foray into lending avoid the pitfalls of the digibanks?
