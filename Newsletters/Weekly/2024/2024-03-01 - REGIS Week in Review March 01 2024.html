REGIS REPORT

01 March 2024

The market continued to consolidate for the week, ending at 6,919.59 (+0.09% WoW) despite breaching the 7,000 resistance level intraday.

Profit takers emerged as 17 out of 30 index names ended in the red.

Trades were mostly driven by earnings along with the MSCI rebalancing that occurred last Thursday.

SM Investments (+3.2% WoW) led the index as profits jumped 25% to P77bn, which is better than estimates (Regis and the Street).  Earnings from its banks and property subsidiary powered the results, which represent a record full-year profit number for any listed company.  For the retail segment, revenues rose 10% for the year while income grew 11%.

Gains in ICT (+1.5% WoW) likewise lifted the market. Recurring profit before perps was at US$677mn in FY23, which is above consensus (US$636mn)  and mildly higher than Regis forecast (US$665mn). Headline FY23 profit came in at US$511.5mn, down 17% YoY due to impairments. Implied 4Q23 profit rose 6% QoQ and 7% YoY to US$182mn.

Meanwhile, BDO Unibank (BDO PM) NPAT for FY23 came in at P73.41bn (=), up 29%, in line with Regis and Street expectations. Profits included a one-off, non-cash P6.1bn gain, which the bank used to accelerate loan loss provisions, accelerate depreciation of some IT assets, and write off some deferred tax assets. Outside of this, the results were pretty solid. Loan growth accelerated to 9% (from 7.5%). NIMs took a bit of a hit (-6bps QoQ), as loans were skewed to quarter-end, while deposit costs spiked due to intense competition. Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio falling to 1.85% (was 1.99% in 3Q), and NPL cover rising to 185% (from 176%). However, midcap banks reported disappointing earnings. Security Bank (-0.6% WoW) saw Php9.1bn profits for FY23 (-14% YoY), falling behind Regis forecasts.

On the other hand, Meralco (MER PM) (+) core income came in at P37bn, +37% YoY.  While full-year results appear mildly ahead of Street estimates (P36bn), there was a clear drop in profits for 4Q alone. The generation business in aggregate turned in a net loss for the quarter due to plant shutdowns at its domestic generation business.  PacificLight, the Singapore genco subsidiary, remained quite profitable, but earnings there appear to be coming off.  The core distribution business, meanwhile, saw electricity sales growth of 4% for the quarter and for the year. 

A few results fell behind forecasts. Semirara’s (+1.4% WoW) FY23 headline profit of P27.9bn (-30% YoY) came in below Regis forecast (P33bn) and consensus (P31.5bn), even though revenues (P77bn) were within expectations. While 4Q23 profit rebounded 82% QoQ to P5.3bn, profits were below estimates after non-cash costs more than doubled and pre-stripping activities remained elevated.  SCC ended FY23 with gross cash at P19bn (P4.50/sh) and net cash at P12.26bn (~P2.9/sh).

Outside earnings, property and conglomerates dragged the market. ALI (-6.5% WoW) and SMPH (-2.3% WoW) fell alongside AC (-2.7% WoW) and GTCAP (-3.3% WoW) following its outperformance the previous weeks.  

On corporate news, Converge (-6.0% WoW) says it is investing P150m (US$2.7m) for its pay TV business. This follows the collapse of the Sky Cable deal between PLDT (TEL PM) and ABS-CBN (ABS PM),  CNVRG’s principals were involved in the cable TV business prior to their entry into broadband.

There were no additions or deletions during the MSCI rebalancing last Thursday, but the market estimated net inflows worth US$7.0m.

Headline inflows registered at US$22.5m for the week.

SM Investments - FY23: Profit rises 25%
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY23 earnings hit P77bn

SM Investments reported net profit of P77bn for FY23, a 25% increase over the previous year. The bottom line figure is above both Regis and consensus estimates. Solid results were delivered across all key segments, with banking accounting for the largest share (47%) of profits. Property was 25% and retail was 19%. 

For 4Q alone, profit (derived) was P21.1bn, which is +12% YoY. This implies a regularization of growth with (1) the property business now working off a more normal base and (2) the effects of the interest rate hikes on banking margins also now fully felt.

Interest rate hikes drive banking profit

Profits at BDO and CHIB rose 29% and 15% respectively as the favorable rate environment lifted interest margins. Gross loans were up 10% and 9%, respectively with asset quality metrics broadly unchanged. We note that both banks produced ROEs of 15%, exceeding our estimated COE.

Mall business still robust; residential presales drop

SM Prime’s FY23 net profit jumped 33% to P40bn. 4Q Philippine mall revenue growth remained healthy at 12% YoY despite the low-base effect dissipating. On the other hand, 4Q property presales declined another 39% QoQ (30% YoY). The company aims to cut inventories down to 12 months from the current 14 months.

SM Retail: FY23 earnings up 11%

Revenue for SM Retail grew 10%, while income rose 11%. Implied 4Q profit, though, was down slightly (-3%) due to “margin dilution”. Management aims to add at least 400 more (+24%) Alfamart stores this year. While it acknowledged the threat from the emergence of hard discount retailers, management believes that the retail market is big enough for all players.

 
Semirara Mining & Power Corp. - 4Q23 misses on higher mining costs, lower power ASPs
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY23 core profit drops 28% YoY to P27.9bn – Below forecast

4Q23 core profit excluding FX adjustments rebounded 87% QoQ to P5.52bn, taking FY23 profit to P27.95bn, down 28% YoY. The full-year figure fell short of Regis (P33bn) and consensus forecasts (P31.5bn) due to higher mining cash costs, lower power ASPs, and higher overall non-cash costs. Despite the disappointing results, we believe our 24E profit and dividend forecast are intact.

Pre-stripping activities persisted in 4Q; Early closure of Molave mine

Profits from the coal mining unit more than doubled QoQ, as coal sales more than doubled to 5.3mn MT and ASPs were broadly unchanged at P3,300/MT. While cash costs (ex-royalties) fell 22% QoQ to P1,458/MT, this was much higher than our forecast (P1,200/MT) due to persistent pre-stripping activities at the Narra mine. Meanwhile, higher mudflow from the Molave mine prompted SCC to cease mining operations at this site in January, a year earlier than planned.

Lower spot prices weighed on power unit’s 4Q23

Spot prices fell 11% QoQ to ~P4.30/kwh in 4Q23, driving profits for the power unit lower by 23% QoQ to P762mn. Management noted that spot prices for Jan-Feb 2024 have been slightly above P4/kwh and guided for “softer” spot prices for 2024E relative to 2023 average of P5.80/kwh. Management also expects to shut down Unit 2 for repairs next week to raise the capacity of the plant to 300MW by end-May from 200MW today.

ICI4 prices trending above ASP forecast; Dividend announcement in end-March

Management indicated that majority of its 2024 coal sales will be indexed to ICI4, which currently trades at US$56/MT, higher than our 24E ASP of US$42/MT. Management sounded firm about the payment of a “good” dividend in April, given its healthy cash pile.

 
Manila Water - Cost cuts boost profits
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY23 core profit rises 74% YoY to P8.4bn – Above forecast

Headline FY23 income of P5.59bn included a P4.1bn impairment charge booked in 4Q, which was related to its investment in Thai-based East Water (EASTW TB). On a “core” basis and before the share of participating prefs, management said that profit jumped 74% YoY to P9.63bn, well above our forecast due largely to lower cash costs at the East zone. This more than compensated for the underperformance of its other investments locally and abroad.

East zone profit beats our estimate

FY23 profit for the East Zone rose 60% YoY to P8.81bn, with implied 4Q23 profit up 42% YoY to P2.37bn. While billed volume growth was flat in 4Q, revenues rose 30% YoY due to the 30% tariff increase implemented earlier in the year, while EBITDA doubled to P4.5bn. These took full-year revenues and EBITDA to P24bn (In line) and P16.9bn (above our P13.7bn forecast), respectively. Capex was 7% lower YoY at P19.2bn, but still broadly in line with its 5-year P100bn commitment and higher than our FY23E of P17bn. Note that the cost reductions appear to be unsustainable and, ironically, could actually result in a tariff clawback.

Poor 4Q performance for non-East zone units

Implied 4Q23 profit for its other PH investments fell 8% YoY to P164mn, which was attributed to (a) the terminated contract in Cebu and (b) slower demand from the industrial sector. Its international unit posted net losses in 4Q due to higher interest expenses and lower water supply at East Water.

Surprise dividend of P1.129/sh

The company declared a dividend of P1.129/sh on commons (6% yield) and P0.113/sh on its participating preferred shares. While the payout ratio of 35% was in line with our forecast, the absolute amount was higher than our forecast of P0.90/sh due to profit outperformance in 2023. We nonetheless believe the trajectory of dividends and profits will be biased to the downside, given its investment commitments in the East zone. Management was also noncommittal about buying back the remaining 22.5% stake of Ayala Corp. (AC PM) in MWC, describing last year’s buyback as “opportunistic”.

 
Nickel Asia Corp. - Caught in an ebbing tide; Initiate with a Hold
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

In a cyclical downturn

Nickel Asia (NIKL) is the Philippines’ clearest direct play on the outlook for nickel internationally. We are, however, cautious about near-term prospects given expectations that the current global oversupply of refined nickel will persist and keep prices low over the near- to medium-term. Consequently, we expect last year’s profit slump to extend to this year. At 15x FY24E PER, the shares are also not cheap relative to peers, which trade at only 9-10x. We initiate coverage on NIKL with a Hold rating and a target price of P4.10/sh.

Nickel market surplus an overhang

The oversupply situation in the global refined nickel market is unlikely to change soon, despite strong demand prospects for Class 1 nickel suitable for EV batteries. This unfavorable outlook will not only adversely affect NIKL’s existing mining operations but could also dash or postpone the company’s hopes for a successful investment in downstream nickel processing, in our view. We estimate NIKL’s profit will drop at a 10% CAGR over 2022-2026, even after assuming (a) a 5% annual improvement in ore shipments and (b) a ramp up of its renewable capacity to 291MW by 2026 from 83MW today.

Still early days for new growth initiatives

The company is trying to improve its green credentials by diversifying its earnings base through a plan to raise its renewable energy portfolio to 2,000 MW from just 100MW today. NIKL is also looking into possible partnerships to expand its exposure in nickel-based processing (i.e. HPAL) that are used in the production of electric vehicle (EV) batteries. These investments could arguably be transformational for NIKL, but we reckon these are still in their early stages and still entail relatively high risk (i.e. fund raising, earnings dilution, execution risks, and regulatory).

Sum-of-the-parts NAV of P4.10/sh,

Risks We use a sum-of-the-parts approach to value NIKL, using a target PER multiple of 10x for its nickel mining unit, replacement cost valuation of US$22k per MT for its HPAL plants, and DCF analysis for its power unit. The key risks relate to commodity prices of nickel and Brent, exchange rates, regulatory, and execution.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Aboitiz and CCEP complete acquisition of Coca-Cola Philippines

Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM) and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP) completed the acquisition of Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines Inc. (CCBPI), the exclusive bottler of Coca-Cola in the Philippines. The acquisition was for US$1.8bn on a debt-free, cash-free basis. CCEP will own 60% while AEV will own 40%. The transaction received clearance from the Philippine Competition Commission. The Philippines is Coca-Cola’s second largest market in Southeast Asia. CCBPI has 73 production lines and 19 plants and it serves more than 1mn outlets.

Meralco commits over P100bn investment for a sustainable future

Meralco announced on Sunday its investment plan exceeding P100bn through 2030 to fund critical projects. Among the goals are to improve the company's distribution network, enhance grid reliability, implement energy efficiency solutions, and expand electric vehicle fleets and infrastructure. The company's long-term strategy is to reduce total emissions by 20%, including direct and indirect emissions from on-site activities and power supply procurement, respectively. Currently, the company is committed to securing 1,500MW of renewable energy supply contracts and developing 1,500MW of attributable green energy generation capacity.

Converge set to enter pay TV market

Converge ICT Solutions, Inc. is investing up to P150mn to launch its pay television (TV) segment this year. Investment is already included in the company’s 24E capex guidance of P15bn and the testing has commenced in Pampanga.

BDO - FY23 NPAT P74bn, in line; loan growth accelerates to 9%

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported NPAT of P73.41bn (before prefs) in 2023, up 29% YoY, and slightly ahead of Regis (P72.85bn) and consensus (P72.77bn) estimates. Regis notes that there seems to have been a one-off gain (P6.1bn gross, about P4.5bn net of tax), a revaluation arising from BDO’s acquisition of SM Keppel Land. According to management, the bank took the opportunity to write off some IT and deferred tax assets, as well as to increase provisioning to smoothen out the impact. “Customer loans” grew 9% YoY, accelerating from the 7.5% clocked in 3Q23. NIMs dipped due largely to a jump in deposit costs.

 

Other highlights:

“Customer loans” grew 9% YoY, picking up pace from the 7.5% clip in 3Q23. (Headline net loans were slower at 7.0% from 7.8% in 3Q.) Deposits grew 10.8% YoY, slowing a tad from the 12.2% in 3Q23, with virtually all the growth coming from time deposits (TDs). CASA ratio held steady at 72% vs 3Q, but was down from 79% at end-2022.

 

NIMs dipped 6bps QoQ, to 4.69% by Regis estimates, but were still up 33bps YoY. Loan yields were still up 10bps QoQ, but deposit costs spiked up 15bps QoQ. Overall asset yields were up 7bps, while total funding costs were up 13bps QoQ.

 

Fee incomes were up 12% YoY for FY23, while Trading/fx gains were up 18% YoY. The bank booked P6.1bn worth of one-off revaluation gains from its acquisition of the 50% of SM Keppel Land that it did not yet own (roughly P4.5bn after tax). The bank used the gain to accelerate provisioning, as well as to write off some deferred tax assets (DTA) and some IT expenses.

 

Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio at 1.85%, down 14bps QoQ, now at its lowest level since before the Covid pandemic. NPL cover jumped to 185% (from 176%). Restructured loans, though ticked up c30bps QoQ.

Meralco 4Q results

Core income of P37bn, +37% YoY.  Meralco’s reported income of P38bn, 34% higher YoY, with core income of P37.1bn (+37% YoY).  While the full-year profit appears to be mildly ahead of Street estimates (P36bn), Regis highlights that 4Q results were much weaker than the prior periods, with core income of P7.1bn lower YoY.

 

Power distribution volumes up 4%; looking strong in 1Q24.  Distribution utility volumes rose 4.2% in 4Q, bringing FY23 volumes up 4.4%.  Regis views the power demand growth to be mildly off estimates as industrial usage was barely up (+0.3%).  Demand from the semiconductor sector (around a quarter of industrial sales) is still down in 4Q23.

 

However, the company did indicate that early 1Q unit sales were strong.  January volumes were up 10% while Feb volumes are looking like they’ll be 7% higher.

 

Reversal of record power generation results in 4Q. The generation business contributed a net loss of around P500m in 4Q.  PacificLight saw lower margins while plant shutdowns of some of the Philippines power generation led to declines in output.  Management is guiding for lower margins for PacificLight this year (2024), which signals that the supernormal profits of 2022-2023 are over.

 

Dividend of P11.235/share.  Meralco declared a final dividend of P11.235/share with respect to prior year earnings.  This put its payout at 60% of prior year core income. Regis reckons Meralco could pay another P9/share in the latter part of the year, taking dividends this calendar year to over P20/share (close to 6% yield).

 

One-off windfall for generation supplier ACEN? Meralco agreed to file an application with the regulator to raise payments to ACEN’s SLTEC (210MW coal) due to the extraordinary increase in coal prices following the Russian invasion of Ukraine.  This is similar to what Meralco did with respect to its supply agreement with San Miguel.  If approved, it could result in a P706m gain for ACEN that will be passed on to consumers.

Nickel Asia terminates deal for Pujada nickel project

Nickel Asia (NIKL PM), Hallmark, and Austral-Asia have agreed to terminate NIKL’s letter of intent to conduct a feasibility study for an HPAL facility in the latter’s Pujada nickel project in Davao Oriental. Hallmark and Austral-Asia Link Mining Corp. are companies under the management of Asiaticus Management Corp. The parties failed to agree on the commercial terms of the definitive agreements.

MPI says IPO for merged toll road firm with San Miguel possible this year

Coca-Cola to spend US$1bn over the next five years

Coca-Cola will spend US$1bn over the next five years to expand operations in the Philippines. A new plant that will be built in Tarlac province if already in the works. Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines Inc (CCBPI) was recently acquired by Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP) and Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM) for US$1.8bn. CCEP owns 60% while AEV owns 40%.

Filinvest REIT 2023 net profit falls

Excluding revaluation gains/losses on investment properties, FILRT’s 2023 net profit fell 2% to P1.28bn. This is higher than consensus forecast of P1.17bn. Revenue fell 7% to P3bn while  office occupancy stood at 83%, flat QoQ but down 6ppt YoY.

Filinvest Land FY23 net profit rises 30%, ahead

Filinvest Land’s FY23 net profit grew 30% to P3.77bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Revenue of the residential and office segments outperformed Regis expectations. FY23 office revenue was flat at P4.66bn (from down 2% in 9M23), while 4Q23 mall revenue fell 31% QoQ and 19% YoY. This could be due to an accounting adjustment.

Globe eyes over 6-fold expansion of data center capacity by 2026

Globe is eyeing to expand its data center capacity to 150MW 2026 from the current 22 MW according to STT GDC Philippines president and CEO Carlomagno E. Malana. STT GDC Philippines is Globe’s joint venture with Ayala Corp. (AC PM) and ST Telemedia Global Data Centres. To date, STT GDC Philippines has five operational data centers in the Philippines which includes its data centers in Fairview and Cavite which will be operational by 2025.

More on Semirara (SCC PM) 4Q23 results

Semirara’s 4Q23 core profit after FX adjustments rebounded 87% QoQ to P5.52bn but the improvement fell short of Regis expectations. The disappointment was due to (a) one-time costs related to an early closure of Molave mine, (b) lower-than-expected power ASPs, and (c) higher non-cash costs. These offset (a) mildly better FY23 sales volumes of 15.8mn MT (FY23E: 15.5mn MT) and (b) plant availability factor of 88% (vs FY23E: 66%). Management’s 2024 guidance point to possible upside risk to coal mining unit. ICI4 price of US$54.5/MT for 2024 (vs. Regis ASP of US$42/MT and spot of US$56/MT). Coal sales volume of “at least” 15mn MT (vs FY24E: 15mn MT).

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

House approves Bill reducing stock transaction tax

The House of Representatives approved on third reading House Bill 9277 or the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act. The Bill seeks to reduce stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1%. It would also lower the dividend tax for non-resident investors from 25% to 10%. It also seeks to remove the 7% gross receipts tax on net trading gains by banks and financial institutions.

NEDA greenlights 23 new infrastructure projects; removes 36 smaller projects

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) Board approved 23 new projects and removed 35 others from its list of infrastructure flagship projects (IFP). The details on adjustments have not been disclosed yet. However, NEDA indicated that it approved an increase in the project cost of the Davao City Bypass Construction Project (ODA financing) to P70.8bn (from P46.8bn previously). The adjustments bring the total number of projects to 185, with a total value of P9.14tn.

 

The previous list involved 197 projects worth P8.7tn. NEDA Secretary Balisacan clarified that certain projects removed from the list will still proceed as planned as they are already part of regular government programs, although now no longer IFPs. NEDA is also evaluating the cost and ridership projects of the Mindanao Railway project. Project is currently estimated to cost P83bn (no funding source determined yet but this was previously under ODA from China).

Government looks to ease regulations for transport network vehicle service (TNVS)

President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. said he is open to legalizing motorcycle taxis and easing regulations for transport network vehicle service (TNVS). Grab Phils. has been pushing for the removal of the supply cap being imposed by the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB). Grab wants to increase ridership to half million per day from 300k today. The Department of Transportation said it needs to study the proposal for the removal of the supply cap, while the bills for legalizing motorcycle taxis are still pending in Congress. The easing regulations on TNVS could benefit Toyota’s sales

 

SEC relaxes public float requirement for power firms

The SEC waived the minimum public float requirement of 20% for listed firms in the energy sector. The minimum float requirement for energy firms are now at 15%. This rule only applies to new listings. The SEC also simplified the registration of securities for power generation companies and distribution utilities that are mandated to sell their shares to the public. The SEC will complete the review of filings within 45 days. The public offering may start within 10 business days from the date of effectivity of registration statement. A company may also issue securities in tranches, to be offered for a period not exceeding three years from its registration statement.

Department of Transportation launches Swiss Challenge for Laguindingan International Airport

The Department of Transportation has started seeking comparative proposals to develop and improve the Laguindingan International Airport in Misamis Oriental. The proposals will serve as competitive challenge to Aboitiz InfraCapital Inc.’s unsolicited proposal in 2018 for the takeover of Laguindingan Airport. The National Economic and Development Board last month approved negotiated parameters, terms, and conditions for the P12.75bn airport project.

 

The prospective challengers will have a 90-day window from the publication of the invitation for submission of comparative proposals. Aboitiz InfraCapital, will be given 30 calendar days to match any responsive comparative proposals. If no proposals are received, the project will be awarded to the original proponent by May 2024. The 35-year contract involves upgrading, O&M, and future expansion of the Laguindingan Airport.

BSP sees Feb inflation at 2.8-3.6%

The BSP expects inflation in Feb to settle at 2.8-3.6%, citing higher prices of key food items (rice, meat, fish), fuel, and electricity. This is still within the BSP’s 2-4% forecast range for 2024. The low end of the forecast range would be unchanged vs the Jan print. Regis expects 4.6% average inflation for 2024.

Budget deficit rises 6% YoY in December; FY23 deficit narrows 6% YoY to P1.5tn

The government recorded a deficit of P401bn in Dec 2023, up 6% YoY. Revenue collections fell 3% YoY. The FY23 deficit fell 6% to P1.5trn. This represents 6.2% of GDP, a little over the 6.1% program but below the 7.3% deficit-to-GDP ratio recorded in 2022. Revenue increased 8% to P3.8trn, slightly above the government’s target, driven by “others”.

Maharlika to raise USD1bn for energy sector

Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) CEO and President Consing is currently seeking board approval to raise USD1bn (P56bn) for energy projects. After determining risk and sectoral limits, about 15% of MIC's P125bn seed capital, amounting to P18.75bn, will be its contribution to the fundraising for the energy sector. MIC intends a limited partnership (LP) with some sovereign wealth funds (SWFs) yet to be disclosed and also eyes private equity to raise the remainder of the intended fund. An official fundraising timeline was yet to be given but MIC plans to announce its first commitment, likely to an energy project, within three months.
