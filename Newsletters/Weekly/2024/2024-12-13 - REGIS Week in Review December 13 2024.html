REGIS REPORT

13 December 2024

The PCOMP retreated for the week and pulled back from the 6,700-level anew, -1.7% or -112.63 pts to 6,616.51.

The local gauge was the week’s worst performer in the Asean region dragged by Conglos (-3.4%) and Property (-3.1%), with large caps SM (-3.0%), ALI (-6.3%), AC (-5.3%), and SMPH (-1.9%) alone accounting for 70% of the index’s drop. Although, the biggest decliner in the index was BLOOM (-9.7%) as investors continued to unload shares ahead of the complete exit of POGOs by EOY, as the regulator confirmed this week that licensees were on track to cease operations by end-December.

Downbeat sentiment flowed into property developers as office and residential vacancies are widely expected to rise with the POGO exit; thus ALI (-6.3%) was among the largest decliners this week as well. Meanwhile, developers continued to buy back shares amid the property slump, with ALI and RLC buybacks active this week and SMPH recently unveiling a P10-bn buyback plan. Bucking the broad market decline was CNPF (+7.1%), the week’s top gainer, along with some other consumer names PGOLD (+3.3%) and URC (+1.7%) that were lifted by bargain hunting following weeks of continued selling.

Market activity remained slow, with ADTO for the week flat at $67m vs last week’s $69m. A few large blocks went through, including ALI’s P2.8bn placement of AREIT shares and FLI’s tender offer that swapped P1.9-bn worth of FLI shares for FILRT shares. Foreigners were net sellers by $4m this week.

Filinvest Land - FLI concludes capped tender offer
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Tender offer accomplishes goals

Filinvest Land accepted the maximum 1.87bn of the 4.24bn FLI shares tendered and exchanged them for 597mn Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) shares. The tender offer accomplishes two goals: 1) buying back FLI shares at cheap valuations, and 2) raising FILRT’s public float, which gives it more room for the next asset injection from Filinvest Land.

What were the terms of the tender offer?

In September 2024, Filinvest Land’s Board approved a tender offer to purchase 1.866bn FLI shares, equivalent to 7.7% of shares outstanding (and 24% of float shares). Instead of paying in cash, however, the company would pay in Filinvest REIT shares, at a ratio of 0.32 FILRT shares for every FLI share tendered.

What happened to public float?

With the conclusion of the tender offer, FILRT’s public float increased from 34.5% to 46.7%, paving the way for another share-for-property asset injection in the future. Meanwhile, Filinvest Land’s public float fell from 32.1% to 26.4%, potentially reducing FLI’s meager liquidity further.

How will this affect EPS and NAV/share?

Based on our preliminary estimates, this transaction could result in a 1% increase in Filinvest Land’s FY25E EPS (to P0.15/share) and a 5% expansion in NAV/share (to P2.26/share).

Ayala Land sells AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 75mn AREIT shares at P37.00/share in an overnight placement on December 9; total of P2.78bn. This represents a 7% discount to the previous closing price. At P37.00/share, AREIT offers a dividend yield of 6.5% (based on consensus forecast). For context, the 10-year government bond yield is currently 5.94%. After this transaction, we estimate that Ayala Land’s ownership in AREIT fell from 57.7% to 55.4% and public ownership increased from 36.0% to c. 38.3%.

ICTSI secures new clients in Ecuador

ICTSI’s Contecon Guayaquil SA (CGSA) or its Ecuadorian business unit, has signed a new commercial agreement with five shipping lines: Wan Hai, Pacific International Lines, Cosco, Evergreen, and Yang Ming. The agreement covers the Asia-South America (ASA) service, which stopped in 2016, but recommenced in the first week of November. Together with the recently signed three-year contract with Cosco Shipping Specialized Carriers’ last September, CGSA should see its volumes rebound strongly beginning 4Q24. Note that CGSA was the key drag to ICT’s volumes in 9M24. Our forecasts assume CGSA’s revenues will rebound by 68% in 2025, after an expected 61% decrease in 2024. We estimate CGSA will account for 6% of ICT’s 25E volumes and 4% of revenues/EBITDA. We have a HOLD rating on ICTSI (ICT PM), as we believe uncertainties surrounding Trump’s trade policies for Mexico could overshadow strong positive earnings momentum near-term.

SPNEC borrows P180bn to fund 3,500MW Terra Solar project; seeks ERC approval for transmission facility

SPNEC is reportedly borrowing a total of P180bn from local banks to fund the solar project under Terra Solar Philippines (TSPI). Terra Solar project is a 3,500MW solar + 4,500MWh BESS facility, which is estimated to cost P200bn . Up to P160bn will be borrowed from a syndicate of banks: BDO, Security Bank, China Bank, PNB, BPI, and Metrobank. Proceeds will be used to fund the Terra Solar project (including transmission), pay off bridge loans, and cover other costs.

Terra Nueva, a wholly-owned subsidiary of SPNEC, will borrow up to P20bn from Security Bank, Chinabank, and PNB. Terra Nueva will use the proceeds to fund land acquisition costs, meet capital commitments to the project, and repay bridge loans. Terra Nueva will own the project site, which will be leased to Terra Solar.

Meanwhile, TSPI is seeking approval from the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) to establish a transmission facility for the same project. TSPI has appointed Meralco Industrial Engineering Services Corp. (MIESCOR) to design and construct the transmission facility. The total contract price amounts to P7.8bn, while about US$116.9mn (approximately P6.8bn) was allocated for offshore equipment.

Terra Solar is 60%-owned by SPNEC; SPNEC is 50.5%-owned by Meralco (MER PM), through its MGen stake of 47.3% and Metro Pacific stake of 3.2%. Project is estimated to be completed in phases: Phase 1: 2,500MW by 1Q26 and Phase 2: 1,000 MW by 2027.

Huawei secures contract for Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) in SPNEC’s Terra Solar

SPNEC, a subsidiary of Meralco, has secured an agreement with Huawei to deliver the BESS component of their Terra Solar megaproject. Huawei will further provide step-up transformers to connect to the 34.5kV busbar at the main substation. Terra Solar intends to have a 4500MWh battery storage paired with a 3500MW solar plant, set to be fully operational by February 2027. These BESS systems are intended to store excess electricity generated during low-demand periods and supply it back to the grid during peak demand. BESS systems are intended for optimizing grid operations, and ensuring a consistent and uninterrupted power supply.

ACEN unit acquires 49% stake in Bangladesh solar project

IBV ACEN Renewables Asia Pte. Ltd. (IBV ACEN) finalized the acquisition of a 49% stake in a 70MW greenfield solar project in Bangladesh from Sonagazi Sun Hldgs. Pte. Ltd. The company said that the acquisition price is “less than 10% of the book value of ACEN as of 30 September 2024.” IBV ACEN is a joint venture between ACEN’s international unit, ACEN Renewables International and ib vogt (Singapore) Pte. Ltd, an affiliate of ib vogt GmbH. Recall that last October, ACEN said that it plans to invest as much as US$18mn for the construction of a 70MW solar project in Bangladesh. Investment is part of ACEN’s expected contribution of up to US$200mn equity investment as IBV ACEN targets a minimum operational capacity of 1,000MW across Asia pacific. ACEN has 6,754MW of attributable RE capacity, of which 3,051MW is operational. In the Philippines alone, ACEN has a total of 2,397MW of capacity, of which 1,330 MW are under construction. Its international att. RE capacity totals 4,357 MW, of which c. 2,400 MW are under construction.

First Gen to build 50MW solar facility in Batangas

First Gen plans to begin construction of a 50MW solar facility in Batangas next year. It will spend P1bn-P2bn and will finish construction in two years. First Gen President and COO Francis Giles Puno said that the company “is very serious about expanding solar installation across the country.” FGEN has 3,668MW of installed capacity.

CREC agrees to contract 1.5GWh of BESS to Sungrow

Citicore Renewable Energy Corporation (CREC PM) has advanced its renewable development with a supply and services agreement of 1500MWh of Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) to Sungrow Power, a leading PV inverter and energy storage system provider. Sungrow will provide CREC with its Power Titan series, capable of supporting both DC and AC coupling, with its lower leverage cost of electricity and higher performance. These BESS systems will be used for CREC’s solar projects nationwide. The new systems should help in terms of grid stability, energy efficiency, and RE integration for consistent delivery of electricity. CREC is reportedly en route to completing its 1GW of renewable energy capacity as they currently stand on total aggregate capacity of 285MW spanning 10 plants.

Asset quality in October much more benign than it seems

As we had predicted, asset quality data was distorted by the wild fluctuations in a) interbank loans, and b) reverse repos (RRPs), which the BSP includes in the “loans” denominator. Headline data shows October NPL and Past Due (PD) ratio deteriorating sharply – reported NPL ratio jumped 13bps MoM from 3.47% to 3.60%, while PD ratio increased 15bps MoM from 4.25% to 4.40%. However, this was only because the RRPs collapsed from P994.7bn in September to P503.5bn in October. That P491bn was equivalent to 3.5% of total loans (ex-interbank and RR). Note that the October RRP was much more similar to the P528.4bn in August.

Using the “clean” denominator of loans, ex-interbank and RRPs, all the asset quality data was much, much more stable.

NPL ratio inched up 1bp MoM to 3.81%, as gross NPLs were up 1.3% MoM vs loans up 0.9%  MoM. Similarly, PD ratio was up 1bp MoM to 4.66%, as PDs were also up 1.3% MoM. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio actually improved, down 3bps MoM to 2.13%, with nominal RLs down 0.6% MoM. NPL cover fell 33bps MoM to 92.98%

Meanwhile, loan growth slowed a touch to 10.9% YoY, from 11.0% YoY in September (but still +0.9% MoM, as above).

We are encouraged by the relative stability in asset quality ratios, despite the increasing share of consumer loans.

Consumer loans of the Philippine banks grew 16.5% YoY to P2,916.8bn

While this was slower than the 19.7% pace in 2Q24, it was still up a respectable 3.9% QoQ. Also, the 16.5% growth was faster than the 11.2% pace for total loans. That said, non-consumer loans accelerated to 9.8% YoY, consistent with banks’ strong corporate loan growth in 3Q. Consumer loans now account for 22.0% of total loans, a new record high. Encouragingly, NPL ratio for the sector improved to 5.67%, down 15bps QoQ

The slowdown was seen across all segments, but was largely driven by mortgages, which saw growth decelerate sharply from 13.5% in 2Q24 to 8.1% YoY in 3Q24. Balances were still up 2.2% QoQ though. NPLs for the segment were broadly stable at 6.78% up a tad from the 6.72% in the previous quarter.

Motor Vehicle (MV) loans grew 17.3% YoY (was 18.7% in 2Q). Car loans were still up 18.7% YoY (vs 20.3%), while motorcycle loans were down 2.6% YoY, the 7th consecutive quarter of YoY contraction. MV NPLs improved to 5.36%, down 36bps QoQ, thanks largely to 4-wheelers, whose NPLs dropped 38bps QoQ to 4.70%. Motorcycle NPLs were still high at 16.97%.

Credit card growth stayed elevated, growing 27.7% YoY, though slowing a bit from the 28.8% in 2Q. NPLs here improved to 4.35%, down 21bps QoQ.

While the slowdown was unexpected, we are comforted by the stable / improving asset quality. Of the banks we cover, BPI and SECB are the most exposed to the consumer loan segment.

Trade deficit expands to $5.80bn in October

The October trade deficit is the largest in 26 months since the US$5.99bn gap in August 2022 and was wider 37% YoY and 14% MoM. Imports rose 11% YoY, 5% MoM to US$11.96bn, primarily driven by increased imports of consumer goods (+29% YoY), as well as electronics and capital goods (both up +21% YoY). These overshadowed the 10% drop in fuel imports and other goods. Meanwhile exports dropped 5% YoY, 1.5% MoM to US$6.16bn as Electronics exports declined 23% YoY to US$2.86bn. The latest reading brings the YT-October trade deficit to US$45.21bn, +4% YoY.

Dollar reserves slip 2% at end-November

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) saw a 2.4% MoM decrease to $108.5bn from $111.1bn at the end of October. This was primarily due to net foreign currency withdrawals to settle foreign currency debt obligations and payment of expenditures. The level of dollar reserves by end-Nov was enough to cover about 4.3x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.8x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The BSP expects the country's GIR to settle at $106bn by end-2024.

Foreign investments dipped by 2% MoM to $91.bn as of end-Nov from 93.1bn end-Oct. Foreign exchange declined 18.2% MoM to $1.75bn from $2.14bn end-Oct. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $11.03bn, down 2.9% MoM from $11.35bn at end-Oct.

The government's borrowing plan this year is set at a 75:25 mix, in favor of domestic sources. While for 2025-2027, the mix is seen to be at 80:20, the majority still from domestic sources.

Agri policy amendments to help PH manage rice woes

President Marcos signed on Monday Republic Act (RA) 12078, which will have amendments made to the Agricultural Tariffication Law of 2019 in an effort to increase the nation's rice output. Rice farmers will receive provisions of farm machinery and equipment, free distribution of high quality inbred certified seeds, and other interventions. The law also extends the life of the Rice Competitiveness Enhancement Fund (RCEF), which is sourced from the tariffs collected from rice importation until 2031. Annual allocation to the RCEF will be increased from P10bn currently to P30bn. The new law mandates that a buffer stock of rice will be maintained, equivalent to 30 days at any given time to address food security emergency situations on rice. Other initiatives of the law include - More stringent inspections and monitoring of warehouses and agricultural facilities; and empowering the Secretary of Agriculture to designate import entities to import rice to stabilize supply and prices.

VAT refund program for non-resident tourists signed into law

Republic Act (RA) 12079 will establish a VAT Refund System for goods purchased at accredited retail outlets, provided that these goods are taken out of the country within 60 days and meet a minimum transaction requirement of P3,000. An estimated 30% increase in tourist spending is projected with the new law in place, and this is expected to benefit both large-scale industries and SMEs. The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) projected that the law will raise P3.3bn to P5.7bn in additional revenues as well as create 4,400-7,100 jobs a year from 2024 to 2028.

Lower House approves electric vehicle zero-tariff measure

The House of Representatives approved on third reading House Bill 10960, which will remove tariffs on imported two-, three-, and four-wheeled EVs and their charging equipment from 2025-2030. The exemption covers BEV (battery), HEV (hybrid), and PHEV (plug-in hybrid). Recall that President Marcos issued EO 12 in 2023, which eliminated tariffs for EVs (battery/hybrid) and their components until 2028. Under the EVIDA Law, BEVs are also exempt from excise taxes while HEVs/PHEVs only need to pay 50% of excise taxes. EV car sales rose by 133% YoY to 14,178 units in 9M24, accounting for c. 4% of new vehicle sales.

ERC approves power supply deals between Meralco, SMC, ACEN

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) grated provisional authority to Meralco (MER PM) to procure a total of 500MW 10-year mid-merit supply from San Miguel (SMC PM) and ACEN Corp. (ACEN PM). PSAs will cover MER’s 350MW mid-merit requirement starting Feb 2025 and will increase by 150 MW by Feb 2026. The ERC, however, noted the applicable rate for these PSAs “shall be fixed at P5.1908 per kWh, without any escalation or adjustment”. The approved fixed rate were based on average rate of other mid-merit supply to Meralco. ERC will need to further evaluate the higher rates, as well as the reserve price set by Meralco (P8.23/kwh). Recall that San Miguel’s San Roque Hydropower’s (SRHI) offered a headline price of P7.10/kwh for 340MW. ACEN’s Gigasol3 unit’s offered a headline price of P8.1819/kwh for 139 MW. ACEN’s Santa Cruz Solar Energy Inc.’s PSA involves 21 MW at a rate of P8.1998/kwh for 21 MW (ERC has yet to release its decision on this PSA).

POGO firms to cease operations by end-December

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that POGO licenses will be canceled by the end of the year and the industry is on track to cease operations by end-December. Recall that during his State of the Nation Address in July 2024, President Marcos announced that a ban on offshore gaming would be effective by the end of the year. Mr. Tengco said there are now only 17 POGO firms operating, from 48 companies earlier this year. We estimate that the POGO industry occupies about 4% of Metro Manila office stock (equivalent to about 600,000sqm). POGO firms are predominantly located in the Manila Bay area; thus, we expect Manila Bay area property to be the most negatively affected by the ban. Colliers estimates that after the ban is fully implemented, Manila Bay area office and residential vacancies will rise from c. 30% currently to 50%. Although the developers we cover generally have minimal direct exposure to POGO office tenants or mainland Chinese residential buyers, a few of them have meaningful exposure to Manila Bay area property: 27% of SM Prime’s NAV is from its coastal redevelopment project in the Manila Bay area; and 10% of Megaworld’s NAV is from its land in the Manila Bay area.

PAGCOR raises 2024 GGR target to P350B

The Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) raised its 2024 gross gaming revenue (GGR) target from P336B to P350B. This would represent an increase of 23% from the 2023 figure (P285bn). 9M24 GGR grew 29% to P265B and represents 76% of the P350B GGR target set by PAGCOR for the full year. The 9M24 GGR breakdown is as follows: PAGCOR operated: +16% YoY to P12bn; Licensed casino: -5% YoY to P149bn; Online gaming: +218% to P103bn.
