REGIS REPORT

24 May 2024

The local benchmark capped the week virtually unchanged at 6619 +0.02% WoW after the initial strength at the start of the week failed to sustain through the subsequent sessions. The main area of focus this week was the sharp weakness in PHP which hit its weakest level vs the US since November 2022 on Tuesday, and since has held steadily above the crucial 58-level. While this appears to have doused investors’ hopes for a dovish policy setting for the second half of the year, note that Governor Remolona reiterated on Wednesday that 25-50bps cuts for the year remain on the table, and that they are not that concerned on the timing of the Fed’s rate cut. In any case, our sense is that any further substantial weakness in the PHP may eventually deter the central bank from cutting rates ahead of the Fed. Note that Fed funds are currently pricing in a nearly 60% chance of an FOMC cut by September, 88% chance by November, and fully pricing in 1 cut and 44% chance of an additional cut by December.

Amid this uncertainty over the rate outlook, Property sector was the stand-out laggard this week, -3.33% WoW, dragged by declines in large caps SMPH -5.82%, which was the most battered PCOMP member this week, and ALI -1.87%. Other notable index laggards include JGS -5.6%, AGI -5.22%, MONDE -4.35%, TEL -2.47% and BDO -2.16%. While on the opposite end, both CNVRG +9.65% and WLCON +6.92% extended their uptrend, being the top 2 index gainers this week. Another notable winner was NIKL +5.67% amid strength in nickel prices.

Market activity was lighter at a daily average of $109.3m, -34% WoW coming from last week’s high base due to $105m OGP (+1.54% WoW) IPO and $224m MWC (+3.57%) block. Furthermore, outside of the notable movement in the peso, catalysts on the ground have dried up with the earnings season now out of the way. Note however there was placement in AREIT -0.58% this week, albeit much smaller than last week’s special blocks at around $55m. Net foreign activity for the week ended skewed to sell by $8.8m but we did see signs of support as foreign investors turned net buyers for the last 2 sessions, marking their first back-to-back days of buying in nearly 4 weeks. Top inflow names this week were GLO +0.3%, ICT +5.69%, ACEN +2.24% and MWC +3.57%, while top outflows were ALI -1.87%, AEV +2.24%, BDO -2.16%, AREIT -0.58%, SMPH -5.82%, JFC +1.77%, and AP -3.53%.

Up ahead, key event next week is the MSCI rebalancing on Friday. As previously announced, the changes for the standard cap index include: deletion of AEV +2.24% (outflow est $100m-$120m vs ADV of ~$1m), down-weights in AC -0.48% and SM +2.4% (outflow est of around $30m each), and small upweight in SMPH -5.82%. While for the small cap index, inclusion of ACEN +2.24%, and deletion of CEB -5.85% and SPNEC -7.41%.

Security Bank - 1Q24 Turning a corner?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

PPOP jumps 33% YoY, provisions to normalize from 2Q

SECB 1Q24 NPAR grew 11% YoY to P2.6bn, slightly behind Regis estimates (FY24E P12.0bn) but ahead of consensus (P10.8bn). PPOP surged 33% YoY to P4.9bn driven by faster loan growth (+12% YoY), and strong fee income. One-off gains and expenses largely canceled each other out. As was previously guided, provisions stayed elevated as the bank cleaned out its problem NPLs. The bank expects credit costs to normalize in coming quarters. SECB’s IT spend should start paying off in 2H with the launch of new apps for retail and business clients.

Loans accelerate to 11.6%; consumers jump 32% YoY, now 31% of total

Loan growth sped to 11.6% YoY (from 7% in 4Q). Consumer/MSME loans jumped 32% YoY, and now account for 31% of total loans, a new high. Corporate loans sped up too, up 4% YoY (-0.5% in 4Q23). NIMs were distorted by interest rate swaps; management reckons “pro-forma” NIMs were 4.48%, down 14bps QoQ (+66bps YoY), though loan yields were flat QoQ (+92bps YoY).

Recurring fees +30%; one-off gains offset by one-off costs

Revenues included a P1.4bn milestone fee from SECB’s bancassurance partner. Even without it, fee incomes were strong, up 30% YoY. The bank also booked one-off charge, brought about by a shift to hedge accounting, which brings the interest rate swap costs back up to interest expense line. Management reckons that the one-off gain and loss cancel each other out at the NPAT level.

Turning a corner? Provisions set to fall, IT spend to start paying off

Provisions stayed high, as the bank previously guided that the cleanup of credit card accounts would end in 1Q24. Management expects credit costs to normalize to 60-70bps from 2Q onwards, from about 110bps in 1Q24, and 150bps in 4Q23, which could quickly lift quarterly profits. After years of catch-up IT spend, SECB is set to roll out its new retail, MSME and enterprise apps in 2H24, which could improve customer acquisition, and lower opex over time.

 
Megaworld - Alliance Global to subscribe to 1.375bn new MEG shares
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Alliance Global to subscribe to new MEG shares

Megaworld’s Board of Directors approved the subscription of its parent company, Alliance Global (AGI PM), to 1.375bn new MEG shares (4% of current shares outstanding) at a total cost of P2.6bn. The subscription price of P1.90/share represents a 5% premium over 30-day VWAP. However, it represents a substantial discount to NAV and would thus result in dilution.

Why is Megaworld issuing new shares?

We find Megaworld’s decision to issue new shares puzzling. The disclosure to the PSE said that AGI’s subscription to new shares will “support growth and future business expansions of” Megaworld. However, with 1Q24 net debt/equity of 28%, we would argue that the company has sufficient debt capacity, particularly in light of relatively moderate expansion plans. We estimate Megaworld’s cost of debt of 7% (pretax) to be well below the FY24E earnings yield of 28% of its shares at the subscription price. Megaworld’s medium-term growth targets are not aggressive. It only intends to expand its rental portfolio (combined office and mall) by 15% over the next three years. Meanwhile, it plans to reduce residential launches by 45% this year (to P40bn).

What is the impact to Megaworld?

Since the subscription price of P1.90/share represents 70% discount to NAV and 0.2X FY24E P/B, we expect the equity issuance to result in dilution for minority shareholders. Our initial estimates suggest that NAV/share will drop by 3% (to P6.12/share) while 1Q24 book value/share will fall 3% (to P7.20/share). We also estimate that AGI’s ownership in MEG will rise c. 1ppt to 71%.

 
Vista Land - Residential revenue rises for third consecutive quarter
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q23 net profit rises 15% YoY

Vista Land’s 1Q24 net profit rose 15% YoY to P2.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Residential operations performed well, with revenue rising YoY for a third consecutive quarter and reservation sales returning to pre[1]COVID levels.

Residential revenue rises YoY for third consecutive quarter

After 15 consecutive quarters of YoY declines, 1Q24 residential revenue rose YoY for the third consecutive quarter, signaling that cancellations may have peaked.
However, operations haven’t quite normalized yet. 1Q24 residential revenue of P4.9bn is still 40% below 2019 levels, even though trailing 12M presales are only about 5% lower than pre-pandemic levels.

Higher-end projects boost reservation sales

1Q24 reservation sales rose 12% YoY (+11% QoQ) to P20.8bn, slightly higher than the 2019 average. The company has increased the launches of higher-end Brittany projects in Mega Manila and launched “resort-type” projects in Palawan, Boracay, and Tagaytay. Since OFWs continue to comprise 60% of Vista’s residential sales, this suggests that there has also been an increase in the relative affluence of its target OFW customers.

Rental revenue rises despite flat revenue of major tenants

Despite the revenue of affiliates AllHome (HOME PM) and AllDay Marts (ALLDY PM) being flat YoY, VLL’s 1Q24 rental revenue rose 13% to P4.1bn. The two affiliates lease a combined 40% of VLL’s mall space. Trailing 12M rental revenue is P16.5bn, now more than double the 2019 level, even though rental floor area is only about 20% larger. Furthermore, Vista’s rental receivables grew from P34.3bn in 4Q23 to P37.3bn, which we estimate is equivalent to 2.25 years of rental revenue.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

GCash eyes 2025 IPO

In response to what’s the most asked question at media interviews, Globe (GLO PM) CEO Ernest Cu says Mynt may go public next year. The timing of the IPO is likely to be driven by market conditions rather than any internal timetable since Mynt is already profitable and does not need any outside cash infusion. Note that Mynt is the 36%-owned fintech affiliate of Globe which owns GCash. Cu also reiterated GLO management’s preference for a listing in the Philippines but said they are open to other options. Recall that Mynt contributed almost P1bn to Globe’s profit in 1Q24 and is set to make over P10bn (almost US$200m) this year if 1Q24 profits were annualized. Regis reckons a valuation of at least US$5bn is not unreasonable on that level of earnings.

Ayala Land sells 98mn AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 98mn AREIT shares, equivalent to 4% of shares outstanding. The shares were sold at a price of P32.45/share, representing a 8% discount to the last closing price. This implies that the total amount that was raised was P3.2bn. Note that in 4Q23, AREIT announced that it would acquire properties from Ayala Land and ACEN in a series of transactions: a hotel from Ayala Land for P1.2bn in cash, P21.8bn worth of office, mall, and hotel assets by issuing 642mn shares to Ayala Land, and P6.8bn worth of industrial land by issuing 199mn shares to ACEN. After all of these transactions are consummated (4Q24 to 1Q25), AREIT’s AUM will increase by 34% to P117bn and its shares outstanding will expand by 35% to 3.2bn. In order to meet minimum public ownership requirements, Ayala Land would have to sell 279mn AREIT shares. Recall that in January 2024, Ayala Land sold 181mn AREIT shares at P31.10/share (total of P5.63bn).

DMCI sets sights on new acquisitions

Apart from the purchase of a majority stake in Cemex Philippines (CHP PM), DMCI is also looking at acquiring other mining assets which may include copper and gold projects and a coal mine in Mindanao, provided these are open pit. Outside of these, DMCI intends to continue with its “aggressive organic growth.” The company hopes to fix certain issues in the next few years, including high inventory levels at DMCI Homes and permitting issues at its nickel mining unit.

Tokyo Gas to acquire 20% stake in FGEN LNG

First Gen (FGEN PM) disclosed that it signed a shareholders’ agreement and a share subscription agreement with Tokyo Gas for the latter to acquire 20% of FGEN LNG. FGEN LNG is the owner and operator of the LNG terminals supplying fuel to FGEN’s gas-fired power plants. These agreements are a continuation of the cooperation agreement that were previously announced.

Cagayan de Oro lawmaker backs Meralco franchise renewal

According to Cagayan de Oro City 2nd District Representative Rufus Rodriguez, renewing Meralco’s franchise for another 25 years will show potential investors that capital-heavy investments can benefit from long-term regulatory stability. Recall that previously, Albay 2nd District Rep. Joey Salceda filed bills seeking to renew MER’s franchise. Note that Meralco’s franchise is set to expire in 2028.

Cebu Pacific may finalize aircraft order by June

Cebu Air (CEB PM) expects to finalize its aircraft supplier for its US$12bn 100-150 aircraft order by June, according to CEB Chairman, Lance Gokongwei. The aircraft will be delivered from 2029 onwards. Note that ongoing negotiations with Airbus and Boeing have created short-term uncertainties on CEB’s capital requirements and has prompted the company to defer its initial planned dividend payment in 4Q24 to next year. CEB’s convertible pref shares (CEBCP PM) accrue an annual dividend of P2.28/sh, which brings accumulated unpaid dividends to P6.9/sh (P2.15bn).

ATI seeks 16.55% hike in Batangas port cargo-handling tariff

ATI has filed a petition with the Philippine Ports Authority to raise its cargo-handling tariffs at the Batangas Port by 16.55%. Note that ATI also filed a petition to raise tariffs for its port in Manila, South Harbour, by 16.55% last month. Note that a similar tariff hike petition was filed by ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal.

San Miguel nearing TPLEX Extension PPP contract signing

San Miguel (SMC PM) may soon sign the contract for the Tarlac-Pangasinan-La Union Expressway (TPLEx) Extension, according to PPP Center Executive Director Jeffrey Manalo. No parties have challenged the unsolicited proposal. TPLEX Extension Project is a 59.4 km 4-lane toll road which will start from the TPLEX in Rosario, La Union and will terminate at San Juan, La Union. The project is estimated to cost P27.5bn and will cut travel time from Rosario to San Juan from 1.5 hours to 40 mins. Civil works could be completed after five years.

Zero import duty on EVs, parts to boost EODB

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto expects the tariff reduction on hybrid vehicles and electric two-wheelers to boost job creation and help cut greenhouse gas emissions. We don’t think it will have much of an impact for Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), however, as it’s only the import tariff that will be cut to zero and the excise tax will remain unchanged. TMP’s imports of hybrids come from either with ASEAN (zero tariff already) or from Japan (0% if vehicle is less than 3.0liter engine or 20% if  more than 3.0 liter engine). Regis reckons that the bigger beneficiaries are likely to be those who import electric two-wheelers from outside ASEAN. Gogoro Philippines, a joint venture among Globe, Ayala Corp (AC PM) and Taiwanese firm Gogoro, is likely to be one.

CREC sets lower final offer price for IPO

Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. (CREC) has finalized the price for its IPO at P2.70/sh, lower than its maximum offer price of P3.88/sh. CREC will offer up to 1.76bn primary common shares, with an overallotment option of up to 267.86mn secondary shares. The IPO size is up to P5.45bn (w/ overallotment option) based on final offer price. The offer period is set from May 20 to May 24, while the listing on June 7. The proceeds will be used to fund capex for pipeline development of solar and BESS projects. Note that CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 175.5MW. Furthermore, 8 solar projects with a combined gross installed capacity of 1,583MW are planned for commercial operations this year as the company is expecting capex of P35bn for 2024. The company earmarked approximately P220bn across 53 projects between 2024 and 2030.

Metro Pacific Tollways taps DMCI for last segment of Cavitex C5 Link

Cavitex Infrastructure Corp (CIC) has signed a P3.3bn agreement with DMCI for the construction of C5 Link Segment 3B, which comprises a two-kilometer, 3×3 lane stretch from the Kaingin area to the RSG Subdivision. The last segment is targeted to be finished by the second semester of the year and will be operational by 2025. As of end-March 2024, DMCI’s order book stood at P38.9bn, down 7% QoQ and 8% YoY. For context, note that construction accounts for 2% of DMCI’s profit.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in April

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 6bps MoM to 20.5% (in April). On the other hand, foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 17bps MoM to 25.0%. We estimate that if the JFC foreign ownership had remained flat, the foreign ownership of the market would have fallen 16bps MoM. Net foreign selling accelerated to US$414mn, from US$45mn (net selling) in March. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 11 saw increases in foreign ownership while 18 experienced declines.

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
JFC – foreign ownership rose 5.01 percentage points MoM to 24.5%. We believe there was a clerical error in the March data, which suggested that JFC foreign ownership fell 5.86ppt MoM.
MBT – foreign ownership increased 53bps MoM to 21.1%. It also experienced the highest net foreign buying in April (US$25.5mn).
ALI – foreign ownership fell 87bps MoM to 28.0%. It also saw the highest net foreign selling for the month (US$68.6mn).
NIKL – foreign ownership declined 78bps MoM to 35.1%.
AC – foreign ownership decreased 44bps MoM to 26.2%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$26.2mn, the second highest for the month.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Peso hits P58:USD1; BSP to intervene

The BSP warned it will intervene in the foreign exchange market as the PHP closed at its lowest level of P58.27 for the first time in over 18 months. The peso’s weakness against the US dollar was in line with other currencies in the region. Beyond just inflation, the weakness/strength of the PHP should also be a key factor in any BSP decision on interest rates. Thus, weakness could postpone any decision to cut policy rates.

Infrastructure spending rises 9% YoY in 1Q24

Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 9.3% YoY to P266bn in 1Q24. This implies that infra spend in March 2024 alone rose 36% YoY to P133bn. The Department of Public Works and Highways posted “strong spending performance” for payment of progress billings for projects and also made advance payments to contractors. Recall that in 1Q24 GDP, the construction sector grew 6.8%, driven primarily by government (+12.4% YoY). The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. To reach this target, infra spend should average P134bn per month for the rest of the year.

BOP deficit widens to US$639mn in April

The country’s overall balance of payments posted a deficit of US$639mn in April, higher than the US$148mn deficit posted in April 2023 and a reversal of the US$1.17bn BOP surplus in March 2024. Officials cited outflows arising from the national government’s net foreign currency debt service. This brings YT-April BOP deficit to US$401mn, a reversal of the US$3.3bn surplus in the same period a year ago. The BOP position reflects the slight decrease in GIR level to US$102.6bn in end-April from US$104.1bn in end-March 2024.

PH foreign container volumes down 1% YoY in 1Q24

The country recorded a 1% YoY decline in foreign container volumes to 1.27mn TEUs in 1Q24, based on data from the Philippine Ports Authority and Cebu Port Authority. Volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) were flat at 664k TEUs while ATI’s (ATI PM) South Harbor recorded a 7% YoY drop in volumes to 281k TEUs. Nationwide import volumes rose 2% YoY while export volumes fell 4% YoY. Meanwhile, domestic container volumes also dipped by 1% YoY, with ICT’s MNHPI recording a 4% YoY drop. Note that during ICT’s 1Q24 briefing, management said that volumes in Manila so far have turned “mildly positive” in 2Q24.

PAGCOR issues more offshore gaming licenses

As of May 15, PAGCOR has now issued licenses to 40 Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL) and 9 provisional licenses have also been issued. Recall that as of January 15, there were only 7 IGL firms and 44 provisional licensees. In 2H23, PAGCOR required all POGO firms to reapply for licenses under the new nomenclature of IGL. From 2020 to 1Q23, offshore gaming firms vacated about 600,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. Since then, they have taken-up an incremental c. 200,000sqm of office space.

Regis estimates that offshore gaming firms currently occupy c. 900,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
45% of Filinvest Land’s NAV – POGOs occupy c. 5% of office space
44% of Megaworld’s NAV – POGOs occupy c. 5% of office space
25% of Robinsons Land’s NAV – POGOs occupy 1% of office space

Senate OKs VAT on digital services

The Senate approved on third and final reading a bill that seeks to impose a 12% VAT on digital services provided by companies with no physical presence in the Philippines. Measure may cover e-commerce firms such as Amazon, Shein, Rakuten, Taobao, AliExpress and Temu. BIR commissioner can order the blocking or suspension of digital providers if they fail to withhold and remit the 12% VAT. Bill exempts online education platforms, services of banks/nonbank financial intermediaries. Department of Finance expects the bill to bring in P83.8bn in revenues from 2024-2028.

No excise tax rate hike amid projected revenue dip–DOF

The Department of Finance (DOF) will not impose a rate hike in excise taxes, particularly on sin taxes. The BIR collected P21.975bn in excise taxes on tobacco in 1Q24, down 20% YoY. Officials cited illicit trade and consumers’ shift to e-cigarettes sold in e-marketplace. Note that the excise tax rate on cigarettes rose to P63 per pack in 2024 and will increase by 5 percent in the succeeding years.

Right of way, budget woes delay buildup of rail projects

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) has warned of construction delays for the biggest rail projects if right-of-way acquisitions and the release of appropriate budgets are further held back. DOTr lamented the piling difficulties in two projects: P874bn North-South Commuter Railway and the P489bn Metro Manila Subway. Projects are now expected to be completed by 2029 or later. Meanwhile, the full completion of San Miguel’s (SMC PM) MRT-7 rail line has been pushed back further to 2028 due to similar issues. Partial operations (i.e. North Avenue to Lagro) could start by end-2025.

 

Government posts lower budget surplus of P42.7bn in April

The national government recorded a budget surplus of P42.7bn in April, down 36% YoY. The decline is due to the 32% YoY jump in expenditures, which outpaced the 22% YoY increase in revenues. Revenues rose 22% YoY to P537bn, with tax collections up 14% and non-tax revenues more than doubling to P77.9bn. BIR collections rose 13% on double-digit growth in income tax and VAT collections during the annual income tax filing. Customs collections went up by 20%. Non-tax revenues doubled, boosted by the higher dividend payout of GOCCs to 75% from 50% previously. Meanwhile expenditures rose on the back of a 30% YoY increase primary spending and 46% increase in interest expenses. The expansion in primary spending was due to higher releases of national tax allotment and subsidies to GOCCs, including releases to PSALM and the 4th tranche of capitalization of Coconut Farmers Industry Trust Fund (CFITF). This brings YT-April budget deficit to P230bn, up 13% YoY.

Red, yellow alerts raised over Luzon, Visayas grids

The Luzon and Visayas grids were placed on red and yellow alerts yesterday with more than 3,000 MW of capacity unavailable. Luzon was placed on red alert from 1pm-5pm and 6pm-10pm and on yellow alerts from 12noon-1pm and 5pm-6pm, 10pm-12midnight. A total of 18 power plants are on forced outage, while three are running derated, leaving 2,525.5 MW unavailable to the grid. Major plants on forced outage are Sual coal fired power plant 1 with a capacity of 647 MW. Derated plants include Sual coal fired power plant 2 with output of 550 MW  and GNPower Dinginin Ltd. with 600 MW.

Meanwhile, the Visayas grid was placed on red alert between 2pm-4pm., 4pm.- 6pm.,7pm and on yellow alert from 12 noon and 2 p.m., 4 p.m. and 6 p.m., and 7 p.m. and 10 p.m. A total of 21 power plants are on forced outage, while three are operating at limited capacity, for a total of 553.1 MW unavailable to the grid. To date, the NGCP has issued yellow alerts on Luzon for 20 days, in the Visayas 22 days, and in Mindanao two days.
