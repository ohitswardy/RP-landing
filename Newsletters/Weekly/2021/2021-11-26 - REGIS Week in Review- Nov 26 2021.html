REGIS REPORT

26 November 2021

The PSEi was flat this week  (-0.03% WoW) as earlier gains on reopening optimism were wiped out by new Covid variant fears. Renewed jitters sent stocks lower, with losers outnumbering gainers 17:12.  

Following the prior week’s outperformance, telcos Globe (-6.3% WoW) and Converge  (-3.5% WoW) were the top laggards.  

Converge saw continued foreign selling post US$210m placement in the previous week.  Overall net selling for the market was US$15m this week.

AC Energy (-0.7% WoW) and Monde (-2.7% WoW) were active this week on the back of MSCI rebal related trades. Both names are to be included in the upcoming rebalancing.  Foreign LOs fed demand ahead of the Monde’s  MSCI inclusion, seemingly disappointed with progress on the alternative meats business.

Among the other decliners were Megaworld (-4.8%),  Security Bank (-3.3% WoW), and Puregold (-3.3% WoW) - -all down on no news.

The week was quiet in terms of corporate news, after the just-concluded 3Q21 earnings season. For 3Q21 alone, aggregate NPAT was up 14-15% YoY, with strict lockdowns and input cost pressures during the quarter. Aggregate earnings for 9M21 were up 44% for PSEi constituents, still 30-35% below their pre-Covid (9M2019) levels.

However, mobility restrictions continue to be eased from 3Q, suggesting better prospects for the next earnings round.   Mobility data are at near Covid-highs.  Meanwhile daily case counts are at the lowest levels since the start of the pandemic. Vaccination rates are also going in the right direction, with the 7-day average hitting over 840k/day and putting the Phils on track to hitting 70% of the total population by 1Q22 (assuming current pace). Around 40% of the country and 70% of Metro Manila has had at least one COVID-19 jab.

Mid-cap conglos LT Group  (+7.6% WoW)  and AGI (+4.3% WoW) outperformed. ICTSI (+3.4% WoW) also gained after losses in the prior week.

The reopening theme was in play, as seen in the gains of retailers Wilcon (+3.7% WoW) and Robinsons Retail (+1.6% WoW). Cyclicals also rose – with property/mall operators, Robinsons Land  (+2.0% WoW) and SM Prime (+1.8% WoW) ending with gains.  BPI (+0.2% WoW) and BDO (+1.1% WoW) likewise rose.

Philippine Consumer - 3Q21 dampened by lockdown but strong 4Q rebound seen
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q recovery underway but cost headwinds to persist

The lockdown in 3Q21 stalled any momentum on consumer spending that had built up in late 2Q. Mobility restrictions, coupled with rising cost inflation, provided multiple pressure points for the consumer/retail companies under our coverage during the quarter. As a result, same[1]store sales deteriorated and margins contracted. While we expect cost inflation to persist over the near term, the sharp jump in personal mobility (and likely consumer spending) could provide some flexibility to pass on cost increases and recover some margin.

Lockdown, cost inflation dampen 3Q21 performance

The 3Q21 sector performance was unsurprisingly weak, dragged mainly by the hard lockdown in Aug. Aggregate sector earnings jumped 42% YoY (flattered by a low base), but was actually down 14% QoQ. The Aug 2021 hard lockdown crimped consumer mobility anew and dampened any momentum that built up in the latter part of 2Q. Meanwhile, cost inflation continued to put pressure on margins across the sector.

Strong 4Q seen as mobility pops to Covid highs…

As of mid-November, consumer mobility had jumped to near pre-Covid levels, particularly for retail and recreation establishments. Barring another lockdown, the trends bode well for the recovery of the sector in 4Q21 and into next year. Increased consumer mobility could likely provide a much-needed boost to non[1]essential retailers and restaurant operators, who were operating at 60-70% of pre-Covid levels for most of the 9M period. Consumption could even receive a boost from election spending in 1H22.

…but continue to watch out for inflation

Notwithstanding the positive top line trends, cost inflation remains a source of concern. Prices of soft commodities continue to rise and global supply chain issues could keep logistics costs elevated over the next few quarters. Thus far, most consumer companies under our coverage have not been able to raise prices enough to pass on these costs. However, more robust consumer demand could provide more flexibility to make more meaningful price adjustments.

 
Philippine Property - 3Q21 recap: sales cancellations surprise; office vacancy jumps
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Each at its own pace

The three major property segments appear to be in varying circumstances with respect to a post-pandemic recovery. (1) For the malls, a sharp rebound looks imminent with the easing of mobility restrictions. 2) Reported cancellations of residential sales spiked in 3Q, but we believe the worst is behind us. 3) Finally, office vacancy rose yet again in 3Q21. There appear to be stirrings of incremental demand, however, which may temper future increases in vacancy rates.

Office vacancy climbs to 17%, rental rates still falling

Leechiu Property Consultants said that Metro Manila office vacancy climbed to 17% in 3Q21 (from 13% in 2Q21), largely driven by new supply. Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that rental rates fell another 1-3% QoQ. Rents in most areas are now 15%-30% lower than 2019 levels. In the Manila Bay area, which has a high concentration of POGOs, rental rates are 30%-40% lower than 2019 levels. Amidst the deterioration, inquiries from prospective BPO tenants have increased and should lead to higher sign-ups in 2022.

Residential revenue drops on cancellations; prices and NPLs flat

Residential revenue of three of the developers dropped sharply QoQ and YoY as sales cancellations caused the reversal of previously recognized revenue. 3Q21 was the first quarter since the start of the pandemic when developers could cancel sales contracts with delinquent buyers. Default rates since last year were said to be comparable to pre-COVID levels but multiple quarters worth of cancellations accumulated and were recorded in 3Q21. Meanwhile, aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover rose 7% QoQ (+6% YoY) to P84bn, 33% below the 2019 quarterly average. Similar to previous quarters, the shift in demand in favor of horizontal projects (house and lot or lot only) in provincial areas (vs. Metro Manila condos) is still evident.

Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) were generally flat QoQ. Prices of most units are 10%-25% below 2019 levels, while some units are being offered at prices that are 25%- 35% lower. Our channel checks show that in the primary market, some units are priced up to 10% lower than 2019 levels (due to discounts). The banking industry mortgage NPL ratio was unchanged at 9.4% in 2Q21.

Mall revenue rises despite lockdown; expecting a surge in 4Q21

Despite the hard lockdown, mall revenue of the three largest landlords grew 15% YoY (2% QoQ). While this is still 62% below the 3Q19 level, the reopening of the economy in 4Q21 should lead to a surge in mall operations, particularly in Metro Manila (which has the highest vaccination rates).

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

LTG declares P0.60/sh special div

LT Group declared a special dividend of P0.60/share to stockholders on record as of Dec 6, payable not later than Dec 15. This brings the total dividend for the year to P1.08/share and is equivalent to 8% of LTG’s share price at the start of the year. This also exceeds Regis dividend estimates of P0.82/share for FY21. Assuming this level is sustained, the annual dividend yield on the current price is 11%. LTG trades at 4.0x FY22E PE and a 68% discount to NAV.

Megaworld launches first projects in Bulacan township

Megaworld launched shophouses and commercial lots in Northwin, Bulacan with 16 hectares and a potential total sales value of P6bn. “Northwin Global City” comprises 85 hectares in the municipalities of Marilao and Bocaue, Bulacan. Northwin can be accessed via the Marilao exit of the North Luzon Expressway. It will also host one of the stations of the upcoming Manila-Clark railway project. Regis initial estimates suggest that Northwin could add c. 2% to Megaworld’s NAV. MEG is currently trading at 57% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 7.3X FY22E P/E.

3Q/9M21 Scorecard - Recovery on the cards

Aggregate earnings were up 47% YoY for stocks under Regis coverage, and 44% for PSEi constituents. But net profits are still, on balance, 30-35% below their pre-Covid (9M2019) levels. Compared to Regis forecasts, the headline numbers suggest an even-ish split of beats vs misses (16 vs 19 respectively). However, note that the banks were only ahead because of Regis high provision forecasts; PPOP was actually quite soft. Ex-banks, the count would skew heavily to the “misses” (12 ahead vs 19 behind).

Illicit cigarette sales up

PMFTC director for illicit trade prevention Raul Academia told the House Committee on ways and means that the tobacco firm’s latest survey showed illicit cigarettes hiked their market share to 8.6% this year from about 5-5.5% last year. Academia said it was hard to determine if bulk of illicit sticks were mainly smuggled or illegally manufactured here. It was flagged in the BIR report that enforcement activities against illicit trade have declined in 2021. Five raids and three tax evasion cases filed in 2021 vs 11-14 raids a year between 2018 and 2020. The growing share of illicit cigarettes could mean lower price hikes relative to the ones implemented in 2019 and 2020.

Cebu Pacific weekly seats up to 33% of pre-pandemic levels in November

Jefferies’ airline capacity survey showed Cebu Pacific’s weekly seat capacity rose 39k from November 1-25, equivalent to 33% of pre-pandemic levels. This is 10% higher MoM; October 2021 stood at 30% of pre-pandemic levels. Additionally, this brings 4Q-to-date capacity to 32% of pre-Covid levels, highest since the pandemic started. Regis estimates CEB will break-even on EBITDA at c. 40% of pre-Covid seat capacity; Cash burn will likely be plugged if capacity reaches 50-60% of pre-Covid levels.

 

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Infra spend flat MoM in September

Government infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) stood at P71.2bn in September (+25% YoY, flat MoM). This brings YT-September overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P807.5bn (+36% YoY). Additionally, this is 79% of the government’s FY21E target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY). For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn on infrastructure (+25% YoY).

BOP posts US$1.14bn surplus in October

The country’s overall balance of payments position posted a surplus of US$1.14bn in October 2021. This is a reversal from the US$412mn BOP deficit In September 2021 but lower than the US$3.44bn surplus in the same period a year ago. The National Government deposited US$1.59bn in the BSP from its Retail Onshore Dollar Bonds during the period. This brings YT-October BOP surplus to US$476mn, lower than the US$10.3bn surplus in the same period last year. The BSP is looking at a BOP surplus position of US$4.1bn in FY21 and US$1.7bn in FY22.

PH reopening to fully vaxxed tourists before yearend; Quarantine shortened for vaccinated

OFWs, balikbayans. The Philippines has approved a plan to allow entry to fully vaccinated foreign tourists from “green list” countries soon according to Tourism Secretary Berna Romulo-Puyat. There are currently 44 countries and territories on the Philippines coronavirus "green list," including China, Japan and India; however, the United States is not on the list. Guidelines are expected to be released before the end of the month. Meanwhile, the quarantine period for fully vaccinated Filipinos from “yellow list” countries has been shortened to 3 days from 5 days previously beginning Nov 22. The rule covers returning overseas Filipinos and OFWs who have undergone pre-departure RT-PCR tests within 72 hours. This will benefit the following companies: Cebu Air (CEB PM), Bloomberry (BLOOM PM), and Megawide (MWIDE PM).

House panel approves bill extending validity of 2021 budget

The House of Representatives’ Appropriations Committee approved House Bill 10373 extending the validity of the 2021 General Appropriations Act until Dec 31, 2022. The Bill will now be submitted to the plenary for deliberation and approval. As of end-September 2021, the government has released 96.5% of its P4.506tn FY21E budget to various government agencies. However, disbursements as of end-September 2021 have reached only P3.376tn (+11.7% YoY).

Bureau of Customs collections up 17% YoY in YT-October

The Bureau of Customs collected P525.4bn in YT-October, up 17% YoY and exceeded its target for the period by 2.4%. The Bureau is tasked to collect P617bn this year (+15% YoY). The YT-October 2021 collections are just a tad below its YT-October 2019 collections of P528bn. This implies collections for October alone rose c. 10% YoY to P55.6bn. The government cited ongoing programs, like fuel marking, helped the agency surpass its revenue targets.

Pace of vaccinations improves further

As of November 21, the 7-day average vaccinations figure rose to 840,973/day from 788,294/day the previous week. Around 31% of the Philippine population (33.6mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 10, 66% of the Metro Manila population (8.9mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 134mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 76mn doses (as of November 21). The Philippines is targeting to administer 5mn doses/day from November 29 to December 1. Beginning December 1, on-site workers for both the private and public sectors must be fully vaccinated against COVID-19. This rule will only apply to areas with “stable” supply of vaccines. The country has started administering booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities.

 

Vaccine willingness improves further in Sept

Willingness to get the Covid-19 vaccine improved again in September, according to a Social Weather Stations (SWS) survey published earlier this month. Nation-wide, 64% of adults were willing to get the jab (including the 35% that had already received at least one dose). This is up from the 55% in June, and 32% in May. Vaccine reluctance dropped to just 18% of adults, from 21% in June and 33% in May. Adults who were “uncertain” also dropped to 19% in September, down from 24% in June and 35% in May.

 

Metro Manila residents were by far more willing to be vaccinated, with 87% willing (including 71% already jabbed), up from 75% in June and 41% in May. Anti-vaxxers were down to just 7%, from 8% in June and 30% in May. Those on the fence fell to just 6% in September, from 16% in June and 30% in May.

 

However, reluctance is still quite high, particularly in areas in the Visayas and in Mindanao, with both at 24-25% (combining the “surely not” and “probably not” responses). For those in Luzon (ex-Metro Manila), about 15% are still anti-vaxxers are about. That said, willingness to get the jab improved across all geographies.

 

This will be a challenge in getting nation-wide vaccination rates up. While Metro Manila has already vaccinated 73% of its population with at least one dose, the national average is just 40%. And while some 77mn doses have been administered, Regis estimates there are close to 60mn doses in inventory, some of which might be nearing expiration dates. Getting the jabs to the provinces is difficult, especially across more than 7,100 islands. Add to that poor infrastructure – from roads to medical centers, to refrigeration – outside the major city/town centers.

 

The government aims to administer 15mn doses over the three days from 29 November to 01 December. To date the highest single-day total has been just over 1mn. Regis is skeptical of the government reaching its goal. But any surge in vaccinations would indeed be a welcome development.

 

 

Mobility at new highs (20-21 Nov)

Mobility data keeps rising, with nation-wide retail already slightly above the Jan/Feb 2020 baseline. All indicators posted new Covid-era highs. Interestingly, though, the nation-wise numbers seem better than Metro Manila. This despite the significantly higher vaccination rates within the capital (c70%) compared to the national average (c30%). One possible explanation is that Metro Manila has adapted more to a) e-commerce, and b) remote work, given much better internet connectivity. Either way, the data is hugely positive, and points to an even better December.

Budget deficit narrows to 6-month low in October

The government ran a P64.3bn budget deficit in October, down MoM and lowest level since April 2021. On a YoY basis, the budget deficit in October was up 5% YoY. Meanwhile, tax revenues improved 3% MoM and 7.5% YoY; Tax revenues for October were 7% below pre-Covid levels. Expenditures in October dropped 23% MoM and was the lowest since January 2021, with primary spending also lowest this year. This brings YT-October budget deficit to P1.2tn, up 28% YoY.
