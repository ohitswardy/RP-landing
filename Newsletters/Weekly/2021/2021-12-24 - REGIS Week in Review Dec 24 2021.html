REGIS REPORT

24 December 2021

Heading into the holidays, the Philippines shed 1.6% WoW, after two straight weeks of gains. 

With traders in holiday mode, average daily turnover was notably thin, reaching only US$90m. Foreigners were sellers for US10m this week with outflows seen in Jollibee (-2.2% WoW), FPH (-1.1% WoW), Ayala Land (+1.1% WoW), and URC (+0.7% WoW).  Metrobank (+3.0% WoW) and AC Energy (+6.0% WoW) however saw inflows. 

On local Covid news,  boosters are now to be made available to fully vaccinated individuals after 3 months from the last jab, down from 6 months previously. This is likely in response to the Omicron variant as well as the growing stockpile of vaccine doses in the country. Regis estimates there are close to 90m doses in inventory across the country; vaccinations are at around 1mn jabs per day. The new 3-month time frame means over 21m Filipinos are now eligible for the booster. The use of the Pfizer Covid-19 vaccine for children aged 5 to 11 was also approved by the Philippine FDA.

Investors also digested Covid-era highs in terms of mobility (data from Google as of Dec 18). While high mobility numbers lend optimism for 4Q21 earnings/GDP, the recent Typhoon (Rai/Odette) that ravaged parts of central and southern Philippines dampened growth expectations.  Regis estimates regions affected by the typhoon account for c22% of the country’s GDP. ‘

Corporate news this week was acquisition-filled.

Union Bank (-3.3% WoW) is acquiring the Citi’s consumer business in the Philippines.  The purchase will include Citi’s credit card and personal loans, personal deposits, and wealth management businesses. It will also include real estate assets (including  the Citibank Square in Eastwood, 3 branches, 5 wealth centers, and 2 “branch lite” locations). Citi is currently the #3 player in credit cards in the Phils.

The total consideration is estimated at P55bn -- a P45.3bn premium to the P9.7bn “equity required” based on the risk weighting of the assets bought. Since UBP is buying a portfolio of assets, price to book is much less relevant than the income/cashflow stream. Management puts the deal at 9.3x PE, lower than UBP’s 14x and thus suggesting a potentially highly accretive transaction.

The acquisition is transformative as it would propel UBP into one of the major players in the consumer lending and wealth management markets. Regis estimates the deal could take UBP to the fourth largest in credit card receivables, and the sixth largest in wealth management AUM.  

The bank’s Board also approved a P40bn rights issue to fund the acquisition. The rights issue will be fully underwritten by UBP’s major shareholders Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+1.6% WoW), Insular Life, and the Social Security System.

Elsewhere, the Century Pacific group announced two acquisitions.  The first one was the purchase of the “Ligo” brand of canned sardines by Century Pacific (-2.2% WoW).  Ligo is a leading brand in the category, with over 60 years of heritage and is brand that commands premium pricing.  Regis estimates that Ligo has 25%-30% share in the sardine category. CNPF is currently #3 in this category with around 15% market share. 

The second transaction involves Shakey’s (+9.7% WoW) acquisition of food kiosk brand Potato Corner.  Potato Corner has over 1,000 stores and over 600 MSME franchisees.  PIZZA will own and operate all company-owned stores and will be the brand owner and franchisor of stores domestically and internationally.

RESEARACH
Philippine Power - The cost of de-carbonization
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Expensive summer ahead

As the world rapidly moves towards decarbonizing power source, countries like the Philippines are left in a bind. With sweeping regulation and financial incentives to steer away from coal, a looming power shortage awaits Filipinos still mostly reliant on fossil fuel. And while we are seeing a rapid build-up in solar and wind farms, the lower availability of RE will mean the Philippines will need to build 4-5x more in headline capacity to even begin displacing coal and meet the country’s growing power requirements.

Coal’s last hurrah

Amidst an imminent shortage, the GN Power Dinginin plant (2x668MW) will be critical in averting blackouts over the next year. Assuming AP’s coal plant, now more than eight years in the making, does go fully operational by end 2022, demand growth in the Philippines will likely be met, albeit with a very slim margin. The plant is substantial enough to keep coal’s contribution to Luzon’s headline capacity at 45% over the next two years, despite more than 1,000MW of solar projects currently in the pipeline. For AP, GNPD will push coal from 58% to just under 70% of attributable capacity.

Mostly RE pipeline not enough supply over medium-term

There is, however, no shortage of renewable energy projects in the pipeline. Of the more than 2,400MW capacity additions expected between 2021 and 2024, about 90% is expected to come from RE, with half of the total amount coming from solar due to improved viability (cost, battery tech). The aggressive movement away from coal means the lowest cost baseload option is removed from the equation and a growing portion of the grid is supplied by intermittent RE plants.

Higher WESM prices, medium-term supply-demand beneficial to those building plants regardless of fuel source

At a time when the Philippines is teetering on the brink of shortage, this means generally higher power prices and decreased security of supply. As in past instances when reserves fell low, we expect electricity spot prices to shoot up, benefiting plants selling to WESM. Prices for short term RES contracts are likely to trend in the same direction, while gencos vying for long term contracts with DUs should also be in a good bargaining position.

Which stocks to buy? AP is our top pick; BUYS on FGEN and MER

Among the gencos, AP is our top pick – it has substantial capacity coming on[1]line which will immediately translate to earnings and about a third of output is sold to RES which reprice more frequently. We also like FGEN’s valuations but near-term growth is unlikely to excite during the reopening. On the other hand, we think ACEN is now overvalued, and risk disappointing investors with less[1]than-perfect execution. Outside the gencos, we have a Buy on Manila Electric (MER PM) but downgrade Semirara (SCC PM) from Buy to Hold.

Consumer loan growth dragged down by car loans; NPLs up for mortgages, car loans

The banking sector’s consumer loans contracted 2.9% YoY in 3Q21, slightly lower than the -2.8% in 2Q21. On a QoQ basis, consumer loans were still down 1.4% in 3Q21. The QoQ drop was entirely driven by the Motor Vehicle segment, which fell another 6.7% QoQ, and 17% YoY in 3Q21. Mortgages were still up 5.6% YoY, but that’s still the slowest YoY print since 2010. To the upside, mortgages were already up 0.9% QoQ. Credit card receivables improved to -0.3% YoY (from -2.5% in 2Q and -10.3% in 1Q21), and already up 0.5% QoQ. Salary loans were down 1.1%, and 5% QoQ.

 

Consumer loan NPL ratios were flat, at 9.90% in 3Q21, from 9.86% in 2Q. The good news is that in nominal PHP terms, consumer NPLs were already down 1.2% QoQ. Mortgage NPL ratio edged up 16bps QoQ to 9.58% (from 9.42% in 2Q21). For Motor Vehicles, NPL ratio jumped another 30bps QoQ to 12.70%, but in nominal terms, NPLs were down 4.4% QoQ. Credit card NPL ratio improved sharply to 7.96% (vs 8.55% in 2Q). Salary loan NPL ratio improved slightly to 7.16%, from 7.30%.

 

During the 3Q21 briefings, banks seemed to be turning more positive on mortgages, and were more willing to relax credit standards. This was likely because the recovery rates on the sale of foreclosures were better than the banks expected. SECB specifically mentioned in their 3Q call that profits from asset sales surprised management. However, they seemed more wary regarding car loans. MBT further explained that, given the shorter tenor of car loan (3-5yrs initial maturity), loans could mature faster than the industry can write new loans, which could result in lower loan balances for at least a few more quarters.

The recovery in consumer loans will depend greatly on the government’s relaxation of Covid-19 restrictions. As the economy opens up, employment should also return, and help repair household incomes and balance sheets.

Alvin Chau to lose control of Suncity listco

Suncity Group said in a filing that it had been informed that Alvin Chau’s privately-controlled company had been served with a letter claiming his 4.99mn shares of Suncity Group Holdings over an unpaid loan. Mr. Chau pledged the Suncity shares as guarantee. The letter stated that a group of lenders together with Wooco Secretarial Services will have the power to sell, transfer or dispose all or any part of the stake previously owned by Mr. Chau. Suncity Group said that enforcement by Wooco may lead to a change in control of the company. As of this announcement, no information is available to Philippine subsidiary Suntrust Home Developers (SUN PM) as to how Wooco will enforce the securities. Suncity is the parent company of Fortune Noble which holds 51% of the outstanding capital of SUN PM.

This is one of a number of measures to promote competition. PLDT introduced new postpaid plans in an apparent attempt to attract switchers, which subsequently prompted Globe to follow suit.

 

UBP to buy Citi retail / wealth mgt assets; announces P40bn rights

Union Bank (UBP PM) disclosed that its board has approved the acquisition of Citi’s consumer business in the Philippines. To fund it, the board also approved a P40bn rights issue. While the purchase will catapult UBP’s consumer and wealth management businesses, it will come at a fairly steep cost of roughly 3.5x P/bk.
 

According to UBP’s disclosure, the purchase will include the following (data as of 1H21, with comparative data in the table, below):

Credit card and personal loans, with a gross value of P59.7bn

Personal deposits worth P67.8bn

Wealth management, with AUM of P95bn

Real estate assets including  the Citibank Square in Eastwood, 3 branches, 5 wealth centers, and 2 “branch lite” locations

 

The acquisition will transform UBP into one of the major players in the consumer lending and wealth management markets. Citi is the country’s 3rd largest credit card issuer. Meanwhile, Regis estimates UBP’s credit card book at only around P8bn (outside the Top 10, and less than 10% of the market leader, BDO). The deal will also more than double UBP’s assets under management. All of this of course depends on whether UBP will be able to retain all of Citi’s 1mn customers after the deal.

 

UBP will pay a P45.3bn premium to net book value of P17.8bn (subject to due diligence, and closing terms), which puts the acquisition at roughly 3.5x Price to book. To fund the transaction, UBP’s board approved a rights issue meant to raise up to P40bn. The bank’s major shareholders – Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM), Insular Life, and the Social Security System (SSS) – have committed to subscribe to their share, as well as to any rights shares not taken up by minorities.

 

The Citi businesses are indeed attractive, and transformative to UBP’s current business. The additional 1mn customers could also help in UBP’s push into digital banking. However, the hefty premium paid gives us pause. Not only will it likely depress ROEs short term. But also, the premium will necessitate that UBP grow these businesses aggressively to overcome the drag.

 

Clarification:

Firstly, the Net Assets of P17.8bn shown was NOT the basis of the deal. Instead, it’s the P9.7bn of “equity required” for the purchase. So the total consideration is P55bn, not P63bn. Again, still subject to closing terms, etc. But in the end Price to Book is not a useful metric. The transaction is basically an asset sale, and ultimately it’s about the cash flow / income stream from the (net) assets purchased. According to our contacts at UBP, they valued the Citi portfolio at 9.3x PE, compared to UBP’s own 14x PE. So it looks potentially highly accretive. As Regis said earlier, though, the PE assumes that all the Citi customers stay with UBP. The “discount” to UBP’s own PE seems to allow for 30-35% reduction in the portfolio’s profitability. The challenge will be a) to limit the churn of existing clients, and b) to grow the businesses quickly.

BOP posts US$123mn deficit in November

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$123mn in November 2021 which is a reversal from the US$1.14bn surplus in October. This brings YT-November BOP surplus to US$353mn, down from US$11.79bn surplus in the same period last year. The BSP is targeting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn by end-2021.

Pace of vaccinations slows further

As of December 19, the 7-day average vaccinations figure dropped to 626,900/day from 676,500/day the previous week. Typhoon Odette (Rai) derailed the government’s second major vaccination drive (from Dec 15 to 17). Around 40% of the Philippine population (43.5mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 69% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 184mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 101mn doses (as of December 19). The government is targeting to inoculate 54mn Filipinos by end-2021; 77mn by end-1Q22; 90mn by end-2Q22.

Senate 3rd Reading Version on Public Service Act Amenments

Regis quotes the most relevant portion (Sec 16) of the version approved on 3rd reading by the Senate:

 

“An entity controlled by or acting on behalf of the foreign government or foreign state-owned enterprises shall be prohibited from owning capital in any public service classified as critical infrastructure:

 

Provided, That the prohibition shall apply only to investments made after the effectivity of this Act;

 

Provided further, That state-owned enterprises which own capital prior to the effectivity of this Act are prohibited from investing in additional capital which will exceed the allowed equity upon effectivity of this Act;

 

Provided finally, that notwithstanding the immediately preceding clause, the sovereign wealth funds and independent pension funds of each state may collectively own up to 30% of the capital such public services.”

 

Note that the bill specifically names telecommunications a being those considered “critical infrastructure”. If you read this Section (16), it actually appears that China Telecom is prohibited from owning any stake in Dito at all (read the first sentence) but it gets a pass since it already owns 40%. However, if you read the second-to-last sentence above, the bill would specifically prohibit it from putting in any more capital that exceeds the allowed equity (which, based on the 1st sentence, seems to be zero). The last sentence I quote above, meanwhile, seems to give Singtel a pass on Globe since Singtel is technically owned by a sovereign wealth fund. If this bill is passed according to how the Senate approved it, I believe Dito is in jeopardy as China Telecom would not be allowed to put in any more capital above zero. The final bicameral version, however, is still to be threshed out. 

Typhoon ‘Odette’ may dampen PHL recovery

Agriculture and productivity losses caused by Typhoon Odette (Rai) could significantly impact fourth-quarter economic expansion according to Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua. The gov’t is awaiting a full impact/damage report and reconstruction efforts could offset the economic damage. A total of 11,859 families or 41,434 individuals were affected by the typhoon in 249 barangays in the regions of Bicol, Western Visayas, Eastern Visayas, Northern Mindanao, and Caraga.

Mobility at new highs, again (18 Dec)

New Covid-era highs. Again. Recall that Google uses Jan/Feb 2020 as a base, not comparing to Dec 2019. But note that they’re all also significantly higher (+20 to 30 %age points) than the data for Dec 2020. Retail/Recreation movement nation-wide, and for Metro Manila both posting numbers exceeding the pre-pandemic bases. Transit Station and Workplace data are still negative, but are slowly closing in on positive territory.

 

CAVEAT!! This data was as of 18 Dec, just about when Typhoon Rai/Odette hit. By our estimates, those regions account for c22% of the country’s GDP. Airports on the islands the region are either not operating, or are at reduced capacity. Many areas are running out of water and fuel, and may not even have electricity until early January.

 

While the relaxation of mobility restrictions from end-October were looking bullish for 4Q21 GDP/earnings, it is difficult to assess the impact of the typhoon. Apart from the obvious tragic loss of life and property, the areas that were worst-hit – Cebu, Bohol, and Siargao – are popular tourist destinations that were expecting a surge in arrivals over the holidays.

Marcos/Sara Duterte lead President/VP survey

The tandem of former senator Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. and Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte-Carpio topped the December 2021 survey of Pulse Asia. For President, 53% of respondents picked Marcos, who also led in terms of geographical areas and majority of classes. Meanwhile, VP Leni Robredo took 2nd place with 20%, up from just 8% in the previous survey while Isko Moreno and Pacquiao dropped to 8% each. For VP, Sara Duterte got 45% while Senate President Vicente Sotto III had 31%. A total of 2,400 respondents participated in the survey, which was conducted from December 1-6 using face-to-face interviews.

Government reduces interval for booster dose

Filipinos can now receive a COVID-19 booster dose 3 months after receiving their second dose from 6 months previously. As of end-September, 21.4mn Filipinos were fully vaccinated. Additionally, 1.15mn booster doses have been administered (as of December 20). Around 41% of the Philippine population (44.2mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. The Philippines has received a total of 190mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 102mn doses (as of December 20).

Duterte declares state of calamity in 6 regions

President Duterte placed regions 4B, 6, 7, 8, 10, and 13 under a state of calamity to hasten relief and rehab efforts. The much needed calamity funds will be released on or before Friday. This will also be an effective mechanism to control the prices of goods and commodities in the areas. The latest tally showed 156 deaths, 275 injured, and 37 missing.

BSP issues memo on bank liabilities from fraud

The BSP issued a Memorandum reminding banks “…any broad and all-encompassing disclaimer of legal liability on the part of the bank for any losses incurred by their customers…” is not in compliance with existing regulations. The terms and conditions should not lead to significant imbalance in the parties’ rights and obligations arising under the contract, to the detriment of the consumer.

FDA approves Pfizer vaccine for 5 to 11 year-old children

The Philippine FDA issued Emergency Use Authorization for the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID vaccine to be used for 5 to 11 year-old children. Vaccines to be administered on this age group will have lower dosage and concentration than those administered on adults. The current vaccines in the Philippines cannot be administered on this age group. Around 42% of the Philippine population (45.3mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. The Philippines has received a total of 192mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 104mn doses (as of December 20).
