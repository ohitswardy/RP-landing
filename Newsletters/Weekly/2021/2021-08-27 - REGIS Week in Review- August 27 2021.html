REGIS REPORT

27 August 2021

The PSEi  advanced 2.3% this week to close at 6786.62 index points, propelled by the telcos.

It was a monster week for the telcos, with Globe (+32.1% WoW) surging on euphoria surrounding its fintech unit GCash.  PLDT (+17.5% WoW), with its own fintech unit Paymaya, surged as well.  Paymaya and GCash have seen widespread adoption of  their services during the pandemic. Excitement in the sector took other telcos Converge (+6.5% WoW)  and DITO (+10.1% WoW) higher, despite the two not having their own e-wallets.

Globe parent Ayala Corp (+10.4% WoW) also led. 

Philippines Clean Energy Holding, Inc  will be launching a tender offer for between 3.0% to 5.7% of the shares of First Gen (+2.0% WoW). It is unclear who is behind the company at this point but many suspect it is still KKR as its unit Valorous Asia Holdings  already owns around 12.59% of First Gen.

Meanwhile, COVID infections remained high (with a recent spike to over 18,000 in daily cases).  Vaccinations for the week ended Aug 22 dropped to 369k/day, down from the averages in previous weeks.  To date, 17m people (16% of population) have had at least one dose with 13m (12%) of those now fully vaccinated. The numbers are higher in Metro Manila – with 54% having at least one dose and 32% fully vaccinated.

Foreigners returned as buyers this week for US$41m amid the e-payments frenzy following big upgrades from the street. GLO and TEL alone saw US$37m and US$12m in inflows respectively. The two telcos accounted for 24% of total turnover.

Philippine Consumer - 2Q21: Low base gives flattering YoY picture, but demand remains soft
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Mobility, inflation crimp consumer spending

The sector saw a sharp YoY bounce in top line and earnings due to an extremely low base in the previous year. Looks, however, can be deceiving as underlying consumer spending is still weak amidst increased unemployment, limited mobility, and high inflation. Much of the sector’s full year performance will now largely hinge on the reopening of the economy during what should normally be a strong 4Q.

Underlying demand remains weak despite YoY bounce

Aggregate sector earnings were up +274% YoY in 2Q21, coming off an exceptionally low base. Restaurants and non-essential retail – which were operating in a very limited capacity during the hard lockdown (“ECQ”) in 2Q20 – saw a turnaround in 2Q21.

Notwithstanding the seemingly strong YoY numbers, underlying consumer demand was weak. We estimate that non-grocery retail and restaurant same[1]store sales were still 30-40% below pre-pandemic levels. Amidst high inflation and increased unemployment, consumers focused their spending on staples (instant noodles, canned food) and cut back on discretionary spending. Higher input cost also weighed on margins as companies held back on price increases until the latter part of the quarter amidst the fragile consumer environment.

Hoping for a 4Q recovery

The sector is unlikely to see a sequential improvement in 3Q21 given the new round of lockdowns in the Greater Manila area in August. While the new round of lockdown measures is less restrictive than previous ones, consumer mobility remains limited. A reopening during the seasonally strong 4Q, coupled with election spending, could provide consumption with the much-needed boost before the year ends

Philippine Telecoms - 2Q Recap: Revenue shifts with mobility
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Home broadband take-up remains robust

Demand for broadband services continues to be buoyed by personal mobility constraints and demand from those working and studying from home. Conversely, mobile revenue has been weak, notwithstanding what the YoY comps say. We reckon mobile revenue should recover when personal incomes and mobility do.

Broadband net adds still rising

Overall broadband revenue (wired and fixed wireless) among the three top operators (PLDT, Globe and Converge ICT) were quite robust in the quarter, rising by a third (+33%) YoY and +5% QoQ. This is notwithstanding fixed wireless revenue is likely being eroded as fiber becomes more available. Broadband (wired) net adds among the three big operators amounted to 372k in 2Q, >20% higher than the previous quarter. While PLDT’s relative share of wired subscribers is declining (down 1pt to 54%), its share of net adds is rising (41% vs 37%). 2Q/1H21 was marked by aggressive migration by both PLDT and Globe of their broadband subscribers from “legacy” DSL to fiber. For 1H, we estimate close to 700k subscribers were switched over to fiber or VVDSL, over 15% of the existing base.

Wireless revenue under pressure; Globe’s mobile data yield drops below PLDT’s

2Q mobile revenue may be up 6% YoY but this was against a depressed base. Revenue was actually lower QoQ (<1%) as incomes remain under pressure and people were confined to their homes. New mobile operator Dito recently claimed to have 2m subscribers already but neither of the incumbents thinks its impact is meaningful (yet). We note that Globe’s yield on mobile data is now down to P21/GB. This marks the first time that Globe’s yield on mobile data is below PLDT’s since we began keeping track of this. It suggests more aggressiveness on price, which is may be responsible for Globe gaining revenue share in 1Q21 and maintaining it in 2Q.><1%) as incomes remain under pressure and people were confined to their homes. PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine E-payments - Going Digital – the future of money; notes from REGIS roundtable series
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

E-payments surging; but more opportunities lie ahead

We hosted BSP Assistant Governor Edna Villa, Mr Paolo Azzola (CSO of PayMaya), Ron Hose (founder of Coins.ph) and Mr Luis Sia (co-founder of PayMongo), discussing the Philippine mobile payments industry. A progressive regulatory framework, and the ongoing pandemic have helped digital payments surge from 1% of transactions in 2013, to 17% as of 1H2020. The BSP aims to hit 50% by 2023. Further initiatives such as the National ID, EGov, and “pull payments” should broaden the products that operators can provide, and ultimately give access to capital and better service to more Filipino consumers and SMEs.

BSP as progressive regulator – financial inclusion through “safe innovation”

Since 2009, the BSP has seen e-payments as key to accelerating financial inclusion. In 2015, it set up the National Retail Payments System (NRPS), allowing for fast, inter-operable system, with strong governance and security. While the Philippines has had mobile wallets since 2003, the NRPS saw electronic transactions explode from an average of 25mn a month in 2013, to 440mn in 2018. The Covid pandemic saw that surge again to 782mn/month as of 1H2020. The BSP estimates that, while only 29% of adults had formal bank accounts in 2019, almost half (49%) had e-money accounts as of 2020.

Further initiatives needed – inter-operability, and identity

The BSP and the private panelists agreed that more can be done to improve take-up and innovation. There is pending legislation to make all government transactions digital, including salaries, social benefits, and payments to suppliers. The panelists looked forward to improved inter-operability, a national QR code standard, and the ability to do “pull payments” (direct debit and request[1]to-pay). The National ID will play a critical role, not only in the ability to provide credit services, but also to protect against fraud, money laundering, etc.

The future of money – crypto, digital PHP, digital banks

While the panelists differed on crypto, the consensus was that it had to evolve beyond a speculative “virtual asset”. The BSP is also studying the potential of a central bank digital currency (CBDC) to further reduce friction in payments. The BSP has also started to issue digital banking licenses. While PayMaya has applied for its own license, the other panelists thought tie-ups could be just as effective.

All about the customer – a shot across the banking sector’s bow?

The surge of e-payments has given smaller banks and electronic money issuers (EMIs) a “seat at the table” in shaping the future of money in the Philippines. That is, money will flow to whoever treats customers well, and not to who has more branches or capital. The BSP supports (safe) innovation, and the panel believes that bringing financial services to Filipino consumers and SMEs (beyond the 5- 10% of the country served by the banks) is a national priority.

 
 

Corporate News

RLC’s REIT IPO priced at P6.45/share

RL Commercial REIT’s IPO was priced at P6.45/share. The ticker wil be RCR and will have a 6.0% dividend yield (based on prospectus). This could raise our RLC NAV by 3% (from P40.00/share currently). The IPO would raise P23.6bn and RCR would have a market cap of P64.2bn. The deal will have a firm offer of 3.34bn shares with an vverallotment option – 305.1mn shares. It will be composed of 14 office buildings –425,000sqm of leasable area which includes offices in Ortigas Center, Makati, Fort Bonifacio, Bridgetowne, Mandaluyong (Robinsons Forum), Cebu, Davao, Naga, and Tarlac. The total appraised value was P73bn and all the buildings are PEZA accredited.  These building have an occupancy rate of 99% with 3% are occupied by POGOs. The land will be leased from RLC for 99 years. Offer period will run from August 25 to September 3 and will have a listing date – September 14. RLC is trading at 9.5X FY22E P/E, 0.8X FY22E P/B, and 58% discount to NAV.

Toyota to cut September output by 40%

Toyota Motor Corp. of Japan will suspend output for several days in September, which would result in a 40% cut (c. 360k units) in production globally. Out of the 360k reduction, 140k will be at 14 Japanese plants, with the rest in US, China, Europe, and other Asian countries. In Japan, production of Rav4, Corolla, Prius, Camry, and Lexus RX will be affected, among others. The company cited spread of Covid-19 in Southeast Asia affecting suppliers in Vietnam and Malaysia. Toyota has maintained its 2021 global production target of 9.3mn vehicles. Meanwhile, Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) said that the reduction will not affect the local business according to TMP First VP Rommel Gutierrez. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 22% of FY21E profit. GTCAP is trading at a 15% discount to its market-based NAV of P615/sh.

 

 

PAL returns 2 planes, postpones new deliveries

Philippine Airlines returned two aircraft to its lessor in July; It also delayed aircraft deliveries meant for 2020/2021 to 2026-2030. As of end-June, PAL’s fleet size was lower at 95 aircraft from 97 in end-2020 with the removal of 1 bombardier and 1 A321-231. PAL’s filing for US Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection has been further delayed after one of the banks providing financing for the process backed out.

 
 

Macro News

BOP surplus widens to US$642mn in July

The country’s balance of payments reversed to a surplus of US$642mn in July from a deficit of US$312mn in June. This was due to proceeds from the government’s US$3bn global bond sale less deposit withdrawals of US$1.68bn, payment of debt obligations of US$465mn, and US$398mn net FX operations. This brings YT-July BOP deficit to US$1.3bn, a reversal from the US$4.117bn surplus a year ago due to a wider merchandise trade deficit.

Philippines receives another 362,700 Pfizer doses

The Philippines received another 362,700 Pfizer COVID vaccine doses. The country expects 22.7mn doses in August, including the 15.0mn that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 48.9mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 30.7mn doses (as of August 23).

Budget deficit narrows to P121bn in July

The National Gov’t recorded a P121bn deficit in July, down YoY and MoM. Revenues rose with both BIR and Customs collections improving. Meanwhile, expenditures modestly rose YoY due to base effects from disbursements of the second tranche of cash aid and timing of subsidy releases for Philheath and National Housing Authority. Expenditures of personnel services and infra outlays rose >6% YoY in July. This brings YT-July deficit to P873bn, up 20% YoY.

Philippines orders 10mn more Sinovac vaccine doses

The Philippines ordered an additional 10mn doses of CoronaVac, Sinovac’s COVID vaccine. The Philippines should receive a total of 176mn COVID vaccine doses by January 2022, including the 48.5mn that already arrived. Around 36mn of the 176mn are from Sinovac. The country expects 22.7mn doses in August, including the 14.6mn that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 48.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 30.4mn doses (as of August 22). 11.4mn doses have been administered in Metro Manila. The government aims to vaccinate 58 Filipinos by end-2021.

Foreign business groups oppose proposals to retain utility status of telecoms

The American, Australia-New Zealand, European, Japanese and Korean chambers of commerce in the Philippines expressed concerns over proposals to retain the utility status of telecoms during ongoing deliberations in the Senate. Senate Bill 2094 could amend the Public Service Act, which would change the definition of public utilities to allow more foreign investment in telecoms. The Constitution limits foreign ownership in public utilities to 40%

The business groups wrote that the PSA amendments would improve quality and pricing of internet connectivity. They added that it would help the country comply with commitments to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations to open investments to other members of the region. Some senators have raised concerns on lifting the foreign ownership restriction on national security concerns.

Gov’t submits 2022 budget to Congress; trims 2022 infrastructure budget to P1.18tn

The Department of Budget and Management submitted a 2022 budget of P5.024tn to Congress. This is 11.5% higher than the 2021 approved budget of P4.506tn equivalent to 22.8% of GDP. Under the proposed budget, P1.18tn will go to infrastructure, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP and is 15% YoY higher than the P1.019tn for 2021. However, infra budget is lower than the initial program of P1.29tn due to the implementation of the Mandanas ruling next year. The budget for public works is slightly down YoY while the transportation budget is 72% YoY higher due to ongoing rail transport projects (P110.9bn for NSCR railway and Metro Manila subway).

Pace of vaccinations slows further

The 7-day average vaccinations fell to 369,000/day; from 475,000/day the week before. The country expects 22.7mn doses in August, including the 14.6mn that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 48.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 30.4mn doses (as of August 22). 11.4mn doses have been administered in Metro Manila. The government aims to vaccinate 58 Filipinos by end-2021.

More hot money flows out in July

Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. “hot money”) reverted to a net outflow of US$340mn in July from a net inflow of US$335mn inflow in June, ending two straight months of net inflow. This was due to a 65% MoM decrease in gross inflows to US$730mn (of which 65% are PSE-listed securities) and a 40% MoM drop in gross outflows to US$1.1bn. The gov’t also cited rising Delta-related COVID infections, re-imposition of hard lockdowns, and vaccine supply constraints. This brings YT-July hot money net outflow to US$446mn, down from a large US$3.76bn net outflow in the same period last year.

ERC urges NGCP to pursue public offering

Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) said the National Grid Corp of the Philippines (NGCP) can pursue public offering by any mode as long as it meets the public listing requirement under its franchise by November 14, 2021. In a disclosure to the PSE, Synergy Grid and Development Philippines (SGP) said the regulator granted its motion for clarification regarding alternative mode of compliance. “NGCP can undertake public listing or in substitution submit proof of the listing of any company in the (exchange) which owns or controls at least 30% of its outstanding capital stock, as these manners of compliance are expressly provided under RA 9511”, quoting the ERC Order. However, the regulator added that this order should not be taken as allowing or prohibiting any mode of ownership dispersal and NGCP’s submission for compliance would still be deliberated by the ERC.
