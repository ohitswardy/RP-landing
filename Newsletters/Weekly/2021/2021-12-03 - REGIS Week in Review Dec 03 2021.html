REGIS REPORT

03 December 2021

The PSEi dropped 3.,1% this week, as global markets reflected concern over Omicron while prospects of accelerated tapering by the Fed added to jitters.  Funds flowed out this week, while fresh concerns and lack of near-term market catalysts prompted some domestic institutions to take some profits as well.

Foreigners were heavy sellers, with US$36m exiting this week (net).  Biggest outflows were spread across big caps URC, Ayala Land, BDO, Metrobank, Ayala Corp, and Globe. 

In response to the Omicron variant, new testing and quarantine protocols for inbound  international are being implemented.  This new hitch to a rebound in international tourism hurt Cebu Air (-9.7% WoW) this week. Unsurprisingly Bloomberry (-7.3% WoW) also reacted, leadding the PSEi laggard list this week.

The drop was nevertheless widespread, with only 3 of the 30 index names avoiding a loss this week.  Restaurant Jollibee (-8.5%) along with Ayala Corp (-5.7% WoW), BDO (-5.5% WoW) led a long list of decliners.

Omicron overshadowed positive on-the-ground COVID developments including new daily Covid-19 infections falling to 679 (7-day moving ave). The government was able to administer 7.6m Covid-19 vaccine doses during its 3-day vaccination campaign (Nov 29 - Dec 1), based on preliminary figures. It is also planning another 3-day period in December for another special drive. Meanwhile, the government is now allowing booster shots for all fully vaccinated adults. In the meantime, economic reopening continued, with Mobility based on Google data reaching another pandemic period high, up another 2-4 ppt WoW.  

Union Bank of the Philippines (+13.0% WoW) replicated its 13% surge last week. Bloomberg reported that  UBP has been selected as the preferred bidder for the consumer banking assets of Citibank in the Philippines.  If confirmed,  the deal could be transformative for UBP and catapult it to the top 3 in the credit card business. In a disclosure, UBP confirmed that it was in talks with Citigroup but that discussions were at a preliminary stage. 

Meanwhile, URC (-1.7% WoW) announced last Friday that it was acquiring a leading Malaysian biscuit firm, Munchy’s, in a ~US$450m deal. Based on 2020 financial statements, Regis estimates the valuation for Munchy’s is 4x EV/sales and 17-18x EV/EBITDA – a premium to current multiples for URC, adjusted for the sale of the Oceania business (2x EV/sales, 10-11x EV/EBITDA). URC outlined several revenue and cost synergies they are targetig including  cross selling products into MY and around the ASEAN region and scale advantages on manufacturing and distribution.

Universal Robina - Announces acquisition of leading Malaysian biscuit company
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

URC to acquire Malaysia-based Munchy Food Industries

URC announced it is acquiring from CVC Capital Partners 100% of Munchy Food Industries (‘Munchy’s’), a leading biscuits company based in Malaysia. The total consideration for the transaction is MYR1.925bn (cUSD450mn) enterprise value. Based on 2020 financial statements, we estimate the valuation for Munchy’s is 4x EV/sales and 17-18x EV/EBITDA – a premium to current multiples for URC, adjusted for the sale of the Oceania business (2x EV/sales, 10-11x EV/EBITDA). We believe the acquisition allows URC to scale up its business in SG/MY and to further expand its footprint around the ASEAN region.

Munchy’s – a leading biscuits player in Malaysia

Based on the company’s website and various news sources, Munchy’s is the largest biscuits player in Malaysia with an estimated market share of 21-22%. It also distributes its products to over 50 countries globally. Munchy’s product portfolio includes savoury crackers, sweet biscuit sandwiches, cookies, wafers, and packaged bread. The company was founded in 1991 by a local Malaysian family and acquired by CVC in 2018.

Valued at 4x EV/sales, 17-18x EV/EBITDA

We estimate the implied valuation for Munchy’s is 4x EV/sales and 17-18x EV/EBITDA based on reported 2020 sales of MYR419mn (USD99mn) and EBITDA of MYR93mn (USD22mnn). This is a premium to current multiples for URC, adjusted for the recent sale of the Oceania business (2x EV/sales, 10-11x EV/EBITDA). The transaction is expected to close in Dec.

Acquisition widens URC’s regional footprint

We view the acquisition as a positive strategic move for URC given its plans to further expand its reach within the region. We estimate Munchy’s will add c4-5% to URC’s consolidated sales and EBITDA, which does not seem significant, but will effectively double’s URC’s business in SG/MY. The Munchy’s brands and products appear to be complementary to URC’s current portfolio and generally target the same market as URC. The question now is if URC can generate meaningful synergies in order to justify the high multiple.

 
Universal Robina - More on Munchy Food acquisition
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Strategic fit, but ‘synergies’ could take time to materialize

URC hosted a briefing to provide further detail on its acquisition of Munchy Food. While URC could not provide specific targets, they outlined the several revenue and cost synergies they hope to achieve. These include 1) cross selling products into MY and around the ASEAN region and 2) scale advantages on manufacturing and distribution. We believe the acquisition is a strategic fit to URC, but any meaningful ‘synergies’ could take another one or two years to materialize.

URC to become #1 biscuit player in Malaysia

The acquisition of Munchy’s will make URC the number one biscuit player in MY with a combined market share of c25% and annual sales of cUSD160mn. Approximately 70% of Munchy’s sales are in MY, where it has 22.4% market share. It competes directly with market leader Mondelez (24.7% market share) and has more premium price points vs URC’s current offerings. The remaining c30% of Munchy’s sales are from its export/OEM business. URC currently generates USD50-60mn in annual sales in MY primarily from salty snacks and chocolates (70% of sales). URC MY has 3% market share in biscuits.

Looking beyond a challenged 2021

Much like the rest of the sector, 2021 has been a challenging year for Munchy’s given Covid-related lockdowns and elevated input cost. However, historical pre[1]Covid growth rates (top and bottom line) have tracked mid-to high-single digits. Between 2019-2020, Munchy’s saw a surprisingly large (160bps) expansion in EBITDA margin, attributable to strategies implemented by CVC, which acquired the company only in 2018.

‘Synergies’ on revenue and cost expected

URC outlined various revenue and cost synergies they hope to achieve, though did not give any specifics on the expected revenue and margin uplift from them. These synergies include 1) scale benefits from a bigger footprint in MY, 2) opportunities to cross-sell URC and Munchy’s products in their respective markets, and 3) consolidation of certain manufacturing processes and shared costs (A&P, R&D, sales & marketing, etc).

Strategic fit, but ‘synergies’ not likely to materialize soon

URC indicated that Munchy’s will continue to be run as a separate entity, at least until 2022. This means that any meaningful synergies (whether revenue or otherwise) could take some time to materialize. Overall, the acquisition of Munchy’s appears to be a good strategic fit for URC. Beyond the expanded footprint in MY, we believe there could be immediate advantages with respect to URC’s regional manufacturing capacity. Meaningful top line synergies will depend on URC’s ability to sell more products in MY using Munchy’s, and to cross-sell Munchy’s products in the broader ASEAN region.

 
Filinvest Land - 3Q21 presales jump on payment schemes; office vacancy flat QoQ at 30%
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M21 earnings flattered by tax benefits

3Q21 net profit rose 71% to P570mn, thanks to a reduction in interest expense. While 9M21 net profit of P3.2bn (+21%) looks in line with our forecast, the earnings are flattered by tax benefits (reduction of deferred tax liabilities) brought about by the CREATE law. Pretax profit and EBIT are well behind our forecasts due to weaker-than-expected margins and residential revenue.

Reservation sales jump on looser payment schemes, demand for houses

3Q21 presales surged 94% QoQ (53% YoY) to P7.0bn, 144% of the 2019 average. We believe the main reason for the jump was looser payment schemes. Buyers can now make a 10% down payment over 24 months, from 12 months pre-COVID. 9M21 reservation sales climbed 18% to P14.0bn, ahead of our forecast and already 90% of the 9M19 figure. Demand for horizontal projects in provincial areas was particularly strong, with house and lot units now comprising 53% of total sales, from 45% in FY20 (32% in FY19). OFW sales also recovered slightly and now account for 24% of residential sales, from 20% in FY20 (31% in FY19).

Buyer accounts were restructured to avoid defaults

Residential revenue grew 36% YoY, implying that sales cancellations are not a major issue. However, to avoid customer defaults, FLI allowed payment deferrals and in some cases, credited buyer payments to cheaper units.

Office vacancy flat QoQ at 30%

9M21 office revenue fell 29% to P3.6bn as office vacancy increased to 30%, from 12% in 3Q20 (30% in 2Q21). The increase in vacancy was almost entirely due to POGO closures. We believe it will not be easy to fill up the space vacated by POGOs since these tenants occupied mostly non-PEZA space. Office vacancy of Filinvest REIT, which comprises 57% of FLI’s office portfolio, increased mildly to 11% (from 10% in 2Q21) while Filinvest Land’s non-REIT portfolio had a vacancy of 52% (flat QoQ).

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

UBP wins Citi assets?

Bloomberg reported that Union Bank (UBP PM) is the “preferred bidder” for Citigroup’s consumer business in the Philippines, citing unnamed sources. If correct, UBP would have bested the Big Three, all of which had expressed interest in the portfolio. If true, the transaction could be hugely transformative for UBP. Regis wrote in a report in April that Citi’s credit card portfolio was estimated at around P70bn at end-2020, making it among the Top 3 in the country. Regis estimates that UBP’s total credit card receivables are less than P8bn. It could also fit in nicely with the group’s digital push, particularly with their digital banking license.

 

On valuations, there are lots of rumors swirling in the market, with some investors claiming to have heard a price of 4x P/bk. Bloomberg itself says that the sale “could value the assets at $1bn”. Trouble is, there is not enough publicly available data to make many conclusions. 

  

A few weeks ago, UBP applied to increase its authorized capital, signaling a potential capital call. As Regis mentioned though, this is all conjecture that is dependent on a) the veracity of UBP’s win, b) the pricing and structure of the sale, and c) where UBP lodges the assets.

 

MONDE and EMP could be added to the PSEi in Feb 2022 (Regis estimates)

Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) and Emperador (EMP PM) could be added to the PSE Index during the mid-Feb 2022 rebalancing, based on Regis estimates. MONDE has already met the liquidity requirements and will almost certainly be added to the PSE Index while EMP would have to meet liquidity requirements in December. If both stocks will be added to the PSEi in February, then the two PSEi constituents most likely to be removed are Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) and Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM). In order for a stock to be added to the PSE Index, it must meet certain criteria:

 

Free float must be at least 15% (20% beginning December 2022)

Its median value traded must be among the top 25% for 9 of the 12 months in review (minimum of 6 months for new IPOs)

Its 12-month average market cap must be among the highest   

 

Stocks that meet the first criteria can enter the PSEi if they are within the top 25 in terms of 12-month average market cap. MONDE has 31% public float while EMP has 15% public float

MONDE and EMP are currently in the top 20 in terms of 12-month average market cap. An existing PSE index constituent can also be removed if it falls to the 36th position (and below) in terms of 12-month average market cap (from the list of companies that pass the public float and liquidity requirements). BLOOM is currently ranked 36th

Macau arrest Suncity CEO over alleged gambling offences

Alvin Chau, the head of Macau’s biggest casino junket operator Suncity was arrested in Macau over his alleged links to illegal cross-border gambling and money-laundering syndicate. The arrest came a day after mainland authorities issued an arrest warrant over his suspected involving in the so called illegal cross-border gambling. Macau Police claims that it had been building its own case against Chau and that the arrests were unrelated to the mainland warrant.

 

Prosecutors in Zheijiang province have accused Chau of being in charge of a gambling syndicate that “causes severe damage to the social order of the country”. In 2019, Suncity was singled out by the mainland state media for cross-border gambling and illegal money flow through proxy betting and online gaming. Suncity Group is the largest junket operator in Macau with a market share of about 40%. It is also the biggest junket operator in the Philippines with rooms in all integrated resorts. It occupies 3 out of 20 junkets rooms in Solaire and its own wing in Okada Manila and Resorts World Manila. Regis estimates it accounts for 15-25% of VIP revenue pre-pandemic. VIP was 45-50% of total GGR pre-pandemic. Suncity Group, through listed firm Suntrust Home Developers (SUN PM), is also building RWM’s Westside City project where the Company shall finance the development and would be the exclusive operator of the Main Hotel Casino at Manila Bay. The continued to crackdown on overseas gambling could also deter the return of POGOs. A few months before the pandemic hit, China pressured the Philippines to ban POGOs because of cross-border money flow and “increased social problems”. In August 2020, China’s Ministry of Culture said it established a blacklist of overseas tourist destinations that promote gambling to mainland citizens.

Bank lending expands 3.5% YoY in October; M3 growth slightly decelerates

Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, rose 3.5% YoY in October. This is faster than the 2.7% YoY increase in September 2021. Meanwhile,loans from production activities rose 4.9% YoY, with sustained recoveries in manufacturing, real estate, transpo/storage, and information/communication sectors.

 

Consumer loans dropped 7.2%, milder than the 7.8% drop in September due to the slight YoY increase in credit card loans and the slower contraction in salary-based general purpose loan. Auto loans still contracting, down by 16% YoY and 1% MoM. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded 8.2% YoY to P14.6tn in October, slightly slower than the 8.3% (revised) growth in September.

 

CEB seat capacity rebounds strongly; More domestic destinations remove COVID tests

The latest airline capacity survey from Jefferies shows CEB’s seat capacity jumped to 56k for the week of Nov 29, which is 46% higher than its 10-week rolling average and about 48% of pre-Covid capacity. This spike is likely partly due to the November 30 holiday, as well as the reopening of domestic tourism. Around 32 domestic destinations remove COVID tests as pre-travel requirement.  More importantly, there are now 32 domestic destinations (from 12 in mid-November) that do not require swab tests. Note that CEB used to fly to 37 domestic destinations pre-Covid. On a side note, CEB is already flying its 459-seater A330 NEO aircraft on short-haul routes (e.g. Davao and General Santos), which suggests NAIA could be slot constrained again.  

 

 

 

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government to hold another vaccination drive from Dec 15 to 17

The government lowered its vaccination target for Nov 29 to Dec 1 to 3mn/day from 5mn/day previously due to lack of syringes. It will hold another vaccination drive from Dec 15 to 17. The government is targeting to inoculate 54mn Filipinos by end-2021; 77mn by end-1Q22 and 90mn by end-2Q22. As of Nov 27, 33% of the Philippine population (35.6mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 66% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 142mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 81mn doses (as of November 27).

Implementation of the Mandanas ruling seen to dampen economic growth

The implantation of the Mandanas ruling expanding the local govt’s share in national taxes next year would lead to a 3% lower economic growth according to Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III. The higher tax allocation for local government units (LGUs) would dampen spending efficiency because the National Government usually spends at double the pace. The government is looking at GDP to grow by 7-9% in 2022. To minimize the impact, the Finance department is reviewing a possible fiscal consolidation plan, or policies aimed at reducing the country’s deficit. Based on the 2022 proposed budget, LGUs will be tasked to spend 22% of the budget, up from 19% in previous years.

Pace of vaccinations drops slightly

As of November 28, the 7-day average vaccinations figure fell to 813.734/day from 840,973/day the previous week. Around 33% of the Philippine population (35.9mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 69% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 142mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 82mn doses (as of November 29). Beginning December 1, on-site workers for both the private and public sectors must be fully vaccinated against COVID-19. This rule will only apply to areas with “stable” supply of vaccines. The government is targeting to inoculate 54mn Filipinos by end-2021; 77mn by end-1Q22 and 90mn by end-2Q22.

Mobility at new highs

Another week, another new Covid-era high for mobility indicators across the board. Visits to retail/recreation are up 2-4 percentage points WoW, while visits to transit stations are up 3 percentage points WoW. To be fair, it’s possible that this was because of the not-quite-long-weekend (Tuesday 30 Nov holiday). But still. Regis thinks the trend will continue into the December holiday season. Anecdotally, the traffic in Metro Manila has built up enough that the mayors have partially reinstated the “coding” for vehicles during rush hour. While the Omicron variant may be an issue for international news, the case count in the Philippines has hovered below 1,000/day for the last week or two. All of which points to a continued uptick in December. Will this lead to a new spike in Covid cases in January?

NG debt swells to P12tn in October

The National Government’s outstanding debt rose to P11.97tn in October. Around 29% was sourced externally, 71% were domestic borrowings. This is equivalent to c. 63% of GDP (estimated at P18.9tn). The government has accumulated P2.18tn of additional debt so far this year and has also breached its end-2021 program of P11.73tn.

 

Senate approves 2022 budget on final reading

Senators unanimously passed the 2022 General Appropriations Bill worth P5.024tn (+11% YoY). Bulk of the budget will go to the government’s pandemic response, including benefits to healthcare workers, hiring of health professionals, development of COVID-19 treatments, among others. The Senate increased the Department of Health’s budget by 26% to P230bn from the original P182bn approved by the Lower House. Additional funds for the health sector were taken from the controversial National Task Force Against Local Communist Armed Conflict (NTF-ELCAC). Senators also increased the budget for education to keep face-to-face classes safe. Meanwhile, the Dept of Education saw a P6.74bn increase in its budget. The budget for State Universities also increased by P26.57bn. The Bicameral Conference Committee will convene next. Congress targeting to approve the budget before Dec. 17.

Gov’t implements new testing, quarantine rules for inbound international travelers today

The Inter-Agency Task Force approved new testing and quarantine protocols for international travelers coming from countries outside the red list. Fully vaccinated travelers will be required to present a negative RT-PCR test conducted 72 hours prior to leaving the country of origin. They will also have to undergo facility-based quarantine and take another test on the fifth day of arrival and quarantine at home until the 14th day. New rules will take effect beginning Dec 3. Previously, vaccinated travelers from non-red list countries were only encouraged to conduct self-monitoring after receiving a negative swab test result.

Government administers 7.6mn doses during three-day vaccination drive

Preliminary figures suggest that the government administered 7.6mn COVID vaccine doses from November 29 to December 1. The government will hold another major vaccination drive from Dec 15 to 17. It is targeting to inoculate 54mn Filipinos by end-2021; 77mn by end-1Q22 and 90mn by end-2Q22. As of December 1, 34% of the Philippine population (36.9mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 69% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 145mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 89mn doses (as of December 1). Beginning December 3, all fully-vaccinated Filipinos are eligible to receive booster shots. If they received their second dose at least 6 months ago.
