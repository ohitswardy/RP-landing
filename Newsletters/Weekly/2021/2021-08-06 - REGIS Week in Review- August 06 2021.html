REGIS REPORT

06 August 2021

After four straight weeks of significant declines in which the market dropped 10.5%, the PSEi managed a 4.3% gain this first week of August.

Gains were across the board, with gainers outnumbering losers 3:2.  The market saw foreign inflow this week of US$8m, with inflows four of the five trading days.  This is the longest streak of inflows in two month.  Top net inflows were seen in BDO (US$12.4m), ICT (US$11.75m), and Converge (US$4.5m).

Volumes were light however, improving only on Friday as locals focused on PSEi names post announcement.

The PSE announced the changes to the composition of the PSEi effective August 16.   AC Energy (+14.1% WoW) will be added to the index and surprisingly, Converge (+16.9% WoW) as well The inclusion of the latter came as a surprise due to rules requiring a stock to be listed for at least a year before it qualified for inclusion. CNVRG was only listed 4Q20.  PSE just changed PSEi inclusion rule, now requiring listing of just 6 months instead of 1 year.

DMCI (-4.5% WoW) and Emperador (-2.7% WoW) will be taken off the PSEi, meanwhile.  The two stocks were among the top decliners this week.

Declining even more was Robinsons Retail (-8.75% WoW), dragged this week by disappointing 1H numbers caused by revenue shortfalls across its different segments.

ICTSI (+18.4% WoW) led the charge with a whopping gain this week, contributing over a fifth of the PSEi’s 270-index point gain this week. Results in 2Q overtook consensus expectations as volumes and yields rose.  Indications of healthy “double-digits” volume growth in 3Q also helped boost sentiment. 

Other top gainers included conglos JG Summit (+8.9% WoW) and Aboitiz Equity (+7.85% WoW). 

Banks BDO (+8.8% WoW) and Metrobank (+8.6% WoW) also led. Both banks comfortably beat forecasts as provisions came in lower.

Reduced provisioning at BDO helped lift SM’s (+5.8% WoW) 1H profit almost three-fold.  SM Prime and Retail also rebounded from the lows last year.  SM Prime’s (+3.8% WoW) mall revenues in the Phils jumped 55% YoY despite continued lockdowns. 

While SM Prime came inline,  Megaworld (-2.2% WoW) came in behind forecasts for 2Q behind a drop in office revenues.  Ayala Land (-1.2% WoW) 2Q21 profit jumped but was dragged by lower residential margins and soft mall operations.

RLC’s (+1.6% WoW) 2Q profit surged but this was due to lot sales to JV.  Operating profit was in line, however.

A one off gain lifted URC’s (+4.2% WoW) 1H earnings but EBIT was in line with expectations.  Revenues  and operating profit  were broadly in line.

URC announced  company announced that it was selling its Australia-New Zealand business.  URC expects cash proceeds of US$476m from the sale of its operations (60%) in Uni Snack. According to its disclosure, URC is selling an initial 9% at an Enterprise Value (EV) of US$1bn and the remaining 51% at US$1.45bn. URC initially acquired the businesses for close to ~US$1bn (EV) a few years ago.

San Miguel FB (-1.8% WoW) and CNPF (unch) meanwhile managed delivered beats. Despite an exceptionally high base last year,  CNPF’s branded revenue growth was notably resilient while tight cost control led to EBITDA margin expansion.

For the telcos, profits came in line with consensus estimates.  For PLDT (+4.1% WoW), broadband revenue growth continued to be strong, up 33% in 2Q while for Globe (+6.7) growth here seemed to slow.  On the wireless side, there seemed to be no material change to revenue market shares QoQ.

Delivering a big beat was Semirara (+4.8% WoW).    2Q core profit soared 87% QoQ, 280% YoY on the back of higher coal ASPs, coal sales volumes, better plant availability, and higher power prices.

Universal Robina - Input cost pressure weighs on 2Q margins; announces exit from Oceania
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H21 operating profit broadly in line

URC reported 1H21 net profit of P8.1bn, up 46% YoY. While this appears to be tracking ahead of our estimates, the bottom line was boosted by a one[1]off gain of P3.3bn from the sale of land assets. Revenues (+2% YoY to P68.5bn) and operating profit (flat YoY at P8.2bn), were broadly in line with our FY21 estimates. For 2Q21 alone, operating profit was down 9% YoY despite flat revenues due to input cost pressure.

2Q21 top line growth cycles through base effects

PH branded consumer food (BCF) sales declined 7% YoY due to base effects from pantry stocking in 2Q20. However, management noted that underlying demand is still soft, and that the company continues to see further consolidation in key categories (e.g. snack foods). Meanwhile, the int’l BCF saw a 16% YoY jump in revenues, driven by the continued recovery in key markets such as VN (+50%) and TH (+10%).

Margins dragged by input cost pressure

Operating margins for both the domestic and int’l BCF segments contracted QoQ and YoY, which management attributed to heightened pressure on prices of key inputs (e.g. palm oil) as well as freight cost. While input cost pressure has not abated, management expects to see the effects of its price adjustments in the coming quarters.

URC to exit Oceania; hints at other potential inorganic opportunities

URC announced plans to divest its remaining 60% stake in URC Oceania to its current joint venture partner, Intersnack Group. The valuation for the transaction has not been disclosed pending regulatory approvals, but management expects to book a “significant gain” from the sale. Subsidiaries Griffins (NZ) and Snackbrands (AU) were acquired in 2014 and 2016, respectively, for a combined value of c.USD1bn. In 2019, URC sold a 40% stake in the business to Intersnack for a total consideration of USD152mn in cash and assets. Oceania accounts for less than 15% of URC’s total revenue, and a smaller proportion of net profit after accounting for interest expense on acquisition financing. We view the divestment as a positive move for URC, which faced operational challenges with respect to Oceania. It has also had mixed success with its initial plan to cross-sell the products into ASEAN as part of its premiumization strategy. The divestment frees up resources (financial and management), which will allow the company to focus on other growth opportunities. Moreover, a stronger balance sheet will provide more flexibility in pursuing other inorganic opportunities which have emerged because of the current environment.

Cemex Philippines - 2Q profit peaks
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H21 profit of P804mn beats on lower costs

Reported 2Q21 profit surged 191% QoQ to P599mn, taking 1H21 profit to P804mn, already exceeding Regis and consensus FY21E. 1H21 EBITDA of P2.3bn (+26% YoY) is also tracking ahead of forecast. Apart from stronger-than-expected volumes and ASPs, profits were also buoyed by lower fuel, distribution, non[1]cash, and financing expenses.

2Q volumes revert to pre-pandemic levels; FY21 volume guidance raised

Sales volumes improved 7% QoQ, aided by favorable weather and higher infra spending. This brought volumes growth for 1H21 to +16% YoY. Management also raised its volume guidance further to +12% to +14% YoY (from +5-7% previously), which would imply 2H21 volumes could rise further HoH. We are however concerned that (a) renewed lockdowns, (b) unfavorable weather, and (c) lower 2H gov’t infra spend could temper the recovery for the remainder of the year.

ASPs surprisingly up QoQ

ASPs surprisingly jumped 2% QoQ, after CHP put through price adjustments. It is worth noting that est. landed costs of imported cement also rose to US$65/MT in 2Q21 from US$61/MT in 1Q21 likely in response to upward cost pressures. The Dept. of Trade and Industry is still considering the anti-dumping lobby against cement imports from Vietnam and has yet to issue a provisional duty.

Favorable hedges keep costs low for now

We estimate cash cost per MT dropped 3% QoQ and 6% YoY in 2Q21, as the company continues to benefit from low fuel (thermal coal and crude) hedges. These hedges, however, are about to end (likely by 4Q) and management signaled record-high thermal coal prices and elevated crude prices could start affecting margins soon. The company also announced the completion date for its Solid expansion project has been deferred to June 2022 (from end-Dec 2021).

Aboitiz Power - 2Q: Higher selling prices and volumes drive underlying income higher
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H21 core income +188%

Aboitiz Power’s 1H core income of P10.1bn was an increase of +188%YoY, tracking broadly in line with Regis’ FY21E but higher than consensus estimates (P15.4bn). This, however, included insurance claims of P2.4bn in 1Q21. In 2Q21 alone, core income dipped 38%QoQ to P3.923bn but was 175% higher vs 2Q20 (height of initial lockdowns last year). Among the key highlights from AP’s result was that underlying demand for power appears on the uptrend, as exhibited by the 11%YoY increase in energy sales and 20%YoY growth in distribution volumes in 2Q21.

Generation: Better volumes and selling prices

Core income from generation & RES came in at P3.850bn, -37%QoQ but 175%YoY. Excluding the effect of liquidated damages collected in 1Q, we estimate that core income from this segment would have been up 6%QoQ. 2Q21 energy sales grew 11%YoY to 5,660GWh, bringing 1H21 volumes 10% higher. Gross margin improved 9%QoQ on the back of higher selling prices for both BCQ (P4.53/kWh in 1H21 vs P4.04 in 1H20 & P4.07 in 1Q21) and spot (P5.70/kWh in 1H21 vs P2.60 in 1H20 & P3.38 in 1Q21). One unit of GMEC remains on outage is only expected to be back online in September. Management cited peak demand in Luzon and Visayas is already hovering above comparable levels in 2019.

Distribution: Commercial and industrial segments jump 9% QoQ

Income from distribution improved to P1.220bn (+12%QoQ, +80%YoY) as electricity volumes continued to rebound. Volumes from commercial and industrial segments jumped to 996GWh (9%QoQ, 33%YoY), marking a substantial turnaround since the lockdowns last year. This brought total distribution volumes for 2Q21 10% higher QoQ and 20% higher YoY. Gross margin was steady at P1.64/kwh, flat QoQ.

More delays to GNPD

Commercial operations for GNP Dinginin are now slated for September 2021 (Unit 1) – pushed back by another month - and late 2Q22 (Unit 2), a push back of another quarter compared to previously announced targets. The capacity additions from these plants were expected to help cool spot prices, and further delays are likely to keep WESM elevated for the remainder of the year.

Ayala Land - Disappointing trends across the major segments in 2Q21
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Trends of the major segments disappoint

2Q21 earnings jumped to P3.3bn from P197mn in 2Q20. However, 1H21 profit of P6.0bn (+34%) is tracking behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. Underlying trends in 2Q (for all the major segments) are not encouraging and the hard lockdown in August makes the near-term outlook more difficult.

Reservation sales fall 31% QoQ

Due to the hard lockdown in 2Q, presales fell 31% QoQ (+45% YoY) to P19.7bn, only 54% of the 2019 average. Cumulative 1H21 presales amounted to P48.2bn, trending behind our FY estimates. While sales are lumpy and could still catch up, the tighter restrictions in August would make this difficult. Like in previous quarters, sales were skewed towards horizontal projects in provincial areas. Development margins drop GPM of condo and office-for-sale projects dropped 5-7ppt in 1H21 due to costs related to new government rules (social distancing, dorms for workers, etc.) Management said GPM was likely to stay at these levels while COVID remains.

Mall revenue falls 25% QoQ; new hard lockdown to hamper malls further

Mall revenue fell 25% QoQ to P1.5bn, only 28% of the 2Q19 figure. This is worse than SM Prime’s malls, which achieved 33% of 2Q19 revenue. We believe the discrepancy is largely because 70% of Ayala Land’s mall space is in Metro Manila (vs. 40% for SM Prime), which has been subject to tighter restrictions. Thus, the Metro Manila lockdown in August should be worse for Ayala malls.

Office vacancy rises 6ppt QoQ to 18%

Office vacancy climbed 6ppt QoQ to 18% due to POGO closures and the completion of a new office building, which added c. 5% to floor area. Ayala Land’s vacancy is now higher than the industry average of 13%. However, while industry rental rates are generally down 15%-25% from pre-COVID levels, management said that rental rates on its new contracts are broadly flat.

Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Blockbuster 2Q results; Raising estimates and TP
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642
 

1H21 core profit rises 2.8x to P6.3bn

Semirara reported 2Q21 profit of P3.98bn, which included one-time deferred tax adjustments related to CREATE. Stripping this out and FX adjustments, we estimate 2Q21 profit soared QoQ to P4.06bn on higher coal ASPs, coal sales volumes, and power ASPs. 1H21 profit is 2.8x higher YoY at P6.3bn, tracking well ahead of Regis and consensus estimates.

Coal mining: Stellar 2Q; 3Q shaping up to be a record quarter

In 2Q21, profits from coal mining almost doubled QoQ, after ASPs and coal sales volumes jumped 31% and 26% QoQ, respectively. Coal sales volumes in 1H21 reached 8.8mn MT, trending ahead of forecast (14mn MT for FY21E). Following a buoyant coal market, the company raised its FY21E sales volume target to 15- 17mn MT, as it expects to sell even its low-grade, unwashed coal inventory. Spot prices for Newcastle thermal coal are now at US$158/MT, much higher than the average in 2Q21 (US$106/MT) and our FY21E assumption US$85/MT.

Higher WESM prices buoy power profits in 2Q

Profit contributions from power also improved sequentially, largely on the back of higher WESM prices. Plant availability was largely unchanged QoQ, as Unit 2 remained on outage. After close to nine months of forced outage, Unit 2 is slated to operate again this month. The company plans to sell at least 20% of available capacity to the spot market and does not intend to sign contracts beyond one to two years. Management believes that while spot prices could soften over the next 12 months, the absence of new supply after 2022 could mean elevated spot prices and better contract prices over the medium- to long-term.

Raising EPS forecasts by 41-80%; TP lifted to P19/sh (+36%)

We upgrade our FY21E/FY22E EPS forecasts by 80%/41%, after raising coal ASP assumptions by 35%/20%. Our revised forecasts assume benchmark coal prices of US$115/US100 per MT for FY21E/FY22E. We also lift our DCF-based price target to P19/sh (+36%). Maintain Buy

SM Investments - 2Q21: Ebb and flow of lockdowns
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H21 profits reach P20bn, with 2Q up QoQ

SM Investments reported net profit of P20.1bn for 1H21, almost 3x the previous year’s P7.1bn. The derived net income of P10.6bn for 2Q21 alone is an improvement over the P9.6bn in 1Q and the net loss for 2Q20. Overall, earnings are trending better than our estimates because lower provisions at the banking business are boosting profits there.

BDO: Provisions reduced

1H21 net income for BDO came in at P21.4bn, 5x the level of the previous year. Profit for 2Q21 alone was P11bn, compared to a P4.5bn loss in 2Q20. The biggest reason for the difference is provisioning, as the bank did not set aside the same massive level of provisions it did last year, ahead of an expected deterioration in its loan book. The NPL ratio rose to 3.1%, and NPL cover dipped to 100%.

SM Prime: China malls back to normal but domestic ones still face challenges

Earnings of SM Prime rose 12% to P11.6bn for 1H21. For 2Q21 alone, income was P5.2bn, which is expectedly much higher than 2Q20 (peak of the lockdowns) but still lower than 1Q21. While its China malls are already operating at pre-pandemic levels, the Philippine ones are still far from there.

SM Retail: 2Q revenues dip marginally QoQ

Revenues for SM Retail were almost unchanged in 1H at P138bn (-1%). This, however, implies 2Q revenue of P68bn – down slightly from 1Q but well above the depressed levels a year ago. Net income for 1H21 was P3.6bn, which is up seven[1]fold from the previous year, due to cost reductions (and rent concessions). For 2Q alone, profit was P2.0bn, compared to a net loss of P0.7bn. We are reluctant to draw too many conclusions about the direction of this business based on these results. The numbers for SM Retail ebb and flow according to what lockdown conditions are in force where their stores are located. Nonetheless, it’s clear that its small-format Alfamart is gaining traction, and could exceed 10% of Food Retail’s revenue this year. Beyond that, we will need to wait for the lockdown noise to dissipate before trying to look for ‘trends’ and extrapolate from them.

Century Pacific Food - 2Q21: EBITDA margin resilient even as sales mix shifts
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Net profit +21% YoY (ahead); margins supported by tight cost control

CNPF reported net profit of P2.7bn in 1H21, up 21% YoY, and trending ahead of our FY21 estimates. Revenues were broadly in line at P27.1bn (+8% YoY), but EBITDA (+9% YoY) is ahead of expectations. For 2Q21 alone, net profit jumped 19% YoY with a further lift from a lower effective tax rate. Tight cost control, however, supported EBITDA margin (+60bps YoY) even as the lower-margin OEM business drove most of the top line growth.

Branded revenues flat from high base; OEM recovery continues

Revenues for the branded segment (79% of total revenue) were flattish YoY in 2Q21, which is notable given the exceptionally high base from pantry stocking in 2Q20. CNPF implemented price increases across its portfolio (+1-3% on average), which suggests volumes declined by low single digit. Meanwhile, the OEM business continued to see recovery with sales +16% YoY.

Tight cost control supports margins amidst shift in sales mix

Gross margin contracted during the quarter, which management attributed to the 1) the shift in sales mix (lower-margin OEM), and 2) heightened cost pressure for other inputs ex-skipjack tuna. Despite this, EBITDA margin managed to expand 60bs YoY due to tight cost control and the absence of one-off Covid[1]related expenses last year.

Underlying demand remains weak amidst new round of lockdowns

Management was cautious on top line growth expectations, particularly on the domestic side, given weak demand seen in June and July. While demand for essentials (e.g canned food) should be resilient, weak consumption could weigh on the growth of some of its more premium products, such as powdered milk and coconut milk. Management expects margins to drive profitability in the coming quarters as 1) majority of its input requirements for the year have been secured, and 2) skipjack tuna prices remain favorable. Another round of price increases is expected in 2H21, which management expects to implement gradually given the fragile consumer environment.

PLDT, Inc - Broadband lifts 2Q
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H core income of P14.3bn (+10%) in line with estimates

PLDT reported headline profit of P12.9bn for 1H21. Stripping out one-offs (but including losses at Voyager), we estimate core income was P14.3bn, +10% YoY, which would be within expectations. For 2Q alone, core income was P7.2bn (+10% YoY). Like in the previous quarter. 2Q was marked by (1) robust expansion of the broadband segment and (2) signs of softness in the wireless one.

Another strong quarter for broadband

Overall service revenue was up +10% in 2Q but broadband did much of the lifting, rising 33%. PLDT recorded net subscriber additions (wired) of 154k in the quarter, a further improvement from 112k in 1Q. Another 113k wired subscribers were also migrated from copper to fiber in this latest period.

Signs of softness in wireless

The 7% YoY growth in 2Q wireless service revenue is masking signs of softness. Wireless revenue was actually marginally down (-1%) QoQ, even as mobile data traffic rose slightly (+3% QoQ). We believe (1) restrictions on personal mobility coupled with (2) rising availability of home broadband, are restraining wireless data usage. Releasing value: Voyager and the tower network

PLDT says, unsurprisingly, that an IPO for its fintech business/es, held under Voyager Innovations, is an option. Voyager, which lost over P2bn (US$40m) in 1H21, aims to be in the black profit-wise by 2023. Management has also changed its mind about its network of telecom towers assets and confirmed a media report that said PLDT was considering its sale. It previously said that it considered the tower assets “strategic” and was not open to a sale. A divestment could release significant amount of capital, and valuations could be higher now while interest rates are at record lows.

ICTSI - 2Q21: Strong sequential rebound in Asia ports
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H21 core profit reaches US$162mn, up 89% YoY

Reported 1H21 profit after prefs of US$165mn (+92% YoY) included a one-time gain on insurance claims. Removing this, 1H21 core profit rose 89% YoY to US$162mn, in line with Regis FY21E but trending ahead of consensus estimates. 2Q21 core profit jumped 21% QoQ, buoyed by a sequential improvement in volumes, yield per TEU, and a lower tax rate but partially weighed down by start[1]up costs at its port in Nigeria.

2Q21 volumes, yields up QoQ; Positive trends sustained so far in 3Q

While volume growth at some ports was more subdued (e.g. Iraq, Madagascar, Ecuador) due to the continuing economic effects of the pandemic and the ongoing shortage in the supply of containers and seafarers globally, group-wide volumes have already exceeded pre-pandemic levels by 8%. Management indicated that positive trends (“double-digit growth”) have been sustained so far in 3Q across its portfolio. Note that to reach our forecast, volumes for the remainder of the year would need to grow by +7% YoY. Yield per TEU improved mildly QoQ to US$162, admittedly short of our forecast for the year (US$164). We believe there is room for yields to rise further, however, due to (1) the tariff hike granted at the port of Manila, (2) a likely rise in ancillary revenues as volumes pick up, as well as (3) other yield-enhancing initiatives management is undertaking.

EBITDA margin dips slightly in 2Q21 but still above trend

SM Prime - 2Q profit jumps YoY despite restrictions but new lockdown to weigh on malls
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q21 earnings surge despite hard lockdown but new hard lockdown to weigh

Despite the hard lockdown for c. two months, SM Prime’s 2Q21 net profit jumped 145% YoY to P5.2bn. The 14% YoY increase in total revenue was driven by both Philippine (+55% YoY) and China malls (+40% YoY). Cumulative 1H21 earnings increased 12% to P11.6bn, behind consensus. If not for the recent increase in COVID cases, we would have said that the company was likely to achieve our full-year forecast. However, the imposition of a new hard lockdown in Metro Manila will weigh on the Philippine mall business.

Philippine mall revenue falls 19% QoQ; minimum rents waived again in August

2Q21 Philippine mall revenue climbed 55% YoY (to P4.8bn), showing that the hard lockdown in 2Q21 was not as restrictive as the one in 2Q20 (partly because it generally affected only major urban areas). However, Philippine mall revenue fell 19% QoQ (only 33% of 2Q19 revenue) as foot traffic fell to c. 25% of pre-pandemic levels (from c.40% in 1Q21) during the lockdown. Moreover, minimum rents in affected areas were waived. With the hard lockdown in August, minimum rents for affected malls have again been waived. They will be reinstated when tenant sales recover.

 

China malls operations at pre-COVID levels 2Q21

China mall revenue surged 40% YoY to P1.5bn (1H21 +48%). China mall foot traffic (and revenue) has been at around pre-COVID levels since 1Q21.

Reservation sales fall 30% QoQ; still at pre-COVID levels

After two consecutive quarters of record presales, reservation sales fell 30% QoQ to P22.7bn. However, this figure is still equivalent to the 2019 average, implying that SM Prime is still performing better than most other developers. It credited its record 1Q presales to expansion of its sales network abroad.

Globe reduced the monthly cost of one of its entry level fiber broadband packages to P1,499. This is supposedly being offered for a “limited” period only. Globe has a cheaper package but that one has data usage capped at 500GB  per month. With this new package, Globe’s pricing now matches Converge’s. PLDT is supposedly considering the sale of its telecom tower assets for as much as US$1bn with the expectation that there will be a leaseback arrangement. BDO 1H21 beats on fees; provisions fall but NPLs up, cover down

BDO in a press release reported 1H21 NPAT of P21.4bn, about 60% of consensus and close to our FY estimate. The beat for us was nearly entirely due to provision expense, as PPOP was broadly in line. NPAT was 5x that of 1H20, as the bank put through massive provisions in 2Q20.

 

Some highlights 

Loans were flat YoY (vs -1% in 1Q21)

NII was -3% YoY, suggesting was still down YoY but flat-ish QoQ.

Other income was up 20%, driven by fees (+20%) and insurance premiums (+31%), which offset a 17% drop I trading/fix income

Opex was up 9%, largely due to insurance operations, without which opex would have been -1% YoY

Provision expense was P6.8bn, down 70% YoY. Recall that BDO put through P20.2bn in provisions in 2Q20 alone.

NPL ratio was up to 3.1% (was 2.8% in 1Q), while NPL ratio was 100% (was 107%)

NIMs were down 29bps YoY, but flattened out (-2bps) QoQ. Loan yields have also flattened out, as have deposit costs. CASA ratio inched up to 84%.

Fees were up strongly YoY, but were down QoQ – service charges (-1% QoQ), trust (-6%), insurance premiums (-18%) and miscellaneous (-33%)

Restructured loans (performing) more than doubled QoQ from P64.4bn in 1Q21 to P130.7bn in 2Q21. The 2Q21 RL figure is equivalent to roughly 5.5% of total loans. To be fair, the bank had previously been guiding for RLs to hit as high as 6% of loans.
