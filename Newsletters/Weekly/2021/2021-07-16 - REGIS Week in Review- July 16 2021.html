REGIS REPORT

16 July 2021

The PSEi fell 2.06% this week as Delta variant fears around the region weighed on sentiment.  A downgrade to the country’s outlook to negative by credit rating agency Fitch also strained confidence.

The Health Department reported 16 new cases on Friday, taking the total Delta variant cases in the country to 35. 

Citing the threat of the COVID 19 Delta variant, the President extended the Metro Manila’s quarantine status  (GCQ or General Community Quarantine) until 31st July.

Vaccination continued to progress. The Philippines has administered 13.8m doses, and has received a total of 22m COVID vaccine doses to date.  

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) declined by US$1.1bn for June, settling at US$106bn. Despite the drop, the  current level of reserves is still near record levels, and gives the BSP ammunition to smooth fluctuations in the FX rate given the large moves recently. The PHP continued to deteriorate this week, dropping 0.3% to PHP50.217/US$.

Volumes were thin, while corporate news was light.  Average daily volumes hit only US110m daily.

Foreigners were seller for US$58m this week, bulk of which came through on Friday; reports of local Delta variant transmission prompted selling ahead of the weekend.  Biggest outflows were seen in ALI, SM, LTG, and  URC. 

Declines were seen across the board, with 23 of the 30 index members ending the week in the red.  LT Group (-10.3% WoW) declined significantly, along with Robinsons Land (-8.0% WoW), Megaworld (-5.4% WoW), and Bloomberry (-5.15% WoW).  Large caps SM (-4.4% WoW) and URC (-4.3% WoW) were also among the top laggards.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Property - Clouds and silver liningsClouds and silver linings
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Vaccines bring hope for malls but…

The recovery of the Philippine economy has been slow, but the improving pace of vaccinations reinforces our view that malls will see a meaningful rebound by late 2021. However, we expect the revival of residential sales to be sluggish and office vacancy to worsen.Our target prices have been adjusted as we roll over to 2022E, but we remain cautious about the near-term outlook of the sector.

Prefer malls and high-end residential

We expect more substantial recoveries for the mall business and high-end residential sales. However, this optimism does not extend to the rest of the residential development business orto office property.

We now project office vacancy to jump from 9% in 2020 to 15% this year, the highest level in over a decade. Furthermore, by 2023E, we forecast vacancy to remain at 13% and rental rates in most areas to still be c. 15% lower than 2019 levels (even lowering some areas).

The mortgage NPL ratio is at a 15-year high of 9.4%, signaling that some buyers are experiencing financial difficulties. Thus, we expect 2023E presales of most developers to reach only 85%-90% of the 2019 level.

REITs could provide a catalyst, RLC the biggest beneficiary

All the developers we cover have announced plans to spin off at least part of their office portfolios into REITs. The REIT listings could provide a catalyst for the share prices as they could (1) produce large one-time gains, (2) improve continuing profitability and (3) raise NAVs. Due to its high proportion of rental assets, we consider Robinsons Land the biggest beneficiary of REITs.

RLC offers the best risk-reward, ALI also a Buy

In our view, RLC offers the best risk-reward in the sector. Its mall operations are poised to rebound and its upcoming office REIT should provide a catalyst. We also find ALI attractive due to its exposure to high-end residential and because its land bank should benefit from upcoming infrastructure. We maintain our Hold ratings on FLI, MEG, SMPH, and VLL. Key risks: COVID, POGOs, and REIT listings

 
 

Corporate News

PLDT, Smart lobby for lower regulatory frees

PLDT Inc and wireless subsidiary Smart Communications said the government needed to implement “affordable and reasonable regulatory” so more investments would be channeled toward building network infrastructure. This was spurred by a recent hearing on the Open Access in Data Transmission Act hosted by Senate Economic Planning Office. Ibay, Smart vice president and head of regulatory affairs, said they could expand in areas lacking coverage via removal of tower fees, inspection fees and audit fees imposed by certain local governments. Smart also wants national government to begin charging uniform spectrum users fees based on a “per kHz per population”. This would replace the prevailing wireless broadband formula (per station, per kilohertz computation) which discourages deployment of more wireless facilities.

 

Asset quality deteriorates in May

The Philippine banking system’s NPL ratio (ex-interbank loans) of rose to 4.63%, up 15bps MoM and 90bps ytd. In absolute terms, NPLs increased 3.4% MoM to P479bn. Every other major asset quality indicator also deteriorated in the month. Past Due (PD) ratio was up 17bps MoM to 5.56%, Restructured loans (RL) ratio increased 20bps MoM to 2.47%, and NPL coverage fell to 80.0%, down 153bps MoM. The May NPL numbers should already fully incorporate the Bayanihan 2 loan grace periods which officially ended in February. However, they would not yet include asset quality issues from the April lockdowns in Metro Manila and surrounding provinces. The one silver lining is that the loan contraction seems to be moderating. Loans (ex-interbank) in May were down 1.9% YoY, better than the -2.6% in April and -4.0% in March. Loans were flat MoM at P10.34bn. It remains to be seen if the deterioration was sector-wide, or if it again was concentrated on a few banks. In 1Q21, the banking industry’s NPLs were up 59bps QoQ. But for the banks we cover – BDO, MBT, BPI and SECB – NPLs were broadly flat (marginally up/down) QoQ.

Filinvest Land plans to add malls to FILRT

Filinvest Land CFO Ana Venus Mejia said that the company plans to eventually infuse mall assets into Filinvest REIT (FILRT). Filinvest Land currently has a mall portfolio of 250,000sqm and an office portfolio of 525,000sqm. This includes the 300,000sqm to be spun off into FILRT. The REIT listing could raise up to P15bn (Up to 1.8bn shares at P8.30s/share). The break down would be secondary shares (1.63bn shares) and an overallotment option (163mn shares). This reit will have 300,000sqm of office space which is 90% occupied and only 2% leased to POGOs. Around 280,000sqm in Alabang while the additional 20,000sqm will be in Cebu. FILRT does not own land. At P8.30/share, FILRT will offer 5.6% 2022E dividend yield (based on prospectus). We estimate that if FILRT were priced at the maximum, FLI’s NAV would rise by 8%. Pricing date – July 19; Offer period – July 26 to August 3; Listing date – August 12.

MerryMart (MM PM) acquires provincial drug store chain

MerryMart announced it is acquiring a 67% stake in Carlos SuperDrug, the largest pharmacy chain in Quezon Province. Carlos SuperDrug has 27 operating branches to date and is EBITDA and net Income positive. MM had 25 stores as of end-2020 and is targeting to hit 1,200 stores by 2030.

PSEi foreign ownership falls in June

We estimate total market foreign ownership fell to 21.4% (-38bps MoM, -43bps QoQ) while PSEi foreign ownership dropped to 24.7% (-11bps MoM, -36bps QoQ) in June. 17 out of 30 PSEi constituents saw declines in their foreign ownership MoM. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were. AP – foreign ownership fell 150 basis points MoM to 2.5%. AP was removed from the MSCI Asia ex-Japan Index at the end of May but the drop in foreign ownership was only reflected in June. It was also one of the top foreign sold stocks in June, with net foreign outflow of US$15.3mn. ALI – foreign ownership declined 127 basis points MoM to 33.6%. Ayala Corp purchased 1.4% of ALI shares outstanding (c. US$140mn) from foreign investors in June. MEG – foreign ownership dropped 111 basis points MoM to 12.6%. MEG was removed from the MSCI Asia ex-Japan Index at the end of May but the drop in foreign ownership was only reflected in June. Net outflow in June was US$11.7mn, among the highest for month. JFC – foreign ownership rose 57 basis points MoM to 24.0%. This was the highest increase among PSEi constituents. Net foreign inflow was US$17.4mn, the third highest for the month.

Cosco Capital’s (COSCO PM) liquor distribution unit to conduct follow-on offering

COSCO’s liquor distribution unit plans to raise up to P7.5bn through a follow-on offering in September with 3bn shares at an offer price of P2-2.50/sh. The subsidiaries – Montosco Inc., Premier Wines & Spirits Inc., and Meritus Prime Distributions Inc. – were injected into The Keepers Holdings Inc (formerly Da Vinci Capital Holdings [DAVIN]) through a share-for-asset swap. The value was approx. P22bn (11.25bn DAVIN shares at P2/sh). DAVIN will be renamed to KEEPR. Collectively, the operating companies are the largest distributor of imported spirits in the Philippines with 74% market share. They hold distribution rights for leading brands such as Alfonso, Johnnie Walker, Jim Beam and Jinro. In 2020, It sold 3.4mn cases, and generated P8.2bn in revenues and P1.2bn in net profit.

 
 

Macro News

Philippines receives another 1mn Sinovac doses

The Philippines received another 1mn Sinovac COVID vaccine doses. The country expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August, including 4.3mn doses that already arrived in July. The Philippines has now received a total of 21.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 13.8mn doses. The country administered 375,000 doses on July 13, a record level. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021. 

Metro Manila 2Q21 office vacancy rises to 13%

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) said that Metro Manila office vacancy rose to 13% in 2Q21. 11% in 1Q21, 9% in 4Q20. Nationwide net office take-up improved to 34,000sqm in 2Q21 from a net vacancy of 130,000sqm in 1Q21. The improvement was largely due to less closures – 135,000sqm in 2Q21 vs 239,000sqm in 1Q21. BPO gross take-up also improved to 93,000sqm from 35,000sqm in 1Q21. LPC estimates that prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 0-5% QoQ, flat from end-2019. However, our own channel checks suggest that Metro Manila condominium prices are down 10%-20% from end-2019. Commercial land prices in the Metro Manila business districts are flat from end-2020. Makati and Fort Bonifacio prices are 10-20% higher than 2019 levels. Manila Bay area prices are 20%-30% lower than 2019 levels. Prices of some high-end beach properties have tripled from 2019.

Food, transport seen to push up inflation in July

Cost of food and transportation to rise in July due to price hikes in the international market according to Finance Undersecretary and chief economist Gil Beltran. However, food price inflation is expected to peak this month, as imports rise to boost domestic supply. Pork imports expanded this year after the government reduced tariff rates for both in-quota and out-quota shipments. Headline inflation moderated to 4.1% in June from 4.5% in May; 1H21 average inflation stood at 4.4%, above the government’s target of 2-4%.

Remittances up 3% MoM, 13% YoY in May

Cash remittances from overseas Filipinos reached US$2.38bn in May, up 13% YoY and 3% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances rose 7% YoY; 3-month moving average growth rate at +10% YoY. May 2020 was still a low base due to lockdown. There were sustained recoveries seen from USA, Asia, and Middle East; Europe and sea-based remittances were still soft. This brings YT-May remittances up 6.3% YoY to US$12.3bn (in PHP terms, up +1% YoY). The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 4% YoY in FY21 (US$31.1bn), implying that remittances should average US$2.68bn per month for the balance of the year.

GIR settles at US$106bn in June

The country’s gross international reserves dropped by US$1.1bn (-1.1% MoM) to US$106bn in end-June and cited falling gold prices, debt payment of foreign currency obligations, and BSP’s forex operations. This number covers 12.1 months of imports. However, this was 13.5% YoY higher than the end-June ’20 level of US$93.46bn. The BSP expects GIR to rise to US$115bn by year-end.

Vehicle sales rise 2% MoM in June; Toyota sales up 4% MoM

CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales jumped 45% YoY and 2% MoM to 22,550 units in June. This brings 1H21 industry car sales to 132,767 units (+56% YoY). Toyota sold 11,242 units, up 50% YoY and 4% MoM and this brings 1H21 sales 63,778 units (+79% YoY). With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales only need to average 6.7k units for the rest of the year. Our forecast assumes Toyota’s sales will reach 117k units, implying that monthly sales for the balance of 2021 should average 8,900 units per month. GTCAP is trading at a 5% discount to its market-based NAV of P634/sh.

Dutertes top Pulse Asia Presidential/Vice-Presidential survey

In polls conducted by Pulse Asia from June 7 to 16, Sara Duterte topped the presidential survey at 28%, followed by Manila Mayor Isko Moreno at 14%, Ferdinand Bongbong Marcos at 13%, Grace Poe at 10% and Emmanuel Pacquiao at 8%. For the vice presidential survey, sitting President Rodrigo Duterte placed first at 18%, followed by Isko Moreno at 14%, Vicente Sotto at 10%, Ferdinand Bongbong Marcos at 10% and Emmanuel Pacquiao at 9%.

Trade deficit widens in May

The country’s trade deficit doubled YoY to US$2.75bn in May. However, this was smaller than the revised US$3.08bn gap in April 2021. This brings YT-May trade deficit to US$12.6bn, up 30% YoY. Imports rose by 48% in May, as fuel imports surged and capital/consumer goods recovered while exports jumped 30% YoY, as electronics exports rose 25%.

Gov’t allows outdoor activities for kids

Children as young as five years old can now go outdoors, as long as they are residing in areas under GCQ and MGCQ. Areas under GCQ “with heightened restrictions” are not covered by the new policy (e.g. Laguna and Cavite). Mixed-use indoor or outdoor buildings and facilities like malls and similar establishments are not included in the allowed outdoor areas for children. Under the previous IATF-EID policy, only persons aged 15 to 65 years old are allowed to leave their residences.

Pace of vaccinations slows

The 7-day average vaccinations dropped to 212,607/day from 254,141/day in the previous week. Some of the Metro Manila cities have slowed the pace of vaccinations due to a lack of vaccine supply. The Philippines received 3.3mn vaccine doses over the past few days. The Philippines expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August including 3.3mn doses that already arrived in July. The Philippines has now received a total of 20.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 13.2mn doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

Fitch Ratings revises Philippines’ outlook to negative

Fitch Ratings maintained its investment grade “BBB” credit rating for the Philippines but revised its outlook to “negative” from “stable”. Revision of outlook reflects increasing risks to the credit profile from the impact of the pandemic and its aftermath on policy-making as well as on economic and fiscal out-turns. General government debt-to-GDP ratio is expected to reach 52.7% and 54.5% in 2021 and 2022. These are much higher than the 34.1% in 2019 and will exceed the median increase for BBB-rated peers. Fitch described the government’s target to vaccinate up to 70% of the eligible population by end-2021 as “ambitious,” given only less than 3% of the population have received two jabs as of end-June. It also warned that the impact of the Supreme Court decision commonly known as the Mandanas ruling – which will increase revenue transfers to local government units from the national government – also poses uncertainty on the fiscal front. The poor execution could lead to underspending by local governments, which the authorities are seeking to address through capacity building. Fitch expects gross domestic product (GDP) to grow by 5% and 6.6% in 2021 and 2022. This is lower than previous than April estimates which were at 6.3% and 8.3%, respectively. Both projections are also below the 6-7% and 7-9% targets set by the government for the 2021 and 2022, respectively.

Gov’t extends “GCQ” quarantine status in NCR Plus

President Duterte extended Metro Manila’s “GCQ” status until July 31, while Iloilo, Cagayan de Oro City, and 7 other areas will be under MECQ. Meanwhile, GCQ with heightened restrictions will be applied to provinces: Laguna, Lucena, Naga City, Aklan, Antique, Bacolod, Cagayan province, Capiz. The gov’t cited threat of the highly infectious Delta variant of COVID-19.

National Gov’t borrows another P540bn from BSP; plans maiden retail USD bonds

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas approved another short-term loan worth P540bn to the National Government (NG). This is the fourth time the NG has received support from the central bank (P300bn in March 2020, P540bn in October 2020 and another P540bn in December 2020). The NG paid the P540bn (last month), and asked for a renewal and the Monetary Board has approved. Meanwhile, the NG is planning to raise more funds with its first-ever retail dollar-denominated bond offering by mid-August but no amount was disclosed. Proceeds will be used to fund the government’s recovery and resilience programs, as well as big-ticket infrastructure projects.
