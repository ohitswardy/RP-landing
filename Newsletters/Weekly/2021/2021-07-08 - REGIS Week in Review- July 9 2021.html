REGIS REPORT

08 July 2021

The Phils fell 2.4% this week to close at 6834.92.  Profit taking pushed the market lower, in the wake of an above-7000 close last week.  Sentiment was on the ground was weighed by PHP weakness as well as vaccine delays.

Various local government units in Metro Manila have suspended (or slowed down) the administration of vaccines amidst a lack of available supply of doses. The lack of supply in the capital may be due to increasingly more doses being sent to provincial areas where cases have risen sharply. Nonetheless, the supply issues should be resolved soon as the  country is now expecting 16m vaccine doses to arrive in July, up from 13.3m previously. It also expects to receive 14m doses in August. To date, the Philippines has received a cumulative 18.6m COVID-19 vaccine doses and administered 12m.

Meanwhile, the PHP continued to weaken, dropping close to 2% this week against the US. This is the fourth straight week of PHP deterioration, despite the easing of inflation to 4.1% in June (real interest rates are still negative).  The PHP breached P50/US$ this week.   

Volumes were very light averaging only US$110m daily.  Foreigners were sellers for US$46.3m, bulk of which went through on Thursday and Friday. Foreigners would have actually been sellers for US$60.5m had it not been for SMPH blocks which clocked in US$14.2m in inflows. The biggest outflows were spread across large caps Ayala Land (-2.4% WoW), Ayala Corp (-3.6% WoW), BDO (-3.4% WoW), and Metrobank (-2.8% WoW).

Losses were across the board this week, with only 5 of the 30 index names managing to close with gains.

Of the handful of gainers, reopening play Bloomberry (+6.1% WoW) led. Globe (+4.2% WoW) also rose, with one of the few names seeing decent inflow. Emperador (+3.3% WoW) also increased. Emperador said that its management is looking into listing in Singapore after its “strong operating and financial performance” caught the attention of global institutional investors.

Meanwhile, the top declines were seen in GT Capital (-5.2% WoW), Security Bank (-5.0% WoW) and Metro Pacific (-4.8% WoW).  Jollibee (-4.7% WoW), LT Group (-4.2% WoW), and JG Summit (-4.1% WoW) also declined.

Semirara (+3.9% WoW) added to its 11.9% gain last week, as regional coal prices continued to soar – providing upside risk to earnings.  Regis assumptions have coal prices at US$85/MT for FY21, vs regional coal prices already at  US$142/MT.  Coal accounts for around 80% of SCC’s profits.

ICTSI’s (-3.2% WoW) flagship port Manila International Container Terminal (MICT), received a tariff increase from regulators covering stevedoring and cargo handling charges. The port regulator is granting a 10% increase of which 2% will take effect as early as next month. The remaining 8% will be implemented six months later, subject to review.  The particular hike will affect ~70% of MICT’s revenues and could lift FY22E EBITDA and profits by 3% and 7%, respectively.

More news on REITs this week.  The Philippine SEC approved the IPO of Filinvest Land’s (+1.8% WoW) office REIT (target listing date of Aug 6). The REIT company will have 300,000sqm of office space, mostly in southern Metro Manila. Meanwhile, Jollibee is planning to take a stake in the industrial property development arm of Double Dragon (-1.0% WoW) that will eventually be listed as a REIT.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Reading the political tea leaves
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

The company one keeps

With national elections in the Philippines less than a year away, Roberto Herrera-Lim of global consultancy firm Teneo offered a historical perspective and shared his thoughts about the upcoming polls at a recent event we hosted. While there are frontrunners this early in the race, it’s too soon to call it for anyone. Beyond just looking at the candidates themselves, Herrera-Lim opines that one also needs to examine the “constraints” on each of them, referring to the backers / advisers who could deter them from extreme policies in the future.

Alternative facts and a polarization of the electorate

Mainstream media has declined as the primary source of information and the ways people get their “facts” now (e.g. social media) has become more fragmented. Viral stories / narratives, regardless of their veracity, are driving politics and political choices. As is also evident elsewhere, this has led to polarization of the electorate.

Polls put Duterte allies and proxies firmly ahead

Opinion polls place President Duterte’s daughter Sara as the current frontrunner. While the 27% she’s polling may not be overwhelming, other Duterte allies and proxies as well as those appealing to similar constituencies are polling almost 30% combined. In the multi-candidate races that Philippines has had historically, a 40% share of the vote has always been enough to win. Note that President Duterte has floated the possibility of running for the Vice Presidency as a way of circumventing the constitutional ban against reelection. Herrera-Lim, however, does not think it is likely he will do so.

New name emerges; opposition looks weak and uncoordinated

One name that’s emerged recently as a potential contender is actor[1]politician Isko Domagoso (12% in the polls), the mayor of Manila who cannot be described as firmly in the opposition camp. Overall, the opposition appears weak and uncoordinated. For its candidate to prevail, it must unite and offer a strong “narrative” to voters.

Tell me who your friends are

Many of the leading candidates appear to have little or no well-formed policies, especially on the economy. Herrera-Lim thus believes it will be important to observe if they have backers or advisers who can serve as important “constraints” on any of their extreme / populist tendencies, just as Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez does for the current President. The candidate likely to have the most populist tendencies, in Herrera-Lim’s view, would be boxer-turned-politician Manny Pacquiao. The possible candidates most identified with the opposition (notably Vice President Leni Robredo and Senator Grace Poe) are likely to be more conventional / orthodox.

 
 

Corporate News

ICTSI's Manila port secures +10% tariff hike

Regulator approves +10% tariff hike for MICT. The Philippine Ports Authority approved a ten percent (+10%) tariff increase on stevedoring and cargo handling charges for both Asian Terminals Inc. (ATI PM) and ICTSI (ICT PM) at South Harbor and MICT, respectively. The tariff hike will be implemented in tranches: (1) +2% after 30 days from publication of the PPA order (likely by August 2021) and (2) 8% after 6 months from the implementation of the first tranche, subject to review (likely Feb 2022). Tariff hike earlier than expected. The periodic tariff adjustment for MICT came in earlier than our expectations (2023) and was a tad higher than our forecast (+9%).  We estimate the adjustment will apply to approx.. 70% of MICT’s revenues and will lift ICT’s consolidated FY22E EBITDA and profit by 3% and 7%, respectively.  The impact to our NAV-based TP (P200/sh), however, will be inconsequential (<1%). Manila North Harbor next?  It also appears that ICTSI petitioned for a tariff hike for its 50%-owned Manila North Harbor,  A public hearing was held last week discussing the petition and we reckon the ruling could come out by September/October. We would welcome the likelihood of a tariff hike for Manila North Harbor. Our forecasts and valuation currently do not assume any tariff adjustments for the port, which we estimate account for c. 5% of ICT’s consolidated profit after perps.   

Converge to double data transmission capacity

Converge ICT Solutions will be doubling its data transmission capacity as it anticipates more demand on its fiber network. The company announced in a disclosure that it will double capacity to 800 gigabits per seconds in response to cloud computing, big data and virtual reality use. The upgrade would allow for more data center capacity, decreasing latency and improving high-speed data transfer. Its fiber optic network has reached over seven million or 28% of total households as of March.

JFC plans REIT for industrial properties

JFC plans to spin off industrial properties currently being utilized by its commissaries through an eventual REIT IPO. It will be done through an investment in CentralHub Industrial Centers Inc, which is currently a wholly-owned subsidiary of DoubleDragon Properties (DD PM). CentralHub plans to eventually register and operate as a REIT with target listing by 2022. JFC’s total investment will be approx P3.9bn. P1.9bn cash for 1.564bn common shares of CentralHub. 16.4 hectares of industrial properties worth cP2bn in exchange for additional common shares in CentralHub. JFC will use the proceeds from the REIT IPO to fund future real estate investments, such as stores and commissaries. These future commissaries will also eventually be folded into the CentralHub REIT.

Emperador mulls second listing in Singapore

Businessworld reported that Emperador is exploring a second listing in Singapore that could raise as much as SGD1bn ($742mn) according to people with knowledge of the matter. The company is working with advisers on the potential offering as soon as the end of this year. AGI and Emperador did not want to comment on the report.

Banks’ bad loan ratio rises to 4.49% in May

Gross NPL ratio (ex-interbank) of Philippine banks worsened to 4.63% in May (vs. 4.48% in April ’21). Gross NPLs stood at P479bn in May vs. P463bn in April. Total loan portfolio (ex-interbank) dropped slightly to P10.337tn in May vs. P10.342tn in April. NPL coverage ratio dropped to 79.96% from 81.48%.

SEC approves Filinvest REIT IPO

The SEC approved the IPO of Filinvest REIT with its ticker to be FILRT. The sale will be up to 1.8bn shares at P8.30s/share (up to P15bn to be raised). The breakdown will be secondary shares with 1.63bn shares and an overallotment option with 163mn shares. FILRT will initially have 300,000sqm of office space; 90% occupied and only 2% leased to POGOs. The location will be 280,000sqm in Alabang and 20,000sqm in Cebu. FILRT will not own land. At P8.30/share, FILRT will offer 5.6% 2022E dividend yield (based on prospectus). Pricing date will be on July 13; Offer period will run through July 19 to 28; Listing date is currently set on August 6.

 
 

Macro News

IATF relaxes interzonal travel rules for fully-vaccinated persons

The government released IATF Resolution 124-B, allowing interzonal travelers who are fully vaccinated against COVID-19 to only present their vaccination card in lieu of a swab test. An individual is considered fully vaccinated two weeks after he/she received the second jab of a two-dose vaccine or the jab of a one-dose vaccine. New rules will also apply to senior citizens. Interzonal travel refers to the movement between provinces, highly urbanized cities, and independent component cities under different community quarantine classifications. The presentation of a COVID-19 domestic vaccination card signed by a legitimate vaccinating establishment, or certificate of quarantine completion issued by the Bureau of Quarantine shall be sufficient alternatives to any testing requirement (before travel or upon arrival) unless LGU’s insist otherwise. All travelers, however, are still required to undergo health and exposure screening protocols upon arrival at their destinations.

Banks’ real estate exposure rises to 21.55% in 2020

According to a report by Philstar, latest data from the Banko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) show that the exposure of Philippine banks in the real estate sector rose to a record high of 21.5% last year from 19.84% in 2019. Investments and loans extended by the banking industry to the property sector increased 5.5% to P2.62trn in 2020 from P2.48trn in 2019. This is the highest on record from a previous peak of 21.04% in end-March 2017. Lending to property developers went up 6% to P2.29trn in end-2020 from P2.16trn in end-2019. Meanwhile, commercial real estate loans rose 4.3% to P1.46trn in 2020 from P1.4trn while residential real estate loans jumped 36% to P833.73bn from P763.98bn. The BSP said the gross non-performing real estate loans of Philippine banks inched up to P105.358bn, translating to non-performing real estate loans of 4.6% in 2020 vs 1.72% in 2019.

30mn vaccines to arrive over the next two months

The Philippines will receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August from previous expectations of 13.3mn in July and 13.6mn in August. The Philippines has now received a total of 17.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 12mn doses. Some Metro Manila city governments have temporarily halted administration of first doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021. 

Headline inflation eases to 6-month low in June

Headline inflation slowed down to 4.1% in June from 4.5% in May. Below consensus which was at 4.3%; and also within the BSP’s monthly forecast range of 3.9-4.7% for the month. The deceleration was due to non-food items, particularly transport inflation (base effects). Food inflation was steady; Utilities (+2.3% from +1.9% in May). This brings 6M21 average inflation rate to 4.4%, above the government’s target of 2-4%.

Infra spend bounces back in May

Infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) jumped to P78.9bn in May, +103% YoY and +36% MoM. The government cited projects of the Dept. of Public Works and payments for the construction of the new Senate building. This brings 5M21 infra spend (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P332bn, up 41% YoY. The government is targeting to spend P761bn (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in FY21, +12% YoY.

Metro Manila cities limit vaccinations

The local government units in Metro Manila have slowed down their vaccination drive amid a shortage in supply. Some city governments have temporarily halted administration of first doses. The Philippines has now received a total of 17.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 11.7mn doses. 1mn AstraZeneca doses (donation from Japan) and 170,000 Sputnik V doses are scheduled to arrive this week. The Philippines expects to receive 13.3mn doses in July and 13.6mn doses in August. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

NG outstanding debt reaches P11tn in May

The National Government’s total debt stock stood at P11.07tn in end-May, equivalent to ~62% of GDP. This is up from P9.8tn in end-2020 (=54.6% of GDP). Domestic borrowings rose 18% from end-2020 to P7.9tn (71% share) while external borrowings grew 2% to P3.2tn (29% share)

Philippines receives 1.1mn AstraZeneca doses

The Philippines received 1.12mn AstraZeneca doses. The donation from the Japanese government. The Philippines expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August, including the 1.12mn doses that just arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 18.6mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 12mn doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.
