REGIS REPORT

05 March 2021

The Philippines gained 1.3% this week, arresting three straight weekly declines.   The market closed the week at 6881.37, down 3.6% YTD.

Foreign selling continued though outflows were lighter this week at US$57m (from US$87m last week).

Vaccinations began Monday, with the arrival of an initial shipment of Sinovac vaccines (600K doses) from China. Meanwhile, the first shipment of the AstraZeneca Oxford vaccines ( 487k doses ) arrived yesterday. Around 11,000 have been inoculated so far using the Sinovac vaccine.

Statements that President Duterte would be more keen to ease movement restrictions with  the vaccine rollout helped sentiment.  The latest Google Mobility data showed a mild improvement in recent weeks after the deterioration following the end of the holiday season.

On the corporate side, 4Q results continued with COVID 19 and resultant lockdowns continuing to take their toll.

BDO’s (+5.0% WoW)  profit beat was seemed to be eclipsed by the asset quality overhang. BDO’s  restructured loan (RL) portfolio already “just above 6%” of total loans.  NPLs jumped to 2.65% from 1.95% in 3Q20, and NPL cover fell to 105% from 138% in 3Q. Security Bank’s (-2.2% WoW) 4Q profit appeared soft at just P768m while provisions stayed high.

Meanwhile, SM’s (+3.2% WoW) Retail business 4Q revenue improved 18% QoQ due to looser lockdown restrictions and the holiday season. Revenue was still down 20% YoY however.

Retailers RRHI (-5.8% WoW) and Wilcon  (-1.1% WoW) also reported. RRHI saw a sequential improvement in sales (+20% QoQ) during the quarter (-11% YoY) partly due to the holidays and some easing in mobility restrictions.  Wilcon meanwhile reported in line profits (P1.4bn -32% YoY). 4Q20 sales grew 6% YoY on the back of +0.5% SSSG and the opening of 6 new Depots throughout the year.

URC’s (+0.3% WoW) bottomline beat was driven by non-operating items. However, FY20 EBIT of P16bn (+7% YoY) was within expectations. As expected, revenue from the Philippine branded consumer food business was soft (-4% YoY in 4Q20) on subdued holiday spending and limited mobility.

In contrast to URC’s YoY EBIT improvement, Meralco (+2.95% WoW) and Manila Water (-1.8% WoW) saw declines. After recovering nicely over 3Q20, electricity volumes dipped again in 4Q (-7%) as mobility restrictions held back demand from commercial establishments.

Robinsons Land (-0.3% WoW) meanwhile saw 4Q20 mall revenue and Philippine presales both falling 67% YoY.

PLDT’s (+0.85% WoW) latest results shows continued momentum in 4Q resulting in another gain in wireless revenue share. FY20 core profits (including Voyager losses) came in at P26.1bn (+4%) on service revenue of P174bn (+8%).

ICTSI’s (+4.2% WoW) 4Q20 core profit bounced 54% QoQ, as volumes across its portfolio continued to recover.  Headline FY20 profit of US$102m included non-cash impairment charges and provisions totaling US$180mn. Without these, core profit after perps rose 10% to US$220mn, well above Regis and street estimates.

Foreigners were 22% of the market but this was lowered by a Jollibee block relating to the consolidation of ownership in JFC (of “founding owners”) into a single holdco.  Adjusting for this, foreigners would have been 35% of the  market.

SM Investments - 4Q20 results: Recovery remains tentative
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY20 profits halve to P23.4bn

SM Investments reported net income of P23.4bn for FY20, down by 48% from the previous year. While all three main segments suffered under the strain of the pandemic and lockdowns, the shortfall vs. our expectations was due more to the property and retail businesses. Business conditions are improving but they are not doing so as quickly as hoped.

BDO: Loan restructuring caps NPL rise

BDO reported net income of P28.2bn, down 36% YoY after it set aside P30bn in provisions, a five-fold increase. NPLs ended the year at 2.65% from 1.95% in 2019. A reason why it didn’t climb more is because the bank’s been aggressively restructuring loans. BDO managed to eke out loan growth of 3%.

 

SM Prime: Concerns over 1Q21 relapse?

SM Prime’s FY20 earnings fell 53% to P18bn with 4Q profit down 65%. Foot traffic in its malls climbed to 50-60% of pre-pandemic levels in Dec but has since retreated to below 50%. One bright spot is the surprising jump in reservation sales, which grew 37% YoY in the last quarter.

SM Retail: Department stores still in the red; Food retail sees drop in 4Q samestore sales

SM Retail showed QoQ improvement due to looser lockdown restrictions and the holiday season but the fragility of consumer spend remains evident. 4Q revenue was still down 20% YoY and income down 52%, aggravated by a P500m writedown. Food retail revenue was down 1% YoY in 4Q, suggesting SSS drop of 6-8%. Overall, full year revenue for SM Retail was down 19% and income by 67%. The group continued to aggressively expand the footprint of Alfamart, the small format grocery operation. It ended 2020 with 1,012 Alfamart stores and intends to add over 200 this year.

BDO Unibank - Earnings beat, but RLs raise asset quality concerns
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Restructuring flatters NPLs, NIMs and earnings

BDO’s FY20 NPAT of P28.2bn was down 36% YoY, but beat our forecasts. Loans grew 3% YoY, and NIMs were flat QoQ, both better than peers. Asset quality deteriorated though, with NPLs up 68bps QoQ to 2.65% and NPL cover down to 109.5% from 138% in 3Q. Restructured loans (RLs) were about 3% of BDO’s book, with another 3% approved for restructuring. The bank views RLs as a win-win, giving borrowers relief, and allowing the bank to accrue interest. However, it suggests much higher “stress” levels in BDO’s books compared to its peer banks.

NIMs steady (+3bps) QoQ, as deposit costs plunge; Fees recovering nicely

While loan growth slowed to 3% YoY from 6% in 3Q, this was better than the industry’s -1.6%. NIMs were flat (+3bps) QoQ at 4.28%, despite loan yields falling 18bps QoQ. Deposit costs fell to a remarkable 0.31% in 4Q, as CASA jumped 17% YoY, taking CASA ratio to 81% (Figs 1&2). NIM pressure could manifest in 1Q21 though, as funding costs are unlikely to fall further. Fee incomes were down 19% in FY20, but 4Q alone was up 26% QoQ, already approaching pre-Covid levels.

NPL (2.65%) and NPL cover (110%) in line with peers; but 6% RLs raise concerns

After a surprisingly benign 3Q, NPLs jumped 68bps QoQ to end the year at 2.65% (Fig 3). BDO put through provision expense of P6.4bn in 4Q, taking the full year to P30.2bn (c135bps credit cost). Despite this, NPL cover fell to 109.5% from 138% in 3Q (Fig 4). Both NPL and cover ratios were in line with peers. However, BDO’s RL level was significantly higher, at just over 6% of loans, compared to 1.1% for BPI and 0.4% for MBT (Fig 5&6). Of this, roughly 3% (P68.6bn) was formally classified as RL while another 3% was “in process”, and already in Stage 2. Management reckons this gives clients hurt by lockdowns, but with otherwise good business models, continued access to credit. And as the loans are current, BDO continues to accrue interest income. While the strategy may ultimately prove sound, it complicates assessment of the risks to the bank’s loan book, particularly when compared to its peer banks.

Robinsons Retail - FY20 core operations ahead; weak demand drags same-store sales
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Non-operating items drag FY20 net profit; core operations ahead

RRHI reported FY20 net profit of P2.9bn (-25% YoY) behind Regis (P3.1bn) and consensus (P3.3bn) FY20 estimates. The underperformance was mainly due to higher interest expense. Operating profit (-25% YoY to P5.9bn) was above our expectations even as sales (-7% YoY to P151bn) came in just slightly ahead of forecasts. For 4Q20 alone, sales were down 11% YoY to P41.5bn, while operating profit declined 35% YoY to P1.6bn.

Supermarkets: 4Q SSSG deteriorates on weak consumption, competition

Supermarket sales (57% of total) dipped 5% YoY in 4Q20 alone, as same-store sales declined by 2%. Management attributed the SSS decline to weak demand exacerbated by competitive pressure from social media marketplaces. Despite the soft top line, EBITDA margin was flat YoY as Rustans’ profitability continued to improve, and supplier support (“other income”) recovered.

Other formats mixed; holiday sales tepid

The 4Q performance of RRHI’s other retail formats was mixed – department stores, convenience stores, and DIY stores (20% of total sales) saw some sequential improvement in same-store sales, partly due to the holiday bump and the partial easing of mobility restrictions. Drug store sales (33% of total) rose 5% YoY due to the consolidation of Rose Pharmacy (beginning Nov), but SSS saw a faster decline (-10% in 4Q vs -3% in 3Q) due to the decrease in outpatient visitation.

Recovery dependent on consumer mobility; e-commerce contribution to rise

Management is targeting SSSG of 0-3% in 2021 (Regis FY21E: +12%), though the recovery is expected to be more significant in 2H21 after the YoY impact of pantry stocking and the lockdown-related store closures subside. The company is also resuming store expansion this year with a target gross addition of 80-120 stores (Regis: 56 net new stores), of which 15-20 are supermarkets, and 30-50 are drug stores. Margins are expected to improve as 1) sales normalize, 2) and trading terms at Rose Pharmacy (EBIT negative in 2020) are aligned with the Group. E-commerce contribution is expected to rise to 2-3% of total sales from c.1% in 2020.

FY20 core profit of P21.7bn falls short of expectations

After appearing on track for a recovery in 3Q, volumes retreated by 3.2%QoQ in 4Q20, falling short of our estimates. The underperformance of San Buenaventura Power (P1.0bn vs our FY estimate of P1.165bn) also contributed to the shortfall.

4Q volumes down 7%YoY, drop from September peak

Cash dividend declared; net cash recovers

MER declared a cash dividend of P7.824/share to shareholders on record as of March 30, payable on April 26. This brings the total payout of 2020 Core EPS to 65%, lower than our 70-75% payout estimate. Net cash recovered to P57bn from P40bn in 3Q, which suggests an improvement in receivable levels.

Addressing the lapsed period: the next best thing While there is no progress on a new rate review, the ongoing hearings on the “lapsed” regulatory period (from July 2015 onwards) are the next best thing. The affirmation of a “final” tariff for the period could remove a major risk, and result in (1) a hefty reversal of accumulated provisions and (2) higher prospective earnings.

With San Miguel (SMC PM) submitting the lowest tariffs for the 1800MW competitive selection process conducted by Meralco (MER PM), work on the 1200MW Atimonan will now have to be pushed back. We believe it will be a tall order for anyone to deliver the 1800MW of new capacity by the committed dates (Dec 2024 and May 2025).

Universal Robina - 4Q20 tempered by weak holiday spending, mobility restrictions
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY20 ahead on non-operating items; operating profit in line

URC’s net profit grew 10% YoY to P10.7bn in FY20, ahead of Regis expectations (P9.7bn) but below consensus (P11.4bn). The outperformance relative to our forecasts was mainly due to lower interest expense. Revenues (-1% YoY to P133bn) and EBIT (+7% YoY to P16bn) were broadly in line with expectations. For 4Q20 alone, operating profit grew 4% YoY to P4.1bn, largely driven by the nonbranded consumer food (non-BCF) businesses.

PH BCF recovery tempered by weak holiday spending, limited mobility

PH BCF sales declined 4% YoY in 4Q20 as consumption was tempered by more subdued holiday spending. Sales growth across the board had slowed, even for key categories, such as coffee and snacks, which were flattish YoY. Limited consumer mobility led to a continued contraction of impulse categories (RTD tea and confectionery), albeit at a slower rate vs 3Q20.

International BCF recovers, but dragged by forex

Int’l BCF sales were down 2% YoY in 4Q20, dragged by the forex. In constant currency terms, we estimate 4Q sales growth was in the low single digit range. Outperformers were Oceania (c40% of segment sales), which benefitted from pantry stocking, and VN (c10% of segment sales), which loosened lockdown restrictions earlier in the year.

Cost inflation manageable for now

Management expects to see a recovery overall in 2021, though admits that it the pace of recovery will be dependent on multiple factors. Generally, the company is more optimistic about the recovery outside of the Phils, where the lockdown measures are less restrictive. Cost inflation could be a near-term risk. The company is proactively managing input costs and says it will try to avoid price increases as much as it can given the current environment. Any further raw material cost inflation will be mitigated by opex savings from its supply chain transformation. At 23x PER for FY21, we think URC still looks attractive despite the challenges facing the Philippine macro outlook.

FY20 net profit -32% YoY to P1.4bn – in line with forecasts

WLCON reported FY20 net profit of P1.4bn, down 32% YoY, in line with Regis estimates. Net sales of P22.6bn (-8%YoY) and operating profit of P2.4bn (-22% YoY) were likewise within expectations. New store openings drive 4Q20 top line

Philippine Retail - REGIS CEO Roundtable Series: Philippine Seven Corp.
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Shifting gears

We hosted Mr. Victor Paterno, President and CEO of Philippine Seven Corp. to give an update on the company’s operations. After a tumultuous 2020, Philippine Seven is pivoting its business model to cater to the new consumer lifestyle, as well as emerging competition from minimarts. The pivot has not come without its own challenges, but the company believes the move is necessary for its survival. Ecommerce, which the company has been an early adopter of, will continue to play a role in its strategy moving forward.

Looking beyond the 2020 challenges

2020 was a turning point for Philippine Seven, with its convenience store (CVS) operations heavily disrupted by the lockdown. With limited consumer mobility, average customer count dropped over 40% in 2020. While basket sizes also grew significantly (+37%), it was not enough to fully offset the drop in customer traffic. Moreover, CVS stores lost at least 1/3 of the day part by not being able to operate 24/7 amidst curfews and other restrictions. Aggressive store expansion plans have since been put on hold as the company shifts its gears.

Accelerating pivot to CVS + minimart format

In mid-2020, SEVN launched “Project Alpha”, a strategy to pivot operations into a CVS + minimart format. The strategy was already in the works as early as 2018 given emerging competition from minimarts, but Mr Paterno felt the need to fast-track its rollout given the pandemic. The initial stages of the pivot were challenging, but Mr Paterno believes that the shift was necessary for survival amidst the changing environment.

Well-positioned for e-commerce

SEVN is well-positioned for e-commerce being an early adopter even prepandemic. The company launched its online grocery (cliqqgrocery.com), which will build on its existing supply chain. The company likewise continues to grow its e-wallet operation and has partnered with digital lending startups. It has also started deploying its cash-recycling ATMs in partnership with 7Bank.

Revenue momentum continues

Consumer NPLs, write-offs drag profits

Consumer account for all 2H20 provisions

Consumers accounted for c 75% of provisions in FY20, and all incremental provisions in 2H20 (Fig 4). Overall NPL ratio eased to 3.90% in 4Q from 4.03% in 3Q (Fig 5) due to aggressive write-offs in the bank’s unsecured loans (mostly credit cards). Outlook improving, but tied to macro recovery

Management noted that consumer delinquencies have steadily improved in 1Q21. However, they remain cautious, expecting a recovery in 2H21 as mobility improves and the Covid vaccine rollout goes into full swing.

TP and earnings downgrades

We downgrade CHP to Sell (from Hold) on a revised TP of P0.95/sh (- 14%) to reflect what we believe will be a deterioration in the company’s near-to medium-term outlook. We reduce EBITDA and profit estimates, after incorporating (a) a muted demand recovery and (b) lower ASPs. Muted volume recovery, ASPs likely to drop further

TP implies 27% potential downside from current levels

Our revised price target of P0.95/sh implies a downside potential of 27% from current levels. Trading at 8.4x FY21E and 7.7x FY22E EV/EBITDA, CHP is at a premium to historical average (7.3x). Upside risks: higher ASPs, faster pick-up in the investment cycle, a higher import duty, and industry consolidation.
