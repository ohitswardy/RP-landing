REGIS REPORT

12 March 2021

The PSEi dropped 2.2% this week with COVID again dominating the headlines. The rising number of cases – now at above 3,000 daily new cases – dampened sentiment. In response to the rise, the mayors of Metro Manila agreed to impose a common curfew policy (10pm to 5am) across the metropolis.

The market was red across the board, with 26 of the 30 index names ending with declines this week.  Inflation also hurt sentiment as the latest Feb print of 4.7% came in the highest print in over two years. The sharp rebound in commodity prices also spells further upside risk to inflation.

Foreign outflows weighed on the market – with net selling at US$259m. A block in PLDT registered US$205m in outflows, however.

Retail investors were active this week, wtih five of the top ten traded names known to be dominated by retail and accounted for 35% of total turnover. 

BPI  (-7.3% WoW) and BDO (-4.2% WoW) saw among the biggest drops while Security Bank (-2.4% WoW) and Metrobank (-2.7% WoW) dropped as well. A proposed law to not only grant the BSP power to regulate loan rates but also to explicitly cap them at a fixed level (12%, or 3% above the short-term benchmark) created an overhang for the sector.

GT Capital (-4.8% WoW) also declined. While Toyota Motor Phils saw a 21% MoM (+6.0% YoY) rise in unit sales in Feb (over 13000 units), the safeguard duty implemented in March is expected to lead to depressed sales.

On results, reminders of a still-weak economy came from AP (unch) results – which showed electricity volumes were still down 9% as commercial-industrial usage plunged 14%. 

San Miguel Food and Beverage (-3.8% WoW) saw a seasonal uplift in beer sales but it was still sharply down (-23% YoY) in 4Q. The bright spots in the final quarter were the volatile poultry and hogs businesses which swung to a profit after huge losses in 2Q and 3Q.

Emperador (+1.2% WoW) said net income rose +18% in FY20, with earnings lifted by international business. 

Net profit of AC (+0.65% WoW) in FY20 was down by half from the previous year as the pandemic hit nearly every operating segment.

Bloomberry’s (-2.0% WoW) 4Q GGR jumped a healthy 21% QoQ jump. The company broke even on a cash basis despite constrained operations, posting EBITDA of P129m in 4Q (P219m excluding Jeju). For the year, BLOOM incurred a wider loss than Regis estimate of -P7.328bn, largely due to a nearly P1bn impairment charge.

DMCI Holdings (-0.4% WoW) reported FY20 core profit of P6.39bn (-47% YoY), which outperformed expectations due to faster completions at the property unit, lower costs at Maynilad, and higher nickel profits.

Ayala Land - Notes from the REGIS CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Full recovery in two to three years

We hosted a call with Bernard Dy, Ayala Land’s President, and Augusto Bengzon, the company’s CFO, for them to share insights on how the firm is handling the pandemic and their expectations over the near-term. Ayala Land expects therecovery to accelerate this year as vaccinations are rolled out in the country.However, Mr. Dy does not foresee a return to the 2019 level of performance until two to three years after.

Some changes are temporary, somewill be long-term trends

The COVID pandemic has changed the way many Filipinos live and work. Mr. Dy believes that some of these are temporary, such as the relative preference for suburban housing as well as the current prevalence of work-from-home schemes. He acknowledges, however, that e-commerce will continue to grow. In addition, he believes that the pandemic has reinforced the attractiveness of integrated communities, which he considers Ayala Land’s strength.

No substantial increase in residential buyer defaults

The company has not seen a meaningful increase in buyer defaults. Management believes there is little credit risk from its residential customers since buyers of its high-end brands, which account for c. 60% of sales, predominantly pay in cash or “deferred cash”(i.e. post-dated checks). Banks also share the view that Ayala buyers are of higher credit quality, as evidenced by their willingness to either provide direct mortgage financing or purchase ALI’s residential receivables.

Mall foot traffic only at 35%-45% of pre-COVID levels

4Q20 mall foot traffic was only 35%-45% of pre-COVID levels while tenant sales were only at 53%. As a result, Ayala Land continues to grant substantial rental concessions.Management believes that malls cannot return to pre-COVID levels of profitability until full mobility is achieved and the CBDs are fully populated with office workers again.

Office vacancy to remain high, rental rates to soften

Ayala Land’s 4Q20 office vacancy increased 7ppt to 12%, partly due to POGO closures. Even though the industry has scaled back office completions for the next few years, Mr. Dy believes that it will take at least two years to return to the 5%-8% vacancy level. Thus, he expects rental rates to soften near-term. No major uptick in construction costs but delays to continue Construction costs have only increased at low single digit levels and Ayala Land does not expect significant increases this year. However, construction delays will continue as the company has only deployed 32,000 workers, about 50% of the pre-pandemic level.

SM Prime - Notes from the REGIS CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Implementing a click and collect strategy

We hosted Jeffrey Lim, SM Prime’s President, and John Ong, the company’s CFO, to get an update on current operations as well as management’s outlook. Mall foot traffic remains weak due to the pandemic, which has also accelerated the adoption of e-commerce. To mitigate the disruption from online retail, SM Prime has adopted a click and collect strategy to complement its existing brick-and-mortar malls.

Mall foot traffic slows down again

Foot traffic in 2M21 has slowed to c. 45% of pre-pandemic levels, from c. 60% in December 2020. Provincial malls can achieve up to 70% of pre-pandemic foot traffic and are generally performing better than Metro Manila malls due to looser quarantine restrictions in those areas. Within Metro Manila, neighborhood malls are stronger than destination malls.

E-commerce – click and collect strategy implemented in 3 malls

To mitigate the disruption from e-commerce, SM Prime has adopted a click and collect strategy. It is still in its infancy and has only been implemented in 3 Metro Manila malls. Tenants from these malls are part of SM Prime’s e-mall platform. Buyers can order from the platform and pick-up their order in the SM mall of their choice. For provincial orders, fulfillment could take 2-3 days. For orders from the e-mall platform, tenants will be charged a lower rate than the brick and mortar rental rate.

Mall REIT delayed; working on an office REIT

As early as 2019, the company had already decided on which malls would be included in its shopping center REIT and even said it intended to raise US$1bn[1]US$1.5bn from a REIT listing. Unfortunately, the pandemic derailed these plans and a mall REIT listing is unlikely this year. Meanwhile, the company is just about to start preparations for an office REIT.

No major uptick in residential defaults; to benefit from CREATE Bill

The company has not seen a spike in residential defaults. However, Mr. Lim acknowledges that this might occur later in the year. If necessary, SM Prime would be willing to extend payment terms for buyers that have already paid at least 10% of the unit value. The corporate tax reform package called “CREATE”, which is expected to become law in 1H21, proposes to raise the threshold for VAT-free residential purchases from P2.5mn/unit to P4.2mn/unit. Thus, the company plans to offer more smaller-sized units in order to take advantage of the P4.2mn ceiling. It plans to launch 15,000-20,000 units this year (from 15,000 units in 2020).

Filinvest Land - Raising up to P15bn from an office REIT
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

REIT could be larger than FLI

Filinvest Land disclosed that it could raise up to P14.9bn from the REIT IPO of its wholly owned subsidiary, Cyberzone Properties Inc (CPI). At the maximum offer price, CPI would have a market capitalization of P40.6bn, larger than Filinvest Land’s P28.1bn. Thus, the REIT listing could be a significant catalyst for FLI’s share price.

300,000sqm of PEZA-accredited office space

Cyberzone Properties owns about 300,000sqm of PEZA-accredited office space, comprising the majority of Filinvest Land’s 520,000sqm office portfolio. About 90% of CPI’s portfolio is located in Alabang while 10% is in Cebu. Notably, CPI does not own the land under the buildings. The Alabang property is owned by Filinvest Land while the Cebu land is held by the government.

90% occupancy

CPI’s 9M20 revenue grew 16% to P2.46bn, largely due to floor area expansion in late 2019. However, pretax profit only increased 7% to P1.56bn due to higher interest expense. As of 3Q20, Cyberzone Properties had lower office occupancy than the industry but also had a lower proportion of space leased to POGOs.

FLI NAV could rise up to 10%

At the maximum offer price of P8.30/share, we estimate that CPI could be valued at c. 5.5% cap rate, lower than the 7% that we assign for office properties. If CPI were priced at that level, we estimate that our FLI NAV would rise by c. 10%. Filinvest Land is currently trading at 5.5X FY21E P/E, 0.4X FY21E P/BV, and 63% discount to NAV.

by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q20 profit -16%yoy

We estimate FB’s net income to equity holders in FY20 was P12.5bn (-32%yoy), based on the reported profits of subsidiaries. This is broadly in line with our estimates, with the outperformance of the alcoholic segments making up for the shortfall in the food segment for the year. In 4Q20 alone, the derived income to equity holders of P4.7bn (31%qoq) showed a marked improvement sequentially, driven by a recovery in food. Beer volumes were however still off by 23% from pre-Covid levels.

Beer volumes still down 23%yoy as bars stay closed

Profits from San Miguel Brewery continued to rebound on the back of an 18%qoq improvement in volumes. It sold an estimated 64mn cases in 4Q, still off by 23% from the same period last year, mimicking the YoY decline in 3Q. Sales outlets were still largely limited to off-premise channels as bars remain closed and most restaurants limit alcohol sales. For the year, beer volumes were down 34% to 202mn cases. Net margin remained elevated (18% in 4Q20 vs 20% 3Q20 and 9% in 2Q20) on substantial cuts to A&P and special events.

Gin volumes +26% in 4Q

Gin volumes remained strong, hitting 9.4mn cases (+26%yoy) in 4Q20 after growing +33%yoy in 3Q20. Ginebra continued to benefit from down-trading and home consumption. 4Q20 profit, however, dipped 43%qoq. Management cited aggressive marketing campaigns for gin, in contrast to the more subdued promotional strategies for beer.

Food segment rebounds with poultry; beneficiary of food inflation

The food segment got a marked uplift from the protein segment in 4Q20. Without citing figures, management credited the turn in broiler prices for the jump in profits (vs poultry losing money in both 2Q/3Q). As prices remain elevated, FB is among the few beneficiaries of the current food inflation, deriving about a quarter of revenues from poultry and hogs. As in previous quarters, sales of prepared and packaged goods continued to grow in the teens.

Philippine Property - Sluggish recovery vs wall of liquidity
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Don’t buck the BSP

The Philippine property sector is on the mend but the pace of recovery has been slow, and we do not expect a return to pre-COVID levels by 2022. Amidst this backdrop, the BSP has flooded the markets with liquidity and pushed real interest rates to negative territory. This wall of liquidity is supporting prices not just of physical property but also of property stocks, despite the near-term operational challenges. After cutting our target prices by (an average of) 45% last year, we raise our target prices now by an average of 17% (see table on right hand side).

Turning more negative on the office segment

We expect more meaningful recoveries for the mall business and high-end residential sales by 2022E. However, we turn more negative on the office segment in particular. We expect further POGO closures, coupled with an increase in completions, to raise office vacancy from 4% in 2019 to 12% in 2021E-2022E. The mortgage NPL ratio jumped to a 14-year high of 8.4% in 3Q20, signaling that some buyers are experiencing financial difficulties. Public transportation remains limited and mall foot traffic has declined after a pick-up during the December holiday season.

Ayala Land offers the best risk-reward

In our view, Ayala Land offers the best risk-reward among developers we cover. 60% of its residential sales are to high-end buyers, who are less affected by the pandemic and benefit more from negative real interest rates. Furthermore, 50% of Ayala Land’s NAV is comprised of land that is near the government’s planned infrastructure projects.

Buy: ALI, RLC; Hold: FLI, MEG, SMPH, VLL

We maintain our Buy rating on Robinsons and upgrade Ayala Land from Hold to Buy. Meanwhile, we downgrade Filinvest and Megaworld from Buy to Hold due to our more bearish view on the office segment. We also maintain our Hold ratings on SM Prime and Vista Land. Key risks: COVID, POGO sector, and interest rates.

Ayala Corporation - 4Q: Glimpses of recovery; all eyes on power
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q20 improves, but demand drivers far from fully recovered

Ayala Corp reported FY20 core net income of P26.0bn (-16%) which excludes the divestment gains in 2019, a partial reversal of the MWC remeasurement loss and significant loan loss provision at BPI. Its actual FY20 headline income of P17.1bn was down 51% and below estimates. The good news is that, on a sequential basis, 4Q20 income continued to improve. Income for the quarter rose 69% QoQ to P5.8bn, driven by better profits from property, water and other one-time items.

Property: POGO departures drive office vacancy to 12%

Profits at Ayala Land climbed 28% QoQ on higher residential revenue (+64% QoQ) as construction activity improved. The FY profit is below forecast, however, as the mall and office segments underperformed. Mall revenues were still down 76% YoY due to rental concessions, while office vacancy rose to 12% as POGOs closed.

Banking: NPLs decrease 30bps QoQ, provisions lift NPL coverage to 115%

BPI’s 4Q income of P4.2bn was down 38%YoY. The bank put through nearly P7bn in provision expense in the quarter, improving NPL coverage to 115% (from 100% in 3Q), but dragging the bottom line to its lowest quarterly print for 2020. Loans contracted 4.6%YoY while NPLs dropped 30bps QoQ to 2.68% on write-offs.

Telecom: No holiday uptick for mobile

Mobile revenues did not see the typical 4Q uplift and was still down 12% YoY in 4Q. Home broadband revenues grew 28% YoY but were not as strong as subscriber adds (+88%YoY). Core income was 15% lower YoY due to the revenue shortfall.

Power: Readies war chest for 5GW attributable renewable capacity

Combined with the GIC investment, the fundraising activities could net the company a little over P30bn this year. The parent company of ACEN also agreed to divest its stake in the 4x135MW GNP Kauswagan coal project for c$453mn, in line with its green energy push and capital recycling strategies.

Bloomberry Resorts - Locals make comeback; regulator turns lenient on capacity limits
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q EBITDA turns positive; loss includes impairment on Jeju asset

BLOOM’s 4Q20 net loss of P2.494bn (flat QoQ) included an impairment charge on the carrying value of its South Korean assets. Excluding this, GGR actually posted a healthy 21% jump QoQ despite mobility restrictions in Manila remaining largely the same. It broke even on a cash basis even on constrained operations, posting EBITDA of P129mn in 4Q (P219mn excluding Jeju). For the year, BLOOM incurred a wider loss than our estimate of -P7.328bn, largely due to a nearly P1bn impairment charge.

GGR improves to 36% of pre-Covid levels on rising foot traffic

The 4Q GGR of P5.312bn (+21%QoQ, -63%YoY) implies a run-rate of P1.8bn which is a little under 40% of pre-Covid levels. Worth highlighting were the GGR increases from mass tables (+78%QoQ) and slot machines (73%QoQ), driven by volume from increased visitation (8k-9k foot traffic, 40% of pre-Covid foot traffic). Despite a 30% stated capacity limit, management explained that PAGCOR has quietly allowed the properties to utilize as much as 50% of capacity. Mass volumes continued to trend higher in the first two months of the year compared to December 2020. VIP, however, was softer than expected. Outside the impact of win rate, volumes dipped 18%QoQ after the initial rebound in 3Q. The limited local player pool will likely lead to lumpy VIP revenues until foreign travel restrictions are eventually lifted.

Operations no longer burning through cash; P23.4bn at end Dec-20

BLOOM ended the quarter with cash of P23.351bn, just marginally lower than the P24.6bn cash level at the end of 3Q20. As revenues already exceeded cash opex, the major uses of cash were capex (P1.3bn) and principal/interest payments (P1.8bn). There was a P1.5bn drawdown from the Solaire North loan facility.

Aboitiz Power - Insurance claims, lower fuel cost & spot prices boost profit; demand still weak
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

FY20 core income -25%

Aboitiz Power’s 4Q20 core income of P6.055bn (+104%QoQ, +106%YoY) included business interruption insurance claims of P1.3bn. 4Q19 of the generation business was also a depressed base due to outages at Therma South and GNP Mariveles. Underlying demand for power, however, still remained weak as shown by the 9% decline of distribution volumes. The 4Q result brings AP’s FY20 core income to P12.5bn (-25%YoY), slightly lower than our estimate (P12.95bn) but higher than consensus (P10.65bn).

Generation: lower fuel costs, spot prices improve margins

Core income from generation & RES jumped to P6.124bn (121%QoQ, 202%YoY). While energy sales were flat (volumes flat QoQ, revenue +3%), we believe a drop in the cost of fulfilling its supply contracts (lower fuel costs and spot prices) led to expanded margins. Management referenced peak demand in Luzon and Visayas hitting pre-Covid levels in the final week of December, but WESM prices actually softened to P1.93/kWh (24%QoQ), less than half the average price in 4Q19.

Distribution: Commercial and industrial segments still down 14%

Income from distribution softened to P730mn (-33%QoQ, -28%YoY). Electricity sales volumes were still down by 9%YoY, similar to the decline in 3Q20. Commercial & industrial segments were off by 14% from pre-Covid, while the growth in residential volumes cooled to 6%YoY (from 12%YoY in 3Q). For the year, gross margin/kWh was higher at P1.68/kWh (vs P1.63/kWh), skewed by disproportionately larger residential volumes.

More delays to GNPD

Commercial operations for GNP Dinginin are now slated for August 2021 (Unit 1) and January 2022 (Unit 2), a push back of another quarter compared to previously announced targets in November 2020. The capacity additions from these plants were expected to weigh down on spot prices, and the delay could provide some respite at least in the earlier parts of 2021.

DMCI: FY20 profit drops 47%; near- to medium-term prospects look patchy

4Q20 profit up 4%. Recurring profit in FY20 fell 47% YoY to P6.39bn, ahead of Regis but in line with consensus. Lower coal prices and the pandemic-induced lockdown affected most of its operating units in FY20, save for nickel mining.  In 4Q20 alone, profits improved for construction and nickel mining units but these were blunted by plant outages, the timing of cost recognition at the property unit, and the lingering effects of the pandemic. We believe profits should bounce strongly in FY21, on the back of higher coal prices and a rebound in the Philippine economy. Beyond this period, however, growth for the group will largely depend on management (a) securing more power contracts, (b) winning construction bids, and (c) securing mining permits (e.g. nickel and limestone). Property: No construction constraints / 4Q reservation sales flat YoY.  The property unit’s FY20 profit fell 36% YoY to P1.94bn, exceeding estimates on higher booked revenues and better margins. Construction: Rebound in 4Q / Participating in P105bn biddings in FY21.   Easing mobility restrictions allowed DMCI’s construction unit to post a profit in 4Q20, bringing FY20 profit down 88% YoY to P109bn (below estimates).  However, the company is still operating at c. 10% below pre-Covid levels. 2020 FDI inflows slump to 5-year low

Preliminary data released by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) showed FDI net inflows shrank by 24.6% to US$6.542bn in 2020 from US$8.671bn in 2019. This is the lowest since 2015, but exceeded the US$6bn full-year projection made by the BSP. In December alone, FDI plunged 62.6% YoY to US$509mn. The govt cited base effects given significantly large inflows from net investments in equity capital and debt instruments in 2019. Meanwhile, equity other than reinvestment of earnings dropped by 89.8% to US$78mn in December. Investments in equity and fund shares likewise fell by 82.2% to US$149mn. Additionally, inflows to debt instruments went down by 31.1% to US$360mn. Reinvestment of earnings declined 2.6% to US$71mn. The BSP expects FDI to reach US$7.5bn in 2021, up 15% from the 2020 level.

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collected P134.27bn in February, in line with its target of P134.18bn. Collections, however, were 5.58% YoY lower. This brings YT-Feb collections to P316.42bn, down 6% YoY and was a tad short of its target (P317.18bn). The BIR is tasked to collect P2.081tn in 2021 (+7% YoY).

Domestic travel now easier

Most local governments around the country have been complying with the uniform protocols which relaxed requirements on domestic travel. 80% of local governments from cities, municipalities and provinces have complied with provisions according to Interior Undersecretary Epimaco Densing III. Last February, the Inter-Agency Task Force for the Management of Emerging Infectious Diseases (IATF) issued Resolution No. 101, which laid down the relaxed requirements on domestic travel. The uniform travel protocols removed the requirements of travel authority documents. Travelers are no longer required to undergo RT-PCR tests but the local government may still impose this.

Extended curfew (10PM-5AM) eyed in Metro Manila

The Department of Interior and Local Government will recommend to the Metro Manila Council an expanded curfew in the National Capital Region (NCR) amid an increase in COVID-19 cases. A unified curfew between 10pm to 5am will be recommended and Special Action Force will be deployed, especially in areas with high COVID-19 cases. Currently Metro Manila cities have varying curfews, but San Juan City recently implemented an extended curfew. Only health workers, essential workers, security personnel, public transportation, and delivery drivers, as well as those who have health emergencies, are exempted from the curfew hours.

Labor participation improves in January 2021 survey

The labor force participation rate improved to 60.5% in the January 2021 Labor Force Survey. This is better than the 58.7% recorded in October 2020 but still below the 61.7% recorded in January 2020. In absolute amounts, there were +1.6mn new jobs created QoQ and +267k YoY

Employment across industries improved QoQ, led by the services, manufacturing, and construction sectors. Only the agriculture sector saw improvements in employment YoY.

Unemployment rate reaches record 10.3% in 2020

Preliminary results of the 2020 Annual Labor and Employment Estimates showed the unemployment rate reached a record high of 10.3% (vs 5.1% in 2019). This was based on the average of four Labor Force Survey rounds (i.e. January, April, July, October 2020). Labor force participation rate (LFPR) stood at 59.5% in 2020, lower than the 61.3% in 2019.

Philippines could get 20mn vaccine doses in 2Q21

Secretary Carlito Galvez Jr. said that the Philippines might receive over 20mn COVID vaccine doses in 2Q21 from Sinovac, Novavax, AstraZeneca, and the COVAX facility. The Philippines has received 1.1mn doses and has administered 44,000 doses.

Inflation climbs to 4.7% in February

Inflation accelerated further to 4.7% in February from 4.2% the previous month within the government’s monthly forecast range of 4.3%-5.1%. This is the highest inflation posted since January 2019. Food inflation continued to creep up (+6.7%), driven by meat (+20.7%) and rice (+0.5%); the latter was attributed to the end of the harvest season. Higher fuel prices also drove inflation higher, with transport inflation accelerating +10.4% YoY and +0.6% MoM. The MoM, inflation decelerated to +0.2% from +1.3% in January which was due to the deceleration in meat inflation, which was attributed to price ceilings imposed on pork and chicken products in the National Capital Region. This brings YT-Feb average inflation rate to 4.5%, above the government’s target range of 2-4%.

Metro mayors agree to enforce common curfew for 2 weeks

Beginning March 15, Metro Manila will have a uniform curfew of 10pm to 5am that will last for two weeks to curb rising COVID-19 infections in the region. Metro Manila now contributes 60% of the country’s covid cases from only 8% in Feb 2-10. However, exempted are essential workers, takeout operations, and 24-hour restaurant operations.

GIR reserves rise to US$109.1bn in end-February

The country’s dollar reserves rose to US$109.082bn in end-February (+0.38% MoM) after dipping slightly in January (US$108.7bn). This was higher by 23.7% YoY. The MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad (+2.4% MoM). End-February GIR is enough to cover 11.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to end the year at US$102bn, down from last year’s record level US$110.1bn.

Vehicle sales rise 12% MoM in February

Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI and TMA), jumped 12% MoM in February to 26,230 units. The MoM recovery was attributed to improvements in sales of Asian utility vehicles and light trucks. This was however 12% lower YoY due to disruptions caused by the Taal volcano eruption last year. This brings YT-Feb industry vehicle sales  down by 7% YoY to 49,610 units. Toyota sold 13,074 units in February, up 6% YoY and 21% MoM and brings YT-Feb unit sales to 23,894 units (+13% YoY). The dealerships began implementing the safeguard duty in March so we expect sales to plunge. Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 14% discount to its market-based NAV of P636/sh.

Loan rate caps proposed – Congress hearings start

When the BSP set a cap on credit card interest rates and fees last year, we feared that it be a slippery slope. And here we are. Congress is set to hold hearings today (Fri 12 March) on House Bill 7967 a bill that proposes to update the defanged Usury Law. The bill’s key features are a maximum rate of 12% for all loans, including credit cards and cash advances. The BSP’s Monetary Board may set loan limits, PROVIDED that the maximum rate does not exceed three percentage points above the 91-day T-bill rate in the prior quarter. The MB may prescribe higher rates for “loans of low priority” such as consumer loans, or loans made by pawnshops, finance companies. The MB may also set the rates on deposits and deposit substitutes. This would be disastrous for banks. If applied today, the average 91-day T-bill rate in 4Q20 was 1.046%, which would put the maximum loan rate at 4.046%. For reference, effective loan yields for the banks we cover in 4Q20 were as follows – BDO at 5.84%; BPI at 5.28%; MBT at 5.76%; and SECB at 7.28%. The bill is clearly populist, and we wonder if it’s just for show. Elections are not that far away (May 2022). It is unclear if the BSP is supportive of the proposal, in spirit if not in letter.
