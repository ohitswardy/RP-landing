REGIS REPORT

10 September 2021

The PSEi closed up 1.1% to 6970.51. It was a lackluster week of trading for the PSEi, amidst a lack of market catalysts. Turnover was extremely thin, however, averaging only US$103m daily.

The absence of large foreign outflows helped keep the market buoyant this week, however.  Foreigners were even, with continued inflows seen in Globe (+5.9% WoW) and AC (+3.0% WoW) as well as consumer laggard Puregold (+1.2% WoW). Selling was however in recent strong gainers PLDT (-2.0% WoW) and Converge (+1.25% WoW). LT Group (+2.5% WoW) also saw outflows.  Prior to this week, the market has seen net inflows on four of the last five weeks. 

The index was split 2:1 between gainers and decliners.

Leading the gainers this week were ICTSI (+7.2% WoW) and  Globe.  Property names Megaworld (+5.6% WoW) and Ayala Land (+4.2% WoW) advanced as well, followed by conglos SMC (+3.9% WoW), Ayala Corp, LT Group, and AEV (+2.45% WoW).

On the other hand, PLDT saw some profit taking while large caps SM Prime (-1.6% WoW), BPI (-0.8% WoW) fell as well. 

Metro Manila remains under modified enhanced community quarantine (MECQ) until Sep 15. However, the government is looking at a pilot GCQ (general community quarantine) with alert level system.  Cases on the ground remain elevated, with the latest new case count at over 22800 (as of Thursday).  Nineteen percent of the total population have now received at least one dose.

Corporate news was thin as well save for news of Philippine Airlines’ (PAL) Chapter 11 filing in New York. Ayala Corp also  incorporated a new company for its logistics business endeavors.

Corporate News

Alfamart to open 200 stores in Luzon this year

Despite the pandemic, Alfamart Philippines plans to open 200 stores in Luzon this year. Harvey Ong, Alfamart Philippines COO, said the company is on track to reach 1,200 stores this year. Alfamart Philippines is a JV between SM Investments and Alfamart Indonesia.

Arbitration Tribunal rejects claims of NLEX on tariff petitions; Terminates Cavitex case

The Permanent Court of Arbitration denied the claims of NLEX Corp for the “unreasonable delay” of its 2012/2014 toll hike petitions. The Toll Regulatory Board already decided on the said petitions in 2018, after it approved upward toll rate adjustments for NLEX that was implemented in March 2019. The Tribunal also ruled that each Party will bear the costs of arbitration in equal shares and will bear their own costs of legal representation and assistance. The Tribunal also terminated the claims of Cavitex Corp. and Philippine Reclamation Authority after the latter sought to withdraw the case. Additionally, the Tribunal said that tariff petitions are now being processed by the TRB. The arbitration decisions do not impact our estimates and valuation for Metro Pacific (MPI PM), as we do not assume tariff hikes. MPI’s tollroad unit accounts for c. 25% of MPI’s gross profit and c. 20% of gross asset value. MPI is trading at a 31% discount to its market-based NAV of P5.70/sh.

Dito passes technical audit

Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that its subsidiary Dito Telecommunity passed the July 2021 technical audit. Dito Tel had 52.75% population coverage and “minimum average broadband speed” of 89.13mbps and 853.96mbps for 4G and 5G, respectively. Under the terms of the auction held three years ago, Dito Tel must achieve 51% population coverage and 55mbps minimum average speed.

Philippine Airlines files bankruptcy

Philippine Airlines (PAL) filed a Chapter 11 bankruptcy petition in the Southern District of New York. The petition includes an agreement with majority of aircraft lessors and lenders to reduce debt payments by over US$2bn. As at end-June, PAL’s net debt stood at P195bn (US$3.9bn); Capital deficiency at P86bn (US$2.2bn). The airline’s top five creditors with the largest “secured claims” (excluding claims of insiders) totaling US$866mn. Philippine National Bank (US$156.51mn); Banco De Oro Unibank, Inc. (US$80.42mn); China Banking Corp. (US$54.83mn); EXIM Guaranteed Loans (US$240.1mn), and PK Airfinance S.A.R.L. (US$334.23mn). It also listed creditors who have the 40 largest “unsecured claims and are not insiders” totaling to more than US$1.4bn. PAL will also reduce its fleet size by over 25% comprising of widebodies and narrowbodies and will return 21 planes. PAL’s fleet stood at 95 aircraft in end-June, of which 31 are widebodies under operating leases. The restructuring plan involves raising US$505mn via equity and debt from a major stockholder immediately. It will also secure US$150mn in additional debt financing from global private investors for post-restructuring activities.

Hong Kong bans Cebu Pacific passenger flights until Sept. 16

Hong Kong’s Centre for Health Protection of the Department of Health said Cebu Pacific was banned from carrying passengers from Manila to Hong Kong from Sep 3 to Sept. 16. Four passengers on Sept 1 were found positive for COVID upon arrival testing. Philippine Airlines was also prohibited from flying passengers from Manila from Aug. 29 until Sept. 11.

PLDT considers selling data centers for $500

PLDT is considering selling its data enters with a potential value of $500mn. PLDT has ten facilities with a combined capacity of 72MW. It is also considering a similar sale-and-leaseback deal for its cell towers which could raise as much as $1bn.

 

MPTC open to adjustments on CTBEX proposal

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) is open to make certain adjustments on its unsolicited proposal to build the P22.43-bn Cavite-Tagaytay-Batangas Expressway (CTBEX). The company is still at the stage of discussing the parameters, terms, and conditions for it to be able to undertake the Swiss challenge. The parameters relating to the right of way acquisition and also some issues on the real property tax are still being clarified with the government. MPTC was granted the Original Proponent Status by the Dept. of Public Works last July 2018. CTBEX is a P22bn, 50.4-km tollroad that will connect west of Silang East Interchange of the Cavite-Laguna Expressway and will pass through Silang, and end at Nasugbu, Batangas. Our forecasts and valuation do not include CTBEX

PAL tells US court it will return to profit in 2022

With the removal of US$2.1bn in debts and a new business plan, Philippine Airlines (PAL) expects to book an operating income of US$220mn next year and US$364mn in 2023 according to PAL bankruptcy documents filed in the US. Buona Sorte Holding Inc., the airline’s largest unsecured creditor, had agreed to waive the recovery of funding advances worth as much as US$358mn under the restructuring plan. The plan involves the return of 21 narrow and wide-body planes to lessors and lenders to bring its fleet size to 70 aircraft. It also cited the likely exit from unprofitable markets such as the US East Coast (New York) and London Heathrow. It also planned to boost US West Coast flights, expand international and codeshare partnerships and target growth markets such as China. PAL would also strengthen its Ninoy Aquino International Airport hub, maintain routes from Cebu and eventually expand in Clark International Airport.

 

Ayala Corp sets up logistics vehicle

Ayala Corp disclosed that the SEC had approved the articles of incorporation of AC Logistics Holdings Corp, a new unit that will serve as its vehicle for investments in the logistics sector. The Ayala Group entered the logistics industry three years ago through its last mile delivery platform Entrego. Entrego has established a nationwide footprint and linked up with major e-commerce players in the country.

Newcastle now at US$177/MT

Thermal coal prices up +32% since end-June.  Newcastle thermal coal prices are still surprisingly strong, with spot now 32% higher than in end-June at US$177/MT.  This is tracking higher than our average forecast of US$115/MT for FY21 and US$100/MT.  The reason seems to be the same: tight supply (China cutting coal mine production and banning high-grade coal imports from Australia, supply disruptions in Australia, South Africa, and Colombia, and most recently, high global gas prices) amidst a rebound in demand. With the incoming winter season, the situation seems unlikely to abate anytime soon. This can present a +29% upside risk on SCC’s FY21E; FCF yield could now be as high as 23% (vs. base case div yield of 12%).  Assuming spot remains at US$177/MT for the rest of 2021, we estimate Newcastle prices could average US$134/MT this year, which could translate to a 29% uplift in our FY21 profit forecast for Semirara (SCC PM).   We estimate SCC’s free cash flow yield could also rise to as much as 23%, higher than our base case div yield forecast of 12%.  Buy.

 
 

Macro News

Gov’t defers GCQ for Metro Manila until after September 15

The Inter-Agency Task Force has postponed the implementation of the general community quarantine (GCQ) with alert levels in Metro Manila. Metro Manila will remain under modified enhanced community quarantine (MECQ) until Sep 15 or until the pilot GCQ with alert level system is enforced, whichever comes first. Indoor and outdoor or al fresco dining and personal care services like beauty salons, beauty parlors, and nail spas will remain closed in Metro Manila at least for another week.

Inflation jumps to 4.9% in August on higher food inflation

The country’s inflation rate accelerated to 4.9% in August from 4.0% the previous month. This is the fastest inflation recorded since January 2019 and higher than consensus forecast (+4.4%). This print fell at the high end of the BSP’s monthly forecast range of 4.1%-4.9% due to higher price increases of food (+6.5%), specifically of meat (+16%), fish (+12%), and vegetables (+16%) on weather-related supply disruptions. For non-food items, inflation of fuel (+8%) and operation of transport equipment (+14%) also accelerated due to higher power generation/transmission prices. Meanwhile, core inflation (excluding selected food and energy items) picked up to 3.3% in August from 2.9% in July. The YT-August average inflation rate at 4.4%, above the BSP’s target of 4.1% for FY21E. The latest CPI outturn is consistent with the BSP’s assessment that inflation could settle close to the high end of the target range (2-4%) in the near term before decelerating back to within the target by year end. In 2022-2023, inflation is expected to decline towards the midpoint of the target.

Labor force participation drops in July

Preliminary data from the Philippine Statistics Office showed the country’s total labor force dropped by 4mn MoM in July. The labor force participation rate dropped to 59.8% in July, lowest since October 2020 (58.7%) and below pre-pandemic level of 61.7%. The number of employed dropped by 3.4mn MoM, largely coming from agri and services sectors. Those who are looking for work (i.e. unemployed) also fell by 690k MoM, bringing the unemployment rate lower at 6.9%.

Pace of vaccinations inches up

As of September 5, the 7-day average vaccinations figure was 391,367/day coming from 387,176/day the week before. Around 25mn doses are expected to arrive in September including about 1mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 52.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 35.8mn doses (as of September 5). The government aims to vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.

PH lifts travel ban on 10 countries

The IATF lifted the travel ban imposed on 10 countries beginning today (September 6). Travel bans lifted for international travelers coming from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, UAE, Oman, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. The travel bans have been in place in early May. IATF also approved to shorten facility quarantine to 7 days (from 10 days previously) for arrivals from “green list” countries. IATF also introduced a “yellow list” of countries; quarantine requirement of 14 days (10 in a quarantine facility + 4 at home).

Philippines registers all-time high 22,415 new COVID-19 cases

The Philippines logged 22,415 new COVID-19 cases on Monday, bringing the country’s total caseload to 2,103,331. The reported number is the higher than the previous all-time high set last August 30. The Department of Health case bulletin indicated that the new cases brought the active tally to 159,633, the highest since the April 13 tally of 165,534. The Philippines conducted 73,264 tests, with a positivity rate of 28.8%.

BSP has injected P2.3trn into Philippine financial system

BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that since the beginning of the COVID pandemic, the Central Bank has injected P2.3trn in liquidity into the financial system:

200 basis point interest rate cuts

Lowering of reserve requirement ratio

P540bn provisional advance to the national government

Purchase of government securities in the secondary market

P20bn advance dividend remittance

Trade deficit balloons to US$3.3bn in July

Preliminary trade data showed the trade deficit at US$3.291bn in July. This is slightly smaller than the revised June 2021 trade deficit of US$3.397bn (previously US$2.8bn deficit). Imports continue to improve (+24% YoY), with higher imports of fuel, consumer goods, and capital goods. Meanwhile, exports rose 13% YoY. The YT-July trade deficit now at US$21.3bn, wider than the US$13.5bn deficit in the same period last year.

Government pushing for reforms on real property taxes

The Department of Finance seeks to standardize the methodology in computing for real property valuation (for the purpose of computing real property taxes). This is the third package of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Program. The House of Representatives already passed its version of the bill but the Senate has yet to act on this proposal. The assessed values used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. For reference, pre-COVID real property taxes accounted for c. 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover
