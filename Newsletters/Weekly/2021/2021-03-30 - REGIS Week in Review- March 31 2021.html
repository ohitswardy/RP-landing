Philippine Strategy - Lockdown – déjà vu all over again
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Enhanced Community Quarantine for Easter week (at least)

New Covid-19 cases spiked to record levels, near 10,000/day. The “bubble” of Metro Manila and four neighboring provinces has been placed under ECQ, the strictest level of lockdown, from 29 March to 4 April. Movement within, to and from the “bubble” is severely restricted, and a curfew from 6pm to 5am has been imposed. Retailers will be shut, except for groceries, pharmacies, hardware stores, and restaurants (though only for take-away and delivery). Given weak mobility, even pre[1]ECQ, we cut our 2021 GDP growth forecast to 5.5% (was 7.0%).

Covid cases surge, near 10,000 a day; some hospitals at or near capacity

Confirmed Covid-19 cases haven risen sharply in recent weeks. From under 2,000/day in Jan/Feb, the case count spiked to as high as 9,838 on Saturday 27 March (see Figure 1). It is unclear whether the surge was due to new variants, or due to “lockdown fatigue”, as the country approached the anniversary of continuous quarantines on 16 March. After a months-long lull, hospitals in Metro Manila are seeing Covid bed occupancy rates rise to alarming levels (Figure 2).

ECQ declared in “NCR-Plus” tightening rules in the “bubble”

On 22 March, in response to the surge, the government restricted movement into and out of Metro Manila (aka National Capital Region or NCR) and the provinces of Bulacan, Rizal, Cavite, and Laguna, or “NCR-Plus”. Citizens could move inside the “bubble”, though a curfew from 10pm to 5am was effected. On 29 March, NCR[1]Plus was placed on ECQ, the strictest lockdown classification. Most businesses will be shut, will operate a skeleton workforce. Malls and retailers will be closed except for essential services such as groceries, pharmacies, and hardware. Restaurants will only be allowed to offer take-away and delivery services. And the curfew has been extended, now running from 6pm to 5am. The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) will review whether to extend the ECQ beyond 04 April.

Reducing GDP forecasts, downside risk to earnings

In February, we reported that the slump in mobility data after the year-end holidays posed downside risks the economy and for company earnings. More recent data shows mobility slowing even further, even before the “NCR-Plus” bubble (Figures 3&4). Bottom-up data from Jan/Feb – toll road traffic (Figure 5), electricity usage (Figure 6), car sales (-10% in 2M21), loan growth (-2.7% YoY in January), etc – all pointed to continued soft demand. We reduce our 2021E GDP growth forecasts to 5.5% (7.0% previously), with 1Q21 GDP likely to show another negative print. Companies whose businesses are more resilient would include retailers Wilcon (WLCON PM), and Puregold (PGOLD PM); beneficiaries of trading down, including Century Food (CNPF PM), and to a lesser degree URC (URC PM). Telco demand should stay firm, though the 3rd player does create an overhang. ICTSI (ICT PM) remains a play on global recovery. The case for Meralco (MER PM) hinges less on demand than on a favorable regulatory turnout.

Puregold - Margins jump in 4Q20 as trade spending recovers; sari-sari stores return
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY20 net profit outperformance driven by 4Q margin jump

PGOLD’s FY20 net profit grew 19% YoY to P8.1bn, ahead of Regis expectations (P6.9bn). The outperformance was largely driven by better-than-expected margins, as net sales (+9% YoY to P168.6bn) was broadly in line. In 4Q20 alone, net profit jumped 35% YoY to P3bn as operating margin expanded 170bps YoY.

K-shaped recovery evident in 4Q20 SSSG trends

Net sales in 4Q20 rose 7% YoY, but the disparate top line trends between PGOLD’s two brands illustrated the K-shaped recovery of the sector. SSS for the core Puregold brand (70% of sales) declined 3% in 4Q20 as lower-income households were likely hit harder by the Covid lockdowns. On the other hand, the higher-end S&R brand saw a rebound in SSSG (+14%) as increased basket sizes more than compensated for lower foot traffic.

Trade spending, favorable forex drives margin expansion

Consolidated gross margin expanded c.400bps QoQ and YoY. Supplier support, which had lagged in previous quarters, rebounded during the quarter, pushing the core Puregold brand’s GPM up 250bps YoY /380bps QoQ. Meanwhile, favorable forex continued to support S&R’s gross margin, which was largely stable on a QoQ basis.

Sari-sari stores to return in 2021 but situation still fluid

Puregold’s wholesale business under the Tindahan ni Aling Puring membership program (c.25% of sales) was one of the main drags to its 2020 performance, with the number of active accounts dropping by as much as 60% during the year. The company has been pro-active in reactivating these accounts through targeted promotions and improving service levels, and management reported that active accounts have now returned to pre-pandemic levels and continue to see MoM improvements. Management did not provide 2021 top line guidance, citing that the situation remains fluid at this point. They are, however, aiming to maintain margins on a FY basis and continue to be pro-active in managing suppliers’ spending commitments throughout the year.

Cebu Air - Challenges remain but recent capital exercise provides sufficient “runway”
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY20 net loss wider than expected

CEB’s FY20 headline net loss came in at P22.2bn, implying 4Q20 net loss was wider QoQ. EBITDAR loss stood at P4.44bn in FY20, a larger loss than expected due to (1) higher ARO (asset retirement obligations) provisions relating to its leased aircraft and (2) aircraft/traffic servicing costs.

Sequential demand recovery but Metro Manila lockdown will derail momentum

4Q20 pax volumes were still 95% below pre-pandemic levels. Fares rose QoQ and YoY largely on the back of higher ancillary revenues. Cargo was the bright spot, rising 39% QoQ and 29% YoY. After the government unified rules for non[1]essential domestic travel last Feb 26, CEB saw bookings spike dramatically, particularly for trunk routes (e.g. Manila to Cebu/Davao). This momentum, however, was quickly reversed after the government re-imposed tighter mobility restrictions for Metro Manila last March 22.

Net capex will be minimal

CEB ended 2020 with a fleet of 74 aircraft (from 75 in end-2019), of which 60% is currently being utilized. Management expects to end-2021 with the same fleet size, after taking delivery of 8 aircraft and returning 8 to lessors. It plans to spend US$600mn on capex this year but net of proceeds from sale and leaseback, capex will only be a fraction (<10%) of the headline capex guidance. This is even smaller than our FY21E net capex of P15bn (c. US$300mn).

Convertible bond issue uncertain CEB raised P28.5bn this month from the convertible pref rights offer and the P16bn syndicated loan. Its average monthly cash burn rate is now at P1.5bn. Management believes these two fund raising exercises alone provide sufficient buffer to weather the worst of the pandemic for another 18 months. Negotiations for the last tranche of its capital raising exercise (i.e. US$250mn convertible bond) are still ongoing. In case it is unable to reach an agreement with the third party investor, management will pursue other options, including a senior notes offering. The company intends to complete the last tranche on or before May. PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - CREATE to lift NPAT by as much as 7%; but it’s a bit more complicated…
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Corporate tax rate drops 500bps to 25%, but…

The CREATE Act was signed into law on 26 March, cutting corporate income tax rates from 30% to 25%. Simplistically, this would lift NPAT forecasts 7%, but differences in actual tax rates bring the average closer to 5%. However, the treatment of taxes for 2H20, as well as deferred tax assets/liabilities could create a lot of “noise” for 2021E profits. The law also rationalizes tax incentives, but the negative impact on exporters and BPOs seem manageable. One disappointment was a veto of higher VAT exemption thresholds for low-cost housing.

Tax cuts for everyone!

The Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act cuts the statutory corporate income tax rate from 30% to 25% for large companies, and to 20% for SMEs, retroactive to July 2020. On the face of it, this would boost corporate profits for listcos by about 7%. However, only a handful of companies we cover pay close to 30% today, with some paying close to nil, and with some in negative tax positions. A rough estimate is the tax cut would add about 5% to our 2021/2022E aggregate profit forecasts, though the range varies greatly. (A summary of our estimated impact from CREATE is on Figure 1, page 4.)

Some questions remain; Deferred taxes could mean one-off gains/losses

It is unclear how the law will treat the tax cut for 2H20, as many firms have already submitted FY20 audited financials. We also don’t yet know how deferred tax (DT) assets/liabilities will be handled. Companies with large DT Assets – such as the banks, and Meralco – could see one-time write-downs, while those with DT Liabilities – most property firms – could see write-backs (Figure 1). If taken through the P&L, these could swing our 2021E profit by as much as 20% in either direction. The law’s implementing rules and regulations (IRR) should be out soon.

Property – good news for office, but bad news for residential

Early iterations of CREATE had exporters and Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) companies worried about losing tax incentives. Under the signed law however, CREATE gives a long 10-year “sunset period”, removing the risk of an exodus particularly for office tenants. To the downside, the President vetoed a provision that would have raised the VAT exemption for residential sales from the current P2.5mn to P4.2mn. We had already assumed the increase in our estimates, and thus could see some downside in our pre-sales forecasts.

A nice boost ahead of the economic turnaround

All in all, the CREATE law is a definite plus for Philippines, Inc. Apart from the lower tax rate, it does simplify tax administration, and rationalizes tax incentives given to different industries and companies. As (and when) the economy recovers from the Covid pandemic, the profit boost will hopefully see the market bounce back earlier too.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
GT Capital - Lot sales lift 4Q20 profit; Toyota margins decline QoQ
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY20 core profit of P7.44bn (-53% YoY)

GTCAP reported FY20 core profit of P7.44bn, down 53% YoY. The results were disappointing across its key operating segments, despite a surprise P2bn gain from lot sales booked in 4Q20. Profits from the property and banking units recovered strongly QoQ. However, margins for the auto business surprisingly contracted QoQ and diluted the sequential improvements in vehicle unit sales.

Auto margins dip in 4Q; Safeguard duty is an overhang

Toyota Motor Phils. (TMP) reported a net income of P3.3bn in FY20, implying 4Q20 profit rose 11% QoQ to P1.2bn. Wholesale volumes bounced 52% QoQ but GPM surprisingly dropped further to 12.8%, despite a favorable PHP:USD exchange rate. Unit sales in 2M21 were strong but the implementation of the safeguard duty in March may have curtailed this momentum (see Pay now, get a refund later (fingers crossed), 23 February 2021). The good news is that the Board of Investments finally completed its audit on Toyota’s fixed investment support under the CARS program, which could pave the way for Toyota to recognize tax credits of as much as P2bn this year.

Massive provisions but capital buffers sufficient

Metrobank reported FY20 profit of only P13.8bn, after expensing massive provisions. (see Metrobank: Dividend surprise (8% yield) despite earnings miss, 18 February 2021). Despite the profit shortfall, the bank is arguably in a position to emerge stronger from the pandemic than its peers.

Sequential improvements in reservation sales; Lot sales to a JV partner

Its property unit continued its recovery in 4Q both on booked profits and reservation sales. The sales momentum was carried to 2M21, with sales at P1.3- 1.4bn per month, despite the absence of launches. GTCAP also signed a joint development agreement with a strategic anchor investor last October to develop 100 hectares of its 700-hectare landbank in Cavite. In line with the JV, GTCAP sold 39 hectares and booked a P2bn gain.

 
 

Corporate News

Pilipinas Shell core income recovers to P400mn for FY20

Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) improved its core income to P400mn in FY20, which implies 4Q20 income jumped to P1.1bn (from P200mn in 3Q20). The headline net loss for the FY20 amounted to -P16.2bn, inclusive of inventory losses (P4.8bn) and refinery one-offs (P11.8bn). The margin improvement came on back of opex/capex savings as a result of the refinery shutdown. Additionally, the marketing volumes were down 22%YoY vs -17%YoY in September.

Suez Canal blockage to impact Philippine trade

The blockage in the Suez Canal will have an impact on global logistics, especially for goods originating from areas using that route according to Philippine Ports Authority general manager Jay Santiago. This may somehow impact the shipment of goods because of the delays caused by the incident. International Container Terminal Services Inc. (ICTSI) is closely monitoring the incident, according to CFO Rafael Consing. Many of these ships will need to divert their vessels to sail around the southern tip of Africa, which could further add five to seven days to their journey. A prolonged blockage would result in higher shipping charges, as well as raw material and product shortages at a time of unprecedented demand and congestion at some of the world’s major ports.

Filinvest Land FY20 net profit falls 41%; office vacancy likely rose in 4Q20

Filinvest Land’s 2020 net profit fell 41% to P3.73bn, in line with REGIS (behind consensus). 4Q20 earnings dropped 43% to P1.1bn. 4Q20 office revenue fell 8% QoQ (-16% YoY), implying that office vacancy increased further in 4Q. Office vacancy rose from 9% in 2019 to 13% in 3Q20 and as of 3Q20, POGOs occupied c. 15% of the office portfolio. The company said that residential reservation sales saw a healthy rebound.

Razon sued by GGAM in NY federal court

Global Gaming Asset Management, LLC (GGAM) sued Enrique Razon in a federal court in New York for illegally terminating the deal to operate Solaire. The lawsuit seeks to confirm a $297mn arbitration award by a Singapore court. GGAM added that Razon managed to evade paying the arbitration award by “forming a vast network of shell companies in the US to conceal his assets”. Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) maintains that the arbitration award can only be enforced by a Philippine court. The Final Award issued in September 2019 ordered Bloomberry to pay GGAM about US$100mn in damages due to the termination of the management services agreement. It also ordered BLOOM to buy GGAM’s 921mn shares in BLOOM for P10.17bn@P11/sh (or allow the sale of the shares and make up the difference between market sale and P10.17bn).

 
 

Macro News

Gov’t to distribute cash aid for areas under ECQ

Budget Secretary Wendel Avisado said 22.9mn poor Filipinos in Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Rizal and Laguna are set to get the aid. The group represents 80% of low-income population in the area. The assistance will be lower than what the government gave under Bayanihan I. Assistance will be P1,000 worth of in-kind aid per person, but no more than P4,000 for each family (previously, households were given P5k-8k). The funds will be sourced from the P23bn unreleased funds under Bayanihan II.

Philippines receives another 1mn doses of COVID vaccine

The Philippines received another 1mn doses of COVID-19 vaccine of Sinovac. The country has now received a total of 2.5mn doses. As of March 27, 656,000 doses have been administered.

National Government debt rises to P10.4tn in February

The National Government’s total outstanding debt rose to a new record of P10.405tn in February. Around 71% are domestic borrowings while 29% was sourced externally. This is equivalent to 52% of GDP, and still below the internationally acceptable threshold of 60% of GDP. The government expects the ratio of debt-to-GDP to rise to 57% by end-2021, up from 54.5% in 2020.

President Duterte vetoes some provisions of CREATE

President Duterte signed the Tax Package Two (also called CREATE), though he vetoed certain provisions. The corporate income tax rate will drop. For small business – from 30% to 20% (retroactively from July 2020). For other companies – from 30% to 25% (retroactively from July 2020). It will also streamline the government’s fiscal incentives system to make it time-bound. New export projects (including BPOs) – can avail of incentives for 14-17 years (from perpetuity currently). The existing export projects (including existing BPO operations) – can continue to avail of existing incentives for another 10 years.

Metro Manila and adjacent provinces on stricter lockdown

Metro Manila, Bulacan, Rizal, Cavite, and Laguna have been placed on Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ), the tightest lockdown from March 29 until April 4. There will also be a curfew from 6PM to 5AM. Only one member per household is allowed to leave the home to procure essential goods. Only essential industries (such as medical services) are allowed to operate at full capacity. Most businesses are only allowed to have a skeleton workforce on site (or no workforce at all). Meanwhile, groceries, pharmacies, and hardware stores are allowed to operate in malls while restaurants can only serve delivery and take-out. Presidential Spokesperson Harry Roque said that the government will provide financial aid. Public transport allowed to operate at 50% of capacity 24 hours a day, following one-seat-apart rule.

BOP posts US$2.02bn deficit in February; end-Feb GIR revised down to US$105bn

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$2.02bn in February 2021. This is a reversal from the US$839mn surplus recorded in Feb 2020 and a larger BOP deficit than in January 2021 (US$752mn). This brings YT-Feb BOP to a deficit of US$2.771bn (vs. deficit of US$516mn in the same period last year). The final gross international reserves level for end-February stood at US$105.16bn, lower than the preliminary figure of US$109.1bn. This is lower than in end-January 2021 of US$108.67bn. The buffer is equivalent to 12 months’ worth of imports.

Spending picks up in February, Budget deficit balloons to P116bn

The government’s budget deficit ballooned to P116bn in February largely due to a rise in spending. Revenues were up 6% YoY, of which tax revenues grew 7%; Non-tax revenues dropped due to lower collections from PAGCOR and investment income. Meanwhile, expenditures rose YoY and MoM due to a P45bn release of equity to government financial institutions for various COVID-19 support programs under Bayanihan II. This brings 2M21 budget deficit to P130bn.
