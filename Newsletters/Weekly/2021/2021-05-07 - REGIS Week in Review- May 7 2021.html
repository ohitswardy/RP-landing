REGIS REPORT

07 May 2021

The Philippines closed -1.8% lower this week with continued dwindling volumes and persistent outflows.  While daily new COVID cases are down from peak levels, the pace of vaccinations kept recovery concerns firmly in place. Only 2.1m doses have been administered since March. The PSEi was down every single day this week.

Foreigners were sellers for a 12th straight week with another US$53m exiting this week. Outflows were heavy despite muted average weekly turnover of US$89m (-12% WoW, lowest this year.

There was very little for the market to digest, apart from 1Q results which were mixed. Losses were broad-based, as only 9 of the 30 index members managed a gain.

The top index gainer for the month was ICTSI (+4.2% WoW). The stock was propelled higher by its 1Q profit beat. Volume growth accelerated to +8% and yields jumped +7and EBITDA margin reached an all-time high of 61%, with EBITDA expanding 25%. 

There were signs of 1Q recovery from the consumer sector. URC (-3.6 % WoW) saw total revenues mildly higher despite last year’s pantry stocking, and DNL (+5.1% WoW) saw food volumes (+2%) trending much better than prior quarter. Profits from the food segment led, rising 37%.   San Miguel FB meanwhile (+1.4% WoW) reported 29% growth in gin volumes, while showing good recoveries across its segments. 

Century Pacific (+5.0% WoW) showed resilience as the branded business grew 4% despite the high base from pantry stocking last year. Its non-branded OEM business bounced back sharply (+45% YoY) as the global food sector recovers.

Metrobank (-1.0% WoW) beat estimates and continued the 1Q trend for banks as 1Q profit growth was driven by sharply lower provisions.  NPLS were benign.  Loans were down 19%, however.

On the other hand, property disappointed.  SM Prime’s (-1.9% WoW) malls were impacted by the re-imposition of harsher lockdowns as were Ayala Land’s (+0.2% WoW) malls (and hotels).  Residential segments fared better nevertheless.

Semirara (+2.6% WoW)  delivered a beat, on the back of a rebound in coal prices and power sales recovery.

For the telcos, it was still  all about broadband. PLDT’s (-0.6% WoW)   service revenue was up 7% YoY led by a robust 31% growth in broadband.  For Globe (-0.1 % WoW), service revenue was up +3% as expected, and that was lifted by a 27% expansion in broadband revenue.

Meantime, conglo earning were mixed.  SM (-1.9% WoW) beat expectations thanks to lower provisioning at BDO.  MPI (-2.2% WoW) meanwhile was dragged by with demand weakness evident in the power, water, and tollroad units.

Wilcon Depot - Margin surprise in 1Q21; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q21 net profit (+84% YoY) ahead on strong margins

WLCON’s net profit in 1Q21 jumped 84% YoY to P604mn, tracking ahead of our estimates. The outperformance was due to 1) strong gross margins (over +200bps QoQ and YoY), and 2) a lower effective tax rate due to the CREATE law. Net sales grew 19% YoY on the back of 9.8% SSSG. Compared to 1Q19, management estimates SSSG was flattish. While sales are trending mildly behind estimates, we expect sales to catch up in the coming quarters as home construction activity resumes. We raise our 21E-22E NPAT estimates by 3-4% and our TP by 5% to P22/sh, largely to account for the lower effective tax rate. Maintaining Buy.

Net sales jumps 19%; SSSG recovers to 9.8%, new stores ramp up

SSSG in 1Q21 recovered to +9.8% as transaction count grew 13% YoY. This was partly due to base effects, as WLCON had to temporarily shut its Luzon stores during the hard lockdown in Mar 2020. Net sales (+19%) was also driven by the continued improvement of its new stores, particularly in Visayas and Mindanao.

Margins jump as provincial stores ramp up

Gross margin jumped 240bps YoY to 36.3%, the highest level in the company’s history. This was despite the contribution of higher margin in-house brands coming down to 49.5% from 50.9% in 1Q20. Management attributed the margin expansion to reduced sales from institutional buyers and small project sales, which generate lower margins vs end user customers. On top of this, EBIT margin expanded by another 200bps YoY due to opex control and operating leverage as its newer stores ramped up.

Gross margins to normalize as construction activity resumes

Management remained optimistic on top line growth given the continued store expansion (9 Depots in 2021), despite the new round of lockdown restrictions in the Greater Manila Area. Management noted that sales slowed down in early-Apr but had improved towards the end of the month. Margins are likewise expected to normalize as construction activity resumes and institutional buyers return.

Universal Robina - 1Q21: market share gains defensive amidst input cost inflation
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q21 net profit jumps 51% on non-operating items; EBIT (+9%) in line

URC reported 1Q21 net profit of P3bn, up 51% YoY, broadly in line with estimates. The big jump was mostly driven by non-operating items (narrower forex losses, lower effective tax rate). Revenues (+3% YoY) were mildly behind FY21 estimates as the branded consumer food (BCF) segment slightly underperformed. While EBIT margin (12.5%) is trending ahead of our FY21 forecast, management highlighted potential near term pressure from the spike in key input costs.

PH BCF gaining market share amidst weak consumption

Sales of the Phils BCF segment declined 4% YoY, attributed to a high base and continued weakness in overall consumption. By category, performance was dragged by coffee, RTD tea, and biscuits, which all posted YoY declines during the quarter. Meanwhile, snack foods and noodles were flattish. However, URC continued to gain market share in nearly all categories – where industry sales declined 20-30% YoY on average – a testament to the strength of URC’s brands.

 

International BCF recovery continues

IInt’l BCF sales rose 10% YoY in 1Q21 as key markets (e.g. Vietnam) continued to recover, even as Oceania was flattish (pantry stocking in 2020). While coming from a low base in 1Q20, int’l BCF sales are at similar levels vs 1Q19. The top line improvement led to a 190bps YoY improvement in EBIT margin.

Input cost pressure intensifies; price increases signaled

Management intends to implement price increases beginning 2Q21 (+2-5%) across its portfolio given the uptick in key input cost (palm oil, wheat, packaging cost, etc). While the company has covered majority of its requirements (70-80%) for the year, contract prices for the latter part of 2021 have trended higher vs 1H21. Management believes that the price increases should be enough to pass on input cost inflation without having an adverse impact on volumes.

Notwithstanding the potential near term pressure on margins, we believe URC’s growth will be defensive given market share gains in the Philippines. We expect these gains to be sustainable as consumers continue to gravitate towards familiar and trusted brands amidst a challenged consumer environment.

Cemex Philippines - 1Q21: Fuel hedges kept margins stable; volume guidance raised
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q21 profit up 131% YoY to P205mn - Ahead

CHP reported 1Q21 profit of P205mn, up 131% YoY and surpassing our full-year expectation. EBITDA was up 15% QoQ to P1.03bn (31% of our full-year estimate), as volumes improved by 14% on better economic activity and fewer weather[1]related disruptions. The impact of rising energy costs was limited by fortunately-timed hedges and, as a consequence, we believe the earnings recovery can be sustained until at least 3Q21. The possible imposition of anti[1]dumping duties on cement imports, meanwhile, could provide further significant near-term upside.

Demand improves; volume growth guidance raised to +5-7%

CHP’s sales volumes fell by 4% YoY in 1Q21 and were very near pre-pandemic levels. Despite residential demand moderating in April due to recent lockdowns, management nonetheless hiked its volume growth guidance to +5-7% from +4- 6% previously. We expect CHP’s volumes to recover by +7%.

ASPs flat QoQ; Anti-dumping duty a big boost

ASPs were flat QoQ (down 4% YoY), which is within expectations and in line with the trends we’ve seen on import landed costs. The government recently initiated an anti-dumping review on cement imports from Vietnam, which could result in a duty ranging between US$1.66/MT to US$6.54/MT based on the preliminary investigation. We estimate an anti-dumping duty could add 2-9% to the estimated import price of cement and will allow domestic players to raise prices. Every 1% hike in ASP could lift EBITDA by 6%, and this will have a disproportionately large (%) impact on profit.

Fuel hedges stabilize 1Q EBITDA margin

EBITDA margin improved YoY and was roughly flattish QoQ at 19.7%. Even though Newcastle thermal coal and crude oil prices jumped 32%/35% QoQ, respectively, CHP’s fuel costs and distribution costs per MT only inched up by 2%/1%. Management said it has hedged its fuel (i.e. thermal coal and diesel) requirements for 1Q21-3Q21 at “very low prices”.

BDO Unibank - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Positive outlook, but prepared to be wrong

BDO’s CEO Nestor Tan, at our Roundtable Series on 27 April, chose an optimistic view of the world. That is, he believes the current pandemic is temporary, and most companies will bounce back as the economy returns to normal by 2022. This view informs the bank’s strategies on growth, NPLs, restructuring, provisioning, IT, branching, M&As, and capital. Far from being a Pollyanna, though, Mr Tan warned the audience to “expect me to be wrong.” But they’re prepared for that, too.

Comfortable with NPLs, provisioning, and restructuring strategy

Mr Tan’s positive outlook is evident in the way the bank has dealt with asset quality. He sees the bank’s Restructured Loans – about 3% formally RL, and another 3% in process – as a positive, allowing good companies breathing space while revenues are currently depressed. He added that the bank’s better credit processes allowed it to book 1Q21 NPLs of 2.81%, compared to the industry’s 4.16% (as of Feb). BDO met with loan clients to build a “bottom-up” picture of the book, then overlayed a sectoral analysis of default risk. The bank is comfortable with its 107% NPL cover at 1Q21. Mr Tan believes deeper “scarring” in smaller commercial clients and consumers make these at risk of new NPL formation.

Loan demand, NIMs to recover in 2022

While he remains positive on the economy, Mr Tan only expects loan growth to recover in 2022, as many companies raised funds in 2H20 for liquidity. Near term, though, this liquidity could keep NIMs soft in 2021, as banks compete aggressively for the best corporate credits. BDO’s 83% CASA ratio, and extremely low deposit cost (0.27% in 4Q20) give it significant advantage. Investing heavily in IT, but branch network still key to client acquisition BDO was caught flat-footed as online/mobile transactions surged 3-4x from pre[1]Covid levels. The bank’s IT upgrade program started in 2017, and it has since completed the infrastructure and cyber-security platforms. It has launched the QR-based “BDO Pay”, and will roll out new products and apps later this year. However, the bank still holds that branches are crucial to client acquisition, as 30% of municipalities still do not have a formal banking presence, and almost 70% of Filipinos remain un-banked. Mr Tan believes BDO should be a “fast follower” in tech, but admits they could be a “faster follower”.

Focusing on growth (not costs), organically and via M&A

Mr. Tan justified BDO’s cost ratios, as it continues to invest in growth areas such as provincial mid-market, MSME loans (via BDO Network), insurance, and wealth management. He likened it to a tech startup, where the focus should be on growing top-line. BDO will consider the Citibank consumer portfolio, but is wary of “overlaps and stickiness” of the client base. With loan demand soft, BDO’s 1Q21 solo CET-1 ratio rose to 13.1%, from 12.2% a year ago.

SM Prime - 1Q21: another quarter of record reservation sales; mall outlook worsens
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Weak mall business weighs on profits

1Q21 net profit fell 21% to P6.5bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, as Philippine mall revenue dropped 48%. Although reservation sales remain robust, the near-term outlook for its biggest segment (shopping malls within the country) has deteriorated.

Philippine mall revenue improves QoQ but outlook worsens

Due to the improvement in foot traffic in late 4Q20, SMPH decided to reimpose, effective January, minimum rents for tenants who pay based on percentage of sales. The minimum rents, even at 10% of pre-COVID levels, coupled with more store re-openings, helped boost Philippine mall revenue by 10% QoQ. However, due to the rise in COVID-19 cases, Metro Manila and four neighboring provinces have been on hard lockdown since end-March, which would mean a substantial deterioration in 2Q21 performance. In addition, the slow pace of vaccinations will likely lengthen the road to economic (and mall) recovery.

China mall revenue back to pre-COVID levels

Revenue from China malls jumped 53% YoY to RMB199mn, comparable to the RMB190mn-RMB200mn average in 2019. Thanks to the containment of the pandemic, China mall operations have returned to normal after just one year.

Another quarter of record reservation sales

Reservation sales grew 31% YoY to P32.4bn, the second consecutive quarter of record presales. The company said that 70% of 1Q21 presales came from newly launched projects, which were mostly located in Makati and the Manila Bay area. This is a contrast to other developers, who are saying projects outside Metro Manila are lifting sales. Mr. Jomari Banzon, the head of SM Prime’s residential division, will give more details of the performance during SM Investments’ briefing on May 5 (at 2PM).

1Q21 profit +78%

We estimate FB’s net income to equity holders in 1Q21 was P6.5bn (+78%YoY, +38%QoQ), derived from the reported profits of its subsidiaries. This is tracking ahead of our FY21 estimates, with the outperformance driven by food and liquor segments. Beer volumes actually remained quite soft, but was offset by cost savings on advertising and marketing. The lower tax rates stemming from CREATE also had a positive impact of about P1.1bn (including about P600mn retroactive impact from 2H20).

Beer volumes still down 11% but cost-cuts help bottom line

Profits at the beer business improved 45%YoY despite volumes remaining soft. It sold 55.5mn cases (-11%YoY, -13%QoQ) in the quarter. As in previous quarters, sales outlets were still mostly limited to off-premise channels, and weakened towards the tail end of March as Covid cases rose. Net margin hit 19.2% in 1Q21 coming from 18% in 4Q20 (and low teens normally), as it made substantial cuts on A&P, marketing and promotional events. They continue to market aggressively through online channels, with management noting that high-profile digital campaigns are much cheaper to implement than traditional campaigns.

Gin volumes +29% in 1Q21 to record quarterly volume

The downtrading effect sustained Ginebra’s sales volumes, growing 29%YoY to hit 11.5mn cases – its highest quarterly volume on record. Income jumped 120%YoY and 90%QoQ to P1.042bn. Robust demand allowed GSMI to raise prices by 5% to pass on the excise tax increase, while SMB has held off on any price adjustments.

Food grows 2x as higher chicken/pork prices drive recovery

Protein continued to lift the food segment, boosted by higher broiler and pork prices. As we cited previously, FB is among the few beneficiaries of the current food inflation, deriving about a quarter of sales from poultry and hogs. Profit jumped 107%YoY to P3.387bn, and we estimate that protein contributed about half this figure. Growth of prepared and packaged goods, however, slowed to 6% (from double digit in previous quarters). Management cited price hikes to pass on higher raw material costs, which probably dented demand.

CNPF’s 1Q21 net profit grew 24% YoY to P1.3bn, tracking well ahead of Regis and consensus estimates. Branded business: new products drive growth despite high base in 2020

Demand for CNPF’s core branded products remained resilient amidst limited consumer mobility and heightened price consciousness of consumers. Non-branded OEM segment recovers

CNPF’s non-branded OEM business recovered sharply, with revenues jumping 45% YoY. This was attributed to 1) new capacity for tuna OEM and 2) the recovery of the food service sector globally. Favorable skipjack tuna prices mitigate inflation of other input cost

Notwithstanding the profit boost from the reduced tax rate, the guidance implies that margins could normalize (i.e. dip) sequentially as 1) the non-branded business continues to outpace the growth of branded, 2) Pacific Meats is consolidated beginning April 2021, and 3) the impact of the higher cost of other inputs ex-skipjack tuna (e.g. tuna, beef, skimmed milk powder, packaging) is felt.

Core income P7.1bn (+11%) in 1Q, likely better than consensus

Headline profits for 1Q21 amounted to P5.8bn, with PLDT claiming “Telco Core Income” was P7.50bn (i.e., excluding Voyager losses). We estimate overall core income (including losses at Voyager) would be just over P7.1bn, up 11% YoY. This would put 1Q on track to be match our FY21 estimate of P30bn, but would appear to be trending better than consensus (P27.9bn). EBITDA hit P23bn (+7% YoY), a record for a single quarter.

Broadband leads revenue expansion

Wireless service revenue was up 7% YoY, a reasonably good expansion. However, it was the broadband business that led the way, with revenue there rising +31%. PLDT added a net 111k subscribers to its wired broadband business in 1Q21, taking it to 2.39m subscribers. On a gross basis (before churn), PLDT connected ~240k new wired broadband subs, with 120k for March alone.

Growth in wireless data usage stalling?

Mobile data traffic for 1Q21 was 777PB, barely up from 4Q20. Management attributed the growth deceleration to more people consuming their data indoors via Wi-Fi with the pandemic lockdowns dragging on and with increased availability of broadband at home. This “migration” of usage was the same reason cited by Globe for mobile revenue weakening beginning 2Q last year.

Preemptive strike

Management acknowledged that its most recent promotion called Double Giga was aimed as a preemptive move against the third operator. Double Giga gives subscribers data allocations for as low as P6 (US$0.12) per GB. This compares with the new operator’s promo providing 25GB for P199 (=P8 per GB), Admittedly, though, there are nuances which make them not strictly comparable.

1Q21 profit up 61% YoY to US$73mn – Ahead of estimates

1Q21 profit of US$73mn (+61% YoY) is trending above expectations. Volume growth was healthy (+8%) and yields surprised to the upside. As a result, underlying operations were robust, with EBITDA up 13% QoQ and 25% YoY to US$265mn, ahead of estimates. Profits were further buoyed by lower losses from associates. The company started operating its newest port in Onne, Nigeria this month and has a very healthy pipeline of potential acquisitions.

Volumes jump 8% on last year’s high base; Yields improve QoQ, YoY

Riding on the strong rebound in global trade, ICT’s volume growth accelerated slightly to 8% YoY even though 1Q20 was still a high base of comparison. Sustained improvements were evident across its portfolio, with volume growth at the port in Manila already in “high-single digits”, reversing the declines seen in the past three quarters. Management expects the strong recovery to continue at least until 1Q22, as some ports are still in the early stages of recovery. Yield per TEU improved substantially, driven up by volume improvements in high yielding ports (DRC, Melbourne, Rio) and the new handling fee for empty containers in Manila. These gains can be maintained throughout the year.

EBITDA margin breaches 60%

EBITDA margin reached an all-time high of 61%. With volumes likely to improve further in succeeding periods, we believe the margin recovery can also be sustained. Meanwhile, equitized losses shrank further, and management attributed this to (a) substantial cost reductions at Manila North Harbor and (b) volume recovery at the port of Colombia. Lastly, ICT’s effective tax rate jumped to 25% in 1Q21 from 19% a year ago, given (a) the termination of MICT’s tax holiday and (b) a one-time US$8mn reversal of tax benefit due to CREATE.

Nigeria port capacity initially at 150k TEUs

The company took over operations of a multipurpose port in Onne, Nigeria this week, after investing US15mn to achieve a capacity of 150k TEU (can be expanded to 350k TEUs depending on demand). Terms are still being negotiated with the port authority. It is also eyeing a port in Southeast Asia with a throughput of 700k TEUs. It is also actively looking for other opportunities in the Philippines, Brazil and Nigeria.

1Q21 core profit down 25% to P2.57bn – Behind expectations

Stripping these out and fair value adjustments, core income fell by 25% YoY to P2.57bn, trailing our full-year forecast due to weaker-than-expected volumes at the power (see Meralco 1Q21: Demand weakness lingers, 26 April 2021), water, and tollroad units. It is worth noting, however, that liquidity at the parent is sufficient to fund ongoing projects, without having to borrow. Management is also focusing on curtailing losses at existing non-performing businesses (e.g. logistics).

Tollroad: Traffic volumes up 4% QoQ; Upside risk from tariff hikes

Despite flattish revenues and EBITDA, profits from the tollroad unit contracted by 15% YoY due to non cash costs. Traffic volumes at its PH tollroads improved 4% QoQ but the most recent lockdown has dampened the recovery. Management is optimistic it can raise tariffs for NLEX in June/July, after completing the Subic freeport expansion. They are also expecting tariff hikes for Cavitex and SCTEX in 3Q/4Q. The tariff hikes are not in our numbers.

Water: Billed volumes drop 4% YoY

Contributions from water fell 38% YoY, dragged down by weak demand at Maynilad and large start-up costs at MetroPac Water. Billed volumes fell 4% due to weak demand from commercial customers and cooler weather. Leakage or non-revenue water (NRW) deteriorated by 6.5ppts YoY to 31.6%, as repairs and pipe replacements have been pushed back by mobility restrictions. Management is close to reaching an agreement with the government on the revised water contract, save for some minor, non-financial issues.

Losses from the rail unit were lower-than-expected due to lower non-cash costs. Underlying operations, however, remain soft due to government capacity limitations. The company is halfway done with the 11-km extension and interest costs are also piling up. Meanwhile, management has started to wind down contracts for its logistics unit, as it plans to exit this operation by year-end. MPI has invested P6bn for this business since 2016.

SM Prime: Near-term mall outlook dims with Covid-19 situation

Earnings of SM Prime dropped 21% to P6.5bn in 1Q21, as revenue from Philippine malls halved. With the rise in Covid-19 cases and the accompanying imposition of harsher lockdown restrictions in late March, the outlook for its most important segment remains challenging near-term. One silver lining is the surprising strength in residential property sales, which jumped 31%, fueled by overseas Filipinos (also a surprise).

SM Retail: Revenues down but earnings up on rent concessions

Revenues at the group’s Retail business declined 14% YoY to P70bn but profits held up, rising 36% YoY to P1.6bn. The company cited a number of cost savings initiatives, but we reckon rent concessions from its biggest landlord (i.e. its mall operator sister company) were the biggest reason. Its Food Group (groceries) saw revenue drop 7% but income still rose 27%. The group’s small format operation, Alfamart, added 50 stores through April this year (vs. 1,012 as of end 2020). 1Q revenue for Alfamart was up a rather disappointing 12% considering its store footprint grew over 25%.

Ayala Land - Notable rebound in 1Q21 presales but operations will be hampered
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Net profit falls 36% despite non-recurring gains

Ayala Land’s 1Q21 net profit fell 36% to P2.8bn, substantially behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Excluding a one-off gain (from the sale of QualiMed to Ayala Corp) and a non-recurring loss (from the write-down of deferred tax assets), we estimate that earnings would have dropped nearly 50% to P2.3bn. Operating revenue fell 19% as mall and hotel sales plummeted c. 60%. The rebound in reservation sales was noteworthy. However, the hard lockdown since end-March and the slow pace of vaccinations will likely curtail the company’s two largest segments (residential and mall) going forward.

High-end provincial projects boost reservation sales

Reservation sales saw a meaningful bounce, rising 35% QoQ and 15% YoY to P28.5bn, 78% of the 2019 average. Sales to foreigners fell 38% but this was more than offset by the 31% jump in sales to local Filipinos (see table on right hand side). Demand was particularly strong for (high-end) Ayala Land Premier and Alveo residential lots in provinces adjacent to Metro Manila.

Malls: strong QoQ recovery but outlook worsens

Mall revenue grew 16% QoQ despite the drop in foot traffic from 40% of pre[1]COVID levels to just 35%. This was due to more store re-openings as well as the reimposition of minimum rents (only 10% of pre-COVID levels) on some tenants beginning January. However, since the lockdown in end-March, Ayala has once again waived minimum rents (for percentage-of-sales based tenants).

Office vacancy stays flat but POGOs still at risk

Ayala’s office vacancy was flat QoQ at 12%, while the industry saw a deterioration to 11%, from 9% in 4Q20. However, we believe that POGO tenants, which still occupy 5% of Ayala’s portfolio (8% for the industry), are still at risk of closing

Metropolitan Bank - Provision drop fuels 1Q21 beat, as NPLs flat at 2.4%; loans contract 19% YoY
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Still in bunker mode, but ready for the (eventual) rebound

Metrobank’s NPAT of P7.8bn in 1Q21 far exceeded our expectations, and those of the Street. PPOP was in line; the “beat” was driven by provisions, which halved YoY, as NPL ratio was flat QoQ at 2.40%. Management maintains its peak NPL guidance of 5% this year, though, given uncertainties brought about by the lockdowns. NIMs have likely bottomed (-22bps QoQ), and fee incomes are back to pre-Covid levels. However, loans were down 19% YoY in 1Q21, potentially making the climb back to “normal” that much steeper.

Loans contract 19%, driving down NIMs; trading, fees lift PPOP

MBT’s loans shrank 19% YoY (Fig 1), with drops across all segments. The 18% fall in commercial lending was driven by working capital loans which plunged 39% YoY. The consumer book was down 16%, led by autos at -28%. The rapid change in asset mix away from loans drove NIMs down 22bps QoQ and 42bps YoY (Figs 2 and 3). Continued CASA growth softened the blow somewhat (Fig 4). To the upside, fee incomes are back to pre-Covid levels, with trust fees +20% YoY. Trading gains also helped bring PPOP in line with our expectations.

Provisions halve, as NPLs stay flat at 2.40%; management still wary

As with its peers, MBT’s NPL ratio was flat, even inching down 1bp to 2.40% (Fig 5). The trends diverged between segments, though. Commercial NPLs jumped to 1.9% from 1.4% at end-2020, while consumer NPL ratio dropped to 4.0% from 5.6%. Management held to its guidance of peak NPL of 5% this year, citing indications of new NPL formation after the new lockdowns in April. NPL cover though stayed high at 166%, up slightly from 163% at end-2020 (Fig 6).

Capital still high – solo CET-1 at 17.9% even after special div in 1Q

Despite the bank’s surprise special dividend in March, its CET-1 ratio was still 17.85% at the parent (solo) level (was 18.62% at end-2020). Management will monitor its capital needs against potential loan growth or an uptick in NPLs.

 
 

Corporate News

Globe matches PLDT’s latest prepaid mobile offering, doubles data allocations

Globe introduced Go+ packages, effectively doubling data allocations for some prepaid packages. The new Go+ packages range from P99 to as much as P400. The lowest package (Go+99) will cost P99 and last for 7 days. The subscriber gets 8GB of open access data and another 8GB for use with specific apps (in theory, =16GB). This matches PLDT’s (Smart) latest offering at the same price (“Double Giga 99”). This also implies that the mobile data yield is P6.2 per GB (16  GB for P99). The latest revenue yields (4Q20) for PLDT and Globe are ~P24/GB. While we don’t expect yields to plunge by 70%, we believe these latest initiatives could exert downward pressure on yields in the coming months.

Ayala Land sells 44mn AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 44mn AREIT shares at P32/share. This was to comply with minimum public ownership requirements. Ayala Land previously announced that it would hold a share-for-asset swap with AREIT. ALI will infuse 205,000sqm of leasable assets for 483mn new AREIT shares (at P32/share). After the sale, ALI’s ownership in AREIT (post share swap) would drop from 69% to 66%. Thus, AREIT’s public ownership would meet the minimum requirement of 33%. ALI is trading at 18.9X FY21E P/E and 36% discount to NAV.

Semirara expects profit to recover this year, but unlikely to return to pre-pandemic levels

Semirara Mining and Power Corp. expects some profit recovery this year, but given operational headwinds, it is not likely to return to its pre-pandemic profit according to Chairman and CEO Isidro Consunji. They cited better coal and power demand and prices but noted that the unscheduled outage of Unit 2 will be a headwind. The company expects its coal business to perform better on the back of recovering consumption and prices. However, it expects mixed results from two of its power subsidiaries. SLPGC is set to stage a strong profit recovery because of higher plant availability and better spot market prices. SCPC is likely to deliver disappointing results because of the forced outage of its Unit 2 beginning December 3 last year; Unit 2 is expected to be operational by 3Q21. Semirara’s FY20 core profit plunged to P3.1bn from P9.4bn in 2019.

Globe completes more than a fourth of 2021 FTTH line target

Globe Telecom said it is on track to complete its on million fiber to the home lines this year after laying 274k lines as of 1Q21. It has completed the expansion of at least 4,210 sites and has installed 318 new cell towers in 1Q21. Globe previously said that all of Globe’s remaining copper lines will be upgraded to fiber.

DNL 1Q21 jumped 35%YoY to P695mn

DNL reported a 1Q21 income of P695 (+35%YoY, +9%QoQ), tracking ahead of our FY21 estimate (P2.529bn). Income from the food segment jumped 37% to P219mn. Meanwhile, volumes from high-margin food was flat while commodity food volumes grew 5%. Oleochemicals, specialty plastics and aerosols grew 17-35%, with volumes up between 8-19%. Overall GPM was slightly down at 16.7% vs 17.5% in 1Q20. GPM for the high-margin segments dipped slightly (23.4% vs 24.0% last year) while commodity margins were largely the same (6.5%).

Maynilad agrees to terms similar to Manila Water deal

West zone concessionaire, Maynilad, has agreed to renegotiated concession terms similar to those accepted by Manila Water (MWC PM) according to Justice Secretary Menardo I. Guevarra. The parties are in agreement on the proposed new concession agreement with Maynilad, except for: Maynilad’s business plan; JICA (Japan International Cooperation Agency) loans; Public listing of shares. Details of the final agreement will be revealed once talks are finalized. Maynilad is a 53%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and 25%-owned by DMCI (DMC PM). Maynilad accounts for 14% of MPI’s NAV and 12% of DMCI’s NAV. MPI is trading at a 25% discount to its market NAV of P5.43/sh. DMCI is trading at a 24% discount to its market NAV of P7.1/sh.

PAL informs lessors of Chapter 11 filing deadline

Philippine Airlines (PAL) has informed its lessors of a plan to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the USA by the end of May according to Cirium. PAL will file before the end of May as long as they have the requisite support from creditors.

SM Prime 1Q21 reservation sales grow 31% on overseas sales

Jose Mari Banzon, head of SM Prime’s residential division, said that overseas sales comprised 81% of 1Q21 reservation sales. This implies that overseas sales jumped 116% while domestic sales halved. Other countries are recovering better than the Philippines and buyers generally have higher paying occupations. Around 77% of SM Prime’s in-house sales agents and third party brokers are based abroad. Mr. Banzon believes that the P32.4bn of reservation sales in 1Q21 could be sustained for the rest of the year. Going forward, SM Prime will increase launches of affordable housing in provincial areas. The average selling price there is below P3mn, from the average of P6mn in 1Q21. SM Prime CFO John Ong said that 1Q21 mall foot traffic was 50% of pre-COVID levels (similar to 4Q20) but fell to 33% in April. SMPH is currently trading at 30.8X FY21E P/E and 11% discount to NAV.

FB consolidated net income up 66%

In a press release, San Miguel Food and Beverage said its consolidated net income grew 66% to P9.68bn in 1Q21. Consolidated EBITDA increased 35% to P15.6bn while operating income jumped 45%YoY to P12.6bn. Against our consolidated estimates (FY21E consolidated income P27.1bn), the headline numbers appear to be tracking ahead. San Miguel Brewery’s net income increased 45% to P5.5bn – in line (FY21E P21.6bn). Ginebra’s income jumped 120% to P1.04bn on the back a 29% increase in volumes. Additionally, San Miguel Foods’ income more than doubled to P3.39bn – well ahead (FY21E P5.1bn). There was an 11% revenue growth of the protein segment (chicken, pork) due to better demand-supply dynamics. Meanwhile, prepared and packaged food segment (canned goods, frozen meats) revenues grew 6%

 
 

Macro News

Customs collection in April exceeds target

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P51.28bn (+50% YoY) in import duties and other taxes in April. This was 6% lower than the P54.7bn collected in March but exceeded the P49.2bn target. Collections included additional revenues from the tax expenditure fund (P121mn) and post-clearance audit group (P13.65mn). 11 of the country’s 17 ports exceeded target collections, including the ports of Manila. This brings 4M21 collections to P200.46bn (+11.5% YoY), in line with its year-end goal of P617bn (+15% YoY).

First batch of Sputnik V doses arrives

15,000 doses of Sputnik V arrived on May1. The Philippines is negotiating to buy as much as 20mn Sputnik V doses from Gamaleya. The country has received a total 4mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 1.8mn doses. The government expect 7mn to 8mn doses to arrive in May. It previously said 140mn doses would arrive by end-2021 to inoculate 70mn Filipinos.

Inflation steady at 4.5% in April

Headline inflation settled at 4.5% in April, broadly the same as in March (4.5%) but faster than the inflation print a year ago (2.2%). This is lower than street forecast (4.7%) and within the 4.2%-5.0% target range of the BSP. Food inflation decelerated, despite another increase in meat inflation. Even with the reduction in pork tariffs, prices of pork still rose, as well as prices of chicken, and beef also increased. These were offset by decelerating inflation for rice, fruits, and vegetables. Additionally, transport costs continued to surge, as tricycle fares surged by +48% YoY, followed by fuel costs (+32% YoY) and jeepney fares (+6% YoY). This brings YT-April average inflation to 4.5%, above the government’s target range of 2-4% for the year.

COVID vaccine target may need to be adjusted

“Vaccine Czar” Carlito Galvez Jr. said that the government’s target to inoculate 70mn Filipinos by the end of the year would have to be tweaked. He now recommends prioritizing areas “that have economic and social importance”. He is now targeting 70% herd immunity in those areas (mostly urban centers). Now targeting 50mn to 70mn vaccine recipients - focused on those areas. The country has received 4.04mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 2.07mn doses.
