REGIS REPORT

08 January 2021

The Phils gained 2.1% WoW to end the first trading week of the year at 7289.88.  The PSEi was down as much as 1.3% YTD as of Wednesday but recovered towards the close. 

Gains were widespread, with 21 of the 20 index names advancing for the week. Large cap cyclicals helped push up the index – with SM Prime (+4.9% WoW) and BPI (+6.9% WoW) contributing 64 index points to the market’s overall 160-point gain. Other cyclicals likewise did well with Ayala Land (+1.6% WoW), BDO (+2.1% WoW) Robinsons Land (+5.2% WoW) and Megaworld (+5.1% WoW) also gaining this week.  

Foreign activity was a mere 23% of turnover for the week. Top traded names DITO (+20.4% WoW), AC Energy (-3% WoW), MerryMart (+24.4% WoW), Apollo Global (+57.0% WoW), Premiere Horizon Alliance (+50.0% WoW) were dominated by local retail and accounted for 38% of total market activity for the week.

GT Capital (-1.0% WoW) declined. The stock was down as much as 10% WTD as of Wednesday before recovering towards the close. The Dept of Trade and Industry (DTI) intends to impose safeguard duties on large classes of imported automobiles in a supposed bid to protect “local manufacturers”. The DTI will slap a duty of P75k and P110k on imported CBUs of light passenger cars (PCs) and light commercial vehicles (CVs), respectively.  The safeguard duty will affect mostly low-to mid-end vehicles from most countries (vehicles from China and India are exempted). Also, the safeguard duty will not cover  the more expensive “luxury” vehicles (“luxury” threshold of US$25k FOB price for PCs and US$28k FOB price for CVs).

For GT Capital’s Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), Regis estimates models comprising half of its unit sales will likely be affected by the safeguard duty and the impact on retail prices is likely to be between 7% and 12% for those affected.  Assuming overall volumes for Toyota decline by 5%, this would translate to a 6% drop in TMP’s profit and 2% decline for GT Capital.

Meanwhile, it seems the POGO industry has been given a reprieve.  Voting 13-1, the Supreme Court granted a restraining order against the implementation of the 5% tax on gross bets imposed by the Bayanihan 2 Act.  If enforced, the 5% tax on gross bets was seen to make many online gaming operations unprofitable.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
LT Group - Only just the beginning
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Dividend thesis taking shape

We believe LTG’s shares have the legs to continue their rally after the company demonstrated its intent to upstream cash to shareholders equitably through increased dividends. We believe LTG’s dividend story is just beginning to pan out amidst an environment of record-low rates where investors are hungry for yield.

Generous dividends even on conservative payout assumptions

LTG paid out P8.765bn in dividends over 2020, the highest payout in its listed history, equivalent to P0.81/share and 7.2% of its price at the start of last year. While our base case assumes dividends of P8.9bn this year (40% payout, P0.82/share, 6.4% yield), we think tobacco unit PMFTC alone is capable of upstreaming up to P18bn/year to LTG (vs previous est P13bn). This implies that LTG could comfortably build up its cash reserves for possible pandemic-related contingencies (e.g. bank recapitalization) even while it pays an attractive yield.

PMFTC more resilient than expected; raising profit forecasts on higher margins

We raise our profit forecast for FY20/FY21 to account for higher price hikes and gross profit margins. Our revised gross margin per pack are higher by 21% than 2020 and 33% than our previous forecasts, all else equal. While we trimmed our FY21 volume assumptions to -14% (from -3%), we estimate that profits will jump to P37.4bn (12%YoY) in FY21E, and to P38.5bn (+3%YoY) in FY22E.

Risks from capital call?

PNB had among the highest NPL ratios and lowest NPL provision coverage reported in 3Q. Despite the drop in 3Q provisions, we trimmed our profit estimates further by 20-49% to reflect higher provisioning down the road. We do not think that a recapitalization event should affect the dividend outlook. LTG was already in a net cash position as of 9M20.

Raising NAV, TP; risks

We raise our NAV estimate by 19% to P32/share on higher valuations for tobacco and liquor subsidiaries. We also account for higher market prices of PNB and Victorias Milling. Our target price is 19% higher at P16/share, using the same target discount of 50% - vs historical average of 38% - to factor in ESG trends and related party concerns. Other risks include the return of illicit trade, tax changes and credit risk.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Property - Supreme Court issues restraining order against higher POGO taxes
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A reprieve for POGOs

The Supreme Court (SC) issued a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) on the collection of franchise taxes from Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs). The Bayanihan II Act, which became law in September 2020, had a provision which imposed franchise taxes on POGOs, equivalent to 5% of bets/turnover/drop.

Why did the SC issue a TRO?

14 foreign-based POGOs argued in their petition that:

  • The provision on POGO franchise taxes is a “rider”. The constitution prohibits provisions that are unrelated to the main subject of the law.
  • The imposition of taxes on offshore income in unconstitutional.
  • Bayanihan II unfairly uses bets as the “tax base” even though that amount includes money that will eventually be paid out to gamblers.

Will this only be temporary?

While only a TRO has been issued so far, we believe the provision on POGO taxes will likely be repealed eventually. Local media reported that majority of the SC justices appear inclined to consider the provision to be a “rider”, making it unconstitutional. Congress can write a new law specifically about POGO taxes. We suspect that Congress would now be more willing to charge franchise taxes based on gross gaming revenue (GGR), which is only a fraction of bets. A 5% tax on bets is too prohibitive since, in most cases, the taxes would exceed GGR.

Will this stop POGO closures?

As of 2019, POGOs occupied 1.3m sqm (11%) of Manila office stock and employed over 300,000 Chinese nationals. However, they vacated about 250,000sqm of Manila office space in 2020 and we believe that over 80% of POGO operations would have eventually shut down if the 5% tax on bets were enforced. While franchise taxes based on GGR would be a major reprieve, there are still some regulatory headwinds (such as travel bans), including from the Chinese government. In addition, our channel checks suggest that some POGO operations are leaving Metro Manila office spaces in favor of provincial areas, putting downward pressure on Metro Manila property prices/rents.

Filinvest Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime more exposed

Filinvest Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime are more exposed to the POGO sector. POGOs occupy about 15% of FLI’s office space and 10% of MEG’s office portfolio. The Manila Bay area also comprised c. 25% of MEG’s 2019 residential launches. Meanwhile, although POGOs have little direct impact on SMPH’s earnings, c. 45% of NAV is comprised of land in the Manila Bay area, the POGOs’ preferred location.

 
 

Corp News

San Miguel Aerocity franchise lapses into law

San Miguel Aerocity was granted the franchise for the P740bn Bulacan airport after the legislation (RA 11506) lapsed into law last month. President Duterte failed to sign the bill within 30 days after receipt. RA 11506 provides tax exemptions during a decade-long maximum construction period. San Miguel can continue to enjoy tax exemptions after construction or until the Bureau of Internal Revenue decides that it has fully recovered its investment costs. The 50-year franchise also entitles the company to a 12% revenue share each year until investment recovery has been determined.

Aboitiz Power on track to double capacity by 2030

Aboitiz Power said it remains on track to more than double capacity by 2030. It will continue to pursue a major shift in energy mix – to an almost even thermal to clean energy ratio. Coal comprises two thirds of AP’s capacity, followed by hydro (13%), oil (11%), geothermal (9%) and solar (1%). In April, the company announced its 10-year plan to double capacity from 4,432MW to 9,300MW.

Regulator directs Meralco to refund P1.4bn but also collect P2.4bn in under-recoveries

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) directed Meralco to refund P1.4bn in over-recoveries. These were relating to transmission charges, lifeline subsidies and senior citizen subsidies. The refunds are to be done over three months. However, Meralco will also be allowed to recover P2.38bn in under-recoveries relating to generation charges and these higher charges will be collected over 24 months. Both items have already been accounted for (provisioned for) in Meralco’s income statement (P&L) and would have no direct impact on its reported income. The impact will only be on cash flow.

Converge raises speeds for entry-level packages

Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) increased the speeds for its entry level package (P1,500/month) from up to 25Mbps to 35Mbps. Converge recently raised the speeds for its higher end plans (by up to 1/3rd) but this action is more significant. A significant majority of its subscribers are on the P1,500 plan. We believe PLDT will likely follow suit and subsequently Globe as well (see table for latest comparison of broadband plans).

Automakers see 30-50% rebound in 2021 car sales

Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines Inc. (CAMPI) president Rommel Gutierrez sees industry car sales rebounding by 30-50% in 2021. The projection includes sales sold by members of the Association of Vehicle Importers and Distributors (AVID). For 2020, CAMPI projects industry vehicles sales to fall by 41% YoY to 240k units. As of 10M20, industry sales dropped 44% YoY to 188k units. We anticipate industry vehicle sales to rise by 30% YoY to 309k units in FY21E, with Toyota Motor Phils (TMP) sales also rising by 30% YoY to 117k units. Every 10% increase in TMP’s vehicle sales forecast could raise GTCAP’s FY21E profit by 5%. Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 10% discount to its market NAV of P652/sh.

ABS-CBN could be granted new franchise

Deputy Speaker Vilma Santos-Recto said she will file a new bill that would grant ABS-CBN a fresh 25-year legislative franchise. Additionally, Senate President Vicente Sotto III has filed Senate Bill 1967, which also seeks to grant ABS-CBN a new franchise to construct, install, operate, and maintain television and radio broadcasting stations for 25 years.

SC junks writ of kalikasan case vs SMC Aerocity

The Supreme Court has junked the petition seeking the issuance of a writ of kalikasan against San Miguel’s Bulacan airport (a.k.a. “Aerocity Airport Project”). SC declared that the petition filed by Oceana and several fisherfolk in Bulacan was “not sufficient in form and substance,” thus warranting its immediate dismissal.

Car firms warn tariffs to make crisis recovery harder

The Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines (CAMPI) warned that the provisional safeguard duty imposed on imported cars will trigger further sales losses and layoffs according to CAMPI President Rommel Gutierrez. The Dept. of Trade and Industry recently imposed provisional duties of P70k for passenger cars and P110k for light commercial vehicles. The duties will be collected in a form of a cash bond, which will be put in escrow for a maximum of 200 days or until the Tariff Commission submits its recommendation to the DTI. The cash bond could be returned to importers if the Tariff Commission recommends a dismissal. We believe this measure could disincentivize distributors from importing cars during the 200-day review period. We also believe it is highly likely that this measure could be revoked. Toyota Motor Phils. (TMP) assembles only two models in the country: Vios (20% of 2019 sales) and Innova (13%). Given this, the measure will negatively affect TMP less than its peers, who import mostly completely built-up units (CBUs). TMP accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV.

Meralco unit earmarks P159mn for additional 20% stake in solar firm

The power generation arm of Meralco (MER PM) said that it spent P158.95mn to acquire a 20% stake from Sunseap Philippines Solar Holdings Pte Ltd. in PowerSource First Bulacan Solar Inc (PFBSI). With the transaction, MGreen now holds a controlling 60% stake in PFBSI. Powersource Energy Holdings Corp holds 36% while Sunseap has the remaining 4%. PFBSI started developing a 50MW solar farm on 72-ha land in December 2019. The P4.25bn project is expected to start commercial operations in 1Q21.

Retailers see partial sales recovery in 2021

The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) sees retail sales in 2021 to still be 20-30% below 2019 pre-pandemic levels, but 10% higher vs 2020. For non-essential retail, we estimate 2021 sales to be 6-7% higher vs 2019 and 2020. The group cited that malls continued to see weak foot traffic, cannibalization from e-commerce, limits on public transportation. Certain categories – such as food, medicine, and fitness equipment – were strong, while clothing and apparel remained weak. Many retailers have offered deep discounts to help spur sales.

Dito technical audit begins

Dito Telecommunity gave notice to the government that it is ready for its technical audit. Dito was required to deliver population coverage of 37% and wireless speeds of 27Mbps by Jul 2020 which was delayed to Jan 7, 2021. Its press release claims it has 1,600 cell sites. KPMG affiliate was hired to do the audit, which will be conducted over 30 days.

AREIT acquires 98,000sqm of industrial land

AREIT acquired four land parcels totaling 98,179sqm in Laguna Technopark from Technopark Land Inc, a 79%-owned subsidiary of Ayala Corp. for P1.1bn acquisition cost (VAT inclusive). This is equivalent to 6.28% 2021E EBITDA yield. Integrated Micro-Electronics (IMI PM) has been leasing the land since 2005 and will continue to do so until end-2027 and has a 5% escalation rate. The headquarters of IMI is located on the property. The acquisition brings the total value of AREIT’s properties to P37bn. AREIT is targeting dividends of P1.62/share ~ 5.6% dividend yield.

 
 

Macro News

Inflation rises to 3.5% in December

Inflation hit a 22-month high of 3.5% in December. This is above street expectations (3.2%) but within the BSP’s forecast range of 2.9%-3.7%. Food inflation (vegetables, fruits, meats) accelerated in December (vs Nov ‘20) due to supply constraints from typhoons and the African Swine Fever. Meanwhile, rice inflation turned positive for the first time since March 2019 (rice tariffication). Additionally, transport (8.3%) and restaurants (2.5%) inflation also spiked. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno is not concerned about the accelerating inflation, saying the uptick in December is transitory, only brought about by the impact of typhoons on food supply and prices. There’s still more room to reduce banks’ reserve requirement ratio.

T-bond rates down on strong demand

The Bureau of Treasury fully awarded P30bn worth of 5-year securities vs a total of tenders amounting to P98.67bn. The 5-year T-bond fetched a rate of 2.536%, down 36.4bps and vs the secondary market rate at 2.495%. For 2021, the government is aiming to borrow P3.03tn from domestic and external sources and for January alone, the Bureau of Treasury is targeting to raise P140bn from domestic borrowings.

Supreme Court issues TRO on higher POGO taxes

The Supreme Court (SC) issued a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) on the collection of franchise taxes from POGOs. The Supreme Court Justices voted 13-1. The Bayanihan II Act, which became law in September 2020, had a provision which imposed franchise taxes on POGOs equivalent to 5% of bets/turnover/drop. In their petition, 14 foreign-based POGOs argued that the provision on higher POGO taxes is illegal because it imposes higher taxes even if Bayanihan II does not create or refer to the imposition of any new tax. Also, the imposition of taxes on offshore income is not constitutional. Bayanihan II unfairly uses bets as the “tax base” even though that amount includes money that will eventually be paid out as winnings to gamblers. The POGOs vacated c. 250,000sqm of Metro Manila office space in 2020 and we estimate they continue to occupy about 1.05m sqm (9% of office stock) in Metro Manila. The POGOs occupy 15% of FLI’s office portfolio and 10% of MEG’s office space. Land bank in the Manila Bay area, the preferred location for POGOs, accounts for c. 45% of SM Prime’s NAV.

BIR, BOC surpass 2020 collection targets

Based on preliminary data, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and the Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P1.899tn (-12.7% YoY) and P539.66bn (-14.38% YoY), respectively. Both agencies exceeded collection targets of P1.69tn and P506.15bn, respectively and the figures will be finalized by Jan 10. This suggests that for December alone, the BIR collected P112bn (-31% YoY, -42% MoM) while the BOC collected P47.3bn (-9.3% YoY, +8.2% MoM). For 2021, the BIR is targeting to collect P2.081tn +10% YoY.

BSP lends another P540bn to gov’t for pandemic response

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) approved another zero-interest bridge loan worth P540bn loan to the national government last Dec 28. This is the third time the BSP threw a credit line to the national government. The BSP loaned P300bn in March (repaid in September) and another P540bn in October 2020 (repaid in December). Under the law, the central bank can lend the government a maximum of about P850bn this year. The 10-year BVAL is at 2.956%, lower than in March (5%).

BSP may keep rates on hold in 1H21

BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that interest rates will likely remain on hold at least through 1H21. The current monetary stance will persist for the next two quarters or even longer. There may be “some play” to further cut banks’ reserve requirement ratio from 12%, but not likely soon.

More on vehicle safeguard duty

Low-to mid-end cars most affected. The DTI is imposing a safeguard duty of P75k and P110k on imported CBUs of light passenger cars (PCs) and light commercial vehicles (CVs), respectively. The safeguard duty will affect mostly low-to mid-end vehicles from most countries (e.g. Toyota WIGO, Toyota Hilux, Nissan Navara). But it will not cover the more expensive (“luxury”) vehicles (e.g. Toyota Land Cruiser, BMW), as well as CVs from China (e.g. MG RX5) and PCs/CVs from India (e.g. Hyundai Accent). It is uncertain at this stage if vehicles that are at or close to the “luxury” threshold of US$25k FOB price for PCs and US$28k FOB price for CVs. (e.g. Toyota Fortuner) will be covered.
