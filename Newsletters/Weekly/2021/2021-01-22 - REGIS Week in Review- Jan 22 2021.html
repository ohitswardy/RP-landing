REGIS REPORT

22 January 2021

The Phils had a rough week, dropping 2.7% to end at 7045.83. The PSEi posted losses every day of the week – pushed lower by accelerated foreign selling.

Average turnover this week reached US$264m.  However, retail-dominated stocks were heavily traded again and accounted for 7 of the top 10  most active names this week. These seven names were over 40% of market turnover.

Net foreign outflows this week breached US$100m, wider than the US$4.5m in net outflows last week. Top foreign outflows were seen in Ayala Corp (-US$10.3m), SM Prime (-US$10.2m), and Ayala Land (-US$8.2m).

The drop was broad-based, with 27 of the 30 index members closing down for the week. Megaworld (-6.3% WoW), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-6.3% WoW) and Alliance Global (-5.4% WoW) were the top index decliners for the week. 

Banks Metrobank (-5.0% WoW) and  BPI (-5.1% WoW) also fell.   BPI  announced it was merging with its wholly-owned thrift bank subsidiary BPI Family Bank in a move that would streamline costs and improve its “solo” capital ratios.

PLDT (-5.0% WoW), URC (-4.7% WoW) dropped as well.

Property names Robinsons Land (-4.3% WoW) and SM Prime (-4.6% WoW) also ended down. SM Prime says foot traffic at its malls reached 50-60% of pre-pandemic levels in December. This would be an improvement from the 35-40% in September.

Meanwhile,  AREIT (+4.8% WoW) signed an agreement to acquire the 30th office and mall complex from Ayala Land  () for P5.1bn.  The complex has 75,000 sqm of leasable area. The deal does not involve the land on which the complex sits. The transfer is being done at P5.1bn, which equates to 6.1% EBITDA yield.

Speaking of REITs, Filinvest Land (+0.9% WoW) announced it was planning to turn one of its subsidiaries into a REIT company. Given its asset portfolio, the expected NAV enhancement from the move to inject these office assets into a REIT should be modest (~5%).

Meanwhile, First Philippine Holdings (-3.5% WoW) is no longer planning to buy out all minorities in Lopez Holdings (+0.3% WoW). It is instead capping its tender offer so that it would not cause the public float to fall below the minimum threshold. A free float of below 10% would have triggered a delisting. The change was apparently due to complaints that the threat of delisting brought undue pressure on minorities to participate. The tender offer price of P3.85 for each LPZ share is at ~40% discount to its underlying NAV (based on Regis estimates), the biggest part of which is accounted for  by its holdings of FPH.

Capping the offer and thus maintaining a separate listing for LPZ would negate one of the key benefits of the tender offer -- the removal of one listed holdco layer and the accompanying NAV discount it creates.

Philippine Retail - Consolidation amidst adversity
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Bigger slices of a reduced pie

Retail sales in the Philippines should see a bounce in 2021, though we believe the recovery to pre-pandemic levels could drag on until a vaccine or treatment is more widely available. Consumer spending is likely to remain soft, particularly for lower-income consumers, who have disproportionately borne much of the lockdown’s impact, and will continue cut back on spending amidst reduced incomes. The sector will remain challenged in 2021 and we believe it is likely to spur consolidation, which should be positive for retailers under our coverage – Puregold (PGOLD PM), Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM), and Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM), who are market leaders in their respective segments. We have Buy ratings on all the names we cover, but WLCON is our top pick.

Partial recovery in 2021 but challenges remain

Retailers should see a rebound in foot traffic in 2021 from the 2020 lows, but we do not expect industry sales to recover to pre-pandemic levels until late21/early-22. Even as lockdown restrictions are eased, we believe the erosion in household incomes will have a longer lasting impact on consumer spending. Amidst a challenging environment, we expect further consolidation within the sector as smaller independent (and informal) players are likely to fall by the wayside. This will be positive for the listed retailers under our coverage, as we expect them to take larger slice of a reduced pie.

K-shaped recovery across formats, income classes; WLCON is our top pick

The pace of recovery is likely to vary across income classes and thus also retail formats. Retailers that (1) cater to mid- to upper income consumer who are less affected by the economic impact of the pandemic, (2) have proportionately more stand-alone stores than mall-based stores will be more resilient over 2021. Among the retailers under our coverage, WLCON fits both criteria and is our top pick in the space.

Adjusting forecasts and target prices; recovery in consumption a key risk

We are adjusting our 2020-22E forecasts for the retailers under our coverage to account for a 4-12% reduction in sales (vs previous forecasts). Broadly, we are expecting average EPS CAGR of 30% for 2020-22E for the retailers under our coverage, mainly driven by improving profitability as store sales recover and margins normalize from the 2020 lows. Our DCF-based target prices are likewise adjusted based on our new forecasts, reduced cost of debt assumptions, higher terminal growth assumptions, and a roll over to 2021E. We are upgrading our ratings for RRHI and WLCON to Buy (from Hold), while we are maintaining our Buy rating on PGOLD. Key downside risk for the sector relates to a prolonged recovery in consumer spending.

Cebu Air - Fortune favors the brave; Upgrade to Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Riding the recovery

We upgrade Cebu Air to Buy (from Hold) on a revised price target of P77/sh (+54%). We expect CEB to emerge from the pandemic stronger, after a capital-raising exercise and cost reduction initiatives. We believe CEB offers attractive value, despite conservative estimates (FY22E pax volumes 23% below 2019 levels), and equity dilution from the planned convertibles. The stock is trading at 5.2x EV/EBITDA on our FY22E forecasts, a discount to peers (7.1x). Our target price – a potential 55% upside – would put the stock at 6.5x FY22 EBITDA (fully-diluted). Reports of financial difficulties at CEB’s rivals could allow it to gain share, which are not yet baked into our forecasts.

Revert to profitability in 2022

2021 will likely still be a terrible year for travel demand but investors should begin to look at 2022 as the impact of the pandemic fades. CEB’s latest monthly cash burn has been reduced to US$30-35mn in end-September (from US$40mn prepandemic) and more initiatives are likely to be executed this year to lower it further. We expect losses to peak in 1Q21 and begin to recede by late 2021, aided by cost reductions, election-related spending, and vaccine availability.

Pricing in convertible issue

Our revised estimates assume CEB will raise US$500mn (P24.3bn) in convertibles by 2Q21, tiding it over and putting it on stronger footing postpandemic even as its competitors become financially challenged. CEB’s fully diluted share count could almost double as a result (we assume a conversion price of P53/sh, a modest premium to recent market). Despite the expected dilution, we believe the shares offer attractive value on a one-year horizon.

Beneficiary of an industry fall out; Revised TP of P77/sh offers 55% return

We upgrade our TP to P77/sh, revising our valuation methodology to a target EV/EBITDA multiple of 6.5x (vs. Gordon growth previously), lower than CEB’s historical multiple of (7.5x) and its peers (7.1x). Our revised price target offers 55% total return and we could potential upside of as much as 200% if the recovery is even sharper than we anticipate (scenario analysts from page 12). Downside risks: higher jet kero prices, weak PHP:USD rate, lower demand.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Bank of the Philippine Islands - Absorbing BPI Family Bank to release capital, streamline business
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Merger with thrift bank subsidiary

BPI disclosed that it plans to merge its wholly-owned thrift bank BPI Family Savings Bank (BFSB) into the parent bank within the year. The move will likely have minimal impact on profits, but could boost parent CAR/CET-1 by 50-100bps. More importantly, the merger will remove extra administrative and regulatory layers that come with having separate legal entities. Ultimately it should improve the customer experience, and see benefits over the medium-term.

The dwindling benefits of the thrift bank license

Many large banks have or had thrift bank subsidiaries in the past. Banks typically used them as regulatory arbitrage, particularly for reserve requirements (RRR). For most of the last 20 years, there has been a 10-12ppt gap between the RRR for universal and commercial banks (UKBs) and for thrift banks (TBs). The current gap is 9ppts, with UKBs at 12% and TBs at 3% (see Figure 1). The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas has indicated it wants to bring down RRRs for UKBs to singledigits over the medium term. The arbitrage also made a lot more sense when interest rates were much higher than they are today (see Figure 2).

Minimal impact to P&L near term, but frees up capital

With short-term Treasury rates at c1-1.5%, we estimate the foregone arbitrage on BFSB’s deposit base of P241bn to be about P300mn, a little over 1% of BPI’s 2019 NPAT (see table at right hand column). We expect, though, that these will be offset by lower opex (eg marketing) and capex (eg IT, branches), and will also reduce bureaucracy (regulatory reporting, etc). The merger will also release capital, as Basel III imposes capital penalties for banks with a holding company structure. Over the years, the gap between BPI’s Solo (Parent) and Consolidated CET-1 ratios ranged from as little as 44bps to as much as 150bps (see Figure 3).

Better customer experience, cross-selling to bring medium-term benefits

Most importantly, absorbing BFSB will help streamline the business. Being separate legal entities makes it difficult to cross-sell products between BPI and BFSB. The merger will now allow BFSB depositors to access the full suite of BPI products (wealth management, insurance, etc). It will also allow for better crossselling of consumer loan products to BPI’s corporate clients (whose payroll accounts are with BPI).

 
 

Corp News

Globe's BPO unit seen to earn at least P500mn annually

In a statement, Globe Telecom said that a unit called ASTI Business Services Inc (ABSI) is looking to earn at least P500mn annually. It aims to provide enterprise services to telecommunications, financial technology, information technology, retail, health, logistics, automotive, banking, education, real estate and aviation companies. Globe cited the P11.25bn market for the local IT-BPO in the Philippines alone. Globe announced the official launch of ABSI on January 13.

Cemex PH expansion underway with installation of new kiln

Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) lifted the new rotary kiln into position yesterday, reaching a key milestone on its 1.5mn MT expansion. Once completed (target is Dec 2021), the new kiln will increase CHP’s kiln capacity (in cement terms) to 6.5mn MT from 5.0mn MT today. Cemex is targeting to spend US$235mn for the project (already in forecast). As of Sep 2020, CHP has spent ~US$134mn. CHP is trading at US$110 EV/MT vs. replacement cost of US$100-150 per MT.

ICTSI completes Manila port expansion

ICTSI has completed the berth expansion at the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT). The 150-meter extension of Berth 7 and the container yard extension have increased MICT’s capacity by 10% to 3.3mn TEUs. Our forecasts already assume the expansion. MICT handled 2.5mn TEUs in 2019; In 9M20, it handled 1.5mn TEUs (-17% YoY). MICT accounts for 25% of ICT’s group-wide volumes, 35% of revenues, and 50% of EBITDA. ICTSI is trading at 9.8x FY21E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers (15x) and historical average (12x).

NLEX Connector Road project on track for completion in 2022

NLEX Corp. said that the first 5-km section of the 8-km Connector Road project will be completed by end-2021 and have a full completion by 2022. The right-of-way delivery for the whole alignment stands at 81% while construction progress is at 16% according to Dept. of Public Works and Highways Secretary Mark Villar. Once complete, the P18bn project will connect C3 up to Skyway 3 (Sta Mesa exit). Our valuation includes the Connector Road project (negative equity value). NLEX Corp. is a 76%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM). MPI is trading at a 26% discount to market NAV of P5.80/sh.

FPH amends tender offer for Lopez Holdings shares

First Philippine Holdings Corp has modified its tender offer to buy common shares of Lopez Holdings Corp by lowering the number of stocks it plans to acquire, according to a PSE disclosure. Its revised tender offer will cover up to a max of 1.57bn common shares, equivalent to 34.5% of the total issued and outstanding common shares. The previous plan was to acquire a minimum of 908.46mn (20%) up to a maximum of 2.07bn shares (45.56%) at a price of P3.85/share (a 25% premium to the stock price when the restructuring was announced, 27% discount to NAV). The tender offer period runs from January 22 to February 19. The reduction will remove the risk of Lopez Holdings falling below the required minimum public ownership and dispense with the need to pursue its petition for a voluntary delisting. FPH President and COO Giles Puno said the company wanted to remove the pressure on LPZ shareholders to tender shares from its plan to delist.

Makati Shangri-La to temporarily close

Makati Shangri-La will close on February 1 and will reopen “when business conditions have improved”. The company has already tried managing costs by implementing salary reductions at the management level, shorter workweeks, hiring freeze, and cuts in non-essential spending. Shangri-La will provide some financial support to affected employees until the end of the year. The company pays a fixed land lease to Ayala Land and has not signified any intention to end or restructure this land lease agreement.

SM mall foot traffic reaches 50-60% of pre-COVID levels

SM Prime said that mall foot traffic in December reached 50%-60% of pre-COVID levels from c. 35%-40% in September. SM Prime has started to reduce the level of rental concessions for some tenants. Last year, it waived minimum rents for percentage-of-sales based tenants and granted rental discounts of 30%-50% for fixed rate tenants. We forecast SM Prime’s same-mall rent to return to 80% of 2019 levels this year. The company also plans to complete 5-7 Philippine malls annually until 2024.  For the past 2 years it only completed 2 malls. SMPH is currently trading at 32.6X FY21E P/E and a 3% premium to NAV.

Globe’s capex guidance at P70bn for 2021

Globe Telecom expects to spend P70bn for capex this year. This is a marked increase from the P50bn it is likely to have spent in 2020. That figure for 2020 was reduced from P63bn due to the pandemic. The firm was supposed to have spent P33bn as of 9M. The P70bn would represent close to 40% of 2021 revenue. To compare, PLDT has indicated it plans to spend P88-92bn. Which would be ~45% of revenue.

BPI to merge BPI Family into parent bank

In a disclosure, Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI PM) said that it plans to merge its 100% thrift bank subsidiary BPI Family Savings Bank (BFSB) into the parent bank. This follows similar moves in recent years by banks such as Security Bank (SECB PM), Rizal Commercial (RCBC PM), and Philippine National Bank (PNB PM). Historically, the main advantage of a thrift bank license was regulatory arbitrage, particularly for deposit reserve requirement ratios (RRR). In the past, commercial banks’ RRR was as high as 20%, while those for thrift banks was 8%. Today, the RRR is 12% for commercial banks and 3% for thrift banks. While the gap is still large, the low interest rate environment has severely reduced the incentive. With short-term Treasury rates at c1-1.5%, the arbitrage would be about P300mn, a little over 1% of BPI’s 2019 NPAT. In contrast, the merger could result in lower opex (marketing, head office), capex (IT, branches), and reduced bureaucracy (regulatory reporting, etc). The net result will likely be neutral to mildly positive for-profit forecasts. The merger will also release capital for BPI, as Basel III imposes capital penalties for banks with a holding company structure. Over the years, the gap between BPI’s Solo (Parent) and Consolidated CET-1 ratios ranged from as little as 44bps to as much as 150bps. That said, BPI’s Solo CET-1 was already high at 16.40% at 9M20. The merger will also allow BFSB customers full access to BPI’s products and services, which today is limited by having separate legal entities. BPI aims to complete the merger within 2021.

PCC clears Scatec Solar’s acquisition of shares in SN Power

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) said it approved the acquisition by Scatec Solar ASA, a Norweigan renewable energy company, of 100% shares in SN Power AS in a decision issued last January 14. The overall value was placed at $1.166bn. After the  acquisition Scatec will assume SN Power’s role as Aboitiz Power’s partner in several hydro projects including Magat, Ambuklao-Binga and Maris.

AREIT finalizes acquisition of the 30th from Ayala Land

AREIT signed an agreement to acquire the 30th office and mall complex from Ayala Land for P5.1bn – to be fully paid by 29 January 2021. This is equivalent to 6.1% 2021E EBITDA yield. The 30th is located in Pasig City and has leasable area of 75,000sqm. The acquisition expands AREIT’s leasable portfolio by 44% to 245,000sqm. The 30th has 48,000sqm of office space; PEZA accredited; 87% leased to BPOs and 27,000sqm of retail space; leased to Ayala Land, which will pay a guaranteed building lease. The office and mall lease rates increase 3%-5% annually. The land is under a 36-year lease from MBS Development Corp. The land lease agreement will be transferred from Ayala Land to AREIT. AREIT is targeting dividends of P1.62/share in 2021 which is roughly equivalent to a 5% dividend yield.

LRT-1 south extension project over 50% finished

The Department of Transportation announced that the LRT-1 South extension project is 51.6% complete as of end-2020. The DoT is targeting partial operations by end-2021. The 11.7-km extension will add to the existing 20.7-km railway and will stretch to Niog, Bacoor, Cavite. Once complete, the project will cut travel time from Baclaran to Bacoor from over an hour to 25 mins. It will also increase the rail line’s capacity to 800k per day from 500k today. The LRT-1 South extension is 36%-owned by Metro Pacific (MPI PM); 25% by Ayala Corp (AC PM), 20% by Macquarie, and 19% by Marubeni.

MerryMart launches wholesale club program

MerryMart (MM PM) has launched its wholesale program “MM Wholesale Club” which will cater to both individual or business members. It also mentioned that delivery will be free for a minimum purchase of P15,000. The program is slated to begin operations by April 2021, in time for the full completion of its central distribution hub in Tarlac. The program will initially cover NCR and selected provinces in Central and South Luzon.

Metro Manila office vacancy to rise this year

Lobien Realty Group (LRG) expects the vacancy rate of Metro Manila offices to rise from 8% in 2020 to 12%-14% this year largely due to POGOs vacating. LRG is also forecasting a 25%-30% decline in rental rates this year and it estimates that rental rates fell c. 5% last year. The office segment comprises 70% of Filinvest Land’s NAV and 55% of Megaworld’s NAV.

Nissan to shut down assembly plant in March

Nissan Motor Philippines will cease production operations for the Nissan Almera in the Santa Rosa plant, effective March 2021. Around 133 employees working on local assembly will be laid off. Honda Cars Philippines, Inc. also shut its local assembly around March last year. Trade Secretary Lopez said that the provisional safeguard measures need to be immediately put in place to protect the domestic industry from further serious injury.

Bidders raise offers for Meralco supply deal

Meralco said at least three interested bidders increased their offered capacities for the 1,800-MW CSP of greenfield baseload capacity for 2024-2025. The aggregate offered capacity now totals 7,000MW, from the original offered 6,050MW when invitation to bid was first published. Submission of bids is scheduled on January 27 and opening of price offers of qualified bidders will be on Feb 10.

FGEN to start construction of LNG terminal in March

In a virtual energy forum, First Gen EVP Jonathan Russel said that they issued a notice to proceed to our EPC contractor McConnell Dowell, with the construction expected to start by March of this year. FGEN earlier said the facility would allow it to introduce LNG to the country as early as 3Q22. The project includes a floating storage regasification unit (FSRU), an LNG carrier than can store LNG.

 
 

Macro News

PH Dollar reserves hit new record of US$109.8bn

Gross international reserves rose to US$109.8bn in end-December 2020, up by US$4.98bn (+4.8% MoM). This was helped by the National Government’s (NG) issuance of US$2.75bn ROP Global Bonds last December 2. The GIR is now equivalent to 11.7 months’ worth of imports.

BSP Governor on medical leave

BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno is currently on medical leave after undergoing a successful procedure to address a blood clot caused by a minor head accident. He has assigned BSP Deputy Governor Francisco Dakila, Jr. as officer-in-charge of the BSP. Additionally, BSP Governor Diokno is expected to be discharged from the hospital in four to five days and is anticipated to return to work subsequently.

T-bills fetch mixed results

The government awarded P32bn worth of T-bills, with total tenders reaching P87.11bn. The 91-day T-bill fetched a rate of 0.984%, up 0.7bps (BVAL: 1.147%); 182-day T-bill fetched a rate of 1.348%, down by 1.2bps (BVAL: 1.384%); and 364-day T-bill fetched a rate of 1.582%, down by 2.3bps (BVAL: 1.604%). The government is programmed to borrow P3.03tn this year from domestic and external sources. For January, the Bureau of Treasury is targeting to raise P140bn from domestic issuances.

Infrastructure spending drops 50% YoY, 29% MoM in November

Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell by 50% YoY, 29% MoM in November to P40.3bn. The govt cited high base effects from completed building projects of the LTO and LTFRB last year. This brings YT-November infra spend to P549bn, down 22% YoY. Some of this was due to delays in construction of some public works, as well as the discontinuance of some projects. For 2021, the government expects to increase infra spend by over 60% to P997bn.

Senate OKs AMLA amendments

The Senate on Monday approved a final reading of a version of the bill strengthening the government’s regulations against money laundering that deviates from the version backed by Pres. Duterte. The Senate Bill 1945 which amends Republic Act No. 9160 or Anti-Money Laundering Act was approved on third and final reading while the Bicameral Conference Committee is scheduled to be convened. The Senate raised the threshold for tax crimes to P25mn under SB1945. The President wanted the bill to set the threshold for tax crimes at P20mn, similar to the approved House Bill No 7904. Pres. Duterte also asked the Senate to adopt the House version’s provisions that gave additional powers for the AMLC. This includes granting AMLC investigative powers available to other law enforcement agencies and subpoena powers. Under SB 1945, the AMLC is authorized to implement targeted financial sanctions in relation to proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and its financing. The Senate and House versions prohibited the courts, except the Supreme Court and Court of Appeals, from issuing a TRO or write of injunction against a freeze order and asset preservation order. It included Philippine offshore gaming operators and service providers as covered persons, and real estate developers and brokers with single transactions amounting to at least P5mn as covered transactions.
