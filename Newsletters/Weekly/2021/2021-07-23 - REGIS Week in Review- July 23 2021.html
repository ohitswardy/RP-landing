REGIS REPORT

23 July 2021

The market declined over 2% for the third straight week, with the drop accelerating to -2.6% WoW.  The index closed at 6520.74.

Investors continued to be fixated on the Covid-19 Delta variant, given the apparent surge in cases in neighboring countries.  In the wake of the Dept of Health confirming local transmission of the Covid-19 Delta variant in the country, the government once again placed NCR plus four provinces under general community quarantine with “heightened restrictions” until 31 July. 

Nevertheless, vaccine supplies continued to improve. A total of 12.5m doses have arrived this month, taking the country on track to receiving the 16m expected in July. The country has now received a total of 29.9m COVID vaccine doses and has administered 16.0m doses. 

Based on the latest full week data, the daily vaccination rate for the latest full week was 271,000, up meaningfully from 212,000/day in the previous week.

The 2Q results season began with BPI the first to release. The bank posted NPAT of P11.8bn in 1H21, up 1% YoY, well ahead of Regis estimates, but in line with consensus of P24bn.  Provisions, down 57% YoY,  was the main reason for the outperformance, falling 57% YoY. NPLs were benign, boding well for the rest of the sector.

PSEi losses were widespread – virtually all index components ended the week in the red.  Only PGOLD (+1.4% WoW) and SM (+1.05% WoW) managed a gain.

This week saw headline net foreign inflow of US$62.7m. This was skewed by US$90m in inflows representing Sumitomo's purchase of a 5% stake in RCBC. If this were excluded, foreigners would have been  sellers for US$27.3m. Biggest outflows were spread across banks MBT (US$7.2m) and BDO (US$4.1m) as well as LTG (-US$5.7m), MONDE (US$4.2m), and AC (US$3.7m).

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Robinsons Land - Notes from the REGIS CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Navigating the waters

We hosted Frederick Go, Robinsons Land’s President, and Kerwin Tan, the company’s CFO, to get an update on current operations as well as management’s outlook. Mr. Go is upbeat about the office business despite the industry-wide rise in vacancies. He expects remote working to be much less prevalent after the pandemic and the company continues to sign up BPO tenants. On the mall business, while RLC expects e-commerce to continue growing rapidly, it nonetheless believes that malls will remain relevant.

Malls – adjusting to the pandemic/e-commerce; waiting for herd immunity

The COVID pandemic has severely affected the mall business. Operational occupancy is just c. 70% while mall foot traffic had dropped to only 40% of pre[1]COVID levels. Mr. Go acknowledges that the pandemic has accelerated the adoption of e-commerce and his attitude is to both “fight it and join it”. Among the adjustments that RLC has made is to provide personal shoppers. Customers can pick up their purchases on the curbside of the mall or have them delivered. The company is also beta-testing its own e-commerce app called “Mall Dash”. It is also tweaking the design and tenant mix to make the malls more lifestyle destinations which will be less susceptible to online shopping. The mall business can only recover substantially when the Philippines achieves herd immunity, which could be in 1Q22. Despite the presence of e-commerce, Mr. Go believes that malls will still be relevant in the long-term.

Offices – Optimistic on BPO office demand; POGOs looking for space

Management is quite positive on the prospects of the office segment. Mr. Go believes that remote work policies will not be widespread after the pandemic. Despite some adoption of such policies, he believes that demand from (BPO) firms will outpace closures. To our surprise, Mr. Go said two POGOs inquired about taking up 10,000sqm of office space each. While POGOs currently occupy only 3% of RLC’s office portfolio, they were a major driver of property demand for the industry pre[1]pandemic and their potential resurgence is something we will be monitoring.

Residential – sales could return to pre-pandemic levels in six months

The residential business has undergone fits and starts, with sales falling when a hard lockdown is implemented but recovering when restrictions are loosened. Mr. Go believes that residential sales could return to pre-pandemic levels in six months, barring any more lockdowns. While aggregate residential sales have not fully recovered, Robinsons Homes, which sells landed/horizontal projects in provincial areas, is achieving record sales. This is similar to the experience of most other developers.

 
 

Corporate News

AC Energy and partners invest $445mn in Vietnam wind farms

AC Energy Corp and partners are pouring in $445mn for the joint development of five wind farms in Vietnam. AC Energy said the funding covers wind projects under construction in various part of Vietnam including the 170MW Mui Ne wind farm in Binh Thuan, 60MW in Lac Hoa and Hoa Dong, 252MW Quang Binh wind farm and 88MW Ninh Thuan wind farm. With completion of its wind farms and upcoming infusion of international assets, AC Energy said it is set to hit 2,500MW of RE projects this year, making up half of its 2025 renewables capacity target

PMFTC volumes rebound sequentially, still down 12%yoy in 2Q

Philip Morris International disclosed last night that cigarette volumes in the Philippines came in at 8.1bn sticks (-12%YoY, +5%QoQ). The volume decline reflected lower market share primarily due to mid-price Fortune. Net revenues increased reflecting a “favorable pricing variance”. Industry volumes (-3%YoY) are down less than PMFTC’s volume. The unit market share dipped to 61.6% in 2Q, but decline is much smaller compared to previous quarters. PMFTC FY21E profit – P37.4bn (+12%YoY). LTG FY21E profit – P24.2bn (+2%YoY).

 

Filinvest REIT IPO priced at P7/share

Filinvest REIT will be priced at P7/share and FLI could raise up to P12.6bn from the IPO. The sale breakdown will be P11.4bn from the sale of secondary shares and P1.1bn overallotment option. FILRT would have a market cap of P34.2bn, higher than FLI’s P27.4bn market cap. FILRT would offer 2022E dividend yield of 6.6% (based on prospectus) which is slightly lower than our 7% assigned cap rate for office properties. Our initial estimates suggest that our FLI NAV would rise by less than 5%. The REIT will initially have 300,000sqm of office space which is 90% occupied with only 2% leased to POGOs. The breakdown will be 280,000sqm in Alabang, 20,000sqm in Cebu. FILRT does not own land. Filinvest Land previously said that it would eventually infuse malls into FILRT. Filinvest Land currently has a mall portfolio of 250,000sqm and an office portfolio of 525,000sqm. This includes the 300,000sqm to be spun off into FILRT. Offer period – July 26 to August 3. Listing date – August 12.

Globe to redeem non-voting preferred shares

Globe disclosed it is redeeming its non-voting perpetual preferred shares. These pref shares were issued at P500 for an aggregate amount of P10bn. The shares paid a dividend (coupon) of 5.2%, which is not tax-deductible but also higher than Globe’s likely cost of borrowing today.

Keepers Holdings: Summary of the offer

The liquor distribution business of Cosco Capital (COSCO PM), in the process of being listed via the shell company Da Vinci (DAVIN PM), plans to conduct a follow-on offering (FOO) to raise as much as P7.5bn. This business will be renamed “The Keepers Holdings” (KEEPR PM). Collectively, the Group is the largest distributor of imported liquor in the Philippines with market shares of 74% of volumes and 67% of retail sales value in 2020 (IWSR). According to the prospectus, KEEPR plans to issue 3bn shares at an offer price range of P2-2.50/sh. Post-FOO, the Lucio Co group (through COSCO and Invescap) will own c.79% of KEEPR. Bulk of the expected proceeds will be used for “strategic acquisition opportunities”, as well as product and distribution expansion.

Notes from call with ACEN CEO Eric Francia

Outlook on power demand: View was there could be oversupply in 2020-21, equilibrium in 2022 then start getting progressively tight beginning 2023. Because of more resilient energy demand, outages and delay of GNP Dinginin, demand-supply situation is already tighter than expected. Concerns on Malampaya – output restrictions suggest depletion has already started. Plants sourcing fuel from Malampaya may need to look for alternatives earlier.  Ilijan’s (1,200MW) contract with Malampaya is expiring mid-2022. Even if they want to continue sourcing from Malampaya, there might not be supply. A lot of contracts with DUs are going to expire over the next 3-5 years, but thinks demand is going to be there. Demand-supply environment should be favorable to generators looking to contract/re-contract during this time. On spot prices – erred on the side of being slightly short on power because they were expecting oversupply this year. Because of this, ACEN is “not making money off high WESM prices”. Between 2023 and 2025, they will have more uncontracted capacity and is gearing to sell into spot where he believes there is upside risk.

BPI’s 2Q21 income jumps 29% to P6.8bn

In a press release, Bank of the Philippine Island said (BPI) its 2Q21 net income hit P6.8bn (+29%YoY, +36%QoQ), helped by lower provisions. This brings 1H21 net income to P11.8bn (+1.2%YoY). Net interest income for 1H was lower by 7% at P33.9bn while NIM contracted by 24bps from 3.56% to 3.32%. Non-interest income for 1H was down by 7% to P14.3bn due to lower trading income while fees & commissions jumped 37.2%. Cost-to-income ratio stood at 50.1%, a 4.75ppt increase. BPI booked provisions of P6.5bn, lower by 55.75% than P14.7bn in the same period last year. NPL ratio was at 2.94% with NPL coverage at 120.3%. Total loans at the end of 1H stood at P1.4trillion, lower by 4.5%YoY. Total deposits at the end of 1H were down 4.5%YoY to P1.7tn. CASA grew 10.7%, which offset a 43.1% decrease in time deposits.

 

UnionBank gets digital banking license

Union Bank of the Philippines has obtained approval from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to establish a wholly owned digital banking subsidiary to be known as Union Digital Bank. UnionBank is expected to fold EON, a mobile-only platform launched in 2016, into this new subsidiary. The clearance increases the list of Philippines’ digital-only bank to 4. Previously issued licenses were to Overseas Filipino Bank of Landbank, Tonik Digital Bank and UNOBank. The BSP requires digital banks to have a minimum capitalization of P1bn.

 
 

Macro News

Average daily vaccinations rebound

The 7-day average COVID vaccinations improved to 271,426/day, a record high coming from 212,607/day the previous week. The country expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August. This includes 10.3mn doses that already arrived in July. The Philippines has now received a total of 27.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 15.1mn doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

DBCC keeps GDP targets; Approves 2022 budget proposal of P5.02tn (+11.5% YoY)

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) retained its GDP growth targets of +6-7% for FY21 and +7-9% for FY22. To reach its target, the economy would have to grow an average of +10% in the remaining three quarters of 2021. It also approved its 2022 budget proposal of P5.02tn (+11.5% YoY). With revenues expected to recover by +14% YoY in 2022, the fiscal deficit ceiling is projected to narrow to P1.67tn (c. 7.5% of GDP) from a target of P1.86tn in FY21 (9.3% of GDP). It also cut its 2023/2024 fiscal deficit-to-GDP targets to 5.9%/4.9%, respectively from 6.3%/5.3% previously and cited spending cuts following reductions in projected revenue shares of local government units from local taxes. The DBCC did not discuss the need for Bayanihan 3, as there’s no directive from the Office of the President. President Rodrigo Duterte ordered agencies in May to declare savings from last year's allotments to fund its COVID-19 response instead.

BIR surpasses 6-month tax take goal

BIR’s collections from January to June amounted to P1.034tn, exceeding its 6-month P1.018tn target by 1.6%. This was also 8.11% higher than the P956bn tax collected in 1H20. The BIR is maintaining its FY21E collection goal of P2.081tn (+7% YoY).

Economic team may rethink stimulus

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) will be meeting today to discuss (among other things) the proposal for a third stimulus package, Bayanihan III. There are some provisions under Bayanihan III that may be considered if there are extra funds available according to Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua. The House of Representatives has approved a third stimulus package worth P401bn but its counterpart measure in the Senate remains pending at the committee level. Bayanihan I and II provided funds of P275bn and P160bn, respectively. Previously, Finance Secretary Dominguez said the government was internally looking for fund sources to cover P173bn for Bayanihan 3 and that any additional expenditure must be “deficit-neutral”.
