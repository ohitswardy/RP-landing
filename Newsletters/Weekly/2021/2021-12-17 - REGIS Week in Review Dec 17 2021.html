REGIS REPORT

17 December 2021

The Phils market rose +1.5% this week to close just shy of 7,300.  Volatility mid-week was caused by the market’s knee-jerk reaction to news that Omicron had reached the country.  The market quickly gained back the losses, however and market-on-close activity on Friday (FTSE rebal related) took the market even higher.

Even as Omicron has been detected on the ground, the race to get citizens vaccinated continued, with the government currently on its second major vaccination drive (Dec 15-17).  Latest numbers have 34% of the population fully vaccinated. Regis estimates the country has some 70m doses in inventory -- enough inventory to take the proportion of fully vaccinated individuals to over 70%.  In the meantime, infections continued to decline. The latest new case count  (7-day moving ave) stood at just 323 cases.

Though the market managed a gain, index gainers and losers were actually split down the middle.  Gains were led by the telcos PLDT (+9.8% WoW) and Globe (+4.8% WoW) with moves triggered by the Senate’s approval of the amendments to the Commonwealth-era Public Service Act. These amendments would remove the classification of telecoms, airlines and domestic shipping from the list of “public utilities”, thus enabling them to be 100% foreign owned. A previous version of the Senate bill contained provisions which would still restrict foreign state-owned entities to no more than 40% ownership of critical infrastructure. If present in the final law, this could limit the avenues for the full takeover of third player DITO by China Telecom. PLDT played catch up to Globe, with the latter amassing a 74% gain YTD, againt PLDT's 42% YTD gain.  

Other gainers were conglos SM (+6.3% WoW), SMC (+3.5% WoW), and GT Capital (+3.5% WoW).  Property and banks were also up, with BPI (+3.7% WoW), Metrobank (+2.9% WoW), SM Prime (+1.8% WoW). 

BDO (+1.7% WoW) also gained despite the recent hack of BDO depositors’ online accounts.  The breach was attributed to its “ten-year old web service” which is in the process of being decommissioned and replaced.  In a call hosted by Regis, management said the total amount involved was “not material”. The  bank has promised to reimburse all affected depositors though the bank is insured against cybercrimes, and will substantially recover the amount lost. Fines or sanctions by regulators, if any, are still unclear at  this point.

ICTSI (+0.3% WoW) is allocating US$230m to expand its port operations in Mexico by over 20% to 1.7M TEUs annually. The port in Manzanilla is one of the largest in ICTSI’s portfolio, accounting for 11% of group revenues and 14% of NAV by Regis estimates. ICT also secured a 15-year extension for its Madagascar port concession which was supposed to end in 2025. The port accounts for 5% of group EBITDA and the  extension should add to its share of group NAV (currently at  2%).

Omicron threats did not spare Bloomberry (-8.2% WoW). Consumer stocks Wilcon (-5.9% WoW), Puregold (-5.2% WoW), Jollibee (-3.9% WoW), and RRHI (-3.0% WoW) were also weak.

Monde (-4.3% WoW), the only addition in the FTSE rebal, closed lower as the liquidity event was met with foreign LO's trimming positions amidst concern on the alternative meats business lagging expectations. 

Headine foreign flow was US$1.6bn. Excluding a block in Aboitiz Power (+4.3% Wow) related to JERA's purchase of a 27% stake from parent AEV which resulted in a US$1.6m inflow and a block in SMPH (US$10m), foreigners were net buyers for US$10m.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Vehicle sales rebound strongly in November

Industry car sales jumped +14% YoY, +17% MoM to 26,456 units in November according to data from the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines Inc. (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA). This is the highest monthly unit sales this year, as COVID restrictions eased and brings YT-November industry sales to 240,643 units (+23% YoY). This is 28% below 2019 and tracking below CAMPI’s year-end target of 295k units (+32% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 12,690 units in November, up 11% YoY and 14% MoM. Their market share slightly dropped to 48% from 49% the previous month. This brings YT-November sales to 116k units (+35% YoY), trending slightly behind TMP’s target of 130k units (+30% YoY). Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit. GTCAP is trading at an 8% discount to its market-based NAV of P625/sh.

 

 

Asset quality continues to improve in October

NPL ratio edged lower to 4.56% from 4.59% in September, and from the peak 4.66% in august. The improvement was seen in nominal PHP terms too. More encouraging is that Past Due (PD) ratio fell 5bps MoM to 5.16%, with the absolute values falling a full 1% MoM. This points to further easing NPLs in the coming months. Even Restructured Loans (RLs) eked out some improvement, down 1bp MoM to 3.08%.. NPL cover crept up to 85% from 84% in September, taking Loan Loss Reserves (LLR) to 3.77%, up 3bps MoM. Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated again, up 3.2% YoY, better than the 2.4% pace in September.

 

While some of the gains were admittedly small, all the numbers are moving in the right direction. Barring a major lockdown from Omicron, it would seem that bank balance sheets are truly on the mend.

BDO depositors hacked

Social media over the weekend was rife with stories from BDO (BDO PM) depositors reporting unauthorized transfers from their accounts of up to P50,000 (roughly $1,000, or the maximum daily limit for online transfers over PesoNet). According to news reports, the funds were transferred to Union Bank (UBP PM) accounts, allegedly to a certain “Mark Nagoyo” – likely fictitious, name as “nagoyo” is a Filipino word for “fooled”. Some reports claimed the funds were then used to buy Bitcoin. Meanwhile, UBP says they have frozen several accounts that may have been used in the scam. UBP management claims some social media reports are exaggerated (including the “Mark Nagoyo” part of the story).

 

On Sunday, BDO President Nestor Tan was quoted by news outlets saying that customer credentials were indeed “compromised”, and that the bank would reimburse their clients’ losses. Mr Tan was further quoted saying the breach “affects a 10-yr old web service” that is being phased out, and that the new system is currently in beta test and will be formally implemented in Jan/Feb 2022.

 

It is unclear at this stage whether the depositors’ data was acquired through phishing scams, or through a hack of BDO’s systems. Apart from the amounts lost, it is also unclear whether either bank will be assessed any fines by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) or other agencies for data privacy breaches. The BSP issued its own press release here.

MREIT to acquire 55,700sqm of offices from Megaworld

MREIT will acquire 55,700sqm of office space (from MEG) by end-2021 with an average occupancy of 99%, WALE of 3.2 years. The breakdown would be: Iloilo – 30,700sqm and McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio – 25,000sqm. The transaction would have a P9.12bn acquisition cost funded by P1.92bn in cash and P7.2bn in debt. Regis estimates that the acquisition is priced at c. 5%-5.5% cap rate. With the acquisition, management expects 2022 DPS to rise from P0.95/share to P1.00/share. This implies a dividend yield of 5.5% (post-acquisition). Megaworld will continue to hold the land under the buildings. MREIT is targeting to have a portfolio of 500,000sqm by 2024 from 225,000sqm currently – 98% occupied, WALE of 4.2 years. Currently Megaworld’s office occupancy is 90%.

 

ICTSI unit in Mexico allotting US$230mn for terminal expansion

ICTSI’s port in Manzanillo, Mexico is investing US$230mn to increase the port’s capacity by 300k TEUs to 1.7mn TEUs annually. The construction of berths, yards, and acquisition of quay cranes/other port equipment will begin in 2H2022. The expansion will complement the Mexican government’s expansion of land access to the terminal. ICT’s port in Mexico is operating at over 80% utilization and is the fastest growing port in the company’s portfolio. Regis estimates the port accounts for 10%/11% of group volumes/revenues and 14% of NAV.

PNOC-EC withholds consent on sale of Shell’s stake in Malampaya

During a Senate committee hearing on energy, the Philippine National Oil Company-Exploration Corporation head Rozzano Briguez said it is withholding its consent on the sale of Shell’s stake in the Malampaya gas project. PNOC-EC has communicated to SPEX (Shell Philippines Exploration B.V.) as of December 13 according to Shell, the deal to sell its 45% stake in Malampaya will not be able to proceed for now. PNOC-EC holds a 10% stake in the Malampaya project.

Notes from BDO call on recent hack/fraud incident

Regis hosted a call with BDO’s Chito Reyes (joined by Richard Tan and Katherine Tan) on 13 Dec to discuss the recent hack of BDO depositors’ online accounts and what remedial measures are being taken. The call discussed the bank’s IT investments in general, and updated on the business in general. Bottomline – the scale of the hack was quite small, and BDO’s new IT systems should be up and running soon. However, it does underscore the risks of cybercrime, especially as BDO embarks on a major digital push.

 

The bank downplayed the episode, saying less than 1,000 accounts had been affected. They would not disclose the total amounts, but said it was “not material”. Mr Reyes did say that the news reports ranged from P30,000 to P50,000 (roughly $600-$1,000) per incident. BDO has promised to reimburse all affected depositors. However, the bank is insured against cybercrimes, and will substantially recover the amount lost. It is unclear, though, whether the bank will be sanctioned/fined by the Bangko Sentral or other agencies for the data breach. The hackers attacked deposits that were on a 10-year old system, which is in the process of being phased out. The new system is in beta-testing and is scheduled to go live in January. BDO claims they have closed the breach and have advised all depositors to change their passwords for added security. No new breaches have been reported since. They are also reaching out to their customers, asking them to migrate to the new version of the app, which is on the new, more secure IT platform.

 

BDO has spent heavily on upgrading their IT systems in recent years. Much of the IT change-out was done earlier this year, but the transition has had to be phased in. Mr Reyes reckons they’re running at around P8-10bn a year on IT spend and expects this level to be maintained at least in the next few years. Cybersecurity enhancement was the main thrust of the upgrade. The hack comes at a crucial time for BDO, which is launching a suite of new products and services. These include their e-wallet BDO Pay, enhanced “cardless” ATMs, and upgraded in-branch kiosks helping transactions go paperless and more efficient. The episode underscores the balance required by digital adoption, between convenience and cost efficiency on one hand, and security on the other.

 

Otherwise business seems to be recovering nicely. Loan growth continues to accelerate, and Mr Reyes is hoping for a “strong finishing kick” in December, as is usually the case (ex-crisis years, of course). Management is looking at an initial estimate of 8-12% loan growth in 2022E. NIMs are forecast to rise heading into 2022 with a recent cut in CASA deposit rates. BDO continues to push its micro/SME, insurance, and wealth management businesses to drive fee incomes.

Market foreign ownership rises in November

Regis estimates market foreign ownership rose 18bps MoM to 21.1% while PSEi foreign ownership increased 30bps MoM to 25.6% (in November). The increase in PSEi foreign ownership was boosted by strong price appreciation (c. 10% MoM) of ICT and GLO, which have high foreign ownership.

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

GLO – saw the largest increase in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents (+34 bps MoM to 58.3%). Globe had among the highest net foreign buying for the month (US$29.72mn).

GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 28 basis points MoM to 21.6%. Foreigners were net buyers for US$6.43mn for the month.

URC – foreign ownership fell 26 basis points MoM to 27.8%. URC had the second-highest net foreign selling for the month (US$31.83mn).

Easing exports temper foreign trade growth in October

External trade rose 15% YoY to US$16.8bn in October, slower than the 17% YoY growth in September. Meanwhile, export growth slowed to 2% from +6% the previous month while imports grew at a sustained rate of 25% YoY due to surging fuel imports and sustained improvement in consumer goods imports. With the import bill growing at a faster pace than export sales, the Philippines’ trade deficit stood at US$4.02bn in October, slightly bigger than US$4bn gap in September. This brings YT-October total trade and trade deficit to US$157bn and US$33bn, respectively.

End-November 2021 GIR Level Settles at US$107.67bn

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, settled at US$107.67bn as of end-November 2021. This is slightly lower than the end-October GIR level of US$107.89bn and equivalent to 10.2 months’ worth of imports of goods. The BSP is targeting an end-2021 GIR level of US$111bn, lower than the previous forecast of US$114b.

BSP revises external sector targets

The BSP revised its end-2021 BOP surplus target to US$1.6bn (0.4% of GDP) from US$4.1bn (1.1% of GDP) previously. This reflects current account deficit of US$4bn (-1% of GDP) from a surplus of US$3.5bn (0.9% of GDP) due to widening of trade-in-goods deficit

OFW remittances growth target retained at +6% for FY21.

Government “drops the ball” on procurement of syringes

Foreign Affairs Secretary Teodoro Locsin Jr. said that the government “dropped the ball again”, referring to the procurement of 50mn syringes. This could slow down the pace of vaccinations in the country. Additionally, the government lowered its target for the Nov 29-Dec 1 vaccination drive from 15mn to 9mn due to a lack of syringes. The government has received 155mn doses and has administered 93mn doses. Around 34% of the Philippine population (37.3mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated.

Pace of vaccinations drops

As of December 12, the 7-day average vaccinations figure dropped to 676,543/day from 1,535,647/day the previous week. The government held a major vaccination drive from Nov 29 to Dec 1. It intends to conduct another vaccination drive from Dec 15 to 17. Around 34% of the Philippine population (37.4mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 69% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 157mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 93mn doses (as of December 12). The government is targeting to inoculate 54mn Filipinos by end-2021; 77mn by end-1Q22; 90mn by end-2Q22.

 

Gov’t revises macroeconomic targets

The interagency Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) raised its 2021 GDP growth target to 5-5.5% which is higher than previous target of 4-5% given in August. The medium-term growth rates kept at 7-9% for 2022 and 6-7% for 2023/2024. The 2021 inflation rate target was raised to 4.3%-4.5% from 2-4% previously; For 2022-2024, inflation forecast kept at 2-4%.

Philippines reports 235 new Covid-19 cases, lowest in nearly 19 months

The Philippines on Tuesday (Dec 14) reported 235 new Covid-19 cases, the lowest number since May 23, 2020 (180 infections). Thursday’s cases bring the country’s total caseload to 2,836,868. The Department of Health recorded 10 deaths, bringing the death toll to 50,351. The

DOH reported a positivity rate of 0.9% out of 24,120 tests.

 

Congress ratifies bicameral report on 2022 budget

Congress ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee report for next year’s P5.024tn budget. The General Appropriations Bill will be sent to President Duterte for his final approval, but this would depend on the completion of printing. President Duterte must sign the measure before Dec 31 to ensure the government does not operate on a reenacted budget. The Department of Education’s budget went up by P2.5bn to P592.7bn and state universities and colleges got an additional P32.47bn to bring its total budget to P104.18bn and cited higher allocation to accommodate face-to-face classes. The Bicam increased the budget for DPWH by P88bn to P786bn but slashed the Transport Department’s budget by P42bn to P75bn. The Department of Health’s budget went up by P1.2bn to P184bn. Around P45-50bn in programmed and unprogrammed funds were allocated for COVID vaccines and boosters.

Progress on the Public Service Act in the Senate

The Senate approved on second reading the bill amending the Commonwealth era Public Service Act.  These amendments are supposed to remove foreign ownership limits on several industries, most significantly in Telecoms, Airlines and Domestic Shipping.

 

The Senate version of the bill (or at least one of them) has several crucial limitations. First is the foreign ownership limit stays at 40% for foreign state-owned entities.  Second, it requires reciprocity for the country of the foreign entity/entities.  Regis reckons these clauses, if present in the final law, could still prevent an entity like China Telecom from taking over Dito completely.

 

The industries that are still considered public utilities are: distribution of electricity, the transmission of electricity, and water pipeline distribution and sewerage pipeline systems, airports, seaports, and public utility vehicles.  This means foreign ownership limits will still apply to Megawide (and its airport) and its hope that this bill will change that are likely to be dashed.

 

Without foreign ownership limits, the telcos could be 100% foreign-owned if they spin off their property assets. From a technical point of view, it would have implications on MSCI weightings et al.

Remittances hit 3-month high in October

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.4% YoY to US$2.812bn in October. This is the highest amount since July. In PHP terms, October remittances rose 7.1% YoY and brings the 3-month moving average growth rate steady at 4.2%. Land-based remittances up 4% MoM but this was offset by a 3% contraction in sea-based remittances. The YT-October remittances reached US$25.9bn, up 5.3% YoY. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +6% YoY in 2021 and +4% in 2022.

Senate approves bill on SIM card registration

The Senate approved on third reading the bill requiring the registration of mobile SIM cards. Users will have to provide identification to maintain or purchase SIM cards for their mobile services. Current prepaid subscribers will have one year to register their SIMs/numbers. The Lower House recently passed a similar piece of legislation so it appears this will be passed into law soon. The proposed law is meant to reduce incidents of fraud and crime as the status quo allows anonymity. Regis expects prepaid churn (currently 4-5% per month) to dip because of this change as users change their mindsets. Additionally, reported prepaid subscriber numbers would arguably become more reflective of ‘reality’.

 

BSP keeps key rate at record low of 2%

BSP kept overnight reverse repurchase rate at 2% for the eighth straight policy meeting in line with street expectations. BSP chief Benjamin Diokno emphasized the need to preserve the ongoing monetary policy support to help sustain the economy’s momentum over the next few quarters.
