REGIS REPORT

25 March 2021

The PSEi managed a gain of 1.7% this week, after falling 6.5% in the two weeks prior.  

New COVID cases hit all-time highs this week, with Thursday data setting a new record of 8773 new cases. The positivity rate has  climbed sharply to 16% even as hospital Covid-bed occupancy has spiked.

Metro Manila and four neighboring provinces were placed in a “bubble” for two weeks starting Monday this week, limiting travel in and out of the area. Within the bubble, more tightening measures were introduced most notable of which was the suspension of dine-in service for restaurants.  Shakeys Pizza (-1.3 % WoW), Jollibee (-0.6%), Max’s (-0.7% WoW) were weak.  

In the meantime, the top gainers for the week were grocery retailers Robinsons Retail (+11.0% WoW) and Puregold (+8.65% WoW). Puregold surprised on the upside with S&R driving topline growth with its SSSG of c. 13%.

Cebu Air (-5.2% WoW) meanwhile corrected as 80% of its domestic passengers pass through the Metro Manila airport. 

Despite record cases, the government’s “bubble” approach (as opposed to a blanket return to a more restrictive lockdown level) seemed to comfort investors already grappling with the long road to economic recovery.

With economic recovery largely contingent on vaccinations, the pace of inoculations bears close watch.  Vaccine czar” Secretary Carlito Galvez announced a cumulative 43mn total doses arriving by July. The government also aims to ramp up inoculations from the current pace of 100k-200k a week to 500k-1m a week from April or May.

Semirara (+8.1% WoW) surged.  Soaring coal (Newcastle ) prices presents significant upside risk to Semirara’s earnings (and dividends).  The company also declared a dividend of P1.25/sh translating to 10 % yield.

Robinsons Retail - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Firing on all cylinders

RRHI’s President and CEO Ms Robina Gokongwei-Pe has been focused on expanding and improving the company’s e-commerce operations since the start of the pandemic. Fulfillment rates are already improving despite a number of hurdles faced during the early days. On its brick[1]and-mortar business, adjustments have been made with respect to product mix, particularly for more disrupted formats, such as department stores and convenience stores. M&A remains a key growth strategy for the company as they continue to seek out potential targets. However, the timing on when these transactions materialize is less predictable, which will likely mean that its cash hoard could stay in its balance sheet for some time.

E-commerce: ironing out the kinks

RRHI had faced several hurdles (fulfillment, availability issues) in the early days of the rollout of its e-commerce initiatives, which include its flagship platform GoRobinsons.ph. Much of the focus over the last few quarters has been on improving delivery times and product assortment. While Ms Gokongwei-Pe believes there is still room for further improvement, fulfillment rates have increased significantly since its early days.

M&A: opportunities abound but timing less predictable

M&A will continue to be a key growth strategy for RRHI, but Ms Gokongwei-Pe admitted that the timing on when negotiations materialize is not as predictable. Many of these small retail chains are still being actively managed by their founders, who tend to have a personal attachment to the business.

Supermarkets: facing competition head on

Competitive pressure from social media marketplaces has stabilized, according to management. The company believes that it was able to recover some lost market share by 1) ensuring completeness of assortment (particularly for fresh produce), 2) maintaining high order fill rates, and 3) continuous engagement with third party e-commerce providers.

Century Pacific Food - Tight cost control supports margins in 4Q20; enters refrigerated food segment
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY20 net profit +24% YoY; acquisition of Pacific Meat Company announced

CNPF’s FY20 net profit came in at P3.9bn (+24% YoY) in line with Regis expectations. Net sales (+19% YoY to P48.3bn) and margins were likewise in line. For 4Q20 alone, net profit grew 16% YoY on the back of a 12% YoY growth in revenues. The company also announced the acquisition of Pacific Meat Company Inc. (PMCI) from its parent company Century Pacific Group, formally marking its entry into the refrigerated food segment.

Branded business: resilient demand even as pantry stocking subsides

Net sales for the branded segment (79% of sales) grew 15% YoY in 4Q, even as pantry stocking during the hard lockdown period in 1H20 faded. Demand for CNPF’s core branded canned products (pantry staples) was resilient even as overall consumption softened. Management maintained its previous guidance to grow top line by 10-15% in 2021(Regis: +1%), which will be driven by sustained demand for its core products, the boost from new product launches, and the recovery of its non-branded OEM business.

Tight cost control cushions input cost inflation

Gross margin contracted 150bps YoY in 4Q due to increasing costs of key inputs (meat, milk, coconut). EBITDA margin, however, was stable due to the company’s tightrein on opex. Skipjack tuna prices (c.25% of total raw mats)remain benign, and will likely cushion the impact of the heightened pressure from other raw materials. Management believes the spike in input prices will subside by 2H21, though prices of key raw materials have been locked in for the next 3-6 months.

Acquisition of PMCI marks entry into refrigerated/chilled food segment

CNPF announced the acquisition of PMCI from its parent company for a total consideration of P650mn (0.5x 2021 EV/Sales). PMCI is an emerging player in the fast-growing refrigerated food segment, with multiple product segments that are sold under the CNPF brands (e.g. chilled meats and fish). PMCI will not likely be a significant contributor to CNPF’s bottomline over the next 3-5 years (3-4% or revenues and EBITDA by 2025), but will widen CNPF’s product portfolio and distribution reach in the long run.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Metropolitan Bank - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Coming from a position of strength

Metrobank CEO Fabian Dee and his management team projected a positive outlook, but without being over-confident. It helps that the bank ended 2020 with the best asset quality ratios, and highest CAR among its peers. While there are early signs of recovery, management remembers (painfully) the 1998 Asian Crisis, when NPLs peaked three years later. But when the economy rebounds in earnest, MBT stands ready with high capital, high liquidity, and low funding costs. Mr Dee also expounded on its surprise dividend, as part of a broader capital management plan to raise MBT’s ROEs over the medium-term.

Learning lessons the hard way, and remembering them

MBT ended 2020 with NPLs at 2.41%, NPL cover of 163%, the best metrics among the banks we cover. The bank also shrank its loan book by 15.5% YoY, and shortened its bond portfolio maturity to under 2 years. Mr Dee attributed the conservative posture to the bank’s experience during the Asian Crisis, after which it took MBT over a decade to fully clean up its books. This time around, management chose to take the pain early, and keep its balance sheet very liquid.

NPLs flattening out, but wary of jump post moratorium, new lockdowns

Past due rates have levelled off, notably in the consumer book. But MBT remains wary of new NPLs as the Bayanihan 2 grace period rolled off only in February. Credit standards have yet to be relaxed. Mr Dee is particularly watchful of corporates, as owners may have held out for a turn in the economy, which has been slow in coming. MBT maintains its peak NPL target of 5% by mid-2021.

1Q21 indications – volumes slowing again; pickup hopefully in 2H

Loan and deposit volumes picked up in December 2020, and management hoped for follow-through momentum. However, indications are that activity has slowed again in 1Q21. Mr Dee is hopeful of an economic pick-up in 2H21, though he conceded this was contingent on a ramp up of Covid-19 inoculations.

Maybe boring, but a reliable partner

Risks clearly remain, whether on loan demand, margins, or on asset quality. But Mr Dee and his team are confident that, once the economy turns upwards, MBT is in a strong position to grow, and even to take market share. Longer-term, the strategy is to deepen relationships with their customers. They may be “boring”, but they aim to be a “reliable partner” to their loan and deposit clients. Over time, this should produce better risk-adjusted returns for shareholders.

Capital management and ROEs

To boost ROEs, MBT aims to manage its capital more proactively. With 2020 CAR at 20%, and with weak growth prospects, the bank declared a special P3/sh cash dividend, taking the stock’s yield to over 8% this year. Post-div, CAR will still be a high 18.2%, which could allow for further specials in the future.

 
 

Corporate News

FGEN FY20 -11%

First Gen’s FY20’s recurring net income was $252mn, down 11%YoY on lower electricity sales across all platforms. The company estimates electricity demand is still down by about 5-7% from pre-pandemic levels. After preferred dividends, we estimate core income was $236mn (-12%YoY), behind our FY estimate of $250mn. The implied 4Q core income $58mn is up 9%QoQ but down 8%YoY. The unplanned outage of San Gabriel in 4Q20 resulted in incurrence of replacement power costs. EDC helped by feed in tariff adjustment for 2016-2020 following ERC resolution in December 2020. Company’s capex guidance is flat for 2021.

LTG 4Q profit down 41%YoY, 19%QoQ due to PNB - behind

LTG reported a net income of P21.0bn (-9%), coming in short due to chunky provisions at PNB, despite tobacco (+9%) coming in bang-in-line with estimates. In 4Q alone, LTG's net income of P4.92bn (-41%YoY, -19%YoY) reversed the trend after it posted a recovery from the 2Q20 lows. PMFTC's contribution continued its uptrend in 4Q20. While cigarette volumes fell to 9.5bn sticks. In 4Q (-19%QoQ, -24%YoY) and 41.7bn sticks In FY20 (-16%YoY), the uplift in margins compensated for the shortfall. The higher-margin Marlboro brand ended the year at a 62% volume contribution, compared to a 56% share in 2019. PNB incurred losses In 4Q after booking a provision expense of P7.9bn (vs just P618mn in 3Q20). NPL cover, however, was just 47% as NPL ratio shot up to 11.11%. Majority of the NPLs arose from real estate, transportation and wholesale/retail trade sectors. Profts of Tanduay contracted due to lower GP margin (<15%, higher alcohol costs) and a P268mn provision arising from an excise tax case. We estimate volumes of Tanduay hit 7.5mn cases. In 4Q20 (+4.4%QoQ, +27%YoY), continuing to outperform beer due to a shift to off-premise brands. Meanwhile, ABI posted a P579mn contribution in 4Q20, contrasting previous losses stemming from its Heineken JV. The restructuring of its partnership with Heineken (to one where ABI is a manufacturer-distributor while Heineken handles marketing and brand-building) could reverse the annual losses (P700mn/year) from this venture.

PNB – NPLs surge to 11.1% coverage down sub-50%

Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) NPAT for 2020 came in at P2.6bn, down 73% YoY. For 4Q alone, the bank booked a P1.25bn loss as provision expense surged to P7.9bn for the quarter. Despite the high provisions, PNB’s NPL cover stood at just 47% as its NPL ratio shot up to 11.11% of total loans (from 6.25% in 3Q20 and 3.05% at end-2019). Needless to say these numbers are pretty horrible, compared to the industry average of 3.70% NPL and 93.7% cover at end-2020. By category, PNB’s corporate NPLs were at 8.8% while consumer NPLs ranged from 19-25%.

Phoenix plans asset sales to manage debt

The board of Phoenix Petroleum (PNX PM) has approved the sale of certain properties, assets and investments “under reasonable and acceptable terms” with any interested entity. As of end 2019, PNX had P50bn in interest bearing debt, 3x that of Chelsea. P44bn in the form of loans and P6bn in LCs. BDO (BDO PM) has the largest exposure at P9.5bn at end-2019 followed by PNB (PNB PM) at P6.9bn. Net gearing was more than 200%.

Manila Water terminates Balagtas water deal

Manila Water announced that its water concession deal in Balagtas, Bulacan had been revoked and terminated by the Balagtas Water District (BWD). BWD will issue an official statement in the coming days regarding the revocation and termination of the notice of award. In April 2018, BWD issued a notice of award for a 25-year concession worth P400mn to Manila Water. Manila Water projected to provide 22 MLD to customers by the end of the concession.

SM begins tender offer for 2GO

SM Investments is making a tender offer for 2Go Group (2GO PM). The tender will be at P8.50/share – the same price that it will pay for Chelsea Logistics’ (C PM) stake in the 2Go. Tender offer period – March 22 to April 21.

Senate approves renewal of Dito franchise

The Senate approved on third and final reading the renewal of the franchise of Dito Telecommunity for 25 years. The Senate version introduced certain amendments from the House version: (1) The frequencies assigned to Dito will be subjected to review at regular intervals. (2) The National Telecommunications Commission can reallocate any redundant frequencies. (3) Dito must submit a report to the President and to Congress any data, assistance, or cooperation given to a foreign government and its instrumentalities. Since there are differences in the House and Senate versions, a bicameral conference committee will be convened. Udenna and Chelsea control 60% of DITO while China Telecom owns the remaining 40%.

 
 

Macro News

Treasury sells P30bn in 10-year reissued bonds

Bureau of the Treasury raised P30 bn from reissued 10-year bonds; bond fetched a rate of 4.614%. Bid rates ranged between a 4.35% and a high of 4.7% (BVAL: 4.4789%). This was higher than the coupon fetched last month, 3.066%.

BIR to extend ITR filing deadline for corporates

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) is extending the deadline for the filing of annual income tax returns (ITR) for corporate taxpayers. This was due to the recent surge in coronavirus cases and the delay in the enactment of the CREATE bill. No new deadline date given (original deadline for filing is on April 15). The BIR is aiming to collect P231.57bn in April. The CREATE bill is now waiting President Duterte’s signature. The bill will lapse into law on March 27.

House bill adjusts smoking age from 18 to 21

A pending bill in the House of Representatives will adjust the minimum age at which individuals will be allowed to smoke from 18 to 21. Rep. Jose Singson Jr of Probinsiyano Ako party-list filed House Bill 6210 due to “public health concerns”. HB also sought to define “smoking” as the act of carrying any lighted or heated cigarette or other tobacco products and its derivatives in whatever form, whether being inhaled, smoked, vaped or consumed by whatever means”.

Metro Manila and 4 adjacent provinces on tighter restrictions

Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, and Rizal will be on tighter mobility restrictions from March 22 to April 4. These areas will be under a single “bubble” – entering the bubble and traveling outside is restricted to essential/authorized individuals as well as returning residents. The restriction also states that operational capacity will be 30%-50% for all sectors. Restaurant operations shall be limited to delivery, take-out, and outdoor dining. Meanwhile, Curfew from 10PM to 5AM except for essential workers, cargo vehicles, and public transportation. People under 18 and over 65, pregnant women, people with comorbidities, and people living with these at-risk individuals are banned from leaving their homes unless they are procuring essential items.

Philippines signs deal for 20mn Moderna vaccine doses

The Philippine government and private sector have signed an agreement with Moderna for 20mn vaccine doses. 13m will go to the government while the private sector will have 7mn. The private sector will provide doses for their employees. Some of these doses may arrive as early as 2Q21.

BSP maintains policy rates

The Monetary Board kept all monetary policy levers unchanged, with the overnight reverse repurchase facility at 2.0%. The decision was made despite their newest inflation forecast, which was raised to 4.2% for 2021 (from 4% previously). The new inflation forecast is above its target of 2-4%. BSP officials say they are able to keep the monetary policy accommodative because inflation drivers are largely supply side in nature and transitory. There is currently no evidence of any spillovers to other commodities apart from those that are subject to the supply shocks.

NEDA sticks to 6.5% GDP growth target

The government is maintaining its economic growth target at +6.5% for 2021. We at Regis forecast +7.0% while consensus is at +7.5%. The government’s GDP growth forecast range is +6.5% to +7.5%. In 2020, GDP fell by 9.5% to P17.5tn. NEDA has been pushing for the reopening of the economy. It also recommended localized lockdowns instead of going back to stricter quarantine measures. It estimates an P2.1bn income opportunity losses for every day Metro Manila and nearby provinces are in lockdown.

House OKs franchise renewal bills of two MVP firms

Franchise extension bills for two major companies of Manny V. Pangilinan are now off to the Senate floor after getting a final nod from the House of Representatives. House lawmakers approved on third and final reading HB 8970 and 8977 which would extend by 25 years each the legislative licenses of Smart Broadband Inc. and Cignal TV. Smart Broadband’s current license was granted in 1997 and would expire by July next year. Cignal TV’s license would lapse in March 2023 if not extended. Both franchise bills will still need to hurdle the Senate before getting the President’s enactment.
