REGIS REPORT

29 October 2021

The Philippines slipped 3.2% WoW in the wake of two straight weeks of gains and as flows reversed outwards. 

Foreigners were net sellers for US$29.7m as lack of catalysts prompted profit taking.  Foreigners were seen selling across the board with big cap liquid names like Ayala Land, SM Investments, BDO, and SM Prime seeing bulk of the outflows.

Losers outnumbered winners 20 to 9 with recent outperformers consolidating this week.  Meanwhile, market favorites that had seen their fair share of profit taking recently resumed their gains.

Last week’s top gainers Robinsons Land (-1.7% WoW), JG Summit (-9.5% WoW), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-5.7% WoW), and Universal Robina (-1.4% WoW) were all lower this week.  Large cap SM (-6.7% WoW) was also down.  Cyclicals likewise declined, led by SM Prime (-8.9% WoW), Ayala Land (-6.4% WoW), Metrobank (-3.2% WoW), and BDO (-3.0% WoW). 

Recent market darlings continued their ascent this week after stumbling recently on profit taking.  Among the top gainers this week were AC Energy (+11.1% WoW), Wilcon (+9.9% WoW), and Converge (+2.9% WoW). Consumer was also strong, with Jollibee (+4.4% WoW), Puregold (+3.3% WoW),  and Robinsons Retail (+2.7% WoW) among the leaders.    

Re-opening continued to be the main theme as COVID cases continued to dip. The latest 7-day moving average of daily cases stood at 4660 vs 6030 the previous week. Meanwhile, the government is expanding the vaccination of minors nationwide by next week. Travel restrictions across the country continue to be relaxed, and the Dept of Transportation (DoTr) is proposing to increase the allowed maximum passenger capacity of public transport vehicles. This would only help mobility data (Google) which show mobility nearing the highest since the initial outbreak and lockdowns.

A few corporates released 3Q results this week. The hard lockdown imposed in August took its toll on Wilcon’s sales revenues (-2%) in 3Q though robust margins allowed for +17% in net profits.  Electrify volumes were still soft (+3% YoY) for Meralco while loan growth (+5%) is slowly resuming for BDO. 

Metro Pacific’s (-9.4% WoW) toll road subsidiary is predicting as much as 32% growth in vehicular volumes as motorists return to the road for FY22.  Meanwhile, Globe (-1.4% WoW) raised US$600m from a new perpetual issue (at par) with an initial coupon (technically a “distribution rate”) of 4.2%. The securities have an “optional redemption” feature and a step-up on the fifth year.

Converge (+2.9% WoW) announced that its submarine cable project to connect that country’s main island groups will be completed this weekend. CNVRG already began offering its broadband service in Cebu and Mindanao earlier this year.

Corporate News

CNPF expands dairy portfolio with new chocolate malt drink

CNPF announced it has launched ‘Choco Hero’, a chocolate malt drink for children. This new product expands its dairy portfolio, which is mostly powdered milk under the ‘Birch Tree’ brand. Dairy accounts for c25% of CNPF’s total revenue.

Wilcon Depot – strong margins drive 3Q21 performance; cost/supply issues loom

WLCON reported 9M21 net profit of P1.87bn, up 111% YoY, broadly within estimates (Regis FY21E: P2.6bn). Net sales (+27% YoY to P20bn) and gross margin (37.1%) are tracking ahead of expectations, while EBIT (+86% YoY to P2.8bn) is in line. For 3Q21 alone, net profit grew +17% YoY to P623mn, lifted by a lower tax rate. The 3Q21 net sales were down slightly (-2% YoY) on the back of -9% SSSG. NCR stores (25% of total network) posted double digit SSS decline due to the ECQ/MECQ imposed in Metro Manila in August. Margins, however, remained healthy with GPM +110bps QoQ to 38% in 3Q. There was minimal change in the contribution of in-house merchandise (49% of total) and management attributed the margin expansion to 1) price increases to pass on additional freight-related cost, and 2) proportionately lower sales from volume buyers (e.g. contractors). WLCON saw a sharp increase in receivable days (23 days as of 9M21 vs 6-7 days historically) due to a jump in advances to suppliers. Management has decided to ramp orders to proactively manage any potential issues from suppliers in China, coupled with the anticipation of increased demand as the economy reopens. Management observed a marked improvement in store visitation in the last few weekends as Metro Manila Alert levels have eased. While this bodes well for the company’s top line recovery, they admitted that growth could come at the expense of margins as volume buyers return. There is also looming cost pressure given potential supply issues from China, though the company is confident that they can pass this on through price increases. WLCON is trading at 2022E multiples of 44x PE and 19x EV/EBITDA, vs regional peers of 31x PE and 18x EV/EBITDA

Globe Telecom prices USD perpetual security

Globe Telecom announced that it successfully priced a $600mn Reg S only non-call 5-year USD denominated senior perpetual capital securities offering with an initial distribution rate of 4.2%. there will be a fixed rate until Year 5, thereafter reset every 5 years to the prevailing 5-year UST + initial credit spread + 250 bps step-up. Additionally, there will be an optional redemption: At the option of the Issuer at par on any business day on or after the date falling 3 months prior to step up date. The final order book was oversubscribed more than 3x, allowing Globe to upsize the transaction to $600mn and tighten final pricing to 4.2% from the initial guidance of 4.5%.

Cebu Pacific says 14 local destinations removes RT-PCR tests as pre-travel requirement

Cebu Pacific announced there are now 14 local destinations in its network that no longer require swab tests for travel including Bohol, Roxas, Mandaue City, and Cebu City. In lieu of swab tests, an official vaccine card or vaccination certificate can be presented. Bacolod, Butuan, Guimaras, Iloilo City, Maguindanao Province, Negros Occidental, and Tacloban already accept vaccination cards in lieu of the swab tests. While Cagayan de Oro, Cebu Province, and Pagadian City require other travel documents such as SPass, medical certificate, or valid government ID, among others.

 

BDO briefing notes

Some notes from BDO’s briefing earlier this afternoon. To the upside, the digital customer base is massive, with 9mn customers enrolled on their online/app platforms, by far the largest among banks. And the BDO Pay e-wallet looks promising. However, there were still many questions/concerns about asset quality, particularly the low credit cost and low NPL cover.

 

Loan growth of 5% was driven by corporates, up 10% YoY, while consumers were +4% and “mid-market” was still at -4%. This is probably what led to the 8bps QoQ dip in loan yields, as (anecdotally) top corporates are borrowing at sub-2% for short-term loans. NIMs were partly held down by a P100bn YTD increase in investment portfolio (to P603bn), in line with the YTD deposit growth. A return to loan growth would be positive for NIMs. The good showing in 3Q gives management some optimism heading into 2022. BDO added 38 branches so far this year, 5 for the main bank, and 33 for BDO Network Bank. Of the 5 BDO branches, only 1 was in Metro Manila while the rest were in provincial areas. Management continues to hold that branches are an integral part of financial inclusion. 

Asset quality – CEO Nestor Tan defended the bank’s relatively low NPL coverage (101% vs BPI’s 130%, and MBT’s 180% [1H]) and low credit cost (60bps vs BPI’s 100bps, and MBT’s 120bps [1H]). He argued that “not all NPLs are created equal”, particularly given the relatively high proportion of their NPLs that are secured (no #s given). Once the Covid pandemic is done, he expects BDO to rebuild its NPL cover, to prepare for the “next black swan event”.

Digital initiatives – BDO’s goal is to have a “competitive digital presence”, giving its customers a broad array of channels to make it “the preferred bank in every market (they) serve”. From 2019 to 1H2021, the bank has spent P29bn on IT. From around 5% of revenues, IT spend was tracking c9% in 1H21. BDO has 9mn registered online banking customers as of 1H21, likely the largest among banks (and probably by a wide margin). BDO estimates that half of those (c4.5mn) would be considered “active” users. In contrast, BPI claims to have about 4.7mn clients enrolled on digital platforms, of which 3.1mn were active. (For perspective, BPI reckons its entire customer base is only about 8.4mn, and MBT’s is only about 5mn)

BDO Pay, its own mobile wallet was launched earlier this year. BDO Pay allows depositors and card holders to transact via QR code with any merchant that is also on the BDO Pay platform. No need to “top-up” a 3rd-party wallet. No data yet on users and usage. BDO also launched online account opening, ATMs using biometrics (instead of cards), and digital in-branch services.

 

Meralco 3Q

Meralco reported net profit of P16.5bn for 9M21, up +47% YoY, with core income of P18.1bn (+15%).  For 3Q alone, core income amounted to P6.66bn, better than expected and lifting the YT-Sept figure in line with our forecasts. While electricity volumes remain on the soft side (+3% YoY), contributions from other subsidiaries took up the slack. Electricity sales for 3Q rose 2.9% to 11.7m kwh but is still trending below expectations due to the recent hard lockdowns. That figure remains 4% lower than 2019 levels. Sales to industrial and commercial customers are picking up (+5.4% and +5.7% YoY) just as residential consumption is normalizing (-1.2%). Profits from its generation segment and other subsidiaries amounted to P1.7bn for 9M21, of which almost P900m came in 3Q alone. Meralco increased its stake in Singapore genco PacificLight to 58% (from 28%) just as the latter’s operations started improving due to changes in its fuel supply contract and rising power prices. Even its modest telecom/broadband business, Radius, delivered profits. There’s been no change in the regulator’s timetable for the completion of Meralco’s rate review (mid-2022). Neither has there been much visible progress either.  We remain doubtful that the tariff reset can be completed in time. Meralco acknowledged the impact that soaring fuel costs (coal, gas) will likely have on (1) gencos unable to pass on the surge in costs and/or (2) power consumers who will have to pay more. Recall Meralco recently signed agreements to purchase sizeable amounts of power at rates that were not only low but also fixed (i.e. with no fuel adjustments) for the initial years. The viability of the projects behind these contracts could be at risk.

Synergy Grid FOO priced at P12/share

Synergy Grid & Development Philippines has priced its follow-on offering at P12/share, the low end of its price guidance. At P12/share, Synergy will raise P13.85bn from the sale of 1.15bn shares. The resulting market capitalization at P12/share is P63bn. Synergy is targeting to list on November 10, following an offer period that will run from October 26 to November 2.

 

Senate Energy Chair claims Udenna-Chevron deal is invalid

Senate Energy Committee Head Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian said that the Department of Energy’s approval of Udenna Corp’s purchase of Chevron Malampaya’s 45% stake in the Malampaya gas field is defective and invalid. Sen. Gatchalian previously questioned the deal between Udenna and Chevron as under Presidential Decree 87, sale transactions involving oil exploration require prior approval from the government. Sec. Cusi said the DoE did approve the sale through a “farm-in” process, referring to a deal where an investor acquires a stake from an existing participant in an oil or gas project. The DoE previously claimed the department was not informed of the transaction and that it did not need to approve the agreement as it was between two private companies. Dennis Uy’s group is also acquiring Shell Philippines Exploration B.V.’s 45% stake in the gas field (approval still pending with DoE) while PNOC-EC holds the remaining 10% of the project.

MPTC sees 32% increase in 2022 traffic volumes

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp (MPTC) expects a 32% increase in average daily traffic volume in 2022 according to MPTC President and CEO Rodrigo Franco. The company expects to open new roads: CCLEX in 1Q22; NLEX Phase 1 in 2Q22 and Phase 2 in 4Q22; Portions of the CALAX Cavite segment and NLEX 8.2 in 3Q22. Our forecasts assume MPTC’s traffic volumes to rise by 38% YoY in 2022. MPTC accounts for c. 20% of MPI’s earnings and gross asset value. MPI is trading at a 43% discount to its market-based NAV.

 
 

Macros News

Budget gap widens to P181bn in September

The National Government recorded a budget deficit of P181bn in September, up 31% YoY and 50% MoM. Tax revenues in September were 1% above September 2019 levels, bringing YT-September tax collections just 3% below 2019. Expenses sustained YoY, MoM improvement, partially boosted by a P10bn Coconut Farmers and Industry Trust Fund transfer. The YT-September budget deficit reached P1.1tn, tracking below the government’s FY21E due to lower-than-programmed interest payments and underspending.

Pace of vaccinations picks up

As of October 24, the 7-day average vaccinations rose to 487,398/day from 375,773/day as of October 17. The Department of Health hopes to increase the pace of vaccinations to 800,000/day in November. Around 24% of the Philippine population (25.7mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID and around 62% of the Metro Manila population (8.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 97.7mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 55.7mn doses (as of October 24). The country might administer booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities in November or December 2021.

BOP position reverts to deficit in September

The country’s Balance of Payments stood at a deficit of US$412mn reversing the surplus worth US$2.104bn a year earlier and US$1.044bn in August due to outflows from the National Government’s debt service payments and a higher trade deficit. The BOP data could improve in October/November following proceeds from the US$1.6bn retail dollar bond issuance and other fund-raising activities. This brings 9M21 BOP deficit to US$665mn (vs last year’s surplus of US$6.878bn). The BSP is targeting a BOP surplus of US$4.1bn in 2021

DOTr backs proposal for 100% PUV capacity

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) is pushing its proposal to raise the passenger capacity in public transport back to 100%. The passenger capacity limit is currently at 50%. The increase will be implemented in Metro Manila. DOTr also requested the Department of Energy to grant P1bn in fuel subsidies to public transport drivers.

Mobility jumps to near-pandemic highs

As we had expected, mobility surged on 16 October, as Metro Manila was downgraded to “Alert Level 3”. Google data showed numbers that were either at or near highs since the pandemic began. Movement has not been this good since just before Christmas last year for retail/recreation and for transit stations. For mobility to the workplace, the data is already at Covid highs. Numbers have come down a bit, after the “revenge spending / eating / travel” over the first weekend. That said, the data so far only goes to Friday, 22 Oct. We would not be surprised to see the numbers back up for the weekend just passed. The “glass half full” recovery/reopening theme remains intact.

Vaccinations of minors to expand nationwide on November 3

The vaccination of children aged 12 to 17 will expand nationwide on November 3 around 18,666 children with comorbidities have been vaccinated in Metro Manila. The Department of Health estimates that there are 12.7mn children aged 12 to 17 in the Philippines or 12% of the total population. As of October 26, 24% of the Philippine population (26.2mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of October 25, 62% of the Metro Manila population (8.4mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 97.7mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 56.8mn doses (as of October 26). The government might administer booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities in November or December 2021.

“Hot money” posts net outflow in September

Foreign portfolio investments (or “hot money”) posted a net outflow of US$24.16mn in September reversing the US$11.51mn net inflow in August 2021 but lower than the US$494mn net outflow posted in September 2020. Majority of investments (or 74%) registered were in PSE-listed securities while the remainder went to investments in Peso government securities. This brings YT-September net outflows to US$459mn, lower than the US$4.4bn net outflows in the same period last year.
