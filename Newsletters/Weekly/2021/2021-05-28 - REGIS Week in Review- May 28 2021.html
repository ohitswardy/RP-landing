REGIS REPORT

28 May 2021

The Phils market rips this week up 7.7% WoW despite MSCI rebalancing outflows. The PSEi closed at 6,674, the highest close since March 12 earlier this year. The recent rally broke through the 6500-resistance level, ironically during the MSCI rebalancing day, when market was expecting to see heavy selling pressure.

Conglomerates were this week’s biggest gainers. JG Summit was up 17.8% followed by Aboitiz Equity Ventures and Ayala Corporation both up, 15.1% and 11.3% respectively. First Gen Corp -4.0% WoW, Globe Telecom -0.5% and BDO Unibank -0.2% were the only stocks in the index that dropped week on week.

In other news this week. Robinsons Land (+8.8% WoW) released more details on its planned REIT listing. Robinsons Land plans to raise up to P26.7b from the sale of secondary shares in RL Commercial REIT. The price will be up to P7.31/share and the deal will have a firm offer of 3.34bn shares with an overallotment option 305.1mn shares. The assets will include 14 office buildings with 425,000sqm of leasable area. Based on the prospectus, at the maximum price, the REIT would offer a dividend yield of 5%, lower than our assigned 7% cap rate. If the REIT were valued at the maximum price, our RLC NAV estimate could rise by 10%-20%.

Meralco (+4.4% WoW) disclosed that electricity sales jumped in April, both on a YoY and MoM basis. The April 2020 base was very low as the hard lockdowns included factory shutdowns, etc. But even the MoM trends are looking good, even despite the new lockdown. We reckon electricity sales are a good measure of broad economic activity. This suggests that the 2Q21 GDP print could be “less bad” than some fear.

Also, out this week was a recent survey on vaccination. Vaccine resistance is high and is one more obstacle the government will have to overcome to inoculate 70% of the population. Only 1/3rd of the country’s population is willing to be vaccinated today. It does not help that bulk of the vaccines received so far are from Sinovac, which President Duterte himself was unwilling to be injected with.

Next week should be intersting to see if the PSEi holds up, but thats it for now. Happy weekend to you guys!

Ayala Corp - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Pruning for growth

We hosted Mr. Fernando Zobel, the recently appointed CEO of Ayala Corp at our Regis CEO Roundtable, where he shared his newly formed Three-Point Strategic Agenda. The core value drivers he identified, whose growth AC will continue to support, were not a surprise. What’s new was AC’s explicit goal to raise as much as US$1bn from “value realization” initiatives by 2023 – a plan we interpret as a welcome pruning exercise for investments that have not been meeting expectations.

Core value drivers

The “three-point strategic agenda” recently unveiled by Ayala Corp relates to its plans concerning its various investments/business which were grouped into three broad baskets. The business in the first basket, described as “core value drivers”, are those that AC intends to continue sustaining and supporting. This basket includes Ayala Land, BPI, Globe and AC Energy. The inclusion of these firms in this first set is not a surprise.

Emerging businesses: Health Care and Logistics

Mr. Zobel identified health care and logistics as being part of AC’s “emerging businesses” that the group will invest more in and grow. AC’s current exposure to these two sectors is small and the expectation here is that M&A will be a key way for its presence here to grow.

US$1bn target for ‘value realization”

A number of AC’s smaller businesses have been grouped under “portfolio investments” and the new CEO’s strategic agenda includes an explicit target to raise US$1bn from what’s called “value realization” moves. Mr. Zobel prefers not to use the word “divest”, but we reckon a pruning of some of these holdings could be a welcome exercise.

Megawide - Airport and landport units weigh on 1Q21 recovery
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q21 net loss of P124mn, wider than Street estimates

MWIDE booked a net loss after pref divs of P124mn in 1Q21, which included an P18mn tax benefit from deferred tax adjustments. The results disappointed, even if we do not adjust for the tax gain. Despite improvements at the construction unit, the airport and landport units remain to be significant drags to the company’s overall recovery. The company successfully restructured the debt at its 60%-owned unit, GMR-Megawide Cebu Airport Corp., which should preserve liquidity both at the airport unit and at the parent level.

Construction: Margins elevated; Order book at P66.1bn

The construction unit’s profit (before pref divs) improved by 21% YoY despite a 10% drop in revenue bookings. 1Q21 EBITDA margin was unsustainably high at 16%, given that majority of its projects are in the early stages of construction. The order book stood at P66.1bn, down slightly from end-2020 (P68bn). Management is aiming to secure P17-30bn worth of infrastructure projects this year, as it is participating in several railway packages.

Airport: Debt restructuring agreement completed; Domestic travel bubbles

The debt restructuring agreement at the airport will allow the company to defer interest and principal payments in 2021-2023 to 2024, which we estimate could total over P7bn. The interest payment deferral will help its cash flow in the medium term but there will be no P&L impact, as it will still be accruing interest. Management believes that the government will be forced to lift domestic travel restrictions to accommodate the 2022 election campaign. The local government of Cebu is also in talks with other local governments to implement a domestic “travel bubble”, wherein cities with low number of cases can open up their borders.

Landport: No more POGO exits?

The landport unit’s profit contributions dropped QoQ and YoY after the exit of a POGO tenant last November. We note that landport revenues trail our forecast but earnings are within expectations. Even though a large proportion of its POGO tenants signaled to exit last year, the company somehow managed to keep the remaining two office tenants, which still occupy 75% of office leasable area. With industry office vacancy rates still rising, we remain skeptical of the earnings sustainability of this segment.

Vista Land - 1Q21 rental revenue falls on tenant closures; rental receivables jump
 
 
by Carl Sy +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q21 earnings fall 15%

Vista Land’s 1Q21 net profit fell 15% to P2.0bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The disappointment was largely due to the limited construction activity, which brought residential revenue down 13% YoY. Meanwhile, rental receivables rose substantially even though leasing revenue fell.

Reservation sales improve before launches of high-end projects

Reservation sales inched up 4% YoY (+16% QoQ) to P16.1bn, 82% of the 2019 average and in line with our forecast. The improvement came even though Vista has not yet launched its planned high-end projects this year (mostly suburban housing just outside Metro Manila). Meanwhile, default rates remained in the low-single digit levels and the 10% of buyer accounts that deferred payments last year are now current (paying on time). Similar to previous periods, OFWs comprised 50%-55% of sales.

 

Rental revenue drops 9% due to tenant closures; rental receivables jump

Rental revenue, which is dominated by malls, declined 9% YoY to P2.0bn. About 5% of mall space was vacated by tenants in 4Q20, likely because Vista generally did not grant rental concessions. Meanwhile, office vacancy also increased from 3% in 4Q20 to 8% in 1Q21. We estimate that rental receivables jumped 70% YoY to P13bn, 185% of annual rent. This suggests that rental revenues are overstated relative to cash rents.

Office REIT listing in late 2021 or early 2022

Vista is targeting to list its office REIT by late 2021 or early 2022. We expect the impact to be small since the office portfolio typically accounts for less than 5% of earnings. Nevertheless, the listing should help lower Vista’s gearing levels. We estimate that as of 1Q21, Vista had net debt of P98bn and net debt/equity of 91%.

Semirara - One man’s trash is another man’s treasure
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Underappreciated coal miner in the region

SCC’s outlook has improved massively following the strong recovery in regional seaborne coal prices since the start of this year. Newcastle spot prices are now at US$105/MT, which will mean SCC’s coal ASPs will likely further improve from 1Q21. We believe SCC can deliver a div yield of 17% over the near-term, the highest amongst regional coal miners and Philippine stocks. There is ample room for upside, in our view, as coal spot prices are trading above our forecast (US$85/MT). Buy.

12%-43% EPS upgrades

We raise our EPS forecasts by 12%-43%, after incorporating 58-61% increases in coal ASP assumptions and lower non-cash costs. These revisions more than offset downgraded assumptions on power plant availability factors. The power division remains the thorn in SCC’s side, in our view, but repairs should be completed by 3Q21 at the latest. Once resolved, this should provide another leg to SCC’s recovery. The Luzon grid’s power supply and demand situation remains tight and gencos, like SCC, should be able to capitalize in the medium-term.

It’s a cliché.. but cash IS king

We anticipate SCC’s free cash flow will improve massively this year from last year’s lows, as operating cash flow improves and capex declines (see Fig 6). The company already paid its regular cash div last month (10% yield) but we believe there is ample room for the company to declare special dividends until next year.

Div yield of 17% is too attractive to ignore; Avenues for growth, re-rating?

Despite the earnings upgrade, we are maintaining our TP at P14/sh. The shares have admittedly de-rated over the past years given ESG trends, and it might take a while before the company’s expansion and diversification plans (cement and power) materialize, and with those hopes for a re-rating. We nonetheless believe the div yield of 17% is too attractive to ignore. Key downside risks: collapse in coal prices; execution risks; an undiversified power portfolio; mine accidents.

Alliance Global - 1Q21: Casino, QSR give up 4Q gains; liquor is sole outperformer
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1Q21 profit -13%YoY - behind

In a briefing, AGI discussed its 1Q21 results that came out days earlier. 1Q21 net income was P2.6bn (-13%Yoy, -14%QoQ). Contributions from Golden Arches, Megaworld, and Travellers softened from more robust performances in 4Q20, and more than offset the outperformance of Emperador. The result is tracking below Regis (P14.3bn) and consensus (P13.4bn) estimates for the full year.

Casino, restaurant contributions soften despite decent top line

Travellers’ losses widened to P1.1bn in 1Q21, coming from just -P100mn in 4Q20 and -P958mn in 1Q20, even as GGR improved 15%QoQ to P4.6bn. VIP contributed about 60% of GGR, as it gained traction with video streaming, and local visitation also appeared to be on the mend, as average foot traffic jumped 44%QoQ to 11.2k/day (40% of pre-COVID). We think EBITDA dipped 81%QoQ due to more aggressive promotions. Meanwhile, Golden Arches’ profit contracted 81%QoQ despite flat revenues, dragged by higher interest and depreciation charges. SSSG was -15.8% in 1Q21 vs -37% in 4Q20.

Lower marketing & advertising expenses help liquor profits

EMP’s 1Q21 was P2.1bn (+43%yoy, -1%QoQ). GPM improved sharply QoQ to 32.9% (24.3% in 4Q20). We suspect they discounted heavily in 4Q20 to meet sales targets. Management cited that lower marketing expenses helped profits for the period, similar to the experience of other alcohol producers as campaigns shifted to digital channels. Whisky contributed 32% to revenues in 1Q21 vs 29% in 1Q20.

Flat reservation sales, rentals still soft

MEG’s 1Q21 profit dropped 33% to P2.4bn, as rental revenues (-27%) continued to be dragged by weak mall (-64%), hotel (-39%) and office (-8%) segments. Reservation sales were flat at P20.7bn, roughly 60% of the pre-covid average. We estimate that sales condominiums fell 18%.

Megaworld Corp. - 1Q21 office revenue falls 8%; REIT will be “sizeable”
 
 
by Carl Sy +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q21 earnings fall 33%

Megaworld’s 1Q21 net profit dropped 33% to P2.4bn, behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. While management appears more optimistic than its briefing last month, we believe the recent hard lockdown and the slow pace of vaccinations will make it difficult for the company to meet our FY forecasts.

Flat reservation sales YoY despite strong lot sales

Reservation sales were flat YoY (+16% QoQ) at P20.7bn, only 56% of the 2019 average. This is despite the doubling of horizontal projects (mostly lots in provinces adjacent to Metro Manila) from 14% of sales to 30%. This implies that condominium sales fell 18% to P14.5bn. Management raised its 2021 launch target from P8bn to P18bn and hinted that the P70bn presales guidance (which implies 3% growth YoY) might be raised later in the year. However, given the recent hard lockdown, we believe it would be difficult for the company to achieve our P100bn forecast for the year.

 

Office revenue drops on higher vacancy; office REIT in the works

Office revenue fell 8% YoY as vacancy increased from 4% in 1Q20 to 10% in 1Q21 (10% in 4Q20). Although floor area expanded by 7% in 4Q20 (to 1.4mn sqm), these new offices are still subject to rent-free periods. Management appears optimistic about the prospects of the office segment. However, we consider the 9% of floor area still occupied by POGOs to be at risk of more closures. The company gave scant information on its office REIT listing but said it would be “sizeable” and that a more detailed announcement will be made next month.

Mall revenue drops QoQ and YoY

Mall revenue fell 7% QoQ (-64% YoY). Unlike some other mall operators, Megaworld did not reimpose minimum rents on tenants. The hard lockdown from end-March to mid-May will likely lead to a deterioration in 2Q21 performance.

Filinvest Land - 1Q21 office vacancy at 24% and could reach 30% by end-2Q21
 
 
by Carl Sy +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q21 net profit falls 45%

Filinvest Land’s 1Q21 net profit fell 45% YoY to P736mn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Residential revenue fell 20% on limited construction activity. However, what caught our attention the most was the 24% vacancy of the office division, much higher than the industry average of 11%.

Office vacancy at 24% and could reach 30% by end-2Q21

Office revenue fell 15% YoY as vacancy jumped to 24% from 9% a year ago (due to POGO closures). Some POGO tenants also gave notice that they would pre[1]terminate their contracts in 2Q21, which would bring FLI’s office vacancy to 30% (unless new tenants are signed). After these tenants close, we estimate that POGOs would still occupy c. 5% of the portfolio. Due to tenant deposits, FLI still recognized revenue from some of the POGOs that already closed. Furthermore, due to a change in accounting standards, FLI now records common area charges as revenue, making it appear that office revenue is trending above our forecast. However, after factoring for these items, office operations will soon trend behind our forecasts.

REIT office portfolio is 90% occupied

FLI’s subsidiary, (now called) Filinvest REIT, holds 300,000sqm of FLI’s 520,000sqm office portfolio and is slated to be listed in 3Q21. The REIT’s portfolio is 90% occupied and POGOs occupy only 2% of space.

Drop in reservation sales cushioned by house and lot projects

Reservation sales fell 29% YoY (+2% QoQ) to P3.44bn, 70% of the 2019 average. The drop was cushioned by sales of house and lot projects, which grew 18% YoY and now comprise 52% of sales (from 31% in 1Q20). 10% of buyer accounts availed of payment deferrals last year. Fortunately, the company said that these accounts are now current (paying on time).

 
 

Corporate News

Meralco sales volume up in 4M21

Manila Electric Co (MER PM) said in its virtual stockholders meeting that it managed to grow its sales volume by 3% in the first fourth months of the year. As of end April, MER’s energy sales rose 3% to 14,261GWh. The net system input also increased by 2% to 15,002GWh. Additionally, the customer count growth rate was 4%, reaching 7.23mn. For 2020, MER’s energy sales slipped 7% to 43,527GWh while net system input also declined 7% to 45,919GWh.

FGEN confirms interest in Malampaya banked gas

First Gen confirmed that it remains interested to purchase the Malampaya banked gas of the Philippine National Oil Company (PNOC) for use by the company’s gas-fired power plants. They formally expressed interest in 2016 when a bidding was conducted by SC 38. Meanwhile, Phoenix denied any knowledge or interest, in response to a news article that quoted PNOC CEO Lista saying Phoenix, FGEN and other parties as potential contenders.

ICTSI raises stake in Congo port

ICTSI has increased its stake by 10% in ICTSI DRC (IDRC). ICTSI purchased the stake from La Societe D’Investissement et de Placement SARL. This transaction increases ICT’s stake in IDRC to 62% from 52%. No other details disclosed. We estimate IDRC accounts for c. 1% of volumes and 5% of revenues and estimate a 100% equity valuation of IDRC at US$330mn (ICT’s share is equivalent to 3% of NAV). As at end-March 2021, ICT’s gross debt (inc. perps) stood at US$4.5bn and cash at US$450mn; net debt-to-EBITDA at 4.2x.

Robinsons Land to raise up to P26.7bn from REIT listing

Robinsons Land plans to raise up to P26.7b from the sale of secondary shares in RL Commercial REIT. The price will be up to P7.31/share and the deal will have a firm offer of 3.34bn shares with an overallotment option 305.1mn shares. The assets will include 14 office buildings with 425,000sqm of leasable area. This includes offices in Ortigas Center, Makati, Fort Bonifacio, Bridgetowne, Mandaluyong (Robinsons Forum), Cebu, Davao, Naga, and Tarlac with a total appraised value of P73bn. This implies P73bn market cap at the maximum price. Based on the prospectus, at the maximum price, the REIT would offer a dividend yield of 5%, lower than our assigned 7% cap rate. If the REIT were valued at the maximum price, our RLC NAV estimate could rise by 10%-20%. All the buildings are PEZA accredited with an occupancy rate of 95%. They also mentioned that only 3% are occupied by POGOs. The land will be leased from RLC for 99 years. The offer period will be from August 31 to September 6 and will have a listing date of September 15. RLC is trading at 9.7X FY21E P/E, 0.8X FY21E P/B, and 55% discount to NAV.

 
 

Macro News

Pace of daily vaccinations rises further

The average daily COVID vaccines administered rose to 162,513/day . This is coming from a 7-day average of 108,540/day as of May 18. The country has received a total of 8.3mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 4.1mn doses. The government expects at least 7mn vaccine doses to arrive in May (4.3mn of which have already arrived). The country aims to vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.

Filipinos evenly split between being willing / uncertain / unwilling to be vaccinated

Last week, the Social Weather Stations (SWS) released a survey that showed only 32% of Filipinos are “willing” to get vaccinated against Covid-19. The balance was roughly evenly split between those who were “uncertain” (35%) and those who were “unwilling” (33%) to be inoculated. Of the 33% unwilling, the vast majority (26%) replied “surely not”. The main reason for the reluctance was fear of side effects (39% among “uncertain” and 30% among “unwilling”). Other reasons included doubts over the safety/effectivity, and (false) reports of fatalities as a result of taking the vaccine. The same survey showed that only half of Filipinos are “confident” in the thoroughness of the government’s evaluation of the vaccines. The figures are worrisome, as we believe a successful Covid-19 vaccination program is critical to the economy’s recovery. A stronger information / PR campaign will be needed to increase the population’s confidence in getting inoculated.

House committee approves bill creating 3-year development plan for domestic auto industry

The House Committee on Trade Industry approved an unnumbered substitute bill to House Bill 1833 or the proposed Philippine Motor Vehicle Manufacturing Industry Act. The Bill authorizes three-year planning for the industry (National Motor Vehicle Manufacturing Development Plan) to be prepared by the Board of Investments (BoI). The bill also provides for investment incentives for motor vehicle manufacturers registered with the BoI such as income tax holidays, deductions for training expenses, and the opportunity to carry over net operating losses, among others.

Budget deficit narrows to P44bn in April as spending slows

The National Government posted a narrower budget deficit of P44bn in April. The base effects distorted YoY figures due to last year’s hard lockdown and the social amelioration program. MoM, however, the deficit in April was substantially below deficits recorded in Feb and March due to (a) timing of income tax payments and (b) moderation of expenditures. This brings 4M21 deficit to P366bn, accounting for 26% of the government’s year-end deficit target (~9.4% of GDP).

Lower House passes Bayanihan III

The House of Representatives on Tuesday approved on second reading the proposed Bayanihan III, which provides funds amounting to P401bn. The approved version on the plenary floor provided for a slightly lower amount than the initial P405.6bn Bayanihan 3 proposal. Around P216bn will be allocated for the provision of cash aid of P2,000 per Filipino regardless of social status, which will be given in two installments. It will also include a one-time cash subsidy of P5k-10k for pandemic-affected households in areas under Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ). The House Committee on Appropriations earlier approved the measure despite the absence of a certification of availability of funds from the Bureau of the Treasury. A certification is a requirement for special appropriations bills under the Constitution. The proposed Bayanihan 3 is among the lower chamber’s top priorities before it adjourns sine die on June.

House approves economic charter change on second reading

The House of Representatives approved on second reading Resolution of Both Houses (RBH) No. 2. RBH No. 2 seeks to loosen constitutional foreign ownership restrictions on natural resources, public utilities, educational institutions, mass media companies, and advertising companies. The House of Representatives can approve RBH No. 2 on third reading next week. However, the Senate has not yet begun its deliberations on the matter. Should both houses of Congress approve RBH No. 2, a plebiscite would still be necessary.
