REGIS REPORT

16 April 2021

The PSEi inched down 0.8% this week to 6494.81.

Foreigners remained heavy sellers as uncertainty amid higher covid cases and prolonged lockdowns continued to weigh on investors. Foreign outflows hit US$85m this week – the largest weekly outflow since early March. YTD net outflows are now at US$1.1bn. Largest net outflows were seen in Ayala Land (US$28.5m), URC (US$7m) and SM Prime (US$4.8m). 

While the lockdown status for NCR Plus has been slightly eased (to MECQ), infection rates have not declined, suggesting there will not likely be a material upward impact to mobility in the near term.

Meanwhile, the current lockdown  in NCR Plus could mean a fresh wave of loan defaults in the next two quarters. The  banking system’s asset quality deteriorated sharply in February. NPL ratio (ex-interbank loans) jumped to 4.16% from 3.80% in January and 3.70% at end-2020. February NPLs grew 9.1% MoM to P427.4bn. NPL coverage dropped to 87.3% from 94.6% the previous month.

Losses were widespread, with 18 of the 30 index names ending the week down as foreign selling weighed. URC (-9.5% WoW) was the top index decliner this week, followed by the property names – Megaworld (-7.4% WoW), Robinsons Land (-5.0% WoW), Ayala Land (-3.6%).

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
GT Capital - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

More than one way to ride on ‘motorization’ theme

Mr. Carmelo Bautista, President of GT Capital, remains optimistic about sustaining the recovery of its investee companies in the near-to medium-term, despite speed bumps from the pandemic and the imposition of the safeguard duty on vehicle imports. Also among the more interesting topics he covered was the group’s foray into the used car business, a way to capitalize and expand on the group’s leading position in the auto sector.

Safeguard duty unlikely to achieve its purpose

Mr. Bautista didn’t provide a strong view on the outcome of the government’s review of the safeguard duty on imported vehicles, but believes the measure will do more harm than good to the industry. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) is opposing the safeguard duty despite being the largest assembler of vehicles in the country. The imposition of the safeguard duty notwithstanding, management believes that unit sales will be higher this year.

Industry ripe for consolidation?

GTCAP, with its partners, will be launching the country’s first used car online auction platform, alongside an inspection/warranty business in 3Q21. Mr. Bautista shared that the market for used vehicles is much larger than the market for new vehicles. It is, however, fragmented and informal, and thus offers an opportunity for consolidation. He believes the group’s leading position in both auto sales (new) and auto finance provide it with an advantage against other operators.

Cavite land bank: Opportunity for long-term value enhancement and realization

The development masterplan for the 700-hectare landbank in Cavite is still being firmed up but management envisions the property to be a “satellite city”, with mixed use developments that will be accessible to toll road infrastructure. It will first develop 100 hectares with its JV partner this year and the entire development will have direct access points to toll roads such as the Cavite[1]Laguna Expressway. Management expects property values in that area will continue to rise over the medium-to-long-term, as these access points are completed.

Century Pacific Food - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Resilient demand, new legs for growth

CNPF’s Executive Chairman, Mr. Chris Po, believes the company is on track to meet its 2021 growth target (+10-15%) despite 1) coming from a high base in 2020, 2) weak consumer sentiment, and 3) potential input cost inflation. CNPF’s core branded products continue to be essential pantry staples and demand is expected to be resilient as consumers flock to familiar, trusted brands. Longer term, Mr. Po expects to drive growth through continued investments in the growing dairy segment, as well as its entry into the refrigerated meats and plant-based protein segments. The latter is also part of the group’s push towards sustainability.

Demand for core products resilient

Mr. Po expects consumer belt-tightening to persist over the near term but believes the demand for CNPF’s core branded products will be resilient. The company is targeting to grow earnings by +10-15% in 2021 despite near term margin pressure from input cost inflation, as current service levels (c.80%) are still tracking lower than where he believes they should be (>90%).

Dairy: investments to continue

The dairy business continues to be on investment mode. Mr. Po admits that it will take a few more years for the business to achieve margins similar to CNPF’s more established categories. Being a challenger in the space, CNPF needs to push growth through innovations, as well as more aggressive price actions to entice consumers to try its products.

Adding potentially new legs for growth

Mr. Po aims to further drive growth through CNPF’s entry into the refrigerated meats and plant-based protein segments. While both segments have very different industry dynamics, Mr. Po believes the potential for both markets is significant, and success there will depend on how the company is able to position itself and execute.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Universal Robina - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Hitting speedbumps, not roadblocks

URC’s President and CEO Irwin Lee remains optimistic on the company’s long term growth despite short term headwinds from weak consumer spending and high inflation. Its Philippines branded consumer food (BCF) segment has outperformed the broader consumer space amidst the pandemic-related lockdowns – a testament to the strength of its brands and scale. This should serve as a strong foundation for further growth and margin improvement over the medium term. Meanwhile, Mr. Lee believes that there is room to improve top line growth on the international side by adding new legs to each of the markets where it operates.

PH BCF: Gaining ground amidst the crisis

Notwithstanding weak consumer sentiment and input cost pressures, Mr. Lee is optimistic that URC’s PH BCF segment will continue to see steady top line growth and margins over the medium term. Since last year’s lockdown, URC has focused on three key aspects that he believes further solidified their position in the various categories where they operate: 1) the strengthening of relationships with the trade, 2) a healthy pipeline of innovations, and 3) the on-going supply chain transformation.

International BCF: Building more legs

Mr. Lee admits that there is room for improvement on the international side, where URC has “one-legged” stools in many of the various markets where it operates. While management teams in the respective countries have done well to improve margins through cost control and rationalization, Mr. Lee intends to focus more of his own time to further drive top line growth in these markets.

Steady expansion domestically + accelerated growth internationally

The company maintains its longer term strategy to achieve high-single to low[1]double digit top line growth over the medium term. This will be driven by steady growth from PH BCF (mid-single digit), its agro-industrial and commodities businesses, and accelerated growth on the international side. Longer term margin improvement will come from the realization of its on-going supply chain transformation strategy, which Mr. Lee believes will mitigate cyclical pressures from input cost.

 
 

Corp News

Max’s Group (MAXS) FY20 recurring net loss wider than expectations

MAXS reported a net loss of P1.7bn in FY20. Excluding one-offs, we estimate the recurring net loss was P953mn, wider than our FY20E of a P832mn loss. FY20 revenue dropped 50% YoY to P7.14bn, behind expectations (FY20E: P7.6bn). Meanwhile, the reported EBITDA loss was P123mn. For 4Q20 alone, revenues hit P2bn +41% QoQ / -51% YoY. The SSS decline for the quarter was not disclosed, but we estimate it was -33-35%.

Emperador renews buy-back program

Emperador’s board of directors has approved a share buy-back program to repurchase up to P1bn worth of common shares. This is equivalent to 0.6% of current market capitalization. The public float is currently 15.7%. The new buy-back program will run from April 12, 2021 to December 31, 2021. The current buy-back program expires on May 16, 2021 (originally approved in May 2017). Roughly P3.0bn worth of shares have been repurchased under the buy-back program.

PLDT doubles allocation for data packages

PLDT subsidiary Smart launches Double Giga packages, providing double the data allocation for the same price. For example, the new Giga Video 99 package provides 2GB of data + another 2GB/day for specific apps (Youtube, NBA TV, etc) for a 7-day period. In theory, this gives the subscriber 16GB data over 7 days for P99 (US$2), only P6 per GB. The previous packages were for 8GB. The incremental cost to PLDT and value to subscriber will not be proportionate since most users are unlikely to be using more than 1GB per day anyway. Optically, however, it appears to raise the competitive bar significantly.

Megaworld 4Q20 office revenue drops, reservation sales jump

Megaworld’s FY20 net profit fell 45% to P9.9bn, broadly in line with our forecast. 4Q20 earnings rose 24% QoQ (-52% YoY) to P2.5bn. Additionally, 4Q20 office revenue fell 40% QoQ (-36% YoY) to P1.8bn. This was likely due to rental concessions that were recorded in 4Q20. Office vacancy might also have increased (from 6% in 3Q20). FY20 office revenue dropped 1% (9M20 office revenue rose 11%). 4Q20 reservation sales jumped 85% QoQ (-33% YoY)to P23.3bn. The FY20 reservation sales dropped 51% to P73.5bn, ahead of our forecast. MEG is currently trading at 7.9X FY21E P/E and 54% discount to NAV.

Philippine Seven (SEVN) profit rebounds in 4Q20

SEVN’s net loss in FY20 hit P420mn. Revenues were down 18% YoY to P43.6bn due to shortened operating hours, temporary store closures, and weak foot traffic. For 4Q20 alone, profit rebounded to P166mn from losses in the last 2 quarters. Revenues saw a 16% improvement QoQ, though still down 22% YoY given the high base in 4Q19. Operating profit turned positive vs 3Q20 due to operating leverage and the recovery of other income.

DoubleDragon to become a conglomerate

DoubleDragon’s board of directors approved the amendment of its primary purpose to transform it into an investment holding company. The company will be renamed from “DoubleDragon Properties” to “DoubleDragon Corporation”. DoubleDragon Co-Chairman Tony Tan Caktiong said that the company was in an excellent position to add investments outside of the property sector.

Market foreign ownership drops in March

We estimate that total market foreign ownership fell to 21.9% (-10bps MoM, -32bps QoQ) in March. We also estimate that PSEi foreign ownership dropped to 25.1% (-39bps MoM, -63bps QoQ). 26 out of 30 PSEi constituents saw declines in their foreign ownership

TEL – foreign ownership reportedly fell 412 basis points MoM to 38.0%. A block sale registered US$205mn in outflows. We estimate that if TEL’s foreign ownership were unchanged MoM, then the PSEi foreign ownership would be 25.2%

SECB – foreign ownership declined 75 basis points MoM to 38.5%.  Consumer loans were hit hard by COVID. As a result, the bank reported higher NPLs (3.9%) at end-2020, compared to the big 3 banks (2.4% to 2.7%), due to the rapid growth in consumer loans from 2015-2019.

ALI – foreign ownership dropped 55 basis points MoM to 36.1%. It was also the top foreign sold stock in March (-US$54.7mn)

ICT – had the biggest increase in foreign ownership at 20 basis points MoM (to 37.4%) and was also the top foreign bought stock in March (+US$14.8mn). The company sustained volume recovery in 4Q20 and indicated positive sequential trends in 1Q21. Prospects for yields in 2021 also look promising

 
 

Macro News

January FDI rises 42%

Net FDI jumped 41.5% YoY to US$961mn while debt instruments rose by +116% to US$535mn. Equity other than reinvestment of earnings grew +0.5% to US$351mn while reinvestment of earnings dropped -9.2% to US$74mn. Investments mostly came from Singapore, Japan, and Netherlands. Investments were mostly made to financial, manufacturing, and scientific and technical industries.

Infra spending picks up in 2M21

Government spending on infrastructure rose +9% YoY to P51bn and +23% YoY to P56bn in January and February, respectively. The increases were attributed to DPWH payments for completed projects and mobilization costs for new projects this year. The figures exclude LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies. This brings YT-Feb infra spend up 14% YoY to P107bn. The government expects to infra spend to further pick up in March, given the trend of increasing expenditures before the validity of cash allocations end.

Metro Manila office vacancy rises to 11%

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) estimates that Metro Manila office vacancy rose from 9% in 4Q20 to 11% in 1Q21. It estimates that 130,000sqm were vacated in 1Q21 (on a net basis). BPO net incremental take-up was 25,000sqm. Meanwhile POGO had a net incremental vacancy of 118,000sqm. Others (traditional) offices had net incremental vacancy of 37,000sqm. LPC also said that office rental rates are mostly 12% lower than 2019 levels while rental rates to POGOs are down 30%. We forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 12% in 2021E (and stay at 12% in 2022E). We also expect office rental rates for PEZA accredited office space to drop c. 10% this year, bringing rents c. 20% below 2019 levels. We expect rental rates in the Manila Bay area to drop c. 15% this year, bringing rents c. 35% below 2019 levels. Office leasing accounts for 74% of Filinvest Land’s NAV and 52% of Megaworld’s NAV. FLI is trading at 5.4X FY21E P/E and 63% discount to NAV. MEG is trading at 8.4X FY21E P/E and 51% discount to NAV.

Gov’t eases quarantine status for Metro Manila, neighboring provinces to MECQ

The government downgraded the quarantine status of Metro Manila and adjacent provinces to the less stringent Modified Enhance Community Quarantine (MECQ). MECQ will be applied to Metro Manila, Cavite, Bulacan, Laguna, and Rizal from April 12 to April 30. Additionally, curfew was shortened to 8pm to 5am from 6pm to 5am previously.

Trade gap widens in February

The country’s trade-in-goods deficit widened to US$2.3bn in February. Imports rose 3% YoY, the first time in 22 months; However, imports were lower by 12% MoM while exports dropped 2% YoY, 4% MoM.

500,000 more Sinovac doses arrive

The Philippines received 500,000 doses of Sinovac vaccine. The country has received a total of 3mn COVID-19 vaccine doses. As of April 6, the Philippines has administered a total of 922,898 doses. The break down of doses are as follows: 872,213 already had their first dose while 50,685 have had their third dose. This brings the 7-day average to 22,932 doses per day.

BIR collections down 17% MoM in March; BOC collections jump

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collected P127.4bn in March (+3% YoY, -17% MoM). This was short of BIR’s target of P146bn for the month due to tighter lockdown restrictions. Additionally, this brings 1Q21 collections to P463bn, down 1% YoY and trailing the government’s FY21E of P2.081tn (+7% YoY). The Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P54.5bn in March (+22% YoY, +16% MoM). It exceeded its collection goal of P47.7bn for the month

1Q21 BOC collections reached P149bn, up 2.5% YoY and tracking within the government’s FY21E of P620bn (+15% YoY).

February NPL ratio highest in 11 years

The nonperforming loan (NPL) ratio rose to 4.08% in February, highest since October 2009 (4.09%). Gross NPLs surged 80% to P431bn (+9.05% MoM). Meanwhile, NPL coverage ratio dropped to 86.64% from 91.2% last year. The past due loans in February reached P551.472bn (+71% YoY), bringing the past due loan ratio to 5.21% from 2.95% a year ago. Restructured loans also surged by more than six times (346% YoY) to P200.986bn. With this, its ratio reached 1.9%, up from the 0.41% in February last year.

Vehicle sales fall 21% MoM in March

Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, fell 21% MoM to 20,702 units (+88% YoY). MoM drop due to the imposition of provisional safeguard duties on imports and renewed lockdown restrictions. This brings 1Q21 industry car sales up 9% YoY to 70,312 units. CAMPI expects industry sales to grow by +20-25% YoY in 2021, slower than its initial projection of +30-35% YoY due to the provisional safeguard duty. Toyota sold 9,201 units, down 30% MoM but up 103% YoY. Its market share dropped 6ppts MoM to 44%. Meanwhile, Toyota’s 1Q21 retail sales are up 29% YoY to 33,095. Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year. To reach our forecast, Toyota will need to sell c. 9,400 units per month. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales should average 7,900 units. Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 13% discount to its market-based NAV of P604/sh.

Duterte certifies three economic bills as urgent

President Rodrigo R. Duterte certified as urgent three bills aimed at easing foreign investment restrictions in the country. The three bills are the Public Service Act (PSA), Foreign Investments Act (FIA) and the Retail Trade Liberalization (RTL) Act. The Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) expects the bills to be passed before Congress adjourns in June. Congress is currently in recess and will resume session on May 17 until June 4. An urgent certification from the President means these bills can be passed on the third reading immediately after the second reading. President Duterte will not certify as urgent the proposed third stimulus law and will instead work to identify sources of stimulus funds that will not bust the 8.9% budget deficit cap.

OFW remittances rise 5% in February

OFW remittances rose 5% to US$2.47bn in February. In PHP terms, remittances fell slightly (-0.2% YoY). This brings the 3-month moving average at +0.8% YoY and brings YT-Feb remittances to US$5.08bn (+1.5%). The BSP expects remittances to rise by 4% this year, after dipping -0.8% in FY20.

President Duterte lifts moratorium on mining projects

President Duterte lifted the nine-year moratorium on new mineral agreements, in a bid to boost revenues as the country recovers from the pandemic. The Executive Order (EO) No. 130 allows the government to enter into new mineral agreements and review existing mining deals for possible renegotiation. The Finance department and DENR were ordered to implement measures to “rationalize existing revenue-sharing schemes and mechanisms. Mining accounts for c. 1% of GDP or approximately 5% of exports.
