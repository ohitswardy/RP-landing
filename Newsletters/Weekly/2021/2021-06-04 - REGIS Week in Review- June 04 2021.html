REGIS REPORT

04 June 2021

The PSEi  ended the week 1.8% higher at just below 6800 index points. The market’s move was driven by a shift in flows, with fundamentals on the ground remaining largely unchanged.

This week, President Duterte announced that NCR Plus would  stay under the current quarantine status “GCQ with restrictions” until mid-month. Some 11.3m vaccines are expected to arrive in June and 15-20m a month starting next month. This would be a drastic increase from the 8.3m the country has received since March.

Meanwhile, the government is also allowing leisure travel from NCR Plus to areas with more eased lockdown conditions.  Cebu Air (+6.3% WoW) bounced this week.

Foreigners were net buyers for US$108m this week, but was bloated by 2GO and SMPH blocks. If we exclude these blocks, foreigners were still net buyers by US$36m -- the first weekly inflow (ex blocks) since early February.  Biggest inflows were focused in big caps SM Prime (+0.3% WoW), Ayala Land (+5.5% WoW), BDO (+5.1% WoW),  Metrobank (+0.4% WoW), and  Ayala Corp (+3.5% WoW), and. Leading instant noodle and biscuit maker Monde Nissin Corp had its maiden listing this week.

On the corporate side, ICTSI (+1.1% WoW) purchased a 100% stake in Manila Harbour Centre (MHC), the sole operator for international bulk and breakbulk cargoes at the port of Manila. The port sits on 10 ha of land and will give ICTSI an even more dominant control over the waterfront of the City of Manila. The acquisition price of P2.45bn looks reasonable at an implied valuation of 8.3x EV/EBITDA.

Also within the group, Enrique Razon has instituted changes in the management / board following his takeover of Manila Water (3.2% WoW). Razon will assume the CEO position and a number of his close aides are joining the team.

Semirara (+4.9% WoW) rose this week. Selling 20% of capacity to spot,  Semirara has the highest exposure to the sector which is now experiencing supply tightness and elevated WESM prices.

It appears  a bill imposing a 5% tax on the gross gaming revenue (GGR) of offshore gaming operators  is close to becoming law after Senate’s approval this week.  The bill imposes a 5% tax on the gross gaming revenue (GGR) of POGOs – lower than the previous 5% of drop based Bayanihan 2. This more stable tax framework should be beneficial for POGOs and hence the developers who are exposed to POGOs such as Filinvest Land (+1.9% WoW) and Megaworld (+2.6% WoW), as well as developers with large exposure to the Manila Bay area, such as SM Prime.

Meanwhile, SM Investments (+2.0% WoW) completed its purchase of 22% of shipping and logistics firm 2GO (+9.3% WoW) from Dennis Uy for P4.7bn.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Property - 1Q21 recap: Mall profits and residential sales improve but FY outlook worsens
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Near-term outlook for every segment worsens

While mall profitability and residential sales improved sequentially in 1Q21, the recent hard lockdown will likely lengthen the road to recovery for both segments. Meanwhile, office vacancy has increased further and BPOs appear hesitant to expand their office footprint meaningfully near-term due to the slow pace of vaccinations in the country.

Office vacancy climbs further; POGOs closing, BPOs waiting to expand

Leechiu Property Consultants estimates that (on a net basis) companies (mostly POGOs) vacated 130,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. As a result, office vacancy rose from 9% in 4Q20 (4% in 2019) to 11% in 1Q21. Colliers estimates that rental rates fell 2%-5% QoQ. Thus, rental rates in most areas are 5%-25% lower than 2019 levels. In the Manila Bay area, which has a high concentration of POGOs, rental rates are 30%-40% lower than 2019 levels. The BPO sector expanded its office footprint mildly. However, while a number of BPOs want to take up more office space, they want to do so when there have been more vaccinations in the country. (see “BPOs waiting to expand”).

Mall revenue recovers due to minimum rents but 2Q21 profits will fall

Mall revenue is still down c. 45% YoY (in aggregate) and foot traffic even declined from c. 45% in 4Q20 to c. 40% of the 2019 average. However, mall revenue recovered QoQ due to the reimposition of minimum rents (on some tenants). Although only 10% of the pre-COVID minimum was reinstituted, this helped mall revenue of the major mall operators to recover by a range of 10% to 25% QoQ. However, the reimposition of a hard lockdown from end-March until mid-May prompted mall operators to waive minimum rents again. Thus, we should expect a substantial deterioration in 2Q21 profitability.

Reservation sales improve but Manila condo prices still dropping, NPLs rising

While the performance was mixed across companies, combined reservation sales of the developers we cover grew 15% QoQ and 10% YoY. Aggregate reservation sales have risen for the third consecutive quarter, with sales of provincial projects generally outpacing those within Metro Manila. However, our channel checks suggest most offer prices for Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) fell slightly QoQ (within 5%). This brought prices of most units 10%-20% lower than 2019 levels, with a few units being offered at prices that are 20%-30% lower. In the primary market, some units are priced up to 10% lower than 2019 levels (due to discounts). The banking industry mortgage NPL ratio also rose to 9.2% in 4Q20, a 15-year high. Thus, the risk of residential defaults remains high.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Puregold - Multiples near all-time lows; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Share price underperformance discounts potential corporate governance concerns

PGOLD’s share price has underperformed the PCOMP by 25% over the past 12 months due in part to the protracted recovery of its core market (lower income households), compounded by stock’s deletion from a widely followed international benchmark. We are upgrading our 2021- 22E EPS estimates by 12-15% due to higher gross margins and lower tax rates following recent results, and we have revised our target price to P42/sh, 14x PER for 2021E. While there are understandable concerns over a recent related party transaction, we think current multiples (near all-time lows) are now attractive relative to the value of its otherwise healthy underlying operations.

K-shaped recovery across brands

The divergent SSSG trends between PGOLD’s two brands highlight the K-shaped recovery of the sector, with lower income households seeing a more protracted rebound as mobility remains limited and rising food inflation puts further pressure on incomes. Meanwhile, the demand from S&R’s target market (mid to upper income households) remains resilient and these consumers are not as limited in terms of mobility.

Margin tailwind in 2021

Both Puregold and S&R brands posted surprisingly wide gross margins in 1Q21. While we expect margins to narrow in succeeding quarters, we expect them to still be better on a full year basis. For Puregold, trade support and increased marketing spend from suppliers are rebounding after lagging for most of 2020. Meanwhile, S&R is benefitting from the favorable forex.

Adjusting TP to P42/sh

While we are upgrading our 2021-22E earnings estimates by 12-15%, we are adjusting our target price to P42/sh (-11%). Our target price represents 2021E multiples of 14x PE and 7x EV/EBITDA, a discount to its historical average and the current average of its regional peers. Key downside risks relate to potential and perceived conflicts of interest within the Lucio Co Group, as well as a protracted recovery in consumption, which could pull down top line growth further.

 
 

Corporate News

PLDT prepares international capacity surge with submarine cable system

PLDT (TEL PM) plans to increase its international capacity by five times by end of the year through the Jupiter submarine telecommunications cable system. The project would increase its international capacity to almost 60 terabits from the current 10 terabit capacity running on its 15 major international cable systems. PLDT said it aims to hep make the Philippines a hub connecting Southeast Asia to United States as a draw for firms expanding loud and network offerings. In 2017, it said it would investment P7bn in a Trans-Pacific cable system. In April, Converge ICT Solutions (CNVRG PM) announced plans to invest P4.8bn in the Bifrost Cable System, a 15,000km undersea cable project from Singapore to the North American West Coast including a branch to the Philippines.

PNB to generate P34bn from monetization of real estate

Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) is set to unlock P34bn in capital through extraordinary gains from the spinoff of its interest of its three most valuable property assets into a real estate unit. PNB disclosed that the transaction would raise P10bn in fresh capital after the issuance of property dividends to stockholders. Plan earlier announced to exchange three prime properties for an equivalent value in shares of stock of PNB Holdings Corp. As a result, the bank will be able to record about P34bn in its books reflecting the difference between the fair value of P46.677bn and the book value of P12.6bn of the properties exchanged for shares. PNB president Wick Veloso said after generating P34bn in additional capital, property dividend declaration would cost around P24bn, leaving p10bn in fresh capital. The change will be reflected in PNB’s income statement as of May 28, 2021.

ICTSI buys Manila Harbour Centre Port Services for P2.45bn

ICTSI acquired 100% of Manila Harbour Centre Port Services Inc. (MHC) from a related party, Prime Strategic Hldgs. MHC owns a 10-hectare land and runs the largest (monopoly) international breakbulk and bulk operation at the port of Manila. It recently secured a Permit to operate (valid until 2027). Total consideration of P2.45bn implies an EV/EBITDA valuation of 8.3x, lower than previous acquisition of Manila North Harbour (14-15x) and ICTSI’s current valuation (10x FY21E). MHC generated c. P1bn of revenues in 2020 and just slightly below 50% EBITDA margin; We estimate the port would add c. 1% to ICTSI’s consolidated revenues and EBITDA. With the acquisition, ICTSI now controls most of the port infrastructure at the port of Manila.

Globe on track to install 1mn fiber lines this year

Globe Telecom said Tuesday that it is on track too install an additional 1 million fiber-to-the-home lines by end of 2021 as part of its P70bn capex program this year. All remaining copper lines will be upgraded to fiber to ensure better data experience and connection.

Razon ties up with Panlilios for resort development

Panlilio-led Boulevard Holdings said it is partnering with Razon to develop a world class resort in Cavite. The disclosure said that JP Guilds (Panlilio-led company) leads the minority consortium of private land holding owners while the major private partner is Razon. BHI will provide an additional 30 hectares of Cavite landbank on top of the 27 hectares it had already committed to the project.

ABS-CBN gets reprieve from lenders

ABS-CBN Corp said it signed a standstill agreement with lenders, not to declare any event of default or to exercise any rights or remedies under existing loan agreements. This is contingent on the company’s compliance with the lenders’ condition of the creation of a mortgage and security interest over certain assets of the company. ABS-CBN had total debt of over P21bn as of 2020, including debt of subsidiaries SkyCable and ABS-CBN International.

SM Investments purchases additional 22% stake in 2GO

SM Investments purchased a 22.36% stake in 2GO (from the Udenna Group) at P8.50/share (P4.7bn). This brought SM’s stake in 2GO to 52.85%. Trident Investments, which is controlled by Archipelago Capital, acquired a 31.73% stake, making it the second largest shareholder. Mr. Frederic Dy Buncio, President and CEO of 2G0 and SM Investments, was also appointed as Chairman of the Board of Directors. The following also joined the Board or Directors of 2G0. Mr. Stephen Ly – served as the Managing Director of DHL Global Forwarding-Singapore from 2015 to 2020; Mr. Chan Kiat – Managing Director/Partner of Archipelago Capital Partners since 2016; Mr. Sam Ang Sing Mein – was the CEO of DHL Global Forwarding Southeast Asia from 2006 to 2015.

Converge buys PLDT’s Luzon cable landing stations

Converge ICT Solutions (CNVRG PM) announced that it had signed separate deals with PLDT’s Digital Telecommunications Phils to buy its stake in Digitel Crossing Inc and Asia Netcome Philippines Corp for $4.84mn and $2.66mn, respectively. Digitel Crossing maintains and operates the cable landing stations in the Philippines for EAC and C2C (City-to-City) cable systems. Asia Netcom Philippines is also a shareholder of Digitel Crossing.

Razon undertakes board, management changes at Manila Water

Enrique Razon is now the Chairman, President and CEO of Manila Water (MWC PM). Fernando Zobel stepped down as Chairman but will remain as a board director. Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala has fully exited his previous vice chairman and board director positions. Rene Almendras also stepped down from his role of President but will remain as a non-executive director. Razon brought his own team including nephew, Global Corporate Head of ICTSI Group Christian Gonzalez and ICTSI CFO Rafael Consing. Details on respective positions were not disclosed. Mr. Razon now has a 34% economic stake and 51% voting stake in Manila Water. Ayala Corp.’s economic stake is now down to 31% (from 52% previously) while its voting stake is now at 32% (from 81% previously).

San Miguel Food & Beverage to spend P30bn for feed mills this year

San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM) CEO Ramon Ang said in its stockholders meeting that it has earmarked P30bn for feed mill expansion this year. The amount will be spent to expand feed mill in Isabela, San Ildefonso, Davao and Cebu, and build 7 new feed mills. FB trades at 26.4x FY21E PE.

 
 

Macro News

NCR plus, other areas under ‘GCQ with restrictions’ in June

President Duterte announced the NCR Plus would be under GCQ “with restrictions” from June 1 to 15. NCR Plus comprises of Metro Manila, Rizal, Bulacan, Cavite, and Laguna which has also been under this quarantine status since May 15. Metro Manila mayors earlier recommended allowing venues of meetings and conferences to accommodate more people but it is not clear yet if the President has approved this.

Bank lending shrinks 5% in April

Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks contracted by 5% YoY in April (vs. -4.5% YoY in March). This is the steepest decline seen since May 2004. Meanwhile, money supply growth slowed to 5.1% in April from 8.3% in March.

POGO tax bill certified as urgent

President Duterte certified Senate Bill (SB) 2232 as urgent. SB 2232 would require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue. This would be in addition to the 2% of GGR paid to PAGCOR. POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P120bn in tax revenue over four years. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover and could slow down POGO closures.

Arrival of private sector-procured AstraZeneca vaccines to be delayed

Presidential Adviser for Entrepreneurship Joey Concepcion said that 1.17mn AstraZeneca doses scheduled for arrival in late June will be delivered in July instead. This means that the country will only receive 11.3mn doses in June vs a previous target of 12.5mn in June. The shipments of COVID vaccines to the Philippines are scheduled to increase to 15 to 20mn doses/month starting July. The country has received a total of 8.3mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 5.2mn doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021. Presidential spokesperson Harry Roque also said that more vaccines will be provided to areas experiencing surges in COVID-19 cases.

National Grid Corp warns of rotational blackouts

The National Grid Corp of the Philippines (NGCP) raised the red alert warning on the Luzon power grid from 2pm to 4pm as few days ago. A yellow alert notice was up from 11am to 2pm and 4pm to 5pm on the same day. A red alert means there is severe power deficiency which could lead to rotational power interruptions. A yellow alert means there were not enough reserves to cover the largest running generating unit but does not necessarily lead to power outages. Energy Secretary Alfonso Cusi said supply was severely affected by several power plants on outage despite demand being well within forecasted levels. Some 3,771MW were off the grid on Monday due to outages including scheduled maintenance: 647MW Sual unit 2; 345MW GNP Mariveles; 300MW Sem Calaca Unit 2; 484MW (out of 1,200MW) Ilijan due to limited gas supply. NGCP implemented manual load dropping to  maintain a balance system in several provinces during the red alert period. Meralco experienced supply deficiency of 163MW and 95MW from 1-2PM and 2-3PM, respectively, which led to manual load dropping within its franchise.

Gov't allows leisure travel from 'NCR Plus' to MGCQ areas

The government is allowing non-essential domestic travel from NCR Plus to areas under general community quarantine (GCQ) and modified GCQ regardless of age from June 1 to 15. Local government units (LGUs) still have the final word if they would allow tourism in their areas. The eased requirements will only apply to point-to-point travel and will only accept travelers with negative RT-PCR tests.

NGCP warns of rotational blackouts for rest of the week

The National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP) warned that rotational power outages experienced on Tuesday may continue in the days to come if shut power plants do not switch on as scheduled. NGCP foresees red alert status from June 2-4 and on June 7. The other day’s red alert was due to a deficit of 185MW, with available supply of 11,408MW vs projected peak of 11,593MW. GNPower Mariveles Unit 2 (316MW) is expected to be back online on June 8.

BOC exceeds May collection target

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P49.3bn against its collection target of P46.3bn. The May data included P50mn from import duties and taxes of state-owned firms (“Tax Expenditure Fund”) and P86.26mn from post clearance audit operations. This figure is 60% YoY higher but down by 5% MoM. This brings 5M21 collections to P249.8bn (+19% YoY), making up 40.5% of the 2021 target collection of P616.8bn (+15% YoY).

Senate approves POGO tax bill

The Senate approved on third reading a bill on POGO taxes. SB 2232 would require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue in lieu of all other taxes. Additionally, POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P120bn in tax revenue over four years. The House Ways and Means Chairman Joey Salceda said that the House of Representatives might adopt the Senate version rather than proceed to Bicameral Conference. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. Thus, the new bill could slow down POGO closures.

Duterte orders nat'l gov't, LGUs: Prepare for full devolution of basic services

President Rodrigo Duterte issued an executive order (no. 138) telling national government agencies to fully transfer the task of delivering basic services to local governments by the year 2024. The EO will provide local governments with more funds (at least 50% more) starting 2022. To prepare for the “phased” devolution, all national government agencies and local governments will prepare “Devolution Transition Plans” within 120 days from the effectivity of the EO. A Growth Equity Fund will be included in the 2022 Budget and proposed by the Committee on Devolution to Congress.
