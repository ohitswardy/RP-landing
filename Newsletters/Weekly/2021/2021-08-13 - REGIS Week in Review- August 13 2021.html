REGIS REPORT

13 August 2021

The PSEi closed the week down 3.4% WoW to close at 6320.19.  The market had been up close to 2% as of Wed (WTD) but a steep drop at the close on Friday wiped out earlier gains.

The  PSEi rebal on Friday, as expected, led to much volatility at the close.  The big drop at close was caused by the downweights in the index. Despite minimal downweights, index heavyweights SM (-5.3% WoW), SM Prime  (-1.2% WoW),  JG Summit (-9.8% WoW),  Ayala Land (-1.9% WoW),  Aboitiz Equity (-8.8% WoW), and ICTSI (-7.7% WoW) saw drops of 3-7% on Friday due to lack of liquidity as investors focused on the index additions and deletions.   Converge (+2.6% WoW) and AC Energy (+2.7% WoW) were added to the index while DMC (+3.0% WoW) and Emperador (-10.3% WoW) were deleted.

Headline net inflows stood at US$12.4m but this was mostly on account of the Filinvest REIT IPO.  Excluding this, foreigners were net sellers for US$361k.

The market also priced in a possible extension (from Aug 20) to the Enhanced Community Quarantine placed over the National Capital Region as cases continued to spike. The number of new daily infections has climbed to over 12,000.

An estimated twenty-nine percent (29%) of the ~14m population of Metro Manila has now been fully vaccinated.  The government is aiming to take the 29% figure to 45% by next week. Meanwhile, another 13% have received at least one dose. On a national scale, 11% have been fully vaccinated while 13% have received at least one dose.

The government reported that 2Q GDP growth was 11.8%, after a 17% plunge in 2Q last year at the height of the lockdowns. Seasonally adjusted GDP for 2Q21 actually contracted 1.3% QoQ, however. With economic output in 2Q worse than 1Q, concerns turned to 3Q economic activity given the hard lockdown in place  in August.  

The Dept of Trade (DTI), as expected,  ordered the removal of the safeguard duty on vehicle imports, paving the way for vehicle prices to drop back to where they were early this year. Regis estimates the safeguard duty represented between 4-12% of the retail prices of the vehicles affected. The duty affected ~50% of GTCAP’s (-1.5% WoW) 51% owned Toyota Motor Philippines' sales.

It was generally quiet on the corporate side outside of the spate of 2Q results.

Consumer results were mixed.  Restaurants Jollibee (-3.4% WoW) and Shakey’s (-1.0% WoW) are still tracking around 25-50% below pre-pandemic sales given the lockdowns in Mar-April in the Phils.  For Max’s (+0.8% WoW)  Regis estimates sales are even lower (30-35%) relative to pre-covid levels given renewed dine-in restrictions.

Monde (-1.0% WoW) continued to see a resilient topline in 2Q, despite coming off a high base from pantry stocking last year.  Puregold’s (-6.2% WoW) sales meanwhile showed continued pressure for key customers of the core Puregold brand (lower-income consumers) while S&R’s SSSG was healthy despite the high base in the previous year.

Conglos were also a mixed bag with DMCI (+3.0% WoW) reporting exceptionally strong numbers (core profit up more than threefold) thanks to its coal mining operations of Semirara (-2.9% WoW).  Provisions at PNB (+1.0% WoW) led to a 2Q loss for LT Group (-5.4% WoW) even as tobacco’s contribution came broadly in-line. Cosco’s (+0.2% WoW) Liquor Distribution, whose vols jumped 74% in 2Q, is trending well ahead of FY21 estimates and already back to pre-pandemic levels.

Ayala Corp (-2.3% WoW) reported 2Q earnings double of last year’s depressed levels.  QoQ, however, earnings were surprisingly down.  IMI dipped back into the red in 2Q, while the losses at the auto business widened.

More details and more companies reporting results below.

Megaworld Corp. - 2Q21 office occupancy flat despite POGO closures; REIT could be a catalyst
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment disappoints

2Q21 net profit jumped 39% YoY to P2.6bn. This brought 1H21 earnings to P5.0bn (-7%), behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. While the office segment was broadly in line, residential revenue and presales both underperformed.

Reservation sales fall QoQ and YoY despite strong lot sales

While most other developers saw large increases YoY, Megaworld’s 2Q21 reservation sales fell 3% YoY (-20% QoQ) to P16.5bn, only 44% of the 2019 quarterly average. 1H21 reservation sales were flat at P37.3bn, trailing behind our forecast. This is despite the doubling of horizontal projects (mostly lots in provinces adjacent to Metro Manila) from 15% of sales to 30%. Management previously hinted that it might raise its P70bn presales guidance but decided to maintain it. With the new hard lockdown in August, it will be difficult for the company to achieve our P89bn presales forecast for the year.

Office occupancy flat QoQ despite POGO closures

2Q21 office revenue fell 5% YoY as occupancy fell 5ppt YoY to 90%. POGOs vacated space and now comprise only 5% of the office portfolio (from 9% in 1Q21). Fortunately, Megaworld signed up new BPO tenants and was thus able to maintain the occupancy level QoQ. Management said that rental rates on new office contracts have generally been flat from pre-COVID levels.

SEC approves office REIT IPO; could be a catalyst for MEG

The SEC recently approved the IPO of MEG’s office REIT. The REIT’s 225,000sqm of PEZA-accredited office space (16% of MEG’s total portfolio) is 93% occupied. MEG could raise as much as P27bn from the sale of secondary shares. At the maximum price, the REIT will have a market cap of P56bn and will offer 4% dividend yield, implying a higher valuation than our assigned 7% cap rate. At that price, our MEG NAV estimate could rise by 9%.

Megawide - The worst appears over; Upgrade to Hold
 
 
by Klyne Resullar Research Analyst +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Not as expensive, but still tread carefully

MWIDE’s shares have lost 63% of its value since the beginning of 2019, underperforming the PCOMP by 56% over the same period. The shares no longer look as expensive as they did, in our view, trading at 14x 2022E EV/EBITDA, now below historical average (16x). However, we believe (1) the airport unit could take time to recover to pre-pandemic levels and (2) liquidity risks for that division have not been dispelled. We upgrade our rating to Hold from Sell.

Gradual path to recovery

We recast our FY21E/FY22E profit estimates to account for the accretive impact of (a) a higher construction order book and (b) a lower tax rate due to CREATE. Among the key assumptions for our revised forecasts are (1) that domestic and international pax volumes at the airport unit to revert to pre-pandemic levels sometime within 2022 and 2024, respectively, as well as (2) there is no further deterioration in office/retail vacancy rates at its landport unit.

Liquidity risks remain for the airport unit

The company was successful at restructuring the debt at its airport unit and has successfully raised new financing at the parent level from a pref share issuance last year. However, we are still concerned that the airport unit will struggle to service its debt in 2022 without (1) additional support from its shareholders and/or (2) another round of debt restructuring.

Revised 12-month price target at P7.20/sh

We raise our NAV-based price target by 27% to P7.20/sh to account for an improved outlook (e.g. lower tax rates, a higher order book), and a rolling forward of our DCF analysis. While there is upside due to its underlying intrinsic value, we believe risks abound due to the debt burden at its airport business, which was hit particularly hard by the pandemic. Upside/downside risks: recovery of airport pax volumes, acquisitions, and macroeconomic/political risks.

Steady top line

The active user base is up 2.8x YoY. GCash now claims more active users than even Instagram, which is remarkable considering Filipinos are among the most active on social media.

Gross transaction value (GTV) on the app is on track to exceed P3tn for 2021 with 10m transactions daily.

Users have now invested P9bn (US$180m) in GSave accounts

1H21 net loss at P13.79bn, wider than expectations

CEB reported a net loss of P13.79bn in 1H21, wider than expectations as the passenger volume recovery is not happening as soon as we anticipated. It is worth noting, however, that despite the challenging environment, CEB was able to reduce costs QoQ and trim EBITDA losses further in 2Q21. Management is also actively negotiating with its suppliers to bring costs down further.

Gains market share in 2Q21, but in a small market

Despite another lockdown implemented last April, 2Q21 pax vols improved 16% QoQ, amidst a capacity improvement of 9% QoQ. Management believes capacity can double by year-end if travel restrictions are eased and inoculations are successful.

Costs contained and will likely be reduced further

Cash costs per ASK dropped 7% QoQ due to (a) deferral of maintenance expenses (due to low utilization), (b) absence of admin fees related to fundraising exercises, (c) successful negotiation of booking fees with providers. Management believes more cost reduction initiatives can still be felt in succeeding periods, after call centers were closed and other digitalization efforts materialize. Cash burn steady at P1.5bn per month, but aiming to bring down further CEB’s monthly cash burn rate stood at P1.5bn per month in 1H21. Management, however, is still in active negotiations with its providers to hopefully reduce the cash burn rate by a further 20%. It also aims to lower its fleet cost by 25-30% by deferring aircraft deliveries once again. It also helps that the industry has more appetite for aircraft purchases this year.

2Q21: core Puregold brand remains under pressure; S&R resilient

We estimate the core Puregold brand saw a 12% decline in SSS in 2Q, partly due to the elevated base last year. We estimate same-store sales are 5-10% below pre-pandemic levels, which management attributed to continued pressure on its walk-in customers (45-50% of pre-pandemic sales), whose mobility and spending power have been limited by tighter lockdown restrictions. Management noted that sales from its Tindahan ni Aling Puring and Puregold Perks customers are already at pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, S&R’s SSSG was resilient despite the high base, growing 4% YoY (estimate) during the quarter.

Trade spending drives margin expansion, offset by pre-opening expenses

Despite the soft top line trend, it was the core Puregold brand that drove much of the margin expansion in 2Q21. The recovery of trade spending and supplier support led to a 270bps YoY jump in gross margin. This was partly offset by pre[1]opening expenses relating to S&R’s new store opened in July.

Margin trends to be sustained despite disruptions from new lockdown

Management expects to sustain current margin trends in the coming quarters despite the tightening of lockdown measures in August. For the Puregold brand stores, the company expects to see proportionately more trade spending as we approach the seasonally strong 4Q. Meanwhile, S&R margins should normalize upward as its new stores ramp up operations.

Eagle Cement 1H21 profit rises 180% YoY to P3.69bn

Eagle Cement’s 2Q21 profit rises almost 16-fold YoY, +15% QoQ to P1.97bn. Revenues surged +317% YoY in 2Q21, with volumes up +334% YoY. Management said that Eagle’s ASPs now the lowest it’s ever been. 2Q21 EBITDA jumped 544% YoY to P2.48bn and brings 1H21 profit up 180% YoY to P3.69bn.

PGOLD 1H21 net profit in line at P4bn (+17% YoY) - margins ahead even as sales fall short

In a disclosure, PGOLD reported 1H21 net profit of P3.99bn, up 17% YoY. This is broadly in line with Regis and consensus estimates. Net sales (-7% YoY to P76.1bn) is falling short of expectations, though operating margin (8.1%) appears ahead. For 2Q21 alone, net sales were down 6% YoY. The core Puregold brand saw a 12% decline in sales – which is largely expected given the high base from pantry stocking last year. S&R sales remain resilient (+6% YoY) despite the high base. SSSG was not disclosed. Operating margin in 2Q21 expanded both QoQ and YoY. This was attributed to “strategic cost controls”, but we believe it is also likely due to the change in the revenue mix (eg higher-margin S&R).

Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) to pay down majority of remaining debt

MONDE plans to pay down majority of its remaining term loans of P15.6bn using IPO proceeds that were previously earmarked for capex. The company expects to realize savings of c.P700mn in interest payments. MONDE confirms that there is no change in its capex program to spend P26.5bn over the next 3 years.

Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) net profit +19% YoY; Liquor Distribution sees sharp recovery in 2Q

COSCO reported 1H21 net profit (after minorities) of P2.9bn, up 19% YoY. By segment - PGOLD came in broadly within expectations, while the Real Estate segment underperformed. The Liquor Distribution was the key outperformer, with 1H21 net profit jumping 68% YoY to P709mn. This is trending well above our FY21 forecasts (FY21E: P1.1bn). Liquor volumes for the quarter jumped 74% YoY to 709k cases. This brings YTD cases sold to 1.7mn, tracking ahead of pre-pandemic levels.

EMP 2Q profit +44%QoQ to P3.0bn

EMP’s 2Q profit jumped 44%QoQ to P3.0bn, buoyed by a sharp expansion in GPM particularly for brandy. The 1H profits increased 53%YoY to P5.084bn. Excluding the effect of a one-time tax adjustment at WMG, management estimates that 1H21 profit would have been P5.75bn (+73%YoY). WMG income fell 29%QoQ; without the tax adjustment, income would have been 63% higher QoQ. Although margins were just slightly lower, the continued growth in off-trade and e-commerce sales lifted revenues 30% higher to P5.1bn. Brandy’s profits jumped 82%QoQ to P2.492bn, as GPM expanded to 45.3% from 28.2% in 1Q21. Management cited lower spend on A&P, handling, trade promos and general opex. We estimate domestic volumes fell by low double digit, compared to GSMI’s 25%QoQ decline and Tanduay’s +7%QoQ.

CEB 2Q21 net loss shrinks further

1H21 P13.79bn net loss, behind estimates.  Cebu Air (CEB PM) released its 17-Q. Cumulative 1H21 net loss stood at P13.79bn, trending BELOW Regis and consensus on disappointing topline. There are signs, however, that cost-cutting efforts have been sustained. 2Q21 EBITDA loss shrinks QoQ on slight volume improvement and lower costs. 2Q21 net loss stood at P6.47bn, down 12% QoQ. EBITDA loss was also 31% lower QoQ, as pax volumes improved 18% QoQ. Despite higher fuel costs, cash costs dropped 8% QoQ on lower maintenance and admin expenses.

PSE approves RLC’s REIT IPO

Robinsons Land plans to raise up to P26.7b from the sale of secondary shares in RL Commercial REIT. The ticker wil be RCR and have a price of up to P7.31/share. This implies a P73bn market cap at the maximum price. There will be a firm offer of 3.34bn shares with an overallotment option of 305.1mn shares. The RCR will be composed of 14 office buildings –425,000sqm of leasable area. This includes offices in Ortigas Center, Makati, Fort Bonifacio, Bridgetowne, Mandaluyong (Robinsons Forum), Cebu, Davao, Naga, and Tarlac with a total appraised value of P73bn. Based on the prospectus, at the maximum price, the REIT would offer a dividend yield of 5%, lower than our assigned 7% cap rate. If the REIT were valued at the maximum price, our RLC NAV estimate could rise by 7%. All the buildings are PEZA accredited and have an occupancy rate of 99% with 3% occupied by POGOs. RLC is trading at 9.0X FY22E P/E, 0.7X FY22E P/B, and 60% discount to NAV.

MAXS 2Q21 - margins rebound despite revenue shortfall

On a recurring basis, Max’s Group (MAXS PM) reported net profit of P15mn, behind Regis estimates. Revenues were behind at P3.6bn (-4% YoY), though a strong margin jump in 2Q21 drove YTD EBITDA to P686mn, broadly within expectations. The company posted consolidated SSSG of 36% in 2Q21. By brand, the rebound was mostly driven by Max’s and Pancake House, which typically generate a higher proportion of sales from dine-in. Despite the sharp jump in SSS, we estimate it is still 30-35% below pre-pandemic levels. On-premise accounted for c22% of sales during the quarter, vs 50-60% historically. Despite the relatively soft top line on a QoQ basis, MAXS saw a sharp jump in EBITDA margin to 22.7% during the quarter. This was driven by cost saving initiatives and margin enhancement strategies, which lead to significant reductions in variable and fixed cost. Among these initiatives include: Streamlining menu offerings across brands and maximizing raw materials across SKU. Optimizing fixed cost, such as labor and rent.

Ayala Corp 1H earnings up +31% YoY

Ayala Corp (AC PM) reported net income of P10.4bn for 1H21, up +31% from the prior year. Earnings for 2Q alone came in at P5.0bn, which is double last year’s depressed P2.5bn. QoQ, however, earnings were surprisingly down (-7%), which management attributed to the stricter lockdowns implemented in April. A look at the equity-accounted profits in AC’s 2Q presentation suggests QoQ drops in contributions from, Globe, power and AC Industrials. The QoQ drop for Globe was due to the one-offs in 1Q21 where it booked deferred tax adjustments relating to CREATE tax reform. The power business reported 2Q contributions of P1.1bn (vs P2.4bn in 1Q21).  The drop is partly because of plant outages, aggravated by  ~P500m in charges relating to the valuation of an embedded derivative (a prepayment option) for its debt as well as real property taxes. This was taken not with the listed AC Energy (ACEN PM), however. AC Industrials saw losses narrow for the half but 2Q losses (P392mn) were still double 1Q’s (P200m). IMI dipped back into the red in 2Q, while the losses at the auto business widened. AC bought back P5.8bn worth of shares in May, taking the total buyback under its P10bn program to P7.2bn (equivalent to 1.7% of shares).  The average price was P691/share, slightly lower than current market.  AC also acquired P10.1bn worth of Ayala Land (ALI PM) shares (=1.8%) at an average price of P36.34/share. With regard to its “value realization” plan, it considers AC’s partial sale of secondary shares in ACEN to GIC earlier this year as part of this initiative.  The deal raised P10.6bn (US$210m).  The reduction of its stake in Manila Water (P4.8bn) was also included.  AC aims to raise ~US$1bn from divestments by 2023.

2Q21 GDP +11.8% YoY, but still soft QoQ; lowering FY21 GDP est to +4.5%

Philippine GDP in 2Q21 jumped +11.8% YoY. According to Bloomberg, consensus estimates had 2Q21 at +10.9%. That took 1H21 GDP growth to +3.7%. Before we break out the bubbly, don’t forget that we were operating off an extremely low base (was -17.0% in 2Q20). Compared to pre-pandemic levels, the 2Q21 print was still 7.3% lower than 2Q19. In contrast, 1Q21 was only 4.6% below 1Q19. In fact, according to the PSA, 2Q21 was -1.3% QoQ compared to 1Q21 on a seasonally adjusted basis. Given the harder lockdown in August (compared to April), it is probably safe to assume that the quarterly weakness could persist into 3Q21. We lower our FY GDP forecast to +4.5%, compared to Bloomberg consensus of about 5.0%.

BSP keeps policy rates unchanged

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) left its key policy rate at a record low for a sixth consecutive meeting. Overnight reverse repurchase facility was left unchanged at 2%, within street expectations. The BSP also retained the overnight deposit and lending rates at 1.5% and 2.5%, respectively. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that the BSP is ready to adjust policy settings as needed, adding that the risks to inflation appeared balanced. But the reimposition of quarantine measures could pose a risk to the ongoing economic recovery. The Monetary Board has three more policy meetings this year. The next one is set on Sept. 23.
