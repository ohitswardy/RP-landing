REGIS REPORT

30 July 2021

The PSEi dropped 3.8% WoW this week to close at 6270.23. A return to a heightened lockdown loomed all week, with the country bracing for a surge fueled by the Delta variant.

On Friday, the government finally placed the capital under the strictest lockdown level (ECQ or Enhanced Community Quarantine) from 6-20 August in an effort to quell the spread of the Delta variant.  Metro Manila will be under GCQ (General Community Quarantine) with heightened restrictions from July 30 to August 5.

Nonetheless, daily vaccinations are rising dramatically, with data from July 27 showing around 659,000 having been vaccinated. This is double the daily average of the previous week. Vaccine reluctance has also dropped significantly – from 33% in May to 21% in June.  For Metro Manila, this number is down to just 8%.Based on the latest data, 11m or 10% of the country’s 109m population has been inoculated with at least one shot. Around 6.3m (5.8%) have had two shots. 

The index is now down for four straight weeks and is off 11% from the recent peak on July 5.  Foreign selling accelerated this week to US$76m.  Selling spiked on Friday (3x the usual outflow) following the announcement of the lockdown. Foreign participation also crept up this week, still heavily skewed on the sell.

Losses were again widespread, with 23 of the 30 index names ending down.

The top decline was from URC (-7.5% WoW), with bulk of the losses coming on Friday. On headline, URC’s 2Q net profit appeared to beat estimates (2Q21 NPAT at 5.05bn +$3% YoY) but his was boosted  by a one-off gain from the sale of land assets amounting to P3.3bn. Operating profit was flat YoY at P8.2bn, tracking in line with our FY21 estimates. 

The company is divesting its businesses in Australia and New Zealand -- Griffins and Snackbrands, which they acquired for a combined value of over US$1bn in 2014 and 2016, respectively. 

Lumpy business interruption claims and liquidated damages from contractors boosted Aboitiz Power’s  (-3.3% WoW) 1H21 numbers. 

Better-than-expected profit was also seen from Emperador (+16.5% WoW) – the weeks top index gainer.  2Q21 profit of P3bn grew 60% YoY and 43%QoQ supported by strong revenues and margins.

On the other hand, Meralco’s (-1.5% WoW) saw 2Q volumes expand 18% YoY, off a depressed 2Q20 base.

There was potential good news for GT Capital’s (-5.1% WoW) auto unit Toyota as the Tariff Commission has come out against the safeguard duty on imported vehicles, citing the lack of evidence that vehicle imports affected local vehicle assembly. The Commission’s stance could be a precursor to a final decision by the Dept of Trade (DTI) removing the duty. The safeguard duty amounted to a chunky 5-12% of the retail price of a majority of vehicles. A reversal should lift vehicle sales slightly just as its initial imposition caused a modest dip.

SMC (-7.1% WoW), BPI (-6.9% WoW), LTG (-6.1% WoW) also dropped markedly for the week. Large caps SM Prime (6.0% WoW), JG Summit (-5.9% WoW) and SM (-5.7% WoW) also dropped.  While AGI (+4.9% WoW), Robinsons Land (+2.4% WoW) and Puregold (+2.0% WoW) gained.

Razon Group - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series with Enrique Razon, Jr
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Uneven recovery; Acquisition strategy key

We hosted Mr. Enrique Razon, Jr., Chairman/President of ICTSI, Chairman/CEO of Bloomberry, and Chairman/President/CEO of Manila Water at our Regis CEO Roundtable. Recovery across Mr. Razon’s listed assets has been mixed, with strong global trade fueling unprecedented growth for his ports business on the one hand, and persistent travel restrictions hampering the casino business, on the other. Despite the uncertainty over how long the effects of the pandemic will persist, Mr. Razon believes being able to find attractive acquisition opportunities through business cycles and finding the right management team will always be the key to business success.

Favorable trade winds

Trade activity has reverted to pre-pandemic levels in many of ICTSI’s ports, despite slow vaccination drives in most countries where it operates. Strong volumes have also prompted ICT to raise tariffs in some key ports, and Mr. Razon noted that ICT will always exert effort to raise yields when possible. He also conveyed his thoughts on the likely benign regulatory risk environment for port tariffs, as port fees account for a very small portion of total freight costs. The global recovery from the pandemic has been slow and tentative, but he is optimistic about the vaccination campaign of the Philippines and believes the country is on the way to inoculate half of the population by Oct/Nov. Lastly, Mr. Razon believes ICT has sufficient liquidity and should be able to take advantage of port privatization opportunities if/when these arise.

Gaming business hit hard

The casino remains the hardest hit among Mr. Razon’s holdings, with international travel having been closed for more than a year now. Although the reimposition of lockdowns poses a risk to any momentum Bloomberry has been able to build, he nevertheless believes that the domestic market has proven to be resilient amidst the pandemic’s economic impact. The recovery of international business is harder to predict, as it relies on the return of international travel. Against this backdrop, he believes that both physical and online platforms are crucial to be in the best position for a post-pandemic recovery. His gaming business was among the more cashed-up ones entering the pandemic, Mr. Razon does not seem keen to pursue M&A in this space, and is instead focused on the ramp-up of its own initiatives.

Vast opportunities in water, locally and globally

Following the conclusion of contract negotiations for the East zone, MWC is focusing on catching up on much-need capex whilst actively looking for expansion opportunities abroad and locally. Mr. Razon acknowledged regulatory risks but noted that he intends to focus on opportunities with less complicated regulatory structures. He believes water is a necessity and a 12% fixed return is attractive.

Wilcon Depot - Margin strength continues in 2Q21; raising estimates and TP
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H21 net profit jumps from low base

WLCON’s net profit in 1H21 jumped nearly four-fold to P1.25bn, tracking ahead of Regis and consensus estimates. Net sales of P13.4bn were broadly in line, and the outperformance was again driven by strong margins. For 2Q21 alone, net profit was P643mn, recovering from the break-even level in 2Q20.

2Q21 net sales flattish QoQ despite stricter quarantine

Net sales in 2Q21 was flattish QoQ even as the government imposed stricter quarantine measures in late-March. On a same-store basis, Metro Manila stores (50-60% of sales) continued to underperform given restrictions on construction activity. Provincial stores, however, made up for the shortfall with most regions already back to pre-pandemic sales levels. Margin strength continues in 2Q21 Gross margin improved to 36.9% in 2Q21 (+70bps QoQ). The margin expansion continued to be lifted by proportionately higher sales to retail customers, which generate higher margins.

Top line recovery remains uncertain but margin strength likely to continue

Management expects top line recovery to be protracted given that new construction activity is likely to remain under pressure throughout the year. On the flipside, this will mean that current margin levels could be sustained as WLCON continues to generate a higher proportion of sales from retail customers.

Maintaining Buy – raising 2021-23E EPS estimates by 6-10%, TP to P25/sh (+14%)

Given the strong 1H21 results, we are raising our 2021-23E EPS estimates by 6- 10% on higher gross margin assumptions. We are likewise raising our TP to P25/sh as we roll over our DCF to 2022E. Notwithstanding the slow recovery in same-store sales, we believe profitability will be supported by strong margins and continued store expansion. Maintaining Buy

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
First Gen Corp - Notes from the REGIS CEO Roundtable with Giles Puno
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Natural gas here to stay

Mr. Giles Puno, President and COO of FGEN, gave his take on the country’s power supply-demand situation, RE and his outlook for the company in a recent CEO roundtable series event that Regis hosted. Mr. Puno says the firm’s LNG FSRU project is in full swing, and he is confident that the supply-demand outlook would keep LNG a key component of the grid despite the momentum gained by solar. Among our other key takeaways: 1) the medium-term supply is looking increasingly insufficient, and 2) pure RE players that have to buy from the spot market to fulfill contracts are at risk.

Current pipeline means demand-supply will remain tight over medium term

Echoing the trends observed by other power companies, Mr. Puno explained that the constant plant outages have resulted in a demand-supply balance that has become tighter much earlier than expected, in spite of the impact wrought by the pandemic. He cited WESM prices averaging over P7/kWh in July, clearing at much higher prices at peak hours. He thinks that if the 1,300MW from GNP Dinginin is further delayed, there could be a serious shortage by next summer. Structurally, he believes that the constant outages reflect the overall age of plants supplying to the grid (in excess of 25 years on average), so these will continue until old plants are gradually replaced by new ones.

Natural gas still has its advantages

Because of the ban on coal, however, there are limited options for reliable supply. Solar has gained momentum due to declining cost, but Mr. Puno believes that current solar technology, even with storage, does not provide a complete solution. He believes natural gas still has the advantage due to quick start-up times and the ability to serve peak and mid-merit loads with less carbon emission than coal. He also highlighted potential risks for pure solar developers like having to buy power from the spot during certain periods in order to fulfill contracts. In his view, solar power capacity makes sense in the context of a portfolio of other assets to backstop.

2022 completion of FSRU LNG terminal hedge against early Malampaya depletion, supply third parties

First GEN’s LNG terminal project is in full swing and is expected to be operational by 2022. The project will help assure fuel source for its plants earlier, especially as the Malampaya depletion appears to be happening earlier than forecasted. Outside its own plants, FGEN should be in a position to supply gas to third parties currently sourcing fuel from Malampaya like the Ilijan plant of San Miguel.

 
 

Corporate News

AP 2Q21 core income +175%YoY to P3.9bn

AP’s 2Q core income jumped 175%YoY to P3.9bn, helped by commissioning revenue from GNPD Unit 1, as well as higher water inflow, higher WESM dispatch and higher demand. For 1H21, AP’s core net income was P10.1bn (+188%YoY). Claims on liquidated damages from the delay in GNPD and business interruption claims from GNP outages added to profits. The generation and supply business’ EBITDA jumped 39% YoY to P20.5bn in 1H21. Capacity sold increased 6% to 3,600MW  while energy sold increased 10% to 11,790 GWh. Additionally, distribution’s EBITDA increased 12% to P3.7bn. Energy sales grew 4% to 2,745GWh as commercial and industrial sales recovered due to looser quarantine restrictions.

Emperador’s 2Q net income jumps 60% to P3bn

In a press release, Emperador said that net profit to owners jumped 60%YoY to P3bn in 2Q21 on revenue growth of 21%YoY. The 2Q21 gross margin improved to 22.7% vs 17.4% in 1Q21. The 2Q result implies a 43% jump in profit QoQ. This brings the 1H21 profit to 5.1bn, up 53%YoY, on the back of an 18% growth in revenues.

Megawide to sell P4bn preferred shares

Megawide filed paperwork with the SEC to sell up to 40mn pref shares at P100/sh. The are shares are “perpetual, cumulative, non-voting, non-participating, non-convertible, redeemable (non-reissuable). The proceeds will go directly to redeeming a previous 7.025% preferred shares series. As at end-March, MWIDE’s parent level net debt (incl. prefs) stood at P20.3bn, parent net debt-to-equity at 2.49x.

Meralco 1H +8%YoY to P11.4bn

Meralco reported core income of P11.4bn for 1H21, up +8% YoY, which is still slightly behind trend for our   Implied core income for 2Q was P6.29bn (+23% YoY). While the results now include Globe Business Power (GBP), we would describe them as being slightly lower than expected.

 

The YoY rise in electricity sales for 2Q alone was 19%, taking 1H21 to +7%.  While this sharp jump can be attributable to the low base in 2Q20 (which was the height of the lockdowns), Meralco took pains to point out that 2Q21 electricity sales were almost at 2Q19 levels (pre-pandemic).  The company expects volumes to be up 7% for the year, slightly below our assumed 9%.

 

The company declared a dividend of P5.057/share with respect to 1H21 income, taking the dividends so far this year to P12.9/share, which is still a tad below our forecast (P15/share). Net cash (including investments) was P19bn as of June, a slight dip from the P25bn in March.  Net cash is much lower YoY because of the purchase and consolidation of Global Business Power.

 

There has been no visible progress on Meralco’s long overdue rate review but the ERC has not (yet) changed its target reset date of July 2022. We would be very surprised if the reset was actually completed by that date. Meralco is considering proposing a 12% return

 

Meralco says it believes it is planning for and has contracted enough power supply for its needs for the medium term. The company having planned for enough contracted capacity and their actual being enough capacity, however, are two very different things.

 

Short-term, Meralco pointed out that the Malampaya will be on maintenance for a few weeks later this year.  The First Gen plants that use Malampaya fuel will run on liquid fuel in the interim. The 1200MW Ilijan plant controlled by San Miguel, however, will have to source replacement power to supply Meralco. We reckon this could result in another supply crunch during the period. This situation will likely result in an outcome that will clearly show why Meralco’s statements of having planned for adequate supply may not sync with realities on the ground.

Mynt to seek US$2bn valuation

Mynt will aim to achieve a US$2bn valuation at its next fund-raising. This would be more than double the valuation at its last one 6-7 months ago. Fintech investor Bow Wave bought into Mynt at an estimated valuation of ~US$900m (post-money). The transactions at its e-wallet operation GCash achieved more than P1trn worth of transactions last year. It is on track to more than double that this year. Mynt is ~40%-owned each by Globe and Ant Financial. We estimate Mynt will likely only need new funding by 2H next year if not later.

Metro Pacific Tollways projects to open in next few months

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) is set to complete in the coming months. The 7.2-km section (Sta. Rosa to Silang East interchange) of Cavite-Laguna Expressway will be opened this month. The first 5-km section of the 8-km NLEX Connector road will be opened by year-end. The 8.5-km Cebu-Cordova Link Expressway project will be opened by February 2022. Tollroads account for c. 19% of MPI’s gross asset value and profits. MPI is trading at a 34% discount to its market-based NAV of P5.38/sh.

 
 

Macro News

House of Representatives to adopt Senate version of POGO tax bill

House Ways and Means Committee chair “Joey” Salceda said that the House of Representatives would adopt the Senate version of the POGO tax bill. The SB 2232 would require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue in lieu of all other taxes. Meanwhile, POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P177bn in tax revenue over five years. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. Thus, the new bill could slow down POGO closures.

Tariff Commission thumbs down safeguard duty on imported vehicles

The Tariff Commission recommended that there should be no safeguard duty imposed on imported CBU passenger and light commercial vehicles. They cited the lack of evidence of increased import quantities of CBUs both in absolute terms and relative terms to domestic production in 2014-2020. The recommendation will be submitted to the Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI); DTI has 15 calendar days to issue a decision. The cash bond should be returned 10 days after issuance of decision. The DTI will not be able to accept another petition from the same industry within one year after date of decision

Duterte 2021 SONA: Key highlights

Accomplishments. Given that this was his last SONA (at least as of now), President Duterte highlighted his accomplishments during his six-year term, including raising salaries for cops and soldiers, as well as the passage of laws providing free tertiary education, universal healthcare, free irrigation, rice tariffication, and the tax reform programs. Unsurprisingly, he spoke at length about his fight against drugs/criminality and corruption, as well as his usual rants about the case against him at the ICC and his stance on having a cordial relationship with China over the country’s rights on the West Philippine Sea.  He noted that he knew he had limited time to accomplish things because the constitution constrained him to a single six-year term.

 

For the remainder of his term, he urged Congress to pass priority bills: Foreign investments Act, Public Service Act, Retail & Trade Liberalization Act. He also reiterated his call for Congress to establish a single agency that will focus on OFWs. 

 

Build! Build! Build! He flaunted his administration’s infrastructure program, raising infrastructure spend-to-GDP ratio to a record high of over 5%. He claimed that his administration was able to solve problems on public transportation, with much shorter waiting times for the MRT-3 (from 10 trains at 30km per hour to 23 trains at 60 km per hour) and reductions in travel times with the completion of the LRT-2 East extension project, Skyway 3, BGC-Ortigas Link bridge.  He noted that he deliberately asked his economic managers to prioritize projects outside of Davao (eg. Davao Coastal road project will only be completed in 2023).  He directed line agencies to be on full speed to ensure flagship projects will be completed within his term, especially those outside Metro Manila.

 

Attacks on water companies/ABS-CBN anew.  He renewed his attacks against the Metro Manila water concessionaires and lauded that the new water contracts will be able to protect consumers at least until 2037.  He admitted that he has not thoroughly digested the new water contracts yet so he has not signed it.  He also accused ABS-CBN again for not paying right taxes on imported equipment and real estate. He threatened to reassign ABS-CBN’s radio frequencies. 

 

Pandemic.  President Duterte thanked the private sector for supporting the government during the pandemic. He committed to “assess” the private sector to regain the commercial vibrance of the country prior to the pandemic.  He indicated that the country cannot afford more lockdowns, but if the Delta variant causes another surge, he will not hesitate to lock down the country again.  He also urged local governments to hasten inoculations and urged Congress to enact a law to create a center for disease control and a Phil. Virology Institute so Philippines can make its own vaccines in the future.  He highlighted the recently signed Executive Order for an employment recovery strategy, which aims to create over 1mn jobs, re-tool workers for better job opportunities by providing online training. 

Daily vaccinations jump further

The 7-day average vaccinations jumped to 300,880/day, a record high and coming from 271,426/day the week before. The country expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August, including 13.5mn doses that already arrived in July. The Philippines has now received a total of 31mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 17.2mn doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

BOP position at US$312mn deficit in June

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$312mn in June 2021. This is a reversal from the US$80mn surplus a year ago and a narrow deficit than in May 2021 (US$1.4bn). The data reflected mainly the outflows arising from the govt’s settlement of foreign currency debt obligations and various expenditures, and the BSP’s net foreign exchange operations. The 1H21 BOP stood at a US$1.94bn deficit, a reversal from last year’s US$4.1bn surplus due to a higher trade deficit.

Infra spend up in June

Infrastructure disbursements (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) amounted to P94.4bn in June, up 50% YoY and 20% MoM. This brings 1H21 infra spend to P427bn, +43% YoY and accounted for 56% of the government’s P761bn full-year target. Overall 1H21 infrastructure spending, which included subsidy and equity injected into state-run corporations as well as transfers to LGUs totaled P535.9bn (+40% YoY). This is broadly on track to reach the government’s year-end target of P1.02tn (equiv. to 5.1% of GDP).

Budget deficit swells to P150bn in June

The country’s fiscal deficit stood at P150bn in June. Revenues dropped MoM and YoY on soft BIR collections while expenditures dropped 13% MoM (due to timing of subsidies for PhilHealth). The 1H21 budget deficit of P716bn is tracking below the government’s FY21E due to underspending while 1H21 revenues surpassed goal by 5%. The underspending was due to timing of subsidies and maintenance spending (awaiting submission of documents/progress billings), interest savings, release of funds under GUIDE bill (pending). The infra spend is tracking slightly ahead of target

659,000 COVID vaccine doses administered in a day

659,000 COVID vaccine doses were administered on July 27. This is the first time that over 500,000 doses were administered in a day. The country expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August, including 14mn doses that already arrived in July. The Philippines has now received a total of 31.4mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 18.1mn doses. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

 

Gov’t debt climbs to new high of P11.2tn

The National Govt’s outstanding debt inched up to a new high of P11.166tn as of end-June. This is equivalent to c. 56% of GDP (Gov’t FY21E Nominal GDP at P19.8tn). The gov’t targeting NG debt-to-GDP ratio to reach 59.1% by year-end from 54.6% in end-2020. Domestic accounts for 71% of total while external is at 29% of total.
