REGIS REPORT

19 March 2021

The market slipped 4.3% this week – as the spike in daily new covid cases (at 4400-5400 cases/day this week) shook sentiment. The FTSE rebal at the close Friday took the market even lower with outflows across the board.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$70m, taking YTD net outflows to US$850m. Around US$20m flowed out on FTSE rebal-related activity, taking the market down another 2% at the close today.

Fears of tighter restrictions took the market down even as longer curfew hours were imposed on Manila starting Monday. Certain cities in the metro also declared liquor bans.  Restaurants’ dine-in limit was reduced to 50% of capacity for Metro Manila and other areas under GCQ. Cinemas, museums etc will also be temporarily closed.

In the National Capital Region, ICU bed occupancy is back at 67%. Minors (18 and below) have been temporarily barred from going out for two weeks starting this week. Age-based restrictions have been one of the reasons for the sluggish economic recovery, as 30-40% of the population is classified within the definition of “minors”.

The market was down across the board – with 24 of the 30 index names ending the week with declines.

Threats of stricter restrictions took Bloomberry (-9.0% WoW) down markedly. 

Macro proxies were down across the board led by SM (-7.3 % WoW), and DMCI (-6.9% WoW). San Miguel (-6.5% WoW) and Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-6.5% WoW) also declined.  

Nonetheless, BPI (-4.7% WoW) managed a gain this week, rising from its 7% drop in the prior week as a proposed law that would translate to a cap in loan rates caused concern.

Meanwhile, defensive names were among the handful of gainers with consumer and utilities rising.  Puregold (+4.2% WoW), Meralco (+3.3% WoW), and URC (+3.2% WoW) gained.

Ayala Corp (-4.6% WoW) announced the finalization of the agreement with GIC affiliate Arran Investments for the latter’s investment in AC Energy (-0.6% WoW). After this investment and ACEN’s planned follow-on-offer, GIC will end up with a 17.5% stake in ACEN.

SM Investments (-7.3% WoW) is buying out the 31% stake held by Chelsea Logistics (+6.0% WoW) (of Davao’s Dennis Uy) in listed logistics firm 2GO Group (+3.0% WoW). This will take SM’s stake in 2GO to just under 66%.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Wilcon Depot - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable series
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Solid position amidst the pandemic; reiterating Buy

WLCON’s President and CEO Ms. Lorraine Belo-Cincochan and COO Ms. Rose Ong joined our CEO RoundtableSeries and gave insight on how the company is adapting to changes in their customers’ behaviors and the industry’s dynamics. While store expansion remains to be at the core of their growth strategy, e-commerce is also playing a larger role. We believe the company is emerging from the crisis in a stronger competitive position given its scale and strong brand equity in what was already a fragmented and under-penetrated market even pre[1]pandemic. We reiterate our Buy rating on WLCON, which continues to be our top pick in the retail space. We expect its recovery to outpace the broader industry as its target market (mid- to upper-income consumers) is likely to continue spending for home construction and improvement even during the pandemic.

Omni-channel approach to e-commerce

Health and safety remain to be the top concerns of WLCON’s core market (mid[1]to upper-income consumers), and this continues to put pressure on stores’ foot traffic even if customers are not particularly hampered by mobility (most own private vehicles). With a larger proportion of retail activity shifting online, the company is adopting an omni-channel approach, which will allow their customers to browse items in WLCON’s online platform, and have the items picked up in the store. Management believes, however, that some product lines (tiles, flooring, building materials) will continue to be purchased in-store.

Scale, brand equity remain to be competitive advantages

Store expansion continues to be at the core of the company’s growth strategy and management says that it is on track to meet its medium term target (100 stores in 5 years). While there has been consolidation in the industry, new players have also emerged, particularly in online marketplaces, especially for items where there is less differentiation. Management believes it still has an edge given its wide range of brands and product lines, as well as reliability of supply.

Slow recovery, but ready for “revenge shopping”

WLCON’s SSSG was flattish by 2H20, though management noted that this was largely due to the resumption of home renovations and increased demand for home improvement (i.e. soft categories). While there continues to be week-on[1]week improvements in early 1Q21, the company believes that they are not likely to see a more sustained recovery until 3Q/4Q21. Conversations with private contractors and new homeowners suggest that new home constructions are already being planned, though have not broken ground given the mobility restrictions imposed. Management is preparing for a surge in demand (“revenge shopping”) once new home construction projects begin.

 
 

Corporate News

Ayala Land to infuse assets into AREIT via property-for-share swap

Ayala Land will infuse P15.46bn worth of assets into AREIT. The transaction will include around 205,000sqm leasable area (P75,435/sqm) and will be in exchange for 483.25mn shares at P32/share. This will increase AREIT’s leasable portfolio from 344,000sqm to 549,000sqm and increase its deposited property from P37bn to P52bn. We estimate that this transaction would bring Ayala Land’s stake in AREIT from 54.4% to 69%. Since this would violate the minimum public ownership requirement of 33%, there will likely be another transaction that would lower Ayala Land’s ownership in AREIT. Our initial estimates suggest that the transaction will result in minimal change to dividend per share. AREIT currently offers c. 5.1% dividend yield.

DNL to issue up to P5bn fixed-rate bonds

D&L Industries (DNL PM) disclosed that its board has approved a plan to issue between P3-5bn in fixed-rate bonds to fund the company’s expansion in Batangas. The bonds have will have a tenure of three to five years. The total capex for the Batangas plant is estimated to reach P8bn, with P4bn remaining to be deployed to complete the plant by end of the year. The plant will be used for DNL’s food exports, oleochemicals and manufacture downstream packaging.

MPI eyes return to real estate

MPI is in talks with the Tiu family for a potential investment in Discovery World Corp and its subsidiaries. There is no definitive agreement signed yet. MPI is trading at a 29% discount to its market-based NAV of P5.35/sh.

Davao’s Dennis Uy selling stake in 2GO

A source of Inquirer said that talks were ongoing for Davao businessman Dennis Uy to divest his minority stake in 2Go Group. The deal would strengthen Chelsea Logistics and Infrastructure Holdings which owns an effective 28% economic interest in 2Go. Previous takeover rumors at 2Go involved the Sy family/SM Investments which is the logistics company’s other major shareholders.

PLDT introduces all data package with no expiry

PLDT mobile subsidiary Smart introduced “Magic Data” with three denominations: P99 for 2GB (=P50/GB); P199 for 5GB (=P40/GB); P399 for 24Gb (=P16.6/GB).

The mobile data allocation is available for use on all sites and does not have an expiry. All previous offerings were time-bound. An associate of Globe, Gomo, currently offers a 30GB package for P299 (=P10/GB). For perspective, the latest average yield per GB for both operators is ~P25/GB.

PAL gets more funds

Philippine Airlines received US$60mn in fresh funds from its chairman, Lucio Tan. The funds were used to finance separation payments of employees. According to the Philippine Star Mr. Tan also advanced US$130mn in 2020. PAL has put together various elements for its recovery, according to new Board director Leo Alejandrino. The last piece of the puzzle counterpart funding from the government on terms that are as secure and profitable as those offered by private lenders. PAL is hopeful the GFIs will provide the same support and urgency extended to Cebu Pacific.

GIC completes P11.9bn investment in ACEN

Investment agreement between GIC affiliate Arran Investment Pte Ltd. and AC Energy Corporation (ACEN) was signed on December 30, 2020. The transaction entailed 4bn primary shares issued at P2.97/share. The private placement is the first tranche of GIC’s investment in achieving a 17.5% stake. After ACEN’s FOO and infusion of international assets, the parent company of ACEN (ACEIC) plans to sell down 2.8bn secondary shares to GIC.

Chelsea Logistics to sell 2GO stake to SM Investments

Chelsea Logistics (C PM) disclosed to the PSE that it will sell its stake in 2Go Group (2GO PM) to SM Investments. This will amount to 867mn shares at P8.50/share, 12% lower than yesterday’s closing price. Total cost is P7.4bn and will raise SM’s stake in 2GO from 30.5% to 65.7%.

 
 

Macro News

Minors in Metro Manila banned from going out anew starting Mar 17

Minors (18 years old and below) residing in Metro Manila are once again not allowed to go out of their homes for two weeks starting Wednesday, March 17.

Gov’t imposes travel ban on foreigners and returning non-OFW Filipinos; Limits NAIA capacity for inbound foreign travelers

The government temporarily suspended the entry of foreigners and returning non-OFW Filipinos from March 20 to April 19. Exempted from this are those holding seaman’s visa, medical repatriation, emergencies/humanitarian reasons. It also limited the capacity of NAIA airport for inbound international travelers to a maximum of 1,500 pax per day from March 18 to April 18. Pre-Covid, NAIA’s international pax volumes stood at >68k per day; In 2020, this went down to 15k per day.

216,000 Filipinos have been administered COVID vaccine doses

As of March 15, 215,997 Filipinos have received one dose of the COVID-19 vaccine coming from 114,500 as of March 10. This implies that in the past 5 days, vaccinations increased to an average of 20,300/day from 11,500/day earlier. The country is slated to receive 1.4mn more Sinovac vaccines this month. The Philippines received 600,000 Sinovac doses in late February. Another 900,000 doses from the COVAX facility are also scheduled to arrive in late March or early April. Additionally, around 525,000 AstraZeneca doses were received in early March.

Liquor ban extends to other cities in Metor Manila

After QC and Paranaque announced the ban on sale of alcohol for the next two weeks, the following cities have followed suit -

San Juan (beginning March 15); Mandaluyong (March 15 – March 31); Pateros (March 13 – March 31); Malabon (March 15 – April 3); Muntinlupa (March 15 – April 14); Valenzuela (March 16 – April 1).

NEDA backs curfew, localized lockdowns

The National Economic and Development Authority backed the imposition of localized lockdowns and curfews by local government units to curb the surge in COVID cases according to NEDA Acting Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua. Several villages in Metro Manila have been placed under localized lockdowns due to rising infections. Uniform curfew hours were also implemented in Metro Manila.

Remittances drop 1.7% to US$2.6bn in January

OFW remittances fell 1.7% YoY and 10% MoM to US$2.6bn in January which means in PHP terms, remittances dropped 7% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate was at -0.6%. Remittances from the USA grew 12% YoY but this was more than offset by declines from other regions. The BSP expects remittances to rise by 4% this year, after dipping -0.8% in FY20.

Imports flat MoM, exports shrink in January

Preliminary trade data showed imports unchanged MoM to US$2.34bn in January 2020. Non-electronic imports dropped slightly while imports of electronics rose 2%. Meanwhile, Fuel imports rose +6% MoM, Capital goods (-5% MoM), Raw material imports (+6% MoM), Consumer goods (-3% MoM). Meanwhile, exports dipped slightly by 4% MoM, led by a 10% drop in electronic exports. The trade deficit stood at US$2.42bn in January.

Infrastructure spending surges MoM in December, exceeds FY20 target

The government spent P132.3bn in December 2020 (-25.6% YoY), excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies. This brought FY20 infra spend to P681bn (-28% YoY), surpassing the government’s P609.3bn target. The govt cited better-than-expected accomplishments by the Dept. of Public Works. This took the government’s overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in 2020 to P869.5bn (-17% YoY), surpassing the target of P825bn. For FY21, the government is targeting to spend P1.17tn on infrastructure (+35% YoY).

Quezon City, Paranaque enforce liquor ban

The local governments of Quezon City and Paranaque have ordered a ban on the sale of alcoholic beverages until the end of the month. The announcement of the liquor restrictions coincide with uniform curfew hours that will be imposed in Metro Manila for two weeks beginning March 15.

DTI to limit dine-in capacity to 50% in GCQ areas

The Department of Trade and Industry said it would issue a circular to reduce the capacity of dine-in restaurants, cafes and personal care services to 50%. The restrictions will be implemented in GCQ areas for two weeks. It will also order the temporary closure of driving schools, cinemas, gaming arcades, museums, cultural centers.

BSP raises BOP forecast

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas raised its Balance of Payments surplus target this year to US$6.2bn (=1.6% of GDP) and cited the improvement in imports and exports as a reason for the upgrade. The BSP maintained OFW remittance growth forecast at +4% while tourism receipts are expected to rise by +15% this year. The current account surplus was upgraded to US$9.1bn (2.3% of GDP). In 2020, the BOP surplus stood at US$16bn (4.4% of GDP), with the current account surplus at US$13bn (3.6% of GDP). The BOP surplus projected to stabilize to US$3.8bn (0.9% of GDP) in 2022. The GIR is also expected to reach US$114bn this year and US$117bn in 2022. The GIR stood at US$110bn in end-December 2020 and US$109bn in end-Feb 2021.

CREATE to provide P1trn in tax relief over 10 years

Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III said that the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act (CREATE) will provide P1trn worth of tax relief over 10 years. The Department of Finance (DOF) previously estimated that CREATE would lead to P251bn in foregone revenues over the next two years.

CREATE would lower the corporate income tax rate

For local small business – from 30% to 20% (retroactively from July 2020); For all other companies – from 30% to 25% (retroactively from July 2020). It would also streamline the government’s fiscal incentives system to make it time-bound. If President Duterte does not sign (or veto) the CREATE bill, it will lapse into law on March 27.

Budget swings to P14bn deficit in January

The National Government posted a P14.1bn budget deficit for January 2021 which was a reversal of the P23bn surplus last year. This is partly due to a 12% YoY contraction in revenues. Tax revenues dropped 8% YoY, with BIR and BOC collections lower by 7% and 15%, respectively. Non-tax revenues dropped 29% YoY due to high base effects of dividend remittances from GOCCs last year. Meanwhile, expenditures rose 1.2% YoY to P275bn, tracking behind the government’s full-year target.

Gov’t to sign a new agreement with Manila Water this month

The Department of Justice is finalizing the government’s new concession agreement with Manila Water according to Justice Secretary Menardo Guevarra.
