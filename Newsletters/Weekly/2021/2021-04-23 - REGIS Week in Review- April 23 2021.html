REGIS REPORT

23 April 2021

The PSEi closed at 6378 this week, down 1.8% WoW, as the recent spike in COVID cases and the slow pace of vaccinations continued to weigh on the market.  Only around 1.6m doses have been administered since vaccinations began in early March. The latest data also suggest that by end-May, the Philippines will have received a total of 8.2mn COVID doses, lower than the previous target of 17.6m.

The lack of positive catalysts as well as continued foreign selling continued pushed the market lower for a second straight week. The market is now down 11% YTD.

Net outflows persisted this week – with another US$91m going out. YTD net foreign selling now stands at US$1.2bn.

The market’s losses were widespread, as decliners outnumbered gainers 2:1.

Among the top decliners were Bloomberry (-9.0% WoW) and JG Summit (-7.7%).

Megaworld (-7.7% WoW) also dropped after recently reporting a 45% drop in its FY20 earnings.  The 40% QoQ drop in office revenue and the flat reservation sales guidance in 2021 did not help either.

Meanwhile, the defensive names such as URC (+2.7% WoW), Puregold (+1.4% WoW) and PLDT (+1.35% WoW) led this week.

BPI (-1.8% WoW) kicked off the 1Q reporting season. Results were in line with consensus.  Of note, BPI reported benign NPLs of 2.76%, just 8bps higher QoQ.

Manila Water - A fresh start?
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 Klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Overhang lifted

The amendment of the East zone’s concession contract lifts the cloud of uncertainty over MWC. The revisions were not too drastic even if they, as expected, make the agreement inferior to the original from the perspective of the concessionaire. Nevertheless, a decent regulatory return of 12% (pretax) and a new controlling shareholder offer the possibility that value can be created. We review our valuation for MWC and peg our TP at P13/share with a Hold rating.

Contract revisions: Much ado about nothing

The new agreement removes income tax recovery but it also fixes the regulatory return to a nominal 12% over the remainder of the concession. This was previously a post-tax, inflation adjusted return reviewed every five years. Financially, the new regulatory return is lower than what was previously granted and assumed in our previous estimates (though returns arguably might have been lower if they were reviewed today). Apart from removing the “free” FX hedge on its debt, the other revisions were largely non-financial but could also make the concession more vulnerable to political winds.

Making lemonade out of lemons

Ports and casino magnate Mr. Enrique Razon is likely to revamp management and take a more aggressive business stance. We see three ways new management could improve and add value to MWC. First, by reducing cost of capital. Second, by leveraging its expertise to grow its non-East Zone investments even more quickly, especially overseas. Third (and most basic), by aggressively rolling out capex spend to improve its regulatory value within the East Zone, especially since regulated capex earns a 12% return (pretax).

Revised TP at P13/sh

We revise our TP to P13/sh from P22/sh after assuming (1) a lower regulatory return, (2) higher net debt, and (3) a higher share count. These revisions more than offset the accretive impact of (a) higher capex, (b) lower WACC, (c) lower tax rate, and (d) the new equity infusion from Mr. Razon (see page 2 for details). The shares are fully valued, in our view, but one could argue that new management could bring (as yet) unquantifiable benefits to MWC. Risks: regulation, water supply, acquisitions.

Vista Land - Construction delays hit 4Q residential revenue; rental revenue still resilient
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY20 earnings fall 45%

Vista’s 4Q20 net profit fell 54% QoQ (-64% YoY) as construction issues, blamed on typhoons, brought residential revenue down 28% QoQ (-43% YoY). GPM and EBIT margin were cosmetically weak because a large amount of interest cost was included as part of cost of goods sold instead of being previously expensed. FY20 net profit fell 45% to P6.2bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. While 4Q20 residential revenue was weak, rental revenue was resilient.

Rental revenue falls “only” 7%; affiliate tenants perform well

FY20 rental revenue, which is dominated by malls, fell only 7% as most of its tenants are considered “essential” and can operate even during the tightest form of lockdown. Affiliate tenants AllDay (supermarket) and AllHome (DIY, furniture) account for c. 50% of space and still grew revenue in 2020. In addition, unlike most other mall operators, Vista only granted rental concessions to a few tenants. However, this resulted in store closures in 4Q20 (c. 5% of floor area).

FY20 presales down 32%; to launch more high-end projects this year

Reservation sales inched up 2% QoQ to P14.1bn, still 28% lower than the 2019 average. This brought FY20 presales down 32% to P53.7bn. There were no meaningful increases in defaults but about 10% of buyers availed of payment deferrals. Similar to 2019, OFWs comprised 55% of sales. High-end projects comprised only 4% of sales but Vista plans to launch more this year to take advantage of stronger demand for high-end suburban housing.

Office REIT listing in the works

Vista plans to spin off its office portfolio into a REIT this year. We expect the impact to be small since the office portfolio typically accounts for less than 5% of earnings. Nevertheless, the listing could lower the gearing ratio (3Q20: 96%).

Megawide - Construction biz resilient; Landport unit faces headwinds
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Construction drives 4Q recovery

MWIDE reported unaudited FY20 net loss after pref divs of P670mn, a smaller loss than expected. The profit figure, however, is bloated by rental revenues at the landport unit that we expect to be reversed. We thus attribute the headline outperformance to (a) higher booked construction revenues and (b) lower non[1]cash costs. We estimate MWIDE turned a profit already in 4Q, thanks to easing mobility restrictions, which allowed its construction unit to complete more projects. The landport unit, however, faces headwinds this year. The airport unit is close to concluding a debt restructuring agreement with its lenders.

Construction order book jumps on more infra/casino contracts

The construction unit sustained improvements in revenue bookings and is now operating near pre-pandemic levels. Its order book jumped by 68% QoQ to P68bn after securing the Malolos-Clark railway and the Suncity casino project. Infra[1]related projects now account for 15% of its order book, up from 5%. The company is also participating in a couple of big-ticket infrastructure biddings this year: Metro Manila Subway and the NSCR (South).

Airport: EBITDA losses persist; Debt restructuring agreement soon

EBITDA losses persisted for the airport unit, as travel restrictions remained. Management is about to reach a debt restructuring agreement with its lenders, which will allow them to defer principal payments up to 2023.

Landport unit faces headwinds in FY21

The landport unit reported a profit of P222mn but this was overstated. The company included P141mn worth of revenues relating to “rent-free” periods during ECQ, which will eventually be reversed. Beyond that, future revenues are at risk, given the exit of its large POGO tenant in late 4Q. Lease receivables that the firm expects to collected within 2021 are now down to P600mn, lower than our FY21E.

Refinancing maturities at the parent; New projects on hold

The company plans to revisit the capital markets this year so it can refinance the existing P4bn Series 1 prefs, which have a step-up in 4Q21. Capex for other projects, such as the landport Lot 2 expansion and the Cebu Carbon market have been deferred to the latter part of FY21.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Megaworld Corp. - 4Q20 office revenue drops sharply; guiding for flat presales
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY20 earnings down 45%

Megaworld’s 4Q20 net profit fell 52% YoY (+23% QoQ) to P2.5bn. This brought FY20 earnings down 45% to P9.9bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Although revenue was within our expectations, opex did not drop as much as we expected. What caught our attention the most was the 40% QoQ drop in office revenue and the flat reservation sales guidance in 2021.

Office vacancy rises to 10%

Office revenue fell 40% QoQ (-36% YoY). As a result, despite 17% floor area expansion in late 4Q19, FY20 office revenue was flat (from growth of 11% in 9M20). Management cited multiple reasons for this:

  • The company granted substantial rent-free periods to new tenants due to difficulty in fitting out the space (this resulted in reversal of revenue that was recognized in 9M20).
  • Vacancy rose from 4% in 2019 to 10% in 2020 (6% in 3Q20).
  • Some tenants renewed contracts at lower rental rates.

Although Megaworld has a more optimistic view on the office segment than we do, it will not be able to complete new office buildings until 2023 due to construction delays.

Provincial projects cushion drop in residential sales; guiding for flat presales

4Q20 reservation sales jumped 41% QoQ (-48% YoY) to P17.9bn, comparable to the 2Q20 figure. For the full-year, presales dropped 54% to P68bn. Sales of provincial lots grew 27% and comprised 25% of sales (9% in 2019). Megaworld raised selling prices of provincial projects by an average of 10-15%. In 2021, the company is guiding P8bn in residential launches and P70bn in reservation sales, much lower than our own presales forecast of P100bn.

BPI 1Q21 – NPLs flat qoq; provisions fall, trading surprisingly resilient

In a press release, BPI reported headline NPAT of P5.0bn, down 22% YoY. However, this already included a one-time write-down of deferred tax assets, due to the CREATE law. Pre-tax profit was actually up 5% YoY driven by resilient trading/fx gains, and a drop in provision expense (-15% YoY and -48% QoQ). The lower provision was due to surprisingly mild NPLs of 2.76%, up just 8bps from the 2.68% reported at end-2020. There were also some accounting changes that created some “noise” in the YoY figures (de-consolidation of their leasing arm).

Loans contracted 5% YoY, slightly worse than the -4.6% at end-2020. Mortgages, though were up 10.9%, while micro-loans were up 10.5%; all other categories were lower YoY. Deposits were flat YoY, but CASA grew 12%, taking CASA ratio to an all-time high 82.6%. NIMs likely contracted, as NII was down 7% YoY and 5% QoQ. This is likely due to lower loan and bond yields, while deposit costs are flattening out. Fee incomes recovered nicely, up 14% YoY and 25% QoQ as banking activity (transaction volumes) seems to be normalizing. BPI booked P1.7bn in trading/fx gains. While this was down 20% YoY, it was a positive surprise, given the sharp jump in interest rates in Feb/March. NPLs were surprisingly benign, at 2.76%, up just 8ps QoQ. This was despite the jump in industry NPLs to 4.16% in February. Provision expense dropped to P3.6bn (-48% QoQ and -15% YoY), which brought NPL coverage up to 123.5% from 115.2% at end-2020. Tax expense nearly doubled YoY. The bank took a one-time write-down of its deferred tax assets (DTA) as a result of the lower corporate income tax rate under the CREATE Act.

 

Overall, the operating figures seem in line with our expectations. We are cautiously optimistic about the benign NPL figures but are wary of jumps in defaults as the Bayanihan 2 Act moratoriums roll off.

Macro News

Dollar reserves slip in March after gov’t pays maturing foreign debt

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, settled at US$104.82bn as of end-March 2021 from the end-Feb GIR level of US$105.16bn. This is equivalent to 12 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The MoM dip reflected outflows mainly from the net withdrawal in the National Government’s foreign currency deposits with the BSP, which were largely used for debt servicing, and a downward adjustment in the value of BSP’s gold holdings due to the decrease in the price of gold in the international market. These outflows were partly offset, however, by the BSP’s income from its investments abroad.

Gov’t expects US$4bn in mining investments

The government expects over US$4bn in capex from projects of Sagittarius Mines, KingKing Mining Corp., and Silangan Mindanao according to Presidential Spokesperson Herminio “Harry” L. Roque. The new mining projects are also expected to generate P40bn in local taxes and P20bn worth of social development projects. Meanwhile, the indigenous groups are also expected to benefit with around P15bn in royalties expected to be collected from the major mining projects. The President lifted the moratorium on new mining projects as Republic Act No. 10963, or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Act, has already doubled the excise tax on minerals from 2% to 4%.

Philippines to receive 8.2mn COVID vaccine doses by May

Health Undersecretary Myrna Cabotaje said that the Philippines will receive:

2.2mn COVID vaccine doses in April and 3.5mn doses in May. These are lower than the previously announced 6.5mn doses in April and 8.6mn doses in May. This would suggest that by end-May, the Philippines will have received a total of 8.2mn COVID doses, lower than the previous target of 17.6mn. The government previously said 140mn doses would arrive by end-2021 to inoculate 70mn Filipinos. The country has received 3mn doses and has administered 1.48mn doses.

BSP wants Bank Secrecy Law eased; Sees GIR ballooning to US$120bn

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas Governor Benjamin E. Diokno will ask President Rodrigo R. Duterte to certify as urgent a bill seeking to ease the Bank Secrecy Law. The House Committee on Banks and Financial Intermediaries in February approved House Bill (HB) 8991. Senate Bill 1802 was also filed last year but is still pending at the committee level. The Bill expands the supervisory powers of the BSP by allowing it to look into the deposit accounts but only if there is reasonable grounds for fraud, serious irregularity or unlawful activity. The International Monetary Fund warned the Philippines that it could be part of the “gray list” of jurisdictions by June 2021. Being part of the “gray list” will result in delayed processing of transactions which could affect business flows and remittances. Meanwhile, the BSP sees the country’s gross international reserves climbing to US$120bn by year-end. As of end-March, the GIR stood at US$104.8bn.

BSP bond buying slowed in Dec

The BSP finally updated its balance sheet data to end-2020 (previously was only to Nov). The numbers clearly show what we had suspected – that the BSP had taken its foot off the monetary pedal in 4Q20. In 2Q/3Q20, the BSP engaged in its own version of Quantitative Easing (QE), with its open market operations (OMO) aggressively buying Treasury paper (mostly long-dated, we gather), peaking at over P400bn bought in June alone. By September, the BSP’s portfolio of Treasury holdings stood at P1.3trn, almost 6x the level before the Covid-19 pandemic. In 4Q20, however, the BSP hung back, increasing its Treasury holdings by just P75bn from the 3Q level. It would appear that the BSP continues to be on the sidelines so far into 2021. This is evidenced by their allowing the 10-year yields to rise sharply in Feb/Mar following the jump in inflation. For contrast, the BSP was the “not-so-invisible hand” in April and June when market rates started moving up. The BSP has publicly admitted the limits of monetary easing, and is likely wary, given that the monetary expansion has not (yet) been complemented with fiscal spending. And despite all the additional liquidity, loan growth has not re-started and has in fact contracted. What does this mean for equities? Without continued liquidity infusions from the BSP, the stock market’s performance will have to be driven by external flows. And with the lockdowns and slow rollout of the vaccine, we could likely be “range-bound” until the macro/earnings recovery are more apparent… hopefully in 3/4Q.

NEDA: P83bn wages lost due to stricter lockdowns

The National Economic and Development Authority estimates P83.3bn in income was lost during the nearly five weeks of stricter lockdown in Manila. Around P19.6bn in workers’ income is sacrificed for every week of enhanced community quarantine (ECQ), while P14.7bn is lost for every week of modified ECQ (MECQ). Last Dec, the gov’t estimated the 2021 nominal GDP to settle at P19.985tn. In February, around 9.3mn jobs were generated, although the unemployment rate rose to 8.8% vs 8.7% the month prior. The gov’t is targeting to bring down the unemployment rate to 4-5% next year. Meanwhile, Labor Secretary Silvestre Bello said the number of OFWs who lost their jobs due to the pandemic reached 647,827 (19% of the 3.5mn documented OFWs).

Supreme Court junks Marcos election protest vs VP Robredo

Voting 15-0, the Supreme Court, acting as the Presidential Electoral Tribunal (PET), dismissed the electoral protest filed by Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. against Vice President Maria Leonor “Leni” Robredo. The PET said that Mr. Marcos “…failed to substantiate his allegations of massive anomalies and irregularities in protestee’s favor”.

Gov’t raises P122bn from euro bond sale

The Philippine government raised Eur2.1bn (P122.4bn) from a triple-tranche offering of euro-denominated bonds. The Eur650mn of 4-year global bonds fetched a coupon of 0.25% (75bps above the tenor’s euro mid swap), the Eur650mn of 12-year notes fetched a coupon of 1.2% (+105bps higher than euro mid swap) and the Eur800mn of 20-year note yielded a coupon of 1.75% (+135bps higher than euro mid swap). This was the largest euro-denominated transaction by the government and the first triple-tranche offer. The 20-year bond was also the longest euro-denominated tenor and largest individual tranche issued by the country. All tranches tightened by 25 bps from the initial price guidance backed by a strong order book which allowed the republic to revise its price guidance twice across all three tranches. The latest borrowing was rated Baa2 by Moody’s Investors Service, BBB+ by S&P Global Ratings and BBB by Fitch Ratings. The government plans to raise P3tn this year both from local and foreign sources to plug its budget deficit seen hitting 8.9% of gross domestic product. About P286bn is estimated to come from global bond issuances.

Pulse Asia Survey: Sara top pick for president, Isko for VP

Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte and Manila Mayor Isko Moreno were still the most preferred candidates for president and vice president, respectively. Around 26% of the 2,400 respondents said they would support a presidential bid of Duterte if the elections were held during the survey period last Feb. 22 to March 3. Sara Duterte led a field of 13 potential candidates for president, followed former senator Ferdinand Marcos Jr. (13%), Sen. Grace Poe and Isko Moreno (12%) and Sen. Manny Pacquiao (11%).

Palace appoints Chua as NEDA secretary

President Rodrigo R. Duterte has officially appointed Karl Kendrick T. Chua as Socioeconomic Planning secretary and director-general of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA). Mr. Chua was named acting NEDA chief on April 17, 2020, after the resignation of Ernesto M. Pernia.
