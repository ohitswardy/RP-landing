REGIS REPORT

14 May 2021

The PSEi was flat this week, closing at 6269.36.  Gains earlier in the week fizzled out, pushed lower by continued lockdowns, slow pace of vaccinations,  and a weak GDP print. The recent MSCI rebal announcement put added pressure on the market, with outflows (US$xx) estimated for the Phils on 27 May.  Rebal deletions Aboitiz Power (-5.4% WoW), Puregold (-7.05% WoW), and Megaworld (-13.6% WoW) declined.  Meanwhile, small cap index inclusion Century Pacific (+5.7% WoW) advanced. 

This week saw headline net foreign inflow of US$17m, driven mostly by US$58m inflow brought about by the ACEN secondary offer. Excluding ACEN, foreigners would have been sellers for US$41m. 

1Q GDP contracted 4.2%  YoY as the impact of the pandemic and lockdowns continued to be felt.  The drop was steeper than Street predictions. Investment (i.e. capital formation) was a drag while consumption held up relatively well. 

The 1Q21 results season continued, with a stream of results out again this week. Needless to say, COVID and the lockdowns continued to figure in the numbers.

Cebu Air (+0.7% WoW) posted another headline net loss (P7.3bn) in 1Q as demand continued to be impeded by travel restrictions.  Lingering demand weakness across its operations also led to a 32% core profit drop for Manila Water (-3.1% WoW). 

The gradual easing in capacity restrictions for casinos benefitted Bloomberry (-2.1% WoW). Its net loss narrowed to P781m (from P2.494bn in 4Q20) as GGR jumped 30 QoQ. GGR was still down 44% YoY however. 

For property, earnings fell below expectations.  For Robinsons Land (-3.0% WoW) foot traffic stayed at 40% of pre-COVID levels (similar to 4Q) but mall revenue grew 22% QoQ as Robinsons reimposed minimum rents on some tenants (beginning January). For Vista Land (-1.15% WoW), residential revenue fell 13% as construction activity still appears hampered.  The company is issuing another US$170m in notes due 2027 at 6.5%, presumably to refinance debt maturing next year. VLL has just around US$2bn of net debt.

The consumer sector however turned in solid numbers.   Puregold (-7.05% WoW) grew its operating profit by 11% YoY despite an 8% YoY decline in sales (owing to the absence of last year’s pantry stocking). Margins were strong across both brands, boosted by increased trade spending for Puregold and a favorable forex for S&R. 

Meanwhile,  Jollibee’s (-4.4% WoW) operating profit came in line. Most of JFC’s international businesses have reportedly recovered to pre-pandemic levels. However,  the Philippines continues to be the main underperformer, with SSS down 26% in 1Q. 

For Shakey’s (+7.4% WoW) the reimposition of dine-in restrictions in late March impacted 1Q revenues (-30% YoY).  The Gokongwei family is infusing P1.25bn fresh capital into PIZZA, suggesting an impending acquisition. Following the transaction, the Gokongwei family will hold a 9% stake and one board seat. 

Emperador (-1.6% WoW) managed a 43% jump in its 1Q profit, boosted by its international business. Hard-liquor companies saw sustained volumes/growth throughout the pandemic as consumer shifted to cheaper products and increased off premise consumption. Tanduay of LT Group (+1.6% WoW) saw volumes rise 5% YoY during the quarter. Beverage as well as Tobacoo (flat profit) helped drive LT Gorp’s 1Q profit higher (+4% YOY, +32% QoQ).  Banking unit PNB’s profit was up significatinly though this was due to a drop in provisions.

DMCI’s (+0.9% WoW) headline profit surged 7-fold to P4.25bn. Recurring profit doubled QoQ to P4.019bn, tracking well ahead of Regis and consensus estimates.  Except for Maynilad, all segments outperformed, aided by faster completions, better margins, higher commodity prices, and better power demand.   

Meanwhile, First Gen’s (-1.9% WoW) core net income (after pref divs) rose 21% to $73m, ahead of our FY21 estimates. Group profits were lifted by natural gas (+46%), helped by Avion’s ancillary services agreement that commenced in 2H20 and lower income tax rates stemming from CREATE.

Puregold - 1Q21: margin expansion despite sales decline
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q21 profit jumps 15% YoY on strong margins, boosted by lower tax

PGOLD’s 1Q21 net profit jumped 15% YoY to P2bn, trending above Regis and consensus estimates. While this was boosted by a lower effective tax rate (24% vs 29% in 1Q20), the surprise was on margins. Operating margin expanded a remarkable 150bps YoY despite an 8% decline in sales.

Divergent SSSG trends across brands

The core Puregold brand (72% of consolidated sales) saw SSS decline of 15% during the quarter, coming off the high base boosted by pantry stocking last year. On the other hand. S&R continued to see healthy SSS growth (+8.8%) highlighting the resiliency of its target market (upper income consumers) and illustrating the divergent situations between income classes.

Margin boost in 1Q21

Gross margin for the core Puregold brand expanded +220bps YoY to 17% - an all[1]time high for a first quarter – was driven by increased trade spending. Meanwhile, S&R’s gross margin (+70bps YoY) was boosted by favorable forex.

Top line growth, margins to normalize

Management expects consolidated sales to grow 4-5% in 2021 (Regis FY21E +9%), with the core Puregold brand growing 2-3% (FY21E +5%). While the company is seeing a more meaningful recovery on its wholesale business (e.g. sales to sari-sari stores), we estimate that avg sales per sqm is still 5-10% below pre-pandemic levels, mainly as pedestrian traffic remains pressured by lockdown/mobility restrictions.

Management admitted that margins for the core Puregold brand are unusually high and they expect this to normalize in the coming quarters as the company (and its suppliers) had frontloaded marketing activities to boost sales. Meanwhile, management is optimistic that S&R can maintain gross margins of 23.5-24% (FY21E: 21.9%) given the tailwind from the strong PHP and supply chain efficiencies, which should offset higher logistics costs.

Philippine Consumer - Defying gravity
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Maintaining Buy on CNPF and URC

We re-affirm our Buy ratings on CNPF and URC after reviewing our outlook and estimates following the most recent results. Both CNPF and URC have steadily gained market share in their respective categories even as overall consumption contracted over the past year. This is a testament not only to the values of their brands, but also to the strength of their operations relative to smaller players. Because of strong brands and operational scale, we believe both firms will continue to deliver top line growth despite the near term challenges for the economy: 1) pressure on consumer spending and 2) rising input cost inflation. Both CNPF (17x 2021E PE) and URC (23x) are trading below their respective historical averages, and we believe the risk-reward at these levels remains favorable.

K-shaped recovery seen, but all consumers likely to be more price conscious

We are expecting a K-shaped recovery for the economy and a marked divergence between upper and lower income households, with the latter likely to see a more protracted recovery. The rise of food inflation over the near term, combined with continued limitations on personal mobility are two factors that should lead to consumers spending more on in-home food consumption (and conversely less on outside dining). Increased in-home food consumption will mean heightened demand for shelf-stable packaged food, such as CNPF and URC’s products.

Well-positioned to mitigate impact of rising input cost

Rising cost of inputs is the key near term risk to margins given sharp moves in prices of key commodities, such as palm oil, wheat, meat, and packaging. Both companies are price leaders in their respective categories and, historically, have been able to pass on higher input cost through price increases, albeit gradually. Longer term, we are optimistic about margin improvement as scale efficiencies materialize.

Adjusting forecasts and target prices, maintaining Buy on CNPF and URC

We are adjusting our forecasts and target prices for CNPF and URC mainly to account for 1) input cost inflation, and 2) lower effective tax rates. Our SOTP[1]based target prices use DCF (12% COE, 3% terminal growth) to value the branded businesses, which imply forward PE multiples of 24-28x 2021E. Key downside risks relate to a prolonged recovery in consumer sentiment and heightened input cost inflation.

Globe Telecom - 1Q21: Top line improves, Mynt losses narrow
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

EBITDA drops as spending rises

Globe reported net income of P7.33bn (+11% YoY) for 1Q, on the back of a +3% expansion in revenue. The headline profit was lifted by tax adjustments due to the passage of the CREATE Law, which cut the statutory tax rate. EBITDA and EBITDA margin were otherwise weaker than expected as spending ramped up. Excluding the impact of the new tax law, we estimate 1Q core income would have been down 24% to a disappointing P5.0bn. Nevertheless, we are encouraged by the improvement in the top line.

Revenue expansion led by broadband; market share gain in wireless

Overall service revenue was up +3% YoY as broadband revenue grew 27%. Globe added ~46k subscribers for the wired broadband segment in the quarter, taking the total to 785k. While 1Q revenue for wireless dipped 2% YoY, we see encouraging signs as it was up +1% QoQ. While Globe restated revenue for prior quarters, we believe it was still able to gain revenue market share (+0.7pt).

Higher spend on marketing, provisions and others lead to EBITDA dip

EBITDA contracted 11% to P18.3bn and the margin dropped to 48% as costs related to marketing, provisions and other services rose sharply. Management, however, is still guiding for EBITDA margin above 50% for the year, implying improvements for the rest of the year.

Mynt losses narrow on improving scale

Globe’s share of Mynt’s losses decreased by 62% to P282m, suggesting that losses are narrowing as its GCash service is achieving scale. Without giving absolute figures, management revealed that GCash’s active user base in March 2021 was 4.3x higher than it was the prior year, and revenues for YT-Mar were 5.4x higher. Delinquency rates for its micro-finance business are supposedly in the “single digits” and “best in class”.

SM Prime - 1Q21 overseas residential sales more than double
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Management believes that record presales can be sustained

SM Prime’s 1Q21 reservation sales grew 31% YoY to P32.bn, the second consecutive quarter of record presales. During SM Investments’ recent briefing, Jose Mari Banzon, head of SM Prime’s residential division, revealed that this was driven by sales from overseas, which now comprise 81% of residential sales. Remarkably, Mr. Banzon also believes that SM Prime can sustain this level of reservation sales going forward.

Overseas sales more than double

The proportion of sales from abroad jumped to 81% from 49% in 1Q20. This implies that domestic sales halved while overseas sales climbed 116%. Mr. Banzon credits the strong overseas sales to the better COVID recovery of other countries. He also noted that SMPH buyers generally have high-paying jobs. Although overseas sales include both OFWs and foreign nationals, we expect foreigners to comprise less than 10% of total presales.

Sales force is growing; most are based abroad

Mr. Banzon said that the company’s sales force (a combination of in-house sales agents and third-party brokers) grew 2,000 to 3,000 in the past year to 17,500, about a 10-20% increase. For comparison, Ayala Land’s sales force shrank from 8,200 in 2019 to 7,800 in 2020. 77% of SM Prime’s 17,500 agents are based abroad. In fact, the company has more affiliated brokers (and correspondingly, more sales) in UAE than the Philippines.

1Q21 sales mostly from Manila, but more provincial projects will be launched

70% of 1Q21 presales came from newly launched projects, which were mostly located in Makati and the Manila Bay area. However, SM Prime believes there is substantial demand for affordable housing in provincial areas and will thus grow launches of such projects. These products will be priced below P3m/unit, much lower than the P6.4mn/unit average in 1Q21.

Robinsons Land - 1Q21 earnings disappoint despite China revenue; mall outlook worsens
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q21 earnings disappoint despite revenue from China

1Q21 net profit fell 8% YoY to P2.9bn. Excluding gains from write-offs of deferred tax liabilities, we estimate that profit would have dropped 65% YoY to P2.1bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Although revenue from China helped grow total revenue by 35%, margins from China and the malls disappointed. While mall revenue improved QoQ, the near-term outlook for this segment has deteriorated due to the hard lockdown as well as the slow pace of vaccinations.

Mall revenue climbs QoQ on reimposition of minimum rents

Foot traffic stayed at 40% of pre-COVID levels (similar to 4Q) but mall revenue grew 22% QoQ as Robinsons reimposed minimum rents on some tenants (beginning January). This is similar to what went on with the other major mall operators. However, since the re-imposition of a hard lockdown in end-March, the company has once again waived minimum rents.

Office occupancy improves QoQ, bucking the industry deterioration

Office occupancy improved to 93% (91% in 4Q20) thanks to new BPO sign-ups. This is a contrast to the industry’s deterioration in occupancy from 91% (in 4Q20) to 89%. Meanwhile, we estimate that RLC’s 2021 office pipeline is 40% preleased, higher than the industry’s 30%. Preleasing of the company’s Metro Manila pipeline appears higher at c. 55%.

Philippine reservation sales drop due to less foreign buyers

Philippine reservation sales fell 28% YoY to P2.8bn, as sales to foreigners shrank nearly 70% and now comprise only 13% of total sales. We estimate that sales to Filipinos dropped by a more modest 10%. China project winding down RLC has launched RMB3.4bn worth of units in China and has sold 93%. It recognized c. RMB1.4bn in 1Q21 and expects to recognize the remaining c. RMB2bn next year. The company said it will not pursue further projects in China.

Security Bank - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 Rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Reaffirming SECB’s commitment to the consumer

At our call last 3 May, Security Bank’s CEO Mr Sanjiv Vohra reiterated the bank’s long-term vision of being “the most customer-centric bank in the Philippines”. While mindful of the current challenges, SECB remains committed to its strategy of growing its consumer business, both within the bank, and through its JV with Krungsri of Thailand. SECB also reckons its continuing IT investments are central to the bank’s goal of getting back into the top 3 banks in terms of ROE.

Pandemic challenges have not changed strategic views

Mr Vohra believes 1H21 will remain challenging, and continues to assess the impact of Covid-19 lockdowns on various sectors. Low loan demand, high liquidity, and competition are also dragging down margins. However, he believes this once-in-a-century event should not alter the bank’s fundamental goals – to move away from Treasury and bring the bank’s focus to the customer. He cited the “three pillars” of the strategy – developing the bank’s talent pool, building a strong risk management infrastructure, and technology.

SECB to focus on mass affluent consumers for loans and fees

The main drivers for the consumer push have not changed – favorable demographics, rising per capita incomes, and low household debt. Consumer loans – in non-Covid times – deliver better risk-adjusted returns compared to corporate loans. The main bank will target the mass affluent through its wealth management products, and through its bancassurance partnership with FWD.

… while Krungsri JV to target mass retail

SB Finance, the 50-50 joint venture between SECB and Bank of Ayudhya of Thailand (aka Krungsri) will be used to serve the mass retail market. SECB and Krungsri agreed to infuse P3bn in new equity into the JV. SB Finance plans to offer new products, leveraging off Krungsri’s expertise in consumer finance in Thailand. The “Car-4-Cash” program will be the first product launched.

On technology and fintech

Mr Vohra views the bank’s IT spend as crucial to both its customer-centric, and its ROE goals. He reckons that a balance must be struck between branches, online/mobile, and alternative channels. Technology also brings down costs, particularly in serving the high-volume consumer segment. Mr Vohra does not view fintech as a threat (yet), and is open to partnerships that will be tech-led.

On ROEs, capital and M&As

SECB’s goal remains to return to the top 3 among banks in terms ROE. Mr Vohra is comfortable with the bank’s current capital (solo CET-1 was 18.8% at end[1]2020) but continues to assess it based on growth opportunities. SECB is always open to M&A: Mr Vohra said the bank will likely take a look at the potential sale of Citibank’s domestic consumer portfolio once the details are released (see our recent report).

Manila Water - PH units pull down 1Q21 profit
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q21 core profit drops 32% YoY to P1.33bn

MWC reported headline 1Q21 profit of P1.29bn and core profit of P1.33bn, down 8% and 32% from 1Q20 figures that were themselves materially increased following a restatement. It is unclear if the core profit figure excludes ~P200mn of deferred tax adjustments but as it is, it trails Regis and consensus forecasts. Operations were weighed down by demand weakness in the Laguna, Boracay, and Clark concessions, as well as in Estate Water. EBITDA dropped 16% YoY, as underlying operations for its PH assets, including its core East zone unit, remained soft, more than offsetting improvements of its regional investments.

East zone: Billed volume decline accelerates to -6% YoY

The East zone’s core profit dropped 29% YoY to P1.28bn in 1Q21, broadly in line with forecast. Billed volumes fell 6% YoY, dropping further from 4Q (-1%). The customer mix was also skewed to residential, which pay lower tariffs, and resulted in a 6% drop in effective tariffs. EBITDA fell 11% YoY to P2.78bn and EBITDA margin was steady at 71%. Expenditures trail our forecast, with 1Q21 capex at just P2.1bn (vs. FY21E: P12.5bn).

Philippine ventures still struggle; Regional investments rebound

PH ops failed to turn a profit in 1Q21, recording a core loss of P47mn. Billed volumes remained soft in Estate Water, Boracay Water, and Clark Water. The pandemic continues to delay projects of Estate Water, which in turn delayed the recognition of supervision fees paid by Ayala Land to Estate Water for connecting the former’s customers to the water network within its horizontal projects. Meanwhile, its investments in Thailand and Vietnam have turned the corner, on the back of better demand and tariff adjustments.

Revised concession agreement effectivity date still pending

The company is currently waiting for (1) the government’s new undertaking letter, which will guarantee the expiration payment, and (b) the revisions to its common purpose facilities agreement. Once complete (likely by September), the new concession agreement for the East zone should already become effective.

1Q21 EBITDA 10x of 4Q; volume shows recovery potential

BLOOM’s net loss narrowed to P781mn (from -P2.494bn in 4Q20) as GGR jumped 30% QoQ to P6.884bn. While mobility restrictions were generally the same in both periods, the regulator quietly allowed casinos to increase capacity from 30% to 50%. EBITDA jumped to P1.4bn, from only P129mn in the previous quarter, as revenues decidedly breached operating break-even levels.

GGR improves to 50% of pre-Covid levels as local patrons return

1Q21 GGR hit P6.884bn (+30%QoQ, -44%YoY), implying an average run-rate of P2.3bn/month (about 50% of pre-Covid level). Mass table GGR sustained its recovery (+27%QoQ), and was largely volume driven. Growth of slots tapered off (+3%QoQ volume, +12%QoQ GGR). Meanwhile, a good win-rate (+4.26%) lifted VIP GGR (+65%QoQ) despite soft volume. As we mentioned previously, the limited local VIP player pool will likely result in lumpy VIP revenues and volatile win rates until foreign players are eventually allowed to travel.

No material developments for online initiative so far

Management said that there was hardly any notable contribution from online initiatives. The current focus is on optimizing the platform, the technologies being used and the payment gateways to make it more appealing to players. Management pointed to 3Q-4Q of this year as an estimate of when the revenue contribution could be more meaningful.

Operations update – there is local visitation amidst restrictions in the city

Stopping short of saying they remained operational throughout the lockdown, management explained that foot traffic has recovered to an average of 3k per day in the first 10 days of May (MECQ) after dropping to a few hundred in early April (ECQ). Although down from 8K in March pre-lockdown, we think it highlights the resilience of visitation from local patrons. It also means GGR could be >10% of pre-Covid levels, despite being technically “closed” to the general public.

Shakey’s Pizza - 1Q21 EBITDA margin improves; Gokongwei family infuses new capital
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q21 revenues trail expectations even as EBITDA margin improves

Revenues for Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA) fell a disappointing 30% to P1.3bn in 1Q21. As a result, the company just about broke even in 1Q21 (net profit of P29mn) and EBITDA (-31% YoY to P240mn) is tracking behind estimates. The reimposition of stricter lockdown measures will push back revenue recovery but cost control measures are mitigating the damage.

1Q21 EBITDA margin continues to improve even as dine-in restricted anew

Revenues during the quarter declined 13% QoQ, which management attributed to seasonality (softer post-holiday sales), as well as the reimposition of dine-in restrictions due to stricter lockdown measures toward the end of the quarter. The contribution of on-premise sales declined to c.25% of total sales from c.30% in the previous quarter. Notwithstanding the continued pressure on the top line, EBITDA margin continued to improve sequentially due to tight cost controls.

Store expansion to resume in 2021, more cloud kitchens planned

Despite the challenging operating environment, management maintained its store opening target to 30 stores in 2021, of which eight were opened in 1Q21. The company also plans to accelerate the rollout of its cloud/satellite kitchens (six currently), adding around five more throughout the year.

Gokongwei family infuses new capital, takes 9% stake in PIZZA

The Gokongwei family, through private holdco JE Holdings, is infusing P1.25bn (152mn new shares at P8.20/sh) fresh capital into PIZZA. Following the transaction, the Gokongwei family will hold a 9% stake and one board seat. We believe the transaction could be a prelude to a potential acquisition, which management alluded to during the briefing. We believe any acquisition will likely be within the same casual dining/mid-market space so as to maximize any potential synergies with the existing operations of PIZZA.

JFC 1Q21 operating income at P1.5bn – in line; to issue PHP prefs to partly buy back USD perps.

JFC disclosed that 1Q21 operating income was P1.5bn from a P1.2bn operating loss last year. AGI’s 4Q profit +48%qoq to P3.0bn; casino and McDonald’s improve on looser restrictions

In a delayed release, AGI said that FY20 income hit P8.8bn, down 50% from last year. The result is slightly better than our FY estimate (P8.6bn), with the outperformance of Emperador largely offsetting the shortfalls from Megaworld and Travellers. In 4Q20 alone, AGI’s net income jumped 48%QoQ, reflecting rebounds in property and Golden Arches, as well as lower losses from the casino. EMP’s 4Q20 profit dipped 18%QoQ to P2.092bn, dragged by lower GP at both brandy and whisky segments. MEG’s 4Q20 profit of P2.5bn was up 23%QoQ but still down 52%YoY. Cignal launches broadband via satellite service

Cignal, the satellite TV operator indirectly owned by PLDT, launched Cignal Connect. Cignal Connect is broadband service delivered via satellite. Four plans are available ranging from P6,500 (US$130) per month for 2mbps to as much P23,500 (US$490) per month for 10mbps. This is 10-20x more expensive than comparable wired broadband services. The service will clearly be a niche service for areas unserved by either wired broadband or mobile data. PLDT holds Cignal through its employee pension fund (PLDT BTF).

Cebu Air posts 1Q21 net loss of P7.3bn; More on convertible bond issuance

1Q21 EBITDA loss at P3.0bn (Behind estimates). Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported a net loss of P7.3bn in 1Q21. The EBITDA loss during the quarter was slightly down QoQ at P3.0bn, trailing our full-year forecast. Pax volumes improved by 78% QoQ to 550k in 1Q21. This improvement, however, was at the expense of a 45% QoQ drop in average fares. Pax volumes in 1Q were about 10% of pre-pandemic levels, which we largely expected. However, the recent lockdown could also mean the recovery we’re expecting in 2Q could be pushed back to 2H21. Note that the government is targeting to inoculate half of the population by year-end, with vaccines arriving mostly in 3Q.  Admittedly, administration of vaccines has been slow so far. Meanwhile, cash costs were higher than expected, due to higher maintenance expenses (+1% YoY) and admin costs (-10% YoY).  We suspect there were still one-off expenses booked in 1Q, such as separation payments for example. The company also provided more information on the US$250mn convertible bond (CB) placement, which will be issued to IFC and Indigo.  Broadly, the terms of the CB mirror the terms of the convertible prefs (CPS), as the CBs (a) will have a conversion price of P38/sh, (b) will have a maturity of six (6) years), and (c) are immediately convertible upon issuance.  The only difference is that the CB holders will receive an interest rate of 4.5%, lower than the interest rate for the CPS (6%) and our forecast (6%). Upon full conversion of the CBs and CPS, CEB’s shares outstanding will rise to 1.248bn shares from c. 600mn today.  Our forecast assumes a fully-diluted share count of 1.252bn.  Note that the lower-than-expected interest rate on the CB will not have an impact to our Regis profit estimate. 

Bank lending contracts by 4.5% in March; M3 growth moderates to 8.3%

Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, further contracted to 4.5% in March from -2.7% in February. Consumer loans fell 10% YoY and 1.8% MoM while loans to retail and manufacturing sectors still soft. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose 8.3% to P14.2tn in March, moderating from the 9.4% expansion in February.

 

Vista Land to issue US$170mn in notes

Vista Land will issues US$170mn in notes due 2027 with a yield of 6.5%. Vista Land currently has US$900mn in US$ debt, which about US$365mn is due in 2022. As of end-2020, Vista Land’s net debt/equity stood at 98%. VLL is trading at 4.6X FY21E P/E, 0.4X FY21E P/B, and 59% discount to NAV.

FGEN 1Q21 net income +21% to $78mn

FGEN’s 1Q21 recurring net income  was $78mn, up 21%YoY on new contracts, lower opex and interest expense. After preferred dividends, we estimate core income was $73mn (+21%), tracking ahead of our FY21E of $262mn. The attributable earnings from natural gas grew 46% to $57mn on the back of higher electricity sales due to Avion’s ancillary services agreement and a lower tax rate. EDC’s attributable net income was up 4% to US$27mn, helped by lower expenses and interest costs and FiT adjustments. Income from hydro was flat – higher revenues were offset by higher replacement power costs.

PNB 1Q21 NPAT +34% YoY; provisions drop despite rising NPLs

PNB reported 1Q21 NPAT of P1.77bn, up 34% YoY. Despite the headline number, we reckon the quality of earnings was pretty poor. The bank cut provisions, which was a bit of a head-scratcher, given a jump in NPLs from 10.20% at end-2020 to 10.74% in 1Q21. This took NPL cover to an in-credibly low 43%. (Even the NPL figure was odd, as by our calculation, the end-2020 ratio was 11.1% based on the bank’s FS.) Loans contracted 6.4%, and NIMs collapsed 72bps QoQ and 43bps YoY. To the upside, fee incomes were very strong, handily surpassing pre-Covid levels.

Gokongwei family busy into Shakey’s Pizza

The Gokongwei family, through JE Holdings (same holdco as RRHI), will be infusing P1.25bn in new capital into PIZZA. After the transaction, JE Holdings will own approx. 9% of PIZZA.

Gov’t eases quarantine status in NCR+; shifts to “GCQ with heightened restrictions”

Metro Manila and 4 adjacent provinces will be under GCQ 'with heightened restrictions' from May 15 until the end of May. This means 18 to 65-year-old persons can leave their homes. Only essential travel in and out of NCR Plus allowed. 20% capacity for indoor dine-in in restaurants, 50% capacity for outdoor or al fresco dine-in dine-in, 30% capacity for outdoor tourist sites, 10% capacity for religious gatherings, gatherings for necrological services, wakes, inurnment, funerals. Meanwhile, outdoor non-contact sports, games, scrimmages outdoors will be allowed. Also, 30% capacity for personal care services which do not require the removal of masks, like salons, parlors, and beauty clinics.

Monetary Board keeps policy settings steady

The BSP’s Monetary Board decided to maintain the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight reverse repurchase facility at 2.0%. This is in line street expectations.

Industry car sales fall 14% MoM in April; Toyota sales drop 6% MoM

Vehicle sales reported by CAMPI/TMA dropped 14% MoM to 17,843 units in April. They cited the reimposition of tighter quarantine measures, as well as tighter bank lending and the safeguard duty as the reason for the weakness. This brings 4M21 industry car sales to 88,155 units (+36% YoY). CAMPI/TMA is forecasting industry sales to grow by +20-25% (with provisional duty) this year. Toyota sold 8,642 units in April, down 6% MoM and brings 4M21 retail sales to 41,737 units (+62% YoY). Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year. To reach our forecast, Toyota will need to sell c. 9,400 units per month. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). Meanwhile, to reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales should average 7,800 units. Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 12% discount to its market-based NAV of P599/sh.

PH trade deficit narrows in March

Preliminary data for March showed a narrower trade deficit of US$2.4bn. Merchandise exports surged 32% YoY, a reversal of the 1.5% contraction in February. Meanwhile, imports sustained its recovery, rising 17% YoY and accelerating from the +9% YoY recovery in Feb. This brings 1Q21 trade deficit to US$7.1bn, lower by 13% YoY.

3.5mn more COVID vaccine doses arrive

3.5mn COVID vaccine doses arrived from May 7 to 9. The breakdown is 1.5mn Sinovac doses and 2mn AstraZeneca doses (through the COVAX facility). The country has received a total of 7.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 2.3mn doses. The government expects at least 3.5mn more vaccine doses to arrive in May.
