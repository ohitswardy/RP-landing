REGIS REPORT

30 April 2021

The PSEi was flat (-0.1%WoW) this week, closing at 6370.87.

Trading was lackluster even as NCR Plus’ lockdown status (MECQ) was extended by another two weeks to May 14.  Curfew hours have been shortened however to 10pm-4am (from 8pm to 5am).  Nonetheless, they daily tally of new infections remained high despite coming off peak levels.

The government is allowing limited dining-in for restaurants in NCR Plus of 10% of seating capacity. Barber shops and salons are also being allowed to re-open. 

Foreign selling continued this week, however, coming to -US$55m. Excluding the AREIT transaction, foreign selling was actually higher at US$76m.  The property sector saw among the top net sells this week with US$91m in net outflows for Ayala Land and US$17m for SMPH.  

Gainers outnumbered losers though 9:5.

Philippine National Bank (+35.7% WoW) surged this week. PNB is distributing 51% of PNB Holdings as property dividend to its shareholders. There is also an intention to list PHC via introduction. PHC is the holding company for three of the bank’s most valuable pieces of real estate which Regis estimates at a sizeable  value of P41bn. This compares to PNB’s market cap of ~P48bn. A PHC listing would be a tax efficient way of monetizing these assets (stocks listed the PSE are only levied a 0.6% tax when selling vs capital gains tax of 6% of value). For PNB parent LT Group (+0.3% WoW), the  transaction should result in an increase in its “visible” NAV. LTG will receive its proportionate share of PHC (~29%), which is likely to be worth ~P12bn based on PHC’s underlying property value.

1Q results continued to come in.

BDO’s (-1.8% WoW) loans as of 1Q contracted 0.7% YoY.  Fee incomes however recovered nicely. NPLs also were benign in 1Q – up only 16bps QoQ to 2.8%.

Wilcon’s (+3.5% WoW) numbers showed signs of recovery from the strict ECQ in the last two weeks of March last year.   Net sales grew 19% on the back of 9.8% SSSG.  On the other hand, RRHI’s (+0.8% WoW) SSS for its Supermarket segment dropped 15% YoY.  SSS for the Supermarket segment (59% of sales) declined 15% YoY largely due to the high base in 1Q20 caused by pantry stocking.

In the meantime, Meralco’s (-0.8% WoW) results showed that demand (at -4% YoY for 1Q) has yet to fully recover.  Aboitiz Power (+1.1% WoW) also reported an 8% decline in electricity demand for its distribution utilities during the quarter.

Globe Telecom - Notes from Regis CEO Roundtable Series
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Thoughts on GCash, the new entrant and more

The most recent guest at our Regis CEO Roundtable Series, Mr. Ernest Cu, CEO of Globe Telecom, discussed the impact of the pandemic on operations and the threat from the new telecom operator. He also shared his thoughts about Globe’s belated push into wired broadband as well the industry’s currently elevated capex levels. The most amount of time, however, was spent on the group’s e-wallet business, GCash, whose outlook he sounded particularly upbeat about.

Momentum behind GCash

The group’s e-money/e-wallet operation GCash, which is held under associate Mynt, has seen a massive increase in adoption due to the pandemic. Transactions were up 4x in 2020 and are likely further up in the “multiples” again this year. Unit costs are also declining, which should accelerate the move to earnings breakeven (which Mr. Cu hopes will come in a “couple of years”).

Thoughts on the 3rd operator

Regarding the threat from the new entrant, Mr. Cu argues that a rational operator intent on generating a positive return will not lead to a destructive situation for the industry. He also contrasted the Philippine situation with India, where the new entrant took five years and massive amounts of money to build its network before launching. In a country where nearly everyone has a SIM card and majority have more than one, it is important to be considered the “primary SIM” for one to generate revenue and become profitable. With a network that is still only a fraction of the incumbents’, it will take a while for the threat to become meaningful.

Better late than never

Globe is now making a more aggressive push into wired broadband (fiber) with a target of more than 1m new FTTH lines to be put into service this year. Mr. Cu acknowledged that Globe was late to this market shift (accelerated adoption of broadband). The rapid rise in home broadband take-up was attributable to the changes in the usage patterns brought about by the pandemic. Nonetheless, penetration of high-speed broadband is still low, and there is room to catch up.

Bank of the Philippine Islands - Benign NPLs (2.76%) boost 1Q21; NPAT dragged by one-off tax write-down
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Flat NPLs – an inflection point, or a pause before another surge?

BPI reported NPAT of P5.0bn in 1Q21, ahead of our expectations, but in line with the Street. The “beat” was largely due to surprisingly benign NPLs (up just 8bps QoQ), resulting in much lower provisions than we forecast. That said, NPLs could still pick up given the recent lockdowns. Meanwhile PPOP was in line as higher trading gains offset lower NIMs. A one-time write-off of deferred tax assets dragged headline profit down, though.

Loans down 5%, NIMs compress; but strong fees, trading boost PPOP

Loan fell 5% YoY in 1Q21; while most segments contracted, mortgages and microfinance loans grew 11% YoY. Deposits were flat, but a 12% YoY jump in low[1]cost deposits took CASA ratio to 82.6%. However, this was not enough to arrest the squeeze in NIMs, which we estimate at -15bps QoQ. The reduction in NII was offset by resurgent fees incomes. And while trading/fx gains of P1.7bn were down 18% YoY, it was still impressive given rising bond yields in Feb/March.

NPLs surprisingly benign; NPL cover rises to 124%; RLs inch up to 1.2%

The bank’s NPL ratio of 2.76% was up just 8bps QoQ, while Restructured Loans were 1.24% of total, up slightly from 1.09% at end-2020. At its earnings call, management noted improving NPL trends across most segments, except credit cards and personal loans. Provision expense was just P3.6bn in 1Q21, for an annualized credit cost of c100bps, much lower than our c150bps forecast for the full year. But this allowed BPI to increase NPL coverage to 123.5% (from 115%). The bank remains wary of new NPLs, as Metro Manila went into lockdown in April. Management is holding to its base case of peak NPLs of 4% in 2021E.

CREATE good for recurring taxes, but one-time DTA write off drags 1Q NPAT

The CREATE Act cut the corporate income tax rate from 30% to 25%. However, as we had expected, the law also forced a P2.7bn one-time write-off of deferred tax assets, all of which was booked in 1Q21

Manila Electric Company - 1Q21: Demand weakness lingers
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Profits fall short as power consumption declines

Meralco reported net profit of P4.33bn for 1Q21, inclusive of ~P2.7bn of write[1]offs of deferred tax assets and P3.2bn of gains relating to the reversal of accruals of local taxes. Stripping out these and other one-off items, the company claims core income of P5.11bn for the quarter, down 11% YoY, which would be trending below estimates. The main reason for the apparent shortfall was the fact that electricity sales declined by 4%.

Electricity sales down 4%

Electricity sales declined by 4% as the lockdown continues to take its toll. Commercial volumes (which includes offices and shopping centers) plunged 18% even as residential consumption rose +7%. Surprisingly, industrial usage actually grew 4%. Initial indications show April volumes rising ~20% YoY but remember that this is off the lowest levels last year when the lockdowns were at their most severe.

Acquisition of GBPC complete

Meralco completed the acquisition of Global Business Power (GBPC) by end of March. Meralco acquired the 86% of GBPC it did not previously own for P34bn and will begin to consolidate its operations from 2Q21. Despite the purchase, Meralco was still in a net cash position of P25bn (US$0.5bn).

New tariffs set by next year?

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has released a timetable aiming for a tariff reset to take effect by July 2022 for Meralco and two other distribution utilities (DUs). We are doubtful, however, that the full tariff review can be completed for implementation by that time. Separately, the ERC is also still evaluating options on how to deal with the lapsed period from July 2015, which could have a huge impact on Meralco’s possible refunds.

BDO Unibank - Resurgent fees, low provisions lift 1Q21 NPAT +19% YoY
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

PPOP in line; NPAT boosted by benign NPLs, low provisions

BDO’s 1Q21 NPAT surged 19% YoY to P10.4bn, handily beating our forecasts. NIMs dropped 29bps QoQ, but this was compensated for by strong fee income, notably from trust and insurance operations. While PPOP was in line, provision expense was significantly lower than we expected, owing to remarkably benign NPLs (+16bps QoQ to 2.81%). However, NPL cover dipped to 107%, leaving little room for any deterioration in asset quality as a result of the new lockdowns.

NIMs fall 29bps QoQ, as deposit costs flatten out; fee incomes recover

Loans contracted 0.7% YoY in 1Q21 (Figure 1), though this was better than the - 3.1% posted by the sector in February. Deposits were up 2% but CASA grew 11% taking the CASA ratio to a record 83%. Despite this, NIMs still compressed by 29bps QoQ. Loan yields continued to fall (-42bps QoQ), while deposit costs seem to have bottomed, down 4bps QoQ to an impressive 0.27% (Figures 2-4). To the upside, fee incomes have recovered nicely, with trust fees up 19% and insurance premiums surging 31% YoY. BDO reckons opex was up just 2% YoY; there was some “noise” though as the bank implemented an accounting change for the trading gains of its insurance business starting end-2020.

NPLs remarkably benign; RLs down QoQ; NPL coverage dips to 107%

BDO’s asset quality was remarkably benign in 1Q12. Its NPL ratio stood at 2.81%, up 16bps QoQ (Figure 5), but better than the sector average of 4.16% as of February (+46bps ytd). Restructured Loans improved to P64.4bn from P68.6bn at end-2020. The bank took the opportunity to drop provision expense down to P2.9bn, or an annualized credit cost of c50bps, significantly lower than our forecast of about 115bps for FY2021E. This took NPL coverage down slightly to 107% from 109.5% at end-2020 (Figure 6). We are wary, though, of a new round of NPL formation as Metro Manila and other provinces went into lockdown for the month of April.

Philippine Banks - Citibank retreat could change the credit card landscape
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Banks express interest in Citi’s consumer loan portfolio

Citibank recently announced it intends to withdraw from consumer banking in the Philippines and 12 other countries. We estimate its domestic credit card receivables to be around P70bn, making it the 3rd or 4th largest player, with around 17% market share. All banks we cover are interested in that portfolio. And by our estimates, all four have sufficient funds and capital for it. The sale would be a game-changer for whichever bank eventually makes the acquisition.

Citibank credit card portfolio – a plum prize

Citibank (Phils) had total loans of P154bn at end-2020. Of this, we estimate some P70bn was credit cards, with a smaller book of unsecured personal loans, and the balance in institutional lending. That would put Citibank’s market share of credit card receivables at a formidable 17% (see Figure 1, below). Citibank’s card holders tend to be higher-income, also likely to be wealth management clients.

A potential game-changer for the winner Figure 1 illustrates how a sale of Citibank’s credit card book could radically change the sector’s landscape. A winning bid from BPI could catapult it from a far #3 to a solid #1 position in the segment. Meanwhile, either BDO or MBT could become by far the largest player. At the other end, SECB taking the portfolio would serve its long-term goal of growing its consumer loan franchise.

Willing and able – all banks we cover can fund it, but…

Publicly, BPI has most keenly expressed interest in Citibank’s portfolio, while BDO was more reserved. Management of both MBT and SECB separately say they view it as an opportunity that they will likely consider. On paper, all four banks we cover have more than enough capital to fund an acquisition (see right-hand column). It is unclear whether the sale will include Citibank’s retail deposits. And, of course, our computations will change depending on what kind of premium the winning bank is prepared to pay for it.

Robinsons Retail - SSS declines in 1Q21 on high base; special dividend declared
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q21 net profit +2%; operating profit (-30%) trending behind forecasts

RRHI’s net profit rose 2% YoY to P945mn in 1Q21. While this appears to be in line with our estimates, the bottomline was boosted by adjustments on tax relating to the tax reform law CREATE. While sales of P35.6bn (-11%YoY) was broadly in line, operating profit was below expectations as it declined a steeper 30% YoY, amplified by fixed costs. Among the various formats, Dept Stores and Specialty Stores (collectively 12% of sales), underperformed due to lockdown restrictions.

Supermarkets: SSS decline due to high base but margins steady

SSS for the Supermarket segment (59% of sales) declined 15% YoY largely due to the high base in 1Q20, when sales were boosted by pantry stocking. Notwithstanding the top line contraction, EBITDA margin for the segment (8.1% in 1Q21) was largely flat YoY. Excluding GrowSari and NoBrand (reclassified from the Specialty Store segment), management indicated that EBITDA margin would have expanded by 20bps YoY due to continued supplier support and the full integration of Rustans.

Drug Stores: Rose Pharmacy achieves profitability

Drug Store segment sales (17% of sales) grew 17% YoY, boosted by the consolidation of Rose Pharmacy. Ex-Rose we estimate sales contracted 19% YoY. We understand that Rose Pharmacy was already profitable as of 1Q21 (EBITDA margin 3.5%), as trading terms with RRHI’s existing drug store operations were aligned. Management expects operating margins to improve in the coming quarters as opex synergies are further realized.

Special dividend declared; 2021 outlook maintained

RRHI declared a regular dividend of P0.83/sh (c40% payout of previous year’s earnings) plus an additional special div of P1/sh. This brings div yield to 3.5% and amounts to a total dividend of P2.8bn, which is only a modest reduction to its cash pile of P30bn. Management intends to maintain the payout ratio, and possibly the special div, if there is no major M&A in the pipeline. Meanwhile, management maintained its FY21 SSSG guidance of 0-3% (Regis: +12%) given the imposition of lockdown restrictions anew, which reduces visibility on near term consumer mobility trends.

Philippine Property - BPOs waiting to expan
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Meeting with IT-BPO Association

We met with the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) to get an update on the industry. Attorney Luis Salvador (Executive Director for External Affairs and Investor Relations) and Carole Gaffud (Senior Research Manager) shared some of their insights on the outlook for the BPO sector, which has important implications for office property as well as the broader economy.

BPO sector bucks the 2020 downturn

While Philippine real GDP fell 9.6% in 2020, the Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) sector was a source of resilience, growing revenue and employment by 1- 2% to US$26.7bn and 1.32mn employees. IBPAP also notes that despite the increase in prevalence of remote work, the sector also had mild net expansion in office footprint last year.

Passage of CREATE removes uncertainty While the recently passed Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act reduces the duration in which BPOs can avail of fiscal incentives, Atty. Salvador views its passage rather positively.

First, the final version essentially preserved the existing incentive structure for BPOs. Previous proposals from Congress would have reduced incentives further and offered a shorter period in which to avail of them.

Second, the passage removed the uncertainty hanging over the sector since 2016 (when a reduction in incentives was first proposed by the government).

BPOs looking to expand office space further but…

Atty. Salvador said that the 14 years of incentives (for new or expansion projects) under CREATE would be sufficient to entice companies to invest in the country. In fact, he is in discussions with potential new entrants to the Philippines as well as existing BPOs looking to expand. However, he confirmed what office brokers have been telling us: a number of BPOs want to take-up more office space but want to do so when there have been more vaccinations so that they can fully utilize the office. Thus, delays in vaccinations could in turn hamper near-term office take-up from the sector.

We still expect office vacancy to increase

Despite the generally optimistic outlook on the BPO industry conveyed during the meeting, we continue to have a negative stance on the office segment. We already expect BPO office demand to return to pre-COVID levels in 2021E-2022E. However, due to POGO closures and supply completions, we forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to rise from 9% in 2020 (11% as of 1Q21) to 12% by the end of 2021E (and stay at 12% in 2022E). Consequently, we also expect office rental rates to drop further (see “Sluggish recovery vs wall of liquidity”).

 
 

Corporate News

PNB declares property dividend – positive for LTG

PNB declared a property dividend covering its ownership of PNB Holdings Corp (PHC). What is the significance?  PHC is the holding company for three of its most valuable properties: PNB headquarters along Macapagal Avenue (near Manila Bay), Allied building on Ayala Avenue and the PNB property at the corner of Gil Puyat (Buendia) and Paseo de Roxas which is inextricably linked to Duterte’s telecom adviser (i.e. the “RJ property”). This means LTG will now have direct majority ownership (56%) of these three property assets.  We are awaiting more detail on these assets. This will be a meaningful direct boost to LTG’s NAV (we will have better idea of the impact after we get details).

PMFTC volumes down 24% in 1Q21

Philip Morris International disclosed the other day that cigarette volumes in the Philippines (PMFTC) for 1Q21 came om at 8.1bn sticks, down 24%YoY and 15%QoQ. “Reflecting the lower total market, lower market share due primarily to mid-price Fortune, partly offset by Marlboro”. Net revenues increased reflecting a favorable pricing variance. The company is “Targeting stable organic net revenues in 2021”. Additionally, IQOS is almost 1% share in Metro Manila at end of 1Q. Volumes continue to feel the impact from the price hikes implemented in October 2020 which was +20/pack across brands (+20-29%). Industry volumes contracted less than PMFTC (-14%YoY, -13%QoQ).

WLCON 1Q21 net profit +84% - in line

In a press release, WLCON reported 1Q21 net profit of P604mn, up 84% YoY, broadly within expectations. Net sales grew 19% on the back of 9.8% SSSG. Two depots were opened during the period, bringing total store count to 65. The strong top line growth was partly driven by base effects relating to the temporary store closures during ECQ in the last week of March 2020. The gross margin improved to 36.3%, tracking above forecasts. While contribution of in-house brands came down (49.5% of sales in 1Q21 vs 50.9% in 1Q20), the actual margin for these products expanded. On top of the GPM improvement, op margin expanded due to proportionately lower opex (23.2% of sales in 1Q21 vs 25.6% in 1Q20).

Aboitiz Power 1Q21 up 197%YoY

Aboitiz Power reported a core income of P6.2bn, up 197%YoY +2%QOQ. The “core” income included liquidated damages from the GNPD delay and business interruption claims from a GNP Mariveles outage last year totaling about P2.4bn after-tax. Excluding these, core income would have been 84% higher. Core income from generation & RES jumped to P6.036bn (+214%YoY). Outside the boost from insurance claims, energy sales grew (rev +3%, volume +8%) while gross margin improved on lower generation costs (-3%) and purchased power (-31%). Management said that weekly peak demand in Luzon and Visayas in 1Q21 was similar to 2019 levels, and that the impact from the restrictions on demand is much less than the original lockdowns in March 2020. Core income from distribution also improved to P1.090bn (+6%YoY). While total power sales were still down 8% (commercial and industrial -13%, resi +3%), gross margin per kwh printed a little higher at P1.64/kwh vs P1.58/kwh in 1Q20 due to the difference in volume mix. While mostly soft in the first two months, spot prices jumped to P3.87/kWh in March after hovering at close to P2 since October. Management said the GNPower Mariveles  is on outage and is targeted to be back only by 3Q22. He mentioned both units of Sual are on outage. Management discussed plans to increase re capacity to 3,900MW by 2030, including the 1,600MW already in the pipeline for the next few years. They are looking to partner with foreign operators including Scatec (new owner of SN Power) and a partner in a previous bid for a Vietnam project.

FGEN says it secured BW Gas’s LNG vessel ahead of others

First Gen Corp disclosed that Norway’s BW Gas Ltd has committed to provide a liquefied natural gas vessel to ahead of a similar deal with another local company. The disclosure was made in response to the Department of Energy approval of Vires Energy Corp’s application to proceed with its planned gas-fired power plant with LNG storage and regasification facility. The Doe referred to BW Paris, which First Gen claims has already been contracted to FGEN LNG Corp since November. Earlier this month, FGEN LNG and BW FSRU IV entered into a five-year agreement for the charter of BW Paris as an FSRU for FGEN’s interim offshore LNG project.

GCash targets P2trillion transactions in 2021

In an online summit, Globe President and CEO Ernest Cu said that Globe is targeting gross transactions of GCash to reach more than P2trillion this year coming from more than P1trillion in transactions processed last year. Gcash saw 38 million registered users, over 1 million merchants and social sellers and 6.8mn daily transactions. In January, Globe Fintech Innovations Inc or Mynt raised more than $175mn in fresh capital from Bow Wave Capital Management.

 
 

Macro News

DTI probes cement imports from Vietnam

The Department of Trade and Industry said it has found sufficient basis to justify a probe for an anti-dumping investigation on cement imports from Vietnam. The application was filed by Cemex Philippines, Holcim Philippines Inc. and Republic Cement Builders and Building Materials Inc. The DTI said the total volume of alleged dumped cement imports from Vietnam rose from 31% of the total Philippine imports for July to December 2019 to 62% in January to June of 2020. A comparison of the export price and normal value of cement from Vietnam for July to December 2019 indicated a dumping margin ranging from US$1.75 per MT to US$5.36 per MT (3.49% to 10.66%). Price undercutting was at 23% in 2019 and 24% in the first semester of January to June in 2020. In addition, the DTI said local producers were unable to take advantage of growth in demand for cement during the period of investigation with the market share of the domestic products declining to 78% in 2019 from 85% in 2018 as imports displaced the share of the domestic industry. “The industry suffered loss of market share, declining domestic sales, production, utilization rate, reduction in employment, increased cost of production and inventory,” the DTI said.

Metro Manila office vacancy rises 290 basis points QoQ

According to Cushman & Wakefield, Metro Manila office vacancy rose 290 basis points QoQ to 10.7% in 1Q21. This is the highest level since 2009 and comparable to the 11% estimate of Leechiu Property Consultants. We forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to increase from 9% in 2020 to 12% in 2021. Office leasing comprises 74% of Filinvest Land’s NAV and 52% of Megaworld’s NAV. FLI is currently trading at 5.2X FY21E P/E and 64% discount to NAV. MEG is currently trading at 7.2X FY21E P/E and 58% discount to NAV.

87% of BPOs expect growth in 2021

Based on a survey by the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines, 87% of BPOs expect 5-15% revenue growth in 2021. Meanwhile, around 13% expect a flat performance. In 2020, sector employees and revenue grew 1-2% to 1.32mn workers and US$26.7bn. A number of firms plan to expand in provincial areas in the next 12-18 months. About 70% of BPO employees are currently working from home.

Philippines to receive 7mn to 8 mn COVID vaccine doses in May

The country’s National Task Force against COVID-19 chief implementer and “vaccine czar” Carlito Galvez Jr. said that the Philippines might receive 7 to 8mn COVID vaccine doses in May due largely to more deliveries from the COVAX facility. This is higher than the previously announced 3.5mn and this would imply that by end-May the Philippines would have received a total of 11.7mn to 12.7mn COVID vaccine doses vs the previous announcement was 8.2mn. The government previously said 140mn doses would arrive by end-2021 to inoculate 70mn Filipinos. The country has received 3mn doses and has administered 1.62mn doses

Budget deficit widens to P191bn in March

The National Government reported a wider budget deficit of P191bn in March. Revenues fell 17% YoY due to base effects from last year’s dividend remittances to fund Bayanihan I. Tax revenues were up 7% YoY, driven by higher collections from the Bureau of Customs. The BIR collections remained soft, down 13.5% MoM. Expenditures rose 22% YoY due to infrastructure disbursements. The 1Q21 budget deficit at P321.5bn (vs full-year target of P1.78tn = 8.9% of GDP). Tax collections up by 0.8% YoY to P626bn in 1Q21, tracking behind the government’s FY21E: P2.7bn (+8% YoY). Expenditures rose by 20% YoY to P1.018tn, 22% of the government’s FY21E. 

MECQ in NCR Plus extended until May 14

President Duterte extended the Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine (MECQ) in Metro Manila and neighboring provinces until May 14 to ease pressure on hospitals that were at near breaking point. The quarantine status is subject to the appeals of the local government units. Meanwhile, Metro Manila mayors have agreed to shorten curfew hours to 10pm to 4am on May 1 (previously 8pm to 5am). To give a “breathing spell” for businesses in Metro Manila, adding more time for malls and other establishments to operate.

Another 500,000 Sinovac doses arrive

The Philippines received another 500,000 doses of Sinovac COVID vaccine. The country has received a total 4mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 1.8mn doses. The government expect 7mn to 8mn doses to arrive in May. It previously said 140mn doses would arrive by end-2021 to inoculate 70mn Filipinos.

US$541mn hot money exits in March

Foreign portfolio investments posted a net outflow of US$540.97mn in March. This was 44% smaller YoY but was up MoM (vs. US$40.41mn in February 2021). The March outflow was the biggest in 10 months.

BoP deficit reaches US$73mn in March

The country’s balance of payments (BoP) stood at a deficit for the third straight month in March, at US$73mn, reversing the US$448mn surplus a year earlier. The deficit in March, however, was smaller than in February 2021 (US$2.019bn). The deficit due to payments of National Government’s foreign debt obligations. This brings 1Q21 BOP deficit to US$2.844bn (vs US$68mn deficit in the same period last year).

Congress to push for new mining tax arrangements

The House Committee on Ways and Means, aims to pass a new tax regime for mining through House Bill 6135 according to Albay Rep. Jose Ma. Clemente Salceda. The measure, if enacted, is expected to generate P7.2bn during the first year of implementation and P37.9bn over the succeeding five years.

IATF allows indoor dine-in, personal care services in areas under MECQ

Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) has cleared indoor dine-in services at 10% capacity. Additionally, personal care services like barbershops and salons can reopen at 30% capacity. Establishments offering dine-in services and personal care services might operate beyond the said limitations provided they comply with the Safety Seal Certification Program. This will apply to areas under MECQ: Metro Manila, Rizal, Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, Santiago City in Quirino province and Abra province.
