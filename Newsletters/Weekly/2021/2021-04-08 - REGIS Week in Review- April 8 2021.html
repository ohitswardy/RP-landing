REGIS REPORT

08 April 2021

Bargain hunting fueled the market’s rise this week (+1.6% WoW) after its recent tumble to below the 6500 level. 

Local buying led the gains this week thought turnover was thin, averaging only US$115m daily this week. Foreigners were still net sellers for US$47m this week and accounted for only 36% of market activity.

The market rose, despite an extension of  the strictest lockdown level (ECQ)  in NCR Plus (NCR and surrounding provinces). ECQ was extended for at least another week until April 11 as COVID infections and hospital occupancy rates remained elevated. 

The market’s rise was broad-based, with index gainers outnumbering losers 4:1.  

Gains were led by MPI (+8.3% WoW) on enthusiasm for Water unit Maynilad’s forthcoming contract with the government. 

The government finally signed a revised agreement with Manila Water (-5.5% WoW) covering its East Zone concession with the deal revisions not too drastic and the outcome not as bad as feared. The nominal return is now 12% (it was last set at an inflation-adjusted 7% in 2018). Combined with a lowered tax rate (now 25%), the unlevered after-tax return would be a pretty decent 9%. The only large difference from a financial perspective is that MWC will no longer be granted a “free” FX hedge on its debt.

There are also new rules that would, in theory, make it easier for the regulator to seek information, audit and impose sanctions. New provisions in the contract also call for the turnover of fully recovered and depreciated assets owned by MWC to the government (MWSS) even before the concession expires. The new rules also provide changes in government protections and limiting arbitration to local arbitrators. 

Megaworld (+2.0% WoW) has shelved its plan for its share-for-perp swap (to issue 1.82bn new shares at P5.70/share to redeem US$200m of perpetual securities) close to two years after it announced its plans to do so.  The reversal avoids value dilution of around 2-3% for Megaworld shareholders,  The holder of the perps, parent Alliance Global (+7.1% WoW) also avoids paper losses of P3.5bn that would have resulted if the swap went through.

The top index decliners for the week were March outperformers Puregold (-4.3% WoW) and Robinsons Retail (-3.9% WoW).

CPI inflation in March came in at 4.5%, lower than Street expectations and a tad lower than the Feb print. According to the BSP, the dip affirms its prevailing accommodative monetary policy.  

The inflation dip was led by softening food prices, ex- meat. Supply issues for vegetables are correcting themselves but livestock supply, ravaged by ASF, should take time to normalize. Inflation concerns, however, are not over. Energy prices are likely to play a role in keeping inflation elevated over the next few months.

Filinvest Land - Presales, office revenue drop in 4Q20; 20% of residential accounts restructured
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY20 profit falls 41%; risks remain

Filinvest Land’s 4Q20 net profit jumped 230% QoQ to P1.1bn, thanks to a 50% QoQ increase in residential revenue. Despite the improvement in 4Q, FY20 net profit fell 41% to P3.73bn, in line with REGIS forecast, as higher net interest expense offset strong revenue and low opex. Risks remain for both the office and residential segments: some POGO tenants have not been paying rent while c. 20% of residential accounts have undergone some restructuring.

Reservation sales drop 26% QoQ ; c. 20% of buyer accounts restructured

After the surprising jump in 3Q20, presales fell 26% QoQ (-15% YoY) to P3.36bn. FY20 presales fell “only” 21% (to P15.3bn) but OFW sales fell by a steeper 50% and now comprise just 20% of total residential sales (from 31% in 2019). FLI allowed c. 10% of buyers to have their payments credited to cheaper units, allowing them to avoid outright default. Another c. 10% of buyers availed of payment deferrals. Even after excluding these restructured payments, reported defaults still increased from 1.2% in 2019 to 2.7%.

Office revenue drops 8% QoQ; POGOs still occupy 15% of the portfolio

4Q20 office revenue fell 8% QoQ (-16% YoY). Although office occupancy was flat QoQ at 88% (from 91% in 2019), 4Q saw lower revenue due to the full quarter impact of POGO closures in 3Q. In addition, while POGOs still occupy 15% of the office portfolio, some of them have not been paying rent. Thus, it might only be a matter of time before they officially vacate more office space.

Office REIT listing planned in 3Q21

FLI plans to spin off 300,000sqm of its 520,000sqm office portfolio into a REIT in 3Q21. The buildings have an occupancy rate of 91% and less than 2% of space is leased by POGOs. At the maximum price, the REIT would have a market cap of P40.6bn (higher than FLI’s current P26.7bn) and could raise our FLI NAV estimate by c. 10%.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Emperador - 4Q: Revenue gains overshadowed by margin decline
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q20 profit -18%QoQ

EMP’s 4Q20 profit dipped 18% sequentially to P2.092bn, dragged by lower GPM at both brandy and whisky segments, and a higher effective tax rate at its international subsidiary. While 4Q20 its down from its peak in 3Q20, full-year profits were still higher by 18% at P7.961bn, helped by the robust performance of whisky in 2Q/3Q and a sharp jump in brandy revenues in 4Q.

Surge in lower-priced brands, blended malt offset by net margin collapse in 4Q

Profits from Whyte & Mackay fell to just P240mn (-75%QoQ, -38%YoY), its lowest quarterly profit this year despite sustained revenue growth (+18% in 4Q). 4Q20 GPM fell to 35.1% (from 40% in 3Q20) on a shift in revenue mix, while net margins collapsed to 4.8% (from 22.8% in 3Q20). We await more detail from its full financial statements.

Management discussed at great length the continued surge in demand for its Whyte & Mackay, Jura and Tamnavulin brands in Europe and North America. In UK alone, management said that Jura and Tamnavulin have become the first and fifth best-selling malts, while Whyte & Mackay was the “2nd largest selling whisky” last year. These brands may have benefited from a shift to off-premise and e[1]commerce purchases, as well as downtrading from more expensive products.

Brandy revenues rebound sharply, but margins lower

Brandy profits improved to P1.852bn (+20%QoQ, +74%YoY) as revenues jumped 56%QoQ. While management did not cite specific volume data, they claimed that its core Emperador brand gained market share vs competition. Its bottom line would have been higher if not for the more than 16ppt collapse in GPM in the period (19.3% in 4Q20 vs 34.5% in 3Q20). We suspect the company may have resorted to steep trade discounts or price cuts to buoy demand, following its underperformance to cheaper products from Ginebra or Tanduay in recent quarters.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Shakey’s Pizza - EBITDA surprise in 4Q20
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY20 net loss wider than expected but EBITDA ahead

Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA) reported a net loss of P247mn in FY20. Excluding non[1]recurring items (restructuring expenses, tax adjustments), the net loss was P261mn, wider than our FY20E of a P235mn net loss. However, recurring EBITDA was ahead of expectations at P782mn even as revenues (-36% YoY to P5.3bn) were broadly in line. For 4Q20 alone, the company just about broke even after deducting non-recurring items.

4Q revenues jump 39% QoQ as dine-in restrictions are loosened

Revenues during the quarter jumped 39% QoQ to P1.5bn, as the decline in SSS improved to -34% from over -40% in 2Q and 3Q. Management attributed the recovery to 1) seasonality and 2) the loosening of mobility and dine-in restrictions. We estimate on-premise sales accounted for c.33% of sales in 4Q20, up from c.23% in 3Q20.

EBITDA margin widens on operating leverage, tight cost control

EBITDA margin (recurring) widened to c.27% in 4Q20, higher not just QoQ but also YoY. This was driven by operating leverage from the top line recovery, further boosted by cost control measures (store closures, right-sizing of organization) implemented by the company.

Store expansion to restart in 2021 but situation remains fluid

The company plans to open 30 new stores in 2021 (Regis FY21E: 10 stores), though these stores will have smaller average footprints (c.20-25% smaller) than existing stores given that dine-in capacities will continue to be limited over the near term. The figure does not yet include satellite kitchens, which management will continue to expand in order to support the delivery business. Management noted that the pace of top line recovery had decelerated in early 1Q21 as the holiday rush subsided. Recent trends under the current (stricter) lockdown measures have been above expectations thus far, though average ticket sizes have contracted given heightened price consciousness of consumers. We believe that PIZZA’s scale and strong brands remain to be key advantages amidst weak consumption and increased competition.

 
 

Corporate News

First Gen picks BW Gas as FSRU provider

First Gen Corp (FGEN PM) has tapped BW Gas Ltd as its floating storage and regassification unit (FSRU) provider for its interim offshore LNG terminal in Batangas. FGEN LNG Corp signed a five year time charter party with BW FSRU IV Pte Ltd for the charter of an FSRU with respect to the LNG terminal that will be built in Batangas City. BW Paris, the FSRU to be provided by BW FSRU IV, has an LNG storage capacity of 162,400 cubic meters and nominal and peak gas send out capacity of 500mn standard cubic feet per day and 750mmscfd, respectively. Nominal send out capacity of BW Paris is 25% more than the production capability of Malampaya (400mmscfd at its peak). The LNG project will allow FGEN’s subsidiary to introduce LNG to the country by third quarter next year to serve the natural gas requirements of existing and future gas-fired plants of FGEN and third parties.

Key provisions of Manila Water’s new water contract

Regulatory return fixed at 12%, lower than previous. The regulatory return is fixed at a nominal rate of 12% from the effectivity date of the new concession agreement (likely March 31, 2021) until the end of the concession. This suggests that the regulatory return will be 9% on a post-tax, nominal basis, lower than the existing regulatory return of 10.4% (post-tax, nominal).  Note that the regulatory return was previously set at 7.39%, calculated on a post-tax and real basis. 

No income tax recoverability. Under the new contract, recovery of expenditures related to tax on income will no longer be allowed.

Removal of FX pass-through. Manila Water will no longer get full protection from currency losses on FX debt, which could raise the company’s cost of debt, as the company will likely need to hedge its FX exposure. Note that MWC’s pre-tax cost of debt is currently at 6.0%.

Tariff hikes. There will be no tariff adjustments until December 31, 2022. There will also be a separation of tariffs related to water and wastewater but the published agreement did not define the tariffs for both. Note that both water and wastewater tariffs will be adjusted according to the following: Inflation. MWC will continue to adjust its tariffs according to inflation annually, but only up to two thirds (2/3) of the change in CPI and only after Dec 31, 2022. Rate rebasing or “R” adjustment.  There will be a tariff adjustment every five years based on (a) the net present value of the Opening Cash Position (OCP) and (b) future cash flows.  It is worth noting that cash outflows incurred prior to the end of the tariff freeze can form part of the OCP, subject to review. Limits to tariff hikes.  Tariff hikes will now be capped at a maximum of 30% for water tariffs and 50% for wastewater tariffs.  These caps will apply to both the “R” adjustment and CPI-related adjustments.

Turnover of recovered assets to MWSS at no cost.  On or before January 2022, Manila Water will be submitting a list of all “Recovered Assets” that are (1) already fully recovered by MWC through tariff adjustments and (2) are fully depreciated in MWC’s books. This suggests that ownership of these recovered assets will be turned over to MWSS effectively at “zero” cost. This mechanism is repeated every rate rebasing exercise, such that assets that are not yet recovered will remain with MWC until the Expiration (or Early Termination) date, at which time these will be automatically vest in MWSS.  For valuation purposes, we believe there should be no terminal value for the DCF analysis.

Contract expiration date stays. The revised agreement has the same expiration date as the old agreement (July 31, 2037).  The government can also pay the concessionaire a lump-sum payment at the end of the concession for all or a portion of investments that were not recovered via tariff adjustments.

More power to regulator, Penalties explicit.  The new contract also appears to be silent on the settlement of disputes arising from rate rebasing adjustments. It appears that the MWSS will have the final say on rate rebasing determinations. Apart from this, the new contract also requires the concessionaire to (1) provide information to the regulator on a more defined timetable and (2) subject itself to a regular audit.  The new contract also sets clearer financial penalties for various types of service shortfalls.

Megaworld redeems US$200mn of perpetual securities

Megaworld bought back all of its outstanding senior perpetual securities from its parent, Alliance Global. The perpetual securities were issued in 2018 and offered interest of 5.375%. Recall, in 3Q19, Megaworld said it would redeem the securities by issuing 1.82bn new MEG shares at P5.70/share. This redemption via issuance of shares has not been consummated because the SEC has not approved the transaction. We estimate that the redemption of senior perpetual securities with cash could raise our MEG NAV estimate and 2021E EPS by 2-3%.

Cebu Landmasters FY20 net profit falls 8%

Cebu Landmasters’ 2020 earnings fell 8% to P1.85bn while reservation sales grew 12% to P14.25bn. Around 69% of sales were from economic housing (29% in 2019) with 45% of sales were from Cebu (58% in 2019). About 5% of buyers availed of the option to defer monthly payments while buyer defaults only increased marginally. The company still increased selling prices last year. Land prices (for land acquisition) generally did not fall.

 
 

Macro News

Duterte extends ECQ in Metro Manila, nearby provinces

President Duterte extended the stringent lockdown enforced in the National Capital Region and the provinces of Bulacan, Rizal, Laguna, and Cavite until April 11. ECQ was supposed to end on April 4. The gov’t will decide in a week if another extension is needed. The economic managers will meet on April 8 to review growth targets.

Bank lending slows further in February

Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, fell by 2.7% YoY, 0.1% MoM in February. Credit activity eased further as demand for loans remained soft. The consumer loans fell 8.3% in February due to the continued decline in credit card and motor vehicle loans as well as the slowdown in salary-based consumption loans. Outstanding loans to major industries continued to decline, particularly to wholesale and retail trade. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose by 9.4% YoY to P14.0tn in February, faster than the 8.9% (revised) growth in January.

More hot money exits Philippines in February

Foreign portfolio investment yielded a net outflow of US$40.41mn in February. This is a reversal of the US$97.92mn net inflow booked in January 2021 and the US$40.06mn in February last year. Gross inflows rose 40.6% MoM to US$1.34bn, of which 39.8% were investments in PSE-listed securities and the balance went to in Peso gov’t securities. Additionally, gross outflows were 62% MoM higher at US$1.38bn. In 2M21, the Philippines recorded a net inflow of +US$57.51mn (vs. net outflow of -US$446mn in 2M20).

Inflation eases to 4.5% in March

Headline inflation eased to 4.5% in March from 4.7% in February. This is lower than consensus forecast (4.9%) but came withing the BSP’s target range (4.2%-5%). Core inflation stood at 3.5%, unchanged MoM. Food inflation, particularly for fish, fruits and vegetables, decelerated due to the lifting of the closed fishing season and improving weather conditions. Inflation of utilities moderated but this was partly offset by accelerating transport inflation due to higher fuel prices. 1Q21 average inflation rate stood at 4.5%, still above the government’s target range of 2-4% for the year.

BIR maintains April 15 deadline for ITR filing

The Bureau of Internal Revenue said it will not extend the April 15 deadline for filing and payment of annual income tax returns (ITR). As a relief for taxpayers, the BIR will allow the filing of a tentative ITR before the deadline and give them until May 15 to amend the returns without penalties. The BIR is aiming to collect P231.57 bn in April, mainly from income tax payments.

Duterte signs EO lowering tariffs on pork imports for one year

President Duterte signed EO No. 128 on Wednesday, temporarily lowering the Most Favored Nation tariff rates on imported pork meat for one year to address the currently supply shortage due to African Swine Fever. This comes after the President asked Congress last month to increase the minimum access volume (MAV) for pork imports this year by 350k MT on top of the 54k MT under the current limit. The EO temporarily lowers the import duties on fresh, chilled or frozen pork to 5% from 30% under the MAV quota for three months after the order takes effect. The rate will be increased to 10% for the succeeding nine months. The tariff rate for imported meat outside the existing quota on the other hand is reduced to 15% from 40% for the first three months. It will then be raised to 20% for the next nine months. Meanwhile, Agriculture Secretary William Dar said that price caps for selected pork and chicken products will expire after April 8. DA will instead implement suggested retail prices for imported pork products.
