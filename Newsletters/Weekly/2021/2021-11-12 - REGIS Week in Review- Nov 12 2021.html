REGIS REPORT

12 November 2021

The PSEi edged up slightly this week (+0.57% WoW) after gaining 4% in the previous week.  The market tested the 7400 level this week, but was met with profit taking in certain stocks. 

3Q GDP came in at +7.1%, beating Regis (4.0%) and Street estimates (4.9%). On the demand side, investments led the rebound, with Capital Formation up 22%, led by construction activity. 3Q GDP last year (2020) contracted 11.6%, placing this year’s 3Q output still ~5% below what it was in 2019. However, improving mobility is prompting optimism for 4Q.

The market was split, with gainers only edging out losers 8:7 this week.

Interest rotated back to telcos – led by the week’s top gainer Globe (+7.4% WoW).  Globe’s broadband business reported its highest ever net adds of 78k wireless subscribers. Converge (+6.9% WoW) was also up significantly.  Converge added 222k broadband subscribers, taking total base to 1.58m, on track to exceed its year-end guidance for 2021.

PLDT (+3.0% WoW) also gained.

Laggard interest meanwhile took Alliance Global (+5.0% WoW), Megaworld (+4.75% WoW), Metrobank (+3.9% WoW), and MPI (+3.35% WoW) higher.

Expectations of continued easing boosted reopening plays Bloomberry (+2.4% WoW) and Jollibee (+3.7% WoW).

Follow through buying post stellar 3Q results pushed ICTSI (2.5% WoW) higher.  The company launched a tender offer US$775m of its existing perpetuals. ICT is aiming to refinance given expectations of US$ rates rise even further.

It was a results-filled week. For the property sector, focus was on the drop in residential revenue due to sales cancellations that had been deferred mainly because of the Bayanihan Act.  This resulted in a drag to SM Prime (+0.3% WoW) and Robinsons Land (unch) results.

For consumer stocks, margin pressure was a big factor.  Monde’s (+3.2% WoW) underperformed mainly due to alternative meats unit Quorn.  Cost pressures also weighed on margins.  For Jollibee, cost inflation (raw materials, labor cost in the US) was the main culprit for the EBIT contraction in 3Q21 (-31% QoQ).  SSSG in the Phils was weighed down by the hard lockdown in 3Q – the same story for Shakey’s topline (+6.0% WoW) during the quarter.

Foreigners were buyers for US$24m this week. The follow-on offering of Synergy Grid clocked in US$24m in inflows, though foreigners were seen selling post-deal, with SGP seeing net foreign outflows US$30m for the three days post listing. The stock closed up 20.8% vs its FOO price.  Foreigners were two-way telcos, buying GLO while selling PLDT and CNVRG.  Foreigners were seen buying property and banks while selling utilities

See below for more details on 3Q results.

SM Prime - 3Q21 residential revenue drops sharply, likely on cancellations
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Net profit disappoints due to the residential segment

3Q21 net profit fell 23% QoQ to P4.0bn, in large part due to the 39% drop in residential revenue. We suspect that the sharpness of the decline was due to sales cancellations, which led to reversal of revenue. 9M21 earnings are still up 9% YoY to P15.6bn, behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts.

Philippine mall revenue grows despite lockdown; more optimistic outlook

Despite the lockdown in 3Q21, Philippine mall revenue grew 31% (+4% QoQ) to P5.0bn, 36% of the 3Q19 figure. Looking ahead, mall operations should improve even further.

Mobility restrictions were loosened beginning late September. Thus, SM Prime started charging minimum rent in October (though only a fraction of pre-COVID levels). Further loosening in Metro Manila this month should further raise tenant sales (as well as minimum rent levels) going forward.

Residential revenue falls sharply

3Q21 residential revenue fell 39% QoQ (27% YoY) to P7.6bn, the lowest level since the pandemic began. The abruptness of the drop was likely due to substantial amounts of residential sales cancellation being recognized. We reckon that 3Q21 was the first quarter since the pandemic began, when developers could recognize cancellations following the extensions of payment terms. The quantum of cancellations could come down in the succeeding quarters.

Reservation sales drop; still performing better than the industry

Reservation sales also declined 7% QoQ (13% YoY) to P21.2bn, 94% of the 2019 average. Despite this slowdown, SM Prime is still performing better than most other developers. The company’s 9M21 reservation sales of P76.3bn (+14%) is 15% above the 9M19 level, while most other developers’ sales are still 20%-50% below their respective 9M19 figures.

 
Robinsons Land - Residential cancellations and mall losses weigh on 3Q21 earnings
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential and mall segments pressure earnings

Despite lower residential earnings and an operating loss in the mall segment, 3Q21 net profit rose 39% YoY to P1.0bn, thanks in large part to c. P300mn in income from forfeitures. Lot sales in 2Q21 brought 9M21 earnings to P6.4bn, above REGIS and consensus estimates. Stripping off the lot sales, however, EBIT would be trending below both forecasts.

The reopening of the economy should lead to better earnings while the recently announced P3bn share buyback program should provide support to the stock.

Malls show second consecutive operating loss but outlook is rosier

Mall revenue fell 6% QoQ (+7% YoY) to P1.8bn, causing this segment to show an operating loss for the 2nd consecutive quarter. However, mall operations should improve substantially soon given the incremental loosening of mobility restrictions since September, particularly in Metro Manila.

Residential revenue drops on sales cancellations

3Q21 residential revenue fell 41% YoY (-60% QoQ) to P1.1bn. About P1.2bn of previously recognized revenue (equivalent to 10% of FY20 residential revenue) was reversed due to sales cancellations. The reversals were unusually large this period because this is the first quarter since the beginning of the pandemic when the company can recognize cancellations. 3Q21 presales fell 16% QoQ to P2.3bn, only 46% of the 2019 average. Nevertheless, 9M21 presales of P7.9bn (+33%), is in line with our forecast.

Office vacancy stays flat

RLC’s office vacancy stayed flat (QoQ) at 6%, much better than the industry’s vacancy rate of 17%. 3Q21 revenue rose 10% YoY on 10% floor area expansion.

Only P1.3bn of unsold residential inventory in China

Only P1.3bn of units remain unsold in China (out of P15bn worth of units in phase 2). After this phase is completed, RLC will not pursue further projects in China.

 
Cebu Air - Fortune favors the brave (part 2)
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Reiterate Buy

Repeated lockdowns have plagued Cebu Air’s shares this year, leading to it underperforming the PCOMP by 17% since end-June and 8% since end-2020. With vaccinations improving, particularly for Metro Manila, and nationwide COVID case counts declining, we are optimistic about the government reopening travel as we enter 2022. With its competitors facing financial constraints, CEB is also likely to gain market share, which could provide a tailwind to its recovery and re-rating of its stock. We reiterate our BUY rating on a TP of P65/sh.

More visible path to reopening; Industry consolidation a tailwind

Inoculations in the country’s capital, the stronghold of CEB’s business, is close to reaching herd immunity, with 64% of the population already fully vaccinated. This, along with declining case counts, has prompted the government to ease some travel restrictions. Admittedly, inoculations outside Metro Manila lag but we remain optimistic that the government will ease travel restrictions further in the coming months as vaccinations improve and case numbers are controlled.

We also believe CEB is well-positioned to take advantage of the financial woes of its competitors in the domestic market, whose actions will likely be restrained to the terms of their debt restructuring agreements.

FY21E adjusted; Positive 4Q21E EBITDA

We revise our FY21E, assuming larger losses due to volatile quarantine restrictions so far which have delayed the rebound in air travel. We expect EBITDA losses to fade by 4Q21 with (1) seat capacity reaching c. 40% of pre-Covid levels and (2) cost reduction initiatives, largely on maintenance, take effect.

TP of P65/sh offers 35% potential return

Our TP of P65/sh is based on a multiple of 6.5x on FY22E EBITDA reaching only 85% of pre-pandemic levels. Our TP offers 35% potential return but returns could be greater (>100%) should the recovery be sharper than our estimates. Risks: higher jet kero prices, weak PHP:USD, lower demand.

 
Metro Pacific - 3Q21: Meralco, lower interest costs offset softness in other units
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M21 core profit up 21% YoY, tracking ahead of forecast

MPI’s 3Q21 core profit of P3.5bn fell just slightly QoQ, aided by Meralco's good performance and lower interest costs at the parent. These factors compensated for the sluggish recovery of its water, tollroad, and rail units. Cumulative 9M21 profit stood at P9.6bn, trending above forecasts. Given the recent re-opening, we believe a stronger rebound could take place in 4Q. Meanwhile, a potential increase in the buyback program remains in the cards.

Tollroad: Lockdown affects 3Q traffic volumes but Oct/Nov higher

The lockdown drove 3Q21 volumes and profit lower QoQ for the tollroad unit. After restrictions were eased in mid-September, the company saw a marked improvement in volumes, with vehicles traveling longer distances especially on weekends. Traffic volumes for the first nine days of November are now at 93% of pre-Covid levels, up from only 85% in 3Q21.

Water volumes drop further in 3Q; Franchise bill to be passed into law soon

Profit contributions from its water units dropped 2% YoY, as start-up costs from Metro Pac Water weighed on the decent performance of Maynilad. Maynilad saw its profit rise 4% YoY, despite a 5% YoY drop in billed volumes due to a reduction in cash costs. Billed volumes in 3Q were still at 97% of pre-Covid levels, as commercial volumes were still lower. The Bicam version of the water franchise bill is just awaiting the President’s signature. The bill authorizes the MWSS to extend the expiration dates of Maynilad’s concession to 2046 from 2037. Once the bill is passed into law, the extension will form part of rate rebasing discussions, which will hopefully commence by March/April and be completed by 4Q22.

Rail volumes lag but capacity limits eased this month

Losses persisted for its rail unit, after the lockdown affected ridership. In 3Q alone, ridership remained at only 24% of pre-Covid levels. Management however noted “spikes” in ridership shortly after the government eased mobility restrictions. After the government raised the capacity limit to 70% (from 30%) last Nov 4, management noted ridership is now at 40% of pre-Covid levels.

 
Megaworld Corp. - 3Q21: office segment outperforms; residential sales remain weak
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

3Q21 net profit climbs on better construction activity

3Q21 net profit jumped 57% YoY (+19% QoQ) to P3.2bn, as higher construction activity propped up residential revenue. 9M21 net profit rose 10% to P8.2bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, as residential revenue is still lagging. Megaworld’s office segment outperformed our expectations but residential sales were muted (especially in Metro Manila).

Office revenue rises 7% QoQ; inquiries pick up in 4Q21

The office portfolio remained the same size QoQ (at 1.4mn sqm) and the vacancy rate was flat at 10%. Nevertheless, office revenue rose 7% QoQ to P2.9bn. The company said that some tenants that vacated space were replaced by higher[1]rent occupiers. We believe that rental escalations and the lapse of rent-free periods contributed further to the QoQ improvement. Megaworld should be able to take advantage of the uptick in BPO office inquiries recently since its office properties are predominantly PEZA-accredited. This, and the fact that the company will have no sizeable completions until 2023, suggest that its vacancy rate will remain better than the industry’s (now at 17%).

Reservation sales disappoint due to limited Metro Manila condo demand

While reservation sales grew 35% YoY (+4% QoQ) to P17.1bn, this is only 46% of the 2019 average. Furthermore, 9M21 reservation sales of P54.3bn (+8% YoY) only represent 47% of the 9M19 figure. This disappointing presales figure is despite the increase in proportion of provincial lots from 23% to 32% of total sales. Selling prices of such lots have risen by as much as 100% over two years.

Residential revenue surges; cancellations not an issue

Residential revenue surged 102% YoY and 26% QoQ. This is a contrast to some other developers, who saw sharp drops due to sales cancellations. Management said that it has not seen any material change in defaults from pre-COVID levels.

 
Puregold - 3Q: margin expansion mitigates soft top line
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat Research Analyst +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M21 net profit +14% YoY / EBIT +7% YoY – slightly behind expectations

PGOLD’s 9M21 net profit came in at P5.7bn +14% YoY, trailing FY21E estimates slightly. Net sales of P115bn (-5% YoY) are trending below expectations, but operating profit of P9.1bn (+7% YoY) is more or less in line. For 3Q21, net profit rose 6% YoY. While net sales were flat YoY, healthy gross margins (+200bps YoY to 18.6%) lifted operating profit +5% YoY.

Lockdowns weigh on 3Q SSSG

Consolidated SSS declined by 4% during the quarter as the lockdowns weighed on the recovery of store traffic. Puregold continued to see a c20% YoY decline in foot traffic during the quarter, while S&R saw low single digit growth. The divergent SSSG/traffic trends highlight the K-shaped recovery within the sector.

Core Puregold brand drives margins

The core Puregold brand continued to be the key driver of margins with gross margin up over 200bps YoY in 3Q. Management attributes the improvement to a number of factors, namely 1) increased marketing support from suppliers, 2) better sales mix as a result of its “smart pricing” strategy, 3) out of store sales (Puregold caravans, etc, which have generated cP4-5bn in sales), and 4) cost savings from supply chain improvements. Meanwhile, higher logistics costs tempered S&R’s margins.

Raising TP to P48/sh; maintaining Buy

Management is optimistic that top line trends should continue to improve as lockdown restrictions are lifted and consumer mobility picks up. Management expects margins at the core Puregold brand to be sustained and to help mitigate any potential cost pressure at S&R. We are tweaking our estimates (+1-4%), rolling over our valuation, and raising our TP to P48/sh (+14%). Maintaining Buy.

by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M21 core net profit in line at P7.1bn (-5% YoY); EBITDA (-13% YoY) behind

MONDE’s core net profit in 9M21 was P7.1bn -5% YoY, broadly within expectations. EBITDA of P11.3bn (-13% YoY), however, is tracking behind forecasts. The underperformance was largely attributable to lower-than[1]expected revenues for Meat Alternatives. Core net profit for 3Q21 alone rose 9% YoY, though EBITDA was -9% YoY as higher input costs pressured margins across the board.

APAC BFB: New products drive 3Q top line even as input costs crimp margins

APAC BFB sales rose 4% YoY in 3Q21 led by instant noodles (+10%). While growth was partly lifted by the +3% price increase during the quarter, management attributes the volume growth to new product launches. Much like the rest of the sector, margins were squeezed by higher input cost (wheat, palm oil) and higher trade/selling expenses. Meat Alternatives: UK retail market contracts from high base but foodservice gaining ground

Quorn sales grew 4% YoY during the quarter, though we estimate that the growth was largely driven by the price increases implemented in 4Q20. UK retail sales continue to be soft, contracting 5-10% during the quarter as consumers spent less time at home. To the upside, food service sales jumped over 60%, though it is still a relatively small contributor (c10%) to the top line.

There are, however, some bright spots: 1) Quorn’s food service sales are expected to continue growing as the company extends its partnership with KFC UK to include other parts of Europe and Asia, 2) increased brand awareness in the US with the launch of its new brand ambassador, and 3) the reopening of the Phils, which could drive the growth of out-of-home categories, such as biscuits.

 
Cebu Air - Pax volumes up QoQ; maintenance costs rise ahead of demand rebound
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

3Q21 losses wider QoQ

CEB reported a net loss of P8.2bn in 3Q21. Excluding FX losses and one-offs, we estimate core net loss was wider QoQ at P6.4bn, taking 9M21 net losses to P20bn, trending below forecast. Despite a 14% QoQ improvement in passenger revenues, 3Q21 EBITDA losses of P2.6bn were larger QoQ due to (1) higher maintenance expenses and (2) flight restrictions between Dubai and Manila.

Pax revenues up 14% QoQ but overall revs up just +2%

Pax volumes improved 11% QoQ and were about 13% of pre-Covid levels, as CEB mounted more domestic flights during the quarter despite the lockdowns. However, flight restrictions between Manila and Dubai since June led to a 13% QoQ decrease in ancillary revenues. Moreover, cargo revs also fell 3% QoQ (+9% YoY) as yields dropped 5% QoQ on competitive pricing. While the Philippines eased restrictions on flights from Manila to Dubai in September, flights from Dubai to Manila remain restricted and are still diverted to Cebu/Davao. Moreover, CEB’s domestic market share dipped to 52% in 3Q21 from 54% in 2Q21, but September alone rose to 55% as it was quick to re[1]introduce leisure flights. The company aims to grab more market share during the peak holiday season.

Higher maintenance costs ahead of demand rebound

Cash costs jumped 8% QoQ, with maintenance costs alone rising 14% due to some parked aircraft returning to service and needing maintenance. CEB now only has 7 parked aircraft, down from 15 in end-2020. Fuel costs also rose 13% QoQ, broadly in line with the increase in pax revenues.

Fleet size/capex guidance lower than forecast

Management is guiding a fleet size of 71 by year-end and 68 by end-2022, after successful negotiations with its providers. 9M21 net capex of P193mn is tracking lower than forecast (P1bn) and management is also guiding a similar capex for 2022 (vs. Regis: P7bn). Its cash burn of P1.5bn per month in 9M21 also declined further to P1.2-P1.3bn following these negotiations.

3Q21 profit -21%QoQ

We estimate FB’s net income to equity holders was P4.2bn in 3Q21 (-21%QoQ, +17%YoY), dragged down by sharply lower contribution from the food segment. On the other hand, profit from the food segment has been trending lower since 2Q on rising pressures from commodity prices, limited pricing power and higher levels of chicken inventory. Beer volumes +6%QoQ; loosening restrictions could mark return of on-premise

Gin extends gains; volumes up 19%QoQ

Gin volumes bounced back to 10.2mn cases in 3Q, 19% higher QoQ. Poultry segment continues to trend lower due to higher inventory levels

The food segment’s profits contracted 50%QoQ to P1.4bn. Management attributed the sharp decline to volatility of the poultry business which saw pricing taper off from 1H due to increased inventories and imports. Bloomberry Resorts - Lower win rates temper surge in volumes; capacity limits to be loosened
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Net loss narrows QoQ; volume trends across the board appear promising

BLOOM incurred a narrower net loss of P1.040bn in 3Q21 (vs -P1.159bn in 2Q21) despite limited operating days due to the hard lockdown in Aug-Sep. EBITDA, however, dipped to P910mn, despite a sharp jump in GGR for the quarter because of a higher VIP contribution and the impact of lower hold rates vs previous quarter. Nevertheless, the 9M21 EBITDA of P3.364bn (+160%) is tracking ahead of Regis estimates.

GGR up 25%QoQ, underlying volumes even better

3Q21 GGR jumped 25%QoQ to P7.089bn, driven by a 72%QoQ jump in VIP revenues and healthy contributions from slots (+42%QoQ). Underlying volumes were robust, with VIP volume tripling from 2Q and mass tables and slot machines higher by 26%/39%QoQ respectively. These were, however, tempered by lower hold rates for both VIP (1.85% from 3.26%) and mass tables (45.8% from 60%). On a hold-adjusted basis, normalized EBITDA would have been P1.6bn in 3Q vs P918mn in 2Q. The limited player pool is likely to result in volatile win rates until tourists return.

Oct foot traffic at 40% of pre-Covid, GGR run-rate close to 70% for mass

Management discussed how foot traffic fell to an average of 3.8k/day in August from a high of 8.5k/day in July. That number immediately bounced back to 6.7k/day after reopening in September and nearly 8k/day in October. Management said that the GGR run rate is trending at a little over 50% of pre[1]Covid levels overall and close to 70% for the mass segment alone.

Lowered Alert level means 50% capacity limit likely to be lifted by PAGCOR

After the government announced the lowering of alert level for Manila, management believes that going up to as high as 90% of gaming capacity is something PAGCOR would not object to. As a strategy, however, management aims to minimize infection risk by prioritizing playing patrons over opening the property to the general public through an expanded invite policy.
