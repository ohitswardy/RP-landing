REGIS REPORT

15 January 2021

The PSEi was inched down 0.7% on a lackluster week of trading.  Market catalysts were scarce, and newsflow remained relatively light.

Local retail names continued to dominate market activity. Out of the five most active names this week,  four were third liners – Apollo Global ($97M T/O +49% WoW), Premier Horizon Alliance ($80M T/O +74.5% WoW), AC Energy ($62.2M T/O -17% WoW) and DITO ($49M T/O -7.9% WoW). These  names accounted for over a quarter (26.5%) of the weeks’ total turnover.  Six of the top ten most active names were retail names and accounted for 34% of activity.

Foreigners remained sellers this week, though net outflow abated to US$4.5m from US$30m in the previous week.

Index losers outnumbered the gainers 19:11, with some declines seen in recent outperformers.

Globe (+4.0% WoW) caught up after its recent underperformance, topping the week’s gainers list.

Globe’s E-wallet firm GCash is receiving another cash infusion from a new investor. Bow Wave is putting in US$115m into Mynt, the holding company for GCash. A total of US$175m of new money will be infused into Mynt however, with the balance coming from existing shareholders including Globe and Ant Financial.   As a result of the buy-in, Bow Wave will own 14% of Mynt. Globe’s stake will be diluted to 40% and the value of that stake based on Bow Wave’s infusion would be ~US$330m. This recent development should book a nice gain for Globe.

GCash claims 33m registered users as of end of 2020, with gross transactions reaching over one trillion pesos over the year.

PLDT (+2.7% WoW) also outperformed this week.

Banks likewise led, with Security Bank (+2.9% WoW) and BDO (+2.75% WoW) also among the week’s top gainers.  For the industry, the NPL ratio crept up to 3.81% in November (3.91% excluding interbank loans). Including Restructured Loans (RLs), the “stress” levels stood at 5.23% of total loans, up 10bps MoM. Meanwhile, loan portfolio of commercial banks are up a mere  +0.3% up YoY. Including other banks, total system loans was already down 0.5% YoY.

First Gen (+3.1% WoW) ended higher  as the green energy theme continued to play out. Bloomberry (+2.5% WoW) also continued to rise on prospects of continued reopening.

GT Capital (-1.1% WoW) fell on profit taking and the continued overhang provided by the government’s plan to impose safeguard duties on imported vehicles. Regis expects the duty to be imposed on around half of the sales of Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), which could raise prices 7-12%.

Sales of TMP in December were 13,710, up +20% MoM. For the full year, Toyota vehicles sales reached 100,013 units, which is 38% lower vs 2019. This is in line with management guidance but better than Regis assumptions (90k).

Corp News

Lopez Holdings covered by new delisting rules

The PSE said that Lopez Holdings (LPZ PM) will be subject to the tighter rules on voluntary delisting that were recently approved by the SEC. This means that the proposed delisting must be approved by at least 2/3 of LPZ board of directors, including the majority of its independent directors. Votes cast against the delisting should not be more than 10% of total outstanding shares. On 1 December 2020, FPH made a tender for 20% to 45.56% of LPZ shares outstanding at P3.85/share. Based on a 12 December 2020 fairness opinion issued by KPMG R.G. Manabat & Co., the fair value of LPZ had a range of P2.34/share to P3.92/share (as of end-September 2020).

Megawide to invest P5.5bn in Cebu’s Carbon market

Megawide entered in a JV with the Cebu City government for the redevelopment of the Cebu Carbon market. The JV agreement is valid for 50 years, extendible for another 25 years. The total investment cost is estimated to be P5.5bn, of which Phase 1 will amount to P2bn. Cebu city will contribute the exclusive use and possession of the project site. The project involves a business center with in-city hotels, restaurant hubs, parks and a “well-designed” public market. We have not included this project in our valuation and forecasts and we estimate MWIDE’s parent level gross debt (inc. prefs) is currently at P27.3bn and cash at P6.2bn.

Bangladesh Bank files complaint against RCBC, BLOOM, before NY Supreme Court

Bangladesh Bank filed a complaint against RCBC, its officers and other parties before the Supreme Court of New York. Bloomberry Resorts is also among the defendants. This pertains to the 2016 Bangladesh Bank heist wherein North Korean hackers allegedly stole US$81m by sending multiple remittance orders out of the Bangladesh Bank’s account with the Federal Reserve Bank of New York to RCBC. The Federal Court of New York dismissed a lawsuit filed by Bangladesh Bank on the 2016 incident.

GT Capital to guarantee debt of subsidiaries

GT Capital is amending its Articles of Incorporation to allow it to provide guarantees in favor of its component companies.

Vehicle sales rise 19% MoM in December; Toyota sales up 20% MoM

Industry vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI and TMA, rose by 19% MoM to 27,596 units. This was, however, still down by 18% YoY and brings FY20 vehicle sales to 223,793 units (-40% YoY). Meanwhile, Toyota’s market share in FY20 stood at 44.69%, up from 43.7% in FY19. This implies Toyota sold 13,710 units in December alone (+20% MoM, -14% YoY). For FY20, sales reached 100,013 units (-38% YoY), in line with management’s target but well above Regis forecast (90k units). For FY21, we are forecasting Toyota’s car sales to reach 117k units, up 17% YoY. Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s profit estimate and 30% of NAV.

Metro Pacific signs US$130mn 5-year term loan

Metro Pacific signed a US$130mn 5-year term loan from Mizuho Bank Ltd.. The proceeds will be used to partially finance investments. As at end-September, MPI’s parent company gross debt (including off-balance sheet debt) stood at P88bn and cash stood at P25bn while net debt-to-equity at 45%.

 
 

Macro News

DOF urges Congress to pass CREATE ahead of tax season

The Department of Finance has called on Congress to immediately pass the CREATE bill before month-end. This will provide taxpayers ample time to comply with adjustments to their returns due to the lowering of income taxes effective July 2020 before the tax filing season ends in April 2021. A bicameral conference committee is expected to harmonize the conflicting provisions of the Senate’s and House of Representatives’ versions. The congress resumes session on Jan. 18. Under the Senate version, domestic corporations with total assets (ex-land) <P100mn and a net taxable income of <P5mnand below will enjoy an outright 10% reduction in their income tax to 20% from 30%; The rest will have an immediate tax rate cut of 5ppts. Under the House version, CREATE proposes a uniform reduction of 5ppts. Meanwhile, foregone revenues from CREATE are expected to reach P133.2bn in 2021 and P117.6bn in 2022 if implemented retroactively to July 2020. Additionally, the Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer (FIST) is now just awaiting the signature of President Duterte after being ratified by Congress last month.

Exports rise in November; Trade deficit narrows to US$1.7bn

Exports rose by 3% in November (vs. -1.2% YoY in October), as electronic exports rose 5%. Imports fell 19% YoY to US$7.5bn (vs. -18.8% YoY in October), extending its 19-month decline. Electronic products were flat YoY; Imports of non-electronics continued to drop. The trade deficit was 53% YoY lower at US$1.73bn, bringing 11M20 deficit to US$19.66bn, down 48% YoY.

Congress prioritizes economic charter change

House Speaker Lord Allan Velasco said that the Committee on Constitutional Amendments will open deliberations on the proposed Resolution of Both Houses (RBH) No. 2, which seeks to change certain economic provisions of the Philippine Constitution. The RBH No. 2 seeks to insert the phrase “unless otherwise provided by law” to certain sections:

-National patrimony and economy

-Education, science, technology, arts, culture, and sports

-General provisions on media and advertising

This could eventually pave the way for greater foreign ownership in certain industries, including utilities, media, and real estate. Changes to the constitution would require a national referendum.

Banks’ NPL ratio hits 3.81% in November

Gross non-performing loan (NPL) ratio accelerated to 3.81% in November from 3.72% in Oct 2020 and 2.19% in Nov 2019. This is the highest NPL ratio since January 2013 (3.48%). The BSP expects NPL ratio to reach 4.6% by end-2020. Meanwhile, gross NPL reached P405bn, up 2.43% MoM and 69% YoY.

Budget deficit reaches P1.36tn in FY20

Based on preliminary data, the budget deficit reached P1.36tn in FY20 which is equivalent to 7.5% of GDP. This is higher than FY19 deficit of P660bn (3.4% of GDP) according to Dept. of Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez. This also implies Dec 2020 budget deficit stood at P294bn (+17% YoY). FY20 Revenues stood at P2.842tn, down 10% YoY and short of the P2.853tn target. Which means Dec 2020 revenues dropped 8% YoY, 9% MoM to P225bn. Meanwhile, expenditures reached P4.205tn in FY20, up 11% YoY but a tad short of target (P4.233tn) and implies Dec 2020 expenditures rose 5% YoY and 39% MoM to P519bn. To plug the deficit, the government borrowed P2.6tn from local and foreign lenders, excluding P540bn borrowed from the BSP. For 2021, the government is targeting a wider budget deficit of P1.78tn (8.9% of GDP). The Debt-to-GDP ratio expected to settle at 57% this year (vs. 51% in end-Nov 20).

Finance Secretary warns scrapping NAIA rehab PPP deal

Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez expressed his dismay to private investors who have offered to rehabilitate NAIA. He believes Megawide did not have the financial capability. The only thing you’ve seen so far is that they have capital, but we have not seen it in actuality”. The government is considering rehabbing NAIA itself, as it refuses to give in to the demands of private investors (e.g. guarantees that translate to contingent debts and paid for by taxes).

DOE sets scope of ban on new coal plants

The Department of Energy (DoE) excluded existing and operational coal-fired power generation facilities from its moratorium. Last November, the DoE declared a moratorium on new coal-fired power plants. Also not covered are: Committed coal power projects and existing power plant complexes that already have firm expansion plans and existing land site provisions. It also exempts projects with substantial accomplishments, particularly those that have signed lease agreement for the projects, and approved permits or resolutions from local government units and the Regional Development Council where the facilities would be locate.

Fitch keeps BBB rating for Philippines

Fitch Ratings affirmed the 'BBB' rating and Stable Outlook for the Philippines. Fitch forecasts GDP to fall by -8.5% in 2020 and to recover by +6.9% in 2021 and +8% in 2022. Meanwhile, the S&P affirmed its BBB+ rating in May 2020; Moody’s kept its Baa2 rating outlook last July.

Loan growth slows to 0.3% in November; Domestic liquidity growth eases to 10.5%

Based on preliminary data, outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, slowed to 0.3% in November from 1.8% (revised from 1.9%) in October. On a month-on-month seasonally-adjusted basis, loans contracted by 0.3%. Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-4.2%), trade (-6.0%) contracted. Meanwhile, consumer loans (credit cards and auto) expanded at a slower pace of 7.1% from 7.9% in October. Additionally, auto loans contracted by -3.3% (from -1.3% in October). Meanwhile, money supply grew by 10.5% in November, slower than the 11.6% growth (revised) in October.

OFW remittances rise by 0.3% in November

OFW cash remittances grew by 0.3% YoY to US$2.38bn in November (-13.4% MoM). In PHP terms, remittances fell 4.6% YoY and 14% MoM and brings the 3MMA growth rate to +4.1%. Land-based remittances rose by +0.5% YoY while sea-based remittances dropped by -0.2% YoY. Additioanly, remittances from Asia, Europe, and Saudi Arabia dropped by 5.7%, 16%, and 7%, respectively. This brings YT-November remittances down 0.8% YoY to US$27bn.
