REGIS REPORT

19 February 2021

The PSEi moved down 0.9% this week to close at 6926.41.  Prospects of stalled economic recovery owing to continued lockdowns chipped away at sentiment.

The Metro Manila Council (17 mayors in the capital region), voted narrowly in favor of easing restrictions in the area. Nine mayors were in favor of easing the lockdown level to “MGCQ” from “GCQ” while the remainder opted to remain at GCQ. The vote is recommendatory; President Duterte will announce changes in the lockdown status before the end of the month.

Restrictions on mobility and transportation have been stunting the recovery of business activity. The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) is now recommending that the government ease restrictions further.

After a respite in outflows, foreigners resumed selling this week. Net outflows this week reached US$59m.

A few results came out this week, with a surprise dividend taking Metrobank (+2.9% WoW) higher. Despite disappointing NPAT of P13.8bn, Metrobank declared a P3/sh special dividend that took div yield to 8%. The miss was due to continued high provision expense in 4Q.  Full year provision of  P40.8bn translates to a credit cost of fully 325bps. Loans contracted 15.5% YoY, the largest decline on record (going back to 1993). NIMs compressed by 31bps QoQ due in part to the credit card rate cap imposed from November.

MBT parent GT Cap (+3.6% WoW) also ended higher.

Jollibee’s (+0.7% WoW) losses were not as bad as feared.   Operating profit, in fact, returned to the black in 4Q20, helped by the restructuring costs which management believes will yield P2.6bn in annual cost savings. FY20 headline net loss of P11.5bn was better than Regis and consensus expectations.

Meanwhile, SM Prime’s (-3.9% WoW) profit for 4Q dropped 65% taking the FY20 figure down 53% to P18.0bn, slightly behind REGIS forecast (and below consensus estimate). Philippine mall revenue grew 39% QoQ as foot traffic improved from c. 40% in September to 50-60% in December. Nonetheless, 4Q20 reservation sales jumped 37% YoY (+33% QoQ) thanks to higher launches.

Banks generally outperformed this week.  President Duterte signed Republic Act 11523 (aka Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer or FIST) into law. The “FIST” Law grants banks tax breaks relating to the disposition of their non-performing assets. The law was rushed in response to the expected bad debt problems arising from the pandemic and should allow banks to recover from asset quality issues more quickly.

Metropolitan Bank - Dividend surprise (8% yield) despite earnings miss
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Show of strength?

Massive provision, and special div Metrobank surprised the market with a P3/sh special dividend, bringing the stock’s dividend yield to 8% based on yesterday’s close. This was despite the bank expensing a whopping P40.8bn in provisions, dragging FY20 NPAT to P13.8bn, missing estimates. While NPLs increased from 2.25% in 3Q to 2.41% in 4Q, the provisions took NPL coverage to 163%. This, together with CAR of 20.2% – provides significant buffers for any new NPLs this year. NIM compression and lower trading gains may weigh on 2021E, but we reckon the discount to book value is unwarranted. Maintain Buy.

NPAT misses, on continued pre-emptive provisioning

MBT reported FY20 NPAT of P13.8bn, down 51% YoY, behind consensus (P16.5bn) and our (P19.5bn) estimates. The miss was due largely to provision expense, which stayed high at P5.4bn in 4Q20, taking the FY figure to P40.8bn, up 304% YoY, and equivalent to credit cost of 325bps. Trading/fx gains also faded in 4Q. Ex-trading, PPOP was broadly in line with our expectations.

Loans shrink 15.5%; NIMs contract, as loan yields follow deposit costs down

Net loans fell 15.5% YoY (gross loans -13%), with contraction seen across all major segments of the book. This was the largest drop in loans on our records (back to 1993). Margins also compressed, down 31bps QoQ in 4Q20, though fullyear NIMs were still up 25-30bps. To the upside, CASA surged 22% YoY taking CASA ratio to 73% and deposits costs down to a record-low 0.37% in 4Q20. However, loan yields collapsed 74bps QoQ on lower benchmark rates, and due to the credit card interest rate cap imposed from November.

A challenging 2021E, but high provision, capital buffers protect book

NIM pressure, and lower trading/fx gains pose profit challenges to 2021E. That said, the stock now trades at 0.68x P/bk on our 2021E forecasts. With NPL cover at 163% and CAR of 20.2% (parent CET-1 of 18.6%), we consider the valuation discount rather harsh. We have a Buy on the stock.

Cebu Air - Adjusting TP, estimates for final rights pricing; Maintain Buy (revised)
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Price set at P38/sh; div rate at 6%

Cebu Air (CEB) fixed the subscription price and dividend rate of its convertible preferred share rights offer at P38/sh and 6%, respectively. Investors that buy CEB shares before the ex-rights date (Feb 22) will be entitled to subscribe to one (1) convertible pref share at P38/sh for every 1.825 common shares they hold. CEB will be offering 329mn convertible prefs and each convertible pref can be converted into a common share beginning forty days after the prefs are listed.

This report is being revised to reflect the revised basis for computing our Regis net profit adjustments and EPS. We now present both assuming full dilution from the conversion of the upcoming convertible preferred shares and convertible bonds.

Pricing in larger dilution

The P38/sh subscription price is below our assumption (P53/sh), and the total number of shares to be issued in the rights offer is also larger than we assumed. In addition, if a similar price is used for the convertible instrument to be issued to third parties, we estimate CEB’s share count could rise to 1.252bn shares from 600mn today. This is 18% larger than our previously estimated fully diluted share count of 1.058bn. Meanwhile, the dividend rate of 6% is also higher than our previous assumption (1.5%). We adjust our FY22 Regis profit by +7% and fully diluted EPS by -9%.

Maintain Buy at revised price target of P65/sh

We use a target EV/EBITDA multiple of 6.5x, lower than historical average (7x). At this target multiple, we estimate CEB’s equity value at c. P80bn, on what we believe to be reasonable estimates of 2022 EBITDA coming in 15% below 2019 levels. This compares with the current market cap of P30bn. We revise our TP to P65/sh (from P77/sh previously), after assuming the larger fully diluted share count of 1.252bn. Despite the larger-than-expected dilution, our revised price target still offers a hefty potential upside of 42% from what could be the estimated theoretical ex-rights price (TERP) of ~P46/sh. A recovery stronger than our expectations (possibly due to market share gains) could further increase upside potential to c. 180%.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Banks - FIST could return focus on growth, valuations
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Law unburdens banks’ equity; refocuses investors, management

The Financial Institution Strategic Transfer (FIST) law encourages banks to sell NPLs and foreclosed assets to SPVs by greatly reducing friction costs (ie taxes). NPAs disposals could also reduce the drag on banks’ capital ratios. It would also limit losses, and allow management – and investors – to take their focus away from asset quality issues, in favor of re-growing the business. While the near-term earnings impact may be limited, FIST could be a catalyst for a sector re-rating.

Tax incentives granted hastens transfer of assets

President Duterte signed the FIST Act into law, which provides a host of tax and regulatory incentives for banks to sell non-performing assets (NPAs). The law does not set up a national asset management company (AMC). Instead, the law envisages private AMCs – called FIST Corporations or FIST-Cs – buying NPA portfolios off banks. However, qualified government entities may take stakes in FIST-Cs. The key provisions as we see it are:

  • Sales of assets from a bank to a FIST-C, and from a FIST-C to a third party, are exempt from stamp duty (DST) and capital gains tax. This is critical for real estate assets, where each transaction would normally be taxed at 6% of the gross value (ie 12% total for both steps).
  • FIST-Cs will be exempt from corporate income tax on its net interest income (NII), and from DST on issuing new loans.
  • NPA sales from banks to FIST-Cs must be “true sales”, ie there must be no recourse to the seller. However, banks will be allowed to take a maximum 10% stake in the buying FIST-C.
  • The incentives for bank NPA sales to FIST-Cs last for 2 years, while incentives for FIST-Cs run for 5 years. The Department of Finance may extend the incentives for another 2 and 5 years, respectively.

Growth and profit expectations, unchanged for now

Asset sales could relieve the burden on banks’ capital, as NPLs and foreclosures carry a 150% risk weight. However, banks we cover have CET-1 ratios that are well above the regulatory minimum of 8.0%; the BSP temporarily suspended the 250bps capital conservation buffer (Fig 6). While high NPL coverage (Fig 5) may mean profits from asset sales, we do not expect banks to book major gains.

Rerating catalyst?

We believe the real upside from FIST will be removing uncertainty over asset quality issues. NPA sales will mean management will be able to focus their efforts to re-growing the business. For investors, FIST could remove the overhang of potential earnings and book impairment, and instead re-focus on valuations. Banks we cover are trading at 25-45% discounts to their 15-year average price-to-book ratios (Figs 7-10).

 
PUBLISHED RESEACH
Philippine Strategy - (Lack of) motion sickness – mobility data bodes ill for 1Q21
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Mobility – unblocking the key bottleneck to recovery

We have consistently identified tough movement restrictions as the main bottleneck to economic recovery. Mobility data slumped again in early February, pointing to another weak quarter for demand. The IATF has recommended looser rules for venues such as theaters, conference / convention centers, and places of worship. More crucially, in our view, the NEDA is pushing for increased capacity in public transportation from 50% to 75%. The question remains: will the leadership at the national, and perhaps more importantly, local government levels listen?

IATF, NEDA call for further loosening of quarantine restrictions…

Over the weekend, the Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) – the body that advises on national Covid policy – allowed the reopening of cinemas, arcades, libraries, museums, conference facilities, and theme parks for areas under General Community Quarantine (GCQ) like Metro Manila. It also allowed places of worship to increase maximum capacity from 30% to 50%. The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) took it a step further, recommending that Metro Manila and other provinces be downgraded to Modified GCQ (MGCQ), the loosest quarantine category. It also recommended, among other things, increasing public transport capacity limits from 50% to 75% (Figures 3 and 4); allowing more provincial buses to operate, and widening the age of those allowed in public to ages 5 to 70, from the current 15 to 65.

… but needs buy-in from 81 governors and 1,634 mayors

The economic cluster of the Cabinet – NEDA, the Departments of Finance, Trade, Tourism etc – has advocated for further opening since at least October 2020. However, provincial and local leaders have resisted. Some Metro Manila mayors have already expressed opposition to NEDA’s MGCQ proposal. The Local Government Code gives the country’s 81 governors and 1,634 mayors significant control over policy in their respective districts. Each city and province currently has its own rules and restrictions for travel into and within the areas. As a result, even domestic air traffic is down c90% from pre-Covid levels (Figure 6).

Mobility data slows in early Feb, puts 1Q21 growth, earnings at risk

Data from Google shows that mobility in the Philippines improved over the December holidays, but has since slumped back down to October 2020 levels (Figures 1 and 2). This bodes ill for 1Q21 GDP and for earnings. Two large caps that have reported 2020 results – Jollibee and SM Prime – noted significant improvement in 4Q20, in line with historic holiday bumps. If the Google data is any indication, we could see much of that fade into 1Q21 as well.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
SM Prime - Reservation sales surge in 4Q20
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Revenue slightly ahead, earnings slightly behind

SM Prime’s 4Q20 net profit fell 65% YoY to P3.6bn. This brought FY20 earnings down 53% to P18.0bn, slightly below REGIS forecast (and well below consensus estimates). Revenue of the major segments improved QoQ and appear in line or above our forecasts for the full year, suggesting that the underperformance was driven by expenses. Meanwhile, reservation sales surged in 4Q20.

Philippine mall foot traffic improves further

Philippine mall revenue grew 39% QoQ as foot traffic improved from c. 40% of pre-COVID levels in September to 50-60% in December, thanks to the holiday season. However, despite the improvement in foot traffic, revenue fell 66% YoY due to the rental concessions that were granted. In January 2021, SM Prime started charging minimum rents on tenants who pay based on percentage-of-sales (though only a fraction of pre-COVID levels). The company hopes to gradually increase this as sales improve. Unfortunately, our channel checks suggest that foot traffic dropped off in January. Limitations on public transportation are hampering the recovery of consumption spending.

Flat China mall revenue

The company claims 4Q20 China mall revenue was broadly flat YoY in local currency terms. Foot traffic has almost fully recovered as the pandemic has been relatively contained. 4Q20 reservation sales jump 4Q20 reservation sales jumped 37% YoY (33% QoQ), bringing the FY20 figure up 10% (vs flat YoY in 9M20). SM Prime launched 8,000 units in 4Q20, from only 7,000 units for the entire 9M20. The company credits the strong demand to its international marketing efforts, which resulted in strong sales to overseas Filipinos, and relative affordability of its products.

 
 

Corporate News

Razon buys Manila Water participating preferred shares

Ayala Corp, through its unit Philwater Hldgs., signed a share purchase agreement with Trident Water Co. to sell 2.69bn worth of participating preferred shares (PPS) in Manila Water (MWC). PPS reflect a 39.09% voting stake and 8.19% economic stake in MWC. The Rights and title to the shares, except voting rights covered by the proxies, shall be transferred upon full payment. Dividend income from the PPS shall accrue to AC/Philwater until full payment. Trident Water is owned by Enrique Razon, Jr. The purchase price is P4.844bn while payment terms are as follows, P100mn upon signing, P2.37bn payable after 4 years, P2.37bn after 5 years. We estimate this deal values MWC at P18/sh. This latest development will allow Mr. Razon to increase its economic stake in MWC to 34% from 25% previously. Previously, Mr. Razon only had proxy rights to the PPS even after deal close.

Dennis Uy open to selling Phoenix Petroleum

Davao-based Dennis Uy said he was open to selling a minority or controlling stake in Phoenix Petroleum (PNX PM). Phoenix Petroleum has 665 retail stations, making it the third-largest oil company in the Philippines. It acquired LPG company Petronas Energy Philippines in 2017 and the local Family Mart convenience store chain in 2018.

Vehicle sales slightly down in January

Members of CAMPI/TMA sold 23,380 units in January, down 1% YoY and 15% MoM. The MoM drop looks typical for a January performance. Last year’s sales were also affected by the Taal volcano eruption. Toyota’s market share stood at 46.28%, implying that it sold 10,820 units in January, up 22% YoY but down 21% MoM. However, sales from February onwards will likely already reflect the impact of the provisional safeguard duty on imported vehicles. The Tariff Commission started its 200-day review of the duty last Feb 4. Toyota estimates the import duty will likely reduce its FY21 sales volume estimate to 104k-108k units (+4-8% YoY) from 130k units (+30% YoY). Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 15% discount to its market-based NAV of P647/sh.

FGEN recommissions San Gabriel plant

The 414MW San Gabriel Combined Cycle Power Plant was restarted and placed online, following the completion of repairs to the generator. The generator was damaged after a trip that occurred in September 2020.

SpaceX and Converge in talks for broadband satellite venture

The Philippine Daily Inquirer reported that representatives of Converge ICT Solutions (CNVRG PM) and Space Exploration Technologies Corp (SpaceX) have met on multiple occasions to discuss a potential broadband satellite venture. In a disclosure to the PSE, Converge said that it is still very premature to talk about a possible partnership with SpaceX. SpaceX previously met with Philippine senators and said that it wanted to connect the Philippines to Starlink, its global broadband satellite project, by 3Q21. However, as an internet service provider, Starlink would be considered a public utility which would require a congressional franchise, a 40% cap on foreign ownership and a certificate of public convenience and necessity from the National Telecommunications Commission. Starlink is available in certain parts of the US and Canada..

JFC FY20 recurring net loss better than expectations

JFC reported a headline net loss of P11.5bn for FY20, better than Regis and consensus expectations. On a recurring basis and after deducting coupon payments to perps, we estimate the FY20 loss was P7.8bn. Meanwhile, FY20 recurring operating loss (excluding business transformation costs) came in at P5.9bn. Both were also ahead of our FY20E. Revenues of P129.5bn (-28% YoY) mildly missed expectations. Excluding one-time expenses from its business transformation (P3.8bn in 4Q20), operating profit turned positive to P1.1bn. This was attributed to cost savings relating to the business transformation, which involved the closure of four commissaries and 262 stores by year-end. Another 224 stores were permanently closed during the year, bringing total store closures to 486 (c.8% of total store network). On a full-year basis, JFC expects to realize c.P2.6bn in cost savings from the business transformation initiative. Revenues in 4Q20 were down 30% YoY, mainly dragged by the Philippine business. Same store sales declines in most markets (ex-CBTL) had already hit single-digit level during the quarter, with the Philippines (60% of systemwide sales) still being the key underperformer. We estimate that average sales per store in the Philippines in 4Q20 is still c.30% below the pre-pandemic average, while other markets have mostly recovered. Amidst increasing input prices (particularly pork and chicken), the company believes cost inflation is manageable for now, and had raised prices for selected brands in the Philippines by c1% in January. They expect to see further inflation by 2Q/3Q, and may raise prices further if needed.

FPH amends tender offer for Lopez Holdings shares

First Philippine Holdings Corp has modified its tender offer to buy common shares of Lopez Holdings Corp (for a second time) by lowering the number of stocks it plans to acquire. Its revised tender offer will cover up to a max of 1.43bn common shares, equivalent to 31.5% of the total issued and outstanding common shares. The original plan was to acquire a minimum of 908.46mn (20%) up to a maximum of 2.07bn shares (45.56%) at a price of P3.85/share (a 25% premium to the stock price when the restructuring was announced, 27% discount to NAV). The first amendment reduced the maximum number of shares to be acquired to 1.57bn shares (34.5%). The tender offer price is unchanged (at P3.85/share). Additionally, the offer period was also extended from February 19 to March 8. The reduction in the number of shares will remove the risk of Lopez Holdings falling below the required minimum public ownership. Thus, LPZ will remain listed after the tender offer.

SEC approves DoubleDragon REIT IPO

The SEC approved the IPO of DDMP REIT with an offer price of up to P2.25/share. The maximum offer will be P14.7bn (including overallotment) which means the maximum market cap of P40bn. DDMP estimates that at the maximum offer price, it would offer a dividend yield of 5.1% in 2021. The company is looking to sell up to 6.5bn common shares of which secondary shares will be 5.94bn shares and the overallotment will be 594.2mn shares. Offer period is set at March 5 to 11 with a listing date of March 19. DDMP REIT has 172,000sqm of office space in the Manila Bay area which is said to be 99% occupied. Around 65% of space is leased to gaming related tenants. Additionally, DDMP owns the land under the offices. DDMP is currently 70% owned by DoubleDragon Properties. DoubleDragon could raise up to P10.3bn from the sale of secondary DDMP shares. Colliers estimates that office rental rates in the Manila Bay area have fallen c. 30% YoY. For comparison, AREIT has an office portfolio of 245,000sqm and a market cap of P34.9bn.

MPI selling stake in Thai tollroad

Metro Pacific Tollways Corp (MPTC) entered into a share purchase agreement (SPA) with several parties for the sale of its entire 29.45% stake in Thailand-based Don Muang Tollway (DMT). MPTC is a 99.9%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM). The transaction is expected to occur within a few days after the signing of SPAs. DMT holds the concession for a 21.9km elevated toll road in Bangkok. The valuation for the stake is US$149.25mn (P7.168bn), which will be paid in cash on closing. This is c. 20% higher than our valuation of P5.9bn. MPI also expects to realize a gain on this transaction. We estimate the transaction will be very mildly value accretive; DMT accounts for c. 3% of MPI’s NAV. MPI is trading at a 26% discount to its market NAV of P5.70/sh.

 
 

Macro News

Remittances dip in December

OFW remittances dipped 0.4% YoY to US$2.89bn in December (+21.5% MoM). That means that in PHP terms, remittances fell 5.7% YoY but rose 21% MoM. Remittances from Europe (-31%) and Asia (-9%) fell, offsetting the 14% jump in remittances from the USA. This brings the 3MMA growth in remittances was at +0.9%. FY20 remittances fell slightly (-0.8%) to US$29.9bn (in PHP terms, remittances fell 5% YoY). The BSP expects remittances to grow by 4% in 2021.

GIR dips slightly to US$109bn in January

​The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, settled at US$108.8bn as of end-January 2021. This is lower than the end-December 2020 GIR level of US$110.12bn. The drop came from outflows mainly from the foreign currency withdrawals of the National Government (NG) from its deposits in the BSP and revaluation of gold holdings. This level is equivalent to around 11.6 months’ worth of imports.

Supreme Court dismisses Marcos electoral protest vs Robredo

The Supreme Court, sitting as the Presidential Electoral Tribunal (PET), dismissed the electoral protest filed by former Senator Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr., for lack of merit. This affirms the election victory of Vice President Ma. Leonor “Leni” Robredo. However, lawyers of Marcos said that the PET only resolved the second cause of action (the manual recount and judicial revision) and not the entire petition. The Marcos camp said that the PET has yet to decide on the third cause of action, which is the annulment of votes in parts of Mindanao due to alleged discrepancies.

Duterte signs FIST into law

President Duterte signed Republic Act No. 11523 (a.k.a. Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer or FIST). FIST will allow financial institutions to offload bad loans to asset management companies. This should be generally positive for banks as this relieves them of the burden of managing bad assets. The key provisions are “At least 2-year tax exemption on asset sales from a bank to the FIST Corporation (FIST-C)” and “At least 5-year tax exemption on asset sales from a FIST-C to a third party”. FIST-Cs will be exempt from income tax on its NII, and DST on new loans. Meanwhile, losses arising from a bank’s sale of NPAs to a FIST-C will be considered an “ordinary loss”. Banks will be allowed to invest up to 10% in the FIST-Cs that will buy their NPAs.

Metro Manila council votes to ease GCQ status to MGCQ

The Metro Manila Council voted in favor of easing quarantine status to modified general community quarantine (MGCQ) in March. Nine out of 17 mayors voted in favor of shifting to MGCQ while eight voted to stay under GCQ. The National Economic Dev’t Authority is pushing for public transportation capacity to be expanded to 75% from 50%, as well as boost operations of inter-province buses.

Arrival of Sinovac and Pfizer vaccines delayed

The initial 117,000 doses of the Pfizer COVID vaccine (through the COVAX facility) will be delivered in early March instead of February. The Philippines has signed the required indemnification agreement but Pfizer has not done so. The indemnification agreement would absolve the manufacturer of liability in case of adverse side effects. The delivery of 600,000 Sinovac vaccines will also be delayed (from the initial schedule of February) because Sinovac does not yet have an Emergency Use Authorization (EUA) from the Philippine Food and Drug Administration (FDA). So far, only Pfizer and AstraZeneca have secured EUAs from the FDA.
