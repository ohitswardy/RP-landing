REGIS REPORT

04 November 2021

The reopening theme carried the market higher this week to its highest close since the start of the pandemic.. The PSEi gained 4.1% WoW to close at 7340.77.

The government announced Thursday night a lower lockdown level for Metro Manila (Alert 2 from Level 3), effectively increasing capacities of malls, restaurants and indoor spaces. Children will now be allowed outside their homes.

The continued easing has been made possible by the continued decline in case counts. The latest 7-day moving average of new daily cases has fallen to below 2,891 from 4,660 in the previous week. Cases in the last two days dipped to around 1591-1766 daily. NCR’s 7-day ave daily cases is down to 625 from its recent peak of 5,900 (mid-September). 

Foreigners turned buyers for US$17m on optimism over a quicker recovery in 4Q on continued loosening of restrictions. Biggest inflows were focused in cyclicals/reopening plays SM Prime and Ayala Land as well as banks BPI BDO. Foreigners also flocked into telcos Globe and PLDT.

In terms of performance, property led this week, with Robinsons Land (+10.6% WoW) and SM Prime (+8.8% WoW) rallying. Ayala Land (+6.8% WoW) gained as well despite 3Q21 earnings falling behind on muted revenues and cost pressures hurting residential earnings. 

Banks also gained, with BPI (+7.5% WoW) taking top spot, followed by BDO (+3.9% WoW). Metrobank’s (+3.1% WoW) latest results are tracking behind consensus, due largely to weak trading/fx gains.  Asset quality improved with NPL ratio falling to 2.1% (from 2.3% in 2Q) and coverage at 191%.

Bloomberry (+10.3% WoW) was also a top gainer as the reopening theme played out;  9M earnings are tracking ahead of forecasts.

The Index was up across the board, with only 1 of the 30 members closing in the red this week.  URC (-4.3% WoW) declined after disappointing earnings that were crimped by margins. Century Pacific’s (-1.9% WoW) 3Q came in line on the other hand, but margin pressure from higher input costs was evident as well.

Meantime, Robinsons Retail (+2.7% WoW), with earnings falling behind due to lockdown measures in 3Q leading to softness for discretionary, mall-based revenues. Easing quarantine restrictions are expected to help in terms of SSSG recovery.

Semirara (-6.7% WoW) corrected. Results came in ahead of estimates but the numbers likely fell short of the market’s expectations given the surge in coal prices in 3Q.  Coal volumes dropped and the company also need to purchase more power from the spot market.

Universal Robina - 3Q21: cost pressures abound as lockdowns crimp top line recovery
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M21 EBIT (-9% YoY) behind estimates
URC’s 9M21 net profit jumped 40% YoY to P10.5bn, boosted by a one-off gain of P3.3bn from the sale of land assets booked in 2Q21. Underlying operations are soft, with revenues (+3% YoY to P102.6bn) and operating profit (-3% YoY to P11.6bn) trailing FY21 estimates. For 3Q21 alone, net profit (+25% YoY) was boosted by forex gains. Cost pressure was apparent with EBIT -9% YoY even as revenues grew 5% YoY.

Delta-related lockdowns crimp top line recovery
Domestic branded consumer food (BCF) sales were flattish YoY as the fresh round of lockdowns in Aug crimped the recovery. Apart from the Phils, Covid- related lockdowns were also imposed in key foreign markets, such as Vietnam and Thailand. We estimate top line in these markets was just flattish during the quarter, coming from double-digit growth in 2Q.

Cost pressures persist; another round of price increases seen
Consolidated EBIT margin contracted to 9.8% (-150bps YoY) due to higher input cost. While the company has been implementing price adjustments since May (+2% cumulatively), these adjustments have not been enough to fully offset the increases in input cost. Prices of key commodities, such as wheat and palm oil, continued to surge toward the end of the quarter, which could mean further price adjustments in the coming months.

Oceania sale expected to close in 4Q; M&A to be a strategic lever
The company expects to close the sale of its Oceania business before the end of 4Q and book a gain of cUSD250mn once completed. With the cash proceeds (c.USD470mn), management believes it will have greater flexibility when pursuing M&A opportunities (whether large or small) over the near to medium term. In the meantime, the company has increased its dividend payout for the year and will soon commence its P3bn share buyback program.

 
Philippine Strategy: Revenge travel - domestic tourism a potential upside surprise for 2022 GDP?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Local tourism is critical, as economy reopens
Nearly every country closed international borders for the pandemic. In the Philippines, though, even travel between provinces was severely restricted. In 3Q21, domestic airline volumes were still down 80% from 2019 levels (Fig 4). The country is much more dependent on local tourism than on foreign (Fig 2&3). In 2019, tourism contributed 13% to GDP (Fig 1); we estimate foreign receipts were only 3% of GDP. In 2020, those plunged to 5.4% and 0.7% of GDP respectively. As vaccination rates rise, local governments are further easing rules. “Revenge travel” could provide a surprise boost to our GDP forecasts for 2022E (+6.5%). Cebu Air (CEB PM) would be the best way to play it, as Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) and Megawide (MWIDE PM) rely more on foreign tourists.

Strict domestic travel restrictions slowly being relaxed
In response to the pandemic, each province came up with its own rules for entry from other parts of the country. At the Covid peak, many provinces required 2- week quarantines even for local travelers. Rules still vary widely. While few now need quarantines, most still require Covid tests before entry. Recently, some have started easing rules for vaccinated travelers, with 14 cities no longer requiring Covid tests. This is significant, an RT- PCR test costs roughly P3,000, while a round trip Manila-Cebu-Manila airline ticket can cost as little as P2,300 (without check-in baggage).

Local tourism: an underappreciated upside risk?
Vaccinations are ramping up – while less than 30% of the total population has been jabbed (at least once), we estimate that Metro Manila is already at 70% (Fig 6), while Metro Cebu and Metro Davao are at around 45%. As more provinces loosen travel restrictions, domestic tourism could provide an unexpected upside lift to the economy. Mobility numbers popped when Metro Manila rules were eased mid-October (Fig 7). We expect another jump from 30 Oct - 01 Nov, the last long weekend until the Christmas holidays.

 
Century Pacific Food: 3Q branded revenues resilient despite weak consumption; raising TP to P33/sh
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat Research +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M21 +21%YoY, broadly in line with expectations
CNPF posted net profit of P4bn in 9M21, up 21% YoY, broadly within Regis expectations. Revenues (+12% YoY to P41.1bn) are tracking slightly ahead, mainly due to the Non-branded OEM segment. Meanwhile, EBITDA of P5.9bn (+11% YoY) is in line with estimates. For 3Q21 alone, net profit rose 22% YoY to P1.3bn, partly boosted by a lower tax rate.

3Q branded sales flat QoQ; overall revenues boosted by OEM recovery
Revenues grew 21% YoY in 3Q21, as the non-branded OEM segment (25% of revs) continued to recover. Branded sales grew 13% YoY, though the YoY growth was largely a result of base effects. On a QoQ basis, revenues were flattish as the Aug lockdowns did not result in as much pantry stocking vs previous lockdowns. Demand, however, continued to be resilient despite weak consumption overall as CNPF’s branded products mainly cater to the value-for-money segment.

Higher input cost pressures margins
Gross margin contracted over 300bps YoY to 23.5% in 3Q. While this was partly driven by the sales mix (OEM lower margin vs Branded), management noted continued cost pressure, particularly on meats, milk, packaging, and freight cost. The company has implemented price increases of 1-3% YTD to mitigate the impact of higher cost.

 

FY21 guidance maintained but cost pressures to persist
Management is maintaining its guidance to grow top line by 10-15% (Regis FY21E: +14%) and net profit by c20% (Regis FY21E: +22%). Management continues to see YoY growth in early 4Q, but noted that there was some trade loading ahead of the price increases implemented. The near-term demand outlook remains challenged as the base of the market continues to see constrained purchasing power. At the same time, management is actively managing cost given the continued spikes in prices of key raw materials and freight cost. To the upside, skipjack tuna prices have remained relatively soft.

Raising TP to P33/sh (+14%); maintaining Buy
Notwithstanding the challenging near term outlook, we remain positive on the longer term growth prospects of CNPF. In 3Q alone, the company launched two new products – a chocolate malt drink and its initial foray into pet food – which we believe could be significant growth drivers over the medium to longer term. Both products are in segments that are relatively underpenetrated with only a few players, giving CNPF an opportunity to carve out its share of the market. We are tweaking our 2022-24E estimates by +1-5% and rolling over our valuations to 2022E. Our TP is raised by 14% to P33/sh, which implies 2022E valuations of 22x PE and 15x EV/EBITDA. Maintaining Buy.

 
Ayala Land: Hard lockdown weighs on 3Q21 but demand should rebound shortly
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M21 earnings disappoint but 4Q looks rosier
3Q21 net profit fell 22% QoQ to P2.55bn as the hard lockdown kept revenue muted and cost pressures weighed further on the residential segment. While 9M21 earnings of P8.6bn is below REGIS and consensus forecasts, lower COVID cases, higher vaccinations, and greater mobility (particularly in Metro Manila) should pave the way for demand recovery beginning 4Q21.

Residential margin cut in half
We estimate that 3Q21 residential EBIT margin halved to 13% from 26% in 1H21. While steel prices have surged this year, the abruptness of the drop leads us to think there might be some non-recurring (or accounting-related) items involved.

Reservation sales improve 11% QoQ on high-end lots
Despite the hard lockdown, reservation sales climbed 11% QoQ to P21.9bn (60% of the 2019 average). Similar to previous quarters, sales of high-end lots in provinces adjacent to Metro Manila were particularly brisk. The reopening of the economy should bode well for future residential demand.

Malls still struggling but should rebound beginning 4Q21
Mall revenue was flat QoQ at P1.5bn, only 32% of the 3Q19 figure. The major operating metrics were also largely unchanged QoQ, with foot traffic at 25%- 30% of pre-COVID levels. However, this segment in particular should see a substantial improvement beginning 4Q21. About 70% of ALI’s mall space is Metro Manila, where vaccinations rates are the highest (c. 65% of total population is fully vaccinated) and mobility restrictions have been loosened the most.

Office vacancy maintained at 18%
Office vacancy was maintained (QoQ) at 18%, in line with industry average of 17%. Among Ayala Land’s major segments, we expect offices to show the slowest growth (post-COVID) due to new industry supply weighing on rental rates.

 
PLDT, Inc: Broadband strength continues into 3Q
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M core income up +10% to P21.8bn, in line with estimates
Headline profit for PLDT came in at P18.8bn for 9M21 (+5% YoY), with core income at P21.8bn (+10%), inclusive of its share of losses at Voyager. We would describe core earnings as being on pace for our FY21E estimate, if not for the losses at Voyager trending higher than assumed. For 2Q alone, core income was P7.5bn (+13% YoY).

Broadband delivers another record quarter
The broadband segment saw 3Q revenue of P11.3bn, +32% YoY and +8% QoQ. PLDT added net 229k subscribers to its wired broadband network, the most in a single quarter. This took its wired broadband base to 2.77m, of which 2.09m are on fiber. PLDT aims to complete full migration to fiber by 1Q22.

Softness in wireless segment continues
The signs of softness in the wireless business that emerged earlier in the year continued into 3Q. Restraints on personal mobility as well as depressed personal incomes are likely holding back mobile spend. Wireless revenue in 3Q dipped very slightly QoQ (-0.4%) and is now only marginally up YoY (+1%).

Update on Voyager and its data centers
PLDT says that Paymaya, the e-wallet/payment solutions firm under 38.5%- owned associate Voyager now has 41m registered users, up 2.7x since the start of 2020. It will also be launching operations for Maya Bank early next year after securing a digital banking license from the Bangko Sentral (BSP). The group’s decision to launch its own digital bank stands in contrast to Mynt’s preference to do tie-ups with other banks instead. Management at PLDT also revealed that Voyager’s latest funding round valued it at US$747m, post-money. PLDT’s stake at that valuation would be worth P67 per PLDT share.

PLDT also expects to complete, by early 2022, its 11th data center, which will be larger than its first ten data centers combined. This new facility is being built for ‘hyperscalers’, and the company is aiming to secure its first anchor tenant by next year.

 
Robinsons Retail: 3Q21 lockdown tempers top line, but outlook improving
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M21 results trail estimates as lockdowns weigh on 3Q performance

RRHI reported net profit of P2.7bn, up 13% YoY, trailing consensus estimates. Net sales were flattish during the period at P108.9bn, while operating profit contracted 10% YoY. For 3Q21 alone, net profit jumped 39% YoY, boosted by non- operating items (e.g. lower tax rate, forex gains). Underlying operations were soft with EBIT mildly up (+3% YoY) during the quarter.

Essential formats hold steady in 3Q21

Consolidated SSSG was flattish (-1.3%) during the quarter. Performance across formats was mixed given the effects of the various lockdown measures both in 3Q20 and 3Q21. Nevertheless, we estimate essential formats (Supermarkets, Drug stores) are already tracking above pre-pandemic levels. These formats collectively accounted for 78% of RRHI’s consolidated sales during the quarter.

Non-essentials weighed by lockdowns

On the flipside, the recovery of RRHI’s non-essential formats was weighed by the Aug lockdowns, during which most of its mall-based stores had to be shut for two weeks. We estimate SSS of these formats were still tracking between 30- 60% of pre-pandemic levels – with Dept Stores and Convenience Stores being the main underperformers.

Sequential improvement seen as quarantine measures eased

Management noted that they started to see a marked improvement beginning September, continuing into recent weeks. The company is seeing double-digit SSSG across all its formats in October and is hoping the momentum will continue as we move into the Christmas season. A continued recovery in RRHI’s top line – particularly for its higher-margin non-essential formats – should lead to improving profitability in the coming quarters.

 
AC Energy Corp: 9M income boosted by acquisitions, finished projects
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M21 income +22%
ACEN reported that its 9M21 net income increased 22% to P4.270bn, tracking slightly behind Regis but ahead of consensus (P5.6bn). This implies 3Q21 profit amounted to P1.580bn, up 11%QoQ and 68%YoY. The company attributed the growth to recovery in electricity demand in Luzon and new capacity from recent acquisitions and projects. QoQ growth of underlying operations was tempered, however, by higher cost of purchased power due to elevated spot market prices. Recall that ACEN was still mildly short WESM as contracts exceeded actual output up until early 4Q.

Capacity update: 3,000MW attributable
After factoring in the increased stakes in UPC/AC Renewables Australia, UPC Renewables Ph and NorthWind that were announced last month, ACEN’s attributable capacity stands at a little over 3,00MW, 60% of its 2025 target of 5,000MW. 36% of this figure is under construction and 83% is from renewable technologies. Including thermal assets and pipeline projects for financial close over the next 12 months, we estimate that ACEN’s attributable capacity should total over 4,200MW.

Remaining equity transactions will reduce AC’s ownership in AC to 62.5% by year-end
The issuance of 16.685bn shares to AC in exchange for the international assets (and listing last October 22) will be followed by two more transactions this year: the sale of 2.689bn shares held by AC to GIC/Arran and the issuance of 1.422bn shares to UPC. By year-end, AC’s ownership will go down from 71.8% to 62.5% of the enlarged capital base of 39.648bn shares.

AC Enexor designated as spin-off vehicle for thermal assets
ACEN again reiterated its commitment to transition to a 100% renewable portfolio by 2025. To this end, it unveiled a plan to inject thermal assets worth P3.39bn into listed subsidiary ACE Enexor (ACEX). After restructuring, ACEX will hold 175MW of diesel plants, the Palawan55 oil exploration service contract and Ingrid development SPV (diesel projects). ACEN will hold 90% of ACEX after the planned asset injections but we expect it will eventually be divested by 2025.

Semirara 3Q21 profit down QoQ

Coal mining EBITDA drops QoQ. Profits from coal mining fell 7% QoQ, as the 18% QoQ improvement in coal ASPs was nullified by a 20% contraction in sales volumes to 3.9mn MT, after heavy rains resulted in a 50% QoQ drop in production. Coal sales volumes in 9M21 reached 12.7mn MT, trending better than Regis forecast of 14.5mn MT. Moreover, the improvement in SCC’s coal ASPs was more subdued than seaborne thermal coal prices, as >40% of its coal sales during the quarter were lower-priced un-washed coal. Currently, about 590MW of its capacity is operational, as one plant is on maintenance outage while another is de-rated. Following contract expiries, the company’s contracted capacity is now down to 390MW and will further drop to 200MW by year-end.

4Q21 earnings outlook; Dividend prospects. We reckon the coal mining unit’s earnings will improve further in 4Q, despite lower coal sales volumes. Despite yesterday’s hefty correction which brought coal futures down to US$145/MT, thermal coal forward contracts still suggest coal prices will average US$167/MT in 4Q21 and gradually drop to US$105/MT in 4Q22, after peaking at US$270/MT in early October. Jefferies’ coal team also thinks coal prices will rise again as the winter season intensifies.

Even on our base case forecast of US$100/MT coal and recent share price run-up, SCC’s div yield and free cash flow yield are still at an attractive 9% and 12%, respectively.

Notes from MWIDE call on Anti-Dummy Law violation

Summary: We attended a meeting with Megawide’s legal team to discuss the anti-dummy violation complaint against the directors and officers of GMR-Megawide Cebu Airport Corp. (GMCAC). A formal case has not been filed and MWIDE believes the complainants do not have a legal basis. With only a few months left until the country transitions to a new government and the possible passage of the Public Service Act bill, we believe this issue will be short lived.

  • No case filed at courts for now. So far, the Department of Justice (DOJ) has not filed a case against GMCAC at the regional trial court of Lapu-Lapu. The DOJ issued a resolution last October 18, recommending the filing of the case and Megawide responded by filing a petition with the Office of the Secretary of Justice, challenging the DOJ’s resolution and to point out the complaint’s lack of probable cause. 
  • Indicted personnel are consultants. The allegation largely focuses on the appointment of foreign nationals in the management of GMCAC, which is considered a public utility. Megawide insists that the indicted personnel are merely consultants or advisors and are not regular employees. The burden of proof lies with the accuser (i.e. National Bureau of Investigation and DOJ) and the company believes it will be difficult to prove that the indicted foreign officers have been involved in the management of the airport. Formal documents (visa, DOLE, PSE, etc) show that these foreign officers are only “advisors” or consultants.
  • Worst case scenario: arbitration. In the event that the court could decide against GMCAC on this alleged violation and order a forfeiture of its franchise, management believes it could raise the matter to international arbitration, given that this particular issue was not included as one of the grounds for contract termination. 
  • Passage of Public Service Act amendments will make any case “moot and academic”. The proposed amendments to the Public Service Act includes the removal of foreign ownership limits of airports. The passage of the bill, therefore, will automatically make the case “moot and academic”. The bill has passed the Lower House but remains pending at the Senate.

Airline capacity survey

Jefferies just published their latest airline capacity survey. Cebu Pacific’s weekly seat capacity in the week-ending November 1 surged to 52k, +54% higher than its 10-week rolling average and the biggest spike we’ve seen so far since the start of the pandemic.  While it’s still at 44% of pre-Covid levels (CEB likely very close to EBITDA break-even that week), Boracay has indicated to remove swab tests as a pre-travel requirement this month and hopefully more will follow.

MBT 9M NPAT P16.1bn, +46%; NPLs drop to 2.1%, cover jumps to 191%

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 9M21 net profit of P16.1bnn, up 46% YoY. For 3Q21 alone, NPAT was P4.4bn, up 131% YoY. The results are tracking behind consensus (P22.4bn for FY21E). And while the headline profit is well ahead of our forecasts, they are actually behind on PPOP, due largely to weak trading/fx gains. The focus, though, should be on asset quality – MBT’s NPL ratio fell to 2.1% (from 2.3% in 2Q), and NPL coverage jumped to 191% (from 179%), and restructured loans (RL) were steady at 0.8%. These are head and shoulders better than industry average, and the best among the Big Three banks.

Some highlights

  • Loans were still -4.2% YoY, though much better than the -13% in 2Q ad -19% in 1Q. On a QoQ basis, loans were already up 1.2%, driven by corporates and credit cards. Deposits grew 7% YoY, with CASA up 13%. This took CASA ratio to 74.9%.
  • NIMs were flat QoQ (+3bps) to 3.21%, as loan yields began to stabilize. Deposit costs were steady at 0.30%, given the system’s very high liquidity.
  • Fees and other income were up 20% YoY, though they seem to be slowing a bit. 3Q alone was up 15% YoY.
  • Opex was flat, up just 1% YoY
  • Provision expense fell 72% to P10bn, with 3Q alone down to P3.0bn. NPL ratio fell to 2.1% from 2.3% in 2Q21. Meanwhile, NPL coverage jumped to 191% from 179% in 2Q21. Restructured loans (RL) stood at just 0.8% of total loans.
BLOOM net loss narrows to -P1.1bn in 3Q21, EBITDA down 9%QoQ despite strong uptick in GGR

Bloomberry’s net loss narrowed to -P1.1bn in 3Q21 from -P1.2bn in 2Q21 despite the reduced operating days from the lockdown in Aug. EBITDA declined 9%QoQ. 

The 9M net loss and EBITDA is tracking ahead of our FY estimates. GGR was up 25%QoQ (Mass table GGR -4%QoQ, Slot machine GGR +42%QoQ, VIP GGR +72%QoQ). 

Solare was closed to the public for 41 out 92 days in the quarter. Cash stood at P24.4bn at the end of 3Q21, a mild increase from P22.3bn at the end of 2Q21.

Pace of vaccinations improves further

As of October 31, the 7-day average vaccinations figure rose to 514,439/day, from 487,398/day the previous week. The government hopes to increase the pace of vaccinations to 1mn-1.5mn/day. 25% of the Philippine population (27.4mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of October 25, 62% of the Metro Manila population (8.4mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 106.2mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 59.3mn doses (as of October 31). The government might administer booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities in November or December 2021. 

Duterte to amend BOT Law’s rules

President Duterte plans to amend the implementing rules of the Build-Operate-Transfer Law to avoid “unwarranted guarantees” in future projects. He has appointed Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua to head the IRR committee tasked to tackle the amendments. NEDA indicated that PPPs with unwarranted guarantees, contingent liabilities and “onerous” contract provisions take up the government’s limited fiscal space. The committee held its first meeting on Oct. 26 and will start stakeholder consultations in December; the Committee plans to publish the amended IRR by 1Q22. 

Mobility pops to new Covid highs on 30 Oct

As we had predicted in our recent “Revenge travel” report, Google mobility data spiked on 30 October, the Friday before the long weekend (Monday, 01 Nov was a holiday). Every major metric we follow was at the highest level since the Covid-19 pandemic began – be it retail/recreation, transit stations, or workplace (which includes the malls, restos, and resorts). Interestingly, even visits to grocery/pharmacies were extremely high (+40% compared to pre-pandemic levels). People were eating out AND grocery shopping for the weekend getaway.

This is very good news as we head into the Christmas holidays, when economic activity tends to peak – including in 2020.

 

We have consistently held – since our first "Glass half-full" note – that the vaccine roll-out would allow for the gradual reopening of the economy. Macro challenges remain – inflation being the most immediate. But we believe the impact of further relaxation of mobility restrictions will be a more powerful macro / earnings driver, at least in the near-term.

Metro Manila eases to Alert Level 2 today

Metro Manila will be placed under a more relaxed Alert Level 2 starting Friday, Nov. 5 until Nov. 21.  Under Alert Level 2, indoor activities will be allowed to operate at 50% capacity, even if unvaccinated.  NCR’s 7-day ave daily cases is down to 625 from its recent peak of 5,900 (mid-September). 

 

Unemployment rate slightly rises in September; Labor force drops MoM

More people lost jobs in September, with the unemployment rate rising to 8.9% from 8.1% the previous month and remains above pre-pandemic level (5.3%).  MECQ was extended in NCR to September 15; weather disturbances also affected agri sector. 

The country’s active workforce also dropped slightly MoM, with the labor force participation rate at 63.3%.  The higher unemployment rate, combined with the lower labor force participation rate, led to a net employment loss of 642,000. Employment increased by 414,000 in the services sector, mostly driven by wholesale and retail trade, tempering the net employment loss in September.
