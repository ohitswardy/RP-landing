REGIS REPORT

20 August 2021

The  Phils market recouped the rebal-related losses it sustained last week and then some.  The PSEi delivered a 4.95% WoW gain to close at 6633.22.

Volumes were light, however,  with foreign MOC’s helping to push the market higher in the first half of the week.

Metro Manila’s (NCR) lockdown status will move a notch lower to MECQ (modified enhanced community quarantine) beginning tomorrow until month-end. The current lockdown classification for NCR is the most restrictive (ECQ or enhanced community quarantine). There seems to be little difference this time between the two lockdown levels in terms of restriction but there will be no cash aid under MECQ. A check on the latest Google mobility data shows the latest imposition of “ECQ” has not resulted in as sharp a drop in mobility vs previous ECQs.

Cases remained elevated, with the new case count clocking in at almost 15,000 last Thursday.  Meanwhile, vaccinations continued though at a slightly decelerated pace equivalent to 475k doses/day (3.3m doses were administered for the week ended Aug 15).  The latest set of data shows the country has now given shots to over 14% of the population. Of that figure, 12% are fully vaccinated.

Gains were widespread, with advances from 90% of the index members.

AGI (+17.8% WoW) to top spot this week, rising along with unit Emperador (+37.7% WoW) which surged following approval from its board of its SGX listing.

Index stock JG Summit (+12.9% WoW) rose, while Globe (+11.5% WoW) also gained significantly.  Globe e-wallet Gcash (~40% owned) is now seeing P300bn worth of transactions in a month on its e-wallet operation. This means it is likely to comfortably exceed P3trn in transactions this year.

The 2Q earnings season wound down this week with the last few results. 

GT Capital (+0.4% WoW) reported a QoQ drop in profits, as the safeguard duty caused a reduction in car sales.   First Gen (+6.7% WoW) saw an earnings uplift (+10%) thanks to high electricity spot prices. 

Filinvest Land (+0.9% WoW) however had a weak 2Q.  Rental revenue fell 38% YoY and office occupancy fell to 70% from 91% a year ago (76% in 1Q21).

Security Bank (-2.65% WoW) meanwhile came in behind consensus forecasts.   Operational trends are encouraging, with NII up 4% QoQ, NIMs stabilizing, and fee incomes staying firm. However, NPLs jumped 50bps QoQ to 3.93%, while NPL cover dropped to 103% (from 118% in 1Q21).

AC Energy Corp. - Greater than the sum of its parts?
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Initiating with a Hold rating

Amidst a global scramble for sustainable investment, AC Energy Corp (ACEN PM) presents investors with a unique, high-growth renewable energy play in the Philippines. It is among the few, if not the only boat to benefit from the rising RE tide in the Philippines where listed power generators are heavily reliant on fossil fuel. However, at 47.0x PE and 23.3x EV/EBITDA for FY22E, its multiples are daunting. It will have to execute its immediate pipeline flawlessly for any further re-rating to take place. We initiate coverage with a Hold rating and a price target of P7.80/sh.

At $3.2mn/MW, sustainability comes at a price

ACEN’s attributable portfolio totals 2,100MW (1,500MW renewable). Including projects in the pipeline, attributable capacity is expected to rise to 3,800MW by 2023/2024, with about 3,000MW from RE. On 3,800MW, ACEN’s market capitalization implies a valuation of $3.2mn EV per MW of capacity (installed + immediate pipeline), a premium to Philippine peers and at the high end of international comps.

ACEN – a transformation year

ACEN raised a war chest of close to P30bn from the transactions this year, namely the 1) stock rights offering, 2) GIC investment and 3) follow-on offering. The infusion of its parent company’s international assets & pipeline into the listco completes its formal transformation into the listed energy platform of Ayala Corporation. When completed, AC (through ACEIC) would own 64.7% of ACEN’s enlarged capital of 38.2bn common shares.

Philippine power supply to remain tight despite demand reset

Despite depressed demand brought about by the pandemic, the supply situation in Luzon has remained remarkably tight. Yellow and red alerts have been triggered over the last months, brought about by plant outages and more delays in the much anticipated 1,300MW GNP Dinginin plant. Amidst a mounting supply pipeline, we note that planned projects are almost always delayed by at least 2 years. Recent announcements have mostly been RE plants, which means average availability will be even lower relative to the headline capacity.

Valuation and risks

We peg our target price at P7.80/share, based on peer valuation using 20x EV/EBITDA on FY22E estimates, putting it within range of international comps. At this TP, ACEN would trade at 40.7x FY22E PE. The implied EV/MW of capacity (installed and pipeline) of $2.8m/mw at this TP is more than triple the actual cost of its current projects and at the higher end of comps. With more than half of capacity under construction, successful execution is thus paramount. Other risks stem from regulation, merchant exposure and developments in technology.

Philippine Cement - Demand improves in 2Q, but outlook remains challenging
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Demand, earnings have peaked in 2Q21

Cement demand was buoyant in 2Q21 but despite this, competition was still intense and EBITDA for two of the three listed cement companies remained below pre-pandemic levels. With input costs rising and demand likely to remain subdued, we believe the near-term prospects for the industry still look dim.

Strong demand in 2Q21 across players; ASPs still below pre-pandemic levels

We estimate industry cement sale volumes surged c. 40% QoQ, with most players benefiting from favorable weather and catch-up spending (see Figure 3). Industry cement demand within 2Q21 alone exceeded pre-pandemic levels, though we estimate only Eagle Cement (EAGLE PM) was able to make the most of this amongst listed players. We estimate sales volumes of independent traders are also at record levels, implying further market share gains. Despite the demand surge, we estimate ASPs are still c. 10% below pre-pandemic levels, albeit improving QoQ. Except for EAGLE, revenues of listed cement companies remain below levels before 2020

But near-to medium-term outlook worsening

The government’s infra spend budget for the remainder of the year is lower HoH and YoY. We reckon this signals a likely substantial moderation in cement demand growth YoY or even a drop in demand for the balance of 2021. With rising execution risks brought about by the Supreme Court “Mandanas ruling”, which mandates higher revenue transfers to local governments, we are not optimistic about a substantial boost in infra spending in the run-up to the 2022 national elections. Meanwhile, we believe the real estate market is also unlikely to pick up the slack.

Industry banking on increased regulation on imports

There is little progress on the ongoing proposal to impose an anti-dumping duty on cement imports. Meanwhile, cement traders recently filed a petition before the Court of Tax Appeals urging the tax court to void the three-year safeguard duty on cement imports. While we believe risks of a premature removal of the safeguard duty remain low, we nonetheless believe the industry will need more regulation on imports immediately to cushion the negative impact of slowing demand and rising costs.

Jollibee Foods Corp. - Over the hump; upgrading to Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Upgrading to Buy

We are upgrading JFC to Buy from Hold, as we raise our target price to P230/sh (+24%). The company has likely seen the worst and we are turning more positive on JFC’s longer-term performance given 1) positive revenue trends in most international markets, 2) improving margins resulting from cost management initiatives, and 3) an imminent turnaround in loss-making businesses (e.g. Smashburger and CBTL). These, we believe, set the stage for a more meaningful margin recovery longer term despite short-term Covid-related issues. We expect operations to normalize to pre-Covid levels by 2023E and are forecasting 90% EPS CAGR over 2021E to 2023E. At 32x 2022E PE, the stock is still trading at a discount its historical average and the current average of its global peers.

Over the hump

After a challenging 2020, the recovery rates across key markets have been mixed thus far. Markets with higher vaccination rates and less mobility restrictions (e.g. North America) are seeing sales recover back to pre-pandemic levels. Conversely, markets such as the Philippines and parts of Southeast Asia are seeing a protracted recovery. Despite the divergent top line trends, margins are nearing pre-pandemic levels due to the cost savings from 1) the business transformation initiative, and 2) narrowing losses for Smashburger and CBTL.

Operating leverage to boost margins as top line recovers

We believe the top line recovery in the Philippines is one of the key catalysts for earnings growth, given that it remains to be JFC’s largest and most profitable market. While we have made cuts (-5-36%) to our 2021-22E net profit estimates because of a delay in revenue recovery, we are also raising our longer-term estimates (2023E and beyond) by 7-10%. Positive developments (2020 business transformation and a turnaround in loss-making businesses) have set the stage for a margin rebound despite the current pandemic environment. We expect the margin improvement to be sustained beyond it.

The worst appears to be over; upgrading to Buy

While we expect near term performance to remain challenging, we believe trends going into 2022 are looking positive with vaccination rates increasing, and a push from election spending in the Philippines. The worst appears over, and we are upgrading the stock to a Buy on a revised DCF-based target price of P230/sh (+24%). Our target price implies 2022E multiples of 38x PE and 13x EV/EBITDA, broadly in line with its historical average. Key downside risks mainly relate to a new surge in cases, which could lead to imposition of lockdowns anew.

Philippine Property - Deterioration across the board in 2Q; near-term outlook remains tepid
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Operations of every major segment worsen in 2Q21

The tighter restrictions were a major roadblock for the property sector in 2Q21 and the recent surge in COVID cases (as well as the related hard lockdown in August) will likely delay a recovery. With only 12% of the nationwide population (c. 30% of Metro Manila) fully inoculated against COVID, more vaccinations would be necessary to boost mall foot traffic, residential sales, and office demand.

Office vacancy climbs further; POGOs closing, BPOs waiting to expand

Colliers said that Metro Manila office vacancy climbed to 13% in 2Q21 (from 11% in 1Q21). The increase was driven by POGO closures as well as new supply. Colliers estimates that rental rates fell another 2-5% QoQ. Rents in most areas are now 15%-25% lower than 2019 levels. In the Manila Bay area, which has a high concentration of POGOs, rental rates are 30%-40% lower than 2019 levels. Even as more supply is being added to the market, BPOs are holding off from new leases until conditions allow more employees to come to work. As a result, Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to worsen further to 16% by end[1]2021 (comparable to REGIS forecast of 15%).

Mall revenue stumbles

The hard lockdown in 2Q21 put a stop to three consecutive quarters of mall revenue recovery. Aggregate mall revenue fell 17% QoQ (+48% YoY), 61% below the 2Q19 figure. The new lockdown in August will likely keep any uptick muted in 3Q21. Nevertheless, we remain optimistic about a more meaningful recovery by early 2022, when a larger portion of the population is vaccinated, particularly in Metro Manila.

Manila condo prices still dropping, NPLs still rising

After three consecutive quarters of increases, aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover also fell 24% QoQ to P78.6bn, 37% below the 2019 quarterly average. Similar to previous quarters, horizontal projects (house and lot or lot only) in provincial areas sold more briskly than Metro Manila condos. Our channel checks show that offer prices for Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) are down slightly QoQ (within 5%). We estimate prices are now 10%-25% below 2019 levels, with some units being offered at prices that are 25%-35% lower. In the primary market, some units are priced up to 10% lower than 2019 levels (due to discounts). Meanwhile, the banking industry mortgage NPL ratio continued to rise (from 9.2% in 1Q21) to 9.4% in 1Q21, a 15-year high. Incomes still appear fragile and this could have implications on demand.

Filinvest Land - 2Q21 office vacancy jumps to 30%, office revenue falls 46% YoY
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Focus on the office segment

Despite a sizeable tax benefit due to the recently passed CREATE law, 1H21 net profit fell 21% to P1.8bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. While the disappointment was largely due to weaker-than-expected residential revenue (brought about by insufficient construction activity), we believe investors should focus on the jump in office vacancy.

Office revenue falls 46% due to POGO closures; difficult to find new tenants

2Q21 office revenue fell 46% YoY (-33% QoQ) to P963mn. The rise in vacancy for FLI’s entire 520,000sqm office portfolio to 30% (vs. 9% in 2Q20 and 24% in 1Q21) was almost entirely due to POGO closures. The impact to rental revenue was disproportionately greater because POGOs paid nearly double the rental rate of non-POGO tenants. We believe it will be difficult for Filinvest to lease the vacated space to BPOs because nearly all of the space vacated was not PEZA-accredited.

P12bn raised from REIT IPO

FLI raised c. P12bn from the sale of secondary shares in Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM). This should bring FLI’s net debt down by 18% (from P66bn in 2Q21). The IPO was priced at P7.00/share but the shares are currently trading at P7.40/share, implying a 2022 dividend yield of 6.2% (based on the prospectus), which is a higher valuation than our assigned 7% cap rate. Vacancy of FILRT’s 300,000sqm office portfolio rose modestly to 11% (from 9% a year ago). However, FLI said that it plans to eventually infuse more buildings into FILRT. Since FLI’s non-REIT offices currently have an average vacancy of c. 45%, FILRT’s vacancy will likely increase in the future.

Reservation sales jump YoY on provincial house and lot projects

2Q21 presales jumped 44% YoY to P3.6bn, 74% of the 2019 quarterly average, thanks to house and lot projects in provincial areas. However, 1H21 presales of P7.0bn (-4%) are still trending below our forecast.

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1H21 NPAT of P3.1bn, down 45% YoY. NPAT was ahead of our FY forecasts, though PPOP was in line to slightly behind. Operational trends are encouraging, with NII up 4% QoQ, NIMs stabilizing, and fee incomes staying firm. However, NPLs jumped 50bps QoQ to 3.93%, while NPL cover dropped to 103% (from 118% in 1Q21). We suspect the consumer loan portfolio accounted for the deteriorating asset quality.

 

Some highlights:

Loans contracted 5.4% YoY (gross loans -4%), and -1.2% QoQ. Consumer loans were down 12% YoY, but were likely already up QoQ. Consumer loans made up 26% of total loans, up slightly from 25% in 1Q (peak was 29% at end-2019). Deposits were up 2% YoY and +0.7% QoQ. NIMs were flat QoQ at 4.07%, but still down 24bps YoY. Loan yields are also flattening out (-7bps QoQ), while deposit costs continued to drop 23bps QoQ to 0.48%. NIMs also benefitted from the maturity of higher-yielding bonds. But the data suggests that maybe businesses and households have adapted, and that there is growing acceptance that the periodic lockdowns are a “new normal”. Maybe it’s partly because this time around, the government telegraphed the ECQ ahead of time. Recall that in April this year, the pre-Easter announcement took everyone by surprise. In any case, the initial drops in mobility (highlighted in yellow) this time seem much shallower than the earlier ECQs.

Partial closure of China port to affect Philippines

The partial closure of the Ningbo-Zhoushan port  would “definitely impact” the Philippines’ supply chain according to Philippine Ports Authority general manager Jay Santiago. However, once normal operations resume, there will likely be an increased volume of inbound cargoes from China. Our recent experience with the long shutdown of Shenzhen was that you will get a drop in cargo and then a sudden surge after opening according to ICTSI Executive Vice President Christian Gonzales. Ningbo and Shenzen send a comparable amount of ships and cargo to the Philippines, we see a drop in China shipments the next few weeks and then a surge after.

Government cuts GDP growth target

The government slashed its economic growth target to 4-5% for this year from 6-7% previously. The government cited mobility restrictions in Metro Manila and other parts of the country continue to crimp economic activity. It nonetheless kept its 2022 GDP target of +7% to +9% for 2022.

365,000 Pfizer doses arrive

Around 365,000 doses of Pfizer COVID vaccine arrived. The country expects 22.7mn doses in August, including the 9.1mn that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 42.9mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 28.3mn doses (as of August 16). The government aims to vaccinate 58 Filipinos by end-2021.

Car sales rise 5% YoY in July

Data from CAMPI/TMA showed the industry sold 21,499 units in July, up 5% YoY (-5% MoM). In mid-July, the Tariff Commission rejected the imposition of safeguard duty on imported vehicles. The DTI ordered the removal of the duty on Aug 11. The re-imposition of lockdown measures in NCR could slow sales in August. This number also brings YT-July industry sales up 46% YoY to 154,266 units. Toyota sold 10,763 units in July, up 22% YoY but was down 4% MoM (Market share steady at 50%). YT-July sales up 68% YoY to 74,541 units, tracking ahead of FY21E (117k). GTCAP is trading at a 14% discount to its market-based NAV of P614/sh.

Metro Manila, Laguna shifts to MECQ until August 31

President Duterte has approved placing Metro Manila and Laguna under Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine from August 21 to 31; Bataan under MECQ from Aug 23-31. Dine-in services and personal care services like beauty salons and barbershops will not be allowed.

Philippines receives 3mn more Sinovac doses

Around 3mn doses of Sinovac COVID vaccine arrived recently. The country expects 22.7mn doses in August, including the 12.1mn that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 45.9mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 29.1mn doses (as of August 18). The government aims to vaccinate 58 Filipinos by end-2021.
