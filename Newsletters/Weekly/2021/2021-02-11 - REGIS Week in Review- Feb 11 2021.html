Philippine Strategy & Consumer - Inflation puts consumption recovery at risk
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Inflation may be “transitory”, but still hurts consumers’ wallets

CPI jumped 4.2% in January, the highest since early 2019, and are likely to stay elevated. Rice has been benign so far, but regional prices are spiking. The BSP deems inflation “transitory” and will not likely alter its monetary stance. But as we warned last week, inflation could stall the recovery in an economy that is c70% consumption. At the Consumer/Retail sector level, restaurants could bear the brunt of weaker demand and rising input prices. The effect on retailers’ SSSG would be mostly neutral to positive. Companies that might benefit include Century Food, San Miguel F&B, and Emperador.

CPI jumped 4.2% YoY; BSP cites “transitory” nature, likely to stay loose

Inflation accelerated to 4.2% in January (Fig 1) from 3.5% in December driven by food (6.2%), specifically meats (17.1%), vegetables (21.2%), and transportation (8.6%). The Bangko Sentral, expectedly, noted that while the print was above its 2-4% target band, the food price hike is “transitory” due to the African Swine Fever, and recent typhoons. Real interest rates are now deep in negative territory, with the BSP policy rate at 2.0%, and the 10-year BVAL at around 3.0% (Fig 2) The Monetary Board meets on Thursday, 11 February; we do not expect a change to its monetary stance, as inflation is not driven by domestic demand.

High food prices – especially rice – could further stall economic recovery

Demand remains soft, given the pandemic and continued mobility restrictions. Food inflation could further set back the recovery in an economy that is c70% consumption. Domestic rice prices (9.6% of the CPI) are still tame (+0.1% YoY in January). But Thailand and Vietnam export prices are rising sharply (Figs 3, 4). The Philippine government is trying to stave off food inflation, imposing price caps on pork and chicken. The Department of Agriculture wants to reduce the tariff on rice imported from countries outside ASEAN, most notably India, where export prices are still around 25% cheaper than Thailand.

Consumer /Retail – winners and losers

The impact on retailers will most likely be neutral to positive (Figs 6, 7), as consumers spend the same PHP amount, but on cheaper alternatives. Mid- to high-end consumers be less affected, which plays into Wilcon (WLCON PM), our top pick in the retail segment. For food manufacturers, San Miguel (FB PM) benefits from high chicken and pork prices. Century (CNPF PM) could also gain from trading down to canned food; also, tuna prices are down YoY. Universal Robina (URC PM) could be neutral, as it has a natural hedge with its commodities business. Liquor also tends to benefit from trading down – more bang for your buck – which could be positive for Emperador (EMP PM). Depressed demand and rising input prices will likely be most negative for restaurant operators such as Jollibee (JFC PM), Max’s (MAXS PM), and Shakey’s (PIZZA PM). LT Group - 4Q cigarette volumes fall 19%QoQ after Oct price increase
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Volume contraction worse than expected

Philip Morris International (PMI) disclosed that cigarette volumes in the Philippines (PMFTC, a unit of LTG) amounted to 41.7bn sticks (-16%YoY) in FY20, coming in below forecast of 43.4bn (-12.9%YoY). 4Q20 volumes fell to 9.5bn sticks (-19%QoQ, -24%YoY), following price adjustments that saw truck prices jump 20-29% at the beginning of October. Despite volumes contracting more than expected, we think the uplift in margin could have compensated for the shortfall.

Truck prices increased by 20-29% per pack in October

Last October 5, 2020, PMFTC increased the truck prices of Marlboro to P115.80/pack (+20%), Fortune to P106.50/pack (+22%) and Jackpot to P87.40/pack (+29%). On a per stick basis, the adjustments translated to a 17- 25% increase from the high-end brand (Marlboro) to the low-end brand (Jackpot). PMI’s disclosure explained that the dip in volumes mainly reflected the lower market share for mid-priced brands Fortune and Hope, but the net impact to revenues was partially offset by “a favorable pricing variance”. We take this to mean a better revenue mix, in favor of the higher price/margin Marlboro products.

Industry posts lower contraction

The industry fell to 15.0bn sticks in 4Q (-14%QoQ, -16%YoY), contracting less than PMFTC. PMFTC’s unit market share has been declining since 3Q19, with the most recent print showing a 3.5 percentage point loss in market share in 4Q alone. The volume decline may prompt a rethink of its pricing strategy, after two years or raising prices by more than 20% p.a. We forecast that the price increases beginning 2022 (P7-10/pack/year) will be closer to the actual tax increases (P3- 5/pack/year).

Dividend outlook intact

The recent volume numbers should not materially affect our dividend outlook for LTG. Our forecast of a 5.5% dividend yield for FY21E is premised on LTG paying out just P8.9bn of the P17-19bn in cash it is expected to receive from PMFTC. Our forecasts assume PMFTC profits of P33.4bn for FY20 (+9%YoY) and P37.4bn (+12%YoY). ore detailed sector comments from page 3.

Philippine Property - Congress passing new law reducing POGO taxes
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A reprieve for POGOs?

The House of Representatives approved on 3 rd reading a bill that would lower Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) taxes from 5% of bets/turnover to 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR). GGR is typically only a fraction of bets. The bill will be transmitted to the Senate, and if approved there, would cut taxes to a more economically rational level, and possibly slow down the exodus of POGOs.

Why is Congress passing a new law?

The Bayanihan II Act, which became law in September 2020, had a provision which imposed franchise taxes on POGOs, equivalent to 5% of bets/turnover. However, the POGOs contested this, citing (among other things) that the law unfairly uses bets as the “tax base” even though that amount includes money that will eventually be paid out to gamblers. Following the Supreme Court’s (SC) issuance of a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) on the collection of these taxes, the congressmen likely took the view that the SC will eventually rule in favor of the POGOs.

Will this stop POGO closures?

We believe that over 80% of POGO operations would have eventually shut down if the 5% tax on bets were enforced. Thus, franchise taxes based on GGR would be a substantial reprieve for the sector. The speed with which the House of Representatives passed the bill on third reading (so soon after the TRO was issued) likely indicates what is at stake for the POGO sector. However, we also refrain from being overly bullish from this new development since there are still some regulatory headwinds (such as travel bans), including those from the Chinese government. In addition, our channel checks suggest that some POGO operations are leaving Metro Manila office spaces in favor of provincial areas, putting downward pressure on Metro Manila property prices/rents. As of 2019, we estimate that POGOs occupied 1.3m sqm (11%) of Metro Manila office stock and employed over 300,000 Chinese nationals. However, they vacated about 180,000sqm of Metro Manila office space in 2020.

Filinvest Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime more exposed

Filinvest Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime are more exposed to the POGO sector. POGOs occupy about 15% of FLI’s office space and 10% of MEG’s office portfolio. The Manila Bay area also comprised c. 25% of MEG’s 2019 residential launches. Meanwhile, although POGOs have little direct impact on SMPH’s earnings, c. 45% of NAV is comprised of land in the Manila Bay area, the POGOs’ preferred location.

Philippine Banks - Dec’20 NPL ratio drops 18bps MoM, likely on write-offs; RLs spike 60bps
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Still “Flying blind” – write-offs, restructurings, and moratoriums

The headline NPL ratio for Philippine banks came in at 3.70% for December, down 18bps MoM. However, we believe the “improvement” was largely cosmetic, the result of large write-offs, particularly of credit card receivables. New NPL formation was also fended off by aggressive restructurings – which spiked 60bps MoM to 1.91% of loans - and the Bayanihan 2 loan grace period. To the upside, NPL cover jumped in December, suggesting banks conservatively put though significant provisions in 4Q20.

Headline NPLs down 18bps MoM to 3.70% in December, likely on write-offs

The reported NPL ratio (ex-interbank) of the Philippine Banking Sector was 3.70% in December, 18bps lower than the 3.88% in November (Figure 1). However, we believe the improvement was at least partly due to large write-offs, particularly in the unsecured portion of the book, including credit cards and personal loans. This is consistent with BPI’s earnings call, where management explained that the 30bps QoQ drop in their NPL ratio was due in part to writeoffs. We also noted that SECB’s 4Q20 Statement of Condition (SOC) had NPLs 13bps lower QoQ; we confirmed with management that this was also due to write-offs. Frustratingly, Philippine banks do not disclose new NPL formation data, and only show period-end balances, net of write-offs and write-backs.

 

Restructured loans spike c50% MoM, taking RL ratio up 60bps to 1.91%

Perhaps more indicative of continued asset quality deterioration, banks’ restructured loan (RL) ratio jumped 60bps MoM to 1.91% in December (Figure 3). In nominal terms, RLs spiked 49.4% from P138.7bn in November to P207.3bn in December. This took the “stressed loan” ratio (NPL + RL) to 5.61% in December from 5.18% in November (Figure 4).

Staying conservative – NPL cover, LLR up; provisions likely still high in 4Q

To the upside, banks remained conservative, as NPL coverage ratios improved to 94% from 88% the previous month (Figure 5), suggesting banks continued to conservatively book large provision expenses in 4Q20. Despite the likely writeoffs, loan loss reserve (LLR) ratios improved slightly to 3.38% (Figure 6). Credit standards stayed tight too, with loans contracting 1.6% YoY in December, the first annual decline in at least 15 years (Figure 4).

Still wary over asset quality, on sluggish economic recovery and inflation

We remain wary of asset quality. Expect NPLs to rise in 1Q21 as the new grace periods under the Bayanihan 2 Act runs off. Furthermore, as we have written in our recent Strategy pieces, the economic recovery has been much more sluggish than we expected. And with inflation risk now creeping in, we could see further stress not only on households, but also on businesses that might see margins squeeze from rising input prices.

Globe Telecom - 4Q20 results: No holiday cheer
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY20 core income short of estimates at P19.5n (-13% YoY)

Globe’s FY20 headline income came in at P18.6bn, down 16% YoY. Profits for the year included large one-offs, among them a write-down of 3G and copper assets and gains from the dilution of its equity interest in Mynt. Excluding the exceptional items, core income was P19.5bn, -13% YoY, and ~10% below our expectations and the Street’s. The profit disappointment is attributable largely to a shortfall in revenue, which did not see the typical lift in 4Q. For the year, service revenue dipped 2%.

Weakness into 4Q

The revenue numbers showed little of the usual bump from 4Q holidays, as incomes and mobility remain affected and savings dwindle without government handouts. Mobile revenue was still down 12% YoY in 4Q. 4Q home broadband revenue was up (+3% QoQ, +28% YoY) but was disappointing relative to the subscriber adds (+9% QoQ and +88% YoY).

Capex guidance of P70bn; More efforts into home broadband

Globe plans to spend P70bn for capex in FY21. This coupled with its dividend of P27/quarter could push its net gearing up further to ~2x. More resources are now being poured into home broadband, as Globe plays catch-up on a segment that it neglected. It added 600k FTTH (fiber) lines last year and will add 1m more this year. For perspective, its end-FY20 subscriber base for wired broadband was only 739k, of which only 30% were on fiber.

Mynt valuation now at ~US$300m

Globe said it registered one-off gains of P2.4bn, largely from the entry of a new investor in Mynt, which holds GCash. We estimate the marked-to-market valuation of Globe’s interest in Mynt would now be ~US$300m (5% of Globe market cap).

 
 

Corporate News

Key takeaways from Solar Philippines Founder Leandro Leviste

Panel, battery costs are down substantially; will continue to drop by c.10% annually. The cost of building a solar plant was $1.5mn/MW as recently as 5 years ago. Today, Leviste said that the capex for a solar panels are down roughly a third – at $400-500k/MW and continue to drop. Battery costs are also down from $1mn/MW to around $150k/MW. At these prices, he says that selling at P3.0-3.5/kwh is already viable. We estimate that at these levels, a plant can generate a gross margin of over 30%. Leviste added that the continued decline of battery cost could spell the end for gas plants. The main constraints are availability of land and interconnection. He estimates that every MW of solar will require 0.7 hectares of land. On top of site scarcity, the connection to the grid is dependent on NGCP’s own investment plans and timetable. Although Leviste thinks there could be as much 10,000MW of solar capacity installed by 2025, we think hitting this capacity will take more time due to said constraints. Partnership with Razon, Ayala groups. Other than being one of the first movers in the space, Leviste thinks that site availability is what they bring to their partnerships with Razon and Ayala Groups. To this end, Solar Philippines is in the process of securing 10k hectares of land this year, and another 10k hectares over the next few years to be able to accommodate most of expected solar expansion in the country. He hinted that the partnership with these two groups are not confined to just the recent projects announced, and talks are ongoing for future ventures. We initially thought the congressional franchise of Leviste’s Solar Para sa Bayan was a key factor in these partnerships. It sounded, however, that the said franchise is meant for an entirely separate initiative.

Higher ATM fees loom

Philippine banks are set to implement the acquirer-based fees for ATM transactions starting April wherein the fees charged to a cardholder for a withdrawal transaction or balance inquiry will be based on the fees of the other banks or ATM owners. BPI said that its clients can continue to use their BPI debit cards on BPI ATMs at no cost. The fees would be charged if clients use local non-BPI ATMS via BancNet. Effective April 7, BPI will charge other bank’s cardholders using BPI ATMs P18 for every cash withdrawal, 20% higher than the current charge of P15 per transaction. Under the previous issuer-based method, the fee is set and charged by the issuing bank and the amount charged to the cardholder remains the same regardless of who the ATM acquiring bank is. Electronic cash transfers via PESONet surged by 376% to 15.3mn in terms of volume and 188% to P951.6bn in terms of value. Meanwhile, volume of low value fund transfers via InstaPay rose by 459% to 86.7mn while the value surged 340% to P463.4bn.

Globe 4Q/FY20 – initial comments

Results were disappointing as revenue did not see the usual sequential lift in 4Q.  FY20 core income of P19.5bn was down 13% YoY and 10% short of estimates. Mobile revenue was still down double digits (-12% YoY) in 4Q. Mobile data usage grew 15% QoQ in 4Q but revenue yield fell to P24.5/per GB (-8% QoQ). The confinement of people indoors/at home is having a bigger impact on Globe, I suspect than PLDT. Home broadband is growing but the pace is a disappointment considering the number of subscribers being added. 4Q revenue was up only 3% QoQ despite 9% increase in subs spread over both wired and fixed wireless services. Dividend of P27/share for 1Q21 was declared. This implies P108/share for FY21, which is just in line with our estimate (P110/share) and is ~5% yield. Meanwhile, capex guidance of P70bn for FY21, from P60bn spent in FY20.

McDonald’s closes 30 stores

McDonald’s Philippines said it shut 30 stores and opened 16 new ones. This brought the store count to 655 at the end of 2020. The company averaged a net opening of 50 stores/year from FY17-FY19.

ICTSI union leader Leonardo Escala shot dead in Manila

The president of ICTSI’s labor union, Leonardo Escala, was shot dead on Sunday evening. Mr. Escala had received death threats in relation to his work as ICTSI union president. Escala had served as president of the union for two terms since 2012, and 2nd vice president of the union for 4 years.

AC Energy, Citicore to develop 50MW Pampanga solar farm

AC Energy Corp and its unit ACE Endevor, Ink. Incked a shareholders agreement with a subsidiary of Citicore Power, Inc. to build a 50MW photovoltaic solar power plant in Arayat and Mexico Pampanga. In a regulatory filing on Friday, AC Energy said that the solar facility is scheduled to go online in November 2021. Under the agreement, Citicore Solar Energy Corp will have 50% of the shares in the solar project’s special purpose vehicle Greencore Power Solutions 3. Meanwhile, AC Energy and ACE Endevor will hold 45% and 5% respectively. AC Energy would be extending a loan of up to P2.675bn to Greencore to finance the design, engineering, construction, O&M of the 50MW facility.

PMFTC cigarette volumes fall -19%QoQ in 4Q following price increase

Philip Morris International disclosed the other day that cigarette volumes in the Philippines (PMFTC) for FY20 came in at 41.7bn sticks, down 16.1% YoY – below our FY20E forecast of 43.4bn (-12.9% YoY). 4Q cigarette volumes fell to P9.5bn sticks (-19% QoQ, -24% YoY) with the company saying, “Mainly reflecting the lower market share for mid-price Fortune” and “Lower cigarette volume partially offset by a favorable pricing variance”. The decline reflects the impact of the price hikes implemented October 5 which was +P20/pack prince increase across brands (+20-29%). Industry volumes contracted less than PMFTC (-12% YoY in FY20, -14% QoQ in 4Q). The unit market share of PMFTC dipped to 63.3%. Our forecasts have PMFTC volumes declining -13% in FY20 to 43.4bn sticks and -14% in FY21 to 37.2bn sticks. PMFTC profit forecasts - FY20 P33.4bn (+9% YoY), FY21 P37.4bn (+12% YoY). LTG profit forecasts – FY20 P23.8bn (-5% YoY), FY21 P24.2bn (+2% YoY).

Converge doubles fiber network in 2020

Macro News

Inflation spikes to 4.2% in January

Headline inflation surged to 4.2% in January, the fastest since January 2019 (4.4%). It surpassed the government’s target range of 2-4% and street estimates (3.5%). The spikes in food prices, particularly meat, vegetables, and fruits, were partly due to the lingering effects of typhoons, as well as the African Swine Flu. In NCR alone, price of fresh pork with bones and pure meat rose by 68% and 77% YoY, respectively. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) said the breach of its inflation target still does not warrant a quick action in monetary policy. The average inflation would still fall within its target range this year as the projected upturn in the first half is seen as temporary. The BSP chief pointed out supply-side shocks are best addressed by non-monetary interventions.

House proposes P420bn for Bayanihan III stimulus

Legislators at the House of Representatives proposed a third stimulus bill worth P420bn. The Bill will be known as “Bayanihan III”. The stimulus packages under Bayanihan I amounted to P275bn while Bayanihan II amounted to P165bn. Bayanihan III’s economic package includes: P108bn for social amelioration program assistance, P100bn for capacity-building among establishments in critically-impacted industries, P70bn in capacity-building for workers in the agri sector, P52bn for subsidies to help small businesses, P30bn for the unemployed; P30bn for internet expenses; P5bn for the rehab of typhoon-hit areas. So far, 115 members of major political parties and power blocs comprising supermajority have expressed support for the bill.

Gov’t eyes P1tn in foreign loans for 2021

The Philippine government is planning to borrow $23.71bn (P1.14tn) from foreign lenders this year to plug the deficit. This will be higher than the US$17.06bn (P820bn) borrowings from external sources in 2020. US$7.67bn (P369bn) will be sourced from multilateral lenders, US$10.54bn (P507bn) from bilateral partners; US$5.5bn (P264bn) from commercial markets. Around 66% or US$15.65bn of the loans will be in the form of project financing, while 34% or US$8.06bn will support the national budget. In 2020, 85% of US$14.52bn was used for budget support while the rest were in project loans. The government expects a budget deficit of P1.78tn this year, around 8.9% of GDP. In 2020, the budget deficit reached P1.36tn, around 7.6% of GDP.

House of Representatives approves bill on POGO taxes

The House of Representatives approved on third and final reading a bill on POGO taxes which would be 5% of gross revenue or receipts. Additionally, POGO employees who earn an annual salary of at least P600,000 will pay 25% in income taxes. The bill will be transmitted to the Senate and Joey Salceda, Chairman of the House ways and means committee, estimates that the government can earn P144bn in revenue from the proposed taxes. The Bayanihan 2 Act, passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. We think the new bill could slow down POGO closures.

Metro Manila could reopen further in March

Trade Secretary Ramon Lopez said that Metro Manila might resume gradual reopening next month if COVID cases remain stable. Presidential spokesperson Harry Roque said that President Duterte was worried about the economy. The President held an unscheduled meeting with Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez about the economy.

Hog inventory down 24%, lowest level since 1994

The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that hog inventory as of January 1 fell 24% to 9.72mn heads, the lowest level since 1994.

Colliers: office rents and residential prices drop in 2020

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy rose from 4.3% in 2019 to 9.1% in 2020. The net vacancy was 180,000sqm, largely due to POGO closures. Metro Manila office rental rates fell 17%. For 2021, Colliers forecasts office vacancy to rise to 12.5%, rental rates to fall another 15%. Meanwhile, Metro Manila residential vacancy increased from 11.0% in 2019 to 15.6% in 2020 and residential prices fell 13%. Additionally, Colliers expects residential vacancy to rise to 16.9%.

Government in talks with Manila Water regarding revisions to contract

Justice Secretary Menardo Guevarra said that the government was in talks with Manila Water for revisions to its water concession agreement. The government will have discussions with Maynilad after the revisions to Manila Water’s contract are finalized. Secretary Guevarra previously said that certain provisions had to be removed, particularly the provision mandating non-interference by the state in the rate-setting mechanism of the water firms with MWSS and the provision warranting indemnification for losses in case of such interference.
