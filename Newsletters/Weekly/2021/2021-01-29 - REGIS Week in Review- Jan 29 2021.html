REGIS REPORT

29 January 2021

The Phils got hammered this week, dropping -6.2% WoW, and closed at 6,612, the lowest in 3 months. The PSEi had a theoretical support level between the 6700-6800 level, so it’s interesting to see how the index does after dropping -3.5% on Friday alone and breaking down that support level in one go. Foreign net selling accelerated this week to -$138.1m from -$100.5m the week prior. This was predominantly seen in the latter part of the week, especially on Friday which saw a foreign net outflow of -$65.2m for the day alone. Average daily turnover dropped -13% WoW to $230m as retail investors churned less. Recall, retail-dominated stocks were heavily traded last week and accounted for 7 of the top 10 most active names. These seven names were over 40% of market turnover last week.

Net foreign outflows this week breached US$138m. Bulk of which was in SM Prime which had a net foreign outflow of -$20m. Other top net foreign sold stocks were JG Summit -$12.8m, URC and SM Investments for -$8.6m each. Only two index names saw foreign inflow this week; Bank of the Philippines and Cebu Pacific for +$2m and +$1.6m respectively.

The drop this week was very broad-based, with 27 of the 30 index members closing down for the week. JG Summit was this week’s biggest index loser, dropping -16.7% WoW followed by Robinsons Land, URC and BDO which were all down over -9.4%. Only Alliance Global was up over +1% amongst index names.

4Q GDP came out this week. Philippine GDP contracted by 8.3% YoY in 4Q20, ahead of our -10% forecast for the quarter, but worse than consensus at -7.9%. This took FY20 GDP to -9.5%, the country’s worst full-year contraction on record (since 1981). It will also likely be the worst 4Q/FY20 GDP print among our Asian peers. The domestic demand picture looks weak, with personal consumption still -7.2% YoY in 4Q20, and capital formation crashing -29.0% YoY. The economy got a lift from government spending (+4.4%) and from a sharp drop in imports (-18.8%).

Also, earlier this week, Cebu Air released initial details of its upcoming rights offering, which will involve the issuance of preferred shares instead of the usual, regular, common shares. While final pricing and entitlement ratios have not been disclosed, the preferred shares will be denominated in US$ and will be convertible into commons at P38-45/share, a discount to current market price. This is lower than our assumed conversion price (P53/share) and would nominally mean a greater dilution, considering a separate convertible issuance to third parties (at similar conversion prices).

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Cebu Air - Sets convertible pref rights issue schedule, subscription price
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Rights issue in February. 

Cebu Air (CEB PM) announced that it plans to issue US$250mn worth of convertible preferred shares to existing shareholders through a stock rights offer at an offer price of USD 0.74/sh to USD 0.84/sh. Existing shareholders will be entitled to subscribe to the convertible pref shares before ex-date (February 5). Holders of the convertible prefs will be able to convert these shares into common shares at the indicative price range of P38/sh to P45/sh.

The company will disclose other details (e.g. entitlement ratio, conversion price, dividend rate, and maturity) by February 4.    

Conversion price range at P38/sh to P45/sh, a discount to market. 

The conversion price range is below our assumption (P53/sh) and the current share price of P50/sh. This suggests that CEB’s share count could expand to 1.14bn -1.24bn (vs. base case of 1.06bn) from 601mn shares currently. 

Even though the potential dilution could be larger than our expectations, it is worth noting that existing shareholders will be able to buy the shares cheaply.

Convertible bond/warrants next.

The issuance of convertible prefs is only part of the company’s US$500mn equity raising plan. CEB plans to raise another US$250mn via a private placement of convertible bonds/convertible warrants/convertible bonds + detachable warrants. We understand both convertible instruments will have the same below market conversion price but could have different coupon rates/maturity dates.  We find the discounted issuance of a convertible instrument to a third party investor peculiar, especially as other airlines have had premium-to-market convertible issuances.  The company plans to complete the convertible bond/warrant issuance soon after the rights issue. 

Maintain Buy.

We use a target EV/EBITDA multiple of 6.5x, lower than historical average (7x).  At this target multiple, we estimate CEB’s equity value at P81bn, on a conservative assumption that its FY22E EBITDA is 15% lower than 2019.  This compares with the current market cap of P30bn.

Assuming a fully diluted share count of as much as 1.24bn would put the equity value of CEB at P65/sh, lower than our current price target of P77/sh but still offering a hefty potential upside of 40% from the estimated TERP (~P47/sh assuming conversion price of P38/sh). A stronger recovery than our expectations (e.g. gaining market share from its competitors) could increase the upside potential to c. 200%.

The Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) board has junked the appeal filed by the consortium of Megawide (MWIDE PM) and India-based GMR according to Justice Secretary Menardo I. Guevarra, who also sits in MIAA’s board. MIAA will issue a statement once an official document is released citing the board’s decision on the matter.

Cavite sees ‘infirmities’ in MacroAsia-CCCC’s Sangley submissions

The Cavite government saw “infirmities” in the required documents submitted by the consortium of MacroAsia Corp. (MAC) and China Communications Construction Co. Ltd. (CCCC) for the US$10bn Sangley Point International Airport project. The consortium sought three extensions citing travel restrictions for the delay. The most recent extension has lapsed, and the consortium has failed to submit the required documents. Meanwhile, Cavite Gov. Jonvic Remulla confirmed that there is an unsigned recommendation from the PPP Selection Committee to terminate the project. Gov. Remulla will wait for the signed recommendation before acting.

PMFTC to merge with one of its shareholders

LT Group (LTG PM) disclosed that PMFTC Inc (PMFTC), a subsidiary through Fortune Tobacco Corporation, approved the merger with Philip Morris Manufacturing Philippines Inc. Philip Morris Manufacturing Philippines Inc. is one of PMFTC’s shareholders. PMFTC will be the surviving entity in the merger that will take effect on June 1, 2021. Additionally, LTG clarified that the merger is part of an internal restructuring process. Lastly, LTG’s beneficial ownership in PMFTC will remain at 49.6%.

ACEN to take majority stake in Solar Philippines Central Luzon Corp

AC Energy (ACEN) announced that it will take a majority ownership in Solar Philippines Central Luzon Corp to develop and construct solar projects. SPCLC is a subsidiary of Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings of Leandro L. Leviste. ACEN said it signed a subscription agreement for P619k worth of common shares in SPCLC. Leviste’s Solar Para Sa Bayan Corporation was granted a congressional franchise to construct, install, establish, operate and maintain distributed energy resources and microgrids in the remote and unviable or unserved areas in selected provinces in the Philippines.

Converge expects 55% household coverage by 2025

Converge ICT Solutions CEU Dennis Anthony Uy said that it hopeful to reach its objective of 55% household coverage by 2025 with our fiber network. After passing 1mn subscribers before the end of 2020, the company is hoping to serve around 4-5mn customers in five years. “We are expecting to complete our national loop within the year, which will allow us to offer fixed broadband connection to other regions that remain underserved”.

Ayala Corp Chairman expects economy to recover to pre-pandemic levels in 4Q22

Ayala Corp Chairman Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala said the Philippine economy might not return to pre-pandemic levels until 4Q22. Despite uncertainties in 2021, he cited some positive developments, such as increasing foot traffic in groceries and restaurants as well as the recovery of the retail sector.

BSP approves equity infusion by PNB into holding firm

The BSP has given Philippine National Bank (PNB) the green light to infuse additional equity investment its wholly owned holding firm as part of a plan to dispose of low-earning assets. The bank has completed the required regulatory approvals. PNB acquired an additional 466.77mn shares of PNB Holdings amounting to P46.67 in exchange for certain real estate properties of the listed bank. Meanwhile, PNB’s CEO Jose Arnulfo Veloso earlier said that the transaction would allow the bank to beef up its lending portfolio by P80bn from the sale of low-earnings assets. PNB’s inventory includes the 10-ha PNB Financial Center in Pasay, office buildings in Makati CBD and the foreclosed 8-ha property of Ramon Jacinto.

This will allow mobile subscribers to keep their numbers even when they switch carriers. We expect the impact to be impact to be felt principally on the postpaid subscriber base. Postpaid subscribers account for 3% of Globe’s (GLO PM) mobile subscriber base and 28% of mobile revenue. For PLDT (TEL PM), the proportions are 3% and 20%. Bicam hard-pressed to meet deadline for CREATE

Negotiations between the delegations sent by both chambers of Congress on the passage of the CREATE bill could run over the self-imposed end-January deadline according to Representative Jose María Clemente S. Salceda. Both sides are still haggling on the measure’s fine details but did not specify these. The House has been pressing for a version that contains more incentives for investment in the countryside. Additionally, CREATE seeks to lower corporate income tax to 25% immediately from 30% retroactive to July 2020. Any delays in passage would give companies little time to prepare their taxes under the new rate structure by the April tax filing deadline.

POGO tax collection rises 12%

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collected P7.2bn of taxes from POGOs in 2020 which was 12% higher than 2019. One of the conditions to resume operations after the hard lockdown ended in May 2020 was to pay franchise taxes equivalent to 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR). The Bayanihan 2 Act raised this to 5% of bets/turnover in September. The Supreme Court has since issued a Temporary Restraining Order on the tax. POGOs vacated c. 250,000sqm of Metro Manila office space in 2020. They occupied a total of 1.3m sqm in 2019.

GDP contracts by 8.3% in 4Q20

GDP shrunk by 8.3% in 4Q20, based on preliminary data. This was better than Regis (-10%) estimates and slightly worse than street estimates (-7.9%) but an improvement from 3Q20 (-11.4%); GDP grew +17% QoQ. Domestic demand fell 12.5% YoY, improving from -16.9% in 3Q20 and -21.5% in 2Q20. This brings FY20 GDP down by 9.5% YoY, in line with the government’s forecast range of -8.5% to -9.5%. On the demand side, private consumption remained soft, falling by 7.2% YoY (+20% QoQ) as mobility remained largely restricted, particularly for children. Given that 40% of the population is below 20 years old and 50% of non-essential retail sales are driven by family spending, age-based mobility restrictions suppressed recovery. Investments sustained the recovery, particularly for private and public construction, albeit at a slow pace, given persistent domestic travel restrictions. Meanwhile, gov’t consumption growth continued to moderate after distributing cash aids in 2Q. On the supply side, the services and manufacturing sectors shrunk by 8.4% and 4.3%, respectively, a sustained QoQ improvement. However, natural calamities and the African swine flu led a 2.5% contraction in the agri sector.

Infrastructure spending reaches P681bn in FY20

Infrastructure spending reached P681bn (-35% YoY) in 2020 and P230bn (-40% YoY) in 4Q20 alone. Figures include subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers, which are NOT comparable with the monthly disbursement data. This is equivalent to 3.8% of GDP. Additionally, this surpassed the government’s downgraded target of P609bn for FY20 by 12%. In 4Q20 alone, actual infra spend exceeded the government’s target by 30%. For 2021, the government is targeting to spend P1.12tn (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers), equivalent to 5.9% of GDP. With a multiplier of 2.27, meaning every peso spent creates another P1.27, some 1.7 million jobs can be created to accelerate the recovery. The government expects FY21 GDP growth to be bet. 6.5-7.5%.

S&P sees PH recovery to be among the fastest in the region

S&P sees the Philippine economy rebounding with a growth of 9.6% this year and 7.6% next year. For 2021, the Philippines will be the second fastest growing economy in Asia, next to Vietnam (+10.9%). For 2022, the Philippines will be the fastest growing economy, followed by Vietnam (+6.8%). Regis expects FY21 GDP to grow by +7% (vs. street: FY21E: +7.5%). Meanwhile, the government expects FY21 GDP growth to be bet. 6.5-7.5%.
