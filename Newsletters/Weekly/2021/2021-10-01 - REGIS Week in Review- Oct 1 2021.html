REGIS REPORT

01 October 2021

The Philippines drifted slightly lower this week (-0.4% WoW) with the index consolidating around the 6900 level. Weak global/overnight markets impacted the index though locals were notably buying the market.  

Losers slightly beat the gainers this week 16:13.

The highest decline was seen in Aboitiz Equity (-5.8% WoW)  – with investors selling on the announcement that it was letting go of a  25% stake in subsidiary Aboitiz Power (-3.1%  MoM) to Japanese power firm JERA for US$1.463m. Share prices of both AP and parent had climbed sharply well ahead of the deal announcement. Since the start of August to last week, AP and AEV were each up around 40%.

Also declining were mid-sized conglos MPI (-4.7% WoW) and LT Group (-4.4% WoW), and GT Capital (-2.35% WoW).    Ayala Land (-3.8% WoW) and PLDT (-3.5% WoW) also fell.

On the opposite end, Converge (+9.45%) was the top index gainer on the back of speculation of an MSCI inclusion during the November rebal.

Consumer names were strong this week, led by Robinsons Retail (+5.3% WoW). Puregold (+3.6% WoW)and URC (+3.1% WoW) also gained.

Positive pandemic news for the country is the steep decline in new COVID infections. The latest 7-day moving average has fallen to 16,530 new cases a day, down sharply from recent peaks. Daily cases this week have fallen to below 13000.   The more somber news is that daily vaccination rates have fallen to below 400k jabs a day.  Hopefully these numbers pick up  as the government is now allowing the general public and adolescent to get vaccinated starting October. Vaccines are ample, with the country having 26m doses in inventory.

Foreigners were sellers for US$28.9m this week. Excluding the registered US$61.7m from the MREIT listing, foreigners would have been sellers for US$90m. Biggest outflows were seen in Monde (-5.6% WoW) after foreign investors were seen selling post US$100mn overnight placement. Foreigners were also seen selling consumers  URC, Wilcon (-4.6% WoW) and banks BPI (-1.2% WoW),  Metrobank (-2.0% WoW),and BDO (-0.9% WoW) while buying telcos Converge and PLDT.

Corporate News

AEV to sell 25% stake in Aboitiz Power

Aboitiz Equity Ventures disclosed that its board of directors approved the sale of a 25.01% stake in Aboitiz Power to Japan-based JERA Co. for around $1.463bn (around P40/share). Based on consensus figures, P40/share implies a valuation of 14.1x PE and 10.0x EV/EBITDA for 2022. We estimate that the transaction price would be equivalent to $1.9mn EV/MW. The planned sale involves 1,840,334,941 common shares in Aboitiz Power. AEV’s stake will go down from 77% to 52% after the sale. JERA will also acquire an additional 1.99% stake from the Aboitiz family, which will bump up JERA’s stake in AP to 27%. The public float is at 19.70%. The partnership includes the joint development of liquefied natural gas for power projects as well as fuel sourcing and management. JERA Asia is a Japan-based energy company with LNG projects in Australia/USA and power generation investments in Asia, Middle East, Europe and Americas.

SEC approves AllDay supermarket IPO

The Villar Group’s AllDay Supermarket could raise as much as P6.0bn from its IPO with a price up to P0.80/share. The plan is to sell 6.86bn primary shares worth up to P5.5bn and an overallotment option of 686mn shares worth up to P548mn. As of 1H21, AllDay had 33 supermarkets with net selling area of 55,881. The company is targeting 45 stores by end-2022 and 100 stores by 2026. Sales in 2020 was P7.9bn while 1H21 was P4.5bn. Net profit in 2020 was P220mn while 1H21 was P180mn. The listing date is set at November 3 for now.

PLDT launches new postpaid plans as mobile number portability commences

PLDT subsidiary Smart launched new postpaid plans called Signature Plan+. The new plans offer double the data allocation previously provided. The entry level plan (PhP999/month) now has 20GB allocation per month (vs 10GB). The top plan now has “unlimited” 4G/5G data. This comes at the start of the implementation of mobile number portability (MNP). We expect Globe to follow suit if it hasn’t already. Around 20% of PLDT’s mobile revenue is from postpaid while the proportion for Globe is ~26%.

PH airline capacity data for September

Jefferies’ airlines team publishes global air traffic data on a monthly basis, which includes high frequency data on the Philippines.  From this chart, we can conclude the following:

 

September is back to the recent peaks in December/early March. The latest data for the week of September 20 show that total flights (domestic + international) are back to around December 2020/early March 2021 levels, though still 76% below 2019. 

3Q21 capacity looking better than 2Q21.  It also appears that there’s more flight capacity in 3Q21 (vs. 2Q21), despite the lockdown in August.  Since there’s no material change in international arrival limits at NAIA since 2Q, we reckon the improvement largely came from more domestic flights.  

We maintain our Buy recommendation for CEB which has underperformed the PCOMP by 27% since end-June 2021 weighed down by the depreciation of the PHP against the USD and the steady increase in jet kero prices. These, along with lockdown/slowing vaccination headlines, have negatively affected sentiment. We believe the QoQ improvement in flight traffic should bode well for 3Q EBITDA/earnings (before FX) of CEB.  Despite headwinds from higher jet kero prices, we expect 3Q21 EBITDA losses to narrow further QoQ, as they mount more flights. 

The airlines have been urging the government to lift travel restrictions for vaccinated individuals and to establish a unified travel policy.  Our TP of P65/sh is based on a multiple of 6.5x on FY22E EBITDA that is 15% below 2019.

 
 

Macro News

BOP surplus hits US$1.044bn in August

​The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$1.04bn in August. This is higher than the US$657mn BOP surplus recorded in the same month last year and the US$642mn surplus in July 2021. This was due to increased special drawing rights from the IMF; IMF allocated US$2.77bn for the Philippines. The BOP surplus in August reduced the cumulative BOP deficit in YT-August to US$253mn. The BOP expects a surplus of US$4.1bn this year

PH Banks’ Evergrande/China exposure

With the troubles of Chinese real estate company Evergrande, clients have been asking about banks’ exposure to the company, the sector, and the country. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Benjamin Diokno downplayed the potential impact to the Philippine banking system, saying total overseas exposure was just 9.4% of total assets. In specific, claims from China (including HK and Macau) are just 0.86% of total assets.

Metro Manila seen staying at Alert 4 based on data

Metro Manila may remain under Alert Level 4 in October, despite the downward trend in COVID-19 infections according to DOH epidemiological bureau director Alethea de Guzman. The government cited cautiousness in interpreting data because of lower output from testing laboratories. Meanwhile, Trade Secretary Ramon Lopez said about P30bn worth of wages could be restored per week if Metro Manila’s restrictions are eased to Alert Level 3.

Pace of vaccinations slows further

As of September 26, the 7-day average vaccinations stood at 359,982 from 381,073/day the previous week. Around 52% of the Metro Manila population has been fully vaccinated against COVID and 19% of the Philippine population has been fully vaccinated against COVID. Around 27mn doses are expected to arrive in September including about 18mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 70mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 44mn doses (as of September 26). The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021. 

Consumer loans -3% YoY, NPLs 9.9% in 2Q21, as auto loans deteriorate

Consumer loans of the total Philippine banking industry contracted by 2.8% YoY in 2Q21, and by 0.7% QoQ. Meanwhile, consumer loan NPLs inched up 11bps QoQ to 9.86%. The main culprit for both the loan decline and the NPL increase was motor vehicle (MV) loans, which were down 15.3% YoY and 5.3% QoQ, and whose NPLs worsened to 30bps QoQ to 12.40%.  Mortgages were up 5.8% YoY, though this was slower than the 7.9% YoY pace in 1Q21 (it’s the slowest YoY growth since 2Q2010). On a QoQ basis, though, mortgages were up slightly (+1.6%). Mortgage NPLs were broadly flat at 9.42%, compared to the 9.44% reported in 1Q21.

 

Credit card receivables were still -2.5% YoY, but much better than the -10.3% YoY posted in 1Q21. Recall that some banks did a big write-down in 1Q21 following the lapse of the Bayanihan 2 moratorium (officially ended Feb). Balances are up slightly (+0.5%) QoQ. Meanwhile, credit card NPLs edged up a bit to 8.55%, compared to 8.48% in 1Q21. Anecdotally, banks we cover/follow have said that they have seen renewed mortgage demand and credit card usage in 3Q21. And so far, they have not seen a material deterioration in asset quality, despite the renewed lockdowns in Aug/Sept. The auto loan picture remains quite weak though. While banks are indeed writing new loans, it’s not fast enough to compensate for the old loans rolling off / maturing.

 

Philippines to begin vaccinating minors in October

President Duterte approved the vaccination of the general population starting October

Priority will still be given to: Medical frontliners, Senior citizens, People with comorbidities,

Frontliners in essential sectors, Indigent population. The Vaccination of children aged 12 to 17 years will also begin in October. The population above 15yrs old and above is 77mn (out of a total population of 109mn); Population above 10yrs old is 87mn. The Philippines is slated to receive a total of 175mn doses from Feb 2021 to Jan 2022, including 70mn that already arrived. The Philippines has received a total of 70mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 44mn doses (as of September 26). The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.

Contact centers to grow 8%-9% in 2021

The Contact Center Association of the Philippines (CCAP) said it expects the contact center industry to expand headcount by 8%-9% this year. The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) previously forecasted the BPO industry to grow 5%-6.5% this year. The industry grew 1-2% in 2020 and now has 1.32mn employees that generates US$26.7bn. Despite the expectation of growth for the BPO sector this year, we still expect office vacancy to rise to 15% by end-2021. Vacancy was 9% as of 4Q20 and 13% as of 2Q21.

Sara Duterte remains top presidential candidate in polls

In the September 2021 Pulse Asia survey, Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte (daughter of President Rodrigo Duterte) remains the number 1 choice to be President in the May 2022 elections. However, she dropped from 28 points in June to 20 points in September. Her numbers fell in every category of geography and social class. Sara Duterte said she would not run for national office. The following are statistically tied at second place: Former Senator “Bongbong” Marcos with 15, Manila Mayor Francisco “Isko Moreno” Domagoso with 13 and Senator Emmanuel “Manny” Pacquiao with 12.

Loans up 1.3% YoY in August; M3 growth accelerates to 6.9% in August

​Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, increased by 1.3% YoY, 1.1% MoM in August. This is Up from the 0.7% decline in July and was the first reported expansion in outstanding loans of U/KBs since late 2020. If similar trend for All Banks, loans could be already +1.5% in Aug (was -0.5% in July). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 6.9% YoY to P14.4tn in August, faster than the 5.9% growth posted in July.

 

Hot money reverts to net inflow in August

Foreign portfolio investments (“hot money”) turned to a net inflow of US$11.51mn in August from net outflows of US$339.7mn in July 2021 and US$126.76mn August 2020.

House approves P5tn budget for 2022

The House of Representatives approved the proposed ₱5.024tn budget for 2022 (House Bill No. 10153). To speed up voting and approval, the House created a small committee led by Appropriations Committee chairman Eric Yap to resolve individual amendments of congressmen. The proposed amendments will be accepted until Oct. 5.
