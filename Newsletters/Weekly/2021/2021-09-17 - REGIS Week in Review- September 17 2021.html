REGIS REPORT

17 September 2021

The Phils dropped 0.8% WoW. After challenging the 7000 index level all week, the market settled at 6912.85.

Foreigners were buyers for US$52m this week but this was bloated by the Robinsons Land REIT IPO. Excluding RCR, foreigners would have been sellers for US$22m.

Focus was on FTSE rebalancing on Friday where Converge (+6.8% WoW) was added while AC Energy (+2.0% WoW) saw a sizable upweight. Despite FTSE rebal addition, Converge was top foreign sold alongside other telcos Globe and PLDT.  Among the other changes were downweights in large caps Ayala Corp (-0.3% WoW), SM Investments (-2.1% WoW), SMPH (-1.3% WoW), and Ayala Land (-2.9% WoW). 

Aboitiz Power (+12.2% WoW) continued to surge this week, taking along with it parent Aboitiz Equity (+8.7% WoW) which was also among the top gainers.  AP played catchup to other peers in the sector. The sector has seen interest propped up by the power supply situation, with recent events at Malampaya and First Gen (-0.6% WoW)  highlighting tight supply.  AP is the only generator with substantial capacity (1300MW) coming online between 2H21-1H22. Market talk continued of a potential investor in AP as well.

Telcos dominated yet again this week, despite foreign outflows.  Converge (+6.8% WoW) indicated a very strong 3Q, with subs likely to hit 1.7m subs by year-end from 1.4m as of end-June. The company also provided  guidance of 65% YoY growth in revenues and P6.5-7bn in profits this year (1H profit at P2.25bn). The market continued to discount the fintech units of the telcos, with Globe (+4.8% WoW) and PLDT (+1.8% WoW) continuing to climb.

The decliners for the week were mixed, led by GT Capital (-6.7% WoW) and URC (-6.5% WoW).

The National Capital Region is now under Alert Level 4, under a new classification level from the government.  Level 4 allows limited indoor restaurant dining and will also allow operation of personal care services. 

Case counts in the country have yet to fall sharply, with the latest 7-day moving average of daily confirmed cases at 20,390.  Based on the latest data, 16% of the total population of the country and 44% of Metro Manila is now fully vaccinated (two doses for a two-dose regime). For those with at  least one dose, it is 20% and 63% respectively. The last 8-day average of vaccinations is at 413,000 a day.

Corporate News

More on PAL’s bankruptcy

There will be a 15% reduction in PAL’s narrowbody fleet post-Chapter 11.  We have a bit more detail on PAL’s fleet size and plan after Chapter 11.  The company is targeting to reduce its fleet size to 70 from 95 in end-June (the filing states 21 planes will be returned but this doesn’t include yet the planes PAL has returned already prior to filing).  PAL focusing more on cutting widebodies but they will also reduce narrowbodies (e.g. 5 A321, 1 A320, 2 Bombardier). 

PAL will end up with a fleet of 70, of which 56 are narrowbodies. It is unclear at this stage if the 9 A320s and 6 bombardiers PAL owns will exit soon (either through retirement or sale), as these planes are quite old already (at least more than 6 yrs old).  Beyond this, we understand PAL is also negotiating with Airbus for the postponement of 13 narrowbody deliveries, with an option to cancel orders beyond 2026.  Thus, their fleet size will unlikely grow in the medium term, in our view. 

We estimate PH airline industry could cut its narrowbody fleet size by 11% from pre-pandemic levels.  With arguably the healthiest balance sheet amongst airlines, we believe CEB will be in a better position for a rebound if things get better.

 

MREIT IPO priced at P16.10/share

MREIT’s IPO was priced at P16.10/share. We estimate that this could raise our MEG NAV estimate by 2%. The REIT will have a market cap of P40.8bn and the IPO will raise P15.3bn. The total number of shares to be offered was reduced from 1.24bn secondary shares to 950mn secondary shares (including overallotment option).

MREIT will offer dividend yield of:

June 2022 – 5.7% (based on prospectus DPS forecast of P0.91/share)

June 2023 – 6.1% (based on prospectus DPS forecast of P0.98/share)

The REIT will have 10 office buildings with GLA of 225,000sqm and MREIT plans to acquire 44,000sqm of office space in January 2022 and will be a debt funded acquisition. MREIT is targeting to have a portfolio of 500,000sqm by 2024. Listing date will be on September 30.

Foreign ownership rises in August

We estimate total market foreign ownership rose to 21.5% (+32bps MoM) while PSEi foreign ownership climbed to 25.4% (+72bps MoM) in August. While foreigners turned net buyers in August (for the first month since October 2019), headline foreign ownership was boosted by the share price outperformance of some high-foreign ownership large caps

GLO – share price +46% MoM (58.1% foreign ownership)

TEL – share price +20% MoM (37.5% foreign ownership)

CNVRG – share price +36% MoM (31.6% foreign ownership)

ICT – share price +20% MoM (37.9% foreign ownership)

Headline foreign ownership of the PSEi was elevated further by the removal of DMC and EMP and the addition of ACEN and CNVRG, which have higher foreign ownership levels

DMC – 5.5% foreign ownership

EMP – 11.2% foreign ownership

ACEN – 15.7% foreign ownership

CNVRG – 31.6% foreign ownership

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were GLO and SMPH. For GLO, in addition to being the best performing PSE index constituent, Globe saw the highest increase in foreign ownership (+33 bps MoM to 58.1%). Meanwhile SMPH saw foreign ownership decline 76 basis points MoM to 23.0%.

NPLs, past due plateau in July - close to the peak?

Asset quality seems to have flattened out in July. NPL and Restructured Loan (RL) ratios posted VERY modest increases MoM. Industry-wide NPL ratio inched up 4bps MoM to 4.65% in July, with nominal PHP amount up less than 1% MoM to P486bn. RLs were pretty much flat MoM, up just 1bp to 3.06%. Meanwhile, the Past Due (PD) levels fell slightly, down 5bps MoM to 5.31% and down 0.5% to P574bn. PD tends to be a leading indicator of the direction of asset quality. NPL Cover was flat MoM at 82%. It is important to note that these figures were right before the current lockdown, in effect since early August. That said, banks that attended our JAF conference last week – The Big Three: BDO, MBT and BPI – all indicated that they have not yet seen an uptick in PD/NPL. All three banks opined that businesses and households may have adapted to the on-again-off-again lockdowns, and those that have survived until now are doing fine. Management of all three said they were officially still holding on to their respective guidance of peak NPL ratio. However, all expressed guarded optimism that the worst is behind us, and that provisions can normalize from here. If the trend holds, the bank with most potential earnings upside from lower provisions – or even write-backs – would be MBT, given their very high NPL Cover at 179% as of 2Q21, followed by BPI (124%), then SECB (103%) and BDO (100%).

 

Malampaya gas field back to normal delivery Tuesday evening

Shell Philippines Exploration BV (SPEx), the operator of the Malampaya gas-to-power project said the natural gas field was expected to go back to normal delivery levels by Tuesday evening. The assurance came after an extended gas restriction following a scheduled production hold cut off gas supply to some power plants in Luzon including four facilities run by First Gen. The planned production hold last weekend was to prepare for the scheduled 20-day maintenance shutdown in October. There was a slight delay in the platform’s startup which led to the prolonged gas supply restriction over the weekend.

Some PPA board members not supportive of petitions to increase port charges

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) said two petitions to increase port charges are currently awaiting a final decision. Some PPA board members are hesitating to increase rates due to the pandemic crisis. The PPA, however, is acknowledging contractual issues that need to be considered. Manila North Harbour (MNH), a 50%-owned unit of ICTSI (ICT PM), has sought to increase rates. MNH accounts for c. 3% of ICT’s profit and 2% of NAV. The Port of Batangas, a wholly-owned unit of Asian Terminals (ATI PM), is also seeking a tariff hike.

Ayala plans note issue, tender offer

Ayala Corp is planning the offer of dollar-denominated perpetual notes to be issued by wholly-owned subsidiary AYC Finance Ltd. AYC is also eyeing a tender offer of its $400mn 5.125% senior fixed-for life notes and $400mn 4.85% senior fixed-for-life notes. The proceeds of the notes issuance will be used to refinance AYC’s outstanding USD undated notes through funding of the tender offer.

AC Energy finalizes sale of two power barges

AC Energy Corp has signed an asset purchase agreement with SPC Power Corp for the sale of two oil-fired diesel barges. The facilities for sale are power barge 102 ad power barge 103, each with a capacity of 32MW. The closing of the sale is expected in 4Q21. PB 102 and PB 103 are currently non-operating.

Emperador files Singapore listing application

Emperador said it submitted on Wednesday the requirements for its secondary listing at the main board of the Singapore Exchange. “Singapore, one of the world’s major financial hubs, will provide a broader audience and greater access to international investors”, according to Emperador CEO Winston Co. It was previously reported that Emperador was considering a secondary listing in Singapore that could raise a maximum of S$1bn.

Converge targets up to 65% revenue growth this year

Converge ICT solutions expects P25bn-P26bn in revenue this year from P15.7bn in 2020. Meanwhile, net profit could reach P6.5bn-P7bn this year from 1H21’s P2.25bn. The company has had a “very strong” third quarter. Converge is targeting 1.7mn subscribers by the end of the year from 1.4mn as of end-June. The company saw the “biggest demand” in terms of installations and applications in August and September. Their nationwide household coverage in the 1H21 was 32% and they have a 2025 target of 44%. The company will increase its presence in Visayas and Mindanao over the next 6 months.

 
 

Macro News

Infrastructure spending rises 39% YoY in July

The national government spent P72.8bn on infrastructure in July, up 39% YoY but down 23% MoM due to DPWH projects, higher spending for defense/PNP projects. This brings YT-July infra spending (excluding subsidies and equity to GOCCs and transfers to LGUs) up 42.6% YoY to P499.4bn. Total infrastructure spending in YT-July (i.e. including GOCC subsidies and LGU transfers) rose 35.6% YoY to P625.2bn. This accounts for 61% of the government’s year-end target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY), equivalent to 5.1% of GDP. For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn (+25% YoY), equivalent to 5.8% of GDP.

44% of Metro Manila population now fully vaccinated

As of September 13, 14.5mn COVID vaccine doses have been administered in Metro Manila. For the first dose there has been 8.5mn (63% of the Metro Manila population) and 6mn have been fully vaccinated (44% of the Metro Manila population). As of September 13, the 7-day average vaccinations figure (nationwide) was 412,900/day vs the as of September 5, the 7-day average vaccinations figure was 391,367/day. Around 25mn doses are expected to arrive in September, including about 5mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 56.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 39.1mn doses (as of September 13). The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021. 

Gov’t readies retail bond offer

The Philippine government is looking to raise at least US$400mn from its first-ever retail dollar bond offering. The government aims to raise US$200mn each from the five-year/ten-year dollar denominated bonds/notes. The offering will run from September 15 to October 1. The government debt stood at P11.6tn in end-July, of which 70% is domestic and 30% is external.

GIR rises to US$108bn in end-August

Preliminary gross international reserves (GIR) stood at US$108.05bn in end-August, US$895mn (+0.8% MoM) higher than in end-July (US$107bn). This represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 12.3 months’ worth of imports. The government cited additional allocation of special drawing rights to the Philippines given the IMF’s efforts to increase global liquidity.

PH COVID cases up 15% WoW

Covid daily case growth rates (7-day average) accelerated WoW to 15%. NCR cases were up 18% WoW while new case counts outside NCR were up 14% WoW. NCR accounts for 28% of active cases, up from c. 25% the previous week but still below peak in March/April (c. 50%). Active cases are mostly asymptomatic; Severe/Critical cases remain low (<1%).

Remittances jump to US$2.85bn in July

Cash remittances rose 2.5% YoY, 8.2% MoM to US$2.85bn in July. In PHP terms, remittances rose 3.5% YoY. This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate at +7.1%. The July remittance is close to the peak remittance of US$2.9bn in December 2019. Sea-based remittances continue to rebound and was very near pre-pandemic levels. Land-based remittances rose 1.6% YoY, 8.0% MoM; Growth appears to have stabilized. For YT-July remittances are at US$17.8bn, up 5.8% YoY (+1.8% YoY in PHP terms) and is on track to reach the BSP year-end target of US$31bn (+4% YoY).

Gov’t raises US$866mn from maiden retail dollar bond offer

The Philippine government raised an initial US$866.2mn from its maiden offering of retail dollar bonds (RDBs). The Bureau of the Treasury (BTr) awarded US$551.8mn worth of five-year RDBs and another US$314.4mn via the 10-year dollar-denominated notes. The five-year bonds, which mature in October 2026, had a coupon rate of 1.375% (vs. 5-year ROP due 2026 indicative offer of 1.086%). The 10-year bonds due in October 2031 fetched a 2.25% coupon rate (vs. 10-year ROP due 2031 indicative offer of 1.82%). The government aims to raise P3tn this year from local and foreign sources to plug its budget deficit seen to hit 9.3% of overall economic output.

Airlines, tourism industry seek greater mobility for vaccinated

Domestic carriers have asked to ease restrictions for fully vaccinated individuals to encourage travel according to Presidential adviser for entrepreneurship Joey Concepcion. Airlines are asking to remove multiple requirements for traveling, especially for domestic destinations requiring a negative RT-PCR test and long quarantine period for passengers of international flights. The Philippine Hotel Owners Association and the bus industry are also seeking to lift mobility restrictions.
