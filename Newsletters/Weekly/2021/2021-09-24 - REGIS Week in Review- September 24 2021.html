REGIS REPORT

24 September 2021

The PSEi  inched up 0.6% WoW to close the week at 6951.53 index points.  The market traded around the 7000 level again this week, even as global markets were preoccupied by the troubles of China Evergrande and the Fed policy pronouncements.

On the pandemic front, things on the ground turned more optimistic. Metro Manila reached a milestone, with 50% of total population now fully vaccinated, hopefully paving the way for reduced infection rates and easing restrictions moving forward. Daily new infections have also subsided:  the latest 7-day moving average of new daily confirmed cases stood at 18720,  below last week’s 20390. For the country, 18% of the population has been fully vaccinated. Meanwhile, the government is pilot-testing face-to-face school classes to see if they can be conducted safely.  

Foreigners were sellers for US$17m this week, of which $7m flowed through on Friday. Foreigners were active in telcos -- rotating out of Globe (unch) and Converge (+5.8% WoW) and into PLDT (+15.5% ) which topped the leaders list this week. Meanwhile, heavy foreign outflows were also seen in URC (-2.8% WoW), SMPH (unch),  and SM (-2.9% WoW).

Boxer Manny Pacquiao declared his intention to run for the Presidency, as did Manila Mayor Francisco Domagoso (aka Isko Moreno – his screen name when he was an actor).  Including Senator Ping Lacson,  a total of three candidates have so far declared their intention to run in next year’s May elections. 

PLDT (+15.5% WoW) led the gainers this week, playing catch up to Globe which has advanced more sharply in the last few weeks. PLDT was lifted by news that its fintech holding company Voyager was granted a digital banking license by the central bank. It will be one of only seven entities receiving a digital bank license in this round.  Voyager’s e-wallet has over 40m registered users.  PLDT owns ~38.5% of Voyager.

GCash, the e-wallet operator partly owned by Globe  released details on the inroads it is making on financial services. Funds in its savings product (GSave) have grown to P9bn (US$180) from P5bn (US$100m) in six months; its micro-finance operation (GCredit) now disburses P1bn (US$20m) in loans a month (cumulative P15bn). Its platform now also accounts for a third of all insurance policies issued in the country. The fintech firm also revealed that it plans to go into the Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) service later this year.

Other top index gainers this week were AC Energy (-12.9% WoW), and LT Group (+12.9% WoW) which recovered from strong selling activity in recent months. Meanwhile, conglos were worse off this week, with AGI (-3.3% WoW) among the top laggards, followed by JG Summit (-3.2% WoW), and SM Investments.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Metro Pacific - Regulatory overhang lifted; Maintain Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Trading at a wide discount to NAV

The shares of Metro Pacific (MPI) are up from recent lows but are still lagging the PCOMP’s performance by 5% over the past year, as its discount to its market-based NAV widened to 41%. We believe the period of de-rating is over following: (1) the lifting of the regulatory overhang, and (2) termination of its loss-making logistics venture, and this discount should narrow. With cash levels healthy, MPI also has room to raise its buyback program, which should limit share price downside while improving NAV/share. Maintain Buy.

Regulatory issues on water, toll roads come to an end

After close to a decade, regulatory issues that have hounded its water and toll road units were finally resolved this year. The outcomes of (a) water contract revisions and (b) toll road arbitration were largely as expected and not as bad as feared. We believe investors should now revisit MPI, as the regulatory overhang has been lifted and regulatory outcomes are more likely to surprise to the upside. Moreover, investor concerns over some of MPI’s investments are being addressed. The company ended its unprofitable logistics venture, focusing instead on increasing shareholder returns via buybacks.

Revising FY21E profit estimate

We reduce our FY21 profit forecast by 13% but maintain our FY22 profit forecast. MPI’s key business units remain affected by the pandemic but with vaccinations underway, we are optimistic about a more meaningful earnings recovery in 2022 (+11% YoY). Raising NAV by 14%;

Revised TP at P5/sh

We lift our SOTP NAV by 14% to P7.37/sh after incorporating: (1) a higher water regulatory return, (2) lower tax rates, (3) the accretive impact of asset sales, (4) DCF rollover, and (5) a lower share count. We continue to ascribe a 30% holding company discount (in line with its historical discount of 28%) to arrive at our revised TP of P5/sh. Downside risks relate to regulatory, water supply, dilution, and the pandemic.

 
PUBLISHED RESARCH
Bloomberry Resorts - Third time’s the charm
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Reiterate BUY on BLOOM as Manila, casinos reopen a third time

As Manila loosens mobility restrictions following the third ECQ (strictest lockdown) since the pandemic started, BLOOM reopens its doors to the public once again at a restricted capacity of 50%. If 1H21 is anything to go by, we are optimistic that the mass segment can ramp-up quickly and return to positive EBITDA by 4Q21. The timetable has been pushed back a number of times, but a path to a reopening is now becoming clearer. BLOOM is not far from its 52-week low, and expectations of a sustainable ramp-up could be more of a catalyst than the actual prints. We reiterate our BUY rating, with a price target of P10.20.

Does it stand to benefit from Macau’s misfortunes?

Talks of increased regulatory oversight for Macau could weigh on regional sentiment near-term. However, a restricted Macau is arguably positive for regional operators outside China over the long run.

EBITDA at 71%/89% of pre-Covid peak in FY22/FY23

We cut our estimates to reflect the delayed reopening timetable following the recent round of lockdowns. Our optimism hinges on the ability of mass market to bounce back immediately following shutdowns (as in 2Q21). The run-rates show that underlying demand, for local mass market in particular, is healthy, despite the pandemic’s supposed impact on peoples’ wallets. Against 2019, we estimate GGR will bounce back to 61%/82% and EBITDA back to 71%/89% of pre[1]pandemic levels (2019) in FY22/FY23E.

Shift in revenue mix, cost rationalization initiatives to overhaul margin profile

From FY21E-FY23E, we estimate that contribution of VIP will range from 23%- 33% of total GGR, lower than 43% in 2019 and 50% historically. The mix, along with pandemic-induced expense rationalization, will lead to expanded headline margins (42-45% EBITDA margin vs 33-41% in previous estimates).

Target of P10.20/share at 11x Solaire’s FY22 EBITDA; risks

We tweak our target price up by 2% (to P10.2), maintaining our target multiple at 11x EV/EBITDA and applying it to Solaire’s FY22E EBITDA, and adding the value of unused landbank. At our target, BLOOM would trade at 12.9x consolidated EBITDA for FY22E and 10.3x for FY23E, a premium to Macau after the latter’s recent collapse. Key risks include repeated lockdowns, prolonged restrictions on travel, competition and regulation.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Glass half full (part 3) – NCR moves to reopen, as vaccinations hits 63%*
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

A path to (finally) reopening?

Metro Manila (aka NCR) implements a new “granular lockdown” with 5 “Alert Levels”, starting at Level 4 from 16 September. The lower Alert Levels allow gradual opening of retail, restaurants, travel, and schools. The current Covid-19 surge is much more severe outside NCR. This is likely because 63% of NCR residents are at least partially vaccinated, compared to just 20% of the nation-wide average. This reinforces our positive outlook on the market, as we head into 4Q21 and into 2022.

Metro Manila: 63% at least one jab, 44% fully vaccinated

As of 13 September, 20% of all Filipinos had received at least one does of a Covid-19 vaccine, with 16% fully vaccinated. The figure is much higher for NCR, though, with 63% of residents with at least one jab, and about 44% fully vaccinated (Figure 1). Looking at just adults (under-18s are not yet eligible for the jab), that translates to 86% partially, and 61% fully inoculated.

Shift to “granular lockdown” offers path to full reopening…

With rising vaccination levels Metro Manila has transitioned to a new “granular lockdown” system with 5 “Alert Levels”, depending on Covid cases, and hospital occupancy levels (Figure 2). NCR starts off at Level 4, allowing partial reopening of restaurants and non-essential retail. At lower levels, allowed capacity levels increase. Crucially, schools could be allowed to restart face-to-face learning for the first time since March 2020. The government estimates that 60% of families have at least one parent skip work to teach their kids. As the schoolyear re[1]started, 4mn people left the workforce in July (-8.5% vs June).

… but the rest of the country needs to catch up

Covid-19 cases have surged to more than 20,000/day, and the numbers are much worse outside NCR (Figure 3). That said, the healthcare system doesn’t seem to be as badly stressed (Figures 4/5). NCR, its surrounding provinces, plus Cebu and Davao together account for c2/3 of GDP. However, a broader reopening will require an acceleration of vaccinations nation-wide

* Percent of population having received at least 1 dose of a Covid-19 vaccine.

 
 

Corporate News

Converge to be traded in US markets via ADRs

Converge ICT Solutions announced on Monday that it will soon be traded over the counter in the United States financial markets in the form of unsponsored American Depositary Receipts (ADRs). The total American Depositary Shares registered amounted to 50 million ADS for a maximum offer price of $5 each or total aggregate offering price of $2.5mn. Dennis Anthony Uy, CEO of Converge, sad “We welcome this development as it opens up another channel for US investors to invest into Converge shares. This signals the confidence of an increasing number of investors in the continued strong growth of the company as we continue to reach the unserved and underserved areas in the Philippines.”

GCash financial services post gains

According to GCash President Martha Sazon, “inclusive growth” services of GCAsh have grown over the last six months. GSave, the savings product of GCash, has almost doubled its assets under management to P9bn from P5bn in just six months. Its investment product GInvestment was able to capture “70% of domestic market of total Unit Investment Trust Fund accounts” in six months. GInsure now accounts for a third of all the new insurance policies issued in the Philippines. GCredit now disburses an average of P1bn worth of credit per month, with a total of P15bn disbursed as of end June. GCash is piloting GLoan, a new service that allows qualified users to borrow as much as P25,000 with repayment spread over 12 months.

SM Group to build monorail and flyover in Pasay City

Transportation Secretary Arthur Tugade led the signing of a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) between Pasay City and the SM Group for the EDSA-Taft Avenue to Diokno Boulevard monorail. This will increase the connectivity of Mall of Asia Complex as well as SM Prime’s upcoming reclamation projects. The monorail project will be built at no cost to the national government. The Pasay City will transfer all components of the project to the national government after 50 years. The Department of Transportation will have a share of revenue: 1% for fares below P15; 2% for rates between P15 and P30; 3% for fares above P30. The SM Group will also build an EDSA-Tramo flyover and no other details were provided.

Senate clears on second reading water franchise bills

Water franchise bills passes Senate. The two bills granting 25-year franchises for Manila Water (MWC PM) and Maynilad cleared the Senate, which could extend the terms of their respective revised concession agreements for the East Zone and West Zone, respectively, to end in 2047 (from 2037 currently).  Apart from this, the franchises will also restore their rights of eminent domains, which could help expedite execution of projects. The contract extensions not guaranteed but highly likely. Under the bills, however, the extension will still be subject to the approval of the regulator, MWSS, so it is not automatic. The franchise bills also state that at the end of the revised concession agreements, MWSS can decide to re-bid the contract or sell its assets and the franchise holder will have the right to match the highest bidder.

 

We believe it is highly likely that the MWSS will grant the 10-year extensions.  We estimate an extension (which will also likely lead to higher committed expenditures) will be NPV-positive. Every 10% increase in capex assumptions as a result of the contract extensions could raise our DCFs (i.e. EVs) for the East zone and Maynilad’s West zone by c. 15%. MWC looks fully valued; Prefer MPI/DMC.  We believe MWC is fully valued, as the share price today (P17.70/sh) already appears to assume an extension and a significant increase in capex (+50%).  In contrast, we believe the market has yet to fully discount the upside risk to Maynilad, as its parents, Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and DMCI (DMC PM), currently trade at 42% and 19% discounts to their respective market-based NAVs.

San Miguel’s P95bn tollroad gets government approval

The government approved the Pasig River Expressway (PAREX) project more than a year after it was proposed by San Miguel (SMC PM). SMC signed the Supplemental Toll Operations Agreement recently. The project was initially proposed last June 2020. The 19.37-kilometer elevated expressway is expected to cut travel time between Manila and Rizal province from two hours to only 15 minutes. The tollroad will run along the banks of Pasig River, from Radial Road 10 in Manila (via Skyway 3) to the portion of C-6 Road in Taguig City near Taytay, Rizal (a.k.a. South East Metro Manila Expressway (SEMME)). The entire P95.4bn project cost will be funded by SMC. The construction may begin within the first three months of 2022 or as soon as SMC gets the Notice to Proceed from the government. The six-lane elevated road can be completed on or before 2027 or within five years.

AP’s GNPD unit 2 pushed back to 3Q22

The management of GNPower Dingin (GNPD) said the timeline of the second unit of its 1,336MW power plant in Bataan had been further pushed back due to travel restrictions. Commercial operations for unit 2 (668MW) is now slated to begin in 3Q22 from 1Q22. Unit 1 (668MW), which is currently undergoing testing and commissioning, is scheduled to go online by fourth quarter of this year which was previously slated for September 2021. GNPD achieved financial close in 2015 and started construction of Unit 1 in 2016.

Ayala Corp refinances perps

Ayala Corp is issuing US$400m of new perpetuals, whose proceeds will be used to buy back previously issued perps. The new perps were priced at 3.9%, and will have no step-up and no reset. AC will likely take a hit of ~US$17m from the purchase of US$400m of its existing perps, as these will have to bought back at above issue price. However, it is expected to save US$4-5m annually in perpetuity. The existing perps carry coupons of 4.85% and 5.125%.

JFC receives SEC approval for pref issuance; sets dividend rates at 3.2821% and 4.2405%

JFC has received SEC approval for its pref share issuance, which aims to raise as much as P12bn. The base offer will comprise of 8mn shares + oversubscription of 4mn shares at P1000/sh. Prefs are cumulative, non-voting, non-participating, non-convertible and redeemable on 3rd (Series A) and 5th (Series B) years

The prefs will carry the following rates:

Series A: 3.2821% p.a. with step-up on year 3

Series B: 4.2405% p.a. with step-up on year 5

Bulk of the proceeds will be used to redeem up to USD250mn worth of perpetual capital securities (USD600mn outstanding)

DITO could replace FGEN in the PSE Index

First Gen (FGEN PM) could be removed from the PSE index during the November 2021 review (or earlier). First Gen’s current public float is 18.74%. Valorous Asia, which currently owns 12.59% of FGEN, made a tender offer and seeks to increase its stake by another 3.0% to 5.7% of shares outstanding by end-September. This could lower the public float below 15%, the minimum requirement to be a PSE index constituent. We consider DITO CME Holdings (DITO PM) the most likely candidate to replace FGEN in the PSE Index. Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM) and DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) are also potential candidates to replace FGEN.

 
 

Macro News

BSP raises remittances growth target

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) upgraded its remittances growth target to +6% from +4% previously according to Zeno Ronald Abenoja, managing director of the BSP’s Department of Economic Research. They cited sustained recovery of remittances in 1H21 and higher global demand for foreign workers. The new target implies remittances could reach US$31.7bn this year. In YT-July, remittances rose 5.8% YoY to US$17.8bn, 56% of the BSP’s revised forecast.

 

Gov’t mulls shifting to PPP for infrastructure funding

The government may revert to public-private partnerships (PPPs) to finance big-ticket infrastructure projects due to funding shortfall as a result of the Mandanas ruling according to Finance Undersecretary and chief economist Gil Beltran. The Mandanas ruling reduces national government resources because the share of local government in revenues will increase. President Duterte’s economic plan currently favors official development assistance (ODA) to bankroll the government’s infrastructure projects and social services.

Boxer Manny Pacquiao to run as President in 2022

Pacquiao accepted the nomination of his political allies during the national assembly of the faction he leads in the ruling PDP-Laban party. PDP-Laban has been the ruling party since 2016 under the administration of President Rodrigo Duterte. Pacquiao’s faction in the party has not expressed support for Duterte’s vice-presidential bid. In July, Pacquiao was voted out as PDP-Laban leader, weeks after challenging Duterte over his position on China and record on fighting corruption, but his ouster was rejected by his faction. Pacquiao trails the front-runners in opinion polls that have been topped consistently by Duterte's daughter, Sara Duterte-Carpio.

Case counts dip WoW; Death rate picks up

The 7-day average daily case count has dropped 5% WoW to c. 20k while the NCR 7-day average daily cases dropped 10% WoW to 5k. Outside NCR, the average daily cases are up 1% WoW to 15k. The country’s positivity rate also dipped by 2ppts to 26%. However, there’s been a surge in deaths over the past week (+80% WoW); 7-day average deaths at 235. The 7-day average death rate has picked up to 1.4% (+0.6% WoW).

Taiwan threatens to take China to WTO over banned apple imports

Taiwan threatened to take China to the World Trade Organization after Beijing said it would suspend sugar apple and wax apple imports from Taiwan due to pest concerns. China’s customs administration said it had repeatedly detected pests in sugar and wax apples from Taiwan and asked its Guangdong branch  to stop customs clearance of these products beginning Monday. Taiwan’s Council of Agriculture claims China’s moves are without scientific evidence and will take the issue to the WTO under its dispute resolution mechanism. In February, China banned imports of pineapples from Taiwan citing “harmful creatures” that could come with the fruit. Del Monte Pacific Limited’s (DELM PM) Philippine operations exports pineapples predominantly to China where its market share of imported pineapples has grown from 20% to over 50% in a span of five years.

Manila Mayor “Isko Moreno” to run for President

Manila Mayor Francisco “Isko Moreno” Domagoso will run for president in the 2022 elections. In a June Pulse Asia survey, Mayor Domagoso trailed the front-runner, Sara Duterte-Carpio, by a wide margin (14 vs 28). Mayor Domagoso’s running mate for vice president is Dr. Willie Ong. Dr. Ong ran for the Senate in 2019 but landed on the 18th spot (out of only 12 incoming senators).

Evergrande debt crisis unlikely to affect Philippine financial system

The Philippines is insulated from shocks that may come from the possible collapse of Evergrande according to Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas Governor Benjamin Diokno. The Philippine banks are largely domestic-oriented with cross border exposures or claims from counterparties in other countries at 9.4% of total banking system assets. In terms of exposures to China, claims from counterparties based in China and its Special Administrative Regions is minimal at 0.86% of total banking system assets.

 

Average daily vaccinations figure drops again

As of September 19, the 7-day average vaccinations figure (nationwide) was 381,073/day vs the as of September 12 7-day average vaccinations figure of 415,362/day. Around 34mn doses are expected to arrive in September including about 13mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 64.9mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 41.4mn doses (as of September 19). The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.

Duterte approves pilot testing of face-to-face classes in low-risk areas

President Duterte has approved the pilot testing of limited face-to-face classes in areas with low COVID-19 cases. The Depart of Education Secretary Leonor Briones said a maximum of 100 public schools which have passed its “readiness assessment” will participate in the pilot test. Around 20 private schools are also expected to join subject to DepEd and DOH validation. The DepEd added that it will gradually increase face-to-face classes if the pilot is safe and effective. Classes in the basic education level opened on September under an online learning setup. The United Nations Children’s Fund Philippines has called for the phased reopening of schools, adding that Philippines and Venezuela are the only remaining countries that have not reopened schools

Senate ratifies Bicam committee report on retail trade

The Senate has ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee report, which seeks to reduce the minimum paid-up capital requirement for foreign retailers. The minimum paid-up capital requirement for foreign retailers was set at P25mn (cUSD500k) with a per store requirement of P10mn. The existing law requires minimum paid-up capital of P125mn (cUSD2.5mn). The amendments are unlikely to materially impact operations for larger retail groups, but could allow small foreign retail shops (e.g. independent specialty retailers) to operate without a local partner.

President Duterte signs POGO tax law

President Duterte signed RA 11590 into law. This will require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue. PAGCOR may also impose a regulatory fee on POGOs, which shall not exceed 2% of GGR. POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P145bn in tax revenue over five years. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new law will lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. Thus, the new law could slow down POGO closures

50% of Metro Manila population now fully vaccinated

As of September 22, 50% of the Metro Manila population has been fully vaccinated against COVID. Around 18% of the Philippine population has been fully vaccinated against COVID. 27mn doses are expected to arrive in September, including about 14mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines has now received a total of 66.0mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 42.7mn doses (as of September 22). The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.

Budget deficit expands to P121bn in August

The National Government’s budget deficit rose threefold to P120.9bn in August, as spending accelerated (+34% YoY). Revenues in August just slightly below (-1%) pre-pandemic monthly average; Tax revenues higher by 2% vs. pre-pandemic levels. Primary spending rose 37% YoY, 12% MoM due to release of cash aid in Metro Manila during ECQ (last year’s cash aid was mostly released in September, a month later than implementation of ECQ in August). The YT-August deficit at P958bn, up 29% YoY.

BSP holds key rate to boost recovery

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) left the key rate at 2%, in line with street expectations. By keeping a steady hand on the BSP’s policy levers will allow the momentum of economic recovery to gain more traction by helping boost domestic demand and market confidence.
