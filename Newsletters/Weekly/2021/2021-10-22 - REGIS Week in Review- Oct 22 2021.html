REGIS REPORT

22 October 2021

The Philippines consolidated its gains this week (+1.1% WoW) to close at 7289.61 as continued improvement in COVID conditions on the ground fueled reopening optimism.

Market proxy conglos dominated the leaderboard with JG Summit (+6.4% WoW), Aboitiz Equity (+5.9% WoW), SM Investments (+4.3% WoW), Metro Pacific (+3.8% MoM), LT Group (+2.8% MoM) and Alliance Global (+2.5% WoW) all among the top ten index gainers this week. 

Cyclicals continued to gain this week, led by  Robinsons Land (+7.9% WoW)  which followed in the footsteps of larger cap property names last week.

URC (+5.1% WoW) and Bloomberry (+4.9% WoW) also gained. 

The government is signaling further easing for Metro Manila to Alert Level 2 if COVID cases continue to come down.  This would bode well for increased activity in 4Q.  The latest 7-day average of new daily cases is now down to around 6,000 from around 9000/day vs a week ago.  The count of new infections has dipped to to less than 5,000/day for the country and around 600/day within NCR in recent days.

Foreigners turned buyers for US$20.8m this week and were seen buying across property, conglos, and consumers. Ayala Land (+1.2% WoW), Ayala Corp (+1.2% WoW), BDO (-3.0% WoW), Jollibee (-2.0% WoW), and DMCI (-3.5% WoW) were the top bought names. Meanwhile, foreigners continued to take profit on recent winners index inclusion bets Converge (-2.8% WoW) and  Monde (-1.1% WoW), as well as telcos Globe (-5.65% WoW) and PLDT (-3.0% WoW).

The 3Q21 earnings season was opened by BPI (unch). The bank’s 9M21 NPAT came in at P17.5bn, flat (+1.8%) YoY, tracking behind consensus (P24.6bn for FY21). For 3Q21 alone, NPAT was about P5.7bn, up 3% YoY. The main drag came from provision expense, which jumped 31% QoQ to P3.8bn, the highest quarterly number so far this year and likely including some write-offs.  NPLs were at 2.76%, down 21bps QoQ.  Loans grew 0.9% YoY, reversing the 5.1% contraction in 2Q21. Growth was driven by mortgages, credit cards, and micro loans.

Also falling on profit taking was AC Energy (-2.5% WoW). The company is buying out its partner in its Australian solar project,  and is also raising its interests in three wind farms by acquiring its partners’ stakes.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Notes from our Philippine Conference, and the Roadshow Popularity Index
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Key takeaways – lockdowns, digitalization, inflation, elections. We held our Jefferies-Regis Philippines Corporate Access Days on 12- 13 October, where more than 200 investors registered to (virtually) meet 46 corporates, and to participate in 5 keynote/round-table discussions. Some key themes emerged: 1) lockdowns mean muted 3Q21 results: 2) companies are still cautious on the 4Q21/2022 reopening theme; 3) e[1]commerce, e-wallets and digibanks were top of mind; 4) inflation and supply chain issues are a problem; 5) a power supply shortage is looming; and 6) the Elections in 2022 are too early to call. We also revisit the Roadshow Popularity Index and wonder if there might be contrarian calls to be had among the less-requested companies (Figure 1). 46 corporates, 3 roundtables, and 2 strategists At the Jefferies-Regis Philippines Corporate Access Days last week, featuring

46 Philippine corporates (including an unlisted pure solar farm operator), and two of Jefferies’ greats, Chris Wood and Sean Darby We also held 3 roundtable sessions on a) restaurants, post-Covid, b) Digital Banks, and c) the upcoming 2022 Presidential elections.

Key themes and takeaways from the conference

Muted 3Q21, on lockdowns – The Covid surge and lockdowns in August/September hurt nearly everyone; unsurprisingly, restaurants, malls and travel/tourism were hardest hit by mobility restrictions. This time though, even the telcos felt the pinch in people’s wallets

Cautiously optimistic on 4Q21/2022 – Most expressed optimism on the reopening theme, with falling cases, and rising vaccination rates, particularly in Metro Manila. Activity already picked up in late 3Q / early 4Q in line with relaxed mobility restrictions. However, few were willing to express outright bullishness, as it was unclear how fast household incomes can recover from more than 18 months of continuous lockdown. Banks, for example, have not yet revised their peak-NPL guidance (4-5%) despite flat trends in August.

 

Inflation is a major concern, not only in that it might blunt consumption-led recovery. But also, companies – most directly food manufacturers and restaurants – are already feeling the squeeze from rising input costs, coupled with an inability to raise selling prices to compensate. But the global supply chain issues are affecting even solar panels, where prices are rising (10-15%) for the first time in a very long time.

Digitalization was a hot topic across all sectors. Telcos Globe and PLDT were center-stage, given their dominance in e-wallets. Among retailers, it seems Robinsons Retail was most ahead of the e[1]commerce curve. The market is also abuzz about the 6 Digital Bank licenses awarded (one to PLDT’s Voyager sub). Of the mainstream banks, BPI seemed most advanced in its digital transformation. The restaurant sector recognizes the need to adopt an omni-channel approach, and to learn to live with aggregators such as foodpanda, GrabFood, etc.

Power and energy were another theme. Semirara was the clear beneficiary of rising coal prices globally. But the market seems to be finally coming around to our views of a looming power shortage – NOT driven by the coal situation. Electricity demand has remained resilient, despite the pandemic. But after Aboitiz Power’s 1,300MW plants come online in 2021/22, most of the new capacity are solar/wind, which are much less dependable. AC Energy continued to be a darling, as a near[1]pure renewable energy play.

The Presidential election in May 2022. While it’s far too early to call, the absence (or not) of Ms Sara Duterte among the Presidential aspirants has thrown the race wide open. For the market, the questions will be who might have a more radical economic policy, or who might have backers/enemies among listed business groups.

 
 

Corporate News

Metro Manila 3Q21 office vacancy rises to 17%

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) estimates that Metro Manila office vacancy rose to 17% in 3Q21; 13% in 2Q21; 9% in 4Q20; 4% in 4Q19. This already exceeds the REGIS 2021E office vacancy forecast of 15%. The increase in vacancy was largely due to new office supply. Metro Manila office stock increased to 13.8mn sqm (from 13.0mn sqm in 2Q21). Metro Manila office rental rates were generally flat QoQ. Office rental rates in most areas are 10%-25% lower than 2019 levels. LPC estimates that secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums are flat QoQ and flat from 2019 levels. This is in contrast to our channel checks, which suggest that condominium prices are generally 10-20% lower than 2019 levels. Land prices in Fort Bonifacio rose 13% QoQ to an accommodation value of P113,000/sqm. This is c. 25% higher than the prices in 2019. Commercial land prices in other areas (in Metro Manila) are flat YTD.

ACEN announces more acquisitions; to raise stakes in 3 wind farms

AC Energy announced that it is raising its interests in three wind farms by acquiring its partners’ stakes in Northwind Power Development Corp and other interests of UPC Philippines Wind Investment Co. In Northwind (52MW wind), ACEN will acquire the 32.2% interest of its partners for P1.09bn; in turn, the sellers will subscribe to 90mn ACEN shares at P11.32/share (P1.02bn). ACEN will raise its interest in Northwind to 100%. We estimate the implied EV/MW for this transaction is $2.2mn/MW (vs $1.6mn/MW at our DCF estimate). ACEN will also acquire the ownership of UPC Philippines and Stella Marie Sutton in 81 MW wind farm North Luzon Renewables (11% stake) and 155MW Bayog Wind project (15% stake) for P4.5bn; in turn the sellers will subscribe to 390mn ACEN shares at P11.32/share (P4.4bn). We estimate the implied EV/MW for this transaction is $2.4mn/MW. ACEN is trading at $4mn/MW

CNPF announces foray into pet food

In a press release, CNPF announced it is entering the pet food category through its brand Goodest. CNPF will initially offer an affordable cat food range in leading supermarket chains and online retail platforms. The brand will be positioned as a more affordable alternative to imported brands that dominate the market.

BPO industry to grow 8% in 2021

The IT and Business Processing Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) now expects the industry to grow 8% this year. IBPAP cited demand from sectors such as financial services and communications. It previously forecasted the BPO industry to grow 5%-6.5% this year. The new 2021 targets are 1.43mn employees and US$28.8bn in revenue. The industry grew 1-2% in 2020. Despite the expectation of growth for the BPO sector this year, we still expect office vacancy to rise to 15% by end-2021. It was at 9% as of 4Q20 and 13% as of 2Q21. Office leasing accounts for: 58% of Filinvest Land’s NAV and 46% of Megaworld’s NAV.

MREIT 1QFY22 revenue reaches P711mn

MREIT’s 1QFY22 (July to September) revenue reached P711mn. This is around 28% of the FY22 target of P2.5bn (based on the prospectus). Occupancy of the portfolio now stands at 100%, from 93% as of end-March. MREIT plans to declare a dividend of at least P0.24/share for the quarter. If we were to annualize this, FY22 DPS would reach P0.96/share. This is higher than the P0.91/share in the REIT prospectus and this implies 5.4% dividend yield. The Philippine government 10-year bond yield is 5.0%. MREIT is targeting to have a portfolio of 500,000sqm by 2024 from 225,000sqm currently. MREIT will pay land lease rent to MEG on the following schedule. Before 1 July 2023 – zero; 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2025 – 2.5% of office/retail revenue; 1 July 2025 onwards – 5% of office/retail revenue. MEG is currently trading at 60% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 6.8X FY22E P/E.

Maynilad warns of water interruptions due to gov’t flood control project

Maynilad announced water interruptions that will range from 25-85 hours between Oct 25 (11am) to Oct 28 (11:59pm). The company cited the realignment of Maynilad’s existing pipeline along Sobriedad corner Cristobal St. in Sampaloc to give way for the government’s flood control project. Interruption will affect 28% (421k) of its service connections, involving 2.9mn customers. Of these, 60k connections in Sampaloc, Manila will have no water for three days. Affected customers: Las Piñas, Makati, Manila, Parañaque, Pasay, as well as parts of Cavite province such as Bacoor, Cavite City, Imus City, Kawit, Noveleta, and Rosario. To cushion the impact of the interruption, Maynilad will deploy 60 mobile water tankers and install 14 stationary water tanks. Maynilad is a 53%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and 25%-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM).

Del Monte, Vinamilk launch new dairy product line

The joint venture of Del Monte Philippines and Vietnam Dairy Products JSC launched a new range of dairy products on Tuesday, marking their entry into the Philippine dairy market. The new products under the JV consist of Del Monte-Vinamilk Fresh Milk, IQ Smart Flavored Milk, YoGurt Drink and Tea Bliss Milk Tea. The JV is targeting at least 10% local dairy market share in the “immediate term”.

PLDT to build Philippines’ biggest data center for global tech giants

PLDT announced on Tuesday that it would build a data center for global technology organizations with immense network, power and space requirements. The data center, which will be telco neutral, is expected to support the needs of global hyperscalers and cloud service providers that are expanding in the region. PLDT Enterprise previously said that the Philippines is now regarded as an emerging data center market as major data center hubs in Singapore and Hong Kong face challenges.

ACEN to raise ownership in UPC/AC Renewables Australia to 100%

AC Energy Corporation’s board approved to acquire the remaining 51.6% stake in UPC/AC Renewables Australia for a total consideration of $243.3mn subject to adjustments. This will raise ACEN’s ownership in the Australia JV to 100%. UPC Renewables Asia Pacific Holdings and Mr. Anton Rohner (sellers) will in turn subscribe to up to 942mn common shares (2% of outstanding common shares) of ACEN with a subscription price of P11.32/share subject to adjustments. The acquisition transforms the UPC/AC Renewables Australia JV into ACEN’s first wholly owned development outside of the Philippines. The transaction include the 520MW New England Solar Farm under construction and a pipeline of over 8,000MW of renewables in Australia. New England Solar 1&2 accounts for 12% of ACEN’s attributable capacity and 5% of our estimated NAV.

PMFTC volumes +5%QoQ, but down 24%YoY

Philip Morris International disclosed last night that cigarette volumes in the Philippines came in at 8.9bn sticks (-24%YoY, +5%QoQ). Volumes of Marlboro and Chesterfield increased, while mid-price Fortune, low-priced Jackpot and More remained lower YoY. The net increased continuing to reflect a “favorable pricing variance”. Industry volumes are down less than PMFTC’s volume YoY, but unit market share is appearing to stabilize after PMFTC’s 5%QoQ volume growth. The unit market share improved marginally to 61.8% in 3Q21 from 61.6% in 2Q21, first uptick after 7 quarters of decline. PMFTC FY21E profit – P37.4bn (+12%YoY); LTG FY21E profit – P24.2bn (+2%YoY).

Okada Manila to go public in US through $2.6bn SPAC deal

Okada Manila has agreed to go public in the US through a merger with blank-check firm 26 Capital Acquisition Corp in a deal that gives the Philippine operator an enterprise value of $2.6bn. The blank check firm is led by Jason Ader, co-founder and chief executive of New York-based buyout firm SpringOwl Asset Management. He served as independent director for Las Vegas Sands Corp between 2009 and 2016. The merger is expected to give Okada Manila cash proceeds of up to $275mn. Its parent company Universal Entertainment will roll its entire equity in the combined company, of which it will own about 88% upon close of the deal. Okada Manila is the largest IR by size – it has capacity to operate up to 599 gaming tables and 4,263 in a property that sits on 44 hectares. The company claims that this number will rise to 974 tables and 6,890 machines by 2022. As of 2Q21, Okada Manila had an 18% GGR market share, the lowest among integrated resorts (Solaire – 38%, RWM – 23%, COD – 21%). Okada Manila’s pre-pandemic EBITDA was $120mn (2019).

PSEi foreign ownership stays flat in September

We estimate PSEi foreign ownership was flat MoM at 25.4% (-4bps MoM, +66bps QoQ) while total market foreign ownership fell 27bps MoM to 21.1% (-24bps QoQ). The MoM decline in market foreign ownership was largely due to the share issuance by DITO (to its parent company) and the IPO of RCR (which has low foreign ownership). If not for these, we estimate that market foreign ownership would have been flat MoM at 21.4%. We estimate that net foreign selling in the market only amounted to US$48mn. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were: GTCAP – saw the largest decline in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents (-103 bps MoM to 21.3%); FGEN – foreign ownership declined 51 basis points MoM to 16.1%; ACEN – had the largest increase in foreign ownership (+40 bps MoM to 16.1%).

 
 

Macro News

OFW remittances rises 5.1% YoY in August

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 5.1% YoY in August but dropped 8.6% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances are up 8.1% YoY and the 3-month moving average growth rate is now at +4.8%. Additionally, YTD, remittances are up 5.7% YoY to US$20.38bn. The BSP looking at remittances reaching US$31.7bn (+6% YoY).

Government targeting to fully vaccinate 50% of population by February

The government hopes to fully vaccinate 50% of the total population by February. Around 22% of the Philippine population (24.5mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. The country is currently administering only 375,000/day. At the current pace, 50% of the population will be vaccinated by April. As of October 18, 59% of the Metro Manila population (7.9mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 91.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 52.8mn doses (as of October 18).

Infrastructure spending rises 60% YoY in August

Gov’t infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 60% YoY to P70.9bn. On a MoM basis, infra spend slipped by 3%. The govt cited spending for public works and airport projects, as well as defense and education. This brings YT-August infra spend (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P570bn, up 44.6% YoY. This accounts for 75% of the government’s FY21E target of P761bn (+12% YoY). Meanwhile, the YT-August overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 38% YoY to P718bn. This accounts for 70% of the government’s FY21E target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY). For FY22, the government has programmed to spend P1.27tn on infra (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), up 25% YoY.

Pace of vaccinations slows down again

As of October 17, the 7-day average vaccinations dropped to 375,773/day from 469,092/day as of October 10. Around 22% of the Philippine population (24.3mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of October 6, 56% of the Metro Manila population (7.5mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 91.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 52.3mn doses (as of October 17).

Dept. of Transportation eyes fuel subsidies to stop fare hike

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) on Monday said it was looking at fuel subsidies and direct aid to both public utility drivers and passengers. Looking at possible uniform discounts on public utility vehicles (PUVs) across all gas stations nationwide. PUVs are allowed to carry half of its maximum passenger capacity. PUV drivers have sought fare hikes but Transport Secretary Tugade has dismissed the petition. The last time the LTFRB approved a fare hike was in 2018, when diesel prices soared to P53 a liter. Transport costs account for 8.1% of the CPI basket. Transport CPI is up 5.2% YoY in September 2021 and up 13.9% vs September 2019.

Philippines logs 3,656 new Covid cases, the lowest since July 13

Wednesday’s cases were the first time the single-day total plunged below 4,000 in over 3 months. The DOH reported a daily positive rate of 11.6% out of 42,296 tests in its bulletin.

DOH: NCR may be placed under Alert Level 2 in coming weeks

Metro Manila may soon ease to Alert Level 2 if COVID cases continue to decline this week. Under Alert Level 2, more establishments can increase capacities and can accommodate unvaccinated individuals. The 7-day average new cases in NCR is now down to <1,200, with the latest daily case data at just 642 (October 20). Metro Manila’s positivity rate is now at 11% vs. 17% national level. Additionally, hospital admissions are also decreasing. Metro Manila is currently under Alert Level 3 from October 16 to 31.
