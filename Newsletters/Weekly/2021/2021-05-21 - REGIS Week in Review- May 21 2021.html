REGIS REPORT

21 May 2021

The Phils dropped 1.1% this week on continued foreign selling ahead of the MSCI rebal next week. The market broke the 6200 level this week, hitting the lowest levels since October last year. The PSEi ended the week at 6199.25.

The market consolidated at the 6200 level with seemingly no themes to the moves this week. Trading seemed directionless with 17 gainers against 13 decliners. The top gainers for the week were Puregold (+11.5% WoW), Megaworld (+10.0% WoW),  and Jollibee (+5.7% WoW). Dropping this week were SM (-5.55% WoW), BDO (-3.8% WoW) and First Gen (-3.55% WoW).

On the ground, the COVID situation showed signs of improvement.  The 7-day average case count has dropped under 6,000, from a peak of over 11,000. Hospital occupancy rates for Covid beds are also sharply down from highs. Meanwhile, the daily rate of vaccinations is up five-fold (to 108k doses/day) from early April, reflecting increased availability of vaccines as a few million doses arrived.  However, the daily vaccination rate will have to rise much more dramatically for the gov’t targets to be reached by year-end (70% of population).

Foreigners were seller for US$54.5m as investors continue to lighten Philippines exposure. Excluding last week (which registered headline inflows due AC Energy’s follow on offering), foreigners would be sellers for 13 straight weeks. Biggest outflows were in large caps AC SM BDO SMPH ALI.

There were very little market catalysts, with results dominating the news. The 1Q earnings  season ended this week, with mixed results and with the pandemic continuing to be a large factor of 1QW performance.

GT Capital - 1Q21: Auto, bank post strong QoQ bounce
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q21 core profit rises 20% YoY to P3.2bn – Ahead of forecast

GTCAP’s 1Q21 profits reached P4.1bn (+60%) boosted by some divestments at its investee companies. Stripping out exceptional items, core profit after pref divs still outperformed, rising by 20% YoY to P3.2bn. Profits were buoyed by lower provisions at the bank, better-than-expected margins at Toyota, and higher property revenue bookings.

Auto sales, GPM contract QoQ; Gaining share but of a smaller pie.

Profits from 51%-owned TMP improved QoQ which management attributed largely to the ownership transfer of the Sta. Rosa dealership to GT Auto Dealership Hldgs. (GTCAD) and a Japanese affiliate of Toyota last January. Retail volumes were down QoQ, after the safeguard duty was imposed in March and GPM contracted to 12.4% due to volume mix changes despite a favorable exchange rate. We reckon headline margins could improve in succeeding periods, as the proportion of higher-margin CKDs and luxury vehicles, which are not covered by the duty, rise.

Market share was 44% in 1Q but has since improved to 46% in April. Management attributed this to (a) its strong online presence and (b) the availability of financing from Toyota Financial Services (TFS), which now accounts for 50% of sales (from 27% in 1Q20). TFS has grown its receivable book to a surprising P100bn (+31% YoY). TFS, however, booked a P77mn net loss in 1Q21, as provisions surged to P130mn while write-offs amounted to P90mn with NPL at 3.3%.

Bank: Provisions halved, NPLs benign

1Q21 profit contributions from its banking arm surged, as provisions halved (see comment on Metrobank results here). NPLs were flat at 2.4% but management remains wary and is keeping its guidance of peak NPL of 5%, given lockdowns.

Property: Reservation sales jump 20% QoQ; Revenue bookings moderate

Property revenue bookings slowed in 1Q21, leading to a QoQ drop in profit. Manpower deployment fell to 59% of pre-COVID levels in January but has since improved to 75% in April, despite lockdowns. Reservation sales bounced 20% QoQ to P3.5bn, due to improved high-end segment sales.

Ayala Corp - 1Q21 still soft; new strategic agenda from management
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Eyes to the future

Ayala Corp held a briefing today to discuss its 1Q results but the more interesting part of the call was not a discussion of the past quarter but rather plans for the near future with new CEO Fernando Zobel baring his new “strategic agenda”.

1Q net profit dips 19%

1Q21 profit for AC came in at P5.4bn, down 19% YoY and trending below estimates. On a headline basis, the disappointments came from the property and banking segments, but the latter was affected by a one-time tax adjustment following changes in tax laws. Conversely, the telecom segment seems to have outperformed, but this was due to tax adjustments in the other direction. We have more on these respective results in previously released notes.

Power: Contributions flat but divestment gains loom

Profit contributions from the power business amounted to P2.4bn, almost unchanged YoY. This includes earnings from assets that have yet to be injected into the listed vehicle AC Energy (ACEN PM). We should see sizeable gains within the year relating to (1) the latter’s recently concluded follow-on offering and (2) a further sale of ACEN shares to Singapore’s sovereign wealth fund.

 

A sharper focus with new CEO?

The highlight of the earnings call were the remarks of its new CEO Fernando Zobel on what he called the “Three Point Strategic Agenda” outlining the group’s intentions on its holdings as grouped into three baskets. Mr Zobel recently took over as CEO after AC decided to split the chairmanship and CEO roles.

Support “core value drivers”

AC identified Ayala Land, BPI, Globe and AC Energy as being its “core value drivers” whose expansion it will continue to support as the country (and world) slowly recovers from the pandemic. Mr Zobel noted that technological megatrends are redefining the banking, telecom and power businesses.

Scale up “emerging businesses”

Two specific businesses ones were identified as being part of its “emerging businesses” whose importance and growth have become evident during this pandemic: health care and logistics. We share management’s view that both industries are fragmented and underdeveloped in the Philippines and could benefit from consolidation (and more professional management). We would not be surprised to see M&A activity in these segments in the near future.

Value realization from “portfolio investments”

A target of US$1bn in proceeds by 2023 through what was euphemistically called “value realization” was revealed. Among the assets grouped under “portfolio investments” were Manila Water. AC Industrials, AC Infra and its education business. A divestment of some of these would, in our view, be welcome.

Jollibee Foods Corp - 1Q21: cost cuts lift margins even as revenues fall short of expectations
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q21 operating profit in line with expectations despite revenue shortfall
JFC reported headline net profit of P153mn for 1Q21. Excluding coupons to perps, we estimate the net loss was P140mn (behind Regis FY21E), with the underperformance largely due to tax-related adjustments. Operating profit improved sequentially (+36% QoQ to P1.5bn, in line) even as revenues declined 6% QoQ.
During the post-results call we hosted with CFO Mr Ysmael Baysa, he seemed optimistic on meeting their own targets for the year despite the slow recovery in the Philippines. He also provided further detail on the company’s planned preferred share issuance.


Philippines underperforms as international business recovers
Most of JFC’s international businesses posted strong SSSG (China +49%, North America ex-CBTL +14%) as the recovery in these markets is well underway. Mr Baysa noted that sales in most of its overseas markets were already at pre-pandemic levels. However, the Philippines (59% of systemwide sale) continued to underperform (-26% SSSG) due to limitations on consumer mobility and the
reimposition of dine-in restrictions beginning end-March.

Cost cuts lift margins nearly back to pre-pandemic levels
JFC’s consolidated gross margin and operating margin in 1Q21 (17.1% and 4.3%, respectively) were nearly at pre-pandemic levels due to significant reductions in opex, which Mr Baysa attributed to last year’s business transformation (BT) initiatives. Meanwhile, operating losses at Smashburger and CBTL further narrowed vs 4Q20.

Price increases initiated to pre-empt input cost inflation
Mr Baysa outlined some key strategies that the company has employed to mitigate the spike in prices of key inputs (particularly pork and poultry): 1) the implementation of price increases as early as 4Q20 to anticipate raw material cost inflation, 2) the sourcing of part of its poultry requirements from its JV with Cargill, and 3) locking in prices of other key raw materials, which is possible due to the advantages of scale.

Cebu Air - 1Q21: Domestic demand improves QoQ, offset by soft international biz
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q21 core net loss at P7.5bn

Headline net loss in 1Q21 stood at P7.3bn while EBITDAR loss shrunk 6% QoQ to P2.95bn. Demand remained soft as travel restrictions persisted and the re[1]imposition of lockdown in late-March will likely derail the recovery. Costs in 1Q were high but will likely taper off in succeeding periods, as (1) one-off IT-related and fund-raising transaction costs dissipate and (2) management executes its plan to reduce maintenance costs further. The company successfully raised P41bn from the issuance of convertibles and loans since March, which will be sufficient to support depressed operations for 24 months.

Domestic pax vols improve, QoQ drop in fares due to limited int’l travel

Pax vols improved QoQ, after domestic travel restrictions were lifted on Feb 26. Average fares declined QoQ due to (1) international travel caps imposed in March and (2) seasonality in international travel. The company pushed its domestic flights in 1Q, which have lower fares than international routes. Management noted that the international pax quota has been loosened but domestic travel remains limited to essential travel, under the latest quarantine status. Seat capacity has been reduced from 16% of pre-Covid levels in 1Q21, but is better than during the ramp-up stage in 3Q20 (6%).

Capex in 5M21 financed via leasebacks

The company took delivery of two aircraft in March and May, which were fully financed from cash generated from another round of sale-and-leaseback transactions. Management guided a fleet size of 74 aircraft by year-end, unchanged from end-1Q21 and end-4Q20. Moreover, the company’s monthly cash burn in 1Q21 stood at P1.8bn, higher than average (P1.5bn) due to the timing of debt and lease service payments (March and Sep).

Comments on competition

Management believes the PAL receivership will likely result in reduced international operations but it will likely keep most of its domestic capacity. Overall, management believes PAL will likely focus on profitability, which we believe should still bode well for overall competitive dynamics. Air Asia PH is only flying four of its twenty four fleet and still has not resolved its financial issues.

SECB NPLs improve 50bps on payments; but NIMs drop 65bps QoQ

Good news / bad news for SECB’s 1Q21, as its NPAT fell 43% YoY to P1.64bn. The good news is that NPLs dropped nearly 50bps QoQ to 3.51%, largely as accounts that were previously NPL turned current. This allowed provision expense to plunge 93% YoY to P402mn while still getting NPL cover to rise slightly to 118%. The bad news is that NIMs collapsed 65bps QoQ, as loan rates continued to compress while deposit costs have bottomed out. The bottom-line was also dragged down by a P1.2bn write-down of deferred tax assets (DTA) due to the CREATE Act. The improvement in asset quality is quite impressive. If the bank can keep NPLs under control despite the new lockdowns, it could be a major upside risk to our forecasts. The NIM compression though is slightly worrisome. With liquidity extremely high across the sector, competition could keep margins down for at least several quarters. Gross loans fell 5% YoY (net -6.6% YoY), with consumers -12%, and corporate/commercial loans -2%. Consumer loans accounted for 25% of total, the same ratio as at end-2020. Deposits grew 3% YoY, more surprisingly deposits were up 18% QoQ. The jump was driven by TDs, which jumped 45% QoQ. The funds were used to boost SECB’s investment portfolio (particularly AFS), as the bank seems to have taken advantage of rising rates at the long end of the curve. NII dropped 18% YoY and 8% QoQ, as NIMs plunged 88bps QoQ. Loan yields fell 63bps QoQ, while deposit costs flattened out (+8bps QoQ). The NIM compression was also due to the change in asset mix towards lower-yielding government bonds vs loans. Fee incomes were flat YoY in 1Q21 and are nearing pre-Covid levels. Trading/fx gains though collapsed 89% YoY as interest rates increased in Feb/March. Opex was flat, -2% YoY. Provision expense fell 93% YoY to just P402mn, owing to a sharp improvement in asset quality. SECB’s NPL ratio fell 49bps QoQ to 3.41% from 3.90% at end-2020. Management attributed the improvement primarily to accounts previously non-performing turning current (6 months of consistent payments), with some due to restructuring, and some to write-offs. Hence, despite the drop in provisions, NPL coverage still increased to 118% in 1Q21 (from 115% at end-2020). Tax expense for 1Q21 included P1.2bn write-off of DTAs due to the passage of the CREATE Act.

Macro News

GIR rises to US$107.25bn in end-April

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by US$2.77bn to US$107.25bn as of end-April 2021. This reflected inflows that were mainly from the proceeds of the National Government’s (NG) ROP Global and Samurai Bond issuances. The buffer is equivalent to 12.3 months’ worth of imports.

OFW remittances rise 4.9% YoY, 1.5% MoM in March

Remittances in March stood at US$2.51bn, up 4.9% YoY and 1.5% MoM. The 3-month moving average growth rate at +2.6% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances were up slightly (+0.1% YoY). This is a reversal from the 4.7% contraction in March 2020. Remittances from land-based OFWs sent US$1.9bn (+5% YoY) while sea-based remitted US$566mn (+4.5% YoY). This brings 1Q21 remittances up +2.6% YoY to US$7.6bn. The BSP expects remittances to rise by 4% this year, after dipping -0.8% in FY20. To reach the target, remittances would have to average US$2.6bn monthly for the balance of the year.

Consumer loan NPLs jump in Dec

In a VERY belated update, the BSP released data for consumer loans as of end-Dec 2020. Total consumer loans grew 4.6% YoY, down from 8.3% in 3Q20 and from 17.7% in 1Q20. This is the slowest YoY growth on our records, going back to 2002 (3Q03 was +5.3%). All segments decelerated, though auto loans actually contracted 8% YoY. NPLs increased across all major segments. Overall consumer NPLs stood at 9.33%, up from 8.51% in the previous quarter. Mortgage defaults were at 9.23% (from 8.37%), and Motor Vehicle NPLs were 10.68% (from 9.73%). The unsecured portion showed lower NPLs – credit cards at 8.85%, and salary loans at 6.60% – likely due to more aggressive write-offs by banks. Anecdotally, the four banks we cover in 1Q21 showed flat-to-lower NPLs QoQ on their consumer books. However, there was also some indication of renewed stress in April due to the new lockdowns (though the uptick seemed much milder). We hope the next data points won’t be this badly delayed…

COVID update - sharp improvement, but vaccines still lagging

In all the hubbub over results, what’s been lost is the big improvement in the Covid situation. The 7-day average case count has fallen below 6,000, from a peak of over 11,000. In terms of active cases, we’re down to about 56,000, well below the peak at nearly 180,000. Hospital occupancy rates for Covid beds are also sharply down from their highs. In late-March / early-April, we were hearing anecdotes of hospitals with Covid bed waiting lists that were over 100 names long. Vaccines are arriving in larger numbers. Thus far, about 2.9mn doses have been administered. Of this, some 680k people have received the 2nd dose and are fully vaccinated. True, the Covid and hospital numbers are still much higher than the Jan/Feb numbers. And the vaccine roll-out is still painfully slow. But directionally, all these trends are promising. The improving data has allowed the Philippine government to slowly ease movement restrictions even within the ECQ category. (It’s tempting to ignore the headline ECQ/GCQ status, as lines are getting blurry!) as of mid-May, mobility seems to have returned to levels from Jan/Feb. Curiously, our Asian peers are just now starting to limit mobility as Covid cases there are rising sharply.  

Pace of COVID vaccinations jumps

The 7-day average vaccinations improved to 108,540/day from an average of 36,491/day two weeks ago. The country has received a total of 7.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 3.3mn doses. The government expects at least 7mn vaccine doses to arrive in May (3.8mn of which have already arrived).  

BIR sets floor price for tobacco, vape products

The Bureau of Internal Revenue issued guidelines to determine floor prices of cigarette, heated and vapor products, serving as implementing rules and regulations for RA 11346. BIR determined the reference rates for taxation purposes in case there are no documents available on the actual price. A floor price of P78.40 will be imposed for cigarettes with production cost of P20, excise tax of P50 and VAT of P8.40, Floor price of P69.44 for heated tobacco with a production cost of P20, excise tax of P42 and VAT of P7.44, Floor price for of P125.44 for vapor products for every 0.7ml pod of nicotine salts and P421.12 for 2.5ml, Floor price for 10ml tank of freebase nicotine was pegged at P112, and P308mn for a 25ml tank. LT Group recently acknowledged that illicit trade has become more rampant following the string of price increases over the last two years.

Infrastructure spending jumps 57% MoM, 41% YoY in March

The government spent P87.7bn on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in March, up 57% MoM and 41% YoY. The government cited payment for completed and partially completed project of the Dept. of Public Works. This brings 1Q21 infra spend to P195.2bn, up 25% YoY. Bulk of the funds were spent on the construction of roads and foreign-assisted projects such as the Metro Manila Subway Project and the Davao City By-Pass Construction Project. The increase in infrastructure disbursements in 1Q21 of P39.1bn was enough to offset the P22.1bn reduction recorded in 1Q20. For FY21, the government is targeting to spend 35% more to P1.17tn on infrastructure (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies).

Philippines receives another 500,000 doses of Sinovac

The Philippines received another 500,000 doses of Sinovac vaccines. The country has received a total of 8.3mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 3.3mn doses. The government expects at least 7mn vaccine doses to arrive in May (4.3mn of which have already arrived). The country aims to vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.
