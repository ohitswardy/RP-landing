REGIS REPORT

26 February 2021

The PSEi dropped 1.9% WoW to close below the 6800 level as a bevy of factors weighed on sentiment.

President Duterte rejected proposals to loosen the country’s lockdown status to “Modified General Community Quarantine” (MGCQ). This drove further concern on the country’s  tenuous path to recovery, as restrictions on transportation and mobility have hampered reopening.

Coupled with inflation concerns, the rate of the 10-year benchmark moved up was 3.588% (as of 24 Feb), up  some 30bps for the week.  The PHP has been weak as well, dropping 0.3% for the week to PHP48.585/US$.

Foreign funds continued to flow out, with net outflows for the week reaching US$87.1m. The MSCI rebal effective today was expected to result in outflows of US$40m but net outflows at the close were at US$20m.  Decliners outnumbered gainers 2:1.

The top traded stock for the week was DITO, accounting for 19% of  turnover.   Of the top ten most active names, 4 are heavy retails names.

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) approved Thursday the application of Meralco (-3.9% WoW) to refund to its customers P13.886bn, representing the amount it has collected in excess of the previously approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.381/kWh since Jul 2015. As the company has  more than fully provided for the amount, there will be no direct impact on the P&L. Moreover, an affirmation of the finality of the IAR could be positive for Meralco as the company has been setting aside annual provisions of least P10bn should rates be cut retroactively. This could mean a potential reversal of massive provisions (could be >P60bn) as well as higher prospective earnings.

Meanwhile, Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) will address the issue of the safeguard duty by asking for a refundable deposit from buyers beginning March 1. The deposits (P78k or P123K per vehicle) will have to be paid upfront and will be refunded, in theory, should the ruling be reversed. Regis expects this deposit to be a major deterrent for new buyers of the 50% of TMP’s model line-up. GTCAP (-1.8% WoW) fell this week.

Ayala Land (+0.1% WoW) announced its plan to fully take over its listed subsidiary Cebu Holdings (CHI PM). ALI will undertake a share swap with CHI, giving 0.19 ALI shares for each CHI share with the aim of swapping the 30% minority shareholders in CHI.

The company’s 4Q20 net profit fell 76% YoY to P2.4bn. While FY20 profit of P8.7bn (-74%) was behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, operating profit was in line as faster construction boosted 4Q20 residential revenue.

FY20 profits of BDO (-2.8% WoW) came in at P28.2bn, down 36% YoY but slightly ahead of forecast. NPLs crept up to 2.65%. Provisions of P30bn brought NPL coverage to 105%.

Ayala Land - 4Q20 office vacancy rises to 12%; ALI expects segments to recover in 2-3 years
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

4Q20 profit falls 76%

Ayala Land’s 4Q20 net profit fell 76% YoY to P2.4bn. This brought the FY20 figure down 74% to P8.7bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The underperformance was driven by items such as interest, taxes, etc. Operating profit was in line as faster construction boosted 4Q20 residential revenue, which in turn offset weak mall revenue and overall margins.

Reservation sales supported by suburban projects

4Q20 presales dropped 44% YoY (6% QoQ) to P21.1bn. Similar to 3Q20, suburban developments (outside Manila) generally sold better and there are still no substantial increases in defaults. FY20 presales fell 44% (to P81.9bn) while unsold inventory rose to 22 months’ worth of presales (from 20 months in 2019).

 

Office vacancy rises to 12% as POGOs close

Office vacancy increased 7ppt QoQ to 12% due to 5% floor area expansion as well as closures of POGOs, which still occupy 5% of the portfolio. Meanwhile, BPOs continue to sign up for space and we estimate that 50% of the 2021 pipeline has been preleased, better than the industry average of 30%.

Mall foot traffic improves further but…

Mall revenue grew 10% QoQ as foot traffic improved from c. 35% to c. 45% (of pre-COVID levels) while sales of tenants (that are open) increased from 41% to 53%. However, revenue was still down 76% YoY due to rental concessions. In January 2021, the company started charging minimum rents (at 10% of preCOVID levels). However, further increases will likely take time since limitations on public transportation are hampering the recovery of consumption spending.

Expecting full recovery in 2-3 years

Management believes it will take 2-3 years to achieve the 2019 performance again. This is comparable to our own expectations that every segment will not return to 2019 levels of revenue/sales by 2022.

 
PUBLISH RESEARCH
GT Capital - Pay now, get a refund later (fingers crossed)
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar Research +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Toyota PH to collect conditional cash deposit for select models by March 1

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) announced that it will be collecting conditional cash deposits on select models beginning March 1 in line with the implementation of the provisional safeguard duty (see Figure 1 below). VATinclusive cash deposits of P78,400 and P123,400 will be collected upon receipt of down payments, which will be held in trust for the customer until the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) issues a definitive duty rate. If the DTI decides in favor of a duty, TMP will treat the deposit as an additional payment for the vehicle. Otherwise, the government will issue a refund that TMP or the dealer will hand back to the buyers. We estimate models affected by the safeguard duty comprise half of TMP’s unit sales. We also estimate the cash deposits would effectively raise retail prices of these affected models by +4% to +12%. More crucially, it also represents a large increase (up to 50%) in the initial cash outlay or down payment for any buyers buying with a bank loan.

Cash deposit vs. outright price adjustment

The government began implementing the provisional safeguard duty last Feb 1 and this will be valid for 200 days or until the DTI makes a final decision based on the Tariff Commission’s recommendation. Some vehicle distributors have already announced price increases as a result. TMP appears to have been the first firm to handle the situation by asking for a returnable cash deposit. We are not fully convinced that TMP’s approach is better than an outright price hike because of what it might mean for the upfront cash payment.

Near-term disruption

As discussed in our report dated 8 January, 2021 (see Government slapping safeguard duty on imported cars), we expect some market disruptions nearterm, with buyers likely to speed up purchases before these adjustments. Unit sales are likely to drop off immediately after. TMP expects to sell 104k-108k units (+4-8% YoY) this year, lower than its previous estimate (130k, +30% YoY) and our own forecast (117k, +17% YoY). Assuming TMP’s forecasts, we estimate our FY21 profit forecast for TMP will drop by 10-14%, which should translate to a 4-5% decline in GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) profit. GTCAP’s share price has dropped 8% since the announcement of the import duty and is now trading at a 16% discount to its market-based NAV.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Manila Electric Company - No P&L impact from P14bn refund; bigger issue still pending
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Two-year refund of P13,9bn has been provided for

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has approved Meralco’s proposal to proceed with a refund of P13.886bn (US$290m) to its customers. The amount represents the difference between (a) Meralco‘s actual tariffs from July 2015 to Nov 2020 and (b) the previously approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.381/kwh. Meralco has more than fully provided for the refund so it will have no direct P&L impact. What the ERC has yet to resolve is whether the IAR will stand as the “final” tariff for the period from July 2015. The resolution of this matter could have an outsized positive impact on Meralco.

Approving the P13.886bn refund proposed by Meralco

The ERC has given approval for Meralco to proceed with the refund of tariff collections in excess of the previously approved interim rate (IAR) of P1.381/kwh for the period beginning July 2015. The amount of P13.886bn is more than fully provided for and should thus have no direct impact on Meralco’s income. In fact, we estimate Meralco is likely to have already set aside over P70bn in provisions relating to these tariff “over-recoveries”.

The bigger issue is still pending

What the ERC has yet to decide on is whether it will affirm the IAR of P1.381/kwh as the “final” tariff for the period beginning July 2015. As those who watched the industry are very much aware, the ERC has been unable to conduct its rate reviews and set a final tariff since 2015. In the meantime, Meralco has been setting aside provisions as if tariffs will be cut as much as 20% (estimate) retroactive to July 2015.

Possible two-fold impact could present significant upside potential

Affirming the IAR as “final” would have a two-fold impact. First, it would remove the risk of a retroactive reduction in Meralco rates and allow the company to reverse all the tariff-related provisions it’s set aside. The one-time impact could be large as P60bn, pretax. Second, the affirmation of the IAR could also mean Meralco would no longer need to set aside over P10bn in provisions annually. This could raise prospective earnings by over 30%. Our current valuation (and target price) assumes Meralco’s tariff will be cut by ~20%, retroactive to July 2015. Thus, any regulatory decision removing that risk could mean a huge win.

 
PUBLISHED RESAECH
Jollibee Foods Corp. - The winding road to recovery; notes from post-results call with CFO
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

The winding road to recovery

We hosted JFC’s Chief Financial Officer, Mr. Ysmael Baysa, to provide more insight on the company’s latest results, and its outlook for the year ahead. JFC is likely to perform better in 2021, though the road to recovery is long, and will likely be met with headwinds. While most markets outside the Philippines have recovered nearly to pre-pandemic levels, lockdown restrictions in its core market will continue to weigh on overall performance. Moreover, rising input cost inflation could pressure margins even as cost savings from its business transformation (BT) in 2020 materialize, and losses from US businesses (Smashburger and CBTL) are reduced.

Protracted Philippine recovery to drag overall performance

The Philippine business (c.60% of revenues) was the main underperformer in 2020, and will likely continue to underperform in 2021 as lockdown measures continue to limit mobility. Even after rationalization efforts, management does not expect the Philippines to return to pre-pandemic sales and margins until mid-2022

International business to carry more weight

Most of JFC’s international brands have returned to or are approaching prepandemic sales, and should see a more meaningful improvement in margins by 2021. Smashburger and CBTL already broke even as of 4Q20 following the business transformation, and should contribute positively to the bottom line in 2021.

2021 guidance dependent on easing lockdown, many factors; maintaining Hold

Notwithstanding the positive trends on the international businesses, management remains cautious in 2021 given that its core market continues to be under pressure. Guidance of P6.4bn profit in 2021 is contingent on a gradual recovery of the Philippine business which may be upset if lockdown restrictions remain tight and are imposed for longer. Until lockdown restrictions (e.g. on public transport) are further eased, the Philippine business will struggle to recover. Cost pressures could also play an outsized role this year as price increases have to be carefully weighed amidst the fragile consumer environment. While we expect earnings to return to and eventually even exceed pre-pandemic levels of profitability, the road there looks longer than investors might anticipate, with near-term risks of disappointment. We maintain our Hold rating.

 
 

Corporate News

NTC declares Dito compliant in its first technical audit

The National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) declared the new operator Dito Telecommunity compliant in its first review. Dito committed to reach 37% population coverage and average wireless data speed of 27mbps under conditions of the bidding conducted in 2018 by Jan 7, 2021. The audit was moved from July 2020 due to pandemic. The technical audit supervised by the NTC showed Dito achieved population coverage of 37.48%.  Speeds supposedly reached a minimum average of 85.9mbps and 507.5mbps. Note thought that Dito has no subscribers yet so the speeds are not representative of real world conditions.

Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) to open 30 stores in 2021

PIZZA announced it plans to open 30 stores across its two brands in 2021. It would be 15 stores each for Shakey’s Pizza and Peri-Peri Chicken. PIZZA had 245 Shakey’s and 34 Peri-Peri stores as of end-2020. The total capex for 2021 is set at P400-450mn (Regis FY21E: P350mn), inclusive of new store openings, renovations, and other projects. The company is set to open smaller store formats, which will translate to reduced investment requirements, shorter payback periods, while keeping returns high. It will also be unveiling a number of ghost kitchens to further strengthen its delivery business.

Dito to begin offering services in March in Vis-Min

New telco Dito plans to begin offering services in 17 cities in Visayas and Mindanao by March 8. It will be the first phase of the firm’s commercial rollout. The nationwide launch is planned by June. The telecom regulator recently announced that Dito passed its technical audit with population coverage of 37.48%. We expect minimal impact on existing impact on the existing telcos this year and have assumed Dito will capture 10% share of market revenue by 2024.

Cebu Holdings to merge with parent Ayala Land

Ayala Land will issue 0.19 ALI shares for every CHI share. At ALI’s previously closing price of P38.20/share, the ratio would price CHI at P7.25/share. Cebu Holdings said this ratio was based on the NAV of ALI and CHI. Since Ayala Land already owns 71% of Cebu Holdings, this transaction will only increase ALI’s total shares outstanding by 0.8%. We estimate that Cebu Holdings accounted for 0.4% of Ayala Land’s 9M20 net profit. This merger should reduce a layer of bureaucracy.

Notes from CEB briefing

The company reiterated initiatives on lowering costs, including (a) right-sizing manpower, (b) streamlining maintenance costs, (c) maximizing use of their more fuel-efficient aircraft (i.e. NEOs).  Note that the company expects costs per ASK to drop to 2.30 centavos in 2022 and 2.15 centavos in 2023 from 2.46 centavos in 2019.  These are c. 22% and 9% lower than our estimate of 2.95 centavos in 2022 and 2.35 centavos in 2023, respectively. Initiatives to kick start travel. They emphasized the push to “test before boarding” (i.e. antigen tests 5 hours before travel from Manila) to address transmission risks concerns of most local governments. Note that at P700 (US$14), CEB’s antigen tests are significantly lower than market prices (P2k and up). Some local governments have opened up to this as a pre-travel requirement (eg. General Santos, Butuan, Dipolog, and Pagadian) and CEB continues to work with other LGUs to allow antigen tests instead of the more expensive RT-PCR test. Management cited IATA’s 2023/2024 global recovery for intercontinental/transcontinental travel. However, management believes domestic travel will revert to pre-Covid levels faster than international, citing the recent experience of China, Vietnam, and Thailand.  Note that our forecast broadly assumes this outlook (i.e. 2022 domestic pax will match 2019 levels; international will be at 40% of pre-Covid).  Note that the government has allowed airlines to be exempted from the 1-meter distancing rule so CEB can maximize its seat capacity. On another note, the CPS are immediately convertible after (a) the company can obtain PSE approval for listing of the underlying common shares to be issued upon conversion of the CPS or (b) 40 days from listing (tentatively on March 29), whichever is earlier. The CPS will be tradeable in the PSE on 29 March. JG Summit will fully underwrite the CPS stock rights offer. Management noted that they are still negotiating with third party investors for its planned US$250mn convertible bond (CB) issuance. They reiterated that they are mindful about the dilutive impact of such issuance to minority shareholders so any financing they come up with will definitely consider this. Note that based on our conversations with management, they initially planned for the prices of the CB and the CPS to be similar. Moreover, they emphasized that there are other fundraising plans they are looking at, including aircraft sales, which are all ongoing.

Sky Fiber adjusts broadband plan speeds

Sky Fiber announced upgraded broadband speeds for their plans. The key change is on their entry level plans where Sky Fiber increased the speed for its P999 plant to 20mbps (from 10mbps) and for its P1499 plan to 40mbps (vs 25mbps). The new speeds/plans now offer better value vs. Converge’s.  The main constraint for Sky, however, is likely to be coverage.  The most recent estimate is Sky Fiber had around 300k subscribers (vs 1m for Converge ICT).

San Miguel submits lowest bids to supply Meralco

Two of San Miguel’s (SMC PM) subsidiaries submitted the lowest prices to supply 1,800MW of power to Meralco. Excellent Energy Resources, Inc (EERI) submitted a bid of P4.1462/kwh (levelized cost of energy) for 1,200MW of power. Additionally, Masinloc Power Partners Co Ltd (MPPCL) gave a bid of P4.2605/kwh LCOE. These bids are lower than Meralco’s own Atimonan coal plant and both firms are subsidiaries of SMC. EERI will supply 1,200MW from a LNG-fired facility beginning Dec 2024 for 20 years. MPPCL will supply 600MW from a coal plant beginning May 2025 for 20 years. Both firms are supposed to provide power from new facilities but can provide replacement power from other sources should the new plants not be available by committed date. We believe it is highly unlikely that the new facilities will be able to begin operations by that target date of the contracts. We remain of the view that there are likely to be no significant baseload capacity additions by 2025 in Luzon after GN Power Dinginin.

BDO NPAT P28.2bn, in line; NPLs jump to 2.65%, cover down to 105%

BDO reported headline FY2020 NPAT of P28.2bn, slightly ahead of Regis (P26.9bn) and consensus (P27.3bn). Details of the press release were scant, but it seems the bank booked some trading gains in 4Q20, which it used to bump up provisions to P6.4bn in 4Q, taking the full year to P30.2bn. NPLs jumped to 2.65% from 1.95% in 3Q20, and NPL cover fell to 105% from 138% in 3Q, despite the 4Q provision. Asset quality could continue to be an overhang into 2021E, considering the size of BDO’s restructured loan (RL) book (was 3% in 3Q, not yet disclosed for FY20). Some highlights from the press release. Loan growth was 3% YoY, down from 6% in 3Q20. Of the Big Three, only BDO has posted full-year growth in loans. No comments re NIMs or deposit growth, though they did say that CASA grew 17% YoY. Non-interest income fell 8%, with “volume-related” business still down due to the pandemic. That said, the 4Q20 alone looked robust at P18.4bn, up 54% QoQ and 12% YoY. The bank probably booked some trading gains. Recall that BDO is unique among banks in that they are more geared to equity markets through their insurance subsidiary BDO Life. The PSEi Index was up around 25% QoQ in 4Q20. Opex was down 2% YoY, likely helped by rental concessions in its mall-based branches. Advertising and marketing expenses were also significantly lower. Provision expense was P30.2bn for FY20, with 4Q alone at P6.4bn. Despite this, NPL coverage fell to 105% from 138% in 3Q. This was due to a jump in NPLs to 2.65%, from 1.95% in 3Q20, which suggests new NPL formation was at least P16bn for the quarter alone. While the headline NPL number is not alarming – BPI was 2.68% and MBT was 2.41% -- we note that BDO had the highest level of RLs at 3% in 3Q20, and the bank was guiding for 5% RLs for FY. For perspective, BPI’s end-2020 RL was 1.1% while MBT’s was 0.4%.

Cebu Pacific expects to take delivery of eight aircraft this year

Cebu Air is expecting to take delivery of eight more aircraft this year according to VP for marketing and customer service, Candice Iyog. Management guiding fleet size to be reduced to 68 this year from 75 in 2020.

 
 

Macro News

NEDA to review GDP goal

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) will have to reassess its GDP target for this year, amid another delay in fully reopening the economy. The government is currently targeting the economy to recover by +6.5% to +7.5% this year. This is premised on: shift to a modified general community quarantine (MGCQ), implementation of the recovery program, and timely COVID vaccination. Regis FY21 GDP forecast: +7%; Consensus: +7.5%.

Senate passes indemnity fund bill

The Senate passed on 2nd and 3rd reading, Senate Bill (SB) 2057, which proposes the creation of a P500mn COVID-19 National Vaccine Indemnity fund. It will be administered by Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) and will be sourced from the Contingent Fund under the 2021 national budget. The bill will be transmitted to a bicameral conference committee.

Duterte defers nationwide shift to MGCQ until vaccinations begin

President Rodrigo Duterte has rejected a proposal to put the entire country to the lowest quarantine status, Modified General Community Quarantine (MGCQ). President wants the vaccine to be rolled out first before easing restrictions. Duterte also rejected the renewed proposal to allow limited in-person classes in low-risk areas. The coronavirus task force had recommended that NCR and 13 other provinces join the rest of the Philippines under MGCQ starting March.

Sinovac’s COVID-19 vaccine receives emergency use authorization

The Philippine Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has granted Sinovac’s COVID-19 vaccine an emergency use authorization. The FDA recommended its use among healthy 18 to 59 year old individuals, but not healthcare workers. The delivery of 600,000 Sinovac vaccines could arrive in late February or early March. Other than Sinovac, only Pfizer and AstraZeneca have secured EUAs from the FDA.

T-bill rates rise

The Bureau of the Treasury raised P20bn from total tenders of P50bn. 91-day T-bill rose by 3bps to 0.875% (BVAL: 0.8504%); 182-day T-bill rose by 2.1bps to 1.067% (BVAL: 1.0747%); 364-day T-bill rose by 11.1bps to 1.527% (BVAL: 1.5187%). The BTr indicated that the moderate rise in rates reflected a correction in relation to rising US Treasury yields.

Cebu removes swab tests for all tourists

Cebu Gov. Gwendolyn Garcia issued Executive Order 12, eliminating swab tests (antigen, rapid, or RT-PCR) as a pre-travel requirement for tourists beginning Feb 23. Tourists only need a valid medical certificate issued within 7 days to travel to Cebu province and show proof of pre-booking at the hotel or resort. This should bode well for Megawide’s Mactan airport unit. Domestic accounted for 66% and 46% of Mactan airport’s total pax volumes and PSC revenues in 2019, respectively. Mactan airport accounted for 43% of Megawide’s core profit after minorities in 2019.

BSP names new deputy governor

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas named Mr. Mamerto Tangonan as its fourth deputy governor. Deputy Gov. Tangonan now heads the BSP’s Payments and Currency Management Sector. Mr. Tangonan is a digital financial services expert with over 20 years of experience.

BOP posts US$752mn deficit in January

The country’s balance of payments swung to a deficit of US$752mn in January. This is the first deficit after 11 months of surplus but was 44.7% YoY lower than the US$1.355bn deficit in January 2020. This also reflected the 1.19% MoM decrease in gross international reserves (GIR) to US$108.67bn in end-January (equivalent to 11.6 months’ worth of imports). The BSP sees the BOP surplus narrowing to US$3.3bn this year (equivalent to 0.8% of GDP). In 2020, the BOP posted a surplus of US$16.02bn.
