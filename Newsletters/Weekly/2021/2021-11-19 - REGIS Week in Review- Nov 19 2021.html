REGIS REPORT

19 November 2021

The PSEi dipped this week (-1.4% WoW) to close at 7280. The index was dragged by CNVRG (-12% WoW) JGS (-5% WoW) and BDO (-3.4% WoW) while only GLO (+5.8% WoW) and GTCAP (+2.0% WoW) had significant gains among the index names. Net foreign reversed from an inflow of +$17.1m last week to an outflow of -$33.9m this week. This brings the total net outflow for the year to $1.7bn.

Converge ICT was down big this week because Warburg Pincus, sold 420m shares of the company in an overnight placement. The sale reduced its stake in CNVRG to 10.2% from 15.8%. The deal was done at P30/share which was at the bottom of the indicative price range (P30-P31/share) and was a 7.2% discount to the prior day’s close. The deal was apparently upsized by 18% despite being priced at the bottom of the range.

Meanwhile, GTCAP released earnings this week. GT Capital’s (+2.0% WoW) 9M21 profit reached P7.6bn, implying 3Q21 profit dropped QoQ to P2.1bn. While the 9M21 figure is trending ahead of forecast, the 3Q21 performance was quite poor for the auto business, which can be attributed to the lockdown. All other key segments fared well, including the bank.

First Gen Corp (-2.3% WoW) also reported this week and said 3Q recurring income declined 9% QoQ due to the impact of an outage of 1 unit in Avion, with attributable earnings from nat gas declining 9% QoQ. The 9M recurring income after pref divs appears broadly in line with our FY21E of $262mn.

Vaccinations have recently been doing better and it was reported that the daily vaccination rate for the week ended Nov 14 (Sunday) was 788k, up from 697k the previous week. At this latest pace and assuming the 3-day vaccination holiday is implemented successfully, the country should be able to reach over 50% vaccination by year-end.

To add to that, our mobility continues to improve, following the lowering of restrictions to “Alert Level 2”. Google Mobility data showed new Covid-19 highs for the three sectors we watch most closely. Trips made to retail/recreation establishments are now just 3% below 2019 levels nation-wide, and just 11% down for Metro Manila. For transit stations, mobility is now down to just 25-30% below pre-Covid levels. And people coming back to the workplace is now just 15-25% below “normal”.

To add to this, Cebu Air (+1.0% WoW) has increased flight frequencies for six domestic destinations, including tourist island Boracay, in a sign that domestic tourism is coming back. Cebu Air CEO Lance Gokongwei was quoted as saying capacity for domestic destinations is at 40% of pre-pandemic levels and is likely to hit 50% by next month.

LT Group - 3Q profit rebounds on lower bank provisions
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q21 rebounds to P6.2bn on lower bank provisions

LTG rebounded from a loss of P2.757bn in 2Q21 to a profit of P6.215bn in 3Q21 (+2%YoY), as PNB booked lower provisions in the quarter. Its tobacco unit’s contribution, meanwhile, grew 8%QoQ on higher volumes. LTG’s 9M21 profit of P9.945bn (-38%YoY) is tracking behind Regis and consensus estimates for FY21E.

Tobacco profits +8%QoQ on back of volume recovery; no price hike yet

Cigarette sales improved to 8.9bn sticks (+5%QoQ), as its market share appeared to have stabilized vs competitors, postings its first uptick after 7 quarters (61.8% in 3Q21 vs 61.6% in 2Q21). PMFTC continues to push Chesterfield (P5/stick) as a means to regain lost share to mid-priced offerings from competition. The tobacco segment’s profit contribution grew 8%QoQ to P4.3bn, while 9M21 came in at P13.3bn (+9%YoY), just midly behind our FY21 estimates. Notably, PMFTC has not yet increased prices ahead of the 2022 tax increase. Over the last two years, the price hikes came a few months before year-end, resulting in a sharp jump in margins in 4Q19/4Q20.

Bank profits recover on lower provisions; gross NPLs climb improve to 10.8%

PNB’s profit contribution recovered to P1.26bn in 3Q, coming from a P13.3bn loss in 2Q without the revaluation of properties under PNB Holdings. Provisions for the period declined to P1.4bn from P16.9bn in 2Q21. Coverage improved to 64% (from 60% in 2Q), while gross NPLs dipped to 10.8% (from 11.47% in 2Q). Management noted that they sold a significant portfolio of NPLs during the period, and will continue to pursue this as a strategy to reduce NPLs further.

Liquor volumes jump 17%QoQ; ABI’s profits contract on lower margins

Income from Tanduay grew 7%QoQ to P396mn in 3Q as volumes jumped 17%QoQ to 6.3mn cases. This was tempered by midly lower GPMs (13.9%vs 14.7%) arising from higher production costs. On the other hand, ABI’s profits fell 48%QoQ to P68mn. We estimate GPMs contracted more than 5ppt in 3Q21. Management cited a shift in sales mix (lower contribution of water) among the reasons for the decline, but we suspect other costs may have also added pressure.

 
GT Capital - Lockdown weighs on auto unit’s 3Q21
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

3Q21 profit falls QoQ

9M21 core profit to common rose 131% to P7.6bn, trailing consensus forecasts. Implied 3Q21 profit dropped sequentially to P2.1bn, as the lockdown had a more severe impact on the performance of the auto unit. This overshadowed the profit improvements at the bank as provision expenses fell.

Lockdown affects 3Q21 profit of auto unit

The auto segment’s 3Q21 profit dropped 23% QoQ, as retail sales volumes were more heavily affected by the lockdown during the quarter. Despite sequentially better volume mix, earnings were weighed down by (a) unfavorable FX, (b) the two-week plant shutdown in August and (c) sustained discounts/subsidies to dealerships, as well as higher promotions. The good news is that the company received P958mn worth of tax credits under the CARS program this month, which could be used to offset excise and/or income taxes. Management expects Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) to raise prices if necessary to protect profitability, even at the expense of market share. TMP’s market share is now at 46%, up from 40% in 2019, thanks in large part to Toyota Financial (TFS) taking a larger share of auto loans. TFS’ NPL ratio in 9M21 also improved to 3.5% from 3.66% in 1H21.

Bank 3Q21 profit rises 131% YoY on lower provisions; Asset quality improves

The bank reported a 131% YoY jump in 3Q21 profit, after provision expense dropped. Asset quality was better than peers, with NPL ratio falling to 2.1% (from 2.3% in 2Q), NPL coverage jumping to 191% (from 179%), and restructured loans (RL) steady at 0.8%. This was, to some extent, at the expense of lending, however, with loans (-4% YoY) lagging peers.

Property: 3Q profit jumps on higher margins; Reservation sales lower QoQ

Implied 3Q21 profit was higher QoQ, as margins normalized. 3Q21 reservation sales halved QoQ to P1.4bn, after sales were heavily affected by the lockdown. After deferring new launches since the pandemic started, the company plans to launch new projects next year, including a portion in Cavite.

 
Vista Land - Residential revenue continues to drop
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M21 earnings disappoint on residential revenue

Despite a sharp drop in residential revenue, 3Q21 earnings rose 6% YoY (23% QoQ) to P2.0bn, thanks to lower interest expense. 9M21 net profit of P5.7bn (+7%) is behind REGIS forecast, as residential revenue is down 20%. Furthermore, we believe the actual performance of leasing operations is not as rosy as the reported 25% increase in 9M21 rental revenue suggests.

Residential revenue falls, likely curtailed by sales cancellations

After dropping in 2Q21, Vista’s residential revenue fell another 31% QoQ (down 36% YoY). Management said this was due to construction constraints caused by weather disruptions as well as the hard lockdown. Income from forfeitures also suggests that sales cancellations (which would have caused a reversal of previously recognized revenue) were a major reason for the drop.

Reservation sales grow despite hard lockdown

Despite the hard lockdown, 3Q21 reservation sales rose 9% QoQ (5% YoY) to P14.5bn, 74% of the 2019 average. 9M21 reservation sales of P44bn (+11%), is in line with our forecast. Similar to previous years, OFWs comprised c. 55% of sales, the Camella brand accounted for c. 80% of sales, and default rates remained at single digit levels.

9M21 rental revenue growth of 25% overstates cash rents

Vista’s 9M21 rental revenue, which is largely comprised of malls, jumped 25% to P 6.2bn, 20% higher than the 9M19 figure. This is a stark contrast to other mall operators, whose mall revenues are about 60% lower than the 9M19 levels.

While anchor tenants AllDay (ALLDY PM) and AllHome (HOME PM) each achieved 22% sales growth, we believe that Vista’s rental revenue is significantly overstated relative to cash rents. Rental receivables on the balance sheet grew to P13.6bn, 160% of trailing 12-month rental revenue.

Megawide: Recovery stalls in 3Q

3Q21 net loss widens to P345mn – Behind Regis and street estimates

MWIDE reported profit after minorities and prefs of P460mn in 9M21, implying 3Q21 net loss alone was wider QoQ at P345mn. The results were weaker than expected, as the construction unit fared poorly during the quarter while higher interest costs pulled down profit for the airport unit. Meanwhile, management believes that the anti-dummy law allegation does not have merit and is unlikely to stop them from winning government bids in the future.

 

Construction: Delay affects 3Q

The construction unit incurred a net loss in 3Q, after experiencing delays in one project due to potential revisions. Management, however, noted that the changes will lead to an increase in the order book. While the company has seen significant increases in steel and re-bar costs, it believes these increases are manageable. The order book dropped further to P60bn but was still 15% higher than end-2019. Management also indicated it won the bid for one package of the Subway project, which will likely add c. P8-10bn to its order book by 4Q.

 

Airport unit still incurring losses; EBITDA break-even guidance

EBITDA loss for the airport unit widened by 18% QoQ, even though volumes and revenues improved. With travel restrictions eased, pax volumes have already improved to 6k per day today from only 3k per day in 3Q. Management estimates its EBITDA break-even point at c. 10k pax volumes per day.

 

Landport unit revenues drop QoQ but lower cash costs lift profits

Profits from the landport unit improved 73% QoQ on the back of lower costs. However, the downward trajectory of office and retail revenues has not abated.

Notes from JFC 3Q/9M21 results briefing

Business Transformation (BT) cost savings to mitigate input cost pressure. Higher input cost and heightened labor cost in the US were the main factors that led to margin contraction in 3Q. The cost pressures are expected to persist over the near term, and the company will likely have to implement another round of price increases this quarter. In addition, management is optimistic that the fixed cost savings from its BT program (P2-3bn annually) could also help mitigate any near-term cost pressure.

 

Smashburger loss widens, CBTL at break even. Given elevated labor cost in the US, Smashburger’s operating losses widened to P200-250mn in 3Q by our estimate (1H21: cP300mn op loss). Underlying trends, however, are encouraging with Smashburger posting +15% SSSG. Franchised stores are also now outpacing the growth of company owned stores. The main drag to profitability continues to be pre-operating expenses for new stores, but management claims that majority of its network is already profitable at the store level. Meanwhile, CBTL’s operations continue to improve and has already hit break even at the operating level in 3Q (1H21: cP100mn op loss).

 

The company is seeing a sequential improvement in the Philippines as lockdown restrictions have eased. At the peak of the lockdowns in 3Q, sales had fallen to nearly 30% below pre-Covid levels. As of Oct-Nov, sales have been tracking c20% below pre-Covid. Management expects a full recovery by 3Q22, barring another hard lockdown.

 

Management expects to accelerate new store openings to 500 stores in 2022 (net new stores = 350-400), further ramping up to c600 stores annually (gross) from 2023 onwards. A larger proportion of the new stores will be overseas and only around 100 new stores will be in the Philippines. 

 

Management did not provide any guidance but indicated that they hope to achieve net margin of 6-8% in the near term. Key margin drivers will be:

Better margins in the Phils vs historical levels (c.6%) due to lower fixed cost resulting from the BT; Improving profitability of Smashburger and CBTL, which are currently generating above average operating margins at the store level. The drag from pre-operating expenses is expected to normalize in 2-3 years as sales of new stores ramp up; Higher proportion of franchised stores – JFC is targeting to increase the proportion of franchised stores to 2/3 of its network from c50% currently; Increased contribution from the overseas business, which generally yields higher margin. Management noted that its new investments (Tim Ho Wan, Milksha tea) have better store economics than some of its current brands.

SECB 9M21 behind consensus; NPLs rise, cover falls to 91%

An overall soft set of results from SECB, as NPAT for 9M21 came in at P4.83bn, behind consensus (just 67% of P7.24bn for FY21E). While the bottomline was ahead of Regis estimates, we note that 9M PPOP was behind. To the upside, loans were flat YoY, improving from the -5.4% in 2Q21, and NIMs inched up 5bps QoQ. However, asset quality was the big disappointment. NPL ratio was up 22bps QoQ to 4.15%. Despite this, provision expense was actually lower QoQ to just P1.65bn, which tool NPL Cover down to 91%.

 

Some highlights:

Loans were flat YoY, an improvement over the -5.4% YoY in 2Q21. On a QoQ basis, loans were up 1.2%; Consumer loans were still down 12% YoY, but still accounted for 25% of total loans; NIMs crept up 5bps QoQ to 4.12%. This was despite a 17bps QoQ drop in loan yields, and an 11bps increase in deposit costs. SECB switched from expensive wholesale funding (bills payable) to less-expensive time deposits. Total deposits were up 20% YoY; Fees were up 24% YoY, and are already back above pre-Covid levels. Trading/fx gains were down 92%, given the extremely high base in 2020; Opex was flat, up just 2% YoY; Provision expense was P4.1bn for 9M21, down 81% YoY. For 3Q21 alone, provisions fell 84% YoY and 19% QoQ to P1.65bn. This was despite a deterioration in NPL ratio to 4.15%, up 22bps QoQ. The low provision charge took NPL coverage down to 91%, down from 103% in 2Q21.

Ayala Corp 3Q results

Ayala Corp reported net income of P19.4bn for 9M, +70% YoY.  For 3Q alone, net income was P9.0bn, up 2.6x YoY as the base was low the prior year and 3Q this year was lifted by P3.5bn in gains from the partial sale of its coal-fired power plant (GN Power Kauswagan).

 

Comparing 3Q21 vs 2Q21, profits appear to have risen sharply (P9.0bn vs P5.0bn). Upon closer inspection, earnings from the underlying operations were actually largely unchanged QoQ after exceptional items are excluded. There was a mark-to-market loss relating to its Manila Water stake in 2Q. Earnings from the key subsidiaries (ALI, GLO, BPI) were largely flat or down QoQ.

 

YT-Sept losses at AC Industrials amounted to P1.12bn, which is an improvement from the P2.06bn loss the prior year.  For 3Q alone, however, losses were P532m.  This was worse both on a QoQ and a YoY basis.

 

AC disclosed it was acquiring for P6.06bn a 60% stake in logistics group Air21. Air21 has operations in express door-to-door delivery, warehousing and transport, freight forwarding and waste logistics and management. The deal is targeted for closing within 1Q22.

 

AC disclosed that Air21’s “attributable” revenue, EBITDA and net income for FY20 were P3bn, P815m and P230m, respectively. Factoring in around P800m to P1bn of net debt, AC’s acquisition price thus implies a multiple of 3.6x sales, 13x EBITDA and 44x PER on a trailing basis. The EBITDA multiple looks reasonable at first glance considering the control premium and the fact they are trailing multiples for what is likely to be fast-growing business.

 

AC sold 32% of its 71% beneficial interest in GN Power Kauswagan at a valuation equivalent to US$453m for 100%. The gain reported on the transaction was P3.5bn (US$70m), inclusive of the revaluation gain of its residual stake. AC expects the full divestment of GNPK to be completed over a period as long as ten years.

 

AC invested P16.9bn so far this year, with P10.1bn of that amount going into the purchase of ALI shares. AC increased its stake in ALI by 1.9ppt YTD.

FGEN 9M recurring income +11% to $212mn

FGEN’s 9M21 recurring net income reached $212mn (+11%YoY) driven by higher electricity sales, lower interest expense and taxes. The implied 3Q recurring income of $64mn is lower by 9%QoQ. After preferred dividends, we estimate 9M21 core income was $206, mildly ahead of our FY21E of $262mn. Nat gas earnings grew 16% in 9M21 due to higher electricity sales from Avion in the earlier part of the year. 2Q21 attributable income from nat gas declined 9%QoQ to $51mn, as one unit of Avion went down in Aug-Sep. Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) 9M21 net profit +13% YoY

COSCO reported net profit of P4.3bn, up 13% YoY. PGOLD’s results were slightly behind estimates. Meanwhile, while 9M21 profit of the liquor distribution segment (KEEPRS PM) has exceeded our FY21E of P1.1bn. The real estate segment continues to lag with 9M profit -7% YoY.

 

Keepers Holdings’ net income in 3Q rose 23% YoY to P401mn, bringing the 9M figure to P1.1bn (+48% YoY). 3Q revenues were up sharply (+28% YoY) though coming from a low base. KEEPRS sold 965k cases in 3Q, up 27% YoY, led by the brandy segment (+33% YoY). COSCO management indicated that it has some new investments in the pipeline, which will be among the main uses of cash in its balance sheet. Briefing takeaways - AGI 3Q profit -41%QoQ to P3.5bn; 9M is nevertheless ahead of FY21E (Regis and consensus)

Alliance Global reported that its 9M12 income was P12bn (+106%YoY), tracking ahead of FY estimates. 3Q profit alone, however, fell 41%QoQ due to lower contributions from Emperador. RWM also swung back to a loss after a one-time gain boosted its 2Q.

 

Travellers swung back to a loss of P700mn, down from a profit of P3.7bn in 2Q. 2Q was boosted by a one-time gain of P4.8bn. Operations nevertheless continued to recover despite the strict lockdown in August. GGR increased 46%QoQ to P4.9bn, driven by a 65% jump in VIP GGR (higher absolute value vs BLOOM). We suspect that its VIP business was probably helped by 1) the partnership with SunCity and/or 2) higher hold rates.

 

The contribution of EMP also dipped, down 26%QoQ. Brandy revenues were up 4%QoQ, while whisky revenues declined 11%QoQ. While margins for whisky were largely stable, that of brandy fell to 28.8% in 3Q from 44.7% in 2Q. Management attributed the decline to product mix and cost pressures on logistics and certain raw materials. We think they may have discounted heavily as well to push volumes locally.

 

GADC posted a profit of P134mn in 3Q21, coming from a loss of P24mn in 2Q21. Underlying operations were not that rosy though. Systemwide sales dipped 2%QoQ while overall sales (company owned + royalties etc) declined 3%QoQ. GP (14.2% vs 23.4%) and EBIT (7.3% vs 9.4%) margins were both sharply lower due to cost pressures and an inability to raise prices in the current environment. The bottom line, was, however, salvaged by items below the EBIT line. We await more detail from the 17Q.

 

MEG’s 3Q profit amounted to P3.150bn, a 19%QoQ improvement, but short of estimates due. Office revenues rose 7%QoQ, helped possibly by rental escalations and the lapse of the rent-free period. Residential sales on the other hand is still lagging at just P17.1bn (<50% of the pre-Covid average). AGI trades at 7x FY22E PE and a 70% discount to market NAV.

Highlights of Globe’s 3Q results.

Globe reported core income (ex- exceptionals) of P5.08bn, which would take core income to P15.8bn (+1%) for 9M by our reckoning.  The core income excludes adjustments on deferred tax arising from the reduction in tax rate booked in 1Q (included in company’s reported core income) which amounted to P2.5bn based on our estimate. Excluding the deferred tax adjustment, core income is below expectations.  If one were to include that item, Globe’s core income would be within expectations.

 

Wireless service revenue was down 2% QoQ and 1% YoY. The absolute figure for 3Q implies a small (-0.3ppt) loss of revenue market share. The company blamed the lockdowns in 3Q for the softness in revenue. Broadband finally picking up.  Broadband revenue of P7.8bn for 3Q was up 10% QoQ (+12% YoY), the fastest % increase in six quarters.  The company added 77k wired broadband subscribers in the quarter. While this is still well below PLDT’s (229k) and Converge’s (222k), it is the biggest such increase for Globe ever.

 

Globe has now laid out 1m fiber broadband lines as of end-Sept. It’s raising its target for FY21 to +1.4m lines by which time it would have migrated all its copper (DSL) subscribers. We also note that Globe reported a net churn of 561k subscribers for its Fixed Wireless (-17%) service as it removed those who have not been topping up.

 

Mynt achieved positive EBITDA and net profit for 3Q21.  Globe’s share of Mynt’s profit in the quarter was P115m. This implies a net profit of ~P287m for the quarter for the entire company – over P1bn annualized.

 

The registered user base of GCash is now over 51m and it is on track to achieve P3.5trn GTV for FY21 (previous guidance was P3trn). Interestingly, GCash claims it has 4x more active users than its direct competitor (which we assume to mean Paymaya).

 

With US$300m in new cash from the latest funding round, Mynt will focus on three avenues for growth. The first will be lending. A buy now pay later (BNPL) product will be launched soon.  Second is a platform for “invest-tech” to allow trading of crypto, among others. Third involves further expansion of its services to the “base of the pyramid” which we assume to mean the financially marginalized.

 

Globe also announced today that it was in discussions to form a joint venture (JV) for its data center business with Singapore firm ST Telemedia. Globe currently has six data centers with 1,500 racks and a maximum capacity of “up to 30MW”, with expansion potential of “up to 45MW”. Globe intends to remain a “significant shareholder” in the JV.

 

A transaction like this is almost certainly going to have an accretive (albeit modest) impact on Globe’s valuation. Revenue-wise, this is not a big business (for now) but could have huge upside potential if it can tap the market successfully. For perspective, PLDT’s data center business has a capacity of 9,000 racks (“up to 72MW”). Media reports suggested a valuation of US$500m in talks with potential investors.

ICTSI to buyback 35% of US$775mn perps

ICTSI announced the tender offer results for the US$775mn perps; It will be buying back the following:

 

*US$184mn of the US$400mn perps with a coupon of 5.875% and callable in May 2022; Tender offer price at US$1,024 per US$1,000.

*US$86mn of the US$375mn perps with a coupon of 4.88% and callable in May 2024; Tender offer price at US$1,044.50 per US$1,000.

Pace of vaccinations improves further

As of November 14, the 7-day average vaccinations figure rose to 788,294/day from 697,025/day the previous week. Around 29% of the Philippine population (31.6mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 10, 66% of the Metro Manila population (8.9mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 123mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 70mn doses (as of November 14). The Philippines is targeting to administer 5mn doses/day from November 29 to December 1. These days might be declared holidays. Additionally, beginning December 1, on-site workers for both the private and public sectors must be fully vaccinated against COVID-19. This rule will only apply to areas with “stable” supply of vaccines. The government will start administering booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities in November 2021.

OFW remittances rise 5.2% YoY in September

OFW remittances rose 5.2% YoY, 4.9% MoM in September to US$2.74bn. In PHP terms, remittances grew 9% YoY. This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate to +4.2%. Land-based remittances rose +6.2% YoY while sea-based remittances rose +1.9% YoY. Remittances from the Middle East and Asia moderated while remittances from USA and Europe posted double-digit growth rates. The 9M21 remittances are up 5.6% YoY to US$23bn. To reach the BSP’s FY21E target of US$31.7bn (+6% YoY), remittances for Oct/Nov/Dec should average US$2.86bn per month.

BSP keeps rates steady

The Monetary Board has kept the overnight borrowing rate at its historic low of 2%. BSP cited latest baseline forecasts are “broadly unchanged” from the previous assessment round. BSP is also looking at an average inflation of 4.3% in FY21, 3.3% in FY22, and 3.2% in FY23. Inflation averaged at 4.5% in YT-October, with October inflation at 4.6%. BSP Governor Diokno however said that the risks to the inflation outlook is to the upside for 2022, considering typhoons and fare increases. The Monetary Board will hold its final policy rate review this year on Dec. 16.
