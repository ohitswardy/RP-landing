REGIS REPORT

05 February 2021

The PSEi bounced 6.15% this week, after shedding the same 6.15% in the prior week.

Foreign selling abated, helping stocks climb amidst lingering concerns of a delayed recovery due to the slow pace of vaccination.

Inflation worries dampened sentiment early Friday with the January print of 4.2% sharply higher than consensus estimates (3.5%) and the 3.5% print in December. However, losses quickly reversed, helped by foreign interest. Net outflows this week subsided to US$55m from US$138m in the previous week.

The advance was widespread with 27 of the 30 index members ending the week with gains.

Leading the charge were conglos LT Group (+12.8% WoW), and large index names Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+12.6% WoW), and JG Summit (+10.2% WoW).

Meralco (+12.6% WoW) was among the top gainers as the outcome of hearings involving its long overdue rate review could be significantly positive for Meralco. The Energy Regulatory Commission  has ordered hearings involving Meralco’s proposal to address the issue of rates governing the lapsed regulatory period (July 2015 - November 2020). If approved, Meralco will have to refund its customers P13.9bn. This is much lower than what it had provided for, and this could immediately add ~10% to Regis’ valuation.  An affirmed interim average rate of P1.381/kWh (until such time that a new rate is determined), could also prospectively increase profit forecasts by ~P9bn annually (>35%), based on Regis estimates.

BPI (+5.7% WoW) closed up.  The bank’s CEO Cezar Consing had a sanguine tone during its results briefing. The bank now expects NPLs to peak at 4% – or lower – in 2H21. Consing also said provisions could be much lower in 2021 vs 2020. And he expects loan growth to turn positive by 2Q21.

GT Capital (+0.75% WoW) was flat. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) estimates the  safeguard duty on imported vehicles will affect 15% of its sales and will likely reduce its FY21 sales volume estimate to 104k-108k units from 130k units previously. This implies a YoY growth of 4-8% vs. +30% YoY previously). Regis currently forecasts TMP will sell 117k units (+17% YoY) this year. Assuming TMP’s forecasts, Regis’ FY21 profit forecast for TMP could drop by 10-14%, translating to a 4-5% decline in GT Capital’s profit.   

The Bureau of Customs has ordered the immediate implementation of the provisional safeguard duty on imported vehicles amounting to P70k for passenger cars and P110k for light commercial vehicles. 

Cebu Air (+1.05% WoW) is revising the denomination of its convertible preferred share rights issue to PHP instead of USD. This was apparently done because of the limited ability of PSE brokers to trade and settle USD securities.  CEB shares will now go ex-rights Feb 22.

Congress has ratified the reconciled version of the corporate tax reform package dubbed CREATE. The bill is now set to go to Malacanang Palace for President Duterte’s signature. The bill would reduce corporate tax rates by 5ppt to 25%. This should increase the after-tax profits for most of covered stocks by between 5-7%. The tax cut would be implemented retroactively to July 2020.

Philippine Banks - Consumer NPLs spike c300bps to 8.5% in 3Q20, still led by mortgages, auto
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Households hit hard by Covid, lockdowns

NPLs for consumer loans shot up 296bps QoQ in 3Q20 to 8.51%, from 5.55% in 2Q20, and from 4.07% at end-2019 (Figure 1). This is far higher than the 3.61% NPL ratio in 3Q for the total loan book (+98bps QoQ), suggesting that households have been hit far harder than companies. Worryingly, the surge was led by mortgages (8.37%, up 290bps QoQ) and auto loans (9.84%, up 461bps QoQ). The 4Q data could again be occluded by the Bayanihan 2 grace period, but given the persistent lockdowns, we do not expect significant improvement in the near term.

Consumer NPLs surge between two moratoriums

Consumer loan delinquencies expectedly surged with the expiry of the first loan grace period under Bayanihan 1 Act in May 2020. Consumer NPLs stood at P168bn, up 51% QoQ and 117% ytd. Despite consumer loans accounting for under 20% of total loans, the segment accounted for more than 60% of incremental NPLs so far in 2020. The Bayanihan 2 Act (effective from mid-Sept 2020) could again defer new NPL recognition into 1Q21.

Mortgages, auto loans again see worrying NPL spike

As in 2Q20, the data was worrying, as the increase in NPLs was most pronounced in the secured segments of mortgages and auto loans. Auto NPLs jumped 461bps QoQ and 602bps ytd to 9.84% (Figure 3), likely explained in part by the plunge in demand for ride-hailing services during Covid. The surge in mortgage NPLs – up 98bps QoQ and 527bps ytd to 8.37% (Figure 2) - was more disconcerting, considering they are typically considered the “least risky” among loan classifications. We suspect that a portion of the new NPLs was due to the departure of offshore gaming operators (POGOs), resulting in vacancies in residential properties used to house POGO employees.

Credit card defaults seemed oddly benign; likely due to write-offs

Counter-intuitively, the NPL ratio for credit cards was just 7.49% (Figure 4), with salary loan NPLs at 7.17% (Figure 5), both lower than the ratios for mortgages. We reckon this is due to banks aggressively writing off delinquent accounts for these segments, as the unsecured nature historically allows for lower recovery rates. The BSP does not require banks to disclose new NPL formation – only ending balances – masking true default rates.

Is the worst behind us?

To the upside, total banking sector NPLs (ex-interbank) as of November was 3.91%, up just 30bps from the end-September figure. As mentioned, though, the Bayanihan 2 Act allowed for a second moratorium period. However, as we recently wrote, continued restrictions on mobility were a major cause for the weak GDP print in 4Q20, and the likely sluggish economic recovery into 1Q21.

Manila Electric Company - ERC to act on proposed tariff and refund for lapsed regulatory period
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Regulator sets Feb-Mar hearings for Meralco refund

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has ordered hearings involving Meralco’s proposal to address the issue of rates governing the lapsed regulatory period (July 2015 - November 2020). If approved as proposed, Meralco will have to refund its customers P13.9bn, much lower than what it had provided for, and this could immediately add ~10% to our valuation. The possible affirmation of the interim average rate of P1.381/kWh until such time that a new rate is determined, could also prospectively increase our profit forecasts by ~P9bn annually (>35%).

P13.866bn refund proposed by Meralco

Meralco proposed to “true-up” its Actual Weighted Average Tariff (AWAT) of P1.4414/kWh for the lapsed period (July 2015 – November 2020) against its approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.3810/kWh. The difference of P0.0604/kWh multiplied by the actual kWh sales during the lapsed period amounts to P13.886bn. While a refund may appear to be bad news for Meralco, the company has already provisioned multiples of that, and our P340/sh valuation already assumes a refund of as much as P73bn (based on the difference between the IAR and a projected tariff of approximately P1.1/kWh over this lapsed period). Thus, if Meralco’s application is approved as proposed, we may actually see a large reversal of provisions for the difference. On our estimates, this alone could add as much as P35-40/share (10-12%) to our valuation.

Affirming the IAR will also add >35% to annual profits

Approving Meralco’s petition could also affirm the IAR of P1.381/kWh as the final rate until such time that a new rate can be determined in accordance with the regulatory reset process. The rate is 34% higher than our assumed rates for 2022-2025E. Applying the IAR would translate to an after-tax increase of c.P9bn to our annual profit estimates (>35%).

A win-win scenario

We think there is a good chance that Meralco’s application (or a form close to it) will be approved by the ERC. While it will mean Meralco giving back P14bn to its customers, it will no longer be at risk of refunding the entire P70+bn. Meanwhile, the ERC will be able to credit itself with lowering rates by P0.1528/kWh (11% of the average distribution rate) over the next two years - not an insignificant amount especially during these times.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Cement - 4Q20 industry preview: recovery stalls
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q20: Squeezed by weak demand and rising energy costs

After the post-lockdown surge, cement demand appears to have weakened in 4Q20, with imports lower YoY and QoQ. Meanwhile, domestic ASPs may have also softened further, following (a) continued drop in the landed cost of imported cement and (b) the 2% reduction in the safeguard duty in October.

Given the 45% QoQ rise in Newcastle thermal coal prices in 4Q20, we anticipate margins of domestic manufactures, including Cemex Philippines (CHP PM), to start shrinking in 4Q20. Despite CHP’s outperformance in 3Q (see Maintenance deferral leads to 9M20 profit beat; 3Q volumes recover QoQ, 29 October 2020), we believe risks to earnings are tilting heavily to the downside.

Imports lower

Data from the Bureau of Customs, which we compiled, showed that the country imported 2.1mn MT of clinker and cement in 4Q20 alone, lower by 10% YoY and 25% QoQ. The sequential drop appears consistent with the data reported by Vietnam Customs, which showed a 43% QoQ drop in exports to the Philippines. We estimate that imports of bagged cement alone dropped 25% QoQ. This brings FY20 imports of clinker and cement lower by 17% YoY to 8.3mn MT.

Share of trader imports down QoQ

We estimate trader imports accounted for 62% of cement and clinker imports in 4Q20 alone, down from 66% in 3Q20 and 70% in 2Q20 but still higher than the FY19 average of 53%. Given the relatively unsophisticated trader market, we believe the QoQ drop in trader imports reflects high domestic inventories.

Estimated landed cost further weakens

Using the VAT-based cost of imports as a proxy for landed cost, we estimate that the landed cost of bagged cement dropped slightly in 4Q20 to US$64/MT from US$65/MT in 3Q20 and further weakening from the peak (US$67/MT).

We reckon ASPs of listed cement manufacturers could soften further in 4Q (likely at least -4% QoQ and -7% YoY), after falling by an estimated 4-8% YoY in 9M20. Note that the Department of Trade and Industry also lowered the safeguard duty by 2% to P9.80 per 40-kg bag (P245 per MT or US$5.10/MT) last October 22.

How strong will cement demand recovery be in 2021?

Our forecasts assume CHP’s cement volumes will revert to 2019 levels by 2021, after an estimated 7% drop in 2020 (9M20 cement volumes down 12% YoY). While government infra spending should rebound strongly this year, further weakness in the property market (c. 70% of demand) could dampen the recovery, in our view.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Pushing on a string (2) – the limits of monetary easing
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Fiscal spending, reopening needed; but watch out for inflation

The BSP’s massive QE and DM have succeeded in holding interest rates down and keeping financial markets liquid. But the -8.3% 4Q20 GDP print has shown the limits of monetary easing. Fiscal spending, relaxing movement restrictions, and vaccines are needed to get the economy moving again. Inflation is picking up – on imported commodity prices – and could stall consumption when unemployment is still high. We would stay long the market, but keep some defensive names in there.

4Q20 GDP -8.3% YoY, 1Q21 likely still soft as mobility remains restricted

GDP shrank 8.3% YoY in 4Q20 (Fig. 1), taking the fully year to -9.5% YoY, the largest contraction on record. A joint statement by the economic cluster of the Cabinet attributed the sluggish recovery to persistently tight restrictions on movement, despite manageable Covid-19 numbers (Fig. 5,6). Indeed, Google data shows mobility in January has fallen back to October levels (Fig. 2-4). The InterAgency Task Force (IATF) has kept Metro Manila and other provinces under General Community Quarantine (GCQ) status at least until end-February. The NEDA warns that 1Q21 GDP growth could still be negative as a result.

Fiscal spending, CREATE could provide a boost

To the upside, the P4.5trn 2021 budget was passed in December, averting a 2019- style delay in spending. On top of this, some P150-200bn worth of unspent portions of the 2020 budget and Bayanihan 2 Act have been carried into 2021. Congress is also likely to pass the CREATE Bill, which lowers corporate tax rates from 30% to 25%. All of these could help kick-start the economy.

Vaccines coming, but ramp-up likely not till 3Q21

The Philippine government has signed up with various vaccine makers to buy about 102mn doses (at 2 doses per person) and aims to have at least 55mn people – about 70% of the adult population – inoculated. While some vaccines are expected in 1Q, we reckon mass quantities will only arrive in 3Q (Fig. 7)

Watch out for (imported) inflation

Inflation jumped to 3.5% in December, due to food price spikes caused by recent typhoons (Fig. 8). A surge in global commodities – oil, coal, and grains – could see higher CPI prints into 2Q21, which could delay the recovery in consumption.

Don’t buck the BSP – QE/DM still positive for markets

As we previously reported, the BSP seems committed to keeping monetary policy loose, holding interest rates down through a combination of policy rate cuts, open market purchases of government bonds, and direct lending to the Treasury (Fig. 9-12). While the BSP tends to downplay cost-push inflation, high CPI prints could stay their hand. We continue to like reopening plays such as WLCON, GTCAP, ICT and CEB. However, we would keep some defensive / yield names like PLDT/GLO and MER as hedges in a negative real interest rate world.

 
 

Corp News

Notes from BPI earnings call

Overall, I was surprised by the sanguine tone Bong took during the briefing. He now expects BPI’s NPLs to peak at 4% – or lower – in 2H21. He said provisions could be much lower in 2021 vs 2020. And he expects loan growth to turn positive by 2Q21. The table below highlights my skepticism. The NPL ratio for BPI’s consumer/SME book averaged around 8.5%. And that’s after netting off write-offs of credit card NPLs that were already fully provisioned. With this kind of stress on the consumer, and with the government retaining GCQ status in NCR, I find it extremely optimistic (almost naïve?) to expect that the stress will not affect the corporate sector. NPLs peaked in November at 3.2%. The drop to 2.68% was due to a) loans becoming current, b) write-offs of loans fully provisioned, and c) de-consolidation of a leasing subsidiary, which would have added 17bps to the NPL tally (to 2.85%). As for provisions, the bank suggested that provisions could be much lower in 2021 vs 2020. This is a reversal from the 3Q earnings call where they said they would likely keep credit cost “elevated” (1.5% to 1.75% credit cost, if I recall correctly, vs c2% in 2020). Meanwhile the bank said they would be happy were flat YoY in 2021. Their base case is a 10bp contraction YoY as asset yields are falling, catching up to funding costs. They will try to cushion this by continuing to grow CASA (+10% YoY target). Digital investments are paying off. The BPI Online / app enrolment was up 17.5% YoY to 4.4mn, with the number of active users surging 41% YoY to 2.7mn. Lastly, branches could see rationalization with the merger of BPI and BPI Family. Though bigger reductions/rationalization probably more likely in 2022.

ERC to hold hearings on Meralco refund for lapsed regulatory period (Jul 2015-Nov 2020)

In an order dated January 11, 2021, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) said it will hold hearings tackling Manila Electric Company’s (MER PM) proposed mechanism to address the issue of rates governing the lapsed period (July 2015-November 2020). Meralco proposed to “true-up” its Actual Weighted Average Tariff (AWAT) of P1.4414/kWh against its approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.3810/kWh. The amount of P13.886bn will be refunded to customers. This was computed using the difference between AWAT and IAR of P0.604/kWh, multiplied by the actual sales in lapsed period. Based on projected sales over the next 24 months, the average refund rate will be P0.1528/kWh.

PAL to cut 2,300 jobs

Philippine Airlines announced it would cut about 2,300 jobs, or approximately 30% of its workforce. The retrenchment program was communicated to employees “as early as October” last year. Last October, PAL announced it would let go of 35% of its workforce (~2,700) but layoffs were pushed back to after Christmas. These are among the steps being taken by PAL as it prepares to file for court protection in the United States as early as December 2020. Last March 2020, PAL cut 300 jobs; Cebu Air (CEB PM) cut 35% of its workforce in 2020.

Consumer loan NPLs shoot up to 8.5% in 3Q20, nearly 300bps QoQ, led by mortgages/auto

Consumer loan NPL ratio for the banking system surged 296bps QoQ to 8.51% as of September 2020, from just 5.55% in June, and 4.07% at end-2019. In nominal terms, NPLs surged 121% YoY and 51% QoQ to P167.8bn. As with the 2Q data, the deterioration was surprisingly led by secured mortgages and auto loans. Mortgage NPLs jumped 290bps QoQ to 8.37%, while those for motor vehicles (cars and motorcycles) spiked 440bps QoQ to 9.73%. Credit card NPLs also increased, but only by 176bps QoQ to 7.49%, while salary loan NPLs were up 131bps to 7.17%. The data suggests that Covid-19 and the lockdowns have had a more severe impact on households than they have had on companies. Total banking industry NPL ratio was 3.58% as of September. Excluding consumer loans, we estimate NPL ratio for corporates/SMEs was just 2.39%.

PMFTC Inc to let go of 300 workers at Marikina plant

According to an Inquirer report, PMFTC Inc – the tobacco JV of LT Group (LTG PM) – is targeting to let go of 303 workers in its Marikina plant. The Marikina plant has about 1,200 workers. In a statement, PMFTC said that “in light of a steep decline in production volumes resulting in significant idle capacity at the Marikina plant, PMFTC Inc. confirms making the difficult decision to streamline its manufacturing operations that has been impacted by the market conditions over the past years”. We expect PMFTC cigarette volumes to decline 13% in 2020 to 43bn sticks and 14% in 2021 to 37bn sticks. This came from 66bn sticks five years ago. We nevertheless expect earnings to improve (+9% in 2020 and +12 in 2021) due to improved margin profile from the price increases arising from the tax hikes.

Bank lending contracts in December

Preliminary data on outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase placements, showed a -0.7% contraction in December vs a 0.5% growth in November. This is the first time that bank lending contracted in 14 years (September 2006: -1.6%). The issues cited were lower appetite or demand from firms or businesses, specifically from large enterprises, and to some extent the relatively stricter bank loan standards. Loans to businesses fell by 0.4%, with the sustained drop in manufacturing (-5.2%) and trade (-6.8%). Loan growth to the real estate sector (mortgages + corporates), however, accelerated slightly to 5.3% from 5.2% the previous month. Meanwhile, consumer loan growth decelerated to 4.4% in December from 7.1% in November. Domestic liquidity (M3 growth) expanded by 9.5% in December to P14.2tn but moderated from November (+10.5%).

Cebu Air redenominates currency of convertible pref stock rights issue to PHP; moves timetable

Cebu Air redenominated its planned US$250mn convertible preferred stock rights issue to PHP. The company cited limitations for trading dollar denominated securities. The amount to be raised will be up to P12.5bn. It also deferred the price setting to Feb 15. Ex-rights date moved to Feb 22 (from Feb 5 previously). Meanwhile, olffer period moved to March 3-9.

AC Energy sets follow-on offering of up to 2bn primary shares

The company’s executive committee approved a follow-on offer (FOO) price range of P6.00-P8.20/share and a primary issuance size of up to two billion shares. The total offering size would include secondary shares, but the number would be determined at a later date. ACEN previously pegged the FOO at an indicative size of 2.25bn common shares. At this price range, the FOO would raise up to P16.4bn. ACEN President and CEO said in a media briefing that ACEN was looking at up to P30bn in cash infusion from share sales this year. Recall, the timeline for ACEN’s projected milestones this year are as follows:

-GIC buys 4bn new shares (1Q21)

-Follow-on offering for an estimated 2.25bn new shares (2Q/3Q)

-ACEN’s parent will swap its international assets into ACEN for estimated 16.7bn new shares (2Q/3Q)

-AC will sell around 2.8bn shares to GIC (4Q)

 
 

Macro News

Gov’t debt-to-GDP ratio jumps to 14-year high in 2020

The end-2020 national government (NG) debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 54.5% in December 2020. This is the highest since 2006 (58.8%). The government accelerated borrowings last year amid the pandemic-induced recession. The total outstanding NG debt amounted to P9.79tn in December. While this was 3.3% lower than the outstanding debt in November due to repayments, this was 26.7% YoY higher than the end-2019 debt level of P7.73tn. Domestic loans accounted for 68.4% of total, up from 66.3% in 2019. For 2021, the programmed P3.03tn in gross borrowings would further hike the debt-to-GDP ratio to 57%.

BOC collections drop 15.6% YoY in January

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P47.14bn in January 2021, down 15.6% YoY. This figure exceeds the BOC’s January target of P44.09bn by 6.92%. The BOC is tasked to collect P616.7bn this year (+14% YoY).

Fort Bonifacio land prices rise 12% in 2020

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) reported that a Fort Bonifacio lot transaction in December 2020 was priced 12% higher than the last transaction in 2019. We expected Fort Bonifacio land prices to fall 10% in 2020. LPC also said that the recent Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) on higher POGO taxes has slowed the exodus of POGOs. There has also been an increase in office inquiries overall.

Bicam approves CREATE Act

The bicameral conference committee approved the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act. Among the provisions reportedly written into the bill by the panel that were not in the versions previously passed in the Senate and the House was on the fair treatment for petroleum refiners. This exempts crude oil intended for refining from duties and excise, and taxes the product upon removal once refined, to avoid stranded value-added tax credits, consistent with international practice. CREATE also lowers CIT from 30% to 20% for small and medium corporations and to 25% for all other corporations. Other fiscal incentives give; up to 17 years of incentives for exporters and for “critical” domestic market enterprises and up to 12 years of incentives for other domestic market enterprises. The future law will also give additional 3 years of income tax holiday for registered enterprises that will fully relocate outside of NCR and additional 2 years of income tax holiday for registered enterprises in areas recovering from disasters or conflict. Other tax relief: Higher VAT exemption threshold from P1.5mn to P2.5mn for socialized and low-cost housing, and from P2.5mn to P4.2mn for house and lot and other residential dwellings.

BOP posts US$4.4bn surplus in December; FY20 BOP surplus at US$16.02bn

The country’s BOP position posted a surplus of US$4.24bn in December, more than 3x higher than the US$1.57bn surplus in the same period last year. This reflects inflows from issuance of US$2.75bn ROP Global bonds. This brings FY20 BOP surplus to US$16.02bn, more than 2x higher than in 2019 and the highest on record. Additionally, due to higher national government foreign borrowings and lower merchandise trade the GIR ended 2020 at an all-time high of US$110.12bn, equivalent to 11.8 months’ worth of imports.

DA files petition to lower most favored nation tariff on rice imports

The Department of Agriculture (DA) has filed a petition with the Tariff Commission to lower the most favored nation (MFN) tariff on rice imports to 35%. The move is aimed at expanding options of traders for sources of rice imports, according to Agriculture Sec. William Dar. At present, the in-quota tariff for rice imports or those with the minimum access volume (MAV) is at 40% while those outside the MAV (out-quota) are at 50%. The DA’s proposal was met with opposition from industry groups that claimed there is no need to reduce tariffs for rice imports outside Asean because of ample supply in the country.

Metro Manila, 7 other areas under GCQ in Feb

Metro Manila will extend its general community quarantine (GCQ) status anew for the entire month of February. The Cordillera Administrative Region (CAR) was also placed under GCQ, including Batangas, Tacloban City, Davao City, Davao del Norte, Lanao del Sur, and Iligan City.

BSP eyes January inflation at 3.7%

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) is forecasting inflation to stay between 3.3% to 4.1% in January 2021, with the midpoint at 3.7%. This is a slight uptick from the December 2020 inflation rate of 3.5%. The BSP cited inflation of fuel, meat, power rates in areas being serviced by the Manila Electric Company (Meralco), and excise taxes on alcoholic beverages and tobacco. Inflation for January 2021 is set to be released on 5 Feb.

US$4.2bn in ‘hot money’ exits the Philippines in 2020 amid pandemic

Foreign portfolio investments posted a net outflow of US$4.2bn in 2020, more than double the US$1.9bn net outflow in 2019. This is the highest net outflow since 2012. The net outflows may be broken down to net outflows in the following instruments: Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE)-listed shares (US$3.3bn); Peso government securities (GS) (US$931mn); Other portfolio instruments (US$22mn). In December alone, hot money posted net outflow of US$523.86mn, up 63.2% YoY. This is the biggest net outflow since the US$1.006bn outflow recorded in May.

Philippines to receive 5.6mn vaccine doses from COVAX in 1Q21

The COVAX facility will provide 117,000 doses of the Pfizer-BioNTech vaccine by mid-February. It will also provide 5.5mn to 9,3mn doses of the AstraZeneca vaccine by 1Q21. The Philippines has signed agreements for about 100mn vaccine doses, most of which will start arriving in 2H20.

KMC Savills sees higher office vacancies

KMC Savills expects Metro Manila office vacancies to rise above 10% this year and remain at double digit levels for the next two years. Some buildings have slashed rents by as much as 30% .
