REGIS REPORT

08 October 2021

The Philippine market inched down 0.2% this week to 6906.86, failing to close above the 7000 level again for several weeks now.  Optimism fueled by drastically lower COVID case counts had helped the market to close above 7000 Wednesday but gains reversed towards the end of the week.

Foreigners were net sellers for US$27.9m, with heavy selling at the start of the week. However, Friday saw foreign buying in cyclicals while local passive funds sold due to the PSEi rebal (all index names saw downweights).

The rebal replaced First Gen (-11.7% WoW)  with Wilcon (+24.2% WoW).  This resulted in a large drop for First Gen this week and an even larger gain for Wilcon.

The removal of First Gen was triggered by buyout firm KKR’s  announcement that it was accepting all the shares tendered in the recently concluded tender offer for First Gen shares, higher than its previously announced maximum. KKR buying 7.3% of FGEN’s shares reduces its float to 11.4% -- below the 15% minimum float rule for the PSEi constituents.

Laggard interest boosted Robinsons Retail (+13.9% WoW) while Converge (-15.9% WoW) succumbed to profit taking. The stock fell further after the company disclosed that the lockup of a significant shareholder expires this week. Semirara (+11.7% WoW) continued to gain as coal prices continued to surge presenting signification upside to its earning (and payout).

Positive news flows on the water franchise bills and two acquisitions helped lift investor sentiment. for Manlia Water (+28.8% WoW). The former could potentially change the regulation of the East zone concession. Meanwhile, the impact of the acquisitions in Pangasinan and Saudi Arabia are estimated to be minimal but would give  MWC a point of entry in these locations. 

On the ground, the COVID situation is more optimistic. New COVID infections continued to decline, with the latest 7-day moving average at of less than 12k, down from 16k in the previous week.  

Around 21% of the country’s population is now fully vaccinated, with 56% in Metro Manila alone. Meanwhile, the pace of vaccinations appears to have picked up a bit in recent days with daily vaccinations averaging 460k in the week ended Oct 6.Vaccination of adolescents is set to start next week (on a limited basis next week), and should boost vaccinations meaningfully.

Manila Water bags bulk water supply contract in Pangasinan

Manila Water received the notice of award from the Provincial Government of Pangasinan for a 25-year concession agreement that contemplates up to P8bn in capex. The bulk water facility aims to deliver a billed volume of 200 MLD by Year 25 (vs. East Zone’s billed volume of c. 1400 MLD). Pangasinan has a population of 3.2mn. This is the second bulk water project of the company in the Philippines; MWC also has two bulk water projects in Vietnam.

GT Capital unit launches warranty services for used cars

GT Capital with its Japan-based partners Mitsui and Premium Group launched Premium Warranty Services Phils (PWSPI). PWSPI will work with a wide range of car brands. Its suite of services includes an extensive 188-point inspection on any used car, coupled with a detailed report on the existing state of the inspected vehicle. GT Capital, through its wholly-owned unit GTCAD, will effectively own 40% of PWSPI. GTCAP is trading at a 36% discount to its market NAV of P816/sh.

China's Power Crisis: Impact on PH coal + cement sectors

Global coal shortage worsening, causing power plants in China to shut down.  China is running out of coal, prompting blackouts as power plants are forced to shut down.  Global demand for coal is up sharply (due to lack of nat gas and higher industrial demand, and winter season) and this has coincided with various supply bottlenecks (i.e. mis-timed global de-carbonization, unofficial ban on coal imports from Australia to China, supply disruptions in South Africa, Colombia, and Indonesia). 

Newcastle thermal coal price at a new record high of US$196/MT.  The last contract price for Newcastle coal (re-priced every Monday) stood at US$196/MT, up sharply from US$136 in end-June and up 237% from a year ago (US$58/MT). Thermal coal futures hit an all-time high of US$218/MT yesterday, indicating next Monday’s contract price could break out above US$200/MT. 

Semirara (SCC PM) is the clearest beneficiary.  Newcastle thermal coal is the main coal price benchmark for Semirara’s coal and the latter’s price is typically discounted for its low quality.  Assuming Newcastle averages at US$135/MT, higher than our base case forecast of US$115/MT, SCC’s FY21E profit could rise by 30%, putting its PER at 5.7x.  Assuming Newcastle prices stay at US$196/MT in 2022 (vs base case of US$100/MT),SCC’s FY22 profit could more than double, implying a PER of 3.0x.   

Majority (>95%) of Semirara’s power supply contracts have mechanisms for full fuel pass-through.  At today’s Newcastle thermal coal price (US$196/MT), we estimate SCC’s contract price could already be close to P6/kwh.

Thermal coal supply shock could lead to tighter supply of cement in the region, hence higher prices.  Interestingly, the coal shortage is also forcing cement plants in China to shut down.  With cement demand unchanged, Johnson Wan believes China’s domestic cement prices could rise.  Johnson did not discuss the potential of higher cement imports to China but this could be a foregone conclusion, if China’s domestic cement production is curtailed. 

PH cement companies could raise prices too. The tighter cement market in China could prompt cement prices in the region to rise and increase the landed cost of Philippines’ cement imports. This should allow local cement manufacturers to raise prices and offset rising input costs.  However, we believe risks are still skewed to the downside, as we believe domestic cement demand in the next 6-12 months could be sluggish or even drop with no new investments in the property sector and potential slowdown in government infra spend.

MREIT is targeting to have a 500,000sqm portfolio by 2024

MREIT currently offers a dividend yield of:

Dennis Uy shelves Clark casino project

PAGCOR has approved the request of Clark Grand Leisure Corp, a subsidiary of PH Resorts Group Holdings of Dennis Uy, to voluntary suspend its provisional casino license for its project in Clark Global City. CGLC sought the voluntary suspension of its license due to lingering uncertainties surrounding the casino gaming industry especially with more competition in Clark. The voluntary suspension allows PHR to ramp up and focus all efforts for the construction and development of Emerald Bay Resort and Casino in Mactan, Cebu. The lifting of the voluntary suspension in the future will be subject to the approval of the PAGCOR board.

ICTSI raises stake in port in Pakistan

ICTSI bought an additional 7.15% of Pakistan International Container Terminal (PICT PK) from Premier Mercantile Services. The acquisition brings ICT’s stake in PICT to 71.68% from 64.54% previously. The purchase price was PKR154/sh, c. 2% discount to previous day’s close and implies an FY22E PER of 5x. The purchase amounted to US$6.9mn, equivalent to US$97mn for 100% equity. PICT accounts for c. 5% of ICT’s consolidated volumes, 3% of revenues, 2% of EBITDA, and 1% of NAV. We believe the acquisition signals confidence on a possible contract extension; PICT’s concession agreement will end in 2023.

ADB to fund closures of coal power plants

The Asian Development Bank (ADB) plans to create funds, possibly next year, to facilitate early closures of coal-fired power plants in Southeast Asia. The ADB will stop extending loans to build new coal-fired power plants. They will also establish funds to help retire existing plants before the end of their life cycles. The ADB aims to set up a fund (undisclosed amount) to buy power plants and another to promote renewable energy in Southeast Asia. It has started consultations with the governments and utilities in coal-dependent countries such as Indonesia, the Philippines and Vietnam. Coal-fired power plants can cost more than JPY300bn (US$2.7bn), depending on their generating capacity. The first purchase is expected in 2022 or 2023. Last June, Finance Secretary Dominguez announced the government’s intention to shift most of Mindanao’s energy requirements to hydropower under the ADB’s coal replacement fund. Coal accounts for 10,800MW of the Philippines’ total generating capacity (43%).

Coal and WESM exposure of gencos

As Klyne flagged the other day, coal prices continue to surge, with regional coal prices up >40% from June and 237% from a year ago. We take a look at the impact on gencos amidst the sharp jump in fuel for about 40% of plants that supply power. High coal prices will mean squeezed margins for those contracted without pass-through provisions, but the resulting increase in spot prices should benefit those selling to the WESM. At face value, Aboitiz Power (AP PM) is the most exposed to coal, with nearly 70% of capacity coming from coal plants. However, its supply contracts to DUs have fuel pass-through provisions. A significant portion of their contracts particularly to RES have no pass-through provisions, but are hedged through financial instruments. It sells 10% of output as a whole to the WESM, which should benefit from rising spot prices. The completion of the 2nd unit of GNP Dinginin (668MW) would bump up exposure to the spot market a bit more by 2022.  First Gen’s (FGEN PM) contracts for it gas plants largely pass through fuel costs. Its unit EDC’s costs are from the steam that they themselves produce, while wind and solar don’t rely on a fuel source.  It sells <10% of output to the WESM and would be a small beneficiary from rising spot prices. Interestingly, AC Energy (ACEN PM) – a largely renewable player with wind and solar plants relying on FIT rate, is exposed through its coal plant SLTEC (244MW or about 20% of operating capacity). It supplies the 310MW fixed price PHP contract with Meralco which has no fuel pass-through provision. ACEN’s output is mostly contracted and does not benefit from rising spot prices.

KKR completes tender offer, to accept shares equivalent to 7.3% of FGEN

KKR announced that the Philippines Clean Energy Holding inc has waived the max limit of 205mn shares under the terms of the tender offer and accepted all of 262,937,672mn common shares of First Gen Corp (FGEN PM) that were tendered by shareholders. The shares tendered and accepted represent approximately 7.3% of FGEN’s outstanding common shares. The Offeror intends to acquire all of these tendered shares at P33/share through a block sale on the PSE on October, 2021. The transaction represents a total investment value of P8.68bn ($171mn). KKR will hold an approximately 19.9% ownership stake in the company after completion. FGEN’s public float was 18.74% prior to the tender offer and will go down to around 11.4%.

Bloomberry appeal against arbitration award denied again

Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) disclosed that the Singapore Court of Appeal denied its appeal against the decision of the Singapore High Court dated May 29, 2020. The Singapore High Court denied their petition to set aside the Final Award of Arbitration Tribunal in favor of GGAM in May 29, 2020. The Final Award of the Arbitration Tribunal ordered Bloomberry to pay GGAM c$100mn in damages due to the termination of the management services agreement. It also ordered Bloomberry to buy GGAM’s 921mn shares in BLOOM for P10.17bn @ P11/sh or allow the sale of the shares and make up the difference between the market value and P10.17bn. This will allow GGAM to file an action to enforce the Arbitral Award in the Philippines where the assets and business of BRHI and SPI are located. Management intends to oppose enforcement on the grounds of fraud during arbitration, violation of Philippine public policy (GGAM’s assignment of the management agreement to GGAM Netherlands, a shell company, constitutes tax evasion and fraud), etc.

JFC launches tender offer to buy back up to USD250mn senior perpetual securities

Jollibee Worldwide Pte Ltd (JWPL) has launched a tender offer to buy back up to USD250mn senior perpetual securities listed in SGX. JWPL is a wholly-owned subsidiary of JFC. JFC currently has USD600mn perps outstanding. The buyback will be partly funded by the issuance of preferred shares. The tender offer period is 4 Oct to 3 Nov 2021.

Globe Telecom sets GOMO’s number portability on October 12

Globe Telecom announced on Thursday that its unit GOMO would start offering number porting service on October 12. This comes after Smart Communications urged the National Telecommunications Commission to investigate why GOMO had failed to take part in the mobile portability launch on September 30. The delay was “due to complexity of multiple functions of its other brands”, Globe said in a statement.

Lucio Tan cuts ties with son-in-law Joseph Chua

The Lucio Tan group announced through a newspaper advertisement yesterday that Mr. Joseph Chua has no authority to represent Dr. Lucio Tan, the Tan family and the Lucio Tan Group of Companies. “Any prior authority of representation given to Mr. Joseph Chua are deemed void and or revoked”. Macro News

President Duterte will not run for Vice President

President Duterte said he will retire from politics and will not run for Vice President in the 2022 national elections. A recent Social Weather Station survey showed that 60% of Filipinos would consider a VP run as unconstitutional. President Duterte’s longtime aide, Senator Bong Go, filed his candidacy for Vice President

PH raises US$1.6bn via retail dollar bonds

The government raised US$1.593bn (P80.9bn) from its first-ever onshore retail dollar bond (RDB) sale. The two-week offering ended last Friday with the government awarding nearly four times as much as the initial US$400mn target. The five-year bonds, which will mature in October 2026, had a coupon rate of 1.375%, while the 10-year bonds due in October 2031 fetched a 2.25% coupon.

Pace of vaccinations slows further

As of October 3, the 7-day average vaccinations stood at 349,511/day from 359,982/day the previous week. Around 20% of the Philippine population (21.8mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. Additionally, 55% of the Metro Manila population (7.4mn people) has been fully vaccinated. Vaccinations of minors will begin later this month. The Philippines has received a total of 77.4mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 46.4mn doses (as of October 3). The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.  

September inflation slows to 4.8%

Inflation slowed slightly to 4.8% in September from 4.9% in August due to decelerating inflation of food (particularly meat, fish) and transport. Consensus estimate at 5.1%; Came in at the low-end of the BSP’s target range of 4.8%-5.6% for the month. September inflation is higher than the 10-year treasury yield of 4.69%. YT-September inflation averaging at 4.5%, still above the BSP’s 2-4% target this year.

Pilot run of limited face-to-face classes to start Nov. 15

Pilot implementation of limited face-to-face classes will start on November 15 according to the Department of Education. The initial run will last until December 22, after which an assessment of the conduct of the in-person classes will be made. The pilot study will end on January 31, 2022. Once approved, the DepEd is planning to start the expanded pilot run of face-to-face classes on March 7, 2022. Duterte approved in September limited face-to-face classes in areas with minimal risk of Covid-19 infection.

VP Robredo to run for President in 2022

Vice President Leni Robredo announced her decision to run as President in the 2022 national elections. Robredo filed her certificate of candidacy yesterday as an independent. Senator Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan is Robredo’s vice presidential pick. The following have also filed certificates of candidacies for President are: Senator Panfilo Lacson, Former Senator Bongbong Marcos, Mayor Isko Moreno, Senator Manny Pacquiao, Leody de Guzman, and Norberto Gonzales. Friday, Oct. 8 is the last day of the filing of certificates of candidacy. The Comelec will release the tentative list of candidates by Oct 29. November 8 is the last day of corrections on the list of candidates to appear on official ballots.

Pace of vaccinations picks up

As of October 6, the 7-day average vaccinations stood at 463,320/day from 349,511/day as of October 3. Around 2mn doses were administered from October 4 to 6. 21% of the Philippine population (22.7mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. Around 56% of the Metro Manila population (7.5mn people) has been fully vaccinated. Vaccinations of minors will begin later this month. The Philippines has received a total of 80.4mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 48.4mn doses (as of October 6).
