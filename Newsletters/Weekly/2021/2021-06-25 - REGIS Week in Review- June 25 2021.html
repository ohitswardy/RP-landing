REGIS REPORT

25 June 2021

The PSEi closed up 1.4% this week to 6950.51 index points.

The mood on the ground was buoyant, thanks to progress on vaccination.  A signed agreement for 40m Pfizer doses this week would raise to around 157m the number of vaccine doses the Philippines is set to receive  -- enough to inoculate 70% of the population. 

Meanwhile, another 2m Sinovac COVID vaccine doses arrived this week, taking total doses the country received in June to 10.2m (16.2m total doses).  This should accelerate further with the government expecting to receive 11.7m doses in July and 17m doses in August. 

A total of 8.4m doses have been administered. And the pace of inculcation has also improved.  Average daily vaccinations according to the latest data climbed to over 200,000, a visible improvement over the previous weeks.  The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

Based on data from a news report, it is estimated that close to 17% of Metro Manila residents have already received at least one dose of a two-shot Covid-19 vaccine (just under 6% nationwide).  It would seem economic centers are on track to achieving the 70% level (of inoculation) earlier than the rest of the country.

Foreigners were sellers for a second straight week, with US$50m exiting this week. Foreign flow was again skewed by blocks in Ayala Land (+2.8% WoW) and Manila Water (+6.4% WoW) which saw big foreign outflows (local buying). Outside these blocks, foreigners were still more of sellers, with biggest outflows seen in SM (-0.5% WoW),  Globe (+2.1% WoW),  LT Group (+2.5% WoW), Security Bank (-4.1% WoW) and Meralco (-1.5% WoW). 

Gainers outnumbered losers 22:7 even as corporate news continued to be sparse.  Clear outperformers for the week were consumer names – Emperador (+12.7% WoW), Jollibee (+7.7% WoW), Robinsons Retail (+5.8% WoW), all topping the index gainers list.  Property followed, with SM Prime (+5.0% WoW) , Megaworld (+4.2% WoW), and Ayala Land (+2.8% WoW) also outperforming.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Glass half full (part 2) – NCR up to 17%vaccination rate
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

What a difference a fortnight makes

We recently turned positive on the market as vaccination rates rise, particularly in Metro Manila (aka NCR). In the last two weeks, we have seen inoculations accelerate further, with another 2.1mn jabs administered, taking the total to 8.4mn as of 20 June. We estimate that roughly 16.9% of NCR residents have received at least one dose of a Covid-19 vaccine, much higher than 5.7% nation-wide average (Fig 1). As doses arrive in larger and larger numbers, and with NCR Covid cases falling, the vaccine rollout will be able to expand to provincial Covid hotspots.

Metro Manila cases falling, vaccine rate rising, mobility improving

Covid-19 cases have declined rapidly in NCR – following the lockdowns in April – from a high of about 5,000 cases a day to around 650/day (Fig 4). Based on Google data (Figs 6/7) mobility within Metro Manila have improved and are now even above the levels seen in 1Q21. As vaccination rates rise, we expect movement restrictions to loosen further. We continue to recommend investors build positions in “reopening plays” in sectors such as restaurants, consumer discretionary, banks, property, and tourism.

Cases spiking elsewhere; enough vaccines to go around?

Outside of Metro Manila, though, Covid cases have stayed high (Fig 5), with new “hotspots” in several provinces. Last week, Congressmen demanded higher allocation of vaccines, a call which President Duterte has responded to. Should cases surge in more provinces, there could be increased pressure to divert doses away from NCR. However, given the sharp increase in vaccine arrivals, we believe that government can increase deployment of jabs to the provinces, while still focusing on the key economic centers of Metro Manila, its surrounding provinces, Metro Cebu, and Metro Davao.

 
 

Corporate News

Globe 5G network now covers at least 88% of NCR

Globe Telecom said on Monday that its 5G network now covers at least 88% of the National Capital Region. Bonifacio Global City CBD and Ortigas CBD having the highest coverage at 97%, followed by Makati CBD at 96%. “We expect more installations of 5G cell sites for the remaining six months of the year, said Globe chief commercial officer Issa Guevarra-Cabreira. Globe earlier announced plans to spend a “record” P70bn capex for 2021 to support aggressive cell site builds, upgrade all sites to 4G/LTE, “fiberization” of homes nationwide and the roll out of 5G technology.

Megaworld seeing stronger domestic residential sales

Megaworld Chief Strategy Officer Kevin Andrew Tan said that the company was seeing “a lot” of domestic residential sales in the past two months but not the same as 2019 levels. In 1Q21, reservation sales were flat at P20.7bn, 56% of the 2019 average. MEG is currently trading at 6.9X FY21E P/E, 0.5X FY21E P/B, and 60% discount to NAV.

GrowSari raises over $30mn in funding from global investors

E-Commerce platform GrowSari said on Monday that it had raised over $30mn this year from two rounds of capital raising efforts. Series A investors include Robinsons Retail, JG Digital Equity Ventures and Wavemaker Partners LLC. Meanwhile, Series B investors include Singapore-based Pavilion Capital Fund Holdings Pte. Ltd, Saison Capital, Tencent Holdings and International Finance Corp. Co-founder Reymund Rollan said in a statement Monday that the fresh funds will help to more than double GrowSari’s existing coverage and service to more than 300k sari-sari stores including those in Visayas and Mindanao. As of end-2020, the app had a user base of 50,000 stores in over 100 municipalities across Luzon.

PAL eyes bankruptcy filing in NY on June 29

Philippine Airlines’ (PAL) anticipated filing of Chapter 11 creditor protection petition in New York is targeted on June 29 according to the Philippine Daily Inquirer. The date was prospective and could be moved to July. Details on the final rehabilitation plan were not yet available, but the sources disclosed that PAL was targeting to exit Chapter 11 within “months” when travel restrictions were relaxed, and more flyers got vaccinated

NCIP issues halt order to 3 hydro plants of AP unit

Aboitiz Power disclosed that a cease-and-desist order was issued by the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples Cordillera Administrative Region to Hedcor that operates three hydroelectric power plants in Bakun, Benguet due to irregularities in the memorandum of agreement signed with Bakun Indigenous Tribes Organization in 2019. The affected plants in Bakun are the 2.4MW Lower Labay, 3.6MW Lon-oy and 5.9MW FLS. Hedcor said it complied with all conditions when it sought the free prior informed consent (FPIC) memorandum of agreement. The affected plants are less than 1% of Aboitiz Power’s NAV, capacity and earnings.

Villar group to raise up to P6bn from IPO of supermarket chain

The Villar group hopes to raise as much as P6bn from the IPO of its supermarket chain, AllDay. The offering could be as early as October. AllDay Chairman Manuel Villar Jr. said that AllDay has 30 to 40 supermarkets across the country.

 
 

Macro News

Philippine government signs supply agreement with Pfizer

The Philippines signed a supply agreement for 40mn doses of the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID vaccine. We estimate that the Philippines has signed agreements for 157mn COVID vaccine doses which includes the 44mn doses from COVAX. The Philippines has now received a total of 14.2mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 8.1mn doses. The country is scheduled to receive 10.2mn doses in June including 5.9mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines expects to receive 11.7mn doses in July and 17mn doses in August. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

Duterte to finish 29 more flagship projects before term ends

President Duterte’s administration will have completed 40 big-ticket infrastructures amounting to P365.2bn of the 119 “flagship” projects in his ambitious “Build, Build, Build” program by next year. 11 of these flagship projects, worth P126.7bn, were already completed (e.g. Skyway Stage 3). Another 29, amounting to P238.5bn, will be finished before the end of next year, after the President steps down on June 30, 2022. According to Presidential adviser on flagship programs and projects Secretary Vivencio Dizon 51 projects amounting to P3.3tn are ongoing and will be finished by 2023 and beyond.

Balance of payments back to deficit in May

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$1.4bn in May 2021. This is a reversal from the US$2.43bn BOP surplus recorded in April 2021 (due to National Govt’s ROP Global and Samurai Bond issuances). The deficit was due to payments of the National Government’s foreign debt obligations. This reflects a slight decrease in GIR level to US$107.25bn in end-May from US$107.71bn in end-April and US$110.12bn in end-2020. The 5M21 BOP deficit stood at US$1.63bn (vs. US$4.03bn surplus in the same period last year) due to a wider merchandise trade deficit and net outflows of foreign portfolio investments. The BSP projects the BOP to be at a US$7.1bn surplus by end-2021 (equivalent to 1.8% of GDP) vs. 2020 BOP surplus of US$16bn (4.4% of GDP).

Budget deficit expands MoM to P200bn in May

The national government’s budget deficit stood at P200bn in May, as expenses accelerated. Tax revenues fell MoM to P234bn due to timing of tax filings in April but the figure for May is broadly the same as the monthly average in 2019. Expenses surged YoY and MoM to P457bn despite the high base from last year’s social amelioration program and cited capital outlay disbursements of the DPWH, education and health programs, Philhealth releases for health insurance premiums. This brings the government’s 5M21 budget deficit to P566bn. Last May, the government hiked its budget deficit target to P1.86tn (~9.4% of GDP) from P1.78tn previously (~8.9% of GDP) due to higher programmed expenditures. The government, however, lowered its FY21E infrastructure outlay target to P1.019tn (5.1% of GDP) from P1.69tn (5.9% of GDP) previously.

Bank deposits up 8% to P11.8trn in end-March

BSP said bank deposits increased by close to 8% to P11.8 trn in end March. Demand deposits increased 2.8% while savings deposits grew 1.8%. Meanwhile, time deposits declined 8.9% while foreign currency deposits slipped by 0.5% to P2trn. Most Philippine banks have lowered the interest on deposits to 0.125%. The BSP reported that the total assets of Philippine banks went up 6.6% to P20trn as of end March.

Consumer loan NPLs up to 9.75% in 1Q21, but decelerating

Consumer loan asset quality deteriorated again QoQ in 1Q21, though the pace has decelerated nicely. Overall consumer loan NPL ratio was 9.75%, up 42bps from the 9.33% at end-2020. That is the slowest QoQ increase since the Covid-19 pandemic started. That’s also slower than the 59bps QoQ increase in total NPLs in 1Q21. Mortgage NPLs inched up 21bps QoQ to 9.44%. The biggest jump was seen in Motor Vehicle NPLs, which surged 142bps QoQ to 12.10% from 10.68% at end-2020. The MV NPLs were driven by a spike in Motorcycle NPLs to 9.60% from 6.20%. Auto loan (4-wheels) NPLs were still higher at 12.26%, up 129bps QoQ. Credit card NPLs improved to 8.48%, down 37bps QoQ, while Salary loan NPLs were flat at 6.60%. As we have cited in the past, banks tend to write off unsecured loans much more quickly, which masks the new NPL formation (data that is not disclosed by banks or the BSP). Overall consumer loans contracted 2.5% YoY and 1.7% QoQ. However, mortgages grew 7.9% YoY and 0.9% QoQ. Banks’ continued appetite for mortgage lending is surprising, considering the high NPL ratio, in what should be a low-risk asset (in theory). We also note that condo prices within Metro Manila are down 10-20% YoY. It is likely that the new mortgages are for landed property outside Metro Manila, and for leisure property, where demand is strong and prices are rising. Motorcycle loans were also up 9.5% YoY and 2% QoQ. This is likely due to a) continued limited public transportation in Metro Manila, and b) the surge in demand for delivery services (eg Grab, Lalamove, etc) given the explosion in e-commerce. The deceleration in consumer NPL ratios in 1Q21 is welcome news. However, note that Metro Manila went into another lockdown in April. That said, the mobility restrictions were nowhere near as bad as they were in 2Q2020. Still, any new NPLs as a result of the recent lockdowns will only hit the banks’ books in 3Q21.

BSP keeps policy rate at record low

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) kept its key interest rate at a record low for a fifth straight meeting. The BSP maintained the rate on the overnight reverse repurchase facility at 2%. The Monetary Board observed that economic activity has improved in recent weeks, but the overall momentum of the economic recovery remains tentative. The BSP raised its inflation outlook for this year to 4%, from the previous forecast of 3.9% (vs. YT-May average inflation at 4.4%).

Another 2mn Sinovac doses arrive

Another 2mn Sinovac COVID vaccine doses arrived. The National government received 1.6mn while the Manila city government got 400k. The Philippines has now received a total of 16.2mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 8.4mn doses. The country is scheduled to receive 10.2mn doses in June including 7.9mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines expects to receive 11.7mn doses in July and 17mn doses in August. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021. The government also added 10 cities in its priority list for vaccine distribution: Bacolod, Iloilo, Baguio, Cagayan de Oro, Zamboanga, Dumaguete, Tuguegarao, General Santos, Naga, and Legazpi.
