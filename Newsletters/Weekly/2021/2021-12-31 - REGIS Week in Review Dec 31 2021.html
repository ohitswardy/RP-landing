REGIS REPORT

31 December 2021

The Phils bid 2021 goodbye with a subdued  -0.8% in the final week of year, taking its 2021 performance to a slight 0.2% decline.  Though coming as no surprise, the spike in infections post the Christmas holidays triggered lockdown fears, sending the market tumbling 2.9% on Friday and wiping out the PSE’s YTD gains.

Twenty-one of the 30 index members closed down for the week. Market proxies ended lower, with GT Capital (-5.3% WoW), Ayala Corp (-4.6% WoW), Aboitiz Equity (-3.6% WoW),  SM Investments (-1.9% WoW),  Alliance Global (-1.7% WoW) and Metro Pacific (-1.5% WoW) all lower. Utils Aboitiz Power (3.9% WoW), and AC Energy (-2.3% WoW) were down as well. Large cap cyclicals SM Prime (-1.9% WoW) and BDO (-1.9% WoW) likewise declined.   

Volumes were thin, with average daily turnover at a mere US$94.6m as traders were in holiday mode.  Foreigners ended the week net buyers by US$13.8m however, with top inflows seen in banks BDO and Metrobank (+2.0% WoW) and in property counters Ayala Land (+4.1% WoW) and SM Prime.

COVID case counts have ticked up recently. New cases hit 1623 yesterday, almost double that of the previous day.  The seven-day moving average of daily new cases stood at 632 as of Thursday, up from 255 on Christmas eve. New cases are still nowhere near the peaks in September (reaching as high as 26k new cases) but a surge is nevertheless expected over the coming weeks.  Hospital occupancy remains low however, and vaccination rates continue to trend up.  Around 52% of the country is now fully vaccinated.

Elsewhere, the first unit of GN Power Dinginin which is majority owned by Aboitiz Power has been  issued a certificate of compliance. This effectively allows the commercial operations of the 668MW Unit and comes more than eight years after the plant was first announced and more than two years after its initial target operation date. The plant is expected to contribute up to 20% of AP's earnings when fully operational.

Meanwhile, the  Department of Environment and Natural Resources, lifted the four-year old nationwide ban on open pit mining.  This development should allow the government to issue mining permits.  The ban affected a number of projects, including the US$5.9bn Tampakan project.  Semirara’s (-1.6% WoW) planned entry into mining also requires a limestone mining permit.  Finance Secretary is supportive of the lifting of the ban, citing DENR estimates wherein 11 pending projects could be immediately started to raise P11bn in annual revenues (tax and non-tax ) for the government, push up yearly mining exports by P36bn, and generate 22,880 jobs in remote towns.

RESEARCH
Philippine Consumer - Century Pacific Group announces two acquisitions
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

CNPF and PIZZA announce respective acquisitions

Over the weekend, the Century Pacific Group announced two meaningful acquisitions across its consumer-related entities, Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM) and Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM). CNPF is acquiring leading sardine brand Ligo, while PIZZA is acquiring one of the largest food kiosk chains in the Philippines, Potato Corner. Further details on valuation and pricing have yet to be disclosed, but we view the acquisitions as providing strategic benefits: one consolidates the market, while the other opens up a new channel for growth.

Ligo acquisition strengthens CNPF’s market positioning in canned sardines

CNPF is acquiring Ligo, one of the largest and oldest brands of canned sardines in the Philippines. The acquisition would solidify CNPF’s #2 position in the category with a total market share of close to 30%. Apart from the operational synergies, the acquisition will also improve CNPF’s pricing power within the category. Industry estimates suggest that the canned sardine market is significantly larger than that of canned tuna, but it is more fragmented. The leader in the space (Mega sardines) accounts for around 30% of the market. We estimate canned sardines currently accounts for 15-20% of CNPF’s branded marine business. On a full year basis, we estimate Ligo could add c3-5% to CNPF’s consolidated top line.

Potato Corner to provide new growth channels for Shakey’s Pizza

Meanwhile, Shakey’s Pizza is acquiring Potato Corner, a leading chain of food kiosks with a network of over 1,000 company-owned and franchised kiosks, of which c20% are overseas. Over the past 5 years, it has consistently ranked among the top three operators of kiosk-based foodservice outlets – a highly diversified and fragmented segment of the market. We estimate Potato Corner could add as much as 20% to PIZZA’s systemwide sales. Outside of the revenue uplift, the immediate synergies with PIZZA’s current operations are less obvious, but we believe the acquisition opens up a new channel of growth for the Group.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Merger of BPI units to be completed by end-2022

BPI president and CEO TG Limcaoco said the merger between Bank of the Philppine Islands (BPI) and BPI Family Savings Bank (BFSB) is set to take effect on January 1 next year and completed in end 2022. Post-merger activities would start in 1Q22. The proposed merger would allow the BPI Group to harmonize products and service offerings and realize savings given the consolidation of branch locations , reduction in tax leakages and streamlining of reportorial requirements.

 

AP's GNPD Unit 1 gets COC

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) released on Monday a Certificate of Compliance to GNPower Dinginin  Unit 1. With the issuance of the COC, it can now commercially operate and inject power to the Luzon grid. GNPD is composed of 2 668MW units and unit 2 is expected to be completed by 4Q22. GNPD is expected to comprise 20% of AP’s total attributable capacity when completed. AP trades at 9.5x FY22E PE.

 

PCC reminds UnionBank, Century Pacific on M&A deals

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) has reminded companies involved in recent merger and acquisition deals to check if they meet the threshold for compulsory notification with the body. The director of the M&A Office at the PCC said the antitrust body has yet to receive notification submissions of UnionBank to acquire Citibank’s consumer bnaking unit, CNPF to buy Ligo Sardines and Shakey’s to purchase Potato Corner. Under the Bayanahan 2 Law which took effect September last year, M&A with transaction values below P50bn are exempt from the compulsory notification with the PCC within two years from effectivity of the law. Before Bayanihan 2, M&A’s that meet the P2.4bn threshold for size of transaction and P6bn for the size of the party are required to be notified with the PCC.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Duterte signs P5tn 2022 budget into law

President Duterte signed into law the P5.024tn national budget for 2022. The 2022 budget is 21.8% of GDP and c. 11% higher than 2021. The health sector’s budget saw the largest increase, with an allocation of P268bn (+28% YoY). Around P80bn of this will be used to subsidize the health insurance premiums of 13mn indigent families and 7mn senior citizens while P56bn will be used to improve health facilities. The DPWH and the DOTr will receive P786.6bn and P75.8bn, respectively, to accelerate the Build, Build, Build program. The program includes Network Development Program (P127bn), Flood Management Program (P129bn), Rail Transport Program (P23bn) and the Land Public Transportation Program (P16bn).

DOH reports 1,623 new Covid-19 cases

The Department of Health (DOH) reported an additional 1,623 additional cases of Covid-19 on December 30, the highest single-day tally since November 21 (2,227 new cases). This brings the total case count to 2,841,260. There are currently 11,772 active cases. The positivity rate of 6.6% is also the highest in almost two months. There were 133 reported deaths and 256 recoveries.

DENR lifts ban on open pit mining

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) issued an administrative order, lifting the nationwide ban on the open pit method of mining metals. The order was published in a newspaper on Dec. 25 and will take effect after 15 days. The government imposed the ban in 2017. Last April, President Duterte lifted the moratorium on new mineral agreements imposed in 2012. This latest development should allow the government to issue mining permits.

 

Semirara (SCC PM) is waiting for its limestone mining permit to be able to proceed with its plans to enter the cement business. Other big-ticket mining projects that are currently on hold are: Tampakan of Sagittarius Mines ($5.9bn project), King-king copper-gold - St. Augistine ($2bn), Silangan project - Philex ($750mn). SM (SM PM) was reportedly interested in taking a stake in Sagittarius Mines.

 

Finance chief backs lifting of open bit mining ban

Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez supports the Environment Secretary’s decision to lift the ban on open-pit mining. Dominguez cited DENR estimates wherein 11 pending projects could be immediately started to generate 22,880 jobs in remote towns, raise P11bn in annual tax and non-tax revenues for the government, plus jack up yearly mining exports by P36bn.

Pace of vaccinations picks up

As of December 26, the 7-day average vaccinations figure rose to 643,573/day from 626,900/day the previous week. Around 43% of the Philippine population (47.2mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 25, 69% of the Metro Manila population (9.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 203mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 105mn doses (as of December 26). The government is targeting to inoculate 77mn by end-1Q22 and 90mn by end-2Q22.

MB approves rate cap on lending firms

Last December 16, the Monetary Board issued Resolution 1717 approving the cap on interest rates and other fees charged by lending firms, financing companies and online lending platforms pursuant to the provisions of the Lending Company Regulation Act of 2007 or the Financing Company Act of 1998. Diokno said these laws empower the Monetary Board the prescribe maximum interest rates that could be charged by lending and financing companies in consultation with the SEC if warranted by economic and social conditions. The BSP has set a nominal interest rate ceiling of 6% per month for covered loans. Effective interest rate was pegged at a maximum of 15% per month – including applicable charges such as processing, handling and other fees. The regulator also set a 5% per month ceiling on penalties for late payment. The BSP chief pointed out that the ceilings on interest rates and fees is subject to periodic review by the BSP, SEC and the industry.

Budget deficit widens in November

The National Government’s budget deficit widened to P128.7bn in November, doubling from the previous month due to higher primary spending (i.e. public works, health, education, national defense, and social welfare agencies). Meanwhile, tax collections improved MoM and YoY. This brings YT-November fiscal deficit to P1.33tn, c. 83% of the government’s downgraded budget deficit target of P1.6tn (c. 8.2% of GDP).

Further reduction of bank RRR still on the table

BSP Deputy Governor Francisco Dakila Jr. said that the further reduction in bank reserve requirement ratio (RRR) remains on the table, depending on domestic liquidity and recovery in credit demand. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno previously said that BSP was targeting to bring the RRR to single digit levels by 2023. In 2020, the BSP lowered the RRR by 200 basis points to 12%.
