REGIS REPORT

24 November 2023

The PSEi managed to stay above its 6200 support level (+0.9% WoW) for the week. Liquidity was dry amid the thanksgiving holidays in the US.

Average daily turnover dropped to US$60.1m (-4.9% WoW).  

Gainers outnumbered losers 2:1. Consumer names led the charge. URC (+8.3% WoW) and MONDE (+5.6% WoW) finally rebounded given its recent underperformance while JFC (+4.2% WoW) continued to soar on their earnings report. JFC saw strong foreign inflows for US$6.6m. The sector also started to see relief after October inflation figures came out softer than expected at 4.9% (vs 5.6% consensus).

Outside the consumer names, trades were generally mixed following the earnings season. Large cap SM (-2.5% WoW) was the biggest drag as foreigners sold the name for US$3.3m.

Meanwhile, CNPF (+4.8% WoW) and SMC (+3.3% WOW) are expected to see upweights in the upcoming FTSE rebalancing on December 15. There will be no additions or deletions.

Earlier this week, ACEN (+0.2% WoW) disclosed that the company will sell 276 hectares of land in Zambales to AREIT (-8.8% WoW) in exchange for 199m new AREIT shares (valued at P6.77bn). ACEN will then lease back the land for an undisclosed amount. This undisclosed rental payment is likely to be above the near-term dividends that may accrue to ACEN from the AREIT shares. AREIT saw heavy outflows at US$6.7m.

Headline inflows stood at US$18.0m, the highest since July 30. However, most of the inflows were skewed in ICT (+3.5% WoW) for US$21.2m due to a cross last Wednesday. Excluding this transaction, the market still saw inflows for US$2.0m.

Philippine Banks - 3Q23 – slowing loans, rising NIMs, benign asset quality – how sustainable?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Similar trends in 3Q

The banks we cover – BDO, BPI, MBT, and SECB – showed strikingly similar trends in 3Q23. Loan growth slowed to the single-digits, but NIMs eked out gains on high interest rates, and stronger consumer loan growth. Asset quality remained benign, at least for now. All four expressed optimism that NIMs and ROEs could be sustained, even should interest rates moderate in 2024E. We make minor adjustments to our forecasts, and raise our TP 10-12% as we roll over to our 2024E estimates, and maintain Buy recommendations across our coverage.

Loans slow, but loan mix, and high rates push NIMs up further

Loan growth for the sector slowed in 3Q23, with individual banks posting 4-9% YoY, driven largely by weak demand from corporates and middle-market customers. Consumer loans, though, grew much faster (14-22% YoY), driven by higher-margin credit card and auto loans. The improved loan mix, and elevated policy interest rates help buoy NIMs, up another 5-9bps QoQ. The four have also built up their Treasury books, taking advantage of high risk-free yields.

How sustainable? NPLs benign for now, high NPL coverage

All three banks expressed confidence that decade-high ROEs are defensible at current levels. Even assuming potential policy rate cuts in 2H24E, the combination of lower deposit costs and higher consumer loans should mitigate NIM compression. Recent IT spend could also improve cost efficiency, and generate much-anticipated operating leverage. Meanwhile, NPLs have, for the most part, stayed benign. For the Big Three banks, NPL cover of 160-190% should provide buffer for any asset quality deterioration.

Staying overweight – TPs raised 4-12% rolling over to 2024E

We remain overweight the sector, and maintain our Buy ratings on all four banks on valuations. Our forecasts have been tweaked for the 9M23 performance, but the NPAT adjustments are minimal as slower loan growth has been offset by higher NIMs. We increase our (ROE-g)/(COE-g) derived TPs by 4-12% as we roll over to our 2024E estimates. Key risks include intensifying competition for both loans and deposits, and asset quality deterioration.

 
Filinvest Land - 3Q23 office occupancy rises, could improve further in 4Q
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M23 net profit rises 22%

FLI’s 9M23 net profit rose 22% to P2.4bn, in line with REGIS estimate. Although residential revenue is tracking behind our forecast, residential 9M23 GPM expanded 2ppt (to 44.1%) thanks to higher selling prices and lower input costs.

3Q23 reservation sales drop

Although 3Q23 reservation sales fell 29% QoQ (down 12% YoY) to P4.2bn, we do not consider this a weak number since it is still 87% of the 2019 average. For the 9M period, reservation sales rose 10% to P15.2bn, slightly higher than the pre[1]COVID level. However, we also have to express some caution since payment schemes today are looser than the pre-COVID period.

Office occupancy improves

Office revenue rose 3% QoQ as occupancy improved from 60% (in 2Q23) to 62%. Management said it expects to sign contracts for another 5% of space soon, potentially raising occupancy further to 67% by end-2023.

First dorm facility starts contributing; industrial leasing to start in 2024 The company’s first dorm facility (located in Clark) is fully leased and started generating revenue in September. Thus, the contribution to revenue should triple in 4Q23 (from P115mn in 3Q23).

Meanwhile, Filinvest’s first two ready-built factories (also in Clark) have also been fully preleased and should start contributing to revenue by 2H24.

Interest cost to rise

Filinvest Land is currently raising P12bn of bonds at 7.0% interest in order to refinance maturing debt (which we estimate currently costs c. 4.5% in interest). We estimate that the refinancing will raise FLI’s cost of debt from 5.3% to 5.7%. This would raise interest cost by P300mn annually, equivalent to 6% of our FY24E pretax profit forecast.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

San Miguel, Ayala Land ink toll road access deal

San Miguel and Ayala Land sealed a key access deal for the ongoing Skyway Stage 4 toll road. The integration agreement will allow the 32.7-kilometer Skyway 4 to have on and off ramps at Ayala Land’s Arca South project. The project is expected to start construction next year.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market inches up in October

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 6bps MoM to 20.4%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index increased 52bps MoM to 24.4%. NIKL replaced UBP (which had low foreign ownership) in the PSE Index. Meanwhile, market net foreign selling fell to US$171mn from US$472mn in September. Of the 29 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 23 saw declines in foreign ownership while 6 experienced increases.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

BLOOM – foreign ownership fell 1.1ppt MoM to 25.5%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$4.4mn

CNPF – foreign ownership declined 81bps MoM to 25.9%.

CNVRG – foreign ownership fell 66bps MoM to 18.4%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$6.4mn

BDO – foreign ownership expanded 10bps MoM to 28.2%.

AEV finalizes acquisition of Coca-Cola Beverages Phils

Aboitiz Equity Ventures, together with Coca-Cola Europacific Partners Plc (CCEP), has finalized the terms of its acquisition of Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines Inc (CCBPI). CCBPI is the exclusive bottler and distributor of Coca-Cola products in the Philippines.

Key terms of the proposed acquisition:

-60:40 ownership structure between CCEP and AEV

-Values 100% of CCBPI at US$1.8 billion on a debt-free, cash-free basis

The transaction will require clearance from the Philippine Competition Commission

 

AREIT to acquire land from ACEN

AREIT plans to acquire 276 hectares of industrial land in Zambales from a wholly-owned subsidiary of ACEN in a property-for-share swap. The acquisition is subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. AREIT will issue 199mn new common shares to the ACEN subsidiary. This will increase shares outstanding by 8%. The land is valued at P6.77bn, implying that the shares will be priced at P34/share, 6% higher than the current price. A different wholly-owned subsidiary of ACEN, Giga 8, will lease the land from AREIT for 25 years at a guaranteed fixed rent escalating every year with the option to renew for another 25 years. AREIT currently has 861,000sqm of building GLA and 9.8 hectares of industrial land. After the acquisition AREIT’s AUM will increase from 87bn to P94bn.

 

AREIT expects this transaction to be yield accretive. Regis estimates that Ayala Land would have to sell about 133mn AREIT shares in order to meet minimum public ownership requirements. AREIT currently offers 7% FY24E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). From ACEN’s perspective, the transaction is expected to result in a gain over the cost of the land. A portion will be recognized once the transaction becomes effective while a portion will be amortized over the 25-year lease period. The potential dividends from the REIT + the amortized gain is expected to offset the lease payments.

Megaworld to sue Datem

Megaworld hired ACCRALAW to pursue criminal and civil complaints against DATEM. The Quezon City Regional Trial Court has issued a Writ of Preliminary Attachment (WPA) on Megaworld for its refusal to pay P873mn to its contractor, Datem, under a mutual agreement signed in September 2022. The WPA is intended to secure Megaworld’s assets in anticipation of a lawsuit. However, Megaworld said that it has not evaded any substantial claim for payments from DATEM.

Vehicle sales rise 19% YoY in October

CAMPI reported that vehicle sales rose 19% YoY to 38,128 units in October. On a MoM basis, vehicle sales fell 1%. This brought YT-October industry sales to 352,971 units, up 26% YoY. Meanwhile, Toyota sold 17,997 units, up 16% YoY and 3% MoM and bought cumulative 10M23 sales up 16% YoY to 162,229 units. This is trending well above our FY23E of 192k units (+10% YoY).

 

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Investors to be offered 135 water projects

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said it will offer 135 water projects to private investors next year. Projects will involve assignment of water rights held by the National Irrigation Administration and repurpose surplus irrigation water for other uses (i.e. hydropower, bulk water, aquaculture, and tourism). The volume of the 135 water rights to be offered amount to 12,000 cubic meters per second (cms). The DENR will start seeking unsolicited proposals from the private sector after Dec 2023.

EO to expedite flagship projects out by yearend

A proposed executive order (EO) aimed at expediting the processing of requirements for infrastructure flagship projects will be ready before yearend according to NEDA Undersecretary Joseph Capuno. The EO will aim to “enable the expeditious processing of licenses, clearances, permits, certifications and authorizations”. It is unclear at this stage how the EO will address right-of-way delays, which is the most significant bottleneck to execution of infra projects.

House committee OK’s amendments to CREATE law

The House Committee on Ways and Means approved the CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy) bill. The Bills seeks to amend Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act.

Proposed amendments include:

-Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%

-Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports

-Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board

-Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises

-IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements

Committee Chairman and Albay Rep. Jose Ma. Clemente Salceda said that they aim to approve the bill by the end of the month

BIR collections surpass target in October

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collected P274.4bn in October, up 47% YoY and 80% MoM. Note that growth figures are distorted by the timing of VAT collections. The collections surpassed the P253bn target for the month. This brings YT-October collections to P2.13tn, up 11% YoY. This is c. 81% of the government’s target for the year of P2.64tn (+13% YoY).

BOP posts US$1.5bn surplus in October

The country’s balance of payments posted a surplus of US$1.5bn surplus in October. This is a reversal of the US$414mn deficit in September and was due to the government’s issuance of US$1.24bn global bonds. This brought YT-October BOP to a surplus of US$3.246bn, a reversal of last year’s US$7.1bn BOP deficit for the same period. The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$100mn by year-end.

International air travel market reverts to 73% of pre-Covid levels in 3Q

Data from the Civil Aeronautics Board showed that international passenger traffic reached 14.53mn in 9M23. This implies that in 3Q23 alone, international pax volumes stood at 5.6mn, or about 73% of 2019 average. This is sequentially better than the 5.1mn pax volumes in 2Q23 (67% of pre-Covid). Data for 3Q is consistent with tourist arrivals data, which showed 1.34mn tourists in 3Q23 vs 1.29mn in 2Q23, with the month of July posting highest level (516k) since Covid. Domestic passenger volumes has grown to 21.85mn in 9M23, near 22.14mn in 9M19. In 3Q23 alone, domestic pax volumes reached 7.29mn, better than the 6.98mn pax volumes in 3Q19 but slightly lower than the 7.5mn domestic pax vols in 2Q23. Change in school calendar shifted from April/May to June-Aug, which also lifted international market (i.e. OFWs shifted travel plans to coincide with graduation and school breaks). The systemwide pax volumes reverted to 86% of pre-Covid levels in 3Q.
