REGIS REPORT

17 March 2023

The PSEi dropped again for another week, this time for 1.8% WoW. This marked the eight straight weeks of weekly losses. Foreigners continued to be net sellers, with net outflows of $497m recorded for the week. However, bulk of the outflow was in ICT and MONDE which saw net outflows of $370m and $88.6m respectively.

This week top index gainers were SCC +5%, DMC +4.9%, BDO 2.7% and SM +2.5%. Laggards on the other hand were WLCON -9.8%, CNVRG -9.7%, MPI -8.4%, BPI -7.2% and ICT -7%.

Even with no pure direct issues connected with the Silicon Valley Bank collapse, the PSEi wasn’t spared from the global risk-off sentiment. Market dropped over 3% this week before recovering over 1% on Friday. 

BPI disclosed that it would distribute 406.18m treasury shares (a result of the BPI Family Bank merger in Jan 2022) via a property dividend, with existing shareholders receiving 0.08964 shares for every 1 share held. The stock goes ex-div on 24 March with record date 29 March. In theory, the transaction works like a 9% stock dividend, though individuals and foreign investors will be taxed like a cash dividend.  

Jollibee reported earnings this week. Revenue momentum continued to be strong in 4Q22, with sales +37% YoY led by the Philippine business (+39% SSSG). This drove FY22 revenues to P212bn, well ahead of Regis FY22E and nearly at FY23E. Operating leverage from the strong top line helped cushion wider losses in China, which was crimped by lockdown restrictions. Nonetheless, the underlying core Philippine business continued to be strong, boosted by “normal” holiday spending. Management is optimistic given that the strong top line momentum has continued into early-2023, but we expect SSSG to moderate to 8% coming from a higher YoY base.

Robinsons Land - 4Q22 residential sales surge to record; management optimistic about 2023
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY22 profit and reservation sales ahead of our FY22 and FY23 forecasts

Robinsons Land’s headline profit grew 21% to P9.75bn, higher than the 2019 level and also above REGIS and consensus forecasts. The outperformance was due to stronger-than-expected residential revenue recognition and office margins. It appears that rising office vacancies will likely cause margins to decline later than we projected. The strong FY22 earnings and reservation sales (of P17.0bn) caught our attention because both are already above even our 2023 forecasts (FY23E net profit – P9.5bn, FY23E reservation sales – P16.2bn). Furthermore, management is confident that 2023 figures will be “much better” than 2022.

4Q22 reservation sales surge to record level

Sales of RLC’s standalone projects and JV projects both performed well in 2022, growing 57%-58% to P17.0bn (85% of the 2019 level) and P9.0bn respectively. 4Q22 reservation sales jumped 124% YoY (+43% QoQ) to P6.45bn, 128% of the 2019 average. However, replicating this will likely be difficult since nearly half the 4Q sales came from a single blockbuster project in Pasig City (called Sync N).

Residential revenue reversals drop to zero

4Q22 Philippine residential revenue/EBIT rebounded dramatically, thanks to the substantial drop in residential cancellations (almost back to pre-COVID levels), particularly for buyers who have paid at least 10% of the unit value. Revenue reversals from residential cancellations dropped to zero in 4Q22, from P400mn in 3Q22 (P2.8bn in 4Q21).

Mall recovery continues in 4Q22; still strong in 2M23

Mall revenue grew 7% QoQ (+68% YoY) to P3.8bn as tenant sales recovered to 104% of 2019 levels. The company also said that malls continued to perform well in January and February.

Newly completed and upcoming office buildings have high vacancy rates

RLC’s office occupancy dropped 2ppt QoQ to 90% as the buildings completed in 4Q22 only had a combined occupancy of 22%. We estimate that the aggregate occupancy level could drop to as much as 84% since the 52,000sqm building due for completion in 2Q23 has not been preleased. This will likely cause margins of the office segment to drop in FY23. Hotel revenue jumps on “revenge travel”

Hotel revenue jumped 63% QoQ (+160% YoY) on “revenge travel”.

While hotel EBIT turned negative due to pre-operating expenses of its newly opened Fili Resort and the upcoming Westin hotel, the segment should revert back to profitability as the new hotels ramp up.

 
Ayala Corporation - Write-downs compound soft 4Q operations
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Write-downs compound disappointments in telecom, power

Ayala Corp reported net profit of P27.4bn for FY22, marginally (1%) lower YoY and well below expectations. Underlying operations were disappointing but this was compounded by write-downs relating to a previously owned coal power plant as well as its investments in the Yoma group.

Write-downs overshadow one-off gains

There were a number of one-off gains at the operating company level booked in 4Q, notably at ACEN and Globe. These were overshadowed by provisions / impairment charges at the holding company level. AC, through an intermediate holding company, took a P6.3bn charge relating to the sale of ACEN’s holding in coal-fired plant operator, SLTEC. The company also took an additional P2.5bn impairment on its investments in Myanmar.

Underlying operations disappointed

BPI and Ayala Land delivered as expected in 4Q/FY22 with earning recovering in line with the rise in interest rates and the re-opening of the economy. Globe, however, disappointed, both on the revenue and cost side in 4Q. The last of AC’s four core holdings, ACEN, saw earnings squeezed by higher power costs and fixed selling price commitments. Its bet on the direction of energy prices, made a while back, has not worked out (so far).

Losses at smaller businesses continue

AC Health reported its first full-year profit of P229m but this was aided by a one[1]off gain. Nonetheless, this segment now looks poised to produce profits beginning this year (2023). The losses at AC Logistics and AC Industrials, however, continue with 4Q red ink at par with or worse than the pace in 9M22. Management is hoping to reach break even for this both these units this year. We reckon this sounds ambitious without drastic action (e.g. divestments especially at AC Industrials). Longer term, without a visible path to value creation for these newer businesses, AC could see its share price continue to lag those of its four core businesses.

Less than a year left on its “value realization” plan

There’s now less than a year left to that part of AC’s strategic agenda which aimed to raise US$1bn from divestments by 2023. AC has so far achieved ~US$600m of its goal and management says it’s working to deliver on the remaining ~US$400m this year.

 
Philippine Banks - Assessing market and funding risks, in light of SVB collapse
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Limited solvency risk

Philippine banks we cover claim to have no exposure to Silicon Valley Bank (SVB). However, the event brought focus to the potential downside risks of higher interest rates, in terms of a) exposure to mark-to-market (MTM) risk on long-dated bond assets and b) rising funding costs. We believe there is little risk of insolvency among banks we cover, given the relatively short duration of their Treasury assets and their high capital ratios. With that said, MBT and SECB are more reliant on Treasury operations and on wholesale funding. BDO and BPI seem to have stronger CASA franchises, which predominantly fund their loan respective books.

Short Treasury durations, and high CET-1 despite MTM hits

All four banks we cover claim to have no exposure to SVB. However, concerns were still raised over natural asset-liability timing mismatches and potential MTM losses on banks’ long-dated bond holdings. The management of all four banks claim that the average durations of their Treasury books are quite short, with BPI and SECB at around or less than 4 years and with BDO and MBT at less than 5 years. True, all four banks still took hits. Unrealized losses on their FVOCI (fair value through other comprehensive income) books have knocked 4-7% off from their respective equity bases (see Figure 1 on page 2). Despite that, capital ratios are still extremely healthy, with CET-1 ranging from 12.81% (BDO) to 15.75% (SECB) at end-2022, well above the 10.5% minimum.

No liquidity crunch; BSP likely to cut RRR further

The financial system remains extremely liquid. According to Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) data, banking sector-wide LDRs stood at just 71.5% as of January 2023. And recently, BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that he was inclined to resume RRR cuts later this year, reiterating the regulator’s commitment to bring reserve requirements from 12.0% currently to 10.0%.

CASA is king – BDO and BPI defending NIMs better

Of the banks we cover, MBT and SECB have historically been more reliant on their Treasury operations. As a result, unrealized MTM losses on their FVOCI books are relatively larger compared to BDO and BPI (Figure 1). Still, with little pressure to sell at a loss, and with short durations, those MTM losses should reverse as the securities mature.

More crucially for the P&L, in our view, is that both MBT and SECB rely more heavily on wholesale funding (time deposits, bills, and bonds). In contrast, BDO and BPI seem to have a much stronger CASA base, making up more than 70% of total funding (ex-equity). As interest rates have risen sharply, BDO and BPI seem to have been better able to manage the increases in their respective funding costs, and thus defend their NIMs better.

 
San Miguel Food & Beverage - Improved margins buoy gin, food; beer vols up on price cuts?
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q22 +32%YoY; FY22 ahead at P22bn (+11%)

FB’s 4Q22 net income to equity holders increased to P5.0bn (12%QoQ, 32%YoY), as higher contributions from food and gin offset beer’s relatively soft performance. Margins for both food and gin were steady (both QoQ and YoY), while beer’s contracted 2.6ppt vs 3Q22. For FY22, FB’s net income increased 11% to P22.0bn, mildly ahead of both Regis (P21.4bn) and consensus (P21.6bn).

Beer volumes +9% in FY22 at 199mn cases; 4Q vols (55mn cases) helped by price cuts?

Profit of San Miguel Brewery inched up 2%QoQ to P5.6bn but was lower by 13% vs. last year. Domestic beer volumes grew 9% to 199mn cases in 2022, implying 4Q22 volumes hit 55mn cases (15%QoQ, 8%YoY). Given the milder increase in revenues vs volumes vs 3Q, we suspect that volume growth was not purely organic and was instead buoyed by price cuts or discounts, resulting in lower EBIT margins (19.5% in 4Q22 vs 22.1% in 3Q22). Beer volumes in 4Q22 were still about 15% lower than pre-Covid.

Gin prints record volumes in FY22 at 44.6mn cases; EBIT margins steady in 4Q albeit lower than normal levels

Gin volumes grew another 15%QoQ and 5%YoY to 12.2mn cases, which brought FY22 sales to 44.6mn cases – a record for both quarterly and annual sales. Margins were somewhat stable in 4Q22 (11.1% vs 11.5% in 3Q22), albeit still lower than normalized levels of 12-13%. Prices for inputs – alcohol, bottles, packaging – as well as A&P started to inch up as early as 3Q22.

Food margins slightly better but still well below 1H22 margins due to volatility in commodities

The food segment posted a P9.2bn profit in FY22 (21%YoY), with more of the income generated in 1H22 due to much better prices of poultry and pork relative to inputs (imported meats, wheat) in the early part of the year. Nevertheless, profits improved 27%QoQ to P1.8bn as EBIT margin inched up to 5.3% in 4Q22 from the low of 5.0% in 3Q22. Branded foods sustained >10% volume growth in the period.

 
Converge ICT - FY22 results disappoint on costs; modest growth guidance
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Net profit well below expectations

Converge ICT reported net income of P7.44bn for FY22, just +3% above the previous year and well below Street and Regis expectations. Full-year revenue (P33.7bn, +27% YoY) met our FY22 estimate but had the benefit of accelerated recognition of deferred revenue relating to installation costs. Profit for 4Q alone was P1.33bn, down 32% YoY as margins fell. Both cash and non-cash costs rose sharply in 4Q leading to narrower EBITDA and EBIT margins. The company took a ~P700m charge relating to modems etc. of subscribers who pre-terminated their contracts (with a corresponding accelerated recognition of deferred revenue).

Net subscriber adds at 32k in 4Q22

Converge ended the year with 1.877m broadband subscribers, just shy of our year-end assumption of 1.89m, and implying net adds of 32k in 4Q alone. We view this as a fair increase from prior quarters, a result of improvement in reported churn rather than in gross adds.

Prepaid service “on soft launch”

Converge has begun test marketing its prepaid service called “Surf to Sawa”. Users will have to pay for installation fees and the modem and would subsequently be able to subscribe to the service (@25mbps) on a daily (P50/day) or monthly (P700/month) basis. We expect the upfront cost for subscribers will be a key hurdle for widespread uptake of this service. Competitor Globe is set to launch its prepaid fiber service within 2Q.

Modest growth guidance

Management is now more modest in its guidance, guiding for revenue growth of 10-15%, with residential broadband growing ~10% and the enterprise segment growing ~25%. This growth target may appear modest, but we believe achieving it will hinge on Converge’s ability to execute successfully on its new, lower priced fiber packages (prepaid and postpaid). The current price packages (P1,500 or more per month) will, in our view, be unable to deliver even that kind of growth.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - 4Q22: Strong PH top line overshadows China losses
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY22 core operating profit (+68% YoY to P10.6bn) – in line

JFC reported headline NPAT of P7.3bn, up 33% YoY. Excluding one-offs (e.g. provisions relating to Dunkin’ China exit) we estimate recurring NPAT of P6.7bn (+46% YoY) underperformed estimates due mainly to a higher-than-expected tax rate. 4Q22 recurring operating profit was P2.5bn (flat YoY), bringing the FY22 figure to P10.6bn (+68% YoY), in line with expectations. Meanwhile, revenues of P212bn (+38% YoY) were well ahead of forecasts as the strong momentum continued in 4Q22.

Philippines leads top line growth in 4Q22; China underperforms

Group revenues rose 37% YoY in 4Q22 led by the Philippines (63% of total), where SSS rose 39% YoY. The strong performance was driven by the reopening, as well as a less restricted holiday season, which is typically the peak of domestic consumption. China continued to be the underperformer with SSS declining 10% YoY due to lockdown restrictions. China, however, should see a sharp recovery in 2023 as restrictions are lifted.

China losses, back-ended A&P dampen operating margin

We estimate 4Q22 recurring operating profit was flat YoY, dragged by losses in China. Excluding these losses, we estimate EBIT would have risen ~20% YoY. Even after accounting for China, we estimate operating margins contracted 60- 70bps YoY as the company spent much of its A&P budget during the quarter. A&P/sales grew 50bps YoY in 4Q22 but was flat YoY in FY22. Notably, gross margin was largely stable YoY during the quarter as operating leverage cushioned proportionately higher raw material cost.

Strong momentum to continue in 2023

Management is optimistic given that the strong top line momentum has continued into early-2023, albeit coming from a higher YoY base. It expects to grow top line by 15-20% this year, driven by 7-10% SSSG (Regis FY22E: +8%), with China likely outperforming. We expect margin improvement to be the main driver of 2023 operating profit growth (Regis FY22E: +30%) given (1) more subdued input costs, (2) improving profitability in China, and (3) operating leverage from continued SSSG across the various markets.

 
Bank of the Philippine Islands - Distributes treasury shares via property dividend (like a complicated stock div)
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Unnecessarily complex, brought on by conflicting regulations

BPI disclosed that it would distribute 406.18mn treasury shares (a result of the BPI Family Bank merger in Jan 2022) via a property dividend, with existing shareholders receiving 0.08964 shares for every 1 share held. The stock goes ex-div on 24 March with record date 29 March. In theory, this works like a 9% stock dividend, and should mean no dilution to investors, and no new capital to BPI. However, a property dividend comes with unnecessary complications, including taxes (which could result in mild dilution for foreign shareholders), and potential delays in share transfers due to documentary requirements. To be fair to BPI, conflicting regulations left them little choice in the matter.

Treasury shares created, but couldn’t be held or canceled…

When BPI merged with its wholly-owned subsidiary BPI Family Bank in Jan 2022, the bank had to issue 406.18mn shares to itself, creating treasury shares. However, the General Banking Law does not allow banks to own treasury shares, and required that these be sold or disposed of within 6 months. Management’s preference was to cancel the shares, which is allowed by the SEC. But we understand that the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) ruled that canceling the shares did not technically qualify as a sale or disposition as the law required.

… and CAR of 17.9% too high to warrant a share placement

The only other option would have been to sell the treasury shares through a placement with or without pre-emptive rights. However, BPI is already arguably over-capitalized, with CAR of 17.9% and CET-1 of 17.0%. A share sale would have added 10-15% to BPI’s equity, creating further drag on ROEs.

Lesser evil? Tax issues, and potentially long delays in share delivery

The property dividend does present some unnecessary complications though. For one, foreign institutional shareholders are subject to a withholding tax ranging from 0-25% (subject to tax treaties depending on the funds’ domicile), which will result in the funds receiving fewer shares. Also, the additional documentary requirements could result in delays in the share transfer. In previous instances, the payment dates were as long as 4 to 7 months after the dividends’ ex-dates.

ACEN, US firm BrightNight tie up for energy projects

AC Energy has partnered with US-based renewable power company, BrightNight LLC to develop and operate large-scale 1.2GW hybrid renewable energy projects in India. ACEN is aiming to deploy US$250mn equity and related performance guarantees to back up BrightNight’s existing portfolio and future growth. ACEN said the hybrid projects will be operational 24/7m with calibrated use of storage technologies. ACEN together with its partners owns a solar farm portfolio of 630-MW direct current (MWdc) or 450-MW alternating current (MWac) of operating projects and under-construction assets in India.

 

PLDT acquiring Sky broadband business

PLDT disclosed that it is acquiring Sky Cable for its broadband business. The acquisition price for 100% is P6.75bn. Last year, the PLDT group attempted to acquire Sky through a series of transactions which involved PLDT selling 49% of its TV broadcasting operation. Price then was around P7.4bn for 100%. The deal was apparently blocked for political reasons. It was revealed last year that Sky had ~350,000 broadband subscribers and ~300,000 pay TV subscribers. Regis believes it could be a reasonably good deal for PLDT if it pushes through this time. However, the competition authorities could be a hurdle.

ACEN signs deal to purchase 8 wind projects in Texas

ACEN’s joint-venture company, UPC Power Solutions LLC, signed a purchase and sale agreement with GlidePath Power Solutions LLC for the acquisition of eight wind projects in northern Texas. The portfolio has a total capacity of 136MW in wind assets. In April 2022, ACEN partnered with PivotGen and UPC Solar & Wind Investments to explore opportunities to acquire operating wind projects in the US, extend their useful life and repowering.

Foreign ownership of the Philippine market drops in February

Regis estimates that the proportion of shares held by foreigners fell 28bps MoM to 20.4% while foreign ownership of the PSE Index fell 1.14 percentage points MoM to 23.8% (in February). The much larger drop in PSEi foreign ownership was due to the index rebalancing – RLC and MEG were replaced by DMC and UBP, which have lower foreign ownership. Market net foreign selling amounted to US$142mn in February (from US$122mn of net foreign buying in January). Of the 28 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 14 saw declines in foreign ownership while 14 experienced increases.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

EMI – saw the biggest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 3.32 percentage points MoM to 19.9%. EMI’s parent, Alliance Global, transferred some of its EMI shares from the Singapore Exchange to the Philippine Stock Exchange. 

GTCAP – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership, which rose 1.10 percentage points to 17.70%. Although net foreign buying only amounted to US$6.2mn in February, it appears that the foreign buying during the last few trading days of January was only registered in the February foreign ownership.

BDO – foreign ownership rose 44 basis points to 25.6%. It has been the top foreign-bought stock for four consecutive months. Net foreign buying amounted to US$31.1mn in Feburary.

FDI posts US$634mn net inflows in Dec 2022; FY22 FDI reaches US$9.2bn

Foreign direct investments (FDI) amounted to US$634mn in Dec 2022, lower by 76.2% YoY and 22.4% MoM. This brings FY22 FDI to US$9.2bn, down 23.2% YoY. Non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments and equity capital dropped by 49.5% YoY and 15.6% YoY, respectively. Reinvestment of earnings increased by 5.9% YoY.

Vehicle sales rise 27% YoY, 5% MoM in February

CAMPI/TMA reported unit sales of 30,905 units in February. This is up 27% YoY on low base effects. On a MoM basis, car sales rose by 5%. This brings YT-Feb sales up 34% YoY to 60,404 units. CAMPI is targeting vehicle sales to rise by +10-15% YoY this year. Toyota sold 14,871 units, up 21% YoY and 11% MoM. Their market share improved to 48.1% in Feb from 45.5% in January 2023. This brings YT-Feb sales up 28% YoY to 28,299 units (YTD market share at 47%, down from 50% in end-2022 but higher than pre-Covid: 40%). Regis forecasts assume 23E sales of 166k units (-5% YoY) and GPM of 10.4%.  Assuming Toyota can sell 184k units (+6% YoY), Regis estimates Toyota’s profit could rise by 17% and GTCAP’s profit could rise by 4%. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 47% discount to its market-based NAV of P976/sh and at 6.6x FY23E PER.

DOE chief to assess taxes on energy sector

DOE Secretary Raphael Lotilla said his agency is looking to assess the taxes imposed in the energy sector to determine how these contributed to the high power cost in the country. Lotilla said the government also needs to look into the possible impacts on power cost if it will impose a carbon tax. Additionally, Lotilla said DOE would like to study the proposal on carbon tax “more deliberately and more systematically” to determine the possible effects of its imposition to the energy sector.

PH posts biggest trade deficit in 5 months

Data released by the PSA showed the trade deficit widened by 27.2% YoY to US$5.74bn in January 2023. Exports plunged -10% MoM and -13% YoY to US$5.23bn in January, the lowest since March 2020, due to softening demand from China and electronics. Imports grew 6% MoM and 4% YoY. Fuel, which constitutes 18.8% of imports, saw a 71% increase YoY.  This is in line with the increase in fuel prices, and Fitch Solutions’ projected increase in oil product imports amid the continued growth in fuel demand in 2023. This is the first monthly rise in imports in three months. January 2023’s trade deficit is the biggest since August 2022’s deficit of US$6.02bn.

 

Remittances up 3.5% YoY to US$2.76bn in January

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.5% YoY to US$2.76bn in January 2023. YoY growth a tad slower than 2022’s average growth rate of +3.6% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances rose 11.1% YoY. On a MoM basis, remittances dropped 12.6% largely due to seasonality. Remittances grew across regions/countries, with USA jumping by 5.7% YoY. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 4% YoY to US$33.8bn. This suggests remittances will need to average US$2.83bn per month for the rest of the year to reach the BSP’s target.
