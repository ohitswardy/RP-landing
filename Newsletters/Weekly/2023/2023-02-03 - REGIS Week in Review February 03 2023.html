REGIS REPORT

03 February 2023

The market closed slightly lower to 7,027.38 (-0.4% WoW) for the week as it continued to consolidate at current levels. Most of the activity focused on the PSEi rebalancing at the end of the week. Earlier, an S&P rebalancing also occurred which increased foreign participation in our market to 49% (vs. 31% last week). 

As expected, DMCI Holdings (-8.5% WoW) and Unionbank (+2.7% WoW) were included in the PSEi, at the expense of Robinsons Land (-3.2% WoW) and Megaworld (+0.4% WoW) which were deletions. Additionally, sizeable outflows were seen in Ayala Land (-10.6% WoW) as a cut in its free float affected its index weight. Investors seem to have reallocated to SMPH (+3.5% WoW), which rose for the week, to retain their exposure to property. 

Daily turnover doubled to US$214.5m (vs. US$111.0m the previous week) as trading was very volatile in light of the rebalances. 

Banking names stayed resilient. BDO (+3.3% WoW), Metrobank (+1.70% WoW), and BPI (+0.3% WoW) all ended higher on continued foreign inflows. Foreigners were net buyers for these banks by US$25.6m, US$7.7m, and US$2.7m respectively. Consumer names JFC (+1.5% WoW) and URC (+5.1% WoW) were also strong on the back of foreign buying. 

Overall, foreigners were net buyers in the market for $9.9m.  

The power space was also active after Meralco (+2.2% WoW) warned of rising rates next month. The two-week shutdown of the Malampaya gas field will force some power plants to switch to liquid fuel especially as demand will increase heading into the summer. This is further aggravated as the Court of Appeal allowed San Miguel (+23.0% WoW) to renege one of its fixed price contracts (670 MW) with Meralco.

Meanwhile, SCC (-11.0% WoW) and DMCI Holdings (-8.5% WoW) dropped on the back of falling coal prices as Newcastle coal plunged 31% last Wednesday.

4Q22 EBIT jumps 23% YoY as non-essential formats recover

RRHI’s FY22 NPAT grew 27% YoY to P5.7bn, surpassing Regis and consensus expectations. EBIT (+43% YoY to P8.7bn) also came in ahead of forecasts, while net sales (+17% YoY to P179bn) were broadly in line. For 4Q22 alone, NPAT was down 27% YoY mainly due to non-operating items (net forex losses, higher effective tax rate). EBIT, however, grew strongly (+23% YoY) as non-essential formats continued to recover. Consolidated sales during the quarter grew 17% YoY on the back of 11.8% SSSG.

Essential formats in line

Supermarkets and Drug Stores (collectively 71% of sales) posted SSSG of 11% and 9%, respectively in 4Q. For Supermarkets, holiday spending fueled traffic growth (+20%) even as basket sizes (-10%) were under pressure due to trading down by consumers. For Drug Stores, higher cases of the seasonal flu drove sales of medicines during the quarter. EBITDA figures for both formats were generally in line with expectations.

Non-essentials continue recovery but DIY underperforms

Holiday spending drove SSS and EBITDA margins for Department Stores and Specialty Stores to above pre-pandemic levels during the quarter. Meanwhile, DIY was the main underperformer with SSS levels still ~10% below pre[1]pandemic, largely attributed to heavy discounting to unwind inventory.

Momentum sustained in January but SSSG to normalize in 2023

Momentum continues to be strong coming into 2023, with most formats posting double-digit sales growth in January. RRHI is, however, guiding for blended SSSG to moderate to 4-6% in 2023 (Regis 2023E: 6%) as the YoY base normalizes. We expect that the main drivers for SSSG in 2023 will be 1) the continued recovery of non-essential formats, 2) product mix, and 3) price increases by suppliers.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco sees rate hike amid Malampaya maintenance shutdown

Meralco said the two-week maintenance shutdown (February 4 to 18) of the Malampaya Deepwater Gas-to-Power facility could impact the cost of its operations resulting in a possible increase in consumer electricity bills. Malampaya gas field fuels power plants that supply a little over 2,000 megawatts to Meralco.  Meralco said they will be eventually forced to source fuel from alternative sources.

CA: Suspension of SMC-Meralco power supply deal stays

In a resolution promulgated January 25, the CA granted the petition for a write of preliminary injunction of SPPC, a subsidiary of San Miguel Global Power. This will keep the suspension of the company’s deal to supply 670MW from its Ilijan gas-fired power plant to Meralco in place. “The purpose of injunction is to prevent threatened or continuous irremediable injury to the parties before their claims can be thoroughly studied and adjudicated,” the CA explained. The CA in November issued a temporary restraining order, which has already lapsed after 60 days. Meralco said last week that it would source 670MW of its power requirements from the WESM after its 30-day emergency supply deal with Aboitiz-led GNPower Dingin expired January 25.

ICTSI ends terminal operations in Indonesia’s South Sulawesi

ICTSI and state-owned PT Pelabuhan Indonesia IV (Pelindo IV) have “mutually agreed” not to renew their cooperation agreement at the Makassar Container Terminal in South Sulawesi, Indonesia. ICTSI has been operating the terminal since May 2006. The agreement was originally due to expire on Sep 30, 2013 but was extended to January 31, 2023. Regis estimates the Makassar Container Terminal accounts for <1% of group-wide volumes, 0.1% of revenues and EBITDA. Recall that ICT acquired a 67% stake in PT East Java Dev’t last July 2022. Management believes the multi-purpose port has more attractive growth potential, given that the port can easily be converted into a container terminal in the future.

 

Colliers: 4Q22 office rents continue to fall; residential prices inch up

Colliers reported that 4Q22 Metro Manila office vacancy stayed rose 1.1ppt QoQ to 18.8%. Gross office take-up dropped 34% QoQ to 111,000sqm as BPOs took up less space, likely due to government policy allowing full implementation of WFH policies (if firms transfer their registration from PEZA to BOI). It estimates that office rental rates fell 0.6% QoQ (rental rates are down 8% YoY). Office rental rates are generally 20%-50% lower than pre-COVID levels (mostly 30%-40% down). Colliers is forecasting Metro Manila office vacancy to jump to 20.2% in 2023E and office rental rates to fall 5% for the full-year. Fort Bonifacio office vacancy is only 9.7%. 60% of Megaworld’s office space is in Fort Bonifacio.

 

Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 0.5% QoQ (+4% YoY). Metro Manila condominium prices are generally 10%-25% lower than pre-COVID levels. Colliers expects Metro Manila condominium prices to rise 2% in 2023. In the primary market, condominiums priced over P8mn comprised 34% of total units sold, from only 5% in 2021.

Megawide to upgrade PITX

Megawide plans to spend P5bn over the next few years to develop Lot 2 of the Paranaque Integrated Terminal Exchange (PITX). Lot 2 will stand in a 1.8-hectare property adjacent to the current 2.7-hectare property of PITX. Based on the blueprint, the second lot will be used to put up a similar structure to the existing terminal made up of four levels. The ground level serves as a bus staging area, the floors above it as commercial leasing spaces. Megawide generated P2.6bn of EBITDA from its construction unit and PITX in 9M22. PITX alone generated revenues of P514mn and EBITDA of P428mn in 9M22. Regis estimates Megawide’s gross cash is currently at P15bn, after receiving proceeds from the sale of the Mactan airport. With gross debt (including prefs) at P33bn, Regis estimates its net debt to equity is at 2.2x

SIM registration hits nearly 27 million a month after rollout

Around 26.64 million subscriber identity module (SIM) cards have been registered as of Jan. 28, 2023, which is 15.76% of the 168,977,773 million subscribers nationwide. PLDT Inc.’s Smart Communications, Inc. reported a total of 13,632,034 SIM cards registered, accounting for 20.05% of its 67,995,734 users. Globe Telecom, Inc., reported 10,883,887 registered users, or 12.39% of its 87,873,936 subscribers. DITO Telecommunity Corp. reported a total of 2,121,594 SIM cards registered, or 16.19% of its 13,108,103 subscribers.

Government debt reaches P13.4tn in end-2022

The National Government’s total outstanding debt settled at P13.4tn as of end-2022. This is up by 14.4% YoY (+P1.69tn) but down by 1.7% MoM (-P225bn). Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% (vs. 69.7%) of total while external debt accounted for 31.4% (vs 30.3% in 2021). The debt stock is equivalent to 60.9% of GDP, below the government’s target of 61.8%. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.4% in 2023. It aims to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and further down to 51% by 2028.

Customs collections up 13% YoY in January

The Bureau of Customs collected P65.8bn as of January 27, up 13% than the P58.2bn collected in the same period last year. Collections also exceeded its target of P58.8bn for the month. For 2023, the agency is expected to collect P901bn (+4% YoY); its own internal target is at P921bn (+7% YoY).

Seven key bilateral deals expected to be inked during PBBM’s Japan visit next week

A total of seven economic and security bilateral agreements and documents will be signed during the working visit of President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. in Japan next week according to the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA). This visit will be from February 8 to 12. The agreements will include the notes for the US$3bn infrastructure loan for the North-South Commuter Rail projects.

Commercial banks lending growth slows to 13.4% YoY in December

U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) moderated to 13.4% YoY in December from a revised 13.9% in November. Loans to the manufacturing, ICT, and finance sectors grew at more moderate paces vs. previous months. Loan growth to utilities, trade, real estate, and consumer loans accelerated. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking system rose by 11.5% in November. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Banks, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth accelerated to 6.4% in December from a revised 5.5% in November.

DOE aims to scrap barriers blocking power investments

Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla said one of the stumbling blocks is the secondary price cap (SPC), a mechanism that puts a ceiling on traded power whenever spot prices shoot up in the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM). While the secondary price cap is being implemented to protect consumers from higher WESM prices, the private sector said the imposition of the cap curtails new investments in the energy sector. A secondary price of P6.245/kWh is triggered upon breaching P9.0/kWh rolling average price over a three-day period.

Marcos approves Philippine tax refund plan for foreign tourists

President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. has approved a value-added tax refund program for foreign tourists by 2024. An executive order will be issued in order to implement the tax refund program

If enacted, foreign travelers will be refunded the 12% VAT imposed on goods and services, if they spend above a minimum amount. The measure is among the proposals a private sector advisory council presented to Marcos recently to boost the tourism industry. Other initiatives include expanding the e-visa program to Chinese, Indian, South Korean and Japanese nationals. The government is targeting 4.8mn foreign tourists this year that will generate P2.58tn in revenues. In 2019, the country received 8.3mn tourists.
