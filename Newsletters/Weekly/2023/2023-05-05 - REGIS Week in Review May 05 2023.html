REGIS REPORT

05 May 2023

The market rallied to 6,685.66 (+0.9% WoW) as the Fed signaled a potential pause in the rate hikes post another 25bp policy rate hike this week to the 5.00-5.25% range. Meanwhile, the country’s April inflation print likewise slowed sharply to 6.6% (vs. est. 7%), giving the BSP room to pause in its next MB meeting later this month.

Most names ended higher as gainers outperformed losers 18:11.

Property names ALI (+6.0% WoW) and SMPH (+1.7%) led the market after underperforming the previous weeks. Foreign funds finally flowed into the sector. ALI saw US$5.7m worth of net inflows, while US$4.2m went into SMPH. Earnings were disappointing, with ALI results falling short of forecasts. ALI saw 1Q net profit at P4.5bn (+42% YoY) dragged by commercial lot sales. High-end projects continued to drive the strength while residential sales rose 15% to P27.7bn.

MPI (-0.2% WoW) also saw strong inflows at $3.7m.  1Q profits were surprisingly strong at P4.4bn (+14% YoY) after its power, water, and toll road units reported better-than-expected results. On the tender, Chairman Manuel Pangilinan defended the firm’s decision to delist, although he suggested that the consortium could reconsider if the acceptance level is low. Currently, the tender offer terms still await approval from the PSE.

Consumer names rebounded as well led by MONDE (+9.1% WoW). URC’s (+0.8% WoW) earnings were in line, although could see further upside as the company could benefit from higher sugar prices. Unlike its peers, WLCON (-2.9% WoW) results were slightly disappointing. The slight miss was due to SSSG turning negative in March as sales began to normalize post pandemic. Their rapid store expansion also led to the decline.  

The power sector underperformed. Meralco (-2.7% WoW) declined after its sharp run up the previous week. ACEN (+2.7% WoW) saw the most outflows at $4.8m although locals supported the name as better profitability is expected in the coming quarters as new plants come online. SCC (+5.2% WoW) headline profits were down 40% YoY although core profit doubled QoQ to P9.5bn. These were mildly better than expectations due to lower coal mining costs and higher power ASPs. The company also guided for another special dividends in 2H, which could potentially add another 11% yield from here based on our estimates.

Turnover improved to US$112.9m (+7.8% WoW) although headline foreign outflows were at US$42.6m due to a 2GO tender offer later in the week. Excluding this, the market saw inflows worth US$7.4m.

Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - High base effects mask good QoQ performance
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q23 profit of P9.46bn – Mildly behind estimates

Semirara’s 1Q23 core profit amounted to P9.46bn, which is down 36% from last year’s record performance. Sequentially, profit were still double QoQ as both coal mining and power rebounded sharply vs 4Q. We would characterize the results as trending mildly behind Regis estimates but in line with consensus.

Coal mining ASPs drop 9% QoQ; Sales volume guidance raised

Despite the 9% QoQ drop in coal mining ASPs to P4,427/MT, profits for the segment still jumped 75% QoQ due to (a) higher coal sales volumes (3.5mn MT, +17% QoQ) and (b) reduced pre-stripping activities. Management raised its sales volume guidance to 15-16mn MT from 14.5mn MT, after securing more domestic clients. To achieve our sales volume forecast of 15mn MT (flat YoY), coal sales should improve to c. 3.9mn MT in succeeding quarters. Meanwhile, ICI4 spot prices, the main index SCC follows, are just marginally lower in 2Q23 so far vs. 1Q23.

Power: A solid start to 2023

Profit contributions from its power unit stood at P2.09bn in 1Q23, rebounding sharply YoY/QoQ. Management attributed this to (a) the resumption of Unit 2’s operations, (b) better ASPs from contracts, (c) a higher proportion of WESM sales, and (d) lower costs from the use of non-commercial coal across all four power plant units. With WESM prices hitting over P8/kwh in 2Q (vs. P6.5/kwh in 1Q), earnings should be stronger in succeeding periods.

Another special dividend in 2H

While the sequential drop in coal ASPs could persist, the good news is that (1) coal sales volumes will trend higher sequentially and (2) the power unit is poised to post record earnings in 2Q. These and the company’s healthy cash position (c. P2/sh post-div) should allow the company to pay another hefty special dividend in 2H.

 
ACEN Corp. - EBITDA improves as it turns net seller to spot; other income augments profit
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q23 income jumps as ACEN covers short WESM position

ACEN reported a profit of P2.026bn (507%QoQ, 400%YoY) in 1Q23. Compared to prior periods (1Q22/4Q22), 1Q23 saw ACEN in a modest net sell position to the spot market, allowing it to avoid the roughly P1bn/qtr hit previously incurred from buying expensive replacement power from spot as well as the mismatch between fuel cost and selling price. ACEN also booked P1.831bn in ‘other income’, a substantial contributor to its profits over the last four quarters. The 1Q23 income is tracking in line with estimates (23%/22% of FY23E Regis/consensus), as new capacity starts contributing in 2H23.

Volatility from spot and coal exposure lessen

Absent the impact of being in a net buyer position in the spot market and of, to a lesser extent, the fuel/selling price mismatch for SLTEC, ACEN was able to grow EBITDA to P2.979bn (814%QoQ, 139%YoY). Management noted that ACEN should be in an even larger net seller position in succeeding periods as new capacities come online in 2H (160MW Pagudpud wind, 44MW Arayat Solar Ph2, 283MW San Marcelino Solar Ph1 and 133MW Cagayan Solar Ph1). Much of the >P1bn hit from needing to buy replacement power last year was due to SLTEC going offline. Substantial capacity additions late this year should lessen its reliance on what has been a particularly unreliable plant.

Other income here to stay

ACEN booked P1.8bn in ‘other income’, equivalent to 80% of its profit in the period. We estimate that the recurring portion of this item to be c. P1.4bn, stemming from the coupon on redeemable preferred shares in project companies abroad. The balance would be from a “realized gain from the settlement of a development loan”. The repayment from Solar PH of a loan using its shares (5% stake, P600mn market value) in SPNEC comes to mind.

Long-term agreement for Australia solar farms not exactly a PPA

Management also explained that the long-term energy service agreement signed by its solar farms in Australia with the NSW government was not a PPA but more an option where it can nominate some capacity at a floor price. The deals provide ACEN downside protection on its upcoming capacity in Australia, but management says it still believes power prices will go up again as coal plants are shut over the medium term.

 
PLDT - 1Q23: Business still sluggish
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income up 6%, lifted by lower depreciation charges

Headline income for PLDT for 1Q23 came in P9.02bn, virtually unchanged YoY. Excluding exceptional items but inclusive of losses at Voyager, core income was P7.92bn (+6% YoY). While the latter appears to be trending within full-year expectations, we view the underlying operating results as being sluggish. Core profit was boosted by a drop in non-cash (depreciation) charges.

Mobile revenue dips YoY

Service revenues were 4% higher YoY in 1Q, but mobile revenues actually contracted 1% in the quarter. Management blamed macro conditions (particularly inflation) as well as the effects of SIM registration, which caused a drop in gross subscriber adds. Broadband revenue rose just 8% with a drop in fixed wireless subscribers. On the positive side, the wired broadband segment had net adds of 32k in 1Q23, an improvement from 4Q. It does not appear that PLDT has immediate plans to go into prepaid fiber broadband.

Accelerated depreciation last year boosts earnings in 1Q23

PLDT’s depreciation expenses dropped P865m (7%) YoY despite record capex over the past four years. The accelerated depreciation charges undertaken last year (P51bn) provided a lift to this year’s income. Without such, we estimate core income for 1Q23 would have likely been 15-20% lower.

Update on SIM registration

As of May 3, PLDT said 44.7m subscribers have registered in accordance with requirements of the law. These represent 67% of its subscriber base (44.7m) and “over 80%” of revenue. While PLDT says registration will have little impact on revenue, we expect a short-term hit after the deadline due to SIM deactivations.

Voyager losses largely unchanged

Attributable losses from Voyager were around P700m, little changed YoY. Its digital bank claims to have amassed P21bn of deposits and disbursed > P6bn of loans. As a whole, fintech arm Maya aims to be EBITDA-positive by end-2024.

 
Wilcon Depot - 1Q23 SSSG tapers as margins continue to expand
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q23 revenues tracking mildly behind estimates as SSSG decelerates

WLCON reported 1Q23 NPAT of P962mn, up 13% YoY. This is tracking mildly behind expectations due to a slightly weaker-than-expected top line. Net sales rose 11% YoY as SSSG moderated to 3.3%. Operating margin of 16.8%, however, is tracking within FY23 forecasts. We update our forecasts for 2022 actuals, trim our 2023-24E EPS estimates by 3-4%, and maintain our Buy rating on WLCON and TP of P34/sh.

Same-store sales growth moderates as pent-up demand tapers

SSSG moderated to 3.3% during the quarter mainly due to the slower growth in average baskets (+4%) relative to 4Q22 (+9%). While there are some YoY base effects, management noted that pent-up demand (e.g. backlog of private construction projects from the pandemic) appears to be tapering. Management also noted some cannibalization from new stores in certain areas, which resulted in flattish same-store transaction count.

Continued GPM expansion cushions pre-operating opex for new stores

Gross margin came in at 39.4% in 1Q23, flattish vs 4Q22 but still >100bps higher YoY. This is due to the impact of price adjustments in 2022, and despite the reduced contribution from in-house and exclusives to 50.4% (from 51.2% in 1Q23). Operating margins also received a boost from additional supplier support even as the company was carrying proportionately higher pre-operating expenses for the new stores.

Scale remains to be key advantage amid heightened competition

With supply chain bottlenecks easing, management noted that competitors have started to return, thereby driving up competition in some areas. WLCON is confident, however, that it has the levers to defend market share given its scale, availability of supply, and favorable store locations. Notwithstanding the near[1]term deceleration in demand growth, we believe that WLCON should see healthy EPS growth (+16% 2023-25E CAGR) as it continues to build out new stores and improve margins.

Tweaking 2023-24E EPS estimates, maintaining Buy

We update our forecasts for 2022 actuals and trim our 2023-24E EPS estimates by -3-4%. We are maintaining our Buy rating on WLCON and TP of P34/sh. Our TP implies 2023E multiples of 32x PE and 15x EV/EBITDA, a premium to peers, which we believe is justified given faster 2024E EPS growth (+17%) relative to the peer average (+14%). Key downside risks primarily relate to intensifying competition and softening consumer demand.

 
Aboitiz Power - Lower spot prices, WESM exposure temper profits
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q23 profit down 6%QoQ as power prices soften

AP’s 1Q23 core profit hit P7.628bn (158%YoY, -6%QoQ), up substantially vs. last year but slightly lower QoQ. While energy sales were much higher compared to same period last year, selling prices for both spot sales and BCQ were lower in 1Q23. GNPD’s beneficial contribution dipped 16%QoQ owing to lower WESM prices and higher sales to BCQ. The result is ahead of consensus estimates for the full year but tracking behind our estimates.

Generation: softer prices, lowered WESM exposure temper profits

Income from Generation & RES amounted to P7.4bn (+126%YoY, -9%QoQ). Although energy sales were up in 1Q23 even on a sequential basis, management noted that average selling prices were lower (spot -8%QoQ, BCQ -3%QoQ) than the levels recorded in 4Q22. Management added that WESM exposure slightly dipped after AP signed emergency PSAs with Meralco that contributed to the lower selling prices on a portfolio basis. In particular, GNPD’s beneficial contribution declined 16%QoQ to P4.3bn as it contracted to sell 300MW at P5.95/kWh up to January 25 in an emergency PSA with Meralco.

From management’s perspective, however, the new contracts at near benchmark prices, while less profitable today, were a necessary risk management move. Note that the more recent contracts were signed at much higher rates (GNPD 300MW at P8.525/kWh up to February 25, 1-year PSA using Therma Luzon for 370MW at P8.14/kWh).

Distribution: Volumes up 8%YoY; delayed pass-through of lower power costs lifts income

Distribution income grew to P1.9bn (155%YoY, 7%QoQ) as fuel costs dipped. Average gross margin jumped to P1.97/kWh on the timing difference between the billing of generators vs. its billing to customers. This reversed the margin squeeze it experienced for most of last year amidst rising power costs. Margins should normalize downwards in succeeding periods. Underlying volumes across its utilities were higher by 8%YoY to 1,404GWh, with residential (7%YoY) and commercial & industrial segments (9%YoY0 growing at similar clips.

 
Metro Pacific - Robust 1Q23 results; Tender offer updates
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q23 core profit rises 41% YoY to P4.43bn

Headline 1Q23 profit fell 12% YoY to P5.68bn. Removing one-offs and consolidation adjustments, core income rose 41% YoY to P4.43bn, trending ahead of Regis estimates but appears in line with consensus. Stronger-than[1]expected contributions from power, water, and the tollroad segments more than compensated for the losses incurred by the rail unit and other start-ups.

Tollroad profit up 3% YoY, as start-up costs drag

Profit contributions from the tollroad unit rose by 3% YoY in 1Q23, even as revenues and EBITDA rose 32% and 29% YoY, respectively, thanks to strong traffic volumes in NLEX (+16% YoY), Cavitex (+19%), and SCTEX (+23%) and tariff adjustments. However, the recognition of non-cash and interest expenses from its new roads (CCLEX and Cavitex Link) weighed. Management noted that traffic volumes continued to be strong so far in 2Q, particularly for NLEX and SCTEX. There is also an upcoming tariff adjustment for NLEX this month while tariff adjustments for Cavitex and SCTEX are still awaiting TRB approval.

Higher water tariffs lift Maynilad’s 1Q23 profit

Contributions from the water unit rose 70% YoY, boosted largely by tariff adjustments and a 3% increase in billed volumes at Maynilad. Capex rose 22% YoY to P4.2bn, trailing the company’s P20bn full-year target (+30%). Management admitted that water supply constraints brought about by El Niño are threatening its near-term outlook, but water supply augmentation projects are in place to mitigate the issue.

Rail volumes revert to 67% of pre-Covid; Tender offer updates

The rail unit recorded a wider loss QoQ due to higher non-cash expenses. EBITDA improved QoQ as rail volumes reverted to 65% of pre-pandemic levels in 1Q23 from 59% in 4Q.

MPI Chairman, Mr. Manuel Pangilinan, who is part of the consortium proposing to take the firm private, said that the consortium could reconsider the bid price if the acceptance level is low. The tender offer terms are still awaiting PSE approval and are targeted for release in 1-2 weeks.

 
Cemex Philippines - 1Q23 EBITDA barely breaks even on soft demand, entry of new competition
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q23 net loss at P356mn

CHP’s results remained dismal, recording a net loss of P356mn in 1Q23. EBITDA disappointed, falling by 90% YoY to a mere P102mn. Demand remained weak during the quarter, amidst more intense competition. Moreover, the company still had high-cost fuel inventory and has yet to benefit from the collapse in global thermal coal prices. While there could be margin relief in succeeding periods with fuel costs lower, we believe the near-term prospects for revenue recovery are uncertain.

Sales volumes drop 16% YoY

CHP reported its sixth straight fall in quarterly sales volumes, with 1Q23 sales volumes down by 16% YoY. Sequentially, sales volumes dropped 2% QoQ. Management estimates industry demand also fell by “mid-single digits YoY”, as demand from private and public sectors contracted. The company lowered its sales volume guidance this year to “low single-digit decrease” from “flat- to low[1]single digit decrease”, implying that it believes sales volumes will post growth rates at some point this year.

ASPs flat QoQ; competition intensifying

ASPs were flat QoQ, even though estimated landed costs of bagged cement dropped 10% QoQ. Management noted that a new player, Century Peak, which has a 1mn MT clinker facility in Cebu, started operations early this year. We thus believe downward pressure on prices could ensue.

High cost fuel inventory in 1Q should fade

EBITDA margins remained low at just 2% in 1Q23, despite the sharp drop in Newcastle thermal coal prices since early February. The company still had high[1]cost fuel inventory for most of the period, but management noted that they should feel the benefits of lower fuel prices beginning 2Q.

ACEN secures contracts for Australia solar farms

ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) disclosed that its Australian units secured long-term energy service agreements for its two solar projects at the New South Wales government’s first renewable energy and storage auction. The energy service agreements are for ACEN Australia’s 720MW New England solar project and the 400MW Stubbo solar project. The LTESAs offer the rights to access a minimum price for generation projects over a 20-year time frame, which protects investors like us from the risk of unexpectedly low wholesale electricity prices”. ACEN Australia has around 1GW capacity under construction and around 8GW capacity in the development pipeline. In March, management said it was in advanced stages of contracting output and was considering 8-10 year contracts at prices that were then lower than spot.

LT Group has officially named Lucio C. Tan II as its president after a year-long transition period, replacing his uncle Michael G. Tan. The 30-year old is the grandson of LT Group Chairman and CEO Lucio C. Tan and the son of the late Lucio Tan, Jr. Mr. Tan III also serves as the president and COO of Tanduay Distillers and vice-president of PAL Holdings. AllHome (HOME PM) posts P1.2bn recurring profit in FY22

HOME posted recurring net profit of P1.2bn in FY22, down 19% YoY. The recurring figure excludes one-off losses from a fire that damaged 3 of its stores located in Alabang in 1Q22. FY22 net sales declined 12% YoY to P12.6bn on the back of a 13% decline in SSS (ex-Alabang stores). This implies 4Q22 SSS was down 18-19%. The company cited weaker sales from the hard categories, which accounted for ~30% of sales as of 9M22. FY22 operating profit declined 18% YoY as margins contracted ~100bps YoY. For 4Q22 alone, Regis estimates operating margins improved mainly on GPM expansion. Regis estimates GPM was at 38.5% in 4Q22.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Budget deficit widens in March

The government recorded a budget deficit of P210bn in March, up 12% YoY and 98% MoM. Revenues dropped at a faster YoY rate than expenditures. Tax revenues fell 8.2% YoY due to the shift to quarterly filing of VAT returns from monthly filing previously. Other revenues fell 29% YoY due to high base effects of dividend remittances. Expenditures fell 2.6% YoY due to the lower national tax allocation shares of LGUs and timing of significant releases for the fuel subsidy program. In 1Q23, the budget deficit narrowed by 14.5% YoY to P270.9bn. Revenues should catch up in succeeding periods to reach the government’s end-2023 deficit target of P1.5tn (6.1% of GDP).
