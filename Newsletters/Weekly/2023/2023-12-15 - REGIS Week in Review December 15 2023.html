REGIS REPORT

15 December 2023

The market ran sharply for the week, gaining 3.9% WoW to 6,478.44 after the Fed paused rates (in line with market expectations) during its last meeting of the year. Fed chairman Jay Powell also commented that rate cuts are finally on the table. The Fed’s “dot plot” now suggests 75bp of rate cuts in 2024, with more in 2025. These remain less aggressive than what markets are pricing in (at least 100bp in 2024).

The Bangko Sentral likewise followed suit, keeping policy rates steady at 6.5%.

Share prices soared on the back of improving macroeconomic conditions. The rally was broad based as gainers outnumbered losers 20:11.

Large cap conglomerates SM (+9.7% WoW) and Ayala Corp (+14.8% WoW) rallied on the back of strong foreign demand. SM and AC saw US$5.5m and US$9.4m worth of inflows respectively.

Property names saw a healthy boost too. Ayala Land  (+4.9% WoW) and SM Prime (+1.4% WoW) led the sector as investors bet that rates may have peaked. Ayala Land was the top bought name by foreign investors for US$9.5m.

Consequently, banks BDO (+2.4% WoW), BPI (-0.3% WoW) and MBT (-1.0% WoW) underperformed the index.

ACEN (-4.4% WoW) was the biggest drag as locals continue to lighten up on their holdings after recently announcing a property -for-share swap with AREIT for 276 ha of industrial land.

The FTSE rebalancing also occurred last Friday. There were no additions or deletions made, but estimated inflows were at US$16m. SMC (-0.2% WoW), BLOOM (+3.1% WOW), and CNPF (-1.8% WoW) were expected to see the biggest upweights for this rebalancing. However, all names ended in the red.

Daily turnover improved to US$99.9m (+29.2% WoW). However, headline outflows still stood at US$1.3m. Top sold names were PGOLD and BPI for US$4.0m and US$3.0m respectively.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Globe, Frontier close deal for sale of 120 telco towers

Globe Telecom disclosed that it has reached another milestone in its P71bn tower sale initiative, after closing another deal with Frontier Tower Associates Philippines, Inc. It has completed the sale of an additional 120 towers to Frontier for P1.5bn. This latest transaction brings the total number of towers acquired by Frontier Towers to 2,214 out of the 3,529 earmarked for sale. The recent closing is part of the agreements signed with MIESCOR Infrastructure Development Corp and Frontier for the sale of 5,709 telco towers for P71bn.

Okada operator signs deal for majority stake in Emerald Bay Resort in Cebu

Universal Entertainment Corp said its subsidiary Tiger Resort, Leisure and Entertainmet, Incorporated (TRLEI), which manages the Okada Manila resort has reached a preliminary deal with a subsidiary of Dennis Uy’s PH Resorts Group Holdings. The agreement is for buying stakes in two of PH Trvel and Leisure Holdings’ subsidiaries – Lapulapu Lesiure and Lapulapu Land, which are working on the Emerald Bay Resort project in Cebu. Uy previously signed agreements with potential buyers, namely Bloomberry Resorts in 2022 and Cebu-based developer AppleOne in 2023. The definitive agreement between the two companies could be executed by July 2024, with the goal of having the Emerald Bay Resort and Casino open in 2026. A new casino resort in Cebu may not materially impact the competitive landscape (primarily Entertainment City operators) given the much smaller market and niche product offering of the location. However, a sale could improve the gearing situation of PH Resorts and the Udenna Group.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Tourist arrivals revert to 64% of pre-Covid in November

Tourist arrivals in November reached 440k, equivalent to 64% of 2019 average. This is up from 407k in October (59% of pre-Covid) due to higher visitor arrivals from Korea, USA, and Japan, which were 71%, 75%, 46% of pre-covid levels, respectively. Chinese tourists still lagging, at just 15% of pre-Covid levels.

Maharlika to start as national development fund

The Maharlika Investment Fund will start as a national development fund in the first five to seven years according to Maharlika Investment Corp President and CEO Joel Consing. Maharlika has an initial fund of P75bn, which will eventually rise to P125bn. Once the fund generates excess funds, then it can go into other investment opportunities, including fixed-income instruments, domestic and corporate bonds. Mr. Consing aims to convert unutilized government land into mega ecoozones for FDI; ecozones will focus on agriculture and forestry, as well as renewable energy. These plans are still subject to the approval of MIC’s board of directors, the members of which are expected to be announced soon by Malacanang.

End-November GIR settles at US$101.3bn

Preliminary data showed the GIR rose to US$101.3bn in end-November from US$101.0bn in end-October. This represents 7.5 months’ worth of imports. This reflected mainly upward valuation adjustments in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings and net income from investments abroad. The BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$99.5bn by end-2023.

MWSS approves second tranche of water rate hike

 

Water regulator, MWSS, approved an increase of P6.41 (+17%) per cubic meter (cum) for Manila Water (MWC PM), and a hike of P7.87 (+20%) per cum for Maynilad Water. The latest adjustment in water rates is the second tranche of the approved tariffs for the 2023 to 2027 period, as well as two thirds of CPI. Recall that in the 2022 rate rebasing, MWC and Maynilad were granted “R” tariff adjustments of +73% and +38%, respectively, which will be spread out over five years. Regis valuation/profit estimates assume these tariff hikes. MWC is a 22%-owned unit of Ayala Corp. (AC PM) and accounts for 3% of its NAV; AC trades at 26% discount to its market-based NAV of P869/sh. Maynilad is a 25%-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM) and accounts for 25% of its NAV; DMC trades at a 24% discount to its market-based NAV of P12.30/sh.

Loan growth accelerates to 7.1% in October

Loan growth for universal and commercial banks (UKBs) accelerated in October to 7.1% YoY, from 6.5% in September. The improvement was seen nearly across the board, especially for loans “for production”.

A few sectors were worth noting:

Construction loans jumped 10.1% YoY, from 5.9% in September.

Wholesale and retail trade loans sped up to 9.3% YoY, up from 6.6%

Real estate loans grew 8.3% YoY, compared to 5.0% the prior month

 

The construction and real estate loans could be indicative of increased capex and/or government spending. Meanwhile, the wholesale/retail loans might mean traders are (finally) building up inventory for the holiday season. During the 3Q23 briefings, some banks were worried that the sluggish wholesale/retail loans might be that traders were anticipating weak consumer demand in 4Q23.

 

On the consumer (ex-mortgage) side, growth slowed to 22.8% YoY in October, from 23.5% the previous month. However, the drag came from salary loans, which decelerated sharply to 6.5% YoY. Meanwhile, credit card growth was steady at 30.4% YoY, while motor vehicle loans sped further to 14.4% YoY (from 13.4%).

 

While one month is not a trend, the improvement is welcome news. Regis forecasts incorporate a return to low double-digit loan growth in 2024/25E. Meanwhile, M3 grew 8.2% YoY in October, the same pace as in September.

Senate to ratify FTA with South Korea by January

The Philippine Senate aims to ratify the Free Trade Agreement with South Korea when Congress resumes session in January. International agreements and treaties require a concurrence of two-thirds of the Senate’s members for ratification. South Korea accounts for 5% of PH exports and 7% of imports. The FTA aims to eliminate tariffs of agricultural and industrial products. Under the deal, the 5% import duty on South Korean cars will be lifted while duties on electric and hybrid vehicles from South Korea will be eliminated within five years. Cars from South Korea (i.e. Hyundai and Kia) accounted for 2% of 2022 car sales. The removal of duties poses a threat to Toyota’s operations. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 28% of profit and gross asset value (37% of NAV).

FDI net inflows fall 42% YoY to US$422mn in September

Net foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows fell 42% YoY to US$422mn in September. This brings 9M23 net FDI to US$5.88bn, down 15.9% YoY. Bulk of gross equity capital placements came from Japan, Singapore, USA, and Germany and these were invested in manufacturing (50%), real estate (14%), and financial/insurance (12%).

 

Congress ratifies P5.77tn 2024 budget

Congress ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee’s report on the P5.77tn 2024 budget. The Bicam approved a P450bn increase in unprogrammed appropriations to P731.4bn. Unprogrammed appropriations are put on standby in case of additional priority programs or projects when revenue collection exceeds targets. No material changes to the proposed budget of the Cabinet.

Trade deficit widens to US$4.18bn in October

Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit widened by 26% YoY to US$4.18bn in October. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit was also higher by 17% compared to September’s deficit of US$3.58bn. Imports fell by 4% YoY to US$10.54bn, slowest monthly decline in 7 months. Fuel and raw material imports fell by 5% and 7%, respectively, due to lower commodity prices. Capital goods imports also fell by 5% but consumer goods imports rose by 5%. Meanwhile, exports collapsed by 17% YoY to US$6.36bn due largely to base effects (Exports peaked at US$7.71bn in Oct 2022). However, Oct exports appear to be recovering relative to the monthly average in 9M23. This brings YT-October trade deficit to US$44.07bn, down 12% YoY.

BSP keeps policy rate steady at 6.5%

The BSP held its key policy rate at 6.5% for the second consecutive meeting, in line with consensus. It said that the risks to the inflation outlook still lean significantly to the upside. The upside risks include transport charges, electricity prices, and oil prices

New inflation forecasts:

2023 – 6.0%, from 6.1% previously

2024 – 4.2%, from 4.4% previously

2025 – 3.4%

Inflation is expected to return to the target 2%-4% range by 1Q24. The BSP then expects it to accelerate in 2Q24 (due to El Nino and the delayed impact of wage hikes) before returning back to the range in 3Q24. BSP Governor Remolona said that “The Monetary Board continues to see the need to keep monetary policy settings sufficiently tight to allow inflation expectations to settle more firmly within the target range”
