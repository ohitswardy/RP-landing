REGIS REPORT

20 April 2023

The market saw a boost later in the week as strong earnings results, especially for banks, came through.

The PSEi closed 1.1% higher led by BPI (+3.5% WoW) and BDO (+2.3% WoW).

BPI saw 1Q23 NPAT jump 52%, way ahead of estimates, at P12.1bn. Though most of the beat was driven by lower provisions, NPLs stayed benign. Loans continued to grow although at a slower pace of 13.6% (vs. 15.3% in 4Q22).

Meanwhile, BDO NPAT came in at P16.5bn (+41% YoY) as NIMs expanded another 11 bps QoQ, and could further expand due to the increase in the credit card rate cap (from 2% to 3% a month). Given the strong results, both banks could expect upgrades from the street.  

BDO saw foreign inflows of US$7.3m and BPI US$1.2m.

PIZZA ran (+4.8% WoW) as headline earnings were already above pre-pandemic levels, despite net margins still lagging their pre-pandemic average (8.6% vs 10-11%), suggesting further upside risk especially if margins are expected to normalize eventually.  

PGOLD’s (+0.6% WoW) 4Q performance were likewise ahead of expectations (NP rose 14% to P9.3b) as it was supported by holiday spending although 2023 outlook remains cautious given continued pressure on consumer wallets.  

Outside earnings, most names were quiet. Daily turnover registered at the lowest for the year at just US$65.2m (vs. YTD average of US$132m).

RESEARCH
Vista Land - 4Q22: residential revenue downtrend continues
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment remains weak

Vista Land’s FY22 headline profit fell 5% to P6.1bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. This came despite a non-cash lift to rental revenues. Meanwhile, the residential segment remained weak, with residential revenue continuing its downtrend and past-due residential receivables staying high.

Residential revenue declines YoY for the 13the consecutive quarter

4Q22 residential revenue fell 30% YoY, marking the 13th consecutive quarter of YoY decline. While management attributes the falling revenue to longer payment schemes, we suspect that cancellations have also risen. Furthermore, we estimate that 37% of residential receivables are more than 90 days past due. Thus, the risk of residential cancellations arguably remains high.

High-end projects sell more briskly

4Q22 reservation sales climbed 16% YoY (+3% QoQ), bringing the FY22 figure up 12% to P65.5bn. Similar to the experience of some other developers, Vista’s high-end brand, Brittany, saw the fastest growth. OFWs continue to comprise 55%-60% of residential sales, implying that the company has been able to tap a more affluent OFW segment. Vista’s Mega Manila land bank has benefitted from infrastructure projects. Thus, management intends to shift its product offerings in these areas from low-cost (Camella) house and lot units in Mega Manila, and instead launch mid-to-high end housing projects as well as mid-to-high rise condominiums.

Rental revenue jumps, already 78% above 2019 level

Although the company’s rental floor area only expanded 1% YoY, FY22 rental revenue jumped 48% YoY to P13.7bn, fully 78% higher than the 2019 figure. However, rental receivables increased 42% YoY to P27.5bn, equivalent to two years of rental revenue, arguably implying that revenue is overstated relative to cash rent.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PGOLD – momentum sustained in 4Q22 but outlook cautious amidst inflationary pressure, competition

PGOLD reported FY22 NPAT +14% yoy to P9.3bn — broadly in line with Regis (P9.5bn) but ahead of consensus (P8.6bn). FY22 net sales +12% yoy to P184.3bn, in line with our estimates. Meanwhile, EBIT was P14.5bn (+12% yoy), mildly behind expectations. For 4Q22 alone, NPAT rose 15% YoY to P2.8bn.

 

On the core Puregold brand:

SSSG accelerated to +10% in 4Q, partly due to a low YoY base. Same-store foot traffic continued to grow strongly (+10% in 4Q by our estimate) due to the reopening, though same-store tickets grew at a softer pace (+1-2%). Management noted that high inflation continues to pressure average baskets for the core Puregold customer, especially in 1Q23 after the bump from holiday spending had subsided. EBITDA margin was largely stable during the quarter. Gross margin mildly expanded YoY due to the increase in supplier support, but this was weighed by proportionately higher opex.

 

On S&R:

SSSG slowed to +15% in 4Q, due to a slowdown in the growth of average baskets. We believe this was largely due to more competitive pricing action, which also led to a 120-bp contraction in gross margins. EBITDA margin was further pressured by higher opex, mostly relating to pre-operating expenses for the new stores in the pipeline. S&R did not open any new stores in 2022 but plans to ramp up expansion with 4 new stores in 2023. The company has earmarked P11.9bn in capex in 2023, of which around half will be used for Puregold and S&R’s store expansion. An additional P3.5bn has been allocated for investments in logistics and distribution centers to support the new stores to be opened over the next few years.

 

Overall, management’s 2023 outlook was cautious given the continued pressure on consumer wallets amidst high inflation (Puregold) and rising competition (S&R). Management expects to grow consolidated top line by 7-8% (Regis FY23E: +8%), and ~100bps gross margin contraction for both Puregold and S&R. The guidance implies a 5-10% contraction in operating profit, which is, in our view, on the conservative side. While there could be pressure on gross margins, we believe there will also likely be operating leverage as the newer stores ramp up.

 

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported NPAT of P16.5bn for 1Q23,

BDO NPAT of P16.5bn for 1Q23 is up 41% YoY (-3% QoQ), accounting for 25% of Regis (P65.94bn) and consensus forecasts (P66.04bn) for FY2023. Revenue growth was driven primarily by NII, which jumped 28% YoY on 8% growth in “customer loans”, and NIMs which expanded 48bps YoY and 11bps QoQ. Despite the sharp rise in interest rates, asset quality remained benign, with NPL ratio a tad higher at 1.98% (from 1.95% in 4Q) and NPL cover up to 170% (from 167%), which allowed the bank to drop provision expense by 15% YoY and 24% QoQ.

 

It would seem that earnings momentum is still tilted upwards. Note that the increase in the credit card rate cap was only raised from 2% to 3% a month in mid-February. Regis is mindful, though of the sudden jump in deposits costs in 1Q, though this is still likely the lowest in the industry. The bank will hold a briefing on Thursday, 20 April.

 

Highlights:

 

Headline net loan growth slowed to 8.9% YoY in 1Q23 from 10.0% at end-2022, while the bank reckons that gross customer loans grew 8%, slower than the 8.9% the previous quarter. No breakdown was given for loan growth in the bank’s press release, or FS. Expect more details at the briefing. Deposits, meanwhile, grew 14%, taking LDR down to 82%.

 

NIMs expanded 11bps QoQ (and +48bps YoY). Loan yields were up 33bps QoQ (+140bps YoY), on higher benchmark rates. The rate cap on credit card rates was also lifted from 2% a month to 3% effective mid-February, which suggests continued upward pricing momentum heading into 2Q23. Meanwhile, deposit costs jumped 30bps QoQ (+86bps YoY), as BDO’s CASA ratio dipped further to 76%, normalizing back to pre-Covid levels.

 

Non-interest income was up 13% YoY. Trading and fx gains were up 25% YoY as market interest rates dropped QoQ. Despite the increase in policy rates, the 10-year BVAL fell c80bps from 7.0% at end-2022 to 6.2% at end-1Q23. Fee incomes were up 20%, while insurance premiums were flat YoY.

 

Opex jumped 17% YoY, driven in large part by a 51% spike in Taxes and Licenses. Regis believes this is due to the near-doubling of time deposits (TDs) YoY, as banks absorb the documentary stamp tax (DST) levied on TDs. Meanwhile, compensation expense rose 14% YoY.

 

PPOP was up 33% YoY as operating leverage kicked in. Provision expense dropped 15% YoY and 24% QoQ as asset quality remained benign despite the sharp increase in interest rates. NPL ratio inched up to 1.98% from 1.95% at end-2022. However, Regis reckons the absolute PHP value was flat QoQ as the loan base was down 1.5% QoQ. Despite the drop in provisions, NPL coverage improved further to 170% (from 167% in 4Q22), giving the bank ample buffer for potential NPL hits. Meanwhile, restructured loans (RLs) were down 28% YoY, and Regis estimates the RL ratio at around 3.3% of total loans, down from close to 6% at the peak in 2Q2021.

 

Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM) FY22 NPAT jumps to P874mn, ahead of expectations and above pre-pandemic levels

PIZZA reported FY22 NPAT of P874mn, ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. The absolute figure is above pre-pandemic levels, though net margin of 8.6% is still behind the pre-pandemic average of 10-11%. FY22 revenues surged 85% YoY to P10.1bn (ahead) on the back of 37% SSSG. For 4Q22 alone, PIZZA posted NPAT of P420mn, up 2.7x from 4Q21. SSS jumped 46% during the quarter, which implies SSS levels (ex-Potato Corner) are already back to pre-pandemic. Gross and EBITDA margins during the quarter contracted nearly 300bps, likely due to pressure from input cost inflation.

NWRB lifts Maynilad’s Angat allocation to avert service interruptions

National Water Resources Board (NWRB) announced the approval of a 52 cubic meters per second (cms) allocation to the MWSS for the April 16-30 period. This is up from just 50 cms between April 1-15. During this period, Maynilad will receive all the extra allocation and should be sufficient to minimize the water interruptions in its area. Maynilad customers have reported up to 12 hours of daily water interruptions. Normally, the two water concessionaries receive 48cms from Angat, of which 60% goes to Maynilad and 40% to Manila Water. Based on the most recent PAGASA forecast, near normal rainfall will be received in the Angat area from May to September 2023.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Prices of building materials continue to soften in March

PSA showed the growth in Consumer building Material Retail Price Index (CMRPI) in the NCR decelerated further to 4.1% in March from 5.4% in February, which marked the eighth consecutive month the CMRPI posted a slower growth rate.

 

The PSA cited the following as primary drivers of the continued downtrend of CMRPI in March:

Miscellaneous construction materials grew at a slower pace of 6.2% in March from 9.3% in February. Plumbing materials posted a 2.2% uptick in March from 4% in the previous month. Tinsmithry materials had a slower increase of 4.7% in March from 5.9% in February.

Remittances rise by 2.4% YoY in February

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew by 2.4% to US$2.57bn in February. This is a slower pace than in January (+3.5%). The 3-month average growth rate at +4% YoY in February from +5% YoY in the previous month. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 9.4%. Land-based remittances rose by 2.6% YoY, lifted by higher remittances from Asia and the Middle East. Meanwhile, sea-based remittances grew at a steady rate of +1.6%. The 2M23 remittances reached US$5.33bn, up 3% YoY. This is broadly in line with the BSP’s FY23 target of +3% YoY.

 

BOP surplus hits US$1.27bn in March

The overall balance of payments position posted a surplus of US$1.3bn in March. This is a reversal of the US$895mn BOP deficit in Feb 2023 but is 68% YoY higher. The govt cited inflows arising mainly from the government’s net foreign currency loans and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. The government received US$1.6bn loans from the World Bank, ADB, and IBRC. In 1Q23, BOP is at a surplus of US$3.5bn, up from US$495mn surplus in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final end-March GIR level stood at US$101.5bn (up from a prelim figure of US$100.2bn). This is higher than the US$98.2bn figure in February and is equivalent to 7.6 months’ worth of imports.

LPC: Metro Manila 1Q23 office vacancy flat QoQ at 18%

Leechiu Property Consultants said that Metro Manila 1Q23 office vacancy was flat QoQ at 18%. LPC estimates that vacancy in Fort Bonifacio was only 9%. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 20%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Total nationwide net office take-up dropped to 161,000sqm, from 177,000sqm in 3Q22. BPO nationwide net office take-up dropped to 78,000sqm, from 127,000sqm in 4Q22. Metro Manila net office take-up was likely about 100,000sqm, on track to meet Regis FY23 forecast of 400,000sqm.

 

Prior to the POGO industry entering the country, Metro Manila annual office take-up was 470,000sqm to 530,000sqm. Regis forecasts Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 20% in 2023E. Supply will increase due to new office completions. Regis also expects demand to be muted due to WFH policies – several BPO firms have transferred their registration from PEZA to BOI, which does not set limits on WFH.
