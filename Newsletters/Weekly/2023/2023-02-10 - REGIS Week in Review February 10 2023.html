REGIS REPORT

10 February 2023

The market dropped -2.1% this week breaking its 7000 support level. 

Daily turnover slowed to US$124.6m (-41.9% WoW) after last week’s PSEi rebal. Nonetheless, residual trades from the rebalancing the previous week continued to flow through while both local and foreign funds also started to act ahead of the upcoming MSCI rebalancing.  As announced late in the week, there will be no additions while ACEN (-6.3% WoW) and GLO (-5.3% WoW) will be deleted. These changes will be effective at the close of February 28, 2023. These two contributed to the decline for the week.                

Meralco (+5.4% WoW) soared after it unexpectedly saw an upweight in the MSCI. Note that its large decline the previous weeks was mostly on speculation of its deletion. 

The rest of the market remained choppy. Local and foreign investors seem to be on opposite sides of the market. Foreign outflows for the week were at US$33.0m as they let go of conglomerates AC (-4.37% WoW) and SM (-3.3% WoW) for US$6.3m and US$6.1m respectively while locals are slowly accumulating across the board especially during pullbacks. 

Consumer stocks fell with MONDE (-7.8% WoW) taking the biggest hit among its peers. 

In other news, Metro Pacific (+6.3% WoW) is set to buy out 34.76% of Axelum Resources (-0.07% WoW) for P5.3bn (Php 4.05 per share). This would bring MPI to be the largest shareholder. No tender offer will take place as the transaction will not bring MPI’s stake to above 35.0%. 

Corporate earnings are starting to come out as well. BPI (-3.5% WoW) results were solid, with loan growth, NIMs and asset quality all improving.  Globe (-5.3% WoW) however saw disappointing results with service and broadband revenues the drags.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property - A tale of three segments
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Each at its own pace

The three main property segments are in various stages of recovery (or lack thereof): (1) mall profitability is approaching pre-COVID levels, (2) the residential segment has begun to bottom out, while (3) the office segment is likely to see worse days ahead.

We reckon that the outperformance of SMPH has run its course and downgrade it to Hold. Investors should, in our view, search for value through other developers, such as RLC, MEG, and ALI.

Malls rebound; residential trends turn positive; office vacancy to rise further

Malls are showing the strongest rebound among the major segments and we forecast revenue to grow another 30% in 2023E. In addition, spin-offs of mall assets into REITs could lead to NAV increases of up to 15% for some developers.

While risks remain in the residential segment, several positive trends have emerged which we expect to continue: rising prices, declining defaults and improving reservation sales. On the other hand, we forecast office vacancy to rise further as work-from-home crimps demand while new supply is added. Thus, we forecast negative rental reversions in 2023E/2024E.

RLC is our top pick in the sector; prefer ALI among the large caps

We forecast RLC to show the fastest earnings growth in the sector (relative to 2019) and, on our estimates, it would be the biggest beneficiary of a mall REIT spin off. We also consider valuations to be compelling (see Figure 33 on page 10). Among the large caps, we prefer ALI due to our strong growth projections over the next two years (presales CAGR of 20%, earnings CAGR of 25%) and the NAV enhancement we expect from infrastructure projects adjacent to its land bank.

Downgrade SMPH to Hold

We made only minor tweaks to most of our target prices but slash our targets on FLI and VLL by 19% as we account for operational struggles and high debt levels. Meanwhile, we downgrade SMPH to Hold on valuations. See pages 9-10 for changes to our estimates. Key risks: POGOs, interest rates, and REIT listings.

 
Bank of the Philippine Islands - FY22 Review: Trends holding – NIMs rising, NPLs falling, IT spend catching up
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

FY22 NPAT in line, maintaining forecasts and Buy recommendation

BPI reported headline FY22 NPAT of P39.6bn, up 66% YoY, in line with Regis forecasts, and slightly ahead (+3.7%) of consensus. Loan growth was sustained at 15% YoY. NIMs continued to expand in 4Q22 – though at a slower pace than in 3Q – and are likely to widen further into 2023. NPL ratio was back to pre-Covid levels at 1.74%. Meanwhile, opex jumped 26% in 4Q22 as IT expenses finally caught up. While management expressed disappointment with customers’ digital adoption, BPI is pushing through with its rollout of its IT initiatives.

Loan growth sustained at 15%, NIM expansion likely to persist into 2023

Loan growth was sustained at 15% YoY, driven by corporate loans (+15.5%) and credit cards (+31%). Mortgages were up just 2.6% YoY due to a drop in developer receivables finance; end-user mortgages were up c10% YoY. Auto loans accelerated to 14% (was 12% in 3Q). Management estimates NIMs expanded 29bps YoY for FY22 to 3.59%. For 4Q alone, the pace of NIMs increase slowed to +9bps QoQ to 3.75%, due to rising funding costs. NIMs should continue to widen into 2023E on further loan repricing, and on a higher credit card rate cap.

NPLs back to pre-Covid levels, coverage tops 180%

NPL ratio fell 18bps QoQ to 1.74%, back to pre-Covid levels. Despite a 42% drop in 4Q22 provisions to P1.67bn, NPL cover increased to 180% from 177% in 3Q22. So far, the bank has not seen asset quality slippage from higher interest rates. Over the near-term, management expects to bring NPL cover down to 150%.

Opex surged 26% in 4Q22 on IT catch-up, to moderate into 2023E

Opex in 4Q22 jumped 26% YoY and QoQ as IT spend caught up due to completion of digitalization projects. While management expressed disappointment with customers’ digital take-up, it continues to push its new e-wallet (VYBE), and will launch new wealth management and stock trading apps in 1H23. For 2023E, the bank expects opex to moderate from the P17.9bn level in 4Q, and sees opex growth in the high-single-digits range for 2023E, in line with our forecasts.

 
Globe Telecom - FY22 earnings disappoint; broadband stalls but corporate data gains
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core earnings disappoint on revenue

Globe reported profits of P34.6bn for FY22, inclusive of exceptional items (e.g. gains from tower sales). Stripping those items out, core income would have been P19.2bn, which is below our estimates. Revenue did not see as big a QoQ bump as was expected, and higher expenses also led to a drop in EBITDA margins.

Revenue lift from new sources

Revenue rose +5% despite mobile revenue growth being only +3%. Mobile data yields dipped another 6% QoQ in 4Q, reflecting continued pricing pressure. Corporate data, an oft-overlook segment, was a bright spot, with FY22 revenue rising +21%. The smaller, non-telco revenue sources also led, doubling YoY.

Broadband subscriber count dips in 4Q

The wired broadband subscriber base was 1.28m as of end-2022, implying a small net dip in 4Q. Management reiterated its view that the post-paid wired broadband market is now at or near saturation. Growth for wired broadband will thus need to come from prepaid users. Fixed wireless broadband subscribers, on the other hand, also continued to decline, ending the year at 1.43m. Home broadband revenue (wired + wireless) was down 8% for the year at P27.1bn.

Non-telco businesses growing

Globe’s share of Mynt’s FY22 earnings was P0.8bn, suggesting Mynt’s profits amounted to ~P2.2bn for FY22 (below expectations) and ~P0.4bn for 4Q. Globe’s non-telco subsidiaries delivered P4.2bn of aggregate revenue (2.7%).

Focus on cash generation as capex drops

Globe says it expects capex to drop from P101bn (US$1.9bn) in FY22 to around US$1.3bn in FY23 as it shifts focus to cash generation. On that capex, we estimate Globe is likely to be around FCF breakeven this year, from being deeply FCF negative in FY21 and FY22. Note Globe is set to receive another ~P61bn of proceeds from the sale of its towers this year.

MPI acquires 34.76% of Axelum Resources Corp.

MPI entered into a sale and purchase agreement with various shareholders of Axelum Resources Corp. (AXLM) to acquire a 34.76% economic stake in AXLM for P3.83/sh. MPI is acquiring 1.19bn common shares (31.3%) in AXLM for P4.05/sh payable in cash to various shareholders. It is also subscribing to 200mn (5.3%) redeemable preferred shares in AXLM for P2.50/sh, which can be converted to 200mn new common shares after 3 years. Total consideration is P5.32bn, payable in cash once regulatory approvals are secured. As at end-Sep 2022, MPI’s parent-level cash and debt stood at P13.1bn and P83.2bn, respectively. Regis estimates the acquisition will be mildly dilutive (<1%, or P100mn) to MPI’s profit, assuming current borrowing rates.

BPI fined P135mn for SEC filing error for ESOP in 2013

BPI disclosed that the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has fined the bank P134.6mn for failing to secure the proper paperwork for its employee stock option plan (ESOP) for 2013-2019. Regis understands from BPI management that this was an oversight on their part. The bank will accept the penalty, but may appeal the size of the fine.

 

Here is the timeline.

In 2013, BPI was about to launch a new seven-year ESOP program for 2013-2019. New share issues need to be registered with the SEC. For ESOPs, companies need to apply for exemption from having to register every stock issue. In 2013, BPI had shares leftover from a previous ESOP. The SEC approved for the bank to use those previously-exempted shares as part of the 2013-19 plan.

 

However, when those shares ran out, BPI continued to issue ESOP shares. There were some personnel movements in BPI, and the new personnel assumed (wrongly) that the entire ESOP for 2013-19 was already approved for exemption. They thus didn’t bother applying for a new exemption from registration.

In 2021, BPI filed with the SEC to put a new ESOP in place. It was only then that the non-filing for exemption for the 2013-19 ESOP was discovered. The SEC issued the assessment only on 03 February 2023. Based on a news report, BPI claimed it was an “error in good faith”, and that it would abide by the SEC’s final decision on the penalty. In our conversations with management, the bank has formally requested for a reconsideration of the initial assessed amount. The fine will be booked in 2023. For context, the P134.6mn fine is just 0.2% of our pre-tax profit forecast for 2023E. Regis believes that this was an honest – if costly – oversight on BPI’s part.

Bounty Agro Ventures mulls IPO

According to various news reports, Bounty Agro Ventures is mulling an IPO in late-2023 that could potentially raise USD400-500mn. Bounty Agro Ventures is part of the Bounty Fresh Group, which is among the largest poultry processing companies in the country. The Bounty Fresh Group was founded in the 1980s by Tennyson Chen, who started with an initial 5,000-head layer farm. Bounty Agro Ventures was established in the late 1990s to operate rotisserie chicken outlets. The company currently operates ~2,000 retail outlets selling roasted chickens and chicken burgers through various brands (Chooks-to-Go, Uling Roasters, Adobo Connection, and Heihei). It also distributes dressed chicken products to supermarkets and other establishments (foodservice, hospitality) under the Bounty Fresh Chicken brand

PLDT facing US class action lawsuit

PLDT and its top executives are facing class action lawsuits in the US. Investor Sophia Olsson filed on behalf of other plaintiffs (who bought PLDT securities between 1 January 2019 and 19 December 2022) the case in the US District Court, Central District of California. The class action lawsuit pertains to the budget overruns of as much as P48bn in relation to its capital expenditures from 2019 to 2022.

With the Malampaya concession is set to expire by 2024, Prime Infra President and Chief Executive Officer Guillaume Lucci said that a 15-year extension application for the Malampaya gas field concession has been filed with the Department of Energy (DoE). “We have absolutely the intention not only to further explore the SC 38, but also to look at the surrounding service contracts in the Philippines”. Inflation zooms to 8.7% in January

Headline CPI rose by 8.7% YoY in January, accelerating further from 8.1% YoY the previous month. It also exceeded consensus expectations (+7.6%) and the BSP’s forecast range (+7.5%-8.3%). On a MoM basis, CPI rose by 1.7%, of which 1.0% was due to food (ie. fish, vegetables, and dairy) and 0.3% was from higher utilities (water, electricity). Core inflation (ex-fuel, food) jumped to 7.4% in January from 6.9% in December. To reach the BSP’s and consensus’ FY23E average inflation target of 4.5%, CPI would have to drop by -0.2% MoM in succeeding months (or average 4.1% for the rest of the year). To reach Regis’ FY23E average inflation target of 5.5%, CPI should be flat MoM (or average 5.4% for the rest of the year). The Monetary Board will be meeting on Feb 16.

GIR rises by 3.7% MoM to US$99.724bn in January

Preliminary data showed that the country’s gross international reserves rose to US$99.7bn in end-January. This is up by 3.7% (US$3.574bn) MoM. Reflecting issuance of US$3bn ROP Global Bonds in early January. The GIR level represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports.

Sangley airport consortium signs agreement with Cavite government

The consortium seeking to develop the Sangley Point International Airport has signed a joint venture and development agreement with the Cavite provincial government. The consortium members include House of Investments (HI PM), Cavitex Holdings, MacroAsia (MAC PM). Foreign members of the consortium include Samsung C&T Corp, Ove Arup & Partners Hong Kong Ltd. And Munich Airport. Once developed, the airport can accommodate 75mn passengers annually. The project will cost US$11bn.
