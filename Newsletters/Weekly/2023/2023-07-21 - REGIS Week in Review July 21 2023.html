REGIS REPORT

21 July 2023

The market ended marginally higher at 6,647.56 (+0.3% WoW), consolidating in the wake of last week’s rally.

Trading lacked conviction, with gainers tied with losers 1:1.

Banks held steady post last week’s surges, as BPI’s (unch) solid 2Q numbers lent optimism for the rest of the sector’s 2Q profits. BPI reported NPAT of Php25.1bn for the first half of the year (+23% YoY), well in-line with Regis full-year forecast of Php50.5bn. For 2Q23 earnings alone, NPAT was up around 44% YoY (and 38% QoQ) excluding a land asset sale done last year. BDO gained +0.6% WoW, after rallying 9% last week.

Foreign interest in the sector was still high. BDO registered US$5.8m worth of inflows, while BPI saw US$3.2m in net foreign buying.

The property sector led the gainers, despite the BSP Governor’s recent comments that further tightening could be a possibility, with investors taking a mid-term view of an eventual cut in rates.  Ayala Land (+6.6% WoW) continued to play catch up after a long bout of underperformance. SMPH (+1.1% WoW) likewise gained. ALI saw US$8.0m worth of foreign demand, being the top bought name for the week.  Market expectations of continued recovery in residential pre-sales as well as 2Q earnings momentum helped buoy sentiment.

An MREIT placement of 279.4m shares at Php13.00/sh also flowed through last Friday (roughly worth Php3.6bn). This was mainly to address the public float requirement after the recent property-for-share swap between Megaworld and MREIT. Most part of the block was done by local investors. Excluding this transaction, foreign investors were 50% of the market last Friday.

Consumer names were mixed. Jollibee (+0.6% WoW) still saw strong foreign demand for US$6.3m. URC (-2.2% WoW) meanwhile lagged after the government’s bid to stabilize domestic sugar prices by allowing local sugar earmarked for exports to be sold domestically. URC is a key beneficiary of higher domestic sugar prices, as it is a net seller of sugar.  PGOLD (+3.5% WoW) outperformed, snapping three straight weeks of declines, with its single digit PER attracting buy interest.

BLOOM (+10.4% WoW) delivered a notable move after gaming agency PAGCOR hiked its 2023 gaming revenue target to Php273bn, implying a 27% growth vs. 2022. This suggests improved trends on the ground, and suggests BLOOM doing well this year despite revenues from foreign (Chinese) patrons well below peak levels.

Overall, headline inflows for the week were strong at US$17.9m, marking the second straight week of inflows. Average daily turnover continued to be thin however, at US$84.5m.

Puregold - Growth amidst headwinds
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Firing on all cylinders

Puregold (PGOLD) has underperformed the broader PCOMP by c15% YTD taking its multiples to historic lows (8x PER and 4.8x EBITDA). While there may be a perception overhang relating to corporate governance, the scale of the stock’s de-rating, exacerbated by management’s overly conservative guidance, is not justified in our view. Our outlook for EPS growth is more upbeat given (1) easing inflation coupled with wage increases, (2) the resumption of promotional activities/events, and (3) the acceleration of organic expansion boosted by M&A. These should, in our view, mitigate the near-term margin pressure from competition and drive sales and earnings growth. We are tweaking our 2023-25E EPS estimates and maintaining our Buy rating with a TP of P50/sh. Our TP implies 13x 2024E PE, broadly in line with the 5-year historical average for PGOLD.

Puregold: Easing inflation, wage increases to boost top line

For the past year, lower-income households have been feeling the pinch from high inflation, resulting in downtrading across categories and reduced average baskets for the core Puregold brand. We believe relief for consumers is imminent, given easing consumer prices and recent wage hikes, which should help boost demand. Margins, however, are normalizing as Puregold resumes promotional activities (e.g. Tindahan ni Aling Puring convention) that were scaled back during the pandemic. These events should, in our view, help support top line growth for the brand (+11% 2023-25E CAGR).

S&R: Market share vs profitability

S&R is accelerating store expansion amidst competitive headwinds. From the previous +7-8% net selling space CAGR from 2019-22, S&R is looking to double the rate of expansion (+14% CAGR) over the next few years. This is likely to result in near-term margin compression from competitive pricing and proportionately higher opex, but we believe the investment in securing attractive locations is necessary to protect long-term market share. Beyond 2023, we expect margins to stabilize as the new stores ramp up.

Tweaking 2023-25E estimates and TP; maintaining Buy

We are tweaking our 2023-25E EPS estimates by 1-4% mainly to reflect lower margins for S&R, while our target price is raised slightly to P50/sh (+5%) as we roll over to 2024E. Our target price represents 2024E multiples of 13x PE and 7x EV/EBITDA, broadly in line with the 5-year historical average for PGOLD. Key downside risks relate to potential and perceived conflicts of interest within the Lucio Co Group, a protracted recovery in consumption, which could drag top line growth, and intensifying competition, which could pressure margins further.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Calm after the storm
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Maintain Hold on FY24E yield of 16%

After a period of phenomenally high prices beginning last year, calm appears to have settled into the coal markets. The resulting decline in coal prices has prompted us to clip our FY23E/FY24E profit forecasts by 7%/36% and reduce our DCF-based TP by 27% to P28/sh. While we expect downward revisions to consensus estimates, we believe most of this downside is already reflected in SCC’s share price. We retain our Hold rating as SCC’s capital returns (16% in FY24E) remain attractive.

Coal price reset

Given that benchmark Newcastle thermal coal price has fallen by c. 70% from the September 2022 highs, we lower our FY23E/FY24E coal ASPs by 20%/36% to US$69/US$42 per MT. We also cut our power ASP assumptions by 6-7% to c. ~P6/kwh. While we anticipate net profit to decline by 15% in FY23E and 50% in FY24E, we believe SCC can still maintain paying dividends of P4.50/P4.00 per share in FY24E/FY25E, after paying another P3/sh in 2H23E

Long-term prospects: Contract renewal may lead to diversification

The Department of Energy is still reviewing SCC’s request to extend its coal operating contract, which is due to expire in July 2027. We estimate a renewal could lift our valuation by at least P5/sh (+17%) based on current coal resource estimates and a long-term Newcastle coal price forecast of US$95/MT. Longer[1]term, a contract renewal could also accelerate the company’s planned investment into cement and another 700MW coal-fired plant. The capex for the latter, however, hinges on resolving the grid’s transmission issues.

Revised TP of P28/sh offers 16% total potential return

We reduce our DCF-based TP by 27% to P28/sh, after lowering our coal and power price assumptions and rolling over to 2024. At our TP, SCC would be trading at 7.0x FY24E PER, which would be in between regional coal names (5.7x) and other local generation companies (9.5x). The key risk to our forecasts/valuation are coal prices and we estimate every 10% increase/(decrease) in our benchmark coal price assumptions could raise/(lower) our profit estimates by 11-13%.

ICTSI wins bid to run Africa’s largest port

ICTSI has been selected as an equity partner to run and expand Durban Container Terminal Pier 2. ICTSI will own 50% of the port while its partner, state logistics company, Transnet SOC Ltd will own 50%+1 share. The contract will be valid for 25 years, which can be extended to a maximum of 30 years in the event that the berth deepening of the North Quay at Pier 2 is delayed. ICTSI was one of six bidders for the contract and an official announcement will follow. No amount was disclosed. ICTSI and Transnet will be expanding the capacity of Pier 2 to 2.8mn TEUs from 2.0mn TEUs today. Pier 2 handles 72% of the Port of Durban’s throughput and 46% of South Africa’s container throughput. Transnet has plans to increase the capacity of the Port of Durban to 11.4mn TEUs from 3.3mn TEUs currently. ICTSI’s group-wide container capacity is c. 21mn TEUs; It handled 12.2mn TEUs in 2022 and Regis expects this to rise to 13.7mn TEUs this year.

Globe Telecom completed the first tranche of a sale-and-leaseback deal with Unity Digital Infrastructure, earning P1.4 billion in the process. The first closing involved 115 towers, with 94% being ground-based and 6% rooftop towers in Luzon, as part of a larger 447-tower deal signed in May. So far, Globe has raised approximately P49.3 billion from the sale of 3,826 out of 7,506 towers.

Proposed project sites on Yindjibarndi Ngurra must first secure the approval of the Yindjibarndi people as part of the agreement with ACEN. Yindjibarndi equity participation is 25-50% in all projects. Yindjibarndi Energy is targeting to reach 750MW of combined wind, solar and battery storage under construction in the coming years. The projects will need an investment of about AUD1bn. The announced JV is not ACEN reported numbers nor Regis estimates. ACEN’s 2030 target of 20GW comprises major investments in both Philippines and Australia. ACEN appears on track to exceed its 5,000MW target ahead of 2025. ACEN trades at 24.5x PER and 12x attributable EV/EBITDA on Regis FY23 estimates.

STT GDC Philippines plans to scale up the program by inviting more universities and training institutions to participate and create a robust talent pipeline for the country's data center industry.

PLDT gets NTC nod for two cable landing stations

PLDT Inc. received provisional authority from the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) to construct two new cable stations. The two stations will host the Apricot cable system, an intra-Asia submarine cable facing the Pacific side of the Philippines. The new cable landing stations will be in Baler, Aurora, and Digos, Davao del Sur. The Apricot cable system will provide telecommunication companies with alternate routes that avoid the usual West Philippine Sea waters. New facilities and cable systems are expected to be completed by 2025 and will connect the Philippines to Japan, Singapore, Indonesia, Taiwan, and Guam. PLDT's international capacity will increase to over 140 terabytes per second with the fully equipped cable system. Improving internet speeds, lower latency, and add resiliency to existing landing stations in Batangas, La Union, and Camarines Norte.

NEDA Board approves P170.6bn solicited NAIA PPP project, 2 other infrastructure projects

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) approved the P170.6bn solicited proposal to privatize the operations of the Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA). The project was proposed by the Department of Transportation and the Manila International Airport Authority. The winning bidder will be able to operate NAIA for a period of 15 years, with an option to extend by another 10 years in the event that construction for the Bulacan airport and Sangley airports are delayed. The project aims to increase the pax volume capacity of the airport to 62mn pax (from 35mn today) and boost air traffic movements to 48 per hour (from 40 today).

 

The NEDA Board also rejected the P267bn unsolicited proposal of the Manila International Airport Consortium (MIAC). MIAC proposed to pay an upfront payment of P57bn and capex of P210bn over a 25-year concession period. Capex proposal aimed to increase the capacity of the airport to 62.5mn by 2028 and 70mn by 2048. The government believes project implementation under the solicited route will be faster. It aims to publish the bid documents by August and auction the project before year-end. The NEDA Board also approved two other infrastructure projects. The P7.48bn Samar Pacific Coastal Road 2 Project and the P12.75bn unsolicited proposal for the upgrade, expansion, and O&M of the Laguindingan airport. (Proponent: Aboitiz InfraCapital, a unit of Aboitiz Equity Ventures, AEV PM)

 

Sugar for export may now be diverted for local use

The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) allows the reallocation of unused sugar originally intended for the global market for domestic use to meet the growing local demand.

SRA issued Sugar Order (SO) No. 8 and SO No. 9 to reinstate homeless "A" (sugar for export to the US) and "D" quedans (for the world market) and reclassify them for the local market, subject to conditions. The move aims to stabilize the supply and selling prices in the local sugar market and alleviate the perceived tightness in domestic sugar balance for crop year 2022-2023

 

Requests for the conversion of unshipped "A" and "D" sugar quedans have been received, with around 11,432 MT for "A" sugar and 5,478 MT for "D" sugar remaining outstanding. Traders filing requests for the reinstatement of homeless "A" and "D" sugar quedans need to pay a fee of P15 per 50-kilogram bag, and the deadline for accepting applications is August 4. In a separate order, the SRA allows the reclassification or conversion of verified unshipped "A" and "D" sugar quedans from crop year 2020-2021 and previous crop years to "B" sugar on a voluntary basis for crop year 2023-2024. Holders of verified unshipped "A" and "D" sugar quedans can apply for conversion to "B" sugar until August 15, and late applications will not be accepted.

Rising rice prices – risk to inflation?

Rice prices around the region have been rising since late-2022. Vietnam and Thailand export FOB prices are up ~20% YoY, and domestic prices are likely to follow. Since the Rice Tariffication Law was passed in 2019, the premium of domestic rice prices vs imported rice has narrowed significantly. Domestic wholesale prices were typically over 2x vs imports before the Rice Tariffication Law, but the premium has since narrowed to ~60%. The premium of domestic prices (35-40%) has narrowed further since the recent jump in prices in VN and TH, which would mean current prices are nearly at parity after accounting for the 35% import tariff. At the retail level, average prices have been creeping up. Average retail prices are at P44-45/kg, higher by ~11% YoY. Further upward pressure on prices could drive up inflation. Rice accounts for 8.9% of the CPI.

Marcos signs Maharlika bill

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. will sign the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) Act into law. The MIF is a sovereign fund that the government will use to invest in major projects as part of the country's economic transformation. Fund has faced criticism over concerns of potential misappropriation of public funds. The MIF will have a total of P125 billion, with contributions from the Land Bank of the Philippines, the Development Bank of the Philippines, and the national government. President Marcos Jr. assures the public that the fund will be independent from the government and managed professionally to ensure the safety of people's pension funds. Representative Luis Raymund Villafuerte Jr. expresses confidence that the MIF will provide the economy with a new growth stimulant, enabling increased spending on public infrastructure and other major programs. The establishment of the MIF comes amid global economic challenges, but the Philippines has secured a higher investment-grade status, providing a bright spot for domestic growth with the MIF attracting private investments.

Cash remittances rise 2.8% YoY in May

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 2.8% YoY to US$2.49bn in May. In PHP terms, remittances in May are up 9.8% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate accelerated slightly to +3.2% in May. For the month, land-based remittances grew 2.9% YoY, lifted by remittances from Singapore, Saudi Arabia, and UAE. Meanwhile, Sea-based remittances were also higher by 2.4% YoY. This brings YT-May remittances to US$12.98bn, up 3.1% YoY and in line with the BSP’s 2013 target (+3% YoY, US$33.5bn).
