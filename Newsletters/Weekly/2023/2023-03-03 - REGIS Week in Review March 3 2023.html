REGIS REPORT

03 March 2023

The market headed lower for a fifth straight week (-0.5% WoW) to close at 6,655.4.

Turnover spiked to US$180m (+145.6% WoW) due to the MSCI rebalancing last Tuesday, February 28. As expected, Globe (-3.7% WoW) and ACEN (-2.2% WoW) saw substantial foreign outflows of US63.6m and US19.9m respectively while Meralco (+1.9% WoW) saw an inflow of US4.9m on the back of its up weight.

Headline foreign outflows stood at US127.3m as market sentiment remained brittle given the lack of catalysts. 

Outside the rebalancing, a few earnings results came through. Banking names BDO (+1.6% WoW) and MBT (+0.5% WoW) reported solid sets of results. BDO saw its FY22 NPAT at P57.1bn (+33% YoY) and increased its dividend policy to Php0.75/sh (from Php0.30/sh) while Metrobank saw loans grow 14.7% and strong trading income. Banks were the strongest sector for the week with BPI (+4.7% WoW), BDO (+1.6% WoW), and MBT (+0.5% WoW) all in the green.

Large cap SM (-0.4% WoW) reported record profits of P61.7bn (+53% YoY).

The top performer for the week was MPI (+11.1% WoW) with gains fueled by speculation of an upcoming tender offer. Should the company turn private, investors are also betting on NIKL’s inclusion on the index. NIKL’s share price soared +4.1% for the week.

Manila Water - 4Q22 profit lifted by lower non-cash costs; FY22 capex up 52% YoY to P21bn
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY22 profit up 65% YoY to P5.17bn – Ahead of estimates

MWC reported FY22 profit after the share of participating prefs of P5.17bn (+65% YoY), well above Regis and consensus estimates. Note that the headline results were lifted by one-offs and adjustments, such as (a) P662mn worth of write[1]backs of FX losses (b) a P450mn reduction of prior year’s provisions for the Clean Water Act, and (c) reduced non-cash costs implemented beginning 4Q22 due to the contract extension. Without these one-time adjustments, we reckon profit would have just been in line with our P4.59bn forecast.

East zone: Capex surges to P21bn, higher than forecast

Billed volume growth accelerated further to 6% YoY in 4Q22 from +1% in 9M22, in line with the re-opening. Revenues grew by 19% YoY in 4Q22 alone, as volume mix normalized. EBITDA grew by 21% YoY in 4Q22 and was flat YoY in FY22. Meanwhile, capex surged to a record of P7.67bn in 4Q22, taking FY22 capex to P20.57bn (+52% YoY), above our forecast P17bn.

Non-East zone units recover in 4Q

Profit contributions from its non-East zone units recovered in 4Q, led by tariff increases at its Philippine units. The international segment recorded a loss in 4Q, weighed down by Thai-based Eastern Water.

Revised concession agreement still up in the air

The government still has not finalized the terms of the revised concession agreement. Given that we already assumed the FCDA mechanism will be reinstated, a reversal of this matter in the final contract would be a risk.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Lower coal grade, pre-stripping hit 4Q22; Cash level ripe for dividend
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY22 EBITDA a mild miss

FY22 headline profit more than doubled to P39.87bn. Excluding one-offs and FX adjustments, we estimate core income stood at P39.01bn (+147% YoY), higher than our forecast by 5% due to lower non-cash expenses. While EBITDA doubled YoY to P47.65bn, the figure was slightly below our full-year estimate of P49.08bn, as coal mining revenues fell short. Despite this, SCC’s end-2022 net cash level of P10bn was much better than forecast (P1.3bn net debt), suggesting the company can pay a chunky dividend this year.

Coal mining profit drops in 4Q22 on lower coal grade, heavy pre-stripping

Profits from the coal unit dropped further to P4.22bn in 4Q22 after peaking at P14.0bn in 1Q22 due to (1) a higher share of unwashed coal sales and (2) pre[1]stripping. Despite the Indonesian Coal Index (ICI4) rising by 11% QoQ, SCC’s ASPs declined by 6% QoQ to P4,861 per MT, after the company sold a larger proportion of unwashed coal. SCC also re-opened its Narra mine in 4Q22 and began mining in a new area of the Molave mine, resulting in a temporary increase in pre[1]stripping costs.

 

The coal segment also booked one-time tax expense of P1.2bn in 4Q22 alone, after the company failed to take the necessary steps to officially defer the tax holiday of Molave mine during the pandemic in 2020. The tax payment, however, will result in lower tax expenses in the future, as SCC’s ITH was extended to 2024, later than our forecast (3Q22). Given that only 35% of SCC’s FY23 coal sales volume target is fixed at SCC’s peak prices, we anticipate ASPs will drop this year. Our base case assumes an ASP of P4,840. Every 10% reduction in ASPs could cut our profit forecast by 12%.

Power unit hit by unplanned outages in 4Q

The power unit’s 4Q22 profit contracted by 67% QoQ and 27% YoY due to unplanned outages of Units 2 and 3. This more than offset higher ASPs.

2022 capex lower than expected; Gross cash at P4.70/sh

While SCC’s FY22 dividend of P21bn (P5/sh) was below expectations, full-year capex of P4.3bn was much lower than we had anticipated (P8.9bn). As a result, SCC ended the year with a gross cash level of P20.1bn (P4.70/sh). Given that expansion opportunities are still delayed, management is still keen on paying out excess cash as dividends in April.

 
Manila Electric Company - FY22: Core income ahead; provisions yet to be reversed
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Core income rises 10% to P27bn

Meralco reported FY22 net profit of P28.4bn, +21% YoY. Excluding FX losses/gains and other items management deems non-core, core income for the year came in at P27.1bn, +10% YoY and better than estimates. Operationally, the core distribution business underperformed slightly even as provisions continue to understate its true profitability and financial condition. Contributions from generation, on the other hand, were quite robust.

Electricity distribution sales up 6%, provisions yet to be reversed

Power sales for its core distribution business were up 6% in 4Q and FY22, just marginally lower than our assumptions. Despite a ruling from the regulator on tariffs for the “lapsed period” (2015-2022), Meralco has yet to reverse the provisions relating to those tariffs, which we estimate could amount to more than P40bn (US$730m).

Meanwhile, there’s been little progress on the tariff review for Meralco’s 5th Regulatory Period (5RP, beginning July 2022). It looks unlikely that the final rates can be set by the end-June schedule. Meralco continues to set aside provisions as if the final tariffs will be at least (we estimate) 15% lower than it currently charges.

Generation was a big contributor; FY23 outlook looks more promising

The generation businesses delivered an estimated P6.3bn, close 1/4th of Meralco profits for the year. Pacific Light’s (58% owned) chunky contributions more than offset losses at GBP. With GBP’s fixed price contracts (a key reason for its losses) having ended last year, results for this segment are likely to be better this year.

Telco tower operation likely to be in the red over the medium term

Meralco holds 51% of MIDC, which agreed to acquire 2,180 towers from Globe for a rather rich P26.2bn (>US$200k per tower).MIDC also secured contracts to build another 1,118 for lease at a capex cost of P10.6bn. Management expects this business to be posting losses for the next four to five years.

LTG’s cigarette operations implements chunky price increase

PMTFC, the tobacco associate of LT Group, raised prices for its products effective today (Feb 27). The price hikes were selective. Metro Pacific Hospital considering up to US$1bn IPO in 1H2024

Metro Pacific Hospital Holdings Inc. (MPHHI) is considering reviving an initial public offering as early as 1H2024 according to Bloomberg sources. It could seek to raise US$500mn to US$1bn in an IPO. It is weighing buying out minority stakes in some of its hospitals to pave the way for an IPO. Considerations for the IPO are at an early stage and there is no guarantee that the deal will proceed.

 

MPHHI is 80%-owned by a consortium led by global investment firm, KKR, while the 20% balance is owned by Metro Pacific (MPI PM). Singapore’s fund, GIC, is also part of the consortium but its ownership has never been disclosed. Recall that KKR initially invested in MPHHI in 2019 at a 100% equity valuation of P77.8bn (US$1.5bn). Prior to KKR’s buy-in, MPI hired advisers in 2019 for an IPO. MPHHI accounts for 8% of MPI’s NAV. Metro Pacific Hospital has investments in 19 hospitals in the Philippines with a combined bed capacity of about 3,800, serving an estimated 3.6 million patients a year.

MBT FY22 NPAT P32.8bn in line; but watch NIMs as depo costs surge in 4Q22

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY22 NPAT of P32.8bn, bang in line with REGIS estimates, but slightly ahead (4%) of the Street. Loan growth came in at 14.7% (vs Regis 15% fcast), with deposits also up 15% YoY. However, NMs were up just 6bps QoQ. This was due to a surge in TDs, up 59% YoY and 26% QoQ (incl LTNCDs), while CASA was flat YoY (+1.2%). This saw MBT’s CASA ratio drop to 67%, from 71% in 3Q22. As a result, deposit costs spiked sharply to 1.1% in 4Q22, from 0.5% in 3Q22 and 0.28% in 4Q21. The deposit growth seems to have been used to fund the gapping out of the Treasury book. While total earning asset yields were up 66bps QoQ, loan yields were up a much milder 24bps QoQ.

 

Meanwhile, some highlights from the FS and press release:

 

Loan growth (net) accelerated to 14.7% YoY, from 13.3% in 2Q22. Gross loans were up 14%, with corporate loans up 15% and credit cards jumping 29% YoY. Deposits were up a similar 15% YoY, but this was driven by a jump in TDs/LTNCDs up 59% YoY and 26% QoQ. CASA ratio dropped to 67%, from 71% in 3Q22, and from 76% at end-2021.

 

NIMs were up 6bps QoQ to 3.50% in 4Q22. Due to the increase in TDs deposit costs spiked sharply to 1.10%, up 60bps QoQ and 82bps YoY. In 4Q22 alone, interest expense doubled QoQ, and trebled YoY. Asset yields were up 66bps QoQ, while loan yields were up 24bps QoQ. This suggests that the  majority of the NIM gains came from gapping out of the Treasury book.

 

Compared to end-2019 levels, loans were still about 4.5% lower, but the Treasury book was almost double that of pre-Covid levels.

 

Trading+forex gains were down 25% YoY. Trading gains were up 90% to P6.4bn, but forex swung from gains in 2021 to losses in 2022. In 4Q alone, though, trading+forex gains were up 53% YoY, as bond yields dropped from their peaks in late 3Q / early 4Q.

Fees and other incomes were up 12% YoY for the full year, though these flattened out in 4Q22 on both a YoY and QoQ basis.

 

Opex grew just 3% YoY for FY22. However, there was a jump in expenses in 4Q22 (+10% YoY). Notably, compensation expenses were up 15% YoY in 4Q22 alone, after being flat to slightly down for the previous three quarters.

 

Provision expense fell 31% YoY as asset quality seems to have stabilized. NPL ratio dipped to 1.9% (from 2.1% in 3Q22) while NPL Cover was maintained at 172%. However, there was a  jump in 4Q22 provisions (+29% QoQ, and 33% YoY), which suggests that some of the NPL improvement could have been due to write-offs.

 

While the results seem solid, Regis is closing monitoring the steepness of the rise in deposit / funding costs. This could continue to limit NIM accretion into 2023. On the other hand, it can be argued that deposit costs have made the full adjustment, while loan yields have yet to reflect rate hikes fully. Regis notes, too, that MBT is a major beneficiary of the recent raising of the credit card rate cap that recently took effect.

 

SM Investments 4Q22 results comment

Net income rises 53% to P61.7bn.  SM Investments reported net income of P61.7bn for FY22, which is 53% above the P40.4bn profit (restated) for FY21.  The results are ahead of Street estimates, with record earnings from the banking as well as retail businesses, a rapidly recovering property segment as well as the retroactive inclusion of the geothermal production operation.

 

Banking and property deliver.  BDO reported record earnings of P57bn for FY22, +33% YoY, ahead of Street expectations. While loan growth was a tad short of estimates (+9% YoY), net interest margins expanded.  Trading gains also contributed to PPOP. Meanwhile. SM Prime’s FY22 net profit rose 38% to P30bn, as mall operations rebounded to near pre-pandemic levels.

 

SM Retail also produces record profit.  Net profit for SM Retail reached P17.9bn.  That profit level is a record and is over 40% higher than 2019 despite revenue being only 3% higher.  Management attributed the improved margins to efficiency improvements through headcount reduction and right-sizing of their retail space.  The improved margins / profitability is most evident with the department store segment (“SM Store”).

 

Portfolio investments contributed 11% of earnings. SM says “portfolio investments” accounted for 11% of FY22 profits.  The biggest contributor here was geothermal field operator PGPC, which provided P4bn of income for the year.  PGPC was acquired mid-2022 but SM’s results were restated to retroactively include PGPC to 2021.

 

Alfamart now at >1400 stores.  Alfamart added 205 stores during 2022 and now has 1412 outlets.  Without giving specific numbers, management said this operation is now profitable with net margin of around 1%.

 

Tender offer for 2GO.  SM also announced that it intends to launch a tender offer for the 15% public float of shipping and logistics firm 2Go (2GO PM). 2Go reported a profit of P312m for FY22, from a net loss the prior year.  SM will engage a 3rd party to come up with a fair price for the tender offer. SM’s disclosure of its intention at this time is curious, as usually a tender offer is launched with a specific price already set. 2GO’s latest market cap is only P19bn.

Megaworld 2022 operating profit jumps 32%, ahead

Megaworld’s 2022 operating profit jumped 32% to P20.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. This was largely thanks to strong residential GPM (+3.8ppt), which likely indicates that the proportion of residential revenue from lots increased. Residential and office revenue were also slightly ahead of our forecasts. Headline net profit was flat at P13.5bn (ahead of REGIS and consensus), mostly due to higher interest expense and forex losses on US$ debt. The 2022 reservation sales grew 49% to P119bn (80% of the 2019 level), ahead of our estimates.

 

Meanwhile, 4Q22 reservation sales rose 30% YoY (-3% QoQ) to P33.4bn, equivalent to 90% of the 2019 average. The 4Q22 mall revenue grew c. 50% QoQ (+55% YoY) to P1.2bn, 73% of the 4Q19 figure. Megaworld likely reduced rental concessions in 4Q22 but has not fully removed them. In the company’s press release, Megaworld Chief Strategy Officer Kevin Tan said he was optimistic of hitting “pre-pandemic numbers” this year. Megaworld’s 2019 net profit of P17.9bn is much higher than REGIS (P14.9bn) and consensus (P14.5bn) 2023E net profit forecasts.

 

Cebu Air adds five more aircraft orders for 2023

Cebu Air announced it will be leasing 5 additional aircraft in 2023, on top of 10 new Airbus NEO deliveries this year. With scheduled 15 aircraft exits, fleet size will be flat at 78 by end-2023 from the initial guidance of 73. Regis estimates CEB ended 2022 with a total fleet of 78 (up from 75 in 2019). The company cited “growing passenger demand” as the reason for the new orders. Three of the five additional aircraft will be used to re-start the Clark base. Two other aircraft will  be used to support overall growth ambitions as CEB recovers fully from the pandemic. The company expects to return to profitability by 1Q23. CEB’s domestic market share has risen to 57% in FY22 from 51% in FY19, based on Civil Aeronautics Board’s data.

 

Megawide, partner invests in data center

Megawide signed a JV agreement with Singapore-based Evolution Data Centers  to design, build, and operate a 69MW data center in Cavite. Megawide will be owning 60% of the 4-hectare land area and it will also own 49% of the operating company. Phase 1 is estimated to cost P16.5bn (US$300mn). This covers a 23-MW development that will be constructed over 30-48 months. The JV will hire a third party contractor. The JV plans to fund 60% of the project cost from equity, suggesting MWIDE will need to shell out c. P5bn over a 5-year period. Regis estimates Megawide’s gross cash is currently at P15bn, after receiving proceeds from the sale of the Mactan airport. With gross debt (including prefs) at P33bn, Regis estimates its net debt to equity is at 2.2x.

SECB FY22 NPAT ahead on provisions; but NIMs plunge 34bps QoQ

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT for FY22 of P10.56bn, up 53% YoY, well ahead of Regis, but broadly in line with consensus. The beat was due largely to much lower provision expense as NPLs significantly improved vs Regis expectations. The bank also made higher profits on the sale of assets than Regis had anticipated. However, the results were still disappointing to us. Net interest income were actually behind Regis forecasts. This is because NIMs collapsed another 34bps QoQ in 4Q22, following a 20bps drop QoQ in 3Q22. Funding costs surged nearly 80bps QoQ – with deposit costs alone up 72bps QoQ – as SECB does not have the same low-cost CASA base as the Big Three.

 

A few highlights:

Loan growth slowed a smidgen to 12.0% (from 12.4% in 3Q22, but more from the 15.0% in 2Q22). Corporate loans grew 10%, while consumer loans jumped 16% YoY, driven by mortgages (+21%), and by credit cards (+34%). Consumer loans made up 24% of total, up from 23% in 2021. Deposit costs grew 15.7% YoY, driven by a 30% YoY increase in TDs.

 

NIMs plunged 34bps QoQ, following an already precipitous 20bps QoQ decline in 3Q. The NIM squeeze was due to a 79bps spike in funding costs, with deposit costs up 72bps QoQ, driven by the aforementioned increase in high-cost TDs.

 

Net interest income disappointed, up just 7% YoY for FY22. NII actually dipped 4% QoQ in 4Q22.

 

Non-interest income took up the slack, driven largely by surprisingly strong gains from sale of assets, presumably foreclosures.

 

Opex was up just 8% YoY in FY22, tracking below Regis expectations.

 

However, in 4Q22 alone, expenses jumped 9% QoQ and 15% YoY, as the bank ramped up its IT spending

 

Provision expense fell 46% to P2.8bn for the full-year, just over half of Regis forecasts. The bank’s NPL ratio improved to 2.95%, down 29bps QoQ from 3.24% in 3Q, and nearly 100bps lower than the 3.94% at end-2021. NPL cover also improved to 101% from 94% in 3Q22.

 

Despite the beat, Regis is still quite wary, particularly given the surge in SECB’s funding costs. To be fair, for 2023, upside to loan yields could come from loan re-pricing, particularly from credit cards, given the recent raising of the credit card rate cap.

Cebu Air expects to exceed pre-pandemic pax volumes in 2023

Cebu Air expects to match, if not exceed, its previous high of 22.47mn pax volumes this year according to CEO Mike Szucs. It expects to serve 23-24mn passengers in 2023, capitalizing on the sustained rise in passenger bookings. Domestic will account for 75% of total. Based on the regulator’s data, CEB’s systemwide pax volumes reached 13.2mn (vs. Regis FY22E: 16mn). Regis forecasts assume pax volumes will reach 20.9mn in 2023. Every 10% increase in pax volumes will raise EBITDA by 25% and profit by 85%, holding other things constant. CEB is trading at 4.2x FY23E EV/EBITDA, below historical average (7x) and peers (11x)

BLOOM FY22 profit, EBITDA a bit short on Korea losses, fx and int expense, but GGR continues to outperform

BLOOM posted a FY22 profit of P5.142bn, revering losses of P4.22bn in 2021, but slightly below Regis estimates (P5.2bn). The result implies 4Q22 profit softened a bit to P1.1bn, down 30%qoq despite GGR hitting P14.7bn, another 10% growth qoq. EBITDA in 4Q however grew 11%qoq to P4.0 bn, which brought FY22 EBITDA to P14.3bn, a smidge short of Regis’ P14.5bn estimate. At first look, it appears interest expenses, fx and losses from Korea were higher than expected in 4Q, but underlying ops of the Solaire property continued to climb. 

 

GGR in 4Q climbed another 10%qoq to P14.7bn, surpassing the same period 2019 revenue of P14.6bn. Slots continued to lead the growth, up 30%qoq and printing a new record in terms of quarterly revenue from the segment. Volumes for VIP and mass tables were nevertheless higher by 12% and 5% respectively, but the GGR impact was tempered by lower win rates. Slots is now well above pre covid, making up for the trailing VIP and mass table segments whose volumes are still down by 30% and 20% vs 2019. 

 

As flagged in November, the reopening of Jeju risked higher losses from Korea due to the end of government subsidies. In 4Q alone the Korea sub incurred >P200mn in negative EBITDA, worse than estimates. Management announced in the call that it sold part of the Jeju Sun property for USD7mn this January. The resulting gain from the transaction will help pare losses from operations in the near term. 

 

It continues to seek buyers for its other remaining assets in Korea. Regis estimates it incurred some P400mn in fx losses in the quarter, which weighed down headline income. BLOOM books gains/losses on its USD assets (cash, etc). BLOOM is trading at 9.0x EV/EBITDA and 11.1x PE on Regis 23 estimates.

Makati City Halls orders closure of PLDT mobile subsidiary’s head office on unpaid tax

Budget deficit widens to P378bn in December; FY22 deficit at 7.3% of GDP

The government’s budget gap widened to P378bn in December, +12% YoY. Tax revenues jumped 13.6% YoY, slowing down from 17.8% in 11M22. Expenditures rose at a faster rate of 13.6% YoY in December from +9.9% YoY in 11M22. The FY22 budget deficit at P1.614tn, lower by 3.4% YoY and represents 7.3% of GDP (vs government target: P1.50tn, 6.9% of GDP). Tax to GDP (i.e. tax effort) improved to 14.6% (vs. peak of 14.5% in 2019) due to improved collections from the Bureau of Customs; BIR tax effort at 10.6% in 2022 (vs. 11.1% in 2019). For 2023, the government is targeting a lower deficit of P1.47tn, equivalent to 6.1% of GDP. The government aims to bring down the deficit to GDP ratio to 3.5% of GDP by 2026.

Commercial bank lending grows 10.4% YoY in January

U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) moderated to 10.4% in January. While this appears to have moderated sharply from 13.7% in Dec 2022, data on U/KB loans from Jan 2022-Dec 2022 were distorted by the merger of BPI/BPI Family Savings. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking system rose by 11.4% YoY in Dec 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow by 12-15% for banks under Regis coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth slowed to 5.5% in January from 6.7% in December.

 

Inflation may breach 9% in Feb. — BSP

BSP is urging banks and financial institutions to reduce or eliminate fees for electronic fund transfers to boost the government’s efforts to transform the Philippines into a cash-lite from a cash-heavy economy. BSP is ready to work with the banking industry to incentivize more Filipinos to use digital payments, such as exploring a cost-sharing system and cutting the reserve requirement of 12%
