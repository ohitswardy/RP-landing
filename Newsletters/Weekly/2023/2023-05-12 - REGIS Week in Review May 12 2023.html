REGIS REPORT

12 May 2023

The market corrected sharply for the week, closing  -1.6% WoW to 6,578.15 as foreigners sold the market. 

Losses accelerated after the MSCI announced changes on the upcoming index rebalancing. MONDE will be deleted while a number of stocks are set to see downweights including  ALI, SMPH, JGS, and TEL.

MONDE (-16.2% WoW) and ALI (-6.2% WoW) dragged the index the most. ALI saw the most outflows at US$7.2m.

Meanwhile, AC (+3.6% WoW), SM (+1.0% WoW), and URC (+3.5% WoW) saw US$6.6m, US$4.5m, and US$3.9m worth of inflows respectively.

The large caps also saw strong earnings. SM 1Q results were ahead of expectations driven by BDO while AC saw net profit jump by 61% to P9.4bn, in line with expectations. URC also reported strong earnings as operating profit for the first quarter is trending ahead of estimates largely on the back of its sugar business (EBIT +39% YoY).

The government reported that the country’s GDP growth for the first quarter was 6.4%, a tad higher than the 6.2% average estimates in the market. On the supply side, the services sector provided the boost to growth while private consumption continues to grow largely in line (+6.3%) with the overall GDP on the demand side.

Market activity was thin however, with turnover  declining to US$94.9m (daily ave) while headline foreign outflows were at US$12.4m.

Ayala Land - 1Q profit disappoints on commercial lots; residential sales to foreigners jump
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q23 profit disappoints on lower commercial lot revenue

Although Ayala Land’s 1Q23 net profit rose 42% YoY to P4.5bn, this is trailing behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. The development business underperformed as revenue from commercial lots fell 19% YoY (see Figure 2). Since commercial lots have high margins, this was likely a major cause for the YoY margin decline as well. While development revenue should climb in the coming quarters as the company launches more commercial lot projects, we believe it will be difficult to meet our full-year profit forecasts.

Reservation sales led by “American” buyers

1Q23 reservation sales grew 15% YoY (flat QoQ) to P27.7bn, led by the 61% jump in sales to foreigners (see Figure 3). Sales to American buyers were particularly strong, soaring 85% YoY and accounting for 63% of sales to foreigners. They mostly purchased Alveo and Avida condominiums in Metro Manila. Management said that most of these American buyers had roots in the Philippines (such as Filipinos who became American passport holders). Meanwhile, local sales rose 13% YoY, led by Ayala Land Premier lots.

Residential cancellations start to moderate

While the company did not provide exact figures, it said that cancellation rates were starting to moderate, and it expects cancellations this year to be 10%-20% below 2022 levels.

Mall revenue stays strong after holidays; occupancy should improve further

Despite 4Q being the seasonally strongest quarter, Ayala Land’s mall revenue inched up 2% QoQ (+71% YoY) to P5.0bn, just a tad belowthe 1Q19 figure. Revenue should improve further as occupancy increases. Average occupancy was only 83% but should rise soon since 89% of space was leased out as of end-1Q23.

Office occupancy inches up; could climb further

BPO firms signed new leases in Ayala Land’s Makati office buildings, raising the firm’s office occupancy by 1ppt to 89%, higher than the 82% occupancy across the industry. Management is targeting to improve occupancy further to the mid[1]90s by the end of this year.

Hotel revenue remains close to record level

Hotel and resort revenue surged 164% YoY to P2.2bn, 15% above the 2019 level, as “revenge travel” lifted both occupancy and room rates. Hotel occupancy jumped 21ppt (to 69%) while resort occupancy climbed 36ppt (to 47%).

 
Universal Robina - 1Q23: Margins recover QoQ
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q23 operating profit mildly better than expected as margins start to recover

URC‘s 1Q23 net profit was largely flat YoY at P3.4bn, broadly in line with expectations. The bottom line, however, was weighed down by the P205mn impairment charges for its farms business as well as higher interest expense. Core operations are tracking slightly ahead of expectations with EBIT at P4.7bn, up 15% YoY. The sugar segment was the main outperformer (EBIT +34% YoY) as it continued to benefit from elevated domestic prices. Meanwhile, branded consumer food (BCF) margins have started to recover, expanding ~200bps QoQ.

Int’l BCF leads segment growth as PH BCF recovers from system transition

Total BCF revenues grew 8% during the quarter, led by the int’l business, which grew +13%. URC’s big three international markets – MY, TH, and VN – posted double-digit growth, buoyed by the 2022 price increases. The PH segment expectedly lagged, with revenues growing only 5% YoY. The company transitioned to a new ERP system beginning 4Q22, which temporarily affected volumes.

BCF margins expand sequentially on price increases, easing cost inflation

Both BCF segments saw EBIT margins expand sequentially as the impact of the FY22 price increases, easing input cost pressures, and operating leverage. In the Philippines, EBIT margin expanded 150bps QoQ even as management held back on further pricing action given the ERP transition during 1Q.

BCF margin recovery to continue

Management expects margin recovery to continue as PH BCF volumes normalize and the full impact of price increases is felt. The company raised prices by another ~4% in April, bringing cumulative price hikes to ~15% since 2Q22. While the price adjustments are likely to take a pause for now, margins should continue to expand sequentially as inventory costs come down. The impact of the price increases will likely drive FY23 top line growth closer to the upper end of management’s target (low double-digit), though EBIT should grow faster as margins continue to recover (Regis FY23E EBIT +20%).

 
Cebu Air - Returns to profitability in 1Q23
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q23 core profit at P1.35bn; EBITDA up 81% QoQ to 59% of pre-COVID

In 1Q23, CEB reported its first quarterly profit since the pandemic, with core profit (ex-FX and one-offs) amounting to P1.35bn. EBITDA also jumped 81% QoQ to P4.13bn, which was about 59% of 2019 average. Revenues matched 1Q19 levels at P20.88bn (in line), up 9% QoQ. The results, however, are trending behind our estimates due largely to (a) higher fuel expenses and (b) non-cash maintenance provisions.

The good news: domestic demand strong, though seasonality has changed

Systemwide pax volumes improved to 86% of pre-COVID in 1Q23, from 79% in 4Q22, with capacity up slightly QoQ to 89% of pre-COVID. Domestic load factors reverted to pre-COVID levels, but fares were still lower, as domestic competition remained intense. With more capacity being re-allocated to international markets in 2Q, domestic fares are finally normalizing as competition eases. Management also flagged the adjustments in seasonal demand patterns, following the change in school holidays to June, July, and August. This has resulted in lower load factors in 2Q but stronger bookings for 3Q relative to pre-COVID.

The bad news: challenges in availability of engines/aircraft

International pax volumes were at only 56% of pre-COVID levels in 1Q from 42% in 4Q, with just a minor improvement in capacity. Lingering global supply bottlenecks limited the company’s ability to ramp up capacity. Management is addressing the issue, with the recently announced five (5) additional leased aircraft deliveries. Given its good credit standing throughout the pandemic, CEB is arguably in a stronger position to negotiate with lessors for aircraft than its competitors.

The outlook: Gradual normalization of EBITDA margins

Management noted that EBITDA margins are getting closer to what they were pre-COVID beginning in 2Q. However, because of the supply issues, the company also needs to keep more aircraft on standby, implying that achieving economies of scale will be more gradual and margins could take a little longer to revert to pre-pandemic levels.

 
D&L Industries - 1Q23: profits fall on margin squeeze in oleochem; food volumes remain weak
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1Q23 behind estimates

DNL’s 1Q23 income fell to P594mn (-24%QoQ, -24%YoY) with the improved profit from food more than offset by the -42% drop in contribution from oleochemicals. Overall volumes are lower by 14%, dragged by a 25% drop in food. On the other hand, the oleochemical segment suffered a 1.9ppt drop in GPMs despite relatively healthy volumes. Margin trends for the different segments have diverged despite stable prices for coconut and palm oil over the past few months. The profit is tracking well below Regis (P3.3bn) and consensus (P3.6bn) estimates for FY23E.

Demand for food ingredients drops in 1Q

Profits from the food segment grew 14%YoY to P204mn in 1Q23 but was down 30% on a sequential basis. Unlike the oleochemical business, food margins were actually much higher (13.6%GPM, +5.6ppts). This masked the underlying volume weakness, with commodities falling 33%YoY and high margin specialties declining 8%YoY. Food volumes have been soft since 4Q22 and management was quick to point to high inventory levels of customers. We think inflation may have also been a factor in the drop in demand.

Oleochemical margins dragged by biodiesel

On the flip side, profits from oleochem fell to P190mn (-32%QoQ, -42%YoY) in 1Q23. Despite the positive volume trends (+3%for high margin products, +54% for commodities), margins fell 1.9ppts. Worth noting that margins for specialty oleochemicals were up by 2.6ppts (22.8%GPM) so the drag was likely due to biodiesel.

Batangas facility opening soon but no substantial orders yet

Management admitted that they don’t have confirmed orders for the new plant even with the imminent opening of the facility by mid-2023. They may start shifting export orders from existing facilities to the new plant to start, but low utilization levels may weigh on profits in the near term.

 
ICTSI - 1Q23: Yields hold up amidst soft volumes
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q23 profit at US$147mn, up 12% YoY

ICTSI’s 1Q23 profit after perps of US$147mn (+12% YoY) was broadly in line with Regis and consensus estimates. EBITDA improved by 5% YoY to US$354mn and was also within expectations. The 1Q results imply that EBITDA and profit growth for the rest of the year will need to be flattish YoY to meet our estimates. This appears achievable, in light of (a) the seasonality of the business, (b) management guidance of sustained QoQ (organic) yield improvements, and (c) easing of fuel-related opex.

Organic volumes slip by -0.7% YoY, -5.9% QoQ

Consolidated volumes rose 9% YoY due to the consolidation of MNHPI. Organic volume actually dipped 0.7% YoY from +3.5% growth in 4Q22, weighed down by volume declines in Melbourne, Pakistan, and Eastern Europe. Outside these ports, volumes for the rest of the portfolio rose.

Organic yield per TEU rises 5% YoY, 2% QoQ to US$192

Consolidated yield per TEU fell 2% YoY to US$179, weighed down by the inclusion of MNHPI. Organic yield per TEU (excluding MNHPI), however, rose further to US$192, up 5% YoY and 2% QoQ, as tariff increases took effect. With scheduled tariff increases in Manila North Harbour, Mexico, and Melbourne in 2Q/3Q, management believes yields will hold up in succeeding periods.

EBITDA margin at 61.9%; Fuel-related expenses a tailwind

EBITDA margin slipped by 2.1ppts YoY to 61.9%, on high base effects from last year’s high storage revenues. Cash costs remained elevated in 1Q, up 15% YoY, but management noted that it has since seen fuel/power costs unwind, which should act as a tailwind for margin expansion in succeeding periods.

Write-off of Sudan receivable but no cash/valuation impact

Management has halted conversations with the government of Sudan until the chaotic political climate subsides. The remaining balance of the unrecovered upfront fee now stands at Euro188mn. However, the carrying value, which could weigh on this year’s headline earnings if written down, is c. Euro 140mn. We note that failure to recover the upfront payment will not affect our valuation nor the company’s dividend payout.

1Q23 +58%YoY - ahead

Generation was also higher as last year’s output was constrained due to a typhoon’s impact to transmission. Century Pacific Food - 1Q23: Cost inflation pressure cushioned by tight cost control

1Q23 NPAT +6% YoY to P1.5bn – in line

CNPF’s net profit rose 6% YoY to P1.5bn in 1Q23, tracking broadly within our estimates. Revenues rose 6% YoY to P15.6bn, trending slightly behind expectations mainly due to the underperformance of the OEM segment (flat YoY). EBITDA margin was flat YoY as tight cost control helped cushion continued pressure from raw material cost.

Branded revenue growth eases on supply issues, downtrading

Branded revenue growth slowed to +6% YoY, partly coming from a high YoY base (Omicron surge in Jan 2022, typhoon Odette in Dec 2021). This implies volumes were down slightly during the quarter, largely attributed to supply issues for sardines. Otherwise, branded revenues would have grown in the mid[1]teens, led by the continued growth in milk (+20%) and the core marine business (+double-digit ex-sardines). Meanwhile, revenues from the meat segment were flattish due to downtrading.

Tight cost control cushions persistent input cost pressures

Gross margin contracted 230bps YoY as CNPF continues to work through high[1]cost inventory. Tight cost control, particularly on A&P, helped cushion the impact, keeping EBITDA margin largely flat YoY. The company implemented a minimal price increase during the quarter, and we estimate the cumulative price impact is 8-9% since 1Q22.

Margins have likely bottomed, but cost uncertainties persist

Management believes margins have likely bottomed in 1Q23 and should improve sequentially driven by (1) operating leverage as sales rise with the normalization of sardine supply, and (2) gross margin improvement as average inventory cost comes down. Management, however, continues to be cautious given the recent spike in skipjack tuna prices (+18% YT-April), though they believe this could be temporary. While CNPF has enough inventory cover until 2Q23, the margin recovery could be delayed if prices of skipjack tuna stay elevated.

Strong mall operations and residential reservation sales

Robinsons Land’s 1Q23 operating profit grew 55% to P3.7bn, slightly ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. The mall business was particularly noteworthy since: 1) revenue was higher than the seasonally strong 4Q, and 2) the EBIT margin was above 2019 levels. Mall revenue inches up QoQ, margins surge

Even more remarkably, the mall EBIT margin climbed 16ppt YoY and 10ppt QoQ to 36.6%, higher than the 2019 level of 32%. While we suspect that part of the QoQ improvement was due to timing of expenses, management said that power cost, which fell QoQ, was the major cause of the swing in mall margins. Thus, fluctuations in power costs going forward could bring about similar volatility in margins. The company also said that despite headlines about reinstituting Alert Levels a few weeks ago, mall operators have not been asked to reinstitute COVID restrictions. Reservation sales soar above pre-COVID levels

Similar to the case with other developers, we suspect that these “Americans” have roots in the Philippines (such as Filipinos who became American passport holders). Meanwhile, JV projects saw even faster growth, with sales growing 165% YoY (+142% QoQ) to P5.0bn, thanks to the launch of a new project with Shang Properties in RLC’s Bridgetowne estate. Thus, it seems that demand for high[1]end projects is even more robust than demand for mid-to-low end projects. Furthermore, revenue reversals from cancellations have been “immaterial” for two consecutive quarters.

Office vacancy inches up

Office vacancy inched up as a BPO tenant did not renew its lease. While vacancy rose 1ppt to 11%, this is much lower than the industry average of 18%. Furthermore, management is confident that office take-up will pick-up in 2H23.

East zone (EZ): Opex and capex trail

1Q23 GDP positively surprises at 6.4%

1Q23 GDP came in at +6.4% YoY, higher than street estimates (6.2%) and decelerating from a revised 4Q22 GDP of 7.1% (previously 7.2%). This is well within the government’s target of 6-7% for FY23. On the demand side, consumption grew 6.3% YoY, slower than in 4Q (7.0%) but normalizing to historical trends. Private investment (ie. capital formation) rebounded, up 12.2% YoY (from 3.8% in 4%) and contributing 2.6ppts to GDP growth due to strong public construction. However, with export growth slowing sharply, net imports dragged GDP growth by 1.6ppts. On the supply side, all major economic sectors recorded positive growth. To reach Regis’ FY23E GDP growth target of 5.5%, GDP for the balance of the year should grow by 5.2%.

Trade deficit widens in March

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit ballooned to US$4.93bn in March (+7% YoY, 26% MoM) due to imports dropping by 3% YoY vis-à-vis a larger decline in exports (-9%). Merchandise exports fell 9% YoY in March, slower than in Feb (-18% YoY) and January (-13% YoY). By commodity, electronics saw the largest decline (-15% YoY), followed by coconut oil (-26% YoY). Exports to China jumped 20% YoY, reversing 25% YoY monthly declines in the past two months. Merchandise imports declined by 2.7% YoY in March, slower than in February (-11.8% YoY). The decline was due to lower fuel imports and raw materials; Offset by higher imports of consumer goods. The Year-to-March trade deficit at US$14.58bn, up 11% YoY.

U/KB loan growth steady at 10.1% YoY March

Preliminary data showed loans from universal and commercial banks (U/KBs) grew by 10.1% YoY in March. This is broadly the same as in Feb 2023: 10.0%. By production activities, loan growth to manufacturing, trade, electricity, and real estate accelerated vs. February. Consumer loans grew at a steady rate of 21% YoY. Meanwhile, Credit cards slowed to 27.9% YoY from 29.4%. Auto loans grew +0.5% after posting negative 1.3% growth in February. M3 growth was steady at 6.0% (unch MoM).

GIR steady at US$101.5bn in April

Preliminary data showed the GIR level settled at US$101.51bn in April, flattish MoM (-US$36.9mn). This represents 7.6 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to end at US$100bn this year.

Inflation cools further to 6.6% in April

Headline CPI declined by 0.2% MoM and rose by 6.6% YoY. This is the slowest inflation rate in eight months and was below Bloomberg consensus forecast (7.2%); BSP forecast range: 6.3%-7.1%. Prices of food (vegetables, fish, and meat) and energy-related items (transport and electricity inflation decelerated further) continued to decline. Core CPI rose by 7.9% YoY, +0.3% MoM, moderately slowing from March (+8.0% YoY, +0.4% MoM). The YT-April average inflation rate is at 7.9%. The BSP’s 2023 inflation target at 6.0%; Regis forecast at 5.5%. To reach the BSP’s inflation target, CPI should rise by +0.5% MoM for the rest of the year. BSP monetary board is set to meet on May 18.
