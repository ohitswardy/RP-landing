REGIS REPORT

26 May 2023

The market headed lower  (-2.0% WoW) for the week to 6,530.20, mostly due to the lack of liquidity. 

Headline turnover was at US$90.0m although skewed by a CNPF placement (US$50.0m) and EEI block (US$19.7m) earlier in the week. Excluding these, average daily turnover was just at US$77.0m.

Losers outweighed gainers 9:10.

Biggest drags to the index were banks BDO (-3.6% WoW) and BPI (-4.7% WoW) as both saw outflows of US$2.0m and US$3.2m respectively.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) signals the reserve requirement to drop to 10% from 12% mid 2023, which should release around P300bn worth of liquidity. However, effects of the cut could be muted given that it only offsets MSME loans that were incentivized during the pandemic (roughly worth P263bn).

The rest of the market saw outflows too in anticipation of the MSCi rebalancing next week. As expected, MONDE (-1.13% WoW) will be deleted from the index while most index names are expected to see downweights. ALI (-4.4% WoW) saw the largest outflows, registering US$6.4m this week.  

ICT (-3.0% WoW) continued to see weakness in trade arising from a global slowdown. 1Q23 organic volumes (excluding new ports) were down slightly -0.7% YoY) although tariff hikes were able to offset volume weakness. 

Only SMC (+2.2% WoW) ended higher while JFC and GTCAP remain unchanged. 

GTCAP subsidiary Toyota Motors Philippines (TMP) has received P2.5bn worth of tax credits and awaits another P1.7bn from the BIR as the government announced the extension of the CARS program by 5 years. This requires TMP to manufacture at least 200,00 vehicles to receive the full tax incentives.

Foreigners were net buyers for the week. Headline inflows stood at US$48.1m, with most inflows into the CNPF (-11.0% WoW) placement. Excluding this, inflows were only worth US$280k.

Philippine Property - 1Q23 recap: divergent trends across the major segments
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A tale of three segments

The three main property segments continue to be in divergent states of recovery (or lack thereof). Malls remained strong after the holidays and have returned to pre-COVID profitability levels. On the other hand, the office segment weakened further, with some contracts renewed at lower rental rates. Meanwhile, the residential business saw mixed results. Although aggregate reservation sales hit record levels, the segment is still plagued by high cancellation rates.

Malls remain strong after holidays, above pre-COVID levels

Although 4Q is seasonally the strongest quarter, mall revenue of the top 3 mall operators was flat QoQ (+79% YoY) in 1Q23, slightly higher than the 1Q19 level. While pent-up consumption demand might subside slightly in 2Q23, the segment should remain robust in 2H23 and developers will likely spin off malls into REITs over the next 12 months.

Office: Negative rental reversions begin, expect more to come

Although the Metro Manila office vacancy rate was flat QoQ (at 18%), developers reported that some of their tenants renewed contracts at lower rental rates. We expect more such cases in 2023 and especially 2024, since 1) office rental rates are currently 20%-50% below 2019 levels, and 2) the contracts up for renewal were previously signed in 2018/2019, when rental rates were close to the peak.

Residential reservation sales rise to record level but cancellations stay high

There were a number of positive trends in the residential segment in 1Q23: prices inched up, the mortgage NPL ratio declined, and most importantly, reservation sales rebounded to a record level. However, some risks remain: mortgage rates are rising and cancellation rates remain high. Nevertheless, we continue to believe that the residential segment is undergoing a bottoming out process (albeit a bumpy one).

Aggregate reservation sales of the developers we cover rose 29% YoY (+24% QoQ) to P133bn, 105% of 2019 levels. However, cancellation rates remain high, as evidenced by the 20% QoQ drop in residential revenue (flat YoY). Thus, relative to the pre-COVID period, reservation sales alone would now be an insufficient gauge of the strength of residential demand.

Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums in the secondary market inched up for the fifth consecutive quarter, rising 5% from end-2021. Prices of most units are still 10%-20% below 2019 levels. The banking sector’s mortgage NPL ratio fell 40bps QoQ (1.8ppt YoY) to 7.5% in 4Q22. However, mortgage rates have risen 100bps-200bps since end-2021 (+25bps-50bps QoQ). This could increase monthly amortizations by 10%-20% and raise mortgage NPLs (or prevent buyers from availing of mortgages).

 
Filinvest Land - 1Q23 office vacancy climbs to 39% and will likely rise further
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Net profit rises 9% YoY

Filinvest Land’s 1Q23 net profit rose 9% YoY to P741mn, behind REGIS forecast. While office revenue was flat YoY and QoQ, the higher vacancy rate will likely lead to a decline in revenue in the coming quarters.

Office vacancy climbs to 39% and will likely rise further

Office vacancy climbed to 39% (from 32% in 4Q22), more than double the industry’s vacancy rate of 18%. While this was partly due to the completion of a new building, some tenants in existing buildings also did not renew their leases.

Office vacancy of Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) rose to 17% (from 12% in 4Q22) as some tenants vacated. Meanwhile, the non-REIT portfolio’s vacancy jumped to 66% (from 60% in 4Q22) due to the completion of Axis 4, a new office building in Alabang. Given the vacancy levels, the office business is likely losing money.

The company is slated to expand its portfolio by another c. 20% over the rest of the year. Since most of these buildings have minimal preleasing, FLI’s office vacancy could rise to 45%-50% by end-2023 if new tenants are not signed. Filinvest is in advanced negotiations which could lead to new contracts soon.

Reservation sales grow 18% but payment schemes remain loose

1Q23 Reservation sales (before back-outs) grew 18% to P5.2bn, as sales of Metro Manila projects rebounded. While the company’s P22bn target for the year is much higher than our own P17.6bn, we refrain from being too positive since payment schemes are still looser than the pre-COVID period. Industrial leasing to be launched over the next year Filinvest will launch industrial leasing products over the next year. The company will offer land for lease as well as ready-built-factories. While this could become a major earnings leg in the long-term, we expect only minor contributions from this segment in the medium-term.

 
Philippine Banks - When is a RRR cut not a RRR cut?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Minimal liquidity release – replacing a quasi-cut with a real cut

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has been guiding for a 200bp cut in banks’ regulatory reserve requirement (RRR) on PHP deposits by 2H23. Normally, this would be positive for banks, as the BSP does not pay interest on RRR. However, the reduction is likely to come together with the June 2023 expiry of Covid-related relief that allowed banks to count MSME loans towards their RRR. Coincidentally, total MSME loans of P263bn are roughly equivalent to the potential 200bps RRR cut (P280- 300bn), resulting in a minimal net release of liquidity.

RRR cut from 12% to 10% to release P280bn-300bn… in theory

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla has repeatedly guided for a reduction in the RRR for universal/commercial banks’ (UKBs) 10%, from the current 12%. It is unclear if the same sized cut will apply to thrift banks, as their RRR is already down at 3% (Figure 1). As of March 2023, total banking sector deposits stood at P17.67trn, of which PHP deposits were P14.98trn. For UKBs, PHP deposits were P14.02trn. A 200bps RRR cut could release P280-300bn worth of liquidity.

… but MSME “alternative compliance” to lapse, worth P263bn

However, recall that in May 2020, early in the Covid-19 crisis, the BSP issued Circular 1087, allowing banks to use its loans to MSMEs to count as “alternative compliance” for RRR, to ensure liquidity to small businesses. This regulation was originally due to expire at end-2022, but was extended to end-June 2023. As of end-2022, MSME loans used as RRR compliance stood at P263bn.

Impact even less than we thought – net neutral

It seems that a 200bps RRR reduction would just about cover the lapsing of the MSME-as-RRR rule, with the P17-37bn net release equivalent to just 0.1-0.25% of PHP deposits. We previously estimated that every 100bps RRR cut would add c1% to our bank NPAT forecasts, without taking into account the MSME rule. For that math to still work, we need to see RRR cuts greater than 200bps.

Maynilad and Manila Water cannot pass income taxes to consumers

The Supreme Court declared Manila Water and Maynilad as public utilities

This prohibits them from including their corporate income tax as part of their operating expenses. Maynilad said it already accepted its status as a public utility when it signed the Revised Concession Agreement with the MWSS earlier this month. Manila Water, for its parts, said it had already excluded corporate income tax as part of its operating expenses since 2013.

ICTSI volume update – April

Volumes at ICTSI’s ports in Mexico and Melbourne remained soft in April, although the decline in April was smaller than in 1Q23, particularly for Melbourne.  To the upside, the two ports have been raising tariffs and the magnitude of the tariff increases are much greater than the volume declines so far.  We thus believe yield improvements will be sustained and will more than compensate for the volume weakness. ICTSI is trading at 11.9x FY23E PER and 7.5x FY23E EV/EBITDA, below its historical average (21x/11x). 

 

Ayala Corp raises P13bn from preferred share issue

Ayala Corp issued 5.24m new preferred shares at P2,500. The proceeds will be used for re-financing. The rest will be for AC Infrastructure, for investment in LRMC.

Lucio Tan III takes over as PAL President

After recently taking the helm of LT Group (LTG PM), Lucio Tan III has also been appointed as President of Philippine Airlines (PAL PM). He replaced Stanley Ng.

Gov’t extends CARS program

The Private Sector Advisory Council announced that President Marcos, Jr. approved the 5-year extension of the CARS program. The vehicles enrolled under the program are the Mirage model of Mitsubishi Motors Philippines Corp. (MMPC) and the Vios model of Toyota Motor Philippines Corp. (TMP). Prior to the five-year extension, MMPC was given until this year to meet the required production levels while TMP had until 2024. Figures from the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) showed that combined production under the program stood at 207,165 units as of December 2022. TMP, which started production in July 2018, accounted for 134,242 units of its Vios. MMPC, which started production in February 2018, produced a total of 72,923 units of its Mirage model. Fiscal incentives of up to P9bn are given to car manufacturers that produce at least 200k units.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Digital payments +30% in year-to-April

According to BSP data, the value of transactions going through PesoNet and Instapay grew another 30% YoY in the first four months of the year to P3.8trn. That’s slightly slower than the 37% growth posted for FY22, but that is to be expected as the technology slowly matures. For PesoNet – batched transactions for larger PHP amounts – the figure was P2,357bn, up 26% YoY. For InstaPay – transactions worth P50,000 or less – the value was P1,447bn, up 39% YoY.

 

For the month of April alone, PesoNet was up 16.4% YoY to P556bn, while InstaPay was up 41% YoY, taking the total to P933bn, up 25% YoY. We suspect the sharper slowdown in PesoNet transactions in April was due in part to the unusually large number of bank holidays that month. The BSP maintains its goal of having 50% of all retail transactions done digitally by 2023. According to the latest data, digital transactions made up 30.3% of retail payments in 2021, up from 20.1% in 2020. No data has yet been published for 2022.

BoP deficit narrows to US$148mn in April

The country’s overall balance of payments (BoP) position posted a deficit of US$148mn in April. This is lower than the US$415mn deficit in April 2022 but is a reversal of the US$1.27bn BOP surplus in March 2023. BSP cited outflows arising from the national government’s payments of foreign currency debt obligations. The cumulative 4M23 BoP position registered a surplus of US$3.3bn, higher than the US$79mn surplus in the same period last year.

Maharlika Investment Fund Bill certified as urgent

President Marcos certified the enabling law for the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) as urgent. The US$1.4bn fund is expected to finance big government projects such as power grids and dams.
