REGIS REPORT

10 March 2023

The market dipped 1.0% this week, falling for a seventh straight week.  Foreigners sold the market, tracking the fragile sentiment from global markets. Net outflows reached US$7.9m this week.

MPI (-10.2% WoW) was the top index decliner, as speculation has quieted down on a potential tender offer.

ACEN (-6.8% WoW) also fell sharply after reporting disappointing results for FY22.  Excluding one offs, core net income was P4.5bn, down 14% - well below estimates (P6.3bn consensus, Regis of P5.6bn). This implies that 4Q22 net profit dropped to P379mn (-80%QoQ, -61%YoY), dragged by ACEN’s net buyer position as well as the mismatch between fuel cost and selling price (particularly for SLTEC).

Declines were widespread, however. Losers outnumbered gainers 2:1. The largest drags were delivered by conglos and property. Ayala Land (-6.7% WoW), SM (-2.3% WoW), AEV (-6.8% WoW), AC (-4.3% WoW) weighed on the market this week.

ICTSI (+6.5% WoW) meanwhile outperformed, with profits coming in well ahead of expectations (FY22 recurring profit +57% YoY to US$601m). Profits were boosted by a higher-than-expected yield per TEU and a lower tax rate. ICT declared cash dividends of Php10.00/sh (Php8.56 regular + Php1.44 special), translating to ~5% yield.

URC (+5.9% WoW) likewise outperformed for the week. Revenue rose 28% in 2022, suggesting strong consumer demand. The 4Q22 performance was mostly driven by the non-BCF segment (4Q EBIT up 2x YoY) as domestic sugar prices surged.

Most of the consumer stocks were down this week however with Jollibee (-3.3% WoW), Wilcon (-5.9% WoW), and Monde (-1.5% WoW) all closing the week with losses.

Among the other gainers were JG Summit (+3.0% WoW), BPI (+1.0% WoW), and SM Prime (+0.6% WoW). 

Average daily turnover slowed to US$107m as investors largely stayed on the sidelines.

Megaworld - 4Q22 presales remain robust; strong rental and residential targets in 2023
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Forex losses shave profits amidst strong residential margins

Megaworld’s 2022 operating profit jumped 32% to P20.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. The outperformance was largely thanks to a higher share of lot sales in revenue, which led to the 3.8ppt expansion in residential GPM. However, headline profit was flat at P13.5bn due to higher interest expense and forex losses on the company’s US$ debt.

4Q22 reservation sales remain strong, thanks to Metro Manila projects

FY22 reservation sales grew 49% to P119bn, also ahead of our forecast. Reservation sales remained above 90% of pre-COVID levels for the second consecutive quarter in 4Q. Sales of Metro Manila projects (particularly in Fort Bonifacio) were especially strong in 4Q22, as their proportion climbed to 68% of FY22 reservation sales, from only 51% in 9M22. Meanwhile, international buyers comprised 20% of FY22 reservation sales (20% in 9M22, 13% in 2021).

Office vacancy remains well below industry average

Megaworld’s office vacancy was flat QoQ at 8% and it should remain below the industry average (of 18%) since the company will not have any large office completions until 2024. Furthermore, the company’s 2024 office pipeline is already 50% preleased.

Mall rental concessions removed in January 2023

4Q22 mall revenue jumped 49% QoQ (55% YoY) to P1.2bn (c. 75% of the 4Q19 figure) as the company reduced rental concessions. This should improve further since Megaworld already removed all rental concessions in January 2023.

Strong rental revenue and reservation sales guidance

Megaworld’s 2023 guidance is ahead of our forecasts. It is targeting P130bn in reservation sales (2019A: P149bn, REGIS 2023E: P122bn) and rental revenue of P20bn (2019A: P16.8bn, REGIS 2023E: P16.6bn), a 28% jump from FY22.

 
Wilcon Depot - 4Q22 net profit jumps 29% YoY as margins expand further
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY22 net profit +50% YoY to P3.9bn – in line

WLCON’s FY22 net income came in at P3.85bn, +50% yoy, broadly in line with Regis and consensus expectations. Net sales in 4Q22 grew 19% yoy to P8.8bn, bringing the FY22 figure to P33.6bn (in line). Operating profit jumped 46% YoY to P5.7bn, in line with our forecasts but mildly ahead of consensus. For 4Q22 alone, net profit grew 29% YoY on the strong sales momentum and further expansion of gross margins.

Same-store sales growth tempers on higher YoY base

SSSG in 4Q22 moderated to +10% from the +24% clip in the previous quarter, though this was largely due to the relatively higher YoY base. Same-store ticket growth (+9%) was the main driver of SSSG, boosted by price increases across products and categories. By category, plumbing & sanitary wares (22% of sales), building materials (11%), and paints (10%) were among the outperformers.

Gross margin expands further

GPM expanded by another +30bps QoQ in 4Q22 to 39.7% - this is despite the share of in-house and exclusive brands remaining unchanged vs 3Q22 at 51.3%. The continued improvement in margins was attribute to more prudent inventory management. Moreover, price increases have allowed WLCON to maintain or even improve product margins amidst the challenging cost environment. Management estimates that compared to other merchandise, its in-house and exclusive brands generate higher GPMs by 15-20ppts.

Store expansion, margin improvement to drive 2023

Even against the relatively more “normal” base in 2022, WLCON is likely to still grow profits by double-digit in 2023. Management expects mid-teens top line growth in 2023 (Regis FY23E: +19%), but this will be mostly driven by store expansion (8-10 new depots / +10-12%) as SSSG moderates to low-to mid-single digits in 2023 (Regis FY23E: +10%). Margins are expected to expand further as it increases the contribution from in-house and exclusives to 52% in 2023 (Regis FY23E: 51.5%).

 
Universal Robina - Non-BCF segment drives 4Q22 as BCF margins stabilize
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY22 EBIT +20% YoY to P15.2bn – in line

URC reported headline net profit of P14bn, up 12% YoY. On a recurring basis, we estimate net profit was +17% YoY to P11.5bn, mildly behind our FY22 forecasts. While FY22 revenues of P150bn (+28% YoY) were well ahead of expectations, EBIT of P15.2bn (+20% YoY) was just in line mainly due to the underperformance of int’l branded consumer food (BCF) margins. For 4Q22 alone, EBIT jumped 51% YoY on the strong performance of the non-BCF segment.

Philippines BCF sales jump +25% boosted by price increases

The strong top line momentum continued in 4Q22 with consolidated revenues jumping 35% YoY. Philippine BCF sales remained healthy (+25% YoY), which management attributed partly to price increases. Volumes across the various categories grew double-digits driven by the reopening as well as holiday spending. Non-BCF sales (+54% YoY) provided an added boost to the top line as domestic sugar prices spiked.

Non-BCF drives 4Q22 EBIT; PH BCF margins stabilize

Consolidated EBIT margin expanded ~120bps QoQ and YoY in 4Q22, though this was mostly driven by the non-BCF segment, which saw EBIT margins surging over 200bps QoQ / 380bps YoY. Philippine BCF margins, however, were unchanged QoQ at 10.5%, suggesting that (1) cost pressures have stabilized and (2) price increases have been sufficient to offset cost inflation. URC implemented another round of price adjustments in the Philippines in 4Q, bringing the cumulative price increase to +12-13% for the full year.

Margin recovery in 2023

Management expects the strong top line momentum for its BCF business to continue into 2023, citing that the company continues to see record monthly sales in Feb. We are expecting top line growth to temper to low single digits this year as the YoY base normalizes toward the latter part of the year. EBIT, however, could grow at a faster pace as margins recover.

 
ICTSI - 4Q22: Profit growth moderates slightly but still outperforms
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY22 core profit beats at US$601mn (+57% YoY)

Headline FY22 net income before perps distributions rose 44% YoY to US$618mn. Removing the one-off impairment charge of US$16mn booked in 4Q22 and perps distributions, recurring income was 57% YoY higher at US$601mn, beating Regis (US$569mn) and consensus forecasts (US$551mn). A better-than-expected yield per TEU and a lower tax rate drove the outperformance. Implied 4Q22 core profit jumped 25% YoY to US$146mn, boosted by (a) +4% YoY organic volume growth, (b) +12% YoY increase in organic yield, and (c) the consolidation of MNHPI in Sep 2022.

Organic revenue growth moderates slightly to 15% in 4Q22

Revenue growth, excluding MNHPI, slowed to 15% YoY in 4Q22 from 16% in 3Q22, as organic volume growth decelerated to 4% from 6%. This was blamed on a reduction in empty container volumes in Ecuador and the cessation of operations in Davao. To the upside, organic yield per TEU further strengthened to US$189 (+12% YoY) in 4Q22, bringing organic yield per TEU to US$184 (+12% YoY) in FY22. With several tariff increases already in place in early 2023, management remains optimistic about maintaining or even raising its group[1]wide yield per TEU, which already averaged US$180 in FY22 even after factoring in the consolidation of lower-yielding MNHPI.

EBITDA margin dips to 61% in 4Q22

EBITDA in 4Q22 rose 20% YoY to US$371mn, taking FY22 EBITDA to US$1.41bn (+24% YoY). Excluding MNHPI, 4Q22 EBITDA grew 14% YoY and EBITDA margin dipped by 90bps YoY to 61%, as higher power and fuel costs remained an issue. The margin decline could be temporary, as management noted margins in early 2023 are already above 4Q22. Apart from tariff hikes, the company also implemented fuel surcharges at some ports to help arrest margin pressure.

Dividends surprise to the upside

The company raised its regular cash dividend to P8.56/sh (+53% YoY) and also declared a special dividend of P1.44/sh (+227% YoY). Total dividends for 2023 amounted to P10/sh (+66% YoY), which is about US$0.18/sh, higher than our forecast of US$0.148/sh for the year. The dividend is equivalent to a yield of 5% on the current share price.

 
Ayala Corppration - Refreshing our numbers
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Target price P900; Buy maintained

We refresh our estimates and valuation, adjusting our target price to P900/share (+10%), as we incorporate changes in numbers for its various operating companies, and assign a wider a holding company discount (25%). Notwithstanding the relative de-rating of holding firms, we believe AC offers attractive value at current levels, and maintain our Buy rating on the back of (1) long-term outlook for its units, (2) the chunky discount at which its shares are trading vs NAV, and (3) efforts to rationalize its underperforming and under-appreciated assets.

Widening discount

The past few years have seen the shares of many holding companies fall out of favor, as shown by the widening discounts to market-based NAV at which the shares of these firms trade. AC has not been an exception, with its shares trading at a 26% average discount over the last three years, from less than 10% the prior decade. As a result, AC has underperformed three of its four key listed operating companies over the past three years.

Four core holdings drive value

The four businesses identified by AC management as core holdings (Ayala Land, BPI, ACEN and Globe) together account for over 90% of AC’s gross assets (and over 100% of NAV). The two sectors identified as growth areas (Healthcare and Logistics) have great LT potential, but neither of them is likely to form a meaningful part of AC’s value within the next two years.

Divestment and redeployment

AC has raised P31bn (~US$600m) to date, as part of its stated goal to raise US$1bn from the divestment of non-core assets. More than half of those funds have since been deployed to buying back its own stock or increasing its stake in Ayala Land.

Target price adjusted to P900

We adjust our target price (TP) for AC to P900, following a refresh of our SOTP NAV valuation. Our new TP now also prices in a larger discount (25% vs 10%) to the underlying SOTP (based on target prices) of P1,211, as holding companies have fallen out of relative favor. Among the key downside risks are those relating to macro and political risks as well as competition.

 
DMCI Holdings - 4Q22 profit declines across most key segments
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY22 core profit up 80% YoY to P30.7bn – Above Regis estimates

DMCI’s core profit reached P30.7bn for FY22, lifted 80% YoY by strong commodity prices. While this is ahead of Regis forecasts, it is below Street estimates as 4Q22 profit dropped 22% YoY and 43% QoQ to P3.95bn. Revenues declined for on-grid power, property, and nickel units while costs surged at the coal mining segment. Given softer commodity prices, the earnings trajectory seen in 4Q could continue through 2023.

Property: Revenue bookings drop in 4Q, FY22 pre-sales at 88% of pre-Covid

The property unit’s 4Q22 profit dropped 51% YoY and 46% QoQ, which management attributed largely to fewer sales in the period that met the company’s 15% booking threshold. Management is guiding for a further decline in revenues for 2023, reflecting the slow take-up during the pandemic.

Meanwhile, reservation sales jumped 50% YoY to P6.57bn in 4Q22, bringing FY22 reservations to P32bn (+63%), which is 88% of pre-Covid levels. Management expects reservations to match pre-pandemic levels in 2023, as it plans to launch eight new projects this year, higher than the three projects launched in 2022.

Construction order book cut to P35bn in end-2022

The construction unit’s order book dropped by 29% YoY, 19% QoQ to P35bn, after a portion of a rail project was removed due to delays in the delivery of right-of[1]way. While it is unclear at this stage if the project will be added back, the company is optimistic about improving its order book this year, given the abundance of projects (e.g. rail and water) that are up for bidding. Net losses for this division have shrunk YoY, QoQ, as revenue reversals dissipated.

Lower nickel shipments negate higher ASPs; Strong sales lift off-grid power

Despite higher ASPs, profits from nickel dropped QoQ/YoY due to lower production. DMCI Mining is focusing on securing more mining permits, which should materially improve sales volumes by next year. Longer term, the company is exploring opportunities in downstream nickel processing. The off-grid power unit grew profit by 26% YoY in 4Q22, buoyed by strong demand. Earnings will likely grow further this year, as the company completes the construction of a 15MW coal-fired plant in Palawan by June.

Corporate actions ahead?

Management is busy looking for expansion opportunities, a number of which may be infused into its 57%-owned, listed unit, Semirara (SCC PM). The group is looking to participate in the bidding of the 160 MW Casecnan hydro power plant in April. It is also studying the possibility of infusing DMCI’s off-grid power and nickels units into Semirara. At the DMCI level, management is also exploring opportunities in the government’s PPP program.

FY22 recurring income of P4.5bn below estimates; excluding gains, 4Q22 profit down to P334mn

Excluding these, we estimate that recurring profit for FY22 was P4.455bn (- 15%YoY) and for 4Q22 was P334mn (-83%QoQ, -66%YoY) – below both consensus (P6.3bn) and Regis (P5.6bn) estimates.

ACEN’s spot and coal exposure dictate 2022 volatility

Management said that it is now in advanced stages of contracting output of its New England plant in Australia (2H23) and is considering 8-10 year contracts on prices that could be much lower than spot (around half). While management reiterated that the numbers would still be well above the levels to justify the investment case for these projects, the tone has turned notably more cautious regarding spot market prospects compared to just two quarters ago.

 
Philippine Banks - Signal or noise? NPL, PD ratios blip up in January
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Early signs of stress? Or just back to “normal”?

Asset quality indicators worsened in January, as sector NPL ratio rose 13bps MoM to 3.38%, and Past Due (PD) ratio blipped up 22bps MoM to 4.01%. This is the first material deterioration since early 2022. It is tempting to interpret the data as early signs of increased borrower stress, as a result of sharply higher interest rates. However, historical data shows both NPL and PD ratios rising MoM every January, at least since 2009 (see Figure 1, right-hand column). Is the January print the start of an upward trajectory in NPLs? Or is it just an indication that we are truly back to “normal” seasonal trends?

NPLs and PD ratios blip up MoM in January; loan growth slows to 10.8% YoY

Data from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) showed banking sector NPL ratios rising 13bps MoM in January to 3.38% (December was adjusted down slightly to 3.25% from 3.26% previously). The jump was more significant for Past Due (PD) ratio, up 22bps MoM to 4.01%. In nominal PHP terms, NPLs were up 1.6% MoM, while PDs were up 3.4% MoM. This is the first significant deterioration in either ratio since early 2022. The ratios were magnified by a 2.3% MoM reduction in loans, a normal occurrence in January after the seasonally strong demand in December. That said, loan growth slowed to 10.8% YoY from 11.3% YoY the previous month.

RL and NPL cover flat MoM

Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RLs) were flat at 2.61%, with the PHP value down 2.3% MoM. NPL coverage slipped a tad to 106.3% in January, from 107.0% at end-2022. The banking system remained extremely liquid with LDR at 71.5%.

Distinguishing the signal from the noise

It is tempting to tie the January print to a narrative of stress among borrowers – and the broader economy – as the BSP’s policy interest rates have spiked 400bps since mid-2022. However, it may be difficult in this case to distinguish the signal from the noise. Looking at historical data, both NPL and PD ratios have risen every year in January (back to at least 2009) compared to their December levels. This could be due to a number of factors. First, as noted, loans tend to contract MoM in January. Second, banks tend to write-off more NPLs in 4Q, as part of the formal audits. Third is probably a seasonal “hangover” from over-spending / over[1]borrowing during the holiday season.

Keep calm… for now

While the January print is definitely cause for caution, we would choose to wait for further data to draw solid conclusions. For reference, February has historically had NPLs and PDs flat MoM. Our forecasts do not assume significant NPL increases in 2023E.

 
Philippine Consumer - Shifting Tides
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Margin recovery on the horizon

Margin relief is on the horizon for the F&B manufacturers under our coverage – Century Pacific (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), and Universal Robina (URC PM). While cost pressures are not totally out of the picture, soft commodity prices have moderated from the 2022 peaks, and the sector has taken substantial pricing action (>10%) over the past year. Despite the impact of high inflation on consumer wallets, demand continues to be surprisingly healthy – highlighting the defensiveness of the sector. We are forecasting an average 17% EPS CAGR for 2023-24E, and reiterate our Buy ratings on CNPF and URC. Meanwhile, we are maintaining our Hold rating on MONDE. Key sector risks primarily relate to inflation and its impact on input costs and consumer sentiment.

Shifting tides

The past 12-18 months have been a balancing act for CNPF, MONDE, and URC – from navigating cost headwinds to keeping up with the surge in demand following the post-lockdown reopening. The tides are now shifting, and we expect earnings growth over the medium term to be driven by margin recovery as (1) soft commodity prices moderate from 2022 levels, and (2) upward price adjustments (>10%) over the past year take full effect. Demand growth should continue to be healthy even as the boost from ‘revenge’ spending subsides, supported by a minimum wage hike and a reduction in personal income tax rates.

 

Margin recovery to drive 2023-24E EPS growth

We are forecasting 17% average EPS CAGR for 2023-24E driven largely by a margin recovery in 2023E. As the impact of the 2022 price adjustments subsides, we expect average top line growth tomoderate to ~6% CAGR over the forecast period, coming from +15-20% growth in 2022. Margins should rebound but we do not expect a full recovery to pre-pandemic margins as cost pressures could persist in the near term.

Maintain Buy on CNPF, URC

We maintain our ratings on CNPF (Buy), MONDE (Hold), and URC (Buy). We adjust our 2023-24E EPS estimates by 5- 9%and our target prices by as much as 19%, We are assuming higher EBIT margins (average +120bps YoY in 2023E) buoyed by lower average input cost and price increases implemented over the last 12-14 months. Our SOTP-based target prices value the respective branded segments using DCF, implying 2023E PE multiples of 23-25x – broadly in line with peers. The key sector risk relates mainly to inflation and its impact on input costs and consumer sentiment.

Consumer loans grow 15.4% YoY in 4Q22; NPLs improve sharply QoQ

Consumer loans in the Philippine banking system grew 15.4% YoY to P2,234bn in 4Q22, the fastest clip since early in the pandemic in 1Q20. This was despite a slowdown in mortgages, which was more than compensated for by a surge in credit card receivables, and in salary loans.  Crucially, consumer loan NPLs improved further, falling 65bps QoQ to 6.54% in 4Q22 compared to 7.19% in 3Q22. Delinquencies improved across nearly every category.

Mortgages grew 5.3% YoY to P938bn, slowing from the 8.6% pace in 3Q22, but were still up 1.2% QoQ. We suspect part of the apparent weakness is due to banks not buying CTS portfolios (receivables) off of residential developers. Mortgage NPLs improved further to 7.55%, down 37bps QoQ. That said, NPLs for the segment is still well above the pre-Covid levels of around 4%.

Motor Vehicle (MV) loans were still down 2.9% YoY to P441bn. However, this was a vast improvement over the 8.2% contraction in 3Q22. On a QoQ basis, MV loans finally turned positive (+0.8%). We expect the YoY figure to turn positive in 1Q23. MV NPLs fell sharply, down 87bps QoQ to 8.44%.

Credit card receivables (CCRs) surged 38.6% YoY to P555bn, accelerating sharply from the 25.5% posted in 3Q22. CCRs were up 9.4% QoQ. NPLs for the segment dropped to 3.88%, 114bps lower QoQ, due likely to aggressive write-offs by banks.

Salary loans exploded 61.7% YoY to P264bn, faster than the 52.4% clip recorded in 3Q22. Meanwhile, NPLs edged up to 4.91%, up 22bps from the level in 3Q22.

Anecdotally, banks all say that they have been loosening credit to the consumer sector, and expect the growth to continue to accelerate into 2023, particularly in the mortgage and MV segments. We are wary, however, of the sharply rising interest rates, and their impact on NPLs, particularly for mortgages and for credit cards. Mortgages re-price annually, and are typically indexed to the 1-year Treasury rate, which is up c400bps in line with policy rates. For credit cards, the BSP recently raised the ceiling on monthly interest rates on unpaid balances from 2.0% to 3.0% per month. That 50% increase is bound to cause some sticker shock when the next billings arrive.

Meralco deferring pass-through rate increases

Meralco is deferring the collection of P1.1bn of generation costs from Feb in order to cushion the impact of higher generation costs. The higher charges will be collected in March and April instead. 

Meralco estimated the generation rates would have been P0.92/kwh higher in Feb if the charges were not deferred.  After adjusting for taxes and others, the net increase would have been P1.11/kwh (> 10% of end-user tariffs).

GIR level settles at US$99.3bn in end-February

 

Preliminary data showed the country’s gross international reserves settled at US$99.3bn in end-February. This is down from a revised US$100.7bn GIR level in January.  Payment of the government’s debt obligations and downward adjustments in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings were cited for the latest print.

The GIR level represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports.

 

Inflation eases to 8.6% in February

Headline inflation eased to 8.6% YoY in February from 8.7% in the previous month. This was below street expectations (8.9%) and within the BSP’s forecast range 8.5-9.3%. On a MoM basis, headline CPI was flat, as prices of food (i.e. vegetables) and petrol (i.e. operation of transport equipment) dropped, offsetting the uptick in utilities. 

The latest print takes 2M23 average inflation to 8.6% (vs. BSP’s 2023 target of +6.1%; Regis: 5.5%). 

However, core inflation (excluding fuel and food) rose further to 7.8% in February from 7.4% in January. On a MoM basis, core CPI rose by 0.5%, slower than +1.0% in January. Assuming core CPI is flat MoM for March, the YoY rate will already moderate to 7.5%.

The BSP’s Monetary Board will be having its next policy meeting on March 23; Fed meeting also on March 23.

Labor force participation deteriorates in Jan 2023 vs Dec 2022

Preliminary Labor Force Survey data for January 2023 showed a MoM decline in the labor force participate rate to 64.5% from 66.4% in December. It is unclear if this is due to seasonality, given that the government only began providing monthly data beginning Jan 2021. On a YoY basis, 3.5mn more people entered the labor force, with the labor force participation rate improving from 60.5% in Jan 2022.

The number of employed decreased by 1.65mn MoM while the number of unemployed increased by 153k MoM. Employment across sectors dropped MoM, with the agri sector alone losing 1.2mn MoM.
