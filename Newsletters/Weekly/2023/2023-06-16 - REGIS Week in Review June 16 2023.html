REGIS REPORT

16 June 2023

The market stayed flat at 6,508.34 (+0.02% WoW) with a lack of domestic catalysts, even as the US Federal Reserve kept policy rates unchanged (at 5.00% to 5.25%). This was in line with Street expectations.

Daily turnover spiked to US$343.0m (average), although was largely skewed by the BPI block and FTSE rebalancing at the end of the week. Based on estimates, the rebalancing should have seen outflows worth US$10m. Inflows were expected in AC and ACEN while outflows in BPI, SMPH, BDO, and ALI.

Contrary to expectations, however, AC (-9.5% WoW) and ACEN (-11.4% WoW) dropped significantly as sellers took advantage of the liquidity event. AC saw outflows worth US$6.9m and US$11.2m for ACEN. Meanwhile, SMPH (3.7% WoW), BDO (+0.00% WoW), and BPI (+2.6% WoW) stayed resilient as inflows were strong at US$9.7m, US$4.6m and US$4.2m respectively.

Excluding the rebalancing and BPI block worth US$764.0m, trades amounted to just US$137.7m.

BPI also reflected the property dividends of 406.2m shares declared last March (equivalent to an 9.0% stock dividend) through a cross last Friday, June 16. This transaction contributed US$148.9m inflows for the week.

The telco sector underperformed as both PLDT (-1.6% WoW) and Globe (-4.2% WoW) fell sharply. Voyager Innovations, the fintech holding firm of PLDT, disclosed plans of raising up to US$150m within this year. Based on PLDT’s disclosed share of Voyager’s P&L, Voyager has been losing Php2bn every quarter. On the other hand, Globe saw a steep sell off despite announcing plans of Mynt’s upcoming IPO.

Investors also called bets off on BLOOM (-3.7% WoW) on the back of the delay of the MPI tender offer as investors have earlier speculated it to replace MPI in the PSE index. Investors also seemed to have discounted an expected normalization from 1Q’s usually high win rate.

ICT (+2.0% WoW) disclosed that the port authority of Pakistan is allowing ICT’s contract for a port to lapse this week, which means ICT should hand over operations back to the government and would need a new concession agreement. This has not yet been finalized although ICT claims to have a right of first refusal over the operations of the terminal as it accounts for 1% of ICT’s profit until the next two years.

Headline inflows registered at US$99.0m, although excluding the BPI dividend transaction, the market saw outflows worth US$49.0m.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Prime to open three Philippine malls this year

Steven Tan, head of SM Prime’s mall business, said that the company would complete three Philippine malls this year and five Philippine malls next year. The company will add 215,000sqm of Philippine mall GFA this year (including expansions of some malls).

SM Prime will also open one new mall in China next year – in Yangzhou City, Jiangsu province.

SM Prime currently has 9.0mn sqm of Philippine mall GFA and 1.4mn sqm of mall GFA in China.

NLEX to implement tariff hike beginning June 15

The Toll Regulatory Board (TRB) approved the implementation of the periodic tariff hikes for the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX). The tariff hikes cover tariff petitions for years 2012/2014 (last tranches) and half of the 2018/2020 increases.

  • Open system (excluding VAT): +P6.25 per entry (+11%) to P61.76
  • Closed system (ex-VAT): +P0.32 per km (+10%) to P3.59
  • End-to-end tariff for Class 1 vehicles will rise to P332 from P299 (+11%)

Regi estimates the effective increase will be +12% after taking into account vehicle traffic patterns.

NLEX is a 76%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM), accounting for 23% of MPI’s gross asset value. Regis estimates the toll hikes will increase the tollroad unit’s FY23E/FY24E profit by 9%/19%, which will consequently increase MPI’s FY23E/FY24E profits by 3%/6%. Regis estimates MPI’s market-based NAV will rise by 4% to P7.80/sh.

Government of Pakistan terminates contract of ICTSI’s Pakistan port

Karachi Port Trust (KPT) is letting the contract of Pakistan International Container Terminal (PICT PK) expire as scheduled on June 17, 2023. All concession assets including equipment and IT systems will remain property of PICT.

PICT has offered O&M services when and if required by KPT beyond June 16 but it is unclear at this time if KPT will take this offer. Meantime, PICT will wait for the terms of a new agreement that will be tendered, wherein PICT has right of first refusal. PICT has the right to operate Berths 6-9 under a 21-year concession agreement, which expires on June 17. The Build-Operate-Transfer agreement means that PICT is bound to transfer concession assets to KPT once conditions are met. All concession and non-concession assets will be fully depreciated by June 17, even though these assets may continue to be used beyond this period

Regis estimates PICT accounts for 4% of FY23E volumes, 2% of FY23E revenues, 1% of FY23E profit. It is unclear at this stage if ICT will need to impair the US$13mn of goodwill related to PICT.

Vehicle sales rebound strongly in May

CAMPI reported vehicle sales jumped by 45% YoY and 25% MoM to 38,177 units in May. Recall that April sales volumes were affected by public holidays. This brings YT-May sales to 166,104 units, up 32% YoY and on track to beat CAMPI’s target of 395k (+12%).

Toyota’s sales volumes rose 21% YoY, 27% MoM to 17,866 units, taking 5M23 sales to 77,194 units, up 18% YoY. To reach Toyota’s 188k FY23 target (+8% YoY), monthly sales should reach 15,800 units for the rest of the year.

Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV.

PLDT unit to raise US$150mn fresh funds for Maya

PLDT’s Voyager Innovations, Inc. is looking to raise US$100-150mn in fresh funds this year to strengthen its integrated money platform, Maya, according to PLDT Chairman Manuel V. Pangilinan.

Voyager is currently negotiating with potential foreign investors to raise the funds, while PLDT is also expected to participate. Voyager last raised new funds (US$210m) in April 2022, at a valuation of around US$1.4bn.

PLDT President and CEO Alfredo Panlilio also said that the market listing of Maya is being considered but it needs to improve fundamentals of the bank first. Maya has been increasing loan disbursements in recent months and management expects the bank’s EBITDA to be positive by year-end.

For FY22, PLDT’s attributable losses from 36.8%-owned Voyager amounted to P3bn, which implies Voyager’s losses reached P8bn (US$150m).

Ayala Land to raise up to P19bn in 5-, 10-year bonds

Ayala Land (ALI PM) plans to raise P14bn in fixed-rate bonds with an oversubscription option of up to P5bn.

  • Series A 5-year notes will pay 6.0253% p.a.
  • Series B 10-year notes will pay 6.2948% p.a.
  • Offer period will run from June 13-June 19

Net proceeds of up to P18.77bn will be used on the following to partially refinance short-term loans, which have interest rates bet. 6.05% - 6.25% (P8.37bn) and to fund capex (P10.4bn).

Max's Group (MAXS) President resigns

MAXS announced the resignation of its President, Ariel Fermin, effective August 31. Mr Fermin was appointed President in 2022 but has served as COO of the company since 2017. Current CEO Robert Trota takes over as President in the interim.

AGI approves buy-back extension, increases budget by P2.0bn

Alliance Global Group (AGI PM) disclosed that its board approved to extend the present buy-back program up to April 8, 2025 (from the original ending period of April 8, 2024) and increase the amount allocated for the program by P2.0bn. A total of P6.928bn (596.852mn shares or 6.6% of outstanding common shares) has been acquired by AGI under a buy-back program that originally started in 2017. The amount appropriated under the current program is P7.0bn.

San Miguel to expand food, power, and infrastructure businesses

San Miguel CEO Ramon Ang discussed the company’s expansion plans during the annual stockholders’ meeting.

The company will build several “mega poultry plants” in strategic locations nationwide over the next 10 years. It began the construction of a new food complex in Davao last year; this, Includes grain storage, feed mill, flour mill, processed meat, and poultry facilities, including broiler, feeder farm, and hatchery.   

San Miguel Power previously announced plans to complete 610 MW of its 1,000 MW target capacity of its nationwide battery energy storage system by the end of 2023. The rest will be completed next year.

Its 1,313 MW Batangas combined cycle power plant is scheduled for completion in 2024.

San Miguel aims to achieve “substantial completion” of land development for its Bulacan airport by 2024. SMC also received regulatory approval for more roads: P148bn Northern Access Link and the P152bn Southern Access Link.

Repower Energy to put up 200-MW wind farm in Quezon

Repower Energy Development Corp. (REDC), a subsidiary Pure Energy Hldgs. Corp. will pursue a 200MW wind farm in Quezon, covering over 4500 has of land.

REDC, which is eyeing to conduct a P1.15bn initial public offering next month is gearing up for an aggressive expansion this year. The tentative listing date of the shares is eyed on July 24.

Senate to probe complaints against Cebu Pacific

The Senate tourism and public services panels are set to conduct an inquiry into the flurry of passenger complaints against budget carrier Cebu Pacific on June 21. This was prompted by a resolution filed by Sen. Nancy Binay, who chairs the tourism panel against Cebu Pacific about overbooking, offloading, and glitches in booking flights. There are also complaints about Cebu Pacific charging passengers multiple times during the company’s Super Pass sale even if their bookings were unsuccessful.

The public hearing will also aim to press both the Department of Tourism and the Civil Aeronautics Board to strongly address this issue, and how affected customers should be compensated. This is not the first Congressional public inquiry on Cebu Pacific. The company faced Congress in 2015 for the same issues.

CNPF's unMEAT expands retail distribution in the US

CNPF is rolling out its unMEAT brand of meat alternatives in close to 2,000 Albertsons outlets in the US. Earlier this year, unMEAT started selling in over 2,000 Walmart stores.

Regis estimate meat alternatives accounts for less than 1% of CNPF's consolidated sales, but is expected to grow exponentially over the next few years.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Trade deficit shrinks to US$4.5bn in April

The country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.53bn in April, down by 15% YoY and 11% MoM. Imports slowed down sharply, falling by 18% YoY due likely to lower commodity prices. Exports also experienced its steepest monthly drop in April, falling by 20% YoY.

The cumulative 4M23 trade deficit is 5% YoY higher at US$19.28bn. The government is forecasting imports to rise by 4% YoY and exports to rise by 3% YoY in FY23. This implies a wider trade deficit of US$61.3bn for FY23E from US$58.2bn in FY22.

Tourist VAT refund seen adding up to P13bn to GDP

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) expressed support to implement a VAT refund for tourists to attract more visitors and increase tourism receipts, according to Undersecretary Rosemarie Edillon during the Senate Committee on Ways and Means hearing.

Based on its study, the additional spending per annum is estimated to be from P6bn to P9bn starting 2024. In comparison, the status quo only yields revenues of P3.4bn to P5.1bn. Considering multiplier effect on other sectors, additional gross value added could be P8.6bn to P12.8bn per year.

Tax revenue loss is estimated at P4bn but the proposal only covers a refund of 85% of the VAT.

World Bank extends US$750mn loan for green investments

The World Bank lent US$750mn for the "Philippines First Sustainable Recovery Development Policy". This is to help the country achieve its renewables target of 50% of generation mix by 2040.

Remittances up 3.7% YoY in April

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.7% YoY in April.  In PHP terms, remittances are up 10.4%. The 3-month average growth rate steady at +3%.

Land-based remittances grew by 4% YoY, slightly accelerating from c.2.9% in Feb/Mar.  Growth was due to faster growth in remittances from the USA (+5.8% YoY in April vs. +3.1% in 1Q23). Sea-based remittances rose 2.7% YoY, fastest monthly increase since Dec 2022.

This took 4M23 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$10.49bn, in line with the government’s target of +3%.
