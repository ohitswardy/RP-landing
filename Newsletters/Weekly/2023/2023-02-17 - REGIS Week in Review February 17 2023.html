REGIS REPORT

17 February 2023

The market dropped to 6,779.02 (-1.4% WoW), with most sectors ending lower on selling pressure. Foreigners were net sellers for US$22.3m. 

The BSP raised rates by another 50 bps to 6%,  the highest level seen in 15 years. The hike came as no surprise, as the January inflation print turned out higher than expected at 8.7%.    

Conglomerates Ayala Corp (4.6% WoW) and SM Investments (-2.3% WoW) fell sharply on the back of foreign selling for US$6.8m and US$6.7m respectively. These were the top sold names for the week by foreign investors and were the biggest drags to the market.

Property name and index heavy weight SMPH (-1.6% WoW) was also weak.  

Banks, which benefit from higher interest rates (in the form of higher NIMs) consolidated, after recent strength.  BDO, BPI and MBT all lost by -0.4%, -0.9%, and -2.2% WoW. Year-to-date, however, large cap banks continue to outperform the market as gains of BDO, BPI, and Metrobank are at +18.3%, +3.4% and +11.1% respectively. 

On the other hand, consumer stocks JFC (+0.6% WoW) and WLCON (+0.9% WoW), underpinned by strong remittance figure in December (US$3.2bn, up 6% YoY), which breached the $3bn mark for the first time in a single month. MONDE (-2.3% WoW) though underperformed against its peers as Quorn issued a product recall in the UK.

GLO was actively traded (+2.5% WoW). Though higher 2.5% for the week, it saw the highest foreign outflow at US$8.7m.

Cemex Philippines - Soft demand, higher costs hit 4Q22; Near-term recovery prospects bleak
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q22 EBITDA hits record low on weak demand, higher costs

CHP’s headline losses widened YoY to P195mn in 4Q, taking FY22 net losses to P1.0bn. EBITDA barely broke even in 4Q22 and was only P2.8bn (down 26% YoY) in FY22. The poor results can be largely attributed to (a) weak demand and (b) higher input costs, particularly fuel and power.

Sales volumes drop in 4Q; 2023 guidance: flat to low-single digit drop

Sales volumes fell 5% YoY in 4Q22, even after falling by 2% YoY due to the typhoon in 4Q21. Demand remained soft throughout 2022, dropping by 10% YoY, as the property sector continued to suffer from the lingering impact of the pandemic and government spending remained muted. For 2023, outlook for demand remains uninspiring, with management guiding a decrease in volumes.

ASPs down by 1% QoQ on discounts/rebates

ASPs declined by 1% QoQ after discounts and rebates in the quarter, following the removal of the safeguard duty on cement. For FY22, ASPs were raised by 9% YoY to offset the impact of input cost inflation. Although China’s re-opening could potentially lift regional cement demand and thus, ASPs, Vietnam’s 4% capacity increase to over 120mn MT this year could dampen this outlook for prices.

Higher fuel costs/plant outage weigh on 4Q margins

EBITDA margin and EBITDA shrunk to a record low of 1% and P46mn, respectively, in 4Q22, after its fuel hedges expired and power supply contracts were recalibrated in 2H22. The scheduled maintenance outages of its two plants also dragged margins during the quarter.

Tender offer: a chance to exit?

CHP’s parent is offering to buy up to 12% of CHP at P1.30/sh. While the price appears well below replacement cost (US$94/MT vs. US$150/MT), near-term prospects remain challenging, which could put downward pressure on the share price once the tender offer is completed on March 16.

 
Globe Telecom - Steady ship
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

More than a dividend play

The sale of more than half its telecom towers combined with last year’s equity-raising exercise should bring Globe’s leverage ratios to comfortable levels this year (1.3x gearing). The near-term outlook for the core business is decent (albeit unexciting), and we believe current valuations are reasonable (14x PER, 5.1x EBITDA and 5% dividend yield), and offer a free, option on its fintech operation GCash/Mynt. We adjust our numbers for Globe, trim our TP to P2,700 but retain our Buy rating.

Steady with a few bright spots; capex set to drop

We expect the core telecom business to grow modestly YoY, with mobile and broadband both likely to show limited gains. What are likely to deliver better growth are its corporate data segment and its new (but still rather small) non[1]telco businesses. With capex set to drop, free cash flow (ex- asset sales) should be close to breakeven after two years being deeply negative.

Value from tower sales and non-telco ventures

Globe will raise over P91bn (gross) from the sale of over 7,000 towers, more than half its network, resulting in a much-improved balance sheet, and also creating significant value for the company. Mynt (36% owned), a rare fintech that’s actually profitable (4% of FY22 core income), continues to show fast-growing operating metrics.

Target price P2,700

We adjust our target price to P2,700/share (-4%) using DCF analysis, after taking into account its recent equity issuance, tower sales, latest results and higher cost of debt/capital. Key risks to the downside relate to a deterioration in the macro conditions while upside risk could come from reduced competition.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Globe completes sale of another 578 towers

Globe closes sale of 578 towers to the Phil-Tower consortium which brings the total number of towers transferred to 2,988. This batch raised P8.6bn for Globe and brings total gross proceeds from the tower sales to P39bn (out of P91bn).

CNPF launches unMEAT in Walmart

CNPF has started distributing its alternative protein brand unMEAT in over 2,000 Walmart stores across the US. This expands unMEAT’s distribution network into a more mainstream channel. Apart from Walmart, unMEAT is also available in select natural grocery stores and specialty e-commerce sites. It is alsocurrently being distributed in the UAE, China, Australia, and Singapore. Domestically, unMEAT has expanded its portfolio to include shelf-stable luncheon meat and “fish-free tuna”, breakfast meat alternatives, and plant-based dairy (unCHEESE). According the CNPF management, its alternative proteins business contributed <1% of consolidated sales in 2021, but expect this category to grow exponentially over the next 2-3 years. CNPF currently trades at 16x 2023E PE.

Stock trading in GCash begins test run

GCash and AB Capital Securities Inc. secured the go-signal from the SEC to begin the month-long in-app testing for the GStocks PH feature before allowing the 76 million users of the app to trade equities listed on the PSE by March this year. A GCash spokesperson said the test run would begin this month and would be monitored by the SEC “until we go full-scale in March.”.

SEC reduces BPI fine to P30 M

SEC has agreed to reduce the penalty it imposed against BPI to P30 million from the original P134.62 million, granting the request of BPI to reduce the penalty.

Citicore prepares for IPO, to invest US$4bn in renewable energy

Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. is planning to go public this year to fund a US$4bn investment in new solar projects over the next five years according to President and CEO Oliver Tan. Citicore will file documents for an initial public offering in the second quarter and complete its listing within the year. Citicore, which has an installed capacity of 241MW via solar panels, plans to invest US$800mn this year to increase output to 1GW. It also plans to spend around US$4bn to reach 5 GW within five years. Citicore is also pursuing seven offshore wind projects with a total capacity of 3 GW. Citicore is the parent firm of Citicore Energy REIT Corp (CREIT PM).

Meralco secures 300MW emergency supply from Aboitiz Power

Meralco signed a 300MW emergency power supply agreement (PSA) with GN Power Dinigin. GNPD is 70% owned by Aboitiz Power. The PSA will be a very short term agreement, expiring on Feb 25 and will partially replace the 670MW PSA that SMC reneged on. This will help smooth out power supply while Malampaya is on shutdown for two weeks this month.

PMFTC cigarette volumes flat QoQ in 4Q22, industry down slightly

PMI disclosed that cigarette volumes sold in the Philippines amounted to 7.6bn sticks in 4Q22, down 12%YoY. The volume held steady from the previous quarter. 3Q volumes saw a slight recovery after price hikes earlier in the year dragged 2Q volumes. For FY22, cigarette volumes declined 6.5% to 32bn sticks, below Regis estimate of 35bn sticks. PMFTC appeared to have gained some market share in the quarter, with unit market share improving to 60.1% (from 59.2% in 3Q).

GCash gets BSP approval to go global, pilots in Australia, Italy and Japan

GCash can soon be accessed abroad with a foreign SIM after it received authority from the BSP to start the beta launch of GCash Overseas, which allows OFWs to remit cash to their families for free. GCash said the pilot run of GCash Overseas will run for a limited time and the full version will be launched later this year. Moving forward, GCash plans to grow its presence outside of Asia by expanding its existing markets in Europe like France, Germany, Italy and the UK.

PLDT to expand its largest data center by 12 MW, build 11th facility

The data center arm of PLDT, ePLDT, said its VITRO Makati 2 would add 12 megawatts (MW) of capacity to power nearly 1,600 new racks, bringing the facility’s capacity to 3,360 racks backed by 20 MW by March 2023. ePLDT said that it will be building its 11th data center,  VITRO Sta. Rosa, in the Philippines at a 5-hectare lot in Sta. Rosa, Laguna. This year, PLDT and its partners are set to activate the $75-million Asia Direct Cable (ADC), which is a 9,400-kilometer (km) subsea cable system led by a global consortium.

Quorn recalls products in the UK

Quorn Foods is recalling Frozen Quorn Swedish Style Balls from UK retail channels because of the possible presence of small pieces of plastic. Quorn generates ~70% of sales in the UK. Regis has yet to receive official comment from Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), but Regis believes the direct impact of the recall could be minimal. Quorn accounts for 20% and 5% of MONDE’s consolidated sales and EBITDA, respectively. MONDE currently trades at 24x 2023E PE.

Megawide eyes EDSA busway, North terminal

Megawide is looking to enter into PPP contracts with the government. It wants to participate in the bidding for the EDSA Busway. The Department of Transportation is currently studying whether the bus operations and station development would be packaged as one. The feasibility study will start in March and will likely take over 6 months to complete. It also plans to submit an unsolicited proposal to build an integrated terminal exchange in Caloocan City to create an EDSA bus loop from Monumento to PITX. The terminal will serve as a counterpart of its own Paranaque Integrated Terminal Exchange (PITX) located in the south.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Remittances rise 5.8% YoY to record high of US$3.16bn in December

Cash remittances coursed through banks reached a record US$3.16bn in Dec 2022. This is up 5.8% YoY and 14.1% MoM. The 3-month average growth rate accelerated to 5.0% in Dec from 4.3% in November. In PHP terms, remittances rose 17.2% YoY during the month. Most sources of remittances saw YoY, MoM improvements. This brought FY22 remittances to US$32.54bn, up 3.6% YoY, broadly in line with the BSP’s target (+4%) and equivalent to 8.0% of GDP. For 2023, the BSP is forecasting remittances to grow by 4%.

Philippine market foreign ownership drops in January

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership fell 32bps MoM to 20.7% while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 6bps MoM to 24.9% (in January). The drop in market foreign ownership appears to be largely due to the 19% appreciation of San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM), which has foreign ownership of only 6.6%. Market net foreign buying amounted rose to US$122mn in January (from US$109mn of net foreign selling in December). 17 PSEi constituents saw declines in foreign ownership while 13 experienced increases.

Philippines to import fresh 440,000 MT of sugar

Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) has greenlighted the plan to bring in 440,000 metric tons of refined sugar to stabilize retail prices and shore up the country’s buffer stock. Once PBBM signs the order, the SRA can proceed with the importation which will be done in three tranches, with the first 100,000 MT expected to arrive by April at the latest. Broken down, 200,000 MT will be allotted for consumer use—which also covers industrial users—while the remaining 240,000 MT will be set aside for the two-month buffer stock. Domestic sugar prices continue to rise and retail price of refined sugar is now at ~USD2/kg, more than double YoY.

BSP raises policy rates by 50 basis points

The BSP raised its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to 6.0%. Overnight borrowing rate of 6.0% is the highest level since November 2008. The BSP has raised its benchmark rate by 400 basis points since May 2022. BSP Governor Medalla said that more increases are possible and that a pause during the March meeting was unlikely. The BSP also raised its 2023 inflation forecast to 6.1%, from 4.5% previously. Inflation accelerated to a 14-year high of 8.7% in January. The Central Bank expects inflation to return to the 2-4% band in 2024. It increased its 2024 inflation forecast to 3.1%, from 2.8% previously. The BSP noted that upside CPI pressures emanate from global food market uncertainties, shortages in key food items, transport fare hikes, elevated oil prices and higher-than expected wage adjustments.

FDI drops by 44% YoY, 14% MoM to US$793mn in November

Foreign direct investments (FDI) recorded US$793mn net inflows in November 2022, down 44% YoY and 14% MoM. This is the lowest monthly figure since Sep 2022. The MoM drop was due to declines in (a) non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments, (b) reinvestment of earnings, and (c) equity other than reinvestment earnings. This brings YT-November net inflows down by 13% YoY to US$8.43bn. The BSP expects FDI net inflows at US$8.5bn by end-2022 and at US$11bn by end-2023.

Unemployment, labor force participation rates mildly worsen MoM in Dec

The latest Labor Force Survey showed a mild deterioration in the labor force participation in December 2022 vs Nov 2022. Labor force participation rate dropped to 66.4% from 67.5%. Meanwhile, the unemployment rate also rose slightly to 4.3% in Dec from 4.2% in November. However, the underemployment rate further improved to 12.6% from 14.4%.

Higher power spot market prices loom

Average wholesale electricity spot market (WESM) prices rose to P7.43/kwh in the first few days of the February billing period. This is up from the P5.76/kwh recorded in January due to a lower supply margin according to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP). For the rest of February, covering also the remaining days of the Malampaya shutdown, IEMOP projects an average market price of P7.55/kwh. Citing a lower overall supply margin equivalent to 3,882 megawatts compared to January’s 4,979 MW.

Marcos says Japan trip gains PH $13-B in contributions, pledges; 24k new jobs seen

The PH’s visit to Japan has raked in 35 investment pledges spanning business interests in infrastructure, energy, manufacturing, healthcare, and telecommunications, among others. Trade Secretary Alfredo Pascual said in an interview that said several investment agreements and pledges are “ready to go”, with some registered already in the Board of Investments.

 

Vehicle sales jump 42% YoY in January

CAMPI/TMA reported auto sales rose by 42% YoY to 29,499 units in January but MoM, vehicle sales fell by 21%. CAMPI has yet to give its sales target for 2023. Toyota sold 13,428 units in January, up 36% YoY (-22% MoM). Market share dipped to 45.5% from 46.2% in December. Regis forecasts assume 23E sales of 166k units (-5% YoY) and GPM of 10.4%. Assuming Toyota can sell 184k units (+6% YoY), Regis estimates Toyota’s profit could rise by 17% and GTCAP’s profit could rise by 4%. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 45% discount to its market-based NAV of P995/sh and at 6.8x FY23E PER.
