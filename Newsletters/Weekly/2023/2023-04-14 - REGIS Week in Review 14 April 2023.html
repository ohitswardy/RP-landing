REGIS REPORT

14 April 2023

The market ended just slightly lower for the week at 6,481.91 as sectors traded mixed while turnover improved to US$101.6m (+44.7% WoW) as investors return from the holiday break.

BDO (+6.3% WoW) was the main focus this week, as multiple foreign buyers gobbled up shares. BDO registered the most inflows worth US$28.2m as sentiment on banks remained favorable. The stock traded as high as 134.80 intraday on Friday, close to its all-time high.

Property names were the biggest drags as multiple foreign funds let go of ALI (-4.33% WoW) and SMPH (-0.90% WoW).

JFC fell 3.8% WoW. The company disclosed its acquisition of a controlling stake in its HK franchisee, which could be a prelude to further its expansion in China.

On the macro front, the BSP revised its inflation target to 6.0% from 6.1% for the year after March CPI figures came out better than expected at just 7.6%.  BSP Governor Felipe Medalla says a pause in rate hikes is possible if the CPI stays flat or comes further down in April.

RESEARCH
Emperador - Brandy segment buoys 4Q on seasonal sales jump
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

4Q22 profit recovers on brandy contribution; FY22 mildly ahead of consensus

EMI’s 4Q22 net income recovered to P2.862bn (46%QoQ, 5%YoY) on seasonally stronger sales particularly for brandy. Overall trends for input costs appear positive, but other costs relating to logistics and marketing specifically for whisky tempered the profit recovery. For FY22, EMI’s income reached P10.1bn (1%), behind Regis but slightly ahead of consensus.

Brandy: margins off the lows, seasonal boost from 4Q sales

Profits from brandy came in at P1.735bn (103%QoQ, -18%YoY) in 4Q22. While markedly higher QoQ, the jump can largely be attributed to the back-ended sales in the Philippines. There was some improvement in EBITDA margin (18.4% in 4Q22 vs 15.7%), presumably the combined result of input cost pressures tapering and the full impact of price increases in the first half. Based on the revenues and the timing of price increases, we can infer that 4Q volumes were up YoY by low single-digits. Despite the improvement, brandy’s FY22 performance was one to forget. EBITDA (P8.1bn) and net profit (P5.5bn) were down by 24-25%YoY. Current margins (22.1% GPM, 18.4% EBITDA margin) are still well below FY23E forecasts (33.5% GPM, 24.5% EBITDA margin).

Whisky: selling costs start to climb as markets reopen

WMG’s profit contribution was stable QoQ at P1.1bn (4%QoQ, 92%YoY). Sales continued to climb (4Q22 rev P6.325bn, 13%QoQ, 22%YoY) which management attributed to single malt sales in Asia, Europe and the return of travel retail. GPM improved while EBITDA margin fell. While input costs fell, supply chain/logistics costs, selling expenses and marketing all went up. A&P expense in particular jumped 37%YoY, while “activation” initiatives across duty free shops have become more aggressive as regional travel resumed. For FY22, WMG’s profit jumped 77%YoY to P4.5bn, accounting for 45% of EMI’s group profit.

Recent trends affirm a medium-term growth outlook of 10-12% p.a. (c.20% for whisky, high single-digit for brandy). However, management will have to stem the slide in brandy’s market share soon or risk further loss of scale.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

JFC buys majority stake in brand’s HK franchisee

JFC’s wholly-owned subsidiary Golden Plate will purchase 60% of Meko Holdings for $16.08 million. Meko is Jollibee brand’s master franchisee in Hong Kong. “Hong Kong is an important market for Jollibee and an important part of JFC China’s strategy to elevate the Jollibee brand in a general population or mainstream market” – JFC.

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) FY22 NPAT rebounds to P2.1bn; margins recover to pre-pandemic levels

Philippine Seven reported FY22 NPAT of P2.06bn, rebounding from the net loss in FY21. For 4Q22 alone, net profit surged 6x YoY on operating leverage as same-store sales recovered. Revenues in 4Q22 jumped 41% YoY as SSSG moderated to 27%, though coming from a higher YoY base. SSS was largely boosted by price inflation, which led to higher average basket sizes (+25-30%). Average customer traffic, however, was still ~20% below pre-pandemic levels as many schools and offices were still on a hybrid set-up. Regis understands SSSG in 1Q23 is trending 15-20%, partly due to the low base from the Omicron surge in 1Q22. SEVN has earmarked P4bn in capex for 2023 primarily for store expansion. The company plans to open 400 stores this year, of which half will be franchised.

ACEN secures financing to develop $1-b wind power project in Lao PDR

ACEN Corp. secured financing to develop the $1-billion Monsoon Wind Power Co. Ltd.’s 600-MW wind project in Lao PDR, making it the first wind power project in Lao PDR and the first cross-border wind project in Southeast Asia. ACEN will have a 24-percent economic stake in the project. ACEN said the power output of the project will then be sold to Vietnam’s state-owned electric ultility.

Bloomberry to build 3rd casino in Cavite

In a disclosure to the exchange, Bloomberry said its third casino, dubbed as the Paniman project, will rise in Cavite. A subsidiary of Solaire previously entered into an agreement with Boulevard Holdings, Puerto Azul, Ternate Development Corp and Monte Sol Development Corp for its purchase of up to 2.8mn sqm of land in Ternate Cavte for roughly P7.5bn. As of December, Solaire’s subsidiary had purchased a total land area of 1.6mn sqm. “SPC intends to develop the Paniman property into an integrated resort and entertainment complex with a world class casino, hotel, golf course, commercial, residential and mixed-use development. The development of the Paniman project is expected to commence after Solaire Resort North in Vertis north, Quezon City has started its commercial operations”

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

NPL/PD ratios inch up in Feb; Loan growth accelerates, back over 11%

Asset quality data was mixed in Feb 2023, with NPL and PD ratios inching up, but with RL ratios down a bit. Banking system NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was up 4bps MoM to 3.42%, while Past Due (PD) ratio was up 3bps MoM to 4.04%. In nominal terms, NPLs and PDs were up 1.5% and 1.4% MoM respectively. Recall that January NPL and PD ratios jumped 13bps and 22bps MoM respectively in January, though Regis noted that this was in keeping with seasonal trends.

 

The February milder MoM uptick was also in line with pre-Covid trends. NPL cover dipped to 105.0% compared to 106.3% in January. Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio inched down 3bps MoM to 2.58%. Loan growth in February accelerated a tad to 11.1% YoY, compared to the 10.8% pace recorded in January. LDRs inched up to 71.7% from 71.5% the previous month.

Car sales jump 19% MoM, 24% YoY in March

CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales jumped 19% MoM and 24% YoY to 36,880 units in March. This brings 1Q23 sales to 97,284 units, up 30% YoY. Toyota sold 16,906 units in March, up 14% MoM and 12% YoY. The March figure is nearing Dec 2017 peak of 17,312 units (prior to excise tax hike). The 1Q23 sales at 45,205 units is up 21% YoY. To reach the company’s FY23 target of 188k units (+8% YoY), monthly sales should only average 15,900 units for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV.

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.88bn in February

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit narrowed to US$3.88bn in February. This is down by 3% YoY and 32% MoM. Imports declined by 12% YoY and 19% MoM, led by lower fuel, capital goods, and raw mat’l goods imports. The drop in imports can likely be attributed to lower commodity prices. Exports fell by 18% YoY and 3% MoM. Exports fell across trading partners, likely reflecting soft global demand. This bought 2M23 trade deficit to US$9.6bn, up 13% YoY.

FDI posts US$448mn net inflows in January 2023

​Foreign direct investment (FDI) posted US$448mn net inflows in January 2023, a 45.7% YoY decline. The gov’t cited global economic uncertainties and high inflation, which continued to weigh on investor decisions. Non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments and equity capital dropped by 57% YoY and 13% YoY, respectively. Meanwhile, reinvestment of earnings increased by 4% YoY.

Employment improves in February

Employment increased to 48.8mn in February, up by 1.4mn MoM and 3.3mn YoY. This was driven by higher employment in agriculture, construction and service sectors, which more than offset lower employment in the manufacturing sector. The number of unemployed increased by 102k MoM; with the unemployment rate steady at 4.8%. Meanwhile, underemployment rate improved further to 12.9%.

 

Marcos orders suspension of fare hike for LRT-1, LRT-2

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has ordered the Department of Transportation (DOTr) to defer fare hikes for LRT-1 and LRT-2 as high inflation continues to hurt consumers’ wallets. The approved rates would have increased boarding fees by P2.29 (21%) and P0.21 per km (21%). The date of implementation would be disclosed after DOTr confers with NEDA; conversations could take “a few months”. The fare increase for LRT-1, LRT-2, and MRT-3 occurred more than eight years ago, in January 2015. Light Rail Manila Corp (LRMC) holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 railroad. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), Macquarie Infra (10%). Regis’ valuation for MPI assumes LRMC’s attributable value at negative P6.86bn (P-0.24/sh).

Headline inflation cools to 7.6% in March

Headline CPI rose 7.6% YoY in March, moderating from 8.6% in Feb. On a MoM basis, headline CPI declined by -0.2%, the first time of negative growth since September 2021. This was due to a decline in prices of food (i.e. vegetables, meat, and oil) and petrol. Sugar prices also rose by just 0.1% MoM, slowest MoM rate in 24 months. These more than offset upticks in rice, flour, and electricity prices. Meanwhile, core inflation accelerated to 8.0% in March from 7.8% in Feb. On a MoM basis, core CPI rose by +0.4%, slightly decelerating from +0.5% in Feb

1Q23 average inflation rate at 8.3%. To reach the BSP’s 6.0% target by year-end, CPI should increase by +0.2% MoM on average for the rest of the year. 

 

Rice prices to rise by up to P5 on reduced imports

The retail price of rice is expected to rise by up to P5 this year due to lower import volume according to Department of Agriculture Deputy Spokesperson, Rex Estoperez. Prices could be kept in check if imports of rice are fast tracked. Domestically-produced regular milled rice is currently priced at P34-40 per kilo in Manila while imported regular-milled rice is selling at P37-44 per kilo. As of March 30, the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI) estimated that the volume of imported rice landed in the country fell 16.20% YoY to 360,196.175 metric tons (MT). Rice inventory declined by 5.1% YoY to 1.77mn MT in end-Dec 2022, after importing 3.8mn MT in full-year 2022.

BSP pause likely in April inflation eases

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that a pause in interest rate increases was possible if the April CPI (CPI) is not higher than the March CPI. “A zero or negative month-on-month inflation” may also support the case for a rate hike pause. Assuming flat MoM, headline inflation rate could slow down to 6.8% in April from 7.6% in March. Headline inflation could only remain at or above 7.5% in April, if MoM rate increase is >+0.8%. Since May 2022, the Monetary Board raised key interest rates by 425bps. Monetary Board’s next meeting is scheduled on May 18; Fed meeting on May 3.

GIR rises to US$100.2bn in end-March

Preliminary data showed the GIR level rose to US$100.2bn in end-March from US$98.2bn in end-Feb. Due to upward revaluation of the BSP’s gold holdings and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. Latest GIR level is equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports.

 

NAIA pax volumes revert to 93% of pre-Covid in 1Q23

The Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) said passenger throughput at NAIA was 10.86mn in 1Q23. This is up 158% YoY and 13% QoQ. The 1Q23 is about 93% of 1Q19 levels. Domestic pax volumes reached 6.16mn in 1Q23, up 94% YoY and +7% QoQ. Domestic pax volumes are now 12% above 1Q19 due to slow recovery of international routes. International pax volumes reverted to 77% of 1Q19. Pre-Covid, NAIA accounted for 80% of the country’s pax volumes. NAIA accounted for 78% of domestic pax volumes and 82% of international pax volumes.

BSP adjusts inflation forecast to below 6%

BSP is further revising downward its inflation forecast to below 6% this year after it cooled to a six-month low of 7.6% in March. BSP would likely step on the brakes of its tightening cycle as early as next month if the downtrend in inflation continues in the coming months – Medalla
