REGIS REPORT

13 October 2023

The index traded in a very tight range for the week, with a low of 6,252 and a high of 6,266 (+/- 14 bps). Conviction remains low given the lack of catalysts.

Trading was generally mixed. Gainers and losers were even at 1:1.

Property names outperformed lead by ALI (+3.0% WoW) and SMPH (+1.0% WoW) as the sector finally rebounded after its recent weakness. Foreign demand lifted the sector, with US$9.3m and US$4.1m flowing in SMPH and ALI respectively.

Meanwhile, banks dragged as BDO (-2.2% WoW) and BPI (-1.2% WoW) fell. BDO saw heavy foreign outflows at US$3.5m.

Headline turnover was at US$103.8m but skewed mostly by blocks that flowed through. Excluding blocks, daily turnover dropped to as low as US$55m last Thursday.

Earlier this week, Singapore sovereign wealth fund GIC sold its 17% stake in Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) to the Century Pacific Group and JE Holdings, holding companies controlled by the Po and Gokongwei families. The transaction price was P9.50/share and puts the total transaction value at P2.7bn. This deal will not alter the public float of PIZZA, which currently stands at 22%, as GIC’s stake was never part of the float. It should also not affect management and the direction of the firm as the Po family is only increasing its controlling stake while the Gokongwei family already holds board representation.

MWC (-0.2% WoW) also bought back its shares from Ayala Corporation (-0.9% WoW) and Philwater Holdings. The company bought 288,998,734 shares at Php17.1614.

On corporate news, MGEN Renewable Energy Inc or MGREEN (Meralco renewables unit) said it has signed an investment agreement with SPNEC and parent Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings Inc in a deal worth P15.9b. Under the agreement, SPNEC will be the vehicle to develop 3,500MW of solar panels and 4,000MW of battery energy storage systems in Luzon. MGREEN will subscribe to 15.7bn common shares and 19.4bn redeemable preferred shares in SPNEC. Upon closing, MGREEN will be the controlling shareholder of SPNEC with a voting control of 50.5%. Separately, SPNEC's board approved the increase in authorized capital stock to 75bn common shares and 25bn preferred shares.

Headline foreign outflows stood at US$58.4m. URC (-2.6% WoW) saw the most significant outflows at US$6.2m.

RESEARCH
Philippine Gaming - Not your grandma’s bingo
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Livestream has gone mainstream

Online gambling in its various forms has broadened its reach over the last several months in no small part due to a supportive regulation and the enabling mechanism provided by digital wallets such as GCash. According to PAGCOR data, gross gaming revenue (GGR) from bingo (electronic and traditional) and e-games operations jumped 120% in 2022 to P28.2bn and is poised to grow another 40% in 2023. Revenue from these outlets previously peaked at P32.2bn in 2019, but the widening of reach and acceleration of rollout from accredited firms means that new records are likely to be set in the near future, in our view.

Integrated resorts jump on the bandwagon

PAGCOR originally issued the remote gaming platform licenses to eight casino operators during the pandemic at the height of the closures. Licenses were generally granted to casino operators (including the four Entertainment City IRs) while one went to a bingo operator (AB Leisure Exponent/BingoPlus). Unlike POGOs, this new framework allowed firms to offer online games to playersbased in the PH. The success of smaller operators has prompted the IRs to revisit the potential of the local online market. Given how fast these have grown alongside Entertainment City, we reckon the markets have little overlap and could offer upside for the IRs like Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) if done right.

A growing niche game operator

Among the more prominent ones that have benefitted from this trend is DigiPlus Interactive Corp (PLUS PM), formerly Leisure and Resorts World Corp. Since launching the BingoPlus online platform in Jan 2022, remote gaming has single[1]handedly lifted it from losses to profits exceeding c.P500mn/qtr. Over the past year, revenue from online bingo has risen significantly, with quarterly GGR up 27x vs 2Q22. BingoPlus’ registered user base has grown to 20mn, helped by its recent addition on the GCash app, the most widely used digital wallet app with >80mn users.

Buy BLOOM at 7.1x FY24E EV/EBITDA

Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) has only recently evolved its online strategy and it is in the process of making a more aggressive push. Specifically, moves to link payments system to leading e-wallets could mean a more mainstream offering in the making. We already like BLOOM for its portfolio approach on the domestic front, and at 7.1x FY24E EV/EBITDA, its multiple is comfortably below historical levels relative to growth. We reiterate our Buy rating and note the potential upside the space offers.

Meralco renewables unit invests P15.9bn for SPNEC control

MGEN Renewable Energy Inc or MGREEN said it has signed an investment agreement with SPNEC and parent Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings Inc in a deal worth P15.9bn. Under agreement, SPNEC will be the vehicle to develop 3,500MW of solar panels and 4,000MW of battery energy storage systems in Luzon. MGreen will subscribe to 15.7bn common shares and 19.4bn redeemable preferred shares in SPNEC. Upon closing, MGREEN will be the controlling shareholder of SPNEC with a voting control of 50.5%. Separately, SPNEC's board approved the increase in authorized capital stock to 75bn common shares and 25bn preferred shares.

Rene Almendras resigns from Manila Water’s board after Ayala Corp’s recent divestment

Mr. Rene Almendras resigned as a Director of Manila Water (MWC PM). This is in line with the divestment of Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) 8% economic and voting shares in MWC, which the latter bought back. Given MWC’s buyback, MWC’s 11-man board will now consist of 5 Razon-elected directors, and 2 Ayala-elected directors, and 4 independent directors.

Manila Water (MWC PM) buys back shares from Ayala Corp (AC PM)

Manila Water disclosed that it bought back 289mn common shares at P17.1647/sh and 436mn participating pref shares (PPS) at P1.7165/sh. Shares were bought from Ayala Corp (AC PM) for a total consideration of P5.7bn or P17.165/sh, 4.5% lower than MWC’s share price prior to deal announcement, 4.6% lower than Regis TP of P18/sh. Recall that the PPS participate in dividends declared to common shares equivalent to 1/10 of the amount declared for the commons. AC’s economic and voting stakes in MWC fell to 22.5% and 23.5%, respectively, from 30.4% and 31.5%. MWC accounts for 3% of AC’s NAV and c. 4% of profit. AC is trading at a 32% discount to its market-based NAV of P902/sh. Meanwhile, the transaction reduced MWC’s fully-diluted outstanding shares by 10% to 2.95bn. Regis estimates the buyback raises EPS by 5% and DCF-based TP by 5%, after adjusting for a higher net debt and lower shares out. Post-transaction, MWC’s treasury shares will consist of 292mn common shares and 436mn PPS. It is unclear at this stage if MWC will ultimately cancel the PPS. Mr. Enrique Razon, will also see his economic stake and voting stake in MWC rise to 39.6% and 58.4%, respectively, from 35.6% and 52.2%.

Dito Telecommunity appoints CEO

Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel) announced it had appointed Eric Alberto as its CEO. Alberto is also the CEO of Dito CME (DITO PM), the listed entity which owns a controlling interest in DitoTel. Alberto takes the place of Dennis Uy, who is the principal shareholder of DITO. Alberto was previously Chief Revenue Officer of PLDT prior to becoming associated with Dito. It’s unclear what prompted the change at this time and it might be related to the entry of new shareholders or to conditions imposed by creditors.

Waterfront Manila Hotel & Casino sets rebuild timeline

Acesite (Phils), the listed operator of Waterfront Manila Hotel and Casino disclosed a tentative completion date for the initial rebuilding process of its property for May 2024. The property was damaged by fire six years ago. The second and third phases of reconstruction plan are targeted for completion by January 2025 and May 2025. Waterfront Manila, previously known as Manila Pavilion, is in UN Avenue, Manila City.

August NPLs steady, but slight uptick in Past Due; Loan growth steady at 9.4%

Asset quality indicators for the entire banking sector were broadly stable MoM in August, though there were some slight divergent moves up/down. There were also some minor revisions to the June/July data, as tardy banks submitted data.

 

NPL ratio (net of interbank loans) was steady at 3.51% in August, with the nominal value up 0.6% to P442.6bn. Slightly worrying, though, was the slight uptick in Past Due (PD) rates to 4.15% in August, up 3bps MoM from 4.12% in July, and 4.09% in June. In PHP terms, PDs were up 1.8% MoM to P538.2bn. On the other hand, Restructured Loan (RL) ratio inched down 2bps MoM to 2.36%, from 2.38% in July. This is the lowest RL ratio since May 2021. NPL cover improved to 103.0% from 102.3% the previous month.

 

Loan growth came in at 9.4% YoY August, slightly slower than the 9.6% (revised) in July. Interestingly, loan growth for “All Banks” is materially higher what was reported for the Universal and Commercial Banks  (UKBs), which was +7.2% in August.

 

Note that the UKB data is only for the Parent bank, and would exclude their Thrift, Rural, and Digital Bank subsidiaries. Loan growth outside of the UKBs has been a staggering 20-25% YoY since January 2023. This could also account for the uptick in system-wide NPL ratios this year, as the TB/RB/DB segments deal largely with consumer, MSME, and micro-finance loans which are by definition higher NPL. Based on BSP data, the latest NPL ratios (ex-interbank) outside of UKBs are 6.94% for Thrift Banks, 8.27% for Rural Banks (as of June), and 9.26% for Digital Banks, significantly higher than the NPL ratio for UKBs at 3.20%. (Caveats galore – 1. The UKB data does not completely match up with what the BSP comes out with in their press releases; 2. The Rural Bank data only comes out quarterly; 3. The Digital Bank data set only began in March 2023.)

 

The data seems to point to relatively benign conditions. That said, inflationary pressures only started picking up in August. Regis will watch the data closely for deterioration in coming months.

Semirara declares better-than-expected P3.50/sh special dividend

Semirara declared a special dividend of P3.50/sh, equivalent to a yield of 10% based on Oct 10 close. This is higher than Regis forecast of P3/sh (highest in Street) and consensus median P1.25/sh. Ex-div date is on Oct 20 while record date is on Oct 23. Payment will be on Nov 8. This takes FY23E dividends to P7.00/sh, better than Regis estimates (P6.50/sh).

GIC sells 17% stake in Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM) to Po and Gokongwei families

GIC, through Arran Investment, sold its 17% stake in PIZZA to the Century Pacific Group (CPG) and JE Holdings, which are private holding companies of the Po and Gokongwei families, respectively. The 17% stake was valued at P9.50/sh – approx P2.7bn (USD48mn). Following the transaction, CPG will own 62% of PIZZA while JE will own 15%. There should be no change in shares outstanding or public float.

 
 
MACRO RESEARCH
 
 
 
 

Trade deficit narrows in August

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit narrowed by 31% YoY, 2% MoM to US$4.13bn due to a mild rebound in exports. Imports fell by 13% YoY, 3% MoM to US$12.27bn partly due to base effects from last year’s higher commodity prices, as evidenced by the 6% YoY drop in fuel imports and 19% YoY drop in raw mat’l imports. Meanwhile Capital goods imports suffered its steepest monthly decline so far this year, falling by 19% YoY in August. Consumer goods imports rose by 4% YoY. Exports rebounded, rising by 4% YoY and 9% MoM to US$6.7bn due to a 19% YoY jump in exports to HK, the third straight month of positive YoY growth rate.

Additionally, electronic exports rose 6% YoY in August, a four-month growth streak. This brings

YT-August trade deficit to US$25.5bn, down 39% YoY. The government is forecasting exports and imports to fall by -4%/-3% YoY, respectively, suggesting a FY23E trade deficit of US$57.29bn (-1.6% YoY, ~13% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 peaked at US$58.24bn (14% of GDP).

Free jeepney and bus rides in Metro Manila from Nov 1 to Dec 31

The government will relaunch its free ride program for public utility jeepneys and buses in Metro Manila from November 1 to December 31. The LTFRB will give priority to modern jeepneys and cooperatives previously enrolled in the service contracting scheme. The Department of Budget and Management has approved funding of P1.3bn for the free rides.

End-September GIR drops to US$98.69bn

Preliminary data showed the GIR level dropped by 0.9% MoM (US$878mn) to US$98.69bn

GIR figure is lowest in 7 months due to a US$607mn MoM drop in foreign investments. The level is equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$99.5bn by year-end.

Gov’t raises US$1.26bn from 2nd tranche of retail dollar bonds

The Marcos government raised US$1.26bn in the second tranche of its retail dollar bond offering. The offer was upsized from the initial offer of US$1bn (bonds will mature on April 11, 2029). This was much higher than the minimum issue size of US$200mn but below the US$1.593bn raised at the maiden retail dollar bond auction in Oct 2019. Offering targeted individuals, mostly OFWs according to Dept. of Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. The five-and-a-half-year bonds fetched a coupon rate of 5.75% and were awarded at rates ranging from 5% to 5.75%, bringing the average to 5.509%.

 

The coupon was 437.5 basis points (bps) higher than the 1.375% rate of the five-year retail dollar bonds and 350 bps higher than the 2.25% set for the 10-year bonds offered in 2021. The demand for the bonds reached US$1.28bn but the Treasury did not make a full award as it did not accept tenders that went beyond the coupon rate. During the initial retail bond offer last Sep 27, the government raised US$611mn; Yield for the 5.5-year bond was also at 5.509% (vs. 5.32% in secondary market benchmark rate). The government is targeting around US$1bn from issuing Sukuk bonds by end-November. The government initially programmed US$5bn overseas bond sales for the year.

NAIA 9M23 passenger volumes revert to 94% of 2019 levels

Passenger volumes at the NAIA reached 33.76mn in 9M23, reverting to 94% of 9M19 levels. Volumes are on track to reach 45mn pax by year-end (vs. FY19 pax volumes of 47.86mn). This implies that for the month of September alone, pax volumes fell 14% MoM to 3.4mn or ~85% of pre-Covid levels. August pax vols. stood at 3.95mn, or ~99% of pre-Covid levels. Note September is typically a lean month.
