REGIS REPORT

01 November 2023

The market rebounded sharply (+2.9% WoW) after falling below the 6000 level the previous week driven by the better-than-expected GDP print for 3Q.

Earlier in the week, the GDP growth number for 3Q came out at 5.9%, well above expectations. Not only was it a sharp acceleration from the 4.3% in 2Q, it was also much higher than what the Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor was hinting at just two weeks ago. The main reason for the surprise was government spending, which rebounded to +6.7% growth from a 7.1% contraction.

CPI figures for the month of October likewise came out slower than expected at 4.9% (vs. 5.6% consensus). Food inflation moderated to 7.3% from 10% in September as rice inflation came down to 13.2% (vs. 17.9%) while vegetable price inflation slowed down to 11.9%. This inflation print likely will hold back any move by the Bangko Sentral (BSP) to hike rates this month.

Gainers outnumbered losers 11:4 while foreigners finally turned net buyers for US$1.5m after 13 straight weeks of outflows.

Property and banks led the rally. Ayala Land gained 5.9% WoW as the company reported 9M23 net profit at P18.4bn (+38% YoY). More importantly, the company’s new CEO, Anna Ma. Margarita Dy, announced her plan to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023). Her plan entails expanding the company’s rental portfolios and increasing the residential segment’s focus on high-end projects. Ayala Land was the top bought name by foreign investors for US$9.0m. SMPH (+3.3% WoW) earnings also came through in line with Regis and consensus forecasts. In contrast to the soft 3Q sales figures of some of the retailers, SM Prime’s 3Q23 mall revenue was resilient while their residential reservation sales fell 15% YoY and 36% QoQ. Although performance for this segment was weak, large quarterly swings are common for this business. SMPH, however, was the top sold name by foreign investors, with outflows at US$4.6m.

BDO likewise gained 4.5% WoW as it saw foreign inflows worth US$3.9m.

SM Investments (+2.0% WoW) led the conglomerates after profits rose 30% to P56bn for 9M, putting it ahead of trend to meet Regis and consensus estimates. The key listed subsidiaries reported results that were within/slightly ahead of expectations while SM Retail delivered fairly robust numbers. Revenue for the latter was up 12% to P291bn, while income rose 19% to P13.7bn. 

On the flipside, power and consumer names underperformed.

ACEN (-4.1% WoW) headline profit of P2.3bn (+6%QoQ, +20%YoY)/P6.6bn (+59%YoY) for 3Q23/9M23 only appeared in line with FY23 due to c.P2.5bn in gains relating to a partial sale of an investment. However, a low wind regime and another outage at SLTEC dragged core operations of ACEN much lower QoQ, with management estimating that “core operating earnings” fell 92%QoQ to just P176mn in 3Q23.

CNPF and Wilcon both fell by -2.1% WoW and -0.5% WoW respectively. CNPF’s branded business continued to grow strongly in 3Q23 (+14% YoY) mainly driven by the dairy segment, which benefitted from downtrading while GPM expanded both QoQ and YoY on favorable input costs, but this did not translate to meaningful NPAT growth (+13% YoY in 3Q) as the company increased A&P spending. Monde Nissin (+6.1% WoW) outperformed its peers as principal shareholders are extending a backstop for Quorn to cover further losses until 2032. The recovery of the instant noodle business also drove 3Q23 performance, along with favorable input costs for wheat and palm oil which led to a 73% jump in segment EBITDA.

Despite the rally, conviction remained low as turnover dropped to US$64.9m (-5.9% WoW).

Semirara Mining and Power Corp. - Heavy rains, lower prices weigh on 3Q23
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M23 profit of P22.4bn (-10% YoY) – Behind estimates

3Q23 profit after adjusting for FX and one-offs fell 69% YoY, 71% QoQ to P2.95bn. This brings 9M23 core profit to P22.43bn, down 10% YoY and trending behind Regis and consensus forecasts. 3Q23 EBITDA of P4.53bn (-60% YoY, 64% QoQ) disappointed, after heavy rains and lower commodity prices adversely affected coal mining shipments and power ASPs. Non-cash cost of P4.9bn in 9M23 (+7% YoY) is also tracking higher than our full-year forecast of P5.7bn, even as 9M23 capex of P3.0bn (-17% YoY) trails our FY23E (P5.7bn, -5% YoY).

Coal mining volumes slump in 3Q

The mining unit saw 3Q23 profits drop 74% YoY to P2.0bn, pulled down by a 22% YoY decline in sales volumes to 2.5mn MT and a 36% YoY drop in ASPs to P3,315/MT (US$59/MT). Management blamed the 3Q softness to poor weather and the lack of coal inventory. Cash costs (ex-royalties) also went up further (28% QoQ, 30% YoY) to P1,871/MT in 3Q23, as pre-stripping activities continued.

Lower ASPs, plant availability drag power unit’s 3Q23 performance

The power unit suffered from plant outages in 3Q23, with average operating capacity falling by 12% YoY to 613MW. Spot prices were also lower, bringing down its 3Q ASP by 23% QoQ/YoY to P4.82/kwh.

Better near-term outlook supports 24E dividend forecast of P4.50/sh

Management is confident that operations for both segments will be better in 4Q. It is still aiming to sell 16mn MT this year, suggesting coal sales could rebound strongly to at least 5mn MT in 4Q. Energy prices are also recovering, with (a) ICI4 spot higher at c. US$60/MT from the US$52 average in 3Q23 and (b) power spot prices also trending higher sequentially. These, as well as reduced pre-stripping activities, suggest earnings should bounce back strongly in 4Q. We maintain our 24E dividend forecast of P4.50/sh, which is premised partly on coal mining ASPs declining by 40% YoY to US$42/MT, lower than spot rates of US$60/MT.

 
Globe Telecom - 3Q23: Encouraging signs
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M23 core income dips 8%

Globe’s 3Q core income (excluding one-offs) amounted to P4.8bn, down 3% YoY, taking 9M core income to P14.8bn. down 8% YoY. While the results could be described as trending below our FY23E forecast, we see encouraging signs. Overall service revenue is up +1% QoQ and +4% YoY, led by mobile revenue. Non[1]telco revenues were also a bright spot, growing 44% in 9M and now account for 3% of total revenue.

Mobile bounces, broadband revenue likely to have bottomed

However, despite the subscriber cull (subscribers down 34% QoQ to 54.7m as of Sept)from SIM registration in 3Q and what is typically seasonally weak 3Q, mobile revenue was up a surprising 2% QoQ (and +7% YoY). Revenues from ‘legacy’ mobile services (voice and SMS) were practically unchanged QoQ even as mobile data service usage continued to rise. Despite talk of “market repair”, mobile data yields dipped 3% QoQ to P14.9 per GB, perhaps because users are maximizing their data plans in these inflationary times.

Home broadband revenue is still down 1% QoQ but we believe 3Q is likely to be the bottom for this segment. After shedding wired broadband subs every quarter since 4Q22, Globe launched its prepaid fiber product in July and added a net 23k wired subscribers in 3Q22.

Mynt contributions still rising

Mynt contributed P648m in profit for the quarter, equivalent to 13% of 3Q’s core income. Mynt’s share of Globe’s core profit has grown steadily since last year.

Tower sales completion pushed to 2024

Globe has completed the sale of P53bn (out of P96bn) worth of its telco towers to date. Because of unforeseen delays, however, it now expects full completion of its tower sales only by 2024. This would push back the expected receipt of proceeds and the recognition of gains.

 
SM Prime - 3Q23: mall revenue remains resilient but residential presales drop
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Operating profit in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts

In a limited press release, SM Prime reported that 9M23 net profit rose 37% to P30.1bn while operating profit grew 29% to P44.5bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. The main highlights in 3Q23 were: the resilient mall revenue and the substantial drop in residential reservation sales.

Mall revenue remains resilient despite pressure on consumer wallets

Despite the pressure on consumer wallets in 3Q23, this did not appear to dampen spending in SM Prime malls. The company’s total mall revenue rose 2% QoQ and 23% YoY. We estimate that Philippine mall revenue inched up 1%-2% QoQ (up over 20% YoY), which is unusual since 3Q is seasonally weaker than 2Q. This would also mean that 3Q23 Philippine mall revenue was about 15% higher than in 3Q19 (c. 5% higher on a same-mall basis). We expect the company’s mall business to continue performing well this 4Q holiday season.

3Q23 residential presales drop substantially but revenue grows

3Q23 residential reservation sales fell 15% YoY and 36% QoQ to P20.8b. While this is a weak number, we do not consider it a cause for concern at this point since large quarterly swings are normal for this business. Meanwhile, residential revenue grew 15% YoY and 10% QoQ to P11.6bn, slightly higher than the 2019 average. This could indicate that cancellation rates continue to improve.

Revenue of other businesses also improved

Revenue of SM Prime’s office and hotel businesses also grew in 3Q23. Office revenue expanded 9% QoQ, likely implying that occupancy improved from the 84% level in 2Q23. Meanwhile, hotel revenue rose 43% YoY (+9% QoQ).

 
PLDT - 3Q23: Revenue growth still elusive
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income up 4% on lower non-cash costs

PLDT’s 9M core income, inclusive of losses at Maya (formerly Voyager) amounted to P24.3bn, +4% higher YoY. The figure appears on track to mildly beat our FY23 estimate but this outperformance is mainly due to a large reduction in non-cash costs (depreciation), without which earnings would have actually declined. Operating revenue growth for 9M is still sluggish (+2%) with wireless revenue still down 1% YoY.

Revenue growth remains a challenge

Mobile service revenue saw a modest rise (+1%) QoQ to P23.7bn, which we believe is attributable to an improving competitive environment leading to “market repair”. The revenue figure, however, is still marginally down YoY. Even broadband, which was supposed to be a faster-growing segment, barely saw growth in 3Q. The segment reported a net decline, albeit small, in wired subscribers of 13k, suggesting that revenues are likely to have peaked until PLDT manages to capture new, lower-end users. We expect the company to introduce a prepaid service to compete against the two other major operators soon.

Maya funding round imminent

Maya (formerly Voyager) is expected to raise funding imminently, which would be in line with our expectations given its cash burn and losses since the last round. The losses from Maya have narrowed slightly YTD but still represent ~7% of PLDT’s core income. An announcement is likely soon and we expect PLDT to maintain, if not increase, its 36%+ stake in Maya with this funding round.

Strategic partner for data centers

Management also revealed that it is undertaking a process to find a strategic partner for its data center business. We expect the partner to not only inject more funds but, more importantly, accelerate its business expansion by securing global clients for the data centers. Recall Globe was able to secure STT GDC as partner last year.

 
Ayala Land - Weak 3Q23 revenue; management aims to double earnings in five years
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Long-term plans revealed

Ayala Land’s 9M23 net profit grew 38% to P18.4bn. While this is tracking ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, 3Q23 development revenue was weak (-15% QoQ, -12% YoY) and we suspect that the unusually strong 3Q23 EBIT margin (+3.8ppt QoQ, +12.2ppt YoY) will not be sustained.

More than the results, however, we believe investors should focus on the long[1]term plans of the new CEO, Anna Ma. Margarita Dy. She expressed optimism about the prospects of each of the major businesses and is targeting to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023), implying a 15% earnings CAGR. Under her leadership, Ayala Land will prioritize utilization of its existing land bank and its residential segment will place more emphasis on high-end projects as well as horizontal ones. The company also plans to expand its mall and office portfolio by about 35% over the long-term and to double the number of hotel rooms.

3Q23 residential reservation sales fall but management expects a rebound

3Q23 reservation sales fell 10% QoQ (down 1% YoY) to P27.6bn, only 76% of the 2019 average. It appears that sales of the Alveo project in Fort Bonifacio, which was a major contributor in 2Q23, have slowed down. Despite this, management is still aiming to launch P53bn of residential projects within 4Q23 alone, from only P36bn over 9M23. Projects worth P21bn have already been launched and ALI is looking to launch a P20bn ultra-high-end condominium project in Makati by year-end.

Management is targeting mid-teens presales growth in 2023 and 2024, broadly in line with our forecasts and faster than the 11% recorded in 9M23. This would imply about 25% QoQ and YoY growth in 4Q23. Unsold inventory also improved to 20 months (of reservation sales) in 3Q23, from 22 months in 2Q23 (24 months in 4Q22).

Residential cancellation rates starting to moderate but…

Cancellation rates remain elevated at about 10% of development revenue, higher than the 5-7% during the pre-COVID period. While residential cancellations have started to moderate, cancellations on office-for-sale projects remain high due to the high industry vacancy rate.

Major mall renovations should not cause a drop in revenue

3Q23 mall revenue rose 4% QoQ as occupancy improved 1ppt QoQ. While four flagship malls (30% of space) will begin major renovation next year (completion in 2026), ALI does not expect mall revenue to drop during the period.

9M23 net profit +10% YoY to P4.6bn - behind

CNPF reported net profit of P1.4bn in 3Q23, +13% YoY, bringing the 9M23 figure to P4.6bn, up 10% YoY. The YTD performance is tracking behind Regis FY23 estimates but broadly in line with consensus. Monde Nissin - 3Q23: Noodle business rebounds; shareholders extend backstop for Quorn
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M23 core net profit flat as noodle business rebounds in 3Q

MONDE reported a 41% jump in 3Q23 core net income to P2.2bn, bringing 9M23 core income to P5.7bn, flat YoY. The 9M figure is behind our estimates but ahead of consensus. Relative to our estimates, the APAC BFB business was broadly in line while Quorn underperformed. 3Q consolidated revenues rose 12% YoY to P20.5bn while EBITDA surged 72% YoY to P3.6bn as the core instant noodle business rebounded.

Core noodle business recovers

Segment sales for APAC BFB jumped 21% YoY in 3Q driven by the instant noodle business. While the YoY growth is flattered by a low base due to the product recall in 3Q22, volume sales in 3Q23 were even higher than during the peak of pantry stocking during the 2020 lockdowns. Underlying volume growth was partly driven by market share gains (+20bps QoQ) resulting from MONDE’s larger kasalo pack, which was a response to competition.

Favorable input prices buoy margins

Operating leverage and favorable input prices led to a sharp jump in EBITDA (+73% YoY) in 3Q23. We expect margins to continue recovering sequentially as MONDE has secured up to 80% of its requirements for key inputs (wheat and palm oil) at favorable prices until 2Q24.

Shareholders extend backstop for Quorn

Quorn continues to operate in a challenged environment with sales down 4% YoY in 3Q23. Restructuring efforts undertaken since early 2023 helped support EBITDA, which was practically breakeven in 3Q.

Meanwhile, MONDE announced that its principal shareholders are extending a “cash top-up support” to Quorn. The family shareholders are effectively covering further losses at Quorn with a maximum guarantee of 2.156bn MONDE shares until Dec 2032. This should limit the impact of any future potential losses or impairment charges from Quorn on MONDE’s balance sheet.

Organic yield growth moderates to 2% YoY in 3Q on sluggish volume growth

Management however sounded upbeat on its near-to medium-term outlook. Volumes at the port of Manila have recovered, with September and October volumes hitting the highest levels for the year. It also secured a major client in Melbourne, which will start calling at VICT in 1Q24. Lastly, they expect to implement a chunky tariff hike at the port of Manila by July 2024.

9M23 EBITDA of US$1.1bn, trending above consensus estimates

EBITDA inched up to an all-time high of US$378mn in 3Q, despite the loss of its contract in Pakistan in June. 9M23 EBITDA rose 7% YoY to US$1.11bn, in line with our forecast but looking on track to beat consensus’ FY23E of US$1.45bn.

Large one-off provision in 4Q? Durban port takeover by April 2024

Management indicated that it will be taking the most “conservative” approach on the accounting treatment of the remaining receivable from the Sudanese government, which has a carrying value of Euro 140mn. We reiterate that failure to recover the receivable will not affect our valuation nor the company’s dividend payout. Lastly, due to a reorganization at Transnet, management reckons an April 2024 takeover of DCT2 in Durban, South Africa is more likely.

Megaworld - 3Q23 reservation sales remain resilient; House Bill on BPO incentives a risk
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M23 profit rises 43%, in line

Megaworld’s 9M23 net profit rose 43% to P12.0bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. While residential revenue is trending slightly behind our estimates, this was offset by stronger-than-expected residential GPM. Beyond the earnings, however, we believe investors should focus on the resilient reservation sales and the risks from potential new regulations on work-from[1]home policies for the BPO sector.

Provincial projects drive 3Q23 reservation sales

Although 3Q23 residential reservation sales fell 3% YoY and 8% QoQ to P33.4bn, we still consider this a respectable performance as it is about 90% of the 2019 average. 9M23 reservation sales grew 28% to P109.5bn, similar to the pre-COVID level. Provincial projects sold more briskly and comprised 58% of 9M23 sales, up from only 50% in 1H23.

Office occupancy stays flat but House Bill could be a key risk

3Q23 office revenue was flat QoQ, as was the occupancy rate of 89% (which is higher than the 82% occupancy level of the industry). Revenue should improve slightly by 1Q24, thanks to the completion of a 69,000sqm building in Fort Bonifacio, which is fully preleased.

A key risk to the office segment is a Bill in the House of Representatives which seeks to allow BPO firms to avail of tax incentives even if employees are fully work-from-home (WFH). Currently, BPO companies, which occupy 75% of Megaworld’s office space, are allowed a regulatory maximum of 30% WFH.

Management believes that BPO firms that occupy space in Metro Manila (especially Fort Bonifacio) offer higher value services and are less likely to implement full WFH policies. We estimate that about 60% of Megaworld’s office space is in Fort Bonifacio (while about 80% of its office space is in Metro Manila).

SM Investments 9M profits rise 30% to P56bn

SM Retail’s results are also looking quite strong with profit of P13.7bn for 9M23 (+19%). Revenue growth of 12% in 9M and income growth of 19%. Profits were uncharacteristically higher in 3Q vs 2Q, driven by Non-Food segments, due to school-related purchases.

Robinsons Land 3Q23 net profit jumps 49%, ahead

Robinsons Land’s 3Q23 earnings grew 49% to P3.1bn. This brought 9M23 net profit to P8.8bn (+31%), ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Multiple segments outperformed. Among the major segments, the key outperformance came from realization of earnings from residential JV projects. 3Q23 mall EBIT jumped 4% QoQ and 68% YoY to P1.5bn, nearly 20% higher than the pre-COVID level. BLOOM 3Q23 EBITDA slows 23%QoQ to P4.2b as win rate, VIP volume drag GGR; 9M23 is nevertheless still ahead of FY23E

BLOOM posted a 3Q23 profit of P1.9bn in 3Q23, (-46%QoQ, 19%YoY) as GGR slowed sequentially to P13.3bn (-12%QoQ, -0.5%YoY). 2Q23 profit also included a P357mn gain on an asset sale. This brings 9M23 profit to P8.3bn (+104%YoY), ahead of Regis (P9.7bn) and consensus (P10.9bn). The 3Q23 EBITDA came in at P4.2bn (-23%QoQ, 15%YoY). The 9M23 EBITDA of P15.4bn (48%YoY) is still tracking ahead of Regis (P19.1bn) as win rate normalizes from the high prints in 1H23. However, it is slightly short of consensus estimates for FY23 (P21.2bn). 3Q23 GGR slowed to P13.3bn (-12%QoQ, -0.5%YoY), reflecting what the company has been guiding.

 

VIP was the biggest decline on a QoQ basis, dragged by a -15%QoQ drop in volume. Mass table GGR meanwhile fell 18%QoQ as win rate dipped 8.10ppts to 29.8%. The win rate for mass averaged 39% in 1H23. The slot machine GGR was also softer by 4%QoQ, even with volumes remaining afloat on a lower win rate (5.6% vs 6.0% in 2Q23). Worth noting on a YoY basis, VIP volume was up 10%, mass table drop up 31% and slots handle up 7%.

Meralco – another strong quarter

Another quarter, another surprise.  Meralco delivered another strong quarter with 3Q core profits soaring 90% to P10.8bn.  As a result, cumulative core income for 9M is now P30.0bn, up +53% YoY.  This is well ahead of expectations (both Regis and consensus) on the back of strong generation profits and improved core distribution volumes. For 9M, the distribution business was 58% of core income, while generation was 34%.

 

Generation provides the lift again. From being a money loser, this segment has seen a massive turnaround. For 3Q and 9M, this business, particularly Pacific Light, has continued to surprise to the upside. Pacific Light has been benefiting from the tight supply situation in Singapore but management seems to hint that period of exceptional earnings will taper off next year.

 

Distribution business volumes rise 6.3% in 3Q. The regulated distribution business saw volumes rise 6.3% in 3Q, led by commercial usage (+9.4%). This lifted 9M volume growth to 4.4%. Industrial volumes are still lagging (+1%).

 

Planning to re-file tariff application.  Meralco has asked the regulator (ERC) to allow it to withdraw its pending tariff review papers for the Fifth Regulatory Period (5RP). It plans to ask the ERC to allow it to treat the first two  years from July 2022 as a “lapsed period” instead and apply 5RP from July 2024 to June 2028. This will mean the current tariff of P1.352/kwh will apply as a final rate for the two years from July 2022 to July 2024.

 

Regis reckons Meralco management thinks it will get better tariffs for 5RP with its new application given (1) higher interest rates today, and (2) a higher asset base, among other things.

 

Core income guidance of only P37bn.  Management is guiding for core income of P37bn this year, despite already reaching P30bn as of 9M. Regis is uncertain how much of this guidance figure is just management attempting to low-ball expectations or there’s something to it (a sharp drop in Pacific Light profits or some write-offs in 4Q).  The last one would be ironic, given that Regis believes Meralco has huge provisions that it can and ought to reverse or write back already.

3Q23 GDP rebounds to 5.9% as government spending picks up

3Q23 GDP came in at 5.9%, accelerating from +4.3% in 2Q23 and beating consensus forecast of +4.7%. The key swing factor was higher government spending, which in 3Q23 rose 6.7% YoY, rebounding from the 7.1% drop in 2Q as government agencies caught up from the 1H shortfall. Private consumption growth modestly slowed to 5.0% in 3Q from 5.5% in 2Q, as consumers felt the brunt of elevated food inflation. Inventory withdrawals continued to drag private investments, with its contribution to GDP growth accelerating to -2.1ppt in 3Q from -1.0ppt in 2Q. Inventory withdrawals also more than offset the recovery in public construction (+26.9% YoY in 3Q vs +0.7% in 2Q) and private construction (+7.8% YoY in 3Q vs -3.2% YoY). Net exports also lifted GDP growth and contributed 1.4ppts to GDP, as imports fell 1.3% YoY in the quarter.

 

On the supply side, growth in key sectors accelerated

Agriculture up +0.9% from +0.2% in 2Q

Industry rose 5.5%, better than +2.1% in 2Q

Services grew +6.8% from +6.1% in 2Q

 

This brings 9M23 GDP growth up +5.9% YoY. To reach the low-end of the government’s target range of +6-7%, 4Q GDP must rise by 7.2% YoY. To downside risk is primary government spending could turn negative (-9% YoY) in 4Q, as this year’s budget (+5% YoY) is constrained by the implementation of the Mandanas ruling. According to the BSP’s business expectations survey, businesses are more upbeat for 4Q23 and the next 12 months

Inflation decelerates to 4.9% in October

Headline CPI rose by 4.9% YoY in October, moderating from 6.1% in September. This came in below consensus median forecast (5.6%) and the BSP’s target range for the month (5.1%-5.9%). On a MoM basis, headline CPI declined by 0.2%, first negative growth rate since April due to lower food prices (rice, vegetables, meat, fish, sugar, oils) and petrol prices (-2.3% MoM vs +4.2%). Rice CPI dropped for the first time in eight months at -4% MoM due to onset of peak harvest and import arrivals (rice CPI rose +13% YoY in Oct from +18% in Sep). The slower food inflation was offset by upticks in (a) housing rental prices (+4.0% YoY in Oct vs +3.9% Sep), (b) gas prices (+6.8% vs +0.2%), and (c) clothing/footwear (+4.8% vs. 4.7%). Core inflation moderated further to 5.3% in October from 5.9% in September.

 

The MoM uptick for core inflation was steady at +0.3%, given higher nationwide jeepney fares (+8%) in Oct 8 and more regions implementing minimum wage hikes (+7-10%). This brings YT-October average inflation rate to 6.4%. The BSP is projecting headline inflation will remain above 4% until July 2024 before coming down to c. 3% or within its 2-4% target band. Assuming the MoM chg in CPI is flat for Nov and Dec, inflation rates for these months will already fall below 4%. The next monetary board meeting is on Nov 16.

Trade deficit narrows further in September

Preliminary data showed the trade deficit shrunk by 27% YoY to US$3.51bn in September 2023. This is the lowest trade deficit figure since October 2022. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit also narrowed by 15% from US$4.13bn in August. Imports fell by 15% YoY in September, a faster YoY decline than in August 2023 (-13% YoY). By commodity group, electronic imports fell 26% YoY while non-electronic imports dropped 11% YoY. By major type of goods, raw material and fuel imports declined by 18% YoY and 27% YoY, respectively, due to base effects from last year’s surge in commodity prices. Consumer goods imports lower by 2% YoY while capital goods imports were down by 11% YoY.

 

Exports fell by 6% YoY in September after rising by 4% YoY in August due to lower exports to Japan (-12% YoY) and HK (-21% YoY), offset by a +16% YoY rebound in exports to China. Meanwhile, electronic exports fell 9% YoY in September. This brings 9M23 trade deficit to US$39.8bn, lower by 15% YoY. The government is forecasting exports and imports to fall by -4%/-3% YoY, respectively, suggesting a FY23E trade deficit of US$57.29bn (-1.6% YoY, ~13% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 peaked at US$58.24bn (14% of GDP).

End-October GIR rises to US$101bn

Preliminary data showed the country’s GIR level rose to US$101.1bn in end-October from US$98.1bn in end-September. The increase was due to (a) issuance of US$1.24bn global bonds, (b) higher gold prices, (c) BSP’s net foreign exchange operations and net income from its investments abroad. This represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$99.5bn by year-end.

House Bill seeks to allow BPO firms to enjoy tax incentives despite full implementation of WFH

House Ways and Means Chair Joey Salceda said that the House Bill “CREATE MORE” seeks to allow BPO companies to enjoy tax incentives despite full implementation of work-from-home policies. Currently, BPO firms registered with PEZA can only implement 30% WFH. Regis estimates that 80%-90% of BPO occupied office space is registered with PEZA. Most BPO firms appear to be implementing WFH for 1-2 days per week. Regis estimates that BPO companies comprise 70%-90% of the occupied office space of the developers Regis covers.

 

Government finalizes Maharlika fund IRR

The Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) have been finalized according to President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. The Law will become effective upon the government’s approval; They are committed to having the MIF operational by year-end. Last October 12, President Marcos suspended the implementation of the MIF pending the study of its IRR. The government wanted to explore methods to enhance and improve the MIF.

Government’s Sukuk bond offer may exceed US$1bn target

The maiden Sukuk bond offering is still pushing through in end-November and could possibly exceed its US$1bn target offer size according to Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. Former National Treasurer Rosalia V de Leon earlier said the government was studying the potential structure of the bonds, which could either be hybrid Wakala, Ijarah, or Marabaha. The government has so far raised US$4.26bn in global bonds this year. The Treasury earlier planned to raise US$5bn.

Secretary of Agriculture plans to reactivate Bureau of Agricultural Statistics (BAS)

Newly appointed Department of Agriculture Secretary (DA) Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said he is planning to reactivate the Bureau of Agricultural Statistics. The gov’t cted that the current database was “somewhat incomplete,” making it difficult to manage domestic production of and demand for agricultural products. Agriculture Assistant Secretary Arnel de Mesa said the responsibility of gathering and analyzing relevant information has been delegated to other agencies and the local government units (LGUs).
