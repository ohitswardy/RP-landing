REGIS REPORT

30 June 2023

The market rebounded for the week at 6,468.07 (+1.2% WoW). Daily turnover improved slightly to US$90.5m (vs US$82.3m last week) although mostly due to a Holcim block that flowed through last Thursday.

Holcim Philippines announced it will take steps to delist itself after major shareholder Holderfin increased its stake to 27%. This purchase of 594m shares (9% stake) from Sumitomo brought the public float down to just over 5%, which triggered a trading suspension. According to the company, the remaining shares will be offered through a tender.

Outside this block, the market rebound was led mostly by consumer names after they declined sharply the previous week. Biggest outperformers were URC (+8.7% WoW) and MONDE (+5.4% WoW) as further clarifications on the bill suggest that instant noodles will not to be part of the food items covered. Instant noodles are 7-8% of URC’s revenue while around ~40% for MONDE. On the other hand, JFC (-0.4%) saw strong foreign demand at US$2.9m.

Power names ACEN (+4.1% WoW), AP (+2.4% WoW), and Meralco (+2.8% WoW) also rallied. ACEN completed its first phase of its transaction to acquire 49% of a portfolio of solar farms in Vietnam. The entire deal, covering 837MW, was first announced last year. Once fully closed, the purchase would add 10% to ACEN’s claimed capacity of 4,177MW (including non-operational capacity).

For the banks, Metrobank (+4.3% WoW) gained sharply after lagging its peers BDO (-1.6% WoW) and BPI (+2.4% WoW) the previous weeks. BDO started to correct after seeing the most foreign outflows at US$1.9m.

Notably, CEB (+2.7% WoW) gained despite talks of a suspension of its franchise. Cebu Air accounts for over half of all domestic air trips in the country and suspension should lead to the disruption of even more travel plans.

Overall, the market still stayed generally quiet given the lack of catalysts. Foreigners turned buyers for US$25.3m.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

DoF: Instant noodles to be exempted from "junk food" tax; seeks sugar liberalization in exchange for expanded SSS tax

Finance Sec. Diokno stated that instant noodles will not be subject to the proposed "junk food" tax despite high sodium content. A pack of noodles typically contains 1500mg to over 2000mg of sodium; recommended amount per day for a person is less than 2300mg. Emphasis was placed on instant noodles being for the poor and will not be covered by higher taxes, despite the higher sodium content of 60%.

DOF consulting with the National Nutrition Council and the DOH to determine the list of foods subject to the tax.  

Meanwhile, Sec. Diokno is also seeking to liberalize sugar imports to cushion the impact of the expanded sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) tax. This would not require the enactment of a measure into law; a directive from the President through the issuance of a Sugar Order would suffice.

NLEX Connector Road may start toll collection by July

NLEX hopes to get regulatory approval to collect toll at its Connector Road by next month. The first 5-km portion of the 8-km Connector Road opened in March and has been losing P473k per day due to non-collection. The first segment will have a tariff of P86 per entry for Class 1 vehicles. Once fully completed by end-2023, the tariff will be P125 per entry.

NLEX Connector Road, an 8-km expressway linking the Caloocan Interchange on Circumferential Road 3 (C-3) to Magsaysay Interchange. Regis estimates 100% equity value of the Connector Road at negative P3.5bn. MPI owns 75% of the Connector Road project.

BAP launches USD-PHP cross currency swap market

The Bankers Association of the Philippines (BAP) launched a dollar-peso cross currency swap market to allow its members to manage foreign exchange and interest rate risks.

The USD-PHP cross currency swap involves a peso fixed rate and US$ floating swap with standard tenors of 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, and 10 years and will use the USD Secured Overnight Financing Rate as the floating rate benchmark. The swap market is supported by Bloomberg and will be traded in Bloomberg’s FIQ.

PNB selling 14% stake in holding firm via private placement

Philippine National Bank (PNB) is putting up for sale a 14% interest in PNB Holdings Corp through a private placement. The wholly owned subsidiary of PNB serves as a holding company of a portfolio of Tan’s real estate assets including PNB Financial Center along Macapagal Avenue, PNB Center along Ayala Avenue and the 8,000sqm property at the intersection of Makati Avenue and Gil Puyat Avenue.

The spin-off of those assets to PNB Holdings from PNB yielded a P33.2bn gain for PNB and P10bn boost to its capital base. To recall, PNB distributed 51% of PNB Holding Corp through a property dividend in 2021.

PHC ownership after distribution:

  • PNB retained 49% of PHC
  • LTG will receive 29% of PHC
  • Public shareholders will receive 22%

PNB planned to list PHC “by introduction”. Since then, the necessary regulatory approvals for the listing have not been secured.

A placement by PNB of 14% of PHC would ensure that the float of PHC is above the 20% requirement for a listing by way of introduction and raise money for PNB.

BSP unwinds relief measure for banks

Starting July 1st, big banks will no longer be allowed to use loans to micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and large corporations as an alternative compliance with reserve requirements (RR). The relief measure for smaller banks, however, is extended until the loans are fully paid, but not later than the end of December 2025. Past due or nonperforming loan, as well as new loans granted after the relief measures expires, will no longer be eligible as alternative compliance with the RR.

The BSP will begin unwinding the temporary relief measure to coincide with the reduction in reserve requirement ratios (RRR) by June 30, 2023. This aims to restore the use of the RR as a liquidity management tool and simplify banks’ compliance with reserve requirements.

BSP plans to cut the RRR for different types of banks, with reductions ranging from 100 to 250 basis points:

  • 250 bps to 9.5% for Universal and commercial banks and nonbank financial institutions with quasi-banking functions
  • 200 bps to 6% for digital banks
  • 100 bps for thrift banks, and rural and cooperative banks to 2% and 1% respectively

The central bank will conduct auctions of 56-day and 28-day securities on June 30 to manage excess liquidity in the financial system.

Meralco announces leadership changes

Mr. Manuel V. Pangilinan stepped down as President but will continue to serve as Chairman and CEO. The company did not name a new president.

Mr. Pangilinan assumed the post of Mr. Rey Espinosa, who was named president and CEO of Meralco in 2019, last May 30.

Meralco Senior Vice President (SVP) and Head of Networks Ronnie Aperocho was promoted to Executive Vice President (EVP) and Chief Operating Officer (COO). Mr. Aperocho will oversee the day-to-day operations.

Lawmaker files resolution to suspend Cebu Pacific franchise

Cagayan de Oro City 2nd District Rep. Rufus Rodriguez filed a resolution seeking the suspension of Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) legislative franchise. The resolution aims to serve as punishment for the airline’s “terrible” service. A Senate probe has also looked into several issues plaguing the airline including overbooking, flight delays and other reported concerns.

Cebu Air is the largest domestic airline, accounting for 52% of domestic air pax volumes and 16% of domestic air and sea pax volumes.

Finance Secretary wants to ban POGO sector

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno reiterated his view that the Philippines should “get rid of POGOs” . While the government would lose some revenue, he believes it would be better for the country in the long run.He opined that the presence of POGO firms might be the reason why the country remained on the Financial Action Task Force’s “gray list”.

At the end of 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-2Q23. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:

  • 51% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
  • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV
  • 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV

POGOs occupy:

  • 5% of FLI’s office portfolio
  • 5% of MEG’s office portfolio
  • 1% of RLC’s office portfolio
Wage board approves P40 hike in minimum wage in Metro Manilla

The regional wage board approved a P40 increase (+7%) in NCR’s minimum wage, which will take effect of July 16. The order will raise the minimum wage to P610 a day for non-agriculture workers. The increase is less than half of the P150 propose in the Senate. Minimum wage in the capital is now P140 below the P750 daily national minimum wage advocated by labour groups.

In June 2022, the government hiked the minimum wage in NCR by P33 (+6.1%). Prior to this, the minimum wage increase was last implemented in 2017 (+4.3%).

It is unclear at this stage if wages in other regions will also be hiked. In 2022, the government implemented wage hikes of +12%-14% throughout the country.

‘Hot money’ exit continues in May

Foreign portfolio investments in the Philippines posted a net outflow of US$124.49mn in May, a 54% drop from the previous year. Gross inflows of hot money in May amounted to US$844.72mn (down 12.5% compared to US$965.62mn last year). Gross outflows decreased by 21.6% to US$969.21mn.

The decrease attributed to an improved economic landscape, the resolution of the US debt ceiling impasse, expectations of the Fed pausing its hiking cycle (at that time), and the slightly deprecating peso. Majority of investment went into PSE listed securities, particularly banks, food, beverage and tobacco, holding firms, property and transportation services.

Top investor countries were the UK, USA, Singapore, Luxembourg, and Hong Kong.

For 5M23 net inflow amounted to US$805mn, reversing the US$1.1bn net inflow recorded in the same period last year. The central bank expects foreign portfolio investments to end the year with a US$2.5bn net inflow.

Holcim Phils parent initiates tender offer

Holcim Phils (HLCM PM) received a notice from its parent, Holderfin, of its intent to conduct a tender offer to all stockholders of record for purposes of delisting.

Holderfin bought 595mn shares (9.22% stake) of HLCM from Japan-based Sumitomo through a block sale at P3.92/sh. The acquisition brought the public float of HLCM down to 5.05%, below the min. public float requirement of the Phil. Stock Exchange. Since HLCM will not be able to raise the required min. public float within a reasonable period, its parent will need to conduct a tender offer.

A tender offer price has not been disclosed. Assuming the tender offer price will be P3.92/sh, Regis estimates this translates to an EV of US$445mn and EV/MT of US$64, well below replacement cost. This will also be well below the proposed buy-in price of San Miguel (SMC PM) back in 2019 (Enterprise value of US$2.15bn or an EV/MT of US$300).

ACEN announces closing of the first phase of Super Energy deal

ACEN disclosed that it completed the first phase of the transaction involving Super Energy’s Vietnam solar farms. The remaining phases are expected to be completed within the year.

The acquisition will be completed in four phases, with the first phase adding 141MW of attributable capacity. To recall, ACEN will acquire 49% of the platform for $165mn under a share purchase agreement signed and announced in February 2022 . The deal involves 837MW (gross) of solar assets in Vietnam. The purchase price for the Vietnam solar plants implies a valuation of roughly $800-900k/MW.

Upon completion of the four phases of the transaction, the Super Energy assets will add 410MW in attributable capacity to ACEN, which reported 4,177MW in attributable capacity as of May 2023. The Super Energy assets are not yet in ACEN’s reported numbers nor Regis estimates.

ACEN appears on track to comfortably exceed its 5,000MW target ahead of 2025.

President Marcos, Jr. appoints Eli Remolona as next BSP Governor

President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. appointed Monetary Board Member (MBM) Eli M. Remolona as the next Governor of the BSP. He will replace incumbent BSP Governor Felipe Medalla, who was appointed last 30 June 2022. Mr. Remolona will be the 7th BSP Governor since the enactment of the New Central Bank Act in 1993.

Mr. Remolona is 70 years old and will serve a 6-year term starting July 3, 2023. He joined the central bank’s policymaking Monetary Board in August 2022. He has extensive international experience in central banking, having worked for 14 years at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and for 19 years at the Bank for International Settlements in Switzerland and Hong Kong. Before moving to the BSP, Remolona served as an independent director of the Bank of the Philippine Islands and chairman of its risk management committee.

Deployment of seafarers to reach pre-pandemic level by yearend

The Department of Migrant Workers expects deployment of seafarers to recover to its pre-pandemic level by year-end, with higher demand from cruise ships. In1Q23, 150k seafarers were deployed in, putting it on track to exceed 500k. In 2019, the country deployed 505,769 seafarers. In 2022, deployment reached 489,852.

Sea-based remittances account for 21% of total cash remittances. As of 4M23, sea based remittances are up 2% YoY (vs. total remittances up +3.2% YoY, 2012-2022 CAGR of +3%).

House eyes tax hike on vehicle owners

The House of Representatives tax panel will consider proposed increases in the motor vehicle user’s charge (MVUC) before moving forward and increasing excise taxes on junk food and sweetened drinks.

MVUC, also known as road user’s tax, is paid upon registration and/annual renewal of registration of vehicles. The new rate could increase MVUC for passenger cars to P10,400 from P2,080, depending on the gross vehicle weight.

For utility vehicles, SUVs, buses, trucks, and trailers with a gross vehicle weight of 4,500 kg or more will be levied an MVUC of P1.0 or more per kg of gross vehicle weight. Tax hike will exempt motorcycles, which means that the proposal will only cover 39% of registered vehicles (i.e. cars, jeepneys, van, trucks).

Albay Rep. Jose Ma. Salceda, chair of the House committee on ways and means, said that he still has questions on the proposed taxes on junk food and sweetened drinks. The government estimates the proposed taxed on junk food and sweetened drinks could generate P76bn in addl revenues on its first year of implementation. Note that the government collected P17.8bn from MVUCs in 2022.

Budget deficit narrows in May

The government recorded a budget deficit of P122bn in May, 17% lower YoY, due to higher revenues (+9.4% YoY) and muted expenditures (+0.9% YoY).

Revenues were boosted by higher tax collections (+2.9% YoY) and higher dividend and PAGCOR remittances, investment income and interest of deposits. The BIR collected P213bn, down -1.5% YoY but is broadly in line with the 4M23 monthly average collection of P210bn. Customs collections jumped 18% YoY (vs. +10% YoY in 4M23).

Government spending marginally increased (0.9% YoY), with primary spending (i.e. excluding interest) lower by 0.85% YoY. Excluding the lower National Tax Allotment (NTA) shares of local government units and net lending assistance to GOCCs, spending grew by 5.7% YoY. Interest payment rose sharply (+22% YoY).

The May print brings 5M23 deficit to P326bn, down 29% YoY and on track to come in lower than the government’s year-end target due to stronger revenues.

Digital payments surge 36% in May to new record highs

Online transactions done over the BSP’s National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped 35.9% YoY in May to P1,075bn (roughly $19bn), a new monthly record. For payments done via InstaPay – P50,000 or less – the value of transactions surged 48.0% YoY to P414bn. For larger transactions done over PesoNet, the total value was up 29.2% YoY to P616bn. Both were all-time highs. For year-to-May, total NRPS transactions hit P4,879bn, up 31.6% YoY, with InstaPay up 41.0% and PesoNet up 26.3%.

The InstaPay data is particularly interesting, in our view. Given the relatively low limit per transaction, it is likely more indicative of peer-to-peer or of retail transactions. Based on the BSP data, the average transaction value over InstaPay was P6,550 (under $120) in 5M22, with May alone at an even lower P6,286 (under $115). In terms of number of transactions, InstaPay was up an astounding 51.4% YoY in May.

This suggests continued rapid adoption of digital payments among Filipino individuals. Note, too, that this data only captures payments made between financial institutions. It does not take into account transactions done by parties within the same platform, ie BDO-to-BDO, or BPI-to-BPI, or GCash-to-GCash, etc. Thus, the BSP’s NRPS data likely vastly understates the actual usage of digital payment services.
