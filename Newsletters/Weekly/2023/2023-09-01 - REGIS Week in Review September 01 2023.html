REGIS REPORT

01 September 2023

The market ended just slightly higher at 6,181.06 (+0.33% WoW) as investors stay cautious due to inflation risks as rice prices continue to soar. Rice is 8.9% of the CPI basket therefore the Bangko Sentral may be hard pressed to ease monetary policy.

The index continued to consolidate within the 6200 levels.

Conglos ended the week mixed. AC managed to lead the index (+1.8% WoW) along with GTCAP on strong foreign demand. GTCAP saw US$4.05m inflows, being the top bought stock for the week. However, gains in the sector were offset by SM (-0.6% WoW), as the reclamation concerns continue to weigh on sentiment.  

Recent underperformers recovered. SMPH managed to bounce (+1.1% WoW) as bargain hunters step in after the stock has lost 15.8% YTD while MONDE rose sharply (+4.70% WoW). MONDE printed US$1.0m worth of inflows for the week.  

Most of the activity happened during the MSCI rebalancing last Thursday. Despite having no additions or deletions to the main index, estimated outflows were at US$53m. AC and ALI were expected to see downweights, while upweights for BPI, BDO, and SMPH. Turnover bloated to US$136.3m (vs. just US$67.7m previously).

RCR (-2.8% WoW) was deleted from the MSCi small cap index and therefore saw share price fall 7.8% on Thursday to 4.86 (an all-time low).

Overall, trading was mixed as investors stay in the sidelines for the meantime. Headline foreign flows registered at US$148.4m. Biggest outflows were in MPI, TEL, and URC for US$10.0m, US$7.3m, and US$5.0m respectively.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

AP initiates buyback program

In disclosure to the exchange, the Aboitiz Power’s board of directors approved AP’s authority to buy back its own shares using internally generated cash. The budget for the buyback was not provided. AP trades at 6.1x PE and 4.6x EV/EBITDA and prospective div yield of 6.7% for FY24E.

Globe, Ayala Corp form JV on e-bike business

Globe, Ayala Corp and Gogoro Inc formed a new company for e-scooters in the Philippines. This partnership was announced earlier this year but details were just finalized. Gogoro Inc has its own line of electric scooters and runs a battery-swapping platform. The company will be called Gogoro PH. Globe will own 49% through 917Ventures (100% subsidiary), while AC will hold 21%.  Gogoro Inc will own the rest’. Gogoro PH will import, sell electric scooters and also develop the battery swapping network in the country. The venture will be formed with US$8.5m in capital.

PNX debt breakdown as of 2022

Based on the 2022 Annual Report, Phoenix (PNX PM) had P49.4bn worth of borrowings, of which P39.0bn was term loans to banks. The breakdowns can be seen below. There was no breakdown given on which banks/entities were exposed via LCs and TRs (Letters of Credit and Trust Receipts).

 

BDO (BDP PM) was by far the largest, with a total of P15bn in exposure, including P2bn from its subsidiary BDO Private Bank. Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) and Rizal Commercial (RCB PM) had about P2bn each. BPI (BPI PM) had about P1.1bn, while Robinsons Bank (which BPI has agreed to merge with) had another P900mn, for a total of P2bn. Union Bank (UBP PM) had about P1bn, while China Bank (CHIB PM) had P436mn in loan exposure.

 

The figures are small. For BDO, the loan is equivalent to 0.5% of total loans, and 2.7% of equity. For BPI, it’s 0.3% of loans, and 1.1% of equity. But since both have fully provided for the loans already, there is no more downside risk to earnings/equity. Metrobank and Security Bank were not on the list of creditors.

Digital payments hit new highs in July; smaller-ticket transactions still surging

Payments made over the BSP’s National Retail Payments System (NRPS) continued to surge in July, up 31.5% YoY to a new all-time monthly high of P1,094bn. For 7M23, total payments stood at P7,021bn, up 307% YoY. The data only captures payments made between financial institutions (eg BDO-to-Metrobank, BPI-to-Maya, etc). It does NOT include payments made between two parties on the same platform (eg BDO-to-BDO, GCash-to-GCash, etc), and thus likely vastly under-estimates the true value of digital payments in the financial system.

 

Most notably, in our view, is the data on InstaPay, ie for real-time transfers of up to P50,000 (roughly $880). The value of InstaPay payment grew 40.3% YoY in July to P426.9bn, with the volume of transactions up an eye-popping 53.7% to 72.2mn. This to us suggests wider adoption of digital payments by either a) more individuals and/or b) individuals using digital payments more often, even for smaller items. Most likely it’s a combination of the two. Year-to-date, InstaPay transactions were up 40.7% YoY to P2,689bn.

 

The story was different for PesoNet, which is used for larger, batched payments. Payments here grew 26.5% YoY to P666.9bn in July, while the number of transactions were up just 4.3%. For 7M23, PesoNet logged P4,332bn in transactions, up 25.1% YoY, with volumes up 9.3%. The July value figures for both PesoNet and InstaPay were record highs. Of the banks we cover, BPI (BPI PM) appears to be the most ardent in embracing the digitalization trend. Among the e-wallets, Globe’s (GLO PM) GCash is by far the dominant player, while rival PLDT’s (TEL PM) Maya leads in merchant acquisition.



ACEN finalizes deal with firm for Indo JV

ACEN disclosed that it has entered into an investment and shareholders agreement with PT Trisuya Mitra Bersama for a JV company. The JV company called PT Puri Prakarsa Batam will serve as a platform for the development of RE projects in Indonesia, focusing on large-scale solar power plants, battery energy storage system and green hydrogren projects. ACEN’s partnership with the Puri Usaha Group was initially announced in May last year.

JFC hit with complaint from US National Labor Relations Board

JFC’s North American subsidiary has been charged by the US National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) for unfair labor practices. The complaint was made by 9 workers in one of Jollibee’s locations in New Jersey, who claim they were illegally fired after organizing a petition for higher wages and better working conditions. The complaint requires Jollibee to comply with the workers’ demands, including reinstatement of the 9 illegally terminated workers. JFC’s North America business (including Smashburger) accounted for 13% of systemwide sales as of 1H23.

ICTSI borrows US$750mn from Metrobank

ICTSI is availing of a US$750mn term loan facility with Metrobank (MBT PM), with a tenor of 6 years. The proceeds will be used to fund short-term obligations and strategic M&A. ICTSI’s maturing obligations for 2H23 and 2024 are US$369mn (of which US$269mn was paid in July) and US$436mn, respectively. ICTSI’s net debt (treating perps, con liab, lease liab as debt) stood at US$4.66bn in end-June 2023. Regis estimates the new loan (assuming an interest rate of 6.2%) will increase net debt to EBITDA to 3.6x (from 3.1x) and reduce pre-tax profit by 5% (c. US$47mn annually). This scenario assumes all loan proceeds are deployed to acquire the port of Durban and the new port will not contribute to profit.

ERC grants Meralco-PEDC move to end supply deal

The Energy Regulatory Commission said it granted the join motion for contract termination filed by the companies on Jun 23, 2022, citing Panay Energy Development Corp (PEDC) PEDC’s inability to meet its contractual obligations because of the losses it incurred. Meralco and PEDC cited the “change in circumstance” provisions in their power supply agreement as the reason for termination. The companies argued that the significant increase in the global cost of coal or fuel prices for 2022 had led to PEDC suffering losses amounting to P962.23mn as of Sep 2022. The regulator said that Meralco and EPDC stipulated in their PSA that they had the option to terminate at the instance of a “change in circumstance”, as in this case. Such conditions specifically defined by “change in circumstance” provisions in their PSA were present in this instance.

DENR declares support for ‘critical minerals’ exploration

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources said it will provide support for efforts to discover deposits of “critical minerals” like iron, cobalt, rare earths, and nickel according to Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo-Loyzaga. Secretary Loyzaga has directed the MGB to focus on enabling mineral exploration next year through partnerships with foreign investors. The effort will be augmented by technical support coming from foreign development partners, including the Australian government and US Geological Survey. DENR remains focused on pursuing a policy that assigns greater weight to “the avoidance of all possible environmental impact.

 

Meanwhile, nickel miners pushes for streamlining of permitting process to increase annual shipments to over 40mn MT annually according to Philippine Nickel Industry Association President Dante Bravo. In 2022, the country’s nickel ore shipments declined by 11% YoY to 29.27mn DMT. While the government is focused on attracting investment in value-added processing, industry development efforts should include exploration and extraction.

 

BIR collections rise 38% YoY in July

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collected P273bn in July, up 38% YoY. They exceeded target collection of P259.91bn for the month. This brings 7M23 collections up 12% YoY to P1.492tn and accounts for 56.5% of the agency’s FY23 target of P2.639tn (+13% YoY).

Bureau of Treasury issues IRR for Maharlika Investment Fund

The Bureau of Treasury issued the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) Act of 2023 on Aug 28. The IRR shall take effect on Sep 12. The Maharlika Investment Corporation (MIC) will have an authorized capital stock of P500bn, of which P125bn will be subscribed at the time of incorporation. The National government will contribute P50bn to the initial capitalization and this will be sourced from (a) 100% of dividends of the BSP for the first and second fiscal years, (b) 10% share from the income of PAGCOR for a period of five years, (c) 10% of revenues from gaming operations of other state-owned gaming operators/regulators, privatization proceeds, and other sources.

 

Other GFIs and government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs) may invest into the MIF as well, subject to their respective investment and risk management strategies. Those providing social security and public health insurance services, however, are absolutely prohibited from investing in the MIF. The MIC’s board of directors will be composed of

Secretary of Finance as ex officio chairperson, President and chief executive officer (PCEO) of the MIC as vice chairperson, President and CEO of the LBP, President and CEO of the DBP and two regular directors with three independent directors from the private sector.

DoTr courts Japanese firms to bid for PPP projects

Transportation Secretary Jaime Bautista is “courting” Japanese companies to bid for PPP projects in railway and aviation sectors. The projects mentioned were the P76.89bn Metro Manila Subway Project, which will involve a 15-year O&M-type of concession, will be auctioned in 4Q23-1Q24, the P204.6bn North-South Commuter Rail, which will involve a 15-year O&M-type of concession, will be auctioned in 4Q23-1Q24 and the P107.6bn NAIA airport.

 

Budget deficit narrows in July; YT-July deficit down 21% YoY to P600bn

The government reported a budget deficit of P47.8bn in July, down 45% YoY. Revenues jumped 33% YoY, largely due to timing changes in VAT payments. The customs collections fell 13% YoY likely due to base effects from high commodity prices and weather disruptions. Expenditures grew 16% YoY, accelerating from anemic growth (+0.4% YoY) in 1H23. Interest payments rose at a faster rate of +22% YoY (vs. 10% YoY in 1H). Primary expenditures rebounded by 15% YoY after dipping 1% YoY in 1H23. The YT-July deficit was at P600bn, down 21% YoY and tracking slightly below the government’s target due to higher revenues.

Domestic liquidity growth slows to 5.9% in June

Preliminary data showed that domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 5.9% YoY to about P16.4tn in June 2023. This is slower than the +6.6% YoY growth rate in May and nominal GDP growth of 11.3% in 1H23.
