REGIS REPORT

20 January 2023

The market went in consolidation mode after the recent run-up, trading in a tight range of 7,014 to 7,095 for the week. The market managed a gain of 1.5% WoW nonetheless to close at 7056.  Turnover declined 18% WoW to US$154.8m daily.

Sectors ended mixed throughout the week given the lack of market catalysts. Top index movers were conglomerates SM (+2.1% WoW) and GT Capital (2.2% WoW). GT Capital disclosed that the group is open to raising their stake in MPI (currently 15.6%) after reports of the Mitsui buy-in.

Rising this week were DMC (+3.93% WoW) and UBP (+5.4% WoW), which continued to see interest after released public float data proved supportive of an inclusion in the PSEi rebalancing in February.

Meanwhile,  Meralco (-3.1% WoW) and ACEN (-2.1% WoW) dragged the index, on speculation that the two would be removed from the MSCI index in the upcoming rebalancing.  A placement in ACEN by Mermac (335m shares at Php6.70) weighed on sentiment. The placed shares represent 0.8% of ACEN’s outstanding shares.

Property stocks saw a modest boost as both ALI (+2.0% WoW) and SMPH (+0.1% WoW) traded higher.

Foreigners were buyers with a net inflow of US$83m this week. Buying was met by locals taking profit after the index gained 9.2% from its low last December, muting overall gains. Top names bought by foreign investors were SMPH (US$8.7m), (MONDE US$7.6m), and MER (US$3.9m).

Philippine Banks - BSP raises credit card interest rate cap from 2% to 3%
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Could add 8-12% to our NPAT forecasts

According to a news report, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has approved the increase in the interest rate cap for credit card receivables from 2.0% to 3.0% per month, or from 24% to 36% pa. Without the official BSP statement, though, it is not yet clear how or when the hike will be implemented. Credit card receivables account for 4-8% of gross loans for banks we cover. Assuming full-year implementation, we estimate the change would add 8-12% to our profit forecasts, with Metrobank (MBT PM) benefiting the most (see table).

A bit of a surprise – details to follow

The Manila Bulletin reported* online on Friday 13 Jan that the BSP approved the hike in the credit card interest rate cap from 2.0% to 3.0% a month. However, no press release or circular has yet been posted on the BSP’s website as of this writing. It is thus unclear for now whether the change will be implemented in one go, or if it will be done gradually. To recall, the 2.0% limit was imposed in 2020 to give relief to consumers hard-hit by the Covid-19 pandemic. In November 2022, the BSP said it would keep the 2.0% ceiling, but that it would review it in January 2023. While the BSP was in favor of the hike, it was reportedly receiving pushback from consumer groups.

MBT to benefit the most, with credit cards accounting for 8% of loans

As of 3Q22, credit cards accounted for 4.1% to 7.8% of loans for banks we cover, with MBT having the highest proportion. Assuming 50% of credit card receivables are levied interest, and assuming full-year implementation, we estimate the rate hike will result in banks’ NIMs widening by 18-23bps, and profits increasing by 8-12% compared to our current 2023E forecasts.

 
Manila Water - East zone rebasing over; What’s next?
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 Klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Maintain Hold

Notwithstanding the sizeable “R” tariff adjustment given to MWC in the recently concluded rate rebasing, we estimate the final determination will only result in a modest improvement in the value of the East zone. Following other tweaks (including for the underperformance of its non[1]East zone assets and other items) we reduce our SOTP NAV-based TP by 6% to P18/sh. With the rate rebasing over, we believe management needs to find other avenues of growth outside the East zone to create more value for shareholders.

Rate rebasing completed

After a review of its business plan for the next 25 years, the regulator approved a chunky “R” tariff hike (+73-77%) for MWC, which will be implemented over a five-year period beginning January 1, 2023. We estimate MWC’s earnings could jump by 60% in 2023 alone but decline gradually over the medium term as investments accelerate further. It is worth noting that despite the sizeable “R”, the other more important determinants of the East zone’s value: Opening Cash Position (OCP), regulatory return, and committed expenditures hewed closely to our original assumptions.

Final concession agreement still hanging

Three years since the government first demanded the revision of the concession agreement/s, the new contract (“Revised Concession Agreement” (RCA)) has yet to be finalized. While the regulator used the last draft of the RCA as the framework for the rate rebasing, discussions are still ongoing on whether (a) the FCDA mechanism can be reinstated and (b) if the inflation index cap (2/3) can be removed. The FCDA mechanism is critical to MWC’s ability to enhance shareholder value by reducing the company’s cost of capital. The inflation-linked cap on annual tariff adjustments is less crucial, in our view. The return accruing to the concession (now fixed at 12%) is the more important driver.

Creating more value for shareholders remains elusive

We believe management can improve shareholder value by (a) expanding outside the East zone by acquiring large water concessions; (b) lowering its cost of capital; and (c) accelerating capex spend to improve its regulatory value within the East zone. Risks: regulation, water supply, acquisitions.

 
Philippine Strategy - Hopping into 2023
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Strong recovery momentum, on attractive valuations

Economic indicators, and earnings surged in 3Q22, with momentum carrying into 4Q22. Demand should get further boosts in 2023E from personal income tax cuts, and from moderating inflation. Our long-term structural concerns over the twin fiscal and CA deficits remain, though falling global commodity prices should provide near-term relief. Meanwhile, market valuations are reasonable, at 12.7x PER, on our FY2023 earnings, well below the long-run average of 15.8x. Foreign positioning remains light, with investors just starting to turn net buyers. We are Overweight on the banking, consumer, and power sectors all on continued demand and widening margins.

Post-recovery demand momentum to carry into 2023E

Consumer demand surged in 9M22, as the economy reopened. While inflation spiked, sentiment was buoyed by a) all-time low unemployment; b) minimum wage hikes in June/July; and c) growth in OFW remittances, especially in PHP terms. Bottom-up indicators – electricity sales, auto sales, loan growth – show sustained growth into 4Q22. For 2023, demand will get a further boost from moderating inflation, and a cut in personal income tax rates. The current administration’s renewed push for PPP-funded infrastructure projects should help support growth as the government consolidates its fiscal position.

Peak inflation, interest rates – good for demand, corporate margins

Given the Philippines’s import dependence, the reversal in commodity prices is a welcome development. Domestic inflation has yet to peak, due to delays in agriculture imports. However, financial markets have already discounted peak CPI, with the local 10-year Treasury down more than 100bps from the highs in 4Q22. Lower input costs will also mean wider margins for consumer companies.

Current Account remains a key concern for the PHP

The PHP fell as much as 14% against the USD in 2022, before recovering in 4Q22. The country’s Current Account Deficit (CAD) ballooned to 6.1% of GDP in 9M22. Easing import prices and the “weak USD” narrative will provide near-term relief to the PHP. However, a persistent CAD could see the PHP under-perform its peers, and will keep the economy vulnerable to future external shocks.

Strong earnings growth, cheap valuations, and an un-crowded trade

We estimate net profits for stocks under Regis coverage to grow by 19% YoY in 2023E, following a 39% jump in 2022E. Meanwhile, valuations on the PSEi Index are quite reasonable, with PER down to 12.7x on our 2022E forecasts, well below the long-term average of 15.8x. Meanwhile, foreign ownership remains close to all-time lows. We are bullish on the market’s prospects in 2023E, and would Overweight the banking, consumer, and power sectors. The reopening of China’s economy would benefit the gaming sector.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

GT Capital interested in raising stake in Metro Pacific

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) is “open to discuss (opportunities)”, according to chairman Alfred Ty. Mr. Ty was asked if GT Capital was keen on expanding its 15.6% stake in Metro Pacific (MPI PM). There is nothing definite yet although MPIC has always been in talks with interested investors. As at end-Sep 2022, GTCAP had P14.2bn in cash at the parent and P93bn in gross debt (including prefs). GTCAP receives c. P8-9bn of dividend income annually, sufficient to cover debt service (c. P5bn).

Upson International to conduct IPO in Feb

Upson International will start its initial share sale in February after delaying it last year. Upson plans to raise as much as P4.3bn (US$78mn). This assumes P5.50/sh, the high-end of price range. The offer period is from Feb 22 to 28.

SEC approves share-for-asset swap between ALI and AC/Mermac

Ayala Land issued 312mn new ALI shares (at P55.80/share) in exchange for property assets of its parent, Ayala Corp, and Mermac (which owns 48% of AC). This will increase Ayala Land’s shares outstanding by 2%. This transaction was announced in January 2022 but was only approved by the SEC recently. It is already in Regis forecasts/estimates.

 

The property assets are:

AC’s 50% ownership in Ayala Hotels – this owns the ALP project Park Central Towers and the land on which Manila Peninsula is built

633 hectares of agricultural land in Darong, Sta. Cruz, Davao del Sur

AC and Mermac’s office units and parking slots

AC’s Honda Pasig Property – which will be converted into an Avida residential project

AC’s Caluan Property – which will be turned into a BellaVita (socialized housing) project

Makati land parcel sold at P1.12mn/sqm

Chemical Industries of the Philippines sold its 1,009 sqm property along Arnaiz Avenue, Makati for P1.13bn. Regis assigns P1.5mn/sqm on Ayala Land’s Makati CBD land bank, which is located in more central areas than the land parcel that was recently sold. Regis believes this transaction implies that land prices are comparable (or slightly higher) than pre-COVID levels.

Philippine market foreign ownership drops in December

Regis estimates that market foreign ownership fell 6bps MoM to 21.0% while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 29bps MoM to 25.0% (in December). If the BDO foreign ownership had remained unchanged MoM, then market and PSEi foreign ownership would have been 21.2% and 25.2% respectively. Market net foreign selling reached US$109mn in December (from US$99mn of net foreign buying in November)

18 PSEi constituents saw declines in foreign ownership while 11 experienced increases.

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

BDO – saw the biggest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 3.77 percentage points MoM to 20.7%. However, this appears inconsistent with trading data, which show that BDO was the top foreign bought stock in November and December.  

GTCAP – foreign ownership fell 2.61 percentage points to 16.2%. The stock was deleted from the MSCI Index in late November but the reduction in foreign ownership was only reflected in the December data. 

MPI – foreign ownership fell 1.88 percentage points to 12.3%. The stock was deleted from the MSCI Index in late November but the drop in foreign ownership was only reflected in the December data.

AGI – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership, which rose 36 basis points to 17.8%.

 

DMC and UBP could enter PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing

Based on our estimates, DMC is highly likely to enter the PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing. Regis expects this to lead to the removal of MEG. Meanwhile, we consider the likelihood of UBP entering the PSE Index to be moderate. This should lead to the removal of RLC from the index. The PSEi rebalancing in February will be based on the 12 months from January 2022 to December 2022. Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap.

 

Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when

1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or

2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 

 

Based on our estimates, DMC and UBP have reached the top 25. In addition to DMC and UBP, we also expect relatively large inflow to SMC and EMI due to increases in their public float. In addition to the PSEi constituents to be removed, we also expect relatively large outflow from ALI due to the reduction in its public float. We estimate US$126mn in total flows (US$63mn in each direction)

SEC approves share-for-asset swap between AREIT and Ayala Land

AREIT will acquire 124,000sqm of Cebu offices from Ayala Land. This will expand AREIT’s rental portfolio by 23% to 673,000sqm. The offices have a combined occupancy rate of 96%. eBloc Towers 1 to 4 at Cebu IT Park, ACC Tower and Tech Tower at Ayala Center Cebu. The offices are valued at P11.257bn and AREIT’s AUM will grow 21% to P64bn. AREIT will issue 252.1mn AREIT shares to Ayala Land.

 

The AREIT shares would effectively be priced at P44.7/share and AREIT’s shares outstanding will expand 17%. AREIT said that the yield on the Cebu offices was 6%. This transaction was first announced in March 2022 and has been factored into our ALI estimates/forecasts. Regis estimates that ALI’s ownership in AREIT would rise from 60% to 66%. AREIT currently offers a dividend yield of 6.5% based on consensus forecast.

 

ICTSI appoints new CFO

Prior to latest appointment, Mr. Pascua was the VP of Global Mergers and Acquisitions. Joel Consing is retiring from his role, after accepting a position in the Office of the Presidential Adviser on Investment and Economic Affairs. Glencore to enter PH for mineral processing

Glencore is expanding into mineral processing in the Philippines according to Communications Secretary Cheloy Velicaria-Garafil. The Philippines is a potential partner to process nickel and copper resources responsibly and sustainably for use in electric vehicle batteries and energy storage units, among others. MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Gov’t to tap mining revenues for Maharlika Investment Fund

The government plans to harness the mining industry to help support the creation of a sovereign wealth fund according to Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. The Philippine mining industry contributed P102bn to the domestic economy in 2020. The USD1.34bn Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) is expected to finance big government projects such as power grids and dams.

Philippine GDP to grow 6.5% this year

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno expects Philippine GDP to grow by 6.5% this year. He said that 2022 GDP likely grew faster than the target of 6.5% to 7.5%.

Benchmark rate seen at 6.25%.

Fitch Group said the benchmark rate may likely settle at 6.25% this year as it sees the BSP raising rates by a total of 75 basis points in the coming policy meetings. “We expect inflation to drop below the BSP’s upper target limit of four percent only in the fourth quarter of 2023”. BSP Governonr Felipe Medalla has said a 25 or 50 bps rate increase is possible during the Monetary Board’s first policy meeting on Feb. 16. Fitch further expects Philippine GDP growth to slow this year to 5.9% from 7.4% in 2022.

Cash remittances up 5.7% YoY in November

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 5.7% YoY to US$2.64bn in November. The amount is the lowest in six months due to seasonality; Note that remittances grew by +5.1% YoY in November 2021. In PHP terms, remittances were up +21% YoY and the 3-month moving average growth rate accelerates to +4.3% YoY. This is faster YoY growth rates across regions for land-based and sea-based remittances. YT-November remittances are up 3.3% to US$29.38bn, trending behind the BSP’s 2022 target (+4%).

BOP posts US$612mn surplus in December 2022; FY22 BOP deficit at US$7.3bn

The country’s balance of payments posted a surplus of US$612mn in December 2022, down 38% YoY. This is a reversal of the US$756mn deficit in November 2022. The surplus reflected inflows arising mainly from the BSP’s net foreign exchange operations and net income from its investments abroad. GIR increased to US$96.1bn in end-Dec 2022 from US$95.1bn in end-Nov 2022; Latest GIR level represents 7.3 months of imports. The FY22 BOP deficit reached US$7.3bn, a reversal of the US$1.3bn surplus in 2021 due to a wider trade deficit. This is lower than the BSP’s latest estimate of US$11.2bn deficit but larger than its initial forecast of a mild US$0.7bn surplus at the start of the year. The BOP deficit is equivalent to 1.8% of GDP, assuming the BSP’s GDP forecast. For 2023, the BSP expects the BOP to remain at a deficit of US$5.4bn, equivalent to 1.3% of GDP.

Government cuts EV tariffs to zero

President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. has approved zero tariff on electric vehicles to encourage consumers to use “cleaner and greener” cars. The Executive Order No. 12 removes the tariff for five years but after five years, electric vehicles will be slapped a 30% tariff.
