REGIS REPORT

07 December 2023

The market ended just slightly lower for the week at 6,234.77 (-0.2% WoW) despite the better-than-expected inflation print for the month of November.

Headline Inflation decelerated to 4.1% in November from 4.9% in October as a result of slower increases in food (ex-rice) and lower petrol prices. The November CPI came in better than consensus forecast of 4.3% and near the low end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.0%-4.8%. The deceleration of food and oil price inflation more than compensated for the uptick in rice prices. Despite the administration’s best predictions (and hopes) about the harvest season, rice price inflation was actually higher in November compared to the previous month (15.8% vs. 13.2%). Regional rice prices have stayed high and have crept up in recent weeks.

These figures, however, were disregarded by the market. Trading remained directionless with gainers barely outnumbering losers at 17:13.

Daily turnover likewise dropped to US$77.3m (-26.8% WoW) in light of the shortened trading week.

ICT (+4.2% WoW) was the top outperformer as foreign demand lifted the stock. ICT saw US$13.5m worth of inflows for the week.

Property names ended mixed. Ayala Land (+1.3% WoW) managed to close in the green although SMPH (-1.1% WoW) corrected after its strong performance recently. Generally though, the sector may potentially see a boost as the government is allowing the accreditation of a number of economic zones within Metro Manila to proceed, even after the Duterte period order issued stopping such. Under the new government ruling, PEZA applications that were already in process but were halted because of the Duterte order will now be allowed to re-submit their applications. Based on Regis estimates, 5% of Ayala Land’s (ALI PM) and 10% of Filinvest Land’s (FLI PM) office portfolio will benefit from this change. However, the direct ban on POGOs is still a key risk to the sector. POGOs occupy ~800,000sqm of office space, equivalent to 5% of total office stock.

Banks were the biggest drags. Large cap banking names BDO (-3.1% WoW) and BPI (-2.5% WoW) fell sharply as foreigners took profits especially as the sector outperformed the market throughout the year. Year-to-date, BDO and BPI are still up 20.9% and 8.9% respectively. Meanwhile, Metrobank (+3.0% WoW) is playing catch up after lagging its peers. Foreign investors are finally seeing interest as the stock is now trading at 0.6x P/B.

On the power sector, Meralco (+1.5% WoW) announced it has paid an initial P7bn to SPNEC (SPNEC PM) as down payment for the subscription of new SPNEC shares. After closing, MER will have 31.4% economic stake and 50.5% voting interest in SPNEC. The parties do not expect that MER will need to undertake a tender offer. ACEN (-4.4% WoW) though saw heavy selling pressure after announcing that they are partnering with a subsidiary of US-based BrightNight LLC for renewable energy projects in the Philippines

Headline inflows registered at US$1.3m.

RESEARCH
Ayala Land - AREIT to increase AUM and shares outstanding by 35%
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

AREIT to acquire assets in a series of transactions

AREIT announced a series of transactions wherein AREIT will acquire properties from related parties Ayala Land and ACEN. There are three main transactions: 1) AREIT will purchase a hotel from Ayala Land for P1.2bn in cash; 2) AREIT will acquire P21.8bn worth of office, mall, and hotel assets by issuing 642mn shares to Ayala Land; and 3) AREIT will acquire P6.8bn worth of industrial land by issuing 199mn shares to ACEN.

AREIT AUM and shares outstanding to expand by 34%-35%

After all of these transactions are consummated (by end-2024), AREIT’s AUM will increase by 34% (to P117bn) and its shares outstanding will expand by 35% (to 3.2bn). This would imply a market cap of P97bn 

Ayala Land to sell 280mn AREIT shares

In order to meet minimum public ownership requirements, Ayala Land will have to sell 280mn AREIT shares, equivalent to P8.6bn (based on the last closing price). These shares will have to be sold prior to getting SEC approval for the transactions, which AREIT expects by 2H24.

Proportion of AUM from industrial land to rise, share of office to decline

After the acquisition of assets from Ayala Land and ACEN, the proportion of AUM from Makati will remain similar (at 61%) but the share of office property will decline. The proportion of AUM from offices will drop from 88% to 76% while the share of industrial land will rise from 1% to 7% (see Figures 1 to 3).

Acquisitions will likely be neutral (mildly accretive) for both ALI and AREIT

Our estimates suggest that the acquisitions will be neutral (mildly accretive) for both Ayala Land and AREIT, thanks to tax advantages that REITs enjoy. AREIT appears to be valuing Ayala Land’s assets slightly higher than our estimates.

However, since they only comprise 3% of ALI’s NAV, the end result will likely be neutral (mildly accretive) for Ayala Land. For AREIT, the property acquisitions are also likely to be DPS neutral (mildly accretive) in the near-term. This means consensus FY24E DPS of P2.27/share is likely to be marginally enhanced on the first year of the acquisition.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Meralco subscribes to new shares of SPNEC

Meralco subsidiary MGen Renewable Energy Inc. (MGreen) has signed a subscription agreement to acquire 15.7bn of solar power developer SPNEC’s (SPNEC PM) common shares and 19.4bn preferred shares. The agreement is in line with a previous announcement of Meralco taking over control of SPNEC. MGreen paid an initial amount of P7bn on Nov 30 and will pay another P8.9bn on the closing date. The closing date is likely to at least mid-2024, if not later. Upon closing, MGreen will have a total voting interest of 50.5% in SPNEC. Meanwhile, the 15.7bn common shares is equivalent to a 31.4% economic stake in SPNEC’s enlarged outstanding shares. The parties do not expect that a tender offer will be necessary.

Telco tower firm allots P3.7bn for PHL expansion

ISOC edotco Towers Inc. is allocating P3.7bn for its network expansion in the country. In total, the company had acquired close to 2,000 towers in 2022 and another 700 towers this year. The EDOTCO Group owns and operates about 54,000 towers across Malaysia, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Philippines, Myanmar and Laos.

PLDT CEO Panlilio retires; Pangilinan re-assumes CEO post

PLDT CEO Alfredo Panlilio is retiring effective end of 2023 for health reasons; Panlilio is 59. He would have been PLDT CEO for less than three years. PLDT chairman Manuel Pangilinan will assume the role of interim CEO until permanent replacement is selected. Pangilinan was CEO for a lengthy period before Panlilio was appointed to his role.

ACEN partners with US firm for projects

ACEN disclosed that it approved a proposed partnership with BrightNight APAC B.V. to establish a local renewable energy (RE) platform. The company did not disclose details of the RE platform it seeks to establish. BrightNight APAC is wholly owned by BrightNight LLC. It has developed a renewable power portfolio of 2.7GW across the Philippines, India, Australia and Bangladesh. 1GW is under development in the Philippines. In March, ACEN signed a partnership with BrightNight to develop projects in India. ACEN currently has 4.4GW of attributable capacity and a medium-term pipeline of 18GW largely in Australia and the Philippines.

Former regulator says Meralco should refund P150bn

Former commissioner of the ERC says Meralco ought to refund as much as P150bn. Alfredo Non, previously an ERC commissioner, says the regulatory return (WACC) of 14.97% should have been cut in a review that was never completed. A lower WACC would have led to a lower rate in the review for rates in 2015, as low as P1.05 (vs. approved tariff of P1.35/kwh over the period). The ERC skipped its rate review for all distribution utilities, including Meralco. The commission either had no quorum or were reluctant to implement any tariff changes under the previous government. Alfredo Non was with the ERC until 2018. Regis believes that Meralco previously set aside as much as P80bn in provisions for this issue, bulk of which has yet to be reversed.

Manila Water terminates Cebu bulk water contract

Manila Water terminated its 30-year bulk water supply contract with the Provincial Government of Cebu (PGC). MWC owns a 56% stake in the project, which was envisioned to provide a minimum of 35MLD to the Metro Cebu Water District. PGC owns 44% of the project. The disclosure did not cite a reason for the termination. Regis estimates the project accounts for <1% of profit and NAV. As a background, MWC completed the bulk water facility in 2015 but due to various issues (i.e. change in water quality/higher costs), the company was not able to deliver the entire 35 MLD capacity. In 2019, PGC issued notices of breach/default and termination, but the dispute was settled in 2022, with MWC proposing to build a 250 MLD dam. Regis suspects the agreement was terminated to allow MWC to focus on the proposed dam

Update on PH Resorts MOU with AppleOne Properties

PH Resorts Group disclosed to the exchange that since the initial memorandum of understanding (MOU) signed by PHR’s subsidiaries with AppleOne Properties for the potential investment of AppleOne in at a majority interest or an asset purchase, no definitive agreement has been entered into. The MOU was signed by the parties in October 3, 2023. “Nevertheless, considering that the MOU was a non-exclusive engagement, PHR is having substantive discussions with other parties.” PHR previously agreed to sell Emerald Bay and its casino license to Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) but the transaction fell through.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government debt rises to P14.48tn in October

The national government’s debt stock reached P14.48tn in October, up 1.49% MoM. The MoM increase due to net issuances of domestic and external loans. Debt stock also 7.9% higher than in end-2022. The government is targeting the debt stock to reach P14.62tn by year-end, equivalent to a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61%. By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028.

Inflation eases further to 4.1% in November

Headline CPI rose at a slower rate of 4.1% YoY in November from 4.9% in October. This is below consensus estimate (4.3%) and near the low-end of the BSP’s forecast range (4.0%-4.8%). The softening was due to slower increases in food (e.g. vegetables, meat, fish, sugar) and oil prices. This was offset by an acceleration in rice inflation (15.8% in November from 13.2% in October). On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.2%, accelerating from -0.2% in October due largely to higher rice prices. Non-food items decelerated further to +0.05% from +0.20% in October.

 

Meanwhile, core CPI also eased to 4.7% from 5.3%, with core non-food item portion also rising at a slower rate of 4.2% (vs. 4.6% in Oct). The average headline inflation rate in 11M23 at 6.2%. The Monetary Board meeting is on Dec 14 (consensus: no change). BSP inflation comment: Monetary Board deems it necessary to keep monetary policy settings sufficiently tight until a sustained downward trend in inflation becomes evident. Accordingly, the BSP will continue to monitor inflation expectations and second-round effects and take appropriate action as needed to bring inflation back to the target, in keeping with the BSP’s price stability mandate.

Government to allow approvals of new Metro Manila ecozones

The Marcos administration amended provisions of the previous administration’s Administrative Order (AO) 18, which halted the processing of new ecozones in Metro Manila. The new order states that those who obtained “Pre-qualification clearances” before AO 18 was implemented in 2019 will be allowed to resubmit their application with certain conditions. In line with this, PEZA will submit a list of applicants with the said clearances to the Office of the President. Regis estimates that nearly 5% of Ayala Land’s office stock and 10% of Filinvest Land’s office portfolio could be affected by the revisions. BPO firms still have a preference for PEZA-accredited space.

 

Regis estimates that about half of the 2.5mn sqm of vacant Metro Manila office space is PEZA-accredited. Regis also estimates there is sufficient PEZA-accredited office space for about three years of take-up from BPO firms. Thus, Regis believes the revision to AO 18 is unlikely to have a near-term impact on the office market.

Customs collections dip in November

Preliminary data showed the Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P75.3bn in November, down 0.5% YoY and 3.3% MoM and surpassed its target of P74.25bn for the month. Customs Commissioner attributed the good performance to better efficiency in collections, citing that “volumes did not increase”. This brought YT-November collections to P813.65bn, up 3.1% YoY. Which is on track to beat its FY23 target of P874bn (+1.4% YoY). The BOC collections account for 23% of government’s revenues.

DOF and NEDA back POGO ban but PAGCOR advocates “proper regulation”

During the hearing of the House Committee on Games and Amusements, National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) Assistant Secretary Sarah Lynne Daway-Ducanes said that the POGO sector accounts for 0.3% of GDP but the Philippines could lose 0.8% to 1% of GDP if China blacklists the Philippines’ tourist sites. Thus, POGOs could have a net negative impact on the Philippines. Meanwhile, the Department of Finance (DOF) cited “social costs” and “reputational risks” which could curtail the country’s efforts to attract FDI.

 

On the other hand, the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, said that shutting down offshore gaming operations was not the appropriate response. It believes that with proper regulation and intensive monitoring, illegal offshore gaming operations as well as their social ills can be eliminated. The POGO sector occupies about 800,000sqm of Metro Manila office space, lower than 1.4mn sqm as of end-2019. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of 3Q23. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

NEDA board to approve extension of reduced tariffs on agricultural products

A proposed executive order (EO) to extend the reduced tariffs on essential commodities like rice, corn, pork and coal until 2024 will be up for approval next week. The current EO 10 is valid until year-end

Listed below are tariffs for each commodity:

Pork – 15% (in-quota) - 25% (out-quota)

Rice – 35%

Corn – 5% (in-quota) – 15% (out-quota)

Coal - zero tariff
