REGIS REPORT

31 March 2023

The market dropped substantially (-1.6% WoW) for the week, closing just slightly below technical support at 6,499.68.  

Trading was volatile due to the lack of liquidity. Headline turnover for the week registered at US$117.8m. However, excluding blocks, volumes were actually lighter, at just US$70.0m daily. Block sales that flowed through were the AREIT (-4.0% WoW)  placement US$60.7m and the Cemex (+6.8% WoW) tender offer at US$38.6m last Wednesday.

Outside of these, earnings results drove the market.

The biggest index drag was MONDE (-13.5% WoW), falling on disappointing results (core profit -20% to Php6.6bn). The massive write-down Monde undertook for its alternative meats business meanwhile resulted in an equity restructuring involving the reclassification of additional paid in capital to retained earnings. This spurred a sell off, with the stock dropping 11.3% on Friday alone. Foreign investors heavily sold the name for US$3.7m this week.

MPI (+1.4% WoW) announced it is taking a 16% interest in SPNEC (+3.5% WoW). The initial tranche will be acquired by MPI for P2.0bn (1.6bn shares at P1.25/share) from Solar Philippines, its controlling shareholder. The latter is expected to advance funds to SPNEC, for SPNEC to be able to pursue its projects.

Cebu Air (+6.8% WoW) meanwhile outperformed despite reporting a loss for 4Q22. Management is positive on the prospects of recovery in 2023 given the current trajectory of their operations.

Headline foreign inflows for the week stood at US$49.6m. Excluding the blocks, the market saw net outflows worth US$2.9m. Biggest outflows were in TEL, GLO, and ACEN for US$5.2m, US$4.9m, US$3.8m respectively. Only banks remained foreign bought, with BDO and BPI seeing strong inflows at US$8.9m and $5.6m.

Monde Nissin - 4Q22: Margins stabilize as input costs peak
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

FY22 core net profit (-20% YoY to P6.6bn) behind as margins fall short

MONDE’s core net profit for FY22 fell 20% YoY to P6.6bn, behind Regis and consensus expectations, while EBITDA was also behind at P11.2bn (-16% YoY). The core profit figure excludes a P20bn impairment charge arising from restructuring efforts on its Meat Alternative business. FY22 net sales of P73.9bn (+7% YoY) were within forecasts, but margins came up short. For 4Q22 alone, core net profit declined 10% YoY due mainly to forex losses; EBITDA grew 16% YoY as proportionately lower SG&A expenses helped cushion input cost pressures across both business segments.

APAC BFB: Instant noodles recover from 3Q-lows; GPM stabilizes as input costs peak

Comparable 4Q22 sales for the APAC BFB segment (see Figure 2) grew 15% YoY during the quarter as instant noodle volumes bounced back from the 3Q lows. MONDE’s market share (Figure 3) settled at ~63% by year-end, but continues to improve as of Feb 2023 (67%). Segment gross margin stabilized at 29.9% in 4Q22 (vs estimated 29-30% comparable for 3Q22) even as MONDE’s internal cost of key raw materials (e.g. wheat and palm oil) peaked. Proportionately lower A&P expenses helped cushion EBITDA margins both QoQ and YoY.

Quorn: EBITDA margin improves due to restructuring efforts

Quorn sales were flat YoY in 4Q22 as strong foodservice/QSR sales (+29% YoY) offset weak retail sales (-3% YoY). Similarly, higher input cost and lower production volumes led to a sharp contraction in gross margin (>500bps YoY / 300bps QoQ). Despite this, EBITDA rose sharply due to recent restructuring efforts.

Margin recovery imminent

We believe margins should start to recover in 1Q23 as (1) instant noodle volumes continue their rebound and (2) the average cost of wheat and palm oil inputs comes down. After holding back on price action in 4Q22, MONDE implemented a ~6% average price increase in 1Q23, which should help margins further in the coming months. A full return to ‘normal’ may not be felt until 2H23 as the company is still carrying higher cost inventory for wheat (until 1Q23) and palm oil (until 2Q23) relative to current market prices.

 
Cebu Air - EBITDA sharply recovers in 4Q22
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

4Q22 “core” net loss shrinks 59% QoQ to P1.2bn

The re-opening of North Asian markets, as well as favorable fuel and currency movements led to a sharp improvement in 4Q22 EBITDA to P2.87bn (+19x QoQ), reverting to 32% of 4Q19. We estimate CEB’s core net loss shrank 59% QoQ to P1.23bn in 4Q, leading to a 51% YoY reduction in FY22 core losses to P12.9bn. Earnings disappointed due to a slower-than-expected revenue recovery and higher than expected costs. Despite the shortfall, we look forward to 2023 as travel normalizes and last year’s macroeconomic headwinds fade.

Pax volumes revert to 79% of pre-Covid in 4Q; Domestic market share drops

System-wide 4Q22 pax volumes rose 6% QoQ to 4.41mn, reverting to 79% of pre[1]Covid levels after North Asian markets re-opened. International pax volumes jumped 76% QoQ to about 10% of pre-Covid levels. However, competition in the domestic market intensified further, as other airlines played catch up in re[1]deploying capacity, flying largely domestic routes whilst international markets were slowly re-opening. As a result, CEB’s market share dropped to 53% in 4Q after peaking at 60% in 2Q.

Opex dropped 2% QoQ; EBITDA margin at 11.9%

Operating expenses fell 2% QoQ, as fuel costs dropped 7% QoQ. Cost (ex-fuel) per available seat km (ASK) fell 11% QoQ to P2.1, heading closer to pre-pandemic levels (P1.6) as 4Q22 ASKs reverted to 78% of pre-Covid levels. EBITDA margin rose to 11.9%, still quite far from 2019 levels of 33%.

2023 outlook: business normalizing back to pre-Covid

Management expects to gain back the market share it lost in 4Q, as airlines re[1]deploy capacity from domestic to international markets, which should lead to a normalization of domestic load factors. With systemwide seat capacity expected to be 10-15% above pre-Covid, management expects pax volumes to return to 2019 levels and for the company to turn a profit as early as 1Q23.

 
D&L Industries - 4Q22: margin improvement masks volume decline
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

FY22 profit ahead of Regis, consensus

DNL’s 4Q22 amounted to P777mn, down 15%QoQ but up 62%YoY. Despite generally softer volumes for food and oleochemicals in the quarter, better margins lifted gross profit as input costs dropped. DNL booked a non-operating loss of P58mn (mostly stemming from unrealized fx loss on receivables), reversing the P181mn gain in 3Q22 and weighing on the sequential performance. The result nevertheless brings FY22 income to P3.318bn (26%YoY), ahead of both Regis and consensus for the full year.

Food segment jumps on GPM improvement

Income from the food segment improved to P290mn in 4Q22 (63%QoQ, 80%YoY) on the back of much higher margins. We estimate that overall GP margin increased to c.11% in 4Q22 from an average of 8.1% in 9M22 as commodity inputs fell. Although just off the lows, the rebound marks the first notable improvement since 2021. The improvement, however, masked the weaker volumes in both high margin specialties (>-10%YoY) and commodity (>-20%YoY) segments. The volume decline is particularly noteworthy amidst generally loose restrictions for hospitality and restaurant industries. Management notes some lingering impact from the pandemic (simpler menu offerings, simpler commodity orders), but inflation may have played a bigger role in the demand decline.

Demand for high margin oleochemicals drops in 4Q

On the flip side, profits from oleochem dropped 31%QoQ to P280mn in 4Q22. Coming off a very good 3Q22 base, positive volume trends for high margin products (surfactants, foaming agents, laurin, exports) reversed from +16%YoY in 9M22 to an estimated -20%YoY in 4Q22. Commodities (largely biodiesel) nevertheless continued to grow (+80%YoY) in 4Q22, extending gains for the year.

Palm, coconut oil prices stable, down >60% from peak

Palm oil and coconut oil prices have somewhat stabilized after falling c.60% from their peak levels. Softer input prices may allow for better margins especially for the high margin business.

 
Filinvest Land - Office vacancy stays flat at 32% in 4Q22 but is poised to climb this year
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Watch out for office vacancy and debt refinancing

Filinvest Land’s FY22 headline profit fell 24% to P2.9bn. Stripping off non[1]recurring gains from 2021, we estimate that recurring profit rose 5% (to P2.9bn). While this is ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, we have a couple of concerns: 1) office vacancy of 32% is poised to climb even higher and 2) 31% of FLI’s debt will mature this year and will likely be refinanced at much higher interest rates.

Office vacancy at 32% and will likely rise further

Office vacancy was flat QoQ at 32%, much higher than industry average of 18%. While vacancy of Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) was a respectable 13%, the non[1]REIT portfolio’s vacancy was about 60%. FLI expects its office vacancy to rise to 43% this year. While it is looking to sign new leases on its existing buildings, it is scheduled to expand its portfolio by 35% (to 710,000sqm) and the new buildings have very few preleased tenants.

Interest cost likely to rise; looking to monetize assets

Filinvest has P23bn of debt maturing in 2023. We estimate that this debt has an average interest cost of c. 4.5%, lower than the current 5-year government bond yield of 6.0%. Thus, a 2ppt increase in average interest rate on the refinancing would be possible. We consider this a cause for concern since FY22 net debt/EBITDA was about 9X. In order to trim its debt burden, the company plans to monetize land assets, including commercial/industrial lots in Cebu and Laguna.

Targeting 22% growth in reservation sales

Filinvest is targeting to grow its FY23 reservation sales by 22% (to P22bn). However, we refrain from being too positive since payment schemes are still much looser than in 2019.

 
GT Capital - Auto margins begin recovery in 4Q22
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

4Q22 core profit up 38% QoQ, 56% YoY to P4.55bn

Headline profit jumped 67% YoY to P18.36bn. Removing the one-time P3.8bn gain at the property unit, other one-offs, and pref dividends, core profit to common came in above our forecast (P14.4bn) but was behind consensus estimates (P16.5bn). In 4Q22 alone, core profit improved 38% QoQ and 56% YoY to P4.55bn, buoyed by (a) higher NIMs and faster loan growth at the bank (see Metrobank results comment), (b) margin improvements at the auto unit, and (c) profit recovery of the hotel segment.

Auto unit recovers sequentially

Favorable currency movements and price hikes led to a 50% QoQ jump in profit for the auto segment. Toyota implemented selective price hikes throughout 2022, which cumulatively amounted to +6%. Inventory levels dropped below one month’s sales by 4Q, from the normal 1.5 months, amidst buoyant demand and persistent supply issues. For 2023, Toyota is targeting unit sales to rise by 8% YoY to 188k units, suggesting it is willing to give up some of the outsized market share it gained during the pandemic. Assuming a more stable currency and the unwinding of supply bottlenecks in 2H, as well as the impact of price adjustments, management is optimistic about a recovery for the auto unit.

Property: Higher equity earnings from hotel JVs lift 4Q

Despite softness in booked revenues in 4Q, the property segment’s profit still improved by 337% YoY, lifted by higher equitized earnings from its hotel JVs. Management cited sales cancellations in the Manila Bay area as the main reason for weaker booked revenues in 4Q and FY22. Reservation sales were up 78% YoY to P4.8bn in 4Q, approximately 79% of 2019 average.

Parent cash at record level of P21 in end-2022 GTCAP ended 2022 with a record cash level of P21bn, with its net debt down to P72bn (from P85bn in 2021) and net debt to equity at 60% (from 79%). It has earmarked P13.6bn for organic expansionary capex (c. 30%) and acquisitions.

 
First Gen - 4Q22 recovers on improved gas supply, higher prices for EDC
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q22 +8%QoQ, 80%YoY to $68.8m

FGEN’s 4Q22 core income (after preferred dividends) increased 8%QoQ to $68.8mn, buoyed by much better performances from nat gas units and EDC. Gas restrictions and derating were considerably less, while EDC saw improved revenues on the back of higher prices of contracts and WESM. The result brings FY22 core income to $256 (5.3%YoY), ahead of both Regis and consensus estimates.

Nat gas recovers on improved gas availability

Earnings from natural gas plants jumped to an estimated $57mn (44%QoQ, 46%YoY) in 4Q22. FGEN cited substantial improvements in gas supply from Malampaya (recall Ilijan’s gas supply contract with Malampaya expired in June 2022) compared to the previous quarter when gas restrictions led to derating. Profits from natural gas plants thus reached $190m (-4%) for FY22 despite the very weak performance in 9M22. Inadequate gas supply (and the subsequent derating of San Gabriel/Avion) previously led to lower capacity charges and forced FGEN to buy replacement power.

EDC sustains upward trend on higher WESM sales, repriced contracts

Attributable income from EDC grew another 11% to $32mn in 4Q22 on the upward trend of tariffs from repriced contracts and WESM sales. The 4Q print was also substantially higher than last year when a number of EDC’s plants were offline and maintenance-related expenses depressed EDC’s income. The 4Q22 result brings EDC’s FY22 to $97mn, higher by 18%YoY.

No LNG supply contracts yet; commercial ops slated for 3Q23

FGEN is still in the process of negotiating two LNG supply contracts with short[1]listed suppliers. Commercial operations of the LNG terminal and regas facility is not expected until 3Q23, which means gas supply will still largely come from Malampaya ($11/mmbtu) or liquid fuel imports ($20/mmbtu). The price at which LNG supply contracts can be signed will greatly influence where power prices go for a good part of the grid.

 
Metro Pacific - Acquiring solar farm developer SPNEC
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Agrees to buy 16% of SPNEC, with option to raise stake to 43%

Metro Pacific announced today that it has entered into an agreement to acquire 1.6bn shares (16%) of SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM) from Solar Philippines Power Projects (Solar PH), a company beneficially owned by SPNEC’s founder and current President and CEO, Mr. Leandro Antonio Leviste. Currently, SPNEC is 57%-owned by Mr. Leviste while the balance is owned by the public. The completion of the transaction is subject to conditions precedent, including (a) regulatory approvals and (b) the execution of a separate agreement which will grant MPI the option to increase its stake in SPNEC to 43% once SPNEC increases its authorized capital stock to P50bn from P10bn through an asset[1]for-share swap. The swap will involve the infusion of Solar PH’s assets into SPNEC. With the exercise of the option, MPI could own up to 19bn of the expanded shares of SPNEC, equivalent to a 43% stake.

First tranche valued P2.0bn (P1.25/sh)

MPI is buying the initial stake for P1.25/sh or P2.0bn in total. The 1.6bn shares is equivalent to a 16% stake prior to the capital changes planned. The purchase price is 26% lower than SPNEC’s closing price on March 27 and is also lower than its rights issue price of P1.50/sh in August 2022. It is, however, higher than the IPO price (P1/sh) of SPNEC in 2021. While conditions precedent to closing for the initial tranche are being gathered, Solar PH will be advancing the P2.0bn proceeds to SPNEC, which the latter will use to fund its land acquisitions in Nueva Ecija.

Mandatory tender offer likely

Mr. Manuel Pangilinan will become Chairman of the Board of SPNEC as soon as the 16% stake is acquired. Given the change in control, MPI will likely be required to conduct a tender offer once it decides to call on its option to increase its stake to 43%. Note that under the rules, a company/group intending to acquire 35% of a public company within a 12-month period is required to conduct a tender offer.

Capital requirements for SPNEC/Solar PH project pipeline are massive

Once MPI acquires 43% of SPNEC, it will be the single largest shareholder of SPNEC, a company that could potentially own over 8,000 MW of solar farms. We understand that only a tiny portion (160MW) of Solar PH/SPNEC’s projects is operating today. This likely means MPI will have to pony up more capital to fund these projects, on top of its investment in SPNEC.

Ayala Land sells 205mn AREIT shares

Ayala Land sold 205mn AREIT shares at P32.10/share, a 6% discount to the previous close. This is to comply with the minimum public ownership requirement. The planned P22.5bn share-for-property swap between AREIT and ALI would have resulted in a breach of that requirement (if Ayala Land will not sell AREIT shares). AREIT will issue 607.6mn AREIT shares (at P37/share) to Ayala Land  in exchange for 190,000sqm of malls and offices. The share-for-property swap and the recent sale of shares will have minimal impact on our Ayala Land estimates. AREIT currently offers FY23E dividend yield of 6.9% (based on consensus forecast). This is higher than the current 10-year government bond yield of 6.3%.

Meralco secures 1-year power supply agreement with SPPC

Meralco said the emergency supply deal for 300MW will start from March 26, 2023 until March 25, 2024. The utility has secured a certificate of exemption from the DOE from the competitive selection process. The EPSA has a two-part tariff composed of a P1.75/kWh fixed cost and variable costed indexed on fuel price movements. The agreement partially replaces the 670MW lost from original contract with SPPC.

Semirara declares P3.50/sh cash dividend for 1H23

Semirara’s Board of Directors approved a regular cash dividend of P1.80/sh and a special cash dividend of P1.70/sh. The total cash dividend amounts to P3.50/sh, more than double 1H22’s dividend. The total div is equivalent to a yield of 11%, based on previous day’s closing price. Ex-dividend: April 5; Record date: April 13. SCC’s total dividends reached a record of P5/sh in 2022

BDO consolidates ownership of Podium Complex

BDO (BDO PM) disclosed that it will buy the remaining 50% stake in SM Keppel Land (SMKL) from Keppel Philippine Properties (KEP PM), making it wholly-owned by BDO. SMKL owns the Podium Complex in the Ortigas CBD, which consists of the Podium Mall, the BDO Corporate Center, and the West Tower. BDO occupies roughly 63% of the total space of the Podium Complex..

 

No price was reported, as the deal is still subject to closing provisions. On BDO’s books, its 50% stake is valued at P1.66bn. It is unclear if SMKL has debt. To the upside, if BDO buys the remaining 50% above its book cost, then it will likely recognize a MTM gain from the transaction. To the downside, the transaction will also cause some drag on BDO’s capital position. Since the property is held in a corporation, the investment will be deducted from qualifying capital at the Solo/Parent level. As of end-2022, BDO’s Solo CET-1 was 12.8%, on a qualifying capital base of P365bn. In both cases, though, Regis expects that the MTM gain and the CET-1 drag will likely not be material.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Marcos OKs DBP-Landbank merger

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said Marcos approved the merger during a sectoral meeting on Tuesday and that this could happen before the end of this year.

The approval was based on the following arguments:

·        The merger will create a bigger and stronger bank to better serve the country’s development needs

·        The combined branches of the two banks results in a wider network of its banking operations

·        The merged bank will become the largest bank in the Philippines in terms of assets and deposit size

·        The merger will eliminate redundancy and inefficiency in operations

·        Projected operating costs savings due to the merger could reach at least P5.3 billion per year, or more than P20 billion over the next four years; this is a conservative estimate

·        The merger complements the strengths and addresses the weaknesses of the two banks

·        The improved financial position will provide a bigger headroom for loans that can be used for development projects

·        The merger will help avoid the need for DBP to recapitalize—from P30 billion to P100 billion—and seek capital infusion from the national government.

·        The merged bank will be in the best position to serve as the sole authorized government depository bank for the entire Philippine government and its instrumentalities. Having a single bank remains the best practice in the region and simplified procedures with counterparties.

President Marcos looking to reform military pension program

Finance Secretary Diokno said that President Marcos was willing to use his political capital to reform the military pension system. Secretary Diokno warned of a fiscal collapse if this does not happen. The government has set aside P120bn-P130bn for the pension program this year. Pensions of retired military personnel are indexed to salaries of active members of the military. Secretary Diokno said that Defense Secretary Galvez and Interior Secretary Abalos agreed with all the options. The retirement age will rise from 56 to 57, active members of the military will contribute to their pensions and pensions of retired personnel will no longer be indexed to salaries of active members of the military.

Government debt at P13.75tn in end-February

The National Government’s outstanding debt rose by P54.26bn (+0.4% MoM) to P13.75tn in end-Feb due to net issuance of domestic securities. Out of the total debt stock, 68.7% was sourced from domestic borrowings while 31.3% was sourced externally. The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually go down to 51% by 2028.

BSP chief says too early for rate hike pause

It may be too soon for the Philippine central bank to pause from raising interest rates at its next policy meeting in May according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla. May is “too early” to pause, “ unless we actually see a price fall. The BSP has raised rates by 425bps since last year, among the most aggressive moves in the region.
