REGIS REPORT

29 September 2023

The index rallied sharply (+2.9% WoW) to 6,321.24, finally bouncing after breaching the 6100 level earlier in the month.  The BSP’s hawkish tone triggered a banks’ rally, with buying interest spreading to other large caps.

Gainers outnumbered losers 7:3.

Turnover bloated to US$242.8m (+201.2% WoW) due to an off-cycle PSEi rebalancing that occurred earlier this week. Last Monday, AP (+4.4% WoW) was deleted from the index and replaced by BLOOM (-6.3% WoW) while MPI (+1.2% WpW) was replaced by CNPF (-4.4% WoW) on Tuesday, September 26, after the tender offer cross was finally reflected in the market. The tender offer freed up US$502.4m worth of funds.

AP saw the most outflows from foreign investors at US$11.0m followed by CNPF at US$5.3m as foreigners took advantage of the liquidity event to take profits.

The PSE also announced another rebalancing effective at the close of Tuesday, October 3. UBP (-7.6% WoW) breached the 20% threshold requirement to be part of the PSEi and will therefore be replaced by NIKL (+16.4% WoW).

Outside these, the rally was broad based as all sectors ended in the green.

Banks led as the BSP guided for a potential rate hike. BDO (+8.3% WoW) saw the most foreign demand worth US$15.0m. BPI (+3.2% WoW), and Metrobank (+0.9% WoW) also ended higher.

Large caps SM (+4.9% WoW) and Ayala Land (+5.75% WoW)  also helped push up the index.

Bloomberry (-6.3% WoW) corrected this week, after a top-up placement in the name was announced Thursday priced at P10/sh (-7.1% discount to Thursday’s close).  

Headline foreign outflows were high at US$179.1m, mostly from the Bloomberry placement which saw US$95.5m of inflows. Excluding the MPI transaction, the market saw inflows worth US$98.4m for the first time since the week of August 4.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Globe secures loan from Metrobank

Globe signed term loan facilities with Metrobank for a total of P15bn, which would be ~1% of MBT’s loan book. The funds to be used will be to finance capex, refinance etc. The pricing (i.e., interest rate/s) is likely to be sub-market. Globe’s capital expenditures are declining after peaking last year at $1.9bn and is expected to fall to US$1.3bn this year and US$1.0bn next year.

Megaworld expects P27bn in sales from Uptown Modern

Megaworld expects P29bn in residential sales from the newly launched Uptown Modern in Fort Bonifacio. It will be completed in 2032. Reservation sales for the company hit P119bn in 2022.

CNPF’s unMEAT expands distribution in Australia

CNPF has expanded distribution of its alternative meat brand unMEAT in Australia. unMEAT will now be available in 960 outlets of Woolworths, the largest supermarket chain in Australia. The brand is current present in mainstream retailers in the US, including Walmart and Albertsons, Carrefour in the UAE, and FairPrice in Singapore, among others.

AP, Vivant set to build Samar wind farm

Aboitiz Power (AP PM), Vivant Corp (VVT PM) and Singapore-based Vena Energy created a joint venture called Lihangin Wind Corp to develop, construct and operate a 206 MW wind power plant in Northern Samar. The RE project is expected to reach financial closing before 4Q23 and ready for commercial operations in 1Q25. AP is in the process of growing its RE capacity to 4,600MW by 2030. Vena Energy is an RE developer in the Asia Pacific region. Its RE portfolio consists of onshore and offshore wind, sola rand stationary transportable energy storage projects totaling 45GW.

HK listed firm to spend $1.2bn on Manila IR

HK listed International Entertainment Corp has agreed to invest up to $1.2bn to development a new integrated resort in Manila after signing a provisional license agreement with gaming regulator PAGCOR. The original provisional casino license was first issued to the group in 2020, but the new agreement sets out the full terms of IEC’s license. IEC said it has submitted its master development plant proposal to PAGCOR for approval, with the entire development set to cover a total gross floor area of at least 250,000 sqm (<half of Solaire’s GFA). IEC will pay license fees on a monthly basis comprising 15% of VIP GGR and 25% of mass GGR. IEC currently co-manages, alongside PAGCOR, a casino in its Manila hotel, New Coast Hotel Manila (formerly New World Manila Bay Hotel, Malate). The company recently announced an agreement with Malaysian gaming equipment distributor RGB to acquire 382 slot machines for use at New Coast Hotel casino (Solaire has 2,500 slot machines)

PSE announces changes to composition of PSE Index: UBP (-), NIKL (+)

The PSE announced that UBP will be removed from the PSE Index. The PSE now considers some UBP shares, which were reported as float, to be non-public. This resulted in the public float of the company dropping below the 20% minimum requirement. NIKL will enter the PSE Index. Regis estimates outflow of US$31.3mn from UBP while an inflow of US$14.7mn to NIKL. All other PSE Index constituents should see modest amounts of inflow. The changes will be effective at the close of October 3 (open of October 4).

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Budget deficit widens to P133bn in Aug

The country’s budget deficit widened to P133bn in Aug from a P72bn deficit in the same period last year. On a MoM basis, the deficit is nearly 3x vs July. The governmanet’s revenues contracted 6.6% YoY. The lower collections were attributed to work suspensions brought about by the heavy rains. According to DoF Sec. Diokno, collections should improve in the following months. Meanwhile, expenditures rose 10% YoY, driven mainly by interest payments (+39% YoY). The YT-August deficit stood at P732.5bn (+12% YoY), tracking behind the gov’t target of P1.5tn.

Two senators reportedly withdraw support for POGO ban

In its Biz Buzz section, the Philippine Daily Inquirer reported that two senators withdrew their support for a ban on the POGO sector. The Senate Committee on Ways and Means recommends a three-month phaseout of POGO firms. Earlier 10 of the 18 members signed the Bill. The two senators reportedly prefer a longer phaseout period of two to three years. The POGO sector occupies about 800,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. This is lower than 1.4mn sqm as of end-2019. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 17% as of 2Q23 and POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

 

Senate approves PPP, internet transaction bills on third reading

Senate approved on third and final reading the proposed (1) PPP Act (Senate Bill 2233) and (2) Internet Transactions Act (SB 1846). The PPP bill aims to amend the over 30-year-old Build-Operate-Transfer Law.

Salient provisions include:

*Raising the threshold for PPP projects to be reviewed by the National Economic Development *Authority to P15bn from P300mn

*Encourage expansion of PPP projects to local government unit (LGU) level; the bill will clarify how LGUs do PPP projects

*Internet Transactions bill aims to protect online consumers with stricter digital commerce regulations

The Bills seeks to subject entities involved in e-commerce subject to domestic laws.

Lower House approves 2024 P5.768tn budget

The House of Representatives approved the 2024 budget on third and final reading. The budget for 2024 is higher by 9.5% YoY and is equivalent to 21.7% of GDP. The Senate is still holding hearings on the budgets of the different agencies and expects to approve the measure on final reading by November. 

Boards approve minimum wage hikes in 3 more regions

Regional wage boards approved increases in daily minimum wages in Cagayan Valley, Central Luzon and the SOCCSKSARGEN region.

*Cagayan valley hike ranges from 7.1%-7.5%

*Central Luzon hike ranges from 8.7%-11%

*Region XII hike ranges from 9.5%-10.1%

All wage orders will take effect on October 16. In 2022, the government implemented wage hikes of +12%-14% throughout the country.

IT-BPM sector on track to meet 2023 targets

The IT-BPO Association said it was on track to meet its 2023 targets of US$35.4mn (+9% YoY), 1.7mn employees (+8% YoY). For comparison, 2022 was US$32.5mn revenue and 1.57mn employees. The BPO sector is one of the major drivers of office demand. However, we expect continued implementation of work-from-home policies to keep office demand below pre-COVID levels. Regis forecasts Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 20% in 2023E (from 17% currently)

Marcos rejects proposal to lower rice tariffs; Price cap to remain

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has rejected a proposal to reduce tariffs on imported rice

“It was not the right time to lower the tariff rates because the projection of world rice prices is that it will go down”. The decision was made in consultation with the economic managers. Dept. of Finance, Dept of Budget and Management and National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) had previously sought to suspend rice tariffs. During the sectoral meeting, NEDA Chief Balisacan and other agriculture officials agreed with the President’s decision. Marcos said the price cap would continue to be in effect since it has to be studied carefully.

Lower House passes MUP Pension System Act

The Lower House approved on third and final reading  the Military and Uniformed Personnel (MUP) Pension System Act (House Bill 8969) .The MUP Pension System Act seeks to address the financially unsustainable military pension system in the country. Under the current system, the government needs to spend P164bn in 2024 to fund MUP pensions and gratuity benefits. Data showed that a guaranteed 3% annual salary increase for 10 years with full indexation of benefits will require P11.8bn in 2024, rising to P165bn by 2033. Unfunded military pension liabilities currently stands at P9.6tn.

SEC supports lower taxes on Philippine equities

The SEC said it supports House Bill 9277, which seeks to lower stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1% and tax on dividends of foreign nonresidents from 25% to 10% among other things. The HB 9277 is pending at the committee level.

Study on reclamation policy could take one year

Environment Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo-Loyzaga said that her department was in talks with scientists from the University of the Philippines Marine Science Institute and the Manila Observatory to conduct a study on reclamation policy. She said that the study could take up to one year. SM Prime’s Manila Bay reclamation projects comprise 37% of our (P48.27/share) NAV estimate. Pasay reclamation – 20% - P9.6/share. Paranaque reclamation – 17% - P8.2/share.

Digital payments +31% YoY in August to another record high, led by InstaPay +45% YoY

Digital payments over InstaPay (real-time transactions under P50,000) and PesoNet (batched, larger amounts) hit all-time highs yet again in August, hitting P1,114bn (just under $20bn), up 29.3% YoY and up 2% MoM. Year-to-date, digital transactions totaled P8,135bn, up 31% YoY.

InstaPay led the growth, surging 44.5% YoY to P431bn in August. This is faster than the 40.3% pace set in July. For 8M23, transactions are up 41.4% YoY to P3,120bn. The growth in the volume of transactions was even higher, up 56.0% YoY for the month. The value per transaction was down 7% YoY (slightly up MoM) to just under P5,989 (about $105). This indicates continued rapid adoption of digital payments at the retail / consumer level.

 

Meanwhile, PesoNet was up a still-respectable 21.3% YoY to P682bn in August, with the 8M23 figure up 24.5% YoY to P5,014bn. In contrast to InstaPay, the volume of transactions was up just 2.5% YoY in August, and 8.3% YoY for 8M23. The value per transaction was up 18% YoY in August, and up 10% YoY for 8M23. The average transaction value was P88,262 for the month.

 

As a reminder, the data from the BSP only includes payments made from one institution to another (eg BPI-to-BDO, Metrobank-to-GCash, GCash-to-Maya, etc). It does NOT count transactions made between parties using the same platform (eg GCash-to-GCash). It would be safe to assume that the total value of digital transactions is significantly larger, perhaps even several times the size presented here.
