REGIS REPORT

18 August 2023

The market fell sharply to 6,290.27 (-1.8% WoW), a new year-to-date low, after the BSP held policy rates steady at 6.25%, despite the Fed’s hike at its last meeting.

The sell-off was across the board as losers outnumbered gainers 2:1.

Biggest drags were still SM (-2.6% WoW) and SMPH (-4.8% WoW) on the back of the review of reclamation projects. ALI (+1.6% WoW) continued to benefit from this, as foreigners switch out from SMPH and into ALI. ALI saw the most foreign inflows for the week at US$11.3m while SMPH saw US$10.6m worth of outflows.

Bright spots in the index were mostly driven by favorable earnings. ICT core profit rose 12% YoY to US$310mn, slightly ahead of forecasts. Revenues and EBITDA were better sequentially, as yield per TEU continued to climb and more than compensated for the very marginal improvement in volumes.

GTCAP likewise reported strong results. Core income in 1H23 more than doubled to Php16.6bn, tracking to exceed both Regis and consensus estimates for the year. Implied 2Q23 profits rose 147% YoY, after Toyota’s profit tripled.

The rest of the market underperformed. Consumer names mostly dragged as rising commodity prices, such as rice, could keep the inflation elevated. MONDE (-14.9% WoW) fell sharply along with URC (-4.4% WoW).

Meanwhile, the telcos ended mixed. GLO outperformed the rest of the market as contributions from Mynt rose sharply. Fintech unit Mynt contributed profit of Php972m in 1H (+63% YoY), although it remains unclear how much of it is driven by increased gambling activity in the Gcash app, which could make it vulnerable to regulatory results. Revenues were soft overall as 1H23 core income declined 10%. On the other hand, TEL (-4.0% WoW) saw the most outflows from foreign investors at US$5.0m, being the top sold stock for the week.

Headline turnover for the week improved to US$151.9m. However, this was mostly due to an SM cross of 24m shares at Php860.00/sh. Excluding this transaction, turnover was lackluster at just US$78.4m while outflows stood at US$11.5m.

Ayala Corp. - 2Q results improve but marred by more write-downs
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

More write-downs; core operations improve

Net profit for 1H23 at Ayala Corp came in at P18.4bn, +13% higher YoY. Excluding what management considers one-off, core earnings amounted to P20.5bn for the half year. Large provisions for the group’s investments under AC Industrials crimped profits. While the headline results are below expectations, we are encouraged by the progress at its underlying businesses as well as efforts to dispose of some underperformers.

Headline income for 2Q was P8.2bn, -3% YoY and -20% QoQ. Stripping out one[1]offs, the bottom line looks better. Core income was P11.1bn, a meaningful improvement (+20%) from the P9.4bn of 1Q.

Core businesses improve

Of the four core listed businesses, BPI contributed the biggest share of income in 1H, with income rising +23% amidst the favorable environment for banks. ALI and ACEN also delivered strong growth numbers (+41% and +94%, respectively). Only Globe saw earnings dip (with or without one-off items).

More write-downs, this time at AC Industrials

The group had one-off gains from the sale of telecom towers booked in 2Q at Globe but also took write-downs / provisions for two investments (STI and MTC[1]Con) held under AC Industrials. AC’s share of the write-downs amounted to an aggregate P4.7bn. While the write-downs are by no-means a good thing, they indicate a delayed recognition about the need for AC to cut its losses on some investments.

In with the new

The four-wheeled business under AC Motors posted, surprisingly, a net profit of P312m for the half year. The most interesting disclosure on AC Motors, however, was the one about the group taking over the distribution rights for BYD in the Philippines. We reckon this could be significant contributor longer-term as electric vehicles (EV) take root. AC made its intentions clear about playing a key role in the EV space in the Philippines, and recently launched an initiative for two-wheeled EVs.

Losses at Health and Logistics

AC Health posted a small loss (P193m) for the period and claims its medical app now has 3.7m registered users. AC Logistics, meanwhile, reported a loss of P667m for 1H, with the 2Q pace similar to 1Q

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - 2Q23 operating profit jumps 51%; int’l business continues to improve
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H23 headline profit behind but EBIT (+51% YoY) well ahead of expectations

JFC’s headline net profit for 2Q23 was down 17% YoY P2.3bn due to a P2.9bn one[1]off gain recognized in the same period last year. Excluding one-offs (P1.1bn gain for 2Q) and after deducting perp coupons and pref divs, we estimate 2Q23 NPAT actually jumped 41% YoY to P2bn, bringing the 1H23 figure to P3.7bn (+2.4x YoY). While this appears to be tracking below expectations, underlying operations are ahead with 1H revs +22% to P116bn and EBIT +51% to P7.6bn. Key markets continued to post healthy SSSG in 2Q23 while a turnaround in int’l operations further boosted margins despite input cost pressures.

China, Philippines 2Q23 SSSG boosted by reopening

Group SSS grew 9% led by the China (+35%) and the Philippines (+11%), with the former boosted by the reopening early this year. The Philippines is also still partly coming from a low YoY base as dine-in restrictions were not entirely lifted until mid-2Q22. Smashburger posted SSSG of 8%, while CBTL was flat coming from a high YoY base.

Operating leverage drives 2Q23 EBIT margin amidst elevated input cost

Gross margin during the quarter was flattish even as inventory cost continued to be elevated (Fig. 3). The company put through minimal price adjustments (+1.5% in 1H) but gains from (1) more efficient store operations, (2) lower fixed G&A resulting from the business transformation initiative during the pandemic years, and (3) improving profitability of the international business, more than offset higher inventory cost. We note that all brands and geographies contributed positively to EBITDA and EBIT in 2Q23.

Macro headwinds persist but longer term strategy to continue

While management maintained its official guidance to grow operating profit by 20-25% in 2023 (Regis FY23E: +30%), it acknowledges that JFC appears on track to exceed this given the strong momentum in 1H23. The company is also looking to harvest more gains from continued investments in store expansion and efficiencies despite near-term macro headwinds in some markets (e.g. stiff competition in China, weak consumer sentiment in Vietnam) and input cost volatility. The company says it will focus on its four main pillars – chicken (Jollibee PH and globally), beef (Smashburger), beverages (CBTL), and Chinese cuisine (Tim Ho Wan) - as part of its longer term strategy.

The tariff hike in January and lower non-cash costs pushed Maynilad’s standalone profit higher by 45% YoY in 1H23, despite anemic billed volume growth (+1%). Capex rose 37% to P9.4bn in 1H23, lagging its P26bn FY23 target.

Updates on other segments

Robinsons Land - 2Q23 reservation sales hit record level; mall margins improve
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H23 earnings rise 23%

Robinsons Land’s 1H23 net profit rose 23% to P5.8bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. The outperformance was largely due to faster-than[1]expected recognition of residential JV projects as well as a 7.8 percentage point (ppt) drop in effective tax rate (to 11.9%). The most eye-catching part of the results, however, was the surge in residential reservation sales.

2Q23 reservation sales hit a record level

2Q23 reservation sales of RLC’s standalone residential projects jumped 120% YoY (+21% QoQ) to P6.8bn, a record level and 35% above the 2019 average. Unlike in 4Q22, when nearly half the sales came from a single project, sales appear to be more broad-based in 2Q23.

Similar to 1Q23 (and FY22), international buyers still accounted for 25% of reservation sales. Most of these were Americans, who we suspect predominantly have roots in the Philippines (such as Filipinos who became American passport holders). RLC also saw strong demand for its JV projects, which are generally higher-end than its standalone projects. Presales of such projects rose 19% YoY in 2Q23.

Mall margins improve on power costs

Unlike the experience of the other major mall operators, RLC’s 2Q23 mall revenue was flat QoQ at P3.9bn (about the same as the 2019 level). However, it compensated with a 1.5ppt improvement in EBIT margin QoQ (+19.7 ppt YoY) to 38.2%, higher than the 2019 level of 32%, thanks to lower power costs. As a result of the higher margins, mall EBIT climbed 163% YoY in 2Q23 (and 1H23).

Office occupancy stays flat but will likely drop in 2H23

Office occupancy was flat QoQ at 89%, higher than the industry average of 83%. However, the 53,000sqm building due for completion in 2H23 is only 13% preleased. Thus, if RLC is unable to sign new tenants, then its office occupancy will drop to 84% later this year.

Hotel revenue also hits record level

2Q23 hotel revenue grew 139% YoY (+28% QoQ) to P1.1bn, a record level and double the 2Q19 figure, thanks to the economic reopening as well as the 23% increase in hotel rooms. RLC completed two luxury hotels in the past year – Fili Urban Resort Hotel (in Cebu) and Westin Manila Sonata Place Hotel.

 
Megaworld - 2Q23 reservation sales remain strong; net office take-up turns positive
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H23 profit rises 34%, in line

MEG’s 1H23 net profit rose 34% to P7.9bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. While residential revenue is trending slightly behind our estimate, this was offset by stronger-than-expected residential GPM. More than the earnings, however, we believe investors should focus on the strong reservation sales.

2Q23 presales remain strong on provincial projects, international buyers

2Q23 reservation sales remained strong, growing 31% YoY to P36.5bn, equivalent to 98% of the 2019 average. Provincial projects (mostly condominiums) comprised 70% of 2Q23 sales, from only 30% in 1Q23.

1H23 reservation sales increased 49% to P76.1bn, tracking ahead of REGIS forecast and company guidance. The increase was largely driven by international buyers (predominantly OFWs), who accounted for 25% of sales, from only 15% in 1H22.

Net office take-up turns positive despite decline in occupancy

Occupancy of MEG’s existing office portfolio fell 1ppt QoQ to 89% (betterthan the industry average of 83%). However, if we were to consider preleasing at buildings to be completed in 2H23, net office take-up was actually positive in 2Q23. Preleasing at the company’s upcoming 69,000sqm Fort Bonifacio building improved to 100% (from 60% in 1Q23) as one financial services BPO signed up for c. 28,000sqm of space.

Mall revenue not back to pre-COVID levels but should pick up

Mall revenue grew 5% QoQ (+66% YoY) to P1.3bn. Unlike the three major mall operators, Megaworld’s mall revenue is only about 85% of the pre-COVID level. Nevertheless, mall revenue should improve further since 91% of space was leased out as of end-2Q23 (similar to the 2019 level). This is higher than the average occupancy of 85% over the period, implying that occupancy will rise.

 
Cebu Air - Supply issues crimp yield management in 2Q23
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H23 EBITDA at P9.59bn – In line

Core profit rose 25% QoQ to P1.69bn, taking 1H23 profit to P3.04bn. While the 1H23 figure is already 74% of Regis’ FY23E and 92% of Street estimates, the core business is performing within expectations excluding the projected impact of fuel hedging losses and taxes. 1H23 EBITDA of P9.59bn was a reversal of last year’s losses and is in line with Regis and consensus estimates.

2Q: Domestic market share improves but overall fares dip due to supply issues

Revenues improved by 9% QoQ, as systemwide pax volumes recovered to 97% of 2019 average and more than offset weak cargo revs. ASKs (available seat per km) rose 18% QoQ to 97% of pre-Covid levels while PLFs inched up 1ppt to 80% (vs 85% pre-Covid). Despite the domestic market share gain (+2ppts QoQ to 54%) and the recovery (albeit slow) of the international market, overall fares dipped 2% QoQ, as aircraft issues during the quarter led to several flight consolidations, which in turn limited excess seats for higher yielding last-minute bookings.

Non-fuel CASK improves 14% QoQ, driving EBITDA up 32% QoQ

2Q EBITDA was higher by 32% QoQ to P5.46bn due to improvements in non-fuel costs. Non-fuel cost per ASK dropped 14% QoQ to P1.8 (US$3.3 cents), below our FY23 assumption of P2.0. We believe this has room to drop further given management’s aim to cut this to pre-Covid levels of P1.6 by year-end. While jet kero prices are up 26% from end-June levels at US$115/bbl, management said it has been passing these on, given favorable competitive dynamics.

2H23 EBIT margin guidance better than forecast

Forward bookings for 2H23 remain resilient despite the elevated level of cancellations in 2Q. With supply issues now more manageable, management noted that it is seeing better booking and yield activity in 3Q. Management also guided for 2H23 EBIT margins of “high single-digit to low double-digit”, an improvement from 1H of 8.6% and much better than our forecast (7%).

 
ICTSI - Sustained yield improvement lifts 2Q23
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H23 recurring profit to common rises 12% YoY to US$310mn

Headline 2Q23 profit of US$159mn (+5% YoY) included a one-off impairment charge related to the termination of its concession agreement in Pakistan. Removing the one-off and the perps distributions, core profit for the quarter stood at US$163mn, up 10% QoQ and 13% YoY. This brought 1H23 profit to US$310mn, up 12% YoY and trending mildly better than Regis’ and Street FY23E.

Yield improvement drives 2Q23 organic revenues +8% YoY higher

Consolidated volumes rose 9% YoY in 2Q while organic volumes (Pakistan already excluded) were little changed (+0.2% YoY in 2Q23 vs -0.7% YoY in 1Q23). What’s notable, however, is the improvement in consolidated yield per TEU, which climbed further to US$181 in 2Q23, reverting to the yield prior to consolidation of MNHPI. On an organic basis, yield per TEU in 2Q23 was up 8% YoY and 1% QoQ to US$194 due to tariff hikes. For the remainder of 2023, management remains optimistic about sustaining yields at c. US$180. Volumes are also beginning to move more positively in 3Q so far, particularly for EMEA and Latin America. Asia remains flat due to weather disruptions in the past weeks.

EBITDA margin improves back to 63%

2Q23 EBITDA improved 6% QoQ and 12% YoY to US$375mn. EBITDA margin rose 130bps QoQ to 63%, as fuel/power-related costs started to unwind. Assuming volume growth accelerates in 3Q, we anticipate operating leverage to lift EBITDA and the bottom-line disproportionately more.

Raising FY23E by 5% and TP to P265/sh (+6%)

We raise our FY23E profit forecast by 5% after incorporating the 1H results. We likewise raise our NAV-based TP to P265/sh (+6%) after rolling our estimates to 2024. Our revised TP implies EV/EBITDA of 8.5x for FY24E, still a discount to historical average multiples (11x) and peers (10x).

Management also touched on its imminent takeover of DCT-2 port in Durban, South Africa, which is the largest gateway port in the African region. Generating throughput of c. 1.6mn TEUs today, the port could add c. 12% to consolidated volumes upon takeover, presumably in 1Q24. Management is still finalizing contract terms with the government, which it hopes to complete in a few weeks.

House panel approves bill seeking to reform military pension system

An ad hoc committee under the Lower House approved a consolidated bill that seeks to reform the military and uniformed personnel (MUP) pension system. Congressman Jose Maria Clemente Salceda said that the measure would guarantee salary increases, pension indexation and funding sources for the MUP pension system. All MUP would now be required to contribute to the pension fund (For active personnel, contributions will be 5%-9% while new entrants will contribute 9% immediately). All MUP will also be guaranteed a 3% salary increase every year for the next 10 years (vs. no salary increases in the past nine years) with a counterpart contribution from the government (21%). Retention of the indexation of pensions for 10 years but will be capped at 50% of the salary increase.

 

The retirement age for all MUP will now be fixed at 57, or after completing 30 years of satisfactory service. A 90% maximum retirement package based on the base pay of all MUP, effectively raising by 5% the previous package for Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) personnel. The measure also includes Philippine National Police (PNP) personnel who leave with less than 20 years’ service in the list of those eligible for a lump sum separation pay. The current policy where an MUP will be promoted to one rank higher upon retirement will also be maintained. Periodic reviews of pension benefits and a possible increase of up to 1.5% per year, although this may depend on economic conditions and actuarial life of the pension fund. The government’s unfunded pension liability for military and police personnel has ballooned to P10tn so passage of this reform will be welcomed.
