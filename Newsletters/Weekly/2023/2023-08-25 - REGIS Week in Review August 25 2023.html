REGIS REPORT

25 August 2023

Foreign selling took the PSEi lower this week (-2.1% WoW), even as markets anticipated Powell’s Jackson Hole speech at the end of the week. On the ground, the weaker-than-expected 2Q GDP print  has weighed on the index since its August 10 release. This week marks five straight weeks of declines for the PSEi. 

The market fell 3 of the 4 trading days this week, taking the index below the 6200 index level.  Foreign selling (net) accelerated to US$29.9m, well above last week’s US$11.45m outflow.

Meanwhile, investors are also bracing for higher inflation, with fears of rice supply issues and rice prices up already around 17%YTD.  Rice is the single biggest component of CPI at around 9% of the basket.

Losses were widespread, with losers overatking gainers 20 to 9. Losses were led by the large caps, with derisking seen in widely held SM (-4.1% WoW), BPI (-5.4% WoW), ALI (-4.75% WoW), and ICT (-4.2% WoW).  The four stocks pushed the market down by 98 index points, accounting for 76% of the market’s total drop (130 index points).

Meanwhile, BDO magned a gain, after dropping 3.7% in the previous week.   Monde (+2.3% WoW) also gained, as interest emerged after the share price hit an all time low on Monday (P6.18/sh).

AP (+1.45% WoW) closed higher for the week. AC (+1.4% WoW) also gained, with its AC Industrials anouncing a divestment of a loss-making unit. AC Industrials is selling its 92.45% stake in MT Technologies GmbH (MT) to a private equity firm. The sale by AC Industrials, a wholly-owned subsidiary of AC, of its stake in the German subsidiary was for a nominal one Euro, and will result in a EUR26m loss. AC already made provisions for this loss in its 2Q results.

Mid cap conglos also outperformed, with LT Group (+1.3% WoW), SMC (+1.15% WoW), AEV (+0.4% WoW) rising. MPI (+1.0% WoW) continued to gain as investors continued to build on their positions ahead of the end of the tender offer period on September 7. The tender offer price is P5.20/sh.

Market activity uninspiring, reaching a mere US$67.7m daily average.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property - 2Q23 recap: Office demand from online gambling rebounds
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

An eye-catching development

In many respects, 2Q23 saw a continuation of trends from earlier in the year. However, the rebound in 2Q23 POGO office demand was attention[1]grabbing. Multiple online gambling firms took up additional space in the Manila Bay area. Meanwhile, mall revenue continued to climb after the unusually strong 1Q. On the other hand, residential revenue remains well below pre-COVID levels despite presales being close to record highs.

Office: Demand from online gambling rebounds

Office take-up from online gambling firms rebounded in 2Q23 (see Figure 1). In addition to POGO firms, which service clients from abroad, companies that cater to Philippine residents also appear to have taken up incremental space. Office take-up from online gambling is likely to have significant implications on residential demand as well.

Although the Metro Manila office vacancy rate fell 1ppt QoQ (to 17%), developers reported that some of their tenants renewed contracts at lower rental rates. We expect more such cases in 2H23 and especially 2024, since 1) prevailing office rents are 20%-50% below 2019 levels, and 2) the contracts up for renewal were previously signed in 2018/2019, when rental rates were close to the peak.

Mall revenue still grows QoQ

Despite an unusually strong 1Q performance, aggregate mall revenue of the top 3 mall operators grew another 3% QoQ (+28% YoY) in 2Q23 to P25.2bn, 9% higher than the 2Q19 level. Consumer spending in malls should remain resilient despite the high inflation environment. The favorable operating environment makes it more likely that some developers will spin off their mall assets into REITs soon.

Presales remain strong while residential revenue remains below pre-COVID

Aggregate (gross) reservation sales of the developers we cover grew 24% YoY (- 2% QoQ) to P129bn, 103% of 2019 levels. Meanwhile, aggregate residential revenue rose 19% YoY (+15% QoQ). Despite the improvement, however, residential revenue is still 20% below 2019 levels.

The low residential revenue (relative to presales) is likely due to a combination of: 1) looser payment schemes delaying revenue recognition, and 2) cancellation rates remaining high. Thus, relative to the pre-COVID period, reservation sales alone would now be an insufficient gauge of the strength of residential demand.

Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that capital values of Metro Manila condominiums in the secondary market inched up for the seventh consecutive quarter, rising 4% YoY. Prices of most units are still 10%-20% below 2019 levels.

The banking sector’s mortgage NPL ratio fell 10bps QoQ (1.9ppt YoY) to 7.5% in 1Q23. However, mortgage rates have risen 100bps-200bps since end-2021 (flat QoQ). This could increase monthly amortizations by 10%-20% and raise mortgage NPLs (or prevent buyers from availing of mortgages).

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

AC unit divests from German car parts maker

Ayala Corp (AC PM) disclosed that AC Industrials completed an agreement on Aug. 18 to sell its 92.45% stake in MT Technologies GmbH (MT) to Callista Asset Management 18 GmbH, an affiliate of Callista Private Equity. The transaction will result in an approximately 26mn euro loss to be booked by AC Industrials and in turn Ayala. The sale will be executed alongside a sale and leaseback by MT of its property in Ingolstadt and assignment of shareholder loans to the purchaser. MT’s minority shareholders also sold their sales, which resulted in 100% ownership of MT by Callista. MT is a German auto parts maker engaged in the design and manufacture of injection molds for metal and plastic car parts.

PLDT targets 20%-25% data center growth

PLDT Head of Enterprise and International Business Groups Albert Locsin said that the growth rate of any market in the data center and cloud professional services ranges from 20%-25% and that is also PLDT’s aspiration. PLDT has 10 data centers and is currently building its 11th , which will have a capacity of 50MW. It will have an IT load of 36, which can cater to the largest hyperscalers. The company is in the first phase of sourcing for the location of its 12th data center.

ACEN finalizes deal with ib vogt

ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) said that the shareholder’s agreement for the joint venture between ACEN Renewables International Pte. Ltd. And ib vogt had been declared effective on August 18 after regulatory approvals had been secured. The deal was originally disclosed in April 2022 when ACEN said it expects to invest up to US$200mn in equity as well as debt funding. The joint venture will focus on shovel-ready projects in Bangladesh, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia and other countries in Asia Pac. It is targeting a minimum operational capacity of 1,000MW. Most of the projects will be derived from ib vogt’s pre-existing projects from its Aisa development pipeline. The company is targeting to expand its RE capacity to 20GW by 2030 from the current 4,200MW in attributable capacity.

Meralco generation arm to invest at least P18bn for RE portfolio

Meralco said that its power generation arm Meralco PowerGen Corp (MGen) will invest at least P18bn to accelerate the growth of renewable energy portfolio. The investment will cover over 2GW of gross RE capacity from solar and wind that MGen and partners plan to build through 2030. Its renewable unit MGreen has an RE portfolio including the 55MW BulacanSol solar plant in San Miguel, Bulacan (partnership with Powersource Energy Holdings), the 68MW solar farm in Currimao, Ilocos Norte (partnership with Vena Energy) and the 75MW solar farm in Baras, Rizal (partnership with Mitsui).

Junket operator OKBet shuts down VIP rooms in the Philippines

According to Inside Asian Gaming, junket operator OKBet Infiinity Inc closed all of its Philippine VIP rooms on August 15, 2023 and was making “necessary arrangements regarding patron accounts”. OKBet, which is said to be based out of Hong Kong, had VIP rooms in multiple Philippine IRs including Okada Manila, Newport World Resorts, City of Dreams Manila, Hann Casino Resort and NUSTAR, offering land-based junket and proxy betting services. According to representative from Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM), OKBet does not have a junket room at Solaire and Solaire does not expect to have a receivables problem arising from the closure. Apart from junket rooms, OKBet operates online sports betting and casino games.

Third-party subscribers that took 13.55% of DITO CME named

Two unrelated third-party subscribers to shares in DITO CME Holdings have cornered as much as 13.55% of DITO CME. In a regulatory filing, DITO CME said one of the subscribers, Xterra Ventures Pte. Ltd., make up 3.76% or 610mn of the firm’s 16.235bn issued and outstanding common shares. Before the share issuance to Xterra, DITO CME issued 1.59bn new shares to Summit Telco Corp. Pte. Ltd, which then brought the firm’s outstanding shares to 15.625bn. Summit Telco now accounts for 9.79% of DITO while Udenna has 68.99%. Xterra Ventures is a Singapore-based entity with an” investment fund engaged in investment activities in the Asia Pacific region”.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Bank regulators maintain interest rate cap on credit cards

Bank regulators maintained the interest cap on credit card transactions at 3% per month of 36% per year, at least through the next six months. The cap was raised to the current level last January 2 from 2% per month or 24% per year. At the same time, the central bank continued to keep the ceiling on the monthly add-on rate that credit card issuers can charge on installment loans at one%. The maximum processing fee on the availment of credit card cash advances remains at P200/transaction. BSP data show that credit card receivables surged by 29% to c.P600bn as of the end of May 2023. Billings jumped 34.6% while nonperforming credit card receivables was lower at P23.4bn vs P29.3bn last year. Ratio of nonperforming credit card receivables to credit card receivables declined to 3.9% at end-May from 6.3% previously.

Balance of payments narrows to US$53mn in July; End-July GIR revised up to US$100bn

The country’s balance of payments stood at a deficit of US$53mn in July. Narrower than the deficit of US$606mn in June and US$1.8bn deficit in July 2022. This brings YT-July BOP to a surplus of US$2.2bn, a reversal of the US$4.9bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP expects a BOP deficit of US$1.2bn by year end (0.3% of GDP), smaller than the US$7.26bn (1.8% of GDP) in 2022. Meanwhile, the BSP revised the GIR level for end-July to US$100bn (from US$99.7bn). This is up from US$99.4bn in end-June and equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports.

BSP Governor says 5% reserves ratio possible

BSP Governor Eli Remolona said that “eventually” the RRR will be reduced to 5% or 0%

RRR is currently at 9.5%. The timing of when to cut RRR is crucial and not while the BSP is still in a hawkish policy stance amid a still elevated inflation expectations. The BSP may reconsider the cut by end-2023. Regis estimates every 100bps RRR cut could increase NPAT for banks under our coverage by 1.2%-1.5%.

Economic team prefers extension of reduced tariffs on key commodities

National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan said that there is a need to address elevated inflation to achieve its 6-7% GDP growth target. Mr. Balisacan said there is a need to extend the executive order that reduced tariffs on key commodities, which is set to expire in December. He expressed support for extending the reduced tariffs for pork, rice, corn, and coal to keep inflation contained. Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said that the economic team will have another meeting next month to review if there is a need to extend tariffs across all commodities.

Government opens bidding for NAIA privatization

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) has officially launched the bidding for the P170.6bn contract to operate and maintain the NAIA. The operator will need to raise passenger capacity to 62mn per year from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years). Based on the bidding schedule, the DOTr will issue the draft concession agreement on Sept. 8 and will host site visits for interested parties between Aug. 28 and Nov. 14.

Infrastructure spending up 2% YoY in 1H23

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 2% YoY to P607bn in 1H23. This implies spending in June 2023 alone fell 15% YoY on high base effects from the lifting of last year’s election ban. The 2Q23 infra spend rose 8% YoY to P363bn. The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn (+1% YoY) in FY23, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP.
