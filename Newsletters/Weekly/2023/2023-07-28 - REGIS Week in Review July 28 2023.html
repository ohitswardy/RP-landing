REGIS REPORT

28 July 2023

The market consolidated at the 6600 levels for the week, failing to break above the medium-term technical resistance of 6750. The index closed just slightly lower at 6,625.26 (-0.34% WoW) with daily turnover dropping to US$68.0m (from $84.5m the previous week).

Investors have been on the sidelines, even as the US Federal Reserve raised the target Fed funds rate by 25 bps to 5.25-5.50% last Wednesday. Trading was mostly mixed as earnings results for the second quarter slowly came out.

Most small cap consumer names reported disappointing results. WLCON’s (-6.1% WoW) revenue for the quarter came in at Php8.6bn, just 4% higher YoY as spending on home improvement slowed down. Profit for 2Q thus dropped 15% to Php856m. Same store sales across the system contracted 3% in the quarter. RRHI’s (-0.5% WoW) revenue growth decelerated to +8%  in 2Q, taking 1H growth to +10%. Net income for 1H was -34%, affected by the financing costs related to RRHI’s purchase of BPI shares. Operating results were also disappointing, withEBIT margins staying low at 4.4%. Only PGOLD (+0.5% WoW) saw net sales rise by 11% in 1H, with net profit up by 5% to Php4.4bn. Bottom-line figures are on track to mete full-year estimate of Php10bn. Margin compression in 2Q is , following the big annual sales event for its Puregold customers and the competitive pressure being addressed by S&R.

On the contrary, AP (+3.0% WoW) saw very strong results. Core income hit Php10.4bn (+69% YoY), therefore cumulative 1H core profit is now at Php17.7bn (+95% YoY), well on its way to exceeding consensus numbers for the year. The strength was driven mostly by the generation business due to higher power prices while the distribution business registered pretty good volumes (+7% YoY).

Overall, foreigners were still active in the market, taking up 49% of trades. Net inflows registered at US$5.5m, mostly into the property names. SMPH (+1.7% WoW) and ALI (+0.4% WoW) saw inflows worth US$10.6m and US$6.5m each.

Wilcon Depot - 2Q23: SSS -3% as YoY base normalizes
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H23 NPAT -2% YoY to P1.8bn - behind

WLCON reported 1H23 NPAT of P1.8bn, down 2% YoY, tracking behind Regis and consensus estimates. For 2Q23 alone, NPAT dropped 15% YoY to P856mn dragged by SSS decline of -3.4%, magnified by negative operating leverage from the weak top line and exacerbated by pre-operating expenses for new stores. Net sales during the quarter rose 4% YoY, bringing the 1H figure to P17.2bn, also tracking behind expectations.

SSS normalizes from high base, slowdown in private construction

SSS declined 3.4% during the quarter on a normalized YoY base following the lifting of lockdown restrictions in 2Q22. Same-store ticket count fell 7%, while baskets grew 4%. Apart from the base effects, we believe consumers postponed home improvement projects and instead spent on other leisure activities (e.g. travel). Holidays and long weekends during the quarter also led to a slowdown in private construction activity. Management noted, however, that average daily sales continue to grow sequentially.

Operating margins crimped by higher opex

Operating profit contracted 12% YoY 2Q due to (1) negative operating leverage from the SSS decline, and (2) pre-operating expenses for new stores in the pipeline. As of 1H23, WLCON opened 4 new stores, with another 7 in various stages of construction. On a more positive note, gross margin during the quarter stayed high at 39.5% due to (1) continued increase in the contribution of in[1]house brands, and (2) easing freight and product cost.

Top line ‘softness’ to persist as YoY base continues to normalize

Management expects SSS softness to persist as the YoY base continues to normalize in 2H, but believes it can sustain mid-single digit SSSG beyond 2023. We believe the top line weakness is temporary and the runway for growth for WLCON continues to be long given low penetration, especially in areas outside Metro Manila.

 
Robinsons Retail - SSSG dips in 2Q23
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H23 core retail operations mildly behind; EBIT +7% YoY to P3.9bn

RRHI reported 2Q23 net profit of P1.3bn, down 19% YoY. This brings cumulative 1H23 net profit to P1.8bn, down 34% YoY, tracking behind Regis and consensus expectations The drag was primarily due to higher interest expense from acquisition financing for BPI. Core retail operations also appear mildly behind with 2Q23 EBIT flat YoY at P2.1bn, bringing 1H23 EBIT to P3.9bn (+7%). 1H23 net sales of P91bn (+10% YoY), however, is within expectations.

2Q SSSG dips

Consolidated SSSG slowed to +3.6% in 2Q23 (+9.2% in 1Q23), mainly due to a higher YoY base. Majority of RRHI’s formats saw slower SSSG, following the initial surge in demand after restrictions were lifted in 2Q22. Drug stores (18% of sales) were the exception (+11% SSSG) due to strong demand for maintenance medication. Meanwhile, supermarkets (57% of sales) SSSG came in at +3%, dragged by a decline in same-store tickets (-12%). Note the base of comparison was also high due to election-related bulk purchases last year.

Higher opex drags operating margin

Operating profit was flat YoY in 2Q23, with margins down 30bps YoY. While this was partly driven by the soft top line, management also noted higher opex, particularly utilities and rent – the latter from the normalization of rental rates (i.e. removal of rental concessions).

Base effects likely to keep SSSG subdued in 2H

We believe SSSG (YoY) will be muted for the rest of the year as operations and the bases of comparison normalize. However, we do expect margins to improve sequentially into the seasonally strong 2H, lifted in part by operating leverage. Higher interest expense will likely remain a drag, though, even as RRHI pares down some of its debt.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - Grass is greener where you water; raising TP to P15.0 (+28%)
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Committed Phils strategy for long-term results; reiterate BUY

We refresh our estimates for Bloomberry and reiterate our BUY rating. We believe the investment case should now shift beyond ‘a return to pre-Covid’,to Solaire North and its local portfolio build-up. The potential from deleveraging, investment rationalization, and China is also underappreciated. After a return to peak profitability in 23E, we estimate 3-yr CAGR of 18% for GGR, 21% for EBITDA and 36% for income. BUY with a revised target price of P15.0 (+28%) as we roll forward to 24E.

Ample capacity for domestic push

Despite record GGR, Solaire is only 70% utilized, which means there is enough capacity to accommodate a more meaningful return of foot traffic. Its satellite in QC is expected to increase capacity by 50%, while the 280-ha beachfront property in Cavite lifts the headroom for long-term development. Although the footprint still includes its underperforming Korean property, the domestic push could be supplemented with some unexpected gains from asset disposals abroad.

Volume trends more stable than GGR

Win rates could continue to be volatile given the smaller market size. We think it is worth focusing then on underlying volumes which have remained on a markedly upward trend since reopening. A higher portion of the mass business coming from the premium segment may be contributing to higher-than-usual table win rates.

FY impact of Solaire North could drive EBITDA >30% higher than 23E

While Solaire North could have a slightly negative profit impact in the early stages of ramp-up, we expect EBITDA/profit on its first full year of operations (25E), to be 30%/33% higher than 23E. Broadly speaking, we think that the property could add c.P10bn in EBITDA and c.P5bn in profit on a fully-utilized basis, which could take between 3 and 5 years.

Revised target price to P15.00/sh (+28%); risks

We raise our target price by 28% as we roll over estimates. We ascribe a target multiple of 10x EV/EBITDA to Solaire’s FY24E and add the market value of the unused landbank. At our TP, BLOOM would trade at 11.4x consolidated EBITDA for FY23E and 9.5x for FY24E – in line with historical multiples and below Macau. Key risks include regulation, competition, tourism and the arbitration award.

Megaworld sells MREIT shares

Megaworld sold 279.4mn MREIT shares at P13/share for a total of P3.6bn. This is equivalent to about 10% of MREIT shares outstanding and reduced Megaworld’s stake in MREIT from 66% to 56%. The sale is in preparation for the acquisition of seven more office buildings from Megaworld via share-for-asset swap. Megaworld needs to reduce its stake in MREIT in order to comply with the minimum public ownership requirement (of 33%). MREIT will likely issue about 840mn news shares to Megaworld.

 

This will expand MREIT shares outstanding by about 30%. The buildings have a combined leasable area of 150,000sqm and have an aggregate occupancy rate of 94%. The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 46% to 475,000sqm. The buildings generated P1.2bn in rental revenue in 2022. MEG is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY24E P/B, 3.8X FY24E P/E. MREIT currently offers 6.6% FY24E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The current 10-year government bond yield is 6.3%.

LTG's 2Q23 cigarette volumes fall to 5.6bn sticks (-20%YoY, -15%QoQ), mkt share down 3.5ppt to 56%

Philip Morris International disclosed that PMFTC’s cigarette volumes in the Philippines fell to 5.6bn sticks in 2Q, down 20%YoY and 15%QoQ. Without providing much detail, the disclosure attributed the volume decline to the performance of the Marlboro brand.  Industry volumes (10bn sticks, -11%YoY, -10%QoQ) are again down by less than PMFTC’s volumes). PMFTC’s unit market share notably fell 6.5ppts YoY, 3.5ppts to 56.0%. Recall that PMFTC raised retail prices of Marlboro, Fortune and Jackpot by around 15% in February this year to an average price of about P120/pack.

 

These were meant to cover the increase in excise taxes at the start of the year (from P55/pack to P60/pack). Impact of the price hike could have spilled over into 2Q. Company has also talked about how illicit trade as a % of total industry may be back to the teens (from under 5% at the end of 2021). The government’s tobacco tax revenue declined for the first time in 2022 – to P160bn from P176bn in 2021 – after the tax rate hit P55/pack. LTG paid out virtually its entire share in PMFTC’s earnings last year as dividends (P15.1bn, P1.40/share, 14% yield) to LTG holders. Assuming the same payout, dividends this year may be closer to P12-13bn (P1.10-1.20/share) for an 11-12% div yield.

 

Megaworld set to open ‘biggest’ hotel by year end

MEGAWORLD Corp. plans to open its 1,500-room hotel in Parañaque City by the end of the year. The hotel, named Grand Westside Hotel, will become the largest hotel property in Megaworld's portfolio and in the entire country. Will be the 19th hotel property launched by Megaworld Hotels & Resorts, with 12 currently operational and seven more in the pipeline

Megaworld has invested around P64 billion to build Westside City, where the hotel is situated.

 

Hotel details:

Various unit types, including queen suites, twin suites, and executive suites, with specific options for specially-abled guests. Two levels of retail and commercial space, as well as amenities such as pools, a gazebo, a zen garden, function rooms, a gym, and massage rooms.

Four dining outlets, including the Grand Westside Café. Megaworld’s hotel portfolio accounts for about 2% of operating profit.  It currently operates about 5,000 hotel rooms (including condotels)

MEG is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY23E P/B, and 4.0X FY23E P/E  

PHL data centre capacity set to soar five times by 2025 — DICT

The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) expects a fivefold increase in data centre capacity in the Philippines, reaching ~300 MW by 2025. Presently, total data centre capacity nationwide is 60 MW – The projection of reaching 300 MW is considered a conservative target. Telecom companies and private sector partners are in the process of constructing additional data centres, with many projects having more than 100 MW capacity within the next 2 years.

 

Hyperscalers are showing interest in the Philippines as an ideal location for hosting data centres, driving demand for data centre capacity. Companies like Globe Telecom, Ayala Corp., ST Telemedia Global Data Centres, and PLDT Inc. have announced plans for new data centres and expansions. The increase in data centre capacity will fuel AI applications, support the business process outsourcing community, and provide data for governance and decision-making by government agencies through an integrated e-government platform.

ACEN Board Oks P12.5 billion preference shares issuance

ACEN Corp. received approval for the issuance of up to P12.50 billion worth of preferred shares as part of a three-year shelf registration program. The preferred share issuance will offer up to 12.50 million preferred shares priced at P1,000 each, with a par value of about P1 apiece. The issuance will be offered in two series, subject to necessary requirements for registration with the SEC and listing with the PSE.

 

ACEN aims to raise funds through the preferred share issuance to support its ambitious plans of investing up to $8 billion in clean energy projects, aiming for an 8-gigawatt (GW) portfolio by 2030. The company plans to expedite the development of its projects to achieve at least 2 GW per year and has allocated a capital expenditure budget of between P50 billion and P70 billion for 2023. Currently, ACEN holds approximately 4,200 megawatts of attributable capacity across various locations, including the Philippines, Vietnam, Indonesia, India, and Australia.

 

AP posts P10.4bn core income in 2Q23, +37%QoQ, 69%YoY; 1H23 ahead of cons, in line with Regis

AP reported a 2Q23 core income of P10.4bn, +37%QoQ, 69%YoY. This brings core income for 1H23 to P17.8bn (95%YoY) – in line with Regis (P34.8bn) but ahead of consensus (P29.4bn). The press release continued to attribute the strong performance to GNPower Dinginin as well as higher availability across its portfolio. Regis suspects that higher power prices in 2Q contributed to better margins in the period. WESM in 1Q ranged from P5.82-P7.62/kwh while 2Q was from P6.95-9.30/kwh. AP’s generation and RES business posted an EBITDA of P30.2bn in 1H23, 31%YoY. This implies 2Q EBITDA of P17.1bn (+31%QoQ). Energy sold in 2Q grew to 9,011GWh (3%QoQ, 17%YoY) while distribution EBITDA was P6bn, +101%YoY. This implies 2Q EBITDA of P3.1bn (7%QoQ). Energy sales grew 12%QoQ, 7%YoY to 1,579GWh in 2Q. AP trades at 7.8x PE in FY23E falling to 6.4x PE in FY24E.

Colliers: 2Q23 office vacancy improves slightly; residential prices inch up

Colliers reported that 2Q23 Metro Manila office vacancy fell 0.3ppt QoQ to 18.4%. It estimates that office rental rates were flat QoQ. Office rental rates are generally 20%-50% lower than pre-COVID levels (mostly 30%-40% down). Colliers is forecasting Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 21% in 2023E and office rental rates to fall 5% for the full-year. These are similar to REGIS forecasts. Office completions will rise substantially in 2H23. It expects mild increases to rental rates in Fort Bonifacio and Makati. Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 0.5% QoQ. Metro Manila condominium prices are generally 10%-20% lower than pre-COVID levels. Colliers expects Metro Manila condominium prices to rise 3% in 2023. This is similar to REGIS forecast.

PH ‘hot money’ records US$1mn net inflow in June; 1H23 net outflow reaches US$803mn

Transactions on foreign investments registered with the BSP (i.e. “hot money”) saw a net inflow of US$1mn, a turnaround from four consecutive months of net outflows. Improvement attributed to the easing inflation trend in both the Philippines and  the United States, as well as the pause in the Fed’s rate hikes. This brings 1H23 net outflow to US$803mn, a reversal of the US$778.3mn net inflow in the same period last year. Philippine economy was showing rapid recovery amid continued reopening, but there was marked volatility in both domestic and international markets, particularly in the United States due to high inflation.

 

P138bn in projects get green lane certificates

Four projects worth P183bn have received green lane certificates from the BoI, indicating significant investments in sustainable projects, including floating solar, data centers, and common towers. SunAsia Energy's floating solar panel project on Laguna Lake tops the list with a P66 billion investment to produce 1,300 megawatts (MW) of power. Nayon Kontrol System Solar One Inc. will establish a P15 billion, 250-MW floating solar project in Caliraya, Laguna. Philtower Consortium Inc. plans to build 7,907 built-to-suit common towers valued at P52 billion for telecommunication infrastructure.

 

Narra Technology Development Park's P50-billion hyperscale data center in New Clark City, Tarlac, aims to support robust, scalable applications and boost digitalization in the Philippines. Eight more projects worth P78.5 billion are under evaluation, including floating solar projects, a micro-hydroelectric plant, a reinforcing steel bars producer, and a section mill project. Projects expected to generate at least 19,250 jobs in total, according to Trade and Industry Secretary Alfredo Pascual. The green lane certification is part of the government's efforts to improve ease of doing business and streamline government processes for strategic investments through Executive Order 18.

Govt. revenues hit P1.9 trillion in the first half

The National Government's revenues grew by 7.7% Yoy to P1.9 trillion in the first six months of the year, driven by higher economic activity and digital transformation in revenue agencies. Tax collections in the January-to-June period rose by 7.5%, while nontax revenues grew by 9.1%. The improved revenue collection attributed to the digitization efforts of the BIR and Bureau of Customs. The government is targeting to raise P3.729 trillion in revenues this year, equivalent to 15.2% of GDP. The BIR Commissioner expressed hope in meeting the P2.6-trillion collection target for this year, emphasizing improved services for taxpayers. The proposed P5.768-trillion 2024 national budget will be submitted to the House of Representatives on August 2, with priorities on infrastructure, food security, digital transformation, and human capital development.

Tax collected from POGO firms increases

The BIR collected P8.88bn in taxes from POGO firms in 2022. This is 127% higher than the P3.91bn collected in 2021. The break down is roughly as follows; Withholding taxes from POGO workers – P4.35bn; Gaming tax – P3.65bn; Other taxes – P878mn. PAGCOR recently raised the capital requirement for POGO firms to P100mn, from P15mn previously. POGO service providers will also be required to meet the same capital requirement pay the same fees as the POGO firms. The new rules will likely prevent the practice of sublicensing. However, Regis does not expect the higher capital requirements to meaningfully shrink the industry. The POGO sector occupies about 800,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. This is lower than 1.4mn sqm as of end-2019. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 17% as of 2Q23. Regis estimates that POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

Gov’t debt service rises 98% YoY to P819bn in 5 months

The Philippines' debt service nearly doubled in the January to May 2023 period, reaching P819.5bn, a 97.92% YoY increase. Amortization of debt payments in the first five months of 2023 amounted to P589.952bn, up 204.72% YoY. Debt service payments was highest in February 2023 at P375.714bn, followed by P204.763bn in April 2023. In May 2023, the debt service contracted by 14.62% YoY to P49.047bn. Interest payments accounted for the bulk of the debt service payments in May, reaching P41.34bn, up 22.21% YoY.

 

Domestic interest payments in May were P29.529bn, while payments for foreign loans reached P11.815bn. Amortization payments in May 2023 reached P7.71bn, lower by 67.38% YoY. Meanwhile, domestic amortization payments in May were P2.656bn and external payments amounted to P5.047bn. The government’s debt stock reached P14.096tn in end-May, up 5.1% from end-2022. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.2% for 2023. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51.1% by 2028.
