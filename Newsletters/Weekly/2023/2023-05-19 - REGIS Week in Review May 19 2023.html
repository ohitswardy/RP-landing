REGIS REPORT

19 May 2023

The market rebounded +1.3% higher to 6,664.55 after the BSP kept policy rates unchanged at 6.25% and as hikes are likely to take a pause in the near term. Inflation expectations were likewise lowered to 5.5% (vs. 6% previously) for 2023 and 2.8% (vs. 2.9% previously) for 2024. 

Large caps lead the market as foreign inflows accelerated late in the week, mostly into banks and conglos. BDO (+3.3% WoW) and BPI (+1.2% WoW) saw US$3.2m and US$3.0m flow through while AC (+3.0% WoW) and GTCAP (+8.4% WoW) saw US$2.7m and US$2.4m. 

BLOOM (+0.4% WoW) also saw the strongest inflows at US$4.0m as it could be a potential candidate for inclusion in the PSEI, should the MPI (+0.4% WoW) tender offer follow through. Currently, MPI parent company First Pacific, commented that the price of Php4.63/sh is not yet final and will depend on a third-party valuation report. Approval for the tender offer is still pending from the PSE. 

Outside of rebalancing related flows, earnings helped boost the market.

GTCAP core profit jumped 46% QoQ and 67% YoY driven mostly by car sales. Toyota alone saw record high profits at P4.5bn (vs. P1.5bn in 1Q23 and P2.1bn in 1Q22) as sales volumes improve. 

JFC (+4.5% WoW) saw 1Q operating income are tracking well ahead of expectations. EBIT jumped 81% YoY to P3.6bn driven by margin recovery on domestic operations while international businesses such as Smashburger, Superfoods, and CBTL improve profitability. However, net profits seem to track behind as other income tends to be volatile.

Meanwhile, MONDE (+3.6% WoW) results fell behind. Core net profit dropped 12% YoY due to wider than expected losses at Quorn although its APAC BFB segment saw profits 5% higher as it recovered its market share in instant noodles and lower cost inventory started to come through.

Small cap consumer names PIZZA (+4.0% WoW) and MAXS (+3.2% WoW) saw similar trends. Both dining operators saw a surge in dine-in sales. Despite coming from a low base YoY, underlying demand remains healthy and operating margins are stable. 

Lastly, BPI declared Php1.68/sh cash dividends for 1H23. This could potentially indicate a dividend yield of about 3.2%. 

Headline turnover was slow at just US$70.2m (-11.4% WoW) while foreigners turned buyers for $84.1m.

Monde Nissin - 1Q23: Divergent trends across business segments
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q23 core NPAT -12% YoY as Quorn losses widen

MONDE’s core net profit declined 12% YoY to P1.9bn in 1Q23 – behind Regis but tracking within consensus expectations. The net profit underperformance was largely due to wider-than-expected losses at Quorn, even after excluding the P177mn one-time “restructuring cost” for the segment. The core APAC BFB business is broadly in line with expectations on healthy top line growth (+15% YoY). Segment margins are trending slightly behind but should catch up as input costs continue to come down.

APAC BFB: Margins recover sequentially as inventory costs come down

APAC BFB sales rose 15% YoY (see Fig. 2), led by biscuits (+19% YoY). Core instant noodles grew 10% YoY as MONDE continued to recover market share (see Fig. 3). Volume growth, however, appears to have normalized (+3%) given the relatively high YoY base. Segment margins are still down ~200bps YoY, but improved QoQ as lower-priced inventory (e.g. wheat) and the impact of the 2022 price increases (~8%) have started to come through. Proportionately lower A&P spend boosted EBITDA margins on a QoQ basis but this is expected to increase in succeeding quarters.

Quorn: Inflation pressures top line, margins

Inflationary pressures in Quorn’s core market (UK) led to a 10% decline in the total meat alternatives segment. While Quorn outperformed (sales -2% YoY) and gained market share, underlying demand was soft. In the US, sales declined 16% YoY as competitive pressures persisted. Inflation also pressured on the cost side, resulting in a P116mn EBITDA loss during the quarter.

Quorn restructuring to continue; equity infusion announced

Management announced a plan for a GBP40mn (PHP2.8bn) equity infusion into MONDE’s wholly-owned UK subsidiary to partially pare down debt (GBP34mn / PHP2.4bn) and cover one-time “restructuring costs” (GBP6mn / P420mn). The latter primarily relates to the closure of some facilities and reduction of manpower across the UK and US. Of the total GBP6mn one-off cost for the full[1]year, P177mn (GBP2.5mn) was incurred in 1Q23.

The equity restructuring should have minimal impact to MONDE’s consolidated balance sheet, but management expects this to result in annual cost (e.g. opex) savings of GBP8-10mn (P560-700mn) beginning 2024, as well as a reduction in the negative carry on its debt.

 
LT Group - Bank profits mask weakness of tobacco unit
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q23 down 2%, broadly in line

LTG reported a 1Q23 income of P6.380bn, -2%YoY, broadly in line with estimates, after a higher-than-expected profit from PNB offset the weak contribution from PMFTC. LTG’s profits from tobacco declined 28%YoY as volumes fell 31%, while contribution from PNB jumped 1.6x, helped by a P2.6bn gain on asset sales.

Tobacco profit falls short, down 28%YoY; illicit trade rising

LTG’s share of profits fromFortune fell to P3.047bn (-7%QoQ, -28%YoY) as cigarette sales fell to 6.6bn sticks (-13%QoQ, -31%YoY). Unit market share dipped to 59.5% from 60.1% in 4Q22. Management pointed to industry-wide price increases amidst a high inflation environment for the depressed demand, but their product portfolio (skewed towards more expensive brands) also contributed to underperformance relative to competition. Volume gains from the low-priced Chesterfield offering were not enough to offset the much bigger declines of Marlboro.

Management also highlighted that illicit trade, by their estimates, was about 14% as of December 2022 (from under 5% in 2021) and could have gone even higher in 1Q23. The successive price increases over the last few years have further incentivized smuggling and illegal production especially in a high inflation environment. Pricing strategies around the annual tax increase will not likely be as straightforward given the mounting pressure on demand.

PNB profit jumps on ROPA gains; provisions dip sequentially

PNB’s contribution jumped to P2.720bn (1,678%QoQ, 69%YoY) in 1Q23. Loan loss provisions dropped sequentially (from P6.5bn in 4Q22 to P1.6bn in 1Q23), while it booked another P2.6bn in ROPA gains. Gross NPL ratio slightly climbed to 6.82% in 1Q23 from 6.82% in 4Q22, albeit remaining much lower than 1Q22 (10.86%). NPL coverage was steady at 90%.

Earnings from beverage units fall as volumes for alcoholic and non-alcoholic segments soften

Tanduay’s contribution fell to P256mn (-58%QoQ, -22%YoY), as liquor volumes fell 6% to 4.8mn cases. GPMs were steady (13%), but opex was 8% higher due to A&P. Asia Brewery contribution, meanwhile, dipped to P75mn (-51%QoQ, -6%YoY). Without going into detail, management said that volumes across most products declined, a reversal of the very strong sales trends in 4Q22 (+30% Tanduay, +15- 20% for ABI).

 
Megaworld - 1Q23 residential reservation sales surge but office occupancy drops
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q23 profit climbs 33%

Megaworld’s 1Q23 headline profit climbed 33% YoY to P4.1bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. While a P660mn forex gain on US$ debt contributed to the bottom line, operating profit of P5.4bn (+15% YoY) was also ahead of REGIS forecast. Despite a lower proportion of revenue coming from lots, residential GPM was flat YoY at 49%, thanks to higher contribution from Bonifacio projects.

Reservation sales surge, led by Fort Bonifacio projects

Reservation sales surged 71% YoY (+19% QoQ) to P39.6bn, 6% higher than the 2019 average. Demand for projects in Fort Bonifacio was particularly strong. Moreover, international buyers, over 90% of whom are OFWs, comprised 25% of total sales, from only 15% in 1Q22 (25% in FY22). Similar to other developers, the United States was the largest international market for Megaworld. Management said that reservation sales remained strong in April.

Office occupancy drops; negative rental reversions begin

Office occupancy fell 2ppt QoQ to 90% as BPO tenants continue to implement work-from-home policies. Moreover, some tenants have renewed contracts at lower rental rates. This is the first time that management mentioned negative rental reversions but we expect more such cases in 2023 and especially in 2024, since the contracts up for renewal were previously signed in 2018/2019, when office rental rates were close to the peak. On a more positive note, a 69,000sqm Fort Bonifacio office building scheduled for completion in 4Q23 is now 60% preleased, from only 50% as of end-2022.

Mall revenue jumps, occupancy to rise further Mall revenue jumped 73% YoY, thanks to the full removal of rental concessions. Furthermore, mall occupancy is slated to rise from the current 90% to 92%, as tenants that have signed contracts start to open their stores.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - Operating leverage, international turnaround drive 1Q23 performance
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q23 operating profit jumps 81% YoY

JFC reported headline net profit of P2.1bn for 1Q23, down 11% YoY. Removing pref divs and coupons to perps, we estimate net profit to common was at P1.8bn. This is trending behind our FY23 estimates due to lower-than-expected “other income”, which tends to swing on a quarterly basis. Underlying operations, however, are trending above expectations with revenues +29% YoY and operating profit +81%. The strong operating performance was driven by (1) operating leverage from the strong top line growth, and (2) a turnaround in international operations.

Strong top line momentum in 1Q23; China recovers sharply

Group revenues rose +29% YoY to P55.1bn in 1Q23, led by the Philippines (61% of sales), which posted SSSG of 32%. While this was boosted by price increases in 2022 (+10-12% by our estimate), underlying demand continued to be strong. Meanwhile, China recovered sharply as lockdown restrictions were lifted, with SSS rebounding +12% after contracting for most of 2022. This implies China SSS levels have recovered to pre-pandemic levels.

Operating leverage, international turnaround drive operating profit

The strong top line during the quarter led to the continued improvement in operating margins both QoQ and YoY even as raw material costs remained elevated. The international business also turned a profit (at EBIT level), driven by the turnaround in Smashburger as well as improving profitability of CBTL and Superfoods.

Momentum continues to be positive even as YoY base normalizes

Management expects the positive top line trends to continue in the coming quarters, though Group SSSG will likely settle at 7-10% (Regis FY23E: 7.6%). Margins should continue to improve as (1) average inventory costs come down, (2) the full impact of the 2022 price increases is felt (negligible price increase in 1Q23), and (3) operating leverage continues to come through. Given the trends, we believe operating profit could come in closer to the higher end of management’s implied guidance of P12-13bn for FY23 (Regis FY23E: P13.9bn).

 
GT Capital - Rebound in auto margins, trading gains at the bank boost 1Q23 profit
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q23 core profit up 67% YoY to P6.42bn – Ahead of forecast

Core income jumped 67% YoY, 46% QoQ to a record high of P6.42bn, on track to beat our FY23E profit forecast (P16.7bn) and consensus estimates (P18.7bn). Apart from the better-than-expected results of Metrobank and Metro Pacific, the auto unit also performed surprisingly well.

Auto unit posts record earnings, amidst robust demand and favorable FX

Standalone profits for Toyota rose 4x QoQ and doubled YoY to a record of P4.51bn. Wholesale volumes rose 28% YoY, faster than retail sales volume growth of 21% YoY, as Toyota restored inventories at dealerships, which were depleted last year. Favorable FX and the 7-8% cumulative price hikes from Jan 2022 drove GPMs higher by 580bps QoQ (+410bps YoY) to 14.5%. Lower advertising and sales promotion expenses also helped boost operating profit, as industry supply remained tight.

Given that (1) the PHP:USD rate has weakened by 3% so far in 2Q from end-March 2023 and (2) retail volumes for April (+3% YoY, -16% MoM) were affected by public holidays, profits may soften sequentially but should still be much better when compared to 2022.

Property: Profit soft, despite strong equitized income from JVs

Profits from its property unit fell 16% QoQ, 8% YoY to P286mn, even though equitized income from JV projects (i.e. Seasons Residences and Grand Hyatt Residences) surged to P685mn (+325% YoY, +61% QoQ). Management blamed the weak revenue bookings on low 2020 sales and heightened cancellations at its projects in the Manila Bay area. Management indicated it intends to book another one-time gain from the transfer of land assets to to its joint venture firm with Nomura (“FNG”) in 2Q and start launching new projects by 4Q.

Leverage ratios continue to improve

Parent level net debt (including prefs) went down to P68.7bn by end-1Q23 from P72.1bn in end-4Q22, with net debt-to-equity down to 55% from 60%. Management plans to exercise its call option on the P4.8bn Series A perpetual prefs in October, avoiding the coupon step-up.

 
Alliance Global - 1Q23: Revenues elevated across all segments; selling costs temper liquor
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

1Q23 profit +20% - in line

AGI reported a 1Q23 net income of P4.678bn (12%QoQ, 20%YoY), in-line with expectations. Outperformances from property, gaming and QSR segments offset the shortfall from liquor. For Emperador (EMI PM) in particular, higher logistics costs and other selling expenses tempered profits relative to a 26% growth in revenues. On sequential basis, AGI benefitted from markedly lower expenses (-72%QoQ) at the parent (typically interest expenses, losses/gains on investments) which dropped to more normal levels.

Casino GGR surges on mass market; Golden Arches systemwide sales +35%

Travellers generated a profit of P334mn, a reversal of the P207mn loss in 1Q22, on the continued recovery in GGR (P8.9bn, -2%QoQ, 36%YoY). While VIP still accounted for a bigger share of revenue (62% vs 35-40% for other operators), it was the mass segment (2%QoQ, 87%YoY) that drove GGR in 1Q23. Increased visitation similarly lifted non-gaming revenues (1%QoQ, 93%YoY).

Golden Arches’ 1Q23 profit also grew to P419mn (62%YoY) as revenues jumped (38%YoY) and GPM remained stable (23.6% in 1Q23 vs 23.8% in 1Q22). Systemwide sales grew 35%YoY, while SSSG was 28.1%.

Marketing and other costs temper revenue growth from brandy and whisky

EMI’s 1Q23 income grew 10%YoY to P2.3bn. Despite overall revenues growing 26% (brandy +28%, whisky +24%), EBITDA and profit only increased 10%. While commodities have eased, inflation pressures continue to be felt especially in packaging and logistics. Selling expenses also surged with the reopening of outlets and travel retail. A&P for both brandy and whisky jumped 60%YoY.

Property segment jumps on Fort Bonifacio projects, strong mall revenues and an fx gain Megaworld’s (MEG PM) bottom line outperformed estimates on the back of a higher contribution from Fort Bonifacio projects and a 73% jump in mall revenue. While office occupancy slightly dipped (2ppt QoQ), it also recorded a P660mn fx gain in the period. Net income jumped 33%YoY to P4.1bn.

 
Shakey’s Pizza - 1Q23: Balancing growth and profitability
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1Q23 net profit surges 2.6x YoY

PIZZA’s 1Q23 surged to P201mn from P76mn in the same period last year. This is tracking broadly in line with expectations, though revenues (+94% YoY to P3.1bn) are ahead. Operating profit grew at a slower pace (+83% YoY) as margins contracted 60bps YoY. Operating leverage partly cushioned margin pressure from higher input costs.

Return of dine-in drives SSSG

Revenues jumped 94% YoY, boosted by the consolidation of Potato Corner (PC). SSS (ex-PC) grew 29% driven by the continued recovery in dine-in. Dine-in sales (60% of total) jumped 2x YoY albeit coming from a low base (e.g. Omicron surge in 1Q22). While same-store sales have recovered to pre-pandemic levels, we estimate average sales per store is still slightly below (<10%) pre-pandemic, as hybrid work arrangements persist.

Operating leverage cushions gross margin hit

Gross margins contracted 400bps YoY, pressured by higher input cost (e.g. potatoes), dilution from smaller brands (e.g. Peri-Peri), as well as heavy promotions. The company raised prices by low single-digit during the quarter to partly mitigate input cost inflation, but it also undertook promotional activities, thereby sacrificing some margin. The strong top line, however, led to operating leverage cushioning the hit to GPM.

Momentum sustained; rolling over TP to P13/sh, maintaining Buy

Management is encouraged by the strong top line trends and continues to see sequential improvement in early 2Q. Input cost pressures are expected to persist in the near term, however, and management has implemented another round of price increases (low single-digit) in May, which should lead to some gross margin relief in succeeding quarters.

We adjust our 2023-24E EPS estimates by 6-7% and roll over our DCF-based TP to P13/sh (+8%). Our TP implies 2023E multiples of 22x PE and 12x EV/EBITDA. Notwithstanding the pressure from input cost inflation, we believe the strong top line momentum should continue to drive near-term earnings growth. Key downside risks relate to inflationary pressures which could negatively affect profitability and consumer sentiment. Maintaining Buy.

PLDT gets P2 billion from 1st tower transfer to Aboitiz

PLDT yesterday transferred 135 towers to Unity Digital Infrastructure Inc., the first batch among the 650 assets sold. For this tranche, PLDT received P1.9 billion, leaving it with P7.3 billion to collect from Unity once the remainder of the towers is closed.

 

First Gen submits highest bid in Casecnan hydro auction

First Gen, through subsidiary Fresh River Lakes, submitted the highest bid in the privatization auction for the Casecnan hydro. The Casecnan hydro is a 165MW run-of-river facility. It is also close to the Pantabangan dam, where First Gen operates the Pantabangan Hydro. First Gen’s bid was US$526m, equivalent to US$3.2m per MW and ~70% higher than the next highest bid (US$299m). First Gen has roughly 2,000MW in gas plants and controls about 1,300MW of geothermal and other RE assets through EDC. It also controls FG Hydro – 133MW Pantabangan-Masiway  hydro plant in Nueva Ecija.

BPI declares P1.68/sh cash div

BPI declared a cash div of P1.68/sh for 1H23. Assuming they pay the same for 2H23 (scheduled for November declaration / December payout), the annual dividend would be P3.36/sh. That would be equivalent to a payout about 42% of NPAT for 2022, and a dividend yield of 3.2% on today’s close of P105/sh. Recall that in 2022, BPI revised its dividend policy from a fixed PHP amount, to a range of 35-50% of prior year’s profit, putting this year’s payout smack in the middle of that range. In 2022, the bank paid out just over 40% of 2021’s profits in cash dividends.

Ayala putting up $1 billion data center, biggest in Philippines

STT GDC Philippines, a joint venture between Globe, ST Telemedia Global Data Centres and Ayala Corporation, will spend about $1 billion to put up STT Fairview, a 124-megawatt data center in Quezon City, to support the country in its pursuit to become a hyperscale hub. The STT Fairview data center campus has over 83,000 square meters of gross floor area and 4 buildings, and will cater to both hyperscalers and enterprises. STT Fairview would start running by 2025 with an initial capacity of 28 MW that can be scaled up depending on incoming demand.

First Pacific (142 HK) suggests MPI tender offer price not final

First Pacific (142 HK) said that the offer price as based on a “preliminary” report by an accredited third-party valuation provider and was not yet final. According to Stanley Yan, First Pacific executive vice president for group corporate development. “The final valuation report has not been released yet, it’s a work in progress by the third party”. Final report was still subject to the approval of the Philippine Stock Exchange. The current tender offer price of P4.63/sh did not take into account MPI’s 1Q23 financials. The valuation provider will have to update their analysis to reflect the 1Q performance. Final report will be released once the tender offer is formally launched after MPI’s annual stockholder’s meeting on June 6.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP pauses monetary tightening

The Monetary Board kept the interest rate on the overnight reverse repurchase facility at 6.25%,  in line with consensus expectations. The gap between the BSP’s policy rate and the Fed funds rate has narrowed to 1.25% from 1.5% previously. The gov’t cited (a) lower inflation expectations and (b) potential moderation in demand indicators in recent months. The BSP lowered its average inflation targets for 2023/2024 to 5.5%/2.8% from 6.0%/2.9% previously. BSP Governor Medalla said that they may continue to pause in the next 2-3 policy meetings. However, they will be mindful of the potential impact of El Nino and food prices and utility rates, as well as the soft global economy.

China to implement tariff rates for PH

The Customs Tariff Commission of the State Council announced on Monday that China will implement agreed tariff rates under the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) for certain imports from the Philippines starting on June 2 when the trade pact is set to take effect in the Philippines after the ratification process is completed.

Vehicle sales drop 17% MoM in April

Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI, fell by 17% MoM to 30,643 units in April. On a YoY basis, vehicle sales were up 22%. Note that CAMPI began including Chery in Sep 2022. This brings 4M23 sales to 127,927 units, up 28% YoY (vs 2023E target of 395k, +12% YoY). Toyota sold 59,328 units in 4M23 (+16% YoY), implying that it sold 14,123 units in April (+3% YoY, -16% MoM). To reach Toyota’s FY23 target of 188k units (+8% YoY), Toyota needs to sell 16k units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 53% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,014/sh.

President Marcos signs deal extending Malampaya service contract

President Marcos Jr. signed an agreement renewing the service contract for the Malampaya gas field by 15 years or until 2039. Service Contract (SC) 38, which covers the Malampaya gas field in offshore Palawan, was originally scheduled to expire on Feb 22, 2024. “We optimistically look forward to the continued production and utilization of the remaining reserves of the Malampaya gas field, as well as further exploration and development of its untapped potential”. The Malampaya gas field is the country’s only indigenous source of natural gas and is expected to be commercially depleted by 2027. It supplies over 20% of the electricity requirements of the Luzon grid.

 

SC 38 is owned by the Malampaya consortium: Razon-led Prime Infrastructure (45% stake), UC38 LLC of the Udenna Group (45%), and PNOC-EC (10%). The consortium is mandated to conduct geological and geophysical studies and drilling of at least two deep water wells from 2024 to 2029. The consortium is also required to conduct exploratory drillings outside the Malampaya production area but still with the Service Contract to retain the exploration areas. SC38 has fueled gas plants owned by First Gen (FGEN PM) and San Miguel Global Power (1,200MW Ilijan, expired in 2022).
