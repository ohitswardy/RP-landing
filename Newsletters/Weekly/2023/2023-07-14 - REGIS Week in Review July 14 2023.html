REGIS REPORT

14 July 2023

The market rallied sharply to 6,624.79 (+3.9% WoW) following the strength in global markets after US inflation figures turn out better than expected at 3% (a two-year low) for the month of June.

The slowing inflation figures triggered the start of a risk-on behavior as investors bet that rates could soon come off. Turnover spiked to US$83.5m (vs. US$55.9m last week) while foreign inflows rose significantly to US$47.3m.

Most sectors ended in the green.

With confidence rising that rates have peaked, the most interest rate sensitive of sectors (real estate) saw a return of foreign buyers. ALI (+7.1% WoW) and SMPH (+3.3% WoW) led on the back of foreign demand. ALI saw US$3.3m in net inflows while US$6.4m (net) entered into SMPH. Fundamentals have also been improving as mall profitability is back to pre-COVID levels and residential prices continue to recover with reservation sales hitting a record level in 1Q23. Office vacancies remains a risk although this accounts for just a small portion of the NAV for these two market leaders.

Banks continue be in favor despite NPLs notching up a bit in May to 3.55% (from 3.51%). BDO (+8.8% WoW) and BPI (+8.6% WoW) continued to register gains as foreign investors now have turned to banks as their market proxies over conglos.

Consumer names were mixed. JFC (+5.8% WoW) ran sharply as international operations are starting to pick up although URC (-1.1% WoW) lagged as the proposition to impose junk food taxes still weigh on sentiment. JFC saw inflows worth US$10.8m, being the top foreign bought stock for the week.

GTCAP (+3.1% WoW) led the conglomerates on the back of strong vehicle sales for the month of June. Vehicle sales reached 36,311 units (+27% YoY), 16,381 of which were from Toyota (+14% YoY).

The peso likewise strengthened to 54.41 (+2.23% WoW), all in line with the general fall of the USD against major currencies this week.

RESEARCH
Philippine Property - Overlooked positives, overblown concerns
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Operational rebound has been ignored

We turn more positive on property stocks, as they are reflecting, in our view, an unduly bearish outlook: ignoring the operational rebound of the mall and residential businesses, while being too focused on concerns about the impact of the office segment.

Despite reporting record-level mall revenue and residential presales in 1Q23, the PPROP has fallen 11% YoY, sharply underperforming the PCOMP (3% increase). While we expect office vacancies to worsen, the office segment comprises only a small portion of the NAV of the large caps. Furthermore, the office leasing operations of some developers are not performing as badly as the industry at large.

Mall REIT IPOs likely soon; residential trends mixed; office vacancy to worsen

Now that mall profitability has returned to pre-COVID levels, the anticipated decline in interest rates could lead developers to spin-off mall assets into REITs, which in turn could result in NAV increases of up to 20% for some companies.

Meanwhile, residential prices continue to recover, and reservation sales hit a record level in 1Q23. While we turn more positive on the segment, we refrain from being overly bullish due to modestly rising credit risks as a result of looser payment schemes and rising mortgage rates.

On the other hand, we forecast office vacancy to rise further as work-from[1]home crimps demand and new supply is added. Thus, we forecast negative rental reversions from 2023E to 2025E.

RLC is our top pick; prefer ALI among the large caps; upgrade SMPH to Buy

We forecast RLC to show the fastest earnings growth in the sector (relative to 2019) and, on our estimates, it would be the biggest beneficiary of a mall REIT spin-off. We also consider current valuations to be compelling (at 66% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 6.3X FY24E P/E).

Among the large caps, we prefer ALI due to our strong growth projections over the next three years (presales CAGR of 15%, earnings CAGR of 19%) and the NAV enhancement we expect from infrastructure projects adjacent to its land bank. The stock is also trading at 52% discount to NAV, 1.2X FY24E P/B, and 13.3X FY24E P/E, all of which are close to historical trough valuations.

Meanwhile, we also upgrade SMPH to Buy as we forecast 16% earnings CAGR (until 2025E) following record results for mall revenue and residential presales.

We raise the RLC target price by 7%

Despite rolling over to 2024E, we made only minor tweaks to most of our target prices. However, we raise our target on RLC by 7% as we lower our mall cap rate by 1ppt (to 8%). See pages 9-10 for changes to our estimates as well as our recommendation table. Key risks: POGOs, interest rates, and REIT listings

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ACEN completes sale of ACEHI Netherlands stock

ACEN disclosed that a subsidiary ACEN Renewables Internationa Pte Ltd (ACRI) completed the sale of 2,424 ordinary shares representing 24.24% of the outstanding capital stock of ACEHI Netherlands B.V. (ACEHI Netherlands) to Star Energy Oil & Gas Pte Ltd. ACEHI Netherlands is a Dutch holding company that has a 19.8% ownership interest in the Salak and Darajat geothermal plants in Indoenesia. SEOG is part of the Star Energy Group, which is ACEN’s current partner for the Salak and Darajat geothermal plants. With this sale by ACRI of shares in ACEHI Netherlands, ACEHI Netherlands has become a jointly controlled entity by ACRI and SEOG. Salaj and Darajat Geothermal is a 655MW power plant in West Java and accounts for 13% of ACEN’s NAV and 8% of its operating capacity.

 

Federal Land unveils township development plans

Federal Land plans to develop 10 master-planned, multiuse properties as part of its Federal Land Communities product lineThe initial properties are set to lay the groundwork for the . development, all of which are planned to be unveiled within the next few years. For its 600-hectare mixed-use development, Riverpark, located in General Trias, Cavite, Federal Land plans to create a central business district. Initial roads and infrastructure works are expected to be completed by the end of the year.

 

The first product is set to be launched in September, under the JV with Nomura Real Estate (Federal Land has a 66% stake in JV with Nomura). SM is also set to develop a regional mall within the site during the year and will be launching its own residential brands. Federal Land has a joint development agreement with SM, wherein it will sell 100 hectares of land in Cavite (only 39 hectares has been sold so far). Federal Land is a wholly-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 5% of profit and 22% of gross asset value. GTCAP is trading at a 53% discount to its market-based NAV of P1118/sh and at 5.1x FY23E PER.

AboitizPower subsidiary APX ties up with UGEP for solar projects

AboitizPower Corp., through its subsidiary AboitizPower Distributed Renewables, Inc. (APX), has partnered with Upgrade Energy Philippines, Inc. (UGEP) to pursue solar rooftop projects in the Philippines. UGEP is focused on the development, engineering, procurement, and construction of renewable energy projects. The group is committed to developing 500 MW of utility-scale and 100 MW of commercial-scale renewable energy by 2030. AboitizPower and UGEP aim to expand solar rooftop and ground-mounted solar projects for commercial and industrial businesses in the country.

 

The partnership aims to provide sustainable and customer-focused solar energy solutions that meet the unique requirements of power consumers APX, AboitizPower's subsidiary, is involved in the development and operations of AboitizPower's distributed energy resources and facilities. APX operates under AboitizPower's renewables brand Cleanergy, which has a target of reaching 4,600 MW of renewable energy capacities by 2030. The joint venture will leverage UGEP's capabilities to encompass the development, construction, and long-term operations and maintenance aspects of solar projects. The partnership aims to accelerate customer-centricity and support the country's energy transition goals. UGEP sees the joint venture as an opportunity to promote the widespread adoption of renewable energy and provide customized solutions for business efficiency and environmental stewardship.

ACEN. Norway joint venture

ACEN Corp. plans to enter a joint venture with Norway's investment fund Norfund to provide solar power to telecommunications towers in the Philippines. The proposed joint venture aims to fund projects by YMP Telecom Power, Inc. to offer sustainable and affordable telecom tower solarization solutions in the country. Norfund is Norway's investment fund for developing countries, with a government commitment of 26.9 billion Norwegian krone ($2.5 billion).

 

YMP is a leading Southeast Asian company in distributed renewable energy, focusing on decarbonizing telecom operators and tower companies by designing, installing, and maintaining solar and solar-hybrid power plants. ACEN and Norfund finalizing the terms of the joint venture, with definitive agreements to be signed later. ACEN's executive committee also approved a one-year extension of the maturity date for a P7.4 billion loan provided to Greencore Power Solution 3, a special-purpose vehicle managing the Arayat solar project. ACEN aims to achieve 20 gigawatts of attributable renewable energy capacity by 2030 and currently has around 4,200 megawatts of attributable capacity across several countries.

SMC says court rules vs ERC on power rate hike

The Court of Appeals has granted San Miguel Corp.'s (SMC) motion for certiorari, voiding, the Energy Regulatory Commission's (ERC) decision to deny its subsidiaries' electricity rate hike petition. The decision pertains to the rate hike petition jointly filed by SMEC, SPPC, and Manila Electric Co. (Meralco) and denied by the ERC on Sept. 29 last year. The ruling favors San Miguel Energy Corp. (SMEC) and South Premiere Power Corp. (SPPC), subsidiaries of SMC, and sets aside the ERC's decision for "grave abuse of discretion." ERC plans to file a motion for reconsideration through the Office of the Solicitor General.

 

The Court of Appeals also grants provisional authority and interim relief for price adjustment to SPPC and SMEC, without prejudice to further requests for adjustments. Meralco will consult with lawyers regarding legal remedies, including a possible appeal to the Supreme Court. Meralco will negotiate with power suppliers for emergency power supply agreements to ensure continuity of electricity supply to help avoid exposure to volatile WESM prices. To help replace any capacity that they might lose if SMEC terminates PSA with Meralco. The Court of Appeals grants a permanent writ of preliminary injunction in favor of SPPC but denies SMEC's motion for partial reconsideration. Consumer groups express concerns about the court's decision and plan to file a motion for reconsideration. The case stems from SPPC and Meralco's request for a rate increase due to surging fuel costs, which the ERC denied, stating that the power supply agreement is a fixed-rate contract.

FGEN to buy first LNG cargo, to commission FSRU in September

First Gen Corp said it decided to award the tender to procure a single cargo of liquefied natural gas to Shell Eastern Trading (Pte) Ltd. For delivery between August 1 and September 30. The floating storage and regasification unit in Batangas is set to commission in September. In June, FGEN announced that it was in talks with Enrique Razon’s Prime Infra for the lease and operation of its LNG storage and regasification terminal that is construction. No definitive terms have been set. FGEN might lease out the facility to Prime Infra and then source its LNG requirements from Prime Infra instead of running it themselves.

Semirara’s Unit 3 extends power outage

Southwest Luzon Power Generation Corp. (SLPGC) announced it is extending the unplanned outage of Unit 1. Unit 1 has been on outage since June 17 due to turbine tripping. The company is still evaluating the cause of the excessive turbine movement. Repair and work are targeted before the end of August. Insurance brokers and adjusters of SLPGC have been informed of the matter and are currently working with Plant Management to process the filing of the insurance claims. Coordination with power industry regulators and other key stakeholders are likewise underway.

 

Regis estimates SLPGC Unit 1 (net selling capacity of 135MW) cumulative outage days by end-August could reach 86 days. Assuming Unit 1 goes on planned outage for 40 days by year-end, the plant availability factor could fall to 66% (vs. forecast: 68% or total outage days of 120 for the year). With the outage, SCC’s power division’s net selling capacity to the spot market will go down to 337MW (64%) from 472MW (71%) previously. The two 135MW units of SLPGC accounts for 3%/5% of FY23E profit/EBITDA.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

GIR dips to US$99.8bn in June

The country’s dollar reserves (GIR) dipped to US$99.8bn in end-June from US$100.6bn the previous month (revised from US$100.3bn). This is also the first time in four months that the GIR level went below US$100bn. The government cited national government’s debt payments and a drop in the value of its gold holdings, as prices fell. The end-June level is sufficient to cover 7.6months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects dollar reserves to hit U$100bn by year-end.

Banks’ asset quality ratios deteriorate further in May

NPL ratio (ex-interbank) rose to 3.55% in May from 3.51% in April and 3.26% in end-Dec 2022. In nominal terms, NPLs rose 1.9% MoM, slower than +3.1% in April. Past due ratio rose to 4.17% from 4.13% in April and 3.8% in end-Dec 2022. Past due loans rose 1.3% MoM to P525bn in May vs. end-Dec 2022: P479bn. Meanwhile, restructured loan ratio improved to 2.46% in May from 2.58% in April. NPL cover dipped to 101.8% in May from 103.3% in April. The NPL data seems at odds with the commentary from management of banks Regis covers.

 

During their respective 1Q23 briefings, all four banks suggested that asset quality ratios were steady into 2Q23, and all claimed that there was no meaningful stress among borrowers. This was likely due to consumer loans, higher yielding/higher NPLs, are growing faster than total loans. As of 1Q23, consumer loans grew 17% YoY (vs. +10.5% for overall banking sector). Consumer loan NPLs dipped 15bps QoQ to 6.39% from 6.54% in end-2022.

Trade deficit narrows to US$4.4bn in May

The country’s trade deficit shrunk to US$4.4bn in May, down 21% YoY and 9% MoM due to a rebound in exports (+2% YoY) and a sustained drop in imports. Imports fell 9% YoY due largely to a drop in the value of fuel imports (-32% YoY) and raw materials (-12%). Regional gasoline and diesel prices were down by 39% YoY in May. Consumer goods imports surged by 25% YoY buoyed by passenger cars, home appliances, and food. Capital goods imports declined by 6% YoY on lower telecom equipment and electrical machinery. Exports rose 1.9% YoY in May, snapping a consecutive five-month decline due to a recovery of exports to China (+23% YoY). For 5M23, the trade deficit is unchanged YoY at US$23.99bn. The government is forecasting a trade deficit of c. US$60bn for 2023 (~14% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 stood at US$58.244bn (14% of GDP).

Vehicle sales rise 27% YoY in June

Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI, jumped 27% YoY in June (-5% MoM) to 36,311 units. This brings 1H23 sales to 202,415 units, up 31% YoY. Toyota sold 16,381 units, up 14% YoY but down 8% MoM. Regis estimates Toyota’s market share dipped further to 45.1% in June from 46.8% in May. This bings 1H23 sales to 93,575 units, up 17% YoY. To reach our target of 192k units for FY23 (+10% YoY), Toyota only needs to sell 16.4k units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP) and accounts for 28% of profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 54% discount to its market-based NAV of P1095/sh.

Yellow alert in Luzon grid triggered by tripping of San Lorenzo plant

The National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP) issued a yellow alert in the Luzon grid on Tuesday from 1pm to 4pm due to a tripping of the San Lorenzo Natural Gas Power Plant Modules 50 and 60 worth 267MW, according to the DOE. Data from NGCP says the available capacity stood at 12,705MW while peak demand in Luzon was 12,222MW. A yellow alert is issued when the operating margin is insufficient to meet the transmission grid’s regulating and contingency requirements. Generators on forced outage total 1,897MW while generators with de-rated capacities total 787MW.

BIR exceeds 5-month collection target

BIR announced that it surpassed its collection target for the January to May 2023 period. The BIR collected a total of P1.056 trillion during the first five months, exceeding the collection goal by P1.331 billion. Compared to the same period last year, the BIR achieved a 9.94% growth in collections, equivalent to P95.454 billion. BIR Commissioner Romeo Lumagui Jr. expressed confidence in reaching or surpassing the annual collection target for 2023. The BIR has set a collection target of P2.599 trillion for the entire year of 2023, a 10.95% increase from the previous year's collection.
