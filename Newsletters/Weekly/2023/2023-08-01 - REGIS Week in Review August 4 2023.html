REGIS REPORT

01 August 2023

The market fell sharply back to 6,450.8 (-2.6% WoW) this week, as heavy outflows during the PSE rebalancing Friday pushed the index lower.  The rebal weighed on the index, despite no changes to the PSEi composition.

The selloff was broad-based with losers outnumbering gainers 6:1.

Index stocks AEV (-10.0% WoW) and JGS (-12.1% WoW) fell sharply, with most of their declines occurring on rebal, as the lack of liquidity exacerbated the moves.

Banks ended mixed. BPI (+1.7% WoW) stayed resilient this week, while  BDO (-4.1% WoW) and Metrobank (-4.7% WoW) closed lower despite reporting solid 2Q earnings.  BDO’s 2Q23 profit hit an all-time high of P18.7bn (+13% QoQ) as NIM expansion continued.  On the other hand, Metrobank reported 1H23 NPAT of P20.9bn, up 34% YoY, also in line with Regis estimates. Growth was boosted by a surge in net interest income. 

Meanwhile, property names continued to outperform. ALI (+3.3% WoW) continued to play catch-up this week and was the top net foreign bought stock for the week (US$21.5m net inflows).  Earnings in 2Q, while falling in line, showed strong growth of both residential and commercial lot revenues.  Reservation sales picked up, growing 10% QoQ.

Megaworld (+0.5% WoW) and SMPH (+0.4% WoW) saw outflows worth US$16.7m and US$6.0m respectively.

Power names likewise reported earnings. Meralco (-7.7% WoW) reported core income of Php19.2bn for 1H23 (+47% YoY), well ahead of Regis full-year estimates. Profits were driven by the generation business, with Singapore genco PacificLight significantly contributing to profit. ACEN (+3.2% WoW) saw profits rise 24% YoY helped by a net-sell position to the spot market and higher overall tariffs.

Headline foreign inflows were strong at US$235.1m, boosted mostly by an RCBC transaction earlier in the week, which boosted Sumitomo Mitsui’s stake in RCBC to 20%. This transaction saw inflows worth US$268.8m. Excluding this, the market saw outflows worth US$33.7m.

Manila Electric Company - 2Q23: Another strong quarter for generation
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Generation continues to deliver; distribution picks up

1H23 net income of Meralco came in at P17.9bn (+36% YoY), while core income was P19.2bn (+46% YoY). The 1H core income figure is on track to handily beat both Regis and consensus estimates. Contributions from power generation continue to be robust (as in 1Q23) and were supplemented by improvements in its retail supply business (“RES”). The core distribution business is picking up but still lags.

Distribution volumes rise 4.7% in 2Q; no progress on tariff review

Volumes for its power distribution business were up 4.7% YoY in 2Q23, an improvement over 1Q23. The commercial segment is seeing a recovery (+9.4%) but industrial volumes are still down (-1.3%). There is no visible progress on Meralco tariff reviewfor the 5th Regulatory Period, as the attention of the ERC is on more pressing matters. While there’s a supposed target of completing the review by 1Q24, we are skeptical that schedule can be met.

Generation and retail supply perform

Meralco’s unregulated businesses accounted for 43% of core income. All major generation subsidiaries delivered significant YoY growth in core profit despite outages in 2Q, with PacificLight still the largest contributor by far. The RES business, on the other hand, “contributed positively” in the period, as contract revisions allowed margins to turn positive. Meanwhile, Meralco’s entry into the telco tower business, MIDC, which began operations recently, lost an undisclosed amount of money and is likely to continue doing so for a few years.

Dividends

Meralco declared regular dividends of P8.52/share after the 1H results, 50% of its core EPS for the half. This takes the total dividends for this calendar year to P19.55/share. On today’s share price, that’s an annual dividend yield of 5.5%.

 
Aboitiz Power - Higher spot prices, WESM exposure boost income in 2Q23
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q23 jumps 40%QoQ to P10.4bn - ahead

AP’s 2Q core profit posted a significant jump to P10.4bn (40%QoQ, 69%YoY). While unit energy sales were up modestly (3%QoQ, 17%YoY), the larger increase in profit arose from higher power prices, arb opportunities from trading and better plant availability. GNPD’s beneficial EBITDA in 2Q23 jumped 23%QoQ due to the same factors. The 2Q23 number brings 1H23 core profit to P17.8bn (95%YoY), tracking mildly above Regis (P34.8bn) but well ahead of consensus (P29.4bn) forecasts for FY23E.

Generation: higher prices, arb/trading opportunities and plant availability

Core income from Generation & RES jumped to P10.3bn (43%QoQ, 50%YoY). Although energy sales in 2Q23 increased to 9,011GWh (3%QoQ, 17%YoY), the bigger impact came from higher overall prices as well as trading/arb opportunities, both of which contributed to better margins. Average selling prices to the spot were at least 10% higher QoQ, which AP took advantage of as WESM dispatch grew (18% of capacity in 1H23 vs 16% in 1Q23).

In particular, beneficial EBITDA from GNPD jumped 23%QoQ to P5.4bn on improved availability and higher effective rates. Recall that contribution dipped in the early part of the year after it signed low-margin short-term contracts with Meralco. More recent contracts saw improved pricing and the merchant portion benefited from the WESM spike in 2Q23. Management pushed back the turnover of the GNPD plants to 1Q24 which means, from a financial reporting perspective, AP’s 2H23 profits will still be clear of depreciation and interest charges relating to the new plant as it ramps up.

Distribution: 2Q23 volumes up 7%YoY; delayed pass-through of lower power costs lifts income

Distribution income grew to P1.7bn (10%QoQ, 110%YoY). While management did not disclose the gross margin in the period, we estimate that it remained close to P2.00/kWh, well above the typical P1.65-1.70/kWh range. The cost of power has been declining so the timing difference between the billing of generators vs its billing to customers (which squeezed its DU margins last year) has been favorable. Margins should nevertheless normalize downwards in succeeding periods as volatility lessens. Underlying volumes across its utilities were higher by 7%YoY to 1,579GWh in 2Q23, with residential (6%YoY) and commercial & industrial segments (7%YoY) growing at similar clips.

 
BDO Unibank - 2Q23 momentum points to upside risks to forecasts
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

In line, but sequential trends suggest room for surprise

BDO’s NPAT in 1H23 came in at P35.2bn, up 47%, almost exactly half of our FY23E of P70.5bn (consensus P69.0bn). However, momentum seems to be building, with 2Q23 net profit up 13% QoQ to a new all-time high of P18.7bn, on NIMs that expanded another 22bps QoQ. We are mindful of asset quality, though, as BDO’s NPLs have been stable, defying a deterioration in sector-wide ratios. Should 2Q trends hold, we see upside risk to our (and Street) forecasts.

NIMs jump 22bps QoQ on loan mix, yield re-pricing

While loan growth was steady at 8% YoY, the mix improved, with consumer loans up 15% YoY, driven by credit card receivables (+23% YoY). Consumers now account for close to 24% of loans, compared to 20-22% pre-Covid. This, together with the raising of the credit card rate cap in February, saw loan yields surge 64bps QoQ. Meanwhile, although deposit competition remains tight, BDO’s high 75% CASA ratio kept deposit cost increase to a milder 26bps QoQ. All told, NIMs jumped another 22bps QoQ and 70bps YoY.

Fees, trading gains up; Opex under control as cost-to-income stays sub-60%

Non-interest income in 1H23 was drive by a 16% YoY growth in fee incomes, and a 40% jump in trading/fx gains. Opex was up 16.5% YoY, though this was due to a 53% surge in Taxes and Licenses due in large part to documentary stamp taxes (DST, or stamp duty) levied on time deposits, which in turn nearly doubled YoY. Despite that, BDO’s cost-to-income ratios stayed below 60%.

Asset quality steady/improving; but mindful of uptick in sector

NPLs BDO’s provision expense fell 17% YoY as NPLs were stable at 1.95% (from 1.98% in 1Q23). NPL cover edged up to 174% from 170% in 1Q. Notably, Restructured Loan (RL) ratio dropped sharply to 2.8% of total, from 3.3% in 1Q23 and from a peak of 5.7% in 2021. We are wary, though, that industry-wide NPLs are on the rise, with the latest print in May up 13bps from 1Q23, and up 30bps ytd.

 
Ayala Land - 2Q23: Strong residential and commercial lot revenue; presales pick up
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Management optimistic on residential demand

ALI’s 2Q23 net profit jumped 52% QoQ (40% YoY) thanks to the strong growth of both residential and commercial lot revenue (see Figure 2). As a result, 1H23 earnings rose 41% to P11.4bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Beyond the results, however, our key takeaway from the briefing was management’s optimistic outlook on residential demand.

2Q23 reservation sales pick up thanks to Bonifacio project

2Q23 residential reservation sales grew 10% QoQ (+21% YoY) to P30.6bn, 84% of the 2019 average. About P5bn of sales came from an Alveo condominium project in Fort Bonifacio, which was only launched in June. Since the project still has about P13bn of unsold inventory, it can still contribute to sales in future periods. Furthermore, management hinted that it might still launch another high-end condominium project in 2H23, which it believes could also sell briskly.

Unsold inventory drops to 22 months

Ayala Land’s unsold inventory level dropped to 22 months’ worth of reservation sales (from 24 months in 4Q22-1Q23). Despite this improvement, the company lowered its 2023 launch target from P110bn to P100bn in order to manage its inventory levels. This implies P61bn worth of launches in 2H23, from P39bn in 1H.

 

Cancellation rates remain elevated but should normalize in 2024

Cancellation rates remain elevated at about 10% of development revenue, higher than the 5%-7% during the pre-COVID period. While residential cancellations have started to moderate, cancellations on office-for-sale projects shot up due to the high industry vacancy rate. Management expects the cancellation rate to normalize by next year.

Commercial lot margins drop sharply

1H23 GPM on commercial lots dropped 21ppt YoY to 52% because most of the revenue came from newer townships. Since several commercial lots were launched from similar townships in the past year, we suspect that GPM will remain in the 50%-60% range over the coming quarters.

Mall revenue climbs 33% YoY on low-base effect; occupancy should improve

Mall revenue jumped 33% YoY to P5.2bn, equivalent to the 2Q19 figure. Occupancy should improve going forward as 89% of space was already leased[1]out as of end-2Q23, higher than the average occupancy of 83% over the period.

Management downplays threat of AI on BPO sector

2Q office vacancy was flat QoQ at 11%. When asked about the threat of artificial intelligence (AI) on the BPO sector, Ayala Land highlighted the BPO Association’s view that AI will augment human performance. Management noted, however, that there was a need for upskilling of Philippine BPO employees.

 
PLDT - 2Q23: Top line growth remains under pressure
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core income up 5% but revenue growth tepid

PLDT reported net income of P18.5bn for 1H23, up +10% YoY. Excluding exceptional items but inclusive of losses at Voyager, we estimate core income was P16.3bn (+5% YoY), which appears in line with our FY23 estimates, if not even slightly ahead. Reduced opex in 2Q and smaller losses at Voyager helped lift core income for the quarter (+16% YoY to P8.4bn) despite revenue that was as sluggish as 1Q.

Challenging revenue growth outlook

Mobile service revenue for 2Q dipped 2% YoY. We believe the softness is due to tighter consumer purse-strings, with mobile data traffic again virtually unchanged QoQ. Meanwhile, the revenue growth of home broadband slowed down to just +2% YoY in 2Q. With 2Q net adds of only 13k for the wired service, growth in 2H is likely to be close to nil.

SIMs representing 96% of revenue were able to register With the deadline for SIM registration already lapsed, PLDT said 52.5m mobile subscribers were able to register.

These represent 99% of its active subscriber base, and 96% of its revenue. The latter figure suggests there will still be a small, short-term hit to mobile revenues in 3Q.

Voyager losses dip

PLDT’s share of losses from Voyager were around P500m in 2Q, down around P200m from 1Q. Its digital bank (“Maya Bank”), still in the red, claims to be the largest digital bank in terms of accounts, deposits and depositors.

Outlook for 2H: revenue, income and leverage

Management reduced guidance on revenue growth to “low single digit” but kept its guidance for telco core income unchanged (P33.5 to P34.0bn). Net debt/EBITDA rose to just below 2.5x due to payment of carryover capex, but management expects this ratio to decline with more tower sales proceeds in 2H.

 
ACEN Corp - Merchant exposure, higher tariffs lift 2Q23
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q23 income jumps on sustained merchant position, improved tariffs

ACEN’s 2Q23 profit rose to P2.205bn (9%QoQ, 24%YoY) as its 1Q23 performance extended into 2Q23, helped by a net-sell position to the spot market. Higher overall tariffs added to sequential improvement. This was nevertheless tempered by a 21%QoQ drop in ‘other income’ due to lower fx gains. The 1H23 profit (P4.2bn, 94%YoY) marks a massive turnaround from last year’s performance, which was hit by purchases of expensive replacement power from spot and a mismatch between high fuel cost and contracted selling price. 1H23 is tracking in line with Regis (P8.6bn) and just mildly behind consensus (P9.6bn) estimates for FY23E.

Net-sell position expected to double in PH by next year

EBITDA improved sequentially to P3.459bn (16%QoQ, -14%YoY) on a sustained net-selling position to the spot market and generally higher tariffs. Overall output increased 4%QoQ to 1,113GWh, while RE output dipped 6%QoQ (the difference being the generation from diesel). For the half, management explained that ACEN was a net seller to the spot market in the Philippines by 334GWh (vs a net buyer by 14GWh in 1H22), while the new capacity additions this year (160MW Pagudpud wind, 44MW Arayat Solar Ph2, 283MW San Marcelino Solar Ph1 and 133MW Cagayan Solar Ph1) could double this figure on an annualized basis.

Meanwhile, average contracted tariffs saw a 14% improvement (to about P5.80/kWh), helped by retail contracts that are now trending in the P6-7/kWh range. WESM prices also peaked in April/May before the rains in June.

Other income dips to what should be a recurring level

Other income dipped to P1.4bn (-21%QoQ, -22%YoY), and is now more reflecting of what we estimate to be the most recurring portion of this line item - the coupons on redeemable preferred shares in project companies abroad. Other income was higher in 1Q23 due to fx and a one-time settlement.

2H23 likely to be stronger - +537MW in Phils, +1,141MW abroad

Apart from the pipeline about to come online in the Philippines, substantial additions from India (Masaya 420MW), Australia (520MW) and Vietnam (410MW attributable) are all scheduled to come within the year. Among them, the brownfield additions from Vietnam will have the more immediate impact.

BDO 1H23 P35.2bn, in line; strong quarterly momentum

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported headline NPAT of P35.2bn, up 47% YoY, broadly in line with Regis estimates (FYE P70.5bn), and ahead of consensus (P69.0bn). However, the quarterly momentum seems strong, with 2Q23 at a record-high P18.7bn, up 53% YoY, and up13% QoQ pointing to potential upside risk to our forecasts.

 

Customer loan growth was steady at 8% YoY, while deposit growth slowed to 11.5% YoY from 14.3% in 1Q23. CASA ratio dipped slightly to 75% from 76% in 1Q23 (was 85% in 2Q22). NIMs jumped another 22bps QoQ (+70bps YoY) to 4.69%, driven by a 64bp QoQ surge in loan yields. Deposit costs were up a much milder 26bps QoQ. This took NII up 29% in 1H23, with 2Q23 alone up 30% YoY and 6% QoQ. Non-interest income was up 11% in 1H23. Fee incomes grew 16% YoY, while trading/fx gains surged 40% YoY. Opex was up 17% YoY, driven by the Taxes/licenses line (+52% YoY), as time deposits (TDs) are levied documentary stamp taxes (DST, or stamp duty). TDs were up 86% YoY. PPOP was up 34% YoY, bang in line with our forecast. Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio at 1.95% (from 1.98% in 1Q) and NPL cover at 174% (from 170%). Notably, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio fell to 2.8%, from 3.3% in 1Q, and from a peak of 5.7% in 2Q21. Credit cost is normalizing at 53bps in 2Q23, with provision expense down 17% YoY (in line). Overall a solid set of results.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Government debt rises to P14.15tn in end-June

The government’s outstanding debt rose by P51.3bn (+0.4%) MoM to P14.15tn in end-June. his is up by P729bn (+5.4%) from end-2022. Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% of total at P9.7tn (+1.2% MoM). External debt accounted for 31.4% of total at P4.4tn (-1.4% MoM). In USD terms, external debt rose by 0.2% MoM to US$80.3mn. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of c. 61% this year, unchanged YoY. Under its medium-term fiscal program, the government expects the ratio to decline to 51% by 2028.

Fiscal deficit widens in June

The government recorded a budget deficit of P225bn in June, higher by 5% YoY. Revenues dropped 8% YoY in June, with tax collections lower by 4% YoY. Note that the government started collecting VAT on a quarterly basis this year vs. monthly previously. Expenditures fell 2.6% YoY, despite a 44% YoY jump in interest payments. The gov’t cited lower National Tax Allotment shares of local governments and slow payment or encashment of suppliers/creditors in various agencies. This brings 1H23 the budget deficit to P552bn, lower by 18% YoY. The government is targeting a budget deficit of P1.5tn by year end, equivalent to 6.1% of GDP.

 

DBM submits P5.77tn national budget to Congress

The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) submitted its proposed P5.768tn national budget for 2024. This is 9.5% YoY higher than the 2023 approved budget and is equivalent to 21.7% of GDP. Combined budgets for public works/transportation are up 3.6% YoY to P1.04tn (c. 18% of total budget). Defense budget hiked by 14% YoY but budget for health cut by 2.7% YoY. Under the proposal, the Department of Transportation (DoTr) is seeking P164bn in funding for the construction and maintenance of its seven railways. North-South Commuter Rail (NSCR) will receive P76.3bn, of which P53bn will be used to fund right-of-way (ROW) acquisition. Metro Manila Subway project will get an allocation of P68bn, of which P21bn will be for ROW and tax expenses. MRT-3 will obtain P10.3bn for its maintenance and rehabilitiation. It will also allocate funding for its portion of the LRT-1 Cavite extension and pre-construction works for MRT 4 and PNR South Long Haul.

House panel approves mining fiscal regime bill

The House Ways and Means Committee approved a new substitute bill establishing a fiscal regime for the mining sector. Large-scale metallic mining corporations within mineral reservations will be subject to a 3% royalty of gross output. Large-scale corporations outside mineral reservations will be slapped with a margin-based royalty on income (e.g. margins of 1-10% will be subject to a 1% rate. The rate could rise to as high as 5% if margins are above 70%). Small-scale miners will be imposed a royalty equivalent to 1/10th of 1% of gross output of minerals or mineral products. A windfall profits tax will also be slapped based on margins (e.g. miners with margins more than 35% up to 40% will be imposed a rate of 1%/ margins of more than 80% will have a 10% rate).

 

Ronald S. Recidoro, executive director of the Chamber of Mines of the Philippines, said its members representing large-scale contractors “feel that further increases (in tax) may not be necessary at this time”. Nonetheless, he told the committee that “the Chamber of Mines manifests its support for the aforementioned House bill”. The proposed new tax structure would result in a higher tax take from the industry “under a profitable scenario.”. The Senate has yet to file a mining fiscal regime bill.
