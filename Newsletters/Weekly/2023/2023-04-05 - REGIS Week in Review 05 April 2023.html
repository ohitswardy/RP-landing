REGIS REPORT

05 April 2023

The market failed to recuperate its losses from last Friday’s selloff, closing slightly lower at 6,488.51 (-0.2% WoW) to end the week.

As expected, trading remained thin ahead of the Easter holidays. Average daily turnover stood at US$70.2m only. Many regional markets were likewise on break this week. 

Better-than-expected Inflation figures came through at 7.6% for the month of March (vs. est. 8.0%), although this had minimal effect on trading.  

Biggest movers were AEV and JGS as both of which rebounded sharply after dropping 10% last Friday on foreign MOC sell baskets.  

Meanwhile, property names ALI (+4.5% WoW) and SMPH (+2.1% WoW) played catch up as the sector has continued to lag the index.

Biggest drags were MONDE (-6.0% WoW) and TEL (-5.9% WoW). Monde continued to lag as sentiment continued to be weighed by its equity restructuring and disappointing earnings while PLDT was sold down after its dividends ex-date

A new offering, UPSON made its debut last Monday, rising as much as 20% to Php2.90/sh from its IPO price at Php2.40/sh.

Foreigners were net sellers for the week for US$4.8m. Outflows were mostly in property names and telcos. ALI and SMPH saw outflows at US$5.3m and US$2.9m while TEL and GLO for US$4.3 and US$2.3m respectively. MONDE was also sold down by foreigners for $3.1m.

RESEARCH
Century Pacific Food - 4Q22: margins still crimped by input cost inflation
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

4Q22 margins hit by higher cost

CNPF reported FY22 NPAT of P5bn, up 6% YoY, and in line with Regis and consensus estimates. Revenues (+14% YoY to P62.2bn) and EBITDA (+6% YoY to P7.6bn) were likewise within expectations. Margins took a hit in 4Q22 as input cost inflation persistent, resulting in flattish NPAT for the quarter at P729mn (+2% YoY). This is despite revenues growing 12% YoY, led by the branded segment (82% of total), which grew 15% YoY.

Volume growth decelerated as consumers traded down…

While overall branded revenues continued to grow strongly (+15% YoY), much of the growth was driven by price increases (+10% average for the full year). This implies that growth across the core branded marine and meat segments decelerated to low-single digits (vs mid- to high-single digit in 3Q) as consumers continued to trade down amidst the inflationary pressure on household wallets.

…while high input and freight costs pressed on margins

Despite the price adjustments, EBITDA margin contracted 190bps due to the further rise of input costs, further aggravated by proportionately higher logistics costs. CNPF implemented a ~1% average price increase during the quarter, bringing the full year average price increase to +10%. Management expects to adjust prices upward further in 2023 by ~5%, in order for margins to catch up.

Margin recovery seen in 2023, but likely delayed

Management expects top line growth of low- to mid-teens in 2023 (Regis FY23E: +7%), to be driven by both price and a recovery in demand (i.e. volumes). However, we believe the margin recovery story for CNPF could be delayed given that (1) the company is still carrying high-cost inventory for key raw materials until 1Q23, (2) further pricing action is needed for margins to catch up, and (3) downtrading by consumers could persist until inflation moderates.

SMC Ilijan to resume operations as LNG terminal starts commissioning.

SMC president and CEO Ramon Ang said its 1,200MW Ilijan gas-fired power plant in Batangas in poised to resume operations with the first LNG import terminal in the country ready for commissioning. Ang said the gas to be delivered by the terminal could already power the 1,200MW Ilijan plant. “In a month it will be up and running, the 1,200MW first, and then by next year, the additional 1,300MW capacity”. Sources confirmed that the Philippines would receive its first LNG cargo in mid-April and will be used to commission the AG&P terminal.

 

Metro Pacific now sees full completion of CALAX by 2024

A unit of Metro Pacific Tollways Corp. (MPTC) said the target for fully completing the Cavite-Laguna Expressway (CALAX) is now 2024 if right-of-way (RoW) issues are resolved. About 35% of Ayala Land’s NAV is comprised of land in Cavite and Laguna.

Mitsui, Metro Pacific still in talks for strategic partnership

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and Mitsui continue to discuss the possible entry of Mitsui as a strategic investor and the structure of the conglomerate after the transaction according to the Philippine Star. Among the details still being threshed out are the different options for MPI after the transaction. Delisting has been discussed among other options; Another possible option is for MPI to increase its authorized capital stock to allow the entry of a strategic investor. MPI said nothing is final yet. As of March 16, MPIC said it has not entered into any definitive agreement with any strategic investor. In February, MPI and Mitsui committed US$600mn worth of joint infrastructure investments in the country. MPI’s public float based on outstanding shares is currently at 36.5%. It has 2.87bn shares under treasury and 6.93bn unissued shares out of its 38.5bn authorized capital stock.

CA upholds ruling favoring San Miguel Global Power

The Court of Appeals has upheld its decision to consolidate the cases filed by units of San Miguel Global Power one of which earlier secured an indefinite suspension of a power supply deal. The CA denied a motion for reconsideration filed by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), which wanted the cases to be tried separately because a decision in one is likely to affect the other. The ERC appealed the decision of the appellate court to consolidate the cases filed by San Miguel Energy Corp (SMEC) with that of South Premiere Power Corp (SPPC) on their power supply agreements with Meralco. The CA also upheld its decision granting a writ of preliminary injunction in favor of SPPC, denying the motion for reconsideration both filed by Meralco and the ERC. Another division of the CA meanwhile denied San Miguel Energy Corp’s (SMEC) petition for a preliminary injunction covering its contract.

U/KB loan growth moderates slightly to 10% in February

Preliminary data show that outstanding loans from universal and commercial banks (U/KBs) grew by 10% YoY in February, slightly slower than 10.4% in January. Loans to manufacturing, utilities, and retail trade grew at slower rates, offsetting faster growth in real estate and consumer loans. Meanwhile, M3 growth accelerated to 6.0% in February from 5.6% in January.

 

Government posts P106bn budget deficit in February

The government posted a budget deficit of P106bn in February, up slightly (+1.5%) YoY. This was a reversal of the P45.7bn surplus in January 2023. Revenue growth decelerated to -0.3% YoY in February, after rising 25.2% YoY in January. To reach the government’s target, revenues should average P315bn per month for the rest of the year. Expenditures were flattish (0.3%) due to the decline of the National Tax Allocation (NTA) shares of local government units resulting from the lower tax collections in 2020. Primary expenditures fell 1.7% YoY but interest payments jumped 21% YoY due to base effects. The 2M23 budget deficit narrower by 53% YoY at P60.6bn.

BSP allows digital identification and verification of bank clients

The BSP has allowed digital identification and verification of bank clients under the rules of customer due diligence. BSP Governor Medalla said that e-KYC (electronic Know Your Customer) will advance the BSP’s financial inclusion agenda

 

Customs collections beat 1Q23 target

The Bureau of Customs’ (BOC) collections grew 13% YoY to P80.1bn in March. This 11% higher than the P72.3bn target. In 1Q23, the BOC’s revenue grew 13% to P213.6bn and is 8% higher than the P197bn target for the period. The BOC credits the improvements to the agency’s priority program such as digitalizing the customs process, simplifying procedures, and curbing smuggling.
