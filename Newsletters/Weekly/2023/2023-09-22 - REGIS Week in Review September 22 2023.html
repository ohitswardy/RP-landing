REGIS REPORT

22 September 2023

The market ended just slightly higher at 6,142.79 (+0.27% WoW) as the index continued to bounce off the 6000 support levels.

Gainers and losers were even 1:1.

This week, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept policy rates unchanged at 6.25%, in line with the market’s expectations. BSP Governor Remolona, however, kept the door for a further hike in November open, and is dispelling the possibility of rate cuts in 1H24. Globally, the US Fed’s FOMC meeting yielded another rate pause but the statements emerging from it are still hawkish. Another rate hike seems to be coming again in Nov and rates are unlikely to be going down for another two quarters (or more).

Investors stayed cautious given the lack of catalysts. SMPH (+2.7% WoW) led the market on the back of strong foreign inflows worth US3.5m. According to the head of SMPH’s residential business, residential reservation sales are likely to reach record levels in 2023. Defensive stocks like Meralco (+1.6% WoW) also saw modest gains. MER saw inflows worth US$1.5m.

Most of the activity focused on the PSE’s announcement of an off-cycle PSEi rebalancing. MPI (+0.4% WoW) and AP (-2.7% WoW) will be removed from the PSEi as their public float falls below the minimum 20% threshold. Earlier this week, MPI disclosed that the total non-public shares tendered constitutes 97.2% of the company while AP’s float fell on the back of the buyback program and insider transactions. AP saw significant foreign outflows at US$15.8m.

Canned food manufacturer Century Pacific (+2.9% WoW) and gaming firm BLOOM (-2.8% WOW) will be included. The changes will be effective at the close next Monday, September 25.

Headline foreign outflows were still high at US$46.9m.

RESEARCH
Jollibee Foods Corp. - Sharper focus
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Maintaining Buy

The stage for JFC’s longer-term growth is being set with its sharper focus on four segments for global expansion: chicken, burgers, beverages, and Chinese cuisine, as it tempers its appetite for acquisitions. This narrowed focus should, in our view, allow JFC to better build scale by expanding its franchise network and thus improve profitability. With international operations now turning the corner, near-term margins should be resilient despite near-term input cost pressure. We are forecasting +15% 2023-25E EPS CAGR on the back of continued store expansion, healthy SSSG (+11% revenue CAGR), and a gradual improvement (+20bps) in operating margins. We are maintaining our Buy rating and our TP of P300/sh, which implies 2024E multiples of 30x PE and 12x EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers.

International operations turn the corner

International operations appear to be turning the corner after having been a drag on profitability since 2018. As a group, they contributed 9% of operating profit as of 1H23 (albeit a marginal loss at the net income level). The turnaround is partly a result of the business transformation initiatives undertaken during the pandemic, but also a function of improving scale and the strengthening of its brand equity (i.e. the Jollibee brand) in other markets. Performance across the various brands and geographies will be mixed in the near term but we expect aggregate contributions to improve.

 

Near-term challenges persist, but longer-term investments continue

JFC continues to execute its long-term strategies through the expansion of its store network despite the volatility from input costs as well as fluctuations in consumer sentiment. Amidst near-term headwinds, we expect margin support to come from (1) business transformation initiatives undertaken during the pandemic years, (2) improving scale as revenue expands, and (3) further reduction of losses from the international business. We are forecasting +15% EPS CAGR from 2023-25E to be driven mostly by top line growth (+11% revenue CAGR) and a gradual improvement in operating margins.

Adjusting estimates; maintaining TP (P300/sh)

We raise our operating profit estimates by 9% but trim our net profit estimates (-7-8%) due to (1) lower gross margin assumptions to account for higher input cost, and (2) reduced impact of “other income” (e.g. reversals of opex accruals) improvements in financial control enhance financial reporting and reduce income volatility from “one-off” items. We are retaining our DCF-based TP of P300/sh, which implies 2024E valuations of 30x PE and 12x EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers. Key downside risks primarily relate consumer demand, raw material prices, competition, and dilutive acquisitions.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Prime residential sales could hit record level in 2023

Jose Mari Banzon, head of SM Prime’s primary residential business, said that sales from January to August are “hitting record levels” and the company could achieve record sales in in FY23. Regis forecasts residential reservation sales to rise 10% to P112bn, a record level. For reference 1H23 reservation sales grew 15% to P68.5bn. SM Prime previously said that 3Q23 mall revenue could be higher than 2Q23 mall revenue, which would imply that mall revenue would also hit a record level. SMPH is trading at 39% discount to NAV, 2.0X FY24E P/B, and 20.1X FY24E P/E

Cebu Air to cut 2024 capacity growth guidance

Cebu Air (CEB PM) announced that it will need to lower its capacity growth guidance for 2024. This follows Pratt&Whitney’s (P&W) announcement of accelerated engine inspections. The recall will affect the number of its active fleet. Prior to this announcement, the company guided its fleet size will rise to 91 by end-2024 from 78 in end-2023. CEB did not provide new guidance. Approximately half of CEB’s fleet are NEOs but we are not certain how much of these will be affected by the recall. Regis forecasts assume 2024E pax volumes and ASKs will rise by 12% YoY and 24%, respectively. Every 5% cut in pax volumes could reduce FY24E EBITDA/profit forecasts by 23%/71%, respectively, ceteris paribus.

Manila Water proposes to extend expiration date of concession agreement to 2047

Manila Water submitted an application to extend the expiration date of its Revised Concession Agreement to July 31, 2047 from July 31, 2037. The application for an extension was made to coincide the RCA’s term with the company’s 25-year legislative franchise. The approval of the extension will not affect our valuation for MWC. Regis valuation already assumed an expiration date of 2047, which is the same date assumed under the Approved Business Plan during the most recent rebasing.

GTCAP to redeem series “A” perpetual prefs

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) will redeem P4.8bn worth of series “A” perpetual prefs, which has a dividend rate of 4.6299%. The perpetual pref’s redemption date is on October 27. Regis profit/cash flow forecasts assumes debt payments will not be refinanced. Earlier this year, GTCAP also paid P6bn worth of bonds that matured. As of end-June 2023, GTCAP’s parent-level gross debt (incl. prefs) stood at P86.4bn and cash was at P17.6bn. The net debt-to-equity ratio at the parent stood at a record low level of 51%. GTCAP is trading at a 49% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,103/sh and at 5.6x/5.1x FY23E/FY24E PER.

AC says 96% of ACEN dividend shares distributed by September 21, 2023

In a disclosure to the exchange yesterday, additional ACEN shares will be distributed on September 21, 2023 covering c.4% (or around 74mn shares) of the total property dividends to be distributed. The total percentage of the property dividends distributed as of September 21, 2023 would be around 96% (1,787mn shares). Ayala Corp (AC PM) said that it has distributed 92% of the common shares of ACEN Corp that it has declared as property dividends as of September 1, 2023. The balance to be distributed after September 21, 2023 is equivalent to another 74mn ACEN shares.

PSE announces changes to composition of PSE Index: MPI (-), AP (-), BLOOM (+), CNPF (+)

The PSE announced changes to the composition of the PSE Index. MPI and AP will be removed. The tender offer for MPI shares will lead to its delisting and the public float of AP has fallen below 20% due to insider buying and the company’s share buyback program. BLOOM and CNPF will enter the PSE Index. All other PSE Index constituents should see modest amounts of inflow. The changes will be effective at the close of September 25 (open of September 26).

Dito secures US$3.9bn financing

Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that its subsidiary Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel) signed a US$3.9bn loan facility, a 15-year long-term project finance facility. Regis understands bulk if not all of the funding will come from existing creditors (mostly Chinese banks). The proceeds will be used to “extinguish” US$1.3bn worth of short-term debt; the rest will be to pay contractors and fund its continuing network rollout. DitoTel has ~US$1.5bn of payables to contractors and ~$0.3bn of payables to affiliates. The US$3.9bn of funding facility will buy DitoTel time to achieve its profitability targets.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Remittances rise 2.6% YoY in July

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.6% YoY to US$2.99bn in July. In PHP terms, remittances were up just 0.8% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate for July was at 2.5% YoY. This brought the 7M23 remittances to US$18.79bn, up 2.9% YoY. This is tracking within the BSP’s FY23 growth target of +3% YoY (US$33.5bn).

Current account deficit narrows in 2Q23, 1H23; BSP lowers BOP deficit targets

The BSP reported the current account stood at a deficit of US$3.6bn in 2Q23. This is lower than the US$4.6bn deficit in 1Q23 and US$4.2bn in 2Q22. The government cited (a) narrowing trade deficit of US$16bn (vs. US$17bn in 1Q23 and US$19bn in 2Q22) and (b) higher receipts from trade services (e.g travel) and secondary income accounts. This brings 1H23 current account deficit to US$8.2bn, lower than the US$12.1bn deficit in the same period last year. Following its quarterly review, the BSP lowered its FY23E BOP deficit target to US$100mn due to a lower current account deficit forecast. The lower current account was due to a smaller trade deficit forecast and higher forecasts for travel receipts. This was offset by lower FDI and foreign portfolio investments forecasts.

Lower House approves mining fiscal regime bill

The House of Representatives approved on second reading House Bill no 8937, which seeks to establish a new fiscal regime for the mining sector. The bill seeks to raise the royalty rate of large-scale miners within mineral reservations to 4% (from 3% currently). For large-scale miner outside mineral reservations, a margin-based royalty scheme will be applied. A tax rate of up to 5% depending on margin (i.e. pre-tax income). A windfall profit tax of 1-10% will also apply to miners with net income margins of more than 35% to over 80%. The government projects to collect an additional P1.93bn in royalties and taxes every year.

BOP deficit narrows to US$57mn in August

The country posted a balance of payments (BOP) deficit of US$57mn in August. This is lower by 90% YoY (vs. Aug 2022 deficit of US$572mn) but higher by 7% MoM (July 2023: US$53mn). This brings YT-August BOP to a surplus of US$2.1bn, a reversal of the US$5.5bn deficit recorded in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$100mn by year-end.

BSP keeps rates but signals rate hike in November

The BSP maintained its key policy rate at 6.25% for the fourth consecutive month. It raised its 2023/2024 average inflation rate forecasts to 5.8%/3.5% (from 5.6%/3.3% previously). The gov’t cited spillovers from weather disturbances, rising global crude oil prices, and the PHP’s depreciation against the USD. In YT-Aug, inflation has averaged 6.6% and the BSP thinks inflation will remain above its 2-4% target range until October. BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. also signaled a hike on November 16. The magnitude of the increase will depend on how bad inflation will be. He also signaled that there will be no rate cut for the first two quarters of 2024.

DTI urges manufacturers to hold off on price hikes

The Dept of Trade and Industry (DTI) will meet with manufacturers of basic necessities and prime commodities to urge them to postpone price increases. The DTI confirmed 14 pending price increase requests from manufacturers covering 43 SKUs. These products include canned sardines, evaporated milk, powdered milk, coffee, instant noodles, bottled water, and canned meat. The planned price increases range from 1-5% for food items and 6-10% for non-food items. Manufacturers are opposing the postponement, citing increasing raw material and fuel cost.

Key Palace meeting to review rice price controls next week

Trade Secretary Alfredo Pascual said that the lifting of retail price ceiling on rice is being given “serious consideration”. The economic team will meet next week to discuss price controls on regular-milled and well-milled rice. Instead, the Department of Finance and National Economic Development Authority are proposing to temporarily slash the tariff rates for rice imports from 35% currently to arrest the surge in rice prices. Such a move can only be approved when Congress is in recess or after Sep 30.
