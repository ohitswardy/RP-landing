REGIS REPORT

06 October 2023

The market retreated to 6,259.95 (-1.0% WoW) as the inflation print for the month of September accelerated to 6.1% (vs. 5.3% estimate). As expected, rice prices were the main driver as it rose 17.9% YoY in September. Hence, the odds for a rate hike in the near term should increase.

The sell-off was broad based as losers outnumbered gainers 19:11.

Large caps dragged the market on the back of heavy foreign outflows. Headline foreign outflows stood at US$45.5m for the week while local funds were also seen selling the market.

Consumer names fell sharply. JFC (-3.7% WoW), URC (-0.9% WoW), and MONDE (-1.0% WoW) all declined on the back of the rising inflation.  

Meanwhile, property stocks ended mixed. ALI dropped 3.4% WoW although SMPH gained +1.3% WoW after recent weakness stemming from the government’s suspension and review of all Manila Bay reclamation projects.

Defensive names outperformed. TEL rose +2.3% WoW as it looks to build an even larger data center facility. Meralco (+0.5% WoW), ACEN (+6.3% MoM), and AP (+3.0% MoM) also outperformed. 

The PSEi also saw another off-cycle rebalancing last Tuesday, October 3. UBP (+0.7% WoW) was deleted and replaced by NIKL (-7.4% WoW).

Daily turnover for the week was still much slower at just US$84.0m (-65.4% WoW).

RESEARCH
SM Prime - Free option on reclamation projects; maintain Buy
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Upside from reclamation projects and REIT IPO; Buy

SMPH shares have fallen 9% since the national government announced the suspension of all Manila Bay reclamation projects (on August 10), underperforming the PCOMP by 6ppt. We reckon that current levels now reflect only current operations and assign no value to its reclamation projects. Conversely, we think there is significant upside should even at least one of them proceed. Beyond the land reclamation issues, significant value creation and a potential re-rating are also likely to result from a possible REIT spin-off. We reaffirm our Buy rating.

Risk to Paranaque reclamation now much higher; Pasay looks more likely

On 10 August 2023, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said that all Manila Bay reclamation projects were under review and were deemed suspended. In light of this, we believe that SM Prime’s 300-hectare Paranaque reclamation is unlikely to ever receive national government approval.

However, we believe the company’s 360-hectare Pasay reclamation project, which received national government approval in 2019 and has already begun physical work, is still likely to push through after the suspension is lifted.

Core business performing well

SM Prime’s Philippine mall business has rebounded remarkably from the pandemic. Its 1H23 Philippine mall revenue of P31.5bn is already 12% above the 1H19 figure. Furthermore, we estimate that same-mall revenue is about 105% of the 1H19 level, vs. about 100% for most other developers.

Cutting NAV and target price but maintaining Buy rating

We reduce our NAV estimate by 17% to P40.00/share as we remove the value of the Paranaque reclamation project. We maintain our assigned target discount of 10%, resulting in a target price of P36.00/share (-17%). The Pasay reclamation comprises P9.04/share (or 23%) of our revised NAV estimate while the existing businesses account for the remaining P30.96/share. Thus, at the current share price, we estimate that the stock only prices in the existing businesses but does not reflect the value of the reclamation projects.

SMPH currently trades at 25% discount to NAV, 2.0X FY24E P/B, and 20.0X FY24E P/E, all of which are below historical averages. Maintain Buy. Key risk: the government might cancel the Pasay reclamation project.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

SM Investments says logistics, geothermal to drive earnings growth

SM Investments projects that its portfolio investments will account for more than 10% of the company’s total earnings in the medium term. This will be led by its logistics and geothermal ventures according to Consultant for Investor Relations and Sustainability Timothy Daniels. As of 1H23, SM’s profit rose by 32% YoY to P36.5bn, of which portfolio investments accounted for 10% of total. SM is trading at a 13% discount to its market-based NAV of P948/sh.

Sandro Aboitiz to become CFO of AP in November 2023

In a disclosure to the exchange, AP said that SVP/CFO Liza Montelibano has resigned as part of a corporate reorganization and will transfer to City Savings Bank. Juan Alejandro Aboitiz will take the position as SVP and CFO in November 2023.

PLDT to add another data center

ePLDT President Victor Genuino told reporters the firm is looking into adding a 13th data center. Focus is to complete VITRO Sta. Rosa, its 11th largest data center by 2Q24 and locking in a location in NCR for its 12th facility. The 11th data enter can hold a total capacity of up to 50 MW. ePLDT wants to double the capacity of the 12th data center to 100 MW. PLDT presently has a data center capacity of 34 MW.

PH Resorts finds investor for gaming project

PH Resorts disclosed that it signed an MoU with AppleOne for an investment plan. “The MoU establishes broad parameters whereby AppleOne can make an investment in the subsidiaries, with the intention of obtaining at least a majority of the equity interest in the subsidiaries, or an asset purchase of the land and improvements of the Emerald Bay Project,” PH Resorts said. Emerald Bay is situated in Mactan, Cebu. The details of investment have yet to be determined. Meanwhile, PH Resorts said its two subsidiaries had restructured their existing outstanding indebtedness with China Banking Corp. (Chinabank) via the execution of agreements for the sale, leaseback, and an option to buy back certain land and improvements. AppleOne is a Cebu-based developer with projects like Sheraton Cebu.

Commercial banks’ loan growth slows to 7.2% in August; Domestic liquidity growth picks up in August

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 7.2% YoY in August, slowing further from 7.7% in July. This is the slowest growth rate since Dec 2021. Loans to production activities grew by 5.5%, moderating from 6.2% in July. This was due to contraction in loans for manufacturing activities, as well as moderating growth rates for utilities and trade activities. There was some offset by accelerating loan growth to finance (+6.1% YoY vs 3.1% YoY) and real estate (+5.7% YoY vs. 5.0% YoY) sectors. Consumer loans rose 22.7% in August (vs 22.6% in July). Regis is forecasting loans to grow by 10-12% for banks under Regis coverage. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth accelerated to 6.8% in August from 5.7% in July. This is also the fastest M3 growth rate in 12 months.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

LTFRB approves nationwide P1 jeepney fare hike

The Land Transportation and Franchising Regulatory Board (LTFRB) approved a P1 provisional jeepney fare increase nationwide. The minimum fare will be adjusted to P13 from P12. The fare hike will be effective by October 8. A provisional increase means the additional fare is temporary until the main petition for an increase is resolved. There’s no increase in fares for succeeding kilometers (currently at P1.80). Road transport fares have a weight of 3.5% in the CPI basket.

Departments of Agriculture, Trade propose lifting of price ceiling on rice

The Department of Agriculture and the Department of Trade and Industry have recommended to lift the price cap on rice according to Bureau of Plant Industry Gerald Glenn Panganiban. The government cited favorable indicators: decreasing rice prices in the domestic market, increasing domestic supply due to the harvest season, and the drop in global prices. Government records show that the supply of rice in the market is enough for 52 days by the end of Sep, rising to 74 days by the end of October. President Marcos Jr., who is the concurrent agriculture secretary, will decide on the matter. Last Aug 31, the government implemented price caps of P41/kg (US$730/MT) on regular milled rice and P45/kg (US$800/MT) on well-milled rice.

Marcos removes price cap on rice

President Marcos Jr. lifted the cap on rice prices. Marcos admitted the government still needs to address the staple grain’s high prices in Metro Manila. Marcos said he had instructed House Speaker Ferdinand Martin G. Romualdez to create a program for the capital region, where rice prices are “most volatile. The government implemented price caps of P41/kg (US$730/MT) on regular milled rice and P45/kg (US$800/MT) on well-milled rice on Sep 5.

Inflation quickens further to 6.1% in September

Headline inflation accelerated to 6.1% YoY in September from 5.3% YoY in August. Higher than consensus forecast (5.3%) and at the high-end of the BSP’s forecast range for the month (5.3-6.1%). On a MoM basis, headline CPI grew by another 1.1% in September. The biggest driver was rice, which grew by 17.9% YoY and 8.4% MoM (vs. August: 8.7% YoY and +4.1% MoM). Prices of vegetables remained elevated, rising by 2.5% MoM and 29.6% YoY (vs. Aug: +11.2% MoM, 31.9% YoY). There were offsets by favorable YoY base effects for electricity and petrol prices (base effect for petrol prices should start fading by Nov). Core inflation slowed to 5.9% YoY in September from 6.1% YoY in August.

 

This brings 9M23 average inflation rate to 6.6%, trending above the BSP’s average inflation rate forecast of 5.8% (BBG consensus median: 5.6% / high: 6.4%). To reach the BSP’s target, headline CPI should decline by -0.5ppts MoM for the rest of the year. If recent MoM trends persist (i.e. +1.1%), headline CPI could average 6.6% YoY this year. Vietnam rice spot price at US$642/MT (vs Sep average: US$631/MT).

Customs collections dip in September

The Bureau of Customs collected P79.225bn in September, lower by 0.06% YoY. Collections surpassed the agency’s target (P76.445bn) for the month by 3.64%. This brings 9M23 collections stood at P660.716bn, up 3.5% YoY and represents 75.6% of its FY23E target of P874bn (+1.4% YoY). Customs collections account for 23% of the national government’s revenues.

Short selling to begin on October 23

The PSE is targeting to launch short selling on October 23. Only constituents of the PSE Index, the MidCap Index, and the Dividend Yield Index will be eligible for short selling. An eligible security must maintain short interest to outstanding shares at less than or equal to 10%. Naked shorting will not be allowed and short selling of a stock can only be done on a price uptick.

Government debt stock rises 0.7% MoM to P14.35tn in August

The national government’s outstanding debt rose to P14.35tn in end-August. This is up by P105bn (0.7%) MoM primarily. The government cited depreciation of PHP against the USD (from P54.83 in July to P56.65 in August). Domestic debt dipped by 0.2% (P253bn) MoM to P9.79tn while external debt rose 2.9% (P127bn) MoM to P4.56tn. They cited upward revaluation of USD-denominated debt of P147bn, partially offset by downward revaluation of third-currency debt (P22bn). The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61% this year. As of end-June 2023, the debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 61%.

Marcos approval rating drops in latest Pulse Asia survey

The approval rating of President Ferdinand Marcos plunged by 15ppts to 65% in September. This came from an 80% score in June 2023. The biggest drop in approval rating (down 29ppts) came from the poorest, Class E. Meanwhile, the approval rating of VP Sara Duterte also dropped by 11ppts to 73% from 84% in June. The survey was conducted from September 10 to 14 among 1,200 respondents. It has a margin of error of ± 2.8%. The key issues that took place during the survey were the rice crisis, inflation, among others.

Dept of Finance: No sponsor yet for proposed tax on junk food

According to the DOF, no one in either the Senate or Congress has sponsored the proposed junk food tax and that no bill has been filed. In June, Sec. Diokno announced the govt’s plan to impose a tax on pre-packaged foods “lacking nutritional value” (P10 per 100g or P10 per 100ml). It also called for the expansion of the current tax on sweetened beverages. Sec. Diokno said the economic team may review its revenue outlook as many of the tax measures may not be passed this year and may propose expenditure cuts.

Tourist arrivals reach 4mn as of Sep 29

International arrivals have breached the 4mn mark as of Sep 29. This is 35% below 2019 levels. The government is targeting 4.8mn tourist arrivals this year or about 58% of 2019 arrivals (8.26mn). The government expects tourist arrivals to reach pre-pandemic levels in 2025. This suggests that tourist arrivals dropped MoM to 344k from Sep 1-29 (~50% of 2019 levels). September is seasonally a lean month.
