REGIS REPORT

07 July 2023

The market headed lower (-1.4% WoW) for the week on very thin volumes. Average daily turnover was  low at just US$55.9m (vs. US$90.5m last week), which are the lowest levels since December 2021.

The inflation print for the month of June came out better than expected at 5.4% (vs. 5.5% est.) and near the lower end of the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) expectation range of 5.3-6.1%. This latest data also reinforces the view that the BSP will have more room to reduce rates later this year. However, the figure was still largely ignored as equities remain out of favor globally.

Most sectors ended in the red. Power names AP (-7.4% WoW) and ACEN (-3.5% WoW) were the biggest drags while property and banks fell towards the end of the week. SMPH saw significant outflows at US$4.6m while US$0.6m for ALI, with renewed concern of a more hawkish Fed after strong jobs data print.  

Bright spots were consumer names JFC (+0.4% WoW) and URC (+0.00% WoW) following its sharp decline after the Department of Finance proposed an imposition of taxes on junk food a few weeks ago. Regardless, management outlook on the sector remains optimistic. JFC CFO Richard Shin says the company is aiming for system-wide sales growth of 15-20% for the year as conditions normalize across its markets. This is based on expectations of same-store sales growth of 7-10% across its portfolio, with international operations seeing an acceleration. Foreigners likewise bought US$1.5m worth of URC and US$1.4m of JFC. These were the top bought names by foreign investors this week.

The long-awaited, revised tender offer price for MPI’s delisting finally came out Php5.20/sh (+12% than previous offer price of Php4.63). The transaction is subject to shareholder approval. Should the desilting proceed, this will be followed by the entry of a new PSEi constituent. Investors speculate that BLOOM (-2.5% WoW) will replace MPI in the index.

Foreigners were net buyers early in the week although selling accelerated after the index broke below 6400 levels. Headline foreign outflows registered at US$7.4m. Following SMPH, ICT and TEL saw heavy outflows too at US$2.0m and US$1.4m respectively.

Century Pacific allots P3.5bn for expansion

CNPF is allocating up to P3.5bn for this year's capital expenditure (capex) to expand its business segments. The capex is higher than the company's usual allocation of P2 - P2.5bn. Funds will be mainly allocated to expand capacity in the meat, pet food, coconut, and other businesses, including packaging manufacturing.

CNPF expects to achieve double-digit top-line growth this year, although it may be on the lower end of the range (10%-11%) due to consumer headwinds in the first half. The company also anticipates mid to high-level single-digit growth in net income this year.  

ACEN approves infusion to units, names CFO

ACEN Corp’s board has approved an equity investment of $70mn in ACEN International Inc. and ACEN Renewables International. The investment is part of ACEN’s regular course of business.

ACEN also announced the appointment of Jonathan Back as chief finance officer effective January 1, 2024, replacing Maria Corazon G. Dizon who is set to retire on December 31. Mr. Back serves as ACEN’s chief strategy officer

Telcos welcome EO cutting red tape in infra permit process

The signing of Executive Order (EO) No. 32 by the President of the Philippines has been hailed by local telecommunications companies.

The EO aims to streamline the permitting process for the construction of telecommunications and internet infrastructure in the country. The issuance of the EO is expected to pave the way for greater digital transformation, bridging the digital divide, and increasing access to digital resources and opportunities.

The EO covers national government agencies, government-owned or -controlled corporations, and LGUs involved in the issuance of permits, licenses, clearances, certifications, and authorizations for telecommunications and internet infrastructure. It encourages transparency and efficiency across government agencies and local government units (LGUs) involved in the permitting process.

Telcos had campaigned for the extension of the streamlined procedure and the institutionalization of the joint memorandum circular (JMC) of the Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA). The streamlined permitting process will benefit infrastructure rollout plans and ensure faster, more affordable, and reliable services for the Filipino people.

The EO exempts certain permits and clearances, and mandates the establishment of one-stop shops for construction permits in cities and municipalities.

Covered agencies and LGUs are required to implement a zero backlog policy and comply with the Anti-Red Tape Authority. A Technical Working Group (TWG) on Telecommunications and Internet Infrastructure will be created to ensure efficient implementation of the EO, chaired by the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT).

Commercial bank lending growth slows to 9.4% YoY in May

Preliminary data showed loans of universal/commercial banks (UKBs) ex-reverse repos in May rose 9.4% YoY to P10.91bn. This is a further moderation from +9.7% YoY in April. On a MoM basis, bank lending inched up +0.4%.

Loans for production rose 7.9% YoY, slower than +8.3% YoY in April. Loans to manufacturing slowed sharply (+0.6% vs. +9.4% in April), as well as trade (+8.6%). Loans to real estate sector grew 5.5% YoY, though it fell by 0.4% MoM.

Meanwhile, consumer loans rose 22.7% YoY(+1.6% MoM), faster than the +22.3% YoY previous month. Credit card loans moderated slightly to 29% from 29.9% the previous month.

Auto loans rose at a faster rate of +4.1% from +1.9% in April. Domestic liquidity (M3 growth) was steady at +6.6% in May (vs 6.6% in April).

Inflation eases to 5.4% YoY but MoM trend accelerates

Headline CPI rose 5.4% YoY in June, moderating further from 6.1% the previous month. This came in slightly below consensus estimates (+5.5%) and at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range (+5.3%-6.1%). Same drivers of easing we have seen in previous months: base effects of food and fuel-related items such as transport and utilities.

On a MoM basis, however, headline CPI rose 0.2%, accelerating further from 0.0% in May. This was due to higher prices of food items (+0.3% MoM vs 0.2% in May), mainly rice (+0.6% MoM, rising for four consecutive months) and vegetables. Prices of alcohol were up +0.5% MoM in June, though decelerating from a +2.0% MoM increase in March 2023. Non-food items were muted (+0.0%), with electricity prices and fuel falling 1.3% MoM and -0.5% MoM, respectively. These offset rising prices for passenger transport services (air and sea).

Core CPI remained stubbornly high, rising by 0.2% MoM, 7.4% YoY. This was on the back of higher prices of fish and vegetables; Non food items: passenger transport (toll fees, air, and sea fares) and housing rental.

1H23 inflation averaged at 7.2%, still above the BSP’s 2-4% target range. Assuming CPI rises +0.2% MoM for the rest of the year, Dec 2023 inflation should already be c. 2%.

Newly appointed BSP Governor Eli Remolona said it will be “dangerous” to cut rates ahead of the Fed.

The next BSP policy meeting is on Aug 17 while the Fed’s next rate decision will be on July 27 (consensus: +25bps).

Government debt rises to P14.1tn in May

The National Government’s total outstanding debt rose by 1.4% MoM (+P185.4bn) to P14.096tn in May. This is also higher by 5.1% (P678bn) from end-2022. The MoM increase due to net issuances of domestic and external debt and the depreciation of the PHP against the USD. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.2% for 2023. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51.1% by 2028.

Domestic borrowings were 1.2% higher from the previous month at P9.59tn, accounting for 68% of total debt. External debt rose to P4.51tn, up 1.2% MoM due to net availment of external loans and the impact of the PHP depreciation against the USD.

Additional P100bn revenues seen from digital transaction tax

The Department of Finance (DOF) expects to generate nearly P100bn in revenues over the next five years from the proposed value-added tax (VAT) on digital transactions. The imposition of a 12% VAT on digital service providers aims to level the playing field between traditional and digital businesses. Digital advertising, subscription-based services, and other services using information and communication technology-enabled infrastructure are included in the plan.

The DOF's estimate assumes a 50% collection efficiency in the first year, improving to 70% by 2025 until 2028. Revenues are projected to reach P72.69bn with 50% collection efficiency over the five-year period and up to P145.37bn with 100% collection efficiency.

The tax system currently covers only traditional businesses, missing substantial revenues from the digital economy. Taxing non-resident digital service providers is seen as fair and equal treatment in taxation, ensuring competition and preventing erosion of the VAT base.

The government aims to collect taxes from the growing digital economy to fund public services, infrastructure, and socioeconomic programs. Neighboring Asian countries have also updated their tax frameworks to capture revenue from digital transactions, with VAT rates ranging from 5% to 11% on digital transactions.

Department of Transportation prefers 15-year concession life for NAIA, with option for 10-year extension

The Department of Transportation stands by its decision to lock the concession period for the NAIA privatization at 15 years. However, they have incorporated an option to extend it by another 10 years in the event that circumstances warrant (or if demand/passenger volumes breach the theoretical limit of 62mn per annum).

They want a total of four gateways to choose from for the Greater Capital Region: NAIA, Clark, San Miguel’s New Manila International Airport, and Sangley, according to Transportation Undersecretary Roberto Lim.

NAIA’s pax volumes reached 47.8mn in 2019; Pax volumes grew at a 6.5% CAGR from 2013-2019. A separate unsolicited proposal from a consortium is seeking for a 25-year concession. The proposal involves an investment of P267bn. NEDA is currently reviewing the two proposals. 

Manufacturing growth weakest since July '22

Philippine manufacturing growth slowed to a one-year low in June, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence. The manufacturing purchasing managers’ index remained above the 50.0 mark but dropped to 50.9 in June from 52.2 in May, indicating the weakest improvement in business conditions since July 2022.

Output and factory orders supported overall growth, but the rates of increase eased, and manufacturers reported weaker underlying demand trends.

Employment in the manufacturing sector fell, with some firms reducing their payroll numbers and not replacing voluntary leavers. On the other hand, no adverse impact on the overall level of unfinished worked, which remained unchanged in June.

Price pressures eased as input price inflation slowed, resulting in the softest rise in average selling prices in over two and a half years.

Manufacturers remain optimistic about production in the next 12 months, although the level of positive sentiment was weaker than the series trend.

Global headwinds could potentially dampen the outlook for manufacturers in the Philippines. The growth potential for manufacturing is affected by elevated rates and China's faltering recovery.

PH set to hit digital payments target

The Philippine payments system aims to convert 50% of retail payments to digital form by 2023. Digital platforms accounted for 42% of transactions in 2022, up from 30% the previous year.

In terms of value, digital payments represented 40% of all transactions, down from 44% in 2021. Total transactions were valued at $195 billion, with $78.17 billion conducted digitally. Government payments were predominantly digital, making up 96% of all transactions. Individuals and businesses made digital payments 55% and 10% of the time, respectively. Merchant payments, person-to-person transfers, and salaries contributed to the growth of digital payments.

 Ownership of transaction accounts, especially e-money accounts, has significantly  increased, indicating increased usage of payments, according to the BSP Financial Inclusion Survey Report.
