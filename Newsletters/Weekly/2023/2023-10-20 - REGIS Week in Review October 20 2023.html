REGIS REPORT

20 October 2023

The market fell below the 6200 level to 6,142.90 (-2.0% WoW), even as investors stay on the sidelines ahead of the 3Q/9M earnings results.

Volumes were thin as daily turnover was just at US$80.3m.

Most sectors fell as losers outnumbered gainers 9:1.

Banks were the biggest drags. BDO (-5.3% WoW) fell on heavy foreign outflows at US$6.2m. BPI (-2.2% WoW) likewise dragged despite posting net profits of P38.6bn for the 9M23 period, coming in slightly ahead of expectations. For 3Q alone, profit of P13.5bn was +33% higher YoY. The key macro trends continue to hold in 3Q: (1) loan growth decelerating, (2) NIMs improving and (3) NPLs creeping up. Outside of those, opex rose by a chunky amount (+21%) due to the bank’s investment in IT and manpower while non-interest income rose 15%.

Property names ALI (-2.2% WoW) and SMPH (-1.0% WoW) also fell as total office property vacancy in Metro Manila rose to 18% (+1ppt over 3Q). While conditions appear to still be deteriorating, the small silver lining is that office property demand is not contracting, despite the work-from-home and the decreasing demand for POGO offices. For the quarter, there was an increase in net demand for office space even if this was outpaced by new supply.

Defensives outperformed. MER (+0.7 %WoW) and the telcos GLO (+0.3% WoW) and TEL (+0.00% WoW) were resilient.

Headline outflows stood at US$23.8m.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ERC approves termination of Meralco-SMC deals

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has approved the termination of power supply deals between distributor Manila Electric Co (Meralco) and subsidiaries of San Miguel Corp (SMC) for 1,800MW of crucial supply that must be delivered by 2024 and 2025. ERC chair Dimalanta explained that the six-month “longstop date” or agreed time frame during which all conditions required for a transaction must be completed had already lapsed. In March 2021, Excellent Energy Resources (EERI) and Masinloc Power Partners agreed to supply Meralco with 1,200MW and 600MW of power respectively. The EERI was to source power from its LNG plant at P4.1462/kWh while MPPCL proposed to provide power via its coal plant at P4.2606/kWh. Both companies filed for contract termination in March this year, citing ERC’s delayed approval.

AC Motors, Bosch to open more PHL service centers

Ayala Corp’s AC Motors has tied up with German engineering firm Bosch in a deal targeting the opening of 60 service facilities in the next five years, with the immediate plan of opening 20 facilities within the first year. Currently, 12,000 Bosch Car Service centers are spread across 150 countries, with 12 of them in the Philippines. Bosch Car Service is said to offer one-stop-shop services covering mechanical repairs to electronic, engine systems, diagnostics and others.

PMFTC volumes down 27% in 3Q23

Philip Morris International disclosed last night that PMFTC’s cigarette volumes in the Philippines fell to 5.6bn sticks in 3Q, down 27%YoY but flat QoQ. The disclosure broadly attributed the volume decline to the performance of the Marlboro brand. This brings PMFTC volumes for 9M23 lower by 27%YoY to 17.8bn sticks and well below estimates. Industry volumes (10.5bn sticks, -19%YoY, 5%QoQ) are again down by less than PMFTC’s volumes and showed a sequential recovery. PMFTC’s unit market share fell 2.7ppts QoQ 53.3%. PMFTC raised retail prices of Marlboro, Fortune and Jackpot by around 15% in February this year to an average price of about P120/pack. These were meant to cover the increase in excise taxes at the start of the year (from P55/pack to P60/pack).

 

The impact of the price hike could have spilled over into succeeding quarters and worsened by competition from illicit tobacco. Company has also talked about how illicit trade as a % of total industry may be back to the teens (from under 5% at the end of 2021). LTG paid out virtually its entire share in PMFTC’s earnings last year as dividends (P15.1bn, P1.40/share, 14% yield) to LTG holders. Assuming the same payout, dividends this year may be closer to P12-13bn (P1.10-1.20/share) for an 11-12% div yield.

BPI 3Q23 up 33% YoY; PPOP up 16% despite slowing loan growth (8.1% YoY)

In a press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported headline 9M23 NPAT of P38.6bn, up 26% YoY, tracking slightly ahead of Regis forecast of P50.5bn for FY2023 and consensus at P49.5bn. PPOP, however was a shade behind, up 10% YoY for 9M23. NPAT was driven by a 60% drop in provision expense to P3.0bn (vs Regis FY of P5.95bn). BPI has guided for lowering provision expense despite slightly higher NPL ratio (1.97% vs 1.88% in 2Q), as NPL coverage remained high at 159% (vs 167% in 2Q23). While net interest income was in line, non-interest income dragged. NII was up 25% in 9M23 and up 19% YoY in 3Q23 alone, as continued NIM expansion more than made up for the slowdown in loan growth to 8.8% YoY in 3Q23 (from 10.5% in 2Q23).

 

While the quality of earnings was less than stellar, Regis notes the resilience of NIMs on growth of higher-margin consumer loans. More during the bank’s briefing on Monday, 23 October at 5pm Manila/HK/SG time.

 

Meanwhile, some highlights:

 

Loan growth slowed to 8.8% YoY from 10.5% in 2Q23, and down from 15.3% at end-2022. The deceleration was driven by corporate loans, which grew just 5.3% YoY, as big-ticket capex loans were delayed by high interest rates. Meanwhile, high-margin credit card loans were still elevated, surging 38% YoY, and auto loans up 22.3%.

NIMs jumped 54bps YoY in 9M23, by BPI’s estimates, driven by the improving loan mix. This, though, suggests a flattening out of margin gains, as the bank estimates 6M23 NIMs were up 56bps YoY. NIMs were still likely up QoQ, as NII was up 3% while loans were up just over 1% QoQ.

Non-interest income was up 15% in 3Q23. For 9M23, the headline drop of 7.7% YoY was due to a large land asset sale in 2Q22. That said, 9M23 non-interest income was still tracking behind our FY23E forecast. No details were provided in the press release.

Opex jumped 21% in 9M23 and 3Q23 on continued IT and manpower spending. This took PPOP up 16% YoY in 3Q22 and 10% in 9M.

Provision expense fell 60% YoY to just P3.0bn, despite a slight (but steady) deterioration in asset quality. NPL ratio crept up 9bps QoQ to 1.97%, and up 21bps YTD. That said, NPL coverage remains high at 159%.

Cebu Air to buy 100-150 jets worth US$12bn in biggest PH aircraft deal

Cebu Pacific plans to order 100-150 narrow body passenger jets worth up to US$12bn (based on currently published prices) to support its long-term growth according to CEO Michael Szucs. As of end-2Q23, the carrier’s fleet plan showed its fleet size will peak at 91 in 2024 and drop to 88 by 2028 as aircraft retires/leases expire. The company cited expectations of a significant improvement in aviation infrastructure once the Bulacan airport starts operating between 2026-2028. The company plans to send out an official request to Boeing and Airbus by end of this week. This will be a “winner-take-all deal” with the manufacturer that would offer the best package to be awarded the entire contract.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market declines in September

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 22bps MoM to 20.3%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 4bps MoM to 23.9%. BLOOM and CNPF replaced AP and MPI (which had low foreign ownership) in the PSE Index. Market net foreign selling accelerated to US$472mn from US$131mn in August. This was partly due to the removal of AP from the FTSE Index as well as the tender offer of MPI. Of the 28 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 21 saw declines in foreign ownership while 7 experienced increases

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

CNVRG – foreign ownership fell 66bps MoM to 19.1%.

TEL – foreign ownership declined 37bps MoM to 35.3%. After AP and MPI, TEL saw the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$15.9mn).

ACEN – saw the largest increase in foreign ownership, which expanded 33bps MoM to 25.5%.

GTCAP – foreign ownership expanded 15bps MoM to 19.0%. Net foreign buying (of US$5.4mn) was also the highest in the market.

Cash remittances rise 2.7% YoY in August

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 2.7% YoY to US$2.796bn in August, in PHP terms, remittances rose 3.5% YoY. This brought the 3-month average growth rate steady at +2.5% YoY. Similar to previous months, land-based remittances continued to drive remittances, rising by +3.2% YoY while Sea-based remittances were up just 1% YoY. The YT-August remittances rose to US$21.58bn, up 2.8% YoY, broadly in line with the BSP’s full-year target of +3% YoY (or US$33.5bn).

Marcos suspends Maharlika fund implementation

President Marcos, Jr. signed a memorandum on Oct 12 directing relevant departments to suspend the implementation of the IRR of the Maharlika Fund Law. According to Exec. Secretary Bersamin, the suspension stands “pending the further study” of the IRR. The Maharlika Fund will source its funds from the initial P125bn capitalization of the Maharlika Investment Corp., which in turn will be sourced from state-run banks (P75bn) and the national government. State-run banks remitted the funds to the Treasury on Sep 14 but soon after sought relief from compliance with the BSP’s rules on capital requirements. The request for relief was preemptive, considering that the contribution—if not considered part of DBP’s capital—would undermine DBP’s ability to comply with BSP regulations.

Agencies’ 9M23 budget usage rate hits 97%

The cash utilization rate of government agencies hit 97% at the end of September, behind the 98% recorded in the same period last year. P3.09tn of the P3.18tn worth of notices of cash allocation (NCAs) issued in 9M23 were utilized, leaving P83.5bn in unused allocations. NCAs are a quarterly disbursement authority, allowing agencies to withdraw funds from the Treasury to support their spending needs. NCA utilization comprises of cash disbursed and outstanding checks that have not yet been encashed.

Philippines to lose at least P60bn this year from illicit tobacco

House of Representatives Ways and Means Chairman Joey Salceda said excise tax collections from cigarettes fell by 7.8% to P160.4bn in 2022 from P173.9bn in 2021. “The biggest decline in excise tax collection since the enactment of the sin tax reform law in 2012”. The decline came amidst excise tax on cigarettes rising to P55/pack and average selling prices of c.P110/pack. Excise tax rose another P5 to P60/pack at the start of 2023 and scheduled to increase 5% annually beginning 2024. Mr. Salceda estimates the government revenue losses due to illicit trade in cigarettes at P60.6bn this year if the trend persists. Separately, BIR Commissioner Romeo Lumagui, Jr. said that the government stands to lose P30.57bn in revenue this year, rising to P42.54bn in 2027. Assuming the same payout, dividends this year may be closer to P12-13bn (P1.10-1.20/share) for an 11-12% div yield.

Balance of payments posts US$414mn deficit in September; End-Sep GIR level revised down to US$98.1bn

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) posted a deficit of US$414mn in September. This is lower than the US$2.3bn deficit in Sep 2022 but higher than the US$57mn deficit recorded in Aug 2023. The government cited net outflows from payment of the government’s foreign-currency obligations. This brings 9M23 BOP to a surplus of US$1.7bn, a reversal of the US$7.8bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a US$100mn BOP deficit by year-end. The BSP also revised the end-Sep GIR level to US$98.1bn from the previously reported figure of US$98.69bn. This suggests that the GIR dropped by US$1.5bn MoM (vs. end-Aug: US$99.568bn) and is equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports. The GIR could rise in October due to the government’s US$1.9bn retail Treasury bond sale.

 

LPC: Metro Manila office vacancy inches up in 3Q23

During its 3Q23 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Metro Manila office vacancy rose 1ppt QoQ to 18% due to an increase in office supply. Vacancy in Fort Bonifacio was flat at only 9%. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 20%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Additionally, Regis estimates that Metro Manila net office take-up was likely 300,000sqm-400,000sqm, tracking ahead of Regis FY23E forecast of 450,000sqm. Prior to the POGO industry entering the country, Metro Manila annual office take-up was 470,000sqm to 530,000sqm. The nationwide net office take-up fell 37% QoQ (down 38% YoY) to 129,000sqm. Meanwhile, the net office take-up of both traditional tenants and POGOs fell on a YoY and QoQ basis.
