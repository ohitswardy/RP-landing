REGIS REPORT

17 November 2023

The index gained +0.8% WoW to close at 6,211.9, although trading was generally mixed as losers and gainers were even at 1:1.

Turnover dropped to as low as US$23m at the start of the week, the lowest volume seen in more than a decade, as investors continued to stay on the sidelines ahead of the monetary board meeting last Thursday.

As expected, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept policy rates unchanged, following similar inaction from the Fed at the latter’s latest FOMC meeting and the surprise drop in Philippine CPI inflation for October. The monetary board has one more policy meeting before year-end, although another rate hike is highly unlikely unless there is a shock in the CPI print for November.

Given the lack of positive catalysts on the macro front, investors mostly focused on corporate earnings.

Conglos outperformed led by Ayala Corp (+3.2% WoW) as net income came in at Php32bn, +35% YoY, well ahead of consensus estimates (although below Regis FY23 forecasts). The two biggest units combined delivered earnings slightly better than expected. Earnings-wise, outside of the listed units, the highlight was the modest profit posted by AC Industrials.  On the other hand, GT Capital (+4.7% WoW) reported its 9M23 core income doubled to P23.25bn. Excluding the P3.2bn of gain from lot sales, core profit grew 80% YoY to P19.61bn after the share of prefs, trending ahead of Regis FY23E (P21.8bn) and consensus estimates (P22.87bn). The Toyota business beat expectations, with its 9M23 standalone profit surging 158% YoY to P10.88bn, already 90% of FY23E.

The consumer sector took a beating. Tighter purse strings due to high inflation and interest rates seem to be a common theme as consumers are trading down. Universal Robina (-1.1% WoW) 3Q23 revenues came in weaker-than-expected, with growth slowing to only +6%. Branded consumer food (BCF) sales took a hit as inflation concerns weighed on consumer sentiment across various markets. Meanwhile, Jollibee (+6.3% WoW) headline net profit for 9M23 (-6% YoY to P6.bn) is trending behind estimates, but operating profit of P11.9bn (+48% YoY) is well ahead of Regis estimates, as well as management’s own guidance (FY23 P12.6-13.2bn). 3Q SSSG was 8.8%, with Phils outperforming (+13%). EBIT jumped 43% YoY with strong operating results across both markets.

Biggest drag was large cap bank BDO (-2.7% WoW) on heavy foreign selling. BDO saw the most foreign outflows for the week at US$2.7m.

Turnover continued to stay muted at US$63.2m while foreigners turned net sellers for US$1.8m.

Universal Robina - 3Q23: Margin recovery mitigates soft top line
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M23 underperforms on soft 3Q top line
URC’s 9M23 net profit rose 4% YoY to P9.7bn, trending behind Regis and consensus expectations. 9M23 consolidated revenues rose 9% YoY to P117.6bn, while EBIT grew 17% YoY to P12.6bn. Revenues for the latest quarter were weaker than expected, with growth slowing to only +6%. The tax rate was higher in 3Q23 (17% vs 14% in 3Q22) and the company reported lower forex gains YoY. 3Q23 net profit was down 2% YoY to P3.1bn but operating profit jumped 19% YoY as the non-branded segment that outperformed during the quarter (segment revs +38% YoY / EBIT +36% YoY). Branded consumer food (BCF) EBIT rose 6% YoY.

Weak consumer sentiment weighs on BCF top line
Total (BCF) revenues were down 4% YoY in 3Q as inflation concerns weighed on consumer sentiment in most markets. In the Philippines, URC continues to see downtrading across categories. Its confectionery business was the most negatively impacted, with URC losing volumes to lower priced players. Other categories like instant noodles and snacks, however, continued to see good growth (high single digit to low double digits). Meanwhile, int’l BCF sales declined 7% YoY due to trade inventory correction following price and SKU adjustments.

Margins stabilize amidst volatile cost environment
BCF EBIT margins (see Fig. 2) were stable QoQ and improved YoY as average inventory costs ease. While prices of key commodities have stabilized, they remain elevated vs historical levels. URC did another round of price increases in late-3Q (low single digit) and will continue to implement other internal cost saving measures to keep margins afloat.

Margin recovery a priority amidst weak consumption
Margin recovery remains a priority though management is likely to pause price increases for now to give consumers some time to absorb the adjustments. Apart from price increases for packaged food, consumers are also dealing with high prices of staples (e.g. rice and vegetables). The company is seeing better trends in early 4Q, but the YoY base is high and the company is looking to reinvest some of the gross margin gains into A&P to drum up volumes. We believe recent wage hikes could provide consumers some much-needed support, but it may take another 1-2 quarters for them to fully adjust to higher prices.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - Operating leverage expands 3Q23 margins
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Margin expansion drives 3Q23 operating profit +43% YoY
JFC reported headline net profit of P6.8bn, trending behind Regis but broadly in line with consensus  estimates. However, core operations are performing well ahead of expectations with 9M operating  profit rising 48% YoY to P11.9bn. Excluding one-offs in the previous year, we estimate recurring NPAT to common (after pref divs and perp coupons) rose 5x YoY to P5.8bn. For 3Q23 alone, JFC posted recurring NPAT to common of P2.1bn, a turnaround from an estimated recurring net loss in the previous year. Revenues grew 11% YoY on the back of ~9% SSSG, while operating profit rose 43% YoY with an expansion in margins.

Philippines drives 3Q SSSG
The Phils outperformed in 3Q, posting SSSG of +13% driven mostly by samestore transactions (+7.7% in 3Q). Despite inflationary concerns to consumers, JFC saw continued growth in dine-in traffic and strong take up of its mix-andmatch meals. Meanwhile, international SSSG slowed to 2.6% due to macro concerns in certain markets, such as China and Vietnam. CBTL (-2.6%) and Superfoods (-12%) saw SSS declines, while SSSG in China slowed to 1.9%.

Operating leverage boosts margins
Revenues were up 11% YoY in 3Q23 but EBIT rose much faster (+43%) as margins improved QoQ and YoY. Inventory cost as a proportion of sales stabilized during the quarter as JFC reaped the benefits of price increases in previous quarters. For 3Q alone, the price increase was minimal (<1%). Much of the margin expansion, however, was a result of operating leverage given the strong top line. This was most apparent on the international side, with segment operating profit rising over 2.5x

Top line trends in the Phils holding up despite inflationary pressures on
consumer; likely to exceed management guidance
Top line trends in the Philippines continue to be sustained in early 4Q according to management, in line with seasonality during the holidays. Management acknowledges that it is likely to exceed its own guidance to grow operating profit by 20-25% in 2023 (Regis FY23E: +52%), but remains cautious given various macro headwinds in some international markets.

 
Robinsons Land: 3Q residential presales remain strong; mall and hotel profits at record levels
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

9M23 earnings rise 31%
Robinsons Land’s 9M23 net profit grew 31% to P8.8bn, ahead of both REGIS and consensus forecasts. The outperformance was largely due to faster-than-expected revenue recognition of residential JV projects as well as a 5.5ppt drop in the effective tax rate (to 11.8%). We suspect, however, that the unusually low tax rate is partly due to a timing issue and will rise substantially in 4Q23, as it did in 4Q22.

Strong residential demand for standalone and JV projects
Although presales of RLC’s standalone projects fell 29% QoQ to P4.9bn, we still consider this a strong performance since it is similar to the 2019 level. For the 9M period, presales jumped 64% to P17.3bn, 15% higher than the pre-COVID period. Management credits its robust residential performance to a larger sales team as well as stronger marketing efforts, particularly overseas. As a result, nearly half of residential sales come from international buyers (mostly OFWs). Demand was also strong for RLC’s JV projects, which are generally higher-end than its standalone projects. Presales of such projects rose 102% in 3Q23 (+81% in 9M23).

Mall margins above pre-COVID levels
3Q23 mall revenue rose 4% QoQ and 13% YoY to P4.0bn, similar to pre-COVID levels. However, the EBIT margin rose 12.4ppt YoY to 38.2%, well above the 2019 level of 32%, thanks to lower power costs. As a result of the higher margins, mall EBIT climbed 68% YoY in 3Q23 to P1.5bn, c. 20% higher than the pre-COVID  level.

Office occupancy drops 2ppt QoQ
Office revenue fell 3% QoQ as office occupancy dropped 2ppt to 87%. A BPO tenant downsized its office space due to a global mandate requiring it to increase the proportion of work-from-home.

We believe a key risk to the office segment is a Bill in the House of Representatives which seeks to allow BPO firms to avail of tax incentives even if employees are fully work-from-home (WFH). However, RLC is of the view that most of its office tenants are already at their desired levels of WFH and would not downsize significantly going forward. 

Hotel revenue hits another record
After hitting a record in 2Q23, hotel revenue grew another 11% QoQ (+114% YoY) to P1.2bn, double the 3Q19 figure. In addition to the revival of MICE events, RLC expanded its hotel portfolio by 27% over the past year. The company completed two luxury hotels in the past year- Fili Urban Resort Hotel (in Cebu) and Westin Manila Sonata Place Hotel.

Cebu Air - 9M23 outperforms; 2024 capacity growth guidance tempered
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M23 core profit reaches P4.85bn – Ahead of forecast
Core income (i.e. excluding one-off/MTM gains, FX adj, interest on CBs) was up 8% QoQ to P1.82bn in 3Q23, bringing 9M23 profit to P4.85bn, a reversal from last year’s P10.91bn loss. The results are trending ahead of our FY23E and consensus estimates due to better-than-expected fares. However, the key highlight of the briefing was management’s tempered capacity guidance for 2024.

Fares up on capacity constraints
3Q23 seat capacity was flat QoQ at 6.3mn or c. 3% below 2019 average, as fleet availability issues persisted. Seat load factor softened by 3ppts QoQ to 84%, still 3ppts below pre-Covid, with China the only drag. However, pax fares more than compensated, improving by 5% QoQ to P3k, already 11% above 2019 levels.
Ancillary revenues per pax were also up 11% QoQ to P1.2k, already 56% above 2019 levels due to higher add-on bundles. EBITDA improved 3% QoQ /46x YoY to P5.65bn, bringing 9M23 EBITDA to P15.24bn, reversing last year’s P1.6bn EBITDA loss and trending better than our full-year forecast of P20.8bn. 

2024 seat growth guidance tempered to 5-8% due to P&W engine issues
Even though CEB’s fleet size will grow to 92 by end-2024 from 79 this year, management estimates about 10-20 aircraft could be grounded next year, up from just 4-5 this year due to the engine recall of Pratt & Whitney (P&W). Management lowered its seat growth guidance for 2024 to a more modest +5-8%, lower than our forecast of +11%. Management also does not expect capacity constraints to ease until 2026. P&W will compensate CEB for all the fixed costs of the affected aircraft but not for opportunity losses.

Management believes, however, that competitive dynamics will remain favorable, with its domestic competitors having difficulties growing their fleet sizes, which are well below pre-Covid. This suggests a fare outlook stronger than our current  forecast (flat YoY in 24E).

Local exporters oppose proposal to hike foreign container storage charges

In a letter signed by Philippine Exporters Confederation, Inc. (Philexport) Sergio R. Ortiz-Luis Jr., local exporters recommended that the planned rate hike undergo a regulatory impact assessment (RIA). Other stakeholders have proposed to defer the rate hike or implement the increase in tranches over a three-year period.

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) held a public hearing last Oct 18 to discuss the proposed tariff: (1) 32% hike in storage charges for containers and (b) introduction of a 150% surcharge on reefer containers. The PPA last adjusted storage fees in 2014. The rate increase, PPA said, is meant to optimize yard space usage and expedite the withdrawal of import containers, thus preventing congestion. Calculation is grounded on the 2013-2022 Consumer Price Index, reflecting the average retail prices for commonly purchased goods and services in a specific region.

Regis forecasts do not assume a hike in storage fees. Storage fees would typically account for c. 15% of port revenues. Regis estimates MICT accounts for c. 25% of ICTSI group revenues/EBITDA. ICTSI trades at 8.0x/7.2x FY23E/FY24E EV/EBITDA, below peers (11x) and historical average (11x).

Converge 9M profit up 4% to P6.4bn
  • 9M net income at P6.4bn (+4%). Converge reported net income of P6.4bn for 9M23, +4% YoY and appearing om trend to match Street estimates for FY23.  The 3Q profit was P2.1bn, down 3% YoY and 1% QoQ. Revenues were up 5% YoY for 3Q and +7% for 9M but costs, especially provisions, rose faster. Provisions for receivables were up 48% for 9M, and this was compounded by a jump in provisions for other assets.
  • Net adds of 79k in 3Q. The key bright spot for the quarter was net subscriber adds, which reached almost 79k. September NPL ratio improve, but PD rates tick up

The NPL ratio (ex-interbank) improved 3bps MoM to 3.48% from 3.51% in August. Also, Restructured Loans (RLs) dipped a smidge to 2.35% from 2.36% the previous month. Meanwhile, NPL cover improved a tad to 103.65% from 103.02% in August.

Past Due (PD) ratio blipped up 6bps MoM to 4.21% in September from 4.15% in August. This is the third consecutive month that PD ratios increased, now a cumulative 12bps up from the June level (4.09%), and fully 42bps up from the end-2022 low of 3.79%. This is slightly worrisome as PDs tend to be leading indicators for NPLs (90 days past due).

The fact that NPL ratios are lower since June, while PDs are up, suggest that banks are writing off NPLs at a faster clip.

There is a potentially positive perspective here, though. The divergence could be a function of changing loan mix. Loan growth (all banks, ex-interbank) decelerated to 8.4% YoY from 9.4% in August, and from 11.2% in Sept 2022. However, BSP data and bank results show that consumer loans are still growing in the mid- to high teens. This in turn is driven by credit cards, which were up a scorching 30% YoY in September (for universal/commercial banks). Not only are delinquencies naturally higher for consumer loans in general, but also banks tend to write off past due credit card receivables much more quickly.

Based on the banks’ 9M23 earnings calls, there doesn’t seem to be any indication significant, broad-based pressure on NPLs. At least not yet. We remain positive on the banks, but see asset quality deterioration as the key downside risk.

The Department of Budget and Management hiked the national budget by P261bn to P5.53tn from P5.23tn. The entire P261bn hike went to unprogrammed appropriations. Funding for unprogrammed appropriations come from the excess of collections, new revenues, and approved loans.
