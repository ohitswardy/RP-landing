REGIS REPORT

13 January 2023

The index gained heftily (+4.3% WoW), closing at 6,951.54 this week. Foreign investors bought up the market towards the end of the week, as risk appetite for EM improved.  Benign US inflation also helped to boost sentiment. 

Average daily turnover surged to US$188.8m (+86.9% WoW).

The rally was broad-based as gainers outperformed losers 13:2.

The biggest market movers were large cap banks BDO (+7.21% WoW) and BPI (+9.3% WoW), with the sector remaining a preferred way to play the improving macro situation.

BPI saw an extra boost as RRHI’s (+1.3% WoW) purchase of an additional 4.4% stake in the bank and the resulting synergies between the two have been viewed favorably. 

Among the top index gainers was MPI (+12.5% WoW) on market talks of a Mitsui buy-in and a possible tender offer. Aboitiz Power (+12.1% WoW) likewise soared, as renewed warnings by the Department of Energy of tight power supply spurred interesr in gencos.

On the flipside, this week’s underperformers were ACEN (-5.2% WoW) and GLO (-1.3% WoW). These names have been speculated to be potential deletions in the upcoming MSCI rebalancing on February.

RESEARCH
Robinsons Retail - Increases effective stake in BPI
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Strategic retail-bank partnership seen

RRHI is acquiring a 4.4% effective equity interest in BPI (BPI PM), one of the country’s leading banks. Following this transaction and the previously announced merger between BPI and Robinsons Bank (RBC), RRHI will hold a 6.5% effective equity interest in BPI. We estimate the transactions to be largely cash flow and profit-neutral to RRHI, but mildly (~3%) NAV-accretive. In our view, while RRHI’s ownership in BPI appears to be strategic in nature given the potential synergies between the two entities, there are also practical reasons for the investment from a broader Gokongwei Group perspective.

RRHI to own 6.5% effective equity interest in BPI

RRHI announced that it is acquiring a 4.4% effective equity interest in BPI from Arran Investments (an affiliate of GIC) through Liontide Holdings. The transaction involves the acquisition of (a) a 3.3% direct equity interest in BPI and (b) a 1.1% indirect stake in BPI through Liontide preferred shares. This transaction follows the merger between BPI and RBC, which was announced in Sep 2022. The merger will be done through a share-for-asset swap, effectively giving RRHI a 2.4% direct interest in BPI. After accounting for dilution following the merger, RRHI’s effective interest in BPI will settle at 6.5%. The Gokongwei Group’s effective interest in BPI will amount to 10.1%, with the remaining 3.6% interest held by JG Summit (JGS PM).

Impact to forecasts, NAV

The effective 4.4% equity stake is being acquired at P99.50 per BPI share or approximately P20bn. RRHI will finance the amount mostly through debt, which it intends to pay down through internally generated cash. We estimate that the transaction will be cash flow and NPAT neutral as financing cost will likely be offset by expected dividends from BPI. The transaction is also not expected to impact RRHI’s capex and dividend payouts. As of 9M22, RRHI had ~P12bn cash at the parent level and was in a consolidated net cash position of roughly P6bn (excluding lease liabilities). We estimate the transaction will be mildly (~3%) NAV accretive to RRHI.

Retail-banking synergies seen

The strategic rationale for the acquisition from RRHI’s perspective involves potential synergies between its retail operations and being part of a larger financial ecosystem through BPI. While there are commercial synergies between the two entities, we believe the more practical reasons for the acquisition by RRHI (vs other entities within the Gokongwei Group) are (a) RRHI’s stronger balance sheet position, and (b) avoidance of potential related party issues (ie DOSRI loans) between BPI and entities under JGS.

 
 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Vehicle sales up 6% MoM in December; Toyota sales up 5% MoM (+28% YoY)

Vehicle sales from CAMPI/TMA rose 6% MoM in December to 37,259 units (+34% YoY). Note that CAMPI/TMA began including Chinese-brand Cherry in Sep 2022. This brought FY2022 car sales to 351k units, +37% YoY. Toyota sold 17,232 units in December, up 28% YoY and 5% MoM and brought FY2022 sales to 174,106 units, +34% YoY, exceeding Toyota’s target of 162k units and Regis 157k units.

BSP may increase credit card rate cap to 3%

The BSP is reviewing the cap on credit card rates. It might increase the cap from 2% to 3%. The 2% cap was set in 2020 but the benchmark policy rates have since been increased by 350 basis points.

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.68bn in November

The balance of trade recorded a deficit of US$3.68bn in November, down 22% YoY. This was faster than the 13% YoY decline in October 2022 (deficit: US$3.31bn). Imports dropped by 2% YoY in November, first monthly decline since January 2021 due to lower capital goods and raw material imports, further weighed down by moderating growth in fuel imports. MoM, imports dropped by 2% while exports rose 13% YoY, third straight month of positive growth rate. Electronic exports rose +23% YoY but MoM, exports dropped by 8%. The YT-November trade deficit at US$53.69bn, up 45% YoY.

 

BSP sees 25-50bps rate in next policy meeting

The BSP is likely to raise benchmark interest rates by 25 or 50bps at its meeting on Feb 16 according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla who cited the need to anchor inflation expectations. BSP expects inflation to average 4.5% in 2023 from 5.8% in 2022. The pressure to match the US Fed’s policy tightening is waning and the need for large policy adjustment is “no longer there” and cited a weaker USD. The BSP has raised policy rates by 350bps to 5.5%; the Fed has raised rates by 425bps to 4.25%

 

DOE warns of thin power reserves this year, expects 12 yellow alerts

In a virtual briefing, Department of Energy Undersecretary Rowena Guevara warned that electricity end-users in Luzon may encounter supply woes throughout the year, especially during the summer months, due to thin power reserves. She added that yellow alerts are projected to be issued once in March, twice in April, the entire month of May, twice in June, one in September one in October and one in November

The DoE noted the Luzon grid is expected to experience peak demand of 13,125MW in May (+8.3%). The estimates made by the DOE factored in the scheduled outage of power plants, and unscheduled outages could trigger red alerts. A yellow alert means insufficient power reserves or ancillary services while a red alert means a supply deficiency exists which may result in power interruptions.

NPL ratio drops further in November

Bad debts held by Philippine banks continued to drop in November, with NPL ratio at 3.35%. This is down from 3.41% in October 2022 and 4.35% in Nov 2021. NPLs fell by 0.9% MoM to P408.097bn in November from P411.6bn in October. Loans of the entire banking system (ex-interbank) grew 11.5% YoY in November, slightly faster than the +11.4% in October. Past due loans fell by 13.2% to P492.5bn from P567.512 bn a year earlier. These borrowings are equivalent to 4.04% of the industry’s total loan portfolio, down from 5.12% a year earlier. Restructured loans fell by 4.9% year on year to P327.760 bn in November, from P344.897bn in the same month of 2021. This brought the ratio to 2.69% in November, from 3.11% in the year prior. Banks increased loan loss reserves by 2.7% to P431.501bn in November, from P419.863bn in the year prior. With this, the ratio went down to 3.54% from 3.79% in November a year ago. NPL coverage ratio — which shows the allowance for potential losses due to bad loans — increased to 105.73% from 87.13% a year earlier.

BSP: Cut in RRR before end-June very likely

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla said the likelihood of the banks’ reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cut happening before end of June is quite high, especially if inflation starts to slow down. The RRR for big banks is currently at 12%, one of the highest in the region. Reserve requirements for thrift and rural banks are at 3% and 2%, respectively. Medalla reiterated that they did not want to slash the RRR while the BSP is raising the interest rates so as not to confuse the market with policy signals. He added that the BSP will buy dollars to beef up its gross international reserves when the peso hits a certain exchange rate.

GDP grew by at least 7.5% in 2022

The Philippine economy likely expanded by at least 7.5% in 2022 according to Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. This implies 4Q22 GDP growth at +6.9%, moderating from +7.7% in 9M22. The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release 4Q22 GDP figures on Jan 26. The government expects GDP growth to slow down to 6.5% in 2023 due to the expected slow down of the global economy.
