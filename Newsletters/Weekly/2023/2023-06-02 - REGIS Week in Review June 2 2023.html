However, AC, BPI, and ICT still saw net outflows for the week as sellers took advantage of the liquidity.

Headline turnover stood at US$152.3m and outflows at US$77.9m.

Outside the rebalancing, the market was generally quiet as trades were only US$80.6m and outflows ex-rebal day was only at US$3.6m.

First Gen (+6.7% WoW) delivered a notable move, aft er signing an MOU with Prime Infra for the latter to eventually lease and operate its LNG terminal. FGEN rose sharply for the week despite the ex-date for its dividend declaration of Php0.40/sh.

RESEARCH
SM Investments - Refreshing our numbers
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Adjusting our estimates and TP

We refresh our numbers for SM Investments, increasing our profit estimates to P71bn for FY23 and P79bn for FY24. Our new estimates include the latest figures for the listed operating companies we cover: SM Prime and BDO as well as geothermal firm PGPC. Our target price for SM has now been lifted to P1,000 (+32%) after the latest changes to the valuations of its key holdings. We are maintaining our Hold rating.

Banking on basics

Banking affiliate BDO has been the heavy lifter for SM in recent years, earnings[1]wise. The gradual return to normal, amidst an environment of rising rates, has driven earnings sharply higher. Meanwhile, mall revenue at property subsidiary SM Prime has rebounded sharply and is now also above pre-pandemic peak. For SM Retail, FY22 was a surprising year, with profits at a record despite revenues just above pre-pandemic levels. The pandemic period seems to have given it an opportunity to restructure its operations and improve profitability.

Contribution of “portfolio investments” no longer negligible

SM’s “portfolio investments”, which comprise holdings not part of the three main segments, are beginning to contribute meaningfully after years of either losses or minimal profits, accounting for 10% of group earnings in 1Q23. Within this group, the biggest investments are geothermal energy firm PGPC, transport firm 2Go and mining company Atlas.

Conglomerates have fallen out of favor, but SM has held up better than most

The conglomerates under our coverage with listed investees / subsidiaries have seen a derating, as demonstrated by a widening of the discounts to NAV at which their shares trade. The derating of SM, however, has clearly been less pronounced, owing in part to its size and success in growing its businesses. We estimate the stock is now trading at <5% discount to its SOTP NAV (based on latest prices of its listed investments), whereas it traded frequently at par with (or even at a premium to) it in the past. In contrast, the average comparable discount for other conglomerates under coverage is 40%.

Target price P1,000 based on 10% discount to TP-based NAV

We adjust our target price to P1,000, which represents a ~10% discount to our sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) NAV estimate of P1,120/share, based on target valuations of its key listed subsidiaries. The 10% discount approximates the recent average discount of SM relative to its market-based NAV. Key upside risks include new value-accretive investments while downside risks include a deterioration of macro conditions and higher loan defaults.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco’s Ray Espinosa resigns; Manuel Pangilinan named President and CEO

Meralco’s President and CEO Ray Espinosa officially announced that he was relinquishing his post. Mr. Manuel Pangilinan, concurrently the Chairman of the company, will replace Mr. Espinosa.

AGI buys out Genting stake in Travellers

In a disclosure, AGI said that its subsidiary Newport World Resorts Properties, Inc. has entered into a share purchase agreement for the acquisition of shares in Travellers International Hotel Group (Travellers) held by Genting Hong Kong (through Star Cruises Philippines, Asian Travellers and Premium Travellers Ltd.). Upon completion of the transaction, Genting Hong Kong will cease to hold a stake in Travellers International, Westside City Inc and Adams Properties Inc. It did not disclose the acquisition price. In March 2022, AGI infused P7.8bn in Travellers from which increased its stake from 44% to c.59%.

UKB loan growth decelerates slightly to 9.6% YoY; auto loans +1.9% YoY

Loans of universal/commercial banks (UKBs) in April grew 9.7% YoY (ex-reverse repos) to P10,864bn. This was slightly slower than the 10.2% posted in March. The March figure was revised upward (from 10.1%) due, Regis suspects, to a late submission by a small bank. Loans were up 0.8% MoM. Loans for “production” slowed to 8.3% YoY (from 9.0%), with deceleration seen across most major segments. Notable slowdowns were seen in Construction (10.8% in April vs 13.6% in March), Water (22.3% vs 24.4%) and Electricity/Gas (12.4% vs 13.2%). The exceptions were Real Estate (which sped up to 4.5% in April from 4.1% YoY in March) and Information and Communication (19.0% YoY vs 17.0%). Meanwhile, Consumer Loans accelerated to 22.3% YoY, from 21.8% in March. Credit cards grew 29.9% YoY (was 28.4%). Auto loans grew 1.9% YoY in April (+0.9% in March) continuing its return to growth from deep declines during the Covid pandemic.

Ex-Jollibee CFO named Filinvest COO

JFC’s long time chief financial officer Ysmael Baysa has moved to the Gotianun Group as chief operating officer of Filinvest Development Corp. FDC’s board likewise appointed Daniel Ang Tan Chai as chief investment officer.

Bayan Muna seeks refund of corporate income taxes from water concessionaires

Bayan Muna Partylist group filed a motion for partial reconsideration with the Supreme Court (SC) to order Maynilad and Manila Water (MWC PM) to refund the corporate income taxes on to consumers. Last December, the SC ruled that the water concessionaires are public utilities and are not allowed to pass on income taxes to their customers. In the same ruling, however, the SC said that income taxes passed on to consumers may no longer be recovered because the right to a refund has already lapsed. An action to contest the water rate should've been filed with National Water and Resources Board within 30 days from effectivity of the rates. Manila Water and Maynilad are now operating under the Revised Concession Agreement, which allows them to earn a 12% pre-tax return. Manila Water is owned by Razon-Led Prime Infrastructure (36% economic stake/52% voting) and Ayala Corp (30% economic/32% voting). Maynilad is owned by Metro Pacific (53%), DMCI (25%), and Japan-based Marubeni (20%).

 

MREIT plans to acquire 150,000sqm of offices from Megaworld

MREIT signed an MOU for the possible acquisition of seven more office buildings from its sponsor, Megaworld. The buildings have a combined leasable area of 150,000sqm and have an aggregate occupancy rate of 94%. Fort Bonifacio – 87,100sqm; Iloilo – 36,300sqm; Davao – 27,100sqm. The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 46% to 475,000sqm. The buildings generated P1.2bn in rental revenue in 2022. Megaworld and MREIT did not disclose the acquisition price of the new buildings but Regis estimates that they will be valued close to P15bn, c. 40% of MREIT’s current market capitalization. Since MREIT previously said that it prefers to acquire via share-for-asset swap, this could mean that Megaworld will sell c. P5bn worth of MREIT shares in order to comply with the minimum public ownership requirement. MREIT currently offers 6.1% dividend yield (based on consensus forecast). The current 10-year government bond yield is 5.8%.

FGEN, Prime Infra sign MOU for lease of LNG terminal

FGEN LNG Corporation and Prime Infrastructure Capital announced the signing of a memorandum of understanding for the proposed lease and operation of the LNG storage and regasification terminal being developed by FGEN in Batangas City. No definitive agreements have been signed by the parties. The lease of the LNG Terminal will form part of Prime Infra’s proposed gas aggregation strategy that is focused on leveraging its existing Malampaya project facilities and gas market expertise to ensure a reliable and low cost supply of gas. FGEN was looking to commission its FSRU in September 2023 and was looking at initial LNG cargo of 160k cubic meters (as of mid May 2023). The LNG terminal costs roughly $300mn. FGEN owns four power plants with a combined capacity of about 2,000MW all running on Malampaya gas. FGEN trades at 4.8x FY23E PE

Infrastructure spending dips by 1% YoY in 1Q23; misses target by 6%

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) dipped by 1% YoY to P243.7bn in 1Q23. This fell short of the government’s 1Q23 target of P258bn. The government cited outstanding checks of the Dept. of Public Works and lower subsidy releases pending requests from GOCCs implementing infrastructure programs. The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn on infrastructure this year (+1% YoY), equivalent to 5.3% of GDP. From 2022-2028, infrastructure spending is expected to rise at a +10% CAGR, slower than 10-year historical CAGR of +18%.

 

Government debt stock hits P13.9tn in April

The National Government’s total outstanding debt reached P13.91tn as of end-April 2023, up 0.4% MoM and 3.7% from end-2022. This is equivalent to 61.3% of GDP. The domestic debt amounted to P9.46tn (+2.7% from end-2022), accounting for 68% of total debt. External debt amounted to P4.45tn (+5.8% from end-2022); In USD terms, external debt stood at US$80.2bn (+6.4% from end-2022). The government aims to cut the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025, and further to 51.5% by 2028.

Bill creating Maharlika fund clears Senate

Senate Bill No. 2020, which seeks to create the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF), passed the Senate with major changes. The Senate version contains a provision that allows the government financial institutions to pitch into the fund following approval of its board of directors, which will include the private sector. Land Bank and Development Bank of the Philippines will pitch in P50bn and P25bn. The national government will also contribute P50bn, which will come from dividend incomes from BSP, PAGCOR, and other royalties. This version also prohibits the use of funds from the pension funds (GSIS and SSS), Philhealth, Pag-IBIG, OWWA, PVAO in the capitalization. The Lower House’s version stated barred the Maharlika from taking on controlling stakes in its investments, but that provision was absent in the Senate’s iteration. The proposed MIF is primed to help bankroll the Marcos administration’s pipeline of 194 infrastructure flagship projects. This was certified as “urgent”, the bill will be tackled at the bicameral conference committee and is expected to be ratified by Thursday.

State agencies told to ramp up spending

The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) has urged government agencies to further ramp up their budget disbursement and spending. As of end-April, the Notices of Cash Allowances (NCAs) for state agencies has only been 90% utilized, slower than last year’s 92% utilization rate. DBM is reminding agencies to avoid underspending, given the limited fiscal space.

Government records P66.8bn budget surplus in April

The budget surplus for April 2023 amounted to P66.8bn, up more than 13x YoY due to strong revenue collections, which outpaced expenditures. Note that the government started collecting VAT on a quarterly basis this year vs. monthly previously. Revenues rose 27% YoY, with tax revenues up 32% YoY. The BIR collections rose 40% YoY while Customs registered a more modest growth of 2.9%. Meanwhile, expenditures grew 9% YoY, with primary expenditures up 7% YoY while interest payments rose 24% YoY. The gov’t attributed the growth in primary spending to subsidy release to PhilHealth and expenditures related to public works. This brings 4M23 budget deficit to P204.1bn, shrinking by 35% YoY and trending better than the government’s full-year deficit target due to higher revenues and softer-than-expected spending
