REGIS REPORT

24 March 2023

The market managed to rebound, closing above 6600 levels (+2.0% WoW) after eight straight weeks of losses. The market was lifted by strong foreign buying late in the week. Buying was scattered across sectors, with top bought names ICT, ALI, JFC for U$S9.4m, US$4.1m, and US$4.0m respectively. Overall, net foreign inflows registered at US$4.0m for the week. However, average turnover remained low at US$84.6m.

The top performer was BPI (+9.3% WoW). BPI is distributing 406m treasury shares via property dividend.  As expected, the stock ran up sharply prior to its ex-date last Friday, March 24. The entitlement ratio is 0.08964:1.  

Apart from BPI, top performers were AC (+9.2% WoW), ACEN (+8.8% WoW), ALI (+8.8% WoW), and ICT (+7.7% WoW).

On the power space, ACEN (+8.8% WoW) announced that it has opened its 521MW New England solar farm in Australia. ACEN President Eric Francia disclosed that they are already exporting c.15% of output and expected to generate the full nameplate capacity in the coming months. This project is the biggest addition to ACEN’s capacity this year and is among the key earnings drivers for 2023/24.

PLDT (+3.1% WoW) released its FY22 results as well, with a net income of Php10.5bn (-60% YoY) mostly marred by massive write-offs of fixed assets. Core income was at Php30bn (+7%) although results were mostly a miss if it were not for lower non-cash charges. Management also announced the sale of another 1,000 telco towers worth P12bn (cumulative of P98bn since last December). The cash will come in handy to reduce debt prospectively and cushion the impact of the budget overruns of P48bn.

Meanwhile, index laggards were DMC (-5.3% WoW), CNVRG (-2.3% WoW), and SCC (-1.6% WoW).

PLDT - 4Q/FY22 results: Write-offs amidst another soft quarter
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 Gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Write-offs lead to sharp profit drop; core income up +7%

PLDT reported net income of P10.5bn for FY22, -60% YoY as the company took significant write-offs (“accelerated depreciation”) on its assets. Excluding non[1]core items but including losses at Voyager, we estimate core income for the year was P30.0bn, +7%. While this appears just ahead of estimates, we note that the outperformance was actually driven by lower non-cash charges.

Mobile revenue share dips; home broadband net adds negligible in 4Q

Mobile revenue contracted by 4% in FY22 and 3% in 4Q. 4Q alone was flat QoQ and did not see the usual bounce. We estimate PLDT ceded 30bp of revenue share in the last quarter, attributable to the relatively lower incomes of its traditional target market (vs Globe’s).

PLDT’s wired broadband business added only 6k subscribers (net) in 4Q. That’s down from 41k in 3Q and the lowest quarterly addition in years.

“Accelerated depreciation” slashes FY22 profits but will lift them from FY23

In 4Q alone, PLDT undertook asset write-downs in the amount of P34bn relating to various assets (P51.2bn for the entire FY22). While this pushed 4Q into an implied loss, it will also result in lower depreciation charges in subsequent years (and thus higher reported income).

Voyager losses climb

Fintech arm Voyager, through its digital bank, ended the year with P15bn in deposits. The deposit generation is coming at a cost: PLDT’s attributable losses from 36.8%-owned Voyager hit P1bn in 4Q, taking the full year figure to P3.0bn.

Capex “overrun” reduced

PLDT also disclosed today that, following negotiations with suppliers, it managed to reduce the capex overrun to P33bn from the previously disclosed P48bn. The results of a forensic review conducted by external parties also identified no evidence of fraud or bad faith conduct on the part of its employees.

 
Philippine Property - Attractive valuations lead to sizeable buybacks
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A closer look at buybacks

Property operations have been badly affected by the pandemic and the sector remains out of favor due to the high interest rate environment. Developers, however, are using their checkbooks to communicate their optimistic views on the sector’s prospects as well as the attractiveness of current share prices. RLC has been the most active at buying back shares while Ayala Land’s parent has been snapping up the most shares.

RLC has the most active buyback program

Since March 2020, Robinsons Land has arguably been the most active (relative to market cap) at buying back its own shares, acquiring nearly 5% of shares outstanding. Furthermore, RLC expanded the size of its buyback program for a second time, to P9bn (from P6bn previously).

Ayala Corp is the most active parent company

Meanwhile, Ayala Corporation has been the most active in purchasing shares of its property subsidiary, Ayala Land. AC has increased its stake in Ayala Land by c. 6ppt since December 2019, predominantly by purchasing ALI shares in the open market.

 
LT Group - Tobacco falls short despite steady sales; beverage units gain on volumes, margins
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

4Q22 falls short as tobacco underperforms

LTG reported a FY22 income of P25.1bn (+24%YoY), slightly behind estimates, dragged by the underperformance from tobacco and a depressed contribution from PNB. In 4Q22 alone, LTG’s net income dipped to P4.726bn (-6%QoQ, - 54%YoY). In particular, LTG’s share from tobacco fell 23%QoQ, despite flat volumes, while PNB’s provision for loan loss reserves jumped to P6.5bn in the quarter.

Tobacco profits -23%QoQ despite flat volume

LTG’s share of profits from Fortune fell to P3.280bn (-23%QoQ, -21%YoY). This was despite relatively steady cigarette sales of 7.6bn sticks in the quarter (- 1%QoQ). Unit market share actually improved to 60.1% in 4Q from the year’s low of 59.2% in 3Q22. Management acknowledged that competition was very tough particularly in the lower-priced segments. Different initiatives, like the launch of a new Marlboro sub-brand or marketing of IQOS (and related products) may have contributed to opex, subsequently weighing down profit in the quarter. Barring any price hikes and a tax increases in the period, we had expected profit from tobacco to have been similar to 3Q.

PNB provisions climb 67% higher than 3Q22

PNB’s income contribution remained muted at just P153mn in 4Q (-14%QoQ, - 97%YoY), after loan loss provisions jumped to P6.5bn in 4Q22 from P3.9bn in 3Q22. Trading losses widened to P1.3bn for the FY22, implying losses were P1.0bn in 4Q22 alone. Meanwhile, it booked an additional P2.3bn in ROPA gains in 4Q22 after more asset sales in the period. Gross NPL ratio ended at 6.34% for the year, while NPL coverage improved to 93.72% (vs 82% as 9M22).

Beverage volumes jump QoQ led by alcohol, GPMs up

Tanduay’s profit contribution jumped to P612mn in 4Q22 (+108%QoQ, 155%YoY), lifted by a substantial rise in volumes (9.1mn cases, 32%QoQ) and margins (13.4% in 4Q22 vs 11% in 9M22). ABI similarly grew to P152mn (+13%QoQ, +138%YoY) on similarly higher GPM in the period (23.2% in 4Q22 vs 22.0% in 9M22). Volumes across the board (energy drink, water, soy milk) were up by 15-20%, presumably on better traffic to the relevant points of sales as mobility resumed.

PLDT sells another batch of towers

San Miguel terminates agreements with Meralco for prospective power deals

San Miguel Corp (SMC PM), through its two subsidiaries, terminated their power supply agreements with Meralco for period beginning 2024 and 2025. SMC was locked in at low prices for the two deals for a combined 1,800MW (around P4.2/kwh). Excellent Energy (EERI) issued a termination notice to Meralco with regard to its 1,200 supply contract. Masinloc Power Partners (MPPCL) did the same for its 600Mw contract. The termination of the agreements will cast a greater shadow on the future of power supply in Luzon. The addition of the power supply, even if delayed and inadequate, would be crucial to the grid. In theory, Meralco (MER PM) can choose to sue and claim damages but it’s unclear whether it will do given previous actions.

 

Foreign ownership of the Philippine market drops in February

Regis estimates that the proportion of shares held by foreigners fell 28bps MoM to 20.4% while foreign ownership of the PSE Index fell 1.14 percentage points MoM to 23.8% (in February). The much larger drop in PSEi foreign ownership was due to the index rebalancing – RLC and MEG were replaced by DMC and UBP, which have lower foreign ownership. Market net foreign selling amounted to US$142mn in February (from US$122mn of net foreign buying in January). Of the 28 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 14 saw declines in foreign ownership while 14 experienced increases

 

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

EMI – saw the biggest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 3.32 percentage points MoM to 19.9%. EMI’s parent, Alliance Global, transferred some of its EMI shares from the Singapore Exchange to the Philippine Stock Exchange. 

GTCAP – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership, which rose 1.10 percentage points to 17.70%. Although net foreign buying only amounted to US$6.2mn in February, it appears that the foreign buying during the last few trading days of January was only registered in the February foreign ownership.

BDO – foreign ownership rose 44 basis points to 25.6%. It has been the top foreign-bought stock for four consecutive months. Net foreign buying amounted to US$31.1mn in February.  

ACEN opens Australia solar farm, plans 8GW more

In a disclosure to the exchange, ACEN said that its Australian subsidiary opened the first phase of its 521MW New England solar farm. This is expected to generate a full nameplate capacity of 521MW in the coming months. The solar farm will have a total capacity of 936MW. ACEN President and CEO Eric Francia said its overseas unit is eyeing to increase its capacity in Australia by 8GW. “ACEN Australia has around 1GW capacity worth $1bn in construction, and more than 8GW capacity in the development pipeline”. The pipeline in Australia represents the largest portion of ACEN’s 18GW pipeline, 6.5GW in Philippines, 1.6GW in Vietnam and 1.8GW in India/others. ACEN is targeting 20GW worth of attributable renewables capacity by 2030.

Max’s Group (MAXS PM) FY22 net profit jumps 8x

MAXS reported recurring FY22 net profit of P622mn, up over 8x YoY. Revenues rose 44% YoY to P11bn on the back of 43% SSSG in the Philippines. Regis estimates average sales per store is ~5% below 2019 levels. Profitability was largely driven by margin improvement as EBITDA margin settled at 19.8%, well above the pre-pandemic level of 15-16%. For 4Q22 alone, revenues jumped 39% YoY, though margins compressed YoY. Assuming 15% earnings growth in 2023, MAXS currently trades at 5-6x 2023E PE.

Converge and the prepaid subscriber market

Out of the 1.877m broadband subscribers Converge had as of end-Dec, around 17,000 of them were on prepaid (added from late 3Q onwards). While it’s correct to say that the share of prepaid to the total business is still negligible, the 17,000 subscriber additions are a significant share of net adds for 2H22.  Converge added net 60k subscribers (postpaid and prepaid) in the last six months of the year. 

 

Putting it another way: Prepaid subs already account for more than 25% of net adds. This also means postpaid net adds are actually lower than initially thought.

All this reinforces Regis’ view that the postpaid market for ~P1,500/month packages is at saturation.  The race now is for the low-end prepaid subscribers.

First Gen unit gets DENR nod on 120-MW hydro facility

First Gen Corp. said its unit had signed an agreement with the DENR for the development of a 120-megawatt (MW) Aya pumped-storage hydroelectric facility in Pantabangan, Nueva Ecija. The signed deal will enable the FGEN to use a 36-hectare portion of the 84,000-hectare Pantabangan-Carranglan watershed forest reserve for the Aya pumped-storage hydroelectric power project. The agreement is valid for 25 years and subject to renewal for another 25 years.

RLC expands share buyback program again

Robinsons Land expanded its share buyback program to P9bn from P6bn. The original P3bn buyback program was announced in November 2021. It was then expanded to P6bn in November 2022. The company has spent P4.1bn to purchase 238mn RLC shares. Regis estimates that the average price was P17.10/share. The remaining P4.9bn represents c. 7% of current market cap. The decision to expand the buyback program signals management’s confidence in the company’s operations and its view that RLC shares are at compelling valuations. RLC trading at 64% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY23E P/B, and 7.6X FY23E P/E

Monde Nissin reallocates spending for meat alternatives business; announces investment in food tech company

MONDE disclosed that ~P2bn in capex and opex previously earmarked for its meat alternative business for FY22 will be reallocated as capex for the APAC BFB business in FY23. This is in line with its efforts to restructure the meat alternative businesses amidst challenging market conditions. Meanwhile, the company also announced that it plans to invest up to USD2mn in US-based food tech company Terramino through the subscription of preferred shares in the latter. The investment is equivalent to 1.89% of Terramino’s outstanding capital stock. Terramino is engaged in R&D and commercialization of koji-based food products. MONDE currently trades at 22x FY23E PE.

Razon pulls out of casino deal with Dennis Uy’ PH Resorts

Monetary Board raises policy rates by 25bps

The Monetary Board decided to raise the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight repurchase facility by 25bps to 6.25%. Decision was based on new information and its assessment of the effects of past policy actions. They also cited the steady increase of core inflation in Feb, despite the modest decline in headline inflation. The BSP tempered its 2023 average inflation forecast slightly to 6.0% from 6.1% previously. It also lowered its inflation forecast for 2024 to 2.9% from 3.1% previously.

Senator seeks ban on offshore gaming

Senator Sherwin Gatchalian, head of the Senate Ways and Means Committee, is seeking a ban on offshore gaming, citing that “the costs of POGOs outweigh their benefits”. He noted that there were 102 offshore gaming-related crimes reported from Jan 2017 to Jan 2023. President Marcos previously said that problems lie with illegal offshore gaming firms, not legal ones. He also said there was a need to examine if there is a social cost. At the end of 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-4Q22.

PAGCOR eyes US$1.47bn from sale of 41 casinos

PAGCOR is considering selling all 41 casinos it operates to raise P80bn (US$1.47bn) in government revenue and in order to focus on its regulatory functions according to Pagcor Chairman Alejandro Tengco. The total GGR will likely hit the P256bn pesos pre-pandemic level by 2024 versus P214bn last year.

BOP deficit widens to US$895mn in February; GIR falls to US$98.2bn

Preliminary data showed  the Balance of Payments widened to an US$895mn deficit in February, up 4.7x YoY (vs Feb 2022: -US$157mn). This is a reversal of the US$3bn BOP surplus in January 2023. The BSP cited government debt payments. This brings YT-Feb BOP to a US$2.2bn surplus, a reversal of the US$259mn deficit in the same period a year ago. The BSP also revised the end-February GIR to US$98.2bn (previously US$99.3bn). This is down from US$100.7bn in January 2023 and is equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports.

Amended Public Service Act to take effect in April

NEDA released the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for R.A. 11647, which amends the Public Service Act. The IRR will take effect on April 4. The law effectively allows full foreign ownership in telecommunications, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports. Under Rule 8, Section 32 of the IRR, telecommunication is the only public service considered as a “critical infrastructure,”. The rules included a reciprocity requirement for foreign investments in critical infrastructure. Foreign nationals cannot own more than 50% of a firm engaged in services classified as critical infrastructure. Sovereign wealth funds and independent pension funds of each state may also collectively own up to 30% of the capital of a public service classified as critical infrastructure.

Government posts P46bn budget surplus in January

The government recorded a budget surplus of P46bn in January. This is a reversal of deficits in January 2022 (P23bn) and in December 2022 (P378bn) due to faster YoY, MoM increases in revenues amidst a slack in expenditures. Tax revenues rose 19.5% YoY and 20.3% MoM, led by sustained improvements in BIR collections. Expenditures barely grew to P302bn due to a 28% YoY drop in interest payments (base effects: FX interest payment due in Feb 2022 was paid in Jan 2022). Primary spending rose 8.3% YoY, outperforming the government’s full-year projection (-1.3%). Net of interest payments, the primary surplus rose two-fold YoY to P92.7bn from last year’s surplus of P42.2bn.
