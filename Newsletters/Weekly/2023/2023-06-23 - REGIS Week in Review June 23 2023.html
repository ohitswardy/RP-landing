REGIS REPORT

23 June 2023

The market fell to 6,393.55 (-1.8% WoW) as foreigners continue sell the local market given the lack of domestic catalysts.  Turnover stayed anemic, with only US$82.3m trading daily (ave), even as foreign outflows stood at US$14.8m.

The consumer sector dragged the index the most after the Department of Finance proposed an imposition of new taxes on “junk food”. The proposal includes taxes on snacks like potato chips equivalent to 20-40% of current retail prices and a further Php6/liter on excise taxes on beverages sweetened by sugar. URC (-9.6% WoW) fell sharply, as 85% of its domestic branded revenue (40% of total consolidated revenue) is comprised of salty snacks and beverage. The impact on MONDE (-11.5% WoW) may also be significant (around 20% of revenues).

Puregold (+4.2% WoW) bounced after dropping close to 7% last week. The company announced its takeover of operations of over 14 locations of Divimart Supermarket and rebranding these into Puregold stores. While the acquisition is relatively small (c3% of current Puregold brand network), it provides PGOLD with instant footprint in new locations, and potentially access to more locations within the Divimart network.

Property names weighed on the index, given continued pressure on the office segment. The hawkish tone of the Fed likewise weighed on sentiment. ALI fell as much as 5.1% last Tuesday as selling accelerated after the share price broke below the Php24 level, reverting back to October 2022 lows. The stock bounced back to end the week mostly flat (-0.2% WoW) although still registered US$0.9m worth of outflows. SMPH (-2.9% WoW) likewise saw heavy outflows worth US$4.6m.

PLDT (+6.5% WoW) outperformed after lagging the previous months. The stock saw strong foreign demand worth US$4.1m as it was the top bought name for the week.

Conglomerates GT Capital (+3.7% WoW) and MPI (+4.6% WoW) also stayed resilient. GTCAP is on track to post record-high earnings this year (Php21.8bn, +43% YoY) while the government approved tariff increases rail lines in Metro Manila starting August. LRT-1 is operated by a consortium which includes MPI. MPI is also interested in the O&M contracts for the new toll roads in Cebu, although there is no clear visibility on these projects at the moment.

On the macro front, the BSP kept the policy rate unchanged at 6.25% following the Monetary Board meeting. This was widely expected by the market. BSP Governor Medalla says he will consider cutting rates if CPI inflation falls within the BSP’s target band (2-4%) for two consecutive months.

Government plans to raise taxes on sweetened beverages; impose taxes on "junk food"

The Department of Finance and the Department of Health plan to impose a tax on "junk food" and raise sweetened beverages taxes to promote healthier food choices and address health whilst generating revenue. According to the DOF, this would result in a 21% reduction in the consumption of junk foods that majorly contribute to diet-related diseases, while generating P76bn in tax revenues in the first year.

The DOF is proposing to impose a tax of P10 per 100g or P10 per 100ml on pre-packaged food deemed to "lack nutrition value". It is proposing to impose a standard P12/liter excise tax on sweetened beverages plus anuual escalation of 4%, regardless on the type of sweetener used. It is also seeking to remove exemptions on the current excise tax for sweetened beverages. Under the TRAIN law, sugar-sweetened beverages are subject to a P6/liter excise tax, while beverages using other caloric sweeteners are subject to a P12/liter excise tax. Currently, these products are exempted from the tax: milk products, 100% natural fruit and vegetable juices w/o added sweeteners, coffee, and meal replacements.

Assuming the proposal in its current form is approved, we estimate the proposed tax is equivalent to 20-40% of the current retail prices of sweet and savoury snacks, and as much as 15% of current retail prices of sweetened beverages.

MONDE and URC would be most affected by this tax, if imposed. MONDE's APAC BFB business generates 30% of segment sales (~20% of consolidated sales) from sweet and savoury biscuits. URC's domestic branded consumer food (BCF) segment - predominantly snack foods and beverages - accounts for 50% and 55% of consolidated sales and EBIT, respectively. Meanwhile, its sugar milling business contributes 20-30% of consolidated annual EBIT.

Cebu Air cites fleet availability issues; Other airlines face the same challenges

During the Senate hearing, Cebu Air (CEB PM) explained the “external issues” that significantly affected its flight schedules in recent months, including supply problems of aircraft engines and damaged planes. It plans to increase the number of spare aircraft from “normal” 1-2 to 6 by year-end, as they lease more aircraft.

Philippine Airlines (PAL) and Air Asia Philippines also experiencing the challenges on global supply chain. Regis estimates the industry’s fleet is currently at 82% of pre-pandemic levels. Systemwide pax volumes for the industry in 1Q23 was at 83% of pre-Covid levels.

Dito launches postpaid services

Dito Telecommunity launched its postpaid services, introducing a number of SIM-only plans and plans that come with handsets. The data allocation for its various plans is more than those for the two incumbent operators.

Pakistan gov't mulls handing over Karachi port to UAE

The government of Pakistan has constituted a negotiation committee to finalize a deal with the UAE for handing over its Karachi port terminals. It aims to reach a deal to hand over Berths 6-9, which were under the administrative control of Pakistan International Container Terminal (PICT PK), to Abu Dhabi Ports (ADP). PICT is an 80%-owned unit of ICTSI (ICT PM).

Sources of the local newspaper, Tribune, suggest that the PICT may pose legal challenges based on the concession agreement. PICT operated the Berths 6-9 from June 2002 for a period of 21 years, which ended last June 17. However, under the Concession Agreement, PICT has the first right of refusal subject to certain conditions.

Regis estimates PICT accounts for 4% of FY23E volumes, 2% of FY23E revenues, 1% of FY23E profit. Given this recent development, it is highly likely that ICT will impair the US$13mn of goodwill related to PICT. The impairment will not affect dividends, which are based on a min. of 50% of recurring profit before perps.

PGOLD to acquire 14 Divimart Supermarkets

PGOLD disclosed that it is taking over operations of 14 Divimart Supermarkets, which will be rebranded into Puregold stores. Majority of the Divimart stores are located outside Metro Manila.

Another 18 sites are being evaluted for possible conversion to Puregold stores. The 14 stores will add c3% to the Puregold network (456 stores as of 1Q23).

Wilcon CEO sees slower growth in 2023; store expansion to accelerate

WLCON President and CEO Lorraine Belo-Cincochan sees muted growth for the company in 2023. This is partly due to high base effects last year driven by the post-lockdown surge in demand; discretionary spending by its customers has also been focused on "revenge travel" and other leisure activities.

Notwithstanding the muted outlook, the company continues to focus on store expansion. From the initial guidance of 8-10 new stores this year (Regis FY23E: 8 new depots), mgmt says they can open as much as 12-14 new stores. This implies the company can hit its 100-store target by 2024, earlier than its initial plan to reach this target by 2025.

Metro Pacific eyes Cebu expressway, Bohol bridge

Metro Pacific is keen to pursue its plans to vie for O&M contract for the proposed P94bn Metro Cebu Expressway (MCE) and the P90bn Cebu-Bohol bridge (CBB).

DPWH was supposed to issue the privatization contract for the MCE in 1Q. The government must select its consultant before it conducts any supplemental study on the project. The DPWH is scheduled to conduct the supplemental study in September and will end it in Feb 2024.The tender for the PPP is scheduled on June 2024-Feb 2025; PPP contract signing on March 2025.

MCE will span 56.94km and will require a budget of P94bn, of which P61.5bn will go to civil works. It will cut travel time from Naga City in the south of Cebu to Danao City in the north.

CBB bridge will be a floating bridge and will link Cordova, Cebu with Olango island. MPTC President Rodrigo E. Franco also expressed concerns about the viability of the project, given the high cost. The project was previously deemed “impossible” by the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) in 2019 due to technological limitations.

BSP extends policy rate hike pause

Monetary Board kept benchmark interest rates unchanged for a second straight meeting. The overnight repurchase rate maintained at 6.25%. 

BSP has raised its policy rate by 425bps since May 2022. The gap between the Federal Funds rate (5%) and the BSP’s policy rate maintained at 125bps (narrower than the average gap of c. 260bps since 2013).

It trimmed its 2023 average inflation rate forecast to 5.4% (vs. 5.5% previously). The BSP expects inflation to average at 2.9% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025.

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that he will consider cutting rates if inflation rate falls below 4% for two consecutive months.  The latest inflation print in May stood at 6.1%, with 5M23 average inflation rate at 7.5%. Assuming CPI MoM is unchanged, headline inflation rate could fall below 4% by Sep 2023.

Economic team to propose P5.768tn budget for 2024

The proposed 2024 national budget of P5.768tn is larger by 9.5% compared to this year’s budget (P5.268tn), according to the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC).

Under the Constitution, the executive branch must submit the proposed national budget to Congress within 30 days after the President’s State of the Nation Address. Based on its plan, the government expects to trim the fiscal deficit to 4% of GDP by 2025 and 3% by 2028 from a peak of 8.6% in 2021.

Government to gauge private sector interest for Central Luzon Expressway project

The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Center will gauge private sector interest for the Central Luzon Link Expressway (CLLEX) concession. It will conduct a market sounding activity next week to gather feedback from relevant stakeholders. The  event aims to gather feedback from relevant stakeholders.

The project involves the O&M contract for CLLEX Phase I and the construction and operation of Phase 2. Phase 1 is a 30-km expressway connecting the provinces of Tarlac and Nueva Ecija; Currently under construction with 18km already operational (Project cost of P11bn and is funded by JICA). Phase 2 is a 36-km expressway connecting Cabanatuan City and San Jose City in Nueva Ecija and is estimated to cost P9.2bn. 

BSP creates overnight reference rate

BSP will peg overnight rate (ON) as the reference rate to replace the LIBOR expiring 30th June. This will serve as a reference rate for overnight borrowing and lending transactions between banks. The BSP is aiming to enhance transparency and efficiency in the financial market. According to the BSP, there is an urgency in having a credible yield curve with macro-financial decisions being based on these benchmarks.

The ON rate will be generated by converting the 28-day BSP bill rate to its ON equivalent, and a 56-day tenor will also be introduced.  Banks were required to submit quarterly reports of their remaining LIBOR-related exposures until the end of 2023 as part of the BSP transition plan.

BOP posts US$439mn deficit in May

The country’s balance of payments recorded a US$439mn deficit in May. This is lower than the US$1.6bn deficit in May 2022 but higher than the US$148mn deficit in April 2023.  The government cited the government’s foreign currency debt obligations for the latest print.

This brings 5M23 BOP to a surplus of US$2.9bn, a reversal of the US$1.5bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$1.2bn this year (0.3% of GDP), lower than last year’s deficit of US$7.3bn (1.8% of GDP).

Government approves new tariffs for LRT-1, LRT-2

The Department of Transportation (DoTr) announced fare hikes for LRT-1, LRT-2 beginning August 2. The minimum boarding fare will increase by 21% to P13.29 from P11. Fare per km will increase to P1.21 from P1.00. The approved increases are lower than the cumulative un-implemented tariff hike of +50% stipulated under the concession agreement.

Last April, President Marcos ordered the deferral of the fare hike implementation due to its economic impact on commuters. The last fare increase for LRT-1, LRT-2, MRT-3 occurred in Jan 2015, before LRMC took over operations on Sep 2015.

Light Rail Manila Corp (LRMC) holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 railroad. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), Macquarie Infra (10%).

Regis  valuation for 100% of LRMC’s equity value is negative P19.16bn (equivalent to an attributable value of -P0.24 per MPI share).

Consortium unveils proposal for multi-phased NAIA masterplan

The Manila international Airport Consortium (MIAC) unveiled its P267bn unsolicited proposal to rehab and develop NAIA over a 25-year concession period. The proposal involves a total capex of P267bn and total concession fee payments of P280bn (covers revenue sharing and taxes) over the life of the concession.

Capex of P267bn will be earmarked for a three-phase expansion:  

  • Upfront payment to the government of P57bn
  • Capex of P57bn for Phases 1&2: Increase terminal capacity to 54mn passengers per annum (MPPA) by 2025 and 62.5 MPPA by 2028 (expansion of terminal floor area, addition of airfield facilities, and cross-terminal transport)
  • Capex of P154bn for Phase 3: Increase capacity to 70 MPPA by 2048 (long-term expansion and development projects to further expand terminal space and airfield capacity)

MIAC includes six Filipino conglomerates + US-based Global Infrastructure Partners:

  • Aboitiz InfraCapital –AEV PM
  • AC Infrastructure – AC PM
  • Asia’s Emerging Dragon Corp. – LTG PM
  • Alliance Global – Infracorp Dev’t – AGI PM
  • Filinvest Development Corp. - FDC PM
  • JG Summit – JGS PM

Based on its 2019 annual report, NAIA recorded a net income of P5.0bn, operating profit of P6.8bn, and revenues of P15.2bn. Revenue breakdown is 36% Passenger service charge, 34% landing/parking fees, and 30%  rent income and others

Valenzuela City to ban POGO firms

The local government of Valenzuela City has approved an ordinance banning POGO firms in the city.

If other areas implement similar ordinances, then this could further shrink the POGO industry. POGO firms have a large presence in the cities of Makati, Pasay, and Paranaque, as well as the province of Cavite.

At the end of 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-4Q22. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:

  • 51% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
  • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV
  • 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV

POGOs occupy:

  • 5% of FLI’s office portfolio
  • 5% of MEG’s office portfolio
  • 1% of RLC’s office portfolio
Current account deficit mildly higher in 1Q23 due to wider trade deficit

The current account deficit was slightly higher at US$4.3bn in 1Q23 vs. US$4.0bn deficit in 1Q22. This is due to wider trade in goods deficit and lower net receipts in primary income account, offset by higher net receipts in services.

Trade in goods posted a wider deficit of US$17bn in 1Q23, up 8.2% YoY, due  to faster decline in exports (-10% YoY) vis-à-vis import drop (-0.5% YoY).

Net receipts of trade in services rose by 40.9% YoY to US$4.5bn due to upsurge in receipts on travel services (US$2.2bn, up 617% YoY) and higher BPO export revenues of US$6.7bn (+8.3% YoY).

Primary income posted net receipts of US$815mn in 1Q23, down by 29% YoY. Secondary income account posted slightly higher (+0.05% YoY) net receipts of US$7.3bn in 1Q23, driven by the 3.2% YoY increase in remittances.

The BSP expects the current account deficit to narrow to US$15.1bn (3.4% of GDP) in 2023 from US$17.8bn in 2022.

BSP trims BOP forecasts

The Monetary Board lowered its BOP, current account deficit projections. This was after taking into account 1Q23 developments.

For 2023, the BOP deficit is expected to narrow to US$1.2bn (-0.3% of GDP), lower than the previous forecast of US$1.6bn (-0.4% of GDP) given a narrower trade gap.   Import growth is likely to slow down sharply following the pullback of commodity prices while export growth is likely to be weaker.
