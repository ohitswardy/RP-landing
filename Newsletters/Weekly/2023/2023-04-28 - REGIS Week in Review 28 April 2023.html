REGIS REPORT

28 April 2023

The market ended +1.6% higher for the week at 6,625.08.

Turnover improved to US$104.7m for the week and headline foreign inflows stood at US$16.6m.

The move was led by banks as foreign demand continued to boost BDO and BPI shares on the back of strong earnings. BDO gained +6.7% WoW while BPI advanced + 4.7% WoW. Further upside can be expected as margins have yet to fully reflect policy rate hikes as well as higher credit card rate caps. 

BDO and BPI saw inflows of US$19.3m and US$8.6m respectively. 

The power sector also saw a boost as spot prices rose by P1.11 per kWh to P7.68, and are expected to stay elevated during the peak months of summer. Meralco (+5.6% WoW) outperformed the index as earnings were ahead of expectations. 1Q profits rose by 40% to Php9.1bn driven mostly by their generation arm. The distribution business only saw 2% growth (vs. 5% average), although this should rebound following the summer season. AP (+1.3%WoW) saw first quarter profits jump 161% YoY to Php7.5bn likewise driven by their generation business.

Earlier in the week, MPI (+14.8% WoW) finally disclosed a tender offer priced at Php4.63/sh. First Pacific, GT Capital, Mitsui, and MPI management are offering to buy the entire public float of 10.5bn shares (36.5% of outstanding shares). Should the tender offer be successful, MPI will be delisted from the exchange. Investors are speculating that BLOOM will take its place in the PCOMP. BLOOM shares soared +15.9% this week.  PAGCOR numbers showed gaming revenue jump 50% YoY and 6% QoQ, suggesting a strong performance from BLOOM. The company’s earnings are set to come out in the next few weeks. NIKL (-1.34% WoW) and CNPF (+4.2% WoW) are also potential candidates to take MPI’s place in the index.

Telcos were quiet as the sim registration deadline looms. PLDT (-5.1% WoW) & GLO (-4.7% WoW) both ended slightly lower as only ~45% of SIM cards have been registered, which could translate to a mild short-term impact on telco revenues. The sector saw the most outflows from foreign investors of US$4.9m for TEL and US$7.8m for GLO.

On the macro front, the BSP Governor Medalla hinted that policy rates may not be raised on the next monetary board meeting in May. This could suggest a near-term weakness in the peso especially as the market expects the Fed to raise rates next month. The peso closed at 55.33 (+1.2% WoW) against the dollar.

BDO Unibank - ROE momentum taking hold; raising TP to P160/sh
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Estimates upped 8% on 1Q23 trends; TP raised to P160/sh

BDO reported NPAT of P16.5bn in 1Q23, up 41% YoY, representing 25% of our previous full-year forecast. However, we expect quarterly earnings to improve through the year, on upward momentum to NIMs. Benign asset quality could provide a further boost. We increase our FY23/24E estimates by around 8%, and raise our target price by 9.6% to P160/sh, offering potential total return of 21% from current levels. The stock is up 28% ytd, vastly outperforming the sector and the broad market. That said, our ROE forecasts are firmly back in the mid-teens range, justifying further share price upside, in our view. Maintain Buy.

1Q23 NIM upward trend likely to hold, mindful of deposit competition

NIMs rose another 11bps QoQ and 48bps YoY in 1Q23, as higher asset yields more than offset slower loan growth (8% YoY vs 9% in 4Q22). Loan yields are still likely to climb, on delayed repricing of recent policy rate hikes, and on full implementation in February of the higher credit card rate cap. We are watchful, though, of intense deposit competition, as a surge in TDs saw deposit costs jump 30bps QoQ. Banks also absorb stamp duties on TDs, which helped push opex up 17% YoY in 1Q23. On balance, though, we reckon NIMs still have some headroom in coming quarters.

Asset quality holding steady (so far); lower credit costs provide upside risk

Provision expense fell 15% YoY in 1Q23, as NPLs were steady QoQ. Despite the lower provisions, NPL cover improved further to 170%. Thus far, the banking system has not seen much stress from higher interest rates. BDO is guiding for credit costs of 50bps this year (from 60-75bps in 2021/22). Should asset quality remain benign, we see further earnings upside from even lower credit costs.

TP raised to P160/sh, Buy maintained on higher sustainable ROEs

We adjust our NPAT estimates by 7.5-8% for 2023/24E. We also lift our target price by 9.6% to P160/sh, based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g), assuming ROE of 14.5% (was 14.0%), COE of 12% and growth of 8%. Despite the stock’s performance ytd, we believe the Buy call is justified by higher ROEs, which we believe can be sustained in the mid-teens range. Downside risks to our views include intensifying loan/deposit competition, and deteriorating asset quality.

 
Manila Electric Company - 1Q23: Blockbuster profits for generation; core distribution sales modestly up
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Generation profits lift core income to P9bn

Meralco reported 1Q23 net income of P8.07bn (+26% YoY), with core net income of P9.05bn, +40% YoY (adjusted). The bottom-line figure/s appear well ahead of Street estimates, as profits from power generation were surprisingly strong; Generation more than made up for the rather weak growth in unit sales for its distribution business.

Unit sales up only 2%, tariff review faces further delay

Unit sales for its power distribution business were up only 2% in 1Q23. While cooler weather was a reason, weaker industrial power use (-3% YoY) was also a factor. Notably, 1Q23 peak demand for both Meralco and the grid was actually marginally lower from last year. On the matter of its tariff review, the ERC has indicated that it will prioritize the rate review of the grid operator (NGCP). This likely means Meralco’s review will be delayed further beyond mid-2023.

Exceptional contributions from generation

For the first time in memory, the core power distribution business accounted for less than half (49%) of core income due to sharply higher contributions from its generation businesses. Its three largest generation investments all reported much better profits, with Pacific Light leading the way. 58%-owned Pacific Light reported income of P5.4bn, up 77% YoY. Even GBP finally reverted to the black with income of P295m. San Buenaventura, on the other hand, reported income of P911m for 1Q23, +139% YoY.

Power supply contracts for FY24-25 terminated

It appears Meralco has acquiesced to the termination of power supply agreements covering 1,800MW due in 2024/2025 from San Miguel. Meralco will now have to find other sources of power amidst a seller’s market that’s likely to persist (if not become more acute) over the next three years

 
Robinsons Land - Malls remain strong after holidays but will COVID restrictions be reinstated?
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q23 net profit jumps 90% YoY

Robinsons Land’s 1Q23 net profit grew 90% to P2.66bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. While multiple segments were slightly ahead of our expectations, the mall business was particularly noteworthy since: 1) revenue was higher than the seasonally strong 4Q, and 2) the EBIT margin was above 2019 levels. Meanwhile, residential sales also grew substantially and were similarly above pre-COVID levels.

Mall revenue inches up QoQ, margins surge

Despite 4Q being the seasonally strongest quarter, RLC’s 1Q23 mall revenue inched up 3% QoQ (46% YoY) to P3.9bn, 96% of the 2019 average. Even more remarkably, the mall EBIT margin climbed 16ppt YoY and 10ppt QoQ to 36.6%, higher than the 2019 level of 32%. We hope to get more details about the mall performance during RLC’s briefing on May 11.

Reservation sales soar above pre-COVID levels

Sales of both RLC’s standalone and JV projects surged in 1Q23. Reservation sales of standalone projects rose 94% YoY to P5.6bn, 112% of the 2019 average. Meanwhile, JV projects saw even faster growth, with sales growing 165% YoY (+142% QoQ) to P5.0bn, implying that demand for high-end projects might be even more robust.

Rise in COVID cases could lead to restrictions in a few provinces

In practice, there have been essentially no mobility restrictions in the Philippines since 3Q22. However, due to the recent increase in the number of COVID cases, the Department of Health reminded the public that a few provinces have officially been under Alert Level 2 since last year. While the developers have not been asked to reinstitute the restrictions yet, Alert Level 2 requires indoor establishments (such as restaurants) to operate at a maximum of 50% of capacity. While “only” about 15% of RLC’s mall area is in places under Alert Level 2 (less than 10% of mall revenue), the greater risk is that COVID cases will increase in more areas, prompting more Filipinos to stay home (whether the government raises Alert Levels or not).

 
Philippine Telecoms - SIM registration period extended
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Extension should mitigate the short-term disruption

The government announced that the period to register mobile SIMs is being extended to July 25, or for another 90 days. The extension should mitigate the short-term disruption to what should otherwise be a broadly revenue-neutral event. Longer-term, we view SIM registration as a positive development that will reduce fraud and hasten the adoption of mobile-based fintech services.

Ninety-day extension should reduce revenue impact

The 90-day extension for SIM registration announced by the government will, in our view, reduce the likely disruption arising from the involuntary deactivation of tens of millions of SIMs. We estimate only around 80m SIMs (out of a total 160m+ that are officially “live”) had been registered two days before the original deadline (April 26). Even after one adjusts for inactive SIMs, possibly 30% of the official number, there would have been tens of millions of SIMs involuntarily deactivated because the subscribers were either (1) unaware of the requirement or (2) didn’t have the IDs to comply with it.

While SIM registration has been viewed as a revenue-neutral event, we believe the short-term disruption will have real world consequences, including on actual mobile revenue. This extension will, in our view, mitigate the disruption, whose revenue impact we now expect to be no more than 5% over a couple of months.

More realistic subscriber numbers, fraud reduction, fintech boost

The official mobile subscriber base in the Philippines has always been bloated by the fluff of inactive SIMs that have yet to be officially disconnected (churned). Annualized churn for prepaid service is over 40% as SIMs are treated as disposable and changed frequently. We expect SIM registration to reduce churn and thus the “fluff”, and ultimately allow the reported subscriber figures (and also ARPU) to be more representative of reality.

Beyond more accurate subscriber numbers, SIM registration is likely to also help reduce fraud and speed up the adoption of mobile-based fintech. The SIM registration mandate comes less than a year after the authorities issued licenses to six digital banks and should be a boon for app-based lenders. The two leading telcos, PLDT and Globe, both have stakes in mobile app operators that offer consumer credit.

 
Metro Pacific - Consortium offers to buy public float, delist shares
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Tender offer for entire public float

A consortium of (a) First Pacific (142 HK), (b) GT Capital (GTCAP PM), (c) a firm jointly owned by Japan-based Mitsui and Japan Overseas Infrastructure Investment (Mit-Pacific), and (d) MPI’s management, led by Mr. Manuel Pangilinan, disclosed that it is undertaking a tender offer for MPI’s entire public float, equivalent to 10.5bn shares and representing 36.5% of its outstanding stock. The tender offer period will run from “late” May 2023 to “late” Jun 2023.

Offer price of P4.63/sh is well below NAV

The consortium is offering a price of P4.63/sh, which is a 22% premium to its one-year VWAP. However, it is a mere 8% premium to the last closing price and well below our estimate of its NAV (P7.50/share) based on the market price of its stake in Meralco (MER PM).

New ownership structure

Should the tender offer be successful, it will pave the way for MPI’s shares to be de-listed from the exchange. First Pacific will remain MPI’s largest shareholder, with its economic stake rising to 49.8% from 46.1%. GTCAP will also raise its stake to 20.0% from 17.1%. The new Japanese investors, Mit-Pacific, will own a 20% stake, while MIG Holdings, a company controlled by Mr. Manuel Pangilinan, will own 10.2%. There is also an agreement between the parties that under certain conditions, Mit-Pacific can exchange all or part of its investment in MPI into shares in one of MPI’s key subsidiaries and/or operating companies, subject to agreement among shareholders.

Cost-benefit analysis of participating in the tender offer

Under the rules of the Philippine Stock Exchange, the consortium needs to hold 95% of MPI’s outstanding shares before the shares can be de-listed. To reach that, the acceptance rate of the tender offer must be at least 86% (or 9bn shares out of the 10.5bn target shares). If this is not met, the consortium may opt not to push through with the tender offer.

Note, however, the disclosure also says that each of the bidders may, at its sole discretion, withdraw, extend, or accept the Tender Offer Shares in accordance with the specific terms and conditions set out in the tender offer. Even though we believe the offer undervalues MPI, refusing to participate in the tender could risk the shareholders getting stuck with shares that will most likely be (a) removed from the PCOMP index and (b) become even more illiquid. On the other hand, MPI has plans to conduct IPOs of its tollroad and water units. This should potentially unlock value and benefit MPI’s shareholders in the future.

MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP chief sees pause in rate hikes next month

The BSP will unlikely mimic the monetary policy actions of other central banks, particularly the Federal Reserve according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla. Medalla said the BSP is likely to pause its tightening cycle as early as next month if the inflation downtrend continues in the coming months. Inflation cooled to a 6-month low of 7.6% in March, with MoM at -0.2%. Assuming flat MoM, April inflation rate could go down to 6.8%. The BSP Monetary Board is set to meet on May 18. The BSP has so far raised interest rates by 425bps, bringing the benchmark rate to a 16-year high of 6.25%. Gap between Fed funds rate and BSP policy rate is 150bps, narrower than historical average of 350bps.

Electricity spot prices rise in April as demand peaks

The Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) said the average electricity spot market price in early April rose by P1.11 per kilowatt-hour (kWh) to P7.68 as Luzon’s peak demand came in at 12,221 megawatts (MW) in April, the high for the year so far, and exceeding the 2022 peak of 12,103 MW recorded in May. Peak demand in the Visayas was 2,380 MW in April, exceeding the 2022 high of 2,297 MW recorded in September. Mindanao’s peak demand in April was 2,363 MW, exceeding the region’s 2022 peak of 2,167 MW recorded in June. As of April 23, IEMOP said average supply was 14,612 MW against average demand of 11,033 MW. In March, average supply was 14,226 MW and average demand 10,244 MW. While demand for electricity increased due to rising temperatures brought about by the summer season, the market remained stable, with ample supply levels across all grids. Given the current trends, especially with El Niño, the probability of prices remaining at the P7 per kWh level in May is high – IEMOP.

Excise tax on soda among measures eyed to boost revenues

The administration is expanding fiscal consolidation, as it looks to widen its revenue streams through an excise tax on sweetened beverages, motor vehicle road user’s tax, and a mining fiscal regime. These proposed tax measures are still being deliberated by House lawmakers.

 

VAT collections up 20% YoY in 2022

The Bureau of Internal Revenue announced that it collected P463bn in VAT in 2022, up 20% YoY. This is also 14% higher than 2019 VAT collections of P406bn. Moving forward, the shift to quarterly filing of VAT returns will afford taxpayers relief from the former system of monthly filing. VAT filing deadline for 1Q is April 25. The BIR is targeting to collect P2.6tn in revenue this year, up 14% YoY. As of 2M23, BIR collections are up 9.6% YoY to P364bn.

Gov’t maintains GDP growth targets; Sees 1Q23 GDP growth “closer” to 7.1%

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) maintained its medium-term economic growth targets. 2023: +6-7%, 2024-2028: 6.5%-8%. NEDA Secretary Arsenio Balisacan projects 1Q23 GDP to be “closer” to the 7.1% print in 4Q22. Note that GDP is scheduled to released on May 11 (consensus: +7.2%). 1Q22 GDP grew by 8.2% YoY. DBCC raised its inflation target to 5-7%, higher than its previous assumption of 2.5%-4.5%. DBCC kept its budget deficit-to-GDP ratio target of 6.1% this year, but raised 2024-2028 revenue assumptions slightly to take into account new taxes. The 2024 revenue is projected to rise to P6.62tn by 2028 (previously P6.58tn) from P3.73tn in 2023. PIFITA (Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act) will generate P8.5bn by 2024 / VAT on digital service providers is expected to yield P13.7bn in revenues by 2024.

 

The two bills are still at the Senate. Economic managers are still pushing for the passage of three tax reform measures to boost revenues, such as the excise tax on sweetened beverages, motor vehicle road user’s tax, and mining fiscal regime. Excise tax on sweetened beverages is expected to generate P53.7bn

modern vehicle road user’s tax will reach P15.8bn in the first year and up to P48.6bn by the third year. The proposed fiscal regime for the mining sector is expected to yield P12.4bn in 2025, P12.9bn in 2026, P13.4bn in 2027, and P13.9bn in 2028. The Lower House approved the mining fiscal regime, which would bring the country’s effective tax rate on mining (considering all taxes) to 51%, up from 38% under the current system.
