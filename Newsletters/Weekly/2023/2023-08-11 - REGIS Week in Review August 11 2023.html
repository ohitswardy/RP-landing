REGIS REPORT

11 August 2023

The PSEi headed lower for the week to 6,405.91 (-0.7% WoW) as trading was generally mixed after 2Q23 GDP figures came out well below expectations at 4.3% (vs. 6% estimate).

Gainers outnumbered losers 5:2.

However, the market still ended in the red as it was dragged by large caps SMPH (-7.4% WoW) and its parent company SM (-2.9% WoW) on the back of the suspension of reclamation projects in Manila Bay. SMPH has two reclamation projects in Manila Bay (in Pasay and in Paranaque). Based on Regis estimates, the impact of this could be very material as it consists of 660 hectares (37% of NAV estimate). SMPH saw US$3.2m worth of outflows. On the flipside, ALI rose +2.3% WoW as it saw foreign inflows worth US$4.1m, being the top bought stock for the week.

Consumer names also ended mixed. URC gained +1.8% WoW despite earnings tracking mildly behind estimates as the ERP system transition earlier this year continued to weigh on 1Q. However, operating margin for the domestic BCF was stable YoY, despite cost pressures from certain raw materials while international BCF saw a more meaningful margin expansion due to operating leverage and other opex initiatives. Meanwhile, JFC (-4.7% WoW) fell sharply despite EBIT figures for 1H23 coming out at Php7.6bn (+51% YoY).

Banks outperformed as the market awaits the Bangko Sentral’s move in the next policy meeting on August 17. In recent briefings, BSP Governor Remolona has spoken of potential tightening to head off potential inflation given the recent spikes in rice and fuel prices. BDO and MBT rose +1.8% WoW and +4.0% WoW respectively.

Earlier in the week, MPI (+3.1% WoW) finally launched its long-awaited tender offer at Php5.20/sh as shareholders approved the plan to delist the company. Consequently, BLOOM (+0.9% WoW) was actively traded as investors bet on its inclusion in the PSEi to replace MPI. BLOOM earnings also came out well ahead of estimates, as volumes were substantially higher across the board. Management also hinted that it may make a more aggressive push into the online casino space. Despite the higher base, the result is still ahead of forecasts and likely to prompt street upgrades.

Headline foreign outflows registered at US$16.3m while daily turnover softened back to US$66.3m (-52.4% WoW).

Universal Robina - 2Q23: Top line crimped by logistics issues, inflation
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H23 net profit +7% to P6.7bn, broadly in line

URC reported 1H23 net profit of P6.7bn, up 7% YoY. While this appears to be trending slightly behind FY23 expectations, we believe operations should improve going into 2H23. Consolidated revenues were in line at P78.6bn (+11% YoY), while EBIT is slightly behind at P8.6bn (+16% YoY). By segment, it was the non-branded consumer food (BCF) segments that outperformed with revenues +23% YoY and EBIT +32% YoY. For 2Q23 alone, URC’s EBIT rose 18% YoY, also driven primarily by non-BCF (EBIT +29% YoY).

PH BCF: top line crimped by system/logistics issues; margins stable YoY

Domestic BCF revenues grew 6% YoY in 2Q23 as volumes across most categories were sustained. Management noted that issues arising from an ERP system transition in early 2023 haven’t been fully resolved and had an impact on sales. Order fill rates, however, are improving MoM (now at c80% from 60% in early-1Q), and we expect URC to put this behind it soon. EBIT margin was stable YoY at 11.4% in 2Q23, despite pressure from the prices of certain commodities (e.g. coffee, potato, sugar) continue to be volatile. URC raised prices by ~4% in April, but the full impact is not likely to be felt until 2H.

Int’l BCF: performance mixed amidst inflationary pressures

Int’l BCF revenues were flat YoY and down 11% QoQ. URC made significant price moves in key markets like Thailand and Indonesia, where the results had been mixed given the stickiness of price points and weak consumer sentiment amidst high inflation. As a response, URC had to recalibrate some of its price points and adjust pack sizes/grammage accordingly to preserve margins. While the top line was relatively soft, EBIT jumped 19% YoY during the quarter on a significant improvement in margins (+130bps YoY).

Management weighs in on proposed taxes on ‘junk food’, single-use plastics

Management is of the view that the ‘junk food’ tax being proposed by the government could pose challenges given the broad definition of what ‘junk food’ could be. We share the view of management that the proposal is a regressive tax that could have knock-on effects on consumer sentiment, particularly for lower[1]income consumers. As for the tax on single-use plastics, the proposed law is targeting carrier bags for retail stores and not packaging for food and beverage products and should not directly affect URC.

Balancing act continues but sequential improvement seen; tweaking estimates and TP (+4% to P172/sh)

We believe the momentum continues to be positive heading into 2H23, notwithstanding the higher base of comparison YoY. It will continue to be a balancing act for URC as it deals with input cost volatility, but price increases and other cost initiatives should help support margins in the near-term. We tweak our 2023-24E estimates and roll over our TP to P172/sh (+4%), which implies 24x 2024E PE. Key downside risks relate to consumer sentiment and cost inflation. Maintaining Buy.

 
SM Prime - Strong 2Q mall revenue and presales; reclamation review raises risks
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Philippine mall business remains strong but residential revenue disappoints

SM Prime’s 1H23 net profit rose 38% to P19.4bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. However, the various segments showed mixed results: residential revenue was below pre-COVID levels while the domestic mall business showed remarkable strength.

Philippine mall revenue well above pre-COVID levels

Consumer spending remained strong in 2Q23, with Philippine mall revenue growing 30% YoY (+4% QoQ). This brought 1H23 mall revenue up 53% to P31.5bn, 12% above the 1H19 figure. Breaking this down, 1H23 rental revenue rose 42% to P26.3bn, slightly ahead of our forecast, while cinema and other revenue saw an even bigger increase (+156%) to P5.2bn (also ahead of our estimates).

Residential revenue remains below 2019 levels despite strong presales

Although 2Q23 reservation sales fell 9% QoQ (up 16% YoY) to P32.7bn, we consider this a strong result since it is still 45% above the 2019 average. On the other hand, while reservation sales have been above pre-COVID levels since 2020, residential revenue has not recovered to 2019 levels, likely due to a combination of: 1) looser payment schemes delaying revenue recognition and 2) cancellation rates remaining high.

DENR to review all Manila Bay reclamation projects

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said that it would review all Manila Bay reclamation projects, citing that several individual projects were approved without considering the combined impact on the environment. We believe the DENR review will raises the risk that SM Prime’s 300-hectare Paranaque reclamation project will not secure final approval from the national government. The implications of non-approval would be significant since the Paranaque reclamation comprises 17% of our SMPH NAV estimate.

1H earnings rise 32% to P36.5bn

Century Pacific Food - 2Q23: Adjusting to elevated costs
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H23 NPAT +8% YoY to P3.2bn; behind as OEM segment underperforms

CNPF’s net profit rose 8% YoY to P3.2bn in 1H23, tracking behind Regis FY23E but broadly in line with consensus. The underperformance was largely due to the non-branded OEM segment, which saw revenues drop 7% YoY. Branded revenues held up despite a high YoY base, rising 11% YoY (in line). Consolidated EBITDA grew 10% YoY, also tracking mildly below estimates. For 2Q23 alone, net profit rose 10% YoY. 2Q saw both CNPF and consumer adjusting to elevated input costs: the former by cost-cutting efforts and the latter by downtrading.

2Q top line growth for branded segment mostly from price increases

Revenues of the branded segment (83% of sales) grew 12% QoQ / 15% YoY. On a QoQ basis, this was due to the normalization of the supply of sardines, which faced some constraints in 1Q. On a YoY basis, this was driven by the cumulative impact of price increases since 2022, which we estimate was 10-12%. Volume growth was relatively soft at 3-5% by our estimate, though the higher YoY base due to election spending was also a factor.

Opex control cushions input cost inflation

Gross margin was flattish, though tight opex control led to a mild expansion (+50bps YoY) in EBITDA. Inventory costs were still proportionately higher due to some inventory carried over from 2H22, though the margin impact was mitigated by price increases. CNPF also held back on discretionary spending (e.g. A&P) to cushion the impact of higher input costs.

Sequential margin improvement seen but inflation continues to weigh on consumer sentiment

Management continues to observe downtrading across the various categories as higher prices of goods continues to pressure the average consumer. With the high base of comparison in 2H22, headline YoY growth in revenue is likely to decelerate further. However, the company is optimistic with respect to margins despite the rise in prices of skipjack tuna given (1) prices of other inputs (meat, milk, coconut) are stable and (2) pre-emptive price actions have already been undertaken.

1H23 profit -28% to P1.2bn - behind estimates

DNL’s 2Q23 income amounted to P646mn (9%, -31%YoY), dragged by depressed profits from oleochemicals (-51%YoY). Contribution from the food segment saw some improvement while still being 13%lower vs last year. By our estimates, overall volumes fell further (-30YoY%) in 2Q23, dragged by both food (-48YoY%) and oleochemical commodities (-34YoY%). The result brings 1H23 profit to P1.2bn (-28%YoY) – behind both Regis (P3.3bn) and consensus (P3.4bn) estimates for the full-year.

Demand for food ingredients drops further but GPM improving

Profit from its food segment amounted to P264mn in 2Q23, a sequential improvement of 29%, but still lower by 13% from last year. We estimate that it benefited from a jump in GPM (>16% in 2Q23 from 13.6% in 1Q23) as raw material prices stabilized. Overall food volumes (-35% in 2Q23 from -25% in 1Q23) weakened further, but the high margin side is starting to see some recovery (- 2% in 2Q23 from -8% in 1Q23). Management cited the inflationary environment earlier in the year for the soft demand, and it expects continued improvement in 2H23 on improved volume and margin outlook.

Competition in biodiesel, low demand for construction chemicals weigh on Oleochem business

Profit from the oleochemical segment was stable QoQ but was 51% lower YoY. Volumes worsened as commodities fell (-34%YoY), reflecting the competitive dynamics in biodiesel as well specialty chemicals typically used in construction. Against the soft demand, we estimate that GPM for the segment improved by 3.2ppt QoQ to 21.6%, mimicking the positive margin trend in food.

Batangas facility open with limited orders, could depress profits until 2024

Full commercial operations at the Batangas plant started in July, which could add at least P400mn in annual expenses. Management said that there is one operational line, but we think utilization is likely to remain well under 20% over the next 6 months. They could shift some export orders from existing facilities to the new plant to help ramp up, but low utilization levels may weigh on profits well into 2024.

SM Prime - Government suspends all Manila Bay reclamation projects
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

All Manila Bay reclamation projects suspended

In a press briefing, Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo-Loyzaga said that all 22 reclamation projects in Manila Bay are under review and are thus deemed suspended. In addition to SM Prime’s 300-hectare Paranaque reclamation project, it appears that the suspension will also affect the company’s 360-hectare Pasay project, which has already begun reclamation. The two projects account for a combined 37% of our SMPH NAV estimate.

Why are Manila Bay reclamation projects being reviewed?

Last week, Secretary Yulo-Loyzaga said that the DENR would review all Manila Bay reclamation projects, citing that several individual projects were approved [by the previous administration] without considering the combined impact on the environment.

What is the status of SM Prime’s reclamation projects? SM Prime has two reclamation projects in Manila Bay. These are in partnership with the city governments of Pasay and Paranaque, which are adjacent cities. The 300-hectare Paranaque reclamation project has not yet received approval from the national government.

On the other hand, the Notice to Proceed for the 360-hectare Pasay reclamation was granted in 2019. Due to the pandemic, however, SM Prime did not start reclamation work until 2021. A 60-hectare island was just completed recently and two 150-hectare islands are still in the process of reclamation.

During the 2Q23 briefing of SM Investments, SM Prime CFO John Ong said that the company has not received a notice to suspend operations and that reclamation was still ongoing. Presumably, the DENR will send such notice soon.

How large is the impact to SM Prime?

Not only does the DENR review reduce the likelihood that the Paranaque project will be approved, but it even places the Pasay project at risk. Thus, the impact to SM Prime could be very large since the Paranaque portion comprises 17% of our NAV estimate while the Pasay portion accounts for another 20% (3% from the completed 60-hectare island and 17% from the two 150-hectare islands still under construction).

In short, if both the Pasay and Paranaque reclamation projects will be canceled, we would slash our NAV estimate (currently at P48.27/share) by about 35%.

Converge ICT - 2Q23: Steady net adds
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Reported profit rises 8%

1H23 income for Converge ICT came in P4.29bn, +8% higher YoY. Implied 2Q23 income of P2.11bn is +7% higher YoY but down 3% QoQ. While the net profit figures look on trend to beat Street estimates, the underlying operations (especially revenue) show a more modest performance. One positive is that net subscriber additions for the quarter improved to 49k, half of which were prepaid.

Revenue lifted by enterprise segment, costs “down” 10%

Revenue for Converge’s enterprise segment rose +26% in 1H, helping to lift overall revenue growth to +8%. The core residential segment (86% of revenue) saw revenue growth of a more modest 6%. In contrast, cost of services was actually down 10% over the half year, but this was largely the result of accounting changes that deferred recognition of customer installation costs.

Prepaid subscribers boost net adds

The net subscriber adds in 2Q (at 49k) improved from 1Q (43k). We view the net adds number positively, as PLDT added only 13k wired subs in 2Q23. Around half (25k) of 2Q net adds were prepaid, bringing total prepaid subscribers to 54k as of June. This places Converge in a good position to reach its goal of 120k by year-end. Converge’s port utilization is just around 25%, implying it has lots of room to aggressively seek new subscribers.

Churn creeps up

Churn rose to 2.04% in 2Q due primarily to customer affordability reasons. This is a potential issue in the future as subscriber growth is no longer as fast. The churn numbers imply that subscribers equivalent to over 80% of gross adds are being disconnected. A slight reduction in gross adds would easily tip the net add figures into the negative.

Capex drop may prompt payment of dividend by next year

Capex for this year is expected to drop to P12-15bn (vs. P19.4bn in 2022), taking free cash flow (FCF) into positive territory. Management may decide to institute a dividend policy by 1Q24 as a consequence of the improved FCF.

The bidders reiterated that the tender offer will not proceed if MPI will not be delisted.

The 2Q23 EBITDA hit P5.5bn (-4%QoQ, 42%YoY) – with 1H23 EBITDA of P11.2bn (+67%YoY) also tracking ahead of Regis (P19.1bn) and consensus (P19.9bn). This was despite early indications of a very weak May GGR performance due to VIP. The strength of the mass segments tempered the impact from the low VIP win rate.

 

The 2Q23 GGR only softened a little bit (-6%QoQ, 15%YoY). While VIP GGR fell 26%QoQ, it was nevertheless 7% higher vs last year. VIP GGR in the quarter of P4.7bn implies some recovery in June from early indications of very low GGR in May. Hold was expectedly lower at 2.73% in 2Q23 (vs theoretical 2.85% and unusually high VIP hold of 3.8% in 1Q23). Slot machine GGR continued to outperform at +13%QoQ and +17%YoY. Mass table GGR increased 3%QoQ and 22%YoY.

DMCI – Construction, mining units weigh on 2Q23 performance

DMCI’s 1H23 recurring profit (ex-forex adj, one-offs) fell 19% YoY to P16.03bn, tracking slightly behind Regis’ FY23E but appears in line with consensus. In 2Q23 alone, profit was lower by 5% YoY at P8.17bn, pulled down by the poor performance of the construction and coal/nickel mining units. With the exception of the power units, slow demand recovery for each business unit was quite apparent. DMCI is trading at a 16% discount to its market-based NAV, broadly in line with historical average (15%). 

Property cancellations moderate. Profit contributions from DMCI Homes jumped 26% QoQ as cancellations moderated to just 14% of revenues from 24%. However, this was higher than last year’s 7% rate. Reservation sales declined by 6% YoY in 2Q23, taking 1H23 reservation sales up 19% YoY to 90% of pre-Covid levels. Though this was largely due to higher ASPs, as unit sales alone were just at c. 70% of pre-Covid levels. Cancellations relative to sold/reserved units also eased, with 1H23 down to just 12% from 19% last year. 

Maynilad: anemic billed volumes. Standalone profit from Maynilad jumped 35% YoY to P2.2bn and EBITDA rose 14% YoY, buoyed largely by the tariff hike in January. Billed volumes remained anemic, rising by just +1% YoY to 99% of 2Q19 levels, as the recovery of the industrial segment remained slow. The 17-Q did not provide data on capex.

Project delays hit construction unit. The construction unit did poorly in 2Q, as revenues fell 21% YoY/7% QoQ. Management attributed this to delays in key infrastructure projects, as well as the deficiency of projects. The order book dropped by 12% YoY and 9% QoQ to P38bn due to the lack of progress in project awarding and rescoping of the NSCR Package 2.

Lower nickel prices offset higher shipments. The nickel unit’s 2Q23 profit collapsed 51% YoY/47% QoQ due to lower ASPs and higher non-cash costs. Shipments rebounded by 33% YoY after securing new permits in January.

Strong performance for off-grid power unit. Energy sales rose 11% YoY and lifted earnings for its off-grid unit by 13% YoY in 2Q23.  Apart from higher demand, the company also increased its market share in Palawan.

 

FGEN 1H23 profit of $168mn (31%YoY) is ahead; 2Q slightly lower on lower availability of gas plants

FGEN’s recurring income for 1H23 hit $168mn (31%YoY), tracking ahead of estimates, on the back of a sharp rise in contribution from EDC. This implies 2Q23 recurring income was $79mn (12%QoQ, 14%YoY). Nat gas earnings dipped slightly in 2Q to an estimated $45mn (-6%QoQ, -9%YoY), coming from 1Q23 that benefitted from high dispatch levels and full availability. Management mentioned that some plants encountered some outages in 2Q23. EDC’s attributable income remained elevated vs the low base last year – (up 4%QoQ, 92%YoY to $38mn in 2Q23).

Compared to last year, EDC wasn’t buying as much replacement power. It continued to benefit from higher power prices both in the spot market and WESM. Regis estimates hydro incurred a loss of about $1.7mn in 2Q23 on lower water levels (seasonal plus some early El Nino impact).

 

Lease of LNG to Razon group is still being negotiated. FSRU is on its way to Subic to receive its first shipment of LNG and is soon entering wet commissioning phase. Commercial operations should start soon.

ACEN to develop 1,000MW floating solar project in Laguna Lake

ACEN Corp said it is set to develop a large-scale floating solar project in Laguna Lake with an expected capacity of 1,000MW. According to ACEN CEO Eric Francia, the company was awarded 800 hectares. The work is currently in the pre-development phase. In 2022, Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA) offered 2,000 hectares of Laguna de Bay for floating solar projects for a 25-year contract. ACEN subsidiaries signed an RE contract area utilization agreement with LLDA for the lease of 800 hectares of renewable energy areas. The company is targeting to expand its RE capacity to 20GW by 2030 from the current 4,200MW attributable capacity.

Megaworld 1H23 net profit rises 34%, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts

Megaworld’s 1H23 earnings rose 34% to P7.9bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. 2Q23 reservation sales grew 31% YoY to P36.5bn, equivalent to 98% of the 2019 average. Sales in Fort Bonifacio were strong – Uptown Bonifacio and McKinley West townships. Office revenue was flat QoQ (+4% YoY) at P3.1bn, suggesting that the occupancy rate was likely maintained at 90%. Mall revenue increased 5% QoQ (+66% YoY) to P1.3bn, 85% of the 2Q19 level.


Robinsons Land’s 1H23 net profit climbs 23%, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts

RLC’s 1H23 earnings climbed 23% to P5.78bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Excluding income from China in 2Q22, 1H23 earnings would have grown 71% YoY (to P5.78bn). Revenue appears to be in line, so the outperformance at the bottom line might have been due to margins or other items (such as interest expense, taxes, etc.). For 2Q23 reservation sales jumped 120% YoY (+21% QoQ) to P6.8bn, a record level and 35% higher than the 2019 average. 2Q23 mall revenue was flat QoQ (+27% YoY) to P3.85bn, similar to pre-COVID levels and broadly in line with Regis forecasts. The mall revenues of Ayala Land and SM Prime both increased c. 5% QoQ . Office revenue grew 1% QoQ (+4% YoY), implying that occupancy was similar to the 1Q23 level of 89%.

Cebu Air (CEB PM) 2Q23 EBITDA reverts to 78% of 2019 average – In line

Recurring profit (excluding one-offs, FX, MTM of CBs) was higher by 25% QoQ to P1.69bn in 2Q23. While 1H23 core profit of P3.04bn is 74% of Regis FY23E, this was largely due to a higher tax benefit. Revenues were 9% higher QoQ, as pax volumes improved 14% QoQ to 5.46mn, 8% below 2Q19. The company cited (a) slow recovery of short-haul international pax volumes, which remained well below 2019 and (b) operational challenges, including global industry challenges and weather-related issues. The 1H23 revenues more than doubled to P44bn, broadly in line with estimates. EBITDA improved 32% QoQ to P5.46bn, 78% of 2019 average (vs 59% of pre-Covid in 1Q23). 1H23 EBITDA stood at P9.59bn (In line), reversing losses in the same period last year.

July tourist data - big jumps in Korean, Chinese, Japanese visitors

Tourist arrivals jumped in July, up 75% YoY and up 15% MoM to 516,304, a new post-Covid high. This is roughly 75% of the 2019 monthly average (72% of July 2019). For 7M23, the Philippines took in 3.21mn tourists, up 189% YoY and coming in at about 66% of 7M2019 levels. July was notable in that Regis saw big jumps in arrivals from Korea, China and Japan, all of which are at post-Covid highs. Korean tourists are already at 84% of the 2019 monthly average, while Japanese visitors are about halfway there. Chinese tourists, meanwhile, are just under a quarter of where they were pre-Covid. It is difficult to estimate, though, how much of the Chinese visitors were POGO employees. The improving tourism picture bodes well for the Current Account, and the economy in general. In terms of stocks, BLOOM and CEB are the likely main beneficiaries.

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.92bn in June

Preliminary data showed that the trade deficit in June shrunk by 33% YoY and 12% MoM to US$3.92bn. Imports dropped 15% YoY on base effects from high commodity prices last year. Fuel and raw material imports fell 43% YoY / 15% YoY. Consumer goods and capital goods imports were flattish YoY. Exports grew 0.8% YoY in June after rising +2.4% YoY in May.

Exports to China posted positive growth rates for two consecutive months. Exports to HK also rebounded (+16% YoY), reversing declines in the past six months. This brings 1H23 trade deficit to US$27.96bn, down 6% YoY. The government is forecasting exports to grow by 1% YoY and imports to grow by 2% YoY in 2023, suggesting a trade deficit of c. US$60bn (~14% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 stood at US$58.244bn (14% of GDP).

GIR settles at US$99.7bn in end-July

Preliminary data showed the GIR level rose by US$315mn (+0.3%) MoM to US$99.7bn in end-July, The government cited increase in gold holdings and FX operations, which more than offset a decrease in net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. The latest GIR level represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports.

Inflation eases further to 4.7% in July

Headline CPI rose 4.7% YoY and 0.1% MoM in July, easing from June (+5.4% YoY, +0.2% MoM). This came in below Bloomberg consensus estimate of 4.9% but at the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.1%-4.9%. Energy-related base effects continued to be the main driver for the YoY deceleration, with transport CPI down 4.6% YoY. Rice prices continued to creep up MoM, up +0.9% in July from just +0.5% in June, pushing overall food CPI up 0.5% MoM in July (vs. +0.3% in June).

Meanhwile, core inflation rose at a slower rate of 6.7% in July from 7.4% in June. Non-food core CPI was up just +0.3% MoM and eased to +5.4% YoY. Note that the minimum wage increase for NCR (+7%) was implemented on July 16. This brings 7M23 average inflation rate to 6.8%. The BSP retained its view that inflation will decelerate back to its 2%-4% target band next quarter. BSP cited higher wages and lingering food supply constraints, recent storms, and higher transport costs as key risks to inflation.

The BSP said that it “stands ready to adjust the monetary policy stance as necessary to prevent the further broadening of price pressures…” Note that the gap between the BSP’s policy rate and the Fed’s policy rate has narrowed to 100bps, after the BSP paused rate hikes while the Fed hiked rates by 50bps since May 2023. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 17; Fed’s next policy meeting is on Sep 21.
