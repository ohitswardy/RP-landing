REGIS REPORT

22 December 2023

The index gained +0.4% WoW and managed to close above 6500, a continuation from its rally from the previous week after the Fed paused rates during its last meeting of the year.

Gainers outnumbered losers 18:13.

Property names led the market. Ayala Land (+3.5% WoW) ran on the back of strong foreign demand. Inflows stood at US$17.1m for the week. SMPH closed unchanged, but laggard RLC (+2.2% WoW) has been playing catch up. Recently, RLC disclosed that Lance Gokongwei will take over as President and CEO of the company starting January 8 next year, after current president Frederick Go has been appointed as Cabinet Secretary. He will serve as the Chairperson of the Economic Development Group, and will supervise the Department of Finance (DOF), National Economic Development Authority (NEDA), Department and Industry, among other agencies.

Consumer names ended mixed after rice prices seem to be on the rise once again as export prices out of Thailand are bouncing back, while those from Vietnam are seeing new highs. URC’s (-3.7% WoW) share price continues to be dampened by heavy foreign selling. Outflows for URC were significant at US$3.3m. Meanwhile, JFC (+1.6% WoW) bucked the trend despite inflationary pressures.

Conglos likewise ended mixed. GTCAP (+3.6% WoW) ran after Toyota continued to gain market share for the month of November, despite vehicle sales slowing down overall. Despite that, Toyota gained market share by growing 11% YoY and 1% MoM. Ayala Corp (-2.3% WoW) declined after it disclosed that it is taking a significant minority stake in a chain of pharmacies in Northern Luzon. St. Joseph Drug has 112 stores, which will add to AC Health’s Generika chain of 742 and bring it closer to its goal of 1,000 stores by 2025.

Among the biggest drags to the market was ACEN (-5.7% WoW). ACEN saw outflows worth US$7.1m as foreign funds are likely liquidating their positions.

Headline daily turnover was decent at US$100.9m. However, this was mostly skewed by an AXLM block that flowed through last Wednesday as MPI bought a stake into the company.

Headline foreign were likewise skewed. Outflows seemed heavy at US$12.8m, but excluding the AXLM transaction, the market actually saw inflows worth US$17.7m.

Robinsons Land - RLC CEO to take new position in Philippine government
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

New government position created for Frederick Go

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. created a new position for Frederick Go, who is currently the Presidential Adviser on Investment and Economic Affairs on top of his role as CEO of Robinsons Land. Since Mr. Go’s new position will be the same level as Cabinet Secretary, it is likely that he will be required to step down as President/CEO of Robinsons Land.

Mr. Go to be Head of the Economic Development Group

President Marcos appointed Mr. Go as the Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (SAPIEA), which is a newly created position/office. As the SAPIEA, Mr. Go will serve as the Chairperson of the Economic Development Group, which was previously chaired by the Finance Secretary, and will supervise the following agencies:

  • Department of Finance (DOF)
  • National Economic Development Authority (NEDA)
  • Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)
  • Other agencies, such as Board of Investments (BOI), Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA), and Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)

Will Frederick Go step down as CEO of RLC?

Given the level of this new position (i.e. Cabinet Secretary level), Mr. Go’s appointment may have to be approved by the Commission on Appointments. If that is the case, we would expect Mr. Go to be required to step down as President and CEO of Robinsons Land.

Who might be the next President of Robinsons Land?

Should Frederick Go indeed be required to step down as CEO, we believe the most likely candidate to replace him is his brother, Faraday Go, who is currently Executive Vice President and Head of the Mall Division, the largest business of Robinsons Land. Prior to leading the mall segment, Faraday Go was the head of the Office Division of RLC. Robinsons Land has not yet disclosed any changes to its management structure to the Philippine Stock Exchange. RLC is trading at 62% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 7.1X FY24E P/E.

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Merger of BPI and Robinsons Bank receives BSP approval

The BSP approved the merger between BPI and Robinsons Bank Corp (RBC). JG Summit (JGS PM) owns 60% of RBC while Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) owns the remaining 40%. BPI will be the surviving entity after the merger. The Gokongwei group will own 6% of BPI after the merger.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market stays flat in November

Regis estimates that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 3bps MoM to 20.2%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index also increased 3bps MoM to 24.5%. Net foreign buying stood at US$18mn, a reversal from US$171mn of net foreign selling in September. Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 19 saw declines in foreign ownership while 11 experienced increases

The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:

URC – foreign ownership fell 29bps MoM to 24.7%. It also experienced US$6.4mn of net foreign selling, the third highest for the month

ICT – foreign ownership increased 30bps MoM to 32.9%. It also saw the strongest net foreign buying, at US$44.2mn

 

Ayala Corp acquires stake in North Luzon-based pharma, St Joseph Drug

Ayala Corp’s healthcare unit, AC Health, acquired a “significant minority stake” in retail pharmacy, St. Joseph Drug (Joleco Resources, Inc.). The definitive agreements were signed last December 15 between St. Joseph Drug and AC Health’s pharmaceutical arm, AHCHI Pharma Ventures Inc. (APV). APV owns Generika Drugstore, the pioneer in generic retail pharmacies, and I.E. Medica and MedEthix, its pharma importation and distribution arms. The acquisition enables AC Health to strengthen its pharma footprint and reach more Filipinos throughout the country. St. Joseph Drug has 112 stores across Northern Luzon. AC Health currently has 742 Generika stores; It is targeting to have 1,000 stores by 2025. AC Health is a wholly owned unit of Ayala Corp (AC PM); AC Health incurred P304mn net loss in 9M23.

PSE allowed to take over PDS

The SEC has approved the application of the PSE for exemptive relief, allowing it to acquire additional shares in the Philippine Dealing System Holdings Corp (PDS). The PSE currently owns 21% of PDS and wants to increase its stake to 100%. This will allow the integration of the equity and fixed-income exchanges. The SEC directed the PSE to submit a detailed and time-bound operational plan, which shall be subject to a separate review and approval by the Commission.

Meralco heeds ERC, revises terms of CSP for 1,800MW

According to Meralco, they heeded the recommendation of the Energy Regulatory Commission to revise the terms of reference of the upcoming competitive selection process for 1,800MW of baseload capacity. The only major change was capping the maximum offered capacity at 1,200MW vs a maximum of 1,800MW previously. The ERC earlier raised the limitation on power plants to allow operational plants vs plants that started operations in 2020-2025. The CSP for the 1,800MW involves a 15-year contract beginning Dec 2024. This is meant to replace the capacity covered by Meralco’s 2024 PSA with San Miguel (SMC PM) which was terminated earlier this year. Firms that have expressed interest include GNPD, First NatGas, SP New Energy, Mariveles Power Generation, Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. and Masinloc Power Partners.

PPA rebids Iloilo port contract

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) decided to re-bid the 25-year contract to manage and operate Iloilo Commercial Port Complex (ICPC) in Iloilo City. This followed after the lone bid submitted by ICTSI (ICT PM) was declared “failed” due to the absence of a notarized Secretary’s Certificate or any document showing proof of authorization. ICTSI met all other eligibility criteria. A new invitation to bid was published on December 21. A pre-bid conference is set for December 29, with the deadline for bid submission and opening on January 11, 2024. Regis anticipates ICT to participate in the bid.  If awarded, ICPC will be its first foray in the Visayas region and will add to its current portfolio of 32 global ports

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Philippine rice imports as of mid-December hits 3.2mn MT

The country’s rice imports as of mid-December have reached 3.22mn MT according to the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI). In 2022, total rice imports reached 3.826mn MT. Vietnam remained the country’s top source of imported rice, accounting for 87% (2.813mn MT) of the total import volume as of December 14. Vietnam FOB rice spot price has climbed to US$666/MT (vs. average of US$652/MT in November).

Remittances rise by 3% in October

Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 3% YoY to US$2.998bn in October. In PHP terms, remittances for October slipped by 0.6% YoY. This was driven by higher land-based remittances from Singapore (+12.5% YoY), Saudi Arabia (6.6%), and the UAE (+10.5%). Additionally, this brings 10M23 remittances up 2.8% YoY to US$27.49bn. The BSP is forecasting remittances to rise by 3% this year.

Current account deficit shrinks to US$2.1bn in 3Q23

Preliminary data showed the current account deficit narrowed by 48% QoQ, 65% YoY to US$2.1bn in 3Q23. The 3Q23 current account deficit equivalent to 2.0% of GDP, down from 3.8% of GDP in 2Q23 and 6.5% in 3Q22. The lower YoY deficit was due to lower commodity prices, tourism recovery, higher BPO revenues and overseas remittances. BPO export revenues rose by 4% YoY to US$7.4bn in 3Q23 and 7.6% YoY to US$21.3bn in 9M23. This brought the YT-September current account deficit to US$10.95bn, down 40% YoY. The BSP raised its BOP surplus forecast for 2023 to US$1.1bn from US$100mn previously due to higher inflows from the government’s foreign loan availments.

Transport Department sticks to bid submission deadline for NAIA PPP

The Department of Transportation will not extend the current bid submission deadline of Dec 27 for the NAIA rehab PPP according to Transportation Secretary Jaime J. Bautista. The government wants to implement the program as soon as possible. This comes after its transaction advisor, ADB, recommended to push back the deadline by a month (to Jan 29) to give potential bidders more time to prepare and submit their bids. The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62mn annually from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years). According to the draft concession agreement, the winning bidder is required to pay an upfront amount of P30bn, an annual payment of P2bn plus a share of revenues (excluding passenger terminal fees)

Balance of payments swings to US$216mn deficit in November

Preliminary data showed the country’s balance of payments swung to a deficit of US$216mn in November after recording a US$1.5bn surplus in October. Recall October BOP surplus was boosted by the government’s issuance of US$1.24bn global bonds. The BOP deficit in November, however, is lower than the US$756mn deficit in November 2022. This brings 11M23 BOP position to surplus of US$3.0bn, a reversal of the US$7.9bn deficit in the same period a year ago. The BSP is forecasting a BOP surplus of US$1.1bn by year-end, a reversal of the US$7.3bn BOP deficit in 2022.

Palace says four new Maharlika directors sworn in

Andrew Jerome Gan – a seasoned corporate executive, currently serving in a audit/compliance firm Capital Markets Integrity Corp
