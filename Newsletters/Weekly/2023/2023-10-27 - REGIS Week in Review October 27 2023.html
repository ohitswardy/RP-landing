REGIS REPORT

27 October 2023

The market this week fell to its lowest level since October last year. The PSEi closed at 5961 down 3% WoW and crucially below the 6,000 technical support level. Average market daily turnover dropped to $57m from $80m WoW with a weekly net outflow of $35.8m. ACEN (3.4%), GTCAP (1.3%) and PLDT (0.8%) where the top index gainers this week. While AGI (-12.3%), WLCON (-9.8%) and Converge (-9.4%) were the week biggest index losers.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) surprised the market with an “off-cycle” increase in the policy rate, raising it by 25bp to 6.50%. The regular Monetary Board meeting, which monetary policy and rates are usually decided, is not until Nov 16. The BSP expects inflation to stay above its target band (2-4%) until July 2024 and did not rule out another rate hike on Nov 16. Additionally, the BSP estimated 3Q GDP growth to be 4.5%, barely higher than the rather weak 2Q number. 

A bit of 3Q numbers started to trickle in this week. BDO delivered a record-matching 3Q, with profit equaling the 2Q net income of P18.7bn. The 9M earnings remain on track to be slightly better than the full-year expectations of both Regis (P70.8bn) and the Street (P71.6bn). At the macro level, the numbers from BDO reflect a trend that’s unsurprisingly similar to BPI’s. With interest rates significantly higher than last year, there’s been a marked deceleration in loan accompanying the expansion in asset yields and NIMs. Loan growth (YoY) is now down to 7.5%, while NIMs were up 6bp to 4.75%.

Meanwhile, Puregold reported a 9M23 net profit of P6.6bn, up 2% YoY, and trailing our FY23 estimates. The company reported SSSG for the 9M period was 4.8% for Puregold and 4.1% for S&R, which implied a slowdown (or even contraction) in 3Q alone. While segmental performance was not disclosed, Regis believes the profit underperformance was driven by S&R, which is facing competitive pressure from Landers.

RESEARCH
Robinsons Retail - 3Q23: Inflationary pressures weigh
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M23 core operations behind expectations

RRHI’s 9M23 net profit dropped 41% YoY to P2.6bn, trending behind Regis and consensus estimates, with EBIT just up 4% to P6.1bn. NPAT was hit by wider[1]than-expected associate losses (e.g. GoTyme) amounting to P541mn. For 3Q23 alone, EBIT was flat YoY as higher opex weighed on margins across formats. Consolidated sales rose 6% YoY to P47.2bn in 3Q on the back of 2.5% SSSG. By segment, there were no major outperformers. Supermarkets and drug stores (76% of consolidated EBITDA) were broadly in line, while specialty stores, convenience stores and DIY underperformed.

Supermarkets: Inflation weighs on basket sizes

Supermarkets’ SSSG decelerated to +1.8% in 3Q as inflation affected consumer spending. Same-store traffic grew 11% but basket sizes contracted by 9-10%. Gross margin was flattish in 3Q, but would have risen by ~30bps excluding Growsari, which now contributes c10% of segment sales.

Discretionary formats a mixed bag

RRHI’s discretionary formats were a mixed bag in 3Q. Department stores (8% of sales) did relatively well (+6% SSSG) due to back-to-school activities. Meanwhile, DIY (6% of sales) continued to underperform (-0.9% SSS) due to competitive pressures.

SSSG guidance trimmed to 4-5%

Management trimmed its FY23 SSSG guidance to 4-5% (from 4-6%), implying a further slowdown in 4Q (Regis FY23E: 3.9%). Apart from a high YoY base, we believe consumer baskets will continue to be pressured by high inflation. Meanwhile, management also trimmed its guidance for new stores for the year to 120-160 new stores (160-190 previously) due to delays in securing sites.

BDO 9M NPAT P54bn, in line; loans slow, but NIMs still gaining

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported 9M23 NPAT of P53.9bn, up 35% YoY, and tracking in line / slightly ahead of Regis estimates (FY23F P70.8bn) and consensus (P71.6bn). For 3Q23 alone, NPAT was P18.7bn, up 16% and flat QOQ. Loan growth slowed to 7.5%, but was offset by continued NIM gains, presumably on loan re-pricing and loan mix (no breakdown given yet). Opex was slightly elevated, with compensation jumping 16% YoY in 3Q23 (was +13% in 1H23). Provision expense is running higher than Regis forecast, with NPLs ticking up slightly QoQ to 1.99% (from 1.95% in 2Q).

 

Some highlights:

 

“Customer loans” grew 7.5% YoY, decelerating from the 8.4% pace in 2Q23, and below the bank’s FY guidance of 8-10%. No breakdown was given in the press release, but Regis suspects that corporate demand was weak. Loan yields improved 20bps QoQ (+172bps YoY) likely on a combination of continued loan re-pricing and better loan mix (ie more consumer).

 

Deposits grew 12% YoY, driven largely by time deposits (TDs), which jumped 76% YoY and 16% QoQ, which saw CASA ratio slip to 72% from 75% in 2Q. Deposit costs jumped 13bps QoQ as a result. It appears that the bank bought funds to build up its investment securities portfolio, which grew 7% QoQ (by cP60bn), largely in the FVTPL/AFS book.

 

NIMs still managed gains, up 6bps QoQ in 3Q23 (+52bps YoY) to 4.75%, though the pace seems to be flattening out. Non-interest income grew 9% YoY in 9M23, though 3Q alone was up just 4% YoY. The 3Q23 was dragged by a 9% drop in Trading/forex gains due to trading losses in the quarter. Fee incomes, though, were still up 8% YoY in 9M and 6% YoY in 2Q23 alone.

 

PPOP of P80.6bn was up 28% YoY in 9M23, tracking ahead of Regis expectations. Provisions came in at P10.7bn, down 12% YoY, but were higher than Regis expectations (FY23E P12.7bn). Credit cost stayed in the mid-50bp range in 3Q23. The bank reported NPL ratio ticked up slightly to 1.99% from 1.95% in 2Q23, while NPL Cover improved a bit to 176% (from 174%). To the upside, Restructured Loans (RLs) continued the downtrend, falling 21% YoY to P73bn, representing 2.6% of total loans (down from 2.8% in 2Q, and from 5.7% of loans at the peak during Covid).

 

PGOLD 9M23 net profit +2% YoY to P6.6bn – behind

In a very limited press release, PGOLD reported 9M23 net profit of P6.6bn (+2% YoY). This is behind Regis estimates but in line with consensus. The underperformance was largely driven by S&R. Net sales rose 9% YoY to P139.3bn while operating profit contracted by 2% YoY to P10bn. Both are also trailing our estimates. For 3Q23 alone, net profit was down 3% YoY mainly on lower operating margin. For Puregold, margins were dragged by the uptick in labor cost due to the minimum wage increase. For S&R, pre-operating expenses weighed on EBITDA margin. As of 9M23, S&R opened it’s 3rd new store (out of 4 for the year) in early October. SSSG was 2.3% for Puregold and -5.3% for S&R. The SSS decline for S&R was attributed to a high YoY base. For Purgold, SSSG was driven by higher foot traffic as consumers continued to downtrade, leading to smaller baskets. PGOLD currently trades at 7x PE and 4x EV/EBTIDA for 2024E.

More ownership changes at Dito CME; Udenna reduces stake

Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that it was Udenna Corp that sold 500m shares of DITO last week. The sale reduced Udenna’s ownership to 10.7bn shares. Udenna is the holding company of the Davao-based Dennis Uy. DITO also disclosed that Summit Telco is subscribing to 3.3bn new shares of DITO at P1.00/share (less than 1/3rd of “market price”). This adds to the 1.59bn shares subscribed to by Summit Telco in Aug 2023, bringing its total stake to 4.89bn DITO shares. Another firm, Xterra Ventures, subscribed to 610m new shares at that time.

 

The total outstanding shares of DITO would rise to 19.535bn after this latest transaction, with Summit Telco holding 25%. Udenna would own 55% of DITO after the deal/s. DITO is the controlling shareholder of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel) but had its stake in DitoTel diluted with the entry of a new investor. New investor was an entity called Summit Global which subscribed to 18.5% of the stock of an intermediate holding company. DITO’s effective stake in DitoTel was thus diluted from 53.7% to 43.7%. The identity of the party/parties behind Summit Telco is unclear.

Ramon Ang takes a seat in Metro Pacific’s board

Mr. Ramon Ang was elected to the board of Metro Pacific on Oct 17, following his personal indirect investment in MPI. Mr. Ang is the president and COO of San Miguel Corp. (SMC PM). SMC, for its part, has not made any direct investment in MPIC.

ACEN subsidiary signs MOU with Rio Tinto for exploration opportunities in Western Australia

ACEN subsidiary Yindjibarndi Energy Corporation (YEC) signed a memorandum of understanding with Rio Tinto for opportunities to collaborate on renewable energy projects on Yindjibarndi country in the Pilbara region of Western Australia. YEC was established in June following an agreement between Yindjibarndi Aboriginal Corporation and ACEN for RE projects within the Yindjibarndi Native Title Determination Areas. YEC’s initial plans include a Stage 1 target of 750MW combined wind, solar and battery storage with construction to commence within the next few years.

 

The collaboration opportunities being considered by Rio Tinto and YEC relate to some of these Stage 1 projects. Meanwhile, Rio Tinto operates four gas-fired power stations in the Pilbara. The company is currently assessing the development of approximately 300MW of solar projects. It is among the largest global mining groups and is a leading producer of iron ore, aluminium, copper and other minerals. ACEN’s 2030 target of 20GW comprises major investments in both the Philippines and Australia. ACEN appears on track to exceed its 5,000MW target ahead of 2025.

Implementation of short selling delayed to November 6

The PSE has moved the implementation date of short selling from October 23 to November 6

To give market participants more time to prepare. Only constituents of the PSE Index, the MidCap Index, and the Dividend Yield Index will be eligible for short selling. An eligible security must maintain short interest to outstanding shares at less than or equal to 10%. Naked shorting will not be allowed. Short selling of a stock can only be done on a price uptick.

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Vehicle sales rise 9% YoY, 5% MoM in September

CAMPI reported that sales of new vehicles rose 9% YoY, 5% MoM to 38,628 units in September 2023. This brings 9M23 sales to 314,843 units, up 27% YoY. CAMPI targeting to reach 423k unit sales by year-end, up 20% YoY. Toyota sold 144,232 units in 9M23, up 15.5% YoY. This implies September unit sales at 17,437 units, up 8% YoY and 5% MoM. To reach Regis FY23E sales forecast of 192k units (+10% YoY), sales for the rest of the year should only average 15,923 units per month. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 28% of net profit and gross asset value (36% of NAV). GTCAP is trading at a 50% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,085/sh and 5.4x/4.9x FY23E/FY24E PER.

Pesonet, Instapay transactions hit P9.2tn in 9M23

Electronic fund transfers through PESONet and InstaPay jumped 30% YoY to P9.24tn from Jan-Sep 2023. By volume, transactions surged by 40.4% to 638.9mn. Recall that in 2022, the value of transactions rose 36% YoY to P9.94tn. PESONet transactions rose 24% YoY to P5.69tn while InstaPay transactions jumped 42% YoY to P3.55tn. The number of banked Filipino adults almost doubled to 56% in 2021 from 29% in 2019.

 

Infrastructure spending picks up in Aug

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 12% YoY to P877bn in 8M23. This implies spending in August rose 49% YoY, 3% MoM to P136bn. Recall that 1H23 infra spend rose by a mere 3% YoY. The govt cited accelerated project implementation for roads and bridges and higher direct payments by foreign creditors for ODA-funded rail projects. The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn (+1% YoY) in FY23, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP. To reach this target, the government only needs to spend P104bn per month for the rest of the year.

Government aims to ramp up use of EVs in mass transport

President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. wants to ramp up the “electrification” of the country’s transport sector with the construction of more charging stations and electricity-powered vehicle (EV) manufacturing plants nationwide according to Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla. Based on data from the Department of Transportation (DOTr), there are currently 29 accredited EV Charging Stations (EVCS). The number of registered EVs is expected to increase this year after the Electric Vehicle Association of the Philippines (EVAP) reported it sold 2,557 units in 1H23, or more than double the 1,072 units, it sold in FY22. Data includes hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) and battery electric vehicles (BEVs).

 

BSP: Off-cycle rate hike is on the table

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) is considering an off-cycle rate hike according to BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona Jr. The decision will depend on the latest inflation data (October inflation will be released on Nov 7). The next Monetary Board meeting is slated for November 17 (Fed meeting on Nov 1).

Budget deficit widens in September/3Q23 as spending accelerates

The government recorded a budget deficit of P251bn in September 2023, up 40% YoY and 89% MoM. Revenues contracted by 12% YoY due to timing difference in VAT payments affecting BIR collections and lower commodity prices hitting the Bureau of Customs. Expenses rose 8% YoY, driven by higher interest payments and primary spending. In 3Q23 alone, the deficit was wider at P432bn, as expenses jumped 11% YoY while revenues grew at a slower rate of +5% YoY. Primary spending accelerated, up 9.4% YoY in 3Q23 (vs. -1% YoY in 1H23). This brings 9M23 deficit to P984bn, down 2.9% YoY. Based on the government’s FY23E deficit target of P1.5tn (6.1% of GDP), the deficit will shrink to P516bn in 4Q23E from P601bn in 4Q22. This suggests that overall expenditures will drop by 6% YoY and primary spending will contract by 9% YoY in 4Q23E.

NAIA airport bidding attracts seven potential bidders

Seven groups have so far bought bidding documents for the auction of the NAIA PPP

Incheon International Airport; San Miguel Holdings Corp.; Manila International Airport Consortium (AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners); Cengiz Insaat Sanayi ve Ticaret AS; GMR Airports International; Spark 888 Management and Asia Airport Consortium. It is unclear at the stage if all seven bidders will submit bids on Dec 27. The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62mn annually from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years).

Colliers: Metro Manila office vacancy inches up in 3Q23,

During its 3Q23 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila office vacancy inched up to 18.7%, from 18.4% in 2Q23 due to an increase in office supply. Vacancy rate in Fort Bonifacio and Makati was only 10%. Rental rates also in most areas continued to fall. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 20%-50% below pre-COVID levels. However, rental rates in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio have been recovering. Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to hit 21.2% by end-2023 due to office buildings scheduled for completion in 4Q23.  Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 1% QoQ. Colliers estimates that they have risen 4% YoY. This is the eighth consecutive quarter that prices have inched up. Prices are generally 5%-20% below 2019 levels.

BSP raises policy rates by 25bps in off-cycle hike

The Monetary Board surprised again, announcing a 25bps off-cycle rate hike. Overnight borrowing rate was adjusted to 6.5% from 6.25%, effective 27 Oct. The BSP cited need to prevent supply-side pressures from inducing second-round effects and further dislodging inflation expectations. The latest baseline projection is headline inflation will remain above 4% until July 2024 before coming down to c. 3% or within its 2-4% target band. It raised its 2024E average inflation forecast to 4.7% from 4.3%. There as no mention of changes to 2023E inflation of 5.8% (YT-Sep ave. inflation rate at 6.6%). The recent demand indicators point to dissipating pent-up demand in the near-term. The 3Q GDP estimate was said to be 4.5% (mild recovery from 2Q’s 4.3%). The monetary board is set to meet on Nov 16; BSP Governor Remolona Jr. did not rule out the possibility of further action during this meeting. The decision will depend on October inflation data (out on Nov 7) and 3Q GDP data (Nov 9).
