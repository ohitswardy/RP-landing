REGIS REPORT

27 January 2023

The market consolidated for the week to close at 7,052.16 (+0.13% WoW). Turnover settled at US$111.0m (-28.3% WoW) with foreign flows light amid the Chinese new year holidays. Foreign participation dropped to 31% (vs 41% previous week).

The 4Q GDP print came in at 7.2%, bringing FY22 growth to 7.6%. Although better than our expectations at 6.9%, the figures were still largely ignored by the market.

The market saw a few transactions announced this week.

ACEN (+6.2% WoW) purchased 500m shares (equivalent to a 5% stake) in SP New Energy (+5.30% WoW). The acquisition will be treated as a prepayment of a P1bn loan facility previously extended to the latter.

MONDE (-4.86% WoW) meanwhile announced its purchase of 820m primary shares of Figaro Coffee Group (+3.33% WoW) at Php1.00/sh, a premium to market levels. FCG currently has a network of 124 stores and derives revenue from its restaurant brands, including Angel’s Pizza.

Cemex Philippines (+42.2% WoW) gained sharply after its major shareholder, Cemex Asia South East Corp launched a tender offer to buy up to 12% of the company. This should bring the public float to just above 10% should it be fully transacted. The tender offer is priced at P1.30/sh.

The PSE officially disclosed the details for index rebalancing. As expected, DMCI (-1.6% WoW) and UBP (+1.7% WoW) will be included at the expense of Robinsons Land (+9.72% WoW) and Megaworld (+5.63% WoW). The changes will be effective on February 6, 2023.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PLDT capex for 2022 to reach P95-97bn

PLDT is guiding for capex to reach P95-97bn in 2022. This is higher than earlier guidance of P85bn and reflects the overruns that have yet to be fully booked. Overrun was estimated to be around P48bn. Management expects capex to remain  elevated in 2022 and 2023, possibly into 2024.

Robinsons Land CEO appointed as Presidential Adviser

Robinsons Land CEO Frederick Go was appointed by the Marcos administration as Presidential Adviser on Investment and Economic Affairs. Mr. Go intends to hold the position concurrent with his roles in Robinsons Land.

BSP adjusts interest rate ceiling on credit card transactions

The Monetary Board decided to adjust the ceilings on credit card transactions by increasing the maximum interest rate by 100bps to 3% per month. This policy aligns with the developments in the macroeconomy and cushions the impact of inflationary pressure on banks’/credit card issuer’s ability to provide quality credit card services to their clients.

Marcos administration examining POGO industry

When asked to react to the calls to ban POGO firms, President Marcos said that problems lie with illegal offshore gaming firms, not legal ones. While legal POGO firms pay taxes, he said there was a need to examine if there is a social cost. He also said that if the Chinese government wanted a crackdown on POGOs, “…we’ll see what the arrangement could be…”. At the end of 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-4Q22. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

Security Bank expects mid-teens loan growth

Security Bank CEO Sanjiv Vohra said that he expects mid-teens loan growth in 2023 (from 12% in 3Q22). He also expects growth of the deposit base to keep pace with loan growth.

House bill seeks higher taxes on distilled spirits, “junk food”

Albay Rep. Joey Salceda says he wants to update the Sin Tax law through a bill that will seek higher taxes on distilled spirits and other beverages. The bill will impose a “non-essential goods tax” on distilled spirits and other beverages priced above P20,000 (USD360) per bottle. They are also studying a “junk food tax”, as well as an update of the tax on sweetened beverages which are currently subject to an excise tax of P6/liter (using sugar) and P12/liter (using high fructose corn syrup).

Converge rolls out 2m ports in 2022

Converge says it rolled out more than 2m fiber ports last year (2022). This implies it now has at least 7.9m ports available. Total homes passed as of Sept 2022 was 14.3m, approximately 53% of households. Converge is likely to have ended 2022 with around 2m subscribers, which would be 1/4th of total ports based on our estimates.

Cemex Phils’ parent conducts tender offer of up to 12% of shares

Tender offer for 1.6bn shares or 12% of shares out. Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) announced its parent, Cemex Asian South East Corp., will be conducting a voluntary tender offer of up to 1.6bn common shares, equivalent to 12% of CHP’s outstanding shares. This means CHP will remain listed, even if the entire 1.6bn shares are tendered, as CHP’s public float will still be above the 10% limit to remain listed. Note that CHP’s public float today is at 22%. The tender offer will commence on Feb 16 and end on March 16. Valuation appears low vs replament cost, regional divestments. The tender offer price is set at P1.30/sh, which is a 51% premium over its closing price on Jan 23 and a 65% premium to its 1-year VWAP.

Cebu Air sees full recovery, profitability this year

Cebu Air (CEB PM) is aiming to be profitable by 1Q22, as it restores 100% of its pre-pandemic network and capacity by March according to President and COO Alexander Lao. This would depend on fuel prices, which have been rising again lately. CEB is optimistic it can be profitable for the full year of 2023, citing the strength of local currency against the USD as among the drivers.

ACEN gets 5% stake in SPNEC as part of loan prepayment

ACEN Corp disclosed yesterday that it will receive 500mn shares in SPNEC (with a market value of P660mn) from Solar PH as a prepayment for part of the loan’s principal and payment of interest and arrangement/security amendments fees in consideration for the ACEN releasing its pledge over shares owned by Solar PH in SPNEC. The 500mn shares in SPNEC translates to a 5% stake in the company. The loan refers to a P1bn loan facility provided by ACEN to an affiliate of Solar PH in June 2021 for purposes of landbanking for future solar projects. In January 2021, ACEN Corp and Solar PH signed an agreement for a proposed joint venture for solar power plant projects in Central Luzon. No size or amounts have been identified for this particular JV.

Monde Nissin to acquire 15% of Figaro Coffee Group (FCG PM)

MONDE announced it is acquiring a 15% stake in FCG for P820mn (820mn primary shares at P1/sh). The implied valuation for FCG is 27-28x trailing PE, based on reported FY22 (end-June) net profit. FCG operates a network of 124 company-owned and franchised restaurants under the brands Figaro Coffee, Angel’s Pizza, Tien Ma’s (Taiwanese cuisine), among others.

SMC fails to get TRO to suspend ERC order

San Miguel Corp failed to secure a temporary restraining order that could have prevented the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) from enforcing its September 2022 order. The Sixteenth Division of the Court of Appeals (CA) denied the petition for a TRO or preliminary injunction filed by San Miguel Energy Corporation (SMEC) that was meant to suspend implementation of ERC’s September 29, 2022 order. The ERC order denied the joint motions of the Manila Electric and SMC Global Power units for price adjustments to serve as temporary relief covering P5.2bn losses incurred from January – May 2022. SPPC and SMEC sought a TRO before the CA, which earlier ruled in favor of SPPC. The CA Resolution dated January 13, 2023 ruled that SMEC failed to prove its right to a restraining order.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

Foreign borrowings down to US$10.3bn in 2022

Foreign borrowings approved by the BSP declined for the second straight year to US$10.32bn in 2022. This is down by 21.4% from US$13.14bn in 2021 due to lower bond issuances, as well as the significant drop in program loans.

Government sees 3,600 infra projects worth US$372bn

The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) have started compiling potential infrastructure projects from various agencies that could be implemented through 2028. So far, NEDA has identified 3,600 projects amounting to US$372bn in total. Of which, 180 projects will cost over US$45mn. 206 projects worth US$159bn will be implemented in the short term, of which 87 projects worth US$54bn have been identified for Public-Private partnerships. NEDA chief, Balisacan noted that the government will release the Public Investment Program (PIP) for 2023-2028 this quarter. This will include the three-year rolling infrastructure program for 2024-2026, which aims to inform the public on the budgeting process for key priority projects. The government plans to spend 5-6% of GDP on infrastructure through 2028.

Infrastructure spending rises 14% YoY to P1.1tn in 11M22

Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 14.1% YoY to P1.10tn in 11M22. This implies that infra spend in November 2022 alone rose 16% YoY and 3.7% MoM to P96.3bn. The government is targeting to spend P1.23tn (+9.5% YoY) in 2022. To reach the target, infra spend for Dec 2022 should be P130bn (down 17% YoY vs. Dec 2021: P157bn). For 2023, the government is targeting to spend P1.25tn (+1.4% YoY).

4Q GDP growth slightly moderates to 7.2%

The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that the economy grew by 7.2% in 4Q22, better than consensus (6.8%) and Regis (6.9%) forecasts. Domestic demand slowed to +7% YoY, broadly in line with historical trend (+6.8% from 2009-2019). Consumption remained buoyant, up 7% YoY and contributed 5.3ppts to GDP while government spending accelerated to +3.3% YoY from 0.8% YoY in 3Q. Private investments slowed sharply to 5.9%, with inventory buildup moderating. Net imports dropped and contributed +1.5ppts to GDP. On the supply side, the services sector was the outperformer, offsetting the decline in agricultural production. The services sector grew at a faster rate of +9.8% on “revenge” and holiday spending. Industry sector normalized and grew by 4.8%, below historical trend of +6%. This brings full-year 2022 GDP growth at +7.6%. 

Trade deficit at US$4.6bn in December; Full-year 2022 trade deficit at US$58bn

Based on preliminary data, the country posted a trade deficit of US$4.59bn in December. While the deficit is lower by 10% YoY, it is nonetheless 24% larger than the US$3.7bn deficit recorded in November. MoM drop can be attributed to (a) exports collapsing to its lowest absolute level since Feb 2021 and (b) imports dropping by 5% MoM. Imports fell 10% YoY, biggest monthly drop since January 2021. This is despite a re-acceleration of fuel import growth to +13% YoY from +8% YoY in November. Capital goods imports fell further, dropping by 11% YoY. Exports dropped 10% YoY, with exports to the US, Japan, HK, Singapore contributing negative 8.7ppts to the drop. This brings full-year 2022 trade deficit to US$58.3bn, equivalent to 14% of GDP.
