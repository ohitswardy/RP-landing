REGIS REPORT

24 February 2023

The market dropped -1.4% to 6,685.9 last week, as selling momentum increased after breaking technical support levels at 6750.

Turnover dropped to nearly half (-45.5% WoW) as investors awaited corporate earnings to provide more direction to the market. The trading week was also shortened after Friday, February 24, was declared a special non-working holiday.

Biggest drags to the market were Ayala Land (-4.4% WoW) and SM Prime (-1.2% WoW), even as earnings came in in line with expectations. SM Prime mall and residential segments showed divergent results in 4Q22. Though mall revenue returned to pre-COVID levels, residential sales dropped sharply. Similarly, Ayala Land’s higher-than-expected residential cancellations offset the outperformance from rental and commercial lot revenues.

Globe (-5.9% WoW) and ACEN (-3.0% WoW) likewise saw heavy selling pressure ahead of the MSCi rebalancing on Tuesday, February 28. Note that these were also the top names sold by foreign investors for US8.3m and US3.5m respectively.

Only three names registered gains for the week – namely SM (+2.9% WoW), CNVRG+2.4% WoW) and UBP (+0.6% WoW).

SM Prime - 4Q: Philippine mall revenue back to preCOVID level but resi sales drop shaply
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Divergent results from domestic mall and residential segments in 4Q

SM Prime’s 2022 net profit jumped 38% to P30.1bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Operating profit of P49.2bn (+52%) outperformed expectations but items below the EBIT line seem to have dragged earnings. The Philippine mall and residential segments showed divergent results in 4Q22. Mall revenue rebounded remarkably while residential presales dropped sharply.

Philippine mall revenue back to pre-COVID levels

Total Philippine mall revenue jumped 19% QoQ (91% YoY) to P15.9bn, equivalent to 101% of the 4Q19 level. Rental revenue is slightly above pre-COVID levels while cinema revenue is still lagging. Philippine rental revenue climbed 16% QoQ (74% YoY) to P13.7bn, 3.5% above the 4Q19 figure. Since the mall portfolio is about 5% larger than in 4Q19, same-mall rent is arguably at pre-COVID levels. Meanwhile, 4Q22 cinema and event ticket sales of P2.2bn (+50% QoQ,+388% YoY) represents 85% of the 4Q19 level.

Residential reservation sales drop sharply

4Q22 residential reservation sales fell 26% QoQ (20% YoY) to P18.1bn, equivalent to 80% of the 2019 average. While the weak 4Q22 could be a blip, this bears watching since reservation sales have fallen for three consecutive quarters. Meanwhile, residential revenue rose 18% QoQ (-14% YoY), likely implying that cancellations continued to improve.

“Revenge travel” lifts hotels; COVID restrictions hurt China malls

The reopening of the economy (and travel, in particular) led to a surge in hotel revenue (+32% QoQ, +109% YoY), which is nearly back to pre-COVID levels. On the other hand, COVID restrictions in China led to a decline in mall revenue.

 
Ayala Land - 4Q22 lifted by rental and commercial lot revenue; ALI optimistic on 2023
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Management optimistic about 2023

Ayala Land’s FY22 net profit jumped 52% to P18.6bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Rental and commercial lot revenue outperformed while higher-than-expected residential cancellations dragged residential revenue. During the briefing, the company conveyed its optimism about its prospects this year. It projects better operations across all of its major business segments.

FY22 reservation sales led by international buyers

4Q22 reservation sales rose 25% YoY (flat QoQ) to P27.7bn, thanks in large part to strong sales of Ayala Land Premier (ALP) horizontal projects. For the full-year, reservation sales jumped 14% to P104.9bn (72% of the 2019 figure), as the weak peso enticed OFWs and foreigners (see Figure 3). In FY22, sales to OFWs surged 58% YoY to P23bn, comprising 22% of total sales. Avida and Amaia condominiums in Metro Manila were popular with OFWs based in the Middle East. Meanwhile, FY22 sales to foreigners climbed 39%. Sales to Americans in particular, rose 47%, and comprised 63% of sales to foreigners.

Positive on residential demand

Management is targeting to launch P110bn of residential projects this year (vs P91bn in 2022) and is even prepared to launch P130bn if demand is stronger. We believe this signifies management’s confidence about residential demand.

Residential cancellations to drop significantly in 2023

4Q22 development revenue rose 23% QoQ (+6% YoY) as record commercial lot revenue (+68% YoY, +214% QoQ) offset the decline in residential revenue brought about by cancellations (see Figure 2). The company is optimistic that cancellations will drop significantly in 2023.

Mall occupancy to rise further

Mall revenue grew 13% QoQ (+63% YoY) and should improve further as occupancy increases. Average occupancy was only 81% as of 4Q22 but should rise soon since 90% of space was already leased out as of end-2022.

Office occupancy increases 4ppt QoQ; could climb further in 2023

BPO firms took up space in Ayala Land office buildings in prime areas, such as Makati CBD and Cebu IT Park. Thus, ALI’s office occupancy rose 4ppt QoQ to 88%, higher than the 82% occupancy across the industry. Management is targeting to improve occupancy further to the mid-90s by end-2023.

Hotel revenue hits record

Revenge travel lifted 4Q22 hotel revenue by 43% QoQ (+132% YoY) to P2.3bn, a quarterly record. Ayala Land also expects hotel operations to pick up further this year (vs. FY22) as more international tourists visit the Philippines.
