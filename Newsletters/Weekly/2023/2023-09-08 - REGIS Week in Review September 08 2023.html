REGIS REPORT

08 September 2023

The market traded in the red for most part of the week, dragged by sharply accelerating inflation.

August CPI inflation came in at 5.3%, coming in well above consensus expectations of 4.7%. Rice drove the acceleration, up 8.7% YoY for the month. Vegetable prices also contributed to the jump, up +32% YoY.

To help contain the rice price hikes, the administration has issued controls on rice prices and said it will be providing subsidies to rice retailers in order for them to comply with the rice price cap. The price cap is up to 20% lower than retailers’ inventory cost.

Given the high inflation print, the BSP is now seeing inflation returning to its 2-4% target band in 1Q24 instead of 4Q23, dashing the market’s hopes for an earlier return to lower rates. Investors now await BSP’s move during the next policy meeting on September 21 although it has become more unlikely for any rate cuts to occur in the near term. 

The index rebounded last Friday to close at 6,222.94 (+0.7% WoW) although the rally remains unconvincing. Turnover was measly at US$55m (vs. an average of US$92.8m for the week).

Large cap conglos JGS (+7.4% WoW) and AEV (+3.9% WoW) outperformed while BDO (-6.4% WoW) and JFC (-2.9% WoW) dragged. Trading was generally mixed given the lack of catalysts.

MPI (-0.2% WoW) and its tender offer continued to be in the news. GSIS raised its stake in MPI to 12% and said it plans to stay invested in the company even after it is delisted.

Meanwhile, the tender results came out with 18.7% of outstanding shares tendered. MPI says that the non-public shares (including the GSIS stake) would already reach 96.87% (of outstanding shares) based on share already tendered.  This is already above the 95% threshold for voluntary delisting. The general offer deadline was extended to September 19 noon (from September 7 noon previously) likely to allow minorities another chance to sell their shares before the official transaction to take it private.

As an offshoot to the likely delisting of MPI, locals continued to bet on BLOOM (+2.0% WoW) to take its place in the PSEi. 

Foreign inflows were concentrated on GTCAP (+3.1% WoW) worth US$5.9m while the rest of the market saw outflows. Headline outflows registered at US$63.5m for the week. Top sold names were MPI, BDO, and BLOOM for US$17.0m, US$9.3m, and US$7.1m respectively.

RESEARCH
Philippine Market Update - Rice crisis redux?
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

A looming test

The alarm over international rice prices has shone a spotlight on the Philippines given its historical position as one of the largest rice importers in the world and the outsized prominence of rice in the local diet and the CPI basket. Without doubt, the country is particularly vulnerable to a spike in rice prices, but we believe a quick, short-term fix is available and the just-announced price controls are not the answer. Longer-term solutions will be needed to overcome the country’s geographical handicaps and achieve food security.

Perennial importer of its most socially important commodity

The Philippines is perennially and chronically short of its most important food staple, whose importance is hard to overstate. Rice contributes 36% of the calories consumed by the average Filipino and accounts for 9% of the CPI basket. It is as much as 18% of the CPI basket for low-income households. In 2022, the country imported 3.8mn MT of rice, equivalent to ~25% of rice consumption. This year, the Philippines is projected to import ~4mn MT.

Liberalization of rice imports has cut prices

The “Rice Tariffication Law” (RTL) of 2019 abolished the monopoly of the National Food Authority (NFA) over rice importation (a monopoly which included the handing out of lucrative import licenses) and allowed traders to import at will subject to prevailing tariffs (35%). Since the RTL, the rice prices have dropped to “only” `40-50% above international prices, whereas it used to cost double. Import liberalization has reduced prices but it also means domestic retail prices are now subject to the influence of international factors.

Thoughts about the price caps

The government’s just-announced imposition of retail price caps (US$730 to $800/MT) are, in our view, likely to be ineffective at best or counter-productive at worst. Imports are the source of marginal supply and the wholesale cost of landed imports (after duties and shipping costs) is likely above US$800 already. Barring illicit trade (smuggling), a shortage may emerge.

Short-term fix is available but long-term solutions will take time

Domestic rice prices should immediately drop if import tariffs are slashed from the current 35%, an action which the President is empowered to do whenever Congress is not in session. This could quickly relieve pressure on consumer wallets and the CPI. Local rice farmers / traders are already seeing much higher prices YoY and are likely to be better off than they were last year.

Longer-term, only sensible policies and sustained investment in agriculture can help achieve the government’s goal of rice self-sufficiency and “food security” amidst the realities of a large population (and higher population growth) and geographical handicaps (lack of a large river delta and 20 typhoons annually).

 
 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Maynilad eyeing up to US$1bn IPO

Maynilad is considering an IPO that could raise US$750mn to US$1bn according to media sources. The listing is planned for 2025. Maynilad is evaluating proposals from financial advisers, according to Maynilad President Ramoncito Fernandez. Maynilad is 53%-owned by Metro Pacific (MPI PM), 25% by DMCI (DMC PM), 20% by Japan-based Marubeni. Regis values Maynilad at a 100% equity value of P166bn (~US$3bn)

Metro Pacific to expand rail investment; eyes Ayala Corp’s LRT-1 stake

Metro Pacific announced it has signed a partnership agreement with Malaysia-based rail service provider, Hartasuma, to jointly develop rail infrastructure (e.g. monorails/cable cars) worth P3bn in the Philippines. MPI Chairman Manuel V. Pangilinan also said that MPI is planning to invest in Hartasuma separately. The company is reconsidering investments on rail projects, given the improved regulatory environment. MPI is eyeing Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) 35% stake in Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC). LRMC holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 rail. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), and Macquarie Infra (10%). Regis valuation for 100% of LRMC’s equity value is negative P19.2bn (equivalent to an attributable value of -P0.24 per MPI share). LRMC, together with Japan-based Sumitomo, submitted an unsolicited proposal to rehabilitate and operate the MRT-3 two weeks ago.

 

Ayala Land President Bernard Dy to retire on Sept 30

Ayala Land President and CEO Bernard Vincent “Bobby” Dy will retire on September 30. Anna Ma. Margarita Bautista-Dy will replace him as President and CEO. Ms. Dy has been COO of the company since April 2023. She heads the residential and mall businesses of Ayala Land. She was previously head of the company’s strategic landbanking group. She has been a member of the management committee since 2008.

GSIS will remain a shareholder of MPI after delisting

In a letter to the PSE, state-run GSIS informed the PSE that (a) its c. 12% stake in MPI will be considered as non-public float and (b) it will remain a shareholder of MPI even after the completion of the delisting. This confirms our view that the recent purchases of GSIS of MPI shares will not derail the delisting process.

New *investor* dilutes Dito CME's stake in DitoTel

Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that its 89.5%-owned subsidiary Dito Holdings issued new shares to a company called Summit Global.  A total of 2.24bn new shares were issued to Summit Global at P1/share and the new shares are equivalent to 18.5% of the capital of Dito Holdings. Note that Dito Holdings is the owner of 60% of the shares of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel).

 

Dito CME’s effective ownership in DitoTel would thus be diluted from 54% to 44%.  This issuance means that Summit Global is buying 11% (18.5% x 60%) of DitoTel for P2.24bn, which implies a valuation of P20bn for 100% equity of DitoTel. Frankly, we don’t think DitoTel is worth even that much. It is unclear who is behind Summit Global, but a recently formed company called Summit Telco acquired P1.6bn worth of Dito CME shares just a few weeks back.

 

The funds raised are supposed to be used to support DitoTel’s operations. Unless it is a prelude to a bigger investment, the amount will at best buy DitoTel a couple of months.

 

NAIA privatization attracts three bidders so far

The Department of Transportation (DOTr) received interest from three (3) potential bidders so far, according to Secretary Jaime Bautista. Manila consortium consisting of six Philippine conglomerates – AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners, San Miguel Corp (SMC PM) and India-based GMR.

 

DOTr will be inviting more foreigners to participate in the auction in the next few weeks. The government prefers an operator with a track record and “very good” financial background. Based on the instructions to bidders document, the bidders will be competing on the government’s percentage share of gross revenues, which will exclude passenger service charge. Financial terms that are fixed are (1) P30bn upfront payment and (b) P2bn annuity payment. The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62mn annually from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years). Submission of bids set on Dec. 27.

 

 
 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 

BSP sees within-target inflation by 1Q24

BSP Governor Eli Remolona now sees inflation landing within its target band of 2-4% by 1Q24, not 4Q23. The BSP will likely revise its inflation forecast of 5.6% for 2023 at its next policy meeting on Sep 21. Average inflation in YT-August stood at 6.6%, with inflation for August alone at 5.3%. Mr. Remolona said the central bank still has space for further tightening

 

Philippines and Vietnam working on long-term rice trade agreement

The Philippine and Vietnam governments are looking to sign rice trade agreement of at least five years. In August, Agriculture Undersecretary Domingo Panganiban said that 300,000 to 500,000 metric tons of additional rice may be imported from Vietnam at discounted prices. Vietnam comprises over 80% of Philippine rice imports

President Marcos secures US$22mn in investment pledges from Indonesian firms

President Marcos secured US$22m in investment pledges from Indonesian firms in the animal health, artificial intelligence and digital sectors. PT Vaksindo Satwa Nusantara plans to invest $2 million on veterinary vaccines with Univet Nutrition and Animal Healthcare Company Philippines. PT WIR Asia, described as the first metaverse company in Indonesia, plans to invest $20 million in the Philippines in the next five years.

Makati subway project hits a snag

Phillipine Infradev Holdings (INFRA PM) wrote to the Makati city government to discuss changes in its plans for its US$3.7bn subway project. Its original alignment is no longer feasible after the recent decision of the Supreme Court (SC) mandating some areas covered by the project to be under Taguig City’s jurisdiction. The transfer of schools, properties, and courts are among the issues the two local governments had to face in the past months since the ruling was handed down. Project is a joint venture with the local government of Makati; Project was awarded to INFRA in 2018.

NEDA wants rice tariffs cut amid rise in inflation

Economic planning agency, NEDA, has called for a review of a temporary reduction in rice tariffs to help lower domestic prices. Price monitoring data from the Department of Agriculture (DA) showed that as of Sept. 4, the retail price of local well-milled rice ranged from P48 to P56 per kilogram, while regular milled rice ranged from P52 to P55 per kilogram. Executive Order (EO) No. 39, which took effect yesterday (Sep 5), set the price cap on rice at P45 per kilogram for well-milled rice and P41 for regular milled rice.

Inflation accelerates to 5.3% in August

Headline inflation for August accelerated to 5.3% from 4.7% in July. This is above street expectations (4.7%) and at the high-end of BSP’s forecast range: 4.8%-5.6%. On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose at its fastest rate in six months at +1.1%. This was due largely to faster price increases in food items, particularly rice (+8.7% YoY in Aug from 4.2% in July), vegetables, and fish due to weather disturbances. Higher fuel prices also pushed transport CPI up 7.9% MoM and tempered the YoY reduction in fuel prices to just 9.1% from 23% in July. Meanwhile, core inflation decelerated to 6.1% from 6.7%. This brings average YT-August inflation rate at 6.6%, still above the government’s target range of 2-4%. The BSP retained its view that inflation will decelerate back to its 2%-4% target band in 4Q. To achieve this, MoM CPI changes for the rest of the year should be flat. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Sep 21; Fed’s next policy meeting is on Sep 20.

Gov’t to provide subsidies to rice retailers affected by price caps

Pres. Marcos said that the gov’t will provide subsidies to rice retailers amid the retail price caps set. The gov’t imposed nationwide price ceiling on rice of P41/kg of regular milled rice and P45/kg for well-milled rice. The price controls are a “temporary measure”, and the gov’t expects the prices of rice to ease when new shipments arrive in mid-September. House Speaker Martin Romualdez is looking to allocate P2bn for the subsidy program.

SEC wants to lower ‘friction costs’ and underwriters’ fees

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is seeking to reduce “friction costs” such as underwriting fees to help boost the country’s capital markets according to SEC Commissioner Kelvin Lester Lee

New Monetary Board appointments

President Marcos appointed National Treasurer Rosalia V. de Leon and former Finance undersecretary Romeo L. Bernardo as the new members of the Monetary Board. Ms. De Leon is not yet signing off from her current post as national treasurer. The seventh member of the Board has yet to be announced. Three seats were vacated in July after terms expired.
