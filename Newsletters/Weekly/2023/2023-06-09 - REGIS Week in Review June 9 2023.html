The market fell to 6,507.15 (-0.1% WoW) as trading remained directionless due to the lack of catalysts.

Property names were the biggest drags. ALI (-4.2% WoW) fell sharply as foreign selling in the name accelerated later in the week. ALI saw US$8.5m worth outflows while SMPH (-0.2% WoW) US$0.4m.

The BSP cut the headline reserve requirement by 250 bps from 12.0% to 9.5% for large banks effective end June. The inflation print for May came out at 6.1% (vs. 6.6% in April) which is in line with market expectations. Headline inflation has been dropping largely due to international commodity prices off their peaks although core inflation is still at 7.7% (vs. 7.9% WoW).

Turnover slowed to US$74.7m following last week’s MSCI rebalancing while headline outflows registered at US$8.4m.

Philippine Banks - BSP cuts RRR by 250bps (but not really) – who wins?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena, +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Limited liquidity boost – but which banks are net winners?
The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) announced a 250bp cut in the reserve requirement (RRR) on big banks’ PHP-denominated deposits, effective 30 June 2023. As we pointed out in a recent report though, this will just about cover the expiration of Covid-era rules allowing banks to use MSME loans as alternative RRR compliance, which will also expire on 30 June. On a per-bank basis, though, there is no disclosed MSME-as-RRR exposure. Banks who used the rule less stand to benefit.

RRR cut 250bps to 9.5% for UKBs, smaller cuts for smaller banks
The BSP cut the RRR for universal and commercial banks (UKBs) by 250bps to 9.5%, down to single-digits for the first time. Meanwhile, the RRR for digibanks was cut from 8.0% to 6.0%, and the RRR for thrift and rural banks were cut 100bps each to 2.0% and 1.0% respectively (see Figure 1, at right). The new RRRs will be effective from 30 June. As of April, total UKB deposits stood at P13.8trn, while the digital, thrift and rural banks combined for less than P1trn. We estimate that the theoretical liquidity that should be released by the RRR cuts is about P355bn. The actual figure may be lower as some deposit products are levied
lower RRRs.

But barely covers P302bn in expiring “alternative compliance” MSME loans
But as we noted in our recent report “When is an RRR cut not an RRR cut” (25 May), the Covid-era rule that allowed banks to use their MSME loans as “alternative compliance” for RRR will expire also on 30 June. As of April, MSME loans used as RRR compliance was P302bn, significantly higher than the P263bn as of end-2022 that we had previously reported.

Who’s up? Who’s down? Either way, impact is surprisingly not material
A key question remains – which banks used the MSME-as-RRR rule less, and could therefore benefit more from the RRR cuts. Oddly, every bank we spoke to claimed that their MSME alternative compliance exposure was equivalent to less than 150bps of their PHP deposits.

Regardless, though, we expect that the impact to banks’ earnings will be minimal. We previously estimated that every 100bps in RRR cuts would add just over 1% to our pre-tax profit forecasts. And that didn’t even account for the expiring MSME alternative RRR compliance rule.

DMCI Power plans to build wind farm on Semirara Island

DMCI Power, a wholly-owned unit of DMCI, plans to build a wind power plant in Semirara island. Management has yet to finalize its intended capacity for the wind project but it is looking at bet. 8MW – 12 MW. The project will be funded and undertaken independently by DMCI Power, which expects it to be operational within a year to 15 months

The company is also looking at solar energy to augment the supply in the island but wind energy has shown the “most promise”. Wind power could potentially deliver 33% plant utilization vs. solar at just 17%. Capex per MW is roughly similar for both wind and solar.

DMCI Power has a 12MW hybrid diesel-solar power station (of which 4MW is solar) in Cataingan, Masbate, which is expected to be completed this month. Outside of renewables, DMCI Power has an installed capacity of 145 MW in Masbate, Mindoro, and Palawan (fuel type: 61% diesel, 29% bunker, 10% coal).

DMCI Power accounts for 2% of DMCI’s profit and 6% of NAV. DMCI is trading at a 12% discount to its market-based NAV of P11/sh.

Globe launches prepaid fiber broadband

Globe launched prepaid broadband fiber under the brand “GFiber Prepaid”. This prepaid service comes after Converge began offering its prepaid fiber product latter part of 2022.

The service is being offered at a premium to Converge’s product. GFiber Prepaid is being priced at P299 for seven (7) days to as much as P999 for 30 days. This compares with the P200 for seven (7) days and P700 for 30 days for Converge’s Surf2Sawa prepaid broadband. 

An upfront installation fee of P1,499 for subscribers is required. Subscribers can pay the upfront fee using GCash’s buy now pay later (BNPL) or installment schemes (“GGives” or “GCredit”). Subscribers can apply either through the Globe One app or through select Puregold stores. 

BSP announces 250bps RRR cut

The BSP will implement a 250bps reduction in the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for universal and commercial banks. This is larger than the 200bps that the BSP governor previously telegraphed. The cut in the RRR will begin on June 3

The reduction in the RRR will coincide with the expiration of alternative modes of compliance with reserve requirements. Thus,  as we recently outlined, the actual impact to overall liquidity will be minimal, as this coincides with the lapsing of the rule allowing MSME loans as “alternative compliance” for RRR during Covid.

The lower RRR does not constitute a shift in the BSP's monetary policy settings (to temper inflation).

Consumer loans surge 17% YoY in 1Q23; NPL ratios fall across major segments

Consumer loans in 1Q23 grew 17.0% YoY, accelerating from the 15.4% pace recorded at end-2022. Mortgages sped up to 7.5% YoY (was 5.3%), while Motor Vehicle loans grew 2.1% YoY punching into positive growth territory for the first time since 2Q20 at the start of the Covid-19 pandemic. Credit Card receivables growth moderated to (a still rapid) 28.4% YoY, from the 38.6% clip at end-2022. Salary loans surged 62.3% YoY (from 61.7%) and Other personal loans jumped 45.1% (from 29.0%).

Consumer loan growth far outstripped overall banking sector loan growth of 10.5% YoY in 1Q23. As a result, Consumer loans now account for 20.1% of total loans (up from 19.3% at end-2022), to a new all-time high.

Asset quality improved, with overall Consumer loan NPLs dipping 15bps QoQ to 6.39% from 6.54% at end-2022. NPL Ratios improved across the three major segments, with Mortgages at 7.46% (from 7.55%), Motor Vehicle loans at 7.89% (from 8.44%), and Credit Cards at 3.85% (from 3.88%). Meanwhile, Salary loan NPLs increased to 5.12% (from 4.91%) and Other personal loans were up to 10.32% (from 10.0%).

The falling NPL ratios came despite sharply higher interest rates, particularly for Credit Cards, whose rate cap was raised from 2.0% to 3.0% a month from mid-February.

In 1Q23, total banking NPLs were up 17bps QoQ to 3.42%. We estimate that NPLs of non-consumer loans (ie corporate, mid-market, and SMEs) increased at an even faster clip, up 21bps QoQ to 2.68% (from 2.47% at end-2022).

NPLs up 9bps MoM in April to 3.51% -- a loan mix issue?

Asset quality ratios deteriorated slightly in April. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) stood at 3.51%, blipping up 9bps MoM, and is now up 26bps ytd compared to 3.25% at end-2022. In PHP terms, NPLs jumped 3.1% MoM, the biggest monthly increase since May 2021. The Past Due (PD) ratio was up 5bps MoM to 4.12% (was 3.79% at end-2022), with the nominal amount of PD loans up 1.9% MoM. On the other hand, Restructured Loan (RL) ratio improved slightly to 2.59% from 2.60% in March and from 2.61% at end-2022. NPL cover dipped slightly to 103%, from 105% in March. Meanwhile, system loan growth slowed to 9.9% YoY in April, compared to 10.6% YoY in March, dipping back below 10% for the first time since August 2022. On a MoM basis, loan portfolios expanded 0.3%.

The NPL data seems at odds with the commentary from management of banks we cover. During their respective 1Q23 briefings, all four banks suggested that asset quality ratios were steady into 2Q23, and all claimed that there was no meaningful stress among borrowers.

One explanation might be that consumer loans – higher yield, higher NPLs – are growing faster than total loans. For banks under Regis coverage,  consumer loans grew 12-18% YoY in 1Q23, compared to overall loan growth of 10.6%. In particular, credit card loans were up more than 30% YoY. By Regis calculations, credit card receivables grew by P35bn between end-2022 and April 2023, accounting for more than 80% of the total loan growth of P41.6bn ytd.

NEDA Board approves P23.4bn TPLEX extension project

The board of the National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) has accepted San Miguel’s unsolicited proposal to extend the Tarlac-Pangasinan-La Union Expressway project. The P23.4bn project is the fastest unsolicited proposal approved by the body, from its submission last March 17. The project will extend the existing 89-km tollroad by another 59-km to connect Rosario, La Union to San Juan, La Union. The extension is expected to be fully completed by 2028. The next steps are to have negotiations with the proponent, a Swiss challenge, and implementation of the project. 

DOTr sets NAIA concession at 15 years

The DOTr has proceeded with its plan to solicit proposals from the private sector for a 15-year contract to maintain and upgrade NAIA. The DOTr and the Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) submitted their joint proposal to pursue the privatization of NAIA through a solicited bidding. Both agencies handed in their joint proposal to the NEDA board for approval, even as they are still evaluating a P100 billion offer from a group of conglomerates to rehabilitate the airport.

Inflation eases to 6.1% in May

Headline CPI rose by 6.1% in May, further moderating from 6.6% in April. This is in line with Bloomberg consensus forecast (6.1%) and came at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 5.8%-6.6%. High base effects led to slower YoY increase in food prices of 7.4% YoY from 7.9% in April. It is worth noting that food prices actually rose +0.2% MoM in May, accelerating from negative MoM prints since February due to higher prices of rice and vegetables. Lower global fuel prices pushed transport and gas inflation to moderate to -0.5% YoY and -9.4% YoY, respectively. Electricity inflation also eased to 13.6% YoY in May from 14.6% in April, with flattish MoM growth rate.

Core inflation also eased to 7.7% in May from 7.9% in April, as core CPI rose just +0.2% MoM. YT-May inflation rate averaged 7.5% YoY, still above the BSP’s target band of 2-4% for the year.

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno, who is part of the Monetary Board, predicts inflation will move closer to the target band by Sep. The next Monetary Board policy meeting is scheduled on June 22.

President Marcos approves export industry blueprint

President Marcos approved the blueprint for the Philippine export industry. Philippine Export Development Plan 2023-2028 will enforce “industry-level” interventions to address challenges facing the sector, which has lagged regional rivals. Philippines remains an export laggard because local companies can’t come up with the quantity and quality of exportable products.

In the past, policies were largely focused at the macro level. Marcos is expected to sign a memo defining the roles of various agencies in the development plan. In 2022, exports of goods and services only reached US$98.5bn, short of the government’s downgraded target of US$105bn. Merchandise exports contributed US$57.5bn while service revenues accounted for US$41bn.

Dollar reserves slip to US$101.3bn in May

Gross international reserves (GIR) as of end-May fell by US$464mn (-0.5% MoM) to US$101.3bn, due to debt servicing, lower value of gold holdings, and exchange rate market operations. This represents a more-than-adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.6 months’ worth of imports.

BSP expects the GIR level to end at US$100bn by Dec 2023, up from US$82bn in Dec 2022 but lower than the peak of US$110bn in end-Dec 2020

Gov’t could boost infra spending to 12% of GDP

The country could boost infrastructure spending to as much as 12% of GDP as early as next year if the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) and PPP go as planned, according to Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. Of this, 5-6% will come from the national budget and the rest will come from PPP and MIF.

The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn on infrastructure this year (+1% YoY), equivalent to 5.3% of GDP. From 2022-2028, infrastructure spending is expected to rise at a +10% CAGR, slower than 10-year historical CAGR of +18%.

NAIA privatization doable by early 2024

The privatization of NAIA could kick off by 1Q24, According to Undersecretary for Aviation and Airports Roberto Lim. The winning bidder must commit to invest P141bn for the expansion and improvement of NAIA within a 15-year concession period. In addition, the private concessionaire will be asked to pay an upfront cost of P30bn and an annuity payment of P2bn. The government will also have a share in revenues from commercial and non-commercial operations.

Last May, the NAIA consortium comprised of 6 local conglomerates submitted an unsolicited proposal valued at P100bn. The Consortium includes six Filipino conglomerates + US-based Global Infrastructure Partners:

  • Aboitiz InfraCapital –AEV PM
  • AC Infrastructure – AC PM
  • Asia’s Emerging Dragon Corp. – LTG PM
  • Alliance Global – Infracorp Dev’t – AGI PM
  • Filinvest Development Corp. - FDC PM
  • JG Summit – JGS PM

Based on its 2019 annual report, NAIA recorded a net income of P5.0bn, operating profit of P6.8bn, and revenues of P15.2bn. In terms of revenues, passenger service charge accounted for (36%, landing/parking fees 34%, and  rent income and others 30%. NAIA handled 48.1m passengers and 306k aircraft movements.
