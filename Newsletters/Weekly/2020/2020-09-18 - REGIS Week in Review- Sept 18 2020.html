PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Ayala Land - Substantial recovery in July/August reservation sales
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Managing cash flow

We hosted a discussion with Ayala Land in the Jefferies Asia Forum. Several members of the management team were present: Bernard Dy (President and CEO), Augusto Bengzon (CFO), Jose Jalandoni (Head of Commercial unit and Chairman of AREIT), and Robert Lao (Head of Residential unit). Ayala Land believes that 2020 and 2021 will be “difficult” but the economy will recover substantially by 2022. As result, management plans to tightly manage cash flow/liquidity in the interim.

Substantial recovery in July/August reservation sales

Reservation sales reached P8.5bn in July and P7bn in August. These are already 60%-70% of the 2019 monthly average of P12.2bn and are significant improvements from the P6bn figure in June. 8M20 reservation sales have already reached P53.8bn, c. 75% of our FY forecast (of P73bn, -50% from 2019). The residential sales recovery has become more broad based. In June, Avida and Amaia had already attained c. 70% of 2019 levels but the higher-end Alveo and Ayala Land Premier just picked up to 60%-70% of pre-COVID levels in July and August. Although Ayala Land has loosened payment schemes, for the most part, it has only granted minor price discounts.

Mall foot traffic still 25%-30% of pre-COVID levels

Similar to June, about 60% of mall tenants are open while foot traffic is only 25%-30% of pre-COVID foot traffic. As a result, the company continues to grant substantial rental concessions to mall tenants. However, despite the weak mall performance, very few tenants have permanently closed. In fact, most have chosen to renew their contracts.

Optimistic on BPO and POGO sectors

Office remains the most resilient segment, with the portfolio still at 95% occupancy. None of the company’s BPO tenants have given any indications of shutting down. Furthermore, Ayala Land recently signed a 22,000sqm preleasing contract with a BPO for space due in 2022. Meanwhile, the company also believes that the POGO industry will recover once travel bans are lifted and Chinese employees can return to the Philippines.

Expect completion delays

While the construction cost increases brought about by government guidelines have been minor, construction delays will be more substantial. Ayala Land has only deployed 19,000 construction workers, about 1/3 of pre-COVID levels. This is largely due to social distancing requirements in construction sites. Ayala Land is currently trading at 16.1X FY21E P/E, 1.9X FY21E P/BV, and 40% discount to NAV.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy - Covid-19 update – path to further reopening (MGCQ) in October?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Case count stays high, but hospitals coping so far

The country’s Covid-19 case count briefly dipped below 2,000/day in early September but has since crept back up to 4,000/day. Despite this, hospital occupancy in Metro Manila and nation-wide has eased from their early-August peaks. The government seems in favor of further reopening the economy, absent a massive fiscal stimulus program. Should daily Covid cases, and hospitalization rates stabilize, could Metro Manila move to less restrictive MGCQ in October?

Daily cases back up to 4,000/day

As of 16 September, the cumulative Covid-19 case count stood at 272,934. In early September, new cases dropped below 2,000/day. However, this likely coincided with the re-tightening to Modified Enhance Community Quarantine (MECQ) from 4-18 August. True enough, two weeks after bottoming, and the 7-day average case count is back up to c4,000/day.

Hospital occupancy eases, as 96%of active cases are mild or asymptomatic

Despite the return to the looser General Community Quarantine (GCQ), and the higher case count, we note that occupancy of hospital beds set aside for Covid treatment has surprisingly fallen to 60-65% in Metro Manila from its 80-90% peak in early August (Figures 3-5). The national average occupancy is around 45- 50%. We reckon this is partly because, according to the Department of Health (DOH), more than 96% of current active cases are either mild or asymptomatic, and do not require hospitalization.

Learning to live with Covid – moving Metro Manila to looser MGCQ in October?

There seems to be a growing consensus among government officials that the economy needs to reopen. As early as July, Secretary of Finance Carlos Dominguez wanted to move Metro Manila and nearby provinces to the least restrictive Modified GCQ; until a vaccine is found, “we will have to live with (Covid).” In mid-August, a DOH Undersecretary said a “balance between health and economics” must be found. Earlier this week, we wrote that the InterAgency Task Force (IATF) is increasing ridership in public transportation, reducing distance requirements per vehicle.

Reopening plays? Risks

Fundamentally, we remain cautious, as the lack of fiscal stimulus likely means a protracted recovery in the economy and in corporate earnings. (See report “Pushing on a string”, 13 Aug). However, we do acknowledge that with all-time low interest rates, and light investor positioning, the market could react to positive news flow. Our “reopening” portfolio includes BDO (BDO PM), Ayala Land (ALI PM), SM Prime (SMPH PM), Ayala Corp (AC PM), Jollibee (JFC PM), Universal Robina (URC PM), GT Capital (GTCAP PM), and Bloomberry (BLOOM PM). We would be wary of new lockdowns, though, if Covid cases increase and hospitals are overwhelmed as a result of looser restrictions.

Corp. News

PLDT group bidding for Sky Cable

Media reports say that the group of Manuel Pangilinan, through PLDT (TEL PM), is bidding to acquire Sky Cable. Sky Cable is a firm offering cable TV, DTH satellite and broadband services and is owned by ABS-CBN (ABS PM) which is apparently being sold after the government refused to renew ABS-CBN’s broadcasting franchise (license). The media report/s suggest a price of P15bn to P18bn. Sky Cable has over 500,000 subscribers for its pay TV services and over 300,000 for its broadband services.

Toyota returns to two-shift production

Beginning Sep 14, Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) will return to two-shift production, signaling normalization of operations according to TMP President Atsuhiro Okamato. The actual capacity will still depend on the demand situation. TMP looking to increase capacity further from October onwards.

Lockdown drives car sales lower in August

Data from the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines, Inc. (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA) showed sales stood at 17,906 units in August, down by 40% YoY, 13% MoM. The culprit cited by the group outside COVID were tighter restrictions (i.e. MECQ) put in place in Metro Manila and nearby provinces in the first two weeks of August. The sales in August brings YT-August industry sales down by 48% YoY to 123,489 units (vs. industry association FY20 target of 240k units, -42% YoY). Toyota sold 8,044 units, down 39% YoY and 9% MoM and brings their YT-August sales to 52,525 units, down 48% YoY. Our forecasts assume FY20 car sales will drop by 44% to 90,372 units.

Airlines await P700mn loan from Bayanihan II

The Air Carriers Association of the Philippines (ACAP) said about P700mn had been allocated for local airlines under Republic Act 11494 (i.e. Bayanihan II). Each airline is expected to receive around P140mn loan assistance under the economic stimulus package according to ACAP Exec. Director and Vice-Chairman Roberto C.O. Lim. The air sector may also get loans of P10bn or P2bn to be divided among the five carriers. The Bayanihan II also mentioned grants on regulatory fees. We believe the financial assistance falls short of the requirements of the airline sector. Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) cash burn per month alone is c. P2bn per month.

SEC denies appeal for tender offer to minority shareholders of GSMI

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) denied the appeal of Josefina Multi-Ventures Corp, for a tender offer to minority shareholders of Ginebra San Miguel (GSM PM). San Miguel Corp sold its 75.6% stake in GSM and 51.2% stake in San Miguel Brewery in exchange for 4.24bn shares in San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM). Josefina, a minority shareholder of GSM, argued that the share swap transaction should be subject to a tender offer as the acquisition of 75.6% stake in GSM breached the 35% threshold. The SEC said in a 2019 resolution that the San Miguel share swap transaction is exempted from the mandatory tender offer because it will not result in a change in control of San Miguel Beer and Ginebra.

AREIT to acquire Teleperformance Cebu

AREIT signed a deed of sale with Ayala Land to acquire Teleperformance Cebu. The intention to acquire Teleperformance Cebu was revealed prior to AREIT’s listing and they paid P1.45bn for the 18,092sqm PEZA accredited building in Cebu. This is 100% occupied with 17,682sqm under Teleperformance and 410 sqm for McDonald’s. The acquisition will increase AREIT’s leasable floor area by 12% to 172,000sqm but only the building will be acquired by AREIT. The land will be leased from Ayala Land at a rate of 5% of rental revenue.

AVID car sales fall 7% MoM in August

The Association of Vehicle Importers and Distributors (AVID) reported that its members sold 5,100 units in August, down by 7% MoM. This brings YT-August car sales down 48.7% YoY to 57,202 units. Combining the data of AVID and CAMPI (adj. for brands present in both), industry-wide vehicle sales fell 11% MoM (-39% YoY) in August to 19,804 units. Toyota’s market share increased to 41% in August (+1bps MoM, YoY). This brings 7M20 industry-wide vehicle sales down by 49% YoY to 134,671 units. Toyota’s market share at 39%, down from 40% in end-Dec.

Macro News

President Duterte signs P165.5bn pandemic stimulus package into law

President Duterte signed the new Bayanihan to Recover as One Act (a.k.a. Bayanihan II) into law, 18 days after Congress ratified the bill. The law provides a P165.5bn COVID-19 relief package, of which P140bn will be sourced from general appropriations while P25.5bn will serve as a standby authority should additional resources become available. The law also grants a 30-day grace period for payments of residential rents and utility bills, falling within the enhanced community quarantine (ECQ) or modified ECQ without incurring interests, penalties and other charges. It also grants a 60-day grace period on all loans, regardless of community quarantine status. Additionally, it will also raise P17.5bn in additional revenues from Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGO), after imposing a 5% franchise tax. The Bayanihan II will be effective until December 19, 2020.

Public transport improving (slowly)

The government seems to be slowly opening mass transit in Metro Manila. By Monday, 14 September, the LFRB will be allowing 17,372 jeepneys  (c32% of pre-Covid levels) to ply their routes again. Also the IATF has allowed a gradual easing of distancing rules inside public transportation vehicles. The first phase runs for 2 weeks, also starting 14 September. We understand that the new IATF rules would bring train/LRT/MRT capacity initially up to 20%, then eventually to 50% by mid-October. It is unclear how the new rules will affect jeepneys and buses currently already running at 50% passenger capacity. Improving public transportation access – gradual as it may be – is critical to reopening the economy. We expect malls, retailers and restaurants – particularly those catering to the mass and middle-income segments – to benefit.

PCC readies rules on notification-exempt mergers

The Philippine Competition Commission will be issuing guidelines on the suspension of M&A reviews with transaction values below P50bn. Due to the two-year moratorium on PCC reviews under the recently signed Republic Act 11494 (i.e. Bayanihan II).

OFW remittances up 7.8% YoY, 12.9% MoM to US$2.78bn in July

Remittances rose for the second consecutive month, increasing by 7.8% YoY and 12.9% MoM to US$2.783bn in July. Land-based remittances rose 12.6% YoY and offset the 9.2% drop in sea-based remittances. Meanwhile, remittances from USA and Asia rose 25% and 23%, respectively. In PHP terms, remittances rose 4.3% YoY and 11% MoM in July. This brings 7M20 remittances down 2.4% YoY to US$16.8bn (in PHP terms, down 5.4% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 5% in FY20.

Foreign direct investments continue to rise in June

Foreign direct investment (FDI) net inflows sustained its growth momentum, rising by 7.1% to US$481mn in June 2020. Net equity capital investments expanded to US$173 million on the back of a 138% YoY increase in equity capital placements to US$185mn and the decline in withdrawals. Bulk of equity capital placements originated from Japan, the UK, and the USA. By economic activity, these equity placements were invested in 1) manufacturing, 2) human health and social work, 3) financial and insurance, and (4) real estate. The net investments in debt instruments in June 2020 dropped by 28.8% to US$229mn. Meanwhile, reinvestment of earnings was also lower by 19.4% at US$80mn. The cumulative net FDI inflows stood at US$3bn, down 18.3% YoY.

 

GIR climbs to US$98.95bn in August

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by US$350mn MoM to US$98.95bn as of end-August 2020. This reflects inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad and was partly offset, by the National Government’s debt payments and revaluation losses from the BSP’s gold holdings. The GIR now is equivalent to 9 months’ worth of imports.

Metro Manila provincial bus operations may resume within the month

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) is preparing for the reopening of some provincial bus routes originating from and ending in the National Capital Region (NCR). The government plans to start with provinces near Metro Manila, such as regions III and IV. The LTFRB is currently in talks with local governments to come up with a uniform set of requirements for passengers to prove they can cross provincial borders (i.e. travel clearance, police clearance, gov’t-issued ID). Meanwhile, some provincial governments have agreed to reopen their borders to accommodate cross-province public transportation. Out of 81 provinces nationwide, at least three have initially agreed to accommodate cross-border travel from Metro Manila, including Antique, Quirino and Bataan.

Infrastructure spending drops in July

National government infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) dropped 31% YoY and 17% MoM to P52bn in July. The government cited lower spending from the DPWH due to the gradual implementation of public works and healthy protocols in place. This brings YT-July infrastructure spend to P350bn, down 9.4% YoY. Last July, the government lowered its infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. For FY21, the infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) is supposed to increase by 43% YoY to P1.12tn. This target is c. 7% higher than what was spent in 2019 for infrastructure (P1.05tn).

Government suspends reduced physical distancing

The government has reimposed the requirement to maintain one-meter distance between passengers of public transportation, pending a decision by President Duterte. On September 14, the government lowered the requirement to 0.75m between passengers. Some doctors’ associations subsequently stated their apprehensions about the lower requirement.

Government sets minimum bid for Malaya plant at P2.2bn

PSALM has set the minimum bid price for the privatization of the 650MW Malaya Thermal Power Plant and its underlying land at P2.2bn. The bid submission deadline is 12 noon on September 23. PSALM conducted two rounds of public bidding for the sale of the Malaya assets last year, both of which failed. It also proceeded with a negotiated sale, which also failed. The minimum bid price in the last bidding and the negotiated sale was P4.48bn.
