LT Group - Announced price increase well above tax hike for 2021
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

P20/pack price increase vs P5/pack tax hike in 2021

LT Group said that its unit PMFTC raised cigarette prices by around P20/pack across its brands, equivalent to an increase of 20-29%, effective October 5. The announced price hikes are well above what we had been anticipating, as well as the scheduled tax increase for January 2021. The magnitude of the price hikes suggests that management is confident in the market’s ability to absorb such increases without materially impacting volumes or profits.

Truck prices higher by 20-29% per pack

Effective October 5, the truck price of Marlboro has been raised to P115.80/pack (+20%), while the truck price of Fortune has been raised to P106.50/pack (+22%). The truck price of its low-end brand Jackpot has also been raised 29% to P87.40/pack. On a per stick basis, the increases translate to a 17%-25% increase from the high-end (Marlboro) to the low-end (Jackpot).

Tax-driven price increases will weigh on volumes, but not profits

The price increases are similar to those implemented last August 2019 that were meant to cover a bigger tax hike of P10/pack in January 2020 (vs the scheduled tax hike of P5/pack in January 2021). The new prices are higher by around 10% vs our assumed prices for 2021, and will translate to an average gross margin per pack that is 40% higher than those in 2020, and 33% higher than our current forecasts, all else equal. While the bigger increase will most likely mean a bigger volume decline vs our existing forecasts (-3% for 2021), we are comfortable that PMFTC can still hit P30bn (+4%) in profit next year. Given that volumes have been relatively resilient after 1) the 20% price increase last year and 2) the effects of the pandemic on distribution channels, we think there is bigger scope for profits to surprise to the upside than to the downside.

Higher dividend payout not unlikely

We think that the improved dividend outlook remains among LTG’s main investment cases. We expect the dividend yield to be an attractive 5% over the next two years on conservative payout assumptions. ICTSI - All aboard! Maintain Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Position for a V-shaped recovery

We upgrade our price target (+32%) and earnings forecasts sharply, after pricing in the 2Q outperformance and an anticipated bounce in global trade post-lockdown. The shares have recovered from recent lows but remain undervalued, in our view, trading at just 9.4x on FY21E EV/EBITDA. Maintain Buy.

Quick return to pre-pandemic levels

Global container trade is bouncing back fairly quickly post-lockdown due to a rise in e-commerce and limited air freight capacity. We believe ICT’s earnings will recover sharply, as trade flows continue to be strong. With cash flows relatively intact and principal maturities termed out, we believe ICT should also be able to take advantage of any brownfield acquisition opportunities.

EPS forecasts up sharply; Consensus to follow

We raise our FY20E-FY22E profit estimates substantially, after lifting our volume and yield assumptions by 5-18% and 1-3%, respectively. Our revised estimates suggest ICT’s earnings will drop by 14% YoY in FY20 and rise by 55% and 27% in FY21 and FY22, respectively, as volumes continue to rise and the company continues to de-lever. We believe consensus estimates are low and Street upgrades are likely.

Revised price target of P145/sh offers 27% total return

After lifting our volume forecasts and rolling-forward our DCF to FY21, we arrive at our revised SOTP NAV-based price target of P145/sh, offering 27% total return. At our price target, the shares will be trading at 10.5x FY21E EV/EBITDA, still below historical average of 11.7x. Downside risks relate to trade, dilutive acquisitions, and labor issues.

Cebu Air - Announces US$500mn capital raising
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Convertible prefs (via rights issue) + convertible bonds

Cebu Air disclosed today its plans to raise up to US$500mn through a combination of (1) a rights issue of US$250mn worth of convertible preferred shares and (2) a private placement of US$250mn worth of convertible bonds. The conversion price is expected to be the same for the two instruments and will be set within the range of P38-45/sh, which represents a premium of 2% to 21% over CEB’s 30-day VWAP. These transactions are still subject to regulatory approvals and other conditions.

Proceeds to plug pandemic-induced cash burn

In our July 6 report (The long flight back, downgrade to Hold), we flagged the potential of a capital raising exercise as early as 4Q20, given depleting cash reserves from persistent travel restrictions. We estimate the US$500mn amount to be raised will likely sustain operations for more than 12 months, after considering its monthly cash burn of US$35-40mn. We believe a successful issuance will boost the company’s liquidity and should remove one of the key overhangs on the share price.

Convertible pref share rights issue to minimize potential dilution

The maximum amount of proceeds (i.e. US$500mn) from this exercise is larger than CEB’s current market cap of P22bn (US$454mn). Thus, we estimate these two fund-raising exercises could, assuming the low-end of the conversion price, double the share count. Shareholders that participate in the preferred share rights issue could reduce the impact of share dilution. We understand that CEB’s parent, JG Summit (JGS PM), intends to participate in the pref share rights issue and will also fully underwrite the deal. Management anticipates the deal to be completed on or before the end of March 2021.

 
 

Corp. News

Restaurants allowed to operate above 50% capacity

The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) issued a Memorandum Circular which permits some industries to operate at 100% capacity, including construction and restaurants. The dine-in services of restaurants can now operate above 50% capacity. This will also be subject to social distancing and other requirements by the local government unit (city or municipality). Our channel checks suggest that only a few restaurants are currently able to fill up the 50% dine-in capacity. Additionally, we estimate dine-in currently accounts for 20-30% of restaurant sales (60-70% pre-lockdown). However, mall-based stores account for 40-50% of total store network for the restaurant operators under our coverage (JFC, MAXS, PIZZA) which could hinder potential capacity. Meanwhile, restaurants occupy about 30% of mall space.

LTG discloses P20/pack increase across product line-up effective Oct 5, 2020

LT Group (LTG PM) disclosed that PMFTC raised prices across its product line-up effective October 5, 2020. The new truck prices are as follows - Marlboro at P115.80 from P96.40 (+P19.4/pack, +20%); Fortune at P106.50 from P87.40 (+P19.5/pack, +22%) and Jackpot at P87.40 from P67.80 (P19.6/pack, +29%). On a per stick basis, new retail prices are - Marlboro at P7 from P6 (+17%); Fortune at P6 from P5 (+20%) and Jackpot at P5 from4 (+25%). The price increase is much higher than the scheduled tax increase of P5/pack scheduled for January 2021. Recall that the last price increase last August (+P20/pack vs tax increase of P10/pack) helped profits grow 40% in 1H20 despite a 26% decline in volume because of improved margins. Our current forecasts for PMFTC assume a price increase of P8/pack for FY21, -3% volume (44.5bn sticks) and +4% profit (P30.2bn).

Converge ICT narrows IPO price range

Converge ICT (CICT) has announced a narrower price range for its upcoming IPO. The price range is now P16.5 to P19.0 per share, previously it had a maximum of P24/share. The IPO could raise up to P32.9bn from 1.51bn offer shares. The break down would be 480.8mn primary shares – up to P9.1bn and 1.0bn secondary shares – up to P19.4bn. It also has around 225.8mn overallotment shares – up to P4.3bn. With an expected 7.46bn shares, post-IPO, the market capitalization would range from P123bn to P142bn (US$2.5bn to 2.9bn). CICT is a fixed broadband service provider with 732,000 subscribers as of Jun 2020.

PPA to impose arrastre fees on “empties”, other new charges

The Philippine Ports Authority has proposed the imposition of arrastre fees of foreign empty containers in international ports in Manila and Batangas. The prescribed rates will ensure that policies of handling empty containers are uniform and updated. It also compensates the terminal/cargo-handling operators for services rendered on empty containers in the yard. The prescribed rates for empty containers were as follows: For South Harbor and MICT, the empty 20’ container arrastre rates would be P4,307.00 for import and P 3,516.00 for export. According to Asian Terminals Inc. (ATI PM), 40% to 45% of volumes handled at Manila South Harbor since 2016 were empty containers.

More on PPA proposal to impose charges on empties and OOG cargo

The Philippine Ports Authority is proposing to impose tariffs on (1) yard services (a.k.a. arrastre) for empty containers and (2) all services for OOG (Out of Guage) shipments at the ports of Manila and Batangas. The proposed rates for empty containers will be similar for the full/laden boxes while OOG shipments will be charged three times more than the basic rate of containers. However, ICTSI (ICT PM) management believes these proposed rates may still be negotiated downwards over the next couple of months. We estimate around 25-30% of MICT’s container volumes are empties. Assuming the rates are only at a 20% discount to full containers, we estimate charging for the empty boxes alone could potentially increase MICT’s revenues by 6% annually and c. 2% to ICT’s group-wide revenues. Given that every 1% change in topline translates to a 5% change in net profit, the earnings impact could be a maximum of 10%. There was no timetable given but management reckons the new charges could be implemented by early 2021. 

Aerocity franchise bill clears second reading at Senate

The Senate approved the bill, which grants San Miguel Aerocity a 50-year franchise for the Bulacan airport greenfield project, on second reading. The passage was pushed to allow the chamber to have it passed on third reading by next week, ahead of the Oct. 17-Nov. 15 session suspension. Last Sep 7, the Lower House approved House Bill 7507 on third and final reading. The franchise bill will also exempt the grantee from direct and indirect taxes during the 10-year construction period. Meanwhile, San Miguel Corp. (SMC PM) president and COO Ramon Ang said he expects the P734bn Bulacan airport to be operational in 5-6 years’ time. Mr. Ang also said that other countries with massive flagship projects get tax incentives. He also said that he has plans to put up his own airline company when the market recovers from the pandemic (or after six years).

Dito hires affiliate for tower rollout

Dito Telecommunity signed a P10bn deal with Udenna Infrastructure Corp to construct cell towers and roll out fiber optic cables. It would involve construction of 1,500 towers and the rollout of 1,400km of fiber. Dito is claiming 859 towers as of Sept, compared to its target of 1,300 initially aimed for July 2020 (now Jan 2021). The company is still hoping to launch operations by March 2021.

FGEN advances Tokyo Gas partnership, announces EPC contractor

First Gen disclosed that it signed a joint cooperation agreement with Tokyo Gas Co., Ltd representing the next phase of their joint development of FGEN LNG Corporation’s interim offshore LNG terminal project. FGEN and Tokyo Gas initially signed a joint development agreement in December 2018 and under the latest agreement, Tokyo Gas will have a 20% participating interest in the project and will provide support in development, construction, o&m to achieve a final investment decision. FGEN also announced the selection of McConnel Dowell Philippines as Preferred Tenderer for EPC of the interim offshore LNG terminal. McConnell Dowell is an Australian-based engineering and construction firm with over 60 years experience in marine design and construction projects. Their recent LNG projects include Donggi Senoro LNG project in Sulawesi, Indonesia and marine works for Tangguh LNG project in West Papua, Indonesia. The project consists of modifying the existing jetty and building an adjunct onshore gas receiving facility. This will allow FGEN LNG to accelerate its ability to introduce LNG to the Philippines as early as 3Q22 to serve the natural gas requirements of existing and future gas-fired plants of third parties and FGEN LNG affiliates.

Cebu Air announces US$500mn fund raising plan

Cebu Air plans to underundergo a fundraising plan (the “Business Transformation Fundraising Plan”) amounting to as much as USD500mn. The Convertible Preferred Shares stock rights offer for an aggregate proceeds of about USD250mn. This will be a private placement of convertible bonds with aggregate subscription price of up to USD250mn. The conversion price is expected to be the same and to be set within P38-45/sh range, representing 2% to 21% conversion premium over CEB’s 30-day volume weighted average price from August 26, 2020 to October 7, 2020. Petron considers shutdown of Bataan refinery

Petron says it may shut down its refinery supposedly because of an “uneven” playing field

CEO Ramon Ang says he will close down the 180,000b/d refinery if talks with government will not succeed. Ang is claiming that it is more viable to just import finished product, blaming it on the tax regime. Petron supposedly pays taxes upon arrival of its crude imports, while importers pay only when the finished goods leave their depots. The facility has not been operating since 1Q20, even before the pandemic, supposedly because of maintenance activity. Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) announced it was shutting down its refinery only two months ago. Petron spent US$2bn to upgrade its refinery and the project was completed in 2014.

PNR Malolos-Clark rail line construction to start this quarter

The Department of Transportation plans to start construction of the PNR Clark Phase 2 project by November or December this year. It recently awarded the first three packages to various contractors, including listed EEI (EEI PM) and Megawide (MWIDE PM). The PNR Clark Phase 2 is a 54-kilometer rail line (est. to cost P283bn), which will connect Malolos, Bulacan to Clark International Airport. PNR Clark Phase 2 (Malolos-Clark) is part of the planned 163-km P778bn North–South Commuter Railway Project. The project is financed by JICA and ADB.

8990 Holdings gets SEC approval to securitize P2.1bn of receivables

8990 Holdings received conditional approval from the SEC to securitize P2.13bn of its receivables. Getting final credit rating for the securities is one of the conditions for this to push through. The breakdown would be P1.59bn – Tranche A and P531mn – Tranche B. As of 2Q20, 8990 had P23.5bn in residential receivables.

 
 

Marco News

Loan growth eases to 4.7% in August; M3 growth mildly slower at 14.2% in August

Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, eased to 4.7% in August from 6.7% in July. The loans to manufacturing and trade declined further in August and this is the 5th straight month of weak loan demand. Meanwhile, money supply grew by 14.2% in August, mildly slower than the 14.7% growth in July.

BoC, BIR exceed nine-month collection target

Based on preliminary data, the BIR and BOC collected a combined P1.82tn in 9M20, beating the lowered P1.682tn target by 8.2% according to Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III. Collections of BIR and BOC for 9M20 represents 80% of FY20E target. In September alone, the two agencies collected P169bn, 8.7% more than their combined target of P155.31bn for the month. BIR collected P118.6bn, down 21% YoY and 31% MoM while the BOC collected P50.2bn, down 14.6% YoY but up 12.5% MoM.

PCC may still review ‘notification-exempt’ deals

M&As that have been exempted from notifying the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) under Bayanihan II law may still be reviewed after a year if they are seen as likely to “substantially lessen” competition. Under Bayanihan II, M&A with transaction values under P50bn will be exempted from PCC review within two years from the effectivity of the law, Sept 11, 2020. These reviews will be done a year after the effectivity of Bayanihan II. It said that parties to mergers and acquisitions with values below the P50bn threshold may still voluntarily notify the PCC.

Inflation eases to 2.3% in September

Headline inflation further eased to 2.3% in September from 2.4% in August. This is within BSP’s expected range of 1.8-2.6%. This was due to a deceleration in food (i.e. meat, milk, cheese, eggs, and vegetables) and alcoholic & tobacco products and offset by higher inflation for transport services and education. Core inflation rose to 3.2% in September from 3.1% in August which brings YT-Sep inflation to 2.5%, within government’s target range of 2-4%.

Sale of state-owned mining assets eyed

The Department of Finance has asked for the revival of the privatization of state-owned mining assets. These assets could include Nonoc Nickel Mines in Surigao City and copper mines in Basay, Negros Oriental, among others according to Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III. The government is firm on reviving its mining assets to boost job opportunities and lift economies around the mine sites.

2021 budget delay seen to hurt recovery

A delay in the passage of the 2021 national budget (P4.5tn, +10% YoY) will likely hobble the Philippine economy’s recovery according to Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua. It may come to a reenacted budget which will use the 2020 budget, which is 9-10% lower than the proposed 2021 budget. The Lower House will vote on the final approval of the proposed national budget for 2021 when it resumes session on November 16. Recall, the session was suspended due to a leadership squabble. Due to the delay in the passage of the bill at the Lower House, senators flagged that it is likely that the government will operate under a reenacted in early 2021. Malacañang may call for a special session of Congress if needed to prevent the delay in the passage of the national budget according to Presidential Spokesperson Harry Roque.
