REGIS REPORT

02 October 2020

The PSEi rose 2.75%, as optimism surrounding a re-opening economy boosted sentiment. The market broke a 4-week losing streak, though volumes as well as foreign participation continued to be anemic. Foreign selling was lighter this week due to several regional markets on holiday, giving share prices a breather.

While President Duterte this week announced that Metro Manila and a few other areas will remain under the current lockdown levels (i.e. general community quarantine or GCQ) for October, government data point to a more contained pandemic situation – the total number of active cases has been declining (53k at end Sep vs 59.7k at end Aug) and hospital occupancy numbers are showing improvement.

Gainers outnumbered losers 5:2.

ICTSI (+7.6% WoW) surged as volumes are expected to recover along with the improvement in global container volumes.

MPI (+7.6% WoW) also gained. The company is launched a P5bn share buyback program from today, until the amount is fully utilized.

The week still saw rotation from defensives to cyclicals. Property names managed to gain, with SM Prime (+5.3% WoW), Megaworld (+2.4% MoM) and Ayala Land (+1.5% MoM) all rising this week despite negative news on the POGOs.

POGOs were in the spotlight again this week. Two property consultants - Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) and Colliers - reported that POGOs vacated over 100,000 sqm of office space YTD. This figures imply POGOs ceased leasing at least 55,000sqm of offices within 3Q alone. The likely culprits for this are travel bans and limits on office space utilization, combined with increasing taxes. POGOs occupy some 1.2m of office space or roughly 11% of Metro Manila office space. Massive POGO closures would have implications on sectors far beyond property.

Meanwhile, defensive names such as First Gen (-0.85% WoW) and Puregold (unch) underperformed.

Foreigners continued to be sellers of the market, with net outflows reaching US$77m this week (US$85m adjusted for SM Prime blocks). Average daily volumes continued to be thin at US$109m (ave).

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Banks - Credit card rate/fee caps - slippery slope
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

A temporary rate cap – or a slippery slope?

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has set an interest rate cap of 24% on unpaid credit card balances, compared to the current rate of around 42%. It has also capped rates on installment loans, and fees on cash advances. We estimate the new limits could cut revenues by 3-5%, and profit by 6-10% for banks we cover. This is the first time the BSP has explicitly set rate/fee limits. Some banks fear it won’t be the last.

BSP caps finance charges at 24% vs current 42%

The BSP issued Circular 1098 imposing limits on interest rates and fees that banks can charge on credit card services (Figure 1), effective from 03 November. The most notable is an interest rate cap of 24% (ie 2% a month) on finance charges on the unpaid portion of the credit card bill, or on “revolvers”). This is much lower than the 42% (ie 3.5% a month) currently charged by most credit cards. The BSP also set caps on installment loans rates, and on cash advance fees, though banks tell us the impact of these is much lower than on revolvers.

Could cut 6-10% off pre-tax profit (2019 base), banks likely to mitigate impact

As we wrote in August, credit cards make up 3-6% of banks’ total loans (see table at right), but yields are as much as 3x the total loan books’ average. Based on our conversations with banks, our (very) rough estimate is that the new caps would reduce pre-tax profit by about 6-10%, using 2019 as a base. However, we do expect banks to try and mitigate the impact, by lowering deposit rates, reducing reward benefits for non-revolvers, increasing other credit card fees, etc.

Slippery slope – a precedent for more rate/fee regulation

Beyond the near-term profit impact, Circular 1098 sets a major precedent. Before this amendment, the Manual of Regulations for Bank (MORB) read, “The rate of interest, including commissions, premiums, fees and other charges… shall not be subject to any regulatory ceiling” (emphasis ours). The Circular inserted the exemption for credit cards. Some banks expressed hope that the cap is temporary, pointing to the provision calling for a review every 6 months. However, we believe that while the rate/fees might be reviewed, the power to set the limits will stay. Other banks feel – as we do – that the precedent opens the door to more regulation of rates/fees, particularly for consumer / MSME / microfinance products

 

 

Corp News

First Gen receives permit to build LNG import Terminal

First Gen received the necessary permit from the Department of Energy regarding its Interim Offshore LNG Terminal located in Batangas City. The project will begin construction in 4Q20 and could be completed by 3Q22.

GCash, PayMaya to start charging for bank transfers

Beginning October 1, mobile wallet GCash will start charging users P15 per bank transfer done through the app. To avoid paying transaction fees, GCash users are advised to link their BPI or UnionBank accounts to their GCash app. The transfers thru these banks will remain free of charge. Additionally, digital financial service firm PayMaya also announced that users would be charged P10 for bank transfer via PayMaya app effective October 1. However, PayMaya to PayMaya transactions will remain free of charge. Sending money to Smart Money would incur a 1.5% fee.

Shell’s Malampaya stake draws interest from MVP Group, SMC

Shell Group’s stake in Malampaya gas-to-power project has drawn the interest of the MVP Group. “The MVP Group is looking at it as part of its long-term vision”, said a source. Forum Energy PLC, a unit of MVP-led Philex Petroleum holds the license for Service Contract 72, an area southwest of the Malampaya gas field. MVP previously said that if there is indeed gas in SC72, the group can build a pipeline and connect it to the Malampaya facility. San Miguel Corporation (SMC PM) disclosed last week that it is interested in acquiring Shell’s stake in the Malampaya project. Also last week, Shell Philippine Exploration BV said it is planning to sell its 45% stake in the Malampaya project. Service contract for Malampaya is scheduled to expire in 2024.

IMI subsidiary VIA Optronics lists on New York Stock Exchange

IMI (IMI PM) subsidiary VIA Optronics priced its IPO of 6.25mn American Depositary Shares (ADSs) at US$15/share. The 6.25mn ADSs are equivalent to 1.25mn ordinary shares which listed on the New York Stock Exchange on September 25 with a ticker code VIAO. The price was based on the closing price of US$10.18/share. The value of IMI’s stake in VIAO is US$126mn, equivalent to 45% of IMI’s current market cap of P13.7bn (US$282mn). IMI purchased its stake in VIAO for EUR47mn in 2016. VIAO is a supplier of enhanced display solutions.

Fire breaks out at JG Summit’s petrochemical complex in Batangas

JG Summit (JGS PM) disclosed that an isolated fire incident broke out the evening of Sep 26 at the JG Summit Petrochemical Complex in Batangas City. The first alarm was raised at 9:05pm and was declared fire out at 11:55pm. The incident occurred at a containment basin near pier and did not spread to manufacturing sites. It was said that five workers were reported injured. The naphtha cracker operations and project construction have been halted while polymer plants/projects continue. JG Summit Petrochem accounts for about 13% of market-based NAV.

Dennis Uy seeks Malampaya takeover with Shell’s 45% stake

Dennis Uy’s Udenna Group expressed interest to acquire Shell’s 45% stake in Malampaya and take over the field. Uy’s UC Malampaya Philippines already holds 45% of the project while 10% belongs to PNOC. The Group reiterated its preemptive and consent rights after San Miguel Corp and MVP Group expressed interest in Shell’s 45% stake.

DTI’s Lopez backs foreign ownership limits for small construction firms

Trade Secretary Ramon M. Lopez is seeking to retain restrictions on foreign ownership for smaller businesses in the construction sector. Contracting businesses with capital of less than P100mn should be reserved for Filipino ownership. The reasons cited difficulties in traceability of foreign contractors if there are problems encountered. House Bill (HB) 7337 is seeking to amend Republic Act (RA) 4556 or the Contractors’ License Law.HB 7337 will allow other nationalities to own >60% of construction companies. Currently, RA 4556 limits construction business ownership to be at least 60%-owned by Filipinos. HB 7337 is currently pending with the House Committee on Trade and Industry since Aug 11, 2020.

Senate approves further easing of foreign ownership limits for retail

The Senate Committee on Trade, Commerce and Entrepreneurship approved Senate Bill (SB) 1840, which seeks to amend the Retail Trade Liberalization Act (RTLA) of 2000. Under SB 1840, the minimum paid-up capital for foreign retail investors will be lowered to USD300k, while the initial investment per store will be USD150k. The RTLA currently allows foreign enterprises with a minimum paid-up capital of USD7.5mn to invest a minimum of USD830k/store. Meanwhile, enterprises with paid-up capital of USD2.5-7.5mn may be wholly owned by foreigners after the first two years. SB 1840 also removes other qualification requirements, such as the USD250k paid-up capital per store for luxury retail. The minimum paid-up capital requirement will also be reviewed every 5 years. The House version of the Bill (HB 59) was passed in early March – it proposes to reduce the minimum paid-up capital requirement to USD 200k.

Megawide targets P8bn from new preferred shares

Megawide will issue P3bn new preferred shares, which will have an oversubscription of up to P5bn. The fund raising targeted to be completed by November. The proceeds from the offering will be used for expansion plans, particularly the following

-The 1.7-hectare Phase 2 of its transport terminal Parañaque Integrated Terminal Exchange (P5bn).

-The initial design stages of its multi-use development at the Mactan Cebu International Airport (MCIA)

-The expansion of its capacity for pre-cast concrete (P2bn)

-The Carbon market project in Cebu (P3bn)

-The NAIA airport rehab (P100bn)

As at end-June, MWIDE’s parent-level debt (including prefs) stood at P21.86bn and P2.17bn in cash and its net debt-to-equity at 2.76x. MWIDE currently pays 7.026% on its existing P4bn prefs, which has a step-up in 2021 (15-year PDST-R2 rate + 4.875%). Our estimates assume a new issuance of P5bn with a div rate of 5% by 4Q20. MWIDE is trading at 17x FY21E EV/EBITDA, in line with historical average; construction (8x) and airport peers (21x).

DoubleDragon in advanced preparations for REIT listing

DoubleDragon said that it was in “advanced preparations” for its first REIT listing for the 248,000sqm of office buildings in DD Meridian Park in the Manila Bay area. Note that DD Meridian Park is not PEZA-accredited which could be a limiting factor for future BPO tenants. The company is targeting a market cap of P51bn and total proceeds of P17bn from the REIT listing which implies 33% public float. Additioanlly, DoubleDragon plans to spin off 200,000sqm of leasing assets every year until 2025 at 6% cap rate. For comparison, AREIT has an office portfolio of 170,000sqm and a market capitalization of P26.5bn.

POGOs vacate more office space in 3Q20

Colliers reported that POGOs vacated 123,000sqm of Metro Manila office space YTD. Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) said that POGOs vacated 103,000sqm YTD. This implies that POGOs vacated 55,000sqm in 3Q20 alone. PAGCOR previously reported that 10 out of 60 POGOs have shut down and only 32 of the remaining 50 POGOs have resumed operations. We believe the main reasons for shutting down (not resuming operations) are the following: (1) Travel bans – some Chinese employees left the Philippines and cannot return. (2) Cap on operations – POGOs are only permitted to operate at a maximum of 30% of capacity. (3) Higher taxes – POGOs are now required to pay 7% of GGR as taxes/fees, from 2% previously. (4) LPC expects more POGO closures in 4Q20 since several POGOs are not operating. In 2019, we estimate that the POGO industry (including service providers) occupied about 1.3m sqm of office space in Metro Manila and likely employed 300,000-400,000 mainland Chinese. POGOs occupy about 11% of Metro Manila office stock and 60% of Manila Bay area office stock. About 70% of the office space occupied by POGOs is not PEZA accredited and would generally not be used by BPOs. POGOs occupy about 25% of FLI’s office portfolio and 15% of MEG’s office portfolio.

MPI P5bn buyback resumes

MPI is launching a P5bn share buyback on October 2 until the allotted amount is fully utilized. In February, the company also started a P5bn share buyback program, which ran for three months. And it was able to buy a cumulative 213.48mn shares worth P703mn as of March 20.

PLDT increases fiber coverage

PLDT says it increased the coverage of its fiber network to 8.1m homes passed as of Aug 2020. The figure was 7.8m as of 1H20 and 7.2m as of Dec 2019. Meanwhile, number of ports for fiber connections is also now at 3.78m (vs. 3.67m in June 2020 and 3.5m in Dec 2019). It also reported that it had 226 5G base stations already.

Meralco issues invitation for power supply bids

Meralco issued bid terms for the competitive selection process to supply 1,800MW of capacity 1,200MW by Dec 2024 and 600MW by May 2025. The contract term will be for 20 years and bids must be for a minimum of 150MW capacity. Unlike in the previous terms, there is no explicit requirement that the plant be a new facility when it begins supplying power. However, the terms do specify that the supplying plants must not be in operation before Jan 2020. There are at least two facilities under construction / expansion today that have yet to be operational. Depending on who wins the bid, it may spur new capacity building in a grid with no meaningful capacity is being built after Aboitiz Power’s (AP PM) Dinginin. The deadline for the submission of bids is Jan 25, 2021.

 

 

 

 

Macro News

Government eyes Pagcor, PCSO privatization to pay debt

The Department of Finance will push through with the plan to privatize commercial functions of the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (Pagcor) and Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSCO) to raise additional revenues. Sec. Dominguez said that it is prepared to work on the privatization of gambling activities, in response to Senator Drilon’s proposal on privatization. Last year, Sec. Dominguez said privatizing Pagcor would generate at least P220bn a year while transferring small-town lottery to private operators would raise P40-50bn a year. There are roughly 30 Pagcor-operated casinos under the Casino Filipino brand.

Overall BOP posts US$657mn surplus in Aug

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$657mn in August. This reflected mainly the inflows from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad. This is up from the BOP surplus of US$493mn in the same period last year and US$8mn in July 2020. This brings 8M20 BOP surplus to US$4.77bn, lower than the US$5.53bn recorded in the same period last year. The surplus was due to higher foreign borrowings by the National Government and lower net deficit in merchandise trade.

Gov’t extends GCQ status for NCR until Oct 31

Metro Manila will stay under general community quarantine (GCQ) from Oct. 1 to 31. Meanwhile, other areas under GCQ status are Batangas, Tacloban City, Bacolod City, Iligan City and Iloilo City. Additionally, Lanao del Sur, including Marawi City, will remain under enhanced community quarantine, a stricter measure.

Full operation of more sectors, businesses pressed to spur economy

During a Senate hearing, Trade Secretary Ramon Lopez appealed to allow more businesses to open at full capacity even under GCQ. He was proposing to allow sectors under Category 3 to operate at 100% of capacity from 50% today. The  sectors under this category include legal and accounting, management and consultancy, architecture and engineering, science and research, advertising and marketing, publishing, film, music, as well as production. Around 90k businesses, mostly MSMEs, remain closed. This accounts for c. 6% of total registered businesses in the country.

Gov’t seeks P540B advance from BSP

The National Government is asking the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) for P540bn in additional short-term financial assistance. The funds will be settled on or before Dec 29 at zero interest. The debt plan comes immediately after the Bureau of the Treasury repaid the P300bn it borrowed from the BSP in March through a repurchase agreement of government securities.

BSP sees September inflation rate at 1.8-2.6%

The BSP expects inflation to settle within 1.8-2.6% range for September 2020. They cited lower rice and oil prices as well as Meralco power rates, along with the appreciation of the peso. The inflation rate stood at 2.4% for August 2020.

BSP keeps interest rates steady

The Monetary Board kept the overnight reverse repurchase rate steady at a record low of 2.25% within street expectations. Additionally, the BSP lowered ave inflation rate forecasts to 2.3% for FY20E (vs 2.6% previously), 2.8% for FY21E (vs 3%), and 3% for FY22E (vs. 3.1%).

SEC wants cap on salary loan interest

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) wants to put a limit on interest and penalty charges that lending companies charge for salary and payday loans. The SEC has been lobbying for the payday loan limit since October last year in a bid to stop “predatory” lending practices. “We are hopeful that the Monetary Board will likewise consider soon the Commission’s proposal for similar interest rates, fees and other charges imposed by lending and financing companies on consumer and payday loans”, said SEC Chairperson Emilio Aquino. He cited the recent cap on credit card charges as timely and much-needed to promote responsible lending.
