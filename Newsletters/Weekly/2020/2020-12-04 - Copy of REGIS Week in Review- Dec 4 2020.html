Corp News

Philippine Retailers Association, market research firms sees lower holiday spending this year

The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) expects holiday spending to be subdued. The December sales will likely generate the highest monthly sales, but shopping activity will be limited and will shift online. Meanwhile, Kantar expects holiday spending this year to be 20% lower vs previous years and according to Nielsen, Christmas holiday sales from 2017-2019 amounted to P97bn. Sales this year will be driven by FMCGs, as consumers celebrate the holidays at home.

PCC clears ACEN, Marubeni JV

AC Energy Philippines Inc (ACEN) said that it received a copy of the PCC decision allowing the proposed transaction among Axia Power, ACE PH, Endevor and Ingrid Power. Axia Power Holdings Philippines is a subsidiary of Marubeni Corp. The companies signed a shareholders agreement last July for the development, construction and operation of the 150MW diesel power plant project in Pililla, Rizal. The project is expected to be operational by 1Q21 and under agreement, Axia will acquire 50% of shares and 50% of economic rights in ACEN’s subsidiary Ingrid Power Holdings. ACEN will hold 50% of shares and 45% of economic rights while Endevor, a wholly-owned subsidiary of ACEN, will have a 5% share in the economic rights.

Moody’s downgrades RCBC, PNB outlook to negative

Moody’s Investors Service has downgraded its outlook on Rizal Commercial Banking Corp (RCB) and Philippine National Bank (PNB) to negative due to heightened asset risks from the pandemic. The change in outlook reflects the risks to asset quality especially in retail and SME segments. Both RCB and PNB have a baseline credit assessment and adjusted BCA of baa3 from Moody’s. The two have a long-term counterparty risk assessment of Baa2 (cr), long-term counterparty risk ratings of Baa2, senior unsecured medium-term note (MTN) program rating of (P)Baa2, short-term deposit and short-term counterparty risk rating of p\P-2, and short-term counterparty risk assessment of P-2(cr).

First Philippine Holdings to conduct tender offer for Lopez Holdings

First Philippine Holdings (FPH PM) will make a tender offer for 20% to 45.6% of its parent, Lopez Holdings (LPZ PM) at P3.85/share. This represents 25% premium over last closing price and 41% premium over 3-month VWAP. The tender represents 908mn to 2.07bn LPZ shares worth P3.5bn to P8.0bn. The ultimate parent company, Lopez Inc, which owns 54.4% of Lopez Holdings, will not tender any of its shares. Tender offer period – 22 January 2021 to 19 February 2021. If the tender offer is successful, LPZ will be delisted from the Philippine Stock Exchange. We estimate that LPZ has a market-based NAV of P5.30/share. Before the transaction, we estimate that FPH’s market-based NAV is P169/share.

PPA estimates port traffic 6-10% below normal levels

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) estimates port traffic to have declined at between 6% and 10% over the course of the pandemic, though it expects an uptick for the yearend holidays according to PPA General Manager Jay Daniel Santiago. The estimate refers to both passenger volume and cargo throughput. In 9M20, container volumes at the key ports in Luzon: MICT, South Harbour, and Batangas fell by 17%, 22% and 18%, respectively. MICT is wholly owned by ICTSI (ICT PM) and accounts for 52% of container throughput in the Philippines. MICT accounts for 35% of ICT’s group-wide revenues and ~50% of profit. ICTSI is trading 9.7x FY21E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers (14x) and historical levels (12x).

Semirara halts mining operations in Molave pit

Semirara has voluntarily deferred mining operations in North Block 7 (NB7), one of four mining blocks in Molave Mine. The deferral is due to water build-up in the sump of NB7, which was caused by water seepages and aggravated by heavy rainfall from the recent typhoons. Coal shipments will not be affected, given that it has 3.4mn MT of coal inventory as of Dec 1.  However, the company also flagged that it may not be able to meet the coal quality specifications of a “few” customers. Semirara sold 8.45mn MT in 9M20, accounting for 84% of our FY20E (10.1mn MT). In 2021E, our estimates assume coal sales volumes of 15mn MT. Every 10% reduction in coal sales volumes reduces FY21E profit forecast by 12%.

Emperador sees further expansion of brandy biz in US

In a press release, Emperador (EMP) said that that volumes of Don Pedro and Presidente brandy products in the US have been growing at “double digits” amid the pandemic. Both brands are part of the Case Pedro Domecq portfolio in Mexico owned by Grupo Emperador Spain. They also saw sales of Fundador in the US grow by “around 23%” from January to September.

BDO expects banking industry NPL to peak in 2Q21

BDO President and CEO Nestor Tan expects the banking industry’s nonperforming loans to peak in 2Q21. This assumes the availability of a COVID vaccine and an economic rebound. The banking sector’s NPL ratio increased to 3.5% in September 2020 (from 2.93% in August 2020).

Megawide raises P4.36bn in pref share offering

Megawide generated P4.36bn in fresh funds from its offering of preferred shares. The offering of Series 2A and Series 2B preferred shares offering was nearly 1.5 times oversubscribed from the base offer of P3bn. The proceeds will be used to fund not only for the rehab of NAIA, but also the development of the Paranaque Integrated Terminal Exchange (PITX) and expansion of its construction capacity. As at end-Sep, MWIDE’s consolidated net debt (including prefs) stood at P50.8bn, of which P23bn was at the parent. The consolidated net debt-to-equity (treating prefs as debt) of the  company is at 4.17x while the parent level net debt-to-equity (with prefs as debt) at 3.22x.

Cignal breached the three million subscriber count and EBITDA is also “strong”

According to PLDT chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan, Cignal is a major recipient of the demise of ABS-CBN’s Sky Direct, thus the growth in subscribers. Sky Direct, the direct-to-home satellite service of Sky Cable with 1.5 million subscribers, was ordered by the National Telecommunications Commission to cease and desist operations last June 30. Cignal is owned and operated by Cignal TV Inc., a subsidiary of the PLDT Group’s media and content arm MediaQuest Holdings. Cignal subscribers hit 3 million.

Manila Water consortium secures management contract in Saudi Arabia

The consortium of Saur SAS, Miahona Company, and Manila Water (MWC PM) has signed a Management, Operation and Maintenance Contract (“MOMC”) with the National Water Company (“NWC”), Kingdom of Saudi Arabia for the latter’s North West Cluster. The MOMC will comprise the management, operations and maintenance of the water and wastewater facilities of the North West Cluster (Madinah and Tabuk) over a 7-year contract period. This will entail the implementation of Enabling Projects and deployment of Key Personnel to manage the Cluster and achieve the Key Performance Indicators target set by the NWC.

 
 

Macro News

Gov’t retains GCQ status in Metro Manila, other areas

President Rodrigo Duterte has extended anew the implementation of general community quarantine (GCQ) in Metro Manila for the entire month of December. Other areas under GCQ include Batangas, Iloilo City, Tacloban City, Lanao del Sur, Iligan, Davao City. Metro Manila mayors have also agreed to adjust curfew hours to midnight to 3 a.m. to allow pre-dawn masses ahead of Christmas.

Philippines launches second USD bond issue

The Philippine gov’t launched another dollar bond offering. The offering of 10.5-year and 25-year US dollar-denominated bonds would carry a yield of around 100bps above the 10-year US Treasury benchmark. The proceeds will be used to support the budget. In April, the government raised US$2.35 bn from its dollar-denominated bond sale. The 2021 budget of P4.5tn (+10% YoY) is currently pending bicam approval. The gov’t is targeting a budget deficit of P1.7tn (8.5% of GDP) in 2021, lower than the budget deficit target of P1.8tn in 2020 (9.6% of GDP).

Duterte orders speedy vaccine procurement

President Duterte has signed Executive Order 121, granting the emergency approval (called “emergency use authorization” or EUA) on COVID-19 medicines and vaccines. The EUA will be issued and remain valid if there is evidence that the drug or vaccine is effective in diagnosing, treating, or preventing the coronavirus. The Gov’t is targeting the arrival of the vaccine by 1Q21 for clinical trials, according to National Task Force against COVID-19 chief implementer Carlito Galvez Jr. The 1Q21 target will involve vaccines from China’s Sinovac Biotech Ltd. and Russia’s Gamaleya. Negotiations are currently ongoing with a base case target of May 2021 and the worst scenario is that the vaccine might be available by 2022. The government is prioritizing its COVID-19 vaccine purchase from four firms: Sinovac, Gamaleya, AstraZeneca plc, and Pfizer, Inc. The government is also holding talks with other foreign firms, including four US vaccine developers and targeting to have at least 10 to 15 or even 20mn vaccine (doses from) each of these vaccine makers.

Unemployment rate drops to 8.7% in October

Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority showed that the unemployment rate dropped further to 8.7% in October 2020 from 10% in July and 17.6% in April but remained higher than the 4.6% unemployment rate in the same period last year. It is worth noting that while the absolute number of unemployed dropped by 758k QoQ, the number of employed dropped more by 1.47mn. Thus, the QoQ drop in the unemployment rate appears to have been partly due to a lower labor force participation rate, which stood at only 58.7% in October (vs. 61.9% in July and 55.7% in April). Employment in the services sector rose slightly QoQ, buoyed by a 48% jump in communications (1% of total employment). However, this was more than offset by decreases in agri (-14%, 21% of total), manufacturing (-10%, 8% of total), and trade (-6%, 21% of total). Meanwhile, employment in the construction sector (10% of total) shrunk 4.9% YoY and 1.5% QoQ. 

DBCC sees deeper economic slump

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) once again cut its FY20 GDP estimate to -8.5% to -9.5%. This is a deeper contraction than the July 28 estimate of -4.4 to -6.6%. They cited the prolonged imposition of community quarantines in various regions in the country, as well as recent typhoons. The government kept its growth forecast for FY21 at 6.5% - 7.5% and raised FY22E to 8-10%.

Gov't looking to extend validity of Bayanihan 2, 2020 budget

In a Thursday briefing, Finance Secretary Carlos Domninguez III said that the unspent portions of the said fiscal measures, together with the proposed 2021 budget are vital to the economic rebound next year. Dominguez added that have another stimulus package may not be an option

In November, several lawmakers filed a version of Bayanihan 3 for additional assistance amid the Covid pandemic, with Bayanihan 2 set expire in less than a month.

Loan growth slows further to 1.9% in October

Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, eased to 1.9% in October from 2.6% in September (revised). On a month-on-month seasonally-adjusted basis, outstanding universal and commercial bank loans, net of RRPs, declined by 0.4%. Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-3.6%), wholesale and retail trade and repair of motor vehicles and motorcycles  (-4.3%) continued to contract. Consumer loans (credit cards and auto) expanded at a slower pace of 8.0% in October from 9.8% in September. Meanwhile, money supply grew by 11.8% in October, mildly slower than the 12.2% growth in September (revised).
