REGIS REPORT

25 September 2020

The Philippines lost 1.2% this week to close at 5838.66.  Market activity was listless as investors were largely sidelined by a dearth of market catalysts.

Metro Manila awaits possible changes to lockdown restrictions next week. New infections and hospital occupancy levels  seem to show some stability which could provide the government justification for further loosening of restrictions.  Prospects of re-opening boosted restaurant Jollibee (+6.0% WoW) as well as casino operator Bloomberry (+4.9% WoW). 

However, losers overwhelmed gainers. Twenty-two of the 30 index names ended the week with losses.

Megaworld (-6.7% WoW) was the top decliner.  Megaworld as well as Filinvest Land (-1.1% WoW) are the most directly exposed to POGOs – a segment which has been seeing stress.  According to regulator PAGCOR, POGO fees are down by 50%.  If not for a minimum guaranteed fee, POGO fees would have been lower. Prospects for POGOs have been dampened by the Chinese clampdown on illegal gambling, recently increased POGO fees, and travel bans. Of the 60 POGO licenses, 5 have already been canceled while 5 have been suspended. And of the remaining 50, only 32 are currently operating. PAGCOR also said that of the 218 service providers, only 111 are operating, while 42 have asked for their accreditations to be canceled.

POGOs account for roughly 11% of Metro Manila office space and mass closures of POGOs would have huge negative consequences beyond just office rents.

Concern also turned to the banks.  Security Bank (-3.4% WoW9, BPI (-3.05% WoW), and BDO (-2.25% WoW) were all decliners this week.

The BSP approved an annual interest rate cap of 24% on credit card transactions in an effort to ease the financial burden from the pandemic. It is unclear at this stage if this is the same as 2% a month, as some news articles suggest (compounds to 27%pa). Currently, credit card companies charge about 3.25% month, which compounds to about 47% annually. It also set caps on rates for installment loans and processing fees.

Of the banks under Regis estimates, credit card receivables account for 3.4% to 5.8% of total loans.  East West’s (-0.5% WoW) credit card receivables are at 13.5% of total loans (2019A).

In the meantime, consumer loan NPLs jumped 109bps QoQ to 5.5% in 2Q20. Consumer loans accounted for c85% of new NPLs in 2Q, with the deterioration even more severe in mortgages (+200bps QoQ) and autos (+87bps QoQ).

Meralco  (-1.0% WoW) has been ordered to refund some P200m worth of charges to its lowest-usage (poorest) customers. The fines stem from customer confusion and “bill shock”. Meralco will appeal the fine.

Trading was lethargic, with average daily volumes hitting only US$86m daily. Against the anemic volumes, foreign selling did not let up, with net outflows reaching a headline figure of US$46m (US$63m adjusted for SM Group blocks).  

Foreigners accounted for only 35% of market activity. Local retail was active, with small cap telco plays Now Corp (+22.6% WoW) and DITO (+11.7% WoW) heavily traded.  Now Corp’s affiliate Now Telecom aims to be the country’s “4th telco”. Its franchise was recently extended, and it was given provisional authority to operate a mobile network. Now Corp plans to list Now Telecom separately.

Philippine Banks - Consumer NPLs surge in 2Q; Mortgage NPLs +200bps qoq; Auto NPLs +87bps
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Covid crisis puts stress on households

Consumer NPLs surged 109bps qoq in 2Q20 to 5.55%, much faster than the 30bps qoq increase in total NPLs, suggesting that households have been hit harder by the Covid-19 lockdowns. Counter-intuitively, the qoq deterioration was most severe in mortgages (+200bps) and auto loans (+87bps), compared to credit card (+42bps) or personal loans (-18bps). Has Covid-19 done permanent damage to household balance sheets, or has the gradual reopening relieved the pressure on incomes?

Consumer NPLs jump, accounting for c85% of all new NPLs in 2Q

Households seem to be bearing the brunt of the Covid-19 crisis. Consumer NPLs jumped 109bps qoq and 148bps ytd to 5.55%, much faster than the increase in total bank NPLs, which grew just 30bps qoq and 52bps ytd to 2.60% in 2Q20 (see Figures 1 and 2). In nominal terms, consumers NPLs increased by P21.4bn from P90bn in 1Q to P111bn in 2Q. Meanwhile, total bank NPLs grew by just P25.2bn qoq, suggesting consumers accounted for c85% of new NPLs in 2Q20.

Increase despite grace period – difference in banks’ NPL recognition policies?

The 2Q increase in NPLs was unexpected, given the grace period from 16 March to 31 May. The discrepancy could be due to differences in NPL recognition. For example, BDO’s (BDO PM) 2Q NPLs jumped 65bps qoq, as its new NPLs included loans that were already past due before the grace period. Most other banks stopped the clock on the 90-day countdown from PD to NPL during the grace period. We expect other banks’ NPLs to catch up in 3Q.

Mortgage NPLs surprisingly surge 200bps qoq, despite loan grace period

Mortgage NPL ratio spiked from 3.47% in 1Q to 5.47% in 2Q (Figure 3), and accounted for c80% of new consumer NPLs in 2Q. The rapid deterioration is surprising to us, as housing loans are generally considered the least risky among consumer accounts, and the last on which a family might default.

Vehicle NPLs up 82bps qoq; Credit cards, salary loans more benign

NPLs for motor vehicle loans increased 82bps qoq to 5.35% in 2Q (Figure 4), with delinquencies coming nearly entirely from auto (4-wheel), as motorcycle NPL ratio improved marginally to 7.68% (was 7.84% in 1Q). In contrast, NPLs for unsecured loans were unexpectedly benign. Credit card NPLs increased 42bps qoq to 5.73%, while personal loan NPLs actually improved 18bps qoq to 5.86%.

Damage done? Or is the worst over?

It is unclear whether the 2Q data reflects early and more permanent damage to household balance sheets. Banking industry NPLs, for example, worsened further from 2.60% in June to 2.75% in July. To the upside, the official unemployment rate fell from 17.7% in April to 10.0% in July. Also, OFW remittances rebounded sharply in June and July (up c8% yoy each), from the 19% yoy contraction in May. Trouble is that consumer loan data is released quarterly; the 3Q numbers will be out in mid-December.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Property - BPOs continue to expand headcount
 
 
by Carl Sy +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Meeting with IT-BPO Association

We met with the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) to get an update on the sector. Attorney Luis Salvador (Executive Director for External Affairs and Investor Relations) and Carole Gaffud (Senior Research Manager) shared some of their insights.

Contact centers continue to hire new workers

Findings from an August 2020 survey by the Contact Center Association of the Philippines (CCAP), whose members account for c. 70% (or 900,000) of Philippine BPO employment, were generally upbeat. 86% of respondents said that they have hired workers since mid-March (when the first hard lockdown began) and intend to increase headcount further by the end of the year. Earlier this month, Concentrix, one of the largest BPOs in the Philippines, partnered with the Department of Tourism for a virtual job fair. The company is looking to hire up to 10,000 displaced tourism workers over the next two months. While some of the positions are only for two to six months, some are for permanent employment.

But few plan to expand their offices

Despite an increase in headcount, most BPOs do not plan to expand their offices in the near term. They would rather increase the number of employees working from home than grow their office footprint. While there are challenges in working from home, there are also higher costs of operating from the office (such as shuttle service for employees, disinfection, etc.) Nevertheless, we previously noted incremental Metro Manila office take-up from the BPO sector in 2Q20. In addition, Atty. Salvador and Ms. Gaffud said that a few BPOs have expanded in Tier 2 cities/provinces, such as in Clark, Bacolod, Iloilo, Davao, and Palawan. About 350,000 of the country’s 1.3m BPO employees are currently in provincial areas.

 

Corporate tax reform to increase costs

The proposed reform of corporate taxes, which is being deliberated in the Senate, would reduce fiscal incentives to the BPO sector (and thus increase costs). IBPAP estimates the proposed changes to the corporate tax code would make BPO operations in the Philippines c. 20% more expensive than those in India (compared to a 10%-15% discrepancy currently). While this could curtail future BPO expansion in the Philippines, they believe the tax changes are unlikely to lead to departures from the country.

Megaworld is the most exposed to the BPO sector

We consider Megaworld to be the most exposed to the BPO sector, through its office leasing division, which accounts for c. 37% of (2019) EBIT and 52% of NAV. MEG is currently trading at 5.5X FY21E P/E, 0.5X FY21E P/B, and 64% discount to NAV.

Corp News

Meralco appeals ERC order on retail consumer subsidy

Meralco is seeking a partial motion for reconsideration on recent order from the regulator, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC). The ERC directed Meralco not to charge lifeline customers for distribution, supply and metering for one month and also imposed a P19m fine. The order was a result of complaints regarding the billing issues of Meralco during the lockdown (e.g. “over-billing”, delayed billing, etc…). Meralco estimates that order could cost it over P200m. The company says it will comply with the order but seek reconsideration from the ERC.

AGI renews buy-back program for another 12 months

The board of Alliance Global Group (AGI PM) authorized a new buy-back program for another 12 months beginning September 24, 2020. The new program authorizes the purchase of up to P2.5bn worth of common shares, equivalent to 3.6% of the current market capitalization. The company has bought back 529mn common shares since 2017, roughly 5.2% of outstanding shares pre-buyback.

Small telco plans listing to fund expansion

Now Corp (NOW PM) is planning to list affiliate Now Telecom through listing by introduction. This will likely be through a spin-off and distribution of Now Telecom shares to the public. Now Telecom recently received an extension to its franchise and its provisional authority to operate a mobile telecom network. The company has been in existence for decades and a niche service (fixed wireless broadband) but has not been able to make meaningful inroads.

Green groups seek halt of DMCI’s coal-fired power generator project

Environmental groups and some community leaders in Narra town filed a complaint against DMCI Power Corp. for its 15 MW coal-fired power plant project. The complaint was filed with the Regional Trial Court in Puerto Princesa. DMCI Power Corp., a wholly-owned unit of DMCI Hldgs. (DMC PM), secured an ECC for the project last year. Our estimates and valuation do not include the 15MW project. DMCI Power accounts for c. 5% of profit and NAV while DMCI is trading at a 39% discount to its market-based NAV of P6.60/sh.

WLCON targets 63 stores by end-2020

In a media briefing, CEO Lorraine Belo-Cincochan said WLCON is slated to open 2 more new stores before the end of the year, bringing total stores to 63 (Regis estimate: 61 stores). This is down from the original target of 65 due to construction delays resulting from the lockdown. WLCON expects to open 9 new stores in 2021, including the 2 stores originally planned for 2020 (Regis estimate: 6 stores). COO Rosemarie Bosch Ong indicated that 3Q performance is “encouraging”, citing pent up demand from its customers.

SEC approves PH Resorts’ follow-on offer

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) approved the application of PH Resorts Group Holdings for a P1.125bn follow-on offering. Basing on previous disclosures, the offer will involve up to 300mn common shares (plus 150mn over-allotment) at a price range of P1-P2.50. The follow-on offer will raise money for the construction of The Emerald in Mactan, Cebu worth roughly US$300mn with a target opening in 2022. PH Resorts also has a license to build a casino hotel in Clark.

Bulacan airport’s 50-year franchise clears Senate panel

Senate bill 1823, which grants a 50-year franchise to the San Miguel Aerocity, Inc, hurdled the Senate Committee on Public Services according to Senator Grace S. Poe-Llamanzares. The Bill will be endorsed for plenary deliberations, subject to the submission of required documents from the concerned agencies. During the hearing, Senators Sherwin T. Gatchalian, Richard J. Gordon, Juan Miguel F. Zubiri, and Emmanuel Joel J. Villanueva expressed their support for the franchise. If enacted, the Senate bill will grant a 50-year franchise, inclusive of a 10-year maximum period for the design, planning and construction of the airport and airport city. It will also exempt the grantee from direct and indirect taxes during the 10-year construction period. Last Sep 7, the Lower House approved House Bill No. 7507, which grants San Miguel Aerocity a 50-year franchise for the Bulacan airport.

Shell plans sale of Malampaya stake

Shell Philippines Exploration (SPEX) is planning to exit from its stake in Malampaya. SPEX owns 45% of and is the operator of Service Contract (SC) 38, which holds the Malampaya natural gas field in the West Philippine Sea. SPEX is part of the Shell group of companies worldwide. The divestment is supposed to be part of plans to rationalize the group’s assets. The sale comes at a curious time as the consortium holding Malampaya sought a 15-year extension to its contract only a few months ago. The service contract for Malampaya is scheduled to expire in 2024. Malampaya supplies natural gas for over 3,000MW of capacity in Luzon, including 2,000MW operated by First Gen (FGEN PM). Udenna, the holding company of Davao-based Dennis Uy, just bought the 45% interest in Malampaya held by Chevron. The purchase price for the 45% held by Chevron was reportedly US$565m in 4Q19, before oil prices crashed. Udenna reportedly has plans to explore and develop the area with a Chinese partner.

29 POGOs permitted to resume operations

PAGCOR reported that there were 55 registered POGOs as of September 8, from 60 as of end-2019. Around 29 of the POGOs are authorized to resume operations. Among the requirements to resume operations are the payment of 5% franchise taxes and income taxes of employees. In 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.3m sqm of office space in Metro Manila and likely employed 300,000-400,000 mainland Chinese.

DNL raises 2H earnings outlook to more than P1bn

In a media briefing, D&L Industries President Alvin Lao said that its 2H bottom line should more than P1bn based on the steady growth momentum it is observing. DNL previously issued 2H guidance of P900-P1bn compared to 1H income of P802mn (-43%yoy). The previous guidance implied earnings decline of as much as 39%yoy, worse than our forecast of a -17% decline

DNL is trading at 15.7x on our 2021 estimates (~95% of 2019).

Security Bank receives approval for sale of 50% of subsidiary

Security Bank has received approval from the Philippine Competition Commission for the sale of 50% of SB Finance Company to Thailand’s Bank of Ayudhya Public Company limited (also called Krungsi).

PGOLD to raise up to P12bn in corporate notes

PGOLD plans to raise as much as P12bn in corporate notes. “An opportunistic strategy to raise long term money at low interest rates” was mentioned. Proceeds will be used to accelerate expansion of Puregold and S&R. As of 1H20, PGOLD had P17bn cash and no interest bearing debt.

 

 

Macro News

BSP may remain accommodative for at least two years

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) may maintain the low interest rate environment in the next two years to provide support to the economy according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. The BSP has taken a deliberate pause in order to gauge how the market took in the estimated P1.4tn liquidity support (~7.3% of GDP). The BSP kept policy rates unchanged last Aug 20 and the BSP’s next policy meeting is set on Oct 1.

T-bill rates fetch higher rates

The Bureau of Treasury made a full award of P20bn worth of Treasury bills against total tenders of P72.9bn. The 91-day T-bill fetched a yield of 1.156%, up by 0.6bps (BVAL: 1.224%), the 182-day T-bill fetched a yield of 1.615%, up by 2.6bps (BVAL: 1.531% while the 364-day T-bill fetched a yield of 1.85%, up by 4.3bps (BVAL: 1.838%). Rates inched up amid expectations that the BSP would keep policy rates steady for the remainder of the year according to National Treasurer Rosalia de Leon.

COVID-19 update

In about a week, the IATF and the President will have to make a decision to either maintain the current GCQ or ease further into M-GCQ. The Covid stats and hospital data look encouraging after the spike in cases/deaths during the return to GCQ in late August, the case count has not worsened. Perhaps more crucially, hospital bed occupancy for Covid cases continues to fall.

Philippine banks implicated in FinCEN Files

A (rather long) piece by the Philippine Center for Investigative Journalism (PCIJ) examining the FinCEN files -- https://pcij.org/article/4297/ph-money-remitters-flagged-in-millions-of-dollars-in-suspicious-transactions -- details how Philippine banks and remittance outfits sent “more than $1billion” worth of “suspicious” wire transfers abroad. Two remittance firms feature prominently in the report – Philrem and Werquick. The two sent money from their accounts in local banks – BDO Unibank (BDO PM), Metrobank (MBT PM), and Rizal Commercial (RCB PM). Recall, Philrem and RCBC were part of the Bangladesh heist in February 2016. These transfers were called “suspicious” as the documents did not have the names of the ultimate sender, and/or that the counterparties were “shell-like or unverifiable entities”. In defense of the banks, the Philippines is one of a very few countries in the world that still has absolute bank secrecy on deposits. Hence banks are constrained in attempts at KYCC (know your clients’ client). The PCIJ article includes comments from BDO and RCB, but MBT reportedly “was unable to participate” in the inquiry. BDO management says that the bank has “complied with AMLA rules”, referring to the Anti Money Laundering Act. It is unclear what kind of liability the listed banks might have. As mentioned, the Bank Secrecy Law does tie their hands a bit. One possible upside is that this could add momentum to pass a Senate bill filed last week that seeks to amend the secrecy ules (https://www.bworldonline.com/senate-bill-seeks-to-allow-bsp-to-look-into-deposits/).

PH records P40.1bn budget deficit in August

The National Government’s budget deficit swelled to P40.1bn in August. Revenues dropped 13% YoY to P243bn, as collections from the BIR and BOC dropped 9% and 17%, respectively. Expenditures were slightly higher (+0.4%) due to a 15% jump in interest expenses and the release of subsidies to PhilHealth. This brings YT-August budget deficit to P740.7bn, accounting for 41% of the government’s FY20E deficit target of P1.815tn (9.4% of GDP). The revenues in 8M20 reached P1.93tn, down 8% YoY and represents 77% of FY20E while the expenditures in 8M20 are up 21% YoY to P2.67tn and represents 62% of FY20E.

More POGOs closing

Finance Secretary Dominguez said that more POGOs were shutting down due to weak demand and the Chinese government’s clampdown on money transfers. We believe that travel bans preventing mainland Chinese employees from entering the Philippines are also a major reason for lower POGO operations. On September 22, a building owner in Makati informed Secretary Dominguez that POGOs and their service providers have started canceling their lease contracts. PAGCOR reported that there were 55 registered POGOs as of September 8, from 60 as of end-2019. 29 of the POGOs are authorized to resume operations while 99 out of 218 service providers are permitted to resume operations. In 2019, we estimate that the POGO industry (including service providers) occupied about 1.3m sqm of office space in Metro Manila and likely employed 300,000-400,000 mainland Chinese.

Gov’t willing to ease NAIA rehab terms

The government is open to extend the contract validity of the NAIA rehab project to 25-30 years from the previously approved 15 years according to presidential adviser for flagship programs and projects Vivencio B. Dizon. The government is also open to deferring its revenue share while the air sector tries to get back on its feet. The government is currently in discussions with the consortium of Megawide (MWIDE PM) and India-based GMR for the project. The NAIA rehab project is estimated to cost P100bn.

Gov’t awards Malolos-Clark Railway project package 1 to Megawide consortium

The government awarded package 1 of the Malolos-Clark Railway project to the consortium of Megawide. The consortium includes Megawide, Hyundai Engineering, and Dong-ah Geological Engineering Co. Package 1 was previously estimated to cost US$500mn and the consortium submitted the lowest bid last Oct 30 2019. Megawide’s share of the project will amount to P10-13bn.

BSP sets cap on credit card charges

The BSP approved an annual interest rate cap of 24% on credit card transactions to ease the financial burden brought about by the COVID pandemic. The implementation will start on November 3 but will be reviewed by the BSP every six months. The BSP also set the limit for monthly add-on rates for credit card installment loans at 1%. Additionally, credit card issuers also cannot impose other charges on credit card cash advances except for a maximum processing fee of P200/transaction. In 1H20, the annualized interest rates on credit card receivables ranged from 18% to 58% with an average of 26%.

PAGCOR online gaming revenues cut in half

Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) AVP for Offshore Gaming Licensing Jose Tria said that monthly income from POGOs is down by almost half from cP600mn/month pre-Covid. He added that the fees should have been lower if not for the minimum guaranteed fee. Meanwhile, according to Tria, only 32 out of 60 POGOs were allowed to resume operations at 30% capacity. Around 111 out 218 accredited POGO service providers were also given clearance to operate. Five POGO licenses were already canceled while another five were suspended. Additionally, 42 service providers have requested the cancelation of their accreditation.
