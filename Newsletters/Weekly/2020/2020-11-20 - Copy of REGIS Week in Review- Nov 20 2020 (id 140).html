Security Bank - 3Q NPLs at 4.0%, huge provisions for consumers; trading gains save P&L again
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Consumers feeling the strain

SECB’s 9M20 NPAT came in at P6.7bn (-13% yoy) despite massive P21bn provision expense. Profit was boosted by widening NIMs and another quarter of monster trading/fx gains in 3Q. SECB’s consumer book contracted 9% qoq, and seems under some strain. Provisions for 3Q20 alone were nearly entirely for the retail book, suggesting most of the NPL spike to 4.03% (was 1.58% in 2Q) came from that segment. The large provision, though, kept NPL cover high at 122%.

9M NPAT of P6.7bn, surpassing our FYE on trading gains, and taxes

NPAT of P6.66bn in 9M20 exceeded our FY20E of P6.5bn (vs P9.0bn consensus). SECB registered monster trading/fx gains of P13.4bn, with P5.6bn coming in 3Q20 alone. The gains likely came from the bank’s foreign currency book, which carries a much lower tax rate. The P21bn provision likely caused in a loss in the PHP book, resulting in a P2.9bn tax benefit (vs our FY20E P1.6bn tax expense).

Consumer loans -9% qoq, but NIMs jump on sharply lower deposit costs

Loans contracted 3% yoy and 4% qoq (Figure 1). Consumer loans shrank 9% qoq, taking the segment down to 26% of total, from 29% at end-2019 (Figure 2). NIMs expanded 27bps qoq and 88bps yoy to 4.58% (Figure 3) despite the changing loan mix. Deposit costs plunged to 0.81%, down 40bps qoq and 141bps yoy (Figure 4), as CASA ratio climbed to 59% from 45% in 2019.

NPLs rise to 4.03%, 3Q provision almost solely for consumer loans

NPL ratio spiked to 4.03% from 1.58% in 2Q (Figure 5). While the bank does not break down NPLs, it does split out provision expense. Of the 9M20 provision of P21bn, about P14bn was for the retail bank (Figure 6). The P10bn provision in 3Q20 seems like it was entirely for the consumer portfolio. While the 9M provision took NPL cover to 122%, further deterioration in asset quality could mean P&L pressure into 2021E. The bank will hold an earnings call on Monday, 16 November.

LT Group - 3Q: Sin sectors deliver; Bank puts off provisions as NPLs rise
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph // Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q profit +11%yoy, 9M tracking ahead

LTG reported a net income of P16.10bn (+9%) for 9M20, tracking ahead of estimates. Profit contributions from tobacco and spirits grew 27% and 113%, offsetting the 39% decline of the bank. In 3Q alone, LTG's net income of P6.08bn (11%yoy, 59%qoq) was much better than 2Q as the bank cut provisioning despite rising NPLs.

Tobacco profit +6% in 3Q, on track to beat FY20 estimate

PMFTC's contribution grew 6%yoy and 23%qoq to P3.941bn in 3Q20. As selling restrictions eased, volumes rebounded 21%qoq to 11.7bn sticks. Although volumes were still 6%lower yoy, profits were well ahead due to better margins. With the announced price increase of P20/pack (+20-29%) in October, we think that further improvement in margin can more than compensate for the expected hit to sales volumes.

NPL coverage drops to 57% on lower provisions; NPL ratio at 6.27% in 3Q

PNB's contribution rose to P1.44bn, +4%yoy and 42x the income in 2Q. Provisions tapered off to just P618mn in 3Q, after booking P8.44bn in 1H. Trading and fx gains moderated to <P500mn In 3Q (from P1.5bn In 2Q), while its loan book contracted 11% to P577bn. Gross NPL ratio increased to 6.27% (from 4.77% In 2Q). As provisions were cut, NPL coverage dropped to 57% (vs well over 100% for the big 3 banks).

Liquor volumes +44%yoy; Brewery losses continue

We estimate volumes of Tanduay jumped to 7.185mn case in 3Q (+63%qoq, +44%yoy), driving 3Q profit to P544mn (+59%qoq,+300%yoy). Like other spirits manufacturers, it appeared to benefit from pent-up demand and a shift to offpremise brands. Meanwhile, we estimate ABI incurred a loss of P36mn in 3Q (vs - P34mn in 2Q) on continued losses from its beer JV. The restructuring of its partnership with Heineken (to one where ABI is a manufacturer-distributor while Heineken handles marketing and brand-building) may allow it to reduce its losses (P700mn/year) from this venture.

Cosco Capital - 3Q20: cost cuts lift Liquor Distribution segment
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M20 net profit -7% YoY – behind expectations

Cosco Capital’s 9M20 net profit after minorities was down 7% YoY to P3.9bn, tracking behind expectations. Operating results for key subsidiaries Puregold (PGOLD PM) and Real Estate were behind forecasts, while the Liquor Distribution segment was broadly in line. For 3Q20 alone, net profit declined 8% YoY to P1.4bn.

Real Estate: QoQ improvement as commercial establishments reopen

Net profit for COSCO’s Real Estate segment declined 24% YoY in 3Q20, though improved sharply (+130%) on a QoQ basis as its commercial establishments reopened post-lockdown. Revenues, however, are still down 20% YoY due to the extension of rental concessions to its tenants.

Liquor Distribution: Cost cuts drive profit improvement

3Q20 net profit of the Liquor Distribution segment jumped 24% YoY and 72% QoQ to P327mn. The QoQ and YoY improvement was largely driven by cuts in sales & marketing expenses, which drove EBITDA margin to expand 390bps QoQ and 900bps YoY. Volumes improved 34% QoQ to 769k cases as liquor bans were lifted postlockdown. Volumes, however, were still down 35% YoY due to the slow recovery in on-premise consumption. Management noted that volumes are improving sequentially in Oct-Nov, but 4Q volumes will still be down YoY as holiday celebrations are likely to be more subdued.

Terms of receivables extended further

Management noted that the terms of its loan receivables from related parties (c.P12bn) were extended as of end-Sept. The payment for around half of these receivables was extended for another 90 days (i.e. due end-2020), while the payment for the balance has been rolled over for another year (i.e. due Sep 2021).

Max’s Group - 3Q20: aggressive cost cuts amidst slower-than-expected sales recovery
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M20 net loss at P979mn

MAXS reported a net loss of P979mn in 9M20, wider than expectations. Revenues were down 50% YoY (behind), while the operating loss was P1.1bn. For 3Q20 alone, the reported net loss was P379mn, though the figure includes nonrecurring expenses. Excluding these non-recurring items (accelerated depreciation on store closures and HQ relocation, inventory write-offs), we estimate the net loss narrowed QoQ to P290mn in

3Q20. 3Q20 revenues jump 31% QoQ as stores reopen

Revenues jumped 31% QoQ as stores resumed operations after being temporarily shut during the hard lockdown period in 2Q20. As of end-September, MAXS had resumed operations for 80% of its 716-store network. SSSG, however, was still down 49% YoY during the quarter due to the slow recovery in dine-in sales. Dine-in accounted for c25% of sales during the period (55-56% preCOVID).

Aggressive cost-cutting initiatives implemented in 3Q20

Excluding the one-off costs in 3Q20, we estimate the operating loss narrowed QoQ to P309mn. Apart from permanent store closures (44 net closures in 9M20 out of 760 in FY20), management also implemented aggressive cost cuts resulting in a 19% YoY reduction in G&A expenses and a 65% YoY cut in sales and marketing expenses.

Sequential recovery in 4Q20 but weaker holiday spending to be a drag

Management indicated that they continue to see a sequential recovery in OctNov, though YoY trends are still down significantly given the likely drop in holiday-related spending this year. Profitability, however, is expected to see a more significant improvement QoQ given the aggressive cost cuts, driving recurring net profit to positive territory by 4Q20. We adjust our 2020E EPS estimates for the recent results and make minimal (+1%) adjustments to our 2021E forecasts. We maintain our TP of P8/sh and Buy rating on the stock.

Jollibee Foods Corp. - An uphill climb; downgrading to Hold
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

An uphill climb

JFC’s share price has outperformed the broader PCOMP by 40% since the lows in March over optimism on the economy’s potential reopening amidst vaccine developments. But in our view, the share price has moved ahead of earnings expectations. We believe JFC’s top line recovery will be an uphill climb across key markets, and profitability beyond 2020 will largely depend on the sustainability of cost savings from its business transformation program. We are adjusting our 2020- 22E EPS forecasts, raising our target price to P185/sh (+9%), but downgrading the stock to a Hold. At current PE multiples (52x 2021E / 30x 2022E), we believe the risk reward is no longer favorable given limited visibility on near term earnings.

REGIS Events: 3Q/9M20 post-results call with CFO

At a call we recently hosted, JFC’s CFO Mr. Ysmael Baysa provided insight on the latest results and on the company’s business transformation (BT) initiatives. The rebound in sales following the outbreak has been uneven so far, with markets outside the Philippines seeing better recovery rates. The implementation of the P7bn BT, which was partly triggered by the pandemic, is broadly on track and should be completed by end-2020.

Business transformation on track…

JFC has spent P2.9bn of the P7bn earmarked for the BT program (P2.2bn in 3Q alone). The company estimates it will save P2.6bn in annual costs from this exercise, mainly from overhead. Overall, management says the program is on track, particularly with regard to commissary closures and headcount rationalization. It is, however, lagging on store closures (particularly for Smashburger) but expects to catch up in 4Q.

…but sales recovery in PH lags

The Philippines was the underperformer, posting the fastest SSS decline in 3Q20 (-46% YoY). While this was a sequential improvement vs 2Q20 (-50% YoY), the pace of recovery is trending behind expectations. Recovery has been an uphill climb amidst limits on public transportation, which has hampered consumer mobility, particularly for JFC’s core market (low- to middle-income consumers).

Positives priced in; downgrading to Hold

We believe the share price bounce has already priced in a recovery more than twelve months forward as 1) same-store revenues are unlikely to match prepandemic levels until 2022 and 2) the next few quarters will remain challenging. We are cutting our 2020-21E EPS estimates but largely maintaining forecasts for 2022E onwards. We raise our DCF-based target price to P185/sh (+9%) on lower cost of debt assumptions, and our new target price implies forward PE multiples of 50x 2021E / 29x 2022E. Key risks relate to the pace of top line recovery across the various markets where JFC operates.

3Q profit -57%yoy to P2.030bn

AGI’s 3Q20 net income of P2.030bn more than doubled from 2Q, but was 58% lower yoy. This brought 9M20 profit to P5.828bn, down 55%yoy, tracking slightly behind our FY20E. While liquor beat expectations, slower-than-expected recoveries from Travellers and Golden Arches caused the mild shortfall.

Whisky segment benefits from surge in off-premise demand

EMP’s 3Q20 income was lifted by the continued strength of its international whisky segment. Profits from whisky hit P995mn (+42%qoq, +72%yoy), as offpremise consumption trends lifted demand for its brands (Whyte & Mackay, Jura and Tamnavulin +19-39% YTD). Brandy profits, on the other hand, grew 7%yoy to P1.548bn, despite a 2% decline in revenues, on aggressive cost cuts. While management did not provide volume data, we estimate that volumes were down in the mid-teens (vs +30-40% growth for GSMI and Tanduay in 3Q). Given the price premium of Emperador, there may have been downtrading even within the low-end segment.

Construction limits cap residential recovery; office revenues +10%yoy

MEG’s 3Q20 profit of P2.0bn was just up 6qoq and down 55%yoy. Office revenues rose 6%qoq & 10%yoy to P3.0bn, helped by increased GLA from new projects. Despite the easing of restrictions, residential revenues came in flat qoq and down 55%yoy at P4.7bn. Construction activity remained capped at 30% of normal.

Losses narrow for casinos and restaurants on slower-than-expected recovery

Travellers’ losses narrowed to P1.7bn in 3Q (from P2.8bn in 2Q) as it gradually ramped up to 30% capacity. Golden Arches incurred a net loss of P258mn in 3Q (vs -P840mn in 2Q). While more than 90% of stores were already operational, SSSG for the quarter was still -46%. Of sales, 38% came from take-out, 29% from drive-thru, 17% from delivery and 16% from dine-in.

Megaworld Corp. - 3Q20 reservation sales fall 26% QoQ despite looser restrictions
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

3Q20 earnings fall 55% YoY

Megaworld’s 3Q20 net profit fell 55% YoY to P2.0bn as lockdowns continued to pressure the residential, mall, and hotel segments. 9M20 net profit fell 42% to P7.4bn, broadly in line with our forecast (but below consensus). However, reservation sales weakened in 3Q and a POGO tenant vacated its office.

Reservation sales fall QoQ due to weak Metro Manila projects

3Q20 presales fell 26% QoQ (- 63% YoY) to P12.6bn, despite looser lockdowns from 2Q. The company said that provincial projects generally sold well but projects in Metro Manila, which comprised c. 60% of 2019 launches, performed poorly. 9M20 presales fell 56% to P50.2bn, tracking behind our forecast. Residential revenue was also flat QoQ as the company’s construction activity was still at c. 30% of pre-COVID capacity.

Office revenue grows despite POGO closure

3Q20 office revenue rose 10% YoY to P3.0bn despite the closure of a POGO tenant. The POGO vacated a 29,000sqm office building (c. 2% of MEG’s office portfolio). However, Megaworld will continue to recognize revenue from the tenant since POGOs pay 6 months of advanced rent and 3 months of security deposit. In addition, the company has already secured new tenants (traditional, non-POGO) for 60% of the vacated building (which is not PEZA-accredited). POGOs still occupy 11% of the company’s office portfolio and recent revisions to tax regulations could cause most of them to cease their operations.

Mall rental concessions to remain

Although mall revenue jumped 117% QoQ to P400mn, this was still a 74% drop YoY. Operational GLA remained tepid at 68% while foot traffic remained muted at 30% of pre-COVID levels. Thus, Megaworld plans to continue granting rental concessions to mall tenants until a more substantial recovery takes place.

9M20 core profit of P3.3bn (-72% YoY) falls short

Elevated provisions weigh on bank profits

Metrobank kept provisions high in 3Q, surprisingly, causing profit to drop 36% QoQ and 78% YoY (see Metrobank: Conservatism vs growth – Provisions kept high in 3Q; loans contract 17% ytd, 30 October 2020). Loans contracted 13% YoY and 8% QoQ.

Reservation sales nearly double QoQ

Its property unit recorded a profit of P172mn in 9M20, which was not as bad as feared (FY20E: P66mn net loss). 9M20 reservation sales stood at P11.3bn, already exceeding our forecast (P10bn). In 3Q alone, reservations sales nearly doubled QoQ to P2.2bn. Management is focusing on developing and monetizing the 702 hectares it acquired from Pro-Friends. It booked a large P900mn gain in October from the sale of a 15-hectare portion and plans to partner with other players to develop the rest of the property, which has direct access to new tollroads.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Vista Land - 3Q20 rental revenue “only” falls 12% on limited mall tenant concessions
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Stronger-than-expected residential and rental revenue

Vista Land’s 3Q20 net profit fell 39% to P1.9bn. This brought 9M20 earnings down 40% to P5.3bn, already 94% of REGIS FY forecast (85% of consensus). 9M20 operating profit shrank 30% to P7.2bn, already 85% of our FY estimate, on milder-than-expected drops in residential and rental revenue. This was further boosted by a low tax rate.

VLL reservation sales performing better than most other developers’

3Q20 presales fell 33% to P13.8bn, bringing the 9M20 figure down 36% to P39.5bn, in line with our forecast. The breakdown of sales remained similar in 9M20, with provincial projects comprising c. 50%, OFWs accounting for 50-55%, and the Camella brand (low-cost housing) contributing 80-90% of sales. Meanwhile, less than 10% of buyers availed of the option to defer payments.

Rental revenue drop cushioned by limited mall concessions

3Q20 rental revenue, which is dominated by malls, fell “only” 12% YoY, compared to the 60%-80% drops for other mall operators. This is mostly because unlike other mall operators, which granted rental concessions to all mall tenants, Vista only granted rental concessions to a few. However, this could lead some tenants to close their stores since foot traffic is only 45%-55% of pre-COVID levels. Related-party tenants (which are unlikely to close their stores) occupy c. 60% of Vista’s mall space. Most related-party tenants have not received concessions. The exceptions would be affiliates that still cannot operate, such as the cinema. Other affiliates such as AllHome (DIY, furniture) and AllDay (supermarket) are seeing much more resilient revenues.

Office REIT listing in the works

Vista plans to do a REIT listing, starting with its office portfolio. The cash raised could help reduce net gearing, which stood at 96% as of 3Q20.

Megawide - 3Q20: Key operating units remain under pressure
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M20 net loss at P822mn, wider than street estimates

Megawide extended its losses in 3Q, with implied net loss after pref divs at P391mn. 9M20 net loss stood at P822mn, which is smaller than our FY20E (P1.5bn) but wider than street estimates (P688mn). However, the beat was underwhelming, as it largely came from (a) higher booked construction revenues, (b) lower non-cash expenses, and (c) lower financing expenses. These masked the operational weakness at its airport and landport units, as well as a lower construction order book.

Construction revenue bookings improve; Order book shrinks to P40.7bn

The construction unit recorded a net loss of only P477mn in 9M20, much lower than forecast. 3Q20 booked revenues more than doubled QoQ to P2.6bn, as its manpower gradually returned. Capacity is already at 60% of pre-COVID levels in September. Its order book was sharply reduced to P40.7bn in end-3Q from P47.7bn in end-2Q, after management removed contracts that are currently being re-negotiated.

Airport: EBITDA losses persist in 3Q

Restrictions for non-essential travel largely remained in 3Q, thus the airport unit's EBITDA for the period was at a negative P83mn. The company is pushing for these restrictions to be lifted, even partially over the coming weeks. Local governments in Region 7 are set to meet this week to discuss allowing rapid tests for non-essential travel within the region, on top of reducing documentary requirements.

Landport books revenue reversal in 3Q

In anticipation of a reduction in contract lives for its tenants, the company preemptively booked a P47mn revenue reversal at its landport unit in 3Q20 due to lease contract changes. A POGO tenant occupying one tower prematurely ended its lease. Contracts for the three remaining office towers are also currently being re-negotiated with another tenant. We could see another revenue reversal (possibly P80mn) depending on the outcome of the negotiations.

Filinvest Land - 3Q20 reservation sales recover but office revenue drops on POGO closures
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Operating profit already hits full-year forecast

Filinvest Land’s 9M20 net profit fell 40% to P2.6bn, which puts it on track to beat our FY forecast. In fact, operating profit for 9M20 has already reached our FY estimate as opex over the period fell 17%, a sharper drop than we anticipated.

Reservation sales recover on looser payment schemes and price discounts

3Q20 reservation sales jumped 83% QoQ (+4% YoY) to P4.55bn, thanks to looser payment schemes and price discounts (of up to 15%) on selected units. As a result, 9M20 reservation sales of P11.9bn (-23%) and is on trend to exceed our FY forecast. Sales in Metro Manila fell an estimated 40% while sales in adjacent provinces rose c. 10%. OFW sales were also under pressure, dropping 50% and now comprising just 20% of residential sales (from 31% in 2019). Filinvest also restructured purchase agreements with 5-10% of buyers. Their payments were credited to cheaper units instead, allowing them to avoid outright default.

Office revenue falls QoQ on POGO closures

Total rental revenue fell 9% QoQ (-15% YoY) to P1.5bn. We estimate that office revenue fell c. 10% QoQ (c. +10% YoY) as POGOs occupying c. 5% of the office portfolio pre-terminated their contracts. Although POGOs pay 6 months of advanced rent and 6 months of security deposit, FLI will recognize these in the income statement at a later period (after pre-termination of contracts). POGOs still occupy another 15% of the office portfolio and we estimate that they pay nearly double the rental rate of non-POGO tenants. Recent revisions to tax regulations could cause most of them to shut down their operations eventually.

Rental concessions to mall tenants to remain

Although mall revenue grew c. 60% QoQ, it is still down c. 70% YoY and mall foot traffic is still only about 40% of pre-COVID levels. Thus, FLI plans to continue granting rental concessions to mall tenants.

Philippine Strategy - 3Q20 scorecard – on the road to recovery, but mind the potholes
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Still more misses in 3Q, but recovery taking shape

The 3Q20 results are all in. And unless there’s a recount, about half the stocks we cover missed our forecasts, which were already below the Street’s. That said, the 45% YoY drop in earnings in 3Q20 was much better than the 80% collapse in 2Q20. In fact, aggregate earnings in 3Q were more than double that of 2Q, with big QoQ rebounds from banks and consumers. As the market anticipates the recovery, we are mindful of a ban on social gatherings in 4Q, and the exodus of POGOs as key earnings risks.

More misses than beats…

Of the 43 stocks under REGIS coverage, just over half (22) came in behind our expectations, with about a third (14) posting beats. And given our analysts’ penchant for conservatism, the misses were even more stark against consensus. Of the 30 stocks in the PSEi, almost two thirds (19) came in below Street estimates.

… but powerful QoQ earnings recovery, led by banks, consumers

To the upside, the 45% yoy decline in aggregate earnings for REGIS covered stocks (-40% for the PSEi) was significantly better than the 80% yoy collapse in 2Q20. In fact, earnings more than doubled +137% qoq for REGIS coverage, and almost trebled +196% qoq for PSEi stocks. The biggest rebounds came from banks, as BDO did a huge one-time provision in 2Q20, and from consumers, particularly restaurant and non-essentials that were closed during the hard lockdown (or “ECQ”) during much of 2Q20. (See table at right column for YoY and QoQ breakdown by sector.)

Surprising strength in liquor and provincial property; retailers disappoint

As was expected, telcos continued to fare better, though more so for PLDT which again took share from rival Globe. Other surprising sub-sectors included liquor (Emperador, LT Group), and non-Metro Manila residential property (Filinvest, Vista). Big disappointments came retailers Puregold and Robinsons Retail, both of which saw demand weaken, even for basic grocery necessities. Bank profits were all over the place, as companies took differing approaches to NPLs and provisioning in 3Q20. (Short sector recaps from page 2.)

Market to focus on cyclical recovery plays, but mind the headwinds

With massive liquidity, and with hopes of vaccines, the market will look to cyclical recovery plays such as banks, consumers, property, and tourism (gaming and airlines). However, we would point to a few risks idiosyncratic to the Philippines. The government remains wary of Covid-19’s spread, and has banned parties during what is typically a big spending 4Q, and could put a damper on 4Q20 consumer spending. Meanwhile, the apparent exodus of POGOs will likely keep a cloud hanging over the property sector.

Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership signed

Fifteen Asia Pacific economies, including the Philippines, signed the world’s largest free trade deal. The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) is a trade pact that includes China, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea and all 10 ASEAN member countries. This is the first time that China, Japan and South Korea are all in a single free trade agreement. The prospective free trade area accounts for a third of global population and economy and builds on existing bilateral and multilateral agreements in the region. Additionally, it also allows for a common “rule of origin” in the region, simplifying regulations identifying if products are made in a country. “The RCEP will further broaden the Philippines’ economic engagements with its trading partners should improve trade and investment, enhanced transparency, integrated regional supply chains and strengthened economic cooperation”, said Trade Sec. Ramon Lopez. RCEP is expected to enhance market access for key Philippine products like garments, auto parts, and agricultural products.
