PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Déjà vu to MECQ – tighter lockdown to weigh on GDP, earnings
 
 
by Rafa Garchitorena – rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph and Gio dela Rosa - gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

MECQ for Metro Manila, nearby provinces as Covid cases surge

President Duterte put Metro Manila and a few provinces back on the tighter Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine (MECQ), as confirmed Covid-19 cases surge past 100,000, with cases hitting over 5,000 a day. Renewed movement restrictions put a damper on recovery hopes. We reduce our GDP estimates for 2020E to -6.5%, and expect a milder +5% rebound in 2021E. Sectors such as restaurants, non-food retail, and malls will likely be hardest hit. A prolonged MECQ could also pose NPL risks for the banks.

About face – MECQ for 2 weeks (at least)

In a midnight address on Sunday night (2 Aug), President Duterte reversed his decision just days earlier to maintain General Community Quarantine (GCQ). The reversion to MECQ will mean stopping all public transport, and restricting movement to essential workers. Retail shops will have to close, except for those selling food, and medicines. Restaurants can open, but only for take-away and delivery. The current MECQ will be in force from 4 to 18 August.

Deeper, longer – revising down GDP forecasts, limited fiscal stimulus

We now expect a deeper economic slump, and a longer recovery period, and forecast 2Q GDP contracting 12-15% (from -10%). We also expect negative yoy prints in 3Q and 4Q, taking our FY2020E GDP estimate to -6.5% yoy (from -2.5%). 

Our 2021E growth forecast is also revised down to +5% from 7-8% previously. The slower recovery is predicated on a limited fiscal stimulus. While there is debt capacity, we see the fiscal deficit hitting 8-9% in 2020E on lower tax revenues, even before factoring in any stimulus spending passed by Congress.

Negative for market earnings – restaurants, malls, potentially banks

Most obviously affected would be restaurant operators like JFC, PIZZA and MAXS, as well as mall operations of property firms SMPH, ALI, MEG, RLC, FLI and VLL. A prolonged MECQ will also raise NPL risks for banks BDO, BPI, MBT and SECB. Our current aggregate earnings call for a 32% decline in 2020E and a 24% recovery in 2021E, ie still about 15% below 2019 levels. The downgraded macro outlook further increases downside risk to market earnings.

Market PE of 14.3x for 2021E, not quite “deep value”

On our forecasts, the PSEi is trading at 17.7x PER in 2020E. For 2021E we have the index at 14.3x PE, compared to the long-term average 12-month forward PE of 15x forward. Not exactly “deep value”. Defensive stock picks include TEL, CNPF, PGOLD, URC and MER.

 
Globe Telecom (GLO PM) 2Q: Surge in home broadband cushions dip in mobile
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa - gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q: Surge in home broadband cushions dip in mobile

2Q core income drops less than expected

Globe reported core income of P4.5bn for 2Q. While down 15% YoY, the figure is better than our expectations as revenues began to pick up by middle of the quarter. Service revenue declined 4% YoY to P35.5bn, as the drop in mobile revenue (-9% YoY in 2Q) was cushioned by a surprising surge in home broadband (+26%). Cumulative 1H20 core income amounted to P11.1bn, 8% lower than last year but ~60% of our FY20 estimate.

Shift from mobile to home

2Q saw a marked behavioral shift that manifested in Globe’s revenue numbers. Mobile revenue fell 7% QoQ (-9% YoY) as people stayed home during the period of the severest lockdown, aggravated by the impact of reduced incomes. Home broadband revenue, on the other hand, grew 16% QoQ (+26% YoY).

Wireless broadband take-up jumps

The surge in home broadband was surprising as Globe adding over 575k fixed wireless and 33k wired subscribers in the quarter alone. 

This is from a base of 1.61m and 645k as of end-March. Overall broadband subs are up a whopping 27% QoQ as consumers hunkered down in their homes.

Provisions up ahead of possible receivable issues

The company bumped up provisions to P2.82bn in 2Q, up 2x YoY (+168% QoQ) in anticipation of possible receivable issues due to the pandemic. 1H20 provisions came to P3.9bn (+57% YoY). Globe gave postpaid subscribers more time to settle their accounts, and expects provisions to normalize at ~P900m in subsequent quarters. We are assuming provisions of P7,1bn for FY20.

Guidance: Revenue, EBITDA and capex

Globe is guiding for revenue drop of “low single digit” (vs -2% for our estimate) and EBITDA margin in the low 50s (vs. 49%). The firm’s capex expectations have also been scaled back to P50.3bn from P63bn (vs. Regis estimate of P40bn).

 
SM Prime (SMPH PM): 2Q20 profit falls 80%; Mega Manila back on tighter lockdown
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA – carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q20 net profit falls 80%

SM Prime’s 2Q20 net profit fell 80% to P2.1bn due to the hard lockdown until the end of May. This brought 1H20 net profit down 46% to P10.4bn, slightly ahead of our forecasts, largely thanks to strong residential revenue. However, the recently announced tighter lockdown in August puts a damper on our recovery expectations in 2H20. Meanwhile, SM Prime’s 1H20 net profit was behind consensus forecast (only 38% of consensus FY20E).

Philippine mall revenue also down c. 80% due to hard lockdown

2Q20 total mall revenue fell 73% (to P4.3bn) as Philippine mall revenue fell c. 80% (to P3.1bn). During the hard lockdown, only essential services, which occupy 20% of mall space, could operate. Even though most of the country was placed on looser restrictions (called General Community Quarantine or GCQ) starting June 1, only 30%-40% of tenants were open as of end-June. Meanwhile, China 2Q20 mall revenue only fell c. 15% YoY, which we consider a good performance since it is the first full quarter of operations since lockdowns were lifted in China. SM Prime said that foot traffic at its China malls was nearly 90% of pre-COVID levels.

Residential segment tracking ahead of our forecasts

Despite restrictions on construction activities, 2Q20 residential revenue was flat YoY (up 8% QoQ) at P12.3bn. This brought the 1H20 figure up 11% to P23.7bn, tracking ahead of our forecast. More remarkably, 2Q20 reservation sales only fell 26% YoY (to P17.6bn), bringing the 1H20 figure up 2%, implying resilient demand for the company’s residential projects.

2Q20 office revenue rises 13%

2Q20 office revenue rose 13% to P1.2bn, bringing the 1H20 figure to P2.5bn (+16% YoY). The completion of NU Tower in 2H19 added about 10% to floor area. BPOs, which occupy c. 90% of the company’s office floor area, were permitted to operate from the office even during the hard lockdown.

Mega Manila back on MECQ

The Philippine government announced that Metro Manila and adjacent provinces (which we estimate account for a combined 70% of Philippine mall revenue) will be placed back on tighter lockdown (called Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine or MECQ) from August 4 to 18. Although up to 80% of mall tenants will still be permitted to remain open (similar to the current situation where c. 70% are actually open), public transportation will be severely restricted and restaurants will not be permitted to serve dine-in customers. This would hamper the ability of shoppers to go to the mall and might prompt some tenants to close again. Given the jump in COVID cases, another prolonged hard lockdown would not be out of the question.

 
Metrobank (MBT PM): Strong trading, NII allow for P23bn provision; 1H20 NPAT -30% to P9.1bn
 
 
by Rafa Garchitorena – rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

PPOP surges 102% in 2Q (+61% in 1H); 2Q provisions 8x yoy

MBT saw PPOP surge 61% in 1H20 – +103% in 2Q alone – as it booked P13bn of trading gains in 1H (P12bn in 2Q). NII was solid, too, up 22% yoy in 1H, despite a 5% contraction in loans. NIMs expanded 72bps yoy, on the back of a 20% increase in CASA deposits. The bank accelerated provisions to P22.8bn in 1H (+395%). While NPL ratio was relatively flat at 1.56% (vs 1.4% in 1Q20), NPL coverage jumped to 188% as MBT anticipates a rise in NPLs in 2H.

Loans -5% yoy but NIMs up 72bps on CASA growth; MBT cuts deposit rates

MBT reported NPAT of P3.0bn in 2Q20 (-52% yoy), taking 1H20 to P9.1bn (-30%). Loans contracted sharply – -4.6% yoy, -7.1% qoq, and -10.1% ytd – with corporate loans dragging. NIMs were up 72bps yoy and 40bps qoq to 4.12% in 2Q due largely to falling deposit costs as CASA grew 20% yoy. NIMs are likely to stay firm in 2H – MBT announced that starting 7 August, it is slashing rates on Peso CASA from 0.25% to 0.125% and on USD CASA from 0.125% to 0.0%.

Trading gains of P12bn in 2Q more than make up for 37% plunge in fees

The bank recognized huge P11.7bn trading/forex gains in 2Q19 as domestic interest rates collapsed following the BSP’s surprise 50bps rate cut in late June. The 1H20 trading gain of P13.1bn far exceeded our FY20E of P9.7bn. Fees, as expected, dropped 37% in 2Q as the country was in hard lockdown.

Provisions accelerated; NPL cover jumps to 188% ahead of 3Q rise

The bank used the strong PPOP to accelerate provisions, taking P17.7bn in 2Q alone and P22.8bn in 1H20, exceeding our full-year forecast of P22.6bn. NPL ratio was up slightly to 1.56% from 1.40% in 1Q20. Meanwhile, NPL cover surged to 188% as the bank anticipates a rise in NPLs in 2H20. We maintain our Buy on MBT as the stock now trades on 0.45x P/bk on our 2021E estimates.

 
SM Investments (SM PM): Rich valuations; maintain Hold
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa - gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Target price revised to P760/share

SM Investments’ (SM PM) share price has been relatively resilient amidst the pandemic. The stock has outperformed the overall market (by 10%) YTD, and, more remarkably, even the shares of its two key listed subsidiaries. We cut our estimates and also adjust our target price to P760/share, reflecting a 5% discount to our target SOTP NAV (P800/share). SM is a key market proxy that many investors flock to, but current valuations are rich. Maintain Hold.

The long pause

The pandemic has led to massive disruption for many businesses, with many pushed to bankruptcy. SM will not be spared from disruption but it is in a strong financial position to weather this once-in-a-generation storm. In the meantime, we are viewing the circumstances today as an extended hiatus from which SM’s operations will eventually recover.

Flight to quality has made valuations rich

The overall market has seen significant selling but SM has been relatively unscathed. In fact, it has managed to outperform BDO (BDO PM) and SM Prime (SMPH PM), by 33% and 16% respectively, and the two combined account for over 70% of its NAV. As a result, the stock is now trading at ~20% premium to its market price-based NAV.

Target price P760

We trim our target price to P760/share (-11%), which is a 5% discount to our revised target sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) NAV for SM Investments of P800/share. This adjusts for the revised estimates and valuations of its key subsidiaries. Key risks (upside and downside) relate to the length of the period when its operations will be affected by the pandemic.

 
Universal Robina (URC PM): 2Q20 EBIT rebounds +16% YoY on cost management
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H20 net profit +8% YoY to P5.5bn – ahead of expectations

URC’s 1H20 net profit rose 8% YoY to P5.5bn, trending ahead of expectations. Consolidated revenues of P67.4bn (+1% YoY) were broadly in line, while EBIT of P8.2bn (+8% YoY) is tracking ahead. For 2Q20 alone, net profit surged 69% YoY to P3.5bn, largely due to non-operating items. However, 2Q20 EBIT still grew strongly (+16% YoY) as margins improved across all business segments even as top line was challenged.

PH branded consumer food (BCF) steady amidst lockdown

Sales of the PH BCF segment were flattish in 2Q20. Category performance was mixed given the lockdown, with key categories seeing strong growth – noodles (+22%), coffee (+8%), and snack foods (+8%). URC gained market share across all categories as it continued to invest in brand building, and to focus on product availability amidst the difficult environment, logistics-wise.

Int’l BCF dragged by lockdown measures, but seeing improvement

Int’l BCF sales contracted 11% YoY due mainly to lockdown measures, particularly in Vietnam (-36%), where URC’s key product (RTD beverages) is consumed mostly outside of the home. Thailand saw a mild improvement (+1% in 2Q vs -11% in 1Q), while Oceania moderated (+5%) following pantry stocking in 1Q (+19%). Trends in Vietnam and Thailand continue to improve MoM as of July, while Oceania is seeing pantry stocking anew.

EBIT margin expansion likely to be sustained given cost management efforts

Consolidated EBIT margin expanded 170bps YoY, with expansion seen across all business segments. While the expansion was supported by subdued raw material cost, management attributed most of the margin improvements to cost management efforts, which were already being implemented pre-pandemic. Among these measures include rationalization of its supply chain, as well as head office cost. Margins are likely to be sustained moving forward even as top line remains challenged amidst the uncertain environment.

 
Aboitiz Power (AP PM): Energy sales fall as lockdowns dampen commercial demand
 
 
by Aaron Salvador – aaron.salvador@regis.ph and Gio dela Rosa - gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H20 core income falls 59% to P3.513bn - behind

Aboitiz Power’s headline profit in 2Q20 fell 67%yoy to P1.677bn. Excluding the impact of fx and other non-recurring items, 2Q20 core income fell 68% to P1.426bn. This dragged 1H20 core income 59% lower to P3.513bn – below expectations. The full impact of the lockdowns weighed on the quarter’s performance, negatively affecting energy sales and selling prices. Plant outages also spilled over to the most recent period.

Energy sales fall 17% due to lockdowns, spillover of plant outages

Core income for the generation and retail electricity supply (RES) segment fell to P1.648bn (-62%yoy, -14%qoq) in 2Q20. On top of plant outages that spilled over into the quarter, energy volumes declined to 5,089 GWh (-17%yoy, - 10%qoq) as quarantine restrictions dampened demand from commercial and industrial customers. Energy revenues fell to P18.4bn in 2Q (-26%yoy, -7%qoq), dragged by lower average selling prices.

Lower commercial and industrial demand drag distribution volumes 20%

Profits from its distribution segment fell 23%yoy and 34%qoq to P679mn in 2Q20. Electricity sales volume in the quarter fell 20%yoy and 16%qoq, as the commercial & industrial segment contracted 32%. Meanwhile, residential volumes improved 12%.

Lockdown updates

Peak demand in Luzon and Visayas fell around 30% in the first weeks of April. Management estimates that peak demand is now back to around 95% of pre-ECQ level, but spot prices are still well below average prices in the same period last year. Construction has resumed at GNPower Dinginin, but progress has slowed due to safety measures and limited availability of technical advisers from abroad.

Target commissioning has been pushed back to 1Q21-2Q21. Meanwhile, TSI has been put back into service as of the second week of May, while Pagbilao unit 1 returned to service in the third week of July.

 
Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM): Supermarkets outperform in 2Q20 amidst lockdown
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H20 net profit broadly in line at P1.6bn (-4% YoY)

RRHI’s 1H20 net profit dipped 4% YoY to P1.6bn, broadly in line with our FY20E. “Core” earnings (excluding equitized earnings and other income) declined 19% YoY to P1.3bn. Results for most formats were tracking within expectations, while Department Stores (5% of sales) and Specialty Stores (8% of sales) underperformed. For 2Q20 alone, net profit fell 33% YoY given the full quarter impact of the Luzon lockdown.

2Q20 Supermarkets SSSG outperforms but moderates vs 1Q

Consolidated same-store sales (SSS) contracted 13.9% in 2Q20 alone as majority of RRHI’s formats had to be temporarily closed during the lockdown. Supermarkets (69% of sales) was the outperformer, as expected, posting SSSG of +14.2%. This continued to be driven by the increase in average basket sizes (>40%), albeit moderating from the >50% levels seen in March amidst pantry stocking. Transaction count declined c.30% as consumer mobility continued to be restricted.

Rustans turnaround partly cushions impact of temporary closures

Operating profit fell 43% YoY in 2Q20 due to the temporary store closures for most of its formats, and partly by lower gross margin for Supermarkets in the absence of supplier promotions. However, we estimate that the contraction was partly cushioned by the continued turnaround of Rustans operations, which we estimate added another 100bps margin YoY. Department Stores, Convenience Stores, and Specialty Stores – now collectively just 13% of sales (27% in 2019) – either posted an EBIT loss or barely broke even during the quarter.

Outlook subdued; guidance tempered

RRHI has resumed operations for over 95% of its stores since it started reopening in mid-May. While it is seeing recovery in some formats (DIY, Appliances), store foot traffic for others (Convenience Stores, other Specialty Retail) remains challenged. Given the operating environment, management scaled down its SSSG guidance for the year further to -5-0% (Regis FY20E: 5%) from 0-3% previously.

 
BDO Unibank (BDO PM) 1H20 briefing notes: re-growing the businesses amid rising NPLs
 
 
by Rafa Garchitorena – rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Early signs of recovery, but still wary of asset quality

We hosted a 1H20 investor briefing with BDO’s Richard Tan and Luis Reyes. Despite the jump in NPLs to 1.95%, the bank believes the P20bn provision in 2Q is sufficient to cover NPLs of up to 4-5%. It has also already restructured / rescheduled roughly 5% of the loan book. To the upside, business seems to be picking up, with branch activity at 90% of pre-Covid levels. And while loan growth is likely to slow, NIMs could stay firm given strong CASA growth.

NPLs, provisions take center stage

Unsurprisingly, asset quality took center stage. While the bank took P23bn in 1H20 provisions, NPLs were also up around P20bn ytd. Management still considers the provision “anticipatory” given the historical loss given default (LGD) ratio of 40-50%. And with NPL cover at 140% in 1H20, the bank reckons provisioning levels can absorb NPL ratios of up to 4-5%. The jump in NPL ratio from 1.28% to 1.95% was largely driven by consumers (ie mortgages and auto loans). With the grace period lifted in June, expect NPLs to rise further in 2H20. Meanwhile, BDO has rescheduled c5% of its loan book, mostly corporates whose short term cash flow is constrained. While restructuring could avert defaults, these loans require higher provisions as they move to Stage 2.

Loan growth to slow, but NIMs to stay firm into 2H20 on lower deposit costs

BDO admitted that credit standards have tightened, particularly for consumers, and for corporates in higher-risk sectors. Management expects loan growth to slow to single-digits by year-end from 11% in 2Q. However, NIMs are likely to stay firm into 2H20. Despite falling benchmark rates, loan yields are proving stickier. Meanwhile, BDO’s deposit costs have fallen to 0.81% in 1H20, near all-time lows, thanks to strong CASA growth (+19% yoy) in 2Q.

Fees recovering as activity normalizes; wary of new lockdown, moratoriums

Since the end of the hard lockdown, BDO’s branch network is nearly up to full operation, and management estimates branch activity is back to 90% of pre-Covid levels. Fee incomes are recovering more gradually, though, as banks have temporarily waived fees for online bank transfers. The General Community Quarantine (GCQ) restrictions remain, preventing a return to “normal” economic activity. Congress is working on several fiscal stimulus packages to jump-start consumption and investment demand. However, with Covid-19 cases on the rise, management is wary of new lockdowns and new loan moratoriums

 

Corporate News

JFC posts recurring net loss of P5bn in 1H20 – behind

JFC reported headline net loss of P12bn in 1H20 – this includes the one-time business transformation provision of P7bn. On a recurring basis, the net loss was c.P5bn. For 2Q20 alone, JFC posted a recurring net loss of P3.2bn, while the operating loss was P5.3bn. While we had expected 2Q to bear the full impact of the lockdowns globally, there may be downside risk to our 2020 estimates given the reimposition of MECQ in Metro Manila. The company had to close 50% of its store network globally at the beginning of 2Q, though operations for 88% of its stores had resumed as of end-June. Consolidated SSS declined 41% YoY mainly due to the absence of or limited dine-in operations (60-70% of store sales in the Philippines).

 

Belle Corp net loss of P238m in 2Q

Belle Corp incurred a net loss of P238mn in 2Q20, stemming from the suspension of gambling activities that began in March. The announcement of stricter quarantine measures beginning August 4 will most likely mean virtually zero gaming revenues for at least another quarter.

 

San Miguel Brewery’s 1H income falls 62%

In a media statement, San Miguel Corporation said that the brewery posted a net income of P5bn in 1H20, a decline of 62%yoy. Revenues fell 39% due to liquor restrictions and closure of bars/restaurants. The brewery’s 1Q20 profit fell 44% due to a late price increase and the impact of the first two weeks of the lockdown. Demand for beer and liquor products remain strong, and if June and July are any indication we’re seeing signs of a strong recovery in the second half of the year. San Miguel Corporation and subsidiaries will hold a briefing on Aug 6 at 4PM.

 

Petron incurs P14.2bn net loss in 1H20

Petron Corp. (PCOR PM) recorded a net loss of P14.2bn in 1H20 vs. 1H19 profit of P2.6bn. Revenues dropped by 40% to P152.4bn during the period. The company cited pandemic and low international oil prices.  These issues were compounded by inventory losses of P15bn.

 

 

Macro and Political News

OFW remittances drop 19% in May

 

OFW remittances fell 19% YoY to US$2.106bn in May. In PHP terms, remittances fell 22%. This is the biggest decline seen in nearly two decades. The biggest drops were seen from the USA (-13%), the Middle East (-39%), and sea-based (-12%). More than 115k OFWs have been repatriated from Feb-Aug 1 due to the pandemic, according to the Dept. of Foreign Affairs. Around 68k are land-based while 47k are sea-based. This brings YT-May remittances down 6.4% to US$11.6bn. The BSP forecasting remittances to drop by 5% this year

 

T-bills fully awarded as rates drop further

 

The 91-day T-bill fetched an average rate of 1.221%, down 11.4bps from last auction (BVAL: 1.388%). The 182-day T-bill fetched an average rate of 1.454%, down 15.1bps (BVAL: 1.609%) The 364-day T-bill fetched an average rate of 1.749%, down 0.9bps (BVAL: 1.794%). The Bureau of Treasury borrowed P20bn as planned; Total tenders reached P76bn

 

Gov’t expands 2021 budget proposal to P4.506tn

 

The proposed 2021 budget was increased to P4.506 tn from the initial P4.357 tn estimate according to Finance Undersecretary Gil Beltran. The new budget proposal accounts for 21.8% of GDP. It is 9.9% higher than the P4.1 trillion budget for 2020 and is also 6.6% higher than the 2020 expenditure target of P4.225 tn. The increased budget reflects expectations of a vaccine by end-2020 (vs previous expectation of Aug/Sep 2020). The spending plan will “sustain” government initiatives for COVID-19 pandemic response.

 

Gov’t awards first two packages of PNR Clark Phase 2 project

 

The Dept. of Transportation awarded the first two construction packages for the 53-km PNR North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR) Phase 2 project over the weekend. Package N-04 was awarded to Acciona Construction Philippines and EEI Corp. (EEI PM). N-04 will cover 6.3km of the main line and 1.6km of the depot’s access line, with one underground station serving Clark airport. Meanwhile, package N-05 was awarded to South Korea’s POSCO Engineering and Construction. N-05 will cover the Clark Railway Depot (33 hectares). The entire NSCR project is estimated to cost P777.5bn and will span 148 km, connecting Metro Manila and Clark airport in Pampanga

 

 

 

Gov’t reverts to MECQ in Metro Manila, other areas

 

President Duterte approved to place Metro Manila and other areas under the stricter modified enhanced community quarantine (MECQ) from Aug. 4 to 18. Other areas placed under MECQ include Cavite, Rizal, Bulacan, and Laguna. Under MECQ, public transportation and non-essential services are prohibited to operate

 

BOP surplus at US$80mn in June

 

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$80mn in June. This is a reversal from the US$404mn BOP deficit in the same period last year. However, this was smaller than the US$2.4bn surplus in May 2020. This brings 1H20 BOP surplus to US$4.11bn, lower than the US$4.79bn surplus a year ago.

 

PSE raises minimum public ownership requirement for small IPOs

 

The PSE now requires the following minimum public ownership (MPO) for IPOs.

 

- Market cap below P500m – 33%

 

- Market cap from P500m to P1bn – 25%

 

- Market cap above P1bn – 20% 

 

The company must maintain the MPO of 20% even after listing. The 20% requirement also applies to companies that list by way of introduction or backdoor listing. Since 2017, the PSE has required all IPOs to have a minimum public float of 20%
