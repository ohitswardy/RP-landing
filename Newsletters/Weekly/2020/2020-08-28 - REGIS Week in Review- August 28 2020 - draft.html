Philippine Consumer - 2Q20: the worst is likely over
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

The worst is likely over
The sector has likely seen the worst in 2Q20, with aggregate sector earnings dropping 52% YoY (-29% YoY ex-restaurants) as strict lockdown measures weighed on consumer mobility. Restaurants and non-essential retail were among the hardest hit amidst temporary store closures, while consumer staples and grocery retailers bucked the trend. While we expect trends to sequentially improve in the coming quarters, a more meaningful recovery in consumer sentiment will depend on the easing of lockdown restrictions and fiscal stimulus. The adoption of online services (shopping, food delivery, payments) has accelerated, and companies will need to adapt more quickly to an environment which is changing even more rapidly.

Recovery hinged on further easing of lockdown restrictions, fiscal stimulus
Channel checks suggest that top line trends had improved sequentially in JuneJuly, but took another pause as the Greater Manila area shifted back to a stricter lockdown (“MECQ”) in early August. While we believe that the worst is likely behind us, the pace of the recovery of the sector will be largely depend on (1) the further easing of lockdown restrictions, which should help improve consumer mobility, and (2) fiscal stimulus, to help drive demand amidst falling OFW remittances and rising unemployment

Sector earnings drop 52% in 2Q20; restaurants, retail hit hardest
Aggregate sector earnings declined 52% YoY in 2Q20, dragged by losses for the restaurants, which was among the sectors hardest hit by lockdown restrictions. Bucking the trend were consumer staples and grocery retailers. Same-store sales for the restaurants and non-essential retail formats plunged 50-60% as stores and shopping malls were shut. Consequently, beer volumes fell more than 60% as bars and restaurants were temporarily closed. Liquor volumes, however, were more resilient, falling 10% YoY. Even as lockdown restrictions gradually eased towards the end of the quarter, foot traffic in commercial and retail establishments continued to be restricted by the limited availability of public transportation, imposition of curfews, and other measures (e.g. “quarantine passes”). The adoption of online services (shopping, food delivery, payments) has accelerated as consumers are confined to their homes.

Defensive plays – PGOLD, URC; recovery play – JFC
We continue to favor defensive names given the uncertain environment – Puregold (PGOLD PM) and Universal Robina (URC PM) would be our top picks. Meanwhile we view the food service sector companies, particularly Jollibee (JFC PM), as recovery plays that could see a sharp rebound once the economy opens up

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Banks - Cap on credit card ratesproposed; new loan grace period
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 - rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Pressed into national service?
The Bayanihan 2 Act –which includes a new 2-month “grace period” on loan payments – will likely become law soon. Meanwhile, the BSP is proposing an interest rate cap of 24%on credit card receivables, about half the ratethat banks charged today. It is difficult to estimate the P&L impact of the “national service” banks are being made to take. The unintended consequencemight be a growing reluctance to lend.

Flying blind, again – loan grace period for another 2 months
The Bayanihan to Recover as One Act (or Bayanihan 2) has been ratified by both the House and Senate, and will likely be signed into law soon. As with its earlier iteration, Bayanihan 2 includes a new 2-month “grace period” on loan payments, although banks can continue to charge interest during the period. However, as we noted in our report “Flying blind”(20 May), banks will have much less visibility on asset quality and the continued creditworthiness of their borrowers.

BSP proposes 24% cap on credit cards
Meanwhile, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has sought comments on a draft Circular that aims to cap finance charges imposed on credit card balances to an effective annual interest rate of 24%. Currently, credit cards charge about 3.25% a month on unpaid balances, which annualizes to roughly 47%. Credit cards also offer installment programs (eg appliances, tuition, etc), but these tend to have effective interest rates below 24%

“Meaningful” impact, but tough to quantify
Credit cards accountfor 3.4% to 5.8% of total loansof banks we cover. But the high yields mean the revenue impact is much greater. In its FY19 briefing, BPI reckoned the yield on credit cards was 19%, more than 3x the average for the whole loan book (see righthand column). It is unclear, though, how much of banks’ credit card books will be covered by the proposed rate cap. One senior bank official merely said that the impact would be “meaningful”.

Unintended consequences – slower loan growth?
The potential unintended consequence of grace periods and loan rate caps could be further reluctance of banks to lend. As of June, loan growth slowed to 5.3% yoy (from 11% yoy in 1Q). More telling is that June loan balances were already down 2.5% qoq vs the end-March levels.

Philippine Property - 2Q20 recap: mall operations plummet; office segment holding up
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 - carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A quarter to forget
The property industry was severely affected by the hard lockdown until end-May, particularly the mall and residential segments. The office segment was more resilient as BPOs continued to operate and in some cases, expand. We expect office property to remain the least affected by the COVID pandemic and continue to believe that Megaworld offers the best risk-reward in the sector in light of its PEZA-accredited office portfolio, low gearing, and cheap valuations.

Mall revenue drops 70%-80%
The ECQ was especially hard on the mall segment. For most of 2Q20, only 20% of space was operational and most mall operators granted full rent condonation to all non-operating tenants. As a result, mall revenue of most of the developers we cover fell 70%-80% yoy in 2Q20. Despite the transition to GCQ in June, foot traffic generally remained below 40% of pre-COVID levels and mall operators handed out substantial rent discounts to tenants that chose to open.
A hard lockdown was reimposed in Mega Manila from August 4 to 18, interrupting the fragile recovery that was taking place. In addition to the inability to operate during the lockdown period, we believe it would also push back the timetable of a full recovery.

Residential sales plummet, then rebound in June
2Q20 reservation sales plunged, with total sales of the developers we cover down 41% yoy. On the bright side, June reservation sales picked up rather remarkably from the April-May lows. We are cautious, however, about extrapolating June sales to the rest of the year.
Our channel checks show that in the secondary market, most offer prices were down 5-10% yoy, with a few units being offered at prices that are 15%-20% lower. In the primary market, developers have generally offered discounts within 5%, with a few developers also loosening payment schemes.

Office: the most resilient segment but cracks in POGOs emerge 2Q20 office revenue of the developers we cover rose 9%. There were very few office contract terminations and nearly all office tenants continued to pay rent promptly. BPO companies, which occupy 70%-90% of the office space of developers we cover, continued to operate (even during the hard lockdown). However, not everything is rosy in this segment. Colliers estimates that Metro Manila office vacancy inched up from 4% in 1Q20 to 5% in 2Q20, as the POGO office footprint shrank slightly. It also said that spot rental rates fell 5-10% qoq

Philippine Telecoms - 2Q20 recap: More than defensive
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 - gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

The new growth phase
While most industries in the Philippines were badly hit in 2Q due to the pandemic, the telecom sector has proven itself to be among the handful of exceptions. Reduced mobility, both voluntary and because of government restrictions, trimmed wireless revenue in the quarter but the foundations for an acceleration in data usage are being set. We expect this to become more apparent in the near future, particularly in home broadband.

Industry-wide wireless revenue dips
Combined wireless revenue of the two operators dipped 3% in 2Q from a year ago and 4% from 1Q20 as personal mobility fell dramatically (based on Google mobility data) in the period. The dip in wireless revenue, however, is by no means an indication that data usage or telecom spend is falling. Rather, we believe, it reflects a shift in usage patterns from mobile to home-based internet for those who can afford or who have access to home broadband.

PLDT gains market share of wireless revenue
We estimate PLDT gained another 1.3ppt of share of wireless revenue in 2Q as it managed to raise wireless revenue in the midst of the pandemic and the associated lockdowns. On our numbers, PLDT has recovered 5ppt of market share since the start of 2019 and is now at ~48%.

Mobile data usage still rising briskly but data yields are now at US$0.5 per GB

An estimated 1,340 petabytes of mobile data traffic went through PLDT and Globe in the quarter. This is 16% higher than 1Q20 and 76% over the previous year. Yields (revenue per GB), however, also fell 16% QoQ and are now at a combined PHP25 (US$0.5) per GB. For PLDT alone, revenue yield on data was P20/GB in 2Q.


Home broadband sees growth spurt
With many people confined (voluntarily or involuntarily) to their homes, data usage on fixed broadband is rising. From a combined base of 5.1m as of March 2020, the two telcos added 953k subscribers to their home broadband business in 2Q alone, mostly on their Fixed Wireless service. With many employees now working from home and young people expected to begin schooling soon under distance learning (“study from home”) arrangements, demand for data can only go up

Philippine Gaming - China issues blacklist for overseas gambling destinations
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 - aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

China steps up campaign against gambling overseas
Several news articles reported that China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism has established a blacklist of overseas tourist destinations that promote gambling to mainland Chinese citizens. While the announcement did not specify how the blacklist will be implemented, it signals closer monitoring of junket operations, proxy betting and online casinos in overseas jurisdictions that allow these activities. The move highlights how Chinese authorities have stepped up their crackdown on illegal cross-border money flows which began last year. 

A warning to the Philippines? 
The announcement did not say which countries have been included in the so called blacklist, nor did it specify exact guidelines. Over the past 18 months, however, Chinese authorities have specifically rebuked online casinos in the Philippines and Cambodia for facilitating capital outflow through underground banks. Other than potential travel restrictions on countries that explicitly target Chinese players, the renewed crackdown could mean tighter monitoring of cross-border money flows, proxy betting and online casinos.  

How much do casinos generate from Chinese players?
Prior to the pandemic-induced closures, we estimate that Philippine integrated resorts such as Solaire of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) generated about one third of gross gaming revenues (GGR) from Chinese players. We also estimate that proxy betting through junkets contributed as much as 15% of total GGR at its peak, prior to the first wave of crackdowns last year. The Philippine casinos may have to focus its efforts, then, on local players (roughly 45% of GGR) once the government gives the green light to open its facilities to the public. As it stands, travel restrictions on inbound foreigners are still in place.  

Possible repercussions on POGO, VIP expansion plans
The announcement also has potential repercussions on the recovery of Philippine Offshore Gaming operators (POGO) – online casinos typically run by Chinese, as these employ up to 400,000 employees who fly in from the mainland. Some POGO staff have been unable to return to Manila because of the travel restrictions. These recent warnings from authorities could further slow the return of POGOs to full operations even after the lockdowns are lifted. On the other hand, Suncity Group, a junket operator that has VIP rooms in all Philippine integrated resorts, is constructing its own US$700mn casino hotel in Entertainment City Manila under a revenue sharing agreement with Travellers/Alliance Global Group (AGI PM). Long term strategies may have to be reassessed, depending on how the enforcement climate develops

 

  

 

Corp News

Competition watchdog clears Razon's entry in Manila Water

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) approved the acquisition of Trident Water in Manila Water (MWC PM). The PCC asserted that the deal does not strengthen Trident/Prime Metroline’s incentive to engage in input or customer foreclosure in the market for the supply of raw water in the East Zone. Raw water supply project is meant to service the East zone beyond the lifetime of Manila Water’s concession. Trident Water is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Enrique Razon’s holding company, Prime Metroline Hldgs. Mr. Razon is buying a 25% (=820mn shares) economic stake in MWC at P13/sh; Assignment of proxy rights on participating prefs will give Mr. Razon a 51% controlling stake. The transaction will dilute Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) economic stake in MWC to 39% from 51% and voting stake to 32% from 80%.

SM Prime looking to raise US$500mn to US$1bn from REIT listing

SM Prime is reportedly seeking to raise US$500mn to US$1bn from a REIT listing of its Philippine malls in 2021. SM Prime President Jeffrey Lim said the company has not issued a formal mandate. Philippine REIT regulations require a minimum public ownership of 33%. We currently value the company’s Philippine malls at P516bn (US$10.6bn) or P17.90/share which is based on 7% cap rate. We estimate that a 1 percentage point drop in the assigned cap rate would raise NAV by 7%. The company intends to spend P15bn for the first 60 hectares of its reclamation project. P165bn for 660 hectares.

Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) signs franchise for Singapore milk tea brand

PIZZA signed a master franchise agreement to bring in Singaporean milk tea brand R&B to the Philippines. R&B currently has over 1,000 outlets worldwide. It is owned by the Koufu Group, one of the largest operators and managers of food courts in Singapore. Under the agreement, PIZZA can put up stand-alone stores or co-brand in select Shakey’s and Peri-Peri outlets.

House grants 25-year franchise for 3rd telco player DITO

The House of Representatives approved on third and final reading a measure that renews the franchise of DITO Telecommunity Corp for another 25 years. The house approved House Bill 7332 – with 240 yes votes and 7 no votes – granting it a 25-year franchise. This allows it to construct, install, establish, operate and maintain wired and/or wireless telecommunications system.

Genting HK talks with creditors after announcing suspension of payments

Genting Hong Kong held a meeting with creditors after announcing that it would temporarily suspend all payments to the group’s financial creditors while seeking additional funding. This is part becasue cruise ship business has been suspended due to the pandemic. The flings show an outstanding borrowing of US$3.37bn. Genting HK is the joint venture partner of Alliance Global in Travellers International – the operator of Resorts World Manila.

Gov’t raises issues on GMR-Megawide’s NAIA proposal

The NEDA-ICC raised issues on the financial capacity and joint and solidary liability agreement of the GMR-Megawide consortium according to Dept. of Transportation Secretary Ruben Reinoso. The government will meet with the proponent to discuss issues. Last July 15, the government awarded the consortium of GMR-Megawide “Original Proponent Status” (OPS) for the P100bn NAIA rehab project. This came after the gov’t revoked the OPS granted to the Superconsortium. The Superconsortium failed to get the govt’s approval to revise project terms to reflect the impact of the pandemic.

Citicore to build more solar capacity in Luzon

Citicore Power expects the installed energy capacity of its renewables subsidiary to reach 238 MW by 2021. The company will start building a P3.3bn, 75 MW solar plant under a 50:50 JV with AC Energy (ACEN PM) by 4Q21. Citicore Power is a wholly-owned power subsidiary of Citicore. Citicore is the parent company of Megawide Corp (MWIDE PM), owning 66% of MWIDE.

House panel approves tax perks for San Miguel’s Bulacan airport project

The House ways and means committee approved a proposal to exempt San Miguel Corp’s. (SMC PM) Bulacan airport project from all taxes for at least 10 years. During the 10-year construction period, San Miguel Aerocity shall be exempt from any and all direct and indirect taxes and fees of any kind. At the end of the construction period, San Miguel Aerocity will remain exempt from income tax and taxes on real estate, buildings and property for the remainder of its franchise. The tax exemptions will expire “as soon as it is determined by a competent authority that the grantee has fully recovered its investment cost” (or project IRR of 12%) on the airport project. Tax exemptions were included in the substitute version of House Bill No. 7241 which seeks to grant San Miguel Aerocity, Inc. a 50-year franchise. Meanwhile, Finance Assistant Secretary Maria Teresa Habitan said that the department is “not okay” with the proposed grant of tax incentives.

China to black list overseas destinations that promote gambling

According to several news reports, China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism announced on Wednesday that it has established a blacklist of overseas tourist destinations that promote gambling for mainland Chinese customers. Cities that will be included in the blacklist are “endangering the personal and property safety of Chinese citizens”. The announcement did not mention any specifics – the countries included in the blacklist, how the travel restrictions will be implemented, etc. Other than potential travel restrictions, the renewed crackdown could mean tighter monitoring of cross-border money flow, proxy betting and online casinos. Philippine Integrated resorts like Bloomberry generate one third of gross gaming revenues from Chinese players. The sharp increase in Chinese arrivals to the Philippines in recent years have been linked to widespread operations of Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGO), online casinos that are typically run by and target Chinese individuals.

Manila Water closes bottled water business

Manila Water (MWC PM) is permanently closing Manila Water Total Solutions Corp. (MWTS) on Oct. 31. MWTS Healthy Family Business Division will close due to recurring losses and the inability to financially sustain its business operations. MWC launched the Healthy Family brand in 2015. MWTS booked EBITDA loss and net loss of P29mn and P169mn, respectively, in 2019.

 

  

 

Macro News

BSP keeps benchmark rates steady; Inflation forecast raised

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas maintained the overnight reverse repurchase rate at an all-time low of 2.25%. They cited manageable inflation and improvement in domestic economic activity post-lockdown backed a “prudent pause”.  The BSP also upgraded its 2020 inflation forecast to 2.6% from 2.3% in June. The  2021 and 2022 forecasts already raised to 3% (vs. 2.6% previously) and 3.1% (3.0%), respectively.

Bicam approves Bayanihan II of up to P165bn

The Bicameral Conference Committee approved the Bayanihan II bill. The Bill will provide P140bn in regular appropriations and P25bn in standby appropriations, if the funds become available. Bayanihan II retained the P5-8k cash assistance to low-income households, but it will now be limited to areas under “specific quarantine conditions” and to households will recently-returned OFWs. A 60-day grace period for the payment of all "existing, current, and outstanding loans," falling due on or before December 31, 2020, irrespective if there is community quarantine or not, will be implemented. Meanwhile, payments of residential and commercial rents, and utility bills falling due within the period of ECQ or Modified ECQ shall also be covered by a minimum of 30-day grace period. Once passed into law, Bayanihan II will be effective until Dec 19, 2020.

Proposed 2021 Budget of P4.506tn allocates more for public works, transport agencies

The Dept. of Budget and Management released details of its proposed budget for 2021. The proposed P4.506tn budget included higher allocations for Dept. of Public Works (+52% YoY) and Dept. of Transportation (+71% YoY). 

Fiscal deficit swells to P140bn in July

The country recorded a budget deficit of P140bn in July, up 86% YoY. Revenues dropped 11% YoY to P235bn, as collections from the BIR and BOC dropped 12% and 9% respectively. Meanwhile, expenses rose 10.4% YoY due to the second tranche releases for the social amelioration program. This brings 7M20 fiscal deficit to P700.6bn (vs. govt’s FY20 target of P1.8tn deficit, 9.6% of GDP). Revenues in 7M20 are down by 7% YoY to P1.69tn, accounting for 67% of the govt’s target (P2.5tn, -20% YoY). 7M20 expenditures up by 24% YoY to P2.39tn, accounting for 55% of FY20 target (P4.34tn, +14% YoY)

Hot money outflow hits US$453.17mn in July

Foreign portfolio investments hit a net outflow of US$453.17mn in July, a reversal of last year’s net inflow of US$15.02mn. From January to July, the Philippines recorded a net outflow of US$7.76bn vs a net outflow of US$706m vs 28mn in the same period last year. The BSP said attributed the larger outflows to uncertainties due to the pandemic, geopolitical and trade tensions and corporate governance issues involving the water concessionaires. The BSP lowered its foreign portfolio investments projection to a net inflow of US$2.4bn, from an original target of US$8.2bn.

 

DOF expects P249bn revenue loss over three years due to Tax Package 2

The Department of Finance estimates that from 2020-2022, Tax Package 2 will lead to P249bn in lower tax collections, largely due to the immediate five percentage point reduction in the corporate income tax rate. Tax Package 2, which is also called Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act or CREATE, seeks to lower the corporate income tax rate from 30% to 25% and extend the applicability of net operating loss carry over (NOLCO) from 3 years to 5 years. Additionally, it also plans to extend the sunset period for companies currently enjoying tax incentives.
