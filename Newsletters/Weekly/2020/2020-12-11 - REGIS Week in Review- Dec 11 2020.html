REGIS REPORT

11 December 2020

The PCOMP continued to gain WoW and was up another +1.6% this week. Most of the gains came in on Friday. The index was just trading within the 7100-7200 range the whole week but broke out to end the week at 7,246. Foreigners continue to be net sellers again but slightly less WoW. This week we saw a net outflow of -$11m vs last week’s outflow of -$49m. On a per stock basis, top net foreign inflows were seen in Ayala Land +$9.3m, SM Prime +$6.9m and SM Investments +$5m. Meanwhile, outflows were seen in Metrobank -$4.8m, Metro Pacific -$3.9m and Meralco -$3.3m. Average daily turnover dropped to $192m from $202m WoW.

This week’s top index gainers were Robinsons Land +7%, DMC +5.2% and Universal Robina Corp. +5%. Meanwhile, the top index loser were all conglomerates. Metro Pacific was down -3.5% while Ayala Corp and Aboitiz Equity Ventures were both down over 2%.

Metro Pacific dropped mid-week but recovered as the week ended. Its subsidiary operating the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX) had its business permit for the city of Valenzuela suspended the other day. The mayor of the city suspended its business permit following massive traffic jams because of bottlenecks at its toll plazas. The toll road operator began implementing a purely cashless (i.e., automated, RFID) toll collection system last week but the transition has not been smooth. MPI is attributing the problem to many commuters having insufficient credit balances in their accounts as many are new to the system. This was compounded by complaints about faulty RFID detectors or tags. Math will tell you that even a small delay in average service times could lead to an exponential increase in waiting times (queues). The business permit suspension means the NLEX will not be able to collect revenue on the seven toll road exits within Valenzuela City. These account for 10% of NLEX revenue and each day means a loss of P2m in after-tax profit for MPI.

Earlier this week, it was reported that the Philippine banking sector NPL ratio increased 22bps MoM to 3.80% in October, up from 3.58% in September (revised upwards from 3.50% as previously reported). In nominal terms, NPLs jumped 5.6% MoM to P391bn. Restructured loans (RLs) also rose 4.8% MoM, with the ratio rising to 1.28% of loans. This takes the “stressed” loan level to 5.1%. Additionally, NPL coverage continued to slip, now below 90% at 88.8%. Meanwhile loan growth slowed further to 0.8% YoY, with loan balances now down 4.3% year-to-date. It would be interesting to see how these numbers look in the coming months.

The govt finally looks like to have made progress in the 2021 budget. Congress passed the 2021 national budget, which amounted to P4.5trn worth of spending (vs P4.1trn for 2020). Among the departments receiving the biggest budget increases were Health (+62% vs 2020) and Public Works (+58%). The allocation for the DOH includes a large lump sum for the procurement of vaccines, while the increase for DPWH is likely reflective of the administration’s view that infrastructure spending is the best way to boost the economy.  Also notable was the large cut in allocation for the Transportation department (-52%). With this approval, the government will hopefully avoid a repeat of the 2019 experience which led to severe underspending. We also note the plan by Congress to allow the budget allocations for 2020, including those under the Bayanihan Acts, to be carried over into next year. Assuming the government can deploy the cash (which is a big IF), we may get a substantial lift from government spending next year. Experience, however, has already taught us to rein in our expectations.

Corp News

Smart to upgrade 40% of sites to 5G by end of next year

Smart Communications, the wireless arm of PLDT, plans to upgrade 40% of its sites to 5G by end of 2021. In a press release, it said it will upgrade 4,000 of its bas stations to 5G next year, along with deploying an additional 4,000 LTE bas stations nationwide. The company rolled out 279 base stations in 3Q to support its 5G network.

Dito begins friendly user tests

Dito, the telecom firm jointly owned by the Davao Dennis Uy and China Telecom, is beginning ‘friendly user tests’ ahead of its technical audit. The tests are being conducted via selected participants and in selected areas. Dito’s technical audit is supposed to be done in a month’s time (Jan 7). The company is committed to deliver 37% population coverage and 27mbps average wireless data speed by that time.

MPI, Keppel Infrastructure Trust offer to buy tank storage company

MPI and Keppel Infrastructure Trust is buying 100% of Philippine Tank Storage International (PTSI). PTSI has the largest petroleum products import storage facility in the country with high double-digit EBITDA margins. Management indicated that the PTSI will immediately contribute to earnings. Keppel Infrastructure said it would pay an estimated US$267mn for 80% of PTSI while MPI will acquire 20% for US$67mn. As at end-September, MPI had gross cash of P25bn and gross debt of P80bn at the parent level. The acquisition is expected to be completed in January.

NLEX Corp. seeking 'dialogues' with Valenzuela on permit, RFID

NLEX Corp. will seek “a series of dialogues” with Valenzuela City Mayor Rex T. Gatchalian. Mayor Gatchalian revoked NLEX’s business permit over the heavy traffic caused by the implementation of its cashless toll payment system. Without a business permit, NLEX Corp. cannot collect toll fees at the seven toll plazas within the jurisdiction of Valenzuela City. The toll plazas include Karuhatan-Mindanao Avenue Toll Plaza, Karuhatan-Harborlink Toll Plaza, Karuhatan-MacArthur Highway Sub-Exit, Mindanao Avenue Toll Plaza, Paso de Blas Toll Plaza North Bound, Paso de Blas Toll Plaza South Bound, and Lawang Bato Toll Booth. These toll plazas account for c. 10% of NLEX’s revenues while NLEX accounts for 20% of MPI’s NAV and profit. A resolution asking the Senate to review concession deals of toll operators has been filed in the chamber following issues in implementing the cashless payments system. MPI is trading at 22% discount to its market NAV (P5.80/sh).

SN Aboitiz Power is looking to scale up floating solar portfolio after its pilot project over Magat Dam has proven its viability

SNAP switched on its first 200-kw floating solar project over Magat Dam in June 2019. The facility provides internal power to SNAP-Magat’s facilities. The SNAP board has approved the project to proceed to engineering design for 67MW and is looking at other locations for floating solar expansion. SN Power was acquired by Scatec Solar from Norfund last October 2020. Scatec Solar is an independent solar power based power producer based in Norway.

Robinsons Land office REIT could raise as much as US$500mn

Bloomberg reported that Robinsons Land could list an office REIT in 1H21. The REIT IPO could raise as much as US$500mn (P24bn) and implies a market capitalization of up to P72bn. We estimate this would value the office properties at 5-6% cap rate. Robinsons Land previously said that it would spin off over 400,000sqm of its 615,000sqm office portfolio into a REIT next year. We estimate that spinning off RLC’s entire office portfolio into a REIT could raise its NAV by 5-12% (from the current P35/share). For comparison, AREIT has an office portfolio of 170,000sqm (245,000sqm when the 30th is acquired) and a market cap of P28.2bn. RLC is trading at 12x FY21E P/E, 0.9 FY21E P/B, and  45% discounted to NAV.

PAGCOR Chair Andrea Domingo discusses online bets for land-based casinos in AG Brief forum

In an online forum hosted by AG Brief, PAGCOR Chairman Andrea Domingo talked about the online gaming for approved land-based casinos. The online gaming platform/license will be called “Liveshots”. There will be various conditions for players and operators. The cost of license will include a P100,000 application fee and 25% of GGR as PAGCOR fee and 5% franchise tax on GGR by BIR. So far, approved land-based casinos are Solaire, Okada Manila, City of Dreams Manila and one casino hotel in Subic. In the same forum, Danny Too, General Manager of Cherry Interactive discussed alternatives to the Philippines POGO framework. He deemed the 5% tax on “turnover” a killer blow to the industry and now Malaysia, Dubai and Myanmar are being pursued as alternatives for the existing POGOs.

Cebu Pacific sees tough road ahead

Cebu Pacific expects a tough road ahead towards recovery, but is confident it will be able to conquer 2021 better according to Cebu Pacific president and CEO Lance Gokongwei. CEB expects to lose as much as P25bn in 2020 and as of 9M20, CEB’s net loss stood at P14.7bn. CEB is currently operating at 16% of its pre-Covid capacity, up from only 10% from June to November.

 
 

Macro News

Inflation jumps to 3.3% in November

Headline inflation rose to 3.3% in November from 2.5% the previous month. This was slightly higher than BSP’s expectations (2.4-3.3%). The uptick in inflation was due to higher food inflation (e.g. fish, fruits, and vegetables) due to weather-related supply disruptions while the African Swine Fever (ASF) continued to adversely affect pork supply. Inflation for alcoholic beverages and tobacco also accelerated. Meanwhile, Core inflation rose to 3.2% from 3.0% in October. This brings YT-November inflation to 2.6%, within the government’s target range of 2-4%.

Bicameral panel approves FIST bill

The Bicameral Conference Committee approved the Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer (FIST) bill. FIST bill will allow financial institutions to offload bad loans to asset management companies. The Bill will likely be ratified by Congress ahead of the Christmas break (Dec 19).

Sin tax collections exceed revised target in October

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and Bureau of Customs (BoC) collected P23.238bn in “sin” taxes, beating the reduced P20.52bn target. This was 9% higher than the P21.332bn generated in the same month last year, but 20% less than the P29.2bn collected in September. Broken down, excise tax collections from tobacco products hit P14.11bn in October, up 34.3%YoY and beating the P12.44bn target. Taxes from alcoholic beverages fell 3.5% to P6.967bn from P7.22bn in the same period last year, but exceeded the P5.53bn target. Additionally, in the first 10 months, “sin” tax collections are 6.7% lower YoY at 212.94bn. Collections from tobacco are 3% lower YoY at P129.02bn while taxes from alcohol products are 8% lower YoY at P57.37bn.

Bicam approves 2021 P4.5tn budget

The Bicameral Conference Committee approved the 2021 P4.5tn national budget. The Bill will now be sent to President Rodrigo Duterte for his signature before Christmas. The Bicam agreed to increase the allocation for the health sector by 42% to P287.47bn. The increase was due to a P72.5bn vaccine allocation, of which P70bn will be “unprogrammed funds”. Meanwhile, the transportation sector’s budget was cut by almost 40% to P87bn (reason not cited).

Congress seeking to extend Bayanihan II and 2020 budget

Both the Senate and the House of Representatives are looking to extend the validity of Bayanihan II to March or June 2021 which is currently set to expire on December 19. The Bayanihan II law provides up to P165bn worth of assistance to sectors hit by the pandemic. Meanwhile, the House panel approved House Bill No. 6656, which seeks to extend the validity of the 2020 budget until December 2021. Senator Juan Edgardo Angara said the Senate is looking to extend some provisions of the P4.1trn 2020 national budget until June or December 2021.

Banks' bad loans hit record-high in October

The banking sector’s gross NPL ratio (ex-interbank) rose to 3.80% in October. This is the highest on record since 2013 and higher than the 3.58% in September 2020. The gross non-performing loans rose 69.9% YoY and 5.56% MoM to P391.4bn. The allowance for credit losses have been trending below NPL over the past 2 months at P347.7bn as of October. Meanwhile, NPL coverage ratio sank to their record-low of 88.83% and non-performing assets surged 48.8% YoY to P504.83bn. Including restructured loans, total distressed assets now account for 5.72% of banks’ real estate properties.
