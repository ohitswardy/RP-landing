REGIS REPORT

06 July 2020

Market Commentary

The PSEi had another lackluster week, ending five trading sessions flat (-0.05% WoW).  Investors remained uninspired as the easing lockdown shifted concern to a smooth economic recovery.

Two pieces of macro data coming out this week underpinned these concerns. Overseas remittances came in up by 2.5% in February to US$2.36bn. Even before the full impact of the pandemic, flows from the Middle East are already down and those from sea-based workers look tenuous as well.

Meanwhile, the banking sector NPL ratio (ex-interbank) increased to 2.26% in 1Q2020 (vs 2.08% at end-2019).  NPL cover also deteriorated to 86%. While the increases seem small, the rate of growth is more pronounced.  In nominal terms, NPLs have been rising in the the 15-25%+ range since 2018. The NPL ratio should deteriorate faster due to the lockdown.

The P568bn stimulus bill (Philippine Economic Stimulus Act or PESA) to address the economic effects of Covid-19 is moving forward, as the House of Rep Economic Stimulus Sub-committee has submitted it to the Defeat COVID-19 Committee for second reading.  It includes P110bn of wage subsidies, P40bn for the loan guarantee fund and P25bn for investment in businesses under crisis. At US$11bn, the version being advanced is relatively modest.

Meantime, the two chambers seem to be closing in on an agreement on Tax Reform Package 2 (renamed from CITIRA to CREATE or  Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act). As far as listed companies are concerned, the key change is the immediate reduction in the corporate income tax rate from 30% to 25% effective July 2020. The previous House version had the rate falling to 20% slowly over a decade. This could immediately increase earnings by 8% — i.e. post tax goes from 70% to 75% of pre-tax profits.

Market volumes were extremely light - averaging only US$80m daily.  Foreign flows still headed out – with net outflows this week at US$34m.  Largest outflows this week were in BDO (US$8m) and Universal Robina (US$6m).

There was a barrage of 1Q results this week – all offering a glimpse to the extent of damage arising from the pandemic and lockdowns.

SM Investments’ (+3.3% WoW) 1Q earnings dropped 16% as contributions from SM Prime (unch), BDO (-3.7% WoW), and SM Retail all muted profits. SM Retail saw 1Q earnings fall 56% to P1.2bn on a loss from the department stores due to  temporary government-mandated shutdowns in mid-March. SM Prime meanwhile saw a 16% decline in Philippine mall revenue, pushing earning down 5% in 1Q.

Meanwhile, GT Capital’s (-7.0% WoW) 1Q net profit was down 16% YoY, muted by higher provisions at Metrobank (-4.2% WoW) as well as the drop in Toyota’s auto sales. Retail vehicle sales fell 23% due to both the Taal eruption and the pandemic.

Philippine Seven’s (+0.4% WoW)  profits were also affected by the lockdown (starting mid-March) with net profit falling 7%.  Filinvest Land’s (-4.0% WoW) residential revenue for the quarter also dropped, resulting in a 25% 1Q20 net profit contraction.

First Gen (-1.7% WoW ) reported recurring income of US$65m for 1Q, down 15% YoY. Recurring earnings came in at $60m, down 16% YoY, as contributions from FG Hydro and Avion declined.

Nonetheless, LT Group (+2.9% WoW)  actually managed to turn in a strong set of results, with 1Q20 earnings rising 41%, putting it on track to meet Regis’ FY20 estimates. LTG’s share of tobacco earnings from PMFTC alone amounted to P5bn for the quarter, while contributions from the others dipped.

PUBLIC RESEARCH
Philippine Banks: Flying blind
 
 
by Rafa Garchitorena — rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Asset quality – how bad could it get?Covid-19 has quickly shifted the focus to banks’ potential asset quality and NPLs. While banks kicked up provisions in 1Q20, one wonders – is it enough? Due to regulatory relief packages though, new NPL formation will only be recorded in 3Q20 at the earliest, potentially even 2021. Flying blind, we assume NPLs will rise from sub-2% in 2019 to 4-5% by 2021 (well below the post-1998 peak). Low ROAs mean our forecasts are slashed by 35-40% in 2020F and by 60-70% in 2021.

Flying blind – 1Q20 provisions jump 2x to 19x yoy; but is it enough?Operating results were in line or ahead; NIMs were stable, and trading/fx gains were strong (ex-BDO), allowing banks to raise provisions from 76% to 1,800% yoy. Since the ECQ lockdown from 16 March, though, a loan moratorium means there will be no new past due accounts until late 2Q20. And regulatory relief measures could delay NPL recognition to late 3Q20 at the earliest, or even into 2021. Worryingly, NPLs were already rising even before Covid-19.

Lessons from 1998 and 2008 – our base case: NPLs to peak at 4-5% in 2021EThe current crisis has no precedent in the Philippines in the last 40 years. After the 1998 Asian Financial Crisis, NPLs peaked at 17% in 2001. Including foreclosures and restructurings, “distressed” loans peaked at 35%. That said, the government stands ready to launch aggressive fiscal, monetary and regulatory measures to support the post-Covid economic recovery. We adjust our forecasts to assume sector NPLs will peak at 4-5% by end-2021 (from 1.8% in 1Q2020).

Forecasts cut 35-70% in 2020/2021E; TPs cut 25-40%Much higher credit cost assumptions (100-240bps) have us cut our earnings estimates by 35-50% in 2020E, and by 50-70% in 2021E – well below consensus – before rebounding strongly in 2022E. We cut target prices by 25-40% to levels that are at or near the bottom of the Street. We have Buys on MBT and SECB (up from Hold), and Holds on BDO and BPI (down from Buy) on valuations. We remain wary, though, as earnings are highly sensitive to NPL and credit costs.

 
GT Capital (GTCAP PM): Taal eruption, pandemic weigh on 1Q20 profit
 
 
by Klyne Resullar — klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q20 core profit falls 16%Headline 1Q20 profit dropped 26% YoY to P2.54bn. Stripping out one-offs at Metro Pacific (MPI PM, Alert: Pandemic blunts tollroad, water, rail 1Q20 profits, 6 May 2020), fair value adjustments, and pref dividends, recurring profit fell 16% YoY to P2.69bn. We attribute the underperformance to the drop in sales volumes at its auto unit, as well as higher provisions at the bank. Like most firms, GT Capital is taking a hit from the pandemic but we remain comfortable about its long-term outlook.

Auto: Disruptions due to Taal volcano eruption, COVID-19Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) 1Q20 profit fell by 20% to P1.43bn, after wholesale volumes dropped 21% YoY due to the Taal volcano eruption in January and the ECQ in mid- March. The drop in sales volumes more than offset margin improvements from a stronger PHP:USD rate, selective price adjustments last July 2019/Jan 2020, and favorable volume mix. After shutting its 71-strong dealer network and manufacturing plant in mid-March, TMP re-opened 20 dealerships last May 1 and will re-open 43 more dealerships across the Philippines from May 16-20. Meanwhile, its manufacturing plant will start on a single shift per day (vs. double shifts pre-COVID) and gradually ramp-up to meet demand. The first tranche of tax incentives under the CARS program could be deferred to the latter part of 2H20 due to regulatory delays caused by the ECQ.

Bank provisions double for COVID-19 impactIts banking arm recorded a 9% YoY drop in 1Q20 profit. Despite PPOP up 18% YoY driven by net interest income, the bank pre-emptively doubled provision expense for the likely increase in borrower stress due to the pandemic.

Property: Faster completions buoy 1Q20; 2020 launches deferredFederal Land saw a 97% YoY jump in 1Q20 profit, buoyed by faster completions and higher margins. Reservation sales rose 86% YoY to P7.9bn in 1Q20, thanks to three projects launched in 4Q19. However, given likely weak consumer confidence due to the pandemic, the company has deferred its plans to launch four new projects this year. It also plans to refinance all of its maturing loans this year and will unlikely need capital infusion from the parent.

Parent company liquidity ampleAs at end-March 2020, parent-level debt inclusive of prefs stood at P94.7bn while cash stood at P8.7bn. Management did not appear worried about reduced dividends from its investee companies this year, particularly dividends from TMP, but hinted that 2021 dividend inflows could be lower. The parent company has sufficient room to borrow, however, if needed.

 
LT Group (LTG PM)- 1Q: Tobacco unit lights up group’s profits
 
 
by Aaron Salvador — aaron.salavador@regis.ph
 

1Q20 profit up 41%LTG reported a net income of P6.21bn, an increase of 41% YoY and on track to meet our full year estimate. Contribution from its tobacco unit jumped 76%, more than offsetting the uninspired performances of its other subsidiaries.

Tobacco profits higher despite volume declineThe contribution of its tobacco unit jumped 76% to P4.99bn. Despite reporting a 9% decline in volumes for 1Q20, margins were much higher across its product line-up due to price hikes implemented last August (P20/pack, >20%). While the excise tax for tobacco already increased effective January 1, 2020 (+P10/pack), the magnitude of the profit increase suggests that the company was still going through old, lower-tax inventory during this period. We expect tobacco earnings to dip in subsequent quarters as the low tax inventory is depleted.

Core lending business doing well, but massive provisions bookedPNB’s profit fell 30% due to a massive P3.35bn provision (10x last year’s level), partly offset by huge trading gains (+160% yoy). Outside trading, its core business did well, with loans higher by 10% and NIMs expanding by 40bps compared to last year.

Beverage segments struggling with price hike impact ahead of Covid-related liquor banTanduay’s profit fell 15% as volumes dipped 4%. Meanwhile, Asia Brewery reported a 10% decline in profit due to larger equitized losses from its beer JV. Prices of alcoholic beverages were increased by around 20% to pass on the increase in excise tax implemented in January.

 

 
SM Prime (SMPH PM) - 1Q20 Philippine mall revenue falls 16%; headwinds remain
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA — carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Net profit falls 5%SM Prime’s 1Q20 net profit fell 5% to P8.3bn. While this is already 30% of our full-year forecast, we consider this a disappointing result, particularly since we expect a sharp drop in 2Q20 earnings.

Philippine mall revenue down 16%Total mall revenue fell 19% (to P12.2bn) as China mall revenue fell c. 40%. China malls were closed from late January to February (early March in some cases). Meanwhile, Philippine mall revenue of P11.3bn was down 16% YoY, worse than the experiences of Ayala Land (-9%) and Robinsons Land (-5%). This is partly because unlike other mall operators, SM Prime is granting full rent condonation to all mall tenants (including operating tenants).

Reservation sales jump 39%Although Philippine residential revenue of P11.4bn is up 23% YoY, this is due to a low base in 1Q19 and we consider this a miss. Meanwhile, presales jumped 39% YoY to P24.8bn, already 39% of our full-year forecast.

Philippine malls start to reopenAs of May 16, c. 80% of all mall tenants in the Philippines are permitted to open. However, there are still several impediments to “normal” operations, including restrictions on the number of shoppers, air conditioning, etc. Furthermore, public transportation is still unavailable in Metro Manila, Laguna, and Cebu, and the malls there accounted for over 70% of Philippine mall revenue in 2019. Even in areas under looser quarantine restrictions (called General Community Quarantine), SM Prime’s early experience is that foot traffic is only about 30% of pre-COVID levels. It continues to grant full rent condonation to all mall tenants nationwide. In China, foot traffic has now returned to pre-COVID levels.

Construction will be slowerConstruction can now resume all over the country. However, a major constraint is that construction workers must practice social distancing. Contractors hesitate to provide exact estimates but they believe that this will slow down the construction process substantially. Thus, building completions (and residential revenue) could be delayed.

Filinvest Land (FLI PM) - 1Q20 residential revenue and OFW sales fall sharply
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA — carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential revenue falls 39% Filinvest Land’s 1Q20 net profit fell 25% to P1.35bn, behind our forecast. Despite only two weeks of Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ) in 1Q, residential revenue fell 39%. We believe this is largely a timing issue. Unfortunately, due to the COVID-19 pandemic (and related lockdowns), it is unlikely for residential revenue to completely catch up within this year.

OFW sales also drop sharply Reservation sales fell 14% to P4.9bn due to the ECQ as well as the eruption of Taal volcano. Note that c. 20% of 2019 launches were close to Taal. OFW sales fell more sharply (30%) and now comprise only 25% of total sales (31% in FY19).

Rental revenue still rises thanks to office portfolio, but mall revenue drops Despite the ECQ, rental revenue rose 4% to P1.8bn. The office segment performed within our expectations but the mall division is trailing substantially behind. Office revenue climbed 15% to P1.4bn on 11% floor area expansion. Meanwhile, mall revenue fell 25% (to P367m), worse than the performances of the other developers we cover (down 5%-16%). This implies that same mall rents were already down even prior to the ECQ.

Possible rent discounts for mall tenants Management said that it would grant deferments of payments to residential buyers and small office tenants during the ECQ. Over 80% of office tenants have been paying promptly while over 50% of residential buyers have chosen to defer payments. FLI does not intend to grant rent discounts to office tenants.

During the ECQ, Filinvest granted rent condonation to non-operating mall tenants. Now that the lockdown is being lifted, operating tenants could rise from 20% of space to c. 80%. FLI said that it might grant rent discounts to these tenants who will begin opening their stores.

Cash preservation mode Due to the COVID pandemic (and its net debt/equity ratio of 81%), the company is preserving cash. It is slowing down the completion of office projects and has yet to make a decision on the cash dividend this year. It is aiming to have an office portfolio of 600,000 sqm by end-2020E. While this would represent a 15% expansion from 2019, it would mean an increase of only half our estimate.

 
Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) - COVID-19 hits 1Q retail volumes, refining margins
 
 
by Enrique Fausto, CFA — enrique.fausto@regis.ph
 

1Q20 adjusted profit at P37mn, down 95% - behind expectations Pilipinas Shell reported 1Q20 headline net loss of P5.55bn. Adjusting for inventory holding losses (“COSA”), we estimate 1Q20 profit was barely breakeven at P37mn, down 95% YoY and significantly behind estimates. The COVID-19 pandemic and ensuing lockdown slashed demand for oil and kept regional refining margins at depressed levels.

Quarantine shoots down retail demand Retail volumes were flat YoY prior to the implementation of the enhanced community quarantine (ECQ) last March 17. Post-ECQ, however, volumes fell 55% YoY bringing 1Q down 11% YoY as transport was heavily restricted. Shell’s retail network remained at 1,126 stations similar to end-2019, which remains 100% open but continues to suffer from anemic volumes (April: -66% YoY).

Commercial volumes holding up in 1Q Surprisingly, commercial volumes were up 16% YoY pre-ECQ, but fell in the latter half of March bringing 1Q up c.13% YoY. The company attributes this to onboarding of new customers as well as stockpiling upon implementation of ECQ. However, we expect weak volumes in succeeding quarters as the lockdown takes its toll on commercial oil demand such as for airlines, power customers, and other industries.

Refinery halts operations, dividends deferred Regional refinery margins have remained at depressed levels as the COVID-19 pandemic drained demand for oil. Lower margins and demand prompted the company to suspend the operations of its Tabangao refinery beginning mid-May this year to cap further losses and conserve cash. The company also deferred the decision to declare dividends to the next board meeting but it is becoming increasingly likely dividends will be deferred altogether in 2020.

 

Corporate News

House recalls ABS-CBN interim permit.

House of Rep reconsidered the second reading approval of House Bill 6732 which seeks to grant a provisional franchise to ABS-CBN until Oct 31, 2020. House Deputy Majority Leader Wilter Wee Palma II said lawmakers recalled the bill to make interpellations and amendments. Albay Rep Edcel Lagman raised constitutional concerns on the passage of the bill in first and second readings on the same day.

Philippine Seven 1Q20 net profit down 7% YoY.

SEVN reported 1Q20 net profit of P104mn, down 7% YoY. Revenues rose 12% YoY to P13.1bn on the back of +2.5% SSSG. SSSG was trending low double digit in the first two months of the year and fell sharply in March when the lockdown was imposed. As of the end of 1Q, approx 75% of its 2,916 stores were operational - of which only 10% were operating 24/7.

MPI sells 10.3% of PT Nusantara.

MPI sold its 10.32% stake in PT Margautama Nusantara (PTMN) to a Japanese tollroad consortium last May 8. The consortium was made of West Nippon Expressway, Ltd., Japan Expressway International Co., Japan Overseas Infrastructure Investment Corporation for Transport & Urban Development. The sale amounted to P1.78bn. MPI previously owned 77.94% of PTMN prior to this transaction; PTMN accounts for c. 3% of NAV. The transaction expected to advance the strategic partnership of MPTC with consortium partners.

House drops ABS-CBN provisional franchise, tackles 25-year franchise.

House Speaker Alan Peter Cayetano said that the House will tackle proposals for ABS-CBN’s (ABSP PM) 25-year legislative franchise instead of the initial five-month provisional one. This came after the House recalled its approval on second reading of House Bill 6732 that proposes a franchise for ABS-CBN only until Oct. 31, 2020. ABS-CBN CEO Carlo Katigbak said earlier at the Senate that the network may resort to retrenching some of its workers in Aug if it can’t resume its television and radio broadcast operations soon.

 

Macro & Political News

OFW remittances rise 2.5% YoY to US$2.36bn in February.

Overseas Filipino Workers (OFW) remittances rose 2.5% YoY to US$2.36bn in Feb. This brings 3-month moving average (3mma) up 3.6% YoY. Land-based remittances rose for Asia (+12.5% YoY) and USA (+13.3%) but fell for Middle East (-7.5% YoY) and Europe (-5.0% YoY) while sea-based remittances also fell 1.0% YoY to US$518mn.

Government cuts infra, education budgets to fund Covid-19 initiatives.

Dept of Budget and Management (DBM) data showed budgets for infrastructure and education programs were cut in favor of cash aid and medical initiatives against Covid-19. The Bayanihan to Heal as One Act allowed the President to realign budget items and as of end-Apr, budget for the Dept of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) was cut by P122bn (-21%) to P459bn while Dept of Education (DepEd) was cut by P22bn (-4%) to P500bn. The Dept of Agri was cut by P11.7bn to P50.6bn and Dept of Transportation was cut by P9bn to P90.6bn, among others. Additional funds went to the Dept of Social Welfare & Dev’t (+P165bn to P330bn), Dept of Finance (+35bn to P54bn), Dept of Health (P1.9bn to P102.9bn), Dept of Labor & Employment (+P1.5bn to P18.9bn).

Government revenues fall in April.

Gov’t revenues fell in April with collections dropping 70% YoY to P71.8bn for the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and down 34% YoY to P34bn for Bureau of Customers (BOC). The Dept of Finance (DOF) attributed the drop to lockdowns imposed to contain the spread of the COVID-19 virus that prompted the BIR to extend the deadlines for filling and payment of income and other taxes to June.

DTI studies import duty.

DTI studies import duty. The DTI is studying tariff measures on imported products to boost state coffers. “If at all, it would be a minimal tariff just to raise funds“ this according to DTI Secretary Ramon Lopez. He did not specify products but noted that locally produced products will be prioritized for the govt’s “Build, Build, Build” program.

House drops ABS-CBN provisional franchise, tackles 25-year franchise.

House Speaker Alan Peter Cayetano said that the House will tackle proposals for ABS-CBN’s (ABSP PM) 25-year legislative franchise instead of the initial five-month provisional one. This came after the House recalled its approval on second reading of House Bill 6732 that proposes a franchise for ABS-CBN only until Oct. 31, 2020. ABS-CBN CEO Carlo Katigbak said earlier at the Senate that the network may resort to retrenching some of its workers in Aug if it can’t resume its television and radio broadcast operations soon.

Tourist arrivals fall 54% YoY in Jan-Apr.

According to Dept of Tourism (DOT) Secretary Bernadette Romulo-Puyat, international visitor arrivals fell 54% YoY to 1.3mn in Jan-Apr this year from 2.8mn last year as April recorded zero arrivals. “We also estimate revenues from foreign tourists arrivals for the period January to April to have reached P79.8bn or a decrease in 55% compared to P180.52bn in January to April 2019”, Puyat said. In 2019, there were 8.26mn foreign tourist arrivals in the Philippines.

Debt to GDP ratio rises to 41.8% in 1Q20.

Finance Undersecretary and Chief Economist Gil Beltran said the country’s debt to GDP ratio raised to 41.8% of GDP as of end-March 2020 vs End-2019 debt/GDP was at 39.6%. The national gov’t’s debt rose to P8.18trn as of 1Q20 (+4.8% YoY, +5.8% QoQ) as GDP fell 0.2% YoY in 1Q20. Beltran added that the debt/GDP ratio is expected to rise to 49.2% by end-2020 to finance the COVID-19 efforts.

No need for bicam if Senate passes ‘fiscally sound’ CREATE bill –Salceda.

House Ways and Means Chair Rep Joey Salceda said that there will be no need for a bicameral conference committee if the Senate passes a “fiscally sound” version of the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act (CREATE). This was previously called Corporate Income Tax and Incentives Rationalization Act (CITIRA). “As long as the Senate’s final output is fiscally vetted, and without weird additions that threaten our financial position, we can go without bicameral conference”, Salceda said. CREATE bill is now pending second reading in the Senate as lawmakers aim to come up with a final version before Congress goes on recess on June 5. CREATE aims to reduce corporate income taxes (CIT) to 25% by July 2020 from 30% currently while the previous version aimed to reduce CIT to 20% over a decade.

Palace refutes Duque on ‘second wave’.

Presidential Spokesperson Harry Roque refuted earlier pronouncements of Dept of Health Secretary Francisco Duque III clarifying that the country is still in the first wave of the COVID-19 contagion. Duque told Senators last Wed that the country is already in the second wave of infections and should brace for the third wave. Dept of Health later apologized and backtracked on its statement confirming the country is still in the first wave of infections.

POGOs cannot restart operations yet.

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) said that POGOs cannot resume their operations because they have not paid their tax obligations. The prerequisites to restart operations include proof of payment of 2019 franchise taxes and withholding taxes from January to April 2020. Only 30% of the employees are permitted to work in the office and these workers are required to have Alien Employment Permits (AEP). BIR Deputy Commissioner Arnel Guballa said that POGOs were “still in the process of complying with the requirements, including payments”. We estimate that POGOs occupy about 1.3m sqm in Metro Manila (11% of office stock) and employ over 300,000 mainland Chinese workers.

BSP Governor warns banks against late fees, additional interest.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Benjamin Diokno reminded lending institutions that they are prohibited from charging late payment fees and interest on interest on all loan payments falling due within the ongoing quarantine period. This follows consumer complaints about policies of some banks that have been charging said fees during the mandatory grace period provided under the Bayanihan Act. Under current regulation, relief shall cover all loans extended by all BSP-supervised financial institutions including salary, personal, housing, vehicle loans, credit cards, etc.

Power demand seen to remain flat this year –DOE.

Based on preliminary forecasts, Dept of Energy (DOE) is now expecting flat growth for electricity demand in the country this year. Prior to COVID-19, DOE was expecting Luzon peak demand to rise 8% YoY to 12,285MW. DOE expects the drop in commercial and industrial segments demand will be offset by a rise in residential power demand. REGIS forecasts power demand to fall 5% YoY in FY20 and to recover in FY21 (+9% YoY).

House passes PESA bill on first reading.

House of Rep Economic Stimulus Sub-committee passed on first reading the P568bn PESA bill (Philippine Economic Stimulus Act) and submitted it to the Defeat COVID-19 Committee for second reading according to Marikina Rep and Co-chair of House Economic Stimulus Cluster Stella Quimbo. The key measures include the ff:

  • P110bn wage subsidies for critical businesses, self-employed, freelances and OFWs
  • P30bn for temporary employment of displaced workers (expansion of Labor dept TUPAD program)
  • P18bn emergency subsidy to COVID-19-affected tertiary students in private higher educational institutions
  • P10bn this year and another P10bn next year for mass testing
  • P50bn expansion of existing loan programs to MSMEs of the Small Business Corp
  • P40bn special guarantee fund this year and another P20bn next year
  • P25bn to National Dev’t Corp (NDC) for businesses under crisis
  • P650bn for 3 years starting next year for enhanced “Build Build Build” program
