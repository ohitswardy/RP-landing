REGIS REPORT

06 November 2020

The PSEi surged 5.7% this week, rising along with global market on a tech-led rally in the US.

Foreigners turned buyers in the last two days of the week, taking total net outflows lower to US$10m.  This is lower than the adjusted US$43m in outflows in the previous week and the outflow levels seen in recent months.

It was a broad rally – with 29 of the 30 index stocks in positive territory this week.

GT Capital (+16.3% WoW) led the gainers followed by reopening plays Jollibee (+15.8% WoW) and Bloomberry (+11.6% WoW).  Gaming regulator PAGCOR approved Bloomberry’s application to accept online bets, suggesting another revenue stream for the company.

Other double digit gains were also delivered by Robinsons Land (+12.0% WoW), and JG Summit (+11.8% WoW), and DMCI Holdings (+10.7% WoW).

Property stocks gained, despite negative news for POGOs.  The Bureau of Internal Revenue is effectively adjusting the tax on POGO operations to 5% of bets, from what was previously 5% of gross gaming revenue or GGR. If properly enforced,  the tax change could have a massive impact on POGO operations with many gambling activities having theoretical win rates below 5%. Unprofitable POGOs would have an impact on office take-up;  the most direct office exposure is with Filinvest (+3.1% WoW) and Megaworld (+6.9% WoW). Meanwhile, many of SM Prime’s (+3.1% WoW) assets are in the Manila Bay area, where POGOs are most concentrated.

There was a steady stream of 3Q results all week with most companies showing better numbers sequentially as lockdowns were loosened.

SM Investments (+6.3% WoW) recovered from a 2Q loss with a 3Q profit of P8.1bn (-19% YoY). Record earnings reported by BDO (+3.5% WoW) helped as did the recovery from SM Retail which saw large sequential jumps  for the department store (+109%) and the specialty segment (+93%).

Bank and property results this week came in behind expectations.

Metrobanks’ (+2.45% WoW) miss came on the back of its decision to keep provision expense very high at P12.6bn in 3Q.  NPL ratio increased 69bps QoQ to 2.25% but NPL cover stayed at 174%. Profit for 3Q20 was down 78% YoY to P1.9bn.

Ayala Land’s (+6.1% WoW) 3Q also came in behind estimates as well, with a 77% contraction in profits. ALI’s 9M20 figure is only 50% of consensus FY forecast. Mall revenue disappointed as it fell 67% YoY. Nonetheless, reservation sales recovered 65% QoQ to P22.5bn (-38% YoY).

Meanwhile, PLDT (+3.9% WoW) came in ahead, as both wireless (+6% QoQ and 9% YoY) and broadband (+9% QoQ and 21% YoY) businesses were solid. PLDT’s wireless revenue performance implies a market share gain of 1pt vs Globe (+1.9% WoW), taking PLDT to 49% market share. Globe’s rebound was less robust, with mobile service revenue rising 2% QoQ (-10% YoY).  Home broadband rose 5% QoQ (+28% YoY).

ICTSI (+8.7% WoW) rose,  with results coming in  better-than-expected.  ICT reported 3Q profit up 14% YoY and 32% QoQ.  Volumes rose 3% YoY in 3Q and cost savings drove EBITDA higher by 13% YoY.

A number of consumer companies also reported.  Both Century Pacific (10.7% WoW) and San Miguel Food and Beverage (-1.2% WoW) reported in line earnings. As expected, CNPF saw a normalization in demand in the absence of pantry stocking while San Miguel FB saw gin volumes bounce 33% YoY in 3Q.  Beer however was down 20% YoY during the quarter.

Meanwhile, Wilcon (+8.4% WoW)  saw a rebound in 3Q store operations.  SSSG was positive for most of the quarter except for August, though much of the recovery was driven by stores outside Metro Manila.

Philippine Property - POGO taxes to be based on turnover (not GGR); a fatal blow?
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

IRR for franchise taxes puts viability of POGO operations in doubt

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) published the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) raising franchise taxes on Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs) to 5% of gross bets (i.e. “drop”), in line with the Bayanihan II Law. If properly enforced, the new regulations could make many online “games” unprofitable and possibly spell the end of most online gambling operations in the country. This would have a major impact on the property sector and adjacent industries.

5% tax based on bets/drop, not on GGR

Despite the wording of Bayanihan II, we had previously expected the BIR to interpret the law loosely and continue requiring POGOs to pay only 5% of GGR as franchise taxes. GGR represents the wins of the gaming operator (loss of the bettor/gambler) and is thus only a fraction of the bets/turnover/drop. Therefore, games with low win rates (such as baccarat) would no longer be viable for POGOs because they would have to pay more in taxes than their revenue from those games.

Expect more POGO closures

Colliers estimates that POGOs vacated 150,000sqm of Metro Manila office space in 9M20. During this period, they were only required to pay 5% of GGR as franchise taxes. Now that the franchise taxes are based on 5% of bets, their profitability will be curtailed even further. It would not surprise us if over 80% of POGO operations shut down over the next few years.

Substantial impact to the property sector, particularly in the Manila Bay area

As of 2019, we estimate that POGOs occupied 1.3m sqm (11%) of Manila office stock and employed over 300,000 Chinese nationals. The impact to the Manila Bay area is disproportionately large as we estimate that POGOs occupy 60% of the area’s office stock. About 70% of space occupied by POGOs is not PEZA accredited and thus would generally not be used by BPOs. As we wrote in “The beginning of the end?” (published on 27 Feb 2020), a rapid departure of the entire POGO sector could lower rents/prices in the Manila Bay area by 50% and 30% in other parts of Metro Manila.

Filinvest Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime are more exposed

We expect Filinvest Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime to be more negatively affected by the (likely substantial) departure of POGOs. POGOs occupy about 25% of Filinvest’s office space and 15% of Megaworld’s office portfolio. The Manila Bay area also accounted for c. 25% of MEG’s 2019 residential launches. Meanwhile, although POGOs have little direct impact on SM Prime’s earnings, about 40% of SM Prime’s NAV is comprised of land in the Manila Bay area, the preferred location of POGOs.

Metropolitan Bank - Conservatism vs growth – Provisions kept high in 3Q; loans contract 17% ytd
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT -78% in 3Q20 on high provisions; loans down 13% yoy

Metrobank reported that 9M20 NPAT halved to P11.0bn, with 3Q20 down 78% yoy. While headline profit seems well behind our FY20E (19.6bn), the drag came from provisions of P12.6bn in 3Q, taking the 9M figure to P35.4bn, more than 4.5x yoy. While NPLs increased to 2.25% (was 1.56% in 2Q), the bank kept coverage high at 174%. Further evidence of the bank’s conservatism was the 13.2% yoy contraction in loans, compared to the sector’s +4% yoy as of August.

Loans contract, taking NIMs down qoq, despite plunging deposit costs

MBT’s loans contracted 13.4% yoy (Figure 1); the pace of contraction is even more stark on a qoq (-8.1%) and ytd (-17.4%) basis. NIMs contracted sharply, down 29bps qoq to 3.83% (Figure 2). Not only were loan yields down some 35bps qoq and yoy, but also as the loan paydowns shifted assets to lower-yielding BSP placements and to the bank’s treasury book (Figure 3). The NIM slide was cushioned as deposit costs plunged to 0.46% in 3Q20, down 35bps qoq and 109bps yoy (Figure 4). CASA deposits surged 22% yoy, taking CASA ratio to 71%.

Provisions kept high, with NPL cover at 174% despite rise in NPLs to 2.25%

MBT’s NPL ratio climbed 69bps qoq to 2.25% (Figure 5). Despite the relatively mild rise, the bank chose to keep NPL coverage at 174% (Figure 6), by far the highest among its peers – at 3Q, BDO (BDO PM) was 138% and BPI (BPI PM) was 100%). Provision expense for the quarter was P12.6bn, still up 4x yoy, taking 9M provisions to P35.4bn, up 4.5x and equivalent to an annualized credit cost of about 385bps. This masked healthy PPOP growth of 12% yoy in 3Q20, and 41% in 9M20. Fee incomes rebounded 50% qoq.

Is there such a thing as too conservative?

We are sympathetic to the bank taking provisions early, while PPOP remains robust. Not only does it pre-empt a further deterioration in NPL, but also it could mean a faster earnings recovery in 2021/22E. However, the decision to contract its loan book this rapidly could hurt its franchise in the medium term.

Wilcon Depot - Operations recover in 3Q20
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M20 net profit down 43% YoY, behind expectations

WLCON reported 9M20 profit of P886mn (-43% YoY), tracking behind Regis and consensus expectations. Net sales declined 12% YoY to P15.8bn mainly due to the temporary store closures during the hard lockdown in 2Q, but WLCON has resumed operations for all of its stores as of end-Sep. 3Q revenue and operating profit were higher YoY (+8% and +10%), but NPAT was still mildly down (-3%) due to non-operating items (lower interest income, higher effective tax rate).

SSSG rebounds in 3Q20 despite mid-Aug lockdown

SSSG in 3Q20 was +0.4%, rebounding from the -48% clip in 2Q20 as store operations resumed after quarantine restrictions were lifted. SSSG was tracking c3% for most of the quarter, but was hit again by the 2-week lockdown in midAug. We estimate same-store transaction count rebounded +7-8% (-25% in 1H20), but was offset by a drop in average basket sizes (-7-8% in 3Q20 vs -5% in 1H). Management noted that the top line recovery was driven by stores outside Metro Manila, where average basket sizes are generally lower.

Favorable product mix partly offsets elevated cost

Gross margin improved 70bps YoY, driven by the increase in contribution from high-margin direct imports to 51.7% in 3Q20 (49.6% in 3Q19). This partly cushioned the impact of proportionately higher fixed cost, such as lease expenses and depreciation.

Sequential recovery sustained in Oct, but high YoY base in 4Q

Management noted that sales continued to improve sequentially in Oct, though the previous year’s base is high and Metro Manila stores (c.40% of 2019 sales) are still underperforming. WLCON is hopeful that sales in these stores will pick up as limits on construction activity ease. The recovery of Metro Manila stores, however, will have implications on WLCON’s gross margin as these stores sell proportionately less of the higher-margin products. Management is also anticipating pricing pressure as smaller players resume operations.

Philippine Strategy - Don’t buck the BSP – massive QE and DM
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Lower rates, for longer; bullish for financial markets

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has launched a combination of Direct Monetization (DM) and Quantitative Easing (QE). As of August, the BSP’s holdings of PHP Treasury assets stood at a staggering P1.5trn, up nearly 7x from just six months earlier (see Figure 1), and is likely to exceed P2trn (>10% of GDP) this year. Together with the 175bps policy rate cuts and 200bps RRR cuts, the BSP seems committed to keeping rates low across the curve (see Figures 2-7 on page 2), which could support market valuations ahead of – or even in spite of – fundamentals.

DM – BSP direct funding of Treasury: from P300bn, to P540bn, to P850bn

In March, the BSP bought P300bn worth of 6-month, zero-coupon securities from the Treasury to ensure the government had ample liquidity to fund regular operations, and stimulus programs during the Covid-19 pandemic. To our knowledge, this is the first time the BSP has ever done DM, despite past financial crises. In October, the BSP upped this to P540bn, until end-2020. Bayanihan 2 raised the maximum* amount the BSP can lend to Treasury to roughly P850bn.

QE – BSP’s open market operations: from P226bn in Feb to P1,217bn in Aug

The BSP buys and sells Treasuries in the open market. Since 1993, its bonds portfolio has not exceeded P330bn, and has stayed below P250bn from 2009-19. This year, though, the BSP has increased its holdings to P1.2trn as of August – more than 5x its holdings pre-Covid – aggressively buying across the curve. Its hand was most visible it added more than P410bn in the month of June alone.

(Much) more to come

Unlike DM, there is no prescribed limit to the BSP’s open market operations (QE). In a Bloomberg report in July, Governor Diokno said that the BSP would likely keep the bond buying program in place “at least for the next… two years.” While there may be longer-term consequences, the wall of money will likely push financial assets – ie share prices – higher over the short- to medium-term.

SM Investments - 3Q: Banking boost; Non-food retail climbing back
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M profits at P15.2bn, 3Q at P8.1bn

SM Investments disclosed that net income for 9M20 reached P15.2bn. While this is down 54% YoY, it implies 3Q profit of P8.1bn (-19%), a turnaround from the net loss in 2Q. Lower 3Q provisions at BDO and an improvement in the retail business pulled consolidated earnings back into the black. Overall, we’d characterize the bottom line figure/s as being within expectations.

BDO: Record earnings as provisions dive

From a net loss in 2Q, BDO’s earnings hit a quarterly record of P12.3bn in 3Q as loan loss provisions fell back to P1.4bn. Deposit costs dropped to less than 0.5% while reported NPLs stayed just below 2% of loans, flat QoQ.

SM Retail: Non-food is climbing back but food segment looking soft

With most of its stores now open, SM Retail recorded a profit of P1.7bn for 3Q (P2.2bn for 9M). There were big QoQ jumps for the department store (+109%) and the specialty segment (+93%) as lockdown restrictions were eased. We note, however, that 3Q revenue for the Food segment (i.e. groceries) was rather soft and only up an estimated 3% YoY (P46bn), even with its small format Alfamart chain growing >20%. The return of trade support from suppliers appeared to have helped boost earnings in the quarter.

SM Prime: Foot traffic and, more importantly, sales recovering

Earnings for 3Q fell 52% to P4,0bn, broadly in line with the pace over 1H as the company continued to provide rental discounts to its mall tenants. Foot traffic is now at 30-40% of pre-pandemic levels but, more significantly, tenant sales are already at 70-75%. As a consequence, the company will only extend the current level of rental discounts until the end of the year.

9M20 profit down 3% YoY to US$643mn – Ahead of forecast

Net profit after perps distributions grew 14% YoY and 32% QoQ to US$53mn in 3Q20, taking the 9M20 figure to US$138mn (-3% YoY). The results are trending above Regis and consensus forecasts, buoyed by lower-than-expected cash costs and equitized losses. 3Q: Volumes up 3% YoY; Yields softer QoQ

Throughput volumes bounced to 2.63mn in 3Q, up 3% YoY and 15% QoQ. Its ports in EMEA, however, could be affected by the second wave of lockdowns in Europe. Other updates: Sudan receivables, acquisition pipeline

Discussions continue with the government of Sudan for the collection of the remaining Euro189mn (~4% of market capitalization). While the government of Sudan continues to show commitment to pay, the inability to reach an agreement on a payment schedule could force the company to set aside an amount for this by year-end. The company also hopes to close in on an acquisition for a port in Angola in early 2021. It is also looking to acquire two more ports located in West Africa and in Indonesia.

Water: Expenditures rise in 3Q

MBT earnings call takeaways

Loan contraction (-13%yoy and -17%ytd) was due to pay-downs from large corporates that issued bonds. On the consumer side, loans were amortizing faster than new loans being written (especially in autos). NIMs will likely compress as loan / asset yields go down, but low funding / deposit costs should help. The NPL is expect to be <3% by end-2020 and 5% by end-2021. NPL stress could persist into 2022, due to uncertainties over vaccine, production, and distribution (ie when will it get to Phils?). Meanwhile, restructuring – P3.5bn at 9M20, about 0.3% of loans (was P2.4bn at end-2019, and 9M19). The bank also booked aggressive provisions while NPAT is strong. Did not want to wait to act when NPLs already apparent. That way, when growth opportunities present themselves, they are ready, and “unburdened” by need to provision. Learned from 1997 experience. Much fewer clients opted in for Bayanihan 2 vs Bayanihan 1. For commercial loans, only c10% opted in under B2. For consumer, only about 20% for B2 (compared to 50% for B1).

Globe 3Q Results First Take

9M20 core income was P15.6bn (-13% YoY) while 3Q core income was P4.53bn (-22% YoY).  The core profit figures are slightly behind our FY20 estimates (P22.3bn before prefs) and likely be to the same for consensus (P22bn for FY20). EBITDA of P56.3bn (-3% YoY) for 9M20 and P17.9bn (-7%) also appear to be trending just short of our own and consensus estimates due to a revenue shortfall. The home broadband revenues are a bright spot. It’s leading the growth (+5% QoQ and +28% YoY) but, frankly, we had expected stronger considering the number of subscribers added in 2Q and 3Q.  For 3Q, much of the rise in subscribers is from Fixed Wireless. Exports up, trade deficit lower in September

Trade deficit in September stood at US$1.71bn, lower than the US$3.4bn trade gap in the same period last year. Exports rose 2% YoY, 13% MoM, snapping a six-month losing streak. Imports dropped 16% YoY but was up 8% MoM (slowest contraction since February). The 9M20 trade deficit at US$16.07bn, down 47% YoY.

Trade deficit at US$2.076bn in August, down 31% YoY

Balance of trade in goods was at a US$2.076bn deficit in August, a slower drop of 31% YoY than in previous months. Exports fell 19% YoY and 10% MoM, with electronic exports dropping 20% YoY. Meanwhile, imports fell 23% YoY and 4% MoM, dragged down by a contraction in oil imports (-48% YoY). The 8M20 trade deficit at US$14.61bn, down 46% YoY

Bureau of Customs exceeds October collection goal

The Bureau of Customs collected P50.9bn in October, down 11.7% YoY but up 1.3% MoM. The October collection was 5.2% higher than the P48.4bn target for the month which brought YT-October collections to P448bn, down 15% YoY. The gov’t downgraded its FY20E BOC collection target to P506bn (from P542bn previously and pre-pandemic target of P731bn).

Manufacturing index falls in October

The Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) fell to 48.5 in October from 50.1 in September. The new orders fell in October after an uptick in September. Domestic demand weakened but was partly cushioned by higher overseas orders. Despite the weaker October figure, manufacturers expect production to increase over the next 12 months.

Infrastructure spend falls 43% YoY in September

Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) plunged 40% YoY to P56.9bn in September. This brings YT-September infra spend at P451.4bn, down 17% YoY. Last July, the government lowered its infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. For FY21, the infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) is supposed to increase by 43% YoY to P1.12tn. This target is c. 7% higher than what was spent in 2019 for infrastructure (P1.05tn).

October inflation fastest in 3 months

Headline inflation for October stood at 2.5%, picking up from the 2.3% pace in September. This marked the fastest pace in three months or since the 2.7% reading in July 2020. This falls within the 1.9-2.7% estimate given by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) for October. Inflation of food and non-alcoholic beverages picked up and they cited adverse weather conditions in recent months and the incidence of swine flu in some parts of the country. This figure brings YT-October inflation at 2.5%. Core inflation was at 3.0%, slower than the 3.2% in the previous month.
