BDO Unibank - Resurgent 3Q20 NPAT, on surprisingly benign NPLs, sharp drop in deposit costs
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT rebounds to P12bn in 3Q, as provisions and deposit cost plunge

Provision expense plunged in 3Q20 to just P1.4bn, due to surprisingly benign NPLs of 1.97% (flat qoq) and the P20bn “pre-emptive” provision in 2Q. This drove 3Q NPAT to P12.3bn, a quarterly record, and brought 9M20 NPAT P16.6bn in line with consensus and our estimates. NIMs were steady as deposit costs crashed to 0.49% in 3Q. Heading into 4Q20 and 2021 though, slowing loan growth, falling asset yields, and asset quality still pose downside risks to earnings.

Deposit costs crash to 0.49%, NIMs firm despite slow loan growth falling yields

Loan growth slowed to 6% yoy in 3Q, and were down c3% qoq (Figure 1). NIMs were flat qoq at 4.25%, despite asset yields falling 24bps qoq (Figures 2 and 3). Deposit costs plunged 24bps qoq and 71bps yoy to an astonishing 0.49% (Figure 4). While total deposits grew just 7% yoy, CASA jumped 16%, taking CASA ratio to 79%. BDO also slashed the rate it pays on CASA to 0.125% in 3Q from 0.25%. While loan rates are likely to continue to fall, the low funding costs should hold it in good stead heading into 2021E.

 

Provision expense fall to P1.4bn, on surprisingly low NPL ratio of 1.97%

BDO reported NPL ratio of 1.97%, flat compared to the 1.95% in 2Q (Figure 5). As we had previously reported, BDO interpreted the PD and NPL rules during the Bayanihan 1 grace period very conservatively. The 3Q figure reflects the bank coming into line with industry practice. Still, the 1.97% is much lower than the 2 industry’s 2.93% NPL as of August. This, combined with the P20bn “pre-emptive” provision in 2Q20 allowed BDO to drop provisions to just P1.4bn in 3Q.

Aggressive restructuring / rescheduling c5% of loans

BDO is in the process of restructuring or rescheduling roughly P100bn in predominantly corporate loans, equivalent to c5% of its book, which could partly explain the low NPL level. The BSP has set conditions for restructured loans to not be classified as Stage 2 during the Covid-19 pandemic.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
SM Prime - 3Q20 Philippine mall revenue drops 72% as COVID impact persists
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

3Q20 earnings fall 52% on mall weakness

SMPH’s 3Q20 earnings fell 52% YoY to P4.0bn as the COVID pandemic continued to weigh on the Philippine mall business. As a result, 9M20 net profit fell 48% to P14.4bn, broadly in line with our forecast (but only 66% of consensus FY20E). On a more positive note, residential sales were remarkably resilient while China mall operations continued to recover.

Philippine mall foot traffic below 40% of pre-COVID level

3Q20 Philippine mall revenue fell 72% to P3.9bn, bringing the 9M20 figure down 56% (to P18.3bn). While the 3Q20 weakness was partly due to the two-week hard lockdown in August, September foot traffic was still below 40% of pre-COVID levels. Our channel checks suggest some modest improvements in October. SMPH has been granting rental concessions to its mall tenants since March and plans to continue granting these until end-2020. Tenants (that have reopened) that pay fixed rent have received rental discounts of up to 50% while minimum rents have been waived for tenants who pay based on percentage-of-sales.

 

China malls recover further

China mall revenue climbed 14% QoQ (and is down just 9% YoY) as lockdowns ended in early 2Q20. Foot traffic has also returned to 90% of pre-COVID levels.

Residential reservation sales performing well

Despite restrictions on construction activities, 3Q20 residential revenue was flat YoY at P10.5bn. More remarkably, 3Q20 reservation sales only fell 3% YoY (and grew 38% QoQ) to P24.3bn, implying resilient demand for SMPH projects. The 9M20 figure of P66.7bn is flat YoY and already represents 93% of our FY forecast.

Hotel revenue falls 80%

3Q20 hotel revenue fell 80% as hotels were generally required to remain closed. They are now allowed to return to 100% of operations (subject to protocols).

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Manila Electric Company - 3Q: Volumes rebound but still falling short
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

3Q core income down 16% as volume drops 6%

Meralco reported 3Q core income of P5.14bn, down 16% from a year ago, as electricity sales dipped 6%. Cumulative 9M core income came in at P15,7bn, down 15% YoY and trending a little short of our FY20 estimates, due to a shortfall in power volumes and contributions from its generation business. It is looking tough to meet our FY20 volume assumption (-5% YoY) with electricity volumes for 9M20 down 7%. Headline income for 3Q was P4.4bn (-30% YoY), which brings 9M income to P11.3bn (-39%).

3Q volumes rebound but early 4Q doesn’t look encouraging

3Q electricity volumes have been recovering from the lows in 2Q, with Sept sales already matching the previous year. The trends so far for Oct, however, don’t look encouraging with volumes reverting to a YoY decline of 6-8%.

 

Net cash dips as payment terms extended

We estimate net cash was at P40bn as of end-Sept, down from P45bn in June after the payment of some dividends, Receivable levels were still unusually high at 47 days (vs 19 days the prior year), as Meralco has effectively been asked to extend payment terms for its customers. The higher receivable levels imply ~P25bn of cash has been tied up.

Guidance on core income

Management expects the decline in profitability to moderate in 4Q compared to prior quarters and for FY20 core income to be P21.2bn to P21.3bn. This would be just around 6% below our FY20 estimate.

Philippine Telecoms - Growth from unexpected places
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Lifting our estimates and target prices

The telcos are among the few businesses thriving during this pandemic, benefiting from sharply higher data usage (wireless and wired), which we expect to be part of the “new” normal. The improved revenue outlook prompts us to raise our estimates for PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) and also our DCF-based target prices to P1,700 (+17%) and P2,300 (+21%), respectively. We re-affirm our Buy rating for PLDT and upgrade Globe to Buy (from Hold).

Pandemic accelerates near-term growth

Demand for internet services has skyrocketed in recent months, with many people practically confined indoors and working or studying from home. We expect a sharp acceleration in home broadband take-up this year as a consequence, but a return to more ‘normalized’ levels in 2021.

Two 3rd operators?

With the rise of an aggressive new wired broadband operator, the two incumbents now seem to be facing “third players” on both the mobile (Dito) and wired (Converge ICT) fronts, coincidentally led by principals with the same name. The risks on the wireless side are clearer (and well known) given the market dynamics and the backer of the third player there.

 

Wireless: How big and imminent is the threat?

The threat from a new operator backed by China Telecom is still the foremost issue for the wireless business. We continue to believe that its entry is fraught with political risk, which its principals are likely to be aware of. If the country’s current leadership changes, as scheduled, in mid-2022, there could also be changes to policies favorable to the new operator and its principals.

Home broadband: Land grab

The lockdown has spurred demand for internet service, creating a scramble to meet the unexpected surge. We believe the next few quarters will show exceptional growth in new subscribers, after which growth will taper off to more ‘normal’ levels.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Puregold - Pantry stocking fades in 3Q20; marketing efforts planned in 4Q
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M20 net profit +11% to P5.1bn – behind Regis and consensus

PGOLD reported net profit of P5.1bn, up 11% YoY, tracking below Regis and consensus FY20 estimates. Net sales rose 10% YoY to P121.1bn, while operating profit grew 12% YoY to P8.5bn – both were likewise trending behind expectations. For 3Q20 alone, net profit declined 4% YoY to P1.7bn as sales were flattish and margins were dragged by reduced supplier support.

Puregold: SSS declines as pantry stocking fades

The core Puregold brand posted SSS decline of -4.5% in 3Q20. Household incomes were pressured by the absence of government hand outs, and limited savings were likely depleted after pantry stocking in 1H20. These factors led to a deceleration in basket size growth, which was not enough to compensate the decline in foot traffic (the latter due to quarantine restrictions). Moreover, quarantine restrictions did not allow 1) sari-sari stores to operate, and 2) Puregold to conduct its annual marketing events, which are typically key drivers of sales and margins (e.g. trade support).

 

S&R: foregone Member’s Treat sale drags SSS but boosts margins

S&R also posted SSS decline in 3Q20 (-2.9%) mainly due to the high base in 3Q19 and the absence of its Member’s Treat sale this year. SSSG would have been positive otherwise. The absence of the sale, however, led to fewer discounting, resulting in a 40-bp YoY improvement in gross margin.

Marketing efforts planned in 4Q20, but holiday spending uncertain

Management noted that trends are improving sequentially in early-October, in line with easing quarantine restrictions. However, management’s guidance for FY20 top line growth of +4-5% is quite low relative to our forecast (Regis: +16%). PGOLD says it is coming off a high base in 4Q19 and the level of holiday spending at this point remains uncertain with revenue picking up more slowly than expected. The company has already lined up a number of marketing events for the balance of the year in order to 1) boost top line and 2) recoup some lost marketing income. E-commerce investments likewise appear to be accelerating in line with changing consumer behavior.

Robinsons Retail - Weak consumer spending weighs on 3Q20 sales
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

9M20 net profit falls 14% YoY – behind expectations

RRHI’s 9M20 net profit declined 14% YoY to P2.4bn, trending below Regis and consensus FY20 forecasts. Net sales were down 6% YoY to P110bn as blended SSS fell 6.4%. EBIT (-20% YoY to P4.3bn) fell short as higher-margin discretionary formats were affected by quarantine restrictions. For 3Q20 alone, net profit of P749mn was down 30% YoY.

Supermarkets: 3Q20 SSSG slows on weak consumer spending, shift to ecommerce

The Supermarket segment (63% of sales) continued to outperform and was the only format to post positive SSSG during the quarter. SSSG, however, slowed to just +2% from the +16% clip in 1H20. The deceleration was attributed to weak consumer spending overall, but also to the shift of consumer behaviors in favor of community-based markets and social media marketplaces. Notwithstanding the soft top line, segment EBITDA grew 7% YoY, which was attributed to improving profitability of Rustans.

Quarantine restrictions continue to weigh on discretionary formats

All other formats continued to see SSS contraction in 3Q20, albeit at a slower rate of decline vs the hard lockdown period in 2Q20. While most stores have resumed operations after being temporarily shut in 2Q, foot traffic, particularly in mall-based stores, has yet to recover. Drug stores (12% of sales), while considered “essential” retail, posted -3% SSS as the decline in out-patient doctor visits led to softer demand for prescription medicines.

Sequential improvement in October but still cautious; Rose Pharmacy expected to be margin dilutive near term

Management noted that trends across its formats were improving sequentially in October in line with easing quarantine restrictions. FY20 blended SSSG guidance was maintained at -5-0%, which suggests a sharp sequential recovery in 4Q20. Meanwhile, the recent acquisition of Visayan drug store chain Rose Pharmacy is likely to be margin dilutive in the near term as RRHI integrates operations, but the acquisition should give RRHI a foothold in the VisMin regions, where it currently has limited footprint.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Semirara Mining and Power Corp - Coal exports drop, power unit recovers in 3Q
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

9M20 core profit falls 61% YoY to P2.82bn, ahead of Regis forecast

SCC reported headline 9M20 net profit of P2.98bn (-64% YoY). Excluding forex adjustments and last year’s one-offs, core profit dropped 61% YoY to P2.82bn, representing 86% of Regis FY20E and 62% of street forecasts. While core profit for 3Q dropped 39% QoQ and 55% YoY to P673mn, the results were not as bad as we feared. We believe core income is on track to beat our FY20 estimates but could lag consensus.

Coal mining: 3Q20 hit by China’s import quota

The coal mining unit saw 3Q20 profit drop 75% YoY, after coal sales volumes and ASPs fell due to the imposition of an import quota by China. While domestic sales volumes improved (external and internal), export volumes however dropped 60% YoY and as a result, coal inventory was a record high of 3.1mn MT in end-September. With China increasing its import quota this month, the company is aiming to sell ~13mn MT by year-end, above our forecast (10mn MT).

Power: Recovering at a slower pace

Following the completion of Units 1&2’s rehab, 3Q20 EBITDA and profit from the power division rebounded QoQ and YoY to P1.37bn and P332mn, respectively. However, the pace of recovery was much slower than what we anticipated, given the surprise reduction in Units 1&2’s power supply contracts, as well as persistent unplanned outages at Units 3&4. Around 70% of its 750 MW capacity (net of own usage) was un-contracted in 3Q20. Management has secured 40MW of long-term power supply contracts in 4Q so far and aims to secure more to reduce its spot exposure to just ~20% within the next 12 months.

Balance sheet healthy; Capex tails off

Cash stood at P4.5bn in end-September. With capex tailing off heading into 4Q/FY21 and operations improving from this year’s lows, we believe the company can continue to pay its regular div of P1.25/sh (~12% yield).

Cemex Philippines - Maintenance deferral leads to 9M20 profit beat; 3Q volumes recover QoQ
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

9M20 core profit of P652mn (+64% YoY) exceeds FY20 forecast Headline

9M20 profit stood at P759mn, down 13% YoY. Stripping out forex adjustments, we estimate recurring profit rose 64% YoY to P652mn, exceeding Regis FY20E (P415mn) and consensus estimates (P320mn). Despite the revenue shortfall, 9M20 EBITDA reached P3.3bn, down 5% YoY and accounted for 89% of FY20E. The outperformance largely came from lower costs, partly arising from the deferral of maintenance activities, offsetting soft topline and higher-thanexpected financial expenses.

3Q20 sales volumes down 3% YoY; ASP stable QoQ

Cement sales volumes rebounded after lockdown and was only 3% lower YoY in 3Q20. Management noted a YoY upward surge in volumes in July, which subsided in succeeding months. With 9M20 sales volumes trending 12% YoY lower, 4Q20 volumes should rebound by +10% to reach our full-year forecast of -7%, which looks like a tall order. Meanwhile, ASPs were down 3% YoY in 3Q20 but stable QoQ. With weak domestic and regional cement markets, we do not expect ASPs to recover anytime soon.

Timing of maintenance activity boosts 3Q EBITDA but could weigh on 4Q

3Q20 EBITDA jumped 33% YoY to P1.43bn, as the company deferred regular maintenance activities to 4Q20 (typically done in 2Q/3Q). Lower power and distribution costs also buoyed EBITDA. Maintenance works for the Solid plant is ongoing but given ample clinker inventory, management reckons this activity should not affect operations. Meanwhile, the company has deferred maintenance works for the Apo plant to next year. Given that 4Q sales are typically affected by weather and with the planned additional maintenance costs in 4Q (P250-300mn per plant), we believe it is unlikely that the 3Q20 EBITDA level will be sustained.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Emperador - 3Q: Off-premise brands get consumption boost
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

3Q20 profit +26%

EMP reported that 3Q20 net income jumped 26%yoy to P2.543bn, lifted by continued strength of its international whisky segment. Profits from whisky jumped 72% in 3Q to P995mn, its highest quarterly profit since its acquisition, with its brands benefiting from the shift to off-premise consumption. Meanwhile, brandy profits increased 7%yoy to P1.548bn as restrictions on the sale of liquor were further relaxed. For 9M20, net income grew 11% to P5.869bn, tracking well ahead of Regis (P4.4bn) and consensus (P6.5bn) estimates for FY20.

Off-premise, e-commerce sales boosts WMG whisky brands

Profits from Whyte & Mackay further improved in 3Q (P995mn, +43%qoq, +72%yoy,). Management attributed the strength to a surge in demand for its brands from off-premise and e-commerce outlets. In the UK, management cited that sales of Whyte & Mackay, Jura and Tamnavulin brands are up 31%, 19% and 39% YTD despite widespread lockdowns. Meanwhile, the absence of travel retail’s revenue contribution has mostly been offset by much lower representation, travel and advertising spend.

Brandy remains resilient despite higher prices

The brandy segment, consisting of Emperador and Fundador, similarly improved from the already resilient numbers it posted in 2Q. Brandy profits increased 32%qoq and 7%yoy to P1.548bn on loosened restrictions on sales. While management did not cite specific volume data, they hinted that sales of its core Emperador brand was stable and “almost the same as last year” despite the impact of >15% price increase. However, the fact that percentage margins remained the same (~33%gpm) despite the price/tax hike implies a drastic reduction in the cost of raw materials and other expenses which may not be sustainable

Corp News

AREIT acquires office and mall complex from Ayala Land

AREIT will acquire the 30th (office and mall complex) along Meralco Avenue, Pasig City, for P5.1bn (VAT inclusive) which would be funded with debt. The transaction will be completed in 1Q21. The land will continue to be owned by a third party which has a land lease agreement (until 2050) which will be transferred from Ayala Land to AREIT. This will grow AREIT’s leasable area by 45% to 246,000sqm. The office building is PEZA accredited and has no POGO tenants and Ayala Land will lease the mall from AREIT at a fixed rental rate. We estimate that the transaction was likely done at 6.5%-7.5% 2021E cap rate. Additionally, we estimate that AREIT’s current market cap implies a cap rate of 6.5%-7.0% for AREIT’s existing assets. Our initial estimates suggest that the acquisition could raise 2021E DPS by 7% to P1.57/share. This could increase AREIT’s dividend yield from 5.7% to 6.1%. AREIT plans to establish credit facilities with banks amounting to P12bn.

MerryMart opens 17th store

MerryMart opened its 17th store at Artonstrip by Rockwell, along Katipunan avenue, in Quezon City. This is the first store under the “market” format. The MerryMart has retail formats which include MerryMart Store, MerryMart Market, MerryMart Grocery and Dark Grocery which will be online. MerryMart is targeting 25 stores by end-2020 and 100 stores by end-2021.

Emperador’s 3Q profit jumps 26% to P2.5bn – ahead

In a press release, EMP disclosed that 3Q income jumped 26% to P2.5bn on the sustained strength of its overseas business. The 3Q profit is also higher by 32%qoq. This brings 9M net income higher by 11% to P5.9bn – well ahead of estimates. “Emperador’s global business saw double-digit growth as it adapted well to new consumption trends”. EMP cited strong growth numbers of Fundador in several areas for 9M20 - +23% in US, +185% in UK, +15% in Italy.

Energy chief bans new coal-run power plants

The Department of Energy (DoE) declared a moratorium on greenfield coal power plant projects. It seeks to shift to a more flexible power supply mix. The ban will remain in effect until such time that the country will be needing additional baseload power. The DoE passed a circular that allows foreign investors to fully own geothermal plant projects in the country with an initial investment of at least US$50mn. The DoE is in the process of updating its Philippine Energy Plan for the next two decades.

Semirara 9M20 profit falls 64% YoY to P3.0bn

In a press release, Semirara reported headline 9M20 profit of P3.0bn, down 64% YoY. This appears ahead of our forecast (P3.3bn) but behind consensus (P4.6bn). In 3Q20 alone, headline profit stood at P750mn, down 68% YoY and 28% QoQ.

PGOLD 9M20 net profit +11% to P5.1bn – behind

PGOLD reported 9M20 net profit of P5.1bn (+11% YoY), tracking behind Regis and consensus FY20 estimates. For 3Q20 alone, net profit was down 4% YoY to P1.7bn as sales were flattish YoY. The underperformance was driven by a mild SSS decline in 3Q20, weighed by weak demand and a reduced supplier support (both QoQ and YoY).

Petron refinery to close “very soon”

Petron President and CEO Ramon Ang said in an online briefing that Petron’s 180k barrel/day refinery in Bataan will be closed “very soon”, citing continued losses and uneven playing field between refiners and importers. Mr. Ang added that the only way to save the refinery is going to Congress and asking lawmakers to amend the tax regime for the downstream oil industry. The statement comes after rival Pilipinas Shell Petroleum announced the permanent shutdown of its Batangas refinery.

SMC Global Power scraps coal projects, to build LNG instead

SMC Global Power is “now switching to LNG” according to SMC President Ramon Ang in a virtual briefing. The proceeds from the recently issued US$400mn senior capital securities will be used to fund capex and investments in LNG and related assets. The firm said it intends to build a line of three 850MW LNG-fired generators for a total of 2,550MW. The new LNG power plants will be constructed in Ilijan and Mr. Ang claims the first 850MW should be up and running by 2022. The company was previously planning to build coal projects with 2,400MW in Luzon, 600MW in Visayas and 628MW in Mindanao.

Colliers: Office occupancy shrinks by 191,000sqm in 3Q20

Colliers reported that the Metro Manila office occupancy contracted 191,000sqm in 3Q20 (113,000sqm in 9M20), implying that new vacancies exceeded new (gross) take-up. This led to an increase in Metro Manila office vacancy to 7.1% in 3Q20, from 4.9% in 2Q20. Office landlords lowered rental rates by as much as 20%. It is estimated that POGOs vacated c. 100,000sqm of office space in 3Q20, implying that c. 90,000sqm of space was vacated by BPOs and traditional tenants (combined). Since our channel checks suggest that BPOs continued to expand office footprint slightly, the 90,000sqm vacancy likely came from traditional tenants. Colliers also said that Metro Manila residential condominiums are down 5-25% from the 1Q20 peak.

Dito reiterates plan for 2021 launch

Dito Telecommunity, through its spokesman, says it is on track to have a commercial launch by March 2021. It says it already has 1,532 towers as of 28 Oct 2020. This implies an increase of close to 700 towers in less than one month, from 859 as of September. Note, this comes from only 300 towers as of July. It is also unclear how “tower” is defined for this count. The number of towers is supposedly enough to meet its committed 37% population coverage as of Jan 2021. The company is, however, saying that SIM cards will not be rolled out until it “goes online to the public by July” according to a media report.

Macro News

Budget deficit narrows to P138.5bn in September on lower expenditures

The National Government recorded a budget deficit of P138.5bn in September, 22% lower than the same period last year. Revenues contracted by 10% YoY, with BIR collections dropping 6.6% YoY and BOC collections dropping 13.7% YoY. Meanwhile, expenditures declined by 15% YoY due to the timing of subsidy releases and high base effects from higher infra spend last year. The full impact of Bayanihan II (P166bn), which was approved last Sept 11, is expected to be felt in succeeding periods. This brings YT-September budget deficit to P879.2bn, up 194% YoY and 32% below the revised program of P1.298tn for the same period. The gov’t is targeting a budget deficit of P1.815tn in FY20. So far, revenue collections for 9M20 are down 7.9% YoY to P2.14tn, tracking ahead of FY20 target. Meanwhile, expenditures for 9M20 reached P3.0tn, up 15% YoY and accounting for 70% of FY20 target.

BOP surplus swells to US$2.1bn in September

Balance of Payments (BoP) surplus stood at US$2.104bn in September, ballooning from the US$38mn surplus in Sep 2019. This is also higher than the US$657mn surplus in August 2020 and the highest since May 2020’s US$2.431bn. The govt cited inflows from the BSP’s forex operations and income from investments abroad and the National Government’s (NG) foreign currency deposits. The GIR level stood at US$100bn in September, equivalent to 10 months’ worth of imports. This brings YT-September BoP surplus to US$6.88bn, up 24% YoY, supported mainly by higher net foreign borrowings by the NG and lower merchandise trade deficit. The NG foreign borrowings surged 85% YoY to P509.69bn in YT-August. Earlier this month, the BSP revised its FY20 BoP projection to US$8.1bn (vs previous US$600mn).

House transmits P4.5-trillion 2021 budget bill to Senate

The House of Representatives transmitted to the Senate its final version of the P4.5tn 2021 General Appropriations Bill (GAB). The chamber was originally set to transmit the budget bill to the Senate on Oct. 28. The early transmittal of the will allow Congress to work on its passage, in time for its signing before Dec. 25.

DTI tightens rules on cement product labels

Imported cement products will no longer be allowed to carry “Product of the Philippines” labels according to the Department of Trade and Industry Secretary Ramon Lopez. Under previous rules, local companies were able to carry this label for imported cement products that are bagged in the country. The Cement Manufacturers Association of the Philippines earlier this year asked the government to investigate the origin of Philcement Corp. products that have been labeled as locally made.

BSP sees slower Dec and Jan inflation rates on base effects

Headline inflation in December and January could momentarily fall below the official 2% to 4% target range set by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) according to BSP Deputy Governor Francisco Dakila, Jr. This temporary decline will not be a factor in setting monetary policy. The BSP’s baseline projection currently stands at 2.8% for FY21 and 3% for FY22. Inflation in September 2020 stood at 2.3%; YT-September inflation rate at 2.5%.

“Hot money” flees for 7th straight month

Foreign portfolio investments (“hot money”) posted a net outflow of US$493.65mn in September. This is more than double the US$231.7mn net outflow in the same period last year. Additionally, this brings YT-September net outflows to US$4.5bn, +238.46% YoY.
