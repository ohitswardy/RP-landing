REGIS REPORT

11 September 2020

The PSEi gained 3.2% this week, to close just about 30 points shy of 6000. Locals led the rally, with hopes pinned on a flattening COVID curve and the economy reopening. 

The easing of domestic travel rules sparked optimism for the domestic travel industry (as well as airlines). Meawnhile, Vista Land (+8.9% WoW) disclosed that its property sales continue to recover in July and August and are likely close to pre-pandemic levels, thanks to improved demand for property outside Greater Manila. The gradual recovery in demand in suburban areas improved sentiment for other developers as well.

Gainers beat losers 21:9. Bargain hunting took drove laggard Alliance Global’s (+13.2% WoW) surge.  GT Capital (+6.8% WoW) was also one of the top gainers.

Large cap property and conglos also led, with Ayala Land (+7.8% WoW), SM Investments (+7.0% WoW), Megaworld (+6.2% WoW), and Metro Pacific (+5.7% WoW).

First Gen (+5.5% WoW) gained as well, despite issues at one of its plants.  The 414MW San Gabriel plant tripped, leaving it unavailable in the meantime.  San Gabriel is fully contracted with Meralco and has 32 of 65 days downtime allowed annually. San Gabriel is part of First Gen’s 2,000MW natural gas-fired capacity.

Philippine National Bank (+21.2% WoW) surged. The bank’s board approved “a plan to realize the market value of the Bank’s prime properties”.  No further details were released and it is unclear whether the properties will be sold outright, developed internally/in JV, etc.  The main assets would include its 10-ha head office property in the Manila Bay area, the former Allied Bank head office on Ayala Avenue in Makati among others. Regis estimates the PNB head office land alone has a market value of at least P20bn, accounting for a large chunk of market cap (~P36bn)

AC Energy (+17.8% WoW) said that its affiliate UAC Energy has sold its 20% stake in Australian renewables firm Infigen to Iberdrola at AUD0.92/sh. The average acquisition price for Infigen was AUD0.794,translating to a nice gain for AC Energy.

Globe (+2.0% WoW) says its different partnerships in the telco tower business will put up some 900 new cell sites. All new towers must allow access to all other telco players, which could accelerate coverage across the country.

Funds continued to flow out however, despite the market’s strength. Foreign net selling this week reached US$44m.  Adjusting for SM group Blocks this week, actual net outflows were higher at US$82m.

Also, turnover remained unexciting at just  US$98m (daily ave).

PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Banks - Past due rates spike to 5.3% in July (for real this time); NPLs rise to 2.8%
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Covid-19 lockdown stress begins to show

Past due (PD) ratios for Philippine banks jumped to 5.28% in July, from 3.49% in June, showing for the first time the stress that the pandemic and lockdowns have had on borrowers. NPL ratios rose at a slower pace to 2.75% in July from 2.60% in June, but are expected to climb into 2H20. Our forecasts of 4-5% peak NPL ratio seem increasingly at risk.

Past due loans surge 89% yoy, 52% mom; for real this time

PD ratio for the banking industry spiked to 5.28% in July, up 179bps from June’s 3.49%, and up 257bps from 2.71% at end-2019 (see Figure 1, page 2). PD loans surged 89% yoy and 52% mom. In nominal terms, PDs grew by nearly P200bn mom, from P378bn in June to P573bn in July (Figure 3). The surge comes as the loan grace period under the Bayanihan Act expired on 31 May. Recall that the April and May PDs also jumped, as some banks kept reporting loans as PD despite the grace period. The PD numbers fell in June as most borrowers became current again.

Stress beginning to show

The PD surge in July is worrisome – if not surprising – as the figures now begin to show the real stress that the Covid-19 pandemic and lockdowns are having on borrowers, and the economy at large. It is still unclear whether the bulk of the new PDs are coming from the corporate or consumer segments.

NPLs creeping up, +15bps mom to 2.75%; likely to accelerate into 3Q

Meanwhile, NPL ratios also rose to 2.75% in July, from 2.60% in June and 2.08% at end-2019 (Figure 2). 

As we noted in our banking sector report “Flying Blind” (20 May), we expect NPL ratios to rise significantly only in September, given the lag between PD and NPL recognition. Given the speed of the rise in PDs, though, our forecast of peak NPLs at 4-5% now seem rather optimistic.

… but new grace period could again reduce visibility on asset quality

The Bayanihan to Recover as One Act (or Bayanihan 2) – expected to be signed into law next week -includes a provision for a new 2-month grace period on loan payments. This could again delay recognition of PDs and NPLs, and could give banks much less visibility on borrowers’ cashflows.

Loan growth slows, +4.8% yoy in July (-2.6% ytd)

The PD and NPL ratios are exacerbated somewhat by the sharp slowdown in loan growth. As of July, loans of all Philippine banks – including thrift and rural banks – decelerated further to 4.8% yoy, from 5.2% in June, and 10.8% in March (Figure 4). Loans were flat (-0.2%) mom, and down 2.55% year-to-date.

Some good news – NPL cover, LLR ratios improve

To the upside, banks used the massive trading gains in 2Q20 to accelerate loan loss provisions. As a result, NPL cover improved to 111% and loan loss reserves (LLR) to total loans rose to 2.96%.

Corp. News

AC Energy partnership divests stake in Australia’s Infigen Energy

UAC Energy Holdings, owned by AC Energy Group and UPC Renewable, divested its 20% stake in Infigen Energy to Spain’s Iberdrola Group at AUD0.92 per share. UAC acquired its stake in Infigen over several months from April to July 2020 at an average price of AUD$0.794 per share. We estimate UAC made a pretax gain of around AUD24mn on the trade. The investment in Infigen along with AC Energy Group’s overseas investments have not yet been injected into the listed AC Energy Philippines.

Globe to add 900 telco towers

Globe Telecom said that its partnerships with tower builders will add 900 more cell sites. Around 200 sites will be in Cebu, Davao, and Subic while around 300 macro sites will be set in Luzon. Addtionally, around 400 sites in other areas, including Visayas and Mindanao. TO help them expand, Globe has partnerships with Aboitiz InfraCapital, ISOC-edotco, Transcend Towers Infrastructure (Philippines), CREI Philippines, and Frontier Towers & Associates.

AirAsia sees more people travelling next year

AirAsia reported that it posted an “impressive” commercial flights sales in August, closing the month with a 140% increase in seats sold. About one third of bookings registered were for 2021, reflecting a demand for future travel. This was most likely helped by AirAsia's recently launched promo, offering “buy1 take 1” tickets for travel from Jan-October 2021.

Vista Land reservation sales improve in July and August

Vista Land said that its reservation sales reached 70% of pre-COVID levels in June and improved further in July and August. Additionally, the company cited demand for residential projects outside Metro Manila. The June reservation sales reached around P4.5bn. For 1H20, reservation sales fell 37% to P25.7bn, 52% of our full-year forecast. Interestingly, some of the other developers also cited further recovery in July reservation sales such as Ayala Land and Megaworld.

House clears San Miguel’s airport city plan in Bulacan

The House of Representatives approved on final reading a bill (House Bill 7507) that will grant San Miguel Aerocity, Inc. a 50-year franchise over the Bulacan airport project. Around 218 lawmakers voted “yes” to the measure while six voted “no”. The franchise will be in effect for 50 years from the effectivity of the act, inclusive of a 10-year maximum period for the design, planning, and construction of the airport and the airport city. On August 27, the House ways and means committee approved a seperate bill (House Bill 7241) that will exempt the grantee from taxes during the 10-year construction period. 

PCC approves URC’s acquisition of Roxas Holdings’ (ROX PM) assets

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) has approved URC’s acquisition of ROX’s sugar milling, bioethanol plant, and other assets of Roxas Holdings' facility in La Carlota City, Negros Occidental. The acquisition price was not disclosed but the facilities will add c.42% (15k tons cane [TC]/day) to URC’s total rated milling capacity (35k TC/day). URC's sugar and renewables business (SURE) accounted for 12% of sales and 23% of EBIT in 1H20.

 

PAL to cut jobs anew

Philippine Airlines is looking to reduce its manpower by 35% in October according to the Philippine Daily Inquirer. Recall, PAL reduced its manpower by 300 in March. For comparision, as of end-Dec 2019, PAL had 8,211 employees. PAL is also negotiating with Macroasia (MAC PM) for a 15-20% reduction in its contracts.

First Gen’s San Gabriel plant trips

The 414MW San Gabriel plant tripped and First Gen’s contractor Siemens is investigating the issue. Because of this the plant is unavailable in the meantime. The facility is part of First Gen’s 2,000MW natural gas-fired capacity in Batangas. San Gabriel is fully contracted with Meralco and it is given 65 days downtime annually of which 32 are still available for the year. We estimate the plant’s effective selling price to be just over P3/kwh given the drop in fuel prices.

Pepsi-Cola Phils (PIP PM) to delist

PIP disclosed it will be delisting from the PSE as its public ownership has fallen below the 10% minimum requirement. The public float stood at 2.1% following the tender offer by controlling shareholder Lotte Chilsung. PIP is the exclusive bottler of PepsiCo's brands in the Phils.

 

 
 

Macro News

Loan growth slows to 6.7% in July; domestic liquidity (M3) posts mildly slower growth of 14.5% in July

Bank lending of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements grew by 6.7% in July. This is slower than the 9.6% loan growth in June. The reason cited for this was weaker corporate sector performance and declining loan demand. Additionally, the loan growth to most sectors slowed. Credit card loans grew by 27%  YoY, broadly steady vs. 28% in June while auto loan growth sharply slowed to 9.6% YoY from 29% in June. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 14.5% to P13.6tn in July. This is broadly steady from 14.9% in June 2020.

Senators question 2021 budget’s priorities amid coronavirus crisis.

Senators questioned the reduced allocations for health, social safety nets, as well as the higher allocation for infrastructure under the proposed P4.5tn budget for 2021. Senator Franklin Drilon questioned the lower allocation for health (excluding PhilHealth allocation) which was slashed by 14% to P131bn from P153bn in 2020. Meanwhile,Senator Risa Hontiveros-Baraquel raised concerns over the P20bn vaccination program, saying that a budget of P189bn is needed for vaccination (18m families of five per household at P700 per vaccine). Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III said the budget for the vaccination program was only based on estimates given by the Health department. Additionally, Mr. Drilon also expressed concern over the lack of funding for a Social Amelioration Program (SAP) next year. Mr. Avisado pointed out that the cash aid program was discontinued after easing of lockdowns. Also questionioning this was Senator Panfilo M. Lacson who asked the economic team to explain why the country’s two major infrastructure agencies still received substantial budget allocations despite their history of underspending. DPWH received the second-largest budget allocation for next year worth P667.3bn, up 52% from the P438.9bn in 2020 while the Transportation department ranked seventh with a P143.6bn budget, up 71% from the P84bn in 2020.

BSP issuing its own securities next week

The Bangko Sentral announced it will be issuing its own securities on Sept 18. The plan is to issue P20bn worth of 28-day BSP bills with the amount to be gradually increased over time. The new BSP charter finally allowed the BSP to issue its own securities, in line with shift to more market-based monetary policy.

BIR, BOC exceed collection targets in August

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) generated P172.06bn in August, down 16.3% YoY but 45.6% higher than the government’s revised target of P118.2bn for the month. This brings 8M20 collections to P1.29tn, down 11% YoY and accounting for 74% of the government’s FY20 target (P1.74tn, -20% YoY). Meanwhile, the Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P44.65bn in August, down 16.7% YoY but 32.6% higher than than target of P33.68bn. This brings 8M20 collections to P348bn, down 16% YoY and accounting for 64% of FY20 target (P542bn, -14% YoY).

Gov’t sells P30bn 3-year bond at a coupon of 2.375%

The Bureau of Treasury raised P30bn in 3-year T-bonds. The offer was more than 3x oversubscribed with total tenders reaching P87.963bn. The bond fetched a coupon rate of 2.375% and an average yield of 2.279%. The coupon was lower than the 4.75% in the July 2019 auction. For comparision BVAL was around 2.380%.

Gov’t allows use of rapid antigen tests for domestic travel

The government allowed the use of rapid antigen test as a substitute for RT-PCR test as a requirement for domestic travel. The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) for the management of emerging infectious diseases issued Resolution No. 69 last September 7.

Gov’t makes full award of T-bills as strong demand keeps rates low

The Bureau of Treasury borrowed P20bn as planned via the T-bills as the offer was almost thrice oversubscribed, with bids reaching P56.68bn. Yields on the short-term papers ended mixed. The 91-day T-bill fetched an average rate of 1.167%, down by 1.3 bps (BVAL: 1.199%), the 182-day T-bill fetched  1.518%, up 9.7 bps from 1.421% previously (BVAL: 1.469%) while the 364-day T-bill fetched 1.807%, up 1.9 bps from last week’s 1.788% (BVAL: 1.806%).

Inflation moderates to 2.4% in August

Inflation slowed down to 2.4% in August from 2.7% in July. This was below the BSP’s expected range of 2.5-3.3% for the month. The government cited slower inflation from food items (meat, fruits, milk, cheese, eggs, and fish), of which rice inflation (9.59% weight) still at negative 1.1%. Meanwhile, core inflation also eased to 3.1% in August from 3.3% in July. This means YT-August inflation rate stood at 2.5%, within government’s target range of 2-4%.

Balance of payments at US$8mn surplus in July

The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$8mn in July. This also reflects inflows from the National Government (NG) foreign loan proceeds and BSP’s income from investments abroad. It also reflects the record high GIR level of US$98.6bn in end-July (~8.9 months of imports). This brings YT-July BOP surplus to US$4.12bn, down from US$5.04bn in the same period last year. BSP’s end-2020 BOP surplus target at US$600mn.

External trade still down in July

Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority showed the trade deficit plunged by 50% YoY to US$1.8bn in July. Imports fell 24% YoY (vs -23% in June) while exports dropped 10% YoY (vs. -13% in June). The sower decline in exports reflects improving demand abroad but continued contraction of imports reflects weak domestic demand. Imports of raw and intermediate goods dropped 14.8% YoY while imports of capital goods shrank faster by 29.8% YoY. This brings 7M20 trade deficit to US$12.5bn, down 48% YoY.

Dollar reserves seen to top US$100bn

BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said it was likely for the gross International reserves (GIR) to top the US$100bn mark before the end of the year. The BSP plans to actively participate in the gold trading market largely because of the change in the price dynamics of gold. The GIR stood at a record high of US$98.6bn in July due to the national government’s foreign borrowings to finance its COVID-19 response. The GIR is equivalent 8.9 months of imports which was higher than the GIR level of US$87.8bn in end-2019.

Past due ratio spikes to 5.3% in July; NPL ratio at 2.75%

The past due (PD) ratios jumped to 5.28% in July, compared to 3.49% in June and to 2.71% at end-2019. In nominal terms, PD loans surged 89% yoy to P573bn. The spike comes as the loan grace periods under Bayanihan Act 1 expired on 31 May. Meanwhile, NPL ratios also rose to 2.75% in July, from 2.60% in June and 2.08% at end-2019. To the upside, banks used the massive trading gains to accelerate loan loss provsions in 1H20. As a result, NPL cover improved to 111% and loan loss reserves (LLR) to total loans rose to 2.96%. The PD and NPL ratios are exacerbated somewhat by the sharp slowdown in loan growth. As of July, loans of the Philippine Banking System (PBS) – including thrift and rural banks – decelerated further to 4.8% yoy, from 5.2% in June, and 10.8% in March. Loans were flat (-0.2%) mom, and down 2.55% year-to-date.
