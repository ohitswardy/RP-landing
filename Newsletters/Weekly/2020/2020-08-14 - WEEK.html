REGIS REPORT

14 August 2020

The Phils jumped 3.95% this week to close above the 6000 mark. 

Foreign outflows somewhat abated, allowing the market to snap five consecutive weeks of declines.

On a headline basis, foreigners turned buyers for US$61m.  Although adjusting for SM group transfers (US$24m net inflow) and the AREIT IPO (US$88m net inflow) the market registered outflows of  US$51m. This lower than last week's adjusted net outflows of US$111m.

Gains were broad-based with only 4 of the 30 index names failing to close the week with gains.

Lockdown favorites such as First Gen (-16.3% WoW) and PLDT (-2.8% WoW) took a backseat this week as investors picked up sold down names. 

PSEi rebal names moved, with Semirara (-11.7% WoW) contracting this week to its deletion from the PSEi. Idex inclusion Emperador (+20.7% WoW) meanwhile surged. JGS (00.16% WoW) and PLDT meanwhile saw downweights.

There was a whole swathe of 2Q results this week and most showed massive drops in profitability.

Within the property space Ayala Land’s (+3.4% WoW) 2Q20 net profit plunged 97%  due to weak residential revenue and losses in the mall business. 2Q20 reservation sales fell 64% to P13.6bn.

The same reasons caused Megaworld’s (+1.0% WoW) 2Q20 profit to fall 58%.  Weakness at the mall division also pushed Robinsons Land’s (+8.0% WoW)2Q20 net profit down 76%.

 

 

Severely curtailed operations led to 2Q net losses across different sectors.

Losses at Cebu Air (+6.65% WoW) ballooned to P7.95bn in 2Q20 alone following the pandemic-induced lockdown.  Also not surprising, Bloomberry incurred a loss of P4.7b in 2Q due to the suspension of casino operations.

Shakey’s Pizza (-4.3% WoW) also reported a 2Q20 loss as revenues plunged 54% YoY.

Ayala Corp’s (+6.9% WoW) 2Q20 net profit fell 96% as contributions from Ayala Land  () and BPI () slide and losses from industrials ballooned. 

Of the few exceptions was Security Bank (+8.2% WoW), which managed to book hefty trading gains, allowing it to post strong profits.  1H profits of P5.7bn are already 90% of REGIS FY20E even after the bank set aside P11bn in provisions.  SECB was the 2nd top gainer of the week.

The top gainer was LT Group (+8.5% WoW). LTG’s 2Q income declined just 21%, bringing 1H to P10.03bn (+9%) – ahead of estimates. Despite shortfalls from PNB and Asia Brewery, profits from tobacco (>80% of profit) grew 6% even on soft cigarette volumes.

Puregold (+2.3% WoW) also delivered a 24% YoY expansion in 2Q20 profits.  Revenue growth was 13% on mid single digit (est) for the. Puregold brand and implied high-teens SSSG for S&R.

 
 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Strategy: Pushing on a string
 
 
by Rafa Garchitorena – rafa.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Monetary push, but without the fiscal pull

The BSP has unleashed a barrage of monetary and regulatory measures in response to the Covid-19 crisis. In contrast, the fiscal response has been underwhelming; we are not likely to see a massive stimulus bill. While we have likely seen the worst – with 2Q20 GDP at -16.5% yoy – we forecast a much slower recovery for the economy and for company earnings. We would stay defensive as a base case, but offer up reopening plays and deep value names for contrarians.

GDP bottoms in 2Q, but Covid-19 surge prompts MECQ lockdown

2Q20 GDP dropped 16.5% yoy as lockdowns hit the country’s consumption-driven economy. While data show sequential improvement, continued restrictions on public transport point to a “steady, yet moderate” recovery. After a surge in Covid-19 cases, stricter Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine (MECQ) rules were re-imposed for two weeks in August, which will take some momentum off. 

Remittances plunge, unemployment jumps – longer-term hit to incomes

The global pandemic has taken its toll on Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs). Remittances fell a record 19% yoy in May across all major geographies. Locally, unemployment spiked to 17.7% in April. While both are likely to improve sequentially, the damage to household incomes and balance sheets could take longer to repair.

Fiscal stimulus underwhelming, constrained by budget deficit

The combined stimulus packages of Bayanihan 1 and 2, and CREATE (corporate tax cuts) will likely reach some 3.6% of GDP. The figure is underwhelming compared to the economic contraction in 2Q, and compared to our Asean neighbors. The government is wary of the budget deficit, which it expects to hit 9.6% of GDP this year and 8.5% in 2021.

BSP’s aggressive monetary, regulatory intervention – pushing on a string?

In contrast, the BSP has moved decisively, cutting policy rates by 17bps ytd to an all-time low 2.25%, cutting RRR by 200bps, and buying Treasuries. It also provided regulatory measures to ease banks capital rules and to promote SME lending. The lack of a “pull” from fiscal stimulus, though, likely blunts the efficacy of loose monetary policy.

Staying defensive, but looking out for recovery opportunities 

Our base case has PSEi earnings falling 35% yoy in 2020E; rebounding 27% in 2021E (still 17% off 2019 though). We stay defensive, with PLDT, Puregold, Century Pacific Food, Meralco and ICTSI as our top picks. We are mindful though that light investor positioning and reasonable valuations (15.3x PE on our 2021E) could see big upswings on Covid treatment/vaccine news.

 

 
LT Group - 2Q: Profit outperforms on tobacco, liquor contributions
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA – gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

2Q profit -21%yoy, but 1H tracking ahead

LTG reported a net income of P3.814bn in 2Q20, a decrease of 21%yoy and 39%qoq, on depressed contributions from its bank and beverage units. Its share in the tobacco segment in 2Q20, meanwhile, registered a growth of 6% from last year’s levels despite lower cigarette volumes. For 1H20, LTG’s net income of P10.027bn grew 9%yoy and is tracking ahead of our estimates for the full year.

Tobacco profit +6% in 2Q, on track to beat FY20 estimate

While sequentially lower, PMFTC’s profit contribution grew 6%yoy to P3.194bn in 2Q20. Profits managed to stay afloat, despite a 26% drop in volumes in the quarter caused by quarantine restrictions. Margins are much higher across its product line-up due to price hikes implemented last August (P20/pack, +20%) that were well above the increase in excise tax. As restrictions on movement ease, cigarette volumes can be reasonably expected to recover from the lows. Profits thus appear likely to beat our full-year estimate (-5%).

 

Bank provisions rise as trading gains, loan growth soften

PNB’s contribution fell to just P34mn in 2Q after it booked an estimated P5.1bn in provisions. Provisions in 1H20 amounted to P8.44bn, 10x last year’s level. Trading gains of P1.5bn were double last year’s level, but down from the P2.2bn booked in 1Q20. Year-to-date, its loan book was up just 1% to P603bn.

Beverage units mixed; liquor industry resilient

Asia Brewery incurred a loss of P34mn in 2Q20 on wider losses from its beer JV and lower convenience store sales of non-alcoholic beverages. On the other hand, Tanduay registered a 138% growth in profit to P342mn in 2Q20. Volumes held up well, down just 4% in 2Q20 despite the liquor ban and a 41% cut in selling and marketing expenses (similar to competitors). Given relatively resilient sales, liquor companies may be prompted to review their advertising strategies moving forward

 
DMCI Holdings - 2Q profit down 57% YoY on lockdown but stock now offers attractive value
 
 
by Klyne Resullar – klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H20 core profit down 60% to P2.61bn

DMCI reported 1H20 profit of P2.03bn, down 69% YoY. Excluding one-offs and forex adjustments, 1H20 core profit fell short at P2.61bn, down 60% YoY. Apart from halting construction activity, the lockdown also resulted in lower than expected power and coal prices, as well as coal sales volumes (see Semirara Alert: Lockdown pares down 2Q profit; Reducing estimates and TP, 11 August 2020). 2Q20 core profit may have been down 57% YoY to P1.59bn but we highlight the QoQ bounce. We cut our estimates (-69%/-46%) and target price (-32%) but maintain Buy.

Reservation sales drop 49% YoY in 1H, 2Q EBITDA margin recovers QoQ

DMCI Homes’ reservation sales in 2Q20 fell 63% to P3.3bn, bringing 1H20 sales down 49% YoY to P10.3bn. Management scaled back its FY20 launch target from to P61-106bn (4-9 projects) from P209bn (20 projects) initially. Profit from this division was 66% YoY lower in 2Q20 due to limited revenue recognition. EBITDA margin recovered to 19% in 2Q, as costs normalized. Management however noted that the ramp-up remains sluggish due strict social distancing protocols.

 

Construction unit posts loss in 2Q

The construction unit posted a loss of P91mn in 2Q20 after the lockdown halted operations, bringing 1H20 contributions to a mere P79mn (-82% YoY). Its order book stood at P65.9bn in end-June, slightly down from P68bn in end-2019. Even though social distancing protocols are relatively easier to implement in infrastructure projects, management noted that it has only managed to deploy c. 70% of its workforce so far due to constraints in public transportation. Reducing estimates and SOTP NAV

We downgrade our FY20/FY21 profit forecasts by 69%/46% to reflect the impact of the pandemic. We also downgrade our SOTP NAV and TP by 33% to P7.30/sh and P4.80/sh, respectively, due to lowered valuations of its key subsidiaries.

 
Semirara Mining and Power Corp - Lockdown pares down 2Q profit; Reducing estimates and TP
 
 
by Klyne Resullar – klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H20 core profit down 63% YoY to P2.14bn

Headline 1H20 profit fell 61% YoY to P2.24bn.  Excluding forex adjustments and other one-offs, we estimate recurring profit dropped 63% YoY to P2.14bn, tracking below Regis and consensus estimates.  We attribute the shortfall to lower ASP and sales volumes for both coal mining and power units.  In 2Q20 alone, core profit dropped 68% YoY to P1.1bn.  

Lockdown hits coal mining unit

The coal mining unit saw 2Q20 profits plunge 65% YoY, 38% QoQ to P990mn.  Apart from the 27% YoY drop in coal ASPs, management blamed the lockdown for the 21% QoQ, 41% YoY drop in 2Q sales volumes.  Sales volumes only reached 5.75mn MT in 1H20, down 27% YoY and tracking well below initial guidance (16mn MT).  The sharp drop in topline, however, was partly cushioned by a 32% YoY drop in cash cost per MT to P860, thanks to lower fuel prices and other cost reduction measures.  

 

Mild recovery for power in 2Q20

The power unit reversed its losses, booking P963mn of EBITDA and P77mn of profit in 2Q20 alone due to the completion of Unit 2’s rehab in May.  While spot prices and volumes may still be lower YoY in 2H20, we believe earnings for this unit will continue to rise sequentially.   Earnings and TP revisions

We reduce our FY20/FY21 earnings forecasts by 73%/62% after lowering estimates on coal ASPs, power ASPs, and coal sales volumes (see Figures 2 and 3).  We also reduce our price target by 46% to P14/sh. At 6x FY21E PER and 13% dividend yield, we believe the stock is undervalued. Maintain Buy.

 
Filinvest Land - Weak 2Q masked by unusual items; residential sales bounce in June
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA – carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Earnings flattered by unusual items

2Q20 net profit fell 27% YoY to P952mn, bringing the 1H20 figure down 26% to P2.3bn. While this appears in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts, residential revenue was actually quite weak. Earnings were helped by a combination of items that are likely unsustainable: an unusually low tax rate (10% in 1H), unusually high equitized earnings (+248% in 1H from a large lot sale), and an arguably unsustainable drop in opex of rental services (-9% in 1H). 

Recovery in June/July reservation sales

Residential revenue dropped 56% YoY in 2Q20 as construction activities were curtailed during the hard lockdown. Moreover, recent government guidelines will make it difficult to ramp up construction. 2Q20 reservation sales fell 54% to P2.4bn, P2.2bn of which came in June. This is already higher than the monthly average of P1.6bn-P1.7bn in 2019/1Q20 and July sales remained strong. However, 1H20 OFW sales fell to 20% of total, from 31% previously. Furthermore, a few sales (from prior years) were restructured. The buyers availed of either longer payment schemes or cheaper units.  

 

Some POGO office tenants restart operations

Office revenue jumped 22% in 1H20 (to P3.0bn) thanks to c. 10% floor area expansion. Most tenants continue to pay rent promptly and there have been no pre-terminations/renegotiations so far. Moreover, FLI’s two largest POGO tenants, which occupy 13% of its office portfolio, have restarted operations.    

Malls recover in July but will take another hit

2Q20 mall revenue fell 82% to P85mn due to the hard lockdown until end-May. As of July, about 60% of mall space was operational (from c. 20% in April) and foot traffic was about 40% of pre-COVID levels. Unfortunately, the mall business will take another hit due to the hard lockdown in August

 
Shakey’s Pizza - 2Q20 net loss as lockdown hits operations; cost rationalization outlined
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat – carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

Recurring 1H20 net loss at P167mn – in line with expectations

PIZZA reported headline net loss of P290mn in 1H20, which includes a one-off expense of P123mn relating to cost rationalization efforts. On a recurring basis, the net loss of P167mn is tracking in line with our expectations. The recurring net loss in 2Q20 alone was P281mn given the full impact of the lockdown. 

Revenues drop 54% amidst lockdown measures in 2Q20

2Q20 revenues dropped 54% YoY in 2Q as the company had to temporarily shut nearly 90% of its total store network at the onset of the lockdown in April. While majority of these stores have reopened since, sales continue to track below prepandemic levels due to limited dine-in sales, which typically account for c.60% of sales.

 

Tight rein on costs cushions EBITDA

Notably, the company barely broke even on a “recurring” EBITDA basis in 2Q20. This was attributed to aggressive cost saving initiatives, including renegotiating trading terms with suppliers, rental waivers with landlords, as well as the suspension of non-essential head office expenses.

Cost rationalization initiatives underway

PIZZA has resumed operations for some 95% of its total store network as of endJuly, though dine-in services had to be suspended beginning Aug 4 as lockdown restrictions in Metro Manila were tightened anew. The company incurred a nonrecurring cost of P123mn in 1H20 relating to cost rationalization efforts to streamline operations, including the permanent closure of approximately 5% of its store network (281 stores total), primarily company-owned stores located in malls. This should provide some cushion on profitability and cash flow as nearterm revenues are likely to remain challenged.

 
Cebu Air Losses swell to P8.6bn in 2Q; Cost reduction initiatives taking hold
 
 
by Klyne Resullar – Klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H20 EBITDAR down 91% YoY to P1.39bn, Core net loss at P9.51bn – In line

CEB posted a core net loss (i.e. excluding FX adj) of P8.65bn in 2Q20 alone, bringing 1H20 core net loss to P9.51bn. The results are in line with our expectations but is tracking behind consensus (P6.14bn net loss).  1H20 EBITDAR collapsed 91% YoY to P1.39bn, after incurring an EBITDAR loss of P2.15bn in 2Q.

Operations remain challenged after lockdown

CEB’s flights dropped  95% YoY in 2Q, with pax volumes down 99% YoY to a mere 62k.  Average fares (pax + ancillary) fell but revenues from cargo (c. 7% of normalized revenues) were not as affected. With travel restrictions still in place post-lockdown, the company has only managed to re-start c. 6-9% of pre-Covid capacity for July and early August.  However, since MECQ was re-imposed in mid-Aug, capacity is now back to 5% of pre-Covid levels.  It is worth noting that the Philippines is the only country in ASEAN where leisure travel is still disallowed.  With varying regulations per domestic airport/local government, the company is also facing difficulties in mounting domestic flights. 

 

Slash-and-burn cost cutting initiatives begin to take hold

Cash as at end-June dropped to P9.7bn from P17.5bn in end-March.  With recent cost reduction initiatives, management noted that its cash burn (i.e. opex, debt service, lease payments) has now gone down to c. US$30-35mn per month from c. US$40mn.  It is about to reach an agreement with its aircraft supplier to defer aircraft deliveries and is also close to finalizing a sale-and-leaseback transaction.  Moreover, the company further reduced it 2020 capex guidance to P8.3bn from P13bn as of May and P28bn pre-lockdown.     

Capital raising remains a possibility

Even though management is taking good strides in managing its costs, they admit that more needs to be done to ensure it can retain its competitiveness.  It is therefore exploring various options to raise capital, including equity.

 
Security Bank - 1H20: Tapping the brakes on consumer loans; massive trading gains again in 2Q
 
 
by Rafa Garchitorena – rafa.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Massive trading gains again in 2Q; 1H20 provisions already at our FYE

SECB reported headline profit of P5.7bn, up 14%yoy, and already accounting for nearly 90% of our FY20E, thanks to another monster quarter for trading gains, and a huge tax benefit. The bank used the gains to accelerate provisioning to P11bn, already equivalent to our FY20F. As management had guided earlier, consumer loan growth slowed to 25% yoy, and actually contracted 3% qoq.

Loans down qoq, reassessing consumer push; NIMs up on 27%CASA growth

Loan growth slowed to 7% yoy, compared to 14% in 1Q. Consumer loans decelerated sharply to +25% from the +44% clocked in 1Q; on a qoq basis, consumer loans were down 3%. At a call we hosted in May, management guided for a "significant deceleration" in consumer loans as it reassesses its retail strategy in light of the Covid-19 pandemic. NII meanwhile jumped 34% on lower funding costs as CASA deposits surged 27% yoy

 

Trading gains, tax benefit used to accelerate provisions to P11bn in 1H

SECB booked another monster quarter for trading gains up 6-fold yoy to P7.1bn, well above our FY20E. The bank had a negative tax position of P1.1bn, as trading gains on the FX book are not subject to local tax. These allowed the bank to further accelerate provisioning to P11bn in 1H, already equal to our FY20E. NPL ratio was flat qoq at 1.58% while coverage jumped to 174%, from 128% in 1Q. However, given the March-May moratorium, NPLs will likely rise in 2H20.

“Core” operations in line

Outside of the large, lumpy numbers, core operations – NII, fees, opex – were broadly in line with our expectations.

Macro and Political News

Lower House approves Bayanihan II on final reading

The House of Representatives approved of third and final reading HB 6953 or Bayanihan II. The Bill seeks P162bn in standby funds to support COVID-19 operations and response. This amount is slightly higher than Senate version (P140bn).

June UKB loan growth slows to 9.6%; total loans probably closer to 5%.

The BSP reported that loans of Universal and Commercial Banks (UKBs) grew 9.6% yoy, slower than the 11.3% recorded in May. However, as we have noted in the past, the UKB data is bloated by the mergers of RCBC Savings into parent RCBC in July 2019, and of PNB Savings into PNB in March 2020. The BSP's UKB data only covers the parent company, and not its thrift/rural bank subsidiaries. In April and May, the BSP reported UKB loan growth rates of 12.7% and 11.3% respectively. However, the data for all banks (Philippine Banking System, or PBS) showed loan growth of just 7.4% and 7.3% respectively. This is why we tend to look at the (PBS) data. While it is slightly delayed, it is not subject to this kind of "noise".

Domestic liquidity growth eases to 14.9% in June.

Domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 14.9% in June to P13.6 tn (-1.1% MoM). This was slower than the 16.7% expansion in May

Government revises fiscal targets

The government revised its fiscal targets, targeting larger budget deficits from 2020-2022. For FY20, the government is targeting a deficit of P1.8 tn, equivalent to 9.6% of GDP (vs. previous deficit-to-GDP target of 8.4%). <eanwhile, for FY21 and FY22, the gov’t is targeting deficit-to-GDP of 8.5% and 7.2%, respectively (vs. 6.6% and 5.0%, previously). Additionally, the government tweaked its infrastructure outlay targets (i.e. includes NG infrastructure, infra subsidies to GOCCs, LGU transfers).
