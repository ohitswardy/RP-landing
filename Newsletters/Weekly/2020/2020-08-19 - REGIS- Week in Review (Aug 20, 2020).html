REGIS REPORT

19 August 2020

The Phils market dropped 1.2% this week on lackluster trading as investors continued to be wary of an economy still struggling to recover.  

Foreign net selling was heavy at US$40m, especially against very quiet volumes of US$109m (ave daily). Adjusted for SM group blocks, net outflows would have been higher at US$77m

This week, the greater Manila area went back to looser lockdown restrictions (“general community quarantine” or GCQ), two weeks after President Duterte tightened them at the request of medical workers. Nonetheless, with imposed curfews, limited public transport capacity, and new  daily cases failing to peak, movement is still expected to be constricted.

The 2Q earnings season concluded this week with the final trickle of results still beset with disappointments and net losses. The last installment of results rounded out a painful quarter, with 51% of results falling below expectations (only 26% came in ahead) and 9 of 43 companies under coverage posting “core” net losses  

GTCAP’s (+1.5% WoW) 2Q net core profit tom common contracted 59% YoY as Toyota and Fed Land incurred losses during the quarter. Nonetheless, Toyota’s sales volumes had recovered by 18% MoM in July.  Given the re-imposition of MECQ early this month though, sales could suffer once again and put Regis’ FY20E sales volume target (90k units) at risk. 

Cosco Capital’s (+1.4% WoW) 2Q also fell behind as key segments outside of PGOLD were negatively impacted by the lockdown in 2Q20. Real Estate saw a 69% drop in profit as it extended rental waivers to its commercial tenants, while the Liquor Distribution business saw a 35% drop in volumes amidst bans on liquor retailing and dine-in at restaurants.

Even defensive Puregold (+0.99% WoW) whose profits grew 24% YoY in 2Q saw its Puregold brand’s SSSG (+4%) decelerate in 2Q20 given limited mobility for its target market (low- to lower-middle income consumers). Nonetheless, S&R's SSSG accelerated to +20% due to continued pantry stocking.

Max's Group (-3.7% WoW) meanwhile reported a net loss in 2Q20 as the company had to temporarily shut operations in late-Mar/early-Apr, which led to a 50% drop in same-store sales.

Meanwhile, Alliance Global (-3.7% WoW) reported a 78% YoY decline in 2Q profits.  The casino and restaurant segments fell short, though better-than-expected volumes at the liquor segment  to P838mn, bringing 1H20 profit 53% lower to P3.8bn – in line. Liquor tempered the shortfalls from casino and restaurants after posting better-than-expected volumes (-15%) and profits (+24%2Q) in 2Q.

First Gen’s (+3.1% WoW) 2Q profits came in line, falling 14% YoY due to lower electricity demand.

 

 
 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Max’s Group - 2Q20 net loss widens due to lockdown
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H20 net loss at P600mn – behind expectations
MAXS reported a net loss of P600mn in 1H20, trailing our FY20E. Revenues dropped 46% YoY to P3.8bn, also tracking behind estimates. The netloss in 2Q20 alone widened to P431mn vs 1Q20 as operations felt the full impact of the lockdown.

2Q20 revenues plunge 71% YoY due to temporary store closures
Revenues dropped 71% YoY in 2Q20 as the company had to temporarily close stores during the lockdown. It completely ceased operations from 25 Mar to 4 Apr, and gradually reopened stores throughout the quarter. By end-June, it had resumed 85% of its 745-store network globally (686 Philippines/59 International). Same-store sales during the period plunged 50% YoY in 2Q20. While the company did not provide specifics by brand, Yellow Cab and Krispy Kreme (collectively c.30% of systemwide sales) outperformed as these are traditionally strong in the delivery and take-away channels. These two brands typically generate less than 15% of sales from dine-in.

Operating loss in 2Q20, despite cost management efforts
The company’s operating loss widened QoQ to P509mn due to the temporary store closures. The loss came even as the company incurred lower opex from waived rents and reduced labor cost.


Cost management strategies under way
The co has outlined several cost management strategies to help mitigate the impact of lost dine-in sales (historically 56% of total sales). Among these are the permanent closure of some stores (33 already closed in 1H20), manpower reduction, and negotiation of rental waivers. For the co’s mall-based stores (47% of PH store network) in particular, rental waivers have been extended by mall operators while these are temporarily not operating.

 
Jollibee Foods Corp. - REGIS Events: 2Q/1H20 post-results call with CFO
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

The worst is likely over, but road to recovery will be long
We hosted JFC’s Chief Financial Officer, Mr. Ysmael Baysa, to provide more insight on the company’s latest results, and its outlook on how the business is adapting to the changing environment. While the company has likely seen the worst in 2Q20, the road to recovery is long, and its trajectory will vary across the different markets where it operates. JFC’s businesses are expected to generate positive operating income by the end of 2020, and this is largely hinged on cost savings realized from its P7bn business transformation amidst continued pressure on top line.


Philippines underperforms on strictest and longest lockdown
JFC’s Philippine business was the key underperformer in 2Q20 amidst one of the strictest lockdowns globally, which limited consumer mobility and disallowed dine-in (c.70% of pre-pandemic sales). Group same store sales in 2Q20 declined 41%, with the Philippines alone plunging 50%. In terms of contribution, the Philippines accounted for just under 50% of systemwide sales, down from c.70% pre-pandemic.

Long road to recovery
While 2Q20 is likely to be the worst JFC has seen, the road to recovery will be long, and the rate of recovery will vary across the different markets where the company operates. Management is more upbeat about markets where GDP per capita is higher and/or the adoption of online services is more widespread (North America and China). Meanwhile, the company is more cautious about the Philippines, where its primary target market (low- to middle-income households) is likely to experience more permanent erosion in incomes amidst rising unemployment and falling OFW remittances.


Cost management amidst volatile top line
We share management’s view that industry dine-in sales will not recover to prepandemic levels within the next 1-2 years given social distancing measures, the increased risk of intermittent localized lockdowns, and general concern of consumers about leaving their homes in the absence of a widely-available cure or vaccine. Management is undertaking steps to manage profitability and cash flow through its business transformation program announced earlier in the year, which will streamline store operations and re-calibrate it to beef up capabilities for other channels (e.g. delivery, take-away) outside of dine-in.

 
Puregold - Lockdown hits 2Q20 SSSG, margins; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H20 net profit +20% to P3.4bn – tracking behind
PGOLD’s 1H20 net profit grew 20% YoY to P3.4bn, tracking slightly behind expectations. The underperformance was partly due to a slowdown in SSSG for the core Puregold brand in 2Q20. For 2Q20 alone, sales growth slowed to +13% YoY (+17% in 1Q20), while net profit jumped 24% YoY.


Impact of lockdown on 2Q20 SSSG between brands mixed
The 2Q20 lockdown’s impact between PGOLD’s two brand was divergent. On the one hand, the core Puregold brand (75% of consolidated sales) saw a moderation in SSSG QoQ (+4.4% in 2Q20) due to the lack of mobility for its target market (lowto lower-middle income consumers) in the absence of public transportation. On the flipside, S&R’s SSSG (+19.7% in 2Q20) accelerated QoQ, boosted by pantry stocking for its target market (upper-middle to upper income households).

Gross margin decline offset by tight opex controls
Gross margin for both Puregold and S&R brands contracted YoY (-60bps and - 100bps, respectively). For Puregold, suppliers held back on trade spending amidst weak sentiment. Meanwhile, S&R implemented heavier discounting in absence of its Members’ Treat sale (usually held in Apr and Oct). The gross margin contraction was offset by proportionately lower opex for both brands.


Tweaking 2020-21E, raising target price to P62/sh (+9%) - maintaining Buy
We are tweaking our forecasts, trimming FY20 profit estimates by 6% and lifting FY21 by 2% following the results and to adjust for new accounting standards. Notwithstanding the relative weakness in 2Q20, we believe trends should improve sequentially as lockdown measures ease. We are maintaining our Buy on PGOLD on a revised target price of P62/sh (+9%), which implies 20x 2021E PE, broadly in line with its historical average (20x) and the average for its peers (21x).

 
GT Capital - Lockdown pushes auto, property units to 2Q loss
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA - gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core profit falls 59% to P2.88bn in 1H20 – Behind estimates
1H20 headline profit fell 61% YoY to P2.74bn. Excluding one-offs at MPI (see Metro Pacific: 2Q profit down 63% YoY; Buyback to resume, 5 August 2020), core profit to common dropped 59% YoY to P2.88bn, tracking behind Regis and consensus estimates, as the 2Q loss for the auto and property segments weighed. Implied 2Q profit to common shares plunged 95% YoY to P183mn.

Auto unit incurs loss in 2Q; stricter lockdown could hurt sales again
The auto business recorded a 77% drop in 1H20 profit to P994mn, implying a net loss of P436mn in 2Q. Vehicle sales plunged 81% YoY in the quarter, as its 71 dealerships and manufacturing plant were closed from mid-March to mid-May. Sales volumes recovered in June (+208% MoM to 7.5k units) and July (+18% MoM to 8.9k) but the re-imposition of stricter lockdowns early this month may adversely affect sales again. The company expressed confidence it can still reach 100,000 unit sales this year (vs actual sales of 42k YT-July) but we think that may be a tall order. One silver lining among the clouds is the improvement in gross margin (+100bp QoQ to 14%), likely from a stronger PHP.

 

Bank: Strong trading gains
Income at Metrobank fell 30% YoY in 1H20 and 53% YoY in 2Q20 (see Metrobank: Strong trading, NII allow for P23bn provision; 1H20 NPAT -30% to P9.1bn, 3 August 2020). The results were a beat, however, as strong trading gains allowed the bank to raise provisions sharply in 2Q. Loans contracted 5% YoY (and -10.5% in 1H20), but NII was strong, up 22% in 1H thanks to a 20% jump in CASA deposits.


Construction halt hits property unit; reservation sales fall
Fed Land dipped to a loss in 2Q as the lockdown put a halt to most construction activity and thus, revenue recognition for its projects. Reservation sales declined to P1.2bn in the quarter from P7.9bn in 1Q

 
Alliance Global - 2Q: Liquor profits temper losses from casino, fast food
 
 
by Aaron Salvador - aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

2Q profit -78% to P838mn
AGI’s 2Q20 net income fell to P838mn (-72%qoq, -78%yoy), dragged by the lockdowns. While the casino and restaurant segments incurred losses because of pandemic restrictions, liquor beat expectations on surprisingly resilient volumes. The result brings 1H20 net income 53% lower to P3.798bn – in line with our FY20 estimate.


Liquor surprises on domestic and international fronts
2Q20 income increased 24%yoy to P1.869bn, lifted by a 103% jump in profit from its whisky segment (P694mn). Brandy, consisting of Emperador and Fundador, was also resilient despite the liquor restrictions in April-May. Revenue for the quarter was down only 4% while profit was flat.

Property – office segment holds up
Megaworld’s 2Q20 profit fell 58% to P1.898bn on curtailed mall operations and residential construction. Residential and mall revenues fell 56% and 85%, respectively. On the other hand, revenues from office segment rose 5%. MEG is among the more defensive property names because of its office portfolio.


Return to stricter restrictions to mean longer recovery time for casino, fast food
Travellers and Golden Arches incurred losses of P2.757bn and P840mn in 2Q, respectively. The segments were hardest hit by the lockdowns that suspended casino operations and limited QSRs to take-out, drive-thru and delivery for most of the quarter. With the return of stricter restrictions in August, there is an increased likelihood that these will remain in the red in 3Q20.

4x PE on 2021 estimates
At 4x PE and 0.3x PB on our FY21E estimates, we think the stock is worth a look, especially for those with longer holding periods

 
Megawide - Posts net loss in 2Q despite outperformance of landport unit
 
 
by Klyne Resullar - klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H20 core net loss at P575mn – In line
Megawide incurred a headline net loss of P398mn in 1H20. Stripping out MTM losses on swaps and preferred dividends, we estimate core net loss to common shares stood at P575mn. The results appear in line with our estimates (P1.5bn net loss for FY20) but behind consensus (P409mn loss), as we anticipate the airport and construction units incurring losses in succeeding periods. Despite the remarkable performance of the landport division, the company still posted a core net loss of P667mn in 2Q20. 2Q20 EBITDA was a mere P39mn, bringing 1H20 EBITDA down 35% YoY to P1.4bn.


Airport: Low and slow recovery
Travel restrictions caused the airport unit to incur a core loss of P280mn in 2Q20 and P372mn in 1H20. Pax volumes and PSC revenues collapsed 99% YoY while non-aero revenues dropped 84% YoY. With Cebu put on lockdown for most of July and stricter lockdown (“MECQ”) re-imposed in NCR early this month, we reckon volume recovery will remain elusive, which could mean losses will continue. The company is still in talks with its lenders to restructure the debt at this unit.

Lockdown freezes construction activity; Road to recovery is riddled with risks
The construction unit incurred a loss of P163mn in 2Q20, after construction activity was stopped from mid-March to May. The order book stood at P47.7bn in end-June, down from P52.4bn in end-Dec. With strict social distancing requirements at sites, the company admitted that it is currently negotiating to pass on some of these cost increases to its clients. Some clients, particularly retail, also signaled intentions to defer projects. Note that the company has historically booked more costs in 4Q, suggesting a muted margin recovery.


Terminal/Landport: Resilient office revenues
Profit contributions from its landport unit jumped 27% QoQ to P121mn in 2Q, bringing 1H20 profit to P255mn, already exceeding our FY20E on lower-thanexpected expenses. Revenues from the office segment (80% of total) were resilient and more than offset the drop in retail. The company continued to bill its office tenants during ECQ.

 
Cosco Capital - Lockdown hits all major segments in 2Q20; target price cut to P9.70/sh (-17%)
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat - carissa.mangubat@regis.ph
 

1H20 net profit (-6% YoY to P2.5bn) lags expectations
COSCO’s net profit declined 6% YoY to P2.5bn, trending behind expectations. All key segments underperformed, largely due to the lockdown in 2Q20. For 2Q20 alone, net profit dropped 19% YoY to P1.07bn.


Lockdown hits all major segments in 2Q20
Excluding PGOLD, which saw profit growth of 24% YoY, all other key segments saw profits decline in 2Q20. For the Real Estate segment, net profit dropped 69% YoY as it extended rental waivers to its commercial tenants in line with lockdown restrictions.


Liquor Distribution: volumes drop 34% amidst liquor ban
Net profit for the Liquor Distribution segment fell 16% YoY to P190mn in 2Q20. Volume sales dropped 34% YoY to 570k cases due to the liquor ban, as well as the ban on dine-in at restaurants. Despite the drop in sales, profits were cushioned by a 130-bp YoY improvement in gross margin due to the favorable forex. While volumes are expected to improve sequentially as lockdown restrictions, we do not expect volumes to recover to pre-pandemic levels in the near term.



Target price cut to P9.70/sh (-17%) on wider NAV discount
We refresh our 2020-22E forecasts for COSCO to account for 1) our new forecasts for PGOLD, 2) lower volume assumptions for the Liquor Distribution business, 3) lower revenues for the Real Estate segment, and 4) the divestment of Liquigaz, which was completed in early-2019. Our target price is cut to P9.70/sh (-17%) as we ascribe a wider holdco discount (50% from 30%) to our sum-of-the-parts NAV of P19.50/sh. The value of COSCO's 51% holding in PGOLD alone is already worth P10.34/sh using the latter's recent price (P50.60/sh). This means the current price of COSCO is already at 50% discount to the market price of its PGOLD holding, even if all other assets are excluded. Maintain Buy.

Macro News

Shorter loan moratorium pushed; Other details of Bicam version of Bayanihan II

The country’s economic managers are recommending shorter loan moratorium than the 60-day loan moratorium given under the Bicameral version of the Bayanihan II. Finance Secretary Sonny Dominguez is recommending a maximum of 45-day grace period on loans. Meanwhile, BSP Governor Diokno does not want to mandate a one-size-fits-all- moratorium.  He wants to leave the banks discretion to deal with the individual debtor, with the promise of some relief from the BSP. The Bicam version rejects Senate’s proposed 30-day grace period and the 1-year extension proposed by the Lower House. The 60-day grace period approved by the joint panel  “will cover everything, including credit card bills”. Moreover, the Bicam version also allowed the three-year suspension of permits to build cell phone towers. Senator Frank Drilon proposed the suspension of most of the 29-35 permits/documentary requirements, except for building permits. The Bill also included a provision saying that only the Supreme Court could issue a TRO against building of telecom infrastructure, including cell sites and towers. Meanwhile, the Bicameral version of the Bayanihan II has followed the House version, which has a higher funding allocation of P162bn (vs. P140bn for Senate version). Final version will determine if there is a need for the House to pass other measures for the banking industry.

GIR level surges to US$98bn in July

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by 2% (US$4.5bn) to US$98bn as of end-July 2020.The MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the revaluation gains from the BSP’s gold holdings (+US$4.6bn MoM). The end-July 2020 GIR level represents an ample external liquidity buffer which can cushion the domestic economy against external shocks. This is equivalent to 8.9 months’ worth of imports.

Vehicle sales recover by 32% MoM in July (-35% YoY); Auto industry seeks help

CAMPI and TMA reported industry vehicle sales fell 35% YoY to 20,542 units (+32% MoM). This brings YT-July vehicle sales to 105,583 units, down 49% YoY. The CAMPI/TMA forecasts industry car sales will reach 240k units (-41.5% YoY). The volume reduction has serious operational and financial impact on the automotive industry. There have been submitted recommendations for industry support to the Dept. of Trade and Industry however, recommendations were not disclosed. Toyota remained the market leader in 7M20 at 42.1% market share, selling 44,481 units in 7M20 (-49.2% YoY). This implies July 2020 sales stood at 8,833 units (-37% YoY, +18% MoM).

Metro Manila, 4 provinces back to less restrictive GCQ

President Duterte announced last night that Metro Manila and the provinces of Laguna, Cavite, Rizal, and Bulacan, will revert to the less restrictive lockdown (“GCQ”) by August 19. The areas are estimated to account for two thirds of GDP. Other areas placed under GCQ are Nueva Ecija, Batangas, Quezon in Luzon; Iloilo City, Cebu City, Lapu Lapu City, Mandaue City, Talisay City, the municipalities of Minglanilla and Consolacion in Cebu province in the Visayas.  The rest of the country were placed under the most lenient quarantine (“MGCQ”).

Remittances rebound strongly in June

Remittances jumped by 7.7% YoY to US$2.47bn in June, reversing three months of negative growth. This is close to the 2019 average monthly remittances of US$2.5bn. In PHP terms, remittances in June rose 4.1% YoY. There was a rebound seen in remittances from the USA (+25%), Asia (+25%), Middle East (+5%) while Sea-based remittances continued to decline (-13%). This brings 1H20 remittances to US$14bn, down 4.2% YoY (in PHP terms, -7% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 5% in FY20.

Infra spend jumps 44% YoY, 61% MoM in June

Infra spend (excluding subsidies to GOCCs/transfers to LGUs) rose to P62.8bn in June, up 44% YoY and 61% MoM. They cited various nationwide projects of the Dept. of Public Works, the military, and Dept. of Education. This brings 1H20 infra spend to P297.9bn, down 4.3% YoY. The Gov’t lowered its FY20 infra spend target (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. This may suggest that comparable infra spend target for FY20 (ex. subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) may have been lowered to P660bn (vs. P943bn, +6% YoY target, pre-COVID).

Bicam raises Bayanihan II allocation to P166.5bn, Senate to tackle CREATE bill next week

The bicameral conference committee has reportedly agreed to increase funding for Bayanihan II to P166.5bn (vs. P162bn in House and P140bn in Senate). However, only P140bn will be funded immediately while P26.5bn will be part of a “standby fund”. Bulk of increase were allocated to the Dept. of Health’s hiring of emergency personnel and Dept. of Labor for its cash-for-work program. The bicam also increased funds for the agri sector, capital infusion in gov’t banks. Congress is aiming to ratify the Bayanihan II bill but the bicam still has around 10-12 provisions of Bayanihan II to reconcile, according to Senator Juan Edgardo M. Angara. The provisions to be ironed out include the support funds for infrastructure and tourism sectors. Meanwhile, the Senate plans to begin deliberations next week on the proposed Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act (CREATE). CREATE bill will immediately cut corporate income tax to 25%. Meanwhile, the proposed amendment to the Public Service Act will be deferred to the latter part of this year according to Senator Grace Poe. The saendment aims to lift foreign ownership restrictions in certain sectors. Congress aims to tackle bills for COVID-19 first and the 2021 national budget.

Metro Manila imposes unified curfew amid GCQ; Most provinces reluctant to admit buses from Metro Manila

The National Task Force Against COVID-19 approved Metro Manila mayors’ recommendation on a unified curfew of 8PM to 5AM in Metro Manila until Aug 31. The curfew is reportedly acceptable to retailers because it is consistent with availability of public transport and it reduces manpower costs by removing shifts. Mall traffic remains low due to limited public transport. However, the Philippine Franchise Association is recommending to shorten the curfew to 10PM to 5AM for food retailers. Existing curfew forces stores to close at 6PM and does not consider closing/cleaning procedures and travel time. Meanwhile, the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) said that reopening of provincial bus routes is running into continued opposition from most provinces.
