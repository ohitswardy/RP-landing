REGIS REPORT

16 October 2020

The PSEi slipped 0.6% WoW in a week of range-bound trading. Market catalysts continued to be scarce though value names managed to gather buy interest. 

Foreigners continued to get out of the market – with net outflows this week reaching US$75m. Average turnover was a mere US$114m daily (ave).  

Defensive names were weak as funds continued to rotate to value plays.  Puregold (-9.0% WoW) was the top index decliner, as company guidance suggests a soft 2H. Other decliners in more defensive names were seen in Universal Robina (-2.9% WoW), Globe (-2.8% WoW), and PLDT (-2.4% WoW). Market proxies JG Summit (-4.9% WoW), SM Investments (-3.9% WoW), Ayala Corp (-2.9% WoW) also dragged. 

Meanwhile, the top index gainer was value name LT Group (+15.8% WoW).   Utilities also gained, led by First Gen (+9.7% WoW) and Meralco (+6.7% WoW) on decent power volume recovery.  Electricity sales at Meralco for September are now practically flat YoY.  Reopening plays SM Prime (+4.6% WoW) and Bloomberry (+4.4% WoW) ended higher 

Banks also ended higher with Security Bank (+3.5% WoW), BPI (+3.4% WoW), and Metrobank (+2.5% WoW) all with gains.

GT Capital (-0.1% WoW) was flat. Sales of Toyota vehicles grew 32% MoM in September, and appear on track to meet Regis’ FY20 assumptions (90k unit sales).

The government announced the easing of transport restrictions, effectively allowing more commuters to occupy each vehicle. The government also earlier allowed more jeepneys to get back on the road.  Regis estimates the number of jeepneys allowed back on the road are now close to half of what they were pre-pandemic.   Transportation has been the key bottleneck of resumption of economic activity. President Duterte has reportedly agreed to proposals by the economic team to ease restrictions further, implying more changes are forthcoming.

Philippine Property - When it rains, it pours
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

COVID pandemic coupled with a shrinking POGO industry

With the economy still reeling from the COVID pandemic, the shrinking POGO industry adds another weight on the property sector. As a result, we do not expect residential sales, office rents, or mall rents to return to pre-COVID levels by 2022E. Thus, we lowered our profit estimates and target prices by as much as 37% and 21% respectively. With this latest round, we have slashed our target prices by an average of 45% this year. However, some property stocks have fallen over 50% since 2Q19 (vs. the PCOMP’s 26% drop) and offer deep value. Among them, we believe Megaworld offers the best risk-reward.

Dim prospects

While there are some subsegments which are relatively more insulated, such as PEZA-accredited office space and provincial residential units, prospects for the property sector in general look dim. 1) Malls are still only generating 30%-40% of pre-COVID foot traffic and landlords thus continue to grant substantial rental concessions. 2) POGOs have vacated 100,000sqm of office space and more POGOs will likely shutter their operations in the country, raising office vacancies further. 3) A rising number of condominiums are being offered at prices that are down 15%-20% YoY and further POGO departures could continue to cap prices.

 

Megaworld offers the best risk-reward

In our view, Megaworld offers the best risk-reward among developers we cover. 90% of its office portfolio is PEZA accredited and its pipeline over the next few years is significantly preleased. Its gearing (of 27%) is also the lowest in the sector and the stock is trading at deep discounts to historical averages.

Buy: FLI, MEG, RLC; Hold: ALI, SMPH, VLL

We believe the share price declines for some of the developers are overdone and thus maintain our Buy ratings on Filinvest, Megaworld, and Robinsons Land. We also maintain our Hold ratings on Ayala Land, SM Prime, and Vista Land. Key risks: COVID-19 and POGO sector. We value developers using discount to NAV.

 
PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Philippine Banks - NPLs up to 2.93% in August, PD rates ease slightly, Restructurings double
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Diverging trajectories

Sector-wide NPL ratio blipped up 15bps mom to 2.93% in August. However, the past due (PD) ratio surprisingly improved, albeit slightly, to 5.28% from 5.30% in July. Both ratios may have benefited from a jump in restructured loans (RLs) from 0.45% to 0.97% of loans. Meanwhile, loan growth slowed again to 4.1% yoy in August, with loan balances now down 3.2% ytd. Expect more “noise” in the PD/NPL numbers for September and October due to the new loan grace period.

NPL ratio rises to 2.93%, but absolute monthly increase moderates

The NPL ratio for the Philippine banking system climbed another 15bps to 2.93% in August from 2.78% in July (Figure 1), which was revised up from 2.75% as previously reported. To the upside, the Peso value of NPLs increased just P12bn mom in August, compared to P15bn in July and P16bn in June (Figure 2).

Past due ratio improves slightly to 5.28%

To the upside, the PD ratio eased – albeit slightly – to 5.28% in August from 5.30% in July (Figure 3). In absolute terms, PD loans were lower by some P7bn mom (Figure 4). The PD data is encouraging, as it could signal a stabilization of NPLs down the line. We note though that the PD figures have been a lot “noisier” this year, in part due to the loan “grace periods” from March to May.

Restructured loans double mom to 1% of loans – just the beginning?

For the first time since the Covid-19 pandemic, restructured loans surged, more than doubling to P105bn, or 0.97% of loans in August, from juts P49bn in July (Figures 5 and 6). We expect this level to rise, as banks reschedule loans to ease pressure on otherwise sound businesses in need of short term cashflow relief.

Bayanihan 2 – new grace period to again reduce PD/NPL visibility

The Bayanihan to Recover as One Act (or Bayanihan 2) called for a new 60-day grace period on loan payments from 15 September, which could again delay recognition of PDs and NPLs. Unlike Bayanihan 1, though, the new grace period will not apply to loans that are already past due. So, while PDs may not rise, we could still see new NPL recognition during the grace period.

Loan growth slows again, +4.1% yoy in August (-3.2% ytd and -1.1% mom)

The NPL and PD ratios were magnified by a continued monthly contraction in loans. While loans still posted a 4.1% yoy gain (from 4.8% in July), the August balance was actually down 1.1% mom and down 3.2% ytd. In fact, loans have contracted every month since March (Figures 7 and 8).

NPL cover, LLR ratios suggest provisions could stay chunky in 3Q20

NPL cover declined slightly in August to 107% from 110% in July. Meanwhile, LLR ratio improved to 3.05% of total loans from 2.97% in July (Figures 9 and 10). Interestingly, August loan loss reserves were P327.4bn, up some P26bn higher than the June level, suggesting provision expense could stay chunky in 3Q20. For perspective, LLR grew P74bn qoq in 2Q20, and P20bn qoq in 1Q20 (Figure 11).

Corp. News

Bank credit ratings – outlook downgraded

S&P downgraded the outlook for BPI (BPI PM) and Security Bank (SECB PM) from ”stable” to “negative”, but kept the BBB+ rating. S&P kept the sovereign BBB+ rating with a “stable” outlook. The outlook downgrade was just for the banking sector, citing risks to asset quality and profitability. Additionally, Fitch downgraded the outlook for Chinabank (CHIB PM) from “stable” to “negative“, while maintaining the BBB- rating. The larger banks’ outlooks were kept at “stable”. Note, S&P does not rate BDO and MBT. Note that the Big Three banks have identical ratings/outlook as covered by Fitch and Moody’s.

China to criminalize overseas entities that lure Chinese to gamble

State-run media China News Service reported that the National People’s Congress is reviewing an amendment to mainland China criminal law that would “criminalize organizing and soliciting by casinos abroad”. The report did not specify proposed penalties for the new crime nor when the proposal would come into effect. The report also highlighted official criticism against “cross-border” gambling for causing capital outflows and “damage to economic security”. In August, China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism said it established a blacklist system for cross-border gambling destinations.

Asia Brewery, Heineken realign partnership in Philippines

Asia Brewery and Heineken announced that they have agreed to transition the AB Heineken Philippines Inc joint venture to a new partnership structure. Heineken will establish a sales and marketing office based in Manila and will engage Asia Brewery to brew and distribute beers in the country. The expected date for completion of the transition process is December 31, 2020. Asia Brewery incurred an equity in net loss of -P726mn in 2019 and -P382mn in 1H20 mostly arising from the AB Heineken Philippines JV. Asia Brewery accounts for less than 2% of LT Group’s net income and 6% of NAV.

Car sales rise 37% MoM in September

Industry car sales reached 24,523 units in September, down 23% YoY but up 37% MoM. According to the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines (CAMPI) and the Truck Manufacturers Assoc. This brings 9M20 industry car sales down 45% YoY to 148,012 units. Meanwhile, Toyota sold 10,657 units, -21% YoY but +32% MoM and brings thier 9M20 sales to 63,182 units, down 45% YoY. Our forecasts assume FY20 car sales will drop by 44% to 90,372 units. Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV.

San Miguel gets Senate OK on airport franchise

With 22 affirmative votes and zero negative, the Senate approved House Bill No. 7507, which grants San Miguel Aerocity a franchise for the Bulacan airport project. The franchise exempts the grantee from direct and indirect taxes during the 10-year construction period after which an income tax and real estate tax exemption will be granted. The exemption, however, will expire once the Bureau of Internal Revenue finds the investment cost of the airport and airport city has been recovered. Upon expiration, San Miguel Aerocity will then be entitled to a revenue share worth 12% of the internal rate of return (IRR) annually. The amount in excess of the 12% IRR will be remitted to the government. The franchise provided that San Miguel Aerocity should be able to commence construction within one year from the signing of the franchise into law, otherwise, the franchise may be revoked.

Bank reserve rules eased anew

Under BSP Circular 1100, banks and financial institutions could use loans extended to business enterprises hardest hit by the pandemic as part of their compliance with the reserve requirements. The loans extended by banks will be to large enterprises that record a 25% drop in gross receipts for one calendar quarter. Critically impacted business shall include the transport industry, tourism industry and export-import industry. Under the previous guidelines, loans to enterprises that experienced at least a 50 percent decline in gross receipts could be used as alternative mode of compliance to the required reserves. Banks may continue to utilize past due or non-performing loans to MSMEs and large enterprises as alternative compliance with the reserve requirements for an additional 30 days from the date the obligations become past due or non-performing, whichever comes first.

BSP’s liquidity support to economy hits US$39.2bn

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has provided about 1.9tn pesos (US$39.2bn), equivalent to 9.6% of GDP, in liquidity to the financial system according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. Amount includes P540bn in advances by national government targeted for release in first week of Oct. The BSP working with government to ensure the pandemic “will leave little permanent scar on the Philippine economy and its people”.

Converge IPO priced at P16.80/share

Converge ICT Solutions priced its IPO at P16.80/share which translates to a market cap of P125.3bn. The IPO could raise up to P29.0bn from 1.73bn offer shares (including overallotment option). The break down of the shares would be 480.8mn primary shares, 1.0bn secondary shares and 225.8mn overallotment shares. The offer period is between October 13 to 19 with a listing date of October 27. The ticker “CNVRG”. Converge is a fixed broadband service provider with 732,000 subscribers as of June 2020.

Power spot market prices on the decline

Prices at the wholesale electricity spot market (WESM) have declined in first half of October as demand for electricity softened due to rains and lower temperature, according to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP). WESM prices declined to P2.19/kwh in the first half of October vs the effective spot settlement price of P3.88/kwh in September. The system peak demand decreased to 12,259MW, -5.4% from the same period last year. Luzon peak demand was 10,303MW while Visayas pea demand was at 2,008MW.

MPI volume update

Metro Pacific circulated an update on the volume recovery of its key subsidiaries. Meralco electricity volumes down 6% YoY in 3Q20. Meralco’s electricity sales dropped 6% YoY in 3Q20, sharply recovering from the 18% YoY fall in 2Q20. In September alone, electricity volume sales were flat YoY and up 9% MoM at 4,007 GWH. This brings 9M20 electricity volumes to 32kGWH, down 7% YoY and on track to reach our full year forecast of 44k GWH (-5% YoY). Meanwhile, domestic traffic volumes down just 20% YoY in 3Q20. Combined traffic volumes at the NLEX, Cavitex, and SCTEX are down by just 20% YoY in 3Q20, gradually recovering from the 62% YoY drop in 2Q20. While traffic volumes dropped marginally MoM in August after the 14-day re-imposition of tighter lockdowns (“MECQ”), they bounced 20% MoM in September and were at ~20% below pre-COVID levels.

ICTSI to invest US$18mn in Ecuador

ICTSI is investing US$18mn to boost the port of Ecuador’s capacity according to José Antonio Contreras, the general manager of ICTSI’s Contecon Guayaquil. The capacity expansion will be done in stages and will allow the port to accommodate larger vessels. The first stage would raise the height of quay cranes five and six from 42 meters to 52 meters, and extend their boom lengths from 50 meters to 56 meters. The second stage involves works to reinforce the docks while stage three will further deepen Pier 1’s draft to 13.5 meters. After this, ICTSI plans to increase the total investment to US$30mn. Last Dec 2019, Contecon Guayaquil signed the 19-year concession extension of the port. These investments are already in our estimates and we estimate the port of Ecuador accounts for 6% of NAV and 7% of revenues. ICTSI is trading at 9x FY21E EV/EBITDA.

Macro News

Transport rules further eased: Riders allowed one seat apart

The government has approved a “one-seat-apart” rule on mass transport during a Cabinet meeting. The cabinet also approved to gradually increase the capacity to allow “sitting together” but with plastic barriers, or ultraviolet light disinfection. The gov’t had previously planned to reduce the distance to 0.75m from 1m today but objections from some Cabinet officials and health experts rolled back the plan. Meanwhile, the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board will release a memorandum circular to define the rule ′′ one-seat apart ′′ and steps that can be taken to increase capacity.

Gov’t allows more jeepneys to operate in Metro Manila

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) has opened 44 more routes and has allowed 4,820 units. The LTFRB said a total of 27,016 traditional PUJs can now ply along the 302 open routes in Metro Manila.

FDI climbs to 7-month high in July

Foreign direct investments net inflows jumped by 35% YoY, 66% MoM to US$797mn. This brings 7M20 FDI net inflows to US$3.795bn, down 11% YoY. The BSP expects FDI inflows to reach US$4.1bn this year, less than half of the US$8.8bn initial target before the pandemic.

Infra spend drops further in August

Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) dropped further by 25% YoY, 15% MoM in August to P44.3bn. This brings YT-August infra spend down 11.5% to P394.5bn. Last July, the government lowered its infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. For FY21, the infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) is supposed to increase by 43% YoY to P1.12tn. This target is c. 7% higher than what was spent in 2019 for infrastructure (P1.05tn).

IATF backs shorter curfew hours for Metro Manila; Oks easing outbound travel limits

The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) for the Management of Emerging Infectious Diseases (IATF) is in favor of easing curfew hours in Metro Manila according to IATF vice chairman and Cabinet secretary Karlo Nograles. It is also eyeing to implement staggered work shifts to prevent crowding in offices and establishments. But staggered work shifts could not be implemented if curfew hours start early. The malls and groceries should be allowed to operate for longer hours. The IATF agreed in principle to relax the outbound travel restrictions on Filipinos who want to be with their foreigner partners and are currently finalizing the procedure. The IATF also must coordinate with accepting countries, airline companies and various agencies to ensure that the relaxed travel protocols are implemented smoothly.

Displaced workers hit 3.6mn

Data from the Department of Labor and Employment showed that 3.393mn workers were affected by either flexible work arrangement (FWAs) or temporary closures from March to Oct. 11. Although many companies have reopened, most firms were forced to reduce workforce. Around 86,391 commercial establishments with a total of 2mn workers opted for temporary closure while 35,328 firms resorted to flexible work arrangements (FWAs) affecting 1.5mn workers. The displaced workers of 3.3mn account for 7% of total labor force of 45.9mn. A total of 282,378 workers were displaced as a result of shutdown and retrenchment in 14,880 establishments nationwide. National Capital Region (NCR) accounted for the biggest number of displaced workers with 151,664 followed by Calabarzon with 43,472 and Central Luzon with 28,673. The highest number of displacements was recorded in June with a total of 50,589 followed by the month of August with 38,636 and July with 36,853.

 

GIR climbs to US$100bn in September

Based on preliminary data, gross international reserves (GIR) level rose by US$1.54bn (+1.5% MoM) to US$100.49bn in end-September 2020. The GIR equivalent to 10 months’ worth of imports.

OFW remittances down 4.1% YoY, 10.8% MoM to US$2.48bn in August

Remittances in August stood at US$2.48bn, down 4.1% YoY and 10.8% MoM. The remittances from Asia and the Middle East dropped by 5% and 28%, respectively. Meanwhile, sea-based remittances continued to drop, albeit at a slower rate of -2%. In PHP terms, remittances dropped 10% YoY and 12% MoM in August. The 3-month moving average growth rate at +3.6% in August. This brings the cumulative 8M20 remittances to US$19.3bn, down 2.6% YoY (in PHP terms, down 6% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 5% in FY20 (FY19A: US$30bn).

Imported vehicle sales drop 46% in 9 months

The Association of Vehicle Importers and Distributors (AVID) reported that its members sold 34,977 units in 9M20, down 45.6% YoY. Combining the data of AVID and CAMPI (adj. for brands present in both), 9M20 industry-wide vehicle sales fell by 46% YoY to 161,212 units. This implies that in September alone, industry-wide car sales dropped 24% YoY but was up +34% MoM to 26,595 units. Toyota’s market share in 9M20 stood at 39%, slightly lower than end-2019 (40%).

SWS – Joblessness still at 40% in September

Last week, Social Weather Stations report showed that adult joblessness remained extremely high at 39.5% in September, down from the 45.5% level in July. This runs counter to the report of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) which said that unemployment improved sharply from 17.7% in April to 10.0% in July. Even if we include under-employment, the combined PSA figure dropped to 27.3% in July, from 36.6% in April. There’s a world of difference between the two sets of data, and what they might mean for a recovery in GDP and indeed for corporate earnings.
