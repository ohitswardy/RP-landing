REGIS REPORT

13 November 2020

The PSEi jumped +4.3% this week as positive news on Pfizer’s COVID vaccine lifted world markets. On Tuesday, the Phils enjoyed its best day since late March with a 5.2% gain, taking the Phils above the 7000 level. The PSEi endedthe week just below the 7000 level at 6969.88.  

The Phils also saw the highest foreign inflow for the year (ex-deals/IPO) at +$47m on Tuesday.  For the week, net inflows were at US$66m. Foreigners however were just 39% of turnover. Expectedly, liquid large caps captured the turn in flow. The top net foreign bought stocks for the week were Ayala Land (+US$29.5m net inflow) and SM Prime (+US$17.9m net inflow).

The recent gains have taken the PSEi’s YTD drop to 11% from as much as -41% YTD sometime in March.

GDP for 3Q came out this week.  The 3Q print was -11.5% YoY. While the contraction was less than the 16.9% clip in 2Q20, the recovery was much more muted than expectations. Sequentially, the 3Q20 print was flat QoQ, up just 0.1% (not seasonally adjusted) from the previous quarter.  Government spending grew only 5.8% YoY; there was no follow-though fiscal stimulus after the Bayanihan 1 Act. Meanwhile, Capital Formation also lagged, with construction activity contracting 19% QoQ.

Ayala Corp (+4.8% WoW) subsidiary AC Energy (+9.7% WoW)announced restructuring plans which will involve a rights issue, a follow-on offering, the injection of its international assets and the entry of GIC as a financial investor for 17.5%. The entire process is seen to be completed a year from now.

Results dominated the week.

Ayala Corp’s 3Q net profit amounted to P3.4bn (-59% YoY), improving meaningfully from 2Q (P1.3bn). Nonetheless, earnings YTD are trending behind Regis estimates as the key listed units, BPI, Ayala Land, Globe lagged.

Earnings at DMCI (+9.3% WoW) amounted to P3.9bn for 9M, down by 58%. The number is already better than Regis FY20 estimate but appears below consensus forecasts. For 3Q alone, recurring profit was P1.85bn (-17% YoY). The construction business still lost money in 3Q but the property business and Semirara (+7.9% WoW) are rebounding.

For property companies Ayala Land (+12.9% WoW) and Robinsons Land (-7.7% WoW.) profits fell a similar 77% and 78% respectively in 3Q.  ALI’s mall revenue for the quarter fell 67% YoY. On a brighter note 3Q20 reservation sales recovered 65% QoQ (-38% YoY) to P22.5bn thanks to improving demand for suburban developments in provinces adjacent to Metro Manila.

Robinsons mall revenue fell 69% in 3Q.  In contrast to ALI though, 3Q Philippine presales fell 80%.  9M20 presales fell 63% as the lack of Chinese bulk buyers reduced foreigners to 22% of total sales (from 43% in 2019).

Megaworld (+8.0% MoM) reported in lines results. 3Q20 net profit (after minority interest) fell 52%.  Interestingly, 3Q20 office revenue rose 7%.

A few consumer names also reported. Jollibee (-1.8% MoM) saw headline 3Q losses of P1.58bn, an improvement over 2Q’s P5.6bn loss. Excluding the impact of the business transformation, the 3Q20 operating loss narrowed QoQ to P1.17bn from P4.5bn in 2Q20. While there is evident recovery, the Philippines operations seem to be underperforming.   The company has earmarked permanent closures of some 500 stores and has spent close to P3bn of the P7bn it set aside for business transformation.

Shakey’s  (+14.3% WoW)  reported 3Q20 recurring net loss improved QoQ to P100m.   Recurring EBITDA turned positive in 3Q20 despite sales still being significantly below pre-COVID levels. 3Q20 revenues grew 15% QoQ as restrictions on dine-in operations were eased.

In the meantime, DNL’s  (+8.3% WoW) 3Q earnings came in at P573m, 2x the level in the previous quarter but down 7% YoY.  The 9M net profit figure is on track to mildly exceed company guidance of P1.6bn to P1.8bn for the year.

Both utilities reporting this week came in slightly behind.

Manila Water’s (+6.7% WoW) implied 3Q20 core profit dropped 49% YoY, pulled down by a higher tax rate, equitized losses from its Asia Pac investments, and higher interest expenses. 

First Gen’s (-1.2%  WoW) recurring income for 9M fell 12% to $190m. Contribution from natural gas plants were weighed by planned outages and lower dispatch of San Gabriel and Avion.  EDC was also lower due to lower selling prices and higher tax payments. The implied 3Q20 core income of $53m is down 17% QoQ and 7% YoY.

Meantime, Bloomberry’s (+6.3% WoW)  losses in 3Q narrowed to P2.5bn, a marked improvement over the P4.7bn loss in 2Q, as gross gaming revenues recovered to 30% pre-COVID levels. Among the segments, VIP had the most substantial jump QoQ (to P2bn from P122m in 2Q) despite the absence of foreign players. GGR from mass tables and slot machines were 4-5x the levels generated in 2Q.

Finally, Cebu Air’s (+6.6% WoW) 3Q20 core net loss (i.e. excluding FX adj and one-time net gain on insurance claims) was lower by 16% QoQ at P7.31bn, as domestic travel restrictions persisted. 3Q20 EBITDAR loss was 24% higher QoQ to P2.7bn, as fuel costs rose and fares dropped sequentially. Capacity in 3Q was at a mere 6% of pre-pandemic levels.

Ayala Land - 3Q20 residential sales and mall traffic improve; but watch out for POGOs
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

3Q20 profit falls

77% Ayala Land’s 3Q20 net profit fell 77% YoY to P1.85bn as lockdowns persisted and construction activity remained limited. As a result, 9M20 net profit dropped 73% to P6.4bn. EBIT appeared broadly in line with our estimates but items such as taxes, interest, etc. dragged the bottom line further. Note however that ALI’s 9M20 earnings only represent 50% of consensus full-year EBIT and net profit. Provincial residential sales and mall foot traffic have improved. However, Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) closures could weaken Manila residential demand as Chinese employees depart.

Reservation sales supported by suburban projects

3Q20 reservation sales grew 65% QoQ to P22.5bn. While this is still down 38% YoY, the company has cited improving demand for suburban developments in provinces adjacent to Manila. The company instituted looser payment schemes for its Premier and Alveo brands but generally granted only minor discounts.

Malls register another operating loss; foot traffic improves further

3Q20 mall revenue fell 67% YoY, resulting in a second consecutive quarterly operating loss. Nevertheless, foot traffic has been showing sequential improvement, rising to 38% in Sept and 41% in Oct (from 23% in Jun). The company plans to continue granting rental discounts until end-2020 and will assess when it is time to reduce such concessions.

Office: POGO closures loom

The office segment has been the most resilient thus far with occupancy at 95% and 9M20 revenue rising 2%. However, POGOs occupy 9% of ALI’s portfolio and recent tax regulations could lead them to shut down their offices.

Gearing drops

Net debt/equity fell to 74% from 87% in 2Q20. The P19bn reduction in net debt was largely due to the listing of AREIT, which raised P14bn at the Ayala Land level.

Robinsons Land - 3Q20 mall foot traffic improves; office REIT listing eyed in 2021
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

3Q20 profit falls 78%

RLC’s 3Q20 net profit fell 78% to P717mn as lockdowns and construction constraints curtailed the mall and residential businesses. We consider 9M20 earnings of P4.4bn (-40%) to be slightly ahead of our forecast since RLC is slated to record P9bn of revenue from a China residential project in 4Q20. The threshold to recognize residential revenue was lowered from 15% (of unit value) to 10% as of 1Q20. If not for this accounting change (which we expected), 9M20 profit would have fallen 63% to P2.7bn, instead of the reported P4.4bn.

Mall foot traffic improves in September/October

Mall revenue fell 69% in 3Q20 and the division registered a loss for the second consecutive quarter. On a more positive note, mall foot traffic improved to 40% of pre-COVID levels in September, from 30%-35% in July/August (20% in June). Management noted further sequential improvement in October. Rental discounts are in place until end-2020 but will be reassessed thereafter.

Office earnings continue to grow but POGOs start to close; REIT listing in 2021

9M20 office EBIT grew 26% thanks to 15% floor area expansion and rental escalations. However, RLC’s office occupancy fell from 95% in 2Q20 to 93%, partly due to the closure of POGOs. Moreover, recent tax regulations will likely cause Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs) to clear out the remaining 3% of the portfolio that they occupy. RLC plans to spin off over 400,000sqm of its 615,000sqm office portfolio into a REIT next year. We estimate that if RLC were to spin off all of its offices into a REIT, it could lead to NAV accretion of 5%-12%

Philippine reservation sales plummet due to foreigners

3Q20 Philippine presales fell 80%, worse than the 3%-40% drops of other developers. 9M20 presales dropped 63% as sales to foreigners fell 80% (due to lack of Chinese bulk buyers) and now comprise only 22% of total (43% in 2019).

9M20 recurring net loss wider than expected

JFC reported 9M20 headline net loss of P13.5bn. Group-wide SSS declined 35%, an improvement from -41% clip in 2Q20. Quarantine restrictions drag PH recovery

SSS in the Philippines continues to recover sequentially in Oct, though the recovery does not appear to be as strong as forecast. Sales continue to be hampered by the limits on public transportation, though JFC is seeing better recovery trends in its free-standing stores vs mall-based stores. We are reviewing our estimates and rating. Regis Partners will be hosting a post-results call with JFC’s CFO on 13 November 2020 at 4pm HKT. 3Q20 profit -7% - mildly below

DNL’s 3Q20 profit of P573mn (+99%qoq, -7%yoy) is double what it earned in 2Q but still slightly lower than pre-Covid level. Despite the sharp qoq jump owing to better margins, profit from food was still down 38%yoy. The collective profits of the non-food segments, on the other hand, were already higher by 8% vs. 3Q19. Its 9M20 profit of P1.374bn (-32%yoy) is tracking slightly lower than our FY estimate (P2.168bn) but is ahead of management guidance (P1.6-1.8bn).

Food profit -38%yoy; margins improve but volumes remain weak

Profit from the food segment was P155mn (+421qoq, -38%yoy), helped by a 180bps sequential improvement in margins. Within food, we estimate that GPM of commodities bounced back to >5% after falling to just 1% in 1H20. Underlying volumes were not all that great, however. Despite the general reopening of the economy, we estimate 3Q volume of high margin food specialties was lower by c.25% yoy (from -12% in 1H20). 3Q volume growth of commodities also moderated to c.30% (from 47% in 1H20). We think the simplification of product offerings for many restaurants may have skewed demand in favor of lowermargin ingredients.

Industrial volumes down just mid-single digits in 3Q; ODM up high-single digit

Profits from olecheomicals (P226mn; +52%qoq, -2%yoy) and plastics (P135mnl; +71%qoq, -0.5%yoy) came in near pre-Covid levels. We estimate that volumes for oleochemicals and plastics were lower by just mid-single digits in 3Q, coming from -24-28% in 1H20. Aerosol volumes were up by high-single digits, on our estimate, on the back of higher sales of sanitation chemicals, disinfectant sprays and alcohol. Profit from this segment jumped to P89mn (+60%qoq, +72%yoy).

Palm, coconut oil prices creeping higher

As palm (9M20 +22%yoy) and coconut (9M20 +32%yoy) oil prices continue to trend higher, margin pressure may start slowing its profit recovery.

Philippine Strategy - 3Q20 GDP misses; but vaccine hopes and QE+DM focus on recovery, value plays
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Reducing GDP forecast, but will the markets care?

GDP contracted 11.5%yoy in 3Q20, taking 9M GDP to -10.0% yoy. A continued ban on large gatherings will likely rein in any rebound in 4Q consumer spending. We now forecast FY20E GDP to contract 10.0%, from -6.5% previously, but bump up our FY21E to +7% from +5%. However, news of a potentially successful vaccine is adding fuel to the QE+DM led bull market, which will likely focus on reopening and deep value plays in the near term.

3Q GDP disappoints, -11.5% yoy, as government stimulus fades

GDP contracted 11.5% yoy in 3Q20 (see table, at right). While this was better than the 16.9% (revised from 16.5% as earlier reported) yoy drop in 2Q20, we had expected a sharper improvement. On a qoq basis, GDP was up just 0.1% (not seasonally adjusted), despite the extremely low base in 2Q20. Personal consumption improved to -9.3% yoy from -15.3% in 2Q as most of the country eased from severe lockdowns in March-May. However, government spending slowed sharply (and was down 28%qoq) as there was no follow-up fiscal stimulus after Bayanihan 1. Capital formation also disappointed, as construction contracted another 19%qoq. Domestic demand was still down 17% yoy, with overall GDP growth saved by a faster recovery in exports vs imports.

New GDP forecasts: 2020E -10%, 20201E +7%

Metro Manila remains under milder lockdown rules (‘GCQ’), and while restrictions on certain industries have been relaxed, large gatherings are still prohibited. This will cap spending in what is typically a high base quarter. As a result, we revise our GDP estimates down to -10% (was -6.5%) for FY20E, but bump up our FY21E to 7.0% from 5.0%.

Vaccine hopes fuel fire lit by QE+DM; potential PE overshoot?

News ripped through markets that one Covid-19 vaccine had 90% effectivity, and could get regulatory approvals soon. There are clearly no guarantees, and any vaccine is unlikely to make it to the Philippines in scale before mid-2021. But it does potentially limit the risk of “worst-case scenario” of a protracted, multiyear pandemic. As we wrote last week, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas has launched massive Quantitative Easing (QE) and Direct Monetization (DM) programs that will likely keep risk-free rates low across the curve. The lower (perceived) risk premium could see valuations overshoot well ahead of earnings.

Reopening and value plays back in focus

Industries hardest-hit by Covid-19 will likely be brought in focus near-term. These include airlines (CEB), casinos (BLOOM, AGI), and restaurants (JFC, PIZZA, MAXS). Property developers (ALI, SMPH, RLC) could see their mall operations rebound, but the POGO exodus clouds the outlook for office and residential demand. Ruling out worst-case scenarios could also bode well for asset quality outlook for banks’ (BDO, BPI, MBT, SECB).

9M20 core net loss at P16.8bn, larger than expected
CEB’s 3Q20 core net loss (i.e. excluding FX adj and one-time net gain on insurance claims) was lower by 16% QoQ at P7.31bn, as domestic travel restrictions persisted. This takes its 9M20 core net loss to P16.8bn, a loss larger than we or the Street estimated. 3Q20 EBITDAR loss was 24% higher QoQ to P2.7bn, as fuel costs rose and fares dropped sequentially.

Capacity still limited but improving

Cash generation initiatives progressing
The company completed the sale-and-leaseback (SLB) of seven engines last September and is about to close a SLB deal for five (5) aircraft (CEOs) by yearend, which we estimate could generate P3-4bn. It will also reduce its aircraft from 75 as of end-2019 to 68 by end-2021.

The recently announced US$500mn equityraising exercise will help plug the gap, as would a possible US$400mn debt
facility to partly refinance its maturities with lessors and lenders. These transactions are expected to be completed by end-1Q21.

Management believes it can easily ramp up capacity (both in personnel and aircraft) should a vaccine kick-start travel again, but noted that the Philippines could also receive the vaccine later than others.

Philippine Banks: “Stress” levels rising – NPLs jump to 3.50%, RLs up to 1.17% in September
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Diverging trajectories, diverging strategies
The banking sector’s NPL ratio spiked up 57bps mom to 3.50% in September, the largest monthly increase so far this year. Restructured loans (RLs) also grew to 1.17%, putting the combined “stress” (NPL+RL) at 4.67%. Past due (PD) ratio improved sharply to 4.77% likely in part
due to the new loan grace period. Banks seem to be taking different tacks dealing with asset quality. While BDO (BDO PM) reported lower NPLs relative to its peers, it was much more pro-active in restructuring loans compared to BPI (BPI PM) and Metrobank (MBT PM).

NPL ratio surges +57bps (+P60bn) in September
Banks’ NPL ratios jumped 57bps mom in September to 3.50%, adding a net P60bn in bad loans from the August balance, the largest monthly jump in NPLs this year. The timing of the surge was expected; as we wrote in our
report Flying Blind, the first loan grace period under the Bayanihan 1 Act ended on 31 May, which meant NPLs were likely to spike from September.

“Stress” showing elsewhere – RLs rise to 1.17%, with more likely to come
Sector-wide RLs increased to 1.17%, up 20bps from 0.97% in August, which itself had more than doubled from 0.46% in July. This would put the combined level of borrowers under “stress” at 4.67% of total loans. We
expect more loan restructurings, particularly given the Bangko Sentral’s memo in August easing rules relating to RLs.

 

Different strokes – BDO’s proactive restructuring, vs MBT’s high provisioning
The Big Three banks – BDO, MBT and BPI – seem to have chosen diverging strategies in dealing with asset quality in this crisis. On one end, BDO has chosen to aggressively restructure or reschedule some P67bn worth of loans, just under 3% of its 3Q20 loan book, expecting the borrowers’ cashflows to recover as the pandemic plays out. On the other, MBT has instead elected to provision heavily
(c300bps in 9M20, even assuming no further provisions in 4Q). Neither MBT nor BPI has restructured on a significant scale. BPI says it will evaluate RL candidates on a case by case basis. As a result, while both have higher reported
NPLs than BDO, their NPL+RL “stress” ratios are significantly lower than BDO’s. Only time will tell which strategy will work better.

NPL cover falls to 92%; likely (much) lower at smaller banks
NPL cover dropped sharply to 91.7% from 107.4% (Figure 5). Coverage ratios were probably much lower among smaller banks, as the Big Three had NPL cover in excess of 100% in 9M20, with BPI at 100.4%, BDO at 138%, and MBT at 174%.

Past due drops again; repayments, restructurings, or grace period? PD ratios fell 51bps mom to 4.77% (Figure 6), likely due to a combination of a) better collection/payments, b) loan restructurings, or c) the new loan grace period (60 days starting 15 Sept) under Bayanihan 2 Act.

Ayala Land has cited demand for high-end lots in its townships in Cavite and Laguna, which are adjacent to Metro Manila. The land in Cavite and Laguna comprise 35% of ALI’s NAV.

DMCI 9M20 core profit down 49% YoY to P4.46bn – Ahead of Regis but behind consensus

DMCI (DMC PM) reported headline 9M20 profit fell 58% YoY to P3.91bn. Stripping out one-off project cancellations under DMCI Homes and forex adjustments, we estimate recurring profit dropped 49% YoY to P4.46bn, already exceeding our FY20E (P4.4bn) but appears to be tracking behind consensus (P6.52bn). The outperformance came from its property and nickel mining units, as well as Semirara and Maynilad. The construction unit disappointed after incurring a P97mn net loss in 9M20. In 3Q20 alone, core profit improved 17% QoQ to P1.85bn, but this was lower by 17% YoY. The property unit rebounded on faster construction rate, with 3Q profit up 321% QoQ and 67% YoY to P1.02bn. This brought 9M20 profit down 40% YoY to P1.08bn, exceeding our forecast on (a) faster completion and (b) better-than-expected EBITDA margin. Reservation sales mildly improved QoQ to P3.4bn (-68% YoY) in 3Q20, with 9M20 reservation sales down 56% YoY to P13.7bn (vs. Regis FY20E: P16bn). The construction unit incurred a larger loss is 3Q, taking 9M20 net losses to P97mn, tracking behind forecast. The company cited that slower completion due to the pandemic and ROW-related costs slashed profit margins of infrastructure projects. 

8990 Holdings 3Q20 net profit rises 31%

8990 Holdings’ 3Q20 net profit grew 31% to P1.8bn. Residential revenue jumped 42% to P4.8bn, a record level. The company delivered 3,083 affordable housing projects in 3Q20, almost equal to the 1H20 figure. 8990 attributes the strong 3Q20 performance to resilient affordable housing demand. This cushioned and brought the 9M20 net profit down 21% to P3.3bn due to lockdowns in 2Q20 which curtailed construction activities

Megaworld 3Q20 net profit falls 52%

Megaworld reported that 9M20 profit (before minority interest) fell 41% to P8.1bn. From this, we estimate that 3Q20 net profit (after minority interest) fell 52% to P2.1bn. The derived 9M20 net profit figure of P7.55bn (-41%) is broadly in line with our forecast but appears behind consensus.

Office revenue rose 7% QoQ to P3.0bn. The increase of office occupancy in Iloilo Business Park to 97% (from 90% before the pandemic) contributed mildly.

Residential revenue was flat QoQ at P4.7bn. GERI residential revenue fell 32% QoQ to P764mn. Megaworld (ex-GERI) revenue rose 10% QoQ to P3.9bn.

MEG is trading at 7.9X FY21E P/E and 51% discount to NAV

Additional takeaways from BLOOM earnings call

Operational status 

VIP segment is operating at 25% of capacity, while mass/slots can go up to 40-50% during peak times.

 

They have been working closely with PAGCOR to allow increased operations particularly for slots where momentum is building. Even without the formal lifting of capacity restrictions, management expects the regulator will allow the gradual ramp-up above 30%.

Online gaming 

Management is working with PAGCOR on the amendment of the license and IRR.

They envision the first phase of its online platform to involve live streaming table games to local patrons (similar to what junkets do for video streaming/proxy betting). At the same time, they are also working on developing systems/applications for an online casino (program accessed thru website or app).

Tax structure has not been established. 

They think they begin dry-run for live streaming within the month and maybe see revenue impact by 1H21

They will focus roll-out on domestic, but will explore international if the amendment to the gaming license allows

Finance Secretary says refinery closures not due to tax

Secretary Carlos Dominguez says the closure of local refineries is “not about the fiscal environment” but rather the “economics of oil refining are drastically changing”. House Ways and Means committee chairman says he is willing to review the tax regime. Petron is claiming that domestic refineries are slapped VAT twice. First when crude is imported and once again when refined product is sold. However, the VAT paid on crude is creditable as input VAT which can be used to offset subsequent VAT payments. Petron is seeking to change tax laws ostensibly to “level the playing field”. There is, however, no apparent tax disadvantage for the refiners.
