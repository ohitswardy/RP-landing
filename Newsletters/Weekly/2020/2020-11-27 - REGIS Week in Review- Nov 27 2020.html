REGIS REPORT

27 November 2020

The PSEi dropped 5.3% WoW to close at 6791.46. Gains from last week’s surprise rate cut were erased as profit taking ensued.

Foreigners were heavy sellers for US$163m -- pushing the market to its worst week since April. Some 40% of outflows were seen on Friday alone on the back of the MSCI rebal. 

The decline was broad based, with 24 of the 30 index names ending in the red this week.

Conglos JG Summit (-10.7% WoW) and Aboitiz Equity  (-9.9% WoW) were the top decliners this week. 

SM Prime (-8.9% WoW) and ICTSI (-8.75% WoW) also declined. ICTSI completed a block placement of 40m treasury shares  at a price of P117/share (4% discount to previous close), raising P4.68bn in gross proceeds. The sale should provide an additional equity buffer (almost US$100m) ahead of FY results that could include provisions for its Argentine investment as well as its unrecovered deposit in Sudan.

Defensives also continued to underperform – with Meralco (-7.4% WoW), URC (-6.0% WoW), Aboitiz Power (-5.74% Wo), PLDT (-4.6% WoW), and Puregold (-4.2% WoW) also declining.

On the ground, the government is allowing over 10,000  motorcycle taxis back on the road. This points to further improvements in transportation options.  Limitations on mobility have been the single biggest constraint to recovery. The availability of public transportation still remains well below what it was before the pandemic.

On the macro side, Senate approved on third reading the second package (CREATE) of the administration’s tax reform program. The bill aims to reform and restructure corporate taxes and rationalize the country’s tax incentive structure. The House of Representatives announced that it will adopt the Senate version in its entirety. The bill is thus expected to become law with the President’s signature before year-end. Among the main highlights of the bill are: 1) A reduction in the corporate tax rate from 30% to 25%, retroactive to July 2020 (reduced by 1ppt every year beginning 2023 until it reaches 20%), 2)  a cap on reduced tax rates for some firms, mostly BPOs, which were previously for perpetuity. 

Corporate news was scarce.

LT Group (-3.9% WoW) declared another P0.15/share dividend, taking total dividends YTD to P0.81/share. LTG has significantly raised its payout following the retirement of its preferred shares.

The toll road regulator has given approval for a modest increase in the toll fees for MPI’s (-1.0% WoW) North Luzon Expressway (NLEX). The toll fee increase comes after the completion of the Harbor Link, which connects Manila’s Port Area to NLEX. End-to-end, the toll fee adjustment represents an increase of less than 4%. This price adjustment is not yet in Regis estimates and could raise estimates for MPI by 3-6%.

Philippine Property - 3Q20 recap: Earnings bottom out but POGO closures accelerate
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 Carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Mall and residential segments rebound but watch out for POGOs

Earnings of real estate companies improved remarkably relative to 2Q20 as the easing of restrictions was particularly beneficial to the mall and residential segments. However, office vacancy increased while rental rates fell as POGO closures accelerated. Furthermore, revisions to POGO tax regulations in October could lead many more to shut down their operations.

POGO closures accelerate

3Q20 office revenue of the developers we cover rose 5% YoY. While this might give the impression of a relatively resilient segment, there are reasons to be concerned. During its 3Q20 briefing, Colliers reported that Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs) vacated a total of 155,000sqm of Metro Manila office space in 9M20. We estimate that POGOs vacated nearly 100,000sqm from the companies we cover in 3Q20 alone and we believe the POGO closures were the primary reason for the rise in Metro Manila office vacancy to 7% (from 5% in 2Q20) and the 5-20% YoY declines in spot rental rates.

Mall revenue improves sequentially but still down 65%-75% YoY

Restrictions were generally looser in 3Q20 than in 2Q20 but mobility remains quite limited compared to pre-pandemic periods and Filipinos generally still did not feel safe leaving their homes. As a result, September mall foot traffic was only about 40% of pre-COVID levels. Thus, mall operators continued to grant rent discounts to tenants. Although aggregate mall revenue improved 25% QoQ, this would still be 65%-75% lower YoY (vs. 70-80% YoY in 2Q20) for the developers under our coverage.

Residential projects in provinces perform better

3Q20 residential reservation sales also improved QoQ (28% in aggregate) but were still down 38% YoY. The range across developers, however, was wide. In general, provincial projects outperformed developments in Metro Manila. Our channel checks show that in the secondary market, most offer prices for Metro Manila condominiums were down 5-10% YoY, with a few units being offered at prices that are 15%-20% lower. In the primary market, developers have generally offered discounts within 10% (for select units).

Bloomberry Resorts - Roadmap to recovery; Upgrade to Buy
 
 
by Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Multiples to remain elevated on online prospects

Among the worst hit during the pandemic (from the operations and stock price points of view), BLOOM now rides on (1) a reopening theme and (2) lower-for-longer interest rates. While there remains a high margin of error for earnings projections, the expected rotation into battered names is likely to push valuations back to at least historical levels. The recent approval for online gambling also adds value to its gaming license and improves long-term growth prospects. We upgrade our rating to Buy.

Amendment to gaming license would open up other markets

An online platform represents the next step in its evolution. While we await the actual terms to the license amendment, we think it opens up opportunities within the Philippines that are substantial enough to merit interest. Outside Entertainment City, more than P80bn of GGR is generated through electronic gaming kiosks, small casino hotels and government-run outlets.

EBITDA in FY21/FY22 at 65-79% of pre-Covid peak

Although we cut FY20 substantially to reflect the prolonged quarantine, we think current run-rates, despite the heavy restrictions, show that underlying demand is present. Against 2019, we estimate GGR to recover to 73%/87% in FY21/FY22. Our EBITDA forecasts for FY21/FY22 are at 65%/79% of pre-Covid levels.

China pivot fizzles out

Increased rhetoric from China against cross-border gambling puts a third of GGR (pre-Covid) at risk. In this regard, we’ve factored in a slower recovery for VIP, which we estimate will not get back to pre-Covid levels until 2024. Should the enforcement climate ease (as it has in the past), expectations for VIP could bounce just as quickly.

Lifting valuations

We raise our target price to P10.0 (+61%) as we increase the target EV/EBITDA applied to the average of Solaire’s 2021 & 2022 EBITDA to 11x, equivalent to its mean since 2015. The vaccine development and the approval for an online platform help chart a path to recovery and could spur a re-rating ahead of the return of earnings. On our revised target, BLOOM would trade at 13.1x EV/EBITDA for 2021 (below Macau at 16x), falling to 11.2x in 2022 (in line with Macau).

Philippine Telecoms - 3Q recap: Demand for data jumps, but shifts indoors
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Broadband demand soars

Telecom revenue showed modest improvement in 3Q over 2Q, notwithstanding the depressed incomes and shrinking businesses of subscribers due to the pandemic and the associated lockdowns. The marked acceleration of broadband demand growth, however, remains the key highlight.

Revenue bounces from bottom; mobile revenue lower YoY

The combined total service revenue of the two operators amounted to P81bn (before eliminations) in 3Q20, representing a nice +5% bounce from 2Q20, despite 3Q being a seasonally weak quarter. Industry mobile revenue was up 4% QoQ but was still down YoY (-2%) in 3Q. In our view, this is reflective of damaged incomes and the shift in data usage to the home. Between the two incumbents, we estimate PLDT (TEL PM) gained 1ppt market share in mobile and is now not far from pulling even with Globe (GLO PM).

1m broadband subscribers added in a single quarter

We saw another quarter of exceptional broadband demand as more people worked or studied from home. We estimate that the industry added 1m broadband subscribers in 3Q alone, +15% increase from mid-year. Bulk of the new subscriptions (739k), however, are on fixed wireless plans, which are easy to secure, are typically prepaid and generate less revenue. Globe dominates this segment (fixed wireless) but its long-term outlook is arguably less secure.

Mobile data traffic growth slows; divergent trends on data revenue yield

After growing at least +15% sequentially over many quarters, growth in mobile data consumption slowed down to only +6% QoQ. Industry-wide revenue yield per GB was stable at P24 (US$0.5) but the trends for the two operators were divergent. Globe’s estimated revenue yield per GB dropped 10% to P27 in the quarter while PLDT’s actually rose to P22.

Philippine Strategy - Surprise 25bp policy rate cut – bad economy, good markets?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph // Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Monetary easing continues – still pushing on a string

The BSP surprised markets, cutting its policy rate by 25bps to 2.0%, a new all-time low. The move was due to the slow recovery in the economy in 3Q20, exacerbated by the recent typhoons. But we have held that to jumpstart the economy, monetary easing needs to be accompanied by fiscal stimulus and easing of movement restrictions. To the upside, the policy rate cuts, RRR cuts, and QE+DM will likely support stock valuations, well ahead of a recovery earnings.

BSP cuts policy rates to 2.0%, as economic rebound disappoints

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cuts its overnight policy rate by 25bps to 2.0%. (Charts on page 2.) Consensus was for another pause, with the next cut expected in early 2021. This surprise move, like the last one in June, was in response to indicators of a much slower economic recovery than expected. While continued movement restrictions are keeping the spread of Covid-19 contained, they have clearly taken its toll on the economy. GDP contracted 11.5% YoY in 3Q20, prompting us to cut our FY20E to -10.0%. This was also reflected in 3Q20 earnings where more results missed our expectations than beat them. And the slow grind seems to be continuing into 4Q20. In the BSP press release for this rate cut, it noted “strong headwinds” including “muted business and household sentiment”, and the recent typhoons. The ban on Christmas parties won’t help, either.

Pushing on a string – fiscal stimulus, easing lockdowns are slow in coming

As we wrote back in August, monetary stimulus, without accompanying fiscal stimulus, is effectively pushing on a string. The Philippine economy is not highly dependent on (formal) financial intermediation. Household debt in the banking sector is less than 10% of GDP. And low interest rates are not enough to incentivize banks to lend. At end-September, bank loans were up just 1.5% YoY, but already -3.2% ytd. Accelerated GDP growth, in our view, will require a) loosening of movement restrictions, including b) further opening of public transportation, and c) strong fiscal stimulus whether in the form of the 2021 budget, or additional “Bayanihan” spending bills.

Market, stock valuations could run well ahead of earnings

That being said, loose monetary policy could be good for stock valuations. Apart from the rate cuts, the BSP has launched aggressive QE and DM to keep interest rates down across the curve. Some will note that the cut means negative real rates, with October inflation at 2.5%. However, even before this cut, market interest rates were already well below the overnight policy rate. The 1-month bill has been sub-1% for most of November. In fact, you’d have to go the 3-year BVAL to get above the (previous) overnight rate of 2.25%. We’ve got the valuation support. Now to get earnings back up

Philippine Banks - Philippine Airlines to file for court protection from creditors
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

PAL in negotiations for rehab / restructuring

Various news sources (eg, Reuters) reported that Philippine Airlines (PAL) plans to file for court protection from creditors as it enters debt restructuring negotiations. Its listed majority owner PAL Holdings (PAL PM) had debt and lease liabilities of P210bn as of 9M20. Of this, only P44bn was notes and debt, equivalent to just 0.4% of banking sector loans. However, the exposure seems concentrated among a few local banks. Of the banks we cover, only BDO (BDO PM) has loans to the airline.

A tough year for PAL, other airlines

2020 has been an dreadful year for airlines, particularly for the Philippines where even domestic travel continues to be severely restricted till today. To illustrate, the country’s main NAIA airport served an average of less than 200,000 passengers a month in July/August, down 95% YoY (Figure 1, righthand column). PAL Holdings reported a whopping P29bn net loss in 9M20.

In itself, likely not a systemic banking risk; government could step in

Excluding long-term lease liabilities, PAL Holdings’s total debt was P44bn as at 9M20. Even assuming all of this is owed to local banks, it would be equivalent to just 0.43% of total banking sector loans (Figure 2, below). According to online news site Rappler, PAL intends to raise $505mn, with roughly half coming from PAL’s controlling Tan family and the balance from private and government banks. The airline also plans to cut a third of its 7,000-strong workforce.

But risk could be concentrated

PAL Holdings has not disclosed its creditor banks, though we understand only a few banks are exposed. Of the banks we cover, only BDO (BDO PM) revealed it has loans to PAL, though the size and nature have yet to be disclosed. The extent of exposure, if any, of PAL’s sister company Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) also remains unclear. PNB is 60% owned by LT Group (LTG PM).

Philippine Consumer - 3Q20 top line improves, but recovery will drag beyond 2020
 
 
by Carissa Mangubat +632 8894 6647 carissa.mangubat@regis.ph // Aaron Salvador +632 8894 6636 aaron.salvador@regis.ph
 

Recovery an uphill climb

The sector is recovering from the lows during the hard lockdown, but pressure on consumer spending is apparent – retail sales are still down significantly YoY, and top line growth has decelerated even for essential segments. The pace of recovery for the rest of the sector has been slower than initially expected, and we believe the continued recovery will be challenging as the impact of the contraction in incomes, particularly for lower income households, could extend beyond 2020.

3Q20 results mixed; recovery slower than expected

Aggregate sector earnings jumped over 2x QoQ as lockdown restrictions were partly lifted, allowing non-essential retail to reopen, restaurants to restart dinein operations (though in a limited capacity), and the sale of alcoholic beverages to resume. Consumer spending, however, was still noticeably weak, and the pace of recovery is tracking slower than expected. Restaurants continued to be the hardest hit segment as concerns about dining out limited foot traffic in stores, even if dine in was allowed to partly resume. Grocery retail sales softened as pantry stocking faded and government handouts were depleted, and downtrading was seen across various F&B segments, such as coffee, snack foods, and alcoholic beverages.

Weak consumer sentiment to weigh on holiday spending in 4Q, consumption beyond 2020

“Cautious optimism” is the buzzword as we move into what is usually a seasonally strong 4Q boosted by holiday spending. However, we believe sales are still likely to be down significantly on a YoY basis even as lockdown restrictions are further eased. Underlying demand will continue to be weak, in our view, as incomes have been eroded, especially for lower income households. This segment has disproportionately borne the lockdown’s impact, with mobility restricted by the limits on public transportation, and incomes pressured by contracting OFW remittances and rising unemployment. In the absence of significant government stimulus, we expect consumption to remain under pressure even beyond 4Q.

 
 

Corp News

Cignal introduces double-play offering

Cignal, the pay TV operation of PLDT held under its employee pension fund (PLDT BTF) has introduced double-play offering. It is bundling pay TV with fiber internet and pricing-wise, the offerings look attractive. While the price of the standalone broadband service is comparable to PLDT’s (and slightly more expensive than Converge’s), the bundled packages (pay TV + broadband) are attractive, with pay TV (with 61 channels) included for only an additional P150 (US$3) per month.

IT-BPO Association lowers its industry targets

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) expects revenue and employment of the sector to be flat this year with 1.3mn employees, US$26.2bn in revenue. The previous IBPAP 2020 projections were 1.33mn-1.37mn workers and US$27bn-28bn in revenue. The increase in demand for healthcare, game development, and global in-house centers offset the decline in the travel and tourism subsectors. IBPAP also lowered its 2022 targets to 1.37mn-1.43mn employees and US$28bn-US$29bn in revenue vs the previous IBPAP 2022 projections of 1.42mn-1.57mn workers and US$29bn-US$32bn in revenue. The BPOs occupy 70%-90% of the office space of the developers we cover. The developers have reported mild net expansion of BPO office footprint YTD. Megaworld is the most exposed to the BPO sector through its office leasing division.

Megawide, partner submit additional requirements for NAIA rehab; DOF-NEDA nix proposal?

Megawide announced it submitted additional documents necessary for its P109bn unsolicited proposal to rehabilitate NAIA. Documents include the statement of joint solidary liability from the Megawide-led consortium as required by the National Economic Development Authority (“NEDA”). Meanwhile, the Businessmirror reported that PBA Rep. Jericho Nograles has a copy of a three-page letter from the Department of Finance (DOF) and the NEDA. The letter addressed to the Department of Transportation (DoTr), DOF and NEDA declared that Megawide’s equity position is insufficient to finance the equity requirement for the proposed expansion project. The letter allegedly affirms the position of lawmakers that the DoTr erred in endorsing Megawide’s unsolicited proposal to expand Naia. Megawide has an equity position of P18bn based on its 2019 financial audit, insufficient to finance the P32.3bn equity requirement of the project. The DOF and NEDA also said that Megawide has yet to officially issue a statement that it would suspend its P200bn Mactan-Cebu International Airport 50-year expansion project to give way for the NAIA project. Megawide is also the proponent of the 50-year integrated development plan of the Mactan-Cebu International Airport (MCIA), which requires another P90.1bn in equity. To date, Megawide has not yet received any official communication from the government.

LTG declares P0.15/sh dividend

LTG declares a P0.15/sh special dividend to shareholders on record as of Dec. 9, 2020. The special cash dividend is authorized to be paid not later than Dec. 16, 2020. This brings the total dividend for the year to P0.81/share, about a 37% payout of trailing 12m earnings. LTG used to pay only 20% of prior year’s earnings in 1Q.

DoubleDragon files REIT IPO

DoubleDragon Properties will spin off DD Meridian Park into a separately listed REIT with an offer price of up to P2.25/share. The maximum offer will be P14.7bn (including overallotment) and would bring the maximum market cap to P40bn. We estimate this would represent c. 6% cap rate. Up to 6.5bn common shares would be offered with secondary shares amounting to  5.94bn shares. The overallotment would be 594.2mn shares. The offer period is set to be on February 10 to 17. DD Meridian Park Corp. will be renamed to DDMP REIT. DD Meridian Park has c. 200,000sqm of office buildings in the Manila Bay area but is not PEZA accredited. Additionally, it owns the land under the buildings. Colliers estimates that office rental rates in the Manila Bay area have fallen c. 20% YoY. For comparison, AREIT has an office portfolio of 170,000sqm (246,000sqm when the 30th is acquired) and a market cap of P28.2bn.

ICTSI to sell up to 40mn treasury shares

ICTSI’s Board of Directors approved the sale of up to 40mn common shares held under treasury. The proceeds will be used to raise funds for general corporate purposes, including funding of committed capex. As of end-September, ICTSI had US$568mn in cash and gross debt (inclusive of perps) of US$4.8bn. We estimate the share sale could reduce its net debt to US$4.1bn from US$4.2bn in end-September.

Macroasia, Chinese partner make progress in Sangley airport

The legal officer of Cavite government’s public-private partnership selection committee said MacroAsia and state-owned China Communications Construction Co. Ltd. (CCCC) submitted on Tuesday documents for their consortium agreement. Last September, the provincial government of Cavite granted MacroAsia and CCCC’s request for an extension of 90 days after commercial air travel resumes between Philippines and China to comply with all conditions. The SPIA project was awarded by the provincial government of Cavite last February to MacroAsia and CCCC. The phase one of the project amounts to US$4bn and involves the development of an interim first runway with annual design capacity of 25mn passengers and the new Sangly connector road and bridge. Meanwhile, phase two will add a second runway, expanding the annual design capacity to 75mn passengers. The CCCC has subsidiaries that are blacklisted by the US for their involvement in reclamation activities in the West Philippine Sea.

ICTSI raises P4.68bn in treasury share sale

ICTSI raised P4.68bn after selling 40mn common shares held in treasury in a placement.  The price was at P117/sh, representing a 3.9% discount to last close. The placement is estimated to increase outstanding shares by 2% and dilute EPS by c. ~1%. As of end-September, ICTSI had US$568mn in cash and gross debt (inclusive of perps) of US$4.8bn. 

 
 

Macro News

TRB approves add-on tolls for NLEX effective November 25

The Toll Regulatory Board approved the following tariff hikes (VAT exclusive) for the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX). For open system it would be P4.00 (+8%) while for the closed system it would be P0.06/km (+2%). The increases will be implemented past midnight on Nov 25. Our forecasts do not assume these tariff hikes. We estimate it could increase MPI’s profit by 3% and NAV by 2%. MPI is trading at a 30% discount to its market NAV of P5.95/sh.

Over 10,000 motorcycle taxi riders back on road

The government has allowed motorcycle taxis, Angkas and Joyride, to operate. It was said that an initial 6,400 riders from Angkas and 4,000 from Joyride can immediately ply routes. The balance of 16,600 drivers for Angkas and 11,000 for Joyride can take on passengers after taking a swab test. The stricter health and safety protocols include: filling up health declaration forms, using your own helmet, and cashless payments. Motorcycle taxis can traverse roads until December 9, at which point players should have in place a mandatory insurance for both rider and passenger, as well as the use of thermal scanners.

Razon group to fund initial vaccine purchase for government

The private sector, led by Enrique Razon Jr’s ICTSI will be buying as many as two million doses of COVID-19 vaccines. According to ICTSI’s Christian Gonzales, 50% of this vaccine will be purely for the use of DOH under equitable distribution to be determined by the government. The other 50% will be for the private entity to determine. The employee and key essential services’ members would be the PPA, Bureau of Customs and other port and logistics workers. Gonzales noted that the vaccine procurement initiative is a tri-party agreement between UK and Philippine governments and AstraZeneca.

Budget deficit widens in October

The government’s budget deficit swelled to P61.4bn in October 2020 from a P49.3bn deficit in the same period last year. Revenues contracted by 13% YoY, as BIR and BOC collections dropped 15% YoY and 12% YoY, respectively. In MoM terms, BIR collections are up +8.2% but BOC collections were flattish (-0.3%). Meanwhile, expenditures dropped by 7% YoY largely attributed to the base effect of the one-off pension differential releases for the military and uniformed personnel (MUP) and lower capital outlays. YT-October deficit at P940.6bn, +170% YoY (vs government’s FY20E deficit target of P1.8tn). Revenues are down 8.4% YoY to P2.37tn, on track to beat the government’s (downgraded) revenue goal of P2.5tn while expenditures are up 12.7% YoY to P3.3tn, accounting for 76% of the govt’s full-year target.

CREATE bill hurdles Senate

Senate passed Senate Bill No. 1357 or “Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act” (“CREATE”). CREATE provides for a reduction of corporate income tax (CIT) to 25%, starting July 2020, and a reduction of one percentage point annually from 2023 to 2027. The Senate version provided to lower the CIT to 20% for businesses with income below P5mn. A Bicameral Conference Committee is no longer necessary given that the House will adopt the version approved by the Senate.

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) previously estimated that the proposed changes from CREATE would make BPO operations in the Philippines c. 20% more expensive than those in India (compared to 10-15% currently). IBPAP believes this could curtail future BPO expansion in the country but would not lead to departures.

Senate approves 2021 Budget

With 22 affirmative votes, no negatives, and no abstention, the proposed P4.506tn national budget for 2021 (House Bill 7727) hurdled Senate yesterday. Bill will now proceed to the bicameral conference committee.

BOP at US$3.44bn surplus in October

The Balance of payments (BOP) surplus stood at US$3.44bn in September, >20x higher YoY and +63% MoM.  GIR level stood at a record high of US$103.8bn in October, equivalent to 10.3 months of imports. The  YT-October BOP surplus at US$10.31bn, higher than the US$7.3bn surplus in the same period last year.  This was supported mainly by higher net foreign borrowings by the NG and lower merchandise trade deficit.  The latest number beats the BSP’s FY20 projection of US$8.1bn.

Hot money registers US$439mn inflow in October

Foreign portfolio investments (“hot money”) posted a net inflow of US$439mn in October, after seven consecutive months of net outflows.  This was also higher than the US$105mn inflow in the same period last year. This brings the YT-October net outflows to US$3.9bn, higher than the US$1.2bn net outflow in the same period last year.
