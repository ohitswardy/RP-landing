REGIS REPORT

19 March 2026

The Philippine benchmark managed to stay above the psychological 6,000-mark this week, although surging oil prices and a falling peso added pressure to local shares, causing intraday breaks of the key support level. The PCOMP ultimately finished lower by 0.7% or 40.32 pts WoW to 6,018.62, weighed down by cyclicals and proxies as investors mulled over the deteriorating macro outlook with Middle East tensions seen persisting.

 

Brent stayed firmly above $100/bbl for the week, rising by over 10% WoW and fueling fears of an inflation shock. Unsurprisingly, pump prices have surged c.60% for gasoline and over 120% for diesel, and the energy department warned electricity prices could jump 16% in April. To ease the impact on the consumer, Pres. Marcos blocked a planned 19% increase in transport fares. Meanwhile, Congress has also approved a bill giving special authority to the president to suspend or cut oil excise taxes. Four-day onsite work arrangements have been put in place across branches of government and some areas within the private sector as well.

 

Inflation fears have dragged the peso further, taking the currency to an all-time-low beyond the 60:$1 mark for the first time ever. The BSP has stated that it has been intervening in the foreign exchange market to "smooth excess volatility" and temper big swings, rather than to outrightly defend a specific level.

 

A few more 4Q earnings reports trickled in. AC (-1.7% WoW) earnings were in-line with forecasts with most of its listed units BPI (-1.0% WoW), ALI (-3.5% WoW), and GLO (+0.6% WoW) all earlier reporting numbers that were within expectations, and only ACEN (-3.4% WoW) falling short. URC (-6.6% WoW) reported post-market close last week, missing estimates as the recovery in its domestic branded business was offset by weakness in the international branded and commodities segments.

 

With 88% of the PCOMP (by market cap) having already reported, aggregate PCOMP earnings are so far higher by 13% YoY in 4Q and by 6% YoY for FY25.

 

As Philippine markets will shut for Eid'l Fitr on March 20th, FTSE rebalancing trades went through on Thursday to effect the deletion of AC and ALI from the large cap index, and their addition into the mid cap index.

 

Foreigners have remained net sellers for the third straight week. The peso lost 0.6% WoW to 60.093:$1. The PH10Y yield jumped 28.2 bps WoW to 6.9072%.

Philippine Strategy - Inflation shock looms as fuel, public transport, and electricity prices soar
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Pump prices surge 60-120%; transport fares +19%; electricity +16%

Philippine consumers are due for a massive inflation shock. Pump prices at petrol stations surged close to 60% YoY for gasoline, and more than 120% for diesel. Regulators also approved a 19% increase in average public transport fares, effective 19 March. And the Department of Energy (DOE) has warned that, if global energy prices stay elevated, then electricity prices could rise by 16% in April. Higher logistics costs will have knock-on effects on food prices, most notably rice. As we noted last week , consumers will bear the brunt of a prolonged conflict in Iran, at a time of already-soft economic conditions.

 

Gasoline price up c60%, diesel up 120%+; public transport fares hiked 19%

Fuel prices at petrol stations are expected to surge this week , with gasoline (RON 95) up 60% MoM and YoY, while diesel prices up a massive 110% YoY and 126% YoY. As we mentioned last week, the Philippines imports nearly all of its petroleum product needs, with no subsidy to consumers. As a result, the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) announced public transport fare hikes averaging 19%, effective later this week.

 

Electricity to rise 16%+ in April, just as demand peaks in our summer

On 13 March, the Department of Energy (DOE) estimated that electricity prices could rise by 16% in April, though that was based on the previous week’s prices for oil, natgas and coal. In 2025, 75% of the country’s electricity generation came from coal and natgas, with the main Luzon grid at an even higher 81%. The timing could not be worse, as electricity demand typically peaks in April-June, which are the country’s hottest months of the year.

Rice is life – food prices next?

The knock-on effect on food prices will be imminent, too. Oil prices have a direct and indirect impact on farmers’ costs of harvesting, as well as fertilizers, plus transportation and logistics costs. The Department of Agriculture has warned of higher rice prices , already noting a P2/kilo (c4-5%) increase last week. As we noted in the past, rice is a key staple in the Philippines, representing 9% of broad CPI, with a heavier 18% weight for the “bottom 30%” of households.

 

Some relief from government? Oil tax suspension; transport and food subsidies

Congress is rushing a bill that would temporarily suspend excise taxes on gasoline (P10/liter or c11% of this week’s pump price), and diesel (P6/liter or c5% off the pump). The Department of Transportation plans to provide free or discounted transport fares by mid-April. Meanwhile, the government continues to provide subsidized rice at P20/kg – less than half the retail price – for qualified households, though limited to 10kg/month.

 

Consumer stocks likely to be hit hard; coal, solar power, and EVs win

As we noted last week, consumer wallets will be squeezed badly by rising prices, crimping discretionary spending, and prompting down-trading. To the upside, coal producer Semirara (SCC PM) and renewable energy companies like ACEN (ACEN PM) stand to book windfall gains. High petrol prices will also accelerate the shift to hybrids and EVs. Ayala Corp (AC PM) is the distribution partner for BYD cars, and also has majority stake in ACEN.

RL Commercial REIT - Making the transition; initiating with a Buy
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Initiating with a Buy; target price P7.75/share

RL Commercial REIT is the largest Philippine REIT in terms of assets under management (AUM). Among the major Philippine REITs, it already has the highest exposure to the mall segment (which we believe has a brighter outlook than the office segment) and we expect the ratio to increase further over the medium term. RCR also has a track record of superior occupancy and consistent DPS growth, which we expect it to maintain. Furthermore, the stock offers 18% total return to our target price of P7.75/share. Thus, we initiate coverage on RCR with a Buy rating.

 

Concentration of malls to increase

About half of RCR’s AUM and leasable area are currently comprised of malls and we expect this ratio to increase over time. RLC can still infuse 1mn sqm of malls and 200k sqm of offices. This would double RCR’s existing portfolio of 1.15mn sqm and increase the proportion of malls to c. 65% of leasable area.

 

We believe that mall REITs should trade at a premium to office REITs (holding all other things constant), as we have a more positive outlook on the mall business rather than the office segment. The average Metro Manila mall occupancy (89%) is higher than the average office occupancy (82%). Correspondingly, we forecast office rental rates to stay flat in the near-term and for mall rental rates to continue rising 6% annually (similar to the historical average). 

The long-term trends also show higher rental rate increases for malls vs offices. We estimate that over the past 15 years, Metro Manila office rental rates grew at a 2% CAGR while mall rental rates grew at a 4% CAGR.

 

Well-managed portfolio enhanced by DPS-accretive acquisitions

RCR has consistently grown its dividends per share (DPS) every quarter (with the exception of one quarter). We credit this largely to: 1) strong occupancy; and 2) DPS-accretive acquisitions. Occupancy across its portfolio has been consistently high, with current office and mall occupancy each at 96%, much better than the Metro Manila averages. Meanwhile, RCR’s sponsor, Robinsons Land (RLC PM), has been injecting assets at DPS-accretive valuations. We forecast RCR’s DPS to grow at a CAGR of 6% from 2025 to 2028E, similar to the 5% CAGR from 2022 to 2024.

 

Initiating with a Buy, TP of P7.75/share; key risks

We are initiating coverage of RCR with a Buy rating and a target price of P7.75/share. Our TP is derived using a discounted cash flow and implies 15.6X FY26E P/AFFO and dividend yield of 5.8% (at the target price), lower than the yields of other Philippine REITs. At the current price, RCR offers a 2026E dividend yield of 6.5% and a total return of 18%. Key risks relate to BPO concentration risk, rental rates, and interest rates.

 
Robinsons Land - 4Q25: mall segment outperforms but residential demand remains weak
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2025 recurring profit grows 9%

Robinsons Land’s FY25 recurring profit grew 9% to P13.5bn, in line with consensus but ahead of REGIS forecast. The mall segment outperformed our expectations, largely thanks to margin expansion. Residential revenue recognition also exceeded our forecasts. However, demand for RLC’s residential projects remains weak.

 

Mall revenue and margins expand

4Q25 mall revenue rose 7% YoY as occupancy increased 1ppt YoY (flat QoQ) to 94%. In FY25, mall revenue grew 10%, on same-mall revenue growth of 8%. RLC’s 4Q25 mall EBIT margin also expanded 2.6ppt YoY to 40.5%. For the full year, it rose 1.6ppt to 41.5%, largely thanks to cost management efforts. While electricity rates are likely to increase due to the conflict in Iran, RLC expects to be largely insulated from this since about 85% of its leasing portfolio’s power requirement is under fixed-rate contracts until 2027 and the company can pass on the increases from uncontracted power.

 

Reservation sales remain weak

Demand for RLC’s standalone and JV residential projects remains weak. 4Q25 reservation sales of JV projects fell 52% QoQ and 63% YoY to P820mn, about 15% of the 2019 average.

The company is cautious about the Metro Manila mid-income condominium market and is managing its inventory level. The value of unsold residential inventory of its standalone residential projects is P44.8bn (-1% QoQ), which we estimate is equivalent to 8.7 years of reservation sales. Meanwhile, 4Q25 reservation sales of standalone projects declined 73% YoY to P1.1bn. While this is a 30% QoQ improvement, it only represents 22% of the 2019 average. Management said that buyers of high-end units (such as RLC’s JV projects) prefer large units, but the unsold JV inventory is mostly comprised of smaller units (such as 1-bedroom units).

 

Office occupancy declines due to completion of new buildings

4Q25 office occupancy declined 3ppt QoQ to 85%, following the completion of 100k sqm of new office space, which expanded the portfolio by 13%. Excluding the newly completed buildings, occupancy rose 2ppt QoQ to 90%. Office occupancy improved 1ppt in 2M26 (to 86%) and on average, office contracts were renewed at flat rental rates (similar to the experience in FY25).

 

Considering buybacks and higher dividends

Management considers its cash flow to be strong and is contemplating an increase in shareholder returns via higher dividends and share buybacks.

House approves bill to suspend or cut oil excise tax

The Lower House passed on final reading House Bill 8418, which gives authority to the President to temporarily halt or reduce excise tax rates on fuel. President may now tweak fuel excise taxes if the average Dubai crude oil based on Mean of Platts Singapore benchmark reaches or exceeds US$80/bbl for a month. Implementation will be for no more than six months and should be recommended first by the Development Budget Coordination Committee. An extension beyond six months will require a joint congressional resolution, but implementation cannot last longer than a year. President may only suspend or reduce excise tax collections on fuel products until Dec. 31, 2028. Excise taxes account for P10/liter for gasoline (or 11-13% of NCR retail prices of P76-96/liter), P6/liter on diesel (5-6% of retail price P94-114/liter), and P3/liter of kerosene (~2% of retail price P128-130/liter). Estimated revenue losses could reach as much as P43bn if suspension lasts for three months and P106bn if extended to a total of six months.

Rice prices to increase due to fuel price hikes, higher freight costs

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) stated that rice prices are set to increase due to the global oil crises as these will increase harvesting and transportation costs. On local rice, some traders and wholesalers have been said to reduce deliveries despite available rice stocks due to costlier fuel. DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that freight rates from Vietnam have doubled since the conflict in the Middle East, with the landed cost of imported rice reaching nearly US$500/MT. Tiu Laurel also said that the gov't is considering a potential P50/kg price cap for imported rice. The price cap for local rice is also being considered but is likely to be implemented after harvest season. Local retail prices of well-milled rice for both local and imported rice have risen to P47.6/kg and P46.4/kg as of March from Oct/Nov lows of P42.7/kg and P42.6/kg, respectively. Prior to higher fuel prices, the rise in domestic rice prices for both local and imported was said to be due to higher farmgate prices, a weaker peso, and muted rice importation in 2M25.

Cost of residential developments to increase

The reinterpretation of a law requiring developers to allocate funds for socialized housing could raise the cost to develop residential projects. Under Philippine law, developers must allocate a certain percentage of a residential project’s cost or area for socialized housing. 5% of the cost or area of condominium projects. 15% of the cost or area of subdivision housing projects. The maximum price of a socialized housing unit is P950,000 for a house and lot and P1.8mn for a condominium. The Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) previously allowed developers to comply with this rule by paying 25% of the requirement to the government prior to getting a License to Sell. 1.25% (or 25% of 5%) for condominium projects. 3.75% (or 25% of 15%) for subdivision/horizontal projects. However, the Department of Justice ruled that developers must pay the entire 5% (for condominiums) or 15% (for horizontal projects).

Government moves to insulate airlines amidst oil price shock

The Department of Transportation, together with airline regulators, ordered to reduce passenger service fees across all airports and shorten the evaluation and implementation of fuel surcharge to 15 days (from 1 month). Moves aim to alleviate the burden on both airlines and passengers affected by the spike in jet fuel costs. Fuel accounts for 36% of CEB’s cash costs and 30% of operating costs. We are forecasting jet kero prices will average US$81/bbl vs. spot at US$198. Every 10% increase in jet kero prices will reduce EBITDA by 10% and profit by 50%, holding other things constant.

Suspension of mandatory biofuel blending bill approved by House, reaches Senate plenary

A bill seeking the temporary suspension of mandatory biofuel blending in order to ease oil prices has been approved by the Lower House, while the Senate is still finalizing its counterpart version of the bill. The bill seeks to amend the Biofuels Act of 2006, in order to allow the President to suspend the mandatory use of locally sourced biofuels for up to one year if the prices of blended fuel are at least 5% higher than pure gasoline or diesel. The bill was tagged as urgent by President Ferdinand Marcos, alongside the fuel excise tax adjustment bill which has been recently approved by both the House and Senate.
