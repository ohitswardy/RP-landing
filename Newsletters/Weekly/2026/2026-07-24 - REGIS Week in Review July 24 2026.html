REGIS REPORT

24 July 2026

Philippine equities snapped a three-week winning streak, pulling back as renewed Middle East tensions pushed oil prices higher which drove the peso down to fresh lows. The US-Iran war flared up again this week, with fresh strikes and retaliatory attacks disrupting tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, pushing Brent crude above $100/bbl for the first time since May.

 

Investors also reacted to a new index methodology released by the PSE early in the week, while profit-taking in index heavyweight ICT (-2.9% WoW) after its record run added to market pressure. The PCOMP fell -1.9% or 123.10 pts WoW to 6,281.01, though it remains up 3.8% YTD.

 

The PSE's new index rules will take effect at the February 2027 rebalancing, featuring the following key changes: (i) easing the minimum public float to 15% (from 20%) for large-caps with market capitalization of at least P250bn ($4.1bn), and (ii) introducing new liquidity screens -- the Median Trading Activity Ratio (MTAR) and Monthly Average Daily Value Turnover (MADV) -- that measure how actively a stock's free float actually trades, not just its headline trading value. Traders immediately began pricing in potential winners and losers: AP (+7.9% WoW) rallied on speculation of possible inclusion due to the relaxed free float rule, while CBC (-2.2% WoW) sold off on deletion risk due to the more stringent MTAR measure on liquidity. Other bets followed from these two outcomes -- SGP (+9.6% WoW) and PLUS (-9.1% WoW) moved on the premise that they would be added or dropped depending on which names ultimately fill or vacate index slots.

 

Elsewhere, the market gave back gains of recent outperformers AC (-4.6% WoW) and SMPH (-4.6% WoW), which were among the biggest index laggards this week. Following closely were JFC (-3.9% WoW) and URC (-3.2% WoW), which led consumer names mostly lower as the spike in oil prices revived inflation concerns.

 

SCC (+8.6% WoW) and parent DMC (+4.1% WoW) were large outperformers, rallying even as uncertainty over the rebidding of the Semirara coal operating contract dragged on. The company disclosed it had petitioned a Makati court to block an order from the Department of Energy (DOE) requiring its disclosure of proprietary data for the use of other contenders for the bid.

 

While it was a slow week for local macro news, investors continued to keep an eye on the ongoing impeachment trial of VP Sara Duterte, with proceedings expected to draw more attention after the Senate impeachment court authorized the subpoena of her bank, tax, and Anti-Money Laundering Council records, as part of the investigation into allegations of unexplained wealth. Meanwhile, a Pulse Asia survey conducted prior to the impeachment trial revealed VP Duterte leads the 2028 presidential preference survey with 49% support, nearly double the 26% of former VP Leni Robredo in second place.

 

Foreigners were net sellers by $7m for the week, following two consecutive weeks of net inflow, while market activity softened to $104m ADTO. The peso touched a fresh low of 61.853:$1, down 0.4% WoW, as higher oil prices weighed on the broad macro outlook.

Semirara asks court to rule on government’s demand for data disclosure; Gov’t says bidding will proceed despite dispute

Semirara (SCC PM) filed a petition for Declaratory Relief before a local court, following repeated demands from the Department of Energy (DOE) to disclose proprietary information of its assets. The DOE directed the company to submit detailed info of its assets, including geological and technical data, and a detailed list of equipment. The DOE stated that the information will be made available to prospective bidders of the Semirara Coal Operating Contract (COC). DOE contends that asset ownership will vest to the government once SCC has recovered its costs. It is also prematurely asserting ownership of these assets even though the contract has yet to expire. Semirara is challenging the DOE’s demands, given the following. Its COC, which is governed by Presidential Decree (PD) 972, does not contain any provision that vests the title of the assets to the government upon cost recovery. PD 972 also states that ownership of movable assets within the exploration and production area transfers to the government only if they remain unremoved one year after the contract expires on July 14, 2027. Meanwhile, DOE Secretary Sharon Garin said that the bidding remains on track for July or August, despite the legal dispute. DOE is still working out the auction structure, given that rules for brownfield projects have not been crafted yet.

Government designates ICTSI’s Subic port as main gateway for Pax Silica trade

The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) signed a partnership with Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA) designating Subic as the preferred maritime gateway for requirements of Pax Silica. Note that SBMA is the regulator of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Subic port. This can support the long-term growth of the Subic port, where ICTSI is increasing capacity to 1mn TEUs from 600k TEUs. The Subic port is 100 kilometers from New Clark City, the site for the 1,619-hectare Pax Silica hub.

Meralco eyes Albay expansion

Meralco said that local officials in Albay is seeking its assistance in addressing power reliability issues in the province. According the Meralco chief external and government affairs officer Arnel Casanova. The Albay Electric Cooperative (Aleco) currently serves as the province’s power distributor. Meralco has been eyeing joint venture opportunities with electric cooperatives across the country (e.g. Batangas and South Cotabato). It plans to enter as an investor with an effective control of the board, while the electric cooperative will continue to manage day-to-day operations. Meralco holds distribution franchises across Luzon, serving areas that represent ~70% of the Luzon grid’s power demand. Meralco’s distribution arm accounted for 58% of 2025 profits.

Government posts P264bn budget deficit in June

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P264bn in June, wider by 9% YoY and 33% MoM as spending outpaced revenues. This brings the 6M26 deficit to P787bn, 3% wider vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in Jun rose 3% YoY. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections decelerated to +5% from +15% the previous month. Note that May 15 was the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return. Customs collections picked up to +12% growth from +6% the previous month. Non-tax revenues fell 43% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances. Recall that dividends from GOCCs (Gov't-Owned and Controlled Corporations) were remitted early this year in Feb. Gov't spending was up 5% YoY, a tad faster than the 4% growth in May. Primary spending for the month increased by +5% vs +1% the month before. This marks the fourth straight month of YoY growth after six consecutive months of YoY declines in gov't spending. Interest payments rose 9% YoY. The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66tn (-5.4% of GDP), 5% wider YoY from P1.61tn (-5.3% of GDP) in 2025.

BOP posts US$3.4bn surplus in June; GIR settles at US$104.7bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Jun posted a surplus of US$3.4bn. Higher than both the US$131mn surplus in the previous month of May and the US$226mn surplus in Jun 2025. This brings the 6M26 deficit to US$3.9bn, narrower than the US$5.6bn deficit for the same period last year. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$10.7bn (2.1% of GDP). Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Jun stood at US$104.7bn, +0.7% MoM. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.8 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $104.0bn.

Government expects US$40bn to US$70bn in investments from Pax Silica hub

The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) expects the Pax Silica hub to generate between US$40bn and US$70bn in investments at full development. The Philippines recorded US$7.8bn in FDI in 2025. It is also projected to generate up to 130,000 to 190,000 direct jobs and 500,000 to 800,000 indirect jobs. Exports could hit up to US$200bn. The 1,600-hectare site in New Clark City has an initial investment target of US$10bn. Timetable. 2026 - Negotiations with prospective partners. 2027 - Planning. 2028 - Start of construction. Participation in the project has expanded to more than 30 countries from fewer than 15 in April. Pax Silica will integrate semiconductor design, fabrication and advanced packaging, artificial intelligence computing, critical mineral processing, energy and data infrastructure, research and development (R&D), logistics, offices, housing, and commercial facilities as part of the Luzon Economic Corridor. The project seeks to move the Philippines up the manufacturing value chain by processing critical minerals domestically instead of exporting raw materials. The industrial hub is expected to require about three gigawatts of power at full development. A 500MW solar project with Saudi Arabia’s ACWA Power is under development. BCDA is also evaluating a proposal from an American company to develop a dedicated LNG plant and pipeline linking Subic and Clark. For water supply, the project will rely on surface water harvesting, storage, treatment, and recycling instead of groundwater extraction. The initial system is designed to supply 120 million liters per day, with capacity expandable to 300 million liters per day.

7.5mn poor households to receive new cash-aid amid rising oil prices

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. announced that the gov't will provide another round of cash assistance to 7.5mn low-income households affected by the rising oil prices given the renewed tensions in the Middle East. 1.5mn low-income workers and their families will receive P2,000 per month from July to December or P12,000 in total for six months. A one-time additional cash aid of up to P2,000 will be given to 3.5mn beneficiaries of the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) and the Walang Gutom Program. 2.5mn poor and near-poor households identified through the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) will also be extended assistance. This will be implemented through the Uplift Assistance program by the Dept. of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) with 37.5mn Filipinos set to benefit.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 8% YoY in June; Toyota sales fall 10%

CAMPI reported new car sales of 37,231 units in June, down 8% YoY but up 11% MoM. Brings 1H26 vehicle sales down 11% YoY to 205k units. Car sales figures that include BYD showed a 9% YoY decline in 5M26. Sales of xEVs (ex-BYD) rose 129% YoY to 6,995 units in June. June sales were largely lifted by the 5-fold increase in BEV sales, which more than offset the 10% YoY drop in hybrid sales. Brings 1H26 xEV sales (ex-BYD) to 31,381 units, with sales of hybrids up 58% YoY to 17,148 units.

Bureau of Customs forms task force to ease Manila’s seaport, airport congestions

The Bureau of Customs has formed a Technical Working Group (TWG) and Task Force to address operational bottlenecks at Manila’s major seaports and airport. High yard utilization at the seaports and collapsed warehouse capacity at the airport have disrupted supply chains. TWG aims to develop coordinated measures to improve cargo movement. TWG will report directly to the Office of the Commissioner and coordinate with the Department of Finance through the Office of the Undersecretary for the Revenue Operations Group. The Bureau of Customs recently issued a draft joint administrative order, which aim to police the movement of cargo.

BSP issues guidance regarding casino junket operations

The BSP issued a guidance paper reminding its supervised institutions to strengthen anti-money laundering and counter terrorism and proliferation financing measures for customers engaged in casino junket operations. Junket operators are companies and persons who arrange gaming-related services for high-value patrons, including travel arrangements, credit extension, and gaming room bookings. The BSP recommends measures to strengthen risk management in five areas. (1) board and senior management oversight. (2) money laundering and terrorism financing prevention program. (3) client acceptance and identification. (4) ongoing monitoring and suspicious transaction reporting. (5) self-assessment and training. It also highlights existing good practices. Enhanced due diligence for high-risk clients. Automated transaction monitoring. Client link analysis. Independent verification with regulatory agencies. Participation in information-sharing initiatives. Licensed casino operators already follow the said guidelines.

Senate impeachment count authorizes subpoena of VP Sara Duterte's tax and bank documents

The Senate Impeachment Court approved subpoenas for the bank, tax, and Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) records of Vice President Sara Duterte, her husband Manases Carpio, and 19 companies allegedly linked to them, as part of the investigation into allegations of unexplained wealth. In a ruling, Impeachment Court presiding Officer Francis Escudero overruled the defense's objections and allowed the production of the documents as the financial records were found to be sufficiently defined, identifiable, and materially relevant to the case. The Senate Impeachment Court ordered the banks to submit the requested records by July 30, 2026.

Vice President Sara Duterte’s net satisfaction rating inches up in June SWS survey

Vice President Sara Duterte’s net satisfaction rating (% satisfied minus % dissatisfied) in the latest Social Weather Stations Survey (SWS) inched up to +31 points in June from +29 points in March. Recall that President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating rebounded from his record low "poor" -15 rating in March to "neutral -7" in June. Duterte's gross satisfaction rating rose from 54% in Mar to 58% in Jun. Gross dissatisfaction inched up from 25% to 26% for the same period. SWS cited the following movements in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas. -2 points in Metro Manila (from “neutral” +5 to "neutral" +3). +5 points in Balance Luzon (from “neutral” +5 to "moderate" +10). Unchanged in the Visayas (steady at "good" +36). +2 points in Mindanao (from “excellent” +82 to "excellent" +84). Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,200 adults from Jun 20-29, 2026.

Sara Duterte leads in Pulse Asia's 2028 presidential preference survey

The Pulse Asia survey on voter preferences for the May 2028 presidential election showed Vice President Sara Duterte leading with 49% support, followed by former Vice President Leni Robredo at 26% and Senator Raffy Tulfo at 14%. The remaining potential candidates each garnered 2% or less. Note that the survey was conducted June 28-July 3 & 6, while the impeachment trial of VP Sara Duterte started on July 6. Duterte posted commanding leads across all major island groups, with her strongest support in Mindanao (87%), followed by the Visayas (53%), while she also led in Balance Luzon (31%) and Metro Manila (39%). Robredo ranked second nationally, drawing her strongest support from Metro Manila (38%) and Balance Luzon (31%), while posting more modest support in the Visayas (27%) and limited backing in Mindanao (8%). Tulfo placed third with relatively broad but lower support across regions, performing best in Balance Luzon (20%) and Metro Manila (16%), followed by the Visayas (10%) and Mindanao (2%). Across socioeconomic classes, Duterte led all segments, topping Class E with 59% support, while Robredo's strongest showing was among Class ABC (34%) and Tulfo's support was relatively consistent across income groups at 10-15%. For the senatorial preferences survey, the results found that the five strongest contenders are all incumbent senators—Robin Padilla (Rank 1–2), Tito Sotto (Rank 1–3), Raffy Tulfo (Rank 2–5), Alan Peter Cayetano (Rank 3–5), and Loren Legarda (Rank 3–5). Other incumbent senators that are within the top 12 are DepEd Secretary Sonny Angara (Rank 8–9), Senator Mark Villar (Rank 8–9), Senator Jinggoy Estrada (Rank 10–17), and Senator Chiz Escudero (Rank 11–19) all remained within or around the projected winning circle. Several candidates remained within striking distance of the Top 12, led by Philip Salvador (Rank 13–25), Jimmy Bondoc (Rank 13–25), and JV Ejercito (Rank 14–26).

PSE revises policy on index management

The PSE revised the criteria for inclusion in its various indices, including the PSE Index (PSEi). The revised policy will be implemented during the February 2027 index rebalancing. Among the most salient amendments are. Liquidity – Median Trading Activity Ratio (MTAR) and Monthly Value Turnover (MADV) will replace the previous liquidity criterion. MTAR is the 12M cumulative sum of the monthly trading activity ratios. Each monthly trading activity ratio shall be calculated by multiplying the median daily value traded by the number of days the security was traded during the month, with the resulting product dividend by the company’s free float market capitalization at the end of that month. Existing PSEi constituents need to have a minimum MTAR of 10% while potential new entrants to the PSEi must have a minimum MTAR of 15%. Public float – Companies with a market capitalization of at least P250bn will be subject to 15% minimum public float requirement. Companies below the P250bn threshold will still be subject to minimum public float of 20%. Market cap – the PSE will adopt a 98% cumulative market capitalization threshold as an additional criterion. Stocks that meet the aforementioned criteria will be ranked by average 12M market cap. Addition to the PSEi. Similar to the current policy, a stock that rises to the 25th position and above (for the current review period) will be added to the PSE Index. A stock that rises to the 30th position and above for four consecutive review periods will also be added to the PSE Index. Removal from PSEi. Similar to the current policy, a PSEi constituent that falls to the 36th position and below will be removed from the PSE Index. A PSEi constituent that ranks 31st and below for four consecutive review periods will also be removed from the PSE Index.
