REGIS REPORT

27 March 2026

Philippine equities retreated for a fourth straight week, seeing the PCOMP fall through the 6,000-handle as the fallout on the domestic economy from the Iran war remained in focus. The PCOMP slipped 0.8% or 45.79 pts WoW to 5,972.83 with investors closely watching the government's response to an impending energy crisis, with oil staying above $100/bbl and the peso falling further to fresh lows.

 

Early in the week, Pres. Marcos declared a state of national energy emergency amid the sharp surge in energy prices and growing concerns around fuel supply. Executive Order 110 directs various government agencies to implement a “fuel and energy allocation plan”, and broad conservation measures to manage the country’s supplies. Although there were no explicit mandates for fuel rationing just yet, some pockets in the economy have already undertaken certain initiatives such as the Energy Regulatory Commission's (ERC) suspension of operations of the electricity spot market, and on mobility, malls reducing their operating hours as well as private companies adding more flexibility to hybrid work arrangements. By the end of the week, government confirmed that the country's crude oil stocks are sufficient until end of June.

 

Meanwhile, the central bank held an emergency off-cycle meeting to discuss monetary policy amid weaker macroeconomic conditions, although they announced no changes to the key rate which was held steady at 4.25%. BSP Gov. Remolona did state that rate hikes are still possible if it can address demand-driven inflation from second-round effects of oil shocks, and added that the BSP is planning regulatory relief measures for the local banking sector, similar to what was done during the Covid-19 pandemic. The BSP hiked its 2026 inflation forecast to 5.1% (from 3.6%), and for 2027 to 3.8% (from 3.2%), assuming crude oil averages at $85/bbl this year and $76/bbl next. Regis and consensus are expecting no more cuts (and no hikes) in the policy rate this year.

 

On the corporate side, a few more 4Q earnings reports were released. MONDE (+5.7% WoW) missed forecasts despite the 4Q recovery in topline, as margins disappointed. GTCAP (-2.9% WoW) was also behind estimates as auto and property underperformed.

 

ACEN (+11.8% WoW) was the top gainer in the PCOMP this week following market chatter of a possible tender offer after an online broadsheet reported that parent AC (-1.8% WoW) is in talks with several foreign funds to sell a stake in its energy unit. Despite mounting risks to inflation, consumer names MONDE and URC (+2.3% WoW) were among top gainers for the week.

 

Meanwhile, PLUS (-9.9% WoW) was the worst performer in the index, extending its pullback to another week following a sharp three-week run that returned nearly 40%.

 

In other corporate stories, RRHI (-0.4% WoW) disclosed that it is subject to voluntary delisting following the intention of majority shareholder JE Holdings to conduct a tender offer at P48.30/sh, representing a 24% premium to its last close. The transaction is still subject to regulatory approvals.

 

Foreigners were net sellers for a fourth consecutive week, trimming YTD net inflows by another $35m to $175m. The PH10Y yield has breached 7%, up 11.2 bps WoW to 7.0194%. The peso fell another 0.8% WoW to a fresh low of 60.553:$1.

Philippine Strategy - “Energy emergency” declared – fuel rationing, power outages likely?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Managing fuel and energy supplies, rationing likely?

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr on 24 March issued Executive Order 110, declaring a state of nationwide energy emergency citing “an imminent danger of a critically low energy supply” (emphasis ours) as a result of the conflict in Iran. This ironically follows government statements the day before that the Philippines was not experiencing an oil crisis. The EO directs various government agencies to implement a “fuel and energy allocation plan”, and broad conservation measures to manage the country’s supplies. There were no explicit mandates for fuel rationing, though we would not be surprised if these were coming.

 

“UPLIFT” to include subsidies and cash handouts

EO 110 calls for a “Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport” (UPLIFT), which includes subsidies for public transport operators, and for commuters, and allows for the reduction or suspension of toll fees, aviation charges, and landing fees. It also includes cash handouts in the form of Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations.

 

Supporting shift to Evs

As we reported last week, retail prices have surged for gasoline (+60%) and diesel (+120%). The EO also called for the formulation of “longterm demand-side solutions” to decrease the country’s reliance on fossil fuels. These include the transition to electric vehicles (EVs) in public transportation, logistics, agriculture, etc

 

Power shortage possible? Potential suspension of electricity spot market

The UPLIFT program also allows for the “suspension of market operations or the declaration of a temporary market failure” of the wholesale electricity spot market (WESM).

 

We had previously noted a likely spike in electricity prices, as 75-80% of the grid is fueled by coal and natural gas. We are particularly concerned about the natural gas-fired capacity, which produces 25% of power in the Luzon grid. Of that, we estimate two-thirds – or about 17% of total – relies on imported LNG. The risk here is not just price (as with coal), but availability of supply. This raises the specter of power outages, particularly as the country enters its summer season, when electricity demand is highest.

 

Also watch out for LPG supply

We would also closely monitor supplies of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), which is the main cooking fuel in households and restaurants. The Department of Energy (DOE) estimates the average supply of petroleum products was 45 days as of 20 March. However, for LPG, the stock was just 23.5 days.

 

Renewables windfall may be capped

We had previously cited renewable energy firm ACEN (ACEN PM) as a rare winner in this crisis. However, the potential suspension of the WESM could cap gains. EV and hybrid adoption would still benefit Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) distributorship of BYD cars in the Philippines.

 
Philippine Banks - Bond yields spike – assessing CET-1 ability to absorb MTM losses
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Potential MTM losses could affect growth, NPL absorption, and dividends

Local bond yields have spiked since the start of the Iran conflict. The 10-yr risk free jumped over 100bps from 5.92% at end-Feb to as high as 7.14% on 23 Mar, before settling at just over 7% (Fig 1, at right). Note that, even before Iran, the 10-yr BVAL had not followed the 225bps cut in the BSP’s policy rate since mid-2024. Sharply higher yields raise the specter of potential MTM losses for banks’ investment portfolios. Unrealized losses would have limited impact on P&Ls, but would reduce CAR/CET-1 ratios, and have knock-on effect on banks’ ability to grow loans, to absorb new NPL formation, and even to declare cash dividends.

 

Assessing banks’ capacity to absorb 5% hit to FVPL and FVOCI

In Fig 2, we attempt to assess banks’ CET-1 ratios and their ability to absorb (an arbitrary) 5% loss on their FVPL and FVOCI portfolios. We then adjusted each bank’s end-2025 solo CET-1 ratios according to our estimated MTM hit to equity.

Metrobank (MBT PM) has the largest exposure, with its FVPL+FVOCI book at over 2.5x its equity base at end-2025. That said, the bank’s high CET-1 of 15.14% gives it enough room to take our estimated 192bp hit. Security Bank’s (SECB PM) low starting CET-1 of 12.10% makes it more vulnerable. But even a 5% MTM hit would leave its CET-1 at 11.3%, above the regulatory minimum.

 

Caveats galore

The exercise is a sensitivity analysis, and by no means describes each bank’s actual or potential MTM losses. We do not have granularity on banks’ mix of securities – either by currency or maturity – and a 5% loss could be overly bearish. Furthermore, these are unrealized “paper” losses; if a bank holds the instruments to maturity, the losses go away over time. We also do not know how bank balance sheets have moved since end-2025. Finally, if push comes to shove, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas has in past crises allowed banks to reclassify bonds – especially sovereigns – from FVOCI to HTM, even back-dating the shift to prevent such MTM losses.

 
GT Capital - Property, auto units drag 4Q25
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 core profit up 8% to P30.5bn – Below Street but Above Regis estimates

Headline FY25 profit reached P33.3bn (+17% YoY), boosted by a P3.5bn revaluation gain from the additional stake in Metrobank (MBT PM) and a P200mn CARS program tax incentive at Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP). Removing these and other one-off items, FY25 recurring profit rose 8% YoY to P30.5bn, short of consensus’ but above Regis’ estimate. Core profit rose by a mere 4% YoY to P4.4bn in 4Q25, buoyed by Metro Pacific (MPI) and the increased stake in MBT. Other key segments such as auto and property units fared poorly.

 

Weak high-end car sales, FX weigh on 4Q25 auto margins

TMP posted standalone profit of P19bn (+19%) in FY25, inclusive of a P392mn tax credit. Adjusting for the tax credit, we estimate 4Q25 profits contracted by 59% QoQ/53% YoY to P1.8bn, as GP margins fell 570bps QoQ/600bps YoY to 9% and offset the 21% QoQ/10% YoY improvement in retail sales volumes. While FX was a drag, management attributed the GP contraction mainly to weaker sales of high-end cars. 

Meanwhile, TMP’s EV sales rebounded 65% QoQ and 58% YoY to 6.5k units (32% of EV market), taking full-year EV sales to 19.5k units (+39% YoY), capturing 33% of the EV market.

 

MBT: Provisions pull down 4Q25 profits; Loan growth slows to 7.4%

Standalone 4Q25 profits at MBT were flat at P12.4bn, even though PPOP jumped 36% YoY on trading/fx gains. Gross loan growth slowed sharply to 7.4% in 4Q from 11.3% in 3Q, while NIMs dropped 9bps QoQ to 3.6%. Provisions jumped 87% YoY in 4Q, with NPL cover dipping to 141% as the bank ran down its reserves.

 

Property: Sales cancellations bite; 4Q25 reservation sales fall sharply

Federal Land FY25 income of P522mn (-30%) was padded by a P1.3bn gain from the sale of GT Tower units to its parent. Without the gain, losses widened to P800mn, driven by P4bn worth of cancellations. Reservation sales fell 10% to P15bn in FY25, implying a sharp contraction in 4Q25 to P2.4bn (-35% QoQ/48% YoY). Management cited that RFO inventory stood at 32 months.

 
Monde Nissin - 4Q25: Positive momentum now clouded by global turmoil
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

FY25 top line up 4%, core profit down 1%

MONDE’s FY25 core net profit amounted to P9.7bn (-1% YoY), behind our expectations, as margins disappointed. Revenues of P86.5bn (+4% YoY), however, were in line with expectations. 4Q revenues increased 6% YoY to P23.2bn, accelerating from +4% growth in 9M and 3Q on the back of volume-led growth in APAC BFB and improvements in Quorn. Group gross margin in the quarter was stable at 33.5% with elevated edible oil costs in the domestic business offset by cost savings in meat alternatives. Soaring energy and oil-related costs will be impossible to avoid, but management hopes these can be mitigated by the costs of power and commodity inputs that have been largely locked in over the next few quarters.

 

Biscuits and “Others” lift revenues; Noodles lag

4Q25 APAC BFB revenues rose 6% YoY to P19.5bn as robust volume growth in biscuits (+7% YoY) and “others” (+11%) compensated for the lingering underperformance in noodles (-1%). Growth of the biscuits segment has prompted management to increase FY26 capex to P7.5bn (P4.7bn in FY25), with bulk allocated for capacity expansion. Management, however, characterized these plans as flexible amidst the ongoing conflict in the Middle East.

 

Partial impairment reversal as Quorn improves

MONDE recognized a small, partial reversal (GBP13m) of the impairment charge on its meat alternative business following the modest improvement in sales and profitability.

Throughout the year, sales growth (+0.3%, constant currency) and gross margin (27.4%) improved sequentially, with a steady uptrend from 1Q to 4Q. Returning to profit and further impairment reversals are a “clear objective” by management. This business, however, is energy-intensive and the current environment certainly doesn’t look favorable.

 

Dividend of P0.24/share is a positive surprise but…

The company declared dividends of P0.24/sh for shareholders as of Apr 24 (payable May 21). This is on track to exceed total dividends declared last year (P0.31/sh) and would imply an 8% yield on current share price should the payout be repeated in 2H. Management, however, cautioned that while the intention is to match or beat the 60% payout policy, cash conservation will be prioritized as a safety net against extraordinary circumstances.

 

Strong 1Q but external risks are hard to ignore

MONDE indicated that 1Q26 top line growth could reach the “high single digits,” accelerating from the growth in FY25. The company, however, was reluctant to provide guidance for the rest of the year, citing heightened uncertainty arising from the effects of the Middle East conflict.

 
Cebu Air - 4Q25 profit shortfall; Iran conflict muddles outlook
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 core profit up 43% to P5.7bn – Behind Regis and Street forecasts

Headline FY25 profit rose >2x to P12.3bn. Removing non-core gains and FX adjustments totaling P6.5bn, we estimate core profit rose 43% YoY to P5.7bn. The results fell short of both Regis and Street expectations due to (1) a higher-than-expected number of grounded aircraft, which constrained seat growth to 10% (vs. initial guidance of 15-20%), as well as (2) operational disruptions in 2H.

 

Strong 4Q25 underlying profits

Core profit fell 33% to P1.3bn in 4Q25 mainly to the reversal of previously recognized tax loss carryovers. Pre-tax core profit otherwise rose 13% to P1.9bn, underpinned by healthy load factors of 82%, higher fares (+6%) and ancillary yield (+15%), as well as muted costs, which offset the modest 1% drop in pax volumes.

Resilient demand so far, despite 20-26% fare increases but…

Since the onset of the Iran war, CEB has implemented fare increases of 20-26% and continues to see healthy load factors, with demand growth holding up in the “low-to mid-teens” during the peak summer months of March to May. That said, management is cautious about pushing fares beyond levels that could negatively affect demand and admits that the fare adjustments so far are insufficient to fully cover all costs. Given the subdued outlook on fuel prices prior to the war, CEB did not hedge its fuel requirements. While its fuel supply is sufficient until end-April, procurement for the succeeding months will complicate the outlook.

 

Dividends on prefs, commons on pause

Given its focus on cash preservation, management is deferring dividend payments on both the convertible prefs and commons until the situation normalizes. With P22bn in cash, it is confident of sustaining operations for two years under the worst conditions and believes it has the option to trim its P35bn capex for 2026 should the conflict be prolonged.

President Marcos declares state of national energy emergency

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. through Executive Order (EO) 110 has declared a "state of national energy emergency" amidst significant concern on the price and supply of fuel/energy. The President has established the "Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food and Transport (UPLIFT)" committee in order to ensure a coordinated, whole-of-government response framework approach to the crisis. The committee is chaired by the President with the various gov't agencies as members in order to oversee and coordinate energy crisis mitigation measures and intiatives. Measures include fuel and energy optimization plans, action against hoarding, profiteering, and supply manipulation, as well as ensuring the stability and adequacy of domestic fuel and energy supply through the Philippine National Oil Company (PNOC). The EO also directs the departments/agencies to monitor key inputs and implement necessary response measures under their scope (i.e., cash relief, food supply, essential goods price monitoring, etc.). One of the ongoing discussions mentioned was the adjustment of the 12% VAT on fuel with Energy Undersecretary Felix Fuentebella citing that they are leaning towards an adjustment of the tax rate rather than an outright suspension. Senator Bam Aquino stated that the upper chamber is willing to work overtime to pass urgent tax reforms if economic managers give the green light. Dept. of Economy, Planning and Development (DepDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan, however, warned of a possible economic slowdown if both excise tax and VAT on fuel are removed.

BSP keeps rates unchanged at off-cycle meeting; revises GDP and inflation forecast

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) held its policy rate at 4.25% during an off-cycle meeting. This marks the first time the BSP held rates steady since Feb 2025, after six consecutive 25bp cuts. The central bank noted that raising the policy rate at this time would delay the economic recovery. BSP Governor Eli Remolona highlighted that the monetary policy would have limited effectiveness against supply-driven inflationary risks. However, he also stated that the BSP may hike rates if it can address demand-driven inflation from second-round effects of oil shocks. Remolona added that the central bank is planning regulatory relief measures for the local banking sector, particularly for lenders from the informal sector and low-income businesses. He elaborated the plan is similar to what was done during the pandemic (Bayanihan 2, to Recover as One Act)—to have a standardized restructuring framework on default loans, postponing some payments depending on the sector. The second out of six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Apr 23. The central bank's GDP forecast for 2026 was trimmed to 4.4% from 4.6% but left its forecast for 2027 unchanged at 5.9%. The BSP raised its inflation forecast for 26/27E to 5.1% from 3.6% and 3.8% from 3.2%, respectively.

BOP posts US$2.3bn deficit in Feb; GIR settles at US$113.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Feb posted a deficit of US$2.3bn. Wider than both the US$373mn deficit in Jan and a reversal from the US$3.09bn surplus in Feb 2025. This brings the 2M26 deficit to US$2.7bn. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP). Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Feb stood at US$113.3bn, 5% higher YoY and up from the US$112.6bn in the previous month of Jan. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

Government posts P165bn budget surplus in Jan

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P165bn in Jan, 142% wider YoY as gov't spending continued to decline. Revenue collections in Jan were flat (+0.4% YoY) given lackluster growth in tax collections and the decline in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were relatively flat, +1%. Customs collections rose a modest 2%. Non-tax revenues fell 12% given a moderation in Bureau of Treasury (BTr) income. Expenditures dipped 24% YoY in Jan due to the steep decline in primary spending. Primary spending for the month fell 40%. Since July 2025, January 2026 marks the sixth consecutive month of a YoY decline in gov't spending amid the slowdown in infrastructure projects following the flood control scandal. Interest payments rose 22% from additional debt and coupon payment timing changes following refinancing.

Marcos signs oil excise tax suspension law

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed into law Republic Act (RA) 12316 which grants him the power to suspend or reduce the excise tax on petroleum products. After signing the law, Marcos emphasized that he would wait for the "best time" to implement it. Fuel excise taxes may now be adjusted if the average Dubai crude oil based on Mean of Platts Singapore benchmark reaches or exceeds US$80/bbl for a month. The adjusted excise tax will automatically revert to the original rate a week after the three-month limit or if the one-month average of Dubai crude is below US$80/bbl as certified by the Dept. of Energy. Implementation for a period cannot exceed three months and if extended, the aggregate period of the adjustment shall not exceed one calendar year. The President may only suspend or reduce excise tax collections on fuel products until Dec. 31, 2028. Estimated revenue losses could reach as much as P43bn if suspension lasts for three months and P106bn if extended to a total of six months.

DOTr and DSWD initiates nationwide rollout of fuel subsidies for PUV operators, drivers

The Dept. of Transportation (DOTr) and the Dept. of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) has started to distribute fuel subsidies to public utility vehicle (PUV) drivers and operators. DOTr Secretary Giovanni Lopez stated that the department will use its P2.5bn fuel subsidy allocation from the 2025 national budget. Depending on vehicle type, PUV operators are expected to receive amounts varying from as low as P1,500 to P10,000, while PUV drivers will receive P1,500 to P5,000 each. The DSWD was said to focus on providing P5,000 in cash relief to TNVS, jeepney, service delivery, and motorcycle taxi drivers.

Department of Energy says oil crisis won’t lead to power outages

The Department of Energy assured a Senate panel that the worsening global oil supply situation will not lead to power outages. Energy Undersecretary Alessandro Sales said that the power sector may be affected, but to a limited degree. Note that natural gas accounts for 25% of the Luzon grid’s energy mix.

Energy regulator suspends electricity spot market

The Energy Regulatory Commission ordered the suspension of the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM), following the declaration of a state of national energy emergency. WESM trading was officially halted at 12:05 am on March 26. During the suspension period, the regulator will implement an administered price (AP) and will oversee dispatch, which will prioritize renewables. The ERC is consulting with stakeholders to finalize a Modified Administered Pricing Mechanism by April 1. The proposed framework introduces technology-specific pricing to reflect fuel costs. Coal plants may receive a fixed rate of P6/kwh, while natural gas plants will be paid based on contracted prices. The suspension of the spot market trading will create uncertainty on how buyers and sellers of power can transact and agree on pricing until the administered pricing mechanism is finalized. For some sellers of spot electricity, the suspension will remove the opportunity to make windfall profits.

Prime Energy targets to deliver new gas from Malampaya expansion by 4Q26

Prime Energy, power-arm of Enrique Razon, stated that new gas from its US$893mn Malampaya Phase 4 expansion is expected to start flowing by 4Q26. The recent successful drilling and completion of Camago-3 is estimated to contain recoverable gas volumes ~2.5x larger than the Malampaya East-1 discovery announced in January, effectively doubling remaining reserves. Camago-3's flow rate was said to be up to 60mn standard cubic feet of gas per day. The two wells are expected to extend the gas field's operating life by around six years. Prime Energy estimates Malampaya gas to cost roughly ~P4.8/kWh vs P10.3/kWh for imported LNG. Additional drilling projects are said to be underway, with the Bagong Pag-asa (north of Malampaya) exploration and drilling said to be in progress.

Department of Energy allows temporary use of dirtier fuel

The Department of Energy is authorizing the temporary and limited usage of Euro II petroleum products, a cheaper but dirtier fuel type, to ensure supply. Order will cover vehicles made in 2015 or earlier, traditional jeepneys, power plants and generators, as well as marine/shipping sectors. Fuel companies intending to offer Euro II fuels must also notify the DoE on retail outlets such products will be made available. Since 2015, the Philippines has limited the motoring industry to the use of Euro IV fuels, which have sulphur content of 50 parts per million (ppm) (vs. 500ppm for Euro II).

Dept. of Agriculture to distribute input cost subsidies to farmers and fisherfolk

Dept. of Agriculture (DA) spokesperson Arnel De Mesa stated that the department is currently distributing P150mn in agricultural fuel subsidies and plans to conduct a separate P10bn broad cost-relief program by early next month. De Mesa also stated that the larger relief program will be drawn from a P10bn fund under the DA's 2026 budget, and cover around 4.1mn beneficiaries with a payout of ~P2,350 each.

Food firms agree on up to 60 days price freeze; CNPF cites ample inventory stock

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. stated that several food manufacturers have committed to keep prices steady for up to two months to cushion the inflationary impact of the Middle East conflict. Some companies that were said to have agreed to keep prices steady were Nestle Philippines and Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM). Digital transactions - decelerating, but still up 42% YoY in February

Digital transactions over PesoNet and InstaPay in February stood at P2,343bn, up 42.3% YoY. This was slightly slower than the 43.1% growth in January and a 9.2% decline MoM following seasonal peaks in Dec/Jan. The 2-month total of P4,929bn was up 42.7% YoY, a shade faster than the 42.0% in FY2025. For smaller-ticket (under P50,000) real-time InstaPay transactions, February reached P1,167bn, up 63.7% YoY versus 65.5% in January. The 2M26 total of P2,410bn grew 64.6%, faster than the 57.3% in FY2025. By volume, InstaPay transactions in February were up 298.6% YoY, bringing the average ticket down to P1,875, suggesting wider adoption for everyday payments. Larger, batched PesoNet transactions reached P1,176bn, up 26.0% YoY, slightly slower than the 27.1% in January. The 2M26 figure of P2,514bn was up 26.6%, also slower than the 30.9% in FY2025. Adoption of digital payments continues to support the growth of digibanks, neo-banks, and online lending, and is contributing to financial inclusion.

Malls to implement shorter operating hours

Mall operators announced that they would reduce their operating period by 1-2 hours per day beginning March 30. Malls are typically open from 10AM to 10PM. New operating hours - 11AM to 9PM from Monday to Friday; 10AM to 9PM on Saturday and Sunday.
