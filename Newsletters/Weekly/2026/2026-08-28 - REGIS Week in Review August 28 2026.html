REGIS REPORT

28 August 2026

The PCOMP ended sharply lower this week, down 4.5% or 282.13 pts WoW to 5,956.33, as persistent foreign selling and continued profit-taking in large-cap names weighed on sentiment. Trading was relatively light for earlier in the week as investors searched for catalysts following an underwhelming 2Q earnings season. The medium-term outlook softened further after the BSP raised its 2027 inflation forecast at this week's policy-setting meeting where they announced a widely-expected 25-bp rate hike.

 

The index finally broke below the 6,000 level on accelerated volume amid broad-based selling toward the end of the week, which closed with rebalancing trades for the MSCI August index review. Friday also saw the peso close at a fresh record low, crossing 62:$1 for the first time in history.

 

Despite its removal from the MSCI Standard Index, ALI (+2.8% WoW) bucked the market weakness and was the best-performing PCOMP name for the week as traders positioned around the rebalancing flows, while parent AC (-2.8% WoW) also bought ALI shares. Meanwhile, upweighted BPI (-6.8% WoW) and BDO (-5.5% WoW) were among the biggest laggards as investors weighed the soft macro outlook.

 

The BSP delivered a 25-bp rate hike as the market had expected -- its third consecutive hike since the tightening cycle resumed in April. While headline inflation has eased, the BSP took a “preemptive” move amid volatile oil prices and looming El Niño and wage pressures. The central bank remains hopeful that no further hikes will be needed, but kept the door open to further tightening, and even sharply raised its 2027 inflation forecast from 4.5% to 5.4%.

 

Meanwhile, top-weighted ICT (-7.9% WoW) was the biggest drag on the index as foreign investors continued to take profits in the stock following its strong run and proximity to record highs. Recent unfavorable news on its Brazilian operations, including Maersk shifting cargo away from ICT’s Suape terminal and the antitrust clearance allowing Maersk or MSC to participate in the Santos port bid, only added to profit-taking pressure.

 

In other corporate stories, the recent excitement around the Lopez names faded, with FGEN (-15.7% WoW) giving back much of its recent gains after parent FPH (-8.7% WoW) rejected KKR's P35/sh offer to acquire an additional stake as a precursor to launching a tender offer for FGEN shares. SCC (-8.0% WoW) was the worst performer on the index after the government released draft rules revising the terms of the rebidding of the Semirara coal mine contract, with the new framework placing greater weight on financial terms and raising uncertainty over the royalty structure and economics of the concession.

 

Elsewhere, mining remained one of the few bright spots as higher gold prices and renewed policy support continued to drive interest in the sector, which saw APX (+4.1% WoW) and PX (+5.6% WoW) extend their gains. This also followed President Marcos Jr.'s recently signed EO 122, which aims to accelerate mineral exploration, streamline permitting, and encourage greater domestic mineral processing.

 

Foreigners remained net sellers for the fourth straight week, with net outflow accelerating to $115m this week. The peso fell 1.0% WoW to a new record low of 62.260:$1.

Dominion Holdings set to take over Tampakan copper-gold project

Dominion Holdings (DHI PM) announced that it is merging with Indophil Resources Phils and Sonar Hldgs. with DHI as the surviving entity. Indophil and Sonar together hold 100% voting rights in Sagittarius Mines, Inc., the owner of the FTAA contract for the Tampakan Copper-Gold project. DHI’s Board has authorized management to determine, fix, and finalize the exchange ratio for the merger. DHI is also seeking SEC approval to increase its authorized capital to P30bn (26.75bn common shares @ P1/sh and 2.5mn preferred shares @ P100) from P3.4bn (3.4bn common and 200k prefs). It is also amending its articles to deny pre-emptive rights of shareholders. Tampakan Copper-Gold greenfield project has an estimated 2.9bn MT of resources, containing an estimated 15mn MT of copper and 17.6mn ounces of gold. Sagittarius Mines has planned a phased development for Tampakan, targeting an initial annual mill production rate of 15mn MT, which is similar to Atlas Mining (AT PM). The project is expected to start commercial operations in two to three years, or as early as 2028.

PCC clears sale of URC's noodle venture control to Nissin

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) has cleared URC's sale of a portion of its stake (21%, equivalent to 39.7mn shares) in Nissin-URC (NURC) to Nissin Foods Asia (NFA). Post-transaction, URC and NFA will own 30% and 70%, respectively. The price has yet to be disclosed but will be finalized by December this year with the transaction to take effect on Jan 7, 2027. Nissin Foods Asia is wholly owned by Nissin Foods Holdings Co. Ltd. (2897 JP). Nissin will take a bigger role in product innovation and brand-building, while URC will remain as the local operating partner and distributor. In FY25, NURC accounted for 6% of URC’s revenue, 10% of EBIT and 6% of profit.

Maynilad partners with New Clark City water infrastructure bidder, K-Water

Maynilad announced that it is partnering with Korea Water Resources Corp. (K-Water) for the latter’s P15bn New Clark City water infrastructure unsolicited proposal. No other details were disclosed, particularly on Maynilad’s stake in the joint venture. K-Water has proposed to build and operate the water and wastewater infrastructure in New Clark City for a period of 25 years. It is currently negotiating the terms of its proposal with the Bases Conversion Development Authority (BCDA). Project is forecasted to reach a capacity of 135MLD (vs. Maynilad’s West Zone demand of ~1,500 MLD and supply requirement of 2,300 MLD). New Clark City is the main location for the proposed Pax Silica initiative.

East West Bank announces P9bn rights offer

East West Bank (EW PM) announced plans to raise up to P9bn in a stock rights offer (SRO). Proceeds will go towards “strategic growth objectives”, including wealth management, IT capex, and loan growth. The P9bn target is roughly equivalent to 11% of the bank’s total equity of P81.7bn as of 2Q26, and about 36% of the bank’s market cap of P24.9bn. The stock trades on just 0.31x P/bk (on trailing 2Q26 book). Loan growth was +10.2%, with NIMs at 8.17% in 2Q26 and PPOP growth at +35%. Rising NPLs resulted in a 66% surge in provision expense, with credit costs at over 500bps for 1H26. NPAT for 1H26 fell 17% to P3.4bn, with 2Q26 alone down 32% YoY to P1.6bn, while ROEs fell to around 8% in 1H26 from the 13% level in 2H25. EW’s Solo CET-1 ratio fell to 11.35% in 2Q26. The bank’s loan book is 85% in consumers, with close to 50% in unsecured credit card and personal/salary loans. This year, the bank paid a cash div of P0.82/sh, for a 7.65% dividend yield.

Makro to open in four Ayala Land estates

Ayala Land has signed land lease agreements with Makro Philippines for the development of new stores in four of its mixed-use estates: Cloverleaf in Balintawak, Quezon City. Broadfield in Biñan, Laguna. Arca South in Taguig City. Evo City in Kawit, Cavite. Makro Philippines is a joint venture between Ayala Corporation and CP Axtra (CPAXT TB).

Megaworld targeting 12,000 hotel rooms by 2032

BSP raises key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%; revises inflation expectations

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised the key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%. The rate hike was in line with expectations. This is the third 25bp rate hike in a row since the rate hike cycle resumed back in April. Despite easing headline inflation, the "preemptive move" was done due to the still volatile oil prices as well as the looming El Niño and wage hike. BSP Governor Eli Remolona said the central bank is hopeful that no further rate hikes will be necessary, although it remains open to further tightening if needed to bring inflation back toward its ±3% (2-4%) long-term target. The central bank revised its inflation estimates for 2026/27E from 6.4%/4.5% to 6.1%/5.4%. July CPI was 6.2%, bringing the 7M26 average inflation rate to 5.0%. This implies that inflation could average 7.6% for the rest of the year. The BSP expects headline inflation to peak in 4Q26 and ease closer to the ±3% target by 4Q27. The BSP also stated that economic growth is expected to rebound in 4Q26, as fundamentals for growth "are still in place". The fifth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled this year will be held on Oct 22.

Government posts P106bn budget deficit in July

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P106bn in July, wider by 462% YoY as spending outpaced revenues. This brings the 7M26 deficit to P893bn, 14% wider vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in July rose a modest 2% YoY given the sharp decline in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections picked up to +7% from +5% the previous month. Customs collections moderated to +7% growth from +12% the previous month. Non-tax revenues fell 40% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances. Recall that dividends from GOCCs (Gov't-Owned and Controlled Corporations) were remitted early this year in Feb. Gov't spending was up 20% YoY, picking up from the 4% growth in June. Primary spending for the month accelerated to +17% vs +5% the month prior. The acceleration in spending was attributed to direct payments for various rail transport projects as well as the continued implementation of the UPLIFT framework projects to address rising oil prices. Recall that primary spending declined YoY for six consecutive months from July 2025 to Jan 2026, following President Marcos' disclosure of the flood control scandal during his SONA. Interest payments rose 9% YoY. The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66trn (-5.4% of GDP), 5% wider YoY from P1.58trn (-5.6% of GDP) in 2025.

BOP posts US$1.5bn deficit in July; GIR settles at US$103.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of July posted a deficit of US$1.5bn. A reversal from the US$3.4bn surplus in the previous month of June but wider than the US$167mn deficit in July 2025. Recall that the gov't raised US$2.5bn through a US dollar bond issuance in June. This brings the 7M26 deficit to US$5.4bn, narrower than the US$5.8bn deficit in the same period last year. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$10.7bn (2.1% of GDP), much wider than the US$5.7bn deficit (1.2% of GDP) in 2025. Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-July stood at US$103.3bn, -1.4% MoM. The BSP attributed the decline to the central bank's net foreign exchange operations and the national gov't's drawdowns on foreign currency deposits for external debt payments. The peso weakened to P61.4/USD at end-July from P61.2/USD at end-June. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.7 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at US$104.0bn, down from US$110.8bn in 2025.

Moody's affirms "Baa2 stable" investment rating for the Philippines

Moody's Ratings retained its "Baa2 stable" investment-grade rating for the Philippines on expectations of a gradual economic recovery alongside continued fiscal consolidation efforts by the gov't. Recall that in April earlier this year, Moody’s also retained its Baa2 stable rating for the Philippines. The "stable" outlook was maintained given the country's medium-term growth potential and expectations of stabilizing fiscal metrics over the next two years despite headwinds. Moody's also cited ample access to financing markets and sufficient international reserves. Concerns include slower private investment, infrastructure execution delay, debt affordability, physical climate risk, and political uncertainty. A ratings upgrade would require sustained fiscal consolidation, a clear decline in gov't debt, and stronger medium-term growth.

DOE publishes DRAFT bidding framework for Semirara coal contract, other natural resources

The Department of Energy posted the draft framework that will likely govern the bidding of the Semirara coal contract. Point 6.3 introduces a “minimum economic threshold” based on the government’s own valuation of confirmed reserves, although the document did not specify the valuation methodology. This threshold amount will not be disclosed to the bidders, but financial offers must meet or exceed this threshold. Under Point 6.9, the financial offer, essentially the royalty payment to the government, will now be the sole determinant of the winning bidder. The basis for determining royalty payments remains unclear, including whether these will be based on gross or net revenues and whether they will be fixed or subject to minimum annual payments. Based on the draft, the terms of the royalty appear to be left to the bidder’s discretion. Recall that the previous auction gave preference to the most technically and financially competent operator. Point 7.4 requires bidders to commit to supplying a defined portion of production to domestic end-users, with the government seeking to dictate both volume and pricing.

President Marcos orders creation of critical minerals policy framework

President Marcos signed Executive Order No. 122, which establishes a national policy framework for the critical minerals industry and reorganizes the Mining Industry Coordinating Council (MICC). The MICC will be jointly chaired by the secretaries of Department of Environment and Natural Resources and Department of Finance. Creation of a comprehensive industry work plan by the MICC within 90 days, in which international sourcing standards, environmental safeguards, and valuation frameworks are outlined. The DENR was directed to issue a Philippine Critical Minerals List within 30 days of the order taking effect. The DENR and the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) will create a streamlined permit processing within six months from the issuance of EO 122, which will entail a fully digital virtual one-stop shop platform to be implemented within a one-year period. The Department of Trade and Industry is tasked to provide incentives for downstream refining, battery production, and side-stream industries. A “use it or lose it” policy will be implemented for critical minerals mining tenements, immediately cancelling and reallocating freed-up mineral areas into declared reservations.

BSP wants digital payment fees to be waived for consumers

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Deputy Governor Mamerto Tangonan said that the Central Bank will now focus on digital payment fees (such as payments to settle utilities). Consumers currently pay a “convenience fee” for digital payment fees. However, Deputy Governor Tangonan said that “There is already an existing rule that such fees should be shouldered by the merchants, utility or collecting firms, not the paying consumer”. “…so they need to make the billers or the merchants pay for it, not the consumer…”. Consumers should be relieved from the payment fee of P5-15 per online payment via an e-wallet or bank account.

Fintech firms ask regulators for understanding on transfer fees

In a panel discussion during the ASEAN Tech Summit Manila, fintech leaders expressed their worries over policies that seem to favor only the interests of consumers. Barbie Dapul, chief operations officer of GCash’s operator G-Xchange Inc., cited as example the BSP Circular No. 1238, which encourages reducing digital transaction fees. She said GCash could only afford to cut transfer fees to P10, as it has to pay for cybersecurity and reliability services to keep the e-wallet running. Ms. Dapul said making regulations fairer for e-wallets would improve their viability. She also noted that e-wallets like GCash cater primarily to unbanked Filipinos by lowering barriers to financial access. The BSP previously clarified that Circular No. 1238, which was implemented on July 4, does not require financial institutions to waive digital transfer fees.

Digital payments top P3trn in July; InstaPay re-surges post fee cuts

Transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) expanded 45.9% YoY to P3,072bn in July, a new record high and crossing the P3trn mark for the first time. Year-to-date, digital payments amounted to P19,162bn, up 44.8% YoY, faster than the 42% pace in FY2025. Payments over PESONet came in at a new all-time high of P1,541bn, up 33.8% YoY, slower than the 37.5% growth the previous month. This took the 7M26 figure to P9,653bn, up 32.2% YoY, compared with 30.9% growth in 2025. Meanwhile, real-time, smaller-ticket transactions over InstaPay also reached a monthly record of P1,531bn, up 60.6% YoY, compared to the 54.8% pace in June. The YTD total was P9,509bn, growing 60.3% YoY, versus 57.3% for FY2025. The volume of InstaPay transactions also re-accelerated to 761mn, up 106.2% YoY, versus +96.2% YoY the previous month.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 2.5% YoY in July; Toyota’s unit sales flattish

Note that the BYD brand is not included in CAMPI’s data; We estimate BYD’s market share stood at ~7% in 1H26. We reckon industry-wide car sales (including BYD) likely returned to positive YoY growth of ~+5% in July 2026. Senator files Bill to ban online gambling advertisements

Senator Francis Escudero filed Senate Bill No. 2347 (SB 2347) or the proposed Gambling Advertising Prohibition Act. Senator Escudero compared the Bill to the Tobacco Regulation Act of 2003. SB 2347 would prohibit gambling advertisements across television, radio, newspapers, magazines, billboards, websites, online platforms, and social media. Gambling advertisements are currently prohibited on print media but are allowed on television and social media. It would also ban celebrity and influencer endorsements, product placements, promotional giveaways, branded merchandise appealing to people under 21 and gambling sponsorships involving sports, concerts, and cultural events. The proposal provides a one-year transition period after the law takes effect, allowing operators, advertisers, broadcasters, and platforms to wind down existing agreements. No new advertising or sponsorship agreement could be entered into during that period except where necessary to conclude existing obligations.

PAGCOR to launch app to promote licensed online gaming platforms

PAGCOR will soon launch an application that will make it easier for players to play exclusively in authorized gaming sites. PAGCOR launched the PAGCOR Guarantee page, an online verification facility that allows the public to check whether an online gaming provider is licensed or not. Users can be redirected to the platforms of licensed operators.

SEC looking at more capital market reforms

SEC Chairperson Atty. Francis Lim is studying potential capital market reforms. The SEC is considering raising the P100mn minimum capital requirement for stockbrokers, which was instituted in 2001. It is also working with the PSE and the Philippine Dealing and Exchange (PDEx) on market-making rules for publicly listed securities (in order to enhance liquidity). It is consulting with the World Bank to review the framework for public offerings, including requirements for debt and equity securities. The review seeks to make capital raising more accessible by making disclosure requirements more proportionate to the type and risks of securities being offered. It is collaborating with the ADB to develop a Philippine Capital Market Master Plan. The goal is to improve the competitiveness of the domestic market.
