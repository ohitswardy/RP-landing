REGIS REPORT

11 September 2026

The PCOMP lost 0.5% or 28.79 pts WoW to 6,061.81, giving back part of last week’s rebound as investors turned cautious on renewed inflation and rate concerns. Oil prices moved back above $100/bbl this week, reinforcing expectations of further tightening from the Fed which pushed bond yields higher, while the peso continued to fall to fresh lows as the domestic macro outlook deteriorated.

 

Banks (-3.1% WoW), Holdings (-3.9% WoW), and Property (-2.0% WoW) weighed down the market, with proxies BDO (-4.8% WoW) and SM (-4.8% WoW) among the worst performers in the index, along with GTCAP (-9.2% WoW) and JGS (-7.0% WoW) that sold off on the weak peso outlook. SM, GTCAP, and JGS all hit new 52-week lows.

 

ICT (+5.4% WoW) was again the top gainer on the index, bucking overall market weakness given its status as a relative "safe haven" for investors wary of domestic macro risk, considering the much larger share of its overseas earnings base and the added translation benefit of a stronger dollar. Mining names also continued to rally led by APX (+5.6% WoW) and OGP (+4.3% WoW) despite the pullback in gold prices.

 

On the M&A front, FGEN (-9.2% WoW) reversed much of its recent rally after KKR sold its entire 20% stake, after its earlier proposal to acquire an additional stake in the company was rejected by parent FPH (-2.0% WoW). FGEN plunged 11.9% on Friday following the news. Meanwhile, DHI shares remain suspended pending more details on its proposed merger with Indophil Resources and Sonar Holdings, which would potentially create the largest mining company in the Philippines. DHI announced that the merger is expected to be fully implemented by 4Q26, while also guiding that it will fix the exchange ratio at "book-to-book value".

 

Foreigners were net sellers for the sixth straight week, though decelerating to a weekly outflow of $12m. The peso depreciated by another 0.2% WoW, closing at a new low of 62.702, while the PH10Y yield jumped 14.3 bps WoW to 7.4313%.

RESEARCH
ICTSI - Lifting our numbers; maintaining Hold
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Robust growth runway in the price

We upgrade our NAV-based TP for ICTSI by 20% to P900/sh, reflecting a stronger operating outlook for its ports in Mexico and Durban, South Africa, as well as a DCF roll-over and a higher PHP:USD exchange rate assumption. We continue to see a compelling near-to medium-term growth story, anchored on strategic capacity expansions at its key ports and the expected turnaround of its Durban terminal. The share price, however, does look pricey following the big outperformance YTD (+64% vs PCOMP), thus our Hold rating.

 

Raising EPS forecasts by 3-10%

Following ICTSI's strong 1H26 results and the favorable developments at its two key EBITDA drivers—Mexico and Manila—we are more confident that the earnings momentum is sustainable and consequently raise our FY26E-28E profit forecasts by 3-10%. We expect its Mexico port to lead the growth, with the market likely to absorb capacity additions instantaneously. 

Even though its ports in Iraq and Suape, Brazil, face near-term headwinds, we believe these challenges are unlikely to derail the momentum.

 

Multiple growth engines

The likely turnaround of Durban port in 2028, as well as potential acquisitions, are compelling reasons to remain upbeat, in our view. While competition for the Santos port concession appears to have intensified, we believe ICT remains a strong contender for the asset. We estimate that a successful bid for Santos could add around P75/sh to our NAV.

 

DCF-based TP reflects premium valuation

Our revised TP of P900/sh is based on a DCF analysis of each port, using WACCs of 6-11%, but does not incorporate any potential acquisitions. ICT trades at 12x FY27E EV/EBITDA and 21x PER, a premium to peers and historical averages. Key upside and downside risks include changes in trade activity, acquisitions, new competition, and regulatory developments.

Dominion Holdings to base Tampakan project injection exchange ratio on book value

Dominion Holdings (DHI PM) announced that it will fix the exchange ratio for the planned merger with Indophil and Sonar at “book-to-book value”. Book values of DHI, Indophil, and Sonar will be based on their audited end-Aug 2026 financial statements. The number of shares to be issued to the shareholders of Indophil and Sonar will be disclosed in due course. Note that Indophil’s book value per share (BVPS) stood at P21 as of end-2025, ~13x larger than DHI’s end-June 2026 BVPS of 1.6. Given that DHI was listed in the PSE in 1997, it will only be required to maintain a minimum public float of 10% following the backdoor listing (see PSE rule here). DHi is currrently owned by the Sy family (70%), the Consunji family (9%), and the public (21%). Meanwhile, the Sy family owns 88% of Indophil and 100% of Sonar while the Consunji family and Alcantara family own 10% and 2%, respectively, of Indophil. For DHI to maintain a 10% public float, we estimate the exchange ratio must not exceed 1.5x DHI shares for every Indophil share. The merger is contingent on approval of stockholders (DHI’s stockholder’s meeting is on Sep 14), Philippine Competition Commission, and SEC. Effective date of the merger will be the first calendar day of the month immediately following the date of receipt of SEC’s issued Certificate of Merger. DHI previously announced that it plans to set the exchange ratio prior to its annual shareholder’s meeting on September 14. Indophil and Sonar hold 56% and 44% voting interests, respectively, in Sagittarius Mines, Inc. (SMI), which holds the FTAA for the largest undeveloped Tampakan copper-gold project. Based on DHI’s disclosures, Indophil and Sonar hold 40% and 31.6% economic interests, respectively, in SMI, with the remaining 28.4% held by Tampakan Mining Corp. and Southcot Mining Corp.

PH dollar reserves at $104.8bn as of end-Aug

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Aug increased 1.4% MoM to US$104.8bn. The MoM increase in the GIR was primarily due to the increase in the price of gold in the int'l market, which offset the national gov't's drawdowns on its foreign currency deposits for external debt payments. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 6.8x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$104.0bn, lower than the US$110.8bn in 2025.

NCR wage board approves new P60 minimum wage hike

The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board-National Capital Region (RTWPB-NCR) approved Wage Order No. 28 on Sep 7, which will raise the current minimum wage by P60 (+9%) from P695 to P755. Recall that Wage Order No. 27's implementation was suspended due to a court order issuance of a TRO (Temporary Restraining Order) and a writ of preliminary injunction following a legal challenge last Jul 31. This was supposed to raise the NCR minimum wage in two tranches—+P60 effective Jul 25, 2026, and the remaining +P25 scheduled for Jan 20, 2027. After the approval from the RTWPB, Wage Order 28 requires affirmation from the National Wages and Productivity Commission (NWPC) before it can take effect. The new wage order may take effect by Sep 26 or 27. Dept. of Labor and Employment (DOLE) Secretary Francis Tolentino stated that the issuance of Wage Order No. 28 was done in order to still provide the first P60 tranche while the legal dispute over the previous wage order is being resolved. He clarified that the following P25 is still slated for next year. Tolentino also added that the two wage orders will not be cumulative and will not add up to a P145 total wage increase. Wage Order No. 28's P60 wage increase is higher than the P50 hike last year and is the highest percentage increase granted by the NCR regional wage board in the past two decades. According to the Dept. of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the hike is estimated to directly benefit 1.1mn minimum wage workers and may indirectly benefit 1.9mn workers earnings above the minimum wage from the correction of wage distortions.

Philippines to bear brunt of El Niño next year—DA

Dept. of Agriculture (DA) spokesperson Arnel de Mesa stated that the full brunt of the incoming drought from the upcoming El Niño may likely be felt during the dry season harvest in 1H27. He added that the DA sees no impact from El Niño this year on overall rice supply. The DA's estimated impact of El Niño on palay (unmilled rice) output this year was a 700,000 MMT (~440,000 MMT of rice) decline but the recent torrential rains cushioned the impact to a 400,000 MMT (~250,000 MMT) decline instead. The country's palay (unmilled rice) output is projected to be 19 MMT (-3.5% YoY) for FY26. Using the PSA's official milling recovery rate of 63%, the estimated palay output equates to approximately 12 MMT of rice. The country's annual rice demand amounts to ~15-16 MMT. Palay output as of 1H26 amounted to 9.1 MMT (-0.6% YoY). According to the state weather bureau PAGASA, a moderate to strong El Niño is currently present in the Tropical Pacific, which is slated to last till 1H27 and may develop to a "very strong" state before year-end. The impact of the expected drought next year will depend on the severity and the length of the El Niño. Total agricultural damage during El Niño in 2024 reached P15bn, with a 3% decline in the country's agriculture and fisheries output.

Sufficient funding in place to sustain P20/kg subsidized rice program—DA

Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that President Marcos' P20/kg subsidized rice program has sufficient funding until the end of his term (June 2028). The subsidized rice program is targeted for vulnerable consumers and is expected to reach as many as 60mn Filipinos till June 2028. The program utilizes the P9bn allocated to the National Food Authority (NFA) to procure rice from local farmers alongside funding specifically set aside for the subsidy program. The agriculture secretary, however, noted that President Marcos is aware that the P20/kg market price for rice, which was one of his key campaign promises in 2022, could no longer be attained. Urea prices have risen to almost P2,600/sack (+62% YoY) and is 3.7x higher than the P700/sack price levels when Marcos made the promise.

Commercial banks’ loan growth accelerates to 10.4% in July

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth accelerated to 10.4% in Jul from 9.8% in Jun. Loans to productive activities picked up to 9.8% vs 9.2% the previous month. Consumer loans grew 17.1%, moderating from Jun's 17.8% rate. This marks the slowest pace of growth since Jul 2022 (14.7%). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth eased to 10.3% from 10.7% the previous month.

Asset quality improves in July (ex-IBL/RRP), but still wary of Credit Card #s

Headline numbers from the BSP showed asset quality deteriorating in July vs June, with reported Gross NPL Ratio of 3.35%, up from 3.29% in June. However, this was because the BSP’s “loan” denominator includes interbank loans (IBLs) and reverse repos (RRPs), which fluctuate wildly on a monthly basis. In July, the IBL+RRP account dropped 29% MoM from P1,307bn in June to P933mn. To avoid the volatility, asset quality ratios ex-IBL/RRPs showed NPL ratio edging down 1bp MoM to 3.54% in July, from 3.55% in June, and down 4bps YoY. Past Due (PD) ratio fell 10bps MoM to 4.47%, from 4.57% the previous month, and 13bps from the 4.60% in July 2025. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) was broadly stable, up 10.4% YoY, a shade slower than the 10.5% pace set in June. Credit cards continued to see deterioration, with NPL ratio up 6bps MoM to 5.34%, and PD ratios up 7bps to 7.85%.

VP Sara Duterte posts bail after arrest order for alleged grave threats

Vice President Sara Duterte posted bail after a trial court ordered her arrest over grave threats linked to her previous comments made about President Marcos, the first lady, and the former House speaker. The case stems from an online press conference that took place in Nov 2024, where Duterte mentioned that she had spoken with an assassin and instructed him to kill Marcos, his wife (Liza Araneta Marcos), and his cousin (then-House speaker Martin Romualdez), if she herself were killed. Duterte has denied the allegations. The VP also faces an ongoing impeachment trial in the Senate where the alleged threats are also part of the impeachment case.
