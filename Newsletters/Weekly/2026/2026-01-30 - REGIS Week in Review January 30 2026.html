REGIS REPORT

30 January 2026

The PCOMP extended its decline to a second straight week as the disappointing 4Q GDP report dragged local shares, although MOC buy flows on Friday's index rebalancing helped reverse some of the weekly losses. The big change in index composition was the addition of RCR (-9.5% WoW) in place of AGI (-4.1% WoW), while there were also modest upweights for AREIT (-0.9% WoW) and PLUS (-11.4% WoW). By the end of the week, the PCOMP inched down by just -0.1% or 4.29 pts WoW to 6,328.97.

4Q25 GDP grew only 3.0%, hugely missing consensus forecast of 3.7% and decelerating sharply from the 3.9% figure in 3Q. This is also the lowest (ex-pandemic) print since 3Q11, and brings FY25 GDP growth to 4.4%, below the gov't's target of 4.8-5.0%, and consensus forecast of 4.9%. The steep decline in capital formation (-10.9%) was the largest drag, attributing to the large decline in construction, while both private (3.8%) and government (3.7%) consumption were also below historical trend.

Property (-4.1% WoW) lagged among sector indices as the slow business and domestic demand conditions captured in the weak 4Q GDP numbers underpinned concerns of a protracted recovery in residential demand. Meanwhile, Mining (+4.0% WoW) outperformed as gold prices touched fresh highs anew. Financials (+1.8% WoW) were mixed -- the underperformance in BDO (-2.2% WoW) reflected the same cautiousness on the local macro outlook, but was offset by the surge in BPI (+6.9% WoW) that saw large MOC buying at the close of the week lift its share price by 9.7% on Friday alone.

Despite substantial passive buy flows expected for the entry of RCR into the PCOMP (estimated inflow of $33.5m), RCR ironically fell 4.9% on Friday (and 9.5% for the week). A P7-bn ($119-m) placement of RCR (by parent, sponsor RLC) early in the week to both foreign and locals essentially satisfied a good chunk of incremental demand ahead of rebalancing day. Moreover, index "speculators" were seen unwinding their RCR shares thus overwhelming  "passive" fund demand at the close of day.

Foreigners remained net buyers for the fifth straight week, this week by $128m due largely to the RCR placement. The peso was stronger by 0.3% WoW to 58.880:$1.

RESEARCH
Puregold - Well-positioned; maintaining Buy
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Adjusting our estimates; maintaining Buy

Puregold (PGOLD) has notably outperformed its peers both operationally and in terms of share returns over the last two years. Despite the mild 9M25 profit disappointment and the more visible presence of competition, we remain comfortable about its outlook. We trim our EPS estimates for FY25/26E by 7/10% but maintain our DCF-based target price at P50/sh as we roll our valuation forward. At <10x PER with a yield of 5%, PGOLD, in our view, still offers attractive value.

 

Execution makes the difference

PGOLD has grown top line at 10% CAGR over the last decade, on the back of both SSSG and store expansion. In our view, it has done arguably better than peers due to better execution both tactically and strategically, despite the much more visible threat from hard discounters and the competing membership shopping operator. We expect this to continue and forecast similar revenue and profit CAGR of 10% over FY25-28.

 

House brands likely to play increasingly important role

We expect private label, “house”, brands to play a bigger role in PGOLD’s strategy and fortunes in the future.

Private label products account for <1% of Puregold sales today but, in the future, are likely to help blunt the threat of hard discounters whose strategies are anchored on private label products. For S&R, private label is already a meaningful share of sales (<10%) but there is plenty of scope for it to rise, helping margins and customer retention.

 

Joining the fray in smaller formats

After acquiring Puremart (153 stores) in 2024, PGOLD intends to finally expand its store count and thus its presence in the fast-growing small-format segment of grocery retail. We reckon PGOLD has two key advantages which it brings to Puremart: (1) size, thus clout with suppliers and (2) geographical spread.

 

Estimates revised; maintaining Buy rating and TP

We adjust our forecasts accounting for the latest results but retain our target price at P50/sh as we roll over our valuation. Our TP implies a 12x PER multiple for FY26E, below historical average, with a 4% yield.

ICTSI is seeking a US$300mn senior unsecured corporate loan from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB). Proceeds will be used to fund the expansion and upgrade of its Philippine ports (i.e. Bauan International Port, Manila International Container, and Mindanao International Container Port). DMCI Power seeks approval for proposed Mindoro-Semirara power grid interconnection

DMCI Power Corp. is awaiting the Department of Energy’s approval on its proposed P3bn, 19-kilometer submarine cable that will connect the power grids of Semirara island and Mindoro. Under the proposal, DMCI Power plans to build a 30MW coal plant, expand its wind plant in Semirara to 100MW (vs. 12.5MW today), and build 2,000MW of solar capacity. Demand in Mindoro is >100MW annually, 60% of which is currently being supplied by high-cost bunker and diesel fuel. Proposal aims to lower the cost of electricity in Mindoro by displacing high-cost fuel. DMCI Power Corp. is a wholly-owned unit of DMCI Holdings (DMC PM). It currently operates an aggregate capacity of 188MW in off-grid areas of Palawan, Masbate, Oriental Mindoro, and Semirara.

Nearly all Ayala Land offices have green certifications

Ayala Land said that it has 1.4mn sqm of offices that have received the EDGE Zero Carbon Certification. This means that nearly all of Ayala Land’s office buildings have green certifications. 4Q25 GDP grows 3.0%, missing 2025 target

GDP grew at 3.0% in 4Q24, decelerating sharply from the already lackluster (revised) 3.9% growth rate in 3Q. This is well below consensus and REGIS expectations of 4.2%. The 3.0% print is the lowest (ex-pandemic) since hitting 3.0% back in 3Q11. On the demand side, weakness was evident all around. The largest factor in the poor GDP print was the even steeper decline in capital formation (-10.9% from -2.8%). Capital formation has declined for two consecutive quarters, mainly due to the significant decline in construction. Consumption, both private (3.8%) and government (3.7%), was also below historical trend. Private consumption was 3.8%, the lowest since 3Q10's 2.6% (ex-pandemic), slowing further from 3Q25's 4.1%. Post-pandemic (2022 onwards), private consumption was at least 4.7% or higher. Food and non-alcoholic beverages decelerated to 3.8% from 4.5% in 3Q as well as alcoholic beverages which was still negative at -7.0%. Net exports, however, saw significant improvement to 1.9% from 0.9% given robust growth in exports at +13.2% accelerating further from +7.4% the previous quarter. On the supply side, growth in all three major sectors (services, industry, and agri) deteriorated relative to the previous quarter. Growth in the service sector slowed a tad to 5.2% from 5.4% in 3Q. Industry sector growth turned negative at -0.9% for the first time (ex-pandemic) since 3Q11's -1.6%. Construction activity fell sharply to -7.1% from -0.4%, as growth remained in the negative given the impact of the flood control scandal on gov't infra spending. Government construction was negative for the third consecutive quarter at -41.9% much worse than the -26.2% growth in 3Q. Household construction contributed to the continued decline in capital formation as it posted a modest 2.2%, moderating from four consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. Bringing FY25 GDP growth to 4.4%, below the government's target of 4.8-5.0% (5.5-6.5% initial target) and the consensus forecast of 4.9%. The full year figure was also below REGIS' FY25E forecast of 4.8% and significantly slower than FY24's 5.7% GDP growth.

BSP uncertain on another rate cut this year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona guided that another rate cut this year is uncertain given current economic conditions. Recall that Remolona previously said that a rate cut in Feb was "on the table". He stated that a cut during the next Monetary Board meeting "… is still a maybe" and not certain. Remolona highlighted that if inflation and 4Q growth are benign and weaker-than-expected, respectively, it may prompt another cut. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%. The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2025/26/27 are 4.6/5.4/6.0%, respectively. For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively. Consensus and Regis Inflation expectations for 2026 are 2.6% and 3.0%, respectively.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in Dec

The Dec trade deficit narrowed to US$3.5bn, contracting 15% YoY, reflecting the faster growth of exports vs imports. This brings the FY25 trade deficit to US$49.2bn, 10% narrower YoY. Imports for the month grew by 7% YoY to US$10.5bn, a reversal from the 2% decline in Nov. The rebound to growth was primarily driven by the surge in capital goods (+20%) and fuel imports (+6%). Notably, consumer goods fell 1%, the fourth consecutive month of a YoY decline—the longest streak since the pandemic in 2020. Exports climbed by 23% YoY to US$7.0bn, the twelfth consecutive month of YoY growth and accelerating from the 21% increase in Nov. Electronics exports jumped by 44% YoY to $4.0bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 58% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 3% to US$3.0bn.

The Development Budget Coordination Committee has reduced the gov't's revenue targets for 2026 onwards. Recall that Finance Secretary Frederick Go recently stated that it will retain its fiscal deficit targets, which implies lower gov't spending moving forward. The revised gov't revenue forecasts are as follows: 2026: P4.82trn from P4.98trn. 2027: P5.12trn from P5.37trn. 2028: P5.57trn from P5.91trn. PH gov't retains fiscal deficit targets

Finance Secretary Frederick Go stated that it will retain its fiscal deficit targets. This is despite the recent downward revision for the 2026 revenue target from P4.98trn to P4.82trn. Recall that the DBCC trimmed the GDP growth targets for 2026 to 5-6% and 2027 to 5.5-6.5% from 6-7% initially. The fiscal deficit targets for 2026/27/28 are P1.65trn (5.3% of GDP), P1.6trn (4.8%), and P1.55trn (4.3%), respectively. The Bureau of Treasury's Dec 2025 and FY25 cash operations report will be released on Mar 3. As of 11M25, revenues amounted to P4.15trn (+1% YoY) while expenses were P5.41trn (+2.5% YoY). The YTD fiscal deficit stood at P1.26trn (+7% YoY). The gov't projects the FY25 deficit to be P1.56trn (+4% YoY).

Imported rice tariff to stay at 15% until March

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) stated that the imported rice tariff will remain at 15% until the end of 1Q26 but will increase by 5ppt to 20% if the average price of 5% broken Vietnam rice falls between US$350/MT and US$367/MT. The average price of the benchmark variety was US$387/MT as of Dec 2025. Recall that the rice import tariff was reduced from 35% to 15% back in July 2024 under EO 62, which is subject to review every four months. The tariff will be reviewed in April with March serving as the reference price point.

IT-BPO Association “cautiously optimistic” about growth in 2026

Jonathan Madrid, President of the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines, said that the association was “cautiously optimistic” about growth in 2026. He said that two factors influencing the growth were: ease of doing business and supply of employable talent. The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) expects the industry to achieve revenue of US$42bn and employ 1.97mn Filipinos by the end of 2026. These represent 4%-5% growth from the 2025 revenue (US$40bn) and employee (1.9mn) figures. Mr. Madrid previously said that the industry’s growth will still be driven by core segments – financial services and healthcare. He also said that he expects Global Capability Centers (GCC) to grow faster. These are offshore units established by multinational corporations to provide specialized services such as finance, IT and customer support to their global operations. IBPAP previously reported that American and Australian companies had plans to expand in the Philippines. BPO firms account for 70%-90% of the occupied office space of the developers we cover.

Marcos approves national broadband connectivity plan

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. during an Economy and Development (ED) Council meeting has approved the National Digital Connectivity Plan (NDCP), an initiative expected to fast-track the rollout of critical broadband infrastructure and lower internet costs. A draft Executive Order (EO) for the NDCP was said to be released in the future to strengthen the implementation of the plan. The Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) elaborated in a press release that the plan was stated to be the gov't's strategic roadmap for every Filipino to have access to fast, reliable, and affordable internet by 2028. The gov't targets to accomplish the creation of 8mn digital jobs, 40-80% reduction in internet costs, and raise average broadband speeds to 225 Mbps for mobile and 275 Mbps for fixed broadband. The DICT also listed down priority initiatives such as: Completion of the National Fiber Backbone. Expansion of submarine cable landing sites. Acceleration of next-generation mobile technologies. Improving connectivity for isolated areas. Cybersecurity and climate-resilient infra measures.

ERC approves recovery of pre-terminated contract costs

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has permitted Meralco to recover a total of P31bn (P0.28/kwh) from end-users over a 36-month period starting in March 2026. Amount is related to cost recoveries involving “change in circumstance” applications from the following: South Premiere Power Corp. (SPPC) – P15.85bn. San Miguel Energy Corp., (SMEC) – P13.4bn. ACEN Corp. – P1.75bn over 12 months. Panay Energy Development Corp. (PEDC) – P380mn over 12 months. The ruling follows a Supreme Court decision in favor of SPPC and SMEC and a Court of Appeals declaration that the price adjustments were executory. SPPC and SMEC sought to terminate its 10-year supply contract, signed in 2019, with fixed fuel costs.

ERC reviewing viability of “forward contracting”

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is currently exploring the viability and legality of “forward contracting” for power supply deals. Under this framework, power utilities may lock in future supply from gencos at a predetermined price to shield consumers from sudden bill shocks. According to ERC chairman and CEO Francis Saturnino Juan. The ERC chief raised concern over the absence of competitive bidding for forward contracts, which could result in limited transparency and reduced consumer awareness.

Supreme Court rejects impeachment case against Vice President with finality

The Supreme Court (SC) dismissed the motions for reconsideration on its ruling that declared last year’s impeachment case against the Vice President unconstitutional. Last year, the SC dismissed the impeachment complaint delivered by the House of Representatives. The SC cited constitutional infirmities, including the apparent lack of due process, et al. The dismissal was not really in doubt since the SC decision to stop the impeachment proceedings last year was unanimous. The SC decision, however, does not stop new impeachment proceedings from being initiated beginning Jan 14.
