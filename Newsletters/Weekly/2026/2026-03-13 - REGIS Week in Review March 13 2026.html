REGIS REPORT

13 March 2026

Local shares continued to weaken as the domestic macro outlook dimmed amid persisting tensions in the Middle East. Brent crude oil prices steeply climbed with the Iran war escalating towards the latter half of the week, looking poised to finish above the critical $100/bbl. This has triggered further selling of the peso to a historic low as well as continued equity outflows. The PCOMP fell another 4.1% or 261.47 pts WoW to 6,058.94.

In response to rising oil prices, BSP Gov. Eli Remolona signaled that the central bank could raise interest rates if oil hits $100/bbl and the USD continues to strengthen. On the fiscal side, the House approved on second reading a bill allowing the President to suspend or reduce fuel excise taxes under certain specific conditions. Also, the Department of Transportation will provide a P5,000 (c.$83) fuel subsidy to public utility drivers in Metro Manila starting March 17, alongside potential toll discounts for buses and trucks. Meanwhile, government bond yields climbed, with the 7-year auction at 6.473%, 61.4 bps above the previous auction, reflecting heightened market caution.

BPI (-8.3% WoW) was the biggest decliner in the index for the week, weighed down by heavy foreign outflows. All sector indices finished in the red with Holdings (-4.9% WoW) and Property (-4.9% WoW) down the most.

On the flipside, SCC (+7.0% WoW) again bucked market weakness despite the 4Q earnings miss, amid growing bets that the company could win the re-bidding of its coal operating contract in Semirara Island. With submissions scheduled on April 28, the Department of Energy also clarified that it will maintain the royalty structure for coal miners. Parent DMC (+1.7% WoW) likewise finished in the green.

Outperforming for a third week was PLUS (+5.2% WoW) riding on broad optimism following its chairman's recent share purchase. ACEN (+3.5% WoW) shares rose after 4Q earnings bore better-than-expected EBITDA as operations rebounded in the quarter.

Another round of 4Q earnings results provided additional market cues for investors. JFC (-4.9% WoW) missed profit estimates due to higher financing costs and tax provisions, although operationally, EBIT was ahead of forecasts as core brands were resilient in 4Q. BLOOM (-11.2% WoW) earnings were worse than expected, with the widening recurring net loss in 4Q attributed to weak tourist arrivals and soft domestic consumption, which saw volumes across categories fall QoQ and YoY. CNVRG (-5.9% WoW) was slightly behind baring a notable slowdown in net subscriber adds for the quarter, which trailed its competitors' numbers. RLC (-5.7% WoW) came in-line with estimates with strong mall results lifted by margin expansion, although residential demand continued to fall in the quarter.

Broad risk-off mood saw net foreign outflows accelerate to $108m. The peso fell 1.2% WoW to a new record low of 59.740:$1. The local currency has already depreciated by 3.6% since the Iran war erupted -- the third worst-performing Asian currency after the THB and KRW.

Philippine Strategy - Iran war’s impact on OFWs, inflation, GDP, and the PHP
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Oil price and supply concerns could drag already-soft GDP

It has been a week since the strikes on Iran, and crude oil prices are up c50% to over $100/bbl. While the region employs many Filipinos, the impact of past conflicts on remittances has historically been minimal. Elevated oil prices have had a more pronounced macro/earnings impact. The Philippines imports virtually all of its petroleum needs, resulting in near-immediate flow-through into inflation and the Current Account. A protracted region-wide conflict would stall consumption and weaken the PHP, compounding the country’s already-soft GDP growth trajectory. Consumer companies will likely be worst hit, while energy producers (coal / renewables) could see windfall gains.

 

Over 1mn OFWs in Middle East, but remittance impact surprisingly limited

Iran is not a major employer of Filipinos, with estimates topping out at under 1,500. However, government data has about 1mn Filipino workers in the Middle East, or roughly half the total of 2.1mn total OFWs. That said, remittances have been remarkably resilient in past crises. In the last 35 years, remittances only declined YoY twice. First was in the aftermath of the 1998 Asian Financial Crisis - but more because the PHP depreciated sharply against the USD, and OFWs were able to send fewer dollars for more PHP. The other was during the Covid pandemic, when even though shutdowns in global trade and industry in 2020, remittances fell just 0.8% YoY that year. During the 2003 Iraq War, remittances were actually up 10% YoY.

Oil price spikes to hit inflation, consumption, CAD and the PHP

The Philippines is highly dependent on imported oil, and, more broadly, energy. We import nearly all of our petroleum requirement, and roughly 70% of our coal consumption. The country does not subsidize fuel or electricity prices, which means shifts in global prices are reflected in domestic prices relatively quickly. On paper, gas and fuel are less than 4% of CPI. However, the pass-through into transportation costs (9% of CPI), and electricity rates (5%) result in strong second- and third-round effects on consumer prices.

 

Gov’t response – suspend oil tax, clampdown on hoarding, 4-day work week

The Philippine government has the power to suspend excise taxes on petroleum products of P10.00/liter on gasoline and P6/liter on diesel (the pump price of both were below P60.00/liter as of Friday, 6 March in Metro Manila). This could provide some limited relief to consumers. However, this only kicks in if the price of Dubai crude stays above $80 for more than 60 days. Various agencies such as the Department of Energy (DOE), police, and local government are cracking down on price gouging and hoarding (here and here). Social media over the weekend showed long lines at petrol stations as consumers rushed to fill their vehicles’ tanks, amid threats by some station owners of potential rationing or closures. Meanwhile, President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr declared that some government agencies would temporarily implement a 4-day work week to reduce fuel consumption.

 
ICTSI - Reversion to the mean
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Downgrade to Hold

ICTSI has been a massive outperformer over the past three years, with the shares rising over three-fold since end-2022. We believe the current share price already discounts the company’s strong near-to medium-term earnings prospects – albeit not the possible upside from future acquisitions. Valuations of 12x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 20x PER no longer offer compelling value, in our view; thus, we downgrade our rating to Hold from Buy.

 

Factoring concession extensions ahead of regulatory approvals

Our revised TP of P750/sh (+34%) adjusts for the recent acquisition in South Africa, as well as pending favorable contract extensions at five ports, including two ports in Manila, which are still subject to regulatory approvals. We also changed our PHP:USD rate assumption to P59 from P57, which we use to convert our USD-based NAV to PHP. Given that the earnings impact of these NAV revisions will mostly be realized in the long run, we only raised our FY26E/FY27E profit estimates by 5/3% to reflect the strong 4Q25 results and likewise introduce our FY28 forecasts in this update.

Balance sheet can support M&As, but greenfields may dilute near-term profits

ICT is one of the contenders for the auction of a 25-year concession involving the construction and operation of a 3.25mn TEU capacity greenfield port (Tecon 10) in Santos, home of Brazil’s largest port. While the opportunity looks promising, as the port of Santos today already appears to be 80% utilized, the project will require significant capital and could dilute near-term earnings.

 

Valuations at historic mean but could re-rate with successful tenders

Current valuations are in line with historical averages, but accretive acquisitions previously led to multiple re-ratings between 2009 to 2015. Contributions from possible future acquisitions cannot be discounted. However, these are difficult to quantify. On the assumption that ICT secures the Santos concession and it generates an unlevered IRR of 16%, we estimate it could increase our NAV by 9% (+P68/sh). Upside and downside risks include changes in trade activity, acquisitions, new competition, and regulatory developments.

 
Jollibee - 4Q25: Core brands resilient, newer ones mixed
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

FY25 profit to common +6%, top line +13%

JFC reported FY25 net profit after divs to prefs of P10.5bn (+6% YoY), which would be behind Regis and consensus expectations. While top line (P305.1bn, +13%) was within Regis expectations and EBIT (P20.2bn, +19%) looks ahead, higher financing costs and tax provisions negated the gains. A much smaller FX loss (vs FY24) helped boost the YoY comparison. Group-wide 4Q25 revenue growth decelerated to +10% YoY (vs 9M25's +14%) due to base effects from the acquisition of Compose Coffee (completed 3Q24). Gross margin in the quarter contracted 130bp to 13.8%, as management held back price adjustments to prioritize volume-driven growth.

 

Jollibee brand is the star

4Q25 domestic SWS grew +10% with SSSG of +5.0% (+1.2% in 3Q). The Jollibee brand and Mang Inasal led growth as various initiatives drove higher average check and foot-traffic alongside faster franchise rollout. The int’l business posted SWS growth of +15% on SSSG of +5.5%. The Jollibee brand likewise led the way across its int’l markets with double-digit SWS growth (i.e., US +20.3%, Vietnam +50.8%).

 

China recovering but Smashburger deteriorates further

China’s recovery continued in 4Q, marking the 3rd straight quarter of positive SWS and SSS growth (+5.0%).

Smashburger saw 4Q SSSG declining further to -9.9% (-4.7% in 3Q), as the company reevaluated its dealings with aggregators. EBITDA loss for Smashburger doubled to P952mn. In the coffee segment, CBTL suffered a setback with the exit of a franchisee in Korea, leading to a loss of ~200 franchise stores.

 

Perp refinancing and Compose acquisition drives higher interest charges

FY25 financing costs rose 18% YoY to P4.2bn with new debt to buy Compose acquisition and refinance the relatively cheap (3.9% coupon) US$600mn perpetual notes redeemed in January 2025.

 

Company tweaks guidance; much hinges on turnaround for int’l ops

Management for 2026 relative to 2025 maintained its guidance on SWS (+8%) and SSSG (+4–6%) but revised upwards its outlook for store network growth to +5–10% (+4–8% in 2025) and EBIT growth to +11–15% (+10–15%). The company is not (yet) publicly declaring any change to its aim of reaching profit of P24bn by 2028, despite plans to spin off its international businesses. Given where FY25 profit was, profits need to grow by >30% CAGR to reach that goal – a tough task even in ideal conditions. A turnaround for JFC’s international portfolio (FY25 net loss of ~P1.4bn) is a necessary step not just to reach that target but also to complete its spin-off and listing.

 
Semirara - 4Q25 volume shortfall; prospects for coal contract bid hopeful
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 profit drops 34% to P13bn – Behind estimates

Semirara reported 4Q25 profits fell 19% to P3.2bn. Excluding FX, we estimate 4Q25 profit dropped 20% to P3.3bn, bringing FY25 profit down 34% YoY to P13.0bn. The results fell short of Regis and consensus estimates, as full-year coal sales volumes of 15.4mn MT (-7%) missed both Regis (16.8mn MT) and management guidance (15.5-16mn). Management conveyed confidence that it can submit the strongest bid for the Semirara coal block auction on April 28, as the evaluation will largely hinge on the mine plan and technical qualifications.

 

Lower coal quality weighs on 4Q sales volumes; ASPs remain soft

Coal mining profits fell 24% YoY to P2.7bn in 4Q, despite a 66% increase in production. Given limited inventory and production of commercial grade coal, sales volumes dropped 42% to 2.5mn MT in the quarter, with export volumes plunging 83%, as the company prioritized domestic sales. ASPs fell 23% to P2,185/MT, broadly in line with benchmarks. Meanwhile, the power unit’s EBITDA was flat at P1.35bn in 4Q, as outages reduced output and negated the benefit of higher ASPs. While a larger share of sales volumes was sold via contracts in 4Q, lifting ASPs, SCC remained a net seller to the spot market, with only 42% of capacity under contracts.

2026 guidance: Better outlook due to new mine, higher coal prices

With commercial grade inventory at 1.7mn MT by end-2025 and production at the higher quality coal mine, Acacia, gradually progressing, despite water seepage challenges, management anticipates improved operating metrics this year. Coal prices (ICI4) have also rebounded due to Indonesia’s coal output cut.

 

Semirara coal block tender updates

After bid applicants submit documents on April 28, the Department of Energy aims to complete its evaluation on May 8 and award the COC on May 22. The bidder able to show the best mine plan, anchored on the highest annual production and supported by organizational, equipment, and financial strength, should be awarded the contract. Talks regarding a potential partnership with Meralco have started but have not yet progressed. Meralco and San Miguel bought bid docs.

 
ACEN Corp. - Operations improve in 4Q25
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 recurring profit up 4%

ACEN reported FY25 profit (before pref divs) fell 60% YoY to P3.8bn. Excluding the P3bn impairment charge for the Vietnam wind projects and other “one-offs”, management estimates recurring profit was 4% YoY higher at P6.3bn, which would be below Regis expectations. We believe the shortfall is likely due to higher financing costs, as core att. EBITDA of P22.5bn (+17%) was better than anticipated. Our EPS forecasts factored in a previously announced rights issue for July 2025, which did not materialize. We believe a capital-raising exercise is now due, if not overdue.

 

Strong 4Q rebound in PH

4Q25 recurring profit rose 152% YoY to P2.0bn while core att. EBITDA rose 39% YoY to P6.9bn, buoyed by higher output (both RE and conventional plants) in the Philippines and higher prices in India. Repairs to wind facilities in north Luzon and stronger output from third-party sources in 4Q25 helped the Philippine portfolio end FY25 with EBITDA up 4%, after a 10% YoY decline in 9M25. Although operations in Australia remained weak due to lower spot prices, stronger prices in India, as well as fresh contributions from the Monsoon wind plant in Vietnam, more than compensated.

Spot prices spike due to Iran war

Management indicated its PH contracted capacity is now at 78%, up from 56% in 2025, following the start of its mid-merit contract with Meralco in Feb 2026. It continues to hold a net long exposure to spot prices, which have jumped by ~P2/kwh (from <P3/kwh) since the war in Iran began. The company believes this event will allow them to secure better priced long-term contracts.

 

2026 capex at P80bn

The company plans to spend P80bn on capex in 2026, up from P55bn in 2025 to complete nearly 2GW of solar, wind, and BESS facilities from now until 2027. Management said a capital-raising exercise remains on the table and will be assessed along with current market conditions. Net gearing (attributable, including unconsolidated operating entities) was already 1.6x as of end-2025, while FY25 net debt/EBITDA stood at 11x.

 

 
Bloomberry Resorts - 4Q25 net loss widens QoQ and YoY
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

4Q25 EBITDA falls 31% QoQ and 67% YoY

Bloomberry’s 4Q25 recurring net loss widened to P2.8bn while 4Q25 EBITDA fell 31% QoQ and 67% YoY to P1.3bn, worse than REGIS and consensus forecasts. While the company largely blamed weak tourist arrivals, we suspect that the recent government corruption scandal, which led to weaker GDP growth, also hurt the company’s performance. Volumes of every category across both Philippine casinos fell QoQ, with Solaire Entertainment City’s (SEC) VIP volume showing a particularly large decline.

 

SEC: Volumes of every category fall QoQ and YoY

SEC’s 4Q25 GGR fell 7% QoQ and 27% YoY to P9.3bn, as volumes of every category declined on a QoQ and YoY basis (see figures 4-6). The company attributed the decline in volumes (particularly in VIP) to fewer players from its main foreign markets, Korea and China. VIP volume fell 29% QoQ and 50% YoY to P51.1bn, the lowest level since 2Q21. Meanwhile, slot volume fell for the 3rd consecutive quarter to P72.7bn, the lowest level since 1Q22. We estimate that SEC’s 4Q25 GGR would have been down another 5% (to c. P8.8bn) if not for the unusually high mass table win rate of 49% (+3.2ppt QoQ and 1.1ppt YoY), much higher than historical average of c. 40%.

 

SN: Sequential declines in volumes of every category

Solaire North’s (SN) 4Q25 GGR rose 4% QoQ (+33% YoY) to P4.8bn, as higher win rates offset sequential declines in volumes across every category.

Mass table volume fell 20% QoQ and 14% YoY to P5.4bn, the lowest level since the opening in 2Q24. Meanwhile, slot volumes were flattish (down 0.3%) QoQ, the first quarter that they did not rise sequentially.

 

MegaFUNalo opex to decline

The company said that despite the full launch of MegaFUNalo in 3Q26, FY26 online gaming opex should be lower than the P2bn spent in FY25.

 

Jeju casino operations sold

BLOOM sold its Jeju casino operations (gaming license and equipment) to a company called Blue One for KRW10bn (c. P400mn). Bloomberry will continue to own the property and it will receive fixed rental payments for the gaming floor while lowering costs. Thus, losses from Jeju operations should narrow this year.

 

Management downbeat due to conflict in Iran

Management seemed more pessimistic than in previous periods, largely due to the conflict in Iran. The spike in fuel prices domestically should lead to higher inflation and weaker consumer confidence. Furthermore, the corresponding increases in airfares will likely lead to a decline in foreign visitors.

Volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port up 18% in February, +11% in 2M26

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that throughput at Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) jumped 18% YoY to 138k TEUs in February. This brings 2M26 volumes to 280k TEUs, up 11% YoY. Note that CMSA is expanding capacity from 1.7mn TEUs to 2mn TEUs (~167k TEUs per month) by 3Q26.

Metro Pacific FY25 core profit up 15% to P27bn

Metro Pacific Investments (MPIC) announced its FY25 core net income rose 15% to P27.1bn while headline profit rose 5% due to a one-off gain in 2024. By segment, the power unit contributed P22.1bn (+12%), water unit at P7.2bn (+16%), and tollroad unit at P6.1bn (-3%). At the parent level, MPIC’s net debt was reduced to P52.5bn in end-2025 from P61.5bn, with gross cash at P7.9bn. Derived 4Q25 core profit rose 24% YoY (-59% QoQ) to P3.5bn. MPIC is a 19.6%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounted for 18% of GTCAP’s 9M25 profits.

RLC FY25 recurring profit rises 9%

Robinsons Land’s FY25 recurring profit grew 9% to P13.5bn. 4Q25 mall revenue rose 7% YoY as occupancy increased 1ppt YoY (flat QoQ) to 94%. In FY25, mall revenue grew 10%. We estimate that same-mall revenue growth was 7% for the full year. RLC’s 4Q25 mall EBIT margin also expanded 2.6ppt YoY to 40.5%. For the full year, it rose 1.6ppt to 41.5%. Reservation sales of JV projects fell 52% QoQ and 63% YoY to P820mn, about 15% of the 2019 average. Reservation sales of standalone projects declined 73% YoY to P1.1bn. While this is a 30% QoQ improvement, it only represents 22% of the 2019 average. 4Q25 office occupancy declined 3ppt QoQ to 85%, due to the completion of 104k sqm of new office space, which expanded the portfolio by 13%. Excluding the newly completed buildings, occupancy rose 2ppt QoQ to 90%.

Aboitiz Power: Lower spot exposure, ancillary revenues lift 4Q25

Aboitiz Power recorded a net loss of P3.9bn in 4Q, after it booked a P13.5bn impairment of goodwill from the GNPower Mariveles (GMEC) acquisition in 2016. As a result, FY25 net income fell to P19.4bn from P34bn a year earlier. While the impairment charge was a result of reduced long-term price assumptions given the expiry of GMEC’s PSAs in 2029, management confirmed that there’s still P26bn of GMEC-related goodwill on its books today. Core profit improved 54% YoY (-2% QoQ) to P10bn while beneficial EBITDA rose 35% YoY to P23.3bn, supported by (a) smaller exposure to spot prices at ~10% and (b) P3.5bn of catch-up revenues from ASPA and FIT recoveries. Despite steady demand of +4%, the distribution segment recorded lower QoQ/YoY contributions in 4Q. AP’s net debt stood at P267bn in end-2025, with net gearing climbing to 1.3x from 0.8x in end-FY24, following acquisitions of LNG PH and CBK. Net debt-to-EBITDA stood at 3.4x, close to the company’s internal target of 4x. For 2026, capex (100% ownership) is projected at P62bn, down from P118bn in the previous year.

Megaworld sells MREIT shares

Megaworld sold 70mn at P13.50/share (total proceeds of P945mn). This represents a 4% discount to the last closing price (of P14.06/share). MREIT previously announced that it would acquire office assets from its sponsor, Megaworld, in a share-for-property swap. MREIT will issue 997mn new shares to Megaworld in exchange for 165,000sqm of offices in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio. The shares will be issued at P16.27/share. This represents a 16% premium to the last closing price. MREIT’s portfolio will grow by 34% 645,000sqm. MREIT’s shares outstanding will expand by 27%.

First Gen earmarks P41.7bn for capex in 2026

First Gen plans to spend P41.7bn on capex this year, higher than last year’s P33.5bn. According to FGEN President and COO Francis Giles Puno at the sidelines of subsidiary, Energy Development Corp.’s, 50th anniversary. Bulk of the capex will be for the completion of Prime Infrastructure’s 2,000 MW Ahunan pumped storage hydro project, which is targeted for completion bet. 2029-2030. Recall that FGEN is buying 40% of the Ahunan project for P75bn. Company is also spending for EDC’s drilling programs and plant upgrades.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP Governor guides for possible rate hike if oil prices continue to surge

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona in an interview guided that the central bank could raise interest rates if global oil prices hit US$100/barrel and the USD continues its rally. As of March 6 (Friday), Dubai crude prices are at US$99.1/bbl, up 45% from US$68.4/bbl end-Feb. The USD-PHP rate is at P58.47 from P57.55 end-Feb. He stated that surging oil prices and a strong dollar could push inflation beyond the central banks' (2-4%) target range. Remolona also cited remittance risk given that cash remittances from the Middle East amounted to US$6.5bn or 18% of total inflows in 2025. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 3.6%. The second out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23.

House approves fuel excise tax adjustment on second reading; DOTr announces P5,000 fuel subsidy to PUV drivers

The House of Representatives approved on second reading the measure authorizing President Marcos, until Dec 31, 2028, to suspend or reduce the excise tax on fuel products, while the counterpart measure in the Senate is up for plenary approval. Senator Pia Cayetano stated that a consolidated bill is eyed to be discussed by Monday next week. Under the draft bill, the President may adjust the fuel excise tax only if one of the two conditions are present: (a) Dubai crude oil price reaches or exceeds US$80/bbl for one month immediately before the order to suspend or reduce is issued, or (b) any order to adjust excise taxes due to emergencies or calamities must be certified by the Energy secretary to confirm that pump prices surged "extraordinarily". The measure sets a duration limit of up to six months unless Congress extends or terminates earlier through a joint resolution. It also requires the adjustment to be lifted if extraordinary conditions no longer exist and automatic reinstatement of the excise tax after the period ends without need for further gov't action. Dubai crude prices as of Mar 11 are at US$113.5/bbl, up 66% from US$68.4/bbl end-Feb. The Department of Transportation (DOTr) announced that the gov't will start distributing a P5,000 fuel subsidy to public utility drivers (PUV) in Metro Manila starting March 17. Toll operators were also said to provide discounts to buses and trucks to reduce costs and offset the impact of higher fuel prices. DOTr Secretary Giovanni Lopez, however, guided that there will be a fare adjustment that will impact commuters but it is currently under evaluation.

Luzon power demand declines 0.6% YoY in February; Spot prices also down 1% YoY

Spot prices (LWAP) in Luzon fell by 0.8% YoY (-17% MoM) to P2.69/kwh in February, as demand declined by 0.6% YoY (+5% MoM). Average supply also declined by 1.3% YoY (+8% MoM) to 14,286 MW, according to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP).

PH dollar reserves at $112.7bn end-Feb

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Feb inched up to US$112.7bn from US$112.6bn the previous month. The continued surge in gold prices was offset by a decline in foreign investments, leading to the flat MoM growth in the GIR. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 4.2x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

Government bond yields rise

The Philippine government sold 7-year bonds at a yield of 6.473%, 61.4 basis points higher than the February 10 auction. Awards totaled P9.45bn vs P20bn; tenders reached P38.5bn. The 7-year BVAL hit 6.5563%, from 6.2689% the prior day.

Commercial banks’ loan growth slows to 9.3% in Jan

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.3% in Jan from 9.6% in Dec, the second month of single-digit growth after a previous 19-month streak of double-digit growth. Loans to productive activities moderated to 8.2% vs 8.4% in Dec. Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 21.3%, a tad slower than Dec's 21.5% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated to 8.6% in Jan from 7.2% in Dec.

Asset quality mixed in Jan, but PD ratios up both MoM and YoY

Banks’ NPL ratio stood at 3.50% in January, up 19bps MoM but down 7bps YoY. In nominal terms, NPLs totaled P24.7bn, rising 4.7% MoM and 7.4% YoY. The Past Due (PD) ratio was 4.52%, up 27bps MoM and 11bps YoY, while the peso value of PDs increased 5.6% MoM and 12.4% YoY. Restructured loans (RLs) were at 2.14%, up 2bps MoM and down 3bps YoY. NPL cover stood at 94.2%, down from 97.2% in December and 95.3% in January 2025. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) slowed to 9.73% YoY in January from 9.88% in December 2025. The loan-to-deposit ratio (LDR) increased to 73.3% from 72.6% at end-2025.

Department of Energy says no change in coal mining royalty structure

The Department of Energy will maintain the royalty structure for coal miners, even as the coal operating contract covering 10 blocks on Semirara Island is set for re-bidding, according to Energy Undersecretary Alessandro Sales. He cited that the royalty structure is legally defined in the Coal Development Act of 1976. Submission of bids is scheduled on April 28.
