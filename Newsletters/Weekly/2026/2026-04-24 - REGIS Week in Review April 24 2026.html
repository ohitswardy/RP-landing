REGIS REPORT

24 April 2026

Philippine shares sold off for a second straight week, taking the PCOMP down another 0.9% or 55.64 pts WoW to 5,943.49 amid persistent foreign outflows and tightening conditions in the local economy. Oil prices steadily rose throughout the week, gaining over 15% in all amid the lack of progress in peace talks between the US and Iran which has kept the Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed as the conflict prolongs.

 

With the weakening local macro backdrop, the BSP delivered a 25-bp rate hike to 4.50% at its monetary board meeting this week, citing the need to anchor inflation expectations as higher oil and fertilizer prices begin to feed through domestic fuel and food prices. This marked the first BSP rate hike since October 2023. Moreover, the central bank raised its average inflation forecast for 2026 to 6.3% (from 5.1% prev.), which suggests they expect inflation to average 7.5% for the rest of the year (from 2.8% as of 1Q26).

 

Initial cracks on the corporate side are also beginning to reflect the early impact of the energy crisis. ALI (-8.1% WoW) was the biggest decliner on the index this week after the company announced it was pausing sales of a luxury condominium project in Makati City, as well as outrightly canceling a mid-income high-rise project in Quezon City, citing rising cost pressures due to the Iran war.

 

Big banks were also weak and among the larger laggards in the index. BPI (-7.2% WoW) fell after 1Q26 results showed provisions are starting to bake in potential asset quality deterioration from slower economic conditions due to the Middle East conflict. BDO's (-3.8% WoW) 1Q26 results release also revealed provisions dragged profits, as the bank is pre-emptively building up reserves in response to the geopolitical uncertainty.

 

Meanwhile, defensive counters in Power & Utilities and Telcos along with yield plays bucked the market weakness, among them CNVRG (+4.6% WoW), PGOLD (+2.2% WoW), SCC (+1.7% WoW),and MER (+1.4% WoW).

 

The peso fell 1.1% WoW to 60.729:$1, nearing historical low levels last seen at end-March, while the PH10Y yield continued to inch up, +13.5bps WoW to 6.7956%.

Philippine Strategy - BSP’s 25bp hike – signals double-digit inflation to come
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Policy rate hike - a signal, not a fix

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) upped its policy rate by 25bps to 4.50%, its first hike since August 2024. While the move was not unexpected, the Street was evenly split on the timing. The bond market, though, has priced in higher rates since March (Fig 1). We had also recently noted that the 4.1% jump in March CPI was driven not only by oil, but also by rice prices, and that the flow-through into broader inflation would likely force the BSP’s hand. We expect the BSP will not stop at one hike. That said, these will likely be more for signaling; in its off-cycle March meeting, Governor Remolona acknowledged monetary policy’s "limited effectiveness" against supply shocks.

 

New forecast implies looming double-digit inflation

Speaking of signaling, the BSP now projects inflation to average 6.3% for FY2026E, up sharply from its March forecast of 5.1%. Given 1Q26’s average of just 2.8%, the BSP’s new forecasts imply an average of 7.5% for the rest of the year, with some months likely hitting double digits for the first time since 2008.

As we reported, rice prices are already up 10% YoY (Fig 4) due to government policies from 2H25, and do not yet reflect rising costs of fertilizer and transportation. Export prices out of Vietnam and Thailand have been soft all year, but have begun to creep up in the last week or so (Fig 5). With real interest rates likely to go negative again (Fig 6), the rate hike was also likely meant to stem the PHP’s depreciation (Fig 7), which would have its own feedback loop into inflation.

 

Few winners, broad losses

The monetary tightening will not help the already-fragile economy. On paper, banks could see some modest NIM tailwind from higher benchmark rates. However, higher borrowing costs could also lead to added stress on borrowers. Consumers, already reeling from higher fuel, electricity and food prices, could feel the additional squeeze. Property demand will likely soften further from higher mortgage rates. Even REITs and other dividend plays could be under pressure as yield spreads compress.

 
ICTSI - The relentless pursuit of growth
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Notes from ICTSI’s Capital Markets Day

ICTSI held its inaugural Capital Markets Day last April 16-17. The event provided investors and sell-side analysts with a comprehensive overview of its assets, organizational structure, investment strategy, and long-term growth drivers. We left the event with an even stronger impression of the business and increased confidence in its medium-to long-term outlook. We maintain a Hold rating on valuation grounds (12x 26E EV/EBITDA, 20x PER).

 

No signs of demand weakness yet (ex-Iraq) from Iran war

The conflict in the Middle East has effectively eroded most volumes at its Iraq port in April, following a strong 1Q26. On a more positive note, its Manila and Subic ports, which we visited, continue to show resilience, with volumes in April so far sustaining the momentum seen in 1Q26. That said, uncertainty persists around fuel availability in the coming months, which could disproportionately affect its operations in Asia (~40% of group revenues), given the region’s reliance on the gulf’s oil supply. Near-term, high oil prices have affected margins, which the company has mitigated through tariff increases at select ports, and in some cases, concession fee adjustments. Admittedly, implementing off-cycle tariff adjustments at the Manila ports have proven more challenging, with a proposal to collect fuel surcharges yet to be approved.

Sees Durban turnaround in <3 years

Since assuming operations in Durban, South Africa in January, management has expressed confidence in an operational turnaround and meaningful tariff hikes over a reasonable three-year horizon. While throughput has improved to around 150k TEUs per month from ~140k TEUs a year ago, reflecting early productivity gains, shifting the workforce culture toward continuous efficiency and profitability improvements remains a work in progress. Meanwhile, concerns over road infrastructure bottlenecks appear overstated, with management instead focused on enhancing port-side flow and operational efficiency.

 

Organic expansions to drive future growth

ICTSI is undertaking capacity expansions across several ports (e.g. Mexico, Manila, Batangas), which are expected to add over 3mn TEUs over the next three years from ~24mn TEUs today. These projects are focused on markets where capacity is currently constrained and/or where long-term growth prospects remain compelling. For instance, upgrades at its port in Ecuador, a highly competitive market, will position the company to benefit from the expected improvements in Asia-Americas trade flows, supported by the port’s free trade zone status. Its port in Mexico, estimated to be the largest revenue contributor in the portfolio, benefits from land availability and direct rail connectivity, positioning it well to capture incremental demand growth in the port of Manzanillo, Mexico’s largest and most important port.

 
BPI - Bracing for a bumpy (consumer) ride; forecasts, TP trimmed
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Staying committed to consumer push, despite near-term stress

BPI’s 1Q26 results were up just 1.7% YoY. While PPOP was up 12% YoY, provision expense started baking in potential stresses from higher inflation and slower GDP due to the Iran conflict. Management remains committed to its consumer growth strategy, though it is tightening underwriting and collection standards near-term. We cut our 2026/27E forecasts by 6-8% on slower loan growth, and higher credit costs, and reduce our target price by 6.3% to P150/sh. We maintain our Buy on valuations – at 7.8x PER, 1.0x P/bk and 5.4% dividend yield on our 2026E – but are mindful of risks to earnings and to investor sentiment.

 

NPLs pick up in 1Q25, though not (yet) from the consumer book

Loans grew 13.5% in 1Q25, driving revenues up 14% YoY, and PPOP up 12% YoY. NPL ratio popped 24bps QoQ to 2.42%, and Cover dropped to 87% due to lumpy corporate defaults, though foreclosures/recoveries could bring both ratios back into line in 2Q. While the consumer/MSME books were not yet a problem in 1Q, the segment is showing early signs of delinquency. The bank bumped up its credit cost to 87bps, anticipating increased stress on households from the war in Iran. We have increased our own FY26E credit cost forecast to 100bps.

Staying committed to consumers, but taking cautious stance near-term

Consumer/MSME loans still surge 25% YoY in 1Q. Management remains committed to growing its loan, deposit, and payments customer base through digital and third-party agency channels. In the short-term, the bank is tightening underwriting standards, and intensifying collection efforts to manage risks. It is also studying BSP rules allowing deferrals of NPL recognition.

 

Forecasts cut 6-8%, and TP cut to P150/sh; Buy kept with stock at 1.0x P/bk

We reduce our FY26/27E forecasts by 8.5% and 6.4% respectively on slower loan growth, and higher provision expense. We also cut target price to 150/sh (was P160), based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g), with long-term ROE of 14.25% (was 14.5%), COE of 12.0% and growth of 8.0%. We maintain our Buy call on valuations, though, with the stock down to 1.0x P/bk and 5.4% dividend yield on our FY26E forecasts. Key downside risks include deteriorating local and global macro conditions relating to the ongoing US-Israel-Iran war.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Ayala Land pauses Laurean Residences project, cancels The Heights

Ayala Land said it was pausing sales of Laurean Residences, an Ayala Land Premier project in Makati City. It cited rising cost pressures and uncertain delivery timelines due to the Iran conflict. “We have reached out to buyers directly to discuss next steps and the range of options available to them, with the care and attention they expect from ALI,” ALI said. The company also canceled The Heights, an Avida (middle-income) project along Katipunan Avenue, Quezon City. The Heights was only launched in 1Q26.

Ayala Land to prioritize stability over aggressive growth

During the company’s stockholders’ meeting, Ayala Land said that the company would prioritize stability over aggressive growth. The company’s strategy includes: Expanding the leasing portfolio. Scaling down capital expenditures. Managing residential launches and reducing inventory. The following plans were already conveyed during Ayala Land’s 4Q25 earnings briefing (in February): After lowering residential launches to P46.6bn (down 42%) in 2025, ALI will reduce this further to P30bn in 2026. Capex will be lowered by c. 20% but leasing capex will remain flat. The company intends to increase the leasing segment’s share of EBITDA to 50% by 2027, from 45% in 2025.

Energy regulator shortens Meralco’s remaining P14bn refund period to 12 months

The Energy Regulatory Commission ordered Meralco to shorten the period of the remaining P14.2bn unrefunded amount to 12 months from 36 months previously. As a result, the average refund rate will increase to P0.25/kwh starting in May from P0.12/kwh previously. This is to mitigate the increase in electricity rates due to higher spot prices.

Water regulator slaps P54mn penalty on Maynilad

MWSS Regualtory Office slapped a P54mn penalty on Maynilad for water service interruptions last May 9-31 on 165,518 accounts. Company cited the Amihan season caused reductions in production levels of its Putatan water treatment facility, which serves the south of Metro Manila. Note that this is the second time this year that the regulator imposed a penalty on Maynilad for a similar issue. In early March, the regulator imposed a P43mn penalty. These penalties are non-recoverable costs.

JAZA retires as chair of Globe Telecom; Consing named successor

Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala (JAZA) has stepped down as chairman and director of Globe Telecom, Inc. (GLO PM) with Ayala Corp. (AC PM) President and CEO Cezar Consing named as his successor. JAZA has served as chairman for Globe since December 1996 and director since March 1989. Mr. Consing has been president and CEO of Ayala Corp. since 2022 and was the president and CEO of Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI PM) from 2013-2021.

Chinabank 1Q26 net profit rises 4%

Chinabank’s 1Q26 net profit rose 4% to P6.8bn. Net interest income grew 14% to P19.5bn, supported by higher revenue and lower interest expense. Net interest margin improved 12bps to 4.61%. Opex increased 5% to P8.8bn. Gross loans climbed 16% to P1.1trn. The bank saw strong demand across both corporate and consumer segments. Deposits increased 13% to P1.5trn. CASA grew 20%, bringing the CASA ratio to 48%, from 46% a year ago. The bank said that asset quality remained steady, with NPL ratio at 1.6% and NPL coverage at 110%.

Alliance Global 4Q25 net profit falls 20% YoY

Alliance Global said that its FY25 “normalized attributable net profit” (which excludes gains from the deconsolidation of GADC) was steady at P17.3bn. Headline profit grew 19% to P20.7bn. Implied 4Q25 attributable net profit fell 20% YoY to P3.4bn, largely due to losses at Emperador. Megaworld’s 4Q25 net profit rose 2% YoY (-3% QoQ) to P5.1bn. 4Q25 operating profit fell 12% YoY (down 19% QoQ) to P6.0bn, due to a 12% YoY (and QoQ) drop in residential revenue. Travellers’ 4Q25 EBITDA fell 13% YoY (up 4% QoQ) to P2.6bn, as higher opex offset the increase in revenue. 4Q25 GGR rose 5% YoY (flat QoQ) to P8.0bn, as strong mass win rate compensated for the the decline in VIP rolling chip. Emperador's 4Q25 profit fell 153% YoY (211% QoQ) to -P813mn, primarily due to a significant increase in opex in the brandy business alongside continued YoY declines in revenue for both the brandy and whisky segments. Both segments saw sales decline YoY but improve QoQ in 4Q as brandy revenues fell 17% YoY to P9.5bn (+21% QoQ) while whisky top line fell 10% YoY to P6.4bn (+23% QoQ).

Nickel Asia acquires 20% stake in Kazakhstan copper mine

Nickel Asia entered into an agreement with Silk Road Resource Ltd. to acquire the latter’s 20% “membership” stake in East Copper. East Copper owns 100% of GRK MLD LLP (GRK), which holds subsoil use rights for the Karchiga copper mine located in Kazakhstan. This transaction is subject to the completion of the due diligence on East Copper and GRK and other closing conditions, including the necessary regulatory approvals. GRK’s annual production capacity is 8.5kt of copper sulfide concentrate at an average grade of 1.8% and 2kt of copper cathode at an average grade of 1.0%. For perspective, the Philippines produced 232kt of refined copper in 2025 while Kazakhstan produced 471kt (see here). Acquisition is in line with the company’s strategy “to expand market capitalization and earnings beyond nickel into a diversified natural resources platform”.

MREIT 1Q26 distributable income rises 34% YoY

MREIT’s 1Q26 distributable income rose 34% YoY to P1.25bn. MREIT acquired 165,000sqm of offices in Fort Bonifacio, which expanded its portfolio by 34%. Although the SEC approval for the share-for-property was only granted in March, the transaction was backdated so that the buildings began contributing to MREIT on January 1. MREIT’s shares outstanding expanded by 27%.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP delivers 25bp rate hike, raises inflation forecasts above 4% ceiling

The Monetary Board raised its policy rate by 25bps to 4.5%, in line with Street expectations. This marked the first rate hike since October 2023. BSP Governor Remolona said the decision was not unanimous, as the board considered a 50bp hike but opted for a more reasonable smaller hike. Move was done to anchor inflation expectations amid the Middle East conflict, with higher oil and fertilizer prices beginning to feed through domestic fuel and food prices. Core inflation has continued to rise, pointing to a broadening of underlying price pressures. BSP raised its average headline inflation rate forecast to 6.3% for 2026 (vs. 5.1% previously) and 4.3% for 2027 (vs. 3.8% in 2027), breaching its 4% ceiling. March CPI stood at 4.1%, bringing 1Q26 average inflation rate to 2.8%. The BSP’s revised 2026 forecast suggest that inflation could average 9.8% for the rest of the year.

Fitch downgrades PH outlook to "negative" from "stable" but affirms credit rating

Credit ratings agency Fitch downgraded its outlook for the Philippines to "negative" from "stable" but affirmed the country's "BBB" investment-grade rating. Fitch cited the country's high exposure to the global energy shock as well as disrupted public investment due to the corruption scandal. Political friction was also noted as a risk to the country's macro outlook. The credit rating agency highlighted that these risks currently outweigh favorable medium-term growth prospects. Note that a "negative" outlook implies the possibility of a future credit rating downgrade if underlying risks continue or worsen in one to two years.

U.S. and the Philippines plan to launch 1,600-hectare economic security zone

United States Undersecretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg announced plans to establish a 4,000-acre (~1,600-hectare) industrial hub in the Luzon Economic Corridor of the Philippines in order "to secure vital inputs to American and global supply chain". The U.S. cited the Philippines' significant reserves of nickel, copper, chromite, and cobalt. The "AI-native investment acceleration hub" is still conceptual with details such as which American companies will participate and what will be built in the Philippines have not been determined yet as of late. The Trump administration will solicit companies for proposals to build in the hub with priority to bids "that will help move critical minerals processing and manufacturing off Chinese suppliers". The US will occupy the rent-free and will administer it as a special economic zone. The hub will have diplomatic-style immunity similar to a U.S. embassy and operate under U.S. common law with a two-year lease renewable for up to 99 years. The agreement is part of the U.S.' Pax Silica initiative, with the Philippines joining as the 13th signatory, as the U.S. aims to reduce dependence on China-linked materials used for semiconductors, AI technology, and critical minerals.

Government budget deficit mildly up in March on energy crisis support; 1Q26 deficit down 20% to P356bn

The government’s budget deficit increased by 2% YoY to P350bn in March. Revenues rose 9% YoY to P305bn, supported by the +46% YoY increase in non-tax revenues due to the early remittance of GOCC dividends. BIR collections rose 6.5% YoY in March, up from the 3% pace in 2M26, but still below the full-year growth target of +10%. Customs collections grew 5.5% YoY in March, accelerating from the 2% pace in 2M26, but also below target (+7.5%). Expenditures rose 5.2% YoY in March, as primary spending rose 4.5% YoY, reversing the mild 1% contraction in 2M26. Primary spending in March was partly supported by the P20bn Emergency Energy program (3% of expenditures), aimed at augmenting the country’s fuel supply. Brings 1Q26 budget deficit to P355.5bn, down 20% YoY and broadly in line with target as weaker revenues were offset by subdued spending.

BOP posts US$2.6bn deficit in March; GIR settles at US$106.6bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of March posted a deficit of US$2.6bn. Wider than both the US$2.3bn deficit in Feb and the US$2.0bn deficit in March 2025. This brings the 3M26 deficit to US$5.3bn. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP). Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Mar stood at US$106.6bn, flat YoY and down from the US$113.3bn in the previous month of Feb. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.0 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

Dept. of Energy to limit fuel price adjustments

Dept. of Energy (DOE) Secretary Sharon Garin stated that oil retailers must adjust prices in line with the range provided by the DOE every week amid the state of national energy emergency. Garin cited that President Marcos' declaration of a "state of national energy emergency" through Executive Order (EO) 110 granted the gov't control over setting the minimum rollback and maximum price increases for fuel price adjustments. Garin noted that the DOE decided to closely monitor the price adjustments given public clamor on quick fuel price increases but slow price rollbacks despite lower global oil prices.

Dept. of Agriculture expands P20/kg rice program

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) expanded its P20/kg rice program for this year as it targets to open 900 additional selling sites nationwide for vulnerable communities in need of affordable rice. The expansion would add to the 932 sites already currently selling P20 rice across the country. Food Terminal Inc. (FTI) has signed an agreement with the National Food Authority (NFA) to secure funding and supply commitments for 2026. P3bn will be allocated for the deal to procure 1.8mn 50kg bags of well-milled rice. Funding will be sourced from the DA's P10bn Rice-for-All program budget. FTI President and CEO Joseph Lo emphasized on the "significant increase" in buyers of P20/kg rice given the unstable macroeconomic environment.

Manufacturers agree with DTI to extend price freeze till May 10

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that the ongoing price freeze of basic and prime commodities will be extended to May 10 from April 30 previously following commitment from manufacturers. This is the second extension after the price halt was initially extended from April 16 to end-April. Goods covered by the price freeze include canned sardines and meat, coffee, processed milk, instant noodles, bread, among others. Trade Secretary Ma. Cristina Roque stated that the decline in fuel prices over the past week enabled the DTI to secure another extension.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 10% YoY in March; Toyota sales down 6.8% YoY

Data from CAMPI showed that vehicle sales fell 10.4% YoY (+0.7% MoM) to 36,104 units in March. Brings 1Q26 sales to 105,642 units, down 9.8% YoY. Sales of electric vehicles (ex-BYD) surged to 6,148 units in March (~17% of total car sales), up >3x YoY and 2x MoM due to the rise in fuel prices. Brings 1Q26 EV sales up 36% YoY to 11,800 units (HEVs: +10% YoY to 8261 units / BEVs: +123% YoY to 2,289 units / PHEVs: +925% YoY to 1,250 units). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,622 units in March, down 6.8% YoY and 1.85% MoM. Brings TMP’s 1Q26 unit sales to 51,922 units, down 6.5% YoY TMP is targeting unit sales to grow “up to 5%” (~229k-240k units) this year.

Restaurants see lower sales

David Sison, President of the Restaurant Association of the Philippines (RestoPH), said that the association’s members have seen lower foot traffic and a drop in sales since the beginning of the Iran conflict. RestoPH has 230 member companies with a combined store count of about 1,500. In a RestoPH survey: 6% said that they experienced a sales decline of over 30%. 79% said that their sales fell between 10%-30%. 12% reported that their sales dropped within 10%. 3% did not experience a decline in sales.
