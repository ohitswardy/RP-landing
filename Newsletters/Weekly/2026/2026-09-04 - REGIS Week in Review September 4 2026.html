REGIS REPORT

04 September 2026

The PCOMP staged a strong rebound this week, up 2.3% or 134.27 pts WoW to 6,090.60, finishing above the 6,000 level after last week's sharp sell-off. The recovery was initially supported by residual MSCI rebalancing flows, with upweighted BPI (+5.4% WoW) and BDO (+4.2% WoW) among the strongest large-cap performers, while ALI (-1.7% WoW) remained under pressure following its deletion from the MSCI Standard Index.

 

Still, trading activity slowed, with turnover falling to just $55m on Friday, the lowest since May, as sentiment remained shaky amid a weak peso which hit fresh historic lows this week, as well as elevated global oil prices.

 

On a positive note, Philippine inflation eased slightly to 6.1% YoY in August from 6.2% in July, which was within expectations. Inflation averaged 5.2% in the first eight months of the year, with the slower increase in food and non-alcoholic beverage prices driving CPI lower for the fourth straight month. Recall though that the BSP raised its policy rate by 25bps to 5.00% last week, while lowering its 2026 inflation forecast to 6.1% but substantially raising its 2027 forecast to 5.4%, keeping inflation risks firmly in focus.

 

Miners rallied for another week as gold prices rose, with APX (+6.0% WoW) among the biggest gainers. SCC (+2.6% WoW) also recovered lifted by bargain hunting, while parent DMC (+3.0% WoW) rose alongside the stock.

 

Meanwhile, ICT (+5.6% WoW) was the top gainer on the index, rebounding following last week's sharp sell-down as profit-taking pressure eased. The stock was further supported by news of its acquisition of TLG, an integrated logistics company with port, warehousing and digital transport operations across South Africa, Mozambique, Namibia, Zambia and Tanzania.

 

Consumer stocks lagged as elevated oil prices continued to raise concerns over higher input costs. Certain names were further pressured by the Department of Finance's proposal to raise excise taxes on non-essential goods. Given the proposed higher taxes on sweetened beverages, URC (-3.7% WoW) was among the largest decliners on the index this week.

 

Foreigners remained net sellers for the fifth straight week, by $22m. The peso fell another 0.6% WoW to a new all-time-low of 62.603:$1. The PH10Y yield inched up by 3.1bps to 7.2885%.

Apex Mining - Going for gold; Initiate with a Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Scalable gold play at a bargain

We initiate coverage on Apex Mining (APX) with a BUY rating and a target price of P19/sh. A well-established gold producer with a solid balance sheet, APX has a robust project pipeline that could double annual gold output to 200k oz by 2030 and unlock economies of scale. The recent pullback in gold prices has driven APX's share price to fall by 13% below its peak. At current levels, APX trades at only 10x PER for FY26E sliding to only 6x in FY27E with a forecast jump in output. This is a significant discount to both domestic (28x/14x) and international peers (16x/12x).

 

Not a one-trick pony; Five-year forecast EPS CAGR of 23%

APX has evolved from a single-mine operator to a multi-tenement miner. We forecast APX to deliver 23% EPS CAGR over 2025–2030, driven by higher output and premised on gold prices averaging US$4,595/oz this year (vs. 1H actual of US$4,700) and peaking at US$5,000/oz in 2027 (vs. spot at US$4,400). We forecast a strong rebound in gold sales volumes to 200k oz by 2030 following an expected 14% YoY decline to 87k oz this year, as the company completes the drain tunnel at Maco by May 2027 and the Sangilo mill expansion by 2029.

At the current gold spot price of US$4,400, APX is still undervalued at a 2027E P/E of 7x with a div yield of 5%.

 

Asset base under-appreciated

Leveraging the proximity of its existing Maco mine and Amacan prospect, APX also offers, in our view, a brownfield expansion story with low execution risk. We believe the company’s current reserve estimates understate the potential of its asset base. The expected release of reserve estimates for Sangilo and Amacan by early 2027, in our view, should pave the way for their development and serve as potential share price catalysts.

 

TP of P19/sh offers 23% total return, excluding Amacan optionality

Our TP of P19/sh is derived by valuing operating mines using DCF analysis, applying a WACC of 10%. While our base case TP does not incorporate the Amacan project, we estimate this could add at least P3/sh (+18%) to our valuation assuming an IRR of 12%. Key downside risks are commodity prices, as well as regulatory and execution risks.

 
ICTSI - Expands African footprint with logistics firm acquisition
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Acquires 100% of integrated logistics company in Africa

ICTSI announced that it signed an agreement to acquire 100% of TLG Acquisition Hldgs (RF) Proprietary Ltd (“TLG”), an integrated logistics company providing port services, warehousing, and digital transport logistics across South Africa, Mozambique, Namibia, Zambia & Tanzania. The sellers, private equity firms African Infrastructure Investment Managers (AIIM) and Mokobela-Shataki Consortium, hold 74% and 26% in TLG, respectively. The transaction value was not disclosed, and closing is subject to regulatory approvals and other conditions precedent.

 

TLG background

Established in 2019, TLG initially operated private ports focused on fruit and mineral exports across Cape Town, Gqeberha and Durban, South Africa.

AIIM and its consortium partners acquired the company for ZAR1.6bn (~US$100mn) in 2022, after which TLG expanded beyond bulk port operations into a diversified, integrated logistics platform present in 14 locations across seven (7) African logistics corridors (see company website here). Majority of its ports handle agricultural and mineral bulk products, with some minimal container handling (see Figure 1 in page 2).

 

Investment rationale?

ICTSI has historically focused on container port operations so this venture into bulk cargo handling is a departure from its usual path. The purchase does significantly expand its geographic footprint in Africa, and it is likely that ICTSI intends to use the facilities of TLG as feeder ports to help accelerate the turnaround of its operations in Durban. We await further details from management.

 
Philippine Property - 2Q26 recap: residential segment improves; leasing segments decelerate
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential segment improves across multiple metrics

The residential segment saw modest improvements across a variety of metrics in 2Q26. Residential reservation sales, residential revenue, and unsold inventory all improved sequentially during the quarter. On the other hand, net office take-up declined QoQ while same-mall revenue growth decelerated.

 

Residential reservation sales rise for second consecutive quarter

Aggregate residential reservation sales of the top four developers rose sequentially for the second quarter, rising 4% QoQ (down 4% YoY) to P69.0bn, thanks to stronger take-up of some provincial projects. While this is arguably a silver lining, we still consider the residential market weak (particularly in Metro Manila), as the reservation sales figure only represents 68% of the 2019 average.

 

Residential revenue also grows sequentially

After falling 29% QoQ (and 16% YoY) in 1Q26, aggregate residential revenue of the top four developers grew 12% QoQ (down 7% YoY) to P47.3bn, around 95% of the 2019 average. This could indicate more tempered cancellations in 2Q26.

 

Unsold inventory value declines

The aggregate value of unsold residential inventory fell 3% QoQ and 9% YTD (to P474bn).

However, the unsold inventory levels of some developers remain high, at over four years of reservation sales (based on sales over the past 12 months).

 

Office take-up declines; Makati CBD and Bonifacio perform better

BPO firms took less incremental space QoQ and YoY while Metro Manila office vacancy remained flat at 18% (see Figures 5 to 8). Similar to previous periods, most of the transactions were recorded in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio, where vacancy rates are much lower (at 9%-11%) than other areas.

 

Spot rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ but remain 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Lease contracts in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio were generally renewed at flat to higher rental rates while most contracts in other areas were renewed at flat to lower rental rates.

 

Same-mall revenue growth decelerates

Excluding the impact of Ayala Land’s divestment of a mall (in 4Q25), we estimate that same-mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators decelerated to 5% YoY (from 7% in 1Q26), due to the conflict in the Middle East.

 
Philippine Gaming - 2Q26 recap: GGR of land-based casinos inches up; online GGR remains flat
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

GGR of on-premise casinos improves; online GGR remains flat

Revenue of land-based casinos inched up QoQ and YoY, thanks to an increase of both VIP and mass GGR. On the other hand, revenue of the e-games segment was flat QoQ and down 37% YoY, as it continues to suffer from the BSP directive delinking e-wallets from online gambling platforms (beginning mid-August 2025). Interestingly, the market leaders of the online and land-based segments saw diverging trends. The market share of Bloomberry expanded while the market share of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) contracted.

 

DigiPlus loses market share

The 2Q26 GGR of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) fell c. 10% QoQ, worse than the flattish performance of the industry. We extrapolate its market share fell by c. 4ppt to 38.5%.

 

Land-based VIP and mass market GGR both increase

The 2Q26 GGR of Metro Manila licensed casinos rose 6% QoQ and 5% YoY to P36.8bn, equivalent to 88% of the pre-pandemic (2019) average, as both the VIP and mass segments saw sequential improvements. Overall, VIP revenue remains well below pre-pandemic levels, while GGR from the mass segment is now above pre-COVID levels.

 

VIP GGR grew for the second consecutive quarter to P7.6bn (+10% QoQ and flat YoY). 

However, we still consider the VIP business to be weak, as the 2Q26 figure only represents 42% of the 2019 average. Meanwhile, mass GGR increased 5% QoQ and 6% YoY to P29.3bn, 23% higher than 2019 levels. Excluding Solaire North, which only began operations in 2Q25, 1Q26 mass GGR was still about 5% higher than 2019 levels.

 

Bloomberry expands market share

Bloomberry’s GGR market share (of Metro Manila licensed casinos) expanded 4ppt QoQ to 44.5%, as its GGR rose 12% QoQ (+15% YoY) to P16.4bn. The improvement was largely thanks to higher VIP rolling chip and mass table win rates in Solaire Entertainment City.

 

Higher Chinese tourist arrivals not resulting in higher gambling revenue

Although the visa-free policy instituted in January 2026 resulted in a 65% increase in 1H26 Chinese tourist arrivals (to 220k), the integrated resort operators said that they have not seen any meaningful increase in gaming activity from mainland Chinese players.

 

New integrated resort to open in 4Q26

Alliance Global (AGI PM) plans to open Westside Resorts Manila in 4Q26, which will add c. 20% to table and gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City. We thus anticipate that competition among land-based casinos will intensify.

Philippine inflation eases slightly to 6.1% in August

Philippine inflation eased slightly to 6.1% YoY in August from 6.2% in July, according to the Philippine Statistics Authority. Core inflation, which excludes selected food and energy items, eased to 4.1% from 4.2% in July. Prices eased mainly due to the slower annual increase in the food and non-alcoholic beverages index during August, the PSA said. Inflation averaged 5.2% in the first eight months of the year. Last week, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 5.00% to guard against inflation risks stemming from a severe El Nino and larger-than-expected wage increases. The BSP also lowered its forecast for average 2026 inflation to 6.1% from 6.4%, although it substantially raised its 2027 forecast to 5.4% from 4.5%. The central bank expects inflation to return closer to its 3% target midpoint by 2028.

Government debt rises to P19.39tn in end-July

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.7% MoM (P323.5bn) to P19.39tn as of end-July. This was due to the net availment of domestic and external debt as well as the revaluation of foreign debt following the depreciation in the peso. Domestic debt increased by 2.1% MoM (P271.3bn) to P13.11tn due to the impact of the net issuance of domestic securities. External debt was rose 0.8% MoM (P52.5bn) to P6.23tn given the P17.1bn net external loan availment and depreciation of the peso. The average USD-PHP rate depreciated from P61.25 in June to P61.60 in July. The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 66.0% as of 2Q26 higher than the 65.2% recorded in 1Q26 and the 63.2% ratio at end-2025. The 66.0% ratio is the highest since end-2004 (71.6%). The ratio is projected to ease to 64.9% by end-2026, 64.4% in 2027, and to 63% by 2030. For 2026, the total debt stock is projected to reach P19.77tn (+12% YoY) by year-end.

Trade deficit widens 35% YoY to $6.0bn in July

The July trade deficit widened to US$6.0bn (+35% YoY). The trade deficit has widened for six consecutive months YoY since the start of the Middle East conflict in Feb. This brings the 7M26 trade deficit to US$37.3bn, 29% wider YoY. Imports for the month climbed 20% YoY to US$14.1bn, steady relative to the increase in June (+20% YoY). The increase in imports was supported by strong growth in fuel (+35%) as well as raw and intermediate goods (+32%). By commodity group, imports of electronics were the primary growth driver, surging 61% YoY vs non-electronics which was up 7% YoY for the month. Exports growth moderated to 11% YoY (US$8.1bn) from 24% the previous month. This marks the nineteenth consecutive month of YoY growth in exports. Growth in electronics decelerated from +35% in June but still remained robust at +22%. These accounted for 59% of total exports in the month. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 2% a reversal from the 11% growth the month prior.

Rice imports estimated to be 5mn tons in 2026

Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that the country may need to import as much as 5 MMT (million metric tons) of rice to secure ample buffer stock given the negative impact of heavy rains and a developing El Niño drought on rice harvest. Rice imports as of Aug 13 amounted to 3.46 MMT, already higher than the 3.39 MMT imported in FY25. Majority of this year's imports were sourced from Vietnam then Thailand at 2.59 MMT (75% of total) and 0.37 MMT (11%), respectively. Tiu Laurel noted that there will be no rice import ban given the need to secure an ample supply of rice with dry conditions expected to persist until early 2027. He also seeks to propose a requirement to buy palay from local farmers in order to secure import allocations for rice. The country's palay (unmilled rice) output is projected to be 19 MMT (-3.5% YoY) for FY26. Using the PSA's official milling recovery rate of 63%, the palay output equates to approximately 12 MMT of rice. The palay output as of 1H26 amounted to 9.1 MMT (-0.6% YoY). The DA secretary also noted that average yield per hectare is rising but overall output has been dampened by the recent heavy rains.

DOF proposes higher tax on motor vehicles and luxury goods

The Dept. of Finance (DOF), through the PROGRESS bill, is contemplating on proposals for higher taxes on luxury and non-essential goods as well as an increase in the motor vehicle users charge (MVUC). Recall that the DOF is proposing measures to offset the gov't revenue losses from the recently proposed tax relief initiatives such as raising the minimum annual income subject to tax to P350,000 from the current P250,000 as well as exempting micro and smell enterprises from the minimum corporate tax. The DOF proposed to raise the motor vehicle users charge (MVUC), by doubling current rates as these have remained unchanged since 2004. The MVUC is a fee charged to vehicle owners upon registration of a new vehicle as well as annually during the renewal of vehicle registration. The excise tax on in non-essential goods is sought to be raised to 25% from 20%. The Dept. is looking to expand the list of non-essential goods covered to also include private aircraft, jets, helicopters, recreational boats (jet skis, speedboats, etc.). The law currently covers jewelry, perfumes, yachts. There are no current estimates on the revenue impact but in 2025 the gov't collected P307mn from the excise tax, 28% lower from P426mn in 2024.

Government set to remove VAT on system loss charges by November

The Bureau of Internal Revenue plans to issue a circular removing VAT on the allowable system loss charge in electricity bills by mid-September. This follows the Energy Regulatory Commission’s (ERC) ruling last Aug 28, classifying system loss as a government-mandated pass-through cost that should not form part of the VAT base. Once BIR’s circular is issued, the ERC said it would likely be implemented in November. Note that system losses account for only ~3% of end-user rates, so removing VAT on this component will only reduce rates by just ~0.36%.

DOE pushes for 5GW coal-fired plant buildout

The Department of Energy is set to issue a coal transition policy that will aid in the development of up to 5GW of coal-fired plants exempt from the existing moratorium. According to Energy Undersecretary Rowena Guevara. Developers that hold certificates of non-coverage from the December 2020 coal moratorium will be required to submit binding work plans. Failure to build according to the plan will be subject to contract termination penalties. Note that several coal projects are exempt from the moratorium, including the following. Meralco’s 1,200 MW Atimonan project. Semirara’s 700MW St. Raphael project in Calaca, Batangas.

Senate Bill to loosen regulations on condominium redevelopment

The Senate approved Senate Bill 2420 or the Condominium Redevelopment Act. Under the current law, condominium buildings can only be redeveloped with the consent of all condominium owners. Under SB 2420. Condominium buildings that are 30-50 years old can be redeveloped with the approval of 2/3 of owners. Condominium buildings that are over 50 years old can be redeveloped with the approval of majority of owners. Over time, this could lead to more condominium redevelopment activity in areas with older buildings, such as Makati, Ortigas Center, and Manila. Due to the large supply of condominium units in Metro Manila, redevelopment is likely to be limited in the near term. SB 2420 also introduces safeguards for buyers when the actual floor area of a completed unit differs from the approved floor plan. Units measuring up to 60sqm can have deviations up to 6%. Units exceeding 500sqm are allowed to have deviations of up to 2%.
