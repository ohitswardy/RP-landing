REGIS REPORT

19 June 2026

The PCOMP moved higher alongside global markets as geopolitical concerns eased amid favorable developments in Iran for much of the week. News that a breakthrough agreement between the US and Iran was close to being reached to end hostilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz sent crude oil prices down close to 10% for the week -- now down by a third QTD.

 

Despite reports coming out Friday that peace talks had been delayed anew due to clashes in Lebanon, the PCOMP managed to finish the week higher by 3.8% or 225.29 pts WoW to 6,135.35. For the week, the PH10Y yield fell sharply by 50 bps to sub-7% and the peso strengthened further, by 1.0% to 60.780:$1.

 

On domestic macro, the BSP hiked the key rate by 25bps to 4.75% as widely expected by the market. This was the second 25-bp rate hike following its last meeting in April, and BSP Gov. Remolona signaled potentially raising rates further to bring inflation back to its 3% long-term target. Recall that May inflation of 6.8%, though cooler-than-expected, still took YTD-May average inflation to 4.5%, which remains well above the BSP's 3-4% target range and with upward pressures from higher oil and food prices persisting.

 

ALI (+13.5% WoW) was among top gainers in the index this week, rebounding from sharp losses in the previous week when shares fell to levels last seen in 2010 on speculation of its deletion from the MSCI Standard Index. Amid heavy foreign outflows, bottom-fishers were further encouraged to pick up the stock after parent AC (+3.6% WoW) disclosed it purchased 35m ALI shares at an average price of P13.07/sh (or P457-m worth in total) early in the week.

 

Big banks BDO (+13.7% WoW) and BPI (+10.4% WoW) were notable outperformers with sentiment held up by rate hikes as well as steadily strong loan growth despite the generally soft macro environment. Manufacturers CNPF (+9.7% WoW) and URC (+9.1% WoW) led gainers among consumer names amid falling oil prices, while PGOLD (-8.5% WoW) succumbed to profit-taking. PLUS (+17.5% WoW) was the top index gainer for the week as bargain-hunting lifted shares from recent lows.

 

The FTSE rebalancing on Friday saw the inclusion of MYNLD (-2.0% WoW) into the Small Cap Index. The stock is widely expected to be included in the PCOMP at the next rebalancing in August.

CNPF provides update on tuna and coconut facilities post-earthquake

Century Pacific Food in a disclosure provided an update on its tuna and coconut manufacturing facilities as operations have partially resumed a week after the 7.8 magnitude earthquake hit General Santos City. CNPF assured that the facilities and inventory are covered by insurance, it also affirmed that it "maintains sufficient inventory… to continue serving customer requirements". The company expects operations to be gradually restored over the next one to two weeks after further safety assessments and repairs are conducted. As of FY25, the Branded Tuna and OEM (tuna and coconut) segments contributed to 31% and 20% of group-wide revenues, respectively.

ACEN selling up to 49% of India solar project to Dutch firm

ACEN executed a Securities Subscription and Purchase Agreement and a Shareholders’ Agreement with Diamond India Renewables One B.V. (DIRO). Agreement pertains to DIRO purchasing up to 49% of Tejorupa Renewables India Project Private Ltd., the entity that is developing a 420MWdc solar project in Rajasthan, India. Transaction is expected to close in states, with DIRO acquiring an initial 10% voting interest in Tejorupa. The valuation for this deal was not disclosed. Deal is subject to contractual and customary conditions precedent. A partnership could ease the project’s funding requirements. ACEN currently owns 100% of the Tejorupa project, which is expected to be completed in 2027. Tejorupa’s capacity is largely contracted under a 25-year fixed price offtake contract.

Cebu Air’s passenger volumes remain soft in May, despite MoM rebound

Cebu Air flew 2.4mn passengers in May, up 6% MoM but down 1% YoY. Domestic pax volumes rose 10% MoM (flat YoY) to 1.8mn while international pax volumes fell 4% MoM/YoY to 575k. Seat load factor rebounded to 78% in May from 74% in April, driven solely by improvements in domestic load factors to 80% from 74% in April. The MoM improvement in demand appears to have been driven by the company’s moves to ease substantial fare hikes implemented in April. While spot jet kero price of US$128/bbl has come off from highs, it remains well above pre-war levels of US$93/bbl.

DITO expects 20-30% revenue growth for 2026

Dito Telecommunity Corp. (Dito Tel) CEO Ernesto Alberto stated that he expects the company's revenue to grow between 20-30% to P24-P27bn from P20.5bn in 2025 driven by subscriber acquisition despite softer market conditions due to inflationary pressures. Dito Tel targets to grow its subscriber base to 20mn by year-end from the current 17mn, including users of its fixed wireless access (FWA) service. Alberto said that the company registers an average of 23,000 to 24,000 new subscribers daily and sees growing traction in its 5G user base. Alberto also elaborated that Dito Tel has a population reach of 86.4% as of late on an infrastructure backbone of 7,347 towers. He said that the ideal goal is to hit 90% but this would require an additional US$1bn to build more cellular towers. In 1Q26, DITO CME Holdings Corp (DITO PM), parent company of Dito Tel, posted a wider net loss of P6.8bn from P1.7bn a year earlier. Revenue in the quarter grew 24% YoY to P5.8bn from P4.7bn.

BSP raises key policy rate by 25bp to 4.75%; revises inflation forecasts

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.75%. This is the second 25bp rate hike in a row and in line with consensus expectations. The move was done to help anchor inflation expectations as well as to mitigate the risk of second-round effects. The BSP noted that there may be potential further hikes to bring inflation back to its 3% point long-term target. BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that global oil and fertilizer prices remain elevated and continue to put pressure on domestic fuel and food prices. He added that the upcoming El Niño could be "unusually big" and that it poses a "big risk" to inflation. However, it was noted that El Niño's potential impact has not yet been factored into the BSP's revised inflation forecast. The central bank slightly revised upwards its inflation forecasts for 2026/27 from 6.3%/4.3% to 6.4%/4.5%. May CPI was 6.8%, bringing the 5M26 average inflation rate to 4.5%. This implies that inflation could average 7.7% for the rest of the year. The BSP expects inflation go back to the 2-4% target band by 2028. For 2026, consensus expectations for inflation and rate hikes are 6.1% and +25bps to 5.00%. Regis expectations are 7.5% and +75bps to 5.50%, respectively. The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled this year will be held on Aug 27.

Dept. of Agriculture sees input cost relief as Hormuz reopens but remains wary of El Niño

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) expects the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to lower farming costs and ease pressure on the agriculture sector. Following months of conflict, US and Iran have reportedly reached a deal to end the Middle East war and reopen the strait as the two states are expected to sign an agreement on Friday in Switzerland. DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that fuel, fertilizer, and freight costs are expected to ease and alleviate pressure from the next planting season and prepare for El Niño. The nation's weather bureau (PAGASA) said that there is an over 80% probability that a full-blown El Niño event will develop and likely persist until early 2027. The DA’s palay (unmilled rice) output projection for 2026 is currently 19.9 MMT (+1% YoY from 19.7 MMT in 2025). The DA warned that the FY26 output projection could decline to 18.8 MMT (-5% YoY) accounting for losses from El Niño and typhoons. The 18.8 MMT palay output figure would translate to approximately 11.8 MMT of rice based on the 63% milling recovery rate reported by the Philippine Statistics Authority. This would be roughly 5.7 MMT short of the USDA's projected rice consumption of ~17.5 MMT for 2026.

Sugar pest outbreak rapidly expands; threatening 60% of PH sugar output

The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) stated that the red-striped soft scale insects (RSSI) infestation nationwide as of June 6 has reached 2,249 hectares with Negros Occidental accounting for over 2,000 hectares of infestation. Negros accounts for 60% of the national sugar output. The infested area is approximately 0.6% of the total sugarcane area planted in the Philippines in 2025. The SRA said that the RSSI infestation is expanding rapidly across Visayas in existing areas and new localities with the total affected area recently increased to 460 hectares in one week. RSSI infestations can slash crop yields by up to 50%. The SRA is currently utilizing drone technology to apply insecticides to minimize crop damage and reduce pest populations.

More than 50 companies interested in Pax Silica hub

Joshua Bingcang, President of the Bases Conversion Development Authority (BCDA), said that more than 50 companies, including trillion-dollar tech firms, are interested in participating in the Pax Silica Initiative. One such firm is in advanced stages of due diligence. The Pax Silica hub will be situated in 1,600-hectare plot in Clark. Mr. Bingcang said the BCDA and the US Department of State are working on a framework agreement that would deal with the legal, commercial and other issues related to developing the hub. He previously said that the Pax Silica AI industrial hub will not be operational until at least two years from now. The Pax Silica coalition includes 14 countries.

BIR collections for May increase by 14%

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) guided that gross revenue in May amounted to P279bn, up 14% YoY—an acceleration from the flat growth (+0.4% YoY) in April. BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza noted that the rebound in collections was due to the extension of the annual income tax return filing deadline by one-month from the original date of April 15. As of 5M26, the BIR collected P1.43tn (+5.5% YoY), this exceeds the bureau's P1.42tn five-month collection target by P9.7bn. The BIR targets to collect P3.43tn (+10% YoY) for 2026.

Remittances rise by 2.0% in April

Cash remittances amounted US$2.7bn in Apr, 2.0% higher YoY—decelerating from the 2.3% growth in Mar. This marks the lowest YoY growth figure since May 2022 (+1.8%). The BSP highlighted that the continued YoY growth reflects the resilience of remittances from Overseas Filipinos despite "prevailing global economic conditions". Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+5.5%), Saudi Arabia (+2.9%), and the UAE (+2.8%).

Government launches second global bond issue

The Bureau of Treasury announced it was offering 5.5-year, 10-year and 25-year US dollar bonds. The initial pricing guidance was 85 basis points (bps) above US Treasuries for the 5.5-year tranche, and 125 bps over US Treasuries for the 10-year bond. The 25-year offering will be a tap of the existing 5.75% 2051 USD bond issued in January. The government is looking to raise at least US$1bn from this round but bond traders expect the government could raise up to US$3bn. Recall that in January, the government raised US$2.75bn from a triple-tranche dollar offering, which were priced at 50-83bps above US treasuries.

BSP moves to lower fees on digital fund transfers

The BSP amended rules on pricing mechanisms for fees imposed on person-to-person electronic fund transfers. It lifted the moratorium, which previously disallowed increases in InstaPay and PESONet transaction fees. However, the new rules mandate that all fees be based on actual processing costs and that fees remain lower than over-the-counter banking fees. New rules are grounded on the implementation of zero fees for small merchant payments and reduce person-to-person transfer fees.

Trust and fiduciary assets rise to P10.2tn in 1Q26

The Philippines’ “Trust and Other Fiduciary Business” assets rose 29% YoY and 11% QoQ to P10,179bn in 1Q26. Growth was driven mainly by the “other fiduciary services” account within the banking sector, following a similar trend in 4Q25. Excluding “other fiduciary services,” trust and agency assets under management grew 9% YoY for banks and 13% YoY for non-banks. BDO remained the largest player with consolidated AUM of P2,611bn, up 12% YoY and 1% QoQ. BPI’s AUM reached P1,947bn, increasing 20% YoY and 6% QoQ. Data for individual non-bank institutions have yet to be released.

Department of Energy plans to set pre-bid auction for Semirara coal contract within June; bid submission by August

The Department of Energy (DOE) said it hopes to schedule the pre-bid conference for the Semirara coal block auction within the month, with the bid submission deadline set 60 days after or by August. According to DOE Secretary Sharon Garin. Recall that the DOE deferred the bid submission deadline originally set on April 28 to ensure that all issues raised by the bidders are fully considered. There are currently only two (2) groups vying for the Semirara coal contract: Semirara (SCC PM) and the group of San Miguel (SMC PM). The DOE plans to have the final consultation on the terms of reference. She also noted that the agency intends to offer a 25-year contract term, departing from its earlier proposal to align the contract duration with the reserve life, subject to renewal based on additional reserves. While financial strength remains a consideration in the selection process, Secretary Garin emphasized that greater weight will be given to a bidder’s technical expertise and operational capability. “They must demonstrate that they know what they are doing. That is what we are still trying to determine”.

Power spot prices spike in May

Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) prices surged in May, after the ERC lifted the WESM’s suspension on May 1. Recall that on March 26, the ERC suspended the WESM to address supply risks and price volatility triggered by the Middle East conflict. Spot prices in Luzon jumped to P7.02/kwh in May, up 66% YoY and 11% MoM.

San Miguel, Metro Pacific tollroad merger likely in 3Q

Metro Pacific (MPI) and San Miguel (SMC PM) are expected to finalize valuation discussions on the proposed merger of their domestic tollroad units by 3Q26. San Miguel is likely to emerge with a 55% controlling stake in the combined entity, given that the merger excludes Metro Pacific’s tollroad assets. PAGCOR orders out-of-home gambling advertisements to be replaced with helpline information

PAGCOR ordered all gaming firms to replace existing gambling advertisements with materials promoting the newly launched 24/7 National Problem Gambling Helpline. The directive covers billboards, digital displays, and other out-of-home (OOH) advertising platforms currently carrying responsible gaming messages. The replacements must be completed on or before July 15 and should remain in place until September 15, 2026.

Senator Gatchalian formally elected as Senate President

Senator Sherwin Gatchalian was elected as Senate President as the majority bloc was able to garner 13 votes following the addition of Senator Joel Villanueva to the majority bloc. Gatchalian replaced Senator Alan Peter Cayetano who was removed as leader on June 3. Senator Tito Sotto III was elected as Senate pro tempore while Senator Migz Zubiri took oath as the Senate majority leader. Senator Cayetano, despite being absent, formally assumed the role as the minority leader following Senator Loren Legarda's move to nominate him. Senator Cayetano alongside his allies in the minority bloc, however, petitioned the Supreme Court to nullify the election of the Gatchalian leadership, arguing that the 12-person quorum was illegitimate. House Deputy Speaker Jay Khonghun stated that the petition does not automatically suspend or delay the impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte. He emphasized that the Senate impeachment court remains bound by its constitutional responsibility to proceed with the trial unless there is a contrary order from the SC. The Senate majority bloc has reportedly agreed to elect Senator Chiz Escudero as the presiding officer of the Senate impeachment court once it convenes on July 6. Escudero was said to be chosen as he is a lawyer and has experience with the impeachment process.
