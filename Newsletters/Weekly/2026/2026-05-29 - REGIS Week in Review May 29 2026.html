REGIS REPORT

29 May 2026

The PCOMP extended its losing streak, falling 3.2% or 192.64 pts WoW to 5,768.76 -- its lowest close in six months -- as re-escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East triggered broad risk-off sentiment. Although global markets were largely supported by hopes of a peace deal between the US and Iran, US military strikes on Iran midweek drove increased volatility in oil prices, which dampened the outlook on inflation and domestic growth. The MSCI May rebalancing at the close of the week further weighed down share prices with over $100-m worth of net foreign outflows on the day.

 

Deleted and down-weighted names at Friday's rebalancing were among the biggest losers for the week -- JFC (-7.0% WoW) was deleted from the MSCI Standard Index but added to the Small Cap Index, although net outflows amounted to $45m from the migration, while MER (-6.2% WoW) was down-weighted due to a decrease in its foreign inclusion factor.

 

PLUS (-17.8% WoW) was by far the biggest decliner on the index with traders continuing to speculate on its potential removal at the PSE Index rebalancing in August. With one month remaining for the index review, it is still a toss-up between PLUS and CNVRG (-1.1% WoW) by a narrow margin, to be replaced by MYNLD (-5.3% WoW).

 

Meanwhile, ICT (-7.7% WoW) pulled back sharply from a fresh high hit early in the week, likewise dragged by rebalancing outflows that reached $17m on the day. Prior to the selloff, ICT had rallied to an all-time high on Monday following the release of a BSP circular raising the single-entity exposure limit for unit investment trust funds (UITFs) from 15% to 20%. The move effectively allows domestic funds to increase allocations closer to ICT’s current index weight, which had long surpassed the previous 15% cap and now even exceeds 20%.

 

Outside of flow-driven market action, investors remained cautious over persisting inflationary pressures, weakening growth, and elevated interest rates. Consumer names CNPF (-10.0% WoW) and PGOLD (-5.5% WoW), along with residential proxy ALI (-5.9% WoW) were among large underperformers for the week.

 

Foreigners were net sellers for the week by $134m, while the peso was slightly stronger by 0.2% WoW to 61.596:$1. The PH10Y yield eased by 22.5 bps WoW to 7.5208%.

Philippine Banks - BSP preparing potential capital relief?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Reclassifying 150bps of CET-1 into “releasable” CCyB

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) issued Circular 1235, reclassifying the current minimum requirement for CET-1 ratio of 6.0%. It reduced the headline minimum to 4.5%, but increased the counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB) from 0.0% to 1.5%. There is no immediate impact to regulatory limits for CET-1. However, the move gives the BSP flexibility to provide capital relief to banks by “releasing” the CCyB in times of crisis, to ensure the system has capacity for continued loan growth to support the broader economy.

 

CCyB and the BSP – a brief history

The CCyB is meant to be capital that banks build up in good times, which is “releasable” during times of stress. The BSP formally introduced the CCyB in 2018, but set it at zero. The new Circular does not increase the overall CET-1 requirement (as some media suggest).

Instead, the BSP reallocated the existing CET-1 ratio, increasing the CCyB from 0.0% to 1.5%, but reducing the minimum CET-1 ratio from 6.0% to 4.5%. All other additional capital buffers like the capital conservation buffer (CCB), and higher loss absorption (HLA) for DSIB banks remain unchanged (Fig 2).

 

Flexibility to provide timely capital relief, if needed

On paper, Philippine banks have sufficient capital, with parent bank CET-1 ratios ranging from 12.0-13.4% (Fig 3), comfortably above the 10.0-10.5% limits (depending on each bank’s DSIB level, etc). However, we noted in a recent report that the spike in bond yields (Fig 1, at right) could result in significant mark-to-market losses on banks’ FVOCI investments, reducing qualifying capital. Rising NPLs would also increase risk weighted assets (RWA) and reduce profit. Reclassifying 150bps from CET-1 to CCyB allows the BSP to adjust the overall capital limits in a timely manner. Notably, increases in CCyB are implemented over a 12-month period, while reductions in CCyB are effective immediately.

ICTSI’s port in Manila still experiencing yard congestion

Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) said that it is experiencing longer container dwell times, with 41% of yard inventory remaining beyond the 5-day “free” storage period. It has called on shipping lines, customers, and consignees to expedite withdrawal of laden containers to ease higher-than-normal yard utilization rates. This development aligns with management’s earlier statement that yield per TEU continues to remain elevated in 2Q.

Meralco sees re-IPO of renewable energy unit by March 2027

Meralco plans to complete the asset injection of its renewable energy assets under MGEN Renewables into SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM) by 4Q26. The merger will then be followed by a re-IPO of the merged entity “MGEN Renewable Energy Holdings, Inc.” by March 2027. Re-IPO is intended to coincide with the target completion of Phase 2 of the MTerra Solar Project (3500 MW + 4500 MWhr BESS). SPNEC is a 57%-owned unit of Meralco and owns an attributable solar capacity of 2,511MW (SPNEC owns 60% of MTerra Solar). MGEN Renewables has an attributable solar capacity of ~580 MW.

Ayala Group enters Thailand market

The Ayala Group signed an agreement with CP AXTRA (CPAXT TB) to strengthen mall development and asset management across Thailand. CP AXTRA operates more than 2,700 Makro and Lotus’s stores across Southeast Asia. Under the deal, Ayala’s consumer retail arm ACx Holdings will advise on retail formats, customer behavior and partnerships, while AyalaLand Malls, Inc. will help sharpen leasing strategy, tenant mix, mall operations and foot traffic across Makro sites. The initial phase covers seven Makro locations.

Government posts P31bn budget surplus in April

The Philippine government recorded a budget surplus of P31bn in April, 53% lower YoY but a reversal from the P350bn deficit in Mar. This brings the 4M26 deficit to P324bn, 14% narrower vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in April rose 3% YoY, decelerating from +6% YoY in March, primarily as collections from the BIR were flat (+0.4% YoY). Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections slowed to +0.4% YoY from +7% the previous month. The disappointing BIR figure was partly attributed to the extension of the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return from April to 15 to May 15 following the declaration of the State of National Energy Emergency. Customs collections picked up to 16%, accelerating from the 6% growth the previous month on continued digitalization efforts. Non-tax revenues rose 7% slowing from +46% in March. Gov't spending continued to recover as expenses were up 11% YoY in April accelerating from the 5% growth in March. Primary spending for the month increased by 8% YoY. This marks the third straight month of YoY growth after six consecutive months of YoY declines in gov't spending. Interest payments rose 37% YoY.

Government urges water conservation as threat of El Niño grows

The Dept. of Science and Technology (DoST) Secretary Renato Solidum Jr. stated that there is a 92% probability that El Niño conditions will develop between June and August and could persist into early 2027. Solidum added that by September to November 2026, the dry spell could strengthen further and reach a "very strong category" which has a 30% chance of occurring. He elaborated that a stronger El Niño does not automatically translate to severe impacts but it implies a higher likelihood of drought, water shortages, and extreme heat. Back in 2024, agricultural damage due to El Niño amounted to P15.3bn impacting over 300,000 farmers and fisherfolk nationwide. Recall that the Dept. of Agriculture (DA) recently revised its total palay (unmilled rice) output projection in 2026 from 20.3 MMT (+3% YoY from 19.7 MMT in 2025) previously to 19.9 MMT (+1% YoY) due to the Middle East conflict and looming El Niño. The DA warned that the FY26 output could decline further to 18.8 MMT (-5% YoY) if fertilizer and fuel prices remain elevated.

Energy regulator mulls review of secondary WESM price ceiling

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is reviewing the secondary price cap of P7.42/kwh. It remains unclear, however, whether the ERC plans to raise the WESM secondary price cap further or take the more consequential step of removing it altogether. The secondary price cap is triggered whenever the 72-hour rolling average electricity price exceeds Php12.4/kwh. WESM prices repeatedly hit the ceiling last May 13-15, while consumers experienced blackouts. Generation output typically declines between 3–4PM as solar production tapers off, highlighting the need for additional fast-response and dispatchable resources during evening peak hours.

BSP amends single-entity exposure limits for UITFs

The BSP issued Circular 1234 amending single-entity exposure limits for unit investment trust funds (UITFs). Under the new rules, the combined exposure of a UITF to any single entity remains capped at 15% of the fund’s market value, but UITFs invested partially or substantially in exchange-traded equity securities may increase exposure to a single issuer up to 20%. The circular specified that any exposure above 15%, up to the 20% limit, must be solely in the form of listed equity securities of the same issuer. Meanwhile, equity index-tracking UITFs may exceed the 20% cap and mirror the actual benchmark weighting of index component issuers. The amendments imply that bond-only UITFs remain subject to the 15% issuer cap, while balanced and equity-focused UITFs may increase exposure to listed equities up to 20% per issuer.
