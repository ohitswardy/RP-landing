REGIS REPORT

01 April 2026

After four straight weekly losses, the PCOMP finally rebounded this shortened trading week, up by 0.4% or 25.85 pts to 5,998.68 as global markets rallied following comments from Pres. Trump that the US could end the war on Iran within two to three weeks.

 

Quarter-end window-dressing helped share prices rebound from the weak start to the week, while oil falling below $100/bbl by Wednesday reinforced the index's upward move, although still closing just shy of the psychological 6,000-mark.
 

Some company-specific developments also moved share prices, particularly in the retail sector. RRHI (+18.2% WoW) was among the biggest gainers for the week after trading of its shares resumed from a halt following its announcement of a tender offer and delisting plan. The tender offer price of P48.30/sh represented a 24% premium to the last closing price. WLCON (+3.3% WoW) and PGOLD (+3.3% WoW) disclosed earnings that were within expectations, with strong 4Q momentum driven by healthy SSSG and margin expansion.

 

Elsewhere, ALI (-3.1% WoW) announced a new P10-bn share buyback program the same day that its shares hit lows not seen since early-2012. This is equivalent to roughly 4% of ALI's outstanding shares.

 

Overall, sectors finished mixed. Mining (+6.4% WoW) led gainers as metal prices rebounded, followed by Holdings (+2.2% WoW) after proxies AC (+5.7% WoW) and SM (+1.9% WoW) rallied. Some cautiousness lingered in the market though, seeing banks (-0.4% WoW) remain generally weak, while outside of retail, consumer names finished mostly lower still with CNPF (-4.3% WoW), URC (-2.1% WoW), and JFC (-2.0% WoW) among the worst performing.

 

With global energy prices still elevated, power and utilities stocks continued to outperform, led by ACEN (+3.9% WoW) and MYNLD (+3.0% WoW).

 

Foreigners were still net sellers by $57m, extending the weekly outflow streak to six consecutive weeks. The peso saw some relief but remained above 60:$1, ending the week +0.6% WoW to 60.196.

Robinsons Retail - Controlling shareholder seeks to privatize RRHI
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

RRHI seeks to voluntarily delist

Robinsons Retail Holdings, Inc. disclosed that its largest shareholder JE Holdings (owned by various members of the Gokongwei family) will be conducting a general offer which is likely to result in the company being taken private and delisted from the PSE. The proposed tender offer price of P48.30/share is a 24% premium to the last closing price. It is, however, well below our target price (P83/share) and at just ~8x PER for FY26E.

 

Tender offer price of P48.30/sh

The proposed tender offer price by JE Holdings of P48.30/sh is at a 24% premium to the latest closing price of P38.95/sh and a 32% premium to the one-year VWAP. It is also not far from the P50.00/sh buyback price for the 22% stake RRHI itself acquired from the Dairy Farm group. At that time, we thought the deal would be meaningfully value-accretive to RRHI.

 

The offer price, however, values RRHI at only 8.3x PE, higher than the current PE of 6.7x but notably lower than historical average.

 

It is also significantly lower than our NAV estimate of P66/share if one were to value its ~6% stake in BPI at market price. Note RRHI’s stake in BPI alone is worth over P30/share (albeit without considering any debt at RRHI). RRHI has been, in our view, persistently undervalued by the market.

 

Persistent undervaluation meets high tender offer threshold

JE Holdings currently holds a 46.1% stake while directors hold another 20.45%. The remainder (33.45%) is held by the public. For the deal to push through, the proponents require that they are able to secure 95% of RRHI’s outstanding shares. This implies owners of 85% of the public float (i.e. 28% of RRHI’s shares) must agree to sell at that price.

 

The controlling shareholders likely view the persistent undervaluation of RRHI with dismay, thus prompting this latest move. This action does offer minority shareholders an exit opportunity at a defined premium, but we are uncertain if the 95% threshold can be achieved.

 
Philippine Property - 4Q25 recap: Residential reservation sales fall to lowest level since 2021
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

A mixed bag in 4Q25

What grabbed our attention the most from the 4Q25 results season was the substantial decline in demand for residential property. After rising sequentially for two quarters, 4Q25 residential reservation sales fell to the lowest level since 2Q21. On the other hand, office demand rebounded QoQ and YoY, led by the BPO sector. Meanwhile, contrary to the slowdown in GDP growth (including personal consumption), mall revenue grew at a healthy pace of 7%.

 

Residential reservation sales fall, led by mid-income condominiums

After rising sequentially for two quarters, aggregate reservation sales of the top four developers fell 28% QoQ and 13% YoY to P61.5bn. This is the lowest level of sales since 2Q21 and only represents c. 60% of the 2019 average. While the high-end market saw a reduction in sales (likely brought about by the recent government corruption scandal), the decline in sales of mid-income condominiums in Metro Manila was even more precipitous. This drop was despite the developers maintaining the same level of promotions on mid-income ready-for-occupancy units, such as cash discounts and rent-to-own schemes.

 

Although developers generally reduced the value of unsold residential inventory QoQ, they remain high for many developers.

Based on our estimates, the unsold inventory levels of some developers represent over three years of reservation sales (based on cumulative sales over the past 12 months).

 

Office take-up rebounds

Although office vacancy remained flat QoQ (at 18%), BPO firms and traditional tenants both took more incremental space in 4Q25.

 

Spot rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ but remain 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. However, rental rates in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio inched up QoQ, since vacancy rates in those areas are lower.

 

On average, office contracts were renewed at rental rates that were slightly lower. Lease contracts in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio were generally renewed at higher rental rates while most contracts in other areas were renewed at lower rental rates.

 

Mall revenue growth remains healthy despite weak GDP growth

Although real GDP growth (including personal consumption) slowed in 4Q25, Philippine mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators still grew 7%, just mildly slower than the 8% pace in 3Q25. We estimate that same-mall-revenue growth stood at 6%, at the midpoint of the historical range (of 4%-8%).

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

PGOLD FY25 results, profit +9% and revenues +11%

Puregold Price Club, Inc. (PGOLD PM) reported that FY25 profit was up 9% to P11.3bn, with top line increasing 11% to P242.4bn. For 4Q25 alone, net profit climbed 15% YoY to P4.0bn, while revenues rose 11% to P74.4bn. FY25 SSSG: Puregold +4.1% (decelerated vs 4.8% in 9M25). S&R +6.1% (accelerated vs 5.4% in 9M25). The company elaborated that Puregold and S&R FY25 SSSG were driven by higher basket size and increased foot traffic, respectively. Derived 4Q25 SSSG: Puregold +2.0% (unchanged vs in 2.0% in 3Q25). S&R +8.2% (faster vs 6.7% in 3Q25). PGOLD President Ferdinand Vincent Co stated that the company will focus on “accelerating store expansion in key provincial markets” for 2026.

Wilcon Depot 4Q25 sees healthy momentum, uncertain runway due to global turmoil

WLCON’s FY25 net profit amounted to P2.4bn (-3% YoY) while FY25 revenues reached P35.4bn (+4% YoY). In 4Q25 alone, net profit climbed 41% to P580mn as EBIT surged 53%. Profitability improved on higher sales and margin expansion, with operating leverage amplifying growth off a low base in 4Q24. 4Q25 exhibited continued top line recovery as revenues rose 7% YoY on higher sales volumes. 4Q25 net sales rose 7% to P9.1bn, accelerating from the +3% growth in 9M25. SSSG was +3.8% on higher ticket size as in-store optimizations bore fruit. Management guided that sales likely improved further in 1Q26, with March ADS stronger than Jan-Feb amid customers reportedly accelerating purchases and “panic buying” due to the Middle East conflict. Although sustainability of this trend is uncertain as higher-priced inventory and inflationary pressures set in. The company declared dividends of P0.40/sh (record as of Apr 17, payable May 12), higher than the P0.36/sh dividends declared last year and implies a ~7% yield on the current share price.

Ayala Land announces P10bn share buyback program

The Executive Committee of Ayala Land approved a P10bn share buyback program. The buyback program will commence on April 1. The previous P28bn buyback program was fully consumed. During the company’s 4Q25 briefing, management said that it did not intend to renew the buyback program. P10bn represents 4% of the current market capitalization.

MTerra Solar adds 250MW to capacity

The MTerra Solar project of SP New Energy Corporation (SPNEC PM) has begun delivering 250MW to the Luzon grid. SPNEC is 57% owned by Meralco (MER PM), through its subsidiary, MGEN Renewable Energy. It has also switched on the first tranche of its battery energy storage system (BESS), allowing the facility to inject up to 450 megawatt hours (MWh) of energy to the grid system at night. The MTerra Solar project is slated for full commercial operations in 2027. Upon full completion, it will have a capacity of 3,500 MWp of solar power, supported by 4,500 MWh BESS.

McDonald's Philippines targets to open 75 stores this year

McDonald's Philippines plans to open 75 new stores in 2026, a tad higher than the 72 opened last year. Current store network is over 850 stores nationwide, with the 1,000th-store milestone targeted to be achieved by 2028. Golden Arches Development Corp, which is 49% owned by Alliance Global (AGI PM), is the mater franchise holder for McDonald’s Philippines.

GTCAP buys a direct 20% stake in Federal Land NRE Global

GT Capital bought a 20% stake in Federal Land NRE Global (FNRE) from its wholly-owned unit, Federal Land. Transaction valued at P9.16bn. Prior to this deal, Federal Land held a 66% stake in FNRE while Nomura Real Estate held 34%. FNRE owns properties in Cavite, Metro Manila, and Cebu, totaling ~220 hectares.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP expects March inflation to settle within 3.1-3.9%

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects March inflation to fall within 3.1-3.9%. BSP expectations for Mar inflation to be at the higher end of the BSP's 2-4% long-term target range. For 2026, BSP expectations for inflation this year is 5.1% (previously revised from 3.6%). Mar inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on Apr 7 (Tue). Upward price pressures are higher prices of domestic petroleum, rice, and electricity, as well as the depreciation of the peso. These may have been partially offset by lower prices of vegetables, fish, and meat. As of end-Mar, the USD-PHP depreciated to P60.74, from P57.55 at end-Feb. Inflation has averaged 2.2% so far for 2M26, while inflation in Feb alone was 2.4%. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Apr 23 (second out of six scheduled this year).

Trade deficit widens to $3.7bn in Feb

The Feb trade deficit widened to US$3.7bn, expanding 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth of imports vs exports. This brings the 2M26 trade deficit to US$36.9bn, 6% wider YoY. Imports for the month grew by 13% YoY to US$11.0bn, a reversal from the 3% decline in Jan. The rebound to growth was primarily driven by the surge in capital goods (+55%) and the reversal to growth in consumer goods imports (+10%). Consumer goods in Feb broke a previous five-month streak of YoY declines. Exports rose 8% YoY to US$7.0bn, the thirteenth consecutive month of YoY growth. Electronics exports climbed 20% YoY to $4.2bn, continuing the momentum from the 19% growth in Jan. These made up the bulk of exports at 58% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 5% to US$3.1bn.

Manufacturers, retailers agree with DTI to halt price increases for two weeks

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that there will be a price freeze for basic necessities and prime commodities from April 1 to 16 as several manufacturers and retailers agreed defer any price adjustments to absorb rising logistics and inventory costs. Participating manufacturers include Century Pacific Food Inc. (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), Universal Robina Corporation (URC PM), San Miguel Foods (FB PM), Nestle Philippines, Proctor & Gamble Philippines, Unilever Philippines, among others. Retailers that also participated include Robinsons Retail Holdings Inc. (RRHI PM), SM Markets—food retail arm of SM Investments (SM PM), Metro Retail Stores Group Inc. (MRSGI PM), etc. The DTI stated that it will monitor compliance and ensure that prices remain stable during the period.

President Marcos approves price cap of P50/kilogram on imported rice

President Marcos approved the National Price Coordinating Council recommended price cap of P50/kilo for imported well-milled rice. The average retail price of well-milled imported rice in Metro Manila is P47.71/kilo. He will issue an Executive Order in relation to this. The government also expanded the P20/kilo rice program from 600 tons to 2,000 tons due to rising demand. President Marcos also said that more than 4 million farmers and fisherfolk are set to receive P2,325 each starting in April. Total of P10bn. Fuel subsidy will also be dispensed to 40,000 farmers, while fisherfolk will be given almost 100,000 fuel cards and fuel vouchers.

Philippines' fuel supply increased to approximately 51 days

The Dept. of Energy (DOE) Secretary Sharon Garin stated that the Philippines' fuel supply has been extended by around six days to 50.9 days as of Mar 27, higher than the 45.1 days as of Mar 20. Given ongoing concerns on fuel supply uncertainty, Garin highlighted that supply continues to increase despite continued consumption which gives more lead time for the gov’t to replenish stocks. Daily demand was noted to remain high, diesel consumption at ~32mn liters and gasoline at ~23mn liters. The Philippine National Oil Co. (PNOC) stated that they are looking to secure additional supply of jet fuel and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG). Petron (PCOR PM) was said to have committed around 19% of its crude supply for LPG production. Petron's overall crude supply was said to last until June.

Petron fuel supply to last until end of June

Petron Corporation (PCOR PM) acquired 2.48mn barrels of crude oil from Russia. With this delivery, Petron said that it has sufficient supplies “that will last up to June 30”. Petron may purchase more crude oil from Russia. Petron has a 30% market share of the country’s fuel supply and operates the only oil refinery in the Philippines.

Energy regulator pushes for staggered increases in electricity bills

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) urged power utilities to implement staggered billing with suppliers to soften the impact of rising electricity rates on consumers. The Department of Energy estimates that electricity prices could rise 16% in April.

Commercial banks’ loan growth picks up to 9.5% in Feb

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.5% in Feb from 9.3% in Jan, the third month of single-digit growth after a previous 19-month streak of double-digit growth. Loans to productive activities picked up to 8.6% vs 8.2% in Jan. Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 20.8%, a tad slower than Jan's 21.3% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) significantly accelerated to 10.3% in Feb from 8.6% in Jan, this marks the highest M3 growth print since Nov 2020.
