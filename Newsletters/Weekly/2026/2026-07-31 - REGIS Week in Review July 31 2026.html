REGIS REPORT

31 July 2026

Philippine equities inched lower this week as investors digested Pres. Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA), a fresh round of 2Q earnings reports, and the latest BSP policy signals. The PCOMP closed lower by 0.7% or 44.57 pts WoW to 6,236.44, tempering its July gains to 3.3% and YTD advance to 3.0%.

 

The SONA was the key macro event, setting the administration's legislative agenda for the remainder of the term. In particular, the headline market-mover was Pres. Marcos’ call to amend the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) and remove system loss charges passed on to consumers, which sent MER (-20.0% WoW) shares to a free-fall. Beyond the expected hit on earnings if system loss recovery is removed, the market weighed the potential drag to its rate rebasing process that was supposed to complete in 3Q. Negative sentiment spilled over to much of the power and utilities space, with ACEN (-4.3% WoW) and AP (-3.2% WoW) weaker on the news as well.

 

On the flipside, the Consumer sector was broadly positive with gains led by JFC (+3.3% WoW) and MONDE (+3.0% WoW), as Pres. Marcos at his SONA also pushed for more tax relief measures such as raising the personal income tax exemption threshold as well as exempting small businesses from the minimum corporate tax.

 

On monetary policy, BSP Gov. Eli Remolona said there was only a "small chance" of a 50-bp rate hike at the August 27 Monetary Board meeting despite inflation and peso risks, reinforcing expectations for a measured policy path.

 

Elsewhere in the market, the PSE Index August rebalancing executed at Friday’s close resulted in selling into the inclusion of MYNLD (-3.4% WoW) and buying into the deletion of CNVRG (+7.9% WoW).

 

Earnings season gathered pace with the "Big Three" banks all holding their earnings calls this week -- BDO (+1.2% WoW), BPI (unch. WoW), and MBT (-0.8% WoW) all delivered broadly in-line results, with loan growth remaining upbeat across the sector, although higher provisioning continued to weigh on earnings as banks stayed conservative amid lingering macro uncertainties. Meanwhile, MER reported 1H26 results that were in-line with estimates, as sequentially stronger distribution earnings offset weaker generation profits from higher gas costs and plant outages.

 

Investors also monitored developments surrounding the Semirara mine rebidding after the government deferred the process further to August to September, while proposing that 60-70% of production be allocated to the domestic market -- a potentially material change to the mine operations' economics if adopted. SCC (-6.0% WoW) and DMC (-3.2% WoW) were thus among underperformers in the index.

 

Foreigners were net buyers for the week by $3m, while the peso strengthened by 1.0% WoW to 61.246:$1. The PH10Y yield slipped 18 bps WoW to 7.430%.

BDO Unibank - 1H26 lags slightly; can NIMs, credit costs help a 2H catch-up?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

2Q26 NPAT -1.4% YoY as NIMs slip, credit costs rise; can 2H rebound?
BDO reported 1H26 headline NPAT of P40.7bn, flat from the P40.6bn the previous year, tracking slightly behind REGIS forecasts (P86.2bn) and consensus (P86.7bn). For 2Q alone, NPAT of P20.6bn was down 1% YoY. Loans grew 15% YoY, but NIMs compressed, with NII 11%. PPOP grew 12% YoY, but this was dragged by provision expense, which jumped 76% YoY for 1H26, as the bank “proactively” provisioned for potential bad debts. Management expressed optimism on NIMs widening on policy rate hikes, but remained more guarded on credit costs.

NIMs flattening out, upside from higher benchmark rates?
Loan growth inched down to 14.7% (vs 15.6% in 1Q), but remained well above sector-wide growth of 12.5% as of May. Growth was surprisingly driven by corporate (+13%) and mid-market (+16%), while consumer loan growth held steady (18%). NIMs for 2Q were down 22bps YoY, driven in part by rising deposit costs, as CASA ratio dipped to 63%, a low since 2014. That said, loan yields recovered, up 12bps QoQ.

Management noted that NIMs were already showing early signs of expansion in June, as policy rate hikes flow into asset yields.

Credit costs stay elevated in 2Q; guarded optimism into 2H…
Fee growth was soft, up 4% YoY due to competitive pricing. To the upside, Opex grew just 7% YoY, pushing PPOP up 12%. However, credit costs stayed high at 67bps. Management was optimistic that their “proactive” stance could see write-backs in the corporate book. However, they remain guarded on the consumer portfolio given macro challengers of slow GDP growth, and high inflation.

Retaining Buy on valuations, below 1x P/bk
The stock bounced from its Iran war lows, but still trades sub-1.0x P/bk on our 2026E estimates. Our target price of P180/sh is based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming LT ROE of 14.0%, COE of 12.0% and growth of 8.0%. We maintain our Buy call as our target implies a total potential return of 45%, including a 3.5% dividend yield. Risks include deteriorating asset quality on macro factors.

 
BPI - 2Q26 – Credit costs jump to 120bps, to normalize in 2H?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

1H26 NPAT P32.8bn in line, despite provision surge; potential 2H recovery?
BPI reported NPAT of P32.8bn in 1H26, flat YoY (in line) with 2Q26 alone down 3% YoY despite an 84% YoY surge in 1H provision expense. Operations seemed robust, with loans growing 12.4%, NIMs expanding, and fees jumping 18% on the continued shift towards consumers and MSMEs. Management explained that the credit cost surge to 120bps in 2Q26 was due in part to adjustments in the ECL macro assumptions, which should not recur in 2H26.

Consumer/MSME driving margins, fees; PPOP +11%
Loan growth slowed to 12.4% YoY as the bank tightened lending controls in the credit card, personal and SME books. Despite that, the overall consumer/MSME book was still up 21%, and now account for 32% of total loans (Figs 1&2). That continued push, together with lower funding costs, helped NIMs recover QoQ (Fig 3). Fee incomes, too, surged 18% YoY in 1H26 and 22% YoY in 2Q alone. PPOP grew a solid 11% YoY.

2Q Credit costs surge to 120bps, on ECL macro changes; back down in 2H?
Provision expense surged 84% YoY, with credit costs at 120bps in 2Q26 (Fig 4), as the bank adjusted ECL assumptions to reflect current macro conditions. Of the P7.8bn provision in 2Q, P2.7bn was due to the ECL change, suggesting credit costs could normalize to the 80-90bps range in 2H26. NPL ratio was steady (Fig 5), as two corporate loans were resolved, offsetting increased delinquencies in the personal loan and SME books.

Retaining estimates, and TP of P150; Buy at 1.07x P/bk
The resilience of BPI’s operations, despite the growing pains of the consumer/MSME push, give us optimism over a NPAT recovery in 2H. The stock trades on 1.07x P/bk based on our FY2026E estimates, while delivering a 5.0% dividend yield. We retain our target price of P150/sh – derived using (ROE-g)/(COE-g) on long-term ROE of 14.25%, COE of 12% and growth of 8%. Downside risks are a further deterioration in the macro environment, putting added pressure on asset quality.

Highlights from Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. delivered his fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA) yesterday, outlining accomplishments and a roadmap for the remaining two years of his term which included a discussion on topics such as gov't accountability, economic reforms, energy security, social welfare, and tax relief.

 

Anti-corruption

Marcos opened his speech by mentioning that cases were filed against contractors, DPWH officials, and lawmakers involved in alleged irregularities in flood control projects, with charges soon to be filed with the Ombudsman against his cousin, former House Speaker Martin Romualdez. He also stated that all the money recovered from corrupt individuals involved would be "immediately returned to the public treasury".

 

Power and energy

President Marcos urged Congress to amend the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) in order to remove system loss charges from electricity bills to make power rates cheaper. He also mentioned plans to diversify the country's power sources through renewable energy as well as nuclear energy. Marcos stated a long-term goal of having 50% of all cars be EVs by 2040, aided by the growth of domestic local industries across the EV value chain (i.e., vehicle assembly and battery manufacturing).

 

Economy

The president highlighted that the country's economic strategy is focused on attracting high-value industries vs traditional manufacturing. He said that the US-backed Pax Silica Industrial Hub has the potential to create quality jobs and increase the country's competitiveness and involvement in advanced technology industries. Marcos urged Congress to also pass a bill on tax relief measures such as expanding the exemption for income tax to include those earning P350,000 and below annually as well as exempting small businesses from the minimum corporate tax.

 

Infrastructure and digitalization

Marcos highlighted that some railways are expected to be operational soon with the MRT-7 scheduled to open 12 stations next year and the North-South Commuter Railway (Valenzuela-Malolos) to partially begin operations next year as well. He emphasized that right of way issues are now more quickly resolved given the recently implemented Accelerated and Reformed Right of Way Act. He also stated efforts to accelerate the growth of our local tech sector through fintech, digital banking solutions, and better financial inclusion for all.

 

Social welfare

Marcos reported that 12mn people have benefited from the P20/kg program. Other food security initiatives such as higher NFA buying capacity, larger buffer stocks as well as cold storage. To address higher oil prices, the gov't has allocated over P60bn to sustain its Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport (UPLIFT) initiatives until the end of the year. Savings recovered from corruption investigations were redirected to education, food programs, infrastructure, and healthcare assistance.

 

Education

Marcos announced a handful of initiatives in order to improve the quality of education in the country. An expanded school-based feeding program for nutritionally at-risk learners for 4.5mn students. 30,000 additional teaching positions were created as well as plans to address the classroom shortages through PPP. He also mentioned AI training programs for 1.8mn individuals for students, parents, and teachers.

DOLE confirms court suspension of P85 NCR minimum wage hike

The Dept. of Labor and Employment (DOLE) confirmed that the implementation of the first tranche of the P85 minimum wage increase in Metro Manila was suspended due to a court order issuance (Temporary Restraining Order [TRO]) following the legal challenge by two construction companies. Readycon Trading and Construction Corp. and R-II Builders Inc. filed a petition to temporarily halt the wage hike, questioning whether the Regional Wage Board adequately considered the factors under Article 124 of the Labor Code, particularly the "fair return of the capital invested and capacity to pay of employers". The companies cited that the hike was issued "without adequate regard to compounding economic conditions". Dept. of Labor Secretary Francis Tolentino stated that he is "saddened" by the court decision but notes that the agency fully respects the judicial process. The current NCR minimum wage prior to the suspension was set to increase to P780 which will be implemented in two tranches. +P60 effective July 25, 2026 and the remaining +P25 effective January 20, 2027.

US imposes 12.5% 'forced labor' tariff on Philippine exports

The U.S. Trade Representative (USTR), following its investigation into the Philippines and other countries over imports of goods allegedly produced using forced labor, has determined to impose a 12.5% tariff on Philippine products. The announcement comes ahead of the July 24 expiration of the 10% temporary tariff imposed by the Trump administration on February 20, the same day the Supreme Court deemed President Donald Trump’s previous enforcement of tariffs unconstitutional. Earlier in the year, the US launched a "forced labor" probe to investigate 60 trading partners on the basis of Section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974 in order to "address unfair foreign practices affecting US commerce". The US is also going to impose a 10% tariff on Cambodia, Canada, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Mexico, among others. All other investigated countries were imposed with a 12.5% tariff rate. The USTR elaborated that it will exempt certain products from the tariff such as raw materials that could lead to the unavailability of domestic supply or economy-wide disruptions. The Philippines' Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) elaborated that ~66% (US$12.0bn) of its exports are exempt from the additional duty. Exempted products include semiconductors and electronics, agricultural products, automotive parts, minerals, aircraft parts, etc. 34% of PH exports to the US or $6.2bn worth of goods are going to be subject to the new tariff. Products impacted by the levy are "labor-intensive" industries including apparel, footwear, toys, among others. The DTI also mentioned that the PH compares favorably with regional peers as 83% and 40% of Indonesia's and Malaysia's exports to the US, respectively, are exposed to the new tariff. The US accounted for the PH's largest export market accounting for 16% (US$13.5bn) of total exports in 2025. As of 5M26, US remained the top destination for PH exports at 18% (US$6.7bn, +24% YoY) of the country's total. Various gov't agencies will form an interagency committee to investigate goods made with forced labor to possibly implement measures banning the importation of such goods.

Marcos approves incentives to attract EV investments

President Marcos Jr. signs Executive Order (EO) 21 establishing the Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy (EVIS) Program. Program provides fiscal incentives for the local manufacture of hybrid and battery electric vehicles and their parts and components. Total fiscal support for the EVIS will be capped at P60bn, with up to P15bn allocated for each enrolled EV model. Qualified manufacturers may register up to two (2) EV models under the EVIS program. To qualify, companies must undertake a capex of at least P5bn and introduce such models to the domestic and export markets within a three-year period. In exchange, qualified participants will receive the following fiscal incentives. A Fixed Investment Support that will be equivalent to 40% of capex for battery EVs and 30% for hybrid EVs, plug-in hybrid EVs and fuel cell EVs. A Production Volume Incentive of up to 12% of the ex-factory unit price but not exceeding P200,000 per unit for domestically manufactured or assembled EVs once production hits at least 10,000 EV units. Participants will be entitled to the FSI and PVI for a maximum of 10 years from the start of production of the enrolled EV models. Registered participants will receive tax payment certificates. Marcos authorizes release of BIR tax records for VP impeachment trial

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has authorized the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) to release the tax records of Vice President Sara Duterte to the Senate impeachment court. Senate Secretary Renato Bantug noted that the submission of the VP's tax records would not have been accepted without presidential approval. Several bank documents were also submitted to the Senate impeachment court. House prosecutors requested for the documents as the records are expected to shed light on Duterte's unexplained wealth. Palace press Officer Claire Castro denied claims that the Marcos' action was politically motivated as she noted that the President's decision was in line with what the law required and the proper legal process with the approval being "necessary in the interest of establishing the truth".
