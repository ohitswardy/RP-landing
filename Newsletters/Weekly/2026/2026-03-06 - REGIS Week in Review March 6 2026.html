REGIS REPORT

06 March 2026

Philippine equities retreated this week, joining in risk-off mood across global markets after the US and Israel carried out joint strikes on Iran over the weekend and with hostilities continuing to escalate through the week. These wiped out the PCOMP's February gains and took the index back to around 6,300 -- for the week, down 4.4% or 290.83 pts WoW to 6,320.41.

 

Onshore, investors turned cautious amid growing uncertainty over the Middle East conflict and its spillover effects on the domestic economy. With the Philippines a net energy importer, concerns were particularly focused on inflation, as crude oil surged nearly 20% for the week to the $80/bbl level. Consumer names and proxies were worst hit -- JGS (-12.7% WoW), GTCAP (-12.2% WoW), URC (-10.4% WoW), and AC (-10.3% WoW) all fell by double digits, while CNPF (-9.9% WoW) and PGOLD (-9.6% WoW) were also among the larger decliners.

 

As for the latest on consumer prices, headline inflation accelerated to 2.4% in February, up from 2.0% in January and in line with the consensus forecast of 2.4%. The print bore faster YoY rises in food and utilities, while rice prices continued to decline for the 14th consecutive month. YTD inflation stands at 2.2%, within the BSP’s target range of 2-4%.

 

Outside of geopolitics and macro, 4Q earnings remained in focus with the three largest PCOMP names having released results this week -- in fact, all beating estimates. ICT (unch. WoW) comfortably beat forecasts with most segments coming ahead of expectations, but the Americas region stood out with persistent congestion issues in Mexico helping push yield per TEU to a record high. SM (-3.5% WoW) earnings also came ahead thanks to the mild beats in property and banks, while the retail business delivered a meaningful improvement in the quarter. On that, BDO (-9.0% WoW) beat estimates on lower provisions and tax expenses, although loan growth was also sustained despite the industry slowdown.

 

With over two-thirds of the index having already reported, aggregate PCOMP earnings are so far higher by 15% YoY for 4Q25 and by 9% YoY for FY25.

 

Less than a handful of names managed to buck the index's broad decline. Despite global macro jitters, ICT was flat for the week sustained by strong earnings. SCC (+0.2% WoW) also stayed afloat; recall the government already launched the bidding for its coal operating contract last week. PLUS (+12.7% WoW) outperformed another week as investors drew confidence from its chairman’s recent share purchases.

 

Amid downbeat sentiment, foreigners turned net sellers by $16m, breaking a nine-week net inflow streak. The peso weakened by 2.3% WoW to 58.995:$1.

ICTSI - 4Q25: Ancillary revenues remain elevated
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 profit up 27% to US$1bn – Above Regis and Street estimates

ICTSI reported FY25 profits after perps of US$1.03bn, up 27% YoY and comfortably beating both Regis and consensus estimates. The beat was broad-based, but the Americas region stood out with revenues well above expectations. Derived 4Q25 profits were up 11% QoQ and 37% YoY to US$293mn, accelerating further from the 9M pace of 27%, driven by continued strength in storage/ancillary revenues. We acknowledge the TP mismatch and are currently reviewing our estimates.

 

Strength in ancillary revenues continues in 4Q25

Group-wide volumes rose 10% in 4Q and 8.5% on a like-for-like basis, just a tad slower than 9M trend of 11%, partly due to the entry of new competition in Croatia and a slowdown in the Philippine ports. Despite volume growth moderating, revenues rose 8% QoQ/24% YoY to US$897mn, after congestion issues at DRC and Mexico, which were initially expected to ease, persisted into 4Q and buoyed yield per TEU to a new record high of US$230. As a result, EBITDA further expanded (+9% QoQ/30% YoY) to a new high of US$601mn in 4Q, with margins also reaching a new high of 67%.

Yield strength sustains in 1Q26 but Middle East war tempers upside

Management noted that yields continue to be strong in the first two months of the year. While management cited the ongoing cartel violence in Mexico as further complicating the port congestion issues, we also note that the port of Manila experienced delays in the withdrawal of import containers due to a truck shortage. To the downside, the Middle East conflict has adversely affected its port in Iraq, with management estimating a 4-5% EBITDA hit every month, assuming volumes fall to zero.


2026 capex guidance (ex-acquisitions) of US$740 (+29% YoY)

ICT spent a total of US$1.3bn in 2025, of which US$572mn was on expansions of its existing portfolio while the balance was spent for the acquisition of Durban (US$618mn) and Batam. For 2026, management is guiding for organic capex to rise by 29% to US$740mn.

 
BDO Unibank - FY25 beats, on lower provisions, tax expenses
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

NPAT P87.2bn, up 6%; 4Q25 up 13% on lower credit costs, taxes

FY2025 NPAT came in at P87.2bn (before prefs), up 6% YoY slightly ahead of our forecast (P86.7bn) and consensus (P85.7bn). For 4Q25 alone, profit was up 13% YoY and 7% QoQ to P24.1bn. Loan growth was sustained at 13% (Fig 1), with NIMs steady QoQ. PPOP was up 4% YoY in FY25, falling a tad short of our expectations. Bottomline was supported by lower provision expense (-5% YoY in 4Q25), despite higher NPLs, and lower tax expenses YoY.

 

NIMs flat on loan mix; Fees hit by competition; Opex growth moderating

Loan growth was driven by higher-yield consumer (+17.9% YoY) and mid-market (+13.1%) segments. Despite the better mix, loan yields dropped 14bps QoQ (Fig 3) on falling benchmark rates. NIMs were steady (-1bp QoQ, Fig 2), bolstered by strong CASA growth (+4% QoQ) keeping funding costs low (Fig 4). Fee income was soft, down 14% YoY in 4Q, driven by intensifying competition in capital markets, and in payments (fintechs/digibanks driving merchant discounts down).

Opex growth moderated in 4Q (+7%), though management guided for high single-digit to low double-digits growth in FY26E on continued investments in branches and IT spending.

 

Lower credit costs – despite higher NPLs – boost bottomline

Provisions of P15bn in FY25 were lower than our forecasts. 4Q25 alone was down 5% YoY and 8% QoQ, as credit cost fell to 40bps from 45bps in 3Q (Fig 5), despite a slight uptick in NPL ratio to P1.83%, from 1.77% in 3Q (Fig 6). Given the higher share of consumer loans, management guided for higher credit costs in 2026E.

 

Back down to 1.0x P/bk, maintain Buy

The stock rebounded from its November low, but has since weakened again, and is back down to 1.0x P/bk on our 2026E estimates. We reiterate our Buy call on the stock. Our target price of P200/sh – based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.0%, COE of 12.0% and growth of 8.0% - implies a total potential return of 52%, including a 3.3% dividend yield.

FY25 profits up +10%; Retail business sees healthy 4Q bounce

SM Investments delivered FY25 profit of P90.5bn (+10%), which would be ahead of Regis (P87bn) and Street (P88bn) estimates. The implied 4Q of P26.5bn would be 21% higher YoY, representing an acceleration from the +6% growth for 9M25. Retail business recovers from soft 3Q

SM Retail reported revenues of P458bn for FY25, up +5% YoY, broadly the same pace as 9M25. For the year, it was the Specialty Retail segment that underperformed, with profits there contracting 19% for the year in contrast to the Dept Store which saw profits rise +30% in 4Q and +11% in FY25.

Food Retail: Revenues up 7% in 4Q on SSSG of 3%

For the year, revenues for Food Retail rose 7% and income grew by a similar amount. Management said SSSG across Food Retail was +3.3% for 4Q, an appreciable, if modest, acceleration from 3Q (+2%). All sub-segments within Food Retail saw positive SSSG for the last quarter. The closely watched Alfamart business saw SSSG improve to +1% in 4Q (vs -5% in 3Q) and ended the year with 2,309 stores (net increase of +217 over FY25).

 

Increasing capital returns

The company declared that it will be pursuing both (1) increased dividends and (2) its share buyback program at the same time. Recall that SM announced a P60bn share buyback last year, with close to 10% of that amount expended so far. While the amount remains small relative to its share price (<2%), SM did increase its dividend to P13/share last year (from P9/share in FY24) and we reckon it plans to increase this further in FY26.

 
Maynilad - Interim OCP rises 12%; Dividends beat
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 profit up 19% to P15.2bn – Ahead of estimates

Maynilad’s FY25 profit came in at P15.2bn, up 19% YoY and beating both consensus and Regis estimates. We attribute the outperformance solely on lower-than-expected interest expenses, which surprisingly fell 13% QoQ/15% YoY in 4Q25 alone after declining 5% YoY in 9M25. Management said there are re-pricing opportunities to lower its cost of funding, but we will have to see if the 4Q interest savings can be sustained. That said, reduced funding costs should boost our valuation.

 

Spending up 4%; Interim OCP up 12% to P163bn

Cash spending (capex + opex) at the West Zone rose 4% YoY to P38bn in 2025, taking cumulative spending since 2023 to P111bn, or half of the P220bn RY2023-2027 commitment. The company also disclosed that the regulatory value of its concession (a.k.a. interim “cash position”) was P163bn, +12% YoY and +63% from end-2022. 

This means the regulatory value has been growing at a compounded rate of 18% over the current regulatory period. Considering 2026 capex earmarked at P30bn (+12% YoY), tariff adjustments (+3%) as well as muted billed volume growth, we anticipate the OCP could rise by ~15% this year.

 

Declares P1.14/sh dividend, higher than forecast

The company also declared a dividend of P1.14/share, above our forecast of P0.97/sh and equivalent to a payout ratio of 56%, above the company’s minimum policy. In absolute amounts, this years’ dividend was at P8.4bn, 32% more than the previous year. The dividend goes ex- on March 6 and will be paid on March 18. Finally, Maynilad’s gross gearing stood at 0.8x while net gearing was at 0.6x in end-2025, providing ample room for higher payouts.

 
Manila Water - Capital returns further improve; East Zone spending trails commitment
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Profit shortfall? Dividends beat Street estimates

Manila Water (MWC) reported FY25 profit before the share of participating prefs (PPS) of P15.8bn, up 51% YoY. Removing one-offs and the share of PPS, we estimate recurring profit to common rose 6% to P13.2bn, which appears below consensus estimate of P14.6bn. Notwithstanding the earnings shortfall, MWC announced dividends of P2/sh, beating the Street’s P1.87/sh estimate and lifting the payout ratio to 39% (vs. 38% last year), above its minimum policy of 35%.

 

East Zone spending flat in FY25; End-2025 OCP estimate down to <P20bn

The East Zone delivered 15%/9% improvements in EBITDA/profit in FY25. However, cash spending (capex + opex) was flat at P32bn, bringing cumulative spend since 2023 to P89bn—about half of its P175bn RY2023–2027 commitment. Management estimates its interim OCP declined to <P20bn by end-2025 from the regulator-approved P28bn OCP in end-2022. We believe the OCP decline raises the risk of a tariff clawback in the next rebasing.

 

Wawa bulk water acquisition completed in Dec but financing deferred to Jan

WawaJV Co., the bulk water operator, was turned over to MWC last December 15 and contributed P101mn to profits.

However, the drawdown of the P27bn loan for the final installment should weigh on earnings over the near-to medium-term.

 

More on acquisition of Mexican water concessionaire, Agua de Puebla

Management revealed that it is exploring fully acquiring Agua de Puebla. While we estimate the initial acquisition cost of MXN3.625bn (~P12bn) for a 75% stake equates to a valuation of ~20x 2024 EV/EBITDA, significantly higher than MWC’s multiple, the operational issues (e.g. water supply issues, high leakage levels, and regulatory issues) of Agua de Puebla are well within MWC’s core competencies, and a successful turnaround could boost shareholder value. MWC is aiming to complete the acquisition of the 75% stake within the year.

 

Parent gross gearing at 1.4x after Wawa

After collecting the remaining P5.4bn subscription receivable from its parent, Trident Water, last December, gross gearing dipped to 1.1x in end-2025 (vs. 1.2x in 2024) but has climbed to 1.4x after the Wawa acquisition loan. This level remains below the 3x covenant limit.

 
Megaworld - Residential sales fall 12% in FY25; management guides for better FY26
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY25 EBIT rises 4%

Megaworld’s FY25 headline profit rose 12% (to P21bn) while its EBIT increased 4% to P28.6bn, in line with REGIS but behind consensus forecast. 4Q25 residential revenue fell 12% QoQ and YoY due to lower revenue recognition from ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units.

 

Residential sales fall 12% in FY25; management more optimistic in FY26

4Q25 reservation sales fell 19% QoQ (down 7% YoY) to P27.7bn, partly because the company’s recently launched P8bn luxury project was fully sold in 3Q25. For the year, reservation sales declined 12% to P116.5bn. Management is more optimistic about residential demand in 2026, particularly for provincial areas. It targets to grow reservation sales by 7% (to P125bn) and plans to launch P65bn worth of projects this year (from P21bn in 2025). It also reinstituted RFO promotions this year, after removing them in 2025.

Office occupancy remains stable QoQ but could drop in 2026

4Q25 office revenue was flat QoQ as occupancy remained stable (at 87%). However, the occupancy might drop since the company has yet to sign up any tenants for the 90k sqm due for completion in 2026. Meanwhile, rental reversions turned slightly negative in 4Q, from slightly positive in 9M25. Although contracts in Fort Bonifacio were generally renewed at higher rental rates, contracts in other areas were mostly renewed at lower rental rates. Management expects office rental reversions to turn positive again in 2026.

 

Mall revenue remains flat in 4Q25

Despite 4Q25 mall occupancy improving to 95% (from 93% in 3Q25/4Q24), mall revenue was flat QoQ and YoY. The company attributes this to weak sales at high-end restaurants, which were likely affected by the corruption scandal. Mall revenue should improve from here as the rent-free periods of new tenants lapse.

Inflation accelerates to 2.4% in February

Headline inflation picked up to 2.4% in Feb from Jan's 2.0%. This was in line with consensus expectations of 2.4% and within the lower-end of the BSP's forecast range of 2.3-3.1% for Feb. The 2.4% print is the highest since Jan 2025 (2.9%) and marks the third consecutive increase in inflation as favorable base effects begin to wane. This brings 2M25 inflation to 2.2%, within the BSP's long-term target range of 2-4% and below their forecast of 3.6% for the year. National Statistician Claire Dennis Mapa guided that inflation may further pick up in the coming months as higher fuel prices impact other basket items. Inflation in Feb accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Jan in food and utilities (electricity, gas, fuel). Food prices overall rose 1.8% YoY, accelerating from the 1.1% pace in Jan, driven by higher prices of fish and vegetables. Rice prices are down 3.3% YoY in Feb, the fourteenth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. Core inflation inched up slightly to 2.9% in Feb from 2.8% in Jan, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 2.9% in Feb from 2.7% in Jan. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 3.6%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.7% and 3.0%. The second out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

Trade deficit narrows to $4.0bn in Jan

The Jan trade deficit narrowed to US$4.0bn, contracting 18% YoY, reflecting the growth of exports and the decline in imports. Imports for the month fell by 3% YoY to US$11.1bn, a reversal from the 7% growth in Dec. The reversal was primarily driven by the drop in fuel imports (-25%) and raw and intermediate goods (-8%). Notably, imports of consumer goods fell 6%, the fifth consecutive month of a YoY decline—the longest streak since the pandemic in 2020. Exports rose by 8% YoY to US$7.1bn, the thirteenth consecutive month of YoY growth but moderating from the 23% increase in Dec. Electronics exports jumped by 19% YoY to $4.0bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 3% to US$3.1bn.

Budget deficit declines by 5% YoY to P313bn in December; FY25 deficit reaches P1.58trn (+5% YoY)

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P313bn in Dec, 5% narrower YoY as gov't spending continued to decline. Revenue collections in Dec were down 3% YoY as the 11% growth in tax collections were offset by the dip in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were up by 11%. Recall that BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza stated that the slowdown in consumer and gov't spending (i.e., pause in payments for infra-related gov't contracts) led to lower-than-expected collections this year. Customs collections rose 10%. The Bureau of Customs (BOC) noted VAT and excise collections as the primary drivers for collections growth alongside strengthened enforcement measures and monitoring of import declarations. Non-tax revenues fell 59% given the recognition of one-off Dec 2024 collections of (a) PPP concession fee (P30bn), as well as fund transfers (167bn) from PhilHealth and Philippine Deposit Insurance (PDIC). Expenditures continued to fall (-4% YoY) in Dec due to the decrease in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 5%. Since July 2025, government spending has continued to decline YoY, including in Dec, amid the pause and slowdown in infrastructure projects following the flood control scandal. Interest payments rose 10% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. This brings the FY25 deficit to P1.58trn, 5% wider YoY and came in mildly higher than the P1.56tn full-year target. Despite the primary underspending, the budget deficit widened more than expected due to revenue shortfalls. FY25 deficit-to-GDP ratio mechanically improved to 5.63% from 5.70% in FY24 but still came in short of the 5.5% target.

Government debt rises to P18.13trn in end-Jan

The government's total debt stock went up by 2.4% MoM and 11% YoY to P18.13trn as of end-Jan. SEC issues draft rules to limit terms of broker directors of PSE

The SEC issued a draft memorandum circular regarding the term limit of broker directors of the Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE PM). The draft rules would limit broker-directors to a maximum cumulative period of 10 years. After serving a cumulative five years, a broker director would need to observe a two-year cooling off period. If the draft rules were to be implemented, four of the current five broker directors would no longer be eligible. Ma. Vivian Yuchangco – has been broker director for 28 years. Eddie Gobing – has been broker director for 25 years. Wilson Sy – has been broker director for 12 years. Diosdado Arroyo – has served for 6 consecutive years. Anthony Te would be the only current broker director who would be eligible for another term. The SEC said “In order to develop our capital markets, there is need to ensure fair and effective representation in an exchange and give qualified brokers opportunities to serve and provide new perspectives in the board of an exchange,”. At least 51 percent of the 15-member board must be non-brokers-- five independent directors and four directors representing the interests of issuers, investors and other market participants, with each sector having at least one representative.
