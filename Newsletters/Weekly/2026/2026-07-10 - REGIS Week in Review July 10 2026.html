REGIS REPORT

10 July 2026

The PCOMP extended its rally to another week, testing the 6,300-mark again as geopolitical noise took a backseat to inflation printing lower in June and foreign inflows coming back to the Philippines. Investors largely shrugged off skirmishes at the Strait of Hormuz and instead pinned hopes that peace talks between the US and Iran continue to progress. And while oil prices headed higher for the week, the index managed to finish in the green, up 1.6% or 98.67 pts WoW to 6,286.70.

 

June CPI eased further to 6.4% YoY, coming in slightly below the consensus median forecast of 6.5% and well within the BSP's 6.0-7.0% forecast range. Inflation also moderated from 6.8% in May, as lower global oil prices filtered through to softer Food inflation (5.2% from 5.7%) and Transport inflation (12.8% from 16.2%). This took average inflation for 1H26 to 4.8%, well below current full-year projections of 6.0% from consensus and 6.4% from the central bank. BSP Gov. Eli Remolona said the economy can withstand another rate hike, with growth seen to recover in 2H26 on the back of stronger government spending despite a softer first half. His remarks come ahead of the BSP's next policy meeting on August 27th, with the policy rate currently at 4.75%.

 

ICT (+7.9% WoW) once again emerged as the best-performing index name lifted by strong foreign inflow, followed by CNPF (+5.5% WoW) that continued to rally from its end-May lows despite a broadly lower consumer sector for the week. Meanwhile, even amid rising rate expectations, ALI (+3.6% WoW) outperformed amid sustained bargain hunting after parent AC (-1.0% WoW) disclosed it has allocated P5bn to purchase ALI shares.

 

Thematically, banks took center stage with the BSP's recent memo aimed at reducing electronic fund transfer fees prompting most large commercial banks along with leading digibanks and e-wallets to slash fees by at least a third for some, and to completely waive fees to zero for most. BPI (+2.0% WoW) which was the first to slash transfer fees to zero led gainers in a mixed sector for the week.

 

At the opposite end, CNVRG (-7.1% WoW) remained under pressure as investors continued to position for the August PSEi rebalancing, with the stock widely viewed as the leading candidate for index deletion. Investors also continued to rotate out of PGOLD (-5.0% WoW) given its relative outperformance within the consumer sector for the year.

 

Off-index, RRHI (-19.1% WoW) concluded the tender offer conducted by JE Holdings, the holding company of the Gokongwei family, which would result in their ownership of 99.69% of RRHI's outstanding shares. RRHI is expected to proceed with its voluntary delisting after exceeding the 95% ownership threshold required by the PSE.

 

In local politics, the impeachment trial of VP Sara Duterte began Monday afternoon at the Senate. The outcome will determine whether she will be acquitted or perpetually disqualified from holding public office, which would end her 2028 presidential bid.

 

Foreigners were net buyers by $59m this week -- the highest weekly foreign inflow recorded since the Iran war broke out. The peso slightly weakened by 0.1% WoW to 61.515:$1, while the PH10Y yield rose 11.5 bps to 7.2634%.

Philippine Banks/Finance - BPI pre-empts BSP rules – sets zero fees for InstaPay, PesoNet transfers
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

BSP sought to lower fund transfer fees; BPI went straight to zero

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas issued Circular 1238 and Memorandum 2026-026 aimed at sharply reducing fees charge by banks and electronic money issuers (EMIs, or e-wallets) on electronic fund transfers (EFTs). On 30 June, BPI (BPI PM) announced that it was making all transfers over InstaPay and PesoNet free, preempting what was likely to become a “race to zero”. BPI estimates annualized EFT fees of about P2.2bn, roughly 1.1% of its 2025 revenues and 2.6% of pre-tax profit. Some banks and e-wallets have followed, cutting or eliminating fees. It is unclear, though, how much of a profit hit they will take. To the upside, digital adoption could accelerate further, making up for lost EFT revenues.

 

BSP issues new rules for P2P transfer fees charged by banks, e-wallets

The BSP has for years tried to lower EFT fees, and in late 2021 issued a moratorium on fee hikes. The new rules require banks to justify the gap between “on-us” payments (ie within the same bank/EMI), and “off-us” transactions (ie outward from one bank/EMI to another), contending that one set of customers should not “unreasonably” subsidize another. As such, the price difference between an on-us and an off-us transaction should approximate only the costs directly associated with the off-us payment, such as switch fees, etc.

 

Most, if not all, institutions waive fees for internal, on-us transfers. However, as of May, banks still charged as much as P25 per transaction for InstaPay, while e-wallets’ rates went as high as P35 (about $0.57). InstaPay is the off-us rail for real-time, smaller-ticket payments. PesoNet is for larger, batched transactions. 

In our view, InstaPay is much more relevant – it makes up just over half of EFTs by value, but 99% by number of transactions. The market share data of bank/e-wallets for InstaPay and PesoNet are not public.

 

BPI leads “race to zero”; other banks, e-wallets cut too

BPI surprised markets by waiving InstaPay and PesoNet fees across all its digital platforms starting 01 July. Since then, RCBC (RCB PM) also offered zero fees, but with conditions. The BSP expects more banks to follow. Meanwhile, two of the largest e-wallets cut their P2P InstaPay fees from P15 to P10 on 4-6 July.

 

Accelerating digital adoption to offset foregone EFT fees?

BPI estimates that it is foregoing P2.2bn in fees on an annualized basis, roughly 1.1% of its 2025 revenues and 2.6% of pre-tax profit. However, they reckon the BSP rules would have drastically reduced that fee anyway. Meanwhile, zero fees generates significant goodwill, which could lead to increased activity from current customers (CASA, other fees), and potentially increase customer count.

 

Outside of BPI, we do not have estimates of how significant EFT fees are to other banks and e-wallets.

 

Digital adoption has skyrocketed in recent years. InstaPay and PesoNet payments combined for P2.8trn (c$45bn) in May up 41% YoY, tracking more than 100% of nominal GDP (P28trn in 2025). This does NOT include “on-us” transfers. There is clear potential upside for financial institutions that are prepared to capitalize on further acceleration of digital financial inclusion.

 
Robinsons Retail - Delisting imminent
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

RRHI set to voluntarily delist as tender offer threshold reached

Robinsons Retail Holdings, Inc. announced that the company is set to delist from the PSE, following the conclusion of the tender offer conducted by JE Holdings (Gokongwei family holdco) from May 25 to July 6. JE Holdings was able to secure 229.6mn tendered shares. As a result, the Gokongwei family’s stake (1.06bn shares) now represents 99.69% of RRHI’s outstanding shares, well above the 95% minimum threshold required to voluntarily delist.

 

The tendered shares will be crossed on July 13. The company will then request for approval from the PSE for the voluntary delisting of RRHI’s common shares.

 

Tender offer price of P48.30/sh plus P2.00/share dividend

JE Holdings was able to reach the 95% threshold without having to adjust the P48.30/sh offer price.

That offer price was 23% higher than the last closing price immediately before the tender offer announcement. If we account for the P2.00/sh dividend paid on June 10 to shareholders record as of May 28, the effective consideration amounts to P50.30/sh, a tad higher than the P50.00/sh buyback price paid for the Dairy Farm group’s 22% stake.

 

Taken private after prolonged undervaluation

The P48.30/share consideration values the company at 8.3x FY26 PE which is much lower than both our implied 26E valuation of 14.3x PE and the historical average. Although the offer may not have fully reflected what we believe to be RRHI's intrinsic value, for third party shareholders, the subdued market sentiment likely made accepting the offer more compelling than waiting for a potential re-rating. The prolonged market undervaluation of RRHI’s shares handed its controlling shareholders an opportunity to consolidate ownership and complete the delisting at a relatively modest valuation.

Inflation moderates further to 6.4% in June

Headline inflation moderated to 6.4% in Jun from May's 6.8%. This was in line with consensus expectations of 6.5% and within the BSP's forecast range of 6.0-7.0% for Jun. The softer inflation print is the second consecutive month of deceleration following five consecutive months of accelerating inflation. This brings 6M26 inflation to 4.8%, above the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) but still below their forecast of 6.4% for the year. Inflation in Jun decelerated given slower YoY increases vs May in food and transport prices. Food prices overall rose 5.2% YoY, decelerating from the 5.7% pace in May, driven by lower prices of meat and lower YoY increases in fish and rice which offset higher vegetable prices. Rice prices jumped 14.9% YoY in Jun, moderating slightly from the 15.6% YoY increase in May. Rice prices are currently at P49.5/kg and P48.0/kg as of end-Jun for local and imported well-milled rice, with prices stable relative to the previous month. Transport prices rose 12.8% YoY, slower than +16.2% the previous month. The increase in diesel prices slowed to +39.0% YoY from +58.5% YoY, while the gasoline price increase likewise moderated to +39.2% YoY from +51.6% YoY in May. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, picked up to 4.4% in Jun from 4.1% in May, driven by both core food (3.3% from 2.9% in May) and core non-food (4.6% from 4.4%). The 4.4% core inflation print is the highest since Dec 2023. The BSP in a statement highlighted that the acceleration in core inflation indicates persistent underlying inflationary pressures despite moderation in headline inflation. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.4% (previously revised from 6.3%). The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Aug 27. The current policy rate is 4.75%.

Philippine economy can absorb one more rate hike—BSP Governor

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the economy can take another rate hike as growth is expected to recover in 2H26. Remolona said that there is still room for a rebound as a significant portion of the gov't's spending program has yet to be disbursed given delays due to the flood control scandal. Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan in a separate interview stated that gov't spending is expected to pick up in the second half as infra implementation accelerates. He also added that putting inflation under control and driving economic growth through reforms (e.g., education, health, digitalization, transparent governance etc.) are key in order to maintain the country's investment-grade credit rating. The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled this year will be held on Aug 27. The current policy rate is 4.75%. The gov't's GDP growth targets for 26/27/28E were reduced to 3.5-4.5%/5.0-6.0%/5.0-6.0% from 5.0-6.0%/5.5%-6.5%/6.0-7.0%. To reach the lower and upper ends of the FY26 growth target band, GDP growth needs to average 3.7% and 5.1%, respectively, for the rest of the year.

PH dollar reserves at $104.8bn as of end-Jun

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Jun increased 0.8% MoM to US$104.8bn. The June GIR figure is a three-month high. The MoM increase in the GIR was primarily due to the national gov't's higher net foreign currency deposits with the BSP which offset the downward valuation adjustments to the BSP's gold holdings. The significant increase in foreign exchange (foreign currency and deposits) was likely related to the gov't's recent US$2.5bn global bond issuance in June. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 6.8x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$104bn, lower than the US$110.8bn in 2025.

Loan growth accelerates in May; NPLs up, but PDs improve

Bank lending growth accelerated to 12.5% YoY in May, a 16-month high, based on data excluding interbank loans (IBLs) and reverse repos (RRPs). Growth was driven by corporate or "loans for production," which increased 11.4% YoY, with Mining (+48%), Utilities (+30-33%), and Transport (+23%) posting the strongest gains, while consumer loans (excluding mortgages) slowed to 19% YoY. The banking system's NPL ratio rose 8bps MoM to 3.63%, up 5bps YoY. In contrast, the Past Due (PD) ratio declined 8bps MoM to 4.60%, though it remained 12bps higher YoY. Restructured loans were flat at 2.10%, while NPL coverage declined to 89%, falling below 90% for the first time since March 2022. Construction continued to record the weakest asset quality, with an NPL ratio of 14.55% and a PD ratio of 16.47%. Higher delinquencies were also recorded in the Trade, Real Estate, and Hotels/Restaurants sectors. Within consumer lending, credit card NPLs rose to 5.32% (+17bps MoM, +55bps YoY), while PDs increased to 7.85% (+14bps MoM, +54bps YoY).

Gov't lifts excise tax suspension on kerosene and LPG

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) said excise tax rates on kerosene and LPG have automatically reverted to their original rates of P5.60/liter and P3.36/kg, respectively, as the average Dubai crude oil price for June was below the US$80/pb threshold. This was made effective on July 8 and came ahead of the July 17 lapse of the tax suspension. Recall that Executive Order (EO) 114 was issued on April 16 to temporarily suspend excise taxes on specific petroleum products for three months to mitigate the impact of rising oil prices. As of end-June, kerosene and LPG prices have fallen to around P98.5/liter and P1,150-1,300 per 11-kg cylinder, respectively, from P154.6/liter and P1,600 per cylinder as of April 16.

Dept. of Energy proposes amendments to Oil Deregulation Law

The Dept. of Energy (DOE) formally recommended amendments to the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act of 1988 through the implementation of safeguards such as establishing a national fuel reserve (equivalent to at least 60 days of supply) and increasing minimum inventory requirement for oil companies from 15 to 30 days. DOE Secretary Sharon Garin, however, clarified that the proposal does not include changes to how fuel prices are set. She added that Congress will ultimately decide what amendments should be included in the revised law as well as whether the gov't should have more control in regulating fuel prices.

VP Sara Duterte impeachment trial starts

The proceedings for the impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte began Monday afternoon with the outcome determining whether she will be acquitted or perpetually be disqualified from holding public office and ending her 2028 presidential bid. Conviction requires the votes of at least two-thirds (16) of the Senate. The first day of trial focused on opening statements and procedural matters with no witnesses or presentation of evidence yet. The proceedings will start with allegations of Duterte's threats against President Marcos then charges involving confidential funds, bribery and unexplained wealth. The prosecution team stated that they are expecting and preparing for the defense's possible bid to dismiss the case. Spokesperson of the prosecution panel, Robert Ace Barbers, said that the team would also seek Duterte's perpetual disqualification from public office if she resigns before the end of the trial.

Ex-DILG Chief Benhur Abalos appointed as Cabinet Secretary

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has appointed former Dept. of Interior and Local Government (DILG) Secretar Benhur Abalos Jr. as Cabinet Secretary. Abalos previously served as DILG Secretary from 2022-2024 prior to being replaced by current Interior Secretary Jonvic Remulla. Abalos' responsibilities as Cabinet Secretary include assisting the President with "timely, relevant, and strategic advice on emerging national, local, and international issues affecting the political landscape". He will also serve as Marcos' liaison to national gov't agencies, LGUs, political organizations, sectoral groups of on matters of political concern, among others. Recall that President Marcos previously abolished the Office of the Cabinet Secretary back in July 2022 to remove "duplicated and overlapping official functions".
