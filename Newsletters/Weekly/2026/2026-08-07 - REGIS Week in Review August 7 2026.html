REGIS REPORT

07 August 2026

Philippine equities extended their gains for a second straight week as easing July inflation and a round of broadly in-line 2Q corporate results outweighed the weaker-than-expected 2Q GDP print. The PCOMP rose 0.9% or 53.91 pts WoW to 6,290.35, taking its YTD advance to 3.9%.

 

The data-heavy week concluded with Friday's 2Q GDP release, which yet again missed expectations. The 2.3% was a marked slowdown from 2.8% in 1Q and hugely missed forecasts for an improvement in the quarter to 2.9%. Growth was weighed down by weaker domestic consumption (at 2.8% from 3.0% prev.) and a deeper contraction in investment (at -9.2% from -3.1%) amid the prolonged Middle East conflict and elevated energy prices. As a result, the big banks BDO (-2.0% WoW) and BPI (-1.5% WoW) as the larger macro proxies underperformed for the week.

 

On the other hand, July inflation out by mid-week surprised to the upside, easing to 6.2% from 6.4% in June and also better than the 6.4% consensus forecast, mainly as Transport CPI continued to drop (at 11.9% from 12.8% prev.). This is the third straight month of declining inflation after the recent peak of 7.2% in April, although the full-year consensus forecast is still at 5.9% against the 5.0% YTD-July average.

 

Nonetheless, consumer names generally outperformed for the week thanks in part to softer consumer prices, led by manufacturers URC (+4.7% WoW), CNPF (+4.0% WoW), and MONDE (+2.7% WoW). Both URC and CNPF also reported 2Q results this week, both coming in-line with forecasts although with flat volume growth for URC's domestic branded business and high-single- to low-double-digit growth for CNPF, highlighting divergent performance in the discretionary vs staples categories. Beyond earnings, URC's dividend declaration translating to over 7% yield drove the stock's advance.

 

Also driven by 2Q results, ICT (+3.8% WoW) continued its outperformance after reporting in-line numbers with robust 26% core earnings growth as strength in the Americas and Asia more than offset weaker contributions from EMEA. Off-index, mining was the strongest sector led by AT (+18.7% WoW), PX (+10.1% WoW), and NIKL (+8.8% WoW) lifted by rallying metal prices.

 

Foreigners reverted to net sellers for the week by $15m, while the peso strengthened 0.6% WoW to 60.910:$1. The PH10Y yield eased by 16bps to 7.2703%.

by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1H26 core profit up 26% to US$600mn – In line with Street, Regis estimates

ICT reported net income of US$590mn (+22% YoY) in 1H26. Excluding the FX charge related to the divestment of its China port in 1Q26 and removing perps distributions, recurring net income would have reached US$600mn, up 26% YoY. We view the results as broadly in line with expectations. In 2Q26 alone, profit rose 23% to US$295mn, driven by strong performance of its ports in the Americas, robust yield growth in Asia, and the accretive contributions of newly acquired ports. These factors more than compensated for weaker contributions from EMEA, particularly its operations in Iraq.

 

Mexico expansion fuels +25% revenue growth, offsetting pressures in EMEA

Revenues increased 25% YoY to US$959mn in 2Q26 with yield per TEU remaining strong at US$232 (+10%), underpinned by a +30% YoY improvement in organic revenues in the Americas and resilient yield expansion in Asia. Throughput in the Americas surged +18% in the quarter, partly reflecting the capacity expansion in Mexico and strong Asia trade. 

However, these strong results were tempered by the impact of the Iran conflict on its Iraq port and persistent competitive weakness at its ports in Poland and Croatia.

 

EBITDA margin sequentially softer at 64% on Iraq, higher fuel costs

EBITDA sequentially softened to US$614mn (+23% YoY) in 2Q. Even though we estimate its new ports improved margins QoQ, overall margins still softened 30bps QoQ and 150bps YoY to 64%, reflecting the full-quarter impact of the Hormuz closure on its Iraq port and higher fuel costs. NPAT P24.9bn, flat YoY; provisions could normalize in 2H

MBT reported 1H26 NPAT of P24.9bn, flat YoY and in line with our FY forecast. Strong loan growth (12.4%) and wider NIMs (+9bps QoQ) were weighed down by weak trading gains given the impact of higher interest rates on the bank’s large securities book. Loan growth accelerates on corporate demand; NIMs up 9bps QoQ / 5bps YoY

This was offset, though by weak trading/fx income as the steepening yield curve did not help the bank’s large securities book.

 

Credit costs jump on ECL changes; NPLs steady, but watch out for credit cards

Provision expense jumped 27% YoY in 1H, with 2Q credit costs spiking to 78bps (Fig 3) as the bank adjusted assumptions in its ECL models to account for slower growth and higher inflation. The bank maintained its 65-70bps FY credit cost guidance, noting that NPL ratio was stable (Fig 4) and NPL Cover remained high at 133%. The bank noted rising delinquencies rising in both the auto and credit card segments (Fig 5&6), but maintained 150% NPL Cover for credit cards.

 

Stock at 0.68x P/bk, 5.9x PE, 7.5% dividend yield

We maintain our Buy on the stock on valuations, as it currently trades on 0.68x P/bk, and 5.9x PER, while paying a 7.5% dividend yield on our 2026E forecasts. Our P100/sh target price is based on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 12.25%, COE of 12% and growth of 8%. Buy.

 
Jollibee - Under pressure
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Revising TP to P220/sh but maintaining Buy

Jollibee Foods Corp. (JFC PM) shares have fallen 33% in the past twelve months on the back of disappointing results. A sharp contraction in margins due to higher input costs led to a 40% drop in 1Q profit. We expect margins to continue facing pressure due to elevated oil prices, a record wage hike, and an impending El Niño event. We slash our profit estimates for FY26/27E by 21/24% and reduce our TP by 12% to P220/sh as we roll forward our valuation. Near-term headwinds aside, we believe that JFC at current valuations offers value, trading at 16x FY26E PER on a 3-year EPS CAGR of 20%.

 

Near-term pressures

JFC’s margins slumped in 1Q as it held back price increases amidst rising costs for both the domestic and international business. The company has since put through staggered price hikes and slashed marketing spend to recoup margins, but costs will likely continue to exert margin pressure due to the impact of the Middle East conflict, the record minimum wage hike for Metro Manila and a potentially severe El Niño event this year.

The company’s 2Q performance should give us glimpse of how things are likely to turn out for the rest of the year. We expect profits this year to be largely unchanged YoY but forecast a 3-year EPS CAGR of 20% on 11% revenue CAGR thereafter.

 

Spin-off of international operations will be challenging

The planned spinoff and listing of JFC’s international businesses by late 2027 will depend on how well that segment performs. Whether the international business can perform well enough to be spun off and listed by 4Q27 is by no means certain. While JFC has not altered its timetable for this initiative, we have our reservations about the readiness of the int’l operations by next year.

 

Refreshing estimates and TP but maintaining Buy rating

We revise our forecasts for the latest results and reduce our target price by 12% to P220/sh in line with our revised SOTP valuation. We slightly adjust our topline estimates (-1-2%) but slash profit forecasts by 21-24% on lower margins. The TP implies a 27E PER multiple of 19.7x (below historical average), and the stock also provides a modest 3% yield at the latest closing price.

2Q26 headline net profit +3% to P2.0bn, revenues +16% to P22.8bn

In 2Q26 alone, consolidated revenue growth remained robust at 16% YoY from 15% the previous quarter, propelled by both Branded (+14%) and OEM (+21%) segments. Group revenue growth has been resilient, sustaining a 15–16% YoY growth rate over the past four quarters.

 

Branded growth remains solid but…

Sales picked up with healthy trends across all subsegments, primarily led by “Milk and Others”. Milk saw “high-teens” growth, while Marine and Meat posted “mid-single digit” growth in the quarter.

 

Management, however, cited a demand slowdown in July relative to 2Q but cautioned that it remains too early to determine the broader demand trend given the uncertain macro and geopolitical environment.

OEM sales (19% of 2Q revenue) remained robust, increasing 21% YoY vs 32% in 1Q, given solid demand in both tuna and coconut.

Looking ahead, management expects the FY26 GPM to normalize toward the FY25 level (25.1%) as rising logistics costs and management reluctance to raise prices further limits margin upside.

Notwithstanding looming headwinds, CNPF maintained its aspiration of delivering double-digit growth this year. Revenue growth is expected to be in the “low to mid-teens” driven by both volume and price, while profit is guided to grow “low double-digit”, supported by measured price increases to mitigate the impact of higher input and distribution costs.

 

The company also kept its elevated P8bn capex target (roughly double its usual annual spend) to expand production capacity underscoring the company’s confidence in its long-term growth path.

Philippine Power - A trigger for change?
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

“A crisis is a terrible thing to waste”

The pronouncement by President Marcos, prompted by backlash against high power prices, that consumers should no longer pay for electricity system losses delivered a jolt to the electricity industry. However, having distribution utilities (DU) absorb systems losses is, in our view, impracticable and will have significant negative unintended consequences. The current brouhaha should nonetheless result in a long overdue examination on why electricity prices in the Philippines are inordinately high. An overhaul of key tenets underpinning the industry could result.

 

What are systems losses?

Systems losses represent the difference between the amount of electricity a DU receives and what goes into the consumer’s household. While some of it may be due to pilferage, the laws of physics are an unavoidable part of it. Losses vary across DUs, ranging from as low as 4% to over 10% and the regulator already imposes a cap on how much a DU can pass on. Meralco’s systems loss in FY25 was close to 6%. For the DUs of Aboitiz Power, the losses range from 4% to 10%.

 

How big would the impact be to consumers? DUs?

We estimate that removing systems losses would reduce consumer power bills by a mere 3%. Forcing the DUs to absorb the entire amount, meanwhile, would reduce pretax significantly and affect the viability of the sector.

For Meralco, we estimate the hit would be over 50% for its distribution business (excluding the non-regulated operations). For Aboitiz Power, the proportion is closer to 40%, but the DU business is only a tenth of earnings. For smaller DUs, however, the impact could be existential.

 

“Investigations” coming

A number of legislators have either filed bills to stop letting consumers “pay” for the systems losses or called for investigations into this matter. We believe an honest examination of this specific issue will result in the conclusion that one cannot force DUs to absorb these losses without significant, damaging consequences. Thus, we believe the DUs will NOT end up absorbing these systems losses (at least not without any “compensation”).

 

Regulatory status quo at risk

This doesn’t mean the DUs will emerge unscathed. An examination into why power rates in the Philippines are high is likely to lead to a review of the “regulatory status quo”. Meralco end-user rates today (US$0.24) are comparable to Singapore (US$0.27), whose GDP/capita is over 20x the Philippines’.

 

At risk from such a review, in our view, would be the rather … “imperfect” rate-setting framework for DUs as well as the affiliate gencos of the DUs.

Philippine 2Q26 GDP growth slows to 2.3%, falls short of expectations

Philippine GDP grew 2.3% YoY in 2Q26, slowing from 2.8% in 1Q26 and missing consensus expectations of 2.9%. This marked the weakest pace of economic growth since 4Q09, excluding the pandemic period. Growth was weighed down by weaker domestic consumption and investment amid the prolonged Middle East conflict and elevated energy prices, with household consumption slowing to 2.8% from 3.0% and investment contracting 9.2% versus a 3.1% decline in the previous quarter. Government spending accelerated to 8.3% from 4.8%, while exports strengthened to 12.2% from 7.8% and import growth moderated to 5.5% from 6.8%. The government had recently lowered its 2026 GDP growth target to 3.5-4.5% from 5%-6%, which implies the economy will need to grow by at least 4.4% in 2H26 to meet the lower end of the target range. Note too that the government has announced additional cash aid, fuel subsidies, and tax relief measures to support households and small businesses.

Inflation moderates further to 6.2% in July

Headline inflation moderated to 6.2% in Jul from Jun's 6.4%. This was within the BSP's forecast range of 5.6-6.6% for Jul. The softer inflation print is the third consecutive month of deceleration following five consecutive months of accelerating inflation. This brings 7M26 inflation to 5.0%, above the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) but still below their forecast of 6.4% for the year. Inflation in Jul decelerated given primarily driven by the slower YoY increase in fuel prices vs Jun. Food prices overall rose 5.2% YoY, unchanged relative to May, as lower prices of meat were offset by higher rice prices. Rice prices climbed 17.0% YoY in Jul, picking up from the 15.0% YoY increase in Jun. Rice prices are currently at P48.5/kg and P47.9/kg as of end-Jul for local and imported well-milled rice. Transport prices rose 11.9% YoY, slower than +12.8% the previous month. The increase in diesel prices was relatively steady at +38.5% YoY from +39.0% YoY, while the gasoline price increase moderated to +34.1% YoY from +39.2% YoY in Jun. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, eased to 4.2% in Jul from 4.4% in Jun, as the moderation in core non-food (4.4% from 4.6% in Jun) offset the acceleration in core food (3.6% from 3.3%). The BSP in a statement highlighted that the acceleration in core inflation indicates persistent underlying inflationary pressures despite moderation in headline inflation. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.4%. The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Aug 27. The current policy rate is 4.75%.

Asset quality steady in June; consumer loan delinquencies worsen

Overall banking sector asset quality improved in June, with the NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) easing to 3.55%, down 8bps QoQ and 1bp YoY, while the Past Due (PD) ratio dipped 2bps MoM and Restructured Loans (RL) improved 5bps MoM to 2.02%. NPL coverage also increased to 93% from 89% in the previous month. Total loans (ex-IBL/RRP) grew 10.5% YoY in June, slowing from 12.5% in May, while outstanding loans declined 0.6% MoM. Asset quality for production loans improved, driven by the Manufacturing and Real Estate sectors, although the Construction sector continued to deteriorate with PD rates rising to 16.50%, while Wholesale and Retail Trade also recorded higher PD and NPL ratios. Consumer loans (excluding mortgages) weakened, with the PD ratio rising 52bps MoM and the NPL ratio increasing 11bps MoM. Salary loan PD rates climbed 246bps MoM to 9.50%, with NPLs rising to 5.64%, while motorcycle loan PD rates increased 128bps MoM to 27.46%. Credit card asset quality was broadly stable MoM, with PD easing to 7.78% and NPLs to 5.28%, although both remained significantly higher than a year ago.

BSP clarifies that digital transfer fees are not required to be zero

The BSP clarified that Circular No. 1238, which was implemented on July 4, does not require financial institutions to waive digital transfer fees. FinTech Alliance PH issued a statement after its meeting with Monetary Board members. “Following constructive dialogue between the BSP Monetary Board members and the FinTech Alliance PH, it is now clear that Circular No. 1238 is a cost-based fair pricing guideline, not a zero-based mandate. Compliance does not require zero fees, nor does it limit off-us P2P electronic fund transfer fees to switch cost alone, and FAQ No. 7 should not be read as a rigid, switch-cost-only formula. Affordability remains central to scaling financial inclusion, but it must go hand in hand with safe, reliable, and sustainable digital financial services.” Fintech Alliance PH chairman Lito Villanueva said that the country’s largest banks can afford to eliminate transfer fees because they earn from a variety of revenue streams like lending and insurance. Mr. Villanueva is also EVP and Chief Innovation and Inclusion Officer of RCBC. However, e-wallets and electronic money issuers (EMIs) depend on fee-based revenues, especially transfer charges, to sustain the business. Mr. Villanueva also said fund transfers entail costs before, during and after an amount is moved digitally. E-wallets invest in compliance checks, customer authentication and fraud screening to build up the integrity and security of their platforms. Thus, the transaction fees reflect both the switching costs and infrastructure costs for customer support. FinTech Alliance PH has over 150 corporate members which include banks, electronic money issuers, and other fintech firms.

Retail industry expects single-digit growth this year

The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) projects the retail industry to grow single digits this year with companies remaining "cautiously optimistic" in spite of global headwinds. ERC readies pricing scheme for gas auction

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) determining a gas auction threshold (GAT) that will serve as the maximum allowable price offer for its planned auction this year. GAT will establish a gas price benchmarking technology that will outline limitations and price caps on allowable pass-through charges to consumers. The ERC will also establish a settlement mechanism to provide winning bidders with full revenue recovery. The auction is open to existing, uncommitted existing, newly built, and upgraded natural gas facilities. Energy Secretary Sharon Garin said that the government hopes to stage the auction this year in time to select the winning bidders by 4Q.

Telcos propose P31bn nationwide undersea fiber network

The Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) stated that Globe Telecom, PLDT, and Converge ICT Solutions have proposed a US$500mn (~P31.6bn) nationwide undersea fiber-optic network to expand connectivity across the Philippines. The cable would run from Batanes to Palawan with links extending to Visayas and Mindanao. It would also connect to international submarine cable systems. The project is expected to provide faster and more reliable internet with approximately 70mn Filipinos poised to benefit. DICT Secretary Henry Aguda said that the department is currently evaluating the proposal which is still subject to gov't approval. The project is expected to be completed within two years once approved. Aguda stated that an additional benefit of the undersea cables would be earthquake and tsunami detection.
