REGIS REPORT

20 August 2026

The PCOMP ended lower this shortened trading week, down 0.9% or 58.84 pts WoW to 6,238.46. Outside of certain corporate stories, trading was largely lethargic as the 2Q earnings season wrapped up and investors awaited fresh catalysts. A sharp midweek sell-off ensued as global risk-off mood from rising US yields and higher oil prices seeped into local equities, but this was followed by a strong rebound that helped trim the weekly loss for the index.

 

News around the Lopez group was still the biggest corporate story earlier in the week. FPH (+4.1% WoW) rejected KKR’s P35/share offer to acquire an additional 8.43% stake in FGEN (-2.4% WoW), triggering a sharp sell-off in FGEN initially, which fell nearly 20% intraday. Both names quickly rebounded strongly, with FPH even hitting record highs and FGEN settling near P30 on heavy local activity, as traders speculated on the deal getting done at even higher than P35.

 

The broader market came under pressure midweek while the peso hovered near record lows as US yields rose amid renewed uncertainty around Iran. Higher global oil prices also gave rise to concerns over the domestic inflation outlook. Easing yields followed the US Treasury's signals to lower borrowing costs, which helped global equities swiftly rebound the following session.

 

Mining took center stage again later in the week. Beyond the rebound in gold prices that lifted miners APX (+10.2% WoW) and PX (+7.0% WoW), speculation over the consolidation of the massive Tampakan copper-gold project into DHI (+6.7% WoW) was finally validated, with DHI’s board approving the merger with Indophil Resources and Sonar Holdings, which together hold 100% of Sagittarius Mines and will give DHI controlling ownership of the project. DHI shares were put on trading halt, while traders lifted shares of ACR (+30.0% WoW) which owns 1.81% of Indophil Resources.

 

The biggest drag on the index this week was ICT (-2.2% WoW) that succumbed to profit-taking that pulled the stock further back from ATHs, followed by large-cap macro proxies SM (-3.5% WoW) and BDO (-2.6% WoW). Meanwhile, defensive counters such as MYNLD (+2.6% WoW), TEL (+2.4% WoW), and RCR (+2.3% WoW) were among the week's top gainers.

 

Foreigners remained net sellers for the third straight week, by $33m. The peso slipped 0.4% WoW to 61.660:$1. The PH10Y yield was relatively steady (-1.7bp WoW) at 7.3052%.

Jollibee - 2Q26: Margins recover but domestic SSSG moderates
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H26 profit down 14% YoY, revenue up 10%

JFC’s net profit to common shares declined 14% to P4.7bn for 1H26. Revenues for the period increased 10% YoY to P162.5bn as SWS rose 12% YoY to P244.7bn. Relative to both consensus and REGIS estimates, revenues appear in line with expectations while profit looks behind. For 2Q alone, profit was up 6% YoY (139% QoQ) to P3.3bn, rebounding from the 40% YoY drop in 1Q, as margins recovered following the implemented price hikes. 2Q revenue grew 11% YoY to P85.9bn on SWS growth of 14%. Operating income growth lagged at +2% YoY due to the P240mn China and Smashburger transition-related costs.

 

Price hikes dampen domestic sales; more hikes planned

The domestic business saw 2Q SSSG moderate further to +1.3% from +3.2% in 1Q. Management said it raised prices by 4% on average, which likely weighed on sales and led to an implied contraction YoY in volumes. Up to four more price increases are being considered for the rest of the year.

 

Management guided that July SSSG trends were “very pleasing”. However, we remain wary of the implications of the additional price hikes given the uncertain domestic macro backdrop.

 

Jollibee brands lead international growth; turnaround plan underway

International SSSG picked up slightly to +4.4% from +4.0% in 1Q on continued SSS momentum from Jollibee International (+6.4%), Compose Coffee (+12.4%), and an improvement in Smashburger (+7.0%).

Despite the steady growth in sales, the international business is still losing money (c.-P190mn in 2Q) as Yonghe King and Smashburger continue to drag down profits.

 

Franchising push

The “transition” programs for Yonghe King and Smashburger involve moving both operations into a business model whose stores will be almost entirely franchised. This will thus involve the sale of existing stores to franchisees. We reckon this is a positive step to reduce further downside risks and to smoothen the path to a full exit, if it becomes necessary.

 

Management trims guidance; expects profit recovery in 2H

FY26 guidance was revised downwards, with SSSG lowered to 3-4% from 4-6%, gross store openings were reduced to 1,000-1,100 from 1,200-1,300, while capex was revised to P13-15bn (vs P18-21bn). Management also cut the operating income growth target to 10-15% (from 15-20%). Even the reduced operating income growth target will be a challenge to meet, given the 7% decline in 1H.

 

About its medium-term target of reaching P24bn profit by 2028, management avoided saying that it was abandoning said goal. Reaching that purely on its underlying core business, however, will be a very challenging task.

 
Puregold - 2Q26: Divergent trends
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H26 net profit +11%

PGOLD’s 1H26 net profit grew 11% YoY to P5.9bn, putting it on track to slightly exceed our FY26 estimates. Revenue is trending within expectations at P121.5bn (+11% YoY). For 2Q26 alone, consolidated sales rose 9% YoY while operating profit grew 7%. Net profit, though, was down 2% YoY as other expenses jumped 70% on the absence of a hefty lease termination gain in 2Q25. Group gross margin expanded 0.3ppt YoY to 18.9% as supplier support in Puregold offset deferred price increases in S&R.

 

Growth trajectories diverge

The company’s two segments were on divergent growth paths in 2Q. Revenues at membership shopping chain S&R grew 5% behind SSSG of +13.6% (+12.0% in 1Q). On the other hand, Puregold sales rose only 5%, with SSSG moderating to +2.2% (+5.4% in 1Q). We view this as an indication that the lower income households are struggling under current macro conditions, while the target market of S&R remains impervious.

S&R accounted for more than half (55%) of group net profit for 2Q.

 

Private label begins to grow in Puregold

The share of private label products in Puregold has improved to 3% of sales from 1% in 1Q, with baked goods and meat gaining the most traction. Private label for S&R accounted for 7-8% of sales. We view the current (low) proportion of private label products as a significant long-term opportunity to create stickiness.

 

Early indications stable but outlook remains uncertain

Management stated that July was steady relative to the 2Q trends for both Puregold and S&R but cautioned that the outlook for the rest of the year is uncertain given the slew of domestic and external factors. We reckon that inflationary pressures from the upcoming El Niño, wage hike, and still-elevated fuel prices may weigh further on already-strained consumer wallets.

AREIT - 2Q26: office rental reversions remain negative; new acquisitions announced
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1H26 net profit rises 36%

1H26 net profit rose 36% to P5.8bn, largely thanks to share-for-property swaps in 3Q25 and 2Q26 which increased AUM by a combined 35%. However, due to the increase in shares outstanding, 1H26 DPS only rose 7% to P1.24/share, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts.

 

Office occupancy stays flat but rental reversions remain negative

Office occupancy remained flat QoQ at 96%, much better than the industry average of 82% in Metro Manila. However, on average, expiring contracts were renewed at rental rates that were 3%-5% lower than the previous contracts.

 

New asset acquisitions to increase AUM by 13%

AREIT will acquire P20bn worth of assets from Ayala Land. The new properties will expand AREIT’s AUM by 13% to P179bn and will reduce the proportion of the office segment from 60% of AUM to 54% (see Figure 2). In addition, we estimate that the acquisitions will increase DPS by c. 2%.

• P17.3bn worth of malls and hotels in exchange for 462.5mn new AREIT shares. This will increase shares outstanding by 11%.

 

• P2.6bn in cash for Fairmont Raffles Hotel Makati

 

Unlike AREIT’s existing mall assets, which are leased to Ayala Land, who then subleases to end-tenants, the upcoming mall infusions will be structured under direct leasing agreements to end-tenants (similar to AREIT’s office portfolio).

Bloomberry Resorts - 2Q26 profitability improves on stronger win rates and VIP rolling chip
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Profitability improves in 2Q26

Bloomberry’s recurring net loss, we estimate, shrank to P345mn in 2Q26, from P530mn in 1Q26 and P1.4bn in 2Q25. Meanwhile, EBITDA for 2Q26 grew 15% QoQ and 35% YoY to P3.4bn, ahead of REGIS forecast. Both of Bloomberry’s properties saw improvements in GGR, but Solaire Entertainment City (SEC) was the bigger driver due to a significant jump in VIP rolling chip as well as strong win rates. Cash opex at both properties only rose 5% QoQ and YoY despite the improvements in GGR.

 

SEC: VIP rolling chip climbs; win rates remain strong

SEC’s 2Q26 GGR rose 15% QoQ and 18% YoY to P11.5bn on the back of a significant increase in VIP rolling chip and strong win rates (see Figures 4 to 6). The mass table win rate expanded 5.8ppt QoQ and 6.1ppt YoY to 55.3%, much higher than historical average of c. 40%. The 2Q26 VIP win rate of 3.6% is also above the theoretical level of 2.9%.

 

VIP volume grew 34% QoQ and 12% YoY to P71.2bn. Management said that the more competitive commission strategy instituted in late 2025 was starting to bear fruit as more junket operators were opening in SEC. The company seems optimistic that VIP rolling chip can continue improving in the coming quarters.

 

After declining for four consecutive quarters, slot volume rose 4% QoQ. On the other hand, the mass table volume fell 4% QoQ and YoY.

However, volumes of mass tables and slots both declined sequentially in 2Q26.

An app for Solaire Online, which caters to Solaire patrons (who are generally more affluent), will also be available in 4Q26.

 

2Q26 online gaming opex rose to P1.1bn, from c. P700mn in 1Q26.

Legislator seeks two-year suspension of property valuation law

House Ways and Means Committee Chairman Romero Quimbo filed House Bill No. 10808 (HB 10808), which seeks to suspend the adoption of new or updated Schedules of Market Values (SMVs) and general property assessments, which are key components of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA). President Marcos signed the RPVARA into law in 2024. Under HB 10808, current SMVs, zonal values, assessed values and other valuation bases will remain in effect during the two-year suspension for purposes of national and local taxation, assessments, fees, charges and government transactions. Mr. Quimbo said the suspension would allow government agencies to continue preparatory work for the implementation of RPVARA. The RPVARA transfers the responsibility of updating the “Schedule of Market Values” (from which real property taxes are based) from Local Government Units (such as city governments) to the national government. While raising real property taxes would expand the coffers of a city government, it could also lead to the mayor (and other officials) losing the next election. As a result, local governments in many places are hesitant to regularly update their Schedule of Market Values. As a result, there are cities/municipalities that might not have updated it for over 10 years. RPVARA is slated to take full effect in 2028.
