REGIS REPORT

20 February 2026

Philippine equities moved higher this shortened trading week, which was no less busy with 4Q25 earnings releases and the BSP's February rate decision, along with certain other corporate stories and developments on politics. Though the market began the week in the red, trading after the Chinese New Year holiday sent local shares decidedly higher, bringing the PCOMP back closer to the 6,500 handle anew. For the week, the index was up 1.3% or 80.54 pts WoW to 6,465.12.

 

Dominating corporate headlines this week was government's decision to re-bid the coal operating contract of SCC (-23.0% WoW), after the justice department ruled this cannot be legally extended beyond its July 2027 expiry. Heavy selling wiped out c.P33bn (c.$560m) in the coal miner's market capitalization, and some c.P17bn (c.$300m) of that of its parent DMC (-11.9% WoW) this week. In a company briefing to discuss this development, SCC management laid out their qualitative and financial competitive advantages, though refrained from committing to pay out special dividends until there is more clarity on the bid terms. The Department of Energy (DOE) is launching the auction next week, February 27, with interested bidders given sixty (60) calendar days or until around end-April to submit their applications.

 

SMPH (-1.9% WoW) drifted lower after it reported earnings that were in-line with forecasts, with stronger-than-expected performance of malls offsetting a weaker-than-expected showing from the residential business that continued to bare declining pre-sales, revenue bookings, and margins. ALI (+2.9% WoW) results, though also just in-line, seemed better received by the market as shares rallied post-release. This was despite similarly weak residential results in 4Q which was only offset by the rental business.

 

MBT (+2.6% WoW) results were broadly in-line though marked by a sharp slowdown in loan growth, declining NIMs, and a big dip in NPL cover. Still, strong foreign inflow lifted the share price. Banks were broadly higher for the week despite continued monetary easing.

 

On that, the BSP delivered a widely-anticipated 25-bp rate cut to 4.25%, taking the key rate to its lowest level since August 2022. The BSP has now lowered rates by a total of 225 bps since the start of the easing cycle in August 2024. BSP Gov. Remolona cited softer-than-expected domestic growth alongside a slowdown in investments, which has also prompted the central bank to lower their GDP growth forecasts to 4.6% (from 5.9%) for 2026, and to 5.0% (from 6.3%) for 2027.

 

Elsewhere, VP Sara Duterte has announced her presidential bid for the 2028 national elections. Note that there remain four impeachment petitions against VP Duterte, and the Speaker has until March 2 to transmit the first two impeachment complaints to the House committee on justice.

 

Foreigners' weekly net inflow streak remains intact for 2026 so far, or now on its eighth consecutive week of net inflow. Average turnover dipped below $100m due to Chinese New Year holidays around the region. The peso weakened by 0.2% WoW to 58.147:$1.

Semirara - Notes from briefing on coal contract bidding
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Arguments supporting a favorable outcome

Semirara management held a briefing to discuss the recent news on the Department of Energy’s plans of auctioning the coal operating contract (COC) over Semirara island. This decision by the authorities definitely raises the level of uncertainty and risk. Management tried to ease concerns by laying out its qualitative and financial competitive advantages. Special dividends, though, are likely to be on hold, pending clarity on bid terms.

 

No official notice yet on COC extension request

The company has not received any official statement to date on its request to extend their existing COC by 13.3 years. Management believes the request is valid, given that the government was the operator prior to when it took over in 1997. With the July 2027 expiration date approaching, management believes that the Department of Energy (DOE) Is seeking a speedy resolution and has thus considered the possibility of re-bidding the contract. Previously, there have been a couple of local conglomerates, who have asked about the contract but have not followed up since.

 

Bidding will be based on qualifications, not financial capability

While the DOE has not officially released the terms for the tender, management understands that the government will focus on technical and operational qualifications, instead of purely financial merits, when choosing the winner.

With its existing equipment, technical expertise, and position as the sole largescale miner in the country, the company considers itself well-positioned in any qualification-based tender process.

 

Limited reserves could dissuade other potential bidders

Moreover, management believes that any new entrant will need to contend with Semirara island’s limited reserves of only 130mn MT, implying a mine life of eight (8) years assuming an annual production of 16mn MT. Given a replacement cost of at least P3bn per 1mn MT of production, returns for a new entrant will likely be unattractive. Lastly, the combination of a 2/3-year permitting timeline and persistent water seepage issues heightens execution risk, which could further place returns for any prospective operator at risk.

 

Deferring decision on capital returns until bid terms are out

Management refrained from committing to paying out special dividends, pending clarity on the bid terms. It also emphasized that its power unit will be a going concern and has coal inventory that could last beyond 2027.

 
Semirara - No deal; Re-bid creates greater uncertainty
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Government opts to re-bid coal operating contract

The Secretary of Energy revealed that the government plans to bid out a new coal operating contract (COC) covering Semirara Island by yearend, after the Department of Justice ruled the current COC held by Semirara Mining (SCC) cannot be legally extended beyond its July 2027 expiry. The decision has jolted the market, which appears to have been expecting an extension. While SCC should have a significant advantage in an auction process, we believe the auction creates considerable uncertainty and may prompt management to postpone paying special dividends until the smoke clears.

 

Extension request denied

The Department of Justice (DOJ) ruled that SCC’s request to extend its COC by another 13 years may violate the 50-year constitutional cap on PPP projects that involve the country’s natural resources. Even though the current controlling shareholders of SCC, the Consunji family, has only been operating the mines in Semirara island since 1997, an auction is arguably the most politically acceptable option for the government.

 

 

Waiting for official auction terms; Binary outcome

It’s not known yet what the criteria / parameters will be for the selection of the winner of the auction, but the Energy Secretary said that (a) SCC will be allowed to join the auction and (b) it plans to select based on the most qualified operator, not merely the best financial offer.

 

We previously estimated a valuation of P21/share if the coal operating agreement is not renewed beyond July 2027 while a 15-year extension under the same terms puts the value of the company at P35/sh. We nonetheless believe that SCC is in a strong competitive position relative to any prospective bidder, given its long track record of operating in Semirara Island, as well as its fully depreciated mining equipment and infrastructure, which will provide cost and lead time advantages when making a financial offer.

 

Capital returns near-term could be in limbo until auction is finalized

We believe a re-bid may prompt management to postpone paying special dividends until the bidding is over, despite free cash flow likely remaining robust (FY26E: P4/sh) with investments likely to be postponed. We cut our FY26E profit estimates by 21% on a reduced coal sales volume of 16.5mn MT (vs. 19mn previously).

 
SM Prime - 4Q25: mall profits climb but residential segment performs poorly
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

4Q25 net profit inches down YoY

SM Prime’s 4Q25 net profit fell 1% YoY (down 9% QoQ) to P11.6bn, as the mall and residential segments moved in opposite directions. Mall revenue growth remained healthy while margins expanded. In contrast, residential revenue declined while margins shrank. For FY25, net income rose 7% to P48.8bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts.

 

Mall margins expand in 4Q25; healthy operations in Jan 2026

Despite the weak GDP growth in 4Q25, SM Prime’s mall revenue grew 7% YoY, the same pace as 9M25. Furthermore, the company expanded its mall EBIT margin substantially in 4Q25/FY25 (see Figure 2). Management attributes this to the improvement in occupancy to 95% (+2ppt YoY) as well as “disciplined cost management” (i.e. solar panels on mall rooftops helped rein in utility costs).

 

The company said that it was “happy” with the mall performance in January 2026 and that sales of certain tenant categories, such as fashion/beauty (+11%) and casual dining (+8%) performed particularly well.

 

Residential segment deteriorates in 4Q25

SM Prime’s residential segment performed poorly on multiple fronts in 4Q25. Unsold inventory increased while reservation sales, residential revenue, and EBIT margin all declined markedly on a QoQ and YoY basis (see Figures 1 and 2). 

The company said cancellations of high-margin units sold prior to 2024 were partly to blame for all of these.

 

The value of unsold residential inventory rose 3% QoQ and 6% YoY to P182bn, about 3.7 years’ worth of reservation sales. While only 12% (c. 3,400) of the 28,000 unsold units are ready-for-occupancy (RFO), this figure is likely to rise substantially as 10,000 unsold units are scheduled for completion this year. Management said that on average, it offered 20% discounts on select RFO units and would likely maintain these discounts in order to liquidate inventory.

 

Since the unsold units are predominantly Metro Manila mid-income condominiums, the company will not launch such projects this year. Instead, it will launch a luxury lot-only project in Metro Manila and lower-priced units (below the VAT threshold of P3.6mn) in provincial areas.

 

SMPH to spend US$1.2bn-US$1.5bn more for reclamation project

The company spent P28bn for its Pasay 360 project in 2025 and it will spend another US$1.2bn-US$1.5bn (P70bn-P90bn cumulative) from 2026-2028. Although management plans to fund this project with debt, it hinted that there could be other means to finance the capex. While some business groups have approached SM Prime for potential purchases of land parcels, we do not expect any such transactions until a Presidential Proclamation is awarded.

Government opts to re-bid Semirara’s coal operating contract before end-2026

The Department of Energy (DOE) has decided to re-bid Semirara’s (SCC PM) coal operating contract (COC), after the Department of Justice ruled the current agreement cannot be legally extended. Coal mining accounts for ~60% of SCC’s profits. We reckon the company may postpone declaring special dividends until after the bidding is finalized. Assuming SCC’s dividend policy of paying out 20% of prior year’s reported profits, regular dividends could only reach ~P0.63/sh. Metrobank FY25 NPAT P49.7bn, +3% YoY

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY25 NPAT of P49.7bn up 3% YoY, coming in slightly behind REGIS forecast of P50.9bn and consensus P50.1bn. For 4Q alone, NPAT was P12.4bn, flat YoY and QoQ. Revenues and PPOP were broadly in line with expectations. However, bottomline was dragged by higher provision and tax expenses. Most importantly for some investors, the bank maintained its P5.00/sh cash dividend for FY26 (P3 regular, P2 special), for a 6.7% yield. Gross loans grew 7.4%, slowing sharply from the 11.3% in 3Q, and clocking the slowest pace since 1Q22. Consumer loans were up 13.9% YoY, though slower than the 15.8% pace in 3Q25. NIMs dropped 9bps QoQ, likely on sharply lower policy interest rates. Loan yields tend to be wonky in 4Q, as there is some reclassification, but even YoY yields were down 15bps from 4Q24. Overall asset yields were down 13bps QoQ while funding costs only dipped 7bps QoQ. Fee incomes were up 6% YoY, while trading/fx gains surged 47% YoY. Opex was kept under control, up just 3% YoY, leading to a 16% jump in PPOP for FY25, and +29% YoY for 4Q alone. Note, though, that 4Q24 was a trading/fx loss, which helped boost 4Q25 PPOP. Provisions jumped 87% YoY to P11.9bn (in line) from a low base in 2024. Credit cost was 65bps in 4Q. However, we note that NPL Cover dipped to 141%, from 147% in 3Q and from 164% at end-2024, suggesting provision expense would have been higher (and NPAT lower) were it not for running down of reserves. Tax expenses were up 34%, also on a low base.

BSP cuts rates further to 4.25%; lowers GDP growth forecasts for 2026 and 2027

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.25%, the lowest since Aug 2022. This is the sixth consecutive 25bp cut (150bp total) since the decision to resume easing back in Apr 2025. The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 225bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024. In a press briefing, BSP Governor Eli Remolona emphasized that rationale for the cut was because softer than expected growth alongside a slowdown in investments. Recall that 4Q25 GDP growth was 3.0%, bringing FY25 growth to 4.4%, much lower than the BSP's expectations of 3.8% growth in 4Q and 4.6% in FY25. Remolona highlighted weak domestic demand as the primary factor behind disappointing economic growth. He cited that latest indictors imply a recovery in 2H26 but growth is dependent on "how quickly confidence recovers". The central bank revised its GDP growth forecast for 2026 and 2027 to 4.6% and 5.9% from 5.4% and 6.3%, respectively. The inflation outlook was said to remain manageable but the BSP revised its inflation outlook citing supply-side factors (electricity, oil prices, rice prices). The BSP now forecasts inflation for 2026 and 2027 to be 3.6% and 3.2% from 3.2% and 3.0%, respectively. The second out of the six Monetary Board meetings will be held on Apr 23. For 2026, consensus expectations for inflation/GDP growth/rate cuts are 2.7%/5.0%/no cut. Regis expectations are 3.0%/5.5%/no cut, respectively. For 2027, consensus expectations for inflation/GDP growth/rate cuts are 3.0%/5.8%/no cut. Regis expectations are 3.0%/5.7%/no cut, respectively.

NTC sets registration requirements for Konektadong Pinoy entrants

The National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) in a memorandum circular outlined registration requirements for prospective Konektadong Pinoy entrants. The memorandum outlined the following requirements for applicants. A minimum asset requirement of P1bn for international gateway facility applicants, P100mn for core or backbone network applicants, P10mn for middle-mile provider applicants, and P500,000 for last-mile providers. All applicants, except middle mile providers, are required to pay a performance bond equivalent to at least 10% of the applicant's projected capex for the first two years, but not exceeding P5mn. A debt-to-asset ratio of 70% for all applicants of all segments. The NTC will periodically review the minimum asset requirements, leverage ratios, and related financial thresholds every three years or shorter intervals when necessary. Recall that the Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) stated that one Philippine company has formally applied to offer internet services following the enactment of the Konektadong Pinoy Act. DICT Secretary Henry Aguda said that the application is under review.
