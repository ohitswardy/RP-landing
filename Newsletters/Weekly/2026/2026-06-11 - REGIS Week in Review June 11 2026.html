11 June 2026

PCOMP posted a moderate decline during the shortened trading week, -0.5% WoW at 5910, as the broad selloff across most benchmark constituents was partially cushioned by the continued upward momentum on ICT.

Index heavyweight ICT capped the week with another hefty +5% gain to another fresh all-time high of 898/share (market cap now at Php1.8T)  after rising nearly +14% last week, supported by continued foreign buying which amounted to nearly $20m – while for context the broader Philippine market only saw a modest net foreign inflow of less than $2m. The stock remains a key focus on the ground and continues to dominate volumes (typically accounting up to 1/3 of market daily activity nowadays) after the recent BSP Circular that raises single exposure limits for domestic UTIFs to 20% from 15%. Furthermore, the company’s fundamentals continue to look positive still with the latest data out of Mexico showing that ICT’s CMSA port, which accounts for almost a quarter of the company’s EBITDA, saw a +26% YoY surge in volumes in May – this is an even faster pace than its +21% growth in April.

Otherwise, breadth of the market looked negative with half of the PCOMP components ending the week with losses of at least -2%. Worst laggard in the benchmark this week was ALI which sank -10% to its lowest level in 16 years, as it was also the top foreign sold name on the board with an outflow of almost $10m. While the outlook of higher rates weighs across the property sector, ALI’s underperformance stands out against peers SMPH -2.9% and RLC -0.6%. On the ground, there have been predictions going around of a potential removal from the MSCI standard cap benchmark in the upcoming August rebalancing. Note that ALI, along with TEL -5.2% and AC -4.9% which also notably underperformed during the week, are the 3 lowest weighted members of the MSCI standard cap index as of end-May. The weakness across all 3 names suggests heightened aversion among investors over more potential rebal outflows on the future.

Up ahead, key event next week are the BSP policy meeting on Thursday and the FTSE rebalancing on Friday. On the former - the central bank is widely expected to hike rates by 25bps after the last CPI reading showed inflation stood at 6.8% in May (albeit much better than 7.8% forecast). While on the rebal, MYNLD -0.4% is expected to be included in the small cap index.

Century Pacific Food - Prospects hold despite bumpy road
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph // Paolo Garcia +632 8894 6636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Adjusting our estimates but maintaining Buy

We refresh our estimates for Century Pacific’s (CNPF PM) shares in light of the latest results as well as the ongoing conflict in western Asia. We expect pressure on both topline growth and margins but believe that CNPF’s diversified portfolio of market-leading core and high-growth emerging products will allow it to better weather near-term headwinds.

 

We reduce our profit estimates for FY26/27E by 9/10% but maintain our TP of P40/sh after rolling forward our DCF valuation. The stock’s down 31% YTD and is now trading at only 13x FY26E PER with a 3-year EPS CAGR of 14%. Despite near-term challenges, we think CNPF’s long-term growth prospects remains intact.


Challenges on margins and demand
CNPF implemented average price hikes of ~4% in May for its branded business (80% of revenues) following material cost increases for key inputs (e.g. packaging, logistics, etc.). Despite said cost pressures and the likely impact on consumer demand from rising inflation, we believe CNPF will be among the resilient names given the diversification and nature of its product portfolio.

 

A margin squeeze is less of an issue for the OEM business, but higher input prices could be a factor affecting customer demand, particularly for tuna.

Glimpse of what’s coming by 2Q; El Niño looms
We should see the early after-effects of the Middle East conflict within 2Q with the price hikes finally implemented. Beyond that, meteorologists are also predicting a severe El Niño coming by mid-2026. This weather pattern typically means higher temperatures and lower rainfall leading to reduced farm output. It could also lead to reduced fish catch and thus affect tuna supply and prices.

 

Refreshing estimates but maintaining TP and Buy rating

We adjust our forecasts for the latest results but retain our Buy rating and target price (DCF valuation-based) of P40/sh after rolling forward our valuation. We trim our revenue estimates slightly (by 2%) but reduce our profit estimates more (by 9-10%) due to higher financing costs (higher capex) and a higher tax rate. The TP implies FY26E PER multiple of 18.8x (broadly in line with historical multiples), while the stock at its latest closing price provides a modest 2% yield.

ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rise 26% in May; Implements 5-10% tariff hikes this month

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that throughput at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo SA (CMSA), rose 26% YoY to a monthly record level of 152k TEUs in May. The growth rate accelerated from the 21% pace in April 2026 and 16% in May 2025. This brings CMSA’s 5M26 volumes up 14% YoY to 717k TEUs, trending well ahead of our full-year forecast (1.66mn TEUs, +8%). Moreover, CMSA implemented tariff hikes of 5-10% on various services, including the following: stevedoring (+5%); arrastre (+8%); storage (+10%). Note that our forecast assumes CMSA’s yield will increase by +10% this year. We estimate that every 10% increase in CMSA’s topline will raise consolidated FY26E EBITDA by 3% and profit by 4%. We estimate CMSA accounts for 10% of ICTSI’s consolidated FY26E volumes, 20% of revenues, and 23% of EBITDA.

At that price, MREIT offers an FY26E dividend yield of 7.6% based on consensus forecast. PLDT board approves plan to list data centers via a REIT

Board of PLDT approved a plan to spin off its data center (DC) assets into a REIT. Under VITRO Inc. Management hopes to raise US$300-400m from the REIT listing. The planned REIT spin-off and listing come after the SEC expanded the criteria for what can qualify as REITs. Amended rules allow digital infrastructure assets. PLDT management has spoken of this plan before. Total capacity for PLDT’s DCs is ~60MW IT load spread across 11 sites. The initial plan is to include 8 of the 11 locations. The newest DC (Vitro Santa Rosa) is the biggest (over 50% of capacity) will not be included. Recall PLDT previously discussed a plan to sell part of its DC business at a valuation of more than US$1bn.

SM Prime opens first hotel in China

SM Prime opened its first hotel in China. 325 rooms in SM Prime’s 10-hectare Xiamen complex in Fujian province. It offers accommodations for RMB700/night (P6,300/night). The hotel is under the label Voco, a four-star hospitality brand under InterContinental Hotels Group. The company previously said that its future expansion in China would only be in Fujian province, where it has stronger brand equity. The hotel segment accounts for c. 3% of SM Prime’s operating profit. The company has 2,600 hotel rooms in the Philippines.

Nickel Asia teams up with ACWA Power for 5GW of RE, gas-fired projects

Nickel Asia’s power unit, Emerging Power Inc. (EPI), signed an agreement with ACWA Power Phils. to explore up to 5GW of renewable energy and gas-fired generation capacity across the Philippines. ACWA Power Phils is a subsidiary of KSA-based ACWA Power (ACWA AB), a private equity with a portfolio of power generation and water desalination assets across 15 countries. EPI and ACWA plan to participate in the government’s Green Energy Auction Program. This partnership is a departure from the company’s recently stated plans of limiting its RE portfolio to 1GW (vs. current operating gross capacity at 458MW).

Citicore REIT expects asset infusions within the year

Citicore REIT (CREIT PM) president and CEO Oliver Tan expects several solar asset infusions within the year. It is in the process of securing regulatory approvals for the planned infusions. Last May 15, CREIT’s board approved the infusion of ~1.7mn sqm of land and 860MWp of solar assets in the provinces of Pampanga, Pangasinan, Batangas, Quezon, and Negros Occidental. Upon completion, CREIT’s gross leasable area is expected to expand by 19% to 8.8mn sqm. The third-party valuation for the proposed asset infusion is currently underway. CREC plans to commission 2.2GW of solar and 45MW of wind projects by year-end, expanding its operating asset base of 1.2GW as of end-March. CREC’s net debt stood at P39bn in end-2026, with net debt-to-equity ratio at 1.6x.

Consumer loans jump 22.6% YoY in 1Q26; NPLs edge higher QoQ

Consumer loans in the Philippine banking system grew 22.6% YoY, the fastest quarterly YoY pace since 1Q2015, and re-accelerating sharply from the 19.1% clocked in 4Q25. That said, the numbers included an unusual 147% surge in “other consumer loans”. While that was a small number – at just 3% of total consumer loans – it did push the overall number up. Excluding the “others” account, consumer loans would have grown 18.9% YoY. Consumer loans now account for 23.2% of total loans, by far a new all-time high. Consumer NPLs inched up 9bps QoQ, but were flat (-1bp) YoY.

 

Apart from the “others” account, growth was again led by Credit Cards, up 28.7% YoY, marking the 15th consecutive quarter of >25% YoY growth (since 3Q22). For perspective, at credit card receivables are now larger than mortgages (P1,237bn vs P1,218bn). However, credit card asset quality continued to deteriorate, with NPL ratio at 4.98%, up 17bps QoQ and 38bps YoY. This is noteworthy considering the fast growth of the denominator – in nominal terms, credit card NPLs were up 39% YoY.

 

Mortgages grew just 8.0% YoY, the slowest pace since 1Q24. NPL ratios in the segment was at 6.11%, up 3bps QoQ but down 24bps YoY.

 

Motor Vehicle loans, meanwhile, decelerated sharply, up 11.2% YoY, from 14.4% in 4Q25, and from 18.5% YoY in 1Q25. NPLs were up 13bps QoQ to 5.18% and up 25bps YoY. Banks have tightened credit standards on car loans, particularly those originated at the dealership. The “incentives” given to dealers have spike to the high-teens (as % of the loan amount), which the dealers get up-front, rewarding them to push for loans for less credit-worthy buyers, and for more unpopular car models. Banks have anecdotally leaned more towards branch-originated loans, and for more well-known car brands.

 

Salary loans jumped 50% YoY, though NPLs here were also deteriorating, up 29bps QoQ (but down 20bps YoY).

 

We remain wary of consumer loan NPLs, particularly in the unsecured segment of credit cards and salary/other loans. Especially so with the sharp increase in inflation in recent months as a result of the US-Iran conflict. 

Magnitude 7.8 earthquake hits Southern Mindanao

A magnitude 7.8 earthquake hit the southern island of Mindanao at 7:37am on June 8. The origin of the earthquake was located 13 kilometers southwest of General Santos City, a coastal city in the province of South Cotabato. Soccksargen (Region 12), which includes four provinces—South Cotabato, Cotabato, Sarangani, Sultan Kudarat, was directly affected being the closest region to the epicenter. General Santos City experienced the strongest reported intensity at "Intensity VII" with buildings exhibiting significant structural damage or total collapse. Soccksargen contributed to 2.5% of the Philippines' GDP in 2025. The quake was also felt in other areas at varying intensities such as: Davao Region (i.e., Davao City) and Northern Mindanao.

PH dollar reserves at $104bn as of end-May

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-May were flat (-0.3% MoM) at US$104.0bn relative to the previous month (US$104.3bn). The May GIR figure is a 16-month low. The slight MoM drop in the GIR was primarily due to central bank's foreign exchange operations given its continued effort to support the peso. The USD-PHP rate depreciated to P61.4 in end-May from P60.3 in end-Apr. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.6x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 6.9x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$111bn.

Commercial banks’ loan growth picks up to 11.4% in April

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth accelerated to 11.4% in Apr from 10.7% in Mar. The 11.4% loan growth rate is the fastest since July 2025 (11.8%). Loans to productive activities picked up to 10.7% vs 9.7% the previous month. Consumer loans grew at a still robust pace of 19.6%, but moderating from Mar's 20.5% rate. This is the first time consumer loan growth was below 20% since Aug 2022 (18.3%). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth inched up slightly to 12.2% in Apr from 12.1% in Mar.

CXAP expects AI to create new contact center jobs

The Customer Xperience Association of the Philippines (CXAP), formerly the Contact Center Association of the Philippines, said it wants to debunk the notion that AI is negatively affecting the contact center industry. It expects AI to create new types of jobs. CXAP is targeting to grow industry revenue to US$35.7bn this year (+5% from US$33.9bn in 2025) and targeting to expand industry headcount to 1.73mn (+3% from 1.68mn in 2025). CXAP is a sub-organization under the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP), which currently expects the industry to achieve revenue of US$42bn and employ 1.97mn Filipinos by the end of 2026. These represent 4%-5% growth from the 2025 revenue (US$40bn) and employee (1.9mn) figures.

Government plans to renew incentive program for ICE vehicles

Trade Secretary Cristina Roque said that the “Revitalizing the Automotive Industry for Competitiveness Enhancement” (RACE) program is back on the table. This marks a turnaround from her pronouncement in April that the program was scrapped in favor of the Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy (EVIS). Guidelines for the RACE and EVIS programs, which aim to provide tax incentives for domestic car manufacturers, will be released “very soon”. Decision was prompted by the lack of electric vehicle (EV) counterparts for commercial vehicles widely used by micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and public safety services. She specifically cited the Toyota Tamaraw, which is currently the only commercial vehicle being manufactured domestically. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) earlier indicated that it plans to enroll the Tamaraw under the program.
