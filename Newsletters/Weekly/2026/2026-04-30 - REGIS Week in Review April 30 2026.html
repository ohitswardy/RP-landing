REGIS REPORT

30 April 2026

Philippine equities suffered a third straight weekly loss with the PCOMP falling 1.8% or 109.85 pts WoW to 5,833.64 as the Iran conflict re-escalated and investors continued to weigh the fallout on the local economy and on corporate earnings.

 

Still unable to secure a deal with Tehran, the US has intensified its naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, which sent oil prices back up to their highest in over four years with crude oil now well above $120/bbl, jumping by over 15% WoW. Back to Philippine shores, the peso crashed to a record low, breaching the 61:$1 mark as rising inflationary pressures weighed on sentiment throughout the week.

 

SCC (-15.2% WoW) led index declines after the DOE postponed the Semirara coal contract bidding “until further notice,” raising investor concerns that delays could allow more competitive bids from outside parties.

 

On 1Q26 earnings, the large property developers SMPH (-4.9% WoW) and ALI (-4.4% WoW) reported numbers that missed market expectations as residential revenues fell by double digits both QoQ and YoY. Elsewhere, with the miss in its property and bank subsidiaries, SM (-3.7% WoW) earnings also fell short of forecasts, with SM Retail's decent results only partly offsetting general weakness in the other cyclical businesses.

 

Defensives in energy producers, utilities, and yield plays remained outperformers for the week, with AEV (+2.5% WoW), MONDE (+1.8% WoW), MER (+1.7% WoW), SMC (+1.3% WoW), and RCR (+1.3% WoW) among the top gainers.

 

Despite the recent BSP rate hike and the Fed holding rates steady this week, the peso fell to a record low, closing the week -1.2% to 61.481:$1, with domestic macro concerns weighing down the currency.

SM Investments - 1Q26: Banking and property disappoint; steady growth at retail ops
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q26 earnings rise 7% to P21.5bn

SM Investments delivered 1Q26 profits of P21.5bn, 7% higher than a year ago and just a tad below expectations. Earnings from two of its biggest subsidiaries, BDO and SM Prime, were mildly disappointing. The retail operations, meanwhile, delivered respectable profit growth of 13%, in line with expectations.

 

Banking and property see some pressure

The results for the latest quarter for both the banking and property business showed modest pressure on earnings from separate issues. Higher provisions for loan losses capped profit growth at 2% for BDO despite loan growth of 16%. Meanwhile, earnings at SM Prime were virtually unchanged YoY. The mall business maintained its steady growth pace but contributions from the residential property side dropped sharply.

Retail revenue grows 6%, profit up 13%

Revenues at SM Retail grew +6% for 1Q26, while profits expanded 13%. Groupwide SSSG was 3.4%, a tad lower than last year’s 4.1%. The largest contributor to the Retail business, Food Retail, reported revenue growth of 6.7% with SSSG of +3.8% and profit growth of 9%. Management also mentioned that Alfamart ended 1Q with 2,337 implying net adds of 28 stores (gross of 61) for the quarter. SSSG for Alfamart was +3% in the quarter.

 

Dividend yield of 2.8%

The company declared a dividend of P17/share for this year, roughly 1/4th of last year’s EPS. This is 31% higher than the dividends paid last year and is equivalent to ~2.8% dividend yield. We see this as the company’s attempt to improve returns and lift the share price given the market investors’ focus on dividends in recent years. Note SM has an active share buyback program that has bought back over P10bn worth of its own shares in just over a year.

 
BDO Unibank - 1Q26 “pre-emptive” provisions drag in-line PPOP; forecasts, TP cut
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

Forecasts cut on provisions, TP revised to P180/sh

1Q26 PPOP was up 16% YoY, broadly in line with our forecasts. Strong loan growth (+16%) was tempered by compressing NIMs, but revenues stayed firm +11% YoY while opex moderated. NPL ratio improved 15bps QoQ to 1.68%. However, the bank doubled provisions YoY as a preemptive move, anticipating higher delinquencies due to the economic drag from the war in Iran, dragging NPAT growth to just 2% YoY. We lower our profit forecasts for FY26E by 8% as we see elevated credit costs for the rest of the year, and cut our TP by 10% to P180/sh. We keep our Buy call on valuations; the stock trades on 0.88x on our FY26E, levels not seen since just after the GFC in 2009.

 

1Q26 loans surge 16%, revenues firm, opex moderates

Gross customer loans accelerated, up 16% YoY (vs 13% in 4Q) with all segments posting double-digit growth (Fig 1). However, NIMs compressed 18bps QoQ as lower benchmark rates squeezed loan yields (Fig 2 and 3). Soft fees (+3%) were offset by strong insurance income (+27%). Overall revenues were firm, up 11% YoY. Meanwhile, opex growth moderated to 6% YoY, taking PPOP up 16% YoY, broadly in line with our expectations.

Provisions double YoY, despite NPLs falling 18bps to 1.68%; cover 132%

Asset quality improved in 1Q26 with NPL ratio falling 18bps to 1.68% (Fig 5), while NPL cover was steady at 132%. Despite this, the bank doubled provision expense to P6.15bn, bringing credit cost to 65bps (Fig 6). The “pre-emptive measure” was taken in light of the ongoing Iran conflict and its expected impact on slower economic growth and sharply higher inflation.

 

TP lowered to P180; but stock already trading at crisis levels

We cut our 2026E forecasts by 9%, expecting credit costs to stay high for the full year. Our target price was also cut 10% to P180/sh, using (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 14.0% (down from 14.5%), COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%. We maintain our Buy call; the stock is already at crisis valuations, trading on P/bk of 0.88x on our FY26E estimates, below even Covid-era levels. Key downside risks include further escalation in the US-Israel-Iran conflict, leading to a prolonged drag on the Philippine economy.

 
SM Prime - 1Q26: leasing profits climb but residential profit falls
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q26 net profit stays flat

SM Prime’s 1Q26 net profit remained flat YoY at P11.66bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Operating profit was similarly flat at P16.7bn, as the leasing and residential segments moved in opposite directions. Every leasing segment saw an increase in revenue and an expansion of margins. In contrast, the residential business saw a decline in revenue and a compression of margins.

 

Mall margins expand in 1Q26; operations “look encouraging” in April

1Q26 mall revenue grew 8% YoY on the back of 7% SSSG, the same pace as 4Q25/FY25. Furthermore, the company expanded its mall EBIT margin by 1.8ppt YoY (to 58.5%), thanks to “disciplined cost management” (i.e., solar panels on mall rooftops helped rein in utility costs).

 

To our surprise, management said that March showed the strongest sales growth in 1Q26 and that April figures “look encouraging”. Moreover, the company believes margins could expand further, as it has reduced mall operations by two hours per weekday.

 

Residential EBIT falls; reservation sales improve sequentially

1Q26 residential EBIT fell 31% QoQ and 37% YoY, likely due to the sales cancellations of high-margin units.

Residential revenue fell 16% QoQ (and 14% YoY) while EBIT margin shrank 6.5ppt QoQ (and 10.6ppt YoY), the fifth consecutive quarter of sequential decline.

 

On a more positive note, reservation sales grew 49% QoQ (+1% YoY) to P12.3bn. Furthermore, this does not appear to fully reflect the c. P1.5bn of reservation sales from the newly launched luxury lot-only project in Metro Manila.

 

The value of unsold residential inventory grew 18% YoY (flat QoQ) to P182bn, equivalent to about 3.7 years’ worth of reservation sales. Since most of these are Metro Manila mid-income condominiums, the company will not launch similar projects this year. Instead, it will focus on liquidating existing inventory and will only launch horizontal projects in provincial areas, as well as new phases of the newly launched luxury lot-only project.

 

SMPH to defer “non-critical capex”

Management said that it would defer “non-critical capex” in light of the conflict in the Middle East. While it did not provide specific projects or figures, the Pasay reclamation does not appear to be among the projects to be deferred. The company said that reclamation works continue and that the project will be delivered to the government in 2028 (as scheduled).

 
Monde Nissin - Bumpy road ahead; updating our estimates
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

TP revised to P9/sh but maintaining Buy

Recent developments show apparent improvement in the APAC BFB and the Meat Alternatives businesses, but we are wary of the near-term disruption the Middle East conflict may inflict on both demand and costs. We update our numbers—slashing profit forecasts for FY26/27E by 16/23% and also reducing our TP to P9/sh (-31%). Notwithstanding this, we still think MONDE offers an attractive investment proposition at 12x FY26E PER with a yield of 6%.

 

APAC BFB: Biscuits and “Others” to lead growth

The core noodles segment (43% of APAC BFB revenue) has lagged in recent periods due to competition and category decline, but, fortunately, the other segments (Biscuits and “Others”) have taken up the slack. We expect these trends to continue and forecast a 6% topline CAGR for APAC BFB over FY26-28E, which would be in line with growth over the last six years.

 

Quorn: Turning point?

Management is working on its plan to revitalize revenue growth and thus, profitability, for the Meat Alternatives business (Quorn) but it is still too early to say things have turned around.

This business managed to arrest its revenue decline in 4Q, but full-year revenue was still down YoY. Further operating improvements for Quorn will be necessary to avoid further impairment for this investment and more, importantly, pave the way for an eventual divestment.

 

Middle East Conflict clouds near-term outlook

The prevailing conflict in the Middle East is likely to have a meaningful impact on both costs as well as end-consumer demand for MONDE. Rising costs for energy, logistics and even packaging will put pressure on margins. Meanwhile, on the demand side, consumers will have to grapple with a looming inflation spike, with implications on overall spending.

 

Revising estimates and TP but maintaining Buy rating

We adjust our forecasts for the latest results and revise our target price (SOTP valuation-based) to P9/sh from P13/sh as we refresh our estimates. The TP implies FY26E PER multiple of 12x, below historical average. The stock also provides a solid 6% yield.

 
Maynilad Water - 1Q26 profit up 10% but external risks weigh on momentum
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Trimming FY26E profit; TP maintained at P24/sh

Maynilad reported its 1Q26 net income rose 10% YoY to P4bn, tracking mildly above our forecast but in line with consensus. We attribute the outperformance to a lower tax rate, as 1Q26 EBITDA of P6.5bn (+7%) is in line. Management indicated that earnings pressures stemming from the oil crisis and the threat of El Niño remain manageable. While acknowledging this view, we remain wary of the prevailing crisis and have thus trimmed our FY26E profit forecast by 2%. That said, any near-term revenue and cost pressures should be recoverable in the next rebasing and are thus, NPV neutral. We maintain BUY with a TP of P24/sh.

 

Cash spending up 9%; Interim OCP rises to P183bn

Total cash spending (capex + opex + concession fees) rose 9% YoY to P9bn, broadly in line with our full-year forecast P41bn (+5%). The company estimates the regulatory value of its concession (i.e. interim cash position) stood at P183bn as of end-March 2026, a 12% improvement on an annualized basis.

Management noted that it plans to mitigate the threat of a super El Niño by drawing from alternative water sources totaling 40 MLD. While the Angat dam comprises c. 90% its water source of 2,700 MLD, it only needs 2,300 MLD of supply to maintain service levels, given that leakage (NRW) levels are down to 30.7%. On the cost side, inflationary pressures have emerged, particularly on transport, utilities, and chemicals (30% of cash costs), though only 15% of costs are subject to variable pricing. Management has deferred discretionary spending to mitigate increases in opex but has retained its capex target of P28-30bn.

 

Ample room for payouts to rise, given low leverage

The company’s net debt stood at P76bn as of end-March 2026, equivalent to a net debt-to-equity of 72%. While Street forecasts (and ours) suggest next year’s dividends will remain broadly in line with the P1.14/sh dividend paid this year, we see scope for upside given the company’s low leverage. Notably, this year’s payout represented 55% of previous year’s profit and 44% of adjusted profit, both exceeding the minimum policy threshold.

Department of Energy postpones bidding of Semirara coal contract

The Department of Energy said that the April 28 deadline for the submission and opening of bid documents has been postponed “until further notice”. Postponement aims to ensure that all issues raised during the pre-submission conferences are “fully considered and addressed” before announcing the revised deadline. Note that the pre-submission conferences have been attended by Semirara (SCC PM) and San Miguel (SMC PM) affiliated companies. SCC’s coal contract is due to expire in July 2027.

SM Investments profits up 7% in 1Q26

SM Investments reported 1Q26 profits of P21.5bn, up 7% YoY. Earnings from BDO increased 2% YoY despite loan growth of 16%, while SM Prime’s earnings were virtually unchanged YoY, with steady growth in the mall business and a decline in residential contributions. SM Retail revenues grew 6% YoY in 1Q26, while profits rose 13% YoY. Group-wide SSSG was 3.4%, compared to 4.1% in the prior year. Food Retail posted revenue growth of 6.7% YoY, SSSG of 3.8%, and profit growth of 9% YoY. Alfamart ended the quarter with 2,337 stores, reflecting a net addition of 28 stores in 1Q26 (gross additions of 61), with plans to add 200–250 stores in 2026 and SSSG of 3% in the quarter. The company declared a dividend of P17 per share, up 31% from the previous year and equivalent to approximately 2.7% dividend yield.

Ayala Land says redevelopment of ABS-CBN property still in planning stages

Ayala Land said that the redevelopment of the three-hectare property it acquired from ABS-CBN in 2025 was still in the planning stages. ALI President Anna Maria Margarita Dy said that planning will not be completed by next year. The property was acquired for P6.24bn. This amount will be paid in installments. The company recently paused the development of Laurean, an ALP Project in Makati. It also recently canceled The Heights, an Avida project in Quezon City.

Aboitiz Power 1Q26 core income up 61% to P7.6bn

In a limited press release, Aboitiz Power disclosed headline 1Q26 net income rose 71% YoY to P7.9bn. After adjusting for FX and other non-recurring items, reported “core” profit was up 61% YoY to P7.6bn, which represents 22% of consensus’ FY26E (P34.4bn). Beneficial EBITDA increased by 35% YoY to P20.3bn, driven by higher plant availability, contracted capacity, and the full quarter contribution of Chromite Gas. Note that Chromite Gas’ subsidiary, Excellent Energy, commenced commercial operations in 2Q25. Generation and RES EBITDA rose 41% YoY to P18.5bn. Energy sales rose 30% to 11,243 GWH, of which 9% was sold to the spot market. EBITDA for the distribution segment rose 13% to P2.3bn on the back of a 6% increase in electricity sales, which picked up from the 4% pace in 4Q25 and 5% in 1Q25. Its net debt to equity ratio was broadly stable QoQ at 1.3x in end-March 2026 but remained above end-2024 levels of 0.84x.

Belle Corp share in gaming revenue of COD Manila falls sequentially

Belle Corp’s share in the gaming revenue of City of Dreams Manila fell 16% QoQ (up 12% YoY) to P486mn. Okada Manila’s 1Q26 GGR rose 9% QoQ (down 17% YoY) to P6.5bn.

1Q26 GDP growth could fall below 3% — DepDev

Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that GDP growth in 1Q26 could fall below 3% given the lingering impact from last year's corruption scandal and the external shocks from the Middle East conflict. This implies that 1Q26 GDP growth would be lower than the 3.0% and 5.4% growth in 1Q25 and 4Q25, respectively. Balisacan stated that the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) will review its growth assumptions and likely revise its 5-6% GDP growth target for 2026. He added that the gov't is working to mitigate the impact of negative shocks on the economy with financial support being extended to the most vulnerable sectors. 1Q26 GDP data will be released on May 7, and consensus expects 1Q26 GDP growth to be 4.1%. FY26 GDP growth expectations for the BSP/consensus are 4.8/4.5%.

Gov’t agencies told to cut spending amid oil crisis

In a budget circular issued on April 23, the Department of Budget and Management directed state entities to adopt “economy measures” following the declaration of a national energy emergency. The DBM expects the cost-cutting measures will generate savings ranging from P12.8bn to P25.6bn from March to December 2026, depending on the level of compliance. These estimated savings appear miniscule relative to the 2026 General Appropriations Act of P6.79tn. Agencies are required to cut at least 20% from selected maintenance and other operating expenses (MOOE), while deferring non-critical capital outlays. All agencies covered by the circular should submit their proposed savings not later than May 15. Circular covers all departments, agencies, and operating units of the National Government, including state universities and colleges, as well as government-owned and -controlled corporations. Offices with autonomy — including the legislative and judicial branches, the constitutional commissions, and local government units — were urged to implement similar measures.

Initial operations of Pax Silica AI hub to take at least two years

Joshua Bingcang, President of the Bases Conversion Development Authority (BCDA), said that the Pax Silica AI industrial hub will not be operational until at least two years from now. The Philippine and the United States have not finalized terms on the costs associated with the construction and operations; as well as the precise location and boundaries of the property. The initial plans are for the hub to be situated in a 1,600-hectare site in New Clark City. Mr. Bingcang said the United States would act as master lessee and take on certain development responsibilities, including building roads—an arrangement he said could save the government over P10bn. The project is still in its early stages and will move forward in phases once formal agreements are in place. Under the BCDA’s proposal to the US government, the agency would grant a two-year grace period on lease payments for the property, serving as an unconditional in-kind contribution to support the initiative. The annual lease rate beginning the third year will be covered in a separate agreement. The facility’s locators will be granted fiscal and non-fiscal incentives under the CREATE MORE law. The hub will not be exclusive to American firms. Trade Undersecretary Ceferino Rodolfo said that five firms in East Asia and America have expressed interest. Mr. Bingcang said that BCDA —not any US entity— would screen and approve companies. He also clarified that there are no provisions granting diplomatic immunity or allowing the site to operate under US law.

Retailers take hit as Mideast war drags consumer spending

The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) said most retailers experienced weaker traffic in the first half of April, as consumers react to high fuel prices and broader market uncertainty. According to PRA president Alice Liu, who is also the president and CEO of Gold ABC Inc., the company behind fashion brands Penshoppe, Oxygen, Regatta, etc. Ms. Liu said that businesses have taken a wait-and-see approach while preparing contingency measures in case the conflict drags on. PRA has over 400 member companies, including SM Retail, Wilcon, 7-11, etc.

Colliers: 1Q26 residential net take-up improves thanks to affordable housing

Colliers reported that 1Q26 Metro Manila residential vacancy was relatively flat QoQ at 25%. Net take-up of Metro Manila condominium units rose c. 60% QoQ to 2,000 units. Most of the sales came from units below P7mn/unit. Many of the units sold were below P4mn/unit and were part of the government 4PH program, which offers subsidies and incentives for low-cost housing. Metro Manila office vacancy also remained flat QoQ at 19%. Makati CBD and BGC vacancy rates also stayed flat at the 9%-10% level. Net office take-up fell 22% QoQ and 6% YoY to 73,000sqm. Office rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ. They inched up modestly in Makati CBD. Construction costs have generally increased 10%-15%. Some contractors have invoked force majeure clauses.

Digital payments jump 50% in March

Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) in March increased 50.1% YoY to P2,767bn, an all-time high, compared to 42.3% growth in February. For 3M26, total payments rose 45% YoY, compared to 42% growth in FY2025. PesoNet transactions totaled P1,415bn in March, up 39% YoY, compared to 26.0% growth in the previous month. For 1Q26, PesoNet payments increased 31% YoY, in line with FY25 growth. InstaPay transactions reached P1,352bn in March, up 64% YoY, matching the February growth rate. For 3M26, InstaPay payments increased 64% YoY to P3,761bn, compared to 57% growth in FY2025. Payment volumes rose 255% YoY.

House justice committee finds probable cause to impeach VP Sara Duterte

The House justice committee, in a unanimous vote, declared that there is probable cause in the two impeachment complaints against Vice President Sara Duterte. The complaints accuse VP Duterte of graft, bribery, culpable violation of the Constitution, betrayal of public trust, and other high crimes. The committee is expected to submit its report on the proceedings and corresponding Articles of Impeachment to the plenary for consideration, where a one-third vote of all House members will be required to impeach VP Duterte and to send the case to the Senate for trial. A two-thirds vote of the Senate impeachment court will then be required for a conviction, which will remove Duterte from office and perpetually disqualify her from any future gov't position. Recall that VP Sara Duterte back in Feb announced her intention to run for president in the 2028 election.
