REGIS REPORT

05 June 2026

The PCOMP staged a solid rebound this first week of June, jumping 2.9% or 169.62 pts WoW to 5,938.38 lifted by bargain hunting after the index closed at a 6-month low at end-May. Further pushing share prices up was a big positive surprise in May inflation that printed much lower than expected. Philippines was a large outperformer for the week amid mixed global equity markets that reckoned with stalling US-Iran peace talks and cooling optimism over the AI trade.

 

Philippine inflation unexpectedly slowed to 6.8% YoY in May from 7.2% in April, better than the 7.8% median Street estimate and also below the BSP's forecast range of 7.1-7.9%. This brought YTD-May inflation to 4.5%, vs the FY26 Street/BSP forecast of 6.3%. The decline was largely attributed to fuel price rollbacks, which took Transport inflation down to 16.2% (from 21.4%); Food also slid to 5.7% (from 6.0%) as did Housing & Utilities to 7.8% (from 8.2%). BSP Gov. Eli Remolona said policymakers would closely monitor inflation as they assess the need for a policy rate adjustment ahead of the central bank’s June 18 meeting.

 

Given broad expectations of rising rates, ALI (-2.9% WoW) saw heavy selling that dragged shares to fresh 52-week lows anew, with negative sentiment over residential property persisting. But it was parent AC (-7.0% WoW) that was the biggest laggard for the week amid heavy foreign outflows. On the flipside, BDO (+3.4% WoW) outperformed with elevated rates seen benefiting NIMs, while system loan growth even continued to accelerate to 12.2% in April.

 

Consumer names also saw some relief from the softer inflation print. JFC (+7.4% WoW) rebounded strongly coming from the sharp sell-off last week from its MSCI deletion, while CNPF (+3.4% WoW) and PGOLD (+3.1% WoW) were also among outperformers for the week.

 

ICT (+13.6% WoW) ran well ahead of the pack once again, with the BSP circular raising the single-entity exposure limit for unit investment trust funds (UITFs) from 15% to 20% set to take effect next week. ICT is now close to 25% of the index, while domestic UITFs are capped at 15% exposure to the name. Another outperformer was ACEN (+4.6% WoW) which continued to benefit from the global resurgence of the clean energy theme as countries grapple with the energy crisis brought about by the Iran war.

 

Foreigners were net sellers by $14m for the week, extending the net outflow streak to a third straight week. The peso continued to modestly strengthen, up 0.2% WoW to 61.479:$1.

Philippine Water Utilities - Water supply risks from El Niño manageable
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Worries understandable but misdirected

Share prices of Manila Water (MWC) and Maynilad (MYNLD) have weakened by 14% and 8%, respectively, from their recent highs following predictions of a drought due to the return of the El Niño weather phenomenon. We believe these concerns (1) reflect a lack of appreciation of the prevailing water supply situation and (2) are guided by a misunderstanding of the regulatory framework governing the two concessions. We believe the value of both concessions and dividend prospects are unlikely to be materially affected, even under a worst-case scenario.

 

Receding dam levels and the threat of a “super” El Niño

Concerns are resurfacing over the ability of Metro Manila’s water utilities to maintain service levels amid the risk of declining water levels at Angat Dam, the key water source for Metro Manila. These concerns are amplified by forecasts of a “super” El Niño arriving as soon as this month and predicted to last until 1Q27. The National Water Resources Board (NWRB), together with other stakeholders, has initiated discussions on contingency measures to mitigate this risk, including potential reductions in water allocations of 4% this month and up to 15% should conditions worsen.

Better prepared

Unlike previous drought episodes, water utilities are now better prepared to manage water supply risks. MWC, for example, benefits from the addition of the Wawa Dam as an alternative source (see Fig 2). MYNLD is also more equipped to address the issue, given the reduction in leakage (non-revenue water, NRW) levels (see Fig 1). It also has access to cross-border supply from MWC.

 

Water utilities to be kept whole in NPV terms in the event of supply disruptions

The government is responsible for the provision of bulk water supply from Angat Dam under the current regulatory framework. In the event reduced water supply leads to a decline in billed volumes, both concessionaires are supposed to be kept whole on an NPV basis. While near-term profitability could be affected by weaker sales volumes or higher cross-border supply costs for MYNLD, we do not expect a meaningful impact on dividends, given that its low leverage provides flexibility to increase shareholder payouts.

 
Philippine Gaming -1Q26 recap: GGR of online and onsite casinos remain flat QoQ; down YoY
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

GGR remains flat QoQ, down YoY

Both the online gaming segment as well as the (land-based) licensed casinos saw flattish GGR QoQ. Revenues for both segments were down YoY, however, as the e-games segment continues to suffer from the BSP directive delinking e-wallets from online gambling platforms (beginning mid-August 2025), while the licensed casinos continue to hurt from the POGO ban, lower tourist arrivals from China and Korea, as well as the flood control corruption scandal (in our view).

 

Online gaming GGR falls 1% QoQ; PLUS market share inches up

The 1Q26 GGR of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) fell 0.6% QoQ, a tad better than the industry’s 1.3% QoQ drop. This implies a small (30bps) market share gain (to 42.6%).

 

Metro Manila GGR flat QoQ; VIP GGR improves sequentially but remains weak

The 1Q26 GGR of Metro Manila licensed casinos was virtually unchanged QoQ (down 11% YoY) at P35bn, about 85% of the 2019 average. After declining for four consecutive quarters, VIP GGR grew 9% QoQ to P6.9bn. However, we still consider the VIP business to be weak, as GGR was 25% lower YoY and only represents c. 40% of the 2019 average. On the other hand, mass GGR dipped 1% QoQ and 6% YoY to P27.7bn, which is about 15% higher than 2019 levels. 

Excluding Solaire North, which only began operations in 2Q24, 1Q26 mass GGR was still 97% of 2019 levels.

 

Bloomberry’s GGR market share expanded 1.7ppt QoQ to 41.4%, still the highest among Metro Manila licensed casinos, as its GGR grew 4% QoQ to P14.7bn.

 

New integrated resort to open in 3Q26

Alliance Global (AGI PM) plans to open Westside Resorts Manila in 3Q26, which will add c. 20% to table and gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City. We thus anticipate that competition among land-based casinos will intensify.

 

PAGCOR proposes potential relinking of e-wallets to e-games operators

The political backlash on online gambling appears to have subsided, as politicians became preoccupied with the flood control corruption scandal, and more recently, the impeachment trial of the Vice President.

 

Meanwhile, PAGCOR said that it was preparing a position paper to persuade the BSP to reverse its order prohibiting e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms. We believe the BSP order is the main reason that online gaming GGR fell c. 40% from 2Q25 levels. Thus, while we do not expect the BSP to reverse course, the potential lifting of the order would be a major win for e-games operators.

Philippine inflation eases in May amid fuel price rollbacks

Philippine inflation slowed to 6.8% YoY in May from 7.2% in April, coming in below the 7.8% median Street estimate and also below the BSP's forecast range of 7.1-7.9%. This cooled from 7.2% in April and brings YTD-May inflation to 4.5%, vs the FY26 Street/BSP forecast of 6.3%. The deceleration was attributed in part to lower fuel prices following progress in ceasefire talks between the US and Iran, which helped ease global energy prices. The development benefited the Philippines, which imports more than 90% of its oil requirements from the Middle East. Fuel price rollbacks took Transport inflation down to 16.2% (from 21.4%); Food also slid to 5.7% (from 6.0%) as did Housing & Utilities to 7.8% (from 8.2%). BSP Governor Eli Remolona said policymakers would monitor the inflation data as they assess the need for a policy rate adjustment ahead of the central bank’s June 18 meeting.

Trade deficit widens 50% YoY to $6.0bn in April

The April trade deficit widened to US$6.0bn (+50% YoY) as imports picked up while exports growth slowed—this is the highest deficit recorded since Aug 2022. This brings the 4M26 trade deficit to US$19.3bn, 17% wider YoY. Imports for the month climbed 22% YoY to US$13.2bn, accelerating from the 12% increase in Mar. The growth in imports was primarily led by the 106% surge in fuel imports as oil prices soared, which offset the 17% decline in consumer goods. Exports growth decelerated to 6% YoY (US$7.2bn) from 20% YoY the previous month, marking the sixteenth consecutive month of YoY growth in exports. Growth in electronics exports significantly slowed to 1% YoY to US$3.4bn from 33% YoY in Mar, while non-electronics exports rose 11% to US$3.8bn, up from 6% in the previous month.

Government debt inches down to P18.49tn in end-Apr

The government's total debt stock went down by 0.1% MoM (P17.6bn) to P18.49tn as of end-Apr due to the repayment of domestic securities, which offset the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to Mar. As of end-Apr, the USD-PHP depreciated to P60.3 from P59.4 at end-Mar. Domestic debt declined by 1.0% (P119.2bn) MoM to P12.41tn due to the impact of net redemption (P121.6bn) with debt maturities exceeding new issuances. External debt increased by 1.7% (P101.6bn) MoM to P6.06tn due to unfavorable currency movements. The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2% as of 1Q26 from 63.2% at end-2025, the highest since hitting 65.7% in 2005. The Dept. of Finance's forecasts as of Aug 2025 projected the debt-to-GDP ratio to hit 61.8% in 2026, before gradually declining to 61.3% in 2027, 60.3% in 2028, and 59.5% by 2029.

President Marcos mulls extra budget to address oil shock impact

President Ferdinand Marcos stated that the gov't is considering a supplemental budget and legislative amendments to existing laws in order to mitigate the impact of the Middle East conflict. A supplemental budget is an additional spending plan approved through legislation in order to fund programs not covered by the national budget. The amount was not specified, but recall that the Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev) back in April stated that the gov't may require P429bn to fund ongoing response measures if the war were to prolong until December. The budget deficit as of 4M26 was P324bn (-14% YoY) and equivalent to ~20% of the P1.65tn deficit projected for this year.

Philippines targets to build strategic petroleum reserves

Dept. of Energy (DOE) Secretary Sharon Garin stated that the gov't is working on establishing a strategic petroleum reserve program, which involves building new stockpiling facilities that are "sufficient enough to weather any effects or disruptions" in global oil production. Garin elaborated that the DOE is working with state-run firms Philippine National Oil Co. (PNOC) and Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) to evaluate options to build at least 30 days' worth of additional reserves. PNOC will lead the effort to secure reserves, while MIC, the country's sovereign wealth fund, will manage funding for the program. She also stated that Japan during the President's recent state visit affirmed support for the program through conducting feasibility studies and providing capacity building. According to the DOE, the country's current fuel supply as of May 29 is equivalent to 46 days of inventory.

Philippines may face new US tariffs

Additional US tariffs may be imposed on the Philippines as a US Trade Representative (USTR) investigation found that it and 59 other economies have not exerted enough effort to curb the importation of goods made with forced labor. The USTR proposed an additional tariff of 12.5% on imports from the 60 countries, stating that "American workers are forced to compete globally on an unlevel playing field". Fifty-four countries were tagged as economies that do not have any measures against forced labor imports and will face an additional tariff of 12.5%, including the Philippines, Australia, China, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam. Six countries, including Canada, Ecuador, the EU, Indonesia, Mexico, and Pakistan, will face an additional tariff of 10% for having failed to effectively enforce a prohibition of forced labor imports. The announcement comes ahead of the July 24 expiration of the 10% temporary tariff imposed by the Trump administration on February 20.

IT-BPO Association to review industry targets

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines said that it will update its projections for its industry roadmap due to the rapid pace of change brought about by artificial intelligence. The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) currently expects the industry to achieve revenue of US$42bn and employ 1.97mn Filipinos by the end of 2026. These represent 4%-5% growth from the 2025 revenue (US$40bn) and employee (1.9mn) figures. The bull case 2028 targets are US$59bn in revenue and 2.5mn employees, representing 14% revenue CAGR and 10% employment CAGR from 2025 levels. The base case 2028 targets are US$48.5bn in revenue and 2.1mn employees, representing 7% revenue CAGR and 3% employment CAGR from 2025 levels.

PD rates blip up in April, led by Real Estate sector; loan growth accelerates to 12.2%

Philippine banks posted faster loan growth of 12.2% YoY in April, up from 11.6% in March and marking a 14-month high, but asset quality metrics weakened during the month. The system-wide NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) rose 4bps MoM to 3.55%, while NPLs increased 2.0% MoM and 11.7% YoY to P11.3bn. Past Due (PD) loans deteriorated more sharply, with the PD ratio climbing to 4.67% from 4.54% in March and 4.49% a year ago, as the value of PDs rose 3.7% MoM and 16.9% YoY to P27.4bn. Asset quality pressures were most evident in the corporate segment, with Real Estate PD ratios surging 70bps MoM to 5.34% and the value of Real Estate PDs rising 14% MoM to P162bn, while Construction PD ratios remained elevated at 15.95%, up 650bps YoY. Consumer lending also showed signs of deterioration, with PD ratios for both credit cards and auto loans rising 20bps MoM to 7.71% and 8.26%, respectively. Meanwhile, NPL cover declined to 90.85% from 91.37% in the previous month and 95.10% a year earlier.

PAGCOR expects gaming revenue to decline in 2026

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that industry GGR is likely to be between P320bn and P350bn in 2026. This range reflects a 12%-19% decline from the 2025 figure of P396bn and implies GGR could decline further from 1Q26 levels. He cited the delinking of e-wallets from online gambling platforms as well as the Middle East Crisis as the reasons for the forecasted decline. In 1Q26, industry GGR fell 16% YoY (up 1% QoQ) to P87.6bn. GGR of bingo + e-games fell 22% YoY (down 1% QoQ) to P39.9bn, while GGR of (land-based) licensed casinos declined 10% YoY (up 2% QoQ) to P44.5bn. GGR of PAGCOR-operated casinos decreased 8% YoY (up 10% QoQ) to P3.2bn.

Gatchalian elected as Senate president pro-tempore

Senator Sherwin Gatchalian was elected Senate President pro tempore, effectively serving as Acting Senate President after all leadership positions were declared vacant. This follows the attendance of Senator Francis "Chiz" Escudero, who was absent since Monday, breaking the 11-11 impasse between the Senate majority and minority, which allowed the 12 present Upper House lawmakers to declare all seats vacant. The chamber needs at least 13 votes to elect a new Senate chief. New committee chairmanships, including finance, basic education, and agriculture, were passed on to members of the new Senate majority, replacing recently appointed chairpersons who are now part of the minority. Former Senate President Alan Peter Cayetano opposed the changes and labeled the move an "illegal coup d'etat", while Gatchalian said a previous Supreme Court ruling deemed 12 out of 22 members a quorum. The Malacañang and House of Representatives both recognized and welcomed the leadership of acting Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian. The final day of the 1st regular session of Congress will be on June 5, with the 2nd regular session scheduled to resume on July 27 during President Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA).

PSE publishes draft amendments to market making rules

The PSE published draft amendments to its market making rules and stakeholders can send their comments until June 23. The current market making regulations only apply to ETFs, but the PSE seeks to expand the scope of products that can be subject to market making, including Global Philippine Depositary Receipts. Under the proposed rules, a Market Maker must ensure two-way quotes (buy and sell orders) with a maximum spread. Market making orders posted by a Market Maker must have a minimum quantity of five board lots. Market Makers shall maintain Market Making Orders for at least 50% of the time for any given trading day.
