REGIS REPORT

10 April 2026

The PCOMP extended its rebound to another week, finishing above 6,000 as the risk backdrop sharply shifted mid-week from the declaration of a temporary two-week ceasefire between US-Israel and Iran. Optimism over the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz sent crude oil back below $100/bbl -- on track to close lower by over 10% for the week. Despite persistent foreign outflows, the PCOMP climbed 1.7% or 99.53 pts WoW to 6,098.21.

 

Inflation was a key domestic driver, with the hotter-than-expected March CPI print of 4.1% exceeding consensus' 3.8% forecast as well as the BSP's 2-4% target range. This was a 20-month high and took YTD inflation to 2.8%. The oil shock from the Iran war sent CPI higher across all categories, most notably for Transport to 9.9% (from -0.3%), Food to 3.0% (from 1.8%), and Housing & Utilities to 4.5% (from 3.5%).

 

This particularly weighed down sentiment on the consumer sector, which was a largely negative space despite the rebound in the broader market. The three worst-performers in the index for the week were JFC (-4.9% WoW), CNPF (-3.9% WoW), and URC (-3.3% WoW) amid growing concerns over higher input costs and dampened purchasing power.

 

Outside of consumer, cyclicals rallied on the back of improving risk appetite. ALI (+6.4% WoW), which is the biggest laggard on the index so far for 2026, was among the week's top gainers, while big banks MBT (+6.8% WoW) and BDO (+3.2% WoW) were also large outperformers. Of course, Mining (+7.4% WoW) was the best sector index following the rally in metal prices.

 

With light news flow on the corporate front, local macro headlines centered around government efforts to combat the impact of higher energy prices. These include proposed measures providing targeted aid, wage support, and subsidies to vulnerable sectors, alongside direct interventions such as fuel subsidies for PUVs and expanded service contracting. Authorities also moved to stabilize prices through electricity spot market price caps and an extended price freeze on basic goods, while exploring policy flexibility such as on WFH arrangements for BPOs.

 

Foreigners have been net sellers now for six consecutive weeks, slashing YTD inflows to $74m from $367m at its peak just before the Iran war. The peso strengthened to sub-60:$1, finishing the week +0.4% WoW to 59.968.

Philippine Strategy - Inflation – Oil prices hurt, but Rice is Life
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

CPI jumps 4.1% YoY in March – higher than expected… on rice prices?

We had warned of an inflation shock, but did not expect it quite so soon. Nor, it seems did anyone else, as the 4.1% print for March came in higher than the BSP’s forecast of 3.1-3.9%, and consensus of 3.8%. The inflation data for gasoline (+27.3%) and diesel (+59.5%) were not a surprise. What did surprise was the +3.6% YoY / 5.0% MoM jump in rice prices, for reasons unrelated to the Iran conflict. Consumers are bracing for even higher fuel, and electricity prices. A further blow from rice prices would be particularly painful, given its 8.9% weight in the overall CPI, and 17.9% weight for the lower-income households.

 

Fuel prices surge another 5-15% this week; electricity prices to follow

Pump prices in Metro Manila climbed again this week with gasoline up 5% WoW and 70% YoY. Meanwhile, diesel prices spiked 15% WoW and a dizzying 170% YoY. Higher world energy prices will likely mean a 20% or so increase in electricity prices, mitigated in part by the suspension of the spot market as per the UPLIFT “energy emergency” package. We previously noted that electricity consumption in 2Q is typically 20% higher than any other quarter due to the hot summer months, which could see households’ bills jump 30-40% in April/May.

 

Rice – policy support gone wrong?

The retail price of rice has been rising since September 2025, despite regional export prices out of Vietnam and Thailand remaining soft. To recall, the El Niño droughts of 2023 caused a surge in prices well into 2024. 

However, the bumper crop that followed saw prices fall below the pre-2023 levels.

 

In a bid to support local rice farmers, President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr imposed an import ban from August to December 2025, pushing up farmgate prices. The government then increased rice import tariffs from 15% to 20% in January 2026, while only allowing limited import volumes. According to a US Department of Agriculture (USDA) report, the import ban caused a depletion of government and private stocks. The rebuilding of inventory further squeezed prices upwards.

 

Higher input costs and price caps could reduce supply, raise prices further

Higher fuel costs – particularly diesel – will feed directly into the cost of production (machinery), and indirectly into transportation costs. Meanwhile, fertilizer prices are also expected to surge. The same USDA report warned that the increase in farmers’ working capital requirements could even reduce output. Meanwhile, the President approved a P50/kg price cap on imported rice to protect consumers. However, the cap is less than 5% above prevailing retail prices, and could backfire by discourage importation, further limiting supply and pushing up prices of local rice.

 

Rice is Life – consumers to take the hit again

As mentioned, rice is an extremely important crop, accounting for 8.9% of the CPI basket overall, and a whopping 17.9% for the “bottom 30%” of households. Rising price increases will put additional pressure on consumers’ wallets that are already squeezed. Significantly higher inflation prints could also force the BSP’s hand to hike policy rates.

 
Puregold - 4Q25: S&R leads solid results
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY25 profit rises +9% to P11.3bn on revenue growth of +11%

Puregold (PGOLD) delivered another solid result with FY25 profit rising 9% YoY to P11.3bn, mildly better than estimates, as revenues rose a healthy 11% to P242.4bn. For 4Q25 alone, top line growth was also 11%, while net profit rose at a faster +15% rate. Consolidated gross margin for 4Q expanded 1.1ppt YoY, driven by supplier support for the Puregold chain and continued supply chain improvements in S&R.

 

S&R leads top line growth

Group-wide revenues for 4Q rose 11% to P74.4bn, led by S&R, which saw top line jump 19%. Revenues for Puregold was up a more modest 6%. 4Q SSSG for both Puregold (+2.5%) and S&R (+7.6%) accelerated on higher foot traffic. Management’s efforts to raise foot traffic is evidently bearing fruit, particularly at S&R. S&R contributed just over half of PGOLD’s consolidated profit for the year.

 

Management also said that the growth momentum was carried over into 1Q26, with sales within expectations and foot traffic being “unabated”. We are wary, however, of the potential negative impact to growth brought about by higher inflation due to the conflict in the Middle East.

Store expansion ramps up led by Puremart

The company raised its FY26 capex budget to P8.8bn (P6.3bn last year), with plans to open 30 new small-format Puremart stores on top of the additional 30 Puregold stores, three S&R warehouses, and ten S&R QSRs. 2026 will mark the first year that PGOLD will be expanding Puremart since it was first acquired in 2024. The company is aiming for Puremart to reach breakeven by this year or the next. Puremart contributed to <1% of sales in FY25.

 

Fifty percent (50%) dividend payout in two tranches

The company declared P1.97/sh in cash dividends, consisting of one tranche payable in May (P1.18/sh) and another payable in Sep (P0.79/sh). The total dividend declaration implies a ~4.9% dividend yield and is consistent with our forecast of a 50% payout ratio.

 

Modest growth guidance amidst heightened uncertainties

Management again offered modest revenue guidance of 5-7% growth, citing the uncertainty from external risks, despite what sounds like another healthy quarter in 1Q26. Management highlighted measures to drive revenue growth, including further store expansion and supply chain optimization. Note that PGOLD has frequently outpaced the guidance it provides early each year

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Semirara sees high barriers for rival bidders; expresses interest in Mindanao coal mine as Plan B

Semirara Chairman Isidro Consunji is confident that other bidders will struggle to beat them in the upcoming auction. Cited that the coal mine in Semirara island is operating below sea level, a condition that is uncommon globally and thus, raises the bar for prospective bidder, given the lack of specialized expertise. The scale of existing equipment, which comprises a fleet of more than 300 dump trucks, poses another significant hurdle, as building capacity for a new entrant could take years. The company said its mining plan is designed to maintain continuous operations, prevent seawater intrusion, and mitigate adverse effects from production disruptions. Meanwhile, Mr. Consunji said it is looking at a coal mine in Mindanao, as a fallback option. The DOE is bidding out 18 coal blocks, of which ten (10) are located in Semirara island. Two parties are currently known to be participating in the auction: SCC and San Miguel (SMC PM). Submission of applications is set on April 28.

DMCI, Japanese partner bag another subway contract

The Department of Transportation (DoTr) awarded Contract Package (CP) 109 to the joint venture of DMCI and Japan-based Taisei Corp. CP 109 amounts to P16bn (ex-VAT) and involves the connection of the subway to the Ninoy Aquino International Airport. We suspect DMCI’s stake in the project will be <50%. DMCI’s construction arm’s order book stood at P39bn in end-December 2025. The only remaining subway contract yet to be awarded is CP 108, covering the segment in Lawton and Senate-DepEd, located in Taguig City. As of February, the Metro Manila subway project reached a completion rate of 27%, moving the completion target to 2032. Government expects to complete ROW acquisition this year; ROW procured so far is at 82%.

PhilWeb signs agreement with Alliance Global’s Travellers International

PhilWeb Corporation (WEB PM) signed an agreement with Travellers International, the gaming arm of Alliance Global. PhilWeb will provide platform technology, system support, gaming content integration, marketing and operational services for Newport World Resorts’ online gaming platform.

Government says Mitsubishi to locally manufacture country’s first hybrid EV

The Department of Finance said that Mitsubishi Motors Corp. (MMC) plans to manufacture hybrid electric vehicles at its existing plant in Santa Rosa, Laguna. According to Finance Secretary Frederick Go, following a meeting of MMC President and CEO Takao Kato and President Marcos Jr. yesterday, MMC is also exploring the possibility of exporting vehicles produced from its Laguna plant. No further details were disclosed regarding the timeline, specific model, and amount of the proposed investment.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Inflation accelerates to 4.1% in March

Headline inflation picked up to 4.1% in Mar from Feb's 2.4%. This was above the BSP's forecast range of 3.1-3.9% for Mar. The 4.1% print is the highest since Jul 2024 (4.4%) and marks the fourth consecutive month of acceleration in inflation. The BSP released a statement on the higher-than-expected inflation print, citing that the central bank will "carefully consider" incoming data at its upcoming meeting given mounting risks to the inflation outlook. The second out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23. This brings 3M26 inflation to 2.8%, within the BSP's long-term target range of 2-4% and below their forecast of 5.1% for the year. Inflation in Mar accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Feb in food, utilities (electricity, gas, fuel), and transport prices. Food prices overall rose 3.0% YoY, accelerating from the 1.8% pace in Feb, driven by higher prices of rice and vegetables. Rice prices are up 3.6% YoY in Mar, the first YoY price increase after fourteen consecutive months of YoY price declines. Rice prices have notably been on the rise to P50/kg and P48/kg as of Apr-26 for local and imported well-milled rice from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25. Core inflation inched up slightly to 3.2% in Mar from 2.9% in Feb, driven by core non-food inflation which likewise rose by 3.2% in Mar from 2.9% in Feb. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 5.1% (previously revised from 3.6%).

Government posts P171bn budget deficit in Feb

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P171bn in Feb, unchanged YoY but a reversal from the P165bn surplus in Jan due to a boost from non-tax revenues and a rebound in spending. This brings the 2M26 deficit to P6bn, 94% narrower vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in Feb rose 43% YoY, accelerating from Jan's flattish growth (+0.4%), primarily led by non-tax revenues which surged 540% YoY. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections picked up to +8% from +1% in Jan. Customs collections rose a modest 3%, slightly up from 2% growth the previous month. Non-tax revenues surged 540% following the early remittance of dividends from GOCCs. Dividends from GOCCs were remitted in May last year. Expenditures rebounded to growth, up 26% YoY in Feb from the 24% decline in Jan as primary spending reverted to growth. Primary spending for the month surged by 29% YoY. This marks the first month of YoY growth after six consecutive months of YoY declines in gov't spending. Interest payments were broadly flat YoY (+1%).

PH dollar reserves at $107.5bn end-Mar

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Mar fell 5% MoM to US$107.5bn from US$113.3bn the previous month. The BSP stated that the MoM drop in the GIR was primarily due to central bank foreign exchange operations and the correction in gold prices in the month of March. The March GIR figure is a seven-month low. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.9x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.1x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

Gov’t infrastructure spending drops 23% YoY in December; FY25 spending drops 14% YoY

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 14% YoY to P1.32tn in FY25. Derived infrastructure spending in December stood at P126bn, down 23% YoY (+83% MoM). In 4Q25 alone, infrastructure spending fell 30% YoY and 11% QoQ to P283bn. Apart from the delays in payment settlements for public work projects amid the flood control corruption scandal, adverse weather conditions in 4Q were also cited as a reason. Note that the government recently reduced its infra spend target for 2026 to ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) from P1.56tn previously (~5.1% of GDP).

BSP revises CAD forecasts for 2026E from 3.0% to 4.0% of GDP

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised its 2026E CAD forecast to $20.3bn (4.0% of GDP) from $15.3bn (3.0% of GDP), with the increase driven mainly by a wider goods deficit due to a higher import bill of $137.9bn versus $130.2bn previously. It also introduced a 2027E CAD forecast of 4.0% of GDP. Note that the 2025 CAD came in at $16.3bn (3.3% of GDP) versus a prior estimate of 3.2%, with travel receipts of $8.7bn (-6.4% YoY).

Government debt rises to P18.16trn in end-Feb

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.1% MoM to P18.16trn as of end-Feb. The increase in domestic debt was offset by the decline in external issuances as the USD-PHP rate strengthened to P58.3 end-Feb from P59.2 end-Jan. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.7% and 31.3% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 1.2% MoM to P12.48trn. External debt declined by 2.2% MoM to P5.68trn. The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) stated that the debt level is within the P19.06trn FY26 borrowing program.

Customs reports 5% YoY growth in collections for March

The Bureau of Customs (BOC), based on preliminary figures, reported that it collected P84.4bn (+5% YoY) in revenues for March. This surpassed its monthly collection target of P83.3bn by 1.4%. The Bureau aims boost its collections through pushing reforms on digitalization, transparency and ease of doing business. The BOC targets to collect P1.0trn (+7% YoY) in 2026. The bureau was said to be on track reaching 24% (P239bn) of its FY target as end-March.

Lawmakers file measures to mitigate impact of soaring fuel prices

Senators and congressmen have proposed measures to address the high prices of fuel due to the conflict in the Middle East by providing financial aid to vulnerable sectors and imposing other relevant measures. House Ways and Means Committee Chairperson Miro Quimbo was confident that the gov't would need to incur additional debt to fund Bayanihan 3 due to improved revenue collections as of the first quarter. He stated that the gov't has enough cash as well as alternative funding sources such as unprogrammed appropriations to sustain assistance programs. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. stated that he would approve recommendations of the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) if the proposals would be beneficial to the Filipino people. The Bayanihan 3 bill currently has several versions in both the Senate and the House of Representatives. Senator Loren Legarda's version seeks to grant financial aid to those vulnerable which will be funded by higher taxes on the wealthy, luxury goods, and alcohol. Legarda's bill also proposes to grant the President authority, through the appropriate agencies, to impose temporary price controls, rationalize distribution margins, among others. Senator Sherwin Gatchalian eyes for the gov't to allot P98-P406bn to provide assistance and subsidies to "qualified beneficiaries" such as those working in agriculture and transportation as well as the bottom 30% income households. House Ways and Means Committee Vice Chairperson Rolando Valeriano proposes a measure to provide wage support of at least P3,000 for minimum wage earners and interest-free loans for MSMEs. Valeriano also seeks to provide tax credits equivalent to the incremental higher cost of fuel and cargo rates for retailers, wholesale firms, and freight forwarders.

Government to contract PUVs, provide P10/liter fuel subsidy

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in a video statement has announced a P10/liter fuel subsidy for drivers and operators of public utility vehicles (PUVs). The P10/liter fuel subsidy would be implemented for three months starting next week with a 150-liter cap per week, this implies that PUVs can avail of weekly savings of P1,500 and a total savings of P18,000 for the program period. The subsidy is separate from the Dept. of Transportation's (DOTr) one-time fuel subsidy of P1,500-P10,000 as well as the Dept. of Social Welfare and Development's (DSWD) P5,000 cash relief for transport workers. The fuel subsidy will be rolled out for PUVs operating in Metro Manila, initially for those in Commonwealth Avenue then other routes such as Quezon Avenue and Marcos Highway, and eventually other routes nationwide. The fuel subsidy will only be implemented in legitimate gas stations, approved and monitored by the Dept. of Energy (DOE). The President has also announced that the DOTr will implement a nationwide service contracting program for PUVs starting April 15, which will cover around 50,000 PUVs and 1,000 operators, and benefit up to 15mn passengers. Contracted PUVs will be paid P40 to P100/km on top of their regular income, and will cover off-peak hours. The gov't will also provide 20% fare discount for commuters. The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that P238bn, sourced from the 2026 budget, in available funding will be used to support fuel subsidies, cash relief assistance, and other targeted social protection programs.

PEZA seeks to allow 100% WFH for IT-BPO firms

The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) has requested the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) to allow up to 100% work-from-home (WFH) arrangements for IT-BPO firms (in PEZA accredited zones). If permitted, IT-BPO firms will continue to enjoy fiscal incentives despite having up to 100% WFH arrangements. Under the CREATE MORE law, firms registered with incentive-granting agencies can only implement WFH for up to 50% of employees. However, CREATE MORE has a provision that allows higher WFH arrangements during emergencies or natural disasters. President Marcos declared a state of national energy emergency and urged the country to conserve energy.

ERC finalizes P6/kwh administered price for coal-fired plants

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) approved a proposal to cap the price (a.k.a. “Modified Administered Price”) for coal-fired plants at P6/kwh in the spot market. Recall that on March 26, the ERC suspended the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) to address supply risks and price volatility triggered by the Middle East conflict. The ERC has implemented technology-specific pricing and managed dispatch, with priority given to renewables.

Manufacturers, retailers agree with DTI to extend price freeze till end-April

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that the ongoing price freeze of basic and prime commodities will be extended to April 30 from April 16 previously following regular meetings with manufacturers and retailers. DTI Secretary Ma. Cristina Roque stated that prices of basic goods under the DTI's list remain stable as of late with regular price checks to ensure continued compliance.

Government eyes additional revenues from excise, global taxes

The Department of Finance (DOF) is considering eight “new and revised” tax measures to bolster its revenues. According to Finance Secretary Frederick Go. The imposition of a global minimum tax on foreign corporations could be the single largest source of additional revenues. The DOF earlier said it is weighing a domestic top-up tax to align the country with the OECD framework, under which multinational enterprises with global revenues of more than €750 million must pay a 15% global minimum tax. It is also looking at additional excise taxes on several industries that are already being taxed with excise taxes. No other details were provided but Secretary Go said that it aims to complete its review within 30 days.

Dept. of Budget and Management to order 20% cut in gov't agencies' non-essential spending

The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that it will issue a circular ordering gov't agencies to reduce non-essential spending by 20% due to the need for additional fiscal space. The measure was said to expect to generate savings of between P12.8bn-P25.6bn depending on the level of compliance. Budget Secretary Rolando Toledo cited measures including limiting official travel, maximizing virtual meetings, energy conservation, and streamlining opex. Education, health, social, general public service, defense ops with frontline services were said to be exempted.

Meralco energy sales drop 1.8% YoY in 1Q26

Meralco reported its distribution unit’s (DU) energy sales volumes fell 1.8% YoY to 12,273 GWH. Residential and commercial sales declined as colder weather curbed consumption. Industrial sales also slipped, weighed by operational constraints and macroeconomic headwinds affecting steel and plastics, along with limited output from embedded generators. Meralco is forecasting DU energy sales to rise by a modest 3% in 2026, after dropping 0.65% YoY in 2025.

Consumer loans +19.1% in 4Q25, NPLs steady

Consumer loan growth slowed to 19.1% YoY in 4Q25 from 21.3% in 3Q25 and 18.0% in 2024, with share of total loans at 22.4% versus 21.1% at end-2024. Consumer loans ended 2025 at P2,388.0bn, with credit card receivables (P1,194.1bn) exceeding mortgage loans (P1,193.7bn) for the first time on record. Overall consumer loan NPL ratio was 5.32% in 4Q25 versus 5.38% in 3Q25 and 5.34% at end-2024. Mortgage loans grew 8.6% YoY with NPLs at 6.08%. Motor vehicle loans grew 14.4% with NPLs at 5.05%. Credit card receivables grew 27.8% with NPLs at 4.81%. Motorcycle loans grew 22.9% with an NPL ratio of 16.16%.

Government eyes P60bn fiscal support for EVs

The Department of Trade and Industry is looking to provide a fiscal package worth P60bn to incentivize investments in EV production in the country (or Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy, EVIS). In the proposed EVIS, there will be four slots with each participant receiving P15bn worth of fiscal support in exchange for investments in EV manufacturing of four-wheeled EVs. Under the proposal, EVIS will not set a production volume target for the participants. Recall that the previous CARS program only provided up to P9bn worth of incentives for each qualified participant, with a production target of at least 200k units for each enrolled model. The DTI expects to issue an Executive Order before the President’s State of the Nation Address in July.
