REGIS REPORT

06 February 2026

Local shares snapped a two-week slump, with the PCOMP notching a 1.0% or 61.94-pt WoW gain to 6,390.91 as investors weighed faster-than-expected inflation and the first round of 4Q earnings releases. Despite risk-off mood spreading across global markets for much of the week, foreign inflows persisted for Philippine equities, although trading activity overall softened WoW.

 

Inflation accelerated for a second straight month in January, from 1.8% to 2.0%. This was a mildly negative surprise against consensus' 1.8% forecast, and was the first time inflation hit 2.0% since February 2025. While food inflation actually decelerated MoM, prices of housing and utilities as well as restaurants and accommodation services ran hotter in January. For now, market seems to still widely expect a February BSP rate cut, especially on the heels of the huge disappointment in 4Q25 GDP data.

 

Meanwhile, the 4Q25 earnings season is off to a decent start. BPI (-5.6% WoW) once again kicked off the reporting season with results that were in-line with forecasts, and with mid-teens loan growth seen continuing to be ahead of peers, although investors remain focused on how aggressive expansion in the consumer loan growth is impacting the bank's credit costs. Despite decent results, the bank finished the week among the biggest decliners in the index.

 

On the other hand, GLO (+9.4% WoW) was among the week's top gainers, as although 4Q results were just in-line with Street estimates, the stronger-than-expected performance of its core business along with a solid dividend announcement prompted investors to accumulate shares. Competitor TEL (+4.3% WoW) was taken along for the ride on speculation of a positive read-through for the mobile segment. However, CNVRG (-12.9% WoW) saw the opposite effect as profit-taking ensued on worries that it could have lost broadband market share to GLO for the quarter.

 

PLUS (+12.7% WoW) was the biggest index gainer for the week after being the biggest underperformer in January. ICT (+3.0% WoW) was yet again the biggest positive mover for the index, as foreign inflows lifted the share price to fresh highs.

 

Among sector indices, Mining (-10.3% WoW) notably lagged as metal prices corrected against a more risk-off backdrop for global commodities as we entered February.

 

Foreigners remained net buyers for the sixth consecutive week, by $22m which took YTD foreign inflow to $245m. The peso was up for the week, +0.5% WoW to 58.586:$1.

BPI FY25 NPA P66.62bn, slightly behind as credit costs near 100bps in 4Q

In a limited press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported FY25 NPAT of P66.62bn, up 7.4%. This was slightly behind REGIS (P68.2bn) but broadly in line with Bberg consensus of P66.0bn. PPOP was +19% was bang in line with REGIS forecasts, but provision expense came in 10% higher than our estimates, and we estimate that credit costs reached close to 100bps in 4Q25. Loans grew 14.7% YoY, the fastest pace since 2022 (excl the RBC merger in 2024). The bank continues to push hard with its consumer/MSME push, with loans to the segments up 25.8% YoY, while institutional loans were up 10.4%. By our estimate, consumer/MSME loans dipped slightly to 30% of total loans, from 31% in 3Q25. This is due to a typical end-year surge in corporate loans. NII was up 16% thanks to the improving loan mix. BPI claims FY NIMs expanded 28bps YoY. Other income grew 11% YoY. Opex growth was kept in check, up 10% YoY, taking cost-to-income ratio down to 47% from 49% in FY2024. PPOP was up 19% to P103.4bn, a shade higher than our forecast of P102.7bn. Provision expense was P17.8bn, up 170% from a low base, but was 10% higher than our forecast. We estimate credit cost for 4Q25 at around 98bps, compared to 76bps in 3Q25. This took FY credit cost to about 73bps, compared to our FY estimate of 67bps. NPL ratio improved to 2.18% from 2.30% in 3Q, but NPL coverage dipped slightly to 94.9% from 96.5% in 3Q25.

Globe FY25 down 3% to P20.9bn

Core income, as reported, for FY25 was down slightly (-3%) at P20.9bn. After preferred share distributions, we estimate core profit to common shareholders at P19.4bn (-4% YoY), which is above expectations. Core operations were unexpectedly strong but contributions from Mynt fell again QoQ. Full-year service revenue was practically unchanged YoY but they were actually quite strong for 4Q alone (+6% YoY, +5% QoQ). This is true particularly for mobile. Mynt contributed P6.1bn for FY25 to Globe’s bottom line, up around 60% YoY. This implies 4Q contribution was around P800m, down by ~40% QoQ. Wireline broadband subs increased by 107k in the last quarter – still a pretty solid outcome. GLO now has 1.795m wireline broadband subs. Capex for FY25 was P46bn (~US$800m), 28% of revenue and management is guiding for capex to be “below US$1bn” for FY26. The company also declared a dividend of P25/share for 1Q26, unchanged from the prior years. This puts it on track to pay P100 for the year.

ICTSI's MICT port volumes dip 2% YoY in 4Q25; Nationwide foreign container volumes flat

Data from the Philippine Ports Authority showed that ICTSI’s MICT saw TEU volumes slip 2% YoY to 776k units in 4Q25, reversing the 8% growth rate in 9M25. In contrast, Asian Terminals (ATI PM) South Harbour saw volumes rise 5% YoY in 4Q25, decelerating sharply from +16% pace in 9M. Market share loss due to South Harbour’s headline 40% capacity increase in May (here). We believe ATI’s market share gains will be short-lived, as MICT’s Berth 8 Phase 2 expansion should be completed in 2026. Brings MICT’s FY25 volumes up 5% to 3.07mn TEUs, just a tad short of our estimate (3.1mn TEUs, +6% YoY). We estimate MICT accounts for 22% of volumes, 23% of revenues, and 20% of EBITDA. Nationwide foreign container volumes were YoY/QoQ flat at 1.5mn TEUs in 4Q25, bringing FY25 volumes up 8% YoY to 6.0mn TEUs.

Inflation inches up to 2.0% in January

Headline inflation picked up to 2.0% in Jan from Dec's 1.8%. This was a tad higher than consensus expectations of 1.8% but still within the BSP's forecast range of 1.4-2.2% for Jan. This is the first time inflation has hit 2.0% since Feb 2025 (2.1%) as the CPI has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the previous ten months. Inflation in Jan accelerated slightly given faster YoY increases vs Dec in electricity, gas, fuel as well as restaurants and accommodation services prices. Food prices overall rose 1.1% YoY, decelerating from the 1.4% pace in Dec—as vegetable prices normalize following the impact of strong rains during 2H25. Notably, rice prices were up MoM by 3.4%—marking the third consecutive MoM price increase. Rice prices are down 8.5% YoY in Jan, the thirteenth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. Core inflation accelerated to 2.8% in Jan from 2.4% in Dec, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 2.7% in Dec from 2.1% in Jan. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.6% and 3.0%. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

Finance Secretary Frederick Go stated that the gov't has set aside P1.4trn in primary spending for 1Q26 to support economic recovery. This follows the recent disappointing 4Q25 and FY25 GDP prints of 3.0% and 4.4%, respectively. The P1.4trn targeted primary spending is 17% higher YoY than 1Q25's P1.2trn figure. As of 11M25, primary spending is up only 0.8% to P4.2trn. Go highlighted that he is constantly coordinating with the Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) on the release of funds in order for the gov't to "get back on track this year". Recall that the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) has reduced the gov't's GDP growth targets for 2026-2027, revising the 2026-2028 6-7% target to 5-6% in 2026 and 5.5-6.5% in 2027, while keeping the 2028 6-7% target unchanged. For 2026/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 5.2/6.0% and 5.5/5.7%, respectively.

The Dept. of Finance stated that the gov't is targeting to sell three significant real estate assets this year, alongside privatization proceeds of Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) last year, which could amount to a combined P101bn in revenues. Note that P101bn was the initial privatization target revenue last year which was cut down to P5bn as the gov't cited "slight delays" in selling properties. The three properties targeted to be sold this year are the Food Terminal, Inc. (FTI) property in Taguig (P40.4bn), Mile Long Complex in Makati (P12.3bn), and the Atrium of Makati (P449mn). The privatization of the 744 MW CBK hydroelectric power complex garnered an offer of P36.3bn from the Thunder Consortium (Aboitiz Renewables, Inc., Sumitomo Corp., and Electric Power Development Co.). The gov't targets P350bn in nontax revenues for 2026, revised upwards from the previous P249bn target.

Colliers: Residential net take-up worsens in 4Q25

Residential vacancy in Metro Manila was relatively flat QoQ at 25%. Net take-up of Metro Manila condominium units fell 80% QoQ (but quintupled YoY) to 1,200 units, due to higher cancellations QoQ. Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy declined slightly to 19.4% in 4Q25, from 19.8% in 3Q25. Makati CBD vacancy inched up c. 1% QoQ to 9%. BGC vacancy fell c. 2ppt QoQ to 10%. Colliers said that the number of unsold condominium units in Metro Manila was equivalent to about 8 years’ worth of sales, down from 12 years in 2Q25. We estimate that in 3Q25, unsold condominium units represented c. 9 years of sales. Net office take-up fell 21% QoQ to 91,000sqm. This was a reversal from net incremental closures of 185,000sqm in 4Q24. Office rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ. They inched up modestly in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio. Colliers estimates that land values in Makati CBD, Fort Bonifacio, and Ortigas Center rose 3%-5% QoQ. Office and residential vacancies in these districts were lower than the Metro Manila averages.

Limited withdrawal of empties causes congestion at Manila ports

Congestion has built up at the two container ports in Manila, following limited withdrawal of import containers during the holiday season. ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) yard utilization rates for reefers was at 106% and overall utilization at 82% as of February 2. Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbour has a utilization rate of 64% of reefers and an overall utilization rate of 89%. ICTSI attributed the congestion to the following: high cargo volume from December to pre-Chinese New Year; limited withdrawal of import containers during the holidays; and lack of trucks to return empty containers to full utilized depots, limiting the ability of trucks to collect laden containers from the ports. The high yard utilization rate of South Harbor of 89%, even after its 40% capacity expansion in May 2025, suggests that market dynamics will remain favorable. While yard utilization usually peaks at this time of year, conditions this year appear more severe than normal and should result in a temporary YoY boost in MICT’s storage revenues. We estimate MICT accounts for 20% of consolidated volumes and 20% of revenues.

House panel dismisses Marcos impeachment complaints

The House Committee on Justice dismissed the two impeach complaints filed against President Marcos, which accused him of corruption, violating the constitution, and betraying public trust, as they were deemed "insufficient in substance". The House is expected to convene for a plenary vote to either uphold or override the committee’s findings. If upheld, the junking of the complaints will trigger the one-year ban rule given the Constitutional rule that an official can only undergo one impeachment proceeding per year.

President Marcos' net satisfaction rating remains neutral in November SWS survey

President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating remained "neutral" at -3 in Nov 2025 from "neutral" -5 in Sep 2025. Note that Marcos’ net satisfaction rating declined to -5 in Sep from "moderate" +10 in Jun, with the drop occurring after his flood control scandal expose during his SONA last July. Marcos' gross satisfaction rating inched up 1% to 40% from Sep to Nov. Gross dissatisfaction dropped a tad to 43% from Sep’s 44%. Gross undecided was stable at 17%. SWS cited the following movements in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas: -15 points in Metro Manila (from “neutral” -2 to “poor” -17), +5 points in Balance Luzon (from “neutral” +8 to “moderate” +13), +10 points in the Visayas (from “neutral” -8 to “neutral” +2), and +1 point in Mindanao (from “poor” -28 to “poor” -29). Marcos' average net satisfaction was zero in 2025, down from +25 in 2024 and +51 in 2023. Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,200 adults from Nov 24-30, 2025.
