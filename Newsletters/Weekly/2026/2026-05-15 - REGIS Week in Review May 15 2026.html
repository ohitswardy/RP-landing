REGIS REPORT

15 May 2026

The PCOMP struggled for direction this week as the final batch of 1Q26 earnings results came in, which investors weighed against persisting tensions in the Middle East that has kept oil prices elevated and global supply chains constrained. For the week, the index clocked a gain of 0.3% or 15.80 pts WoW to 5,976.77 on slightly softer turnover and with foreigners only slightly net buyers for the second straight week.

Outside of corporate stories and global geopolitics, investors also watched the local politics scene  that saw the impeachment of VP Sara Duterte by the Lower House, and a shake-up in Senate leadership with Minority Leader Alan Cayetano replacing Vicente "Tito" Sotto III as Senate President. With the body now effectively controlled by Senators that are allied with the Duterte camp, VP Sara Duterte's conviction becomes even less likely, according to political commentators.

The complete picture painted by 1Q results broadly showed demand conditions at least remained relatively healthy, although the outlook points to increasing pressure on corporate profitability from margin compression and higher financing costs, as well as growing cautiousness from consumers and businesses. Aggregate PCOMP earnings managed to grow by mid-single-digits, although earnings misses outnumbered results that were in-line or ahead of forecasts.

The biggest laggard for the week was JFC (-14.5% WoW), whose 1Q earnings trailed behind estimates as margins were hit by the sharp increase in direct costs. Selling was exacerbated by the announcement of its deletion from the MSCI Standard Index at the May rebalancing, although it will be added into the Small Cap Index with changes to officially take effect on the last trading day of the month. The next two largest decliners also sold off on index exclusion bets, with speculators torn between CNVRG (-8.2% WoW) and PLUS (-7.2% WoW) to be replaced by MYNLD (-0.4% WoW) at the PCOMP semi-annual rebalancing in August.

Investors also took cues from this round of earnings releases. AC (-5.1% WoW) was among the worst performers as results were behind forecasts, dragged by the misses in BPI (+2.6% WoW)  and ALI (-1.4% WoW) which already reported in the last weeks. Its power unit ACEN (-4.3% WoW) also missed estimates due to higher-than-expected financing costs. The bright spot was GLO (+6.7% WoW) which this week reported earnings that were within expectations, marked by solid mobile and broadband growth. Outside of earnings, GLO was the top gainer for the week after an online broadsheet reported that the IPO of its fintech arm Mynt (GCash) was set for July, according to unnamed sources.

Other top performers for the week were RCR (+5.8% WoW), which surged Friday on strong foreign inflow, as well as ICT (+4.6% WoW), which closed above the P800-mark for the first time ever, also lifted by foreign buying.

Foreigners were marginally net buyers for the second consecutive week, by $5m. The peso weakened to an all-time-low, -1.8% WoW to 61.725:$1.

PLDT - 1Q26: Modest growth; refreshing our TP
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q revenue and income grow 3%

PLDT reported headline profit of P8.85bn for 1Q26, down 2% YoY, on the back of service revenue of P54.9bn (+3%). Excluding one-off or exceptional items, we estimate core income for PLDT was also around P8,85bn, +2% higher vs. the comparable figure the previous year. Both top line and bottom line growth were, while modest, within expectations.

 

Mobile revenue up 1%

Wireless service revenues were 1% higher YoY, but we view it as creditable performance as it was able to maintain revenue market share compared to 4Q25. Mobile data traffic grew 10% YoY also implying increased data usage per subscriber albeit less than the competition.

 

Broadband revenue dips 1%; subscriber adds lower

Broadband revenue was just a bit lower YoY despite the fact that subscriber count was 9% higher. Management attributed it to a decision not to collect from those in some calamity-hit areas but the overall drop in implied ARPU is almost certainly due to new, lower-revenue subscribers.

Net subscriber additions for wireline broadband amounted to only 44k, the lowest since 4Q24. The company attributed this to its migration to a new system, which hindered new installations. Demand for the service was otherwise strong.

 

Capex guidance

PLDT declined to provide guidance on anything apart from capex (“mid-50bn peso range”) as it faces an uncertain environment due to soaring energy prices.

 

Adjusting our estimates and target price

We adjust our near-term profit estimates slightly (3% or less), updating for the latest results. We also lift our target price to P1,600 after rolling forward our DCF-based valuation and reducing our capex estimates closer to guidance. While not an exciting growth story, we think the offers good value and an attractive dividend yield (~8%).

 
ACEN Corp - Higher financing costs lead to 1Q26 miss; Revising forecasts/TP
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

ACEN 1Q26 Core Profit Falls 35% YoY to P948mn – Behind Estimates

ACEN reported profits of P2.9bn in 1Q26, up 50% YoY and inclusive of one-time net gains amounting to P1.5bn. The gains reflect the remaining collectible claim from the pre-terminated contract with Meralco, as well as the revaluation of some of its India assets. These were offset by a large provision from the ongoing FIT issue in Vietnam. Removing one-offs and preferred dividends, we estimate recurring profit to common fell 35% YoY to P948mn, trailing both Regis and Street estimates on higher-than-expected financing costs.

 

1Q26 EBITDA Dips QoQ; Beneficiary of Elevated WESM

Core attributable EBITDA improved 20% YoY to P6.7bn on base effects from last year’s (a) wind farm repairs in the Philippines and (b) lower solar irradiance, as well as higher output from its new RE facilities. Amid cooler weather conditions, ACEN reduced output from its 244MW coal-fired facility, leading to a mild QoQ decline in EBITDA. With conditions reversing so far in 2Q, management said the company is currently benefiting from elevated spot prices, with ~30% of capacity un-contracted.

While pleased with the resulting outsized profits, management intends to use the opportunity to lock in long-term contracts and further reduce exposure to the spot market.

 

Recalibrating Forecasts

We revise our EPS estimates, with the most significant change driven by the removal of our assumption for the previously planned stock rights offering (SRO). The indefinite deferral of the SRO resulted in higher projected net debt and, consequently, higher financing cost assumptions, which more than offset the impact of higher spot price assumptions. While we cut our profit forecasts, we raise our EPS estimates by 9–32% due to the lower share count (see Fig. 1 on page 2). We likewise trim our TP to P3.00/sh from P3.20/sh previously (see Fig. 2).

 

While the SRO appears to have been deferred, we believe ACEN will still need to raise capital through other means soon, with its forecast net debt-to-EBITDA now at 8x.

 
Converge ICT - 1Q26: Further slowdown
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q profits flat YoY as revenue growth slows to +4%

Converge ICT reported 1Q26 profit of P3.02bn practically unchanged YoY and trending below our estimates. Revenue for the quarter was P11.2bn, up 4% YoY, a growth rate below our estimates as well as company guidance (+8-10%). The rebound in net subscriber additions is a positive signal that 4Q25 weakness was just a blip.

 

Revenue, especially residential segment revenue, disappoints

Group revenue grew 4% in 1Q26 but revenue from the residential segment (82% of total revenue) was barely up (+1%) even though subscriber count was 14% higher. The drop in average ARPU is clear as virtually all net subscriber adds over the past year were for the lower end and prepaid plans. CNVRG added a net 105k subscribers in 1Q26 but saw a net decrease in subscribers for its main (and higher end) Fiber X service.

In fact, the net decline was also the case in 4Q25. The Enterprise segment (18% of revenue) was the brighter spot, with revenue there rising 16% YoY.

 

Hoping to limit churn

Churn (disconnections) improved to 2.4% in 1Q26 (2.9% in 4Q) and management hopes to cut it further to 2.0%. Churn has been a key factor behind the relative slowdown in net subscriber adds. Management believes the deterioration in service levels has been a reason for the rise in churn and it is now working to improve service partly by adding staff.

 

Sticking to guidance of 8-10% revenue growth

Management is sticking to its guidance of 8-10% growth in revenue for this year despite the rather soft 1Q26. We reckon this goal will be challenging to meet.

 
Robinsons Land -1Q26: reservation sales of standalone projects weaken, JV sales rebound
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

1Q26 Net Profit Rises 2% YoY

Robinsons Land’s 1Q26 net profit rose 2% YoY to P3.54bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. The weak profit contribution from the joint venture (JV) residential segment was offset by stronger-than-expected operating profit of the malls and the standalone residential business (see Figure 2).

 

Reservation Sales of Standalone Projects Weaken Further; JV Projects Rebound

While reservation sales of RLC’s standalone projects weakened further, sales of its JV projects showed remarkable improvement. Despite maintaining aggressive promotions, reservation sales of standalone projects fell 46% YoY and 59% QoQ to P455mn, which only represents only c. 10% of the 2019 average. Furthermore, the value of unsold residential inventory of its standalone residential projects is P44.4bn (-1% QoQ), which we estimate is equivalent to 9.3 years of reservation sales (after cancellations).

 

On the other hand, sales of JV residential projects, which are predominantly high-end, rebounded (from P820mn in 4Q25 and P47mn in 1Q25) to P3.3bn, as the company instituted more aggressive payment schemes (but not price discounts). The unsold inventory level of JV projects is also more manageable at P11.2bn, equivalent to 1.8 years of reservation sales (also after cancellations).

Same-Mall Revenue Growth and Mall EBIT Margin Fall on High Base

Mall revenue rose 7% YoY on the back of a 2% increase in floor area and a 1ppt YoY increase in occupancy level (to 94%). Same-mall revenue growth slowed to 4%, at the low end of the historical range, after the strong figure of 8% in FY25. Although the mall EBIT margin fell 2.1ppt YoY, this was largely due to the high base of 45% (in 1Q25), the highest level in the past five years. The 1Q26 mall EBIT margin of 42.9% is above the historical average of c. 40% and management expects margins to rise further as it signs power supply contracts at lower rates over the coming months.

 

Office Occupancy Improves QoQ

Office occupancy increased 1ppt QoQ to 86%, better than the industry average of 82%. On average, RLC renewed office contracts at flat rental rates, similar to the experience in FY25.

 

RLC Raises Dividend Per Share (DPS) by 33%

RLC raised its DPS by 33% to P1/share, which represents 36% of FY25 earnings and translates to a yield of 5.6%.

 

Cost Impact from Middle East Conflict Contained So Far

Management said that the increases in construction costs brought about by the Middle East conflict are within its budgeted buffers (so far).

 
Manila Water - 1Q26: East Zone spending drops 25%
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1Q26 Core Profit Dips to P3.1bn on Higher Acquisition Financing Costs

Headline 1Q26 net profit of P4.42bn (+24% YoY) was flattered by a P1.0bn gain arising from refinancing WawaJV’s project loan. Reported core profit declined 1% YoY to P3.54bn, and after accounting for the share of participating preferreds, we estimate core profit to common stood at P3.1bn, looking short of consensus’ P15bn full-year estimate. Despite the 12% tariff hike at its core East Zone (EZ) unit, profit was flattish due to higher financing costs related to the Wawa acquisition. Crucially, cash spending for the EZ fell 25% YoY.

 

1Q26 Profits of EZ+Wawa Down 5% YoY

Management deemed it best to consolidate Wawa into EZ’s financials. Despite EBITDA/EBIT improving by 20%/11% to P6.4bn/P5.2bn, core profit from this segment fell 5% to P3.08bn. Cash spending (opex + capex) fell 25% YoY to P4.4bn, taking cumulative spending to P69bn, trailing its 2023-2027 regulatory commitment. While a catch up is not impossible, there are opportunity losses from the spending lag, given the regulatory framework.

Leverage Ratio Rises

With additional loans, consolidated net debt rose 26% QoQ to P157bn, equivalent to a net gearing of 167% and net debt-to-EBITDA of 5.4x. Meanwhile, parent gross debt to equity of 1.5x is below the covenant threshold of 3x.

 

Agua de Puebla Acquisition Updates

Management is undertaking regulatory engagements to complete the acquisition of a 75% stake in Agua de Puebla (ADP), which it is acquiring for MXN3.6bn (~P12.7bn). Management is hopeful the deal will be completed by 2H and reiterated that gaining full control of ADP remains a key objective. AGP holds the concession for Puebla City and nine other municipalities in the state of Puebla, serving a population of 1.5mn. The concession expires in 2044 but is extendable up to 2074. AGP’s billed volumes reached 245MLD in 2024, equivalent to about 17% of the East Zone’s volumes.

 
Nickel Asia - 1Q26: Weather disruptions hit mining unit
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q26 Profit of P372mn Trails Regis’ FY26E

NIKL reported its 1Q26 profit was lower by 26% YoY to P372mn. Excluding last year’s one-off gain, this year’s P372mn profit marked a turnaround from the recurring P26mn loss posted a year earlier, due to the commissioning of its 120MWp solar facility. However, results are trailing our full-year forecast due to weaker-than-expected ore sales volumes.

 

We make minor EPS revisions to our model to reflect higher benchmark LME nickel prices (US$18k/MT versus spot prices of US$19k/MT) and elevated energy costs. We maintain our BUY rating with a TP of P6.40/sh.

 

Ore Sales Volumes Drop 8% YoY in 1Q26; ASPs Up 10%

Revenues from the sale of nickel ore was mildly up (+3%) to P2.5bn in 1Q26 and so was EBITDA of P869mn (+2%). NIKL prioritized the delivery of limonite ore to the HPAL facilities, as inclement weather adversely affected output, with overall sales volumes dropping 8% YoY to 2.27mn MT. 

ASPs rose 10% YoY to US$18/MT, driven by higher benchmark nickel LME prices (+11%) and a more favorable pricing for HPAL ore sales.

 

We estimate cash cost per MT rose by 11% YoY to ~US$12, even before the new taxes under the enacted mining fiscal regime law (Republic Act, RA 12253). NIKL noted that the Department of Finance has yet to issue the corresponding revenue regulations, even though the Implementing Rules and Regulations was already issued last February. Our forecasts reflect the full-year impact of RA 12253.

 

Commissioning of New Solar Plant Buoys 1Q26 Power Performance

The commissioning of the 120MWp solar facility, a project undertaken with Shell Philippines, drove the power segment to record a profit of P91mn in 1Q26, reversing last year’s losses. We do not expect these profits to be sustainable, however, as the RE portfolio remains relatively small and largely contracted, while the 10MW diesel facility is likely to come under pressure from elevated fuel prices.

 
Philippine Seven - 1Q26: Improving revenue growth; expansion costs weigh on profit
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1Q26 net profit +5%, revenue +14%

Philippine Seven reported net profit of P629mn in 1Q26, up 5% YoY, behind estimates. EBIT and revenue were within expectations, rising 8% and 14% to P1.1bn and P24.8bn, respectively, while systemwide sales increased 13% to P26.1bn. Profit lagged revenue growth on higher interest expense on lease liabilities as well as lower interest income from internally funded working capital and continued network expansion. The company opened 84 stores (net) in the quarter and is aiming to hit 5,000 stores by year-end from 4,575 as of 1Q.

 

Sales momentum strengthens in 1Q

Revenue growth accelerated to +14% in 1Q26 from +9% in 4Q25 as SSSG also improved to +4.4% from +3.1%. Although the YoY comparisons were partly aided by a low base, management cited “improved consumer demand” which was reflected in higher average ticket size and increased consumption in key categories such as tobacco, non-alcoholic beverage, and RTE (ready-to-eat). The continued rollout of card payment terminals also contributed to higher basket sizes with 83% (3,820) of stores covered as of end-Mar with 100% implementation by 2Q26.

Card payment terminal sales accounted for 8% of total sales, up from 6% in 4Q25.

 

Initial 2Q trends remain encouraging

Management guided that the first two months of 2Q are tracking similarly to the +4.4% SSSG registered in 1Q. Growth remained resilient despite rising inflation, with the company attributing stronger RTE demand to potential consumer downtrading from casual dining and QSRs. Management aims to expand RTE availability further to meet growing consumer demand.

 

Margin pressures to persist on store rollout and elevated fuel costs

Higher opex from store expansion and rising fuel costs are putting pressure on margins but this was partially offset by supplier distribution subsidies, resulting in a 0.2ppt drop in operating margin to 4.4%. The company is implementing mitigation measures by streamlining logistics and securing cheaper power for its stores.

 
Cebu Air - 1Q26 core profit up 3x but headwinds ahead
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q26 EBIT Rises by 50% YoY but FX Losses Drive 1Q26 Headline Loss

Cebu Air reported a headline loss of P420mn in 1Q26 largely from a chunky FX loss. Underlying operations were strong, with EBIT up 54% YoY to P3.0bn and EBITDA up 26% YoY to P8.4bn. Management noted that the impact of the oil price shock has yet to be reflected in its 1Q results and warned of potential losses ahead amid record-high jet fuel prices.

 

Pax Volumes +8% in 1Q26; 11% Rise in Ancillary Yields Offset Mild Drop in Fares

Passenger volumes grew 8% YoY to 7.5mn, buoyed by the tail-end of the holiday season and the early summer travel period. Average fares declined 2% YoY to ~P3k due to the cancellation of Middle East flights in March, though this was offset by an 11% increase in ancillary yields. Fuel expenses were flat, reflecting soft oil prices prior to the war.

 

Balancing Volume Growth and Fare Increases

The ongoing conflict has created significant uncertainty over the demand outlook. While fares were initially raised by 30–40% in April, softer demand prompted the company to scale back these increases, which helped demand to recover in May. 

Management has tempered its full-year seat growth guidance to 9–11%, even as it acknowledged that further recalibration may be necessary amid the uncertain environment.

 

Given record-high fuel prices, management’s priority is to minimize losses by ensuring flights at least cover variable costs. While fuel supply is not a concern, the company is seeking to lock in fuel prices, having hedged ~11% of its 3Q fuel requirements through collar structures to mitigate the volatility.

 

2026 Capex Guidance Trimmed, with Spending Back-Loaded

Capex was trimmed to P32bn from the previously planned P35bn, with much of the spending pushed to the latter part of the year. Financing for these investments has already been secured and, together with its P20bn cash balance, provides ample liquidity runway. The company also has several unencumbered aircraft and engines that could be monetized should additional liquidity be required.

Aboitiz Power 1Q26 earnings surge on improved plant availability

The company reduced WESM exposure to 8% of baseload capacity from 30% in 1H25, while management indicated that higher WESM prices in early 2Q have remained beneficial and may support securing higher-priced long-term contracts. Distribution unit EBITDA increased 13% YoY to P2.3bn, driven by a 6% increase in sales volumes during the quarter. Security Bank 1Q26 sees higher provisions continue to weigh on earnings

Asset quality metrics weakened, with the NPL ratio increasing to 3.08% from 2.85% and NPL coverage declining to 81%, although management maintained its FY26 credit cost guidance of 190bps and indicated that provisioning pressures have likely peaked.

PH dollar reserves at $104.1bn end-Apr

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Apr declined 2.4% MoM to US$104.1bn from US$106.6bn the previous month. The April GIR figure is a 15-month low. The MoM drop in the GIR was primarily due to the central bank’s foreign exchange operations. The USD-PHP rate depreciated to P60.3 in end-Apr from P59.4 in end-Mar. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 6.9x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

Marcos sets P50/kg price cap on imported rice

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. set the price ceiling for imported rice at P50/kg, he highlighted the need to protect consumers by containing prices given the national energy emergency and by deterring market manipulation (e.g., profiteering, unjustified price increases). Executive Order (EO) 118 puts a cap on the price of 5% broken imported rice for an initial period of 30 days and takes effect immediately. The National Price Coordinating Council is tasked to review the cap every 15 days for possible adjustment or discontinuation. Rice prices are currently at P50.3/kg and P48/kg as of end-May for local and imported well-milled rice, rising from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25.

Banks' asset quality stable in March as loan growth accelerates

Philippine banks' asset quality remained broadly stable in March despite macroeconomic pressures, while loan growth accelerated to 11.6% YoY. The non-performing loan (NPL) ratio excluding interbank loans and reverse repos was unchanged month-on-month at 3.51% and improved by 5bps YoY, although nominal NPLs rose 2.7% MoM and 10.2% YoY to P568bn. Past due (PD) ratios increased only marginally to 4.54%, with the value of PD loans rising 2.9% MoM and 13.9% YoY to P736bn, while restructured loan ratios improved to 2.09%. Sector data showed the most pronounced deterioration in construction, where NPL and PD ratios reached 13.15% and 16.08%, respectively, while manufacturing and trade also showed early signs of stress. In contrast, real estate asset quality remained stable and credit card delinquencies increased only gradually. Recent bank earnings commentary indicated that increases in bad loans among larger banks were primarily linked to corporate and commercial borrowers affected by issues surrounding flood control projects.

Philippine port regulator implements fuel surcharge

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) has issued guidelines on the implementation of fuel surcharge adjustment (FSA) that port operators may impose as a temporary cost-recovery measure due to the increase in fuel prices. The FSA will be applied to stevedoring and arrastre tariffs, and the formula will be based on the Department of Energy’s published fuel prices. FSA will not be part of the PPA’s revenue share, concession fees, or similar charges.

PAGCOR sets caps on rebate and cashback programs on e-games

PAGCOR instituted caps on rebate and cashback programs on e-games. These caps will be effective beginning May 15. Rebates based on player turnover or deposits: For slot machine games, electronic bingo, numeric games and sports betting, operators may offer cash rebates of up to 1.5% based on player turnover or deposits. Casino table games and arcade-type games are excluded from the turnover-based rebate provision. Cashbacks based on player net losses: Cashback programs based on player net losses may be offered across all types of electronic games, capped at a maximum rate of 15%.

House impeaches Vice President Sara Duterte

The House of Representatives has voted to impeach Vice President Sara Duterte on vote with 257 lawmakers in favor, 25 against, and nine abstaining. The 257 votes were more than enough for the minimum one-third or 106 House members vote requirement for the articles of impeachment to be transmitted to the Senate for trial. This marks the second impeachment of VP Duterte after the Supreme Court voided the first case initiated last year over procedural flaws, with the current complaint garnering support from more lawmakers than the previous impeachment backed by 215 legislators. The articles of impeachment allege culpable violation of the Constitution and betrayal of public trust by VP Duterte from misused confidential funds, bribery of officials, amassed unexplained wealth, and an assassination plot against the President.

House transmits articles of impeachment vs VP Duterte to Senate

The House of Representatives announced that it had formally transmitted the committee report that contains the articles of impeachment against VP Sara Duterte to the Senate, two days after she was impeached by the House. The House stated that the transmittal marks the start of the next constitutional phase of the impeachment process and paves the way for Senate trial. Recall that the newly installed Senate President Alan Peter Cayetano stated that he vows to convene the impeachment court after transmittal to start the trial "forthwith". An impeached official is "convicted" (or acquitted) if at least two-thirds of the Senate find the official guilty (or not guilty) of the charges filed by the House.

Senate Minority Leader Alan Cayetano voted as new Senate President

Senate Minority Leader Alan Cayetano has replaced Senator Vicente "Tito" Sotto III as the new Senate President after securing votes from thirteen senators, compared to nine for Sotto, while two senators abstained from the vote. The reappearance of Senator Ronaldo "Bato" Dela Rosa, who was absent from the Senate since November 2025, was crucial in securing the minimum majority of thirteen votes in support of Cayetano. Senator Tito Sotto in a comment stated that they (the new minority) will insist that the impeachment articles, once submitted to the Senate, are to be taken up.
