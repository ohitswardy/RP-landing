REGIS REPORT

14 August 2026

Philippine equities extended gains to another week, with the PCOMP inching up 0.1% or 6.95 pts WoW to  6,297.30, bringing its YTD advance to 4.0%. Corporate action and M&A themes drove outsized gains for certain names, while the last batch of 2Q earnings results also weighed on stock performance.

Dominating corporate headlines was KKR's proposal to acquire an additional 8.43% stake in FGEN (+59.1% WoW) from FPH (+14.4% WoW) and launch a voluntary tender offer for FGEN's entire 11.67% public float, which would pave the way for a delisting. Media reports indicated a P35/share price for FGEN, versus P19.60/share before the announcement, sending FGEN shares through the roof with FPH also outperforming.

Meanwhile, SM (+1.2% WoW) announced plans to divest its 34% stake in AT (-5.7% WoW) to another listed company in 2027, shortly following DHI’s (+26.0% WoW) disclosure of its acquisition of a 20.4% stake in AT from APO (-43.9% WoW). DHI will pay P857m in cash and assume P2.39bn in remaining subscription payable. Traders are heavily speculating that DHI could also be the buyer of SM’s stake and potentially become AT’s controlling shareholder, as part of the consolidation of the Sy family's mining assets, possibly including its interest in the Tampakan project which it jointly controls with the Consunjis.

Thus DMC (+8.5% WoW) shares also rallied -- the top gainer for the PCOMP this week -- as the mining angle continues to unfold. This is a stark contrast to the slump in subsidiary SCC (-7.8% WoW) -- in turn the biggest laggard in the index -- after the government announced it is deferring the Semirara coal mine auction by at least another two months into 4Q to rewrite contract terms.

The final batch of 2Q earnings also drove stock-specific moves. Beyond M&A speculation, DMC was supported by strong 2Q results driven by its nickel, property, cement and construction businesses. JFC (+2.5% WoW) advanced for the week despite lowering FY26 guidance following softer domestic SSSG. CNVRG (-8.1% WoW) missed expectations as high disconnections took net subscriber adds for the quarter to negative territory. TEL (-4.9% WoW) results were also slightly behind forecasts as it lost revenue share in both mobile and broadband, although the sharp sell-down at the close of the week was due more to its disclosure that internal control deficiencies on its financial reporting had prompted its auditor to withdraw its opinions on the company's 2025 financials, with an independent review still ongoing.

On the property sector, MSCI announced results of its latest index review that will see the deletion of ALI (-4.1% WoW) from the Standard Index at the August rebalancing. Meanwhile, an overnight placement for c.420m shares of SMPH (+0.4% WoW) took place midweek, priced at P18.12 which represented at the time a 7.45% discount to the previous close.

Foreign outflow accelerated to $60m for the week, largely due to the SMPH placement. The peso depreciated by 0.9% WoW to 61.443:$1, while the PH10Y yield rose 5.2 bps to 7.3219%.

SM Prime - 2Q26: mall margins expand; residential revenue improves but inventory rises
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q26 net profit remains flat YoY

SM Prime’s 2Q26 net profit was flat YoY at P12.9bn. As a result, 1H26 earnings were similarly flat at P24.5bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. However, operations were stronger than the headline profit figure would suggest. 2Q26 revenue and EBIT both grew 9% YoY and 15% QoQ while 1H26 revenue and EBIT both increased 5%.

 

Mall margins expand

2Q26 mall revenue rose 8% YoY on the back of 6% SSSG, slightly slower than the 7% in 1Q26. Meanwhile, the mall EBIT margin expanded 1.1ppt QoQ and 2,1ppt YoY (to 59.4%). Management attributed the margin improvement to shorter mall operating hours as well as “disciplined cost management” (i.e., solar panels on mall rooftops helped rein in utility costs).

 

Residential reservation sales inch up QoQ

2Q26 residential reservation sales inched up 1% QoQ (down 9% YoY) to P12.4bn, with P1.7bn coming from the first Signature Series project (in Susana Heights).

Residential revenue and margins improve sequentially

2Q26 residential revenue grew 10% YoY and 48% QoQ to P12.3bn. Management attributed the faster revenue recognition to higher down payments by buyers as well as a higher proportion of sales from projects that are near completion. Meanwhile, the 2Q26 residential EBIT margin expanded 5.2ppt QoQ (to 35.5%) after four consecutive quarters of decline. The improvement was likely due to sales of completed lots from the Signature Series.

 

Unsold inventory climbs

On the other hand, the value of unsold residential inventory grew 10% QoQ and 15% YoY to P200bn, equivalent to about 4.2 years’ worth of reservation sales. Since most of the inventory are of Metro Manila mid-income condominiums, the company will not launch similar projects this year. Instead, it will focus on liquidating existing stock and will only launch projects in provincial areas.

 

Hotel and office occupancy rates improve

Revenue of the hotel and office segments each rose 9% YoY in 2Q26/1H26 as occupancy improved. Hotel occupancy expanded 5ppt (to 67%) while office occupancy increased 6ppt (to 77%).

 
Ayala Land - 2Q26 earnings fall 16% YoY; management remains cautious on residential
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q26 net profit falls 16% YoY, behind REGIS and consensus

Ayala Land’s 2Q26 net profit fell 16% YoY to P6.1bn as development revenue declined 16% YoY (flat QoQ). As a result, 1H26 earnings fell 19% to P11.5bn, well behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Although management attributed the 22% decline in 1H26 development revenue partly to timing, it said that profits in 2H26 will be similar to 1H26.

 

Core segment leads decline in reservation sales

2Q26 residential reservation sales fell 21% YoY (and 7% QoQ) to P22.3bn. While sales of premium projects grew 12% YoY to P15.2bn, sales of core units declined 51% YoY to P7.1bn (see Figure 3). 1H26 residential reservation sales were down 22% to P46.4bn and ALI expects 2H26 figures to be similar.

 

Unsold inventory falls 17% QoQ

On a more positive note, since the company did not launch any residential projects in 1H26, unsold residential inventory shrank 17% QoQ to P110bn (30% down from end-2025). Management estimates this is equivalent to 15 months of reservation sales (before cancellations), lower than the 18 months as of 1Q26.

Residential launch guidance reveals cautious stance

We believe that the company’s launch guidance shows that management remains cautious on the residential segment despite the improvement in unsold inventory levels. ALI only plans to launch P5bn of residential projects in 2H26/FY26, much lower than the P47bn launched in 2025.

 

Mall revenue improves

1H26 mall revenue grew 4% YoY to P12bn. However, excluding ATC, which was sold in 4Q25, 1H26 mall revenue grew 9% (from 8% in 1Q26). With the redevelopment of the four flagship malls now complete, mall revenue could improve further in the coming quarters. Management said that in some instances, rent/sqm rose as much as 25%. ALI will complete about 200k sqm of mall space this year, which will add 10% to mall floor area.

 

Office occupancy remains flat QoQ

2Q26 office revenue increased 3% QoQ and YoY. Although office occupancy remained flat QoQ (at 88%), management said that rental rates on contract renewals were about 5% higher.

 

Renovations lift hotel operations

2Q26 hotels revenue expanded 26% YoY to P2.9bn thanks to the contributions from newly renovated hotels and the acquisition of a hotel in 3Q25. The 1H26 hotel EBITDA margin also expanded 8ppt YoY to 30%.

 
Monde Nissin - 2Q26: APAC BFB growth slows; Quorn margins continue to improve
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H core profit up 16%—ahead

MONDE 1H26 core net profit amounted to P5.5bn (+16% YoY), ahead of REGIS and consensus estimates. 1H group net sales were within expectations at P44.3bn (+7% YoY). For 2Q alone, core profit jumped to P2.2bn, up 24% YoY from a low 2Q25, driven by a 2.5ppt YoY expansion in gross margin. Net sales in the quarter rose 5% YoY to P21.5bn. Growth from APAC BFB was modest at 3% YoY. Quorn’s 3% revenue growth (in constant currency) was magnified to 12% due to the depreciation of the PHP.

 

APAC BFB sales moderate as Noodles volumes soften

Net sales for APAC BFB grew 3% YoY to P17.8bn, decelerating from the 9% growth in 1Q. Group-wide sales volumes were just slightly up YoY (+0.3%) with the key category of Noodles actually seeing a 6.1% contraction. Strong volumes (+12.2%) from the Strategic Growth segment (previously “Others”, includes yogurt drinks, packaged cakes, etc.) helped keep aggregate volume growth positive. Noodles were affected by higher competitive pressure from the premium segment and the ~4% price increase implemented in the quarter. Yogurt drinks continued to grow even though market share fell to 83.9% due to the entrant of a new competitor.

 

Pricing provides support amid margin headwinds

APAC BFB margins expanded 2.1ppt YoY to 35.0% reflecting the 2.5% average price increase rolled out from mid-May through June. 

Further margin upside may be limited moving forward, due to the sharp rise in packaging costs. Contemplated price increases and hedges on key inputs such as palm oil and wheat may help mitigate the impact.

 

Quorn continues to be profitable; impairment less likely

The Protein business saw sales rise 2.7% on a constant currency basis, improving modestly from +1.4% in 1Q. Gross margin improved on a YoY basis for the ninth consecutive quarter, up 5ppt to 30.1% on sustained gains from its “transformation program”. Core EBITDA rose 10x to P249mn, while EBIT also remained positive for the second straight quarter. Management noted that the continued improvements reduce the likelihood of further impairment on this business.

 

Early 3Q trends positive but outlook remains uncertain

Management gave positive early indications for 3Q, saying July and August are currently faring better than the 2Q average. The company also cited that Noodles historically sees better demand during periods of heavier rainfall, which the country has been seeing the past weeks. The prevailing El Nino phenomenon could quickly reverse that, though. With further price increases being planned amidst what we reckon will be a period of high inflation combined with still-tight consumer wallets, the couple of quarters could remain challenging for the top line.

 
PLDT - Revenue softness in 2Q26
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H revenue up 2%, core profit dips

PLDT reported service revenues of P109bn for 1H26, up 2% YoY, with mobile revenue barely up (+1%) and broadband flat YoY. Corporate data helped lift growth, rising +5%. Core profit for 1H26, meanwhile, amounted to P17.1bn (-3% YoY), which we’d view as being slightly short of expectations.

 

Key segments barely up

Mobile revenue for 1H26 grew 1% to P48bn. For 2Q alone, mobile revenue was up +2% YoY (flat QoQ). This implies ~0.5pt market share loss relative to its biggest competitor in the latest quarter. Management did say, however, that mobile top-ups improved in early 3Q. Broadband revenue, meanwhile, was virtually unchanged for the quarter (P13.2bn) and the half (P26.4bn), also lagging the competition.

 

Belt-tightening

It’s apparent that PLDT has been on a debt-reduction and belt-tightening mode.

This involves asset sales (e.g. property, copper, and data centers) as well as cost reduction initiatives. Among other things, A&P expenses for 1H26 dropped 9%. While we can’t conclusively attribute the market share weakness to this belt-tightening, we wonder if it played a role.

 

Maya contributions dip in 2Q

PLDT’s share of earnings from fintech Maya dipped slightly QoQ (-5%, on our estimates), which was blamed on one-off costs. Management says the underlying trajectory of the business remains on track.

 

Cash flow focus

PLDT sounds intent on keeping its free cash flow positive to reduce debt while paying out 60% of core income as dividends. Capex for 1H26 was P21bn but will reach mid-50s (Pbn) for the year. This would still be below last year’s P60bn.

 
Converge ICT - 2Q26: Profits drop 16%; surprise dip in subscriber count
 
 
by Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Subscriber growth halts; 1H26 income down 8%

Converge saw top line growth decelerate further (+2% YoY) in 2Q after the marked slowdown the previous quarter. For 1H26, revenues were up +3% to P22.4bn, lifted by the Enterprise segment (+15%). The core residential business grew 1% for 1H and was barely up in 2Q. The surprising dip (albeit small) in subscriber base was a factor.

 

Net income was thus down 8% to P5.5bn for 1H and 16% to P2.5bn for 2Q alone, putting these results below expectations.

 

Small net reduction in subscriber count

The company’s subscriber base surprisingly dipped <2k in 2Q, because of an increase in churn (disconnections) to 2.7% (vs 2.4% in 1Q). This is the first quarterly net decline in subscriber count in memory. It appears that efforts to reduce churn were unsuccessful.

 

Higher churn from prepaid subscribers

Management noted that bulk of disconnections came from CNVRG’s prepaid service Surf2Sawa and blamed the tough economic environment for this turn of events. In fact, its core postpaid service (FiberX) saw positive net adds for 2Q. We note, however, that the prepaid subscriber count of the competition still rose during the quarter. It is possible that CNVRG’s latest prepaid experience will be felt industrywide in succeeding periods.

 

Revising guidance

The company reduced its revenue growth guidance for FY26 to 4-6% (from 8-10%). Even that lower target range now looks tough to achieve this year following 1H results.

 

Management also reduced capex guidance to P17-20bn (vs P18-23bn).

 
ACEN Corp - 2Q26 profit miss on Australia underperformance
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H26 recurring profit drops 31% YoY to P1.74bn – Behind estimates

ACEN reported headline 1H26 profit (before preferred dividends) of P3.9bn (+411% YoY) and core profit of P4.2bn (+21% YoY). Adjusting for the one-off gain from the pre-termination of its contract with Meralco booked in 1Q26 and removing pref dividends, we estimate recurring profit declined 31% YoY to P1.74bn in 1H26, falling short of both Regis and consensus expectations. Core attributable EBITDA, excluding the one-off gain, rose 24% YoY to P12.96bn in 1H26, though this trails the +48% YoY improvement we had anticipated for the year due to the underperformance of its solar facilities in Australia.

 

Core EBITDA up 28% YoY in 2Q but recurring profits down 25%

In 2Q26 alone, recurring profit fell 25% YoY to P795mn, weighed down by higher non-cash and interest charges. Core EBITDA, however, improved 28% YoY to P6.25bn in the quarter, boosted by strong spot prices in the Philippines and higher RE output in Vietnam and India. These gains were partly offset by weaker spot prices and lower solar irradiance in Australia.

While the Australian market remains challenging, with management expecting continued softness in operational performance near-term, it, however, remains bullish on the country’s medium-to long-term structural demand prospects.

 

Adding more battery storage; 1H capex up 57% to P10.6bn

To capture higher electricity prices during peak evening periods, ACEN is accelerating the construction of ~1,660 MWh of battery storage systems at its solar facilities in Zambales, Philippines. Capex across its consolidated units reached P10.6bn in 1H, up 57% YoY. We anticipate capex to ramp up further as the company completes 2.4GW of solar and wind projects in the Philippines and overseas through 2027, expanding its current 4.4GW operating capacity.

 

Prioritizing third-party capital to fund capex requirements

Net attributable obligations further climbed to P244bn by end-June 2026, equivalent to 8x annualized 1H26 EBITDA of P14.7bn. Management said it is prioritizing securing third-party equity to alleviate balance sheet concerns, while putting the stock rights offer on hold.

 
Robinsons Land - 2Q26: reservation sales of standalone and JV projects fall YoY
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q26 net profit rises 8% YoY

Robinsons Land’s 2Q26 net profit rose 8% YoY and 4% QoQ to P3.7bn, as faster recognition of JV residential projects cushioned the impact of lower mall and office margins. This brought 1H26 earnings to P7.2bn (+5% YoY), in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts.

 

Reservation sales fall

Demand for RLC’s standalone and residential projects weakened. Despite continued promotional payment schemes for ready-for-occupancy (RFO) units, reservation sales of standalone projects shrank 61% YoY (up 99% QoQ) to P907mn, which is about 20% of the 2019 average. Furthermore, the value of unsold residential inventory of its standalone residential projects barely changed (-1% QoQ) at P44.2bn, which we estimate is equivalent to 13 years of reservation sales (after cancellations). Meanwhile, reservation sales of JV projects fell 90% QoQ and 35% YoY to P342mn, only 7% of the 2019 average. Fortunately, the unsold inventory level of JV projects is more manageable at P11bn, equivalent to 1.8 years of reservation sales (also after cancellations).

 

Mall EBIT margin declines

2Q26 mall revenue grew 5% YoY on the back of a 1% increase in floor area and 4% same-mall revenue growth, the same pace as 1Q26. Meanwhile, despite shorter mall operating hours, 2Q26 mall EBIT margin fell 3.5ppt QoQ (and 20bps YoY) to 39.4%, due to higher power costs as well as a high base in 1Q26. Management expects margins to recover after it recently signed power supply contracts at lower rates.

 

Office occupancy improves QoQ

On average, RLC renewed office contracts at slightly lower rental rates in order to retain tenants. This helped office occupancy improve 1ppt QoQ to 87%, better than the industry average of 82%. Meanwhile, office EBIT margin contracted 1.2ppt QoQ and YoY (to 63.2%) on the back of higher power costs. As a result, office EBIT only increased 3% YoY, slower than the YoY revenue growth of 5%.

 

Targeting another asset infusion into RCR this year

After the recent infusion of 160,000sqm of malls into RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM), RLC said it is targeting another share-for-property swap with RCR this year. We estimate that RLC’s current stake in RCR is now worth c. P88bn, 7% higher than RLC’s current market cap.

 
Manila Water - 1H26: East Zone capex drops sharply
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H26 core profit down 6% YoY to P6.5bn – Behind Street estimates

Headline 1H26 profit rose 6% YoY to P8.47bn. Stripping out the one-time debt restructuring gain in 1Q, core profit fell 6% YoY to P6.54bn in 1H, trending below consensus’ P14bn estimate. 2Q26 profit declined 8% YoY to P3.54bn, weighed down by higher financing costs.

 

Further spending shortfall for East Zone

Cash spending (capex and opex) at the EZ fell 25% YoY to P5.5bn in 2Q26, bringing 1H26 spending to P10bn (-25% YoY). Management attributed the 38% YoY drop in 1H capex to P5.74bn to the usual permitting delays. While it expects to spend P8.5bn in 2H, its full-year capex target of P14.2bn would still be 40% below last year’s level. Given that the concession framework provides an annual 12% return on investments, we see this year’s capex shortfall as a significant opportunity loss to earn that return.

Ample buffer against a severe El Niño

Management highlighted that it has sufficient water supply to fully mitigate the risk of a severe El Niño. The completion of water treatment plants with a combined capacity of 420MLD this quarter will enable the company to utilize the full 520MLD of raw water available from its alternative source, Wawa Dam. This provides a more-than-adequate buffer against declining water levels at its primary source, Angat Dam.

 

Non-EZ updates

Profit contributions from its non-EZ domestic and international amounted to P1bn in 1H, up 19% YoY and accounting for 14% of group profits, thanks in large part to the ~30% tariff hike at Clark Water and higher supervision fees at Estate Water. Management continues to await the conclusion of discussions between the seller of Agua de Puebla and the Mexican government, which are needed to clear the way for the sale.

1H26 core profit of P4.1bn (+158% YoY) – Within expectations but…

While 1H sales volumes of 8.7mn MT are in line with our forecast, recent developments in the nickel ore market suggest that the sharp increase in ore prices seen in 2Q may not be sustained in 2H. As a result, our ASP assumptions and profit estimate for the year may prove challenging to achieve.

 

Nickel ore ASPs jump +22% YoY in 2Q on strong nickel LME

Its mining unit’s EBITDA doubled YoY to P6.4bn in 2Q26, supported by (a) a 22% increase in ASP to US$33/MT, (b) a 19% rise in sales volumes, and (c) an 8% YoY strengthening of the USD against the PHP. LME nickel prices strengthened further in 2Q following the disruption in sulphur exports from the Middle East, a key input for HPAL plants. Meanwhile, stronger LME nickel and cobalt prices helped its HPAL associate turn a profit in 2Q.

Commissioning of new solar plant, better ASPs lift power unit

The power segment generated an EBITDA of P698mn (In line) in 1H, up 71% YoY, on the back of the commissioning of a 120MW solar plant late last year. The company reduced its WESM exposure to nearly zero in 2Q26, but management noted that ASPs remained above last year’s levels.

 

Reducing 2026E capex guidance to P7bn on construction delays

1H26 capex stood at P3.6bn (+160% YoY) but management reduced its 2026E capex guidance to P7bn (-26% YoY) from P12bn (+22% YoY) previously due to construction delays at some of its solar facilities.

 

2H outlook; special dividend intact

Despite moderating nickel ore prices, management remained upbeat, noting that nickel pay factors remain strong despite noise around a potential increase in Indonesia’s RKAB quotas. It also disclosed that the final consideration for its 20% stake in Kazakhstan-based East Copper will be US$30mn, with closing expected by September. Wilcon Depot - 2Q26: Demand pick-up, low base prop up SSSG
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio Dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

1H26 profit up +4% YoY—behind

WLCON’s 2Q26 net profit rose 2% YoY to P641mn, bringing 1H26 profit to P1.2bn (+4% YoY). The profits were broadly in line with consensus but behind REGIS forecasts. 1H26 net sales of P19.0bn (+11% YoY) are trending within expectations, with 2Q net sales increasing 13% YoY to P9.8bn on SSSG of +8.5%. Revenue growth outpaced net profit growth in the quarter due to lower gross margins and higher interest accretion on long-term lease liabilities following recent lease renewals.

 

Robust revenue growth and SSSG but…

Both revenue growth and SSSG accelerated in 2Q to +13% YoY and +8.5% from +9% YoY and +4.7%, respectively, in the previous quarter. YoY comparisons for 2Q were also assisted by the low base (-6.1% SSSG in 2Q). This quarter’s SSSG was driven more by ticket size (+5.4%).

Management also estimated that prices for certain items were raised by ~5% over the last quarter. Moving forward, management cautioned that the sales growth figures will not appear as robust given the higher base in 2H25. Additionally, the recent torrential rains were cited to have led to a slowdown in sales in the past three weeks. Drier weather conditions given the upcoming El Niño may provide some upside.

 

Margins slip on sales mix

2Q gross margin contracted 0.9ppt YoY to 37.5% as sales contribution from inhouse brands fell 2.8ppt YoY to a multi-year low of 49.5% due to stockouts. Some pricing and bundling promos also contributed to lower margin in a bid to lower days inventory and preserve market share. Management, however, is guiding for potential upside in margins on the replenishment of stock for inhouse brands and a more optimized product mix.

 
Megaworld - 2Q26: provincial projects boost reservation sales; unsold inventory falls
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

2Q26 net profit inches up

Megaworld’s 2Q26 headline profit inched up 1% YoY to P5.7bn. As a result, 1H26 headline earnings rose 2% to P10.9bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. However, operations were stronger than the headline profit figure would suggest. 1H26 EBIT grew 6% to P16.0bn, ahead of our forecast, largely thanks to the 3.7ppt expansion in the residential GPM (to 53.9%), which was brought about by better product mix and “disciplined cost management”.

 

Provincial projects boost reservation sales

2Q26 grew 12% QoQ and 20% YoY to P33.3bn, equivalent to about 90% of the 2019 average. Management attributes the increase largely to brisk take-up of provincial projects, which comprised 48% of 1H26 sales (from 45% in 1Q26, 43% in 1H25). Office occupancy remains stable but could drop in 2H26

Office occupancy remained stable QoQ at 87%. However, this might drop since MEG has signed up very few tenants for the 90k sqm due for completion in 2H26. Meanwhile, average rental reversions remained slightly negative. Mall revenue grows on higher occupancy and tenant sales

2Q26 mall revenue grew 7% YoY, thanks to a 2ppt YoY increase in occupancy (to 95%) as well as higher tenant sales.

 

DPS rises 12%

The company declared a dividend of P0.117/share, which is 12% higher YoY and represents a dividend yield of 5.1%. Management also said that the payout ratio could increase in the future (from less than 20% currently).

Marcos asks Congress to suspend implementation of property valuation law

During a Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting, President Marcos asked members of Congress to suspend the implementation of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA). President Marcos signed the RPVARA into law in 2024. The RPVARA transfers the responsibility of updating the “Schedule of Market Values” (from which real property taxes are based) from Local Government Units (such as city governments) to the national government. While raising real property taxes would expand the coffers of a city government, it could also lead to the mayor (and other officials) losing the next election. As a result, local governments in many places are hesitant to regularly update their Schedule of Market Values. As a result, there are cities/municipalities that might not have updated it for over 10 years. RPVARA is slated to take full effect in 2028.
