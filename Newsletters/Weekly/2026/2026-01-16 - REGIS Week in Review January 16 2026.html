REGIS REPORT

16 January 2026

Philippine equities extended their positive momentum, with the PCOMP rallying for a fourth straight week, up +1.8% or 116.53 pts WoW to  6,464.67 as sustained foreign inflows continued to support share prices. Foreigners have now been net buyers for all trading days of the year so far, totaling over $90m YTD, helping push the index to its highest level since July despite intermittent profit-taking as it nears technical resistance at the 6,500-level. Sentiment was generally constructive, underpinned by strong participation in large-cap names and improving risk appetite.

Conglomerates were the big drivers of the index advance this week. AC (+10.8% WoW) surged as the top foreign-bought name for the week, while JGS (+7.0% WoW) rallied after announcing it is in early discussions with potential buyers of its petrochemical business, which the company recently disclosed it is finally writing off.

Among sector indices, Mining (+6.2% WoW) led as gold prices pushed to fresh highs, lifting gold miners APX (+8.4% WoW), PX (+8.3% WoW), and OGP (+7.6% WoW). Financials (+2.7% WoW) followed, with the Big Three banks BDO (+2.7% WoW), BPI (+3.2% WoW), and MBT (+3.0% WoW) all outperforming the index. Property (+2.5% WoW) also outperformed, supported by gains in ALI (+4.7% WoW) and SMPH (+3.2% WoW).

In corporate stories this week, JFC (+3.6% WoW) disclosed that strong operating momentum seen in 4Q25 carried into 1Q26. Meanwhile, BLOOM (+7.7% WoW) shares climbed following the government’s announcement of relaxed visa rules for Chinese tourists, allowing visa-free entry for stays of up to two weeks, which boosted sentiment toward gaming and tourism-linked names.

ADTO continued to improve to $117m, while net foreign inflow accelerated to $51m for the week.

The peso weakened further, hitting a fresh low of 59.470 before ending the week -0.2% WoW to 59.354:$1. The PH10Y yield inched up by 2.1 bps to 6.0483%.

CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI's Mexico port volumes rebound in December, up 28% YoY

Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), recovered by 28% YoY to 150k TEUs in December. This was reversal from the 3%/6% YoY declines in October/November. CMSA regained market share, given that combined volumes across all ports in Manzanillo fell 8% YoY, with other ports (ex-CMSA) experiencing a 28% YoY drop in December. Brings 4Q25 volumes at CMSA to 410k TEUs, up 6% YoY and just a tad slower than 3Q25 volume growth of 7%. Takes FY25 volumes to 1.5mn TEUs, up 6% YoY. We are positive on CMSA’s 12-month outlook, supported by the anticipated completion of its ~20% capacity expansion by 1Q26. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA. ICTSI trades at 11x EV/EBITDA and 19x PER vs peer average (12.6x EV/EBITDA and 18x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA/20x PER).

JG Summit in early talks to sell petrochemical unit

JG Summit Holdings has started talks with potential foreign buyers for its petrochemical unit, JGS Olefins Corp. According to President and CEO Lance Gokongwei, “Some buyers are looking to operate it in the Philippines, some are looking to moving it abroad.” Recall that JGS plans to fully write off JGS Olefins Corp, amounting to P114bn (equivalent to 30% of JGS’ book value per share of ~P50/sh). The company is still spending P100–150mn per month to maintain the plants in good condition for an eventual sale. A sale would remove this continuing cost.

JFC reports continued solid operating momentum since Dec update

JFC stated in a press release that recent business conditions have been consistent with the "solid operating momentum" guided in its previous guidance disclosure back in December. The company stated that since its last Dec update, results are still consistent with FY25 guidance: 8-12% SWS growth, 4-6% SSSG, and 4-8% Store network growth. Customer demand was noted to be stable across key markets given steady domestic performance and resilient international operations. Domestically, Jollibee's Champion Brands (Jollibee, Chowking, and Mang Inasal) were said to continue to significantly contribute to domestic SWS and SSSG with overall improving demand in-line with 4Q seasonality. In the int'l space, Jollibee Vietnam was said to sustain its high double-digit SWS and SSS growth alongside positive performance from the Tim Ho Wan brand. Jollibee Vietnam as of 9M25 posted SWS and SSS growth of 37% and 21%, respectively. The company's asset light growth strategy was said to progress well as network expansion continues given franchise-led expansion. JFC emphasized that the company remains confident in its growth trajectory and reiterated its aspiration to triple NIAT to P24bn and reach 20% ROIC by 2028. We note that there was no mention of or update on Smashburger and the China brands (Yonghe King, Hong Zhuang Yuan) which are the primary concerns of the int'l business. Improvements in terms of operations and profitability of these brands remain key to the overall profitability of the int'l business and management's triple NIAT target to P24bn by 2028.

Jollibee opens largest regional commissary in Cebu

JFC has opened its largest commissary yet, serving the Visayas and Mindanao region. The commissary supplies ingredients and products to the Jollibee brand stores (Jollibee, Chowking, Mang Inasal, Greenwich, Red Ribbon, Burger King) distributing to nearby regions. Joseph Tanbuntiong, CEO of Jollibee Group Philippines said growth in Visayas and Mindanao is central to the company’s strategy, as the company seeks to accelerate growth outside of Luzon. The facility, which formally opened Jan 8, is located in Danau City, Cebu. At full capacity, the facility is expected to create ~800 jobs, with currently over 60% of employees coming from Danay City. In line with their sustainability targets, the facility operates on 99% renewable electricity. The Philippines accounts for 3,445 (33%) of JFC’s 10,304 stores globally and 55% of total SWS as of 9M25.

Megaworld launches 37th township

Megaworld will spend P8bn to develop its 37th township over the next 7 to 10 years. The township will be called “The Sugartown.” It spans 97 hectares in Talisay City, Negros Occidental. We estimate this new township will account for less than 5% of MEG’s NAV. MEG currently offers 4.1% dividend yield and is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY26E P/B, and 3.4X FY26E P/E.

Megaworld to expand office portfolio in key provincial areas

Megaworld formed a new office group, called Megaworld Global Offices, which will collaborate with the existing office leasing team, to accelerate growth of the company’s portfolio in key provinces: Ilocos Region, Pampanga, Cavite, Bacolod, and Cagayan de Oro. These provinces currently comprise less than 5% of Megaworld’s office portfolio. The company also seeks to attract more Fortune 500 companies. Megaworld Global Offices will be led by Francisco Roxas, a former Ayala Land executive. MEG currently offers 4.1% dividend yield and is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY26E P/B, and 3.4X FY26E P/E.

ATI seeks contract renewal for Manila South Harbor beyond 2038

Asian Terminals Inc. (ATI PM) has applied to renew its contract with the Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) to operate Manila South Harbor (MSH) beyond 2038. This follows its competitor, ICTSI (ICT PM), who also applied to renew its contract to operate the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT). Recall that the Maharlika Investment Corp. is launching a tender offer to buy up to 101mn (5%) of ATI’s shares at P36/sh, a move that will delist ATI from the PSE. Maharlika Investment Corp said that it expects to hold approx. 11.2% of ATI’s outstanding capital stock (after completion of tender offer, subject to level of tenders received). ATI’s public float is currently at 15.7%. We estimate the offer price values ATI at just 7x trailing EV/EBITDA, 13x PER, and ~US$520 EV/TEU, a discount to ICTSI (12x trailing EV/EBTDA, 22x PER, and US$1,200 EV/TEU).

Okada Manila 4Q25 GGR falls YoY and QoQ

Okada Manila’s 4Q25 GGR fell 15% QoQ and 34% YoY to P5.9bn, the lowest level since 3Q21. This is the fourth consecutive quarter of GGR decline. FY25 GGR decreased 20% to P27.8bn, the lowest level since 2021. Volume and GGR of every category fell on a YoY and QoQ basis.

Hann Casino and PhilWeb announce strategic partnership

PhilWeb Corporation (WEB) and Hann Casino announced a strategic partnership to expand Hann’s online gaming presence. PhilWeb will support the operation and management of Hann’s online gaming platform. Hann Casino Resort is an integrated resort in Clark with 274 gaming tables, 1,721 gaming machines, and over 800 hotel rooms from Swissotel Clark and Clark Marriott. Integrated Resort operators pay lower fees to PAGCOR than pure online gaming firms. Pure online gamine operators pay 38% of e-casino GGR as fees/taxes to the government while integrated resort operators pay only 29% of their e-casino GGR.

Cebu Air's pax volumes up 5% YoY in December; FY25 pax volumes miss, rising only 10%

Cebu Air reported it flew a record 2.7mn passengers in December, up 5% YoY and reversing declines in Oct/Nov. However, systemwide seat load factor (SLF) slipped 140bps YoY to 83.8% due to soft international demand, with International SLF falling 520bps to 79.4%. This brings 4Q25 pax volumes down 2% YoY to 6.9mn and FY25 volumes up 9.5% YoY to 26.9mn, well below our expectations of 28.5mn (+16%). Seat capacity grew 10% YoY in FY25, below management’s downgraded seat growth guidance of 11-13%. We attribute the volume shortfall to the lingering global aircraft/engine supply issues. CEB trades at 4.5x FY26E EV/EBITDA, below peer average of 7x.

Aboitiz acquires Singapore animal nutrition firm

Aboitiz Foods, Aboitiz Equity Ventures' (AEV PM) food unit, through its subsidiary Gold Coin Management Holdings, has acquired Diasham Resources Pte. Ltd., a Singapore-based animal nutrition company. Founded nearly 50 years ago, Diasham is engaged in animal health products and feed supplements. The company distributes products across Asia-Pacific markets, including Thailand, Indonesia, China, Hong Kong, South Korea, Taiwan, the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, and Bangladesh. According to Tristan Aboitiz, CEO of Aboitiz Food, the company’s strong industry track record was a key factor behind the acquisition. The acquisition gives Aboitiz Food a more complete range of products, enhances Aboitiz’s nutrition portfolio, and strengthens manufacturing capabilities. The transaction remains subject to customary closing adjustments, including cash, debt-like items, and working capital.

Ombudsman investigates Congressman Leviste’s alleged sale of solar franchise

Batangas Representative Leandro Leviste is being investigated for “selling” the franchise of his solar energy business to Meralco. Ombudsman Jesus Crispin Remulla alleged that the franchise was not properly approved by the House of Representatives. Recall that Republic Act 11357 was signed into law in July 2019, which provided a 25-year franchise to Cong. Leviste’s Solar Para Sa Bayan Corporation (SBPC). Meralco’s wholly-owned unit, Meralco PowerGen Corp. (MGEN), reiterated that it bought Leviste’s shares in SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM), which is a separate corporate entity to SBPC. MGEN said the business operations of SPNEC do not depend on the franchise held by SBPC. MGEN now owns 57% of SPNEC’s commons and 69% of voting shares, after exchangeable notes held by Leviste-owned Solar Philippines were converted into common equity.

DOE slaps P24bn penalties over canceled Solar Philippines contracts

Energy Secretary Sharon Garin said that the agency terminated 11,427 MW worth of contracts awarded to Solar Philippines for its failure to deliver on its production commitments. The Department of Energy (DOE) seeks to collect P24bn in penalties from Solar Philippines, which covers performance bonds and other contractual obligations. DOE consistently sent notices and show cause orders along with requests for Solar Philippines to renew their bonds, but to no avail. Solar Philippines committed to deliver 17,904MW of renewable energy projects, including projects awarded under the Green Energy Auction Program (GEAP). These projects, which were supposed to be delivered in 2024 and 2025, are outside of SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM). Solar Philippines is a company owned and controlled by Congressman Leandro Leviste. Note that the DOE said it has terminated 163 service contracts, covering 18,000MW across hydro, solar, wind, geothermal, and biomass projects. We understand that SPNEC’s 280MW Sta Rosa Nueva Ecija 2 solar project could be among the terminated contracts; SPNEC submitted a P2.8bn performance bond for the project.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP says 2026 inflation could be below gov't target range

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas iterated its expectations for 2026 inflation, stating that inflation risks are tilted to the downside with a 45% likelihood that inflation will fall below 2%. Subdued global commodity prices leading to moderate oil and food inflation were cited as the primary downside pressures.

Remittances rise by 3.6% in November

Cash remittances amounted US$2.9bn in Nov, 3.6% higher YoY—accelerating from the 3.0% growth in Oct. This brings 11M25 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$32.1bn. Weak remittance growth from North America (e.g. USA: 0.5%) has been partially offset by stronger contributions from the rest of the world.

BIR collections rebounded in Dec 2025 but likely to miss FY25 target

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) stated that revenue collections in Dec rose ~7% YoY, accelerating from the 3% YoY growth in Nov. Despite the rebound in Dec, the BIR guided that it will likely not meet its initial 2025 target of P3.2trn (+12% YoY) collections due to lackluster revenue growth in 2H25. BIR Commissioner Charlie Mendoza expects 2025 revenues to amount to P3.1trn (+9% YoY) as "revenue collections efforts were significantly affected in 2H when the flood control investigations started". BIR tax revenues amount to ~70% of the FY25 total gov’t revenue target. Note that the gov't has revised its economic growth targets for 2026-2028 but tax revenue target revisions have yet to be announced.

Infra spending target set at 4.3% of GDP for 2026

The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) revised the 2026 infrastructure spending target to 4.3% of GDP (~P1.30trn) from 5.1% (~P1.56trn) previously. The cut in infra spending follows the flood control corruption scandal which weighed on gov't spending and economic growth last year. The 4.3% of GDP allocation for infra is the lowest since 2017. Acting Budget Secretary Rolando Toledo stated that the gov't sees little risk of delays in infra projects for 2026 given the "clean" budget process. Toledo said that the absence of "ghost projects" and fully specified allocations across programs should help streamline infra construction progress.

Commercial banks' loan growth unchanged at 10.3% in Nov

Preliminary data showed that loans of commercial banks was 10.3% in Nov, unchanged from the previous month of Oct. Loans to productive activities posted steady growth at 9.0% vs 9.1% in Oct. Consumer loans still grew at a heady pace of 22.9%, though a tad slower than Oct’s 23.1% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) decelerated to 7.6% in Nov from 8.3% in Oct.

SEC revises REIT regulations

The SEC issued Memorandum Circular No. 1, which revises the regulations regarding the REIT law. The SEC expanded the definition of “income generating real estate”. Income-generating real estate includes properties with steady cash flow, such as rentals, toll fees, user fees, ticket sales, parking fees and storage fees. They include toll roads, railways, airports and air navigation facilities, ports, information and communications technology infrastructure, energy infrastructure assets and data centers. REITs are permitted to directly or indirectly own real estate, including special purpose vehicles. The SEC also extended the period to reinvest in Philippine real estate to two years (from one year previously). Reinvestment may take the form of investments in equity, the extension of loans or purchase of debt instruments or the repayment of debt in relation to any real estate or infrastructure projects in the Philippines. We do not expect the revised regulations to materially affect existing REITs. However, they may lead to more listings of REITs from sectors such as telecommunications, power, infrastructure, etc.

Chinese nationals allowed visa-free entry to PH starting Jan 16

The Dept. of Foreign Affairs (DFA) announced that Chinese nationals will be allowed to enter the Philippines with no visa requirement for a stay of up to 14 days. The visa-free 14-day stay is non-extendable and non-convertible to any other type of PH visa category. Requirements for Chinese nationals are a passport valid for at least six months, confirmed accommodation/booking, and a return ticket to next country of destination. The visa-free privilege is only valid through Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA) in Metro Manila and Mactan-Cebu International Airport (MCIA) in Cebu. The visa-free entry will be in effect for one year and will be reviewed prior the expiry of the arrangement. As of 11M25, Chinese tourists account for 5% of total tourist arrivals, much lower than the 18% contribution to total back in 2019.

PSE seeks revival of GSIS, SSS stock investment loan program

PSE President Ramon Monzon stated that he is in talks to try to convince the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) and Social Security System (SSS) to revive the stock investment loan programs to in an effort to increase liquidity of the stock market. The loan programs were unprofitable and has been shelved by the state-owned pension funds. The GSIS previously offered the Stock Purchase Financing Program where a member could apply for a loan five times their monthly basic salary and repay through sale of stocks, cash payment, and deduction from any benefit. The SSS in the past offered the Stock Investment Loan Program (SILP) which enabled members to borrow funds to buy company stocks. Monzon eyes a revamp of these programs with a tie up with the current personal and equity retirement account (PERA) initiative. He stated that routing the stock loan and placing it in PERA may be more sustainable than the previous program versions by being better structured for long-term equity investments. PERA's tax incentives were also said to be a potential driving factor to encourage more retail participation. As of end-2024, PERA contributions amounted to P491mn (+24% YoY) from 5,912 (+6% YoY) contributors. Resident Filipinos can invest P200,000/year, while those based overseas have a limit of P400,000/year.

Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in December

We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 57 bps MoM to 19.5% (in December) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index fell 17bps MoM to 23.4%. The increase in foreign ownership of the overall market was largely due to the 45% MoM share price decline of Villar Land/Golden MV Holdings (HVN PM), which has zero foreign ownership (and a market cap of P644bn as of end-December 2025). Foreign activity reversed to net selling of US$202mn in December, from net foreign buying of US$78mn in November. Among the PSEi constituents, 5 saw increases in foreign ownership while 25 experienced declines. The notable movements among PSEi constituents were: URC – Foreign ownership fell 1.26 percentage points MoM to 19.0%, as net foreign selling hit US$8.2mn, the tenth highest for the month. Furthermore, we reckon foreign selling in late November was only recorded in the December foreign ownership figure. AGI – foreign ownership dropped 1.09 percentage points MoM to 15.8%, as net foreign selling reached US$17.6mn, the fifth highest for the month. ALI – foreign ownership decreased 65bps MoM to 23.1%, as net foreign selling hit US$20.7mn, the fourth highest for the month. BDO – foreign ownership declined 59bps MoM to 27.2%, as it saw the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$40.2mn). AREIT – foreign ownership climbed 48bps MoM to 5.0%. GTCAP – foreign ownership increased 22bps MoM to 23.1%, as net foreign buying hit US$4.5mn, the second highest for the month. ICT – foreign ownership expanded 17bps MoM to 35.9%, as it saw the highest net foreign buying for the month (US$15.5mn).
