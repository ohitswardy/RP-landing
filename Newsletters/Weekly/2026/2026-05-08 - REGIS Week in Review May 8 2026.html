REGIS REPORT

08 May 2026

Heavy on macro releases for the week, the local market continued to take broad cues from geopolitical developments offshore that saw Iran tensions seem to cool mid-week before re-escalating again towards the week's close. Corporate earnings releases also drove stock-specific moves, while a small PSE rebalancing Friday effected upweights to a few names due to float changes. Despite the macro disappointments and Middle East tensions flaring up anew, the PCOMP joined most global equities in the green for the week, up by 2.2% or 127.33 pts WoW to 5,960.97.

 

April inflation accelerated to 7.2% vs 4.1% in March, blowing past consensus estimate of 5.5% as well as the BSP's 5.6-6.4% forecast range amid elevated oil prices. Largest culprit for the higher print was Transport CPI that surged to 21.4% (from 9.9%), while big jumps were also seen in Food to 6.0% (from 2.9%) and Housing & Utilities to 8.2% (from 4.7%). Meanwhile, 1Q26 GDP slowed to 2.8%, its slowest pace since Covid and hugely missing consensus forecast of +3.3%. Soft capital formation was the main drag, -3.3% on top of the previous quarter's 9.4% drop. Notably, private consumption was soft, just up by 3%.

 

Interestingly, consumer names were among the outperformers for the week as 1Q26 results came out stronger than the high-level macro data would have presented. CNPF (+9.5% WoW) and MONDE (+4.7% WoW) both beat expectations with high-single- to low-double-digit topline growth from the branded segments. PGOLD (+2.3% WoW) and URC (+1.6% WoW) also grew 1Q revenues at the same pace, while a positive read-through for JFC (+3.5% WoW) appeared to lift its share price.

 

ICT (+8.5% WoW) again rallied to fresh highs, touching the P800-level intraday for the first time ever after 1Q earnings strongly beat estimates on higher-than-expected yields. But ACEN (+15.8% WoW) was the runaway winner for the week after operations resumed at the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) and investors speculated on windfall gains for under-contracted energy producers that should benefit from the spike in WESM prices. SCC (+5.8% WoW) also rallied on this theme.

 

Friday's PSE index rebalancing saw upweights in EMI (+3.0% WoW), RCR (-0.1% WoW), and PLUS (+0.9% WoW) due to higher float, though all three were ultimately down on rebal day as flows were already widely anticipated.

 

After nine consecutive weeks of outflows, foreigners turned marginally net buyers for the week, but by only less than $1m. The peso was stronger by 1.4% WoW to 60.626:$1.

Philippine Strategy - Inflation surges to 7.2%; rice up 13.7%, more to come
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Fuel, transport, rice prices surge; BSP policy rate hike likely

April CPI inflation of 7.2% YoY blew past consensus of 5.5% and the Bangko Sentral’s target range of 5.6-6.4%. Oil-related items expectedly surged – private transport costs +66%, and household electricity and gas +17%. Despite our own warnings, rice prices still surprised us, up 13.7%. Gasoline and diesel prices have eased considerably in recent weeks. However, rice prices are likely to keep rising, and could be up 40-50% YoY by 3Q/4Q26. This could push headline inflation well into the double digits. The BSP will almost certainly hike rates again, though they may be wary of choking already-weakening consumption.

 

Watch out for rice prices – DA warns of P62/kg by Sept (+40-50% YoY)

As we reported on 8 April, the price of locally produced rice was already rising due to import restrictions imposed in 2H25. The April print of 13.7% does not yet fully incorporate second- and third-round effects of the ongoing Iran conflict. 

Last week, the Department of Agriculture (DA) said rice prices could hit P62/kg in September, given the near doubling in the cost of fertilizer. That would translate to a 40-50% YoY rice inflation rate, partly on the low base last year. It is unclear if the DA’s estimates already assume a potential El Niño, that could hit production here and regionally, as it did in 2023. Recall that rice is 9% of the nation-wide CPI basket, and 18% for lower-income households.

 

BSP rate hikes – how aggressive? Another off-cycle meeting?

The BSP will almost certainly raise its policy interest rate again, as it will have to revise its inflation estimate upwards. Real interest rates are headed deep into negative territory without a rate hike. Prior to the April CPI print, the Street was expecting rates to rise another 50bps. The BSP is in the unenviable position of needing to tighten, but wary of its impact on already-fragile GDP growth. The next Monetary Board meeting isn’t scheduled until 18 June. Given the rapidly-changing environment, we would fully expect another off-cycle meeting as they had in March.

 
ICTSI - 1Q26 profit up 29%; Mgt sees yields staying high
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q26 core profit up 29% to US$305mn – Ahead of Regis, Street estimates

Headline profit rose 23% YoY to US$294mn. Removing the one-off FX charge from the sale of its China port, as well as perps distributions, core profit was 29% higher YoY at US$305mn, trending ahead of estimates.

 

Volumes up 18% but organic growth slows sharply to 1.4%

Group-wide volumes rose 18% YoY due to new acquisitions in Batam (Sep 2025 takeover) and Durban (Jan 2026 takeover). Organic volume growth, though, decelerated sharply to 1.4% in 1Q26 from an estimated 6.5% pace in 4Q25, due to the substantial drop in volumes at its port in Iraq, as well as stiff competition in Poland and Croatia. Like-for-like, volume growth in Asia was broadly steady at 5.7% while ports in the Americas collectively grew volumes by 10%.

 

Yields up 10% YoY (flat QoQ) to US$230

Revenues jumped 29% YoY to US$961mn in 1Q25, with organic revenue growth robust at 19% in 1Q26. Group yield per TEU improved 10% YoY to US$230 (ahead of estimates), with organic yield per TEU improving to US$250 (+20% YoY) on better volume mix and tariff hikes.

Management expressed confidence in maintaining yields at ~US$230 for the full year, which would be a positive surprise.

 

EBITDA margin lower on new acquisitions

EBITDA rose 26% YoY to US$618mn, while margins narrowed by 140bps YoY to 64.3%, reflecting the consolidation of lower-margin, newly acquired ports. On an organic basis, we estimate EBITDA still grew by 20%, with margins holding at ~67%.

 

Impact of fuel crisis mitigated by tariff adjustments, fuel surcharges

Management highlighted that the surge in oil prices has had a more immediate and pronounced effect in the Philippines, where fuel cost increases have quickly permeated the broader economy. While demand has remained resilient, even in the Philippines, management acknowledged that a prolonged oil crisis could weigh on both economic activity and volume growth. To mitigate rising fuel costs, management has introduced fuel surcharges at its ports in the Americas, as well tariff adjustments where possible.

 
Metrobank - 1Q26 NPAT, CAR weighed down by trading/MTM losses
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

No NPL stress yet, but trimming forecasts, keeping TP

MBT’s 1Q26 NPAT of P12.6bn (+3% YoY), was slightly behind REGIS’s previous FY26E of P54.5bn, weighed down by a 78% plunge in trading/fx gains due to the spike in bond yields. As we had warned, MTM losses on FVOCI assets also dragged on CET-1 ratios. Asset quality remained benign, with NPLs flat at 1.75% (vs 1.73% in 4Q25) and NPL cover still high at 137% (vs 141%). While management is not yet seeing significant increase in past due rates, we incorporate higher credit costs in FY26E, reducing our NPAT forecast by 10%. We keep our TP and Buy call on valuations, trading on 5.7x PER, 0.66x P/bk, and 7.8% div yield.

 

Core business steady, but bond yields hit P&L, CET-1

Loans grew 9% YoY with consumer loans up 11%, helping support NIMs. Fee incomes rebounded +12% (was +6% for FY25) partly driven by a 67% jump YoY in Trust AUMs. However, the bank’s Treasury portfolio (c40% of total assets) was hurt by sharply rising interest rates. This caused a 78% YoY plunge in trading/fx gains. The bank also saw a P16bn unrealized MTM loss on its FVOCI portfolio.

CETI-1 ratio fell 190bps QoQ to 14.2%, of which management estimates 80bps was due to MTM losses, with about 95bps coming from dividend payments.

 

No material stress (yet); but we assume some deterioration in new numbers

Management says asset quality is not yet showing signs of stress from the Iran conflict, even in its 30-day past due buckets into April. However, we note that inflation is spiking, which could crimp household balance sheets, and overall economic growth. We adjust our credit cost forecasts from 62bps to 74bps, and reduce FY26E NPAT by 10%.

 

Buy on valuations, dividend yield close to 8%

Despite the revisions, we maintain our target price of P100/sh and Buy recommendation, as the stock trades 5.7x PER, 0.66x P/bk, and pays a div yield of 7.8%. We value the stock on (ROE-g)/(COE-g) assuming long-term ROE of 12.25%, COE of 12.0%, and growth of 8.0%. Key risks include asset quality deterioration. Further MTM hits to CET-1 ratios could also limit the bank’s ability to pay special dividends.

 
Ayala Land - 1Q26: residential segment performs poorly; ALI turns even more cautious
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q26 net profit falls 23%

ALI's 1Q26 net profit fell 23% to P5.4bn, well below REGIS and consensus forecasts. The hotel segment was the bright spot, boosting leasing revenue 9% YoY despite flattish revenue growth from the mall and office businesses. However, consolidated results were dragged by the 27% decline in development revenue. ALI said this was not due to construction delays or cancellations but rather sales “bookings” (when revenue recognition can begin). Since reservation sales rose annually from 2020-24 (and stayed flat in 2025), we suspect that the magnitude of the drop in revenue was partly a timing issue. Thus, we expect sequential improvements in revenue and a more modest decline for the full year.


Reservation sales fall YoY across the board; unsold inventory declines

1Q26 residential reservation sales fell 22% YoY (flat QoQ) to P24.4bn, as sales of both premium and core segments declined (see Figure 3). On a more positive note, the value of unsold residential inventory fell 25% YoY and 14% QoQ to P150bn. Since the company did not launch any residential projects in 1Q26, this means that all the sales for the period were from existing inventory. We estimate that ALI’s unsold inventory is now equivalent to 15 months of reservation sales (before cancellations), from 19 months in 4Q25.

 

Same-mall sales grow 10% while hotel revenue jumps 30%

Mall revenue rose 2% YoY to P5.8bn. 

However, excluding ATC, which was sold in 4Q25, mall revenue grew 8% YoY, on the back of 10% same-mall sales growth. On the other hand, hotel revenue grew 30% YoY (+6% QoQ) thanks to the contributions from newly renovated hotels and the acquisition of a hotel in 3Q25. Furthermore, resort occupancy increased 15ppt YoY to 71%.

 

Management turns even more cautious on residential segment

During the 4Q25 briefing in Feb 2026, ALI said it would reduce capex to P70bn-80bn (from P92bn in 2025) and residential launches to P30bn (from P47bn in 2025). Since the Middle East conflict began, it has turned even more cautious. The company canceled a mid-income condo project, The Heights, which was launched in early 1Q26, and “paused” the selling and development of Laurean, a luxury project in Makati (launched in 2025), until the middle of 2027. ALI might launch horizontal projects in 2H26 but no longer intends to launch condominium projects this year. Moreover, it lowered its FY26 capex budget further (to P50bn) and is maintaining its target of “minimal” incremental debt this year.

 

Rental portfolio completion plans stay the same

Despite its lower capex budget, Ayala Land is maintaining its target rental completions for the year: 200k sqm of malls (which will add c. 10% to mall floor area) and 70k sqm of office (which will add 5% to office floor area).

 
Universal Robina - 1Q26: Branded revenues rebound
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Core income dips 2% despite revenue growth of 9%

URC reported 1Q26 core income of P3.8bn, -2% YoY, despite overall revenue growing 6%. While after-tax income is a mild disappointment (for a quarter where non-branded earnings are seasonally high), there are hopeful signs for the branded business, particularly on the domestic front.

 

Domestic BCF revenue rises 10%

Domestic BCF sales for 1Q grew 10% to P22bn, driven by volumes, with nearly all key categories showing QoQ market share gains. The company’s sustained A&P push was likely a factor behind these gains and, on the flipside, for the decline in EBIT margins. EBIT was up only 1% for the quarter.

 

Revenue for international ops up 1% but EBIT jumps 11%

The International BCF business saw sales growing only 1% YoY (on constant currency) but EBIT rose a respectable 11% on improved efficiencies. URC cited a strong Malaysia business. The Vietnam operation also appears to be showing signs of a recovery after government regulations affected many industries there last year. Note the gains were magnified in PHP terms due to relative currency movements.

Commodity business hit by lower sugar prices

The Commodities segment saw sales drop 5% YoY and EBIT plunge 29%. Weak sugar prices drove profits lower, and this was only partly mitigated by strong numbers from Flour. Management expressed concern about the next season’s sugarcane crop given soaring fertilizer prices. Ultimately, however, it is government policy that will be primary determinant of the profitability of the sugar industry given its closed, protected nature.

 

Early 2Q indications still look good

Management indicated that April numbers are looking strong, suggesting that the impact of soaring energy prices has yet to bite. Nevertheless, it’s hard to see how consumption, especially of discretionary, impulse-driven products can stay unaffected by what’s likely to be further acceleration of inflation.

 

Guidance for EBIT growth

Notwithstanding the dip in EBIT (-2%) despite the 6% growth in sales for 1Q, URC is guiding for mid-single growth in revenue (led by the branded businesses) and EBIT rising faster than that. Management says EBIT growth will be backed by “operational efficiencies” and “cost optimization”. Lower input prices (e.g. coffee) as well as an easier YoY base of comparison likely figured into the calculus too.

 
Monde Nissin - 1Q26: Solid quarter for APAC BFB; Quorn sees sharp margin jump
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Core profit up 11% on revenue growth of 9%

MONDE 1Q26 reported core profit of P3.3bn (+11% YoY), trending above estimates. Consolidated net sales were in line with expectations, growing 9% YoY to P22.8bn. Revenue accelerated from the +6% growth in 4Q driven primarily by sustained volume-led growth in APAC BFB. Group-wide gross margin rose 1.3ppt YoY on continued improvements in Quorn, which saw gross margin expand 8.8ppt YoY.

 

Noodles bounce while solid growth at Biscuits and “Others” continues

APAC BFB revenues in the quarter rose 9% YoY to P19.1bn as volumes increased across all three categories. The company highlighted that noodle volumes were still able to grow (+3% YoY) despite price hikes. This is the first such volume increase after four consecutive quarters of YoY decline, with improvements across all noodle subsegments (dry, wet, cups). Meanwhile, biscuits (+5%) and “Others” (+16%) continue to see faster growth than noodles.

 

Quorn turns EBIT positive as gross margins jump

The Meat Alternatives business (Quorn), which management is now referring to as the “Protein” business, saw sales inch up +1.4% on a constant currency basis, with volumes down 2%. Gross margin saw surprising improvement QoQ (+4.4ppt) and YoY (+8.8ppt) on progress from its “transformation program.”

Quorn also posted its highest EBITDA in a quarter since 3Q21 at P325mn (+132% YoY) with EBIT turning positive despite ramped up A&P spend. Although margins and profitability have improved, the absence of revenue growth remains a concern.

 

Early 2Q numbers still strong

The company signaled a strong April for APAC BFB but opted not to provide full-year guidance given ongoing geopolitical and macro uncertainties. Management specifically cited noodles growing above 10%, which it believes could be due to more difficult economic conditions (since noodles are a cheap food source.)

 

Macro risks cloud outlook

The threat of rising raw material costs (e.g. wheat, palm oil) will be mitigated over the next two quarters with hedges in place. However, margins remain vulnerable to a prolonged conflict and increases in other costs (e.g. logistics) will be impossible to avoid.

 

This means price adjustments are almost inevitable to maintain margins. Such price increases, however, will have to be done in “manageable chunks” to minimize impact on consumer demand, which is already facing significant pressure from sharply higher inflation.

Century Pacific - 1Q26: Resilient demand
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1Q26 net profit +10% to P2.1bn, revenues +15% to P23.0bn

CNPF’s 1Q26 net profit grew 10% YoY to P2.1bn, while revenues rose 15% to P23bn—both trending slightly above consensus expectations. Revenue growth momentum was carried over from 4Q25 for both the OEM (+32% from 29% in 4Q) and the Branded business (+11% from +13% in 4Q). Group-wide gross margin contracted 1.0ppt on higher input costs but was offset by further cost rationalization efforts. EBITDA margin thus rose 0.2ppt YoY and absolute EBITDA rose 17%. A higher tax rate, however, led to net profits growing less.

 

Resilient volume-driven growth amid rising inflation

The Branded business saw revenues rise 11% YoY to P17.9bn on volume-led growth across the board. Dairy and Meat saw “double-digit” and “high single digit” growth, respectively. Growth for the Marine segment was “single-digit” due to temporary supply issues, despite higher-than-expected demand.

 

Management highlighted that the continued strength of the Branded business despite rising inflation reflects the resilience of its diversified portfolio, with consumers having the option to down-trade to its more affordable SKUs.

OEM sustains rebound

OEM sales (22% of revenue in 1Q) continued to climb (+32% YoY from +29% in 4Q) from a still-low base as well as stabilized tuna prices. Healthy demand for coconut products also supported continued OEM growth, with ongoing capacity investments aimed to capture long-term growth prospects.

 

Positive April indications but…

Management stated that April saw sustained gains for the Branded business as growth “accelerated close to mid-teens”. The company is guiding for “measured price increases” to mitigate margin compression which is being aggravated by the energy crisis.

 

The imminent price hikes from CNPF, as well as price hikes for many other products (food, transportation, utilities, etc.), will certainly affect consumer behavior. What should give some comfort is that many of CNPF’s products (e.g. sardines) have been a refuge for consumers during tougher times.

Coal mining profits up 3% YoY

Coal contract bidding update

While management expressed disappointment over the April 28 bidding deferral, it remains optimistic that the process could proceed by the third or fourth week of June. The ongoing uncertainty around the coal mining contract has led management to trim its sales volume guidance to 14–15mn MT (vs. 15.4mn MT in 2025), reflecting reduced capacity following the deferral of re-fleeting capex.

Implementation of real property valuation reform law to take place in 2028-2029

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) is targeting full implementation of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA) in 2028 or 2029. RPVARA will adjust appraisal practices and valuation standards. It transfers the authority to adjust the “schedule of market values” from local government units (LGUs) to the national government. However, local government units will retain authority over tax rates and assessment levels. Since LGUs can lower tax rates if the “schedule of market values” rises substantially, we expect only a modest decline in profitability of developers from the implementation of RPVARA (likely less than 5% reduction to net profit).
