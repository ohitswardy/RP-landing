REGIS REPORT

17 April 2026

From trading in a tight range for much of the week, the PCOMP ultimately fell below the 6,000 handle, down by 1.6% or 99.08 pts WoW to 5,999.13 as investors weighed improving developments in the Iran war against the deteriorating domestic macro outlook. Optimism around peace negotiations between US-Israel and Iran pushed US stocks to record levels, while most other Asian equity benchmarks have largely recovered to their pre-war levels, or otherwise are now just modestly below them.

The PCOMP however remains the biggest laggard in the region, still down 9.3% from end-February. Although global oil prices have started to ease -- now down over 15% from recent highs --  they remain significantly elevated vs pre-war prices. The Philippines' vulnerability as net energy importer has foreign funds continuing to exit the market, with net outflow of $79m for the week wiping out YTD inflows that amounted to close to $370m before the Iran war.

On monetary policy, after March CPI surprised at 4.1%, breaching the BSP's 2-4% target range, BSP Gov. Remolona signaled more room for rate hikes and stated that the central bank will focus on tempering second-round effects of the oil price shock. Thus consumer names remained under pressure on growing concerns over inflation and weaker purchasing power, with JFC (-3.5% WoW) among the worst performers. Residential proxy ALI (-4.9% WoW) also underperformed following the hawkish BSP signals. Conglomerates broadly pulled back after leading gains in the prior weeks, with AC (-7.5% WoW) and JGS (-6.3% WoW) also among the biggest laggards in the index.

Meanwhile, MER led gainers (+3.9% WoW) alongside SMC (+3.8% WoW), MONDE (+3.3% WoW), and LTG (+1.3% WoW), benefiting from rotation into defensives and yield plays.

The PH10Y yield inched up 6.2bps WoW to 6.6604%. The peso weakened -0.2% WoW to 60.077:$1.

Century Pacific - 4Q25: Top line growth momentum to be tested by global headwinds
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY25 net profit +11% to P7.1bn, top line +10% to P83.3bn

CNPF’s FY25 profit rose 11% YoY to P7.1bn as revenues grew 10% YoY to P83.3bn. The results are behind REGIS estimates and a bit short of consensus. For 4Q alone, profit and revenues increased 21% and 13%, respectively. Top line growth for the quarter was led by the sustained growth in the branded business (+13% YoY from +12% in 9M) and a recovery in the OEM business (+29% YoY from -5% in 9M). Consolidated gross margin in 4Q narrowed by 0.3ppt YoY as input costs continued to normalize, but streamlined opex spend helped keep EBITDA margin up (+0.3ppt).

 

Volume-driven momentum for Branded business

Branded business revenues grew 13% YoY to P17.0bn for 4Q, a tad faster than the 12% growth in 9M. The slight acceleration was driven mostly volume-driven across all categories. The “Milk and Others” is now the largest segment within the Branded business at 31% of FY25 revenues. This segment grew “mid-teens” in 4Q, outpacing Marine’s high-single digit and Meat’s mid-single digit growth. The company indicated that the Branded business still saw double-digit growth in 1Q26, but rising inflation brought about by the Middle East conflict may pose a risk. That said, management remains optimistic on the demand for staples.

 

OEM rebounds but headwinds arise

Revenue for the OEM business (21% of revenue in 4Q) climbed 29% YoY to P4.5bn.

A lower base in 4Q24 (vs 9M24) as well as an improvement in overseas demand due to normalized tuna prices helped spur growth. The company, however, flagged the rapid increase in tuna prices as of late to the ~US$2.0k/ton level which usually prompts OEM buyers to hold back. Robust demand for coconut products, however, could help partially offset softness in tuna.

 

Margins remain under pressure but mitigation measures in place

4Q EBITDA margin expanded 0.3ppt in 4Q despite a 0.3ppt contraction in gross margin as reduced opex spend mitigated the impact of higher input costs in the quarter. CNPF guided for more cost-cutting measures such as lower A&P and controlled product launches to minimize any need to pass on price increases as higher input costs bite. Several key inputs (e.g., raw materials, ingredients, packaging) were also hedged for 3–6 months.

 

Aiming for double-digit top line growth and a sharp jump in capex

Management maintained its double-digit guidance for top line in 2026, though this remains “aspirational” amid demand and cost uncertainties. Meanwhile, capex this year is programmed to rise sharply to P8–9bn from the usual P3–4bn to fund capacity expansion across all segments.

 
Philippine Seven - 4Q25 - SSSG turns positive; pressing on with growth despite macro uncertainty
 
 
by Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

FY25 net profit -5%, revenue +7%

Philippine Seven’s FY25 profit amounted to P3.6bn (-5% YoY) which is within REGIS expectations. Revenue increased 7% to P95.1bn, while EBIT was up a modest 2% to P5.7bn. Systemwide sales, meanwhile, grew 6% to P99.5bn.

 

For 4Q alone, profit was practically unchanged YoY at P1.2bn as higher interest expense offset the growth in EBIT (+3%) and revenues (+9%). Margins contracted on higher interest expense and store rollout costs. Revenues were up 9% to P25.9bn as systemwide sales grew 12% and store count rose 9%. The company added 361 stores (net) in 2025, bringing the total store network to 4,491 as of end-2025.

 

SSSG returns to positive territory

4Q SSS reverted to growth (+3.1%) after four consecutive quarters of negative SSSG on operational improvements alongside the benefit of a low base in 4Q24.

Management noted better demand for RTE (ready-to-eat) offerings alongside continued improvements on the availability of vape products and e-wallet uptime. The addition of card payment terminals also contributed to growth as the initial 1,000 stores reported an increase in new customers and basket size.

 

According to the company, 4Q momentum carried over into 1Q26 on the sustained ramp-up of card payment acceptance (>3,000 stores) and better sales in key categories (e.g., non-alcoholic beverages, RTE meals, tobacco). The spillover effects of the global energy crisis could, however, poses a risk to such a rebound.

 

Accelerated store rollout despite external risks

Philippine Seven emphasized that it is firm on its goal of opening more than 500 new stores this year as it targets to hit 5,000 by end-2026. However, given inflationary and global risks, management stated that expansion will be guided by a “strategic market development plan” and affordability initiatives.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI raises tariffs at some ports to offset fuel costs

ICTSI chairman and president Enrique Razon Jr. said that they have already implemented tariff adjustments to make up for the differential in diesel and fuel prices across its terminals. In the Philippines though, MICT in particular, any change in tariff will require approval from the port authority. He further noted that there is so far no disruption on (fuel) deliveries, but noted that prices are very high. ICTSI is “constantly working” with the government and other agencies throughout the world “to make sure that the Philippines has a continuous (fuel) supply.” The impact of the shortage with the closure of the Strait of Hormuz is mostly felt in Asia, unlike Latin America, which has big suppliers like the United States, Canada, Mexico, and Brazil. Power and fuel collectively comprise roughly 10% of ICTS’s cash costs.

ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rise 3% in March; 1Q26 volumes up 8%

Mexico’s port authority reported that throughput at Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) rose 3% YoY to 137k TEUs in March. Growth slowed from the 18% print in February due to congestion issues. Management previously indicated that the that congestion has led to elevated yields. This brings CMSA’s 1Q26 volumes up 8% to 417k TEUs. Note that CMSA is expanding capacity from 1.7mn TEUs to 2mn TEUs (~167k TEUs per month) by 3Q26.

Subic port regulator reduces charges amidst energy crisis

The Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA) is implementing relief measures, which include the following. A 5% reduction on stevedoring charges, including harbor fees, berthing/anchorage fees, and harbor cleaning fees. 5% reduction in wharfage fees and storage fees, as well as an extension of the free storage period by two days. SBMA s also reducing its revenue share of cargo-handling (stevedoring/arrastre) services by 5%. Measures took effect on April 8 and will remain in force until conflict subsides. Based on 2026 revenue projections, SBMA estimates these measures will provide a total fiscal relief of P76mn over a one-year period. ICTSI (ICT PM) is the concession holder of the port in Subic, which accounts for ~1% of revenues.

Jollibee provides update on Smashburger

JFC, in a press release, provided an update on Smashburger stating that SSSG for company-owned stores improved from negative mid-teen levels in 3Q25 to positive double-digit growth by Mar-26 on higher transaction volumes following its "Summer of Smash" campaign. The company noted improvements in "menu innovations and value offerings" as the drivers for the recovery with company-owned stores seeing transaction and average daily sales growth in the high teens. Smash targets to open 10-12 new stores in 2026 through franchise expansion. Recent new store openings in both traditional (in malls or standalone) and non-traditional (e.g., sports arenas, airports, universities) formats saw healthy customer demand and attractive returns. Smashburger had 192 stores as of end-2025, down from 214 in end-2024. In 2025, 62% of stores are company-owned while 38% are franchised stores. The brand contributed to 3.0% of total SWS in FY25, down from 4.0% in FY24. For 2025, growth in SWS and SSS was -12.9% and -8.2%, respectively.

Jollibee provides update on Highlands Coffee and Compose Coffee

JFC, in two separate press releases, provided updates on Highlands Coffee and Compose Coffee elaborating on Highlands' strong performance as of 1Q26 and Compose' expansion outside of Korea. Highlands posted high-single digit in SSSG for 1Q26 and high double-digit revenue growth in FY25 exhibiting "sustained demand and disciplined execution". JFC noted the solid store-level profitability and the continued growth of digital channels. Compose saw a strong pre-opening debut for its first store in Taiwan with "one cup sold every 20 seconds" and wait times stretching up to two hours. Management highlighted the brand's "unlimited potential to become a leading global brand" with quality coffee at accessible prices. The company also reiterated its plans to enter the Philippine market this year. Store counts for Highlands Coffee and Compose Coffee are at 2,972 and 985, up from 2,629 and 850 at end-2024, respectively. Highlands and Compose comprise 2.4% and 7.4% of JFC's FY25 consolidated SWS.

Jollibee secures antitrust regulatory approval for South Korean hot pot brand, Shabu All Day

JFC via its 70%-owned subsidiary Jolli-K, secured Korea Fair Trade Commission approval for its US$87mn (~P5.1bn) acquisition of 100% of All Day Fresh Co., Ltd., operator of Shabu All Day. According to JFC, the acquisition price implies a 4x EV/EBITDA multiple. Haidilao International Holding Ltd. (6862 HK), global Chinese hot pot chain, currently trades at 8.9x FY26E EV/EBITDA. Upon completion of the transaction, Shabu All Day will be immediately consolidated into JFC's financial statements and the company estimates that it will add +2% to revenues, +8% to Global EBIT, and +1% (170 stores) to store count in 2026. The consolidation impact is based on All Day Fresh's annualized FY25 results vs JFC's inhouse FY26 forecast.

Meralco’s P4bn cost recovery cleared by regulator

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) allowed Meralco to collect Excellent Energy’s (EERI) fuel cost recovery, amounting to P3.67bn, from consumers. Cost can be billed for 12 months, starting in September, translating to a rate hike of P0.1099/kwh. EERI is a 40%-owned unit of Meralco, 33% of San Miguel (SMC PM), and 27% of Aboitiz Power (AP PM). The ERC said it “recognizes” EERI’s commercial operations date.

DigiPlus receives license to operate in Western Cape, South Africa

DigiPlus Interactive received licenses to operate from the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB) in South Africa. The South African online gaming market is estimated to have generated US$5bn in revenue in 2025. 31% (c. US$1.5bn) came from Western Cape. DigiPlus generated P84bn in revenue in 2025 (in the Philippines). Philippine online gaming revenue (including bingo) grew 30% to P200bn (c. US$3.3bn) in 2025. 4Q25 industry GGR fell 12% to P40.4bn.

Bloomberry online gaming platform could generate profit in 2027

During the company’s stockholders’ meeting, Bloomberry Chairman Enrique Razon Jr. said its online gaming platform will relaunch in 2Q26. Rebranding from “MegaFUNalo” to “FUNaloMAX”. Mr. Razon said that 2027 was the earliest that the platform could generate a profit. He also said that on premise gaming will remain challenging this year, especially VIP.

Okada Manila 1Q26 GGR improves sequentially

After declining for four consecutive quarters, the 1Q26 GGR of Okada Manila rose 9% QoQ (down 17% YoY) to P6.5bn, thanks to an improvement in the VIP segment. After declining for five consecutive quarters, VIP rolling chip climbed 82% QoQ (+ 9% YoY) to P57.6bn. However, volumes of both (live) mass tables and slots fell on a QoQ and YoY basis. Mass table volume fell 4% QoQ and 29% YoY to P7.4bn, the lowest level since 4Q23. Slot handle declined 7% QoQ and 4% YoY to P46.4bn, the lowest level since 2Q22.

Cebu Air’s pax volumes rise 11% YoY in March; 1Q26 up 8%

Cebu Air reported its passenger volumes rose 11% to 2.5mn in March, accelerating from the 8% pace in February. Cited the start of summer school breaks and sustained momentum in the international segment. This was despite the >20% increase in base fares starting from the second week of March, after the surge in jet fuel prices. Brings 1Q26 pax volumes up 8% YoY to 7.5mn, trending below our FY26E forecast of 31mn (+17% YoY). Every 5% cut to our pax volume forecast reduces EBITDA by 18% and profit by 90%, holding other things constant. For 2Q26, management said that they are adopting a cautious and deliberate strategy, adjusting flight frequencies as needed and prioritizing routes with higher demand. Recall that fuel surcharges, which we estimate account for <10% of CEB’s fares, more than doubled beginning April 1 so the real impact on demand will become apparent then. Fuel availability is a larger concern, in our view. CEB previously indicated that its fuel buffer is likely sufficient until end-May.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP governor sees room for rate hikes

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the central bank has room to raise policy rates given mounting pressures on inflation and economic growth due to the Middle East conflict and the lingering effects from the flood control scandal. According to Remolona, the BSP will focus on tempering second-order effects of the oil price shock but will avoid any excessive tightening. March inflation was 4.1%, higher than BSP's 3.1-3.9% forecast and above the 2-4% target band. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 5.1% (previously revised from 3.6%). Inflation has averaged 2.8% as of end-March. The second out of six scheduled Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23.

S&P downgrades PH outlook to "stable" from "positive" but affirms credit rating

S&P Global Ratings downgraded its outlook for the Philippines to "stable" from "positive" but affirmed the country's "BBB+" investment-grade rating. The S&P cited that the Middle East conflict has further complicated the Philippines' economic outlook following the fallout from the flood control corruption scandal. S&P also highlighted the potential need to absorb a higher deficit as the gov't ramps up support measures to mitigate the impact of elevated fuel prices. The agency added that a future credit rating upgrade remains anchored on a lower gov't deficit, stable debt and accelerated fiscal consolidation. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona noted that the central bank will continue to monitor upcoming data to implement policies aimed to safeguard price and financial stability given heightened uncertainties.

Moody's retains "Baa2 stable" investment rating for the Philippines

Moody's retained its "Baa2 stable" investment-grade rating for the Philippines. The ratings agency noted key strengths such as a stable banking system, ample foreign-currency reserves, pipeline of PPP projects, and resilient OFW remittances. Credit challenges include low capita per income, "weak rule of law and control of corruption", high climate and natural disaster risk. A ratings upgrade could be considered if there is rapid improvement in fiscal and gov't debt metrics but a downgrade may occur if such metrics deteriorate.

DBCC opposes gas and diesel excise tax suspension

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) has determined that the suspension of gas and diesel excise taxes "would not likely provide meaningful relief, as any reduction in retail pump prices would be marginal and largely offset by prevailing market dynamics". Finance Secretary Frederick Go highlighted that the suspension of excise taxes on kerosene and LPG directly provides relief on the most vulnerable. The Philippine Statistics Authority survey exhibited that 48% of total kerosene usage is from the bottom 30% of households, while 56% of LPG users are from the bottom 70%. The reduction for kerosene and LPG is equivalent to P5.60/liter and P3.36/kg (P37 per tank). The gov't anticipates P4.1bn in foregone revenue for the next three months due to the suspension but would be offset by P13bn in additional VAT collections assuming crude stays at US$100/bbl. If the suspension on gas and diesel excise tax were approved, the gov't stated that it would have incurred P43.6bn in foregone revenues. Excise taxes are at P10/liter for gas and P6/liter for diesel.

Marcos suspends excise tax on kerosene and LPG; gas and diesel unchanged

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. approved the suspension of excise taxes on kerosene and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) but did not remove the levies on gasoline and diesel as it was still up for discussion. The reduction for kerosene and LPG would be equivalent to P5.60/liter and P3.36/kg (P37 per tank), respectively. Kerosene and LPG prices are currently around P154-P177.2/liter and P1,000-P1,600/tank. Marcos stated that the Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport (UPLIFT) Committee will meet to discuss and decide on the possible reduction or suspension of gasoline and diesel excise taxes. Excise taxes are at P10/liter for gas and P6/liter for diesel. The President stated that he was not supportive of calls to cut or suspend the VAT on oil as revenues are needed to fund aid programs for the public. However, Marcos mentioned that he remains open for a future review if necessary. Note that there currently is no law permitting the President to suspend the VAT on fuel, so a measure needs to be passed into law through Congress. The Dept. of Finance (DOF) estimated a revenue loss of P120bn should the VAT on fuel be reduced or removed.

Government allows up to 90% WFH for IT-BPO firms for one year

The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) allowed Registered Business Enterprises (RBE) to continue enjoying fiscal incentives while having up to 90% work-from-home arrangements. This will be permitted for one year beginning 24 March 2026, unless lifted or extended by the President. The FIRB permitted incentive granting agencies, such as the Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA), to grant a lower maximum WFH threshold. However, RBEs must be allowed to avail of at least 50% WFH. PEZA previously requested the FIRB to allow up to 100% work-from-home (WFH) arrangements for IT-BPO firms (in PEZA accredited zones). Under the CREATE MORE law, firms registered with incentive-granting agencies can only implement WFH for up to 50% of employees. However, CREATE MORE has a provision that allows higher WFH arrangements during emergencies or natural disasters. Recall President Marcos has declared a state of national energy emergency and urged the country to conserve energy.

BSP gives banks flexibility to extend loan payments

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) announced that it has extended regulatory relief measures to BSP-supervised financial institutions (BSFIs) to support consumers and businesses amidst rising costs due to the energy crisis. The BSP has permitted banks to give borrowers more time to settle loans. Subject to bank assessments, grace periods of up to six months (up to one year for agricultural loans) can be granted according to the BSP. The loans may also temporarily not be classified as past due or non-performing by banks for up to one year. The central bank also urged banks to temporarily suspend online transaction fees to ease costs for consumers and businesses.

BIR net collections for March increase by 7%

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) guided that collections net of tax refunds in March amounted to P187bn, up 7% YoY. BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza noted that the performance is encouraging "especially at this time of economic strain". Following President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s order to extend the income tax return filing deadline by one-month from the original deadline of April 15—the BIR may potentially miss its April collection target of P499bn. The BIR targets to collect P3.43trn (+10% YoY) for 2026.

Remittances rise by 2.6% in February

Cash remittances amounted US$2.8bn in Feb, 2.6% higher YoY—decelerating from the 3.5% growth in Jan. Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+3.8%), Saudi Arabia (+5.1%), and the UAE (+5.2%).

Foreign ownership in telco, retail sectors, and gov't infra projects eased

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., through Executive Order (EO) 113, issued the 13th Foreign Investment Negative List which provides updates on the guidelines on specific sectors where foreign ownership is restricted or prohibited. This will take into effect 15 days after its publication. Full foreign ownership is allowed for firms involving the operation and management of telecommunications provided that there is reciprocity from the investor's home country. Without such reciprocity, only 50% foreign equity is allowed for such firms. Retail trade enterprises with paid-up capital of less than P25mn are now able to have up to 40% foreign equity, previously foreigners were completely prohibited. 75% foreign ownership is now permitted for gov't infrastructure projects but limited to projects that require special skills or technologies that local companies lack. Up to 40% foreign equity is allowed in the exploration, development, and utilization of natural resources, including the use of water from natural sources, subject to existing laws and regulations.

Government rejects unsolicited Iloilo airport proposal

The Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines (CAAP) rejected the P21bn unsolicited proposal of Prime Asset Ventures to upgrade and operate the Iloilo international airport. Cited that the the group’s proposal lacked an endorsement to the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev). The government is opting to privatize the airport through a solicited mode instead. Prime Asset Ventures is a company owned by the Villar family.

Philippine gaming industry feeling strain from higher fuel prices

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that elevated fuel prices have begun to weigh on the Philippine gaming industry. “At PAGCOR, we will adjust what we need to do. We have to be in tune with the times and ensure that responsible gaming remains at the center of what we do.” PAGCOR previously deferred the implementation of Minimum Guaranteed Fees (MGF) of P9mn/month on e-casino operators. The implementation was deferred from April 1 to June 1.
