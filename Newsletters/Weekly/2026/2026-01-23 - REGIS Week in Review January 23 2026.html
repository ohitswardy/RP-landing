REGIS REPORT

23 January 2026

Philippine equities drifted lower for the week as foreign buying slowed and share prices succumbed to profit-taking, with the index retreating from the 6,500-handle. The PCOMP fell by 2.0% WoW or 131.41 points to close at 6,333.26 as early gains for the year gave way to consolidation amid lighter volumes.

 

Net inflow from offshore funds only amounted to $4m for the week with SM (-5.0% WoW), JFC (-4.9% WoW), and PLUS (-6.3% WoW) bearing the brunt of foreign selling. These names were among the worst performers for the week along with ALI (-6.4% WoW) and BPI (-6.9% WoW).

 

A clear risk-off tone prevailed, with cyclicals leading the market lower. Financials (-3.5% WoW) and Property (-3.0% WoW) were the weakest sector indices as concerns over slowing loan growth and soft reservation sales weighed on sentiment, while investors mulled over the central bank's repeated cues for the near end of the easing cycle, with one remaining 25-bp rate cut for the year expected for the February policy-setting meeting.

 

In contrast, Mining (+5.2% WoW) once again outperformed supported by rising global metal prices, with nickel miner NIKL (+10.5% WoW) and gold producer APX (+9.5% WoW) leading gains. Meanwhile, select conglomerates bucked the downtrend, with JGS (+6.7% WoW) and GTCAP (+3.3% WoW) extending their rally from the previous week and emerging as the top index gainers this week.

 

Defensive strategies also remained apparent with dividend payers SCC (+2.0% WoW), DMC (+1.5% WoW), AREIT (+1.4% WoW), and TEL (+0.7% WoW) all outperforming, along with ICT (+1.9% WoW) which was again the top foreign bought name for the week.

 

Elsewhere on macro, the Marcos administration unveiled its 'Big Bold Reforms' program aimed at regaining trust and boosting investment, with improvements eyed in ease of doing business and public infrastructure spending, among many other initiatives. On politics, impeachment complaints were filed against Pres. Marcos largely over his alleged involvement in the flood control corruption scandal. Given the perceived soft merits of the case, ground-level chatter from the opposition and legal experts alike suggests this was a procedural filing intended to trigger the constitutional one-year bar on subsequent impeachment filings.

 

Market activity was lighter this week with ADTO at $104m. The peso bounced from record lows, +0.5% WoW to 59.085:$1. Yield on the PH10Y continued to inch higher, +1.5bp WoW to 6.0635%.

Nickel Asia - Rising tide; Upgrade to Buy
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Turn of fortune

The sustained strength in nickel ore prices has prompted us to raise our FY25E/FY26E profit forecasts by 12%/47% and lift our TP to P6.40/sh. The Indonesian government’s curbs on domestic ore production have resulted in firmer prices for Philippine ore exports. Even after outperforming the PCOMP by 48% in the past year, the stock continues to trade at an undemanding 8x FY26E PER and offers an attractive 7% dividend yield. We upgrade to BUY from Hold.

 

Favorable conditions for Philippine nickel miners

Nickel ore prices have outperformed LME nickel prices and are expected to stay elevated in the near-to-medium term, as the Indonesian government continues to tighten regulations governing ore production, which is ultimately aimed at eradicating the global supply glut in refined nickel. While it remains uncertain if these measures will be sufficient to push the refined nickel market back into a deficit by 2028 (although LME nickel prices have rallied sharply so far this year), the policy direction is nonetheless supportive for Philippine nickel miners.

Raising profit forecasts by 12%-47%

Robust ore prices and the production ramp-up of its three new mines should drive a strong increase in NIKL’s earnings and dividend prospects. We upgrade our FY25E and FY26E profit estimates by 12% and 47%, respectively, after incorporating 30%/49% increases in our nickel ore ASP forecasts to US$26/US$29 per MT whilst tempering assumptions on power ASPs. Note that our nickel ore ASP forecasts are premised on benchmark LME nickel prices averaging US$15-16.5k in FY25E-FY27E, below current spot rates of US$17.6k.

 

Revised TP of P6.40/sh offers 47% total potential return

We upgrade our SOTP NAV-based TP by 56% to P6.40/sh, after incorporating higher nickel ore ASP forecasts and a valuation roll-over to 2026. Our TP suggests 47% total potential return. Longer-term, the completion of exploration activities at its gold prospect, following encouraging initial results, as well as the possible nickel processing partnership with DMCI Mining, could potentially unlock further upside. Downside risks relate to lower commodity prices, as well as regulatory and execution risks.

 
AREIT - Asset acquisitions continue
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Raising target price to P41.00/share but maintaining Hold rating

We raise our target price on AREIT by 6% to P41.00/sh as we roll over to FY26E and account for recently announced acquisitions. However, we maintain our Hold rating on the stock. Although AREIT has superior occupancy and a track record of consistent DPS growth, its portfolio is still heavily concentrated (68%) in the office segment, where we expect only modest improvements over the next few years. Furthermore, we forecast AREIT’s DPS CAGR to decelerate to 5% in 2025-2027E.

 

Well-managed portfolio enhanced by DPS-accretive acquisitions

AREIT has been able to consistently grow (or at least maintain) its dividends per share (DPS) every quarter. We credit this largely to: 1) strong portfolio occupancy; and 2) DPS-accretive acquisitions. Its portfolio’s occupancy has been consistently high, with current occupancy at 99% and office occupancy at 96%, much higher than the Metro Manila average of 82%. Meanwhile, AREIT’s sponsor, Ayala Land (ALI PM), has been injecting assets at DPS-accretive valuations.

Still concentrated in the office segment; DPS CAGR to slow down

Although AREIT has a history of consistently growing its DPS, we expect growth to slow down over the next few years. We forecast DPS CAGR to decelerate to 5% in 2025-2027E, from 9% in 2021-2025. This is predominantly because of two reasons: 1) 68% of AREIT’s AUM is in office property, a segment whose current outlook is uninspiring; and 2) as AREIT grows larger, it will become increasingly difficult to inject assets that will be meaningfully DPS accretive.

 

Raising target price to P41.00/sh

We raise our target price by 6% to P41.00/sh as we roll over to FY26E and account for asset acquisitions in FY25-FY26E. Our TP is derived using a discounted cash flow and implies 14.8X FY26E P/AFFO and dividend yield of 6.1% (at the target price), lower than the 6.9% average of other Philippine REITs (based on consensus forecast). At the current price, AREIT offers a 2026E dividend yield of 5.7%, lower than the 5.9%-8.3% yields of other Philippine REITs (based on consensus forecasts). Key risks relate to office vacancy and interest rates.

ICTSI on track to operate Batangas port by 2028

ICTSI said that it is progressing with the construction of the first phase of South Luzon Container Terminal (SLCT) in Batangas, with commercial operations targeted for 2028. Frist phase will have a capacity of 800k TEUs and will have a controlling depth of up to 18 meters and an 11-hectare container yard. This construction update is in line with our expectations. We anticipate SLCT will address the long-term capacity limitations of existing ports in Manila. SLCT accounts for 11% of our NAV-based TP of P560/sh.

Jollibee provides update on Coffee and Tea and Chinese Cuisine segments

JFC in another press release provided more guidance on recent positive developments, the disclosure elaborated about the expansion-fueled growth of its Coffee and Tea and Chinese Cuisine segments. It highlighted its asset-light growth strategy through franchising and capital efficiency optimizations leading to accelerated payback periods of ~two years for company-operated Hong Kong and China stores. This is the company's third guidance disclosure after the recent update last Jan 15 and the initial guidance disclosure back in Dec 17. On the Coffee and Tea segment: The segment was noted to have "recorded strong percentage growth" given successful marketing campaigns in Compose Coffee and high traffic-driven SSSG in Highlands Coffee. Store counts for Compose Coffee and Highlands Coffee are estimated at ~3,000 and ~1,000, up from 2,629 and 850 at end-2024, respectively. The segment contributes to ~21% of total SWS as of 9M25, led by CBTL at 9.9% and Compose Coffee at 7.7%. For the Chinese Cuisine segment: Yonghe King was said to have ramped up opening of franchised stores as part of the company's effort to get China "back to financial viability and sustainable growth". Tim Ho Wan in Hong Kong was said to now be profitable within six months post-acquisition and plans to increase North America stores to 20 by 2028. The segment contributes ~6% of total SWS as of 9M25, led by Yonghe King at 3.9% and Tim Ho Wan at 1.8%.

Jollibee in another press release, elaborated further on Compose Coffee's robust expansion-led growth and highlighted its plan to expand outside of South Korea and into Southeast Asia. In 2025, Compose Coffee's total product sales were said to have increased by more than 10% YoY. Store count for Compose Coffee has hit 3,000 as of 9M25, up from 2,629 in end-2024, respectively. The Coffee and Tea segment contributes to ~21% of total SWS as of 9M25, led by CBTL at 9.9% and Compose Coffee at 7.7%.

Globe announces partnership with Starlink

Globe announced that it is offering a direct-to-cell (DTC) satellite service through a partnership with Starlink. The service will allow subscribers to use their regular LTE mobile phones even in areas without Globe tower/transmitters. Connectivity will be provided through Starlink’s network of low earth orbit satellites. Users will thus be able to connect even in remote areas or during periods when terrestrial networks are not working or out of reach. DTC will, however, require direct unobstructed view of the sky. Globe didn’t disclose how much the service will cost but we expect it should be more expensive than “regular” cellular service. Globe also didn’t disclose commercial arrangements between itself and Starlink. This service, like Starlink satellite broadband, is likely to have its niche market.

PH gov't cuts 2026 revenue target

The gov't has cut its revenue target for this year after reducing its economic growth forecasts given its expected drag on collections and overall fiscal performance. The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) lowered the 2026 revenue target to P4.82trn from P4.98trn previously. Recall that the DBCC trimmed the GDP growth targets for 2026 to 5-6% and 2027 to 5.5-6.5% from 6-7% initially. The detailed revenue breakdown revisions are as follows. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) is now expected to collect P3.43trn (+11% YoY) vs P3.58trn previously (+15% YoY). BSP expects rebound in economic growth by 2027; guides for end of easing cycle

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona discussed with President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. to discuss the country's economic outlook. Remolona stated expectations of modest economic growth in 1H26, then a rebound in 2027 driven by earlier policy easing. Other drivers were noted to be gov't infra spending recovery as well as improvements in investor and consumer sentiment. He also signaled that the end of the central bank's easing cycle is near as any future easing is likely to be limited. Note that he has previously guided that one more potential rate cut to 4.25% in Feb is "on the table". Inflation was said to likely be within the BSP's 2-4% target range. The central bank's outlook on inflation was cited as "broadly benign with expectations well-anchored". The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2025/26/27 are 4.6/5.4/6.0%, respectively. For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%. Consensus and Regis Inflation expectations for 2026 are 2.6% and 3.0%, respectively.

BOP posts US$827mn deficit in Dec; GIR settles at US$110.8bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Dec posted a deficit of US$827mn. Wider than the US$225mn deficit in Nov but narrower than the $1.5bn deficit in Dec 2024. This brings the YT-Dec deficit at US$5.7bn, a sharp reversal from the US$609mn surplus in the same period last year. The reversal to a FY deficit was said to have been likely driven by the trade gap, harsher-than-usual weather, and corruption-related issues impacting domestic demand and foreign investment. The FY figure is narrower than the lowered US$6.2bn deficit target of the BSP for 2025 (US$6.9bn previously). For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn. Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Dec stood at US$110.8bn, 4% higher YoY and down from the US$111.3bn in end-Nov. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. This is higher than the BSP's forecast of $109bn for 2025. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

Philippine production of palay (unmilled rice) amounted to 19.68 MMT (+3% YoY) in 2025. The FY25 output fell short of the Dept. of Agriculture's (DA) projected output of 20.35 MMT. The DA projects palay production this year to be 20.3 MMT (+3% YoY), with imports forecasted to range between 3.6 MMT and 3.8 MMT. Nov Asset quality stable MoM, better YoY; loan growth slows a tad

Philippine banks’ asset quality metrics were broadly stable-to better MoM in November, but significantly improved YoY. (We quote ratios using loans excluding reverse repos (RRP) and excluding interbank loans (IBL), as these can swing wildly on a monthly basis, and throw the ratios off.) NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) was flat MoM at 3.53%, but a full 20bps lower compared to the 3.73% posted in Nov 2024. In nominal terms, NPLs were up 1.5% MoM to P544.7bn while loans were up 1.4% MoM. Past Due (PD) ratio inched down 1bp MoM to 4.51% from 4.52% in Oct, and was down 5bps from the 4.55% the year prior. PD loans were up 1.2% MoM to P696.0bn. Restructured Loans (RL) improved 4bps MoM to 2.15%, but was up 5bps YoY. NPL Cover was steady MoM at 95%, but up from 93% in Nov’24. Loan growth (ex-RRP/IBL), meanwhile, slowed slightly to 10.6% YoY, from 10.8% in October. This is the slowest YoY print since September 2024. System-wide LDRs eased slightly to 72.7% vs 73.1% the previous month. By bank type, asset quality generally improved among the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs) and thrift banks (TBs), while they deteriorated quite badly with the smaller digibanks, with NPL ratio up 22bps MoM and with PD ratios up 59bps MoM. That said, the digibank industry is still quite small, and is rapidly growing (loans up 57% YoY), which does account for higher volatility of their numbers.

Impeachment complaint filed against President Marcos

An impeachment complaint has been filed against President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. over his alleged involvement in a corruption scheme concerning unprogrammed appropriations. The complaint also cited the alleged kidnapping and surrender of former President Rodrigo Duterte to the International Criminal Court, alleged failure to veto unprogrammed appropriations and other unconstitutional provisions in past budgets, alleged kickbacks from budget insertions, among others. This is the first impeachment complaint against Marcos since he assumed presidency in 2022. The Committee on Justice will determine if the complaint has probable cause to proceed; otherwise, it may recommend dismissal of the complaint. The House plenary may override the dismissal recommendation if at least one-third of all House members vote to proceed with the impeachment trial. If the impeachment complaint is dismissed before a Senate trial, another cannot be initiated against him for one year, as prohibited in the Constitution.
