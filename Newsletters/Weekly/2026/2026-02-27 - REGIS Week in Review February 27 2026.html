REGIS REPORT

27 February 2026

Philippine shares extended their rally to another week, zooming past 6,500 to levels last seen over a year ago amid persistent foreign inflows while the market digested the slew of 4Q corporate earnings released this week. The index breached the 6,600 handle for the first time since January 2025, and managed to stay afloat despite month-end selling from the MSCI February rebalancing. For the week, the PCOMP jumped 2.3% or 146.12 pts WoW to 6,611.24.

The week was heavy with 4Q earnings reports. For banks and property, BDO (-1.1% WoW) slightly beat forecasts as loan growth unexpectedly picked up in the quarter. AREIT (-3.3% WoW) results were ahead though negative rental reversions show the office segment remains quite soft. MEG (+0.4% WoW) was in-line with forecasts although residential results appear to have been hit by cancellations while malls were curiously flattish in the quarter.

In the utilities space, MER (+3.6% WoW) results were ahead of estimates as contribution from its LNG terminal acquisition offset soft distribution volumes. TEL (+0.7% WoW) results were in-line marked by an acceleration in mobile revenues and gains in its share of broadband adds. MYNLD (+6.3% WoW) earnings beat estimates while the company also announced higher-than-expected dividends, paying out higher than its policy.

On consumer, RRHI (+2.4% WoW) results came in-line with forecasts, with growth accelerating in 4Q as SSSG improved across all formats -- a positive read-through for sector earnings for the quarter.

And finally for the miners, PX (+12.6% WoW) earnings beat estimates as higher selling prices offset lower gold and copper output. NIKL (-1.6% WoW) results were also ahead as better-than-expected profits from the mining unit more than made up for the shortfall in the power segment.

Outside of earnings, the MSCI rebalancing saw the inclusion of MYNLD and APX (+8.4% WoW) into the MSCI Philippines Small Cap Index. Among the moves on the main index were the upweights in ICT, BDO and BPI (-2.3% WoW) and the downweights on ALI (-1.6% WoW), SMPH (+2.6% WoW) and SM (+0.9% WoW).

This week also saw the P3.7-bn ($64-m) placement of AREIT shares, involving sponsor ALI's sale of 88m shares at P42 apiece. This was at a 3% discount to the previous close and offers 6% yield at deal price.

In other notable moves, after lagging for much of the year thus far, PLUS (+15.8% WoW) staged a strong rebound, leading index gainers for the week amid market chatter of insider buying -- a reversal from talks of insider sellers in the prior weeks. SCC (+9.6% WoW) followed, bouncing after its sharp sell-off last week due to news of the rebidding of its coal operating contract. The biggest positive mover for the index remained to be ICT (+5.6% WoW), which is still notching new historic highs week after week on sustained foreign inflow.

Foreigners have remained net buyers for 9 straight weeks now -- an unbroken streak for 2026 so far -- taking YTD inflow to over $360m. The market also recorded net inflow of $16m on MSCI rebalancing day alone, against expectations of small net outflow for the Philippines this round. The peso bounced this week, up 0.8% WoW to 57.660:$1.

Ayala Land - 4Q25 reservation sales fall; targeting flattish sales in 2026
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY25 recurring profit rises 8%

Ayala Land’s FY25 headline profit jumped 38% to P39.1bn, as the company recorded P8.5bn in gains from the sale of ATC mall in 4Q25. Excluding the gain, recurring profit rose 8% to P30.6bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Although revenue declined 1%, operating profit increased 6%, thanks to margin expansion brought about by faster revenue growth of high-margin rental segments and lower revenue from low-margin service businesses.

 

Core segment leads decline in 4Q25 residential reservation sales

4Q25 residential reservation sales fell 30% QoQ and 8% YoY to P24.4bn. The decline was led by the core segment, which saw sales drop 55% QoQ and 27% YoY to P6.7bn (see Figure 3), despite the maintenance of aggressive promotions.

 

Managing unsold inventory levels

We estimate that unsold residential inventory fell 7%QoQ and 12% YoY to P175bn. Management estimates that its unsold inventory is now equivalent to 19 months of reservation sales (from 20 months in 3Q25, 22 months in 4Q24) and is targeting to bring this down to 17-18 months in 2026.

 

Residential targets suggest cautious outlook on Metro Manila condominiums

The company’s residential targets in 2026 are lower than the 2025 figures, signifying a cautious outlook. It is guiding for presales to hit a minimum of P120bn, slightly lower than FY25’s P125bn.

Moreover, after lowering residential launches to P46.6bn (down 42%) in FY25, management intends to reduce this further to (a minimum of) P30bn in FY26. The launches this year will predominantly be horizontal projects in provincial areas, suggesting a more subdued outlook on Metro Manila. Taking a more positive angle, ALI believes it can reach approximately the same level of sales despite lowering launches.

 

Mall revenue growth to accelerate in 2026

4Q25 mall revenue grew 15% QoQ and 8% YoY, as the redevelopment of two of its flagship malls was completed. Management expects faster revenue growth in 2026 (from 5% in 2025) as the redevelopment of the two remaining flagship malls is completed and the company expands leasable area by 9% (200k sqm).

 

Excluding the malls being redeveloped, FY25 mall revenue grew 10%, with samemall revenue growing 7% (near the high end of the historic range of 4%-8%).

 

More optimistic on the leasing segment

Management is more optimistic on the leasing segment, particularly on malls. After doubling leasing capex in FY25, it intends to maintain the figure while reducing total capex by c. 20%. Moreover, it expects to increase the segment’s share of EBITDA to 50% by FY27 (from c. 45% currently). With strong recurring cash flow and lower capex, ALI expects “very little incremental debt” in FY26.

 
AREIT - More muted office rental declines in 2H25; negative reversions persist in 1Q26
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

FY25 net profit rises 28%

AREIT’s FY25 net profit (excluding revaluation gains/losses) rose 28% to P9.4bn, in line with REGIS and slightly ahead of consensus forecast.

 

Office occupancy stays flat QoQ; lease expiries to increase

Office occupancy stayed flat QoQ at 96%, much better than the industry average occupancy of 82% in Metro Manila. However, the occupancy level could change as lease expiries are set to increase to 113k sqm and 128k sqm in 2026 and 2027 (from 42k sqm in 2025). These represent 16% and 18% of the office portfolio (8% and 9% of the total building portfolio).

 

Negative office rental reversions persist in 1Q26

On average, expiring office contracts in FY25 were renewed at rental rates that were 6% lower than the previous contracts. This is an improvement from the 10%-15% decline in 1H25. These negative office rental reversions have persisted in 1Q26 (so far).

One tenant was renewed at a rental rate that was 17% lower than the previous contract. Excluding this tenant, renewed contracts have seen a 6% decline in rental rates in 1Q26. Management expects the magnitude of the decline to be more muted by the end of the year.

 

New P19.5bn share-for-property swap to increase proportion of malls

AREIT shareholders approved the acquisition of P19.5bn worth of malls from Ayala Land. AREIT will issue 441mn new AREIT shares to ALI in exchange for 375k sqm of malls. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 14% and shares outstanding by 12%. After the swap, malls will increase to 29% of AUM (from 19%) and offices will decline to 60% (from 69%). Our forecasts already account for this swap.

 

In order to meet minimum float requirements, we estimate that the Ayala Group will need to sell c. 50mn AREIT shares (c. P2.2bn) ahead of the acquisition.

 
Robinsons Retail - Healthy pick-up
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

Improved 4Q lifts revenue and EBIT growth for the year

Robinsons Retail (RRHI) reported 4Q net profit of P2.6bn (+5% YoY), which brings FY25 headline net profit to P5.7bn (+6% YoY excluding one-off items). Core operations were healthy as topline rose 6% YoY to P210.4bn, while EBIT grew 7% YoY to P10.5bn, in line with expectations. Notably, for 4Q25 alone, net sales and EBIT grew faster, +8% and +13% to P61.1bn and P3.8bn, respectively. Overall topline growth in 4Q was driven by increased basket sizes across food formats alongside robust growth of the drugstore and specialty segments. Blended SSSG accelerated to 3.6% from 1.6% the previous quarter.

 

SSSG improves across all formats in 4Q

RRHI saw SSSG accelerate across all formats in 4Q25 vs the previous quarter as management cited the increase in basket sizes alongside stable inflation. The Food segment saw topline rise 6% on the back of +2.9% SSSG and store additions. Within the Food segment, convenience stores saw positive SSSG (+3.1%) for the first time since 2Q24. Drugstores’ net sales rose 12.5% YoY on +8.9% SSSG driven by price adjustments and private label penetration (~10% of sales). The Food and Drugstore segments accounted for 76% of revenue in 4Q25.

The recent acquisition of PremiumBikes boosted the Specialty segment’s 4Q topline growth to 20% YoY to P5.5bn; excluding the December consolidation, the segment would have grown 9% YoY.

 

Private label penetration to bolster margins and mitigate competition

The company is aiming to raise private label penetration for both the food and drugstore segments (8% and 10% of FY25 sales, respectively). Raising the share of private label revenues should help bolster margins and improve customer loyalty.

 

2026 guidance similar to last year; growth momentum seen to continue in 1Q

For 2026, management’s targets were largely unchanged: 2-4% SSSG, 130-170 net store additions, and P5-7bn capex but a lower gross margin expansion expectation of +15-20bp. Meanwhile, Jan and Feb were said to be within 2-4% SSSG guidance on continued growth momentum from 4Q.

 
Nickel Asia - Core profits up 2x in 4Q25; Scales back RE targets
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

FY25 core profit up 78% YoY to P5.5bn - Ahead

Headline FY25 profits grew 313% YoY to P6.27bn. Removing FX gains, we estimate recurring profits rose 78% YoY to P5.5bn, underpinned by strong nickel ore ASPs (+26% YoY to US$26/WMT) and higher sales volumes (+9% to 18.6mn WMT). Better-than-expected profits from the mining unit more than made up for the shortfall in the power segment.

 

4Q profits double as volume surge offsets 6% drop in ASPs

Derived 4Q25 profits doubled to P1.1bn, supported by a 37% YoY expansion in sales volumes. Management monetized its lower grade limonite ore stockpile, resulting in a 6% YoY decline in blended ASPs. Meanwhile, its associate HPAL unit turned a profit in 4Q, driven by elevated cobalt prices.

 

Power unit profits halve on soft spot prices; RE target halved to 1GW

Contributions from the power segment halved to P91mn in FY25, weighed down by lower WESM prices.

With 120MW of new solar capacity brought online, total gross capacity reached 293MW by end-2025; however, 15% of the capacity remained exposed to the spot market. The company has reduced its renewable energy capacity goal to 1GW by 2028 from the prior 2GW target, opting to prioritize projects backed by firm offtake agreements.

 

2026 capex guidance up 25% YoY to P12bn

Management guided for capex of P12bn in 2026, up 25% from last year’s capex of P9bn. Bulk (>90%) of the budget is earmarked for the buildout of its RE pipeline and will be deployed mostly within 1Q26.

 

Dividend of P0.14/sh in 1H

The company declared a regular dividend of P0.14/sh, implying a 31% payout ratio. There was no special dividend in this year’s initial tranche, given the anticipated front-loaded capex requirements. We still expect special dividends in 2H, underpinned by expectations of firm nickel ore prices. The more measured power expansion plans should, in our opinion, support improved capital allocation and shareholder returns.

Meralco FY25 profit up 12% to P50.6bn

Meralco reported FY25 headline net income of P51bn (+11%) and core net income of P50.6bn, just mildly exceeding Street expectations despite soft volume growth. Profit growth for the year was driven by contributions from the LNG terminal acquisition, a strong reserve market in Visayas, and provision reversals. These factors outweighed softer distribution volumes and lower margins at the RES business. Derived 4Q25 core profits rose by 5% to P10.5bn, moderating from the 9M25 growth rate of 14%, as volume weakness persisted at the distribution unit (DU) amid weather disruptions and broader economic softness. Management noted that cooler temperatures and macroeconomic challenges continued to weigh on electricity demand in the first two months of 2026. It is nonetheless hoping for a modest demand rebound of 3% this year. Meralco is also slowly progressing on the energization of Terra Solar Phase 1, with only 1,288MW PV panels installed as of end-January and another 200hectares land acquisition pending.

PLDT FY25: 4Q revenue bounce

FY25 core income P34.5bn (+1%). PLDT’s core income for FY25 reached P34.5bn, up marginally (+1%) YoY. While this profit figure appears below our estimates (P35.6bn), we are generally positive about the results. Mobile revenue rose 5% QoQ, the same pace as Globe. The key reason for the profit miss was the sharp rise in depreciation charges in 4Q, otherwise core income would have been in line. Headline profit, including exceptional items, was P30bn, down 1% YoY. Note PLDT took another P6.8bn in accelerated depreciation expenses in FY25. The company treated this as an one-off item which is excluded in the calculation of core income. This will unduly show a more flattering profit in subsequent periods. We note the meaningful improvement in 4Q wireless revenue, up 5% QoQ. While it may be only 1% higher YoY, the 4Q number implies PLDT maintained market share vs Globe in the final period of FY25. Wireline broadband subscribers rose 126k for the last quarter, which is a significant improvement as it outpaced Globe’s for the first time since 3Q24. Fintech Maya contributed P681m in earnings to PLDT (which owns 37.6% of the former). The implied 4Q contribution, however, mean a QoQ decline in Maya’s earnings. Management attributed the decline more to “non-operating items” (suggesting a clean-up ahead of an IPO) than to the de-linking of e-wallets to gaming apps, which affected fee incomes. For FY25, Maya posted a profit of P1.7bn.

Finance Secretary expects new 10% Trump tariff to have minimal impact on Philippine goods

Dept. of Finance Secretary Frederick Go stated that US President Donald Trump's recent declaration to impose a 150-day 10% global duty (effective Feb 24) will have minimal impact on Philippine goods. Go highlighted that majority of Philippine exports were already exempted (i.e., semiconductor and key agricultural exports such as coconut and banana). The new global tariff declaration by Trump follows the US Supreme Court's decision to void the earlier imposed Trump tariffs. Recall that the prior US-imposed tariff on Philippine goods was 19%. In 2025, Philippine exports to the US amounted to US$13.3bn (+11% YoY) and comprised 16% of total exports.

BIR collections flat in January

Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Commissioner Charlito Mendoza guided that preliminary collections for January amounted to P358bn, +0.8% YoY. The January figure is around 10% of the agency's P3.58trn (+15% YoY) revenue goal for 2026. In 2025, the BIR fell short of its initial P3.22trn (+13% YoY) target as FY revenue amounted to P3.11trn (+9% YoY). Mendoza expects revenue collections to be driven by the rebound in GDP growth to 5-6% this year from 4.4% in 2025 as well as from the acceleration of gov't infra projects. Trust AUMs jump 25% YoY in 4Q25

Data from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) shows that Trust Assets of banks and non-banks jumped 24.6% YoY to P9,183bn ($160bn) as of end-2025. The AUMs of Bank trusts surged 28% YoY to P5,696bn. BDO (BDO PM) continues to be by far the market leader, with P2,588bn ($45bn) in AUMs, up 12.2% YoY, with a market share of 28%. BSP tightens liquidity requirements on e-wallets

The BSP issued a draft circular which proposes tighter liquidity requirements for e-wallets. Currently, these rules apply only to banks, which must hold at least 50 percent of outstanding e-money balances in trust. The remainder must be kept in liquid assets, including bank deposits, government securities or settlement accounts with the BSP. All liquid assets must also be unencumbered. E-wallets would also be required to develop formal recovery plans to navigate periods of financial stress. The BSP is also seeking to enhance its crisis intervention powers for nonbank e-money issuers in financial trouble. The BSP may declare when a company can no longer continue operations without external support and has no reasonable prospect of recovery within a practicable period.

Bill to scrap travel tax progresses at House Committee

The House Committee on Tourism approved a bill proposing the removal of travel tax imposed on Filipino travelers flying overseas. Bill’s passage would mean travel tax imposed under Presidential Decree No. 1183 and the Tourism Act of 2009 would be repealed. Exempt from travel tax are overseas Filipino workers, Filipino permanent residents overseas who stayed less than a year in the Philippines, and children aged two years and below. The travel tax amounts to P1,620 (US$28) for economy passengers and P2,700 (US$47) for first class. The government estimates annual revenue loss of P8bn (<1% of total tax revenues) from the removal of the tax. The removal of the tax will be positive for airlines. International routes respectively account for ~25% and ~40% of Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) and Philippine Airlines’ (PAL PM) passenger volumes.

House initiates impeachment proceedings against VP Duterte

Impeachment proceedings against Vice President Sara Duterte have been formally initiated as the four filed complaints were sent to the House Committee on Justice. Members of the committee have 60 days to determine if the complaints are sufficient in form and in substance. The committee will come up with a recommendation whether to approve or dismiss the complaints. The House would then need a one-third vote to approve and elevate the impeachment complaint against Duterte to the Senate. Recall that Duterte recently announced in a press briefing that she will run for president in the 2028 election.

SEC reduces public float requirement for large IPOs

The SEC reduced the minimum public float for the IPO of firms with a market cap of at least P50bn. From 20% to 15%. “Exceptionally large” issuers with market capitalizations of at least P200bn may request for a lower float from the PSE. However, the public float cannot go below 12%. Other tiers were retained. 33% minimum public float for market cap below P500mn. 25% minimum float for market cap between P500mn and P1bn. 20% minimum float for market cap between P1bn and P50bn.
