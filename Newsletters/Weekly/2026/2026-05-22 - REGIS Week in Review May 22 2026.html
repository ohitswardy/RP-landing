REGIS REPORT

22 May 2026

Philippine equities pulled back this week, trading in a narrow range and ultimately inching lower by 0.3% or 15.37 pts WoW to 5,961.40 with investors largely remaining on the sidelines following the close of the 1Q26 reporting season and amid the absence of fresh market catalysts. While growing hopes for a US-Iran peace deal along with unwavering enthusiasm over the AI trade drove global equities broadly higher for the week, rising global bond yields further weighed down risk appetite onshore, with the PH10Y yield even hitting its highest level since 4Q18.

 

On domestic macro, BSP Gov. Eli Remolona said the Monetary Board is considering an off-cycle rate hike prior to its June 18 meeting, although they may wait until after May inflation data is released on June 5 before making its next monetary policy decision. Recall that the BSP expects inflation to average 6.3% for the year, from an average of 3.9% so far YTD, which implies inflation should average out to 7.5% for the balance of the year. The market is expecting two more 25-bp BSP rate hikes for a policy rate of 5.00% by YE-26.

 

Index decliners outnumbered advancers 3 to 2, with modest gains in ICT (+1.3% WoW) providing a large offset to sharper declines across other heavyweights. The stock closed the week just shy of fresh highs as foreign inflows into the name persisted, which took its index weight to 23.7% -- way above 16% at the start of the year and 11% a year ago.

 

Meanwhile, MER (-3.5% WoW) was the biggest drag on the index along with BDO (-2.1% WoW) and BPI (-2.2% WoW), as foreigners were largely sellers outside of ICT. Elsewhere, ALI (+0.8% WoW) managed to finish the week higher buoyed by bargain-hunting after it hit a fresh 52-week low early in the week.

 

Net foreign flows reverted to outflows following two weeks of only slightly positive prints, with net selling for the week amounting to $22m. The peso rebounded from a fresh low of 61.745:$1 earlier in the week, finishing only +0.1% WoW to 61.691:$1. The PH10Y yield was up 13 bps WoW to 7.7461%.

Ayala Corp - Telco unit shines, but other core units lag in 1Q26
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q26 core profit slips to P11.2bn, trailing Regis, Street estimates

Reported 1Q26 net profit before pref dividends declined by 5% YoY to P12bn while core profit slipped by 1% to P11.2bn. The results are trending behind both Regis and Street forecasts, as the solid performance of Globe (GLO PM), narrower losses at several portfolio investments, as well as lower parent expenses only partly cushioned the shortfalls at ALI, BPI, and ACEN.

 

AC Mobility unit sales up 34% YoY; BYD sales double

AC Mobility’s net loss narrowed 73% QoQ/35% YoY to P109mn, partly due to the 12% QoQ/34% YoY increase in unit sales to 12.3k units, with BYD sales doubling. The divestment of loss-making businesses was also a key factor in the earnings improvement. Relative to the broader industry, AC estimates its market share improved by 1.2ppts to 8.5%, with management targeting a 12.5% share by year-end. However, the influx of new brands has reduced its share of the New Energy Vehicle (NEV) segment to 64% from 85% previously. We are not concerned about the NEV share drop given the structural industry shift, with the ongoing oil crisis likely to accelerate demand further.

Bonuses lead to EBITDA drop at AC Health; IMI core profit up 21%

AC Health posted a wider net loss of P143mn. Even though revenues rose 19%, EBITDA fell 21% YoY to P209mn due to the late accrual of last year’s employee bonuses. Despite lower revenues, lMI’s core profit rose 21% YoY to US$4.5mn due to lower opex and financing costs. Cost rationalization initiatives at AC Logistics continued to gain traction, with losses narrowing to P167mn in 1Q.

 

Capital allocation

Management remains committed to supporting the growth of ACEN and the capital requirements the latter needs to achieve its targets. The group however says it does not plan to stray from the prudent path amidst the prevailing macroeconomic challenges. AC’s expanded buyback program will be continued, but in a calibrated manner to avoid adversely affecting stock liquidity and index weightings.

 
Jollibee - 1Q26: Margin drop cuts earnings sharply
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Paolo Garcia +632 88946636 paolo.garcia@regis.ph
 

1Q26 profit drops 40% despite revenue growth of +9%

JFC’s 1Q26 net profit (after dividends to prefs) dropped 40% to P1.4bn on narrowed gross and operating margins. Gross margins contracted 2ppt YoY, a decline which management attributed principally to higher input costs for the domestic business. As a result, operating profit fell 18% YoY even after an 18% YoY reduction in A&P expenses. Revenue was within expectations, growing 9% YoY to P76.5bn on +3.5% group-wide SSSG, while SWS rose 10% YoY.

 

Overall, the operating results were a disappointment and should prompt a review of Street estimates on near-term earnings. Nonetheless, while we are wary of the firm’s appetite for acquisitions, we don’t think these results should necessarily derail the long-term trajectory of the business.

 

SSSG moderates but still healthy

Both the domestic and international business saw growth in SWS (+8.0% and +13.5%) and SSS (+3.2% and +4.0%) moderate relative to the previous quarter. Group SSSG decelerated to +3.5% in 1Q26 from +5.2% in 4Q25. The domestic business faced a high YoY base (+8.5% in 1Q25), while the int’l segment was weighed down by weakness in Yonghe King (-5.0%) and CBTL (+1.4%).

 

Operating income decline for both domestic and international ops

Gross margin in the quarter contracted 1.8ppt QoQ and 2.0ppt YoY as input costs (e.g., chicken, beef, rice) for the domestic business rose sharply while the company held back on raising prices.

Operating profit for the domestic business thus declined 17%.

 

While management attributed the profit fall principally to the domestic business, we have to note that operating profit for the international side also fell >20%. This suggests that the margin pressure was felt on both fronts.

 

Margin recovery will take time

The company began implementing “measured” price increases in April in order to offset mounting cost pressures and should continue doing so in the coming months. For fear of affecting consumer demand, the company is reluctant to pass on cost increases too quickly. The energy crisis and its continuing impact on costs will likely mean full margin recovery will take time.

 

Company to reassess guidance; int’l spinoff ambition remains

JFC says it will reassess guidance and disclose any changes by 2Q. While 1Q SWS growth of 10.3% and SSSG of 3.5% were broadly in line with company expectations, store network and EBIT growth targets may be revised following potential capex adjustments and the 18% EBIT decline. The company also stated that it is “absolutely” still targeting the same date (late 2027) for the international spin-off.

 
Philippine Property - 1Q26 recap: residential revenue falls even as reservation sales rise
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Residential revenue falls sharply

The key highlight of the 1Q26 results season for the property sector was the disconcerting fall in residential revenue across a number of developers. The sequential improvements in reservation sales and unsold inventory provided a contrasting, if not contradictory, picture of the state of the residential property market.

 

Residential revenue falls QoQ and YoY

Aggregate residential revenue of the top four developers fell 16% YoY (and 29% QoQ) to P42.4bn, the lowest level since 3Q23, partly due to an increase in cancellations and partly due to timing of “bookings” (when revenue recognition can begin). The magnitude of the decline was a shock to us, given the reservation sales trends from 2021-2024 as well as management assurances that cancellations had been contained.

 

Residential reservation sales remain weak but improve sequentially

After declining QoQ and YoY in 4Q25, aggregate reservation sales grew 9% QoQ (down 6% YoY) to P66.9bn. While the improvement is arguably a silver lining, we still consider the residential market weak, as the reservation sales figure only represents c. 65% of the 2019 average. The sequential increase in sales came from both high-end and mid-income units. While developers have implemented promotions on mid-income ready-for-occupancy units since a year ago, some of them have now instituted more aggressive payment schemes for high-end projects as well.

Unsold inventory value declines

The aggregate value of unsold residential inventory fell 6% QoQ and 4% YoY (to P504bn). However, the unsold inventory levels of some developers remain high, at over three years of reservation sales (based on sales over the past 12 months).

 

Office take-up declines sequentially; Makati CBD and Bonifacio perform better

BPO firms took less incremental space QoQ and Metro Manila office vacancy remained flat at 18% (see Figures 5-8). Similar to previous periods, most of the transactions were recorded in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio, where vacancy rates are much lower (at 8%-10%) than other areas. Spot rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ but remain 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Lease contracts in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio were generally renewed at higher rental rates while most contracts in other areas were renewed at flat to lower rental rates.

 

Mall revenue growth remains healthy despite weak GDP growth

Although real GDP growth slowed in 1Q26, Philippine mall revenue growth of the top three mall operators still grew 7%, the same pace as in 4Q25. Excluding the impact of Ayala Land’s divestment of a mall, we estimate that same-mall revenue growth of the top three operators remained healthy at 7%.

 
GT Capital - 1Q26: Auto margins bounce QoQ but outlook uncertain
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph / Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

1Q26 core profit drops 9% to P7.9bn – In line with Street and Regis estimates

GTCAP reported its net income declined by 3% YoY to P8.9bn, inclusive of its share of Toyota Motor Philippines’ (TMP) utilized tax incentive, as well as other one-offs. Removing the one-offs and pref dividends, core profit fell 9% YoY to P7.9bn, tracking within Regis and Street expectations. Contributions from Metrobank (here) and Metro Pacific (MPI) improved, but profit contributions from other segments weakened. We nonetheless believe the shares offer compelling value at 3x PER and 56% discount to market NAV. Maintain BUY.

 

TMP: Soft demand, unfavorable FX and mix dragged 1Q profits down 46% YoY

Standalone profits from TMP rose stood at P5.3bn (-16% YoY), inclusive of a P1.9bn tax credit from the CARS program.  On a recurring basis, profits fell 46% YoY to P3.4bn due to softer unit sales (-6% YoY), as well as unfavorable FX and mix. Sequentially, GPM rebounded to 13% in 1Q26 from 9% in 4Q25, as sales of higher-margin vehicles improved to 13% of total from 11% in 4Q. TMP’s EV sales (inclusive of hybrids) jumped 40% YoY to 5.5k but its market share of this category dropped to 22% from 42% in the prior year.

Management highlighted that its financing arm, Toyota Financial, accounted for 38% of TMP’s sales in 1Q26, up from 27-30% in previous quarters, as local banks started to tighten lending practices.

 

Metro Pacific 1Q26 profit up 5%; Losses persist at property unit

Infrastructure arm, MPI, posted 5% YoY growth in standalone core income, moderating from +15% pace seen in FY25, amid continuing demand weakness at Meralco’s (MER PM) distribution unit and the dilutive impact of the water unit’s IPO. Meanwhile, the property unit recorded a 1Q26 profit of P3.8bn, driven entirely by a chunky gain from the sale of a 20% stake in JV entity Federal Land NRE Global to its parent, GTCAP. Underlying operations remain weak, with the segment posting an EBIT loss and reservation sales falling by 36% YoY to P3.8bn, nearly half of pre-Covid sales but 27% above the latest four-quarter average sales.

 

Estimates revised; Maintain BUY with stock at 3x PER, 56% discount to NAV

We cut our FY26E profit forecast by 4% to adjust for our latest outlook but maintain our BUY rating and TP of P930/sh, as the stock remains deeply undervalued. 

 
Bloomberry Resorts -1Q26: another net loss despite high win rates
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

Another net loss in 1Q26

We estimate that Bloomberry’s 1Q26 recurring net loss hit P530mn, a reversal from the recurring profit of P445mn in 1Q26 (P2.8bn recurring net loss in 4Q25). Meanwhile, EBITDA fell 32% YoY (up 123% QoQ) to P3.0bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The deterioration was led by Solaire Entertainment City (SEC), which would have seen an even larger decline if not for unusually high win rates, which also contributed to the sequential improvement in profitability.

 

SEC: volumes of every category fall YoY; GGR declines despite high win rates

SEC’s GGR fell 18% YoY (up 7% QoQ) to P9.8bn, as volumes of every category fell on a YoY basis, particularly from foreigners. Furthermore, slot volume declined for the fourth consecutive quarter to P68.9bn, the lowest level since 1Q22. GGR would have fallen even more if not for the unusually high win rates in VIP and mass tables (see Figures 4 to 6). Although the mass table win rate fell 8.2ppt YoY, the 1Q26 figure of 49.5% is still much higher than the historical average of c. 40%. Meanwhile, the VIP win rate expanded 50bps YoY and 1.4ppt QoQ to 3.7%, higher than the theoretical level of 2.9%. If not for the elevated win rates, we estimate that SEC’s GGR would have fallen another 12% to P8.8bn, the lowest level since 4Q21.

Solaire North sees some positive trends

Solaire North’s GGR was flattish YoY and QoQ (at P4.7bn). While the property has been underperforming our forecasts, there were some positive trends in 1Q26.

• Slot volume grew 19% YoY (+3% QoQ) to P43.9bn, the highest level in SN’s history.

• The mass table win rate improved for the third consecutive quarter to 35.2% (+4.8ppt YoY, +2.2ppt QoQ), which is also a record for SN. While management acknowledged this was partly due to luck, it had been making some changes, such as introducing certain side bets.

• SN has been attracting more foreigner players, who comprised 10% of 1Q26 GGR, from less than 5% in 1Q25.

 

Online gaming opex falls

Opex on BLOOM’s online gaming platform declined 68% QoQ to P230mn. Although the company will fully relaunch the platform in 3Q26, management said that FY26 opex will be lower than last year’s P2bn.

 

Korean operations could hit breakeven in 2H26

Now that BLOOM’s gaming operations in Korea have been sold, management believes that the hotel operations in Korea could hit breakeven by 2H26.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI secures US$300mn loan from AIIB

ICT secured a US$300mn loan from Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) to finance capacity expansion and technology upgrades at three Philippine container terminals. The capacity of Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) is slated to expand to 3.7mn TEUs by 2027 (from 3.6mn TEU currently). Mindanao Container Terminal’s capacity is targeted to hit 1mn TEUs by 2028 (from 300,000 currently). The capacity of South Luzon Container Terminal is slated to reach 800,000 TEUs by 2028 (from 400,000 TEU currently).

AGI 1Q26 core attributable profit rises 4% YoY

AGI’s core attributable profit for 1Q26 rose 4% YoY to P5.2bn. The higher profit contribution from Megaworld offset the declines from Emperador and Travellers. Emperador’s contribution fell because AGI’s ownership shrank from 79% in 4Q25 to 75% in 1Q26. Megaworld 1Q26 EBIT rose 4% on the back of 3% revenue growth and a 3.2ppt YoY expansion in residential GPM (brought about by better product mix and “disciplined cost management”). Travellers’ EBIT fell 50% QoQ and 27% YoY to P800mn. This was largely due to the 33% QoQ and 43% YoY decline in VIP GGR (to P2.0bn), which was brought about by lower VIP rolling chip. Meanwhile, mass GGR fell 8% QoQ (up 2% YoY) to P4.5bn. Emperador's 1Q26 profit rose 4% YoY and reversed from a P813mn net loss in 4Q25 to P1.9bn. Profit growth was driven by improvements in topline and margins for both the brandy and whisky segments. Brandy (65% of total revenue) reverted to positive revenue growth (+1% YoY following two consecutive quarters of YoY decline as sales performance across all brands both int'l (Terry, El Presidente) and local (Emperador) were "very encouraging" during the quarter. Whisky likewise returned to positive sales growth (+2% YoY) after four consecutive quarters of YoY decline led by Dalmore. Margins improved for both segments on primarily due to a favorable product mix for Brandy and improvements in operational efficiencies for Whisky.

Cebu Air’s pax volumes drop 0.7% YoY in April amid fuel-driven fare hikes

Cebu Air flew 2.27mn passengers in April, down 0.7% YoY on seat growth of +11.9%. Seat load factors fell 940bps YoY to 74.4%, the lowest level since 3Q22. Management attributed the weakness to base fare adjustments and fuel surcharges implemented in response to elevated fuel prices, which softened demand. The company has since recalibrated pricing strategies, including targeted seat sales, with early signs of a recovery in bookings emerging in late-April and continuing into May. At its results briefing, management noted that the company could reach profit break-even should jet fuel prices ease to US$120–130/bbl and cash break even at US$145-150/bbl (vs. spot US$159/bbl).

DITO reports wider 1Q26 net loss

DITO CME Holdings Corp. (DITO PM), parent company of DITO Telecommunity Corp., reported a attributable net loss of P6.8bn in 1Q26, wider than the P1.7bn loss for the same period last year. Revenues rose 24% YoY to P5.8bn. Services revenues amounted to P5.7bn, while non-service revenues were P143mn. DITO Tel's mobile subscribers for mobile grew by 460k to 16.63mn as of end-March from 16.17mn as of end-2025. Fixed wireless access subscribers rose by 70,000 to 470k from 400k as of end-2025. The company targets to be profitably by 2028.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

BSP considering off-cycle rate hike

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona in an interview stated that the Monetary Board is considering a second straight hike prior to its June 18 meeting (3rd out of 6 this year). Remolona elaborated that while the likelihood of an off-cycle hike is not likely, "we are considering it". He also added that the board may wait until after May inflation data is released on June 5 before making the next monetary policy decision. The BSP Governor also stated that the central bank can likely tolerate the peso hitting P63.50 to the dollar given that it moves in a "measured" way which would not stoke inflation. The USD-PHP rate has depreciated from P58.8 at the start of the year to P61.7 as of May 21. The BSP and consensus expects inflation to average 6.3% for 2026. Consensus expectations for the policy rate is for two more 25bp hikes to 5.00% by year-end.

BOP posts US$2.1bn deficit in April; GIR settles at US$104.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of April posted a deficit of US$2.1bn. Narrower than both the US$2.6bn deficit in the previous month of March and the US$2.6bn deficit in April 2025. This brings the 4M26 deficit to US$7.4bn, wider than the US$5.5bn deficit for the same period last year. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn. Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Apr stood at US$104.3bn, down 1% YoY and 2% lower MoM. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.9 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

Remittances rise by 2.3% in March

Cash remittances amounted US$2.9bn in Mar, 2.3% higher YoY—decelerating from the 2.6% growth in Feb. This marks the lowest YoY growth since June 2023 (+2.1%). Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+3.1%), Saudi Arabia (+2.5%), and the United Arab Emirates (+2.8%).

NCR wage board consultations to start on May 22

The National Capital Region (NCR) Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) will begin its consultation process on May 22, first with the labor sector, then with employers on May 28, and a public hearing on June 18. The last minimum wage adjustment that took effect was last July 18, 2025, where a P50 (+8%) minimum wage hike was approved, which then increased the NCR daily minimum wage to the current P695. Relative to the past decade, the previous P50 wage hike is the highest percentage increase granted by the NCR regional wage board. The hike was estimated to directly benefit 1.2mn minimum wage workers as well as indirectly benefit 1.7mn full-time wage workers from correction of wage distortions.

Dept. of Agriculture eyes P53/kg SRP for local rice

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) is targeting to implement a suggested retail price (SRP) for locally grown rice at P53/kg in order to mitigate the impact of rising food crisis amid the ongoing oil crisis. This implies that the P53/kg SRP would be advisory only and sellers will not be legally obligated to comply. DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that the SRP may vary per region in order to control the impact on rice millers and local rice traders. Recall that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. recently signed Executive Order (EO) 118, which puts a cap on the price of 5% broken imported rice for an initial period of 30 days. Tiu Laurel added that the imported rice price cap "may be extended for another month or two". Rice prices are currently at P50.3/kg and P48/kg as of May for local and imported well-milled rice, rising from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25.

Dept. of Trade and Industry reviewing potential price increases for canned sardines and instant noodle products

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) Secretary Cristina Roque stated that it is currently reviewing proposed price adjustments for canned sardines and instant noodles before issuing an updated suggested retail price list. Roque elaborated that several manufacturers of such goods have petitioned for price increases of less than 10% of prewar prices. She added that manufacturers for other basic goods such as canned and processed meat, processed milk and bread have yet to propose plans for price adjustments. Recall that the DTI previously secured commitments from manufacturers to defer price increases until May 10.

Dept. of Transportation extends fuel support for public utility vehicles until December

The Dept. of Transportation (DOTr) stated that it will extend its Recalibrated Transport Support Package for public utility vehicle (PUV) drivers and operators till the end of this year. The program was initially slated to run for three months on a P1.5bn budget from April to July. 151,605 PUV units are expected to benefit from the fuel subsidy program. The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) said that funding has been secured to sustain UPLIFT (Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport) programs for the rest of the year. The gov't has launched a website which includes details on support programs and key statistics on food prices and the country's fuel supply.

Multiple US firms interested in Luzon Economic Corridor AI hub—US Pax Silica envoy

Jacob Helberg, in an interview during his visit to New Clark City, stated that more than a dozen US companies are in the Philippines and have expressed interest in investing in the Luzon Economic Corridor (LEC), under the Pax Silica initiative. Pax Silica is the US' program on artificial intelligence (AI) and supply chain security. The 4,000-acre hub will house manufacturers for chipsets and connectors while utilizing the country's nickel, copper, chromite, and cobalt reserves. An agreement for an embedded 400-MW solar farm is currently in the works in order to address concerns on the high cost of power. Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) President and CEO Joshua Bingcang stated that the project will be governed by Philippine laws—the Investors' Lease Act and the BCDA Law. Both Bingcang and Helberg dismissed previous reports that the hub would operate under US law or grant diplomatic immunity to investors. Bingcang clarified that it will be a regular business development contract, with no special treatment to be accorded to the US. Helberg stated that the first step is negotiating a long-term arrangement for the land, as well as determining the build-out as well as the industries and sectors that are to be prioritized. Technical assessments and engineering site surveys are scheduled to be conducted next month, with Helberg expecting that "work will definitely have commenced by next year".

Government infrastructure spending declines by 20% YoY in March; YTD infra spend down 26% YoY

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 26% YoY to P236bn in 3M26. Derived infrastructure spending in March stood at P108bn, rebounding 68% MoM but still down 20% YoY. The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that the YoY decline was due to the "ongoing completion of carry-over projects" as well as the stricter validation process for billing. The DBM expects infra spending to recover in 2Q26 as spending ramps up and construction picks up during summer. The government is targeting infra spend of ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) this year, broadly flat from last year’s P1.32tn (~4.7% of GDP).

Philippine gambling revenue falls 16% YoY

PAGCOR reported that Philippine GGR fell 16% YoY (+1% QoQ) to P87.6bn. The decline was led by e-games – GGR fell 22% YoY (down 1% QoQ) to P39.9bn. We believe the reduction in e-games GGR is largely due to the prohibition of e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms, which took place in mid-3Q25. We estimate that the e-games market share of PLUS rose to 42.8% in 1Q26, from 42.6% in 4Q25. GGR of licensed (land-based) casinos fell 10% YoY (up 2% QoQ) to P44.5bn.

Senate convenes as impeachment court for the trial of VP Duterte

The Senate has convened as an impeachment court for the trial of Vice President Sara Duterte following the House of Representatives' transmittal of the articles of impeachment. Note that this does not indicate the start of the trial as the initial period will be for the defense and the prosecution to submit their pleadings, with a pre-trial to be held for submission of evidence. The VP's defense team stated that they will comply with the Senate's request for a response to the articles of impeachment within the prescribed ten-calendar day window. Recall that the Senate had a change in leadership last May 11, where Duterte-allied senators were put under the majority bloc with Senator Alan Peter Cayetano as the new Senate President.

President Marcos appoints new budget chief

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has appointed Kim Robert de Leon as the new Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) Secretary given his expertise in "fiscal management, organizational reform, and digital transformation". De Leon graduated magna cum laude and valedictorian from University of the Philippines National College of Public Administration and Governance back in 2014. He served as an undersecretary first at the DBM, then at the Dept. of Transportation (DOTr) for administration and finance. He will be the youngest member of the Marcos Cabinet at 32 years old. Acting Budget Secretary Rolando Toledo will be assigned to the Board of Trustees of the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS). Toledo replaced Amenah Pangandaman, who resigned in the wake of the flood control scandal.

SEC imposes term limits on broker directors of exchanges

The SEC will impose a term limit for broker directors of exchanges. This should include the Philippine Stock Exchange. The maximum cumulative term limit is ten years. After serving a cumulative five years, a broker director would need to observe a one-year cooling off period. The SEC has provided a two-year transition period for incumbent broker directors, who may complete their current term and remain eligible for election in the next two annual elections. After this transition period, four of the current five broker directors would no longer be eligible. Ma. Vivian Yuchengco has been broker director for 28 years. Eddie Gobing has been broker director for 25 years. Wilson Sy has been broker director for 12 years. Diosdado Arroyo has served for 6 consecutive years. Anthony Te would be the only current broker director who would be eligible for another term. At least 51 percent of the 15-member board must be non-brokers-- five independent directors and four directors representing the interests of issuers, investors and other market participants, with each sector having at least one representative.
