REGIS REPORT

26 June 2026

PCOMP ended the week on a lower note, -1% WoW at 6072.24, but did fare much better than most markets in the region where countries with AI exposure led the decline.

Majority of local benchmark’s drop was due to index heavyweight ICT which capped the week -3.1%. The stock was quite volatile through most of the week, posting daily moves of at least 5% in either direction before settling down towards the end of the week. Headlines on the name were mixed – on one end, its port in Suape, Brazil, where it currently enjoys 100% market share, is set to face competition after APM Terminals announced the completion of a 400k TEU port. While on the other end, ICT’s Australia port managed to secure a 26-year extension to 2066.

Also contributing to the index’s decline was the weakness on banks after a sector outlook downgrade by Fitch which cited the slowing GDP growth outlook and rising credit costs. Worst laggard in the PCOMP was BDO -8.5% WoW, while BPI -2.7%.

Another conspicuous weak spot was SCC -5.1% and its parent DMC -6.6% as investors continue to await the official bidding for the coal mining contract in Semirara Island. Recall that the Department of Energy previously said that it aims to hold a pre-bid conference for the auction within the month. That said, the weakness was spread across the broader mining sector with Mining and Oil subindex posting a sharp -9.6% WoW slump amid the weakness in global metal prices. The worst performers include NIKL -16.2%, APX -14.7%, PX -9.4% and OGP -5.3%.

Outside the benchmark, water utilities also suffered a widespread selloff with MYNLD -11.3% and MWC -7.5% potentially due to fears over water supply shortage due to El Nino.

On the opposite end, ALI +5.8% and its parent AC +5.6% continued to outperform this week. Note that ALI is now +20% since marking a new low since 2010 two weeks ago. AC disclosed it acquired another 10m shares of ALI at Php14.14/share after acquiring 35m shares last week. There was also news of ALI’s premium condo sales reportedly posting a 30% growth as of 5M26 – although no concrete figures have been given so far.

Foreign investors reverted to selling this week with a net outflow of $35m, wiping out last week’s $26m inflow. The selling was spread out across a handful of names with the top sold ones being JFC +1.8%, BDO -8.5%, SM +0.3 and MER -2.5%. While on the opposite end, top bought still was ICT -3.1%, followed by GTCAP +11% which was the best performer in the index this week.

Philippine Banks - BSP allows no MTM loss on some FVOCI assets, for capital relief
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

New capital relief measure, but might be moot?

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) issued Memorandum 2026-027, which allows banks to not deduct unrealized mark-to-market losses on PHP denominated government securities (GS) on banks’ FVOCI books when computing CAR and CET-1 ratios, using 28 February as the pricing date. As with the previous measure on countercyclical buffer (CCyB), the aim is to maintain banks’ capacity to lend, protecting them from the temporary impacts from the Iran conflict. However, the recent peace negotiations may render this moot, as PGP GS yields have fallen back to end-1Q26 levels (Fig 1, at right), when the major banks’ CET-1 ratios were still well above the minimum levels (Fig 2, below). That said, these moves show that the BSP stands ready to provide appropriate regulatory support to the financial system when needed.

MTM relief for PHP-denominated GS

The salient points of the Memorandum are as follows:

  • The relief only covers PHP-denominated GS. Even USD-denominated Philippine bonds (ROPs) are not covered by the Memo.
  • Banks may choose to not deduct these MTM losses on their FVOCI books when computing for CAR and CET-1 ratios. Note that this is not mandatory; banks may continue reporting capital ratios as standard.
  • However, the full MTM losses must still be reflected on banks’ financial statements – balance sheet, other comprehensive income, equity, etc.
  • Banks that take the relief measures may be subject to more intense monitoring by the BSP, and may be limited in their ability to tap some of the BSP’s liquidity facilities.
  • The relief period ends on 1 Jan 2027, when all banks revert to standard reporting of CAR and CET-1 ratios.
 
Robinsons Land - RCR to grow mall portfolio via another injection from RLC
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

RCR announces mall acquisition  

RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) announced that it would acquire 160,000sqm of provincial malls from its sponsor, Robinsons Land. In turn, RLC will subscribe to 1.29bn new RCR shares, expanding shares outstanding by 7%. RLC will subscribe to the new shares at P8.25/share, a ~ 16% premium to RCR’s last closing price.

RCR will have even higher exposure to malls

RCR will acquire six provincial malls from RLC, which will expand RCR’s total floor area by 7% (c. 1.3mn sqm). After the acquisition, we estimate that provincial malls will comprise c. 50% of RCR’s portfolio (from 42% currently) while malls overall will account for c. 60% of floor area (from 53% currently). 

We consider the proportion of mall property to be a differentiating factor for RCR, as we have a more optimistic outlook on the mall business than the office segment, which is the main exposure of most Philippine REITs.  

Yield accretive for RCR

We expect the acquisition to be yield accretive for RCR. We estimate that the transaction would yield c. 7% at P8.25/share. However, at RCR’s current price of P7.09/share, we estimate that the assets would yield c. 8%, higher than RCR’s FY27E dividend yield of 6.7%. Thus, the dividend yield could rise to c. 7% after the property acquisition. 

After the transaction RLC’s ownership in RCR will rise from 55% to 58%.    

Fundamentally neutral for RLC but could be a positive catalyst

Although we expect the transaction to be fundamentally neutral for RLC, we also believe it could be a positive catalyst for the stock. RLC will only infuse buildings and will still receive land lease payments from RCR which, combined with lower taxes on REIT assets, would leave RLC’s EPS and NAV practically unchanged. 

However, the transaction could be positive for RLC’s share price. Once the transaction is completed, the value of RLC’s 12.2bn RCR shares would be P86bn (at the RCR closing price of P7.09/share), higher than RLC’s current market cap of P79bn. This highlights how undervalued RLC shares are, particularly since the company still has 1.1mn sqm of malls (and 300,000sqm of offices) that can be infused into RCR in the future.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

ICTSI’s Suape, Brazil port faces new competition as Maersk unit completes port project

APM Terminals announced it has completed the construction of a 400k TEU port in Suape, Brazil, paving the way for the start of commercial operations soon. New port will increase the container handling capacity of the Suape Port Complex by 57%. Note that ICTSI’s Tecon Suape (TSSA) port had a market share of 100% in the Suape Port Complex. Our estimates already assume TSSA’s revenues will decline by 30% in 2027, reflecting our expectation that APM Terminals’ port will begin operations only by then. We estimate TSSA accounts for 4%/3% of FY26E/FY27E volumes, 5%/3% of revenues, 7%/4% of EBITDA and 4% of NAV.

Ayala Land premium condominium sales grow nearly 30% in 5M26

Ayala Land said that its premium condominium sales grew 29% in 5M26 but did not cite specific figures. Sales were comprised of projects in Makati, BGC, Arca South, Parklinks, and Vertis north. Recall that in 1Q26, premium reservation sales fell 20% YoY to P16.6bn; and vertical reservation sales in particular fell 10% YoY to P14.2bb. This likely suggests a substantial YoY improvement in 2Q26 premium condominium sales.

Ayala Land in talks with BCDA to extend land lease of Market! Market! Mall

Ayala Land is in negotiations with BCDA for the extension of the land lease of Market! Market! Mall by another 10-15 years. The current lease is set to expire in 2027. We estimate that Market! Market! accounts for c. 5% of ALI’s mall revenue and the mall business comprises c. 25% of operating profit.

MGEN eyes 1Q27 start for second phase of MTerra Solar project

Meralco’s power generation unit, MGen, is eyeing to complete the second phase (1,000 MW) of the 3,500MW MTerra Solar project by 1Q27, following the completion of the first phase of 2,500MW. However, securing sufficient land remains a key requirement to achieve this target. Under its contract with Meralco, MTerra Solar has committed to provide 650MW of mid-merit capacity by Feb 2026 and the entire 850MW mid-merit capacity by Feb 2027. At full build, we estimate the project’s standalone annual profits could reach P15bn. Meralco effectively owns 34% of Terra Solar, through its 57% economic ownership of SPNEC PM (SPNEC owns 60% of Terra Solar).

Lance Gokongwei acquires stake in PhilWeb

Lance Gokongwei will subscribe to 159.5mn common shares and 93.8mn redeemable preferred shares of PhilWeb Corporation (WEB PM) at P8/share for a total subscription price of P2.03bn. Mr. Gokongwei’s subscription price is 45% lower than the last closing price (of P14.48/share). The shares will be created pursuant to the proposed increase in the company’s authorized capital stock from P2.6bn to P3.6bn, which is subject to shareholder approval. The 159.5mn common shares would represent 10% of the company’s common shares outstanding (upon issuance). Assuming full conversion of all the 93.8mn preferred shares, Mr. Gokongwei would own 15% of the company’s expanded common shares. PhilWeb is a B2B online gaming solutions provider which has agreements with Travellers, Okada, Hann Resorts, and NUSTAR (of the Gokongwei group).

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Gov't cuts 2026 GDP growth target to 3.5-4.5%; expects recovery in 2H26

Dept. of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that the GDP growth target for 2026 has been reduced to 3.5-4.5% from 5.0-6.0% previously given the impact of the Middle East conflict and gov't corruption crackdown. Recall that 1Q26 GDP growth was 2.8%, the slowest since 2009 excluding the pandemic. To reach the lower and upper ends of the FY26 growth target band, GDP growth needs to average 3.7% and 5.1%, respectively, for the rest of the year.

Balisacan added that he expects government spending to rebound in 2H26, noting that the gov't is seeing an improving fiscal outlook in the second half of this year. He remains optimistic that the Middle East crisis did not erode the country's growth narrative with household consumption and services expected to continue to anchor growth. He also added that the return to high growth can be done through mitigating external risks and inflationary pressures, prioritizing high-impact infra projects, as well as restoring public trust.

For 2026, consensus and Regis expect GDP to grow 4.0% and 3.8%, respectively.

BSP expects subdued lending growth this year

In an emailed statement to BusinessWorld, The BSP said that “Credit growth is projected to remain positive but at a more measured pace, reflecting tighter financial conditions and more selective lending.” This is due to higher borrowing costs and uncertainties surrounding the war in the Middle East. It also cited that banks have begun to tighten credit standards for sectors exposed to higher fuel costs, logistics pressures, and foreign exchange volatility. We forecast loan growth of 8%-13% for the banks that we cover. Banking sector loan growth hit 12% in April.

Government infrastructure spending drops 28% YoY in 4M26

Infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 28% YoY to P300bn in 4M26. This trails the government’s full-year target of P1.29tn, down 3% YoY and equivalent to 4.2% of GDP. Derived infra spend for the month of April alone fell 37% YoY and 40% MoM to P64bn. Officials cited slow completion and billings in the Department of Public Work’s projects.

Government posts P198bn budget deficit in May

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P198bn in May, 37% wider YoY but a reversal from the P31bn surplus in April, which brings the 5M26 deficit to P522bn, flat (-0.3%) vs the same period last year.

Revenue collections in May declined 7% YoY, a reversal from +3% YoY growth in April as the 12% growth in tax collections were offset by the 64% dip in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections accelerated to +15% from +0.4% the previous month but this huge swing can be attributed to the extension of the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return from April to 15 to May 15 following the declaration of the State of National Energy Emergency. Customs collections moderated to 6% growth from +16% the previous month amid the the temporary suspension of excise taxes on LPG and kerosene. Non-tax revenues plunged 64% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances as well as one-off receipts booked from the Bases and Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) (recall that dividends from GOCCs were remitted early this year in Feb.)

Meanwhile gov't spending was up 4% YoY, moderating from the 11% growth in April, as primary spending for the month slowed to 1% from 8% the month before, while interest payments rose 21% YoY.

BOP posts US$131mn surplus in May; GIR settles at US$104.0bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of May posted a surplus of US$131mn, marking a reversal from both the US$2.1bn deficit in the previous month of April and the US$298mn deficit in May 2025. This brings the 5M26 deficit to US$7.3bn, wider than the US$5.8bn deficit for the same period last year. For FY2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn.

Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-May stood at US$104.0bn, down 1% YoY and flat (-0.3%) MoM. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.7 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

US FCC proposes onshoring of call centers

The US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) is seeking comment on proposals that would encourage businesses to bring call center jobs back to the US

The FCC alleges that outsourcing:

o “…took jobs away from communities across the country…”

o Led to poor customer service

o Poses risks related to data protection and national security

The proposals include:

o Empowering consumer to transfer calls to a US based location

o Requiring calls that involve certain types of sensitive information be handled domestically

o Requiring covered providers to disclose the location of the call center

o Requiring call center workers to be proficient in American Standard English and to be trained appropriately for resolving issues with US customers

Philippine ambassador to Washington Jose Manuel Romualdez said the Philippine government is working with lawmakers and industry stakeholders to seek exemptions for the country. For context, contact center outsourcing generated US$33.9bn in revenue and employed 1.68mn Filipinos in 2025. About 70% of the revenue of the BPO sector is from North America. BPO firms occupy 70%-80% of the office space of the developers we cover.

Digital payments +41% in May - InstaPay surges past PesoNet in May

Payments over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) grew 41% YoY to P2,793bn ($48bn) in May. For the 5M26, the figure was P13,181bn, up 44% YoY. The monthly growth rate has decelerated over the last two months, from 45.6% YoY in April and from 50.1% in March.  For perspective, that’s already more than 100% of GDP…! And note further that this does NOT include payments made WITHIN a platform (eg Bank A to Bank A, and Wallet A to Wallet A).

Note too that transactions through InstaPay (the rail for real-time, smaller-ticket payments) overtook PesoNet (larger, batched transactions) for the first time in May.

InstaPay clocked payments worth P1,465bn for the month, up 57.4% YoY slightly slower than the 58.4% the previous month. For the first 5 months, InstaPay transactions hit P6,559bn, up 62% YoY. Meanwhile, PesoNet processed P1,327bn in May, up 26.4% YoY, decelerating sharply from the 34.9% clip in April. For 5M26, PesoNet was up 31% YoY to P6,622bn

The continued surge in smaller-ticket payments indicates sustained broadening of digital adoption across the economy.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 16% YoY in May; Toyota unit sales down 13%

CAMPI reported vehicle sales dropped 16% YoY to 33,532 units in May (+24% MoM). This brings YT-May industry unit sales down 12% YoY to 167k units. Note though that BYD’s sales are not captured by CAMPI’s data. Various news outlets reported that industry sales reached 35,200 units in May (+6% MoM) if estimates of BYD and Denza sales are included in the tally which suggests that BYD and Denza brands combined accounted for 5% of the market in May. It further suggests that on a month-on-month basis, derived sales of the BYD brand fell to 1.7k units in May from almost 6k units in April. BYD/Denza is the third best-selling car brand after Toyota (48.5%) and Mitsubishi (15.4%), besting other brands, such as Suzuki (4.1%), Ford (3.6%), and Isuzu (2.6%). Meanwhile, sales of xEVs (including BYD and Denza sales estimates) reached 7,695 units in May, accounting for 22% of car sales.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,076 units in May, down 13% YoY but up 20% MoM which brings 5M26 sales to 83,282 units, down 9% YoY. To reach our full-year forecast of 227k units (-1% YoY), sales for the rest of the year should average 20.5k units per month. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 22% of FY26E profit and 23% of NAV.

Dept. of Agriculture pushes to revive shift to 5% coconut-biodiesel fuel blend

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) is seeking to revive the mandated shift to 5% coco-biodiesel blend given "more favorable market conditions." DA Secretary Tiu Laurel endorses the shift, citing efforts to improve the country's energy security and lessen reliance on imported fuels. Recall that the transition was put on hold by the National Biofuels Board  (chaired by the Energy Secretary) after international coconut prices spiked in 2025.

The Biofuels Act of 2006 mandates that liquid fuels are to be blended with biofuels. The nationwide diesel biodiesel blend remains at B3 or 3% coconut methyl ester (CME), with the scheduled transition to B4 in October 2025 and B5 in October 2026 currently suspended.

D&L Industries' (DNL PM) Chemrez Group, the company's wholly owned subsidiary which produces petrochems and other products, contributed to 48% of DNL's net profit in FY25.
