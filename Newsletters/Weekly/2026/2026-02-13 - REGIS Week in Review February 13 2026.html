REGIS REPORT

13 February 2026

The Philippine benchmark finished the week modestly lower after another failed attempt at breaking past the 6,500 level, with investors hesitating near the psychological resistance ahead of fresher cues from the bulk of 4Q earnings releases in the coming weeks and as risk-off mood took over global markets later in the week. The PCOMP was lower by just 0.1% or 6.33 pts WoW to 6,384.58.

 

By mid-week, the index had managed to climb to a YTD-high just under 2 pts shy of 6,500, before profit-taking ensued and further selling from broad risk-off sentiment brought the index back to red territory by the end of the week. Local shares were not spared by the "AI Scare Trade" late in the week that spread from US markets into the rest of the globe, dragging sectors seen vulnerable to AI disruption.

 

Onshore, 4Q earnings took a pause with more reports due beginning next week, giving investors room to digest early releases from GLO (-0.9% WoW) and BPI (unch. WoW) that both held their briefings this week. Their peers notably posted drastically divergent results. Foreign flows determined the performance of the two other big banks, with BDO (-3.1% WoW) among top foreign sold names for the week and MBT (+4.2% WoW), in contrast, among the top foreign bought. Meanwhile, the other two telcos reversed their performance from the previous week, with TEL (-0.5% WoW) modestly pulling back and CNVRG (+7.4% WoW) rebounding from sharp losses.

 

Elsewhere, index heavyweight ICT (-0.5% WoW) remained most actively traded, seeing heavy two-way flows during the week but still ending as the largest net foreign bought name. ICT notched a fresh all-time-high this week at P689, although the late sell-off in global transport and logistics stocks erased its weekly gain.

 

Foreigners have been net buyers for seven weeks straight now, with net inflow of $22m for the week taking YTD net inflow to $267m. This has helped support the peso, which strengthened by 1.0% WoW to 58.023:$1 and is now up by 1.4% YTD.

RESEARCH
Maynilad Water - Value compounder
 
 
by Klyne Resullar +632 8894 6645 klyne.resullar@regis.ph
 

Initiating coverage with a Buy

We initiate coverage on Maynilad Water Services (MYNLD) with a BUY rating and a target price of P24/share, 22% from current levels. Elevated non-revenue water levels and low wastewater coverage indicate great operational challenges but also imply significant opportunities to invest. Under its regulatory framework, these investments allow MYNLD to grow its regulatory value at a compounded rate of 12%. With net gearing levels at only 60%, we believe it has room to raise dividends. Our target price implies 4% dividend yield and, more importantly a modest 1.5x to the regulatory value of the West Zone.

 

Focus on cash spending, lower funding costs

Maynilad’s West Zone concession operates under a regulatory framework that guarantees a fixed 12% pre-tax return on net cash expenditures, making investment outlays and cost of capital central to tariff hike prospects and valuation. An easing interest rate environment could translate into reduced funding costs, offering upside to our valuation.

Accounting profits are irrelevant, so are usual valuation multiples

Due to the nature of the West Zone’s regulatory framework, accounting profits have less bearing on its underlying economics than many realize. Consequently, conventional valuation metrics - such as P/E, EV/EBITDA, and P/B - offer little analytical relevance. We nonetheless acknowledge that accounting profits are relevant to the extent of predicting dividends, given management’s dividend policy. Our FY26E/FY27E dividend projections currently equate to a yield of 5%.

 

DCF-based TP offers 27% total potential return

Our target price of P24/sh is based on a DCF analysis of cash flows from 2026 to 2046, using a WACC of 7.0% and no terminal value. Our DCF values MYNLD at 1.5x over its regulatory value, or “Opening Cash Position” (OCP). At current levels, MYNLD trades at only 1.1x its OCP, a large discount to East Zone operator, Manila Water (MWC PM), which we estimate trades at over 5x its regulatory value. Key risks relate to regulatory, execution, dilutive acquisitions, and water supply issues.

 
BPI - 4Q PPOP surges 32% on consumer push, but credit costs at >90bps blunt gains
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph
 

FY25 NPAT P66.6bn, in line; as consumer/MSME loans drive PPOP +19%

BPI reported NPAT of P66.6bn for FY25, up 7% YoY, slightly behind Regis’s FY25E estimates. 4Q25 alone saw PPOP surge 32% YoY on strong revenues. However, this was dampened by credit costs, which jumped to >90bps. Loan growth sped up to 14.7% YoY, with “non-institutional” loans up 25.7% YoY, keeping NIMs and fees firm, but also pushing up provisions. Management has guided for loan growth of 11-13%, credit costs of 80-90bps, as well as a likely bump up in dividend payout ratio for 2026E.

 

Consumer/MSME focus continues to bear fruit…

BPI ended 2025 with loan growth of 14.7% YoY. The consumer/MSME book continued to expand 25.8%, compared to institutional loans at 10.4%, taking the share of consumer/MSME loans to 30%. NIMs dipped 4bps QoQ despite the improved loan mix as lower policy rates compressed corporate loan yields. PPOP grew 19% YoY in FY25, with solid growth from both NII (+16%) and fee incomes (+11%), and helped by moderating opex growth (+10%).

… but credit costs are jumping, due for recalibration

However, provisions surprised to the upside with credit cost of 98bps in 4Q25, taking the FY25 figure to c72bps versus our forecast of 67bps. Higher delinquencies came from younger borrowers, and those with lower incomes. Management said the bank is re-tightening underwriting standards, and is guiding for FY26E credit costs to normalize in the 80-90bps range.

 

Estimates trimmed, but P160/sh TP maintained; higher div payout due?

We adjust our FY25-27 forecasts down by 1-3%%, due largely to higher credit costs. While we believe the consumer/MSME push is the correct strategy, it also comes with growing pains. BPI stock currently trades on 1.22x P/bk on our 2026E. We retain our target price of P160/sh – derived using (ROE-g)/(COE-g) on long-term ROE of 14.5%, COE of 12% and growth of 8%. Management guided for slower loan growth (11-12%) in 2026E, and discussed the potential for higher dividend payout (vs 37% in 2025).

 
Globe Telecom - Robust core telecom ops in 4Q
 
 
by Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

Core operations see 4Q bounce

Globe reported core income (before preferred divs) of PHP20.9bn for FY25, down 3% YoY. After pref divs, we estimate core income would be P19.4bn (-4% YoY). Despite core income dipping slightly, we would describe these results as being better than forecast. 4Q revenue was surprisingly robust, led by a bounce (+6% YoY) on the mobile segment. Service revenue for the year ended up being flat YoY despite being down 2% for 9M25.

 

Mobile revenue bounces; decline in churn lifts net subscriber adds

Mobile revenue grew 6% YoY (+5% QoQ) in 4Q and allowed Globe’s mobile revenue to be practically flat YoY at P117bn. Net adds for 4Q (+2.6m) were particularly high because of unusually low churn (disconnections), which management credited to churn management initiatives using hyper-personalized offers.

Broadband net adds >100k for fourth straight quarter

Globe added 107k subscribers (net) to its wired broadband service in 4Q, marking the 4th straight quarter of over 100k net adds after it chose to offer a prepaid service. There appears to be a subscriber shift as prepaid fiber subs advances even as postpaid subs dip. Globe now has 1.8m wired broadband subscribers. Overall, revenue for the broadband segment was up 4% YoY for the quarter.

 

Associate contributions drop in 4Q. Profit contributions from associates amounted to P6.8bn (up ~60% YoY) for the year, implying ~P1.1bn for 4Q alone. While this is up more than three-fold from 4Q24, it is down from the P1.63bn in 3Q25.

 

Capex down to 28% of revs; guidance of low to mid-single digit rev growth

The company spent P46bn (US$780m) for capex in FY25, down by 18% from a year earlier and equivalent to 28% of service revenue. For FY26, management is guiding for capex to stay “below US$1bn” and for revenue growth to be in the low to mid-single digits.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

Meralco's energy sales dip in January

Meralco’s consolidated energy sales reached 4,048 GWH in January, declining by 0.3% YoY. Cited lingering effects of a cooler weather pattern. According to Meralco senior VP and chief revenue officer Ferdinand Geluz. The company continues to see energy sales rebound in 2026, after declining 0.6% YoY in FY25. FY25 energy sales dipped, with residential sales (~35% of total) down 2% YoY, commercial sales (~38%) dipping 0.4% YoY and industrial sales (~27%) rising by 1% YoY. 2026 sales rebound would depend on an economic rebound and normalized weather conditions.

Meralco seeks over 70% increase in distribution tariffs to P2.34/kwh

Meralco is seeking approval for an average distribution tariff of P2.34/kwh, above its current tariff of P1.35/kwh. Tarif is based on capex of P272bn over the next four years. The planned capital expenditure includes P51.8bn for 2027, P61.2bn for 2028, P61.1bn for 2029, P73.1bn for 2030, and more than P20bn for carry-over projects. Distribution accounts for ~60% of MER’s profits.

ICTSI's Mexico port volumes rise 4% in January

Container volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), rose by 4% YoY to 143k TEUs in January. Combined volumes at the port of Manzanillo rose 8% YoY. We’ve modeled a 13% YoY increase in CMSA’s FY26 volumes to 1.75mn TEUs, given the port’s ~20% capacity expansion. To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year should average 146k TEUs per month. We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA.

Globe raises P25bn from pref share sale

Globe issued P25bn worth of non-convertible, non-voting perpetual preferred shares. Series “A” prefs’ dividend rate at 6.1179%. Series “B” prefs’ dividend rate at 6.7631%. Note that the company has US$600mn senior perpetual capital securities (4.2% distribution rate) that is callable on August 2, 2026.

Cebu Air deliberates on first common share dividend in 7 years

Cebu Air (CEB PM) CEO Michael Szucs in an interview to InsiderPH stated that a common share cash dividend is "very much on the cards and in discussion at the moment" given ample cash liquidity. Szucs said that the company's current options are either to pay down debt or pay dividends. CEB last paid out dividends to common back in July 2019, the company resumed payouts to prefs last Oct 2025. As of 9M25, CEB's retained earnings as well as cash and cash equivalents amounted to P12bn and P15.8bn, respectively.

Aboitiz-led consortium taking over CBK hydro complex

Thunder Consortium has taken over operations of the 797MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydro complex from state-run PSALM. Thunder Consortium comprises Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (64% stake), Sumitomo Corp., and Electric Power Development Corp. Consortium offered P36bn for the facility. As of end-September 2025, AP’s consolidated net debt stood at P237bn and net debt-to-equity stood at 1.08x.

WLCON targets 8 new store openings this year

Wilcon Depot Inc. (WLCON) eyes to open eight new stores this year—with three already opened last Jan, five more stores are set to open in the remainder of the year. WLCON opened six new stores last year bringing the total store count to 106 by end-2025. The eight new stores slated to open this year will be a mix of traditional depots and smaller formats. COO Rosemarie Bosch-Ong guided that the company saw improvement in financial performance towards the end of 2025. As of 9M25, WLCON's net sales rose 3% to P26.3bn while net profit fell 12% to P1.9bn.

Fort Bonifacio property to be redeveloped

The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) will open up a 10-hectare property in Fort Bonifacio for redevelopment. The property includes the land on which Ayala Land’s Market! Market! mall is located. Ayala Land’s lease with the BCDA is set to expire in 2027. Market! Market! comprises less than 1% of our ALI NAV estimate. BCDA said that in addition to Ayala Land, some Japanese developers have expressed interest. An agreement with the Department of Transportation has been signed to provide access for right-of-way for three subway stations of the Metro Manila subway project.

Monte Sur to conduct tender offer for Dominion Holdings

Monte Sur Equity Holdings will conduct a tender for 11.4% of Dominion Holdings (DHI PM) at P1.68/share-P1.69/share. The tender offer price is 60% below the last closing price of P4.20/share. Tender offer period – Feb 10 to March 11. Monte Sur recently acquired a 70% stake in Dominion Holdings from BDO at the P1.68/share. BDO continues to hold an 18.5% stake in DHI.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

Government debt rises to P17.71trn in end-Dec

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.3% MoM and 10% YoY to P17.71trn as of end-Dec. The increase was driven by the net issuance of debt and the depreciation of the peso. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.4% and 31.6% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 0.3% MoM to P12.12trn. External debt rose by 1.1% MoM to P5.59trn. The P17.71trn year-end figure exceeded the P17.36-trn end-2025 projection by 2%. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of end-2025 rose slightly to 63.2% from 63.1% as of 3Q25 and is also higher vs 60.7% back in end-2024. The 63.2% debt-to-GDP figure is above the 61.3% projected 2025 target by the gov't and is the highest since the 65.7% recorded back in 2005.

PH dollar reserves at $112.5bn end-Jan

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Dec increased 1.5% MoM to US$112.5bn from US$110.9bn the previous month. The increase primarily reflected the surge in gold prices alongside a significant increase in foreign exchange holdings. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 4.1x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

Marcos pushes for Congress to pass 21 priority bills before fifth SONA

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. during the 3rd Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting ordered Congress to expedite the passage of 21 priority bills as soon as possible to "uplift the living conditions" of Filipinos. He stated that he targets the passage of the priority bills by June, in time for his fifth State of the Nation Address SONA). Some of the 21 priority bills are as follows:. Anti-corruption/gov't transparency related bills such as Anti-political Dynasty Act, Party-list System Reform Act, the creation of the Independent People's Commission Act, amendments to the Bank Deposits Secrecy Law, and Citizen Access and Disclosure of Expenditures for National Accountability (CADENA). Infra and utilities bills like the creation of the Department of Water Resources Bill, Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA), Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) Strengthening Bill. Other bills such as the abolition of the travel tax, amendments to the 4Ps conditional cash transfer social welfare program, amendments to the Rice Tariffication Law. Aside from the 21 priority bills endorsed by President Marcos, 31 priority bills are still pending in the 20th Congress—bringing the total to 52 priority measures. 12 of the 52 LEDAC measures have already passed third and final reading which include:. EPIRA, the proposed Dept. of Water Resources, Bank Deposits Secrecy Law amendments, etc. Other pending bills that are in earlier stages of passage include online gambling regulation, digital payments, etc.

BSP sees ongoing but slower-than-expected business confidence rebound

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona noted that business confidence has started to show signs of a rebound. He noted, however, that the recovery in sentiment is "not as fast we would like, but it's coming back". Remolona cited the following as indicators that sentiment may be improving:. The Philippines’ manufacturing PMI rising to a nine-month high of 52.9 in Jan. The easing of 10-year government bond yields to below 6%. The PSEi’s recovery from sub-6,000 levels in Nov 2025. Remolona also noted that the potential revision to their 2026 GDP growth forecast of 5.4% is still under review. The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2026/27 are 5.4/6.0%. For 2026/27, consensus and REGIS expectations for GDP growth are at 5.2/6.0% and 5.5/5.7%, respectively. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

Commercial banks' loan growth slows to 9.2% in Dec

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.2% in Dec from 10.3% in Nov, breaking a 19-month streak of double-digit growth and marking the slowest pace since Feb 2024 (8.6%). Loans to productive activities decelerated to 8.0% vs 9.0% in Nov. Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 21.4%, though slower than Nov's 22.9% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) moderated to 7.0% in Dec from 7.6% in Nov.

BSP tightens regulations on rural banks with online platforms

The BSP issued a draft circular requiring rural banks using online platforms to maintain certain thresholds. Accounts of customers whose registered addresses are outside the bank’s physical areas of operations are to be capped at 30 percent of total customer accounts. The BSP “reserves the right to deploy appropriate enforcement actions” against rural banks whose out-of-area customer accounts exceed 30 percent. The bank may be required to comply with regulations applicable to a digital bank, including meeting a minimum capital of P1 billion within one year and maintaining a supervisory assessment framework rating of “3” or higher. The BSP is accepting feedback on the draft circular until February 25.

BSP lowers reserve requirements on bank bonds and other instruments

The BSP reduced the reserve ratio on bonds issued by universal, commercial and digital banks to 2 percent from 3 percent. It also removed the 4 percent reserve ratio on long-term negotiable certificates of time deposits. Reserve requirements on trust and other fiduciary accounts were also lowered to zero. Previous requirements:. Universal and commercial banks – 17%. Thrift banks – 9%. Rural banks – 4%. The BSP also removed the 6 percent reserve requirement on bonds, mortgage bonds and chattel mortgage certificates issued by thrift banks. “Based on BSP’s computation, the excess liquidity that will be released based on these reductions in the required reserves is not expected to pose significant impact on monetary aggregates and economic indicators,”.

Kaliwa Dam on track for completion by 2028

The Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) stated that the P15.3bn Kaliwa Dam project is set to be completed by 2028. As of end-2025, the project has accomplished a progress rate of 26%. The dam is designed to deliver an initial capacity of 600mn liters per day which will alleviate reliance on the Angat Dam, which supplies 90% of Metro Manila's water needs. Capex for the conveyance/transmission system of Kaliwa dam are already included in the approved business plans of Maynilad (MYNLD PM) and Manila Water (MWC PM).

PAGCOR tightens regulations on online gambling

In a Senate hearing, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the regulator has tightened know-your-customer (KYC) protocols and advertising regulations on online gambling. PAGCOR Electronic Gaming Licensing Department officer in charge Jessa Mariz Fernandez said the updated KYC process requires a valid government ID and a real-time selfie before the initial deposit can be made. TV and radio ads during primetime are no longer allowed. PAGCOR might expand the ban to the entire day. Other implemented measures include online self-exclusion tools on gaming platforms, accreditation of treatment and rehabilitation centers for problem gamblers, stricter KYC enforcement, and the removal of gambling-related billboards and other outdoor advertisements.

Marcos establishes new economic affairs body

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., through the signing of Executive Order (EO) No. 108, transferred the functions of the Office of the Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (OSAPIEA) to the new established Office of Economic Affairs (OEA) in order to streamline the administration's investment and economic initiatives. OEA, the new investment and economic policy body, will be headed by Executive Secretary Ralph Recto. Recall that the OSAPIEA was previously under Finance Secretary Frederick Go prior to his appointment to the Dept. of Finance (DOF).
