REGIS REPORT

03 July 2026

Philippine stocks began the second half on a positive note, joining broadly green global equity markets with the PCOMP recovering last week's losses to finish higher by 1.9% or 115.79 pts WoW to 6,188.03. Despite mixed indications on progress of peace talks between the US and Iran, investors took a more positive stance with oil prices continuing to ease while the dollar came off peak levels.

 

Onshore, Mynt's IPO filing took center stage, boosting shares of parent GLO (+4.7% WoW) which owns a third of the company and derives a quarter of its profits from the fintech business. The offering is expected to raise up to P92.3bn ($1.5bn) which will be the largest IPO in Philippine history. Riding the value-unlock theme, TEL (3.8% WoW) shares followed higher -- potentially a delayed reaction to the IPO filing of its data center unit, VITRO, which was announced last week. Both VITRO and Mynt are eyeing a 4Q listing.

 

Elsewhere, investors were broadly bargain-hunting, lifting shares of AC (+13.9% WoW) and JFC (+10.9% WoW) which were among the largest underperformers in 2Q but had come out on top for the week.

 

On domestic macro, the National Capital Region (NCR) wage board approved an P85 daily minimum wage hike in Metro Manila, increasing the daily minimum wage to P780/day. This translates to a 12% wage hike, which is the largest percentage increase in the last 20 years. Other than JFC, CNPF (+2.7% WoW), PGOLD (+2.6% WoW), and WLCON (+2.7% WoW) advanced within a mixed consumer sector as investors weighed the potential uplift to consumer demand against higher labor costs to be incurred by these listed companies.

 

Banks also outperformed, led by BPI (+3.6% WoW) and BDO (+2.1% WoW). In-step with recent BSP circulars aimed at reducing costs for digital transactions and payments, BPI announced this week that it was slashing interbank fund transfer fees to zero, and likewise waiving deposit and withdrawal fees at its agency banking partners.

 

Foreign selling continued to slow, with net outflow of $3m for the week, although trading activity also notably softened to $85m ADTO. The peso weakened by 0.2% WoW to 61.426:$1, while the PH10Y yield inched higher by 22 bps to 7.1482%.

Philippine Strategy - 12% wage hike in Metro Manila; good for consumers, but who pays?
 
 
by Rafael Garchitorena +632 8894 6644 rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph / Gio dela Rosa, CFA +632 8894 6642 gio.delarosa@regis.ph
 

NCR wage board grants staggered P85/day (+12.2%) wage hike

The Regional Tripartite Wage Board (RTWB) of the National Capital Region (NCR, or Metro Manila) granted an P85/day increase in the daily minimum wage, or 12.2% from the P695/day base today. Of the hike, P60 will be implemented in July, with the P25 effective January 2027. The hike is significantly higher than inflation, which should boost consumption, particularly for lower-income households. However, it could produce second-round inflation, as companies will likely pass on the cost increases to higher prices of goods and services.

 

Consumption boost, as hike beats inflation (again)

The NCR wage hike took us by surprise, as it will be the largest increase since 1999 (12.9%). It is also significantly higher than the 6.5% average inflation forecast of the government for FY26E (Fig 1). This will be the 5th consecutive year of higher wage hikes relative to inflation. Note, too, that the RTWBs of provinces have in recent years granted wage hikes higher than NCR. Lower-income households are likely to benefit the most from the consumption boost.

Who pays? Labor costs to jump, likely passed through to further price hikes

In 2025, we estimate that manpower expenses accounted for an average of c70% of pre-tax profit for companies we cover (Fig 2), with some over 100%. To be fair, overall salaries are not likely to jump the full 12%, since not all employees are on minimum wage. But there will definitely be upward pressure at least for salaries of rank-and-file staff. Companies are likely to pass these on to the consumers, which will in turn lead to second-round inflationary price moves.

 

Short-term hit, but long-term positive for consumer sector

Consumer companies have among the highest ratio of labor cost to profit and could see a short-term hit. We believe, however, that some names are still likely to come out as net winners over time, especially those catering to lower-income households such as Century Pacific (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), Puregold (PGOLD PM) and Universal Robina (URC PM). Within each sector, there are companies less labor-dependent could reap some competitive advantages.

 
Bloomberry Resorts - A series of unfortunate events; downgrading to Hold
 
 
by Carl Sy, CFA +632 8894 6646 carl.sy@regis.ph
 

When it rains, it pours

Bloomberry’s operations have deteriorated substantially due to the POGO ban, the decline in Korean and Chinese tourists, and, we suspect, the recent government corruption scandal, which led to slower GDP growth. Looking ahead, operations are likely to remain under pressure from the impact of the conflict in the Middle East as well as greater competition in PAGCOR Entertainment City. Thus, we forecast net losses until FY28E, reduce our target price (by 63%) to P1.65/share, and downgrade BLOOM to a Hold.

 

Twin challenges – supply and demand

After recording a recurring net loss of P5.5bn in 2025, Bloomberry is set to face challenges on both demand and supply. Demand from both local and foreign players is likely to remain weak as the conflict in the Middle East has increased the cost of travel and raised Philippine inflation. Meanwhile, Alliance Global (AGI PM) will open a new resort which will add c. 20% to gaming table and slot capacity in Entertainment City (in 2H26).

Furthermore, Solaire Entertainment City’s (SEC) mass table win rate was unusually high in FY25/1Q26. A reversion to more statistically normal levels could mean a significant reduction in revenue.

 

Revising estimates; expecting net losses until FY28E

We revise our estimates in light of the operational challenges facing the company. As a result, we lower our FY26E/FY27E EBITDA estimates by 30%-40% and forecast net losses until FY28E.

 

Lowering target price; downgrading to Hold

We reduce our target price by 63% to P1.65/share. Our new target price is based on 7.5X FY26E EV/EBITDA, equivalent to the median of Macau gaming firms but lower than BLOOM’s pre-COVID average of 11X.

 

Despite our forecasts of net losses until FY28E, BLOOM is trading at 7.6X FY26E EV/EBITDA, similar to the Macau median. The stock also trades close to our target price. Thus, we downgrade the BLOOM to a Hold.

 
CORPORATE NEWS
 
 
 
 

BPI removes InstaPay/PESONet transfer fees; expands branchless banking services

Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI) announced that effective July 1, it has waived all InstaPay and PESONet interbank transfer fees across its BPI app, BPI Online, VYBE, BanKo, and BizKo platforms, benefiting around 9.5mn app users. The move follows the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) recent circular directing banks to align interbank transfer fees more closely with intra-bank transfer charges as part of efforts to lower digital payment costs. Separately, BPI also expanded its branchless banking services by allowing customers to deposit and withdraw cash for free through cashiers at over 1,300 partner supermarkets, pharmacies, pawnshops, department stores, and convenience stores using a barcode generated via the BPI app. The bank also partnered with more than 7,000 retail outlets nationwide where customers can open bank accounts and apply for credit cards through trained cashiers. According to BPI, the initiative aims to decongest physical branches, improve financial inclusion, and extend banking access beyond traditional banking hours. Finance Secretary Frederick Go welcomed both initiatives, expressing hope that more banks will follow in reducing fees and expanding access to digital financial services.

CNPF reaffirms capex spend and double-digit growth targets for 2026

Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM) in its annual stockholders' meeting stated that the company is maintaining its P8-9bn capex program as well as its double-digit growth targets for both revenue and profit this year. CNPF President and CEO Teodoro Alexander T. Po stated that the capex spend for expanded capacity for all its segments across the board is "necessary and required" with a significant portion allocated to expand OEM production capacity with results expected to show beginning next year. Po also emphasized that despite 2Q being "quite challenging", the company is still seeing double digit growth on both the branded and OEM business segments. He expects 2H26 to be similar or slightly better than 1H26 driven by increased brand investments and alleviating geopolitical pressures. CNPF in 1Q26 saw consolidated revenue and net profit grow 15% YoY and 10% YoY, respectively. The branded and OEM business posted YoY growth of 11% and 32%.

Meralco projects earnings growth this year

Meralco expects its 2026 profits to exceed last year’s record core profit of P50.6bn (+12%). According to Meralco Chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan. Cited that growth will be driven by its power generation and RES units, as well as higher energy sales volumes from its distribution unit. Mr. Pangilinan however declined to set a formal 2026 earnings target unit 1H26 results are released. Recall that Meralco’s 1Q26 profit rose by 2% to P11.4bn.

SEC approves ALI-AREIT share-for-property swap

The SEC approved a share-for-property swap between AREIT and Ayala Land. This transaction was first announced in 4Q25. AREIT will acquire P19.5bn worth of malls from Ayala Land in exchange for 441mn new AREIT shares. This will increase AREIT’s AUM by 15% to P158bn. AREIT’s shares outstanding will increase by 12% to 4.2bn shares. This implies that the AREIT shares will be priced at P44.15/share. This is 19% higher than the last closing price of P37.20/share. AREIT will acquire two malls with a combined GLA of 375,000sqm. Ayala Center Cebu and Ayala Malls Feliz.

 
MACRO NEWS
 
 
 
 
 

P85 minimum wage hike approved by NCR wage board

The National Capital Region (NCR) Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved the P85 (+12%) minimum wage hike, increasing the current NCR minimum wage to P780 which will be implemented in two tranches. +P60 effective July 19, 2026, and the remaining +P25 effective January 20, 2027. The P85 wage increase is higher than the P50 hike last year and is the highest percentage increase granted by the NCR regional wage board in the past two decades. According to the Dept. of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the hike is estimated to directly benefit 1.1mn minimum wage workers and may indirectly benefit 1.9mn workers earnings above the minimum wage from the correction of wage distortions.

Tariff Commission starts rice safeguard duty probe

The Tariff Commission (TC) has initiated a formal investigation to determine whether to impose a definitive safeguard measure on imported rice. This action follows after Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel signed Department Order (DO) No. 18 last month. DO 18 revealed that the agency found causal link between increased rice imports and serious injury to the domestic industry.

BSP expects June inflation to settle within 6.0-7.0%

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects June inflation to fall within 6.0-7.0%, likely accelerating further from the 6.8% print in May. BSP expectations for May inflation are well above the BSP's 2-4% long-term target range. For 2026, BSP expectations for inflation this year is 6.4% (previously revised from 6.3%). June inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on July 7 (Tuesday). Upward price pressures stem from higher prices of electricity and vegetables. These may have been partially offset by lower prices of domestic petroleum, rice, and meat. Inflation has averaged 4.5% so far as of 5M26. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 27 (fourth out of six scheduled this year).

PH gov't revises GDP growth, inflation, and fiscal program targets

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) has adjusted the gov't's various macroeconomic assumptions following the impact of domestic and geopolitical developments. The gov't's GDP growth targets for 26/27/28E were reduced to 3.5-4.5%/5.0-6.0%/5.0-6.0% from 5.0-6.0%/5.5%-6.5%/6.0-7.0%. 1Q26 GDP growth was 2.8%, the slowest since 2009 excluding the pandemic. To reach the lower and upper ends of the FY26 growth target band, GDP growth needs to average 3.7% and 5.1%, respectively, for the rest of the year. Inflation expectations for 26/27E were revised upwards to average 6-7%/4-5%, respectively, before returning to target to 2-4% from 2028-20230. The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66tn (-5.4% of GDP) from P1.61tn (-5.3% of GDP) previously. For 2027, the deficit is expected to widen further to P1.69tn (-5.1% of GDP) from P1.59tn (-4.8% of GDP). 2028-2030 fiscal deficits are expected to be -4.8%, -4.2%, and -3.5% of GDP. Revenue projections for 26/27/28E were adjusted to P4.81tn/5.21tn/P5.52tn from P4.82/P5.12tn/5.57tn. The disbursements for 26/27/28E were revised higher to P6.47tn/P6.90tn/P7.25tn from P6.43tn/P6.71tn/P7.11tn. The 2027 proposed budget is P7.2tn or 21.7% of GDP, +6% YoY vs 2026's P6.79tn budget.

Trade deficit widens 51% YoY to $5.5bn in May

The May trade deficit widened to US$5.5bn (+51% YoY) as imports growth continued to outpace exports growth. This brings the 5M26 trade deficit to US$25.2bn, 26% wider YoY. Imports for the month climbed 22% YoY to US$13.4bn, steady from the 22% increase in Apr. The increase in imports was supported by strong growth across the categories such as raw and intermediate goods (+33%), capital goods (+23%), and fuel (+36%), which offset the 5% decline in consumer goods. Exports growth picked up to 8% YoY (US$7.9bn) from 6% the previous month. This marks the seventeenth consecutive month of YoY growth in exports. Growth in electronics exports rebounded to +12% from +1% in Apr. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up +3%, moderating from +11% the previous month.

Government debt inches up to P18.55tn in end-May

The government's total debt stock went down by 0.4% MoM (P76.1bn) to P18.55tn as of end-May. This was due to the net incurrence of domestic securities as the gov't continued to raise funds to support financing needs. Domestic debt increased by 0.65% MoM (P80.1bn) to P12.50tn due to the impact of the net issuance of domestic securities (P80.2bn). External debt was flat MoM (-0.07%) at P6.05tn as the favorable currency movements offset new external debt. The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2% as of 1Q26 from 63.2% end-2025. The 65.2% ratio is the highest since hitting 65.7% back in 2005. For 2026, the total debt stock is projected to reach P19.06tn by year-end.

BSP adjusts BOP forecasts for 2026-2027

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its quarterly review, slightly adjusted its forecasts for the balance of payments (BOP) position of the country in 2026-2027, still projecting deficits in both the BOP position and current account (CA). The assumed deficit is hinged on higher import costs and weaker capital flows due to elevated geopolitical tensions. For 26/27E, BSP projections (as % of GDP) for the overall BOP position was revised to a wider deficit of -2.1%/-2.1% from -1.5%/1.6%, respectively. The wider BOP deficit is driven on expectations of lower financial account inflows and remittances. The CA deficit for 26/27E is forecasted to still projected to widen relative to 2025 (-3.3% of GDP) but less than expected to -3.6%/-3.7% from -4.0%/-4.0% previously. The narrower projected widening in the CA deficit reflects slower import growth due to subdued domestic demand alongside expected softness in services exports. The GIR for 26/27E is expected to be lower to US$104bn/US$105bn from US$111bn/US$112bn previously. The increase in GIR was likely prompted by the depreciation of the peso and moderation in gold prices.

Monetary Board member sees the Philippines at risk of losing investment-grade ratings

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board member Benjamin Diokno in an interview stated that the country is at risk of losing its investment-grade sovereign credit ratings given the threats of the oil crisis and a prolonged flood-control corruption scandal. Diokno warned that the corruption scandal may be more damaging than external shocks given the potential loss of confidence and credibility as well as a stoppage in infra spending. Recall that Fitch Ratings recently affirmed the country's BBB rating but downgraded the outlook to 'negative' from 'stable'. S&P retained its BBB+ rating for the Philippines but cut its outlook to “stable” from “positive”. Moody's retained its "Baa2 stable" investment-grade rating for the Philippines. BSP Governor Eli Remolona, however, previously said in a separate interview that the negative outlook does not automatically lead to a rating downgrade. Remolona added that securing an A credit rating is still possible despite Fitch's outlook downgrade as the revision was "not due to domestic weaknesses" but due to the external environment.

Metro Manila likely to see new economic zones

Finance Secretary Frederick Go and Trade Secretary Cristina Roque endorsed the lifting of Administrative Order (AO) 18. President Marcos would need to sign a new AO to supersede AO 18, which was signed by President Duterte in 2019. AO 18 prohibits the approval of new economic zones in Metro Manila. If a registered enterprise operates within an economic zone, it can avail of incentives from the government. Within Metro Manila, the registered enterprises that avail of incentives are predominantly BPO firms. We do not expect the lifting of the moratorium to have any significant impact near term, since there are still sufficient PEZA-registered buildings.

Over 25 US execs and investors to visit PH in July — DTI

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that at least 25 American business executives and investors will visit the country this July for a five-day business trip to explore opportunities in tourism, business, and investment. From July 6-10, the delegates will visit Manila, Clark, Subic, and Corregidor. Recall that New Clark City is the designated location for the United States planned Pax Silica hub, a 1,619-hectare industrial complex focused on AI-related advanced manufacturing.

Consumer sentiment and outlook in 2Q26 declines amid rising inflation — BSP survey

The latest Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) results exhibited that current sentiment for the overall consumer confidence index (CI) was more pessimistic deteriorating to -42.0% in 2Q26 from -15.8% the previous quarter. A negative CI implies more respondents are pessimistic than optimistic about the country's economic condition, family financial situation and income. The survey was conducted from April 6-18 with 5,503 households surveyed nationwide. The more pessimistic sentiment in 2Q stemmed from concerns over higher inflation, corruption in the gov't, and insufficient gov't policies and programs to mitigate rising prices. -42.0% is the lowest since 4Q20 (-47.9%). Households indicated an inclination to prioritize essential goods while cutting back on discretionary spending. For the next quarter, consumer sentiment deteriorated to -16.3% from 1.8% the previous quarter. For the upcoming 12 months, consumers were less optimistic on economic prospects improving, with a neutral CI at 0.2%, down from 9.6% the previous quarter. Consumers expect higher unemployment and a weaker Philippine peso. The CES results are taken into account in the BSP's decision-making for monetary policy. Inflation has averaged 4.5% so far as of 5M26. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 27 (fourth out of six scheduled this year).

BSP amends e-payments framework

The BSP issued Circular No. 1238, which states that fees for person-to-person e-payments across banks, e-wallet operators, and other payment service providers (PSPs) should not be materially different from fees within the same institution. Since transfers within one bank or e-wallet are often free, any difference in pricing should mainly reflect fees paid to the network switch operator. These institutions must also have an analysis of their costs for delivering electronic payment products and services. The BSP may require this in the conduct of its oversight activities.

BSP sets regulations for digital financial marketplaces

The BSP issued Circular No. 1237, which covers digital marketplace activities by BSP Supervised Financial Institutions (BSFI). A digital financial marketplace must include at least three providers that are not affiliated with or part of the BSFI’s group or conglomerate. To ensure neutrality and impartiality, the marketplace operator shall not favor affiliate or related companies in the presentation, comparison, recommendation, or access to products and services in the digital financial marketplace. Gambling “or any activities that are not aligned with the promotion of financial soundness or well-being of customers, shall not be displayed or presented in the marketplace operated by a BSFI.” To qualify as an operator, a bank or electronic money issuer must have a net worth or combined capital of at least P1bn.

BSP to implement consumer protection model beginning 2027

The BSP issued a draft memorandum which would guide all financial consumer protection supervisory assessments starting Jan. 1, 2027. The Financial Consumer Protection Risk-and-Impact Supervisory Model will link a BSP-supervised institution’s potential consumer impact and financial consumer protection risk profile to the level of supervisory engagement it will receive. The framework has three major components: consumer impact, consumer protection risk profile and level of supervisory engagement. The framework will initially cover banks and non-bank electronic money issuers.

ERC lowers open access threshold to 100kw

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) lowered the threshold to qualify under open access (RCOA) and the Retail Aggregation Program (RAP) from an average monthly peak demand of 500kW to 100kW. New rule took effect on June 26, 2026. The reduction will allow more consumers to choose their own electricity suppliers. The ERC said that 77 contestable customers have been scheduled for their RCOA switch, including 58 customers in Luzon. 40 retail aggregated groups will be covered by RAP, of which 11 are in Luzon.

DOE to issue show-cause orders to 175 power firms over plant outages

Energy Secretary Sharon S. Garin said the department will issue show-cause orders (SCOs) to 164 generation companies operating in on-grid areas and 11 in off-grid areas. SCOs will be issued over alleged prolonged unplanned outages and other violations that have strained the country’s power supply. The agency cited that some power firms failed to meet the minimum fuel reserve requirement. In Luzon alone, about 60-80 generation companies will receive SCOs while 20/40 firms will be issued SCOs in Visayas/Mindanao. However, Secretary Garin declined to identify the companies to avoid “complications”.

ERC extends suspension of GEA-ALL collection for July-August 2026

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has approved the extension of the temporary suspension of the collection of the Green Energy Auction Allowance (GEA-All) for the July and August 2026 billing months. Ruling covers the GEA-All rate of PhP 0.0371 per kilowatt-hour (kWh) (~0.3% of MER’s generation rate), as the GEA-All Fund balance of P707mn remains financially sufficient to support expected disbursements. GEA-All is a uniform charge collected from consumers to fund payments to participants of the government’s Green Energy Auction Program.
