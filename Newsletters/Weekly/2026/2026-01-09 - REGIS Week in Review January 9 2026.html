REGIS REPORT

09 January 2026

Philippine equities saw a strong start to 2026, with the PCOMP delivering a 3.5% or 213.08-pt WoW advance to 6,348.14 in the first full week of the year. This takes the Philippine benchmark's YTD gain to 4.9%, ahead of Asean peers after being a big underperformer last year.

 

This week's market action was marked by the index breaking above a key technical resistance at its 200-day moving average, and chartists note this could open the door for further upside toward the 6,500-level. Market sentiment remained upbeat throughout the week even as December inflation surprised to the upside and after recent comments from the central bank chief signaled a less dovish tone on monetary policy. Foreign accumulation supported share prices, although turnover notably slowed as the week progressed.

 

ICT (+12.5% WoW) led weekly gainers, hitting fresh all-time highs after officially taking over operations of Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 as of January 1. JFC (+12.3% WoW) followed closely with the biggest corporate story for the week -- its announcement of plans to spin off and pursue a US listing of its international business by 2027, a move welcomed by investors who have long viewed its overseas operations as a drag on returns. We previously estimated DCT-2 could add P30/sh, or around 6%, to ICTSI’s valuation. ICT remains the top foreign-bought name and now accounts for roughly 17% of the PCOMP. Other notable outperformers included 2025 laggards AEV (+11.3% WoW), CBC (+7.0% WoW), and URC (+4.9% WoW), all of which suffered double-digit losses last year.

 

December inflation accelerated faster-than-expected to 1.8%, against consensus expectations of 1.4% and from 1.5% in November. This brought FY-2025 inflation to a still-benign 1.7%, the lowest in nine years. For 2026, Street forecasts average inflation of 2.6% while Regis sees this at 3.0%. On monetary policy, BSP Governor Remolona signaled that the easing cycle is nearing its end, with one more potential rate cut to 4.25% in February “on the table.” He added that there could be room for more than one cut should domestic growth slip below 5%. The first of the six Monetary Board meetings this year is scheduled for February 19th. Both Regis and consensus expectations point to a single 25-bp cut for 2026.

 

Foreigners were net buyers for the week by $37m. Offshore funds have been net buyers of the market every day this year so far, largely focused on the large caps. ADTO for the week at $112m.

 

The peso weakened to by 0.7% WoW to 59.259:$1, recovering from a fresh low of 59.365 at mid-week.

ICTSI takes over operations of Durban port

South Africa’s state-owned unit, Transnet, formally turned over management control of Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2) to ICTSI on January 1, 2026. We previously estimated DCT-2 could add P30/sh (6%) to ICTSI’s valuation (here). ICTSI trades at 10x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 17x PER vs. peer average (12.6x EV/EBITDA / 18x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA / 20x PER).

Energy sales in Meralco's franchise area decline by 0.65% in 2025; Company sees modest 3% rebound in 2026

Indicative figures showed energy sales within Meralco’s franchise area fell 0.65% YoY to 53,257 GWH in 2025. Meralco has yet to consolidate figures from Clark Electric Distribution Corp. and other distribution utilities (DU). Meralco’s franchise area accounts for 99% of total DU sales. According to Meralco Senior VP and Chief Revenue Officer Ferdinand O. Geluz. Recall that Meralco’s consolidated DU sales dipped by 0.4% YoY in 9M25. In 2025, Meralco’s residential and commercial sales dropped 2% and 0.5%, respectively, while the industrial segment grew by 1%. For 2026, Meralco is forecasting a 3% growth in energy sales, supported by higher customer connections and normalizing temperatures. Meralco’s distribution unit accounted for 55% of 9M25 profit while its unregulated generation/RES units accounted for the balance.

ACEN to increase capacity of solar project in Zambales

In a filing with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources, ACEN will add a 1,325MWh battery energy storage system (BESS) to its existing solar plant in Zambales. Inflation inches up to 1.8% in December

Headline inflation picked up to 1.8% in Dec from Nov's 1.5%. This was higher than consensus expectations of 1.4% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.2-2.0% for Nov. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past ten months. Inflation in Dec accelerated slightly given faster YoY increases in vegetable prices. Food prices overall rose 1.4% YoY, accelerating from the 0.1% pace in Nov—driven by the +11.6% YoY jump in vegetable prices vs +4.0% the previous month. Notably, rice prices were up MoM (+2.8%)—marking the second MoM price increase since Jun 2024. Rice prices are down 12.3% YoY in Dec, the twelfth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P43.6/kg and P44.9/kg as of Dec 2025 vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan 2025, respectively. Core inflation was unchanged at 2.4% in Dec relative to the previous month, core non-food inflation decelerated to 2.1% in Dec from 2.3% in Nov but was offset by the acceleration in core food inflation to 2.9% from 2.5%. This brings the FY25 headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target but in line with the BSP's 2025 expectation of 1.7%. The 1.7% FY inflation print number brought is lower than the 3.2% in 2024 and the lowest in nine years since 2016. Consensus and Regis 2025 inflation expectations were 1.7% and 1.8%, respectively. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.6% and 3.0%. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

BSP guides easing cycle nearing its end

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona signaled that the end of the central bank's easing cycle is near, with one more potential rate cut to 4.25% in Feb "on the table". Remolona stated that no cut or one more cut are the likely scenarios, as more than one cut would require a deterioration beyond the BSP's base outlook. He added that there may be grounds for more than one cut if 2026 growth goes below 5%. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%. The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2025/26/27 are 4.6/5.4/6.0%, respectively. For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively.

PH gov't revises GDP growth targets

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) has reduced the gov't's GDP growth targets for 2026-2027, revising the 2026-2028 6-7% target to 5-6% in 2026 and 5.5-6.5% in 2027, while keeping the 2028 target unchanged. The previously revised growth target in 2025 is still expected to be 4.8-5.0% (vs 5.5-6.5% initially). As of 9M25, GDP growth averaged 5.0%. This implies that 4Q25 GDP growth must be at least ~4.2% to hit the lower-end of the target range. Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development Secretary Arsenio Baliscan cited "short-term fluctuations" from recent governance challenges and still present global uncertainties but notes that the economy's overall trajectory remains firm. He stated that the revision of growth targets will not derail fiscal planning as improving the quality of growth will remain a top priority. Balisacan expects growth to pick up in the latter half of this year as 1H is expected to "still not quite be as rosy as we would want it to be". Consumption is still expected to drive growth which will be supported by employment, growth and remittances as well as a rebound of consumer confidence. For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively.

Government debt inches up to P17.65trn in end-Nov

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.5% MoM to P17.65trn as of end-Nov. The increase was driven the net issuance of domestic and external debt. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.7% and 31.3% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 0.6% MoM to P12.12trn. External debt rose by 0.3% MoM to P5.53trn. The P17.65trn figure exceeds the P17.36-trn end-2025 projection by 1.7%. Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury previously stated that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as fundraising activities wind down. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 3Q25 stood at 63.1% of GDP unchanged vs 2Q25 but up from 60.7% in 4Q24. The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028.

BSP resisting pressure to defend currency

BSP Governor Remolona said that despite strong pressure to support the peso, the central bank has held back from intervening. When asked what BSP would do should the peso hit 60, Remolona said “nothing, it’s just a number”. Regis is forecasting the PHP to depreciate further to P61 by year-end while Bloomberg consensus expects it to appreciate to P58.3.

PH dollar reserves at US$110.9bn end-Dec

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Dec decreased 0.3% MoM to US$110.9bn. The slight increase primarily reflected the drop in foreign exchange holdings due to the BSP's intervention in the forex market but was offset by the continued surge in gold prices. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 4.0x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.4x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The end-2025 GIR figure of US$110.9bn is 4% higher YoY and ahead BSP's expectations of US$109bn by year-end. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

Customs falls short of 2025 target

The Bureau of Customs (BoC) stated that revenue collections rose to P934.4bn (+2% YoY), falling short of the P958.7bn (+5% YoY) FY25 target. Challenges during the year were lower import volumes, rice importation suspension, increased illicit trade, and global commodity price volatility. Rice tariff collection amounted to P13.7bn, 60% lower than the P34bn in 2024. Lower rice import volume (-26% YoY) and a lower rice tariff rate at 15% from 35% initially contributed to the decline in collections. Confiscated smuggled goods from illicit trade amounted to P62bn in 2025 which is 27% lower than the P85bn seized in 2024. For 2026/27/28, the bureau targets revenues of P1.01trn (+6%), P1.07trn (+6%), and P1.14trn (+6%), respectively.

Marcos signs 2026 budget

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has signed the 2026 national budget into law with notable increase to education, health, agriculture as well as some reduction in unprogrammed appropriations. The Dept. of Budget and Management emphasized the administration's commitment for greater transparency for a "clean, disciplined, responsive" budget. The president highlighted the following budget provisions in his speech. Education received the highest allocation at P1.34trn to increase teaching positions and support classroom construction. The healthcare sector was allocated its highest budget ever at P448bn for better healthcare and benefit packages. P297bn allocated for agriculture for food security and modernization. P270bn for social welfare for quality of life and human capital development improvements.

President Marcos vetoes P93bn in unprogrammed funds, including CARS incentive

President Marcos vetoed over P93bn of unprogrammed appropriations in the recently signed 2026 General Appropriations Act, including the following. P35.8bn of Philippine government counterpart funding for foreign-assisted projects. P4.3bn of fiscal support arrearages under the Comprehensive Automotive Resurgence Strategy (CARS) program. P50bn for the revised Armed Forces of the Philippines modernization program. Exec. Secretary Ralph Recto said that they will work on identifying a mechanism to ensure payment of CARS arrearages. Note that our forecasts do not assume Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) will receive any tax incentives under the CARS program. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 35% of profit. The veto cut unprogrammed funds to P150bn, the lowest level of unprogrammed appropriations since 2019.

Joint congressional oversight body to be created to monitor budget use

Senate Finance Committee chairperson Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian stated that a Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on Public Expenditures will be created to monitor budget utilization and project implementation, especially infra projects. Gatchalian stated that the creation of the oversight body is already mandated under the General Appropriations Act (GAA) but was not implemented in previous years. The committee is to focus on key issues such as the underutilization of agency budgets as well as the implementation of infra and other key gov't projects. The oversight body will also scrutinize projects included in the National Expenditure Program (NEP) in order to prevent questionable project insertions. The oversight committee will be co-chaired by the chairpersons of the Senate Committee on Finance and the House Committee on Appropriations.

Lower House repasses Department of Water Resources bill

The Lower House of the 20th Congress approved House Bill 6789, which is a measure seeking to create the Department of Water Resources. Recall that the Lower House approved a similar proposal on third reading in the 19th Congress last December 2023 but the bill was not tackled by the Senate. The bill’s salient provisions are: Creation of a “Department for Water Resources”, which will solely supervise the country’s water resources (except fisheries and aquaculture), sanitation, flood risk, and drought risk management. Creation of a “Water Regulatory Commission”, which will be an independent, quasi-judicial regulatory body with overall authority and powers on all public and private providers. Reconstitution of the National Water Resources Board as the “National Water Resource Allocation Board”, which would serve as the approval body for using water resources, including dam construction. We would view the passage of this bill positively, as a more streamlined policies could help draw investments in the water sector.

FX loans drop, deposits up in 3Q25; PHP LDR tightens to 85%

Based on BSP data on Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDUs) of banks in 3Q25, FX-denominated loans contracted by 3.9% YoY to $15.13bn. Perhaps more importantly, that figure was down 5.0% QoQ from the $15.93bn level in June. This is in contrast to overall loans (including RRP and interbank loans) of 9.4% YoY. Meanwhile, FCDU deposits grew 5.7% YoY to $60.73bn, only marginally up QoQ. In PHP terms, though, FCDU deposits were up 11% YoY. Meanwhile, PHP LDR is now at 84.9% (by our estimate), much higher than the headline LDR of 74.9% suggests. The FCDU book’s LDR is down to just 24.9%. This suggests that the BSP’s recent aggressive RRR cuts on PHP deposits were probably in response to increasing tightness in the banking system’s PHP books. We would not be surprised if this is also the rationale for BSP Gov Remolona’s statements that he would be “happy” to see the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) on banks’ PHP deposits down from 5.0% currently, to 2.0% “in a year or so”.

PAGCOR to implement minimum fees on online operators

PAGCOR will start collecting minimum monthly guaranteed fees from online gaming operators this year. 1 April 2026 to 30 September 2026. Operators with e-casino operations – minimum fee of P9mn/month. Operators without e-casino operations (such as sports betting, bingo, etc.) – minimum fee of P3mn/month. 1 October 2026 onwards. Operators with e-casino operations – minimum fee of P10.5mn/month. Operators without e-casino operations (such as sports betting, bingo, etc.) – minimum fee of P4mn/month. E-casino operators pay 30% of GGR as fees to PAGCOR. A minimum fee of P10.5mn/month would correspond to GGR of P35/month. We do not expect any direct impact on DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), which pays substantially higher than the minimum guaranteed fees. However, Atty. Marie Antonette Quiogue, CEO of Arden Consult International, estimates that “roughly 40” of the 65 licensees would likely fall short of the GGR threshold of new guaranteed fee required.
