REGIS REPORT

03 February 2021

A bit late to talk about 9M20 bank data, we know. But we work with what we can get.

Consumer loan data was just released, and it shows consumer NPLs shot up to 8.51% in September, from 5.55% in June. Most disconcerting was the 290bp QoQ jump in mortgage NPLs to 8.37%. One would have thought that housing would be the last loans a consumer would default on. Auto (4-wheel) NPL ratio was 9.84%, likely from people who bought cars for ride-hailing services (Grab, etc), demand for which collapsed during the pandemic. Oddly, credit card NPL ratio at 7.49% was even lower than that for mortgages. We suspect that banks are aggressively writing off NPLs for credit cards; while this is conservative, it does mask the true “distress” levels here.

Is the 9M20 data still relevant? Possibly. With households feeling this much stress, it would seem logical to expect cashflow problems to creep into corporate sector into 1H21.

The relevant committee approved the proposals for amending the Constitution at the Lower House, taking it a step forward in what remains a tight schedule. The House is bent on approving changes to the Constitution to allow/ease foreign ownership of certain industries, including public utilities and media. One of the most immediate consequences of lifting such ownership restrictions would be to allow China Telecom to take majority control of the new telco Dito Telecommunity.

MACRO
House panel Oks Charter Change resolution
  • The House committee on constitutional amendments has approved a resolution amending restrictive economic provisions in the Constitution while retaining the ban on foreign ownership of land
    • Voting 64-3-3, the committee approved on Monday Resolution of Both Houses (RBH) No. 2 by Speaker Lord Allen Velasco
  • Under RBH 2, the phrase “unless otherwise provided by law” would be added to Articles 12 (national patrimony and economy), 14 (education, science and technology) and 16 (general provisions) of the Constitution
  • The approved amendments would ease restrictions on the ownership and management of mass media, public utilities, educational institutions, investments and capital for foreign investors
    • Lawmakers agreed to drop the proposal to allow foreigners to own private land in the Philippines
  • Under RBH 2, the House and Senate would vote separately to approve the economic amendments
  • Plenary debates would start in mid-February
  • The plebiscite could be held simultaneously with the 2022 national elections
  • House Committee on Ways and Means Rep. Salceda projected an increase in FDI of P211bn, a 0.55% increase in the country’s GDP and potential generation of 422k jobs by 2022, if RBH No. 2 were to be passed in 2021
BANKS
Consumer loan NPLs shoot up to 8.5% in 3Q20, nearly 300bps QoQ, led by mortgages/auto
  • Consumer loan NPL ratio for the banking system surged 296bps QoQ to 8.51% as of September 2020, from just 5.55% in June, and 4.07% at end-2019. In nominal terms, NPLs surged 121% YoY and 51% QoQ to P167.8bn.
  • As with the 2Q data, the deterioration was surprisingly led by secured mortgages and auto loans. Mortgage NPLs jumped 290bps QoQ to 8.37%, while those for motor vehicles (cars and motorcycles) spiked 440bps QoQ to 9.73%. Credit card NPLs also increased, but only by 176bps QoQ to 7.49%, while salary loan NPLs were up 131bps to 7.17%. We suspect banks were (and are) aggressively writing off these soured unsecured loans – given the low chance of recovery – masking the true delinquency rates.
  • The data suggests that Covid-19 and the lockdowns have had a more severe impact on households than they’ve had on companies. Total banking industry NPL ratio was 3.58% as of September. Excluding consumer loans, we estimate NPL ratio for corporates/SMEs was just 2.39%. One has to wonder, though. If consumers are under such stress, is it just a matter of time before companies feel the pain?
