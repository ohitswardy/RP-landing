REGIS REPORT

08 March 2021

Inflation rose to 4.7% in Feb, up from 4.2% in Jan. This is the highest monthly CPI print in over two years as food inflation continues to rise.

Local bond yields have already moved in reaction to the jump in CPI, with the 10-year sovereign yield just a smidgen below 4%.  However, we don’t expect the Bangko Sentral to act to tighten. The BSP is likely to dismiss this uptick as a supply side issue that it should not act on unless it threatens to affect future inflation expectations. It will, however, probably dissuade the BSP from further easing.

Only if CPI continues to remain elevated for a few more months, will the BSP be compelled to act. The risk of that, though, is rising given how sharply commodity prices have rebounded. Oil crossed US$70 per barrel again.

+++

Domestically, eyes are transfixed on new Covid-19 infections data as headline all over are citing the rising number of cases.  The daily new case count pierced back above 3,000 yesterday after falling to below 1,500 for a number of weeks. The silver lining is that the death rate remains low as does hospital occupancy (albeit rising).

Like the situation on inflation and monetary, we do not expect the government to impose tighter lockdown restrictions for now but this uptick will likely make easing (next month) very unlikely.

+++

DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) reported FY20 core profit of P6.39bn (-47% YoY), which outperformed expectations due to faster completions at the property unit, lower costs at Maynilad, and higher nickel profits.  These more than offset disappointments at Semirara and construction. The outlook looks better in FY21, with elevated commodity prices and the economy recovering from the pandemic but beyond this, growth will depend on management executing on its plans.

New telco Dito’s introductory package for its initial operation is priced at P199 (US$4), which will give subscribers unlimited data for 30 days as well as unlimited on-net SMS and voice calls. The latter part of the package is well pretty much useless for now. This service will be available only in the areas in and around the cities of Cebu and Davao for now.

For perspective, the closest 30-day packages of the two incumbents would set back subscribes by ~P450 (US$9).

MACRO
Inflation climbs to 4.7% in February
  • Inflation accelerated further to 4.7% in February from 4.2% the previous month
    • Within the government’s monthly forecast range of 4.3%-5.1%
    • This is the highest inflation posted since January 2019
    • Food inflation continued to creep up (+6.7%), driven by meat (+20.7%) and rice (+0.5%); the latter was attributed to the end of the harvest season
    • Higher fuel prices also drove inflation higher, with transport inflation accelerating +10.4% YoY and +0.6% MoM
  • MoM, inflation decelerated to +0.2% from +1.3% in January
    • Due to deceleration in meat inflation, which was attributed to price ceilings imposed on pork and chicken products in the National Capital Region
  • Brings YT-Feb average inflation rate to 4.5%, above the government’s target range of 2-4%

CONGLOS
DMCI: FY20 profit drops 47%; near- to medium-term prospects look patchy
  • 4Q20 profit up 4%. Recurring profit in FY20 fell 47% YoY to P6.39bn, ahead of Regis but in line with consensus. Lower coal prices and the pandemic-induced lockdown affected most of its operating units in FY20, save for nickel mining.  In 4Q20 alone, profits improved for construction and nickel mining units but these were blunted by plant outages, the timing of cost recognition at the property unit, and the lingering effects of the pandemic.

We believe profits should bounce strongly in FY21, on the back of higher coal prices and a rebound in the Philippine economy. Beyond this period, however, growth for the group will largely depend on management (a) securing more power contracts, (b) winning construction bids, and (c) securing mining permits (e.g. nickel and limestone). 

  • Property: No construction constraints / 4Q reservation sales flat YoY.  The property unit’s FY20 profit fell 36% YoY to P1.94bn, exceeding estimates on higher booked revenues and better margins. Management noted that it is not experiencing construction constraints after beefing up capacity even before the pandemic.  Reservation sales bounced strongly in 4Q20 (+70% QoQ) due to the launch of one project. Reservation sales fell 47% YoY to P19.6bn but the company is aiming to launch 14 projects worth P149bn in 2021 (vs. 2 in 2020), depending on the speed at which it can secure permits.
  • Construction: Rebound in 4Q / Participating in P105bn biddings in FY21.   Easing mobility restrictions allowed DMCI’s construction unit to post a profit in 4Q20, bringing FY20 profit down 88% YoY to P109bn (below estimates).  However, the company is still operating at c. 10% below pre-Covid levels.

The order book dropped 14% YoY and 7% QoQ to P58.7bn, with majority at the early stage of construction. The company is participating in 18 projects worth P105bn in FY21, bulk of which are infrastructure projects (e.g. North-South Commuter Railway, Metro Manila Subway, South Commuter Railway) that will be bid out in 2Q and 3Q. 

  • Nickel unit riding high but depletion of reserves looms.  Its nickel mining unit contributed P483mn in FY20, up 165% YoY on a 41% increase in sales volumes (1.6mn WMT) and 11% increase in ASPs.  However, reserves at its Berong and Zambales mines are likely to be depleted this year so it can only sell up to 1.4mn WMT – 1.7mn WMT (-15% YoY to +3% YoY) in FY21.  Beyond this year, its sales volumes will depend on management securing more permits in nearby properties.
