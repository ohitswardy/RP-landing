REGIS REPORT

08 November 2021

Headline CPI inflation dipped to 4.6% in Oct as food inflation declined. The latter offset the rise in energy prices, thus tempering CPI growth. This overall drop, however, does not look likely to be sustained given where commodity prices are. With many companies seeing cost pressures, price hikes to consumers are expected to follow.

Meanwhile, the trade deficit for Sept hit US$4bn, as imports rose 15%. This took the YT-Sept trade deficit to US$29bn (+63%). The rising current account deficit is emerging as a risk, as its puts pressure on the PHP, aggravating expectations of widening interest rate differentials as the US Fed begins to “taper”.

MACRO
Inflation eases to 4.6% in October
  • Inflation slowed to 4.6% in October from 4.8% in September
  • Due to moderating prices of meat, vegetables, and sin products
  • Offset acceleration of fuel-related products
  • YT-October average inflation at 4.5%

MACRO
Trade deficit widens further in September
  • Total trade in September improved further, up 17% YoY
    • Trade deficit was larger at US$4bn, with imports up 25% YoY
    • Fuel imports driving the recovery; Consumer and capital goods imports very close to pre-Covid levels
    • Exports also sustained rebound and is now above pre-Covid levels
  • YT-September trade deficit at US$29bn

BANKS
Additional takeaways from MBT briefing
  • The Q&A from the call focused more on MBT’s loan book, and how management intends to re-grow, considering the sharp reduction in loans (down c20% from peak). MBT still guiding very mild loan growth in 2022, though they do admit there could be strong upside if the economy does reopen in earnest.
  • Asset quality is undoubtedly top notch, and is probably the biggest source of earnings upside in the near-term, if NPLs stay benign.
  • Was surprised at how low MBT’s digital #s were. Of their retail customers, only 27% are enrolled online (tho a very high 88% of them are active). This compares to c50% of customers for BDO/BPI.
BANKS
Voyager to seek fresh funding to maintain momentum
  • Voyager Innovations Inc’s president Shaelesh Baidwan said the group will ask its existing investors to infuse more capital to sustain its momentum, as it is set to introduce it digital bank platform
  • PLDT, KKR, Tencent and IFC are the major investors in Voyager
  • By 1Q22, Voyager will be launching Maya Bank, the digital bank counterpart of PayMaya
  • As of its last funding round in June that raised an additional $167mn, Voyager was valued at $747mn
  • PayMaya Philippines has 41mn users and is on track to grow its gross transaction value by 50% this year
  • It claims to have 380,000 payments touchpoints
  • “In the next 12 to 18 months, we will invest to grow the Maya Bank business and that will set us up for a better valuation before an IPO”, added Baidwan
PROPERTY
MREIT to pay P1/share in dividends in 2022
  • MREIT plans to pay a dividend of P1/share in calendar year 2022
    • Represents 5.5% dividend yield
    • Previous target – P0.95/share
  • In 3QCY21, MREIT’s office occupancy improved to 98%
    • From 93% in 2QCY21
  • WALE of 4.2 years
  • MREIT will acquire 55,700sqm of office space (from MEG) over the next six months
    • Average occupancy of 99%, WALE of 3.2 years
    • December 2021 – 30,700sqm in Iloilo
    • March 2022 – 25,000sqm in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio
  • The properties will be acquired with debt
  • MREIT is targeting to have a portfolio of 500,000sqm by 2024
    • From 225,000sqm currently
  • Megaworld’s office vacancy was maintained (QoQ) at 10%
  • MEG is currently trading at 58% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 7.0X FY22E P/E
