REGIS REPORT

12 March 2021

The mayors of Metro Manila have agreed to impose a common curfew policy across the metropolis in response to the recent rise in Covid-19 cases. The curfew will be from 10pm to 5am. In itself, the curfew is unlikely to have much direct impact either on Covid-19 transmissions or business activity. There will be a psychological impact, however, as it’s a signal that government is concerned and there might be more restrictions to come.

+++

Vehicle sales for the month of Feb improved from Jan, rising 12% MoM based on the data from CAMPI/TMA (but still down YoY). This is likely to be the calm before the storm, however. The price adjustments due to the safeguard duty took effect at the start of March, and will depress sales for a while.

On the results front, we have highlights from the briefings of Ayala Corp (AC PM) as well as San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM).

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – Net profit came in at P17.1bn for FY20, down by half from the previous year as the pandemic hit nearly every operating segment. Outside the listed units we directly cover, attention was on AC Energy (ACEN PM) which is raising a P30bn war chest for its renewable energy push.

The House of Representatives will begin hearings on a proposed law to (1) not only grant the Bangko Sentral (BSP) power to regulate loan rates but also to (2) explicitly cap them at a fixed level (in this case 12%, or 3% above the short-term benchmark). We are doubtful that the measure will be passed in its current form but the risk of populist, interventionist policies is clearly on the rise. More below.

MACRO
Metro mayors agree to enforce common curfew for 2 weeks
  • Beginning March 15, Metro Manila will have a uniform curfew of 10pm to 5am that will last for two weeks to curb rising COVID-19 infections in the region
    • Metro Manila now contributes 60% of the country’s covid cases from only 8% in Feb 2-10
  • Exempted are essential workers, takeout operations, and 24-hour restaurant operations
MACRO
GIR reserves rise to US$109.1bn in end-February
  • The country’s dollar reserves rose to US$109.082bn in end-February (+0.38% MoM) after dipping slightly in January (US$108.7bn)
    • This was higher by 23.7% YoY
    • MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad (+2.4% MoM)
  • End-February GIR is enough to cover 11.7 months’ worth of imports
  • The BSP expects the GIR to end the year at US$102bn, down from last year’s record level US$110.1bn

MACRO
Vehicle sales rise 12% MoM in February
  • Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI and TMA), jumped 12% MoM in February to 26,230 units
    • MoM recovery was attributed to improvements in sales of Asian utility vehicles and light trucks
    • This was however 12% lower YoY due to disruptions caused by the Taal volcano eruption last year
    • Brings YT-Feb industry vehicle sales  down by 7% YoY to 49,610 units
  • Toyota sold 13,074 units in February, up 6% YoY and 21% MoM
    • Brings YT-Feb unit sales to 23,894 units (+13% YoY)
  • Dealerships began implementing the safeguard duty in March so we expect sales to plunge
  • Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year
    • With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%)
    • Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively
    • GT Capital is trading at a 14% discount to its market-based NAV of P636/sh

BANKS
Loan rate caps proposed – Congress hearings start today
  • When the BSP set a cap on credit card interest rates and fees last year, we feared that it be a slippery slope. And here we are. Congress is set to hold hearings today (Fri 12 March) on House Bill 7967 a bill that proposes to update the defanged Usury Law. The bill’s key features are:
    • A maximum rate of 12% for all loans, including credit cards and cash advances.
    • The BSP’s Monetary Board may set loan limits, PROVIDED that the maximum rate does not exceed three  percentage points above the 91-day T-bill rate in the prior quarter.
    • The MB may prescribe higher rates for “loans of low priority” such as consumer loans, or loans made by pawnshops, finance companies.
    • The MB may also set the rates on deposits and deposit substitutes.
  • Needless to say, this would be disastrous for banks. If applied today, the average 91-day T-bill rate in 4Q20 was 1.046%, which would put the maximum loan rate at 4.046%. For reference, effective loan yields for the banks we cover in 4Q20 were as follows – BDO at 5.84%; BPI at 5.28%; MBT at 5.76%; and SECB at 7.28%.
  • The bill is clearly populist, and we wonder if it’s just for show. Elections are not that far away (May 2022). It is unclear if the BSP is supportive of the proposal, in spirit if not in letter.

OTHERS
Foreign ownership trends – February 2021
  • We estimate total market foreign ownership fell to 22.0% (-10bps MoM) while PSEi foreign ownership dropped to 25.5% (-20bps MoM)
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    1. RLC – foreign ownership fell 87 basis points MoM to 19.0%.
    2. RRHI – foreign ownership declined 75 basis points MoM to 25.7%.
    3. MBT – foreign ownership dropped 74 basis points to 21.3%. It was the top foreign sold stock in February (-US$33.2mn).
    4. BPI – had the biggest increase in foreign ownership of 22 basis points (to 29.2%). It was also the top foreign bought stock in February (+US$13mn). In a briefing at the end of January, management had a more optimistic tone about the outlook for nonperforming loans, expecting the NPL ratio to peak at 4% or lower by 2H21.
