REGIS REPORT

23 December 2021

The latest (credible) opinion poll showed Ferdinand (“Bongbong”) Marcos, Jr firmly in the lead in the contest for the Presidency, with a little over half (53%) of the probable voters. The next closest candidate, Vice President Leonor Robredo, was a distant second with 20%. None of the other candidates polled above 10%. Voters who previously backed the Dutertes (including his daughter Sara) appear to have quickly gathered their support around Marcos, combining their forces with what’s been described as the “Solid North”.

With such a commanding lead, it now looks like the race for Malacañang is Marcos’ race to lose. Over the next few months, investors and businessmen will likely be trying to figure out what a Marcos presidency redux might look like. While the legacy and record of Marcos Sr is pretty apparent, the beliefs of Marcos Jr are less so, despite the latter being a politician (on and off) for four decades.

Union Bank (UBP PM) disclosed that its board has approved the acquisition of Citi’s consumer business in the Philippines. To fund it, the board also approved a P40bn rights issue.

The purchase will include a) Citi’s credit card and personal loans, personal deposits, and wealth management businesses. It will also include real estate assets including  the Citibank Square in Eastwood, 3 branches, 5 wealth centers, and 2 “branch lite” locations. The acquisition will transform UBP into one of the major players in the consumer lending, and wealth management markets. Citi is currently the #3 player in credit cards in the country. The deal will also more than double UBP’s AUM. All of these will obviously depend on UBP retaining all of Citi’s clients after the transaction.

UBP will pay the net book value (about P17.8bn) plus a premium of P45.3bn, valuing the transaction at roughly 3.5x Price to book. Closing is expected by 2H22.

The rights issue will be fully underwritten by UBP’s major shareholders Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM), Insular Life, and the Social Security System.

The latest mobility data from Google show what has been apparent to anyone venturing out of their homes the past weeks. People are back out, and in droves. This certainly is a good sign for businesses for this holiday season though many in central and southern Philippines will have to contend with the aftermath of Typhoon Rai (Odette).

Marcos/Sara Duterte lead President/VP survey
  • The tandem of former senator Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. and Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte-Carpio topped the December 2021 survey of Pulse Asia
  • For President, 53% of respondents picked Marcos, who also led in terms of geographical areas and majority of classes
    • Meanwhile, VP Leni Robredo took 2nd place with 20%, up from just 8% in the previous survey
    • Isko Moreno and Pacquiao dropped to 8% each
  • For VP, Sara Duterte got 45% while Senate President Vicente Sotto III had 31%
  • A total of 2,400 respondents participated in the survey, which was conducted from December 1-6 using face-to-face interviews

MACRO
Mobility at new highs, again (18 Dec)

Yep. New Covid-era highs. Again. Recall that Google uses Jan/Feb 2020 as a base, not comparing to Dec 2019. But note that they’re all also significantly higher (+20 to 30 %age points) than the data for Dec 2020. Retail/Recreation movement nation-wide, and for Metro Manila both posting numbers exceeding the pre-pandemic bases. Transit Station and Workplace data are still negative, but are slowly closing in on positive territory.

Caveat - this data was as of 18 Dec, just about when Typhoon Rai/Odette hit. By our estimates, those regions account for c22% of the country’s GDP. Airports on the islands the region are either not operating, or are at reduced capacity. Many areas are running out of water and fuel, and may not even have electricity until early January.

While the relaxation of mobility restrictions from end-October were looking bullish for 4Q21 GDP/earnings, it is difficult to assess the impact of the typhoon. Apart from the obvious tragic loss of life and property, the areas that were worst-hit – Cebu, Bohol, and Siargao – are popular tourist destinations that were expecting a surge in arrivals over the holidays.
