REGIS REPORT

15 November 2021

The circus has come to town. The election circus, that is.

Sara Duterte filed her papers as candidate not for President but for Vice President on Saturday. The move is a surprise given the expectations that she would run for the top post and not for the largely ornamental one that is VP. Sara Duterte was subsequently “adopted” by the camp of Ferdinand Marcos, Jr (Bongbong) as his running mate.

The most important consequence of the above developments is that it would cement a Marcos-Duterte alliance, and would make Bongbong Marcos far and away the overwhelming favorite in the 2022 polls.

The hitch to this situation, however, is that President Duterte’s trusty aide, Senator Christopher “Bong” Go filed his papers to run for President himself, with the open blessing of his patron, the elder Duterte.  The elder Duterte’s other right hand man, Senator Ronald “Bato” dela Rosa, who previously filed to run for President, withdrew his candidacy almost simultaneously.

As it turns out, the Marcos-Duterte juggernaut may not appear as real or solid as believed.

To add to the confusion, President Duterte also says he plans to run for Vice President in the coming elections, competing directly with his daughter. President Duterte is apparently peeved that Sara chose to run as VP despite leading in the opinion polls for President and he appears to have said he will run as VP out of pique. Whether he will actually do so is something we will find out later today.

Today, Nov 15, is the final day for political parties to file to substitute candidates. It would be the last opportunity for the “real” candidates to step into the shoes of placeholders.

More results today:

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) - Ayala Corp’s net income for 9M21 amounted to P19.4bn (+70%), implying 3Q income of P9.0bn, up sharply QoQ.  We note, however, that the 3Q profit included P3.5bn in gains from the partial divestment of GN Power Kausawagan and 2Q profit was affected by mark-to-market losses relating to its stake in Manila Water (MWC PM).  Otherwise, continuing earnings from its operating companies were largely flat or lower QoQ.  More below.
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) - 9M21 NPAT came at P4.8bn, tracking behind consensus. The big disappointment was in asset quality, as NPLs blipped up 22bps QoQ, and NPL cover fell to 91% (from 103% in 2Q). Despite this, provision expense fell to P1.65bn in 3Q, with 9M at P4.1bn (-81% YoY). Profits would have fallen even further had the bank kept NPL cover at or above 100%. Loans were flat YoY (improving from -5% in 2Q), and NIMs inched up 5bsp QoQ. More after the briefing on Wednesday at 5pm.
  • Globe (GLO PM) (-) – Core income for 3Q income came in aty P5.1bn, down 12% QoQ but up 12% YoY.  Mobile service revenues are stagnant, down 2% QoQ, implying a modest revenue market share drop (-0.3ppt).  We also note a meaningful pick-up in revenue from the broadband segment, which saw a 10% rise QoQ after barely seeing growth since 2Q last year.  Some more tidbit below and even more after today’s briefing.
  • LT Group (LTG PM) (-) – LTG bounced back to a profit from its 2Q loss due to much lower provisioning (P1.4bn in 3Q21 vs P16.9bn in 2Q21) from PNB. The tobacco business posted improvements for both volume (+5%QoQ) and profits (+8%QoQ), albeit tracking slightly behind FY estimates.

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp 3Q results
  • Net income P19.4bn for 9M. Ayala Corp reported net income of P19.4bn for 9M, +70% YoY. For 3Q alone, net income was P9.0bn, up 2.6x YoY as the base was low the prior year and 3Q this year was lifted by P3.5bn in gains from the partial sale of its coal-fired power plant (GN Power Kauswagan).

Comparing 3Q21 vs 2Q21, profits appear to have risen sharply (P9.0bn vs P5.0bn). Upon closer inspection, earnings from the underlying operations were actually largely unchanged QoQ after exceptional items are excluded. There was a mark-to-market loss relating to its Manila Water stake in 2Q. Earnings from the key subsidiaries (ALI, GLO, BPI) were largely flat or down QoQ.

  • Losses at AC Industrials continue. YT-Sept losses at AC Industrials amounted to P1.12bn, which is an improvement from the P2.06bn loss the prior year.  For 3Q alone, however, losses were P532m. This was worse both on a QoQ and a YoY basis.
  • Acquiring 60% of Air21 for P6bn. AC disclosed it was acquiring for P6.06bn a 60% stake in logistics group Air21. Air21 has operations in express door-to-door delivery, warehousing and transport, freight forwarding and waste logistics and management. The deal is targeted for closing within 1Q22.

AC disclosed that Air21’s “attributable” revenue, EBITDA and net income for FY20 were P3bn, P815m and P230m, respectively. Factoring in around P800m to P1bn of net debt, AC’s acquisition price thus implies a multiple of 3.6x sales, 13x EBITDA and 44x PER on a trailing basis. The EBITDA multiple looks reasonable at first glance considering the control premium and the fact they are trailing multiples for what is likely to be fast-growing business.

  • Divestment of GNPK first tranche complete. AC sold 32% of its 71% beneficial interest in GN Power Kauswagan at a valuation equivalent to US$453m for 100%. The gain reported on the transaction was P3.5bn (US$70m), inclusive of the revaluation gain of its residual stake. AC expects the full divestment of GNPK to be completed over a period as long as ten years.
  • Purchase of ALI shares accounts for bulk of capex budget. AC invested P16.9bn so far this year, with P10.1bn of that amount going into the purchase of ALI shares. AC increased its stake in ALI by 1.9ppt YTD.
TELCOS
Globe (GLO PM) 3Q results
  • 3Q core earnings P5.1bn (+12%), taking 9M to P15.8bn (+1%). Globe reported core income (ex- exceptionals) of P5.08bn, which would take core income to P15.8bn (+1%) for 9M by our reckoning. The core income excludes adjustments on deferred tax arising from the reduction in tax rate booked in 1Q (included in company’s reported core income) which amounted to P2.5bn based on our estimate. Excluding the deferred tax adjustment, core income is below expectations.  If one were to include that item, Globe’s core income would be within expectations.
  • Mobile service revenue dips QoQ. Wireless service revenue was down 2% QoQ and up +1% YoY. The absolute figure for 3Q implies a small (-0.3ppt) loss of revenue market share.
  • Broadband finally picking up. Broadband revenue of P7.8bn for 3Q was up 10% QoQ (+12% YoY), the fastest % increase in six quarters. The company added 78k wired broadband subscribers in the quarter. While this is still well below PLDT’s (229k) and Converge’s (222k), it is the biggest such increase for Globe ever.

BANKS
SECB 9M21 behind consensus; NPLs rise, cover falls to 91%

An overall soft set of results from SECB, as NPAT for 9M21 came in at P4.83bn, behind consensus (just 67% of P7.24bn for FY21E). While the bottomline was ahead of Regis estimates, we note that 9M PPOP was behind. To the upside, loans were flat YoY, improving from the -5.4% in 2Q21, and NIMs inched up 5bps QoQ. However, asset quality was the big disappointment. NPL ratio was up 22bps QoQ to 4.15%. Despite this, provision expense was actually lower QoQ to just P1.65bn, which tool NPL Cover down to 91%.

Some highlights:

  • Loans were flat YoY, an improvement over the -5.4% YoY in 2Q21. On a QoQ basis, loans were up 1.2%. Consumer loans were still down 12% YoY, but still accounted for 25% of total loans.
  • NIMs crept up 5bps QoQ to 4.12%. This was despite a 17bps QoQ drop in loan yields, and an 11bps increase in deposit costs. SECB switched from expensive wholesale funding (bills payable) to less-expensive time deposits. Total deposits were up 20% YoY.
  • Fees were up 24% YoY, and are already back above pre-Covid levels. Trading/fx gains were down 92%, given the extremely high base in 2020.
  • Opex was flat, up just 2% YoY
  • Provision expense was P4.1bn for 9M21, down 81% YoY. For 3Q21 alone, provisions fell 84% YoY and 19% QoQ to P1.65bn. This was despite a deterioration in NPL ratio to 4.15%, up 22bps QoQ. The low provision charge took NPL coverage down to 91%, down from 103% in 2Q21.

The bank will hold an earnings call on 17 November (Wednesday) at 5pm.

CONSUMER
Notes from JFC 3Q/9M21 results briefing
  • Business Transformation (BT) cost savings to mitigate input cost pressure. Higher input cost and heightened labor cost in the US were the main factors that led to margin contraction in 3Q. The cost pressures are expected to persist over the near term, and the company will likely have to implement another round of price increases this quarter. In addition, management is optimistic that the fixed cost savings from its BT program (P2-3bn annually) could also help mitigate any near-term cost pressure.
  • Smashburger loss widens, CBTL at break even. Given elevated labor cost in the US, Smashburger’s operating losses widened to P200-250mn in 3Q by our estimate (1H21: cP300mn op loss). Underlying trends, however, are encouraging with Smashburger posting +15% SSSG. Franchised stores are also now outpacing the growth of company owned stores. The main drag to profitability continues to be pre-operating expenses for new stores, but management claims that majority of its network is already profitable at the store level. Meanwhile, CBTL’s operations continue to improve and has already hit break even at the operating level in 3Q (1H21: cP100mn op loss).
  • Philippines: recovery in 4Q. The company is seeing a sequential improvement in the Philippines as lockdown restrictions have eased. At the peak of the lockdowns in 3Q, sales had fallen to nearly 30% below pre-Covid levels. As of Oct-Nov, sales have been tracking c20% below pre-Covid. Management expects a full recovery by 3Q22, barring another hard lockdown.

  • Store expansion to accelerate. Management expects to accelerate new store openings to 500 stores in 2022 (net new stores = 350-400), further ramping up to c600 stores annually (gross) from 2023 onwards. A larger proportion of the new stores will be overseas and only around 100 new stores will be in the Philippines.
  • Targeting 6-8% net margins over the next few years. Management did not provide any guidance but indicated that they hope to achieve net margin of 6-8% in the next 2-3 years. Key margin drivers will be:
    • Better margins in the Phils vs historical levels (c.6%) due to lower fixed cost resulting from the BT
    • Improving profitability of Smashburger and CBTL, which are currently generating above average operating margins at the store level. The drag from pre-operating expenses is expected to normalize in 2-3 years as sales of new stores ramp up
    • Higher proportion of franchised stores – JFC is targeting to increase the proportion of franchised stores to 2/3 of its network from c50% currently
    • Increased contribution from the overseas business, which generally yields higher margin. Management noted that its new investments (Tim Ho Wan, Milksha tea) have better store economics than some of its current brands.
