24 March 2021

What will Diokno do?

The Treasury just issued P30bn worth of 10-year bonds, at a rate of 4.614%, significantly higher than the 3.066% rate they paid last month. In the secondary market, the 10-year BVAL rate is now at around 4.5%, from just under 3.0% at end-Jan. The yield curve has steepened sharply, with the short-end (1-year BVAL) up just c40bps in the same period. The moves upward are partly due to spiking inflation, and partly in response to higher comparable yields abroad (notably the US).

Surprisingly, the Bangko Sentral has not intervened in the market. In 2020 at the height of the Covid pandemic, the BSP aggressively bought the long end of the curve when market rates were moving counter to policy rates. This time around, they seem content to let the market move.

BSP Governor Diokno has said he is “not inclined to tighten monetary policy”, given that inflation is driven by supply-shocks, and there is “little evidence of second-round effects”. In 2018 though, when inflation spiked on rice and oil prices, the BSP eventually raised rates. That said, the economy was much more robust in 2018 that it is today. The currency has been surprisingly benign, which does give the BSP some leeway to run with hugely negative real interest rates.

Some bits and bobs in today’s news:

Dennis Uy’s (of Davao) Phoenix Petroleum (PNX PM) disclosed that it is looking to sell assets and investments to help it manage its gearing levels (last at >200%). Late last year, PNX struggled to redeem its preferred shares. Based on PNX’s financials, its biggest lenders are BDO and PNB. This follows the Dennis Uy group’s sale of logistics firm 2GO to SM Investments, which is currently conducting a tender offer for 2GO. Mr Uy has other investments in casinos, education, restaurants. Most notably, he is the partner of China Telecom in the 3rd telco player DITO Telecommunity.

A Congressman has filed a bill raising the legal age for smoking from 18 to 21 years old. In theory, this could be negative for LT Group (LTG PM)’s tobacco operations. In practice though, some 80% of cigarettes are sold in informal channels (sari-sari stores, street vendors, etc), which will make enforcement difficult. We doubt the bill’s chances of passage. A cynic might say the bill was filed to stir lobby groups ahead of the 2022 elections. Surely not…?

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) says it will extend the filing of income tax returns for corporates. Typically, the deadline is 15 April. However, the CREATE bill is likely to be signed into law only this week, and the implementing rules won’t be out for at least another few weeks (if not months). A key feature of CREATE is the reduction in corporate tax rates from 30% to 25% retroactive to July 2020. Auditors will be working overtime (and billing for it).
