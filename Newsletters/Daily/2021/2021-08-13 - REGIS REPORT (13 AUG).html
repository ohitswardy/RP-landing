REGIS REPORT

13 August 2021

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept policy rates unchanged after yesterday’s Monetary Board (MB) meeting. Inflation forecast for this year, though, was tweaked upwards (now 4.1% from 3.9%) to reflect the realities of the first seven months of 2021. The BSP’s own inflation expectations would naturally play a part in how it sets monetary policy, which has so far remained ultra-loose.

+++

More results again today.

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – AC reported profit of P10.4bn for 1H21, up 31% YoY.  For 2Q alone, profit was P5.0bn, double the previous year’s. Earnings were down QoQ despite the rise in contributions from BPI (BPI PM) and Ayala Land (ALI PM). Losses at AC Industrials, while down YoY, are worse sequentially. Contributions from the power business were also lower for both operating and non-operating reasons.
  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) (=) – 1H21 core net profit was down 13% YoY as higher input cost and A&P investments dragged margins. Revenues across the two business segments were flattish in 2Q, both coming off high bases from pantry stocking last year. The company expects to see faster top line growth in 2H, driven by Quorn, which has seen a +34% YTD increase in distribution points. A&P investments for both APAC BFB and Quorn will continue, but price increases for the former should provide some margin relief amidst heightened input cost.

  • Alliance Global (AGI PM) (+) - In a press release, AGI said its 1H21 profit grew 124%YoY to P8.5bn, tracking ahead of FY estimates. Liquor’s outperformance (2Q +44%QoQ) as well as a P4.8bn one-time gain at Travellers more than offset the shortfalls from property and quick service restaurants. In particular, Golden Arches swung back to a loss of P24mn in 2Q (vs a P73mn profit in 1Q), dragged by the April ECQ and various cost pressures.
  • Vista Land (VLL PM) (-) – 1H21 net profit rose 9% to P3.7bn, tracking behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. Despite generally looser restrictions compared to a year ago, 2Q21 residential revenue fell 13% YoY. Furthermore, the jump in rental receivables suggests that the actual performance of the leasing operations is not as good as the rental revenue implies. Meanwhile 1H21 reservation sales rose 14% to P29.4bn, on track to meet our FY forecast, which is about 75% of the 2019 figure.

  • Max’s Group (MAXS PM) (-) – The company barely broke even in 2Q21 even as SSSG rebounded to +36%. We estimate sales were still 30-35% below pre-covid levels given renewed dine-in restrictions. However, significant cost rationalization measures drove EBITDA margins well above pre-pandemic levels.

We also have some highlights from the briefing of Cebu Air’s (CEB PM) 2Q results, which we previously noted.

The airline’s 2Q21 pax volumes, seat load factors, and fares improved QoQ driven by a strong demand rebound at some domestic tourist destinations. CEB’s domestic market share improved QoQ to 54%. Seat capacity marginally improved QoQ to 17% of pre-COVID levels from 15% but can easily go to c. 40% by 4Q21 if vaccination rates improve further and travel restrictions are eased.

Given the challenging environment, CEB is focusing on reducing costs and capex further. Their monthly cash burn rate is at P1.5bn, down from a peak of P2.5bn and management is looking to bring this down further.  Click on the link for more.

+++

Megaworld’s (MEG PM) REIT subsidiary MREIT (MREIT PM) received PSE approval. We thought we’d highlight something that might have been overlooked by potential subscribers to the deal.  MREIT does not own the land on which its buildings stand (not different from Ayala Land’s (ALI PM) AREIT).  However, it is also currently not paying rent to the landowner (its parent Megaworld) either. This arrangement will not be persisting indefinitely, however.  MREIT will begin paying rent in July 2023 and the amount will rise further two years later.

Potential investors will need to be aware of this as near-term earnings (up t June 2023) may not be reflective of the true economics.

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp 1H earnings up +31% YoY
  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) reported net income of P10.4bn for 1H21, up +31% from the prior year. Earnings for 2Q alone came in at P5.0bn, which is double last year’s depressed P2.5bn. QoQ, however, earnings were surprisingly down (-7%), which management attributed to the stricter lockdowns implemented in April. A look at the equity-accounted profits in AC’s 2Q presentation suggests QoQ drops in contributions from, Globe, power and AC Industrials.
  • The QoQ drop for Globe was due to the one-offs in 1Q21 where it booked deferred tax adjustments relating to CREATE tax reform.
  • The power business reported 2Q contributions of P1.1bn (vs P2.4bn in 1Q21). The drop is partly because of plant outages, aggravated by ~P500m in charges relating to the valuation of an embedded derivative (a prepayment option) for its debt as well as real property taxes. This was taken not with the listed AC Energy (ACEN PM), however.
  • AC Industrials saw losses narrow for the half but 2Q losses (P392mn) were still double 1Q’s (P200m). IMI dipped back into the red in 2Q, while the losses at the auto business widened.
  • AC bought back P5.8bn worth of shares in May, taking the total buyback under its P10bn program to P7.2bn (equivalent to 1.7% of shares). The average price was P691/share, slightly lower than current market. AC also acquired P10.1bn worth of Ayala Land (ALI PM) shares (=1.8%) at an average price of P36.34/share.
  • With regard to its “value realization” plan, it considers AC’s partial sale of secondary shares in ACEN to GIC earlier this year as part of this initiative. The deal raised P10.6bn (US$210m). The reduction of its stake in Manila Water (P4.8bn) was also included. AC aims to raise ~US$1bn from divestments by 2023.

CONSUMER
MAXS 2Q21 - margins rebound despite revenue shortfall
  • On a recurring basis, Max’s Group (MAXS PM) reported net profit of P15mn, behind Regis estimates. Revenues were behind at P3.6bn (-4% YoY), though a strong margin jump in 2Q21 drove YTD EBITDA to P686mn, broadly within expectations.
  • The company posted consolidated SSSG of 36% in 2Q21. By brand, the rebound was mostly driven by Max’s and Pancake House, which typically generate a higher proportion of sales from dine-in. Despite the sharp jump in SSS, we estimate it is still 30-35% below pre-pandemic levels. On-premise accounted for c22% of sales during the quarter, vs 50-60% historically.
  • Despite the relatively soft top line on a QoQ basis, MAXS saw a sharp jump in EBITDA margin to 22.7% during the quarter. This was driven by cost saving initiatives and margin enhancement strategies, which lead to significant reductions in variable and fixed cost. Among these initiatives include:
    • Streamlining menu offerings across brands and maximizing raw materials across SKUs
    • Optimizing fixed cost, such as labor and rent
    • Leveraging its multi-brand portfolio to maximize scale benefits on supply chain and off-premise sales channels
  • Given the cost rationalization initiatives, management believes operating leverage should further drive margin improvement as dine-in restrictions are eased post-lockdown.

PROPERTY
PSE approves Megaworld REIT IPO
  • Megaworld plans to raise up to P27.3bn from the sale of secondary shares in MREIT
  • 1.24bn shares at up to P22/share
    • Includes overallotment option
  • Represents 49% of shares outstanding
  • 10 office buildings with GLA of 225,000sqm
  • Properties in Eastwood (Quezon City), McKinley Hill (Fort Bonifacio, Taguig), and Iloilo Business Park
  • MREIT does not own land and will pay land leases to MEG
    • Before July 2023 – no rent
    • 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2025 – 2.5% of office/retail revenue, 1.5% of hotel revenue
    • 1 July 2025 onwards – 5% of office/retail revenue, 3% of hotel revenue
  • At the maximum price, market cap of MREIT would reach P55.7bn
  • At the maximum price, MREIT would offer a dividend yield of 4% (based on prospectus)
    • This could raise our MEG NAV estimate by 9%
  • If we were to lower our assigned cap rate on Megaworld’s entire 1.4mn sqm office portfolio by 1ppt (to 6%), then our MEG NAV estimate would rise by 8%
  • Pricing date – September 9
  • Offer period – September 14 to 20
  • Listing date – September 30
  • MEG is currently trading at 6.2X FY22E P/E, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 63% discount to NAV
