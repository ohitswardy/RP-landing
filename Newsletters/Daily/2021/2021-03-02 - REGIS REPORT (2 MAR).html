REGIS REPORT

02 March 2021

We have another round of results today, with Universal Robina (URC PM) leading off. The consumer food firm reported YoY EBIT improvement in the last quarter despite the pandemic challenges. This is in contrast to Meralco (MER PM), Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM) and Manila Water (MWC PM), all of whom suffered declines.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (+) - FY20 EBIT of P16bn (+7% YoY) was within expectations, and the profit outperformance was driven by non-operating items. Revenue for the Philippine branded consumer food (BCF) business was soft (-4% YoY in 4Q20) as consumption remained weak amidst more subdued holiday spending and limited mobility. Cost inflation is a concern but management thinks it is manageable (for now), and price increases are likely to be a last resort amidst a fragile consumer environment.

  • Manila Electric (MER PM) (-) – After recovering nicely over 3Q20, electricity volumes dipped again in 4Q (-7%) causing FY20 core income of P21.7bn (-9%) to come up mildly short. Mobility restrictions held back demand from commercial establishments even as they help boost power usage at home. Its new power plant was also partly responsible for the shortfall.

  • Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM) (=) - FY20 net profit (P1.4bn -32% YoY) was in line with expectations. 4Q20 sales grew 6% YoY on the back of +0.5% SSSG and the opening of 6 new Depots throughout the year. Operating margin was down c.190bps YoY due to pre-operating expenses for new stores. The company will be hosting a briefing this afternoon at 4pm.
  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (-) - FY20 core profit to common fell 22% YoY to P4.79bn. Despite a 58% QoQ jump in 4Q20 profit, EBITDA still fell 25% QoQ as underlying operations for its Philippine assets remained soft.  The East zone business alone saw a 4% QoQ drop in billed volumes, which partly led to the 23% QoQ decline in EBITDA.  Discussions on the new contract have begun and there are now only a “handful” of issues being resolved.

Cement manufacturer Holcim Philippines (HLCM PM) also reported 4Q results and the numbers show a weakness that’s become the norm for nearly every firm that’s reported. The quarter’s volumes dropped 17% YoY.

+++

Meanwhile, the REIT listing of Double Dragon’s (DD PM) office division has been approved by the PSE (PSE PM). We are concerned about the prospects of the leasing operations of DD’s office business because of its dependence on offshore gaming operations, regardless of whether these tenants are technically licensed POGOs.

The reclamation project proposed by the Solar (Tieng) group has now cleared all regulatory hurdles and can proceed, leaping ahead of SM Prime’s (SMPH PM) Paranaque project in terms of approvals.  This project is in the city of Manila and involves 148 hectares of land to be reclaimed.

RETAIL
Wilcon Depot FY20 net profit -32% YoY to P1.4bn – in line
  • In a press release, WLCON reported FY20 net profit of P1.4bn, down 32% YoY, and in line with expectations
  • Net sales of P22.6bn (-8% YoY) were just mildly behind forecasts, though margins came in better-than-expected
  • For 4Q20 alone, net profit declined just 3% YoY to P563mn
  • Net sales rose 6% YoY on the back of 0.5% SSSG and the opening of 6 new stores throughout the year
  • Operating margin was down nearly 200bps YoY, which was attributed to new store expansion
  • 2 stores slated for opening in 2020 were delayed to 1Q21 due to construction delays

The company will be hosting a briefing today at 4pm Manila time.

UTILITIES
Manila Water 4Q20: Underlying operations remain soft
  • FY20 core profit drops 22% to P4.79bn.  Manila Water reported FY20 profit of P4.5bn (-18% YoY). Excluding one-offs related to (a) probable losses (b) the new accounting treatment of supervision fees for Estate Water, and (c) fair value adjustments for its investment in Thai-based East Water, as well as the share of participating preferreds, we estimate core profit to common dropped by 22% YoY to P4.79bn, ahead of consensus forecast (P4.67bn).  Implied 4Q20 EBITDA dropped 25% QoQ to P2.49bn but core profit jumped 58% QoQ to P1.31bn due to lower non-operating items. Despite a recovery in operations for its regional investments, underlying operations for most of its Philippine units remained soft in 4Q.
  • East zone: EBITDA up in FY20; Capex trails.  4Q20 EBITDA for the East zone (EZ) unit dropped 23% QoQ to P2.36bn, after billed volumes dropped 4% and cash costs ramped up. The company spent P9.84bn on capex for the EZ alone in FY20, down slightly from last year’s P9.9bn and trailing its regulatory commitments (at least P12bn annually).
  • Other updates: Concession agreement/water regulation. MWC has been unofficially discussing terms of the revised concession agreement with the government for the past month and management believes the new contract will likely be released soon, as there are now only a “handful” of issues being discussed. The new contract, which will be patterned exactly after the New Clark City water contract, will have a fixed nominal regulatory return of 12% without tax recovery. To compare this with the current ADR of 7.39%, which is calculated on a post-tax and real basis, suggests that the revised water contract could have a lower return of 6% on a post-tax, real return basis.
  • There are also potential major changes to the regulation of water companies that are currently filed in Congress. These bills aim to convert the water sector into a utility-like franchise model wherein water assets will be owned by the company. Management expressed its preference for this model.
  • Together with the team of Mr. Enrique Razon, MWC is exploring water investment opportunities in Brazil and Colombia.

CEMENT
Holcim Philippines FY20 profit down 42% YoY
  • Holcim Philippines reported FY20 profit down 42% YoY to P2.06bn
    • 4Q20 profit fell 39% YoY to P1.037bn but it recovered by +69% QoQ due to lower cement/clinker imports and higher sales of higher-margin aggregates
  • Revenues dropped -22% in FY20 and -26% in 4Q20
    • Volumes fell -17% YoY in 4Q20 and -14% YoY in FY20
    • Implied ASPs dropped by 9% YoY in 4Q20 and 8% YoY in FY20
  • 4Q20 EBITDA fell 39% YoY to P1.53bn with EBITDA margin down 4ppts YoY to 21% due to lower sales and bad weather
  • Despite lower distribution costs due to higher pick-up sales and lower fuel prices, EBITDA dropped by 29% YoY to P4.73bn and EBITDA margin contracted by 2ppts to 18% in FY20

INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide to issue up to 40mn pref shares
  • Megawide’s Board approved the offering of up to 40mn preferred shares
  • In line with this, the Board also approved to increase the authorized capital of Megawide to P5.080bn from P5.054bn, broken down as follows:
    • 4.93bn (unchanged) voting common shares with a par value of P1/sh
    • 150mn (previously 124mn) perpetual preferred shares with a par value of P1/sh
  • Last November 2020, MWIDE raised P4.36bn worth of preferred shares
  • We reckon the next tranche of pref shares could be used to redeem the 7.025% P4bn preferred shares due this year, which have a step-up (15-year PDST-R2 +4.875%)
  • We estimate MWIDE’s parent level gross debt (inc. prefs) is currently at P27.3bn and cash at P6.2bn
    • Net debt-to-equity at 2.96x

MVP Group eyes SPEx stake in Malampaya project
  • Manuel V. Pangilinan said that his group had submitted a non-binding offer for the stake of Shell Philippines Exploration BV (SPEx) in the Malampaya gas-to-power project
    • Offer for 45% share of SPEx made through First Pacific Co. Ltd. and Philex Mining Corp made last Friday (Feb 26)
  • Forum Energy PLC, a unit of Philex Petroleum holds the license for SC 72, an area southwest of Malampaya gas field
    • MVP previously said that if there is gas in SC72, the group can build a pipeline to connect it to the Malampaya facility
  • San Miguel Corporation (SMC) disclosed in September 2020 that it was interested in acquiring Shell’s stake
  • Udenna Corp has also expressed interest in acquiring the Shell stake
    • Udenna already holds a 45% stake in Malampaya (from Chevron)
