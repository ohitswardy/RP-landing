REGIS REPORT

15 December 2021

The government revised its macro assumptions/forecasts. The most notable changes were the higher forecasts for GDP and inflation in FY21. The big numbers for subsequent years were kept unchanged, suggesting a ‘status quo’ view on the situation, particularly on inflation, which is seen to fall next year. The Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor said he was in the camp of those who think inflation is “transitory”, not long before the US FED dropped that adjective.

If the BSP’s view remains unchanged and if that view informs BSP policy, the divergence of policy actions between it and the US Fed will have its consequences on the domestic markets.

MACRO
Philippines reports 235 new Covid-19 cases, lowest in nearly 19 months
  • The Philippines on Tuesday (Dec 14) reported 235 new Covid-19 cases, the lowest number since May 23, 2020 (180 infections)
  • Thursday’s cases bring the country’s total caseload to 2,836,868
  • 0.4% or 10,526 are active cases
  • The Department of Health recorded 10 deaths, bringing the death toll to 50,351
  • DOH reported a positivity rate of 0.9% out of 24,120 tests

MACRO
Gov’t revises macroeconomic targets
  • The  interagency Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) raised its 2021 GDP growth target to 5-5.5%
    • Higher than previous target of 4-5% given in August
    • Medium-term growth rates kept at 7-9% for 2022 and 6-7% for 2023/2024
  • 2021 inflation rate target was raised to 4.3%-4.5% from 2-4% previously; For 2022-2024, inflation forecast kept at 2-4%
  • Budget deficit targets were also revised downwards, after upward revisions in revenue targets and downward revisions in expenditure targets

BANKS
Notes from BDO call on recent hack/fraud incident
  • We hosted a call with BDO’s Chito Reyes (joined by Richard Tan and Katherine Tan) on 13 Dec to discuss the recent hack of BDO depositors’ online accounts and what remedial measures are being taken. We also discussed the bank’s IT investments in general, and updated on the business in general. Bottomline – the scale of the hack was quite small, and BDO’s new IT systems should be up and running soon. However, it does underscore the risks of cybercrime, especially as BDO embarks on a major digital push.
  • The bank downplayed the episode, saying less than 1,000 accounts had been affected. They would not disclose the total amounts, but said it was “not material”. Mr Reyes did say that the news reports ranged from P30,000 to P50,000 (roughly $600-$1,000) per incident. BDO has promised to reimburse all affected depositors. However, the bank is insured against cybercrimes, and will substantially recover the amount lost. It is unclear, though, whether the bank will be sanctioned/fined by the Bangko Sentral or other agencies for the data breach.
  • The hackers attacked deposits that were on a 10-year old system, which is in the process of being phased out. The new system is in beta-testing and is scheduled to go live in January. BDO claims they have closed the breach, and have advised all depositors to change their passwords for added security. No new breaches have been reported since. They are also reaching out to their customers, asking them to migrate to the new version of the app, which is on the new, more secure IT platform.
  • BDO has spent heavily on upgrading their IT systems in recent years. Much of the IT change-out was done earlier this year, but the transition has had to be phased in. Mr Reyes reckons they’re running at around P8-10bn a year on IT spend, and expects this level to be maintained at least in the next few years. Cybersecurity enhancement was the main thrust of the upgrade.
  • The hack comes at a crucial time for BDO, which is launching a suite of new products and services. These include their e-wallet BDO Pay, enhanced “cardless” ATMs, and upgraded in-branch kiosks helping transactions go paperless and more efficient. The episode underscores the balance required by digital adoption, between convenience and cost efficiency on one hand, and security on the other.
  • Otherwise business seems to be recovering nicely. Loan growth continues to accelerate, and Mr Reyes is hoping for a “strong finishing kick” in December, as is usually the case (ex-crisis years, of course). Management is looking at an initial estimate of 8-12% loan growth in 2022E. NIMs are forecast to rise heading into 2022 with a recent cut in CASA deposit rates. BDO continues to push its micro/SME, insurance, and wealth management businesses to drive fee incomes.
OTHERS
Market foreign ownership rises in November
  • We estimate market foreign ownership rose 18bps MoM to 21.1% while PSEi foreign ownership increased 30bps MoM to 25.6% (in November)
    • The increase in PSEi foreign ownership was boosted by strong price appreciation (c. 10% MoM) of ICT and GLO, which have high foreign ownership
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • GLO – saw the largest increase in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents (+34 bps MoM to 58.3%). Globe had among the highest net foreign buying for the month (US$29.72mn).
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 28 basis points MoM to 21.6%. Foreigners were net buyers for US$6.43mn for the month.
    • URC – foreign ownership fell 26 basis points MoM to 27.8%. URC had the second-highest net foreign selling for the month (US$31.83mn).
