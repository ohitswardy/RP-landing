REGIS REPORT

10 September 2021

With elections only eight months away, the government is pushing for the passage of third part of its tax reform program, this one involving reforms on property taxes. This law would reform the valuation system that forms the basis for property taxes across the country. Property valuation for taxation purposes is unevenly updated, with some areas not having valuations updated for more than two decades. 

If this is passed, it would bring in more revenue for both national and local governments who rely on property taxes for revenue. Property owners, will ultimately be the one paying more and those whose properties are most out of sync with current valuation will be affected most. Firms that have more idle land (i.e. large land banks) should be affected disproportionately more, so we suspect it is companies like Ayala Land (ALI PM), Filinvest (FLI PM) and Vista (VLL PM) who will be most affected by the tax increases.

This proposal was unveiled early during the Duterte administration but has taken a back seat to other more urgent and important tax legislation, including what is now called TRAIN and CREATE. With the May elections looming, the chances of this reform being enacted are becoming slimmer by the day.

Coal prices have continued to go up, notwithstanding environmental issues becoming an increasing concern and coming to forefront of investor and financial activism.

Spot Newcastle coal is now at US$177/MT, which is a third higher than what it was mid-year. No prizes for guessing who will benefit from this -- Semirara (SCC PM).

The clear losers, on the other hand, are those who use it. This includes the cement companies and power consumers as close to half of the grid uses coal as fuel.

Ayala Corp (AC PM) incorporated a new company for its endeavors in the logistics business. Logistics and healthcare are two “new” areas where AC is planning to invest more the coming years.

PROPERTY
Government pushing for reforms on real property taxes
  • The Department of Finance seeks to standardize the methodology in computing for real property valuation (for the purpose of computing real property taxes)
  • This is the third package of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Program
  • The House of Representatives already passed its version of the bill but the Senate has yet to act on this proposal
  • Assessed values used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas
  • Pre-COVID, real property taxes accounted for c. 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover
POWER
Newcastle now at US$177/MT
  • Thermal coal prices up +32% since end-June. Newcastle thermal coal prices are still surprisingly strong, with spot now 32% higher than at end-June at US$177/MT. This is tracking higher than our assumption of an average US$115/MT for FY21 and US$100/MT. The reason seems to be the same: tight supply (China cutting coal mine production and banning high-grade coal imports from Australia, supply disruptions in Australia, South Africa, and Colombia, and high global gas prices) amidst a rebound in demand. With the incoming winter season, the situation seems unlikely to abate anytime soon. 
  • +29% upside risk on SCC’s FY21E; FCF yield could now be as high as 23%. Assuming spot stays at US$177/MT for the rest of 2021, we estimate Newcastle prices could average US$134/MT this year compared to our current assumption of US$115/MT.  his could translate to a 29% uplift in our FY21 profit forecast for Semirara (SCC PM). We estimate SCC’s free cash flow yield could also rise to as much as 23% vs 19% on our current assumptions. This would also be almost double our base case div yield forecast of 12%.

  • With the removal of US$2.1bn in debts and a new business plan, Philippine Airlines (PAL) expects to book an operating income of US$220mn next year and US$364mn in 2023

  • Buona Sorte Holding Inc., the airline’s largest unsecured creditor, had agreed to waive the recovery of funding advances worth as much as US$358mn under the restructuring plan
  • Plan involved the return of 21 narrow and wide-body planes to lessors and lenders to bring its fleet size to 70 aircraft
    • It also cited the likely exit from unprofitable markets such as the US East Coast (New York) and London Heathrow
    • It also planned to boost US West Coast flights, expand international and codeshare partnerships and target growth markets such as China
    • PAL would also strengthen its Ninoy Aquino International Airport hub, maintain routes from Cebu and eventually expand in Clark International Airport.
