REGIS REPORT

14 July 2021

Remittances from overseas amounted to US$2.38bn in May, which is not a number to get too excited about (despite it being +13% YoY). Nonetheless, there’s positive signs with flows from the Middle East recovering with the rise in oil prices.

Meanwhile, the country’s gross international reserves (GIR) declined by US$1.1bn for June, settling at US$106bn. The Bangko Sentral (BSP) attributed the drop to a fall in gold prices and the payment of some foreign debt obligations. The current level of reserves is still near record levels, and gives the BSP ammunition to smooth fluctuations in the FX rate given the large moves recently.

+++

Pulse Asia released its latest survey of preferred presidential candidates and Sara Duterte remains ahead with 28% of those polled. Manila mayor Francisco Domagoso is a distant second with 14% in the multi-candidate survey. Sara Duterte’s lead looks formidable, all things considered, with the elections ten months away. Because there are no run-offs in the country’s elections, the opposition (or at least those who are not overtly with President Duterte and/or his daughter) will have to coalesce around one candidate to stand a chance.

+++

The foreign ownership levels in the PSE continue to drop, dipping ~40bp MoM. Among those who saw the biggest foreign outflow were the names that were deleted from the MSCI indices: Aboitiz Power (AP PM) and Megaworld (MEG PM).

MACRO
Remittances up 3% MoM, 13% YoY in May
  • Cash remittances from overseas Filipinos reached US$2.38bn in May, up 13% YoY and 3% MoM
    • In PHP terms, remittances rose 7% YoY; 3-month moving average growth rate at +10% YoY
    • May 2020 was still a low base due to lockdown
  • Sustained recoveries seen from USA, Asia, and Middle East; Europe and sea-based remittances still soft
  • Brings YT-May remittances up 6.3% YoY to US$12.3bn (in PHP terms, up +1% YoY)
    • BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 4% YoY in FY21 (US$31.1bn), implying that remittances should average US$2.68bn per month for the balance of the year

MACRO
GIR settles at US$106bn in June
  • The country’s gross international reserves dropped by US$1.1bn (-1.1% MoM) to US$106bn in end-June
    • Cited falling gold prices, debt payment of foreign currency obligations, and BSP’s forex operations
    • Covers 12.1 months of imports
    • However, this was 13.5% YoY higher than the end-June ’20 level of US$93.46bn
  • BSP expects GIR to rise to US$115bn by year-end

CONSUMER
Vehicle sales rise 2% MoM in June; Toyota sales up 4% MoM
  • CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales jumped 45% YoY and 2% MoM to 22,550 units in June
    • Brings 1H21 industry car sales to 132,767 units (+56% YoY)
  • Toyota sold 11,242 units, up 50% YoY and 4% MoM
    • Brings 1H21 sales 63,778 units (+79% YoY)
  • With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%)
    • To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales only need to average 6.7k units for the rest of the year
    • Our forecast assumes Toyota’s sales will reach 117k units, implying that monthly sales for the balance of 2021 should average 8,900 units per month
  • GTCAP is trading at a 5% discount to its market-based NAV of P634/sh

POLITICS
Dutertes top Pulse Asia Presidential/Vice-Presidential survey
  • In polls conducted by Pulse Asia from June 7 to 16, Sara Duterte topped the presidential survey at 28%, followed by Manila Mayor Isko Moreno at 14%, Ferdinand Bongbong Marcos at 13%, Grace Poe at 10% and Emmanuel Pacquiao at 8%
  • For the vice presidential survey, sitting President Rodrigo Duterte placed first at 18%, followed by Isko Moreno at 14%, Vicente Sotto at 10%, Ferdinand Bongbong Marcos at 10% and Emmanuel Pacquiao at 9%

TELCOS
PLDT, Smart lobby for lower regulatory frees
  • PLDT Inc and wireless subsidiary Smart Communications said the government needed to implement “affordable and reasonable regulatory” so more investments would be channeled toward building network infrastructure
  • Spurred by a recent hearing on the Open Access in Data Transmission Act hosted by Senate Economic Planning Office
  • Ibay, Smart vice president and head of regulatory affairs, said they could expand in areas lacking coverage via removal of tower fees, inspection fees and audit fees imposed by certain local governments
  • Smart also wants national government to begin charging uniform spectrum users fees based on a “per kHz per population”
    • This would replace the prevailing wireless broadband formula (per station, per kilohertz computation) which discourages deployment of more wireless facilities
OTHERS
PSEi foreign ownership falls in June
  • We estimate total market foreign ownership fell to 21.4% (-38bps MoM, -43bps QoQ) while PSEi foreign ownership dropped to 24.7% (-11bps MoM, -36bps QoQ) in June
  • 17 out of 30 PSEi constituents saw declines in their foreign ownership MoM
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were
    • AP – foreign ownership fell 150 basis points MoM to 2.5%. AP was removed from the MSCI Asia ex-Japan Index at the end of May but the drop in foreign ownership was only reflected in June. It was also one of the top foreign sold stocks in June, with net foreign outflow of US$15.3mn.
    • ALI – foreign ownership declined 127 basis points MoM to 33.6%. Ayala Corp purchased 1.4% of ALI shares outstanding (c. US$140mn) from foreign investors in June.
    • MEG – foreign ownership dropped 111 basis points MoM to 12.6%. MEG was removed from the MSCI Asia ex-Japan Index at the end of May but the drop in foreign ownership was only reflected in June. Net outflow in June was US$11.7mn, among the highest for month.
    • JFC – foreign ownership rose 57 basis points MoM to 24.0%. This was the highest increase among PSEi constituents. Net foreign inflow was US$17.4mn, the third highest for the month.
