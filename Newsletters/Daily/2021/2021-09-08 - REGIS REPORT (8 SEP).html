REGIS REPORT

08 September 2021

The government backtracked on its initial announcement that there will be a general easing of lockdown restrictions and more localized lockdowns. Instead, it declared that the restrictions as of yesterday (Sept 7) will be enforced until Sept 15. It appears the government was not ready with the rules for its new lockdown approach (and certainly not its implementation).

The re-opening of businesses will have to be pushed back a bit amidst a period where Covid-19 cases are hitting new peaks.

Meanwhile, the latest vaccination data showed the country administered just below 400,000 vaccines per day last week. This is below the government’s 500,000/day target again for the fourth week in a row. Nineteen percent of the total population (not just adult population) have now received at least one dose.

+++

Inflation spiked to 4.9% in Aug, driven by food prices. The price of meat, which we were expecting to soften already because of relaxed import rules, was the main driver of the jump. Despite the rise in CPI, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) doesn’t appear to be deviating from its loose money policies.

The other significant pieces of macro data yesterday were the unemployment numbers. Or should we say labor force participation. The headline unemployment rate dropped to 6.9% -- a level which looks even lower than the unemployment (%) figures pre-pandemic. This is by no means a cause for celebration, however.  

Sorry to be party-poopers, but the real story is that labor force participation dropped below 60%. In layman’s terms, the reason why “unemployment” is “low” is because many have stopped looking for jobs.

+++

There are two eyebrow-raising but not altogether surprising regulatory events of note today.

The first was the ruling of a local arbitration panel against the NLEX toll road operator (a subsidiary of Metro Pacific (MPI PM). The panel denied the claims of NLEX Corp for the “unreasonable delay” in its tariff adjustment. The tariff adjustments were straightforward, contractually-agreed, CPI-based adjustments that were supposed to be implemented in 2012 and 2014. One has to wonder: if a nine-year delay is not “unreasonable”, what is?

The second was the disclosure that the government said that Dito Telecommunity, a subsidiary of Dito CME (DITO PM), passed its mandated technical audit. Under the “audit”, Dito Tel was able to show 52.75% population coverage and minimum average broadband speed (wireless) of 89mbps and 854mbps for 4G and 5G respectively. Dito Tel was granted a license and spectrum on a number of conditions, including that it achieve 51% population coverage and minimum average speed of 55mbps by July 2021.

The results of the audit stand in stark contrast to the recently released results of OpenSignal, which showed average download speed of ~17mbps in the country’s largest urban centers. Go figure.

Gov’t defers GCQ for Metro Manila until after September 15
  • The Inter-Agency Task Force has postponed the implementation of the general community quarantine (GCQ) with alert levels in Metro Manila
    • Metro Manila will remain under modified enhanced community quarantine (MECQ) until Sep 15 or until the pilot GCQ with alert level system is enforced, whichever comes first
    • Indoor and outdoor or al fresco dining and personal care services like beauty salons, beauty parlors, and nail spas will remain closed in Metro Manila at least for another week
MACRO
Inflation jumps to 4.9% in August on higher food inflation
  • The country’s inflation rate accelerated to 4.9% in August from 4.0% the previous month
    • Fastest inflation recorded since January 2019
    • Higher than consensus forecast (+4.4%); Fell at the high end of the BSP’s monthly forecast range of 4.1%-4.9%
    • Due to higher price increases of food (+6.5%), specifically of meat (+16%), fish (+12%), and vegetables (+16%) on weather-related supply disruptions
    • For non-food items, inflation of fuel (+8%) and operation of transport equipment (+14%) also accelerated due to higher power generation/transmission prices
    • Core inflation (excluding selected food and energy items) picked up to 3.3% in August from 2.9% in July
  • YT-August average inflation rate at 4.4%, above the BSP’s target of 4.1% for FY21E
  • Latest CPI outturn is consistent with the BSP’s assessment that inflation could settle close to the high end of the target range (2-4%) in the near term before decelerating back to within the target by year end
    • In 2022-2023, inflation is expected to decline towards the midpoint of the target

MACRO
Labor force participation drops in July
  • Preliminary data from the Philippine Statistics Office showed the country’s total labor force dropped by 4mn MoM in July
    • Labor force participation rate dropped to 59.8% in July, lowest since October 2020 (58.7%) and below pre-pandemic level of 61.7%
  • The number of employed dropped by 3.4mn MoM, largely coming from agri and services sectors
  • Those who are looking for work (i.e. unemployed) also fell by 690k MoM, bringing the unemployment rate lower at 6.9%

MACRO
Pace of vaccinations inches up
  • As of September 5, the 7-day average vaccinations figure was 391,367/day
    • From 387,176/day the week before
  • 25mn doses are expected to arrive in September
    • Including about 1mn doses that already arrived
  • The Philippines has now received a total of 52.8mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 35.8mn doses (as of September 5)
  • The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021

TELCOS
Dito handily passes government technical audit
  • Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that its subsidiary Dito Telecommunity passed the July 2021 technical audit.  And if the reported results are any indication, Dito Tel passed it handily, without even having to break a sweat.
  • According to the government audit, Dito Tel had 75% population coverage and “minimum average broadband speed” of 89.13mbps and 853.96mbps for 4G and 5G, respectively.  Under the terms of the auction held three years ago, Dito Tel must achieve 51% population coverage and 55mbps minimum average speed. It appears to have demonstrated to the satisfaction of the Duterte government that it has provided a world-beating telecom network.
  • The results of the government technical audit stand in contrast to those published by OpenSignal a few days ago. Based on OpenSignal’s assessment, Dito’s average speed was only ~17mbps in the key urban areas.
INFRASTRUCTURE
Arbitration Tribunal rejects claims of NLEX on tariff petitions; Terminates Cavitex case
  • The Permanent Court of Arbitration denied the claims of NLEX Corp for the “unreasonable delay” of its 2012/2014 toll hike petitions
    • Toll Regulatory Board already decided on the said petitions in 2018, after it approved upward toll rate adjustments for NLEX that was implemented in March 2019
    • The Tribunal also ruled that each Party will bear the costs of arbitration in equal shares and will bear their own costs of legal representation and assistance
  • The Tribunal also terminated the claims of Cavitex Corp. and Philippine Reclamation Authority after the latter sought to withdraw the case
    • Tribunal said that tariff petitions are now being processed by the TRB
  • Arbitration decisions do not impact our estimates and valuation for Metro Pacific (MPI PM), as we do not assume tariff hikes
  • MPI’s tollroad unit accounts for c. 25% of MPI’s gross profit and c. 20% of gross asset value
  • MPI is trading at a 31% discount to its market-based NAV of P5.70/sh
