REGIS REPORT

10 November 2021

The government’s GDP estimate for 3Q provided a positive surprise, with a print of +7.1%. This is well above both Regis (4.0%) and Street estimates (4.9%). On the demand side, investments led the rebound, with Capital Formation up 22%, led by construction activity. Note that 3Q GDP last year (2020) contracted 11.6% (revised), so output for this year’s 3Q is still ~5% below what it was in 2019.

With mobility improving and the economy reopening in the recent weeks, the outlook for 4Q is looking more optimistic.

+++

On the results front, we have Cebu Air (CEB PM) today.

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) – Core losses widened QoQ to P6.4bn, bringing 9M21 losses to P20bn. The disappointment came from higher maintenance costs, as some previously parked aircraft returned to service ahead of a rebound in volumes. This more than offset the 15% QoQ improvement in pax revenues. We hope to get more detail after tomorrow’s briefing.

We also have additional detail on the results reported by Jollibee (JFC PM) and DMCI (DMC PM) yesterday.

For Jollibee, top line trends are clearly improving as the economy reopens. However, cost pressures are also evident. Cost inflation (raw materials, labor cost in the US) was the main culprit for the EBIT contraction in 3Q21 (-31% QoQ). JFC has cumulatively raised prices by c4% since 4Q20 and will likely have to do another round of price hikes in the next 1-2 quarters. For those interested to find out more, we are hosting the company’s earnings call on Friday at 8pm HKT.

DMC, on the other hand, saw 3Q21 results surprise to the upside with a YoY doubling of profit, thanks to strong coal and nickel prices, as well as improved residential completions. These offset the lackluster recoveries of its construction and water units. Among our key takeaways during their briefing were (1) relatively low cancellation rates for its property unit and (2) the intensifying competition in some infra-related construction biddings.

Davao City mayor Sara Duterte-Carpio, daughter of the President, announced she was no longer running for mayor and was withdrawing her re-election bid. The move is expected to presage an announcement that she will instead seek another post.

Sara Duterte, like her father before her, has repeatedly disavowed plans to seek the Presidency. Allies and opponents alike have largely not taken her disavowals at face value. She will have until Nov 15 to take the place of any candidate who’s previously filed his/her candidacy papers.

Converge (CNVRG PM) increased the speeds for its broadband service packages. Its entry level package, which goes for P1,500 (US$30) per month, now provide speed of 50Mbps.

MACRO
3Q GDP up 7.1%, beating forecasts
  • GDP grew +7.1% YoY in 3Q21, stronger than street (4.9%) and Regis (4.0%) forecasts
    • On a seasonally adjusted basis, 3Q GDP expanded by 3.8%
    • 3Q21 GDP was 9% below 2019 average
    • 9M21 GDP growth at 4.9%, tracking above Regis forecast (+4.5%)
  • Domestic demand was strong, up 11% YoY in 3Q
    • Consumption was resilient despite renewed lockdowns, up 7.1% YoY (broadly unchanged QoQ)
    • Investments also grew double-digits, with construction unhampered; Public and private construction rose 55%/12% YoY respectively
    • Net exports were higher, on strong import growth
  • On the supply side, industry and service sectors expanded but agri sector lagged (cited typhoons and African Swine Flu)

CONSUMER
JFC 3Q21 operating profit weighed by lockdowns, cost inflation
  • 9M21 net profit within expectations but core operations soft. JFC reported 9M21 headline net profit of P2.7bn. After coupons to perps, net profit was P1.8bn, tracking broadly within expectations. Operating profit, however, is behind at P3.8bn. For 3Q21 alone, net profit after perps was P1.3bn, nearly double QoQ. This is despite a 31% QoQ contraction in operating profit. Non-operating items (forex gains, taxes) resulted in a better bottomline trend relative to operating profit.

  • Lockdowns temper Philippines recovery. 3Q21 revenues jumped 24% YoY, partly due to base effects. SSSG in most markets have recovered to pre-pandemic levels, excepts for the Phils and Vietnam (SuperFoods), which were weighed by lockdowns during the quarter. The company is seeing a sequential recovery in these markets as lockdown restrictions are being lifted.

  • Cost inflation weighs on margins. The main drag on margins was cost inflation – specifically, higher input and packaging cost, and higher labor cost in the US. We estimate that Smashburger losses likely widened vs the previous quarter. Across the system, JFC has raised prices by around 4% since 4Q20 (effectively c1% per quarter). Prices of key inputs (beef, potatoes, etc) remain elevated, and, coupled with higher freight cost could lead to another round of price increases in 4Q.

Regis is hosting the company’s briefing on 12 Nov, Friday 8pm HKT (Registration link).

AIRLINES
Cebu Air: Maintenance costs jump, EBITDA losses wider QoQ
  • Core loss at P6.4bn in 3Q.  Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported a net loss of P8.2bn in 3Q21. Excluding FX losses and small one-offs, we estimate CEB’s core loss was wider QoQ at P6.4bn, taking 9M21 net losses to P20bn.  3Q21 EBITDA losses also widened QoQ to P2.6bn, despite a mild improvement in revenues. The results disappointed, as maintenance expenses remained significantly above pre-Covid levels.
  • Revenues up just 2% QoQ, as ancillary and cargo revs were soft.  9M21 pax volumes reached 1.9mn, implying pax volumes in 3Q21 alone improved 11% QoQ and were about 13% of pre-Covid levels.  Despite the QoQ improvement in pax vols and load factors, ancillary and cargo revs sequentially dropped 13% and 3%, respectively.
  • Maintenance costs jump 14% QoQ in 3Q.  Cash costs jumped 8% QoQ, with maintenance costs alone rising 14%. We reckon this could be partly due to some parked aircraft returning to service and needing maintenance. CEB’s fleet size dropped to 73 in end-September from 74 in June.  Given the current schedule of AC lease returns, we expect its fleet size will drop to 71 by year-end.

CONGLOS
DMCI: Semirara, property, nickel drive 3Q21 recovery; construction, water still lag
  • 3Q21 core profit more than doubles to P3.9bn. DMCI sustained its recovery in 3Q21, with core profit up 112% YoY to P3.9bn. Cumulative 9M21 profit stood at P12.2bn (+173% YoY), tracking ahead of Regis and consensus forecasts.

Profits for the quarter and 9M21 are above pre-pandemic levels, thanks to strong coal and nickel prices, better plant availability, and faster residential completions. However, profits for its construction and water units are still below pre-pandemic levels. We believe similar trends could take shape in succeeding periods, given elevated commodity and power prices.

  • Property: Opex spikes in 3Q; Lockdown affects 3Q reservation sales. Despite recognizing P135mn worth of income from forfeitures, DMCI Homes’ 3Q21 profit still dropped 14% YoY, as opex related to rising RFO units jumped. Revenues were up 4% YoY, as cancellations were low (<2% of FY20 revenues) and completions picked up. Unsold inventory jumped slightly QoQ to P40bn from P38bn, with reservations sales down 27% QoQ to P3.9bn. 9M21 reservation sales are still 40% below pre-Covid sales.
  • Construction unit still below pre-Covid. Profits from construction dropped QoQ, as completions for infra projects were bogged down by bureaucratic delays and weather disturbances. There was also a slight uptick in the order book in 3Q, owing to newly secured building contracts and change orders. While bids are still ongoing for some packages of the Metro Manila subway project and NSCR South Line, management noted that competition has intensified, with foreign contractors massively driving down bids.
  • Elevated prices buoy nickel profits; Off-grid units EBITDA up 20%/15%YoY in 3Q/9M. The nickel unit continued to benefit from elevated nickel prices, with 9M21 profit already exceeding our full-year forecast. Meanwhile, the off-grid power plants also saw EBITDA jump strongly YoY on higher power prices and volumes.
  • De-carbonization initiatives. The company is exploring various opportunities to diversify, including biomass, solar, and other renewables. However, initiatives here remain small and slow, in our view.
