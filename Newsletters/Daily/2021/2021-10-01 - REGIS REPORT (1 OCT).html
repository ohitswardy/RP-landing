REGIS REPORT

01 October 2021

An early sign of a turn in the pandemic crisis? Universal and commercial banks (UKBs) posted loan growth in August at +1.3% YoY the first positive YoY print since late 2020 (was -0.7% YoY in July). Loans were also up 1.1% MoM. Encouragingly, the Manufacturing and Trade sectors both showed strong MoM moves, up 3.7% and 1.6% respectively. We note that these increases were during the latest lockdowns. With loans on the uptrend, and with NPLs seeming to have levelled off (as of July), the banking sector looks to be at the early stages of a sustained earnings recovery.

To the downside, consumer loans remained sluggish – in particular, auto loans continue to contract, down 15.6% YoY and 2% MoM. However, banks’ reluctance to lend seems to have opened the doors to the likes of Toyota Financial Services (TFSI), part-owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM), whose loan book is now 15-20% the size of the total banking industry’s auto loan portfolio. The availability of funding has allowed Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) to increase its market share to 46% as of 8M21 compared to 40% in 2019.

+++

The unemployment rate rose to 8.1% in Aug, from 6.9% in July.

The numbers behind the headline, however, do not provide us much confidence about their accuracy. There seems to be an inexplicably large jump, for a single month, in the total population (15 years old and above) and total work force. Inaccuracies there would skew all the other data and ratios that come afterward.

+++

The House of Representatives approved the 2022 national budget submitted by the Executive Department without revisions, allowing it to be immediately sent for deliberations in the Senate.  The speedy approval is a ministerial step as revisions at the House are unlikely to matter much. Ultimately, it will be the bicameral committee (of select House and Senate members) that will write the final version of the budget.

As it stands, the 2022 national budget proposed by the Executive Department allocates P5.02trn of spending, equivalent to 23% of next year’s projected GDP (P22trn). Assuming things go as planned, the deficit/GDP would be 7.5%. The budget also includes a P272bn (US$5.4bn) increase in allocation for local governments, which is equivalent to a 3.5ppt increase in its share of the government budget.

Loans up 1.3% YoY in August; M3 growth accelerates to 6.9% in August
  • Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, increased by 1.3% YoY, 1.1% MoM in August
    • Up from 0.7% decline in July and was the first reported expansion in outstanding loans of U/KBs since late 2020
  • If similar trend for All Banks, loans could be already +1.5% in Aug (was -0.5% in July)
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 6.9% YoY to P14.4tn in August, faster than the 5.9% growth posted in July
