REGIS REPORT

20 October 2021

Philip Morris (PM US) disclosed overnight that its Philippine business (under the partnership with LT Group (LTG PM)) sold 8.9bn sticks in 3Q21.  This is up +5% QoQ and down 24% YoY.  Note the volumes in 3Q20 (last year) were lifted by trade loading ahead of the price hikes. While volumes are trending slightly short of estimates (4Q will be better), tobacco earnings are not.

The shares of LTG are up from the lows but valuations remain compelling, though the company’s businesses (tobacco and alcohol) are admittedly, not particularly ESG-friendly. The stock is on less than 4x PER with a dividend yield approaching 10%.  Its NAV of P30/share already reflects the bombed out price of Philippine National Bank (PNB PM). The impending receipt of the property dividend covering 29% of PNB’s choice property assets could add another P1/share to LTG’s visible value.

AC Energy (ACEN PM) is buying out its partner in its Australian solar project, through what is almost like a share swap.  While it is acquiring 51.6% of UPC/AC Renewables Australia for US$243m, it is also issuing 942m new shares to the selling parties for ~US$210m.

The headline valuation for this purchase of the Australian project/s (initial capacity of 937MW) translates to US$1m per MW (vs. our imputed valuation of US$700k/MW for this project). ACEN, on the other hand, is trading at US$4m/MW. This deal suggests a good trade for ACEN as it is using the richly valued currency of its own shares to buy assets at 1/4th of the relative value.

Media reported that PLDT (TEL PM) was building its 11th data center, a facility which is bigger than all its previous ten data centers combined. This facility is aimed at tapping the market for “hyperscalers” (jargon for the business from tech giants like Google, Facebook), which apparently has suddenly become a growth opportunity because of geopolitical tensions between China and the U.S..

PLDT revealed its desire to seek a strategic partner for its data center operation, which previous reports suggest could be worth US$500m.

+++

The government announced it was aiming to vaccinate 70% of the “target population” of adults by Feb 2022, which is roughly 50% of the total population. The revision is an acknowledgement that it will be unable to meet its goal of achieving the 50% threshold by end of the year.  Note that the country already received enough doses to fully vaccinate over 40% of the population by mid-Oct. Tens of millions of doses are still coming.  It is now a question of being able to put those shots into people’s arms.

In the meantime, case counts are continuing to fall, with new infections yesterday at below 5,000.  This will provide more impetus for easing of restrictions in the coming weeks.

Even before that, however, data from Google Mobility already seems show mobility rebounding from lows in August.

However, as of 12 October, the numbers were still about 2-3ppts below the July highs.  We would have expected movement to be better, considering that the rules are much more relaxed under the new “granular lockdown” scheme, particularly in Metro Manila.

To be fair, anecdotal evidence is there has been a surge after Metro Manila was placed under “Alert Level 3” (down from 4) only as of 16 October. News outlets reported, “People flock to malls” over the weekend, and there were long queues at bus stations with people heading to work. We also noted that restaurants were difficult to book / get into. Some resorts said that reservations were fast filling up as the government also relaxed inter-region travel restrictions.

That said, the Philippines continues to lag the recovery in mobility of our peers in the region. As the government steadily (if slowly) eases mobility restrictions, though, we hold to our “Glass half full” thesis, and would continue to advocate positioning in “reopening plays” in the country.

Government targeting to fully vaccinate 50% of population by February
  • The government hopes to fully vaccinate 50% of the total population by February

    • The pace of vaccinations would have to increase to c. 500,000/day
  • The country is currently administering only 375,000/day
    • At the current pace, 50% of the population will be vaccinated by April
  • As of October 18, 59% of the Metro Manila population (7.9mn people) has been fully vaccinated
  • The Philippines has received a total of 91.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 52.8mn doses (as of October 18)

INFRASTRUCTURE
Infrastructure spending rises 60% YoY in August
  • Gov’t infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 60% YoY to P70.9bn
    • On a MoM basis, infra spend slipped by 3%
    • Cited spending for public works and airport projects, as well as defense and education
  • Brings YT-August infra spend (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P570bn, up 44.6% YoY
    • This accounts for 75% of the government’s FY21E target of P761bn (+12% YoY)
  • YT-August overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 38% YoY to P718bn
    • Accounts for 70% of the government’s FY21E target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY)
    • For FY22, the government has programmed to spend P1.27tn on infra (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), up 25% YoY

CONSUMER
PMFTC volumes +5%QoQ, but down 24%YoY
  • Philip Morris International disclosed last night that cigarette volumes in the Philippines came in at 8.9bn sticks (-24%YoY, +5%QoQ).
  • Volumes of Marlboro and Chesterfield increased, while mid-price Fortune, low-priced Jackpot and More remained lower YoY
  • Net increased continuing to reflect a “favorable pricing variance”
  • Industry volumes are down less than PMFTC’s volume YoY, but unit market share is appearing to stabilize after PMFTC’s 5%QoQ volume growth
  • Unit market share improved marginally to 61.8% in 3Q21 from 61.6% in 2Q21, first uptick after 7 quarters of decline
  • PMFTC FY21E profit – P37.4bn (+12%YoY)
  • LTG FY21E profit – P24.2bn (+2%YoY)
  • LTG is trading at 3.9x FY22E PE and a 69% discount to market NAV of P31.50 (including P1.1/share impact from the PNB Holdings property dividend)
  • Despite the soft volumes, we expect earnings from PMFTC to be slightly higher this year than 2020, which means the absolute value of dividends from the tobacco unit (and consequently the dividends that LTG pays out) would be unaffected
  • Our base case is for a dividend of P0.82 ever year (8% div yield)
