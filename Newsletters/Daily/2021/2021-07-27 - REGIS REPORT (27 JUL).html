REGIS REPORT

27 July 2021

President Duterte delivered his annual State of the Nation Address (SONA) yesterday, with bulk of it devoted to an enumeration of his administration’s alleged accomplishments, sprinkled with the usual threats.

Among the few forward-thinking messages in the SONA was his call for the passage of three pieces of legislation to further liberalize the entry of foreign participants in the country’s businesses. Arguably the most significant one is the proposal to amend the Public Services Act, which could lift the foreign ownership limit on telecom companies and possibly pave the way for China Telecom taking full control of new wireless operator Dito.

Related to the pandemic, he called for the establishment of a center for disease control and a virology institute. Notably, there was no call for any stimulus to help boost a post-pandemic recovery.

+++

The total number of vaccinations administered in the week ending July reached 300,000.  This is a further improvement from the 271,000 the previous week. Vaccinations need to ramp up to 500,000/day to match the arrival of doses.

+++

Power utility Meralco (MER PM) reported 1H21 core income of P11.4bn on the back of a 7% increase in electricity volumes. 2Q volumes leaped 18% YoY, off a depressed 2Q20 base. The company expects volumes to be 7% higher this year, which would be slightly off our assumptions (+9%).  More detail below.

+++

Globe’s (GLO PM) fintech arm Mynt says it aims to achieve a US$2bn valuation on its next fund-raising round.  This would value Globe’s ~40% stake at US$800m, compared to its current market cap of US$5bn.  Based on its last funding round a little more than half a year ago, Mynt achieved a valuation of close to US$900m.  We don’t expect a new funding round until at least next year, however.

+++

We published our the key highlights from our recent event with First Gen (FGEN PM) president Giles Puno.  From our perspective, the most important takeaway is that power supply will be tight in the medium-term. Not only are old plants breaking down, fuel supply from Malampaya is on the wane and new projects (renewable or not) will likely be unable to meet demand adequately and on time. This implies that FGEN’s natgas-fired facilities will not be in danger of being displaced.

MACRO
Duterte 2021 SONA: Key highlights
  • Accomplishments. Given that this was his last SONA (at least as of now), President Duterte highlighted his accomplishments during his six-year term, including raising salaries for cops and soldiers, as well as the passage of laws providing free tertiary education, universal healthcare, free irrigation, rice tariffication, and the tax reform programs. Unsurprisingly, he spoke at length about his fight against drugs/criminality and corruption, as well as his usual rants about the case against him at the ICC and his stance on having a cordial relationship with China over the country’s rights on the West Philippine Sea.  He noted that he knew he had limited time to accomplish things because the constitution constrained him to a single six-year term.
  • For the remainder of his term, he urged Congress to pass priority bills: Foreign investments Act, Public Service Act, Retail & Trade Liberalization Act. He also reiterated his call for Congress to establish a single agency that will focus on OFWs. 
  • Build! Build! Build! He flaunted his administration’s infrastructure program, raising infrastructure spend-to-GDP ratio to a record high of over 5%. He claimed that his administration was able to solve problems on public transportation, with much shorter waiting times for the MRT-3 (from 10 trains at 30km per hour to 23 trains at 60 km per hour) and reductions in travel times with the completion of the LRT-2 East extension project, Skyway 3, BGC-Ortigas Link bridge.  He noted that he deliberately asked his economic managers to prioritize projects outside of Davao (eg. Davao Coastal road project will only be completed in 2023).  He directed line agencies to be on full speed to ensure flagship projects will be completed within his term, especially those outside Metro Manila.
  • Attacks on water companies/ABS-CBN anew.  He renewed his attacks against the Metro Manila water concessionaires and lauded that the new water contracts will be able to protect consumers at least until 2037.  He admitted that he has not thoroughly digested the new water contracts yet so he has not signed it.  He also accused ABS-CBN again for not paying right taxes on imported equipment and real estate. He threatened to reassign ABS-CBN’s radio frequencies. 
  • Pandemic.  President Duterte thanked the private sector for supporting the government during the pandemic. He committed to “assess” the private sector to regain the commercial vibrance of the country prior to the pandemic.  He indicated that the country cannot afford more lockdowns, but if the Delta variant causes another surge, he will not hesitate to lock down the country again.  He also urged local governments to hasten inoculations and urged Congress to enact a law to create a center for disease control and a Phil. Virology Institute so Philippines can make its own vaccines in the future.  He highlighted the recently signed Executive Order for an employment recovery strategy, which aims to create over 1mn jobs, re-tool workers for better job opportunities by providing online training. 
UTILITIES
Meralco 1H +8%YoY to P11.4bn
  • Core income for 1H of P11.4bn (+8%).  Meralco reported core income of P11.4bn for 1H21, up +8% YoY, which is still slightly behind trend for our   Implied core income for 2Q was P6.29bn (+23% YoY). While the results now include Globe Business Power (GBP), we would describe them as being slightly lower than expected.
  • Electricity sales climb 19% in 2Q.  The YoY rise in electricity sales for 2Q alone was 19%, taking 1H21 to +7%.  While this sharp jump can be attributable to the low base in 2Q20 (which was the height of the lockdowns), Meralco took pains to point out that 2Q21 electricity sales were almost at 2Q19 levels (pre-pandemic).  The company expects volumes to be up 7% for the year, slightly below our assumed 9%.
  • Dividend of P5.06/share declared, net cash dips.  The company declared a dividend of P5.057/share with respect to 1H21 income, taking the dividends so far this year to P12.9/share, which is still a tad below our forecast (P15/share). Net cash (including investments) was P19bn as of June, a slight dip from the P25bn in March.  Net cash is much lower YoY because of the purchase and consolidation of Global Business Power.
  • Regulator has not changed its rate-reset target schedule (yet).  There has been no visible progress on Meralco’s long overdue rate review but the ERC has not (yet) changed its target reset date of July 2022. We would be very surprised if the reset was actually completed by that date. Meralco is considering proposing a 12% return in line with what was granted to the water distribution concessionaires.
  • Enough supply? Meralco says it believes it is planning for and has contracted enough power supply for its needs for the medium term. The company having planned for enough contracted capacity and their actual being enough capacity, however, are two very different things.
  • Short-term, Meralco pointed out that the Malampaya will be on maintenance for a few weeks later this year.  The First Gen plants that use Malampaya fuel will run on liquid fuel in the interim. The 1200MW Ilijan plant controlled by San Miguel, however, will have to source replacement power to supply Meralco. We reckon this could result in another supply crunch during the period. This situation will likely result in an outcome that will clearly show why Meralco’s statements of having planned for adequate supply may not sync with realities on the ground.

 

BANKS
BPI briefing notes

 

Key message was that earnings are on the upswing.

  • Loan demand is back, initially from consumers and SMEs, and corporates should bring total loans to flat YoY, from -4.5% at 1H21.
  • NIMs are down, but have likely bottomed; management is guiding for -10bps for the full year, from -31bps in 1H21.
  • Fees have been extremely strong, and are likely to remain firm into 2H21 (and beyond?)
  • Opex is under control, with rationalization of branches and headcount on-going. Much of opex growth will come from IT spend.
  • BPI aims to be the leader in digital banking, whether through its own systems, or through partnerships with other online/e-wallet platforms.

Loans

  • Loans contracted 4.5% in 1H21, driven by corporates (-6.3%) and SMEs (-5.8%). Consumer loans were mixed, with contractions in auto (-3.7%) and personal loans (-10.2%). To the upside, there was strong recovery in mortgages (+16.3%), microfinance (+15.5%), and credit cards (+6.1%)
  • New loan releases for consumer loans for the month of June are already close to the pre-Covid levels.
  • On mortgages, though, it was noted that the main driver was from receivables bought from property developers.
  • Corporates have begun inquiring, and BPI is optimistic that corporate loan demand will recover in 2H21.
  • Mgt is guiding for flat loans YoY for the full-year (down from previous guidance of +5%)
  • Mgt also said it will likely get more aggressive in loans for top-tier corporates

NIMs

  • BPI estimates that NIMs were 3.32% in 2Q down 17bps YoY, but up marginally from 3.31% in 1Q21.
  • Mgt is hopeful that NIMs have bottomed, and are guiding for FY NIMs -10bps YoY
  • Asset yields are rising. With a steeper yield curve, BPI is looking to gap out its fixed income portfolio.
  • Loan yields are also rising for most consumer products. And, as consumer loans recover faster, NIMs will benefit from better loan mix.
  • Corporate loan yields are still down. But BPI reckons it can still get 200-250bps yield pickup if it is able to get good-quality corporate loans, particularly if they can get LT loans.
  • Funding costs will also come down. BPI has a relatively high-yield bond maturing in 2H, which it doesn’t intend to roll over (lots of liquidity).
  • BPI is also mindful of the gap in deposit costs vs BDO/MBT. BPI is rationalizing its deposit products, particularly the “special savings” accounts where BPI pays close to TD rates.

Fees

  • Fee incomes were very strong, up 37.7% in 1H21, and up 49.5% YoY in 2Q alone. Granted, 2Q20 was a low base, but fees are tracking better than pre-pandemic levels. Growth was seen across all (non-trading/fx) products. Notable increases in 1H21 came from asset management (+25.8%), service charges (+37.8%), and transaction banking (+41.2%).
  • New fee growth drivers included ATM and digital channels (102.8%), as the BSP approved higher fees for withdrawals from ATMs outside a depositor’s home bank’s network. Investment banking and stock brokerage jumped 70.1%, driven by IPOs (Monde) and follow-ons (ACEN), as well as much higher retail participation.

Opex

  • Opex was up 3% in 1H21, but up 8.8% YoY in 2Q alone.
  • Headcount was down 7% YoY to 19,782, but employee costs were flat due to union negotiated increases.
  • Branches were reduced by 24 to 846 (BPI + BPI Family Bank), with another 34 identified for “co-location”. BPI expects up to 135 branch closures over two years. However, premises expense was actually up slightly YoY as more branches are now fully open, and rent concessions have ended.
  • IT spend is up, though. Moving forward, BPI intends for IT spend to hit 10% of revenues (was 8.5% in 1H21).

Asset quality

  • NPL ratio was 2.94%, up just 18bps QoQ. The mild increase was due to a combination of slower new NPL formation, and write-offs of credit card and personal loans.
  • Based on their ECL models, Stage 2 loans were down 32% QoQ, mostly driven by improving corporate loan quality.
  • Despite the positive surprise, management is keeping its guidance of 4% peak NPLs and provision expense of P!5bn (was P6.5bn in 1H21).
  • Restructured Loans were at 1.88%, up from just 1.13% at end-2020. Mgt said the bulk of the increase came from the hospitality, construction and real estate sectors (including a mid-sized property developer).

Digital strategy

  • BPI maintains its goal of becoming the “undisputed leader” in digital banking.
  • Account opening can now be done 100% online. In June/July alone, the bank opened more than 33,000 accounts digitally. This was even before the formal launch of the product (in the coming weeks). BPI claims its technology can detect fake IDs, etc.
  • Launching new app for BanKo, and a separate app for HNW clients.
  • BPI’s “e-gov” product – clients can pay gov’t fees online – is now the largest among the banks.
  • BPI is partnering with e-commerce, e-wallet, and other platforms. Apart from G-Cash, they are also in talks with PayMaya, and will soon offer products on Shopee. Products on these 3rd party platforms will include credit cards, personal loans, and deposits. Platforms will be paid on a per-subscriber acquired basis, and/or a fee sharing scheme.
  • BPI will launch its own e-wallet product in 2H21
