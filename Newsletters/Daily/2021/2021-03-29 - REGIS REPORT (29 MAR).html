REGIS REPORT

29 March 2021

It’s a bad-news-good-news sorta day. In true Regis fashion, we start with the bad news.

Groundhog day. “NCR-Plus” – Metro Manila and 4 neighboring provinces – have been placed under the strictest lockdown called Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ) from today until 4 April. The rules include:  a) severely restricted movement within, into and out of the NCR-Plus “bubble”; b) curfew from 6pm to 5am; c) limited public transport; d) closure of retail, except for groceries, pharmacies and hardware; and e) restaurants are only open for take-away and delivery.

The move was in response to a continued surge in Covid-19 cases, hitting close to 10,000/day over the weekend (from around 2,000/day in Jan/Feb). As we pointed out last Friday, hospital Covid bed occupancy has jumped, and many hospitals are at capacity. The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) will decide whether to ease or extend the ECQ status.

In a report last month, we noted that mobility data has been quite disappointing after the jump in December, posing downside risk to our economic and earnings forecasts. The numbers showed even slower numbers in March, even before the “bubble” and ECQ announcements. Recent bottom-up data (loan growth -2.7%, toll road traffic -13%, electricity sales -4%, etc) points to another negative GDP print in 1Q21.

We downgrade our FY2021E GDP growth forecast to 5.5%. Our previous 7.0% target assumed a gradual relaxation of movement restriction, and a slow ramp-up of overall economic activity. The new lockdowns likely push back our recovery story well into 2022E.

 

 

+ + +

Some good news though! The CREATE Act was signed into law on Friday, though the President issued a lengthy comment, vetoing a number of provisions. To recall, the key feature of the law will be the reduction in corporate income taxes from 30% to 25%, retroactive to July 2020. This should add 5-7% on average to corporate profit forecasts, all else equal. As with most things, it’s a bit more complicated. We will have to wait for the implementing rules to determine how the law will treat 2H20 taxes, considering the deadline for filing is on 15 April. It is also unclear how it will treat Deferred Tax Assets (DTA) – this could mean a one-time write-down for companies with high DTAs like banks and Meralco.

+ + +

The government says it has finalized the new contract proposal for Manila Water (MWC PM). To recall, the President attacked the water concessionaires MWC and Maynilad in late 2019 for “onerous” provisions in their contracts. As we understand it, the new deal brings the regulatory framework closer to a standard franchise. Government expects MWC to sign the deal in the coming days. After that, we expect a shorter negotiation period with Maynilad.

MACRO
President Duterte signs CREATE into law but vetoes some provisions
  • Tax Package Two (also called CREATE) was signed into law by President Duterte, though he vetoed certain provisions
  • The corporate income tax rate will drop
    • For small business – from 30% to 20% (retroactively from July 2020)
    • For other companies – from 30% to 25% (retroactively from July 2020)
  • It will also streamline the government’s fiscal incentives system to make it time-bound
    • New export projects (including BPOs) – can avail of incentives for 14-17 years (from perpetuity currently)
    • Existing export projects (including existing BPO operations) – can continue to avail of existing incentives for another 10 years
  • Among the provisions that were vetoed was the proposed increase in the threshold for VAT-free property purchases from P2.5mn to P4.2mn
    • Thus, the threshold for VAT-free property purchases will remain at P2.5mn
MACRO
Metro Manila and adjacent provinces on stricter lockdown
  • Metro Manila, Bulacan, Rizal, Cavite, and Laguna have been placed on Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ), the tightest lockdown
  • March 29 until April 4
  • There will be a curfew from 6PM to 5AM
  • Only one member per household is allowed to leave the home to procure essential goods
  • Only essential industries (such as medical services) are allowed to operate at full capacity
  • Most businesses are only allowed to have a skeleton workforce on site 
  • Groceries, pharmacies, and hardware stores are allowed to operate in malls
  • Restaurants can only serve delivery and take-out
  • Public transport allowed to operate at 50% of capacity 24 hours a day, following one-seat-apart rule
  • Presidential Spokesperson Harry Roque said that the government will provide financial aid
MACRO
BOP posts US$2.02bn deficit in February; end-Feb GIR revised down to US$105bn
  • The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$2.02bn in February 2021
    • A reversal from the US$839mn surplus recorded in Feb 2020 and a larger BOP deficit than in January 2021 (US$752mn)
    • Cited national government repayment of foreign loans and the trade deficit
  • This brings YT-Feb BOP to a deficit of US$2.771bn (vs. deficit of US$516mn in the same period last year)
  • The final gross international reserves level for end-February stood at US$105.16bn, lower than the preliminary figure of US$109.1bn
    • Lower than in end-January 2021 of US$108.67bn
    • Buffer is equivalent to 12 months’ worth of imports

Pilipinas Shell core income recovers to P400mn for FY20
  • Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) improved its core income to P400mn in FY20, which implies 4Q20 income jumped to P1.1bn (from P200mn in 3Q20)
  • The headline net loss for the FY20 amounted to -P16.2bn, inclusive of inventory losses (P4.8bn) and refinery one-offs (P11.8bn)
  • Margin improvement came on back of opex/capex savings as a result of the refinery shutdown
  • Marketing volumes were down 22%YoY vs -17%YoY in September
