REGIS REPORT

04 October 2021

President Duterte announced he was retiring from politics and would no longer run for Vice President in next year’s elections. Instead, his longtime Man Friday, Christopher “Bong” Go will run in his place. As recently as last week, Duterte accepted the nomination of his political party to be the VP candidate next year.

He also said his daughter Sara will now run for the Presidency. Sara Duterte previously said she will not seek the Presidency if her father was also running (for the VP post).

Sara Duterte already filed her candidacy for the mayoralty of Davao City. That candidacy, however, can be withdrawn. The deadline for candidate substitution is mid-Nov, so a candidate will have, in theory, until that time to make up his (or her) mind.

+++

The breathtaking surge in coal prices because of demand from China has been hogging international business headlines the past few days. International coal prices were already on a tear but the apparent panic-buying pushed prices up another >50%. Regional coal prices are now more 230% higher versus the same time last year.

It doesn’t take a genius to spot the beneficiary from this: Semirara Mining (SCC PM). Notwithstanding its de-rating, the stock’s been one of the best performing names this year (+62% ex- dividends). And if coal prices hold where they are (~US$200/ton), the stock would still be on 3x PER only next year. Note that SCC’s dividend payout is among the highest in our coverage so we expect much of the profits could be distributed as divs.

If there are winners, there will also be losers. Consumers of coal will be losers and among them are the cement companies. More than half of cement variable cost is energy so a margin squeeze looks inevitable for Cemex Philippines (CHP PM).

The rise in coal prices will also have a direct impact on electricity prices. The trebling of coal cost will push up wholesale prices by over 50% for coal plants, who produce ~40% of power. This is separate from the impact of rising gas prices from Malampaya, which are indexed to oil. Note that, despite the surge in local natgas prices, we are now faced with an unusual situation where coal-fired power is more expensive than natgas-fired power.

Headline inflation will thus see unavoidable upward pressure in the coming months. Electricity, gas and fuel account for 7.4% of the CPI basket.

+++

Asset quality remained surprisingly benign in August, despite the lockdowns in Metro Manila.

Banking industry Past Due (PD) ratio was flat, at 5.32% in August, compared to 5.31% in July (and still lower than the 5.56% peak in April). In nominal terms, PDs were up about 1.0% MoM. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was also flat at 4.66%, up just 1bp MoM from the 4.65% in July. In nominal terms, NPLs were also up about 1% MoM. Restructured loans (RL), too, were up just 1bp Mom to 3.07%. These data are consistent with anecdotes from banks that the lockdowns did not result in material deterioration in loan collections.

Loan growth, meanwhile, turned positive for the first time since November 2020. Loans (ex-interbank) were up 1.3% YoY, compared to the 0.5% contraction posted in July. On a MoM basis, loans were up 0.8%.

(Relatively) benign asset quality could pose significant upside earnings risk for banks’ earnings. Metrobank (MBT PM) could be the biggest potential beneficiary, having the lowest NPL ratio (2.30%), lowest RL ration (0.5%), and by far the highest NPL cover (179%) among banks we cover as of 2Q21. POWER
China's Power Crisis: Impact on PH coal + cement sectors

Jefferies’ commodities team published a report discussing the ongoing power shortage in China. Here are the key takeaways:

  • Global coal shortage worsening, causing power plants in China to shut down. China is running out of coal, prompting blackouts as power plants are forced to shut down. Global demand for coal is up sharply (due to lack of nat gas and higher industrial demand, and winter season) and this has coincided with various supply bottlenecks (i.e. mis-timed global de-carbonization, unofficial ban on coal imports from Australia to China, supply disruptions in South Africa, Colombia, and Indonesia).
  • Newcastle thermal coal price at a new record high of US$196/MT. The last contract price for Newcastle coal (re-priced every Monday) stood at US$196/MT, up sharply from US$136 in end-June and up 237% from a year ago (US$58/MT). Thermal coal futures hit an all-time high of US$218/MT yesterday, indicating next Monday’s contract price could break out above US$200/MT.
  • Semirara (SCC PM) is the clearest beneficiary. Newcastle thermal coal is the main coal price benchmark for Semirara’s coal and the latter’s price is typically discounted for its low quality. Assuming Newcastle averages at US$135/MT, higher than our base case forecast of US$115/MT, SCC’s FY21E profit could rise by 30%, putting its PER at 5.7x. Assuming Newcastle prices stay at US$196/MT in 2022 (vs base case of US$100/MT),SCC’s FY22 profit could more than double, implying a PER of 3.0x.   

Majority (>95%) of Semirara’s power supply contracts have mechanisms for full fuel pass-through. At today’s Newcastle thermal coal price (US$196/MT), we estimate SCC’s contract price could already be close to P6/kwh.

  • Thermal coal supply shock could lead to tighter supply of cement in the region, hence higher prices. Interestingly, the coal shortage is also forcing cement plants in China to shut down. With cement demand unchanged, Johnson Wan believes China’s domestic cement prices could rise. Johnson did not discuss the potential of higher cement imports to China but this could be a foregone conclusion, if China’s domestic cement production is curtailed.
  • PH cement companies could raise prices too. The tighter cement market in China could prompt cement prices in the region to rise and increase the landed cost of Philippines’ cement imports. This should allow local cement manufacturers to raise prices and offset rising input costs. However, we believe risks are still skewed to the downside, as we believe domestic cement demand in the next 6-12 months could be sluggish or even drop with no new investments in the property sector and potential slowdown in government infra spend.
