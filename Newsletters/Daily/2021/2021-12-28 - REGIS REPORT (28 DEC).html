REGIS REPORT

28 December 2021

The pace of vaccinations inched up slightly the past week to 644k/day. A total of 47.2m individuals have now been ‘fully vaccinated’ according to government records, equivalent to 43% of the total population.

With the recent arrival of a large number of vaccines, the country has now received 203m doses to date, of which only 105m have been administered. The stockpile of almost 100m doses is now enough to fully vaccinate practically the entire remaining unvaccinated population. If the pace of vaccination does not speed up dramatically, many of these doses will end up unused and discarded after their “best before” dates.

+++

The Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) Monetary Board is imposing a maximum lending rate for finance firms, including online platforms. The amount covered is small (no more than P10,000 or US$200). While the rate ceiling is still pretty generous (6% nominal rate per month), it shows the BSP stepping into an area that it has not, until recently, gone into in the past: rate caps.

+++

The energy regulator issued a certificate of compliance covering the first unit of GN Power Dinginin (majority owned by Aboitiz Power (AP PM)), effectively allowing the commercial operations of the 668MW Unit. This comes more than eight years after the plant was first announced and more than two years after it initial target operation date.

The entry of GNPD Unit 1 will go some way in easing the supply situation during the dry summer months next year but supply will remain precarious over the next few years.

+++

The Ligo brand of sardines that Century Pacific Foods (CNPF PM) is acquiring is actually smaller than we initially thought, after recent market share losses. Nevertheless, the logic behind the deal remains the same. Consolidation in this rather fragmented business would confer benefits in terms of scale and pricing power.

BANKS
MB approves rate cap on lending firms
  • Last December 16, the Monetary Board issued Resolution 1717 approving the cap on interest rates and other fees charged by lending firms, financing companies and online lending platforms pursuant to the provisions of the Lending Company Regulation Act of 2007 or the Financing Company Act of 1998
  • Diokno said these laws empower the Monetary Board the prescribe maximum interest rates that could be charged by lending and financing companies in consultation with the SEC if warranted by economic and social conditions
  • The BSP has set a nominal interest rate ceiling of 6% per month for covered loans
  • Effective interest rate was pegged at a maximum of 15% per month – including applicable charges such as processing, handling and other fees
  • Regulator also set a 5% per month ceiling on penalties for late payment
  • The BSP chief pointed out that the ceilings on interest rates and fees is subject to periodic review by the BSP, SEC and the industry
