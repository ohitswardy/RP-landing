REGIS REPORT

12 July 2021

We published a new report on the Philippine property sector over the weekend.

As the title “Clouds and silver linings” suggests, we believe the prospects of the industry are neither completely rosy nor very bleak. The pandemic has hit the sector hard and the impact will be felt for a while across all the key sub-sectors: retail, office and residential property. We think the worst is not behind us for some of them. Nevertheless, there are glimmers of recovery.

Retail property (i.e. shopping malls) has been hardest hit but will likely be the first to recover. The revival of residential sales, however, will likely be sluggish given the wall of mortgage NPLs (non-performing loan) with the banks. On the other hand, the office sector will be struggling for a while, as we expect vacancy rates to further worsen from here. Not only has demand has shrunk due to POGO exits, supply is still growing,.

Among the positive catalysts that may help support share prices (albeit not the underlying operations) is the rush of planned REITs from the major developers. Our new report examines these and shows the potential impact and the sensitivity to cap rates.

In our view, Robinsons Land (RLC PM) offers the best risk-reward in the sector right now. Among the more liquid names, we prefer Ayala Land (ALI PM) due to its exposure to the more resilient high end-residential segment and because it should benefit from upcoming infrastructure.

MACRO
Trade deficit widens in May
  • The country’s trade deficit doubled YoY to US$2.75bn in May
    • However, this was smaller than the revised US$3.08bn gap in April 2021
    • Brings YT-May trade deficit to US$12.6bn, up 30% YoY
  • Imports rose by 48% in May, as fuel imports surged and capital/consumer goods recovered
  • Exports jumped 30% YoY, as electronics exports rose 25%

  • The Philippines received 2.2mn vaccine doses over the weekend
    • 2.028mn AstraZeneca doses from COVAX
    • 170,000 Sputnik V doses
  • The Philippines expects to receive 16mn COVID vaccine doses in July and 14mn in August
    • Including 3.3mn doses that already arrived in July
  • The Philippines has now received a total of 20.6mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 12.5mn doses
  • The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021 

MACRO
Gov’t allows outdoor activities for kids
  • Children as young as five years old can now go outdoors, as long as they are residing in areas under GCQ and MGCQ
    • Areas under GCQ “with heightened restrictions” are not covered by the new policy (e.g. Laguna and Cavite)
  • Mixed-use indoor or outdoor buildings and facilities like malls and similar establishments are not included in the allowed outdoor areas for children
  • Under the previous IATF-EID policy, only persons aged 15 to 65 years old are allowed to leave their residences
BANKS
Asset quality deteriorates in May
  • The Philippine banking system’s NPL ratio (ex-interbank loans) of rose to 4.63%, up 15bps MoM and 90bps ytd. In absolute terms, NPLs increased 3.4% MoM to P479bn. Every other major asset quality indicator also deteriorated in the month. Past Due (PD) ratio was up 17bps MoM to 5.56%, Restructured loans (RL) ratio increased 20bps MoM to 2.47%, and NPL coverage fell to 80.0%, down 153bps MoM.
  • The May NPL numbers should already fully incorporate the Bayanihan 2 loan grace periods which officially ended in February. However, they would not yet include asset quality issues from the April lockdowns in Metro Manila and surrounding provinces.
  • The one silver lining is that the loan contraction seems to be moderating. Loans (ex-interbank) in May were down 1.9% YoY, better than the -2.6% in April and -4.0% in March. Loans were flat MoM at P10.34bn.
  • It remains to be seen if the deterioration was sector-wide, or if it again was concentrated on a few banks. In 1Q21, the banking industry’s NPLs were up 59bps QoQ. But for the banks we cover – BDO, MBT, BPI and SECB – NPLs were broadly flat (marginally up/down) QoQ.
