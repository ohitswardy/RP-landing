REGIS REPORT

26 April 2021

BPI (BPI PM) and BDO (BDO PM) showed strikingly similar trends in their 1Q21 results. Benign NPLs, and lower provisions boosted profits, making up for contracting loans and NIM squeeze. PPOP for both were broadly in line. If asset quality remains this low, it could provide significant upside for our forecasts. We are wary, though, of another round of NPLs with the rising Covid cases and renewed lockdowns.

BDO (BDO PM) (+) – headline NPAT of P10.4bn was up 19% YoY, ahead of REGIS and consensus. The “beat” was due largely to provision expense of just P2.9bn, or a credit cost of about 50bps (annualized), due in turn to benign NPLs of 2.81%, up just 16bps QoQ. Loans contracted 1% YoY and NIMs dropped by about 20bps QoQ. Fee incomes recovered nicely, bringing PPOP in line with our expectations. The bank will not hold a briefing, but you can sign up here to our call with management tomorrow, 27 April at 4pm.

BPI (BPI PM) (+) – NPLs were up just 8bps QoQ to 2.76%, prompting the bank to book much lower provisions than we had forecast (P3.6bn equivalent to 100bps credit cost). Management noted NPLs improved across most segments except for credit cards and unsecured personal loans. But they are holding to their 4-6% peak NPL expectations, and brought NPL coverage up to 124% (from 115% at end-2020). Loans were down 5% YoY, and NIMs fell about 15bps QoQ. As we had warned, though, BPI booked a one-time write-off of P2.7bn in deferred tax assets (DTAs) due to the CREATE Act.

Citibank recently announced it will be pulling out of the consumer banking business in 13 countries, including the Philippines. Both BPI and BDO have publicly expressed their interest in acquiring the business. In our conversations with management, SECB also said they would take a look, as they have always been open to inorganic growth opportunities. We would not be surprised to see MBT say the same. According to its end-2020 SOC, the local operation had total loans of P99bn. We estimate that Citi’s credit card receivables portfolio alone at around P70bn with a market share of roughly 17% – about equal to BPI’s book. This would be a game-changer – it could put any of 5 players in the #1 position for credit cards, and could put SECB (or anyone else for that matter) in contention with the Big Three.

The four banks we cover have more than sufficient cash and capital to make the purchase without needing an equity call.

We would expect a highly competitive bid if and when Citi puts the portfolio up for sale.

BANKS
BDO – low NPLs, provisions boost 1Q21 NPAT +19%YoY

During its AGM, BDO reported headline NPAT of P10.4bn, up 19% YoY, well ahead of our FY21E expectations (P25.7bn) and ahead of consensus (P33.6bn). The main reason for the “beat” was the low P2.9bn provision expense – just over 10% of our full-year forecast. This was due to remarkably benign NPL ratio of 2.81%, up just 16bps QoQ from 2.65% at end-2020. NPL cover dipped slightly to 107%, from 109.5% at end-2020. PPOP was in line with our forecasts.

BDO will NOT be holding a 1Q21 earnings call. However, REGIS will be hosting BDO management on Tuesday, 27 April at 4pm. If you haven’t done so yet, please register using this link.

Some highlights

  • Loans were down 1% YoY, compared to +3% at end-2020. Deposits were up 2%, but CASA was up 11%, taking CASA ratio to a record-high 83% (was 81% at end-2020).
  • NIMs compressed, down to 4.07% (BDO estimate) compared to our estimate of 4.28% in 4Q20, on lower interest  rates, credit card rate caps, etc. While the CASA growth was impressive, deposit costs were already quite low (0.31% at 4Q20), and the pace of decline was slower than that of loan/asset yields.
  • Fee incomes continued to recover, with Insurance premiums surging 31% YoY.
  • Opex was up just 2% YoY; there was an accounting change implemented in FY20, on how the bank books trading/fx gains of its insurance subsidiary.
  • NPLs was remarkably low at 2.81%, considering the industry ratio stood at 4.16% as of February (+46bps from end-2020). NPL coverage fell slightly to 107% from 109.5% the quarter earlier. The bank remains hopeful that NPLs stay below 3%.

CEMENT
DTI probes cement imports from Vietnam
  • The Department of Trade and Industry said it has found sufficient basis to justify a probe for an anti-dumping investigation on cement imports from Vietnam
    • Application was filed by Cemex Philippines, Holcim Philippines Inc. and Republic Cement Builders and Building Materials Inc.
  • The DTI said the total volume of alleged dumped cement imports from Vietnam rose from 31% of the total Philippine imports for July to December 2019 to 62% in January to June of 2020
  • A comparison of the export price and normal value of cement from Vietnam for July to December 2019 indicated a dumping margin ranging from US$1.75 per MT to US$5.36 per MT (3.49% to 10.66%)
    • Price undercutting was at 23% in 2019 and 24% in the first semester of January to June in 2020
  • In addition, the DTI said local producers were unable to take advantage of growth in demand for cement during the period of investigation with the market share of the domestic products declining to 78% in 2019 from 85% in 2018 as imports displaced the share of the domestic industry.
  • “The industry suffered loss of market share, declining domestic sales, production, utilization rate, reduction in employment, increased cost of production and inventory,” the DTI said.
