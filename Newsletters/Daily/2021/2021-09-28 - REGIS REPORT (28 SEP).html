REGIS REPORT

28 September 2021

Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM) announced yesterday that it was selling a 25% stake in subsidiary Aboitiz Power (AP PM) to Japanese power firm JERA for US$1.463m. JERA will also purchase an additional 2% stake directly from the Aboitiz family. Because the deal falls below the threshold of 35%, we don’t expect a tender offer to be triggered.

The transaction amount translates to ~P40/share, which is around 14x PER for FY22 (consensus). This is also equivalent to around US$1.9m per MW of capacity, based on a total attributable capacity of 4,430MW, inclusive of the much delayed, soon-to-be-operational GN Power Dinginin. Bulk of its capacity is coal-fired, with renewables (hydro and geothermal) accounting for 20% of MW’s.

For perspective, KKR’s most recent tender offer for First Gen (FGEN PM) shares translates to roughly US$1.2m per MW. In 2019, AP acquired thermal assets from AC Energy at an estimated valuation of US$2.9m per MW.

According to the Aboitiz group’s press release, JERA’s entry will pave the way for cooperation in LNG, an industry which only started being developed locally a couple of years back. We reckon having a strategic partner will help AP expand and improve its operations as it goes into new technologies, including renewables. With a power supply crunch looming in 2-3 years (notwithstanding GNP Dinginin), AP is arguably in the best position to benefit from tightness in the market because of its contract profile.

For AEV, apart from the benefits accruing through AP, the transaction raises a chunky amount of cash and reduces its dependence on power, while lifting its NAV. However, our back-of-the-envelope numbers suggest that the NAV/share (~P50/share) is still below the current price (P53.1/share), which had been climbing sharply well ahead of the announcement of this deal.

Bangko Sentral (BSP) data showed consumer loan balances in the banking system declined 3% YoY in 2Q. The drop was led by a contraction in motor vehicle loans, which fell 15%.

It is interesting to note that Toyota Financial (TFS, 40% owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM)) saw a 30% expansion YoY in auto loans, taking advantage of the heightened risk aversion of banks to raise market share. In fact, we estimate TFS now accounts for over 20% of new auto loans in the entire system.

+++

The good news on the vaccination front is that the country now has 26m Covid-19 vaccine doses in inventory. This is equivalent to over two months of vaccinations at the most recent pace.

The bad news is that the pace of vaccinations in the country slowed down again to 360,000/day the past week. Clearly, vaccine supply is no longer the constraint. The government will need to adjust its vaccination strategy in order to improve the vaccination rate in the coming weeks and months.

POWER
AEV to sell 25% stake in Aboitiz Power
  • Aboitiz Equity Ventures disclosed that its board of directors approved the sale of a 25.01% stake in Aboitiz Power to Japan-based JERA Co. for around $1.463bn (around P40/share)
  • Based on consensus figures, P40/share implies a valuation of 14.1x PE and 10.0x EV/EBITDA for 2022
    • We estimate that the transaction price would be equivalent to $1.9mn EV/MW
  • The planned sale involves 1,840,334,941 common shares in Aboitiz Power
  • AEV’s stake will go down from 77% to 52% after the sale
  • JERA will also acquire an additional 1.99% stake from the Aboitiz family, which will bump up JERA’s stake in AP to 27%
  • Public float is at 19.70%
  • The partnership includes the joint development of liquefied natural gas for power projects as well as fuel sourcing and management
  • JERA Asia is a Japan-based energy company with LNG projects in Australia/USA and power generation investments in Asia, Middle East, Europe and Americas
  • We estimate AEV’s market-based NAV was P47.6/share before the transaction
    • Factoring in the sale at P40 for AP, AEV’s NAV would be P49.7share
BANKS
Consumer loans -3% YoY, NPLs 9.9% in 2Q21, as auto loans deteriorate
  • Consumer loans of the total Philippine banking industry contracted by 2.8% YoY in 2Q21, and by 0.7% QoQ. Meanwhile, consumer loan NPLs inched up 11bps QoQ to 9.86%.
  • The main culprit for both the loan decline and the NPL increase was motor vehicle (MV) loans, which were down 15.3% YoY and 5.3% QoQ, and whose NPLs worsened to 30bps QoQ to 12.40%. 
  • Mortgages were up 5.8% YoY, though this was slower than the 7.9% YoY pace in 1Q21 (it’s the slowest YoY growth since 2Q2010). On a QoQ basis, though, mortgages were up slightly (+1.6%). Mortgage NPLs were broadly flat at 9.42%, compared to the 9.44% reported in 1Q21.
  • Credit card receivables were still -2.5% YoY, but much better than the -10.3% YoY posted in 1Q21. Recall that some banks did a big write-down in 1Q21 following the lapse of the Bayanihan 2 moratorium (officially ended Feb). Balances are up slightly (+0.5%) QoQ. Meanwhile, credit card NPLs edged up a bit to 8.55%, compared to 8.48% in 1Q21.
  • Anecdotally, banks we cover/follow have said that they have seen renewed mortgage demand and credit card usage in 3Q21. And so far, they have not seen a material deterioration in asset quality, despite the renewed lockdowns in Aug/Sept. The auto loan picture remains quite weak though. While banks are indeed writing new loans, it’s not fast enough to compensate for the old loans rolling off / maturing.
