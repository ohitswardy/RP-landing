REGIS REPORT

06 September 2021

After some delay, Philippine Airlines (PAL) filed for Chapter 11 in New York. Among the highlights of the filing cited in the press:

  • The debt payments of PAL will be reduced by US$2bn. Based on filings with the PSE, PAL’s debt was close to US$4bn.
  • Its biggest secured creditors are Philippine National Bank (PNB PM), BDO (BDO PM) and China Bank (CHIB PM)
  • PAL’s shareholders will inject US$505m in equity and debt
  • The airline will reduce its fleet by 25% (it had 95 planes it had as of June). The media articles said that PAL will return 21 planes that are no longer needed in its long-term business plan.

It is unclear, for now, how much of a haircut PAL’s creditors will take (notwithstanding what was said about debt payments being reduced by US$2bn) and who the unsecured creditors are. Outside of PNB, the exposures of the other secured creditors doesn’t look large relative to their overall loan books.

For Cebu Air (CEB PM), the what’s and where’s of PAL’s fleet reduction is arguably the most important aspect of its plan. Beyond just the immediate cuts to PAL’s fleet, what also matters is what kind of fleet growth PAL’s revised business plan entails.

The government task force (IATF) on Covid-19 is recommending a change in the lockdown strategies beginning Sept 8. Instead of uniform city-wide or province-wide lockdown restrictions, the IATF is planning to enforce “granular” lockdowns limited to specific neighborhoods or streets. The IATF is changing its strategy in order to allow more businesses to open and for the restrictions on the general population to ease. This should be a boost for firms laboring under the current restrictions, including restaurants and those who need to work around the curfews.

If President Duterte approves the IATF proposals (expected), they will take effect two days from now.

Meanwhile, the country also lifted the travel bans on ten countries, including the UAE, Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia. Quarantine rules for arriving passengers were also tweaked (eased).

AIRLINES
Philippine Airlines files bankruptcy
  • Philippine Airlines (PAL) filed a Chapter 11 bankruptcy petition in the Southern District of New York
    • Petition includes an agreement with majority of aircraft lessors and lenders to reduce debt payments by over US$2bn
    • As at end-June, PAL’s net debt stood at P195bn (US$3.9bn); Capital deficiency at P86bn (US$2.2bn)
  • The airline’s top five creditors with the largest “secured claims” (excluding claims of insiders) totaling US$866mn
    • Philippine National Bank (US$156.51mn),
    • Banco De Oro Unibank, Inc. (US$80.42mn),
    • China Banking Corp. (US$54.83mn),
    • EXIM Guaranteed Loans (US$240.1mn), and
    • PK Airfinance S.A.R.L. (US$334.23mn)
    • It also listed creditors who have the 40 largest “unsecured claims and are not insiders” totaling to more than US$1.4bn
  • PAL will also reduce its fleet size by over 25% comprising of widebodies and narrowbodies
    • It will return 21 planes
    • PAL’s fleet stood at 95 aircraft in end-June, of which 31 are widebodies under operating leases
  • Restructuring plan involves raising US$505mn via equity and debt from a major stockholder immediately
    • It will also secure US$150mn in additional debt financing from global private investors for post-restructuring activities
