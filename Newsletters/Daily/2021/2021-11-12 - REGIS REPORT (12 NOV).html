REGIS REPORT

12 November 2021

We have a whole slew of results again today but today’s dominated by disappointments.

  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) (-) – 9M21 EBITDA (-13% YoY to P11.3bn) came below expectations mainly due to the underperformance of Quorn. 3Q21 sales for Quorn were up 4% YoY, but underlying UK retail sales contracted even as food service (10% of revs) jumped over 60%. For APAC BFB, instant noodle sales were strong (+10%), partly lifted by price increases and new SKUs. The price increases, however, were not enough to mitigate heightened cost pressure, which weighed on 3Q margins.

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (-) – 9M21 net profit (+14% YoY to P5.7bn) came in just a shade below expectations as the Aug lockdowns weighed on SSSG of the core Puregold brand (SS traffic -20% in 3Q). 3Q margins, however, were lifted by trade spending at Puregold even as higher logistics costs pressured S&R’s margins. We tweak our estimates, roll over our valuation to 2022E, and raise our TP to P48/sh, implying 14x 2022E PE.

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) - CEB’s 3Q21 passenger volumes improved QoQ but net losses were larger due to higher maintenance costs since the company prepared some parked aircraft to return to service ahead of an expected demand rebound. One of the key takeaways during the briefing was the successful negotiations with its lessors/AC providers, which has lead to (a) a much lower capex than what we’re forecasting and (b) a reduction in the cash burn to P1.2-1.3bn per month from P1.5bn in 9M21.

  • San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM) (+) – 3Q21 fell 21%QoQ, dragged down by a 50% contraction in profits from the food segment due to poultry, higher raw material prices and limited pricing power. Meanwhile, beer volumes grew 6%QoQ while gin volumes jumped 19%QoQ.

  • Emperador (EMP PM) (+) – In a limited press release, Emperador said its 9M21 income grew 24% to P7.3bn. While the headline figure is in line with consensus (but ahead of Regis), the implied 3Q21 profit of P2.2bn means a -27%QoQ and -13%YoY decline on much thinner margins (17% in 3Q vs 23% in 2Q).
  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (-) – Implied 3Q21 core profit to common collapsed to P494mn, as operations of its PH businesses remained soft. Profits during the quarter were also pulled down by FX losses and higher interest expenses. 9M21 core profit of P2.97bn (-13% YoY) is tracking well below Regis and consensus forecasts. The new management team is busy crafting a new business plan for the East zone, which they hope to implement over a much longer time frame once the water franchise bill is passed into law soon.  More below.
  • Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) (-) – The Aug lockdowns weighed on 3Q performance, bringing SSS levels down to 60-65% of pre-Covid (vs. 70-75% in 1H). This dragged overall profitability, and the company saw losses widening on a QoQ basis. Management, however, is optimistic on 4Q given the sequential positive trends seen in October.
  • Globe (GLO PM) (-) – Globe reported core income (ex- exceptionals) of P5.08bn, which would take core income to P15.8bn (+1%) for 9M by our reckoning.  The core income excludes adjustments on deferred tax arising from the reduction in tax rate booked in 1Q (included in company’s reported core income). We estimate a small market share loss in wireless revenue but also note the continued improvement in its broadband business with its highest-ever net adds of 78k wired subscribers.

Converge (CNVRG PM) also reported 9M/3Q results, with 9M income rising 76% to P5.2bn. 3Q income was P1.95bn, up +109%. The company added 222k broadband subscribers, taking total base to 1.58m, on track to exceed its year-end guidance for 2021. The net add figure roughly matches PLDT’s (TEL PM) of 229k in 3Q.

+++

Good news all around for the banks in September. Asset quality improved across nearly all metrics. More importantly, the figures are better not only in ratio terms, but also in absolute PHP terms. Loan growth also accelerated.

  • NPL ratio (ex-interbank) fell to 4.58% in September, down 8bps MoM from 4.66% in August. In PHP terms, NPLs dipped slightly to P485bn, from P491bn in August.
  • Perhaps more interesting is the drop in Past Due (PD) rates, as these are more indicative of future direction of NPLs. PD ratio fell to 5.20% in September, down from 5.32% in August. This is also the lowest ratio since Jan 2021, and has been seeing a steadily improving trend since May. Absolute PHP value of PDs also fell to P570bn, from P580bn in August.
  • Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RL) were up a smidge (2bps) MoM to 3.09%.
  • NPL cover also improved slightly to 84.4% in September, from 83.5% in August.

Loan growth accelerated slightly to +2.3% YoY in September, from +1.3% in August. The slow recovery in loans could still pose some downside risk heading into 2022. LDRs remain extremely low at 70%. The high system liquidity means NIMs might take a bit longer to recover than banks would like.

+++

Vehicle sales in Oct rose slightly (+5%) MoM, although they remain 10% below what they were the prior year.  For Toyota, unit sales were up 12% MoM (to 11,157), implying another month of market share gain.

Ayala Corp (AC PM) announced it was acquiring 60% of logistics firm Air21 for P6.06bn. Air21 is a logistics firm owned by the Lina family. AC has been eyeing expansion of its presence in the logistics space.

CONSUMER
Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM) – 3Q lockdowns drag top line recovery
  • Shakey’s Pizza reported a net loss of P35mn in 9M21, below consensus expectations. Revenues were flattish at P3.8bn, while EBITDA saw a sharp improvement +41% YoY, albeit coming from a low base. For 3Q21, the net loss widened QoQ to P49mn.
  • SSSG in 3Q21 slowed to 7% YoY as 1) YoY base effects normalized and 2) on-premise dining was restricted anew. Given the strict NCR lockdown in Aug, we estimate the contribution of on-premise sales dipped to c15% (from 25% in 1H21), dragging same-store sales down to 60-65% of pre-Covid levels (c75% in 1H21).
  • EBITDA contracted 18% QoQ to P135mn in 3Q. While this was partly due to negative operating leverage, this was also driven by the unfavorable sales mix. Off-premise sales tend to yield lower gross margins given higher packaging cost. The market also became highly promotional as the revenue pie shrank with the restrictions on on-premise dining.
  • Management is optimistic that it can swing to profitability by year end given the strong sequential top line improvement as early as October, when restrictions started easing. Input costs have been manageable thus far and improving top line trends should allow for more flexibility to raise prices and mitigate any further input cost pressure.

UTILITIES
Manila Water - PH biz, FX drag 3Q21 profit
  • 9M21 core income down 13% YoY to P2.97bn, tracking below estimates. MWC reported a 15% YoY drop in 9M21 core income before the share of participating prefs to P3.5bn, tracking well below Regis and consensus forecasts. Implied 3Q21 profit dropped 62% QoQ and 36% YoY to P600mn, weighed down by continued softness in billed volumes for the domestic business as well as higher financing costs. The company began recognizing FX losses during the quarter, without offsetting FCDA adjustments, in line with the East zone’s contract revisions.  
  • East zone EBITDA drops YoY, as expenses accelerate. Profits for the East zone fell 53% YoY in 3Q21, with EBITDA down 12%. Billed volumes fell 3% YoY in 3Q and was c. 3% below 2018 levels. Cash expenses surged by 19% YoY in 3Q21 alone after last year’s low base. Capex for 9M21 jumped 23% YoY to P7bn and the company hopes to surpass its 2020 capex of P9.8bn. Management continues to discuss its capex shortfall with the regulator to avoid incurring penalties.
  • Domestic investments outside East zone incurred higher losses QoQ; Regional investments stable. Manila Water Philippine Ventures continue to weigh on profits, posting larger losses QoQ. While operations in Laguna and Boracay have started to recover, Estate Water and Clark remained soft. Profit contributions from its regional investments also stabilized QoQ.  
  • Other key updates. MWC’s new management team is currently busy crafting a new business plan for the East zone, which it plans to submit to the regulator by 1Q22. Management believes the water franchise bill, which will likely be passed into law soon, will allow them to implement capex over a much longer time frame.
