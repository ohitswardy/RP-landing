REGIS REPORT

26 November 2021

Data from Google show personal mobility rising to another pandemic period high.  Retail/recreation mobility is already practically where it was already immediately before the pandemic. The data for workplace/s, however, shows mobility still ~15% below what it was in Jan-Feb 2020.

In line with this, information on airline capacity shows weekly seats for Cebu Air (CEB PM) rising further as more people travel due to easing restrictions.  We estimate CEB was operating at 33% of seat capacity so far this month (Nov 2021), which is up from 30% the previous month.  As also seen in the Google mobility data, the upward trend is looking clear.

+++

Covid vaccine hesitancy is continuing to decline. Based on the latest opinion poll, only 18% did not want to be vaccinated as of Sept. That figure is down from 21% in June and 33% in May.

Meanwhile, the latest data from the government showed 31% of the country is now fully vaccinated with the proportion for Metro Manila now at 66%.

+++

A local court in Cebu issued arrest warrants for various executives of Megawide (MWIDE PM) and its JV partner in the airport business GMR, supposedly due to their violation of the Anti-Dummy Law. Foreign individuals and entities are, strictly speaking, not allowed to own more than 40% of a public utility or serve as executive officers of a public utility. The allegation here is that the MWIDE-GMR venture is using “dummies” to circumvent that restriction. 

Such issues have always been a gray area and one would normally expect such to be settled and remedied without having to resort to criminal proceedings. The fact that it’s come down to the issuance of arrest warrants suggests something else is going on behind the scenes.

+++

Universal Robina (URC PM) announced it is acquiring Malaysia-based snackfoods maker, Munchy Food Industries Sdn Bhd. Based on the latter's company website, it is the #1 local biscuit maker in Malaysia.

The disclosure was thin on detail, but the transaction is being valued at approx P22.9bn (USD450mn) EV. The amount is equivalent to roughly the net cash proceeds (USD470mn) that URC is getting from the recent sale of its Oceania business. We expect to get further detail in the coming days.

Budget deficit narrows to 6-month low in October
  • The government ran a P64.3bn budget deficit in October, down MoM and lowest level since April 2021
    • On a YoY basis, the budget deficit in October was up 5% YoY
    • Tax revenues improved 3% MoM and 7.5% YoY; Tax revenues for October were 7% below pre-Covid levels
    • Expenditures in October dropped 23% MoM and was the lowest since January 2021, with primary spending also lowest this year
  • Brings YT-October budget deficit to P1.2tn, up 28% YoY

MACRO
Mobility at new highs (20-21 Nov)
  • Mobility data keeps rising, with nation-wide retail already slightly above the Jan/Feb 2020 baseline. All indicators posted new Covid-era highs. Interestingly, though, the nation-wise numbers seem better than Metro Manila. This despite the significantly higher vaccination rates within the capital (c70%) compared to the national average (c30%). One possible explanation is that Metro Manila has adapted more to a) e-commerce, and b) remote work, given much better internet connectivity.
  • Either way, the data is hugely positive, and points to an even better December.

MACRO
Vaccine willingness improves further in Sept
  • Willingness to get the Covid-19 vaccine improved again in September, according to a Social Weather Stations (SWS) survey published earlier this month. Nation-wide, 64% of adults were willing to get the jab (including the 35% that had already received at least one dose). This is up from the 55% in June, and 32% in May. Vaccine reluctance  dropped to just 18% of adults, from 21% in June and 33% in May. Adults who were “uncertain” also dropped to 19% in September, down from 24% in June and 35% in May.
  • Metro Manila residents were by far more willing to be vaccinated, with 87% willing (including 71% already jabbed), up from 75% in June and 41% in May. Anti-vaxxers were down to just 7%, from 8% in June and 30% in May. Those on the fence fell to just 6% in September, from 16% in June and 30% in May.
  • However, reluctance is still quite high, particularly in areas in the Visayas and in Mindanao, with both at 24-25% (combining the “surely not” and “probably not” responses). For those in Luzon (ex-Metro Manila), about 15% are still anti-vaxxers are about. That said, willingness to get the jab improved across all geographies.
  • This will be a challenge in getting nation-wide vaccination rates up. While Metro Manila has already vaccinated 73% of its population with at least one dose, the national average is just 40%. And while some 77mn doses have been administered, we estimate there are close to 60mn doses in inventory, some of which might be nearing expiration dates. Getting the jabs to the provinces is definitely difficult, especially across more than 7,100 islands. Add to that poor infrastructure – from roads, to medical centers, to refrigeration – outside the major city/town centers.
  • The government aims to administer 15mn doses over the three days from 29 November to 01 December. To date the highest single-day total has been just over 1mn. We may be skeptical of the government reaching its goal. But any surge in vaccinations would indeed be a welcome development.

AIRLINES
Cebu Pacific weekly seats up to 33% of pre-pandemic levels in November
  • Jefferies’ airline capacity survey showed Cebu Pacific’s weekly seat capacity rose 39k from November 1-25, equivalent to 33% of pre-pandemic levels
    • This is 10% higher MoM; October 2021 stood at 30% of pre-pandemic levels
  • Brings 4Q-to-date capacity to 32% of pre-Covid levels, highest since the pandemic started
  • We estimate CEB will break-even on EBITDA at c. 40% of pre-Covid seat capacity; Cash burn will likely be plugged if capacity reaches 50-60% of pre-Covid levels
