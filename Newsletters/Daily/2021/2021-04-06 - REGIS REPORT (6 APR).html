REGIS REPORT

06 April 2021

The revised water concession contract signed by Manila Water (MWC PM) and the government has been made available and after taking a look, our brief conclusion on the deal revisions is that the changes are not too drastic and the outcome was not as bad for MWC as feared.

Sure, the headlines today all proclaim that MWC will not be able to recover income tax anymore. What maybe lost in the self-congratulatory press releases is that the nominal return is now 12% (it was last set at an inflation-adjusted 7% in 2018). Combined with a lowered tax rate (now 25%), the unlevered after-tax return would be a pretty decent 9%. From a financial perspective, the only other big difference is that MWC will no longer be granted a “free” FX hedge on its debt.

Beyond that, there are new rules imposed that would, in theory, make it easier for the regulator to seek information, audit and impose sanctions. New provisions in the contract also call for the turnover of fully recovered and depreciated assets owned by MWC to the government (MWSS) even before the concession expires. Changes in government protections and limiting arbitration to local arbitrators could also, in theory, make regulation more politically vulnerable. One can argue, however, that the last agreement didn’t exactly protect MWC much anyway.

After a roller coaster eighteen months, it seems all the tantrums, threats and hullabaloo, was much ado about nothing if the outcome of this sordid and damaging drama is what we go by.

UTILITIES
Manila Water, government sign revised water concession contract

The revised water concession contract signed by Manila Water (MWC PM) has been made available and, after taking a look at the new agreement, we believe the revised contract is not too different from the old one, particularly with regard to the regulatory framework and safeguard, and financial returns. The outcome was not as bad as feared as the new deal is arguably only “mildly” worse than the previous agreement.

Below are our thoughts on the key changes in the provisions of the new concession contract:

  • Regulatory return fixed at 12%, lower than previous.  The regulatory return is fixed at a nominal rate of 12% from the effectivity date of the new concession agreement (likely March 31, 2021) until the end of the concession. This suggests that the regulatory return will be 9% on a post-tax, nominal basis, lower than the existing regulatory return of 10.4% (post-tax, nominal).  Note that the regulatory return was previously set at 7.39%, calculated on a post-tax and real basis.
  • No income tax recoverability.  Under the new contract, recovery of expenditures related to tax on income will no longer be allowed.
  • Removal of FX pass-through.  Manila Water will no longer get full protection from currency losses on FX debt, which could raise the company’s cost of debt, as the company will likely need to hedge its FX exposure.  Note that MWC’s pre-tax cost of debt is currently at 6.0%.
  • Tariff hikes.  There will be no tariff adjustments until December 31, 2022. There will also be a separation of tariffs related to water and wastewater but the published agreement did not define the tariffs for both. Note that both water and wastewater tariffs will be adjusted according to the following:
    • Inflation. MWC will continue to adjust its tariffs according to inflation annually, but only up to two thirds (2/3) of the change in CPI and only after Dec 31, 2022. 
    • Rate rebasing or “R” adjustment.  There will be a tariff adjustment every five years based on (a) the net present value of the Opening Cash Position (OCP) and (b) future cash flows.  It is worth noting that cash outflows incurred prior to the end of the tariff freeze can form part of the OCP, subject to review.
    • Limits to tariff hikes.  Tariff hikes will now be capped at a maximum of 30% for water tariffs and 50% for wastewater tariffs.  These caps will apply to both the “R” adjustment and CPI-related adjustments.
  • Turnover of recovered assets to MWSS at no cost.  On or before January 2022, Manila Water will be submitting a list of all “Recovered Assets” that are (1) already fully recovered by MWC through tariff adjustments and (2) are fully depreciated in MWC’s books. This suggests that ownership of these recovered assets will be turned over to MWSS effectively at “zero” cost. This mechanism is repeated every rate rebasing exercise, such that assets that are not yet recovered will remain with MWC until the Expiration (or Early Termination) date, at which time these will be automatically vest in MWSS.  For valuation purposes, we believe there should be no terminal value for the DCF analysis.
  • Contract expiration date stays. The revised agreement has the same expiration date as the old agreement (July 31, 2037).  The government can also pay the concessionaire a lump-sum payment at the end of the concession for all or a portion of investments that were not recovered via tariff adjustments.
  • More power to regulator, Penalties explicit.  The new contract also appears to be silent on the settlement of disputes arising from rate rebasing adjustments. It appears that the MWSS will have the final say on rate rebasing determinations. Apart from this, the new contract also requires the concessionaire to (1) provide information to the regulator on a more defined timetable and (2) subject itself to a regular audit.  The new contract also sets clearer financial penalties for various types of service shortfalls.

POWER
First Gen picks BW Gas as FSRU provider
  • First Gen Corp (FGEN PM) has tapped BW Gas Ltd as its floating storage and regassification unit (FSRU) provider for its interim offshore LNG terminal in Batangas
  • FGEN LNG Corp signed a five year time charter party with BW FSRU IV Pte Ltd for the charter of an FSRU with respect to the LNG terminal that will be built in Batangas City
  • BW Paris, the FSRU to be provided by BW FSRU IV, has an LNG storage capacity of 162,400 cubic meters and nominal and peak gas send out capacity of 500mn standard cubic feet per day and 750mmscfd, respectively
    • Nominal send out capacity of BW Paris is 25% more than the production capability of Malampaya (400mmscfd at its peak)
  • The LNG project will allow FGEN’s subsidiary to introduce LNG to the country by third quarter next year to serve the natural gas requirements of existing and future gas-fired plants of FGEN and third parties
