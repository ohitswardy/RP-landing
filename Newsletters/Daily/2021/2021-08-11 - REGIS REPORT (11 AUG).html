REGIS REPORT

11 August 2021

The government reported that 2Q GDP growth was 11.8%. Note that the Philippine convention is to report GDP on a YoY basis. Given that 2Q20 was when the peak of the lockdowns happened (resulting in GDP plunging 17%), it should come as no surprise that GDP in 2Q21 is up sharply on a YoY basis.

But is the economy really on the mend already? A look at the QoQ GDP growth (or drop) tells us that the short answer is NO. Seasonally adjusted GDP for 2Q21 actually contracted 1.3% QoQ. In layman’s terms, economic output in 2Q was worse than 1Q on an apples-to-apples basis.

Put the bubbly back in the fridge.

+++

The non-performing  loan ratio of the commercial banking system saw a tiny dip (-2bp vs May) in June to 4.61% of total loans. Instinctively, this is a good sign. One, however, has to watch out for the restructured loans (RL) figure as well. RLs represent loans that are still in some danger of going into default, and that ratio rose to 3.05% from 2.47%.

+++

Here is today’s results round-up:

  • DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) (+) – 1H21 core profit rose more than threefold to P8.24bn, tracking ahead of Regis and consensus forecasts. 2Q was exceptionally strong for its coal mining, nickel mining, and power units, thanks to elevated commodity prices and favorable power demand-supply dynamics. 2Q profit for the construction and property units were softer QoQ on provisions and higher input costs, respectively.

Following the 2Q results, we raised our FY21/FY22E profit forecasts by 50%/30% after putting through the coal price upgrades at Semirara (SCC PM) and making some post-results adjustments. We also lifted our TP by 35% to P6.50/sh. At a 33% discount to its market-based NAV and at 5x PER, the shares are undervalued relative to historical levels. With improving cash flows from key its subsidiaries, the dividend yield of 12% (with special div) also looks enticing. We reiterate our Buy rating.

  • Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) (=) – Consolidated profit was broadly in line, though trends across various segments (ex-PGOLD) was mixed. The Real Estate segment was behind, while the Liquor Distribution is trending well ahead of FY21 estimates. Sales vols for the latter jumped 74% YoY in 2Q21 alone, already back to pre-pandemic levels.
  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (-) – 1H21 core profit to common down 7% YoY to P2.5bn, trending below Regis and consensus expectations. Profits were still soft for most of its PH units, with billed volumes at the East zone alone dropping 3% YoY in 2Q21. East Water and its Vietnam bulk water projects were the main bright spots.

As expected, the Dept of Trade (DTI) has ordered the removal of the safeguard duty on vehicle imports, paving the way for vehicle prices to drop back to where they were early this year. We estimate the safeguard duty represented between 4-12% of the retail prices of the vehicles affected. It was applied to vehicles representing ~50% of Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sales. TMP is majority-owned (51%) by GT Capital (GTCAP PM).

MACRO
2Q21 GDP +11.8% YoY, but still soft QoQ; lowering FY21 GDP est to +4.5%
  • Philippine GDP in 2Q21 jumped +11.8% YoY. According to Bloomberg, consensus estimates had 2Q21 at +10.9%. That took 1H21 GDP growth to +3.7%. Before we break out the bubbly, don’t forget that we were operating off an extremely low base (was -17.0% in 2Q20).
  • Compared to pre-pandemic levels, the 2Q21 print was still 7.3% lower than 2Q19. In contrast, 1Q21 was only 4.6% below 1Q19. In fact, according to the PSA, 2Q21 was -1.3% QoQ compared to 1Q21 on a seasonally adjusted basis.
  • Given the harder lockdown in August (compared to April), it is probably safe to assume that the quarterly weakness could persist into 3Q21.
  • We lower our FY GDP forecast to +4.5%, compared to Bloomberg consensus of about 5.0%.

BANKS
NPL, PD ratios improve in June; RLs jump MoM
  • Asset quality metrics broadly improved MoM in June for the Philippine banking system. The sector’s NPL ratio dipped 2bps MoM to 4.61%, as a mild increase in the Peso value of NPLs was offset by a 1.2% MoM increase in total loans (ex-interbank). The improvement was also likely helped by write-offs, particularly of unsecured loans (eg credit cards). NPL cover increased to 82.4% in June from 80.0% in May, while Loan Loss Reserve (LLR) ratio ticked up 10bps MoM to 3.69%. Perhaps more notable is that the Past Due (PD) ratio dropped 20bps MoM, as the absolute amount fell by P24bn, or almost 3% MoM.
  • The one ratio that worsened was Restructured Loans (RLs), which jumped to 3.05%, up 58bps MoM from the 2.47% in May. This was expected, though, as BDO’s (BDO PM) RL (performing) ratio jumped to 5.5% in 2Q21 from sub-3% in 1Q21, and accounting for roughly 2/3 of the new RLs for the industry in 2Q21. As early as 4Q20, management had been guiding that some 6% of loans were either already RL or in the process of restructuring.
  • The NPL and PD improvements are impressive, in our view, as we had been bracing for a deterioration in asset quality due to the Covid lockdowns that were in force in April/May. Could this bode well in light of the new lockdowns in August? Have industries and households adapted enough to the “new normal” of intermittent restrictions to mobility?

Fingers crossed…

PS – One slight worry (outside of asset quality) is that LDRs continued to drop, albeit slightly, to 70.2% in June. Anecdotally, we have noticed competition intensifying as banks drop loan rates for good credits. We note that some listed companies are taking out short-term working capital loans at rates as low as 1.8%, below policy rate of 2%, though just slightly higher than the one-year benchmark of c1.6%.
