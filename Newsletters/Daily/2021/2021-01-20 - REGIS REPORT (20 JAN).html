REGIS REPORT

20 January 2021

In a new piece on the retail sector (“Consolidation amidst adversity”) released yesterday, Carissa (carissa.mangubat@regis.ph) upgrades Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) and Wilcon (WLCON PM) to BUY. Her BUY rating on Puregold (PGOLD PM) was also maintained.

This comes after changes to our estimates for RRHI and WLCON, which prompt us to lift our target prices for the two by 30-53%. While the prognosis for the immediate term is still difficult, our TPs have been lifted due to upgraded longer term estimates, higher terminal growth assumptions, and reduced cost of capital (debt) assumptions. Our new TPs for all three companies are broadly in line with their respective historical averages, and current averages for their peers.

After the carnage of 2020, retail sales in the Philippines should see a pick-up in 2021, though we believe overall consumer spending will continue to be challenged by the lingering impact of lockdown-related measures, which led to reduced mobility and belt-tightening. We expect the recovery to be K-shaped, with mid- to upper-income consumers likely recovering faster than lower income consumers, who have borne much of the lockdown’s impact. The shape of this recovery will have implications for the various retailers depending on their core markets.

Against this challenging backdrop, we expect further industry consolidation to be spurred as smaller, independent (and informal) players are marginalized. This will be positive for the retailers under our coverage because they have the scale and the deeper pockets to take a bigger slice of what is a temporarily diminished pie. We are mindful, however, of the potential threat from e-commerce, which has grown significantly as a result of the pandemic and is likely to become meaningful in a few years.

Overall, we forecast average EPS CAGR of 30% for 2020-22E for the retailers, mainly driven by improving profitability as store sales recover and margins normalize from the 2020 lows. While we now have Buy ratings for all three retailers under coverage, we expect retailers that 1) have proportionately more stand-alone (as opposed to mall-based) stores in their network, and 2) cater to mid- to upper-income consumers to do better and bounce back more quickly. WLCON meets both criteria and is our top pick in the space.

Congress is set to ratify changes to anti-money laundering rules under the threat of being included in the international money laundering gray list.

The bicameral conference committee on the law agreed on a final version that includes these key provisions: Tax crimes in excess of P25m (~US$500k) would now be a ‘predicate’ for the investigation of bank accounts. Real estate transactions above the threshold of P7.5m (US$150k) would also have to be reported to the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC). In addition, media reported that offshore gaming firms (POGOs) and their service providers will now be “covered”, though what this coverage means is unclear.

MACRO
Infrastructure spending drops 50% YoY, 29% MoM in November
  • Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell by 50% YoY, 29% MoM in November to P40.3bn
    • Cited high base effects from completed building projects of the LTO and LTFRB last year
  • This brings YT-November infra spend to P549bn, down 22% YoY
    • Due to delays in construction of some public works, as well as the discontinuance of some projects
    • For 2021, the government expects to increase infra spend by over 60% to P997bn

MACRO
Bicameral panel approves stricter ‘dirty money’ rules
  • The Bicameral Conference Committee on Tuesday approved the bill strengthening the anti-money laundering law, although a key amendment pushed by Malacanang was not included in the final version
  • The panel on Tuesday reconciled House Bill No. 7904 and Senate Bill No. 1945 which amends Republic Act No. 9160 or the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) of 2001 to comply with the recommendations of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF)
  • The Bicameral Conference Committee adopted the Senate’s proposal to set the tax crime threshold to excess of P25mn  (vs House Version threshold set at P20mn)
  • The Bicam also agreed to require the submission of reports on all real estate transactions involving an excess of P7.5mn to the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC)
    • Both bills originally proposed P5mn, much higher than the initial proposal of P1mn
  • The measure will also now include Philippine offshore gaming operators (POGOS) and their service providers among the covered persons
  • The Bicam retained the House provisions granting the AMLC investigative powers available to other law enforcement agencies, subpoena powers, search and seizure
  • AMLC will also be authorized to implement targeted financial sanctions in relation to the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and its financing
TRANSPORT
ICTSI completes Manila port expansion
  • ICTSI has completed the berth expansion at the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT)
    • The 150-meter extension of Berth 7 and the container yard extension have increased MICT’s capacity by 10% to 3.3mn TEUs
    • Our forecasts already assume the expansion
  • MICT handled 2.5mn TEUs in 2019; In 9M20, it handled 1.5mn TEUs (-17% YoY)
  • MICT accounts for 25% of ICT’s group-wide volumes, 35% of revenues, and 50% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI is trading at 9.8x FY21E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers (15x) and historical average (12x)
CEMENT
Cemex PH expansion underway with installation of new kiln
  • Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) lifted the new rotary kiln into position yesterday, reaching a key milestone on its 1.5mn MT expansion
    • Once completed (target is Dec 2021), the new kiln will increase CHP’s kiln capacity (in cement terms) to 6.5mn MT from 5.0mn MT today
  • Cemex is targeting to spend US$235mn for the project (already in forecast)
    • As of Sep 2020, CHP has spent ~US$134mn
  • CHP is trading at US$110 EV/MT vs. replacement cost of US$100-150 per MT
TELCOS
Globe's new BPO unit seen to earn at least P500mn annually
  • In a statement, Globe Telecom said that a unit called ASTI Business Services Inc (ABSI) is looking to earn at least P500mn annually
  • It aims to provide enterprise services to telecommunications, financial technology, information technology, retail, health, logistics, automotive, banking, education, real estate and aviation companies
  • Globe cited the P11.25bn market for the local IT-BPO in the Philippines alone
  • Globe announced the official launch of ABSI on January 13
