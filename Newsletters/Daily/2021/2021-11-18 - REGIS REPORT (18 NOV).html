REGIS REPORT

18 November 2021

Mobility continues to improve, following the lowering of restrictions to “Alert Level 2”. Google Mobility data shows new Covid-19 highs for the three sectors we watch most closely over the weekend just passed (13/14 November). In particular, trips made to retail/recreation establishments are now just 3% below 2019 levels nation-wide, and just 11% down for Metro Manila. For transit stations, mobility is now down to just 25-30% below pre-Covid levels. And people coming back to the workplace is now just 15-25% below “normal”.

The data is unequivocally positive for the reopening plays like restaurants, malls, and tourism sectors. We recently highlighted the potentially huge upside risk from “revenge travel”, as domestic tourism accounts for a disproportionately large share of GDP. We followed that up with a note on Cebu Air (CEB PM) as the best way to play it.

We expect mobility to continue to improve as we head into the all-important Christmas holiday season. Fingers crossed that Covid case counts stay low…

ICTSI (ICT PM) announced it is buying back US$270m of its previously issued perps through its tender offer. The buyback is part of its refinancing / debt reduction exercise to not only term-out debt maturities but also lower financing costs. We reckon the exercise will reduce financing costs going forward by more than US$10m per year at the cost of a short-term hit.

+++                                                                       

A new company is hoping to cash in on the renewable energy mania through an IPO. Solar Philippines Nueva Ecija is planning to raise as much as P2.7bn selling 33% of its shares to the public in an IPO. The company is in the process of building a 225MW (DC) solar farm by next year. At the top of the price range, the company’s market cap would be P8.1bn (US$160m).

+++

Globe (GLO PM) CEO Ernest Cu says Mynt, the holding company for GCash, will not be going public for now. Mynt just raised US$300m from new and existing investors, valuing the company at “over US$2bn”.

Globe’s share price has soared 87% in the last three month because of excitement over the explosive of Mynt/GCash. It is quite apparent that current levels already assume valuations well beyond just the worth of its core telecom operation. The question now is, exactly how much?

Globe’s current market cap is just a tad below US$10bn. Inclusive of net debt, its Enterprise Value rises to US$14bn.

Assigning a 6x EBITDA multiple to its core operations would produce a valuation of US$10bn. This means the current share price already values Globe’s 35% stake in Mynt at US$5bn, implying a value of US$14bn for 100% of Mynt. Relative to its registered user base (not all of whom are active) of 51m, the valuation already looks lofty.

ICTSI to buyback 35% of US$775mn perps
  • ICTSI announced the tender offer results for the US$775mn perps; It will be buying back the following:
    • US$184mn of the US$400mn perps with a coupon of 5.875% and callable in May 2022; Tender offer price at US$1,024 per US$1,000
    • US$86mn of the US$375mn perps with a coupon of 4.88% and callable in May 2024; Tender offer price at US$1,044.50 per US$1,000
  • The buyback will be funded from the US$300mn 10-year notes issue, which has a rate of 3.5%
  • ICTSI also plans to call the remaining principal for the US$400mn 5.875% perps
  • We estimate the refinancing exercise could lift annualized profit to common by c. US$11mn (c. 3%)
    • However, buying back the prefs at a premium and the mild negative carry could reduce earnings in the short-term
  • As at end-September 2021, ICTSI’s net debt (inc. perps/concession liab/leases) stood at US$4.3bn, equivalent to 3.9x net debt-to-EBITDA and 6.6x net debt-to-equity

BANKS
SECB notes from 3Q21 earnings call
  • SECB was “cautiously optimistic” given sequential improvement in quarterly profits. Management sees loan growth opportunities, though competition is tight which could keep loan yields (corp) low. More optimistic on corporate demand near-term. Retail likely to follow, as SECB loosens credit policies. Commercial / mid-market is still challenged, particularly in the construction/hospitality segments.

This has meant NPL recoveries (writebacks) on retail loans. And hence the ECL adjustments. They expect to get back to above 100% cover “in one or two quarters”.
