REGIS REPORT

19 May 2021

Seems like there’s a glimmer of hope in the Covid story – case count is falling, and vaccination is picking up. We’ve also got some (very) late consumer loan data from the BSP.

Bur first, some results comments from Jollibee and Cosco Capital, both about in line with our expectations.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) – in line – We hosted CFO Mr Baysa to provide more detail on the results, and he seemed optimistic that the company would meet their own targets for the year. Cost cuts resulting from last year’s business transformation initiatives, as well as narrowing losses at CBTL and Smashburger, should offset the slow top line recovery in the Philippines. Input cost inflation seems to be under control (for now), with price increases already implemented as early as 4Q20.

  • Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) – in line – Strong PGOLD results offset relatively soft numbers for the other segments. Liquor Distribution profit was flat YoY despite a 4% decline in revenues. Volumes grew 8% YoY led by brandy (+19%), though lower priced SKUs led much of the growth. Related party receivables continue to be a key issue for the Group.

In other news, Maynilad has signed the revised concession agreement with the MWSS, which should remove the overhang for its listed owners Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and DMCI (DMC PM). And surprising nobody, President Duterte signed a law renewing the franchise of DITO Telecommunity for another 25 years.

+ + +

The BSP finally release end-2020 data for consumer loans, which grew just 4.6% YoY, the lowest on our records (dating back to 2002). Unsurprisingly, consumer NPLs continued to trek upwards to 9.33% from 8.51% in 3Q20. Most concerning was the very high figure for mortgage NPLs, which stood at 9.23%, up from 8.37% in 3Q. Anecdotally, consumer defaults seem to be levelling off for the banks we cover. Hopefully we won’t have to wait another 3 months for the 1Q21 industry data…

+ + +

Some good news for a change? In all the hubbub over results, what’s been lost is the big improvement in the Covid situation. (Lots of charts, below.)

  • The 7-day average case count has fallen below 6,000, from a peak of over 11,000. In terms of active cases, we’re down to about 56,000, well below the peak at nearly 180,000.
  • Hospital occupancy rates for Covid beds are also sharply down from their highs. In late-March / early-April, we were hearing anecdotes of hospitals with Covid bed waiting lists that were over 100 names long.
  • Vaccines are arriving in larger numbers. Thus far, about 2.9mn doses have been administered. Of this, some 680k people have received the 2nd dose and are fully vaccinated.

True, the Covid and hospital numbers are still much higher than the Jan/Feb numbers. And the vaccine roll-out is still painfully slow. But directionally , all these trends are promising.

The improving data has allowed the Philippine government to slowly ease movement restrictions even within the ECQ category. (It’s tempting to ignore the headline ECQ/GCQ status, as lines are getting blurry!) as of mid-May, mobility seems to have returned to levels from Jan/Feb. Curiously, our Asian peers are just now starting to limit mobility as Covid cases there are rising sharply.

The hope, clearly, is that the further relaxation will not see a renewed uptick in case count, and a renewed return to lockdowns. Vaccination remains the key to a more sustained reopening of the economy.

BANKS
Consumer loan NPLs jump in Dec
  • In a VERY belated update, the BSP released data for consumer loans as of end-Dec 2020. Total consumer loans grew 4.6% YoY, down from 8.3% in 3Q20 and from 17.7% in 1Q20. This is the slowest YoY growth on our records, going back to 2002 (3Q03 was +5.3%). All segments decelerated, though auto loans actually contracted 8% YoY.
  • NPLs increased across all major segments. Overall consumer NPLs stood at 9.33%, up from 8.51% in the previous quarter. Mortgage defaults were at 9.23% (from 8.37%), and Motor Vehicle NPLs were 10.68% (from 9.73%). The unsecured portion showed lower NPLs – credit cards at 8.85%, and salary loans at 6.60% – likely due to more aggressive write-offs by banks.
  • Anecdotally, the four banks we cover in 1Q21 showed flat-to-lower NPLs QoQ on their consumer books. However, there was also some indication of renewed stress in April due to the new lockdowns (though the uptick seemed much milder).
  • We hope the next data points won’t be this badly delayed…

CONGLOS
COSCO 1Q21: PGOLD outperformance offsets soft results from other segments
  • Cosco Capital’s net profit in 1Q21 was flattish YoY at P1.4bn. The strength in the PGOLD results (1Q21 net profit +15% YoY) offset declines in its other segments
  • The Liquor Distribution segment’s net profit in 1Q21 was flattish YoY at P228mn, tracking slightly behind our FY21E. Meanwhile, the Real Estate segment saw a 32% decline in profit, also behind our estimates.
  • The Liquor Distribution segment’s revenues were down 4% YoY during the quarter even as volume growth recovered to +8%. Lower priced SKUs continued to drive growth, particularly for the brandy segment (+19% YoY) and other spirits (+36% YoY). We estimate average revenue per case declined 12% YoY but improved sequentially (+11%) vs 4Q20.
  • COSCO currently trades at a 64% discount to our market-based NAV estimate of P13.64/sh.

UTILITIES
Maynilad, MWSS sign revised concession agreement
  • Maynilad and Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) signed on May 18, 2021 the revised concession agreement
  • Key highlights are the following:
    • Confirmation of the continuation of the concession period until July 31, 2037;
    • Imposition of a tariff freeze until 31 December 2022;
    • Removal of Corporate Income Tax from among Maynilad’s recoverable expenditures as well as the Foreign Currency Differential Adjustment;
    • Capping of the annual inflation factor to 2/3 of the Consumer Price Index;
    • Imposition of rate caps for water and sewerage services to 1.3x and 1.5x, respectively, of the previous standard rate;
    • Removal from the Republic of the Philippine’s (“RoP”) Letter of Undertaking of the non-interference of the Government in the rate-setting process, and the limitation of the RoP’s financial guarantees to cover only those loans and contracts that are existing as of the signing of the RCA;
    • Replacement of the market-driven Appropriate Discount Rate with a 12% fixed nominal discount rate; and
    • Retention of the rate rebasing mechanism where, subject to the rate caps
  • Maynilad is a 53%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM), 25%-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM), and Japan-based Marubeni Corp (20%)
  • MPI is trading at a 31% discount to its market-based NAV of P5.50/sh
  • DMC is trading at a 23% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.10/sh
