REGIS REPORT

02 February 2021

We are beginning to receive more detail on what is likely to be the “final” CREATE bill.

Apart from the much talked about cut in corporate tax rates, there are provisions to increase VAT exemptions for real estate sales and for increased tax incentives for companies moving operations outside Metro Manila. Also among the “new” provisions is one that specifically exempts crude oil imports from taxes and duties, which we believe could unduly favor the one remaining oil refinery in the country.

Beyond the impact on specific companies and sectors, there is also the impact on the long-term fiscal balance that one should be watching out for. How much will this tax reform package affect continuing revenues? Until all the fine print is read and analyzed, we doubt anyone knows yet. But it is certain that the impact will be in the hundreds of billions of pesos.

+++

There are two items in the news today which should give us a glimpse of one area of growing concern: inflation.

The government is planning to lower duties on rice imported from non-ASEAN countries in order to head off a spike in domestic prices as supplies from Vietnam and Thailand tighten. President Duterte also signed an order imposing price caps on pork and chicken for 60 days. We believe the inflation bugbear will be rearing its ugly head very soon. It’s a question of how quickly and how severe it will be.

+++

Overlooked over the past few weeks is an order from the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) to conduct hearings over the next few weeks on a specific petition from Meralco’s (MER PM).

The matter involves Meralco seeking an affirmation of its tariff for the lapsed period from July 2015, since which time the ERC has been unable to set any final tariff. In this petition, Meralco is asking the ERC to affirm its tariff of P1.38/kwh which will mean that the distribution utility (DU) will need to refund P13.9bn in “over-collections” since July 2015.

What might be missed by the casual observer here is that Meralco has already provisioned for a much lower rate (and thus much larger refunds). In fact, we estimate Meralco has accumulated over P70bn in provisions on the assumption that tariffs will be cut to ~P1.10/kwh from July 2015 onwards. And we have already built such into our current valuation (P340/share).

Thus, an affirmed tariff of P1.38/kwh coupled with a refund of P13.9bn is actually going to be massively positive for Meralco. Not only would there likely be a big reversal of provisions, there will also likely be a jump in prospective recurring earnings as future provisions are reduced or eliminated.

Watch this space.

+++

Our research team published a new piece re-affirming our positive view on the market. While there are near-term issues (e.g. inflation), the passage of CREATE, improved fiscal spending and the eventual arrival of vaccines towards the latter part of this year will sow the seeds of recovery on the soil made fertile by super-charged liquidity from the Bangko Sentral (BSP).

Our favored picks on the reopening theme are Wilcon (WLCON PM), ICTSI (ICT PM), GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and Cebu Air (CEB PM). We reckon it’s also worth placing some defensive, yield plays into the basket: PLDT (TEL PM), Globe (GLO PM) and Meralco (MER PM).

MACRO
Bicam approves CREATE Act
  • The bicameral conference committee approved the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act
    • Among the provisions reportedly written into the bill by the panel that were not in the versions previously passed in the Senate and the House was on the fair treatment for petroleum refiners (Section 15)
    • Exempts crude oil intended for refining from duties and excise, and taxes the product upon removal once refined, to avoid stranded value-added tax credits, consistent with international practice
  • Lower CIT from 30% to 20% for small and medium corporations and to 25% for all other corporations
  • Fiscal incentives
    • Up to 17 years of incentives for exporters and for “critical” domestic market enterprises
    • Up to 12 years of incentives for other domestic market enterprises
  • Countryside development
    • Additional 3 years of income tax holiday for registered enterprises that will fully relocate outside of NCR
    • Additional 2 years of income tax holiday for registered enterprises in areas recovering from disasters or conflict
  • Other tax relief: Higher VAT exemption threshold from P1.5mn to P2.5mn for socialized and low-cost housing, and from P2.5mn to P4.2mn for house and lot and other residential dwellings
MACRO
BOP posts US$4.24bn surplus in December; FY20 BOP surplus at US$16.02bn
  • The country’s BOP position posted a surplus of US$4.24bn in December, more than 3x higher than the US$1.57bn surplus in the same period last year
    • Reflected inflows from issuance of US$2.75bn ROP Global bonds
  • Brings FY20 BOP surplus to US$16.02bn, more than 2x higher than in 2019 and the highest on record
    • Due to higher national government foreign borrowings and lower merchandise trade
    • GIR ended 2020 at an all-time high of US$110.12bn, equivalent to 11.8 months’ worth of imports
MACRO
DA files petition to lower most favored nation tariff on rice imports
  • The Department of Agriculture (DA) has filed a petition with the Tariff Commission to lower the most favored nation (MFN) tariff on rice imports to 35%
  • The move is aimed at expanding options of traders for sources of rice imports, according to Agriculture Sec. William Dar
  • At present, the in-quota tariff for rice imports or those with the minimum access volume (MAV) is at 40% while those outside the MAV (out-quota) are at 50%
  • The DA’s proposal was met with opposition from industry groups that claimed there is no need to reduce tariffs for rice imports outside Asean because of ample supply in the country
CONSUMER
Duterte imposes price ceiling on pork, chicken products for 60 days
  • President Duterte signed an executive order Monday that imposes price caps on selected pork and poultry products in National Capital Region for 60 days
  • EO 124 sets the price ceiling for pork (kasim/pigue) at P270/kilo and pork liempo at P300/klo
  • It also set a price cap of P160/kilo for chicken
  • The Department of Agriculture had recommended the imposition of price caps due to supply shortage caused by the outbreak of African Swine Fever
  • San Miguel Food & Beverage (FB PM) derives as much as 20% of consolidated revenue from poultry and about 2% from pork
  • Its poultry segment incurred losses in 2Q20/3Q20 due to chicken glut
UTILITIES
ERC to hold hearings on Meralco refund for lapsed regulatory period (Jul 2015-Nov 2020)
  • In an order dated January 11, 2021, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) said it will hold hearings tackling Manila Electric Company’s (MER PM) proposed mechanism to address the issue of rates governing the lapsed period (July 2015-November 2020)
  • Meralco proposed to “true-up” its Actual Weighted Average Tariff (AWAT) of P1.4414/kWh against its approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.3810/kWh
    • The amount of P13.886bn will be refunded to customers
    • Computed as the difference between AWAT and IAR of P0.604/kWh, multiplied by the actual sales in lapsed period
    • Based on projected sales over the next 24 months, the average refund rate will be P0.1528/kWh

  • Our valuation for MER of P340/sh factors in a refund of P73bn
    • This assumes that the difference between the IAR (P1.3810/kWh) and the projected new tariff (P1.032/kWh) will be refunded for the lapsed period
    • If MER’s proposal is approved, this would immediately add the difference (P73bn less P13.9bn), adjusted for tax, to our valuation (taking into account the PV of refunds
    • We estimate this would add roughly P35-40/share
  • Using the IAR (P1.3810/kWh) would also mean an increase of cP9bn in profit annually going forward
