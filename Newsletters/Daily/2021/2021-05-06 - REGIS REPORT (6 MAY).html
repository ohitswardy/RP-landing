REGIS REPORT

06 May 2021

We have a few results to cover today and are seeing a number of positive surprises for the first quarter. The quality of the beats is varied, however and 2Q/3Q will show how sustainable the outperformances will be.

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – Earnings rose 5% in the quarter to P9.5bn with the better-than-expected bottom line largely a result of banking contributions (lower provisioning expenses). The property and retail businesses did not fare as well as the impact of rising Covid-19 infections is still manifesting itself. The retail business reported a 14% drop in revenue but managed to increase profits by 36% due to cost reductions, which we reckon is due to rent concessions.

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (+) – 1Q21 net profit growth of +24% was boosted by reduced taxes, but core operations were tracking slightly above estimates. The branded business managed to grow 4% despite the high base from pantry stocking last year, highlighting that demand for its core products remained resilient. Meanwhile, the non-branded OEM business bounced back sharply (+45% YoY) as the global food sector recovers.

For local investors >> Century Pacific Food – 1Q21 core operations ahead, profit boosted by lower tax

  • Metro Pacific (MPI PM) (-) – 1Q21 headline income of P7.03bn included one offs from the sale of Global Business Power and Don Muang Tollway. Core operations remained soft, with core profit dropping by 25% to P2.57bn. Operations continue to feel the pinch of the pandemic, with demand weakness evident in the power, water, and tollroad units. The company also announced plans to exit its logistics operations by year-end to curtail losses.

  • D&L Industries (DNL PM) (+) – DNL’s 1Q21 profit of P695mn (+35%YoY, +9%QoQ) was helped by price increases in the food and oleochemical segments. Although still soft, volumes for food (+2%) trended much better than previous quarters. Industrial volumes on the other hand sustained their robust growth trajectories (oleochem +8,  plastics +11%, aerosols +19%).

  • San Miguel Food & Beverage (FB PM) (+) – Consolidated income jumped 66% to P9.68bn, lifted by robust recoveries across all segments. Its outperformance relative to estimates was driven by its food segment, which saw income more than double to P3.39bn due to high prices of chicken and pork. Within alcohol, beer volumes were probably flattish, while gin delivered an impressive 29% volume growth.

+++

Inflation for April came in 4.5%, unchanged from March and below predictions. This print should put off pressure for the Bangko Sentral (BSP) to act, at least by another month.

+++

The government is walking back its previously announced target to vaccinate 70m people by year-end and is now aiming to do 50-70m instead.  For perspective, the population of the Philippines is ~110m.

This comes after President Duterte had himself inoculated with the Sinopharm vaccine, a vaccine that the government has yet to approve and is not available for the general population apart from select members of the Presidential security team. Duterte blamed his doctor for using the Sinopharm jab instead of the Coronavac one made by Sinovac (the one most widely available now and being administered even to senior citizens).

To date, the country has received 4m doses and administered 2m. The government seems to be congratulating itself for doing a good job, claiming the country is #3 within SE Asia it terms of number of doses administered. Someone must have forgotten that the country’s population is second only to Indonesia within SE Asia and the Philippines has many many times more people than Singapore.

MACRO
Inflation steady at 4.5% in April
  • Headline inflation settled at 4.5% in April, broadly the same as in March (4.5%) but faster than the inflation print a year ago (2.2%)
    • Lower than street forecast (4.7%) and within the 4.2%-5.0% target range of the BSP
  • Food inflation decelerated, despite another increase in meat inflation
    • Even with the reduction in pork tariffs, prices of pork still rose, as well as prices of chicken, and beef also increased
    • These were offset by decelerating inflation for rice, fruits, and vegetables
  • Transport costs continued to surge, as tricycle fares surged by +48% YoY, followed by fuel costs (+32% YoY) and jeepney fares (+6% YoY)
  • Brings YT-April average inflation to 4.5%, above the government’s target range of 2-4% for the year

PROPERTY
SM Prime 1Q21 reservation sales grow 31% on overseas sales
  • Jose Mari Banzon, head of SM Prime’s residential division, said that overseas sales comprised 81% of 1Q21 reservation sales
    • This implies that overseas sales jumped 116% while domestic sales halved
    • Other countries are recovering better than the Philippines and buyers generally have higher paying occupations
  • 77% of SM Prime’s in-house sales agents and third party brokers are based abroad
  • Mr. Banzon believes that the P32.4bn of reservation sales in 1Q21 could be sustained for the rest of the year
  • Going forward, SM Prime will increase launches of affordable housing in provincial areas
    • Average selling price below P3mn, from the average of P6mn in 1Q21
  • SM Prime CFO John Ong said that 1Q21 mall foot traffic was 50% of pre-COVID levels (similar to 4Q20) but fell to 33% in April
  • SMPH is currently trading at 30.8X FY21E P/E and 11% discount to NAV

CONSUMER
FB consolidated net income up 66%
  • In a press release, San Miguel Food and Beverage said its consolidated net income grew 66% to P9.68bn in 1Q21
  • Consolidated EBITDA increased 35% to P15.6bn while operating income jumped 45%YoY to P12.6bn
  • Against our consolidated estimates (FY21E consolidated income P27.1bn), the headline numbers appear to be tracking ahead
  • San Miguel Brewery’s net income increased 45% to P5.5bn – in line (FY21E P21.6bn)
    • Revenues up 2% to P28.8bn
    • “Volumes remain depressed”
  • Ginebra’s income jumped 120% to P1.04bn on the back a 29% increase in volumes    
  • San Miguel Foods’ income more than doubled to P3.39bn – well ahead (FY21E P5.1bn)
    • 11% revenue growth of the protein segment (chicken, pork) due to better demand-supply dynamics
    • Prepared and packaged food segment (canned goods, frozen meats) revenues grew 6%
OTHERS
COVID vaccine target may need to be adjusted
  • “Vaccine Czar” Carlito Galvez Jr. said that the government’s target to inoculate 70mn Filipinos by the end of the year would have to be tweaked
  • He now recommends prioritizing areas “that have economic and social importance”
  • He is now targeting 70% herd immunity in those areas (mostly urban centers)
    • Now targeting 50mn to 70mn vaccine recipients - focused on those areas
  • The country has received 4.04mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 2.07mn doses
