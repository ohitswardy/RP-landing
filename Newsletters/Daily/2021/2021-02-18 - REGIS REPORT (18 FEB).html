REGIS REPORT

18 February 2021

Metrobank missed but surprised. Despite disappointing NPAT of P13.8bn, the bank declared a P3/sh special dividend that took div yield to a whopping 8.3%. To be fair, the miss was due to continued high pre-emptive provision expense in 4Q; the P40.8bn full-year provision translates to a credit cost of fully 325bps, and helped them end the year with 163% NPL coverage, despite NPLs rising to 2.41%. The bank’s “core” business was mixed, though. Loans contracted 15.5% YoY, the largest decline on record (going back to 1993). NIMs compressed by 31bps QoQ due in part to the credit card rate cap imposed from November. While deposit costs plunged to a record-low 0.37% (-9bps QoQ), asset yields are fast catching up (down?). The outlook for 2021E may look uninspiring – for the bank and for the sector – but MBT stock is now trading at 0.68x its trailing 2020 book, sounds a bit harsh.

President Duterte signed the unfortunately-acronymed FIST Act into law, giving tax incentives to banks that sell NPLs/NPAs to asset management companies (AMCs) or FIST-Cs. We believe the near-term P&L impact will be negligible. But we believe FIST could remove the NPL/provision overhang, and could serve as a re-rating catalyst for the sector. The banks we cover are trading at 25-45% discounts to their long-term average P/bk ratings.

The Philippines signed new agreements with Moderna and Johnson & Johnson for them to supply the country with 20m and 5m doses of their Covid-19 vaccines. We estimate that the Philippines has signed agreements for 127m doses so far, with most coming only from 3Q21 onwards.

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) disclosed it was selling its 29% stake in Don Muang Tollway in Thailand for P7.2bn, which is slightly better than our valuation. DMT accounts for 3% of MPI’s and its sale should boost MPI’s coffers more ahead of its heavy investment mode.

The SEC has approved the listing of Double Dragon’s (DD PM) REIT, with could have a maximum market cap of P40bn. We are, however, concerned about the sustainability of DD’s office rental revenue streams as we reckon two-third of its space is leased to POGO and POGO-related tenants, despite disavowals. While it’s true that many of these tenants are not strictly POGOs, they are, however, firms who provide services to them.

First Philippine Holdings (FPH PM) is lowering (again) the maximum number of shares it is offering to buy in its tender for Lopez Holdings (LPZ PM) shares. The new, revised cap is 1.43bn shares or 31.5% of LPZ’s share capital, from 1.57bn shares (34.5%) previously. It was initially 2.07bn shares (45.6%) which would have been the entire float of LPZ. After announcing a plan that would result in LPZ being delisted, FPH now appears to be doing its utmost to avoid the slightest possibility of LPZ becoming delisted.

Advocacy group Action for Economic Reforms (AER) is calling for a presidential veto of certain provisions in “CREATE”, the corporate tax reform law passed by Congress.  Among those AER is calling into questions are last minute insertions to favor the local oil refinery, and a provision that would emasculate the ability of the executive department to review tax incentives previously granted by legislative franchise. We believe there is solid rationale to veto these provisions but whether or not Duterte will choose to do so is another matter altogether. We shall see in a few weeks.

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported NPAT for FY20 of P13.8bn, down 51% YoY, and falling well behind consensus of P16.5bn and our own P19.5bn forecast. The miss was due primarily to continued large provisions – P5.4bn for 4Q alone – with FY at P40.8bn. This took the annual credit cost to an extremely high 325bps. We would characterize the FY provisioning as pre-emptive, given the bank’s year-end NPL cover of 163% (though down from 174% in 3Q). To the downside, NIMs crashed 31bps QoQ as asset yields rapidly caught up with rock-bottom funding costs. Loans contracted 15.5% YoY, contributing to the NIM squeeze.

The bank surprised the market with a P3/sh special cash dividend, on top of its P1/sh regular div. The combined P4/sh is equivalent to a div yield of 8.3%. The bank could easily afford it, with its consolidated CAR of 20.15%.

Some more highlights from the FY results:

  • Loans contracted 15.5% YoY, by far the largest full-year drop on our record (back to 1993). While loans were up 2% QoQ, it was slower than the typical 4Q bump (averaging 5-10% in the last decade). Deposits grew 4.8% YoY, driven largely by CASA, which surged 22% YoY, taking CASA ratio to 73%, from 63% in 2019.
  • NIMs crashed 31bps QoQ in 4Q20, though we estimate FY20 margins were still up roughly 30bps YoY. Deposit costs plunged 11bps QoQ to a record-low 0.37% in 4Q20, thanks to the strong CASA growth. However, the improvement in funding costs is moderating, given the rock-bottom rates. Meanwhile, asset yields are finally following the downward trend. Loan yields fell a whopping 74bps QoQ to 5.76%, likely due in part to the interest rate cap on credit cards which took effect in November 2020. We reckon NIM compression will likely remain a feature in 2021E, though likely at a less steep pace than in recent quarters.
  • Fee incomes fell 18% YoY in FY20, and 31% YoY in 4Q20, again likely due in part to the fee caps on credit cards.
  • Trading/fx gains were a staggering P19.2bn in FY20 on falling bond yields, though this moderated to P1.4bn in 4Q alone. Heading into 2021, though, trading could be challenged with rising inflation causing the yield curve to steepen. Roughly 85% of MBT’s investment book is in the FVOCI portfolio, with another 12% in the FVTPL category. To be fair, the impact of market moves on the FVOCI will not be seen in the P&L, but will be a drag on book.
  • Opex was up 4% YoY for FY20, with compensation costs up 5% YoY.
  • Provision expense surged 304% YoY in FY20 to P40.8bn, as the bank used massive trading gains to pre-emptively provide for potential asset quality deterioration. NPL ratio ticked up slightly to 2.41% from 2.25% in 3Q20 and from 1.30% at end-2019. NPL cover stayed high at 163%, though this was down from 174% in 3Q and 188% in 2Q. As with the rest of the industry, the new NPL formation was largely due to the consumer book, notably auto, mortgages and credit cards. We note that MBT wrote off some P8.5bn worth of credit card debt in FY20.

The outlook for 2021 isn’t particularly rosy, what with downward NIM pressures, and a likely lack of trading gains. To the upside, MBT has built up a decent loan loss reserve (LLR) pile, which could cushion the blow of further asset quality deterioration this year.

That said, the stock now trades at 0.68x its trailing 2020 book. With NPL cover at 163% and CAR of 20.2%, we consider the valuation discount rather harsh. We have a Buy on the stock.

The bank will hold an earnings call on Monday 22 February at 4pm (Manila/HK Time).

INFRASTRUCTURE
MPI selling stake in Thai tollroad
  • Metro Pacific Tollways Corp (MPTC) entered into a share purchase agreement (SPA) with several parties for the sale of its entire 29.45% stake in Thailand-based Don Muang Tollway (DMT)
    • MPTC is a 99.9%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM)
    • The transaction is expected to occur within a few days after the signing of SPAs
    • DMT holds the concession for a 21.9km elevated toll road in Bangkok
  • Valuation for the stake is US$149.25mn (P7.168bn), which will be paid in cash on closing
    • This is c. 20% higher than our valuation of P5.9bn
    • MPI also expects to realize a gain on this transaction
  • We estimate the transaction will be very mildly value accretive; DMT accounts for c. 3% of MPI’s NAV
  • MPI is trading at a 26% discount to its market NAV of P5.70/sh

GAMING
Singapore court denies appeal of Bloomberry
  • The Singapore Court of Appeals denied the appeal of Bloomberry subsidiaries against the decision of the Singapore High Court
    • In January 2020, The Singapore High Court dismissed their petition to vacate and oppose the enforcement of the award by the Arbitration Tribunal
  • In a September 2016 Partial Award, an Arbitration Tribunal found that the Management Services Agreement with GGAM was wrongfully terminated and the Tribunal affirmed GGAM’s ownership and right to sell 921.2mn BLOOM shares
    • The Final Award issued in September 2019 ordered Bloomberry to pay GGAM about US$100mn in damages due to the termination of the management services agreement
    • It also ordered BLOOM to buy GGAM’s 921mn shares in BLOOM for P10.17bn, equivalent to P11/sh (or allow the sale of the shares and make up the difference between market sale and P10.17bn)
  • Bloomberry’s counsel reiterated its view that the Partial award may only be enforced in the Philippines through an order of a Philippine court
CONGLOS
FPH amends tender offer for Lopez Holdings shares
  • First Philippine Holdings Corp has modified its tender offer to buy common shares of Lopez Holdings Corp (for a second time) by lowering the number of stocks it plans to acquire
  • Its revised tender offer will cover up to a max of 1.43bn common shares, equivalent to 31.5% of the total issued and outstanding common shares
    • The original plan was to acquire a minimum of 908.46mn (20%) up to a maximum of 2.07bn shares (45.56%) at a price of P3.85/share (a 25% premium to the stock price when the restructuring was announced, 27% discount to NAV)
    • The first amendment reduced the maximum number of shares to be acquired to 1.57bn shares (34.5%)
  • The tender offer price is unchanged (at P3.85/share)
  • The offer period was also extended from February 19 to March 8
  • The reduction in the number of shares will remove the risk of Lopez Holdings falling below the required minimum public ownership
  • Thus, LPZ will remain listed after the tender offer
