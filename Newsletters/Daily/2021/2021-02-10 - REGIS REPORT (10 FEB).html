REGIS REPORT

10 February 2021

Globe (GLO PM) announced FY20 results last night, with core income for the year coming in at P19.5bn, down 13% YoY.  This core income figure is ~10% short of not just our estimates but also the Street’s.

The disappointment comes from the shortfall in revenue, which did not experience the customary 4Q bounce. 4Q mobile revenue was up only 1% QoQ and was still down 12% YoY. The toll from the pandemic and lockdowns is still quite evident.  The revenue disappointment wasn’t limited to the mobile segment as revenue from home broadband also didn’t grow as briskly as we anticipated despite subscriber gains. The emerging bright spot is that Globe added 53k wired broadband subscribers in the last quarter, the largest such increase in years.

The company’s earnings briefing is being held this morning and we should have more color after that.

+++

NPLs reportedly dropped to 3.70% in December from 3.88% in November. However, we believe the “improvement” was more cosmetic, likely driven by large write-offs of credit card and unsecured personal loans. Our assertion re write-offs is supported by comments by management of BPI during their earnings call, as well as our conversations with other banks. Banks do not disclose new NPL formation, which makes assessing borrowers’ health tricky. But continued “stress” is evident in a 50% MoM surge in restructured loans, with the RL ratio rising from 1.31% in November to 1.91% of loans in December. To the upside, banks seem to remain conservative, and likely put through another round of large provisions in 4Q20, as evidenced by an improvement in NPL coverage to 94%.

We remain wary of asset quality deterioration into 1Q21, given the run-off of the Bayanihan 2 grace periods. The combination of continued mobility restrictions and inflation are likely not helping.

TELCOS
Globe 4Q/FY20 – initial comments
  • Results were disappointing as revenue did not see the usual sequential lift in 4Q.  FY20 core income of P19.5bn was down 13% YoY  and 10% short of estimates.
  • Mobile revenue was still down double digits (-12% YoY) in 4Q. Mobile data usage grew 15% QoQ in 4Q but revenue yield fell to P24.5/per GB (-8% QoQ). The confinement of people indoors/at home is having a bigger impact on Globe, I suspect than PLDT.
  • Home broadband is growing but the pace is a disappointment considering the number of subscribers being added. 4Q revenue was up only 3% QoQ despite 9% increase in subs spread over both wired and fixed wireless services.
  • Dividend of P27/share for 1Q21 was declared. This implies P108/share for FY21, which is just in line with our estimate (P110/share) and is ~5% yield.
  • Capex guidance of P70bn for FY21, from P60bn spent in FY20.

 

PROPERTY
Colliers: office rents and residential prices drop in 2020
  • Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy rose from 4.3% in 2019 to 9.1% in 2020
  • Net vacancy was 180,000sqm, largely due to POGO closures
  • Metro Manila office rental rates fell 17%
  • 2021: Colliers forecasts office vacancy to rise to 12.5%, rental rates to fall another 15% 
  • Meanwhile, Metro Manila residential vacancy increased from 11.0% in 2019 to 15.6% in 2020
  • Residential prices fell 13%
  • 2021: Colliers expects residential vacancy to rise to 16.9%
BANKS
Higher ATM fees loom
  • Philippine banks are set to implement the acquirer-based fees for ATM transactions starting April wherein the fees charged to a cardholder for a withdrawal transaction or balance inquiry will be based on the fees of the other banks or ATM owners
  • BPI said that its clients can continue to use their BPI debit cards on BPI ATMs at no cost
    • Fees would be charged if clients use local non-BPI ATMS via BancNet
    • Effective April 7, BPI will charge other bank’s cardholders using BPI ATMs P18 for every cash withdrawal, 20% higher than the current charge of P15 per transaction
  • Under the previous issuer-based method, the fee is set and charged by the issuing bank and the amount charged to the cardholder remains the same regardless of who the ATM acquiring bank is
  • Electronic cash transfers via PESONet surged by 376% to 15.3mn in terms of volume and 188% to P951.6bn in terms of value
  • Volume of low value fund transfers via InstaPay rose by 459% to 86.7mn while the value surged 340% to P463.4bn
