REGIS REPORT

07 January 2021

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) ‘rolled over’ its P540bn loan to the national government, ensuring the Treasury will not need to go to the public markets and compete for funding and ultimately drive up rates. The BSP has already signaled that it intends to maintain the current low-rate environment, which may extend for another couple of years.

+++

We publish a new note adjusting our numbers for LT Group (LTG PM), and re-affirming our Buy rating with a refreshed target price of P16/share. In this environment of record-low interest rates, its dividend yield of 6% (which could potentially double), should be tempting for investors looking to meet their hurdle rates.

Our earnings forecasts have been raised by 8-14% for FY20-FY22E on the back of higher earnings of the tobacco (+14-23%) and liquor (+112-156%) units. These offset the 20-49% cut to PNB (PNB PM) earnings. We also increased our NAV estimate by 19% to P32/share. These would put LTG on 5.8x PE for 2021 and 60% discount to NAV – off the lows – but still among the most inexpensive names in the market, despite 1) earnings actually performing well during the pandemic and 2) dividends tripling in 2020 from 2019.

Dividend-wise, we reckon LTG can sustain dividends of P0.82-0.84/year – 6.4% yield at a 40% payout. These are conservative assumptions in our view, as the dividend represents less than half of what they upstream from PMFTC annually. Even with this payout, we expect LTG would still be able to build up cash reserves for other contingencies (capital infusion to PNB?) to the tune of P9-10bn per year. In a blue skies scenario, the yield could rise to as high as 12%.

Despite the rally last year, we think the shares have legs to continue on the back of a dividend outlook that continues to improve. Our new target price of P16/share (based on a 50% discount to NAV) presents an upside of 23%. Check out the note from Aaron (aaron.salvador@regis.ph) -

MACRO
BSP lends another P540bn to gov’t for pandemic response
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) approved another zero-interest bridge loan worth P540bn loan to the national government last Dec 28
  • This is the third time the BSP threw a credit line to the national government
    • The BSP loaned P300bn in March (repaid in September) and another P540bn in October 2020 (repaid in December)
    • Under the law, the central bank can lend the government a maximum of about P850bn this year
  • 10-year BVAL at 2.956%, lower than in March (5%)
