REGIS REPORT

14 May 2021

The government is moving the lockdown classification of Metro Manila and adjacent areas (“NCR plus”) to a supposedly looser level. This time it’s being called “general community quarantine (GCQ) with heightened restrictions” (GCQ-WHR?), adding to the panoply of acronyms being used for the lockdown statuses (ECQ, MECQ, GCQ etc).

From a practical perspective, however, the changes from the current “MECQ” will be minimal. For example, the allowed capacity for restaurant dining-in will be raised to 20% from 10%. Most other rules will be the same.

+++

Our consumer analyst Carissa (carissa.mangubat@regis.ph) released a new report on the consumer goods manufacturing names, Universal Robina (URC PM) and Century Pacific Foods (CNPF PM).

She re-affirms her BUY ratings on the two, arguing that they are not only weathering the pandemic well but, from some perspectives, actually benefiting from it. Both URC and CNPF are seeing jumps in market share as (1) thinner consumer wallets crowd towards more trusted brands and (2) smaller players struggle to operate under the challenging pandemic conditions and the associated costs. She’s thus raised her estimates and target prices for the two. We reckon valuations are attractive for both, but the multiple vs growth profile of CNPF looks especially compelling.

Vista Land (VLL PM) is issuing another US$170m in notes due 2027 at 6.5% (yes, that’s correct: 6.5% for six-year paper), presumably to refinance debt maturing next year. VLL has just around US$2bn of net debt, which is now more than double its market cap (US0.9bn).

+++

Broadband via satellite technology is now available in the Philippines and no, it’s not from Elon Musk’s Starlink. PLDT’s (TEL PM) indirect subsidiary Cignal launched its CIgnal Connect service. The pricing, however, confirms what we suspected it would be: an expensive niche service catering to places where both wired broadband and mobile data are unavailable.

+++

The Monetary Board of the Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept policy rates unchanged at 2%, as expected. With local inflation likely to accelerate over the near-term and increasing pressure for US rates to go up, the calls to raise rates may also rise. We suspect the current BSP leadership, however, is inclined to be dovish and will resist calls to lift rates for as long as it can.

Gov’t eases quarantine status in NCR+; shifts to “GCQ with heightened restrictions”
  • Metro Manila and 4 adjacent provinces will be under GCQ 'with heightened restrictions' from May 15 until the end of May
    • 18 to 65-year-old persons can leave their homes
    • Only essential travel in and out of NCR Plus allowed
    • 20% capacity for indoor dine-in in restaurants, 50% capacity for outdoor or al fresco dine-in
    • 30% capacity for outdoor tourist sites
    • 10% capacity for religious gatherings, gatherings for necrological services, wakes, inurnment, funerals
    • Outdoor non-contact sports, games, scrimmages outdoors
    • 30% capacity for personal care services which do not require the removal of masks, like salons, parlors, and beauty clinics

BANKS
PNB 1Q21 NPAT +34% YoY; provisions drop despite rising NPLs
  • PNB reported 1Q21 NPAT of P1.77bn, up 34% YoY. Despite the headline number, we reckon the quality of earnings was pretty poor. The bank cut provisions, which was a bit of a head-scratcher, given a jump in NPLs from 10.20% at end-2020 to 10.74% in 1Q21. This took NPL cover to an in-credibly low 43%. (Even the NPL figure was odd, as by our calculation, the end-2020 ratio was 11.1% based on the bank’s FS.)
  • Loans contracted 6.4%, and NIMs collapsed 72bps QoQ and 43bps YoY. To the upside, fee incomes were very strong, handily surpassing pre-Covid levels.

OTHERS
Foreign ownership falls slightly in April
  • We estimate that total market foreign ownership fell 7bps MoM to 21.7% while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 4bps MoM to 25.0%
  • 25 out of 30 PSEi constituents saw declines in their foreign ownership
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were
    • ALI – foreign ownership fell 97 basis points MoM to 35.2%. Ayala Land was the top foreign sold stock for the second consecutive month, with net foreign selling of US$91.3mn in April. This represented about 1/3 of foreign outflows in the market
    • MBT – foreign ownership declined 38 basis points MoM to 21.0%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$13.3mn in April, the third highest among Philippine stocks
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership increased 8 basis points MoM to 24.6%. BLOOM had the greatest increase in foreign ownership among PSE index constituents
