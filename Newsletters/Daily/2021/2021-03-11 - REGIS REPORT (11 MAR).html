REGIS REPORT

11 March 2021

Select jurisdictions across Metro Manila have started imposing curfews in response to the recent jump in Covid-19 cases. This is a setback for businesses, especially restaurants as they’d have to close early to allow staff to arrive home before the curfews. The national government has been trying to help businesses recover by coordinating uniform regulations for travel and we expect some positive steps here. However, the spike in cases will likely lead to tighter restrictions on other aspects.

+++

Revenue collections by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) met target for Feb with collections reaching P134bn (-6%). However, the impact of the tax reform package dubbed CREATE, still not signed into law, has yet to be felt. CREATE will reduce tax rates for large corporations by 5ppt (to 25%) and for small firms by 10ppt (to 20%).

+++

San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM) announced that its consolidated income was P22.4bn in FY20, down 30%. Lest one forgets, we remind everyone that that figure is inclusive of the share of minorities and taking it out would likely cut the figure in half, which would be well below Street estimates. Our FY20 estimate is for P21bn in consolidated income (inc. minorities) and P12bn (after share of minorities).  Some detail below.

+++

Filinvest Land (FLI PM) revealed plans to spin off some of its office portfolio into a REIT company. At the maximum price, the proposed REIT firm would have a market  cap of P41bn, almost 50% bigger than Filinvest Land. Maybe this IPO would finally serve as a catalyst for FLI’s share price and a narrowing of its discount to NAV.

+++

The results briefing of Aboitiz Power (AP PM) reminds us yet again that economy is still quite weak. FY20 core income (which includes business insurance claims) was P12.5bn (-25%), slightly lower than our estimates. 4Q electricity volumes were still down 9% as commercial-industrial usage plunged 14%. AP’s large coal plant project GN Power Dinginin is looking like it will be delayed yet again.  Completion of GNPD is now looking like it will be pushed back to late 2021 and/or early 2022. This would mean the project will be over two years late. More from Aaron (aaron.salvador@regis.ph) here:

2020 FDI inflows slump to 5-year low
  • Preliminary data released by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) showed FDI net inflows shrank by 24.6% to US$6.542bn in 2020 from US$8.671bn in 2019
    • Lowest since 2015, but exceeded the US$6bn full-year projection made by the BSP
  • In December alone, FDI plunged 62.6% YoY to US$509mn
    • Cited base effects given significantly large inflows from net investments in equity capital and debt instruments in 2019
    • Equity other than reinvestment of earnings dropped by 89.8% to US$78mn in December
    • Investments in equity and fund shares likewise fell by 82.2% to US$149mn
    • Inflows to debt instruments went down by 31.1% to US$360mn
    • Reinvestment of earnings declined 2.6% to US$71mn
  • The BSP expects FDI to reach US$7.5bn in 2021, up 15% from the 2020 level

BIR collections down 6% YoY to P134.27bn in February
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collected P134.27bn in February, in line with its target of P134.18bn

Domestic travel now easier
  • Most local governments around the country have been complying with the uniform protocols which relaxed requirements on domestic travel
    • 80% of local governments from cities, municipalities and provinces have complied with provisions
    • According to Interior Undersecretary Epimaco Densing III
  • Last February, the Inter-Agency Task Force for the Management of Emerging Infectious Diseases (IATF) issued Resolution No. 101, which laid down the relaxed requirements on domestic travel
    • The uniform travel protocols removed the requirements of travel authority documents
    • Travelers are no longer required to undergo RT-PCR tests but the local government may still impose this
PROPERTY
Filinvest Land to raise up to P15bn from REIT IPO
  • Cyberzone Properties Inc (CPI), a wholly owned subsidiary of Filinvest Land, will conduct a REIT IPO
  • Maximum offer price of P8.30/share – maximum offer size of P14.9bn
    • Secondary shares – 1.63bn shares
    • Overallotment option – 163mn shares
  • At the maximum price, CPI would have a market cap of P40.6bn, higher than FLI’s market cap of P28.3bn
  • CPI owns and operates office buildings in Alabang and Cebu
    • PEZA accredited
    • It does not own the land under the buildings
  • CPI comprises c. 300,000sqm of FLI’s 525,000sqm office portfolio
  • It has an occupancy rate of c. 90%
    • Less than 5% of its portfolio is occupied by POGOs
  • Its 9M20 office revenue grew 16% to P2.46bn
    • 57% of FLI’s office revenue
  • We estimate that at the maximum offer price, CPI would be valued at a cap rate of c. 5.5%
  • We estimate that if Filinvest Land’s entire office portfolio were to be spun off into a REIT (at 6% cap rate), FLI’s NAV could rise by 12%
    • The NAV enhancement of spinning off only CPI assets into a REIT would likely be c. 7%
  • FLI is currently trading at 5.5X FY21E P/E and 62% discount to NAV
