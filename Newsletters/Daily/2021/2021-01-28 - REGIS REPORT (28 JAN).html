REGIS REPORT

28 January 2021

Philippine GDP contracted by 8.3% YoY in 4Q20, ahead of our -10% forecast for the quarter, but worse than consensus at -7.9%. This took FY20 GDP to -9.5%, the country’s worst full-year contraction on record (since 1981). It will also likely be the worst 4Q/FY20 GDP print among our Asian peers. The domestic demand picture looks pretty weak, with personal consumption still -7.2% YoY in 4Q20, and capital formation crashing -29.0% YoY. The economy got a lift from government spending (+4.4%) and from a sharp drop in imports (-18.8%).

Our current base case is for the economy to recover +7% in 2021. However, this is highly contingent on the Philippine government easing mobility restrictions. Early indications are that the Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) will recommend that Metro Manila remains under the current General Community Quarantine (GCQ) status at least  until end-February. The Google mobility data isn’t particularly promising, with movement declining steadily in the first three weeks of the year, with the latest data point (22 January) back to around late 3Q / early 4Q levels.

Much then will depend on the government’s ability to procure and distribute vaccines, to provide herd immunity. Thus far, we have signed deals for roughly 75 mn doses with various manufacturers. The latest news is that the Philippines is negotiating for 20mn doses from Moderna and 10mn doses from Johnson & Johnson. In a recent interview, Secretary of Finance Carlos Dominguez said the government aims to inoculate 55mn Filipinos, representing 70% of the population above the age of 18 (since the vaccines are not yet approved for children).

The trick will be to get the vaccines in a timely manner. Already, rich nations are squabbling over supplies, which are unsurprisingly hitting delays. This could put further pressure on developing countries like the Philippines. The Economic Intelligence Unit (EIU) estimates that countries like the Philippines will only get to 60% vaccination of the population by 3Q2023 (Source).

To the upside, there are a number of other vaccines under development. And many of the newer vaccines do not have the production and logistics concerns (ie super low storage temperatures) of the mRNA ones like Pfizer’s. Hope, as they say, springs eternal.

+++

Mobile number portability (MNP) will finally be implemented by end of Sept, if the telecom regulator’s timeline is to be believed. The law mandating MNP, which will allow subscribers to keep their numbers even when they switch carriers, was passed two years ago.

This measure is one of those introduced under this government aimed at easing the path for new carriers (e.g. Dito). However, we believe the overall impact of MNP will be fairly modest, and will be more felt via increased churn on the postpaid business. Postpaid subscribers are likely to be more attached to their mobile numbers, enough to bother with the paperwork that comes with switching. Postpaid subscribers account for ~3% of total subscriber base and 20% of mobile revenue for PLDT (TEL PM) and 28% for Globe (GLO PM).

One other consequence of MNP is that it should accelerate the erosion of the value of mobile packages offering unlimited on-net (intra-network) calls and/or SMS. Subscribers will find it more difficult to identify which numbers are with which carrier once MNP is implemented.
