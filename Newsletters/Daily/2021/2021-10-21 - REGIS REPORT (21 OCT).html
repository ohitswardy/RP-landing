REGIS REPORT

21 October 2021

BPI (BPI PM) reported in a press release that its 9M21 NPAT came in at P17.5bn, flat (+1.8%) YoY, tracking behind consensus (P24.6bn for FY21). For 3Q21 alone, NPAT was about P5.7bn, up 3% YoY. The main drag came from provision expense, which jumped 31% QoQ to P3.8bn, the highest quarterly number so far this year. We reckon this included some write-offs, as the bank reported NPLs of 2.76% (down 21bps QoQ) and NPL cover of 131% (up from 120% in 2Q).

Some highlights from the limited release:

  • Loans grew 0.9% YoY, reversing the 5.1% contraction in 2Q21. Growth was driven by mortgages, credit cards, and micro loans.
  • Deposits were up 6.6% YoY. However, CASA ratio fell to 80% in 3Q, from 83% in 2Q. We estimate that BPI added a whopping P75bn in TDs in 3Q21 alone (+26% QoQ). Part of the TD increase was due to the maturing of its P33bn worth of bonds.
  • NIMs were flat QoQ at 3.31%, by BPI’s estimate. Funding costs likely improved on the bond maturity, while we reckon loan yields were probably still under pressure.
  • Non-interest income was down 13% YoY in 9M21, due largely to the high base of trading gains in 2020. Fee incomes were up 27% YoY in 9M21, but seem to have fallen QoQ (1H21 included some large IPOs / follow-ons).
  • Provision expense was down 51% YoY to P10.3bn in 9M21, but were up sharply QoQ in 3Q to P3.8bn. This was despite NPL ratio falling 21bps QoQ to 2.73%. It is likely that the provision included some write-offs, as well as continued pre-emptive provisioning, as NPL cover rose to 131% from 120% in 2Q

The bank will hold an earnings call tomorrow, Friday at 430pm Manila/HK time.

AC Energy (ACEN PM) disclosed more transactions to consolidate its ownership in its projects. This time the purchases involve the wind farms in Northern Luzon. While the transactions are technically not share swaps, from all appearances they are. As they say, if it walks and quacks like a duck…

The deals (US$110m total value) will not move the need much in terms of ACEN’s valuation. However, it demonstrates how ACEN is using its very richly valued currency to acquire assets on the cheap. More below.

+++

Century Pacific (CNPF PM) is venturing into a high growth and very profitable market – pet food. We reckon this business has a large long-term potential.

The BPO industry association lifted its expectations for the industry’s full year growth to 8% (previously 5-6.5%). The upward revision is a positive for the office property market but we still expect vacancy rates to climb as supply rises amidst tenant (e.g. POGOs) departures.

CONSUMER
CNPF announces foray into pet food
  • In a press release, CNPF announced it is entering the pet food category through its brand Goodest
  • CNPF will initially offer an affordable cat food range in leading supermarket chains and online retail platforms
  • The size of the domestic pet food industry is estimated to be USD150-200mn, but this figure only captures the branded pet food market
    • The company estimates branded pet food penetration to be c1%
  • CNPF's pet food products will be positioned as a more affordable alternative to imported brands (Pedigree, Alpo, Whiskas, Royal Canin, etc) that dominate the market
POWER
ACEN announces more acquisitions; to raise stakes in 3 wind farms
  • AC Energy announced that it is raising its interests in three wind farms by acquiring its partners’ stakes in Northwind Power Development Corp and other interests of UPC Philippines Wind Investment Co
  • In Northwind (52MW wind), ACEN will acquire the 32.2% interest of its partners for P1.09bn; in turn, the sellers will subscribe to 90mn ACEN shares at P11.32/share (P1.02bn)
    • ACEN will raise its interest in Northwind to 100%
    • We estimate the implied EV/MW for this transaction is $2.2mn/MW (vs $1.6mn/MW at our DCF estimate)
  • ACEN will also acquire the ownership of UPC Philippines and Stella Marie Sutton in 81 MW wind farm North Luzon Renewables (11% stake) and 155MW Bayog Wind project (15% stake) for P4.5bn; in turn the sellers will subscribe to 390mn ACEN shares at P11.32/share (P4.4bn)
    • We estimate the implied EV/MW for this transaction is $2.4mn/MW
PROPERTY
BPO industry to grow 8% in 2021
  • The IT and Business Processing Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) now expects the industry to grow (both revenue and employees by) 8% this year
    • Cited demand from sectors such as financial services and communications
    • It previously forecasted the BPO industry to grow 5%-6.5% this year
  • New 2021 targets:
    • 1.43mn employees
    • US$28.8bn in revenue
  • The industry grew 1-2% in 2020
  • Despite the expectation of growth for the BPO sector this year, we still expect office vacancy to rise to 15% by end-2021
    • 9% as of 4Q20
    • 13% as of 2Q21
  • While some BPOs want to sign-up for more office space, they are hesitant to do so while regulations prevent them from fully utilizing the office
  • Office leasing accounts for:
    • 58% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
    • 46% of Megaworld’s NAV
  • FLI is currently trading at 60% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 5.6X FY22E P/E
  • MEG is currently trading at 59% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 6.9X FY22E P/E
