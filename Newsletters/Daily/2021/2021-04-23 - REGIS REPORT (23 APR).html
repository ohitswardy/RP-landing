REGIS REPORT

23 April 2021

BPI (BPI PM) is first of the blocks for results for 1Q.

The bank reported a headline 1Q21 NPAT of P5bn (-22% YoY), which was ahead of REGIS estimates, and in line with consensus. There was some “noise” from trading gains (+) and write-offs of deferred tax assets (-). Most crucial, though, was the drop in provision expense. BPI reported remarkably benign NPLs of 2.76%, just 8bps higher QoQ, in stark contrast to the sharp jump in NPLs for the industry (4.16% as of Feb). The bank will host an earnings call at 4:30pm this afternoon.

BPI also expressed interest in the credit card portfolio of Citibank, which announced plans to exit the consumer banking business in the Philippines (together with a dozen other markets). Given BPI’s recent re-engagement in the consumer loan market, Citi’s portfolio would indeed be a prime target. As of 2020, BPI’s was a far third to BDO and MBT in terms of credit card receivables. It is unclear how much of Citi’s P99bn loan portfolio at end-2020 was credit cards. However, BPI management was quoted saying that acquiring Citi’s book “will almost double” BPI’s portfolio. If true, an acquisition could catapult them to the #1 spot. Though we doubt the other banks will sit idly by…

Vista Land’s (VLL PM) FY20 net profit fell 45% to P6.2bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, due to construction delays in 4Q20. FY20 rental revenue, dominated by malls, fell only 7%, as the anchor tenants still did well, relatively. However, Vista Land’s decision to offer rental concessions to only a few tenants could affect its position longer-term, as tenants occupying 5% of mall space chose to close permanently. Meanwhile, FY20 reservation sales fell 32% to P53.7bn. The company plans to launch more high-end projects this year to take advantage of stronger demand for high-end suburban housing.

Government raises P122bn from euro bond sale
  • The Philippine government raised Eur2.1bn (P122.4bn) from a triple-tranche offering of euro-denominated bonds
    • Eur650mn of 4-year global bonds fetched a coupon of 0.25% (75bps above the tenor’s euro mid swap)
    • Eur650mn of 12-year notes fetched a coupon of 1.2% (+105bps higher than euro mid swap)
    • Eur800mn of 20-year note yielded a coupon of 1.75% (+135bps higher than euro mid swap)
  • This was the largest euro-denominated transaction by the government and the first triple-tranche offer. The 20-year bond was also the longest euro-denominated tenor and largest individual tranche issued by the country
  • All tranches tightened by 25 bps from the initial price guidance backed by a strong order book which allowed the republic to revise its price guidance twice across all three tranches
  • The latest borrowing was rated Baa2 by Moody’s Investors Service, BBB+ by S&P Global Ratings and BBB by Fitch Ratings
  • The government plans to raise P3tn this year both from local and foreign sources to plug its budget deficit seen hitting 8.9% of gross domestic product
    • About P286bn is estimated to come from global bond issuances

BANKS
BPI 1Q21 - flat QoQ; provisions fall, trading surprisingly resilient

In a press release, BPI reported headline NPAT of P5.0bn, down 22% YoY. However, this already included a one-time write-down of deferred tax assets, due to the CREATE law. Pre-tax profit was actually up 5% YoY driven by resilient trading/fx gains, and a drop in provision expense (-15% YoY and -48% QoQ). The lower provision was due to surprisingly mild NPLs of 2.76%, up just 8bps from the 2.68% reported at end-2020. There were also some accounting changes that created some “noise” in the YoY figures (de-consolidation of their leasing arm).

Some highlights:

  • Loans contracted 5% YoY, slightly worse than the -4.6% at end-2020. Mortgages, though were up 10.9%, while micro-loans were up 10.5%; all other categories were lower YoY. Deposits were flat YoY, but CASA grew 12%, taking CASA ratio to an all-time high 82.6%.
  • NIMs likely contracted, as NII was down 7% YoY and 5% QoQ. This is likely due to lower loan and bond yields, while deposit costs are flattening out.
  • Fee incomes recovered nicely, up 14% YoY and 25% QoQ as banking activity (transaction volumes) seems to be normalizing.
  • BPI booked P1.7bn in trading/fx gains. While this was down 20% YoY, it was a positive surprise, given the sharp jump in interest rates in Feb/March.
  • NPLs were surprisingly benign, at 2.76%, up just 8ps QoQ. This was despite the jump in industry NPLs to 4.16% in February.
  • Provision expense dropped to P3.6bn (-48% QoQ and -15% YoY), which brought NPL coverage up to 123.5% from 115.2% at end-2020.
  • Tax expense nearly doubled YoY. The bank took a one-time write-down of its deferred tax assets (DTA) as a result of the lower corporate income tax rate under the CREATE Act.

Overall, the operating figures seem in line with our expectations. We are cautiously optimistic about the benign NPL figures but are wary of jumps in defaults as the Bayanihan 2 Act moratoriums roll off.

BPI will host an earnings call today, Friday 23 April, at 4:30 pm.
