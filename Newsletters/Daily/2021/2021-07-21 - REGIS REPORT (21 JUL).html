REGIS REPORT

21 July 2021

The weekly update on the government’s vaccination drive released Monday shows 15.1m shots have been administered, with 10.4m (9.5% of the population) having received at least one dose.

The daily vaccination rate for the latest full week was 271,000.  This is a meaningful improvement over the previous week (212,000/day) as vaccine supplies improved.  Daily vaccinations, however, will have to more than double (to over 500,000/day) for the government to reach its goals of vaccinating half the population by year-end. Putting it another way, at the current pace (~270,000/day), full vaccination of 70% of the population will only be achieved a year from now.

Given our rather low expectations, we find the improvements in vaccine administration and the projected increase in supply over the coming weeks as encouraging. Concerns over the Delta variant, however, loom.

Philip Morris International (PM US) disclosed that cigarette volumes for its Philippine business, held under PMFTC, amounted to 8.1bn sticks in 2Q.  While this is down 12% YoY, it is sequential improvement (+5%) over 1Q21. We thus expect 2Q profits from tobacco to be better for LT Group (LTG PM), which owns 49.6% of PMFTC.

Cigarette sales volumes have been declining annually since tobacco taxes were first restructured and raised in 2012. From 93bn sticks in 2012, PMFTC’s cigarette volumes are now down to a mere 43bn (as of 2020).  Notwithstanding this, profits have almost trebled over the same period from P12.3bn in 2012 to P33.6bn last year.

The office REIT (“FILRT PM”) that Filinvest Land (FLI PM) is spinning off and listing has been priced at P7/share, allowing it to potentially raise as much as P12.6bn. This could place its market cap at P34bn, which would be even higher than FLI’s own market cap of P27bn.  FILRT will hold 300,000sqm of office property but will not own the land where the office properties are located.

At the offer price, we estimate the dividend yield would be at 6.6% and FLI’s NAV is likely to improve by close to 5%.  For those interested, we have more detail below.

MACRO
DBCC keeps GDP targets; Approves 2022 budget proposal of P5.02tn (+11.5% YoY)
  • The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) retained its GDP growth targets of +6-7% for FY21 and +7-9% for FY22
    • To reach its target, the economy would have to grow an average of +10% in the remaining three quarters of 2021
  • It also approved its 2022 budget proposal of P5.02tn (+11.5% YoY)
    • With revenues expected to recover by +14% YoY in 2022, the fiscal deficit ceiling is projected to narrow to P1.67tn (c. 7.5% of GDP) from a target of P1.86tn in FY21 (9.3% of GDP)
  • It also cut its 2023/2024 fiscal deficit-to-GDP targets to 5.9%/4.9%, respectively from 6.3%/5.3% previously
    • Cited spending cuts following reductions in projected revenue shares of local government units from local taxes
  • The DBCC did not discuss the need for Bayanihan 3, as there’s no directive from the Office of the President
    • President Rodrigo Duterte ordered agencies in May to declare savings from last year's allotments to fund its COVID-19 response instead
