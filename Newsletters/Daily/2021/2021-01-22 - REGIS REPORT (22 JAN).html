REGIS REPORT

22 January 2021

We released a new report upgrading Cebu Air (CEB PM) to a Buy with a revised price target of P77/sh. Some investors will probably scratch their heads, saying: “Buy an airline stock now??”

While 2021 will still be difficult year, we believe investors should already position ahead of a re-opening of the economy and widespread vaccine availability, which is reasonable to expect over the next twelve months. Addressing concerns of the immediate horizon, we note that cost reduction initiatives have reduced cash burn, and the US$500m it is set to raise should be more than adequate to tide it over. For 2022, we expect EBITDA losses to reverse and rise to P24bn, assuming recovery of revenue to 70% of 2019 levels and the impact of cost reduction initiatives. 

Operationally, losses are likely to be peaking now and should begin to recede by late 2021, aided by cost reductions, election-related spending, and vaccine availability. The convertible issuance, which could happen by 2Q21, should put CEB in a much better financial footing relative to its competitors. 

We believe CEB offers compelling value, even assuming the potential dilution from the convertible issuance. On our forecasts, which assume 2022 pax volumes and EBITDA will still be 23% and 15% below 2019, respectively, the stock trades at 5.2x EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers (7.1x) and historical average (7.5x). Employing a target EBITDA multiple of 6.5x, lower than historical average, yields an equity value of P81bn.  This compares with the current market cap of P30bn and the potential convertible issuance of US$500m (or P24bn). Even if we assume the share count doubles due to the converts, it would still yield an equity value per share of P77/sh, offering a 55% shareholder return. 

We also highlight the current troubles facing its main competitor Philippine Airlines. Should the latter scale back operations due to its financial problems, it could provide further tail winds to CEB’s operations and would further increase the potential shareholder return to 200%.

Nissan Philippines is shutting the assembly plant for its Almera sedan in the country. While the government is saying this illustrates the necessity of the recently-announced safeguard duty (P70,000 for passenger cars and P110,000 for commercial vehicles), we wonder if this is more a case of shutting the barn door when the horse has bolted.  It would take much more than a safeguard duty to resuscitate the industry and keep jobs. Years of targeted industrial policy will be required.

In the meantime, we understand the safeguard duty announced has yet to implemented. It was supposed to take effect a couple of days ago and it’s unclear what the reason for the delay is. From the pronouncements of the Department of Trade (DTI), however, this decision of Nissan will likely just harden the DTI’s resolve.

From a near-term perspective, we believe this safeguard duty is likely to be a positive for Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP). Only half of Toyota’s vehicle sales will be directly affected by the duty. This proportion is lower than most car companies and distributors. The Toyota Vios, for example, is one of only a handful of vehicles still assembled locally and thus not subject to the duty.

As we noted before, the glaring exceptions are for luxury cars and, more importantly, for vehicles imported from China and India. A playing field tilted in favor of Chinese imports would amplify their threat to the other car firms in the long run.

On the power sector, First Gen (FGEN PM) says construction for its LNG terminal will begin next month. The LNG facility is supposed to be operational by 3Q22.

Meralco (MER PM) says bidders for its power supply agreement increased their offered capacities for its 1,800MW tender for supply in 2024-25. The deadline for the submission of bids in Jan 27. The results of that bidding could determine what capacities will come on line in 2024-25, with implications for the demand-supply balance.
