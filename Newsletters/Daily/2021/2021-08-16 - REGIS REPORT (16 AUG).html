REGIS REPORT

16 August 2021

The daily Covid-19 case count continues to rise, and is now approaching 15,000. The latest number took the 7-day average to 12,000.

The current surge, unlike the one in March-April, is more geographically dispersed and not as concentrated in Metro Manila (NCR). This is probably attributable to higher vaccination rates in NCR, now likely at 40% already. The strain on health services in NCR has thus been less severe so far. 

A check on the latest Google mobility data shows the latest imposition of “ECQ” has not resulted in as sharp a drop in mobility as last time. This may hint at the economic impact being less severe but it may also lead to subsequently higher infection rates.

+++

We have a few more results comments as the earnings season begins to wind down.

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (+) – 1H21 NPAT was P3.1bn, down 45% YoY, ahead of Regis (P5.13bn), but well behind consensus (P7.64bn). Operational trends were encouraging – loans and NIMs have stabilized QoQ, fees are holding at above pre-Covid levels, and opex seems under control. Consumer loans seem to have stabilized too, rising to 26% of total from 25% in 1Q21. However, NPLs jumped 50bps QoQ to 3.93%, while NPL cover dipped to 103% (from 118% in 1Q), which drove provision expense up to P2bn in 2Q from just P402mn in 1Q. The bank will hold a briefing on Wednesday, 18 August.
  • First Gen (FGEN PM) (+) – Core net income (after divs to preferred shares) increased 10% to $138m, mildly ahead of estimates. Group profits were lifted by natural gas (+21%), as Avion generated higher sales from ancillary services. EDC’s income was flat, due to higher plant and steam field maintenance expenses.

Alliance Global (AGI PM) held its briefing on Friday and it yielded some more detail on the latest results. The losses at Golden Arches, the operator of the McDonald’s franchise, were on account of inventory cost pressures and higher depreciation expenses. These offset the 5%QoQ improvement of company-owned sales. On the other hand, we suspect the P4.8bn gain recognized by Travellers due to “advisory services” may have involved payments by its partner in Westside City as part of their operating agreement. We also highlight the results from two other names we don’t officially cover, Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) and East West Bank (EW PM).

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) saw 2Q revenues jump 16% YoY coming from a low base. While majority of stores have resumed operations, around 50% of its store network operate around schools, offices, and commercial centers and this led to another quarter of losses.

For East West Bank (EW PM), 1H profit was P3.8bn in 1H21, down 15% YoY. For 2Q alone, profit was down 19% YoY and down 13% QoQ. Apart from lower trading/fx gains, we note that NII was lower YoY, but crucially was down 5% QoQ in 2Q21. Loans contracted 12% YoY and NIMs were down c100bps. While the press release did not mention NPLs, the bank’s SOC showed parent company NPL ratio was at 10.09% compared to 10.20% in 1Q21, and NPL cover was flat QoQ at 47.6%

MACRO
Mobility - ECQ not as bad this time around
  • We’re just over a week into the new ECQ lockdowns. In theory, the current restrictions are harsher than the ones set in April/May. However, based on Google data, mobility seems better than the three previous lockdowns. See the charts below for key activities, both nationwide and for Metro Manila.
  • The data suggests that maybe businesses and households have adapted, and that there is growing acceptance that the periodic lockdowns are a “new normal”. Maybe it’s partly because this time around, the government telegraphed the ECQ ahead of time. Recall that in April this year, the pre-Easter announcement took everyone by surprise.
  • In any case, the initial drops in mobility (highlighted in yellow) this time seem much shallower than the earlier ECQs.

BANKS
SECB 2Q21 - Revenues up, but so are NPLs
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1H21 NPAT of P3.1bn, down 45% YoY. NPAT was ahead of our FY forecasts, though PPOP was in line to slightly behind. Operational trends are encouraging, with NII up 4% QoQ, NIMs stabilizing, and fee incomes staying firm. However, NPLs jumped 50bps QoQ to 3.93%, while NPL cover dropped to 103% (from 118% in 1Q21). We suspect the consumer loan portfolio accounted for the deteriorating asset quality. The bank will hold a briefing on Wednesday 18 August at 4pm.

Some highlights:

  • Loans contracted 5.4% YoY (gross loans -4%), and -1.2% QoQ. Consumer loans were down 12% YoY, but were likely already up QoQ. Consumer loans made up 26% of total loans, up slightly from 25% in 1Q (peak was 29% at end-2019). Deposits were up 2% YoY and +0.7% QoQ.
  • NIMs were flat QoQ at 4.07%, but still down 24bps YoY. Loan yields are also flattening out (-7bps QoQ), while deposit costs continued to drop 23bps QoQ to 0.48%. NIMs also benefitted from the maturity of higher-yielding bonds.
  • Fee income was up 26% in 1H21 (+68% in 2Q alone due to low base), and are firmly back to pre-pandemic levels.
  • Trading/fx gains were down 87% YoY in 1H due to the extremely high base in 2020
  • Opex was flat in 1H21. Compensation expenses were up 7% while occupancy costs jumped 21% (rent concessions in 2Q20).
  • Provision expense were down 78% in 1H21, but were up sharply QoQ in 2Q to P2bn. NPL ratio jumped 50bps QoQ to 3.93%, after improving in 4Q20 and 1Q21. NPL cover fell 15bps QoQ to 103%.
  • Income taxes moderated QoQ in 2Q; recall that the bank booked P1.2bn in write-offs of deferred tax assets (DTA) in 1Q21 as a result of CREATE.

BANKS
EW 1H21 NPAT at P3.8bn -15%; 2Q21 remains soft

In a press release, East West Bank (EW PM) reported 1H21 NPAT of P3.8bn, down 15% YoY. For 2Q alone, NPAT was about P1.8bn, down 19% YoY and down 13% QoQ. The disclosure had very little information, but below are some highlights:

  • Trading/fx gains accounted for a chunk of the decline.
  • However, we note that the slump was also due to lower NII, which was down 14% in 1H21, due to a combination of lower loans (-12% YoY) and NIMs (-100bps YoY, EW estimate). For 2Q21 alone, NII was down 17% YoY and down 5% QoQ.
  • Fee incomes were up 23% YoY in line with industry.
  • Provision expense fell 74%. While the press release did not mention NPLs, their SOC showed parent company NPLs at 10.09%, down from 10.20% in 1Q21, and NPL coverage of 47.6% (flat QoQ). EW’s management reckons that “the worst is over”, though they continue to monitor credit risk.

More when the FS is released.

  • The implied 2Q21 recurring net income of $70mn is lower by 10%QoQ

    • 2Q21 attributable income from nat gas was lower by 12%QoQ
  • EDC’s attributable income in 1H21 was flat at $24mn, dragged by higher plant and steam field maintenance
    • 2Q21 attributable income fell 30%QoQ
  • Earnings from hydro grew 20% to $6mn – higher revenues due to commencement of contract with Meralco and merchant sales

TRANSPORT
Partial closure of China port to affect Philippines
  • The partial closure of the Ningbo-Zhoushan port  would “definitely impact” the Philippines’ supply chain
    • According to Philippine Ports Authority general manager Jay Santiago
    • However, once normal operations resume, there will likely be an increased volume of inbound cargoes from China
  • Our recent experience with the long shutdown of Shenzhen was that you will get a drop in cargo and then a sudden surge after opening
    • According to ICTSI Executive Vice President Christian Gonzales
    • Ningbo and Shenzen send a comparable amount of ships and cargo to the Philippines, we see a drop in China shipments the next few weeks and then a surge after
