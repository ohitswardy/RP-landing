Two index heavyweights announced FY20/4Q20 results on Wed: Ayala Land and BDO.

Beyond the 76% drop in 4Q profit, the most notable highlights of Ayala Land’s (ALI PM) were the spike in office vacancies (now at 12%), the sluggish improvement in mall revenue (+10% QoQ) and weak reservation sales (-44% YoY).  Full year earnings of P8.7bn (-74%) is below our and the Street’s estimates.  All the key highlights were those of disappointment. Provisions of P30bn (up fivefold) brought NPL coverage to 105% but we believe asset quality concerns still linger.  The bank will hold its earnings briefing later but in the meantime Rafa (rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph) offers his initial two cents’ worth below.

MACRO
Inflation watch: 10-yr BVAL jumps 65bps MoM; PHP down 1.2%MoM
  • The rate on the 10-year benchmark (BVAL) was 3.588% today, about 65bps higher than the end-Jan level of 2.937% (was 2.996% at end-2020). The move has been clearly driven by the inflation surprise in Jan (4.2%); and continued negative news flow is finally alerting many to even higher inflation expectations.
  • On the domestic front, pork and chicken prices are up sharply (anecdotally 50-100% YoY) driven by supply issues, exacerbated by the mishandling of the African Swine Fever (ASF) here. The government has tried to impose price controls, but suppliers and growers are responding by not selling their livestock. On the global side, we’ve already seen large up-moves in oil, and are now seeing prices of metals (nickel, copper) and soft commodities (soybeans, palm oil, etc) rise as well. Trade and logistics bottlenecks aren’t helping either.
  • As we warned earlier this month (here and here) inflation could become a serious problem in the Philippines.
    • First, it complicates the BSP’s monetary policy decision process.
    • Second, it poses a threat to the economic recovery, as it will likely dampen consumption.
    • Third, it could squeeze companies’ margins input costs (incl transport) rise amidst weak demand.
    • Fourth, higher commodity prices will widen the trade deficit, putting pressure on the PHP to depreciate.
    • Fifth, rising interest rates AND a weak PHP are hardly ever good for stock market valuations.
  • First, it hand-cuffs the BSP, and limits its ability to further loosen monetary policy. We sympathize, and agree that the inflation is cost-push, and no amount of monetary intervention will create more pigs or chickens, or have any impact on global oil and soybean prices. But as we saw in 2018, the BSP had to act, albeit belatedly. This difference is that the economy was much more robust in 2018 than it is today. Will the BSP hold policy rates down? Will they intervene in the bond market as they did in April and June 2020, and drive the 10-year back down? What will this mean for the PHP, which is already down 1.2% against the USD YTD?
  • Second, rising food inflation will weigh on consumption, which accounts for c70% of GDP. With persistent lockdowns, and weak employment data, higher food prices will hurt, particularly at the lower end of economic spectrum. Our 7% GDP growth target for the year is clearly at risk. The NEDA already warned of a negative GDP print in 1Q21 due to the continued Community Quarantine restrictions.
  • Third, we had already warned about input prices for food manufacturers and restaurants. However, rising oil/energy prices would have broader implications to transport costs for all goods/services. Rising costs amid weak demand won’t be pretty.
  • Fourth, the PHP was the surprise out-performer in 2020 with the 50% collapse in the trade deficit. While overall demand was weak, we reckon it was helped in large part by low import prices for commodities, particularly for oil (nearly all of which we import). The PHP has already depreciated 1.2% against the USD so far this year, all of it in the last two week alone. The PHP is the 2nd worst performing currency in the region YTD.
  • Finally, none of this is good for the market. The PSEi is already the worst-performing market in Asia, both in local currency (-5.4%) and in USD terms (-6.5%).

MACRO
BOP posts US$752mn deficit in January
  • The country’s balance of payments swung to a deficit of US$752mn in January
    • First deficit after 11 months of surplus but was 44.7% YoY lower than the US$1.355bn deficit in January 2020
    • Cited national government payments of foreign debt obligations
  • Reflected the 1.19% MoM decrease in gross international reserves (GIR) to US$108.67bn in end-January (equivalent to 11.6 months’ worth of imports)
  • The BSP sees the BOP surplus narrowing to US$3.3bn this year (equivalent to 0.8% of GDP)
    • In 2020, the BOP posted a surplus of US$16.02bn
BANKS
BDO NPAT P28.2bn, in line; NPLs jump to 2.65%, cover down to 105%
  • BDO reported headline FY2020 NPAT of P28.2bn, slightly ahead of Regis (P26.9bn) and consensus (P27.3bn). Details of the press release were scant, but it seems the bank booked some trading gains in 4Q20, which it used to bump up provisions to P6.4bn in 4Q, taking the full year to P30.2bn. NPLs jumped to 2.65% from 1.95% in 3Q20, and NPL cover fell to 105% from 138% in 3Q, despite the 4Q provision. Asset quality could continue to be an overhang into 2021E, considering the size of BDO’s restructured loan (RL) book (was 3% in 3Q, not yet disclosed for FY20).
  • The bank will release full FS on Friday 26 Feb, and will host an earnings call at 1pm on the same day

Some highlights from the press release

  • Loan growth was 3% YoY, down from 6% in 3Q20. Of the Big Three, only BDO has posted full-year growth in loans. No comments re NIMs or deposit growth, though they did say that CASA grew 17% YoY.
  • Non-interest income fell 8%, with “volume-related” business still down due to the pandemic. That said, the 4Q20 alone looked robust at P18.4bn, up 54% QoQ and 12% YoY. The bank probably booked some trading gains. Recall that BDO is unique among banks in that they are more geared to equity markets through their insurance subsidiary BDO Life. The PSEi Index was up around 25% QoQ in 4Q20.
  • Opex was down 2% YoY, likely helped by rental concessions in its mall-based branches. Advertising and marketing expenses were also significantly lower.
  • Provision expense was P30.2bn for FY20, with 4Q alone at P6.4bn. Despite this, NPL coverage fell to 105% from 138% in 3Q. This was due to a jump in NPLs to 2.65%, from 1.95% in 3Q20, which suggests new NPL formation was at least P16bn for the quarter alone. While the headline NPL number is not alarming – BPI was 2.68% and MBT was 2.41% -- we note that BDO had the highest level of RLs at 3% in 3Q20, and the bank was guiding for 5% RLs for FY. For perspective, BPI’s end-2020 RL was 1.1% while MBT’s was 0.4%.
