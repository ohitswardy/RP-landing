REGIS REPORT

24 September 2021

The Bangko Sentral (BSP), as expected, kept its policy rate of 2% unchanged at yesterday’s Monetary Board meeting. A move to return to more “normal” monetary policy is unlikely to be in the cards until at least late next year.

+++

The revised taxation for offshore gaming companies (“POGOs”) will now take effect after it was signed into law yesterday. The law reduced the tax on POGOs to 5% of gross gaming revenue, from 5% of gross bets which was unsustainably high. We reckon this law provides a new tax structure that is attractive enough, while formalizing government recognition of what’s been an “industry” operating on a gray area.  Note, however, that POGOs have decamped from the Philippines in the past year, largely due to pandemic restrictions.

+++

Vaccination rates reached a key milestone with 50% of the total population of Metro Manila now fully vaccinated. This should help reduce infection rates going forward, paving the way for easing of restrictions. Daily new infections have subsided from record levels in the past couple of weeks.

+++

The satisfaction ratings (gross) for President Duterte based on the Social Weather Stations poll was 75% in May-June. While this is down from the 84% last year, it is still a pretty high number. On a net basis, the satisfaction rating was 62%, which has been roughly the average over the course of his term.

The polling should give some idea where Duterte stands when it comes to the elections in May, where he says he intends to run for Vice President. His plan to run for VP, on the dubious rationale that it would protect him from suits likely to be filed against him, only makes sense if an ally or proxy succeeds him next year.

So far, none of these allies or proxies have formally declared intent to contest the Presidency. While his daughter Sara and close aide Christopher Go have supposedly turned down proposals to run, their disavowals are not being taken at face value. Losing vice presidential candidate Bongbong Marcos, who is still claiming that he was cheated (familiar?) despite a unanimous Supreme Court decision against him, might just serve Duterte’s purpose.

+++

We published a new note on Bloomberry (BLOOM PM), reiterating our Buy rating with a target price of P10.2/share. The stock has been beaten down following repeated lockdowns over the past months. As Manila reopens again this September, we think a path to recovery for casinos is now becoming clearer. With BLOOM not far from its 2020 lows, we think the stock presents a good reopening play ahead of the actual jump in operations. We estimate that GGR will get back to 61% of pre-pandemic levels by 2022 and 82% by 2023. Meanwhile, we expect EBITDA to get back to 71%/89% of its pre-covid peak during the same time.

Our P10.20/share TP is based on 11x EV/EBITDA of Solaire’s FY22 numbers plus the value of its unused landbank. At our TP, BLOOM would trade at a 12.9x falling to 10.3x EV/EBITDA, a slight premium to Macau. We argue that Macau’s premium may no longer be warranted over the long run, given more favorable risk-reward profile of operators that rely on domestic mass segments vs Chinese VIPs.

PROPERTY
President Duterte signs POGO tax law
  • President Duterte signed RA 11590 into law
  • This will require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue
  • PAGCOR may also impose a regulatory fee on POGOs, which shall not exceed 2% of GGR
  • POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000)
    • Minimum of P12,500 in annual taxes
  • The government expects the passage of this law to raise P145bn in tax revenue over five years
  • The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs
  • This new law will lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover
  • Thus, the new law could slow down POGO closures
  • We assume that Metro Manila office take-up from POGOs will be:
    • 2020 - -175,000sqm (net vacancy)
    • 2021E - -280,000sqm (net vacancy)
    • 2022E – zero
    • 2023E - +130,000sqm
  • POGOs occupied c. 11% of Metro Manila office stock in 2019 but now occupy only c. 7%
  • Office leasing comprises 58% of Filinvest Land’s NAV and 46% of Megaworld’s NAV
  • Manila Bay area land comprises c. 35% of SM Prime’s
  • FLI is trading at 5.4X FY22E P/E, 0.3X FY22E P/B and 62% discount to NAV
  • MEG is trading at 6.3X FY22E P/E, 0.5X FY22E P/B and 63% discount to NAV
  • SMPH is trading at 24.4X FY22E P/E, 2.6X FY22E P/B and 18% discount to NAV

Potential rebalancing of the PSE Index in 4Q21
  • First Gen (FGEN PM) could be removed from the PSE Index during the November 2021 review (or earlier) due to the ongoing tender offer
  • First Gen’s current public float is 18.74%
  • Valorous Asia, which currently owns 12.59% of FGEN, made a tender offer and seeks to increase its stake by another 3.0% to 5.7% of shares outstanding by early October
  • This could lower FGEN’s public float below 15%, the minimum requirement to be a PSE index constituent
  • We stress that the PSE has substantial discretion with respect to potential PSE Index rebalancing
  • The main candidates to replace FGEN are: DITO CME (DITO PM), Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM), and DMCI Holdings (DMC PM)
  • We believe the most likely scenarios are:
  • Scenario 1: FGEN’s public float remains above 15%
    • In this scenario, FGEN will remain in the PSE Index until the Feb 2022 rebalancing
  • Scenario 2: FGEN is removed in November, DITO PM is added
    • If FGEN will be removed from the PSEi in November, the PSE will use data as of September
    • In determining the potential entrants to the index, the PSE will use 12-month VWAP as a basis. However, the policy is silent on shares outstanding. Since, DITO issued new shares in September 2021 and raised its shares outstanding to 14.0bn (from 2.8bn), it can leapfrog the other potential candidates.  
  • Scenario 3: FGEN is removed in November, WLCON PM is added
    • If the PSE will not use DITO’s end-September shares outstanding, then WLCON will likely replace FGEN in the PSEi since it would have the highest market cap among non-PSEi constituents (based on 12-month VWAP)
  • Scenario 4: FGEN is removed in October, DMC PM is added
    • Once it is shown that FGEN’s public float is below 15%, the PSE has the discretion to remove FGEN ahead of the scheduled November review
    • The PSE would also have the discretion to use 12-month trading data as of August (instead of September)
    • If this were to happen, DMC would most likely replace FGEN since (at that time) it had the highest market cap among non-PSEi constituents (based on 12-month VWAP)
