REGIS REPORT

21 January 2021

BPI (BPI PM) announced it was merging with its wholly-owned thrift bank subsidiary BPI Family Bank in a move that would streamline costs and improve its “solo” capital ratios. This follows similar moves by other banks in recent years: Security Bank (SECB PM), Rizal Commercial (RCB PM) and PNB (PNB PM).

Such a set-up made financial sense in the past because it allowed a means of regulatory arbitrage. Regulations (e.g. reserve requirements) were more lenient for thrift banks.  The convergence of regulations and the narrowing of spreads meant the costs of maintaining a separate legal entity now outweigh the benefits.

+++

Globe (GLO PM) disclosed it was planning to spend P70bn for capex this year, which would be 40% jump from what was likely to have been spent in 2020.  That figure should be equivalent to over 35% of revenue.  PLDT (TEL PM) announced late last year that it was aiming to spend ~P90bn in 2021, which would be around 45% of its revenue.

The capex levels are elevated in part because of the threat of a new operator who is likely to launch commercially within the year. We should get a better idea over the final eighteen months of President Duterte’s term whether this long-term threat from the new entrant will crystallize.  A big factor, in our view, is the success or failure of efforts to lift foreign ownership limits on telecom firms, as this would allow China Telecom to take control of the new operator.

+++

First Philippine Holdings (FPH PM) is no longer planning to buy out all minorities in Lopez Holdings (LPZ PM) but is instead capping its tender offer so that it would not cause the public float to fall below the minimum threshold. If the float fell below the threshold of 10%, it would have triggered a delisting. The change was apparently due to complaints that the threat of delisting brought undue pressure on minorities to participate.

The tender offer price of P3.85 for each LPZ share is, we estimate, at ~40% discount to its underlying NAV, the biggest part of which is accounted for, ironically, by its holdings of FPH.

The cap in number of shares to be bought by FPH should lead to downward pressure on the LPZ stock price. From a bigger picture perspective, capping the offer and thus maintaining a separate listing for LPZ would negate one of the key benefits of the tender offer.  The hoped-for corporate streamlining with the removal of one listed holdco layer (and the accompanying NAV discount it creates) would no longer happen.

+++

Filinvest Land (FLI PM) announced it was planning to turn one of its subsidiaries into a REIT company. Given its asset portfolio, we expect the NAV enhancement from the move to inject these office assets into a REIT to be modest (~5%).

SM Prime (SMPH PM) says foot traffic at its malls reached 50-60% of pre-pandemic levels in Dec, an improvement from the 35-40% in Sept. We expect foot traffic to only improve gradually ahead of widespread Covid-19 vaccine availability.

BANKS
BPI to merge BPI Family into parent bank
  • In a disclosure, Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI PM) said that it plans to merge its 100% thrift bank subsidiary BPI Family Savings Bank (BFSB) into the parent bank. This follows similar moves in recent years by banks such as Security Bank (SECB PM), Rizal Commercial (RCBC PM), and Philippine National Bank (PNB PM).
  • Historically, the main advantage of a thrift bank license was regulatory arbitrage, particularly for deposit reserve requirement ratios (RRR). In the past, commercial banks’ RRR was as high as 20%, while those for thrift banks was 8%. Today, the RRR is 12% for commercial banks and 3% for thrift banks. While the gap is still large, the low interest rate environment has severely reduced the incentive. With short-term Treasury rates at c1-1.5%, the arbitrage would be about P300mn, a little over 1% of BPI’s 2019 NPAT.
  • In contrast, the merger could result in lower opex (marketing, head office), capex (IT, branches), and reduced bureaucracy (regulatory reporting, etc). The net result will likely be neutral to mildly positive for profit forecasts.
  • The merger will also release capital for BPI, as Basel III imposes capital penalties for banks with a holding company structure. Over the years, the gap between BPI’s Solo (Parent) and Consolidated CET-1 ratios ranged from as little as 44bps to as much as 150bps. That said, BPI’s Solo CET-1 was already high at 16.40% at 9M20.
  • The merger will also allow BFSB customers full access to BPI’s products and services, which today is limited by having separate legal entities (see bureaucracy, above).
  • BPI aims to complete the merger within 2021.

TELCOS
Globe's capex guidance at P70bn for 2021
  • Globe Telecom expects to spend P70bn for capex this year
  • This is a marked increase from the P50bn it is likely to have spent in 2020
  • That figure for 2020 was reduced from P63bn due to the pandemic

  • To compare, PLDT has indicated it plans to spend P88-92bn. Which would be ~45% of revenue
PROPERTY
SM mall foot traffic reaches 50-60% of pre-COVID levels
  • SM Prime said that mall foot traffic in December reached 50%-60% of pre-COVID levels
  • From c. 35%-40% in September
  • SM Prime has started to reduce the level of rental concessions for some tenants
    • Last year, it waived minimum rents for percentage-of-sales based tenants and granted rental discounts of 30%-50% for fixed rate tenants
  • We forecast SM Prime’s same-mall rent to return to 80% of 2019 levels this year
  • The company also plans to complete 5-7 Philippine malls annually until 2024
    • It only completed 2 malls annually in 2019 and 2020
  • SMPH is currently trading at 32.6X FY21E P/E and a 3% premium to NAV
PROPERTY
Filinvest Land to establish REIT
  • Filinvest Land will convert its wholly owned subsidiary, Cyberzone Properties Inc (CPI), into a REIT
  • CPI owns and operates office buildings in Alabang and Cebu
    • PEZA accredited
  • CPI comprises c. 300,000sqm of FLI’s 525,000sqm office portfolio
  • Less than 5% of CPI’s portfolio is occupied by POGOs
  • Its 9M20 office revenue grew 16% to P2.46bn
    • 57% of FLI’s office revenue
  • We estimate that if Filinvest Land’s entire office portfolio were to be spun off into a REIT, FLI’s NAV could rise by 11%
    • The NAV enhancement of spinning off only CPI assets into a REIT would likely be c. 5%
  • FLI is currently trading at 5.0X FY21E P/E and 67% discount to NAV
TRANSPORT
NLEX Connector Road project on track for completion in 2022
  • NLEX Corp. said that the first 5-km section of the 8-km Connector Road project will be completed by end-2021
    • Full completion by 2022
  • Right-of-way delivery for the whole alignment stands at 81% while construction progress is at 16%
    • According to Dept. of Public Works and Highways Secretary Mark Villar
  • Once complete, the P18bn project will connect C3 up to Skyway 3 (Sta Mesa exit)
  • Our valuation includes the Connector Road project (negative equity value)
  • NLEX Corp. is a 76%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM)
  • MPI is trading at a 26% discount to market NAV of P5.80/sh

 

CONGLOS
FPH amends tender offer for Lopez Holdings shares
  • First Philippine Holdings Corp has modified its tender offer to buy common shares of Lopez Holdings Corp by lowering the number of stocks it plans to acquire, according to a PSE disclosure
  • Its revised tender offer will cover up to a max of 1.57bn common shares, equivalent to 34.5% of the total issued and outstanding common shares
  • The previous plan was to acquire a minimum of 908.46mn (20%) up to a maximum of 2.07bn shares (45.56%) at a price of P3.85/share (a 25% premium to the stock price when the restructuring was announced, 27% discount to NAV)
  • Tender offer period runs from January 22 to February 19
  • The reduction will remove the risk of Lopez Holdings falling below the required minimum public ownership and dispense with the need to pursue its petition for a voluntary delisting
  • FPH President and COO Giles Puno said the company wanted to remove the pressure on LPZ shareholders to tender shares from its plan to delist
