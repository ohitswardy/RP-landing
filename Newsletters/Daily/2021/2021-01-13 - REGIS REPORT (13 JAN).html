REGIS REPORT

13 January 2021

The government ended the FY20 with a budget deficit of P1.36trn, which would be ~7.5% of GDP and just around double the prior year’s deficit. The rise in the deficit is attributable, arguably, more to the fall in revenue due to the pandemic rather than a rise in expenditures.

+++

The Dept of Energy (DOE) provided a little more clarity on the “moratorium” on coal plants it announced a few months ago. As we expected, the moratorium will apply only to new facilities. Existing coal projects will not be affected and neither will those that have already received permits or achieved substantial progress.

Excluding the imminent completions (e.g. GN Power Dinginin), we believe there are at least 3,000MW of potential coal capacity that may still qualify to proceed despite the ban.  Notwithstanding this, we have to point out all these are in very early stages, and shovels have not hit the ground for any of these projects. The power supply situation is thus likely to become progressively tighter after this year as new baseload capacity is unlikely to be available in Luzon before 2025.

Check out the chart below.  Keep in mind that the Luzon grid was already experiencing intermittent outages during the peak dry season of 2019, despite the apparently adequate reserve margins. One doesn’t have to try too hard to imagine what the situation could be by 2023/24.

Beyond the medium-term supply tightness in Luzon, the longer-term implications are clear. Incremental demand for power will have to be satisfied by non-coal sources. The initial option will likely be LNG, and alternative, renewable sources (solar and wind) should follow suit.

+++

The central bank of Bangladesh (Bangladesh Bank) filed a lawsuit against Rizal Commercial Bank (RCB PM) and Bloomberry (BLOOM), among others, with the New York State Supreme Court in connection with the incident where hackers made off with US$81m of its funds. This comes after a previous suit was thrown out. Bangladesh Bank has been unable to find the actual perpetrators (suspected to be North Korean) and is opting to go after the entities through which the money allegedly passed.

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) amended its corporate articles to allow it guarantee the debt of its subsidiaries, in line with the other major conglomerates.

MACRO
Budget deficit reaches P1.36tn in FY20
  • Based on preliminary data, the budget deficit reached P1.36tn in FY20
    • Equivalent to 7.5% of GDP
    • Higher than FY19 deficit of P660bn (3.4% of GDP)
    • According to Dept. of Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez
    • Implies Dec 2020 budget deficit stood at P294bn (+17% YoY)
  • FY20 Revenues P2.842tn, down 10% YoY and short of the P2.853tn target
    • Implied Dec 2020 revenues dropped 8% YoY, 9% MoM to P225bn
  • Expenditures reached P4.205tn in FY20, up 11% YoY but a tad short of target (P4.233tn)
    • Implies Dec 2020 expenditures rose 5% YoY and 39% MoM to P519bn
  • To plug the deficit, the government borrowed P2.6tn from local and foreign lenders, excluding P540bn borrowed from the BSP
  • For 2021, the government is targeting a wider budget deficit of P1.78tn (8.9% of GDP)
    • Debt-to-GDP ratio expected to settle at 57% this year (vs. 51% in end-Nov 20)

POWER
DOE sets scope of ban on new coal plants
  • The Department of Energy (DoE) excluded existing and operational coal-fired power generation facilities from its moratorium
    • Last November, the DoE declared a moratorium on new coal-fired power plants
  • Also not covered are: Committed coal power projects and existing power plant complexes that already have firm expansion plans and existing land site provisions
  • It also exempts projects with substantial accomplishments, particularly those that have signed lease agreement for the projects, and approved permits or resolutions from local government units and the Regional Development Council where the facilities would be locate
