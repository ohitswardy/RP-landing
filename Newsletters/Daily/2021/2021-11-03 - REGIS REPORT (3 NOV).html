03 November 2021

Semirara’s (SCC PM) 3Q results appeared to be trending better than our estimates but the numbers are likely to be disappointing for the broader market, given where coal prices were during the quarter. Production was quite low in the latest period and it didn’t help that there were outages at its power plant while spot electricity prices were going up. For those less concerned about being environmentally and politically correct, the stock still does look attractive with 9% yield.

A rights issue presumes that existing shareholders will be participating pro rata. Having said that, however, a subsequent FOO is likely not off the table.

POWER & UTILITIES
Semirara 3Q21 profit down QoQ
  • 9M21 core profit at P10.1bn, ahead of estimates. Stripping out FX adjustments, Semirara’s 3Q21 core profit dropped 6% QoQ to P3.8bn despite higher coal prices. Coal volumes dropped and the company also need to purchase more power from the spot market. Cumulative core profit for 9M stood at P10.1bn, tracking ahead of Regis and consensus forecasts.
  • Coal mining EBITDA drops QoQ. Profits from coal mining fell 7% QoQ, as the 18% QoQ improvement in coal ASPs was nullified by a 20% contraction in sales volumes to 3.9mn MT, after heavy rains resulted in a 50% QoQ drop in production. Coal sales volumes in 9M21 reached 12.7mn MT, trending better than Regis forecast of 14.5mn MT. Given that management is guiding coal sales volumes of 15-16mn MT this year, 4Q sales volumes could drop further sequentially to as much as 3.3mn MT.

Moreover, the improvement in SCC’s coal ASPs was more subdued than seaborne thermal coal prices, as >40% of its coal sales during the quarter were lower-priced un-washed coal. Management indicated that 4Q ASPs so far are trending more in line with movements in seaborne coal prices and are reflecting a higher proportion of better quality coal.

  • Power unit affected by outages in 3Q21. The power division’s profit contribution collapsed 68% QoQ, 45% YoY in 3Q21, following the extended plant outage of Units 2 and 3, which consequently led to higher spot purchases. The performance of this division was also further exacerbated by the absence of fuel pass-through provisions on some of its power contracts

Currently, about 590MW of its capacity is operational, as one plant is on maintenance outage while another is de-rated. Following contract expiries, the company’s contracted capacity is now down to 390MW and will further drop to 200MW by year-end.

  • 4Q21 earnings outlook; Dividend prospects. We reckon the coal mining unit’s earnings will improve further in 4Q, despite lower coal sales volumes. Despite yesterday’s hefty correction which brought coal futures down to US$145/MT, thermal coal forward contracts still suggest coal prices will average US$167/MT in 4Q21 and gradually drop to US$105/MT in 4Q22, after peaking at US$270/MT in early October. Jefferies’ coal team also thinks coal prices will rise again as the winter season intensifies.

Even on our base case forecast of US$100/MT coal and recent share price run-up, SCC’s div yield and free cash flow yield are still at an attractive 9% and 12%, respectively.

INFRASTRUCTURE
Notes from MWIDE call on Anti-Dummy Law violation

Summary: We attended a meeting with Megawide’s legal team to discuss the anti-dummy violation complaint against the directors and officers of GMR-Megawide Cebu Airport Corp. (GMCAC). A formal case has not been filed and MWIDE believes the complainants do not have a legal basis. With only a few months left until the country transitions to a new government and the possible passage of the Public Service Act bill, we believe this issue will be short lived.

  • No case filed at courts for now. So far, the Department of Justice (DOJ) has not filed a case against GMCAC at the regional trial court of Lapu-Lapu. The DOJ issued a resolution last October 18, recommending the filing of the case and Megawide responded by filing a petition with the Office of the Secretary of Justice, challenging the DOJ’s resolution and to point out the complaint’s lack of probable cause. 
  • Indicted personnel are consultants. The allegation largely focuses on the appointment of foreign nationals in the management of GMCAC, which is considered a public utility. Megawide insists that the indicted personnel are merely consultants or advisors and are not regular employees. The burden of proof lies with the accuser (i.e. National Bureau of Investigation and DOJ) and the company believes it will be difficult to prove that the indicted foreign officers have been involved in the management of the airport. Formal documents (visa, DOLE, PSE, etc) show that these foreign officers are only “advisors” or consultants.
  • Worst case scenario: arbitration. In the event that the court could decide against GMCAC on this alleged violation and order a forfeiture of its franchise, management believes it could raise the matter to international arbitration, given that this particular issue was not included as one of the grounds for contract termination. 
  • Passage of Public Service Act amendments will make any case “moot and academic”. The proposed amendments to the Public Service Act includes the removal of foreign ownership limits of airports. The passage of the bill, therefore, will automatically make the case “moot and academic”. The bill has passed the Lower House but remains pending at the Senate.

Duterte to amend BOT Law’s rules
  • President Duterte plans to amend the implementing rules of the Build-Operate-Transfer Law to avoid “unwarranted guarantees” in future projects
    • He has appointed Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua to head the IRR committee tasked to tackle the amendments
  • NEDA indicated that PPPs with unwarranted guarantees, contingent liabilities and “onerous” contract provisions take up the government’s limited fiscal space
  • The committee held its first meeting on Oct. 26 and will start stakeholder consultations in December
    • Committee plans to publish the amended IRR by 1Q22

MONDE and EMP could be added to the PSEi in Feb 2022
  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) and Emperador (EMP PM) could be added to the PSE Index during the mid-Feb 2022 rebalancing
    • MONDE would have to meet liquidity requirements in either November or December
    • EMP would have to meet liquidity requirements in both November and December
  • If both stocks will be added to the PSEi in February, then the two PSEi constituents mostly likely to be removed are Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) and Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM)
  • In order for a stock to be added to the PSE Index, it must meet certain criteria:
    • Free float must be at least 15% (20% beginning December 2022)
    • Its median value traded must be among the top 25% for 9 of the 12 months in review (minimum of 6 months for new IPOs)
    • Its 12-month average market cap must be among the highest
  • Stocks that meet the first criteria can enter the PSEi if they are within the top 25 in terms of 12-month average market cap
  • MONDE has 31% public float while EMP has 15% public float
  • MONDE and EMP are currently in the top 20 in terms of 12-month average market cap
