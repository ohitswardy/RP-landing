When is a lockdown not a lockdown? On the IATF’s recommendation, the government placed Metro Manila and four neighboring provinces in a “bubble” for two weeks (22 March-4 April), limiting travel into and out of the areas, but allowing unimpeded travel with the bubble. Within that bubble, the rules have been tightened again. Most notably, restaurants are not allowed to serve dine-in customers, unless they have adequate outdoor dining facilities. Take-away and delivery services are ok.

The most obviously negatively affected would be the likes of Jollibee Foods (JFC PM), Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) and Max’s (MAXS PM). Less obvious would be the malls, where foot traffic was slowly rising, in part for their restaurant tenants. Cebu Air (CEB PM) would also be hurt, as 80% of its domestic passengers pass through the Metro Manila airport. To the upside, less eating out could again (slightly) benefit grocery retailers like Puregold (PGOLD PM) and Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM).

A quick roundup of earnings reports from Friday:

  • First Gen’s (FGEN PM) 4Q profit improved 9%QoQ to $58mn, helped by better contribution from EDC. A feed-in-tariff adjustment for the 2016-2020 periods helped lift EDC’s earnings, partially offsetting the impact of San Gabriel’s outage in the quarter. Management expects earnings to be flattish in the near-term. To the upside, increased investor interest in renewables could see valuations re-rate.
  • LT Group’s (LTG PM) 4Q income was dragged by hefty P7.9bn provision expense at PNB (see comments below). Meanwhile, profits from tobacco improved 19% QoQ, with the margin uplift from the Oct price hikes more than offsetting the volume decline. Although LTG’s dividends are likely to remain elevated, the bank’s struggles will almost certainly weigh on group earnings in succeeding quarters/years.
  • Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) – the highlight was less the loss they booked in 4Q. Instead, it was the asset quality data – as of end-2020, the bank’s gross NPL ratio was 11.1% (vs industry of 3.70%), NPL cover was 47% (vs industry 94%), and restructured loans of 7.5% (vs industry 1.91%).

MACRO
Metro Manila and 4 adjacent provinces on tighter restrictions
  • Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, and Rizal will be on tighter mobility restrictions from March 22 to April 4
  • These areas will be under a single “bubble” – entering the bubble and traveling outside is restricted to essential/authorized individuals as well as returning residents
  • Operational capacity of 30%-50% for all sectors
  • Restaurant operations shall be limited to delivery, take-out, and outdoor dining
  • Curfew from 10PM to 5AM except for essential workers, cargo vehicles, and public transportation
  • People under 18 and over 65, pregnant women, people with comorbidities, and people living with these at-risk individuals are banned from leaving their homes unless they are procuring essential items

LTG 4Q profit down 41%YoY, 19%QoQ due to PNB - behind
  • LTG reported a net income of P21.0bn (-9%), coming in short due to chunky provisions at PNB, despite tobacco (+9%) coming in bang-in-line with estimates.  
    • In 4Q alone, LTG's net income of P4.92bn (-41%YoY, -19%YoY) reversed the trend after it posted a recovery from the 2Q20 lows.
  • PMFTC's contribution continued its uptrend in 4Q20. While cigarette volumes fell to 9.5bn sticks In 4Q (-19%QoQ, -24%YoY) and 41.7bn sticks In FY20 (-16%YoY), the uplift in margins compensated for the shortfall.
    • The higher-margin Marlboro brand ended the year at a 62% volume contribution, compared to a 56% share in 2019.
  • PNB incurred losses In 4Q after booking a provision expense of P7.9bn (vs just P618mn in 3Q20). NPL cover, however, was just 47% as NPL ratio shot up to 11.11%. Majority of the NPLs arose from real estate, transportation and wholesale/retail trade sectors.   
  • Profts of Tanduay contracted due to lower GP margin (<15%, higher alcohol costs) and a P268mn provision arising from an excise tax case. We estimate volumes of Tanduay hit 7.5mn cases In 4Q20 (+4.4%QoQ, +27%YoY), continuing to outperform beer due to a shift to off-premise brands.
  • Meanwhile, ABI posted a P579mn contribution in 4Q20, contrasting previous losses stemming from its Heineken JV. The restructuring of its partnership with Heineken (to one where ABI is a manufacturer-distributor while Heineken handles marketing and brand-building) could reverse the annual  losses (P700mn/year) from this venture.

BANKS
PNB – NPLs surge to 11.1% coverage down sub-50%

Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) NPAT for 2020 came in at P2.6bn, down 73% YoY. For 4Q alone, the bank booked a P1.25bn loss as provision expense surged to P7.9bn for the quarter. Despite the high provisions, PNB’s NPL cover stood at just 47% as its NPL ratio shot up to 11.11% of total loans (from 6.25% in 3Q20 and 3.05% at end-2019). Needless to say these numbers are pretty horrible, compared to the industry average of 3.70% NPL and 93.7% cover at end-2020. By category, PNB’s corporate NPLs were at 8.8% while consumer NPLs ranged from 19-25%.

Other operating highlights:

  • Loans contracted by 8.8% YoY on a net basis (-5.4% on a gross basis). Corporate loans fell 4%, while other accounts saw huge contractions: retail SME (-41%), mortgages (-25%), credit card (-21%) and auto (-14%).
  • Deposits grew 7.8% YoY. What’s surprising, though, was the massive 21% QoQ jump in deposits, particularly in TDs, which nearly doubled QoQ (P237bn vs P121bn in 3Q).
  • NIMs jumped 15bps QoQ in 4Q, driven by a surprising 48bp QoQ increase in loan yields, and a continued decline in deposit costs.
  • Fees were still down 16% YoY in FY20, though were showing steady recovery QoQ. PNB recorded a small trading loss in 4Q20. Opex was up 7% YoY in the full-year, led by compensation (+6.2%).
  • Provision expense surged, up almost 6x from 2019 levels to P16.9bn, equivalent to a credit cost of 266bps. This was due to the bank’s NPL ratio exploding to 11.1% from 6.2% in 3Q and 3.1% at end-2019. Despite the jump in provision expense, PNB’s NPL coverage plunged to 47%, from 89% in 2019 (no quarterly NPL coverage data given).

It is unclear at this stage if the NPL figures already take into account the bank’s exposure to Philippine Airlines (PAL). The bank also does not disclose restructured loan data.
