REGIS REPORT

06 October 2021

Inflation decelerated to 4.8% for the month of Sept, as softening food prices helped offset rising energy prices. Over the near-term, skyrocketing energy prices are likely to keep CPI inflation elevated, though this should be tempered by food inflation which appear to have peaked, particularly for meat. We don’t expect this inflation print to prod the Bangko Sentral (BSP) to unwind its ultra-loose monetary policy until at least latter part of next year.

+++

Buyout firm KKR announced it was accepting all the shares tendered in the recently concluded tender offer for First Gen (FGEN PM) shares, higher than its previously announced maximum. This means KKR will be buying 7.3% of FGEN’s shares, raising its stake to 19.9%.

One of the unintended consequences of KKR’s purchase is that the public float of FGEN will fall to 11.4%, which will make FGEN ineligible to remain a component of the PSEi (i.e. “PCOMP”). The interesting question some investors will have is which company will replace FGEN when it is deleted from the index?

Frankly, there is room for subjectivity and interpretation of the PSE’s own rules so there is much uncertainty on that topic. In fact, even a change in the rules themselves cannot be discounted, as was seen in the case of Converge’s (CNVRG PM) inclusion even before it was listed for twelve months.  Nevertheless, for those who are interested to hear our predictions (premised on an interpretation of current rules), we think the most likely candidates are DMCI (DMC PM), Wilcon (WLCON PM) and Dito (DITO PM). There’s even a chance that Monde (MONDE PM) might get in if the re-balancing takes place in Dec.

+++

The Singapore High Court denied Bloomberry’s (BLOOM PM) appeal against the adverse arbitration ruling on its dispute with GGAM. It doesn’t look like Bloomberry intends to abide by it, however. It plans to continue contesting the enforcement of that ruling in local courts.

Jollibee (JFC PM) announced it was conducting a tender offer to buy back up to US$250m of perps it issued last year, using funds it raised from the more recent issuance of PHP prefs. This exercise is being done to reduce its US$ exposure.

With coal prices where they are, we examine how exposed (or not) many of the gencos are to the surge. We threw in comments on their exposure to power spot market as well for good measure, since rising fuel prices will inevitably affect power prices too.

MACRO
September inflation slows to 4.8%
  • Inflation slowed slightly to 4.8% in September from 4.9% in August
    • Due to decelerating inflation of food (particularly meat, fish) and transport
    • Consensus estimate at 5.1%; Came in at the low-end of the BSP’s target range of 4.8%-5.6% for the month
    • September inflation is higher than the 10-year treasury yield of 4.69%
  • YT-September inflation averaging at 4.5%, still above the BSP’s 2-4% target this year

Bloomberry appeal against arbitration award denied again
  • Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) disclosed that the Singapore Court of Appeal denied its appeal against the decision of the Singapore High Court dated May 29, 2020
    • The Singapore High Court denied their petition to set aside the Final Award of Arbitration Tribunal in favor of GGAM in May 29, 2020
  • The Final Award of the Arbitration Tribunal ordered Bloomberry to pay GGAM c$100mn in damages due to the termination of the management services agreement
    • It also ordered Bloomberry to buy GGAM’s 921mn shares in BLOOM for P10.17bn @ P11/sh or allow the sale of the shares and make up the difference between the market value and P10.17bn
  • This will allow GGAM to file an action to enforce the Arbitral Award in the Philippines where the assets and business of BRHI and SPI are located
  • Management intends to oppose enforcement on the grounds of fraud during arbitration, violation of Philippine public policy (GGAM’s assignment of the management agreement to GGAM Netherlands, a shell company, constitutes tax evasion and fraud), etc.

POWER
Coal and WESM exposure of gencos
  • Following the Jefferies commodities report on global coal shortage and record high coal prices, we assess the potential impact on gencos from 1) the use of coal as a fuel source and 2) rising wholesale electricity spot market (WESM) prices.
  • Coal prices continue to surge, with regional coal prices up >40% from June and 237% from a year ago.
  • Generally speaking, high coal prices will mean squeezed margins for coal plants whose contract have no fuel pass-through provisions.
  • Higher spot prices will benefit gencos that sell to the WESM.
  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) is the most exposed to coal at face value, with nearly 70% of capacity coming from coal plants.
    • Supply contracts to DUs have fuel pass-through provisions.
    • A significant portion of their contracts particularly to RES have no pass-through provisions, but are hedged through financial instruments.
    • However, we expect higher coal prices to have minimal impact on AP.
    • It sells 10% of output as a whole to the WESM, which should benefit from rising spot prices. The completion of the 2nd unit of GNP Dinginin (668MW) would bump up exposure to the spot market a bit more by 2022.
  • First Gen’s (FGEN PM) contracts for it gas plants largely pass through fuel costs.
    • Its unit EDC’s costs are from the steam that they themselves produce, while wind and solar don’t rely on a fuel source. 
    • It sells <10% of output to the WESM and would be a small beneficiary from rising spot prices.
  • AC Energy (ACEN PM) – a largely renewable player with wind and solar plants relying on FIT rate, is exposed through its coal plant SLTEC (244MW or about 20% of operating capacity).
    • It supplies the 310MW fixed price PHP contract with Meralco which has no fuel pass-through provision.
    • ACEN’s supply contracts slightly exceed generation output, so higher WESM prices is bad for them.
      • Incoming capacity from two solar plants in 4Q21 would square their short spot exposure.
  • Semirara (SCC PM) – SCC is a net beneficiary of rising coal prices, with c. 80% of profit derived from its coal mining unit.  It largely prices its coal according to movements in Newcastle thermal coal prices. 
    • The power unit, which accounts for c. 20% of profit, has fuel pass-through provisions for most of its power supply contracts.
    • It is also selling c. 130MW of its operating capacity of 690MW (1 plant is currently on maintenance outage) to the WESM.
