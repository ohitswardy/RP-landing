REGIS REPORT

03 March 2021

We have highlights from the results briefings of home improvement retailer Wilcon (WLCON PM) and property firm Robinsons Land (RLC PM). We also offer our initial take on the 4Q numbers from Security Bank (SECB PM) and D&L Industries (DNL PM) ahead of a more detailed look after their respective earnings briefings. Check them out:

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (+) - SECB’s P7.4bn NPAT for FY20 beat our forecast, but lagged the Street’s. The bank’s 4Q profit appeared soft at just P768mn, as trading/fx gains faded sharply while provisions stayed high. To the upside, NIMs were firm QoQ on lower deposit costs, and fee incomes were recovering nicely. Provision expense was P26.4bn for the FY, with 4Q alone at P5.3bn. Headline NPL ratio improved to 3.90% from 4.03% in 3Q, but this was due to aggressive write-offs (we estimate c 1.5-2% loan write-offs for the year). The write-offs helped drag consumer loans down 10% YoY, taking consumers down to 25% of the book from 29% at end-2019. The bank will host an earnings call at 2:30pm tomorrow, 4 March.
  • D&L Industries (DNL PM) (-) - DNL’s FY20 profit came in at P2.012bn, down 23%YoY, below expectations. Despite a recovery in overall profits in 4Q (+8%YoY, +11%QoQ), the contribution from the food segment (-45%) remained well below pre-Covid levels.
  • Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM) (=) – 4Q20 sales growth of +6% YoY was largely driven by the opening of 6 new Depots throughout the year, bringing total store count to 63. SSSG was flattish – while Metro Manila stores improved vs 3Q, provincial stores were slower because of base effects. Management expects 2021 SSSG to be at least double digit (Regis FY21E +10%) due to the temporary store closures (during lockdown) last year. A more meaningful recovery is expected in 2H21 as construction activity also recovers.

  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (-) – 4Q20 net profit fell 63% YoY, bringing the FY20 figure down 39% to P5.3bn, behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts. If not for the delay in recognition of China residential revenue, earnings would have been ahead of our forecast. 4Q20 mall revenue and Philippine presales both fell 67% YoY and both segments are likely to remain weak. Meanwhile, the company hopes to raise P20bn-P25bn from a REIT listing in 4Q21. If this is successful, we estimate that our RLC NAV estimate could rise by 5%-20%.

MACRO
Mobility data update as of February 26
  • After the big drop in early Feb, looks like data has rebounded a bit, but still flat on January, and still round the levels of mid- to late-3Q. Data for NCR is slightly lower than the Jan levels. This is not surprising given that the quarantine restrictions did not materially change in Feb. Most crucially – in my view – there has been no change in policy for public transport.
  • March data might be a bit better, with loosening of restrictions on cinemas, meeting halls, etc. Also, the release of unified rules for inter-province travel might help. While the LGUs (governors, mayors, etc) have final say, having a national policy might be easier for them relax (and blame national government if things go bad again).
  • All this continues to point to a weak 1Q21 GDP / earnings print. Inflation could add another layer of softness.

BANKS
Bank lending declines 2.4% in January; Domestic liquidity rises 9%
  • Loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, decreased by 2.4% YoY in January
    • Slower than the 0.7% drop in December
  • Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-7.4%), trade (-6.9%) dropped further
  • Consumer loans (credit cards and auto) collapsed by 6.9% YoY (vs. +4.1% in December)
    • Credit cards fell 10% while auto loans dropped 5.8%
  • Meanwhile, money supply grew by 9% in January, slower than the 9.5% growth (revised) in December

BANKS
SECB FY20 P7.4bn, weak trading in 4Q; NPL improves QoQ on write-offs

SECB reported FY20 NPAT of P7.4bn, down 26% YoY. The result beat our forecasts (P6.5bn), but were behind consensus of P8.1bn. In 4Q alone, the bank’s NPAT was just P768mn (-68% YoY), as trading/fx gains dropped sharply to just P379mn, from P3.5-5bn a quarter in the first 3 quarters. Meanwhile, provisions stayed high at P5.3bn in 4Q20, taking the full-year expense up to P26.4bn, fully 6% of loans. Headline NPL ratio improved to 3.90% at end-202 from 4.03% in 3Q. This was due to aggressive write-offs of NPLs, likely mostly from the credit card portfolio. NPL cover dipped slightly to 115% from 122% in 3Q. We estimate that SECB’s restructured loans was 1.2% of total loans. The bank will hold an earnings call on Thursday 4 March at 2:30pm.

Some highlights:

  • Loans (net) dipped 2% YoY, though gross loans were still up 1% YoY. Consumer loans dropped 10% YoY, due in part to the write-offs, and now account for 25% of total loans, from a peak of 29% in 2019.
  • NIMs held up surprisingly well, still rising 19bps QoQ to 4.72%. As with other banks, this was driven by a sharp drop in deposit costs, which plunged to 0.63% in 4Q, as the bank paid down their expensive TDs.
  • Fee incomes were strong QoQ, and are nearing pre-Covid levels
  • Trading/fx income crashed in 4Q to just P379mn.
  • Provisions stayed high at P5.3bn, taking FY provision expense to 26.4bn, fully 6% of total loans.
  • Reported NPLs improved to 3.90% from the 4.03% in 3Q20. This was due to aggressive write-offs, likely from the credit card portfolio. We estimate that total write-offs in 2020 were equivalent to nearly 2% of total loans. NPL cover eased slightly to 115% from 122% in 3Q20. We estimate RLs were at 1.16% at end-2020, putting “stressed” loans at around 5.1%

Overall, we reckon the core business performed reasonably well, and are encouraged by the NII / fee income recovery. We will be watching the NPL issues of SECB’s consumer book very closely, particularly post-Bayanihan 2.
