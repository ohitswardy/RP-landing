REGIS REPORT

11 May 2021

1Q21 GDP results are out later this morning, with consensus still going for another contraction, in the 3.5% range. Given the slew of disappointing results (more below) and shrinking loans (-4.5% YoY in March), we would not be surprised if the figure comes out below the Street’s expectations.

+ + +

Asset quality deteriorated again in March, with system-wide NPLs at 4.33%, up 17bps MoM and up 43bps from end-2020. Nearly every metric worsened with Restructured Loans (RL) and Past Due (PD) ratios rising MoM and YoY. NPL cover also fell to 83% from 87% in February and 94% at end-2020.

The industry data runs counter to the results of BDO, BPI and MBT, all of whom showed flat NPLs QoQ. All of which begs the question – who’s holding the proverbial bag?

+ + +

A flurry of results today, all of them behind our expectations (AGI’s was a VERY delayed 2020 result, which doesn’t count)

  • Alliance Global (AGI PM) – in line - AGI’s much delayed 2020 press release yielded a net profit of P8.8bn (-50%YoY), slightly better than estimates, on the back of the outperformance of the liquor segment. The shortfall in property was on account of a very weak office segment. Meanwhile, looser mobility restrictions in 4Q20 prompted a decent recovery in Travellers (net loss of P100mn) and McDonald’s (back in the black).

For foreign investors >> Alliance Global – 4Q20: Casino & food improve on looser restrictions

For local investors >> Alliance Global – 4Q20: Casino & food improve on looser restrictions

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) – behind – Cebu Air reported a headline net loss of P7.3bn and EBITDA loss of P3.0bn in 1Q21, as demand continues to be weak due to travel restrictions. Cash costs were also higher-than-expected but we believe these still included one-offs that should gradually come down in succeeding periods. The company will hold a briefing on Friday, May 14 at 3pm HKT.
  • Manila Water (MWC PM) – behind – 1Q21 core profit dropped 32% to P1.33bn, as demand weakness lingered across its PH operations. The results are tracking behind forecast. The company’s regional operations did very well, on the back of a rebound in demand and tariff hikes.

  • Vista Land (VLL PM) – behind – 1Q21 net profit fell 14% YoY to P2.0bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Residential revenue fell 13% as construction activity still appears hampered. On a more positive note, reservation sales grew 4% YoY (14% QoQ) to P16.1bn, already 82% of the 2019 average.

BANKS
Bank lending contracts by 4.5% in March; M3 growth moderates to 8.3%
  • Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, further contracted to 4.5% in March from -2.7% in February
    • Consumer loans fell 10% YoY and 1.8% MoM
    • Loans to retail and manufacturing sectors still soft
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose 8.3% to P14.2tn in March, moderating from the 9.4% expansion in February

AIRLINE
Cebu Air posts 1Q21 net loss of P7.3bn; More on convertible bond issuance
  • 1Q21 EBITDA loss at P3.0bn (Behind estimates). Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported a net loss of P7.3bn in 1Q21. The EBITDA loss during the quarter was slightly down QoQ at P3.0bn, trailing our full-year forecast.
  • Pax volumes up 78% QoQ but fares drop 45%.  Pax volumes improved by 78% QoQ to 550k in 1Q21. This improvement, however, was at the expense of a 45% QoQ drop in average fares.  Pax volumes in 1Q were about 10% of pre-pandemic levels, which we largely expected. However, the recent lockdown could also mean the recovery we’re expecting in 2Q could be pushed back to 2H21. Note that the government is targeting to inoculate half of the population by year-end, with vaccines arriving mostly in 3Q.  Admittedly, administration of vaccines has been slow so far.
  • Cash costs larger than expected.  Meanwhile, cash costs were higher than expected, due to higher maintenance expenses (+1% YoY) and admin costs (-10% YoY).  We suspect there were still one-off expenses booked in 1Q, such as separation payments for example.  We hope to get more detail on the results, as well as updates on operations and cost reduction initiatives at the briefing on May 14, 3pm HKT. Registration details (link)
  • CBs broadly mirror CPS, pays 4.5% interest.  The company also provided more information on the US$250mn convertible bond (CB) placement, which will be issued to IFC and Indigo.  Broadly, the terms of the CB mirror the terms of the convertible prefs (CPS), as the CBs (a) will have a conversion price of P38/sh, (b) will have a maturity of six (6) years), and (c) are immediately convertible upon issuance.  The only difference is that the CB holders will receive an interest rate of 4.5%, lower than the interest rate for the CPS (6%) and our forecast (6%).    

    Upon full conversion of the CBs and CPS, CEB’s shares outstanding will rise to 1.248bn shares from c. 600mn today.  Our forecast assumes a fully-diluted share count of 1.252bn.  Note that while the lower-than-expected interest rate on the CB will have an impact on headline NPAT, it will NOT have an impact to how we present Regis or “core” profit. 

PROPERTY
Vista Land 1Q21 earnings drop 14% YoY
  • Vista Land’s 1Q21 net profit fell 14% to P2.0bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • While rental revenue was in line with our forecast, residential construction still appears hampered
  • Residential revenue fell 13% to P6.3bn
  • Rental revenue “only” fell 9% to P2.0bn
    • Unlike other mall operators, Vista did not grant mall rental concessions to all mall tenants
    • Affiliate tenants AllDay (supermarket) and AllHome (DIY, furniture), which occupy c. 50% of mall space, have resilient revenue
  • Reservation sales grew 4% YoY (+14% QoQ) to P16.1bn
    • 82% of the 2019 average
    • In line with REGIS forecast
  • VLL is trading at 4.6X FY21E P/E and 59% discount to NAV
