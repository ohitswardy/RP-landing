Even though the momentum could moderate in succeeding periods, we believe street upgrades could still be massive.

Domestic pax vols improved substantially QoQ after travel restrictions were lifted in late-Feb but this was offset by softness in international vols, which also resulted in lower average fares. Management also gave its views on the financial troubles of PAL and Air Asia Philippines, and what these would mean to the competitive landscape.

We also have a few more from those not under official coverage:

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM), the country’s operator of 7-11 convenience stores, reported a net loss in 1Q.  Same store sales (-20% YoY) continues to be challenged by mobility restrictions, which limits foot traffic. The company’s strategy to pivot to a minimart format – as CEO Victor Paterno discussed during our CEO Roundtable series – will likely materialize in the coming quarters.

AC Energy (ACEN PM) reported that its 1Q21 income jumped 54% to P829mn. The figure will continue to evolve as it finishes construction of onshore assets and infuses the international business. In the briefing, ACEN said it has identified 12,000MW worth of renewable projects which will allow it to handily exceed its 5,000MW target, even if some of these don’t push through. Management also talked about precarious power supply situation (yellow alerts triggered despite depressed demand), prompting a rethink of its contracting strategy in the short to medium term.

Broadband service provider Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) announced net income of P1.6bn (+170% YoY) and EBITDA of P3.1bn (+102%) for 1Q21.  This was on the back of 84% jump in revenue.  The company ended 1Q with 1,182k residential broadband subscribers, implying net adds of just under 144k over the first three months. For perspective, PLDT (TEL PM) added a 112k subs (net) while Globe (GLO PM) did 46k.

+++

Metro Pacific’s (MPI PM) NLEX toll road received another tariff adjustment to be implemented this week.  While the hike is minimal (1%), it is the second such increase in six months and suggests regulators are more accommodative to the toll road operator.

CONGLOS
DMCI: Strong 1Q21 profit rebound
  • 1Q21 core profit doubles QoQ to P4.04bn – Ahead of forecast. DMCI’s 1Q21 recurring profit doubled QoQ P4.0bn, after benefitting from (a) a surge in commodity prices, (b) eased mobility restrictions, and (c) improved power sales. While we believe its 1Q21 performance should not be annualized, particularly after recent outages at Semirara (see Twin-track recovery in 1Q21 but a poor 2Q looms for power segment, 6 May 2021), we nonetheless believe massive street upgrades are likely.
  • Property: Revenues jump further in 1Q21; Reservation sales still soft. The property unit reached a new record in revenue bookings in 1Q21 but the recent lockdown could slow this momentum. EBITDA margin jumped to 24%, which management believes is sustainable. Un-booked revenues stood at P77bn, which can sustain revenue and profit recognition until 2023. Default rates improved to 6.9% from 8.6% but remain above pre-pandemic levels (3-4%). Meanwhile, reservation sales dropped 14% YoY (In line) and remains 30% below pre-pandemic levels. Foreign sales now account for 11% of sales from 15%. 
  • Construction: Operations improve but challenges remain; Bid pipeline healthy. Profits of the construction unit improved further in 1Q21, as infra projects ramped up. Like the property unit, the recent lockdown has reduced productivity. The order book dropped to P55bn from P59bn in end-2020. The company is currently participating in over fifteen (15) civil works packages under the ODA-funded Metro Manila Subway (P140bn) and the NSCR south line (P180bn), which will be bid out this year.
  • Nickel biz still awaiting permits; Off-grid plants improve on improved sales. The nickel unit had another blockbuster quarter but depleting reserves still blur the medium-term outlook. The government lifted the moratorium on new mining permits but has yet to issue specific guidelines (“IRR”). Meanwhile, the off-grid plants posted better profits on better demand and fuel efficiencies.

BANKS
SECB NPLs improve 50bps on payments; but NIMs drop 65bps QoQ

Good news / bad news for SECB’s 1Q21, as its NPAT fell 43% YoY to P1.64bn. The good news is that NPLs dropped nearly 50bps QoQ to 3.51%, largely as accounts that were previously NPL turned current. This allowed provision expense to plunge 93% YoY to P402mn while still getting NPL cover to rise slightly to 118%. The bad news is that NIMs collapsed 65bps QoQ, as loan rates continued to compress while deposit costs have bottomed out. The bottom-line was also dragged down by a P1.2bn write-down of deferred tax assets (DTA) due to the CREATE Act.

The improvement in asset quality is quite impressive. If the bank can keep NPLs under control despite the new lockdowns, it could be a major upside risk to our forecasts. The NIM compression though is slightly worrisome. With liquidity extremely high across the sector, competition could keep margins down for at least several quarters.

Some highlights (charts below):

  • Gross loans fell 5% YoY (net -6.6% YoY), with consumers -12%, and corporate/commercial loans -2%. Consumer loans accounted for 25% of total, the same ratio as at end-2020.
  • Deposits grew 3% YoY, more surprisingly deposits were up 18% QoQ. The jump was driven by TDs, which jumped 45% QoQ. The funds were used to boost SECB’s investment portfolio (particularly AFS), as the bank seems to have taken advantage of rising rates at the long end of the curve.
  • NII dropped 18% YoY and 8% QoQ, as NIMs plunged 88bps QoQ. Loan yields fell 63bps QoQ, while deposit costs flattened out (+8bps QoQ). The NIM compression was also due to the change in asset mix towards lower-yielding government bonds vs loans.
  • Fee incomes were flat YoY in 1Q21 and are nearing pre-Covid levels. Trading/fx gains though collapsed 89% YoY as interest rates increased in Feb/March. Opex was flat, -2% YoY.
  • Provision expense fell 93% YoY to just P402mn, owing to a sharp improvement in asset quality. SECB’s NPL ratio fell 49bps QoQ to 3.41% from 3.90% at end-2020. Management attributed the improvement primarily to accounts previously non-performing turning current (6 months of consistent payments), with some due to restructuring, and some to write-offs. Hence, despite the drop in provisions, NPL coverage still increased to 118% in 1Q21 (from 115% at end-2020).
  • Tax expense for 1Q21 included P1.2bn write-off of DTAs due to the passage of the CREATE Act.

Overall, the result seems very strong compared to our forecasts, largely on the provision front. We would watch the NIM trends closely, though, as intense competition could persist into 2022.
