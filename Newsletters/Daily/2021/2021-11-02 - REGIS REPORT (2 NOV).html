REGIS REPORT

02 November 2021

The 3Q results season is taking off.  Leading off today is Universal Robina (URC PM), whose operating results were affected by rising cost pressures. The same can be said about the 3Q numbers of Cemex PH (CHP PM).

The initial numbers from Semirara (SCC PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM) are not looking bad – we ought to get more detail after their respective earnings calls.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) - Underlying operations were soft with 3Q EBIT -9% YoY. Cost pressures were apparent and price increases were not enough to fully offset the jump in key input prices (palm oil, wheat). While top line trends heading into 4Q are positive with the lifting of lockdowns across the region, near term margins could continue to be pressured by cost inflation.

  • Semirara (SCC PM) (+) - Headline 9M21 profit rose 244% YoY to P10.3bn, tracking ahead of Regis and consensus.  3Q21 profit was slightly down QoQ to P4.01bn, on lower sequential coal sales volumes and higher power costs.  These factors offset the 18% QoQ jump in coal mining ASPs.

 

  • Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) (+) - Reported 3Q21 profit plunged to P94mn, with EBITDA dropping 23% QoQ, 30% YoY to P1bn. Sales volumes dropped 4% QoQ and growth has slowed to just +1% YoY in 3Q21. 9M21 profit of P897mn (+18% YoY) have outperformed but we believe a demand slowdown and rising input costs are formidable headwinds.

 

  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (=) - AP’s 2Q21 core income was P5.6bn (+89%YoY, +44%QoQ). It saw commissioning revenue from GNPD Unit 1, augmented by higher water inflow for hydros and higher availability from its coal plants. Electricity sales for its distribution utilities grew 5% in 9M21 to 4,197GWh, implying 7%YoY and 1%QoQ improvements for 3Q21.

+++

Mynt, the holding company that owns 100% of e-wallet GCash, received over US$300m in new investment that valued it at “over US$2bn”.  This new funding round involves capital from both existing and new investors, with the exact split unclear. Globe’s (GLO PM) stake in Mynt is estimated to dip close to 35% post-transaction, which would imply a minimum valuation of US$700m or ~P270 per Globe share though the actual figure is likely to be higher.

We reckon the current share price of Globe (~P3,000/share) already implies a valuation between US$4 - 5bn.

+++

The total loan portfolio of the country’s commercial banks was up +2.7% in Sept, an improvement from the +1.3% in Aug. Banking lending is now slowly recovering though that is not the case for some segments (consumer, auto lending).

CEMENT
Cemex Phils: 3Q21 demand slows; dimmer near-term prospects
  • 3Q21 EBITDA, profit plunge. Headline 3Q21 profit plunged QoQ and YoY to P94mn, with 3Q21 EBITDA also dropping by 23% QoQ and 30% YoY to P1bn. Management attributed the poor 3Q to weather-related demand softness and a plant maintenance outage. While CHP’s 9M21 profit is outperforming relative to our forecast and consensus, we believe near-term prospects look dim, considering elevated global thermal coal prices and likely weakness in demand.
  • 3Q21 volumes drop QoQ; Risks to volumes skewed to the downside. Sales volumes fell 4% QoQ in 3Q21 but were up +1% YoY, bringing 9M21 sales volumes up 11% YoY. The company maintained its +12-14% volume growth guidance for the year, suggesting 4Q21 volumes should grow much higher than +11%. Note that weather is typically a problem for CHP’s 4Q operations.

Management believes the election ban in late-March and the transition of the new government could adversely affect demand in 1Q-3Q22. However, they are cautiously optimistic about demand growing at “mid-single digits” next year.

  • ASPs up slightly QoQ; Trader imports lose share; Price increase in early 2022. ASPs improved by +1% QoQ, as the company raised prices further. CHP estimates trader imports only accounted for 17% of industry cement demand in 3Q21 from 21% in 2Q21. Rising freight costs may have started to affect margins of traders, who do not usually have long-term shipping contracts. The company plans to raise prices in early 2022.
  • Plant outage leads to surge in import costs in 3Q; Higher fuel costs to affect 4Q. EBITDA margins dropped to 19% in 3Q due to higher importation of clinker after shutting down its kiln in APO for maintenance. While fuel and power costs remained low in 3Q, management noted that its recent coal shipments have already reflected higher global prices.
  • Debt restructuring talks progressing well. Management is optimistic it can reach an agreement with its creditors to restructure its debt, given delays in the operations of its new kiln.
