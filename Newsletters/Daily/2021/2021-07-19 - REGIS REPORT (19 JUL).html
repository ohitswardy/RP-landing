REGIS REPORT

19 July 2021

Last Friday, the government disclosed it has identified sixteen new cases of the Covid-19 Delta variant in the country. While the Dept of Health’s statement only confirms what was, in our view, inevitable anyway, we reckon this headline should depress sentiment. Until very recently, the DOH hardly even tested for the new variant/s.  Nevertheless, the confirmation that the Delta variant is present raises the specter that cases will spike.

Meanwhile, another 5.8m vaccine doses arrived in the country, more than half of them of the J&J brand (which requires only one dose).  The country has now received 27.8m doses.  The government will release data on the number administered later today. It’s likely we will see the number hit 15m, with close to 10% of the population having received at least one dose.

+++

The government economic team says it will consider a new round of stimulus in response to the pandemic.  Congress has already tabled Bayanihan III, which the administration previously expressed opposition to.  The DOF wants any new stimulus to be deficit/revenue-neutral.  That may appear oxymoronic at first blush, but what it means is that new revenue sources (e.g. asset sales, dividends from government investments) will have to be found for the administration to agree.

+++

We share a few thoughts on the recently announced plan to do a follow-on offering for Keepers (KEEPR PM) below.  Keepers is the liquor distribution business of the Puregold (PGOLD PM) group, which is being listed via a reverse takeover.

CONSUMER
More on Keepers Holdings (KEEPR PM)

Summary of the offer

The liquor distribution business of Cosco Capital (COSCO PM), in the process of being listed via the shell company Da Vinci (DAVIN PM), plans to conduct a follow-on offering (FOO) to raise as much as P7.5bn. This business will be renamed “The Keepers Holdings” (KEEPR PM). Collectively, the Group is the largest distributor of imported liquor in the Philippines with market shares of 74% of volumes and 67% of retail sales value in 2020 (IWSR).

According to the prospectus, KEEPR plans to issue 3bn shares at an offer price range of P2-2.50/sh. Post-FOO, the Lucio Co group (through COSCO and Invescap) will own c.79% of KEEPR.

Bulk of the expected proceeds will be used for “strategic acquisition opportunities”, as well as product and distribution expansion.

Valuation

The implied valuation for KEEPR from the proposed FOO does not stray too far from our own estimates as included in our valuation for Cosco (over P20bn, pre-money). This operation generated P8.2bn in revenue and P1.2bn in profits for FY20. Assuming our 2022E profit forecast (P1.2bn) for COSCO’s liquor distribution segment, we estimate the implied post-money valuation for KEEPR is 23-29x 2022E PE.

Key points

  • Largest distributor of imported liquor in the Philippines. KEEPR is the largest distributor of premium imported liquor in the Philippines with 2020 market shares of 74% of industry volumes and 67% of retail sales value (IWSR). It holds exclusive distribution rights or is the exclusive third-party distributor in the Phils for a number of global liquor brands, such as Alfonso, Johnnie Walker, Jim Beam and Jinro. In 2020, its top 10 suppliers accounted for 81% of its total cost.

KEEPR primarily sells its products to retailers and sub-distributors. Around 90% of its sales in 2020 was off-premise. Its top 5 customers accounted for 56% of 2020 sales, of which c.25% is to PGOLD.

  • Portfolio caters to value and luxury segment. The brandy category has been the key driver for KEEPR’s sales volumes over the past few years. In 2020, the category accounted 73% of its sales volumes (3.4mn cases) in 2020. Within the category, its main brand is Alfonso is positioned within the value segment at cP200 per 700ml bottle. However, KEEPR also has products that are positioned in the premium/luxury segment, retailing for as much as P180k (USD3600) per bottle.

  • Scale, track record are key advantages. Approx 30% of KEEPR’s volumes are from brands it does not own. Agreements with suppliers and brand principals are typically renewed every 2-3 years (in line with industry practice), but the group has a long track record of maintaining relationships with its principals. For its top 10 suppliers, we estimate the average relationship around 15 years. Its longest supplier relationship is 24 years. This is a key advantage when trying to secure new brand distribution agreements. Its scale and its relationship with PGOLD – one of the largest retail distribution networks in the country – is also a key advantage.
  • Exposure to forex. Given that it is an importer, KEEPR is exposed to currency fluctuations, particularly USD and EUR. Large swings in the exchange rate could lead to some near term margin volatility, though the Group has historically been able to mitigate its impact either through pricing negotiations with suppliers or pricing adjustments to its customers.
