REGIS REPORT

15 September 2021

A ray of hope for banks? Sector-wide NPL ratio was flat, up just 4bps MoM to 4.65% in July (ex-interbank loans), with Restructured Loans (RLs) up just 1 bp MoM to 3.06%. Even more hopeful was that the Past Due (PD) ratio was down 5bps MoM to 5.31%. In theory, the lower PD should point to slower new NPL formation in coming months. A word of caution, though. The figures were before the current lockdowns, which took effect in early August. That said, based on meetings with the Big Three at last week’s JAF conference, management has not seen a material uptick in PD or NPL, at least so far.

If NPLs have indeed peaked, then there could be significant upside risk to near-term earnings, particularly for Metrobank (MBT PM), which had the lowest NPL ratio (2.3%) and by far the highest NPL cover (179%) among banks we cover in 2Q21.

The data released yesterday by the government showed 16% of the total population of the country and 44% of Metro Manila is now fully vaccinated (two doses for a two-dose regime). The proportions for those with at  least one dose are 20% and 63% respectively.

The pace of vaccinations seems to have picked up only marginally (413,000/day over the past eight days). Vaccine supply does not appear to be the main bottleneck now as the country has ~18m doses in inventory (equivalent to over a month at a pace of 500,000/day). We reckon it’s likely due to logistics and the limited capability of smaller local governments (LGU) (as opposed to the larger, more organized urban centers).

+++

Natural gas flow from Malampaya was supposed to have resumed last night, which should ease the fears about a spike in power prices. The outage over the past few days, however, is symptomatic of the bigger issues surrounding Malampaya: the depletion of the resource compounded by the absence of investment. We already see a probable physical shortage in power supply by 2024, and the apparently fast deterioration in Malampaya will exacerbate that.

Malampaya fuels a quarter of the Luzon grid’s output (in actual kwh) and a switch to more expensive fuel (diesel or LNG) will make it more painful for consumers. The power generator who will likely have the most available capacity would be Aboitiz Power (AP PM), as its RES contracts expire and as its much-delayed GNPower Dingin finally goes into operation. However, even those who don’t have a lot to spare today, e.g. First Gen (FGEN PM), will also benefit as the looming supply tightness becomes obvious ahead of contracts expiries and renegotiations.

BANKS
NPLs, past due plateau in July - close to the peak?
  • Asset quality seems to have flattened out in July. NPL and Restructured Loan (RL) ratios posted VERY modest increases MoM. Industry-wide NPL ratio inched up 4bps MoM to 4.65% in July, with nominal PHP amount up less than 1% MoM to P486bn. RLs were pretty much flat MoM, up just 1bp to 3.06%. Meanwhile, the Past Due (PD) levels fell slightly, down 5bps MoM to 5.31% and down 0.5% to P574bn. PD tends to be a leading indicator of the direction of asset quality. NPL Cover was flat MoM at 82%.
  • It is important to note that these figures were right before the current lockdown, in effect since early August. That said, banks that attended our JAF conference last week – The Big Three: BDO, MBT and BPI – all indicated that they have not yet seen an uptick in PD/NPL. All three banks opined that businesses and households may have adapted to the on-again-off-again lockdowns, and those that have survived until now are doing fine. Management of all three said they were officially still holding on to their respective guidance of peak NPL ratio. However, all expressed guarded optimism that the worst is behind us, and that provisions can normalize from here.
  • If the trend holds, the bank with most potential earnings upside from lower provisions – or even write-backs – would be MBT, given their very high NPL Cover at 179% as of 2Q21, followed by BPI (124%), then SECB (103%) and BDO (100%).

    • Due to DPWH projects, higher spending for defense/PNP projects
  • Brings YT-July infra spending (excluding subsidies and equity to GOCCs and transfers to LGUs) up 42.6% YoY to P499.4bn
  • Total infrastructure spending in YT-July (i.e. including GOCC subsidies and LGU transfers) rose 35.6% YoY to P625.2bn
    • This accounts for 61% of the government’s year-end target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY), equivalent to 5.1% of GDP
    • For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn (+25% YoY), equivalent to 5.8% of GDP
