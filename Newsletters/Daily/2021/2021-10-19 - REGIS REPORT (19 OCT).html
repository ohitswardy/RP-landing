REGIS REPORT

19 October 2021

The pace of vaccinations did not pick up but rather even dropped. For the week ended Oct 17, the daily rate of vaccinations was 376,000 – a drop of over 20% from the previous week. Yes, more people are getting vaccinated but the latest pace still leaves much to be desired.

The supply of vaccines is no longer the constraint for vaccinations. It is bureaucracy, logistics and vaccine hesitancy. In fact, the country received 2x more doses (5m) than it administered last week (2.6m). The Philippines now has almost 40m doses in inventory – this is not far from the total doses already administered in the past seven months (52m) and is enough for three months of vaccinations at the current pace.

With the government now starting to administer doses to minors, hopefully the pace will improve. Around 22% of the total population of the Philippines has now been fully vaccinated.

+++

With oil prices soaring, the government is now considering fuel subsidies for public utility vehicles (PUV) like jeepneys in a bid to restrain inflation. It is likely however that the cost of the subsidies will be borne by end-users anyway. In the past, oil companies were persuaded to cut prices specifically for PUVs which were compensated for by higher prices on other products.

Transport costs account for 8.1% of the CPI. The 5.1% increase for the component seen in the last print is almost certainly going to rise in the coming months with oil prices where they are.

Indications for MREIT’s (MREIT PM) revenue and dividend look like they will be a tad ahead of previous guidance. More below. MREIT is the REIT spun off from Megaworld (MEG PM).

MACRO
Pace of vaccinations slows down again
  • As of October 17, the 7-day average vaccinations dropped to 375,773/day
    • From 469,092/day as of October 10
  • 22% of the Philippine population (24.3mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID
    • As of October 6, 56% of the Metro Manila population (7.5mn people) has been fully vaccinated
  • The Philippines has received a total of 91.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 52.3mn doses (as of October 17)

MACRO
Dept. of Transportation eyes fuel subsidies to stop fare hike
  • The Department of Transportation (DOTr) on Monday said it was looking at fuel subsidies and direct aid to both public utility drivers and passengers
    • Looking at possible uniform discounts on public utility vehicles (PUVs) across all gas stations nationwide
  • DOTr is also pushing to lift capacity restrictions for PUVs
    • PUVs are allowed to carry half of its maximum passenger capacity
  • PUV drivers have sought fare hikes but Transport Secretary Tugade has dismissed the petition
    • The last time the LTFRB approved a fare hike was in 2018, when diesel prices soared to P53 a liter
  • Transport costs account for 8.1% of the CPI basket. Transport CPI is up 5.2% YoY in September 2021 and up 13.9% vs September 2019

UTILITIES
Maynilad warns of water interruptions due to gov’t flood control project
  • Maynilad announced water interruptions that will range from 25-85 hours between Oct 25 (11am) to Oct 28 (11:59pm)
    • Cited the realignment of Maynilad’s existing pipeline along Sobriedad corner Cristobal St. in Sampaloc to give way for the government’s flood control project
  • Interruption will affect 28% (421k) of its service connections, involving 2.9mn customers
    • Of these, 60k (c. 4% of total) connections in Sampaloc, Manila will have no water for three days
    • Affected customers: Las Piñas, Makati, Manila, Parañaque, Pasay, as well as parts of Cavite province such as Bacoor, Cavite City, Imus City, Kawit, Noveleta, and Rosario
  • To cushion the impact of the interruption, Maynilad will deploy 60 mobile water tankers and install 14 stationary water tanks
  • Maynilad is a 53%-owned unit of Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and 25%-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM)
PROPERTY
MREIT 1QFY22 revenue reaches P711mn
  • MREIT’s 1QFY22 (July to September) revenue reached P711mn
    • 28% of the FY22 target of P2.5bn (based on the prospectus)
  • Occupancy of the portfolio now stands at 100%, from 93% as of end-March
  • MREIT plans to declare a dividend of at least P0.24/share for the quarter
  • If we were to annualize this, FY22 DPS would reach P0.96/share
    • Higher than the P0.91/share in the REIT prospectus
  • This implies 5.4% dividend yield
    • The Philippine government 10-year bond yield is 5.0%
  • MREIT is targeting to have a portfolio of 500,000sqm by 2024
    • From 225,000sqm currently
  • MREIT will pay land lease rent to MEG:
    • Before 1 July 2023 – zero
    • 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2025 – 2.5% of office/retail revenue
    • 1 July 2025 onwards – 5% of office/retail revenue
  • MEG is currently trading at 60% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 6.8X FY22E P/E
