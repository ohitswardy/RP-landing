REGIS REPORT

14 April 2021

President Duterte certified as urgent three pieces of proposed legislation, ostensibly to ease foreign investment restrictions in the country.  The three involve amendments to existing laws, namely:  (1) the Retail Trade Liberalization Act,  (2) the Foreign Investments Act and (3) the Public Services Act.

Among the three, the measure involving retail trade looks the least impactful from a macro perspective, at least immediately. Large retailers are already allowed to be 100% foreign owned (e.g. H&M, Ikea).  The new changes propose to liberalize even small retail outlets like groceries and restaurants. That grocery or eatery at the street corner does not have to be surreptitiously owned by their “real” foreign owners anymore.

The proposed changes to Foreign Investments Act, on the other hand, would allow the practice of foreigners in a number of professions, including accountancy, architecture and medicine. This would bring expertise and competition to local professionals.

The highest profile changes, however, are likely to be those for the Public Services Act, as they propose to allow foreign control of many industries that currently have nationality restrictions. The most prominent among these industries today is the telecom sector. In fact, it is plausible to think  that the main target of this proposal is to allow control of the third telecom operator by China Telecom. Passage of this has met with some skepticism, notably from the chair of the committee handling this matter (Sen Grace Poe) who has expressed concerns over national security given the territorial issues with China.

Given the large majority that government allies have in Congress, it is possible that even Poe would not be able to stand in the way of the bill on Public Services being rubber stamped. If the bill does not get passed, we reckon the chances of the third operator becoming a major threat would be significantly diminished.

+++

Here comes the surge… in NPLs. After four  months of stability (Sept-Jan), the banking system’s asset quality deteriorated sharply in February. NPL ratio (ex-interbank loans) jumped to 4.16% from 3.80% in January and 3.70% at end-2020. In absolute terms, February NPLs grew 9.1% MoM to P427.4bn. Psat due (PD) rates also increased to 5.19% from 4.77% in January. Worryingly, NPL coverage dropped to 87.3% from 94.6% the previous month. In a bit of good news, Restructured Loans (RLs) were steady at 1.89%, up just 6bps MoM. That said, the combined NPL+RL ratio topped 6%

We reckon the deterioration in (reported) asset quality is due to the rolling off of the Bayanihan 2 moratorium. Presumably most took the 2-month grace period early, ie in mid-September 2020. Recall that borrowers could invoke the grace period until end-2020, so the full impact of NPLs will likely spill over to March and April.

We would be wary again of NPLs as a result of the current lockdowns in “NCR-Plus” that will run at least until the end of April. This could bring a new wave of defaults in 2Q/3Q.

This does not bode well for banks’ 1Q21. Loans contracted 3.1% YoY, accelerating from -2.7% in January. NIMs are compressing on lower loan yields. The hope was that provision expense might be able to fall. But with NPLs rising again, we might have to brace for another round of provisioning.

While vehicle sales for the month of March (for CAMPI) were up sharply YoY, it was only because the base in 2020 was very much depressed.  MoM, unit sales dropped 21% with the imposition of the safeguard duty on select vehicles.

MACRO
Duterte certifies three economic bills as urgent
  • President Rodrigo R. Duterte certified as urgent three bills aimed at easing foreign investment restrictions in the country
    • Public Service Act (PSA), Foreign Investments Act (FIA) and the Retail Trade Liberalization (RTL) Act
    • The Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) expects the bills to be passed before Congress adjourns in June
    • Congress is currently in recess and will resume session on May 17 until June 4
  • An urgent certification from the President means these bills can be passed on the third reading immediately after the second reading
  • President Duterte will not certify as urgent the proposed third stimulus law
    • Will instead work to identify sources of stimulus funds that will not bust the 8.9% budget deficit cap

MACRO
Vehicle sales fall 21% MoM in March
  • Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, fell 21% MoM to 20,702 units (+88% YoY)
    • MoM drop due to the imposition of provisional safeguard duties on imports and renewed lockdown restrictions
    • Brings 1Q21 industry car sales up 9% YoY to 70,312 units
    • CAMPI expects industry sales to grow by +20-25% YoY in 2021, slower than its initial projection of +30-35% YoY due to the provisional safeguard duty
  • Toyota sold 9,201 units, down 30% MoM but up 103% YoY
    • Market share dropped 6ppts MoM to 44%
    • Toyota’s 1Q21 retail sales are up 29% YoY to 33,095
  • Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year
    • To reach our forecast, Toyota will need to sell c. 9,400 units per month
  • With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%)
    • To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales should average 7,900 units
  • Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively
  • GT Capital is trading at a 13% discount to its market-based NAV of P604/sh

BANKS
February NPL ratio highest in 11 years
  • The nonperforming loan (NPL) ratio rose to 4.08% in February, highest since October 2009 (4.09%)
    • Gross NPLs surged 80% to P431bn (+9.05% MoM)
    • NPL coverage ratio dropped to 86.64% from 91.2% last year
  • Past due loans in February reached P551.472bn (+71% YoY), bringing the past due loan ratio to 5.21% from 2.95% a year ago
  • Restructured loans also surged by more than six times (346% YoY) to P200.986bn. With this, its ratio reached 1.9%, up from the 0.41% in February last year
CONSUMER
Emperador renews buy-back program
  • Emperador’s board of directors has approved a share buy-back program to repurchase up to P1bn worth of common shares
    • Equivalent to 0.6% of current market capitalization
  • Public float is currently 15.7%
  • The new buy-back program will run from April 12, 2021 to December 31, 2021
  • The current buy-back program expires on May 16, 2021 (originally approved in May 2017)
    • Roughly P3.0bn worth of shares have been repurchased under the buy-back program
