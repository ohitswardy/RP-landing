President Duterte meets with the Cabinet today to discuss the recommendations of the Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) and Metro Manila mayors to downgrade the nation’s capital to Modified General Community Quarantine (MGCQ), the least restrictive of the quarantine classifications. It is unclear, though, if the President will necessarily make his decision today. As we have repeatedly pointed out, though, opening up public transportation might be more crucial to the economic recovery.

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) approved Thursday the application of Meralco (MER PM) to refund to its customers P13.886bn, representing the amount it has collected in excess of the previously approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.381/kWh since Jul 2015. We stress that the refund will have no direct P&L impact as the company has more than fully provided for it already.

The application is part of Meralco’s proposal to address the issues relating to its lapsed regulatory period (Jul 2015 onwards), throughout which the regulator was unable to set a final tariff. With the refund, Meralco is now effectively seeking a declaration that the IAR is no longer just “interim” but already “final”.

Such a declaration affirming the finality of the IAR could prove massively positive for Meralco as the company has been setting aside annual provisions of least P10bn in the event of ruling cutting rates retroactively. This would have significant implications not just relating to a reversal of huge provisions (likely over P70bn) already made but also on prospective earnings.

POWER
San Miguel submits lowest bids to supply Meralco
  • Two of San Miguel’s (SMC PM) subsidiaries submitted the lowest prices to supply 1,800MW of power to Meralco
  • Excellent Energy Resources, Inc (EERI) submitted a bid of P4.1462/kwh (levelized cost of energy) for 1,200MW of power
  • Masinloc Power Partners Co Ltd (MPPCL) gave a bid of P4.2605/kwh LCOE
  • These bids are lower than Meralco’s own Atimonan coal plant
  • Both firms are subsidiaries of SMC
    • EERI will supply 1,200MW from a LNG-fired facility beginning Dec 2024 for 20 years
    • MPPCL will supply 600MW from a coal plant beginning May 2025 for 20 years
  • Both firms are supposed to provide power from new facilities but can provide replacement power from other sources should the new plants not be available by committed date
  • We believe it is highly unlikely that the new facilities will be able to begin operations by that target date of the contracts
  • We remain of the view that there are likely to be no significant baseload capacity additions by 2025 in Luzon after GN Power Dinginin
TELCOS
Sky Fiber adjusts broadband plan speeds
  • Sky Fiber announced upgraded broadband speeds for their plans (link)
  • The key change is on their entry level plans where Sky Fiber increased the speed for its P999 plant to 20mbps (from 10mbps) and for its P1499 plan to 40mbps (vs 25mbps).
  • The new speeds/plans now offer better value vs. Converge’s. The main constraint for Sky, however, is likely to be coverage. The most recent estimate is Sky Fiber had around 300k subscribers (vs 1m for Converge ICT).

  • The message here is that this space is becoming more competitive as broadband providers increase the speeds of their offerings. However, the competition is more a question of coverage, in our opinion, rather than value or price. At least for now.
TRANSPORT
Notes from CEB briefing
  • Emphasized lowering already “world-class” costs.  The company reiterated initiatives on lowering costs, including (a) right-sizing manpower, (b) streamlining maintenance costs, (c) maximizing use of their more fuel-efficient aircraft (i.e. NEOs).  Note that the company expects costs per ASK to drop to 2.30 centavos in 2022 and 2.15 centavos in 2023 from 2.46 centavos in 2019.  These are c. 22% and 9% lower than our estimate of 2.95 centavos in 2022 and 2.35 centavos in 2023, respectively.
  • Initiatives to kick start travel.  They emphasized the push to “test before boarding” (i.e. antigen tests 5 hours before travel from Manila) to address transmission risks concerns of most local governments. Note that at P700 (US$14), CEB’s antigen tests are significantly lower than market prices (P2k and up). Some local governments have opened up to this as a pre-travel requirement (eg. General Santos, Butuan, Dipolog, and Pagadian) and CEB continues to work with other LGUs to allow antigen tests instead of the more expensive RT-PCR test. 
  • Recovery outlook.  Management cited IATA’s 2023/2024 global recovery for intercontinental/transcontinental travel.  However, management believes domestic travel will revert to pre-Covid levels faster than international, citing the recent experience of China, Vietnam, and Thailand.  Note that our forecast broadly assumes this outlook (i.e. 2022 domestic pax will match 2019 levels; international will be at 40% of pre-Covid).  Note that the government has allowed airlines to be exempted from the 1-meter distancing rule so CEB can maximize its seat capacity.
  • Convertible pref (CPS) mechanics.  The CPS are immediately convertible after (a) the company can obtain PSE approval for listing of the underlying common shares to be issued upon conversion of the CPS or (b) 40 days from listing (tentatively on March 29), whichever is earlier. The CPS will be tradeable in the PSE on 29 March. JG Summit will fully underwrite the CPS stock rights offer. 
  • Dilution from convertible bond.  Management noted that they are still negotiating with third party investors for its planned US$250mn convertible bond (CB) issuance.  They reiterated that they are mindful about the dilutive impact of such issuance to minority shareholders so any financing they come up with will definitely consider this. Note that based on our conversations with management, they initially planned for the prices of the CB and the CPS to be similar. Moreover, they emphasized that there are other fundraising plans they are looking at, including aircraft sales, which are all ongoing.

    • Montosco Inc.
    • Meritus Prime Distributions Inc.
    • Premier Wine and Spirits Inc.
  • Terms of the share swap have yet to be disclosed
  • COSCO currently trades at a 62% discount to NAV based on current market prices (P14.27/sh)
