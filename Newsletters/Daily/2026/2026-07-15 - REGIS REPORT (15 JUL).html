15 July 2026

Indonesian firm Barito Renewables (BREN IJ) has reportedly made overtures to buy Philippine geothermal energy firm Energy Development Corp (EDC) for US$5bn equity value (US$7bn enterprise value). EDC is controlled by First Gen (FGEN PM), which holds 46% economic interest and 60% voting interest in the former.

 

EDC, on some measures, is either the largest or second largest operator of geothermal power generation assets in the world with ~1,000MW of capacity. It also has other RE assets in its portfolio. At the valuations mentioned above, EDC would be on ~40x trailing PER and 22x trailing EBITDA.

 

The last transaction for EDC involved private equity investors taking a 54% economic stake at an equity valuation of just over US$2bn in 2017-2018. Using the valuation at that time, we estimate the NAV for First Gen would be ~P47/share. Assuming a US$5bn valuation for EDC would lift FGEN’s NAV to ~P70/share.

 

Note there are a few caveats worth highlighting. First BREN’s market cap looks big (US$25bn) but we are not certain that it can raise the US$5bn to buy 100% of EDC. BREN’s EBITDA last year was only ~US$520m and it already has net debt of over US$2bn. It’s likely that BREN will need a partner if it desires to buy out 100% of EDC.

 

Second, nationality restrictions on EDC’s operations will probably mean an entity like BREN will need a local partner (if it indeed wants some form of control).

President Marcos signed Executive Order #119, which establishes a framework for government data residency. The EO mandates that a portion of the government’s data will have to be stored within the Philippines (i.e. not in the cloud, in data centers abroad). This would cover data classified as “Top Secret” and “Secret”. Data classified as “Confidential” should also be stored domestically, with exceptions allowed.

 

The total data center (DC) capacity in the Philippines is only around 130MW IT load. In contrast, it’s estimated that total government data would need 200-300MW of DC IT load. It’s not clear, however, how much of that would need to be migrated locally. The EO calls for the data migration to be completed within three years

 

Notwithstanding the lack of clarity at this point on the quantum that needs to be relocated, this new government policy will definitely still mean more incremental demand for DC capacity.

 

PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) account for most of the ~130MW DC capacity in the country.  PLDT is also just about to spin off and list its DC business into the VITRO REIT.

 

+++

 

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) reduced its 2028 BPO industry revenue and headcount targets. It now targets revenue to hit a range of US$43.3bn to US$50.5bn, representing 3%-8% CAGR from the US$40bn revenue in 2025. What is more concerning is that it now expects BPO headcount of 1.85mn to 2.14mn by 2028. While this means the industry could grow up to 4% annually, it also means IBPAP believes the industry’s employment base could shrink over the next few years. Since the BPO sector occupies about half of the Metro Manila office stock, its headcount expansion (or contraction) could have large implications on office demand.

  • President Marcos signed Executive Order (EO) 119 that establishes a “data residency framework” for all government data
  • The EO mandates that some of the government’s data will need to be stored within the Philippines
    • Such data includes those classified as “Secret” or “Top Secret”
    • “Confidential” data should also be stored domestically, with exceptions
    • Other data can be stored in the cloud
  • Total government data currently housed in data centers/cloud is estimated to be equivalent to 200-300MW worth of IT load

  • Government agencies (and thus third-party providers) will have three years to transition and comply
  • This EO will ultimately mean greater demand for local data centers
