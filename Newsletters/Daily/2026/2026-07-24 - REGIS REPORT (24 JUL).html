REGIS REPORT

24 July 2026

Oil prices are back up again, with Brent surpassing US$100/barrel, a level approaching the peak/s following the US attack on Iran. The energy-consuming world is again hostage to the calculations of a man and his party’s mid-term election prospects.

 

This surge in energy prices is likely to put an end to the collective sighs of relief when oil prices plunged in June. Concerns over transport / logistics and utility costs and their 2nd and 3rd order effects are now back on the table. Apart from re-surging energy costs, we have two more concerns: an unusually high minimum wage hike for Manila and the onset of a “super” El Nino, both of which could have a meaningful impact on CPI.

 

Local pundits predicting softening inflation and thus possibly lower interest rates could be forced to change their tunes soon. Our own estimates for average CPI inflation (6.7% for the year) and the year-end policy rate (5.25%) are higher than the Street’s for now. But we think the Street could be tweaking their numbers upward soon.

 

  

+++

 

Vehicles sales as reported by CAMPI were down 8% YoY in June, with unit sales for Toyota down 10% YoY. Cumulatively, unit sales for CAMPI members are down 11% for the first six months. Note CAMPI represents a large majority of units sold in the country but does not cover all firms/brands (i.e. it does not include BYD, whose distributorship is held by Ayala Corp (AC PM)).

 

Notably, xEVs (hybrids and BEVs) for CAMPI members sold were up 2.3x in June to almost 7,000 units.

 

+++

 

The government’s budget deficit reached P264bn for June (+9% YoY), taking the YT-June deficit to P787bn (+3%). For the month, government revenue grew 3% YoY while spending was up 5%. Primary spending was also up 5%.

 

+++

 

The government’s Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) says it expects to generate as much as US$40-70bn of investments from the Pax Silica hub once it reaches full development. The Pax Silica hub is part of the US-led initiative to secure tech/AI technology supply chains and diversify them from China.

 

If it happens, this initiative could be transformative for the country and, more specifically, the area in around the nexus of the hub (the provinces of Tarlac and Pampanga).

 

+++

 

Maribank Philippines (formerly SeaBank, part of the Sea / Shopee group), became the country’s 7th digibank, after the BSP approved the upgrade of its license from a rural bank. The Shopee group has been operating the bank since 2021 when it acquired Banco Laguna. As of 1Q26, Maribank had total gross loans of P36.8bn, which would have supplanted Maya Bank at the largest digibank by loans. By deposits, Maribank would come in second place at P57.2bn compared to Maya’s P75.6bn. (See charts below.)

 

It is unclear how much of the group’s BNPL business out of Shopee (SPayLater) is incorporated into Maribank. Based on the bank’s data, they reported NPL ratio of 1.74% and ROE of 41.0% in 1Q26. It’s possible that they write off bad loans quickly, as ROE was much lower at 13.21% in 4Q25. It’s also possible that they only transfer the “better quality” loans over to Maribank.

 

The news further highlights the lack of visibility into the non-bank finance company (NBFC) world. Since these are regulated by the SEC, and not the BSP, there is little by way of timely individual or aggregated data on the industry. To put it into perspective, Home Credit Philippines is more than double the size of Maribank, with NET loans of over P85bn in mid-2025, up from P74bn at end-2024 (of which GROSS loans in 2024 was P82.5bn).

 

It also rekindles the debate over whether large fintech firms should eventually become banks. On the one hand, banks allow for access to deposits and leverage. On the other hand, the BSP’s regulatory oversight can be quite… burdensome.

MACRO
Government posts P264bn budget deficit in June
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P264bn in June, wider by 9% YoY and 33% MoM as spending outpaced revenues
    • This brings the 6M26 deficit to P787bn, 3% wider vs the same period last year
  • Revenue collections in Jun rose 3% YoY
    • Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections decelerated to +5% from +15% the previous month
      • Note that May 15 was the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return
    • Customs collections picked up to +12% growth from +6% the previous month
    • Non-tax revenues fell 43% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances
      • Recall that dividends from GOCCs (Gov't-Owned and Controlled Corporations) were remitted early this year in Feb
  • Gov't spending was up 5% YoY, a tad faster than the 4% growth in May
    • Primary spending for the month increased by +5% vs +1% the month before
      • This marks the fourth straight month of YoY growth after six consecutive months of YoY declines in gov't spending
    • Interest payments rose 9% YoY
  • The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66tn (-5.4% of GDP), 5% wider YoY from P1.61tn (-5.3% of GDP) in 2025

 

 

MACRO
Government expects US$40bn to US$70bn in investments from Pax Silica hub
  • The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) expects the Pax Silica hub to generate between US$40bn and US$70bn in investments at full development
    • The Philippines recorded US$7.8bn in FDI in 2025
  • It is also projected to generate up to 130,000 to 190,000 direct jobs and 500,000 to 800,000 indirect jobs
  • Exports could hit up to US$200bn
  • The 1,600-hectare site in New Clark City has an initial investment target of US$10bn
  • Timetable
    • 2026 - Negotiations with prospective partners
    • 2027 - Planning
    • 2028 - Start of construction
  • Participation in the project has expanded to more than 30 countries from fewer than 15 in April
  • Pax Silica will integrate semiconductor design, fabrication and advanced packaging, artificial intelligence computing, critical mineral processing, energy and data infrastructure, research and development (R&D), logistics, offices, housing, and commercial facilities as part of the Luzon Economic Corridor
  • The project seeks to move the Philippines up the manufacturing value chain by processing critical minerals domestically instead of exporting raw materials
  • The industrial hub is expected to require about three gigawatts of power at full development
    • A 500MW solar project with Saudi Arabia’s ACWA Power is under development
    • BCDA is also evaluating a proposal from an American company to develop a dedicated LNG plant and pipeline linking Subic and Clark
  • For water supply, the project will rely on surface water harvesting, storage, treatment, and recycling instead of groundwater extraction
    • The initial system is designed to supply 120 million liters per day, with capacity expandable to 300 million liters per day
