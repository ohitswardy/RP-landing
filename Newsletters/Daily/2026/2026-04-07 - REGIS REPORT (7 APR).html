REGIS REPORT

07 April 2026

The government statistics office just declared that CPI inflation for the month of March was 4.1%. This is a sharp acceleration from the Feb inflation figure of 2.4% and is also higher than the Street estimate of 3.8%.

 

This latest data print is not just further confirmation that there will be no more rate cuts from the Bangko Sentral (BSP) but also that consumers are facing an environment that is only starting to get more challenging. The initial wave from the direct impact of oil price hikes is visible from the CPI data (i.e. on transport costs, etc.) and the secondary wave on utilities and others (especially food) should be felt in succeeding months.

 

 

+++

 

The power sector regulator has finalized the temporary measures governing the electricity spot market to soften the impact of the energy crisis. The ERC will impose a modified administered price for coal plants and also remove some plants (e.g. gas-fired or oil-fired plants) in the determination of spot prices. These steps should help temper but not halt the increases that end-users will face over the next few months.

 

+++

 

Meanwhile, lawmakers have submitted a myriad of proposals in order to mitigate the effects of the ongoing energy crisis. Broadly, these all involve subsidies in some form and they all face the same issue: funding. While there’s a proposal for a tax on luxury goods or a wealth tax to help defray these costs, we are doubtful these can be imposed and collected.

 

+++

 

DMCI (DMC PM) and its Japanese partner secured another contract job for the Metro Manila Subway, this time worth P16bn.

 

+++

 

Online gaming firm Philweb (WEB PM) announced the signing of an agreement with Travellers, the gaming arm of Alliance Global (AGI PM), to provide the latter with technical support for its online gaming operations. Recall that WEB also signed agreements with other gaming firms recently, including Okada and Hann Resorts.

MACRO
Lawmakers file measures to mitigate impact of soaring fuel prices
  • Senators and congressmen have proposed measures to address the high prices of fuel due to the conflict in the Middle East by providing financial aid to vulnerable sectors and imposing other relevant measures
    • House Ways and Means Committee Chairperson Miro Quimbo was confident that the gov't would not need to incur additional debt to fund Bayanihan 3 due to improved revenue collections as of the first quarter
      • He stated that the gov't has enough cash as well as alternative funding sources such as unprogrammed appropriations to sustain assistance programs
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. stated that he would approve recommendations of the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) if the proposals would be beneficial to the Filipino people
  • The Bayanihan 3 bill currently has several versions in both the Senate and the House of Representatives
    • Senator Loren Legarda's version seeks to grant financial aid to those vulnerable which will be funded by higher taxes on the wealthy, luxury goods, and alcohol
      • Legarda's bill also proposes to grant the President authority, through the appropriate agencies, to impose temporary price controls, rationalize distribution margins, among others
    • Senator Sherwin Gatchalian eyes for the gov't to allot P98-P406bn to provide assistance and subsidies to "qualified beneficiaries" such as those working in agriculture and transportation as well as the bottom 30% income households
    • House Ways and Means Committee Vice Chairperson Rolando Valeriano proposes a measure to provide wage support of at least P3,000 for minimum wage earners and interest-free loans for MSMEs
      • Valeriano also seeks to provide tax credits equivalent to the incremental higher cost of fuel and cargo rates for retailers, wholesale firms, and freight forwarders
POWER
ERC finalizes P6/kwh administered price for coal-fired plants
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) approved a proposal to cap the price (a.k.a. “Modified Administered Price”) for coal-fired plants at P6/kwh in the spot market
  • Recall that on March 26, the ERC suspended the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) to address supply risks and price volatility triggered by the Middle East conflict
    • The ERC has implemented technology-specific pricing and managed dispatch, with priority given to renewables.
  • The suspension should help temper some of the massive projected price hikes for consumers in the coming months and correspondingly reduce what could have been windfall profits for some generation companies
CONGLOS
DMCI, Japanese partner bag another subway contract
  • The Department of Transportation (DoTr) awarded Contract Package (CP) 109 to the joint venture of DMCI and Japan-based Taisei Corp
    • CP 109 amounts to P16bn (ex-VAT) and involves the connection of the subway to the Ninoy Aquino International Airport
    • We suspect DMCI’s stake in the project will be <50%
    • DMCI’s construction arm’s order book stood at P39bn in end-December 2025
  • The only remaining subway contract yet to be awarded is CP 108, covering the segment in Lawton and Senate-DepEd, located in Taguig City
  • As of February, the Metro Manila subway project reached a completion rate of 27%, moving the completion target to 2032
    • Government expects to complete ROW acquisition this year; ROW procured so far is at 82%

Government says Mitsubishi to locally manufacture country’s first hybrid EV
  • The Department of Finance said that Mitsubishi Motors Corp. (MMC) plans to manufacture hybrid electric vehicles at its existing plant in Santa Rosa, Laguna
    • According to Finance Secretary Frederick Go, following a meeting of MMC President and CEO Takao Kato and President Marcos Jr. yesterday
    • MMC is also exploring the possibility of exporting vehicles produced from its Laguna plant
  • No further details were disclosed regarding the timeline, specific model, and amount of the proposed investment
