REGIS REPORT

25 March 2026

President Marcos signed an Executive Order yesterday, declaring a “state of national energy emergency” amidst the global energy crisis sparked by the US-Israeli attack on Iran.

 

Apart from the creation of yet-another committee, this time to coordinate action relating to this issue, the government will be implementing measures to mitigate the impact of the crisis. These include fuel conservation programs as well as measures against hoarding / profiteering.  According to the Dept of Energy (DOE), the country has ~45 days of oil inventory, with levels lowest for cooking gas (LPG).

 

The government is already directing subsidies to specific sectors (public utility vehicle drivers, etc). A lawmaker is also now proposing shorter operating hours for shopping malls to conserve energy.

 

The DOE is trying to assure the public by saying that there is ample power despite the crisis. We note, however, that around 25% of power generated for Luzon is from natural gas and the indigenous Malampaya gas field is only able to supply around 1/3rd of that. There is a chance, in our view, that the Luzon grid will see power cuts during the peak season in April/May should physical LNG supply become unavailable.

MACRO
President Marcos declares state of national energy emergency
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. through Executive Order (EO) 110 has declared a "state of national energy emergency" amidst significant concern on the price and supply of fuel/energy
    • The national energy emergency declaration will remain effective for one year from issuance unless extended or lifted by the President
  • The President has established the "Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food and Transport (UPLIFT)" committee in order to ensure a coordinated, whole-of-government response framework approach to the crisis
    • The committee is chaired by the President with the various gov't agencies as members in order to oversee and coordinate energy crisis mitigation measures and intiatives
  • Measures include fuel and energy optimization plans, action against hoarding, profiteering, and supply manipulation, as well as ensuring the stability and adequacy of domestic fuel and energy supply through the Philippine National Oil Company (PNOC)
  • The EO also directs the departments/agencies to monitor key inputs and implement necessary response measures under their scope (i.e., cash relief, food supply, essential goods price monitoring, etc.)
  • Ongoing discussions mentioned was the adjustment of the 12% VAT on  fuel with Energy Undersecretary Felix Fuentebella citing that they are leaning towards an adjustment of the tax rate rather than an outright suspension
    • Senator Bam Aquino stated that the upper chamber is willing to work overtime to pass urgent tax reforms if economic managers give the green light
    • Dept. of Economy, Planning and Development (DepDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan, however, warned of a possible economic slowdown if both excise tax and VAT on fuel are removed
MACRO
DOTr and DSWD initiates nationwide rollout of fuel subsidies for PUV operators, drivers
  • The Dept. of Transportation (DOTr) and the Dept. of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) has started to distribute fuel subsidies to public utility vehicle (PUV) drivers and operators
  • DOTr Secretary Giovanni Lopez stated that the department will use its P2.5bn fuel subsidy allocation from the 2025 national budget
    • Depending on vehicle type, PUV operators are expected to receive amounts varying from as low as P1,500 to P10,000, while PUV drivers will receive P1,500 to P5,000 each
  • The DSWD was said to focus on providing P5,000 in cash relief to TNVS, jeepney, service delivery, and motorcycle taxi drivers

Lawmaker proposes shorter mall hours to conserve power
  • In House Resolution 893, Davao Oriental Representative Cheeno Almario urged the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Department of Energy (DOE) to study a proposal to reduce mall operating hours in order to limit power consumption 
  • Malls as % of EBIT

    • SMPH – c. 70%
  • It also comprises about half of RCR’s leasable area and AUM

POWER
Key takeaways from meeting with CREC
  • Key developer in Philippine RE sector. Citicore Renewable Energy Corp (CREC PM) is one of the key proponents of solar and wind energy in the country with 468MWdc of attributable RE capacity today. A significant expansion in capacity this year will make it the biggest operator of solar farms domestically by the end of the year. CREC is controlled by Edgar Saavedra, the principal behind construction firm Megawide (MWIDE PM).

 

  • Poised to grow attributable capacity more than sixfold to ~3.6GW by year-end.  CREC is on course to deliver 1.1GW of incremental solar capacity within 1Q26 and an additional 2GW by year-end, substantially expanding its existing attributable solar capacity of 468MW today.  Although the timetable for these projects has been pushed back by roughly a year, management expressed confidence in delivering its pipeline, having secured the required land and addressed prior transmission constraints through the installation of battery storage systems. 

 

  • Pertamina is strategic partner. Indonesian state-owned PT Pertamina Power acquired 2.2bn shares at P3/sh in June 2025.  This is equivalent to 20% of CREC’s outstanding shares and gives Pertamina one (1) board seat in the company’s 7-member board. 

 

  • Projects are largely contracted, execution risks manageable. CREC’s projects are largely backed by contracts under GEA-2 and GEA-4, which have set prices of ~P4.4/kwh for ground-mounted solar and P5.4/kwh for Solar+BESS technologies. The company aims to add 1GW of new capacity annually, which management believes is achievable given its engineering capabilities and clustering strategy, enabling expansions within existing project sites.    

 

  • Aggressive expansion to require new capital; asset infusions into REIT vehicle a funding source. CREC is earmarking US$2bn (~P119bn) in capex for 2026 to support the 2GW incremental capacity slated for energization in the latter part of this year. With cash at P14bn and net debt-to-equity ratio at 1.2x as of end-September 2025, the company will require additional funds for the equity portion of its capex (~P36bn). The most immediate source of funding will be a potential injection of assets into its REIT vehicle, Citicore REIT (CREIT PM, Market cap: P22.6bn) and the sale of some of its shares there. Beyond 2026, the company aims to add 1GW of new capacity annually, a target that will necessitate even greater capital outlays.
