REGIS REPORT

10 August 2026

The GDP numbers reported last Friday were well below expectations of both the market and policy makers. GDP growth for 2Q came in at only 2.3%, a further deceleration from the 2.9% in 1Q. Still the key drag was capital formation (i.e. investment). Capital formation contracted further, shrinking 9.2% in the quarter as government construction dropped 32%. Clearly, the effects of last year’s corruption revelations are still very much present.

 

The 1H GDP growth number stands at 2.6%. We should see plenty of downward revisions for the macro estimates in the coming weeks.

The Senate has twenty-four (24) priority bills in its in-tray for the next twelve months. Among the most significant are the tax reform bill “PROGRESS”, the Anti-Political Dynasty Bill, Party-List System Reform Bill, and the Amended Power Reform Act Bill (EPIRA Amendments).

 

The Senate, however, is currently preoccupied with the impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte. This raises the likelihood that many of these priority bills will not be passed on time (if at all).

 

+++

 

President Marcos asked members of Congress to suspend the implementation of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA), which is slated to take full effect in 2028. The RPVARA could lead to higher real property taxes since it will require the “Schedule of Market Values” (from which real property taxes are based) to be updated by the national government. Thus, the possible suspension of RVARA could benefit property developers by slowing down the increases in real property taxes in the coming years.

It is likely, in our view, that DHI will also acquire the AT stake held by SM Investments (SM PM) in order to streamline the SM group’s ownership of mining interests.

CONGLOS
DMCI 2Q26 profit up 60%; coal mining the sole laggard
  • DMCI 1H26 profit up 25% to P11.3bn. DMCI reported its core profit rose 60% YoY to P6.42bn in 2Q26, with profit improvements across most segments, except for the coal mining unit of Semirara (SCC PM, see results comment here). This brought 1H26 core profit up 25% YoY to P11.29bn, trending ahead consensus’ FY26E of P16bn.

 

  • Property unit reservation sales drop 5% QoQ. Profit contributions from the property unit rose 55% YoY to P1bn in 2Q26, driven by higher revenue bookings from sales secured during the pandemic and lower revenue reversals. Cancellations surged, leading to a 5% QoQ drop in 2Q26 net reservation sales to P6bn. On a YoY basis, reservations sales still rose 28% YoY in the quarter due to higher RFO sales, bringing 1H26 reservation sales to P12bn, up 3% YoY.

 

  • Nickel mining 2Q profits up 4x. With production at its new Palawan mine now in full swing, the nickel unit saw profits surge 194% QoQ and 276% YoY to P1.3bn in 2Q. The strong performance came despite a 10% YoY decline in ASPs (flat QoQ), as the company continued to capitalize on higher nickel ore prices by selling its lower-grade inventory. After lifting 1H sales volumes by 78% YoY to 2mn MT, management is targeting 3mn MT (+58% YoY) this year, with a goal of reaching 9mn MT by 2029.

 

  • Cement unit’s EBITDA turnaround gains further traction in 2Q. Net losses from the cement unit narrowed further in 2Q26, as EBITDA surged 151% QoQ to P553mn, reversing the losses recorded last year. The strong improvement was driven by a 11% QoQ/10% YoY increase in ASPs, reflecting the company’s efforts to pass through higher energy costs, as well as efforts to sell more Ordinary Portland Cement, a product not offered by cement importers.  The latter is helping the company capture market share, with sales volumes up 10% YoY in 2Q and 24% YoY in 1H.

 

  • Higher completions of rail projects drive construction profits.  Despite lower revenues from building projects, construction profits improved 315% QoQ, 195% YoY on faster completions of rail projects under joint ventures.  The order book was flat QoQ (+18% YoY) to P43bn, as booked revenues were offset by an increase in change orders for the North-South Commuter Rail project. As long as right-of-way is delivered, management remains confident that the gains will be sustained, as these rail projects are largely insulated from labor and other input cost pressures.

 

MACRO
2Q26 GDP grows 2.3%, behind expectations
  • GDP growth was +2.3% in 2Q26, behind consensus expectations of 2.9% and decelerating further from +2.8% in 1Q26
    • This is the fourth consecutive quarter of deceleration in GDP growth
    • The 2.3% print is the lowest (ex-pandemic) since +1.8% back in 4Q09
  • The 1H26 average GDP growth of 2.6% is trailing behind the gov't's (3.5-4.5%) as well as consensus’ (3.9%) and REGIS' (3.8%) FY26 forecasts
    • GDP growth needs to average ~4.5% in 2H to hit 3.5% or average ~6.5% to reach 4.5%
  • The continued slowdown in GDP growth in 2Q26 vs 1Q26 stemmed from the continued decline in capital formation and still weak household consumption
    • One bright spot was net imports—net imports declined 10.7% YoY as exports (+12.2%) outpaced imports (+5.5%), narrowing the trade deficit and contributing 1.2ppt to 2Q's 2.3% GDP growth
      • Growth in exports was driven by electronics, particularly semiconductors
  • On household consumption (+2.8% from +3.0% in 1Q)
    • Private consumption grew 2.8%, slowest since 3Q10's +2.6% (ex-pandemic), and a tad slower than 1Q's 3.0%
    • A key driver for the slowdown relative to the previous quarter was the sharp deceleration in restaurants and hotels to -0.2% from +4.5% in 1Q which offset the modest rebound in food and non-alcoholic beverages (+2.4% from +0.4%)
  • On gov't consumption (+8.3% from +4.8% in 1Q)
    • Gov't spending recovered to +8.3% after recently hitting the previous low of +0.7% in 4Q
  • On capital formation (-9.2% from -3.1% in 1Q)
    • Despite the rise in gov't spending, gov't construction continued to deteriorate given the still sharp 32.4% decline from -31.5% the previous quarter
      • This is the fourth consecutive quarter of double digit contraction in gov't construction
    • Household construction contributed to the continued decline in capital formation as growth slowed to +0.8% from +4.4% the previous quarter
  • Breaking it down by sector, growth in agri (+2.7%) and the services sector (+4.5%) was offset by a decline in industrials (-2.4%) driven by subsector declines in mining (-9.8%) and construction (-13.9%)

PROPERTY
Marcos asks Congress to suspend implementation of property valuation law
  • During a Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting, President Marcos asked members of Congress to suspend the implementation of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA)
    • President Marcos signed the RPVARA into law in 2024
  • The RPVARA transfers the responsibility of updating the “Schedule of Market Values” (from which real property taxes are based) from Local Government Units (such as city governments) to the national government
  • While raising real property taxes would expand the coffers of a city government, it could also lead to the mayor (and other officials) losing the next election. As a result, local governments in many places are hesitant to regularly update their Schedule of Market Values. As a result, there are cities/municipalities that might not have updated it for over 10 years
  • RPVARA is slated to take full effect in 2028
