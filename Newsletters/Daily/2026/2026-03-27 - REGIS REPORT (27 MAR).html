The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) suspended the operation of the electricity spot market ahead of what’s anticipated to be a massive spike in prices over the next few weeks. In lieu of the usual market operations, the ERC will oversee dispatch and impose “administered pricing” with special emphasis on the oil-fired and natural gas-fired plants. This action should help temper some of the massive projected price hikes for consumers in the coming months and correspondingly reduce what could have been windfall profits for some generation companies.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) Monetary Board held an emergency off-cycle meeting which concluded with no adjustment in policy rates. The BSP, however, released updated forecasts for key macro variables. It’s now forecasting GDP growth of 4.4% (vs 4.6%) and average inflation of 5.1% (vs. 3.6%) for the year.

POWER
Energy regulator suspends electricity spot market
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission ordered the suspension of the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM), following the declaration of a state of national energy emergency
    • WESM trading was officially halted at 12:05 am on March 26
  • During the suspension period, the regulator will implement an administered price (AP) and will oversee dispatch, which will prioritize renewables
    • The ERC is consulting with stakeholders to finalize a Modified Administered Pricing Mechanism by April 1
    • The proposed framework introduces technology-specific pricing to reflect fuel costs. Coal plants may receive a fixed rate of P6/kwh, while natural gas plants will be paid based on contracted prices
  • The suspension of the spot market trading will create uncertainty on how buyers and sellers of power can transact and agree on pricing until the administered pricing mechanism is finalized
  • For some sellers of spot electricity, the suspension will remove the opportunity to make windfall profits
MACRO
BSP keeps rates unchanged at off-cycle meeting; revises GDP and inflation forecast
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) held its policy rate at 4.25% during an off-cycle meeting
    • This marks the first time the BSP held rates steady since Feb 2025, after six consecutive 25bp cuts
  • The central bank noted that raising the policy rate at this time would delay the economic recovery
    • BSP Governor Eli Remolona highlighted that the monetary policy would have limited effectiveness against supply-driven inflationary risks
    • However, he also stated that the BSP may hike rates if it can address demand-driven inflation from second-round effects of oil shocks
  • Remolona added that the central bank is planning regulatory relief measures for the local banking sector, particularly for lenders from the informal sector and low-income businesses
    • He elaborated the plan is similar to what was done during the pandemic (Bayanihan 2, to Recover as One Act)—to have a standardized restructuring framework on default loans, postponing some payments depending on the sector
  • The second out of six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Apr 23
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect rates to hold steady at 4.25%
  • The central bank's GDP forecast for 2026 was trimmed to 4.4% from 4.6% but left its forecast for 2027 unchanged at 5.9%
    • Consensus and Regis expectations for 26/27E GDP growth are at 5.0/5.6% and 4.5/5.7%, respecitvely
  • The BSP raised its inflation forecast for 26/27E to 5.1% from 3.6% and 3.8% from 3.2%, respectively
    • The inflation forecast assumes global crude oil prices for 26/27E will average US$85/bbl and US$76/bbl
    • Consensus and Regis expects 26/27E inflation to average 2.9%/3.0% and 4.0%/3.0%

POWER
Prime Energy targets to deliver new gas from Malampaya expansion by 4Q26
  • Prime Energy, power-arm of Enrique Razon, stated that new gas from its US$893mn Malampaya Phase 4 expansion is expected to start flowing by 4Q26
    • The recent successful drilling and completion of Camago-3 is estimated to contain recoverable gas volumes ~2.5x larger than the Malampaya East-1 discovery announced in January, effectively doubling remaining reserves
      • Camago-3's flow rate was said to be up to 60mn standard cubic feet of gas per day
    • The two wells are expected to extend the gas field's operating life by around six years
  • Prime Energy estimates Malampaya gas to cost roughly ~P4.8/kWh vs P10.3/kWh for imported LNG
  • Additional drilling projects are said to be underway, with the Bagong Pag-asa (north of Malampaya) exploration and drilling said to be in progress

CONGLOS
Toyota Motor Philippines sees EV models taking 20% of 2026 sales
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) expects the share of electrified vehicles (EV) to account for 20% of its sales in 2026, up from just 8.5% in 2025
  • TMP introduced two new electrified variants (Toyota Urban Cruiser BEV and the Toyota Rav4 HEV) in March and has plans to introduce more models throughout the year:
    • Land Cruiser 300 hybrid EV
    • Land Cruiser FJ
    • Hilux BEV
    • Lexus ES BEV
    • Lexus RX PHEV
  • TMP is expecting retail sales to rise to a max of +5% in 2026, which would translate to ~229k-241k units
    • We are forecasting sales volumes of 247k units in 2026, up 7% from last year’s sales of 229k units
    • Every 5% reduction in our sales volume forecast will cut TMP’s profit estimate by 2% and GTCAP’s profit by <1%
    • We are also forecasting a GPM of 13% this year; Every 100bps reduction in GPM will cut TMP’s profit by 15% and GTCAP’s profit by 4%
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 23% of FY26E profit
    • GTCAP trades at a 54% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,120/sh and at 3.6x PER
