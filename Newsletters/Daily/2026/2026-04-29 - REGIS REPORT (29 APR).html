The 1Q26 earnings of SM Prime (SMPH PM) was flat YoY at P11.66bn, trailing REGIS and consensus forecasts, as the leasing and residential segments moved in opposite directions. Every leasing segment saw an increase in revenue and an expansion in margins. In contrast, the residential business saw a decline in revenue and compression of margins.

 

1Q26 mall operations remained healthy, as EBIT margin expanded 1.8ppt YoY and SSSG stayed at 7%. To our surprise, management said that despite the Middle East conflict, April figures “look encouraging” and margins might continue to expand.

 

On the other hand, residential EBIT fell 31% QoQ and 37% YoY, as revenue and margins both shrank on a QoQ and YoY basis. The residential EBIT margin contracted for the fifth consecutive quarter. We suspect this is likely as a result of a jump in sales cancellations, which, if true, would not be a good sign for the state of the residential property market.

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital payments jump 50% in March

Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS ) in March jumped 50.1% YoY to P2,767bn a new all-time high, accelerating sharply from the 42.3% pace in February. For the 3M26, total payments were up 45% YoY, faster than the 42% growth in FY2025.

 

The surge was driven by PesoNet – the rail for larger, batched transactions – which posted P1,415bn in payments, up 39% YoY, also a record high, much faster than the 26.0% clip the previous month. That took first quarter payments up 31% YoY matching the pace of FY25.

 

Transactions over InstaPay – for real-time smaller-ticket payments – continued to surge to P1,352bn, up 64% YoY, matching the February growth rate. For 3M26, InstaPay payments were up 64% to P3,761bn, up from 57% in FY2025. Notably, the volume of payments were still up 255% YoY, indicating continued extremely rapid adoption of digital payments for everyday needs.

 

The accelerating pace of digitalization is good news for e-wallets, e-commerce and digital lending platforms and digibanks. It also points to the need for traditional banks to step up their digital offerings, in the face of intensifying competition from alternative platforms.
