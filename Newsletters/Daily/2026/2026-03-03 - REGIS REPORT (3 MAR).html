Here’s our take on the results of a few corporates post- their FY25 earnings calls. The results of SM Investments (SM PM) saw a lift from the retail business in 4Q. For BDO (BDO PM) and Security Bank (SECB PM), there were interesting insights into the near- and medium-term outlook for each bank.

 

  • BDO Unibank (BDO PM) (+) – The bank noted that loan demand was surprisingly strong in Jan/Feb. Large and mid-sized corporates were finally drawing on lines, having pushed back capex and inventory build-up in 2H25 due to negative sentiment over the flood control corruption scandal. To the downside, loan and fee pricing competition is intensifying, and IT spend could stay elevated for longer (3 years). These were reflected in their FY25 NPAT, where PPOP fell a tad short, but was supported by lower provision and tax expenses. The stock is back down to 1.0x P/bk on our 2026E numbers. Buy.

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) - SECB’s new CEO, Mr Victor Lee, gave a sneak preview of the new direction he wants to take the bank. This includes ratcheting down loan growth to single-digits, particularly for low-margin corporate loans, and focus on consumer loans and fees to lower capital consumption. These, together with cost management, should help get the bank back to double-digit ROEs in 2-3 years. But part of that journey will include a re-focusing of the consumer loan book. FY25 PPOP was up an impressive 25% YoY, but provisions on credit cards surged again in 4Q, and will likely keep dragging profit into 2026. The full 3-5 year plan is set to be unveiled in June/July. Read more below.

 

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – Profits for FY25 rose 10% to P90.5bn, on modest beats from its key subsidiaries and a rebound in the 4Q trading operations of the Retail segment. Focusing on the latter, we note an improvement, albeit modest, in the SSSG of the various retail businesses in the last quarter, despite expectations of a slowdown due to the poor 4Q GDP print.

The 24% growth in revenue was thanks to the acquisition of 157k sqm of offices in 4Q24. DPS growth was more muted, at just 1%. BANKS
SECB FY25 P11.7bn, behind, provisions drag - new CEO, new strategy?

SECB FY25 NPAT P11.6bn, up 3% YoY, behind REGIS and consensus (both at P11.9bn), as provision expense again dragged profits. Loan growth decelerated to just 3% YoY. Consumer loans grew 14% YoY (vs +24% YoY in 3Q). NIMs widened 6bps QoQ despite a 5bps QoQ decline in loan yields as funding costs dropped 16bps QoQ. Provisions surged 88% YoY to P12.5bn (vs our P10bn forecast), with 4Q alone at P3.9bn, as the bank did big write-offs, NPL ratio down to 2.85% from 3.02% in 3Q25.

 

Some highlights:

  • Loan growth slowed sharply to 2.8% YoY, from 7.9% in 3Q and 25.9% in 2024. Consumer loans grew 14% (slower than the +24% in 3Q) and now account for 32% of total loans. Corporate loans contracted 4% YoY. The bank said it was focusing on profitability over growth. It is also tightening its credit card business, reining in the risk profile.
  • NIMs expanded 6bps QoQ to 4.82%. Lower interest rates pushed loan yields down 5bps QoQ. However, funding costs fell even faster, down 16bps QoQ given SECB’s reliance on wholesale funding (CASA Ratio 49%). The larger consumer loan book also helped slow the decline in loan yields.
  • The bank booked P2.8bn in trading/fx gains, with 4Q alone at P1.0bn
  • Fee incomes were flat YoY, but recall that 2024 included a chunky payment from SECB’s bancassurance partner. Fees were up 12% YoY in 4Q25. Meanwhile, equity income – largely from Home Credit Phils, starting 2Q25 – came in at P746mn (in line) for just 8 months (acquisition finalized in May).
  • Opex grew 20% YoY on sustained branch expansion (+31 to 384) and IT investments.
  • PPOP jumped 25% YoY, beating our forecasts.
  • Provision expense surged 88% YoY to P12.5bn, much higher than our P10.0bn forecast, with 4Q25 alone at P3.9bn. Credit costs jumped to 223bps in 4Q alone, despite management’s previous guidance of a “peak” in 3Q25. The high provision expense included large write-offs, which explained the drop in NPL ratio to 2.85% from 3.02% in 3Q25. NPL Cover was steady at 86%. As mentioned above, the bank is repositioning its credit card portfolio. They admit that “called the peak too early”, and are now looking at a peak in 1Q26, with FY26E credit cost still elevated at 190bps. Management are already seeing the fruit of the shift, with new NPL formation tapering off.

 

New CEO, new strategy? Low loan growth, higher ROEs

  • New CEO Mr Victor Lee and team discussed the early stages of a shift in strategy. A new 3-5 year roadmap will be unveiled in early 2H26.
  • Focus on profitability, not growth. For 2026E, management is looking at low single-digit loan growth. Plan is to off-load corporate loans, and grow consumer/MSME loans, though mindful of risk parameters
  • Target ROE of 10%++ in 2 years
    • Bring cost to income ratio down from current 60% to mid-50s
      • Branch expansion to moderate
      • IT spend to taper off
    • Improve NIMs, and fee incomes
    • Bring down credit costs
  • With slower loan growth, and higher profitability, aim is to see CARs rise while improving ROE

POLITICS
House rules 3rd, 4th Impeachment complaints against VP Duterte sufficient in form
  • The House Committee on Justice has ruled that the third and fourth impeachment complaints filed against Vice President Sara Duterte were "sufficient in form"
    • This follows as the first impeachment complaint was deemed noncompliant with the one-year bar rule and the second complaint was withdrawn voluntarily in order "to expedite the discussions"
  • VP Duterte faces impeachment complaints based on allegations of misuse of confidential funds, threats against ranking officials, bribery, violation of the Constitution
  • Recall that the committee was recently given 60 days to determine if the complaints are sufficient in form and in substance
    • The committee will come up with a recommendation whether to approve or dismiss the complaints
    • The House would then need a one-third vote to approve and elevate the impeachment complaint against Duterte to the Senate

Department of Justice subpoenas members of the Villar family
  • The DOJ filed subpoenas to the members of the Villar family in connection with the criminal complaint filed by the Securities and Exchange Commission against Villar Land Holdings Corporation (VLC PM) as well as its officers for market manipulation, insider trading and misleading disclosures
  • In January 2026,  the SEC filed the complaint which stated that the respondents "distorted the company's share prices and misled the investing public."
  • Villar Land said that it is “…confident that an impartial panel will find that no violation of law was committed…”
