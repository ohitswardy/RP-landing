23 January 2026

Gaming agency PAGCOR chairman Alejandro Tengco revealed that gross gaming revenue from e-games (i.e. online gambling) was over P200bn last year (2025) from P156bn in 2024. That P200bn figure implies that 4Q e-gaming revenue was at least P40bn, which would be ~13% down QoQ from 3Q25. This is likely to be better than what the market anticipated in the aftermath of the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) ban on linking of e-wallets to gaming apps.

 

Meanwhile, Tengco also said he is working on a paper to convince the BSP to lift that ban and again allow e-wallets to link directly to gaming apps. We are, however, a bit doubtful about the BSP agreeing to such a request from PAGCOR.

Over 26,000 BYD vehicles were sold in the Philippines in 2025, more than 5x the number in 2024 (=4.8k). The figure suggests BYD had a unit market share of ~6% for the year, from ~1% the year before. BYD’s rapid growth has made it a key player in the auto market, as it is now almost certainly the third largest brand. AC Mobility’s distributorship contract renewal (for five years from Jan 2026), revealed yesterday, is thus a significant development for Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) efforts in the auto/EV industry.

 

+++

 

We upgraded our rating on Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) to BUY from Hold in a new report, reflecting our increased confidence that strong nickel ore prices will be sustained. Indonesia, the world’s largest producer of nickel, continued to tighten regulations, cutting its annual ore production quota to 250-265mn MT this year, which is well below its demand of ~300mn MT and benefitting Philippine nickel exporters. 

 

We thus raise our 25/26 profit estimates by 12%/47% and TP by 56% to P6.40/sh, after lifting ASP forecasts by 30%/49% to US$26/29/MT. Our ASP assumptions remain conservative, forecasting benchmark LME nickel prices of US$15–16.5k over FY26E–FY27E, below current spot levels of US$17.6k. 

 

Despite outperforming the PCOMP by 48% over the past year, the stock remains undemanding at 8x FY26E PER and offers an attractive 7% dividend yield.

GAMING
GGR of e-games hits over P200bn in 2025
  • In an interview with Inside Asian Gaming, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the e-games sector produced more than P200bn in gross gaming revenue in 2025
    • This would imply growth of at least 29% from the P156bn generated in 2024
  • This suggests that 4Q25 GGR was at least P40bn
    • This means that the QoQ decline was less than 13% (from the P45.7bn GGR in 3Q25)
  • Mr. Tengco also said that PAGCOR was preparing to present a position paper to the BSP regarding the re-linking of e-wallets to online gambling platforms
  • The BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms (beginning August 17)
  • As a result, 3Q25 GGR of e-games fell 28% QoQ (up 14% YoY) to P45.7bn
  • September e-games GGR was only P12.2bn, a 45% drop from June 2025
  • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is the market leader in e-games, with a 41% market share (as of 3Q25)
  • PLUS is trading at 4.6X FY26E P/E (based on consensus forecast)

MACRO
PH gov't cuts 2026 revenue target
  • The gov't has cut its revenue target for this year after reducing its economic growth forecasts given its expected drag on collections and overall fiscal performance
  • The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) lowered the 2026 revenue target to P4.82trn from P4.98trn previously
    • Recall that the DBCC trimmed the GDP growth targets for 2026 to 5-6% and 2027 to 5.5-6.5% from 6-7% initially
  • The detailed revenue breakdown revisions are as follows:
    • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) is now expected to collect P3.43trn (+11% YoY) vs P3.58trn previously (+15% YoY)
      • Note that the BIR will resume letters of authority [LoA] issuances (tax audits) within 1Q26 given its key role in revenue collection once planned digitalization and safeguard reforms are finalized
        • Issuance of LoAs were suspended back in Nov 2025 due to complaints of extortion and corruption
    • The Bureau of Customs (BOC) target was revised to P1.00trn (+7% YoY) from P1.01trn (+8% YoY)
    • Non-tax revenues were however, revised upwards to P350bn from P249bn
      • This was raised in order to offset lower tax revenues, non-tax revenues include privatization proceeds, remittances from Gov't-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GOCCs), fees/charges and grants
