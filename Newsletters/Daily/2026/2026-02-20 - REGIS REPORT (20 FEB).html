REGIS REPORT

20 February 2026

As widely expected, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) reduced its policy rate by 25bp to 4.25% -- the lowest since Aug 2022. BSP Governor Eli Remolona cited weakness in the GDP growth numbers as a factor as the BSP cut its GDP growth forecast to 4.6% for FY26 (5.4%) and 5.9% for FY27 (from 6.3%).

 

More interestingly, the BSP raised inflation forecast to 3.6% (vs 3.2%) for FY26 and 3.2% (vs. 3.0%). The FY26 forecast is now near the high end of the BSP’s inflation target range (2 to 4%). We would interpret this as a signal that CPI inflation is likely to be a constraint against further cuts based on current visible data points.

 

Note as well that the differential between policy rates of the BSP and the US Fed is now at its narrowest.

In a press release, AREIT (AREIT PM) reported that FY25 net profit rose 28% to P9.4bn, in line with REGIS and slightly ahead of consensus forecast. The increase in profit was largely due to the full-year contribution of the P30bn worth of assets acquired in 2024 as well as contribution from the P21bn worth of assets acquired in 3Q25. Since both of these tranches were acquired via share-for-property swaps, dividends per share paid in 2025 grew by a more modest 5% to P2.37/share. The company says it will pay P0.62/share in dividends in 1Q26, flat QoQ and up 7% YoY.

 

In FY26, the company plans to expand its AUM by 14% by acquiring P19.5bn worth of assets from its sponsor, Ayala Land (ALI PM), in a share-for-property swap.

MACRO
BSP cuts rates further to 4.25%; lowers GDP growth forecasts for 2026 and 2027
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.25%, the lowest since Aug 2022
    • This is the sixth consecutive 25bp cut (150bp total) since the decision to resume easing back in Apr 2025
    • The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 225bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024
  • In a press briefing, BSP Governor Eli Remolona emphasized that rationale for the cut was because softer than expected growth alongside a slowdown in investments
    • Recall that 4Q25 GDP growth was 3.0%, bringing FY25 growth to 4.4%, much lower than the BSP's expectations of 3.8% growth in 4Q and 4.6% in FY25
  • Remolona highlighted weak domestic demand as the primary factor behind disappointing economic growth
    • He cited that latest indictors imply a recovery in 2H26 but growth is dependent on "how quickly confidence recovers"
  • The central bank revised its GDP growth forecast for 2026 and 2027 to 4.6% and 5.9% from 5.4% and 6.3%, respectively
  • The inflation outlook was said to remain manageable but the BSP revised its inflation outlook citing supply-side factors (electricity, oil, rice prices)
    • The BSP now forecasts inflation for 2026 and 2027 to be 3.6% and 3.2% from 3.2% and 3.0%, respectively
  • The second out of the six Monetary Board meetings will be held on Apr 23
    • For 2026, consensus expectations for inflation/GDP growth/rate cuts are 2.7%/5.0%/no cut
      • Regis expectations are 3.0%/5.5%/no cut, respectively
    • For 2027, consensus expectations for inflation/GDP growth/rate cuts are 3.0%/5.8%/no cut
      • Regis expectations are 3.0%/5.7%/no cut, respectively

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital payments surge 42% YoY in FY2025; Jan 2026 still +43%

Digital transactions done over the National Retail Payment System (NRPS) jumped to P24,745bn ($426.6bn), up 42% YoY in 2025. We note that this was an acceleration over the 35% YoY growth posted in 2024, which itself was faster than the 29% in 2023. For December alone, the growth was 49.5% YoY to P2,691bn,  new monthly record-high. Meanwhile, January 2026 was still up 43.1% YoY to P2,581bn though it eased slightly MoM (given the seasonal Dec peak).

 

For larger transactions over PesoNet, FY25 hit P13,191bn, up 31% YoY, slightly faster than the 29% pace in 2024 and 22% in 2023, with December 2025 posting P1,346bn, up 36.0% YoY. For January 2026, PesoNet grew 27.1% to P1,338bn.

 

More crucially (perhaps), real-time smaller-ticket (up to P50,000 or about $862) over InstaPay jumped 57% YoY in FY25 to P11,554bn. This was significantly faster than the 46% growth in 2024 and 42% in 2023. December 2026 alone surged 66% to P1,345bn, a new all time high. Note that the December InstaPay value was just a smidgen smaller than PesoNet. For January 2026, InstaPay growth was sustained at 65.5% YoY to P1,242bn.

 

Staggeringly, the volume of transactions over InstaPay continues to explode, up 350% YoY. This took the average ticket size down to P1,832 ($31.60), indicating continued adoption for everyday payments.

 

The rapid expansion of digital adoption lays the groundwork not only for payments, but also for financial inclusion opportunities for fintech, digi-/neo-banking and broader e-commerce.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MINING
OceanaGold Philippines’ FY25 profit up 152% YoY to US$76.5mn
  • OceanaGold Philippines (OGP PM) reported a net profit of US$76.5mn in FY25, up 154% YoY and exceeding consensus’ estimates of US$62.5mn
    • In 4Q25 alone, profits rose 25x YoY (flat QoQ) to US$27.5mn on the back of a 62%/29% increases in gold/copper prices, which offset a slight dips (-1%) in gold/copper sales volumes
    • For the full-year, ongoing underground flooding issues weighed on gold production but elevated gold/copper prices mitigated the impact
  • Net cash position stood at US$84mn in end-2025, after paying dividends of US$112mnn
  • OGP trades at 17x FY26E consensus PER (vs. global peer average of 19x / 8x PER for APX PM / 14x PER for PX PM)
MACRO
BOP posts US$373mn deficit in Jan; GIR settles at US$112.6bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Jan posted a deficit of US$373mn
    • Narrower than both the US$827mn deficit in Dec and the US$4.08bn deficit in Jan 2025
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP)
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Jan stood at US$112.6bn, 4% higher YoY and up from the US$110.8bn in end-Dec
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • This is higher than the BSP's forecast of $109bn for 2025
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn
