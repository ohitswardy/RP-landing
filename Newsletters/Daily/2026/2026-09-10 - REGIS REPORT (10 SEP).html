REGIS REPORT

10 September 2026

In a strange turn of events, the regional wage board of the National Capital Region (NCR) approved Wage Order No. 28, which will raise the current minimum daily wage by P60 (+9%) from P695 to P755 by the end of September.

 

Recall that Wage Order No. 27 was supposed to raise the NCR minimum wage in two tranches—+P60 effective 25 July 2026, and the remaining +P25 scheduled for 20 January 2027. However, its implementation was suspended due to a court order issuance of a TRO (Temporary Restraining Order) and a writ of preliminary injunction shortly before it was set to be implemented

 

Although the government still seeks to lift the injunction on Wage Order No. 27, Labor Secretary Tolentino clarified that the two wage orders would not lead to a cumulative increase. Instead, as previously intended, a P60 minimum daily wage hike would be implemented this year and a P25 minimum daily wage hike would begin in January next year, leading to a combined 12% wage hike.

 

This 12% wage would be the highest in over 20 years and is likely to lead to margin squeezes for many companies (especially those employing many low-skilled workers). Second and third-order effects on inflation are also probable consequences. The wage hike will also, however, have positive effects on consumption spending

 

The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) estimates the wage hike will directly benefit 1.1mn minimum wage workers and indirectly benefit another 1.9mn workers earnings slightly above the minimum wage.

BYD Cars Philippines, a unit of Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) automotive arm, announced that it doubled sales to 28,399 units in the first eight (8) months of 2026, already surpassing its full-year 2025 sales.  The rapid growth of the BYD brand has been buoyed by consumers’ shift toward electric vehicles (EVs) amid elevated fuel prices.  BYD has now become the country’s third-largest car brand, with an estimated ~9% market share as of 7M26, up from 3% in 2025 and virtually nothing in 2024. While AC’s auto arm, AC Mobility, is still loss-making, we see it as well-positioned for a turnaround, as it benefits from the growing consumer preference for EVs. 

 

The growing preference for EVs (inclusive of hybrids) is also evident at market leader, Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), which saw its own EV sales increase 23% YoY in 1H26, making it the second-largest EV brand with ~22% market share of EVs, behind BYD’s ~31% EV market share.  TMP’s expanding EV lineup has also helped it broadly maintain its dominant overall market share of ~45%.  TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for ~20% of the latter’s profits.

Excluding interbank loans and reverse repos, the banking sector’s NPL ratio declined 1bp MoM (down 4bps YoY) to 3.54% in July. Notably, the Past Due (PD) ratio fell by a more substantial 10bps MoM (down 13bps YoY) to 4.47%, as the manufacturing and construction sectors saw significant improvements MoM. On the other hand, credit cards continued to see steady deterioration, with NPL ratio up 6bps MoM to 5.34%, and PD ratios up 7bps to 7.85%.

BANKS
Asset quality improves in July (ex-IBL/RRP), but still wary of Credit Card #s

Headline numbers from the BSP showed asset quality deteriorating in July vs June, with reported Gross NPL Ratio of 3.35%, up from 3.29% in June. However, this was because the BSP’s “loan” denominator includes interbank loans (IBLs) and reverse repos (RRPs), which fluctuate wildly on a monthly basis. In July, the IBL+RRP account dropped 29% MoM from P1,307bn in June to P933mn. The P418bn monthly drop was equivalent to 2.5% of total loans (ex-IBL/RRP) in July.

 

To avoid the volatility, we look at asset quality ratios ex-IBL/RRPs. On this basis, asset quality improved MoM. NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) edged down 1bp MoM to 3.54% in July, from 3.55% in June. The NPL ratio was also down 4bps YoY. In nominal PHP terms, NPLs were flat MoM, while loans were up 0.2% MoM. Notably, Past Due (PD) ratio fell a more substantial 10bps MoM to 4.47%, from 4.57% the previous month, and 13bps from the 4.60% in July 2025. Restructured Loans (RLs) inched up 2bps MoM to 2.07%, but were down 12bps YoY. NPL Cover was flat MoM at 92.5%.

 

Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) was broadly stable, up 10.4% YoY, a shade slower than the 10.5% pace set in June.

 

By loan segment, loans for productive activities saw NPLs edge down 1bp MoM to 3.27%, while PD ratios dropped 9bps to 3.77%. Manufacturing saw steeper drops with NPL ratio down 5bps MoM to 3.96%, and PD ratio down a whopping 39bps to 4.05%. Encouragingly, Construction saw huge improvement in the PD ratios, down a full 100bps MoM to 15.50%, though these were still elevated, nearly 300bps higher YoY. Both the Trade and Real Estate (incl mortgages) sectors saw their PD and NPL ratios stabilize MoM. (See charts below.)

 

Consumer loans (ex-mortgages) NPLs edged up 5bps MoM to 5.49%, but PD ratios actually dropped 12bps to 8.66%. Credit cards continued to see steady deterioration, with NPL ratio up 6bps MoM to 5.34%, and PD ratios up 7bps to 7.85%.
