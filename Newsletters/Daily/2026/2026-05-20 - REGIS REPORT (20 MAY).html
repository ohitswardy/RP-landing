REGIS REPORT

20 May 2026

Dito CME (DITO PM), the major shareholder of Dito Telecommunity (Dito Tel), posted an attributable net loss of P6.8bn for 1Q26. Service revenues for the quarter were P5.7bn, up 24% YoY and 4% QoQ. Note Dito Tel’s service revenues include revenues from both fixed wireless and mobile services.

 

Dito Tel, we estimate, had a loss of over P17bn, with the loss on the translation of FX debt at over P9bn. With operations at just around EBITDA breakeven (after adjusting for leases), the company survives as a going concern on the forbearance of its creditors who hold close to P300bn of its liabilities.

 

+++

 

The government’s infrastructure spending is still down 20% in March. The consolation here is that it’s a big improvement over the first two months of the year. YT-March infrastructure spend was P236bn, 26% lower YoY.

 

+++

 

The country’s BOP recorded a deficit of US$2.1bn for the month of April and, drove the 2% drop in international reserves to US$104.3bn.

TELCOS
DITO reports wider net loss in 1Q26
  • Dito CME Holdings Corp. (DITO PM), parent company of Dito Telecommunity Corp. (Dito Tel), reported an attributable net loss of P6.8bn in 1Q26, wider than the P1.7bn loss for the same period last year
  • Revenues rose 24% YoY to P5.8bn
    • Service revenues amounted to P5.7bn (+4% QoQ), while non-service revenues were P143mn
    • We estimate Dito CME is just about breaking even on an EBITDA basis but is still posting massive losses after depreciation and financing charges
  • Dito Tel’s net loss for the quarter reached P17.7bn, with FX losses amounting to P9.7bn
  • DITO Tel's mobile subscribers for mobile grew by 460k to 16.63mn as of end-March from 16.17mn as of end-2025
    • Fixed wireless access subscribers rose by 70,000 to 470k from 400k as of end-2025
  • The company targets to be profitable by 2028
MACRO
Government infrastructure spending declines by 20% YoY in March; YTD infra spend down 26% YoY
  • Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 26% YoY to P236bn in 3M26
    • Derived infrastructure spending in March stood at P108bn, rebounding 68% MoM but still down 20% YoY
    • The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that the YoY decline was due to the "ongoing completion of carry-over projects" as well as the stricter validation process for billing
      • The DBM expects infra spending to recover in 2Q26 as spending ramps up and construction picks up during summer
  • The government is targeting infra spend of ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) this year, broadly flat from last year’s P1.32tn (~4.7% of GDP)

 

 

MACRO
BOP posts US$2.1bn deficit in April; GIR settles at US$104.3bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of April posted a deficit of US$2.1bn
    • Narrower than both the US$2.6bn deficit in the previous month of March and the US$2.6bn deficit in April 2025
    • This brings the 4M26 deficit to US$7.4bn, wider than the US$5.5bn deficit for the same period last year
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Apr stood at US$104.3bn, down 1% YoY and 2% lower MoM
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.9 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn
