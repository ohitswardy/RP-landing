REGIS REPORT

25 May 2026

The BSP issued two circulars late last week. The first will likely see a rush of liquidity into ICTSI stock, and the second paves the way for potential capital relief for banks.

 

BSP Circular 1234 raises the limit for UITFs’ equity exposure to a single entity from 15% to 20%. The local equity fund managers have been lobbying for this change specifically to address ICTSI (ICT PM), which we estimate is already more than 20% weight on the main PSEi Index. The new limit affects actively managed pure equity and balanced funds. For index trackers, there continues to be no limit, as they are meant to mimic the index regardless of weight. The new limits come into effect sometime after 5 June.

 

Meanwhile, in Circular 1235 allows the BSP to provide capital relief. The BSP has reallocated banks’ 6.0% minimum CET-1, reducing it to 4.5%, but increasing the counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB) from 0.0% to 1.5%. On paper, the net result is neutral, as it has no impact on the total CET-1 requirement of 10.5%-ish (4.5% minimum, plus 1.5% CCyB, plus 2.5% CCB, plus DSIB HLA). However, it does give the BSP the flexibility to “release” the CCyB – ie adjust it downwards – In times of economic stress, then build it back over time once the crisis is over. We had recently written that sharp upticks in bond yields could negatively impact banks’ capital ratios due to mark-to-market losses on their FVOCI books. The new Circular should ensure the banks can continue to lend, and not have capital issues further strangle economic growth.

MARKET
New BSP Circular amending single-stock exposures

The BSP issued Circular 1234 on 20 May 2026 (here) which tweaks the rules on unit investment trust fund (UITF) exposure to single entities (stocks).  The pertinent paragraph reads:

 

Exposure limits. The combined exposure of the UITF to any entity shall not exceed fifteen percent (15%) of the market value of the UITF: Provided That, a UITF invested, partially or substantially, in exchange-traded equity securities shall be subject to a twenty percent (20%) exposure limit to a single entity/issuer; Provided further That any exposure exceeding fifteen percent (15%). up to the applicable twenty percent (20%) limit, shall be solely in the form of investments in exchange-traded equity securities of the same entity/issuer. Provided finally That, in the case of an equity index-tracking UITF, the exposure limit shall be the actual benchmark weighting of the index component issuer, which may exceed twenty percent (20%).

 

As we read it, it looks like:

 

    1. The 15% single-entity limit still pertains to the entire UITF.
    2. But, if a UITF is invested in the stock market, then the limit is 20% per entity.
    3. Any exposure above 15% must be in listed stocks (ie not bonds or other instruments)
    4. If the UITF is explicitly an index-tracking fund, then the UITF may/must mirror the index, regardless of weight.

 

It’s items #2 and #3 I’m a bit fuzzy on. If our interpretation is right, then:

 

    • A bond-only UITF would be capped at 15% per issuer.
    • But a “balanced fund” with equity exposure can go up to 20%, as long as the excess over 15% is equity.
    • An equity-only fund can go up to 20%.

 

If this is correct, then there could be adjustments in the equity holdings of active funds. Many funds are now meaningfully underweight ICTSI, for example, because its weight in the PSEi has grown to 24%. This rule change could lead to inflows of local funds into ICTSI.

BANKS
Capital relief for banks? - BSP circular on Counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB)

The BSP issued Circular 1235 (press release here, and full pdf here), belatedly implementing rules on banks’ counter-cyclical buffers (CCyB), which could potentially free up 150bps of CET-1. The CCyB is meant to be extra capital banks build up in good times, but which is “releasable” during times of stress. The BSP implemented Basel III in 2014, formally introduced the CCyB in 2018, but set it at zero. Ironically, the 20 May circular does not increase the overall CET-1 requirement (as some media suggest). Instead, the BSP merely reallocated the existing CET-1 ratio – it increased the CCyB from 0.0% to 1.5%, but reduced the minimum CET-1 ratio from 6.0% to 4.5%. The Circular says that the new ratios shall take effect in one year for universal and commercial banks (UKBs), and 2 years for Digibanks.

 

On the face of it, this sounds like non-news. As seen in the table below, the changes ultimately have no impact on the total capital ratios of each bank. However, the devil is in the details. According to the Circular, the BSP can set the CCyB from 0.0% to 2.5%, and can adjust the ratio up or down “as conditions warrant”. Here the thing – any increase takes effect after one year, but any decrease is effective immediately. The new CCyB rules effectively give the BSP flexibility to reduce banks’ total CET-1 requirements in times of economic stress – like potentially now? – to allow bank lending to continue.

 

We had noted in a previous report that sharply higher interest rates could negatively impact banks’ capital ratios due to mark-to-market losses on their FVOCI books. For banks we cover, CET-1 ratios as of 1Q26 ranged from 12.0% to 13.4%, which seem comfortably above the 10.5%-ish (including DSIB HLA capital) total minimum. However, 10-yr bond yields have jumped another 80bps since end-March with the recent spike in inflation. That said, the vast majority of banks’ Treasury holdings are Philippine sovereign bonds. If the capital reduction is solely due to mark-to-market losses, the BSP may find it prudent to relax the CCyB, to prevent a further stifling of economic growth.

 

 

 

 

CONGLOS
Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 19% YoY in April; Toyota sales down 13% YoY
  • CAMPI reported that vehicle sales slumped 18.9% YoY to 27,225 units in April
    • Brings 4M26 car sales to 133k units, down 12% YoY
    • Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI
    • Cited oil price shock discouraging vehicle purchases
  • Sales of electric vehicles (including hybrids) jumped 288% YoY to 5,855 units
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 14,284 units in April, down 13% YoY
    • Brings TMP’s 4M26 sales to 66,205 units, down 8% YoY and trending below our forecast of 227k units (-1% YoY)
    • To reach our forecast, TMP will need to sell 20k units per month for the rest of the year
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP) and accounts for 22% of FY26E profit and 23% of NAV
    • GTCAP trades at a 57% discount to its market-based NAV of P1108/sh and at 3x PER

 

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s port in Manila still experiencing yard congestion
  • Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) said that it is experiencing longer container dwell times, with 41% of yard inventory remaining beyond the 5-day “free” storage period
    • It has called on shipping lines, customers, and consignees to expedite withdrawal of laden containers to ease higher-than-normal yard utilization rates
  • This development aligns with management’s earlier statement that yield per TEU continues to remain elevated in 2Q (see here)
  • We estimate MICT accounts for 19% of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 18% of EBITDA for FY26
    • Every 1% increase in our yield per TEU estimate of US$220 could increase EBITDA by 2% and profit by 4%
  • ICTSI trades at 12.7x FY26E EV/EBITDA, 22x PER (vs. peer average of 11x EBITDA/16x PER and historical average of 11x EBITDA/19x PER)

 

 

MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in April
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 16bps MoM to 20.1% (in April) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index was flat MoM at 23.6%
  • Net foreign selling amounted to US$209mn, from net foreign selling of US$231mn in March
  • Among the PSEi constituents, 9 saw increases in foreign ownership while 21 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • URC – Foreign ownership declined 1.19 percentage points MoM to 18.0%.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership fell 87bps MoM to 23.4%.   
    • JFC – We estimate that foreign ownership decreased 80bps MoM to 23.4%. It also saw the third highest net foreign selling for the month, at US$25.5mn.
    • PLUS – foreign ownership climbed 8.69 percentage points MoM to 30.1%. We suspect this was an error, as the trading data suggested that PLUS experienced net foreign selling in April
    • JGS – foreign ownership increased 19bps MoM to 13.7%.
