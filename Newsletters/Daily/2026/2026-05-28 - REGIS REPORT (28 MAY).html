REGIS REPORT

28 May 2026

Security Bank (SECB PM) announced the appointment of a new CFO with the departure (abrupt) of its current one. The incoming CFO, John David Yap, is currently part of the SECB team already.

 

+++

 

Energy Secretary Sharon Garin said that the auction framework for the right to operate the coal mine at Semirara island could be “refined”, with an addendum requirement for a minimum percentage of coal output to be allocated for domestic sales. She further stated that the auction, which was originally set last April 28 but was postponed, could be re-started by mid-year.  We await further details on the proposed revisions, but this development adds to the uncertainty surrounding the mining unit of Semirara (SCC PM).

 

+++

 

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is reviewing the secondary price cap (P7.42 or US$0.12 per kwh) for the spot market, which is triggered when average spot prices rise to a certain level.

 

Given the current level of fuel prices, especially gas prices, the variable cost of running a gas-fired power plant is likely higher than the cap. Gas-fired plants account for ~25% of power generation in Luzon.

 

Raising the cap (or removing it entirely) would help avoid a situation where the gas-plants sell below cost. For the other players (those running coal plants or solar farms, for example), it would allow them to sell into WESM more profitably.  Conversely, it also means higher prices for consumers.

 

+++

 

The government reported a budget surplus of P31bn for April.  April is typically a surplus month because of the tax return deadline, but the surplus is lower than last year because of the deadline was extended.

 

Primary spending (i.e. excluding debt service) was up 8% to P442bn but this was padded by a P60bn transfer to the national health insurance agency Philhealth.  Primary spending would have still been down otherwise.

 

+++

 

Ayala Corp (AC PM) is expanding its cooperation with Thailand’s CP Group with an agreement on the redevelopment of some of its Makro sites.  There’s no information on financial terms at this point or on possible ownership, if any, by AC or Ayala Land (ALI PM) in the redevelopment initiatives.

 

+++

 

The net satisfaction rating of President Marcos dropped to -15 in March, which is likely a reaction to the surge in prices (especially fuel) following the US-Israel military operation against Iran. This rating is the lowest since Marcos took office.

MINING
Department of Energy eyeing revisions to auction framework of Semirara coal contract
  • At the sidelines of an event, Energy Secretary Sharon Garin said that the agency is refining the auction framework for the Semirara mine coal block before re-starting the bidding by mid-year
    • Secretary Garin is eyeing to include a provision that would require the winning bidder to supply an undetermined minimum percentage of coal output for domestic sales
    • It is also ironing out some concerns raised by other bidders, including contract duration, the status of Semirara Mining & Power Corp.’s (SCC PM) existing equipment, as well as the expected production volumes
  • The bidding was postponed indefinitely from the initial bid submission date of April 28
  • We note that under the existing coal operating contract of SCC, ownership of mining equipment, port facilities, and other infrastructure in the island will be retained by SCC
  • We believe the proposed changes to the bidding framework introduce additional uncertainty regarding SCC’s ability to secure the contract
    • The previous bidding framework focused on selecting the bidder that submitted the best three-year mine plan
POWER
Energy regulator mulls review of secondary WESM price ceiling
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is reviewing the secondary price cap of P7.42/kwh
    • It remains unclear, however, whether the ERC plans to raise the WESM secondary price cap further or take the more consequential step of removing it altogether
  • The secondary price cap is triggered whenever the 72-hour rolling average electricity price exceeds Php12.4/kwh
  • WESM prices repeatedly hit the ceiling last May 13-15, while consumers experienced blackouts
    • Generation output typically declines between 3–4PM as solar production tapers off, highlighting the need for additional fast-response and dispatchable resources during evening peak hours
MACRO
Government posts P31bn budget surplus in April
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget surplus of P31bn in April, 53% lower YoY but a reversal from the P350bn deficit in Mar
    • This brings the 4M26 deficit to P324bn, 14% narrower vs the same period last year
  • Revenue collections in April rose 3% YoY, decelerating from +6% YoY in March, primarily as collections from the BIR were flat (+0.4% YoY)
    • Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections slowed to +0.4% YoY from +7% the previous month
      • The disappointing BIR figure was partly attributed to the extension of the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return from April to 15 to May 15 following the declaration of the State of National Energy Emergency
    • Customs collections picked up to 16%, accelerating from the 6% growth the previous month on continued digitalization efforts
    • Non-tax revenues rose 7% slowing from +46% in March
  • Gov't spending continued to recover as expenses were up 11% YoY in April accelerating from the 5% growth in March
    • Primary spending for the month increased by 8% YoY
      • This marks the third straight month of YoY growth after six consecutive months of YoY declines in gov't spending
    • Interest payments rose 37% YoY

POLITICS
President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating hits record low in March SWS survey
  • President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating deteriorated from "neutral" at -3 in Nov 2025 to "poor" -15 in Mar 2026
    • Recall that Marcos’ net satisfaction rating first became negative back in Mar 2025 (-12) following Former President Rodrigo Duterte's arrest by the ICC and turned negative again in Sep 2025 (-5) after his flood control scandal expose during his State of the Nation Address last Jul 2025
  • Marcos' gross satisfaction rating declined from 33% in Nov to 40% in Mar
    • Gross dissatisfaction rose from 43% to 49% in Mar
  • SWS cited the following movements in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas:
    • -14 points in Metro Manila (from “poor” -17 to "bad" -31)
    • -11 points in Balance Luzon (from “moderate” +13 to "neutral" +2)
    • -17 points in the Visayas (from “neutral” +2 to "poor" -15)
    • -11 points in Mindanao (from “poor” -29 to "bad" -40)
  • Marcos' average net satisfaction was zero in 2025, down from +25 in 2024 and +51 in 2023
  • Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,500 adults from Mar 24-30, 2026
