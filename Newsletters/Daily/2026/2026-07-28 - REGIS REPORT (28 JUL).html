REGIS REPORT

28 July 2026

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr delivered his 5th State of the Nation Address (SONA) to Congress yesterday. This time though, there weren’t the same fireworks as last year’s explosive exposé on the flood control corruption scandal. A year on, the President promised more high-profile arrests, including his cousin and former Speaker Martin Romualdez. He also highlighted the potential economic benefits from the country signing up to the Pax Silica alliance. He also touted some big infrastructure projects, which seemed incongruous with the sharp drop in infra spending (-28% YoY in 5M26). He also reiterated his goals to have EVs represent 50% of all cars sold by 2040.

 

We were expecting big announcements to help consumers cope with high inflation (and to boost his administrations sagging popularity ratings). Among those mentioned were

  • Proposal to Congress to lower income tax rates for the middle class, and to raise the level of income exempted from income taxes (P350,000 a year, from the P250,000 currently).
  • Lower electricity costs by a) having distributors absorb systems losses, b) promote nuclear energy, and c) incentivize residential solar panel installation
  • Continue with the subsidized P20/kg rice (vs retail market price of P40-50/kg)
  • P60bn worth of cash handouts as part of the UPLIFT program

 

Nothing in the SONA changes our broad macro views. The ongoing corruption investigations will likely mean muted infra spending well into 2H26. We could see some mild rebound in consumer spending, though we were underwhelmed by the measures mentioned. We remain concerned over persistently high inflation should El Niño push rice prices higher.

 

To the upside, Ayala Corp (AC PM) could benefit from the EV push through their BYD joint venture. Meanwhile, Meralco (MER PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM) could see profits hit should they be forced to absorb systems losses.

 

+++

 

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported 1H26 NPAT of P40.7bn, flat YoY and slightly behind Regis and consensus estimates. For 2Q alone, profits were down 1.4% YoY. Topline growth was muted, up 9% YoY, due to NIM compression and weak fee income growth. To the upside, controlled opex lifted PPOP 12% YoY. Profits were again dragged by provisions, which jumped 76% YoY, as the bank claimed a “proactive” stance kept credit costs at 67bps. More details after the briefing at 4pm this afternoon.

 

 

+++

 

President Marcos’ utterances on the power industry, specifically on the electricity systems losses, likely received a figurative ovation from consumers. A closer appreciation of the issues, however, suggests the solution for expensive power lies elsewhere.

 

We estimate forcing electricity utilities to absorb the entire systems loss wholesale will likely reduce consumer bills by ~3%. It will have large unintended consequences. For a party like Meralco (MER PM), the pretax impact is likely to be around P20bnm, roughly 1/3rd of pretax income. The impact on the distribution utilities (DU) segment of Aboitiz Power (AP PM), which is ~10% of group EBITDA, is also likely to be substantial. DUs that are even smaller, however, are likely to be unable to absorb it fully and could end up going bust.

 

This pronouncement is, in our opinion, misinformed. Any change in the regulatory scheme will have to still consider the financial impact of these unavoidable physical losses for the industry to be viable.

 

+++

 

Government infrastructure spending dropped 27% in May, taking YT-May expenditures down 28%. Spending here has fallen sharply following the corruption scandal involving funds for “flood control” projects.

 

+++

 

MREIT (MREIT PM) provided more details on its upcoming share-for-property swap. It will acquire 300,000sqm of malls, offices, and hotels from its sponsor, Megaworld (MEG PM), as well as Travellers International, the gaming arm of Alliance Global (AGI PM), in exchange for 1.64bn new MREIT shares. The acquisition would expand MREIT’s floor are by 47% (to c 950.000dwm) and its shares outstanding by 35%.

 

+++

 

ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) announced it has begun construction of ~1,660 MWh of battery storage systems at its solar facilities in Zambales, Philippines. The project will allow ACEN to capture higher electricity prices during peak evening periods, adding as much as 6-7% to EBITDA annually.

 

+++

 

It’s official. The PSE announced that Maynilad (MYNLD PM) will be part of the benchmark PSEi (“PCOMP”). It will enter the index at the expense of Converge ICT (CNVRG PM).

BANKS
BDO 1H26 NPAT flat, behind

BDO reported 1H26 headline NPAT of P40.7bn, flat from the P40.6bn the previous year. The result was slightly behind REGIS forecasts (P86.2bn) and consensus (86.7bn). For 2Q alone, NPAT of P20.6bn was down 1% YoY. Loans grew 15% YoY, but NIMs compressed, with NII up a lower 11%. PPOP was up 12%, slightly behind our estimates. Provision expense jumped 76% YoY for 1H26. However, NPLs and NPL cover were broadly flat, suggesting some write-offs in the quarter.

 

Some highlights

  • Customer loans grew 15% YoY, slightly slower than the 15.6% pace in 1Q26. However, this is still significantly faster than the sector, which grew loans at 12.5% in May.
  • NIMs in 2Q were flat QoQ, but down 23bps YoY,  resulting in Net interest income growing 11% YoY. Loan yields improved 12 bps QoQ, but were still down 19bps YoY. However, deposit costs were also up 6bps QoQ, and were flat YoY, leading to the squeeze. CASA ratio also dropped to 63% from 66% the previous quarter, and from 69% a year ago.
  • Non-interest income was soft, up just 4% YoY in 1H26, despite insurance income growing 14% YoY. For 2Q alone, fee incomes were up just 1% YoY.
  • Opex growth was kept steady at +7% YoY, leading to a 12% YoY growth in PPOP, a bit shy of our FY estimates
  • Provision expense jumped 76% YoY to P12.8bn in 1H26, as the bank reiterated its “proactive” stance given the local and global macro challenges. Credit cost jumped to 67bps in 2Q. This was despite NPL ratio improving slightly QoQ to 1.64%, and NPL cover flat QoQ at 132%. This suggests there were some loan write-offs in 2Q26.

 

More after the briefing tomorrow at 4pm. Key questions will likely revolve around provisions remaining elevated in 2Q, and how management sees this evolving through the rest of the year.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Highlights from Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. delivered his fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA) yesterday, outlining accomplishments and targets for the remaining two years of his term which included a discussion on topics such as gov't accountability, economic reforms and tax relief, energy security, infrastructure, social welfare
  • Anti-corruption
    • Marcos opened his speech by mentioning that cases were filed against contractors, DPWH officials, and lawmakers involved in alleged irregularities in flood control projects, with charges soon to be filed with the Ombudsman against his cousin, former House Speaker Martin Romualdez
    • He also stated that all the money recovered from corrupt individuals involved would be "immediately returned to the public treasury"
  • Power and energy
    • President Marcos urged Congress to amend the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) in order to remove system loss charges from electricity bills to make power rates cheaper
    • He also mentioned plans to diversify the country's power sources through renewable energy as well as nuclear energy
    • Marcos stated a long-term goal of having 50% of all cars be EVs by 2040, aided by the growth of domestic local industries across the EV value chain (i.e., vehicle assembly and battery manufacturing)
  • Economy
    • The president highlighted that the country's economic strategy is focused on attracting high-value industries vs traditional manufacturing
    • He said that the US-backed Pax Silica Industrial Hub has the potential to create quality jobs and increase the country's competitiveness and involvement in advanced technology industries
    • Marcos urged Congress to also pass a bill on tax relief measures such as expanding the exemption for income tax to include those earning P350,000 and below annually as well as exempting small businesses from the minimum corporate tax
  • Infrastructure and digitalization
    • Marcos highlighted that some railways are expected to be operational soon with the MRT-7 scheduled to open 12 stations next year and the North-South Commuter Railway to partially begin operations (Valenzuela-Malolos) next year as well
    • He emphasized that right of way issues are now more quickly resolved given the recently implemented Accelerated and Reformed Right of Way Act
    • He also stated efforts to accelerate the growth of the local tech sector through fintech, digital banking solutions, and better financial inclusion for all
  • Social welfare
    • Marcos reported that 12mn people have benefited from the P20/kg program
      • Other food security initiatives such as higher NFA buying capacity, larger buffer stocks as well as cold storage
    • To address higher oil prices, the gov't has allocated over P60bn to sustain its Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport (UPLIFT) initiatives until the end of the year
    • Savings recovered from corruption investigations were said to have been redirected to education, food programs, infrastructure, and healthcare assistance
  • Education
    • Marcos announced a handful of initiatives in order to improve the quality of education in the country
      • An expanded school-based feeding program for nutritionally at-risk learners for 4.5mn students
      • 30,000 additional teaching positions were created as well as plans to address the classroom shortages through PPP
    • He also mentioned AI training programs for 1.8mn individuals for students, parents, and teachers
MACRO
Government infrastructure spending drops 28% YoY in 5M26
  • The government’s infrastructure disbursements (incl. infra components of GOCC subsidies and LGU transfers) fell 28% YoY to P401bn in 5M26
    • This trails the government’s full-year infra spend target of P1.29tn (-3% YoY)
    • The government will need to spend P127bn per month for the rest of the year to reach its target
  • Derived infra spend for May alone stood at P101bn, down 27% YoY

 

 

PROPERTY
MREIT to acquire assets from related parties via share-for-property swap
  • MREIT will acquire 304,000sqm of mall, office, and hotel assets from Megaworld and Travellers International via share-for-property swap
    • 160,000sqm of malls
    • 117,000sqm of offices
    • 26,500sqm – 737-room Holiday Inn Express Manila in Newport
  • The acquisition will increase its portfolio by 47% to 950,000sqm
    • After the acquisition, MREIT’s portfolio breakdown will be:
    • 77% office, 20% retail, 3% hotel
  • MREIT will issue 1.64bn new MREIT shares at P16.50/share
    • Total value of P27bn – this would increase MREIT’s AUM by 28% to P122bn
    • This would expand shares outstanding by 35%
    • P16.50/share represents an 18% premium to the previous closing price
  • We estimate that the AGI Group’s ownership in MREIT will rise from 54% to 66%
    • Megaworld’s ownership in MREIT will rise from 53% to 59%
  • The assets have a combined occupancy rate of 91%
    • MREIT’s current occupancy is 90%
  • The assets to be acquired have a combined WALE of 5.3 years
    • MREIT’s current WALE is 3.1 years
  • MREIT said that the acquisition will be DPS accretive
  • MREIT currently offers 7.6% dividend yield
    • The 10-year government bond yield is 7.6%
  • MEG currently offers 4.3% dividend yield and is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.3X FY26E P/E
POWER
ACEN starts construction of 1,660 MWH BESS in Zambales solar facilities
  • ACEN announced that it commenced construction of 1,660 MWh of Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) across its solar facilities in Zambales province
    • These facilities will complement its 948 MW Palauig and SanMar solar facilities in the province, of which 450MW is operational and the balance set for completion in 2027
    • BESS will allow the projected 1,400 GWH of energy from the 948MW solar plants to be stored and delivered during evening peak hours and periods of solar generation
  • We estimate ACEN will need to spend an additional ~US$330mn (~P20bn) or US$200k per MWH for the BESS
    • Given that the existing solar plants are fully merchant, these plants will now have the ability to sell at peak hours, when WESM rates are also at highest
    • Assuming these solar plants can sell at P6.50/kwh (vs. P4.50/kwh base case), we estimate revenues and EBITDA for these plants could rise by 45% and NOPAT by ~30%
    • At the ACEN level, we estimate this investment could boost ACEN’s FY28E attributable EBITDA by ~P2bn (+5%) to P43bn and profit by ~P1bn (+9%) to P12bn
