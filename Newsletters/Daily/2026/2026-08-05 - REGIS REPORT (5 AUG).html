REGIS REPORT

05 August 2026

Headline CPI inflation eased again to 6.2% YoY for June, driven solely by the drop in oil prices which led to a slowdown in transport inflation. However, every other major category was either flat or accelerated MoM. We are likely to see inflation stay elevated in 2H26, with a) the approved (but postponed) P85/day NCR wage hike, and b) the impact of El Niño on food (rice) prices. We also expect the BSP to raise its policy rate by another 50bps in 2H26 to 5.25%.

 

Meanwhile, jeepney drivers are calling for a three-day strike on 10-12 August if they aren’t granted chunky fare hikes. Note that the government has kept public transport fares at pre-Iran War levels, rescinding an approved fare increase in March. The government has instead been providing fuel subsidies to drivers and owners, which it announced it will extend for an extra month into August.

Earnings coming in thick and fast.

 

  • Maynilad (MYNLD PM) - Ahead - Maynilad reported its net income rose 17% YoY to P4.5bn in 2Q26, bringing 1H26 earnings to P8.5bn (+14%). The results are broadly in line with consensus but ahead of Regis estimates. The earnings beat was partly driven by accounting-related items, which should be welcomed by dividend-focused investors. However, we place greater emphasis on the company's cash spending, which increased 14% YoY to P19.1bn in 1H26, trending mildly behind our full-year forecast of P41bn. Given sustained investments, management estimates the regulatory value of its West Zone concession stood at P184bn in end-June, up 12% on an annualized basis.

  • Globe (GLO PM) – Ahead – Core NPAT for 1H26 of P10.2bn was down 2% YoY, but the operating figures looked to be in line or even slightly ahead of Regis’s expectations. Service revenues were 8% YoY in 2Q26, with Ebitda up 6% YoY. Overall profit growth was pulled down by Mynt, which lapped a very high base in 2Q25, before the de-linking of online gaming. More during the briefing on Monday, 10 August.

 

 

 

  • DigiPlus(PLUS)  – Behind - 2Q25 recurring profit rose 7% QoQ (down 43% YoY) to P2.4bn, behind consensus forecast. Although revenue fell 9% QoQ, EBITDA margin expanded 2.9ppt QoQ (to 18.2%) due to a decline in A&P spending as well as salaries and benefits. Separately, DigiPlus also said it would apply for an online gaming license in New Zealand, which just recently passed its online gambling law.

We issued a report on Jollibee (JFC PM) updating our numbers ahead of the 2Q results, reducing our forecasts by 21-24% across our forecast period. We cut our target price by 12% to P220/sh, as we switch to SOTP methodology. We do expect continued near-term headwinds, as we expect margin pressure to persist, particularly with the recently declared wage hike, and the impending El Niño patterns. That said, the share price has already under-performed, down 33% YoY, prompting us to maintain our Buy call, on valuations as the stock now trades on 16x PER, nearing 10-year low multiples. We continue to watch the international business closely, as the recent disappointments could weigh on their spin-off plans.

Gov't reviews proposals for fare hikes and expanded fuel subsidy

    • Castro, however, emphasized the need for "orderly dialogue rather than constantly taking to the streets"

GAMING
DigiPlus 1H26 recurring net profit falls 45%
  • DigiPlus Interactive’s 2Q26 headline profit rose 148% QoQ and 66% YoY to P7.0bn
  • Stripping off gains from convertible notes of International Entertainment Corp (1009 HK), we estimate that 2Q26 recurring profit rose 7% QoQ (down 43% YoY) to P2.4bn
    • We estimate that 1H26 net profit fell 45% to P4.6bn, below consensus forecast
  • 2Q26 revenue fell 9% QoQ and 37% YoY to P15.6bn
    • The number of Monthly Active Users was flat QoQ at 5.75mn (c. 8.5mn in 2Q25)
    • The number of Monthly Active Bettors and Depositors grew 26% QoQ to 4.68mn
  • However, EBITDA rose 7% QoQ (down 37% YoY) to P2.8bn
  • EBITDA margin expanded 2.9ppt QoQ (flat YoY) to 18.2%
  • The QoQ margin expansion was largely thanks to the 23% QoQ decline in A&P (to P4.2bn) as well as the 12% QoQ decrease in salaries and other benefits (to P1.1bn)
