REGIS REPORT

12 January 2026

The Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has released the new regulations covering REITs. The new rules (Memorandum Circular #1) make it easier for REITs to comply with regulatory requirements. More importantly, however, the circular would expand what may qualify as REITs to cover businesses like toll roads, airports, telecom infrastructure, power generation and others.

 

This revision could likely spur listings of businesses not traditionally considered to be REIT-able. Among the obvious candidates are telecom tower firms, toll road operators and even data centers. REITs were among the key outperformers over the past two years and, as a result, they now trade at 14x PER (and 6.7% yield) for FY26. This puts them on ~50% premium to the rest of the market. This earnings multiple is, in our view, quite decent/rich and might open the door to more firms raising capital via REIT listings. Note this higher valuation is on top of the significant tax advantages (and resultant higher earnings) that REITs enjoy.

PROPERTY
SEC revises REIT regulations
  • The SEC issued Memorandum Circular No. 1, which revises the regulations regarding the REIT law
  • The SEC expanded the definition of “income generating real estate”
    • Income-generating real estate includes properties with steady cash flow, such as rentals, toll fees, user fees, ticket sales, parking fees and storage fees
    • They include toll roads, railways, airports and air navigation facilities, ports, information and communications technology infrastructure, energy infrastructure assets and data centers
  • The SEC also extended the period to reinvest in Philippine real estate to two years (from one year previously)
    • Reinvestment may take the form of investments in equity, the extension of loans or purchase of debt instruments or the repayment of debt in relation to any real estate or infrastructure projects in the Philippines
  • We do not expect the revised regulations to have any material direct impact on the existing REITs
  • However, they may lead to more listings of REITs from sectors such as telecommunications, power, infrastructure, etc.
TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rebound in December, up 28% YoY
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), recovered by 28% YoY to 151k TEUs in December
    • This reversed the 3%/6% YoY declines in October/November
    • CMSA regained market share, given that combined volumes across all ports in Manzanillo fell 8% YoY, with other ports (ex-CMSA) experiencing a 25% YoY drop in December
  • Brings 4Q25 volumes at CMSA to 410k TEUs, up 6% YoY and just a tad slower than 3Q25 volume growth of 7%
    • Takes FY25 volumes to 1.5mn TEUs, up 6% YoY
  • We are positive on CMSA’s 12-month outlook, supported by the anticipated completion of its ~20% capacity expansion by 1Q26
  • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 11x EV/EBITDA and 19x PER vs peer average (12.6x EV/EBITDA and 18x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA/20x PER)

 

 

 

MACRO
BIR collections rebounded in Dec 2025 but likely to miss FY25 target
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) stated that revenue collections in Dec rose ~7% YoY, accelerating from the 3% YoY growth in Nov
  • Despite the rebound in Dec, the BIR guided that it will likely not meet its initial 2025 target of P3.2trn (+12% YoY) collections due to lackluster revenue growth in 2H25
    • BIR Commissioner Charlie Mendoza expects 2025 revenues to amount to P3.1trn (+9% YoY) as "revenue collections efforts were significantly affected in 2H when the flood control investigations started"
  • Note that the gov't has revised its economic growth targets for 2026-2028 but tax revenue target revisions have yet to be announced

 

 

POWER/UTILITIES
Ombudsman investigates Congressman Leviste’s alleged sale of solar franchise
  • Batangas Representative Leandro Leviste is being investigated for allegedly “selling” the franchise of his solar energy business to Meralco (MER PM)
    • Recall that Republic Act 11357 was signed into law in July 2019, which provided a 25-year franchise to Cong. Leviste’s Solar Para Sa Bayan Corporation (SBPC)
    • Any change in the control over entities that have franchises granted by Congress needs to be approved by Congress
  • Meralco’s wholly-owned unit, Meralco PowerGen Corp. (MGEN), reiterated that it acquired control in SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM), an entity separate corporate entity from SBPC
    • SBPC was not part of SPNEC when MGEN acquired the latter
    • MGEN owns 57% of SPNEC’s commons and 69% of voting shares 
MACRO
BSP says 2026 inflation could be below gov't target range
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas iterated its expectations for 2026 inflation, stating that inflation risks are tilted to the downside with a 45% likelihood that inflation will fall below 2%
    • Subdued global commodity prices leading to moderate oil and food inflation were cited as the primary downside pressures
