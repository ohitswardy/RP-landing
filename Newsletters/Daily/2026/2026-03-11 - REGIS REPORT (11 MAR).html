11 March 2026

We lifted our target price for ICTSI (ICT PM) to P750/share but reduce our rating to Hold from Buy in a new report. 

 

The +34% increase in TP is driven by (1)the acquisition of Durban port in South Africa, as well as (2) the assumed success in the renewal of three more concessions currently still pending. We are also raising our FY26/FY27 profit estimates by 5%/3% – lower than our valuation increase, as the source of the valuation upgrade will mostly materialize only over the long run.

 

ICT’s shares are up a blistering >3x since end-2022 and we believe current valuations – 12x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 20x PER – already reflect known positive catalysts. We remain quite positive on the company’s long-term outlook given its execution track record, but investors might be better off waiting for more attractive entry points. Accretive acquisitions are a potential source of upside, with a successful bid for a new port in Brazil possibly lifting valuations by +9%.

For today’s results round-up, we have press statements and thus preliminary info from restaurant chain operator Jollibee (JFC PM) and coal mining and power firm Semirara (SCC PM). We expect to get more granularity from the respective earnings briefings.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – FY25 net profit came in at P10.9bn, up +5% YoY, which would be below estimates. Top line growth was a healthy +13%, buoyed in part by acquisitions (e.g. Compose Coffee) while EBIT growth was +19%. The Jollibee brand operations remain the bedrock both domestically and abroad while the performance of the international operations is patchy. Ahead of the earnings call, check out some details below.

 

  • Semirara (SCC PM)  (-) – The company’s press release this morning reported that FY25 profit fell 33% YoY to P13.1bn, which would be below Regis and consensus estimates. Coal sales volumes fell short despite strong production, more than offsetting the power segment’s outperformance. Coal inventory rose 5x YoY and ended at 5mn MT in 4Q25, of which 1.7mn MT (+3x) are commercial grade. We hope to know more after management’s briefing tomorrow at 4pm.

CONSUMER
JFC FY25 Results – Profits rise 5% to P10.9bn, below estimates
  • JFC FY25 net profit amounted to P10.9bn (+5% YoY), below Regis and consensus expectations as the company noted higher financing costs and tax provisions. FY25 EBIT (P20.2bn, +19%) looks ahead of estimates but topline (P305.1bn, +13%) was within expectations. In 4Q alone, EBIT jumped 42% to P4.1bn largely on the effect of a low base. Management cited "expenditure optimizations" but we note that 4Q is when a lot of accounting adjustments are thrown in, which may distort the quarterly comparisons.

 

On a consolidated basis, the 4Q25 topline was up 10% YoY (decelerating from 9M25's +14% growth). The moderation in 4Q growth in was likely due to base effects following the consolidation of Compose Coffee in Aug 2024.

 

  • Jollibee brand growth momentum continues. In 4Q alone, domestic SWS grew +10% with SSSG of +5.0%, improving from +1.2% the previous quarter given stronger-than-usual rains in 3Q. The int'l business posted SWS growth of +15% on SSSG of +5.5%, moderating from the +6.2% growth in 3Q. Both segments were driven by the strong 4Q SSSG from the Jollibee brand (PH +5.1%, US +12.9%, Vietnam +33.3%), which offset the underperformance in Chowking PH (+0.8%), Greenwich (-4.6%), CBTL (+0.8%), and Smashburger (-9.9%).

 

  • China recovering but Smashburger deteriorates further. China continues to recover with +5.0% SSSG in 4Q, the third consecutive quarter of positive SSSG. Smashburger, meanwhile, continued to struggle with SSSG declining further to -9.9% in 4Q vs -4.7% in 3Q, the business also notably more than doubled its EBITDA loss in 2025 to P952mn from P434mn in 2024. This dragged the overall profitability of the int'l business, accounting for -12.5% (-P1.4bn) of total NIAT in 2025 vs -11% (-P1.1bn) the previous year.

 

  • Company expects similar SWS and SSSG, higher store network and EBIT growth. Management for 2026 relative to 2025 maintained its guidance on SWS (+8-12%) and SSSG (+4-6%) but revised upwards its outlook for store network growth to +5-10% (+4-8% in 2025) and EBIT growth to +15-18% (+10-15%). More details after the briefing at 4pm today—register here.

 

MINING
Semirara FY25 profit (ex-FX) down 32% to P13.0bn – Behind Regis and Street estimates
  • Semirara reported FY25 profits reached P13.1bn, down 33% YoY
    • Excluding FX adj, we estimate core profit fell 34% YoY to P13.02bn, below Regis (P13.4bn) and Street estimates (P13.5bn)
    • We attribute the shortfall largely on lower-than-expected coal sales volumes (actual: 15.4mn MT, -7% YoY vs. Regis: 16.8mn MT, flat)
  • In 4Q25 alone, profits fell 20% YoY to P3.3bn, as coal sales volumes dropped 42% YoY to 2.5mn MT and ASPs fell 23% YoY to P2,185/MT
    • Export volumes contracted 83% YoY / 76% QoQ to 400k MT in 4Q
    • We note that coal production rose 66% YoY to 4.8mn MT in 4Q25, taking FY25 production to 19mn MT
    • Coal inventory jumped to 5mn MT (+79% QoQ / +5x YoY) in end-2025, with commercial grade coal inventory at 1.7mn Mt (+3x YoY)
  • Management is holding a briefing at 4pm tomorrow, register here

 

 

MACRO
Government bond yields rise
  • The Philippine government sold 7-year bonds at a yield of 6.473%
    • 61.4 basis points higher than the February 10 auction
    • Awards totaled P9.45bn vs P20bn; tenders reached P38.5bn
  • The 7-year BVAL hit 6.5563%, from 6.2689% the prior day
BANKS
Commercial banks’ loan growth slows to 9.3% in Jan
  • Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.3% in Jan from 9.6% in Dec, the second month of single-digit growth after a previous 19-month streak of double-digit growth
    • Loans to productive activities moderated to 8.2% vs 8.4% in Dec
    • Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 21.3%, a tad slower than Dec's 21.5% rate
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated to 8.6% in Jan from 7.2% in Dec
