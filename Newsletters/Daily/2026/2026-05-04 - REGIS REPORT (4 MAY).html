04 May 2026

We offer our thoughts on the 1Q results of Ayala Land (ALI PM), Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) and Semirara (SCC PM) today.

 

  • Ayala Land (ALI PM) (-) – 1Q26 net profit fell 23% to P5.4bn, well below REGIS and consensus forecasts. The residential segment performed poorly, as both earnings and reservation sales declined substantially. Development revenue fell 27% YoY due to lower sales “bookings”. Meanwhile, reservation sales fell 22% YoY as sales of both the premium and core segments declined.

 

Furthermore, management has turned even more cautious since the conflict in the Middle East began. It lowered the FY26 capex budget to P50bn (from P92bn in 2025 and the previous budget of c. P75bn). The company will also launch fewer residential projects than it previously planned P30bn. It might launch horizontal projects in 2H26 but no longer intends to launch condominium projects this year. In fact, it already canceled a mid-income condo project and “paused” the Laurean, an Ayala Land Premier project in Makati.

  • Robinson Retail (RRHI PM) (-) – Profits for 1Q contracted 36% to P489m, as higher financing costs from the buyback of shares from Dairy Farm and larger losses from associates (principally the O!Save business) cut into earnings. Core operations were otherwise actually within expectations, with revenue growing 10% and EBIT rising 4%. The key near-term issue for the company (stock), however, is the planned tender offer by its controlling shareholder.

  • Semirara (SCC PM) (+) – Core profit (ex-FX) fell 10% YoY to P3.9bn, which is trending ahead of both Regis and consensus estimates. Despite the power unit’s shortfall, which was weighed down by outages, coal mining ASPs were flat at ~P2,500/MT (+13% QoQ), on trend to beat our full-year forecast.  Management is holding a briefing at 3:30 pm today.

POWER
Semirara: 1Q26 core profit drops 10% YoY on plant outages
  • Headline 1Q26 profit declined by 12% YoY to P3.8bn.  Removing FX adjustments, we estimate core profit fell 10% to P3.9bn, trending above Regis (P12.8bn) and Street (P13.5bn) estimates.
    • We attribute the outperformance to higher-than-expected coal ASPs, which more than offset the disappointing performance of the power unit
  • Profits from coal mining improved 6% QoQ and 3% YoY, as ASPs were flat (+13% QoQ) at P2,479/MT (vs. FY26E: P2,136/MT, -7% YoY) and cash costs fell 7% YoY
    • Sales volumes remained soft (-4% YoY) at 4.5mn MT, despite production increasing by 4% to 5.9mn MT
  • Profit contributions from the power unit fell 31% YoY to P1.1bn due to unplanned outages
  • The company’s net cash position stood at P4.8bn, with gross cash at P10.6bn
    • With the coal contract bidding postponed indefinitely, the declaration of cash dividends is also expected to be delayed
  • Management will hold a briefing on Monday, 3:30 PM (link)

 

 

POWER
Energy regulator re-opens spot market
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) ordered the resumption of Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) effective May 1
  • ERC Chair and CEO Francis Saturnino Juan said that prolonging the suspension of WESM could lead to unintended charges in market settlements
    • Recall that on March 26, the ERC suspended the WESM to address supply risks and price volatility triggered by the Middle East conflict
    • During this period, the ERC implemented technology-specific pricing and managed dispatch, with priority given to renewables
  • The ERC said it would not hesitate to redeclare a market suspension should circumstances warrant a return to administered pricing
  • The normalization of WESM operations could lead to supernormal profits for gencos with exposure to the spot market
    • ACEN’s PH operations (c. 43% of FY25 core att. EBITDA) have ~25% of capacity exposed to the spot market
    • SCC’s power unit has 45% of capacity exposed to the spot market
    • AP has ~10% of capacity un-contracted
MACRO
Gov’t infrastructure spending plunges by 30% YoY in 2M26
  • Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. infra disbursements of national and local gov’t units, as well as GOCCs) fell 29.7% YoY to P128.6bn in 2M26
    • Cited (a) slow progress billing and (b) base effects from last year’s election spending
  • The government noted that infra spend would likely remain soft for at least until 1H26
  • The government is targeting infra spend of ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) this year, broadly flat from last year’s P1.32tn (~4.7% of GDP)
MACRO
Trade deficit remains steady YoY at $4.5bn in March
  • The Mar trade deficit remained steady at US$4.5bn, unchanged YoY
    • This brings the 3M26 trade deficit to US$12.8bn, 3% wider YoY
  • Imports for the month grew by 12% YoY to US$12.7bn, a tad slower from the 13% increase in Feb
    • The growth in imports was primarily led by the 35% jump in fuel which offset the decline in consumer goods
  • Exports climbed 20% YoY to US$8.2bn, the fourteenth consecutive month of YoY growth
    • Electronics exports jumped 33% YoY to $4.8bn, accelerating from the 20% growth in Feb
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 59% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 6% to US$3.3bn

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
BSP expects April inflation to settle within 5.6-6.4%
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects April inflation to fall within 5.6-6.4%, accelerating from 4.1% in March
    • BSP expectations for Apr inflation are well above the BSP's 2-4% long-term target range
      • For 2026, BSP expectations for inflation this year is 6.3% (previously revised from 5.1%)
    • Apr inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on May 5 (Tue)
  • Upward price pressures are higher prices of domestic petroleum, rice, fish, meat, and electricity, as well as the depreciation of the peso
    • These may have been partially offset by lower prices of vegetables and fruit
    • As of end-Apr, the USD-PHP depreciated to P61.51, from P60.74 at end-Mar
  • Inflation has averaged 2.8% so far for 3M26
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expectations are at 3.2% and 4.0%, respectively
  • The next Monetary Board meeting is on Jul 18 (third out of six scheduled this year)
    • For 2026, consensus expects the BSP to hold rates steady at 4.50%

 

MACRO
High fertilizer costs may bring rice prices to P62/kg by Sept—DA
  • Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that rice prices are estimated to possibly rise to P62/kg by next harvest season in September as the price of fertilizer has surged from P1,650 to P3,000
  • Laurel stated that the DA is looking into possible interventions such as rice blending
    • Rice blends such as a 70-30 imported-local rice blend or white corn with rice grain blend are being considered to bring down the average rice price
