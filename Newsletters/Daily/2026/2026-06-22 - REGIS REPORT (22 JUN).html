The BSP rolled out new relief measures to protect banks’ capital ratios. It is temporarily allowing banks to NOT deduct mark-to-market losses on PHP-denominated government securities (GS) from their CET-1 and CAR ratio calculations. Banks will still need to recognize the MTM losses on their financial statements. This, together with the recent regulation on counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB), demonstrate that the BSP stands ready to allow banks to continue to lend. In practical terms, the new rule may be moot, with the sharp drop in the yield curve with the US-Iran peace negotiations. Assuming they hold, of course…

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital payments +41% in May - InstaPay surges past PesoNet in May

Payments over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) grew 41% YoY to P2,793bn ($48bn) in May. For the 5M26, the figure was P13,181bn, up 44% YoY. The monthly growth rate has decelerated over the last two months, from 45.6% YoY in April and from 50.1% in March.  For perspective, that’s already more than 100% of GDP…! And note further that this does NOT include payments made WITHIN a platform (eg Bank A to Bank A, and Wallet A to Wallet A).

 

Note too that transactions through InstaPay (the rail for real-time, smaller-ticket payments) overtook PesoNet (larger, batched transactions) for the first time in May.

 

InstaPay clocked payments worth P1,465bn for the month, up 57.4% YoY slightly slower than the 58.4% the previous month. For the first 5 months, InstaPay transactions hit P6,559bn, up 62% YoY. Meanwhile, PesoNet processed P1,327bn in May, up 26.4% YoY, decelerating sharply from the 34.9% clip in April. For 5M26, PesoNet was up 31% YoY to P6,622bn

 

The continued surge in smaller-ticket payments indicates sustained broadening of digital adoption across the economy.

 

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS
BSP - regulatory relief to protect CAR/CET-1 from FVOCI losses

The BSP issued Memorandum 2026-027, which protects banks’ CAR and CET-1 ratios from mark-to-market losses on their FVOCI books. The salient points are:

 

  • The relief covers all unrealized losses on PHP-denominated government securities (GS) from 28 February 2026, the start of the US-Iran conflict. MTM losses on all other financial instruments – including USD-denominated Philippine sovereigns (ROPs) are not covered by the Memorandum,
  • Banks are allowed to NOT deduct these MTM losses on PHP GSs from qualifying capital for the computation of CAR and CET-1 ratios
  • However, banks must still report the full MTM values in their financial statements, such as other comprehensive income (OCI), etc
  • The relief period ends on 01 January 2027

 

Note that this is not mandatory. That is, banks may choose to avail themselves of the relief, or they can keep with the standard reporting, taking the full MTM losses to their CAR/CET-1 ratios.

 

We believe the major banks – including those we cover – have solo CET-1 ratios that are well above the 10.0-10.5% minimum. Recall, too, that the BSP recently laid the groundwork for potentially lowering the CET-1 floors further using the counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB).

 

Furthermore, the PHP GS bond yields have crashed in recent days with the optimism over the US-Iran peace negotiations. The 10-year BVAL fell from a high of 7.8% in late May, to 6.97% as of last week. That’s roughly where it was at end-1Q26.

 

At a minimum, the Memorandum and the recent Circulars show that the BSP stands ready to provide regulatory support, particularly for MTM losses that should, in time, disappear as those bonds come closer to maturity dates.

  • ALI is trading at 74% discount to NAV, 0.6X FY26E P/B, and 6.7X FY26E P/E

 

 

MACRO
Government infrastructure spending drops 28% YoY in 4M26
  • Infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 28% YoY to P300bn in 4M26
    • This trails the government’s full-year target of P1.29tn, down 3% YoY and equivalent to 4.2% of GDP
  • Derived infra spend for the month of April alone fell 37% YoY and 40% MoM to P64bn
    • Cited slow completion and billings in the Department of Public Work’s projects

 

 

MACRO
BOP posts US$131mn surplus in May; GIR settles at US$104.0bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of May posted a surplus of US$131mn
    • A reversal from both the US$2.1bn deficit in the previous month of April and the US$298mn deficit in May 2025
    • This brings the 5M26 deficit to US$7.3bn, wider than the US$5.8bn deficit for the same period last year
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-May stood at US$104.0bn, down 1% YoY and flat (-0.3%) MoM
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.7 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn
