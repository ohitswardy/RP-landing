  • PLDT (TEL PM) (=) – Core income for 1Q26, by our reckoning, was P8.85bn, 3% higher YoY.  Revenue growth was a modest 3% but growth in the key segments was even less than that. Mobile revenue was up 1% while broadband revenue down 1%.  One bright spot was capex declining further this year (guidance), prompting us to cut our capex expectations, and thus lifting our target price (now P1,600/share).

BANKS
SECB 1Q26 NPAT P2.7bn, behind again on provisions

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT of P2.7bn for 1Q26, down 5% YoY, and tracking well behind REGIS (P15.3bn) and consensus (13.2bn) full year forecasts. PPOP was up 24% YoY, but was again dragged down by a 63% increase in provision expense to P3.9bn – for an annualized credit cost to 228bps – as the bank continues the cleanup of its credit card portfolio. Loan growth was 5% (vs 3% in 2025), as the bank moves away from low-margin corporate loans.

 

The results seemed broadly encouraging, with the new management team re-focusing on the bank’s core customer base, which should redound to improving asset quality over time. Cost and capital management should also help get ROEs pointing in the right direction.

 

Highlights

    • Loans grew 5% YoY, slightly faster than the 3% clocked in 4Q. Consumer loans were up 6.6%, while MSMEs jumped 30.7%. Corporate loans were up just 3.1% YoY.  Deposits grew 11.5%, with CASA ratio improving to 51% from 49% in 4Q.
    • NIMs were not comparable YoY, as the bank (again) changed its accounting for swap products. Reported NIMs were up 52bps YoY to 5., but the bank reckons “pro-forma” margins would be 4.77%, down 5bps QoQ. Loan yields were down 23bps, while deposit costs were flat QoQ, thanks to the refocusing on lower-cost CASA.
    • Fee incomes were up 10% YoY. SECB also recognized P347mn in associate income from its 25% stake in Home Credit Philippines (HCPH). The bank booked P712mn in trading losses, as interest rates jumped on the Iran conflict. It also recorded P978mn in FX losses, of which a significant chunk was due to the revised accounting for swap products.
    • Opex was up just 2% YoY, as the bank sought to contain discretionary spend.
    • PPOP jumped 24% YoY, tracking ahead of our forecasts.
    • Provision expense stayed high, at P3.9bn, up 63% YoY, equivalent to about 228bps in annualized credit costs, as the bank continues to clean up its legacy credit card book. Management guides that credit costs have peaked, with provisioning for consumer loans down QoQ. NPL ratio blipped up to 3.08% from  2.85bps%, and NPL cover dropped to 81% from 85%. However, this was largely due to a large-ish corporate account, for which the bank has adequate collateral cover. The bank maintained its guidance of 190bps credit cost for FY26E, though it admitted that a prolonged Iran conflict could add 20bps to that.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS
Asset quality surprisingly stable in March; loan growth speeds up to 11.6%

Philippine banks’ asset quality was remarkably stable in March, despite the Iran conflict. More startling was the sharp acceleration in loan growth to 11.6% YoY, despite the spike in inflation, and the drag on economic growth. As usual – note that we use data excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs), as these can be extremely volatile on a monthly basis, throwing off the denominator for the ratios, as per the last chart below.

 

NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) was 3.51% in March, flat MoM, and down 5bps YoY. In PHP terms, NPLs stood at P568bn, up 2.7% MoM and 10.2% YoY. Granted, NPL ratios will not show up for 90-days. However, even Past Due (PD) ratio only inched up slightly, at 4.54%, up 0.6bps MoM, and up 9.1bps YoY. The nominal value of PD loans was P736bn, up 2.9% MoM and 13.9% YoY. Restructured Loans (RLs) ratio improved to 2.09% down 4bps MoM and  6bps YoY.

 

By bank type, counter-intuitively, the larger universal and commercial banks (UKBs) saw worse numbers compared to the smaller thrift banks (TBs) and digital banks (Digibanks), especially on a YoY basis.

 

By sector, the most significant deterioration came from the Construction sector, which saw NPL ratios at 13.15% and PD ratios at 16.08%. The Manufacturing and Trade sectors have also started to show some signs of strain. Surprisingly, the Real Estate sector has been stable, despite all the concerns over over-supply and inventory. For Credit Cards, PD and NPL ratios have slowly crept up over the past 12 months, but nothing suggesting significant stress.

 

The data is supported by recent earnings calls by the larger banks, whose NPL upticks were attributed to corporate / commercial borrowers that were affected by the “flood control” corruption scandals from 2H25.
