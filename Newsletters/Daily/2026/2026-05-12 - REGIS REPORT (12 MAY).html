REGIS REPORT

12 May 2026

There were two big, and related, developments in the political front yesterday.

 

Opponents of Vice President Sara Duterte-Carpio have succeeded in getting her impeached for various offences, including corruption and a plan to assassinate President Marcos.  With a vote of 257 votes in favor (vs 25 against) in the House of Representatives, the articles of impeachment will now be sent to the Senate for a trial.

 

Almost simultaneously, however, her allies took control of the Senate after winning support from a handful of erstwhile administration allies. This means a Duterte ally, the newly installed Senate President Alan Cayetano, will now preside over the impeachment trial of Sara Duterte-Carpio (should it even be held).  While the Vice President’s conviction was always unlikely (because she had enough allies to prevent a 2/3rds vote against her), control by her allies of the Senate will likely mean even the presentation of the case against her (e.g. bank, documentary evidence, etc.) will likely be blocked.

 

Beyond just the impeachment case, with Senate control passing to what could be described as parties openly “hostile” to the administration, the Marcos government now looks visibly weaker and prospects for advancing its legislative agenda are thus dimmer.

Here’s our results summary for today:

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – 1Q26 profits declined 39% to P1.47bn despite revenues and systemwide sales rising 9% and 10%, respectively. Same-store sales growth (SSSG) group-wide was 3.5%, with Philippine SSSG lower at 3.2%.  Operating income fell 18% due to a sharp increase in direct costs (+12%) and despite a reduction in A&P spend (-18%). Management also said it was considering price adjustments and also reviewing its plans for the rest of the year. JFC will hold a briefing later this week.

 

  • Globe (GLO PM) (+) – Core income for 1Q rose 9% to P4.9bn, looking ahead of Regis expectations but within Street expectations.  Service revenues were up 5% YoY with mobile revenue rising 6%.  Notably, broadband revenues, which have been lagging for years, were also up 6%, finally. It was non-telco revenues which were down YoY.  The company will hold its earnings briefing tomorrow so we’ll have to wait to get more detail.

 

  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (=) – 1Q26 net profit rose 2% YoY to P3.54bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Reservation sales of standalone projects fell 46% YoY and 59% QoQ to P455mn, which only represents c. 10% of the 2019 average. Furthermore, we estimate that the unsold inventory of P44.4bn is equivalent to 9.3 years of reservation sales. On the other hand, sales of JV projects rebounded to P3.3bn, as the company instituted more aggressive payment schemes. Meanwhile, same-mall revenue growth slowed to 4%, at the low end of the historical range, after the strong figure of 8% in FY25.

  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (-) – Recurring profit was P7.9bn for 1Q, 61% higher YoY from a depressed 1Q25 base. The improvement came from the generations and RES business. The distribution utility (DU) segment, meanwhile, saw healthy 6% unit growth, in contrast with Meralco’s (MER PM) 2% drop. Management, however, says there are signs that power consumption is see a material slowdown.

 

  • Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) (-) – Nickel Asia reversed last year’s recurring loss and recorded a profit of P400mn in 1Q26, buoyed by higher ore ASPs and higher power capacities. However, the results are trailing our full-year forecast due to lower-than-expected ore sales volumes, which fell 8% YoY on weather disruptions. Nevertheless, management is optimistic it can catch up on production in succeeding periods and has maintained its 20mn MT sales volume target (+8%).

POWER
Aboitiz Power: 1Q26 profits up 61% on higher plant availability
  • 1Q26 profits up 61% to P7.6bn – Behind Street estimates. Headline profit rose 71% YoY to P7.9bn in 1Q26. Excluding one-offs and FX adj, recurring profit was 61% YoY higher at P7.6bn, slightly trailing Street’s FY26E (P34bn). The strong growth was underpinned by (a) base effects from higher plant availability factors, as well as (b) lower WESM exposures.

 

  • Generation/RES units drive beneficial EBITDA up 35%.  EBITDA attributable to the generation and RES units rebounded 42% and 38% YoY, respectively, driven by fewer outages. Recall that last year, AP concentrated most of its maintenance activities in 1Q to minimize disruptions during the May elections. Although WESM rates were modestly higher in 1Q26, AP reduced its exposure to WESM to 8% of its baseload capacity from 30% in 1H25. Its hydro facilities are also largely merchant. Despite the WESM suspension from March 26 to April 30, management noted that it remains to be a net beneficiary of higher WESM prices so far in 2Q, but intends to capitalize on the elevated rates by securing longer-term higher-priced contracts.

 

  • DU volumes up 6% in 1Q26 but seeing softness in 2Q so far. The distribution unit saw EBITDA jump 13% YoY to P2.3bn, underpinned by a 6% increase in sales volumes.  However, management noted that it has seen demand growth moderate materially in 2Q26 so far from its commercial/industrial clients, who account for 68% of sales. Management expressed concern on the potential long-term impact of the conflict on demand.

POLITICS
House impeaches Vice President Sara Duterte
  • The House of Representatives has voted to impeach Vice President Sara Duterte on vote with 257 lawmakers in favor, 25 against, and nine abstaining
  • The 257 votes were more than enough for the minimum one-third or 106 House members vote requirement for the articles of impeachment to be transmitted to the Senate for trial
    • This marks the second impeachment of VP Duterte after the Supreme Court voided the first case initiated last year over procedural flaws, with the current complaint garnering support from more lawmakers than the previous impeachment backed by 215 legislators
  • The articles of impeachment allege culpable violation of the Constitution and betrayal of public trust by VP Duterte from misused confidential funds, bribery of officials, amassed unexplained wealth, and an assassination plot against the President
POLITICS
Senate Minority Leader Alan Cayetano voted as new Senate President
  • Senate Minority Leader Alan Cayetano has replaced Senator Vicente "Tito" Sotto III as the new Senate President after securing votes from thirteen senators, compared to nine for Sotto, while two senators abstained from the vote
    • The reappearance of Senator Ronaldo "Bato" Dela Rosa, who was absent from the Senate since November 2025, was crucial in securing the minimum majority of thirteen votes in support of Cayetano
  • Senator Tito Sotto in a comment stated that they (the new minority) will insist that the impeachment articles, once submitted to the Senate, are to be taken up

 

 

MACRO
More countries join Luzon Economic Corridor
  • In a joint statement, the governments of the Philippines, United States, and Japan said that seven more countries would support the development of the Luzon Economic Corridor
  • The United Kingdom “is deploying its full Growth and Investment Partnerships (GIP+) toolkit in the LEC, providing technical assistance, PHP411bn (US$6.8bn) in export finance, and mobilizing capital towards infrastructure and energy projects.”
  • Australia, through its Manila Deal Team, will provide technical assistance under the Partnerships for Infrastructure program and a new P1.9bn (US$32.6mn) partnership with the Philippines on inclusive economic growth
  • South Korea will also contribute to “enhanced transport and digital connectivity, and sustained economic growth along the LEC, through Official Development Assistance and Public-Private Partnership initiatives, including a P1.5bn (US$25.6mn) grant to establish the National Cyber Security Center and the Ninoy Aquino International Airport modernization PPP Project.”
  • Denmark will help revitalize the Philippines shipbuilding sector by “advancing green maritime innovation, and fostering investments, jobs, and sustainable industrial development.”
  • France will be “financing 100 bridges through official development assistance, and industrial capacity building through a foreign direct investment project in the aeronautics sector.”
  • Italy will help in the “development of quality, resilient, and sustainable infrastructure by increasing its public financial support in order to facilitate private sector investment from Italian companies in the transport, semiconductors, and manufacturing sectors.”
  • Sweden is “contributing to Luzon’s Subic-Clark-Manila-Batangas freight railway through a P74mn (US$1.2mn) grant to fund a feasibility study on signaling systems and operational models.”
  • The Luzon Economic Corridor is part of Pax Silica initiative of the United States
  • There is a plan to establish a 1,600-hectare AI Industrial hub in New Clark City
