REGIS REPORT

28 August 2026

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised policy rates by 25bp, taking them to 5.0% following yesterday’s Monetary Board meeting. This is the 3rd rate hike since April and conforms to expectations (for both Regis and the Street).

 

The BSP’s rate action comes despite what appears to be a deceleration in inflation over the last couple of months. What’s worth pointing out, however, is that the BSP’s just-revised inflation target for this year (6.4%) still implies a significant jump in CPI inflation over the last few months of FY26. This is a significant contrast with the 5.6% inflation that the Street is expecting for the year.

 

Apart from the low base for food (rice) prices, we suspect the BSP, like Regis and unlike the Street, may have also penciled in impact from El Niño. If El Niño does indeed lead to sharpy higher prices in 4Q, further rate increases should be likely,

 

+++

 

The Dept of Energy (DOE) published a new draft framework for the upcoming bid for the coal concession on Semirara island. There are a couple of things from this draft that stand out.

 

First, the financial component is proposed as being the sole determinant of the winning bid. This is a 180-degree change from the original bid terms. Second, the government seeks to dictate the volumes and price for sales to domestic coal users.

 

This framework, if adopted, could significantly affect the economics of the coal mine and also alter the bid competition dynamics between Semirara Mining (SCC PM) and San Miguel (SMC PM).

East West Bank (EW PM) announced a P9bn (US$150m) rights offering. At that amount, the issuance will raise the bank’s equity by around 11%. The bank’s CET-1 ratio is around 11.35%, with ROE around 8%.

The government’s budget deficit rose to P106bn in July, over 5x higher YoY. Spending for the month rose 20%, with primary spending up 17%. Revenue collections, on the other hand, were up just 2%.

 

For the period YT-Jul, the deficit now stands at P893bn, +14% higher YoY.

  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised the key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%
    • The rate hike was in line with expectations
    • This is the third 25bp rate hike in a row since the rate hike cycle resumed back in April
  • Despite easing headline inflation, the "preemptive move" was done due to the still volatile oil prices as well as the looming El Niño and wage hike
    • BSP Governor Eli Remolona said the central bank is hopeful that no further rate hikes will be necessary, although it remains open to further tightening if needed to bring inflation back toward its ±3% (2-4%) long-term target
  • The central bank revised its inflation estimates for 2026/27E from 6.4%/4.5% to 6.1%/5.4%
    • July CPI was 6.2%, bringing the 7M26 average inflation rate to 5.0%
      • This implies that inflation could average 7.6% for the rest of the year
      • The BSP expects headline inflation to peak in 4Q26 and ease closer to the ±3% target by 4Q27
  • The BSP also stated that economic growth is expected to rebound in 4Q26, as fundamentals for growth "are still in place"
  • For 2026, consensus expectations for inflation and rate hikes are 5.6% and one more 25bp hike to 5.25% by year-end
    • Regis expectations are 6.7% and +25bp to 5.25%, respectively
  • The fifth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled this year will be held on Oct 22

 

 

BANKS
East West Bank announces P9bn rights offer

East West Bank (EW PM) announced plans to raise up to P9bn in a stock rights offer (SRO). Proceeds will go towards “strategic growth objectives”, including wealth management, IT capex, and loan growth. The P9bn target is roughly equivalent to 11% of the bank’s total equity of P81.7bn as of 2Q26, and about 36% of the bank’s market cap of P24.9bn. The stock trades on just 0.31x P/bk (on trailing 2Q26 book).

 

It has been a challenging 1H26 for EW. Loan growth has been healthy, at +10.2%, with NIMs jumping to 8.17% in 2Q26. PPOP growth has been strong at +35%. However, rising NPLs as seen a 66% surge in provision expense, with credit costs at over 500bps for 1H26. Recall that 85% of EW’s loan book is in consumers, with close to 50% in unsecured credit card and personal/salary loans, which have been hit by the slowing economy and high inflation. As a result, NPAT for 1H26 fell 17% to P3.4bn, with 2Q26 alone down 32% YoY to P1.6bn. ROEs also fell to around 8% in 1H26, from the 13% level in 2H25.

 

With the high asset growth, and reduced profitability, EW’s capital ratios have been under some downward pressure. Its Solo CET-1 ratio fell to 11.35% in 2Q26, the lowest among banks we follow closely, and hovering near the 10.0-10.5% minimum (depending on DSIB level).

 

As mentioned, the stock trades on 0.31x trailing P/bk. This year, the bank paid a cash div of P0.82/sh, for a 7.65% dividend yield.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Government posts P106bn budget deficit in July
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P106bn in July, wider by 462% YoY as spending outpaced revenues
    • This brings the 7M26 deficit to P893bn, 14% wider vs the same period last year
  • Revenue collections in July rose a modest 2% YoY given the sharp decline in non-tax revenues
    • Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections picked up to +7% from +5% the previous month
    • Customs collections moderated to +7% growth from +12% the previous month
    • Non-tax revenues fell 40% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances
      • Recall that dividends from GOCCs (Gov't-Owned and Controlled Corporations) were remitted early this year in Feb
  • Gov't spending was up 20% YoY, picking up from the 4% growth in June
    • Primary spending for the month accelerated to +17% vs +5% the month prior
      • The acceleration in spending was attributed to direct payments for various rail transport projects as well as the continued implementation of the UPLIFT framework projects to address rising oil prices
      • Recall that primary spending declined YoY for six consecutive months from July 2025 to Jan 2026, following President Marcos' disclosure of the flood control scandal during his SONA
    • Interest payments rose 29% YoY
  • The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66trn (-5.4% of GDP), 5% wider YoY from P1.58trn (-5.6% of GDP) in 2025

 

 

MINING
DOE publishes draft bidding framework for natural resources

The Department of Energy posted the draft framework that will likely govern the bidding of the Semirara coal contract and other natural resources (see here). Note that this draft is open to public comments until today and is still subject to change. There are several material changes from the original framework published in February, including the following:

 

  • Point 6.3 – There is now a “minimum economic threshold” based on the government’s own valuation of confirmed reserves, although the document did not specify the valuation methodology. Oddly, this threshold amount will not be disclosed to the bidders, but financial offers must meet or exceed this threshold.

 

  • Point 6.9 – The financial offer, essentially the royalty payment to the government, will now be the sole determinant of the winning bidder. The basis for determining royalty payments remains unclear, including whether these will be based on gross or net revenues and whether they will be fixed or subject to minimum annual payments. Based on the draft, the terms of the royalty appear to be left to the bidder’s discretion.  Recall that the previous auction gave preference to the most technically and financially competent operator. 

 

  • Point 7.4 – Bidders must also commit to supplying a defined portion of production to domestic end-users, with the government seeking to dictate both volume and pricing.  This provision could eliminate any transfer pricing opportunities for bidders that are vertically integrated, as well as have implications on potential external buyers.
