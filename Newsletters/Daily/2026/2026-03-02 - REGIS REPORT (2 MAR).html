The US/Israel strike on Iran, and the latter’s retaliations, could have wide-ranging effects globally, depending on how long either side is willing/able to continue. For the Philippines, the Middle East is crucial for OFW employment and remittances. Any prolonged conflict will also bring up oil prices, which will have its impact on inflation, and on the Current Account.

 

  • The Middle East accounts for 18% of total OFW remittances -- $6.5bn out of $35.6bn -- for FY2025. That’s roughly 1.3% of GDP.
  • According to the PSA, roughly 1mn Filipinos are formally employed there, or just under half of the official 2.1mn overseas workers (excluding permanent residents and migrants, and of course informal).
  • The Philippines imports virtually all of our petroleum product, worth about $17bn in 2025. For reference, the BSP forecasts the country’s Current Account Deficit for 2026 at $15bn or about 3% of GDP
  • Global oil prices also have a direct and indirect impact on inflation. (See chart)

 

Here’s hoping for a fast (not just furious) Operation Epic Fury.

SM Investments (SM PM) (+) reported that net income rose 10% to P90.5bn in FY25, which would be above Street and Regis estimates.  SM Prime (SMPH PM) and BDO (BDO PM) both already reported earnings that were in line with / modestly ahead of estimates.  SM Retail, on the other hand, delivered revenue growth of 5% and net income growth of 1%.  The company will hold its briefing later today.

 

Meanwhile, the two water utilities – Manila Water (MWC PM) and Maynilad (MYNLD PM) - held their FY25 results briefings last Friday.  Even though MWC’s FY25 recurring profit to common of P13.2bn (+6%) appeared below Bloomberg consensus’ estimate of P14.6bn, it nonetheless declared dividends of P2/sh, which was higher than Street estimates and above its minimum payout ratio of 35%.

 

Both utilities surprised positively on dividends, though Maynilad appears better positioned to sustain payouts in the near-to medium-term, with a gross debt-to-equity ratio of 0.8x.  In contrast, MWC’s parent gross gearing of 1.4x, while comfortably below its covenant ratio of 3x, arguably leaves less headroom to further ramp up capital returns, especially given its M&A pipeline.

The Department of Energy (DOE) officially launched the bidding for the mining rights over ten (10) coal blocks on Semirara island last Friday. The DOE’s requirements for interested applicants centered on technical specifications (e.g. work program, financial capacity, track record) but the disclosures were absent of an evaluation criteria. We understand the launch was well-attended by various mining and power firms, including Meralco (MER PM), Nickel Asia (NIKL PM), and San Miguel (SMC PM), but judging from the qualification requirements of the DOE, Semirara (SCC PM), is in the best position to win the contract on April 28. 

 

+++

 

Shareholders of International Entertainment Corporation (1009 HK), which owns and operates an integrated resort in Manila,  approved the HK$1.6bn convertible notes agreement with DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM). This will pave the way for DigiPlus to own 54% of IEC (when the notes are converted). Integrated resort operators are charged c. 9ppt less on their e-casino GGR than pure online operators. If this were to be applied to DigiPlus Interactive’s trailing 12 months operations (including 54% of the net loss of IEC), we estimate that net profit would have been about P19.4bn (c. 40% higher than the actual trailing 12M profit of P14.0bn).

Mr. Raphael Lotilla stepped down as Environment Secretary and will be replaced by Undersecretary Juan Miguel Cuna, who has been with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) since 2008. The replacement of the Environment Secretary could cause a delay in the Presidential Proclamation of SM Prime’s (SMPH PM) Pasay 360 project.

MACRO
BSP expects February inflation to accelerate and settle within 2.3-3.1%
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects Feb inflation to fall within 2.3-3.1%
    • Consensus expects Feb inflation to be 2.4%
  • If the BSP forecast holds, this would mark a jump from Jan 2026's 2.0% print and mark a 13-month high since Jan 2025's 2.9%
    • BSP expectations for Feb inflation are around the higher-end of the BSP's 2-4% long-term target range
      • For 2026, BSP expectations for inflation this year is 3.6%
  • Upward price pressures are higher prices of rice, fish, petroleum and electricity prices
    • These may have been partially offset by lower prices of vegetables, fruits, meats, and the appreciation of the peso
  • For 2026, consensus and Regis expectations are at 2.7% and 3.0%, respectively
  • Feb inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on Mar 5 (Thu)
  • The second out of the six Monetary Board meetings will be held on Apr 23

 

 

MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to $4.0bn in Jan
  • The Jan trade deficit narrowed to US$4.0bn, contracting 18% YoY, reflecting the growth of exports and the decline in imports
  • Imports for the month fell by 3% YoY to US$11.1bn, a reversal from the 7% growth in Dec
    • The reversal was primarily driven by the drop in fuel imports (-25%) and raw and intermediate goods (-8%)
    • Notably, imports of consumer goods fell 6%, the fifth consecutive month of a YoY decline—the longest streak since the pandemic in 2020
  • Exports rose by 8% YoY to US$7.1bn, the thirteenth consecutive month of YoY growth but moderating from the 23% increase in Dec
    • Electronics exports jumped by 19% YoY to $4.0bn
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 3% to US$3.1bn
