REGIS REPORT

13 February 2026

Container throughput at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port in Mexico, CMSA, rose 4% YoY to 143k TEUs in January, broadly within expectations. The data also indicates that the Mexican government’s higher tariffs on Chinese imports have not adversely affected demand. We estimate CMSA accounts for 25% of ICT’s group EBITDA. 

 

+++

 

After cutting reserve requirement ratios (RRR) for bank deposits, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) also announced it is also reserve requirements for other instruments (effective Feb 27). These other instruments include bank bonds, long-term negotiable certificates of time deposits (LTNCD) as well trust and fiduciary accounts.

 

For the banks, it will mean a modest reduction in intermediation costs. The impact on the broader economy of the increased liquidity should be even more minimal.

 

+++

 

Wilcon (WLCON PM) says it is adding eight new stores this year after ending 2025 with 106 stores. The home improvement retailer also said that 4Q numbers saw some improvement compared to 9M. REGIS is forecasting a 4% increase in revenue to P35.7bn and a 6% decline in profit to P2.4bn for FY25.

 

+++

 

Foreign ownership of listed Philippine stocks inched up 6bp to 19.5% in Jan. For the narrower subset of PSEi components, foreign ownership rose 9bp to 23.5%. The increase is consistent with our estimate of US$225m of net foreign inflows for the month.

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rise 4% in January
  • Container volumes at ICTSI’s Mexico port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), rose by 4% YoY to 143k TEUs in January
    • Combined volumes at the port of Manzanillo rose 8% YoY
  • We’ve modeled a 13% YoY increase in CMSA’s FY26 volumes to 1.75mn TEUs, given the port’s ~20% capacity expansion
    • To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year should average 146k TEUs per month
    • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 12x EV/EBITDA and 21x PER vs peer average (13x EV/EBITDA and 19x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA/19x PER)

 

 

 

MACRO
BSP lowers reserve requirements on bank bonds and other instruments
  • The BSP issued Circular No. 1229, which will lower the reserve ratio on bonds and other instruments starting the reserve week of Feb. 27
  • The BSP reduced the reserve ratio on bonds issued by universal, commercial and digital banks to 2 percent from 3 percent
  • It also removed the 4 percent reserve ratio on long-term negotiable certificates of time deposits
  • Reserve requirements on trust and other fiduciary accounts were also lowered to zero
    • Previous requirements:
    • Universal and commercial banks – 17%
    • Thrift banks – 9%
    • Rural banks – 4%
  • The BSP also removed the 6 percent reserve requirement on bonds, mortgage bonds and chattel mortgage certificates issued by thrift banks
  • “Based on BSP’s computation, the excess liquidity that will be released based on these reductions in the required reserves is not expected to pose significant impact on monetary aggregates and economic indicators,”
CONSUMER
WLCON targets 8 new store openings this year
  • Wilcon Depot Inc. (WLCON) eyes to open eight new stores this year—with three already opened last Jan, five more stores are set to open in the remainder of the year
    • WLCON opened six new stores last year bringing the total store count to 106 by end-2025
    • The eight new stores slated to open this year will be a mix of traditional depots and smaller formats
  • COO Rosemarie Bosch-Ong guided that the company saw improvement in financial performance towards the end of 2025
  • As of 9M25, WLCON's net sales rose 3% to P26.3bn while net profit fell 12% to P1.9bn
  • WLCON is trading at 10.5X FY26E P/E and offers 5% dividend yield
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market inches up in January
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 6bps MoM to 19.5% (in January) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index increased 9bps MoM to 23.5%
  • Foreign activity reversed to net buying of US$226mn, from net foreign selling of US$202mn in December
  • Among the PSEi constituents, 17 saw increases in foreign ownership while 13 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • GTCAP – Foreign ownership increased 1.04 percentage points MoM to 24.2%, as net foreign buying hit US$19.1mn, the third highest for the month.
    • AC – foreign ownership climbed 47bps MoM to 26.5%, as net foreign buying reached US$28.2mn, the second highest for the month.   
    • PLUS – foreign ownership decreased 77bps MoM to 22.3%, as it saw the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$15.5mn). 
    • GLO – foreign ownership declined 36bps MoM to 52.7%, as net foreign selling hit US$10.4mn, the second highest for the month.
