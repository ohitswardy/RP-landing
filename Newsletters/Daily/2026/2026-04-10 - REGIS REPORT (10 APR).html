REGIS REPORT

10 April 2026

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) reported that after the substantial decline in 4Q25, Metro Manila residential sales rebounded in 1Q26, rising 19% YoY and 65% QoQ to 7,732 units. The increase appears to have come from mid-income units. While the improvement in headline sales is a good sign, we would caution against being overly bullish for now since the sales figures LPC uses are before sales backouts.  

 

 

 

+++

 

Electricity volumes for Meralco (MER PM) dropped 1.8% YoY in 1Q26, which is being blamed partly on cooler weather. The weakness, however, was not isolated to the residential users, whose usage is traditionally more sensitive to temperature changes. Volumes for both commercial and industrial users were also lower YoY.

 

This does not bode well for economic activity in the quarter. One silver lining from this demand weakness, though, is that it will further diminish the possibility of a supply crunch arising from fuel (esp. LNG) shortages.

 

+++

 

The government is exploring options to avert an imminent transport crisis caused by soaring fuel prices. Diesel prices have trebled since early March are now such that public utility vehicles (PUV) lose money every time they ply their routes, as passenger fares have been frozen. The measures being considered will all require significant funding.

 

One government measure already announced -- a P10 (US$0.17) per liter fuel subsidy – is unlikely to have any appreciable impact. The government is also exploring ways to directly pay PUV operators to continue plying their routes.  

 

Meanwhile, the Dept of Trade & Industry (DTI) says an incentive program worth as much as P60bn (US$1bn) is being considered to encourage the location of manufacturing of electrified vehicles (EV) in the country. Such a program could be announced by the President in his State of the Nation Address in July.

POWER
Meralco energy sales drop 1.8% YoY in 1Q26
  • Meralco reported its distribution unit’s (DU) energy sales volumes fell 1.8% YoY to 12,273 GWH
    • Residential and commercial sales declined as colder weather curbed consumption
    • Industrial sales also slipped, weighed by operational constraints and macroeconomic headwinds affecting steel and plastics, along with limited output from embedded generators
  • Meralco is forecasting DU energy sales to rise by a modest 3% in 2026, after dropping 0.65% YoY in 2025
PROPERTY
LPC: 1Q26 Metro Manila residential sales rise YoY and QoQ
  • Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) reported that Metro Manila condominium sales increased 19% YoY and 65% QoQ to 7,732 units
  • Unsold condominium inventory was flattish QoQ and YoY at about 81,000 units
    • We estimate this is equivalent to about 3 years of reservation sales (based on trailing 12M)
  • Meanwhile, Metro Manila office vacancy was flat QoQ and YoY at 18%
  • Nationwide net office take-up fell 21% QoQ to 133,000sqm due to weaker net demand from both BPO and traditional tenants
  • Although net take-up of both BPO and traditional tenants also fell YoY, total net office take-up increased 53% YoY due to incremental POGO closures in 1Q25
    • BPO net office take-up fell to 51,000sqm, from 85,000sqm in 4Q25 and 130,000sqm in 1Q25
    • Net take-up from traditional tenants fell to 82,000sqm, from 84,000sqm in 4Q25 and 97,000sqm in 1Q25

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Government to contract PUVs, provide P10/liter fuel subsidy
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in a video statement has announced a P10/liter fuel subsidy for drivers and operators of public utility vehicles (PUVs)
    • The P10/liter fuel subsidy would be implemented for three months starting next week with a 150 liter cap per week, this implies that PUVs can avail of weekly savings of P1,500 and a total savings of P18,000 for the program period
    • The subsidy is separate from the Dept. of Transportation's (DOTr) one-time fuel subsidy of P1,500-P10,000 as well as the Dept. of Social Welfare and Development's (DSWD) P5,000 cash relief for transport workers
  • The fuel subsidy will be rolled out for PUVs operating in Metro Manila, initially for those in Commonwealth Avenue then other routes such as Quezon Avenue and Marcos Highway, and eventually other routes nationwide
    • The fuel subsidy will only be implemented in legitimate gas stations, approved and monitored by the Dept. of Energy (DOE)
  • The President has also announced that the DOTr will implement a nationwide service contracting program for PUVs starting April 15, which will cover around 50,000 PUVs and 1,000 operators, and benefit up to 15mn passengers
    • Contracted PUVs will be paid P40 to P100/km on top of their regular income, and will cover off-peak hours
    • The gov't will also provide 20% fare discount for commuters traveling through routes that are connected to trains and primary bus routes
  • The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) separately stated that there is P238bn, sourced from the 2026 budget, in available funding will be used to support fuel subsidies, cash relief assistance, and other targeted social protection programs
MACRO
Government eyes additional revenues from excise, global taxes
  • The Department of Finance (DOF) is considering eight “new and revised” tax measures to bolster its revenues
    • According to Finance Secretary Frederick Go
  • The imposition of a global minimum tax on foreign corporations could be the single largest source of additional revenues
    • The DOF earlier said it is weighing a domestic top-up tax to align the country with the OECD framework, under which multinational enterprises with global revenues of more than €750 million must pay a 15% global minimum tax
  • It is also looking at additional excise taxes on several industries that are already being taxed with excise taxes
  • No other details were provided but Secretary Go said that it aims to complete its review within 30 days
MACRO
Dept. of Budget and Management to order 20% cut in gov't agencies' non-essential spending
  • The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that it will issue a circular ordering gov't agencies to reduce non-essential spending by 20% due to the need for additional fiscal space
    • The measure was said to expect to generate savings of between P12.8bn-P25.6bn depending on the level of compliance
  • Budget Secretary Rolando Toledo cited measures including limiting official travel, maximizing virtual meetings, energy conservation, and streamlining opex
    • Education, health, social, general public service, defense ops with frontline services were said to be exempted
CONSUMER
Manufacturers, retailers agree with DTI to extend price freeze till end-April
  • The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that the ongoing price freeze of basic and prime commodities will be extended to April 30 from April 16 previously following regular meetings with manufacturers and retailers
  • DTI Secretary Ma. Cristina Roque stated that prices of basic goods under the DTI's list remain stable as of late with regular price checks to ensure continued compliance
MACRO
Government eyes P60bn fiscal support for EVs
  • The Department of Trade and Industry is looking to provide a fiscal package worth P60bn to incentivize investments in EV production in the country (or Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy, EVIS)
  • In the proposed EVIS, there will be four slots with each participant receiving P15bn worth of fiscal support in exchange for investments in EV manufacturing of four-wheeled EVs
    • Under the proposal, EVIS will not set a production volume target for the participants
    • Recall that the previous CARS program only provided up to P9bn worth of incentives for each qualified participant, with a production target of at least 200k units for each enrolled model
  • The DTI expects to issue an Executive Order before the President’s State of the Nation Address in July
MACRO
Gov’t infrastructure spending drops 23% YoY in December; FY25 spending drops 14% YoY
  • Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 14% YoY to P1.32tn in FY25
    • Derived infrastructure spending in December stood at P126bn, down 23% YoY (+83% MoM)
    • In 4Q25 alone, infrastructure spending fell 30% YoY and 11% QoQ to P283bn
    • Apart from the delays in payment settlements for public work projects amid the flood control corruption scandal, adverse weather conditions in 4Q were also cited as a reason
  • Note that the government recently reduced its infra spend target for 2026 to ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) from P1.56tn previously (~5.1% of GDP)

 

 

MACRO
PH dollar reserves at $107.5bn end-Mar
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Mar fell 5% MoM to US$107.5bn from US$113.3bn the previous month
    • The BSP stated that the MoM drop in the GIR was primarily due to central bank foreign exchange operations and the correction in gold prices in the month of March
      • The March GIR figure is a seven-month low
    • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.9x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.1x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn
