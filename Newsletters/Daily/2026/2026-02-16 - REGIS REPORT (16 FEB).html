REGIS REPORT

16 February 2026

Jollibee (JFC PM) issued a press release today, announcing solid 4Q top line numbers across its units. The domestic business saw systemwide sales (SWS) growth of 9.7% on the back of 5.0% SSSG for the quarter. International SWS, meanwhile, rose 15.4% as SSSG rose 5.5%.

 

The 4Q results place full year SWS growth at 16.6%, which is well above the guidance for the full year (+8-12%).

 

+++

 

The Dept of Energy (DOE) announced that it will auction off the right to operate the coal mine/s on Semirara Island instead of extending the existing contract of Semirara Mining (SCC PM). The decision is likely to jolt the market, which appears to have been expecting an extension to the existing arrangement, which expires July 2027.

 

An auction for the right to operate the mines on Semirara Island is arguably the most politically acceptable option for the government. It’s not known now what the criteria / parameters will be for the selection of the winner of the auction, but the DOE Secretary said that SCC will be allowed to join the auction.

 

For SCC, the loss of the hoped-for extension creates significant uncertainty not only for its mining operations but also its power plants, which feed on coal from those mines. We previously estimated a valuation of P21/share if the coal operating agreement is not renewed beyond July 2027.

 

Having said the above, SCC is likely to have a significant advantage over any new bidder for the contract. Not only has it been running the mines for decades, it also has the equipment and facilities already on site (and already significantly depreciated, accounting-wise), a significant advantage in terms of marginal costs and lead time. We previously estimated a value of P35/share for SCC should it receive a 15-year extension on the current terms.

 

We note the government’s move to bid out the contract could also have an effect on SCC’s decision to pay dividends, given the higher level of uncertainty surrounding its future operations.

 

+++

 

First Gen (FGEN PM) is taking a 40% stake in the two hydro power projects being developed by Prime Infrastructure, the infrastructure holding company of Enrique Razon. The Wawa and Ahunan hydro projects will have a combined 2,000MW capacity. First Gen will pay P75bn (US$1.3bn) for the stake.

 

Recall First Gen sold a controlling interest in its gas-fired assets (which include ~2,000W of power capacity) to Prime Infrastructure for P50bn a few months ago.

 

+++

 

The 4Q results of City of Dreams Manila were quite weak, with gross gaming revenue (GGR) for the property falling 11% QoQ and 26% YoY to P6.5bn. This result mirrors the 4Q numbers from one of the other key casino properties in the area, Okada Manila. This also likely signals a challenging 4Q for Bloomberry (BLOOM PM).

MINING
Government opts to re-bid Semirara’s coal operating contract before end-2026
  • The Department of Energy (DOE) has decided to re-bid Semirara’s (SCC PM) coal operating contract (COC), after the Department of Justice ruled the current agreement cannot be legally extended
    • DOE Secretary Sharon Garin said that the agency intends to conduct a formal bidding process before the end of 2026, ahead of the existing contract’s expiry July 14, 2027
    • SCC will be eligible to bid and holds a “competitive technical advantage”
    • Secretary Garin said that the agency’s objective is to appoint the most qualified operator, not merely the best financial offer
    • Semirara accounts for over 20% of the country’s coal consumption
  • Coal mining accounts for ~60% of SCC’s profits
    • We reckon the company may postpone declaring special dividends until after the bidding is finalized
    • Assuming SCC’s dividend policy of paying out 20% of prior year’s reported profits, regular dividends could only reach ~P0.63/sh
    • We believe SCC has a competitive advantage over a re-bid, given its long track record, as well as its ownership of mining equipment and infrastructure in Semirara island
    • Given that coal is a natural resource, any competing operator must be at least 60%-owned by Filipinos
    • We previously estimated a non-extension will result in a valuation of P21/sh while an extension puts the valuation at P37/sh
  • SCC is a 57%-owned unit of DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) and accounts for ~50% of NAV and profits
    • DMC trades at a 10% discount to its market-based NAV of P12/sh

TELCOS
NTC sets registration requirements for Konektadong Pinoy entrants
  • The National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) in a memorandum circular outlined registration requirements for prospective Konektadong Pinoy entrants
  • The memorandum outlined the following requirements for applicants:
    • A minimum asset requirement of P1bn for international gateway facility applicants, P100mn for core or backbone network applicants, P10mn for middle-mile provider applicants, and P500,000 for last-mile providers
    • All applicants, except middle mile providers, are required to pay a performance bond equivalent to at least 10% of the applicant's projected capex for the first two years, but not exceeding P5mn
    • A debt-to-asset ratio of 70% for all applicants of all segments
  • The NTC will periodically review the minimum asset requirements, leverage ratios, and related financial thresholds every three years or shorter intervals when necessary
  • Recall that the Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) stated that one Philippine company has formally applied to offer internet services following the enactment of the Konektadong Pinoy Act
    • DICT Secretary Henry Aguda said that the application is under review
