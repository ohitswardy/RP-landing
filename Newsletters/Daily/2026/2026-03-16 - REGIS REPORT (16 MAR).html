Here’s today’s review of the latest for FY25 corporate results:

 

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) – FY25 core income dipped 4% to P11.6bn even as revenue grew 4% to P168bn. While the overall results were a disappointment relative to expectations, we note visible improvements in its Domestic BCF business, which remains its largest and most important segment, for 4Q. This recovery appears to have been sustained into 1Q26. That rebound, however, seems under threat now given the impact of the war in Iran.

On the other hand, the company’s residential unsold inventory remains high, at P44.8bn, which we estimate is equivalent to 8.7 years of reservation sales.

FY25 core profit up 7% to P48.3bn – Above Regis but below Street estimates

FY25 net income (before pref divs) rose 46% YoY to P61.4bn. Stripping out the revaluation gain from AC Ventures and the gain from the Ayala Land’s (ALI PM) sale of ATC mall (here), core profit rose 7% to P48bn, below Street estimates but above Regis’. 4Q25 core profit rose 41% YoY to P11.7bn, supported by improved operating results at BPI (here), Globe (here), and ACEN (here). Two of its unlisted units, AC Mobility and AC Health, saw improved operations but remained in the red.

 

AC Mobility: Unit sales up 30% QoQ but transition-related costs weigh on 4Q

AC Mobility’s FY25 core loss declined by 17% to P386mn, driven by a fivefold increase in BYD unit sales to 25k, capturing the third largest market share at 6% (up from ~1% in 2024). Despite a 30% QoQ jump in 4Q sales volumes, core losses widened to P404mn due to transition costs from its exit from Honda and VW dealerships, as well as clearance of aging Kia inventory. AC Mobility is aiming to reach profitability in the next 1-2 years and recently extended its distribution agreement with BYD by another five years effective Jan 2026.

 

AC Health: Nearing breakeven

Excluding one-offs, we estimate AC Health’s core losses narrowed to P68mn in 2025 (vs P352mn in 2024) as EBITDA rose 68% YoY to PP1.4bn, behind a 36% jump in revenues. AC Health is guiding for 20–30% revenue growth, 30–40% EBITDA expansion, and core income profitability by end-2026.

 

Group capex guidance at P220-230bn, bulk for ACEN

Management is guiding for group capex to reach P220bn this year, up from P181bn in 2025, of which 40% was attributable to ACEN. At the parent level capex, a significant but undisclosed portion will be allocated to support ACEN’s equity raising. AC also plans to allocate P3.7bn for AC Logistics to finalize the acquisition of a cold chain solutions provider, P3.9bn for its retail ventures, and an additional P5bn for M&A opportunities.

MACRO
Current account deficit drops by 50% YoY in 4Q25; YTD CA deficit lower by 12% YoY to US$16.3bn
  • The current account deficit plunged 50% YoY, 39% QoQ to US$3.2bn (equivalent to 3.3% of GDP)
    • Driven by a narrower trade-in-goods deficit, higher net receipts from trade in services, and the continued modest increase in remittances
    • Trade deficit declined by 14% YoY to US$17.5bn due to strength in electronic exports (+87%), machinery exports (+52%), and gold exports (+54%)
    • Total estimated BPO export revenues, including computer and other business services, reached US$9.4bn in 4Q25, a 3.8% increase from US$9.1bn in 4Q24
  • This brings FY25 current account to a narrower (-12% YoY) deficit of US$16.3bn, equivalent to 3.3% of GDP
    • The narrower YoY deficit is due to (a) higher electronic exports (+20%) as well as gold (+76%) and other mineral exports (+27%)
    • The FY25 CA deficit figure is wider than expected and is behind the BSP’s year-end CA deficit target of US$15.5bn (3.2% of GDP)

 

 

 

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air’s pax volumes rise by 8% in February, +7% in 2M26
  • Cebu Air saw its passenger volumes rise by 8% YoY to 2.3mn in February, with domestic pax volumes up 8% YoY to 1.7mn while international pax volumes up 9% to 610k
    • Systemwide seat load factors remained healthy at 85.6% in February, though lower than 86.8% achieved in Feb 2025
  • This brings 2M26 pax volumes up 7% to 5.1mn, trending below our FY26E forecast of 31mn (+17% YoY)
    • Every 5% cut to our pax volume forecast reduces EBITDA by 18% and profit by 90%, holding other things constant

 

 

 

TRANSPORT
Government moves to insulate airlines amidst oil price shock
  • The Department of Transportation, together with airline regulators, ordered to (a) reduce passenger service fees across all airports and (b) shorten the evaluation and implementation of fuel surcharge to 15 days (from 1 month)
    • Moves aim to alleviate the burden on both airlines and passengers affected by the spike in jet fuel costs
  • Fuel accounts for 36% of CEB’s cash costs and 30% of operating costs
    • We are forecasting jet kero prices will average US$81/bbl vs. spot at US$198
    • Every 10% increase in jet kero prices will reduce EBITDA by 10% and profit by 50%, holding other things constant
