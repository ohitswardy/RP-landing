Majority of the results in today’s 2Q round-up delivered disappointing numbers. The tougher macro environment from higher energy prices, shown in the weak 2Q GDP numbers, is evident. Check out our summaries:

 

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – 1H core income declined 7% to P22bn as a number of its key subsidiaries (BPI, ALI and ACEN) reported softer-than-expected numbers. For 2Q alone, profit amounted to P11bn (-12% YoY). Earnings from its smaller businesses, including AC Mobility, also delivered lower earnings, collectively.  More below.

 

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (-) – Revenues rose 2% in 1H while core income declined 3% to P17.1bn based on our estimates. Mobile and broadband revenue barely grew in the quarter, while Enterprise was up 5%. Contributions from fintech unit Maya were slightly lower QoQ due to “one-off” items but PLDT remarked that the positive earnings trajectory for Maya remains on track.

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (+) – 1H profits grew 11% to P5.9bn on sales growth of +13%. The income in 2Q was slightly lower QoQ due to a rise in “Other” expenses (interest accretion on capitalized leases), but the 1H numbers are still mildly ahead of estimates.   • Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) (-) – Profits for 1H rose +4% to P1.8bn, which appears mildly behind estimates. Despite this, the underlying metrics seem to be looking good: revenues grew 15% for both 1H and 2Q.   • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (=) – Reported 1H26 core profit fell 14% YoY to P15.5bn, lagging consensus but in line with Regis’ expectations. 2Q26 core profit declined 19% YoY to P7.6bn, dragged by Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), which offset stronger contributions from Metrobank and Metro Pacific. TMP’s 2Q26 profits fell 29% QoQ, 60% YoY, weighed by lower ICE vehicle sales, a weaker PHP, and an unfavorable product mix. We should get more color from management during its briefing later at 2:30pm.

 

During its briefing, Megaworld (MEG PM) said that thanks to its strong 2Q26 reservation sales (+12% QoQ and 20% YoY), unsold residential inventory fell 6% QoQ and YoY to P19bn, equivalent to about a year’s worth of residential sales (before cancellations). Furthermore, the company declared a dividend of P0.117/share, which is 12% higher YoY and represents a dividend yield of 5.1%. Management is also considering a higher payout ratio in the future.

Ayala Corp - Profits from core units decline in 2Q26 while other segments show modest gains

1H26 core profit drops 7% YoY to P22.1bn – Behind estimates

Ayala Corp. reported 1H26 core profit fell 7% YoY to P22.1bn, which suggests that profit in 2Q26 alone dropped 12% YoY to P10.9bn. The results trail both consensus and Regis estimates. Profit declines were recorded across its listed units in 2Q26 (see results commentaries for BPI, Ayala Land and ACEN), alongside weaker collective profits from its smaller businesses.

AC Mobility: FX pressures weigh on 2Q; BYD market share climbs to ~8%

Derived 2Q26 core profit from AC Mobility fell 74% YoY to P52mn, weighed by a weaker PHP:USD exchange rate and weaker ICE sales of Kia and Isuzu. Despite intensifying competition from new, mainly Chinese brands, we estimate BYD sales volumes rose 14% QoQ/70% YoY to 8.4k units in 2Q26, lifting its market share to 7.7%. Management maintained optimism in reaching profit break-even by year-end, although it admits that operating conditions remain challenging.

AC Health: Solid topline momentum boosts EBITDA and trims losses

AC Health reported a wider core net loss of P167mn in 1H26, mainly due to the recognition of bonuses in 1Q. Implied 2Q26 core loss narrowed 41% YoY to P24mn, buoyed by a 30% YoY increase in revenues. Topline growth was driven by sustained gains in patient census and higher average spending across its clinics and hospitals, as well as +15% SSSG in its Pharma retail business.

IMI 2Q26 core profit up 120% YoY; AC Logistics turnaround continues

Its electronics manufacturing arm, IMI (IMI PM), reported a 120% YoY increase in profit to US$9.5mn in 2Q26, following the disposal of underperforming assets. Recurring revenues grew 4% YoY in 1H and 8% in 2Q, driven by higher volumes. Meanwhile, AC Logistics’ losses narrowed 58% YoY to P264mn in 1H, supported by continued cost rationalization and the exit from unprofitable ventures.

Ayala Land share buyback

Following P3.6bn worth of Ayala Land share purchases as of Aug 5, AC has P1.4bn remaining under its buyback program and will need board approval to expand the size.

SEVN 1H net profit +4% YoY, revenue +15% YoY
  • Philippine Seven Corporation [PSC] (SEVN PM) reported 1H26 net profit of P1.8bn (+4% YoY), behind estimates due to a 38% surge in net interest expense in 2Q
    • Higher net interest expense was driven by the accretion on lease liabilities and lower interest income in the quarter
  • Key operating metrics were in line—1H operating profit increased 8% YoY to P3.0bn, while revenues grew 15% YoY to P53.5bn
    • Opex grew faster than sales on expansion-related costs as well as higher utilities and logistics expenses
    • Ongoing mitigation measures have been implemented to alleviate the continuing impact of higher fuel prices on margins
  • 2Q26 revenue growth remained solid at +15% YoY (+14% YoY in 1Q26)
  • 2Q SSSG was +7.8%, an acceleration from the +4.4% in 1Q
    • The improvement in SSSG was led by 5% growth in average ticket size as well as strong growth in Metro Manila (SSSG in 2Q: +12.0%, 1Q: +3.9%)
    • Card payment terminals now cover 98% of all stores (83% in 1Q), management mentioned that the nationwide rollout contributed to the strong sales figures and recovery in certain categories (non-alcoholic beverages and tobacco)
  • More details after the briefing on Aug 18 (Tuesday, 2:45pm)—register here https://events.pse.com.ph/pse-star-1h/

PIZZA 1H core profit down 26% YoY, revenue up 9% YoY

1H core profit falls 26%. PIZZA's net income amounted to P315mn for 1H26, down 26% YoY, behind consensus expectations. 1H revenue rose 9% to P8.2bn. For 2Q26 alone, core profit fell 17% while sales increased 7% YoY. Profit fell given margin pressure from elevated utilities costs, store rollout expenses, and marketing activities.

 

Full-service brands remain challenged in 2Q. Revenues grew 7% to P4.2bn, while SWS rose 11% YoY to P6.6bn. SSSG decelerated into negative territory (-1.0%) from the flat growth figure in 1Q. Full-service brands, Shakey's and Peri-Peri, continued to struggle on flattish and negative SSSG, respectively. Weakness in the two brands offset resilience from Potato Corner which saw SSS grow mid-single digit. The network restructuring of Peri-Peri is still underway (nine stores closed in 2Q) in order to shutter underperforming stores and preserve cash.

 

Challenging outlook for the rest of the year. Management stated that July demand showed signs of improvement before heavier-than-usual rainfall disrupted momentum. Margins are likely to remain under pressure from elevated utility and rising labor costs, partly offset by locked-in input costs and operational efficiencies. Further price increases to offset rising input costs could weigh on demand amid still-tight consumer wallets. Net store openings were reduced to 150-200, half the usual amount.

  • The US Trade and Development Agency (USTDA) announced that the Philippines, US and Japan will stage the inaugural Luzon Economic Corridor (LEC) Investment Forum on September 10 and 11 in Manila

  • It also seeks to connect investors with infrastructure projects ready for possible financing
  • Participating countries in the forum include Australia, Canada, Denmark, France, Italy, South Korea, Sweden and the UK
