REGIS REPORT

20 April 2026

The US State Department issued a statement announcing plans for a 4,000-acre (1,600 hectares) industrial hub within a previously announced Luzon Economic Corridor. Among other things, this site will be administered as a special economic zone, under a long-term lease and where U.S laws will apply. The Philippine government doesn’t seem to have made such an announcement (yet). 

 

The deal if it indeed involves extraterritorial jurisdiction, as reported in the press, will likely need to involve Philippine Congress. And getting it through Congress will not be easy.

 

+++

 

Two consumer names held briefings late last week on their FY25/4Q25 results: Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) and restaurant operator Shakey’s (PIZZA PM). Check out the highlights below.

 

+++

 

The Dept of Agriculture (DA) is aiming to double the number of sites (currently ~900) that sell subsidized rice at P20/kg. The slashing of the budget allocation for public infrastructure has freed up funds for programs like these.

 

Meanwhile. the government has also “persuaded” key manufacturers to hold off price increases until May 10. These manufacturers previously agreed to keep prices until April 30. This could result in a short-term margin squeeze for some of these firms.

 

Both actions form part of the administration’s attempts to keep inflation, especially food inflation, in check.

MACRO
U.S. and the Philippines plan to launch 1,600-hectare economic security zone
  • United States Undersecretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg announced plans to establish a 4,000-acre (~1,600-hectare) industrial hub in the Luzon Economic Corridor of the Philippines in order "to secure vital inputs to American and global supply chain"
    • The U.S. cited the Philippines' significant reserves of nickel, copper, chromite, and cobalt
  • The "AI-native investment acceleration hub" is still conceptual with details such as which American companies will participate and what will be built in the Philippines yet to be finalized of late
    • The Trump administration will solicit companies for proposals on what to build in the hub with priority given to bids "that will help move critical minerals processing and manufacturing off Chinese suppliers"
  • The US will occupy the hub rent-free and will administer it as a special economic zone
    • The hub will have diplomatic-style immunity similar to a U.S. embassy and operate under U.S. common law with a two-year lease renewable for up to 99 years
  • The agreement is part of the U.S.' Pax Silica initiative, with the Philippines joining as the 13th signatory, as the U.S. aims to reduce dependence on China-linked materials used for semiconductors, AI technology, and critical minerals
CONSUMER
SEVN 4Q/FY25 Briefing key takeaways
  • 4Q SSSG growth momentum carried over into 1Q. Philippine Seven in 4Q25 posted 3.1% growth in SSS, a recovery after four consecutive quarters of negative SSSG. The company elaborated that growth momentum continued into 1Q as SSSG remained positive despite elevated inflationary pressures arising from the Middle East conflict. Management cited downtrading as a driver for growth with increasing demand for its ready-to-eat (RTE) meals as a cheaper alternative vs. restaurants. Stores situated in offices and transit hubs saw less foot traffic given higher fuel prices, partially offset by stronger demand in residential areas as consumers shifted closer to home. The addition of card payment terminals also contributed to the growth momentum with average basket size 50-60% bigger than those paying in cash.

 

  • Competition heats up but store innovations and VisMin expansion aim to minimize impact. Concerns on higher competitive pressure from hard discounters and minimarts arose as competitors continued to ramp up store openings. Management, however, emphasizes minimal customer overlap as 7-11 customers opt for 1-2 items usually consisting of an RTE meal and/or chilled beverage as opposed to others looking to purchase an entire basket of grocery goods.

 

To further mitigate the impact of competition, the company targets to launch the CQSR (CVS+QSR) format which includes more non-food items, a larger dine-in space, and kitchen to serve nearby stores. 800 stores are currently operating under CVS+, with basket size reportedly increasing as essentials are priced competitively. Overall network expansion is sought to ramp up further to 500 new stores (gross) targeted this year (vs 399 opened in 2024), with the bulk in Visayas and Mindanao as SEVN seeks to capitalize on the first mover advantage.

 

 

CONSUMER
PIZZA 4Q/FY25 Briefing key takeaways
  • Shakey's Pizza Asia Ventures' (PIZZA PM) FY25 net income fell 32% to P820mn as gross margin contracted 2.3ppt to 22.9% on expansion-related costs. This offset the growth in SWS (+14% to P24.8bn) and revenue (+11% to P16.1bn). As of end-2025 the total store network stood at 2,970 as the company added (net) 351 stores.

 

  • Expansion-led top line growth but SSSG remains lackluster. 4Q SWS grew 14% YoY, while revenue rose 9% YoY as expansion propped up sales with 163 stores opened in the fourth quarter alone. SSSG saw a slight recovery from -1% the previous quarter to +1% in 4Q. The flattish SSSG figure was weaker than expected figure following the softer-than-expected seasonal lift in the holiday season. The "healthy performance" of kiosks offset the "softness" in casual dining (Shakey's, Peri-Peri).

 

Management guided that 1Q saw double digit SWS both before and during the war, although SSSG remains muted. Potato Corner stands to be the most resilient as of late given its strong value proposition as casual dining continues to be challenged on tighter consumer budgets.

 

  • Margins contract on store rollout and advertising expenses. Gross margin contracted 3.7ppt in the quarter, driven by expansion-related costs and elevated A&P spend, with muted 4Q sales exacerbating margin pressure. Given the onset of higher input costs, the company has ensured a stable supply of raw materials having secured several months of supply while enforcing cost discipline. Price hikes are guided to be strategic and delayed to lessen the burden on already strained consumers with promotions geared to emphasize value to support demand.

 

 

MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture expands P20/kg rice program
  • The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) expanded its P20/kg rice program for this year as it targets to open 900 additional selling sites nationwide for vulnerable communities in need of affordable rice
    • The expansion would add to the 932 sites already currently selling P20 rice across the country
  • Food Terminal Inc. (FTI) has signed an agreement with the National Food Authority (NFA) to secure funding and supply commitments for 2026
    • P3bn will be allocated for the deal to procure 1.8mn 50kg bags of well-milled rice
      • Funding will be sourced from the DA's P10bn Rice-for-All program budget
  • FTI President and CEO Joseph Lo emphasized on the "significant increase" in buyers of P20/kg rice given the unstable macroeconomic environment

 

 

 

CONSUMER
Manufacturers agree with DTI to extend price freeze till May 10
  • The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that the ongoing price freeze of basic and prime commodities will be extended to May 10 from April 30 previously following commitment from manufacturers
    • This is the second extension after the price halt was initially extended from April 16 to end-April
  • Goods covered by the price freeze include canned sardines and meat, coffee, processed milk, instant noodles, bread, among others
  • Trade Secretary Ma. Cristina Roque stated that the decline in fuel prices over the past week enabled the DTI to secure another extension
