REGIS REPORT

08 May 2026

A bad run of macro data this week. After the high inflation print, came the extremely disappointing GDP growth of 2.8% for 1Q26, the slowest pace since the Covid pandemic. The sharp slowdown in personal consumption (to 3.0%) was broadly expected, though was probably worst in March considering the strong momentum in Jan/Feb seen in listed consumer companies (see results comments, below). But what continued to disappoint was public construction spending, still down 32% YoY, on extended reaction to the flood control scandal in 2025. The weak GDP print also did not help the debt-to-GDP ratio, which reached 65.2%; this is the highest ratio since 2005 (ex-Covid).

+ + +

Results! Results! As discussed above, we saw a slew of consumer companies (and Megaworld) reporting. Comments below:

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) – Revenues grew 6% while core income dipped 2% to P3.8bn in 1Q26, which we’d describe as a mild disappointment. The bright spot, however, is that Domestic BCF revenues grew 10% in the quarter even if EBIT there was only up 1%, with management indicating that early 2Q was even stronger. The recovery here is a positive sign but we are cautious about how the rest of the year will shape up as the US attack on Iran starts hitting consumer wallets.

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (+) – Sales grew a solid 15% to P23bn while net income rose a slower 10% to P2.1bn, due to higher tax rates.  The main branded business categories all continued to show respectable growth, while the OEM side grew sharply from a low base. Like many of the other consumer names, indications for April show no slowdown.

For Philippine-based investors>>Century Pacific - 1Q26: Resilient demand

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (+) – For 1Q, net profit jumped 24% to P3.3bn, while sales grew 12% to P59bn – a positive surprise for the market.  Operating profit was up 20%. The SSSG were also remarkable – Puregold stores saw SSSG of +5.4% while S&R saw an exceptional +12.0%.  The company holds its briefing this morning.
  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (=) – The company reinstituted aggressive promotions on ready-for-occupancy units, which helped to grow reservation sales, boost residential revenue, and reduce unsold inventory. Sales to foreigners also increased to 11% of reservation sales, from less than 5% a year ago. Despite lowering its residential launch plans, Megaworld is maintaining its FY26 reservation sales target of P125bn, which represents 7% growth from 2025.

MACRO
1Q26 GDP slows to +2.8% on subdued capital formation, food consumption
  • The Philippine economy grew at its slowest pace since Covid, at just +2.8% in 1Q26
    • Growth likewise fell short of consensus’ +3.3% forecast for the quarter
  • On the demand side, capital formation remained subdued, posting a -3.3% decline on top of the previous quarter’s 9.4% drop
    • Private consumption grew by a mere +3%, weighed down by food and non-alcoholic consumption, which barely grew (+0.3%)
    • Construction declined by 4.5%, extending the 9.2% drop in 4Q25, dragged down by weak government infra spending
  • On the supply side, both agriculture and industry declined by -0.2% and -0.1%, respectively, while services grew +4.5%

 

 

MACRO
Government debt inches up to P18.49trn in end-Mar; debt-to-GDP ratio highest since 2005
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 1.8% MoM (P328.4bn) to P18.49trn as of end-Mar
    • This was due to the net incurrence of new domestic as well as the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of February
    • The peso weakened to P60.74 as of end-Mar from P57.55 end-Feb
  • Domestic debt rose by 0.44% MoM (P55.4bn) to P12.53trn due to the impact of net borrowings
  • External debt increased by 4.8% MoM (P273.0bn) to P5.95trn primarily driven by unfavorable currency movements
  • The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2% as of 1Q26 from 63.2% end-2025
    • The 65.2% ratio is the highest since hitting 65.7% back in 2005
  • The Dept. of Finance's (DoF) forecasts in Aug 2025 projected the debt-to-GDP ratio to hit 61.8% in 2026, before gradually declining to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% in 2028
    • By 2029, the DoF expects the ratio to fall below 60% to 59.5%
