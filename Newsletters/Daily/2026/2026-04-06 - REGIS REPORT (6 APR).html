PROPERTY
PEZA seeks to temporarily allow 100% WFH for IT-BPO firms
  • The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) has requested the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) to temporarily allow up to 100% work-from-home (WFH) arrangements for IT-BPO firms (in PEZA accredited zones)
    • If permitted, IT-BPO firms will continue to enjoy fiscal incentives despite having up to 100% WFH arrangements
  • Under the CREATE MORE law, firms registered with incentive-granting agencies can only implement WFH for up to 50% of employees
  • However, CREATE MORE has a provision that allows higher WFH arrangements during emergencies or natural disasters
  • President Marcos declared a state of national energy emergency and urged the country to conserve energy
  • We expect this to have only minor and temporary impact on BPO demand for office space 
MARKET
MYNLD likely to be added to PSE Index during August 2026 rebalancing
  • Based on our estimates, Maynilad Water Services (MYNLD PM) will likely be added to the PSE Index during the August 2026 rebalancing
  • If MYNLD will enter the PSE Index, then there are a few candidates for removal from the PSE Index
    • We believe the most likely to be removed is Converge Information and Communications Technology Solutions (CNVRG PM)
    • Given its current market cap, we consider DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) to be the next in line for removal 
  • The PSE Index rebalancing in August 2026 will be based on the 12 months from July 2025 to June 2026
  • Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap
    • For companies that have been listed for less than 12 months (such as MYNLD), the stocks must be among the top 20% in terms of median daily trade per month for at least six months or 75% of the total number of months it has been listed, whichever is higher 
  • Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when
    • 1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or
    • 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 
  • Based on our estimates, MYNLD is ranked within the top 25
    • If this will remain the case by the June 2026, then the lowest ranked PSE Index constituent will be removed

 

 

MINING
Semirara sees high barriers for rival bidders; expresses interest in Mindanao coal mine as Plan B
  • Semirara Chairman Isidro Consunji is confident that other bidders will struggle to beat them in the upcoming auction
    • Cited that the coal mine in Semirara island is operating below sea level, a condition that is uncommon globally and thus, raises the bar for prospective bidder, given the lack of specialized expertise
    • The scale of existing equipment, which comprises a fleet of more than 300 dump trucks, poses another significant hurdle, as building capacity for a new entrant could take years
  • The company said its mining plan is designed to maintain continuous operations, prevent seawater intrusion, and mitigate adverse effects from production disruptions
  • Meanwhile, Mr. Consunji said it is looking at a coal mine in Mindanao, as a fallback option
  • The DOE is bidding out 18 coal blocks, of which ten (10) are located in Semirara island
    • Two parties are currently known to be participating in the auction: SCC and San Miguel (SMC PM)
    • Submission of applications is set on April 28
MACRO
BSP revises CAD forecasts for 2026E from 3.0% to 4.0% of GDP, same for 2027E

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has released its latest forecasts (as of 1Q25) for the country’s Balance of Payments (BOP) and Current Account Deficit (CAD). Unsurprisingly, the new projections are markedly worse than their forecasts from 4Q25, due largely to higher fuel import prices due to the Iran conflict, consistent with our assessment early in the crisis.

 

Focusing on the CAD, the 2025 preliminary data came in at $16.3bn, or 3.3% to GDP, slightly larger than the 3.2% they forecasted as recently as December 2025. About half of the miss came from tourism (travel receipts) which came in at just $8.7bn, down 6.4% YoY. But the big changes really came to their 2026E forecasts, where the BSP now sees a CAD of $20.3bn, 33% larger than the $15.3bn they forecast last quarter. As a % of GDP, that’s up to 4.0%, up from their previous forecast of 3.0% as of. Of that $5bn increase in the CAD estimate, the largest chunk ($3.6bn) comes from the goods deficit, which in turn was based on a much larger expected import bill ($137.9bn vs $130.2bn) due mostly to prevailing fuel prices. Notably, though, the BSP also nudged down their forecasts for BPO receipts to $34.8bn from $35.2bn, as they see the sector “constrained by skills shortages and the uneven transition toward greater AI exposure”.

 

The BSP also introduced their 2027E forecasts, which call for a similar CAD of 4.0% to GDP.

 

 

 

MACRO
Customs reports 5% YoY growth in collections for March
  • The Bureau of Customs (BOC), based on preliminary figures, reported that it collected P84.4bn (+5% YoY) in revenues for March
    • This surpassed its monthly collection target of P83.3bn by 1.4%
  • The Bureau aims to boost its collections through pushing reforms on digitalization, transparency and ease of doing business
  • The BOC targets to collect P1.0trn (+7% YoY) in 2026
    • Customs was said to be on track, reaching 24% (P239bn) of its FY target as end-March

 

 

MACRO
Government debt rises to P18.16trn in end-Feb
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 0.1% MoM to P18.16trn as of end-Feb
    • The increase in domestic debt was offset by the decline in external issuances as the USD-PHP rate strengthened to P58.3 end-Feb from P59.2 end-Jan
  • The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.7% and 31.3% being external borrowings
    • Domestic debt increased by 1.2% MoM to P12.48trn
    • External debt declined by 2.2% MoM to P5.68trn
  • The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) stated that the debt level is within the P19.06trn FY26 borrowing program
