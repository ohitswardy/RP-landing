10 March 2026

Oil prices corrected sharply overnight. But that just highlights the volatile nature of the ongoing Middle East conflict. We published a report looking into the impact of the Iran war on the Philippine economy. On OFW Remittances – the Middle East accounts for just under half of all registered OFWs, and about 18% of total remittances. However, past conflicts in the region have not caused remittances to contract YoY. The larger macro impact comes from the price of oil and other energy. The Philippines imports nearly all of our oil consumption, and about 70% of coal, which means a straight pass-through into inflation, with strong second- and third-round effects onto electricity prices and logistics costs. If crude oil stays at $100/bbl, we could see CPI jump to 6% by next month. The BSP has already warned of potential rate hikes. The other major effect would be on the Current Account Deficit (CAD) and the PHP. If oil prices stay at or above $100/bbl, our CAD could rise to 4.5%-5.0% of GDP (vs the BSP’s forecast of 3.0% for 2026E). A weak PHP could then feed back into higher inflation on other imported goods (food, etc).

 

Higher oil/energy prices would be negative for consumers as inflation again crimps wallets. Higher interest rates should be good for banks, but we are wary of the impact on loan growth and on delinquencies. Windfall profits could be seen at Semirara (SCC PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM) and other renewables companies. The situation could push consumers towards EVs / hybrid vehicles. Ayala Corp (AC PM) owns a 50% stake in the distribution of BYD Philippines, and also has a 59% stake in ACEN.

The House is rushing to approve legislation granting the President to suspend oil excise taxes during periods of emergency. The current excise tax for gasoline is P10/liter while for diesel it’s P6/liter. These represent almost 20% and ~11% of the respective pump prices as of yesterday.

 

Meanwhile, the feed-through to consumer pump prices is already being felt as stations began implementing huge adjustments today. Effective today, March 10, gasoline prices are being raised as much as 20% while diesel prices are being raised even more (>30%).

POWER
Aboitiz Power – Lower spot exposure, ancillary revenues lift 4Q25
  • 4Q25 headline loss on sizeable impairment charge.  Aboitiz Power recorded a net loss of P3.9bn in 4Q, after it booked a P13.5bn impairment of goodwill from the GNPower Mariveles (GMEC) acquisition in 2016. As a result, FY25 net income fell to P19.4bn from P34bn a year earlier, below Street estimates. While the impairment charge was a result of reduced long-term price assumptions given the expiry of GMEC’s PSAs in 2029, management confirmed that there’s still P26bn of GMEC-related goodwil on its books today. 

 

  • Core profit, EBITDA rebound strongly in 4Q.  Core profit improved 54% YoY (-2% QoQ) to P10bn while beneficial EBITDA rose 35% YoY to P23.3bn, supported by (a) smaller exposure to spot prices at ~10% and (b) P3.5bn of catch-up revenues from ASPA and FIT recoveries. Despite steady demand of +4%, the distribution segment recorded lower QoQ/YoY contributions in 4Q.

 

  • CBK acquisition earnings accretive. The company completed the acquisition of the 797MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydro power complex in December, with the facility officially turned over to the winning consortium in February. AP holds a 64% stake in the asset, and its share of the P36bn purchase price was funded largely through debt. While CBK is currently uncontracted, management estimates that its group-wide contracted capacity declined only modestly to 86% (from 90%) after the acquisition and expects the plant to be earnings accretive. 

 

  • Net gearing rises to 1.3x while net debt-to-EBITDA at 3.4x. AP’s net debt stood at P267bn in end-2025, with net gearing climbing to 1.3x from 0.8x in end-FY24, following acquisiitons of LNG PH and CBK. Net debt-to-EBITDA stood at 3.4x, close to the company’s internal target of 4x. For 2026, capex (100% ownership and inclusive of M&A) is projected at P62bn, down from P118bn in the previous year.
