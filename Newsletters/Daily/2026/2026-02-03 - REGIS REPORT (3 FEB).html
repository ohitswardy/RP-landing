REGIS REPORT

03 February 2026

BPI was again first out the gates reporting FY25 NPAT of P66.62bn. This was slightly below REGIS forecast of P68.2bn, but broadly in line with consensus. Operating figures were within expectations, with PPOP up 19% bang in line with our numbers. However, provision expense once again dragged bottomline, with the FY figure coming in 10% above our forecasts. We estimate that credit costs for 4Q were in the mid- to high-90bps, compared to the mid-70bps range in 2Q/3Q. It is unclear at this stage if there were any ‘clean-up” efforts as part of the write-offs, or if this is the “new normal”. For reference, our FY26E forecasts bake in a credit cost of c80bps. Management will hold its briefing on 10 Feb. Doubtless credit costs in the consumer/MSME segment will take up much of the conversation.

BANKS
BPI FY25 NPA P66.62bn, slightly behind as credit costs near 100bps in 4Q

In a limited press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported FY25 NPAT of P66.62bn, up 7.4%. This was slightly behind REGIS (P68.2bn) but broadly in line with Bberg consensus of P66.0bn. PPOP was +19% was bang in line with REGIS forecasts, but provision expense came in 10% higher than our estimates, and we estimate that credit costs reached close to 100bps in 4Q25. Some highlights from the PR:

 

  • Loans grew 14.7% YoY, the fastest pace since 2022 (excl the RBC merger in 2024). The bank continues to push hard with its consumer/MSME push, with loans to the segments up 25.8% YoY, while institutional loans were up 10.4%. By our estimate, consumer/MSME loans dipped slightly to 30% of total loans, from 31% in 3Q25. This is due to a typical end-year surge in corporate loans.
  • NII was up 16% thanks to the improving loan mix. BPI claims FY NIMs expanded 28bps YoY. Other income grew 11% YoY.
  • Opex growth was kept in check, up 10% YoY, taking cost-to-income ratio down to 47% from 49% in FY2024.
  • PPOP was up 19% to P103.4bn, a shade higher than our forecast of P102.7bn.
  • Provision expense was P17.8bn, up 170% from a low base, but was 10% higher than our forecast. We estimate credit cost for 4Q25 at around 98bps, compared to 76bps in 3Q25. This took FY credit cost to about 73bps, compared to our FY estimate of 67bps. NPL ratio improved to 2.18% from 2.30% in 3Q, but NPL coverage dipped slightly to 94.9% from 96.5% in 3Q25.

 

BPI management will host its earnings call on 10 Feb. Expect much of the attention to be on the consumer/MSME book, both on uplift to NIMs, and on guidance for credit costs heading into 2026.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

PROPERTY
Colliers: Residential net take-up worsens in 4Q25
  • Residential vacancy in Metro Manila was relatively flat QoQ at 25%
  • Net take-up of Metro Manila condominium units fell 80% QoQ (but quintupled YoY) to 1,200 units, due to higher cancellations QoQ
  • We estimate that the number of unsold condominium units in Metro Manila rose c. 1% to 76,900
  • Colliers said that the number of unsold condominium units in Metro Manila was equivalent to about 8 years’ worth of sales, down from 12 years in 2Q25
    • We estimate that in 3Q25, unsold condominium units represented c. 9 years of sales  
  • Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy declined slightly to 19.4% in 4Q25, from 19.8% in 3Q25          
    • Makati CBD vacancy inched up c. 1% QoQ to 9%
    • BGC vacancy fell c. 2ppt QoQ to 10%
  • Net office take-up fell 21% QoQ to 91,000sqm
    • This was a reversal from net incremental closures of 185,000sqm in 4Q24
  • Office rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ
    • They inched up modestly in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio
  • Colliers estimates that land values in Makati CBD, Fort Bonifacio, and Ortigas Center rose 3%-5% QoQ
    • Office and residential vacancies in these districts were lower than the Metro Manila averages
  • ALI is trading at 62% discount to NAV, 0.9X FY26E P/B, and 9.8X FY26E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY26E P/B, and 3.4X FY26E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY26E P/B, and 6.1X FY26E P/E
  • SMPH is trading at 52% discount to NAV, 1.2X FY26E P/E, and 12.2X FY26E P/E

BSP open to another rate cut, reassesses GDP growth outlook
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the 4Q25 GDP growth was worse than expected at 3.0% vs the BSP's 3.8% forecast prompting the possibility of another rate cut in Feb and a revision in the 2026 GDP growth forecast of 5.4%
  • Remolona said that as long as inflation stays low "there can be another rate cut, but we'll see"
    • Jan CPI data will be released on Feb 5
  • The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2026/27 are 5.4/6.0%
    • For 2026/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 5.2/6.0% and 5.5/5.7%, respectively
  • Consensus and Regis expect 2026 inflation to settle at 2.6% and 3.0%, respectively
  • The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%
