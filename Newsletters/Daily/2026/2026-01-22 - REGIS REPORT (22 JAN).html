REGIS REPORT

22 January 2026

The BSP released its macro forecasts for 2025/26/27E, in which they expect GDP growth to fully recover to 6.0% YoY in 2027E. Interestingly, their 2025E GDP growth estimate is VERY bearish, at 4.6% (vs consensus at 4.9% and REGIS at 4.8%), suggesting that they expect 4Q25 GDP to fall below the already-disappointing 4.0% print in 3Q25 (9M25 was 5.0%). For 2026E, the BSP sees a slow recovery in 1H, with momentum picking up in 2H, for a FY26E of 5.4%. As we noted in a previous report, we expect the impact from the corruption investigations to drag government infra spend for 4-6 quarters. The potential upside risk was outlined by Finance Secretary Frederick Go in a recent forum with investors, saying the government would push disbursements in 1H26 for infra projects delayed in 2025.

 

Relatedly, the Cement Manufacturers Association of the Philippines (CEMAP) said that cement demand dropped 3-4% in 4Q25, supporting the BSP’s bearish view. While the CMAP hopes demand will recover this year, there is no evidence yet in the January numbers.

 

The BSP reiterated its stance that the weak GDP and benign inflation means that one more 25bp rate cut in Feb is “on the table”, but also that we are likely near the end of the rate cut cycle.

 

+ + +

 

Despite rapid growth in recent years, there seems to still be significant upside in unsecured consumer lending in the Philippines. The Credit Card Association of the Philippines (CCAP) estimates that there are 18.5mn cards-in-force (CIF), out of a total 70mn (ish) adults in the country. The CCAP did not, however, specify how many of those cards were issued to unique individuals (we suspect an average of at least 2 per user).

 

Meanwhile, the SEC is considering lifting the 2021 ban on new online lending licenses for non-bank financial companies (NBFCs), but is also contemplating raising the minimum capital from P1mn (about  $170k) to as much P5mn or P10mn.

 

The unsecured lending market has grown at a dizzying clip, with credit card receivables nearly tripling in the last 4 years (CAGR of >30%). Digibank loans, too, have picked up momentum since licenses were first granted in 2022. Annoyingly, there is no aggregated data on the NBFC sector, whether online or offline lending. While there is clearly concern over credit quality, we note the rapid improvements in the data collection of the national credit bureaus, as well as the (admittedly patchy) rollout of the National ID system.

Megaworld (MEG PM) announced the opening of the group’s second convention center, this time in Mactan, Cebu. The facility has a 2,500 seating capacity, and will reportedly be the venue for the ASEAN Summit later this year, which the Philippines will be hosting.

MACRO
BSP expects rebound in economic growth by 2027; guides for end of easing cycle
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona discussed with President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. the country's economic outlook
    • Remolona stated expectations of modest economic growth in 1H26, then a rebound in 2027 driven by earlier policy easing
      • Other drivers were noted to be gov't infra spending recovery as well as improvements in investor and consumer sentiment
    • He also signaled that the end of the central bank's easing cycle is near as any future easing is likely to be limited
      • Note that he has previously guided that one more potential rate cut to 4.25% in Feb is "on the table"
    • Inflation was said to likely be within the BSP's 2-4% target range
      • The central bank's outlook on inflation was cited as "broadly benign with expectations well-anchored"
  • The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2025/26/27 are 4.6/5.4/6.0%, respectively
    • For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively
  • The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%
  • Consensus and Regis Inflation expectations for 2026 are 2.6% and 3.0%, respectively

Cement association says 4Q25 demand dipped 3-4%; hopeful for rebound in 2026
  • The Cement Manufacturers Association of the Philippines, Inc. (CEMAP) is cautiously optimistic that cement demand will recover this year, after declining 2-3% last year
    • According to CEMAP President John Reinier H. Dizon
    • “Hopefully, it grows. Right now, we are not seeing it yet”
  • Mr. Dizon said that cement demand dipped by around 3-4% in 4Q25, as the government held back on spending and approval of contractor licenses were delayed
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Credit card penetration still low despite growth
  • Credit Card Association of the Philippines (CCAP) executive director Alex Ilagan stated that credit card penetration remains low despite growth in card issuances
  • In a quarterly survey, the industry had 18.5mn outstanding cards as of 4Q25
    • Ilagan noted that penetration is very low given the country's adult population of around 70mn
    • Number of outstanding credit cards was said to have been growing by around 12% YoY
  • He cited potential upside from consumers trading up from buy now, pay later (BNPL) products to credit cards as well as 60mn unique data subjects from the rollout of the national ID system
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
SEC mulls lifting ban on registration of new online lenders
  • The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is studying the potential lifting of the moratorium on registering new online lending platforms
    • Recall that the SEC imposed the moratorium back in Nov 2021 in order to work on rules to curb predatory lending and abusive debt collection
      • However, this had led to the proliferation in unregistered online lending platforms
  • The SEC is reviewing the P1mn minimum capital requirement for online lenders as it may be increased to P5mn or P10mn
  • The regulator targets to release draft rules by 1Q26

Government raises US$2.75bn from global bond offering
  • The Bureau of Treasury raised US$2.75bn from a triple-tranche dollar offering, yielding tighter spreads than previous offerings
    • The US$500mn 5.5-year bond carried a coupon of 4.25% and had a yield of 4.347% or 50bps above US treasuries

  • The government has ~US$5bn worth of bonds maturing this year
    •  US$1.5bn bond maturing in March
    • JPY17.9bn (~US$100mn) maturing in August
    • PHP208bn (~US$3.5bn) maturing in February, with the principal to be paid in USD
