REGIS REPORT

24 April 2026

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) delivered a 25bp policy rate hike, in response to the threatened consumer price spiral triggered by the US-Israel attack on Iran. This is the first rate increase since Oct 2023.

BSP Governor Remolona’s statements after the decision also revealed that some members of the Monetary Board (MB) were actually inclined to raise rates even more (50bp). It was also notable that the BSP increased its inflation forecast for FY26 further to 6.3% (vs 5.1% last month).  Both these developments suggest that further rates have become more likely with the BSP taking the view that it should contain 2nd and 3rd order effects of the spike in energy prices.

+++

The government’s budget deficit crept up 2% YoY to P350bn in March.  Revenues rose 9%, bolstered by a jump in non-tax revenues (+46%). Expenditures, on the other hand, grew 5% for the month.

+++

The remarks of Ayala Land (ALI PM) chairman Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala (“JAZA”) at its annual stockholders’ meeting yesterday point to increased caution at the company amidst the challenges facing the property industry. 

+++

An association of restaurant operators says a large majority of its members saw drops in sales following the eruption of war in the Gulf. Discretionary spending is likely among the first casualties of the war.

MACRO
BSP delivers 25bps rate hike, raises inflation forecasts above 4% ceiling
  • The Monetary Board raised its policy rate by 25bps to 4.5%, in line with Street expectations
    • This marked the first rate hike since October 2023
    • BSP Governor Remolona said the decision was not unanimous, as the board considered a 50bp hike but opted for a more reasonable smaller hike
  • Move was done to anchor inflation expectations amid the Middle East conflict, with higher oil and fertilizer prices beginning to feed through domestic fuel and food prices
    • Core inflation has continued to rise, pointing to a broadening of underlying price pressures
  • BSP raised its average headline inflation rate forecast to 6.3% for 2026 (vs. 5.1% previously) and 4.3% for 2027 (vs. 3.8% in 2027), breaching its 4% ceiling
    • March CPI stood at 4.1%, bringing 1Q26 average inflation rate to 2.8%
    • The BSP’s revised 2026 forecast suggest that inflation could average 9.8% for the rest of the year
MACRO
Government budget deficit mildly up in March on energy crisis support; 1Q26 deficit down 20% to P356bn
  • The government’s budget deficit increased by 2% YoY to P350bn in March
    • Revenues rose 9% YoY to P305bn, supported by the +46% YoY increase in non-tax revenues due to the early remittance of GOCC dividends  
      • BIR collections rose 6.5% YoY in March, up from the 3% pace in 2M26, but still below the full-year growth target of +10%
      • Customs collections grew 5.5% YoY in March, accelerating from the 2% pace in 2M26, but also below target (+7.5%)
    • Expenditures rose 5.2% YoY in March, as primary spending rose 4.5% YoY, reversing the mild 1% contraction in 2M26
      • Primary spending in March was partly supported by the P20bn Emergency Energy program (3% of expenditures), aimed at augmenting the country’s fuel supply
  • Brings 1Q26 budget deficit to P355.5bn, down 20% YoY and broadly in line with target as weaker revenues were offset by subdued spending

PROPERTY
Ayala Land to prioritize stability over aggressive growth
  • During the company’s stockholders’ meeting, Ayala Land said that the company would prioritize stability over aggressive growth
  • The company’s strategy includes:
    • Expanding the leasing portfolio
    • Scaling down capital expenditures
    • Managing residential launches and reducing inventory
  • These plans were already conveyed during Ayala Land’s 4Q25 earnings briefing (in February)
    • After lowering residential launches to P46.6bn (down 42%) in 2025, ALI will reduce this further to P30bn in 2026
    • Capex will be lowered by c. 20% but leasing capex will remain flat
    • The company intends to increase the leasing segment’s share of EBITDA to 50% by 2027, from 45% in 2025
  • ALI currently offers 3.1% dividend yield and is trading at 70% discount to NAV, 0.7X FY26E P/B, 7.6X FY26E P/E
PROPERTY/REITS
MREIT 1Q26 distributable income rises 34% YoY
  • MREIT’s 1Q26 distributable income rose 34% YoY to P1.25bn, in line with consensus forecast
  • MREIT acquired 165,000sqm of offices in Fort Bonifacio, which expanded its portfolio by 34%
    • Although the SEC approval for the share-for-property was only granted in March, the transaction was backdated so that the buildings began contributing to MREIT on January 1
  • MREIT’s shares outstanding expanded by 27%
  • MEG currently offers 4.3% dividend yield and is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.3X FY26E P/E
  • MREIT currently offers 7.5% FY26E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.67%
CONSUMER
Restaurants see lower sales
  • David Sison, President of the Restaurant Association of the Philippines (RestoPH), said that the association’s members have seen lower foot traffic and a drop in sales since the beginning of the Iran conflict
    • RestoPH has 230 member companies with a combined store count of about 1,500 
  • In a RestoPH survey:
    • 6% said that they experienced a sales decline of over 30%
    • 79% said that their sales fell between 10%-30%
    • 12% reported that their sales dropped within 10%
    • 3% did not experience a decline in sales
