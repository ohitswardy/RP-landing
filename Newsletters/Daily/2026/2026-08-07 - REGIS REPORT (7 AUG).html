TRANSPORT
Cebu Air: Jet fuel shock hit 2Q26
  • Swings to losses in 2Q. Cebu Air reported a net loss of P5.5bn in 2Q26. Excluding one-offs and FX adjustments, we estimate its core net loss stood at P4.9bn, halting the airline’s positive trajectory. The quarter proved particularly challenging as jet fuel prices spiked to record levels following the Iran conflict. While the company initially attempted to fully pass through higher fuel costs via fare hikes in April, it later rolled back these increases after demand softened. Average fares still rose 19% QoQ and 17% YoY to ~P3,500, but passenger volumes declined 8% QoQ and were flat YoY to 6.96mn, a surprising outcome during the peak summer travel season. Seat load factors contracted 510bps QoQ and 730bps YoY to 79%, its lowest level since 4Q22. The poor 2Q26 turnout brought 1H to a core net loss of P3.3bn, coming in below Street expectations.

 

  • Market consolidates in 3Q, with long-term positive effects. While jet kero prices have eased from their peak, they remain elevated, prompting CEB to trim international capacity to preserve margins. Load factors recovered to 83.6% in June, with gains sustained in July/Aug, as competitors cut capacity even more aggressively. Air Asia Philippines (AAP), for instance, has reduced capacity by 30-40% YoY and, somewhat surprisingly, downgauged aircraft from A320s to A220s through the December peak season. Although management expects a similar level of core losses in the seasonally weaker 3Q, it believes its cost leadership and strong competitive position will enable it to sustain domestic market share gains at 60% (vs. 57% in 1Q26), beyond this year.

 

  • Out-of-the-money convertible debt (CB) maturing in May 2027 adds another layer of complexity. Management hopes its convertible bond investors, Indigo Partners and IFC, will remain long-term strategic shareholders. However, with the share price trading below the conversion price of P38/sh, conversion appears unlikely ahead of the April 12, 2027 exercise date. Even though CEB’s net debt-to-EBITDA (treating CBs as debt) stood at 6.9x in end-June, management believes refinancing is highly plausible, given its excellent credit standing.

Nickel Asia 1H26 profit up 158% YoY to P4.1bn
  • Headline net income rose 93% YoY to P4.06bn in 1H, which looks in line with both Street and Regis estimates
    • Excluding last year’s one-offs, profits are up 158% YoY on a recurring basis
  • In 2Q26 alone, profit jumped 10-fold QoQ and twofold YoY to P3.68bn, supported by strong ASPs and sales volumes for both its nickel ore and power units
    • Its HPAL unit also reported a P70mn profit in the quarter, reversing losses in prior periods due likely to the strength in cobalt prices
  • The company also disclosed that is paying a total of US$30mn for a 20% stake in a copper mine in Kazakhstan
    • It already paid US$10mn in 2Q and will settle the remaining US$20mn subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals

CONSUMER
Retail industry expects single-digit growth this year
  • The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) projects the retail industry to grow single digits this year with companies remaining "cautiously optimistic" in spite of global headwinds
  • PRA President Alica Liu said that the industry will see outliers with double-digit growth this year, with the food and beverage sector expected to be more resilient
    • She also noted that 1Q was "very good" but the growth momentum was halted due to the Middle East conflict
    • Liu added that although weather disruptions are seasonal, they are further weighing on already subdued demand
