It appears that Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) is now set to be delisted following a tender offer by its biggest shareholder JE Holdings. Only 0.3% of RRHI’s shares remain in public hands after the tender offer, fulfilling the condition set by JE Holdings (minimum 95% acceptance) and also the regulatory requirement.

 

+++

 

The dip in CPI inflation to 6.4% is understandably view with some relief. This drop, however, is by no means a sign that things are going back to “normal”.

 

Food price inflation slowed a bit, but rice price inflation remains still quite steep at 15%. Consumer goods manufacturers already began raising price mid-May and the implementation of wage hikes could lead to further upward pressure on prices. Finally, the El Nino weather phenomenon and its effects on rainfall and temperatures introduce a significant risk on crop outcomes and thus the price of food (esp. rice). We reckon there could still upside risk to inflation in the coming months.

 

+++

 

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) has reportedly asked several banks and e-wallet firms to explain why they’ve not cut fees on interbank fund transfers as deeply as it expected. The BSP circular essentially told the financial institutions that the difference between fees on interbank vs intra-institution fund transfers should approximate only the difference in direct costs.

 

It is unclear what regulatory actions can be taken by the BSP against those institutions that are not cutting fees as much as it expected.

 

+++

 

The government aims to sign the framework agreement that would govern the technology hub under the Pax Silica Initiative. The site of the hub is expected to be in New Clark City in Tarlac province, adjacent to the Clark Freeport Zone. The government is hoping to sign up Taiwanese firm Foxconn as an “anchor investor”.

 

A key issue for investors is likely to be policy continuity for this initiative should there be a change in administration once President Marcos steps down in 2028.

MACRO
Inflation moderates further to 6.4% in June
  • Headline inflation moderated to 6.4% in Jun from May's 6.8%
    • This was in line with consensus expectations of 6.5% and within the BSP's forecast range of 6.0-7.0% for Jun
    • The softer inflation print is the second consecutive month of deceleration following five consecutive months of accelerating inflation
  • This brings 6M26 inflation to 4.8%, above the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) but still below their forecast of 6.4% for the year
  • Inflation in Jun decelerated given slower YoY increases vs May in food and transport prices
    • Food prices overall rose 5.2% YoY, decelerating from the 5.7% pace in May, driven by lower prices of meat and lower YoY increases in fish and rice which offset higher vegetable prices
      • Rice prices jumped 14.9% YoY in Jun, moderating slightly from the 15.6% YoY increase in May
      • Rice prices are currently at P49.5/kg and P48.0/kg as of end-Jun for local and imported well-milled rice, with prices stable relative to the previous month
    • Transport prices rose 12.8% YoY, slower than +16.2% the previous month
      • The increase in diesel prices slowed to +39.0% YoY from +58.5% YoY, while the gasoline price increase likewise moderated to +39.2% YoY from +51.6% YoY in May
  • Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, picked up to 4.4% in Jun from 4.1% in May, driven by both core food (3.3% from 2.9% in May) and core non-food (4.6% from 4.4%)
    • The 4.4% core inflation print is the highest since Dec 2023
    • The BSP in a statement highlighted that the acceleration in core inflation indicates persistent underlying inflationary pressures despite moderation in headline inflation
  • For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.4% (previously revised from 6.3%)
    • Consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 6.0% and 7.5%, respectively
  • The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Aug 27
    • The current policy rate is 4.75%
      • For 2026, consensus expectations for rate hikes are +100bp total to 5.25% by year-end
      • Regis expectations are for +125bp total worth of hikes to 5.50%

RRHI to delist after Gokongwei family secures 99.7% ownership
  • Robinsons Retail Holdings (RRHI PM) in a disclosure announced that the company is set to delist from the PSE after JE Holdings alongside various members of the Gokongwei family have secured 99.69% of RRHI's issued and outstanding capital stock, with the company's public float to decrease to 0.31%
  • JE Holdings accepted 229.6mn shares tendered by shareholders worth about P11.1bn (P48.30/sh)
    • The 229.6mn shares acquired exceed the 179.6mn shares needed to reach the 95% ownership threshold required to delist
    • Following the tender, the company will request for approval from the PSE for the voluntary delisting of RRHI's common shares
  • Tendered shares are expected to be crossed on July 13, 2026
  • Recall that a P2.0/sh cash dividend was distributed on June 10 to shareholders of record as of May 28
