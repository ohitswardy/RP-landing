It’s another busy day for 2Q results, which frankly, were disappointing for the most part. The tough macro environment (high energy prices, tighter consumer budgets, and slower GDP growth) does seem to be evident.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – Headline profit for 1H was P4.9b, down 13% YoY, with 2Q at P3.4bn, up +6%. The latest quarter saw a marked improvement in profits with a bounce in gross margins (+2.0pt QoQ). This follows price increases that management flagged earlier. SSSG for the domestic business was +1.3% for 2Q, suggesting a small drop in sales volumes. The company will hold its briefing next week. We discuss a little more below. 

 

  • ACEN (ACEN PM) (-) - Core attributable EBITDA rose 28% YoY to P6.25bn in 2Q26, buoyed by strong spot prices in the Philippines and higher output in some international facilities. While the 2Q EBITDA growth of 28% was decent, it came in slower than the 48% YoY growth we’re anticipating for the full year, as weaker spot prices weighed on the operational performance of its facilities in Australia. Recurring profit fell 25% YoY to P795mn, bringing 1H26 profit down 31% YoY to P1.74bn, lagging both our estimate and Street expectations.

CONSUMER
JFC 2Q26: Margins recover but domestic SSSG moderates

Margins rebound

JFC’s 1H26 net profit after estimated divs to prefs dropped 14% to P4.7bn. Revenues for 1H increased 10% YoY to P162.5bn as SWS rose 12% YoY to P244.7bn. Relative to both consensus and REGIS estimates, revenues appear in line with expectations while profit looks behind. For 2Q alone, net profit after estimated pref divs was up 6% YoY (139% QoQ) to P3.3bn, rebounding from the 40% YoY drop in 1Q, as margin recovered following the implemented price hikes. 2Q revenue grew 11% YoY to P85.9bn as SWS grew 14%.

 

Gross profit margin was up 2.0ppt QoQ but still 0.6ppt lower YoY. As a result, operating and net margin likewise expanded 2.0ppt QoQ (but still down YoY) to 7.2% and 3.8%, respectively.

 

Domestic business moderates while international remains steady

For the domestic business, 2Q SSSG was +1.3%, moderating further from +3.2% in 1Q as sales were likely dampened by the price increases and tighter consumer wallets from inflationary pressures. The SSSG suggests that like-for-like volumes contracted YoY.

 

The int'l business, meanwhile, saw 2Q SSSG pick up slightly to +4.4% from +4.0% in 1Q, driven by Jollibee NA (+8.6%) and Jollibee Vietnam (+17.9%) as well as an improvement in Smashburger (+7.0% from +2.4% in 1Q).

 

Management revises guidance downwards

FY26 guidance was revised downwards, with SSSG lowered to 3-4% from 4-6%, gross store openings were reduced to 1,000-1,100 from 1,200-1,300, although net store growth remains unchanged at 5-10% given ongoing network rationalization, and operating income growth guidance was cut to 10-15% from 15-18%. Capex was revised slightly to P13-15bn from P13-16bn.

 

Management noted that transition-related costs will continue to weigh on operating profit and overall margins as Yonghe King and Smashburger undergo ongoing turnaround efforts, including store closures and conversions to a franchise model.

 

Series B preferred shares to be fully redeemed

The company also announced that it will redeem all outstanding Series B Preferred Shares (worth P9.0bn) on Oct 14 at the offer price of P1,000 plus accrued and unpaid cash dividends.

 

More details after the briefing next week, Monday, Aug 17.

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes surge 52% YoY in July
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that throughput at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo SA (CMSA), grew 52% YoY to 168k TEUs in July
    • While growth was flattered by a low base from last year’s customs-induced congestion, we note that the absolute throughput volume reached a record high
    • CMSA also surpassed competitor, SSA Mexico’s, volumes for the first time, making it the largest terminal in the port of Manzanillo
  • This brings 7M26 throughput volumes up 20% YoY to 1.02mn TEUs, comfortably on track to beat our full-year forecast of 1.66mn TEUs (+8% YoY)
    • Every 10% increase in CMSA’s topline will raise consolidated FY26E EBITDA by 3% and profit by 4%
  • We estimate CMSA accounts for 10% of ICTSI’s consolidated FY26E volumes, 20% of revenues, and 23% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 15x/14x FY26E/FY27E EV/EBITDA, above peer average of 11x/10x and historical average of 11x

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Proposed P7.2trn 2027 national budget puts education and infra as top priority
  • The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) provided further details on the P7.2trn (+6% YoY) 2027 National Expenditure Program (NEP), with education and infrastructure identified as the government’s top priorities, accounting for 16% and 14% of the total budget, respectively
    • The proposed budget will be discussed in and revised by Congress before it is enacted into law
  • Education (DepEd and CHED) was allocated the biggest portion of the budget at P1.15trn (-5% YoY, 3.5% of GDP)
    • DepEd alone is set to receive P976bn, the largest allocation among gov't departments
    • The allocation for education includes P61bn for universal access to tertiary education, P38bn for gov't assistance, P31bn for facilities, among others
  • Infrastructure (Dept. of Public Works and Highways [DPWH] and Dept. of Transportation [DoTr]) was given the second largest allocation at P947bn (+41% YoY, 2.9% of GDP)
    • The allocation for infra was increased by 41% YoY as the budget for the DPWH and DoTr was raised by 21% and 113% YoY, respectively
    • The higher allocation stems from the general infra spending (e.g., road network, basic infra, etc.) as well as the P195bn budget for the railway transport program as the admin seeks to fast track completion for the latter
  • Overall infra investment (which includes classrooms, health centers, other infra not under the DPWH and DoTr) was set to be higher than 2026 at P1.47trn (+14% YoY, 4.4% of GDP) vs the P1.29trn (-2% YoY, 4.2% of GDP) allocated for this year
  • Other notable movements in the 2027 budget was the 12% YoY decrease (to P277bn) in allocation for the Dept. of Agriculture
  • The House of Representatives and Senate will each pass their respective versions of the bill, which will then undergo a bicameral conference to reconcile differences and produce the final General Appropriations Bill (GAB)
    • The GAB will subsequently be submitted to the President for approval, with the President able to veto specific items or return the bill to Congress.
    • If no new budget is signed by year-end the previous year's budget will be utilized
