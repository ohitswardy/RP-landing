REGIS REPORT

09 March 2026

Converge (CNVRG PM) issued a statement announcing that its profit reached P11.86bn for FY25, up 10% YoY. The full-year profit figure is just a tad below estimates for both Regis and the Street.

 

Revenues for FY25 were up 10%, with Enterprise revenues outpacing the Residential side. Notably, subscriber adds (net) for 4Q alone amounted to 58k, which is the lowest among the three main operators. This is also the lowest for Converge in over three years.

 

The company will hold its briefing later this week (March 13).

 

+++

 

Luzon power demand for Feb, like Jan, dipped again. Demand for the month was lower by 0.6% YoY – certainly not a good sign for how the overall economy is faring.

 

+++

 

Crude prices pierced US$100/barrel over the weekend, with the latest news flow from the Middle East. Brent crude is now up over 50% to US$108/barrel since Israel and the U.S. first launched attacks on Iran.

 

Even before this latest oil price surge, the government was already moving quickly into crisis mode. It already announced the imposition of a four-day work week for many government offices effective today (March 9). It’s a sign, in our view, that the government is concerned about actual physical oil shortages because of low inventories.

 

Meanwhile, BSP Governor Eli Remolona raised the possibility of the BSP raising rates because of the oil price surge and its impact on inflation. We are doubtful about the wisdom of raising rates due to an extraneous (and hopefully short-lived) supply shock and believe the BSP will come to the same conclusion as us.

TELCOS
Converge FY25 profits rise 10% YoY to P11.86bn
  • Converge reported FY25 profits rose 10% to P11.86bn, which would be just slightly behind Street and Regis expectations (P12bn)
  • Revenue growth for the year was +10% with a modest acceleration in 4Q (+11%)
  • There was a marked slowdown in net subscriber adds for 4Q. CNVRG added 58k subscribers (net), which is the lowest among the three major operators and also the lowest since 2Q23.
  • The company declared a cash dividend of P0.49/share (better than expectations) which would be ~3.5% yield on last close
  • Guidance is for 8-10% revenue growth for FY25 with ROIC of 15.5% to 16.5%, which would be at the low end of estimates

 

 

 

MACRO
BSP Governor guides for possible rate hike if oil prices continue to surge
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona in an interview guided that the central bank could raise interest rates if global oil prices hit US$100/barrel and the USD continues its rally
    • As of March 6 (Friday),
      • Dubai crude prices are at US$99.1/bbl up 45% from US$68.4/bbl end-Feb
      • The USD-PHP rate is at P58.47 from P57.55 end-Feb
  • He stated that surging oil prices and a strong dollar could push inflation beyond the central banks' (2-4%) target range
    • Remolona also cited remittance risk given that cash remittances from the Middle East amounted to US$6.5bn or 18% of total inflows in 2025
  • For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 3.6%
    • Consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.7% and 3.0%
    • Consensus and Regis expectations for the USD-PHP rate are at P57.9 and P61.0, respectively
  • The second out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%

 

 

POWER
Luzon power demand declines 0.6% YoY in February; Spot prices also down 1% YoY
  • Spot prices (LWAP) in Luzon fell by 0.8% YoY (-17% MoM) to P2.69/kwh in February, as demand declined by 0.6% YoY (+5% MoM)
    • Average supply also declined by 1.3% YoY (+8% MoM) to 14,286 MW
    • According to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP)
  • The following gencos are net sellers to the spot market:
    • Semirara (SCC PM) – 60% of capacity is un-contracted
    • ACEN Corp. (ACEN PM) – 44% of capacity in PH is un-contracted
    • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) – 10% of capacity is un-contracted

MACRO
PH dollar reserves at $112.7bn end-Feb
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Feb inched up to US$112.7bn from US$112.6bn the previous month
    • The continued surge in gold prices was offset by a decline in foreign investments, leading to the flat MoM growth in the GIR
    • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 4.2x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn
