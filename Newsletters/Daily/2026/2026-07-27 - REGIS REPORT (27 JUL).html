REGIS REPORT

27 July 2026

The Department of Energy (DOE) plans to revise the bidding terms for the Semirara coal contract by requiring the winning bidder to allocate 60–70% of production to the domestic market, in a bid to reduce reliance on Indonesian coal imports. This rule, if enforced, would diminish the earnings prospects of whoever assumes the mining concession next year.

 

While we do not believe this new regulatory imposition will materially reduce Semirara Mining's (SCC PM) chances of winning the contract, it will have significant implications on the mine’s future profitability. SCC currently produces around 16mn MT of coal annually but sells only about 8mn MT domestically, as a large portion of its output is low-grade coal that cannot be utilised by most Philippine power plants.

Based on Simpar’s EBITDA guidance of US$35-49mn, the deal would be on 7-10x FY26E EV/EBITDA, lower than ICT’s own multiple. We estimate the port could add 1-2% to ICT’s EBITDA.

POWER
Government eyes domestic supply mandate for winning Semirara coal bidder; Auction deferred to Aug/Sep
  • Energy Secretary Garin wants to include a provision in the terms of reference, requiring the winning operator to sell 60-70% of output to the domestic market
    • Despite recognizing that some power plants in the country cannot use Semirara coal, the DOE will still recommend that 80-90% of coal production must be used by power plants
    • Secretary Garin cited that this action was prompted by changes in Indonesia’s export guidelines, which would affect the Philippines’ coal contract auction
    • The DOE is aiming to re-launch the bidding in August/September
  • This requirement could materially lower the annual production rate of the Semirara coal mine from 16mn MT today
    • Lower production could reduce the mine’s profitability, as well as the government’s royalty
    • To the upside, SCC generates higher margins on domestic sales due to its freight advantage
  • Note that SCC currently sells ~8mn MT annually to the domestic market, accounting for ~20% of the country’s coal consumption
    • Of the ~8mn MT domestic sales, ~6mn MT are sold to power plants, including its own plants
TRANSPORT
ICTSI acquires bulk port in Brazil

ICTSI (ICT PM) announced it signed an agreement to purchase 100% of Brazil-based conglomerate Simpar SA’s (SIMH3 BZ) port unit, HSIM (a.k.a. CS Portos), for BRL 650mn (~US$130mn), with an additional earn-out payment of BRL 100mn (~US$20mn) if certain milestones are met.  CS Portos owns ATU12 and ATU18, which are newly expanded, adjacent bulk ports with an annual capacity of 10mn MT and are located in Aratu Port in the State of Bahia, northeastern Brazil. ATU12/ATU18 hold public lease agreements valid until 2047/2037 and renewable up to 2091. The deal is subject to usual regulatory approvals, etc.

 

EBITDA accretive, given valuation of ~10x EV/EBITDA

ICT is acquiring these ports for an enterprise value of up to BRL1.75bn (~US$350mn) after considering net debt of BRL1bn (~US$196mn). Based on Simpar’s projected FY26E EBITDA of BRL180-250mn (~US$35-49mn), the transaction implies and EV/EBITDA multiple of up to 10x, lower than ICT’s current valuation of 13-14x. Upon deal completion, we estimate these ports will add ~2% to ICT’s EBITDA. 

 

A strategic play on Brazil’s agricultural boom

Given Aratu Port’s long distance from ICTSI’s existing ports in Brazil and its focus on bulk cargo, a business distinct from ICTSI’s core container operations, we believe the acquisition is driven primarily by the port’s standalone growth prospects, with limited potential for meaningful synergies. 

 

ATU12 and ATU18 are natural deepwater and modern terminals exclusively serving agricultural cargo at the Port of Aratu. The terminals are well positioned to capitalize on the fastest-growing agricultural regions of Brazil, collectively known as MATOPIBA, which grew agri production by ~13% annually in the past 10 years.

MACRO
US imposes 12.5% 'forced labor' tariff on Philippine exports
  • The U.S. Trade Representative (USTR), following its investigation into the Philippines and other countries over imports of goods allegedly produced using forced labor, has determined to impose a 12.5% tariff on Philippine products
    • The announcement follows the July 24 expiration of the 10% temporary tariff imposed by the Trump administration on February 20, the same day the Supreme Court deemed President Donald Trump’s previous enforcement of tariffs unconstitutional
  • Earlier in the year, the US launched a "forced labor" probe to investigate 60 trading partners on the basis of Section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974 in order to "address unfair foreign practices affecting US commerce"
    • The US is also going to impose a 10% tariff on Cambodia, Canada, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Mexico, among others
    • All other investigated countries were imposed with a 12.5% tariff rate
  • The USTR elaborated that it will exempt certain products from the tariff such as raw materials that could lead to the unavailability of domestic supply or economy-wide disruptions
  • The Philippines' Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) elaborated that ~66% (US$12.0bn) of its exports are exempt from the additional duty
    • Exempted products include semiconductors and electronics, agricultural products, automotive parts, minerals, aircraft parts, etc.
    • It was estimated that US$6.2bn worth of goods (34% of PH exports to the US) are going to be subject to the new tariff
      • Products impacted by the levy are "labor-intensive" industries including apparel, footwear, toys, among others
    • The DTI also mentioned that the PH compares favorably with regional peers as 83% and 40% of Indonesia's and Malaysia's exports to the US, respectively, are exposed to the new tariff
  • The US accounted for the PH's largest export market accounting for 16% (US$13.5bn) of total exports in 2025
    • As of 5M26, the US remained the top destination for PH exports at 18% (US$6.7bn, +24% YoY) of the country's total
  • Various gov't agencies will form an interagency committee to investigate goods made with forced labor to possibly implement measures banning the importation of such goods
