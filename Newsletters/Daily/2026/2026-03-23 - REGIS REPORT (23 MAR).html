REGIS REPORT

23 March 2026

Beneath the headline government budget surplus of P165bn budget surplus for Jan is the worrying (much more) part: primary expenditures by the government plunged by 40% (!).  It’s been half a year since monthly primary spending was lower YoY but the Jan figure is indeed alarming.

 

+++

 

Amidst the global issues, the government is allowing the use of “dirtier” fuels in order to expand allowable supply.  Under current regulations, fuels at the pump must comply with the Euro-4 standard but the administration will allow fuels below that standard to be used locally.

 

+++

 

Representatives of San Miguel (SMC PM) and Semirara (SCC PM) attended the Department of Energy’s pre-bid conference for coal blocks that include coal assets in Semirara island last Friday.  The absence of Meralco (MER PM) from the bidding suggests it will not be participating directly. Conversely, SMC’s presence signals its firm intention to compete for the Semirara assets on April 28. 

 

+++

 

The SEC approved the share-for-property swap between MREIT (MREIT PM) and its sponsor, Megaworld (MEG PM). MREIT will acquire 165,000sqm of Fort Bonifacio offices from Megaworld in exchange for 997mn new MREIT shares. The transaction will increase MREIT’s portfolio by 34% (to 645,000sqm) and its shares outstanding by 27%.

Jefferies’ global strategist Chris Wood talks again about the one topic on everyone’s mind (and of course what it means for investors): the Iran war.  Wood devotes a section on the Philippines, sharing our views on what this crisis means for investors here. Have a look at the attached file.

MACRO
Government posts P165bn budget surplus in Jan
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget surplus of P165bn in Jan, 142% wider YoY and a reversal from the P313bn deficit in Dec, as gov't spending deteriorated
  • Revenue collections in Jan were flat (+0.4% YoY) given lackluster growth in tax collections and the decline in non-tax revenues
    • Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were relatively flat, +1%
    • Customs collections rose a modest 2%
    • Non-tax revenues fell 12% given a moderation in Bureau of Treasury (BTr) income
  • Expenditures dipped 24% YoY in Jan due to the steep decline in primary spending
    • Primary spending for the month fell sharply by 40%
      • Since July 2025, January 2026 marks the sixth consecutive month of a YoY decline in gov't spending amid the slowdown in infrastructure projects following the flood control scandal
    • Interest payments rose 22% from additional debt and coupon payment timing changes following refinancing

 

 

 

MACRO
Department of Energy allows temporary use of dirtier fuel
  • The Department of Energy is authorizing the temporary and limited usage of Euro II petroleum products, a cheaper but dirtier fuel type, to ensure supply
    • Order will cover vehicles made in 2015 or earlier, traditional jeepneys, power plants and generators, as well as marine/shipping sectors
    • Fuel companies intending to offer Euro II fuels must also notify the DoE on retail outlets such products will be made available
  • Since 2015, the Philippines has limited the motoring industry to the use of Euro IV fuels, which have sulphur content of 50 parts per million (ppm) (vs. 500ppm for Euro II)
MINING
Semirara, San Miguel attend pre-bidding conference of coal blocks
  • Representatives from San Miguel (SMC PM) and Semirara (SCC PM) attended the Department of Energy’s (DOE) pre-submission conference last Friday
  • The DOE is bidding out 18 coal blocks, of which ten (10) are located in Semirara island
  • Submission of applications is set on April 28 and the notice of qualification to enter into a coal operating contract with the government is targeted for issuance on May 27
PROPERTY
SEC approves share-for-property swap between MREIT and Megaworld
  • The SEC approved a share-for-property swap between REIT and Megaworld
  • MREIT will issue 997mn new shares to Megaworld in exchange for 165,000sqm of offices in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio
    • The transaction is valued at P16.2bn
  • MREIT’s portfolio will grow by 34% 645,000sqm
  • MREIT’s shares outstanding will expand by 27%
  • We estimate that Megaworld’s ownership in MREIT will increase from 55% to 64%
  • MEG currently offers 4.2% dividend yield and is trading at 67% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.3X FY26E P/E
  • MREIT currently offers 7.8% FY26E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.785%
CONSUMER
Food firms agree on up to 60 days price freeze; CNPF cites ample inventory stock
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. stated that several food manufacturers have committed to keep prices steady for up to two months to cushion the inflationary impact of the Middle East conflict
    • Some companies that were said to have agreed to keep prices steady were Nestle Philippines and Century Pacific Food (CNPF PM)
    • Marcos emphasized that the gov't will continue to coordinate with the sector to ensure stable supply and prices of essentials
  • Dept. of Trade Secretary Cristina Roque stated that current food supply is at least 30 days for basic necessities and prime commodities
  • Century Pacific Food, Inc. (CNPF PM) COO Gregory Banzon stated that the company has sufficient inventory at 45 days and production capacity despite global disruptions
    • Banzon notes CNPF's ongoing initiatives to mitigate input cost increases such as cost cutting measures and a diversified pool of suppliers from Asia, EU, US, and Oceania
    • Logistics costs (i.e., transport, freight) and utilities account for ~1.1% and ~4.8% of CNPF's total costs (COGS + OPEX), respectively
  • Mega Prime Foods Inc., a local sardine brand, however, said that it cannot comply with the gov't's request for a price freeze with low fish catch and high cost of fuel as some fishing companies halted operations
    • Mega Prime Foods' current stock inventory was said to be four weeks (~30 days)
