The German retailing giant ALDI is reportedly taking a stake in local hard discount chain DALI in a deal that could transform the latter from upstart to a major operator. DALI has around 1,100 stores today and reported sales of P48bn (US$840m) last year. It is one of two emerging hard discount grocery chains (the other being O!Save) that sell principally through small format (180-300sqm each) stores.

 

The business model of DALI arguably pits it most directly against Alfamart, controlled by the SM (SM PM) group in the Philippines, and Puregold (PGOLD PM), whose Puregold stores cater to lower income consumers and mom-and-pop sari-sari stores. One can also argue that DALI competes, albeit less directly, against the likes of Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) and Robinsons Retail, which owns a minority stake in O!Save.

Gaming firm PhilWeb (WEB PM) disclosed a series of transactions that would see it swap stakes with a gaming technology provider called JKS Tech Solutions. JKS will end up owning a 5% stake in WEB while WEB will take a larger stake (% not disclosed) in JKS Tech.

Vista Land (VLL PM) is apparently seeking to sell some property assets ahead of a large debt maturity next year. These assets include two “non-core malls”. VLL has a US$420m bond maturing next year.

We have a new note out on ICTSI (ICT PM) where we raise our FY26E-FY28E EPS by 3-10% and NAV-based TP by 20% to P900/sh. The upgrade reflects the expectation that capacity expansions at its Mexico ports will be absorbed immediately, as well as higher yield assumptions for its Durban port beginning in 2028.

 

Our projections imply a 21% EPS CAGR from 2025-2028, driven by multiple growth engines of strategic capacity expansions at key ports and the anticipated turnaround of Durban. Acquisitions remain a key source of upside, with the upcoming Santos port bid potentially adding P75/sh to our valuation. 

 

ICTSI has been the key market outperformer this year, with the share price up 65% year-to-date against the PCOMP’s flattish performance. At 21x FY27E PER and 12x EV/EBITDA, the shares are looking rich. We thus maintain our Hold rating.

CONSUMER
German retailer ALDI SÜD Group acquires stake in DALI
  • According to a media outlet, the ALDI SÜD (SOUTH) Group acquired an undisclosed minority stake in Philippine hard-discounter grocery retailer DALI Everyday Grocery
    • Hard Discount Philippines, Inc. will remain as the sole operator of DALI with ALDI's investment eyed to support store network expansion and the local supply chain
    • DALI has a network of 1,131 as of 1H26
    • In FY25, DALI posted a net loss of P1.83bn (-7% YoY), revenues amounted to ~P48.3bn (+42% YoY)
  • ALDI SOUTH group CFO Marcus Almeling stated that the group looks forward to supporting DALI as a partner through the long-term investment
    • Almeling highlighted that DALI is well-positioned for growth given that "the Philippines is a high-growth market with strong consumer demand for quality groceries at low prices"
    • ALDI SOUTH operates 7,500 stores across 11 countries

MACRO
PH dollar reserves at $104.8bn as of end-Aug
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Aug increased 1.4% MoM to US$104.8bn
    • The MoM increase in the GIR was primarily due to the increase in the price of gold in the int'l market, which offset the national gov't's drawdowns on its foreign currency deposits for external debt payments
  • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 6.8x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$104.0bn, lower than the US$110.8bn in 2025
