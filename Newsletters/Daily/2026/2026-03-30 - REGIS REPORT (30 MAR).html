Robinsons Retail’s (RRHI PM) major shareholders, the Gokongwei family, announced a tender offer at P48.30/sh, with the intention to take the company private and delist from the PSE, with the prolonged undervaluation of RRHI likely prompting the move. On the one hand, the offer price is a 24% premium to the latest closing price, and 32% higher than the one-year VWAP. However, the offer price is a huge discount to our estimates of the company's underlying value. We reckon its NAV is P66/sh, assuming its c6% stake in BPI at market price, which would be worth about P30/sh to RRHI. The offer price also values the stock at 8.3x PE, notably lower than historical average, and below the average 10.3x PE for domestic retailers we cover (ex-RRHI). It is also well below our target price of P83/sh.

 

While the offer provides an opportunity to exit at a certain premium, the likelihood of reaching the required 95% of outstanding shares threshold remains uncertain.

The government has rolled out a series of measures to help ease the impact of the Gulf crisis on consumers.

 

  • The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) has gotten assurance from major consumer manufacturers and retailers to maintain food prices for at least 2 weeks (1-16 April)
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has asked electricity utilities to stagger the increase in electricity rates over coming months

 

Meanwhile, Petron (PCOR PM) the country’s fuel largest retailer an only oil refiner says it received 2.48mn barrels of oil from Russia, and that it had enough inventory until the end of June.

 

And in last week’s off-cycle meeting, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) said that they were preparing policies for regulatory relief for consumers and SME borrowers. These might include standardized restructuring of defaults, payment deferrals, etc as they did during the Covid-19 pandemic.

We published 4Q25 results recap on the property developers. What grabbed our attention the most was the substantial decline in demand for residential property. After rising sequentially for two quarters, reservation sales of the top 4 developers fell 28% QoQ and 13% YoY to P61.5bn. This is the lowest level since 2Q21 and only represents c. 60% of the 2019 average.

The country’s trade deficit expanded 23% YoY to $3.7bn in February, as imports jumped 13% YoY, faster than the 8% growth in exports. To the upside, the main driver was a 55% surge in capital goods, suggesting that capex spending is returning. Or at least it was, until the Iran conflict. We remain wary though, as the spike in energy prices – oil, gas, coal – will be reflected in the March/April numbers.

CONSUMER
Manufacturers, retailers agree with DTI to halt price increases for two weeks
  • The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that there will be a price freeze for basic necessities and prime commodities from April 1 to 16 as several manufacturers and retailers agreed to defer any price adjustments and absorb rising logistics and inventory costs during the period
    • Participating manufacturers include Century Pacific Food Inc. (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), Universal Robina Corporation (URC PM), San Miguel Foods (FB PM), Nestle Philippines, Proctor & Gamble Philippines, Unilever Philippines, among others
    • Retailers that also participated include Robinsons Retail Holdings Inc. (RRHI PM), SM Markets—food retail arm of SM Investments (SM PM), Metro Retail Stores Group Inc. (MRSGI PM), etc.
  • The DTI stated that it will monitor compliance and ensure that prices remain stable during the period

 

POWER/UTILITIES
Energy regulator pushes for staggered increases in electricity bills
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) urged power utilities to implement staggered billing with suppliers to soften the impact of rising electricity rates on consumers
  • The Department of Energy estimates that electricity prices could rise 16% in April
MACRO
Petron fuel supply to last until end of June
  • Petron Corporation (PCOR PM) acquired 2.48mn barrels of crude oil from Russia
  • With this delivery, Petron said that it has sufficient supplies “that will last up to June 30”
  • Petron may purchase more crude oil from Russia
  • Petron has a 30% market share of the country’s fuel supply and operates the only oil refinery in the Philippines
MACRO
Trade deficit widens to $3.7bn in Feb
  • The Feb trade deficit widened to US$3.7bn, expanding 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth of imports vs exports
    • This brings the 2M26 trade deficit to US$36.9bn, 6% wider YoY
  • Imports for the month grew by 13% YoY to US$11.0bn, a reversal from the 3% decline in Jan
    • The rebound to growth was primarily driven by the surge in capital goods (+55%) and the reversal to growth in consumer goods imports (+10%)
      • Consumer goods in Feb broke a previous five month streak of YoY declines
  • Exports rose 8% YoY to US$7.0bn, the thirteenth consecutive month of YoY growth
    • Electronics exports climbed 20% YoY to $4.2bn, continuing the momentum from the 19% growth in Jan
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 58% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 5% to US$3.1bn
