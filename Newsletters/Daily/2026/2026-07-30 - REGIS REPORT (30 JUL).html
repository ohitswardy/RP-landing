At BPI’s (BPI PM) briefing yesterday, management explained (in great detail) that the spike in credit cost to 120bps in 2Q was largely due to “one-off” adjustments to the macro assumptions on their ECL model (ie lower GDP growth, higher inflation, etc). Of the P7.8bn provision expense in 2Q, they estimated P2.7bn (a bit more than a third) was due to the ECL adjustments. Meanwhile, NPL ratio was flat QoQ. Perhaps the focus should be that, despite an 84% increase in provisions, earnings were still flat YoY. Upside potential for 2H26 and beyond could be a) higher NIMs from rate hikes, and b) normalizing credit costs. And the fact that the stock is at under 11.x P/book…

Meralco (MER PM) reported core income of P26.5bn for 1H26, up +4% YoY, which would be in line with Street expectations.  Volumes for 2Q alone were up just +1%, which brought 1H volume to just slightly negative (<1%). This is in contrast to the volume growth of Aboitiz Power’s (AP PM) distribution assets over the same period. The particularly high power rates within Meralco’s franchise are probably the biggest factor depressing demand.

 

Beyond the latest quarter’s operating results, Meralco has been facing the calls for it and other DUs to absorb electricity systems losses following the President’s policy speech this week. We reiterate our view on this matter: It is highly unlikely that the DUs will be compelled to absorb all the systems losses without any offsetting compensation (e.g. a direct government subsidy). However, an examination of the reasons behind the exceptionally high end-user power rates is very likely to unearth issues that could put some power firms (e.g. Meralco) under uncomfortable scrutiny. The biggest issue, in our view, relates to the power supply contracts between DUs and their affiliate gencos.

Aboitiz Power (AP PM) reported 1H core income of P18bn, +41% YoY and appearing in line with consensus estimates. AP attributed the strength to higher 2Q spot prices even as DU volumes grew +7%.

BDO (BDO PM) took full control of its thrift bank subsidiary BDO Network Bank (BNB), buying out the 14% held by their Singapore partner. On the face of it, it seems BDO overpaid, with the P5.9bn price tag translating to 2.1x P/bk on BNB’s 1Q26 numbers, with its ROE in the 10-11% range. The upside could come from BNB’s high growth (21% loan growth in 1Q), and high margins (7.1% in 1Q), which could see ROEs ratchet upwards. In the end, the transaction is quite small, with the acquisition at less than 1% of BDO’s equity base.

POWER/UTILITIES
Meralco: Genco profits slide in 2Q26 but other units offset
  • 1H26 core income up 4% to P26.5bn – In line with Street estimates. Meralco reported an 11% YoY increase in 1H26 net income to P26.3bn. Excluding FX movements and one-off items, management said core net income rose 4% YoY to P26.5bn, broadly in line with Street expectations. We note that profit growth was reduced by a significantly smaller tax-related provision reversal (net of provisions for over-recoveries) at its core distribution utility (DU). Removing the impact of provision reversals, we estimate MER would have delivered core profits up 9% in the period.

 

  • DU volumes post modest rebound in 2Q; El Niño fuels momentum in 2H. Warmer temperatures beginning in May drove a modest +1% YoY recovery in the DU segment’s electricity sales. With El Niño now upon us, management said July electricity sales growth accelerated to +6-7% YoY and expects full-year volume growth of +2-2.5%. This implies a sharp improvement in 2H26, with electricity sales likely to grow by more than 5% YoY, compared with the 0.5% contraction recorded in 1H26.

 

  • Gas plants weigh on genco segment’s 2Q performance. Derived 2Q26 profits for the generation unit fell 14% YoY to P5.3bn. Management attributed the weakness to outages of its plants in Panay and Sarangani, as well as a sharp increase in natural gas prices, which its Ilijan gas plant could not fully recover due to price caps in the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) up to April 30. In response to elevated LNG prices, management opted to source fuel for its Ilijan plant from the Malampaya gas field instead and in exchange, Meralco’s DU segment agreed to further extend its power supply agreement with the Sta. Rita gas plant until Dec 25, 2026.

 

  • Scrutiny over high power bills could delay rate review. Management expressed frustration over proposals to disallow the recovery of system losses, arguing that such a move would have broad implications for the entire power industry. Management also admitted that ongoing efforts to amend the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) could delay its tariff reset, despite the regulator's earlier indication that the process would be completed within 3Q.

 

  • Dividend of P11.758/sh.  The company declared a regular dividend of P11.758/share, with an ex-dividend date of Aug. 27 and payment scheduled for Sept. 23. On an annualized basis, this implies a dividend yield of ~4%. While the interim dividend represents a 50% payout of 1H earnings, Meralco typically declares dividends semi-annually, resulting in a full-year payout ratio of >60%.

Aboitiz Power: Strong WESM prices buoy 2Q26 core profit up 28% YoY to P10.5bn
  • In a limited press release, Aboitiz Power reported its core net income reached P18bn in 1H26, up 41% YoY and broadly in line with consensus’ full-year estimate of P36bn
    • Headline net income, which includes FX and one-offs, stood at P18.4bn, up 45% YoY
  • In 2Q26 alone, core profit rose 28% YoY to P10.5bn, boosted by the strong performance of its generation/RES unit
  • Group beneficial EBITDA improved 13% QoQ and 21% YoY to P23bn in 2Q26
    • Generation/RES EBITDA grew 15% QoQ and 20% YoY to P21bn, underpinned by (a) strong WESM prices and (b) fresh contributions from the Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) acquisition, as well as (c) its new solar facilities
    • These factors more than compensated for outages of coal-fired plants
    • Despite the +7% increase in energy sales, its distribution unit’s EBITDA fell 13% QoQ and 18% YoY to P2bn in 2Q26 due to the “early incurrence of expenses” from expansions

BANKS
BDO takes full control of BDO Network Bank - did it overpay?

According to a news report, BDO (BDO PM) bought 14.06% of BDO Network Bank (BNB) from their partners Osmanthus for P5.9bn, taking their stake up to 99.93%. The transaction values BNB at P41.96bn. Based on BNB’s published balance sheet, its book value was P19.98bn at 1Q26 and 19.11bn at end-2025. This is translates to a valuation of 2.1x P/bk using 1Q26 and 2.2x for end-2025. The same balance sheet data shows ROE of

9.75% in 1Q26 and 11.78% in 4Q25. (Although we note that the quarterly data can be a bit wonky. BNB’s FY2025 annual report had ROE at 11.7%).

 

The acquisition seems dilutive to BDO, given its own valuation at 1.05x P/bk on 1Q26 numbers, though its ROE has also come down to 12.7% in 1H26 from 14.4% in FY25.

 

That said, the dilution should be minimal, given the relatively small size of the transaction. BNB’s loan book as of 1Q26 was P127bn (net), equivalent to 3.3% of BDO’s consolidated loans. Furthermore, the P5.9bn purchase price isn’t even 1% of BDO’s equity base.

 

To the upside, BNB is growing its loans faster, up 21% YoY in 1Q26, and its NIMs are much higher at around 7.4%. This suggests BNB’s ROE could improve at a steep trajectory.
