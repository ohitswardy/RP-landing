Metrobank’s (MBT PM) 1H26 came in at P24.9bn, flat YoY with 2Q down 2%. Loan growth accelerated to 12.4%, and NIMs widened, but revenues were pulled down by trading/fx income. As with BDO and BPI, earnings were dragged by provision expense as the bank adjusted its macro assumptions for its ECL model. Overall NPLs were flat-ish, while NPL cover stayed high at 133%. That said, MBT’s credit card NPLs have been rising sharply in the last two years, though management claims NPL cover for the segment is150%. The stock is on P/bk of 0.7x, PE of 6x, paying a 7.5% yield.

The power regulator issued two contrasting orders to Meralco (MER PM), one directing a “refund” (P9.5bn) and one authorizing increased collection (P8.7bn).  Both will have no impact on Meralco’s P&L, apart from some minimal financing cost due to the working capital implications. The one difference between the two orders is that the refund will be completed over six months while the higher charges will be collected over 36 months (yes, 6x longer). This is obviously an attempt to make rates lower today.

Consistent with the banks that have reported, asset quality across the sector seemed benign in June, with NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) down 8bps MoM, and Past Due (PD) ratio down 2bps MoM. However, a deeper dive shows vastly diverging trends among sectors. Corporate loans improved overall, with Manufacturing and Real Estate sectors posting lower delinquencies, but with Construction and Trade figures deteriorating. Consumer loans (ex-mortgages) saw PDs jump 52bps MoM, driven by a massive 246bp MoM spike in Salary Loan PD ratios. Are consumers finally caving to the inflation pinch?

 

+++

 

Good news for fintechs! The BSP clarified that its recent Circular did NOT require banks and e-wallets to drop InstaPay and PesoNet fees to zero, nor even to just the P1.50 interchange fee. We had reported, though, that most banks had already followed BPI in waiving transfer fees completely. Meanwhile the two largest e-wallet operators cut their fees from P15 to P10 per transaction. We continue to argue that these moves will further accelerate the adoption of digital transactions, broadening overall financial inclusion.

Holcim has apparently agreed to sell its Philippine cement operations to Chinese firm Huaxin for US$807m. It’s unclear if this includes debt, but if does, it means it’s equivalent to US$115 per MT of capacity. Holcim previously attempted to sell its operations to San Miguel (SMC PM) for US$2.15bn but that deal was blocked due to anti-competition concerns.

BANKS
Asset quality steady in June; but Consumer NPLs surging (salary, motorcycle loans)

There was a huge divergence in asset quality trends in June, with corporate loan ratios relatively stable, while consumer loans saw big spikes in PD and NPL ratios especially from the salary loan and motorcycle loan segments.

 

Overall asset quality improved MoM in June, with NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) easing to 3.55bps, down 8bps QoQ and 1bp YoY. Meanwhile, Past Due (PD) ratio dipped 2bps MoM, but were up 8bps YoY. Restructure Loans (RL) also improved 5bps MoM to 2.02% (and down 4bps YoY). NPL Coverage popped up to 93% from 89% the previous month. As usual, we present the data exclusive of interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repo (RRPs), as these can be quite volatile on a monthly basis.

 

The asset quality improvements are notable given the (oddly) sharp slowdown in June’s loan growth to 10.5% YoY, from 12.5% in May. June’s loans (ex-IBL/RRP) were also down 0.6% MoM, which itself is odd. Historically, June has been quite strong, with loans typically up 1-3% MoM. In the last 10 years, June 2020 (Covid) was notable exception, and even that was only down 0.1% MoM.

 

By segment:

  • Loans for Production saw PD and NPL ratios drop sharply MoM
    • Improvements were driven by the Manufacturing sector, and more so by the Real Estate (incl mortgages), which posted 58bps MoM decline in PD ratio to 4.64% and a 46bp decline in NPL to 3.38. It is possible that this was related to large loan resolutions, and/or large foreclosures.
    • However, the Construction sector continued to deteriorate, with PD rates up a tad to 16.50%. The Wholesale and Retal Trade sector also saw PDs rise 9bps MoM while NPLs were up 21bps MoM.
  • On the flipside, Consumer loans (excl mortgages) saw a sharp increase in Past Due (+52bps MoM), with NPL ratio up 11bps MoM.
    • Credit Cards were surprisingly benign, at least on a MoM basis, with both PD edging down 7bps to 7.78%, and NPLs -4bps to 5.28%. However, both ratios are still up sharply YoY, with Credit Card PDs +78bps and YoY and NPLs up 76bps YoY.
    • Salary Loan PD rates exploded, up 246bps MoM to 9.50% (from 7.04% in May), while NPLs were up 57bps MoM to 5.64%. Meanwhile, Motorcycle loans jumped 128bps MoM to 27.46%, though that’s actually already MUCH better than the 32.18% in June 2025.

 

The data is consistent with the results out of the Big Three banks, all of which reported steady corporate/commercial asset quality, with credit card and personal loans deteriorating. This likely highlights the stress of slower GDP growth and higher inflation on household incomes and savings, particularly for lower-income borrowers.

 

What’s fascinating, though is that the data is NOT showing up in the Digibank sector, whose PD rates were down 69bps MoM, though NPL ratio was up 20bps MoM. On a YoY basis, though, both PD and NPL ratios were down by 420bps and 290bps respectively. Could this suggest that their business models are specifically tuned to these markets, while those of the “trad banks” are not? 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
BSP clarifies that digital transfer fees are not required to be zero
  • The BSP clarified that Circular No. 1238, which was implemented on July 4, does not require financial institutions to waive digital transfer fees
  • FinTech Alliance PH issued a statement after its meeting with Monetary Board members
  • “Following constructive dialogue between the BSP Monetary Board members and the FinTech Alliance PH, it is now clear that Circular No. 1238 is a cost-based fair pricing guideline, not a zero-based mandate. Compliance does not require zero fees, nor does it limit off-us P2P electronic fund transfer fees to switch cost alone, and FAQ No. 7 should not be read as a rigid, switch-cost-only formula. Affordability remains central to scaling financial inclusion, but it must go hand in hand with safe, reliable, and sustainable digital financial services.”
  • Fintech Alliance PH chairman Lito Villanueva said that the country’s largest banks can afford to eliminate transfer fees because they earn from a variety of revenue streams like lending and insurance
    • Mr. Villanueva is also EVP and Chief Innovation and Inclusion Officer of RCBC 
  • However, e-wallets and electronic money issuers (EMIs) depend on fee-based revenues, especially transfer charges, to sustain the business
  • Mr. Villanueva also said fund transfers entail costs before, during and after an amount is moved digitally. E-wallets invest in compliance checks, customer authentication and fraud screening to build up the integrity and security of their platforms
    • Thus, the transaction fees reflect both the switching costs and infrastructure costs for customer support
  • FinTech Alliance PH has over 150 corporate members which include banks, electronic money issuers, and other fintech firms
INDUSTRIALS
Holcim selling Philippine unit for US$807mn
  • Holcim (HOLN SW) announced it has agreed to dispose its Philippine operations in a two-step exit to China’s Huaxin Building Materials
    • An initial 67.6% stake will be sold for US$527mn
    • The remaining 31% stake will be sold for a minimum floor price of US$280mn within a three-to five-year time frame
    • Holcim can benefit from additional cash upside based on the incremental value creation during this period
  • Assuming the valuation is on an enterprise value basis, we estimate the deal is worth ~US$115 EV/MT of cement-equivalent clinker capacity
    • This is higher than Holcim’s tender offer valuation when it de-listed from the PSE in 2023 of ~US$90
    • We estimate Holcim’s cement-equivalent clinker capacity stands at 7mn MT
  • The deal is subject to customary and regulatory approvals, with the initial divestment targeted to close in 1H27
  • A new Chinese entrant could further intensify competition in an already fragile domestic cement competitive landscape
TELCOS
Telcos propose P31bn nationwide undersea fiber network
  • The Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) stated that Globe Telecom, PLDT, and Converge ICT Solutions have proposed a US$500mn (~P31.6bn) nationwide undersea fiber-optic network to expand connectivity across the Philippines
    • The cable would run from Batanes to Palawan with links extending to Visayas and Mindanao, it would also connect to international submarine cable systems
    • The project expected to provide faster and more reliable internet with approximately 70mn Filipinos poised to benefit
  • DICT Secretary Henry Aguda said that the department is currently evaluating the proposal which is still subject to gov't approval
    • The project is expected to be completed within two years once approved
    • Aguda stated that an additional benefit of the undersea cables would be earthquake and tsunami detection

MACRO
BSP expects July inflation to settle within 5.6-6.6%
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects July inflation to fall within 5.6-6.6%, note that the June inflation print was 6.4%
    • BSP expectations for July inflation are well above the BSP's 2-4% long-term target range
      • For 2026, BSP expectations for inflation this year is 6.5%
    • July inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on Aug 5 (Wednesday)
  • Upward price pressures stem from higher prices of fuel, electricity, fish, and the depreciation of the peso
    • These may have been partially offset by lower prices of rice, meat, vegetables, and fruits
    • The USD-PHP rate was at P61.43 end-Jul vs P61.23 end-Jun
  • Inflation has averaged 4.8% so far as of 6M26
    • For 2026, consensus expectations are at 5.9%
    • Regis expects inflation to average 6.7% this year
  • The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 27 (fourth out of six scheduled this year)
    • The current policy rate is 4.75%
      • For 2026, Regis and consensus expectations for rate hikes are +100bp total to 5.25% by year-end
