We initiated coverage on RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) with a Buy rating and a target price of P7.75/share.

 

RCR has, among the major Philippine REITs, the highest exposure (~50%) to the mall segment and we believe that mall REITs should trade at a premium to office REITs. Our view of the prospects for the mall business are more positive than for the other leasing segment (notably office). The average Metro Manila mall occupancy (89%) is higher than the average office occupancy (82%). Correspondingly, we forecast mall rental rates to grow faster than office rental rates.

 

The share of mall rents in RCR’s business is also set to expand further over the next few years.  About half of its portfolio is currently comprised of malls and with future infusions from its sponsor, Robinsons Land (RLC PM), this segment could comprise c. 65% over the medium term.

 

We also note that RCR has consistently grown its dividends per share (DPS) as its sponsor has been injecting assets at DPS-accretive valuations.

 

The stock currently offers a 6.6% FY26E dividend yield and a total return of 20% to our target price.

Change in regulatory compliance requirement to increase costs for residential developments
  • The reinterpretation of a law requiring developers to allocate funds for socialized housing could raise the cost to develop residential projects
  • Under Philippine law, developers must allocate a certain percentage of a residential project’s cost or area for socialized housing
    • 5% of the cost or area of condominium projects
    • 15% of the cost or area of subdivision housing projects
  • The maximum price of a socialized housing unit is
    • P950,000 for a house and lot
    • P1.8mn for a condominium
  • The Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) previously allowed developers to comply with this rule by paying 25% of the requirement to the government prior to getting a License to Sell
    • 1.25% (or 25% of 5%) for condominium projects
    • 3.75% (or 25% of 15%) for subdivision/horizontal projects
  • However, the Department of Justice ruled that developers must pay the entire 5% (for condominiums) or 15% (for horizontal projects)
  • In the medium to long term, this reinterpretation will lower the profitability of residential development
  • The impact should be minor in the near term because developers still have a lot of unsold inventory and will only launch a few new projects (if any)
  • We estimate that unsold inventory represents
    • ALI – c. 2 years of sales
    • MEG – c. 1.5 years of sales
    • RLC – c. 8.5 years of sales
    • SMPH c. 3.5 years of sales 
    • ALI - 60% of EBIT
    • MEG - 47% of EBIT
    • RLC - 22% of EBIT
    • SMPH - 23% of EBIT

MACRO
House approves bill to suspend or cut oil excise tax
  • The Lower House passed on final reading House Bill 8418, which gives authority to the President to temporarily halt or reduce excise tax rates on fuel
    • President may now tweak fuel excise taxes if the average Dubai crude oil based on Mean of Platts Singapore benchmark reaches or exceeds US$80/bbl for a month
    • Implementation will be for no more than six months and should be recommended first by the Development Budget Coordination Committee
    • An extension beyond six months will require a joint congressional resolution, but implementation cannot last longer than a year
    • President may only suspend or reduce excise tax collections on fuel products until Dec. 31, 2028
  • Excise taxes account for:
    • P10/liter for gasoline (or 11-13% of NCR retail prices of P76-96/liter)
    • P6/liter on diesel (5-6% of retail price P94-114/liter)
    • and P3/liter of kerosene (~2% of retail price P128-130/liter)
    • Estimated revenue losses could reach as much as P43bn if suspension lasts for three months and P106bn if extended to a total of six months
MACRO
Rice prices to increase due to fuel price hikes, higher freight costs
  • The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) stated that rice prices are set to increase due to the global oil crises as these will increase harvesting and transportation costs
    • On local rice, some traders and wholesalers have been said to reduce deliveries despite available rice stocks due to costlier fuel
    • DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that freight rates from Vietnam have doubled since the conflict in the Middle East, with the landed cost of imported rice reaching nearly US$500/MT
  • Tiu Laurel also said that the gov't is considering a potential P50/kg price cap for imported rice
    • The price cap for local rice is also being considered but is likely to be implemented after harvest season
  • Local retail prices of well-milled rice for both local and imported rice have risen to P47.6/kg and P46.4/kg as of March from Oct/Nov lows of P42.7/kg and P42.6/kg, respectively
    • Prior to higher fuel prices, the rise in domestic rice prices for both local and imported was said to be due to higher farmgate prices, a weaker peso, and muted rice importation in 2M25
