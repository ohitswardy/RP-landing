06 March 2026

At yesterday’s briefing, ICTSI’s (ICT PM) management noted that congestion issues at its ports in Mexico and DRC persisted in 4Q25, which contributed to 5% QoQ/12% YoY increase in yield per TEU to a new high of US$230. Profits for the quarter jumped 11% QoQ/37% YoY to a new record of US$293mn, taking full-year profits up 27% to US$1.03bn. The issue has not abated in the first few months of 2026, but the ongoing Middle East conflict has tempered the earnings upside. Management sounded very upbeat about its prospects and guided for a 29% YoY increase in 2026 capex to US$740mn, which will support its “strategic expansions” (ex- acquisitions) and incrementally add over 3mn TEUs to its current capacity of ~24mn TEUs in the next three years.

Meanwhile, results from another Razon-controlled firm are also in. Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) reported a recurring net loss hit P5.5bn, worse than REGIS and consensus forecasts. 4Q25 recurring net loss widened QoQ and YoY to P2.8bn, as the flood control corruption scandal continued to hurt the company’s performance. Volumes of every category across both Philippine casinos declined QoQ, with SEC’s VIP volume showing a particularly precipitous decline.

 

+++

 

Aboitiz Power (AP PM) announced its FY25 results yesterday in a press release. Recurring income as reported by the company was supposed to be 2% higher at P33.1bn, but this figure excludes recurring charges (depreciation and financing) at its GNPower Dinginin plant. Including those, earnings would have actually been lower by 2% and below expectations.

 

Note AP took a P13.9bn impairment charge in 4Q relating to its GNPower Mariveles facility, which was not included in the calculation of recurring income.

 

+++

 

The Feb inflation print of 2.4% appears to have been lifted by utility costs and a bit of food, compounded by the fading effects of a lower base. With energy prices skyrocketing, the CPI inflation number for March is likely to be appreciably higher.

 

+++

 

The SEC has issued a draft regulation that would impose limits on how long stockbrokers can remain directors of the Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE PM). SEC chairman Francis Lim was a former president/CEO of the PSE and is almost certainly more familiar with the capital markets and the PSE than anyone else who’s been at his current post.

POWER
Aboitiz Power FY25 recurring profit drops 2% to P33.1bn; Incurs headline net loss of P3.9bn in 4Q25
  • In a limited press release, Aboitiz Power reported headline FY25 net income reached P19.5bn, down 43% YoY
    • In 4Q25 alone, it booked a P13.9bn one-off loss related to the impairment of goodwill from the GNPower Mariveles acquisition, resulting in a net loss of P3.9bn
    • Excluding extraordinaries, FX adj, and the impact of non-cash and interest charges of GNPower Dinginin, management said that FY25 recurring profit rose 2% YoY to P33.1bn, which appears above Street estimate of P30.9bn
    • Considering the non-cash/interest costs for GNPower Dinginin, we estimate recurring profit would have been down 2% YoY
  • 4Q25 Beneficial EBITDA was up 5% QoQ and 35% YoY to P23.3bn, taking FY25 EBITDA up 9% to P80bn
    • Generation/RES segment sold 43,718GWH in FY25, up 21% YoY and suggests that in 4Q25 alone, it sold 11,580 GWH, down 9% QoQ but up 27% YoY
    • DU energy sales rose 4% YoY in FY25, implying that sales were also up 4% in 4Q25; Management cited that Beneficial EBITDA (excluding one-offs) for this segment rose 2% YoY to P8.9bn
  • The company declared a total dividend of P2.28/sh, inclusive of a P0.93/sh special dividend (ex-date: March 18, payment on March 27)
    • The dividend is lower than the prior year’s dividend of P2.35/sh and equivalent to a yield of 5%
  • Management is holding a briefing on March 9 at 3:45pm (registration link)

 

 

MACRO
Inflation accelerates to 2.4% in February
  • Headline inflation picked up to 2.4% in Feb from Jan's 2.0%
    • This was in line with consensus expectations of 2.4% and within the lower-end of the BSP's forecast range of 2.3-3.1% for Feb
    • The 2.4% print is the highest since Jan 2025 (2.9%) and marks the third consecutive increase in inflation as favorable base effects begin to wane
  • This brings 2M25 inflation to 2.2%, within the BSP's long-term target range of 2-4% and below their forecast of 3.6% for the year
    • National Statistician Claire Dennis Mapa guided that inflation may further pick up in the coming months as higher fuel prices impact other basket items
  • Inflation in Feb accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Jan in food and utilities (electricity, gas, fuel)
    • Food prices overall rose 1.8% YoY, accelerating from the 1.1% pace in Jan, driven by higher prices of fish and vegetables
      • Rice prices are down 3.3% YoY in Feb, the fourteenth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025
  • Core inflation inched up slightly to 2.9% in Feb from 2.8% in Jan, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 2.9% in Feb from 2.7% in Jan
  • For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 3.6%
    • Consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.7% and 3.0%
  • The second out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%

 

 

 

 

 

MARKET
SEC issues draft rules to limit terms of broker directors of PSE
  • The SEC issued a draft memorandum circular regarding the term limit of broker directors of the Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE PM)
  • The draft rules would limit broker-directors to a maximum cumulative period of 10 years
  • After serving a cumulative five years, a broker director would need to observe a two-year cooling off period
  • If the draft rules were to be implemented, four of the current five broker directors would no longer be eligible
    • Ma. Vivian Yuchangco – has been broker director for 28 years
    • Eddie Gobing – has been broker director for 25 years
    • Wilson Sy – has been broker director for 12 years
    • Diosdado Arroyo – has served for 6 consecutive years
    • Anthony Te would be the only current broker director who would be eligible for another term
  • The SEC said “In order to develop our capital markets, there is need to ensure fair and effective representation in an exchange and give qualified brokers opportunities to serve and provide new perspectives in the board of an exchange,”
  • At least 51 percent of the 15-member board must be non-brokers-- five independent directors and four directors representing the interests of issuers, investors and other market participants, with each sector having at least one representative
