ICTSI (ICT PM) is divesting its entire 51% stake in its port in Yantai, China for RMB775mn (~US$112mn), exceeding our valuation of US$52mn. We estimate the implied exit multiple of US$220 EV/TEU and ~15x EV/EBITDA, which would be reasonable, if not attractive, considering the port’s very low utilization and yield per TEU.

 

Just as important as the valuation, this deal demonstrates again management’s willingness to exit from operations at the right price (or if circumstances change).

Bond yields have risen sharply since the start of the Iran war, with the 10-year risk-free now at 7.14%, up more than 120bps since end-February. This could have significant mark-to-market (MTM) implications to banks’ bond portfolios. We note at the outset that these would largely be unrealized losses, but would affect banks’ capital position. We did a stress test of CET-1 ratios, simplistically assuming a 5% potential hit to the valuations of banks’ FVPL and FVOCI investments. Of the banks we cover, Metrobank (MBT PM) has by far the largest investment security book, at 40% of total assets. But it also has relatively high capital with a solo CET-1 of 15.14% at end-2025. However, Security Bank (SECB PM) is starting off at a relatively low 12.10% solo CET-1 at end-2025, and a relatively large FVPL+FVOCI book. That said, even our “stressed’ CET-1 would be well above the 10-10.5% regulatory minimum. Many caveats apply. Bottom line is that, broadly speaking, all banks we cover or closely follow have enough capital buffer to absorb a 5% MTM hit. However, lower capital ratios could affect potential to a) grow the loan book, b) absorb new NPLs, and/or c) declare dividends.

BANKS
Banks - bond yields spike, potential MTM hit on CET-1

Bond yields in the Philippines have spiked since the US-Israel-Iran war started. The 10-yr BVAL is up around 120bps to 7.14% from 5.92% at end-Feb. Note that, even before the conflict started, the 10-yr rate was barely changed despite the 225bps cut in the BSP’s policy rate since mid-2024 (see chart below). However, the recent jump in bond yields raises the question of potential MTM losses for banks. This would have potential knock-on effects on capital ratios, and therefore on their ability to expand balance sheets, and to absorb credit quality deterioration.

 

We did a (very) basic exercise below. Using the banks’ 2025 FS, we first looked at the overall investment securities books of the major listed banks, as a % of total assets. Unsurprisingly, MBT was the most heavily invested, at roughly 40% of its balance sheet. We then zoomed in on the FVPL and FVOCI books – which are subject to MTM – as a % of banks’ equity bases. Again, MBT was the highest at 253%. But we were surprised at how many banks had 90% or more of their equity exposed to FVPL+FVOCI – SECB at 127%, PNB at 98%, and CBC at 92%.

 

We then looked at banks’ capital bases, and their ability to absorb MTM losses. It is difficult to estimate the exact impact per bank given lack of detail (duration, currency, etc) of their portfolios. For simplicity’s sake, we assumed a 5% hit across banks’ portfolios. While this would mean a potential 13% reduction to MBT’s equity, we note that their solo 15.14% CET-1 ratio was high. Even at over 190bps reduction to CET-1, they would still be at 13.22%. CBC and PNB also have high enough solo CET-1 ratios to absorb potential hits.

 

The most at-risk then would be SECB, given its already-low solo CET-1 ratio of 12.10% as of end-2025. Assuming a 5% MTM hit to its FVPL+FVOCI book, this could knock off 77bps, and take solo CET-1 down to 11.3%. While this would still be well above the 10.5% (ish) BSP minimums, it could limit the bank’s ability to grow its loan book, and/or limit its tolerance for new NPL formation (150% risk weight).

 

CAVEATS, CAVEATS – Note that this exercise is purely academic. We would highlight a few caveats. Firstly, these are only MTM losses. If the banks hold them to maturity, then the losses go away over time. Secondly, we are not privy to the banks’ mix of bonds, either by currency, or by maturity, so the 5% loss we assumed could be wildly off, and overly bearish. In 2022, for example, the 10-yr BVAL also jumped 100-140bps. That year, MTM losses ranged from 3-5% for banks we cover. Third, we also don’t know how each banks’ books have moved in the last 2.5 months. Finally, if comes to it, the BSP has in past crises allowed banks to move bonds – especially sovereigns – from FVOCI to HTM – even back-dating the shift – to prevent such MTM losses.

 

To reiterate, this is just a sensitivity analysis of banks’ balance sheets, and is by no means a description of actual MTM losses.

MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture to distribute input cost subsidies to farmers and fisherfolk
  • Dept. of Agriculture (DA) spokesperson Arnel De Mesa stated that the department is currently distributing P150mn in agricultural fuel subsidies and plans to conduct a separate P10bn broad cost-relief program by early next month
  • P75mn fuel subsidies will be allocated equally between farmers and fisherfolk with P5,000 to farmers who use fuel-powered machinery and P3,000 for fisherfolk who own boats of three gross tons or less
    • The program's funding will be sourced from unspent funds carried over from the 2025 General Appropriations Act
  • De Mesa also stated that the larger relief program will be drawn from a P10bn fund under the DA's 2026 budget, and cover around 4.1mn beneficiaries with a payout of ~P2,350 each
MACRO
BOP posts US$2.3bn deficit in Feb; GIR settles at US$113.3bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Feb posted a deficit of US$2.3bn
    • Wider than both the US$373mn deficit in Jan and a reversal from the US$3.09bn surplus in Feb 2025
    • This brings the 2M26 deficit to US$2.7bn
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP)
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Feb stood at US$113.3bn, 5% higher YoY and up from the US$112.6bn in the previous month of Jan
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn
