REGIS REPORT

11 June 2026

ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port in Mexico, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), sustained its strong momentum, posting 26% YoY volume growth in May and lifting cumulative 5M26 volumes up 14% YoY, tracking comfortably above our full-year forecast. The port also started to roll out 5-10% tariff increases across various services this month.  We estimate CMSA accounts for 20% of FY26E revenues and 20% of EBITDA. 

+++

Consumer loans re-surged in 1Q26, growing 22.6% YoY, the fastest pace of growth since 1Q2015, and now account for 23.2% of total loans, a new all-time high. Growth was again led by Credit Cards, up 28.7% YoY, marking nearly 4 full years of 25%++ CAGR. Credit card receivables of P1,237bn are now bigger than Mortgages, at P1,218bn. It’s not all good news though – Credit card NPLs were up 17bps and 38bps YoY, with the nominal PHP value of credit card NPLs up 39% YoY. While we believe the consumer credit market is still under-penetrated (at least in the formal sector), the deterioration of asset quality is a clear downside risk for the sector.

+++

Megaworld (MEG PM) sold 246m shares of MREIT (MREIT PM) at P13.75/share, equivalent to 5% of the latter’s share capital.  This increases MREIT’s float ahead of an expected asset injection from MEG.

+++

We refresh our estimates for Century Pacific (CNPF PM), trimming our profit estimates by 9-10% near-term given the challenging environment on the cost side.

With price increases finally implemented last month, margins should normalize. Rising inflation, however, will be a challenge. The looming “super” El Nino could also be a factor – it helped fish (tuna) catch/supply the last time around (2023) but affected it differently in prior instances.

The stock’s down over 30% YTD, sharply underperforming the Philippine market, and is now at 13x PER (vs historical average of 18x). Given its consistent, dependable track record, we think this weakness is an opportunity. BUY.

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes rise 26% in May; Implements 5-10% tariff hikes this month
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that throughput at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo SA (CMSA), rose 26% YoY to a monthly record level of 152k TEUs in May
    • Growth accelerated from the 21% pace in April 2026 and 16% in May 2025
    • Brings CMSA’s 5M26 volumes up 14% YoY to 717k TEUs, trending well ahead of our full-year forecast (1.66mn TEUs, +8%)
  • Moreover, CMSA implemented tariff hikes of 5-10% on various services (here), including the following: stevedoring (+5%); arrastre (+8%); storage (+10%)
    • Our forecast assumes CMSA’s yield will increase by +10% this year
  • Every 10% increase in CMSA’s topline will raise consolidated FY26E EBITDA by 3% and profit by 4%
  • We estimate CMSA accounts for 10% of ICTSI’s consolidated FY26E volumes, 20% of revenues, and 23% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 24x FY26E PER and 14x EV/EBITDA (vs. peer average of 16x PER/11x EBITDA and historical average of 19x PER / 10x EBITDA)

BANKS
Consumer loans jump 22.6% YoY in 1Q26; NPLs edge higher QoQ

Consumer loans in the Philippine banking system grew 22.6% YoY, the fastest quarterly YoY pace since 1Q2015, and re-accelerating sharply from the 19.1% clocked in 4Q25. That said, the numbers included an unusual 147% surge in “other consumer loans”. While that was a small number – at just 3% of total consumer loans – it did push the overall number up. Excluding the “others” account, consumer loans would have grown 18.9% YoY. Consumer loans now account for 23.2% of total loans, by far a new all-time high. Consumer NPLs inched up 9bps QoQ, but were flat (-1bp) YoY.

 

Apart from the “others” account, growth was again led by Credit Cards, up 28.7% YoY, marking the 15th consecutive quarter of >25% YoY growth (since 3Q22). For perspective, at credit card receivables are now larger than mortgages (P1,237bn vs P1,218bn). However, credit card asset quality continued to deteriorate, with NPL ratio at 4.98%, up 17bps QoQ and 38bps YoY. This is noteworthy considering the fast growth of the denominator – in nominal terms, credit card NPLs were up 39% YoY.

 

Mortgages grew just 8.0% YoY, the slowest pace since 1Q24. NPL ratios in the segment was at 6.11%, up 3bps QoQ but down 24bps YoY.

 

Motor Vehicle loans, meanwhile, decelerated sharply, up 11.2% YoY, from 14.4% in 4Q25, and from 18.5% YoY in 1Q25. NPLs were up 13bps QoQ to 5.18% and up 25bps YoY. Banks have tightened credit standards on car loans, particularly those originated at the dealership. The “incentives” given to dealers have spike to the high-teens (as % of the loan amount), which the dealers get up-front, rewarding them to push for loans for less credit-worthy buyers, and for more unpopular car models. Banks have anecdotally leaned more towards branch-originated loans, and for more well-known car brands.

 

Salary loans jumped 50% YoY, though NPLs here were also deteriorating, up 29bps QoQ (but down 20bps YoY).

 

We remain wary of consumer loan NPLs, particularly in the unsecured segment of credit cards and salary/other loans. Especially so with the sharp increase in inflation in recent months as a result of the US-Iran conflict.

MACRO
Business confidence falls further in April but outlook improves for the year ahead
  • The BSP’s Business Expectations Survey (BES) showed that current-month confidence index worsened from -24.3% in March to -35.8% in April
    • This marks the second straight month of decline
    • Firms attributed the sentiment decline to elevated oil prices due to the Middle East conflict which put pressure on both operating costs and consumers' purchasing power
      • Other notable concerns were domestic competition, weak demand, and high interest rates
  • The confidence index for three-months and 12-months ahead, however, rebounded to -7.5% and 19.5% from -17.3% and 11.7%, respectively
    • Firms were less pessimistic about the next three months given the expectations that the resumption of classes will “drive demand for loans and financing products as well as for clothing and apparel”
    • 12-months ahead, survey respondents cite expectations of higher sales and income on improved macroeconomic conditions and a possible resolution of the conflict in the Middle East
  • A random sample of 507 firms out of the top 7,000 corporations were surveyed nationwide
