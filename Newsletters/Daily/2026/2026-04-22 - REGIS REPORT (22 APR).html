The Alliance Global (AGI PM) group held an earnings briefing yesterday for its FY25 results, as the group reported groupwide FY25 recurring profit being flat at P17.3bn. For 4Q alone, we estimate that recurring profit fell 20% YoY to P3.4bn, largely due to losses at Emperador (EMI PM), which suffered from a YoY decline in revenue and an increase in opex. 4Q25 EBITDA for EMI fell 57% YoY as revenue of both the brandy and whisky businesses dropped.

 

The other subsidiaries fared better than Emperador but still saw declines in operating profit. 4Q25 operating profit of Megaworld (MEG PM) fell 12% YoY due to a 12% decline in residential revenue while 4Q25 EBITDA of Travellers fell 13% YoY due to higher cash costs.

 

Management also shared its views regarding the impact of the Iran conflict on the various businesses:

 

  • Megaworld – Most cost items have been contracted at fixed-rates but there might be demand erosion for hotels and affordable housing
  • Travellers – Utility costs have been contracted at fixed rates, but the cancellation of some MICE events will lead to lower revenue
  • Emperador – The company is seeing downtrading on both the whisky and brandy segments while its supply chain is experiencing disruptions

Jaime Augusto Zobel (“JAZA”) stepped down from the chairmanship of Globe (GLO PM) at yesterday’s annual shareholders’ meeting, with Cezar Consing taking over the role. JAZA also relinquished his board seat at the telco in favor of daughter Mariana Zobel. JAZA’s son Jaime Alfonso, meanwhile, was elected co-vice chairman.

 

JAZA was the driving force behind the Ayala (AC PM) group’s investment into the telecom sector more than three decades ago, through what became Globe Telecom. His retirement marks the final transition from his generation to the next.

CONGLOS
Alliance Global 4Q25 net profit falls 20% YoY
  • Alliance Global said that its FY25 “normalized attributable net profit” (which excludes gains from the deconsolidation of GADC) was steady at P17.3bn
    • Headline profit grew 19% to P20.7bn
  • We estimate that 4Q25 attributable net profit fell 20% YoY to P3.4bn, largely due to losses at Emperador
  • Megaworld’s 4Q25 net profit rose 2% YoY (-3% QoQ) to P5.1bn
    • 4Q25 operating profit fell 12% YoY (down 19% QoQ) to P6.0bn, due to a 12% YoY (and QoQ) drop in residential revenue 
  • Travellers’ 4Q25 EBITDA fell 13% YoY (up 4% QoQ) to P2.6bn, as higher opex offset the increase in revenue
    • 4Q25 GGR rose 5% YoY (flat QoQ) to P8.0bn, as strong mass win rate compensated for the decline in VIP rolling chip
  • Emperador's 4Q25 profit fell 153% YoY (down 211% QoQ) to -P813mn, primarily due to a significant increase in opex in the brandy business alongside continued YoY declines in revenue for both the brandy and whisky segments
    • Both segments saw sales decline YoY but improve QoQ in 4Q as brandy revenues fell 17% YoY to P9.5bn (+21% QoQ) while whisky top line fell 10% YoY to P6.4bn (+23% QoQ)
  • Regarding the Iran conflict:
  • Megaworld
    • Malls and offices – the company said that 100% of power requirements are contracted from renewable energy providers; demand should not be affected by the conflict
    • Residential -  Its construction costs are locked in via contracts, contractors have not asked to renegotiate terms; demand for low/affordable housing could be affected
    • Hotels – Occupancy could decline due to fewer international tourists
  • Travellers International
    • There has been no impact on gaming demand so far
    • However, some MICE events have been cancelled so non-gaming revenue will be affected
    • Utilities are mostly locked in at fixed rates; there could be some cost increases on food
  • Emperador
    • Demand for its emerging brandy products (CLVB Emperador and Fundador Super Special) remained strong in April, continuing momentum from 4Q25 and 1Q26
    • "Deep downtrading" in whisky continues with affordable to premium products resilient but rare and aged SKUs remain challenged
    • The company stated that there have been delays in shipments given higher fuel costs and rerouting of ships due to the Middle East conflict
      • Shipments of imported products are being expedited alongside measures to mitigate rising costs amid elevated inflationary pressures
