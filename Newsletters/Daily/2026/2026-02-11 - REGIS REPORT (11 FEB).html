11 February 2026

We initiate coverage of Maynilad Water (MYNLD PM) with a BUY rating and a target price of P24/sh. Maynilad is the exclusive holder of the concession for the West Zone of Metro Manila, which covers a population of over 10mn and provides for an attractive guaranteed return of 12% on net cash expenditures. Given that leakage or non-revenue water (NRW) is still above 30% and wastewater coverage remains low, the West Zone concession poses operational challenges, but it also offers significant opportunities to invest and grow its regulatory value (or “Opening Cash Position”/OCP) at a compounded rate of 12%. 

 

At the current share price, the shares are attractively valued, trading at just 1.1x its regulatory value or OCP, a large discount to Manila Water (MWC PM), which we estimate now trades at over 5x the regulatory value of the East Zone. Cash flows are relatively predictable and given management’s dividend policy of paying out the higher of 50% of earnings or 40% of adjusted profits, we anticipate the company to pay dividends of at least P1/sh, which is equivalent to a yield of 5%.

 

Moreover, with net gearing at only 60%, the balance sheet has ample headroom to raise dividends. Given that the concession agreement is agnostic on the capital structure, payouts could theoretically be increased to 100%, potentially doubling our projected yield to 10%.

According to a filing with the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), Meralco (MER PM) is proposing a distribution tariff of P2.34/kWh, a >70% increase from the current P1.35/kWh. The ERC is supposed to come out with a decision by June 2026, though it is worth noting that Meralco’s previous tariff review has been pending since 2015.

 

We will need to review the filing, particularly assumptions on the regulatory WACC. While a simplistic calculation suggests a potential P30-35bn earnings uplift, we think it is unlikely that the ERC will approve such an increase.   

 

+++

 

Banks’ asset quality ratios improved dramatically in December, with NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) dropped 21bps MoM to 3.32%, and PD ratio fell 26bps MoM to 4.25%. However, we note that this was likely helped by chunky write-offs, as the nominal PHP amounts fell 3.3% MoM for NPLs, and 3.1% MoM for PDs. Moreover, system-wide loan loss reserves (LLR) dipped 1.3% MoM, taking the LLR ratio to 2.98%, down 17bps MoM.

 

Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) slowed to 9.9% YoY from 10.3% in November, and marking the first dip back into single-digit territory since Sept 2024.

President Marcos ordered Congress to expedite the passage of 21 priority bills. BANKS
Asset quality dramatically improves in Dec, helped by large write-offs

Headline asset quality data show sharp improvement in NPL and PD ratios. However, this seems to have been helped in large part due to significant write-offs (for the ex-IBL/RRP data) and by a chunky increase in IBL/RRP for the reported data (which is why we use ex-IBL/RRP data anyway).

 

Sector-wide NPL ratio (ex-IBL.RRP) dropped to 3.32%, lower by 21bps MoM and 15bps YoY. In PHP terms, NPLs stood at P526.5bn, down 3.3% MoM (supporting our write-off theory) but still up 5.2% YoY. Past Due (PD) ratio fell 26bps MoM to 4.25%, from 4.51% in November, but was up slightly (6bps) YoY from 4.19% at end-2024. In nominal terms, PDs totaled 674bn, down 3,1% MoM, but up 11.4% YoY. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio was 2.12%, down 3bps MoM and YoY. NPL Cover was 97%. Further supporting our write-off theory, sector-wide loan loss reserves (LLR) shrank 1.3% MoM, with the LLR ratio down 17bps MoM to 2.98%.

 

Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP), meanwhile, decelerated to 9.9% YoY, dipping just below double digits for the first time since September 2024.

 

By bank type, it seems the large write-offs were done at the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs) and the smaller Digibanks.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Marcos pushes for Congress to pass 21 priority bills before fifth SONA
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. during the 3rd Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting ordered Congress to expedite the passage of 21 priority bills as soon as possible to "uplift the living conditions" of Filipinos
    • He stated that he targets the passage of the priority bills by June, in time for his fifth State of the Nation Address SONA)
  • Some of the 21 priority bills are as follows:
    • Anti-corruption/gov't transparency related bills such as Anti-political Dynasty Act, Party-list System Reform Act, the creation of the Independent People's Commission Act, amendments to the Bank Deposits Secrecy Law, and Citizen Access and Disclosure of Expenditures for National Accountability (CADENA) 
    • Infra and utilities bills like the creation of the Department of Water Resources Bill, Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA), Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) Strengthening Bill
    • Other bills such as the abolition of the travel tax, amendments to the 4Ps conditional cash transfer social welfare program, amendments to the Rice Tariffication Law
  • Aside from the 21 priority bills endorsed by President Marcos, 31 priority bills are still pending in the 20th Congress—bringing the total to 52 priority measures
    • 12 of the 52 LEDAC measures have already passed third and final reading which include:
      • EPIRA, the proposed Dept. of Water Resources, Bank Deposits Secrecy Law amendments, etc.
    • Other pending bills that are in earlier stages of passage include online gambling regulation, digital payments, etc.
