27 April 2026

BDO’s (BDO PM) 1Q26 NPAT of P20.1bn was up 2% YoY, behind REGIS and consensus dragged by “pre-emptive” provisioning. Operations were actually respectable, with PPOP +16% YoY. Loans grew 16% YoY, though NIMs were down 18bps QoQ and 24bps YoY on a combination of loan mix (likely strong corporate loans), strong growth in the Treasury book, and a surge in expensive TDs and bills. Fee incomes were soft (+3%), but were offset by insurance income (+27%). But provision expense doubled YoY to P6.1bn, for a credit cost of about 65bps (vs 44bps for FY2025). This was despite NPL ratio improving 15bps QoQ and NPL cover staying high at 132%. The press release noted the “evolving geo-political risk conditions” as a reason for pre-emptively hiking reserves.

We reduce our profit forecast for FY2026E by 8%, raising our credit cost assumption to 71bps (from 51bps previously) as we believe the economic stress from the US-Israel-Iran conflict will persist for the balance of the year. We also cut our target price to P180/sh (from P200/sh). We maintain our Buy on valuations – the stock is now trading on 0.88x P/bk on our 2026E forecasts, lower than during Covid, and levels not seen since just after the Global Financial Crisis in 2009.

Terms of the agreement – the actual location, the costs, etc – are not yet final. The Department of Energy (DOE) has indefinitely postponed the bidding of coal contracts, including that of Semirara (SCC PM). The bid was scheduled for tomorrow, 28 April, but another potential bidder, San Mugel (SMC PM) had asked for more time to evaluate the project.

POWER
Department of Energy postpones bidding of Semirara coal contract
  • The Department of Energy said that the April 28 deadline for the submission and opening of bid documents has been postponed “until further notice”
    • Postponement aims to ensure that all issues raised during the pre-submission conferences are “fully considered and addressed” before announcing the revised deadline
  • Note that the pre-submission conferences have been attended by Semirara (SCC PM) and San Miguel (SMC PM) affiliated companies
  • The delay only aggravates the uncertainty surrounding SCC’s coal contract, which is due to expire in July 2027, as well as prolongs the DMCI (DMC PM) group’s dividend declaration
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in March
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 25bps MoM to 20.3% (in March) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index declined 8bps MoM to 23.6%
  • Net foreign selling amounted to US$231mn, a reversal from net foreign buying of US$143mn in February
  • Among the PSEi constituents, 12 saw increases in foreign ownership while 18 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • BDO – Foreign ownership declined 36bps MoM to 27.0%. It also saw the highest net foreign selling for the month, at US$49.7mn
    • BPI – foreign ownership fell 35bps MoM to 17.9%, as net foreign selling hit US$27.5mn, the third highest for the month   
    • ICT – foreign ownership climbed 43bps MoM to 36.8%. It also saw the highest net foreign buying for the month, at US$31.2mn.
    • JGS – foreign ownership rose 28bps MoM to 13.5%, as net foreign buying reached US$8.0mn, the second highest for the month

MACRO
Initial operations of Pax Silica AI hub to take at least two years
  • Joshua Bingcang, President of the Bases Conversion Development Authority (BCDA), said that the Pax Silica AI industrial hub will not be operational until at least two years from now
  • The Philippine and the United States have not finalized terms on the costs associated with the construction and operations; as well as the precise location and boundaries of the property
    • The initial plans are for the hub to be situated in a 1,600-hectare site in New Clark City
  • Mr. Bingcang said the United States would act as master lessee and take on certain development responsibilities, including building roads—an arrangement he said could save the government over P10bn
  • The project is still in its early stages and will move forward in phases once formal agreements are in place
  • Under the BCDA’s proposal to the US government, the agency would grant a two-year grace period on lease payments for the property, serving as an unconditional in-kind contribution to support the initiative
    • The annual lease rate beginning the third year will be covered in a separate agreement
  • The facility’s locators will be granted fiscal and non-fiscal incentives under the CREATE MORE law
  • The hub will not be exclusive to American firms
    • Trade Undersecretary Ceferino Rodolfo said that five firms in East Asia and America have expressed interest
  • Mr. Bingcang said that BCDA —not any US entity— would screen and approve companies
  • He also clarified that there are no provisions granting diplomatic immunity or allowing the site to operate under US law
MACRO
Gov’t agencies told to cut spending amid oil crisis
  • In a budget circular issued on April 23, the Department of Budget and Management directed state entities to adopt “economy measures” following the declaration of a national energy emergency
    • The DBM expects the cost-cutting measures will generate savings ranging from P12.8bn to P25.6bn from March to December 2026, depending on the level of compliance
    • These estimated savings appear miniscule relative to the 2026 General Appropriations Act of P6.79tn (here)
  • Agencies are required to cut at least 20% from selected maintenance and other operating expenses (MOOE), while deferring non-critical capital outlays
  • All agencies covered by the circular should submit their proposed savings not later than May 15
    • Circular covers all departments, agencies, and operating units of the National Government, including state universities and colleges, as well as government-owned and -controlled corporations
    • Offices with autonomy — including the legislative and judicial branches, the constitutional commissions, and local government units — were urged to implement similar measures

 

MACRO
Business confidence hits lowest level in 25 years    
  • The BSP’s Business Expectations Survey (BES) showed that current-month confidence index plunged fell to -24.3% in March from 8.2% in February
    • This is the lowest level since 2001
    • Firms attributed the decline to the conflict in the Middle East, which led to higher prices of fuel and other goods
  • The confidence index for the quarter ahead also fell to -17.3% from 37.4% previously
  • Meanwhile, the confidence index for the year ahead dropped to 11.7% from 51.1%
RETAIL
Retailers take hit as Mideast war drags consumer spending
  • The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) said most retailers experienced weaker traffic in the first half of April, as consumers react to high fuel prices and broader market uncertainty
    • According to PRA president Alice Liu, who is also the president and CEO of Gold ABC Inc., the company behind fashion brands Penshoppe, Oxygen, Regatta, etc
    • Ms. Liu said that businesses have taken a wait-and-see approach while preparing contingency measures in case the conflict drags on
  • PRA has over 400 member companies (here), including SM Retail, Wilcon, 7-11, etc.
