REGIS REPORT

26 February 2026

Meralco (MER PM) reported FY25 core income of P50.6bn, up 12% YoY. The bottom-line figure is ahead of Street estimates despite the soft electricity volumes (-0.6%) for its distribution utility (DU) business.

 

While underlying demand is unexciting in the near term, the company is facing a key event this year: a tariff reset for its DU. We are inclined to think there is a greater chance of Meralco finally completing its tariff reset now (by June this year) than at any time in the past decade.  Without doubt, the outcome of this reset will be the single most important factor in its earnings trajectory over the next four years.

 

More on Meralco below.

 

+++

 

In a limited press release, Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) reported FY25 profits up 312% YoY to P6.27bn. Ex FX gains, we estimate recurring profits stood at P5.5bn, up 1.8x YoY on the back of a 28% YoY jump in nickel ASPs and 9% YoY increase in sales volumes. The results were broadly in line with Street estimates but exceeded our forecast.

 

The company declared a regular dividend of P0.14/sh, implying a 35% payout ratio and an approximate 3% yield. While the minimum payout ratio was raised, no special dividend was declared in this initial tranche. We nevertheless anticipate special dividends to be announced in 2H.  Management is holding a briefing at 2pm today (registration link). 

 

+++

 

Vehicle sales for January are down 10% based on data from CAMPI. Including data from other non-CAMPI members (e.g. BYD), vehicle sales would still be down 9%.

 

+++

 

Fintech holding firm Maya is reportedly considering a dual listing which will involve an initial listing in the U.S., followed by a listing in the local exchange. We reckon a listing in the U.S. would likely allow a better exit price for some of its shareholders in an IPO but would run the risk of the name becoming an orphaned stock once its bookrunners drop coverage soon after. A local listing at the PSE would improve the chances of it having some sell side coverage longer-term.

 

PLDT (TEL PM) holds a stake of ~37% in Maya and is its single largest shareholder.

POWER
Meralco FY25 profit up 12% to P50.6bn (ahead)
  • FY25 core profit up 12% to P50.6bn (ahead).  Meralco reported FY25 headline net income of P51bn (+11%) and core net income of P50.6bn, just mildly exceeding Street expectations despite soft volume growth.  Profit growth for the year was driven by contributions from the LNG terminal acquisition, a strong reserve market in Visayas, and provision reversals.  These factors outweighed softer distribution volumes and lower margins at the RES business.  Derived 4Q25 core profits rose by 5% to P10.5bn, moderating from the 9M25 growth rate of 14%, as volume weakness persisted at the distribution unit (DU) amid weather disruptions and broader economic softness. 

 

  • Soft electricity demand persisting into 1Q26.  Management noted that cooler temperatures and macroeconomic challenges continued to weigh on electricity demand in the first two months of 2026. It is nonetheless hoping for a modest demand rebound of 3% this year.

 

  • Terra Solar Phase 1 at 75% completion.  Meralco is slowly progressing on the energization of Terra Solar Phase 1, with only 1,288MW PV panels installed as of end-January and another 200hectares land acquisition pending. Terra Solar is, in theory, committed to provide 600MW of mid-merit power to Meralco’s distribution unit by this month (Feb 2026).  Although the installed PVs remain below the 2,500MW initial energization target, management remains confident it can complete the project by August, when replacement power obligations commence.

 

  • Open to partnership over Semirara coal contract. The company indicated that its interest in the contract for the coal mining rights in Semirara island remains preliminary, although it is open to exploring a potential partnership with Semirara (SCC PM) to help secure long-term fuel supply for its existing and future coal-fired plants.  In our view, Meralco’s comments suggest it is unlikely to pursue a serious competing bid against SCC.

 

  • Declares dividend of P16.67/sh. The company declared dividends of P16.67/sh for its 2H results, equivalent to a 62.5% dividend payout (vs. policy of minimum 50%) Note Meralco pays dividends twice a year.

 

  • Aiming for a tariff reset by June.  Meralco management expressed optimism that its pending tariff application for a P2.34/kwh rate will be settled by June.  The P2.34/kwh application is under what the regulator now calls its “First Regulatory Period” (1RP) and represents ~70% increase over the current tariff of P1.35/kwh.  Among other things, the tariff application assumes a large inflation adjustment for its regulatory asset base (RAB) from the last reset and a regulatory return of 14.6%, pretax.  The ERC is also aiming to settle the tariff applications of three other DUs at the same time.

Nickel Asia FY25 core profit up 2x to P5.5bn; Declares 1H div of P0.14/sh
  • In a press release, Nickel Asia reported a net income of P6.27bn, up >2x YoY
    • Removing the P800mn FX gain in 1Q, we estimate recurring profit stood at P5.5bn, up 1.8x YoY due to (a) 28% YoY jump in nickel ASPs and (b) 9% YoY increase in sales volumes
    • The core profit figure is mildly below consensus (P5.6bn) but ahead of Regis’ P5.3bn forecast
  • Derived 4Q25 profit of P2.4bn was up nearly 5x YoY (-15% QoQ)
  • The company declared regular dividends of P0.14/sh, which would represent 46% of our full-year forecast (P0.31/sh) and would equate to ~35% payout ratio (vs. policy of min. of 30% payout)
    • The dividend yield on this tranche is 2.6%
  • More after the briefing at 2PM today (registration link)
CONGLOS
Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 10% YoY in January
  • CAMPI data showed that vehicle sales fell 10% YoY to 33,696 units in January
    • Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI
    • Including non-CAMPI members, CAMPI estimates car sales stood at 35,053 units, which would be ~9% YoY lower
  • Sales of electric vehicles (ex-BYD) totaled 2,610 units, with hybrids accounting for 79% of total EV sales, PHEVs at 11% and BEVs at 10%
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 16,346 units, down 10% YoY
    • We are forecasting sales volumes of 247k units in 2026, up 7% from last year’s sales of 229k units
    • Every 5% reduction in our sales volume forecast will cut TMP’s profit estimate by 2% and GTCAP’s profit by <1%
    • TMP is targeting unit sales to grow up to 5% (~229k-240k units) this year
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 23% of FY26E profit
  • GTCAP trades at a 47% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,208/sh and at 4.6x PER
