25 August 2026

The Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is aiming to introduce reforms in order to improve the domestic capital markets. Among them is a plan to increase (enforce) minimum capital requirement for stock brokers. The SEC imposed a rule requiring brokers to have a minimum capital of P100m way back in 2001 (yes, twenty-five years ago) but the rule was never enforced in order to give time for brokers to raise capital.

 

Now, twenty-five years later, the SEC appears to be revisiting this regulation. We wouldn’t hold our breath waiting for the reforms, however. One should anticipate opposition from some areas that aim to stymie/water down their implementation. Do not be surprised if many of these (like the term limits on broker directors of the PSE) end up getting stuck in the court system.

President Marcos signed a new Executive Order (EO #122) establishing a policy framework for critical minerals. Among other things the EO seeks to identify these “critical minerals” and establish an industry work plan covering them. It also orders the creation a one-stop shop for permit processing for related activities and the provision of incentives for the related downstream activities. Interestingly, a “use it or lose it” policy covering mineral rights will also be established, which could affect vast mining concessions that have been idle for decades.

Digital payments topped P3trn for the first time in July, growing 46% YoY to P3,072bn. Notably, InstaPay re-accelerated to P1,531bn, up 61% YoY (from +56% in June), with the volume of transactions re-surging 106% YoY (from +96% in June), likely driven by the reduction / waiver in fees in early-to mid-July. This should keep the financial inclusion momentum up, supporting banks, fintechs, online lenders, etc.

Credit rating agency Moody’s kept its Baa2 stable rating for the Philippines despite the slowdown in economic growth and shortfalls in government revenue collections.

 

+++

 

Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) is set to delist from the PSE on Aug 31 following years of being traded at abysmally low valuations, like many other names on the Exchange. The Gokongwei family were able to secure 99.7% of RRHI’s shares after a tender offer.

MARKET
SEC looking at more capital market reforms
  • SEC Chairperson Atty. Francis Lim is studying potential capital market reforms
  • The SEC is considering raising the P100mn minimum capital requirement for stockbrokers, which was instituted in 2001
  • It is also working with the PSE and the Philippine Dealing and Exchange (PDEx) on market-making rules for publicly listed securities (in order to enhance liquidity)
  • It is consulting with the World Bank to review the framework for public offerings, including requirements for debt and equity securities
    • The review seeks to make capital raising more accessible by making disclosure requirements more proportionate to the type and risks of securities being offered
  • It is collaborating with the ADB to develop a Philippine Capital Market Master Plan
    • The goal is to improve the competitiveness of the domestic market
MINING
President Marcos orders creation of critical minerals policy framework
  • President Marcos signed Executive Order No. 122, which establishes a national policy framework for the critical minerals industry and reorganizes the Mining Industry Coordinating Council (MICC)
    • The MICC will be expanded to include strategic cabinet members, local authorities, and indigenous representation
    • Creation of a comprehensive industry work plan by the MICC within 90 days, in which international sourcing standards, environmental safeguards, and valuation frameworks are outlined
    • The DENR was directed to issue a Philippine Critical Minerals List within 30 days of the order taking effect
    • The DENR and the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) will create a streamlined permit processing within six months from the issuance of EO 122, which will entail a fully digital virtual one-stop shop platform to be implemented within a one-year period
    • The Department of Trade and Industry is tasked to provide incentives for downstream refining, battery production, and side-stream industries
    • A “use it or lose it” policy will be implemented for critical minerals mining tenements, immediately cancelling and reallocating freed-up mineral areas into declared reservations
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital payments top P3trn in July; Instapay re-surges post fee cuts

Transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) expanded 45.9% YoY to P3,072bn in July, a new record high, and crossing the P3trn mark for the first time. For some scale, that likely already exceeds monthly nominal GDP. The July growth number was a tad faster than the 45.4% clocked in June. Year-to-date, digital payments amounted to P19,162bn, up 44.8% YoY, faster than the 42% pace in FY2025.

 

Payments over PESONet – for larger, batched transactions – came in at a new all-time high of P1,541bn, up 33.8% YoY, slower than the 37.5% growth the previous month. This took the 7M26 figure to P9,653bn, up 32.2% YoY a shade faster than the 30.9% growth in 2025.

 

Meanwhile, real-time, smaller-ticket transactions over InstaPay also reached a monthly record of P1,531bn, re-surging 60.6% YoY, compared to the 54.8% pace in June. The YTD total was P9,509bn, growing 60.3% YoY, versus 57.3% for FY2025. The volume of InstaPay transactions also re-accelerated to 761mn, up 106.2% (vs +96.2% YoY the previous month).

 

We expect the value – and more so the volume – of transactions to re-surge in coming months. Recall that in early July (as we reported) banks and e-wallets responded to a BSP Circular, and dropped bank transfer fees, with many waiving charges altogether. This should further spur financial inclusion, supporting banks, fintechs, online lenders, etc.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Moody's affirms "Baa2 stable" investment rating for the Philippines
  • Moody's Ratings retained its "Baa2 stable" investment-grade rating for the Philippines on expectations of a gradual economic recovery alongside continued fiscal consolidation efforts by the gov't
    • Recall that in April earlier this year, Moody’s also retained its Baa2 stable rating for the Philippines
  • The "stable" outlook was maintained given the country's medium-term growth potential and expectations of stabilizing fiscal metrics over the next two years despite headwinds
    • Moody's also cited ample access to financing markets and sufficient international reserves
    • Concerns include slower private investment, infrastructure execution delay, debt affordability, physical climate risk, and political uncertainty
  • A ratings upgrade would require sustained fiscal consolidation, a clear decline in gov't debt, and stronger medium-term growth
