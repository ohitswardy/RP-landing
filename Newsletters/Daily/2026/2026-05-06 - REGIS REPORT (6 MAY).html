The spike in inflation for April is being rightfully blamed on the obvious and expected (oil, energy prices). But what many might overlook is how food prices are behaving (especially rice) even before the full impact of higher logistics and fertilizer costs have been felt.

 

Fixed income markets are already reacting to the inflation jump, with yields markedly up.  All this will certainly prompt a reassessment of inflation expectations and Bangko Sentral (BSP) action.

MACRO
Inflation surges to 7.2% in April
  • Headline inflation accelerated to 7.2% in Apr from Mar's 4.1%
    • This was higher than consensus expectations of 5.5% and above the BSP's forecast range of 5.6-6.4% for Apr
    • The 7.2% print is the highest since Mar 2023 (7.6%) and marks the fifth consecutive month of acceleration in inflation
  • The BSP in a statement stated that it will take "necessary actions" to ensure that inflation returns to its 3% (± 1ppt) target within a reasonable time
    • The third out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Jun 18
      • For 2026, consensus expects one more rate hike to 4.75% by year-end
  • This brings 4M26 inflation to 3.9%, within the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) and still below their forecast of 6.3% for the year
  • Inflation in Apr accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Mar in food, utilities (electricity, gas, fuel), and transport prices
    • Food prices overall rose 6.0% YoY, accelerating from the 3.0% pace in Mar, driven by higher prices of rice, fish, and vegetables as post-harvest and transportation costs rose
      • Rice prices are jumped 13.7% YoY in Apr, accelerating from the 3.6% YoY increase in Mar
      • Rice prices are currently at P50.6/kg and P48/kg as of end-Apr for local and imported well-milled rice, rising from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25
    • Utilities and transport prices climbed to 16.9% YoY and 21.4% YoY faster than +7.2% and +9.9%, the previous month
      • Diesel and gasoline prices spiked 122.7% YoY and 59.6% YoY in Apr
  • Core inflation picked up to 3.9% in Apr from 3.2% in Mar, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 4.0% in Apr from 3.2% in Mar
  • For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.3% (previously revised from 5.1% and 3.6%)
    • Consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 3.2% and 4.0%

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS
MBT 1Q26 P12.6bn up 3% despite plunging trading/fx gains

Metrobank (MBT PM) booked 1Q26 NPAT of P12.6bn, up 3% YoY, slightly behind REGIS (FY26F P54.5bn) but in line with consensus (P51.7bn). The miss was due largely to the 78% plunge in trading/fx gains due to the spike in bond yields. Loan growth was 9%, with consumer loans +11%. NIMs were oddly up, despite lower loan yields. Provisions were up 29% to P3.4bn, with credit costs at 67bps, NPLs up slightly at 1.75% (vs 1.7%) and NPL cover at 137% (vs 141%). CET-1 ratio dropped sharply to 14.2% from 16.1% at end-’25 due to MTM losses – the bank booked P16bn worth of MTM losses in its OCI, as we had warned earlier.

 

  • Gross loan growth edged up to 9.2% from 8.8% in 4Q25, with consumer loans up 11.2% YoY.
  • NIMs were oddly resilient, flat QoQ and up 13bps YoY. Asset yields were down18bps QoQ and 16bps YoY (loan yields plunged 48bps YoY). However, this was offset by an odd -23bps QoQ drop in deposit costs (tho still up 12bps YoY), which led to overall funding costs to fall 21bps QoQ and 35bps YoY. (We also noticed a sharp increase in derivative liabilities, which could indicate that the bank had returned to the interest rate swap market… we will confirm during the briefing)
  • Fee incomes rebounded, up 12% YoY, significantly better than the 6% growth in FY2025. However, this was dragged down by a 78% YoY plunge in trading/fx income, likely due to the spike in interest rates in March.
  • Opex was contained, up 10% YoY.
  • Provision expense was up 29% YoY, with credit costs at about 67bps (vs our FY26F of 62bps). NPL ratio edged up to 1.75% from 1.70% at end-2025, while NPL coverage dipped to 137% from 141%.
  • Capital ratios took a big hit, with consolidate CAR and CET-1 ratios down nearly 200bps QoQ. This was due largely to MTM revaluation of the bank’s treasury book, with the bank showing a P16bn MTM loss in the Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) portion of the P&L. The bank also declared dividends in 1Q.

PROPERTY
Implementation of real property valuation reform law to take place in 2028-2029
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) is targeting full implementation of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA) in 2028 or 2029
  • RPVARA will adjust appraisal practices and valuation standards
  • It transfers the authority to adjust the “schedule of market values” from local government units (LGUs) to the national government
  • However, local government units will retain authority over tax rates and assessment levels
  • We previously estimated that real property taxes accounted for 2%-7% of the pretax profit of developers
  • Since LGUs can lower tax rates if the “schedule of market values” rises substantially, we expect only a modest decline in profitability of developers from the implementation of RPVARA (likely less than 5% reduction to net profit)
