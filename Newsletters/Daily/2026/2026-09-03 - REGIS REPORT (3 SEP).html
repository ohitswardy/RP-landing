REGIS REPORT

03 September 2026

The Secretary of Agriculture is saying that the country may import as much as 5m MT of rice this year, given projections of lower rice harvests due to recent rains as well as the developing El Niño weather patterns. The amount (5m MT) would represent ~30% of annual consumption nationwide and would be the highest amount ever imported in a year.

 

Rice import policy has been inconsistent and a bit ad hoc. Over the past year, the administration has swung from reducing rice tariffs (now 15%) to banning imports, to allowing them again.

 

In the meantime, regional rice export prices are already creeping up. We expect rice prices to be a key factor in the direction of CPI inflation over the next few months.

The Senate approved on third reading a bill that would make it easier for old condominiums to be redeveloped. The House previously approved a similar bill.

 

Under current regulations, a condominium cannot be redeveloped without a unanimous vote of all unit owners. This makes redevelopment of many old condominiums very difficult, if not practically impossible. This Senate bill would allow a condominium between 30 and 50 years old to be redeveloped with a 2/3rds vote. For condominium above 50 years, only a simple majority needs to be secured.

 

The enactment of this bill would allow many older condominiums to be torn down and sold, unleashing opportunities for redevelopment. This is more relevant for districts/areas with more “old” condos, including within the Makati CBD.

The role of another member of the Zobel family with the Ayala group has been expanded. Jaime Urquijo will take over responsibility for “Regional Estates” with the retirement of another long-time executive at Ayala Land (ALI PM). The new generation of the family has taken more senior and consequential roles within the group in recent years as the older executives retire.

The Dept of Finance (DOF) is proposing expanding excise taxes for motor vehicles and “luxury goods” in order to partially offset the revenue losses from the proposed tax relief on income. However, it doesn’t sound like the numbers from this proposal will be very meaningful.

The government is set to remove the imposition of the 12% VAT on systems loss charges passed on to consumers. We estimate the impact to be no more than 0.4% for consumers.

MACRO
Rice imports estimated to be 5mn metrics tons in 2026—DA
  • Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel stated that the country may need to import as much as 5 MMT (million metric tons) of rice to secure ample buffer stock given the negative impact of heavy rains and a developing El Niño drought on rice harvest
  • Rice imports as of Aug 13 amounted to 3.46 MMT, already higher than the 3.39 MMT imported in FY25
    • Majority of this year's imports were sourced from Vietnam then Thailand at 2.59 MMT (75% of total) and 0.37 MMT (11% of total), respectively
  • Tiu Laurel noted that there will be no rice import ban given the need to secure an ample supply of rice with dry conditions expected to persist until early 2027
    • He also seeks to propose a requirement to buy palay from local farmers in order to secure import allocations for rice
    • Recall that rice imports were banned from Sep 2025 to end-2025 in order to increase the farmgate price for domestically produced palay (unmilled rice)

      • The rice import tariff was cut to 15% from 35% in 2024

  • The country's palay (unmilled rice) output is projected to be 19 MMT (-3.5% YoY) for FY26,
    • Using the PSA's official milling recovery rate of 63%, the estimated palay output equates to approximately 12 MMT of rice
      • The country's annual rice demand amounts to ~15-16 MMT
    • Palay output as of 1H26 amounted to 9.1 MMT (-0.6% YoY)
      • The DA secretary also noted that average yield per hectare is rising but overall output has been dampened by the recent heavy rains

Senate Bill to loosen regulations on condominium redevelopment
  • The Senate approved Senate Bill 2420 or the Condominium Redevelopment Act
  • Under the current law, condominium buildings can only be redeveloped with the consent of all condominium owners
  • Under SB 2420:
    • Condominium buildings that are 30-50 years old can be redeveloped with the approval of 2/3 of owners
    • Condominium buildings that are over 50 years old can be redeveloped with the approval of majority of owners
  • Over time, this could lead to more condominium redevelopment activity in areas with older buildings, such as Makati, Ortigas Center, and Manila
    • Due to the large supply of condominium units in Metro Manila, redevelopment is likely to be limited in the near term 
  • SB 2420 also introduces safeguards for buyers when the actual floor area of a completed unit differs from the approved floor plan
    • Units measuring up to 60sqm can have deviations up to 6%
    • Units exceeding 500sqm are allowed to have deviations of up to 2%

DOF proposes higher tax on motor vehicles and luxury goods
  • The Dept. of Finance (DOF), through the PROGRESS bill, is contemplating on proposals for higher taxes on luxury and non-essential goods as well as an increase in the motor vehicle users charge (MVUC)
    • Recall that the DOF is proposing measures to offset the gov't revenue losses from the recently proposed tax relief initiatives such as raising the minimum annual income subject to tax to P350,000 from the current P250,000 as well as exempting micro and smell enterprises from the minimum corporate tax
      • The estimated annual revenue loss from the proposed tax relief measure is ~P109bn
  • The DOF proposed to raise the motor vehicle users charge (MVUC), by doubling current rates as these have remained unchanged since 2004
    • The MVUC is a fee charged to vehicle owners upon registration of a new vehicle as well as annually during the renewal of vehicle registration
  • The excise tax on in non-essential goods is sought to be raised to 25% from 20%
    • The Dept. is looking to expand the list of non-essential goods covered to also include private aircraft, jets, helicopters, recreational boats (jet skis, speedboats, etc.)
      • The law currently covers jewelry, perfumes, yachts
    • There are no current estimates on the revenue impact but in 2025 the gov't collected P307mn from the excise tax, 28% lower from P426mn in 2024

 

 

MACRO
Government set to remove VAT on system loss charges by November
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue plans to issue a circular removing VAT on the allowable system loss charge in electricity bills by mid-September
    • This follows the Energy Regulatory Commission’s (ERC) ruling last Aug 28, classifying system loss as a government-mandated pass-through cost that should not form part of the VAT base
  • Once BIR’s circular is issued, the ERC said it would likely be implemented in November
  • Note that system losses account for only ~3% of end-user rates, so removing VAT on this component will only reduce rates by just ~0.36%
