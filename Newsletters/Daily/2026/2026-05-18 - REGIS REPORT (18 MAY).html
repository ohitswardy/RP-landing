Results roundup time! We do a quick breakdown below – spoiler alert, it’s not pretty.

 

Before that, some comments on the stragglers, below:

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – 1Q26 profit plunged 40% YoY to P1.4bn (less divs to prefs) on disappointing margins. Consolidated revenues were actually quite firm – +9% YoY on +3.5% SSSG – broadly in line with expectations. Management attributed the 2.0ppt YoY gross margin contraction to higher input costs alongside the decision to defer prices increases for the domestic business. The international business also saw margin pressure as operating profit for the segment declined >20% YoY. Margins will likely remain squeezed considering that (a) price increases will be gradual to lessen the impact on consumers, and (b) input cost inflation will likely linger in the near-term.

  • Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) (-) – We estimate that BLOOM registered a recurring net loss of P530mn, a reversal from recurring profit of P445mn in 1Q25. Meanwhile, EBITDA fell 32% YoY to P3.0, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The deterioration was led by Solaire Entertainment City, which would have seen an even larger decline if not for unusually high win rates.

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (=) – Recurring 1Q26 profit fell 9% YoY to P7.9bn, as improved profit contributions from Metrobank (MBT PM) and Metro Pacific were eclipsed by sharply lower recurring earnings at Toyota Motor Philippines, Federal Land, and other smaller units. While the results appear broadly in line with expectations, we have nonetheless trimmed our FY26E profit forecast by 4% to reflect a weaker outlook amid macroeconomic headwinds.

+ + +

 

And speaking of results – all 1Q26 numbers are in (bar one or two stragglers), and the numbers are unsurprisingly weak. More than half (56%) of REGIS-covered stocks missed, with just a quarter exceeding our expectations. Aggregate earnings were up 4% YoY, trailing forecasts of 9%, which were made pre-Iran war. Interestingly, revenue numbers were pretty solid, up 7% YoY, with consumers, and banks posting double-digit top-line figures. Consumer demand was rebounding strongly from 4Q25, helped (in our view) to the sharp drop in online gambling. But margins are already starting to see early signs of strain in March. For banks, loan growth was already accelerating, up 11.6%, but NPAT was pulled down by “pre-emptive” provisioning.

 

The picture in 2Q26 will likely be even softer, with inflation already blipping up to 7.2% in April, with more pressure to come from rice and electricity prices.

 

 

 

 

+ + +

 

Remittances grew 2.3% YoY in March to $2.87bn. While this was slower than the 2.6% pace in Feb, the growth defies early fears of a sharp drop-off as a result of the ongoing Middle East conflict. That said, a slowdown in the region’s economies could result in layoffs, or at least weaker demand for Filipino labor. To the upside, the weak currency meant that the PHP value of the remittances were up 5.8% YoY.
