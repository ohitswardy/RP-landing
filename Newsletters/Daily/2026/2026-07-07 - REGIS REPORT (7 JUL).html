REGIS REPORT

07 July 2026

The statistics office just announced that June CPI inflation was 6.4%, a deceleration from the May figure (=6.8%) but still well above the Bangko Sentral (BSP) target range (2-4%).  The June inflation number is within expectations of the Street but below those of Regis.  

 

The slowdown was attributable to the sharp moderation in energy prices, which drove down the YoY inflation for transport (12.8% vs 16.2%) and fuel-related items. Food price inflation also slowed for the month (5.2% vs 5.7%). Subsiding food prices are welcome, but we are wary about the risks posed by what meteorologists predict could be the most severe El Nino in decades.

 

Meanwhile, even before this latest CPI data point, and after two rate hikes this year, the BSP Governor is already guiding for the next one with his pronouncements that the economy “can handle” another one. This suggests we will likely see a 25bp increase at the next Monetary Board meeting in Aug.

 

+++

 

A few more banks have eliminated fees on interbank transfers following the Bangko Sentral’s circular on such and BPI’s (BPI PM) anticipatory action. Union Bank (UBP PM) and Land Bank both announced they will waive fees for Instapay transfers beginning today (July 7).

 

The BSP is motivated by an aim to spur digital adoption and financial inclusion. The cuts in such fees should result in a hit to the revenue of these financial institutions. The hope is that the use of digital financial tools will rise as a result.

 

+++

 

The Dept of Energy (DOE) is proposing amendments to existing laws that would pave the way for the creation of national oil reserve and the imposition of mandate on oil firms to maintain inventories of at least 30 days. The latter rule would ultimately require most oil importers and retailers to put up more capital.

Philippine economy can absorb one more rate hike—BSP Governor

  • Remolona said that there is still room for a rebound as a significant portion of the gov't's spending program has yet to be disbursed given delays due to the flood control scandal
    • Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan in a separate interview stated that gov't spending is expected to pick up in the second half as infra implementation accelerates
      • He also added that putting inflation under control and driving economic growth through reforms (e.g., education, health, digitalization, transparent governance etc.) are key in order to maintain the country's investment-grade credit rating
  • The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled this year will be held on Aug 27
    • The current policy rate is 4.75%
      • For 2026, consensus expectations for rate hikes are +75bp total to 5.00% by year-end
      • Regis expectations are for +125bp total worth of hikes to 5.50%
  • The gov't's GDP growth targets for 26/27/28E were reduced to 3.5-4.5%/5.0-6.0%/5.0-6.0% from 5.0-6.0%/5.5%-6.5%/6.0-7.0%

Dept. of Energy proposes amendments to Oil Deregulation Law
  • The Dept. of Energy (DOE) formally recommended amendments to the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act of 1988 through the implementation of safeguards such as establishing a national fuel reserve (equivalent to at least 60 days of supply) and increasing minimum inventory requirement for oil companies from 15 to 30 days
  • DOE Secretary Sharon Garin, however, clarified that the proposal does not include changes to how fuel prices are set
    • She added that Congress will ultimately decide what amendments should be included in the revised law as well as whether the gov't should have more control in regulating fuel prices
