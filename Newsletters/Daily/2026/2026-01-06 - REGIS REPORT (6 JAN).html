REGIS REPORT

06 January 2026

President Marcos signed the national budget for 2026 yesterday. While final details of the budget are still not publicly available, Marcos was reported to have exercised his veto over around P93bn of allocations. The bicameral version of the budget was for a total P6.79trn, which was supposed to be 7% higher from the prior year. 

 

This national budget appears to have returned to the more “normal” allocations prior to recent periods (e.g. lower allocation for Public Works, higher amounts for Education, Health, etc.). Based on preliminary information, Education is now *back* as the largest item in the budget (20%) while Health saw a big increase (+63%, now at 7%).

 

It was also reported in the press that Marcos vetoed P93bn of allocations, including P50bn for the military modernization program and P4bn for the tax refunds for the auto incentive program (aka “CARS”).

 

+++

 

The government also officially revised its GDP growth estimates for the country. For 2026, its official target is now 5-6% GDP growth (vs. 6-7%), while for 2027, its revised target is 5.5% to 6.5% (previously also 6-7%).

 

+++

 

The just-released CPI data by the statistics office showed inflation at 1.8% for December, an acceleration from Nov (1.5%) and higher than consensus. Similar to elsewhere in the region, food prices dragged CPI up (though rice inflation remained tame).  For the year, CPI inflation averaged 1.7%.

 

 

+++

 

Jollibee (JFC PM) announced its intention to spin off its international businesses into a separate entity to be listed in the US.

 

Based on what was disclosed in the press release, JFC will distribute ownership in the new subsidiary holding all international operations to existing JFC shareholders. The spin-off will thus create two separate entities, one for the domestic Philippine operations and one for all international operations.

 

This transaction will, hopefully, result in a higher aggregate valuation than is currently the case for the combined JFC entity today. The target execution date of this transaction is sometime in 2027 which, frankly, is still some ways off.

 

+++

 

Banking sector FX-denominated loans contracted 3.9% YoY to just $15.1bn in 3Q, accounting for an all-time low 5.6% of total loans. Meanwhile, FX deposits were up 5.7% YoY to $60.7bn; in PHP terms, they were up 11.1% YoY. As a share of total deposits, FX deposits are up to 17.4%, a high since 2012. These to us suggest that banks’ customers expect the PHP weakness to persist (a view we hold). On a positive note, the data also means that banks are at a low risk to NPLs in the event of a currency shock.

 

The data also means that the system-wide headline LDR of 75% is misleading. Given the 25% LDR of the FX book, we calculate that the banks’ PHP books are already at 85% LDR on average. Larger banks like BDO and BPI have headline LDR in the 87-88% range, suggesting their PHP books are close to 100% LDR. This is likely why banks’ deposits costs have not come down sharply, despite the 200bps worth of policy rate cuts, and 450bps in RRR cuts since late 2024. BSP Gov Remolona recently said he would be “happy” to see RRR come down from 5% currently to 2% in the next year or so.

BANKS
FX loans drop, deposits up in 3Q25; PHP LDR tightens to 85%

Based on BSP data on Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDUs) of banks in 3Q25, FX-denominated loans contracted by 3.9% YoY to $15.13bn. Perhaps more importantly, that figure was down 5.0% QoQ from the $15.93bn level in June. This is in contrast to overall loans (including RRP and interbank loans) of 9.4% YoY. Meanwhile, FCDU deposits grew 5.7% YoY to $60.73bn, only marginally up QoQ. In PHP terms, though, FCDU deposits were up 11% YoY.

 

These (to us) suggest that bank customers continue to expect the PHP to weaken against the USD. In fact, these trends have been going for the last 5-7 years. The ratio of FX loans to total loans is at an all-time low at just 5.6%. Meanwhile, FX deposits now account for 17.4% of total deposits, the highest level since 2012. This ratio has been on an upward trend since 2021.

 

To the upside, this does mean that the banking system is not at significant risk of NPLs due to PHP weakness.

 

The other noteworthy trend is that the PHP LDR is now at 84.9% (by our estimate), much higher than the headline LDR of 74.9% suggests. The FCDU book’s LDR is down to just 24.9%. This suggests that the BSP’s recent aggressive RRR cuts on PHP deposits were probably in response to increasing tightness in the banking system’s PHP books. We would not be surprised if this is also the rationale for BSP Gov Remolona’s statements that he would be “happy” to see the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) on banks’ PHP deposits down from 5.0% currently, to 2.0% “in a year or so”

 

The tight PHP LDR also is likely why banks’ deposit costs have remained relatively sticky, despite the 200bps worth of policy rate cuts in the last 18 months.

 

We would not be overly concerned about the tight LDRs, though. Banks are managing their funding costs by issuing short- to medium-term bonds to cover expensive (and tax inefficient) time deposits (TD). Banks are also pushing nigh net worth (HNW) clients to their Trust Departments, where banks happily collect fees without carrying the funding costs on books.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

GAMING
PAGCOR to implement minimum fees on online operators
  • PAGCOR will start collecting minimum monthly guaranteed fees from online gaming operators this year
  • 1 April 2026 to 30 September 2026:
    • Operators with e-casino operations – minimum fee of P9mn/month
    • Operators without e-casino operations (such as sports betting, bingo, etc.) – minimum fee of P3mn/month
  • 1 October 2026 onwards
    • Operators with e-casino operations – minimum fee of P10.5mn/month
    • Operators without e-casino operations (such as sports betting, bingo, etc.) – minimum fee of P4mn/month
  • E-casino operators pay 30% of GGR as fees to PAGCOR
    • A minimum fee of P10.5mn/month would correspond to GGR of P35mn/month
  • We do not expect any direct impact on DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), which pays substantially higher than the minimum guaranteed fees
  • However, Atty. Marie Antonette Quiogue, CEO of Arden Consult International, estimates that “roughly 40” of the 65 licensees would likely fall short of the GGR threshold of the minimum guaranteed fees
