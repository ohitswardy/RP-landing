We highlight two news items that are positive for ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Philippine ports. 

 

The government has only recently begun addressing congestion at Manila's seaports and airport by establishing a task force to resolve cargo supply chain bottlenecks.

   

The congestion weighs on the broader economy but, ironically, benefits port operators, in the form of storage fees. Given that the peak cargo season is already underway, we expect the congestion to persist, allowing ICTSI's Manila port to continue benefiting from elevated storage revenues through year-end or until the backlog is cleared.

 

In another development, the government has formalized a partnership with two agencies designating the ICTSI’s Subic port as the primary gateway for the planned Pax Silica hub, a development that should support the port's long-term growth.

The Senate Impeachment Court approved subpoenas for the bank, tax, and Anti-Money Laundering Council records of Vice President Sara Duterte, her husband Manases Carpio, and 19 companies allegedly linked to them, as part of the investigation into allegations of unexplained wealth.

The country's balance of payments registered a large surplus of US$3.4bn for the month of June, thanks to a US$ bond offering. However, gross international reserves grew by a more modest 0.7% (US$760mn) for the month, largely due to the decline in gold prices.

TRANSPORT
Bureau of Customs forms task force to ease Manila’s seaport, airport congestions
  • The Bureau of Customs has formed a Technical Working Group (TWG) and Task Force to address operational bottlenecks at Manila’s major seaports and airport
    • High yard utilization at the seaports and collapsed warehouse capacity at the airport have disrupted supply chains
  • TWG aims to develop coordinated measures to improve cargo movement
    • TWG will report directly to the Office of the Commissioner and coordinate with the Department of Finance through the Office of the Undersecretary for the Revenue Operations Group
  • The Bureau of Customs recently issued a draft joint administrative order, which aim to police the movement of cargo
  • Move comes amid a prolonged cargo congestion crisis at ports, including ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT)
    • The congestion began in 1Q26 and has not abated; This suggests that MICT could be generating higher-than-normal storage revenues, which could persist until the congestion is addressed
    • We estimate MICT accounts for 20% of revenues and 18% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 16x FY26E EV/EBITDA, above peer average and historical average of 11x

 

TRANSPORT
Government designates ICTSI’s Subic port as main gateway for Pax Silica trade
  • The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) signed a partnership with Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA) designating Subic as the preferred maritime gateway for requirements of Pax Silica
    • Note that SBMA is the regulator of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Subic port, which we estimate today accounts for ~2% of group revenues/EBITDA
  • This should support the long-term growth of the Subic port, where ICTSI is increasing capacity to 1mn TEUs from 600k TEUs
    • The Subic port is 100 kilometers from New Clark City, the site for the 1,619-hectare Pax Silica hub
POLITICS
Senate impeachment count authorizes subpoena of VP Sara Duterte's tax and bank documents
  • The Senate Impeachment Court approved subpoenas for the bank, tax, and Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) records of Vice President Sara Duterte, her husband Manases Carpio, and 19 companies allegedly linked to them, as part of the investigation into allegations of unexplained wealth
    • In a ruling, Impeachment Court presiding Officer Francis Escudero overruled the defense's objections and allowed the production of the documents as the financial records were found to be sufficiently defined, identifiable, and materially relevant to the case
  • The Senate Impeachment Court ordered the banks to submit the requested records by July 30, 2026
MACRO
BOP posts US$3.4bn surplus in June; GIR settles at US$104.7bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Jun posted a surplus of US$3.4bn
    • Higher than both the US$131mn surplus in the previous month of May and the US$226mn surplus in Jun 2025
    • This brings the 6M26 deficit to US$3.9bn, narrower than the US$5.6bn deficit for the same period last year
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$10.7bn (2.1% of GDP)
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Jun stood at US$104.7bn, +0.7% MoM
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.8 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $104.0bn
