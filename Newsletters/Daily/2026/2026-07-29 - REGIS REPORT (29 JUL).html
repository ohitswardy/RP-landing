REGIS REPORT

29 July 2026

As expected, a few legislators have already tabled measures to reduce the pass-on electricity charges to consumers after the speech by President Marcos. The proposals cover issues related to who should pay for electricity losses as well as taxes on various electricity-related items.

 

We remain very doubtful that a measure mandating that electricity distribution utilities (DU) absorb all electricity losses without any offset can be implemented. Having said that, we see ways to cosmetically hide the pass-on charges (e.g. raising tariffs or returns elsewhere to offset the systems losses). The government can also opt to subsidize these amounts.

 

Ultimately, we don’t think DUs like Meralco (MER PM) and the Aboitiz Power (AP PM) subsidiaries will be forced to absorb the electricity losses (at least fully). The issue of very high power costs, however, is now in the spotlight (as it should be). This could lead to a review of industry regulations, norms and practices which could open a can of worms for some industry names (across all industry sub-segments).

 

+++

 

The Lower House is now also considering measures to increase tax relief for lower income workers as well as small businesses. These tax relief measures, however, will mean revenue losses of as much as P50bn and the House will likely need/want to offset these with new or higher taxes elsewhere.

 

Among the proposals is to slap / increases taxes on (1) vape and heated tobacco products and (2) sweetened beverages. Sweetened beverages now mostly pay P6/liter. Sweetened RTD tea makes up around 8% of Universal Robina (URC PM) sales. Coffee (mostly mix) is currently not taxed but comprises 7% of URC revenue. For Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), sweetened milk-related beverages (also no excise taxes today) are ~6% of revenue.

 

+++

 

The President signed a new Administrative Order that would again allow new ecozones in Metro Manila. This new order reverses a previous order by the Duterte administration that was aimed at forcing ecozones (and the BPO firms that occupy them) to move operations outside Metro Manila.

POWER
Senators file measures to slash power bills via VAT, system loss cuts; Power firms urge caution
  • Senator Erwin Tulfo filed two bills and a resolution aimed at lowering electricity bills
    • Senate Resolution No. 537 seeks an inquiry into the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) provision allowing system loss charges to be passed on consumers
    • Senate Bill No. 2239 seeks to repeal the Batas Pambansa Blg. 36 or Energy Tax Law, which imposes a tiered tax on residential customers exceeding monthly consumption of 650kwh
    • Senate Bill No. 2340 proposes exempting system loss charge from the 12% VAT
  • Senator JV Ejercito also filed SB 2342 and SB 2343
    • SB 2342 prohibits distribution utilities and electric cooperatives from passing systems losses to consumers
    • SB 2343 seeks to remove the 12% VAT on electricity bills
  • Meanwhile, Meralco (MER PM) urged lawmakers to consider the impact of scrapping system loss charges, considering it “inherent” in operating an electric distribution system
  • The Philippine Rural Electric Cooperatives Association, Inc. (PHILRECA) said that it can support the removal if the government directly shoulders these costs through a dedicated subsidy system
    • PHILRECA is the national organization of 121 electric cooperatives in the country
    • The group further pointed out that disallowing the recovery of system loss without subsidy would bankrupt nonprofit electric cooperatives
  • The Senate Committee on Energy, which Tulfo chairs, is set to continue its hearings on proposed amendments to the EPIRA Law on Thursday
MACRO
Marcos' proposed tax relief considered a high priority by lawmakers; implementation eyed by Jan 2027
  • The House Committee on Ways and Means stated that it will prioritize legislation implementing President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s proposed tax relief despite revenue concerns as the gov't has plans to offset the estimated revenue P50bn revenue loss
  • Committee Head Marikina Rep. Romero Quimbo says that lawmakers are prepared to immediately advance the administration's tax agenda including raising the tax exemption level, exempting small businesses from the minimum corporate income tax and granting a broader tax amnesty
    • Quimbo targets to approve the measure on third and final reading by August, with implementation eyed by Jan 1, 2027
    • Senate President Pro Tempore Vicente Sotto III filed a counterpart bill in the Upper House yesterday which seeks to raise the minimum annual income subject to tax to P350,000 from the current P250,000
  • Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that the economic team has discussed potential measures to offset the revenue losses such as higher excise taxes on vape products, heated tobacco, and sugar-sweetened beverages
    • Universal Robina Corp's (URC PM) RTD tea is approximately 8% of total revenues
    • Monde Nissin's (MONDE PM) flavored milk accounts for around 6% of total revenues
PROPERTY
Metro Manila likely to see new economic zones
  • President Marcos signed a new Administrative Order reversing Administrative Order (AO) 18, which was signed by President Duterte in 2019
  • AO 18 prohibits the approval of new economic zones in Metro Manila
    • If a registered enterprise operates within an economic zone, it can avail of incentives from the government
  • Within Metro Manila, the registered enterprises that avail of incentives are predominantly BPO firms
    • We do not expect the lifting of the moratorium to have any significant impact near term, since there are still sufficient PEZA-registered buildings
MACRO
BSP chief sees small chance of 50bp hike despite renewed volatility
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that there is a "small chance" for an aggressive 50bp rate hike to 5.25% from 4.75% currently given renewed inflationary pressures
    • Remolona added that the peso's recent slump to a new-record low against the USD at P61.8 may contribute to accelerating inflation on higher import costs
    • However, the BSP chief remained optimistic for a rebound of the Philippine economy this 2H26 despite rising inflation
  • The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Aug 27
    • The current policy rate is 4.75%
      • For 2026, Regis and consensus expectations for rate hikes are +100bp total to 5.25% by year-end
