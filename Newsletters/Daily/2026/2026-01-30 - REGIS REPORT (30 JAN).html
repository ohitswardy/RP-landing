REGIS REPORT

30 January 2026

The 4Q GDP growth number of 3.0% jolted the market yesterday. This figure is the lowest since 3Q of 2011 and well below any of the Street’s predictions.

 

Looking through the numbers, the biggest drag was clearly the steep(er) contraction in capital formation (investment). Capital formation plunged 10.9%, even sharper than the -2.8% in the previous quarter. Government spending on infrastructure likely slowed to a trickle, with corollary effects on other parts of the economy.

 

As a result, beyond just capital formation, even consumption (both private and government) was uncharacteristically but unsurprisingly weak. Private consumption grew only 3.8% while government consumption grew 3.7% in the quarter.

 

Everyone expected GDP growth to be weak, and it was worse than people anticipated. The GDP number is, however, a backward-looking figure. The more important question now is what the outlook is for the rest of this year and beyond. The last big corruption scandal resulted in six quarters of sub-par growth.

 

This GDP print should increase the odds of another policy rate cut by the Bangko Sentral (BSP) next month. We also expect government to double down on efforts to jump-start the economy by more targeted spending. Those measures, however, can only do so much. Visible results on anti-corruption efforts will be needed to restore confidence (if not, then the collective memory loss of the Filipino population).

The Supreme Court (SC) affirmed its previous ruling declaring last year’s impeachment proceedings against Vice President Sara Duterte as unconstitutional on procedural grounds. The ruling thus kills last year’s impeachment efforts and was not really in doubt since the initial ruling was unanimous.

 

The decision, however, does not stop new impeachment proceedings against Duterte to be initiated again immediately. Impeachment cases have also been filed against President Marcos recently, so we can expect impeachment hearings against both to be conducted soon.

Ayala Corp (AC PM) says it expects to launch twelve Spinney’s stores across Metro Manila. Spinney’s is a higher-end supermarket chain. We expect it to help speed up tenancy for Ayala Land’s (ALI PM) malls, which typically take longer to secure third party supermarket chains as anchor tenants.

MACRO
4Q25 GDP grows 3.0%, missing 2025 target
  • GDP grew at 3.0% in 4Q25, decelerating sharply from the already lackluster (revised) 3.9% growth rate in 3Q
    • This is well below consensus and REGIS expectations of 4.2%
    • The 3.0% print is the lowest (ex-pandemic) since hitting 3.0% back in 3Q11
  • On the demand side, weakness was evident all around
    • The largest factor in the poor GDP print was the even steeper decline in capital formation (-10.9% from -2.8%)
      • Capital formation has declined for two consecutive quarters, mainly due to the significant decline in construction
    • Consumption, both private (3.8%) and government (3.7%), was also below historical trend
      • Private consumption was 3.8%, the lowest since 3Q10's 2.6% (ex-pandemic), slowing further from 3Q25's 4.1%
      • Post-pandemic (2022 onwards), private consumption was at least 4.7% or higher
      • Food and non-alcoholic beverages decelerated to 3.8% from 4.5% in 3Q while alcoholic beverages was still negative at -7.0%
    • Net exports, however, saw significant improvement to 1.9% from 0.9% given robust growth in exports at +13.2% accelerating further from +7.4% the previous quarter
  • On the supply side, growth in all three major sectors (services, industry, and agri) deteriorated relative to the previous quarter
    • Growth in the service sector slowed a tad to 5.2% from 5.4% in 3Q
    • Industry sector growth turned negative at -0.9% for the first time (ex-pandemic) since 3Q11's -1.6%
      • Construction activity fell sharply to -7.1% from -0.4%, as growth remained in the negative given the impact of the flood control scandal on gov't infra spending
        • Government construction was negative for the third consecutive quarter at -41.9% much worse than the -26.2% growth in 3Q
        • Household construction contributed to the continued decline in capital formation as it posted a modest 2.2%, moderating from four consecutive quarters of double-digit growth
  • Bringing FY25 GDP growth to 4.4%, below the government's target of 4.8-5.0% (5.5-6.5% initial target) and the consensus forecast of 4.9%
    • The full year figure was also below REGIS' FY25E forecast of 4.8% and significantly slower than FY24's 5.7% GDP growth

 

 

POLITICS
Supreme Court rejects impeachment case against Vice President with finality
  • The Supreme Court (SC) dismissed the motions for reconsideration on its ruling that declared last year’s impeachment case against the Vice President unconstitutional
    • Last year, the SC dismissed the impeachment complaint delivered by the House of Representatives
    • The SC cited constitutional infirmities, including the apparent lack of due process, et al
  • The dismissal was not really in doubt since the SC decision to stop the impeachment proceedings last year was unanimous
  • The SC decision, however, does not stop new impeachment proceedings from being initiated beginning Jan 14
CONGLOS
Ayala and Spinneys to launch 12 Metro Manila stores starting in Q4
  • Ayala Corp. and UAE-based premium supermarket chain, Spinneys, plan to open 12 stores across Metro Manila in 4Q26
    • According to Ayala Corp. Managing Director and Ayala Land Head of Leasing and Hospitality Mariana Zobel de Ayala
    • Spinneys’ first stores will open at Ayala Malls U.P. Town Center in Quezon City and San Antonio Plaza Arcade in Makati City
    • Note that AC initially said that it would open its first store by 4Q26
  • Ayala Corp. (AC PM) owns a 60% stake in the partnership while Spinneys owns 40%
  • The group’s entry into retail could help speed up securing tenants for its malls; it would also offer competition with other retailers particularly in high-income areas
  • AC trades at a 27% discount to its market-based NAV of P717/sh and 6.7x PER
POWER
DMCI Power seeks approval for proposed Mindoro-Semirara power grid interconnection
  • DMCI Power Corp. is awaiting the Department of Energy’s approval on its proposed P3bn, 19-kilometer submarine cable that will connect the power grids of Semirara island and Mindoro
  • Under the proposal, DMCI Power plans to build a 30MW coal plant, expand its wind plant in Semirara to 100MW (vs. 12.5MW today), and build 2,000MW of solar capacity
    • Demand in Mindoro is >100MW annually, 60% of which is currently being supplied by high-cost bunker and diesel fuel
    • Proposal aims to lower the cost of electricity in Mindoro by displacing high-cost fuel
  • DMCI Power Corp. is a wholly-owned unit of DMCI Holdings (DMC PM)
    • It currently operates an aggregate capacity of 188MW in off-grid areas of Palawan, Masbate, Oriental Mindoro, and Semirara
