21 April 2026

BPI (BPI PM) issued a press statement on its 1Q26 results, with profits for the quarter reaching P16.9bn, just barely up (+1.7%) YoY. Loan growth was a still healthy 13.5%, with consumer/MSME lending growing 24.9%.

 

The key item that’s probably going to raise the most eyebrows would be the asset quality numbers. The NPL ratio rose 24bp to 2.42% and coverage declined to 87% despite a big increase in provision expense. Investor attention is likely to focused on this issue at tomorrow’s earnings briefing.

 

+++

 

Ayala Land (ALI PM) announced that it was cancelling one condo project in Quezon City and putting another one (the high profile “Laurean Residences”) in Makati on pause. The sharp increase in costs recently was cited as a reason for these actions.

 

We are uncertain what putting the Laurean development “on pause” even means. Or if management has already even made up its mind on how to deal with those who’ve already made reservation payments. This action(s), however, may have implications for confidence in the property market.

 

+++

 

March vehicle sales for brands under the CAMPI umbrella declined 10%. Note that this excludes some brands, the most prominent of which would be BYD. We believe overall unit vehicle sales would have been close to flat YoY if these other brands were included.

 

The first takeaway here is that the demand for four-wheeled vehicles is sluggish, even if not it’s not contracting as sharply as headlines suggest. The second takeaway here is that electrified vehicles (hybrids, full battery EVs) are taking significant share from internal combustion engine vehicles. We estimate more than 1/4th of vehicles sold in March were already EVs.

BANKS
BPI 1Q26 PPOP disappoints slightly, as asset quality slips

In a press release, BPI reported headline 1Q26 NPAT of P16.9bn, flat (+1.7%) YoY, tracking slightly behind REGIS (FY26E P71.23bn) and consensus (P70.27bn). However, we note that asset quality deteriorated QoQ and YoY, with NPLs up 24bps QoQ and 16bps YoY to 2.42%, while NPL cover dropped nearly 8 percentage points QoQ to 87.2%. The thinning buffer will likely raise concerns, especially in light of surging inflationary pressures as a result of the Iran conflict.

 

Management will host a briefing on Wednesday, 22 April at 4pm. Ahead of that some highlights from the PR:

 

  • Loan growth slowed to 13.5% YoY from 14.7% in 4Q25 and from 13.7% in 1Q25. “Institutional” loans were up 8.9%, slower than the 10.4% pace in 4Q25. Consumer/MSME loans expanded 24.9% YoY , also slower than the 25.8% recorded in 4Q. This was led by “business banking” (+96.3%), credit cards (+33.3%) and personal loans (+26.9%).
  • BPI estimates that NIMs widened 7bps YoY. However, this would suggest a low-teens NIM contraction QoQ, likely owing to yield pressure on corporate loans given the sharp drop in policy rates.
  • Non-interest income was up 14.5% YoY driven by credit card fees.
  • Opex jumped 15.8% on continued IT investment and headcount costs.
  • PPOP was up 12% YoY slightly behind our expectations (23% of FYF)
  • Provision expense was P5.5bn up 83% from a very low base in 1Q25, but was down from the P6bn posted in 4Q. The lower QoQ provision was despite a jump in NPL ratio to 2.42% (vs 2.18% in 4Q), which in turn led to a sharp drop in NPL coverage to 87.15% from 94.9% the previous quarter. Credit cost eased slightly QoQ to 86bps, in line with management guidance, but lower than our forecast of 94bps.

 

Had BPI maintained NPL coverage at 95%, reported NPAT would have been down YoY. We expect most of the questions during Wednesday’s earnings call to be centered around NPL formation and NPL coverage.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

CONGLOS
Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 10% YoY in March; Toyota sales down 6.8% YoY
  • Data from CAMPI showed that vehicle sales fell 10.4% YoY (+0.7% MoM) to 36,104 units in March
    • Brings 1Q26 sales to 105,642 units, down 9.8% YoY
    • Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI; We suspect BYD’s market share may have risen substantially from ~7% in 4Q25
  • Sales of electric vehicles (ex-BYD) surged to 6,148 units in March (~17% of total car sales), up >3x YoY and 2x MoM due to the rise in fuel prices
    • Brings 1Q26 EV sales up 36% YoY to 11,800 units (HEVs: +10% YoY to 8261 units / BEVs: +123% YoY to 2,289 units / PHEVs: +925% YoY to 1,250 units)
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,622 units in March, down 6.8% YoY and 1.85% MoM
    • Brings TMP’s 1Q26 unit sales to 51,922 units, down 6.5% YoY and tracking below our forecast of 247k units (+7% YoY)
    • Every 5% reduction in our sales volume forecast will cut TMP’s profit estimate by 2% and GTCAP’s profit by <1%
    • TMP is targeting unit sales to grow “up to 5%” (~229k-240k units) this year
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 23% of FY26E profit
    • GTCAP trades at a 56% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,129/sh and at 3.5x PER

 

 

 

PROPERTY
Ayala Land pauses Laurean Residences project, cancels The Heights
  • Ayala Land said it was pausing sales of Laurean Residences, an Ayala Land Premier project in Makati City
  • It cited rising cost pressures and uncertain delivery timelines due to the Iran conflict
  • “We have reached out to buyers directly to discuss next steps and the range of options available to them, with the care and attention they expect from ALI,” ALI said
  • We estimate that Laurean contributed less than 1% of recurring profit in FY25
  • The company also canceled The Heights, an Avida (middle-income) project along Katipunan Avenue, Quezon City
  • The Heights was only launched in 1Q26 and did not contribute to reservation sales or profit in FY25
  • ALI currently offers 3.0% dividend yield and is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.7X FY26E P/B, 7.9X FY26E P/E
MACRO
Fitch downgrades PH outlook to "negative" from "stable" but affirms credit rating
  • Credit ratings agency Fitch downgraded its outlook for the Philippines to "negative" from "stable" but affirmed the country's "BBB" investment-grade rating
    • Fitch cited the country's high exposure to the global energy shock as well as disrupted public investment due to the corruption scandal
    • Political friction was also noted as a risk to the country's macro outlook
      • The credit rating agency highlighted that these risks currently outweigh favorable medium-term growth prospects
  • Note that a "negative" outlook implies the possibility of a future credit rating downgrade if underlying risks continue or worsen in one to two years
UTILITIES
Water regulator slaps P54mn penalty on Maynilad
  • MWSS Regualtory Office slapped a P54mn penalty on Maynilad for water service interruptions last May 9-31 on 165,518 accounts
    • Company cited the Amihan season caused reductions in production levels of its Putatan water treatment facility, which serves the south of Metro Manila
  • Note that this is the second time this year that the regulator imposed a penalty on Maynilad for a similar issue
    • In early March, the regulator imposed a P43mn penalty
  • Even if the amounts of the penalty are not material, these are still costs that are non-recoverable
    • On one hand, we reckon persistent service disruptions could also weaken the regulator’s perception on the company and elevate regulatory risk
    • On the other hand, we understand that Maynilad plans to boost the capacity of its water treatment facility in the south, which will entail capex
MACRO
Dept. of Energy to limit fuel price adjustments
  • Dept. of Energy (DOE) Secretary Sharon Garin stated that oil retailers must adjust prices in line with the range provided by the DOE every week amid the state of national energy emergency
    • Garin cited that President Marcos' declaration of a "state of national energy emergency" through Executive Order (EO) 110 granted the gov't control over setting the minimum rollback and maximum price increases for fuel price adjustments
  • Garin noted that the DOE decided to closely monitor the price adjustments given public clamor on quick fuel price increases but slow price rollbacks despite lower global oil prices

 

 

MACRO
BOP posts US$2.6bn deficit in March; GIR settles at US$106.6bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of March posted a deficit of US$2.6bn
    • Wider than both the US$2.3bn deficit in Feb and the US$2.0bn deficit in March 2025
    • This brings the 3M26 deficit to US$5.3bn
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP)
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Mar stood at US$106.6bn, flat YoY and down from the US$113.3bn in the previous month of Feb
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.0 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn
