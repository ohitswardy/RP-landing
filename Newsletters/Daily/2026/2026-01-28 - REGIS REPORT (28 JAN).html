28 January 2026

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is directing Meralco (MER PM) to begin charging consumers for P31bn (US$520m) in costs relating to contracts that Meralco suppliers reneged on.  This follows a Supreme Court decision in favor of the gencos, which were controlled by San Miguel Corp (SMC PM) at the time the contracts were terminated.

 

As a background, a number of gencos agreed to sell power to MER at fixed prices a few years ago. But the outbreak of the war in Ukraine upended their assumptions by causing a huge spike in fuel costs (coal, gas). These gencos tried to pass on the higher costs to consumers, which Meralco seemed to have initially resisted. Ultimately, these contracts were subsequently terminated and the gencos asked to be paid for the higher fuel costs during the interim period prior to termination (citing a “change in circumstances”). This petition was granted by the Supreme Court despite the gencos committing to fixed prices from the start.

 

The amount involved is certainly big and it appears that Meralco may now be a key beneficiary (we’ll need to confirm this) as it is now the biggest shareholder (40%) of the biggest genco involved (South Premiere, which owns the 1,200MW Ilijan plant).  Another firm that will receive this windfall would be ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) because its fixed price contract with Meralco was identical to those held by SMC.

POWER
ERC approves recovery of pre-terminated contract costs
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has permitted Meralco to recover a total of P31bn (P0.28/kwh) from end-users over a 36-month period starting in March 2026
  • Amount is related to cost recoveries involving “change in circumstance” applications from the following:
    • South Premiere Power Corp. (SPPC) – P15.85bn
    • San Miguel Energy Corp., (SMEC) – P13.4bn
    • ACEN Corp. – P1.75bn over 12 months (1-year impact represents 14% of FY26E pre-tax profit)
    • Panay Energy Development Corp. (PEDC) – P380mn over 12 months
  • The ruling follows a Supreme Court decision in favor of SPPC and SMEC and a Court of Appeals declaration that the price adjustments were executory
    • SPPC and SMEC sought to terminate its 10-year supply contract, signed in 2019, with fixed fuel costs
POWER
ERC reviewing viability of “forward contracting”
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is currently exploring the viability and legality of “forward contracting” for power supply deals
    • Under this framework, power utilities may lock in future supply from gencos at a predetermined price to shield consumers from sudden bill shocks
    • According to ERC chairman and CEO Francis Saturnino Juan
  • The ERC chief raised concern over the absence of competitive bidding for forward contracts, which could result in limited transparency and reduced consumer awareness

ACEN to finalize capital raising plans by 2Q
  • ACEN expects to finalize its capital raising plans by 2Q, with a stock rights offer still under consideration
    • According to ACEN President and CEO Eric Francia
  • Given the pace of its capital deployment, it may need to raise capital by 2H

MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in Dec
  • The Dec trade deficit narrowed to US$3.5bn, contracting 15% YoY, reflecting the faster growth of exports vs imports
    • This brings the FY25 trade deficit to US$49.2bn, 10% narrower YoY
  • Imports for the month grew by 7% YoY to US$10.5bn, a reversal from the 2% decline in Nov
    • The rebound to growth was primarily driven by the surge in capital goods (+20%) and fuel imports (+6%)
    • Notably, consumer goods fell 1%, the fourth consecutive month of a YoY decline—the longest streak since the pandemic in 2020
  • Exports climbed by 23% YoY to US$7.0bn, the twelfth consecutive month of YoY growth and accelerating from the 21% increase in Nov
    • Electronics exports jumped by 44% YoY to $4.0bn
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 58% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 3% to US$3.0bn
