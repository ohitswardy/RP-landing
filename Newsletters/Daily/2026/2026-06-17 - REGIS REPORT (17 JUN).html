REGIS REPORT

17 June 2026

Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel expects the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to lower farming costs and ease pressure on the agriculture sector. However, he warned that El Nino could still reduce the country’s rice output by 5% this year. This would imply milled rice production of about 11.8 MMT, about 5.7 MMT short of the projected consumption this year (of 17.5 MMT). Secretary Tiu Laurel previously warned that the price of rice could increase to P62/kg later this year, from P50/kg currently.  

 

+++

 

The government is moving to contain the sugar pest outbreak in the province of Negros Occidental with the use of pesticides. While the areas infested are still small, the risk of infestation spreading is high. This could deal a blow to sugar output in the province which accounts for over 50% share nationally. Universal Robina (URC PM) is among the largest sugar millers in the country, and this segment accounts for c. 20% of EBIT.

 

Having said that, if there’s significant crop damage, government policy on importation could be the single biggest factor affecting the profitability of the millers.

 

+++

 

The government said that over 50 companies expressed interest in the “Pax Silica” hub in Clark, with one firm already in advanced stages of due diligence.

 

The Philippines raised US$2.5bn from its second global bond sale this year. Given optimism over the potential US-Iran peace deal, the spreads over US treasuries were close to record lows.

MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture sees input cost relief as Hormuz reopens but remains wary of El Niño
  • The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) expects the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to lower farming costs and ease pressure on the agriculture sector
    • Following months of conflict, US and Iran have reportedly reached a deal to end the Middle East war and reopen the strait as the two states are expected to sign an agreement on Friday in Switzerland
  • DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel said lower fuel, fertilizer, and freight costs are expected to ease cost pressures in the upcoming planting season while providing farmers more flexibility to prepare for El Niño
    • The nation's weather bureau (PAGASA) said that there is an over 80% probability that a full-blown El Niño event will develop and likely persist until early 2027
  • The DA’s palay (unmilled rice) output projection for 2026 is currently 19.9 MMT (+1% YoY from 19.7 MMT in 2025) 
    • This would be roughly 5.7 MMT short of the USDA's projected rice consumption of ~17.5 MMT for 2026
    • The DA warned that the FY26 output projection could decline to 18.8 MMT (-5% YoY) accounting for losses from El Niño and typhoons
    • The 18.8 MMT palay output figure would translate to approximately 11.8 MMT of rice based on the 63% milling recovery rate reported by the Philippine Statistics Authority
CONSUMER
Sugar pest outbreak rapidly expands; threatening 60% of PH sugar output
  • The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) stated that the red-striped soft scale insects (RSSI) infestation nationwide as of June 6 has reached 2,249 hectares with Negros Occidental accounting for over 2,000 hectares of infestation
    • Negros accounts for 60% of the national sugar output
    • The infested area is approximately 0.6% of the total sugarcane area planted in the Philippines in 2025
  • The SRA said that the RSSI infestation is expanding rapidly across Visayas in existing areas and new localities with the total affected area recently increased to 460 hectares in one week
  • RSSI infestations can slash crop yields by up to 50%
    • The SRA is currently utilizing drone technology to apply insecticides to minimize crop damage and reduce pest populations
  • In FY25, Universal Robina's (URC PM) net sales and operating income totaled P168bn and P16bn, respectively—with the SURE (Sugar and Renewables) segment contributing 19% of both total revenues and EBIT

Government raises US$2.5bn from second global bond offering
  • The Bureau of Treasury raised US$2.5bn from the issuance of 5.5-year, 10-year and 25-year US dollar bonds
    • The US$500mn 5.5-year bond was priced at 55bps above US Treasuries (vs. previous auction spread of 50bps); Re-offer price 99.645 to yield 4.699% (coupon of 4.625%)
    • The US$1.65bn 10-year bond was priced at 92.5bps spread over US Treasuries (vs. previous spread of 80bps); Re-offer price 99.77 to yield 5.355% (coupon of 5.325%)
    • The US$300mn 25-year offering will be a tap of the existing 5.75% 2051 USD bond issued in January (yield of 5.85%)
  • Credit spreads for such debt are near record-tight levels and premiums are falling on default swaps, given renewed risk appetite from a US-Iran peace deal
  • The government plans to borrow P2.68tn (US$44.4bn) this year, of which ~11% will come from the foreign debt market
    • The government’s debt currently stands at ~P18tn, equivalent to a debt-to-GDP ratio of 65%
