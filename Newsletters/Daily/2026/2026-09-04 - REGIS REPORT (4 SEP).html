After decades of dormancy, the Philippine mining industry is entering a potentially transformative growth cycle, aided by high metal prices and a recently approved regulatory framework. Mining firms are suddenly profitable again and long-sleeping claims are now being developed, including the ‘mother of all mining projects’ in the Philippines, Tampakan.

 

We are thus initiating coverage on one of the miners that is poised to propel this growth: Apex Mining (APX PM), with a BUY rating and TP of P19/sh. Apex ranks as the country's second-largest gold miner and boasts of an industry-leading gold reserve grade of 4.8 grams per tonne. The company has strong near-to medium-term growth prospects, with the ongoing mill capacity expansions of its two operating mines, Maco and Sangilo, and the eventual development of its Amacan copper-gold prospect. 

 

Our forecast 23% 2025-2030 EPS CAGR is underpinned by gold output doubling to 200k ounces by 2030 and sustained elevated gold prices (peaking at 5,000/oz in 2027 and tapering off to a long-term price of US$3,500). The forecast production increase is supported by the mill capacity expansions at existing mines (3.5kt/day to 5.5kt/day) and the anticipated improvement of mill-feed grades, with higher-grade zones likely to be unlocked once the Tagbaros drain tunnel is completed next year. Beyond the base case, Apex also offers meaningful optionality from its adjacent Amacan copper-gold prospect, which could add another P3/sh (+18%) to valuation once developed.

 

APX trades at just 10x FY26E PER, sliding to 6x in FY27E, a steep discount to domestic peers (28x/14x) and international miners (16x/12x). Even under a conservative scenario that gold prices hold at US$4,500 (10% below base case but at par with spot), APX would still look undervalued at 7x FY27E PER.

+++

 

CPI inflation for Aug came in at 6.1%, in line with Street expectations. The headline inflation rate is slightly lower than the previous month’s 6.2%.

 

Among the notable details of this month’s CPI outcome are (1) a dip in Food inflation to 4.6% (vs 5.2%) despite rice inflation jumping to 19.4% and (2) a re-acceleration in Transport inflation to 13.5% (vs 11.9%).

 

Despite this month’s dip, we believe inflation risk is still to the upside and the Bangko Sentral (BSP) is likely to raise rates later this year as a result.

 

 

+++

 

Meralco’s (MER PM) power supply agreement with indirect subsidiary MTerra Solar is now live. The first phase of this contract is for 600MW mid-merit capacity (50% capacity factor) at a fixed price of P7.87/kwh with an incremental 250MW for the second phase (due Feb 2027). We estimate net profit from this project could reach ~P15bn on annualized basis, once operational.

MTerra Solar is 60% owned by SPNEC (SPNEC PM), which itself is 57% held by Meralco.

 

+++

 

Government debt rose 1.7% MoM to P19.39tn as of end-July. Debt/GDP reached 66% as of mid-year and it’s looking like the government’s target of 64.9% is unlikely to be achieved.

 

+++

 

MREIT (MREIT PM) executed a previously announced share-for-property swap with Megaworld (MEG PM) and Travellers International, the gaming arm of Alliance Global (AGI PM). It acquired 304,000sqm of mall, office, and hotel assets in exchange for 1.64bn new MREIT shares. This transaction increased its portfolio by 47% (to 950,000sqm) and its shares outstanding by 35% (to 6.3bn).

POWER/UTILITIES
MTerra Solar starts 600MW mid-merit supply contract with Meralco
  • MTerra Solar has officially commenced its 600MW mid-merit power supply contract with Meralco
    • This follows the plant’s declaration of commercial operations on August 26, with 950MWac of solar capacity and an 825MW BESS (equivalent to 3,300MWH)
    • Note that this operational capacity falls short of the company’s phase 1 target capacity of 1,750MWac (2,500MWdc)
  • We estimate that the minimum required daily output to meet the contract is 7,200MWH, as the contract requires a minimum plant capacity factor of 50%
    • Given the plant’s operational capacity of 950MWac and BESS of 3,300MWH, MTerra Solar will likely have a contract deficit
    • We nonetheless understand that the project’s “energized” or commissioning capacity is much higher than its operational capacity so it might not need to provide replacement power
  • Under its contract with Meralco (MER PM), MTerra Solar has committed to provide 650MW of mid-merit capacity by Feb 2026 and the entire 850MW mid-merit capacity by Feb 2027
  • At full build, we estimate the project’s standalone annual profits could reach ~P15bn
    • Meralco effectively owns 34% of MTerra Solar, through its 57% economic ownership of SPNEC PM (SPNEC owns 60% of Terra Solar)
MACRO
Government debt rises to P19.39tn in end-July
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 1.7% MoM (P323.5bn) to P19.39tn as of end-July
    • This was due to the net availment of domestic and external debt as well as the revaluation of foreign debt following the depreciation in the peso
  • Domestic debt increased by 2.1% MoM (P271.3bn) to P13.11tn due to the impact of the net issuance of domestic securities
  • External debt was rose 0.8% MoM (P52.5bn) to P6.23tn given the P17.1bn net external loan availment and depreciation of the peso
    • The average USD-PHP rate depreciated from P61.25 in June to P61.60 in July
  • The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 66.0% as of 2Q26 higher than the 65.2% recorded in 1Q26 and the 63.2% ratio at end-2025
    • The 66.0% ratio is the highest since end-2004 (71.6%)
    • The ratio is projected to ease to 64.9% by end-2026, 64.4% in 2027, and to 63% by 2030
  • For 2026, the total debt stock is projected to reach P19.77tn (+12% YoY) by year-end
