REGIS REPORT

04 June 2026

Banks’ asset quality numbers for April were worse than they seem at first glance. NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) was up 4bps MoM to 3.55%, and down 2bps YoY. However, remember that NPLs are only recognized after 90 days of delinquency. The Past Due (PD) ratio jumped a more aggressive 13bps MoM to 4.67%, and up 19bps YoY. Interestingly, the jump in PDs was starker from the corporate loan book. In particular, the Real Estate sector PD ratio surged 70bps MoM to 5.34%, which translates to more than P20bn (just under $330mn) in new PD loans in one month. The Construction sector improved slightly MoM, but PD ratio was still elevated at 16% due to government payment delays from the flood control scandal.

 

The good news is that loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) accelerated to 12.2% YoY, a 14-month high. Stronger loan demand, plus higher interest rates, could help boost margins. However, we remain vigilant over potential drag from credit costs. Recall that NPLs are 90-days delinquent, which means the stress from the Iran War won’t be seen until the May numbers at the earliest.

BANKS
PD rates blip up in April, led by Real Estate sector; loan growth accelerates to 12.2%

Asset quality metrics for Philippine banks deteriorated in April. The severity was partly masked by surprisingly fast loan growth of 12.2% YoY, up from 11.6% in March, and 14-month high. Note that we use loan numbers excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs) that tend to distort the ratios on a monthly basis.

 

System-wide NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) edged up 4bps MoM to 3.55%, though it was down 2bps YoY. In nominal Peso terms, NPLs stood at P11.3bn, up 2.0% MoM and 11.7% YoY. More alarming was Past Due (PD) loans, jumping to 4.67%, up 13bps MoM from the 4.54% in March, and up 19bps from 4.49% a year ago. In PHP terms, PDs increased by 3.7% MoM and 16.9% YoY to P27.4bn. Restructured Loans (RL) inched up 1bp MoM to 2.10%, but was down 4bps YoY. Meanwhile, NPL cover dipped to 90.85% from 91.37% the previous month, but was down sharply from 95.10% in April 2025.

 

The distress levels were ratcheting up in the corporate segments, with consumers starting to show early signs.

    • By bank type, the asset quality deterioration was seen more in the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs), while the thrift banks (TBs) and digital bank (DBs) were more mixed.
    • By sector, we noted big up-moves in Real Estate, with PD ratios surging 70bps MoM to 5.34% (up 49bps YoY). For further perspective, the PHP value of Real Estate PDs was up P20.3bn, or up 14% MoM to P162bn. Construction sector PDs eased slightly, down 13bps MoM, but were still elevated at 15.95%, up a whopping 650bps YoY, as the sector is still reeling from the government’s “flood control” infrastructure corruption scandal from 2H25, where even legitimate contractors are not getting paid. On the consumer segment, we saw PD ratios up 20bps MoM for both the Credit Card segment (to 7.71%) and for Auto loans (to 8.26%).

 

While the loan growth numbers are encouraging, we are keeping close watch over the PD and NPL numbers, particularly for the consumer segment, especially with the rapid inflation acceleration since the start of the Iran conflict.

Gatchalian elected as Senate president pro-tempore
  • Senator Sherwin Gatchalian was elected Senate President pro tempore, effectively serving as Acting Senate President after all leadership positions were declared vacant
  • This follows the attendance of Senator Francis "Chiz" Escudero, who was absent since Monday, breaking the 11-11 impasse between the Senate majority and minority, which allowed the 12 present Upper House lawmakers to declare all seats vacant

  • New committee chairmanships (i.e., finance, basic education, agriculture, among others) were passed onto members of the new Senate majority, replacing the recently appointed chairpersons who are part of the current minority
  • Former Senate President Alan Peter Cayetano opposed the changes in leadership and labeled the move an "illegal coup d'etat"
    • Gatchalian, however, affirmed that the move was legal as a previous Supreme Court ruling deemed 12 out of 22 members a quorum
    • The Malacañang (Office of the President) and House of Representatives, in separate statements, both recognized and welcomed the leadership of acting Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian
  • The final day of the 1st regular session of Congress will be on June 5 (Friday), with the 2nd regular session scheduled to resume on July 27, during President Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA)

CAAP demands AirAsia Philippines to settle outstanding fees by June 6
  • The Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines (CAAP) had reportedly issued a cease-and-desist order dated June 2 directing the AirAsia Philippines (AAP) to halt operations at airports for over P270mn in unpaid fees

Philippines may face new US tariffs
  • Additional US tariffs may be imposed on the Philippines as a US Trade Representative (USTR) investigation found that it and 59 other economies have not exerted enough effort to curb the importation of goods made with forced labor
  • The USTR proposed an additional tariff of 10 or 12.5% on imports from the 60 countries as "American workers are forced to compete globally on an unlevel playing field"
    • 54 countries were tagged as economies that do not have any measures against forced labor imports, these countries will face an additional tariff of 12.5%
      • Countries include the Philippines, Australia, China, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam
    • Six countries such as Canada, Ecuador, the EU, Indonesia, Mexico, and Pakistan will face an additional tariff of 10% for having failed to effectively enforce a prohibition of forced labor imports
  • The announcement comes ahead of the July 24 expiration of the 10% temporary tariff imposed by the Trump administration on February 20, the same day the Supreme Court struck down President Donald Trump’s tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act
