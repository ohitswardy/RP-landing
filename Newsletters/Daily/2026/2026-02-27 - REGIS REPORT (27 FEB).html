FY25/4Q25 results is moving along and we have a few here in our round-up:

 

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (-) – FY25 core profits came in at P34.5bn, up +1% YoY. While the reported core income was a bit lower than our estimates, we don’t view the results too negatively as the “shortfall” was due to higher non-cash costs. Mobile revenue rose 5% QoQ in the last quarter – the same pace as Globe (GLO PM). Dividends declared (P46/share) might have been a tad short, however, as it is based on the company’s reported core income. More below.

 

  • Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) (=) – FY25 headline profit was P5.7bn (+6% ex-one-off items) and is in line with expectations. 4Q numbers saw modest sequential improvement with topline and EBIT growing +8% and +13% to P61.1bn and P3.8bn, respectively. 4Q blended SSSG picked up to +3.6% from +1.6% the previous quarter as all formats saw an improvement. The Food and Drugstore segments (76% of total revenues) continue to be the primary drivers with healthy topline growth of 6% and 12.5%, respectively. The results, in our view, are an encouraging indicator of improving consumption spend.

  • Nickel Asia’s (NIKL PM) (+) – We have a bit more detail on 4Q25 results at the briefing yesterday. The doubling of profits in 4Q25 was largely supported by (1) a 37% YoY jump in sales volumes mostly from higher sales of lower grade limonite ore, as well as (b) a turnaround at the HPAL unit due to higher cobalt prices. While blended ASPs fell 6% YoY in the quarter due to unfavorable mix, management indicated that ore prices continue to trend higher in 1Q26.  The company tempered its renewable energy ambitions, reducing its 2028 capacity target to just 1GW from a prior target of 2GW. We see this as a pragmatic move that should enable management to focus on its core mining operations.

TELCOS
PLDT FY25: 4Q revenue bounce
  • FY25 core income P34.5bn (+1%).  PLDT’s core income for FY25 reached P34.5bn, up marginally (+1%) YoY.  While this profit figure appears below our estimates (P35.6bn), we are generally positive about the results. Mobile revenue rose 5% QoQ, the same pace as Globe.

 

The key reason for the profit miss was the sharp rise in depreciation charges in 4Q, otherwise core income would have been in line.  Headline profit, including exceptional items, was P30bn, down 1% YoY. Note PLDT took another P6.8bn in accelerated depreciation expenses in FY25. The company treated this as an one-off item which is excluded in the calculation of core income. This will unduly show a more flattering profit in subsequent periods.

 

 

  • Wireless revenue numbers improve in 4Q.  We note the meaningful improvement in 4Q wireless revenue, up 5% QoQ.  While it may be only 1% higher YoY, the 4Q number implies PLDT maintained market share vs Globe in the final period of FY25.

 

  • Fixed broadband net adds rise.  Wireline broadband subscribers rose 126k for the last quarter.  We view this as a significant improvement, as it outpaced Globe’s for the first time since 3Q24. Broadband revenue, however, has yet to pick up meaningfully.

 

 

  • Maya sees dip in 4Q earnings.  Fintech Maya contributed P681m in earnings to PLDT (which owns 37.6% of the former).  The implied 4Q contribution, however, mean a QoQ decline in Maya’s earnings.  Management attributed the decline more to “non-operating items” (suggesting a clean-up ahead of an IPO) than to the de-linking of e-wallets to gaming apps, which affected fee incomes.  For FY25, Maya posted a profit of P1.7bn.

 

 

  • IPO for data centers? The company is considering a listing for its data centers as means to raise funds to reduce debt.  The current plan is for the spin-off to involve only PLDT’s first eight DCs and not include its biggest and newest DC (Vitro Santa Rosa). This is likely being considered because Vitro Santa Rosa is still only <30% utilized. 

 

  • Dividend of P46/share.  PLDT declared a dividend of P46/share with respect to FY25 earnings. While there is likely to be another dividend later this year, this may be viewed as a mild disappointment as the market is expecting closer to P100/share for this year.

 

  • Capex drops and will drop further.  Capex for FY25 dropped 23% to P60.3bn, equivalent to 28% of service revenue.  As a result of the drop and further tower sales, PLDT was able to become free cash flow positive for 2H. For FY26, PLDT is guiding for capex to decline further to the mid-50 billion peso range.

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
BSP seeks to tighten liquidity requirements on e-wallets
  • The BSP issued a draft circular which proposes tighter liquidity requirements for e-wallets
  • Currently, these rules apply only to banks, which must hold at least 50 percent of outstanding e-money balances in trust
  • The remainder must be kept in liquid assets, including bank deposits, government securities or settlement accounts with the BSP
  • All liquid assets must also be unencumbered
  • E-wallets would also be required to develop formal recovery plans to navigate periods of financial stress
  • The BSP is also seeking to enhance its crisis intervention powers for nonbank e-money issuers in financial trouble
  • The BSP may declare when a company can no longer continue operations without external support and has no reasonable prospect of recovery within a practicable period
