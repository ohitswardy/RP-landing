14 January 2026

The Dept of Energy (DOE) has served notice to terminate ~18,000MW of contracts awarded, mostly under the Green Energy Auction Program (GEAP), because the parties awarded the contracts have been unable to deliver on their commitments to build the capacities promised.  Corresponding penalties are also being imposed.

 

Bulk of the cancelled contracts (~12,000MW) are held by Solar Philippines. Solar Philippines is controlled by Congressman Leandro Leviste, who was also the controlling shareholder of SP New Energy Corp (SPNEC PM) before he sold the latter to Meralco (MER PM).

 

It is unclear at this point who else holds the approximately 6,000MW worth of contract outside of Solar Philippines.  We understand that SPNEC, now controlled by Meralco, holds a 280MW contract that it has been trying to get out of.  The DOE had previously rejected SPNEC’s attempt to hand back the contract, and we expect the DOE would be imposing penalties for SPNEC’s failure to deliver the contracted power.

Regis Partners hosted a briefing yesterday with Jollibee (JFC PM) management to discuss its recently announced plan to spin off its international operations.  Management reiterated much of what was previously disclosed and was otherwise rather frugal with further details of the planned spin-off (partly for legal reasons).

 

Among the key things that JFC acknowledged were that there would be implications on operating costs and possibly financing costs due to the de-merger. Management did say it would take a disciplined approach to costs resulting from the spin-off.

 

There were a few important issues that weren’t explicitly addressed by management during the call. First is brand ownership and potential payment of royalty fees between the two entities. Second is the structure / mechanics of the distribution of ownership in the new “Jollibee International”. We assume it will be done through a property dividend, but this wasn’t explicitly said. Third is the current profit target of P24bn for the consolidated business by 2028. It is likely that JFC will no longer adhere to that target.

 

We believe many of these are issues that even management itself has yet to decide on at this point. That’s why the target completion of the spin-off and listing is not until “late 2027”.

CONSUMER
JFC - More on spin-off of international operations

We hosted JFC’s CFO, Mr. Richard Shin to provide more detail on the company’s announced spin-off and listing of the international business in the US. Below are our key takeaways, structured around the Q&A discussion during the call:

 

What is the rationale behind the decision to spin-off and separately list the international business?

The company further elaborated on how the separation of the int’l business can be beneficial strategically while potentially “unlocking value” valuation-wise.  Management cited sharper focus from a more business-specific approach to capital allocation and strategy execution. While the separation of the stable and cash-generative domestic business from the currently loss-making int’l operations, could improve JFC’s valuation and support a potential re-rating.

 

How will shares be distributed to existing shareholders?

JFC shareholders will receive shares in JFC International (JFCI) corresponding to their prevailing interest in JFC, implying a one-for-one distribution of JFCI shares for each JFC share held. However, the specific process that pertains to how exactly the shares will be distributed (i.e., through property dividend or opening of a foreign brokerage account, etc.) cannot be disclosed as of late.

 

How will the two businesses operate post-separation? Any changes to management, store operations, and franchise partners?

The domestic and int’l business will operate fully independent after the transaction with each entity having their own board and management team. There will be no immediate changes to roles and management structure. Store operations and franchise arrangements were also said to be business as usual.

 

What is the impact on costs? financing?

Additional cost concerns due to duplication or inefficiencies were said to be mitigated by cost-disciplined operating models and utilizing shared services. Existing group-level financing structures was said to remain in place in the near term, while post-separation financing arrangements are being deliberated.

 

Why is late 2027 the target date? What can accelerate or delay the timeline?

The transaction execution date is targeted for late 2027 given the numerous pre-requisite requirements for the separation of the two companies and potential listing of the int’l business. The target date, however, is subject to change should there be changes in the timeline due to factors such as regulatory approvals, audit readiness, financing considerations, market conditions, etc.

 

Well-intentioned but some questions remain unanswered

Overall, the proposed spin-off is well-intentioned but execution and market conditions remain key risks to a meaningful valuation. Other concerns such as the transaction’s implications on brand ownership and royalties, as well as management’s P24bn profit target by 2028 were not clarified during the call. We have a BUY rating on JFC with a DCF-based TP of P250/sh, which implies 17/14x PE (below historical average) for 26/27E on the back of 19/22% EPS growth.

Jollibee opens largest regional commissary in Cebu
  • JFC has opened its largest commissary as of late, serving the Visayas and Mindanao region
    • The commissary supplies ingredients and products to the Jollibee brand stores (Jollibee, Chowking, Mang Inasal, Greenwich, Red Ribbon, Burger King) distributing to nearby regions

    • In line with their sustainability targets, the facility operates on 99% renewable electricity
  • The Philippines accounts for 3,445 (33%) of JFC’s 10,304 stores globally and 55% of total SWS as of 9M25
