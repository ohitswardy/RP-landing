02 July 2026

BPI (BPI PM) just dropped another bombshell. BPI announced that it is also dropping all fees for cash deposits and withdrawals at many of its agency banking partners. This free service will initially be available at 1,300 of BPI’s partner stores (of which 800 are in the Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) network), but will be rolled out to 8,000 stores and pawnshops nationwide. The bank has been making waves this week, earlier announcing zero fees for bank transfers over InstaPay and PesoNet. While these will clearly cost the bank fee revenues near-term, they are aimed to attract more customers to the bank (and its wallet Vybe?), bring in / retain CASA, and hopefully to use its other fee-based services. So far, no other bank / fintech has responded to either move.

The government is looking to review tariffs on rice imports, current set at 35%. The Agriculture Secretary said that increased importation was harming domestic rice production. However, many decades of tariff and non-tariff protections have not helped improve domestic rice production. Meanwhile, a growing population means demand has risen well above production. Retail rice prices are already up c20% from the 4Q25 lows. The looming “super-El Niño” is already pushing up Vietnam and Thailand export prices. A tariff hike would be disaster for rice prices and for inflation – and/or would encourage smuggling.

 

Consumer sentiment is already down in the dumps. The BSP’s survey shows sentiment is at its lowest point since Covid. While the collapse in global oil prices will help, rising rice and food prices will disproportionally hurt the lower-income households.

 

+ + +

 

The World Bank says that the Philippines graduated to “upper middle income” status. While this sounds like something to be proud of, it potentially means reduced access to aid and official development assistance (ODA) from multilateral agencies.

Consumer sentiment and outlook in 2Q26 declines amid rising inflation—BSP survey
  • The latest Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) results exhibited that current sentiment for the overall consumer confidence index (CI) was more pessimistic deteriorating to -42.0% in 2Q26 from -15.8% the previous quarter
    • A negative CI implies more respondents are pessimistic than optimistic about the country's economic condition, family financial situation and income
    • The survey was conducted from April 6-18 with 5,503 households surveyed nationwide
  • The more pessimistic sentiment in 2Q stemmed from concerns over higher inflation, corruption in the gov't, and insufficient gov't policies and programs to mitigate rising prices
    • -42.0% is the lowest since 4Q20 (-47.9%)
    • Households indicated an inclination to prioritize essential goods while cutting back on discretionary spending
  • For the next quarter, consumer sentiment deteriorated to -16.3% from 1.8% the previous quarter
  • For the upcoming 12 months, consumers were less optimistic on economic prospects improving, with a neutral CI at 0.2%, down from 9.6% the previous quarter
    • Consumers expect higher unemployment and a weaker Philippine peso
  • The CES results are taken into account in the BSP's decision-making for monetary policy
    • Inflation has averaged 4.5% so far as of 5M26
      • For 2026, consensus expectations are at 6.0%
      • Regis expects inflation to average 7.5% this year
    • The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 27 (fourth out of six scheduled this year)
      • For 2026, consensus expects the BSP to conduct one more 25bp rate hike to 5.00%
      • Regis expects three more 25bp hikes to 5.50% by year-end

 

MACRO
Philippines attains 'upper middle income' status—World Bank
  • The World Bank stated that the Philippines has attained upper-middle-income status as the country's gross national income (GNI) hit a record $4,850, which is within the $4,636 and $14,375 upper-middle-income threshold
    • Prior to the latest reclassification, the Philippines was a lower-middle-income economy since 1987 as economic expansion could not keep up despite population growth
    • The World Bank notes that the country attained an upgrade in rank through "broad-based expansion" with gains across all major industries in an economy-wide shift as "GDP grew at an average of 5.8% annually over the past five years"
  • The promotion, however, may reduce access over time to official development assistance (ODA) and concessional financing from multilateral lenders
    • Other repercussions include the possibility that certain Philippine exports will be deemed ineligible for preferential tariff schemes
    • Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan notes that although concessional financing may decline—stronger economic fundamentals and improved access to private capital are expected to outweigh the adjustments
