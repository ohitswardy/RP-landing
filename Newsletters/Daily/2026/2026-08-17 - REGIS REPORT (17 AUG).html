BANKS
SECB 1H26 - strong PPOP +21%, but provisions drag as cleanup continues

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1H26 NPAT P6.1bn up 4% YoY. Profit for 2Q alone was P3.4bn, up 25% QoQ and 11% YoY. Loan growth slowed sharply to 1% YoY, in line with mgt guidance to focus loans towards a “higher quality” portfolio. NII/NIM was muddied by the return to accounting treatment of deposit swaps, but management estimates that “pro-forma” NIMs in 2Q26 were up 9bps QoQ and up 23bps YoY. Revenues were up 10% and Opex was up just 3%, which took PPOP up an impressive 21% YoY. The bank to provision heavily, up 50% to P7.6bn, despite NPL ratio improving to 3.04% and NPL cover rising to 85%, as the cleanup of the consumer book continues. Overall, a pretty encouraging set of results.

 

Management will unveil their new 3-year plan on 27 August. Ahead of that, a few highlights from the earnings call.

 

  • Loan growth slowed to +1% YoY from +5% in 1Q26 and from 16% in 2Q25. New management had guided for a shift to “quality loans”, returning to their core customer base of wholesale loans, and high net worth and mass affluent retail. Retail loans were flat YoY and down 2% QoQ, while wholesale loans were flat YoY and QoQ. Deposits were flat YoY, largely as the bank let go of high-cost TDs, and instead saw CASA up 8%, taking CASA ratio up to 52% from 49%.
  • 2Q NIMs, on a “pro-forma” basis, improved 9bps QoQ and 23bps YoY per management estimates. Recall that the bank returned to accounting for deposit swap costs in the FX gain/(loss) line in 1Q26.
  • Fee incomes were soft, -1% in 1H26, though management noted strong growth in loan and deposit fees (in the 20%+ range YoY). The bank said InstaPay and PesoNet fees were less than 0.5% of total revenues, and that they had already cut the InstaPay rate from P25 in 2025 to P10 in early 2026. Non-interest income got a boost from 25%-owned HomeCredit PH, providing about P600mn in equitized profit to SECB. Trading and FX losses dragged, though.
  • Opex was well controlled, up just 3% YoY, though management warned that some expenses would be back-ended. Cost-income ratio was 55%, and is expected to rise slightly in 2H.
  • PPOP surged 21% YoY in 1H as a result of the “positive JAWS” of faster growth in revenues vs opex.
  • Provision expense remained elevated, up 50% YoY as the bank continued the cleanup / repositioning of its loan book, particularly the consumer/MSME portfolio. NPL ratio improved to 3.04% and NPL Cover increased to 85%. Credit costs stayed high at 220bps; the bank expects credit costs to stay at these levels for the remainder of the year.
  • ROE improved to 8.7% despite the high provision expense.

 

The 2Q results were encouraging, in our view, given the strong revenue growth, and cost control. Any progress in reducing provisions could result in significant operating leverage and drive ROE improvement. We look forward to management’s disclosure of their 3-year plan.

Alliance Global 1H26 core profit rises 2%
  • Alliance Global 1H26 headline profit fell 23% YoY to P10.8bn
  • However, its core profit, which excludes gains from the deconsolidation of Golden Arches in 1Q25, grew 2% to P10.8bn, ahead of consensus forecast
  • Megaworld’s 2Q26 EBIT grew (3% QoQ) 7% YoY to P8.1bn, largely thanks to a 4.1ppt YoY expansion in residential GPM
    • The margin expansion was brought about by better product mix and “disciplined cost management”
  • Travellers’ 2Q26 EBITDA climbed 59% QoQ and 42% YoY to P2.7bn, thanks to higher revenue as well as “improved operating efficiency and cost discipline”
    • We estimate that cash opex was flat YoY (+6% QoQ) at P5.6bn despite the improvement in GGR
    • 2Q26 GGR grew 20% QoQ and 4% YoY to P7.9bn
    • 2Q26 VIP GGR increased 40% QoQ (down 3% YoY) to P2.8bn, thanks to “above average” win rates
    • 2Q26 mass GGR rose 13% QoQ and 9% YoY to P5.1bn despite “below average” win rates
      • The company said that its online platforms were generating c. P200mn/month in GGR. This implies that mass GGR would likely have fallen YoY if not for the online segment
  • Emperador's 2Q26 EBITDA fell 4% YoY to P3.0bn despite higher revenue, as EBITDA margin narrowed 1.6ppt YoY to 22.1%
    • 2Q26 revenue rose 22% QoQ and 8% YoY to P16.2bn
      • Brandy revenue grew 7% YoY to P10.5bn, while whisky rose 14% YoY to P5.8bn
        • Brandy growth was supported by the improved availability and continued demand for new products, (e.g., Club Emperador, Fundador Super Special)
        • The rebound in whisky was driven by a recovery across multiple geographies while also propelled by a low base (-24% YoY in 2Q25)
    • Brandy EBITDA was flat YoY, while whisky EBITDA fell 11% YoY
      • Near-term profitability was weighed by higher A&P spend
  • AGI is trading at 4.6X FY27E P/E

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air pax volumes rise 5% YoY in July
  • Cebu Air reported it flex 2.2mn passengers in July, up 4.7% YoY, driving overall seat load factor down to 84.4% from 85.6% a year ago
    • Domestic pax volumes rose 9% YoY to 1.7mn pax, with seat load factor dropping ~5ppts YoY to 84.4%
    • International pax volumes dropped 9% YoY to 485k, with seat load factor up 8ppts YoY to 83.8%
    • Rebound in July can be attributed largely to domestic market share gains, as competitors reduce capacity
  • Brings 7M26 pax volumes up 4% YoY to 16.7mn, well behind our full-year forecast of 31mn (+16% YoY)
