BANKS
Nov Asset quality stable MoM, better YoY; loan growth slows a tad

Philippine banks’ asset quality metrics were broadly stable-to better MoM in November, but significantly improved YoY. (We quote ratios using loans excluding reverse repos (RRP) and excluding interbank loans (IBL), as these can swing wildly on a monthly basis, and throw the ratios off.)

 

NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) was flat MoM at 3.53%, but a full 20bps lower compared to the 3.73% posted in Nov 2024. In nominal terms, NPLs were up 1.5% MoM to P544.7bn while loans were up 1.4% MoM. Past Due (PD) ratio inched down 1bp MoM to 4.51% from 4.52% in Oct, and was down 5bps from the 4.55% the year prior. PD loans were up 1.2% MoM to P696.0bn. Restructured Loans (RL) improved 4bps MoM to 2.15%, but was up 5bps YoY.  NPL Cover was steady MoM at 95%, but up from 93% in Nov’24.

 

Loan growth (ex-RRP/IBL), meanwhile, slowed slightly to 10.6% YoY, from 10.8% in October. This is the slowest YoY print since September 2024. System-wide LDRs eased slightly to 72.7% vs 73.1% the previous month.

 

By bank type, asset quality generally improved among the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs) and thrift banks (TBs), while they deteriorated quite badly with the smaller digibanks, with NPL ratio up 22bps MoM and with PD ratios up 59bps MoM. That said, the digibank industry is still quite small, and is rapidly growing (loans up 57% YoY), which does account for higher volatility of their numbers.

 

Overall, asset quality trends look benign. We are watching both the loan growth and delinquency levels closely, particularly given the sharp GDP slowdown due to the recent corruption scandals.

ICTSI on track to operate Batangas port by 2028
  • ICTSI said that it is progressing with the construction of the first phase of South Luzon Container Terminal (SLCT) in Batangas, with commercial operations targeted for 2028
    • First phase will have a capacity of 800k TEUs and will have a controlling depth of up to 18 meters and an 11-hectare container yard
  • This construction update is in line with our expectations
    • We anticipate SLCT will address the long-term capacity limitations of existing ports in Manila
  • SLCT accounts for 11% of our NAV-based TP of P560/sh

 

 

POLITICS
Impeachment complaint filed against President Marcos
  • An impeachment complaint has been filed against President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. over his alleged involvement in a corruption scheme concerning unprogrammed appropriations
    • The complaint also cited the alleged kidnapping and surrender of former President Rodrigo Duterte to the International Criminal Court, alleged failure to veto unprogrammed appropriations and other unconstitutional provisions in past budgets, alleged kickbacks from budget insertions, among others
  • This is the first impeachment complaint against Marcos since he assumed presidency in 2022
  • The Committee on Justice will determine if the complaint has probable cause to proceed; otherwise, it may recommend dismissal of the complaint
    • The House plenary may override the dismissal recommendation if at least one-third of all House members vote to proceed with the impeachment trial
    • If the impeachment complaint is dismissed before a Senate trial, another cannot be initiated against him for one year, as prohibited in the Constitution
CONSUMER
JFC provides update on Coffee and Tea and Chinese Cuisine segments
  • JFC in another press release provided more guidance on recent positive developments—the disclosure elaborated about the expansion-fueled growth of its Coffee and Tea and Chinese Cuisine segments
    • It highlighted its asset-light growth strategy through franchising and capital efficiency optimizations leading to accelerated payback periods of ~two years for company-operated Hong Kong and China stores
    • This is the company's third guidance disclosure after the recent update last Jan 15 and the initial guidance disclosure back in Dec 17
  • On the Coffee and Tea segment:
    • The segment was noted to have "recorded strong percentage growth" given successful marketing campaigns in Compose Coffee and high traffic-driven SSSG in Highlands Coffee
    • Store counts for Compose Coffee and Highlands Coffee are estimated at ~3,000 and ~1,000, up from 2,629 and 850 at end-2024, respectively
    • The segment contributes to ~21% of total SWS as of 9M25, led by CBTL at 9.9% and Compose Coffee at 7.7%
  • For the Chinese Cuisine segment:
    • Yonghe King was said to have ramped up opening of franchised stores as part of the company's effort to get China "back to financial viability and sustainable growth"
    • Tim Ho Wan in Hong Kong was said to now be profitable within six months post-acquisition and there are plans to increase North America stores to 20 by 2028
    • The segment contributes ~6% of total SWS as of 9M25, led by Yonghe King at 3.9% and Tim Ho Wan at 1.8%
POWER
CREC doubles capex to US$2bn; aims to raise capacity to 3GW by year-end
  • CREC plans to spend about US$2bn (~P119bn) in 2026, double its 2025 capital expenditure program of US$1bn
    • Capex program will allow the company to raise its gross installed capacity to 3GW by year-end from an operating capacity of 524MW in end-September 2025
    • Note that CREC’s market capitalization currently stands at P49bn (~US$830mn)
    • The company has secured 1.2 GW of renewable energy contracts under the fourth Green Energy Auction Program (GEA-4)
  • CREC plans to fund its capex requirements through a mix of 70% debt and 30% equity
    • CREC plans to inject around 1 GW of operating assets into Citicore REIT (CREIT PM), with proceeds going to the funding of its projects
    • CREIT’s market cap stands at P23bn (~US$390mn)
    • CREIT is 33%-owned by CREC and 29%-owned by SM Investments (SM PM)
  • As of end-September 2025, CREC’s gross debt stood at P42bn and cash stood at P14bn
    • Net debt-to-equity stood at 1.14x

 

CONGLOS
CARS program funding to be sourced from 2025 DPWH “savings”
  • The government clarified that existing budgetary items under the 2025 General Appropriations Act (GAA) remain available to settle valid obligations under the CARS program
    • According to a joint statement of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and Department of Finance (DOF)
    • Funding will come from the FY2025 declared and verified savings of the DPWH, which will be used to augment existing fiscal support arrearages of the Board of Investments
  • Note that our forecasts do not assume Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) will receive any tax incentives under the CARS program
    • TMP has P4.5bn worth of un-credited tax incentives under the CARS program, equivalent to ~30% of TMP’s FY26E profit
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 23% of FY26E profit
  • GTCAP trades at a 46% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,180/sh and a 4.5x FY26E PER
