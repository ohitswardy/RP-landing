We have a new report our, lowering our target price on Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) by 63% to P1.65/share and downgrading the stock to a Hold.

 

The company’s operations have deteriorated substantially due to the POGO ban, the decline in Korean and Chinese tourists, and the flood control corruption scandal. Looking ahead, operations are likely to remain under pressure from the impact of the conflict in the Middle East as well as the opening of a new integrated resort in PAGCOR Entertainment city which will add c. 20% to gaming capacity in the area.

 

Thus, we reduce our profit estimates and forecast net losses until FY28E.

We break down the potential impact of the monster 12% minimum wage hike for Metro Manila – the largest since 1999. Given the outsized nature – well in excess of inflation – the move should be net positive for consumers, particularly for lower-income households. Companies like CNPF, MONDE, PGOLD, and URC should be direct net beneficiaries. However, the initial costs must be borne by the companies, whose higher manpower expense will be a near-term drag on profitability. Within each sector, firms with lower reliance on labor should have an edge. More broadly, the hikes – this was just for NCR, with the other regions likely to follow suit – will lead to price hikes, which will only add to already elevated inflationary pressure.

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
BSP to implement consumer protection model beginning 2027
  • The BSP issued a draft memorandum which would guide all financial consumer protection supervisory assessments starting Jan. 1, 2027
  • The Financial Consumer Protection Risk-and-Impact Supervisory Model will link a BSP-supervised institution’s potential consumer impact and financial consumer protection risk profile to the level of supervisory engagement it will receive
  • The framework has three major components: consumer impact, consumer protection risk profile and level of supervisory engagement
  • The framework will initially cover banks and non-bank electronic money issuers
MACRO
Government debt inches up to P18.55tn in end-May
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 0.4% MoM (P76.1bn) to P18.55tn as of end-May
    • This was due to the net incurrence of domestic securities as the gov't continued to raise funds to support financing needs
  • Domestic debt increased by 0.65% MoM (P80.1bn) to P12.50tn due to the impact of the net issuance of domestic securities (P80.2bn)
  • External debt was flat MoM (-0.07%) at P6.05tn as favorable currency movements offset new external debt
  • The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2% as of 1Q26 from 63.2% end-2025
    • The 65.2% ratio is the highest since hitting 65.7% back in 2005
  • For 2026, the total debt stock is projected to reach P19.06tn by year-end
