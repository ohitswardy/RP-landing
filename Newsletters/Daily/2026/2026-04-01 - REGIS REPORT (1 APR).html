REGIS REPORT

01 April 2026

The government unveiled a new batch of relief measures in the face of the Iran conflict. President Marcos announced a price cap of P50/kg on imported rice, which last week was selling at P47/kg according to the Department of Agriculture. Marcos also expanded the subsidized P20/kg rice program, and announced direct handouts and fuel subsidies for farmers and fisherfolk

The country also seems to have procured significant amounts of petroleum products. The Department to Energy (DOE) disclosed that the country had an average of 51 days’ worth of fuel as of 27 March, up from just 45 days in 20 March. In the last few days, government officials and private companies claim to have secured supplies to last until end-June. Of course in times of crisis, contracts can be reneged upon…

Meanwhile, the BSP expects inflation for March to come in between 3.1% and 3.9%, as it expects the high oil prices to have been offset by lower prices of fish, meat, and vegetables. The flow-through into inflation will likely be felt harder in April/May.

+ + +

Ayala Land (ALI PM) announced a new P10bn share buyback program, after its shares hit lows not seen since early 2012. On our estimates, the stock trades on a 70% discount to NAV, 0.7x P/bk and 7.5x PE, levels no one at REGIS remembers seeing (and some of us have a seen a lot…). We estimate that the previous P28bn buyback, which ended in February, bought back about 7% of total outstanding shares. The P10bn is roughly 4% of outstanding shares due to the lower share price.

Puregold (PGOLD PM) reported FY25 NPAT of P11.3bn, up 9% YoY on revenue growth of 11%, in line with REGIS, but slightly ahead of consensus. Full-year SSSG was driven by S&R, at 6.1% with the main Puregold brand up 4.1%.  The results are consistent with most consumer companies’ results, showing strong momentum in 4Q25. Management will hold a briefing on 7 April.

Further highlighting the apparent recovery into 1Q26 was the re-acceleration of loan growth to 9.5% in February, up from the 9.3% clocked in January. Notably, the improvement was driven by the “productive” sector, up 8.6% YoY from 8.2% the previous month, while consumer loans decelerated slightly to 20.8% from the 21.3% in January. Curiously, loans to residents were up much faster, up 10.1% YoY, with a major drag coming from non-residents down 13.2% YoY.

CONSUMER/RETAIL
PGOLD FY25 profit rises 9%, in line with REGIS estimate
  • Puregold Price Club (PGOLD PM) reported that FY25 profit rose 9% to P11.3bn, while top line increased 11% to P242.4bn—results are in line with REGIS but ahead of consensus 
  • For 4Q25 alone, net profit climbed 15% YoY to P4.0bn, while revenues rose 11% to P74.4bn 
  • FY25 SSSG: 
    • Puregold +4.1% (decelerated vs 4.8% in 9M25) 
    • S&R +6.1% (accelerated vs 5.4% in 9M25) 
    • The company elaborated that Puregold and S&R FY25 SSSG were driven by higher basket size and increased foot traffic, respectively 
  • Derived 4Q25 SSSG: 
    • Puregold +2.0% (unchanged vs in 2.0% in 3Q25) 
    • S&R +8.2% (faster vs 6.7% in 3Q25) 
  • PGOLD President Ferdinand Vincent Co stated that the company will focus on “accelerating store expansion in key provincial markets” for 2026
  • More details after the briefing next week, Apr. 7 (Tuesday) 

 

MACRO
BSP expects March inflation to settle within 3.1-3.9%
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects March inflation to fall within 3.1-3.9% 
    • BSP expectations for Mar inflation to be at the higher end of the BSP's 2-4% long-term target range 
      • For 2026, BSP expectations for inflation this year is 5.1% (previously revised from 3.6%) 
      • Mar inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on Apr 7 (Tue) 
  • Upward price pressures are higher prices of domestic petroleum, rice, and electricity, as well as the depreciation of the peso 
    • These may have been partially offset by lower prices of vegetables, fish, and meat 
    • As of end-Mar, the USD-PHP depreciated to P60.74, from P57.55 at end-Feb 
  • Inflation has averaged 2.2% so far for 2M26, while inflation in Feb alone was 2.4% 
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expectations are at 2.9% and 4.0%, respectively 
  • The next Monetary Board meeting is on Apr 23 (second out of six scheduled this year) 
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect the BSP to hold rates steady at 4.25% 

 

MACRO
President Marcos approves price cap of P50/kilogram on imported rice
  • President Marcos approved the National Price Coordinating Council recommended price cap of P50/kilo for imported well-milled rice
    • The average retail price of well-milled imported rice in Metro Manila is P47.71/kilo
  • He will issue an Executive Order in relation to this
  • The government also expanded the P20/kilo rice program from 600 tons to 2,000 tons due to rising demand
  • President Marcos also said that more than 4 million farmers and fisherfolk are set to receive P2,325 each starting in April
    • Total of P10bn
  • Fuel subsidy will also be dispensed to 40,000 farmers, while fisherfolk will be given almost 100,000 fuel cards and fuel vouchers 

 

 

MACRO
Philippines' fuel supply increases to approximately 51 days
  • Dept. of Energy (DOE) Secretary Sharon Garin stated that the Philippines' fuel supply has been extended by around six days to 50.9 days as of Mar 27, higher than the 45.1 days as of Mar 20 
    • Given ongoing concerns on fuel supply uncertainty, Garin highlighted that supply continues to increase despite continued consumption which gives more lead time for the gov’t to replenish stocks 
  • Daily demand was noted to remain high, diesel consumption at ~32mn liters and gasoline at ~23mn liters 
  • The Philippine National Oil Co. (PNOC) stated that they are looking to secure additional supply of jet fuel and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) 
    • Petron (PCOR PM) was said to have committed around 19% of its crude supply for LPG production 
      • Petron's overall crude supply was said to last until June 

 

BANKING
Commercial banks’ loan growth picks up to 9.5% in Feb
  • Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.5% in Feb from 9.3% in Jan, the third month of single-digit growth after a previous 19-month streak of double-digit growth 
    • Loans to productive activities picked up to 8.6% vs 8.2% in Jan 
    • Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 20.8%, a tad slower than Jan's 21.3% rate 
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) significantly accelerated to 10.3% in Feb from 8.6% in Jan, this marks the highest M3 growth print since Nov 2020

CONGLOS/PROPERTY
GTCAP buys a direct 20% stake in Federal Land NRE Global
  • GT Capital bought a 20% stake in Federal Land NRE Global (FNRE) from its wholly-owned unit, Federal Land
    • Transaction valued at P9.16bn
  • Prior to this deal, Federal Land held a 66% stake in FNRE while Nomura Real Estate held 34%
    • FNRE owns properties in Cavite, Metro Manila, and Cebu, totaling ~220 hectares
    • We estimate GTCAP’s acquisition implies a P/BV of ~1x
  • The property segment accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s NAV
  • GTCAP trades at a 54% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,092/sh and 3.6x PER
