REGIS REPORT

03 November 2023

Japanese Prime Minister Kishida Fumio is set to arrive in the Philippines for a two-day visit.  Among other things, he is set to make an address to a joint session of the Philippine Congress tomorrow – a rare event for a foreign leader, and on a Saturday.

This visit (including the address to Congress) underscores the growing diplomatic and commercial ties with Japan which have deepened since the Aquino administration’s diplomatic shift and despite Duterte’s pro-China pivot.

+++

Following the early bird results from the banks, interims from the other names are starting to trickle in.

  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (+) - Despite core income coming in 15% lower QoQ, AP’s 3Q23 print is still meaningfully higher than typical profits, buoyed by a new normal for its generation business. Operationally, volumes for both generation (8%YoY) and distribution (5%YoY) jumped. The timing of refunds, however, dragged EBITDA from its distribution utilities 61% lower QoQ. AP’s 9M23 income of P26.7bn (45%YoY) implies it is well on its way to exceed Regis (P34.8bn) and consensus (P30.8bn) estimates for the full year. The company will hold a call on Tuesday.

 

  • Semirara (SCC PM) (-) – 3Q23 core profit fell 71% QoQ, 69% YoY to P2.95bn, as heavy rains affected coal sales volumes.  This brought 9M23 profit down 10% YoY to P22.4bn, lagging Regis’ and consensus forecasts.  Even though the biggest source of disappointment was lower coal sales volumes, the higher inventory level and recovery of the Indonesian Coal Price Index (ICI) so far in 4Q23 suggest the company can still catch up in 4Q.  We hope to get more after management’s briefing at 4pm today (Register here).   

 

  • DMCI (DMC PM) (-) – Following Semirara’s (SCC PM) disappointing 3Q23 results, DMCI also reported lower-than-expected 3Q23 profit of P3.84bn, down 53% QoQ and 44% YoY.  The nickel mining and construction units also fell short of expectations but was compensated by higher-than-expected contributions from the property and water segments.  Management is holding a briefing on November 9, 4pm (Register here).

 

Union Bank (UBP PM) was among the banks reporting 3Q numbers last week.  We don’t cover the name but we note a few things worth highlighting. Net profit for 3Q was down 57% to P1.7bn, which brought 9M profit to P8.1bn (-20% YoY, and looking below consensus trends).  Unlike the three big banks, UBP saw pretty strong loan growth (+18%), but this was paired with deposit growth of only +6%.  Some more detail below ahead of the bank’s briefing later this week.

+++

The Dept of Trade (DTI) is planning the pilot phase of its EV subsidy program in 3Q24.  Funding for the program will come from overseas and will partially defray the cost of public transportation vehicles as well as three-wheelers and two-wheelers.  For now, it’s unclear how this subsidy will be handed out.  How the subsidies will be distributed could affect the speed of the adoption of EVs, especially two-wheelers. Globe (GLO PM) and Ayala Corp (AC PM) are introducing the Gogoro two-wheelers and Meralco (MER PM) is also planning something similar.

+++

A week after the regulator allowed the termination of the previous contracts with San Miguel (SMC PM), Meralco (MER PM) is moving to replace them with the launch of a new competitive selection process for 1,800MW of capacity.  It’s unlikely, in our view, for any party to deliver new capacity by the target dates (Dec 2024, May 2025), even SMC (which opted to cancel the contracts because they become financially unviable).  Our view on the tightness of supply in the Luzon grid over the medium term is unchanged.

POWER
AP 3Q23 softer QoQ as distrib biz corrects; 9M23 core income of P26.7bn (45%YoY) still tracking ahead of est
  • AP reported a 3Q23 core income of P8.8bn (-15%QoQ, -5%YoY)
  • It was a soft quarter even compared to last year, which management attributed to timing of refunds for distribution utilities (3Q distrib EBITDA was down 33%YoY). We also note that 3Q23 is seasonally lower QoQ due to the trajectory of energy demand and prices after the summer peak season.
  • This nevertheless brings core income for 9M23 to P26.7bn, (45%YoY), mildly ahead of Regis (P34.8bn) but well ahead of consensus (P30.8bn)
  • AP’s generation and RES EBITDA for 9M23 was P46.7bn, an increase of 20%YoY
    • Implies 3Q EBITDA of P16.5bn (-3%QoQ, 4%YoY)
    • Energy sold in 9M (21%YoY) implies 3Q was 8,757GWh (-3%QoQ, 8%YoY)
  • Distribution EBITDA amounted to P7.2bn in 9M23 (50%YoY)
    • Implied 3Q EBITDA was P1.2bn (-61%QoQ, -33%YoY)
    • Energy sales hit 1,586GWh (0%QoQ, 5%YoY)
  • AP trades at 6.2x PE and 4.7x EV/EBITDA with a dividend yield of 6.6% on our FY24E

 

POWER
Semirara 3Q23 core income of P2.95bn (-71% QoQ, 69% YoY) disappoints on lower coal volumes, power ASPs
  • Semirara reported headline 3Q23 profit fell 66% YoY to P3.40bn
    • Stripping out FX gains and a one-off refund, we estimate core income fell 71% QoQ, 69% YoY to P2.95bn
    • 9M23 core profit stood at P22.4bn, down 10% YoY and trailing Regis’ FY23E: P33.3bn and consensus estimates: P32.7bn
    • We attribute the underperformance to lower-than-expected coal mining sales volumes and lower power ASPs
  • Contributions from mining fell 74% QoQ/YoY to P2.0bn in 3Q23, as coal shipments dropped 22% YoY to 2.5mn MT and ASPs fell 20% QoQ (36% YoY) to P3,315/MT
    • Coal shipments fell due to heavier rainfall and lower exports of high-grade coal to South Korea
    • This takes 9M23 coal sales volumes to 10.5mn MT, down 13% YoY and trending behind management’s guidance (15-16mn MT)
    • Coal inventory rose to 3.0mn MT in end-Sept, up from 2.8mn MT in end-2022, suggesting ample inventory to catch-up in 4Q
    • 9M23 ASP per MT stood at P3,964 (-24% YoY) vs FY23E: P3,895; ICI4 spot at US$62/MT vs 3Q23 average of US$52/MT
  • Contributions from power fell 54% QoQ and 35% YoY to P997mn in 3Q23 due to lower plant availability and spot prices
    • Recall that SPLGC Unit 2 went on extended outage from mid-June to end-Aug; Other plants also suffered from occasional deration
    • Power ASPs fell 22% QoQ/23% YoY to P4.82/kwh in 3Q23, bringing 9M23 ASP to P5.75/kwh (+2% YoY) – trending below forecast (P6.14/kwh)
  • As of end-Sep 2023, net cash stood at P19bn (P4.5/sh); Recall that SCC declared a special div of P3.5/sh (ex-div: Oct 20; Payment: Nov 8)
  • Management is holding a briefing on Nov 3, 4pm – Register here

CONGLOS
DMCI 9M23 profit falls short at P19.87bn (-25%)
  • DMCI’s headline 3Q23 profit fell 44% YoY to P4.09bn
    • Excluding FX adj and one-offs, we estimate core income fell 53% QoQ and 44% YoY to P3.84bn
    • 9M23 core profit at P19.87bn, down 25% YoY and trailing Regis’ FY23E: P27.6bn and consensus: P28.3bn
    • The disappointment largely came from Semirara, nickel mining and construction.  These offset higher-than-expected contributions from property and Maynilad
  • Property unit recorded profits of P1.37bn (+21% YoY) in 3Q23 and P3.86bn (flat) in 9M23
    • Profit is trending better than forecast due to higher-than-expected income from forfeitures
    • EBITDA fell 18% YoY to P3.08bn (In line) in 9M23 due to higher revenue reversals; It is worth noting that revenue reversals are declining sequentially (-9% QoQ in 3Q23)
    • Reservation sales surged 122% QoQ to P11.9bn (net of cancellations), thanks to new launches. This brings 9M23 reservation sales to 28.4bn (+11% YoY), trending well ahead of forecast (P30bn)
  • Contributions from Maynilad rose 70% YoY, 28% QoQ to P605mn in 3Q23 due likely to better water supply; Billed volume growth accelerated to 3% in 3Q23 from 1% in 2Q23
  • Construction profits fell 32% YoY to P459mn in 9M23, trending behind forecast due to slower completions and lower net margins
  • Nickel mining unit recorded a P154mn net loss in 3Q23 after ASPs plunged 59% QoQ, 52% YoY to US$20 per MT due to lower global prices and nickel grade
  • 3Q23 profit from the off-grid power unit improved by 16% QoQ and 26% YoY due to better margins from its new plants in Palawan and Masbate
  • Management is holding a briefing on Nov 9, 4pm – Register here

 

BANKS
UnionBank 9M23 net profit falls 19%, behind consensus forecast
  • UnionBank’s 9M23 net profit fell 19% to P8.1bn, behind consensus FY forecast of P13.1bn
  • Opex grew 63% to P33.5bn due to the full-year impact of the acquired Citi consumer business and UnionDigital
    • These new businesses only started contributing to costs in 2H22
    • The bank has spent a total of P3.6bn on one-time expenses coming from the integration of the Citi consumer business
  • Net interest income rose 34% to P37.3bn
    • Net interest margin stood at 5.3%, thanks to the higher proportion of consumer loans (56% of total loans)
  • Loans expanded 18% to P531bn, led by consumer loans (+22%)
  • Deposits +6% to P725bn
  • Not interest income jumped 93% to P15.5bn, largely due to recurring fee-based income on consumer transactions
  • UBP now has 13mn customers, from about 5mn customers in 2019

Meralco starts CSP for 1,800MW supply needs
  • Meralco has commenced the competitive selection process (CSP) for 1,800 MW of power supply
    • Will involve a 15-year contract beginning Dec 2024
    • This is meant to replace the capacity covered by Meralco’s 2021 PSA with San Miguel (SMC PM), which was terminated earlier this year
  • Submission of interest for interested power generation first is due on Nov 13
    • Bid submission deadline set on Dec 26
AUTOS
EV subsidy pilot program set in 3Q24
  • The Department of Trade and Industry plans to begin the pilot phase of the subsidy program for buyers of electric vehicles by 3Q24
    • Subsidies are initially set at P500k per unit for public utility vehicles, P20k per unit for three-wheelers and 10k for e-bikes
  • Subsidy program will be funded by the United Nations Industrial Development Organization and the Global Environmental Facility
  • To date, there are more than 16k EV units in the country (data includes hybrids)
    • Sales of EVs grew to 2,536 units in 1Q23, up 495% YoY
    • The EV Association of the Philippines (EVAP) targets to grow EV sales to a cumulative 6.61mn units by 2030, majority of which will be two-wheeled vehicles such as e-motorcycles
