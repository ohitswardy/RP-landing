REGIS REPORT

03 August 2023

We have a couple more results out today:

  • Ayala Land (ALI PM) (=) – 1H23 earnings rose 41% to P11.4bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts, thanks to strong residential and commercial lot revenue in the second quarter. Meanwhile, 2Q23 residential reservation sales grew 10% QoQ (21% YoY) to P30.6bn. About P5bn of this came from an Alveo project in Fort Bonifacio, which was only launched in June. Since the project still has P13bn of unsold inventory, it can still contribute to sales in future periods. Furthermore, sales could receive another boost since the company is looking to launch another high-end condominium project in 2H23.

  • Metrobank (MBT PM) (=) - Rounding out the Big Three banks, Metrobank reported 1H23 NPAT of P20.9bn, up 34% YoY, bang in line with Regis (50% of FY23E P41.75bn) and ahead of consensus (P40.2bn). Loan growth (gross) slowed to 8.6% YoY, but this was made up for by NIMs, which were up 10bps QoQ and 49bps YoY. This was driven by strong growth in consumer loans (+14%), particularly higher-margin credit cards (+29%) and auto loans (+17%). A drop in trading/fx gains dragged non-interest income. Meanwhile, asset quality remained stable, with NPL ratio flat QoQ at 1.8%. Despite the solid results, the stock trades at 0.74x book, 6.3x PER and pays a dividend yield of over 5%. Metrobank will hold its earnings call at 4pm today.
  • ACEN (ACEN PM) (=) – ACEN said that its 2Q23 hit P2.2bn (9%QoQ, 24%). The trends that helped its 1Q recovery - no fuel cost-contract price mismatch, net-seller position to the spot - extended into 2Q. Higher WESM prices arguably buoyed the performance, similarly seen in the performance of Aboitiz Power (AP PM). Overall renewables output in 2Q23 hit 994GWh (-6%QoQ, 13%YoY) by our estimates, while new capacity (both organic and acquisitions) should buoy 2H output further. ACEN’s 1H23 profit of P4.2bn (94%YoY) is 49%/44% of Regis/consensus estimates for FY23E, in line with ours and just mildly behind the street.

+++

Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM) surprised the market yesterday after disclosing it had submitted a bid, together with Coca-Cola Europacific Partners PLC (CCEP), for the bottling operations of Coke Philippines (Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines, Inc or CCBP). The bid was for an enterprise value of USD1.8bn on a “debt-free and cash-free basis” and would be split 60:40 between CCEP and AEV.

The foray into the consumer space follows a series of acquisitions triggered by the sale of a significant stake in AP to JERA (USD1.6bn). The acquisition is undoubtedly transformative from a portfolio perspective but the financial merits are ambiguous. Ownership of Coke’s bottling operations in the Philippines had already changed hands a number of times and the economics have always proven challenging.

++++

We have more detail on the proposed P5.768tn national budget for 2024, which was submitted to Congress yesterday. While the overall budget is up 9.5% YoY, the combined allocation for the Department of Public Works and Transportation is only up by 3.6% YoY and most of which are meant to fund right-of-way acquisitions. This suggests that the probability of a strong recovery in cement demand could be low. 

+++

The Ways and Means committee of the Lower House approved a new substitute bill that will revise the fiscal regime of the mining sector, essentially raising taxes. Meanwhile, the massive Tampakan mine copper-gold project is finally underway, with initial production targeted by 2026. Although the Philippines is rich in mineral resources, a lot of these are untapped due to regulatory instability. The current administration is seeking to revive the industry, which should add another leg to the country’s growth and help trim the current account deficit.

MACRO
DBM submits P5.77tn national budget to Congress
  • The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) submitted its proposed P5.768tn national budget for 2024
    • This is 9.5% YoY higher than the 2023 approved budget and is equivalent to 21.7% of GDP
    • Combined budgets for public works/transportation are up 3.6% YoY to P1.04tn (c. 18% of total budget)
    • Defense budget hiked by 14% YoY but budget for health cut by 2.7% YoY
  • Under the proposal, the Department of Transportation (DoTr) is seeking P164bn in funding for the construction and maintenance of its seven railways
    • North-South Commuter Rail (NSCR) will receive P76.3bn, of which P53bn will be used to fund right-of-way (ROW) acquisition
    • Metro Manila Subway project will get an allocation of P68bn, of which P21bn will be for ROW and tax expenses
    • MRT-3 will obtain P10.3bn for its maintenance and rehabilitiation
    • It will also allocate funding for its portion of the LRT-1 Cavite extension and pre-construction works for MRT 4 and PNR South Long Haul

BANKS
MBT 1H23 up 34%, in line; loans slow, but NIMs up 10bps QoQ
  • Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 1H23 NPAT of P20.9bn, up 34% YoY. This was in line with Regis expectations (50% of our FYE P41.7bn), but ahead of consensus (FYE P40.2bn). Loan growth (gross) slowed, though, to 8.6% YoY from 12.5% in 1Q23 (net loans slowed to 9.3% from 13,5%). However, consumer loans grew faster at 14.1% YoY, driven by credit cards (+29%) and auto loans (17.5%). This helped loan yields surge 67bps QoQ, helped in part by the full-quarter impact of the raised credit card rate cap in February. However, deposit costs spiked, up 43bps QoQ. Despite this, NIMs managed to gain 10bps QoQ.
  • Non-interest income was dragged by a drop in trading/fx gains. Fee incomes, though, were up 10% YoY. Opex grew 14%, driven largely by taxes/licenses from the growth in TDs, and by higher IT expenses.
  • Asset quality was stable, with NPL ratio at 1.8% (flat QoQ) and NPL cover at P184%.
  • The bank will host an earnings call tomorrow, 03 August, at 4pm

POWER
ACEN posts P2.2b profit in 2Q23 (+9%QoQ, 24%YoY); 1H23 is in line with Regis but tracking mildly lower than cons
  • ACEN said disclosed that its 2Q23 net income amounted to P2.2bn (9%QoQ, 24%YoY), lifted by higher operating capacity and a stronger wind regime.
    • The higher capacity also led to a sustained net seller position to the spot market, which allowed them to benefit from the strong WESM prices in 2Q23.
  • For 1H23, ACEN’s net income amounted to P4.2bn (94%YoY) and in line with our estimates.
    • Although tracking mildly behind consensus estimates, capacity additions should lead to a better performance in 2H.
  • For the 1H, total renewable output grew 21% to 2,052 GWh, implying 2Q23 output was 994GWh (-6%QoQ, 13%YoY)
  • Renewable capacity is up to 4.3GW (from 4.1GW in 1Q23) from both operational and under-construction plants
  • We hope to get more from the briefing later at 4PM

AEV, Coca-Cola Europacific Partners PLC (CCEP) bid for Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines
  • Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV) and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners PLC (CCEP) have a submitted a non-binding bid to The Coca-Cola Company (TCCC) for its 100% ownership in Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines, Inc (CCBP) at an EV of USD1.8bn on a debt-free/cash-free basis
  • AEV is in advanced discussions with CCEP regarding the potential transaction based on a 60:40 ownership structure between CCEP and AEV

    • ~650mn unit cases
    • 1mn outlets, 19 manufacturing plants
    • 69% sparkling value share, 43% NARTD value share
  • AEV would record its prospective 40% stake under the equity method
  • It intends to fund its share of the transaction through a mix of debt and cash

    • Likely raise additional debt
  • From AEV’s perspective, potential synergies can be derived from the bank (financing distributors), data mining for analysis and cross-selling and power (bottling facilities)
  • Market NAV is about P46/share (P259bn), with parent net debt of P42bn as of 1H23
MINING
House panel approves mining fiscal regime bill
  • The House Ways and Means Committee approved a new substitute bill establishing a fiscal regime for the mining sector
    • Large-scale metallic mining corporations within mineral reservations will be subject to a 3% royalty of gross output
    • Large-scale corporations outside mineral reservations will be slapped with a margin-based royalty on income (e.g. margins of 1-10% will be subject to a 1% rate. The rate could rise to as high as 5% if margins are above 70%)
    • Small-scale miners will be imposed a royalty equivalent to 1/10th of 1% of gross output of minerals or mineral products
    • A windfall profits tax will also be slapped based on margins (e.g. miners with margins more than 35% up to 40% will be imposed a rate of 1%/ margins of more than 80% will have a 10% rate)
  • Ronald S. Recidoro, executive director of the Chamber of Mines of the Philippines, said its members representing large-scale contractors “feel that further increases (in tax) may not be necessary at this time”
    • Nonetheless, he told the committee that “the Chamber of Mines manifests its support for the aforementioned House bill”
    • The proposed new tax structure would result in a higher tax take from the industry “under a profitable scenario.”
  • The Senate has yet to file a mining fiscal regime bill
MINING
Sagittarius Mines to start work at largest copper-gold project in 4Q
  • Sagittarius Mines (SMI) said it is ready to start construction in 4Q23 and produce its first copper concentrate in 2026
    • According to SMI external affairs and communications manager Roy Antonio
    • SMI owns the US$5.9bn Tampakan copper-gold project, which is one of the largest undeveloped copper-gold deposits in the world
    • The site is estimated to contain 15mn tons of copper and 17.6mn tons of gold
    • SMI is wholly-owned by Indophil Resources Phils, whose beneficial owners are the Alcantara and Sy families (not part of SM Investments)
  • SMI projects to contribute P76.6bn in national taxes, P4.8bn in local taxes, and P6.6bn in royalty for the Blaan indigenous people in its first 10 years of operation
    • Despite not operating, SMI has invested P31bn in developing the project and provided P2.6bn in national taxes, P39mn in local taxes, and P1.4bn in community and social dev’t programs
