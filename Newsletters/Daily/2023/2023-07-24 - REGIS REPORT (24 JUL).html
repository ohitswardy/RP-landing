We released a new report on Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM), with Aaron (aaron.salvador@regis.ph) reiterating his BUY call on the stock with a revised target price of P15.0/share (+28%).

As investors turn the page on the pandemic, the investment case shifts to growth arising from the build-out of its domestic portfolio. After returning to peak profitability in 2023, we project Solaire North’s ramp up to result in an 21%/36% CAGR for EBITDA and profit until 2025. Solaire, only 70% utilized, has the capacity to accommodate a more meaningful return of foot traffic, while the opening of Solaire North in 2024 should spur more frequent visitation from players coming from that part of town. The 280-ha Cavite property, meanwhile, lifts the headroom for long-term development plants.

At 9.3x/7.6x EV/EBITDA for FY23E/FY24E, BLOOM is trading below historical levels and well under the Macau average (17x/10x). We maintain the view that the gap between regional and Macau operators is no longer warranted given more visible prospects and lower oversight risk.

LT Group’s (LTG PM) tobacco operations saw a 20% YoY drop in tobacco volumes for 2Q23, based on disclosures by its partner Philip Morris (PM US), which owns the other half of PMFTC.  Unit sales for 2Q were 5.6bn, representing market share 56$%, down another 3.6ppt.  Industry volumes declined 11% YoY to 10bn sticks.  The drop in sales suggests that earnings from PMFTC, and thus dividends (P1.40 last year, 14% yield based on last price), could decline.

Beyond PMFTC’s market share loss, what’s more concerning is that the industry appears to be shrinking more than the tax/price increases warrant – and it’s not simply because of lower tobacco use. The overall collection of tobacco excise taxes for FY22 declined 9% to P160bn, despite a ~10% tax increase.  Illicit trade, it seems, is on the rise.

Megaworld (MEG PM) sold 10% of MREIT (MREIT PM) to 56%, ahead of the injection of new assets into the latter. The move is to keep the public float above 33%. We estimate the FY24 dividend yield of MREIT to be 6.6%.

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr will make his second State of the Nation Address (SONA) this afternoon.  The SONA is the once-a-year speech that the President makes to the joint Houses of Congress. It is part political theater and part a laundry list of government priorities.

Last year, the SONA outlined Marcos’ call for PPP infrastructure projects, amendments to the power sector laws, taxes on digital services, creation of a water department, ending of Covid-era restrictions and the write-off of farmer debt relating to agrarian reform.

Of the list, two big items were accomplished, so far: the lifting of the public health emergency relating to Covid and the passage of a law condoning P57bn of debt owed by the beneficiaries of agrarian reform.

MACRO
Gov’t debt service rises 98% YoY to P819bn in 5 months
  • The Philippines' debt service nearly doubled in the January to May 2023 period, reaching P819.5bn, a 97.92% YoY increase
    • Amortization of debt payments in the first five months of 2023 amounted to P589.952bn, up 204.72% YoY
    • 5M23 interest payments rose 4.1% YoY to P230bn
  • Debt service payments was highest in February 2023 at P375.714bn, followed by P204.763bn in April 2023
  • In May 2023, the debt service contracted by 14.62% YoY to P49.047bn
    • Interest payments accounted for the bulk of the debt service payments in May, reaching P41.34bn, up 22.21% YoY
      • Domestic interest payments in May were P29.529bn, while payments for foreign loans reached P11.815bn
    • Amortization payments in May 2023 reached P7.71bn, lower by 67.38% YoY
      • Domestic amortization payments in May were P2.656bn and external payments amounted to P5.047bn
  • The government’s debt stock reached P14.096tn in end-May, up 5.1% from end-2022
    • The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.2% for 2023
    • Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51.1% by 2028

CONSUMER
LTG's 2Q23 cigarette volumes fall to 5.6bn sticks (-20%YoY, -15%QoQ), mkt share down 3.5ppt to 56%
  • Philip Morris International disclosed that PMFTC’s cigarette volumes in the Philippines fell to 5.6bn sticks in 2Q, down 20%YoY and 15%QoQ. Without providing much detail, the disclosure attributed the volume decline to the performance of the Marlboro
  • Industry volumes (10bn sticks, -11%YoY, -10%QoQ) are again down by less than PMFTC’s volumes). PMFTC’s unit market share notably fell 6.5ppts YoY, 3.5ppts to 56.0%.
  • Recall that PMFTC raised retail prices of Marlboro, Fortune and Jackpot by around 15% in February this year to an average price of about P120/pack. These were meant to cover the increase in excise taxes at the start of the year (from P55/pack to P60/pack). Impact of the price hike could have spilled over into 2Q. Company has also talked about how illicit trade as a % of total industry may be back to the teens (from under 5% at the end of 2021).
  • The government’s tobacco tax revenue declined for the first time in 2022 – to P160bn from P176bn in 2021 – after the tax rate hit P55/pack
  • LTG paid out virtually its entire share in PMFTC’s earnings last year as dividends (P15.1bn, P1.40/share, 14% yield) to LTG holders. Assuming the same payout, dividends this year may be closer to P12-13bn (P1.10-1.20/share) for an 11-12% div yield.
