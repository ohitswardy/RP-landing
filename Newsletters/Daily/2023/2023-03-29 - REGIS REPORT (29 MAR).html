REGIS REPORT

29 March 2023

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) announced it was taking a 16% interest in solar power developer Solar Philippines New Energy Corp (SPNEC PM), with an eye to taking control via an option to increase the stake to 43% of an expanded capital base. SPNEC has 160MW of operating assets in it (or about to be injected), and claims a development pipeline of over 8,000MW.

The initial tranche of 16% will be acquired by MPI for P2.0bn (1.6bn shares at P1.25/share) from its controlling shareholder Solar Philippines (Solar PH). Solar PH is expected to advance the funds to SPNEC to allow the latter to pursue its projects. SPNEC has minimal assets to its name now but is set to acquire land (and rights to) and permits held by Solar PH via a share-for-asset swap.

After a short hiatus, we are back with more results today:

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (=) – FY22 NPAT of P9.3bn (+14% YoY) came in within Regis expectations but ahead of consensus. 4Q22 net sales grew 16% YoY on the back of +12% consolidated SSSG (+12%), accelerating from 3Q. Trends across both brands were mixed – we reckon due to base effects – but underlying growth appears to be healthy. There was some margin compression in 4Q22, which we believe was likely driven by front-loaded opex for the new 3 new S&R stores earmarked for 2023.
  • Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (+) – Stripping off non-recurring gains from CREATE in 2021, we estimate that FY22 recurring profit rose 5% to P2.9bn. However, some operational trends were weak in 4Q22. Office revenue fell 10% QoQ while reservation sales fell 28% QoQ. The reasons for the declines will be revealed during the briefing at 3PM today.
  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) – While Cebu Air continued to report a net loss in 4Q, the positive trajectory in its operations is clear, if delayed. For FY22, the airline’s loss was P14bn, smaller than the previous year’s P24bn. Loss in 4Q was P1.9bn, while EBITDA was a positive P2.3bn, around 1/3rd of pre-pandemic levels – a figure we expect to improve sharply as demand reaches “normal” and the fall in fuel prices is reflected in its P&L over the next few months.

The government says it will attempt to reform the pension system for uniformed personnel, a move that’s likely to be politically difficult. The national government has an unfunded pension liability with regard to its military and police personnel which has become massive (likely at least US$200bn) because of its generous benefits and low retirement age. The amount set aside for those pensions in the national budget this year alone is ~P120bn – equivalent to 0.5% of GDP and over 2% of the national budget. In fact, that figure is even slightly larger than the entire budget for the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP).

MACRO
Pres. Marcos looking to reform military pension program
  • Finance Secretary Diokno said that President Marcos was willing to use his political capital to reform the military pension system
  • Secretary Diokno warned of a fiscal collapse if this does not happen
  • The government has set aside P120bn-P130bn for the pension program this year
  • Pensions of retired military personnel are indexed to salaries of active members of the military
  • Secretary Diokno said that Defense Secretary Galvez and Interior Secretary Abalos agreed with all the options
    • Retirement age will rise from 56 to 57
    • Active members of the military will contribute to their pensions
    • Pensions of retired personnel will no longer be indexed to salaries of active members of the military
RETAIL
PGOLD FY22 NPAT P9.3bn (+14% YoY); SSSG accelerates in 4Q
  • In a press release PGOLD reported FY22 NPAT +14% yoy to P9.3bn — broadly in line with Regis (P9.5bn) but ahead of consensus (P8.6bn)
  • FY22 net sales +12% yoy to P184.3bn, in line with our estimates. Meanwhile, EBIT was P14.5bn (+12% yoy), mildly behind expectations.
  • Segmental data has yet to be disclosed but here are some highlights from the consolidated 4Q22 results:
    • SSSG for the core Puregold brand accelerated to +10% (+6% in 3Q), though partly driven by a low YoY base. Meanwhile, S&R posted SSSG of +15%
    • 4Q EBIT margin contracted 30bps QoQ / 50bps YoY. This was largely driven by (1) a lower GPM and (2) proportionately higher opex – we believe these likely relate to S&R given the peso depreciation and 2023 expansion plans (3 new stores)
  • PGOLD currently trades at 9x 2023E PE.

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air: EBITDA sharply recovers in 4Q22
  • Headline net loss shrunk to P1.93bn in 4Q22
    • Excluding MTM losses on its CB option, one-off losses on aircraft sales, and FX adj, we estimate core losses narrowed by 59% QoQ to P1.23bn in 4Q
    • Cumulative FY22 core loss was wider than we had anticipated at P12.1bn (-51% YoY) due to a slower-than-expected recovery and higher than expected costs
  • EBITDA sharply recovered to 34% of pre-Covid levels in 4Q22, up by 19x QoQ to P2.28bn
    • Due to (a) re-opening of North Asia (ex-China) and (b) favorable fuel and FX
  • System-wide 4Q22 pax volumes rose 6% QoQ, reverting to 79% of pre-Covid levels from c. 75% in 2Q/3Q
    • Domestic pax volumes slightly declined QoQ, as CEB’s market share dropped to 53% in 4Q from 57% in 3Q and 60% in 2Q (pre-Covid market share was at 52%)
    • Management cited intensified competition as other airlines caught up in re-deploying capacity largely in domestic routes, given slow ramp-up of international
  • Management is optimistic about its outlook for 2023, as economics of business normalizes back to pre-Covid
    • It sees a return to profitability as early as 1Q23
    • Net operating fleet additions of +6 aircraft, taking end-2023 fleet to 76 (Other carriers have smaller fleets today vs. pre-pandemic and will not expand)
    • System-wide seat growth of +30-35% YoY, which will take seat capacity 10-15% above 2019 levels
    • It expects to gain back domestic market share to 57% by end-2023 from 53% in end-2022, with load factors normalizing back to pre-Covid levels as other airlines re-deploy capacity to international
    • Systemwide pax volumes is expected to be 22-23mn this year, broadly in line with pre-Covid (22.5mn)
    • Cost per ASK (ex-fuel) will continue to improve sequentially and revert to pre-Covid levels in 2H, as fleet utilization normalizes

PROPERTY
Filinvest Land 4Q22 office revenue falls
  • Filinvest Land’s FY22 headline net profit fell 24% to P2.9bn
    • Stripping off non-recurring gains from CREATE in 2021, we estimate that recurring profit rose 5% (to P2.9bn)
    • Ahead of both REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • FY22 office revenue fell 3% to P4.67bn
    • 4Q22 office revenue fell 10% QoQ and 6% YoY, likely signaling a drop in occupancy
    • Occupancy could fall further since as of 3Q22, FLI’s office buildings scheduled for completion in 2023 had no preleased tenants
  • 2022 presales rose 12% to P18.0bn, 93% of the 2019 figure
    • However, payment schemes are still looser than in 2019
    • 4Q22 reservation sales fell 28% QoQ (up 4% YoY)
  • FY22 mall revenue more than doubled to P1.68bn
    • 4Q22 mall revenue climbed 21% QoQ to P536mn
  • Separately, RCBC (RCB PM) disclosed to the PSE that it is looking to sell some of its property subsidiaries to FLI
  • Filinvest Land will hold a briefing at 3PM today
  • FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY23E P/B, and 5.5X FY23E P/E

BANKS
Pres. Marcos OKs DBP-Landbank merger
  • Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said Marcos approved the merger during a sectoral meeting on Tuesday and that this could happen before the end of this year.
  • The approval was based on the following 10 arguments:
    • The merger will create a bigger and stronger bank to better serve the country’s development needs
    • The combined branches of the two banks results in a wider network of its banking operations
    • The merged bank will become the largest bank in the Philippines in terms of assets and deposit size
    • The merger will eliminate redundancy and inefficiency in operations
    • Projected operating costs savings due to the merger could reach at least P5.3 billion per year, or more than P20 billion over the next four years; this is a conservative estimate
    • The merger complements the strengths and addresses the weaknesses of the two banks
    • The improved financial position will provide a bigger headroom for loans that can be used for development projects
    • The merger will help avoid the need for DBP to recapitalize—from P30 billion to P100 billion—and seek capital infusion from the national government
    • The merged bank will be in the best position to serve as the sole authorized government depository bank for the entire Philippine government and its instrumentalities
    • Having a single bank remains the best practice in the region and simplified procedures with counterparties.
