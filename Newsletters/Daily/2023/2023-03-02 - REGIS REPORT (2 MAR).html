REGIS REPORT

02 March 2023

For today’s results round-up, we have Megaworld (MEG PM) and Security Bank (SECB PM), both of whom are still to hold their respective earnings calls.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (+) – FY22 operating profit jumped 32% to P20.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. The outperformance was largely due to the 3.8ppt expansion of residential GPM, which was likely brought about by a higher proportion of revenue coming from lots. Meanwhile, FY22 reservation sales grew 49% YoY to P119bn, also ahead of our estimates, as sales remained strong for a second consecutive quarter. Looking ahead, Megaworld Chief Strategy Officer Kevin Tan is optimistic that the company can hit “pre-pandemic numbers” this year. This would imply a much stronger performance than what we (or the street) are expecting.
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) – Security Bank (SECB PM) FY22 NPAT managed to simultaneously beat, and to disappoint. Net profit came in at P10.56bn, up 53% YoY, roundly beating our estimates, though roughly in line with consensus. The outperformance was nearly entirely driven by provision expense, which was nearly half of our FY forecast. To be fair, NPL improved significantly, down 29bps QoQ and nearly 100bps YoY to 2.95%. However, the big disappointment came in NIMs, which plunged 34bps QoQ in 4Q22, following a 20bps QoQ drop in 3Q. While loan yields in 4Q22 climbed 48bps QoQ, this was negated by a 72bp QoQ surge in deposit costs. Net interest income for 4Q was actually down QoQ, despite loans growing 4% QoQ. This highlights SECB’s lack of low-cost CASA deposits. In contrast, the Big Three banks still saw NIMs expand by 6-13bps QoQ. While their funding costs still increased, the Big Three’s much larger CASA bases helped mitigate the rise.

The bank will hold its earnings call this afternoon at 4pm.

+++

Megawide (MWIDE PM) announced plans to invest in the data center business with a Singapore-based partner. The intention is to initially build a 69MW facility in Cavite. MWIDE will own 49% of the operating company.

+++

The government ended FY22 with a budget deficit of P1.61trn, wider than the target of P1.5trn, despite healthy revenue numbers. The deficit is equivalent to 7.3% of GDP. The government is aiming to cut the deficit to 6.1% of GDP this year and further to 3.5% by 2026.

+++

Cebu Air (CEB PM) is adding five aircraft orders, which implies that its fleet size will be roughly flat this year (vs last year) amidst a surging rebound in demand. The airline now expects to return to profitability in 1Q23.

MACRO
Budget deficit widens to P378bn in December; FY22 deficit at 7.3% of GDP
  • The government’s budget gap widened to P378bn in December, +12% YoY
    • Tax revenues jumped 13.6% YoY, slowing down from 17.8% in 11M22
    • Expenditures rose at a faster rate of 13.6% YoY in December from +9.9% YoY in 11M22
  • FY22 budget deficit at P1.614tn, lower by 3.4% YoY and represents 7.3% of GDP (vs government target: P1.50tn, 6.9% of GDP)
    • Tax to GDP (i.e. tax effort) improved to 14.6% (vs. peak of 14.5% in 2019) due to improved collections from the Bureau of Customs; BIR tax effort at 10.6% in 2022 (vs. 11.1% in 2019)
    • For 2023, the government is targeting a lower deficit of P1.47tn, equivalent to 6.1% of GDP
    • The government aims to bring down the deficit to GDP ratio to 3.5% of GDP by 2026

BANKS
SECB FY22 NPAT ahead on provisions; but NIMs plunge 34bps QoQ
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT for FY22 of P10.56bn, up 53% YoY, well ahead of Regis, but broadly in line with consensus. The beat was due largely to much lower provision expense as NPLs improved significantly vs our expectations. The bank also made higher profits on the sale of assets than we had anticipated. However, the results were still disappointing to us. Net interest income were actually behind our forecasts. This is because NIMs collapsed another 34bps QoQ in 4Q22, following a 20bps drop QoQ in 3Q22. Funding costs surged nearly 80bps QoQ – with deposit costs alone up 72bps QoQ – as SECB does not have the same low-cost CASA base as the Big Three.
  • The bank will host an earnings call today, 02 March at 4pm Manila/HK/SG time.
  • Ahead of that, a few highlights:
    • Loan growth slowed a smidgen to 12.0% (from 12.4% in 3Q22, but more from the 15.0% in 2Q22). Corporate loans grew 10%, while consumer loans jumped 16% YoY, driven by mortgages (+21%), and by credit cards (+34%). Consumer loans made up 24% of total, up from 23% in 2021. Deposit costs grew 15.7% YoY, driven by a 30% YoY increase in TDs.
    • NIMs plunged 34bps QoQ, following an already precipitous 20bps QoQ decline in 3Q. The NIM squeeze was due to a 79bps spike in funding costs, with deposit costs up 72bps QoQ, driven by the aforementioned increase in high-cost TDs.
    • Net interest income disappointed, up just 7% YoY for FY22. NII actually dipped 4% QoQ in 4Q22.
    • Non-interest income took up the slack, driven largely by surprisingly strong gains from sale of assets, presumably foreclosures.
    • Opex was up just 8% YoY in FY22, tracking below our expectations. However, in 4Q22 alone, expenses jumped 9% QoQ and 15% YoY, as the bank ramped up its IT spending
    • Provision expense fell 46% to P2.8bn for the full-year, just over half of our forecast. The bank’s NPL ratio improved to 2.95%, down 29bps QoQ from 3.24% in 3Q, and nearly 100bps lower than the 3.94% at end-2021. NPL cover also improved to 101% from 94% in 3Q22.
  • Despite the beat, we are still quite wary, particularly given the surge in SECB’s funding costs. To be fair, for 2023, upside to loan yields could come from loan re-pricing, particularly from credit cards, given the recent raising of the credit card rate cap.

PROPERTY
Megaworld 2022 operating profit jumps 32%, ahead
  • Megaworld’s 2022 operating profit jumped 32% to P20.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts
    • This was largely thanks to strong residential GPM (+3.8ppt), which likely indicates that the proportion of residential revenue from lots increased
    • Residential and office revenue were also slightly ahead of our forecasts
  • Headline net profit was flat at P13.5bn (ahead of REGIS and consensus), mostly due to higher interest expense and forex losses on US$ debt
  • 2022 reservation sales grew 49% to P119bn (80% of the 2019 level), ahead of our estimates
  • 4Q22 reservation sales rose 30% YoY (-3% QoQ) to P33.4bn, equivalent to 90% of the 2019 average
  • 4Q22 mall revenue grew c. 50% QoQ (+55% YoY) to P1.2bn, 73% of the 4Q19 figure
    • Megaworld likely reduced rental concessions in 4Q22 but has not fully removed them
  • In the company’s press release, Megaworld Chief Strategy Officer Kevin Tan said he was optimistic of hitting “pre-pandemic numbers” this year
    • Megaworld’s 2019 net profit of P17.9bn is much higher than REGIS (P14.9bn) and consensus (P14.5bn) 2023E net profit forecasts
  • MEG is currently trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY23E P/B, and 4.1X FY23E P/E
  • The company will hold a briefing on March 6

INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide, partner invests in data center
  • Megawide signed a JV agreement with Singapore-based Evolution Data Centers  to design, build, and operate a 69MW data center in Cavite
    • Megawide will be owning 60% of the 4-hectare land area and it will also own 49% of the operating company
  • Phase 1 is estimated to cost P16.5bn (US$300mn)
    • Covers a 23-MW development that will be constructed over 30-48 months
    • The JV will hire a third party contractor
  • JV plans to fund 60% of the project cost from equity, suggesting MWIDE will need to shell out c. P5bn over a 5-year period
  • We estimate Megawide’s gross cash is currently at P15bn, after receiving proceeds from the sale of the Mactan airport
    • With gross debt (including prefs) at P33bn, we estimate its net debt to equity is at 2.2x
AIRLINES
Cebu Air adds five more aircraft orders for 2023
  • Cebu Air announced it will be leasing 5 additional aircraft in 2023, on top of 10 new Airbus NEO deliveries this year
    • With scheduled 15 aircraft exits, fleet size will be flat at 78 by end-2023 from the initial guidance of 73
    • We estimate CEB ended 2022 with a total fleet of 78 (up from 75 in 2019)
  • The company cited “growing passenger demand” as the reason for the new orders
    • Three of the five additional aircraft will be used to re-start the Clark base
    • Two other aircraft will  be used to support overall growth ambitions as CEB recovers fully from the pandemic
  • The company expects to return to profitability by 1Q23
    • CEB’s domestic market share has risen to 57% in FY22 from 51% in FY19, based on Civil Aeronautics Board’s data
    • CEB trades at 4.1x FY23E EV/EBITDA, well below historical average (7x) and peer average (11x)
