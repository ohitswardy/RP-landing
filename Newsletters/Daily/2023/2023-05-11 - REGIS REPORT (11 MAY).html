REGIS REPORT

11 May 2023

Today is probably “peak” day for results, with many of the big caps reporting their 1Q numbers.  Overall, the numbers are mixed with a slight positive bias.

 

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – 1Q23 profit of P17.3bn (+33%) appears to be tracking better than Street estimates for SM.  The two key listed subsidiaries churned out good numbers as did SM Retail.  The retail segment saw profits rising 51% to P3.9bn as revenues rebounded 22% from a pandemic-depressed base.  We note margins at SM Retail are higher today than they were before the pandemic.  More below.

 

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (=) – Headline profit came in at P10.2bn, 31% higher YoY and tracking within consensus expectations.  With the exception of Ayala Land (ALI PM), the three other core listed businesses delivered profits in line with expectation.  Losses at the other smaller units still persist and the outlook there is mixed.  More detail below.

 

  • Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) (+) – Bloomberry bested Regis and consensus estimates after posting a profit and EBITDA of P3.0bn and P5.7bn in 1Q23. All segments operated at full capacity in the quarter, with GGR jumping to P16.0bn (9%QoQ, 80%YoY). A strong VIP segment (volumes +13%QoQ, +103%YoY) led the quarter’s growth, lifting its GGR share to 40%. The result is well ahead of consensus and is likely to prompt upgrades to street forecasts.

 

  • DMCI (DMC PM) (-) – DMC’s 1Q23 profit dropped 30% YoY to P7.86bn, trending a tad behind our forecasts but appears in line with consensus . Earnings were softer across key segments, weighed down by (a) lower thermal coal prices, (b) weak revenue bookings at  construction and property, and (c) lower nickel shipments. The good news, however, is that management is confident about paying another special dividend in the 2H.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (+) – In a press release, Megaworld reported that 1Q23 net profit rose 33% to P4.1bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. The key highlight was that reservation sales jumped 71% YoY (+19% QoQ) to P39.6bn, 6% higher than the 2019 average, thanks to strong sales of Fort Bonifacio projects.  More below.

 

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (=) – Net profit for 1Q23 rose 6% to P1.5bn, which is line with our expectations despite some underperformance at the top line (+6%).  Gross margins were down 230bp but the company managed to keep EBITDA margins stable due to cost cuts (including on A&P).  While gross margins are likely to have bottomed, the price of skipjack tuna bears watching.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – 1Q headline profits were 11% lower at P2.1bn (P1.8bn after preferred shares), but we reckon the more important figures to focus on are the revenue and operating profit lines.  On those metrics, the results are actually better than expected. Operating leverage magnified the impact of revenue growth (+29%) on operating profit growth (+81%).  The underperformance at the net income line was due to “Other income/expense” and the tax rate.  JFC will hold a briefing on its results tomorrow.

 

 

+++

 

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla is hinting about a reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cut in lieu of a policy rate reduction.  The BSP has been compelled to raise rates in step with the US Federal Reserve to keep the PHP stable, even though the local inflation numbers arguably have little to do with domestic monetary policy.  A RRR cut would be welcome for the banks as would the maintenance of a high(er) rate environment.

CONGLOMERATES
SM Investments 1Q profits up 33%
  • 1Q23 net income P17.3bn.  SM Investments reported net income of P17.3bn for 1Q23, which is up 33% from a restated P13.0bn the previous year. The results appear on track to mildly beat Steet estimates for the year (P70bn).  Banking accounted for 47% of income, property 26%, retail 17% and portfolio investments 10%.

 

  • BDO and SM Prime perform.  As previously reported, BDO and SM Prime both posted good results.  BDO’s income grew 41% to P16.5bn lifted by expanding NIMs.  Profits at Chinabank were up only slightly (+3%) at P5.0bn.  Income at SM Prime, meanwhile grew 27% to P9.4bn, with the mall business doing particularly well.  Tenant sales are now 20% above what they were before the pandemic even if foot traffic is still 15% below.

 

  • Profits from retail up 51%.  Revenues at SM Retail grew 22% to P91bn, led by the recovery in its department store segment.  Profit growth was even higher, with profits rising 51% to P3.9bn.  Department stores delivered P0.7bn profits, from virtually nil in 1Q22.

 

We note that the Food retail business (i.e. groceries) saw revenue growth of 14% in the quarter, in line with Puregold’s (15%) and the supermarket segment of RRHI (+14%). Alfamart added 57 stores, pushing store count to 1,469.

 

  • Portfolio investments contributed 10%.  The group’s portfolio investments, which include PGPC, 2Go, Atlas, contributed 10% of earnings.  PGPC is the largest contributor, posting P1.1bn of profits almost certainly because of higher electricity prices.
CONGLOMERATES
Ayala Corp net profit P10.2bn, +31%
  • Net income for 1Q23 at P10.2bn. Ayala Corp reported net income of P10.2bn for 1Q23, +31% YoY.  Excluding what management considers non-core, income was P9.4bn, up 61% YoY. We’d consider the bottom line figure to be in line Street estimates (P37bn).

 

  • Most core units in line.  The results of most of the core businesses were largely in line with only Ayala Land (ALI PM) underperforming slightly.  BPI (BPI PM) profits rose 52% on loan growth and NIM expansion. Globe’s (GLO PM) core income was flat YoY, after stripping out divestment gains from both 1Q22 and 1Q23.  ACEN’s (ACEN PM) 1Q profits were up 5x, boosted by it finally being a net seller to the spot market as well as a gain recognized due to it being repaid for a loan. ALI’s earnings were up 42% but fell short on the residential development segment.

 

  • Losses at other businesses.  Losses at both AC Health and AC Logistic widened.  The positive news is that losses at AC Industrials narrowed as AC Motors turned a profit and lower losses at IMI. While things do appear to be improving at AC Industrials, the target to break even by the end of this year does not look easy to meet.

 

Among the three segments, we are most upbeat about AC Health.  AC Health is set to open its cancer hospital within the year and is aiming to achieve P1bn profit by 2025.

 

  • REIT for ACEN? Management floated the possibility of ACEN raising funding to achieve its 20GW capacity target via a REIT listing. Citicore REIT (CREIT PM) has paved the way for using the REIT structure for solar power operations.

 

  • Bid for Manila Airport.  AC is part of a seven-member consortium that submitted a P100bn proposal to rehabilitate the Manila airport.  Its foreign partner is expected to take a 30% stake while the six local business groups will split the rest equally.   The consortium is proposing to a 25-year BOT arrangement that will involve doubling the airport’s capacity.

JFC 1Q23 net profit to common (-11% YoY to P1.8bn) behind but operating income (+81% YoY to P3.6bn) ahead

  • JFC reported headline net profit of P2.1bn for 1Q23, down 11% YoY. The YoY profit decline was mainly due to (1) a P1.8bn one-off gain from land conveyance in 1Q22, and (2) a higher effective tax rate in 1Q23 vs the same period last year.
  • Excluding pref divs and coupons to perps, we estimate net profit to common was at P1.8bn, behind our FY23 estimates. The underperformance was mainly due to lower-than-expected “other income”.
  • However, operating income jumped 81% YoY to P3.6bn, tracking ahead of FY23 expectations. This was mostly driven by operating leverage, even as the company saw proportionately higher raw material cost as a % of revenues (+80bps YoY). Operating income from the international business turned positive (P447mn) from breakeven in 1Q22.
  • Total revenues rose +29% YoY to P55.1bn, trending ahead of estimates. Growth was led by the Philippines (+35% YoY), which accounted for 61% of revenues.
  • SSS in the Philippines grew 31.6%, though still coming from a low YoY base. Same-store transactions grew 17%, while average checks increased by 12%, with the latter primarily driven by price increases. Meanwhile, China SSS rebounded to +13%, now tracking in line with pre-pandemic levels by our estimate.

BLOOM profit of P3.0bn ahead of est on GRR of P16bn (80%YoY); VIP key driver as it swings back to 40% of rev

  • BLOOM posted a 1Q23 profit of P3.0bn, +338%YoY, ahead of Regis (P9.3bn) and well ahead of consensus (P6.9bn).
  • 1Q23 EBITDA hit P5.7bn, +100%YoY, also tracking ahead of Regis (P19.3bn) and well ahead of consensus (P17.1bn).
  • All segments operated at full capacity in the quarter. Total GGR hit P16.0bn (+80%YoY) and 8%QoQ from a seasonally strong 4Q.
    • The GGR figure is not yet higher than the P17.2bn record quarter registered in 3Q19 but is above the 2019 quarterly average (P15bn)       
    • GGR also appears on track to exceed the 2019 record of P60bn and Regis FY23E of P55bn.
  • Volumes for VIP and mass tables were up 36%QoQ and 10%QoQ, respectively.
    • Slot volumes saw a 15%QoQ decline, in line with the typical seasonality from 4Q to 1Q associated with more casual play (locals, casual tourists).
    • VIP revenue growth was even higher (36%QoQ, 142%YoY) due to a high win rate of 3.8%
    • Mass table win rate of 40.5% is consistent with win rate levels recorded in the past three quarters. 1Q22 win rate of 55% was unusually high.
