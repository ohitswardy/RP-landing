REGIS REPORT

13 April 2023

In continuing signs of strong demand recovery, car sales surged 19% MoM and 24% YoY to 36,880 units, just a shade below the levels seen in late 2019 (a little over 37,000). This takes total sales for 1Q23 to 97,284 units, up 30% YoY. The continued increase in sales comes despite fears of a general slowdown brought about by inflation pressures.

Meanwhile, Toyota sold 16,906 units in March, up 14% MoM and 12% YoY. For 1Q23, Toyota sales were 45,205, up 25% YoY. While this seems to show market share loss, we note that Toyota’s market share gain (to north of 50%) in 2022 was unusual, given competitors’ supply chain issues. More importantly, in our view, is that the 1Q23 figure is tracking well ahead of the company’s FY23 target of just 8% growth to 188,000 units. We believe this poses upside risk to our (and the Street) forecasts for GT Capital (GTCAP PM).

The jump was likely helped by banks’ re-focusing on consumer loans, after tightening up during the pandemic. As we saw in the banks’ consumer loan data, auto loan balances are no longer shrinking, as banks are now finally writing new loans faster than the old (pre-Covid) loans are maturing.

Speaking of banks – and recovery and inflation… Banking sector NPLs and Past Due (PD) ratios were up just 4bps and 3bps respectively MoM in February. This suggests that borrowers are not (yet) experiencing significant distress despite high inflation and interest rates. As we noted in our recent report Signal or noise? we continue to watch for signs of new NPL formation. Meanwhile, February loan growth re-accelerated to 11.1% YoY, up from the 10.8% recorded in the previous month.

Both PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) are asking the regulators to extend the 26 April deadline for SIM card registration. According to the Department of Information and Communication Technology (DICT), only 39.4% of all SIMs industry-wide have been registered as of 11 April. PLDT claims a significantly higher figure at 46%, while Globe’s is much lower at 32%. Both telcos argue that the slow progress is due to a lack of acceptable government-issued identification, as well as “challenged digital literacy”. Also, a not-insignificant number of users are still on basic, “feature phones”.

While we do expect the DICT to extend the deadline, we do consider the SIM registration an under-appreciated risk for telco revenues at least near-term. Once the (revised) deadline is passed, we could see a small number of users temporarily kicked off the network (ie not just the rationalization of users with multiple SIMs).

BANKS
NPL/PD ratios inch up in Feb; Loan growth accelerates, back over 11%
  • Asset quality data was mixed in Feb 2023, with NPL and PD ratios inching up, but with RL ratios down a bit. Banking system NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was up 4bps MoM to 3.42%, while Past Due (PD) ratio was up 3bps MoM to 4.04%. In nominal terms, NPLs and PDs were up 1.5% and 1.4% MoM respectively. Recall that January NPL and PD ratios jumped 13bps and 22bps MoM respectively in January, though we noted that this was in keeping with seasonal trends. The February milder MoM uptick was also in line with pre-Covid trends. NPL cover dipped to 105.0% compared to 106.3% in January. Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio inched down 3bps MoM to 2.58%.
  • Loan growth in February accelerated a tad to 11.1% YoY, compared to the 10.8% pace recorded in January. LDRs inched up to 71.7% from 71.5% the previous month.
  • As we noted in our recent report Signal or noise? we are watching the data closely for signs of significant deterioration given the rapid increase in interest rates. So far, the February data seems to show no material deviation from historical trends. Fingers crossed for March.

TELCOS
Telcos struggle to meet SIM registration deadline
  • Smart Communications, the wireless subsidiary of PLDT (TEL PM), said that it is necessary to extend the SIM registration period, which is currently only until April 26
    • To date, only 46% of its total number of subscribers have registered
    • Subscribers need more time to secure government IDs to register their SIMS
  • Globe Telecom (GLO PM) said that only 32% of its mobile subscriber base have registered so it is also seeking an extension
  • The telcos have submitted formal letters of request to the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) and The Department of Information and Communication Technology (DICT)
    • According to the DICT’s latest records on April 11, 66.22mn SIMs have been registered, representing 39.41% of the total number of subscribers in the country
  • DICT Assistant Secretary Jeffrey Ian Dy said that they will discuss the possible extension and the expansion of identification cards to include school IDs
  • The DICT, under Republic Act 11934 or the SIM Registration Act, has the prerogative to extend the SIM registration process for another 120 days
