REGIS REPORT

14 September 2023

The BSP came out with bizarrely conflicting July loan growth data this morning. On the one hand, it said that loans of the universal and commercial banks (UKBs) slowed to 7.7% YoY from 7.8% YoY in June. However, based on the data set for asset quality, which includes all banks – including thrift, rural, and digital banks – the growth rate accelerated to 9.5% YoY in July from 8.8% in June.

We suspect several things were happening here. First, the data is “preliminary”, which means that one or more banks missed the deadline for submission of data, and were not included in the total count. Second, the growth is likely much stronger in the thrift/rural bank segment. We note that in 2Q23, salary loans are up 50-60% YoY, and likely the vast majority of that would be in the smaller banks (or in the big banks’ small bank subsidiaries). Third, we have no hard data on the Digibank sector, which we presume have been growing rather quickly.

Meanwhile, NPL ratio for all banks was steady at 3.52%, flat MoM. Past Due rates picked up 3bps MoM, but Restructured Loans fell 9bps.

We would take the flat NPL and accelerating loans (for All Banks) to be a positive surprise, given fears over the weak GDP print and higher-for-longer interest rates.

BANKS
Asset quality stable in July; loan growth speeds up to 9.5% YoY
  • Preliminary BSP data shows the banking sector’s asset quality metrics were broadly flat MoM in July. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was steady at 3.52%, with the nominal Peso value up 0.7% MoM to P440bn. Past Due (PD) ratio edged up 3bps MoM to 4.12% from 4.09% in June, as absolute PDs rose 1.4% MoM to P528bn. Restructured Loan (RL) ratio improved, falling 9bps MoM to 2.37% as RLs contracted to P304bn, down 2.8% MoM. NPL cover inched up to 102.4% from 101.9% in June.
  • Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) accelerated to 9.5% YoY in July, from the 8.8% pace in June. Loans were up 0.7% MoM. LDRs blipped up to 72.8% (was 71.5% in June), the highest since end-2020, as deposits contracted 1.2% MoM.
  • The data points are a positive surprise, given concerns raised by the soft GDP print in 2Q23, and the fears of defaults due to high interest rates. We will be watching the ratios closely in August/September wary of the impact of rising CPI inflation.

OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in August
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 48bps MoM to 23.9%, while foreign ownership of the overall market rose 39bps MoM to 20.6% (in August)
  • Market net foreign flows reversed to an outflow of US$131mn in August (from US$332mn of inflow in July)
    • Trades on the last two trading days of the month are reflected in the foreign ownership data of the succeeding month. Thus, the net foreign outflow during the MSCI rebalancing on August 31 will be reflected in the September foreign ownership figures. 
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 20 saw increases in foreign ownership while 10 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • AC – foreign ownership rose 230bps MoM to 27.1%.
    • ALI – foreign ownership increased 225bps MoM to 27.7%. It also experienced the highest net foreign buying for the month (at US$38.4mn).
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership expanded 185bps MoM to 18.8%. It also saw the second highest net foreign buying for the month (at US$7.2mn).
    • BDO – foreign ownership grew 152bps MoM to 28.2%. Net foreign buying of BDO (at US$3.6mn) was the third-highest in the market.
    • ICT – foreign ownership increased 132bps MoM to 32.8% on net foreign buying of US$2.8mn.
    • MPI – saw the largest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 28bps MoM to 11.5%. It also experienced net foreign selling of US$18.2mn, the third highest for the month.
