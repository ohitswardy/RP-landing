REGIS REPORT

25 May 2023

Megawide’s (MWIDE PM) management held a briefing yesterday to discuss its FY22 and 1Q23 results, which, frankly, were quite poor. Its two assets, construction and landport, are still suffering from the lingering effects of the pandemic and the probability of full recovery in the near-to medium-term remains low. Management was also vague about the possibility of paying another special dividend, as (1) it has earmarked bulk of the proceeds from the sale for future expansions, including a US$300mn data center project, and (2) it needs to manage its interest-bearing liabilities, given that the construction unit is still facing difficulties collecting receivables.

+++

ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) is applying to issue as many as 25m preferred shares (P25bn @ P1,000/share) to raise funding for its expansion plans, which involve reaching 20GW of capacity by 2030. Prefs are typically not a cost-efficient way of raising funding (vs normal debt) due to the fact that interest on “regular” debt is tax deductible. However, the absence of a tax shield (taxable income) at the holding company level would negate that advantage.

The foreign ownership % of the market was largely flat MoM, based on official data. This reported MoM change was, however, marred by the rectification of an error that occurred the previous month on Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) reported foreign ownership.Without this correction, reported foreign ownership levels would have declined by ~20bp.

BANKS
When is a RRR cut not a RRR cut?
  • BSP Governor Felipe Medalla has repeatedly guided towards a 200bps reduction in banks’ regulatory reserve requirement (RRR) on PHP deposits from 12% currently to 10% by 2H2023. The consensus is that this would be incrementally positive for banks, since the BSP does not pay interest on RRR. However, there’s a strong argument that a 200bps RRR cut will be broadly neutral.
  • According to a news report, MSME loans that were used as “alternative compliance” for banks’ RRR stood at P263.1bn as of end-2022. Recall that this regulatory measure was put in place during Covid (in 2Q2020) to encourage banks to keep lending to MSMEs. With Covid ending, the regulation was scheduled to expire in December 2022, but was extended to end-June 2023.
  • As of March 2023, total deposits of the entire banking sector stood at P17.67trn. Of this, PHP deposits were P14.98trn. Cutting 200bps from the RRR would free up just under P300bn. However, if we assume that the 200bps cut will only apply to universal/commercial banks (UKBs), then the PHP deposit base was P14.02trn as of March, and a 200bps cut would release P280bn.
  • Thus, bringing the RRR for UKBs from 12% to 10% would barely offset the expiration of the MSME “alternative compliance”, and result in a net release only P17-37bn, or 0.1-0.25% of total deposits. Still positive, but significantly less so than even we estimated.
  • Clearly any further RRR cuts below 10% would be incrementally positive. The BSP is already dangling more cuts to entice banks to cut bank transfer fees (over InstaPay) for small transactions. But that’s a different conversation altogether.
INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide: Construction unit still soft; Landport occupancy rates improve
  • Core losses widen in 4Q22, 1Q23.  Megawide (MWIDE) held its briefing for FY22 and 1Q23 results yesterday.  Headline FY22 profit of P2.83bn included a one-time P6.6bn gain from the sale of the airport.  Removing the one-off and pref dividends, we estimate core losses widened to P3.50bn in FY22, of which P2.47bn was incurred in 4Q22 alone due to a P1.9bn impairment charge on construction receivables.  Meanwhile, 1Q23 results were also poor, as the company incurred a net loss of P117mn.  The construction and landport units are still suffering from the lingering effects of the pandemic and a turnaround remains tentative, particularly for the construction division.
  • Construction unit’s receivables still elevated.  Even though revenue bookings are now 12% ahead of pre-pandemic levels, GPMs plunged to a record low of 9% (vs. normal: 14%), which management blamed on timing differences.  More importantly, receivables are still climbing and are now at P18bn, implying receivable days of more than 1 year.  This prompted the company to borrow more short-term debt to fund the gap on working capital.  Abnormally high impairment charges have been quite frequent in recent years, and we believe this may not abate anytime soon. The order book dipped 7% YoY, 6% QoQ to P52.6bn in end-March 2023, of which 85% are still dominated by residential/office projects while 15% are infrastructure projects.     
  • Landport unit incurs losses but occupancy rates improving.   After declining over the course of 2022 due to the exit of POGO tenants, the landport incurred losses in both EBITDA and profit until 1Q23.  The good news, however, is that occupancy rates for the office towers have steadily improved since the beginning of 2023 to 47% in 1Q23 and 60% in 2Q23, after securing long-term contracts (2-5 years) with new tenants, which are BPOs and logistics players.  Management believes it can secure more tenants, given the connectivity the landport offers and the dormitory the company is providing. Lease rates, however, are 30-40% lower than pre-pandemic levels. 
  • Special dividends not sustainable?  After declaring cumulative dividends of P1/sh this year, which was equivalent to a payout ratio of 70% of FY22 headline profit, management was vague about the sustainability of the special dividend.  While the company could book another “one-off gain” once the second tranche of the sale is completed by March 2024, management noted that it has earmarked to use bulk (P7bn) of the P15bn gross sale proceeds for “future investments” (e.g. US$300mn data center).  We note that leverage is still high, with net debt (inclusive of prefs) currently at P22.9bn vs. an equity base of P10.1bn, and management does not have plans to pay this down aggressively, as it is still relying on improving collections to pare down debt over the next 12-24 months.

ACEN to sell up to 25mn non-voting preferred shares
  • In a disclosure to the exchange, ACEN Corp said that it filed a registration statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) with respect to a proposed public offer and sale of up to 25mn preferred shares (12.5mn with an oversubscription of up to 12.5mn) at an offer price of P1,000/share
    • To be offered as the first tranche under the company’s preferred shares program (50mn preferred shares with a par value of P1/share)
  • The preferred shares to be issued are non-voting
  • Last week, ACEN announced that its board of directors had approved a plan to borrow about P7.8bn from Mitsubishi UFJ and China Bank
  • “These are just ongoing financing initiatives that we have to support all our renewable energy expansion,” Eric Francia told reporters
  • ACEN trades at 27.8x PE and attributable EV/EBITDA of 13.4x for FY23E
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market remains flat in April
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of Philippine shares rose 6bps MoM to 20.1% while foreign ownership of the PSE Index increased 3bps MoM to 23.3% (in April)
  • Market net foreign flows reversed to an inflow of US$34mn in April (from US$498mn of outflow in March)
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 16 saw declines in foreign ownership while 13 experienced increases
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • AC – saw the largest increase in foreign ownership, which rose 6.01 percentage points MoM to 24.7%. This suggests that the 6.22 percentage point reduction in March was an error. 
    • BDO – foreign ownership rose 42 basis points MoM to 26.3%. It was the top foreign-bought stock in April (US$53mn of net foreign inflow).   
    • TEL – foreign ownership declined 36 basis points to 35.6%. Net foreign selling hit US$14.1mn, the second highest in April.
