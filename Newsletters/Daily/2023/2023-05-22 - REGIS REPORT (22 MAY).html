REGIS REPORT

22 May 2023

The government announced the extension of the CARS program by five years, allowing the two auto firms in the program more time to avail of the tax incentives.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) and Mitsubishi Motors Philippines are the two firms in the program and are required to manufacture (assemble) at least 200,000 vehicles of a specific model in order to receive the full tax incentives. So far, TMP has received P2.5bn worth of tax credits, and is awaiting another P1.7bn from the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). It will be eligible to receive another P2bn+ after it reaches the 200,000 goal.

TMP is 51% owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 20% of the latter’s NAV.

Digital payments continue to grow rapidly. According to BSP data, combined InstaPay and PesoNet transactions were up 30.4% YoY in the year-to-April to P3.8trn (about $68bn). Note that this data only captures transactions between counterparties, and does not include transfers within one institution (for example, BDO-to-BDO, or GCash-to-GCash, etc). Interestingly, the value going through InstaPay – for smaller transactions of up to P50,000 (under $900) – are still accelerating, up 39.2% in 4M2023, faster than the 30.8% clip in FY22. Meanwhile, for PesoNet – for larger transactions – the pace slowed to 25.5%, from 41.2%. This suggests that individual adoption of e-Payments continues to ratchet up.

The question is – how to monetize it? GCash is the clear leader in e-wallets. And among the banks, Union Bank and BPI seem to be taking the lead. But the BSP is pushing to drive fees down to zero (at lease for smaller transactions). Payments by itself may not be a profit driver for very long. The challenge will be who is able to sell products / services – loans, investment products, insurance, etc – to those digital customers.

MACRO
BoP deficit narrows to US$148mn in April
  • The country’s overall balance of payments (BoP) position posted a deficit of US$148mn in April
    • This is lower than the US$415mn deficit in April 2022 but is a reversal of the US$1.27bn BOP surplus in March 2023
    • BSP cited outflows arising from the national government’s payments of foreign currency debt obligations
  • Cumulative 4M23 BoP position registered a surplus of US$3.3bn, higher than the US$79mn surplus in the same period last year
CONSUMER
Gov’t extends CARS program
  • The Private Sector Advisory Council announced that President Marcos, Jr. approved the 5-year extension of the CARS program
    • Vehicles enrolled under the program are the Mirage model of Mitsubishi Motors Philippines Corp. (MMPC) and the Vios model of Toyota Motor Philippines Corp. (TMP)
    • Prior to the five-year extension, MMPC was given until this year to meet the required production levels while TMP had until 2024
  • Figures from the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) showed that combined production under the program stood at 207,165 units as of December 2022
    • TMP, which started production in July 2018, accounted for 134,242 units of its Vios
    • MMPC, which started production in February 2018, produced a total of 72,923 units of its Mirage model
    • Fiscal incentives of up to P9bn are given to car manufacturers that produce at least 200k units
  • To date, TMP has collected P2.5bn worth of tax incentives under the CARS program and is waiting for the BIR to issue tax certificates for another P1.7bn
    • Once the company sells 200k units, it could potentially received another P2bn of tax incentives
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital and accounts for c. 20% of NAV/profit
  • GTCAP is trading at a 51% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,035/sh
BANKS
Digital payments +30% in year-to-April
  • According to BSP data, the value of transactions going through PesoNet and Instapay grew another 30% YoY in the first four months of the year to P3.8trn. That’s slightly slower than the 37% growth posted for FY22, but that is to be expected as the technology slowly matures. For PesoNet – batched transactions for larger PHP amounts – the figure was P2,357bn, up 26% YoY. For InstaPay – transactions worth P50,000 or less – the value was P1,447bn, up 39% YoY.
  • For the month of April alone, PesoNet was up 16.4% YoY to P556bn, while InstaPay was up 41% YoY, taking the total to P933bn, up 25% YoY. We suspect the sharper slowdown in PesoNet transactions in April was due in part to the unusually large number of bank holidays that month.
  • The BSP maintains its goal of having 50% of all retail transactions done digitally by 2023. According to the latest data, digital transactions made up 30.3% of retail payments in 2021, up from 20.1% in 2020. No data has yet been published for 2022.
