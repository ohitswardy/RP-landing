REGIS REPORT

01 September 2023

President Marcos approved the imposition of price controls on rice in the whole country through Executive Order #39. 

Based on the official statement released by the Palace, the retail price ceiling for regular milled rice is P41.0/kg (US$730/MT) while the ceiling for well-milled rice is P45.0/kg (US$800/MT). On those prices, there is a high degree of probability that shortages will occur, in our view. We reckon such price controls are likely to be ineffective at best and disastrously counter-productive at worst.

The Philippines produces ~80% of its rice needs and has to import the rest. Thus, the landed cost of imported rice, being the “marginal supply”, will determine local prices for a properly functioning market. Price controls upset the proper function of the market. The latest FOB export price from Vietnam is US$560/MT. Factoring in the 35% import duty as well as shipping costs would push it above US$800/MT even before retailer margins. This will be above the retail price cap and would thus discourage imports, unless it is through illicit channels (i.e., smuggling).

Rice is 8.9% of the CPI basket and is a particularly important commodity as it provides more than 1/3rd of the daily calories of a typical Filipino adult. The Bangko Sentral (BSP) would be hard pressed to ease monetary policy if inflation is stubbornly high.

+++

Speaking of monetary policy, the domestic liquidity growth for the month of June was +5.9% YoY, down from +6.6% in May. The fact that the liquidity growth is below nominal GDP growth suggests tight monetary conditions.

+++

ICTSI (ICT PM) is borrowing US$750m from Metrobank (MBT PM) to partly pay down maturities and finance the upcoming Durban port investment. The cost of this loan is likely slightly above the current average debt cost of 5.5%. For Metrobank, this loan will immediately add 3% to its loan book, a welcome increment as it struggles to keep pace with the two other big banks.

+++

Meralco (MER PM) and Panay Energy Devt Corp (PEDC) were allowed by the regulator (ERC) to terminate their power supply agreement because the “change in circumstances” clause in their contract specifically allows such. This reduces losses for PEDC, which is ultimately owned by Meralco anyway (through Global Business Power). At the end of the day, the higher power prices will be borne by the consumer.

MACRO
Budget deficit narrows in July; YT-July deficit down 21% YoY to P600bn
  • The government reported a budget deficit of P47.8bn in July, down 45% YoY
  • Revenues jumped 33% YoY, largely due to timing changes in VAT payments
    • Customs collections fell 13% YoY likely due to base effects from high commodity prices and weather disruptions
  • Expenditures grew 16% YoY, accelerating from anemic growth (+0.4% YoY) in 1H23
    • Interest payments rose at a faster rate of +22% YoY (vs. 10% YoY in 1H)
    • Primary expenditures rebounded by 15% YoY after dipping 1% YoY in 1H23
  • YT-July deficit at P600bn, down 21% YoY and tracking slightly below the government’s target due to higher revenues

MACRO
Domestic liquidity growth slows to 5.9% in June
  • ​Preliminary data showed that domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 5.9% YoY to about P16.4tn in June 2023
    • This is slower than the +6.6% YoY growth rate in May and nominal GDP growth of 11.3% in 1H23

TRANSPORT
ICTSI borrows US$750mn from Metrobank
  • ICTSI is availing of a US$750mn term loan facility with Metrobank (MBT PM), with a tenor of 6 years
    • Proceeds will be used to fund short-term obligations and strategic M&A
  • ICTSI’s maturing obligations for 2H23 and 2024 are US$369mn (of which US$269mn was paid in July) and US$436mn, respectively
  • We estimate the term loan could have an interest rate of 6.2%, higher than the company’s pre-tax cost of debt of 5.9% as of 1H23
  • ICTSI’s net debt (treating perps, con liab, lease liab as debt) stood at US$4.66bn in end-June 2023, equivalent to a net debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 3.1x
  • ICTSI is trading at 8x/7x FY23E/FY24E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers (11x/9.8x)

CONSUMER
JFC hit with complaint from US National Labor Relations Board
  • JFC’s North American subsidiary has been charged by the US National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) for unfair labor practices
  • The complaint was made by 9 workers in one of Jollibee’s locations in New Jersey, who claim they were illegally fired after organizing a petition for higher wages and better working conditions
    • The complaint requires Jollibee to comply with the workers’ demands, including reinstatement of the 9 illegally terminated workers
  • JFC’s North America business (including Smashburger) accounted for 13% of systemwide sales as of 1H23
  • JFC currently trades at 25x 2023E PE
POWER
ERC grants Meralco-PEDC move to end supply deal
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission said it granted the join motion for contract termination filed by the companies on Jun 23, 2022, citing Panay Energy Development Corp (PEDC) PEDC’s inability to meet its contractual obligations because of the losses it incurred
  • Meralco and PEDC cited the “change in circumstance” provisions in their power supply agreement as the reason for termination
  • The companies argued that the significant increase in the global cost of coal or fuel prices for 2022 had led to PEDC suffering losses amounting to P962.23mn as of Sep 2022
  • The regulator said that Meralco and EPDC stipulated in their PSA that they had the option to terminate at the instance of a “change in circumstance”, as in this case
    • Such conditions specifically defined by “change in circumstance” provisions in their PSA were present in this instance

  • JV company called PT Puri Prakarsa Batam will serve as a platform for the development of RE projects in Indonesia, focusing on large-scale solar power plants, battery energy storage system and green hydrogren projects
  • ACEN’s partnership with the Puri Usaha Group was initially announced in May last year

BLOOM remains top candidate to replace MPI in PSE Index 
  • We estimate that BLOOM remains the most likely candidate to replace MPI in the PSE Index (should MPI be removed)
  • There is a 20% public float requirement to be part of the PSE Index
  • We forecast US$31.3mn of outflow from MPI and inflow of US$23.9mn to BLOOM
    • All other PSE Index constituents should see modest amounts of inflow
  • We forecast total flows of US$62.6mn in total flows (US$31.3mn in each direction)

If both AP and MPI will be removed from the PSE Index in September, then the stocks most likely to replace them are BLOOM and CNPF
