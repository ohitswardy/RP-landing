REGIS REPORT

27 February 2023

BDO (BDO PM) reported FY22 NPAT of P57.05bn, up 33% YoY. This was in line with REGIS forecast of P56.04bn, but well ahead (+8%) of Bloomberg consensus of P52.66bn. Loan growth slowed to 9% YoY from 10% in 3Q22. NIMs expanded 13bps QoQ, as loan yields increased 55bps QoQ, faster than the 35bps QoQ increase in deposit costs. Strong trading/fx gains powered non-interest income, while opex was up 9%, in line. NPL ratio improved to 1.95%, while NPL cover increased to 167%.

Meanwhile, BDO sharply increased its dividend policy from a quarterly payout of P0.30/sh to P0.75/sh, taking the annual dividend yield to close to 3%.

Overall a solid set of results.

+++

PMFTC, the tobacco affiliate of LT Group (LTG PM), announced chunky price increases across some of its key products effective today. The wholesale price of its flagship brand Marlboro was raised by P20 (+14%) to P155/pack while Fortune and Jackpot prices were also raised by P20 (+17%) to P126/pack. The company maintained the prices of Chesterfield and Crafted.

Note the tax increase that took effect in January was only P5/pack, so the price hike is well above the tax hike (at least for those brands that saw price increases). Despite the expected drop in volumes from the price increase, we believe the net effect of the price adjustment on PMFTC’s earnings will be net positive.

+++

Last Thursday, Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY22 NPAT of P32.8bn, bang in line with REGIS estimates, but slightly ahead (4%) of the Street. Net interest income came in slightly behind, compensated for by better trading, and miscellaneous income. Loan growth came in at 14.7% (vs our 15% fcast). However, NMs were up just 6bps QoQ. This was due to a surge in TDs, up 59% YoY and 26% QoQ (incl LTNCDs), while CASA was flat YoY (+1.2%). This saw MBT’s CASA ratio drop to 67%, from 71% in 3Q22. As a result, deposit costs spiked sharply, up 60bps QoQ to 1.1% in 4Q22. Interest expense doubled QoQ, and tripled YoY. While total earning asset yields were up 66bps QoQ, loan yields were up a much milder 24bps QoQ. This suggests that MBT’s NIM gains came primarily from higher yields on its Treasury book, likely as a result of the bank gapping out its maturities.

MBT will host an earnings briefing this afternoon at 4:30pm.

+++

Metro Pacific Hospital Holdings Inc. (MPHHI) is reportedly considering a plan to undertake an IPO to raise as much as US$1bn by 1H2024. Plans for an IPO are not surprising, given that MPHHI is (80%) majority-owned by private equity funds, led by US-based KKR. It is unclear whether Metro Pacific (MPI PM), which holds a 20% stake in MPHHI, will want to sell down further, given the company’s preference to diversify into more ESG-friendly ventures (e.g. agriculture, healthcare).

BANKS
MBT FY22 NPAT P32.8bn in line; but watch NIMs as depo costs surge in 4Q22
  • Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY22 NPAT of P32.8bn, bang in line with REGIS estimates, but slightly ahead (4%) of the Street. Loan growth came in at 14.7% (vs our 15% fcast), with deposits also up 15% YoY. However, NMs were up just 6bps QoQ. This was due to a surge in TDs, up 59% YoY and 26% QoQ (incl LTNCDs), while CASA was flat YoY (+1.2%). This saw MBT’s CASA ratio drop to 67%, from 71% in 3Q22. As a result, deposit costs spiked sharply to 1.1% in 4Q22, from 0.5% in 3Q22 and 0.28% in 4Q21. The deposit growth seems to have been used to fund the gapping out of the Treasury book. While total earning asset yields were up 66bps QoQ, loan yields were up a much milder 24bps QoQ.
  • More after the bank holds its briefing on Monday, 27 Feb at 4:30pm
  • Meanwhile, some highlights from the FS and press release:
    • Loan growth (net) accelerated to 14.7% YoY, from 13.3% in 2Q22. Gross loans were up 14%, with corporate loans up 15% and credit cards jumping 29% YoY. Deposits were up a similar 15% YoY, but this was driven by a jump in TDs/LTNCDs up 59% YoY and 26% QoQ. CASA ratio dropped to 67%, from 71% in 3Q22, and from 76% at end-2021.
    • NIMs were up 6bps QoQ to 3.50% in 4Q22. Due to the increase in TDs deposit costs spiked sharply to 1.10%, up 60bps QoQ and 82bps YoY. In 4Q22 alone, interest expense doubled QoQ, and trebled YoY. Asset yields were up 66bps QoQ, while loan yields were up 24bps QoQ. This suggests that the  majority of the NIM gains came from gapping out of the Treasury book.
    • Compared to end-2019 levels, loans were still about 4.5% lower, but the Treasury book was almost double that of pre-Covid levels.
    • Trading+forex gains were down 25% YoY. Trading gains were up 90% to P6.4bn, but forex swung from gains in 2021 to losses in 2022. In 4Q alone, though, trading+forex gains were up 53% YoY, as bond yields dropped from their peaks in late 3Q / early 4Q.
    • Fees and other incomes were up 12% YoY for the full year, though these flattened out in 4Q22 on both a YoY and QoQ basis.
    • Opex grew just 3% YoY for FY22. However, there was a jump in expenses in 4Q22 (+10% YoY). Notably, compensation expenses were up 15% YoY in 4Q22 alone, after being flat to slightly down for the previous three quarters.
    • Provision expense fell 31% YoY as asset quality seems to have stabilized. NPL ratio dipped to 1.9% (from 2.1% in 3Q22) while NPL Cover was maintained at 172%. However, there was a  jump in 4Q22 provisions (+29% QoQ, and 33% YoY), which suggests that some of the NPL improvement could have been due to write-offs.
  • While the results seem solid, we are mindful of the steepness of the rise in deposit / funding costs. This could continue to limit NIM accretion into 2023. On the other hand, it can be argued that deposit costs have made the full adjustment, while loan yields have yet to reflect rate hikes fully. We note, too, that MBT is a major beneficiary of the recent raising of the credit card rate cap that took effect today.

    • MPHHI accounts for 8% of MPI’s NAV
  • Metro Pacific Hospital has investments in 19 hospitals in the Philippines with a combined bed capacity of about 3,800, serving an estimated 3.6 million patients a year
    • The country has 1,289 hospitals and 110k beds nationwide; majority (55%) of which are privately-owned
