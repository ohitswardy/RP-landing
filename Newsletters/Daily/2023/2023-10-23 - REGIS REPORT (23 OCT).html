REGIS REPORT

23 October 2023

Vehicles sales as reported by CAMPI amounted to 38,628 for the month of Sept. While that figure is up “only” 8% YoY, the absolute number of units sold is actually respectable as it was actually up 5% MoM. With cumulative unit sales to Sept now at 314k+ (+27% YoY), the auto distributors are on track to meet their full-year forecast of 423k units (+20%).

Toyota sales for the month were roughly in line with the rest of CAMPI’s, with unit sales of 17k, also up 8% YoY and 5% MoM.

+++

The implementation of short selling in the PSE has been delayed by a couple of weeks to Nov 6 (from today, Oct 23). We understand the taxation issues relating to stock lending/borrowing, arguably the biggest hindrance to this initiative, have been sorted out. While we don’t expect that allowing short selling will have a significant impact, it is an incrementally positive step towards enhancing market activity and liquidity, which will hopefully improve investability.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) hosted an event to discuss its outlook on a key growth sector that it will be investing heavily in: electric vehicles (EV). The group recently announced it was taking over the distribution of BYD vehicles in the country.

Among the highlights were the group’s belief (hope) that EVs will account for 20% of new car sales in 5-years and its plans to put around 100 charging stations over the next year. Check out what else they had to say in the short summary below.

Key takeaways from Philippine EV Summit hosted by Ayala Motors

We attended Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) asset tour last Friday, October 20. A section of the tour was dedicated to AC Motors, who participated at the Philippine Electric Vehicle (EV) Summit. Mr. Jaime Alfonso Zobel de Ayala explained his team’s plans in the Philippine EV space, including strategies to address near-to medium term challenges. 

  • AC Motors’ plans. During the tour, AC Motors showcased its EV portfolio under two brands – BYD and Kia. The EV industry is still in a nascent stage in the Philippines, accounting for just c. 1% of new vehicle sales but AC Motors believes this could rise to as much as 20% in 5-7 years, as market adoption and infrastructure challenges are addressed. AC Motors is targeting to sell 1k-1.5k BYD vehicles on its first year and it plans to roll out over 100 public charging stations within Greater Metro Manila in the next year. To complete its offerings, AC Motors is also investing in an EV-dedicated app, which aims to geolocate all charging stations owned by AC Motors itself, as well as third parties, providing real-time information on usage.
  • Government supportive of EVs. The Philippine government has been supporting the use of electric vehicles. It passed Republic Act 11697, or the EV Industry Development Act (EVIDA) last April 2022, which provides tax incentives to EV importers (i.e. lower import duty rates) and non-fiscal incentives (e.g. exemptions from mandatory number coding schemes). President Marcos signed executive order 12 early this year, which temporarily scrapped tariffs on completely built-up e-vehicles for a period of five years. The Philippines also finally adopted the European EV plug standard, which is compatible with Chinese brands but will likely limit the importation of US-branded EVs (e.g. Tesla). 
  • EVs have large upfront costs but significantly low operating costs. From the lowest price point (e.g. BYD Dolphin) to the highest (e.g. Kia EV6), EVs appear to be much costlier than an Internal Combustion Engine Vehicle (ICE) at the date of purchase. The SRP for the most affordable EV, BYD Dolphin, is P1.4mn, which already includes an AC Type 1 charger. Meanwhile, similar ICE hatchbacks, such as the Toyota Wigo (PP630k-729k) or Honda City (P1.1mn), cost significantly less. A Toyota Vios also costs less at P696k-P1mn.

However, EVs are more competitive in terms of operating costs. A BYD Dolphin will likely cost c. P1k per full charging cycle while a “full tank” of a similar ICE will cost c. P3k at today’s prices. Given that EVs do not have engines/transmission parts to maintain, operating costs are also as much as 50% less than ICEs. 

  • Installation of AC/DC charging stations costly. Given that the cheapest charger, AC Type 1, could take >20 hours to charge, most EV buyers will likely want to install their own AC Type 2 chargers in their homes, as charging with the latter will only take c. 7 hours. While an AC Type 2 charger could cost c. P100k per unit, owners will have to also take into account the cost of retrofitting their electric system to be able to use the charger, which could cost another P200-400k. The large upfront cost could limit the adoption of full EVs in the medium term. 
  • Banks still hesitant to provide financing for EVs. BPI was the only bank that had a stall during the EV summit, as most banks are still hesitant to provide financing given the high residual value risk of EVs relative to ICEs. Philippine banks often provide up to five years for vehicle financing but given the constant changes in technology, most are uncertain if they could even provide these tenors to EVs. With 90% of vehicle acquisitions done using financing, we believe resolving this issue is an essential key to unlocking the adoption of EVs in the country.
OTHERS
Implementation of short selling delayed to November 6   
  • The PSE has moved the implementation date of short selling from October 23 to November 6
    • To give market participants more time to prepare
  • Only constituents of the PSE Index, the MidCap Index, and the Dividend Yield Index will be eligible for short selling
  • An eligible security must maintain short interest to outstanding shares at less than or equal to 10%
  • Naked shorting will not be allowed
  • Short selling of a stock can only be done on a price uptick
