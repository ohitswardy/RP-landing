REGIS REPORT

24 January 2023

The chairman of the House committee on ways and means, Rep Joey Salceda, is proposing new tax measures to raise revenue. This time around, the bill he filed proposes to impose taxes on more expenses distilled spirits and other beverages and possibly even “junk food”.

Expensive spirits are a tiny niche market and that segment will generate only a minute amount of revenue. Taxing “junk food” is likely to produce much more. The question is what will fall into that category. 

Roughly a quarter of Universal Robina’s (URC PM) consolidated revenue is derived from sweet and salty snacks, which include food items as diverse as chips (crisps), wafers, cookies / biscuits and chocolates / confectioneries. Meanwhile, Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) derives 20-25% of consolidated revenue from sweet and savory biscuits. The last time the government imposed taxes on non-essential food items was when a sweetened beverage tax was implemented in 2018. The tax led to price increases of ~20% and loss of sales volumes. Clearly, a new tax on these products could have a meaningful impact on demand both directly (by raising its prices/costs) and indirectly (by encouraging illicit trade).

Will such a measure pass Congress?  New tax measures are never easy to enact and are frequently watered down. It often takes the resources and full backing of the administration to get it past the finish line. While the Finance Secretary has expressed his desire to continue the tax reform agenda of the previous government, the administration has yet to visibly outline its revenue plans and crack the whip to get them passed.

The economic planning agency NEDA is compiling a long list of infrastructure projects that it is hoping to implement between now and 2028, when the term of the current administration expires. Apparently, a list of 3,600 projects worth US$372bn has been identified so far. How many of these end up in the list that the government plans to implement is the question.

The new administration has yet to come up with a list of big ticket projects (PPP or otherwise) that it will implement.

+++

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) is suggesting the removal of non-tariff barriers and the importation of more goods in order to ease inflationary pressures. Much of the recent spikes in inflation are traceable to shortages of food items that are heavily protected (sugar, onions, etc.), which could be easily addressed by more relaxed import rules. The Marcos government, however, appears decidedly protectionist with regard to its stance on agriculture, with the President himself expressing such views.

MACRO
Government sees 3,600 infra projects worth US$372bn
  • The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) have started compiling potential infrastructure projects from various agencies that could be implemented through 2028
  • So far, NEDA has identified 3,600 projects amounting to US$372bn in total
    • Of which, 180 projects will cost over US$45mn
    • 206 projects worth US$159bn will be implemented in the short term, of which 87 projects worth US$54bn have been identified for Public-Private partnerships
  • NEDA chief, Balisacan noted that the government will release the Public Investment Program (PIP) for 2023-2028 this quarter
    • This will include the three-year rolling infrastructure program for 2024-2026, which aims to inform the public on the budgeting process for key priority projects
  • The government plans to spend 5-6% of GDP on infrastructure through 2028
CONSUMER
House bill seeks higher taxes on distilled spirits, “junk food”
  • Albay Rep. Joey Salceda says he wants to update the Sin Tax law through a bill that will seek higher taxes on distilled spirits and other beverages
  • The bill will impose a “non-essential goods tax” on distilled spirits and other beverages priced above P20,000 (USD360) per bottle
  • They are also studying a “junk food tax”, as well as an update of the tax on sweetened beverages
  • Sweetened beverages are currently subject to an excise tax of P6/liter (using sugar) and P12/liter (using high fructose corn syrup)
    • Applies to sweetened juice drinks, sweetened tea, all carbonated beverages, flavored water, energy and sports drinks, other powdered drinks not classified as milk/juice/tea/coffee, cereal and grain beverages, and other non-alcoholic beverages that contain added sugar
  • URC - sweet and salty snacks account for ~25% of consolidated sales, while RTD tea accounts for ~12-15%
  • MONDE - sweet and savory biscuits account for ~25% of consolidated sales

Security Bank expects mid-teens loan growth
