REGIS REPORT

03 July 2023

Growth in lending by commercial banks moderated again to 9.4% in May, based on preliminary data from the Bangko Sentral (BSP).  This is below the 12% expectation we have baked into our estimates for the sector. Among the other items worth highlighting are the acceleration of the consumer loan book (+22.7% YoY), particularly the auto loan book.  Auto loans (+4.1%) turned positive a few months ago and rebounding nicely.

Meanwhile, the BSP also issued a report claiming that 42% of retail payments were done digitally last year (2022).  This suggests it is on track to meet the BSP’s goal of having 50% of all retail payments done digitally by this year (2023).

Jollibee (JFC PM) CFO Richard Shin says that the company is aiming for system-wide sales growth of 15-20% for the year as conditions normalize across its markets.  This is based on expectations of same-store sales growth of 7-10% across its portfolio, with international operations seeing an acceleration.

Some headlines are focusing on Shin’s statement that he expects earnings growth to slow in 2Q, but we believe this deceleration is to be expected given the bases in 1Q/2Q last year.  As the attached table below shows, JFC’s operating profit was up +81% in 1Q23.

Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM) issued a press release this morning to deny a news article saying that the group acquired controlling interest in Air Asia Philippines.  The purchase would have made the group the partner of the parent company of Air Asia (CAPITALA MK) for the latter’s Philippine operations.

Recall AEV acquired the Mactan Cebu International Airport from Megawide (MWIDE PM) a few months ago.  Having the Aboitiz group as a partner in Air Asia domestically would give the airline much needed financial and political backing, allowing it to become a more serious threat to Cebu Air (CEB PM).

BANKS
Commercial bank lending growth slows to 9.4% YoY in May
  • Preliminary data showed loans of universal/commercial banks (UKBs) ex-reverse repos in May rose 9.4% YoY to P10.91bn
    • Moderating further from +9.7% YoY in April
    • MoM, bank lending inched up +0.4%
  • Loans for production rose 7.9% YoY, slower than +8.3% YoY in April
    • Loans to manufacturing slowed sharply (+0.6% vs. +9.4% in April), as well as trade (+8.6%)
    • Loans to real estate sector grew 5.5% YoY, though it fell by 0.4% MoM
  • Meanwhile, consumer loans rose 22.7% YoY(+1.6% MoM), faster than the +22.3% YoY previous month
    • Credit card loans moderated slightly to 29% from 29.9% the previous month
    • Auto loans rose at a faster rate of +4.1% from +1.9% in April
  • Domestic liquidity (M3 growth) was steady at +6.6% in May (vs 6.6% in April)

 

 

MACRO
BSP sees fifth straight month of lower inflation
  • Inflation in the Philippines is expected to have continued its downward trend for the fifth consecutive month in June
    • Primarily driven by lower meat and fruit prices and cheaper LPG.
  • The BSP projects inflation to range between 5.3% and 6.1% in June, following a 12-month low of 6.1% in May.
    • Despite the decline, inflation averaged 7.5% from January to May, exceeding the BSP's target range of 2-4%.
    • Latest inflation projection midpoint is 5.7%, and the last time inflation was below 6% was in May 2022, at 5.4%.
  • Price pressures in June were driven by higher prices of rice, vegetables, fish, domestic oil, electricity rates, and the depreciation of the peso.
  • The BSP will continue to monitor inflation and economic growth, aligning its monetary policy formulation accordingly.
  • Despite the recent economic growth and downward inflation trend, the BSP maintained its policy rates on June 22.
  • The BSP expects inflation to average 5.4% this year and slightly increased its inflation forecast for 2024 to 2.9% (from 2.8%) due to the reopening of the economy and a more hawkish US Federal Reserve.
    • Inflation is projected to average 3.2% in 2025.
  • Outgoing BSP Governor Felipe Medalla believes inflation will ease within the target range of 2-4% by October or November, thanks to the tightening cycle by monetary authorities and non-monetary measures implemented by the government.
    • Expects inflation to drop below 2% by January 2024 due to high base effects 

 

MACRO
PH set to hit digital payments target
  • The Philippine payments system aims to convert 50% of retail payments to digital form by 2023.
  • Digital platforms accounted for 42% of transactions in 2022, up from 30% the previous year.
    • In terms of value, digital payments represented 40% of all transactions, down from 44% in 2021.
    • Total transactions were valued at $195 billion, with $78.17 billion conducted digitally.
    • Government payments were predominantly digital, making up 96% of all transactions.
    • Individuals and businesses made digital payments 55% and 10% of the time, respectively.
  • Merchant payments, person-to-person transfers, and salaries contributed to the growth of digital payments.
  •  Ownership of transaction accounts, especially e-money accounts, has significantly  increased, indicating increased usage of payments
    • According to the BSP Financial Inclusion Survey Report.

AIRLINES
AEV group denies takeover of Air Asia Philippines
  • The Aboitiz group issued a statement, denying a local news report (here), which stated that the group bought a 60% stake in Air Asia Philippines (AAP)
    • The article claimed that the Aboitiz group took control of AA Com Travel Philippines in June
    • AA Com Travel Philippines is the company which bought the 60% stake in Air Asia Philippines (AAP) in June from Rep. Michael Romero’s F&S Holdings
  • Aboitiz president and CEO, Sabin Aboitiz, reportedly talked to AirAsia CEO Tony Fernandez during his visit in the Philippines in June
    • The Aboitiz group committed to inject the cash that AirAsia Philippines requires in building up its fleet, which at present consists of 16 A320s
    • Capital A Bhd holds the other 40% stake in AAP
  • AAP is the third largest airline in the country, accounting for 18% of domestic pax volumes

PSE Index Aug 2023 rebalancing predictions: no additions/deletions
  • We expect no additions or deletions during the PSE Index rebalancing in August 2023
  • The PSEi rebalancing in August will be based on the 12 months from July 2022 to June 2023
  • Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap
  • Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when
    • 1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or
    • 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 
  • Based on our estimates, neither of the two criteria has been met
