We have another results-filled day with a number of key firms headlining the list.

  • Ayala Land (ALI PM) (-) – Ayala Land’s 1Q23 net profit rose 42% YoY to P4.5bn, trailing behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts, as commercial lot revenue fell 19% YoY. Meanwhile, reservation sales grew 15% YoY, led by the 61% jump in sales to foreigners. The rental segments also showed positive trends, with revenue and occupancy levels rising.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (=) – While 1Q23 net profit was seemingly in line, operating profit during the quarter (+15% YoY) is trending mildly ahead of FY23 estimates due to the continued strong performance of the sugar business (EBIT +39% YoY). Revenues from the core branded consumer food (BCF) segments were broadly within expectations (+8% YoY) even as the domestic BCF was temporarily soft (+5% YoY). Notably, margins have started to recover on a sequential basis, with BCF EBIT margins expanding c200bps QoQ, reflecting the impact of the price increases over the past year. Margins should continue to recover in the coming quarters as inventory costs continue to ease.

BANKS
MBT 1Q23 NPAT at P10.5bn, up 31% YoY; ahead on trading/fx gains; deposit costs surge again

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 1Q23 NPAT of P10.5bn, up 31.3% YoY. The figure is ahead of REGIS (P38.6bn) and consensus (P38.0bn) forecasts for the full year, due largely to trading/fx gains of P2.1bn in 1Q22. Loan growth (gross) slowed to 12.5% YoY (from 13.9% at end-22), dragged by corporate loans, while consumer loans accelerated. NIMs were up 11bps QoQ, despite a continued surge in deposit costs. Provisions surprisingly still rose 17% YoY, despite a drop in NPLs and a jump in cover, suggesting some write-offs.

Some highlights:

  • Loan growth slowed to 12.5% YoY (was 14% YoY in 4Q22), as corporate loans decelerated to 12.7% (from 15%). Consumer loans sped up to 11.8% YoY (up from c9% YoY at year-end), driven by credit cards (+30% YoY). Auto loans accelerated to 10.7%, continuing its recover from +3.6% in FY22, and -23% in FY21. Deposit growth slowed to 11% YoY, from 15% in 4Q. However, growth continued to be driven by TDs, which were up 43% YoY and 16% QoQ. As a result, CASA ratio dropped to 62% from 67% in 4Q, and from 71% in 1Q22.
  • NII jumped 29%, and 6% QoQ, as NIMs expanded 11bps QoQ. Margins were driven by an astounding 95bps QoQ increase in loan yields. However, we think there are some internal accounting issues here, as overall asset yields were up a more modest 43bps QoQ. Meanwhile, deposit costs spiked another 31bps QoQ due to the drop in CASA, taking total funding costs up 36bps QoQ. The surging funding costs are a bit concerning, with interest expense quadrupling YoY, and up 30% QoQ. We note, though, that loan yields still have a bit of upside, with the raising of credit card rates in February.
  • Trading/fx gains were strong, at P2.1bn in 1Q23, already accounting for 40% of our full-year forecasts. Fee incomes were up 13% YoY, but miscellaneous income was down 20%.
  • Opex increased 14% YoY. The press release explained that this was due to the taxes/licenses line (as TDs are levied a 0.75% stamp duty, absorbed by the banks), and IT spend.
  • Provision expense was up 17% YoY to P2.4bn. This came despite improving asset quality, as NPL ratio declined to 1.8% (from 1.9% at end-2022) and NPL cover jumped to 189% from 172%. We suspect that this was partly due to write-offs of NPLs, likely in the credit card portfolio.

The bank will hold a briefing on today at 4pm.

TELCOS
Globe 1Q23 core profits unchanged YoY
  • Globe core income flattish at P5.1bn. Headline income for Globe in 1Q23 was P7.3bn, down by 47% with one-offs lifting profits in both periods. Stripping those out, Globe core income came in at P5.14b, virtually unchanged YoY. We would consider this as in line with expectations. Service revenues were up a modest 2% with mobile revenue up only 1%.
  • Mobile revenue share gains; home broadband yet to bounce. Mobile revenue inched up 1% YoY and QoQ. While this is not what we’d consider a strong performance, it still managed to gain ~0.8pt revenue market share. Home broadband is still in decline with fixed wireless subscribers dropping 16% to 1.2m in a quarter. Wired broadband subs, on the other hand, dipped by 12k to 1.12m. Corporate data is the only segment growing by double digits YoY (+15%) but even that looks like it’s tapering off.
  • Mynt profits up. Mynt contributed net income of P405m in 1Q23. This is 8% of Globe’s core income and 23% higher YoY.
  • Half of SIMs registered. Out of 86.7m SIMs for Globe as of Dec 2022, a total of 43m were registered as of May 2, roughly half. PLDT registered approximately 2/3rds as of May 3.
