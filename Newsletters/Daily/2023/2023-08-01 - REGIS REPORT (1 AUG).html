REGIS REPORT

01 August 2023

Two of the big caps (by Philippine standards) released their results yesterday: BDO (BDO PM) and Meralco (MER PM). The bottom-line figures for both are unquestionably strong. There are, however, indicators for the big picture (i.e., macro) that are not quite as strong. In the case of Meralco, industrial power usage is still down YoY while BDO’s printing loan growth (8%) that’s below nominal GDP growth.

  • BDO (BDO PM) (=) – BDO posted solid results, with 1H23 NPAT of P35.2bn, up 47% YoY. While the 1H was in line at 50% of our FYE of P70.5bn, the sequential quarterly trend points to potential upside risk to our estimates. Profit for 2Q23 alone was P18.7bn up 13% QoQ, and an all-time high. NIMs continued to surge in 2Q, up 70bps YoY and 22bps QoQ, driven by a jump in consumer loans (+15% YoY, vs total loans +8%), and the full-quarter impact of the higher credit card rate cap. Asset quality improved, too, most notably in the sharp drop in Restructured Loan ratio to 2.8%, from 3.3% in 1Q. During the bank’s briefing, management reiterated their view that ROEs can be sustained in the mid-teens level. Our numbers don’t quite show this yet. But if the 2Q23 trends hold, we’d have to rethink our forecasts, which are already above consensus.  More below.
  • Meralco (MER PM) (+) – The power firm reported core income of P19.2bn for 1H23, up +47%, which would put it well on track to beat full-year estimates of both Regis and the Street. The source of the outperformance, again, was the generation business, with Singapore genco PacificLight contributing the lion’s share of profit. The core distribution is still lagging marginally, with 1H volumes up +3% and 2Q up by 4.7%. Notably, industrial electricity volumes are still lower YoY. Click the link for more.

We also have Union Bank (UBP PM), which we don’t officially cover. UBP reported 1H23 NPAT of P6.4bn, up 6% YoY, tracking well behind consensus estimates (P15.3bn for FY23E) with 2Q23 alone at P3.0bn, down 12% YoY, dragged by the cost of integrating the Citi assets that the bank purchased in 2H21 (closed in July 2022). The acquisition of Citi’s high-yield credit card business caused NII to surge 41% YoY and drove a more than doubling of fee incomes. However, opex also ballooned 82% YoY. UBP reckons P2.4bn worth of opex was due to integration costs in 1H23. NPLs increased to 5.0% from 4.8% in 1Q, driven by their consumer book at 6.3% (from 6.1% in 1Q).

BDO 1H23 P35.2bn, in line; strong quarterly momentum

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported headline NPAT of P35.2bn, up 47% YoY, broadly in line with Regis estimates (FYE P70.5bn), and ahead of consensus (P69.0bn). However, the quarterly momentum seems strong, with 2Q23 at a record-high P18.7bn, up 53% YoY, and up13% QoQ pointing to potential upside risk to our forecasts.

  • Customer loan growth was steady at 8% YoY, while deposit growth slowed to 11.5% YoY from 14.3% in 1Q23. CASA ratio dipped slightly to 75% from 76% in 1Q23 (was 85% in 2Q22).
  • NIMs jumped another 22bps QoQ (+70bps YoY) to 4.69%, driven by a 64bp QoQ surge in loan yields. Deposit costs were up a much milder 26bps QoQ. This took NII up 29% in 1H23, with 2Q23 alone up 30% YoY and 6% QoQ.
  • Non-interest income was up 11% in 1H23. Fee incomes grew 16% YoY, while trading/fx gains surged 40% YoY.
  • Opex was up 17% YoY, driven by the Taxes/licenses line (+52% YoY), as time deposits (TDs) are levied documentary stamp taxes (DST, or stamp duty). TDs were up 86% YoY.
  • PPOP was up 34% YoY, bang in line with our forecast.
  • Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio at 1.95% (from 1.98% in 1Q) and NPL cover at 174% (from 170%). Notably, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio fell to 2.8%, from 3.3% in 1Q, and from a peak of 5.7% in 2Q21. Credit cost is normalizing at 53bps in 2Q23, with provision expense down 17% YoY (in line)

Overall a solid set of results.

  • Its foreign ownership will increase from 33.6% to 44.1%

  • As of 1Q23, RCBC’s book value was P116bn, inclusive of P14.5bn in hybrid perpetual securities
  • 1Q23 BVPS (excluding hybrid perpetual securities) was P50/share
    • The sale of shares to SMBC will increase BVPS to P53.30/share
