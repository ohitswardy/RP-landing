We have a long list to cover this morning following the deluge of 2Q/1H results released yesterday. The results are truly a mixed bag, with half doing better than expected and the other half falling below expectations.

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – Profits for 1H jumped 32% to P36.5bn, with 2Q income at P19.2bn (+31%). Operating results across the key segments (banking, property, and retail) were all solid. On the retail side, we note that only department store revenue is still below pre-Covid levels. Management also noted that work on its Pasay reclamation is continuing, after the government announced that all but one of the Manila Bay reclamation projects have been suspended.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – Underlying operations are healthy, with 1H23 EBIT +51% to P7.6bn, tracking above Regis and consensus expectations. 2Q revenues +17% with SSSG (+9%) led by China (+35%) and the Phils (+11%). Operating leverage drove EBIT +31% YoY despite elevated inventory cost – a result of the cost saving initiatives done during the pandemic years. We expect to get further detail during tomorrow’s briefing. (Registration link)
  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) (-) – Both segments saw a challenging 2Q due to the effects of rising prices (on demand and on margins) as well as competitive pressure – APAC BFB sales rose 5% during the quarter, but volumes for the core instant noodles were down 9% YoY. While this was partly attributed to a high YoY base (e.g. election spending), MONDE also ceded some market share to URC, with the latter recovering from a relatively soft 1Q. Meanwhile, segment margins were relatively stable and should improve further as the company has fully depleted its high-cost inventory for key commodities (wheat, palm oil).

Just in this morning: the statistics office announced that 2Q23 GDP growth was 4.3%, well below what the Street was forecasting (6.0%). This is a marked slowdown from the 1Q GDP growth of 6.4%.

Consumption grew 5.5%, underscoring the fact that it has been the economy’s primary engine since …. well, as far as we can remember. But the economy can’t run on one engine alone and the others were sputtering. Government spending for the quarter dropped 7.1% while growth in capital formation was nil (0%). 

While Philippine GDP is not usually viewed (by convention) on a QoQ basis, one might want to take note that the GDP for 2Q23 contracted 0.9% vs 1Q23 on a seasonally adjusted basis.

CONSUMER
JFC 1H23 recurring NPAT (+2.4x to P3.7bn) behind but operating profit (+51% to P7.6bn) tending above expectations
  • JFC reported 2Q23 headline net profit of P2.3bn, down 17% YoY mainly due to a P2.9bn one-off gain recognized in the same period last year. This brings headline 1H23 NPAT to P4.4bn, down 14% YoY. Excluding one-offs and after deducting perp coupons and pref divs, we estimate 2Q23 NPAT jumped 41% YoY to P2bn, bringing the 1H23 figure to P3.7bn (+2.4x YoY). While this appears to be tracking below expectations, underlying operations appear ahead with 1H revs +22% to P116bn and EBIT +51% to P7.6bn.

  • 2Q23 revenues rose 17% YoY on the back of 9% SSSG. SSSG was led by China (+35%), which continued to recover post-lockdown. We estimate same-store sales in China have reached pre-COVID levels. Meanwhile, the Philippines continued to see strong SSSG (+11%), driven by Chowking (+13%) and Mang Inasal (+20%). Smashburger posted SSSG of 8%, while CBTLwas flat.
  • Operating profit jumped 31% YoY during the quarter, driven by a 70-bp YoY margin improvement. Gross margin was flattish even as cost of inventories remain slightly elevated. This was mitigated by operating leverage as store and manufacturing cost were controlled. Proportionately lower G&A expenses – a result of the cost-saving initiatives done during the pandemic years – helped lift operating margins. By geography, while the Philippine business led profitability, margins were boosted by a turn in the international business.

  • JFC currently trades at FY24E multiples of 23x PE and 11x EV/EBITDA

DMCI – Construction, mining units weigh on 2Q23 performance
  • DMCI’s 1H23 recurring profit (ex-forex adj, one-offs) fell 19% YoY to P16.03bn, tracking slightly behind Regis’ FY23E but appears in line with consensus. In 2Q23 alone, profit was lower by 5% YoY at P8.17bn, pulled down by the poor performance of the construction and coal/nickel mining units. With the exception of the power units, slow demand recovery for each business unit was quite apparent. DMCI is trading at a 16% discount to its market-based NAV, broadly in line with historical average (15%). 
  • Property cancellations moderate. Profit contributions from DMCI Homes jumped 26% QoQ as cancellations moderated to just 14% of revenues from 24%. However, this was higher than last year’s 7% rate. Reservation sales declined by 6% YoY in 2Q23, taking 1H23 reservation sales up 19% YoY to 90% of pre-Covid levels. Though this was largely due to higher ASPs, as unit sales alone were just at c. 70% of pre-Covid levels. Cancellations relative to sold/reserved units also eased, with 1H23 down to just 12% from 19% last year. 
  • Maynilad: anemic billed volumes. Standalone profit from Maynilad jumped 35% YoY to P2.2bn and EBITDA rose 14% YoY, buoyed largely by the tariff hike in January. Billed volumes remained anemic, rising by just +1% YoY to 99% of 2Q19 levels, as the recovery of the industrial segment remained slow.  The 17-Q did not provide data on capex.
  • Project delays hit construction unit.  The construction unit did poorly in 2Q, as revenues fell 21% YoY/7% QoQ. Management attributed this to delays in key infrastructure projects, as well as the deficiency of projects. The order book dropped by 12% YoY and 9% QoQ to P38bn due to the lack of progress in project awarding and rescoping of the NSCR Package 2.
  • Lower nickel prices offset higher shipments.  The nickel unit’s 2Q23 profit collapsed 51% YoY/47% QoQ due to lower ASPs and higher non-cash costs. Shipments rebounded by 33% YoY after securing new permits in January.
  • Strong performance for off-grid power unit. Energy sales rose 11% YoY and lifted earnings for its off-grid unit by 13% YoY in 2Q23.  Apart from higher demand, the company also increased its market share in Palawan.

  • FGEN’s recurring income for 1H23 hit $168mn (31%YoY), tracking ahead of estimates, on the back of a sharp rise in contribution from EDC
  • This implies 2Q23 recurring income was $79mn (12%QoQ, 14%YoY)
  • Nat gas earnings dipped slightly in 2Q to an estimated $45mn (-6%QoQ, -9%YoY), coming from 1Q23 that benefitted from high dispatch levels and full availability
    • Management mentioned that some plants encountered some outages in 2Q23   
  • EDC’s attributable income remained elevated vs the low base last year – (up 4%QoQ, 92%YoY to $38mn in 2Q23)
    • Compared to last year, EDC wasn’t buying as much replacement power
    • It continued to benefit from higher power prices both in the spot market and WESM
  • We estimate hydro incurred a loss of about $1.7mn in 2Q23 on lower water levels (seasonal plus some early El Nino impact)
  • Lease of LNG to Razon group is still being negotiated
    • FSRU is on its way to Subic to receive its first shipment of LNG and is soon entering wet commissioning phase
    • Commercial operations should start soon
  • FGEN trades at 4.9x PE and 3.5x EV/EBITDA on our FY23E estimates with a div yield of 3.7%

ACEN to develop 1,000MW floating solar project in Laguna Lake
  • ACEN Corp said it is set to develop a large-scale floating solar project in Laguna Lake with an expected capacity of 1,000MW
  • According to ACEN CEO Eric Francia, the company was awarded 800 hectares
  • Work is currently in the pre-development phase
  • In 2022, Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA) offered 2,000 hectares of Laguna de Bay for floating solar projects for a 25-year contract
  • ACEN subsidiaries signed an RE contract area utilization agreement with LLDA for the lease of 800 hectares of renewable energy areas
  • The company is targeting to expand its RE capacity to 20GW by 2030 from the current 4,200MW attributable capacity
  • ACEN trades at 16.8x PE and attributable EV/EBITDA of 10x for FY24E
