REGIS REPORT

13 December 2023

Some good and bad news for banks this morning. The bad news is that asset quality deteriorated a bit in October. System-wide NPL ratio edged up 4bps MoM to 3.52%, while the Past Due (PD) ratio was up 5bps MoM to 4.26%. The ratios were not helped by a slight decline in loans (-0.1% MoM). On a YoY basis, loan growth slowed a smidge to 8.3% YoY (from 8.4% in September).

The good news is that it could be partly due to the loan mix, given the rapid growth of consumer loans, which were up 16.5% in 3Q23, from 13.3% in the previous quarter. This is also the fastest pace since just before the Covid pandemic. Mortgages rebounded, up 6% YoY (from flat in 2Q); Motor Vehicle loans shot up to 12.6% YoY (from 7.0%), and credit card growth was steady at 30.4% YoY. Even better, consumer loan NPL ratios improved to 6.01%, compared to 6.25% in 2Q23, with notable drops in mortgage (down 52bps QoQ to 7.10%) and motor vehicles (down 55bps to 6.96%).

We suspect that the overall NPL ratio increase was at least partly driven by the loan mix. That is, even if consumer loan NPL ratios are falling, they are still nearly double the average NPL of the total loan book. And the increased weight of consumer loans – now 21% of total loans, an all-time high – could have tipped the scales.

Our forecasts already impute higher NPLs and credit costs for 2024/25E. Despite this, we expect ROEs to remain elevated, and maintain our Buy call on the sector.

It appears that SP New Energy (SPNEC PM) will buy out Prime Infrastructure following its “shootout” with the latter over its joint venture.  SPNEC will pay P6bn to take full control of what would have been a 50-50 solar joint venture (“Terra Solar”) to provide Meralco (MER PM) with 850MW of power.

Terra Solar is building what is to be the world’s largest solar farm (on a single contiguous site).  The capacity of the farm will be 3,500MW upon completion and it will be complemented by 4,000MWh battery storage facilities.

Meralco has signed a deal to take control over SPNEC through the subscription of new common and preferred shares.  Prime Infrastructure is the infrastructure investment firm of Enrique Razon.

BANKS
Consumer loans jump 16.5% YoY in 3Q23, NPLs fall
  • Consumer loans grew 16.5% YoY in 3Q23, rebounding from the slower 13.6% pace in 2Q23. On a QoQ basis, consumer loans jumped 6.8% from 2Q23’s levels. The re-acceleration was due largely to mortgages, which grew 6.0% YoY and 7.4% QoQ. (We suspect there were some data issues in 2Q, as mortgages were down 3% QoQ, the largest quarterly drop on our records, since 2001.) Motor vehicle (MV) loans jumped 12.6% YoY, the fastest pace since mid-2018, with 4-wheel loans up 14.3%, dragged by motorcycle loans which were down 7% YoY. Credit card growth was steady at 30.4% YoY. Salary loans, meanwhile, moderated to 29.7% YoY from the heady 40-60% pace in the previous four quarters.
  • The continued growth in the consumer loans is remarkable, in our view, considering the impact of inflation on household incomes, and the sharply higher interest rates particularly for mortgages.
  • Consumer loan growth far outstripped overall loan growth of just 6.8%. We estimate that ex-consumer, loans were up just 4.5% YoY. As of 3Q23, consumer loans accounted for 21.0% of total loans, a record high
  • Asset quality was mixed, though overall consumer loan NPLs improved to 6.01% from 6.25% in 2Q23. NPL ratios improved sharply for mortgages and for auto loans, while credit card delinquencies deteriorated. Mortgage NPL ratio fell 52bps QoQ and 82bps YoY to 7.10%. Meanwhile, MV NPLs stood at 6.96%, down 55bps QoQ and 235bps YoY; NPLs for 4-wheelers dropped 61bps QoQ while motorcycle NPLs jumped 87bps QoQ. Credit card NPL ratio increased to 4.10%, up 31bps QoQ from 3.79% the previous quarter.
  • Accelerating consumer loan growth and falling NPLs are a positive sign for the banking sector, given the higher margins of the segment. Continued momentum could help preserve NIMs in a falling interest rate environment in 2024.  

 

BANKS
Asset quality slips a smidge in October
  • Asset quality metrics slipped a tad in October, reversing a slight improvement in September. However, this was partly due to a slight contraction in loans (-0.1% MoM). Sector-wide NPL ratio (ex-interbank) edged up 4bps MoM to 3.52%, as the nominal value increased 1.2% MoM to P449.1bn. Past Due (PD) ratio also rose 5bps MoM to 4.26%, with the PHP value up 1.3% MoM to P557.1bn. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio crept up 2bps MoM to 2.37% with a slower 0.6% MoM increase in value to P309.2bn. NPL Cover also deteriorated a smidgen, down just under 110bps MoM to 102.6% from 103.7% the month prior.
  • Meanwhile, loan growth slowed a again to 8.3% YoY (from 8.4% in September), and down 0.1% MoM as mentioned.
  • Quick Caveat – the data is still “preliminary” which usually means one or more banks failed to submit data to the BSP on time. This will likely mean a revision upwards for loan growth. The impact on asset quality ratios is uncertain.
  • As we recently emailed, consumer loans continued to grow rapidly into September (+16.5%). The deterioration in the overall sector NPLs could be due to higher NPL ratios in the consumer space, while the drop in total loans likely came from the corporate/commercial segments. In short, it might be a loan mix issue.
  • Asset quality remains among the key risks to our positive outlook for the banking sector.

 

MACRO
Trade deficit widens to US$4.18bn in October
  • Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit widened by 26% YoY to US$4.18bn in October
    • On a MoM basis, the trade deficit was also higher by 17% compared to September’s deficit of US$3.58bn
  • Imports fell by 4% YoY to US$10.54bn, slowest monthly decline in 7 months
    • Fuel and raw material imports fell by 5% and 7%, respectively, due to lower commodity prices
    • Capital goods imports also fell by 5% but consumer goods imports rose by 5%
  • Exports collapsed by 17% YoY to US$6.36bn due largely to base effects (Exports peaked at US$7.71bn in Oct 2022)
    • However, Oct exports appear to be recovering relative to the monthly average in 9M23
  • Brings YT-October trade deficit to US$44.07bn, down 12% YoY

 

POWER
SPNEC buys out Prime Infra’s stake in Terra Solar
  • SP New Energy Corp (SPNEC PM) disclosed that its board approved the acquisition of Prime Infrastructure’s stake in Terra Solar Philippines, Inc. (TSPI) for P6bn
    • The acquisition will result in SPNEC becoming the beneficial owner of 100% of TSPI
  • In October, Meralco subsidiary (MER PM) MGEN Renewable Energy announced that it will be investing P15.9bn in SPNEC through common and preferred shares
    • The investment will give MER a 50.5% voting interest in SPNEC
  • Terra Solar has an agreement with MER to supply 850MW for a fixed rate for 20 years
    • 600MW by February 2026
    • 250MW by February 2027
  • Prime Infra previously invested in SPNEC affiliates Solar Philippines Tarlac (P1.5bn for 50%) and Solar Philippines Tanauan (P2bn for 50%)
    • Solar Philippines Tarlac – 100MW operating, 100MW ongoing expansion
    • Solar Philippines Tanauan – 140MW under construction
    • In February 2023, Prime Infra and then SPNEC controlling shareholder Solar Philippines agreed to amend ownership such that Prime Infra would own 100% of Solar Tanauan while Solar Philippines would own 100% of Solar Tarlac
