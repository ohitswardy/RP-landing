REGIS REPORT

11 August 2023

Yesterday’s weak GDP growth print of 4.3% YoY in 2Q23 was a bit of a shock, to say the least, even for Regis with our bottom-of-the-Street FY2023 estimate of 5.5%. To be fair (to us), we were watching consumption trends much more closely, and the normalization of Personal Consumption Expenditure (PCE) to 5.5% YoY (from 6.4% in 1Q23) was broadly in line with – if maybe a tad weaker than – our expectations. We were blindsided by the sharp drop in Government expenditures (-7.1% YoY), and the flat (0.0%) performance of Investments. That said, the GDP report contained a number of head-scratching data, similar to the 2Q22 print last year. For example, PCE was down 3.0% QoQ (seasonally adjusted -1.0% QoQ), which doesn’t quite jibe with our high-frequency data (car sales, consumer loans, company results, etc), or with historical trends (pre-Covid, 2Q was typically up 3-5% vs 1Q)

The country’s economic planners continue to hold on to its 6-7% growth target for the year, which now looks optimistic at best. Can government spending rebound in 2Q23? Finance Secretary Diokno recently told government agencies to “come up with (a) catch-up plan”, given the sluggish spending in 1H23. He specifically cited the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and the Department of Transportation (DoTr) as key culprits to the under-spending.

This could complicate the BSP’s policy posture in its next policy meeting next week (17 August) . In recent briefings, BSP Governor Remolona has spoken of potential tightening to head off potential inflation, particularly given the recent spikes in rice and fuel prices. However, the GDP print clearly shows that the 425bps increase in policy rates is strangling private investments, which are much more sensitive to interest rates than household spending. Will they raise rates, and risk slamming the brakes even harder on growth? Or will they pause (or cut?) and risk a weak PHP if inflation re-accelerates?

Management appears to be making progress on its acquisition initiatives, as they expect to gain more traction here by next year.

The Secretary of Environment and Natural Resources, Toni Yulo-Loyzaga, announced the suspension and review of all 22 reclamation projects in Manila Bay.  The announcement comes after unusually persistent floods affected a number of areas, including towns in Pampanga and Bulacan (north of Manila).  This review process (which may result in the revocation of the reclamation approvals) is likely to be politically fraught with powerful interests and deep pockets involved. We note that most of these projects were approved by the previous (Duterte) government, which makes it more possible for these to be canceled.

Arguably the most “high profile” among these reclamation projects are those by SM Prime (SMPH PM).  And the impact on SMPH could be very material as its two reclamation projects (in Pasay, Paranaque) are for a combined 660 hectares and account for 37% of our NAV estimate. Note the Paranaque portion has yet to receive final government approval.

MACRO
2Q23 GDP slows to 4.3% on soft government spending, private investments
  • 2Q23 GDP came in at 4.3%, slowing from 6.4% in 1Q23 and falling short of consensus estimates (+6%)
  • On the demand side, both government spending and private investments dragged
    • Government spending declined by 7.1% due to the implementation of the Mandanas ruling, which transferred spending to more inefficient local governments, as well as base effects from last year’s election spending
    • Private investments barely grew, dragged down by a sharp deceleration in construction activity (+2.1%) and inventory withdrawals
      • Slowdown in construction was due to government (+0.8%) and private households (-3.9%)
    • Consumption normalized to +5.5%, lifted by transport (+30%), restaurants/hotels (+23%), and housing/utilities (+6%) but offset by clothing (-27%), alcoholic bevs and tobacco (-9%) and household furnishings/equipment (-2.7%)
    • Net exports lifted GDP, contributing +0.9ppts to growth
  • On the supply side, the service sector was the sole driver of GDP growth, thanks to strong motor vehicle sales, transport, accommodation/food services
    • Fisheries pulled down overall agri sector to just +0.2%
    • Industry sector moderated to 2.1% due to sharp deceleration of construction
  • Brings 1H23 GDP growth to +5.3% YoY
    • We’re forecasting FY23 GDP of 5.5%; To reach this, GDP growth for the rest of the year should be +5.6%
    • To achieve the government’s +6-7% target, GDP needs to grow by at least 6.6% in 2H
    • The government believes their target is still attainable, as they “formulate catch-up plans, accelerate, and even frontload the implementation of programs and projects”

AIRLINES
July tourist data - big jumps in Korean, Chinese, Japanese visitors
  • Tourist arrivals jumped in July, up 75% YoY and up 15% MoM to 516,304, a new post-Covid high. This is roughly 75% of the 2019 monthly average (72% of July 2019). For 7M23, the Philippines took in 3.21mn tourists, up 189% YoY and coming in at about 66% of 7M2019 levels.
  • July was notable in that we saw big jumps in arrivals from Korea, China and Japan, all of which are at post-Covid highs. Korean tourists are already at 84% of the 2019 monthly average, while Japanese visitors are about halfway there. Chinese tourists, meanwhile, are just under a quarter of where they were pre-Covid. It is difficult to estimate, though, how much of the Chinese visitors were POGO employees.  
  • The improving tourism picture bodes well for the Current Account, and the economy in general.
  • In terms of stocks, BLOOM and CEB are the likely main beneficiaries.
