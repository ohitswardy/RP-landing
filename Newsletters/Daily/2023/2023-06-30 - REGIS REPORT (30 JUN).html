REGIS REPORT

30 June 2023

The minimum wage in Metro Manila is being raised by 7%.  The regional wage board for the National Capital Region (NCR) approved an increase of P40/day to P610 (US$11) for non-agricultural workers.  The increase takes effect July 16.

The adjustment comes just a little over a year since the last increase (+6% in June 2023) and does not appear unreasonable.  Inflation over the past year, like many other places, has been running hot, with the latest CPI print (for May) at +6.1%.

We ought to expect a small bump in labor costs, which will likely be felt most by firms with employing a large number of low-skilled staff (e.g., manufacturers, retailers and restaurants).

+++

Holcim Philippines (HLCM PM) announced it will take steps to delist itself after its major shareholder Holderfin increased its stake by 9ppt to 27% yesterday.  The purchase of the 9% stake in HLCM by Holderfin from Sumitomo brought the public float down to just over 5%, triggering a delisting which can be accomplished either voluntarily or involuntarily. Holderfin will attempt to do it voluntarily with a tender offer for the remaining 5% that the group does not own.

Holderfin acquired the stake from Sumitomo for P3.92/share, which translates to a market cap of P25bn (US$0.45bn) and enterprise value of P24.5bn (US$0.44bn). While earnings today are depressed, we reckon the implied valuation is low at a mere US$64 per annual MT of cement. To be clear, the purchase price for Sumitomo’s stake is not necessarily the price at which the upcoming tender offer will be set.

For perspective, Cemex just undertook a tender offer for its Philippine subsidiary Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) at an implied valuation of US$94 per annual MT, In fact, one may recall that San Miguel (SMC PM) offered to buy HLCM in 2019 for US$2.1bn, more than 4x of the implied value of what Holderfin is paying Sumitomo.  The SMC offer was, however, ultimately rejected by regulators on competition concerns.

+++

ACEN (ACEN PM) completed the first phase of its transaction to acquire 49% of a portfolio of solar farms in Vietnam.  The entire deal, covering 837MW, was first announced last year.  Once fully closed, the purchase would add 10% to ACEN’s claimed capacity of 4,177MW (which includes capacity not quite operational yet).

+++

Digital transactions through the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) National Retail Payments System (NRSP) rose 36% in May to P1.075trn (US$19bn).  This is indicative of the rapid adoption of digital payments, with implications for the banking industry as well as fintech firms.

MACRO
Wage board approves P40 hike in minimum wage in Metro Manilla
  • Regional wage board approved a P40 increase (+7%) in NCR’s minimum wage, which will take effect of July 16
  • The order will raise the minimum wage to P610 a day for non-agriculture workers
    • The increase is less than half of the P150 propose in the Senate
    • Minimum wage in the capital is now P140 below the P750 daily national minimum wage advocated by labour groups
  • In June 2022, the government hiked the minimum wage in NCR by P33 (+6.1%)
    • Prior to this, the minimum wage increase was last implemented in 2017 (+4.3%)
  • It is unclear at this stage if wages in other regions will also be hiked
    • In 2022, the government implemented wage hikes of +12%-14% throughout the country

 

 

 

BANKS
Digital payments surge 36% in May to new record highs
  • Online transactions done over the BSP’s National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped 35.9% YoY in May to P1,075bn (roughly $19bn), a new monthly record. For payments done via InstaPay – P50,000 or less – the value of transactions surged 48.0% YoY to P414bn. For larger transactions done over PesoNet, the total value was up 29.2% YoY to P616bn. Both were all-time highs. For year-to-May, total NRPS transactions hit P4,879bn, up 31.6% YoY, with InstaPay up 41.0% and PesoNet up 26.3%.
  • The InstaPay data is particularly interesting, in our view. Given the relatively low limit per transaction, it is likely more indicative of peer-to-peer or of retail transactions. Based on the BSP data, the average transaction value over InstaPay was P6,550 (under $120) in 5M22, with May alone at an even lower P6,286 (under $115). In terms of number of transactions, InstaPay was up an astounding 51.4% YoY in May.
  • This suggests continued rapid adoption of digital payments among Filipino individuals. Note, too, that this data only captures payments made between financial institutions. It does not take into account transactions done by parties within the same platform, ie BDO-to-BDO, or BPI-to-BPI, or GCash-to-GCash, etc. Thus, the BSP’s NRPS data likely vastly understates the actual usage of digital payment services.

 

 

 

 

 

POWER
ACEN announces closing of the first phase of Super Energy deal
  • ACEN disclosed that it completed the first phase of the transaction involving Super Energy’s Vietnam solar farms
  • The remaining phases are expected to be completed within the year
  • The acquisition will be completed in four phases, with the first phase adding 141MW of attributable capacity
  • To recall
    • ACEN will acquire 49% of the platform for $165mn under a share purchase agreement signed and announced in February 2022  
    • The deal involves 837MW (gross) of solar assets in Vietnam
    • The purchase price for the Vietnam solar plants implies a valuation of roughly $800-900k/MW
  • Upon completion of the four phases of the transaction, the Super Energy assets will add 410MW in attributable capacity to ACEN, which reported 4,177MW in attributable capacity as of May 2023
  • The Super Energy assets are not yet in ACEN’s reported numbers nor Regis estimates
  • ACEN appears on track to comfortably exceed its 5,000MW target ahead of 2025
  • ACEN currently trades at 24.5x PER and 12x attributable EV/EBITDA on our FY23 estimates

 

CEMENT
Holcim Phils parent initiates tender offer
  • Holcim Phils (HLCM PM) received a notice from its parent, Holderfin, of its intent to conduct a tender offer to all stockholders of record for purposes of delisting
  • Yesterday, Holderfin bought 595mn shares (9.22% stake) of HLCM from Japan-based Sumitomo through a block sale at P3.92/sh
    • The acquisition brought the public float of HLCM down to 5.05%, below the min. public float requirement of the Phil. Stock Exchange
    • Since HLCM will not be able to raise the required min. public float within a reasonable period, its parent will need to conduct a tender offer
  • A tender offer price has not been disclosed
    • Assuming the tender offer price will be P3.92/sh, we estimate this translates to an EV of US$445mn and EV/MT of US$64, well below replacement cost
    • This will also be well below the proposed buy-in price of San Miguel (SMC PM) back in 2019 (Enterprise value of US$2.15bn or an EV/MT of US$300)

 

 

MACRO
‘Hot money’ exit continues in May
  • Foreign portfolio investments in the Philippines posted a net outflow of US$124.49mn in May, a 54% drop from the previous year
    • Gross inflows of hot money in May amounted to US$844.72mn (down 12.5% compared to US$965.62mn last year)
    • Gross outflows decreased by 21.6% to US$969.21mn
  • Decrease attributed to an improved economic landscape, the resolution of the US debt ceiling impasse, expectations of the Fed pausing its hiking cycle (at that time), and the slightly deprecating peso
  • Majority of investment went into PSE listed securities, particularly banks, food, beverage and tobacco, holding firms, property and transportation services
  • Top investor countries were the UK, USA, Singapore, Luxembourg, and Hong Kong
  • 5M23 net inflow amounted to US$805mn, reversing the US$1.1bn net inflow recorded in the same period last year
    • The central bank expects foreign portfolio investments to end the year with a US$2.5bn net inflow
