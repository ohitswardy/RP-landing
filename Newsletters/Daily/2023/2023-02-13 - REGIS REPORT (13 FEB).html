REGIS REPORT

13 February 2023

Philip Morris (PM US) reported sales volumes in the Philippines (under a partnership with LT Group (LTG PM)) of 7.6bn sticks (-12%YoY, -1%QoQ). This brought cigarette sales for 2022 to 32.0bn sticks, down 6.5%, weighed lower by a hefty price increase last March (17-21%). LTG’s profits from tobacco underperformed up until 9M22 due to a late implementation of the price hike last year. Margins suffered as it absorbed the higher excise tax in the early part of the year.

If LTG’s dividend last December (P0.50/sh out of a total of P1.4/sh for 2022) provides any indication, however, it would be that profits from tobacco very likely showed a recovery despite the soft volume trends. The stock is trading at just under 4x PER and looks poised to pay a yearly dividend of at least 12%.

+++

Electricity prices are already climbing again as we inch closer to the peak dry season. The market operator says that spot prices rose to P7.43/kwh in the first few days of Feb (up >30% from Jan), ahead of the full impact of Malampaya maintenance shutdown.

Meralco (MER PM) had apparently secured a short-term, three-week, deal for electricity supply to partially replace the 670MW that San Miguel (SMC PM) has ceased supplying. This deal will keep prices stable but is unlikely to keep them from rising in the next few months.

Meanwhile, Prime Infra, a firm controlled by Enrique Razon, appears to settling its issues with partner Solar Philippines, the controlling shareholder of SP New Energy (SPNEC PM). Prime Infra and Solar Philippines have a joint venture to supply Meralco with 850MW of power beginning 2026. The disclosures yesterday by SPNEC suggest a parting of ways with Prime Infra and Solar PH each taking 100% ownership in one of two 50-50 owned entities.

+++

Japanese group Mitsui, together with other parties (including Metro Pacific (MPI PM)), signed an agreement to invest US$600mn in the Philippines. This was one of the many supposed investment pledges signed during President Marcos’ trip to Japan last week. Note that there have reports that Mitsui is among the parties taking part in the proposed privatization of MPI.

+++

BPI (BPI PM) managed to convince the Philippine SEC to lower the fine related to its filings for its ESOP from P135mn to P30mn.

++

The preliminary employment numbers show a slight dip in people employed in Dec (from Nov), with a drop in labor participation. Reported unemployment rate was still a very low 4.3%, from 4.2% the previous month.

MACRO
FDI drops by 44% YoY, 14% MoM to US$793mn in November
  • Foreign direct investments (FDI) recorded US$793mn net inflows in November 2022, down 44% YoY and 14% MoM
    • Lowest monthly figure since Sep 2022
    • MoM drop due to declines in (a) non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments, (b) reinvestment of earnings, and (c) equity other than reinvestment earnings
  • Brings YT-November net inflows down by 13% YoY to US$8.43bn
  • The BSP expects FDI net inflows at US$8.5bn by end-2022 and at US$11bn by end-2023
MACRO
Unemployment, labor force participation rates mildly worsen MoM in Dec
  • The latest Labor Force Survey showed a mild deterioration in the labor force participation in December 2022 vs Nov 2022
    • Labor force participation rate dropped to 66.4% from 67.5%
  • Unemployment rate also rose slightly to 4.3% in Dec from 4.2% in November
  • However, the underemployment rate further improved to 12.6% from 14.4%

CONSUMER
PMFTC cigarette volumes flat QoQ in 4Q22, industry down slightly
  • PMI disclosed that cigarette volumes sold in the Philippines amounted to 7.6bn sticks in 4Q22, down 12%YoY
  • The volume held steady from the previous quarter,
    • 3Q volumes saw a slight recovery after price hikes earlier in the year dragged 2Q volumes
  • For FY22, cigarette volumes declined 6.5% to 32bn sticks, below Regis estimate of 35bn sticks
  • PMFTC appeared to have gained some market share in the quarter, with unit market share improving to 60.1% (from 59.2% in 3Q)

POWER
Higher power spot market prices loom
  • Average wholesale electricity spot market (WESM) prices rose to P7.43/kwh in the first few days of the February billing period
    • This is up from the P5.76/kwh recorded in January due to a lower supply margin
    • According to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP)
  • For the rest of February, covering also the remaining days of the Malampaya shutdown, IEMOP projects an average market price of P7.55/kwh
    • Citing a lower overall supply margin equivalent to 3,882 megawatts compared to January’s 4,979 MW
POWER
Meralco secures 300MW emergency supply from Aboitiz Power
  • Meralco signed a 300MW emergency power supply agreement (PSA) with GN Power Dinigin
    • GNPD is 70% owned by Aboitiz Power
  • The PSA will be a very short term agreement, expiring on Feb 25
    • Will partially replace the 670MW PSA that SMC reneged on
  • Will help smooth out power supply while Malampaya is on shutdown for two weeks this month
POWER
Prime Infra to raise stake in Solar Tanauan to 100%, exit Solar Tarlac
  • In a series of disclosures, SP New Energy (SPNEC) said that parties involved have resolved to continue their JV in Terra Solar Philippines Inc (TSPI)
  • It also disclosed agreements that would streamline Prime Infrastructure’s (Prime) ownerships in SPNEC affiliates, including -
  • The sale of SP’s common shares in Solar Tanauan for P1bn
    • Prime would own 100% of Solar Tanauan after the sale
    • Prime has a 50% stake in Solar Tanauan, after an investment of P2bn in December 2021
    • Solar Tanauan has a PSA with Meralco for 50MW and is under construction
  • Redemption of shares heled by Prime in Solar Tarlac for P1.5bn that would result in SP owning 100% of Solar Tarlac
    • Prime invested P1.5bn for 50% of Solar Tarlac in July 2020
    • Solar Tarlac has a PSA with Meralco for 85MW, has a 100MW operating plant with 50MW under construction

Citicore prepares for IPO, to invest US$4bn in renewable energy
  • Citicore Renewable Energy Corp. is planning to go public this year to fund a US$4bn investment in new solar projects over the next five years
