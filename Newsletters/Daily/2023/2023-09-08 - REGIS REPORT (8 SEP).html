REGIS REPORT

08 September 2023

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) pushed back its expectations for when inflation will return to its target band of 2-4%, and with that the market’s hopes for an early return to lower policy rates.

At the core of the matter, obviously, is the rebound in inflation due to higher food prices. The government announced that it was hoping to sign a 5-year bilateral agreement for rice supply from Vietnam, which provides >80% of Philippine rice imports. If this supply is priced according to the international market, it would still offer no relief.

Those who think rice is the only issue for inflation here would be mistaken, however. Vegetable food inflation was, in fact, 32% in Aug and its impact on Aug CPI was larger than the impact of rice. Tomatoes (>U$5/kg) are now 70% more expensive than chicken (US$3/kg). What’s happening now is not dissimilar to all the episodes of food price spikes (sugar, onions and rice) the last twelve months. Weather-related supply disruptions may be part of the problem, but the situation is being exacerbated by misguided protectionist policies. A stubborn, widespread belief in the conspiratorial nature of the price spikes will not help.

The general offer for Metro Pacific (MPI PM) is being extended by twelve calendar days to Sept 19 (noon). The offer period is being extended despite MPI’s claims that the non-public shares (including the GSIS stake of 12%) would already reach 96.87% based on the shares already tendered. We reckon MPI (and the consortium seeking to take it private) are offering minorities one last chance to sell their shares before the official transaction to take it private.

Any transactions coming after the public float officially falls below the minimum 10% would have to be conducted outside the exchange and would be subject to capital gains taxes.

+++

The Manila airport (NAIA) privatization has apparently attracted three parties so far: the Manila International Airport Corp (aka the “super consortium”), Indian group GMR and San Miguel (SMC PM). We think only the first two are likely to be serious in considering a bid, which is supposed to be submitted by Dec.

+++

Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that a new investor was taking an 11% indirect stake in Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel) for P2.24bn. The beneficial stake of Dito CME in DitoTel will be diluted to 44%. The funds, combined with funds previously raised, should buy DitoTel a few months. DitoTel will, in our view, need much more than that to become viable.

TELCOS
New investor dilutes Dito CME's stake in DitoTel
  • Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that its 89.5%-owned subsidiary Dito Holdings issued new shares to a company called Summit Global. A total of 2.24bn new shares were issued to Summit Global at P1/share and the new shares are equivalent to 18.5% of the capital of Dito Holdings. Note that Dito Holdings is the owner of 60% of the shares of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel).
  • Dito CME’s effective ownership in DitoTel would thus be diluted from 54% to 44%. This issuance means that Summit Global is buying 11% (18.5% x 60%) of DitoTel for P2.24bn, which implies a valuation of P20bn for 100% equity of DitoTel. Frankly, we don’t think DitoTel is worth even that much. It is unclear who is behind Summit Global, but a recently formed company called Summit Telco acquired P1.6bn worth of Dito CME shares just a few weeks back.

The funds raised are supposed to be used to support DitoTel’s operations. Unless it is a prelude to a bigger investment, the amount will at best buy DitoTel a couple of months.

INFRASTRUCTURE
NAIA privatization attracts three bidders so far
  • The Department of Transportation (DOTr) received interest from three (3) potential bidders so far, according to Secretary Jaime Bautista
    • Manila consortium consisting of six Philippine conglomerates – AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners
    • San Miguel Corp (SMC PM)
    • India-based GMR
  • DOTr will be inviting more foreigners to participate in the auction in the next few weeks
  • The government prefers an operator with a track record and “very good” financial background
    • Based on the instructions to bidders document, the bidders will be competing on the government’s percentage share of gross revenues, which will exclude passenger service charge
    • Financial terms that are fixed are (1) P30bn upfront payment and (b) P2bn annuity payment
  • The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62mn annually from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years)
    • Submission of bids set on Dec. 27

  • Tender offer period will remain open until Sep 19, 12pm from (Sep 7 previously)
