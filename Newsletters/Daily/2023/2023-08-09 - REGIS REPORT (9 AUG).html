REGIS REPORT

09 August 2023

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) shareholders approved the plan to delist the company, paving the way for the long-discussed tender offer to be officially launched. 

To recap, the consortium making the tender offer aim to delist MPI which can be achieved if 31.6% of MPI shares (=9.1bn shares) are tendered.  This would take the public float to below 5%.  Alternatively, MPI can also be delisted if the regulators/exchange agree to have MPI delisted even if the public float remains above 5% after the exercise.  Frankly, we are uncertain about the leanings of the exchange on this issue (providing exemptive relief) but the fact that it is out there should raise pressure on minorities to sell their shares in the exercise.  The consortium says it will not proceed with the tender if MPI cannot be delisted.

The consortium members are First Pacific (142 HK), GT Capital (GTCAP PM), Mit-Pacific (a firm jointly owned by Mitsui and Japan Overseas Infrastructure Investment) and MIG Holdings (holding company owned by MPI chairman Manuel Pangilinan).  The tender offer price is P5.20/share, ~50% above the undisturbed price before talk of the deal began circulating.

Beyond the direct impact on MPI, one consequence of the probable delisting would be the entry of a new constituent into the PSE Index (PSEi or PCOMP).  By our reckoning, the name likely to replace MPI would be Bloomberry (BLOOM PM).

+++

Speaking of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM), the company issued a press statement on its results this morning.  The company delivered 2Q23 profit of P3.4bn (15%QoQ, 90%YoY), bringing 1H profit ahead of estimates, driven by strong EBITDA growth and a P357mn gain on an asset sale. EBITDA for 1H23 is tracking ahead of estimates at 59%/56% of FY23E. While there were early indications of a very weak May, it appears GGR recovered a bit in June to buoy GGR to P15.1bn (-6%QoQ, 15%YoY). VIP dipped as win rate normalized, while mass continued to outperform (GGR +3%QoQ, 22%YoY). We hope to get more after the briefing this afternoon.

Jollibee (JFC PM) announced headline profit of P4.4bn for 1H23, down 14%.  After pref and perp distributions, we estimate earnings to common shares would be P3.7bn.  While the bottom-line figure is looking below expectations, the EBIT figure of P7.6bn (+51% YoY) is outperforming them, suggesting strength in the underlying business. The company will hold its briefing this Friday.

Meanwhile, San Miguel Food and Beverage’s (FB PM) income to equity holders came in at P5.1bn for 2Q23 (-19% YoY) and P10.8bn for 1H23 (-14%), broadly on trend for our full year figures.  Softness across its key businesses (beer, hard liquor, proteins) is apparent.  More from Aaron (aaron.salvador@regis.ph) here:

MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to US$3.92bn in June
  • Preliminary data showed that the trade deficit in June shrunk by 33% YoY and 12% MoM to US$3.92bn
  • Imports dropped 15% YoY on base effects from high commodity prices last year
    • Fuel and raw material imports fell 43% YoY / 15% YoY
    • Consumer goods and capital goods imports were flattish YoY
  • Exports grew 0.8% YoY in June after rising +2.4% YoY in May
    • Exports to China posted positive growth rates for two consecutive months
    • Exports to HK also rebounded (+16% YoY), reversing declines in the past six months
  • Brings 1H23 trade deficit to US$27.96bn, down 6% YoY
  • The government is forecasting exports to grow by 1% YoY and imports to grow by 2% YoY in 2023, suggesting a trade deficit of c. US$60bn (~14% of GDP)
    • The trade deficit in 2022 stood at US$58.244bn (14% of GDP)

 

 

 

CONGLOS
MPI shareholders approve voluntary delisting; tender offer launched
  • Over 77% approve delisting, 1% vote against. MPI secured shareholder approval for a voluntary delisting during its Special Stockholders Meeting, paving the way for the commencement of the tender offer process today (Aug 9).  Shareholders representing more than 77% of total outstanding and listed shares approved the voluntary delisting proposal (vs. requirement: min of 66%) while less than 1% voted against the delisting (vs. requirement: max 10%).  The balance abstained or did not attend the meeting.

 The tender offer period will run from August 9 to about September 7.  If the offer period is not extended, the delisting will become effective on October 9. 

  • May seek exemptive relief if 95% threshold is not met.  The tender offer report indicated that the bidders must acquire at least 9.1bn common shares (31.6% of MPI’s shares out) to be able to own 95% of MPI’s total listed and outstanding shares and secure the PSE’s approval for a delisting.  If the tendered shares are less than 9.1bn, MPI will seek exemptive relief from the PSE to proceed with the delisting.  If this is not met, the bidders may withdraw the offer.  Moreover, the report did not mention a partial tender offer or any clause that alludes to a possible reduction in the target tendered shares.  The bidders reiterated that the tender offer will not proceed if MPI will not be delisted. 

 

  • Interim dividend.  Even though the tender offer report indicated that tendering shareholders will receive any 2023 interim dividend that may be declared on August 14, we believe that such dividend may not be substantial.  Even if we assume MPI will receive the dividend of Meralco (MER PM) in September, we estimate MPI’s cash level (before debt service and opex) could only be c. P0.52/sh.  Given its large capital requirements, MPI has also kept its annual dividend constant at just P0.11/sh in the past five years and it already paid P0.076/sh in April. 

 

  • BLOOM to enter PSEi if de-listing is successful. Assuming MPI is delisted, BLOOM will likely take its place in the PSE Index. 

 

 

GAMING
BLOOM 2Q23 profit of P3.4bn (+90%YoY) brings 1H well ahead of estimates; mass segments temper impact from low VIP win rate
  • BLOOM posted a 2Q23 profit of P3.4bn, (15%QoQ, 90%YoY), on the back of strong EBITDA growth and a P357mn gain on an asset sale
    • This brings the 1H23 profit to P6.4bn (158%YoY), ahead of both Regis (P9.7bn) and consensus (P9.5bn) estimates for FY23E
  • 2Q23 EBITDA hit P5.5bn (-4%QoQ, 42%YoY) – with 1H23 EBITDA of P11.2bn (+67%YoY) also tracking ahead of Regis (P19.1bn) and consensus (P19.9bn)
    • This was despite early indications of a very weak May GGR performance due to VIP
    • The strength of the mass segments tempered the impact from the low VIP win rate
  • 2Q23 GGR only softened a little bit (-6%QoQ, 15%YoY)
    • While VIP GGR fell 26%QoQ, it was nevertheless higher vs last year
      • VIP GGR in the quarter of P4.7bn implies some recovery in June from early indications of very low GGR in May
      • Hold was expectedly lower at 2.73% in 2Q23 (vs theoretical 2.85%) and unusually high VIP hold of 3.8% in 1Q23
    • Slot machine GGR continued to outperform, with GGR growth of 13%QoQ and 17%YoY
    • Mass table GGR increased 3%QoQ and 22%YoY
      • Mass table win softened QoQ but remained elevated at 37.9% (from 40.5% in 1Q23)
  • Earnings call is scheduled for later at 4PM - 2Q/1H2023 Results Call
  • On our 24E numbers (+12% EBITDA growth), BLOOM trades at 8x EV/EBITDA
