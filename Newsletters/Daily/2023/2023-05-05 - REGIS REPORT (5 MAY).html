We have a big set of results this morning: 

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (-) – Headline income for 1Q23 came in at P9.0bn, largely unchanged YoY. Core income, inclusive of losses at Voyager, was P7.9bn (+6%). While the core profit figure looks like it is trending within full-year estimates, we note that depreciation expenses are lower this year due to ‘accelerated depreciation’ / write-offs undertaken last year. Core income would have otherwise been ~15% lower. The underlying business is looking sluggish with mobile revenue dipping YoY.

  • ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) (=) - We received a little more color on ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) from its earnings call yesterday. The jump in EBITDA, and consequently profit (P2.026bn, 507%QoQ, 400%YoY), happened as it swung to a net seller position in the spot market. Capacity from new plants in 2H is expected to bring ACEN to a bigger net seller position, in line with management’s the view that prices are likely to be elevated over the next few years. There was also some help from non-operating items (as in prior periods), with 1) interest from a financial instrument and 2) a gain from a settlement of loan chipping in a combined P1.8bn.

  • Semirara (SCC PM) (-) – After diving deeper into Semirara’s 1Q23 results and hearing management’s thoughts during the briefing, we reckon 1Q23 profit of P9.46bn (+101% QoQ) is just a tad behind our forecasts. Despite weakness in regional thermal coal prices, investors can take comfort from (a) the forthcoming improvement in coal sales volumes and (b) higher power prices. These and the company’s healthy cash position (c.P2/sh post-div) should allow the company to pay another hefty special dividend in 2H.

Globe (GLO PM) also released its own 1Q23 results this morning. Core income for the quarter was P5.1bn, virtually unchanged YoY, and looking on track to meet full-year consensus and Regis estimates (P20bn). Service revenues were up 2% YoY while EBITDA improved 4%. We note Globe appears to have gained ~0.8pt of mobile market share in 1Q vs the previous quarter (4Q22). We should have more after the briefing on Monday.

In own press release, Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 1Q23 NPAT of P10.5bn, up 31.3% YoY. The figure is ahead of REGIS (P38.6bn) and consensus (P38.0bn) forecasts for the full year. The outperformance was due largely to trading/fx gains of P2.1bn in 1Q22. Loan growth slowed to 12.5% YoY (was 14.7% YoY in 4Q22), as corporate loans decelerated to 12.7% (from 15%). Consumer loans grew 11.8% YoY (up from c9% YoY at year-end), driven by credit cards (+30% YoY) and an acceleration in auto loans to 10.7% (was 3.6% at end-22). NIMs seem to have widened QoQ. Opex jumped 14% YoY due to taxes/licenses (likely stamp duty on time deposits), and IT spend. Asset quality continued to improve, with NPL ratio down to 1.8% (from 1.9%) and NPL cover jumped to 189% from 172%.

Full FS should be out later today. The bank will hold a briefing on Monday 8 May at 4pm.

+++

CPI inflation for April decelerated sharply to 6.6% in Apr, well below Street expectations. The inflation figure for Mar was 7.6%. While core inflation for Apr is still high at 7.9%, it is also a tad lower MoM.

This CPI print should mean less reason for the Bangko Sentral (BSP) to hike rates later this month. The Fed’s rate hike action yesterday, however, is likely to figure in the BSP’s calculus.

  • ICTSI’s (ICT PM) organic volume growth may have slowed sharply or even turned negative in 1Q23, after compiling port volume data from the port authorities in the Philippines and Melbourne. Recall that ICTSI’s organic volumes grew 3% YoY in 4Q22. The company flagged the moderation in briefings and meetings, citing the extended lunar new year as the reason.

Notwithstanding the organic volume slowdown, we believe risk to earnings remains tilted to the upside, given our conservative yield estimates (see table below). Management is guiding that at the very least, yields will hold up this year. 

  • MICT volumes down 1% YoY in 1Q23. Data from the Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) showed that foreign container volumes at the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) slipped by 1% YoY to 666k TEUs, accounting for just 23% of our full-year forecast (2.9mn TEUs, +3% YoY). MICT’s market share fell to 69% from the peak of 73% in 1Q22 but this remains above pre-Covid levels of 65%. Overall volumes at the two international ports of Manila rose 5% YoY during the quarter, moderating from +11% YoY in FY22. At this point, we cannot explain the market share loss. We estimate MICT accounts for 21% of 2023E consolidated volumes and 26% of revenues.
  • Volumes at the three ports of Melbourne dropped 10% YoY in 1Q23. International container volumes in Melbourne dropped 10% YoY in 1Q23. It is worth noting, however, that VICT raised landside tariffs in January 2023 by at least 10%, which should more than offset any volume weakness. We estimate VICT accounts for 7% of consolidated volumes and 8% of revenues.

  • Domestic container volumes in Manila soar to record levels. Meanwhile, domestic container volumes in its 50%-owned unit Manila North Harbour Port, Inc. (MNHPI) rose 36% YoY and 32% QoQ to a record 470k TEUs, which is already 33% of our FY23E. We estimate MNHPI accounts for 10% of consolidated volumes but only 4% of revenues. ICTSI began consolidating MNHPI in Sep 2022, hence, consolidated FY23E volume growth rate will appear stronger.
  • The company is holding a briefing on Monday, May 8, at 5pm HKT (to register, email: ir@ictsi.com). We hope to get more detail then.
