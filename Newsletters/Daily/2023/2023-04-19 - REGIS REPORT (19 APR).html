REGIS REPORT

19 April 2023

Reporting season for 1Q2023 got off to a strong start yesterday.

BDO’s (BDO PM) NPAT for 1Q23 came in at P16.5bn, up 41% YoY, broadly in line, accounting for 25% of Regis (P65.9bn) and the Street (P66.0bn) forecasts for FY2023. While customer loan growth slowed to 8% YoY (from 9% in 4Q22), NIMs expanded another 11bps QoQ and 48bps YoY. Margins could see another quarter bump up, with the increase in the credit card rate cap (from 2% to 3% a month) only made effective from mid-February 2023. We are mindful though of the sharp jump in deposit costs (+30bps QoQ), as much of the deposit growth (14% YoY) came from TDs (+92% YoY). Provision expense fell 15% YoY as asset quality remained benign – NPL ratio inched up to 1.98% from 1.95%, while NPL cover increased to 170% from 167% at end-2022.

Overall a solid set of results. The bank will hold a briefing tomorrow, Thursday, 20 April.

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported NPAT of P16.5bn for 1Q23, up 41% YoY (-3% QoQ), accounting for 25% of Regis (P65.94bn) and consensus forecasts (P66.04bn) for FY2023. Revenue growth was driven primarily by NII, which jumped 28% YoY on 8% growth in “customer loans”, and NIMs which expanded 48bps YoY and 11bps QoQ. Despite the sharp rise in interest rates, asset quality remained benign, with NPL ratio a tad higher at 1.98% (from 1.95% in 4Q) and NPL cover up to 170% (from 167%), which allowed the bank to drop provision expense by 15% YoY and 24% QoQ.

 

It would seem that earnings momentum is still tilted upwards. Note that the increase in the credit card rate cap was only raised from 2% to 3% a month in mid-February. We are mindful, though of the sudden jump in deposits costs in 1Q, though this is still likely the lowest in the industry. The bank will hold a briefing on Thursday, 20 April.

 

Meanwhile, some highlights:

 

  • Headline net loan growth slowed to 8.9% YoY in 1Q23 from 10.0% at end-2022, while the bank reckons that gross customer loans grew 8%, slower than the 8.9% the previous quarter. No breakdown was given for loan growth in the bank’s press release, or FS. Expect more details at the briefing. Deposits, meanwhile, grew 14%, taking LDR down to 82%.
  • NIMs expanded 11bps QoQ (and +48bps YoY). Loan yields were up 33bps QoQ (+140bps YoY), on higher benchmark rates. The rate cap on credit card rates was also lifted from 2% a month to 3% effective mid-February, which suggests continued upward pricing momentum heading into 2Q23. Meanwhile, deposit costs jumped 30bps QoQ (+86bps YoY), as BDO’s CASA ratio dipped further to 76%, normalizing back to pre-Covid levels.
  • Non-interest income was up 13% YoY. Trading and fx gains were up 25% YoY as market interest rates dropped QoQ. Despite the increase in policy rates, the 10-year BVAL fell c80bps from 7.0% at end-2022 to 6.2% at end-1Q23. Fee incomes were up 20%, while insurance premiums were flat YoY.
  • Opex jumped 17% YoY, driven in large part by a 51% spike in Taxes and Licenses. We believe this is due to the near-doubling of time deposits (TDs) YoY, as banks absorb the documentary stamp tax (DST) levied on TDs. Meanwhile, compensation expense rose 14% YoY.
  • PPOP was up 33% YoY as operating leverage kicked in.
  • Provision expense dropped 15% YoY and 24% QoQ as asset quality remained benign despite the sharp increase in interest rates. NPL ratio inched up to 1.98% from 1.95% at end-2022. However, we reckon the absolute PHP value was flat QoQ as the loan base was down 1.5% QoQ. Despite the drop in provisions, NPL coverage improved further to 170% (from 167% in 4Q22), giving the bank ample buffer for potential NPL hits. Meanwhile, restructured loans (RLs) were down 28% YoY, and we estimate the RL ratio at around 3.3% of total loans, down from close to 6% at the peak in 2Q2021.

 

Overall, a solid set of results. More after the briefing on Thursday.
