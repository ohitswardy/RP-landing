REGIS REPORT

20 April 2023

In a press release, BPI 1Q23 NPAT P12.1bn, ahead of REGIS (P45.0bn) and Street (P45.3bn) FY estimates. Revenue, opex, and PPOP were broadly in line. The major driver of the beat was provision expense, which dropped to just P1bn for 1Q23 (Regis FY23 P11.8bn), as NPLs stayed benign. Loans grew 13.6% YoY, slowing from 15.3% at 4Q22, though we saw consumer loan segments accelerate, with credit cards +39% YoY in 1Q23 vs 31% in 4Q22, and auto loans +16% vs 14%. Corporate loans slowed to 12.6% YoY compared to 15.5% at end-2022. NIMs jumped +52bps YoY, though we estimate this flattened out to single-digit bps QoQ. Margins were constrained by a jump in TDs, with CASA ratio dropping to 70%, from 74% at end-2022 and 81% at 1Q22. Overall, results were solid. If NPLs do not deteriorate materially, provision expense could provide upside to our and Street estimates for FY2023. Earnings call on 3 March.

+++

Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) reported that 1Q23 net office take-up declined for the second consecutive quarter. While Metro Manila office vacancy was flat QoQ at 18%, we forecast it to rise to 20% by the-end of this year, as new buildings are completed and work-from-home policies crimp demand. On a more positive note, LPC said that sales of Metro Manila condominiums continued to recover in 1Q23.

+++

We got more detail on Puregold’s (PGOLD PM) 4Q22 results at yesterday’s briefing. The 4Q performance was supported by holiday spending, but underlying trends across both brands show 1) inflationary pressures to the core Puregold customer and 2) rising competition for S&R. These factors have led management to provide guidance that appears to be too conservative, in our view. While near term gross margins could be under pressure, we believe this should be cushioned by operating leverage as the new stores ramp up.

At 2023E multiples of 9x PE and 4x EV/EBITDA, the stock trades at a significant discount to peers, both regionally (23x PE / 12x EV/EBITDA) and locally (RRHI at 15x / 5x EV/EBITDA). We believe current levels reflect a discount that is wide enough to account for potential and perceived corporate governance concerns, and we continue to believe that the runway for growth remains long for PGOLD as it drives penetration and consolidation of the modern retail sector.

BANKS
BPI 1Q23 NPAT P12.1bn up 52% YoY; ahead on sharp drop in provisions

In a press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported 1Q2023 net profit of P12.1bn, up 52% YoY, and ahead of REGIS (P45.0bn) and consensus (P45.3bn) forecasts for FY2023. However, the main outperformance came from a sharp drop in provision expense to just P1bn (-60% YoY, and a fraction of our P11.8bn full-year forecast). Revenues, opex, and PPOP were in line with our estimates. Loan growth slowed to 13.6% YoY, more than offset by NIMs which jumped 52bps YoY.

Some highlights from the limited press release:

  • Loan growth moderated to 13.6% YoY from the 15.3% recorded in 4Q22. Note that the low base effect fades in 2Q22 when the bank participated in the chunky tower sale funding. For 1Q23, growth was driven by accelerating credit cards (+39% in 1Q vs 31% in 4Q) and auto loans (+16% vs 14%), while corporate slowed sharply (12.6% vs 15.5%).
  • NIMs widened 52bps YoY, by BPI’s estimate, which suggests that QoQ margins were broadly flat. This is likely due to the increase in TDs, which carry a documentary stamp tax of 0.75%. While all banks absorb the DST, only BPI includes it in their deposit costs, while every other bank books it as an opex. This makes BPI’s margins not directly comparable to other banks.
  • Other income grew 19% YoY, likely getting a boost from trading gains as market interest rates fell QoQ.
  • Opex was up 20% YoY as the bank continued its aggressive IT investment. Despite this, BPI’s cost to income ratio (CIR) dropped to 47.5%.
  • Provision expense plunged 60% YoY to just P1bn in 1Q23. Asset quality remains benign, and management had guided to lowering NPL coverage. NPL ratio edged up to 1.82% from 1.76% at end-2022. However, we reckon that absolute NPL levels were flat QoQ, and that the higher ratio was due to the c2.5% QoQ contraction in loans. NPL cover came down slightly to 176.7%, compared to 180.1% in 4Q22.

Overall, the results look quite good. If NPLs do not materially deteriorate, provision expense could provide upside risk to our and Street forecasts.

PROPERTY
LPC: Metro Manila 1Q23 office vacancy flat QoQ at 18%
  • Leechiu Property Consultants said that Metro Manila 1Q23 office vacancy was flat QoQ at 18%
    • LPC estimates that vacancy in Fort Bonifacio was only 9%
    • We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 20%-50% below pre-COVID levels
  • Total nationwide net office take-up dropped to 161,000sqm, from 177,000sqm in 3Q22
    • BPO nationwide net office take-up dropped to 78,000sqm, from 127,000sqm in 4Q22 
  • Metro Manila net office take-up was likely about 100,000sqm, on track to meet our FY23 forecast of 400,000sqm
    • Prior to the POGO industry entering the country, Metro Manila annual office take-up was 470,000sqm to 530,000sqm
  • We forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 20% in 2023E
    • Supply will increase due to new office completions
    • We also expect demand to be muted due to WFH policies – several BPO firms have transferred their registration from PEZA to BOI, which does not set limits on WFH
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 51% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY22E P/B, and 5.7X FY23E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 4.1X FY23E P/E
    • 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 64% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 7.6X FY23E P/E
  • LPC also said that Metro Manila condominium sales were recovering and prices in the primary market are generally up 5-10% YoY
  • ALI is trading at 47% discount to NAV, 1.5X FY23E P/B, and 16.6X FY23E P/E

RETAIL
PGOLD – momentum sustained in 4Q22 but outlook cautious amidst inflationary pressure, competition
  • PGOLD reported FY22 NPAT +14% yoy to P9.3bn — broadly in line with Regis (P9.5bn) but ahead of consensus (P8.6bn). FY22 net sales +12% yoy to P184.3bn, in line with our estimates. Meanwhile, EBIT was P14.5bn (+12% yoy), mildly behind expectations. For 4Q22 alone, NPAT rose 15% YoY to P2.8bn.
  • On the core Puregold brand:
    • SSSG accelerated to +10% in 4Q, partly due to a low YoY base. Same-store foot traffic continued to grow strongly (+10% in 4Q by our estimate) due to the reopening, though same-store tickets grew at a softer pace (+1-2%). Management noted that high inflation continues to pressure average baskets for the core Puregold customer, especially in 1Q23 after the bump from holiday spending had subsided.
    • EBITDA margin was largely stable during the quarter. Gross margin mildly expanded YoY due to the increase in supplier support, but this was weighed by proportionately higher opex.
  • On S&R:
    • SSSG slowed to +15% in 4Q, due to a slowdown in the growth of average baskets. We believe this was largely due to more competitive pricing action, which also led to a 120-bp contraction in gross margins.
    • EBITDA margin was further pressured by higher opex, mostly relating to pre-operating expenses for the new stores in the pipeline. S&R did not open any new stores in 2022 but plans to ramp up expansion with 4 new stores in 2023.
  • The company has earmarked P11.9bn in capex in 2023, of which around half will be used for Puregold and S&R’s store expansion. An additional P3.5bn has been allocated for investments in logistics and distribution centers to support the new stores to be opened over the next few years.
  • Overall, management’s 2023 outlook was cautious given the continued pressure on consumer wallets amidst high inflation (Puregold) and rising competition (S&R). Management expects to grow consolidated top line by 7-8% (Regis FY23E: +8%), and ~100bps gross margin contraction for both Puregold and S&R. The guidance implies a 5-10% contraction in operating profit, which is, in our view, on the conservative side. While there could be pressure on gross margins, we believe there will also likely be operating leverage as the newer stores ramp up.

TOURISM
Recovery in foreign tourist arrivals stalls in March
  • Tourist arrivals in March slipped by 2% MoM to 67% of pre-Covid (from 68% in Feb)
    • Due to the 18%/10% MoM declines in Korean/American travelers
    • The slack could be partly explained by seasonality, as arrivals from these countries typically peak in December and diminish in succeeding months
  • The recovery of Chinese tourist arrivals remains slow, with March arrivals back to just 13% of pre-Covid, up from 10% in Feb
  • The good news is overseas Filipinos are still more than 6x of pre-Covid levels, up 10% MoM
    • Although, this is down 20% YoY
  • Brings 1Q23 tourist arrivals to 67% of pre-Covid levels, or up 34% QoQ and more than 8x YoY  
  • Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) and Cebu Air (CEB PM) are our top tourism plays
