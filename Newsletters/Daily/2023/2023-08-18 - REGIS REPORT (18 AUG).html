REGIS REPORT

18 August 2023

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) held policy rates steady yesterday at 6.25%, despite the Fed’s hike at its last meeting. The statements from the BSP appear mildly hawkish, possibly due to the rising threat of rice prices for CPI inflation. Those counting on rate cuts will have to wait longer.

+++

On the topic of rising rice prices, President Marcos is reportedly backing a “review” the Rice Tariffication Law (RTL) in response. The most important result of the RTL (enacted in 2019) is that the government (National Food Authority or NFA) lost its monopoly over rice importation (which included the issuance of licenses for such). Prior to RTL, the NFA controlled all rice imports, and had the role of buying rice at above-market levels to be sold retail at subsidized prices. The latter role meant huge deficits for the NFA and ultimately the national government.

Since the RTL took effect, the exorbitant premium of domestic rice prices (vs international prices) has narrowed considerably (see chart, particularly the bars showing the price gap between PH and Vietnam prices). Vested interests, however, are likely to be unhappy with that and are expected to pander to “nationalist” and “food security” arguments to rationalize a return to the old system. Many continue to blame “cartels” and “smugglers” for rising prices (not appreciating the contradictions there) and want a hunt for ghosts to blame.

We argue that the opposite of government control is what’s needed. A reduction in the 35% import tariff on rice, even if just temporarily, would immediately reduce upward price pressure. Handing back a monopoly to the NFA or subsidizing rice prices will be bad on many fronts.

Puregold (PGOLD PM) held a briefing yesterday to discuss its 1H results. 2Q23 margins are under pressure and it appears they were crimped by promotions for both Puregold and S&R – the former from its annual sari-sari store convention, while the latter from rising competitive pressure from Landers. Top line momentum, however, remains healthy given healthy customer traffic across both brands. The company raised its top line growth guidance to +8-10% (from +7-8% previously), though we believe PGOLD can grow faster given the pace of store expansion this year.

Meanwhile, we are refreshing our forecasts for Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM), taking into account the recent acquisition of BPI. The underlying retail business of RRHI, for the most part, remains healthy. Despite the near-term earnings drag from the purchase of the BPI stake and wider associate losses (e.g. GoTyme), the core retail operations are expected to grow +10% CAGR from 2023-25, driven by continued store expansion and healthy SSSG, particularly for the “essentials” (i.e. Supermarkets, Drug Stores), which 75% of group sales. We raise our TP to P82/sh (+8%) and maintain our Buy rating.

For international investors >> Robinsons Retail - Steady retail operations, strong free cash flow; maintaining Buy

For Philippine-based investors >> Robinsons Retail - Steady retail operations, strong free cash flow; maintaining Buy

Both retailers, in our view, have de-rated over the past years, partly as a function of decelerating/normalizing earnings growth. We believe both companies will continue to be key agents of the growth and consolidation of the modern retail industry, which is still relatively underpenetrated, especially in areas outside Metro Manila. Both are highly cash generative, and should be able to support capex for store expansion, as well as dividend payouts. The implied dividend yields for both names is ~4%.

Former Senator Manny Villar, the principal behind Vista Land (VLL PM) and Allhome (HOME  PM), says he is planning to build two casinos after receiving the licenses from the Duterte administration. The casinos would definitely increase ‘supply’ and thus competition for the existing operators like Bloomberry (BLOOM PM). For those evaluating the possible threat to existing casinos, the execution track record of the group should be considered.

MACRO
BSP keeps policy rates steady
  • The Monetary Board decided to keep the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight reverse repurchase facility at 6.25%
    • In line with Street expectations
  • However, the signaling for policy settings remain hawkish
    • BSP Governor Remolona said that the BSP is “ready to tighten”, ruling out a rate cut at next month’s meeting
    • It is also unlikely to reduce the RRR
    • “The [policy] rates are not that elevated, it is still low enough to not be a factor in growth”
    • Monetary tightening was not a major contributor to the slower GDP growth in the second quarter but weak government spending, which are being addressed
  • Cited risks to inflation remain to the upside, with new threats emerging including a weak peso and higher rice prices
    • BSP raised its average inflation forecasts for 2023/2024 to 5.6%/3.3% (from 5.4%/2.9% previously)
    • 7M23 average inflation rate at 6.8%, with the latest inflation print in July at 4.7%

MACRO
President Marcos backs review of Rice Tariffication Law
  • President Marcos is supporting the review of the Republic Act 11203 or the Rice Tariffication Law (RTL)
    • According to Agriculture Assistant Secretary and deputy spokesman Rex Estoperez
  • RTL lifted the quantitative limits on rice trading, and placed a minimum of 35% tariff on imports
    • It also removed the various regulatory powers of the National Food Authority (NFA) from import licensing to warehouse protection
  • Agricultural groups blamed the high prices on a rice cartel among traders, importers, and millers
INFRASTRUCTURE
Government plans to monetize NAIA once new airports operate
  • Transportation Secretary Jaime Bautista bared that the government’s long-term plan is to monetize the NAIA complex once the Bulacan and Sangley airports are completed
    • Estimated proceeds are P7tn from the 70-hectare prime lot, valuing the land at P1mn per sqm
  • NAIA handled 48mn passengers in 2019
    • San Miguel’s (SMC PM) Bulacan airport is expected to have a capacity of 100mn once it completes construction by 2028
    • Sangley airport is expected to have a passenger capacity of 70mn once it operates in 2028
