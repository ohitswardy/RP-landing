REGIS REPORT

20 March 2023

San Miguel (SMC PM) issued notices to terminate two more power supply deals with Meralco (MER PM) to provide a combined 1,800MW of new capacity. Under the two PSAs which SMC won through public bidding, it had committed to deliver 1,200MW of new gas-fired capacity by Dec 2024 and another 600MW of coal-fired capacity by May 2025.

It was already looking unlikely that SMC would be able to deliver the supply by 2024/2025. The rise in the cost of fuel (coal and LNG) likely pushed SMC to put the final nail on the coffin and attempt to escape the PSAs.

This new development further highlights the looming, and now almost unavoidable, threat of power shortages ahead. We had already been warning of a probable supply crisis for a while, but with SMC choosing to renege on these two supply commitments (two years or less before it’s supposed to deliver the capacity), the situation may become even more acute.

On the results front, LT Group’s (LTG PM) FY22 profit of P25.1bn (24%YoY) fell short of estimates, dragged by the underperformance of tobacco and banking units (4Q22 profit of P4.7bn, -6%QoQ -54%YoY). The bank’s profit remained depressed as provisioning climbed upward, while contribution from its tobacco unit unexpectedly dipped 23%QoQ despite flat volumes. We suspect marketing initiatives (new products or pricing strategies) at PMFTC may have contributed to the decline, given there were no tax increases in the period.

PLDT (TEL PM) announced it was selling another 1,000 telco towers worth P12bn worth. Including the last batch (for P9bn) in Dec, PLDT has now signed deals to sell tower worth a cumulative P98bn. The cash will come in handy to reduce debt prospectively and cushion the impact of the budget overruns (P48bn) disclosed last year.

+++

Finally, we have two modestly positive items on the country’s twin deficit.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) has tweaked its FY23 expectations for the country’s current accounts deficit. It’s lowered it to US$17.1bn (from US$19.9bn), equivalent to 4.0% of GDP. 

The government also reported that it posted a budget surplus of P46b for January. While timing issues distorted the expenditure side (particularly interest payment), what can’t be denied is that tax revenues were surprisingly strong: rising 20% YoY.

MACRO
Government posts P46bn budget surplus in January
  • The government recorded a budget surplus of P46bn in January
    • Reversal of deficits in January 2022 (P23bn) and in December 2022 (P378bn)
    • Due to faster YoY, MoM increases in revenues amidst a slack in expenditures
  • Tax revenues rose 19.5% YoY and 20.3% MoM, led by sustained improvements in BIR collections
  • Expenditures barely grew to P302bn due to a 28% YoY drop in interest payments (base effects: FX interest payment due in Feb 2022 was paid in Jan 2022)
    • Primary spending rose 8.3% YoY, outperforming the government’s full-year projection (-1.3%)
    • Net of interest payments, the primary surplus rose two-fold YoY to P92.7bn from last year’s surplus of P42.2bn

MACRO
BSP expects lower BOP deficit this year
  • The BSP is looking at a narrower BOP deficit of US$1.6bn in 2023 from US$7.3bn in 2022
    • This is also narrower than its previous projection of a US$5.4bn deficit
    • Cited higher expectations of FDI, BPO revenues, tourism/travel-related accounts, as well as easing global fuel prices

TELCOS
PLDT sells another batch of towers

    • Frontier Tower is a subsidiary of Pinnacle Towers, which is backed by KKR

      • P42bn for 2,973 towers to edotco Group in April 2022

POWER
San Miguel terminates agreements with Meralco for prospective power deals
  • San Miguel Corp (SMC PM), through its two subsidiaries, terminated their power supply agreements with Meralco for period beginning 2024 and 2025
    • SMC was locked in at low prices for the two deals for a combined 1,800MW (around P4.2/kwh)
    • Excellent Energy (EERI) issued a termination notice to Meralco with regard to its 1,200 supply contract
    • Masinloc Power Partners (MPPCL) did the same for its 600Mw contract
  • The termination of the agreements will cast a greater shadow on the future of power supply in Luzon
    • The addition of the power supply, even if delayed and inadequate, would be crucial to the grid
  • In theory, Meralco (MER PM) can choose to sue and claim damages but it’s unclear whether it will do given previous actions
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of the Philippine market drops in February
  • We estimate that the proportion of shares held by foreigners fell 28bps MoM to 20.4% while foreign ownership of the PSE Index fell 1.14 percentage points MoM to 23.8% (in February)
    • The much larger drop in PSEi foreign ownership was due to the index rebalancing – RLC and MEG were replaced by DMC and UBP, which have lower foreign ownership
  • Market net foreign selling amounted to US$142mn in February (from US$122mn of net foreign buying in January)
  • Of the 28 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 14 saw declines in foreign ownership while 14 experienced increases
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • EMI – saw the biggest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 3.32 percentage points MoM to 19.9%. EMI’s parent, Alliance Global, transferred some of its EMI shares from the Singapore Exchange to the Philippine Stock Exchange. 
    • GTCAP – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership, which rose 1.10 percentage points to 17.70%. Although net foreign buying only amounted to US$6.2mn in February, it appears that the foreign buying during the last few trading days of January was only registered in the February foreign ownership.
    • BDO – foreign ownership rose 44 basis points to 25.6%. It has been the top foreign-bought stock for four consecutive months. Net foreign buying amounted to US$31.1mn in February.
