REGIS REPORT

17 August 2023

Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed yesterday that it was issuing 1.59bn new shares to an unnamed new third-party investor. The 1.59bn new shares would represent 10.1% of the enlarged capital (15.625bn shares) of DITO. Issue price for the new stock is P1/share.

This was followed by another disclosure this morning that DITO is issuing another 600m new shares to yet another investor (also unnamed). This would further increase the share count of DITO to 16.225bn.

It’s unclear how DITO plans to deploy the cash it raises (P2.2bn or US$40m based on these two disclosures). It certainly won’t be nearly enough for the telecom venture. DITO is the holding company that controls 54% of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel), the newest mobile operator. With over P200bn of interest-bearing liabilities and a negative capital position, DitoTel will need significant amounts of cash to recapitalize and continue operations. By law, China Telecom, which owns 40% of DitoTel, is not allowed to buy even a single more share in DitoTel, so an infusion from China Telecom does not look likely.

DITO, however, also has small IT-related businesses outside of DitoTel. That kind of funding would go a long way for those businesses.

One complicating factor for any party seeking to buy into DITO is that it is the controlling shareholder of an entity (DitoTel) with a franchise granted by Congress. Such franchises typically require approval from Congress for a change in control.

The Villar group is supposedly seeking to take over the operations and expansion of the LRT-1. Recall that Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and Ayala Corp (AC PM) jointly took over the operations of LRT-1 after it was privatized by the government in 2014. It’s not a secret that AC wants to dispose of its interest in the project as part of its US$1bn disposal plans.

We have details from the earnings briefings of two more names today:

Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) (+) – SSS grew 16% in 2Q23 as the momentum from the recovery in dine-in sales continued. Management remains optimistic given trends in early 3Q and raised its top line and profit growth guidance to 30%, from +20% previously. While certain pandemic-induced behaviors (e.g. hybrid work) which have affected average foot traffic may persist, management has set out other strategic initiatives to drive store sales. Prices and supply of key raw materials (e.g. potatoes) have also been locked in for the rest of the year.

Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (-) – Residential sales showed remarkable improvement, with 2Q23 reservation sales rising 22% YoY to P5.8bn, 20% above pre-COVID levels. TELCOS
Dito CME issues new shares to unnamed investor
  • Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that it issued 1.59bn new shares to a new “third party” investor
  • The new shares were priced at P1/share, less than half the current share price. This implies DITO is raising P1.59bn (~US$29m) in capital
  • DITO’s outstanding shares would rise from 14.035bn to 15.625bn and the unnamed investor would own 10.1% of the share capital
  • DITO owns 54% of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel), the newest mobile operator in the country which is 40% owned by China Telecom
  • The funds raised are nowhere near enough to help fund DitoTel
  • DitoTel has a congressional franchise (license) to offer telecom services. Such franchises typically require approval from Congress before a change in control.
INFRASTRUCTURE
Villar proposes to take over a portion of LRT-1 Cavite extension project
  • Prime Asset Ventures Inc. (PAVI) has proposed to take over the next phase of the LRT-1 Cavite extension project
    • Proposal is to take over the remaining segments of the extension (Sucat to Molino Boulevard  to Governor’s Drive in Cavite)
    • PAVI also plans to extend it further to Silang, Cavite long term
    • PAVI is the infrastructure arm of the Villar family, the owner of listed company Vista Land (VLL PM)
  • Currently, Light Rail Manila Corp (LRMC) is building phase 1 covering the first seven (7) km of the extension (five stations: from Redemptorist to Sucat)
    • Phase 1 is currently 88% complete and is expected to be completed by 4Q24
    • The remaining phases (4km) will cover the construction of the Las Piñas, Zapote, and Niog stations, which have not yet started because of right-of-way issues
  • LRMC holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 rail
    • LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (MPI PM, 36%), Ayala Corp (AC PM, 35%), Sumitomo (19%), and Macquarie Infra (10%)
