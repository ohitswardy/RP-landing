REGIS REPORT

07 August 2023

Universal Robina’s (URC PM) 1H net profit (+7% to P6.7bn) is tracking mildly behind estimates, though we expect operations to improve going in 2H. Issues arising from an ERP system transition earlier this year continued to weigh on 2Q, particularly for the domestic branded consumer food (BCF) segment. Order fill rates, however, are improving MoM (now at c80% from 60% in early-1Q), and we expect URC to put this behind it soon.

Despite the relatively soft top line, the margin recovery story is playing out. Operating margin for the domestic BCF was stable YoY, despite cost pressure from certain raw materials (e.g. coffee, potatoes, sugar), while the int’l BCF saw a more meaningful expansion in EBIT margins (+130bps YoY) due to operating leverage and other opex initiatives. Headline inflation came in at 4.7% for the month of July, slowing further from June (+5.4%) due to base effects from lower energy prices. Rice prices, however, have been rising MoM/YoY and can eventually become an issue. 

The moderating trend of inflation should give reason for the BSP not to adjust policy rates next week. 

+ + +

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said it would review all Manila Bay reclamation projects, citing that individual projects were approved without considering the combined impact on the environment. This could disrupt several reclamation projects, including: the Gatchalian Group’s 318-hectare Manila Waterfront City, Ulticon Builders’ 260-hectare Pasay Harbor City, as well as SM Prime’s (SMPH PM) reclamation projects in Pasay and Paranaque City. While SM Prime’s 360-hectare Pasay reclamation project is ongoing, the DENR review could prevent the 300-hectare Paranaque reclamation from being approved by the national government. This would be a key downside risk for the company, since the Paranaque reclamation comprises 17% of our SMPH NAV estimate.

+ + +

Over the weekend, motoring websites reported that AC Motors, the automotive subsidiary of Ayala Corp (AC PM), is set to take over the distributorship of the BYD automotive brand in the country. Ayala’s automotive unit already distributes the Chinese auto brand Maxus in the Philippines, along with Honda, Isuzu, Volkswagen, Kia and KTM. Past initiatives in this space, however, have not exactly been impactful.  

This new partnership could potentially have the scale and headroom to ultimate turn the tide at long fledgling industrials unit of AC. BYD, which stands for Build Your Dreams, is among biggest car manufacturers in China and the top seller of EVs globally.

Speaking of AC Industrials, its chip manufacturing subsidiary Integrated Micro-Electronics (IMI PM) is selling 80%-owned STI Enterprises to a PE firm for 1/10th of its book value and will report a loss of $84mn in the process. While the divestment highlights the scale of the Ayala unit’s underperformance in this space, it should nevertheless be viewed as a welcome exit.

MACRO
Inflation eases further to 4.7% in July
  • Headline CPI rose 4.7% YoY and 0.1% MoM in July, easing from June (+5.4% YoY, +0.2% MoM)
    • Came in below Bloomberg consensus estimate of 4.9% but at the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.1%-4.9%
    • Energy-related base effects continued to be the main driver for the YoY deceleration, with transport CPI down 4.6% YoY
    • Rice prices continued to creep up MoM, up +0.9% in July from just +0.5% in June, pushing overall food CPI up 0.5% MoM in July (vs. +0.3% in June)
  • Core inflation rose at a slower rate of 6.7% in July from 7.4% in June
    • Non-food core CPI was up just +0.3% MoM and eased to +5.4% YoY
    • Note that the minimum wage increase for NCR (+7%) was implemented on July 16
  • Brings 7M23 average inflation rate to 6.8%
    • The BSP retained its view that inflation will decelerate back to its 2%-4% target band next quarter
    • BSP cited higher wages and lingering food supply constraints, recent storms, and higher transport costs as key risks to inflation
  • The BSP said that it “stands ready to adjust the monetary policy stance as necessary to prevent the further broadening of price pressures…”
    • Note that the gap between the BSP’s policy rate and the Fed’s policy rate has narrowed to 100bps, after the BSP paused rate hikes while the Fed hiked rates by 50bps since May 2023
    • The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 17; Fed’s next policy meeting is on Sep 21

PROPERTY
DENR to review all Manila Bay reclamation projects 
  • The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said that it would review all Manila Bay reclamation projects
  • Early last week, the US embassy in the Philippines cited its concerns that a reclamation project in Manila Bay had China Communication Construction Co. (1800 HK) as its contractor
    • The said project was the 318-hectare Manila Waterfront City reclamation project of the Gatchalian group
    • The World Bank and Asian Development Bank have said that CCCC engages in fraudulent practices
  • SM Prime’s reclamation contractor is China Harbour Engineering Company and the company has not been asked to suspend its reclamation work
  • The Manila Bay reclamation projects with the Pasay and Paranaque city governments comprise 37% of our SMPH NAV estimate
    • Pasay reclamation – 20%, Paranaque reclamation – 17%
  • The Pasay reclamation comprises 360 hectares, 60 hectares of which, has been completed
  • The 300-hectare Paranaque reclamation project has not received approval from the national government
  • SMPH is trading at 30% discount to NAV, 2.5X FY23E P/B, and 26.1X FY23E P/E

AP to invest in 102MW wind farm in Rizal and Laguna
  • Aboitiz Renewables (ARI), the renewable energy subsidiary of Aboitiz Power Corp, will invest in a 102-MW wind power project of Singapore-based Vena Energy
  • The project is a wind farm that straddles the provinces of Rizal and Laguna
  • It is expected to reach financial close in 2024 and commercial operations in 2025
  • AP is targeting a 50:50 renewable to thermal capacity split by 2030 by building 3,700MW of renewable energy
  • Vena Energy has a total operational capacity of 2.7GW in onshore, offshore and battery assets in Asia Pacific
    • Vena Energy operates 5 solar plants and a wind farm in the Philippines with a combined capacity of 330MW
