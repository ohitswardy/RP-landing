REGIS REPORT

26 April 2023

After weeks of keeping the public in suspense, the government announced yesterday that the deadline for registration of all SIMs has been extended by 90 days.   The extension would give consumers more time to register their SIMs (now up to July 25).

For the telcos, the extension will reduce some of the short-term disruption arising from the involuntary deactivation of “live” SIMs, including on revenue.  A large chunk of the 160m+ SIMs officially active are actually inactive – as much as 30%.  This compares with the close to ~50% that have yet to register as of yesterday.

While the SIM registration will likely be revenue-neutral over the longer term, we expect a short-term impact.  The 90-day extension, however, should reduce this potential short-term revenue impact on the likes of PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM).

Union Bank’s (UBP PM) 1Q23 NPAT grew 30% YoY to P3.34bn, well behind Bloomberg consensus of P18.1bn for FY23E. Note, too, that the EPS likely came in lower YoY given the two rights issues conducted in the intervening period between 1Q22 and 1Q23. Earnings seems to have been dragged by deposit costs, which surged 144bps YoY and 43bps QoQ. Also, the bank put through a large P2.14bn provision charge, representing an annualized credit cost of over 175bps. No NPL data was disclosed in the bank’s press release. It is unclear if this quarter’s provision represents a “normalized” number, or if there is some element of a “cleanup” from the acquisition. UBP will host an earnings call on Tuesday 02 May, at 3pm

+++

Electricity spot prices rose to as high as P7.68/kwh in April with the rise in power demand.  With temperatures soaring, power demand and spot prices should stay elevated (if not climb further) over the next month or so.

+++

State gaming agency PAGCOR reported gaming revenue of P16.9bn in 1Q23, suggesting a further rise (QoQ) in revenue from the privately run integrated casinos, including Solaire, owned by Bloomberry (BLOOM PM).

BANKS
UBP 1Q23 NPAT +30%, well behind consensus
  • Union Bank (UBP PM) reported 1Q23 NPAT of P3.34bn, up 30% YoY, but far lagging consensus estimates of P18.1bn for FY23. Also, recall that since 1Q22, UBP has conducted two rights issues (in 2Q22 and in 1Q23). Based on shares outstanding at the end of each period, EPS in 1Q23 would be down 16% YoY. Loan growth of 39% YoY included the consolidation of the Citi assets as of 3Q22. On a QoQ basis, loans were up 2.2%, which runs counter to the normal seasonality of a QoQ drop in loans. Deposits were up 20% YoY, but down 2.6% QoQ.
  • UBP seemed to feel the margin squeeze from intense deposit competition. NIMs were up just 5bps QoQ, despite a spike in loan yields (+108bps QoQ) with the BSP’s increasing of credit card rate ceilings in mid-Feb. Deposit costs surged 43bps QoQ, and 144bps YoY. Net interest income was up 43% YoY, due almost entirely to the Citi acquisition. The bank disclosed that of the 1Q23 NII, P3bn came from Citi. This suggests that on an apples-to-apples basis, NII was up just 5% YoY. In addition, given the P12bn rights issue in 1Q23 (late Jan), it is worth wondering what NIMs would be without the “free money” from the proceeds. To the upside, we see additional lift to NIMs from higher credit card rates, which only took effect in mid-Feb.
  • Provision expense jumped QoQ and YoY. The bank did not disclose NPL and NPL cover data.
  • The bank will host a briefing on Tuesday, 02 May, at 3pm.

TELCOS
SIM registration period extended by 90 days
  • Government extended the period to register SIMs by another 90 days
    • The deadline would have been April 26 (today)
  • Just around half of all SIMs (>160m) have been registered so far
  • However, many of these SIMs are not really active
  • Deactivation of those unable to register in time would have led to some disruption, including of telco revenue
  • The extension would likely reduce the number of SIMs that may be deactivated

 

POWER
Electricity spot prices rise in April as demand peaks
  • The Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) said the average electricity spot market price in early April rose by P1.11 per kilowatt-hour (kWh) to P7.68 as Luzon’s peak demand came in at 12,221 megawatts (MW) in April, the high for the year so far, and exceeding the 2022 peak of 12,103 MW recorded in May.
  • Peak demand in the Visayas was 2,380 MW in April, exceeding the 2022 high of 2,297 MW recorded in September.
  • Mindanao’s peak demand in April was 2,363 MW, exceeding the region’s 2022 peak of 2,167 MW recorded in June.
  • As of April 23, IEMOP said average supply was 14,612 MW against average demand of 11,033 MW. In March, average supply was 14,226 MW and average demand 10,244 MW.
  • While demand for electricity increased due to rising temperatures brought about by the summer season, the market remained stable, with ample supply levels across all grids
  • Given the current trends, especially with El Niño, the probability of prices remaining at the P7 per kWh level in May is high - IEMOP

GAMING
PAGCOR doubles profit in 1Q23
  • Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) reported a profit of P1.34bn in 1Q23, a 97% increase YoY
  • Gaming revenues in 1Q23 surged 50%YoY to P16.88bn
    • Income from gaming operations showed a 6% rise QoQ vs P16bn in 4Q22
    • PAGCOR derives income from gaming operations from its own casinos, fees from licensed operators and other operations (bingo, e-games, POGO)
    • The figure is about 15% off pre-pandemic levels but has been on a strong upward trajectory since 2Q22
  • Barring any outliers, the PAGCOR figures potentially preview sustained QoQ growth for the integrated resorts vs a seasonally strong 4Q
