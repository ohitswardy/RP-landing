REGIS REPORT

15 November 2023

With many of the listed subsidiaries already done reporting, it’s now the turn of some of the conglomerate holding companies.  We have three of them in our results round-up today.

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – Net income for Ayala Corp came in at P32bn, +35% higher YoY.  While it’s trending below our official FY23 forecast, it’s looking comfortably ahead of consensus estimates. The two biggest units combined delivered earnings slightly better than expected.  Earnings-wise, outside of the listed units, the highlight was the modest profit posted by AC Industrials.  The briefing held yesterday also provided some interesting nuggets on a number of group initiatives.
  • Alliance Global (AGI PM) (-) – A hefty P1.4bn expense at the parent level weighed down an already soft showing from its liquor (-7%YoY) and gaming (a net loss of -P13mn) units. For Travellers in particular, VIP GGR fell 22% due to a lower win rate while overall gaming volumes declined 12%. Meanwhile, Golden Arches’ SSSG in 3Q23 remained healthy at 14.8%, albeit decelerating slightly from the previous quarter.

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – The QoQ contraction in Toyota’s 3Q23 profit was largely attributed to FX and higher promotional/marketing expenses. Despite this, 9M23 core profit for Toyota is trending well ahead of forecast. Most of GTCAP’s segments reported better-than-expected 9M results, with only Metrobank . We reiterate our BUY recommendation. The stock is trading at a 50% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,095/sh and 5.4x/5.0x FY23E/FY24E PER.

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (+) – Our key takeaway from yesterday’s briefing was management’s positive outlook on fares, despite ongoing supply challenges. Forward bookings for 4Q23/1Q24 are trending above pre-Covid levels. Even though management cut its 2024 seat growth guidance to +5-8%, competitive dynamics remain very favorable, suggesting fares could be stronger than we are anticipating.

  • Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) (+) – 3Q23 net profit grew 26% YoY, bringing the 9M figure to P746mn, ahead of expectations. SSSG remained healthy at +8% in 3Q, albeit easing from the previous quarters. Margins were supported by price increases amidst higher input cost. More detail after today’s briefing.

For Philippine Seven (SEVN PM), profits there for 9M23 jumped 72% YoY to P2.3bn, surpassing FY22 net profit (P2.1bn). For 3Q alone, net profit grew 69% YoY on operating leverage, boosted by financial leverage. SSSG during the quarter accelerated to +13% on higher same-store traffic, though baskets were flattish amidst downtrading, much like the rest of the sector.

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp 9M income rises 35% to P32bn
  • 9M net income up 35% to P32bn.  Net income for Ayala Corp (AC PM) rose 35% to P32.3bn for 9M23. Core income, as reported by AC, jumped 42% to P30.9bn.  Either of those two numbers would appear to be trending comfortably ahead of consensus estimate of P39bn for the year. Profit results for the two biggest units (BPI and ALI) came in ahead of expectations for 9M, while Globe underperformed slightly. ACEN, on the other hand, booked re-measurement gains to offset weak operating results.

 

  • Losses at new ventures, Health and Logistics, continue.  AC Health posted a loss of P304m while Logistics had a loss of P974m for 9M.  Those are roughly in line with their respective trends in 1H/2Q.  We are more positive about the outlook for Health (vs Logistics) at this point given its visible expansion pipeline.
  • AC Industrials profitable in 3Q? AC Industrials was uncharacteristically profitable in 3Q (P245m) after the write-down and divestment of two subsidiaries (STI and MTC-Con).  For the 9M, the loss at AC Industrials was still at P4.8bn – from both operations and asset write-downs. AC Motors reported income of P386m for the 9M.
  • Ayala Land to halve land bank? Among the most interesting highlights of the call was AC’s revelation that subsidiary ALI plans to halve its land bank in five years.  Given the massive size of ALI’s landbank (>10,000 hectares), we can imagine why it would seek to do so (to improve asset turns/utilization). But the sheer size also means reducing it by half will not be easy.  Aggressive actions need to be taken to achieve that goal, if indeed it is the plan.
  • Divestment target likely only by next year. AC aimed to raise US$1bn (P50bn at that time) from the sale of various non-core assets by 2023 but has so far only raised P37bn. The amount includes the last two divestments (the MCX toll road  and partial sale of Manila Water). It is unlikely that AC will be able to reach P50bn (or US$1bn) by year-end but management says it is expecting to do so sometime next year.

RETAIL
Philippine Seven 9M23 NPAT +72% YoY to P2.3bn
  • Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) reported net profit of P2.3bn in 9M23, up 72% YoY. Revenues rose 26% YoY to P55.8bn while operating profit jumped 47% YoY to P3.4bn
  • For 3Q23 alone, revenues grew 22% YoY on the back of 13% SSSG
    • Same store traffic grew 12% but average baskets were flattish – this suggests downtrading
    • Trends continue to hold up in early-4Q, with foot traffic boosted by the recent Nov holidays
    • Average store traffic is still ~20% below pre-pandemic, but trending higher vs 2022
  • 3Q operating profit jumped 41% YoY due to operating leverage. The bottomline growth (+69% YoY to P720mn) was magnified further by financial leverage.

 

BANKS
More from BPI’s 9M23 FS

BPI’s just released their FS. The main insight vs the PR/briefing was better granularity on the drivers of NIM expansion.

  • On our numbers, NIMs improved 8bps QoQ and 40bps YoY to 4.13% in 3Q23. This was in line with peers (MBT +9bps QoQ, and BDO +6bps QoQ).
  • Loan yields jumped 23bps QoQ and 168bps YoY, driven by both higher policy rates, as well as better loan mix. Recall that while overall loans grew just 8.8%, consumer loans were up 20% YoY in 3Q23.
  • Overall asset yields were up slightly more, +26bps QoQ. This indicates faster improvement in yields of non-loans. The bank has built up and termed out its investment book, though to a much slower pace than its peers. BPI’s fixed-income portfolio was 22% of its total assets, while BDO’s was 21, and MBT’s was 36%.
  • Deposit costs were up 21bps QoQ, as CASA ratio dipped below the 70% level. While deposits grew 1.3% QoQ, TDs were up 4.4% QoQ. Overall funding costs were also up 21bps QoQ.
