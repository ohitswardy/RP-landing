REGIS REPORT

19 October 2023

The speculation that President Marcos will be replacing Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno has emerged in mainstream media. Talk has been swirling about Diokno’s replacement for weeks, but one mainstream outlet finally saw fit to dignify and publish such talk. There’s been neither confirmation nor denial yet from Malacañang Palace.

Diokno appears to have struggled to get his proposals accepted or promoted by President Marcos or the government in general. No congressman has sponsored his tax proposals on junk food and single use plastics. His (1) suggestion to cut rice tariffs and (2) opposition to the rice price caps were all but ignored. The government has also been underspending, notwithstanding his vehement denouncement of a previous government’s failure to spend adequately.

The bruited replacement for Diokno is Rep Ralph Recto, who was previously senator. One of Recto’s claims to fame is that he was a key proponent of the expansion of the VAT (passed in 2004), for which he paid a high political price, losing his reelection bid. His other claim to fame is that he’s married to one of the most popular actresses in the country, one who also happened to have been a very successful local government official.

+++

The government has reportedly signed a deal with India to import 295,000 MT of rice. The deal is an exception to the rice export ban imposed by the Indian government. For perspective, the full-year rice shortfall in the Philippines is expected to be close to 4m MT (roughly 25% of consumption).

Meanwhile, international rice prices remain elevated, with Vietnam FOB export prices still over US$600/MT (P34/kg). This is before shipping costs, a 35% tariff and distribution/retail margins.

+++

Foreign ownership of the Philippine equity market continued to dip in Sept, declining 22bp for the month. Net foreign selling in the market reached US$472m following the tender offer and delisting of Metro Pacific and the deletion of Aboitiz Power (AP PM) from the FTSE.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) is putting more in the automotive space with an agreement with Bosch to open as many as 60 auto service centers. This foray is not likely to, to use what is now a cliché, “move the needle”.

MACRO
Media speculates that the Finance Secretary will be replaced
  • According to the Philippine Daily Inquirer’s “Biz Buzz” section, Ralph Recto might replace Benjamin Diokno as Finance Secretary
    • Mr. Recto is a House Deputy speaker and a former three-term senator
    • He holds a bachelor’s degree in commerce major in business management from De La Salle University
    • He also earned master’s degree units in public administration from the University of the Philippines and took up a leadership course at Harvard University’s John F. Kennedy School of Government   
  • Mr. Diokno might take the last unfilled slot in the Monetary Board
MACRO
PH to import 295k MT of non-basmati rice from India
  • The Indian government has approved the exportation of more than 1mn MT of non-basmati white rice to several countries, including the Philippines
  • Allocation for the Philippines is 295k MT, which is <10% of the projected import requirement of 3.1-3.5mn MT for the year
  • Based on data from the Bureau of Plant Industry, the Phils has imported 2.7mn MT of rice as of Oct 12, of which ~90% is from Vietnam
    • Vietnam export FOB prices remain elevated at ~USD600mn/MT
  • Last month, Pres. Marcos imposed a price cap on retail prices of regular-milled and well-milled rice. The cap was lifted on Oct 6

INDUSTRIALS
AC Motors, Bosch to open more PHL service centers
  • Ayala Corp’s AC Motors has tied up with German engineering firm Bosch in a deal targeting the opening of 60 service facilities in the next five years, with the immediate plan of opening 20 facilities within the first year
  • Currently, 12,000 Bosch Car Service centers are spread across 150 countries, with 12 of them in the Philippines
  • Bosch Car Service is said to offer one-stop-shop services covering mechanical repairs to electronic, engine systems, diagnostics and others
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market declines in September
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 22bps MoM to 20.3%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 4bps MoM to 23.9%
    • BLOOM and CNPF replaced AP and MPI (which had low foreign ownership) in the PSE Index
  • Market net foreign selling accelerated to US$472mn from US$131mn in August
    • This was partly due to the removal of AP from the FTSE Index as well as the tender offer of MPI. 
  • Of the 28 PSEi constituents that were not replaced, 21 saw declines in foreign ownership while 7 experienced increases
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • CNVRG – foreign ownership fell 66bps MoM to 19.1%.
    • TEL – foreign ownership declined 37bps MoM to 35.3%. After AP and MPI, TEL saw the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$15.9mn).
    • ACEN – saw the largest increase in foreign ownership, which expanded 33bps MoM to 25.5%.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership expanded 15bps MoM to 19.0%. Net foreign buying (of US$5.4mn) was also the highest in the market.
