REGIS REPORT

10 July 2023

In our latest sector report, Carl (carl.sy@regis.ph) argues for exposure to the property sector, notwithstanding macro (interest rate)and industry-specific (high office vacancies) concerns. He believes that the PPROP’s underperformance (-11% YoY vs PCOMP’s +3%) is not justified in light of the operational rebound of the bigger segments (mall and residential development).

Mall profitability is now back to pre-COVID levels and the anticipated decline in interest rates should lead developers to spin-off their mall assets into REITs, which in turn could result in NAV increases of up to 20% for some companies. Meanwhile, residential prices continue to recover, with reservation sales hitting a record level in 1Q23.

Although we forecast the office segment to worsen, with vacancy rising and rents falling, we note that this segment only comprises a small portion of the NAV of the large caps. Moreover, office leasing operations of some developers are not performing as poorly as the industry at large.

Robinsons Land (RLC PM) is our top pick in the sector. Not only do we forecast it to show the strongest earnings growth in the sector (relative to 2019), but we also expect it to be the biggest beneficiary of a mall REIT spinoff. Furthermore, we consider current valuations to be compelling (66% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 6.2X FY24E P/E)

Among the large caps, we prefer Ayala Land (ALI PM) due to our strong growth projections over the next three years (15% presales CAGR and 19% earnings CAGR). It should also see NAV enhancement thanks to construction of infrastructure projects adjacent to its land bank. The stock is also trading close to historical trough valuations (at 53% discount to NAV, 1.2X FY24E P/B, and 13.1X FY24E P/E).

We also upgrade SM Prime (SMPH PM) to Buy, as we forecast 16% earnings CAGR (until 2025E) following record results for mall revenue and residential presales.

The banking sector’s asset quality metrics deteriorated (if just slightly) again in May. System-wide NPL ration (ex-interbank) inched up 4bps MoM to 3.55% from 3.51% in April. In absolute terms, NPLs were up 1.9% MoM. The pace of NPL growth, though, slowed from the 9bps MoM recorded in April. Past Due (PD) ratio went up by a similar 4bps MoM to 4.17% in May from 4.13% in April. To the upside, Restructured Loans (RL) improved, down 8bps MoM to 2.46%.

We have been keeping an eye on asset quality for a few months now, citing it as a key risk to the sector in our recent report “Another leg up” in June. With this latest data, NPLs are now fully 30bps higher than they were at end-2022. However, we also noted that the Big Three banks had extremely high NPL coverage at 167-189% as of 1Q23, giving them ample cushion. We remain positive on the banks but maintain a close watch on NPL/PD ratios.

+++

The unplanned shutdown relating to one unit of Semirara’s (SCC PM) Calaca plant will take until end of Aug. This will take out 135MW of SCC’s generation capacity, around 20% of its total.

In other power sector news, First Gen (FGEN PM) signed an agreement for the delivery of its first LNG cargo. The delivery and subsequent use of that LNG would mark the initial operation of FGEN’s LNG terminal. The terminal would allow FGEN to run ~2,000MW of its generation assets even in the absence of gas from Malampaya.

GIR dips to US$99.8bn in June
  • The country’s dollar reserves (GIR) dipped to US$99.8bn in end-June from US$100.6bn the previous month (revised from US$100.3bn)
    • This is also the first time in four months that the GIR level went below US$100bn

  • End-June level is sufficient to cover 7.6months’ worth of imports
  • The BSP expects dollar reserves to hit U$100bn by year-end

BANKS
Banks’ asset quality ratios deteriorate further in May
  • NPL ratio (ex-interbank) rose to 3.55% in May from 3.51% in April and 3.26% in end-Dec 2022
    • In nominal terms, NPLs rose 1.9% MoM, slower than +3.1% in April
  • Past due ratio rose to 4.17% from 4.13% in April and 3.8% in end-Dec 2022
    • Past due loans rose 1.3% MoM to P525bn in May
    • vs. end-Dec 2022: P479bn
  • Restructured loan ratio improved to 2.46% in May from 2.58% in April
  • NPL cover dipped to 101.8% in May from 103.3% in April
  • The NPL data seems at odds with the commentary from management of banks we cover.
    • During their respective 1Q23 briefings, all four banks suggested that asset quality ratios were steady into 2Q23, and all claimed that there was no meaningful stress among borrowers.
    • Likely due to consumer loans, higher yielding/higher NPLs, are growing faster than total loans
    • As of 1Q23, consumer loans grew 17% YoY (vs. +10.5% for overall banking sector)
    • Consumer loan NPLs dipped 15bps QoQ to 6.39% from 6.54% in end-2022

POWER
Semirara’s Unit 3 extends power outage
  • Southwest Luzon Power Generation Corp. (SLPGC) announced it is extending the unplanned outage of Unit 1
    • Unit 1 has been on unplanned outage since June 17 due to turbine tripping
    • Unit 1 has a net selling capacity of 135MW vs. SCC’s total net selling capacity of 660MW
  • The company is still evaluating the cause of the excessive turbine movement
    • Repair and work are targeted before the end of August
    • Insurance brokers and adjusters of SLPGC have been informed of the matter and are currently working with Plant Management to process the filing of the insurance claims
    • Coordination with power industry regulators and other key stakeholders are likewise underway
  • We estimate SLPGC Unit 1’s cumulative outage days by end-August could reach 86 days
    • Assuming Unit 1 goes on planned outage for 40 days by year-end, the plant availability factor could fall to 66% (vs. forecast: 68% or total outage days of 120 for the year)
    • With the outage, SCC’s power division’s net selling capacity to the spot market will go down to 337MW (64%) from 472MW (71%) previously
  • The two 135MW units of SLPGC account for 3%/5% of FY23E profit/EBITDA

FGEN to buy first LNG cargo, to commission FSRU in September
  • First Gen Corp said it decided to award the tender to procure a single cargo of liquefied natural gas to Shell Eastern Trading (Pte) Ltd. For delivery between August 1 and September 30
  • The floating storage and regasification unit in Batangas is set to commission in September
  • In June, FGEN announced that it was in talks with Enrique Razon’s Prime Infra for the lease and operation of its LNG storage and regasification terminal that is construction
    • No definitive terms have been set
