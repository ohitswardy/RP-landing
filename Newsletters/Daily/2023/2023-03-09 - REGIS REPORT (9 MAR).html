REGIS REPORT

09 March 2023

It’s one of those good news / bad news days for Philippine banks.

First the bad news – Asset quality metrics deteriorated in January, with system-wide NPL ratio up 13bps MoM to 3.38%, and Past Due (PD) ratio up 22bps MoM to 4.01%. This is the first significant increase in both since early 2022. It is tempting to interpret the data as evidence of stress, as a result of sharply higher interest rates. However, we note that there is surprising seasonality to NPLs/PDs. Since 2009 (at least), both ratios have increased in January vs December. Every single year. In fact, the most recent MoM change is arguably “normal”, or even slightly lower than average. We would caution against drawing firm conclusions from the recent print, and would wait for more data points to distinguish the signal from the noise.

Now the good news – Consumer loan growth accelerated further to +15.4% YoY in 4Q22, despite a slowdown in mortgages (+5.3% vs +8.6% in 3Q). Motor vehicle loans, while still down 2.9% YoY, are already up 0.8% QoQ; we expect these to turn YoY positive in 1Q23. Meanwhile, 4Q22 saw a massive surge in credit card receivables (+39% YoY) and salary loans (+62% YoY). It is unclear whether this is due to banks’ increased willingness to lend, or to consumers needing to borrow more to cope with rising costs. For now we’ll take the positive view, and see this as potential for further NIM expansion into 2023. Consumer loans now account for 19.3% of total loans, from 17.8% at end-2021.

Meanwhile, Ayala (AC PM) group power firm ACEN (ACEN PM) reported a FY22 net income of P13.1bn, which included an P8.6bn net gain from accounting adjustments and provisions. The one-time adjustments include 1) revaluation gains on the acquisition of the Australia platform, 2) provisions for a Supreme Court decision voiding the PEMC’s administered/regulated pricing, and 3) a provision for its Lac Hoa & Hoa Dong Wind project in Vietnam.

Excluding these, we estimate that core net income for the FY22 was P4.5bn, down 14% - well below estimates (P6.3bn consensus, even vs Regis of P5.6bn). That would imply that 4Q22 net profit dropped to P379mn (-80%QoQ, -61%YoY) vs P1.9bn in 3Q and P1.8bn in 2Q.

BANKS
Consumer loans grow 15.4% YoY in 4Q22; NPLs improve sharply QoQ
  • Consumer loans in the Philippine banking system grew 15.4% YoY to P2,234bn in 4Q22, the fastest clip since early in the pandemic in 1Q20. This was despite a slowdown in mortgages, which was more than compensated for by a surge in credit card receivables, and in salary loans.  Crucially, consumer loan NPLs improved further, falling 65bps QoQ to 6.54% in 4Q22 compared to 7.19% in 3Q22. Delinquencies improved across nearly every category.
  • Mortgages grew 5.3% YoY to P938bn, slowing from the 8.6% pace in 3Q22, but were still up 1.2% QoQ. We suspect part of the apparent weakness is due to banks not buying CTS portfolios (receivables) off of residential developers. Mortgage NPLs improved further to 7.55%, down 37bps QoQ. That said, NPLs for the segment is still well above the pre-Covid levels of around 4%.
  • Motor Vehicle (MV) loans were still down 2.9% YoY to P441bn. However, this was a vast improvement over the 8.2% contraction in 3Q22. On a QoQ basis, MV loans finally turned positive (+0.8%). We expect the YoY figure to turn positive in 1Q23. MV NPLs fell sharply, down 87bps QoQ to 8.44%.
  • Credit card receivables (CCRs) surged 38.6% YoY to P555bn, accelerating sharply from the 25.5% posted in 3Q22. CCRs were up 9.4% QoQ. NPLs for the segment dropped to 3.88%, 114bps lower QoQ, due likely to aggressive write-offs by banks.
  • Salary loans exploded 61.7% YoY to P264bn, faster than the 52.4% clip recorded in 3Q22. Meanwhile, NPLs edged up to 4.91%, up 22bps from the level in 3Q22.
  • Anecdotally, banks all say that they have been loosening credit to the consumer sector, and expect the growth to continue to accelerate into 2023, particularly in the mortgage and MV segments. We are wary, however, of the sharply rising interest rates, and their impact on NPLs, particularly for mortgages and for credit cards. Mortgages re-price annually, and are typically indexed to the 1-year Treasury rate, which is up c400bps in line with policy rates. For credit cards, the BSP recently raised the ceiling on monthly interest rates on unpaid balances from 2.0% to 3.0% per month. That 50% increase is bound to cause some sticker shock when the next billings arrive.

POWER/UTILITIES
Meralco deferring pass-through rate increases
  • Meralco is deferring the collection of P1.1bn of generation costs from Feb
    • Move is being done to cushion the impact of higher generation costs
  • The higher charges will be collected in March and April instead
  • Meralco estimated the generation rates would have been P0.92/kwh higher in Feb if the charges were not deferred
    • After adjusting for taxes and others, the net increase would have been P1.11/kwh (> 10% of end-user tariffs)
PROPERTY
AREIT and Ayala Land to conduct new share-for-asset transaction
  • AREIT’s Board of Directors approved a share-for-asset swap with Ayala Land
  • AREIT will acquire 190,000sqm of malls and offices from Ayala Land
    • Makati offices – 113,000sqm – One Ayala Avenue East and West, Glorietta BPO buildings
    • Malls – 77,000sqm – Glorietta mall and MarQuee mall (in Angeles, Pampanga)
    • This will expand AREIT’s rental portfolio by 28% to 863,000sqm
  • The properties have a combined occupancy rate of 99% and a WALE of 14.5 years
    • This likely means that similar to previous mall transactions, the mall space is leased to ALI and it subleases to tenants
  • The properties are valued at P22.48bn
    • AREIT’s AUM will grow 36% to P87bn
  • AREIT will issue 607.6mn AREIT shares to Ayala Land
    • The AREIT shares would effectively be priced at P37/share
    • AREIT’s shares outstanding will expand 33%
  • We estimate that ALI’s ownership in AREIT would rise from 64% to 73%
    • Thus, ALI would have to sell about 139mn AREIT shares (P4.9bn at yesterday’s closing price)
  • AREIT currently offers a dividend yield of 6.9%
    • Based on consensus forecast
    • The interest rate on the 10-year government bond is 6.4%
  • ALI is trading at 43% discount to NAV, 1.6X FY23E P/B, 17.9X FY23E P/E
    • We expect this transaction to have minimal impact on Ayala Land
