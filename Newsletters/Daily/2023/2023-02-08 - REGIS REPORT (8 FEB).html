REGIS REPORT

08 February 2023

Globe (GLO PM) issued a press release announcing FY22 results yesterday. While headline profit rose sharply to P34.6bn, core income was actually disappointing, barely up (+1% YoY) at P19.2bn.

Mobile revenue was up +3% YoY for both 4Q and the full year. Broadband revenue, though, was still lower YoY with attrition of fixed wireless subscribers. Notably as well, wired broadband subscribers ended the year at 1.128m, implying a slight contraction in the subscriber base.

Globe will hold its briefing tomorrow morning (Feb 9) and we should get more detail then.

+++

In this latest report on the property sector, Carl (carl.sy@regis.ph) highlights that the three main property segments are in various stages of recover (or lack thereof): 1) mall profitability is approaching pre-COVID levels, 2) the residential segment has begun to bottom out, while 3) the office segment is likely to see worse days ahead.

Malls are already showing a sharp rebound and we forecast mall revenue to grow another 30% this year. In addition, spin-offs of mall assets into REITs could lead to NAV increases of up to 15% for some developers. Meanwhile, we expect several positive trends in the residential segment to continue: rising prices, declining defaults, and improving reservation sales. On the other hand, we forecast office vacancy to rise further as work-from-home crimps demand while new supply is added.

Mid-cap Robinsons Land (RLC PM) is our top pick in the sector. Not only do we forecast it to show the strongest earnings growth in the sector (relative to 2019), but we also expect it to be the biggest beneficiary of a mall REIT spinoff.

Among the larger caps, we prefer Ayala Land (ALI PM) due to our strong growth projections over the next two years (20% presales CAGR and 25% earnings CAGR). It should also see NAV enhancement thanks to construction of infrastructure projects adjacent to its land bank.

A closer look at the Jan CPI numbers shows that the Housing and Utilities segment was also a main driver of inflation, with prices for that segment rising 1.6% MoM. With electricity prices likely to climb in the next few months, we should pressure for CPI inflation to remain elevated over the near-term.

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) confirmed it was buying 34.8% of Axelum for P5.32bn -- part of its plan to invest more in agriculture. We expect the transaction to largely earnings neutral or very mildly negative for MPI.

BPI (BPI PM) is being fined P135m (US$2.4m) by the SEC for an embarrassing oversight relating to its filing for its ESOP. The bank is appealing to get that figure down but, as it is, it’s 0.2% of annual profit.

MACRO
Inflation zooms to 8.7% in January
  • Headline CPI rose by 8.7% YoY in January, accelerating further from 8.1% YoY the previous month
    • It also exceeded consensus expectations (+7.6%) and the BSP’s forecast range (+7.5%-8.3%)
    • On a MoM basis, CPI rose by 1.7%, of which 1.0% was due to food (ie. fish, vegetables, and dairy) and 0.3% was from higher utilities (water, electricity)
  • Core inflation (ex-fuel, food) jumped to 7.4% in January from 6.9% in December
  • To reach the BSP’s and consensus’ FY23E average inflation target of 4.5%, CPI would have to drop by -0.2% MoM in succeeding months (or average 4.1% for the rest of the year)
  • To reach Regis’ FY23E average inflation target of 5.5%, CPI should be flat MoM (or average 5.4% for the rest of the year)
  • The Monetary Board will be meeting on Feb 16

TELCOS
Globe 4Q/FY22 results - initial comment

  • Core income at P19.2bn for FY22.  Core profit for Globe came in at P19.2b, barely changed YoY (+1%) and a disappointing result.  Service revenue was a mild disappointment and but it was expenses thar made a bigger difference.  EBITDA for the year came in at P79bn, up +6% with 4Q EBITDA at P18.8bn (-5% QoQ, +4% YoY).
  • Wired broadband subscribers dip in 4Q.  Wired broadband subscribers ended the year at 1.128m, which implies GLO lost 2k subs in 4Q.  This is a clear disappointment, and does not augur well for the growth outlook for the rest of the sector in 4Q.  Globe has shifted focus to prepaid fiber broadband in order to maximize usage of its network.

Meanwhile, GLO’s fixed wireless broadband subs dropped 9% to 1.43m, continuing its decline as subscribers churned out as the urgency resulting from the pandemic faded.

  • Capex at P101bn.  Capex for FY22 hit P101bn (US$1.9bn), a record high and equivalent to ~63% of revenue.  Globe expects capex to drop to US$1.3bn this year and further fall to US$1bn in 2024.
  • GCash reaches 76m users.  GCash added another 5m users in 4Q, taking its registered user base to 76m (71m in 3Q).
BANKS
BPI fined P135mn for SEC filing error for ESOP in 2013
  • BPI yesterday disclosed that the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has fined the bank P134.6mn for failing to secure the proper paperwork for its employee stock option plan (ESOP) for 2013-2019. We understand from BPI management that this was an oversight on their part. The bank will accept the penalty, but may appeal the size of the fine.
  • Here is the timeline, as we gathered from speaking with management:
    • In 2013, BPI was about to launch a new seven-year ESOP program for 2013-2019.
    • New share issues need to be registered with the SEC. For ESOPs, companies need to apply for exemption from having to register every stock issue.
    • In 2013, BPI had shares leftover from a previous ESOP. The SEC approved for the bank to use those previously-exempted shares as part of the 2013-19 plan.
    • However, when those shares ran out, BPI continued to issue ESOP shares. There were some personnel movements in BPI, and the new personnel assumed (wrongly) that the entire ESOP for 2013-19 was already approved for exemption. They thus didn’t bother applying for a new exemption from registration.
    • In 2021, BPI filed with the SEC to put a new ESOP in place. It was only then that the non-filing for exemption for the 2013-19 ESOP was discovered.
    • The SEC issued the assessment only on 03 February 2023.
  • Based on a news report, BPI claimed it was an “error in good faith”, and that it would abide by the SEC’s final decision on the penalty. In our conversations with management, the bank has formally requested for a reconsideration of the initial assessed amount. The fine will be booked in 2023.
  • For context, the 6mn fine is just 0.2% of our pre-tax profit forecast for 2023E. We believe that this was an honest – if costly – oversight on BPI’s part.
CONGLOS
MPI acquires 34.76% of Axelum Resources Corp.
  • MPI entered into a sale and purchase agreement with various shareholders of Axelum Resources Corp. (AXLM) to acquire a 34.76% economic stake in AXLM for P3.83/sh
    • MPI is acquiring 1.19bn common shares (31.3%) in AXLM for P4.05/sh payable in cash to various shareholders
    • It is also subscribing to 200mn (5.3%) redeemable preferred shares in AXLM for P2.50/sh, which can be converted to 200mn new common shares after 3 years
  • Total consideration is P5.32bn, payable in cash once regulatory approvals are secured
    • As at end-Sep 2022, MPI’s parent-level cash and debt stood at P13.1bn and P83.2bn, respectively
  • We estimate the acquisition will be mildly dilutive (<1%, or P100mn) to MPI’s profit, assuming current borrowing rates
