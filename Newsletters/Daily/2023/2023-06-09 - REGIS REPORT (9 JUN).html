REGIS REPORT

09 June 2023

Banking sector news bonanza! A RRR cut (but not really), an NPL increase, and 1Q23 consumer loan data, all in one day. Oh joy.

The BSP cut the headline RRR for large banks by 250bps from 12.0% to 9.5% effective 30 June. This is larger than the 200bps that the BSP had previously telegraphed. Smaller cuts were made for smaller banks. However, we estimate that the cut will not result in a meaningful increase in system liquidity. As we reported recently (link here), the RRR cut is meant to replace the Covid-era rule allowing banks to use MSME and other loans as “alternative compliance” for RRR. The total loans that qualified for alternative RRR ballooned from P263bn at end-2022 to P302bn as of April. Hence, the increase in the RRR cut from 200bps to 250bps was NOT out of the goodness of BSP Governor Medalla’s heart, but because the quasi-cut he had to replace got that much bigger. If the BSP did NOT cut by 250bps, then it would have resulted in a net tightening of monetary policy.

True, some banks likely used the MSME-as-RRR rule more than others. In fact, ALL banks we speak to said they used it less, ie that they would be net beneficiaries of the RRR cut. This seems counter-intuitive, or else it means that lots of other banks are net losers. In any case, we previously estimated (link here) that RRR cuts would have a surprisingly small impact on NPAT forecasts, even without the MSME offset.

The market might ignore the nuance, though, and still take it as a positive catalyst for the banks and the financial markets in general.

Banking system NPLs (ex-interbank) ticked up in April, rising 9bps MoM to 3.51% from 3.42% in March. In nominal PHP terms, NPLs jumped 3% MoM, the highest monthly increase since March 2021. Past Due (PD) loans also worsened, but at a much slower pace, up just 5bps MoM to 4.12%. To the upside, Restructured Loans (RLs) eased a tad to 2.59% from 2.60% the previous month. NPL cover slipped, though, to 103.2% from 105.2%. Loan growth (ex-interbank) slowed to 9.9% YoY from the 10.6% pace in March. The trend is slightly worrisome, if not entirely unexpected due to sharply rising interest rates.

Some insights (and some comfort), though, might come from consumer loan data that just came out for 1Q23. Consumer loans surged 17.0% YoY, faster than the 15.4% pace set at end-2022, and now account for an all-time high 20.1% of total loans. Most notably, Motor Vehicle loans grew 2.1% YoY, punching back into positive territory for the first time since 2Q20.

Meanwhile, Consumer loan NPLs fell to 6.39% from 6.54% in 4Q22. Improvements were seen across the major segments, with Mortgage NPLs at 7.46% (from 7.55%), Motor Vehicles at 7.89% (from 8.44%), and Credit Cards at 3.85% (from 3.88%). In absolute PHP terms, NPLs were up just 0.3% QoQ.

This suggests that the corporate/mid-market/SME segments were the major drivers of NPL growth, at least in 1Q23. We estimate that NPL ratio for non-consumer loans jumped 21bps QoQ to 2.68% in 1Q23 from 2.47% at end-2022.

Annoyingly, the BSP only releases Consumer loan data on a quarterly basis. So we can’t tell whether the April NPL figure saw the same trends.

The positive takeaway is that NIMs could continue to be supported – even as benchmark interest rates pause/fall – as the banks’ loan mix improves in favor of higher-yielding consumer loans.

BANKS
BSP announces 250bps RRR cut
  • The BSP will implement a 250bps reduction in the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for universal and commercial banks
    • This is larger than the 200bps that the BSP governor previously telegraphed 
  • The cut in the RRR will begin on June 30
  • The reduction in the RRR will coincide with the expiration of alternative modes of compliance with reserve requirements
    • Thus,  as we recently outlined, the actual impact to overall liquidity will be minimal, as this coincides with the lapsing of the rule allowing MSME loans as “alternative compliance” for RRR during Covid
  • The lower RRR does not constitute a shift in the BSP's monetary policy settings (to temper inflation)

 

BANKS
Consumer loans surge 17% YoY in 1Q23; NPL ratios fall across major segments
  • Consumer loans in 1Q23 grew 17.0% YoY, accelerating from the 15.4% pace recorded at end-2022. Mortgages sped up to 7.5% YoY (was 5.3%), while Motor Vehicle loans grew 2.1% YoY punching into positive growth territory for the first time since 2Q20 at the start of the Covid-19 pandemic. Credit Card receivables growth moderated to (a still rapid) 28.4% YoY, from the 38.6% clip at end-2022. Salary loans surged 62.3% YoY (from 61.7%) and Other personal loans jumped 45.1% (from 29.0%).
  • Consumer loan growth far outstripped overall banking sector loan growth of 10.5% YoY in 1Q23. As a result, Consumer loans now account for 20.1% of total loans (up from 19.3% at end-2022), to a new all-time high.
  • Asset quality improved, with overall Consumer loan NPLs dipping 15bps QoQ to 6.39% from 6.54% at end-2022. NPL Ratios improved across the three major segments, with Mortgages at 7.46% (from 7.55%), Motor Vehicle loans at 7.89% (from 8.44%), and Credit Cards at 3.85% (from 3.88%). Meanwhile, Salary loan NPLs increased to 5.12% (from 4.91%) and Other personal loans were up to 10.32% (from 10.0%).
  • The falling NPL ratios came despite sharply higher interest rates, particularly for Credit Cards, whose rate cap was raised from 2.0% to 3.0% a month from mid-February.
  • In 1Q23, total banking NPLs were up 17bps QoQ to 3.42%. We estimate that NPLs of non-consumer loans (ie corporate, mid-market, and SMEs) increased at an even faster clip, up 21bps QoQ to 2.68% (from 2.47% at end-2022).

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS
NPLs up 9bps MoM in April to 3.51% -- a loan mix issue?
  • Asset quality ratios deteriorated slightly in April. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) stood at 3.51%, blipping up 9bps MoM, and is now up 26bps ytd compared to 3.25% at end-2022. In PHP terms, NPLs jumped 3.1% MoM, the biggest monthly increase since May 2021. The Past Due (PD) ratio was up 5bps MoM to 4.12% (was 3.79% at end-2022), with the nominal amount of PD loans up 1.9% MoM. On the other hand, Restructured Loan (RL) ratio improved slightly to 2.59% from 2.60% in March and from 2.61% at end-2022. NPL cover dipped slightly to 103%, from 105% in March. Meanwhile, system loan growth slowed to 9.9% YoY in April, compared to 10.6% YoY in March, dipping back below 10% for the first time since August 2022. On a MoM basis, loan portfolios expanded 0.3%.
  • The NPL data seems at odds with the commentary from management of banks we cover. During their respective 1Q23 briefings, all four banks suggested that asset quality ratios were steady into 2Q23, and all claimed that there was no meaningful stress among borrowers.
  • One explanation might be that consumer loans – higher yield, higher NPLs – are growing faster than total loans. For banks we cover, consumer loans grew 12-18% YoY in 1Q23, compared to overall loan growth of 10.6%. In particular, credit card loans were up more than 30% YoY. By our calculations, credit card receivables grew by P35bn between end-2022 and April 2023, accounting for more than 80% of the total loan growth of P41.6bn ytd. Frustratingly, the BSP has yet to release consumer loan and NPL data even for 1Q23 (most recent is end-2022). We will verify/refute our theory when the data comes out (whenever that may be).
