Apart from the briefings of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and Globe (GLO PM), we also have fresh results from Vista Land (VLL PM) and Megawide (MWIDE PM).

  • Globe (GLO PM) (-) – Among our biggest takeaways from the Globe results call were (1) that SIMs accounting for 99% of its revenue were able to register and (2) management’s reduced guidance on revenue growth this year. A big data center also broke ground, which would increase capacity of its DC venture by >6x. More here:

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – GTCAP’s core income to common (excluding the P3.2bn after-tax gain from the land transfer) came in at P13.12bn in 1H23, up 69% YoY and on trend to beat Regis’ and consensus’ FY23E. While contributions from the auto segment dropped 22% QoQ due to FX and the timing of recognition for tax incentives, cumulative 1H23 profit for the auto unit on a standalone level was at P8.0bn (+147% YoY), tracking well ahead of our full-year forecast.

  • Vista Land (VLL PM) (=) – Operating profit expanded 19% to P6.3bn, broadly in line with REGIS forecast (but behind consensus). However, residential revenue fell YoY for the 15th consecutive quarter. Furthermore, Vista’s rental revenue remains overstated relative to cash rent. This is evidenced by the rental receivables of P26.6bn, which represents 630 days of rental revenue and is larger than the VLL market cap (of P20bn).

  • Megawide (MWIDE PM) (+) – Megawide recorded a profit of P330mn in 2Q23, reversing five quarters of net losses, after completions accelerated for the construction unit and office occupancy rates improved for the landport unit. To the downside, we estimate net debt today is c. P30bn, even after receiving all the proceeds from the sale of the Mactan Cebu airport. The company’s leverage is much higher than pre-Covid levels of P16bn. 

Finally, there are 1H results from one name we don’t cover but always attracts investor interest: Philippine Seven (SEVN PM). SEVN’s 2Q23 earnings saw a sharp jump (+47% YoY), with revenue growth (+29%) magnified by operating leverage. SSSG was +11%, and while average sales per store has already surpassed pre-pandemic levels, customer count is still ~20% below. We hope to get more detail during the post-results discussion on 18 Aug (hosted by Regis).

Remittances grow 2.1% YoY in June
  • Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.1% YoY to US$2.81bn in June
    • In PHP terms, remittances grew by 3.7% YoY, slowing from previous months
    • 3-month moving average growth rate also slowed to +2.8% YoY in June
  • Land-based remittances from Japan, UAE, and Qatar lifted overall remittances while sea-based remittances grew 1.9% YoY
  • Brings 1H23 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$15.793bn
    • The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +3% YoY this year
    • To reach this, remittances for the rest of the year should average US$2.95bn per month

Vehicle sales jump 33% YoY in July

INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide (MWIDE PM) – 2Q23: Construction back to normal; Landport unit over the hump
  • Returns to profitability in 2Q23. MWIDE reported a profit of P330mn in 2Q23, reversing five quarters of net losses. Cumulative 1H23 profit stood at P197mn, a turnaround from last year’s P397mn net loss. The results are on track to beat consensus estimates (FY23E: Net loss of P240mn).
  • Faster completions boost construction unit. Construction staged a strong recovery in 2Q23, with revenues and EBITDA rising 57% QoQ to P6.7bn/P871mn, exceeding 2019 levels. Management cited faster completions for a casino project, as well as the North-South Commuter Rail. Notably, receivable days “normalized” to 250-300 days, with management indicating it no longer expects impairment charges this year.  The order book, however, dropped 5% QoQ and 6% YoY to P50bn, just a tad below end-2019 levels of P52bn but much lower than the peak of P68bn in 2020.
  • Landport occupancy rate improves. The landport unit also saw a 12% QoQ improvement in 2Q revenues to P101mn, as office occupancy rates improved to 60% from 47%. Revenues are c. back to 70% of pre-Covid levels but management is optimistic it can achieve 100% occupancy rate by year-end, as it is now getting interest from back office operations of new casinos. This unit recorded net losses of P36mn in 2Q23 and P85mn in 1H23.
  • Leverage higher vs. pre-Covid. Net debt (including prefs) was higher by 7% QoQ at P24.6bn, after cash levels dropped 18% to P10.2bn in end-June. The company’s leverage is also higher compared to its end-2019 parent level net debt of P15.8bn. Given that the related party purchase of PH1 World Developers took effect only on July 17, we estimate net debt is even higher today at c.P30bn, after accounting for the P5.2bn purchase.
  • PH1 World to be larger than construction in 2-3 years. Management also allotted a large portion of the analyst briefing to discuss its rationale and outlook for its new real estate arm, PH1 World Developers. PH1 is a real estate company focusing on the “affordable to mid-income” segment of the market, with a mix of vertical and horizontal projects in Luzon. The acquisition is in line with the company’s strategy to diversify out of the low-margin construction business (single-digit net margin) to a higher margin business (20-25% net margin). Management expects its property unit to eclipse profit contributions from construction in 2-3 years.

The P5.2bn valuation was largely based on a DCF of ongoing projects, which will mostly be completed after 2025 and assumed a GPM of c. 50%. Reservations sales in 2022 was only P2bn but it is targeting to increase this to P8bn this year.  Its landbank is over 200 hectares and most of these are jointly owned. Funding for these projects will come from banks (e.g. BDO) but management was vague if it needs to raise equity to fund the growth of this venture.

RETAIL
Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) 1H23 net profit jumps 74% YoY
  • Philippine Seven reported net profit of P1.5bn in 1H23, up 74% YoY
  • Revenues +29% YoY to P36.6bn, while operating profit jumped 50% YoY to P2.3bn
  • For 2Q23 alone, revenues grew 23% YoY on the back of +11% SSSG
    • Average sales per store has surpassed pre-pandemic levels, but customer count is still ~20% below 2019
  • Operating profit grew at a faster pace (+34% YoY) due to operating leverage from the top line improvement
  • SEVN ended the period with 3,538 stores
    • The company plans to add 400 stores in 2023, with a medium-term target of 5,000 stores by mid-2026
