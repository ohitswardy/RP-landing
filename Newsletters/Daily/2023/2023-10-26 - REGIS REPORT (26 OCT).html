REGIS REPORT

26 October 2023

BDO (BDO PM) delivered a record-matching 3Q, with profit equaling the 2Q net income of P18.7bn. The 9M earnings remain on track to be slightly better than the full-year expectations of both Regis (P70.8bn) and the Street (P71.6bn).

At the macro level, the numbers from BDO reflect a trend that’s unsurprisingly similar to BPI’s (BPI PM). With interest rates significantly higher than last year, there’s been a marked deceleration in loan accompanying the expansion in asset yields and NIMs. Loan growth (YoY) is now down to 7.5%, while NIMs were up 6bp to 4.75%.

Additional detail below ahead of the bank’s briefing on Nov 7.

+++

The government’s budget deficit widened to P251bn in Sept (+40% YoY) as expenditures rose 8%. The widening of the deficit for the month was exaggerated by timing changes in VAT collections which led to a 12% drop in revenue.

The rise in expenditures represents a catch-up from the lag in 1H spend, which could help lift economic activity in 3Q.

+++

Seven parties have purchased bid documents for the auction of the operations of the Manila airport, NAIA. Among the seven are San Miguel (SMC PM), the “super consortium” whose members include the biggest business groups in the country, Incheon International Airport, GMR and a few others. The bidding is scheduled to happen on Dec 27.

+++

Property consultancy firm Colliers estimates that office vacancy inched up to 18.7% in 3Q, from 18.4% in 2Q. This rise in office vacancies as estimated by Colliers was primarily the result of new supply coming into the market, and is broadly in line with the assessment of other property consultants.

MACRO
Budget deficit widens in September/3Q23 as spending accelerates
  • The government recorded a budget deficit of P251bn in September 2023, up 40% YoY and 89% MoM
    • Revenues contracted by 12% YoY due to timing difference in VAT payments affecting BIR collections and lower commodity prices hitting the Bureau of Customs
    • Expenses rose 8% YoY, driven by higher interest payments and primary spending
  • In 3Q23 alone, the deficit was wider at P432bn, as expenses jumped 11% YoY while revenues grew at a slower rate of +5% YoY
    • Primary spending accelerated, up 9.4% YoY in 3Q23 (vs. -1% YoY in 1H23)
  • Brings 9M23 deficit to P984bn, down 2.9% YoY
    • Based on the government’s FY23E deficit target of P1.5tn (6.1% of GDP), the deficit will shrink to P516bn in 4Q23E from P601bn in 4Q22
    • This suggests that overall expenditures will drop by 6% YoY and primary spending will contract by 9% YoY in 4Q23E

BANKS
BDO 9M NPAT P54bn, in line; loans slow, but NIMs still gaining

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported 9M23 NPAT of P53.9bn, up 35% YoY, and tracking in line / slightly ahead of Regis estimates (FY23F P70.8bn) and consensus (P71.6bn). For 3Q23 alone, NPAT was P18.7bn, up 16% and flat QOQ. Loan growth slowed to 7.5%, but was offset by continued NIM gains, presumably on loan re-pricing and loan mix (no breakdown given yet). Opex was slightly elevated, with compensation jumping 16% YoY in 3Q23 (was +13% in 1H23). Provision expense is running higher than we forecast, with NPLs ticking up slightly QoQ to 1.99% (from 1.95% in 2Q).

Overall, a solid set of results. More detail during the briefing on Tuesday 7 November. Ahead of that, some highlights:

  • “Customer loans” grew 7.5% YoY, decelerating from the 8.4% pace in 2Q23, and below the bank’s FY guidance of 8-10%. No breakdown was given in the press release, but we suspect that corporate demand was weak. Loan yields improved 20bps QoQ (+172bps YoY) likely on a combination of continued loan re-pricing and better loan mix (ie more consumer).
  • Deposits grew 12% YoY, driven largely by time deposits (TDs), which jumped 76% YoY and 16% QoQ, which saw CASA ratio slip to 72% from 75% in 2Q. Deposit costs jumped 13bps QoQ as a result. It appears that the bank bought funds to build up its investment securities portfolio, which grew 7% QoQ (by cP60bn), largely in the FVTPL/AFS book.
  • NIMs still managed gains, up 6bps QoQ in 3Q23 (+52bps YoY) to 4.75%, though the pace seems to be flattening out.
  • Non-interest income grew 9% YoY in 9M23, though 3Q alone was up just 4% YoY. The 3Q23 was dragged by a 9% drop in Trading/forex gains due to trading losses in the quarter. Fee incomes, though, were still up 8% YoY in 9M and 6% YoY in 2Q23 alone.
  • PPOP of P80.6bn was up 28% YoY in 9M23, tracking ahead of our expectations
  • Provisions came in at P10.7bn, down 12% YoY, but were higher than we expected (FY23E P12.7bn). Credit cost stayed in the mid-50bp range in 3Q23. The bank reported NPL ratio ticked up slightly to 1.99% from 1.95% in 2Q23, while NPL Cover improved a bit to 176% (from 174%). To the upside, Restructured Loans (RLs) continued the downtrend, falling 21% YoY to P73bn, representing 2.6% of total loans (down from 2.8% in 2Q, and from 5.7% of loans at the peak during Covid).

More after the briefing next week.

NAIA airport bidding attracts seven potential bidders
  • Seven groups have so far bought bidding documents for the auction of the NAIA PPP
    • Incheon International Airport
    • San Miguel Holdings Corp.
    • Manila International Airport Consortium (AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners)

  • The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62mn annually from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years)
PROPERTY
Colliers: Metro Manila office vacancy inches up in 3Q23
  • During its 3Q23 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila office vacancy inched up to 18.7%, from 18.4% in 2Q23
    • Due to an increase in office supply
    • Vacancy rate in Fort Bonifacio and Makati was only 10%
  • Rental rates in most areas continued to fall
    • We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 20%-50% below pre-COVID levels
    • However, rental rates in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio have been recovering
  • Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to hit 21.2% by end-2023
    • Due to office buildings scheduled for completion in 4Q23
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 72% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, and 4.2X FY24E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY24E P/B, and 3.7X FY24E P/E
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY23E P/B, and 6.6X FY24E P/E
  • Meanwhile, secondary market prices of Metro Manila condominiums rose 1% QoQ
    • Colliers estimates that they have risen 4% YoY
    • This is the eighth consecutive quarter that prices have inched up
    • Prices are generally 5%-20% below 2019 levels
  • ALI is trading at 46% discount to NAV, 1.4X FY24E P/B, and 15.2X FY24E P/E
  • The 3Q23 performances are generally in line with our forecasts
