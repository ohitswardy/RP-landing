REGIS REPORT

24 October 2023

Two separate disclosures from Dito CME (DITO PM) were made yesterday, revealing changes in its ownership structure with the entry of a new significant shareholder. First, DITO said that its controlling shareholder Udenna (of the Davao-based Dennis Uy) sold 500m DITO shares to an unknown buyer. Second, DITO also disclosed that it is issuing 3.3bn new shares (@ P1.00/share) to a company called Summit Telco. Note that Summit Telco already previously subscribed to new shares in DITO, which is the controlling shareholder of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel), the new mobile operator.

The two transactions combined would result in Udenna’s ownership in DITO falling to ~55%. More importantly, Summit Telco now owns at least 25% of DITO, even if we assume it was not the buyer of the 500m (2.5%) shares sold by Udenna.

The real owners behind “Summit Telco” have not been revealed but the amount of money invested in DITO so far (<$200m) does not look big enough to be impactful. We believe DitoTel will require a capital injection in excess of US$1bn to become a viable operator. Thus, an infusion by a significant and strategic shareholder is likely to be needed.

In relation to this, there are two things that we believe need to be highlighted. First, DitoTel has a legislative franchise (license) to provide telecom services and such franchises typically require Congressional approval for a change in control, a requirement that covers its controlling shareholder. Second, the law prohibits China Telecom, as a foreign state-owned entity, from increasing its stake in DitoTel (directly or indirectly).

+++

The Dept of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD, i.e., the Dept of Housing) is raising the price caps on residential developments that qualify as socialized housing. The price caps are being raised by more than 40% from the previous threshold, which was last set in 2018. For example, the price ceiling for socialized subdivision housing will be raised to P850k (US$15k) from P580k (US$10k).

The last price caps have now become unrealistically low now, given the rise in land prices and construction costs. Developments that qualify as socialized housing are given various incentives, including tax holidays. This increase should give a boost to mass housing as well as to some developers that disproportionately relied on socialized housing in the past (e.g., Vista Land (VLL PM)).

+++

Digital transactions through InstaPay and PESONet rose 30% to P9.24trn for the first nine months of the year. We expect the digital retail payments to meet the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target of accounting for 50% of all transactions by this year. The proportion for 2022 was 40.4%, by value.  The increased ubiquity of digital transactions is exemplified by the exponential rise in the usage of e-wallets like GCash, partly owned by Globe (GLO PM), and Maya, partly owned by PLDT (TEL PM).

+++

Puregold (PGOLD PM) reported 9M23 net profit of P6.6bn, up 2% YoY, and trailing our FY23 estimates. Reported SSSG for the 9M period was 4.8% for Puregold and 4.1% for S&R, which implies a slowdown (or even contraction) in 3Q alone. While segmental performance was not disclosed, we believe the profit underperformance was driven by S&R, which is facing competitive pressure from Landers. We hope to get further detail in the coming days.

PROPERTY
Government raises price ceilings for low-cost housing projects
  • The Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) said that it signed a Joint Memorandum Circular with National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) adjusting the current price caps that had been in effect since 2018
  • Ceiling for socialized subdivision projects was raised to P850,000 from P580,000 for units with a minimum floor area of 28sqm and a loft of at least 50% of the base structure
  • Ceiling for four-story socialized condominium projects is now set at P933,320 for 22sqm; P1,060,591 for 25sqm and P1,145,438 for 27sqm
  • For projects with five to nine stories, pricing is P1mn for 22sqm, P1,136,364 for 25qm, P1,227,273 for 27sqm
  • For projects with 10 floors and above, P1,320,000 for 22sqm, P1,500,000 for 25sqm and P1,620,000 for 27sqm
  • If land development cost is included, the socialized condominium projects’ price ceiling is set at a maximum of P1,800,000
  • The Urban Development and Housing Act provides tax incentives to developers of socialized housing projects, including exemption from project-related income taxes, capital gains tax on raw lands for the project, transfer tax for both raw and completed projects, donor’s tax for lands donated for socialized housing purposes, etc.
  • Acuzar and Balisacan said that the current price ceiling for socialized housing discourages the private sector from building affordable houses for low-income families

TELCOS
More ownership changes at Dito CME; Udenna reduces stake
  • Dito CME (DITO PM) disclosed that it was Udenna Corp that sold 500m shares of DITO last week
    • Sale reduced Udenna’s ownership to 10.7bn shares
    • Udenna is the holding company of the Davao-based Dennis Uy
  • DITO also disclosed that Summit Telco is subscribing to 3.3bn new shares of DITO at P1.00/share (less than 1/3rd of “market price”)
    • This adds to the 1.59bn shares subscribed to by Summit Telco in Aug 2023, bringing its total stake to 4.89bn DITO shares
    • Another firm, Xterra Ventures, subscribed to 610m new shares at that time
  • The total outstanding shares of DITO would rise to 19.535bn after this latest transaction, with Summit Telco holding 25%
    • Udenna would own 55% of DITO after the deal/s
  • DITO is the controlling shareholder of Dito Telecommunity (DitoTel) but had its stake in DitoTel diluted with the entry of a new investor
    • New investor was an entity called Summit Global which subscribed to 18.5% of the stock of an intermediate holding company
    • DITO’s effective stake in DitoTel was thus diluted from 53.7% to 43.7%
  • The identity of the party/parties behind Summit Telco is unclear
