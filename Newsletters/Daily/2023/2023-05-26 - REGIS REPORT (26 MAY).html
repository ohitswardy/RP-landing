REGIS REPORT

26 May 2023

The bill to create the Maharlika Investment Fund has been certified as “urgent” by the President, which should accelerate its passage by Congress. This creation of this fund is arguably the most visible legislative measure that the administration is pushing.  Based on what has been going around so far, the fund will be deployed to invest in large government projects (mostly infrastructure).  It is supposed to receive seed money from various government institutions but will eventually be open to outside investors.

The fund will effectively be a vehicle through which government expenditures can done outside the annual budgeting exercise of Congress.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) disclosed that it raised P13bn from the issuance of new preferred shares at a coupon of ~6.3%.  The funds will be used to refinance/pay down current and maturing debt, with a small portion to be used to fund its investment in the LRT-1 operator.  Unsurprisingly, the cost of the new funds will be more expensive than the debt it is paying down (e.g. bonds due in July carry a coupon of 3.92%).

MACRO
‘Hot money’ exits Phils in April
  • Foreign portfolio investments, also known as ‘hot money’ yielded a net outflow of $351.87mn in April according to BSP data
    • This was a reversal of the $1.41bn net inflow in April 2022
  • Net outflows in April increased fivefold from $70.26mn outflows in March
    • Also the highest in two months
  • Majority of investments (57.3%) went to Philippine Stock Exchange-listed securities
    • Balance was invested in Peso government securities
  • Gross outflows of hot money climbed 29.3% to $1.06bn in April from $823mn in the same month last year
  • Year to date, the BSP-registered FPI’s yielded net outflows of $680mn, a reversal of the $1.39bn net inflows in the same period in 2022

 

MACRO
Maharlika Investment Fund Bill certified as urgent
  • President Marcos certified the enabling law for the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) as urgent
  • The US$1.4bn fund is expected to finance big government projects such as power grids and dams
  • While the initial funds will come from government banks, there is a plan to eventually open the fund to external investors
