REGIS REPORT

20 June 2023

The government, in a surprise move, approved tariff increases for two of the rail lines in Metro Manila.  Fares for the LRT-1 and LRT-2 were increased 21%, effective Aug 2.  The minimum fare will now be P13.29 (24 US cents) from P11.0 (20 US cents).

LRT-1 is operated by a consortium which includes Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and Ayala Corp (AC PM) under a concession agreement. 

These fare adjustments are long overdue and are the first adjustments since 2015. This administration has been taking steps to allow many of these promised and overdue tariff increases to come through.  It’s one way of convincing the private sector that it intends to live up to commitments made in concession agreements for previous public-private partnership (PPP) projects.  Among the tariff increases granted by the administration so far are (1) water rate increases for Manila Water (MWC PM) and Maynilad, (2) toll fee hikes for various toll roads, and (3) now this one.

Meanwhile, also on the topic of PPP, the Manila International Airport Consortium submitted its revised unsolicited proposal to rehabilitate and operated the Manila airport NAIA.  The revised proposal involves capex of P267bn (US$4.8bn), more than doubling NAIA’s capacity to 70m per annum and payments to government of as much as P280bn (US$5bn).  The sticking point, however, appears to be the concession period as MIAC wants a 25-year contract while the government wants a shorter 15-year one.

+++

A re-start of the PPP program will, hopefully, provide a boost to spending on much-needed infrastructure.  The rehabilitation and upgrade of NAIA, for example, will have significant benefits for the overall travel and tourism sectors (should it happen).  Near term, this will also require plenty of construction manpower and materials.

But even before those, the expenditures to undertake the airport upgrade will need what is a huge amount (by Philippine standards) of funding.  The banks will certainly be among the key sources of this, if not the main source. A revival of credit demand will definitely be a boost to the financial sector.

INFRASTRUCTURE
Government approves new tariffs for LRT-1, LRT-2
  • The Department of Transportation (DoTr) announced fare hikes for LRT-1, LRT-2 beginning August 2
    • Minimum boarding fare will increase by 21% to P13.29 from P11
    • Fare per km will increase to P1.21 from P1.00
    • The approved increases are lower than the cumulative un-implemented tariff hike of +50% stipulated under the concession agreement
  • Last April, President Marcos ordered the deferral of the fare hike implementation due to its economic impact on commuters
    • Last fare increase for LRT-1, LRT-2, MRT-3 occurred in Jan 2015, before LRMC took over operations on Sep 2015
  • Light Rail Manila Corp (LRMC) holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 railroad
    • LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), Macquarie Infra (10%)
    • Our valuation for 100% of LRMC’s equity value is negative P19.16bn (equivalent to an attributable value of -P0.24 per MPI share)
  • MPI is trading at a 42% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.59/sh
  • AC is trading at a 24% discount to its market-based NAV of P806/sh
INFRASTRUCTURE
Consortium unveils proposal for multi-phased NAIA masterplan
  • The Manila international Airport Consortium (MIAC) unveiled its P267bn unsolicited proposal to rehab and develop NAIA over a 25-year concession period
  • Proposal involves a total capex of P267bn and total concession fee payments of P280bn (covers revenue sharing and taxes) over the life of the concession
    • Capex of P267bn will be earmarked for a three-phase expansion
      • Upfront payment to the government of P57bn
      • Capex of P57bn for Phases 1&2: Increase terminal capacity to 54mn passengers per annum (MPPA) by 2025 and 62.5 MPPA by 2028 (expansion of terminal floor area, addition of airfield facilities, and cross-terminal transport)
      • Capex of P154bn for Phase 3: Increase capacity to 70 MPPA by 2048 (long-term expansion and development projects to further expand terminal space and airfield capacity)
  • MIAC includes six Filipino conglomerates + US-based Global Infrastructure Partners:
    • Aboitiz InfraCapital –AEV PM
    • AC Infrastructure – AC PM
    • Asia’s Emerging Dragon Corp. – LTG PM
    • Alliance Global – Infracorp Dev’t – AGI PM
    • Filinvest Development Corp. - FDC PM
    • JG Summit – JGS PM
  • Based on its 2019 annual report, NAIA recorded a net income of P5.0bn, operating profit of P6.8bn, and revenues of P15.2bn
    • Revenue breakdown: Passenger service charge (36%), landing/parking fees (34%), rent income and others (30%)
    • NAIA handled 48.1mn passengers and 306k aircraft movements
RETAIL
Wilcon CEO sees slower growth in 2023; store expansion to accelerate
  • WLCON President and CEO Lorraine Belo-Cincochan sees muted growth for the company in 2023
  • Partly due to high base effects last year driven by the post-lockdown surge in demand; discretionary spending by its customers has also been focused on "revenge travel" and other leisure activities
  • Notwithstanding the muted outlook, the company continues to focus on store expansion
  • From the initial guidance of 8-10 new stores this year (Regis FY23E: 8 new depots), mgmt says they can open as much as 12-14 new stores
  • This implies the company can hit its 100-store target by 2024, earlier than its initial plan to reach this target by 2025
  • WLCON currently trades at 23x 2023E PE
PROPERTY
Valenzuela City to ban POGO firms
  • The local government of Valenzuela City has approved an ordinance banning POGO firms in the city
  • We are not aware of any licensed POGO firms operating in Valenzuela City but if other areas implement similar ordinances, then this could further shrink the POGO industry
  • POGO firms have a large presence in the cities of Makati, Pasay, and Paranaque, as well as the province of Cavite
  • At the end of 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm
  • Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-4Q22
    • POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 51% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV
    • 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV
  • POGOs occupy:
    • 5% of FLI’s office portfolio
    • 5% of MEG’s office portfolio
    • 1% of RLC’s office portfolio
  • FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY23E P/B, and 5.4X FY23E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY23E P/B, and 4.0X FY23E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY23E P/B, and 7.8X FY23E P/E
