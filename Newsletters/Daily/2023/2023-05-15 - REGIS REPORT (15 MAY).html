15 May 2023

Today’s results line-up is a little more mixed. Three of them held their results calls on Friday, with the last (Security Bank (SECB PM)) scheduling its call for Wed.

  • LT Group (LTG PM) (=) – LT Group reported net income of P6.4bn for 1Q23, a slight dip of 2% YoY, but within expectations. The not-so-good news is that the earnings mix is arguably less ideal, with implications on future dividend streams. Profits from tobacco, the cash-generative segment, dropped 28% and it was the bank that made up for the shortfall this quarter.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – While 1Q net profit figure looks unexciting, the underlying operations are showing a different picture. Operating profit (EBIT) jumped +81% YoY, driven by (1) operating leverage following the continued strength in the top line, and (2) improving profitability of the international operations, particularly CBTL, Superfoods, and Smashburger. We believe there is upside risk to operating profit estimates given the positive top line trends, coupled with easing inventory costs.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (+) - At its briefing held Friday, the company revealed that its office occupancy fell 2ppt QoQ to 90%. Furthermore, management said that some tenants have renewed contracts at lower rental rates. We expect more such cases in 2023 and 2024, since the contracts up for renewal were previously signed when office rental rates were close to the peak.

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) – 1Q23 NPAT of P2.36bn, down 13% YoY, tracking behind Regis (FY23E P10.3bn), and more so compared to consensus (P11.5bn) estimates. Even at the PPOP level, earnings were down 3% YoY. Loan growth slowed sharply to 5% YoY, as corporate loans grew just 1%. To the upside, consumer loans surged 18% YoY. This helped loan yields jump 30bps QoQ, and NIMs rise 12bps QoQ reversing two quarters of sharp margin contraction. Despite that, PPOP was dragged by a 12% YoY increase in opex, due to manpower and IT spend. NPL ratios increased QoQ, though this was more due to the seasonally weak loans; in PHP terms, NPLs were flat QoQ. Overall the results look soft, but the upward momentum in consumer loans and NIMs give us some hope for succeeding quarters. The bank will hold a briefing on Wednesday, 17 May at 5pm.

+ + +

Banking sector NPLs (ex-interbank) were flat MoM at 3.42%. Other asset quality indicators were also steady – Past Due rates edged up 3bps to 4.07% and Restructured Loan rate was up 2bps to 2.60%. The stability of asset quality is remarkable, given the sharp increase in interest rates in the last 12 months. We consider a deterioration in NPLs as a key downside risk to bank earnings. To the upside, continued benign asset quality could see credit costs fall, and provide support for sector profits.

BANKS
SECB 1Q23 NPAT P2.36bn, -13% YoY, and behind Regis and Street
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT of P2.36bn for 1Q23, down 13% YoY, behind the full-year forecasts of Regis (P10.30bn) and well behind that of the Street (P11.49bn). Loan growth slowed sharply to 5% YoY (was 12% in 4Q22), as corporate loans grew just 1% YoY. To the upside, consumer loans surged 18% YoY, and now account for 26% of total loans (from 23% in 1Q22 and 24% at end-22). This helped NIMs expand 12bps QoQ, arresting the steep declines in the previous two quarters. While overall revenue growth was decent at 7% YoY, opex jumped 12%, taking PPOP down 3% YoY. Provision expense dragged YoY, but was down by almost half QoQ. NPL ratio was up 17bps QoQ to 3.12%, but this was due more to the 2.8% QoQ drop in loans; in PHP terms, NPLs were down 1% QoQ.
  • Management will hold a briefing on Wednesday 17 May at 5pm. Ahead of that, some highlights, below:
    • Loan growth slowed to 5% YoY from 12% in 4Q22. The rapid deceleration was due to corporate loans, up just 1% YoY, from 10% YoY in 4Q22. Meanwhile, consumer loans accelerated to 18% YoY (from 16% in 4Q). Consumers now account for 26% of total loans, up from 24% in 4Q22 and from 23% in 1Q22.
    • Deposits contracted 1% YoY and 13.4% QoQ, as the bank let go of expensive TDs (26% QoQ) in favor of wholesale funds – bills payable surged 63% QoQ.
    • NIMs expanded 12bps QoQ. Loan yields jumped 30bps QoQ, on both higher policy rates, better loan mix, and the raising of the credit card rate cap. Deposit costs were up a milder 11bps QoQ, though overall cost of funds were up 29bps.
    • Fee incomes were up 2% YoY. Other income was dragged by trading/fx gains, which were down 24% YoY.
    • Opex growth stayed elevated, up 12% YoY due to higher staffing costs and IT expenses as the bank ramps up its digitalization initiatives. This dragged PPOP down 3% YoY.
    • Provision expense came off a (very) low base in 1Q22. The P616mn expense was equivalent to roughly 50bps credit cost. NPL ratio increased 17bps QoQ to 3.12%, though this was largely due to the 2.8% QoQ drop in loans. In PHP terms, management said NPLs were down 1% QoQ. NPL coverage, though, dipped to 99% from 101% at end-2022.
  • The results look quite soft. However, we are encouraged by the resurgence of high-margin consumer lending, which could continue to support NIM expansion going forward.
  • More after the briefing on Wednesday.

BANKS
Asset quality stabilizes in March, NPL ratio flat MoM
  • Banking sector asset quality stabilized in March, following two months of mild deterioration. Sector NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was 3.42%, flat MoM, while Past Due (PD) ratio was up a tad to 4.07% from 4.04% in February, while Restructured Loans (RL) ratio was up 2bps MoM to 2.60%. In PHP terms, NPLs were up 0.8% MoM, while PDs and RLs were up 0.9%. The MoM growth was in line with prior March prints (pre-Covid). NPL cover improved a tad to 105.2% from 105.0% in February.
  • Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) slowed slightly to 10.6% YoY from the 11.1% pace the month prior, though still up 0.8% MoM. Deposits, though, jumped 2.5% MoM, taking LDRs down to 70.5% from 71.7%.
  • Overall, the figures allay some concerns we flagged in our report (“Signal or noise?”). We continue to watch closely, as asset quality deterioration remains a key risk, as interest rates have risen sharply YoY

International visitor arrivals surpass 2mn YTD
  • The Philippines recorded 2,002,304 international visitor arrivals from Jan. 1 to May 12, 2023, exceeding the 2022 full year target of 1.7 million foreign visitors
  • South Korea delivered the highest number with 487,502; followed by the United States with 352,894; Australia with 102,494; Canada with 98,593; and Japan with 97,329
  • The DOT is keeping its 4.8-million target arrivals for 2023
