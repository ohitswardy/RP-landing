REGIS REPORT

13 November 2023

Misses outnumber the beats for today’s results tally. Tighter purse strings due to high inflation and interest rates seem to be a common theme among many, even the companies not directly in the consumer space. Consumers are trading down and, in some cases, to illicit goods and companies have become acutely aware of the high cost of money.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) - 3Q23 revenues came in weaker-than-expected, with growth slowing to only +6%. Branded consumer food (BCF) sales took a hit as inflation concerns weighed on consumer sentiment across various markets. Despite the soft top line and volatile cost environment, BCF EBIT margin managed to grow 6% YoY on a 100-bp improvement in margins. Margin recovery remains a priority though management is likely to pause price increases for now to give consumers some time to absorb the adjustments.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – Headline net profit for 9M23 (-6% YoY to P6.bn) is trending behind estimates, but operating profit of P11.9bn (+48% YoY) is well ahead of Regis estimates, as well as management’s own guidance (FY23 P12.6-13.2bn). 3Q SSSG was 8.8%, with Phils outperforming (+13%). EBIT jumped 43% YoY with strong operating results across both markets. Management will be hosting a briefing on Nov 15th.
  • Converge (CNVRG PM) (-) – Net profit for 9M was up +4% to P6.4bn, which would be broadly on trend to meet consensus estimates (below ours). Revenues for 3Q alone rose 5% but profits were down 3% with provisions (receivables and others) rising sharply. Capex plans for the year and 2024 are still looking high, and management sounded cautious about a possible dividend next year. More below.
  • LT Group (LTG PM) (-) - While 9M23 (P19.2bn, -6%YoY) earnings appear on track to meet FY23E estimates, the underlying tobacco business (profit of P3.2bn, 15%QoQ, -25%YoY) and therefore its dividend outlook remains challenged. Management estimates that illicit trade has grown to 17% of total industry volumes and is as high as 38% in some parts of the country.

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) – Trading/fx losses worth P944mn in 3Q23 alone were the main culprit in the disappointing (vs Regis and Street) 9M23 NPAT of P7.57bn, -12% YoY. Core operations, though, look quite strong. While overall loan growth remained tepid at +3.8% YoY (better than the -1.1% in 2Q), higher-yielding consumer/MSME loans accelerated, up 22% YoY (was +19% in 2Q). The improved loan mix helped NIMs expand 31bps QoQ, far stronger then the Big Three. Adding momentum was the surprising 19bps QoQ drop in deposit costs, suggesting less intense competition for funding. Asset quality deteriorated a bit, with NPL ratio up 9bps QoQ to 3.15%, while NPL cover fell to 92% (from 100% in 2Q23). The bank will hold an earnings call on Wednesday, 15 November, at 3:30pm Manila/HK/SG time.
  • First Gen (FGEN PM) (+) – Earnings improved on back of a much better contribution from natgas units ($53mn, 23%QoQ, 32%YoY), buoyed by better availability and higher dispatch levels. This more than offset EDC’s performance, whose earnings dipped 33%QoQ as power prices softened in the period.  More below.
  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM)  (+) – During its briefing, RLC revealed that while its mall and residential business were performing well, its office segment was less than stellar. Office occupancy fell 2ppt QoQ to 87%, as a BPO tenant increased its implementation of work-from-home (WFH). Looking ahead, management is of the view that most of its office tenants are already at their desired levels of WFH and would not downsize significantly going forward.

+++

The government, using a little bit of legal legerdemain, is increasing its annual budget by P261bn (5%) this year. While it’s unlikely that the agencies can spend that additional amount before year-end, it does open the door for spending to ramp up quickly next year (as the allocations can be carried over). Spending for this 4Q could be relatively weak given budget constraints but this move may help raise it.

+++

Exporter are expressing opposition to what appears to be an impending increase in container storage charges to be implemented across ports in the Philippines. If approved as proposed, the tariff increase would be 32% on storage fees, with a 150% surcharge on reefers. Storage fees account for ~15% of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) group revenues.

MACRO
Government hikes 2023 budget program by P261bn to P5.53tn
  • The Department of Budget and Management hiked the national budget by P261bn to P5.53tn from P5.23tn
    • The entire P261bn hike went to unprogrammed appropriations
    • Funding for unprogrammed appropriations come from the excess of collections, new revenues, and approved loans
  • The additional budget must be released before year end but implementation can be varied
    • Personnel services can be implemented until year-end but maintenance and capital outlay can be spend until end-2024
  • Assuming the entire additional P261bn will be spent on primary spending this year, then primary spending in 4Q23 could jump by 12% YoY instead of declining by 9% YoY

TRANSPORT
Local exporters oppose proposal to hike foreign container storage charges
  • In a letter signed by Philippine Exporters Confederation, Inc. (Philexport) Sergio R. Ortiz-Luis Jr., local exporters recommended that the planned rate hike undergo a regulatory impact assessment (RIA)
    • Other stakeholders have proposed to defer the rate hike or implement the increase in tranches over a three-year period
  • The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) held a public hearing last Oct 18 to discuss the proposed tariff: (1) 32% hike in storage charges for containers and (b) introduction of a 150% surcharge on reefer containers
    • The PPA last adjusted storage fees in 2014
    • The rate increase, PPA said, is meant to optimize yard space usage and expedite the withdrawal of import containers, thus preventing congestion
    • Calculation is grounded on the 2013-2022 Consumer Price Index, reflecting the average retail prices for commonly purchased goods and services in a specific region
  • Our forecasts do not assume a hike in storage fees
    • Storage fees would typically account for c. 15% of port revenues
    • We estimate MICT accounts for c. 25% of ICTSI (ICT PM) group revenues/EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 8.0x/7.2x FY23E/FY24E EV/EBITDA, below peers (11x) and historical average (11x)
TELCOS
Converge 9M profit up 4% to P6.4bn
  • 9M net income at P6.4bn (+4%). Converge reported net income of P6.4bn for 9M23, +4% YoY and appearing om trend to match Street estimates for FY23.  The 3Q profit was P2.1bn, down 3% YoY and 1% QoQ. Revenues were up 5% YoY for 3Q and +7% for 9M but costs, especially provisions, rose faster. Provisions for receivables were up 48% for 9M, and this was compounded by a jump in provisions for other assets.
  • Net adds of 79k in 3Q. The key bright spot for the quarter was net subscriber adds, which reached almost 79k. The company has seen sequential quarterly improvement in net adds since 3Q/4Q22, when it first tried out its prepaid service. Its prepaid service accounted for 40% of its net adds over the period.  Even without the prepaid subs, the company is reporting a reasonably decent level of broadband net adds. Apart from the prepaid service, Converge also introduced Bida Fiber, which is a lower-priced postpaid option.
  • Revenue guidance of P35-36bn for FY23. Management estimates full-year revenue will be P35-36bn, which is equivalent to 4-7% growth YoY. This implies 4Q revenue of P8.8bn to P9.8bn (vs 4Q22 revenue of P9.2bn).  Consensus is at just over P36bn.
  • Catch-up capex in 4Q. The company has spent only P5bn of its target capex of P12-15bn for FY23.  However, it expects a chunky outlay soon because of the purchase of cable capacity (“indefeasible right to use” or IRU). Capex in 2024 is likely to still be high, possibly at P15-17bn.
  • Dividends in 2024? Management did not rule out a dividend in 2024 but sounded quite cautious about it given the higher interest rate environment.  It says it wanted to be prudent about it and will re-evaluate it in April 2024. This presumably is because its capex plans might need to be reevaluated as well.

BANKS
SECB 9M23 P7.6bn, behind, on trading/fx losses; NIMs jump on falling deposit costs

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 9M23 NPAT of P7.57bn, down 12% YoY, and BEHIND Regis and Street forecasts (P10.6bn), due primarily to hefty trading/fx losses in 3Q23. Apart from this, “core” operations were quite strong, and profits would otherwise be in line or even ahead of our estimates. Loan growth recovered to +3.8% YoY (from -1.1% in 2Q), driven entirely by consumer loans (up 22% YoY) while corporate/commercial loans were still -3% YoY. Consumer loans now make up 29% of total loans, back to the 2019 peak. NIMs jumped 31bps QoQ (+65bps YoY) due to the better loan mix, and to a drop in deposit costs as competition seems to have eased.

More after the briefing on Wednesday, 15 Nov at 3:30pm. Meanwhile, some highlights from the disclosures:

  • Loan growth recovered to +3.8% YoY (from -1.1% in 2Q23). While corporate loans were still down 3% YoY,  the higher-margin consumer/MSME book surged 22% YoY, and now accounts for 29% of total loans. Consumer loans were strong across products, with mortgages +15%, auto loans +23%, credit cards +34%, and MSME +71%.
  • NIMs jumped 31bps QoQ (+65bps YoY). Loan yields were up 20bps QoQ largely due to the better loan mix. More notably, though, is that deposit costs dropped 19bps QoQ. We are slightly wary, though, as other banks had warned that deposit competition seems to be heating up in 4Q.
  • Fee incomes were up 10% YoY in 9M23, but were up 15% YoY in 3Q alone, presumably due to the growth in consumer loans, particularly credit cards.
  • SECB booked P944mn worth of trading/fx losses in 3Q23 alone, causing a major drag to profitability. There was another P600mn worth of unrealized losses and translation adjustments in the other comprehensive income (OCI) accounts for 3Q23.
  • Opex grew 14% YoY on continued IT and staff investments. The bank is also selectively adding branches, opening two in 3Q23, taking its total to 319.
  • Provision expense was up 61% YoY in 9M23, as NPL ratio edged up to 3.15% (from 3.06% in 2Q). Despite the jump in provisions, NPL coverage fell to 92% from 100% in 2Q. As with NIMs, we expect the seeming deterioration in asset quality was due to the shift in loan mix towards consumer loans, rather than to any broad stress in the book.

The stock currently trades at 0.4x P/bk, and on 5x PER on our 2023E forecasts.

JFC 9M23 net profit -6% YoY; operating profit (+48% YoY) outperforms
  • JFC reported headline net profit of P6.8bn, trending behind Regis but broadly in line with consensus estimates. Excluding one-offs in the previous year, we estimate recurring NPAT jumped 5x YoY to P5.8bn
  • Total revenues grew 18% YoY, in line with estimates. Meanwhile, operating profit jumped 48% YoY to P6.8bn – well above Regis and consensus estimates, and even the company’s own guidance for FY23 (P12.6-13.2bn)
  • For 3Q23 alone, EBIT grew 43% YoY as margins continued to improve for Philippines and international

 

  • Revenues grew 11% YoY to P61.5bn on the back of 8.8% SSSG; Phils outperformed, posting +13% SSSG during the quarter
  • International revenues grew 6% on 2.6%, dragged by SSS declines in CBTL (-2%) and Superfoods (-12%)
  • Consolidated EBIT margins grew 160bps YoY, driven by the international business. Int’l accounted for 18% of EBIT in 3Q
  • Meanwhile, Phils EBIT margin contracted 80bps QoQ, which was likely driven by recent minimum wage hikes

 

  • We hope to get more detail during the company’s briefing on 15 Nov
