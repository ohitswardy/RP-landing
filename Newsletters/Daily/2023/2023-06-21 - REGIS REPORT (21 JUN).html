REGIS REPORT

21 June 2023

Telecom operator Dito Telecommunity, subsidiary of Dito (DITO PM), introduced its postpaid services a couple of days ago. While Dito Tel’s postpaid plans provide more data allocation than the two incumbents, we don’t think they can have a meaningful impact on the wireless market. Postpaid clients account for ~3% of total subscribers and demand in that segment is quite price inelastic. Furthermore, prepaid services are actually much cheaper than postpaid services for all three operators.

+++

The Pakistani government appears to be getting ready to hand over the terminal operated by ICTSI (ICT PM) to Abu Dhabi Ports (ADP), according to local Pakistani media, despite ICT’s rights under its concession contract. This action, if true, suggests that the termination of ICT’s contract by the government there was a result of other, bigger considerations outside of just the port’s operations. This port operation is likely lost for ICT, but the impact of the termination on ICT’s valuation will be minuscule.

+++

Puregold (PGOLD PM) announced it is taking over operations of 14 locations of Divimart Supermarket (a local chain with stores mostly outside Metro Manila) and rebranding these into Puregold stores. While the acquisition is relatively small (c3% of current Puregold brand network), it provides PGOLD with instant footprint in new locations, and potentially access to more locations within the Divimart network.

+++

We release a new note raising our profit forecasts for GT Capital (GTCAP PM) by 23-31%, taking into account revised estimates for its two largest assets – Metrobank (MBT PM) – see Philippine banks – Another leg up) and Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), the latter benefiting from the recovery of vehicle sales and currency movements. 

As a result, GT Capital is on track to post record-high earnings this year (P21.8bn, +43% YoY). The improved profitability and low gearing are also likely to prompt a sharp increase its dividend payments.

The shares have been ignored for a while and now offer, in our view, compelling value at 54% discount to NAV (market-based) and less than 5x PER. While the stock has outperformed the PCOMP by 14% year-to-date, its absolute price is still significantly lower than it was in 2019 despite profit looking likely to hit a record high this year. We reiterate our BUY rating with a target price of P900/share.

MACRO
BOP posts US$439mn deficit in May
  • The country’s balance of payments recorded a US$439mn deficit in May
    • This is lower than the US$1.6bn deficit in May 2022 but higher than the US$148mn deficit in April 2023
    • Cited government’s foreign currency debt obligations
  • Brings 5M23 BOP to a surplus of US$2.9bn, a reversal of the US$1.5bn deficit in the same period last year
  • The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$1.2bn this year (0.3% of GDP), lower than last year’s deficit of US$7.3bn (1.8% of GDP)
TELCOS
Dito launches postpaid services
  • Dito Telecommunity launched its postpaid services, introducing a number of SIM-only plans and plans that come with handsets.
  • The data allocation for its various plans is clearly more than those for the two incumbent operators (see comparison shown below)
  • We don’t believe there will be much impact on the market
    • Postpaid subscribers are ~3% of the market, <30% of revenue
    • There’s a lot of ‘inertia’ on the postpaid market, and the postpaid market is price-inelastic
    • Real competition for postpaid is against prepaid; prepaid services remain cheaper
      • Data revenue (net, ex- taxes) per GB is less than P17 (= US$0.30)

TRANSPORT
Pakistan gov't mulls handing over Karachi port to UAE
  • The government of Pakistan has constituted a negotiation committee to finalize a deal with the UAE for handing over its Karachi port terminals
    • It aims to reach a deal to hand over Berths 6-9, which were under the administrative control of Pakistan International Container Terminal (PICT PK), to Abu Dhabi Ports (ADP)
    • PICT is an 80%-owned unit of ICTSI (ICT PM)
  • Sources of the local newspaper, Tribune, suggest that the PICT may pose legal challenges based on the concession agreement
    • PICT operated the Berths 6-9 from June 2002 for a period of 21 years, which ended last June 17
    • However, under the Concession Agreement, PICT has the first right of refusal subject to certain conditions
  • While PICT’s value is inconsequential to our NAV (US$5mn vs. NAV of US$8.5bn), we assumed it will continue to contribute to ICTSI’s profits until 2024
    • We estimate PICT accounts for 4% of FY23E volumes, 2% of FY23E revenues, 1% of FY23E profit
    • Given this recent development, it is highly likely that ICT will impair the US$13mn of goodwill related to PICT
    • The impairment will not affect dividends, which are based on a min. of 50% of recurring profit before perps
