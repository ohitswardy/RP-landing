REGIS REPORT

06 February 2023

BPI (BPI PM) kicked off reporting season for the banking sector, with FY22 NPAT rising 60% YoY to P39.6bn, a shade lower than our forecast of P40.3bn, but 3.7% ahead of consensus. The trends seen in 3Q22 seem to be holding – Loan growth was sustained at 15.3% YoY (+6% QoQ), NIMs expanded 28bps YoY (faster than the 23bps In 9M22), and NPL ratio dropped back to pre-Covid levels at 1.76%. As management had telegraphed, opex (belatedly) jumped 26% YoY and QoQ in 4Q22, taking FY opex up 14% YoY, as IT spend finally caught up.

Overall, the results were pretty solid, and broadly in line with our bullish views on the stock and the sector overall. However, there is some concern over the apparent deceleration in NII growth – up 5% QoQ in 4Q22 vs +9% QoQ in 3Q22. Given the limited information in the press release, it is unclear if this was due to a slowdown in asset yields, or an acceleration in funding costs.

The bank will hold a briefing this afternoon at 5pm Manila/HK/SG time. You can register using this link.

Gaming regulator PAGCOR, said that income from gaming operations jumped 68%YoY to P55bn in 2022, implying that its 4Q revenues were up 10% from the previous quarter. This suggests that revenues for the overall industry extended their upward trajectory, contrary to expectations that it would drop off after the big reopening spike in 2Q/3Q.

While the reported figure for PAGCOR includes revenues from other sources (gov’t casinos, fees from provincial operators, POGOs, etc), we believe trends from Entertainment City operators like Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) likely behaved similarly, if not better.

BANKS
BPI FY22 P39.6bn, in line; loans +15%, NIMs up, NPLs sharply lower
  • In a press release, BPI (BPI PM) reported headline NPAT of P39.6bn for FY22, up 66% YoY, in line with REGIS forecast of P40.3bn, and slightly ahead (+3.7%) of consensus. Excluding the chunky sale of a Makati property in 2Q22, NPAT was P35.9bn, up 50% YoY. The bull case remains – loan growth was sustained at +15% YoY, NIMs expanded 28bps YoY by management estimates, and NPLs improved sharply to 1.76%.
  • In good/bad news, opex jumped 26% YoY and QoQ in 4Q22 to P17.9bn. This was likely driven by a delayed surge in IT spend, as management had telegraphed in earlier quarterly briefings. This dragged PPOP down 15% QoQ. However, this was balanced out by a sharp drop in provision expense as NPLs fell and NPL cover increased.
  • Management will host a briefing on Monday, 06 February at 5pm Manila/HK/SG time.
  • Ahead of that, some highlights from the press release:
    • Loans expanded 15.3% YoY, slightly faster than the 15.2% pace in 3Q22. Growth was led by credit cards (+31.1% YoY), corporates (+15.5%), and auto loans (14.0%). Deposits grew 7.2% YoY, with CASA up a slower 3.5%. This brought the CASA ratio down to 74.4% from 76.1% in 3Q22.
    • NIMs expanded 28bps YoY by management estimates, which saw NII surge 22% YoY to P85.1bn. For 4Q22 alone, NII was up an even faster 27% YoY and 5% QoQ to P23.45bn.
    • Other income was up 20% YoY, though this included the P5bn sale of Makati property in 2Q22. Without this, other income would have been flat (+2.4%) YoY.
    • Opex jumped 26% YoY and QoQ in 4Q22; we presume this was due to delayed booking of IT expenses. In previous earnings briefings, management said that some IT charges were pushed back as many project payments tend to be back-ended, and some projects faced delays. The increase in IT spend has been well telegraphed, and is an integral part of BPI’s aggressive digitalization push. The bank launched their own e-wallet called VYBE in 4Q22.
    • PPOP in 4Q22 dropped 15% QoQ, due to the above mentioned blip in opex. For the full-year, PPOP was up 30% YoY.
    • Provision expense fell 30% YoY in FY22 to P9.2bn. For 4Q22 alone, provisions plunged 41% YoY and 32% QoQ. This was due to an 18bps QoQ improvement in NPL ratio to 1.76% from 1.94% in 3Q22. Despite the drop in provisions, NPL cover improved to 180.1% from 176.9% in 3Q22.

 

GAMING
PAGCOR's 4Q income from gaming ops +10%QoQ
  • Gaming regulator PAGCOR released its income statement for 2022 where it disclosed income from gaming operations of P55bn, an increase of 68% vs last year. The FY number implies 4Q accelerated to nearly P16bn (an improvement over P14.5bn in 3Q22, P13.4bn in 2Q and P11.3bn in 1Q). PAGCOR derives income from gaming operations from 1) its own casinos, 2) fees from licensed operators (including Entertainment City) and 3) others (bingo, e-games, POGO). The FY figure is still c.30% off from pre-pandemic levels, but the interims have been on a decidedly upward trajectory since 4Q21.
  • Meanwhile, GGR from Entertainment City operators appear to be faring similarly, if not better than the industry figures, at least as of 9M22. Bloomberry’s (BLOOM PM) GGR as of 9M22 was down by just 22% from 9M19, while the most recent public GGR print (3Q22) is just 10% off from the quarterly average in 2019 (P15bn/qtr). Still largely without Chinese tourists, Solaire’s performance has relied on mass market and locals for most its recent recovery, with GGR from mass tables and slots making up for the lack of Chinese tourists (as much as 40% of GGR previously). Barring any outliers, the PAGCOR’s FY figures potentially preview sustained growth through 4Q for the integrated resorts as well.
  • China’s relaxation of Covid controls over the past few weeks have created some excitement for regional gaming names on the prospects of players finally returning. BLOOM is up more than 20% YTD, but we still see upside to our TP of P11.70/sh, which is based on a 10x multiple on our 23E EBITDA (P19bn ~ back to pre-pandemic).
  • On our base case, we already have GGR/EBITDA growing at a CAGR of 22%/21% up to FY24E. China is clearly not the revenue driver anymore for Solaire, but a stronger-than-expected return of Chinese tourists provides upside to robust expectations.
