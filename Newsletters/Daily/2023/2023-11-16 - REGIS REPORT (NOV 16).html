We have a few more results today, from a number who are just beating the deadline.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – Thie Philippines outperformed with SSSG of +13%, bucking the trend for the rest of the sector. Same-store traffic continued to be healthy (+8%), while average checks (+5%) were buoyed by value-for-money offerings. Operating leverage was the main driver of profitability, particularly for the international business, which saw op profit surge 2.5x YoY. Management maintained its operating profit growth target for the year (+20-25%), but admitted they are likely to exceed that.

  • Vista Land (VLL PM) (+) – Vista Land’s 9M23 earnings grew 74% to P6.8bn. Stripping off gains from insurance proceeds in 2Q23, we estimate that recurring profit rose 38% to P5.4bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. 3Q23 residential revenue jumped 143% YoY (+27% QoQ), snapping a streak of 15 consecutive quarters of YoY declines.   • Megawide (MWIDE PM) (-) – Megawide swung back to a loss in 3Q23, recording a net loss after pref div of P167mn. The construction unit accounted for bulk of the losses, as it continued to face project delays and margin compression. Its newly acquired property arm, PH1, also contributed to the group’s losses, registering a net loss of P27mn. The landport unit broke even for the first time since the pandemic, as occupancy rates improved.

Banks’ asset quality indicators sent mixed signals in September. The good news was that NPL ratios (ex-interbank) improved 3bps MoM to 3.48%, while Restructured Loans (RLs) edged down 1bp to 2.35% of. The bad news is that Past Due (PD) ratio deteriorated, up 6bps MoM to 4.21%, marking the third consecutive month of increases. Given the 90-day lag between PD and NPL, one would have expected to see NPLs ratios rising by now.

We suspect that the divergence is due to the shifting loan mix towards consumer loans – specifically credit card receivables which are still growing in the 25-30% YoY range despite headline overall loan growth of just 8% in September. Credit cards not only have naturally higher delinquency rates, but also banks tend to write off credit card debt much more quickly, leading to deceptively low end-period NPL ratios. Given the credit cards segment’s high yields (mid-20%) and strong fee generation, the segment can take higher credit cost… up to a point.

Remittances for Sept were up 2.6% YoY to US$2.91bn – nothing particularly remarkable. The figure translated into PHP, however, is up just 1.4%. Sept is probably the last month this year where the growth of PHP-translated remittances will be positive due to the higher base (stronger USD) in 4Q.  Like the GDP figures, remittances will be lapping an elevated base of comparison for the Oct-Dec period.

BANKS
September NPL ratio improve, but PD rates tick up

Some mixed signals from the banking sector’s asset quality data in September. The good news is that NPL ratio (ex-interbank) improved 3bps MoM to 3.48% from 3.51% in August. Also, Restructured Loans (RLs) dipped a smidge to 2.35% from 2.36% the previous month. Meanwhile, NPL cover improved a tad to 103.65% from 103.02% in August.

The bad news, though is that Past Due (PD) ratio blipped up 6bps MoM to 4.21% in September from 4.15% in August. This is the third consecutive month that PD ratios increased, now a cumulative 12bps up from the June level (4.09%), and fully 42bps up from the end-2022 low of 3.79%. This is slightly worrisome as PDs tend to be leading indicators for NPLs (90 days past due).

The fact that NPL ratios are lower since June, while PDs are up, suggest that banks are writing off NPLs at a faster clip.

There is a potentially positive perspective here, though. The divergence could be a function of changing loan mix. Loan growth (all banks, ex-interbank) decelerated to 8.4% YoY from 9.4% in August, and from 11.2% in Sept 2022. However, BSP data and bank results show that consumer loans are still growing in the mid- to high teens. This in turn is driven by credit cards, which were up a scorching 30% YoY in September (for universal/commercial banks). Not only are delinquencies naturally higher for consumer loans in general, but also banks tend to write off past due credit card receivables much more quickly.

Based on the banks’ 9M23 earnings calls, there doesn’t seem to be any indication significant, broad-based pressure on NPLs. At least not yet. We remain positive on the banks, but see asset quality deterioration as the key downside risk.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

CONSTRUCTION
Megawide swings back to a loss in 3Q23
  • 9M23 profit at P30mn, well below consensus forecasts. Megawide reversed its gains in 2Q23 and incurred a P30mn net loss in 3Q23.  After removing pref divs, losses for the quarter stood at P167mn, bringing 9M23 profit to a mere P30mn, trending well below consensus forecast of P117mn.  The construction unit accounted for bulk of the losses while its newly acquired real estate arm, PH1, dragged. The landport unit had a decent 3Q but operations are still way below pre-Covid levels. 
  • Construction very soft. Project delays and higher costs affected the construction unit’s 3Q/9M performance. Its order book fell 16% QoQ and 28% YoY to P42bn. Management said that majority of its collection issues have been resolved. However, this doesn’t seem to be translating to lower debt levels, with short-term loans up 7% QoQ to P15.9bn in end-Sep and 9% from end-December.  Parent-level gross debt (including prefs) has risen 3% QoQ to P36bn while net debt is up 26% to P31bn after paying c. P5bn for PH1 in July.
  • Landport EBITDA improves on better occupancy rates; Real estate arm a drag.  The landport unit registered an EBITDA of P130mn in 3Q, though this level is just half of what it used to generate prior to the exit of POGOs. Office occupancy rates improved to 63% in 3Q from c. 56% in June.

Meanwhile, MWIDE started recognizing losses from PH1 in 3Q23 but management noted that 9M23 revenue bookings collapsed to P125mn from P509mn last year, which they blamed on slower completions and fewer accounts that have met its 10% booking threshold.  Reservations sales are up 5.7x YoY to P3.6bn in 9M23.
