REGIS REPORT

06 December 2023

PLDT (TEL PM) CEO Al Panlilio is retiring effective end of the year, ostensibly because of “health reasons”. The suddenness of the retirement, the relative youth of Panlilio (59) and the shortness of his tenure are prompting some speculation about other possible reasons. Regardless, for PLDT, what’s important is who will lead it and how come 2024 and beyond.

Manny Pangilinan’s resumption of his role as interim CEO will raise concerns given his age (77) and his many other roles within the group, not least of which is his role as CEO of Meralco (MER PM). PLDT, in our view, needs to have a ‘permanent’ CEO quickly and can’t afford another lengthy period looking for one.

+++

With CPI inflation dipping further to 4.1%, the likelihood of a policy rate increase next month should be pretty low. In fact, the outlook for the headline CPI inflation number over the next three months will appear good because of the high base of comparison in early 2023. The key risk by March (and beyond), we believe, will be commodity prices – not just energy but also food, especially rice.

+++

ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) announced yesterday that they are partnering with a subsidiary of US-based BrightNight LLC for renewable energy projects in the Philippines. Although it did not provide the scale of the partnership, we learned that BrightNight, on its own, already has 1GW worth of projects under the development in the country. BrightNight is another addition to its growing list of partners as it aims to develop at least 7GW of new projects in the Philippines and 18GW including international projects over the next seven years.

Separately, ACEN said that it could accelerate the transition of the SLTEC coal plant to clean technology to as early as 2030 (from 2040) with the use of “transition credits”. It has, through developments in COP28 Dubai, partnered with The Rockefeller Foundation and the Monetary Authority of Singapore to develop a “Transition Credit” which would use carbon finance to phase out coal-fired plants and replace it with renewable energy.

We published a report on Ayala Land (ALI PM), detailing the upcoming property acquisitions of AREIT. After the series of transactions, the proportion of AREIT’s AUM from offices will drop from 88% to 76%. We also estimate that the acquisitions will be neutral (mildly accretive) for Ayala Land’s NAV.

MACRO
Inflation eases further to 4.1% in November
  • Headline CPI rose at a slower rate of 4.1% YoY in November from 4.9% in October
    • This is below consensus estimate (4.3%) and near the low-end of the BSP’s forecast range (4.0%-4.8%)
    • Softening due to slower increases in food (e.g. vegetables, meat, fish, sugar) and oil prices
    • Offset by an acceleration in rice inflation (15.8% in November from 13.2% in October)
  • On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.2%, accelerating from -0.2% in October due largely to higher rice prices
    • Non-food items decelerated further to +0.05% from +0.20% in October
  • Core CPI also eased to 4.7% from 5.3%, with core non-food item portion also rising at a slower rate of 4.2% (vs. 4.6% in Oct)
  • Average headline inflation rate in 11M23 at 6.2%
  • Monetary Board meeting on Dec 14 (consensus: no change)
  • BSP inflation comment: Monetary Board deems it necessary to keep monetary policy settings sufficiently tight until a sustained downward trend in inflation becomes evident. Accordingly, the BSP will continue to monitor inflation expectations and second-round effects and take appropriate action as needed to bring inflation back to the target, in keeping with the BSP’s price stability mandate.

 

MACRO
Government debt rises to P14.48tn in October
  • The national government’s debt stock reached P14.48tn in October, up 1.49% MoM
    • MoM increase due to net issuances of domestic and external loans
    • Debt stock also 7.9% higher than in end-2022
  • The government is targeting the debt stock to reach P14.62tn by year-end, equivalent to a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61%
    • By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP
    • Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028

POWER
ACEN partners with US firm for projects
  • ACEN disclosed that it approved a proposed partnership with BrightNight APAC B.V. to establish a local renewable energy (RE) platform
  • The company did not disclose details of the RE platform it seeks to establish
  • BrightNight APAC is wholly owned by BrightNight LLC
    • It has developed a renewable power portfolio of 2.7GW across the Philippines, India, Australia and Bangladesh
    • 1GW is under development in the Philippines
  • In March, ACEN signed a partnership with BrightNight to develop projects in India
  • ACEN currently has 4.4GW of attributable capacity and a medium-term pipeline of 18GW largely in Australia and the Philippines
