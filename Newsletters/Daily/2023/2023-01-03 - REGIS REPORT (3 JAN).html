REGIS REPORT

03 January 2023

Dominating the headlines today was the breakdown of the air traffic management system of the entire country, which greeted travelers during what is supposed to be peak travel season. What began as a problem with the uninterruptable power supply (UPS) of the Communications, Navigation and Surveillance/Air Traffic Management (CNS/ATM) system led to the standstill of all traffic across the country’s airspace beginning the morning of Jan 1. No flights could take off or land for a number of hours across the entire Philippines until operations were partially restored late afternoon. Hundreds of flights were canceled, affecting likely more than 100,000 travelers by now.

Operation of CNS/ATM has been partially restored but full operation is expected only by tomorrow (Jan 4). The system was last fully upgraded in 2019, after a process that began almost nine years prior.

This fiasco is likely to lead to an examination of the country’s transportation systems, which could reopen the debate to privatize more of the country’s infrastructure.

+++

The deadline for firms to transfer their registration (to avail of fiscal incentives) from PEZA to the BOI has been extended by one month to Jan 31. The BOI has more flexible rules with regard to work-from-home (WFH) than PEZA, and allowing more employees to work remotely would lead to diminution of demand for formal office spaces.

Despite the return of on-site work for most firms, we expect vacancy rates for office space to rise further from the 18% in 2022.

+++

Meanwhile, the Dept of Agriculture (DA) is forecasting that shortages in rice as well as pork will continue into 2023. The DA is expecting a shortage of at least 3m MT of rice, equivalent to ~20% of total consumption, this year. The gap, which would have to be filled by imports, would be higher than it has typically been (~2m MT) because of lower rice production due to higher costs (fertilizer, fuel).

The pork shortage, on the other hand, is a result of the African swine fever (ASF), which ravaged the hog population, cutting it by a quarter. The restoration of domestic hog supply is expected to take years.

Shortages of agricultural products are likely to be a recurring theme over the next few years as the new administration grapples with balancing the President’s protectionist instincts with the security and affordability of food supply.

MACRO
Hot money nets US$489mn inflow in November
  • Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. hot money) recorded a net inflow of US$489mn in November
    • Higher than the US$83mn net inflow in October 2022 and US$110mn net inflow in November 2021
    • Note that the Philippine Stock Exchange recorded foreign net outflows of US$99.4mn in November 2022
  • Gross inflows amounted to US$1.1bn, up 64% MoM, while gross outflows were up 0.8% MoM to US$566mn
  • 11M22 foreign portfolio investments yielded net inflows of US$794mn, a turnaround from the US$570mn net outflow in 11M21
MACRO
Tourist arrivals hit 2.65mn in 2022
  • The Philippines received 2.65mn international tourist arrivals in 2022
    • Of which, 2.02mn were foreigners while 628k were returning Filipinos (or Philippine passport holders who are permanently residing abroad)
    • Top markets by volume were US (19%), Korea (16%), Australia (5%), Canada (5%)
  • For 2023, the government is targeting 4.8mn international tourist arrivals
  • In 2019, tourist arrivals hit 8.26mn
    • Top markets by volume were Korea (24%), China (21%), USA (13%) and Japan (8%)
MACRO
Rice, pork shortages expected in 2023
  • The country will be in short supply of rice by more than 3mn MT this year due to high fertilizer and fuel costs
    • According to Agriculture Undersecretary Fermin Adriano
  • Apart from rice, Mr. Adriano warned there will also be a shortage of pork due to continuing disruptions to grower operations posed by the African Swine Fever
    • The country lost three million head of swine to ASF from our hog population of 12 million
    • Assuming repopulation 400,000 hogs per year, it will take more than five years to repopulate our stock to pre-ASF levels
  • Rice and meat account for 8.9% and 6.4%, respectively, of the CPI basket
PROPERTY
Deadline for BPO firms to transfer to BOI extended
  • The deadline for BPO firms to transfer their registration from PEZA to BOI was extended to 31 January 2023
    • The previous deadline was December 2022
  • Only 786 out of 1,100 applications have been endorsed by PEZA to BOI
  • BOI does not set limits on work-from-home but PEZA sets a cap
    • Beginning 1 January 2023, PEZA only allows 10% of employees to work from home
  • The number of applications suggest that most BPO firms would like to flexibility to fully implement work-from-home policies
  • BPO firms, which are predominantly registered with PEZA, occupy 60%-90% of office space of the developers we cover
  • We forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 22% in 2023E (from 18% in 2022)
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) comprise:
    • 55% of FLI’s NAV
    • 41% of MEG’s NAV
    • 30% of RLC’s NAV
  • FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY23E P/B, and 6.0X FY23E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY23E P/B, and 4.2X FY23E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 60% discount to NAV, 0.6X FY22E P/B, and 7.7X FY23E P/E

PROPERTY
Filinvest REIT to acquire more office buildings from Filinvest Land
  • Filinvest REIT is looking to acquire eight office buildings from its sponsor, Filinvest Land, from 2023 to 2025
  • The office buildings have a combined leasable area of 164,000sqm
  • Six of the buildings are in Clark, Makati, and Pasay
  • Two of the buildings are in existing FILRT areas (Alabang or Cebu)
  • We estimate that Filinvest Land’s non-REIT portfolio has a vacancy rate of about 60%, much higher than FILRT’s vacancy rate of 12%
  • FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY23E P/B, and 6.0X FY23E P/E
  • FILRT currently offers a 2023E dividend yield of 7.6% (based on consensus forecast)
