REGIS REPORT

19 June 2023

The current account deficit (CAD) for 1Q23 as reported by the government came in at US$4.3bn, slightly higher than the US$4.0bn of the previous year. Despite this, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) tweaked its FY23 CAD forecast downwards to US$15.1bn, equivalent to 3.4% of GDP. The latest revision was driven by the fall in commodity prices and the impact on imports and the trade deficit.

The country is facing the proverbial twin deficits: on the current account as well as on the national budget (6% of GDP). Taming them will be important steps to improving investor confidence.

+++

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) says it’s interested in the O&M contracts for two new toll roads in Cebu. We are doubtful, however, about whether these projects will happen soon or at all. MPI already operates one toll road (Cebu Cordova Link Expressway or CCLEX) and vehicular traffic there has been rather poor.

+++

The BIR is cracking down on a manufacturer of sweetened beverages for deficiencies in the payment of excise taxes for its products. The company is a toll manufacturer for SM’s (SM PM) supermarkets and Puregold’s (PGOLD PM) S&R. We understand that both retailers have severed ties with the company following recent raids on several SM and S&R stores.

The sweetened beverage tax is collected from manufacturers and accounts for approximately 20% of the retail price. The quantum of the tax provides a strong incentive for manufacturers to reduce and/or avoid paying it.

This is not unlike the situation for cigarettes but, of course, the financial incentive for illicit trade there is much higher. Taxes account for more than half of the retail price per pack of cigarettes.

MACRO
Current account deficit mildly higher in 1Q23 due to wider trade deficit
  • The current account deficit was slightly higher at US$4.3bn in 1Q23 vs. US$4.0bn deficit in 1Q22
    • Due to wider trade in goods deficit and lower net receipts in primary income account, offset by higher net receipts in services
  • Trade in goods posted a wider deficit of US$17bn in 1Q23, up 8.2% YoY
    • Due to faster decline in exports (-10% YoY) vis-à-vis import drop (-0.5% YoY)
  • Net receipts of trade in services rose by 40.9% YoY to US$4.5bn due to upsurge in receipts on travel services (US$2.2bn, up 617% YoY) and higher BPO export revenues of US$6.7bn (+8.3% YoY)
  • Primary income posted net receipts of US$815mn in 1Q23, down by 29% YoY
  • Secondary income account posted slightly higher (+0.05% YoY) net receipts of US$7.3bn in 1Q23, driven by the 3.2% YoY increase in remittances
  • The BSP expects the current account deficit to narrow to US$15.1bn (3.4% of GDP) in 2023 from US$17.8bn in 2022

MACRO
BSP trims BOP forecasts
  • The Monetary Board lowered its BOP, current account deficit projections
    • This was after taking into account 1Q23 developments
  • For 2023, the BOP deficit is expected to narrow to US$1.2bn (-0.3% of GDP), lower than the previous forecast of US$1.6bn (-0.4% of GDP)
    • Cited narrower trade gap, with import growth likely to slow down sharply following the pullback of commodity prices and export growth likely to be weaker

INFRASTRUCTURE
Metro Pacific eyes Cebu expressway, Bohol bridge
  • Metro Pacific is keen to pursue its plans to vie for O&M contract for the proposed P94bn Metro Cebu Expressway (MCE) and the P90bn Cebu-Bohol bridge (CBB)
  • DPWH was supposed to issue the privatization contract for the MCE in 1Q
    • The government must select its consultant before it conducts any supplemental study on the project
    • The DPWH is scheduled to conduct the supplemental study in September and will end it in Feb 2024
    • Tender for the PPP is scheduled on June 2024-Feb 2025; PPP contract signing on March 2025
    • MCE will span 56.94km and will require a budget of P94bn, of which P61.5bn will go to civil works
    • It will cut travel time from Naga City in the south of Cebu to Danao City in the north
  • CBB bridge will be a floating bridge and will link Cordova, Cebu with Olango island
    • MPTC President Rodrigo E. Franco also expressed concerns about the viability of the project, given the high cost
    • The project was previously deemed “impossible” by the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) in 2019 due to technological limitations
CONSUMER
BIR cracks down on sweetened beverage manufacturer for deficiency in excise tax payments
  • A few weeks ago, the Bureau of Internal Revenue raided several outlets of S&R (PGOLD) and SM Supermarkets (SM Retail) after cases of sweetened beverages manufactured by Vermirich Food Corp. were found in its shelves
  • Vermirich Foods supposedly failed to pay the excise taxes on its sweetened beverage products from 2018 onwards
  • It was reported that SM Retail and S&R have terminated its relationship with Vermirich following the incident
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market remains flat in May
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the PSE Index and the overall market both fell 1 basis point MoM to 23.3% and 20.1% respectively (in May)
  • Market net foreign flows reversed to an outflow of US$81mn in May (from US$34mn of inflow in April)
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 15 saw declines in foreign ownership while 15 experienced increases
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • MONDE – we estimate that foreign ownership fell 1.3ppt to MoM to 59.3%. It was removed from the MSCI Index in May and saw the highest net foreign selling (at US$22.6mn). 
    • ACEN – foreign ownership dropped 30 basis points MoM to 25.1%. It also experienced the second-highest net foreign selling in May (at US$16.7mn).   
    • MPI – foreign ownership increased 28 basis points MoM to 11.9%.
