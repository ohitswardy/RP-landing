REGIS REPORT

15 November 2024

We are now heading into the homestretch of 3Q results, with yesterday's batch reporting stronger-than-expected profits. 

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – 3Q24 profits rebounded by 11% QoQ and 15% YoY to P7.46bn, bringing 9M24 profits up 7% to P20.9bn, ahead of REGIS forecasts but is broadly in line with consensus. Its key business units delivered strong results, including Toyota, which reported a 32% QoQ, 62% YoY increase in profits, buoyed by the favorable FX movements and higher wholesale volumes. The property unit weighed down profits, after booking wider losses on higher cancellations.   

Security Bank (SECB PM) (=) – 9M24 NPAT came in at P8.46bn, up 12% YoY, with 3Q alone at P3.0bn, putting it broadly in line with our P11.7bn forecast for FY24, but ahead of the Street (P10.9bn). Loan growth surged to 24% YoY, driven by corp/commercial loans accelerating to 19% YoY vs 14% in 2Q. Consumer loans sustained its momentum, up 34% YoY (was 33% in 2Q). Profit was boosted by trading gains in 3Q24, which offset provision expense as credit costs stayed above 100bps. The bank will host a briefing at 4pm today.

Manila Water (MWC PM) (+) – 9M24 profit to common rose 39% to P8.9bn, with 3Q24 profits up by 44% YoY to P2.8bn. The results are trending ahead of consensus and has already exceeded our full-year forecast. As with previous periods, the beat largely came from lower-than-expected cash costs at its core East zone unit, as the company continued to underspend relative to its regulatory commitments. 

Vista Land (VLL PM) (+) – 9M24 net profit rose 9% to P7.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. What was more noteworthy, however,  was that presales grew 4% QoQ and 10% YoY, bucking the industry downtrend.

+++

Figures released by the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, suggest that 3Q24 gross gaming revenue (GGR) of e-games rose 13% QoQ (+237% YoY) to P40bn. The e-games market has been the fastest growing segment of the gaming market and is closing the gap with licensed casinos, whose GGR grew 3% QoQ to P50.7bn.

Digital payments in September sustained its strong momentum, rising by 33% YoY. See more below.

CONGLOMERATES
GTCAP: 9M24 core profit up 7% to P20.9bn - In line with consensus but ahead of Regis estimates
  • We estimate 9M24 core profit to common grew 7% YoY to P20.93bn, which appears on trend to meet consensus forecast of P27.8bn but is well ahead of Regis FY24E.
  • In 3Q24 alone, profit rose 14% YoY to P7.46bn, buoyed by higher profits from Metrobank (+11%), Toyota (+62%), and Metro Pacific (+47%)
    • These were offset by (a) net losses booked at the property unit and (b) higher parent expenses
  • Implied 3Q24 profits at its 51%-owned unit, Toyota Motor Philippines, jumped 32% QoQ and 46% YoY to P4.63bn
    • The sequential improvement was largely driven by the weakness in the USD against the PHP, as well as higher wholesale volumes (+10% QoQ vs flat retail vols)
  • The strong earnings recovery at Metro Pacific in 3Q (+20% QoQ, 32% YoY) was driven by both strong profits at Meralco, Maynilad, and the tollroad unit
  • The property unit saw net losses widen 50% QoQ to P123mn in 3Q24, which was driven by higher revenue reversals from sales cancellations and higher opex
    • Reservation sales rose 13% QoQ but was down by 47% YoY to P4.3bn in 3Q24, as the company launched its first project this year (Hartwood – horizontal project in Biñan)
    • 9M24 reservation sales are lower by 33% YoY to P12bn due to lower launches and depleted inventory in BGC
    • Unsold inventory was P30bn, of which P7bn are RFOs; We estimate this is equivalent to ~2years’ of sales
  • GTCAP trades at a 48% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,253/sh and <6x PER

BANKS
SECB 9M24 P8.5bn, in line; loans surge 24%

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 9M24 NPAT of P8.46bn, up 12% YoY. On the surface, it seems that the result is behind our P11.7bn forecast for FY25E. However, note that the 3Q24 alone was P3.0bn. As such, we would classify the 9M figure as in line with our estimates but ahead of consensus (P10.9bn). Revenues were boosted by strong trading gains in 3Q24, but profits were dragged by persistently-high credit cost (115bps in 3Q24).

Some highlights, ahead of the briefing today at 4pm:

  • Loan growth surged to 24% YoY, accelerating from the 19% pace in 2Q24. Consumer loans were still robust, up 34% YoY (was 33% in 2Q). Corporate and commercial loans jumped 19% (was 14% in 2Q). Consumer loans were still 32% of total loans up from 29% in 2023.
  • NIMs seemed to have collapsed to 4.55% from 5.01% in 2Q. However, recall that the prior quarters were bloated by the interest rate swaps, whose swap portion was booked as FX losses. Recall too that there was an accounting change in 1Q24 that brought swap costs as part of interest expense. We suspect that the 3Q24 reported figure was closer to the “pro-forma” NIM data. In 2Q24, the pro-forma NIM was 4.61%
  • Fee incomes jumped 55% in 9M24, due in part to progress payments from SECB’s bancassurance partner in 1Q24. Fees in 3Q24 alone were still solid, up 20% YoY.
  • Profits were boosted by trading gains of P889mn in 3Q24 alone, as interest rates fell. The reversal of the swap accounting also meant that the bank booked FX gains, instead of the massive losses in previous quarters.
  • Opex was up 24% due to staffing and IT costs.
  • Provisions jumped 84% YoY in 9M24 to P5.1bn. We suspect there were chunky write-offs, as end-period NPL ratio fell to 3.08% (was 3.31% in 2Q). Credit cost remained high (115bps in 3Q alone). It seems as though the bank’s forecast of 90-100bps for FY24 will be exceeded.
  • ROE climbed to 8.6% for 3Q24. The bank earlier guided for a possible ROE of 10% by 4Q24.

A mixed bag, but the trends seem to be tilting towards the positive side. We will learn more tomorrow at the briefing.

BANKS
Digital payments accelerate +33% YoY in September

Payments made using digital channels in September jumped to P1,473bn, up 33% YoY, accelerating from the 29% pace in August. This took the 9M24 figure up to P12,369bn, up 34% YoY.

Transactions over PesoNet – larger, batched payments – came in at P850.5bn, speeding up to 25.9% YoY, compared to the 19.7% clip in August. Given the larger amounts involved, we do expect greater volatility in the monthly growth figure. Year-to-date, PesoNet handled P7,225bn worth of payments, up 27% YoY.

Meanwhile, InstaPay transactions – smaller-tickets and in real-time – continued to surge 45.1% YoY to P622bn, fast catching up with PesoNet’s monthly figure. The rapid expansion has been relatively consistent all year, with the 9M24 level up 44.9% YoY to P5,144bn.

The sustained growth of InstaPay speaks to the continued growth in adoption of digital payments by Filipino individuals, and acceptance by retailers. We note that the number of transactions over InstaPay was up an impressive 67% YoY in September, though that’s a tad slower than the 71% YoY pace for the 9M24 total.

While banks are all making bold IT investments, we reckon that of the big banks, BPI (BPI PM) has taken a sizeable lead in terms of digitalization on the deposit, loan, and payments segments. Meanwhile, Globe’s (GLO PM) e-wallet Mynt / GCash remain the dominant player in the payments space.
