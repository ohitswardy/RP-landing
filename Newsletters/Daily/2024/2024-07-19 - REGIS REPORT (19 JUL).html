19 July 2024

Chris Wood, in this week’s edition of GREED&fear , highlights that the Philippines is “likely to rebound sharply on… Fed easing expectations”. He echoes what we’ve been saying since the start of the year – the market is cheap at 10.2x PE, with 13% earnings growth, and a decent yield of c4%. And investor positioning is extremely light. Foreigner ownership is at c20% near historic lows. Local AUMs are up, but their equity weights are extremely low.

Chris also cites our recent work on rice prices, and their impact on CPI, real interest rates, and consequently the BSP’s likely policy rate cuts in August. Have a read of the attached PDF (Philippines discussion starts on page 11).

We kick off the 2Q2024 reporting season with a strong print from BPI (BPI PM), posting 1H24 NPAT of P30.6bn, up 22% YoY, and tracking ahead of Regis estimates (FY24E P59.4bn), which are already near the top of the Street. Loan growth remained robust at 18% YoY (we estimate c11% excluding the Robinsons Bank merger), driven by explosive personal/SME/microfinance loans. The high-yield portfolio boosted NIMs to 4.26% (from 4.19% in 1Q, by mgt estimates), though these also pushed NPLs up to 2.20% (from 2.12% in 1Q). In line with management guidance, BPI continues to bring NPL coverage down to 128% (from 136% in 1Q). The bank will host an earnings call on Friday, 26 July at 4:30pm HK/PH time (registration link here). Check out Rafa’s short note.

The two parties that submitted the lowest bids for Meralco’s (MER PM) power supply auction (aka CSP or “competitive selection process”) were San Miguel (SMC PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM).  SMC submitted a bid of P7.10/kwh for 350MW, while ACEN submitted a bid of P8.2/kwh for 160MW.

What’s interesting here is how high the prices of power are under these contracts.  The peak for spot prices this year was in May when prices averaged P8.65/kwh, and the (simple) average was P6.29/kwh. These contract prices (P7.1 and P8.2 per kwh) are higher than the average for 1H23 which occurred under very tight supply conditions.

For additional perspective, Terra Solar’s (now 100% by SPNEC (SPNEC PM), itself a subsidiary of Meralco) contract for 850MW of mid-merit power supply was priced at only P5.80/kwh. Terra Solar intends to supply the power from combined solar farm – battery storage facilities.

We view the pricing outcome of the CSP to be indicative of the expected tightness of Luzon power supply.

  • San Miguel’s (SMC PM) San Roque Hydropower Inc. (SRHI) offered the lowest total headline rate of P7.10 per kilowatt-hour (kWH) for 340 megawatts (MW) of the total requirement

  • To date, Meralco has contracted 1,880 MW of RE capacity, surpassing its initial target of 1,500 MW
  • RE is expected to account for 22% of the company’s supply portfolio over the next 6 years
