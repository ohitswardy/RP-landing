REGIS REPORT

15 January 2024

We publish our 2024 market outlook reiterating our bullish view, notwithstanding the expectations of some that 2024 will be another case of a strong start that quickly fizzles out.

Among the highlights:

  • Valuations today remain compelling 11x earnings after successive years of profit growth with no commensurate price expansion
  • Dividends have emerged as an attraction, with yields of 4-5% not uncommon
  • The big picture driver will be the global interest rate cycle. With global / US rates expected to fall, the Philippine market is a geared beta play.
  • We would overweight the property and consumer sectors and also conglomerates as they’d likely be the first port of call for any inflows into the domestic market
  • Our top picks are: Ayala Corp (AC PM), Ayala Land (ALI PM), GT Capital (GTCAP PM), Jollibee (JFC PM), ICTSI (ICT PM) and Globe (GLO PM)
  • As always, we also have to be wary of risks.  The main ones we see are on the impact of El Nino and the current account.

BANKS
Commercial bank loan growth slows to 7.0% in November; M3 growth decelerates to 7.0%
  • Preliminary data showed commercial bank loans grew 7.0% YoY in November, mildly slowing down from 7.1% in October
    • Loans for productive activities grew 5.7% YoY, decelerating from 5.9% in October
      • Loans to manufacturing sector fell at a slower rate of 0.1% YoY from -3.6% in Oct
      • Real estate loan growth accelerated to 11.9% YoY from 8.3% YoY the previous month
    • Consumer loans rose 23.6% YoY, faster than the 22.8% YoY growth in October
      • Driven by the faster increase in credit card loans, motor vehicle loans, and salary-based general purpose consumption loans
    • We forecast loan growth of 12% across the period 2023-25E
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew at a slower rate of 7.0% in November from 8.1% in October

 

TRANSPORT
Losing bidders for South Africa’s Durban port demand basis for selection of ICTSI
  • Companies that lost out to ICTSI (ICT PM) for the right to operate DCT-2 Durban port in South Africa have demanded to know why it won the deal
    • According to state-owned Transnet SOC Ltd.
    • Transnet is in the process of responding to any requests for information
    • Transnet previously said that it shortlisted 10 bidders in Aug 2022 and there were six bidders at the final stage
  • Last July, Transnet selected ICT to be its equity partner to run and expand Durban Container Terminal Pier 2
    • Under the agreement, ICTSI will pay an undisclosed amount to own 50%-1 share of the a newly formed JV company while its partner, Transnet will own 50%+1 share
    • Assets at the terminal are currently worth R2.5bn (~US$130mn) and at the end of the contract, Transnet will buy the shares back from the partner
    • The transaction is expected to be competed by April 2024
  • Our forecasts and valuation do not assume the acquisition of Durban; ICTSI also has not signed the concession agreement nor has it paid for its equity stake
  • ICTSI trades at 7.8x FY24E EV/EBITDA, well below peers (11x) and historical average (11x)
TELCOS
GCash could conduct IPO in 2H24
  • Oscar Reyes Jr., President and CEO of G-Xchange, the operator of GCash, said that the company is preparing internally to that it can be ready for an IPO by 2H24
  • Mynt, the parent of GCash, contributed P648mn (13%) to Globe’s 3Q23 net profit
  • Globe owns 36% of Mynt
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in December
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 18bps MoM (-61bps YoY) to 20.4%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index also increased 9bps MoM (-43bps YoY) to 24.6%
  • Net foreign buying stood at US$18mn, flat from November
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 18 saw declines in foreign ownership while 12 experienced increases
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • ICT – foreign ownership rose 61bps MoM to 33.5%. It also experienced the second highest net foreign buying for the month (at US$25.6mn)
    • ALI – foreign ownership increased 42bps MoM to 28.2%. Net foreign buying was US$37.9mn, the highest for the month.
    • CNPF – foreign ownership dropped 54bps MoM to 25.3%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$3.1mn
    • PGOLD – foreign ownership declined 32bps MoM to 18.0%. Net foreign selling hit US$4.4mn
    • URC – foreign ownership fell 25bps MoM to 24.4%. It also experienced the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$14.9mn)
