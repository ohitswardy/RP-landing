REGIS REPORT

01 August 2024

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported NPAT for 1H24 of P23.6bn, up 13% YoY, in line with Regis (50% of FY24E P46.9bn) and consensus (P47.1bn) estimates. As with BPI and BDO, profits were boosted by a sharp 77% drop in provision expense to P1.0bn, as the banks wind down their over-provisioning. The main difference, though is that MBT’s NPL went down a smidge (-4bps QoQ) to 1.66%. Gross loan growth accelerated to 14.9% YoY from 12% in 1Q, again driven by corporate loans, up 15.2%, while consumer loans grew a respectable 13.7%,

We maintain our Buy call on MBT on valuations, as the stock trades at 6.5x PER and 0.80x P/bk on our FY2024E estimates.

Management will hold a briefing tomorrow, Friday at 4pm. Ahead of that, some highlights:

  • Gross loans expanded 14.9% YoY (net +16.3%), accelerating from the 12% clip in 1Q24. Growth was driven by corporate loans, up 15.2%. Consumer loans were up 13.7%, driven by credit cards (+21.4%) and auto loans (+16.6%). Deposits grew 8% YoY, though CASA ratio dipped slightly to 58%.
  • Net interest income was up 15%, though NIMs edged lower by 4bps QoQ to 3.74%.  Loan yields were up 14bps QoQ, and deposit costs were flat (+1bp qoQ). However, it seems overall funding costs climbed more (+9bps QoQ) as the bank issued new bonds at much higher rates than those refinanced.
  • Fee incomes rebounded, up 7% YoY in 2Q, from -5% in 1Q24. Meanwhile, the bank booked trading/fx losses of around P1.1bn in 2Q alone.
  • Opex was kept under control, up 8% in 1H24 (+10% in 2Q), as compensation expenses moderated in 2Q.
  • PPOP was up 6% YoY in 1H24, in line with our forecasts.
  • Provision expenses plunged 77% to P1.0bn, with credit cost of around 11bps, well below management guidance of a “normalization” to 50-60bps. NPL ratio improved slightly to 1.66% (from 1.70%). As with BDO and BPI, NPL cover dropped to 163% (from 174%) as the bank winds down excess provisioning. We suspect there could have been significant write-offs in 2Q.

Overall, a solid set of results. More after the briefing.

Wilcon’s (WLCON PM) 2Q results showed that, while things are less bad, a recovery is not yet evident.  Net profit of P1.5bn for 1H was 17% down YoY.  For 2Q alone, profit was down 10% to P770m. Same-store sales (SSS) was still down 2% in the quarter despite a relatively favorable base of comparison. The overall market still looks weak due to consumer pressures. We are optimistic things are ready to turn once inflation pressures on the consumer ease.  Have a look at Carissa’s (carissa.mangubat@regis.ph) note:

+++

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) says July inflation is likely to be above 4%.  This isn’t a scenario that’s totally unexpected given that the July 2023 base is low. The BSP’s forecast, however, and its guidance of a rate in Aug should help manage expectations if CPI inflation does indeed exceed 4% at next week’s report.

 

Meanwhile, perplexingly, rice imports seem to have slowed down after the tariff reduction. Rice import tariffs were cut to 15% from 35% after July 5 but importers seem to have imported little if the official data is to be believed.

 

+++

A restraining order against Meralco’s (MER PM) competitive selection process (CSP) to procure as much as 1,000MW of power supply has been issued.  The power supply contract is supposed to commence in Sept 2025 (more than a year from now). Parties associated with the Malampaya gas field operations sought the order as they questioned the alleged bias against power gencos (i.e. plants owned by First Gen (FGEN PM)) that buy fuel from Malampaya that is embedded in the terms of reference (TOR).  A senator (Sen Alan Cayetano) previously questioned the rationale behind requiring the supply power plant to be less than five years old.

 

+++

In its 2Q24 report, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila condominium sales fell 58% YoY in 1H24. As a result, unsold condominium inventory now represents 4.9 years’ worth of take-up, a rapid deterioration from 3.6 years in 1Q24. Meanwhile, it estimates that the POGO ban could lift office vacancies from the current 18% to 22%.

 

+++

Trade Secretary Alfredo Pascual resigned effective Aug 2.  It’s unclear what’s the story behind the departure but there has been a stream of attacks against him.  No replacement has been named.

MACRO
BSP predicts July inflation likely breached upper 4% target
  • The BSP expects headline inflation for the month of July to settle between 4-4.8%
    • This would be higher than June inflation of 3.7% and would be the first time in seven months that inflation would be above 4%
  • Cited higher electricity rates and domestic oil prices, as well as higher food prices (vegetables, meat, and fruits)
    • Expected to be offset by lower rice prices and the appreciation of the PHP against the USD
  • BSP Governor Remolona previously flagged that they expect inflation to breach its target band of 2-4% in July before returning to its band by 3Q
    • Despite a possible uptick in July, the BSP said it is already considering to cut its policy rate by 25bps on Aug 15, and possibly another 25bps in 4Q
  • The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release July CPI data on Aug 6 (consensus: 4.0%)
MACRO
Rice imports slow down as traders still cautious
  • Data from the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI) showed that rice imports levied with 15% tariff rate reached 56,073 metric tons as of July 20 (vs monthly average of 400,000mt in 1H24)
    • Note that the reduction in rice tariffs took effect on July 5
  • Department of Agriculture (DA) said it is possible that rice importers and traders are still unloading earlier stocks imported at the 35% tariff before bringing in new stocks
    • Some rice traders and importers were hesitant to import pending the decision of the Supreme Court on Executive Order (EO) 62
  • Nevertheless, the DA expects import arrivals to pick up in the coming weeks
    • Substantial volume is said to have a “significant” effect on pulling down retail rice prices
    • Prevailing rice prices were said to fall below P50/kg once imported rice stocks with lower tariff rates start to be sold in the market
    • The retail price of imported well-milled rice in Metro Manila ranges from P51 to P53/kg while regular-milled rice is P47-48/kg

POWER
Taguig court issues TRO vs Meralco’s power supply bidding; Resolution filed at Senate
  • The Regional Trial Court of Taguig granted the petition of the members of the consortium operating the Malampaya gas field to issue a 72-hour temporary restraining order (TRO) against Meralco’s conduct of competitive selection process for its 600 MW and 400 MW power supply requirements
    • Plaintiffs were: Prime Energy, Prime Oil and Gas Inc, UC38 LLC, and the Philippine National Oil Exploration Corp (PNOC-EC)
  • Meralco earlier invited bids for a contract capacity of 600 MW (bid deadline on Aug 2), which is set to take effect in September 2025
    • It also invited bids for an additional contract capacity of 400 MW, effective September 2025
  • In a previous petition, the Malampaya consortium stressed that pursuing the bidding can put indigenous natural gas at a disadvantage, “violat[ing] the preference given” by existing laws
    • “Increased reliance on imported sources of fuel threatens the country’s energy security and energy sovereignty because these are greatly susceptible to a volatile market,” the previous petition said.
    • It also said Meralco’s bidding done through the Competitive Selection Process (CSP) was “flawed, skewed or supplier-driven and grossly violative of existing laws, rules and regulations.”
    • It said that awarding PSAs only to suppliers using imported fuel will also harm the exploration and development of indigenous resources, and will affect the Malampaya project, which has been reliably and securely supplying natural gas to power plants that provide 30 to 40 percent of Luzon’s electricity requirements
  • Meanwhile, Senator Alan Peter Cayetano filed Resolution 1090 on July 29, to call on Meralco to defer its CSP “until its terms of reference (TOR) are reviewed"
    • Senator Cayetano said the TOR remains unclear and may put certain bidders at an advantage at the expense of power suppliers that use indigenous natural gas
POWER
SPNEC expects entry of foreign investors in Q4
  • SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM) wants to secure foreign investments for its P200bn Terra Solar development as early as October this year
    • A “very long list” of actively interested foreign investors eager to partner with them and support the project
    • They are keen to welcome “at most two” investors, though “most likely one.”
    • According to Meralco Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, Manuel V. Pangilinan
  • Actual bid date for the binding offer is on Aug. 15
  • Company is willing to sell as much as 40% of Terra Solar project, which is deemed to consist of 3,500MW of solar panels and 4,000 MWh of battery storage
    • The first phase, with a target capacity of 2,500 MW, will be finished by 2026, while the second phase with a capacity of 1,000 MW is targeted for completion by 2027
PROPERTY
Colliers estimates 1H24 condominium sales fell 58% YoY
  • In its 2Q24 report, Colliers reported that 1H24 Metro Manila condominium sales fell 58% YoY to 6,200 units
    • 2Q24 unit sales fell 64% YoY (up 6% QoQ) to 3,200 units
  • As a result, unsold condominium inventory in Metro Manila represents 4.9 years’ worth of take-up 
    • From 3.6 years in 1Q24, 3.1 years in 4Q23
  • Meanwhile, it also reported that Metro Manila office vacancy improved 1ppt QoQ to 18%, thanks in large part to office transactions by government agencies in the Manila Bay area
  • However, it estimates that the POGO ban could lift the Metro Manila vacancy rate to 22%
    • We estimate that the POGO ban could also lift the Manila Bay area vacancy rate to 50%
