REGIS REPORT

16 December 2024

According to the Bangko Sentral (BSP), the country’s current account deficit (CAD) widened to US$5.7bn for 3Q24, taking the YT-Sept CAD to US$12.9bn (+19% YoY).

The trade deficit for 9M, as tracked by the BSP, amounted to US$50bn, unchanged YoY. The surplus in net service receipts, however, narrowed due to an increase in service imports.

Overall, the CAD for the nine-month period was equivalent to 3.9% of GDP, much wider than target for the year (=1% of GDP). The wider CAD signals natural pressure for the PHP to depreciate, especially since foreign direct investments (FDI) into the Philippines has historically been small.

Megaworld (MEG PM) CEO Kevin Tan says the company will be launching three new townships in 2025 as he remains bullish about the luxury residential segment of the industry. We expect the townships to be in provincial areas.

Residential property sales, particularly for lower/mid-income projects with Metro Manila, have been struggling in recent quarters. Only high-end residential property has been able resist the headwinds.

+++

DoubleDragon (DD PM) provided very ambitious long-term targets for various operating and financial targets for 2035, including for revenue and profit as well as its presence in overseas. The company failed to say how many CityMalls it aims to have by that time. Some of the group’s (including affiliates’) previous financial and business targets appear to have been abandoned in favor of newer businesses.

MACRO
Current account deficit widens to US$5.7bn in 3Q24
  • The BSP reported that the country’s current account deficit widened to US$5.67bn in 3Q24, up 10% QoQ/154% YoY and accounted for 5.2% of GDP
    • Due to a 10% QoQ, YoY expansion in the trade deficit to US$18.5bn, on the back of lower exports and higher imports
    • Electronic exports fell 27% YoY to US$4.6bn, which the BSP attributed this to a shift in international demand for new electronic products (e.g. AI, EVs, solar panels, batteries, and high-end semiconductors)
    • Higher imports driven by the 31% YoY expansion in iron and steel imports to US$1.4bn, as well as miscellaneous manufactures, such as magnetic tapes suitable for computer use, which rose by 15.1% to US$1.5bn
  • Brings YT-September current account deficit to US$12.93bn, up 19% YoY and equivalent to 3.9% of GDP
    • This is trending higher than the BSP’s FY24 projection of a US$4.7bn current account deficit (1.0% of GDP)
