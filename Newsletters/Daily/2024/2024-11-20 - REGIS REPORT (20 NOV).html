REGIS REPORT

20 November 2024

Results for 3Q are all in, and aggregate earnings for 40 stocks under REGIS coverage were up 9% in 3Q24 and 8% for 9M24. While this seems behind our 11% FY24E forecast, we note that the 4Q23 totals were quite a low base, putting the 9M figures broadly in line. Meanwhile, total NPAT for the PSEi Index constituents were up 16% YoY in 3Q24 and 9% YoY in 9M24. The 3Q figure, though, was pushed up by San Miguel Corp (SMC PM, not rated) whose 3Q was boosted by a massive P25bn FX gain in the quarter alone. Excluding SMC and the mining-oriented companies (DMCI, Semirara and NIKL), PSEi profit was up 12% in 9M24.

The spread was surprisingly polarized, with relatively few “in line” results, and with the “beats” nearly matching and negating the “misses”. This was all the more impressive as some of the misses were quite severe, including unexpected losses from CEB and BLOOM.

UTILITIES surprised to the upside, led by Meralco (MER PM) and Manila Water (MWC PM), though MWC’s outperformance was due to under-spending, which is bad news longer-term. BANKS again delivered strong results. Apart from accelerating loan growth, bottom line was propped up by trading gains. PROPERTY reported NPAT was ahead, but only because the P&L was recognizing past sales. Reservation sales were pretty poor, falling another 19% QoQ in 3Q24. To the upside, cancelation rates seem to have peaked. For TELCOS, we witnessed a slowdown in industry-wide mobile revenue growth (+4% in 3Q vs +6% in 2Q) but a sharp jump in new broadband subscriptions (up 1.5x QoQ).

The CONSUMER sector was mixed, though skewed to the “miss” end of the spectrum, as inflation took its toll. What was encouraging, though, was the near-consensus among companies that 3Q24 seems to have marked the bottom, with demand seeing a rebound due to a) lower inflation, b) minimum wage hikes, and c) early election spending.

As mentioned above, the TRANSPORTATION and GAMING sectors were dragged by surprise losses from CEB and BLOOM.

We remain OVERWEIGHT the BANKS and CONSUMER sectors heading into 2025E. While falling interest rates should benefit property demand, we believe the oversupply issues in both office and residential (specifically, Metro Manila mid-income condos) could take a while to clear.

+++

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona reiterated his view that the BSP was still in an easing cycle, signaling a possible policy rate cut next month and as much as 100bp in reductions next year. Outside of the rate cuts, the monetary easing also involved a surprise 2.5ppt cut in reserve requirements, with the possibility of even more in FY25.

Regis is expecting the BSP to reduce policy rates by 25bp next month to 5.75%.

+++

The local operator of McDonald’s (Golden Arches or GADC), which is partly owned by Alliance Global (AGI PM), reported a sharp drop (-63% YoY) in 3Q earnings, squeezed by declining same store sales (-2%) and higher costs. We note the contrast with Jollibee (JFC PM) which reported SSSG of +6% for 3Q.

+++

The port authority reported (uncharacteristically) that volumes at the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) rose +10% YoY in Oct. MICT’s volume growth for 9M24 was +4%. MICT is operated by and is the largest operation of ICTSI (ICT PM).

+++

Carl (carl.sy@regis.ph) published a 3Q24 recap on the property sector, which clearly deteriorated in the third quarter, particularly the residential segment. After already falling 10% QoQ in 2Q24, presales fell another 19% QoQ (down 16% YoY). Furthermore, the level of unsold condominium inventory in Metro Manila increased from c. 5.0 years of reservation sales in 2Q24 to c. 5.5 years in 3Q24.

BSP Governor signals easing on course
  • BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said that either a 25bps cut or a pause is on the table at the Monetary Board’s final policy meeting on Dec 19
    • “We are still in the easing cycle. Either we cut in Dec or we cut in the next meeting. But gradual cuts only”
    • A total of 100bps of rate cuts could be expected next year
  • He also said that the BSP is intervening in the foreign exchange market, focusing on preventing excessive volatility
  • We anticipate the BSP to cut its policy rate by another 25bps in December, in line with Jefferies’ expectation of a 25bps cut at the Fed’s Dec meeting
MACRO
Government keen on retaining rice tariff cut
  • The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) is recommending keeping the present rice tariff rate of 15% for now
    • According to Senator Cynthia Villar during plenary deliberations on the proposed 2025 budget of the Department of Agriculture
    • Cited foreign exchange rate concerns, which would impact rice prices
  • Under Executive Order 62, the tariff rate shall be subjected to a review every four months, with the next review set in February 2025
  • Agriculture Secretary Tiu Larel Jr. said that “a lot bought “palay” (unmilled rice) at a high price of P35 per kilo in July-Aug” and they will need about six months to sell to avoid incurring huge losses, which explains the gradual decrease in the retail price of rice

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Manila port posts record high volumes in October
  • The Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) saw record high monthly volume in October of 275,028 TEUs, according to a PR from the Philippine Ports Authority
    • This suggests volumes grew by 10% YoY, accelerating from the anemic 4% pace in 9M24
    • Import volumes alone rose to a record 147,935 TEUs, up by 9% YoY, faster than the 4% growth rate in 9M24
    • MICT accounts for over half of the country’s foreign container volumes
  • Brings YT-October volumes up 5% YoY to 2.41mn TEUs, trending just slightly behind Regis FY24E of 2.9mn TEUs (+7%)
  • We estimate MICT accounts for 23% of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) volumes, 22% of revenues and 20% of EBITDA
  • ICT trades at 7.9x FY25E EV/EBITDA, below peer average (11x) and historical average (11x)
