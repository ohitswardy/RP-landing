REGIS REPORT

05 June 2024

The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA), with President Marcos as chairman, approved the reduction of rice tariffs from 35% to 15%, with the lower tariff in effect until 2028. Marcos will still need to issue an Executive Order (EO) to effect the change. Ironically, Marcos firmly opposed a tariff cut when it was first proposed in Sept 2023, just two months after rice prices started spiking. Doubly ironically, the tariff cut will now be implemented as export prices out of Vietnam (where we source 80-90% of our rice imports) are already falling. Late as it may be, the tariff cut should bring down the landed cost of imported rice by 15%. If Vietnam prices keep falling, the retail price of rice could drop even more than that.

Consumer/retail companies are the likely immediate beneficiaries, in our view, as the cuts bring relief to consumers, most especially those in the lower-income households. The 1Q24 results showed strong margin recovery, but blunted by weak volumes. Easing inflation could see demand rebound. URC and PGOLD are well positioned, and both trade at compelling valuations.

As for the BSP’s monetary policy – it’s complicated. Rice accounts for 8.9% of CPI. Sharply lower rice prices could see inflation fall below 2% in 3Q24, given the high base effects. Governor Remolona has already hinted at the possibility of a rate cut by August. However, this does risk further weakness in the PHP, which is already down 5-6% against the USD year-to-date, under-performing the region. A weak currency could feed back into higher import prices.

Speaking of inflation, the statistics office just announced that May CPI inflation was 3.9%, an acceleration from April’s 3.8% but just a tad lower than Street predictions (4.0%). For the month, the cost of housing & utilities, as well as transport, contributed to the headline inflation increase. Food inflation, while still high (5.8%), crept down.

Core inflation for the month, as reckoned by the government, was 3.1%, down from April’s 3.2%.

+++

Power firm ACEN (ACEN PM) says it will need US$6bn in equity to fund capex to reach its goal of 20GW capacity by 2030.  This does not mean, however, that the entire US$6bn will be funded externally. A portion of that will be from internally generated funds and some will be from the sale of existing holdings (i.e. “capital recycling”).

Having said that, CEO Eric Francia did explicitly acknowledge ACEN could sell shares over the next 12 to 18 months.  ACEN claims an attributable capacity of 4,852MW as of latest count, with 30% of that yet to be operational.

ACEN is among the best ways to play the structural growth in RE as well as the medium-term issues on Philippine power demand-supply.
