REGIS REPORT

06 March 2024

Two names from our coverage held their briefings yesterday to shed more light on their previously disclosed results. Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) gave us a glimpse into what to expect with the imminent opening of Solaire North, while Megaworld (MEG PM) provided us a closer look at the situation on the pre-selling market for residential property.

BLOOM management reminded us that its first integrated resort, Solaire Entertainment City, only took one quarter to reach EBITDA breakeven and management believes that it should be even easier for the upcoming second site (in Quezon City) because: 1) Quezon City is a densely populated area, 2) it can capture clients of other resorts in Entertainment City and Clark, and 3) Solaire has a stronger brand now than when the original site was completed.

Meanwhile, arguably the most important takeaway from MEG’s briefing was management’s cautious outlook on the residential market. Half of MEG’s P148bn in unsold inventory is now comprised of ready-for-occupancy units (vs. just 20% for most other developers). As a result, the company will cut launches by nearly 40% in order to manage inventory levels.

Two other firms, Aboitiz Power (AP PM) and DMCI Holdings (DMC PM), issued their own press releases ahead of their respective earnings briefings.

Aboitiz Power reported that FY23 net income rose 27% to P35bn while core income went up 20% to P32bn. The 4Q numbers disappointed with core income for the final quarter alone coming in at only P5.2bn, the lowest print since 1Q22.  AP will only hold its briefing next week (March 15), so we’ll have to wait till then to get more insight.

Finally, DMCI’s 4Q23 recurring profit rose 21% YoY and 25% QoQ to P4.78bn, bringing FY23 profit down 19% YoY to P24.66bn.  The full-year figure is BELOW Regis (P27.6bn) and consensus (P27.3bn) forecasts due shortfalls from Semirara (SCC PM), construction (lower booked revenues) and nickel mining units (lower shipments). 

+++

The acceleration of CPI inflation in Feb should remind the markets that the battle to tame it isn’t quite over. Rice prices are still climbing and the fall in other CPI components (vegetable and transport) wasn’t quite enough to keep headline inflation down.

We reckon it’s the CPI data in 2Q that will prompt the Bangko Sentral (BSP) to act, or not, to ease monetary policy. And CPI for that period will be influenced to a large extent by food prices because of El Nino. The ball is, in our view, in the Department of Agriculture’s court.

MACRO
Inflation accelerates to 3.4% in February
  • Headline CPI rose 3.4% YoY in February from 2.8% in January
    • This was above consensus (3%) and came in at the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 2.8%-3.6%
    • Uptick due to faster increases in food CPI (4.6% in Feb from 3.5% in Jan) and fuel-related transport CPI (+1.2% in Feb vs -0.4% in Jan)
      • Rice price inflation persisted, with rice CPI climbing 1.0% MoM and 23.7% YoY in Feb (base effects could start to fade in Sep 2024)
        • According to NEDA, international rice prices have started to ease and local supply is expected to increase with the dry season harvest beginning this month through April
      • Meat prices climbed 0.7% YoY and 1% MoM in February after being fairly muted in previous months
      • Base effects resulted in lower vegetable prices (-11% YoY) but this will certainly fade in March
      • Fuel-related transport inflation rose 1.3% MoM and 1.2% YoY, accelerating from January (-0.4% YoY, flat MoM)
  • Core CPI slowed to 3.6% in February from 3.6% in Jan
    • However, the “non-food” core items were broadly unchanged at +3.6% YoY for both months
  • BSP said that the risks to the inflation outlook have receded but remain tilted toward the upside
  • BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. earlier discounted the possibility of a rate cut within the first half of the year, saying such a policy move is “too soon.”
    • BSP’s next policy meeting is on April 4

BANKS
Banks' sensitivity to credit card rates
  • The BSP is currently conducting its review of the rates and fees that banks charge on credit card transactions and balances. The review is done every 6 months; in August 2023, the BSP recommended to keep interest rates capped at 3.0%/month, or 36% annually. We expect the BSP to again maintain the rates/fees charged.
  • However, we lay out below the sensitivity of each bank to potential changes to the interest rate caps. We estimate that every 25bps increase will raise pre-tax profit by between 2.5% and 4.1%, using 2023A as a base, and assuming no change in NPLs as a result of the hike. Of the banks we cover, Metrobank (MBT PM) has the largest proportion of its loan book in credit card receivables, at 9.3%. As a result, it also has the highest potential earnings upside among the Big Three banks. The impact to profit seems high for Security Bank (SECB PM) only because the 2023 earnings base was especially low, due to a cleanup of its credit card portfolio. On a normalized basis, we estimate the impact would be closer to 3%.

 

POWER
Aboitiz Power FY23/4Q23
  • FY23 income (headline) was P35bn, +27% YoY.  Core income, as reported by the company, was P32bn, +20% up YoY. The core income is in line with consensus but well below our FY23 estimates.
  • Note the 4Q core income was P5.2bn, down sharply both YoY and QoQ.  The 4Q core income was the lowest quarterly profit since 1Q22.  Management cited lower spot market prices for the drop in 4Q income but we suspect there may have been other causes.
  • For the distribution business, AP saw electricity sales rising by 6% for the year.
  • The company also declared a dividend of P2.30/share, which is ~50% of core income for FY23.  That translates to a yield of just over 6% on today’s share price.
  • Briefing is next Friday (March 15).

CONGLOS
DMCI FY23 profit down 19% YoY to P24.66bn - BELOW Regis and Stree
  • DMCI’s 4Q23 recurring profit rose 21% YoY and 25% QoQ to P4.78bn, bringing FY23 profit down 19% YoY to P24.66bn.  The full-year figure is BELOW Regis (P27.6bn) and consensus (P27.3bn) forecasts due shortfalls from Semirara (SCC PM), construction (lower booked revenues) and nickel mining units (lower shipments).  These more than offset better-than-expected numbers from property (higher non-operating income), water (better volume mix), and off-grid power divisions. 
  • Property – Reservation sales in 4Q dropped 37% QoQ but were up 14% YoY to P7.47bn.  The YoY improvement was attributed to the launches of Anissa Heights (Pasig) in November and the leisure-oriented project, Solmera Coast in August.  This took its FY23 reservation sales to P35.9bn, up 12% YoY and just a tad below FY19 (P36.7bn). Unsold inventory rose 7% YoY to P62bn due to a 17% YoY jump in RFO units to P18bn.
