REGIS REPORT

08 January 2024

Headline CPI inflation declined to 3.9% in Dec (vs 4.1% in Nov), falling to within the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target band of 2-4%.

The prices of most commodity groups saw a drop in CPI inflation, with rice being the glaring exception.  Rice inflation for Dec rose to 19.6% from 13.5% as regional export prices continued to go up (MoM and YoY).  If it were not for rice, the headline CPI inflation number would be almost 2%, near the bottom of the BSP’s target range.

Barring anything unusual, CPI inflation is likely to dip further next month (Jan 2024) on base effects alone. Some might be tempted to predict early policy rate cuts as a result.

+++

Meralco (MER PM) revealed that the lowest bids for its 1,800MW tender for power supply were from Aboitiz Power (AP PM) and San Miguel (SMC PM).  Meralco did a re-bid after the original contracts for the power supply (from Dec 2024) were terminated by San Miguel.  This new result saw AP capturing 300MW with the rest (1,500MW) still going (back) to SMC.

The other big change is that the prices for these new supply contracts (~P7/kwh) are 70% higher than they would have been (~P4/kwh) under the terminated deals bid out in 2021.

+++

The push for government, now through the Maharlika Investment Corp, to invest in (or buy into) National Grid Corp (NGCP), the private concessionaire running the country’s power grid, is becoming more visible.  Last week’s three-day island-wide blackout on Panay, which many are blaming on NGCP, has made calls for sanctions (or revocation of its license or takeover) even louder.  Synergy Grid (SGP PM) has an effective 40% economic interest in NGCP while State Grid (of China) also holds 40%.  SGP has a market capitalization of only around US$700m.

The CEO of the Maharlika fund, Joel Consing, seems amenable to the idea of investing in NGCP.  We reckon a deal to buy out State Grid (of China), for geo-political reasons, could be in motion.

MACRO
Inflation cools further to 3.9% in December; 2023 average inflation rate at 6.0%
  • Headline inflation slowed to 3.9% YoY in December from 4.1% in November
    • Slightly better than consensus: 4.0% and came in near the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.6-4.4%
    • Inflation of food and non-food items decelerated to 5.4%/2.6% in December from 5.7%/2.9%
    • Headline inflation averaged at 6.0% in 2023, higher than 5.8% in 2022
  • On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.2% in December, broadly the same rate as in November
    • Rice CPI grew at a faster MoM rate of 3.5% in December from 2.7% in November and accounted for bulk of the acceleration in food items (+0.7% MoM in Dec vs +0.2% in Nov)
    • Non-food items dipped -0.2% MoM, slowing further from +0.1% in Nov thanks to lower electricity prices and fuel prices
    • The average MoM change for the whole of 2023 was +0.3%, slower than +0.6% in 2022
  • Core inflation (excluding volatile food and fuel items) decelerated to 4.4% from 4.7%
  • We anticipate the BSP will begin cutting rates in 2Q24 (50bps), with the rest of the cuts (100bps) by 2H24

POWER
Aboitiz Power, San Miguel submit lowest bids for Meralco 1,800MW power supply
  • Meralco announced that three bidders, out of six, submitted the lowest bids to supply it 1,800MW of power from Dec 2024
  • The three are GNPower Dinginin (300MW), Mariveles Power Generation (300MW) and Excellent Energy (1,200MW)
  • GNPower Dinginin is a subsidiary of Aboitiz Power (AP PM), while the other two are subsidiaries of San Miguel Corp (SMC PM)
    • GNPower offered a total levelized cost of electricity rate of P6.8580/kWh for 300MW of the total requirement
    • Mariveles Power offered to supply another 300MW at P6.9971/kWh
    • Excellent Energy put in the bid for the biggest 1,200MW at P7.1094/kWh
  • The power supply agreements under the process will cover Meralco’s future baseload requirements for a 15-year period
  • The bidding for 1,800MW is meant to replace the capacity covered by Meralco’s 2021 power supply deals with Excellent Energy and Masinloc Power that were terminated in 2023
    • Excellent Energy was to source power from its LNG plant at P4.1462/kWh while MPPCL proposed to provide power via its coal-fired plant at P4.2605/kWh on a fixed basis
