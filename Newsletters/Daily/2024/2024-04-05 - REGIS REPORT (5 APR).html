REGIS REPORT

05 April 2024

We initiate coverage of convenience store operator Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) with a Buy and target price of P135/sh. SEVN is the licensee and operator of 7-Eleven in the Philippines and is the market leader in the chained small format grocery space. The segment is underpenetrated and growing rapidly, and thus also attracting both new and old players. Despite the presence of more competition today, we believe SEVN has significant first-mover advantages in securing key locations (Mega Manila) and greater economies of scale with respect to its distribution and franchise network. These have allowed it to produce (and maintain) ROEs of ~30%, well above those of its grocery retail peers.

On our estimates, SEVN should deliver EPS CAGR of 18% for 2024-26 which will be largely driven by store expansion (450-500 net new stores per year). Margins are likely to be stable as we expect some upward risk on rent/lease expenses, which may offset margin gains from economies of scale as franchising ramps up. At current levels (P82/share), SEVN is trading at 16x PER and 6x EBITDA, multiples which we believe undervalue the company.  Our DCF-based target price of P135/share (+65% upside) implies 2024E multiples of 27x PE and 10x EV/EBITDA, which would only put it broadly in line with regional CVS/minimart peers.

+++

The government trimmed its GDP growth targets for this year and next, and raised its budget deficit estimates as well. The new government estimates reflect more modest (realistic) growth and revenue assumptions.

The new GDP growth band for FY24 is now 6.0%-7.0% (from 6.5%-7.5%) while for FY25 it’s 6.5%-7.5% (6.5%-8.0%). The target FY24 budget deficit was also raised to P1.48trn (vs P1.40trn), equivalent to 5.6% of GDP (5.1%).

+++

Asset quality for the Philippine banks stayed broadly flat MoM in February. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dipped slightly to 3.52% from 3.53% in January, while Past Due (PD) ratio ticked up 4bps to 4.31% from 4.27% the prior month, and Restructured loans (RLs) fell 10bps MoM to 2.16%. The stability of asset quality is positive, in our view. As the saying goes, “no news is good news.”

Meanwhile, in real good news, loan growth re-accelerated to 10% in February, compared to 8.8% in January. We are forecasting system-wide growth of 10-12% for 2024E.

+++

DMCI (DMC PM) declared total cash dividends of P0.72/sh yesterday, which was equivalent to a yield of 5.9%. Given that spot coal price (ICI4) of US$56/MT is trading better than our forecast of US$42 for its main cash cow, Semirara (SCC PM), we believe there is still room to declare another special dividend in Oct/Nov, barring any large acquisitions.   

+++

CPI inflation for March came in at 3.7%, an (expected) acceleration from Feb (3.4%) but just a tad below Street estimates (3.8%). This print will likely mean little change on market expectations on the near-term direction of interest rates.

    • 2024 budget deficit raised to P1.48tn (5.6% of GDP) from P1.39tn (5.2% previously)
    • Budget deficit to GDP ratio targets are higher due to (a) Mildly higher disbursements (+2-3%) and (b) lower nominal GDP targets (-3% to -4%)
    • The deficit program is expected to gradually decline from 6.2% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2028

  • In an advisory, the Department of Energy (DOE) requested the National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) and all distribution utilities (DUs) to fully prepare for the implementation of the Interruptible Load Program (ILP) amid the dry season
