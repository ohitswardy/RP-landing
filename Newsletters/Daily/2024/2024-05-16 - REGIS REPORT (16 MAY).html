16 May 2024

The results line-up today is dominated by consumer names, all of whom underperformed relative to our numbers (in varying degrees).

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – 1Q24 net profit to common (+32% YoY) came in just a shade below expectations, but operating profit (+14% YoY) is tracking broadly in line with estimates and management’s own guidance. The Philippine business was the outperformer with robust SSSG of +6.9%. International segments were mixed but should see better results towards the latter half of the year.

  • Globe (GLO PM) (=) – Core income rose 13% to P5.8bn, which is within Regis expectations but likely ahead of the Street’s.  The two biggest highlights were, first, the 8% jump in mobile revenue. This contradicts the fears raised that have persisted since the third operator was selected.  Second is the big jump in contributions from Mynt, which are now at 17% of core income.

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (-) – Net profit rose just 3% YoY during the quarter as margins were crimped by competition and higher opex, particularly for the core Puregold brand. We believe margins for Puregold could continue to trend downward in the coming quarters (beyond the usual seasonality) as it continues to implement its price matching strategy and other promotional activities to help drive sales.

  • Alliance Global (AGI PM) (-) – 1Q24 net profit fell 10% to P4.2bn, due to: 1) higher forex losses on US$ debt, 2) a drop in whisky and brandy sales of Emperador, and 3) lower VIP volume and win rate at Travellers.
  • Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) (-) – SSSG was flattish during the quarter as full-service brands succumbed to downtrading. Gross margin gains (from lower input cost) were reinvested into promotional activities, leading to a 16% YoY reduction in operating profits. Accelerating sales arising from a rebound in consumer sentiment coupled with operating leverage should lead to a more meaningful margin recovery in 2H24.

  • Emperador (EMI PM) (-) – 1Q24 net profit fell 25% YoY as revenues declined 16% YoY. Both main segments – Whisky and Brandy – experienced significant revenue declines amidst downtrading by consumers.
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) – Net profit grew 11% to P2.63bn in 1Q, below Regis estimates.  This appears, however, to be well ahead of consensus. The acceleration of loan growth to 12% (vs 7% in 4Q) is a key highlight.  The others are a chunky payment that boosted fee income and a doubling of provisions.

BANKS
SECB 1Q24 loans jump 12%; NIMs still muddied by interest rate swaps

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT for 1Q24 of P2.63bn, up 11% YoY, seemingly just a shade behind REGIS (22% of FY24F of P12.0bn) but well ahead of consensus (FY24F P10.8bn). That said there were one-off gains and expenses, though the bank claims these canceled each other out. The nature of the one-offs was not mentioned in the press release. We presume there will be more clarity after the briefing on Friday, 17 May at 5pm.

Some highlights:

  • Loan growth accelerated to 12% YoY, from 7% in 4Q23. Consumer/MSME loans again took the lead, surging 32% YoY (from 29% in 4Q23). But corporate loans also posted 4% YoY growth, reversing the -0.5% print in 4Q23. On the consumer side, mortgages grew 18% YoY, while the bank saw explosive growth in auto loans (+46%), credit cards (+49%), and MSME loans (+74%). Consumer/MSME loans now account for 31% of total loans, up from 29% at end-2023, and a new record high by our reckoning.
  • NIMs were again muddled by the accounting of interest rate swaps, which showed artificially low deposit costs, but which booked the differential as a forex loss. On the upside, loan yields stayed flat QoQ, despite the increase in corporate loans. As before, we expect management to give their estimates of “pro-forma” NIMs assuming the swaps were treated as interest expense.
  • Other income was boosted by a chunky (not quite one-off) P1.8bn milestone fee from SECB’s bancassurance partner, without which fees would be flattish YoY.
  • Provision expense more than doubled YoY. This is consistent with management’s guidance in the FY23 briefing of one more quarter of cleanups relating to legacy credit card delinquencies. NPL ratio was flat QoQ at 3.4%, while NPL cover edged down to 81% from 83% in 4Q23.

We expect to get more clarity on the one-off items, and hopefully get a better sense “normalized” profit trajectory for future quarters during the briefing.

 

CONGLOS
Alliance Global 1Q24 net profit falls 10% YoY
  • Alliance Global’s 1Q24 net profit fell 10% YoY (37% QoQ) to P4.2bn, trailing behind consensus FY forecast
  • Losses at the parent level increased to P800mn (from P500mn in losses in 1Q23)
    • Due to forex losses on US$ debt
  • Attributable profit from Emperador declined 20% YoY to P1.5bn
    • Whisky sales fell 10% YoY while brandy sales declined 19% YoY
  • Attributable profit from Travellers fell 96% YoY to P10mn
    • Mass GGR grew 28% to P4.3bn while VIP GGR fell 42% to P3.2bn (due to both lower win rate and volume)
  • Attributable profit from Megaworld rose 11% YoY to P3.2bn
    • Megaworld’s operating profit grew 28% on strong residential revenue and margin expansion
  • Attributable profit from Golden Arches increased 19% to P300mn
    • Systemwide sales grew 13% while SSSG was maintained at 6.3%
    • It is targeting to grow its store network to 790 in 2024 (from 740 in 2023, 744 in 1Q24)

 

CONSUMER
Emperador – 1Q24 performance weighed by downtrading
  • EMI’s net profit in 1Q24 fell 25% YoY to P1.7bn, behind Regis and consensus expectations
