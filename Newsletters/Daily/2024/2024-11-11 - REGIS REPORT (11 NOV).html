REGIS REPORT

11 November 2024

President Marcos formalized the ban on offshore gaming (that he first announced during his State of the Nation Address in July) by issuing Executive Order 74 (EO 74). Since POGO firms are predominantly in the Manila Bay area, we expect the ban to be most negative for property in that vicinity. While the developers we cover have minimal exposure to POGO office tenants or mainland Chinse residential buyers, SM Prime (SMPH PM) and Megaworld (MEG PM) have meaningful exposure to Manila Bay area properties.  

Interestingly, EO74 explicitly exempts offshore gaming “…conducted in PAGCOR-operated casinos, licensed casinos, or integrated resorts with junket agreements.” Thus, we might see some offshore gaming activity transfer from office buildings to integrated resorts, such as Solaire of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM).

We have another long list of results to go through today:

  • ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) (-) – We took additional time to go through the results (released Thu last week) to gather some clarity on some items. Simply put, 3Q results were worse than we anticipated as core income for 3Q was only P815m, taking 9M core income to P5.76bn.  While this was better than last year’s 3Q core loss, it was down ~70% QoQ. The QoQ profit drop was blamed on a seasonal drop in RE output, but what might have been overlooked was the drop in output from SLTEC, which was on outage for more than a month in 3Q.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (=) – Megaworld’s 9M24 headline profit grew 14% to P13.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. 3Q24 reservation sales increased 5% QoQ (flat YoY) while 9M24 reservation sales fell 7% YoY. Although Megaworld’s reservation sales have not been strong, the company has improved its unsold inventory level substantially. It was able to reduce its unsold inventory from P142bn in 2Q24 to P135bn in 3Q. More importantly, we estimate that it sold c. P5bn of RFO units in 3Q and c. P40bn in 9M24.

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (-) – PGOLD’s disclosure/s on Friday reveal how much management mindset and strategy have changed with the flood of small format groceries from competition the past few years. 9M profits were up only 4.5% to P6.9bn, but sales grew 9% to P152bn as PGOLD cut prices to meet competition head-on. Beyond that, PGOLD also announced the acquisition of PureMart from its principal Lucio Co for a small sum (~US$10m). PureMart operates around 120-130 (small format) stores. More below.

 

  • DMCI (DMC PM) (+) – 3Q24 recurring profit grew 4% to P4.0bn, with earnings rebounding for construction, water, nickel mining and power (on-grid and off-grid) units. However, profits from the coal mining and property units remained soft. Cumulative 9M24 profit fell 24% YoY to P14.98bn, which appears to be tracking behind consensus forecasts but on trend to beat Regis estimates.  Management is holding a briefing today at 4pm.
  • Monde (MONDE PM) (+) – The 9M core profits rose 31% to P7.45bn on the back of a margin recovery, even if sales growth remained anemic (+3%).  We reported this previously, but you can check out our published note on this.

MACRO
President Marcos issues Executive Order banning offshore gaming
  • President Marcos issued Executive Order No. 74 (EO 74), which bans offshore gaming in the Philippines   
    • He first announced his decision to ban offshore gaming during his State of the Nation Address in July 2024
  • Existing offshore gaming activities, including those of licensed POGO/IGL firms and their service providers must cease operations by December 31
    • PIGO firms, such as DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), which serve domestic clients, are exempt from EO 74
    • EO74 also explicitly exempts PAGCOR-operated casinos, licensed casinos, or integrated resorts with junket arrangements – implying that offshore gaming can be done from these casinos
    • BLOOM is trading at 6.8X FY25E EV/EBITDA
  • POGO firms are predominantly located in the Manila Bay area
  • Thus, we expect Manila Bay area property to be the most negatively affected by the ban
  • Colliers estimates that after the ban is fully implemented, Manila Bay area office and residential vacancies will rise from c. 30% currently to 50%
  • Although the developers we cover generally have minimal direct exposure to POGO office tenants or mainland Chinese residential buyers, a few of them have meaningful exposure to Manila Bay area property
  • 27% of SM Prime’s NAV is from its coastal redevelopment project in the Manila Bay area
    • SMPH is trading at 32% discount to NAV, 1.8X FY25E P/B, and 18.1X FY25E P/E
  • Land in the Manila Bay area comprises c. 10% of Megaworld’s NAV
    • MEG is trading at 67% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY25E P/B, and 3.6X FY25E P/E
CONSUMER
PGOLD -- 9M profits rise 4.5%, below expectations; enters small format segment
  • Puregold’s (PGOLD PM) net income for 9M came in at 6.9bn (+4.5% YoY) on revenues of P152bn (+9%). The bottom line is trailing behind expectations, but the top line appears pretty decent.
    • NPAT for 3Q24 is only P1.96bn (-11% YoY), despite the strong SSSG. They took a hit on margins in a conscious bid to get share as well as stimulate sales.
    • The program to address Dali and other discounters (by price matching on key SKUs that both Dali and Puregold carry).  During the NDR, Puregold highlighted that these SKUs they price match would be equivalent to under 4% of Puregold’s sales (implying that they can keep at it on price matching) ; it is however more than half of Dali’s sales
  • PGOLD is acquiring the Puremart chain from Lucio Co. It’s paying P567.5m for at least 120 to 130 stores.  PGOLD’s entry into this format (small grocery ops) could change the competitive landscape for the segment as well as PGOLD’s own trajectory.
    • The PHP 567 million includes inventory;  if physical stores only , it is about PHP 300mn+  (so per store, would be under PHP 3 million). 
    • Puremart is still losing money , which is not surprising given the lack of scale
  • PGOLD also said it is no longer renewing its buyback program for FY25.  If the funds are instead spent on its entry into small format groceries, it would not be a bad thing.

CONGLOMERATES
DMCI 9M24 profit drops 24% to P14.98bn - Behind consensus / Ahead Regis
  • DMCI’s headline 3Q24 net profit grew 3% YoY to P4.0bn.  Stripping out one-offs and FX adj, we estimate core profit rose 4%.
    • Brings 9M24 recurring profit to P14.98bn, 24% YoY lower. This is on trend to miss consensus full year forecast of P21.8bn but appears ahead of Regis estimates.
  • Outside of Semirara (see 3Q results comment here), 3Q profits were pulled down by persistent weakness in the property unit
    • These were offset by a rebound in construction and nickel mining profits, as well as sustained strong contributions from Maynilad and its off-grid power units
    • Property reservation sales fell 24% QoQ, 38% YoY to P7.39bn on a 38% YoY decline in units sold and higher sales cancellations. These were cushioned by a 15% YoY increase in ASPs
    • Sales cancellations (sales below 14.5% revenue recognition threshold) surged to 35% in 3Q24 from 21% in 2Q24 and 11% in 3Q23.  This was blamed on the rise in building turnovers (11 vs 7 last year) and fewer residential units sold
  • Management is holding a briefing at 4PM this afternoon

UTILITIES
Maynilad eyes IPO in April or July 2025
  • Maynilad plans to file its application for an IPO by 1Q25, with a listing date in either April or July 2025
    • According to Maynilad President and CEO Ramoncito Fernandez
    • There are no talks yet on whether the dilution amongst current shareholders will be equal but Marubeni wants to retain its 20% stake
  • Under its water franchise, Maynilad is required to issue at least 30% of its shares to the public on or before January 2027
    • Maynilad is 53%-owned by Metro Pacific, 25% by DMCI (DMC PM), and 20% by Japan-based Marubeni
    • Maynilad accounts for 24% of DMCI’s market-based NAV of P12.90/sh; DMC trades at a 13% discount to NAV (in line with historical average of 15%)
