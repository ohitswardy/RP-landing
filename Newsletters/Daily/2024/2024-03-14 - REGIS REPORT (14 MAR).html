REGIS REPORT

14 March 2024

We have results from Ayala Corp (AC PM) and Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) this morning, both of whom disclosed bottom line numbers below expectations.

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – AC reported FY23 income of P38bn, +38% YoY, below Street expectations.  The results are again affected by more write-offs (impairment charges, et al) in 4Q, this time on Manila Water (MWC PM), Yoma and Zalora. Another slew of such charges is not a good thing.  The positive spin to all of this is that management is taking steps to clean up / divest problematic assets.  Assets that have been perennially bleeding are finally being let go.  This will, hopefully, set the stage for management’s challenging target of reach P65bn in ‘core income’ by 2026.
  • Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) (-) – FY23 net profit fell 53% YoY to P3.7bn, well below Regis and consensus estimates, after (a) mining ASPs disappointed and (b) the HPAL plants recorded larger-than-expected equitized losses of P1bn.  Derived 4Q23 profit was only P54mn, with the HPAL plants incurring P620mn worth of equitized losses during the quarter.

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) announced dividends totaling P8.00/share, P5.00 of which will paid next month (April 12).  The dividends declared are above consensus expectations (P5.00/share) but also well below our forecast of P30/share).  We had been counting on GTCAP increasing its payout even more given the higher dividends from Metrobank (MBT PM) and Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP).  The announced dividends would still leave GTCAP with a lot of funds to either reduce debt or invest more in new ventures.

Ayala Corp (AC PM) FY23: Clean-up time
  • Profits rise to P38bn, below expectations.  AC reported net income of P38.1bn for FY23, 38% higher YoY.  Excluding what AC considers non-core items, profit was P41bn (+48%).  The results are below Street expectations as AC took another P3.3bn in losses (charges) for 4Q23. Among its listed units we cover, BPI and ALI delivered earnings in line or better than estimated while Globe and ACEN’s bottom line results fell below.
  • Cleaning the slate. AC’s P3.3bn “non-core” loss included (1) a loss of P1.5bn from the partial sale of its stake in Manila Water (MWC PM), (2) a P900m impairment of its Yoma investment and (3) a provision of P600m against the remaining value of its Zalora investment. The investment in Yoma is now carried at US$95m, 40% of the initial investment cost.

Apart from these, last mile logistics firm Entrego is also being shut down. While impairments like these are never a good thing, we view it as a positive signal that management is moving past these ill-fated investments.

  • Core income target of P65bn by 2026.  Management announced it was aiming to reach P65bn of core income by 2026.  This represents an ambitious target of growing core income by 60% over three years, equivalent to 17% CAGR. Given our views on the listed businesses, we this can only be achieved on the back of (1) accelerated growth of ACEN and (2) the reversal of losses of its unlisted business units.  Frankly, achieving this goal will be a steep challenge and we hope it will not be achieved through asset sales conveniently classified as “core income”.
  • Driving to the future. Among the group’s newer initiatives, we are most optimistic about the prospects of AC Health and the auto segment (now under “AC Mobility”). AC Health reported a net loss of P233m for the year (with a modest profit in 4Q).  It aims to deliver P1bn in EBITDA this year and possibly a small profit.  AC Mobility posted a small net loss (P99m) for FY23.  It expects industrywide EV sales to reach 5,000 this year as adoption picks up steam. AC distributes EVs under the Kia and BYD brands and aims to capture 50% of the EV market.
