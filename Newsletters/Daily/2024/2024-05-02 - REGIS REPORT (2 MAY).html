REGIS REPORT

02 May 2024

Metrobank (MBT PM) net profit for 1Q24 came in at P12.0bn, up 14% YoY, well ahead of Regis (27% of our FY24E of P44.8bn) and consensus (P46.6bn) estimates. The beat came largely from provisions, which dropped 77% YoY to just P562mn, as the bank reduced over-provisioning while NPLs were benign. Loan growth accelerated to 12.1% YoY (gross) from 8.4% in 4Q23, driven by a jump in capex loans, driving the corporate book up 11.2% YoY, compared to 5.5% in 4Q23. NIMs were down slightly (-2bps QoQ) on higher funding costs. ROE for the quarter hit 13.7%, an 18-year high, helped by the special dividend declared in March (payout of 53% on 2023 earnings, and 7% dividend yield). Management will host a briefing at 4pm today.

+++

Information on two of ICTSI’s (ICT PM) overseas ports bolster our confidence about the 1Q results of the port operator. Volume indications for the ports in Manzanilla (Mexico) and Melbourne (Australia) look quite good and strengthen our positive outlook ahead of its results announcement.

+++

The energy regulator is suspending the pricing mechanism of the WESM during periods of shortages.  The move would cap prices and certainly help consumers especially during the current period, when consumers are facing inflationary pressure. The longer-term consequence, of course, is that gencos will be discouraged from investing in capacity for peak demand without contracts.

Among the gencos, Semirara (SCC PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM) have arguably the most exposure to spot prices today.  SCC has roughly half of its power capacity available for spot sales, and power is around 30% of earnings.  AP sells over 15% of output to spot.  ACEN (ACEN PM) is also poised to sell ~25% of its Philippine kwh output to the spot beginning this year as new capacity has come online.

+++

President Marcos, in his Labor Day message, called on the regional wage boards to review minimum wages across the country.  Despite relative high inflation over the past two years, wage increases have actually outpaced the growth in CPI.

+++

Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) completed the purchase of 921m of its own shares (8% of capital) from its former management team GGAM.  The purchase price (P18.32/share, P16.9bn in total) roughly represents the amount (US$300m) an arbitration panel awarded GGAM years ago. The price is unlikely to be representative of the value of the shares estimated by either party.

The purchase / settlement, announced weeks ago, puts to rest that dispute with GGAM. It, however, also raises BLOOM’s net debt/EBITDA to almost 4x.

POWER
ERC to suspend WESM trading when Luzon and Visayas are under red alert
  • The ERC ordered the suspension of trading in the WESM during red alerts to prevent a spike in electricity prices
    • During the suspension, the administrative price, or the price imposed by the market operator to the trading participants during market suspension or market intervention, will be applied
    • If a dispatch interval is subject to both a price mitigation and the administered price, “the lower of the two prices shall apply in the settlement of transactions for such interval
    • Suspensions will be lifted “only if the regional available capacity, less actual regional demand, reaches above zero for 24 consecutive hours.”
  • The ERC said the average spot prices per day increased by 11% in Luzon and by 53% in the Visayas when alert notices were issued due to the high heat index
  • The ERC’s move will reduce the supernormal profits that could accrue to generators during periods of tight supply (e.g. now)
    • It would also have implications on the willingness of gencos to invest, especially in the absence of contracts
  • Among those who have significant power capacity available for sale to the spot market are: Aboitiz Power (AP PM), Semirara (SCC PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM)
TRANSPORT
ICTSI (ICT PM) 1Q24 volume preview for Mexico / Melbourne

Strong underlying demand was evident at the ports of Manzanillo, Mexico and Melbourne in 1Q24. We are particularly encouraged by the strong volume recovery in Melbourne, which was a key drag to organic volume growth in 2023.  We believe ICTSI (ICT PM) could post strong organic volume growth rates when it reports results on Monday, May 6 (5pm – Register  investor@ictsi.com). 

  • CMSA volumes up 36.5% YoY in 1Q24. Volumes at Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA) rose 36.5% YoY, 6.8% QoQ to 358k TEUs in 1Q24, averaging 119k TEUs per month and trending well above our full-year forecast of 1.39mn TEUs (+9% YoY).  CMSA’s market share improved further to 37% in 1Q24 from ~34% in 2023. With the completion of CMSA’s 250k TEU expansion by mid-year, we anticipate the company could continue to capitalize on the strong trade activity in Mexico.  We estimate CMSA accounts for 11% of consolidated volumes and 20% of revenues. 

 

  • Melbourne ports recover.  Volumes at the three container ports in Melbourne jumped 11% YoY, +5% QoQ to 727k TEUs in 1Q24, the first material increase since 3Q22.  We suspect volume growth at Victoria International Container Terminal (VICT) may have been much stronger than the industry figure of +11%, after a major client transferred all its ships at VICT beginning January.  With the volume recovery, we anticipate VICT’s yield per TEU could also rise by at least double-digits this year, as tariff hikes that were eroded in 2023 due to unfavorable volume mix should reverse this year.  We estimate VICT accounts for 7% of group-wide volumes and 8% of revenues. 

 

  • MICT volumes still likely soft in 1Q24.  The port authority has not released 1Q24 volume data yet but we reckon demand may have still been lackluster for ICT’s port in Manila (MICT).  Recall that container volumes in the Philippines rose by a mere 1% in 2023, with MICT’s volumes falling by 2%.  Weak demand, however, could be offset by the approval and implementation of a 17% tariff hike sometime in 3Q.
  • Valuations remain inexpensive relative to peers and historical average.   ICTSI has outperformed the PCOMP by 30% year-to-date but valuations remain inexpensive, in our view.  At 16x FY24E PER and 8x EV/EBITDA, ICT still trades below peers (17x/11x) and historical valuations (22x/11x).
GAMING
Bloomberry subsidiary purchases BLOOM shares of former management team
  • In relation to the settlement between Bloomberry and GGAM, Bloomberry’s wholly-owned subsidiary, Sureste Properties, purchased 921mn BLOOM shares from GGAM
    • Represents 8% of shares outstanding
  • Purchased at P18.32/share – total of P16.9bn
  • This transaction was previously announced in March 2024
  • This transaction will raise net debt (by P16.9bn), reduce equity (by P16.9bn), and (effectively) lower shares outstanding (by 921mn shares or 8%)
  • As a result:
  • Gearing - We estimate that FY23 consolidated net debt/equity will rise from 1.2X to 2.0X. We also estimate that FY23 net debt/EBITDA will rise from 2.9X to 3.9X
  • EPS – If BLOOM were to raise P16.9bn in debt at an interest rate of 7%, we estimate that 2025E EPS would only drop c. 1% (due to the reduction in shares outstanding)
