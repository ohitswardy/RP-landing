REGIS REPORT

19 December 2024

With the US Federal Reserve reducing the target Fed funds rate by 25bp (to 4.25 – 4.50%) overnight, it’s now pretty much certain that the Bangko Sentral (BSP) will do the same this afternoon.

The question now is how much rates can be reduced over the next months. The Fed is now signaling two more 25bp cuts (=50bp total) next year.  Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, meanwhile, has expressed his view that the BSP may cut as much as 75bp in 2025.  The direction of and divergence between US / global rates and PH rates will obviously have a big bearing on financial markets domestically.

+++

President Marcos has postponed the signing of the national budget, pending a review and the expected veto of certain provisions.  Among the contentious provisions in the budget passed by Congress for FY25 were the budget reduction for the Dept of Education (DepEd) and the slashing (removal) of subsidies for the national health insurance agency (PhilHealth).

Under the Constitution, Marcos has the power to issue line-item vetoes of specific provisions / lines of the national budget. We expect the President to move to restore some funding to the affected agencies at the expense of “unprogrammed” allocations.

Without an approved FY25 budget, the government will have to function under a reenacted FY24 budget. However, we do expect the new FY25 budget to be finalized quickly.

+++

Tyme Group raised US$250m in new money from both new and existing shareholders, giving it additional capital to inject into its digital banking operations, including in the Philippines.  Tyme is the partner of the JG Summit (JGS PM) group in GoTyme, one of six digital banks given licenses by the BSP. The expected capital infusion into GoTyme should allow it to pursue lending activities more aggressively.

+++

Finally, tourist arrivals for Nov were disappointing, and were actually slightly down YoY. Tourist arrivals from China remain only a fraction of what they were before the pandemic.

  • Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that it is highly likely that the BSP will cut its policy rate by 25bps at its policy meeting today

  • These projections are in line with REGIS forecasts
MACRO
Marcos postpones 2025 budget signing for further review
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. postponed the signing of the proposed P6.35tn General Appropriations Act (GAA) which was originally scheduled for tomorrow, December 20
  • Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin stated that the President is personally leading the review and is consulting with major government departments
  • He also confirmed that Marcos will veto some items but did not disclose which items specifically
    • The rationale behind the items to be vetoed would be "in the interest of public welfare, to conform with the fiscal program, and in compliance with laws"
  • Legislative leaders remain optimistic that the GAA will be signed before the year ends
    • It is to note that the GAA must be enacted before year-end to avoid a reenacted budget, which would limit the implementation of the government's new programs and initiatives
BANKS
NuBank invests $150mn in Tyme SG - to fund GoTyme expansion
  • NuBank of Brazil invested $150mn for a 10% stake in Tyme Group (SG), as part of the Series-D funding round raising a total of $250mn, and valuing the group at about $1.5bn. M&G took a $50mn slug, with the existing shareholders (including the Gokongwei group) subscribing to the $50mn balance. Proceeds will fund Tyme Group’s expansion in Asia, including GoTyme Bank here in the Philippines. GoTyme is one of six digibanks, and is a JV between the Gokongwei group (60%) and Tyme Group (40%).
  • GoTyme is an odd one among the 6 Digital banks. As of June 2024, it had just P7mn (with an “m”) in total loans. It has an enormous deposit base of P17.3bn, but this is mostly placed in bonds (P11.7bn) and RRPs (P1.9bn). In 1H24, it reported an eye-watering negative 200% ROE.
  • The equity infusion likely signals GoTyme’s readiness to push into lending. To be fair to GoTyme, the digibank sector’s early lenders went through a LOT of pain. Past due rates were an extremely high 35-38% for the first 5 months of this year. Following a cleanup, it looks like the sector is again re-starting to grow its loan book. As of October, customer loans hit P27.6bn, up 59% YoY. Meanwhile, PD rates are down to a more manageable 20%.

 

 

 

MACRO
International arrivals reach 5.31mn as of end-November, behind target
  • Data from the Department of Tourism (DoT) exhibited that tourist arrivals for the month of November declined slightly 2% YoY and MoM to 432k
    • This was 63% of 2019 average
    • Korean tourists remain as the top source of visitor arrivals but visitors from Korea was flat both YoY and MoM
    • Chinese tourists declined 19% YoY, marking five consecutive months of YoY decreases
  • The DoT stated that visa liberalization delays and geopolitical issues may have impacted projections
    • i.e., the DoT anticipated 2mn Chinese visitors with the e-visa system implementation which was suspended by the DFA
  • Bringing YT-November tourist arrivals to 5.3mn tourist arrivals, up 9% YoY and 71% of 2019 average
    • The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, which is up 41% YoY or 93% of 2019 levels
    • 2023 tourist arrivals hit 5.5mn or 66% of pre-covid
