REGIS REPORT

11 March 2024

Robinsons Land’s (RLC PM) FY23 net profit rose 24% to P12.1bn, ahead of both REGIS and consensus forecasts. Mall EBIT rose 94% YoY to P5.85bn, in line with our forecast, and 13% above the FY19 level. Meanwhile, operating profit of all other segments outperformed our expectations. Demand for high-end residential projects drove FY23 JV presales up 117% (to P19.5bn) while full-year presales of standalone projects climbed 26% (to P21.3bn). However, similar to the experience of many other developers, presales fell for the second consecutive quarter, with 4Q23 presales falling 17% QoQ (37% YoY).

+++

NPL and Past Due (PD) ratios seemed to jump sharply MoM in January. However, we believe that was largely cosmetic due to write-offs in December. The NPL ratio (ex-interbank), for example, was up 21bps MoM to 3.53%, compared to 3.32% in December, but was flat (+4bps) against the 3.49% posted in November. The same is true for PDs, with the January ratio 4.27% up 31bps MoM, but up just 5bps against the November print. The increases were further exacerbated by the seasonal drop in loans (-3.6% MoM). While we are mindful of asset quality as a key earnings risk for banks, we would caution reading too much into the January print.

+++

Tourist arrivals continue to recover, with Feb arrivals +23% higher YoY (+1% MoM). The latest figures are 85% of 2019 levels and most groups are at/near pre-pandemic numbers, with one notable exception: tourists from China. The Feb numbers for Chinese tourists are only 34% of what they were in 2019.

BANKS
NPLs, PD ratios blip up in January -- partly cosmetic?
  • Asset quality seems to have taken a turn for the worse in January 2024. However, we suspect it was partly due to sizeable write-offs done in December 2023, particularly in the credit card portfolio, which saw big MoM improvements in asset quality ratios. Evidence for this can be gleaned from the sharp drop in loan loss reserves (LLR) ratio from 3.46% in November, to 3.30% in December, only to bounce back to 3.45% in January. We saw a similar trend in January 2023, which we wrote about here. When compared to November 2023, the asset quality data for January is flattish.
  • NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was 3.53% in January, up 21bps MoM, while Past Due (PD) ratio jumped 31bps MoM to 4.27%. But when compared to November, the NPL ratio was up just 4bps, while the PD ratio was up 5bps. Restructured Loan (RL) ratio, was up 8bps MoM to 2.26%, but was actually down 3bps versus the November level. NPL cover dipped to 100.3%, down from 101.7% in December.
  • Meanwhile, loan growth slowed to 8.8% YoY from the 10.3% pace in December. This is fairly typical, as the December figure tends to be boosted by year-end window-dressing. We expect loan growth to steadily pick up as the year progresses.
  • We are not (overly) concerned by the January print. However, we will continue to watch the data closely in coming months.
