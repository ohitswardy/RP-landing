REGIS REPORT

17 December 2024

Overseas remittances for Oct were up 2.7% to US$3.08bn. This takes YT-Oct remittances to US$28.3bn (+3.0%), which is broadly in line with the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) forecasts.

+++

A local court in South Africa rejected the motion of ICTSI (ICT PM) to lift the interdict ruling that stopped Transnet from awarding the Durban port concession to ICTSI. The KwaZulu-Natal High Court said that it will proceed with the hearings in March before rendering a decision.

This ruling isn’t too surprising. In fact, given the likely back-and-forth relating to the litigation / dispute, it should surprise no one should this case drag on for a year or two more. 

Meanwhile, there’s positive news on ICTSI’s port in Mexico, now its biggest contributor (by EBITDA). The CMSA terminal saw volumes leap by 20% in Oct as it took market share from competitors following the completion of its capacity expansion.

+++

ACEN (ACEN PM) aims to have 1.2GW (=1,200MW) of additional capacity operational by end of next year.  This compares with the 3,051MW that ACEN currently claims is operational. Note that some of the 1,200MW that will be “completed” next year have already been generating revenue this year (e.g. 160MW Pagudpud) so the % increase in 2025 will not be as big as the initial numbers suggest.

MACRO
Remittances rise 2.7% YoY in October
  • Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.7% YoY to US$3.08bn
    • Primarily driven by the acceleration in land-based remittances from Singapore up 10.4% YoY and 17.1% MoM
    • Sea-based remittances growth remain slow at 0.6%
    • In PHP terms, remittances rose 3.6%, an increase from September (+2.0%) but still the second slowest for the year
  • Bringing YT-October remittances up 3% YoY to US$28.3bn
    • Broadly in line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year

TRANSPORT
South African court denies ICTSI appeal on Durban port ruling, prefers to proceed with hearings in March
  • The KwaZulu-Natal High Court denied ICTS’s bid to appeal the interdict ruling against Transnet’s selection of ICTSI as the concessionaire for DCT-2 port in Durban, South Africa 
    • Court said that it is “not in the interests of justice to permit an appeal,” adding that hearing of the case is just three months away in March 2025
    • It also cited that the first respondent [Transnet], which is the party interdicted and restrained by the order, has not itself sought leave to appeal
  • Our forecasts/valuation for ICTSI do not include DCT-2
    • We estimate the acquisition of DCT-2 would have added US$211mn (6%) in revenues and US$74mn in EBITDA (3%) to ICTSI’s consolidated numbers on its first full-year of operations
TRANSPORT
ICTSI Mexico port volumes up 20% YoY in November
  • Volumes at ICTSI’s port in Mexico (CMSA) grew by 20% YoY to 132k TEUs in November
    • On a MoM basis, CMSA’s volumes dipped by 5% MoM
    • ICTSI continued to take market share, as volumes at competing ports in Manzanillo fell 7% YoY in November
  • Brings YT-November volumes at CMSA up by 15% YoY to 1.33mn TEUs
    • This is in line with our FY24E of 1.47mn TEUs (+15%)
    • We estimate CSMA accounts for 12% FY25E consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, 26% of EBITDA
  • ICT trades at 7.9x FY25E EV/EBITDA, lower than peer average of (11x) and historical average of (11x)

 

 

POWER
ACEN targets to add 1.2GW of new capacity by end-2025
  • ACEN expects to complete the construction of a total of 1.2 GW of new RE capacity within and outside the Philippines by end-2025
    • According to ACEN President and CEO Eric Francia
  • Among these projects are:
    • 520 MW Stubbo Solar in New South Wales, Australia
    • 146MW Monsoon Wind project in Laos 
    • 123MW hybrid-solar/wind in India
    • 109MW Stockyard wind project in Texas, USA
    • 160MW Pagudpud and 57MW Capa Wind projects in Ilocos Norte, Philippines
    • 60MW Pangasinan solar project in Phils
  • ACEN has 6,754MW of attributable RE capacity, of which 3,051MW is operational
    • In the Philippines alone, ACEN has a total of 2,397MW of capacity, of which 1,330 MW are under construction

Its international att. RE capacity totals 4,357 MW, of which c. 2,400 MW are under construction
