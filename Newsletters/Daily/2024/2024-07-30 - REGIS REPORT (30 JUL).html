REGIS REPORT

30 July 2024

Meralco’s (MER PM) 2Q core income was a surprise, as core income for the quarter alone jumped 29% YoY to P13bn.  For 1H, core income was P23bn, +21%.

The rise in electricity usage was not a surprise given the heat wave in April-May.  What did surprise us was how much money the generation and RES segments (“unregulated businesses”) made selling power amidst the environment of high spot prices. It seems those units under Meralco had excess uncontracted power after the brouhaha the past year affecting large users.  The controversy involved Meralco reneging on fixed price commitments to accommodate San Miguel’s (SMC PM) strategic error not to hedge fuel costs.

More detail on BDO’s (BDO PM) results – 1H24 NPAT of P39.4bn (+12% YoY) was in line with REGIS and consensus estimates. We were encouraged, though, by the strong sequential growth in 2Q24 with NPAT of P20.9bn, up 13% QoQ vs 1Q24. This was driven by a sharp rebound in non-interest income, with fees alone up 17% YoY in 2Q, vs 5% YoY in 1Q (fees were up 18% QoQ). Loan growth was steady at 13% but was driven by accelerating consumer loans (+16% YoY vs +13.5% in 1Q) and “mid-market” loans (+10.5% YoY vs 6.7% in 1Q). These helped NIMs eke out a 2bps gain QoQ despite higher funding costs. NPAT was boosted by provision expenses, which fell 16% YoY in 2Q24, despite an 18bp increase in NPL ratio to 2.06%. We remain Buyers of BDO, now trading at 9x PER, 1.3x P/bk, on ROE of 15% on our FY2024E forecasts.

Gaming regulator says one possible loophole in the “POGO ban” announced by the President is the gaming licenses issued by the Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA), which is not governed by PAGCOR and whose operations are autonomous.

+++

Jollibee (JFC PM) announced it was dropping the planned P8bn preferred share issue.  While the company cited various reasons (i.e. it was generating more cash now, capex plans are lower, etc.), we reckon the real reason is that it realized the cost of the prefs would be higher than it would have liked, especially in the face of likely declines in interest rates.

+++

The Budget Department (“DBM”) has submitted its P6.35trn national budget proposal for FY25 to Congress. As a projected share of GDP (21.9%), the national budget will be barely up for FY25. After accounting for debt service payments, the actual primary spend will actually be a tad lower. 

+++

The passage of the proposed law on digital service taxes seems imminent with the ratification by the Senate. As far as we know, the tax will be imposed on digital services providers as well as e-commerce platforms. Apart from raising revenue, the tax will help protect local retailers against foreign retailers operating through various platforms.

MACRO
Budget Department submits 2025 P6.35tn budget proposal to Congress
  • The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) submitted its proposed P6.35tn 2025 budget to the Lower House
    • Budget is 10% higher than the approved 2024 budget and is equivalent to ~22% of GDP
  • Key items to note are:
    • Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively
    • Allocation for debt payments is up 27% YoY
    • Allocation to local government units is up 17% YoY
  • Under its proposal, the government's debt stock is expected to increase by 8.1% to P17.35tn from the projected P16.06tn debt at the end of 2024
    • The bulk comes from the outstanding domestic debt which is expected to rise by 9.64% to P11.98tn by end-2025 from P10.92tn by end-2024
    • The outstanding external debt is expected to rise by 4.76% to P5.38tn by end-2025 from P5.13tn by end-2024
  • The NG’s debt as a share of GDP stood at 60.2% at the end of the first quarter, while the government aims for a 60.3% debt-to-GDP ratio by year-end
    • This suggests that the 2025 NG debt will be roughly 60% of GDP
    • Government aims to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 51% by 2028 (vs. trough of 39.6% in 2019)

MACRO
Senate ratifies bicameral report on 12% VAT on foreign digital service providers
  • The Senate adopted the bill imposing the 12% value-added tax (VAT) on digital service providers without a physical presence in the Philippines (e.g. Amazon, Shein, Taobao)
  • The reconciled version of the bill reinforces the Bureau of Internal Revenue’s (BIR) power to “impose and collect VAT on digital services and transactions”
  • The Department of Finance (DoF) set withholding tax rates for companies falling under the P3mn VAT threshold under the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) law to protect small online businesses
  • Nonresident digital service providers and electronic marketplaces must register with the BIR for the remittance of VAT on their services
  • Under the bill,  5% of the revenues from digital service VAT (around P900mn) will be used to support the digital creative industry
  • Bill is expected to generate as much as PHP18 billion on its first year
POWER
Meralco 1H24 results comment
  • Core income rose 21% in 1H to P23bn.  Meralco reported net income of P22.4bn for 1H, up 26%. Stripping out non-core items, “core” income was P23.2bn, +21% YoY.  This is well ahead of expectations, not just of Regis but also the Street.  Surprisingly, the company’s unregulated businesses delivered exceptional numbers in 2Q after the relative slowdown in 1Q.

 

  • Volumes stayed strong in 2Q.  Volumes for 2Q rose 8.5%, still strong historically but a tad lower than 1Q24.  As a result, 1H volumes were 8.7% up.  For 1H, residential volumes rose 12.6% while commercial volumes grew 9.9%.  Industrial volumes rose 2.4%, with management noting that demand from semiconductor firms rose 3%.

 

  • Blockbuster profits from Generation and RES. Beyond the decent (and not unexpected) numbers from the core distribution business, the “unregulated businesses” delivered exceptional profits (see yellow highlight in table below).  We estimate the “unregulated businesses” (generation, retail supply and others) delivered P6.2bn of profit in 2Q alone. This would be the largest ever contribution from the non-DU segments ever in a single quarter.  We believe Meralco managed to take advantage of the volatility and high prices in the spot market in the latest quarter.

 

  • ERC rejects plan to withdraw old tariff application. The regulator rejected Meralco’s proposal to withdraw its tariff application (under 5RP or 5th Regulatory Period) and fix the tariff for 2022-2024 at current rates (P1.35/kwh).  Simply put, the ERC wants Meralco to continue undergoing a tariff review that will cover a period (2022-2026) that is already half over.

 

  • Dividends and guidance.  Meralco declared a dividend of P10.295 per share (record date Aug 28) with respect to its 1H profits.  Combined with its dividend earlier this year, Meralco is paying a total of P21.52/sh this FY24.  This is 5.6% of the current price (and >6% of the price earlier this year).  Meralco also provided an upgraded its guidance for core earnings to P43bn (from P40bn).  This implies profits will be lower in 2H vs 1H.

PROPERTY
PAGCOR sees complications regarding POGO ban
  • During a Senate hearing, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the ban on offshore gaming includes Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL)
    • In 2023, all POGO firms were deemed suspended and were required to apply for an IGL
  • However, he sees complications in implementing the ban with respect to the Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA), which can issue its own [gaming] licenses
    • CEZA is not under the jurisdiction of PAGCOR
  • Prior to the issuance of POGO licenses in 2016, offshore gaming firms operated in Metro Manila through the CEZA license
  • If existing offshore gaming firms are permitted to apply for CEZA licenses, then the POGO ban will likely have minimal impact

Jollibee drops P8bn preferred shares plan, lowers 2024 capex
