REGIS REPORT

05 July 2024

The NPL ratio for the entire banking industry rose to 3.66% in May, a meaningful deterioration from the 3.53% in Apr. Sector-wide loan growth, meanwhile, decelerated to 10.3% YoY from 11.6% the previous month.

In absolute terms, total NPLs were up 3.1% MoM to P496bn, a rather chunky increase equivalent to P15bn.

In terms of loan growth and asset quality, this latest monthly data print is not looking great for the banks.

+++

House Speaker Martin Romualdez has reportedly agreed to support a law allowing land leases of up to 99 years, Current laws allow leases of up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years.

Proponents of Constitutional change in the House have been citing the constitutional prohibition on land ownership by foreigners as one provision hindering investment. The Speaker’s agreement to this proposed law seems a tacit admission that the land ownership issue is not the real obstacle to FDI. This tacit admission, however, does not mean the moves to amend the Constitution are dead.

+++

The June CPI numbers have just been released and the CPI inflation figure for the month was 3.7%.  This is down from 3.9% in May and is lower than Street predictions.

Lower energy costs helped trim the YoY CPI inflation with MoM declines in utilities costs as well as transport costs.  CPI inflation would have been lower if not for a further rise in food prices. The imminent reduction in rice import tariffs (and thus rice prices) should help drive the headline inflation number even lower in the next couple of months.

Farmers’ groups, however, have filed a petition with the Supreme Court to stop the reduction in import tariffs on rice, which is supposed to take effect over this weekend. The petitioners cited the apparent lack of consultation with industry participants prior to the decision, and the alleged failure of previous tariff cuts to reduce consumer prices.  A restraining order (TRO) against the tariff reduction will likely cause a bounce in rice prices.

MACRO
Supreme Court asked to issue TRO against rice tariff cut
  • A group of farmers asked the Supreme Court (SC) to issue a temporary restraining order (TRO) against Executive Order (EO) 62, which reduced the tariff on rice imports to 15% from 35%
    • The signatories to the TRO petition, Samahang Industriya ng Agrikultura, Federation of Free Farmers (FFF), United Broiler Raisers Association, Sorosoro Ibaba Development Cooperative, and Magsasaka Party-list
  • The group argued that (1) their right to be consulted was violated and (2) lowering of tariffs would threaten their livelihoods
MACRO
Government debt rises to P15.35tn in May
  • The national government’s outstanding debt reached P15.35tn in end-May, up 2.2% MoM (+P330bn)
    • Increase largely due new debt issuances and the impact of local currency depreciation on FX debt
  • Domestic debt increased by 1.3% MoM (+P134bn) to P10.44tn largely due to the net issuance of government securities
  • External debt rose to P4.9tn, up 4.2% MoM (+P196bn) due to net foreign loan issuances and the upward revaluation of USD debt
  • For 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio is expected to settle at 60.6%, slightly higher than 2023’s 60.2%
    • After 2024, the government aims to gradually lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 56% by 2028

 

BANKS
Banks' NPL ratio rises in May
  • The banking industry’s nonperforming loan ratio (net of interbank) rose to 3.66% in May from 3.53% in April
    • Gross of inter-bank, the NPL ratio rose to 3.57% in May from 3.45% in April
  • Non-performing loans climbed 3.1% MoM, 13.7% YoY to P496bn
  • Loan growth (net of interbank) decelerated to 10.3% YoY in May from 11.6% YoY in April
    • On a MoM basis, loans were flattish at P13.52tn in May from P13.59tn in April
  • Past due loans dropped 1.6% MoM in May to P608.07bn but rose 15.7% YoY
    • Past due ratio in May was 4.37%, lower than 4.43% in April but higher than the 4.17% ratio in May 2023
  • Restructured loans stood at P295.89bn, up 1.9% MoM but fell 4.6% YoY
  • Loan reserves inched up by 0.7% MoM in May (from P471.35bn in April to P474.88bn in May)
    • On a YoY basis, loan reserves rose 6.9% (from P444.03bn in May 2023)
    • Loan loss reserve ratio was up at 3.42% from April’s 3.37%, but lower than in May 2023 of 3.52%
  • NPL coverage ratio slipped to 95.81% from 98.07% in April, and 101.81% in May 2023

 

POWER/UTILITIES
Major energy players eyeing Meralco’s 600-MW supply contract
  • The government requires distribution utilities to select the cheapest electric supply through a competitive bid
  • Last month, Meralco initiated the bidding process for a 15-year power supply agreement to fulfill its requirements starting August 26, 2025
  • 8 companies have expressed interest and participated in the pre-bid conference for the baseload supply on Thursday, including
    • Subsidiaries of First Gen Corp.
    • San Miguel Global Power Holdings Corp.
    • Aboitiz Power Corp.
    • Semirara Mining and Power Corp. and its subsidiary Southwest Luzon Power Generation Corp.
    • Quezon Power (Philippines) Limited Co.
PROPERTY
Congress seeks to allow 99-year land leases for foreigners 
  • Leaders of the Senate and the House of Representatives support the extension of land leases to foreigners to (up to) 99 years
    • Currently, the Foreign Investors’ Long Term Lease Act allows foreigners to lease land for up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years (total of 75 years)
  • The Senate has already filed a Bill to amend the Foreign Investors’ Long Term Lease Act
  • While there is no counterpart Bill in the House of Representatives yet, Speaker Ferdinand Martin Romualdez and House Committee on Ways and Means Chairman Joey Salceda both support the initiative to lengthen the maximum land lease term for foreigners
