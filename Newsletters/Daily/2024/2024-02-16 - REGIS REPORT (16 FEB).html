REGIS REPORT

16 February 2024

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept its policy rate (RRP) unchanged at its most recent Monetary Board meeting. This is widely expected given the BSP’s own pronouncements and the US Fed’s moves to keep rates steady.

Outside of the maintenance of the policy rate at 6.5%, we note that the BSP also lowered its inflation forecast for this year.  Its baseline forecast was reduced to 3.6% from 3.7%. Its “risk-adjusted” forecast (whatever that means) was cut to 3.9% from 4.2%.  The BSP’s inflation forecasts (and their revisions) have been viewed as a signaling tool on the direction of policy rates.

+++

In a sign of how relations between the House of Representatives (HoR) and the Senate have soured over opposing views on Constitutional amendments, the HoR is reportedly proposing an even more generous (outrageous) wage hike proposal of P400/day.  The Senate just approved, on the second of three readings, a proposal to raise wages by P100/day (16% to 32%) in what we view as attempt to generate public sympathy in its ongoing tussle with the HoR.

Our base case remains that the cooler (and more rational) heads will prevail.  Let’s hope this one-upmanship does not end catastrophically for businesses and  for the country.

+++

Overseas remittances for Dec amounted to US$3.28bn, which is 3.8% higher YoY.  This is the highest absolute amount of remittances in a month on record. For FY23, total remittances hit US$33.5bn, 2.9% higher compared to the previous year.

+++

Ayala Land (ALI PM) says it is spending P13bn (US$260m) to redevelop four of its flagship malls.  We estimate these four malls constitute 35% of ALI’s mall space and roughly half of its mall revenue. The redevelopment, to be done over three years, will not cause a reduction in overall mall revenue.  Malls are ~25% of ALI’s EBIT.

MACRO
BSP maintains policy rate
  • The Monetary Board left the target reverse repurchase rate at 6.5% for the third straight meeting
  • BSP adjusted its risk-adjusted inflation forecasts
    • 2024 “risk-adjusted” forecast was lowered to 3.9% from 4.2% previously while “baseline” forecast was lowered to 3.6% from 3.7%
    • 2025 “risk-adjusted” forecast was raised to 3.5% from 3.4% while “baseline” forecast was kept at 3.2%
  • BSP Senior Assistant Governor Iluminada T. Sicat said there is “scope for a rate cut” this year as soon as they see a firmer inflation downtrend
    • Inflation might continue to ease this quarter due to base effects, but it might pick up above the 2-4% target in the second quarter
    • Inflation will go back to the target in the second half
    • Stated that higher-than-expected minimum wage increases in the Philippines could reverse slowing inflation

 

MACRO
Remittances rise 3.8% YoY in December; FY23 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$33.5bn
  • Remittances coursed through banks rose 3.8% YoY to US$3.28bn in December
    • This was the fastest pace since Dec 2022 and the absolute amount was also a new high
    • In PHP terms, remittances were up 3.7% YoY in Dec
  • Growth accelerated in almost all key remittances sources, except for Kuwait, Singapore, and UAE
    • Remittances from Kuwait fell for the 8th straight month
    • Remittances growth from Singapore and UAE slowed to 2.4% and 3.8% in Dec from 3.3% and 9.3% the previous month
    • These were more than offset by stronger growth rates in most countries
  • For FY23, remittances grew by 2.9% YoY to US$33.5bn, broadly in line with the BSP’s forecast growth rate of 3%
    • The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% in 2024

 

 

MACRO
Lower House looking at wage hike of as much as P400
  • The House of Representatives is seeking to pass a P400 across-the-board wage increase for workers in the private sector
    • According to House Deputy Majority Leader and Iloilo Rep. Janette L. Garin, citing instructions from Speaker Ferdinand Martin G. Romualdez
    • Recall that last Feb 14, the Senate approved on second reading a bill calling for a P100 across-the-board minimum wage increase
  • Ms. Garin said the wage increase should be balanced by the capacity of companies to pay since some might be forced to lay off workers or even shut down

 

PROPERTY
Ayala Land to spend P13bn to redevelop four flagship malls
  • Ayala Land said it would spend P13bn to redevelop four flagship malls
    • Glorietta, Greenbelt, Trinoma, and Ayala Center Cebu
  • The four malls comprise c. 35% of Ayala Land's mall floor area
  • The renovations will begin in 1Q24 and will be completed by end-2026
  • They will be done in phases to reduce disruptions to operations
    • The company does not expect the renovations to result in lower mall revenue
    • Ayala Land’s target to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023) will remain intact
  • The spending for the redevelopment as well as the resulting lower occupancy are already in of our forecasts
  • Malls account for about 25% of Ayala Land's operating profit 
  • ALI is trading at 33% discount to NAV, 1.8X FY24E P/B, and 19.1X FY24E P/E
