REGIS REPORT

13 November 2024

We have a whole slew of 3Q results today across a few sectors. We are seeing disappointments and slowdowns in 3Q all around, with significant misses from a few bigger names.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) – Net profit for 9M dropped 18% to P8.0bn, with 3Q profit falling 54% to P1.4bn.  While the biggest reason for the underperformance was the crash in the sugar segment, the domestic branded (BCF) business is not doing great either.  Domestic BCF revenue is still down 3% YoY, in contrast to other major consumer food firms.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (=) – Net income attributable to shareholders for 9M rose 24% to P8.47bn, appearing on track to meet our estimates.  Revenues were up 11% in 9M, with a mild slowdown in 3Q (+10%). SSSG for its Philippine ops was also a bit slower (+6.4%) in 3Q (9.1% in 2Q). JFC will hold a briefing later today.
  • PLDT (TEL PM) (=) – Core income (including Maya losses) rose 6% to P25.7bn, broadly on track to match our FY24 estimate. In what has become an oft-repeated refrain across industries this quarter, revenue growth at its core mobile business slowed down to virtually zero in 3Q. The big surprise was the 105k net adds for its wired broadband segment for 3Q.

  • Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) (-) – BLOOM reported an operating loss of P470mn in 3Q24. As a result, 9M24 earnings fell 58% to P3.5bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. While Solaire North (SN) is ramping up faster than we expected, Solaire Entertainment City (SEC) is performing worse than we forecasted. EBITDA of SEC fell 16% YoY in both 3Q and 9M, as both the VIP and slot segments dragged. The company will hold its briefing this afternoon.
  • Shakey’s (PIZZA PM) (-) – 9M profits declined 10% to P671m, notwithstanding a 10% jump in revenue.  SSSG actually accelerated to 4% in 3Q, in contrast to other firms, boding well for the business. Management is still sticking to its profit guidance of double-digit growth, despite the 9M decline.
  • Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (+) – 9M24 net profit rose 8% to P2.6bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. However, we were surprised by: 1) the sharp drop in 3Q24 reservation sales, and 2) the high level of unsold inventory. 3Q24 reservation sales fell 57% QoQ and 26% YoY. Meanwhile, the company said that it had about P60bn worth of unsold inventory level (equivalent to c. 3 years of presales), 35% of which was comprised of ready-for-occupancy units.

JFC 9M24 profit jumps by 24% to P8.47bn
  • JFC reported 3Q24 profit attributable to shareholders grew 15% to P2.81bn
    • This brings 9M24 profit higher by 24% YoY to P8.47bn, equivalent to 71% of Regis FY24E and 79% of consensus estimates (P10.7bn)
  • Revenues grew 10% YoY to P67.7bn in 3Q24, mildly slower than the 1H24 growth rate of 11%
    • Philippines SSSG slowed to 6.4% in 3Q24 from 9.1% in 2Q24
  • Operating profit rose 12% YoY to P4.81bn, with EBIT margin mildly improving to 7.1% in 3Q24 from 7.0% in 3Q23
    • 9M24 operating profit is up by 17% YoY to P13.97bn, equivalent to 77% of Regis FY24E and 80% of consensus estimate of P13.97bn

CONSUMER
PIZZA: 3Q24 SSSG mildly accelerates to 4%
  • 9M24 profit drops 10%; “Double-digit profit growth” guidance maintained. PIZZA’s 3Q24 profit dipped by 3% YoY to P250mn, bringing 9M24 profit down by 10% to P671mn. The results are broadly in line with Regis and consensus expectations, given that management maintained its guidance of growing its bottom line by “double digits” this year (Regis FY24E: +13%).
  • SSSG accelerates to 4% in 3Q from 3% in 2Q.  PIZZA sustained SSSG improvements in 3Q, posting 4% SSSG during the quarter from 3% in 2Q.  Management mentioned that the acceleration was more pronounced in the latter part of the quarter, likely driven by easing inflation.  The company also made very modest price increases (~1%) this year and credited most of the SSSG improvement in 3Q24 to higher volumes.  Management was also upbeat about a strong 4Q rebound in consumer spending.
  • High investment phase starting to abate. GPMs contracted to 24% in 3Q24 from 26% in 3Q23 as the company continued to re-invest gains on input costs into product improvements and store renovations. While EBIT margins still dropped 190bps YoY to 10.5%, management noted that EBITDA margin was only lower by 40bps YoY to 16.9%, with EBITDA for the quarter growing by 13% YoY to P596mn. This trimmed the EBITDA decline to just 1% YoY for 9M24 to P1.58bn.

Management noted that future investments will largely focus on growing its organization to support its international ambitions. With minimal investments needed in 4Q, it expects gains on input costs to be more noticeable.

BANKS
Asset quality (Sept) improves... but not really

Based on headline reported BSP data, asset quality of Philippine banks improved dramatically in September with Past Due (PD), and NPL ratios falling 17bps and 12bps MoM respectively. However, the BSP data is pretty “dirty”, due mostly to the “wrong” denominator that is used when calculating asset quality ratios. While asset quality ratios did improve MoM, it was much, much milder than the reported numbers suggested. (Trigger warning: Rant coming.)

When the BSP reports loans data for their “loan growth” press release, they net out interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs, both with the BSP and with other banks). However, when it comes to the asset quality data, the “total loan portfolio” denominator INCLUDES both IBL and RRP. And this is a problem, because both IBL and RRP can move up and down quite aggressively on a monthly basis. For example, for September alone, the RRP base for all banks increased from P528bn in August to P995bn in September. That growth of P466bn is equivalent to 3% of the “total loan portfolio” base!

From here on out, I will present the reported ratios, alongside the more relevant data (excluding IBL and RRP), which I will refer to as “adjusted”. (Ok. Rant over.)

As reported, NPL ratio dropped 12bps MoM to 3.47%. However, for the adjusted NPL ratio, the improvement was much milder, down 1bp MoM to 3.80%. In nominal terms, gross NPLs were P517.5bn, up 0.9% MoM. The sector’s Past Due (PD) ratio saw bigger improvements, with the reported PD down 17bps MoM to 4.25%, while the adjusted PD ratio dipped 4bps MoM to 4.65%. The Peso value of PDs crept up just 0.2% MoM to P632.9bn. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio fell 7bps MoM to 1.98% as reported, with the adjusted RL ratio down 2bps MoM to 2.16%. The absolute level of RLs was up 0.5% MoM.

It would appear that at least some of the improvement in asset quality came from write-offs at both the Thrift Bank (TB) and Digibank (DGB) levels. The Peso value of NPLs fell 1.5% MoM for TBs and a whopping 17.5% MoM for DGBs. Both segments also saw big MoM drops in their loan loss reserves (LLR), supporting our theory. The LLR dip was milder in the All Banks data. But the LLR do tend to dip every quarter-end – even more dramatically at year-end – coinciding with earnings reporting.

Loan growth, ex-IBL/RRP, was steady at 11.0%, up slightly from the 10.9% posted in August. This was milder than the reported data that showed a 14.1% YoY growth, due to the massive jump in RRPs, as discussed above. Excluding IBL/RRPs, system-wide LDRs were flat at 69.6%, from 70.3% in August, indicating significant room for loan growth.
