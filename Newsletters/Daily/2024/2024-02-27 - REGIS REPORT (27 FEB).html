REGIS REPORT

27 February 2024

The 4Q/FY23 results season continues with a few index heavyweights today.

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) NPAT for FY23 came in at P73.41bn (=), up 29%, in line with Regis and Street expectations. Profits included a one-off, non-cash P6.1bn gain, which the bank used to accelerate loan loss provisions, accelerate depreciation of some IT assets, and write off some deferred tax assets. Outside of this, the results were pretty solid. Loan growth accelerated to 9% (from 7.5%). NIMs took a bit of a hit (-6bps QoQ), as loans were skewed to quarter-end, while deposit costs spiked due to intense competition. Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio falling to 1.85% (was 1.99% in 3Q), and NPL cover rising to 185% (from 176%).

For 2024, management guided during the briefing that it expects loan growth of “high-single-digits to low-double-digits”. They see NIMs as broadly stable, with a) higher consumer loans, and b) lower deposit costs offsetting drops in loan yields from lower rates. Profits could also be supported by falling provisions, given their high NPL cover. Management reckons they can sustain “mid-teens” ROE in the medium-term.

Interestingly, similar trends were seen across the Big Three banks – a) accelerating loan growth, b) stable NIMs, and c) improving NPL ratios. We remain constructive on the sector, and maintain our BUY ratings on the banks we cover.

On the other hand, Meralco (MER PM) (+) core income came in at P37bn, +37% YoY.  While full-year results appear mildly ahead of Street estimates (P36bn), there was a clear drop in profits for 4Q alone.  The generation business in aggregate turned in a net loss for the quarter due to plant shutdowns at its domestic generation business.  PacificLight, the Singapore genco subsidiary, remained quite profitable, but earnings there appear to be coming off.  The core distribution business, meanwhile, saw electricity sales growth of 4% for the quarter and for the year.  More below.

+++

Indicative numbers for SM Prime’s (SMPH PM) mall segment point to revenue growth of “high single digits”, according to the head of the segment, Steven Tan.

Finally, Metro Pacific (MPI) chairman Manuel Pangilinan says a listing for its toll road business, which is to be merged with San Miguel’s (SMC PM), is still possible this year.

BANKS
BDO - FY23 NPAT P74bn, in line; loan growth accelerates to 9%

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported NPAT of P73.41bn (before prefs) in 2023, up 29% YoY, and slightly ahead of Regis (P72.85bn) and consensus (P72.77bn) estimates. We note that there seems to have been a one-off gain (P6.1bn gross, about P4.5bn net of tax), a revaluation arising from BDO’s acquisition of SM Keppel Land. According to management, the bank took the opportunity to write off some IT and deferred tax assets, as well as to increase provisioning to smoothen out the impact. “Customer loans” grew 9% YoY, accelerating from the 7.5% clocked in 3Q23. NIMs dipped due largely to a jump in deposit costs.

The bank held a briefing yesterday and here are some highlights:

  • “Customer loans” grew 9% YoY, picking up pace from the 7.5% clip in 3Q23. (Headline net loans were slower at 7.0% from 7.8% in 3Q.) Deposits grew 10.8% YoY, slowing a tad from the 12.2% in 3Q23, with virtually all the growth coming from time deposits (TDs). CASA ratio held steady at 72% vs 3Q, but was down from 79% at end-2022.
  • NIMs dipped 6bps QoQ, to 4.69% by our estimate, but were still up 33bps YoY. Loan yields were still up 10bps QoQ, but deposit costs spiked up 15bps QoQ. Overall asset yields were up 7bps, while total funding costs were up 13bps QoQ.
  • Fee incomes were up 12% YoY for FY23, while Trading/fx gains were up 18% YoY. The bank booked P6.1bn worth of one-off revaluation gains from its acquisition of the 50% of SM Keppel Land that it did not yet own (roughly P4.5bn after tax). The bank used the gain to accelerate provisioning, as well as to write off some deferred tax assets (DTA) and some IT expenses.
  • Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio at 1.85%, down 14bps QoQ, now at its lowest level since before the Covid pandemic. NPL cover jumped to 185% (from 176%). Restructured loans, though ticked up c30bps QoQ.

 

POWER/UTILITIES
Meralco 4Q results: Generation business dips
  • Core income of P37bn, +37% YoY.  Meralco’s reported income of P38bn, 34% higher YoY, with core income of P37.1bn (+37% YoY).  While the full-year profit appears to be mildly ahead of Street estimates (P36bn), we highlight that 4Q results were much weaker than the prior periods, with core income of P7.1bn lower YoY.

 

  • Power distribution volumes up 4%; looking strong in 1Q24.  Distribution utility volumes rose 4.2% in 4Q, bringing FY23 volumes up 4.4%.  We view the power demand growth to be mildly off estimates as industrial usage was barely up (+0.3%).  Demand from the semiconductor sector (around a quarter of industrial sales) is still down in 4Q23.

However, the company did indicate that early 1Q unit sales were strong.  January volumes were up 10% while Feb volumes are looking like they’ll be 7% higher.

 

  • Reversal of record power generation results in 4Q. The generation business contributed a net loss of around P500m in 4Q.  PacificLight saw lower margins while plant shutdowns of some of the Philippines power generation led to declines in output.  Management is guiding for lower margins for PacificLight this year (2024), which signals that the supernormal profits of 2022-2023 are over.

 

  • Dividend of P11.235/share.  Meralco declared a final dividend of P11.235/share with respect to prior year earnings.  This put its payout at 60% of prior year core income. We reckon Meralco could pay another P9/share in the latter part of the year, taking dividends this calendar year to over P20/share (close to 6% yield).
  • One-off windfall for generation supplier ACEN? Meralco agreed to file an application with the regulator to raise payments to ACEN’s SLTEC (210MW coal) due to the extraordinary increase in coal prices following the Russian invasion of Ukraine.  This is similar to what Meralco did with respect to its supply agreement with San Miguel.  If approved, it could result in a P706m gain for ACEN that will be passed on to consumers.
PROPERTY
SM Prime mall revenue rises in January 
  • Steven Tan, who heads SM Prime’s mall division, said that the company’s mall revenue rose at a high-single digit level in January 
    • This is in line with our 6% same-mall revenue growth forecast for the year
  • Mr. Tan also said that SM Prime will open 5 malls in the Philippines and 1 mall in China this year
    • These also appear broadly in line with our forecasts 
  • Some of the new Philippine malls will have unique attributes, such as a mall with a “surf vibe” in La Union and a mall with a one-hectare garden in Sta. Rosa, Laguna
  • SMPH is trading at 26% discount to NAV, 2.2X FY24E P/B, and 21.9X FY24E P/E
MINING
Nickel Asia terminates deal for Pujada nickel project
  • Nickel Asia (NIKL PM), Hallmark, and Austral-Asia have agreed to terminate NIKL’s letter of intent to conduct a feasibility study for an HPAL facility in the latter’s Pujada nickel project in Davao Oriental
    • Hallmark and Austral-Asia Link Mining Corp. are companies under the management of Asiaticus Management Corp.
  • The parties failed to agree on the commercial terms of the definitive agreements
INFRASTRUCTURE
MPI says IPO for merged toll road firm with San Miguel possible this year
  • Manuel V. Pangilinan, CEO and President of Metro Pacific, said he expects the IPO of the potential merged entity of Metro Pacific Tollways and the tollroad unit of San Miguel (SMC PM) can take place this year
    • EBITDA of combined Philippine operations is at least P50bn
    • Both companies are currently at the stage of exchanging information of respective tollways
  • Timetable, however, does not consider potential delays from regulatory approvals in Indonesia and Philippines
