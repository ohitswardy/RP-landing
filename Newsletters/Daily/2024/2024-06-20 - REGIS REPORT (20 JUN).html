REGIS REPORT

20 June 2024

The big news today is the resignation (accepted by President Marcos) of Vice President Sara Duterte-Carpio from her post as Secretary of Education.

The resignation should mark the final break in the political alliance between the Marcos administration and the Duterte clique ahead of the elections and possible plebiscite next year.  Frankly, the rupture has been out in the open for a while. For the past months, the Dutertes have been going around the country holding anti-Marcos rallies disguised as religious gatherings. These actions have apparently been response to what the Dutertes view as threats to their interests.  Among them were the withholding of confidential funds requested by Duterte-Carpio, proposed changes to the Constitution and the attempt to arrest a close Duterte ally indicted for crimes both in the US and the Philippines.

We don’t expect the departure of Duterte-Carpio to have much of an impact on how the administration runs things.  We do reckon the biggest impact, near-term, will be on the shape and outcome of the probable Charter change plebiscite next year.

+++

Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu-Laurel is suggesting a shorter period for the 20pt tariff reduction on rice.  Instead of five years, Tiu-Laurel wants the reduction in effect for only a few months until the domestic and international situations normalizes.

Tiu-Laurel’s pronouncement reminds us that the rice tariff reduction is not fait accompli. Until the EO is finally issued, anything can still happen. And even if the EO is issued, vested interests can make importation difficult for the regular folks.

Whatever happens to rice tariffs, however, we believe that headline inflation will begin declining from August, despite the likely increases for the next two months. A strong case can thus be made for monetary loosening soon, including a possible cut in BSP policy rates.  A reduction in rice tariffs will make such a case even stronger.

+++

The local BPO industry association says it expects to hit 7% growth in headcount and revenue for this year.  In aggregate, the BPO industry is, by far, the largest tenant for office property in the country so its health is crucial.

MACRO
DA chief seeks shorter period for rice tariff cut
  • Agriculture Secretary: rice tariff reduction should be between 6-12 months instead of the 5-year period saying it would affect rice farmers
    • Said tariff reduction must also have a regular review to review efficiency of the measure
  • Increase in domestic prices were said to be caused by external shocks (e.g. India’s ban on non-basmati rice exports) putting pressure on global rice supply
  • Agriculture Undersecretary proposed to revert rice tariffs to 50%, citing that eduction in tariff revenues could jeopardize enhancement projects for the rice industry
  • Members of Cabinet have not discussed a proposal to cut import duties on rice to 15% from the current 35%
  • Marcos has until July 21 to modify tariff rates through an EO
  • Inflation rose to 3.9% in May
    • Food inflation slowed to 6.1%, with rice easing to 23%
    • Local regular milled rice is sold for P45-52 while well-milled rice is sold at P48-55, approx 20% higher than the same period last year
PROPERTY
IT-BPM industry likely to hit 7% growth in 2024
  • The Information Technology and Business Process Management (IT-BPM) industry is on track to achieve 7% growth in revenue and headcount this year, in line with the industry’s “baseline scenario”
    • Thus, by the end of the year, the industry should employ 1.82mn people and generate US$38bn in revenue
    • In 2023, the industry employed 1.7mn individuals and generated US$35.5bn in revenue
  • IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) President Jack Madrid admitted that the industry is unlikely to reach the “aggressive targets” of 1.84mn in headcount and US$40bn in revenues this year
  • Under IBPAP’s 2028 roadmap, the industry is targeting to reach 2.5 million jobs and generate $59 billion in revenues by 2028
  • BPO firms occupy 60%-80% of office space of the major listed developers
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, and 5.1X FY24E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 72% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, and 3.3X FY24E P/E
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY23E P/B, and 6.3X FY24E P/E
GAMING
Junket operator Dowinn ceases operations
  • Junket operator Dowinn has ceased its operations in Philippine casinos
  • It had operations in Okada, Solaire Entertainment City, City of Dreams Manila, D’ Heights, and Royce
  • Bloomberry expects minimal impact from its closure, implying that it is not a large contributor to BLOOM revenue
