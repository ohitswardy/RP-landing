We have two big consumer names on our results round-up today, Universal Robina (URC PM) and San Miguel Food and Beverage (FB PM).

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) – FY23 results came in behind expectations as soft demand persisted, resulting in flat Philippines branded consumer food (BCF) sales in 4Q23. A notable improvement in margins, particularly for int’l BCF, helped lift 4Q EBIT (+7% YoY) despite the soft top line. Management is guiding for mid-high single digit top line growth in FY24 and continued margin improvement, broadly in line with our FY24 expectations.

We take a dive into the arena of digital finance in a piece published yesterday. Digitalization was meant to vastly improve financial inclusion, in a country where total consumer loans (mortgages, auto loans, credit cards and personal loans) in the formal banking sector account for just over 10% of GDP. Clearly the adoption of digital payments has been explosive, with small-ticket transactions over InstaPay surging 42% YoY by value and 53% YoY by volume in 2023. However, the lending side has been… challenging. The six digibanks saw loans peak at P20bn in August last year, only to fall back down to P17bn as of January. Meanwhile, asset quality deteriorated sharply with past due (PD) rates of around 30%.

For now, the cleanest way to play the theme is Globe (GLO PM), which owns GCash/Mynt, by far and away the dominant e-wallet and retail payments platform. Its ubiquity gives it access to enormous amounts of its users’ data, which allow it to overcome the lack of a national ID and a national credit bureau. Most crucially, Mynt is profitable, printing about P6.5bn (about $120mn) in NPAT in 2023. Globe’s 36% share of Mynt’s profits accounted for more than 12% of group earnings. While some think a Mynt IPO may be a ways away, we think investors are paid to wait. Globe’s traditional mobile business has turned, with revenues growing 8% in 4Q23. Capex is falling, which should see free cashflow improve dramatically. The stock pays a 5-6% dividend yield. All of that, plus the option value on GCash/Mynt. What’s not to like?

MACRO
DOF refines CTRP Package 4 proposal
  • The Department of Finance refined its proposal for Package 4 of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Program (CTRP)
    • refined proposal seeks to maintain the structure of some products and instruments while deferring the implementation of certain provisions by 2028
    • Dividend income tax will remain unchanged until 2027, with a proposed harmonization of 10% in 2028
    • The stock transaction tax will gradually be reduced annually by 0.1%, from 0.6% to 0.1% in 2028
  • Refined proposal was presented by Undersecretary Karlo Adriano to finance sector stakeholders in a briefing on March 1, 2024
  • House Bill 9277 was approved by the Lower House and was transmitted to the Senate on March 6
    • HB9277 called for an immediate reduction in the stock transaction tax to 0.1% and dividend income tax to 10%
INFRASTRUCTURE
NAIA PPP contract signing set on Monday
  • The Department of Transportation (DOTr) has scheduled the signing of the 15-year concession for the rehabilitation of NAIA on March 18 at the Malacañang
    • This means the San Miguel consortium complied with all of the post-bidding requirements of the DOTr, and that it can begin the transition program for the airport
  • For the DOTr, the goal is to turn over the operations and maintenance of NAIA to the SMC-led group on or before Sept. 11

CONSUMER
San Miguel Food & Beverage FY23 results – Food segment drives 4Q23
  • FB posted net profit to equity holders of P23bn (+5% YoY) in FY23, ahead of expectations. The implied NPAT to equity holders in 4Q23 alone was P7bn, up 40% YoY. The food segment was the outperformer, with segment profit doubling YoY in 4Q. Sales grew 4% YoY (flat in 9M23)on better volumes and ASPs. Segment EBIT margin continued to improve (+400bps QoQ/YoY) on favorable input prices.
  • San Miguel Brewery sales rose 5% YoY in 4Q23 while EBIT flat. Domestic volumes declined 2% YoY, an improvement from the 4% decline in the previous quarter. We estimate volumes are now at ~20% pre-pandemic levels. Segment EBIT margin contracted 80bps YoY as the company incurred more A&P expenses to drive consumption (eg. large-scale events, on-the-ground activations, etc).
  • Ginebra sales grew 15% YoY in 4Q23, though volume growth was +3% YoY. Management indicated that volumes are tracking better in Jan-Feb and market shares are improving.

Germany's wpd to invest P56bn on top of earlier commitments in Philippine wind projects
  • Germany's wpd GmbH is undertaking pre-development activities for five onshore projects in the Philippines with an estimated investment value of P56bn and combined RE capacity of 565MW
    • Projects are to be located in the Ilocos Region, Aklan, Iloilo, Antique, and Bulacan, and are expected to be completed between 2027 and 2030
  • DTI said the P56bn investment is separate from the US$4bn investment deals presented to Marcos during the business forum held during his visit and outside of wpd's P394.2bn investment registered by the BOI last year
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in February
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 11bps MoM to 20.5%, while foreign ownership of the PSE Index also climbed 22bps MoM to 24.8%
  • Net foreign buying rose to US$129mn, from US$80mn in January
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 17 saw increases in foreign ownership while 13 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • ALI – foreign ownership rose 56bps MoM to 29.1%. Net foreign buying amounted to US$17.2mn in February
    • NIKL – foreign ownership expanded 51bps MoM to 36.0%
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 41bps MoM to 19.4%. Net foreign buying stood at US$9.2mn.
    • URC – foreign ownership fell 1.18 percentage points MoM to 22.4%. It also saw the second highest net foreign selling in February (at US$14.9mn)
    • CNVRG – foreign ownership dropped 53bps MoM to 17.5%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$4.8mn, the third highest for the month
