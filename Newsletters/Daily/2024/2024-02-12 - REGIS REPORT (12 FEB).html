REGIS REPORT

12 February 2024

The consortium led by San Miguel Corp (SMC PM) submitted the highest financial bid, by a mile, for the concession to run Manila’s main airport, NAIA. The bid offered 82.2% share of airport revenues to the government (i.e. the consortium will only keep 17.8% of revenue), way more than the two other groups whose financial bids were revealed (33% and 26%). The official award of the P170bn (US$3bn) deal is expected in the next few days.

The “super consortium”, which included six of the largest business groups in the country, submitted the lowest of the three financial bids opened (26%).

Much is riding on the success of this PPP project. Not only is the NAIA rehabilitation important in terms of easing transportation bottlenecks into/from the country’s capital, it is also the first PPP project to be bid out under the Marcos administration. Failure would not bode well.

Having said that, an award to the SMC-led consortium could be subject to some risk and possible controversy. SMC is already developing the competing P700bn (US$13bn) Bulacan airport. While SMC holds a 33% stake (maximum allowed under the bid rules) in the consortium, questions are being floated about its true economic stake.

Regis’s research co-brand partner Jefferies updated their outlook for the US economy entitled Growth, Everlasting? Unlikely (attached). They have drastically altered its Fed rate cut view, now only calling for 75bps worth of cuts in 2024E (from 225bps in their December report), and another 50bps in 2025E. The main source of the change in view is the surprising resilience of the US consumer, where the expected “pullback” in spending only happened in October, and has since rebounded. Jefferies are now looking at a much milder recession in 2H24E, and even this is due largely to high base effects in 2023.

The forecasts still officially call for a 25bps reduction in the Fed funds rate in 1Q24. But they do warn that if growth stays robust, even that expectation could get pushed back to 2H24.

 

+ + +

Bank asset quality indicators improved dramatically in December, on both lower bad accounts MoM, and accelerating loan growth. Preliminary data show NPL ratio (ex-interbank) fell to 3.31%, down 18bps MoM from the 3.49% in November. The PHP value of NPLs dropped 1.6% MoM to P446.7bn, while loans were up 3.8% MoM to P13.5trn. Past Due (PD) ratio fell even more steeply, down 27bps MoM to 3.95% from 4.22% the previous month, as PD loans shrank 2.8% MoM. Both NPL and PD ratios were the lowest since December 2022. Restructured Loan (RL) ratio also dipped 10bps MoM to 2.19%, the lowest since April 2021. NPL cover improved slightly to 102.1% from 101.5% the prior month.

Meanwhile, loans (ex-interbank) surged 10.1% YoY in December, picking up pace from the 9.3% print in November and 8.4% in October. Note that the December number is preliminary – ie incomplete likely due to one or more banks submitting late. We thus expect the loan growth figure to be revised upwards in the coming weeks.

We remain positive on the banks. While the sector may not be thematically consistent with potential rate cuts, we note that accelerating loan growth – despite the higher interest rates – could be a positive catalyst for continued share price performance.
