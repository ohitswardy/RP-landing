REGIS REPORT

01 March 2024

More results today, with Manila Water beating expectations, but with misses coming from D&L, Security Bank, and Semirara.

D&L Industries (DNL PM) – behind – FY23 net profit (-31% YoY to P2.3bn) came in behind Regis and consensus expectations as higher costs from the company’s new Batangas plant weighed on margins. 4Q revenues were down 8% YoY, though volumes rebounded during the quarter as the oleochem segment recovered. Management is more optimistic for 2024 given easing inflationary pressures.

Security Bank (SECB PM) – behind – Earnings disappointed, with FY23 profit of P9.1bn falling 14% YoY, well behind Regis (P10.5bn) and consensus (P10.2bn) estimates. Loan growth accelerated to 7.1% (was 3.8% in 3Q), driven by high-yielding consumer/MSME loans (+29% YoY). However, earnings were dragged by provision expenses, which jumped 64% YoY on deteriorating asset quality. NPL ratio blipped up 21bps QoQ to 3.36%, from 3.15% in 3Q. Despite the large provision charge, NPL cover plunged to 82% from 92% in 3Q. The company will have its briefing on Monday 4 March at 5pm.

Semirara (SCC PM) – behind – Semirara held its briefing yesterday and one of our key takeaways was management’s firm stance on paying a “good” dividend in April. Even though mining costs could remain elevated this year, we take comfort in the fact that ICI4 prices (spot: US$56/MT) are hovering above our 24E coal ASP of US$42/MT. More below.

Manila Water (MWC PM) – ahead – Headline profit FY23 profit of P5.59bn included a P4.1bn impairment charge related to its investment in Thai-based East Water. On a recurring basis, profit of P9.63bn (+74% YoY) was well above our forecast due to lower cash costs at the East zone. The company also declared a dividend of P1.129/sh, which is higher than our forecast and is equivalent to a yield of 6%.  We nonetheless believe the trajectory of earnings and dividends is skewed to the downside, given the company’s investment commitments in the East zone.

The country’s 2023 budget deficit improved to P1.5trn, down 6% YoY. Revenues grew 7.9% YoY, while expenses were up just 3.4%. However, expenses excluding interest costs were up just 1.1%. The fiscal deficit to GDP ratio improved to 6.2% from 7.3% in 2022, though it was still a bit higher than the government’s forecast of 6.1% deficit to GDP. The large deficit will continue to constrain spending in 2024, with the government’s budget up just 5% YoY. Much will depend on the planned PPP projects to boost infrastructure spending.

The Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) hopes to support infra spend. It recently announced plans to raise as much as $1bn for energy projects, in partnership with other countries’ sovereign wealth funds (SWFs)

The BSP expects February inflation to come in between 2.8% and 3.6%, which would be faster than the 2.8% recorded in January. The BSP cited rising costs of food, fuel, and electricity. We expect the BSP to begin cutting policy interest rates in 3Q23.

BANKS
SECB FY23 NPAT P9.1bn, down 14% YoY, BEHIND on higher NPLs

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported FY23 NPAT of P9.1bn, down 14% YoY, well BEHIND Regis (P10.5bn) and consensus (P10.2bn) forecasts. The main drag came from provision expenses, which jumped 64% YoY on higher NPLs QoQ. To the upside, loan growth accelerated to 7.1%, from 3.8% the previous quarter. As before, the figures were slightly confusing, due to the accounting of currency swaps which nominally boosted NIMs, but recorded large trading losses (see below).

The bank will hold a briefing on Monday, 4 March, at 5pm. Ahead of that, some highlights from the disclosures:

  • Loans grew 7.1% YoY, accelerating from the 3.8% clip in 3Q23. Growth was driven by consumers/MSMEs, which jumped 29% YoY (vs 23% in 3Q23), while “wholesale” corporate/commercial loans were flat YoY. Within consumers, growth was led by credit cards (+44%), auto (+36%), and mortgages (+18%), while MSME loans surged 77% YoY.  Consumer/MSMEs accounted for 29% of total loans, up from 24% in 2022. Deposits were flat YoY.
  • NIMs appear to have spiked 31bps QoQ to 5.0%. However, this is misleading. Recall that SECB entered into interest rate swaps, with low nominal interest rates. The swap costs, however, are booked as forex losses. The gap between reported NIM and what the bank estimates its adjusted NIM has been widening every quarter this year. Considering the large FX loss (P1.3bn) in 4Q23, management estimates 4Q23 NIMs were 4.6% still up a decent 27bps QoQ. Loan yields edged up 6bps QoQ.
  • Fee incomes were up 15% YoY driven by credit cards, remittances and bancassurance.
  • Opex was up 15% YoY, driven by continued IT and manpower costs.
  • Provision expense jumped 64% YoY to P4.7bn, as asset quality deteriorated, with NPL ratio up 21bps QoQ to 3.36% from 3.15% in 3Q. Despite the higher provision charge, NPL cover fell sharply to 82%, from 92% in 3Q23.

The deterioration of asset quality is of some concern, especially as the bank ramps up its consumer/MSME growth. We expect these to be the main topic of focus during the briefing next week.

 

INDUSTRIALS
DNL FY23 net profit -31% YoY; volumes rebound in 4Q
  • DNL reported FY23 net profit of P2.3bn, down 31% YoY, behind Regis and consensus forecasts. FY23 revenues (-23% YoY to P33.5bn) were behind expectations, but the bottom line was weighed further by higher opex and interest expense.
  • For 4Q23 alone, net profit fell 35% YoY to P503mn. Excluding the impact of the Batangas plant, net profit would have been P656mn (-28% YoY).
  • Revenues were down 8% YoY, though volumes rebounded during the quarter (+7% YoY by our estimate)
    • High margin specialty products grew sharply (+40%), driven by a recovery in oleochem. This is, however, flattered by a low YoY base.
    • Meanwhile, the commodities segment continued to see a decline in volumes (-12% YoY by our estimate), albeit a slower pace of decline from 9M23 (-28%)
  • 4Q23 EBITDA fell 32% YoY driven mainly by higher depreciation and opex relating to the Batangas plant. Note that some of these fixed costs are also allocated to cost of goods sold and was a driver of GPM compression despite the recovery in volumes
  • Management is more optimistic going into 2024 with easing inflation and interest rates, which should lead to better demand and profitability. Given that it is coming from a low base, management expects 2024 net profit to grow at least 10% YoY, implying at least P2.5bn NPAT (Consensus FY24: P3.4bn)
  • DNL currently trades at 15x 2024E consensus PE
  • Based on management guidance, DNL currently trades at 20x 2024 PE
