REGIS REPORT

08 November 2024

3Q24 GDP growth came in at 5.2%, surprisingly weaker than the 6.4% print in 2Q24 and also well short of consensus estimate of 5.7%.

A sharp slowdown in government spending and lower exports brought down the GDP print. Outside of these, consumer spending and private investments have begun to pick up and will likely continue to do so as inflation slows further and the impact of interest rate cuts flow through the economy in succeeding periods.

We argue that the case for further monetary easing is now even more justifiable. Not only will it help private consumption and investment, it could also free up government budgets by easing the debt service burden. We thus still expect a 25bp policy rate reduction in Dec.

Here’s our results round-up for today:

  • Monde (MONDE PM) (=) - Core profit for 9M rose 31% to P7.45bn, which would be within our expectations. Top line growth remained modest (+3%) but margins recovered due to lower input costs. Management reckons gross margins could be stable near-term, but some of these gains will be plowed back into marketing spend.  Management also took note of an improvement in Oct, which we think could bode well for 4Q.

 

  • Globe (GLO PM) (=) – Core income reached P17.6bn for 9M, +19% with 3Q core income at P5.9bn (+22%). While bottom line numbers look in line with estimates, we note the slowdown in service revenue growth. This was saved, in part, by continued expansion of Mynt’s profits, which were already 22%  of core profit in 3Q alone.

 

  • ACEN Corp (ACEN PM) (-) – 3Q core profits (ex- value realization and other gains) amounted to ~P850m, down sharply from the pace in 1H. This took 9M core income to P6.35bn, well below trend for our FY24 expectations (>P10bn).  Management blamed seasonal variations in RE output but we believe this was aggravated by downtime at coal plant SLTEC.  One potentially positive news for the company is the impending operation of the trading market for renewable energy certificates (RECs).

 

  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (-) – 9M24 recurring profit rose 4% to P9.3bn, slightly behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. The company cancelled P8bn of presales due to tightening of its credit standards. Thus, the cancellations are not necessarily indicative of a drastic change in demand conditions in 3Q24. Meanwhile, residential revenue (of standalone RLC projects) fell for the second consecutive quarter, leading to an operating loss for the residential segment.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (=) – 9M24 headline profit rose 14% to P13.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Meanwhile, office revenue grew 6% QoQ and YoY, possibly suggesting an improvement in occupancy rates.  We hope to find out more during the briefing this afternoon.

 

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (=) – We threshed out and published our views on its 3Q numbers (9M profits up 14% to P5.3bn) in a short piece yesterday. The outlook for the consumption seems to be looking up, which should be good for CNPF and many of the other consumer companies.

MACRO
3Q24 GDP slows to 5.2% on weak government spending, lower exports
  • GDP grew at 5.2% in 3Q24, moderating from the 6.4% growth rate in 2Q
    • This came in below consensus forecast of 5.7%
  • On the demand side, the slowdown can be largely attributed to the softness in government spending (+5.0%) and lower exports (-1.0%)
    • Private consumption grew at a faster rate of 5.1% in 3Q from 4.7% in 2Q
    • Private investments likewise accelerated to 13.1% in 3Q24 from 11.6% in 2Q and rebounded from the anemic -0.3% growth rate in 3Q23 on the back of stronger durable equipment and inventory re-stocking
    • Construction slowed to 8.9%, after public construction moderated to 3.7% (from 22% in 2Q) and offset sustained growth in private construction (11.9% from 10.3%)
    • Net imports grew, after goods exports fell 3%, weighing on overall GDP
  • On the supply side, the agri sector continued to fare poorly, declining by 2.8% from 2.3% the previous quarter
    • Industry and services sectors also slowed to 5.0% and 6.3%, respectively
  • Brings 9M24 GDP growth to 5.8%, below the government’s target of 6-7% but in line with Bloomberg consensus median forecast of 5.8%
    • To reach Regis’ FY24E of 6.0%, 4Q GDP should pick up to 6.5%

CONSUMER
Monde 9M core profits rise 31%
  • 9M core profit up 31% on margin recovery.  Monde posted core profit of P7.45bn for 9M, up 31% as margins expanded on lower input costs. For 3Q alone, revenues rose 3% while EBITDA jumped 14% YoY. Core income for 3Q was up only +8% due to FX losses on its USD cash holdings (management treats them as “core”). We view these results as being a tad better than Regis expectations as well as the Street’s.

 

  • APAC BFB revenue up 5% in 3Q. Revenue growth for its core APAC BFB operations grew 5% YoY in 3Q, roughly the same pace as 2Q.  Top line growth was driven almost entirely by volumes / mix (i.e. not by price). We note a marked pick-up in biscuit volumes for 3Q as capacity bottlenecks are gradually resolved. Resolution of some capacity issues for noodles should also lift sales in 4Q.

 

Meanwhile, 3Q margins stayed high (38% gross) due to favorable input costs and productivity gains. Management says the gains in gross margins are being partly reinvested in marketing spend (and partly retained for shareholders).

 

  • EBITDA breakeven for alternative meat segment. Monde’s alt meat business was able to break even on EBITDA but still reported a P169m net loss for the quarter. While cost cuts are helping to trim losses, the more important problem of contracting revenue faces no easy resolution. Revenue was down 5% YoY in 3Q and 2% in 9M.

 

  • Buyer’s remorse for Figaro? Management expressed what we would described as “buyer’s remorse” regarding its investment in restaurant operator Figaro (FCG PM).  Monde invested P820m to buy 15% of Figaro last year, with hopes to be able extract product synergies. It appears those hopes have been dashed.

 

  • Near-term outlook improving, aiming to pay more than 60% of profits. Management said that Oct seemed to have picked up with revenues hitting a monthly record. This bodes well for 4Q even if management continues to express caution about domestic economic conditions. The company is aiming to pay more than its target 60% of profits next year, depending on how cash flows shape up.

TELCOS
Globe 3Q results - First Take
  • Core income for 9M at P17.6bn (+19%).  For 3Q alone, core income was P5.9bn (+22% YoY).  We would describe the results as being within expectations even as service revenue growth slowed.

 

  • Service revenue growth slows down to +3% in 3Q as mobile revenue expanded +3%.  Broadband revenue declined 6%.

 

  • Mynt growth continued as profit contributions for 3Q reached P1.3bn.  Mynt accounted for 22% of Globe’s core income in 3Q.

 

Briefing is not until Monday (Nov 11).

PROPERTY
Megaworld 9M24 net profit rises 14%; in line
  • Megaworld’s 9M24 net profit rose 14% to P13.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • Residential revenue grew 30% in 3Q24 and 9M24, likely suggesting that cancellation rates have moderated (when the buyer has paid at least 10% of the unit value)
  • 3Q24 office revenue grew 6% QoQ and YoY, which could signal an improvement in occupancy rates
  • 3Q24 mall revenue grew 11% YoY to P1.5bn, similar to the 2019 level
  • Megaworld has about US$600mn in unhedged US$ debt. Thus, the company will record forex losses from a depreciation of the Philippine peso (vs the US$)
  • The company will hold a briefing this afternoon

POWER/UTILITIES
Operation of renewable energy certificate market eyed by Dec
  • The Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) is aiming to launch commercial operations for trading of renewable energy certificates (REC)
    • RECs are supposed to be certificates that serve as evidence of RE generation
    • RECs are similar in concept to “carbon credits” and can be traded
  • Firms who generate RE are entitled to RECs as verified by government, which can be sold
  • The REC market will allow distribution utilities to (indirectly) comply with government mandates to increase RE use
    • Mandate is under the Renewable Portfolio Standards (RPS)
  • The market will provide transparent pricing for the value of RECs
