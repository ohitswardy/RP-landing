REGIS REPORT

22 May 2024

The PHP/USD exchange rate pierced through the P58 level yesterday, prompting a warning from the Bangko Sentral (BSP) that it may intervene. Beyond just inflation, the weakness/strength of the PHP should also be a key factor in any BSP decision on interest rates. Thus, weakness could postpone any decision to cut policy rates.

In the meantime, also on FX-related news, the country recorded a deficit in its balance of payments (BOP) of US$639m for April. YT-April, the BOP is in a US$401m deficit.

+++

Meanwhile, the last name on our coverage finally reported 1Q results.  Vista Land’s (VLL PM) 1Q24 net profit rose 15% to P2.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Residential revenue rose YoY for the third consecutive quarter (after 15 consecutive quarters of declines), likely signaling that cancellations have peaked and have begun to normalize. Meanwhile, rental revenue rose 13% YoY even though the combined revenue of AllDay (ALLDY PM) and AllHome (HOME PM), which occupy 50% of VLL’s mall space, fell 2% YoY. Rental receivables stood at P37.3bn, equivalent to 2.25 years of rental revenue. This suggests that rental revenue is overstated relative to cash rent received.

+++

First Gen (FGEN PM) disclosed that it signed a shareholders’ agreement and a share subscription agreement with Tokyo Gas for the latter to acquire 20% of FGEN LNG.  FGEN LNG is the owner and operator of the LNG terminals supplying fuel to FGEN’s gas-fired power plants.  These agreements are a continuation of the cooperation agreement that were previously announced.

+++

Foreign ownership in the stock market declined in May by 6bp, based on data from the Philippine Stock Exchange. We reckon it would have declined even more (16bp) if not for what we believe was the correction of a clerical error in the data for one stock (Jollibee).

Peso hits P58:USD1; BSP to intervene

    • According to BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr.
    • Peso’s weakness against the US dollar was in line with other currencies in the region
MACRO
BOP deficit widens to US$639mn in April
  • The country’s overall balance of payments posted a deficit of US$639mn in April
    • This was higher than the US$148mn deficit posted in April 2023 and was a reversal of the US$1.17bn BOP surplus in March 2024
    • Cited outflows arising from the national government’s net foreign currency debt service
  • Brings YT-April BOP deficit to US$401mn, a reversal of the US$3.3bn surplus in the same period a year ago
  • BOP position reflects the slight decrease in GIR level to US$102.6bn in end-April from US$104.1bn in end-March 2024
INFRASTRUCTURE
Infrastructure spending rises 9% YoY in 1Q24
  • Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 9.3% YoY to P266bn in 1Q24
    • Implies that infra spend in March 2024 alone rose 36% YoY to P133bn
    • Department of Public Works and Highways posted “strong spending performance” for payment of progress billings for projects and also made advance payments to contractors
  • Recall that in 1Q24 GDP, the construction sector grew 6.8%, driven primarily by government (+12.4% YoY)
  • The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP
    • To reach this target, infra spend should average P134bn per month for the rest of the year
INFRASTRUCTURE
Right of way, budget woes delay buildup of rail projects
  • The Department of Transportation (DOTr) has warned of construction delays for the biggest rail projects if right-of-way (ROW) acquisitions and the release of appropriate budgets are further held back
    • DOTr lamented the piling difficulties in two projects: P874bn North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR) and the P489bn Metro Manila Subway
    • Projects are now expected to be completed by 2029 or later
  • Meanwhile, the full completion of San Miguel’s (SMC PM) MRT-7 rail line has been pushed back further to 2028 due to similar issues
    • Partial operations (i.e. North Avenue to Lagro) could start by end-2025
CONGLOS
DMCI sets sights on new acquisitions
  • Apart from the planned purchase of a majority stake in Cemex Philippines (CHP PM), DMCI is also looking at acquiring other mining assets
    • Mining assets may include copper and gold projects and a coal mine in Mindanao, provided these are open pit
  • Outside of these, DMCI intends to continue with its “aggressive organic growth”  
    • The company hopes to fix certain issues in the next few years, including (a) high inventory levels at DMCI Homes and (b) permitting issues at its nickel mining unit
PROPERTY
Vista Land 1Q24 net profit rises 15%, ahead
  • Vista Land’s 1Q24 net profit rose 15% to P2.5bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • Residential revenue expanded 10% YoY
    • This is the third consecutive quarter of YoY increases, after 15 consecutive quarters of YoY declines
  • Rental revenue rose 13% YoY to P4.1bn
    • This is double the 2019 average rental revenue
    • The combined revenue of AllDay (ALLDY PM) and AllHome (HOME PM), which occupy about 50% of VLL’s mall space, fell 2% YoY
  • Rental receivables stood at P37.3bn, which we estimate is equivalent to 2.25 years of rental revenue
  • VLL is trading at 71% discount to NAV, 0.1X FY24E P/B, and 2.5X FY24E P/E

 

OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in April
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 6bps MoM to 20.5% (in April). On the other hand, foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 17bps MoM to 25.0%
    • We estimate that if the JFC foreign ownership had remained flat, the foreign ownership of the market would have fallen 16bps MoM
  • Net foreign selling accelerated to US$414mn, from US$45mn (net selling) in March
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 11 saw increases in foreign ownership while 18 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • JFC – foreign ownership rose 5.01 percentage points MoM to 24.5%. We believe there was a clerical error in the March data, which suggested that JFC foreign ownership fell 5.86ppt MoM. 
    • MBT – foreign ownership increased 53bps MoM to 21.1%. It also experienced the highest net foreign buying in April (US$25.5mn).
    • ALI – foreign ownership fell 87bps MoM to 28.0%. It also saw the highest net foreign selling for the month (US$68.6mn).
    • NIKL – foreign ownership declined 78bps MoM to 35.1%.
    • AC – foreign ownership decreased 44bps MoM to 26.2%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$26.2mn, the second highest for the month.
