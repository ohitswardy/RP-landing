REGIS REPORT

29 October 2024

3Q results season is now definitively upon us, and the big banks are among the earliest ones to report.  Today, we have numbers from BDO (BDO PM) and Metrobank (MBT PM), as well as Meralco (MER PM), and Wilcon (WLCON PM).

  • Meralco (MER PM) (+) – Meralco reported core income of P35bn for 9M24, +17% YoY, powered by its non-regulated businesses (generation, retail supply).  Volume growth normalized to +4% in 3Q (+7% in 9M) as temperatures moderated. Beyond the 3Q interims, the briefing provided an update on two major pending issues: its tariff reset, and the acquisition of the LNG complex controlled by San Miguel (SMC PM).  More below.

 

  • Wilcon (WLCON PM) (-) – 9M profit fell 22% to P2.1bn, well behind expectations. For 3Q alone, earnings declined 33% P608m as sales slid. Margins (gross and EBIT) contracted on the drop in sales (-3% YoY) and SSSG (a disconcerting 8.5%). Management indicated that SSSG for Oct so far is now close to breaking even, but it might be too early to say a recovery is on its way.

 

  • BDO (BDO PM) (=) – The bank provided more color on its 3Q results with its briefing yesterday.  Outside headline profit of P60.6bn (+12%), some of the key highlights were (1) loan growth of 12.8%, (2) a slight dip in NIMs for the quarter, (3) NPL ratio dipping to 1.82%.  Management also mentioned that the bank has yet to book trading gains from its bond portfolio, as they felt it was too early to cash in.  More below.

 

+++

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 9M24 NPAT of P35.7bn, up 12% YoY, slightly ahead of Regis (FY 24E P46.9bn) and consensus (P47.9bn). Gross loan growth accelerated to 15.6% (from 14.9^% in 2Q), driven by strong corporate loan demand +17%. However, NIMs were hit hard though, crashing 27bps QoQ. Funding costs jumped 25bps QoQ, as the bank let go of P160bn worth of TDs QoQ, and replaced it with wholesale funds (bonds)  While the headline cost may be higher, it is possible that the all-in costs were lower (TDs carry 75bps stamp duty, 25bps deposit insurance, and RRR). NPAT was boosted by P5.6bn trading gains (+56% YoY), making up for weak fee incomes. Provision expense fell 48% in 9M24, as NPLs improved to 1.59% (was 1.66%) and NPL cover was flat 162% (was 163%).

The bank will hold its earnings call at 4:30pm today.

BANKS
BDO 9M24 P60.6bn, up 12% - without (yet) booking trading gains

BDO (BDO PM) reported 9M24 NPAT of P60.6bn, up 12% YoY, broadly in line with Regis (FY24 P82.3bn)  and slightly ahead of consensus (P80.7bn). The result is noteworthy in that the bank DID NOT book strong trading gains, as management felt it was too early to monetize its bond portfolio given downside to interest rates. The bank also kept NPL coverage high (178%), though they did guide to potentially lowering it going forward.

(Side note: The bank made a number of accounting format changes that make it difficult to compare a) with our historical data, and b) with our current forecasts. The big changes include a) Insurance income is now presented as just one item, instead of line-by-line consolidation; and b) stamp duties on PHP time deposits are now included in interest expense, instead of in opex. These serve to lower NIMs, but also lower cost-to-income ratios. There was no impact on NPAT.)

That out of the way, some highlights from the FS and the briefing:

  • “Customer loan” growth was 12.8% YoY, steady from the 13.0% in 2Q24, with consumers leading at +15.7% (same as 2Q). Mid-market loans accelerated to 12.2% (from 10.5%) while top-tier corporate loans slowed to 11.7% YoY from 12.9% in 2Q and 16.6% in 1Q24. Deposits were up 10%, with CASA Ratio stabilizing at 69%.
  • NIMs, as calculated by management, were down 12bps QoQ to 4.23%. This was due to two factors. Asset yields came down, as the bank chose to park excess funds in short-duration instruments, in preparation for loan disbursements. Meanwhile, funding costs increased due to the refinancing of sustainable bonds at much higher rates (previous bonds were issued in 2022). Management did mention, though, that competition was getting quite intense on loan pricing, while time deposit rates are not coming down despite the rate cuts.
  • Trading/fx gains were flat YoY as the bank chose not to realize gains on its bond portfolio just yet. Management believes it is too early, as there is still significant downside to interest rates, with the market expecting several more policy cuts in coming quarters.
  • Opex moderated to +13% in 9M, and just 10% YoY in 3Q. While IT spend continues to be elevated, the bank is nearing the tail end of the upgrade spend, and expects IT spend as a % of revenues to stabilize / fall from current levels. The bank continued to expand its branch network, adding 26 in 9M24 (22 for Network Bank, and 4 for the parent), to a total of 1,746 at end-September.
  • Asset quality improved, with NPLs down 24bps QoQ to 1.82% and with NPL cover up to 178% (from 169% in 2Q24). The improvement was driven in part by some accounts turning current again, as well as some write-offs (esp credit cards) that were supposed to be done in 2Q24. Credit cost remained low at c46bps. While the bank said it wanted to keep a buffer for future “black swan” events, management hinted that if asset quality remained benign, it could afford to reduce NPL cover in the near- to medium-term.

Overall, the 9M results seemed pretty solid. While we are wary of NIM pressures, the bank has some potential NPAT cushion heading into 2025E from a) trading gains, and b) lower provisions.

 

UTILITIES
Meralco 9M core profit rises 17%
  • Core income up 17% for 9M24. Meralco reported net income of P33.8bn (+19% YoY) for 9M. Adjusting for one-off items, core income was P35.1bn (+17%). Derived 3Q core income was P11.9bn (+10% YoY) which we believe is ahead of Street estimates.  The core distribution business is growing steadily while the non-regulated businesses continue to do well, even if the profits are now well off their peaks.

 

  • 3Q volume growth moderates to +4%. Volume growth for 3Q slowed to 4.1%, bringing 9M unit growth to +7.1%. Most segments saw a slowdown in growth but did not contract in 3Q. Residential volumes still grew 6.5%, which management said was largely due to higher temperatures. Industrial volumes grew only 0.1% in the quarter.

 

  • Regulatory decision on current regulatory period (“5RP”). Management brought up media reports quoting the ERC chair, who said that a vote within the ERC has been made regarding Meralco’s 5th Regulatory Period (5RP). The vote apparently favored Meralco’s application to treat 5RP as a lapsed period and thus fix its tariff to what it was for 4RP (= P1.35/kwh). Such a decision, if indeed promulgated, would have a substantial impact on Meralco’s immediate profit as well as for the entire period up to Jun 2026. We estimate the annualized impact would be in the range of P6bn (post-tax), excluding the impact of provision reversals.

 

  • Chromite deal unlikely to be closed by year-end, greenfield plant now delayed. We reckon the likelihood of the Chromite deal being completed by year-end is becoming slimmer by the day.  “Chromite” is the name of the deal for Meralco to buy 40% of the LNG complex (~2,500MW of LNG plants + LNG terminal) controlled by San Miguel.  With two of its key conditions still pending (approval by competition authorities and regulatory approval of a key power supply agreement), we believe deal completion is unlikely by year-end.

 

Furthermore, management also indicated that only one unit (437MW) of the greenfield Excellent Energy (EERI) LNG plant, part of the Chromite deal, could be exporting power by Dec. We assume this means only one unit (out of three) could be in the commissioning phase by Dec. The next two units are now supposed to be undergoing the same by Jan and Feb. The entire ~1,300MW EERI facility was supposed to be in commercial operation by Dec 2024, in time for its contractual commitments to Meralco.  We believe further delays cannot be discounted.

 

  • Guidance: core income “north of P43bn”.  Management provided updated guidance for FY24 core income, saying  it will be “north of P43bn”. This appears to be too conservative a guidance to be particularly meaningful.

RETAIL
Wilcon 9M24 net profit falls 22%; behind
  • Wilcon’s 9M24 net profit fell 22% to P2.1bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • 3Q24 earnings declined 33% YoY and 21% QoQ on both lower revenue and margins
  • 3Q24 net sales fell 3% YoY and 4% QoQ
    • SSSG worsened to -8.5%, from -2.0% in 2Q24
    • Transaction count fell 3.8% YoY while basket size dropped 4.8% YoY (vs flat transaction count and a 2% contraction in basket size in 2Q24)
  • GPM fell 90bps QoQ and 80bps YoY (to 38.8%)
    • The company is discounting certain SKUs in order to liquidate old inventory
  • EBIT margin fell 440bps YoY, partly due to higher expansion-related expenses on new stores
    • The company owned three new stores in 3Q24, bringing the total number to 98
  • Management expects weakness to persist due to the oversupply of condominiums in Metro Manila
    • New building permits also remain low
  • WLCON is trading at 18.2X FY24E P/E
