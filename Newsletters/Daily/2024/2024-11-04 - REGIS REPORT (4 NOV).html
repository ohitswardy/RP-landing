We refresh our numbers for Metrobank (MBT PM) following the 9M24 results. After filtering out the noise – from the cross-currency swaps, last year’s asset sales, and retirement accruals – we increase our FY24-26E NPAT forecasts by 3-4%. While there are real NIM pressures, we expect accelerating loan growth, high potential for trading gains, and improving asset quality, as supportive of NPAT and ROE. The bank’s high CET-1 ratio also suggests that the current dividend is sustainable, at least in the near-term. We raise our target price to P100/sh (from P88), representing a total potential return of 38% from current levels. Still a Buy.

Two other banks we don’t officially cover reported their numbers last week. Their results, however, could offer insight on what’s happening on the ground in the banking sector.

  • Union Bank (UBP PM) – reported in a press release that 9M24 NPAT was P8.6bn, up 8% YoY, seeming to be behind consensus of P13.6bn for FY24. Loans were still down 1.5% YoY, and provision expense was still elevated, both of which suggest continued cleanup of the loan book, possibly still at the UnionDigital level. On the positive side, IT expenses were down 17% following the termination of the transition service agreement (TSA) on the Citi assets. More when the FS is released.
  • East West Bank (EW PM) – posted strong 9M24 NPAT of P5.8bn, up 20% YoY, with 3Q24 alone up 50% to P2.3bn. While loan growth slowed (13.3% YoY vs 14.8% in 2Q), NII still grew 23% YoY in 9M24. Profit was boosted by P1.4bn in trading/fx gains in 3Q24 alone. The data in the press release was quite limited; expect the full FS to be out next week.

We have a new report out on Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM), updating our numbers ahead of 3Q interims.

Solaire North, which opened last May, doubled the company’s slot capacity and increased its table capacity by 40%. While its first casino, Solaire Entertainment City, will face challenges near-term, Solaire North should ramp up over the coming quarters, leading to net profit CAGR of 17% from 2024E-2026E (for the entire company).

The stock’s down 22% YTD, underperforming the PCOMP by 35%, after disappointing results.  As a result, BLOOM is now down to just 6.8X FY25E EV/EBITDA and offers 38% total return even to our revised target price of P10/40/share. We think the stock is worth picking up now: BUY.

The chair of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) was reinstated late last week, following the lifting of her suspension by the anti-graft authorities (Ombudsman).  Mona Dimalanta’s reinstatement will hopefully allow the resolution of some important, pending issues.  Among them are the decision on treating Meralco’s (MER PM) Fifth Regulatory Period (5RP) as “lapsed” and the approval of the power supply agreement (PSA) between Meralco and San Miguel’s (SMC PM) EERI.  The latter is a condition precedent to Meralco’s buy-in into SMC’s LNG complex.

BANKS
UBP 9M24 P8.6bn - asset cleanup continues?

In a (very) limited press release earlier this week, Union Bank (UBP PM) reported 9M24 NPAT of P8.6bn, up 8% YoY. The PR was a bit confusing, as it claims that the 3Q24 figure alone was up 76% YoY and 14% QoQ, but our derived figures show very different numbers. UBP reported loans of P523.2bn, which would be down 1.5% YoY, but up 1.6% QoQ. It would appear that there might still be some clean-up going on at the Union Digital level. While provision and asset quality data have not yet been released, the data seems to suggest that credit costs were still quite high in 3Q24. To the upside, it appears that opex is coming down as promised, with total opex down 2% YoY in 9M24, led by a 17% drop in IT expenses with the end of the trasnsition service agreement (TSA) for the purchased Citi assets. As a result, PPOP surged 28% YoY.

The 9M24 profit seems behind consensus of P13.6bn for FY24. That said, once the asset cleanup is done, we could see a sharp drop in credit costs and boost NPAT.

Full FS should be out closer to 15 Nov.

 

BANKS
EW 3Q24 NPAT jumps on trading gains

East West Bank (EW PM) reported in a press release that NPAT for 9M24 grew 20% YoY to P5.8bn, with 3Q24 alone surging 50% YoY due in large part to hefty trading/fx gains of P1.4bn in 3Q24 alone (1H24 was close to zero gains). Outside of these, core operations looked pretty solid, too. Loans grew 13% YoY, slower than the 14.8% posted in 2Q, with consumer loan growth also slowing to 17% YoY (from 22% in 2Q). That said, consumer loans still account for 83% of total loans, extremely high compared to banks we cover. Fee income was also robust, up 23% YoY for 9M24.  

Not much by way of coverage on the stock. But looks like the “consensus” figure of P7bn looks on the low side, due likely to trading/fx gain assumptions. But based on those figures, the stock is currently trading on 0.3x P/bk, 2.2x PER on 2024E and pays a dividend yield of 5.3%.

Full financials won’t be out until 15 Nov.
