REGIS REPORT

04 December 2024

ACEN (ACEN PM) confirmed that is planning to replace SLTEC’s coal-fired 246MW plant with a 1,400MW solar farm (cum battery storage) when the coal plant is retired even earlier than currently planned.

The retirement of the SLTEC coal plant, completed in 2016, was already accelerated to 2040 under the Energy Transition Mechanism (ETM) program of the ADB.  ACEN is now pursuing a plan to shut down SLTEC as early as 2030 and replace it with a solar farm.

Earlier, ACEN managed to offload SLTEC from its balance sheet through an innovative (complex) equity restructuring exercise, but much of the plant’s economics still flow through ACEN’s P&L. ACEN supplies fuel to the plant and sells its output. In fact, SLTEC is the source of more kwh of electricity than any other project in ACEN’s portfolio.  The even earlier retirement of SLTEC and the new solar farm would put an end to that. The question now is: at what price? Will the incentives (transition credits) be sufficient to cover the financial costs?

+++

SP New Energy (SPNEC PM) signed an EPC contract with a Chinese contractor for the latter to build one phase of its gigantic 3,500MW solar farm in Central Luzon.  SPNEC is ~50% owned by Meralco (MER PM).

+++

The national government’s debt crossed P16trn as of Oct, rising 0.8% MoM. The higher debt levels and debt service costs already prompted an upward revision in the government’s official medium-term debt/GDP targets.  With the disappointment in 3Q GDP growth and government infra budgets restrained, there should be significant economic and political incentive for further monetary easing (policy rate cuts) over the next twelve months.

+++

The DMCI (DMC PM) group completed the acquisition of Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) last December 2 at a final equity valuation of US$272mn, lower than the indicative price of US$306mn. The final valuation translates to price of ~P1.2 per CHP share, below the tender offer price of P1.42/share.

MACRO
Government debt rises 0.8% MoM to P16.02tn as of end-October
  • The national government's outstanding debt rose to P16.02tn in end-October, 0.8% (+P127bn) higher than the end-September level of P15.89tn
    • Primarily driven by valuation impact of peso depreciation against the USD from P56.01/USD at end-Sept to P58.19/USD end-Oct
  • Domestic debt declined slightly by 0.4% (-P46bn) MoM to P10.89tn due to the net redemption of government securities
  • External debt rose 3.5% (+P173bn) MoM to P5.13tn due to net foreign loan availments of P20.5bn as well as foreign exchange movements
  • The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.6% by year-end and is seeking to reduce the ratio further to 56.3% by 2028
    • As of end-Sep 2024, the government's debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 61.3% vs. 60.9% end-Jun 2024

       

CONGLOS
DMCI group completes acquisition of Cemex Philippines
  • The DMCI group completed the acquisition of Cemex Phils (CHP PM), after acquiring 100% of CHP’s parent, Cemex Asian South East Corp. (CASEC) for an equity value of US$272mn
    • The purchase price was lower than the initial purchase price of US$306mn (P1.42/sh) after adjusting for debt, cash, and working capital at the completion date on Dec 2
    • Note that those who participated in the CHP tender offer received P1.42/sh; Tender offer documents indicated that the bidder will not claim a refund if there’s a downward adjustment in the final price
    • It is unclear at this stage if the initial Enterprise Value guidance of US$660mn has also changed
  • At the financial close, DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) secured a 51% effective stake in CHP, while Semirara (SCC PM) and Dacon Corporation accounted for 10% and approximately 29%, respectively.
  • DMC Board Advisor, Executive Vice President and Chief Finance Officer, Herbert M. Consunji, has been appointed as President and Chief Executive Officer of CHP
    • He will lead turnaround efforts to further streamline operations and unlock synergies
    • Company plans to enhance CHP’s logistics network, optimize product mix, manage production and operating costs, and leverage on potential operating synergies within the DMCI ecosystem
  • As of end-September, CHP's gross debt stood at P21.1bn while cash stood at P1.1bn
    • CHP incurred an EBITDA loss of P316mn and net loss of P2.87bn as of 9M24
    • We estimate CHP’s cement-equivalent clinker capacity is at 5.0mn MT. This will rise to 6.5mn MT after it completes its 1.5mn MT expansion by early 2025
POWER
ACEN plans to replace 246MW coal plant with US$1.5bn solar facility by 2030
  • ACEN is looking to build a mid-merit 1,400-megawatt peak (MWp) solar plant + 1,600 megawatt-hour (MWh) battery energy storage system to replace the 246MW SLTEC coal plant
    • Company expects to shut down the plant by 2030, ten years earlier than scheduled
  • Solar facility is expected to cost US$1.5bn
    • It expects to fund the project through revenues generated from selling transition credits and additional funds from its partnership with Temasek and Singapore’s Keppel Ltd.
    • Plans are still subject to final Board and relevant regulatory approvals
POWER
SPNEC signs EPC contract with Power China for a portion of 3,500MW Terra Solar
  • SPNEC signed an engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contract with Power Construction Corp. of China and its affiliates (Power China) to build the east section of its 3,500 MW Terra Solar project
    • The East section will cover 1,050MW encompassing roughly 1505 hectares in Nueva Ecija
    • Power China will be handling the procurement, design, permitting, manufacturing, logistics, and long-term operational protocols for that specified section
    • There has been no further disclosure as to whether Power China will continue to develop the rest of Terra Solar or if Meralco will contract with another firm.
  • Power China, is a global leader in energy infrastructure, and has been active in the Philippines, building coal-fired power plant and transmission projects
    • Some of its completed projects in the Philippines include the 4x135MW Kauswagan and 2x660MW Dinginin (Mariveles) coal fired plants
    • They have also constructed the transmission line for the NGCP’s Mindanao-Visayas Interconnection Project (MVIP)
  • Terra Solar is set to be commercially operational by 2027, with 2500MW expected to be operational by Feb 2026
    • The 3,500 MW solar + 4500MWh BESS facility is estimated to cost P200bn
    • Terra Solar is 60%-owned by SPNEC; SPNEC is 50.5%-owned by Meralco (MER PM), through its MGen stake of 47.3% and Metro Pacific stake of 3.2%
