REGIS REPORT

14 May 2024

More result this morning with consumer firms giving us a glimpse of their 1Q performance ahead of their results briefings.  One must not forget that there are a couple of “extraordinary” factors at play this 1Q. First is the early Holy Week (Easter Week), which came in March. Second is the fact that 2024 is a leap year, which means there’s one extra day in the quarter.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (=) – Headline profit for JFC came in at P2.6bn for 1Q24, +27% higher YoY.  After distributions to perps and prefs,  we estimate net income to common shares was P2.3bn (+32%). Consolidated group-wide SSSG was +5.5%, with PH stores lifting the average. JFC will hold its briefing tomorrow.

 

  • LT Group (LTG PM) (+) – Reported profit of P6.4bn for 1Q24 looks ahead of Street expectations but underlying operations don’t look so rosy.  PNB profits rose 10% on lower loan loss provisions but the volumes from the important “vice” businesses (tobacco and liquor) are down double digits. There is good news, though, as PNB is finally close to distributing the property dividend it declared three years ago. The property dividend relates to the bank’s ownership in three prime real estate assets.

 

  • Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) (=) – Earnings for 1Q rose 21% to P639m as revenues expanded 19% and SSS rose 8.8%.  Operating profit was up only 9% but we understand there were timing issues relating to promotional subsidies. SEVN ended 1Q24 with 1,829 stores.

 

  • Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) (=) – Bloomberry issued a press release this morning, saying 1Q24 EBITDA was P4.9bn (-14%) and profit was P2.6bn (-11%). Note 1Q23 presented a relatively high base of comparison due to high volumes and win rates (and thus GGR).  The EBITDA figure may appear behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, especially if one expects EBITDA losses at Solaire North later this year. The slot machines registered record drops, but total gross gaming revenue fell 8% YoY due to declines in VIP and mass table volumes.

 

+++

 

ICTSI (ICT PM) revealed plans to build a brand-new container terminal at its Batangas facility. We reckon this plan is meant to address the long-term constraints of the Port of Manila. The investment for this new facility should be modest initially, with room to scale up if demand grows.

 

+++

 

Megaworld (MEG PM) announced it will be injecting 157k sqm of leasable space into MREIT (MREIT PM) in exchange for shares.  MREIT’s share count will increase by 33%, as a result and MEG’s ownership in MREIT will rise to 66% from 54%.

CONSUMER
JFC 1Q24 net profit to common +32% YoY to P2.3bn – in line
  • In a press release, JFC reported headline net profit of P2.6bn, up 27% YoY. Excluding perp coupons and pref divs, we estimate net income to common was P2.3bn, up 32% YoY, trending broadly in line with expectations.
  • Revenues rose 11% YoY to P61.3bn (in line) on the back of 5.5% consolidated SSSG. SSSG by geography:
    • Philippines +6.9%
    • China -3.1% - coming from a high base last year, coupled with weak consumer spending
    • CBTL +2%
    • Smashburger -4.1%
  • Operating profit grew 14% YoY to P4.1bn (in line) as margins mildly improved on better gross margins
  • The company maintains its 2024 guidance:
    • Consolidated SSSG of 5-7% (Regis FY24E: +4.8%)
    • Store network increase of 7-8% (Regis: +4.5%)
    • Operating profit growth of 10-15% (Regis: +12%)
  • We expect to get further detail during the company’s briefing on May 15
  • JFC currently trades at 22x 2024E PE

GAMING
Bloomberry 1Q24 EBITDA falls 14% YoY
  • Bloomberrry’s 1Q24 GGR fell 8% YoY (up 7% QoQ) to P14.8bn
  • The unusually high win rates were not enough to offset the 36% YoY decline in VIP rolling chip and 8% YoY reduction in mass table drop
  • The decline in total GGR was partly cushioned by slot GGR, which hit a record level of P5.9bn (+24% YoY, +10% QoQ)
    • Slot handle grew 17% YoY (+7% QoQ) to P98bn, also a record level
  • EBITDA fell 14% YoY to P4.9bn
  • Net profit fell 11% YoY to P2.6bn
    • Excluding the impact of P280mn in pre-operating expenses, EBITDA would have falllen 10% YoY (to P5.2bn) and net profit would have fallen 3% YoY (to P2.9bn)
  • The company’s second site, Solaire Resorts North, will open later this month
  • The company will hold its briefing at 4PM today

Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) 1Q24 net profit +21% YoY to P639mn – in line
  • SEVN reported 1Q24 net profit of P639mn, up 21% YoY. This is tracking broadly within Regis expectations.
  • Revenues +19% YoY to P20.6bn on 8.8% SSSG. SEVN ended the period with 3,829 stores, 11% higher vs 1Q23
  • Operating profit rose 9% YoY as margins dipped 40bps YoY
    • Gross margin contracted 120bps YoY due to the timing effect of the collection of other income and price subsidies from cigarette manufacturers. This is expected to recover in succeeding quarters
    • The GPM contraction was cushioned by higher service income as a % of sales (+20bps YoY) and operating leverage
  • SEVN currently trades at 22x 2024E PE

CONGLOMERATES
LTG 1Q24 results comment
  • Profits up slightly in 1Q24.  LTG reported net profit of P6.42bn for 1Q24, barely up (<1%) from the prior year.  Contributions from the bank rose but the tobacco segment continued to shrink. While the headline profit looks better than Street estimates, the business outlook is looking challenging, especially for tobacco.  However, the long-awaited distribution of PNB’s property assets could finally boost LTG’s underlying value.

 

  • Tobacco segment remains under pressure. Profits for the tobacco business dropped 13% to P2.66bn. Tobacco volumes dropped 18%, worse than the (legal) industry’s 11%, as PMFTC continued to lose ground.  PMFTC’s market share is now down to 53% vs 55% last year.  This situation is compounded by the continuing rise of illicit tobacco.

 

  • PNB income up 10% on lower provisions.  PNB’s income was up 10% to P5.31bn, despite lower PPOP. Loan growth was 4% up and net interest income rose 12%, but fee income and other income  were both lower. Provision expenses were reduced by P1bn to P620m.

 

  • Liquor volumes contract. Tanduay’s liquor volumes fell 13% and revenue dropped 5% due to price increases. Profits, however, managed to stay almost unchanged (-1%). The company claims it managed to increase market despite the fall in volumes. Based on estimates from third party surveys, market share of Tanduay was 31.6% in 1Q24, up from 29.1%.

 

  • Property dividend finally?  Almost  exactly three years ago, PNB announced the distribution of 51% of its 100% ownership of PNB Holdings (PHC) to its shareholders as a property dividend.  The bank says it has finally received the eCARs relating to the property dividend covering shareholders owning 97% of its shares. The distribution of the PHC shares (as property dividends) would begin in the next few months.

 

LTG, being owner of 56.5% of PNB, would receive 28.8% (=56.5% x 51%) of PHC as a dividend. Assuming PHC is worth P35bn (our estimate), the firm would receive ~P10bn worth of PHC shares.

TRANSPORT
ICTSI (ICT PM) Batangas greenfield expansion
  • Greenfield expansion in Batangas. ICTSI announced it is investing a total of US$800mn to convert its wholly-owned Bauan International Port, which largely services Ro-Ro vessels today, into an international container port, with a fully built-out capacity of over 2mn TEUs.  The investment will be in phases, with the construction of Phase 1 slated to start in 1Q25 and will be fully completed with a capacity of 800-900k TEUs by end-2027.  Meanwhile, investments for Phase 2 will be volume dependent. 

 

  • New port will likely address capacity limits of Manila ports. Batangas is currently only being serviced by one gateway port, Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) Batangas Container Terminal (BCT), which is as an alternative port for shippers based in the Calabarzon (Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal, and Quezon). Once built, ICT’s port in Bauan will directly compete with ATI’s port. 

 

We believe, however, that the key rationale for this investment is to address the long-term capacity limitations at the ports of Manila. Combined throughput volumes at the three foreign container ports servicing Metro Manila and southern Luzon reached 4.3mn TEUs in 2023 while combined capacities stood at 5.2mn TEUs. We anticipate overall capacity at these ports can only grow up to 5.7mn TEUs by 2027 largely coming from ICT’s ongoing Berth 8 expansion.  With incremental demand at these ports projected to grow by c. 200k-250k TEUs annually, we anticipate capacity bottlenecks at the three ports by 2028/2029 with ICT’s Bauan port likely to get future incremental volumes.  This assumes, however, that ATI will not expand the Batangas port and other announced, possible competing ports will not push through (i.e. Bataan port of India-based Adani Ports).

 

  • Similar yields as MICT, without port fees. Management envisions the Bauan port will have similar yields as MICT (>US$200 per TEU). Even though this guidance looks high relative to the tariffs of BCT, we believe such yields can only be achieved if capacity at existing ports will not be sufficient to meet demand.  Moreover, given that the port will be privately-owned and will not require a license from the government, ICT’s Bauan port will also not be paying any port fees. 

 

  • Funding for phase 1 is manageable. Management plans to spend US$300mn over the first three years for the construction of Phase 1.  ICT’s gross debt (inclusive of perps and lease/concession liabilities) amounted to US$5.3bn while net debt stood at US$4.2bn in end-March 2024. Even if the entire additional US$300mn of borrowings are assumed, we estimate its net debt-to-EBITDA will rise to just 2.9x from 2.7x in end-March.

REITS
MREIT to acquire 157k sqm of MEG offices via property-for-share swap
  • MREIT will acquire six Megaworld office buildings via a property-for-share swap
  • MREIT will issue 926.16mn new shares to Megaworld at P14.20/share
    • Total transaction value of P13.15bn
    • This will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 33%
  • Megaworld’s stake in MREIT will rise from 54.2% to 65.6%
  • The buildings have a combined leasable area of 156,600sqm and have an aggregate occupancy rate of 94%
    • McKinley West, Fort Bonifacio – 94,300sqm
    • Iloilo Business Park, Iloilo City– 36,200sqm
    • Davao Park District, Davao City – 26,100sqm
  • The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 48% to 480,000sqm
  • The transaction was done at a cap rate of 7.4%
    • We estimate that the acquisition will be mildly DPS accretive
  • MEG is trading at 72% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, 3.3X FY24E P/E
  • MREIT currently offers 7.9% FY24E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The current 10-year government bond yield is 6.9%
