REGIS REPORT

27 August 2024

We have a new report out on the consumer food manufacturers from Carissa (carissa.mangubat@regis.ph) over the weekend.

We are bullish on the sector and our optimism is fueled by our outlook on consumer confidence (and spending) as inflation takes a more convincing downward turn over the few weeks. Beyond just the arithmetical drop in CPI inflation beginning this month, however, there is the predicted fall in food prices, led chiefly by rice. Consumers care little about a cosmetic decline in CPI inflation due to base effects, but an actual drop in the price of rice at the neighborhood store would have a positive real-world impact.

A meaningful recovery by 4Q can be expected, and thus share price outperformance. We are reiterating our BUY ratings on Century Pacific (CNPF PM), Monde (MONDE PM) and Universal Robina (URC PM).

In related news, some local rice retailers say the Vietnam rice exporters are reneging on contracts to seek higher prices.  Data from Bloomberg, though, is showing Vietnam export prices continuing to fall. At the latest data point (FOB), prices are down >20% YTD.  This is exclusive of the impact of a 20pt cut in rice import tariffs.

While it does appear that issues in the importation process are delaying it, we reckon a price drop for rice is inevitable. Rice is too high profile a commodity and the administration has invested much capital in this matter for it not to act decisively to address what we can only euphemistically described as “bottlenecks”.

MACRO
Rice importers blame Vietnamese exporters for elevated rice prices
  • A group called Philippine Rice Industry Stakeholders Movement (PRISM) said that contracts signed by local importers are being canceled by Vietnamese suppliers, who are seeking higher prices
    • Cited that Vietnamese rice export prices have steadily increased in recent months due to higher demand in neighboring countries like Philippines and Indonesia
    • PRISM co-founder Orly Manuntag claims that rice is now being priced at US$680 per MT from US$600 per MT previously
    • PRISM blames the slowdown in imports due to contract cancellations of Vietnamese exporters
  • As a result, rice importers claim that they are having a hard time bringing down local rice prices to its estimate of P42-46/kg, following the reduction on rice tariffs to 15% from 30% previously last July 5
    • As of the week of Aug 17, retail prices of imported well-milled rice in NCR stood at P52.97/kg (down slightly from P53.45 from the week of July 1-6)
  • However, according to Bloomberg Finance LP, prices of Vietnamese rice exports have fallen to US$523 per MT, down from ~US$550 on June 30 and ~US$660 at the start of 2024\

MACRO
Trust AUMs up 13% YoY to new record high P6.6trn
  • Trust AUMs of banks and non-banks regulated by the BSP grew 13% YoY and 3% QoQ to P6.61trn in 2Q24 (about $117bn), new all-time high. Bank AUMs grew 1% YoY to P4.1trn, while non-banks surged 39% YoY to P2.5trn. This discrepancy is partly due to RCBC (RCB PM), which spun off its trust department into a “stand alone trust” in 1Q24, thus classifying the AUM from bank to non-bank. BPI did the same thing in 2017.

 

  • Among banks we cover/follow, the big gainer seemed to be Philippine National Bank, whose AUMs grew 4.5% QoQ to P178.8bn, and Union Bank grew 3.4% QoQ. On the other end, Security Bank (SECB PM) lost 4.6% of AUMs QoQ to P103.8bn. Among the Big Three, BPI gained 2.5% QoQ to P,1.384bn, while BDO added 1.4% to P2,025.6bn. Metrobank AUMs, meanwhile, shed 1.1% to P497.7bn.

 

  • BDO remains by far the biggest at P2,026bn (close to $36bn), for a 30.6% market share, with BPI at 20.4%, and Metrobank at a far third at 7.5%.

 

  • As we’ve said in the past, local Trusts could prove a major driving force for the Philippine equities market. Anecdotally , only 10-12% of AUMs are in local equities, down from a peak of close to 25%. Falling interest rates could see a reallocation of funds away from fixed-income and into the PSE.

MACRO
Zero tariffs drive demand for EVs, but sales are expected to be less than 10% of total vehicle sales this year
  • Auto industry group, CAMPI, said the expansion of Executive Order (EO) No. 12 to include hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) and plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) in the tariff exemption will drive demand for EVs 
    • Full EVs, HEVs and PHEVs can enjoy zero tariffs until 2028
    • As of 1H24, sales of electricfied vehicles (i.e. full EVs, HEVs, and PHEVs) accounted for 4% of total new car sales
  • However, CAMPI said that sales of electrified vehicles are unlikely to reach 10% of new car sales this year, as demand for ICEs is also still increasing
POWER
Meralco signs agreement with Vena Energy
  • Meralco’s wholly owned subsidiary, MGen Renewable Energy, signed an agreement with Singapore-based Vena Energy for the development of the 450MW alternating current (MWac) Bugallon solar power project in Pangasinan
  • The project will start construction in 3Q24 and will begin commercial operations in 4Q25
  • Based on a recent filing with the DENR’s Environmental Management Bureau, the project will have a capacity of 650.1 MWpeak or 530.4 MWac and will cost P31.9bn
  • The major components of the project are: solar PV panels, battery energy storage system, HV transformer, buildings, and other structures
