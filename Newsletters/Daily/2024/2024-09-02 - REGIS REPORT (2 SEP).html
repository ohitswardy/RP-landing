REGIS REPORT

02 September 2024

Loan growth for universal/commercial banks (UKBs) sped up slightly to 10.4% YoY in July, compared to 10.1% in June. Oddly, all major categories showed a MoM slowdown (and/or even MoM decline). However, there was one item (Professional, Scientific and Technical Services) that went from P45bn in June to P218bn in July. The segment typically accounts for 0.3-0.4% of total loans; it seem unusual that a segment like this could absorb a full P175bn (more than $3bn) in new loans. It could either be a) a mis-classification of new loans, or b) a reclassification by the BSP of other loans into this account. Consumer loans (ex-mortgages) stayed buoyant, up 24.3% YoY (vs 25.0% in June), with credit cards still up 28%, auto loans up 20%, and salary loans up 17%.

SM Prime (SMPH PM) is expecting to complete all 360 hectares of its Pasay City reclamation project by next year (2025).  This will put it on track with its plans to begin pre-selling land there by 2026.  Development of the land should commence after reclamation is done.  The Pasay reclamation accounts for 27% (=P11.5/share) of SMPH’s NAV on our estimates.

+++

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) expects CPI inflation for Aug to between 3.2% to 4.0%, a decline from the July figure of 4.4%.  The drop will largely be a result of the higher CPI base in Aug 2023, which was when rice prices first surged.

Frankly, we were expecting Aug CPI inflation to be even lower than that because of the rice tariff cut. Recent channel checks and media reports suggest that rice prices are not falling as quickly as we were anticipating.  Thus, the CPI decline is also unlikely to be as sharp as we thought it would be.

BANKS
Commercial bank loans expand by 10.4% in July; M3 growth accelerates to 7.4%
  • Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 10.4% YoY in July, mildly faster than the 10.1% pace in June
    • Largely due to big rebounds in professional/scientific sectors and sustained growth in other key sectors
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose by 7.2% in July from 6.6% in June and the fastest pace since Oct 2023
