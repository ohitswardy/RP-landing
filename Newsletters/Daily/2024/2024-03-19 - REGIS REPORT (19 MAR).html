REGIS REPORT

19 March 2024

Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) disclosed what’s been going around the media the past few days: it has reached a settlement with its former management team under GGAM.  The settlement will require BLOOM, through its subsidiary, to buy 921.2m BLOOM shares from GGAM at P18.32/share.  Effectively, the settlement amounts to US$300m, which approximates the amount awarded to GGAM by a Singapore arbitration panel in 2019.

The 921.2m shares represent the ownership of GGAM that has been “trapped” by litigation for a decade and are equivalent to 8.0% of BLOOM’s outstanding shares.

The 8.0% owned by GGAM is presently not considered part of “public float” by the PSE, so a buyback will not reduce the float shares of BLOOM, even if shares outstanding are reduced.

Finally, at P18.32/share, the shares will be purchased by BLOOM at a 59% premium to last close.  Using the last price as the benchmark for “fair” value, the negative impact on BLOOM’s value would be P6.3bn (~US$110m). Spread over 10.5bn shares outstanding (post-buyback), that is equivalent to ~P0.60/share.

Some more results today:

  • Converge (CNVRG PM) (=) – Reported net profit was P9.1bn, up 22%.  While this appears above Street estimates, we note profits (reported EBITDA) were padded by a few chunky adjustments.  Underlying operating results were not as good as headlines suggest.  One positive here was the net adds figure of 80k for 4Q23.  More below.
  • LT Group (LTG PM) (-) – LTG income for FY23 was practically unchanged (+1%) at P25bn (below expectations).  The tobacco unit is seeing tremendous pressure from both its legitimate competitors as well illicit trade (now at ~37-38% of the market), resulting in a 26% drop in volumes. While PNB profits are recovering nicely, LTG’s dividends are dependent on cash flow from tobacco. More below.

MACRO
House passes CREATE MORE legislation on final reading
  • The Lower House approved House Bill No. 9794 or CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy)
  • Proposed amendments include:
    • Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%
    • Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports
    • Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board
    • Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises
    • IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements
TELCOS
Converge ICT profits at P9.1bn (+22%) for FY23
  • Provision reversals and “Other income” mask soft 4Q.  Converge reported net income of P9.1bn for FY23, +22% YoY.  While this appears higher than Street estimates, it is so only because of (1) a chunky reversal of provisions in 4Q resulting from a reevaluation of provisioning policies and (2) a jump in other income (P222m) which management said was from associates.  Without those, bottom line results would have likely been below consensus expectations.

Operating revenue for 4Q alone was actually down 1% YoY, due to the 4% drop in revenue from residential customers. Enterprise revenue (14% of consolidated revenue) was a bright spot, growing 16% in 4Q.  

  • Net adds of 80k in 4Q.  Arguably a silver lining in the results was CNVRG’s announcement that it added 80k subscribers (net) in 4Q, of which 47k were postpaid. These are, frankly, good numbers, in the context of the numbers previously reported by PLDT and Globe. However , we need to be convinced that these numbers will stick and will translate to sustainable revenue.

 

  • Capex deferred to 2024, no dividends this year.  Capex spending for 2023 amounted to P9.0bn, well below previous guidance (P12 to 15bn) due to project delays.  The delays mean capex this year (2024) will be higher by around P2bn (P17-19bn).  Some amount will also be delayed further into 2025.  Management said any possible dividend will be pushed into 2025 given the elevated capex this year.
  • Guidance for FY24.  For FY24, management is guiding for 7-8% growth in revenue with EBITDA margins of 58-59% and ROIC of 15-16%. The guidance figures imply 2-5% growth in FY24 EBITDA.

 

CONGLOS
LT Group FY23 income up 1% to P25bn
  • LT Group (LTG PM) reported net income of P25.4bn for FY23
    • Lower than Street estimates (P30bn)
  • Profits from tobacco were P11.38nbn, down 26%
    • 4Q contributions were P2.35bn, down 29%
    • For the year, volumes declined 26%
    • Illicit trade is still rising, with the estimated share now at ~37% in FY23
    • Even within legitimate market, market share continues to fall (55.4% in FY23 vs. 60.3% in FY22)
  • Banking profits rose 63%
    • PNB profits rose to P17.97bn (+55%)
    • Income growth driven by net interest margin expansion and reduced provisions
    • Loan growth was 5% and gross NPL ratio was 6.26% (coverage of 88.5%)
  • Contributions from Tanduay were up modestly (7%) while Asia Brewery’s was virtually unchanged (-1%)
INFRASTRUCTURE
Government, San Miguel consortium sign NAIA PPP contract
  • The consortium New NAIA Infrastructure Corp. has been given a 15-year concession that can be extended for another 10 years
    • NAIA Infrastructure Corp. is composed of San Miguel Holdings Corp. (SMC PM); RMM Asian Logistics, Inc.; RLW Aviation Development, Inc.; and Incheon International Airport Corp.
    • The government will rake in as much as P900bn from the privatization, comprising P30bn upfront, P2bn in annuity cost and 82% revenue share
  • NAIA Infrastructure Corp. said that they have already secured funds for the project, which will partly involve building a new terminal with a total capacity of 35mn pax per year (vs. current capacity of all four terminals at c. 32mn)
    • New terminal will be built on the site of the Philippine Village Hotel within the Nayong Pilipino complex and will clear up 30% of space at the existing terminals
    • Proposed building will be constructed within three years after the approval of the Transport Department
    • Over a 25-year period, the New NAIA Infrastructure Corp. will spend as much as P122.3bn in the rehabilitation and upgrade of the airport
  • NAIA Infrastructure will take over the operations of the airport by September
POWER
First Gen eyes 400MW of hydropower capacity by 2028
  • First Gen Corp. said it is expecting a capacity of around 400MW from hydropower facilities in Nueva Ecija by 2028, and these are as follows
    • 132MW Pantabangan-Masiway hydroelectric power plant (HEPP)
    • 165MW Casecnan HEPP
    • Planned 100-120MW Aya pumped-storage hydropower project
POWER
Alternergy awards contract for Alabat, Tanay wind projects to China firm
  • Alternergy Holdings Corp. announced that it has awarded Guangdong Electric Power Design Institute Co. Ltd. (GEDI) the contract for its Alabat and Tanay wind power projects with a combined 163MW of capacity
  • Company has handed over the balance of plant (BOP) and engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contract to GEDI and its Philippine subsidiary GEDI Construction Development Corp.
  • GEDI has presence in more than 50 countries with over 700 EPC projects worldwide
