REGIS REPORT

13 March 2024

The reported P7.4bn profit of ACEN (ACEN PM) was well below expectations. Income for 4Q23 alone, which was devoid of any non-operational gains (one-off items), came in P830m. This is up 2.5x from the previous year but looks soft compared to the earlier periods of 2023.

While the operating numbers are disappointing, partly because of delays on new projects, what’s arguably most crucial is whether ACEN can execute its project pipeline (and also operate them) according to plan. The company has attributable capacity of 2,608MW at the start of the year. If things go according to the latest plan, ACEN will add another ~1,700MW by year’s end, an increment of over 65%.

MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to US$4.2bn in January
  • Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit stood at US$4.22bn in January, down 24% YoY and flat MoM
  • Imports fell 8% YoY to US$10.2bn in January on lower commodity prices
    • Imports of fuel fell 35% YoY while raw materials dropped 5% YoY
    • Consumer goods imports jumped 16% but capital goods imports still posted a decline of 6% YoY
  • Exports rose 9% YoY due to base effects (Jan-Apr 2023 were the weakest months for exports in 2023)
    • Electronics exports rose 16% YoY
    • Due to a strong recovery in exports to the USA (+23%) and HK (+79%)

 

POWER
ACEN FY23 profit of P7.4bn below expectations
  • ACEN reported net income of P7.4bn for FY23, down 43% YoY and below estimates.  This is well below both Regis and Street estimates.  The profit decline from P13.1bn was due to the large one-off (P8.9bn) recorded the prior year relating to the partial sale of Indonesian geothermal assets and the accompanying remeasurement gain. Nonetheless, the underlying results for FY23 do appear weak relative to expectations.  Derived 4Q23 income was only P830m.  While this was up more than 2x on a recurring basis from the prior year, it looks poor in comparison to 9M23.
  • Attributable capacity now at 2,608MW, with another 2,245MW on the way.  ACEN claims attributable capacity of over 4,800MW, of which 2,608MW is already operational.  Given the frequent experience of delays, we are rather hesitant to count the chickens that have yet to hatch.  In addition, employing the 4,800MW figure to calculate EV/capacity ratios without an accurate projection of debt to complete the projects in the future would lead to misleading conclusions. Management does claim, however, that ~1,700MW of the new capacity will be fully online within this year.
  • ACEN has a professed target to have 5,000MW of attributable capacity (both under construction and operational) by 2025.  This aim should not be hard to beat given where they are today.

 

POWER
ACEN strikes deal with Australian firm for clean electricity
  • ACEN Corp.'s wholly-owned subsidiary in Australia has signed an eight-year power purchase agreement (PPA) with SmartestEnergy Australia
    • Offtake will be 25% output of ACEN Australia's 400MW New England Solar project, which is currently still in development and is planned for 720MW of total capacity
  • SmartestEnergy Australia said the renewable energy purchased from ACEN will be made available to commercial and industrial customers throughout Australia
MINING
SEC clears OceanaGold (Philippines) IPO
  • The SEC has cleared the P7.88bn IPO of OceanaGold (Philippines) Inc.
    • Up to 456mn common shares owned by selling shareholder OceanaGold (Philippines) Holdings Inc. will be offered to the public at a price of up to P17.28 per share
  • OceanaGold is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Canada-listed OceanaGold Corp.
    • Company operates the Didipio gold and copper mine in the Nueva Vizcaya province
    • Mine has reserves of 1.1mn ounces of gold and 140k MT of copper as of end-2023 (estimated mine life of 10 years)
    • The ongoing underground “optimization work” at the mine has the potential to increase production of larger volumes of higher-grade ore and an increase in the mine life
  • IPO is targeted to run from April 22 to 26, with listing on the PSE scheduled for May 7
TELCOS
SC backs CA ruling on PLDT worker regularization
  • The Supreme Court (SC) upheld a decision of the Court of Appeals (CA), which had found that the Labor department acted improperly in ordering the regularization of PLDT Inc. support staff
    • CA ruled that regularizing contractual workers should be the discretion of management (i.e. janitorial, clerical, information technology, back-office support, call center, sales, and other such services)
  • Former Labor Secretary Silvestre H. Bello III had ordered PLDT to grant regular status to 7,344 workers
    • The SC affirmed the CA finding that Mr. Bello had committed grave abuse of discretion in issuing his resolutions
    • The SC held that labor contracting is not illegal per se, as management enjoys some leeway in deciding which functions can be performed by regular workers
