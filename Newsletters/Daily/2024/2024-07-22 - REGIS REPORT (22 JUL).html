REGIS REPORT

22 July 2024

President Marcos will be delivering his third annual State of the Nation Address (SONA) today. 

While there are expected to be the usual self-congratulatory elements in the speech, we are more interested in the likely issues that Marcos may raise.  We reckon the single most important issue for most citizens will be the cost of living (i.e. inflation). The online gambling sector (a.k.a. “POGOs”) and any possible changes to the government’s policy on it would also be a key topic. The administration’s stance on constitutional amendments (“Cha-Cha” or charter change) could also be revealed.

+++

Ahead of the SONA, Marcos also signed into law two pieces of legislation.  These are (1) the New Government Procurement Act (NGPA) and (2) the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA). The NGPA is supposed to streamline government procurement processes and do away with the mandate to always buy from the supplier with the lowest price, regardless of other aspects (suitability, longevity, and quality).  The AFASA, on the other hand, is supposed to help combat rising incidence of financial fraud. It criminalizes acting as “money mules” and also social engineering schemes while granting the Bangko Sentral (BSP) more powers to probe financial accounts suspected of involvement in fraud.

+++

Sky Cable signed an agreement with Converge (CNVRG PM) that will allow the former to use some of the latter’s fiber network assets.  In theory, the agreement can allow Sky to provide better broadband services with it having to spend on capex.  For Converge, the deal can generate incremental revenue on some of its under-utilized assets of its network.

+++

Cebu Air (CEB PM) is reportedly in talks to acquire AirSwift, the niche carrier owned by Ayala Land (ALI PM).  AirSwift operates a handful of routes to tourist destinations.

MACRO
Marcos OKs new laws to enhance procurement, fight online scams
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed into law the New Government Procurement Act (NGPA) and the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA)
  • The NGPA law updates the Government Procurement Reform Act (RA No. 1984), streamlining the procurement process from 3 months to just 60 days by standardizing procurement forms and institutionalizing electronic procurement
    • The NGPA features  11 new procurement modalities "that will afford greater flexibility for government agencies in choosing the manner of acquiring the best goods and services”
    • The NGPA also ensures best prices for Filipinos through the “Most Economically Advantageous Responsive Bid”
  • The AFASA aims to combat the rising tide of online financial scams
    • The law mandates financial institutions to implement stronger safeguards to protect Filipino financial accounts
    • The AFASA also criminalizes money muling and social engineering schemes, and grants the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) authority to probe financial accounts involved in illegal activities
  • Data from the Philippine National Police recorded 19,000 cybercrimes in 2023 (68.98% increase YoY)
    • Majority of these cases were online scams, totaling 14,030 incidents (an increase of 7,208 cases or 94.64% YoY)

POWER
Lawmakers back bill on natural gas development
  • Lawmakers have vowed to support the Philippine Downstream Natural Gas Industry Development Act
  • Sen. Pia Cayetano stressed that the government has tagged natural gas, including those coming from the Malampaya field off Palawan province, as a transition fuel to full renewable energy
    • Said that the government must have policies that will support more exploration of natural gas
  • The Malampaya deepwater gas-to-power project ran by Filipinos is the country’s first and only indigenous gas resource supplying 20% of Luzon’s electricity needs
    • Such project has been significantly contributing to the nation’s energy independence since 2001
  • Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian backed Prime Energy’s (Malampaya’s operator) commitment to further explore Philippine natural gas resources
TELCOS
Converge to partner with Sky Cable
  • A partnership between Converge and Sky Cable was announced today, July 22
    • A “commercial arrangement” that will enable Sky to upgrade its network and improve its services
  • The terms and nature of the “agreement” are vague, but we understand that Sky will pay Converge for the use of some of its assets
  • In theory, use of the fiber assets of Converge will allow Sky to become more competitive, without necessitating the rollout of its own network
    • From the perspective of Converge, the deal should help generate incremental revenue from under-utilized assets
  • Sky Cable’s net loss stands at P653mn as of 2023 and it currently owes P4.53bn to its lenders
  • Sky Cable has also lost its right to offer direct-to-home services when ABS-CBN failed to land a fresh franchise in 2020
  • Prior to this, PLDT Inc. was supposed to buy out Sky Cable for P6.75bn, but the deal was called off in February
  • An earlier report by InsiderPH said the agreement would allow Sky Cable to ride on Converge’s fiber network, converting it from cable TV to digital TV
    • The report also included that CNVRG will extend P2bn to Sky Cable to repay some of its liabilities
