REGIS REPORT

11 June 2024

Good morning! A bunch of bank news today – a new sector report, and fresh BSP data on consumer loans.

We reiterate our bullish stance on the sector, as the Big Three banks try to hang on to decade/s-high ROEs. 1Q24 results were strong, as loans accelerated across our coverage to 11-13%. Consumer growth remained robust. But the surprise was corporate loan demand which jumped for all banks we cover. Latest central bank data shows loans again accelerated in April, to a five-year high above 11%. To help boost ROEs, the Big Three banks are running down their NPL cover (130-180% in 1Q). Meanwhile, SECB has completed its credit card portfolio cleanup, and should see provisions fall. The Big Three have also increased dividend payments to improve capital efficiency. Metrobank (MBT PM) was the standout with its special div payment that took div yield to close to 7.5%.

The big risk is competition in loan and deposit pricing, which is tempering NIMs this early ie even before rate cuts. The risk is asset quality. While NPLs have been steady, Past Due rates have been creeping up this year, and are at 2-year highs.

BDO (BDO PM) is our top pick. The stock has been sold down, largely due to its status as “market proxy”, and now presents 35% upside to our TP. Despite MBT gaining 35% ytd (plus 7% div), the sock is still only on 0.8x book on our 2024 estimates. Security Bank (SECB PM) has been shunned for years and is now in “deep value” territory on 4x PER and 0.3x book. It looks like profits have turned a corner. Given the stock’s size/liquidity, though, it may need to show multi-quarter improvement before it attracts investor attention.

Meanwhile. the BSP finally released 1Q24 data on consumer loans, which jumped to 17.6% YoY (from 17.0% end-2023. NPLs were benign, edging up 3bps QoQ to 5.80%. The good news is that both mortgages (+7.9%) and auto loans (+17.6%) saw accelerating demand, and NPL ratios for both were stable to down. The bad news is that NPLs for credit cards and salary loans were creeping up. GT Capital (GTCAP PM) would be the best way to the theme, both through MBT and its exposure to Toyota Motor Phils.

Meanwhile, the IMF cut its GDP forecasts for the Philippines. Oddly, their new estimates mirror ours, with GDP growth of 6.0% for 2024E and 6.2% in 2025E. The IMF is also forecasting 3.4% average inflation for the year, which would have to assume a rapid deceleration in 2H24.

MACRO
FDI net inflows up 23% YoY in March, 42% YoY in 1Q24
  • Foreign direct investments (FDI) posted a net inflow of US$686mn in March, up 23% YoY
    • Cited 19% YoY increase in non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments to US$465mn while net investments in equity capital (ex-reinvestment) rose 67% to US$157mn
    • Equity capital placements were primarily from Japan, Singapore, and USA and these were largely invested in manufacturing, financial/insurance, and real estate industries
  • Brings 1Q24 FDI to US$3bn, up 42% YoY
  • BSP is targeting FDI net inflows of US$9.0bn this year, slightly higher than 2023’s FDI of US$8.9bn
MACRO
IMF cuts Philippine growth outlook
  • IMF now sees PH GDP expanding by 6% (still within 6%-7% target), lower than their 6.2% forecast in its World Economic Outlook (WEO) in April
    • Growth forecast was lowered after weaker-than-expected GDP data
  • They expect growth to rebound 6% in 2024 and 6.2% in 2025 thru stronger consumption demand, higher public and private investment, and a recovery in exports
    • Growth in 2025 is expected to be driven by easing inflation and a pickup in household consumption and investments
  • Economy still continues to perform well despite external challenges and policy tightening
  • IMF sees PH inflation settling at 3.4% this year (lower than earlier forecast of 3.6%, also lower than BSP’s 3.5%)
    • Food price inflation will come down faster due to recent tariff amendments
  • Inflation is projected to decline towards the target of 3% in the second half of the year
BANKS
Consumer loans jump 18% in 1Q24
  • Consumer loans for all banks grew 17.6% YoY in 1Q24, accelerating from the 17.0% pace in 4Q23. This is the fastest YoY print since 1Q20, just before the Covid pandemic started. Consumer loans also expanded at a significantly faster pace than total loans (+7.4%). As a result, consumers in 1Q24 accounted for 21.4% of total loans, an all-time high.
  • Growth was again driven by credit cards, up 30.0% YoY in 1Q24. This is the sixth consecutive quarter of more than 25% expansion YoY. Motor vehicle loans zoomed 17.6% YoY, the 9th consecutive quarter of accelerating YoY growth. Mortgage growth sped up to 7.9% YoY, up from 7.0% at end-2023. Meanwhile, Salary loans decelerated to +23.5% YoY, understandably, as it lapped the heady 62% growth posted in 1Q23.
  • NPL ratio for the consumer segment ticked up slightly ln 1Q24 to 5.80% up 3bps QoQ. This is not particularly worrisome, as 4Q is typically when most banks do significant write-offs. On the secured side, Mortgage NPLs edged up 1bp QoQ to 6.94%, while motor vehicle NPLs fell sharply to 5.36% from 5.62% in 4Q23. For unsecured consumer loans, NPLs increased for credit cards (+16bps QoQ to 4.13%) and for salary loans (+29bps QoQ to 5.41%). The increase in unsecured delinquencies could be a sign of increased stress on household cashflows given due to inflation.
  • Among the banks we cover, Security Bank (SECB PM) has the highest exposure to consumer loans at 31% of loans. Of the Big Three, its BPI (BPI PM) at 26%.
  • The growth in motor vehicle loans is positive for GT Capital (GTCAP PM), which part owns the Toyota franchise in the Philippines.
