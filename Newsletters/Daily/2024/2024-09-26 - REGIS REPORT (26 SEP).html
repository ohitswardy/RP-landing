REGIS REPORT

26 September 2024

We have a new report out on ICTSI (ICT PM), upgrading our FY24E-26E profit estimates by 7-14%, on the back of higher yield per TEU forecasts. We also raised our TP by 16% to P470/sh to reflect the earnings upgrades, as well as adjustments in capital structure and PHP:USD assumptions.

We believe the period of profit outperformance is not over, as Street forecasts still not pricing in the 10% tariff hikes in Manila and Melbourne in 3Q that will have a magnified impact on profits in 2H. Our revised forecasts suggest 2H profits could rebound by 50% YoY from the 21% pace in 1H, which vastly differs from consensus’ expectation of just 22%.

Over the medium term, the ongoing brownfield expansions in Mexico, Melbourne, and Manila should allow ICT to deliver earnings CAGR of at least 17%, underpinned by sustained market share and yield gains.  Consensus forecasts are at least 14% lower than our estimates, suggesting that upgrades will again follow.  

Over the past year, ICT has been a terrific outperformer doubling over that period.  Despite that, the shares don’t look overvalued as growth in earnings and EBITDA has preceded the stock price move.  ICT still trades at just 14x FY25E PER and 8.5x FY25E EV/EBITDA, below peer average of 16x/11x and historical average (21x/11x).  Reiterate BUY.

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona signaled openness to another 50bp of policy rate cuts before year-end.  This statement comes after the Fed’s 50bp rate reduction as well as the surprise 250bp cut in reserve requirements (RRR) announced by the BSP last week.  The BSP policy rate currently stands at 6.25%. A 50bp reduction by year-end would be in line with Regis expectations.  We believe there’s a justified case for more monetary easing given prevailing real interest rates and growth in money supply.

+++

The national government’s budget deficit was P59bn in Aug, down 59% YoY.  Expenditures were virtually flat YoY (-0.7%) but revenues jumped 24%, boosted by non-tax receipts.  The Dept of Finance (DOF) has been making an effort to raise money through asset sales, in light of the tight fiscal situation and its aim of trimming the debt/GDP ratio (currently just above 60%). In fact, as part of these fund-raising efforts, the DOF has revealed its plan to sell its 3% stake in Semirara (SCC PM), among other assets.

Meanwhile, the Lower House approved the 2025 national budget, 10% higher than the current (2024) one. The budget proposes an 8% rise in primary spending, an increase that’s better than that in the previous years.

+++

Filinvest Land (FLI PM) is trying to hit two birds with one stone via a buyback deal that would also reduce its stake in subsidiary Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM).

FLI says it will conduct a tender offer for 1.866bn FLI shares, which represents 7.7% of shares outstanding (and 24% of float shares). Instead of paying in cash, the company will pay with FILRT shares, at a ratio of 0.32 FILRT shares for every FLI share tendered. At FILRT’s share price of P3.11/share, the tender offer would value FLI shares at P1.00/share, ~20% higher than the last closing price of P0.83/share. Note again that only 1.866bn FLI shares will be accepted in the tender offer, equivalent to just below 1/4th of FLI’s total float so the arbitrage opportunity is not so straightforward.

By paying for the tender offer with FILRT shares, FILRT’s public float will rise from 34.5% to 46.7%, potentially paving the way for a share-for-property infusion in the future.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) disclosed it was selling 3.07m of its own shares currently held in treasury to an unnamed buyer.  The transaction price (P720/share) is at a slight premium to market. These shares represent 0.5% of AC’s outstanding shares and roughly 1/5th of its shares in treasury.

Even following the recent run-up, AC remains attractively valued, with the shares still at ~35% discount to NAV.

MACRO
Budget deficit narrows to P54bn in August
  • The government’s budget deficit narrowed by 59% YoY to P54bn in August
    • Decline due to 24% YoY jump in revenues, alongside a mild contraction in expenses
  • Revenues rose 24% YoY, driven by strong non-tax collections
    • Tax collections grew 10%, with BIR collections rising by 12% and Customs revenues by 5%
    • Non-tax revenues surged >3x to P70bn, on the back of PSALM’s P10bn settlement of guarantee fee arrears, along with higher PAGCOR income
  • Expenses declined by 0.7%, despite a 24% YoY increase in interest expenses
    • Primary spending (net of interest payments) fell 3% YoY
  • Brings YT-August budget deficit to P697bn, down by 5% YoY
    • The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP

    • Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively
