REGIS REPORT

06 June 2024

The slight acceleration in the CPI inflation rate (to 3.9%) announced yesterday does not come as a surprise.  And given the lower base of comparison (in June-July 2023), it is quite probable that the headline YoY inflation numbers will be a little higher over the next couple of months.  Overall, however, we don’t think the Bangko Sentral (BSP) will be pushed to alter monetary policy (cut rates) over the near-term, until at least Aug.

Meanwhile, in related news, the Dept of Agriculture (DA) says it expects the cut in rice import tariffs to reduce prices by P6-7/kg from the current ~P50/kg retail price as soon as July.  The predicted reduction in price does not deviate from our views. The Executive Order (EO) on the tariff reduction has not been released so we are unclear exactly when the lower tariffs will take effect. What could stymie this would be vested interests blocking imports through non-tariff bureaucratic restrictions.

If the tariff cut does have its predicted impact of lowering domestic prices by ~15%, the reported inflation figure could fall closer to 2%.  This would strengthen the case for a rate reduction sooner rather than later.

+++

The Dept of Energy (DOE) says as much as P31trn of investments is needed to achieve the government’s aspirational goals, which include 50% renewable energy (RE) sourcing.  The transmission grid appears to be the biggest bottleneck and, so far, investments in it are inadequate to meet rapidly growing needs, especially in light of the transition to RE.

+++ 

RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) announced a very large property-for-share swap agreement with its sponsor, Robinsons Land (RLC PM). RCR will expand its portfolio by 72% (to 828,000sqm) by acquiring 11 malls and two offices. In turn, RLC will subscribe to 5bn new RCR shares, expanding shares outstanding by 42%. Our initial estimates suggest that the transaction will be yield accretive for RCR.

MACRO
Inflation accelerates to 3.9% in May
  • Headline CPI rose 3.9% YoY in May, slightly faster than the 3.8% print in April
    • This was however a tad lower than consensus forecast of 4% and came in at the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.7%-4.5%
  • The mild uptick was due to faster transport inflation (+3.5% YoY in May vs. +2.6% in April) and a milder deflation in electricity CPI (-8.5% YoY in May vs -11% in April)
  • Food inflation slowed to 5.8% from 6.0%
    • Rice CPI dipped 0.2% MoM, the first MoM decrease in seven months, resulting in moderating rice inflation to +23.0% in May from 23.9% in April
  • Core inflation slowed further to 3.1% in May from 3.2% in April, with non-food “core” CPI moderating to 3.3% from 3.2%
  • Headline inflation averaged at 3.5% in 5M24
    • BSP’s risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2024 is 3.8%
  • The BSP said the risks to the inflation outlook continue to lean toward the upside in the near-term due to the possible impact of adverse weather conditions on farm output
    • Nonetheless, the BSP expects full-year average inflation to settle within the target range of 2-4% for 2024 and 2025
    • Next BSP policy meeting is scheduled on June 27

 

MACRO
Lower rice tariffs to bring down retail prices as early as July
  • Slashing tariffs on imported rice could bring avg retail prices to P6-P7/kilo as early as July
    • Current prices of imported rice: P52-P55 (well-milled), P49-P51 (regular milled)
  • NEDA approved medium-term plan to lower tariffs on agri and industrial products
    • Includes of reduction in rice tariffs (from 35% to 15%) until 2028
  • DOF is willing to forego an estimated P10bn in tariff collections to lower rice prices
    • DA will be plugging potential funding gaps for the RCEF
    • Priority is to ensure that farmers continue to benefit from RDEF
  • DA is seeking to extend RCEF and bring back regulatory powers to NFA to bring down rice prices
POWER
CREC listing to proceed tomorrow
  • The listing of Citicore Renewable Energy Corp (CREC) will proceed as planned tomorrow, June 7
  • CREC is offering up to 1.79bn primary shares, with an overallotment option of up to 267.86mn secondary shares at P2.70/sh
    • IPO size as much as P5.45bn (w/ overallotment option) based on final offer price
    • Public float of 23% assuming full exercise of overallotment option; Market cap of P23.7bn
  • P4.5bn net proceeds will be used to fund 2024/2025 capex for pipeline development of solar and BESS projects
    • CREC currently owns 8 operating power plants with a gross installed capacity of 285MW
    • It has a project pipeline of 6,446MW (gross) of solar and 813MW (gross) of onshore wind, and 26MW of hydro plants, which will take overall gross installed capacity of 7,570MW by 2028
    • Approximately P220bn in capex, of which P35bn will be spent in 2024 alone
POWER
Department of Energy eyeing P31tn clean energy investment by 2040
  • The country needs to invest P20-31tn in renewable energy investment by 2040 to achieve its targets
    • By 2040, the country wants to achieve 5% energy savings, 10% electric vehicle penetration and 50% RE share in the generation mix
    • According to Energy Undersecretary Rowena Guevara
  • Based on the demand, the target is to achieve a total capacity of over 52.8 gigawatts of RE, with 27 GW of solar, 16.6 GW of wind, six GW of hydro, 2.5 GW of geothermal and 360 megawatts of biomass
  • However, the country faces several challenges, including financing, permitting, and grid interconnection issues
    • Currently, our transition system is able to support about 28 GW of generation, but in order to move 52.8 GW of RE by 2040, we have to almost double our transmission system
PROPERTY/REIT
RCR to grow portfolio by 72% in property-for-share swap
  • RLC will acquire 279,000sqm of malls and 69,000sqm of offices from Robinsons Land via property-for-share swap
    • The 11 malls to be acquired will mostly be in provincial areas: Novaliches, Cainta, Luisita, Cabanatuan, Lipa, Sta. Rosa, Imus, Los Banos, Palawan, Ormoc, and Davao
    • The offices to be acquired are: Giga Tower in Bridgetowne, Quezon City, and Cybergate Delta 2 in Davao City 
  • RLC will subscribe to 5.0bn primary RCR shares at P6.80/share (total of P33.9bn)
    • The subscription price is 36% higher than the last closing price
    • RCR’s shares outstanding will expand by 47%
  • The acquisition would expand the portfolio size by 72% to 828,000sqm
  • AUM would also grow by 42% to P114bn
  • We estimate that the acquisition will be yield accretive for RCR
  • After the transaction, RLC’s ownership in RCR will rise from 50% to 66%
  • RLC is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 6.3X FY24E P/E
  • RCR currently offers 8.1% dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
