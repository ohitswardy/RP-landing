REGIS REPORT

30 October 2024

Metrobank (MBT PM) posted a confusing set of 9M24 results. Headline NPAT was up 12% YoY to P35.7bn, slightly ahead of Regis and Street estimates. While loan growth stayed strong (+15.6% YoY), reported NIMs took a big hit, crashing 27bps QoQ. It turns out that the bank terminated a popular time deposit product with a cross-currency swap. The product would show a lower nominal interest rate (in USD) while the swap portion (to bring it back to PHP) was booked as a trading/fx loss. This product was more widely used by mid-sized banks (like Security Bank, Chinabank, etc). However, many analysts and investors were blindsided by the magnitude of MBT’s use of the swaps. Management was also unable to adequately explain the impact to prior periods’ NIMs, making like-for-like historic analysis – and forecasting – difficult.

The confusion distracted from the solid loan growth, asset quality, and trading gains in 3Q24. NPLs improved to 1.59%, the lowest since just before the Covid pandemic, while NPL cover stayed high at 162%. The bank also booked strong trading gains of P6bn for the quarter alone. Notably, the bank showed a whopping P40bn in unrealized mark-to-market gains in the Other Comprehensive Income account, which indicates potential profit MBT an bring on-books over time.

While we view the results positively, there may be some backlash from investors from the perceived lack of transparency.

At its regular briefing, property consultants Colliers pointed to a deterioration in both the office and residential markets. Due to POGO closures, net office take-up in Metro Manila turned negative for the first time since 4Q21. Furthermore, Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise from 18.5% to 20.5% by the end of the year, as more POGO firms shut down. Meanwhile, the level of unsold residential inventory increased to 70 months’ worth of presales, from “only” 60 months in 3Q24. While lower interest rates should provide some support to the property sector, the underlying fundamentals are clearly far from robust.

+++

Based on PAGCOR’s 9M24 revenue figures, it appears that gross gaming revenue (GGR) of e-games fell 31% QoQ. This likely has implications for the market leader in that space, DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), which had a c. 50% market share as of 2Q24. On the other hand, GGR of licensed casinos grew 5% QoQ. Since Okada Manila reported that its GGR fell 8% QoQ during the period, the other operators should show better results.

+++

News media reported the possibility of a P16bn refund to consumers from Meralco (MER PM) that could arise from a regulatory ruling on its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP).  We believe any refunds will only be applicable to over-collections above P1.35/kwh (possibly due to changes in customer mix).

In fact, previous media reports say that a ruling on 5RP in favor of Meralco’s application has been made. Meralco is seeking a tariff fixed at P1.35/kwh (unchanged vs 4RP), which implies no refunds except for over-collections.

BANKS
Metrobank 9M24 - strong trading gains make up for NIM collapse

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported 9M24 NPAT of P35.7bn, up 12% YoY, slightly ahead of Regis (FY 24E P46.9bn) and consensus (P47.9bn). However, the “quality” of the earnings leaves something to be desired. While loan growth remained robust (15.6% YoY gross, 16.6% net), margins were crushed (-27bps QoQ) by sharply higher funding costs as the bank shifted from TDs to wholesale funding. Fee incomes were soft, too, down 19% YoY. These were offset by extremely strong trading/fx gains of P6bn in 3Q alone (P5.6bn for 9M). To the upside, asset quality improved, with NPL ratio down 7bps QoQ to 1.59% and NPL cover steady at 162%.

Ahead of that, some highlights:

  • Gross loans grew 15.6% YoY accelerating a tad from the 14.9% in 2Q24 (net loans +16.6% vs 16.3%). Corporate loans led the growth, up 16.6% YoY, while consumer loans were up 12.3%. Within consumers, credit cards were up 16.6% and auto loans were up 15.7%. Loan yields were broadly flat QoQ
  • Deposits fell 2.7% YoY and 6.0% QoQ, as the bank let go of about P160bn worth of TDs QoQ. These were replaced with wholesale funds (short-term bills/bonds) which nearly doubled QoQ. Funding costs surged 25bps QoQ as a result. That said, the move MAY have been justified. Time deposits have lots of “hidden costs” including a 75bps stamp duty (DST), c25bps deposit insurance, and the RRR (recently reduced from 9.5% to 7.0%). Hence even if wholesale funds are more expensive on a headline basis, the net cost could be lower than TDs. Bigger picture, though, it does highlight that MBT is far more reliant on expensive funding compared to BDO/BPI.
  • NIMs collapsed 27bps QoQ and 33bps YoY in 3Q24 owing largely to the funding issues.
  • Fee incomes remained soft, up 3% YoY. There was also a 17% drop in miscellaneous income in 9M24 (-49% YoY in 3Q).
  • The bank booked monster trading/fx gains of P6bn in 3Q24 alone, making up for the softness of other revenues.
  • Opex grew 11% in 9M24. However, for 3Q24 alone, opex jumped 17% YoY, with compensation expenses surging 21% YoY and 15% QoQ.
  • Provisions fell 48% in 9M24m but were up 17% in 3Q alone. Asset quality improved QoQ, with NPL ratio down 7bps to 1.59%, while NPL cover was broadly stable at 162% (vs 163% in 2Q).

 

PROPERTY
Colliers: net office take-up turns negative in 3Q24, unsold residential inventory level rises
  • During its 3Q24 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila 3Q24 net office take-up turned negative for the first time since 4Q21
  • Net office take-up was -33,000sqm, from +98,000sqm in 2Q24 (+17,000sqm in 3Q23)
    • 57,000sqm of office space vacated by POGOs
  • As a result, Metro Manila office vacancy rose 0.2ppt to 18.5%
    • Due to the POGO ban, Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 20.5% by end-2024
    • POGO firms still occupy c. 400,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila 
  • Rental rates in most Metro Manila business districts fell c. 1% QoQ
    • We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, and 5.8X FY24E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 64% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY24E P/B, and 4.0X FY24E P/E
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 5.9X FY24E P/E
  • Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that the level of unsold residential inventory represents c. 70 months’ worth of presales (from c. 60 months as of 2Q24)
    • This appears to be due to an increase in unsold inventory level and a reduction in residential take-up
  • 3Q24 residential take-up fell c. 20% YoY to 3,100 units (flat QoQ)
  • ALI is trading at 37% discount to NAV, 1.7X FY24E P/B, and 18.1X FY24E P/E

UTILITIES
Meralco refunds of P16bn on ERC 5RP ruling?
  • Meralco head of regulatory affairs Atty Ronald Valles reportedly claims Meralco may give consumers a refund of as much as P16bn
    • Valles claims this could arise once the ERC decision on its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) is released
  • We think the claims of refund could be misleading / confusing
    • Meralco is, in fact, asking the regulator to fix the tariff for its 5RP (@ P1.35/kwh) by considering 5RP as a “lapsed” period
      • Fixing the tariff at P1.35/kwh would mean Meralco would NOT be issuing refunds, except for over-collections above P1.35/kwh
      • Valles previously noted media reports that ERC chair Mona Dimalanta says a vote favoring Meralco has been made (fixing the tariff at P1.35/kwh)
    • P1.35/kwh is the current provision approved tariff (while 5RP is not settled) and is the approved tariff for 4RP
MACRO
Government infrastructure spending drops in August
  • Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 12.9% YoY to P989.4bn in 8M24
    • Growth moderated sharply from the 17% pace in 7M24
    • To reach the government’s FY24E target of P1.47tn (+4% YoY), spending for the rest of the year could fall by 11% YoY
  • Implies infrastructure spending for August alone fell by 8% YoY to P125bn
    • Cited project delays of major infrastructure projects, timing issues on encashment of payments to contractors, and adverse weather conditions
  • For 2025, the government plans to spend P1.53tn, up 4% YoY and equivalent to 5.4% of GDP
    • The government aims to spend 5-6% of GDP annually for infrastructure through 2028

GAMING
PAGCOR 3Q24 revenue from e-games falls 31% QoQ
  • PAGCOR reported that its revenue from e-games (such as e-Bingo and e-casino) fell 31% QoQ to P7.56bn (in 3Q24)
    • This could imply weakness in the GGR of DigiPlus, which had a 50% market share as of 2Q24
  • Meanwhile, PAGCOR revenue from licensed casinos grew 5% QoQ to P8.44bn
    • Since GGR of Okada Manila fell 8% QoQ in 3Q24, the GGR of other licensed casinos should show stronger improvement
    • Solaire North likely showed stronger GGR in 3Q24, since it only contributed for one month in 2Q24
