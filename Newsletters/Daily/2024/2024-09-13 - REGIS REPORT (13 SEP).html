REGIS REPORT

13 September 2024

Two senators are proposing a blanket ban on online gaming (gambling) due to the supposed social ills from the activity.  Such a ban would spell the end for the operations of companies like DigiPlus (PLUS PM).  It is, however, still early days for this proposal. We can expect intense lobbying against this measure. The arguments that will be raise in favor of online gaming are easy to predict: (1) revenue for the government in terms of fees and taxes and  (2) reduction of the illegal forms of gaming, which breed crime.

Online gambling (legal, licensed) is on track to produce over P100bn (~US$2bn) of gaming revenue this year, close to one-half of 1% of GDP.  Gaming revenue is effectively how much people (in this case, entirely local Philippine residents) are losing on these platforms on bets as small as less than 10 U.S. cents.

The Dept of Energy (DOE) rejected the attempt by SP New Energy (SPNEC PM) to abandon its commitment, under the Green Energy Auction Program (GEAP), to deliver 280MW of solar capacity by Dec this year.  SPNEC previously committed sell power at below P4/kwh but clearly is in no position to deliver the 280MW within the next three months.  SPNEC, now under Meralco (MER PM) control, could face significant financial penalties if it is unable to deliver.

In the meantime, Meralco organized a briefing yesterday to discuss SPNEC’s biggest undertaking: Terra Solar.  Terra Solar is building the world’s largest integrated (solar + battery) RE project. We came away with the thinking this project is likely to be quite lucrative for SPNEC and, by extension, Meralco.

POWER
Highlights of Terra Solar briefing

Key Points

 

  • World’s largest integrated RE project.  Terra Solar is building a 3,500MW solar farm with 4,500MWh of battery storage in Central Luzon across a couple of provinces. It will be the largest such integrated (solar + battery) RE project in the world once completed. Terra Solar will sell 850MW of power to Meralco under 20-year contracts beginning Feb 2026 (600MW) and Feb 2027 (250MW). The contracts are for mid-merit power, with capacity factor of 50%, thus necessitating battery storage and much higher headline capacity vs the contract capacity.

 

  • Attractive returns, magnified.  Terra Solar is looking like a very attractive project with project IRR “in the teens”.  With leverage and the entry of a new partner at a substantial premium, the equity returns for SP New Energy (SPNEC PM) are likely to be many times that (easily >50%).

 

  • Effective tariffs much higher than P5.7 per kwh. Meralco’s effective purchase price for power from Terra Solar (which it controls anyway) is likely to be close to P8/kwh, after “cost of capital” adjustments embedded in the agreement. This would be ~40% higher than the much-publicized P5.7/kwh in the press releases just two years ago. For perspective, ACEN’s winning bids for 160MW of power at similar terms were at just under P8.2/kwh.

 

  • On schedule so far.  Conscious and keenly aware that project delays are common, SPNEC / Meralco management has been working early on issues that might delay completion. Work on transmission (grid connection) is supposedly ahead of schedule. Transmission is frequently a reason for delay. Most of the rest of the project work is within schedule so far for Phase 1. Phase 1 involves 2,500MW of the total 3,500MW solar capacity.  Delays, however, are always because of events that are unanticipated.

 

  • Actis injecting US$600m for 40%.  New UK-based investor Actis will inject US$600m (~P35bn) into Terra Solar for a 40% stake,  This means SPNEC will only need to pony up P15bn for its 60% share in the equity of Terra Solar.

 

  • P10bn contribution for SPNEC? Employing back-of-the envelope calculations, we reckon Terra Solar can contribute over P10bn in profit to SPNEC (for its 60% stake). Note SPNEC’s equity contribution is likely to be only around P15bn.  The profit figure also implies EPS of at least ~P0.20 based on outstanding shares of 50bn.  Meralco is expected to take ownership of just over 50% in SPNEC with the exchangeable instrument it acquired from SPNEC founder Leandro Leviste.
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market inches up in August
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 5bps MoM to 20.2% (in August) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index expanded 14bps MoM to 25.0%
  • Net foreign buying increased to US$143mn, from US$60mn in July
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 19 saw increases in foreign ownership while 11 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • CNVRG – foreign ownership rose 63bps MoM to 17.9%. Net foreign buying hit US$15.3mn, the fifth highest for the month.  
    • CNPF – foreign ownership increased 26bps MoM to 24.6% while net foreign buying amounted to US$7.8mn.
    • URC – foreign ownership fell 28bps MoM to 22.0%.  Net foreign selling reached US$31.0mn, the highest for the month.
    • BLOOM - foreign ownership declined 26bps MoM to 17.0%. It also experienced the third highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$7.0mn).
