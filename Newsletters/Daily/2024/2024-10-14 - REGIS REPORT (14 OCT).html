REGIS REPORT

14 October 2024

Rice prices – for domestically-grown rice – finally dropped below P44/kg for the first time this year. That’s down 5-6% in the last two weeks, and roughly 10-12% year-to-date. Current prices seem to be finally reflecting the YTD move in export prices out of Vietnam/Thailand, suggesting that retail prices still do not reflect the cut in tariffs from 35% to 15%. Combined with minimum wage hikes of 6-13% in recent weeks, lower rice prices should give a big boost to consumption, particularly from lower-income households, for whom rice is a significantly larger share of CPI. We expect companies such as URC, MONDE, CNPF, and PGOLD to show sequential volume improvement heading into the 4Q24 holiday season, and for momentum to continue into 1H25 with election spending for the mid-terms.

This should push inflation even lower than the 1.9% print in September, which should give the BSP every excuse to cut policy rates this week (Wed, 16 Oct), and then again in December.

Semirara (SCC PM) is proposing to spend P291bn (US$5bn) on its coal mining operations, as part of its negotiations with the Dept of Energy (DOE) to get the latter to extend its coal operating contract. SCC’s coal operating contract expires July 2027.

Airlines, Cebu Air (CEB PM) among them, are asking the regulator to approve them collecting an additional P75 (US$1.4) for domestic flights and up to P300 (US$5+).  The “terminal enhancement fee” is supposed to help offset the fee increases imposed by the new operator of the Manila airport (NAIA).

There’s nothing stopping the airlines from increasing fares unilaterally, but a regulator-approved surcharge will help airlines deflect the blame. Such a surcharge will allow airlines to pass on higher costs on flights that have already been pre-booked.

We estimate that foreign ownership of the Philippine listed equity market rose 15bp in Sept to 20.4%, while foreign ownership in PSEi names rose 20bp to 25.2%. The rise reflects the revived interest from foreign funds in the last few weeks following renewed hopes of US Fed rate reductions.

POWER
Semirara proposes P291bn coal mine expansion
  • Based on documents submitted to the Department of Environment and Natural Resources, Semirara plans to invest P291bn to expand its coal mining operations
    • We suspect the headline investment cost includes operating expenses and other costs
  • Expansion is based on the discovery of additional coal resource and reserve of 90mn MT and 72mn MT, respectively, from the Acacia pit
    • Acacia is located northwest and part of the upthrown block relative to Molave pit
    • SCC expects to start coal mining operations at the Acacia prospect by 2025
    • Currently, SCC is operating the Narra mine, whose coal reserves (45mn MT) are expected to be depleted by 2026
  • We suspect investments for this project will hinge on the renewal of SCC’s coal operating contract, which is expected to expire on July 2027
PROPERTY/REITS
SEC approves MREIT-MEG property-for-share swap
  • The SEC approved the property-for-share swap agreement between Megaworld and MREIT
  • MREIT will acquire six Megaworld office buildings via a property-for-share swap
  • MREIT will issue 926.16mn new shares to Megaworld at P14.20/share
    • This is 4% higher than the last closing price of MREIT
    • Total transaction value of P13.15bn
    • This will expand MREIT’s shares outstanding by 33%
  • Megaworld’s stake in MREIT will rise from 51.3% to 63.4%
  • The buildings have a combined leasable area of 156,600sqm and have an aggregate occupancy rate of 94% (similar to the 95% occupancy of MREIT’s existing portfolio)
    • McKinley West, Fort Bonifacio – 94,300sqm
    • Iloilo Business Park, Iloilo City– 36,200sqm
    • Davao Park District, Davao City – 26,100sqm
  • The acquisition will expand MREIT’s portfolio by 48% to 480,000sqm
  • MEG is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY24E P/B, and 3.7X FY24E P/E
  • MREIT currently offers 7.2% FY24E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The current 10-year government bond yield is 5.8%
RETAIL
Puregold plans expansion in five provinces
  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) said it is planning to expand its presence in at least five provinces
    • The prospective areas include Iloilo, Davao, Cavite, Bulacan, and Metro Manila
  • Puregold is aiming to strengthen its presence in key growth regions, paving the way for a broader market share and investment opportunities.
    • The company stated that its concerted focus on regional markets, particularly in Visayas and Mindanao is part of a larger strategy to drive sustainable growth.
  • As of the 2Q24, Puregold has a total of 581 stores nationwide
    • This includes 498 Puregold stores, 28 S&R membership shopping warehouses, and 55 S&R New York-style QSRs.
  • Regis is forecasting 30 new stores every year, in line with company guidance
  • Puregold plans to spend P8.1bn in capex this year
    • P2.1bn for 30 new Puregold sores
    • P3.8bn – 4 S&R warehouses and 6 S&R QSRs
    • P500mn for logistics capex
    • P1.4bn for maintenance capex, solar projects, and IT upgrades  
TRANSPORT
Airlines seek approval to implement new fee to cover higher NAIA fees
  • Domestic carriers, Philippine Airlines (PAL), Cebu Air (CEB PM), and Air Asia Philippines (AAP), are asking the Civil Aeronautics Board to approve the collection of a “terminal enhancement fee” to help recover the increasing cost of flying in NAIA
    • All three airlines are seeking to have a terminal enhancement fee of P75 per way for domestic flights
    • PAL and CEB are seeking a P300 fee for foreign flights while AAP is seeking for a P275 fee
  • The new private operator of NAIA has doubled, if not tripled, airport costs since Oct 1; Landing and takeoff fees alone have increased by 400%
    • CEB said that the hike is costing the company P9.3mn per day or P3.4bn a year, equivalent to 43% of its 2023 profit
    • Proposed terminal enhancement fee only covers <50% of costs, as it does not have plans to pass on the entire cost to be able to sustain travel demand
  • CAB is scheduled to hold a board meeting toward the end of this month to discuss the proposals for a terminal enhancement fee
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market rises in September
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market rose 15bps MoM to 20.4% (in September) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index expanded 21bps MoM to 25.2%
  • Net foreign buying increased to US$346mn, from US$143mn in August
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 18 saw increases in foreign ownership while 11 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • ALI – foreign ownership rose 51bps MoM to 28.1%. Net foreign buying hit US$35.3mn, the third highest for the month.  
    • MBT – foreign ownership increased 48bps MoM to 22.1% while net foreign buying amounted to US$27.7mn, the fifth highest for the month.
    • JFC – foreign ownership expanded 47bps MoM to 23.8%.  Net foreign selling reached US$18.6mn, the eighth highest for the month.
    • SMC - foreign ownership fell 42bps MoM to 4.9%. It also experienced the highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$16.1mn), due to its deletion from the FTSE mid-cap index.
