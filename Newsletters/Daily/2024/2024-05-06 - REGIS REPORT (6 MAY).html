REGIS REPORT

06 May 2024

We have results from a few names this morning, notably Universal Robina (URC PM) and sister firm Robinsons Land (RLC PM), both of whom held their earnings call last Friday.  Semirara (SCC PM), meanwhile, also issued a press release on its results.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (=) – Net profit rose 21% to P4.1bn on the back of improved margins.  EBIT margins for the branded consumer food categories improved sharply in 1Q24 due to favorable input cost and opex savings. While top line trends in the coming quarters could be mixed due to various factors (seasonality, weather, competition, etc.), management believes the recent margin improvement is sustainable given more structural operating cost improvements.

  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (+) – 1Q24 recurring net profit rose 21% to P3.3bn, in line with consensus and slightly ahead of REGIS forecast. However, what caught our attention was the 88% YoY (83% QoQ) drop in reservation sales of standalone residential projects to P684mn, the lowest level in over 15 years. While management attributes this to an increase in reservation fees and the internal restructuring of its residential division, the 1Q24 figure is unequivocally weak and it raises concerns for the rest of the sector. On the brighter side, there has been some improvement in April, with sales from a single project reaching P1.8bn.  Meanwhile, consumer spending in RLC malls remained healthy, with mall revenue growing 14% YoY and flat QoQ (from the seasonally strong 4Q).

  • Semirara (SCC PM) (+) – In a limited press release, SCC reported its 1Q24 profit fell 28% YoY to P6.5bn, trending well above Regis forecast (P16.8bn) and consensus (P21.3bn).  We attribute the outperformance to better-than-expected coal mining ASP of P2,978/MT (US$53/MT).  Management is holding a briefing later at 4pm (Registration link) to discuss the 1Q results and the announced purchase of Cemex Philippines (CHP PM). 

+++

The Department of Agriculture expects domestic rice prices to begin softening next month, after international prices declined in the last few weeks. The price of rice has been a key driver of the CPI the past year and a drop would be a welcome respite for consumers.  As has often been cited, rice is 9% of the Philippine CPI basket.

Export prices for rice out of Vietnam and Thailand are down c10% ytd. TH prices are up slightly (c2%) from their 2024 lows, but VN prices continue to fall. Meanwhile, the retail price of rice in Metro Manila is holding steady-ish at around P50/kilo for well-milled rice.

Meralco (MER PM) says it expects 2Q volume growth to be 6-8%, due to the uncharacteristically warm (scorching) weather this year.  For the year, Meralco says volumes are likely to grow 5-6%, which seems a tad low relative to what’s been seen so far.  It already reported a 9% jump in unit sales in 1Q and indicated April volume growth to be ~8%.

The higher expenditures for electricity usage this season (due to both volumes and prices) could be a factor affecting consumer spending this quarter.
