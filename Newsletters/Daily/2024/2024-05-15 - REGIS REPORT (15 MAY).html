REGIS REPORT

15 May 2024

The roster of 1Q results we have today is populated mostly by “beats” relative to the Street.

 

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (+) – AC’s headline 1Q net profit of P13.1bn was 28% while core profit rose 26% to P11.8bn.  We reckon this is above Street expectations. All major listed subsidiaries saw double digit core profit increases. The unlisted subsidiaries, however, are still losing money.  The asset sales and write-downs over the past few years raise our hopes that the bleeding will be staunched soon.

 

  • Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) (+) – The highlight of 1Q was the strong net subscriber add figure of almost 125k. Revenues rose 10% while net income rose 17% to P2.55bn. We are less enthused by the latter, however, as the increase in net income could be attributed in large part to the swing in “Other income”.

 

  • Globe (GLO PM) (+) – Core income rose 13% to P5.8bn, a fairly strong result for 1Q24, Mobile revenues rose 8% - a sign that the competitive environment remains benign.  The other key highlight in 1Q was the 138% jump in profit contributions from Mynt. The implied 1Q profit of Mynt suggests it is on track to post a profit of ~US$200m this year.  The company holds its earnings briefing today.

 

  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (+) – 1Q24 profit after the share of participating prefs jumped 37% to P2.74bn. Even though profitability appears to be trending better than forecast, the beat largely came from lower-than-expected cash costs at its core East zone concession. The company has been underspending relative to its committed cash opex/capex targets, which we believe could have implications on future tariffs.

  • Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) (-) – Profits dropped 79% YoY to P202mn in 1Q24, tracking below Regis and consensus estimates.  Lower global nickel prices was the main culprit, diminishing the improvement in sales volumes.

 

We also have a published comment on convenience store operator Philippine Seven (SEVN PM), shedding more light on its interim results (see link).

Finally, in addition to what was reported yesterday, Bloomberry (BLOOM PM), at its briefing, said the record level GGR of slots bodes well for Solaire Resorts North (SRN), even as management acknowledged the VIP business was lackluster,  SRN will have the same number of slot machines as Solaire Entertainment City.

 

+++

The Lower House advanced the amendments to the Rice Tariffication Law beyond the committee level.  Among the amendments is one that gives the power to the National Food Authority to buy (import) rice and sell it at subsidized prices.  Frankly, we don’t think this amendment is a good thing. However, if that’s all they want to change, then it’s not as bad than we feared.  In other words, it could be much worse. We will have to see its final form before we draw any conclusions.

MACRO
House approves amendments to Rice Tariffication Law on 2nd reading
  • The Lower House approved on second reading a bill that seeks to amend the Rice Tariffication Law
  • The amendments will allow the NFA to sell rice at subsidized prices during emergencies, including shortages
  • The measure also extends the validity period for the Rice Competitiveness Enhancement Fund (RCEF) for six more years and increases its budget to P15 billion from P10 billion
  • The law, signed in 2019, stripped the NFA of its regulatory and import licensing functions and allowed the private sector to import freely, subject to a tariff system
TELCOS
Converge 1Q24 results – Strong subscriber additions
  • Subscriber additions rise to 125k, highest since 3Q21.  Converge reported net subscriber additions of 125k in 1Q24, of which 46k (37%) were prepaid.  This is the highest net adds figure since 3Q21 and is, in our view, the highlight of the quarter’s results. In contrast, PLDT saw a net subscriber reduction (14k) for its wired broadband business.
  • Reported earnings up 17%. Revenues rose 10% due to the increased subscriber count. Net income, however, jumped 17% to P2.55m (vs P2.17bn), with much of that increase due to the swing in “Other Income” (P381m, pretax), which came from lower provisions on modems, as well as FX gains and higher interest income.  Note that its self-reported EBITDA of P5.8bn, up +13%, includes “Other income”, which makes it not comparable with other telcos.
  • Capex deferred. CNVRG reduced capex guidance to P12-14bn for FY24 (vs. P17-19bn), but this is really a deferral of expenditures to FY25. Delays in its international sub-sea cable investment was the reason for this drop but FY25 will rise as a result.
  • Dividends in 2025. Management intends to set out its dividend policy by late this year and pay out its first dividend in 2025.

TELCOS
Globe – Mobile momentum in 1Q24
  • Core income rises 13%.  Globe reported net income of P6.8bn for 1Q24, down 7% due to less tower sales.  Core income, on the other hand, rose 13% to P5.8bn, which we believe is better than Street expectations. Overall service revenue may have grown only 3%, but this was due to a fall in non-telco revenue (principally due to the de-consolidation of a subsidiary).  More importantly, recovery in mobile growth, first seen in 3Q23, is still continuing.
  • Mobile revenue up 8%, home broadband down 6%. Globe’s core mobile business saw revenue growth of 8% YoY, a continuation of the rebound first seen in 3Q23. Mobile data revenue increased 10. Home broadband revenue, however, declined 6% due to the erosion of its fixed wireless subscriber base. We had been expecting the latter to have stopped dropping already.
  • Mynt contributions soar.  Income contribution from 36%-owned Mynt rose 138% to P962m and is now 17% of post-tax profit. Grossing up Mynt’s 1Q contribution would imply its 1Q income was P2.7bn. At this pace, Mynt could make more than P10bn this year (almost US$200m).
  • Capex down 22%.  Capex for 1Q was P13.7bn, lower by 22% YoY.  The company previously guided that it is aiming to reduce capex this year to around US$1bn (~P57bn). Globe is targeting to be free cash flow positive by next year.

MINING
NIKL: 1Q24 profit declines on lower ASPs

GAMING
Bloomberry 1Q24 EBITDA falls 14% YoY, behind
  • Bloomberry’s 1Q24 net profit fell 11% to P2.6bn, as the unusually strong win rate and record slot volumes were not quite enough to offset declines in VIP rolling chip and mass table drop
  • Meanwhile, EBITDA fell 14% to P4.9bn
    • Since we expect Solaire Resorts North to post EBITDA (and net profit) losses during its first six months of operations, we would consider this result as being behind
  • Pre-operating expenses from Solaire Resorts North amounted to P280mn
    • If these were added back, then EBITDA would have fallen 10% YoY (to P5.2bn) and net profit would have declined 3% YoY (to P2.9bn)
  • VIP rolling chip fell for the third consecutive quarter to P107bn (-36% YoY, -18% QoQ), as volumes from junkets (across the region) were particularly weak
    • Management expects the VIP business to remain weak since there continue to be limitations on the movement of funds from China
  • Slot chip-in rose 17% YoY and 7% QoQ to P98bn, a record level and 50% higher than the 2019 average
    • The company noted that in 1Q24, some mass table players migrated to slots
  • Solaire Resorts North, which will open on May 25, will have about the same number of slot machines as Solaire Entertainment City
