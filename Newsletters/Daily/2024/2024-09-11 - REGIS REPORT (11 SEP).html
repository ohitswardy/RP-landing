REGIS REPORT

11 September 2024

The bicameral reconciliation of the CREATE MORE bill was completed much more speedily than we anticipated, with a “final” version approved yesterday (contrary to our expectations of it taking a couple of months).

We highlight some of the key provisions as reported by media:

  • Firms in special economic zones can keep fiscal incentives even if they have up to 50% of employees working from home
  • Small firms will enjoy a lower income tax rate of 20% (vs 25%)
  • Larger tax deductions on power costs will be given to registered business enterprises (RBE)
  • Fuel providers for tax-exempt entities will be eligible for tax refunds for taxes already paid
  • The Finance Department will take over the VAT refund process from the tax bureau

The most obvious, visible impact (at least initially) will be on demand for office space from firms in PEZA properties. This would have consequences on vacancy rates. Our property analyst Carl (carl.sy@regis.ph) reckons vacancy rates (~16%), already bracing for the looming exodus of POGOs, could stay higher for longer.

+++

Meralco (MER PM) and DMCI (DMC PM) are both seeking “exemptions” from the government’s moratorium on new coal plants.  For Meralco, the firm is hoping to revive the 1,200MW Atimonan coal-fired plant. The Atimonan facility was granted approvals before the coal plant moratorium but was practically abandoned.  DMCI, on the other hand, is hoping to build coal-fired plants in small, “missionary” islands in place of oil-fired power facilities.

The fact that firms are still seeking to build coal plants despite the moratorium suggests that they see an urgent need for baseload power -- power that’s cheaper than what gas (LNG) or oil can provide.

+++

The trade deficit for July rose to US$4.87bn (vs. US$4.1bn in July 2023 and US$4.3bn in June 2024).  Exports were flat YoY while imports rose 7%.

+++

Several news publications reported that PAGCOR, the gaming regulator, will lower license fees “to 30% for brick-and-mortar licenses”. We find this puzzling since integrated resorts only pay 15% of VIP GGR and 25% of mass GGR to PAGCOR, already lower than then 30% mentioned in the articles. We suspect that PAGCOR was talking about the lower fees to online operators (30% of GGR) beginning January 2025 and there was some misinterpretation on the part of the reporters.

MACRO
Trade deficit widens to US$4.87bn in July
  • Preliminary data showed the country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.87bn in July
    • This is wider than the US$4.1bn deficit in July 2023 and US$4.3bn deficit in June
  • Exports were flat at US$6.25bn, following a 12% YoY drop in electronic exports
  • Imports rose 7% YoY to US$11.1bn due to higher imports of capital goods (+10% YoY), raw materials (+13%), and consumer goods (+3%) offset by lower fuel imports (-7%)
    • Imports of electronic products rose 12% YoY, which could indicate a possible rebound in exports in succeeding months
  • Brings YT-July trade deficit to US$29.9bn, lower by 6% YoY

MACRO
Bicam approves CREATE MORE bill
  • The Congressional Bicameral Conference Committee approved the CREATE MORE bill, which aims to draw in more foreign direct investments by enhancing tax incentives
    • Senate agreed to adopt provisions in the House version regarding fuel VAT refunds and local taxes on economic zone locators
    • Bicam version allows fuel suppliers serving tax-exempt entities to be eligible for tax refunds and allows local government units to set the local tax for registered business enterprises at up to 2% of gross income
  • Salient provisions of the CREATE MORE bill (Bicam report) are:
    • Reduction of corporate income tax rates to 20% from 25% for (a) corporations with less than P5mn net taxable income and assets less that P100mn and (b) Registered Business Enterprises under the enhanced deductions regime
    • Introduction of the “Registered Business Enterprise (RBE) Local Tax”, which is capped at 2% of gross income and will replace all local taxes and fees
    • Allowing RBEs 100% deduction on power expenses (from 50% previously), which could lower power costs by P3/kwh for manufacturing
    • Transfer of the responsibility of processing VAT refund claims to the Department of Finance from the Bureau of Internal Revenue to cut delays
    • Allowing firms to maintain work-from-home setup for 50% of workforce to cut costs
    • Reinstate powers of the President to give fiscal and non-fiscal incentives to enterprises without the need for a recommendation from the Fiscal Incentives Review Board
    • Exemption of international carriers from paying VAT on imported fuel, goods, and supplies used for international shipping or air transport operations
MACRO
FDI net inflows fall 29% YoY to four-year low in June
  • FDI net inflows fell 29% YoY, 28% MoM to US$394mn in June, the lowest level since April 2020
    • Decline resulted from lower net inflows across all major FDI components
    • Investments in debt instruments fell by 30% YoY to US$213mn, net investments in equity capital fell 33% to US$74mn, while reinvestment earnings fell 23% to US$107mn
    • By source, equity capital placements were mainly from Japan (47%), followed by the United States (15%), Sweden (14%) and Singapore (14%)
    • These were mainly invested in the manufacturing (48%), real estate (18%), wholesale and retail trade (16%) and financial and insurance (11%) sectors
  • Brings 1H24 net inflows to US$4.4bn, up 7.9% YoY
    • The largest sources of equity capital placements were the UK (52%), Japan (30%), and USA (7%)
    • These were largely invested in manufacturing (77%) and real estate (10%)
    • BSP expected FDI net inflows to reach US$9.5bn this year, up from US$8.9bn in 2023
POWER
Meralco plans to revive its 1,200 MW supercritical coal-fired Atimonan greenfield project
  • Meralco is seeking certification from the Department of Energy to confirm that its Atimonan power project is exempt from the 2020 coal moratorium
    • According to MGen President and CEO Emmanuel Rubio
  • Meralco initially proposed to develop a 1,200 MW ultra-supercritical coal-fired power plant under the environmental compliance certificate issued in 2015
    • It later decided to repurpose the coal-fired project to a 2,400MW natural gas-fired combined cycle gas turbine and an LNG terminal with floating storage unit
    • Meralco Chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan said that they are still undecided on the fuel type for the project but cited that the project is currently “licensed, permitted, and authorized to do a coal plant”
POWER
DMCI Power wants SPUG power plants to be exempted from coal ban
  • DMCI wants the coal moratorium to exclude Small Power Utilities Group (SPUG) power plants operating in off-grid areas
    • According to DMCI Chairman and CEO Isidro Consunji
    • Cited that new plants will be replacing diesel, which is the most expensive fuel
  • DMCI Power is a wholly-owned unit of DMCI and has 160 MW of installed capacity in off-grid areas (Palawan, Masbate, Oriental Mindoro)
    • 55% of capacity is sourced from diesel, 19% from coal, and 26% from bunker
    • DMCI Power accounts for 6% of DMCI’s profits
