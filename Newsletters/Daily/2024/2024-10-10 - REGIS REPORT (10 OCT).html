REGIS REPORT

10 October 2024

ICTSI’s (ICT PM) bid to secure the 25-year contract for South Africa’s largest port, Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2) was dealt a setback yesterday, after the Durban High Court imposed an injunction prohibiting state-owned Transnet from taking any further action to complete its award to ICT, until the second set of hearings is completed. This ruling came about after a losing bidder, Maersk, complained about irregularities in the bidding process. 

The issues cited by Maersk involved, chiefly, the use of ICTSI’s market capitalization in the computation of its solvency ratio. This supposedly overstated ICTSI’s financial capabilities. It was also revealed in the court filings that ICTSI’s financial bid itself was over 20% higher than Maersk’s.

The setback should induce a negative short-term reaction for the shares. However, we would reiterate that the acquisition of DCT-2 is not considered in our forecasts and valuation.  ICT also has not made any payments to secure the project.  However, we anticipate the market’s knee-jerk reaction would be negative, a reaction we would take as an opportunity to BUY.  Outside of the fact that valuations are still relatively inexpensive (9x FY25E EV/EBITDA, lower than peers and historical average of 11x), we believe there are several positive catalysts in the horizon:

  • Completion of the 250k TEU capacity expansion in the fully utilized market of Manzanillo, Mexico this month. Management indicated that there are three shipping lines bidding to take up 200k TEUs of this expansion. We believe the ongoing port congestion in Manzanillo also provides room for the company to hike tariffs further there.
  • Implementation of the second tranche (+6%) of MICT’s tariff hike in Feb 2025, after implementing the +10% hike in August.  
  • Revenues in Ecuador (a key underperformer in 1H24) will likely recover by 4Q24, after securing a new client.
  • Consensus forecasts are at least 14% lower than our estimates. We believe consensus has not factored in the impact of tariff hikes in Melbourne and Manila in July and in August. We estimate ICT’s 2H24 earnings will rebound by 55% YoY, stronger than consensus estimate of +22% and 1H24 profit growth of +26%.

Property broker Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) estimates that net office take-up was +68k sqm for 3Q, compared to over 200k in 2Q.  This was despite a net decline in occupancy by POGOs.  As a result, vacancy rates for office space stayed flat QoQ at 17% nationwide.

Vacancy rates are likely to rise in the coming periods as the online gambling operators previously called POGOs de-camp from their rented office spaces.

+++

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona says inflation is now on a “target-consistent” path. Remolona has indicated the BSP is likely to cut policy rates by 50bp by year-end and we don’t expect deviation from that.  The BSP Monetary Board will meet next week and the first 25bp reduction is likely then.

+++

The government’s infrastructure spending target under the proposed 2025 budget is virtually unchanged from this year’s (2024).  This implies government infra spending will be declining as a % of GDP next year as belts are still being tightened due to fiscal constraints. The economy will need to rely on private sector spending to get investment in infrastructure going.

TRANSPORT
Durban High Court issues injunction on award of DCT-2 to ICTSI
  • The High Court in Durban (KwaZulu-Natal High Court) has interdicted state-owned Transnet from taking any steps to implement its decision to award the 25-year concession for Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2) to ICTSI
    • The interdict will remain in place until the hearing of "Part B" of the court challenge brought by APM Terminals, a subsidiary of Maersk
    • Transnet and ICTSI were also ordered to pay APM Terminals' costs
  • APM Terminals argued that the bidding process was characterized by a litany of irregularities
    • ICTSI was allowed to use its market capitalization to calculate its solvency ratio, which bloated its ratio to 1.28. If it had used its annual FS, its ratio would have fallen short of the minimum 0.4 requirement
    • Transnet had argued that the solvency requirement was not mandatory and had been intended to simplify the proof of a financial capacity
    • Judge Mossop noted that Transnet had obtained two expert opinions which had both "in no uncertain terms" informed it that what ICTSI had done was not acceptable
  • Court documents revealed that ICTSI’s bid of ZAR11.1bn was ZAR2bn (~22%) higher than APM’s
    • Bid suggests that ICT valued the project at a 100% Enterprise Value of US$1.26bn, as ICT would have owned a stake of 50% minus 1 share of the project
    • We estimate the bid translates to an EV/EBITDA of ~17x, higher than ICT’s valuation of 8.8x FY25E EV/EBITDA
    • We estimate DCT-2 would have added US$211mn (6%) of revenues and US$74mn of EBITDA (3%) on a consolidated or 100% basis in the first full year of operation
    • Even though the deal appears to be dilutive at the onset, we believe there are numerous opportunities for value creation over the 25-year contract, particularly on efficiencies and tariffs
  • Our SOTP NAV of P470/sh and earnings forecasts do not incorporate DCT-2
    • ICT trades at 8.8x FY25E EV/EBITDA and 14.5x PER, lower than peer average (11x/16x) and historical average (11x/21x). Maintain Buy.
PROPERTY
LPC: Office take-up declines in 3Q24
  • During its 3Q24 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy was flat QoQ (at 17%)
    • Metro Manila office vacancy was also flat QoQ (at 16%)
    • We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels      
  • Nationwide net office take-up fell 75% QoQ (down 47% YoY) to 68,000sqm
    • BPO net office take-up fell 51% QoQ and YoY to 51,000sqm
    • Net office take-up from traditional tenants (including government agencies) declined 52% QoQ (up 400% YoY) to 70,000sqm
    • Meanwhile, POGO demand reversed from net take-up of 20,000sqm in 2Q24 to net closures of 53,000sqm
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 62% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, and 5.8X FY24E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY24E P/B, and 3.8X FY24E P/E
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 64% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY24E P/B, and 6.0X FY24E P/E

  • Note that the government projected expenses for debt service will rise by 27% YoY to P848bn

Inflation now on a ‘target-consistent path’ says Remolona
  • According to BSP Governor Eli M, Remolona, inflation is now on a “target-consistent path” which provides room for further easing
    • Governor Remolona stated that BSP now has room for a more calibrated shift to a less restrictive monetary policy stance.
  • Recall that Governor Remolona earlier said that the Monetary Board can cut by up to 25 bps at each of its last two meetings this year, slated on Oct. 16 and Dec. 19
  • BSP kicked off its easing cycle in August with a 25bp rate cut, bringing back the benchmark rate to 6.25% from an over 17-year high of 6.5%.
  • BSP chief noted that cutting ahead of the Fed did not have any significant impact on the financial system.

SEC clears P2.87bn Topline IPO
  • The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has approved the planned P2.87bn initial public offering (IPO) of Cebu-based fuel retailer Top Line Business Development Corp. (Topline)
    • The IPO will consist of up to 3.68 billion primary common shares at a maximum price of 78 centavos per share, and an overallotment option of 368.31 million secondary common shares
    • The company will not receive proceeds from the exercise of the overallotment option.
  • The offer period will be from Nov. 6 to 12, with listing on the main board of the Philippine Stock Exchange by Nov. 22
  • Topline aims to generate up to P2.75bn in net proceeds from the sale of primary offer shares, which will fund the construction of fuel depots and service stations, acquisition of fuel tankers and tank trucks, working capital, as well as for general corporate purposes
  • 73% of Topline is owned by Topline Equity Corp and the remaining 23% is owned by founder, Lapasaran Lim Family
  • Topline has two subsidiaries: 
    • Light Fuel Corporation: Operates gas pump station
    • Topline Logistics and Development Corp: Trading, distribution, and marketing of liquid fuel and other petroleum-based products, including the importation, transportation and storage thereof
