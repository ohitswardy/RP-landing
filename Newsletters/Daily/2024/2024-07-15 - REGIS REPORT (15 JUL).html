REGIS REPORT

15 July 2024

Total loans of the commercial banking were up 10.1% in May, accelerating from 9.6% in Apr.  Notably, consumer lending was up 25.6% (vs 25.3%).  Also of note was the growth in M3 which at 6.5% (vs 5.6%), which remains below nominal GDP growth.  Monetary expansion below nominal GDP growth suggests tight monetary conditions.

+++

Information released by state gaming agency PAGCOR showed gaming revenue (GGR) across the sector grew 9% QoQ (33% YoY) to P89.4bn   Gaming revenue for licensed casinos (P49.5bn, includes the integrated resorts) was virtually flat QoQ and the electronic gaming segment (P35.7bn) was the driver, growing 31% QoQ (+231% YoY).  E-gaming GGR is now not far from the GGR of the licensed casinos, and could, in fact, overtake the latter in two more quarters at the current pace.

Additionally, the data also suggests a relatively soft 2Q for the integrated resorts, which we had expected to show a rebound from 1Q24. More below.

+++

Vehicle sales for the CAMPI grouping amounted to 39k for the month of June, up 7.6% YoY and slightly (2.9%) down MoM.  Sales of Toyota-branded vehicles were up 10.5% but also 3% lower MoM.  This implies stable market share at ~46%.

+++

The latest Pulse Asia survey underscores the urgency for the Marcos government to act on food prices. Seventy-six percent (76%) of the population disapprove of the government’s handling of inflation and this is certainly the paramount issue for the general public.

The tariff cut on rice imports (from 35% to 15%) took effect in the first week of July and we expect retail prices to begin declining in the subsequent weeks.  Rice alone is 9% of the broad CPI and 18% is the CPI for the bottom 30% of the population. We expect a fall in rice prices to have a meaningful impact not only on CPI and thus monetary policy decisions, but also on consumer confidence and spending.

+++

Foreign ownership of the Philippine equities market tumbled 42bp to 20.1%. This is almost the lowest it’s been since such data has been compiled.  More granularity below.

BANKS
Commercial banks’ loan growth accelerates to 10.1% YoY in May; M3 growth accelerates to 6.5%
  • Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 10.1% YoY in May, accelerating from 9.6% YoY in April
  • Loans for production activities grew by 8.4% in May from 7.8% in April
    • Due to faster growth in loans for manufacturing (+10%), retail (+11%), transport (27%), and real estate (+13%)
  • Consumer loan grew at a steady pace of 25.6% YoY, with credit card loans up 29% YoY and auto loans up 21% YoY
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth accelerated to 6.5% YoY in May from 5.6% YoY in April and was the fastest growth rate since November 2023’s 7.0%

 

CONSUMER
New vehicle sales rise 7.6% YoY in June; Toyota sales up 10.5% YoY
  • CAMPI reported industry-wide car sales rose 7.6% YoY to 39,088 units in June, mildly accelerating from May’s 5.5% YoY pace
    • On a MoM basis, industry car sales were down 2.9%
    • Brings 1H24 industry car sales to 226,279 units, up 11.8% YoY
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) cornered 46.1% of the market, selling 104,350 units in 1H24, up 11.5% YoY
    • For the month of June alone, TMP sold 18,093 units, up 10.5% YoY but down 3.1% MoM
    • To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.4k units for the rest of the year
    • To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 17k units for the rest of the year
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses)
  • GTCAP trades at a 51% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,214 (vs. historical discount to NAV of 14%) and 5.4x FY24E PER

 

CONSUMER
Illegally imported Century Pacific, San Miguel food products subject to USDA public health alert
  • The US Department of Agriculture Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) has issued a public health alert on illegally imported Filipino ready-to-eat food products
    • Products involved are cans of Argentina Corned Beef (Century Pacific Food [CNPF PM]), Purefoods Corned Beef (San Miguel Food & Beverage [FB PM]), Chunkee Corned Beef, and jars of Lady’s Choice Chicken Spread
  •   While there are no confirmed reports of adverse reactions due to consuming these products, the warning was issued as the products do not bear any producing establishment information
    • The Philippines is not eligible to export meat and poultry products to the United States
    • According to FSIS rules, the entry of small quantities of mealt, poultry, and egg products for personal consumption is permitted subject to its regulatory requirements
  • CNPF stated that its products are sourced from countries qualified by the USDA, including Brazil, Australia, and the US
    • Branded exports account for less than 10% of total sales
  • CNPF currently trades at 17x 2024E PE, while FB trades at 12x 2024E PE
GAMING
2Q24 GGR of licensed casinos stays flat QoQ; e-games GGR grows 30% QoQ
  • PAGCOR reported that gross gaming revenue (GGR) in the Philippines grew 9% QoQ (+33% YoY) to P89.4bn in 2Q24
    • GGR of licensed casinos (including integrated resorts) was flat QoQ (-4% YoY) at P49.5bn
    • GGR of bingo and electronic games grew 31% QoQ and 231% YoY to P35.7bn
    • GGR of PAGCOR operated casinos fell 11% QoQ and 15% YoY to P4.2bn
  • Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) is trading at 7.2X FY24E EV/EBITDA
  • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is trading at 8.7X FY24E P/E (based on consensus forecast)
CONGLOS
SM’s geothermal unit starts exploration for new sources
  • The Philippine Geothermal Production Co., Inc. (PGPC) said it has started the exploration and development of new geothermal energy sources in various parts of Luzon
  • It aims to establish fully operational geothermal fields capable of generating between 250-400 MW of additional renewable baseload capacity
    • Projects are slated for completion within the next five to seven years
    • PGPC currently operates the Tiwi field in Albay and the Makiling-Banahaw (Mak-Ban) steam field spanning Laguna and Batangas
  • PGPC is a wholly-owned unit of SM Investments (SM PM) and accounts for 5% of its market-based NAV of P945/sh
    • SM trades at a 7% discount to its market-based NAV (vs. historical average of 3%)
CONGLOS
Maynilad plans IPO in 2025
  • Maynilad plans to conduct an IPO as soon as 2025
    • According to CEO Ramoncito Fernandez
  • The company recently issued P15bn worth of blue “sustainability” bonds due in 2029 and 2034 with interest rates of 6.7092% and 7.0931%, respectively
    • Bond issuance will finance its capex of P30bn this year
    • Maynilad issued P10bn of bonds early this year
    • As of end-2023, Maynilad’s net debt stood at P57bn, translating to a net debt-to-equity of 83% and net-debt-to-trailing EBITDA of 3.46x
  • Under its water franchise, Maynilad is required to issue at least 20% of its shares to the public on or before January 2027
    • Maynilad is 53%-owned by Metro Pacific, 25% by DMCI (DMC PM), and 20% by Japan-based Marubeni
    • Maynilad accounts for 24% of DMCI’s market-based NAV of P13.20/sh; DMC trades at a 16% discount to NAV (in line with historical average of 15%)
OTHERS
Pulse Asia survey shows 76% of Filipinos disapprove of government’s performance on inflation
  • 76% of Filipinos (from 71% in 1Q24 survey) disapproved of how the government handled inflation
  • Only 5% of respondents gave their approval, while 19% were undecided
  • The same poll revealed that the government’s attempts at reducing poverty had the second-highest disapproval percentage (51%, from 1Q24’s 42%)
  • Inflation levels are the only concern that majority (57%) of Filipinos want to hear from the President during his SONA on July 22
    • Inflation levels are also the issue that 72% of Filipinos find most urgent
    • Other issues are territorial defense (5%) and charter change (1%)
OTHERS
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in June
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 42bps MoM to 20.1% (in June) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index declined 22bps MoM to 24.7%
  • Net foreign selling slowed to US$104mn, from US$174mn (net selling) in May
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 8 saw increases in foreign ownership while 22 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • AEV – foreign ownership fell 2.36 percentage points MoM to 3.2%. It was removed from the MSCI Index at the end of May, but it appears the trades related to this were only reflected in June. AEV saw net foreign selling of US$71mn in May (the highest for the month) and US$20mn in June (the second highest for the month). 
    • WLCON – we estimate that foreign ownership declined 70bps MoM to 16.0%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$10.4mn (the fifth highest for the month).
    • ACEN – foreign ownership increased 24bps MoM to 25.2%. Net foreign buying reached US$5mn, the fourth highest for the month.
