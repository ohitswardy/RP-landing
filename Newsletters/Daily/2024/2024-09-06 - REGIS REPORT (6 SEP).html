REGIS REPORT

06 September 2024

Consumer loans surged 19.7% in 2Q24, accelerating from the 17.6% pace, and clocking in the fastest YoY growth rate since end-2016. As a result, consumer loans now account for a record-high 21.8% of total loans. Meanwhile, overall NPL ratio was flat, inching up 2bps QoQ to 5.82%. But, as they say, it’s complicated. To the upside, big ticket loans – mortgages and car loans – saw sharply accelerating growth trends and improving NPLs. On the downside, unsecured products – motorcycle loans, credit cards, and salary loans – saw either slowdowns or even YoY contractions, with NPLs deteriorating. Might this be a further sign of the “K-shaped” recovery?

Growing the consumer book will be crucial as banks defend NIMs as interest rates fall, while managing credit costs. Among the banks we cover, Security Bank (SECB PM) has the highest share of consumer/MSME loans, at 32% to total. Other banks that have large consumer exposure include Union Bank (UBP PM) and East West (EW PM).

The growth in mortgages would also be positive for the property sector, and the growth in car loans would benefit GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) Toyota business.

+++

Headline CPI inflation dropped to 3.3% in Aug (vs 4.4% in July) and we expect this will not be the last such decline.  The Sept print is likely to be even lower and could, in fact, drop below 3%.  This fall in headline CPI inflation (a YoY figure) is attributable more to base effects (i.e. a higher CPI base of comparison the previous year) than to any appreciable decline in consumer prices. It is thus, admittedly, a relatively backward-looking measure. But it is a measure that the Bangko Sentral (BSP) relies on in determining monetary policy.

For the general public, however, only an absolute decline in food prices (especially rice) will matter.  A drop in prices (not just a cosmetic decline in YoY inflation stats) should power a rebound in consumer confidence and spending. The Dept of Agriculture (DA) is in a position to have an immediate impact here through a speedier, open, transparent and equitable import permit issuance process.

+++

Century Pacific (CNPF PM) is acquiring a coconut processing firm for US$40m to increase its own capacity by 30%.  The coconut segment is around 10%+ of CNPF’s revenue and utilization is already maxed out.  This investment is already part of the programmed P5bn capex for the year.

+++

The chair of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) Monalisa Dimalanta has been suspended by the Ombudsman, the top anti-graft official in the government.  The supposed reason for the suspension? Allowing Meralco (MER PM) to purchase power from the spot market and pass on these costs to consumers, and for causing delays to the rate review of Meralco.

These supposed “reasons” seem a little … shallow.

Dimalanta was one of three members of the ERC who tried but failed to stop San Miguel (SMC PM) and Meralco from passing on higher fuel costs to consumers despite a fixed price contract.

MACRO
Inflation eases to 3.3% in August
  • Headline inflation eased to 3.3% in August from 4.4% in July
    • This was below consensus forecast of 3.6% and came in at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.2%-4.0%
    • Slowdown due largely to (a) favorable base effects on rice CPI, (b) slower price increases/negative base effects in other food items such as meat, fish, vegetables, and sugar, and (c) lower fuel prices, which drove transport inflation lower
    • These items more than compensated for the upticks in electricity prices
  • Core inflation also eased to 2.6% in August from 2.9% in July, with the “non-food” core inflation moderating to 3.0% from 3.3%
  • Brings 8M24 average headline inflation rate to 3.6%, within the BSP’s range of 2-4%
  • BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. previously signaled for another 25bps cut in the policy rate this year, after implementing a 25bps cut last August
    • Monetary Board’s next meeting is schedule on Oct 17

 

BANKS
Consumer loans jump 20% YoY in 2Q24; at a record-high 22% of total loans

Consumer loans in 2Q24 surged 19.7% YoY to P2,807bn, accelerating from the already-heady 17.6% pace in the previous quarter, and the fastest YoY clip since 4Q2016. On a QoQ basis, consumer loans expanded 4.2% vs 1Q24. The growth in consumer loans far exceeded the 10.3% YoY pace of total banking sector loans, suggesting that corporate loans grew 7.9% YoY in 2Q24. As a result, consumer loans now account for 21.8% of total, a new all-time high.

 

Asset quality deteriorated slightly, with NPL ratio up 2bps QoQ to 5.82%, but still down 43bps YoY. Interestingly, the NPL slip was much more benign than overall loans, which saw NPL ratio jump 19bps QoQ to 3.69%. We estimate that non-consumer loan NPLs were up 23bps QoQ to 3.10%. In nominal terms, consumer NPLs grew 4.6% QoQ, while non-consumer loans jumped 10.4% QoQ.

 

  • Mortgages grew 13.5% YoY to P1,039bn, accelerating sharply from the 7.9% pace in 1Q24, and posting the fastest growth since 3Q2018. Meanwhile, mortgage NPL ratio improved further to 6.72%, down 22bps QoQ and 90bps YoY. In PHP terms, NPLs fell 1.1% QoQ to P69.8bn
  • Motor Vehicle (MV) loans also sped up, growing 18.7% YoY to P557bn (was +17.6% in 1Q24, and fastest since 1Q2018). With MVs, auto loans surged 20.3% YoY to P526bn, while motorcycle loans contracted 3.9% YoY to P31bn. Asset quality improved dramatically with NPL ratio down 27bps QoQ to 5.72%. The drop was driven by auto loans (5.08% vs 5.36% in 1Q), while motorcycle NPLs blipped (16.57% vs 16.10% in 1Q).
  • Credit card receivable growth slowed slightly, up 28.8% to P797bn (vs 30.0% in 1Q). To the downside, NPL ratio jumped 43bps QoQ to 4.56%. To be fair, some banks (notably BPI) have consciously eased their risk metrics, contending that credit card yields are high enough to warrant higher credit cost.
  • Salary loans grew 27.2% YoY to P366bn, though this was a slowdown from the 29.3% in 1Q24 and 54.5% pace in 2Q23. NPL ratios jumped 55bps QoQ to 5.94%.

 

The continued acceleration of consumer loans should help banks defend NIM compression as benchmark interest rates fall. We are slightly wary though of rising NPLs, particularly in the credit card and salary loan categories, as these could indicate increased stress among households.

 

Of the banks we cover, Security Bank (SECB PM) has the highest proportion of consumer/MSME loans at 32% to total, which is reflected in higher average loan yields compared to the Big Three.

  • The Office of the Ombudsman ordered the six-month preventive suspension of Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) Chair Monalisa Dimalanta
    • Due to alleged neglect of duty in connection with a complaint filed by consumer group, National Association of Electricity Consumers for Reforms, Inc. (Nasecore)
    • Nasecore complained about the ERC’s failure “to recalculate the rate of Meralco that protects the interest of the public”
