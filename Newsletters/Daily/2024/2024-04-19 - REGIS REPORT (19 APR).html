REGIS REPORT

19 April 2024

BDO (BDO PM) kicks off the 1Q24 reporting season, reporting headline NPAT of P18.5bn, up 12% YoY, broadly in line with Regis (FY24E P79.6bn) and consensus (P80.6bn) forecasts. What stood out from the limited press release was that customer loan growth accelerated to 13% YoY, compared to 9% in 1Q23, and faster than our FYE of 12%. Asset quality was stable-ish, with NPL ratio at 1.88% (vs 1.85% in 4Q) and NPL cover at 181% (vs 185%). No data yet on NIMs; however, the disclosed NII growth of 13% would be flat QoQ, suggesting NIMs could be flat to slightly down QoQ. We expect full FS later this morning, with their briefing set for Monday 22 April.

On the other hand, Puregold (PGOLD PM) finally held its earnings call to shed more light on its previously announced FY/4Q23 results.  Margins for the last quarter were crimped by competitive pressures on multiple fronts. The core Puregold brand has started price-matching hard discounters (e.g. Dali), while S&R continues to defend market share against Landers. Management expects promotional activities to taper in the coming quarters, but we believe the competitive pressures could persist in the near term.  More from Carissa (carissa.mangubat@regis.ph) here:

On the gaming front, Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) chair Enrique Razon announced at the company’s AGM that the firm’s new casino will open on May 25. The new integrated resort will add 35% to BLOOM’s table capacity and 110% to its slot capacity.

Meanwhile, rival casino operator Okada Manila revealed that its 1Q24 gross gaming revenue (GGR) fell 24% to P8.78bn, as VIPGGR fell 43% (due to a decline in win rate). We could see a trend of YoY declines in GGR for the other operators as well, particularly since Solaire and Newport Resorts are coming off high bases in 1Q23.

+++

ACEN (ACEN PM) revealed it was collaborating with the Rockefeller Foundation on the first "Coal to Clean Credit Initiative (CCCI)”.  The proposal will hasten the closure (by 2030, from 2040) of the 270MW SLTEC coal plant in Batangas, replacing it with RE + batteries.  Nominally, ACEN no longer has coal exposure via SLTEC even though it still captures, through complicated arrangements, bulk of the economic benefits of SLTEC’s coal-fired generation facilities.  This initiative would align reality with what’s on paper by 2030.

+++

The Department of Agriculture (DA) is proposing a 2025 budget that’s 2.5x bigger than this year’s to spend on infrastructure for the sector.  Assuming the administration indeed pushes through with this reinvigorated investment in agriculture (executed properly), we see this as a very positive move.  Agriculture in the Philippine has not kept up with the rest of the world and this is arguably one of the key reasons why the Philippines is less competitive in many other industries because of its impact on cost of living and wages.

GAMING
Okada Manila GGR falls 24% in 1Q24
  • Tiger Resort Leisure and Entertainment, the operator of Okada Manila, saw a 24% YoY drop in 1Q24 GGR to P8.78bn
  • VIP GGR fell 43% to P2.59bn
    • VIP win rate fell from 3.4% to 2.8%
  • Mass table revenue declined 6% to P2.96bn
    • Mass table win rate rose from 26.3% to 27.9%
  • Slot revenue fell 17% to P3.23bn
  • The total number of visitors was flat at 1.46mn

AGRICULTURE
Department of Agriculture to propose P514bn infra-heavy budget for 2025
  • The Department of Agriculture (DA) said it will propose a P514bn budget for 2025
    • This would be more than double the agency’s budget for 2024 of P209bn
    • Agriculture Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel, Jr. said that the funds will support the construction of irrigation and postharvest facilities
  • P514bn budget will be allocated to the following, which will broadly modernize the fisheries and farm sectors, and address food safety and anti-smuggling efforts:
    • P294bn for rice (largely for irrigation of 1.2mn hectares to boost production and reduce imports
    • P50.6bn for fisheries
    • P34.5bn for cross-cutting programs
    • P32bn for high value crops
    • P29bn for livestock,
    • P14bn for foreign-assisted projects
    • P11bn for corn
    • P3bn for credit programs
UTILITIES
Manila Water acquires 70% stake in Equipacific
  • Manila Water Philippine Ventures is acquiring a 70% stake in Equipacific Holdco, Inc. (EHI) for P1.15bn
    • Sellers are Equi-Parco Hldgs Corp. (30%), Metropac Water Investments Corp. (30%), and TwinPeak Hydro Resources Corp. (10%)
    • Customary conditions precedent for transactions of similar nature (e.g., submission of corporate approvals and other third-party consents)
    • P920mn will be paid upon closing and the balance will be paid within six months after the closing date
  • EHI is the investment company that holds a 90% stake in Laguna Water District Aquatech Resources (LARC)
    • LARC is a joint venture with the Laguna local government that operates water systems for five towns in Laguna (Los Baños, Bay, Calauan, Victoria, and Nagcarlan)
    • Details on LARC’s operations were not disclosed
    • Acquisition expands MWC’s presence in Laguna; it currently handles the water distribution for three towns (Sta Rosa, Biñan, Cabuyao)
CONGLOS
Ayala streamlines logistics business
  • Ayala Corp. is integrating Entrego’s business and operations with the AIR21 group to maximize asset utilization and create greater efficiencies
    • Since the start of the year, Entrego’s courier, express and parcel services were subcontracted by AIR21
    • Entrego, which began in 2018, officially stopped its services on April 1
    • AIR21 was acquired by AC Logistics in August 2022 initially for a 60% ownership stake; It raised its stake to 80% in March 2023
  • AC trades at a 32% discount to its market-based NAV of P836/sh
POWER
ACEN, Rockefeller Foundation plans first CCCI pilot project
  • ACEN Corp. and the Rockefeller Foundation are collaborating on the first "Coal to Clean Credit Initiative (CCCI)" which involves replacing a coal plant with clean energy
  • Pilot project aims to decommission the 246MW South Luzon Energy Corp. (SLTEC) coal plant in 2030, ahead of its supposed retirement by 2040, and replace it with clean power and battery storage
  • ACEN is coordinating with SLTEC's owners for the early closure of the plant and aims to finalize the project design document in 2024, and is also finalizing buyer discussions for the CCCI
