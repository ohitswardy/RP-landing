REGIS REPORT

29 November 2024

The DMCI (DMC PM) group’s tender offer for Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) resulted in barely anyone selling to the former.  Only 12.9m shares, equivalent to <0.1% of CHP’s outstanding shares, were submitted.  This would result in the public float dropping to 10.005% (from 10.1%).

+++

For the nth time, media reported that Mynt (the operator of GCash) is contemplating an IPO, this time by the latter part of next year (FY25). What’s possibly different is that the reports say that Mynt will not be pursuing NASDAQ dual listing but would rather list only in the Philippines.

We don’t think there is any urgency for Mynt to list after two back-to-back transactions recently. New money was infused and some of its shares held by PE investors were bought by strategic partners. An IPO next year will likely come only if a respectable valuation can be achieved.

Globe (GLO PM) holds 34% of Mynt while Ayala Corp (AC PM) holds ~6.5%.

+++

Digital transactions done through the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) in the month of Oct were up 40% YoY to P1.6trn.  This is an acceleration from the +33% in Sept and points to continued adoption of digital payment platforms over traditional methods (cash / cheques).

+++

The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) says that the government’s debt/GDP will remain elevated and is unlikely to return to pre-pandemic levels in the medium term. The government’s own target is for debt/GDP to dip to 56% (vs 60% as of end-2023).  That ratio fell to as low as 39.6% in 2019 but blew up to 61% over the course of the pandemic.

DIGITAL FINANCE/TELCOS
October Digital payments surge 40% to P1.6trn a new record high

Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) surged 40.3% YoY in October, accelerating sharply from the 33% in September, and 29% in August. Combined PesoNet and InstaPay payments also jumped 10% MoM to P1,620bn (roughly $27.5bn), a new all-time high. Both platforms – Instapay and PesoNet – also hit record highs for the month. For the 10M24 period, digital transactions hit P13,989bn (c$218bn), growing 35% YoY, faster than the 29% pace in FY2023.

Real-time, small-ticket transactions over InstaPay amounted to P676bn, up 48.0% YoY and 9% MoM, posting the fastest monthly YoY growth since February this year. This took the year-to-date total to P5,820bn, up 45.3% YoY, and faster than FY23’s 42% growth . By number of transactions, InstaPay volumes grew 53% YoY. While still quite impressive, this was much slower than the 67% YoY pace the prior month.

For larger payments over PesoNet, the October figure surged 35.3% YoY and 11% MoM to P944bn. The pace of growth was markedly faster than the 26% in September, and 20% in August. The 10-month total stood at P8,168bn, up 27.9% YoY, compared to the 22% growth in FY23.

Adoption could improve further if the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) succeeds in eliminating all P2P (person-to-person) transaction costs. At present, banks and wallets charge as high as P25 (about $0.42) per transaction over InstaPay, or about 0.5% of the average InstaPay payment of about P5,250 in 10M24. Bringing the fee to zero would encourage even lower-ticket payments to be done digitally.
