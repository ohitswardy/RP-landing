REGIS REPORT

27 December 2024

Despite the drop in the international price of rice as well as the reduction in import tariffs (from 35% to 15%), domestic rice prices have only seen a moderate decline. Thus, Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. is looking for ways to bring the price lower, such as by having the government procure rice and compete with traders and by charging "unscrupulous" rice traders and importers with profiteeting. 

Lower rice prices would benefit consumer stocks, as the staple comprises 18% of the CPI basket of the "bottom 30%" of households.

The government's budget deficit grew 130% YoY to P213bn in November. Revenue collections were flat YoY while expenditures rose 27% YoY (18% MoM). The YT-November budget deficit grew 6% YoY to 1.2tn, trailing behind the government government's full-year target of P1.47trn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

One Duterte ally (Senator Christopher "Bong" Go) will also likely be among the 12, while another ally (Senator Ronald "Bato" dela Rosa) still has a fighting chance.

MACRO
Government records budget deficit of P213bn in November
  • The government recorded a budget deficit of P213bn in November, 130% higher than the deficit of P93bn in Nov 2023
  • Revenue collections in November were flat YoY (-0.6%), with tax revenues up 13% YoY but offset by the significant decline in non-tax revenues of 68%
    • BIR collections rose 18% YoY in November, accelerating from the 14% pace in 10M24 due to higher collections of VAT, personal/corporate income taxes, documentary stamp tax, excise taxes
    • Customs collections dropped slightly by 2% YoY in Nov, vs. the 5% growth in 10M24
  • Expenditures accelerated 27% in November as both significant primary expenses and interest payments were incurred in the month
    • Primary spending rose 26% YoY, faster than the 10% pace in 10M24
      • The continued increase in primary spending was attributed to the (a) larger tax allotments disbursed to LGUs (local government units) (b) higher spend on road, defense infra, and social/educational projects, (c) personnel services requirements
    • Interest payments are up 37% as coupon payments were made to domestic debt and global bond issuances alongside forex fluctuations
  • Bringing YT-November budget deficit to 1.2tn, higher by 6% YoY
    • The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP
    • As of 9M24, the budget deficit-to-GDP ratio stood at 5.14%

 

MACRO
BIR and BOC target P4.26bn revenue collection target for 2025 amid tax reforms
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and Bureau of Customs (BOC) are aiming for double-digit increases in their revenue targets for 2025
    • The BIR's revenue goal increased to P3.23tn from P2.85tn in 2024 (+13% YoY)
    • While for the BOC it increased to P1.06tn from P930.60bn (+13% YoY)
  • For the year 2024, the BIR is on track to achieve its target, while the BOC is running slightly behind
  • The revenue targets are to be supported by several key tax measures such as 
    • Excise taxes on single use plastics, mining fiscal regime rationalization, Motor Vehicle Users' Charge reforms

 

MACRO/INFRA
Infrastructure spending rises 11% YoY in 10M24
  • Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 11% YoY to P1.28tn in 10M24
    • Government raised its target to P1.54tn, up 8.5% YoY (from P1.47tn, up 4%) and equivalent to 5.8% of GDP
    • Cited the accelerated implementation of infrastructure activities both from ongoing and carryover projects of the DPWH and foreign-assisted railway projects of the DOTr, including those financed from prior year’s unprogrammed appropriations
  • For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP

 

MACRO
Agriculture Secretary to clamp down on rice traders over price gouging
  • Rice prices have barely declined even though world market prices have fallen and tariffs were reduced from 35% to 15%
  • Thus, Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. is examining multiple options against unscrupulous rice traders
    • The Department of Agriculture will examine whether provisions of the Consumer Price Act can be used to charge unscrupulous traders and importers with profiteering
    • It is also exploring the possibility of invoking the food security emergency provision under the Rice Tariffication Law, which would allow the National Food Authority (NFA) to release its buffer stocks in order to stabilize prices
    • Another option is for the Food Terminal Inc. (FTI) to procure rice and directly compete with traders and importers
  • The Department of Agriculture also issued a new regulation prohibiting the labeling of imported rice as “special” or “premium”
    • Secretary Tiu Laurel said that some retailers and traders are misleading Filipino consumers by labeling imports as premium or special, thereby justifying high rice prices

 

POLITICS
Pulse Asia senatorial pre-election preference polls see Tulfo, Sotto at the top
  • Based on the survey conducted end-November, 14 Senate candidates have a statistical chance of winning any of the 12 seats that are available for contention
    • Erwin Tulfo led with 62.2%, followed by Sotto at 59.2%, and Bong Go and Ben Tulfo with 54.7% and 52.7%, respectively—taking the four top spots.
    • Pia Cayetano (46.5%), Manny Pacquiao (45%), and Panfilo Lacson (44.1%) were among those ranking in the fifth to tenth range.
    • Abby Binay (40.1%), Lito Lapid (39.2%), Imee Marcos (37.5%), Bato Dela Rosa (36.9%), and Camille Villar (36.5%) were positioned between seventh and 15th places.
  • Notably, only one of Duterte's allies, Senator Bong Go holds a winning ranking range of rank 3-4 vs. Sen. Bato Dela Rosa rank of 10-15 which may fall outside of the top 12. While the other four PDP Laban Senate Candidates are out of contention with low rankings
  • This was Pulse Asia's first non-commissioned survey after the filing of certificates of candidacy last October
  • The 2025 Philippine Midterm Elections will be held on 12 May 2025
