REGIS REPORT

23 July 2024

President Marcos delivered his State of the Nation Address (SONA) yesterday in front of the government’s assembled officialdom, which included the Cabinet and members of both Houses of Congress. The SONA, the Philippine equivalent of the State of the Union Address of the US, is part legislative / policy agenda (akin to the King’s Speech in the UK) but still 100% political theater.

Among the key priorities Marcos outlined were (1) ongoing agricultural programs to reduce food prices, (2) proposed changes to the power sector law (“EPIRA”), (3) healthcare programs and (4) “continuous’ implementation of infrastructure projects.  The proposed changes to EPIRA could, in our view, pose some risk to some of the power companies, including Meralco (MER PM).

He reserved the latter (last) part for two topics which elicited by far, the biggest reactions, most likely be design. It’s notable that this administration’s policies on these particular issues are directly contradictory to those promoted by the previous government:

  • He reiterated his administration’s stance on the South China Sea (West Philippine Sea), expressing the aim of pursuing a peaceful solution to the competing claims without giving up the country’s rights. The previous government pursued a policy that can arguably be described as appeasement. Frankly, it looks like the new government policy aims will be difficult if not impossible under the current environment.
  • Marcos announced he was banning POGOs, the offshore gaming firms that have mushroomed in the country, and whose operations have dominated recent headlines because of the criminal activities associated with them. The previous government conferred official recognition on POGOs in exchange for a share of revenue of their gaming activities. Marcos’ policy reversal would have significant repercussions on the presence of foreign nationals (mostly from China) and demand for property, particularly in certain locations.

+++

Undoubtedly the most attention-grabbing portion of President Marcos’ SONA was his announcement that he would ban the POGO sector immediately. He would, however, allow existing POGO firms until the end of the year to cease operations.

We expect this ban to be detrimental to the Metro Manila office market and to all forms of property in the Manila Bay area.

POGO firms occupy about 6% of Metro Manila office stock and 30% of Manila Bay area office space. Thus, a ban on the POGO sector should result in Metro Manila office vacancy rising to 22% (from 16% currently) and Manila Bay area office vacancy soaring to about 50%. In such a scenario, it would be reasonable for office rental rates to fall c. 10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area.  POGO firms employ over 100,000 foreign nationals (our estimate), a large portion of whom currently reside in the Manila Bay area. Their departure could lead to significant drops in Manila Bay area property prices.

Regardless of the likely knee-jerk reaction, the annual profit hit for the developers should be manageable at less than 5%, based on our estimates.

Counterintuitively, SM Prime (SMPH PM) will likely take larger cut to its NAV than its office exposure might suggest. If Manila Bay area property prices fall 20%, we estimate that SM Prime’s NAV will drop by 5%-10%, with the drop largely coming from the Pasay Coastal Development project.

In addition to the property companies, casinos in PAGCOR Entertainment City will be negatively affected by a POGO ban. About 20% of the GGR of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) comes from Chinese citizens living in the Philippines, some of whom are likely POGO employees. This number should drop soon with the recent opening of Solaire Resort North.

+++

Vista Land (VLL PM) is issuing US$300m of bonds.  What’s sure to raise eyebrows is the indicative yield for these planned five-year bonds: 9.5%. This is ~500bp above US Treasuries and ~400bp above the recent yields of Philippine corporate US$ issues with similar tenors.

MACRO
Highlights from Pres. Marcos’ 3rd SONA
  • In his latest State of the Nation Address (SONA), President Marcos highlighted the following:
  • His administration’s ongoing agriculture programs, which ultimately aim to lower prices of food
    • Implementation of temporary mandated price ceilings on rice
    • Reduced tariff rates to facilitate importation of rice, corn, and pork until the end of 2024
      • Infrastructure to support distribution of agricultural products
      • Local Government Units (LGUs) no longer charge fees for those transporting goods through local roads
    • Over P9bn worth of compensation was given to farmers and fishermen as insurance from El Niño
      • The government is preparing to roll out vaccines against swine fever
  • Urged Congress to pass amendments to EPIRA law, which could bring down electricity prices
    • Mr. Marcos said that his administration is addressing power shortages and has supported investments in energy to mitigate power outages and connectivity issues
    • More energy projects are expected to be completed which would increase the nation’s power supply
  • Healthcare was also one of his priorities, pushing for enhanced medical and allied health programs, scholarships, reviews, and upskilling programs
  • Continuous implementation of infrastructure projects
    • Gov’t targeting to implement 350 airport, seaport projects by end of term, the largest of which is the privatization of the main gateway, NAIA
  • Ordered total ban on POGOs; Target is to cease all POGO activity by year-end
    • The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) is expected to help Filipinos affected by the banning of POGOs look for new jobs
  • On the conflict with China, he wants to pursue a peaceful solution without giving up rights to the West Philippine Sea
    • There are two proposed laws: (1) define the country’s maritime areas and (2) define archipelagic sea to designate the sea areas and air space for foreign vessels and aircrafts to pass
    • The Maritime Zones Act has been approved at the bilateral committee level and is pending before both chambers of the Congress
    • The Archipelagic Sea Lanes bill has been approved by the House and is pending before the Senate
    • A day before the SONA, the Philippines announced it had reached an arrangement with China on Ayungin Shoal with the hopes of bringing tensions down and avoiding confrontations
PROPERTY/GAMING
President Marcos to ban offshore gaming  
  • During his State of the Nation Address, President Marcos said he would ban offshore gaming
  • Existing POGO firms have until the end of the year to cease operations
  • We reckon that a ban on offshore gaming would be detrimental to the Metro Manila office market and to all forms of property in the Manila Bay area
  • We estimate that POGO firms occupy c. 900,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila (about 6% of office stock)
    • We estimate that POGO firms employ over 100,000 foreign nationals who will soon vacate the Metro Manila business districts
  • However, POGO firms are concentrated in the Manila Bay area and we estimate they occupy over 30% of office stock in that vicinity
    • Thus, a ban on offshore gaming could raise Metro Manila office vacancy to 22% (from 16% currently) and Manila Bay area office vacancy to c. 50% (from c. 20% currently)
  • We estimate that office rental rates could fall c. 10% in most parts of Metro Manila and c. 20% in the Manila Bay area. Prices of other Manila Bay area properties (such as land) are likely to fall 10%-20%.
  • Based on our initial estimates, the annual profit hit for the developers we cover should be within 5% over the next few years
    • While POGO firms should vacate their offices quickly, negative rental reversions from contract renewals of other tenants will be staggered
    • Megaworld would likely take the biggest hit to profit from a drop in office earnings  
  • Unintuitively, SM Prime might take a larger hit to its NAV than its office exposure might suggest
    • The Manila Bay reclamation comprises 27% of our SMPH NAV estimate. Thus, a 20% drop in Manila Bay land prices would result in a 5%-10% reduction in SM Prime’s NAV
