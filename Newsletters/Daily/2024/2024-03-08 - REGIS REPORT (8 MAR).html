We cover a few results this morning, leading off with PLDT (TEL PM). The telco revealed results which, depending on who you ask, could be described as a glass half full or half empty. DMCI (DMC PM) numbers, meanwhile, were below estimates but the impending dividend is probably what investors are really watching out for.  Finally, small cap Filinvest (FLI PM) provides another piece to the puzzle of residential presales demand.  Check out our comments:

The Senate passed on second reading the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act. Property valuations used for property tax purposes are 10-20 years out of date in many areas. Understandably, the government wants these values updated more regularly (by forming a new national government agency). Thus, the Bill could raise real property taxes and would likely be most negative for developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land (ALI PM), Filinvest Land (FLI PM), and Vista Land (VLL PM).  

+++

Despite having already delisted, Metro Pacific invited a few analysts to a briefing on what’s going on with the group.  While its own shares are no longer publicly available, its plans and projects offer more than just interesting read as they provide insight to what’s going with the group that may have bearing on the business environment and other listed companies.

BANKS
Consumer loans up 17% in 2023; NPLs drop QoQ/YoY

Philippine banks’ consumer loans grew 17% YoY in 2023 to P2,614bn, accelerating from the 16.5% in 3Q23. This was significantly faster than total loans, which grew 7.4% YoY (up from 6.8% in 3Q23. This suggests that non-consumer loans (corporate and commercial) grew just 5.1%, though this was also faster than the 4.5% in 3Q23. Consumer loans maintained its 21.0% share of total loans (flat QoQ).

Meanwhile, the NPL ratio of consumer loans improved to 5.77%, down 24bps QoQ and 77bps QoQ. Overall NPLs were down 12 bps QoQ to 3.43% (up 16bps YoY), suggesting NPLs for corporate/commercial loans was 2.80%, down 9bps QoQ but up 31bps YoY.

By segment:

  • Mortgages grew 7.0% YoY, up from 6.0% in 3Q23, crossing the P1trn mark for the first time. NPL ratio fell to 6.93%, down 17bps QoQ and 62bps YoY. The NPL ratio remains high likely due to banks not writing off mortgages, given the high collateral values. Both the growth and the improving asset quality are impressive, in our view, given the sharp rise in mortgage rates since 2022.
  • Motor Vehicle loans jumped 15.1% YoY, up sharply from the 12.6% in 3Q, and marking 9 consecutive quarters of accelerating loan growth. Despite that, balances were still 15% lower compared to pre-Covid levels, indicating further upside potential. NPLs improved sharply too to 6.29%, down 67bps QoQ and 215bps YoY. Within the segment, 4-wheelers grew even faster, up 17.1% YoY. Motorcycle loans shrank, down 8% YoY, with NPLs at a high 16.4%.
  • Credit card receivables continued to surge 30.1% YoY, just a shade slower than the 30.4% clocked in 3Q23. NPL ratio dipped to 3.97%, from 4.10% in 3Q23. We suspect a good portion of the improvement was due to write-offs.
  • Salary loans were up 26% YoY to P334bn, slowing from the 29.7% pace in 3Q23. NPL ratio jumped 20bps QoQ to 5.12%

The continued strength in consumer loans bodes well for banks’ NIMs, which are likely to come under downward pressure once benchmark interest rates fall. Meanwhile, the accelerating pace of auto loans is positive for GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM, Buy) Toyota business.

 

CONGLOS
Key takeaways from MPI briefing
  • LNG deal valuation breakdown.  Mr. Pangilinan revealed that the disclosed US$3.3bn valuation for San Miguel’s (SMC PM) natural gas plants and Linseed Field Corp’s LNG import and regasification terminal represents 100% combined equity values for these assets.  The 2,598 MW gas plants were valued at approximately US$1mn per MW while the regasification terminal was valued at ~US$550mn. The transaction is expected to be completed in June or July. 

 

  • Leveraged buyout.  Meralco’s (MER PM) wholly-owned unit, Meralco PowerGen Corporation (MGEN), will end up owning 40% of these assets.  SMC’s stake will be diluted to 33% (from 100%) while Aboitiz Power (AP PM) will own 27%. The group plans to fund the acquisition effectively through a leveraged buyout, wherein c. 50% of the funding will come from debt that will be injected into the operating companies.  Meralco will be shelling out c. US$700mn to fund its portion of the acquisition.  Given the deal structure, Mr. Pangilinan deems the acquisition will be accretive to MER, with Ilijan likely to start contributing to MER’s profit by 2H24 and Excellent Energy by 2025. 

 

  • Rationalized structure for gas supply.  The group envisions forming a consortium, along with Prime Infrastructure, owners of the Malampaya gas field, and possibly First Gen (FGEN PM).  The consortium, which will earn through a tolling arrangement, will aggregate the country’s gas supply to improve bargaining power with the Middle East and lower the cost of imported LNG.  Given the incoming gas supply projects, MER believes that the country will likely require over 10mn MT of natural gas.  This also suggests that MER/AP/SMC will need to invest more to expand the capacity of the existing LNG storage facilities. It is also unclear at this stage who among these companies will lead the aggregator consortium, as MER estimates Prime Infrastructure’s Malampaya gas will only account for no more than 20% of total gas supply. 

We believe the above transactions could face intense scrutiny from the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC).

  • Approval of Meralco franchise extension by year-end? Mr. Pangilinan believes that the two bills at the Lower House, which seeks to extend the franchise of Meralco, could be passed before the President’s State of the Nation Address in July.  Management is hoping for the bill to be signed into law by year-end.
  • El Nino risks to Maynilad manageable; IPO still in the works.  Maynilad believes that they are better prepared for El Nino this year, given that they have already completed a 200 MLD water treatment plant last year and are about to complete another 100 MLD plant in 2Q. The company is also hopeful about the potential arrival of La Niña in June.

Management indicated that it is looking to list at least 30% of Maynilad’s shares in an IPO in 2025 or 2026, “depending on market conditions”.  The listing could also include its non-West zone assets. 

  • Tollroad volumes up 6% in Jan-Feb 2024; Valuation process ongoing for merger.  Management noted that traffic volumes for its tollroads in the Philippines rose 6% YoY in the first two months of 2024, on a like-for-like basis. This appears to be a tad softer than the +9% growth rate in 2023 and historical average of +7%.

Meanwhile, management is still at the valuation stage for the planned merger with San Miguel’s tollroad unit.  Given the number of government approvals both companies must obtain, management believes an IPO this year is highly unlikely.  It is also likely that San Miguel will be responsible for the day-to-day operations of the merged entity. 

  • Looking to buy more of hospital unit. MPI is planning to form a consortium that will acquire the ~55% stake of US-based private equity firm, KKR, in Metro Pacific Hospital Hldgs. (MPHHI).  One potential partner they are looking at is Singapore-based GIC, which today holds c. 25% stake in MPHHI.  MPI is also inviting more investors to join the consortium, given the reported asking price of US$3bn for 100% of MPHHI.
PROPERTY
Filinvest Land presales fall for 2nd consecutive quarter
  • Filinvest Land’s FY23 net profit rose 30% to P3.8bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts  
    • Revenue of the residential and office segments outperformed our expectations
    • Interest expense and tax rate were also lower than our forecasts
  • 4Q23 presales fell 5% QoQ and YoY to P4.0bn
    • This is the second consecutive quarter of decline
    • FY23 presales rose 6% to P19.2bn
  • Unsold inventory stood at P45bn, equivalent to 28 months of presales
    • RFO units comprised 25% of these
  • Office occupancy improved 2ppt QoQ to 62%, thanks to take-up from traditional tenants
    • Office inquiries could add another 5ppt to 10ppt of occupancy (if they lead to signed contracts)
  • FLI is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY24E P/B, and 5.1X FY24E P/E

 

PROPERTY
Senate approved Bill on property valuation reform
  • The Senate approved on second reading Senate Bill 2386, the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act
  • This seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units
  • Assessed valued used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas
  • We estimate that real property taxes account for 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover
  • This law would likely have a large impact on developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land, Filinvest Land, and Vista Land
