Meralco (MER PM) produced good results for 1Q24 as power usage jumped an unusual 9%, despite the decline in contributions from generation. Core income rose 11% to P10bn and looks set to handily top FY24 estimates (both Regis and Street). The retail supply (“RES”) business was a key contributor as it recovers from losses after terminating fixed priced contracts (on which it was losing money) late 2022.

The key event in the next few quarters will be the acquisition of the LNG complex (power plants + terminal) owned by San Miguel’s (SMC PM) and its partner/s. Unconditional approval by the competition authority is not, in our view, a foregone conclusion.  Furthermore, we believe expectations that the 1,300MW gas-fired will be commercially available by late this year could be optimistic. Over the last two decades, no new (significant) power plant in Luzon became commercially available according to promised timelines.

Union Bank (UBP PM, not covered) reported 1Q24 NPAT of P2.0bn, down 40% YoY and well behind consensus of P15.4bn. That said, management said that the results included "one-offs" of around P2bn -- P1.1bn from the finalized migration of Citi customers to UBP, and about P800mn from additional provisions relating to “legacy wholesale accounts”. Loan growth slowed to 6.3% from 9.7%, and NIMs contracted to 10bps QoQ. Both were due in part to UnionDigital pausing its lending, owing to high NPLs. With the Citi migration complete, and UnionDigital lending re-started, management seemed confident of a strong rebound in NPAT in the coming quarters. Gains, though, will still be hurt somewhat by the P10bn rights issue.

Digital payments over InstaPay and PesoNet grew 22.8% YoY in March, slowing sharply from the 40.6% pace in February. We reckon the deceleration was due to the Easter holidays, which were in March this year vs April in 2023. We expect a sharp bounce next month, given the low base last year. Despite the slowdown, the number of InstaPay transactions were still up an astounding 70.8% YoY, indicating continued rapid adoption of digital payments by individuals.

BANKS
UBP 1Q24 NPAT P2.0bn, -40%, well behind consensus

In a press release and limited financial disclosure, Union Bank (UBP PM) reported headline NPAT of P2.0bn, down 40% YoY, and far behind consensus of P15.4bn. Profits were dragged largely by provision expense, which has been elevated since 3Q23. Loan growth slowed to 6.3% YoY, from 9.7% in 4Q23, with deposits flat (-0.1%) YoY. We estimate that NIMs were up 97bps YoY, but down 10bps QoQ.

Opex was up 10% YoY, but down 3% QoQ. Expenses in 1Q24 included P1.1bn worth of one-time integration costs relating to the migration of Citi customers. With the transfer completed in 1Q24, opex should fall sharply from 2Q onwards as the Transition Service Agreement (TSA) falls away.

The high provision expense continues to be a concern. The PR and limited FS did not disclose any NPL or NPL cover data, nor did it explain the high credit cost.

Some takeaways from the briefing –

  • Customer count now at 14.7mn, up from 13.9mn at end-2023, and up from 13.0mn in 1Q23.
  • UnionDigital stopped lending, due to extremely high NPLs (24% in 4Q23, per their SOC). This had its impact on group loan growth, NIMs, and provision expense. UD has restarted lending, refocused back to “less risky” loans, and improved collection systems.
  • 1Q24 NPAT was dragged by one-off charges including:
    • P1.1bn worth of integration costs relating to the final stages of the Citi client migration to UBP.
    • P800mn worth of provisions on both the “legacy wholesale loans” and UnionDigital
  • Without one-offs, and with TSA ending in March, UBP expects profitability to materially improve from 2Q onwards
  • Citi integration going well.
    • Customer count from 1.065mn in Aug 2022 to 1.386mn at 1Q24 (+30%)
    • Loans up from P71.5bn to P100.2bn (+70%)
    • AUM from 149.9bn to 152.4bn (+2%)
    • Upside from cross-selling to UBP clients (14.7mn)
  • Guidance for 2024
    • Loan growth – double digits
      • Corporate – high single digits
      • Consumer – mid- to high-teens
    • NIM to rise on
      • Stronger consumer growth
      • Falling funding costs

 

BANKS
Digital payments - growth slows in March (due to Easter timing?)
  • Digital transactions over Instapay and PesoNet grew 22.8% YoY to P1,276bn in March 2024, decelerating from the heady 40.6% pace in February. We suspect, though that the slowdown was due in part to the Easter holidays, which fell in March this year vs April in 2023. Looking at the 2023 data, April was indeed a low month, which should bode well for a rebound in the YoY data next month. For 1Q24, payments were up 32.8% YoY to P3,812bn, still accelerating compared to 29.4% clip in FY23.
  • For InstaPay (small-tickets up to P50,000 per transaction), payments grew 38.9% YoY to P540bn, slowing from 48.9% in February. For 1Q24, growth was still extremely healthy at 44.3% YoY to P1,544bn, from 42.0% in FY23. By volume, transaction count was up 70.8% YoY in March, taking 1Q24 up 78.6% YoY. As before, the continued fast pace of growth indicates sustained adoption of digital payments for smaller retail transactions.
  • PesoNet (larger, batched payments) transactions were P735bn, up 13.1% YoY but slowing sharply from the 35% clip in February. For 1Q24, payments reached P2,268bn, up 26.0% YoY (was +22.4% in FY23). The volume of transactions fell 1.7% YoY, likely due to business closures over the Easter break.

CONGLOS
DMCI eyes quick CHP turnaround; unveils recovery roadmap
  • In a press release, DMCI identified its plans of turning around Cemex Holdings Philippines’ (CHP PM) operations by 2025
    • Cited lowering power, fuel, and other production supplies costs, which represent 73% of CHP’s cost of sales, by sourcing from Semirara (SCC PM)
    • It also plans to lower administrative and selling expenses, which accounted for 52% of prior-year operating expenses, by lowering talent and business process onshoring initiatives
    • Initiatives will also be aided by the completion of CHP’s 1.5mn MT production line by Sep 2024
  • Semirara plans to supply 500k MT of coal annually to CHP, up 227% from 2024 levels, and it will also supply 50 MW of electricity and fly ash
  • DMCI and DMCI Homes are estimated to source around 500k MT of cement from CHP initially, which can gradually ramp up once the outlook for its construction and property units gets better
