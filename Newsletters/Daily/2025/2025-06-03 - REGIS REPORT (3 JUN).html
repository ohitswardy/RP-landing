REGIS REPORT

03 June 2025

Trust assets under management (AUM) of banks and non-banks surged 22.9% YoY to P7,867bn ($141.4bn), and up +6.7% QoQ from end-2024 levels. The pace of growth improved from the 18.6% YoY set in 4Q24. Bank Trusts saw a massive 19% YoY improvement to P4,693b (+5.4% QoQ), accelerating sharply from the 6% growth in 4Q24. Meanwhile, for non-banks, AUMs were up 30% YoY to P3,174bn (+8.7% QoQ), though the growth was slower than the 44% YoY pace in 4Q24.

 

The strong YoY growth of the banks is partly due to the low base in 1Q24, when RCBC (c4% of bank AUMs) was spun off into an unconsolidated non-bank entity. The banking sector AUMs have been steadily growing at 3-5% QoQ for all of 2024. For non-bank AUMs, the continued surge is likely due in part to SunLife’s Trust entity’s being allowed to manage the assets of the insurance sister company only from 3Q24. Unfortunately, we do not yet have 1Q25 data on non-banks (including RCBC’s non-bank Trust affiliate).

 

By bank, on a YoY basis, SECB posted the fastest growth, up 45% YoY. That said, the big banks also posted pretty impressive YoY growth figures, with MBT up 24.0%, BPI up 23.8%, PNB up 19.2%, and BDO up 16.9%. On a QoQ basis, MBT led, growing 7.4%, with BPI up 6.4%. In contrast, BDO was up just 1.3% QoQ and SECB up 2.6% from end-2024.  

 

The Big Three in aggregate saw some mild market share decline, taking 58.3% of total AUMs, down from 59.9% at end-2024. Of the three, only MBT managed to eke out some gains, at 7.94% vs 7.89% the previous quarter. BDO remained by far the largest at P2,337bn ($42bn), with market share at 29.7% (vs 31.3% at in 4Q24), followed by BPI at P1,629bn ($29bn) or a market share of 20.7%, a shade lower than the 20.8% in 4Q24.

 

The continued growth in AUMs is encouraging for financial markets. However, it seems that AUMs have not moved back into domestic equities in a meaningful way, if market turnover is any indication. The hope is that the return of the interest rate cut cycle could see that shift. Anecdotally, we are already seeing inflows initially into income / dividend equity funds, which performed quite well in 2024 / 1Q25. More aggressive moves by the BSP could see more funds flow out of fixed income and into equities. Fingers crossed…

The Coral Bay HPAL plant which buys ore from Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) will cease operations by 2028. NIKL sold 14% of its ore to this facility in FY24 and would need to find an alternative buyer after the plant is retired.

 

+++

 

The government is moving to contain the sugar pest outbreak in the province of Negros Occidental with the use of pesticides. While the areas infested are still small, the risk of infestation spreading is high. This could deal a blow to sugar output in the province which accounts for over 50% share nationally. Universal Robina (URC PM) is among the largest sugar millers in the country, and this segment accounts for 17% of EBIT before corporate overhead (20% after corporate overhead).

 

Having said that, if there’s significant crop damage, government policy on importation could be the single biggest factor affecting the profitability of the millers.

BANKS
Commercial banks’ loan growth moderates to 11.2% in April; M3 growth slows to 5.8%
  • Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.2% YoY in April, slower than the 11.8% rate in March
    • Loans to productive activities grew 10.3%, moderating from 10.8% in March due to slower growth in retail (+9.9% from 11.6%) and real estate (+8.9% from 9.6%)
    • Consumer loans grew 24.0%, relatively unchanged from the 23.9% pace in March
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 5.8% in April, slower than the 6.2% growth rate in March

 

 

 

MINING
Coral Bay HPAL to cease operations by 2028; Impact on Nickel Asia (NIKL PM)
  • Coral Bay Nickel Corporation (CBNC) plans to cease operations of its HPAL plant in Palawan once it fully utilizes its tailings storage capacity by 2028
    • CBNC is one of NIKL’s customers, accounting for 14% of 2024 sales volumes
    • This announcement follows NIKL’s exit from CBNC early this year (here)
  • In theory, the planned retirement of CBNC could raise NIKL’s ASPs should it find an alternative buyer for the limonite ore it sells to CBNC
    • NIKL claims it has been selling limonite ore to HPAL plants, CBNC and THNC below market
    • CBNC is majority-owned by NIKL shareholder, Japan-based Sumitomo Metal (5713 JP)
  • NIKL trades at 7x FY25E PER, below Indo miners (14x)
CONSUMER
SRA seeks emergency powers to contain sugar pest outbreak in Negros
  • The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) is requesting emergency powers to expedite pesticide procurement and deployment amid an outbreak of Red-Striped Soft Scale Insects (RSSI) on Negros island, which produces 60% of the country’s sugar
    • The SRA cites ongoing delays relating to procurement and permits for the emergency use of pesticides for sugarcane
  • The RSSI infestation, first detected in late March, has spread to 425 hectares as of May 30 and poses a risk of up to 50% yield loss in affected areas.
  • The SRA cites ongoing delays relating to procurement and permits for the emergency use of pesticides for sugarcane
  • Note that in FY24, URC's net sales and operating income totaled P162bn and P20bn, respectively—with the SURE (Sugar and Renewables) segment contributing 18% of total revenues and 17% of EBIT

 

 

BANKS
BPI P10 InstaPay transfer fee made permanent
  • Bank of Philippine Islands (BPI) permanently reduced its InstaPay transfer fee (inter-bank fund transfers made online) to P10 from P25 originally
    • Previously, the lower P10 fee was a temporary promo (March 7 to May 31)
  • Head of consumer banking Maria Cristina Go noted that there was strong demand with increased transactions throughout online and mobile banking channels throughout the promo period
    • She highlighted that the lowering of the fee is part of BPI's commitment for more affordable banking
  • For other banks, BDO and UnionBank charge P10 per InstaPay transaction, while Security Bank and Metrobank charge P25, GCash and Maya charge P15
