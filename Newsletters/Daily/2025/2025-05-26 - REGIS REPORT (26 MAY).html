REGIS REPORT

26 May 2025

Aggregate recurring net profits of the PSEi constituents were up 4% YoY, tracking well below our FY25E forecast of around 10%. However, we note that the miss was driven by a handful of companies that had the own specific issues. The only real sector disappointment was telcos, with earnings down 7% YoY due to a seeming return of intense price competition in the mobile segment. Globe (GLO PM) profit was down 22% YoY, despite the strong showing of Mynt/GCash, while PLDT’s (TEL PM) was down 3% YoY. In contrast, Converge (CNVRG PM) posted strong results, up 18% YoY. Among stocks we cover, the only other major miss came from ACEN (ACEN PM), whose core NPAT fell 16% YoY on costly repairs to its wind farms, lower output at its solar farms, and lower spot prices. Of the conglos we don’t cover, the surprise profit in San Miguel (SMC PM) canceled out the sharp profit drop in Aboitiz Equity (AEV PM).

 

Perhaps the main story is that there weren’t enough “beats” to offset the “misses”? Of the 41 stocks REGIS covers, only 6 came in ahead, while 15 were behind our expectations.

 

The results reflect an economy that seems to have been just “muddling through”. We believe, though, that we could see some upward momentum from slower inflation and lower BSP policy interest rates. We continue to prefer the consumer sector, particularly stock catering to lower-income households. We also still like the banks as loan growth stays elevated (12%). While lower benchmark rates could squeeze margins, we could see relief from a) stronger consumer loan growth, and b) cumulative 450bps RRR cuts since October 2024.

The first changes to the Marcos Cabinet have been made public, though possibly just as important (or maybe even more important) are the announcements of who will not be replaced.

 

First, most of the key Cabinet members in the economic team are NOT being replaced. These are the heads of Finance (Recto), Trade (Roque), DepDev (Balisacan) and Budget (Pangandaman) as well as the Special Assistant for Investment and Economic Affairs (Go).

 

The Departments that will see change are: Foreign Affairs (Lazaro replaces Manalo), Environment (Lotilla replaces Yulo), Energy (Garin provisionally replacing Lotilla) and Housing (Aliling replacing Acuzar).

 

There are a number of outstanding and/or pending issues at these Departments that these changes may or may not affect.

 

For Foreign Affairs, the key issue is how much it will affect the country’s strategy and tactics with regard to relations with China and the U.S. For Environment, it would be how mining and reclamation will (or will not) be affected. There are many reclamation projects approved under the previous administration that yet to receive final approval / clearance. For Energy, one pending issue is the extension Coal Operating Agreement of Semirara (SCC PM) that has yet to be acted on. For Housing, the question is whether the change can lead to a material improvement in housing supply, especially low-income housing.

 

The full list of changes has yet to be announced. We are likely to hear more announcements in the coming days.

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona says he is of the view that there’s more room to ease monetary policy and we could see another 50bp of policy rate reductions this year, on top of the 25bp already implemented.

 

Remolona also said that the BSP is considering a shift to point target for inflation rather than a band (currently at 2-4%). Many countries aim for a “point” and frankly, a 2-4% band does look rather wide.

 

The BSP’s Monetary Board will meet on June 19.

MACRO
BSP expects at least two more rate cuts this year, RRR cut next year
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees to cut rates at least twice more in 2025 after the first 25 bps cut last April
    • This would bring the current benchmark rate from 5.5% to 5.0% by year-end
  • BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. elaborated that there is more leeway for easing and that a rate cut is on the table for the next Monetary Board policy meeting given the slowdown in inflation
    • Inflation cooled to 1.4% in April from 1.8% in March, YT-Apr inflation currently stands at 2.0%
  • Remolona also stated that the BSP is looking to shift to a point target for inflation, from the current 2-4% target band
    • Implementation for the change was said to not be anytime soon but next year at the latest
    • The BSP is currently working with the IMF on studying the shift to an inflation point target
  • Expectations for reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cuts were said to be unlikely for the rest of the year but more likely to be done next year
    • Last March, the RRR for universal and commercial banks was reduced by 200bp to 5%, while the ratio for digital banks was cut by 150bp to 2.5%, and the RRR for thrift lenders was cut 100bp to 0%
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (third out of six this year) is on June 19
  • Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be at 2.2% and projects 75bp total rate cuts this year to 5.00%
    • Regis expectations for full-year inflation are at 3.0% while our policy rate expectations are 50bp worth of cuts to 5.25%
