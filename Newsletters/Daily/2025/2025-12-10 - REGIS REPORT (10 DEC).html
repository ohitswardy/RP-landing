REGIS REPORT

10 December 2025

Asset quality data was mixed in October, with NPLs improving 3bps MoM to 3.53%, while Past Due (PD) loas worsening 4bps MoM to 4.52%. To be fair, both ratios were much better than last year, with NPLs down 29bps YoY and PDs down 14bps YoY. Slightly worrying was the continued deceleration in loan growth to 10.8%, the slowest pace since Sept 2024, possibly due to concerns over the ongoing corruption scandal. To the upside, it seems that the weak GDP in 3Q and the corruption issues have not led to significant delinquencies. This month’s numbers also highlight why we look at loan data excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repo (RRP), whose wild MoM swings cause weird, distorted readings.

 

+++

 

President Marcos is urging Congress to prioritize the passage of four pieces of legislation amidst the current corruption scandal. These four are bills (1) to limit the election of political “dynasties”, (2) to reform the party-list system that’s been coopted by entrenched vested interests, (3) to create an independent body to investigate and prosecute corruption involving infrastructure projects and (4) to enable the digitalization and publication of government budget information.

 

Under normal circumstances, it is hard to imagine that one or more of these bills can get passed. The current environment provides the best hope that some of these decades-overdue reforms will finally see the light of day. We are, however, inclined to believe that these bills, even if passed, will be watered down significantly to limit their efficacy.  We would nevertheless be happy to be proven wrong.

 

+++

 

The Senate approved its version of the 2026 national budget, paving the way for the bicameral reconciliation to begin on Friday. Legislators are under pressure to complete the passage of the final budget so that it’s ready for the President’s signature before year-end (by Dec 29).

BANKS
Asset quality mixed in Oct - NPLs improve, but PDs up MoM

October banking sector asset quality indicators were mixed. Excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs), system-wide NPL ratio improved to 3.53%, down 3bps MoM from 3.56% in September, and down 29bps YoY from 3.81% in Oct 2024. It is also the lowest NPL ratio so far in 2025. The ex-IBL/RRP runs counter to the headline report NPL ratio (3.33% up 2bps MoM) to the sharp drop in IBP/RRPs in October. In nominal terms, NPLs were down 0.4% MoM to P536.8bn

 

However, Past Due (PD) ratio was up 4bps MoM to 4.52%, though this was down 14bps YoY. In Peso terms, PDs increased 1.5% MoM and 7.3% YoY. Restructured Loans (RL) were flat, inching down 1bp MoM to 2.19%, but up around 6bps YoY. NPL Cover dipped slightly to 94.6% from 95.1% the previous month.

 

Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP), meanwhile, decelerated 10.8% YoY, compared to the 11.1% pace in September. That’s the slowest monthly YoY growth since September 2024.

 

By bank type, it was the large universal/commercial banks (UKBs) that saw a large PD spike, up 6bps MoM, while the smaller thrift banks (TBs) and digibanks (DGBs) posted big MoM improvements of 21bps and 57bps MoM respectively. The reverse was true for NPLs, though, with UKBs improving 2bps MoM, while the TBs and DGBs reporting 20bps increases in NPL ratio MoM.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

POLITICS
Marcos urges Congress to prioritize four anti-corruption-related bills
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. during a Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) meeting has directed Congress to prioritize the passage of four proposed legislative measures in an effort to tackle corruption and promote good governance
    • Anti-Dynasty bill
      • To prevent the consolidation of power given more than half of incumbent public officials are from dynasties
      • Sen. Hontiveros, author of the bill, described political dynasties as relatives up to the fourth degree of consanguinity or affinity who run at the same time for national or local posts, including party-list seats
    • Party-list System Reform Act bill
      • To ensure the genuine representation of marginalized sectors and to prevent the use of the party-list system as a backdoor for political dynasties or large corporations
    • Independent People's Commission (IPC) Act bill
      • The creation of a law-created oversight body to replace the Independent Infrastructure Commission (ICI)
    • Citizens Access and Disclosure of Expenditures for National Accountability (CADENA) Act bill
      • Publication and digitalization of all public budget data given scrutiny over the budget and the flood control project scandal
  • Opposition lawmakers, however, are pressing for Marcos to certify the measures as "urgent" in order to further fast track the passage of these bills
    • When the President certifies a bill as urgent, Congress can pass it on second and third reading on the same day, bypassing the required three-day gap
MACRO
Senate passes 2026 budget on third and final reading
  • The Senate passed on third and final reading the proposed P6.79trn 2026 national budget on 17 affirmative votes, zero objections, and zero abstentions
  • Finance committee chairman Senator Gatchalian stated that the amendments made reflects a "more transparent, more disciplined, and more accountable" budget
    • The Senate stated that all 2026 budget documents will be posted online and that the bicam livestream will push through
  • Gatchalian highlighted that key amendments made vs the House' version of the budget were:
    • The allocation for the education sector being ~4.5% of GDP at P1.37trn, P91.2bn higher than previously allocated
    • Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) budget was reduced by P54bn to P570.5bn
  • The bicameral conference meeting is scheduled for Dec 12, where the bicam panel will work together to author a reconciled version of next year’s national budget
  • The current schedule for remaining budget deliberations is as follows:
    • Bicam meetings are scheduled for Dec 12-14 followed by the ratification of the budget by Dec 19
    • Tentative signing of the General Appropriations Act (GAA) by the President on Dec 29
  • The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than 2025's P6.33trn (+10% YoY) budget
  • Note that the budget breakdown table is subject to change and that the "2026 Pending" budget is a rough estimation from snippets from various news sources
