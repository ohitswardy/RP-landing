06 October 2025

The chairman of the SEC Francis Lim is signaling that the upcoming revision of the REIT regulations will expand what qualifies as “income generating assets” that can be included in REITs. Among the examples of such assets he noted were electric towers, power plants, toll roads and telecom towers.

 

An expansion of what can be “REIT-ed” would allow more firms to consider listings for their assets.  Among them: Meralco (MER PM) (electric towers, telecom towers, power plants), Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (electric towers, power plants, telecom towers), ACEN (ACEN PM) (power plants), First Gen (FGEN PM) (power plants) and San Miguel (SMC PM) (power plants, toll roads).

 

Encouraging REIT listings would arguably expand the local equities market and investor appetite for it.  High dividend payers, including listed REITs, have been the best performers in the PSE recently and now command better valuations that similar companies with lower payouts. These high dividend stocks have an investing audience that extends to firms requiring long-term assets matching their long-term liabilities (e.g. insurance firms).

We published a lengthy report on the property sector over the weekend.

 

Despite the challenges facing the sector, we expect the office and residential segments to see a positive turnaround next year. After five years of negative office rental reversions, we forecast rental reversions to begin turning positive next year. Meanwhile, we expect reservation sales to rise annually in 2026E-2027E, thanks to enticing promotions on ready-for-occupancy units, strong provincial sales, and resilient demand for high-end lots.

 

With valuations near record lows and interest rates set to decline further, we maintain our Buy ratings on the four major property developers.

 

Our top pick is Robinsons Land (RLC PM), which we believe offers compelling valuations (see below). In fact, the value of its stake in RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) is about 25% higher than its current market cap.

 

Among the large caps, we prefer SM Prime (SMPH PM), which we expect to continue hitting record level profits. Its plan to launch high-end residential and commercial lots should also lead to better reservation sales. Furthermore, we reckon the current share price does not place any value on the company’s coastal development project, which comprises 22% of NAV.

MACRO
Customs' revenues up 8% in September
  • Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P82bn in revenues for the month of Sept, up 8% YoY
    • Customs Assistant Commissioner Vincent Maronilla previously guided a recovery in collections during the '-ber' months as previous months saw lackluster volumes
  • Confiscated smuggled goods from illicit trade is expected to amount to P60bn this year or just 40% of the P150bn seized last year
    • Maronilla attributed the lower haul due to the suspension of their enforcement operations outside Customs premises
  • Total customs collections as of 9M25 amounted to P704bn, 2% higher YoY
    • The figure is 73% of the BOC's P959bn (+4.5% YoY) revised full-year collection target
      • The BOC's initial target set for this year was P1.06trn (+16% YoY)

 

 

TELCOS
Draft Konektadong Pinoy IRR seeks stronger cybersecurity and competitive safeguards
  • The Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) eyes to tighten the cybersecurity requirements of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) ahead of the potential entry of new players
    • The draft implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the KPA exhibit a tighter due diligence process to encourage fair competition and to reinforce cybersecurity protocols
  • Some of the draft rules and regulations are as follows:
    • The DICT will formulate guidelines which will detail the risk profile per data transmission industry participant (DTIP) segment with considerations for market share, network coverage, among others
    • DTIPs must follow DICT cybersecurity rules under the National Cybersecurity Plan, including a response team, secure SDLC, and zero-trust framework
    • DTIPs need to adopt global standards for internet security, submit risk plans, vulnerability tests, and incident reports
    • DTIPs are required to obtain a third-party issued cybersecurity certification and certificate of DICT compliance within two years from registration
    • Non-compliance will lead to suspension, license revocation, and permanent ban from PH operations
POWER
DOE clears Meralco’s 200-MW power supply CSP
  • Energy Undersecretary Mario C. Marasigan said the department had issued a certificate of conformity (CoC) for Meralco’s proposed 200MW competitive selection process (CSP)
    • 200MW CSP will cover renewable baseload capacity requirements from Jan 26, 2026 to Jan 25, 2030
    • It is intended to replace ACEN Corp’s terminated baseload contract with Meralco
    • The issuance of a CoC by the DoE is a regulatory requirement before a CSP can begin
  • Bidding forms part of Meralco’s plan to secure over 2,100 MW of capacity under its long-term supply procurement plan covering 2026 to 2046
    • The Department of Energy has yet to issue COCs for Meralco’s proposed CSPs:
      • 450MW mid-merit (Feb 2028 to Feb 2029)
      • 600MW baseload (Feb 26, 2028)
      • 900MW baseload (15 years from Feb 26, 2030)

Aboitiz, partners get ERC nod for wind farm grid access
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has given Aboitiz-backed Lihangin Wind Energy Corp. (LWEC) the green light to build transmission facilities for its 206MW San Isidro Wind project in Samar
    • The project’s interim grid connection will be through the Calbayog substation of NGCP, pending completion of the proposed 138-kilovolt San Isidro substation for its permanent link
    • LWEC is a joint venture of Aboitiz Renewables Inc., Vivant Energy Corp. and Singapore-based Vena Energy
