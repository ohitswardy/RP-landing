REGIS REPORT

04 April 2025

Overnight markets fell out of bed, with the US and Europe down 3.5-5%, as the world digests the full ramifications of the “reciprocal tariffs” announced on 02 April. The potential of a global economic slowdown saw even commodity prices tumble 3%, with oil down around 6-7%.

 

As we wrote yesterday (report links below), the Philippines seems to have come out of “Liberation Day” in a better position, at least relative to the rest of Asia. The 17% (or 18% as per the White House page) is much, much lower than the 32-48% for the rest of ASEAN (ex-Singapore). And on a relative basis, the Philippines us much, much less dependent on exports as an economic driver.

Two potential positives, as unintended consequences?

  1. Falling commodity prices are good for the Philippines, as we are net importers of many goods, especially petroleum products. High US tariffs could also mean exporting countries may look to sell more stuff to non-US countries (like the Phils) at lower prices to offload excess production.
  2. The massive tariff differential could make the Philippines competitive as a manufacturing hub, at least for light industry. While our labor / energy costs may be higher, the tariff gap could enough of an offset.

 

Speaking of low prices, March CPI inflation came in at 1.8% YoY, near the low-end of the BSP’s forecast, and the lowest print since late 2019. As we pointed out in our recent piece, sharply slower inflation should give the BSP’s Monetary Board (MB) lots of room to cut policy interest rates in its meeting on Thursday 10 April.

 

We recommend leaning into the Philippines’s strength as a domestic consumption-driven economy, and continue to overweight consumer names – URC, WLCON, JFC, PGOLD – to play the rebound in lower-income household purchasing power. While lower interest rates are positive for property stocks, we remain cautious on the fundamentals of the sector. We prefer banks as the proxy for the economy. Finally, we would recommend buying high dividend yield stocks as a way to play the falling interest rate environment.

MACRO
DOF, BSP cautions elevated risks to PH growth from US tariffs
  • The Department of Finance in a statement noted that the 17% reciprocal tariff on PH exports may hamper PH growth as the expected decline in international trade and global growth slowdown may bring about risks of supply chain disruptions, accelerated inflation, and elevated interest rates
  • Despite the headwinds, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto highlighted that the consumption-driven economy provides resiliency against trade wars and can withstand economic shocks
  • He also noted potential upside on opportunities from global trade development, with the PH positioned to possibly be a hub for global value chains, in industries such as electronics, textiles, food, and automobiles
    • The country's exports for coconut-based products and garments could also increase in market share as major competitors face higher tariffs
  • In an effort to diversify export markets, the gov't continues to pursue new and expand free trade agreements with economies such as the UAE, EU, Chile, and Canada
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) commented that there may be further pressure on the peso-dollar exchange rate from the reciprocal tariffs
    • But Remolona noted that the analysis is still being done on how the US Tariffs may impact consumer prices and the peso
MACRO
DTI secretary sees potential opportunities from Trumps' tariff push
  • Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) Secretary Cristina Roque commented that US President Trump's sweeping tariffs could present an opportunity for the Philippines as it was hit with a reciprocal levy of 17% which is lower relative to its neighbors
    • She noted that significantly higher tariffs were put on Vietnam (46%), Thailand (36%), and Taiwan (32%)
  • Roque highlighted that the US remains a "crucial export market" as the US is the top destination for PH exports, comprising a ~16% share of the country's total exports in 2024
  • The DTI Secretary also stated that they are prepared to discuss "enhanced market access" for key US export interests which include automobiles, dairy products, frozen meat, and dairy products
    • She mentioned that she has already reached out to her US counterpart to set up dialogue
POWER
March WESM prices spike to highest in 7 months
  • Nationwide power spot prices soared by 95.5% MoM to P5.34/kwh in March from P2.73/kwh in February (here)
    • Cited that nationwide supply slipped by 4% MoM to 19,611 MW while demand rose 6% MoM to 13,670 MW
    • Arjon Valencia, IEMOP corporate planning and communications manager, attributed the increase in demand to the “extreme heat” during the first week of March
  • For the Luzon grid, spot prices soared by 103% MoM to P5.50/kwh after supply fell 6.5% MoM to 13,530 MW while demand rose 7.1% MoM to 9,713 MW
  • Assuming the power prices for March, we estimate spot prices in 1Q25 may have averaged P3.75/kwh, down 12% QoQ and 25% YoY
    • Semirara (SCC PM) has the largest exposure to the spot market (~70% of capacity). Lower spot prices could mean lower profits for its power unit (~30% of profits).
