REGIS REPORT

26 December 2025

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P158bn in November, 26% narrower YoY. Unfortunately, the decline was arguably for the wrong reasons, with revenue collection up just 1% YoY and government spending down 10% YoY.

 

Primary spending fell 13% YoY in November, the fifth consecutive month of YoY decline. Furthermore, In Oct-Nov, primary spending was down 11% YoY, potentially signaling weak GDP growth in the fourth quarter.  

 

+++

 

Speaking of government spending, the Department of Agriculture will expand the government’s P20/kg subsidized rice program to P23bn next year, from P5bn this year. In turn, it is targeting to reach 15mn households (over 50% of the population), from just 3.3mn households in 2025.

 

+++

 

A Makati land parcel was acquired by LFM Properties Corp (LPC PM) at P1.2mn/sqm, slightly higher than the P1.12mn/sqm recorded for a Makati property in 2023. This suggests that unlike condominium prices, Makati land prices have risen slightly over the past few years.  

 

+++

 

Trust AUMs of entities regulated by the BSP grew 13.7% YoY (+3.4% QoQ) to P8.2trn (about $140bn) in 3Q25. While the growth in AUMs remains impressive, we continue to be disappointed by the lack of inflow into the domestic equities market. Anecdotally, the vast majority of new flows are still going into fixed income and white-labeled US ETFs. Within local stocks, flows are concentrated on high dividend yield paying stocks, including REITs.

 

Interestingly, Trust AUMs have grown significantly faster than deposits and are now equivalent to just under 40% of banking sector deposits (P21.1trn as of Sept). This suggests a few things – a) bank clients are looking for better yields, b) the financial system is much more liquid than the headline LDR of 74% would suggest.

MACRO
Government posts P158bn budget deficit in Nov; P1.26trn deficit as of 11M25
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P158bn in Nov, 26% narrower YoY as gov't spending continued to decline
  • This brings the 11M25 deficit to P1.26trn, 7% wider YoY
    • The YTD budget deficit was said to be in line with the P1.56trn full-year program and the gov't's "commit to prudent fiscal management and consolidation efforts"
    • The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues
  • Revenue collections in Nov were flattish, only up 0.7% YoY, given the modest 3% growth tax revenues
    • Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were up by 3%
      • Recall that BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza stated that the slowdown in consumer and gov't spending led to lower-than-expected collections this year
    • Customs collections likewise rose 3%
      • Recall that the Bureau of Customs (BOC) stated that foregone revenues due to illicit trade are estimated to be around P150bn in 2025 (vs. just P30-40bn last year)
    • Non-tax revenues dipped 42% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024
  • Expenditures continued to decline by 10% YoY in Nov due to the decrease in primary spending
    • Primary spending for the month was lower by 13%
      • Similar to the previous four months, the continued gov't spending decline YoY in Nov was primarily caused by the slowdown in infra spending due to the flood control scandal
    • Interest payments rose 16% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt
  • The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024

 

 

 

MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture targets to expand P20/kg rice program for mid-income homes
  • The Dept of Agriculture (DA) is preparing to expand the gov't's P20/kg subsidized rice program to also include lower-middle-income households and other population groups
    • The program is currently also open to indigent sectors (e.g., solo parents, PWDs, minimum wage earners, etc.)
  • The broader goal is to reach 15mn Filipino households by 2026 with the initial launch to begin in eight provinces in Jan-Feb next year on the back of a P23bn budget
    • The previous goal was to distribute to 3.3mn households this year with a P5bn allocated budget
    • The purchase limit is set at P30/kg a month for senior citizens and P10/kg a month for other beneficiaries
  • DA Secretary Fransico Tiu Laurel stated that eligible households are to register in the digital platform for the P20/kg rice program for proper documentation and smoother collection of allocated rice
    • He also added that the subsidized rice program budget would come from the P4bn remaining from the President's contingency fund, P9bn from the National Food Authority (NFA), and P10bn from the general appropriations fund
  • According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P42.7/kg and P43.5/kg as of Nov vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan, respectively

PROPERTY
Government releases implementing rules on 99-year land leases
  • The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI ) and the Board of Investments (BOI) announced the signing of the implementing rules and regulations for Republic Act No. 12252, which allows foreigners to lease land for up to 99 years
    • The maximum of 99 years is in line with Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia
    • Previously, foreigners were only permitted to lease land for up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years (total of 75 years)
  • Under the IRR, landowners are required to have leased contracts annotated on the land title
    • The DTI said this ensures that lease agreements are binding to the public and strengthens legal protection for both landowners and lessees
  • Foreigners will be allowed to sublease properties unless their contracts prohibit them from doing so
  • The lease contract can be terminated if the foreign investor fails to commence the project within three years of signing
  • The law allows long-term land lease for “the establishment of industrial estates, factories, assembly or processing plants, agro-industrial enterprises, land development for industrial or commercial use, tourism, agriculture, agro-forestry, ecological conservation and other similar priority productive endeavors.”
