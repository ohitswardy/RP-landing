REGIS REPORT

02 June 2025

Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) announced it was buying back the 22% stake in itself held by the Dairy Farm group. The consideration for the stake (=315m RRHI shares) is P15.7bn or P50/share. We outline our key thoughts on the announced deal:

 

  • While the transaction price is 32% higher than the last close, we reckon it’s a good deal for RRHI. At P50/share, the transaction would be on 11x PER for FY25E. With pretax cost of debt probably in the ~7% range (~5% post-tax), the deal will be immediately EPS accretive (likely >10% annualized).

 

  • Because the stake held by Dairy Farm is not considered “public float”, the buyback would actually increase the % public float to ~36% (from 28%), albeit against a smaller market cap.

 

  • The market cap of RRHI at Friday’s close would be P42bn, assuming the reduction of the 22% stake held by Dairy Farm. This is now lower than the value of RRHI’s 6.5% stake in BPI (BPI PM).

 

+++

 

Another blockbuster transaction was also revealed over the weekend with First Gen (FGEN PM) disclosing it was selling a 60% interest in its natural gas assets to Prime Infrastructure, a holding company controlled by Enrique Razon.  These assets include ~2,000MW of natgas-fired power plants and an LNG terminal

 

The 60% interest is being sold for P50bn (~US$900m), but with additional payments subject to the achievement of certain targets. The transaction price implies an equity value (100%) of P83b (US$1.5bn). Inclusive of estimated debt, the enterprise value is likely to be around US$1.8bn. Assigning the P83bn valuation to our FGEN NAV estimate would yield a NAV of P40/share, more than 2x the last price.

 

Strategically, we believe this deal with the Razon group makes sense for both parties.  The latter controls the Malampaya gas field, the main supplier of fuel for the natgas plants of FGEN. The interests of both parties would now become much better aligned.

 

Beyond this transaction and after the deal is completed, FGEN will have P50bn that can be used to (1) pay down debt, (2) invest in new ventures and/or (3) increase its stake in geothermal operators EDC.

President Marcos just signed a new law (“CMEPA”) that would, among other things, reduce the stock transaction tax to 0.1% (from 0.6%) and rationalize other investment related taxes. Have a read of our comments here:

 

For international investors>>Philippine Strategy - Stock sales tax cut to 0.1% (from 0.6%), to spur market liquidity?

 

For Philippine-based investors>>Philippine Strategy - Stock sales tax cut to 0.1% (from 0.6%), to spur market liquidity?

 

Note the CMEPA bill also includes a provision that re-instates excise taxes on pickup trucks, reversing the exemption under the TRAIN Law of 2017. Pickup trucks with single-cab chassis (e.g. Toyota Tamaraw) remain exempted but pickup trucks outside this category (e.g. Toyota Hilux) will likely see a hefty 20% price increase once the law is enacted.  

 

The Hilux accounts for 13% of Toyota Motor Philippines’ sales volumes. We anticipate demand for this vehicle could increase ahead of implementation but could fall once taxes are implemented.

Hann Holdings, which operates an integrated casino resort in Clark, filed for an IPO. It plans to sell 500mn shares at up to P23.60/share. Including a 50mn overallotment option, the IPO could raise up to P13bn.

 

At the maximum price, Hann’s market cap would hit P59bn, higher than the P48bn market cap of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM). For reference, Clark Freeport is a much smaller market than Entertainment City. Its 1Q25 GGR stood at just P7bn, vs P34bn for Entertainment City.

Following the end of May, we updated our computations for the August 2025 PSE Index rebalancing (which will be based on trading from July 2024 to June 2025). It now appears even more likely that Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) will be removed from the PSE Index and DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) will take its place.

The SEC approved the share-for-property swap (which was first announced in January 2025) between Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) and its sponsor, Filinvest Land (FLI PM). Filinvest REIT will acquire the 122,000sqm main wing of Festival Supermall in exchange for 1.626bn new FILRT shares.

CONSUMER
RRHI buys back 315mn shares for P15.7bn from Dairy Farm International Group
  • Robinsons Retail Holdings, Inc. (RRHI PM), in a disclosure last Friday (May 30), stated that they have reacquired 315mn common shares at P50/sh from GCH Investments Pte. Ltd., a subsidiary of the DFI Retail Group through a duly approved special block sale worth P15.7bn
    • The company funded the transaction through a mix of internal resources and external borrowings, while the transaction price was determined through a mutual agreement between RRHI and GCH
  • The P50/sh transaction price is a 32% premium to last Friday's closing price but a 46% discount to when DFI bought its first stake in RRHI at P94/sh back in 2018
    • The P50/sh transaction implies a valuation of 11x PE vs. its current PE of 8.4x
  • Post-transaction, we estimate 2025E earnings, on an annualized basis, will likely decline ~P800mn given the impact of additional debt and less cash
    • However, due to the 22% decrease in outstanding shares (as DFI's stake is not considered public float), 2025E EPS will improve by 13% on an annualized basis—this implies that the buyback will be meaningfully accretive to EPS
      • Public float will increase to 35.7% from 27.8% given the reduction in outstanding shares
  • The remaining balance of the amount for the buyback program as of late is P166mn after deducting the P8bn initially allocated and the P15.7bn transaction cost
  • RRHI elaborated that the transaction is a "strategic move to optimize capital allocation and create more value to its shareholders."
    • The company also emphasized their belief that current market prices do not completely reflect the underlying financial strength and growth prospects of RRHI

First Gen selling 60% stake of natgas assets to Razon-led Prime Infrastructure

    • Amount is subject to adjustments and modification as may be agreed by the parties.
    • Deal provided that “First Gen will be entitled to receive additional amounts, subject to the fulfilment of certain conditions”
  • First Gen’s natgas assets include the following:
    • 1,000-megawatt Santa Rita,
    • 500MW San Lorenzo,
    • 450MW San Gabriel,
    • 97MW Avion
    • Offshore LNG terminal
    • planned 1,200MW Santa Maria gas-fed plants
  • A valuation of P50bn for 60% would imply a valuation of P83bn for 100%. This compares with our valuation of US$1,779mn (P99.6bn)
    • At P83bn for 100% of its gas assets and assuming our valuation for all other assets, we estimate a NAV of P143bn or P40/share
  • Prime Infrastructure is the operator of the Malampaya natural gas field, which is a major source of gas for FGEN’s plants
MACRO/MARKET
Capital Market Efficiency Promotion Act signed into law
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed the Capital Market Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA) into law as Republic Act No. 12214 on Friday, May 29
    • The law was said to modernize the tax system on passive income to be more competitive, aligned with regional peers, and investor-friendly
  • As of late, details on the Republic Act have not been uploaded yet to the Official Gazette, where finalized details (post-any revisions) on the law will be stated
  • Some key features of the law that was discussed in this article are as follows:
    • Reduction in the tax on stock transactions to 0.1% from 0.6%
    • Cut in the documentary stamp tax (DST) on original issue of shares of stock to 0.75% from 1% of the par value of the shares of stock
    • Exemption of DST on the original issuance, redemption or other disposition of shares or units of participation in a unit investment trust fund
    • Introduction of an allowable deduction of 50% of an employer’s contribution to their employees’ personal equity and retirement accounts
CONGLOS
Excise taxes to be imposed on pickup trucks
  • Under the ratified bill, Capital Market Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA), pickup trucks will now be slapped with excise taxes
    • We anticipate a 20% excise tax rate will apply to most pickup trucks
    • News reports indicate that single-cab chassis would still be exempted
    • Note that pickup trucks were previously granted exemption under the TRAIN Law passed in 2017
    • We await further details; As of today, the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) for the law has yet to be published
  • The Hilux accounted for 13% of Toyota Motor Philippines’ (TMP) sales
    • We estimate every 5% decrease in TMP’s sales could decrease TMP’s profit by 10% and GTCAP’s profit by 2%
    • When the TRAIN Law took effect, vehicle sales fell 15% YoY
    • TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year (Regis FY25E at 201k units)
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses
  • GTCAP is trading at a 58% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,300/sh and 4.8x FY25E PER

PROPERTY
SEC approves Filinvest REIT acquisition of Festival Mall from Filinvest Land
  • The SEC approved the acquisition of Festival Mall by Filinvest REIT
  • Fillinvest REIT will acquire the main wing of Festival Mall from Filinvest Land via share-for-property swap
    • The main wing of Festival Mall is a 122,000sqm mall in Alabang, Muntinlupa
    • The transaction will not include the expansion wing of Festival Mall
    • The acquisition will increase FILRT’s GLA by 37% to 452,000sqm
  • Upon completion of the acquisition, FLI will lease the mall back from FILRT under a 30-year contract and sublease to third parties
    • FLI will pay a fixed rental rate with an annual escalation rate of 5%
    • FILRT said that “the DPS growth resulting from the infusion will be 5.65%”
  • After the infusion, FILRT’s occupancy will increase from 83% to 88%
    • Its weighted average lease expiry (WALE) will rise from 7.3 years to 14.6 years
  • Filinvest REIT will issue 1.626bn new FILRT shares at P3.85/share – P6.26bn
    • This represents a 30% premium to the 30-day VWAP
    • This will increase FILRT’s shares outstanding by 35%
  • At the current price of P3.19/share, the total cost is effectively P5.6bn
    • This is lower than our estimated value of P7.6bn (for Festival Supermall main wing)
    • We estimate that the transaction will reduce FLI’s NAV by c. 8% to P2.00/share
  • Upon completion of the transactions, FLI’s ownership in FILRT will rise from 51% to 63%
  • FILRT currently offers 7.5% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.3%
  • FLI currently trades at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, 5.0X FY25E P/E
MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in April
  • The March trade deficit widened to US$4.1bn, up 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in exports and the decline in imports
    • This brings the 4M25 trade deficit to US$12.7bn,13% up YoY
  • Imports for the month declined 7% YoY to US$10.2bn, a reversal from the 12% increase in Mar
    • Primarily driven by the decrease in fuel and raw & intermediate goods imports
  • Exports grew 7% YoY to US$6.7bn, the fourth consecutive month of growth
    • Electronics exports fell 5% to $3.4bn, a reversal from the 1% YoY increase in Mar
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 50% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports jumped 22% YoY to US$3.4bn
      • Growth in non-electronics exports was led by other manufactured goods, coconut oil, machinery and transport equipment
