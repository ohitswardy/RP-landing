REGIS REPORT

05 May 2025

Chinabank (CBC PM) reported headline NPAT of P6.5bn, up 10% YoY. We note, though, that PPOP was flat, which suggests the NPAT growth came from either lower provisions and/or lower taxes. To the upside, loan growth remained elevated, expanding 19% YoY (was 18.5% in FY24), significantly faster than the industry average 12-13%. It is quite difficult to comment on the rest of the P&L given a) the lack of detail in the press release, and b) the apparent continued use of cross-currency swap products, which distort NIMs / FX losses. Asset quality seems to have improved, with NPL ratio at 1.5% (from 1.6% in 4Q24.) The bank will hold its earnings call on Thursday, 8 May, at 11am.

In its latest 17-Q, Semirara (SCC PM) disclosed that its 1Q25 profit fell 33% YoY to P4.35bn. The results appear to be tracking below Regis and consensus estimates, after the coal mining unit recorded weaker-than-expected ASPs and higher-than-expected cash costs. Despite lower spot prices, the power unit managed to increase contributions by 263% QoQ and 6% YoY.

 

Management will be holding a briefing tomorrow at 4pm, where we hope to get more clarity on the surprise jump in mining cash costs and on any possible guidance revisions. 

 

+++

 

In its Manila Bay Cumulative Impact Assessment, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) concluded that the ongoing and proposed reclamation projects could cause “severe environmental threats”. Thus, the DENR will tighten regulations regarding the issuance of Environmental Compliance Certificates (ECC), including ECCs and area clearances that have already been granted. While SM Prime (SMPH PM) was not specifically mentioned, this could cause revisions to the company’s Pasay Coastal Redevelopment project, which comprises 27% of our NAV estimate. However, tighter regulations on the granting of ECCs would also mean fewer new reclamation projects, suggesting less competition in the future.

Chinabank - 1Q25 up 10%, but...

In a limited press release, Chinabank (CBC PM) reported headline NPAT of P6.5bn, up 10% YoY. There is no real coverage, with only one estimate last updated in August 2024.

 

  • Loans grew 19% YoY, slightly faster than the 18.5% reported in 4Q24, and significantly faster than the industry average of around 12-13%
  • NIMs were steady at 4.5%, according to the company’s estimates. However, we note that the bank still offers cross-currency swaps, which flatter the NIMs, as the swap portion of the product is taken as a trading/fx loss. At the earnings call for the FY24 results, their CFO estimated that if the swap costs were accounted for as interest expense, their NIMs would be a full 100bps lower.
  • To that point, non-interest income was a loss in 1Q25, likely due to the swap costs being booked as trading/fx losses. No mention of fee incomes.
  • Opex was up 17% YoY, to boost “digital, infrastructure, and talent capabilities.”
  • PPOP was flat YoY, and down 25% QoQ. We reckon there were chunky one-offs (write-backs/write-ups) that were booked in 4Q24.
  • The 10% increase in NPAT therefore due to either a) lower provision expense and/or b) lower taxes. The bank said NPL ratio fell to 1.5% from 1.6% in 4Q. For NPL cover, there were some restatements – previously, the BSP allowed banks to report NPL cover including appropriated retained earnings. The BSP reversed that. CBC’s NPL cover excluding the RE appropriation was at 112%, up from 108% at end-2024. We do not have quarterly data on NPL cover ex-RE appropriation. But we do have them annually, which we show in the chart below.
  • ROE came in at 15.1%, easing slightly from the 15.6% for FY2024.

 

Difficult to draw much more from the press release. We await the full FS (due closer to the 15 May deadline), and the briefing for further color.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MINING
SCC: 1Q25 profit drops 34% YoY on lower mining profits
  • 1Q25 core profit of P4.27bn (-34% YoY) is trending behind forecasts. Headline 1Q25 profit fell 33% YoY to P4.35bn. Removing FX adj, we estimate core profit fell 34% YoY to P4.27bn. We estimate EBITDA dropped 19% YoY to P6.94bn, which looks in line with consensus forecast of P27.4bn but appears below our P31.5bn forecast.

 

The 1Q25 results look a tad softer than we had anticipated. Nevertheless, a strong catch up in the latter part of the year remains possible with (a) the scheduled opening of a new higher-grade mine (i.e. Acacia), as well as (b) higher power plant availability and (c) higher coal production above 16mn MT.

 

  • Mining contributions drop 45% YoY, 23% QoQ. Profit contributions from the mining unit fell 45% YoY, 23% QoQ to P2.7bn, trending below forecasts due to lower-than-expected ASPs of P2,481 (vs. FY25E: P2,792/MT) and higher cash costs (ex-royalties) of P1,385/MT (vs. FY25E: P1,204).  ASPs (-12% QoQ, -17% YoY in PHP terms) slid more than the drop in benchmark prices (see chart below) due to a higher proportion of lower-quality and non-commercial grade coal. Meanwhile, sales volumes dipped 2% YoY to 4.7mn MT, trending above forecast of 15.5mn MT (-7% YoY). Despite lower crude oil prices, cash costs (ex-royalties) surprisingly jumped +26% QoQ, 6% YoY.

 

  • Higher power capacity, contracted prices lift power profits up 6% YoY. The power unit contributed P1.6bn in 1Q25, up 263% QoQ and 6% YoY.  ASPs slightly dipped to P4.42/kwh from P4.47/kwh due to better BCQ prices, which offset the 10% YoY drop in spot prices.  Sales volumes jumped 17% QoQ and 11% YoY due to the completion of Unit 2’s rehab, lifting average capacity to 793MW. Plant availability factor fell to 89% in 1Q25 from 92% in 1Q24 due to scheduled downtimes at SLPGC.

 

  • Management will hold a briefing tomorrow, May 6 at 4pm (registration link).

 

 

MACRO/PROPERTY
DENR to tighten ECC rules following Manila Bay assessment
  • The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said it would tighten its guidelines on the issuance of Environmental Compliance Certificates (ECC) after its Manila Bay Cumulative Impact Assessment revealed severe environmental threats
    • This could include ECCs that have been issued
  • The assessment revealed that the 21 proposed and ongoing reclamation projects in Greater Metro Manila could threaten marine ecosystems and fisherman’s livelihoods, and worsen flooding in urban areas
  • Environment Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo Loyzaga said that Manila Bay will be part of a continuing review
  • “There is in the ECC a section that will allow us to modify any approval that has been granted, and we take that section very seriously in light of the results of the cumulative impact assessment phase one,” 
  • Part of the review process will be a consultation with the Philippine Reclamation Authority, proponent local government units, and stakeholders to determine whether the ECCs need to be revised or amended
  • There could be revisions to SM Prime’s Manila Bay reclamation project
    • The Pasay Coastal Development project comprises 27% of our SMPH NAV estimate
  • SMPH is trading 43% discount to NAV, 1.5X FY25E P/B, and 15.3X FY25E P/E 
MACRO
Infrastructure spending rises by 19% YoY in the first two months of 2025
  • Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 19% YoY to P182.9bn in 2M25
    • For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.51tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP
      • Recall that the gov't spent 1.55tn (+9% YoY) or 5.8% of GDP back in 2024
  • The DBM (Department of Budget and Management) noted that while disbursements likely accelerated before the March 28 election ban on public funds, spending may temporarily slow in April but is expected to pick up again from late May to June once the ban is lifted
