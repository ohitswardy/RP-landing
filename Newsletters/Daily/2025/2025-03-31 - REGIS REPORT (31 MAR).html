REGIS REPORT

31 March 2025

The FY24/4Q24 numbers for Wilcon (WLCON PM) reported last Friday are still looking weak.  Revenues contracted 1% for the year and 2% for the quarter. Net profit for FY24 dropped 27% to P2.5bn, with 4Q profit falling 46% to P410m. It’s clear that demand is soft, almost certainly due to the weakness in residential property activity.

 

Same store sales (SSS) for 4Q alone were down a rather steep 7%. The silver lining (?) there is that the SSS contraction was now driven almost entirely by price; transaction count was flat. This suggests management’s efforts to perk up demand are having an impact. We view management’s visible shift to be more price competitive (through “price refreshes”) as a necessary and positive move to maintain its long-term growth trajectory, despite the immediate impact on profitability. The short-term macro outlook, however, still looks challenging.

CONSUMER
WLCON 4Q24: Results disappoint as demand remains weak

FY24 net profit drops 27%

WLCON posted a FY24 profit of P2.5bn, 27% lower YoY—behind consensus expectations. For the 4Q alone, net profit dipped even lower by 46% YoY to P410mn as operating profit fell by 41% to P682mn.

 

SSS continues to contract

Revenue in 4Q was down 2% YoY, still weak relative to the 1% decline in 9M24 as demand remained soft in the quarter. SSS continued to be negative all throughout the year, down 7% in 4Q, even worse than 9M24 (-5.9%). Management attributed the weakness in demand to the downturn in residential property. The possible silver lining in SSS contraction is that transaction count was virtually flat (-0.2%), suggesting efforts to perk up demand are having an impact.

 

Margins down on “price refreshes” and higher opex

Gross margin declined in the 4Q as it fell 90 bps QoQ to 37.9% as WLCON undertook what it calls “price refreshes” in a bid to boost demand. Operating margin for the quarter deteriorated further dropping 3.4ppts from 11.4% in 3Q to 8.0% in 4Q as the company ramped up marketing expenses in all categories and saw higher labor costs.

 

Focus on top line

Management made it clear that it was focusing on reviving top line growth, even at the expense of short-term margins. Apart from the actions on pricing, the company is introducing organizational changes as well as changes to its store layout at a few stores in an attempt to boost sales. We should see whether these efforts will bear fruit in the next few quarters.

 

Subdued guidance; modest pick-up in early 2025

Management is guiding for mid-single digit revenue growth, despite the planned addition of as many as 8 new stores (from 100 as of end-2024). These figures imply that SSS for FY25 will be flat at best. Notably, feedback on the first months of 2025 was positive as they see modest improvement YTD in terms of average daily sales but whether this can manifest into a meaningful turnaround will hinge on the success of new strategies and overall market recovery.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.16bn in Feb
  • The February trade deficit narrowed to US$3.16bn, down 11% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports and growth recovery in exports
    • This is the lowest deficit in four years
  • Imports for the month inched down 2% YoY to US$9.41bn, a significant slowdown from the 11% increase in Jan
    • Primarily driven by the decline in fuel imports, which was down 23% YoY to US$1.3bn, negating the increases in other major goods categories
  • Exports grew 4% YoY to US$6.25bn, the second month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction
    • Electronics exports grew by 3% YoY to $3.52bn, a turnaround from the 3% YoY decline in Jan
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 6% YoY to US$2.73bn

SSI Group acquires Rustan Marketing Corp
  • SSI Group (SSI PM) has acquired Rustan Marketing Corp. (RMK) for P232mn
  • SSI said that the acquisition will allow the Group to become a listed multi-channel distributor (specialty stores, supermarkets, wholesale distribution and department stores) of premier brands in the Philippines.
  • RMK, owned by the Tantoco family- who is the principal shareholder of SSI, is a multi-channel, wholesale distribution company, with a network of more than 1,300 wholesale and retail outlets
  • It is the exclusive wholesale distributor of brands such as Samsonite, American Tourister, Tefal Cookware, Lacoste Fragrances, Maison Margiela Fragrances, Spanx, Nuxe Skincare, OPI Nail Polish and Nine West Footwear.
  • In 2024, RMK reported unaudited revenues of P1.085bn, EBITDA of P111.2mn, and net income of P44.2mn.

CNPF targets mid-teen growth in 2025
  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) Chief Operating Officer Gregory Banzon guided for mid-teens growth for both top line and bottom line
    • He highlighted that the company had consistently posted ~13% sales and income growth and thus seeks to be consistent
  • The COO also gave guidance on capex, allotting about P3bn mainly for boosting production capacity for the tuna, coconut, and pet food segments
    • This was similar to 2024's capex spend of P3-4bn
  • Banzon also noted that the company cut prices for their canned meat, tuna, and sardines products as commodity prices corrected to ease the burden of the consumer
  • CNPF has yet to report its FY24 earnings but as of 9M24 posted a net income of P5.3bn (+14% YoY) with revenues up 13% to P56.9bn on the back of better exports and branded segments
    • Regis and Consensus expectations for CNPF's FY24 net profit is at P6.53bn (+17%)
