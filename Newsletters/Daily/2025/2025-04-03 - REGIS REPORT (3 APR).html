REGIS REPORT

03 April 2025

The US proposed new “reciprocal tariffs” scheduled to take effect next week 09 April, which will likely cause a massive reordering of the world’s economic landscape. In this sense, the Philippines could be among the least affected – at least directly – by the announcement. Which isn’t to say it couldn’t still be bad. But it’s all relative. Philippine exports will be levied a tariff of 17%, not far off the 10% baseline for all exports into the US from any country. Within Asia, that’s very close to the bottom, higher only than Singapore.

 

 

On paper, the Philippines exports just $12bn to the US (and imports $8bn from there). That’s equivalent to about 2.5% of nominal Philippine GDP. Also, we understand that the tariffs – at least for now – exempt semiconductors. We note that in 2023, semiconductors accounted for 33% of our exports to the US. Another 10% was classified as “Electronic Data Processing”, which may or may not be included in the exemption. Of course it’s much more complicated than that. A good portion of PH exports to Japan / China, etc are probably re-exported to the US. But that’s much tougher to estimate.

 

The economy is also much less dependent on manufacturing in general. Industry accounts for just 28% of GDP, with Services taking the lion’s share at 63%, and Agriculture at 9%.

 

Among the listed companies, the direct impact will likely be very limited.

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) – roughly 22% of revenues in 9M24 was OEM (presumably mostly tuna and coconut water). Management estimate “mid-single-digits” is exported directly to the US.
  • Emperador (EMI PM) – about 40% of revenues is whiskey, though we don’t know how much is sold directly to the US.
  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) -  its subsidiary IMI (IMI PM) has 68% of its revenues linked to the global auto supply chain. IMI is already loss-making to the tune of $50mn in 2024.
  • Meralco (MER PM) – roughly a quarter of electricity sales in their franchise area goes to industrial users
  • Banks – roughly 10% of total loans are to the manufacturing sector

 

The bigger question will be for ICTSI (ICT PM), and how “Liberation Day” will affect overall global trade. While the company claims most of their volumes come from imports into the economies of their host countries, there will surely be some impact.

 

Is there a silver lining for the Philippines? The tariffs could mean that exporting countries will look to sell more goods to non-US economies cheaper to offload inventory, which could benefit us in the short-term. Also, with lower tariffs compared to our Asian / ASEAN neighbors, could the Philippines benefit from some manufacturing / re-export capacity moving to here?

BANKS
FX deposit growth slowed sharply in 4Q24 (down QoQ)

Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDUs) of the banking system saw FX-denominated deposits at $55.46bn, up just 1.9% YoY, decelerating sharply from the 11% in 3Q and 14% YoY pace in FY2023. More notably, FCDU deposits were actually lower by 3.5% QoQ, which is unusual as 4Q is typically a strong quarter. To be fair, the PHP had begun to strengthen in late 4Q, which could have prompted selling back into PHP. Given the USD’s weakness so far YTD, we would not be surprised to see modest growth in FCDU deposits in 1Q25.

 

In PHP terms, FX-denominated deposits grew 6.4%, but contracted 0.4% QoQ. This compares to the 7.0% YoY growth in PHP-denominated deposits for the 4Q24 (+4.9% QoQ).

 

Meanwhile, FCDU loans grew 4.3% YoY to $15.82bn, picking up from the 1.6% YoY in 3Q24 and from the 3.9% YoY contraction in 4Q23.

 

FCDU deposits made up 16.1% of total deposits, while FCDU loans were just 6.2% of total loans. Both ratios were steady compared to 2023.

 

Crucially, the LDR of the banking system’s FCDU books was just 28.5%. While this was up slightly from the 27.9% in 2023, it is still far lower than the pre-pandemic peak of 44% set in 2019.

 

This means that the banks’ PHP LDR was already quite tight at 81.1%. We believe this is partly the reason that the BSP cut RRR by 250bps in October 2024, and by another 200bps last week (to 5.0%).

February gov't budget deficit widens 4.1% YoY to P171.4bn
  • The government recorded a budget deifict of P171.4bn which is wider by 4.1% YoY but a reversal from the surplus of P68.4bn in Jan 2025
    • Revenue collections in Feb grew 12.4% YoY to P251.8bn, primarily driven by growth in tax revenues which is up 10.9% YoY
      • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 15.7% to P159.7bn mainly due to an increase in collections of corporate and personal income taxes, value-added tax, excise tax on tobacco and alcohol, and documentary stamp tax.

      • Non-tax revenues increased by 12.6% to P17.4bn from higher treasury income, PAGCOR profit, and privatization proceeds
  • Expenditures are up 8.9% YoY in Feb to P423.2bn as both primary expenses and interest payments were up 1.3% and 9.9%, respectively
    • Primary spending for the month rose 9.9% YoY to P374.8bn
      • The increase in primary spending was attributed to the (a) capital expenditures of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), (b) health and social protection programs
    • Interest payments increased slightly by 1.3% from higher coupon payments on domestic securities but offset by lower foreign payments
  • The gov't for 2025 targets the budget deficit to narrow to 5.3% of GDP from 5.7% in 2024, and the government aims to further reduce this to 3.7% in 2028

 

 

 

MARKET
SEC relaxes investment limits for equity and multi-asset funds
  • The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in a memorandum circular issued on March 28, eased the limits imposed on various investment funds investing in any single business group (SBG)
    • The limits will be eased for equity funds, balanced funds, and multi-asset funds exposed to equity securities
  • Previously, investment companies were prohibited from allocating more than 20% of their net assets to transferable securities, money market instruments, deposits, and OTC financial derivatives issued by any single business group (SBG)
    • i.e., a conglomerate and its listed entities under the old rule will be classified as one SBG and will be subject to the 20% aggregate SBG limit
  • Under the new rule, the limit now applies to investments issued by a single entity or issuer rather than a single business group, provided the funds do not have actual exposure to financial derivatives
    • An SBG is defined as a company and its related entities, including subsidiaries and parent firms
    • This SBG limit was imposed back on May 15, 2020, and was revisited following the requests from fund managers
  • The new rules will be effective immediately
  • Additionally, the SEC will not impose fines or penalties for breaches of the SBG limit from May 15, 2020, to March 27, 2025. However, violations of the revised single-issuer cap will be subject to penalties under existing regulations
    • Funds that have cross-border offerings in other ASEAN countries however must comply with the 20% SBG limit in compliance with ASEAN standards

Batangas LNG plants to attain full capacity by May — DoE
  • The Department of Energy (DoE) expects sufficient power supply during the summer, as two gas-fired power plants in Batangas are set to generate a full combined capacity of 2,500 megawatts (MW) by mid-May 
    • According to Energy Undersecretary Rowena Cristina L. Guevara, instead of the 1,350 MW produced from the two plants there will now be 2,500 MW.
  • South Premiere Power Corp. (SPPC) and Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. (EERI) implemented a three-day temporary shutdown of their gas fired power plants in Batangas, and operations have since resumed, increasing output to 1,350 MW from the previous 1,300 MW
  • Major energy firms, including Meralco PowerGen, Aboitiz Power, and San Miguel Global Power, have invested $3.3 billion to launch the country’s first LNG facility.
    • Under the agreement, MGen and Therma jointly invested in two of SMGP’s gas-fired power plants: the 1,278 MW Ilijan power plant and the new 1,320 MW combined cycle power facility
  • According to Energy Assistant Secretary Mario C. Marasignan, while electricity supply is adequate, there is still a  possibility of yellow or red alerts due to reserve shortages
  • The DoE urges consumers to adopt energy-saving measures, as peak demand is projected at 14,769 MW for Luzon, 3,111 MW for Visayas, and 2,789 for Mindanao. Maximum adjusted available generating capacity is 15,504 MW for Luzon, 3,040 MW for Visayas, and 3,313 MW in Mindanao
