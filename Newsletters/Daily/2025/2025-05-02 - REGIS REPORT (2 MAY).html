Metrobank (MBT PM) reported NPAT of P12.3bn, up 2.1% YoY, bang in line with our expectations. There were many issues – cross-currency swaps, low provisions in 1H25 – that make the YoY numbers a bit noisy. Overall though, the result was quite good. Loan growth of 16% YoY was a shade slower than the 17.0% posted in 4Q. However, this was still much faster than the 12-13% reported by peers BDO and BPI, suggesting MBT is taking market share, particularly in the corporate segment. Margins were up 5bps QoQ on lower funding costs. We published a report, refreshing our 2025/26E and introducing new 2027E forecasts. MBT stock is trading on 0.83x P/bk and under 7x PER, while paying a dividend yield of 6.5%. Maintain Buy with a target price of P100/sh.

The numbers for Aboitiz Power (AP PM) based on the press release it issued on Wed look quite disappointing. Core income fell 40% to P4.7bn (vs P7.7bn).  The company attributed the decline to “scheduled maintenance” outages as well as weaker electricity prices.  The company will hold its briefing on May 13.

Based on our preliminary estimates, it’s looking increasingly probable that there will be changes in the composition of the PSEi this coming Aug and those changes could involve the deletion of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM). BLOOM is already outside the top 35 biggest market cap firms based on the averages from July 2024. With BLOOM’s share price now even lower, it appears a tall order for the stock to bounce back to within the top 35 by June.  If BLOOM indeed exits, the likely replacement will be another gaming name: Digiplus (PLUS PM).

Property research firm Colliers estimates that Metro Manila office vacancy for 1Q was largely unchanged at 19.7%, despite closures of the POGOs.

Gaming agency PAGCOR’s revenue from gaming operations grew 14% YoY in 1Q25. Despite the reduction in fees on e-games operators, revenue from this segment climbed 48% YoY to P14.32bn. Meanwhile, 1Q25 revenue from licensed casinos increased 3% YoY, despite the POGO ban.  

 

 

Belle Corp (BEL PM) hopes to get a license for a casino in Clark by late-2025. Should it secure a license, it will get an operator for the integrated resort. This is similar to its structure in Entertainment City, where it has a partnership with Melco Resorts.

The country’s trade deficit for Mar widened 23% to US$4.1bn as import growth (12%) outpaced export growth (6%). YT-March, the trade deficit stands at US$12.7bn (+7%).

  • We believe the likelihood of Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) being removed from the PSE Index during the August 2025 rebalancing has increased
    • Should it be removed from the PSEi, DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) will likely take its place

    • 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35

MACRO
Trade deficit widens to $4.1bn in Mar
  • The March trade deficit widened to US$4.1bn, up 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in imports than exports
    • This brings the 1Q25 trade deficit to US$12.7bn,13% up YoY
  • Imports for the month grew 12% YoY to US$10.7bn, a reversal from the 2% decline in Feb
    • Primarily driven by the growth in imports across all categories except fuel
  • Exports grew 6% YoY to US$6.59bn, the third month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction
    • Electronics exports were flat at $3.64bn, a slowdown from the 3% YoY increase in Feb
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 13% YoY to US$2.95bn

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
BSP guides for April inflation to settle within 1.3 to 2.1%
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) forecasts April inflation to remain low and be between 1.3% to 2.1%
    • The prior month ahead inflation forecast range was 1.7% to 2.5%, with March inflation at 1.8%
    • Note that the inflation rate has continued to be on a downtrend since 2.9% in Jan to 2.1% in Feb, and to 1.8% in March
  • Easing prices of rice, fruits, and vegetables from improving domestic supply conditions alongside lower oil prices and peso appreciation contributed to downward price pressures
    • But this may be offset by higher electricity rates and LRT-1 fares
  • The gov't targets to keep inflation within 2-4% in 2025
    • Bloomberg median consensus for the full year average is at 2.8%
    • We expect inflation to average 3.0%
  • The Philippine Statistics Authority will announce March inflation figures on May 6, Tuesday
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of six this year) will be on June 19
MACRO
Review of minimum wage rates to start this May
  • The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) is set to start the review of minimum wage rates in some regions and National Capital Region (NCR) in the month of May
    • Labor consultation and employer consultation will be conducted in May, while a public hearing will be held in June
    • The result of the wage deliberations is expected to be announced in July
  • Numerous labor groups have been urging the President to certify the P200 legislated wage hike bill for private sector workers as urgent before the 19th Congress ends on June 30, 2025
    • The P200 wage hike bill (House Bill 11376) was approved on second reading last February and is currently on the third and final reading in The House of Representatives
    • While The Senate's version of the bill (Senate Bill No. 2534) which seeks a P100 daily minimum wage hike has been approved on third and final reading last February
    • Note that a bill becomes law only when it is passed by both houses of Congress, then processed through a bicameral conference committee, and approved by the President
  • Recall that last July 2024, the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved the P35 hike to P645 for the daily minimum wage of workers in NCR
    • To note, 16 regions have also implemented wage hikes and 28 wage orders have been approved since June 2024
    • The last legislated (nationwide) wage hike that was approved was back in 1989 which created the RTWPBs and set a P25 daily wage increase
  • President Marcos in his Labor Day speech mentioned that they are listening to the call for better wages as the regional wage boards are reviewing current pay rates
    • But he also emphasized the importance of considering the effect wage hikes will have on the growth of businesses, jobs, and the economy
