REGIS REPORT

14 July 2025

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr will meet with US President Donald J Trump during the former’s visit to the US on 20-22 July. The agenda will be to discuss the 20% tariff announced by the US, among other things. A delegation of Philippine officials will be sent ahead of the trip, to negotiate with their US counterparts. Recently, members of the Cabinet have suggested that the talks could include free trade agreements either on a broad, or targeted basis.

 

The Philippines is less reliant on trade than our Asian/ASEAN neighbors. However, there was some hope that a large tariff differential could encourage some manufacturing capacity to move to the Philippines. A much tighter gap between the tariff on the Philippines vs, say Vietnam, could dash these hopes.

 

+ + +

 

Pressure continues to mount against online gambling. The regulator PAGCOR is seeking to severely limit the advertising of e-gaming, most recently saying it will stop TV ads during prime time. In addition, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto says he is working with Congress to impose a tax on online gambling, hopefully by end-2025.

 

Meanwhile, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has released a new Draft Circular on online gambling and e-payments. Among the proposals is requiring the user to create of a special wallet-within-a-wallet called an Online Gaming Transaction Account (OGTA) which will be only way to fund gaming deposits. The BSP aims to limit daily deposits into the OGTA to just 20% of a user’s average daily balance. More interestingly, the BSP says that once a user creates an OGTA, the user will not be able to access loans from that wallet provider.

 

+ + +

 

Foreign ownership of Philippine stocks edged down again in June, to a new low of 18.9% (down 4bps MoM). For the PSEi constituents, the figure dropped 9bps MoM to 23.9%. Among the notable drops in foreign participation include Bloomberry (BLOOM PM), ACEN (ACEN PM), and  PLDT (TEL PM). Bucking the trend were ICTSI (ICT PM), GT Capital (GTCAP PM), and AREIT (AREIT PM), which all saw increases in foreign ownership MoM.

GAMING
BSP issues draft regulations on online gambling payment services
  • The BSP issued draft regulations on online gambling payment services
    • In order to participate in online gambling, a specific account, called Online Gambling Transaction Account (OGTA) must be created
    • Transfer of funds to the OGTA shall be subject to a daily limit not exceeding 20% of the average daily balance of the owner’s transaction account
    • Online gambling payment services can only be offered within a 6-hour window daily
    • Upon creation of the OGTA, all lending options in the same digital platform shall be disabled
    • The account owner will also be able to further restrict the daily limits to be transferred to the OGTA and will have the option to disable the OGTA
  • Separately, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that a new law taxing online gambling could be passed within the year
    • Online gaming firms currently pay 38% of their e-casino and e-bingo GGR to the government. They pay 24.25% of sports betting GGR to the government

MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls slightly in June
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 4bps MoM to 18.9% (in June) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 9bps MoM to 23.9%
  • Net foreign selling fell to US$72mn, from net selling of US$259mn in May
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 11 saw increases in foreign ownership while 19 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • BLOOM – saw the biggest decline in foreign ownership for the third consecutive month. Foreign ownership fell 1.16 percentage points MoM to 11.3%. BLOOM saw US$9.4mn of outflow on the last trading of May (partly due to its deletion from the MSCI small cap index) and this was only reflected in the June foreign ownership figure. 
    • ACEN – foreign ownership dropped 44bps MoM to 24.2%, as it experienced net foreign selling of US$6.7mn.   
    • TEL – foreign ownership declined 32bps MoM to 34.6%, as net foreign selling hit US$12.0mn, the third highest in the market.
    • AREIT – foreign ownership rose 4.1 percentage points MoM to 8.2%. We suspect that this was an error.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership increased 39bps MoM to 21.8%. Net foreign buying amounted to US$7.3mn, the fifth highest for the month
    • ICT - foreign ownership increased 31bps MoM to 34.4%, as net foreign buying hit US$21.6mn, the second highest for the month.

 

POWER
Malampaya’s $893mn MP4 drilling project is on track for completion in 2025
  • The $893mn Malampaya Phase 4 (MP4) drilling project of the Malampaya gas field in offshore Palawan is on track for completion this year, with all drilling developments expected to be completed by November 2025
  • The project aims to prolong the production life of the Malampaya deep water gas-to-power project and deliver a new gas supply by 2026
  • It involves drilling two new deep-water wells, Camago-3 and Malampaya East-1, along with a new exploration field, Bagong Pag-asa-1
    • The drilling of Camago-3 is expected to be finished by late July or early August
  • The Malampaya gas field, which supplies about 20 percent of Luzon’s electricity, is the country’s first and only indigenous gas resource, governed by Service Contract 38
  • Razon-led Prime Energy Resources Development and its partners, UC38 LLC, PNOC Exploration Corp., and Prime Oil and Gas Inc., operate the contract, which has been extended until February 2039
POWER
Power rates rise in July due to higher generation costs and weak peso
  • The overall power rate for a typical household will increase by P0.49/kWh or 4% after two months of rollbacks
    • Households that consume 200 kWh of electricity will see their bills go up by around P98 as the effective rate climbs to P12.64/kWh
    • This offsets the P0.86/kWh or 6% reduction from May and June
  • Generation charges up 3% as global tensions push fuel prices higher
    • Generation costs rose P0.34/kWh while transmission and taxes added P0.07 and P0.07 respectively
    • PSA and IPP rates jumped P0.45 and P0.50/kWh due to the weaker peso and higher global fuel prices after the 12-day Israel-Iran conflict
  • The decline in spot market rates in the month of June helped temper the hike
    • WESM charges dropped P0.17/kWh or 1.5% as Luzon demand fell by 770 MW in June, more than offsetting 490 MW in power plant outages
