REGIS REPORT

23 April 2025

The leadership of Globe (GLO PM) was officially passed on to Carl Raymond Cruz from Ernest Cu yesterday. Cu retired as CEO after taking Globe to the leading spot on the mobile sector, from being a far #2.  Cruz was previously with Airtel and Unilever in Nigeria before returning home to the Philippines. In his first public interview upon taking over, Cruz says he will aim to overtake PLDT (TEL PM) and make Globe the biggest telco (not just the biggest mobile firm).

 

Possibly the biggest takeaway from that is that Globe could pour significantly more resources into the non-mobile segments: enterprise and broadband. The broadband and enterprise segments are still expanding in the 8-10% range, while mobile revenue is growing in the 2-3% range. Such a focus could mean greater competitive intensity in the broadband and enterprise sectors.

 

+++

 

Meanwhile, PLDT (TEL PM) issued a press release saying that its 37.7%-owned associate Maya Innovations recorded a positive net profit in 1Q25. Maya is the holding company that houses Maya Bank and the Maya e-wallet and payments enabler. PLDT management has been guiding for Maya’s bottom line moving into the black this year.

 

We have to wonder if this will precipitate PLDT paying a high price to increase its stake beyond 37.7%. One of Maya’s other shareholders was previously reported to be eyeing a sale of its 20% interest in Maya.

 

+++

 

FY24 net profit of Alliance Global (AGI PM) fell 12% to P17.2bn, as earnings of three of its subsidiaries (Emperador, Travellers, and Golden Arches) declined.

 

  • Emperador (EMI PM) whisky revenue fell 2% while its brandy sales declined 9%.
  • Although mass GGR of Travellers grew 9%, VIP GGR fell 20%, leading to a 6% decline in aggregate GGR.
  • Despite the 9% increase in the systemwide sales of Golden Arches (which operates McDonald’s Philippines), EBIT fell 3% due to higher input costs and minimum wage increases.
  • Attributable profit from Megaworld (MEG PM) increased 13%, with revenue of every segment outperforming our expectations.

 

+++

 

Foreign ownership (%) of PSE-listed stocks declined another 17bp to 19.1% in March, despite the nominal net foreign buying of US$50m

MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in March
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 17bps MoM to 19.1% (in March) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 26bps MoM to 24.1%
    • The reduction in foreign ownership was partly due to the removal of JGS and URC from the MSCI Index in February. The net foreign outflow on the last trading of February was only recorded in March.
    • Furthermore, PSE Index constituents with high foreign ownership, such as MONDE, GLO, ICT, TEL, and SM, underperformed the index, bringing down the foreign ownership level of the PSE Index (and the market).  
  • Net foreign buying amounted to US$50mn, a reversal from net foreign selling of US$145mn in February
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 9 saw increases in foreign ownership while 21 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • URC – foreign ownership fell 1.52 percentage points MoM to 20.2%. Although it was removed from the MSCI Index in February, the net foreign selling only the last trading of the month was only reflected in March.  
    • JGS – foreign ownership dropped 1.40 percentage points MoM to 12.9%. Although it was removed from the MSCI Index in February, the net foreign selling only the last trading of the month was only reflected in March.  
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership declined 1.06 percentage points MoM to 13.9%, as it experienced $7.7mn of net foreign outflow.
    • ALI – foreign ownership declined 70bps MoM to 25.3%. It also saw $32.5mn of net foreign outflow, the highest in the market.
    • AC – foreign ownership rose 34bps MoM to 27.4%. Net foreign buying hit US$6.5mn, the fifth-highest for the month,
    • BDO – foreign ownership increased 21bps MoM to 28.3%, as it saw the highest foreign buying for the month, at US$28.3mn.
