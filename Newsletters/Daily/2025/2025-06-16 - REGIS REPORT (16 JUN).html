REGIS REPORT

16 June 2025

Gaming firm Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) says it aims to capture 20% of the online gambling market within two years. BLOOM is revamping its online gaming service and will be launching its app-based product in a few weeks. Online gaming has seen massive growth over the past three years and is now a P200bn/year industry, around the same size as all privately-run brick-and-mortar casinos. The market leader in online gaming is Digiplus (PLUS PM) with market share of roughly half.

 

Meanwhile, BLOOM also announced the appointment of Greg Hawkins as its president / COO. Hawkins was made interim COO following the departure of Tom Arasi.

GAMING
Bloomberry aspires to be market leader in online gaming
  • Cyrus Sherafat, Head of Bloomberry’s gaming operations, said that the company is targeting 20% market share in online gaming in two years
  • We estimate this would be equivalent to about P40bn in GGR
    • This could increase our FY27E GGR forecast by about 45%, our FY27E EBITDA forecast by about 30% (to c. P26bn), and raise our FY27E net profit forecast by about 110% (to P11bn)
    • BLOOM’s FY24 GGR was P62bn
  • The company aspires to eventually be the market leader in online gaming
    • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is currently the market leader, with c. 45% market share
  • Bloomberry also appointed Gregory Francis Hawkins as President and COO
  • He had been Acting President and COO Since December 2024, when his predecessor, Thomas Arasi, retired
  • Mr. Hawkins joined Bloomberry in November 2023 as head of Solaire North
  • He was previously Chief Casino Officer with the Star Entertainment Group, CEO of Crown Melbourne, President of City of Dreams Macau, and CEO of Altira Hotel Macau
  • BLOOM is trading at 8.7X FY25E EV/EBITDA, similar to the Macau gaming firms 
MACRO
PH current account deficit balloons to $4.2bn in 1Q25
  • The country posted a CA deficit of US$4.2bn (3.7% of GDP) in 1Q25, doubling from the US$2.1bn (1.9% of GDP) deficit in 1Q24
  • The widening deficit was driven by the Philippines' wider merchandise trade gap and slower services trade
    • The trade-in-goods deficit was wider 15% YoY to US$ 16.8bn, given the faster growth in imports (+8%) vs. exports (+1.2%)
    • Net receipts from services trade fell 9% YoY to US$3.3bn, as imports grew 1.7% while exports slowed 1.5%
      • The decline in services receipts was primarily due to lower earnings from transport services (from US$1.1bn to US$755mn) and technical, trade-related, other business services (from US$5.5bn to US$5.4bn)
      • This was offset by the growth in BPO export revenues which grew 1.3% to US$7.2bn from US$7.1bn last year
  • For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to hit US$19.8bn or -3.9% of GDP
    • This was revised earlier in the year from the previous US$12.1bn CA deficit (2.4% of GDP) projection back in 4Q24
    • Recall that in 2024, the country posted its second largest CA deficit at US$17.5bn (3.8% of GDP)—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of US$12.4bn (2.8% of GDP)

 

DIGITAL FINANCE/TELCOS
Maya expects continued profitability
  • Maya’s wallet, merchant acquiring, and banking segments all reported positive EBITDA and EBIT through May, extending overall profitability from its first profitable month last December 2024
  • The platform generated P311 million in net income for Q1 2025 and remained profitable in April and May, supported by positive cash flows over the first five months
    • Management projects Maya will contribute about P1 billion in profit for 2025. PLDT chairman Manuel Pangilinan said there are no immediate plans to raise PLDT’s equity stake
  • Total lending to date has reached P120 billion after Maya disbursed P28 billion in new loans in 1Q25
  • Deposits reached P43.6 billion as of March, roughly twice UnionDigital’s P22 billion and equivalent to a 61% share of the total deposits held by the country’s six licensed digital banks
  • PLDT owns 37.7% of Maya Innovations, which holds Maya Bank
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in May
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 13bps MoM to 18.9% (in May) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 7bps MoM to 23.9%
  • Net foreign selling climbed to US$259mn, from net selling of US$54mn in April
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 13 saw increases in foreign ownership while 17 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • BLOOM – saw the biggest decline in foreign ownership for the second consecutive month. Foreign ownership fell 76bps MoM to 12.4%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$12.4mn, the fourth highest for the month.  
    • ALI – foreign ownership dropped 58bps MoM to 24.6%, as it experienced net foreign selling of US$37.3mn, the highest in the market.   
    • URC – foreign ownership climbed 69bps MoM to 20.9%, as net foreign buying hit US$28mn, the second highest in the market.
    • CNVRG – foreign ownership rose 29bps MoM to 16.3%. It saw net foreign buying of US$5.3mn, the sixth highest for the month.
    • ICT – foreign ownership increased 23bps MoM to 34.0%. Net foreign buying amounted to US$50mn, the highest for the month

 

 

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air (CEB PM) pax volumes jump 22% in May
  • CEB flew 2.4mn passengers in May, up by 22% YoY
    • Domestic pax volumes grew 20% YoY to 1.8mn with seat load factors (SLF) up 22bps YoY to 91%
    • International pax volumes rose 28% YoY to 600k on seat growth of 26% (SLF up 99bps to 75%)
  • Brings YT-May pax volumes up 24% YoY to 11.7mn, in line with our FY25E of 28.5mn (+16%)

 

 

 

POWER
ERC sets final reserve prices for GEA-4
  • The ERC has issued the final ceiling prices for the fourth round of Green Energy Auction (GEA-4), covering 10,653 MW of solar and wind capacities
    • The green energy auction reserve (GEAR) price for ground-mounted solar (3,940 MW) was set at P5.68/kwh
    • GEAR for rooftop solar (48 MW) at P4.4832/kwh
    • GEAR for floating solar (3,175 MW) at P6.5258/kwh
    • GEAR for onshore wind (2,390MW) at P6.0859/kwh
    • GEAR for solar with BESS (1,100MW) at P5.4028/kwh
  • Auction is set for Sep 2; List of winning bidders will be released on Oct 22
  • Capacity is targeted for commercial operations from 2026-2029
UTILITIES
LWUA chief says overwhelmed with consumer complaints over Prime Water
  • The Local Water Utilities Administration (LWUA) said that they have been overwhelmed with complaints received over the poor service of Prime Water
    • According to LWUA Administrator Jose Salonga
  • LWUA wants Prime Water to address the water problems immediately
    • When asked if LWUA is considering a takeover if issues are not address, Mr. Salonga said “we’ll cross the bridge if we get there”
  • Prime Water has joint ventures with local water districts in Pampanga, Bulacan, Cavite, Leyte, Bacolod City and Malaybalay, Bukidnon
