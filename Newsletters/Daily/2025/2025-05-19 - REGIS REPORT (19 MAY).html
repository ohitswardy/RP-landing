REGIS REPORT

19 May 2025

At GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) 1Q25 analyst briefing last Friday, management admitted that Toyota Motor Philippines’ exceptionally high margin of 16.8% in 1Q25 is unsustainable and will likely normalize once pent-up demand of higher-margin vehicles subsides and the favorable PHP:USD exchange rate reverses. Meantime, reservation sales at its property unit rebounded strongly in 1Q25 due to strong commercial lot sales and sales of residual units at its BGC projects, bringing inventory levels down to a years’ worth of sales. De-leveraging at the parent has continued, with net debt levels down to P60bn from the peak of P88bn, with management prioritizing investing its capital for acquisition opportunities instead of raising dividends further.

Alliance Global (AGI PM) announced 1Q results on Friday, with profit at P8.4bn, inclusive of a gain (~P3.4bn) upon the de-consolidation of McDonald’s franchisee Golden Arches (GADC). Without that, income would have been P5bn, up 18% YoY.

 

Among the key highlights

 

  • Emperador (EMI PM) lost further market share domestically in 1Q25, but this appears to have been offset by better international numbers

 

  • Gaming revenue at Travellers was up 5% in 1Q, in contrast to the drops seen by other integrated resort operators

 

  • GADC profits dropped 42% in 1Q25.  AGI will also no longer consolidate GADC into its own accounts, supposedly because of possible disclosure conflicts. It previously did so despite owning only 49% of GADC.

 

+++

 

Banks’ asset quality improved marginally in March. By our calculation, industry NPL ratio improved 3bps MoM to 3.55%, while Past Due (PD) ratio was flat MoM at 4.55%. The numbers we quote are different than the headline reported number, since we exclude interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs) from the denominator of “total loans”. This is because the two can be quite volatile. In March, for example, RRPs surged close to P400bn MoM – equivalent to 2.5-3% of total loans. Loan growth for the month slowed slightly to 12.2% YoY from 12.5% in Feb. (In contrast, the headline data showed loan growth of 14.2%, solely due to the RRPs. By bank type, Digibanks saw a sharp deterioration in asset quality, with NPL ratio up 12bps MoM to 9.76%, while PD ratio surged 42bps to 17.33%.

Dito CME (DITO PM) finally released FY24 numbers on Friday. Its FY24 consolidated loss was P41bn with losses to Dito CME shareholders at P15bn. Consolidated debt on the balance sheet is now over P200bn, and the company is just around breakeven on an EBITDA basis.  Also worth pointing out is that revenue market share (estimate) is around 7.4% for the last quarter, a small uptick from 3Q.

TELCOS
DITO reports another large loss for FY24

DITO CME (DITO CME) finally released FY24 audited accounts.  Here are some key highlights: 

 

  • Consolidated net loss of P41bn for FY24, of which P15bn accrues to DITO CME shareholders.  Net loss for FY24 (inclusive of minority interests) was P41bn, while net loss for DITO CME (the listed entity) was P14.7bn.  The larger figure (P41bn) should be closer to what the net loss of Dito Telecommunity (Dito Tel) was for FY24.  Note that Dito Tel is consolidated into DITO’s financials, and accounts for virtually all of the P&L and Balance Sheet.

 

  • Economic stake in Dito Tel is 36%. DITO has been reducing its stake in the intermediate holding companies that own Dito Tel and its economic stake is only 35.6% despite it consolidating Dito Tel’s accounts (due to voting control). Recall a firm called “Summit Telco” is presently seeking to acquire voting control of Dito Tel. A change in control over Dito Tel, however, would require approval from Congress.

 

  • Revenue market share of ~7%.  We estimate 4Q25 market share of mobile revenue was 7.4% which is just marginally higher than the 7.3% in 3Q. 

 

  • Positive EBITDA of P0.4bn.  We estimate DITO managed to post a positive EBITDA of ~P400m for the year FY24, just above breakeven.  However, given that lease expenses were capitalized (and amortized), one can argue that DITO is really still below EBITDA breakeven.

 

  • Debt load of P184bn and capital deficiency of P73bn.  DITO’s consolidated debt was P184bn, roughly US$3.2bn, and leases would add another P24bn (US$420m).  This still does not include P32bn (US$560m) of payables to vendors.  The company has very little cash left (P1bn).  Consolidated capital deficiency (i.e. negative equity) has now ballooned to P73bn (US$1.3bn).
BANKS/DIGITAL BANKS
March - Asset quality improvements much milder than headlines suggest

This is why we look at loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) AND reverse repos (RRPs)… Headline NPL ratios improved 8bps MoM to 3.30%, and headline past due (PD) ratios dropped 6bps MoM. However, this was only due to a jump in RRPs to the tune of nearly P400bn MoM, equivalent to about 2.7% of total loans. We suspect this was due to the 200bps RRR cut from 7% to 5%, which became effective on 28 March 2025. Reported loan growth (including IBL/RRP) was 14.2%, when ex-IBL/RRP the “real” gross loans were up just 12.2% YoY. Aaaaaanyway… all numbers quoted below are ex-IBL/RRP.

 

Banking sector asset quality was flat-ish MoM in March. NPL ratio was 3.55% down 3bps MoM and down 4bps YoY. In nominal terms, NPLs were up 0.5% MoM and 11.1% YoY. Past Due (PD) ratio was flat MoM at 4.45%, but 10bps lower YoY, as the Peso value of PDs grew 1.3% MoM and 9.8% YoY. Restructured Loan (RL) ratio improved to 2.15%, down 2bps MoM, and down 13bps YoY, with nominal RLs up just 0.1% MoM to P311.bn. NPL Cover, meanwhile was 95.1%, slightly lower than the 95.4% in Feb, but more than 5%age points lower than the 100.7% in March 2024.

 

As mentioned, loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) in March was 12.2% YoY, slowing down from the 12.5% pace in February. System-wide LDR was 72.0%.

 

By bank type, the universal/commercial banks (UKBs) and thrift banks (TBs) saw improving PD and NPL trends. However, Digibanks saw NPL ratio jump 12bps MoM to 9.76%. Perhaps more worryingly – digibanks’ PD ratio jumped 42bps MoM to 17.33%.

 

The figures are encouraging. They are also consistent with the benign data from the Big Three banks. Of the banks we cover, only Security Bank (SECB PM) saw significant bump in NPL ratio (+25bps QoQ, vs all banks +9bps), which management says will be explained further at their briefing on 20 May, at 2pm.
