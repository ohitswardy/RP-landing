REGIS REPORT

17 March 2025

Here’s today’s snapshot of FY24 results:

  

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – FY24 profits came in at P42bn (+10% YoY) with core income, as reported at P45bn (+10%). Among the larger, listed entities, there were underperformances from Globe (GLO PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM). Losses at its unlisted units were not quite behind us, despite efforts to sell / write-off many over the past few years.  More below.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – Headline profits came in at P10.3bn, +18% YoY, which appears below Street estimates. While core operations look only slightly lower than expectations, the company took a charge relating to the transfer of its ownership in the Tim Ho Wan (THW) business.  The core domestic business looks in good condition but the same can’t be said for its China business. The company will hold its earnings briefing tomorrow. More below.

 

  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (-) – Core profit, as reported, grew 5% to P33.7bn, with EBITDA (“beneficial”) up 13% to P73bn. EBITDA growth was driven by improved margins and volumes, but the growth was trimmed at the income level by higher depreciation and financing charges.  More below.

 

  • DMCI (DMC PM) (=) – FY24 core profit of P18.97bn (-21% YoY) was in line with Regis estimates but came in below consensus forecast. Outside of Semirara, earnings disappointed for its unlisted subsidiaries, but these were made up for by better-than-expected profits at its associate, Maynilad. 4Q24 profits fell 11% YoY to P4.0bn, as its property and construction units suffered from weak demand and project delays.  Management is holding a briefing at 4pm today. 

 

+++

 

The country’s current account deficit for 2024 reached US$17.5bn, +41% wider than the previous year. This was 3.8% of GDP.

CONSUMER
JFC FY24 income up 18% to P10.3bn
  • Headline profit P10.3bn (+18% YoY).  This appears below Street estimates of P11bn.  While there’s a mild disappointment in operations, the big miss was really from a non-recurring item (below).  The results are not as “bad” as headline profit miss suggests. Without it, we believe earnings would have likely be in line with Street expectations.

 

  • Operating results a mild miss. Revenues grew 11% for the year and 10% for 4Q.  Operating profit rose 17% for FY24, with 4Q alone up 16%.

 

  • EBITDA for 4Q alone was down 11% but this was driven by the paper loss on its investment in Titan (~P560m – our estimate).  JFC includes earnings/losses on JVs and associates in its EBITDA calculation.

 

  • SSSG slowdown for PH, but China still struggling.  SSSG for PH was +7.5%, practically unchanged from 9M.  Meanwhile, SSS for the China business deteriorated further to -11.2% (from -9.9% in 9M).

 

  • One-off loss from Titan.  JFC recorded a loss of P711m for 4Q from JVs and associates (vs. P83m profit previous year).  We understand bulk of this (estimated P560m) is from a one-off adjustment relating to its purchase of Tim Ho Wan from Titan and should not recur.  JFC’s recorded a loss when its absorbed THW (as JFC paid a premium).

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp FY24 income up 10% to P42bn
  • Profits up 10% to P42bn.  Ayala Corp (AC) reported headline income of P42bn, up 10% YoY.  Its core income, as reported by AC, was also 10% higher at P45bn. We believe this is below Street expectations. Beyond the profit underperformances at Globe and ACEN, losses at smaller and unlisted subs are not quite behind us. The company took large impairment losses for its stake in the LRT-1 operation as well as for the last mile logistics provider Entrego.

 

  • IMI losses balloon.  IMI reported a net loss of US$50m for FY24, implying a loss of US$40m for 4Q alone. The losses were partly due to clean-up and turnaround initiatives, including impairment charges as well as the costs of plant shutdown/s and rationalization.  For the year, the Industrials segment (of which IMI is the biggest part) posted a loss of P2.4bn. AC management expects IMI to return to profitability this year (on “core” operations) with a full turnaround in 2-3 years, after which, we believe, a divestment may finally be on the cards again.

 

  • Vehicle sales ramping up. Vehicle sales under AC Mobility rose 46% to 23.5k but we expect investor focus should be on its BYD business.  From virtually nothing in FY23, the number of BYD units sold hit 5,106 (which is ~1% market share).  We believe market share can exceed 3% this year, if not more.  AC estimates it had 82% of the “NEV” market (EVs and hybrids) already.  To boost adoption, it aims to have 700 charging points by end-2025 (from 215 at end-2024). AC Mobility, however, still reported a P465m loss in FY24.

 

  • AC Health losses widen.  Losses at AC Health widened to P610m (vs P233m), partly due to impairment charges for Konsulta MD (KMD), the online health app that was eventually sold to the Metro Pacific group. AC Health’s target to reach EBITDA of P1b and profit breakeven has now been pushed back by one year to 2025.

 

  • AC Logistics secures partner. AC Logistics (ACL) saw losses rising to P2.2bn (vs P1.8bn).  ACL and AC signed an agreement for AP moller Capital (APMC) to take up to 40% interest in AC Logistics. This will hopefully provide expertise for the ACL to finally turn things around.

 

  • Parent capex at P31bn, bulk for ACEN.  For FY25, AC earmarked P31bn for capex at the holdco level of which more than half (P15.9bn) will be for ACEN.  ACEN’s intention to raise capital this year is well-flagged and this info from AC should give an idea of how much may raise . Much of the rest of the AC parent capex will be for AC Mobility and ACL.
POWER
Aboitiz Power’s FY24 core profit up 5% to P33.7bn
  • Core profit up 5% for the year; 4Q24 EBITDA slips but still up 20% YoY.  FY24 core profit rose 5% YoY to P33.7bn, with beneficial EBITDA up 13% YoY to P73bn. Derived 4Q24 EBITDA was P17.2bn while up 20% YoY, was actually down 13% QoQ due to seasonal factors. AP also booked ~P1bn of income in 4Q related to the suspended reserve market collections in 1Q.

 

  • Note income growth is lower than EBITDA growth in FY24 principally because of the depreciation and financing charges from GNPD, which became fully operational in 2Q24. These charges amounted to over P5bn (estimate) in FY24.

 

  • Stable margin outlook for generation biz.  Despite management’s subdued view on WESM prices and its spot exposure of ~30%, management believes it can maintain generation margins this year, given (a) lower Newcastle thermal coal and (b) the recently signed emergency power contracts. The company aims to lower its spot exposure to 10% by year-end.

 

  • Chromite completed; adding 388MW of RE in 2025; FY25 capex at P78bn.  AP started operating 500MW of RE capacity in 2024, taking its total RE capacity to 1.4GW, accounting for ~28% of its 5GW genco portfolio. Another 388 MW of solar plants could be added this year, with the company targeting to reach 4.6GW of RE capacity by 2030.  Note AP’s purchase, through the SPV “Chromite”, of an effective 26% stake (40% of 66%) in the 2,500MW gas plants of San Miguel (SMC PM) was also completed last month.

 

  • Given these expansions, the company is earmarking P78bn (attributable is >P50bn) for capex this year, up from last year’s P27bn.

 

  • Soft demand in 1Q25 so far. Energy sales from its distribution arm rose by 7% YoY in 4Q24, a notch lower than the 8% rate in 9M24. Residential demand slowed to 9% in 4Q while commercial/industrial was steady at +5%. However, demand has moderated in 1Q25 so far, specifically in the commercial/industrial segments in Cebu and Davao. The slowdown was also apparent in its RES clients in the cement and steel sectors.

 

 

CONGLOS
DMCI FY24 profit drops 21% YoY to P18.97bn – below consensus/In line with Regis FY24E
  • FY24 core profit (ex-FX adj) fell 21% YoY to P18.97bn, equivalent to P1.43/sh
    • The results are in line with Regis FY24E but below consensus’ EPS forecast of P1.7/sh
    • Outside of Semirara (SCC PM), profits disappointed across its key units, except for Maynilad, which only outperformed our forecasts due to lower-than-expected cash costs
  • In 4Q24 alone, core profit fell 11% YoY to P4.0bn due to lower thermal coal and power prices, infrastructure project delays, and higher revenue reversals at its property unit
    • These factors offset (a) higher nickel prices and (b) higher water tariffs
    • DMCI Homes’ reservation sales fell 34% QoQ, 35% YoY to P4.9bn, taking FY24 reservation sales to P33bn, 7% lower than the previous year and 11% below pre-Covid levels
    • Its cement arm, Concreat Holdings (CHP PM), recorded a wider EBITDA loss of P297mn for the month of Dec 2024 (vs. P213mn a year ago), as revenues declined 5% on lower ASPs
  • Management is holding a briefing at 4pm today (register here)

 

MACRO
PH current account deficit widens to $17.5bn in 2024
  • The country posted its second largest CA deficit at $17.5bn, equivalent to 3.8% of GDP—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of $12.4bn or 2.8% of GDP
    • For context, 2022's CA deficit was the record largest at $18.3 or 4.5% of GDP
    • The 2024 deficit breached the BSP's forecast of a $10.4bn (2.2% of GDP) CA deficit for the year
  • The larger than expected deficit was driven by the Philippines' heavy import bill and slower services trade
    • Recall that the country imported $68.7bn more goods than what was exported in 2024
      • Due to the FY24 seeing higher consumer goods imports which were up 6% YoY to $25.8bn and lower electronics exports down 7% to $38.1bn
    • There was also smaller net earnings posted from services trade to $14.6bn from $18.2bn in 2023
      • Service exports were only up 7.5% to $52bn relative to the 24% jump in service imports to $37bn
    • These led to more dollar outflows which added pressure on peso volatility
  • For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to be $12.1bn or 2.4% of GDP
UTILITIES
Maynilad gears up for P49.15bn IPO
  • In a disclosure to the Philippine Dealing System Holdings Corp., Maynilad’s Board of Director approved IPO plans that could raise as much as P49.15bn
    • It plans to offer up to 1.81bn common shares at an offer price of P20/sh
    • Filing also includes an overallotment option of 266.31mn common shares and an upsize option of up to 379.29mn common shares
    • Under its franchise, Maynilad needs to offer up to 30% of its shares to the public by Jan 2027
  • Maynilad’s board also approved an annual payout ratio of at least 50% of prior year’s income
  • Maynilad is owned by Metro Pacific (53%), DMCI (25%), and Marubeni (20%)
  • We value Maynilad at a 100% equity value of P166bn
TRANSPORT
Cebu Air - Feb pax volumes up 26% YoY, seat load factors improve further
  • Pax volumes rose 26% YoY to 2.2mn in February 2025, with domestic volumes growing by 29% and international up 20%.
    • Growth moderated from the 33% pace in January due to the timing of Lunar New Year
    • Seat load factors rose by 154bps YoY and 30bps MoM to 86.8%, as the market continues to fully absorb its capacity growth, which in Feb 2025 expanded by 24% YoY
    • The sequential improvement in load factors suggest that the market has absorbed the incremental capacity that was introduced in Sep 2024
  • Brings 2M25 pax volumes up 30% YoY to 4.7mn, trending ahead of our FY25 pax volume forecast of 27.7mn (+13% YoY)
