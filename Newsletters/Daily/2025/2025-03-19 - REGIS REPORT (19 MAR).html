REGIS REPORT

19 March 2025

Jollibee (JFC PM) held its FY24 earnings call yesterday to discuss its latest published results and its future plans. While earnings are broadly in line with our estimates, the divergence between the domestic and international segments couldn’t be more apparent.

 

Domestic business produced systemwide sales of P232bn (+10%) and revenue of P164bn (+9%), as same-store sales (SSS) rose 7.5%. We note that the segmental breakdown showed the domestic business delivered net income of P11.4bn, higher than the groupwide profit. This of course implies that the international business as a group contributed a net loss for the year.

 

The China business and Smashburger are clearly struggling, but management is steadfast in its belief that they can/will be turned around. Turning them around is a key condition for JFC to reach its goal of trebling profit to P24bn by 2028.

In its latest Monetary Policy Report, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) flagged quite a few risks (as it should, given its role overseeing monetary policy and also the banking sector).  The latest market situation relating to the inflation risks outlined in report, however, seems to be subsiding, notably on oil and food prices, and the direction of the PHP (vs USD).

 

The postponement of the Monetary Board’s (MB) meeting to April 10 (from April 3) allows it to see the March CPI print (Apr 4) before it makes a decision. We expect a policy rate reduction at that meeting.

MACRO
BSP wary of possible inflation target breach and slower economic growth
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its latest Monetary Policy Report stated that inflation could exceed the target range of 2-4% in the latter part of 2025, this year—given risks of supply chain disruptions, volatile commodity prices, and forex fluctuations
    • Specific key risk factors that will lead to a target breach cited by the BSP were: geopolitical tensions/production cuts pushing Dubai crude oil prices to average $100/pb in 2025, food supply disruptions (i.e., African swine fever), depreciation of the peso, potential wage hikes as well as transport and utility rate adjustments
    • The continued decline in rice prices however should alleviate risks to guide inflation  lower
  • The baseline inflation forecast for 2025 was revised to 3.5% from 3.3% last Dec but maintained its 3.5% 2026 projection
    • While the risk-adjusted inflation forecast was also raised to 3.5% from 3.4% but kept unchanged its 3.7% 2026 projection
    • Consensus expects inflation to be at 3.0% for 2025
  • Additionally, the BSP stated that economic activity may be negatively impacted—with 2025-2026 growth projected to be "near the lower bound" of the Marcos administration's target range of 6-8%
  • Considering inflation and growth risks, the BSP guided for "a continued measured transition towards less restrictive monetary policy settings" but will still further assess the impact of global policy uncertainty
    • Note that March inflation data is set to be released on April 4 while the next Monetary Board meeting is on April 10
    • For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps worth of cuts to 5.00% while we expect 50 bps
MACRO
January gov't budget surplus down 22.3% YoY to P68bn
  • The government recorded a budget surplus of P68bn which is down 22.3% from Jan 2024's surplus of P88bn but a reversal from the deficit of P330bn in Dec
    • Revenue collections in Jan grew 10.8% YoY to P467bn, primarily driven by growth in tax revenues which is up 13.6% YoY
      • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 15.1% to P355bn mainly due to an increase in VAT and income taxes as well as stronger collection efforts, crackdown on illicit trade, and digital transformation projects
      • Customs saw 8% growth to P79bn in collections from higher VAT and excise collections
      • Non-tax revenues dipped 26% to P30bn given the one-time gain recognized in Jan 2024
  • Expenditures jumped 19.5% YoY in Jan to P399bn as both primary expenses and interest payments were up 13.4% and 40.7%, respectively
    • Primary spending for the month rose 13.4% YoY to P294bn
      • The increase in primary spending was attributed to the (a) infrastructure/other capital outlays of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), (b) health and social protection programs, (c) higher National Tax Allotments (NTA) releases to gov't corporations, and (d) Commission on Elections (COMELEC) expenses for the 2025 National and Local Elections preparatory activities
    • Interest payments surged 40.7% given shifts in coupon payment timing with the re-issuance of treasury bonds that were issued in January last year alongside an earlier coupon payment for a Global Bond
  • The gov't for 2025 targets the budget deficit to narrow to 5.3% of GDP from 5.7% in 2024

 

 

 

POLITICS
Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences see 8 from the Marcos-admin slate
  • Based on the Pulse Asia survey conducted in late February, 13 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 available seats in the upcoming midterm elections on May 12, 2025
    • Bong Go (58.1%) and Erwin Tulfo (56.6%) secured the top spots, ranking 1st-2nd
    • Tito Sotto (49.0%) and Bong Revilla (46.1%) followed closely, ranking 3rd-6th
    • Bato Dela Rosa (44.3%), Willie Revillame (42.3%), and Ben Tulfo (40.7%) placed 4th-11th
    • Manny Pacquiao (39.9%), Lito Lapid (39.4%), Abby Binay (37.6%), and Pia Cayetano (37.5%) ranked between 6th-13th
    • Camille Villar (36.6%) and Ping Lacson (35.8%) secured places 8th-13th
  • Notably, only four of the probable 13 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go and Dela Rosa are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo, and Revillame are independent
  • This was Pulse Asia's third senatorial preferences survey after the filing of certificates of candidacy last October

GTCAP declares P5/sh dividend
  • GTCAP declared a regular dividend of P3/sh and a special dividend of P2/sh
    • The dividend is equivalent to a yield of <1%
    • Ex-date on April 1 and payment date on April 16
  • Recall that in 2024, GTCAP declared total of dividends of P8/sh, of which P5/sh was declared in 1H
    • Bloomberg consensus FY25E DPS at P8/sh
  • At GTCAP’s market price of P510/sh, it is trading at a 59% discount to market-based NAV of P1,256/sh and at 4.5x FY25E PER
