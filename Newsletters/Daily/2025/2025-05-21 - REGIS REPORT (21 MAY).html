REGIS REPORT

21 May 2025

At the briefing held yesterday, Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) provided some more insight into its 1Q results (released last week).  

 

The reported 1Q profit (P601m, -6% YoY) was a disappointment but the underlying numbers, while still trending below expectations, were not as bad as initially feared. Systemwide sales and revenue were both up +5% and, encouragingly, operating profit grew +9%. We share management’s view that the exodus of POGOs were an important factor in the softness.

 

More detail below.

 

+++

 

A small overhang on BDO shares? The Sandiganbayan (anti-corruption court) ordered the release of 33mn shares in BDO to the Romualdez family (heirs of the youngest brother of former First Lady Imelda R Marcos). The shares were seized by the Presidential Commission on Good Government back in 1986 just after the revolution that ousted Ferdinand Marcos, Sr. The shares were initially for PCI Bank, which merged with Equitable Bank in 1999, then later acquired by and merged with BDO in 2007. The shares are equivalent to about 0.625% of BDO’s outstanding shares, worth about P5.1bn (c$92mn) at yesterday’s close. That’s about 8.5 days of daily trading, or equivalent to a small overnight placement.

 

+ + +

 

Security Bank (SECB PM) revealed at its earnings call yesterday that its credit card portfolio caused the sharp jump in credit cost (c150bps) in 1Q25. Of the P2.4bn provision expense for the quarter, roughly P1.6bn was for credit cards, equivalent to about 3% of the P54 portfolio at end-2024. The cleanup of the book will continue into 2Q/3Q25. Management expects provisions to normalize in 3Q/4Q25 and is guiding for FY25 credit cost at 120-130bps (up from 90-100bps previously).

 

The frustration is that the credit card book was supposedly already cleaned up in 4Q23/1Q24. Our investment case for SECB was for a) strong revenue growth on consumer push, b) widening NIMs as interest rates fall (lower funding costs), c) moderating opex growth, and d) lower normalized credit costs. PPOP in 1Q25 was on track, up 24% YoY – impressive given the high base (one-off gain) in 1Q24. The repeat surge in bad debts in the credit card book pushes bank the bank’s goals of crossing above 10% ROE.

 

+++

 

The government is eyeing a minimum US$200m from the privatization of the CBK hydro facility, which is scheduled for next month. The US$200m minimum bid looks quite low, especially relative to its capacity (797MW).

CONSUMER
SEVN 1Q25 Post-Briefing Comments

1Q25 net profit -6% YoY to P601mn

Philippine Seven Corp (PSC) held a briefing yesterday to discuss 1Q25 results. Last week, the company reported net profit of P601mn in 1Q25, down 6% YoY, behind Regis expectations. The miss can be attributed to weaker-than-expected same store sales (SSS) and lower interest income. System-wide sales and revenues were both of up a disappointing 5% to P23bn and P21.8bn, respectively, while SSS contracted 1.2%. This implies that topline growth was primarily driven by new stores. Operating profit (P1bn +9% YoY) is also tracking behind expectations as higher pre operating expenses offset benefits from gross margin expansion.

Store rollouts counter decline in SSSG

Revenues rose a rather disappointing 5% YoY to P21.8 billion, as SSS contracted 1.2%. SEVN added 77 stores in 1Q, ending the period with 4,200. Revenue for the quarter were hampered by the impact of Holy Week timing (Apr ’25 vs Mar ’24), which is typically strong for convenience stores, as well as the leap year base effect. Management also believes that SSS was negatively affected by the POGO ban, which led to higher vacancies in office locations.

Higher pre-operating costs offset gross margin gains

Operating margin in 1Q was relatively unchanged YoY (+10bp) at 4.6%. The benefit from the gross margin improvement to 26.4% from 24.9% last year was outweighed by an increase in pre-operating expenses. Gross margins expanded as discounts from vendor support continue to increase in tandem with higher sales volumes. Pre-operating expenses in the quarter rose due to permitting delays.

Store expansion continues; improvements underway

The company is set to open 500 new stores this year with a capex budget of P5.5bn (vs. 399 new stores in 2024). Improvements on prior issues that burdened sales such as the lack of inventory of a specific vape brand (~1-2% of sales) and e-wallet payment downtime are supposed to be underway. Management says restocking for the popular vape brand is expected be resolved by July, while an upgrade of the e-payment system by 2H this year should improve downtime issues.

P5bn worth of BDO shares to be released from escrow
  • The Sandiganbayan (a special court that deals with cases over graft and corruption) has ordered the release of ~6mn PCIBank shares to the family of late Benjamin Romualdez
    • The PCIBank shares are equivalent to around 33mn BDO shares which is worth ~P5.1bn from yesterday's closing price
    • Note that the bank shares had changed hands due to mergers and are now part of BDO Unibank Inc. (BDO PM)
  • The case stems from Civil Case 0035, filed over 30 years ago, which accused Romualdez of illegally acquiring the shares through dummy buyers
    • These shares which were held by Romualdez' firm, Trans Middle East (Phils.) Equities, Inc. (TMEE), were sequestered by the Presidential Commission on Good Government (PCGG) decades ago
  • In July 2022, the Supreme Court ruled that the Sandiganbayan acted with grave abuse of discretion for not returning the shares after voiding the original sequestration order, but instead putting the shares in the Land Bank of the Philippines in escrow
    • This Supreme Court decision became final and executory in October 2023
  • In light of the decision, the Sandiganbayan can no longer retain legal custody over the previously sequestered assets and has tasked the PCGG and BDO to return the shares
    • PCGG and BDO were asked to report their actions on the matter within 15 days of the May 16, 2025 resolution
