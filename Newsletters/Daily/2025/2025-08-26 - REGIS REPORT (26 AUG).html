REGIS REPORT

26 August 2025

The new hotly debated bill further liberalizing the telecom / “data transmission” industry lapsed into law on Aug 24, thirty days after it was submitted to the Palace. The absence of the President’s signature or his veto on the “Konektadong Pinoy Act” (KPA) suggests an ambivalence to this piece of legislation, despite the fact it was part of the list of the administration’s priority bills. We reckon this ambivalence is a result of some provisions in the Act that may have been perceived as flawed.

 

Overall, we expect the KPA to increase competition in the telecom sector. We believe the changes envisioned under the KPA will have a bigger impact on the competitive environment for broadband than for mobile. Broadband is likely more vulnerable to competition because (1) new operators can easily come in on a localized basis and (2) broadband prices in the Philippines are still relatively high relative to the region (see Thailand and Vietnam prices). In contrast, one cannot offer a mobile service in a very limited geographical area only (thus significant initial resources are needed from day one). Furthermore, mobile data prices in the Philippines are already relatively low by global standards.

 

Overall, we expect this to be a net negative for the telecom operators, with the initial impact likely to be felt over the next one to two years.

TELCOS
Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) lapses into law
  • Konektadong Pinoy Act becomes law.  The bill further liberalizing the telecom sector (“Konektadong Pinoy Act”) has become law, thirty days after the approved bill was submitted to President.  The bill was the subject of fierce lobbying from both supporters and opponents.  While KPA is now law, the battles over its implementation are not over.  A possible Supreme Court fight looms and, simultaneously, jockeying over the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) can be expected.

 

  • No franchise needed for operators.  The Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) will allow operators to engage in “data transmission” services without needing to secure a franchise from Congress. This removes one barrier to entry for new operators but it is certainly not the only one. While that obstacle is not insignificant, it is an obstacle that has historically been overcome by acquiring small operators that already have franchises (recall Mislatel, which is now known as Dito Telecommunity).

 

  • Spectrum management policy framework. The new law also calls for the establishment of a “spectrum management policy framework” (SMPF) to govern the allocation and assignment of spectrum. The SMPF will also allow for the recall of spectrum in line with the SMPF.  The incumbent operators today each control a large swath of spectrum, and the implementation of a SMPF could put this at risk.

 

  • Mandatory, non-discriminatory sharing of infrastructure. The government will publish a list of infrastructure assets (“access list”) that it deems necessary to open up in order to increase competition and improve services. The entities that own these assets (“access providers”) are required to allow new operators to use such on a non-discriminatory basis.

 

  • Expansion of powers of DICT / NTC. The new law provides more powers to the DICT and NTC. Among these powers are the assignment and recall of spectrum, the issuance of licenses to new operators and also the inclusion or exclusion of assets that will existing operators will be required to share (and also the terms for such sharing).

 

  • Devil is in the details. The DICT will have some time to craft the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the KPA. How the IRR will be worded will determine how impactful this new law will be. Even if the incumbents are unable to overturn this law with a challenge on its constitutionality with the Supreme Court, we can expect them to exert their powers of persuasion to influence the final form of the IRR.
POWER
ERC asks Meralco, First Gen to explain impact of power contract extension; approves 100MW PSA between Meralco, GNPower Dinginin
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) asked First Gas Power Corp. and Meralco (MER PM) to explain the impact of their proposed contract extension to consumers
    • First Gas, a wholly-owned unit of First Gen (FGEN PM), owns the 1,000 MW Santa Rita natgas plant, which had a 25-year power purchase agreement with MER that was supposed to expire on Aug 17
    • ERC directed the companies to explain “whether the extension of contract will not result in an excess contracted capacity as well as a simulation on the impact thereof on the generation charge”
    • Based on MER’s generation charge in June, First Gas’ average generation cost stood at P9.52/kwh
  • ERC has approved a 15-year PSA between Meralco and GNPower Dinginin (GNPD), covering 100 MW
    • ERC granted a relief to conduct the PSA with a P2.3055/kwh total base fee starting Aug 26
    • Recall that GNPD offered to supply 400MW of mid-merit requirement at P7.6816/kwh in a competitive selection process (CSP) last Sep 2024
    • GNPD owns the 1,336MW coal-fired plant; Aboitiz Power (AP PM) owns an effective 70% stake in GNPD while Ayala Corp.’s (AC PM) ACEIC owns 20%
GAMING
Clark integrated resort operator Hann to defer IPO
  • According to Bloomberg, Hann Holdings CEO Dae Sik Han said that the company would defer its IPO
    • The company was previously slated to list in September 2025
  • It intends to offer 500mn primary shares at up to P23.6/share (total of P11.8bn)
    • With the overallotment option of 50mn shares, the maximum offer size is P13bn 
  • The deferment was due to prevailing market conditions
  • The company said that it would resume its listing “once market and industry conditions improve”
CONSUMER
DALI FY24 net loss widens by 5% to P1.97bn, revenues up 52% to P34bn
  • Hard Discount Philippines, Inc. (HDPI), operator of DALI Everyday Grocery, reported a net loss of P1.97bn for 2024, 5% wider than 2023's P1.9bn net loss
    • Revenues rose 52% to P34bn from P22bn
    • Gross margin expanded by ~3ppt to 9.8%
    • Total equity fell 73% to P729mn dragged by the company's wider retained earnings deficit from P3.26bn back in 2023 to P5.24bn end-2024
  • SyCip Gorres Velayo & Co. (SGV) noted that the wider deficit (+60% to P5.24bn) and surging liabilities (+111% to P20.3bn) "…indicate that material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern"
  • HDPI expects that profit margins will improve over the next five years with cost-efficiency measures to be put into place
    • The company noted that stockholders infused P7.6bn in additional capital (deposits for future stock subscriptions) as of end-2024
  • HDPI is a subsidiary of Singapore-incorporated HDPM Sin Pte. Ltd., with Switzerland-based Dali Discount AG as its ultimate parent

GAMING
Brazilian senator files bill to tighten rules on online gambling
  • Senator Humberto Costa filed a bill that seeks to tighten online gambling regulations in Brazil
    • Raising the minimum legal betting age from 18 to 21
    • Restricting online gambling advertising to between 22:00 and 06:00
    • Limiting monthly betting to R$1,502 (approx. $290) per person
    • Banning sponsorships of sporting, cultural, or educational events by betting companies
  • Brazil’s soccer clubs opposed the banning of sponsorships/advertising by betting firms, with 50 teams signing a joint statement urging lawmakers to vote against restricting advertising
  • Previously, Representative Luis Carlos Hauly, a federal congressman from the state of Parana, filed a bill to ban online gambling (including sports betting) throughout Brazil 
  • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) has an online gaming license in Brazil and is slated to begin operations in September 2025
