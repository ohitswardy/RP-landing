REGIS REPORT

22 October 2025

The power regulator, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), is reinterpreting the provision of the power reform law (“EPIRA”) requiring a public offering for power firms. This reinterpretation would give gencos and distribution utilities (DU) more time to comply with this provision.

 

The existing regulations have been in place for a while, but we are not aware of any firms that have been sanctioned for non-compliance with the public offering mandate. We are uncertain if that will change now if those non-compliant will be unable to meet this more relaxed requirement. Note, however, there are calls for amendments to EPIRA to repeal this provision requiring a public offering.

In an apparent attempt to revive tourism from China, the government is resuming the eVisa program for tourists from there beginning Nov. So far this year, tourism from China is down 22% from last year and is still 77% below pre-pandemic (i.e. 2019) levels. Chinese tourism to some areas in SE Asia (e.g. Thailand) has fallen sharply in favor of other destinations (e.g. Vietnam).

POWER
Power firms given more time to comply with public offering rules
  • The ERC has revised the rules requiring power generation firms and distribution utilities to offer 15% of their common shares to the public
  • They were previously required to offer shares to the public five years after the ERC authorized the company to begin operations
  • Now, the five-year window begins after the firm has met the prerequisites for a share offering
  • The PSE has certain prerequisites before listing on the SME Board
    • At least three years of operations prior to listing
    • Cumulative EBITDA, excluding non- recurring items, of at least Php15 million for the three (3) fiscal years immediately preceding the application; OR Cumulative operating revenues of at least Php150 million for the last three (3) fiscal years immediately preceding the filing of the listing application 
  • Only 40 of 264 generation companies have complied with the POR
  • Non-compliant firms represent nearly 14,000 megawatts of generation capacity

PH to resume eVisa program for Chinese tourists

TELCOS
Converge eyes to build 100 MW data center in Visayas or Mindanao
  • Converge ICT Solutions Inc. (CNVRG PM) CEO and Co-founder Dennis Anthony Uy stated that they seek to build a 100 MW data center either in Visayas or Mindanao for better internet reliability and speeds for subscribers in the VisMin region
    • The facility would generate an initial capacity of 20 MW but can be raised to as high as 100MW, depending on demand
    • This would be its largest data center aside from the data centers in Pampanga (10 MW) and Caloocan City (3 MW)
  • The plan to build a data center follows the recent linkage of the Bifrost Cable System to Davao which enables faster data transmission between the Singapore and US as well as other countries in close proximity
