REGIS REPORT

12 November 2025

We have the two major telcos, PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) in today’s results round-up, as well as DMCI (DMC PM) and Robinsons Land (RLC PM).

 

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (-) – Core income for 9M as reported by management came in at P28.5bn (+1%), with 3Q core income at P8.2bn (flat YoY). We note mobile revenue was up +1% in 3Q, though PLDT actually lost market share vs the previous quarter.  Broadband was a source of some optimism, given improved net adds and revenue.

  • Globe (GLO PM) (=) – Globe reported core income of P15.5bn, down 12% YoY, with 3Q at P5.0bn (-15% YoY). Broadband revenues are finally (back) above P6bn a quarter. Contributions from Mynt were down 27% QoQ to P1.5bn.

Gaming regulator PAGCOR reported that 3Q25 GGR of the e-games sector fell 28% QoQ to P45.7bn. For the month of September, when the full impact of the delinking of online gaming platforms from e-wallets was felt, GGR stood at just P12.2bn. Throughput volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Mexico port declined 3% YoY in October after a 23% surge in September, as operations returned to normal following the bottlenecks that began in May. We remain bullish on its Mexican operations, which we estimate accounts for ~20% of revenues, as its upcoming 20% expansion is well-positioned to benefit from the physical capacity constraints of its competitors.

CONGLOS
DMCI: 3Q25 profits down across segments
  • 9M25 recurring profit of P11.5bn (-23%) underperforms – Headline 9M25 profit fell 22% to P11.8bn. Excluding FX and one-offs, we estimate recurring profit stood at P11.5bn, down 23% YoY and lagging consensus’ FY25E of P18bn. Profits for 3Q25 alone fell 38% YoY/QoQ to P2.49bn, pulled down by lower thermal coal and spot prices at Semirara (see results comment), as well as broad-based weakness across other key segments. Note that DMCI did not report contributions from its associate, Maynilad (MYNLD PM), which will release results separately.

 

  • Property reservation sales rise 23% QoQ on RFO demand. Reservation sales rose 23% QoQ (-7% YoY) to P6.9bn in 3Q25, boosted by a 22% sequential improvement in RFO sales (largely under rent-to-own schemes). Cumulatively, 9M25 sales declined 31% YoY to P19.7bn, equivalent to about 65% of pre-Covid sales. Given the challenging environment, management is prioritizing launches of leisure projects in Baguio and Laguna next year. Booked revenues and profits in 3Q25 were lower by 6% YoY and 22% YoY, respectively, which management attributed to slower completions.

 

  • Construction margins dip, despite 20% revenue growth. The construction unit’s profits fell again in 3Q, even as revenues rose 20%. Management cited higher fixed costs from certain delayed projects as the main reason for the margin compression, which it hopes to recoup once these projects are completed. The order book slipped 10% QoQ to P32.8bn, though management expects the award of two subway packages in the next periods to boost its backlog.

 

  • Nickel unit swings to loss in 3Q as weather disrupts shipments. Heavy rains weighed on the nickel unit in 3Q, causing sales volumes to drop 34% YoY despite the opening of a new mine in Zambales. Even though the company secured its mining contract (i.e. MPSA) for Long Point in Palawan in 2Q, it is still awaiting approval of its tree cutting permit. On a positive note, nickel ore prices remain elevated, which should support profitability once operations resume.

 

  • Cement unit posts positive EBITDA in 3Q. With the cease-and-desist order lifted in May, Concreate Cement (CHP PM) staged a recovery in 3Q25, posting its first positive EBITDA of P154mn, as stronger volumes (+10% QoQ/YoY) and lower cash costs offset weaker prices. Management remains focused on further cost optimization and client acquisition in the next quarters, amidst weak demand linked to the corruption scandal.

 

 

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes dip 3% YoY in October but prospects for expansion still positive
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), saw its throughput slip 3% YoY, 8% MoM to 136k TEUs in October
    • Volumes have normalized, following the 23% surge in September as the port congestion eased
    • CMSA shed a bit of market share, as combined volumes at its three competitors in Manzanillo rose 3% YoY
  • Brings YT-October volumes at CMSA to 1.27mn TEUs, up 5% YoY and lagging our full-year forecast of 1.6mn TEUs (+12%)
    • To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year will need to average 179k TEUs per month
    • Despite the volume shortfall, we remain positive on CMSA’s 12-month outlook, supported by the anticipated completion of its ~20% capacity expansion by year-end
    • Note that Mexico’ nationwide port volumes are up 3% YoY in 9M25, despite Trump’s tariffs; ICT’s port is well positioned to capture incremental volumes, given capacity limits of its competitors
    • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of volumes, 20% of revenues, and 24% of EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 9.7x FY26E EV/EBITDA, below peer average of 12x and historical average of 11x

 

 

 

GAMING
E-games GGR falls 28% QoQ; GGR of licensed casinos rises 3% QoQ
  • PAGCOR reported that 3Q25 GGR of the e-games (including bingo) sector fell 28% QoQ (up 14% YoY) to P45.7bn
    • The BSP prohibited from redirecting to online gaming platforms beginning August 17  
    • E-games GGR (including bingo) for the month of September stood at P12.2bn 
  • GGR of market leader DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) fell by a more modest 23% QoQ
    • We estimate that PLUS’ market share increased 2ppt QoQ to 41% 
  • Meanwhile, GGR of licensed casinos rose 3% QoQ to P45.6bn
    • GGR of Okada Manila and COD Manila combined rose 7% QoQ
    • This could mean that the combined GGR of Newport Resorts and Bloomberry Resorts GGR was approximately flat QoQ

 

 

 

MACRO
Tax bureau likely to miss 2025 collection target
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Commissioner Romeo Lumagui guided that the P3.22trn (+13% YoY) collection target this year will likely not be met
    • Lumagui stated that there are ongoing discussions to reduce the BIR's collection target for the year
  • Overall BIR collections as of 9M25 rose 11% to P2.32trn which is 2.5% behind the P2.38trn expected collections for the period
  • He stated that "overall performance is low" dragged by sluggish gov't spending which weighed on overall tax receipts
    • Collections across the categories (e.g., VAT, income, excise taxes) YTD were lagging full-year targets
  • Excise tax collections were at P251bn as of 9M25, 1% lower than the P254bn goal for the period
    • There have been improvements, however, YoY with cigarette excise taxes up 26% to P106bn while e-cigarette taxes surged to P2.1bn from P449mn

 

 

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
BSP eyes to implement 24/7 payment system by 2026
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Deputy Governor Mamerto Tangonan stated that the central bank plans to expand the Philippine Payment and Settlement System (PhilPaSS) Plus operations to run 24/7
  • The new 24/7 real-time gross settlement system will be able to settle more transactions in real-time which may include (remittances, cross-border e-commerce flows, bond trading, etc.)
  • The PhilPaSS Plus system, owned and operated by the BSP, is currently only accessible from 9:00am to 5:45pm during weekdays
