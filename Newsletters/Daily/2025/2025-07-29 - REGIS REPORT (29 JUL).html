REGIS REPORT

29 July 2025

We take a closer look at the 2Q/1H results from two big caps this morning:

 

  • Meralco (MER PM) (+) – Core income, as reported by the company, rose 10% to P25.5bn for 1H25. Contributions from the generation business (+51%) and a reversal of provisions (which management considers part of “core income”) gave earnings a boost. Despite distribution utility (DU) volumes being barely up (+1%), Meralco said core income for that segment was up +7% to P13.7bn in the half. More below.

 

  • BDO Unibank (BDO PM) (-) - We issued a report on BDO (BDO PM) overnight following yesterday’s 1H25 disclosure and analyst briefing. While revenues were strong – up 10% YoY on 14% loan growth and 17% fee growth – NPAT was up just 3% YoY for 1H25, with 2Q25 alone flat YoY. This was due to sticky opex growth of 15% in 1H25, which brought PPOP growth to just 2% YoY. A 36% YoY jump in provision expense in 2Q25 caused further drag. Management guided for a) moderating opex growth, b) “low-40s” credit cost, and c) 15% ROE for 2025E. We have taken a more conservative stance, though, and reduce our forecasts by c4% across our FY25-27E forecast range. Have a look at our note, links below.

+++

 

Yesterday’s State of the Nation Address (SONA) by President Marcos spent less time on big picture issues but instead focused on bread-and-butter matters regular people deal with -- water, food prices, flooding, power availability, and daily transportation. This shift appears to be in large part a reaction to the perceived disappointment with progress under the current administration, manifested in the results of the mid-term elections. Marcos acknowledged as much and is now hoping to reverse these perceptions as he begins the final half of his term.

 

The SONA was also remarkable for what was not taken up: online gambling. It was widely expected (including by us) that online gambling would be brought up given the recent headlines, but the issue was not mentioned at all. We interpret this as Marcos not even considering a ban on gaming at all, which should be a relief for investors in the sector (e.g. Bloomberry (BLOOM PM), Digiplus (PLUS PM). This, however, does not preclude restrictions being imposed.

 

+++

 

Vehicle sales reported by CAMPI were at 40,483 for the month of June, up 3.6%. YT-June, unit sales reached 230,912 (+2%). We estimate Toyota-branded vehicles accounted for 48% share of CAMPI sales. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) is 51% owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM).

 

Note those figures do not include BYD, which is likely to have reached ~5% market so far, from <1% last year.  AC Mobility, a subsidiary of Ayala Corp (AC PM), is the distributor of BYD in the Philippines.

POWER
Meralco core income up 10% to P25.5bn, lifted by power generation
  • Core income up 10% for 1H.  Meralco reported net income of P23.6bn for 1H25, up +5% YoY.  “Core income” as reported by management, came in at P25.5bn, +10% YoY. We believe the market is likely to view the results as being within its expectations. Note, however, that core income for 1H was boosted by provision reversals at the distribution utility (DU) business. The core DU business would have otherwise been disappointing with sales volumes barely up (+1%) for the half. The generation business continued to provide upside surprise.

 

  • DU volumes dip YoY in 2Q. Electricity sales at the core DU business grew only 1% in 1H25, which implies 2Q volumes were just a bit lower YoY (-0.3%). 2Q last year was a high base due to the exceptional heat. Despite volume being practically unchanged YoY, 1H core income as reported rose 7% to P13.7bn. We estimate provision reversals added over P1bn to core income, i.e. core income would have been down otherwise.  For the year, management is now guiding for volume growth of only 1-2% (vs the previous, and rather unrealistic 4-5%)

 

  • Genco profits still soaring. Contributions from generation rose +51% in 1H to P9.4bn, and +70% in 2Q alone.  While the performance across assets, including Pacific Light in Singapore, was generally good, the key variance was the entry of the newly acquired LNG assets. The latter contributed P2.7bn in core income for the six month period.

 

  • Dividend of P11.328/share. The company declared dividends of P11.328 per share, 50% of core income for 1H.  This would translate to a yield of 5% if simply annualized. Meralco typically declares cash dividends twice a year.

 

  • Reviving Atimonan coal. Management says it has received confirmation from the government that its Atimonan coal project is not covered by the moratorium on new coal power plants. It thus intends to revive this project as a 1,200MW coal-fired power plant. Plans for this project will hinge on Meralco securing a contract for it (with itself) in an auction process that’s expected by Oct.

 

  • Tariff review in limbo… again. Meralco’s rate review for 5RP (5th Regulatory Period) should have been completed and ready for implementation by this month (July 2025). Due to some regulatory setbacks and the departure of three (of five) ERC commissioners, it is now looking unlikely that the 5RP review can be completed soon. We will not be surprised if the delay is over one year, if not longer.

 

MACRO
Highlights from Marcos' fourth State of the Nation Address
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. delivered his fourth State of the Nation Address (SONA) yesterday, discussing the administration's accomplishments and progress for ongoing initiatives in key areas but controversial topics such as online gambling, the 19% US tariffs on PH goods, and any mention of the Vice President were not brought up
  • Marcos also talked about additional social welfare programs such as expanding PhilHealth coverage, higher financial assistance limits in public hospitals, more funding for free tertiary education programs, free bus transportation in certain cities, among others
    • These were likely in response to public dissatisfaction that showed in the administration slate's poor performance in the recent midterm elections last May
  • Economy
    • President Marcos acknowledged the lackluster delivery of gov't services and improvements in the quality of life of the everyday Filipino despite better macroeconomic indicators of slowing inflation, falling unemployment, improving business confidence
  • National budget
    • Marcos emphasized that he would veto any 2026 budget proposal that is not aligned with the admin's economic roadmap, stating that he would do so even if it leads to a reenacted budget
  • Infrastructure
    • The "Build Better More" program will continue to be pushed with core projects such as railways, expressways, ports, and flood control projects
    • Marcos ordered the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) to audit all flood control projects started or completed over the past three years and make the list public
  • Power
    • Marcos pledged full electrification by 2028
    • 2.5mn households since 2022 have gained access to power, with ~200 new power plants under development
  • Agriculture
    • The P20/kg rice program was stated to have been a success as he highlighted that is feasible without harming earnings of farmers
    • Rice traders that continue to engage in price manipulation were warned to be charged with economic sabotage
  • Online gambling
    • No clarity was given for the gov't's stance on online gambling as the issue was not discussed
    • Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin a few days ago cited that Marcos has not yet reached a final decision as they are "still looking into it"
CONGLOS
Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) rise 4% YoY in June; Toyota unit sales up 9% YoY
  • CAMPI reported vehicle sales grew 3.6% YoY, 1.8% MoM to 40,483 units in June
    • Note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD’s sales
    • Brings 1H25 sales up 2% YoY to 230,912 units; Assuming BYD’s market share was at least 5%, we estimate industry car sales would have likely grown over 7% YoY
  • Sales of electrified vehicles (ex-BYD) fell 15% MoM to 3,057 units, accounting for 7.6% of overall new car sales
    • Cumulative 1H25 EV sales reached 13,490 units, accounting for 5.84% share of overall sales
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 19,624 units in June, up 8.5% YoY (-0.51% MoM)
    • Brings TMP’s 1H25 sales volumes to 111,276 units, up 6.6% YoY
    • TMP is targeting to sell 235k units this year (+8% YoY). To reach this target, sales should average 20.6k units per month for the rest of the year
    • We are forecasting unit sales of 231k units (+6% YoY)
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV (24% of gross asset value) and 28% of net profit
  • GTCAP is trading at a 45% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,197/sh and 5x FY25E PER

 

 

MACRO
BSP on track for two more rate cuts this year
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona Jr. stated that "we're still on that same easing cycle" (to cut rates two more times this year) and highlighted that the central bank is on track
    • This implies 100bp total rate cuts for 2025, bringing the key policy rate to 4.75% by year-end from the current 5.25%
  • Remolona stated that it would be a good time for another cut if the data shows worsening growth and still benign inflation
  • He also mentioned that the US Federal Reserve's outlook will carry some weight but not as much as before given the peso's relative strength despite not closely matching the Fed's rate path
  • The BSP Governor emphasized that the domestic economy is in good shape given solid fundamentals, ample reserves, stable remittances, and slowing inflation
  • The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled for Aug 28 (third out of six this year)
    • For 2025, Bloomberg median consensus and REGIS expects 75bp total worth of cuts to 5.00%

 

 

UTILITIES
Maynilad secures one cornerstone investor; another one likely by September
  • Maynilad Chairman Manuel V. Pangilinan said that one of the cornerstone investors already confirmed approval, while another will hopefully be secured by September
    • The two cornerstone investors are both foreign entities
  • Maynilad has postponed its IPO to late October to allow cornerstone investors to conduct their due diligence
  • Maynilad is offering 2.289bn (1.9bn primary and 355mn secondary) shares to the public at a maximum offer price of P20/sh
    • At the max. offer price and full subscription of offer shares, Maynilad’s market cap could be P151bn, and public float will be 30%
    • Under its franchise, Maynilad is required to offer at least 30% of its outstanding capital stock to Filipinos by Jan 21, 2027
POWER
Citicore seeks to expand to Cambodia and Myanmar
  • Citicore Renewable Energy (CREC PM) President Oliver Tan said that the company is looking to expand to Cambodia and Myanmar
  • Pertamina’s renewable energy unit acquired a 20% stake in CREC in June for $120 million
    • CREC seeks opportunities to build solar facilities in Indonesia and export to Singapore (over the next two years)
  • CREC has a total installed capacity of 285MW
    • It is targeting to hit 1,300MW by end-2025 and to add about 1,000MW annually over the next five years
