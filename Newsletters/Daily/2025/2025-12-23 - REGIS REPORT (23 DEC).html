REGIS REPORT

23 December 2025

A flurry of transactions to close out the year:

 

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) sold 98mn MREIT shares at P13.50/sh, raising P1.3bn for MEG. The price is equivalent to 7.9% div yield.
  • Sumitomo Mitsui disclosed bought an additional 4.46% in RCBC (RCB PM), taking its take up to 24.5%. The transaction was priced at P59.50/sh (roughly 1x P/bk), more than double the price at the time of the purchase last week (P24-25)
  • Rockwell Land (ROCK PM) bought 75% of the 17-hectare Alabang Town Center mall from the Madrigal family, who just last week bought out their 50-50 partner Ayala Land (ALI PM). ROCK will pay P21.6bn for the stake in three tranches until 2027. That said, ROCK’s market cap is just P10.5bn, and total assets stood at just P88bn as of 3Q25…

 

+ + +

 

Some not-so-cheerful macro news on the last trading day before Christmas.

 

  • The Secretary of Finance, Mr Frederick Go, said that the government was likely to miss its revenue targets this year owing to the slower economic activity, as well as sharply lower seizure of smuggled goods. Expenditures were sluggish, too owing to the ongoing corruption scandal. While slower spend would lower the fiscal deficit, it doesn’t bode well for GDP growth in 4Q25 and into 2026.
  • ICTSI (ICT PM) said that their MICT port in Manila handled a “record” 3mn TEUs in the year until 22 December, up ~5% YoY. While up, the 9M figure was +8% YoY, which would suggest that the 4Q throughput was down some 2% YoY, which in turn would suggest weak domestic consumption demand.

 

+ + +

 

Ending on a positive note, digital payments adoption continues to power through, despite the weak overall economy. Data for November shows that transactions over PesoNet and InstaPay were still up 41% YoY to P2.3trn, a new record high. The rapid digitalization of payments should break open the market for fintech and digital lending for NBFCs, banks and digibanks.

MACRO
DOF: BIR and BOC likely to miss 2025 revenue targets
  • The Dept. of Finance Secretary Frederick Go stated that gov't revenue collections from the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and Bureau of Customs (BOC) will likely be below official targets this year given "extraordinary" circumstances amid the flood control corruption scandal
  • The BIR initially targeted P3.23trn (+13% YoY) in tax collections, the tax bureau commissioner's revised conservative estimate amounts to P3.1trn (+9% YoY)
    • BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza stated that the slowdown in consumer and gov't spending led to lower-than-expected collections
  • Customs initial collection goal was P1.06trn (+16% YoY) with the revised goal being P959bn (+3% YoY)
    • Lower rice tariffs, rice importation ban, harsher-than-usual rainy season, and rampant illicit trade were attributed to the lackluster collections

      • The BOC last estimated around P150bn (vs. just P30-40bn last year) in foregone revenues for 2025 due to illicit trade

  • Go highlighted that "proper prosecution, proper restitution, and genuine reform in the public works" are key for a potential economic rebound by 1Q26

 

 

 

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Manila port, MICT, throughput reaches 3mn TEUs in 2025; Implies 4Q25 volume contraction?
  • Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) handled a record 3mn TEUs as of December 22
    • This is slightly (2%) higher than the port’s 2024 throughput of 2.9mn TEUs but trails our full-year forecast of 3.1mn TEUs (+6%)
    • Suggests that 4Q25 volumes may reach 778k TEUs (assuming full quarter adjustment), down 2% YoY
    • Recall that 9M25 volumes at MICT rose 8% YoY to 2.3mn TEUs
  • We estimate MICT accounts for ~20% of EBITDA and 23% of NAV
    • Note that consensus and REGIS’ FY25E EPS growth rates of +22% YoY and +21% YoY, respectively, imply a degree of softness in 4Q, given 9M25 actual EPS growth of +24%
  • ICTSI trades at 10x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 17x PER, below peer average (12.8x/18x) and historical average (11x/21x)
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital payments breach P2.3trn in Nov, +41% YoY

The National Retail Payments System (NRPS) recorded a new record-high P2,305bn worth of digital transactions in November 2025, up 41.0% YoY, This puts the 11M25 figure at P22,054bn, up 41.2% YoY, faster than the 35% clip in FY2024. As always, recall that the NRPS data only counts payments made between platforms (ie Bank A to Bank B, or Wallet A to Wallet B, etc), and does not include payments made within an institution (ie BDO-to-BDO or GCash-to-GCash, etc).

 

Larger, batched transactions over PesoNet grew 26.8% YoY to P1,165bn, faster than the 23.7% pace set in October. The PHP figure though, was a shade lower MoM compared to October. Year-to-date, PesoNet payments totaled P11,845bn, up 30% YoY just a tad faster than the 29% YoY recorded in FY24.

 

For InstaPay, which is for real-time transfers up to P50,000 (about $852), November payments amounted to P1,140bn, up 59.2% YoY, slower than the 66.1% posted in October. That said, the 11M25 growth rate of 56% YoY is still much faster than the 46% pace set in FY24. Perhaps more importantly, the volume of InstaPay transactions continues to surge, up 320% YoY for the month. This indicates further broadening out of digital adoption for ever-widening use case for the service, for smaller and smaller value transactions.
