04 March 2025

The U.S. government is tearing up previously signed agreements as it unilaterally imposes additional duties on imports from China, Mexico and Canada in a few hours.  An additional 10% tariff will be slapped on Chinese goods while a 25% tariff will be levied on goods from Mexico and Canada.

While such an action from the American government will have no direct impact on the Philippines, investor eyes will likely be on at least one Philippine company with operations in North America: ICTSI (ICT PM).

 

Around 1/4th of ICT’s EBITDA is derived from its terminal in Mexico.  While the terminal in Manzanillo does not directly send much cargo (if at all) to the U.S., majority of its inbound cargo (~60%) is from China and its fate is ultimately tied to Mexico’s own economic prospects.

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 2024 NPAT of P11.24bn, up 23% YoY, in line with consensus, but a shade behind Regis (P11.7bn) on high tax expense. Loan growth sped up to 26% YoY, with consumers +37% and corporates surprisingly strong +21%. NIMs took a bit of a hit in 4Q24 due to a large P20bn bond issue in late 3Q24. Expect margins to recover in the coming quarters as proceeds are deployed to higher-yielding assets. Provision expense finally moderated in 4Q24, with credit costs falling to 91bps, as NPL ratio dropped sharply to 2.85%, from 3.08% in 3Q. With the stock’s recent under-performance, valuations are again compelling, at 3.5x PER and 0.34x P/bk on our FY2025E forecasts. The bank will hold an earnings call tomorrow, Wednesday, 5 March, at 5pm.

BANKS
SECB FY24 NPAT +23% to P11.2bn, loans +26%
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported FY24 net profit of P11.24bn, up 23% YoY, in line with consensus, but slightly behind our forecast of P11.7bn. The main source of drag was income taxes, as strong NII was offset by higher opex. Loan growth accelerated to 26% YoY (from 24% in 3Q24), more than double the industry average pace of 12%. Growth was driven by consumer/MSME, up 37% YoY (steady vs 38% in 3Q24), while corporate loans improved to +21% (from 19%).
  • The bank will hold an earnings call on Wed, 05 March at 5pm. Ahead of that, some details from the disclosures (lots of charts below):
  • Loan growth sped up to 26% YoY, up from 24% in 3Q, and much faster than the sector’s 12% clip. Consumer/MSME loan growth was steady at 37%, driven by credit cards (+64%), auto (+54%), MSMEs (+54%), and mortgages (+19%). Corporate loans sped up to 21% YoY. Deposits grew 32% YoY, driven by TDs (+57%), though CASA grew at a respectable 16% YoY.
  • NII grew 26% in FY24, though this was a slowdown from the 31% in 9M24. For 4Q alone, NII was up just 13%. This was due in part to a P20bn, 5-yr bond issue in late 3Q24 (upsized from P5bn initially), with some timing issue in deployment to bonds/loans. This dragged on NIMs, which dipped 14bps QoQ to 4.41%. Loan yields were firm (flat QoQ), while deposit costs eased 6bps QoQ despite the high TD growth, reflecting easing pressure in deposit pricing. The bonds pushed up overall funding costs. We expect asset yields to catch up in 1H24.
  • Fee income grew 47% YoY in FY24, though this included a lumpsum payment in 1Q24. The 4Q24 fee growth was still solid at +27% YoY. Trading/fx losses moderated in FY24, with 2H24 already posting profits, as the bank’s accounting for interest rate swaps now properly reflect the costs as part of interest expense.
  • PPOP was up 31% YoY, bang in line with our forecast.
  • Provision expense jumped 43% YoY to P6.7bn, though for 4Q24 alone, the P1.55bn was a sharp moderation, down 24% YoY and 17% QoQ. Credit cost finally dipped below 100% (at c91bps) in 4Q24. Gross NPL ratio improved sharply to 2.85% (from 3.08% in 3Q24), while NPL cover was up slightly to 81% from 79%.
  • Overall the result was pretty solid, with tax expense accounting for the “miss” against our forecasts. More after the briefing on Wednesday.
BANKS
PDIC deposit insurance coverage doubled to P1mn
  • The Philippine Deposit Insurance Corp (PDIC) last Friday (Feb 28) announced that the maximum insurance coverage on banks’ PHP deposits would be doubled from the current P500,000 to P1mn (roughly USD17,300) effective 15 March.
    • The last time the maximum coverage was adjusted was in 2009 (from P250,000) in the wake of the Global Financial Crisis.
  • According to the PDIC’s press release, the increased coverage will NOT result in higher fees charged to the banks.
  • The number of accounts fully insured will only rise by 100bps, from 97.6% previously, to 98.6%.
    • However, that 100bps will increase the total value insured by 34% to P4.8trn, equivalent to 24.5% of total banking sector deposits (up from 18.3% previously).
  • According to our estimates, banks currently pay premiums to PDIC equivalent to approximately 20-25bps of deposits.
    • This – along with other friction costs such as documentary stamp taxes (stamp duties) on time deposits (TDs), and reserve requirements (RRR) – is why banks at times choose to tap the bond markets instead of paying up for expensive TDs.
MACRO
Finance Secretary sees budget deficit falling within 2025 target
  • Finance Secretary Ralph G. Recto has stated that the national gov't should be able to narrow the budget deficit below the P1.54tn ceiling and the 5.3% of GDP target for this year
    • This comes after the budget deficit for 2024 was relatively unchanged at P1.51tn (but narrower at 5.7% of GDP vs. 6.1% in 2023) and narrowly missing the P1.48tn ceiling (5.6% of GDP) target
    • In terms of the gov’t’s revenue target, it is said to be P4.62tn (+4.5% YoY) or 75% of the P6.18tn (+4.3% YoY) 2025 expenditure target
  • Recto emphasized that hitting the deficit to GDP target is part of the gov't's macro fiscal framework to
    • Ensure macro stability, progress towards a credit rating upgrade, exhibit a seal of good housekeeping and macro stability for a flourishing business environment
    • He also commented that the DBCC is on track with its targets and hence, does not see the need to revise in its upcoming March meeting
  • Budget Undersecretary and Principal Economist Joselito R. Basilio cited lower interest rates and improved price conditions which will be the primary factors in meeting the target
    • He also cited fiscal discipline and better revenue performance through
      • better tax collection in the BIR and Customs (digitalizing the tax system, stricter compliance, illicit trade crackdown)
      • new tax measures such as the proposed excise tax on single-use plastics (pending in the Senate), rationalization of the mining fiscal regime (due for bicam after election break)
