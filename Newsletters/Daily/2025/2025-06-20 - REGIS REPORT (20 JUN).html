REGIS REPORT

20 June 2025

As was widely expected, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) cut policy rates by 25bp at yesterday’s meeting. When the cut takes effect, the policy rate would be 5.25%, down 125bp from the recent peak. Notably, BSP Governor Eli Remolona also signaled that another reduction could be coming soon.

 

For perspective, the policy rate was as low as 2.0% before the BSP began to tighten in the aftermath of the Covid era, spurred by the inflation surge from the Ukraine war. Note, however, it was 4.0% immediately before the pandemic (still meaningfully lower than today).

 

The domestic macro environment does suggest there’s room to cut rates further. Inflation is lower than it’s been in a long time, and money supply growth remains low (~6%). The difference between BSP policy rates and CPI inflation (“real interest rates”) is also near peak at ~4%.

 

However, the BSP has to contend with the fact the policy rate differential with the US Fed is also at or near its narrowest historically. Check out the charts.

RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM)  will acquire 324,000sqm of malls from Robinsons Land (RLC PM) in exchange for 3.83bn new RCR shares. Our preliminary estimates suggest that similar to previous transactions, this share-for-property swap will be mildly accretive for both parties.

 

RCR has grown substantially over the past few years thanks to the infusion of assets from its sponsor. As a result, the value of RLC’s 57% stake in RCR is now higher than its own market cap (of P61bn). This gap could increase further as the share-for-property swap will increase RCR’s shares outstanding (and market cap) by another 24%.

MACRO
BSP trims policy rate by 25 bps, signals one more cut and easing inflation ahead
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board cut key policy rates by 25bp to 5.25%
    • BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that they will probably cut one more time this year if things "stay on track" and depending the data
    • The BSP policy rate has been reduced by a total of 125bp from 6.50% since the easing cycle started back in Aug 2024
  • Adjustments were also made to the BSP's inflation forecasts, with 2025 inflation projected to average 1.6% from 2.4% previously
    • Upward revisions (+10bp) were made for 2026 and 2027 inflation projections to 3.4% and 3.3%, respectively
    • The revisions were made considering heightened risks to inflation from geopolitical tensions and US trade policy uncertainty
  • Remolona elaborated that the BSP may need to intervene more seriously if the peso depreciation continues further given the possible impact on inflation
    • The USD-PHP exchange rate has slumped to P57.4 since P55.7 at end-May
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (4th out of 6 this year) will be on Aug 28
    • For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps total worth of cuts to 5.00%
    • We were forecasting 50 bps worth of cuts to 5.25% this year but the likelihood of more is higher now given latest inflation data

MACRO
BOP posts US$298mn deficit in May; May GIR settles at US$105.2bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$298mn
    • A reversal from the US$2.0bn surplus in May 2024 but narrower than Apr 2025's US$2.6bn deficit
  • The deficit reflected the national gov't's drawdowns on the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits for external debt obligations
  • This brings the YT-May deficit at US$5.8bn, reversing from the US$1.6bn surplus for the same period last year
    • The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade gap, higher net outflows in the financial account, and front-loaded external debt repayments
  • The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$4bn deficit (-0.8% of GDP)
    • The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-May stood at US$105.2bn, down slightly from the US$105.3bn in end-Apr
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.1 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025
