15 December 2025

Property consultancy Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) has released its condo take-up estimates for the year (yes, before the year’s even over).  The numbers released by LPC show a 4% decline in condo take-up (i.e. sales) for the year.  The full year figure (24,732 units) cited by LPC, however, implies 4Q take-up of only `3,900 units.  If correct, this means 4Q condo sales were down by half compared with the previous quarter. This would be a particularly weak sales figure and would bode badly for the property developers’ 4Q sales.

Last week the SEC formally set an effective interest rates (EIRs) cap of 12% a month (or c144% a year) for loans of non-bank finance cos (NBFCs), such as HomeCredit, Mynt/GCash, Tala, AsiaLink, etc. The EIR includes a cap of 6% monthly headline interest rate, and includes all fees/charges (excluding late/non-payment fees). The caps are only imposed on loans of up to P10,000 (c$180) with tenors of up to 4 months. We do not expect the BSP to follow and impose similar caps on banks.

At first glance, the rate cap still seems usurious. However, we noted in a previous report that credit costs are also extremely high – default rates averaging 20-30% or more. It would also seem easy enough to skirt the caps by offering larger loan amounts, or longer tenors. However, it is important to note that the fast-growing products include small-ticket loans with extremely short-term loans (14 days or less). If, say, an NBFC charges a flat 5% processing fee per loan, a 14-day loan effectively translates to 10%/month which would leave little space for the actual interest rate. The new SEC ruling will affect the viability of those loans, and/or could push borrowers back out into the illegal/informal lending market with even higher rates, and dodgy collection practices.

This to us merely highlights the urgent need to quickly scale up the national credit bureau, and the National ID system.

Energy Secretary Sharon Garin says she is waiting for the decision from the Dept of Justice (DOJ) regarding the legality of extending the coal operating contract of Semirara (SCC PM). Garin is hoping to see a decision if not within the year, then by 1Q26.

SCC’s contract with the government expires in July 2027.  Allowing it to expire abruptly could have serious consequences not only for SCC also for the country as a whole as SCC supplies >20% of the country’s coal requirements.

POWER
Energy Secretary says decision on Semirara’s coal contract extension by 1Q26?
  • The Department of Energy is awaiting the Department of Justice’s opinion before deciding whether to amend Semirara’s contract or open it for bidding
    • According to Energy Secretary Sharon Garin
    • “I’m hoping (for the decision) within this year, but probably that will be in the first quarter of next year,”
  • Recall that Semirara’s coal operating contract is set to expire on July 2027
    • The outcome is binary; We estimate a 15-year extension puts SCC’s valuation at P37/sh while a no extension scenario puts its valuation at P21/sh (vs. current share price of P28.10/sh)
    • In the event of a non-extension of its coal operating contract, Semirara will retain ownership of all its mining equipment, port facilities, and other infrastructure in the island
MACRO
Current account deficit drops by 39% YoY in 3Q25; YTD CA deficit lower by 6% YoY to US$12.5bn
  • The current account deficit plunged 39% YoY, 37% QoQ to US$3.2bn (equivalent to 2.8% of GDP)
    • Driven by a narrower trade-in-goods deficit, higher net receipts from trade in services and sustained modest increase in remittances
    • Trade deficit declined by 5% YoY to US$17.5bn due to strength in electronic exports (+32%) and mineral exports (+73%)
    • Total estimated BPO export revenues, including computer and other business services, reached US$9.1bn in 3Q25, a 7.8% increase from US$8.4bn in 3Q24
  • This brings 9M25 current account to a narrower (-6% YoY) deficit of US$12.5bn, equivalent to 3.6% of GDP
    • This appears broadly in line with the BSP’s year-end CA deficit target of US$16bn (3.3% of GDP)
    • Improvement is due to (a) higher electronic exports (+7%) and mineral exports (+52%)
