REGIS REPORT

28 November 2025

We published a recap of the 3rd quarter performance of the property sector, which was remarkably resilient in the face of weak GDP growth and a corruption scandal. Despite concerns that the flood control corruption scandal would soften residential demand, reservation sales still grew on a QoQ and YoY basis. Meanwhile, contrary to the slowdown in GDP growth, mall revenue grew at the same pace. On the other hand, net office take-up fell due to weaker demand from both BPO firms and traditional tenants.

It was reported that First Gen (FGEN PM) is open to delisting. The prospect of delisting for FGEN is not new given the low share liquidity and deep discount at which the shares are trading. The group also has a chain of listed vehicles one on top of the other: Lopez Holdings (LPZ PM), First Philippine Holdings (FPH PM) down to First Gen.

 

We estimate a NAV of around P40/share for FGEN, which would be more than 2.5x current share price. Note any plan to delist would have to consider the fact that KKR owns 20% of FGEN.

 

+++

 

S&P affirmed its BBB+ credit rating for the Philippines with a “positive” outlook. The outlook seems a little optimistic considering the government’s estimated debt and deficit ratio are weakening.

 

+++

 

Sugar output in the Philippines is forecast to stay flat for the 2025-26 crop year despite the previously reported threat of crop pests, suggesting relative stability in prices. Universal Robina (URC PM) is the largest sugar miller in the country and derives over 10% of EBIT from the business.

Foreign container volume growth across Philippine ports moderated to 7% in 3Q25 from 12% in 1H25 amid slower exports. Volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port in Manila (MICT) also saw growth decelerate to 4% in 3Q25 from the 10% pace in 1H25 and ceded share to Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbor.

CONSUMER
Philippine sugar output projected to remain flat next year
  • The US Dept. of Agriculture (USDA) estimated that local raw sugar production for the 2025-2026 crop year would remain unchanged from the 2.09mn metric tons (MT) output in current crop year 2024-2025
    • The flat projection stemmed from the negative impact of the excess rainfall and ongoing red-striped soft scale insect (RSSI) infestation affecting sugar plantations
  • According to the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA), the estimated area affected by the RSSI infestation is less than 2% of the total area planted to sugarcane
    • Total sugarcane area in the country is expected to grow 2% to 400,000 hectares from 392,000 hectares this year
  • Sugar consumption was reported to likely remain steady at 2.2mn MT
    • The USDA is expecting zero sugar exports and no raw sugar importation as the gov't is set to prioritize and protect the local industry
  • Universal Robina (URC PM) typically generates 10-20% of EBIT from its sugar milling business
    • As of 9M25, the SURE business segment comprises 20% of total EBIT before corporate overhead

 

 

TRANSPORT
Philippine ports’ foreign container volume growth moderates to 7% in 3Q25 on slower exports
  • Data from the Philippine Ports Authority showed that nationwide foreign container volumes grew by 7% in 3Q25, moderating from the 12% pace in 1H25
    • Growth decelerated largely due to moderating export volume growth of +8% in 3Q25 from +14% in 1H25
    • Import volumes rose 7% in 3Q25, mildly accelerating from the 6% rate in 2Q25
    • Brings 9M25 nationwide foreign container volumes up 11% YoY
  • ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) also saw volume growth decelerate to 4% in 3Q25 from 6% in 2Q25 and 10% in 1H25
    • MICT’s market share loss persisted, after South Harbour of Asian Terminals Inc (ATI PM) saw volumes increase by +9% in 3Q25 (vs. +21% in 1H25)    
    • Note that ATI expanded South Harbour’s capacity by over 40% to 2mn TEUs in May (here)
    • We believe ATI’s market share gains will be short-lived, as MICT’s Berth 8 Phase 2 expansion should be completed in 2026
    • MICT’s cumulative 9M25 volumes are up 8% YoY to 2.3mn TEUs, broadly in line with our FY25E of 3.1mn TEUs (+6%)
  • We are forecasting MICT’s volumes will grow by 7% in 2026
    • Assuming flat volumes for MICT, we estimate ICT’s FY26E EBITDA and EPS could decline by 1% and 2%, respectively, holding others constant
    • We estimate MICT accounts for 22% of FY26E volumes and 23% of revenues
  • ICTSI trades at 10x FY26E EV/EBITDA, below peer average of 12x and historical average of 11x

ICTSI Batam begins integration of operations with state-run port
  • ICTSI’s Batu Ampar Container Terminal (BACT) in Indonesia will officially merge operations with state-run Batam Terminal Petikemas starting Dec 1
    • We estimate the integration will allow BACT to increase market share in Batam to 70% from 45% when it took over operations last Sep
    • Our estimates and valuation already assume this development
  • We estimate BACT accounts for 4% of FY26E volumes and 1% of revenues
GAMING
DigiPlus partners with Pay&Go
  • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) announced a “strategic partnership” with Pay&Go, one of the leading providers of self-service payment kiosks in the country
  • Users of DigiPlus platforms will be able to fund their wallets in the 3,500 self-service kiosks
  • A cashout feature is being eyed for 2026
  • Pay&Go is a self-service payment kiosk network operated by BTI Payment Philippines, a subsidiary of Australia-based The Banktech Group
  • PLUS is trading at 7.4X FY26E P/E (based on consensus forecast)
