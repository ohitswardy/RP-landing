REGIS REPORT

31 January 2025

The country’s GDP growth for 4Q came in at 5.2%, bringing FY24 growth to 5.6%.  Both numbers are below Street and Regis expectations as well government targets.

On the demand side, we note the softness in private consumption (+4.7% in 4Q) as well as capital formation (+4.1%).  We believe the unusually disruptive weather was a factor in the last quarter.  On the supply side, Industry grew 4.4% while Agri posted another contraction (-1.8%).

The relatively disappointing growth numbers should give authorities more reason (excuse) to ease monetary policy further.  As it is, we believe current monetary conditions are restrictive/tight and there is justification for easing domestically (whatever the Fed does).  We expect policy rates to be reduced by 50bp and reserve requirement ratios (RRR) to be cut by 2ppt this year.

+++

A Lower House committee approved a bill to implement a P200/day increase in minimum wages.  This has not been the first or the second such proposed legislated wage increase from either House.  We don’t believe the bill has a chance of passage into law within this Congress.

A separate House committee, meanwhile, proposed the revision of excise taxes on cigarettes, aiming to temper future increases. The proposed changes, however, look too modest and are unlikely to have any impact at all if implemented.  Excise tax collections on cigarettes have plunged despite substantial increases in tax rates. We reckon that (1) misguided proposals to raise rates further and (2) ineffective enforcement have caused the drop.  Industry estimates put the share of illicit trade to be back up to over 30%.

+++

Ayala Land (ALI PM) CFO Augusto Bengzon said that the company aims to achieve earnings growth at twice the pace of GDP growth over the next five years. This appears broadly in line with our own forecasts, as well as consensus estimates. However, it appears slightly slower than the 15% CAGR (until 2028) that the company previously announced.

+++

Revenue of the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, grew 41% to P112bn in 2024. Revenue from e-games grew c. 125% while revenue from licensed casinos remained flat. Based on our estimates, there were likely QoQ revenue improvements in 4Q24 for both categories – c. 10% for licensed casinos and c. 25% for e-games. This could mean better operations for both Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) and DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM).

+++

The changes in the PSEi (PCOMP) composition and weights will take place at the close of business today (start of the day, Monday Feb 3).  Apart from the addition of China Bank (CBC PM) and AREIT (AREIT PM) and the removal of Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) and Wilcon (WLCON PM), there will also be reductions in the weights of most other PSEi constituents.

MACRO
4Q24 GDP grows 5.2% but misses 2024 target given weak consumption
  • GDP grew at 5.2% in 4Q24, unchanged vs. the 5.2% growth rate in 3Q
    • This is below consensus expectations of 5.5%
  • On the demand side, the lackluster growth in 4Q stems from the slowdown in private consumption (4.7% from 5.2% in 3Q) and a slowdown in private investments (4.1% from 13.7%)
    • Private consumption may have been dampened by the six typhoons that hit the country from Oct to Nov as well as the lingering effects of elevated inflation
    • The slowdown in private investments was mainly due to durable equipment growth coming off from a high base due to significant aircraft orders in 2023
    • Net imports narrowed to -0.3% from -3.0%, but still weighed down on overall GDP growth
  • On the supply side, the agri sector exhibited a decline for the third straight quarter, contracting by -1.8% (albeit at a slower pace than the -2.7% of the previous quarter)
    • Growth in the service sector also improved and was faster at 6.7% from 6.3% but the industry sector slowed to 4.4% from 5.1%
    • Construction activity slowed to 7.8% (vs. 3Q24: 9.0%) as construction of private sector projects moderated sharply to 5.7% from 14.2%.
    • Government construction mildly accelerated to 4.9% in 4Q from 3.7% in 3Q. Smaller-ticket household construction grew at a faster rate of 12.8% in 4Q24 from 9.0% in 3Q.
  • Bringing FY24 GDP growth to 5.6%, below the government's target of 6-6.5% and the Bloomberg consensus median forecast of 5.8%
    • The full year figure was also below Regis' FY24E forecast of 5.9%
  • Recall that the gov't revised its GDP growth expectations for this year last Dec 2024
    • The 2025 GDP growth target is set at 6.0-8.0% a wider band than the previous 6.5-7.5% range given "evolving domestic and global uncertainties"

 

MACRO
House Committee approves P200 minimum wage hike
  • The House Committee on Labor and Employment approved a substitute bill, proposing a P200 across-the-board daily wage increase
    • The approval follows Speaker Martin Romualdez's announced support for the passage of the wage hike
    • Recall that the Senate approved a bill increasing the daily minimum wage in the private sector by P100
  • Note that the 19th Congress will adjourn on Feb 8 for the mid-term elections and will resume on June 2 to June 13
    • A new set of lawmakers (20th Congress) will be appointed by July 28
  • Minimum wages in NCR today are between P608-645 while the region with the lowest min. wages (P316-361) is BARMM
    • The proposed P200 daily wage hike would thus represent an increase of 31% and 63% over current minimum wages
MACRO
Lower House approves bill to lower tax rates on cigarettes, sought to improve collection
  • The House of Representatives approved the bill which will lower the excise tax increases for cigarettes, tobacco, vapor (e-cig/e-vape) products as to mitigate the decline in tax collection from the goods
    • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) was P52bn short of its P185bn tobacco excise tax target last year
  • The new bill seeks to lower the increase to 2% every even-numbered year effective Jan 2026 and maintain 4% every odd-numbered year effective Jan 2027 till Dec 2035
    • Current law has the annual increase on excise tax for tobacco products at 4%
    • A projection from the House ways and means committee says the measure can net the gov't P66bn from 2026 to 2030
  • The bill also gives the President the option to increase the tax rate up to 5% if the national gov't deficit of the previous year exceeds the program deficit by an equivalent of 2% of GDP of the previous year

Ayala Land aims to grow earnings at double the pace of GDP growth
  • Ayala Land CFO Augusto Bengzon said that the company aims to grow net profit at double the pace of GDP growth over the next five years
    • He did not specify if he was referring to real GDP growth or nominal GDP growth
  • The company previously said that it plans to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023)
    • This implies 15% CAGR
  • We forecast earnings to grow 13%/14%/11% in FY24E/FY25E/FY26E
    • Consensus forecasts: +16% in FY24E, 15% in FY25E, and 12% in FY26E
  • ALI is trading at 54% discount to NAV, 1.2X FY25E P/B, and 11.6X FY25E P/E

PAGCOR revenue grows 41% in 2024
  • Revenue of the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, grew 41% to P112bn in 2024
    • Revenue from e-games (including bingo) climbed 125% to P48.8bn
    • Revenue from licensed casinos was flat at P33.1bn
  • This could mean that 4Q24 GGR of licensed casinos will improve c. 10% QoQ (15% YoY) to P57bn
    • Okada Manila’s 4Q24 GGR grew 9% QoQ (-4% YoY) to P9bn
  • GGR of e-games (including Bingo) might have doubled YoY (c. +25% QoQ) to P50bn in 4Q24
    • PLUS is trading at 7.4X FY25E P/E (based on consensus forecast)
GAMING
Pacific Online to acquire 37.5% stake in e-casino operator
  • Pacific Online Systems Corporation (LOTO PM) entered into an agreement with HHR Philippines and the latter’s principal shareholders
  • LOTO will acquire 37.5% of the expanded outstanding capital (after LOTO’s investment) for P150mn
  • The proceeds will be used by HHR to fund its expansion program
  • HHR Philippines has an online gaming (e-casino) license from PAGCOR and operates under the brand “Buenas”
