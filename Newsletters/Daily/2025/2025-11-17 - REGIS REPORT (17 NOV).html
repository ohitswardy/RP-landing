CONSUMER
JFC 3Q25 Initial Comments—9M profit up 2% to P8.65bn; EBIT and top line increase 15% and 14%

JFC 9M profit was up a modest 2% to P8.65bn despite EBIT and top line rising 15% and 14% to P16.0bn and P224.2bn, respectively. In 3Q alone, profit increased 8% to P3.0bn as revenue rose 13% to P76.4bn on the back of 17% systemwide sales (SWS) growth.

 

Domestic growth tempered by bad weather. For the domestic business, 3Q SSSG was +1.2%, decelerating from +6.4% in 2Q given adverse weather conditions.

 

Jollibee int'l business growth continues. Int'l same-store sales saw growth pick up to 6.2% from 4.1% in 2Q as NA Asia Brands' (Jollibee, Red Ribbon, Chowking) SSSG accelerated further to +9.8% from +7.8% in 2Q.

 

China and Smash improves. China’s SSS continued its recovery in 3Q, accelerating to +8.0% from +3.9% in 2Q after breaking a five-quarter streak of negative SSSG. Smashburger, meanwhile, still saw negative SSSG at -4.7%, albeit at a slower decline vs -9.9% in 2Q.

BANKS
SECB 9M25 NPAT P9.1bn -- strong PPOP +24%; have credit costs peaked?

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 9M25 NPAT of P9.1bn, up 7% YoY, slightly ahead of our FY25E of P11.9bn (consensus P12.1bn), with 3Q25 alone at P3.2bn. Notably, the 3Q25 performance came despite a massive P3.55bn provision charge (or >200bps credit cost). SECB management have been guiding that 3Q25 would be peak credit cost as part of the ongoing credit card cleanup. If they’re right (if… if…), then the potential profit uplift could be significant. PPOP surged 24% in 9M25, with 3Q25 PPOP ROE at c20%.

 

SECB will hold its briefing on Monday, 17 November, at 4pm.

 

Ahead of that, some details from the disclosures:

    • Loan growth slowed sharply to 8% YoY, from 16% in 2Q25. This is in line with management guidance to end the year in high-single-digits to low-double-digits to conserve capital. That said, consumer loans still surged 24% YoY, driven by autos (+42%)
    • NIMs climbed 12bps QoQ to 4.76%, driven by rising loan yields (+13bps QoQ) given continuing improvement in loan mix. Overall asset yields were up 16bps QoQ while funding costs were flat (+3bps QoQ) helped by falling policy interest rates and RRR cuts.
    • Non-interest income jumped 19% YoY in 3Q25 alone. Fee incomes were up 15% YoY, while the bank booked healthy trading/fx gains of over P1bn. Notably, Equity Income surged to P341bn in 3Q25 alone, which included the first full quarter contribution from Home Credit (HCPH)
    • Opex stayed elevated, up 20.5% in 9M25, though the 3Q25 figure moderated to just +11.8% YoY.
    • PPOP was up an impressive 24% YoY in 9M25, with 3Q25 alone +32% YoY.
    • Provision expense surged 51% YoY in 3Q to P3.55bn, which we estimate translated to a credit cost of 211bps. Management had earlier flagged that it expected credit costs to peak in 3Q25 as part of its ongoing clean-up of its credit card portfolio. Worth noting that NPL ratio improved to 3.02% (from 3.16% in 3Q) while NPL cover increased to 86% from 79% in 2Q.

Our forecasts for FY2026E of P15.3bn is the highest on the Street (avg P14.1bn), assuming that credit costs “normalize” to around 105-110bps. On our FY26E numbers, the stock is trading on 3.3x PER and 0.30x P/bk.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Finance Secretary rules out off-cycle rate reduction, expects 25bp cut in Dec
  • Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto dismissed an off-cycle move on monetary policy to reduce rates despite weaker-than-expected 3Q GDP growth of +4.0%, but he notes of a high chance of a rate cut at the BSP's next meeting in Dec
  • BSP Deputy Governor Zeno Abenoja stated that they did not discuss any possible off-cycle monetary policy easing and elaborated that it was "probably just rumors"
  • Recall that BSP Governor Eli Remolona previously guided the possibility of another 25bp cut in Dec and further cuts in 2026 to stimulate the economy amid a slowdown from the flood control corruption scandal
  • The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11
    • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.5%
    • For 2026, consensus expects a 25bp cut to 4.25%, while Regis expects 50bp cut total to 4.00%
  • 2025 GDP growth expectations for consensus and Regis are at 5.2% and 4.8%, respectively
    • 2026 GDP growth expectations are both at 5.5%
TELCOS
PLDT revives REIT listing plan for ePLDT
  • PLDT Chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan has put a REIT listing back on the table to reduce debt and spur liquidity for PLDT
  • The REIT listing is an alternative given plans to finalize the sale of a minority stake in ePLDT to Japan's Nippon Telegraph and Telephone Corp. (NTT) has failed to progress
  • The company, however, remains in talks with a multinational group for a possible acquisition of stake in PLDT's data center arm—ePLDT Inc.
    • ePLDT is currently negotiating with several companies to sell 49% of its data center business, valued at US$1bn
