REGIS REPORT

03 December 2025

We issued a report on the SEC’s proposal to set lower and more stringent interest rate caps on loans issues by non-bank fincos (NBFCs), which will include fintech companies like GCash/Fuse, Home Credit, etc. The plan is to lower then “effective interest rate” or EIR from 15% a month currently, down to 10% a month, and vastly broaden the universe of loans covered by the caps. While we admit that even the lowered figure seems sky-high, we note two things. First, the financial inclusion is still low, with unsecured lending within the banking system at under 2% of GDP. Second, the rates need to be high, because default rates are also very high, with credit cost is a whopping 20-30% of loans, or even more. Over time, the improvements in data from the Credit Information Corp (CIC) and from the National ID system should bring these down, but that’s for another conversation…

 

The unintended consequence of the presumably well-meaning move is that it could push borrowers back into the informal/illegal lending market where rates are even higher, and collection practices are unregulated. We will be watching the space closely and expect a compromise rate cap to be reached.

The Secretary of the Dept of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) says he is aiming for a 40% decrease in “internet” prices as early as next year, following the implementation of the “Konektadong Pinoy Act” (KPA). From the sounds of it, he was referring specifically to broadband prices, which would conform with our view that the KPA would affect the fixed broadband sector more than mobile.

 

A typical prepaid fixed broadband package in PH would cost as low as P700 (US$12) per month for speeds of up to 50mbps. This is still expensive relative to the rest of the region.

 

We believe KPA could hasten the decline in retail broadband prices by making it easier for competition to enter the market. Converge (CNVRG PM) derives ~85% of revenue from home broadband, while PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) derived ~25% and ~15% respectively.

Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) completed the rather curious acquisition of motorcycle retailer Premium Bikes the other day. The transaction adds 2% to its revenue and is likely to be accretive from day one. It does, however, raise further questions about the strategic direction of RRHI.

TELCOS
DICT aims to cut “internet” prices by 40%
  • In a press briefing, Dept of Information Communication Technology (DICT) Secretary Henry Aguda says he is aiming for a 40% cut in “internet costs” as early as next year
    • Press briefing was held following the release of the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA)
    • Aguda’s statements imply he’s referring to reductions in broadband prices
  • Aguda claims the Philippines is #6 in ASEAN in terms of affordability of internet
    • A reduction of at least 40% would be needed for PH to reach #1 or #2
  • We are of the view that the KPA will affect the broadband sector (and thus broadband prices) more than mobile
    • Broadband accounts for 84% of Converge’s (CNVRG PM) revenue; 26% of PLDT’s (TEL PM) and 15% of Globe (GLO PM)

RRHI completes P146mn acquisition of Premiumbikes
  • Following recent approval from the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) last month, RRHI has completed its acquisition of 100% of Premiumbikes Corp.
    • The deal, executed through wholly owned subsidiary Robinson's Supermarket Corp., closed on Dec 1
    • The P146mn price tag was 1x 2024 P/B
  • Premiumbikes was previously owned by Lance Gokongwei
  • In 2024, it had a store network of 214 stores on P4.2bn (+15% YoY) of sales
    • RRHI's total sales in 2024 was P200.5bn (+4% YoY)

MACRO
Government debt inches up to P17.56trn in end-Oct
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 0.6% MoM to P17.56trn as of end-Oct
    • The increase was driven by net issuances of domestic and external liabilities alongside the depreciation of the peso against the US dollar
      • The peso averaged P58.3 per US dollar in Oct vs P57.3 in Sep
  • The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.6% and 31.4% being external borrowings
    • Domestic debt increased by 0.6% MoM to P12.1trn
    • External debt rose by 0.6% MoM to P5.5trn
  • The P17.56trn figure exceeds the P17.36-trn end-2025 projection by 1.2%
    • Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury stated that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down"
  • The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 3Q25 stood at 63.1% of GDP unchanged vs 2Q25 but up from 60.7% in 4Q24
  • The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025
    • The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028
