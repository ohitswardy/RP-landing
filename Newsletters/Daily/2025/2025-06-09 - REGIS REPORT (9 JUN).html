REGIS REPORT

09 June 2025

Asset quality figures seem to have deteriorated sharply MoM in April – NPL ratio up 9bps to 3.39%, PD ratio up 12bps to 4.26%, etc. But as we noted last month (see below) there was a huge P389bn MoM spike in interbank loans (IBL)  and reverse repos (RRPs) in March, likely due to the 200bps RRR cut (28 March). The IBL+RRP figure fell by an even larger P483bn MoM in April. This is a stark reminder why we use the ex-IBL/RRP data, to avoid wild swings in the denominator.

 

Asset quality was broadly flat to slightly worse MoM in April. NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) inched up 1bp to 3.56%, with gross NPLs up 0.6% MoM to P519bn. Past Due (PD) ratio increased 4bps MoM to 4.49% from 4.45% in March, as the Peso value of PDs climbed 1.1% MoM to P653bn. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio, meanwhile, improved 1bp MoM to 2.14%, as nominal RLs were up just 0.1% MoM. NPL Cover was steady, up 5bps to 95.10% from 95.05% in March. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) slowed to 11.2% YoY, slower then the 12.2% pace in March, and posting the slowest YoY figure since October 2024. Loans were up just 0.3% MoM. Loan to deposit ratio (LDR) jumped to 73.7% from 72.0% in March, and much higher than the 68.9% recorded in April 2024.

 

The MoM deterioration was driven largely by the Digibanks, which saw NPL ratio jump 40bps MoM to 10.16%, and PD ratios surge 174bps MoM to 19.07%. That said, both ratios are already significantly lower than where they were in April 2024 (see table below). For Thrift Banks (TBs), NPL ratio was down 10bps MoM, while PD rates jumped 9bps. This suggests to us that there may have been some write-offs in April.

 

We take comfort in the relative stability in asset quality, though we are mindful of the MoM jumps in PD ratios in smaller TBs and Digibanks, which could portend future deterioration in the consumer portfolios of the larger banks.

With CPI inflation for May dipping to a five-year low of 1.3%, the likelihood of a policy rate reduction by the Bangko Sentral (BSP) next week has increased further. Core inflation, as reported, was 2.2%, higher than the headline figure but still near the bottom end of the BSP’s inflation target range of 2-4%. The inflation figure/s released last week were the last major data points ahead of the Monetary Board meeting on June 19.

 

+++

 

The country’s gross international reserves were practically unchanged MoM at US$105.5bn, based on preliminary data. There was no major inflow or outflow (debt repayment) last month.

MACRO
Inflation rate inches down to 1.3% in May
  • Headline inflation eased further to 1.3% in May from 1.4% in the previous month of April, marking the lowest print since Nov 2019 (1.2%)
    • This is in-line with the consensus forecast of 1.3% and stands well-within the BSP's forecast range of 0.9-1.7%
    • Inflation has continued to be on a downtrend since the start of the year, falling from 2.9% in Jan to 1.3% as of May
  • Primary drivers for easing inflation were MoM price declines in rice -2.4%, electricity -0.2%, and fuel-related transport prices -2.9%, which offset rising prices of meat and vegetables
    • Notably, rice prices are on their eleventh consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024
      • Rice prices are now down 12.8% YoY in May
      • According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Jun 3), prices for local and imported well-milled rice both ranged from P38-48/kg, respectively vs. P40-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 3
  • Core inflation was relatively unchanged at 2.2% in May vs. Apr. While core non-food inflation in Apr inched down slightly to 2.0% from 2.1% in Apr
  • This brings the YT-May headline inflation rate to 1.9%, below the lower-end of the 2-4% gov't target for this year
    • Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.2%
      • Regis expects the full year average to be 3.0%
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of 6 this year) is on June 19
    • For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps total worth of cuts to 5.00%
    • We expect 50 bps worth of cuts to 5.25% but more could come if inflation stays benign or if the US Fed cuts rates more than 75 bps

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
PH Dollar reserves at $105.5bn end-May
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-May was relatively unchanged, up 0.1% MoM to US$105.5bn from US$105.3bn at end-Apr
    • The increase primarily reflected the upward valuations in BSP's gold holdings, net income from BSP's investments abroad, and net foreign exchange deposits of the national gov't with the BSP
    • The level of dollar reserves by end-May was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • Reserve position in the IMF fell 3.5% MoM to US$716mn
  • Buffers in the form of gold were valued at US$13.7bn, which rose 2.9% MoM as gold prices remained elevated in May
  • Foreign investments decreased by 0.3% MoM to US$86.4bn
  • Foreign currency deposits increased 4.7% MoM to US$704mn
  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$110bn by end-2025

 

 

 

MACRO/CONSUMER
PH consumer sentiment turns more pessimistic in 1Q25
  • The latest Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) results exhibited that current sentiment or the overall consumer index (CI) fell to -13.0% in 1Q25 from -11.1% the previous quarter and -10.9% back in 1Q24
    • Concerns over rising prices, declining wages, and fewer job opportunities were cited as the main reasons
    • Notably, “political noise” was also cited as a reason given respondents’ concerns on good governance and the gov’t’s ability to deliver their services amidst political turbulence
    • A negative CI implies more respondents are pessimistic than optimistic
  • Notably, consumer sentiment for the next quarter reverted to negative -0.5%, down from 4.2% the previous quarter and 2.7% in 1Q24
  • Consumers expect economic prospects to improve in the next 12 months, with a positive CI at 12.4%, flat from the previous quarter but lower than the 13.4% last year
  • The CES results are taken into account in the BSP's decision making for monetary policy
    • In light of the survey results, the BSP reiterates its commitment to keep inflation manageable as it is viewed to be within the 2-4% target range till 2027
