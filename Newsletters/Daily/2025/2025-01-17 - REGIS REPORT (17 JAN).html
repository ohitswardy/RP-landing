REGIS REPORT

17 January 2025

The Dept of Agriculture plans to declare a “food security emergency” relating to rice, allowing it to release its buffer stocks of the grain into the market.  While rice prices have already come off from their peaks last year, they remain too high, especially considering the tariff reduction implemented in July.

We share the view of the DA that prices have much room to come down should market forces be allowed to prevail. This means unseen bottlenecks in importation will need be removed.  If market forces work as they should, we think retail prices could fall another 10-15% from here.  That alone would shave off 1pt or more from CPI inflation.

+++

Our channel checks show a volume growth slowdown at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port in Mexico for the month of December, which we reckon could just be due to seasonal de-stocking. We will continue to monitor this situation closely. 

Despite the relative softness in December, the port in Mexico, ICT’s largest EBITDA contributor, delivered good TEU volumes in 4Q24, which should bode well for the company’s results for the period.

+++

Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported its passenger volume growth accelerated sharply to 32% YoY in 4Q24 from the 13% pace in 9M.  The substantial increase in capacity of 31% during the quarter was fully absorbed by the market.  

+++ 

Foreign ownership of the Philippine stock market fell 22 basis points MoM to 19.5% in December, reflecting the net foreign selling during the month. Meanwhile, foreign ownership of the PSE Index firms also dropped 6 basis points MoM to 24.9%.

MACRO
Department of Agriculture plans to declare food security emergency
  • The National Price Coordinating Council (NPCC) has approved a resolution urging the Department of Agriculture (DA) to declare a “food security emergency for rice” as prices remain high
    • Declaration would allow the National Food Authority (NFA) to release buffer stock rice to stabilize local prices
    • The NFA would replace the released stocks with locally-produced rice to support Filipino farmers
  • Agriculture Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. welcomed the resolution, as it will complement with the DA’s ongoing efforts
    • The DA will implement a maximum suggested retail price (SRP) of P58 per kilo for imported rice, effective January 20
  • Rice prices remain elevated despite reduced import tariffs.
    • As of January 15, imported rice prices in Metro Manila ranged from P44 to P65 per kilogram, depending on the type
TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volume growth slows to 4% YoY in December; 4Q24 volume growth accelerates to 16% YoY
  • Data from Mexico’s port regulator showed that volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), grew by 4% YoY to 117k TEUs in December
  • CMSA lost a bit of market share in Dec, the first time it ceded share since it completed its expansion in end-September
    • Industry-wide volumes grew by 6% YoY in Dec (ex-CMSA, volumes at other competing ports in Manzanillo rose by 7% YoY)
    • It is worth noting that import volumes softened across all ports in Manzanillo, suggesting the start of de-stocking phase
  • Brings 4Q24 volumes at CMSA up by 16% YoY, accelerating from the 2% pace in 3Q24 and 13% in 9M24
    • For FY24, CMSA’s volumes grew by 14% YoY to 1.45mn TEUs, broadly in line with Regis FY24E of 1.47mn TEUs
    • For FY25E, we’ve built in a 15% YoY increase in CMSA’s volumes
    • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of FY25E consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, 26% of EBITDA
  • ICT trades at 7.9x FY25E EV/EBITDA, lower than peer average of (11x) and historical average of (11x)

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air posts 32% YoY growth in 4Q24 pax volumes, SLF stable
  • CEB reported that it carried 2.6mn pax in Dec 2024, a 31% YoY increase, resulting in a mild improvement in seat load factor (SLF) to 85.2% from 84.8%
    • This brings 4Q24 pax volumes higher by 32% YoY to 7.0mn, accelerating sharply from the 13% growth rate in 9M24, with SLF broadly unchanged QoQ, YoY at 83.1%
  • For FY24, pax volumes grew by 18% YoY to 24.5mn, within Regis’ FY24E of 24.6mn
    • Seat load factor slightly improved to 84.4% from 84% in FY23
    • Domestic pax volumes rose by 16% to 18.5mn while international pax volumes grew 25% to 6.0mn
  • To the downside, the airline continued to invest heavily in 4Q, ending 2024 with a fleet of 98, up from 91 in end-Sep 2024 and 85 in end-Dec 2023 (vs. 74 pre-Covid)
    • This suggests costs may have remained elevated in 4Q
  • Management guiding seat growth of 20-25% in 2025 on a net fleet addition of one aircraft, following the end of the company’s investment phase
    • Operating leverage should start kicking in, with aircraft utilization rates likely improving
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in December
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 22bps MoM to 19.5% (in December) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 6bps MoM to 24.9%
  • Net foreign selling hit US$104mn, lower than the US$349mn in November 
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 6 saw increases in foreign ownership while 24 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • URC – foreign ownership fell 39bps MoM to 22.2%. Net foreign selling was the sixth highest for the month, at US$10.1mn.  
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership dropped 39bps MoM to 16.0%.
    • GLO – foreign ownership increased 12bps MoM to 54.6%. Net foreign buying was the fourth highest for the month, at US$3.2mn.
    • SCC – foreign ownership increased 11bps MoM to 4.1% as net foreign buying hit US$9.5mn, the second highest for the month.
