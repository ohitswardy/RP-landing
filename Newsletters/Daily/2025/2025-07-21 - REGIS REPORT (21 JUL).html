REGIS REPORT

21 July 2025

Online gambling remains the hot button issue both in the stock market and the broader public realm, and now even President Marcos has weighed in.

 

In his official YouTube channel, Marcos mentioned (in Filipino) that many families are being destroyed by online gambling. His statements now make it virtually certain that the government’s planned actions on the gambling industry will be part of his State of the Nation Address (SONA) next week. The SONA will outline his administration’s priorities over the following year, and we expect action to address the proliferation of online gambling to be among them.

 

Many politicians have advocated for different measures, including an outright ban on online gambling. The leading operator of online gambling in the country, Digiplus (PLUS PM), is predictably supporting tighter regulation in lieu of an outright ban.

 

We shall see how all this shapes up in the coming weeks.

 

+++

 

The new chairman of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) outlined his initial priorities for the commission, and we are inclined to think that Meralco’s (MER PM) long-delayed tariff review (due three weeks ago) may be delayed even further, notwithstanding the fact that the leadership at the ERC is now complete again.

 

+++

 

Maya, PLDT’s (TEL PM) fintech affiliate, continues to be in the black for 2Q, according to PLDT chairman Manny Pangilinan. Among the big issues of the day is Maya’s exposure to online gambling, and on this topic, Pangilinan mentioned it is no more than 10% of revenue. The company clarified that online gambling is ~10% of enterprise revenue.

 

 

+++

 

The tax bureau says it reached its 1H25 collection target of P1.5trn, which would mean its revenues were up at least 10% YoY. It is, however, looking to revise its target for FY25 downwards from P3.23trn (+13%).

 

+++

 

Meanwhile, the SEC has approved the IPO application of Hann Holdings, which operates Hann Resorts, an integrated resort (gambling + hotel) located in Clark Freeport in Pampanga. Hann is aiming to raise up to P13bn (US$228m) in the IPO. More below.

GAMING
DigiPlus Interactive supports tighter regulation of online gaming
  • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) expressed its support for balanced regulation that protects players, ensures industry accountability, and sustains economic value generated by the legal online gaming sector 
  • Some of the proposed regulations are already implemented in its platforms, including government ID verification, age restrictions, self-exclusion options, and cooling off periods
  • DigiPlus has also partnered with NGOs to promote financial literacy and digital responsibility among players 
  • The company intends to roll out further initiatives such as affordability checks, referral pathways to licensed mental health experts, and an in-app community space to engage players in responsible gaming conversations and peer support
POWER
New ERC chair lists five focus areas for agency
  • In a radio interview, newly appointed Chairman of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), Francis Saturnino Juan, listed his top priorities once he assumes office on Aug 8
    • Re-review of the performance-based rate-setting methodology for private distribution utilities, such as Meralco (MER PM)
    • Revisit the approval process of Power Supply Agreements that pass through the Competitive Selection Process to fast track
    • Review the current yardstick regulation used for setting the on-grid electric cooperatives’ (ECs) rates and allow individual rate applications to help distressed ECs earn sufficient revenues
    • Consider allowing utilities, such as the National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) to start implementing their proposed capex, after three months from submission
    • Consider the possibility of deeming as approved, all certificates of compliance (COC) applications after 30 days from filing
  • Mr. Juan aims to address the backlog at ERC by streamlining processes and simplifying requirements
DIGITAL FINANCE/TELCOS
Maya maintains positive profits in 1H25; online gaming at <10% of enterprise revenues
  • Maya chairman Manuel Pangilinan said that the company was “doing very well” and showed “good profitability” in 1H25
    • Recall that Maya recorded a profit of P300mn in 1Q25, a turnaround from a net loss of P400mn in the same period a year ago
    • Mr. Pangilinan previously said that the company’s good start may enable it to exceed P1bn in earnings by end of year
  • Meanwhile, Pangilinan said that the platform’s exposure to online gambling is not that significant or “at least less than a tenth of revenues”
    • The company clarified that ~10% of enterprise revenue is from online gambling
  • PLDT owns 37.7% of Maya Innovations
MACRO
BIR achieves first half collection target of P1.5tn
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) hit its revenue collection target with more than P1.5tn collected (+10.3% YoY) in the 1H25
    • The increase was primarily driven by better tax compliance and a broader taxpayer base
      • Notably, small taxpayers (gross revenues between P3mn-P20mn) rose more than 80% YoY
      • Excise tax collections were also said to have increased in the first half
  • BIR Commissioner Romeo Lumagui noted that the BIR could start collecting the online gambling tax (proposed by the Dept. of Finance) in 4Q26 if ratified on schedule
    • It was guided that the online gambling tax can contribute billions in revenue
  • Lumagui is upbeat after hitting the 1H collection target but guides that the BIR may need to revise downward its P3.23tn (+13.4% YoY) goal for 2025
    • The 1H tax collection of P1.5tn is ~46% of the P3.23tn full-year target
  • The national gov't revenue target for 2025 is P4.52tn (+2.3% YoY)
    • This was previously revised downwards from P4.64tn (+5.0% YoY)

GAMING
SEC approves IPO of Clark integrated resort operator
  • The SEC approved the IPO of Hann Holdings, which operates Hann Resorts, an integrated casino resort in Clark
  • 500mn primary shares at up to P23.60/share (primary offer of P11.8bn)
    • Plus an overallotment option of 50mn shares
    • Maximum offer size of P13bn
  • Including the overallotment option – the IPO shares would represent 22% of expanded shares outstanding
  • At the maximum price, the market cap would be P59bn
    • The company recorded net profit of P1.7bn 2024. This implies 34.7X FY24 P/E at the maximum offer price. 
    • This would be higher than BLOOM’s current market cap of P46bn
  • Pricing date – September 4
  • Offer period – September 9 to 15
  • Listing date – September 23
  • Hann Resorts, which is located in Clark, has 240 gaming tables and 1,230 slots/ETG
    • Solaire Entertainment City has about 350 gaming tables and 2,700 slots/ETG
  • The IR includes hotel brands such as Swissotel and Clark Marriott
  • The company is developing Hann Reserve, a 450-hectare mountain resort in New Clark City
    • It will feature three PGA affiliated championship golf courses, luxury hotels, convention facilities, luxury residences, and an international school
  • In 1Q25, Hann generated P3.4bn in GGR, representing 48% of Clark’s gaming market
  • 1Q25 GGR of the NCR integrated resorts stood at:
    • Solaire Entertainment City – P12.1bn
    • Newport Resorts – P7.9bn
    • Okada Manila – P7.8bn
    • City of Dreams Manila – P6.3bn
    • Solaire North – P4.6bn
MACRO
BOP shifts to US$226mn surplus in June; GIR settles at US$106.0bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$226mn
    • A turnaround from both the US$155bn deficit in June 2024 and May 2025's US$298mn deficit
  • The surplus reflected the national gov't's foreign currency deposits with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) and income from BSP investments
  • This brings the YT-June deficit at US$5.6bn, reversing from the US$1.4bn surplus in the same period last year
    • The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade gap, higher net outflows in the financial account, and frontloaded external debt repayments
  • The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (-1.3% of GDP)
    • The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-June stood at US$106.0bn, up from the $105.2bn in end-May
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025
