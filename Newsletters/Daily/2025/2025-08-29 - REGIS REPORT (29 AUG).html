REGIS REPORT

29 August 2025

As expected, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) reduced its policy rate by 25bp to 5.00% yesterday. The move was telegraphed early by the BSP Governor Eli Remolona so it should have been no surprise to anyone.

 

Remolona is now saying that current rates are in the “sweet spot”, implying rates are at a “neutral” level. Despite this, he is not closing the door on another policy rate reduction before the year ends.

 

There’s room for policy rates to go down further, in our view. Relative to history, current real interest rates are still high (>4%) while and CPI inflation is low (=0.9%). Meanwhile, GDP growth has been rather disappointing (5.5% for 2Q). Money supply growth (~6%) also suggests the economy may need more monetary stimulus. We still expect another rate cut this year which will trim the policy rate to 4.75%.

MACRO
BSP cuts rate by 25bp to 5.00%; forecasts inflation to settle at 1.7% for 2025
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%
    • This is the third consecutive 25bp cut this year since previously cutting rates in April and June
  • BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that the current policy rate level is the "sweet spot" or 'Goldilocks' rate given the latest data
    • However, he added that another cut this year is still a possibility depending on how the data develops for the rest of the year (e.g., weakness in demand)
  • The inflation outlook was noted to be very manageable and is seen to settle at 1.7% for 2025, 3.3% for 2026, and 3.4% for 2027
    • Primary risks were said to be potential electricity rate adjustments and increased rice tariffs
    • Average inflation for 7M25 is 1.7%
  • The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively
  • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75% and inflation to average 1.8%

 

TRANSPORT
Mexico plans to raise tariffs on Chinese imports?
  • The government of Mexico is reportedly planning to raise tariffs on Chinese imports (here)
    • Article did not specify the extent of the tariff increase
    • High-value Chinese imports (value >US$117) are currently taxed at 19% while low-value imports are now taxed at 33.5% (started last Aug 15)
    • We suspect this is part of Mexico’s concessions to win Trump's favor and avoid a cancellation of USMCA
  • ICTSI’s port in Mexico is estimated to generate ~30% of throughput from imports of Chinese goods (i.e. 48% of total throughput are imports, of which ~60% originate from China)
    • We estimate the port in Mexico accounts for ~11% of volumes and ~25% of EBITDA
MACRO
Government posts P19bn budget deficit in July; P784bn deficit as of 7M25
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P19bn in July, which is narrower both by 34% YoY and 92% MoM
  • This brings the 7M25 deficit to P784bn, 22% wider YoY
    • The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "well within target and on track with the P1.56trn full-year program" as the gov't continues to ramp up public spending to support economic growth
    • The wider YTD deficit results from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues as customs collections continues to lag behind the full-year target
  • Revenue collections in July increased by a modest 3% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues
    • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 5% YoY mainly due to higher revenues from corporate and personal income tax
    • Customs collections accelerated to 6% from the 1% growth seen in 6M25 given higher value-added tax and excise collections for the month
    • Non-tax revenues fell 10% which was offset by remittances from the Bureau of Treasury and interest income from national gov't deposits
  • Expenditures inched up 1% YoY in July as primary spending was weighed down by delayed disbursements
    • Primary spending for the month was lower by 5%
      • The spending slowdown was said to stem from timing of big-ticket disbursements of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways, Dept. of Social Welfare and Development, and Dept. of National Defense
    • Interest payments jumped 34% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt
  • The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024

 

 

 

 

POWER
ACEN completes 600MW Monsoon Wind project; plans 2GW expansion in Australia
  • ACEN’s 600MW Monsoon Wind project reached its Commercial Operation Date (COD) on 22 August, after constructing the project within 27 months
    • Project, located in Lao PDR, will sell power to Vietnam’s national grid
    • ACEN owns an effective 24% stake in the project
    • On a full-year basis, we estimate the project could add ~3% to ACEN’s core att. EBITDA
  • Meanwhile, ACEN Executive Chairman Jose Maria Zabaleta said that the company plans to develop more than 2GW GWdc of RE projects in Australia over the next three years
    • Among the projects set for construction are: 900MW Valley of the Winds project (completion by 2030); 780-MWdc Birriwa Solar project
    • ACEN currently has over 1,000 MW of operating plants in Australia, which we estimate contributes 9% to core att. EBITDA
  • ACEN has 7,053 GW of RE capacity, of which 3,598 MW are operating, 2,941 MW are under construction, and 514MW are “committed”

BYD on track to operate 77 dealerships this year
  • BYD Cars Philippines managing director Bob Palanca said that it is on track to hit its goal of having 77 operational dealerships in the country by year-end
    • There are currently 40 operational BYD dealerships (full service and showrooms) in the country today
