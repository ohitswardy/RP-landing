REGIS REPORT

15 October 2025

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that the country may experience slower economic growth until early 2026 because of the flood control corruption scandal. History would suggest he is probably right. After the PDAF scandal erupted in 2013, GDP growth was weak for over a year, due in large part to a slowdown in government spending.

Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Commissioner Romeo Lumagui Jr. is also blaming weak tax collections to the corruption scandal.

 

On a more positive note, Secretary Recto believes there could be long-term upside from reforms to be instituted.

MACRO
Finance Secretary says corruption scandal may slow GDP growth until 1Q26; hopeful for another rate cut
  • Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that the Philippine economy may experience a slowdown in growth until early 2026 given the flood control corruption scandal
    • Recall that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. flagged irregularities in flood control projects with investigations initiated by the Senate, the House, and the recently formed Independent Commission for Infrastructure
  • Recto guided that there will be a slow down on public spending but highlighted that there will also be upside from reforms moving forward
    • He said that if the budget was utilized properly, GDP growth may have grown 6.0-6.2% as well as tax and customs collections would have been higher
    • Recto is optimistic that the economy will hit the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% GDP growth target this year
      • 1H25 GDP growth was at 5.4%
      • Regis expects GDP growth to be 5.5% this year
  • The Finance Secretary said that there will "hopefully, [be] another rate cut" but it will be hinged on inflation staying within target (2-4%)
    • Inflation averaged 1.7% for 9M25
      • Regis expects inflation to average 1.8% for 2025
    • The BSP has reduced rates by a total of 100bp to 4.75% so far, with one more meeting by Dec 11
      • Regis expects a total of 100bp cuts this year to 4.75% and 25bp to 4.50% for next year
MACRO
BIR expects lower tax collection due to corruption scandal; Finance Secretary still expects to hit revenue target
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Commissioner Romeo Lumagui Jr. stated that its tax collections have declined or slowed down in September likely due to the lingering issues regarding the flood control corruption scandal
  • Lumagui guided that BIR collections may decline in September
    • The BIR has collected P2.14trn as of 8M25, 11% higher YoY
  • Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said that the gov't remains on track to meet its revenue target of P4.52trn (+16% YoY) this year
    • Recto stated that he expects non-tax revenues to cover the lower-than-expected tax and customs collections
