REGIS REPORT

08 May 2025

Results! Results! We have comments on 1Q25 earnings from SM, CNPF, MEG and PLUS, which broadly reflect our theme of a strong recovery in consumer demand.

 

But before that, some macro news bits. First, the government has approved 207 infrastructure flagship projects (IFPs) worth a total P10trn ($178bn). As impressive as that sounds, it seems only 13 will be completed this year (no size/value reported), and it is unclear how many will be launched / bid out in the near term. Since the privatization of Manila’s main NAIA airport, there has been no major PPP project announced. Meanwhile, government debt inched up to P16.7trn, close to its target P17.4trn by yearend (c60.4% of GDP). Finally, Gross International Reserves fell 2% MoM to $106.7bn, though this was still very healthy at 7.2x import cover, and 3.6x cover on short-term external debt maturities.

 

Now, onto the results:

 

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (=) – 1Q profits grew 9% to P20bn, which should in line with consensus expectations.  SM Retail delivered 18% profit growth on the back of 7% revenue growth, rebounding from last year’s 1Q decline. The performance of the key listed subsidiaries (BDO, SM Prime and CBC) were otherwise largely within expectations and previously discussed.

 

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (=) – Profits for 1Q25 rose 11% to P1.9bn as revenues grew 10%, well within expectations and management guidance.  Noteworthy was the acceleration in branded revenue growth to 13%, which carried the top line amidst the slight dip in OEM revenue.  Management is flagging a slight drop in gross margins in subsequent quarters but will try to manage this by reducing opex (e.g. A&P costs) to keep EBITDA margins stable.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (=) – 1Q25 net profit rose 16% to P5.1bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Office revenue grew 17% YoY as the rent-free periods of some tenants lapsed. Meanwhile, mall revenue increased 12% YoY, thanks to stronger tenant sales. No presales numbers were released, though we expect them to be disclosed at the briefing later today.

 

  • Digiplus (PLUS PM) – net profit for 1Q25 doubled, up 110% YoY to P4.2bn. However, it seems operations are stabilizing. On a QoQ basis, NPAT was up 8% with revenues down 3%.

 

Speaking of gaming, PAGCOR reported that gross gaming revenue (GGR) of licensed casinos in 1Q25 fell 5% OoQ (down 1% YoY) to P49.3bn. This is a more muted drop than we would have expected, considering that the POGO ban only became effective in January. Since Okada Manila’s GGR fell 13% QoQ, the other operators (in aggregate) should have seen GGR fall by less than 5% QoQ. Meanwhile, industry GGR from bingo and electronic games remained flat QoQ (up 88% YoY) at P51.4bn. This matches the figures from e-games market leader DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM), whose GGR fell 3% QoQ (up 69% YoY), despite spending more on advertising and promotions.

Alliance Global (AGI PM) disclosed it would sell warrants that could increase common shares outstanding by 25%. The exercise price for the conversion is P12%, nearly double the current share price. Terms of the offer have yet to be set.

CONSUMER
SM / SM Retail - Comments on 1Q25
  • SM Investments profits rose 9% to P20.1bn in 1Q25, which appears in line with Street expectations

 

  • Profits for SM Retail rose 18% to P3.6bn on the back of 7% increase in revenue to P100bn.  Note 1Q25 represents a rebound from the depressed 1Q24 profit (P3.1bn). 1Q25 profit is actually still lower than 1Q23 (=P3.9bn).

 

  • Revenue for Food Retail (62% of retail revenue) rose 8% in 1Q25 to P61.5bn, with profits growing 19% YoY. Note that the big jump comes from a low 1Q24 base.  Recall that RRHI’s Food segment reported 3% revenue growth while Puregold reported 11% growth for 1Q25.

 

  • SSSG for the aggregate retail business was +4.1%, with Food Retail, the biggest component, showing +4.3%.  SSSG for SM Stores (Dept Stores) was +4.6% and for Specialty Stores was +3.2%.  Note that two factors distorted SSSG numbers, in possibly opposite directions for most formats.  These two factors are (1) the timing of Holy Week and (2) the extra calendar day because of the leap year (1Q24).

 

Holy Week timing had a positive effect on most formats this year (except Alfamart) while the extra day in 1Q24 would have a negative effect. Alfamart’s SSS for 1Q was supposed to have contracted by 1%, due to the effect of both the timing of Holy Week and the leap year last year.  Alfamart added 52 stores in the quarter, taking it to 2,144.

 

 

PROPERTY
Megaworld 1Q25 net profit rises 16%
  • Megaworld’s 1Q25 net profit rose 16% YoY to P5.1bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • The company likely recognized forex gains on US$ debt thanks to the appreciation of the peso in 1Q25
  • Revenue of the various segments was generally above our expectations:
    • Residential revenue grew 8% YoY (flat QoQ)
    • Office revenue rose 17% YoY (-2% QoQ) due to the lapse of rent-free periods for a building in Bonifacio, which accounted for about 5% of total space; some contracts were also renewed at higher rental rates
    • Mall revenue increased 12% YoY (-7% QoQ) on higher tenant sales
    • Hotel revenue climbed 26% YoY, partly due to the c. 15% increase in the number of rooms 
  • Operating profit rose 9% YoY (+10% QoQ) to P7.6bn, above REGIS and consensus forecast
    • Residential GPM of 50% is higher than our FY25E forecast of 48%
  • MEG is trading at 71% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 3.1X FY25E P/E.
  • The company will host a briefing this afternoon

 

 

GAMING
PLUS 1Q25 net profit rises 110% YoY
  • Net profit of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) grew 110% YoY to P4.2bn, broadly in line with consensus forecast
  • Despite a 5ppt QoQ reduction in fees charged by PAGCOR, revenue fell 3% QoQ (up 69% YoY) to P23.1bn
  • PLUS had c. 45% market share of the e-games segment in 1Q25, flat from 4Q24
  • PLUS is trading at 10.8X FY25E consensus forecast

 

 

GAMING
Philippine gross gaming revenue rises 27% YoY in 1Q25
  • PAGCOR reported that the industry’s GGR rose 27% YoY (-2% QoQ) to P104bn
  • GGR from Bingo and electronic games grew 88% YoY (flat QoQ) to P51.4bn
    • For the most part, the fees charged by PAGCOR on e-games fell 5ppt QoQ to 30%
  • GGR from licensed casinos fell 1% YoY (down 5% QoQ) to P49.3bn
    • Solaire North was only opened in late 2Q24
    • Okada Manila’s 1Q25 GGR fell 13% QoQ and 12% YoY to P7.8bn
    • We forecast BLOOM’s Philippine GGR to stay flat in FY25E (at P61bn); we project SEC’s GGR to fall 23% YoY to P40.9bn

 

Note: since Okada Manila’s GGR fell faster than the industry’s, the rest of the industry (in aggregate) should have seen GGR fall by less than 5% QoQ

Alliance Global to issue warrants
  • Alliance Global will issue and offer warrants covering up to 2.226bn common shares exercisable at a minimum price of P12 per underlying common share, to eligible stockholders
  • The underlying common shares to be issued after the exercise of the warrants shall have the same features and right as existing common shares
  • The exercise of the warrants would expand common shares outstanding by 25% to 11.1bn shares
  • The exercise price of P12/share is 90% higher than the last traded price

  • The proceeds of the offer will be used to finance capital expenditures, repayment of debt obligations, and general corporate purposes
MACRO
PH gov't approves more infrastructure flagship projects
  • The Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev) stated that the gov't has approved more infrastructure flagship projects (IFP) to 207 total projects that amount to US$178bn (~P9.86tn)
    • This is an increase from 186 projects worth P9.6tn that were initially slated late last year
  • Majority of the IFPs are related to physical connectivity/transportation at 139 projects
    • These are followed by 32 projects in water resources, nine in agriculture, six in digital connectivity, five in health, three in power and energy, two in housing, and two in education, among other sectors
  • The gov't targets to complete 13 IFPs this year
    • Seven IFPs were completed last year
  • For 2025, total infrastructure disbursement is expected to be at least 5.4 percent of GDP
    • The Marcos administration aims to spend 5-6% of GDP on infrastructure annually
MACRO
PH Dollar reserves down 1.9% MoM at end-April
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Apr saw a 1.9% MoM decrease to US$104.6bn from US$106.7bn at end-Feb
    • The decrease primarily reflected the national government's drawdowns on foreign currency deposits with the BSP to meet external debt obligations as well as the BSP's net foreign exchange operations
    • The level of dollar reserves by end-Apr was enough to cover about 3.6x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • Reserve position in the IMF jumped 13.6% MoM to US$742mn
  • Buffers in the form of gold were valued at US$13.3bn, which rose 4.5% MoM as gold continues hit new all-time highs in April
  • Foreign investments decreased by 3.2% MoM to US$86.1bn
  • Foreign currency deposits surged 23.5% MoM to US$649mn
  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$110bn by end-2025

 

 

 

MACRO
Government debt inches up to P16.68tn in end-Mar
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 0.31% MoM to P16.68tn as of end-Mar
    • This was due to the net issuance of new domestic and external debt which was offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P57.897 end-Feb to P57.382 end-Mar) as well as net repayments
  • Domestic debt rose by 1.4% (+P155.8bn) MoM to P11.38tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P157.9bn)
  • External debt decreased by 1.9% (+P103.9bn) MoM to P5.3tn given a decrease in the peso equivalent of USD debt (P66.2bn) due to local currency appreciation, and net repayments (P60.8bn)
  • The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.2% and 31.8% being external borrowings
  • The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn (see here)
    • This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%
    • The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%
