REGIS REPORT

11 December 2025

After an extended delay due to the suit filed by a losing bidder, port operator ICTSI (ICT PM) finally signed the contract to take over Durban Container Terminal Pier 2. Recall that ICT won the right to run the terminal with a bid of ZAR11bn (~US$650m) for a 50% stake (less 1 share). It’s now scheduled to take over operations of the port at start of 2026. We view this deal as a significant transaction given that Durban is the largest port in the largest economy in Africa.

 

The Durban terminal is, by accounts, run quite inefficiently which implies that there will be plenty of room for operations there to be improved. We previously estimated this acquisition will add P30/share to the DCF value of ICT. Incremental value from this deal will depend on the company’s ability to extract more synergies / efficiencies.

 

+++

 

The local SEC released its order capping the interest rates that non-bank financing firms can impose on borrowers to 6% per month, with an effective interest rate cap (including fees, etc.) of 12% per month. This cap is slightly higher than what was previously proposed in a draft circular that we previously wrote about (link to report for int'l investors, for local).

 

The rate ceiling imposed by the SEC covers only non-bank financing firms and does not cover banks, including digibanks. It will take effect from Apr 2026 onwards.

With the US Federal Reserve reducing its target Fed funds rate by 25bp (to 3.50% - 3.75%), the Bangko Sentral (BSP) now looks almost certain to follow with its own policy rate reduction this afternoon. The current policy rate is 4.75% and the market (including Regis) expects this to be reduced by 25bp today.

TRANSPORT
ICTSI, Transnet sign concession agreement for Durban port
  • ICTSI and South Africa’s state-owned Transnet signed a 25-year partnership agreement for Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2)
    • Agreement will take effect by January 1, 2026, when ICTSI will take over operations of the port
    • ICTSI will own a 50% less one (1) share interest in the joint venture entity
  • Earlier reports indicated that ICTSI would make an upfront payment of ZAR11bn (~US$650mn) to Transnet
  • DCT-2 is expected to increase its capacity from 2mn TEUs to 2.8mn TEUs and improve Gross Crane Moves per Hour (GCH) from 18 to 28 as well as Ship Working Hour (SWH) from 60 to 120
  • We previously estimated DCT-2 could add P30/sh (6%) to ICTSI’s valuation (here)
  • ICTSI trades at 10.7x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 18x PER vs. peer average (12.6x EV/EBITDA / 18x PER) and historical average (11x EV/EBITDA / 20x PER)
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
SEC sets monthly interest rate cap on loans up to P10,000
  • The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), through a memorandum circular, has issued an order limiting the interest rate that financing and lending companies can charge on loans of up to P10,000 and loan tenor of up to four months
    • The circular will take effect on April 1, 2026
    • Banks, investment houses, savings and loans associations, insurance companies, cooperatives, and other credit institutions already regulated by law are not subject to the new circular
  • The prescribed ceilings are as follows:
    • The nominal interest rate is capped at 6% per month or 0.2% per day
    • The effective interest rate is limited to 12% per month or 0.4% per day
      • Effective interest rate is the total nominal interest paid plus other fees and charges but excluding penalty and late payment fees
    • Penalties for late or non-payment is capped at 5% per month on the outstanding scheduled amount due
    • A total cost cap of 100% of the total amount borrowed (applying to all interest, other fees and charges, and penalties), regardless of the time the loan has been outstanding
  • Non-compliance with the prescribed ceilings will be subject to a fine of P50,000 on first offense, a larger monetary penalty plus suspension of financing and lending activities for 60 days for the second offense, and a revocation of Certificate of Authority and Certificate of Incorporation by the third offense
  • Any circumvention or attempt to circumvent the ceilings shall also constitute a violation
MACRO
PH dollar reserves at $111.1bn end-Nov
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Nov increased 0.8% MoM to US$111.1bn, a 13-month high
    • The increase primarily reflected the surge in global gold prices which offset the drop in foreign exchange holdings due to the BSP's intervention in the forex market
      • The peso depreciated against the US dollar from an average of P58.3 in Oct to P58.9 in Nov
    • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.4x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$105bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn
