REGIS REPORT

21 January 2025

ACEN (ACEN PM) says it will spend P80bn (US$1.4bn) in capex this year, even higher than last year’s P55bn (<US$1bn), as part of its continuing growth plans. 

 

Given its spending plans and current financial position, ACEN will, in our view, need more capital soon. Net gearing (attributable share, inclusive of unconsolidated operating firms) was already 2x as of Sept 2025 and net debt/EBITDA (also attributable share) was likely >10x for FY25.

 

With its stock rights issue seemingly postponed indefinitely, an alternative capital-raising plan is likely in the works.

 

+++

 

We release a new report on AREIT (AREIT PM), raising our target price by 6% (to P41.00/share) as we roll over to FY26E and account for recently announced acquisitions. However, we maintain our Hold rating on the stock.

 

Although AREIT has superior occupancy and a track record of consistent DPS growth, its portfolio is heavily concentrated in the office segment, where we expect only modest improvements over the next few years. Furthermore, we forecast AREIT’s DPS CAGR to decelerate to 5% in 2025-2027E.

 

At the current price, AREIT offers an FY26E dividend yield of 5.7%, lower than the 5.9%-8.3% yield of other Philippine REITs. It is also trading at FY26E P/E of 15.8X, higher than the PSEi’s FY26E P/E of 10X. While AREIT is not particularly expensive, its sponsor and parent firm Ayala Land (ALI PM), offers much better value, in our view. ALI is trading at 59% discount to NAV, 1.0X FY26E P/B, and 10.4X FY26E P/E. ALI would offer 73% total return to our target price of P38.20/share.

POWER
ACEN eyes P80bn capex in 2026; outlook upbeat
  • ACEN Corp. is set to spend P80bn on capex this year, higher than last year’s capex of P55bn
    • According to President and CEO Eric Francia
    • Over P60bn of its 2026 budget will be allocated to the development of its major renewable energy projects in the Philippines
    • Company aims to raise its attributable operating capacity to 5GW by end-2026 and 7GW by end-2027, up from 4.3GW in November 2026
  • The company also anticipates improved performance this year compared with 2025
    • Cited (a) additional output from newly energized plants, (b) completion of repairs of its north Luzon wind plants, and (c) higher price for its mid-merit contract with Meralco, which will take effect in Feb 2026
  • Note that ACEN’s att. net obligations (inclusive of prefs and leases) stood at P236bn in end-September 2025 vs.

    equity of P133bn (net of prefs)

    • Company estimates its interest coverage ratio stood at 1.3x
    • We anticipate ACEN’s core attributable EBITDA will improve to P29bn in 2026 and P37bn in 2027 from an estimated P22bn in 2025 (9M25A: P15.6bn)
CONSUMER
Jollibee's Compose Coffee to expand in Southeast Asia

MACRO
Rice output falls short of 2025 target
  • Philippine production of palay (unmilled rice) amounted to 19.68 MMT (+3% YoY) in 2025
    • The FY25 output fell short of the Dept. of Agriculture's (DA) projected output of 20.35 MMT
  • The DA projects palay production this year to be 20.3 MMT (+3% YoY), with imports forecasted to range between 3.6 MMT and 3.8 MMT
    • Rice imports in 2025 amounted to 3.4 MMT, significantly down from 4.8 MMT back in 2024
  • Some farmers guided that output improvements despite investments from the gov't may not manifest given the sharp decline in farmgate prices
    • Average farmgate prices in 2025 fell 17% to P17.7/kg from P21.3 in 2024
    • Recall that President Marcos reduced the rice tariff to 15% from 35% back in June 2024, leading to an influx of imports and the decline in farmgate prices

 

 

MACRO
BOP posts US$827mn deficit in Dec; GIR settles at US$110.8bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Dec posted a deficit of US$827mn
    • Wider than the US$225mn deficit in Nov but narrower than the $1.5bn deficit in Dec 2024
  • This brings the YT-Dec deficit at US$5.7bn, a sharp reversal from the US$609mn surplus in the same period last year
    • The reversal to a FY deficit was said to have been likely driven by the trade gap, harsher-than-usual weather, and corruption-related issues impacting domestic demand and foreign investment
    • The FY figure is narrower than the lowered US$6.2bn deficit target of the BSP for 2025 (US$6.9bn previously)
    • For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn
  • Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Dec stood at US$110.8bn, 4% higher YoY and down from the US$111.3bn in end-Nov
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • This is higher than the BSP's forecast of $109bn for 2025
    • For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn
