REGIS REPORT

06 November 2025

Noodle and biscuit maker Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) reported a +4% increase in core profit to P2.5bn, behind a 5% increase in revenue (to P22bn) for 3Q25.  For 9M25, the top line was up 3% while core profit dipped 4%. The bottom-line results were behind estimates due to weaker margins, but the sales figures still look decent.

 

Among the highlights: (1) APAC BFB revenue growth was volume-led, with notable strength in biscuits and “others” (beverages, etc.); (1) noodle volumes, particularly the soupy variant, were still down, continuing 2Q trends; and (3) Quorn managed to break even despite the absence of revenue growth.

The government issued the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) yesterday, paving the way for its effectivity 15 days later. Pending some time to parse through the IRR and gather comments from the operators, we’d highlight the key provisions of the KPA.

 

  • Removal of the need for a franchise (license) from Congress to offer data services
  • Mandatory sharing of infrastructure with other operators at non-discriminatory rates
  • Establishment of a “Spectrum Management Policy Framework”
  • Provides for consumer rights and expectations on performance standards

 

We expect the KPA to lead to greater competition, particularly for the broadband sector.

PLDT (TEL PM) chairman Manuel Pangilinan says 3Q revenue for the telco will be better, helped by the home and enterprise segments. He also mentioned that Maya was doing better despite the hit from the mandated de-linking of its platform from gaming apps. Notable in Pangilinan’s remarks was the absence of the mobile segment.

The CPI inflation figure of 1.7% reported yesterday was held lower by food prices. Rice prices are still down MoM (-0.5% MoM, -17.0% YoY) and helped restrain the jump in vegetable prices (+2.1% MoM, +16.6% YoY). More below.

TELCOS
Government issues IRR for Konektadong Pinoy Act
  • The Dept of Information & Communications Technology (DICT) has released the final implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA)
    • The publication of the IRR paves the way for the implementation of the KPA
    • The IRR takes effect 15 days from publication (Nov 5)
  • Among the highlights of the KPA:
    • New operators no longer need to secure a franchise (license) from Congress to offer services
    • Carriers will be required to share their infrastructure with other operators at non-discriminatory rates
    • Government will establish a “Spectrum Management Policy Framework” to govern management, issuance and recall of spectrum
    • Provides for consumer rights and expectations on performance standards
  • We expect implementation of the KPA to usher in greater competition, particularly for the wireline broadband sector
  • DICT claims that “three” foreign operators are planning to enter the market because of the KPA
TELCOS
PLDT expects higher 3Q revenue
  • According to PLDT Inc. (TEL PM) Chairman and CEO Manuel V. Pangilinan, he expects the company to report higher 3Q revenue behind its home and enterprise businesses
  • Mr. Pangilinan noted that 3Q is "slightly better than the two previous quarters" but also added that Maya still boosted profits despite being slightly affected by the delinking from gaming sites
    • PLDT owns 37.7% of Maya Innovations
  • He also commented that its mobile subsidiary, Smart, is still looking for a replacement for former COO Anastacio Martirez, who recently resigned last month after being linked to employee complaints on an alleged toxic workplace environment
  • PLDT will hold its 3Q25 briefing next week, Nov 11 (Tues)—register here
    • PLDT in 2Q saw core income rise 7% to P9bn with service revenue up 3% YoY to P52.9bn
MACRO
Inflation holds steady at 1.7% in October
  • Headline inflation remained unchanged at 1.7% in Oct relative to the previous month of Sep
    • This is in line with the consensus forecast of 1.8% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.4-2.2% for Oct
    • Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past eight months
  • Inflation held steady in Oct as price upticks in fish and utilities (electricity, gas, and fuel) were balanced by slower increases in meat and vegetable prices
    • Food overall was up 0.5% YoY, slower than the 1.0% pace in Oct
    • Notably, rice prices are on their sixteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024
      • According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P42.6/kg and P42.9/kg as of Oct vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan, respectively
    • Electricity, gas, and fuel saw an acceleration to 2.5% YoY from the 0.9% increase the previous month likely driven by the recent depreciation in the peso
      • The peso depreciated to P59 per US dollar by end-Oct from P58.15 in the beginning of the month
  • Core inflation inched down to 2.5% in Oct from 2.6% in Sep, core non-food inflation likewise went down to 2.3% from 2.4% the previous month
  • This brings the YT-Oct headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year
  • For 2025, the BSP expects inflation to be 1.7%
    • Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8% in 2025
  • The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11
    • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.5%
