Carl (carl.sy@regis.ph) gives us a recap of the latest quarter in the gaming sector, which has seen its share of ups and downs. After growing 23% QoQ in 2Q25, GGR of bingo and e-games fell 28% QoQ in 3Q25, as the BSP prohibited e-wallets from redirecting to online gaming platforms (beginning August 17). Based on the September figure, we reckon that the online gaming sector could still see a further decline in GGR in 4Q25. On the other hand, 3Q25 GGR of physical casinos inched up QoQ.

CONGLOS
DMCI, Japanese partner bag P22bn subway package
  • The Department of Transportation (DOTR) awarded Contract Package (CP) 105 of the Metro Manila Subway to the joint venture of DMCI and Japan-based Nishimatsu Construction
    • Contract amounts to P21.7bn (ex-VAT)
    • CP 105 covers a 0.66-kilometer tunnel, a 242.2-meter station in Kalayaan and a 436.05-meter station in Bonifacio Global City
    • DMCI said that it may take at least 67 months to complete the construction
  • DMCI’s order book stood at P32.8bn in end-September
  • The Metro Manila Subway spans 33km and will have 17 stations from Valenzuela City to the Ninoy Aquino International Airport
    • It was planned to partially operate by 2028 but due to right-of-way delays, the DOTR delayed the completion to 2032
MACRO
Economy Secretary maintains 6-7% GDP growth target for 2026-2028
  • Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that the medium-term GDP growth target will remain at 6-7%
    • He stated that 6-7% is "doable" assuming that the gov't will address current issues
    • This is higher than former Finance Secretary Ralph Recto's recently estimated guidance of 5-6% GDP growth for 2026
  • Balisacan cited that "the economy's overall trajectory remains firm" despite short-term fluctuations and headwinds
    • He highlighted that the economy is anchored on "sustained growth, easing inflation, health labor market, manageable fiscal deficit and public debt, broadly stable currency, external position, and a robust banking system"
  • For 2025, current gov't GDP growth projections are ~4.7-4.8%, lower than the initial 5.5-6.5% target
  • 2025 GDP growth expectations for consensus and Regis are at 5.0% and 4.8%, respectively
    • 2026 GDP growth expectations are both at 5.5%

 

 

MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market stays flat in October
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 1 basis point MoM to 18.4% (in October) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 8bps MoM to 23.7%
  • Foreign activity reversed to net selling of US$100mn, from net foreign buying of US$47mn in September
  • Among the PSEi constituents, 10 saw increases in foreign ownership while 19 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • PLUS – Foreign ownership fell 1.79 percentage points MoM to 23.0%. Since trading data show that PLUS experienced net foreign buying in October, we suspect that the reported reduction in foreign ownership was an error.  
    • AC – foreign ownership declined 83bps MoM to 26.2%, as net foreign selling reached US$29.5mn, the second highest for the month.   
    • ALI – foreign ownership decreased 75bps MoM to 24.3%, as net foreign selling hit US$52.5mn, the highest for the month. 
    • CNVRG – foreign ownership climbed 53bps MoM to 15.9%, as it saw net foreign buying of US$4.1mn, the fourth highest for the month.
    • ICT – foreign ownership increased 33bps MoM to 35.4%, as it experienced the highest net foreign buying for the month (US$48.1mn).

 

 

MACRO
BOP posts US$706mn surplus in Oct; GIR settles at US$110.2bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Oct posted a surplus of US$706mn
    • Higher than the US$82mn surplus in Sep and a reversal from the $724mn deficit in Oct 2024
    • The higher surplus in Oct reflected the seasonal rise in remittances and strong earnings from BPO firms as well as softer import growth
  • This brings the YT-Oct deficit at US$4.6bn, reversing from the US$4.4bn surplus in the same period last year
    • The reversal to a deficit stems from the timing of gov’t debt issuances and the continued trade in goods gap
  • The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP)
    • The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Oct stood at US$110.2bn, up from the US$109.1bn in end-Sep
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • GIR is expected to slightly decrease from $106.3bn in 2024 to $106.0bn in 2025
