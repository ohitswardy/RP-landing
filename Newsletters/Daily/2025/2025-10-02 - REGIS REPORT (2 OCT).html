REGIS REPORT

02 October 2025

The BSP expects that inflation in September accelerated again to a range of 1.5-2.3%, from the 1.5% print for August. Consensus is smack in the middle of that range, at 1.9%. The faster pace is expected, as the base effects of high rice prices fades 3Q/4Q. This is baked into our average inflation forecasts for 2026E of 3.0%. That said, if CPI overshoots it could put future policy rate cuts at risk. On paper, the BSP’s mandate is purely inflation targeting; it does not have a growth/employment mandate like the US Fed does. This, ironically, could prompt the BSP to cut policy rates early, while headline CPI is still relatively low. The inflation figure will be released on Tuesday, 7 October, while the Monetary Board meets on Thursday, 9 October.

 

In other macro news, the government has made upward adjustments to its forecasts for national debt and for the current account deficit (CAD). At the start of the current administration, it was targeting fiscal deficit to GDP to fall from 6% to 3.5% by 2028E, and for the debt-to-GDP to fall from 65% to the low-50s. However, the fiscal deficit has stayed sticky in the 5.5-6% range, and the debt-to-GDP is now expected to stay above 60% until the end of Marcos, Jr’s term. Meanwhile, the BSP is now projecting the CAD at 3.3% for 2025E and 2.9% for 2026E, as disappointing tourism receipts outweigh a modest reduction in the trade deficit.

Ayala Land (ALI PM) said that its residential sales have been “a little bit subdued” since the flood control corruption scandal has been front and center. This confirms what we were expecting. As we wrote last week, conspicuous consumption – luxury goods, casino visits, and high-end property – could be hard hit from the fallout.

MACRO
BSP expects higher inflation in September
  • The BSP projects September 2025 inflation to settle within the range of 1.5% to 2.3%
    • Cited higher prices of rice and fish, elevated domestic fuel costs
  • Bloomberg consensus expects September inflation to tick up to 1.9% from 1.5% in August
    • For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%
    • Consensus and Regis expect full-year inflation to average 1.8%
  • The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release September CPI on October 7
  • The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively
    • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%
MACRO
BSP raises BOP/current account deficit forecasts
  • The BSP made upward adjustments to its current account deficit forecasts, largely to reflect lower tourism receipts
    • 2025/2026 current account deficits revised to US$16.4bn/US$15.5bn from US$16.3bn/US$13.6bn
    • It made modest downward revisions to 25E/26E trade deficit forecasts to US$69.6bn/US$70.2bn from US$70.5bn/US$71.9bn
    • It maintained BPO revenue and OFW remittances forecasts at US$33.5bn and US$35.5bn, respectively
    • 25E/26E Tourism receipts were downgraded by 12%/19% to US$9.4bn/US$9.7bn
  • It raised its BOP deficit targets to US$6.9bn/US$3.4bn in 2025/2026 due to higher current account deficit forecasts and restrained capital inflows

UTILITIES
Manila Water completes acquisition of Wawa bulk water project
  • Manila Water said the closing conditions of the former’s acquisition of WawaJVCo from Prime Infrastructure have been met on September 30
    • WawaJVCo is the owner of the soon-to-be 712MLD bulk water supply facility in Rizal, which has a 518MLD, 30-year take-or-pay agreement with the MWC’s East zone concession
  • Recall that MWC committed to acquire full ownership of WawaJVco for P37.8bn, to be settled in two tranches:
    • P6.3bn will be paid by Dec 15
    • The balance will be paid by Jan 30, 2026, subject to adjustments to balance sheet items

MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in Aug
  • The Aug trade deficit narrowed to US$3.5bn, contracting 19% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports alongside the growth of exports
    • This brings the 8M25 trade deficit to US$32bn, 6% narrower YoY
  • Imports for the month fell by 5% YoY to US$11.4bn, a reversal from the 2% increase in July
    • Primarily driven by the significant decrease in fuel imports by 34%
  • Exports grew by 5% YoY to US$7.1bn, the eighth consecutive month of growth and moderating from the 17% increase in July
    • Electronics exports climbed by 9% YoY to $3.9bn
      • These made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total
    • Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were flat % at US$3.2bn

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
Government debt inches down to P17.47trn in end-Aug
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 0.5% MoM to P17.47trn as of end-Aug
    • Domestic debt decreased by 0.5% MoM to P12.1trn
    • External debt fell by 1.4% MoM to P5.4trn
    • The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.2% and 30.8% being external borrowings
  • The P17.47trn figure already exceeds the P17.36-trn full-year projection for 2025
    • Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury said that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down"
    • The rising debt figure was driven by sustained borrowing needs given the 25% wider YoY fiscal deficit of P869bn as of 8M25
  • The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 1H25 stood at 63.1% of GDP up from 60.7% in 4Q24
  • The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025
    • The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028
