REGIS REPORT

10 November 2025

More detail on the disastrous GDP numbers from Friday. The 4.0% YoY figure was far below consensus (5.2%). The largest drop came from Capital Formation (-2.8% YoY) as the corruption allegations around flood control and other infrastructure projects saw public construction plunge 26% YoY. What struck us, though, was the reported sharp deceleration in Private Consumption, to 4.1% from 5.5% in 2Q25. We thought this was odd, considering lower inflation (rice, etc.) compared to 2024, as well as food handouts and subsidies. The report also claimed a 7.5% YoY contraction in tobacco and alcoholic beverages, which don’t quite jibe with results from listed spirits makers.

 

As we had noted in our previous report, the corruption fallout could drag GDP growth for another 3-5 quarters as infrastructure projects get delayed. While the government has promised shifting funds to “social spending” programs, the boost to consumption will not have the same multiplier effect.

 

The only upside potential is that the slower GDP print could prompt the BSP to cut policy interest rates early, in its last Monetary Board meeting on 11 December, with some calling for a “jumbo” rate cut of 50bps.

MACRO
3Q25 GDP grows 4.0%—behind expectations
  • GDP grew at 4.0% in 3Q, much slower than the 5.5% growth in 2Q25 and the 5.2% growth rate back in the same period last year 
    • The 4.0% GDP growth is significantly behind consensus expectations of 5.2% 
    • The print also missed the government’s 5.5–6.5% target range 
    • It was the lowest growth rate since 3Q11's +3.0% 
  • On the demand side, the improvement in net exports (+1.0% in 3Q from -0.1% in 2Q) was offset by deteriorating metrics across the expenditure accounts—private consumption (+4.1% from 5.3%), gov't consumption (+5.8% from +8.7%), and capital formation (-2.8% from +1.2%). 
    • If we exclude the pandemic YoY declines from 2020-2021, 
      • Private consumption growth at +4.1% was the lowest since 3Q10's +2.6% 
      • Private investments were negative for the first time at -2.8% since 3Q19's -0.7% 
        • The decline was likely caused by the slump in construction (-0.5%) given the corruption scandal involving infrastructure projects  
      • Gov't construction declined 26.2% in 3Q, falling further from -8.2% in 2Q and offset the double-digit growth in private construction (+14.4%) and households (+13.3%) 
      • Exports grew for the fourth straight quarter (+7.0%), while imports tempered to 2.6% from 3.5% in 2Q 
  • On the supply side, there was a broad deceleration in growth across the sectors 
    • The service sector (67% of GDP) exhibited a below 6% growth rate at 5.5% for the first time since 4Q14 (+5.7%) 
    • The agri sector slowed to 2.8% from 7.0% given harsher-than-usual rains in the quarter 
    • The industry sector slowed to 0.7% from 2.1% manufacturing decelerated to +1.2% from +2.5% 
  • Bringing 9M25 GDP growth to 5.0%, below lower end of the government's target of 5.5-6.5% and consensus and Regis expectations of 5.5% GDP growth for 2025 
    • GDP for 4Q25 needs to grow by at least 6.9% to hit the minimum full-year target 
    • Economic Secretary Arsenio Balisacan commented that current and next year macroeconomic forecasts are under review 

 

 

 

POWER
ACEN Corp: 3Q25 core EBITDA rebounds 37%
  • 9M25 profit of P4.58bn (-19%) - In line. Headline 3Q25 profit fell 44% YoY to P1.03bn while reported core profit rose 18% YoY to P820mn. Despite ongoing softness in its Philippine portfolio, the ramp-up of new renewable facilities in Australia and Vietnam helped offset the weakness. Cumulative 9M25 profit reached P4.58bn, down 19%. With repairs on its north Luzon wind facilities (217MW) nearly complete, and absent any unforeseen events, ACEN appears on track to meet both REGIS and consensus full-year estimates. Management also stated it plans to complete its SRO to 2Q26 or 3Q26.
  • Soft WESM prices still a drag for PH in 3Q but positives underway 

EBITDA from its Philippine operations fell 10% YoY in 9M25, a narrower decline from the 15% drop in 1H. Although low WESM prices continued to weigh on its legacy coal plants, stronger contributions from renewables offset the impact, with implied 3Q25 EBITDA for PH up about 3% YoY.  

Management noted several tailwinds in the coming periods that could offset soft WESM prices, including the approval of the P8.18/kwh tariff (vs. provisional tariff of P5.19/kwh) for its 139MW mid-merit contract with Meralco, as well as the resumption of operations at its north Luzon facilities. Beyond the expanded opportunities in the RES market, management is also hopeful that stricter enforcement of Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) will help unlock additional value.   

  • International EBITDA contributions up but Vietnam FIT tariff still hangs 

We estimate ACEN’s international portfolio (Australia, Vietnam, India, Indonesia) collectively grew att. EBITDA by 42% YoY, 37% QoQ to P3.8bn in 3Q25, boosted by the ramp-up of Stubbo Solar in Australia and Monsoon Wind in Vietnam.  Management expects the outstanding FIT tariff concerns in Vietnam to be resolved shortly. Given that its P&L already reflects a favorable outcome, any final decision could either result in additional cash inflows if successful, or a potential impairment of receivables if not.  

 

  

POWER
Aboitiz Power: 3Q EBITDA rebounds on hydro strength, lower WESM exposure
  • 9M25 core profit drops 15% to P23.1bn, within consensus estimates.  Aboitiz Power reported its 9M25 core income amounted to P23.1bn, down 15% YoY and appearing broadly within consensus full-year forecast of P30.6bn. Even though beneficial EBITDA jumped 12% YoY in 3Q25, derived 3Q25 profit rose a mere 3% to P10.4bn, as higher non-cash and interest charges from GNPD still weighed. 
  • Generation/RES EBITDA rebounds 12% YoY.  EBITDA for its generation/RES unit jumped 12% YoY (+16% QoQ) to P20.1bn, ending a two-quarter downtrend, boosted by higher contributions from its hydro facilities and increased contracting levels (~90% in 3Q25 vs. 87% in end-June 2025 and 70% in 2024). Management expects to increase its contracting levels further in the coming periods, noting that as much as 1.6GW in contracts could be offered under CSPs over the next 12 months or so.
  • DU volume growth accelerates to 6% in 3Q on improvements in commercial/industrial.  EBITDA from its distribution unit (DU) rose 5% YoY to P2.5bn in 3Q25 and 4% YoY to P7bn in 9M25.  Sales volumes grew at a faster pace of 6% in 3Q from 4% in 1H, thanks to new mall openings and a rebound in the steel sector.  Residential volumes also rose by 5% in 3Q25, slightly accelerating from the 2% pace in 2Q25.  The contrast between the 3Q volumes of AP’s DUs and Meralco (MER PM) is quite stark.
  • CBK financial close by year-end. Management stated that it could reach financial close for the acquisition of the 797MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan (CBK) hydro complex by year-end and expects the asset to be turned over to the winning bidder, the Thunder consortium, by Feb 2026. It also revealed that it will own a 64% stake in the consortium, which offered P36bn for the asset.

MACRO
Marcos implements flexible rice tariff starting next year
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. Signed Executive Order (EO 105), modifying the import duty on rice depending on global rice prices 
  • Starting Jan 1, 2026, Most Favored Nation (MFN) rice tariffs are to be adjusted based on global rice prices: 
    • The duty will increase by 5ppt for every 5ppt drop in the int'l rice price or decrease 5ppt for every 5ppt rise—the MFN tariff cannot fall below 15% or exceed 35% 
  • The current 15% base tariff (which has been lowered from 35% originally back in June 2024) will be maintained when imports resume in Jan, with a newly established Inter-Agency Group on Rice Tariff Adjustment to set guidelines on future tariff changes 
  • Agriculture groups commented that they were not supportive of the new EO as they argue that the tariff rate should be based on local not int'l rice prices, they also highlighted the possibility that tariff adjustments could encourage more speculative behavior and smuggling
