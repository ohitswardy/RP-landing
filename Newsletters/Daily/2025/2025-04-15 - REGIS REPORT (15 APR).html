REGIS REPORT

15 April 2025

FY2024 results season is coming to a close (today is the deadline). We had consumer names Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) and Emperador (EMI PM) reporting quite soft results for 4Q24.

 

  • SEVN showed weak topline in 4Q24, with SSSG falling 2.3% YoY due in part to the successive storms leading to work cancelations. However, there seem to be some deeper demand issues, as sales continue to be soft into 1Q25. Management believes the reasons might include a) the POGO ban, and b) the surge in online gambling (competing for discretionary spend). Despite the softness, SEVN still plans to add 500 stores this year, on top of the 4,130 that they ended 2024 with.
  • EMI, meanwhile, saw FY24 NPAT fall 27% YoY to P1.45bn. The brandy business booked a net loss of P62mn, as the company noted that consumers were down-trading to bottom-shelf products. The whiskey segment, meanwhile, was strong, with profit up 43%. The company is guiding for better brandy numbers in 2025, given widening margins. However, it is cautious about the whiskey business, given the new import tariffs imposed by the US.

CONSUMER
SEVN 4Q/FY24 Results: SSSG Weakness Persists

4Q SSSG weakness extended into 1Q. SEVN for the 4Q posted -2.3% SSSG on what should historically be a strong consumer spending quarter. The disappointing SSSG figures in 3Q24 and 4Q24 were initially thought to be a one-off from temporary worse-than-usual inclement weather. But what was surprising is that management noted that the negative SSSG seen in 4Q carried over into 1Q25, which implies there could be other factors that drove the decline.

 

Competitive pressures and POGO departures. We understand that management attributed the continued SSSG slowdown to a stronger preference for value and possible spillover effects from the POGO ban. The company noted pressure from the continued expansion of lower priced competitors [Alfamart, Dali, O!Save] and a grocery retailer that just posted good results [Puregold]. Although consumers have better capacity to spend given that inflation has eased from its highs, they may have modified their behavior to be more price sensitive and lean more towards value. The next quarters will be key in gaining more insight on any potential shifts in consumer behavior.

 

The POGO ban announced last July 2024, may have also had adverse impact ln sales. It is of note that a big portion of SEVN’s total stores is clustered in residential areas, aside from transit, offices, and schools. The higher disposable income of POGO workers may have been a big driver of sales as they opt for the proximity of a CVS over value/lower prices in a grocery store.

 

Expansion continues. Despite the disappointing 4Q performance and downbeat 1Q25 indicative guidance, SEVN will continue its additional 500 store expansion plan this year with 50-60% of new stores to be in the Visayas and Mindanao areas. VisMin will be the primary growth engine as the company seeks to improve operating leverage through maximizing utilization of its existing DC network in across the two areas. It was highlighted that stores situated in VisMin have the highest sales and saw more muted SSSG slowdown as competitors have less presence in the area vs. Luzon.

 

Strategy moving forward. In terms of initiatives to spur growth, the company notes that store expansion especially into VisMin as well as the ongoing product mix optimization through its "CVS+" strategy will be the growth drivers. This includes relying on high margin products to monetize foot traffic, pushing food service with new offerings, rolling out iced coffee machines to compete with lower priced coffee chains, and continuing with promotions.

 

 

 

CONSUMER
Emperador FY24 net income down 27% to P6.32bn

FY24 Profit down on lower revenues and continued investments, this is behind consensus expectations of P6.76bn. Full year net profit for both brandy and whisky were both down 51% and 10%, respectively. For 4Q alone, EMI's net income came in at P1.45bn which improved QoQ +43% but was down 22% YoY. Profit from brandy for the 4Q dipped into negative territory (-P62mn) on lower revenues and continued expenses, which offset the net income from whisky (P1.49bn +43% YoY) as revenues were more resilient.

 

Brandy: Demand and margin pressures continue. 4Q Brandy segment revenues were down 7% YoY despite the boost from holiday spending as it was drove sequential growth, +36% QoQ to P11.35bn. Management commented that downtrading to bottom-shelf products was persistent which likely tempered any further gains from the seasonally strong quarter. Margins were under pressure for the segment as gross margin was down -3ppts to 17.9% due to elevated input costs and peso weakness. EBITDA fell 48% to P1.01bn for the quarter on the back of higher A&P and opex.

 

Whisky: Segment resilience. Whisky revenues in 4Q rose 11% YoY, with management noting that demand in the category remained 'resilient.' Margins followed the positive topline momentum as gross and EBITDA margins for the quarter were up 2.5ppts and 6.5 ppts YoY, respectively. The segment now contributes to 71% of EMI's profit in 2024 vs. 58% the previous year.

 

Guidance. The company guided that for brandy, 2025 will be better than 2024 as it expects higher margins for the year and will continue to pursue its premiumization strategy. Whisky will continue to be resilient with gross margin to be above 40%. However, it was noted that the environment may continue to be challenging especially given the new trade policies from the US.

Vehicle sales rise 8% YoY, 3% MoM in March; Toyota sales up 14% YoY, 2% MoM

  • CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales of 40,306 units, up 7.6% YoY and 2.9% MoM
    • Brings 1Q25 vehicle sales up 6.8% YoY to 117,074 units
    • Note that CAMPI/TMA data does not include BYD
    • CAMPI is targeting sales of 500k units for 2025 (+7% YoY)
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 18,907 units in March, up by 13.9% YoY and 2.1% MoM
    • Brings 1Q25 retail sales up 11.8% YoY to 55,513 units
    • TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year; To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 19.9k units per month
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses
    • GTCAP is trading at a 59% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,279/sh and 4.1x FY25E PER

 

 

 

POWER
Department of Energy (DoE) flags delay of EERI 1,320 MW capacity
  • In a virtual briefing, Energy Assistant Secretary Mario C. Marasigan said not all units of the gas-fired power plant of Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. (EERI) are in full commercial operation
    • All three 440MW units are still under testing and commissioning, which means energy output remains inconsistent.
    • Note that EERI initially targeted to declare commercial operations last Dec 2024.
    • DoE said that the completion of Linseed Field Corp’s LNG storage Unit 1 may be pushed back to month-end, which could delay commercial operations target date of May 15
  • EERI is jointly owned by Meralco (40%), San Miguel (33%) and Aboitiz Power (27%)
  • EERI is the single largest new baseload capacity to be added to the Luzon grid this year and its timely completion is crucial to avoiding tight supply during the ongoing peak summer months
    • We have been skeptical about the timely completion of EERI
    • Peak demand in the Luzon grid stood at 14,011 MW in 2024; While headline dependable capacity in Luzon is at almost 20GW, actual supply stood at only 14,600 MW from March 31-Apr 6
