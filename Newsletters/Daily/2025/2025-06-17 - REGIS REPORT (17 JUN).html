REGIS REPORT

17 June 2025

Overseas remittances amounted to US$2.66bn in April, +4% higher YoY.  YT-April, remittances were up 3% to US$11.1bn.

+++

The terminal of ICTSI (ICT PM) in Manzanillo, Mexico saw volumes rise 16% in May, defying expectations of a slowdown following the unilateral actions of the US to renege on the North American free trade agreement/s.

The other terminals in the area did not fare as well, with overall volumes dropping 10%, exacerbated by a workers’ strike that affected some operations in the port. In any case, ICTSI appears to have grabbed market share.

+++

At its AGM held yesterday, Wilcon Depot (WLCON PM) was guiding for this year’s profit to be flattish compared to last year’s P2.5bn and for top line to be growing in the single digits (%).  Frankly, we think achieving a bottom line similar to last year will be a tall order. 1Q25 profits declined 28% and our forecast calls for profits to dip 6% for the year.  The headline 2Q numbers are likely to be poor, as they are likely to be exacerbated by base effects from the Holy Week timing this year, so one shouldn’t be too shocked to see a sharply negative SSSG for the quarter.

+++

Century Pacific (CNPF PM) extended its supply deal with Vita Coco for another five years. CNPF produces coconut water for the latter under a long-term agreement. We estimate the coconut water export business is <5% of CNPF’s revenue.

MACRO
OFW Remittances up 4.0% YoY in April
  • Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 4.0% YoY to US$2.66bn, accelerating from the 2.6% growth seen in March
    • Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth
    • Sea-based remittances growth accelerated to 3.8% YoY from 1.0% in Mar
    • In PHP terms, remittances grew by 3.8%

 

 

 

 

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s CMSA grows volumes by 16% in May; Sustains market share gains
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), rose by 16% YoY to 121k TEUs in May
    • Growth accelerated further from +14% YoY in April, as exports (46% of total) grew at a faster rate of +33% YoY in May from +29% in April
    • CMSA took market share, with volumes of ports across Manzanillo falling by 10% YoY in May
  • Moreover, CMSA implemented tariff hikes of 5% (e.g. yard, storage) to 12% (stevedoring) in May
    • These tariff increases are broadly in line with our forecast
  • Brings YT-May volumes at CMSA to 630k TEUs, up 10% YoY
    • This is trending below our forecast of 1.69mn TEUs (+16% YoY)
    • Note that our forecasts assume the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) treaty will be cancelled only by July 2026
    • Every 5% decrease (increase) in CMSA’s FY25E volumes will shave off (increase) EBITDA by 1.6% and profit by 2.4%
    • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of group EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 8.6x on Regis’ FY25E EV/EBITDA (consensus: 9x), in line with peer median 8.7x

 

 

 

CONSUMER
Wilcon expects conservative growth this year
  • Wilcon Depot Inc. (WLCON PM) management guided for conservative growth this year—eyeing a bit higher than mid-single digit top line growth and flattish to very low growth in earnings on the back of a second half recovery
  • COO Rosemarie B. Ong elaborated that the slowness of the overall market can be felt in Metro Manila but some bright spots such as the Cavite-Laguna area and other areas outside of Metro Manila are seeing better growth
  • Management noted ongoing initiatives such as store rightsizing to streamline inventory, space, and optimize expenses
  • The company targets eight new store openings to end this year with 108 stores
  • Capex budget for 2025 is P3.2bn, with P652mn spent as of the first quarter
    • This is higher than the P2.2bn capex spent last year
    • P2.2bn will be allocated for the construction of new stores and warehouses, P568mn for renovations, P327mn for store and transportation equipment, P137mn for IT infrastructure
CONSUMER
Century Pacific renews long-term partnership deal with Vita Coco
  • Century Pacific Food Inc. (CNPF PM) has renewed its supply agreement with The Vita Coco Company Inc. (COCO US), in a deal valued at around P14bn
    • The five-year contract extension will be effective from January 2026 onwards
  • The deal ensures continued production to supply Vita Coco's base coconut water requirements at CNPF's coconut manufacturing facilities in Gen. Santos City, Mindanao
  • The renewed partnership implies continuing their 2024 incremental long-term agreement which covered Vita Coco's additional volume requirements of approximately 90mn liters of coconut water over five years
    • To meet these additional requirements, Century Pacific back in Sept 2024 invested US$40mn in acquiring Coco Harvest., a coconut processing facility located in Misamis Occidental, Mindanao
  • CNPF's OEM business in 2024 posted P16.2bn (+36% YoY) in revenues, contributing 21% to the company's P75.5bn total
    • This was driven by favorable commodity prices and strong demand
