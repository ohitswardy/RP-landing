REGIS REPORT

05 March 2025

The reported CPI inflation number for Feb dropped to 2.1%, a positive surprise for the Street and likely even the Bangko Sentral (BSP). The average prediction from the Street was 2.6% while the BSP’s own predicted (wide) range was 2.2% to 3.0%.

 

From the data, the drop was a consequence of slowing food inflation as well as inflation for housing and utilities. Transport inflation also dipped into negative territory, presumably on lower oil prices. Within the CPI basket for food, we note the rice prices declined 1.6% MoM or 4.9% YoY. We expect rice prices to continue dropping in the coming weeks.

 

Softening food inflation (rice inflation in particular) is a key factor in our positive outlook on the market as well as consumer sector. Not only will it help spur monetary easing, it also means more money in consumer wallets.

Finance Secretary Ralph Recto has come out supporting the continued operations of online gambling firms after calls from the Senate President to ban them. Recto has instead proposed to tighten rules on online gaming, including the stricter implementation of age restrictions.

 

E-games delivered P49bn (US$0.8bn) in revenue to the coffers of government gaming agency PAGCOR last year.

The FY24 net profit of Megaworld (MEG PM) rose 8% to P18.7bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecast. Alliance Global (AGI PM) provided a lengthy explanation why there’s a discrepancy between its reported ownership in Emperador (EMI PM) as shown in its audited financials (reported to be 81%) and its Public Ownership filing (where it’s 79.5%).  In essence, AGI claims there was a typographical/clerical error in its financial statements since 2021.

 

EMI is a constituent of the PSEi and must have a public float of at least 20% to stay a constituent. If AGI’s stake is above 80%, EMI could be removed from the PSEi.

 

We are unsure how the Exchange will deal with this. However, if EMI is removed from the PSEi, the most likely candidate to replace it would be Philippine Seven (SEVN PM).

Data showed 4Q nationwide air passenger volumes finally exceeded pre-Covid numbers. The same data shows Cebu Air (CEB PM) continuing to gain market share.  For the full year 2024, passenger volumes are still just below 2019 numbers.

  • Among the rules he would likely to be implemented are:
    • A warning on gambling websites that “…gambling is not good for you”
    • Requiring the use of the national ID, rather than the player merely declaring that he is of legal age
  • FY24 GGR of e-games grew 170% to P155bn

CONGLOS/CONSUMER
AGI clarifies ownership discrepancy in EMI, cites human error
  • In a response to the PSE's request for Alliance Global Group Inc. (AGI PM) to explain the discrepancy between its reported ownership in Emperador Inc. (EMI PM), AGI clarified that human error was the cause for the misstatement in a disclosure
    • AGI stated that EMI's Public Ownership Reports (POR) show the exact ownership percentages to two decimal points, calculated by issued shares minus treasury shares
    • While AGI's quarterly and annual reports exhibit consolidated, rounded-off ownership percentages which reflected AGI's effective ownership, including 65.48mn shares held by its subsidiary, Emperador International Limited (EIL)—treating EMI and its subsidiaries as one unit and thus AGI counted EIL's shares in EMI as part of EMI's own treasury shares
  • Prior to AGI's 3Q21 report, there was no issues from the difference in calculation but by the release of the quarterly report, the discrepancy arose
    • Which recorded its effective ownership in EMI at 86% instead of 85%, and 81% instead of 80% in year-end 2022 — a mistake carried over in subsequent reports
    • The company attributed the error to oversight due to an unintentional mistake of a rounding error or computational error
  • AGI and EMI reaffirmed their intent to comply with PSE regulations and expressed their commitment to expanding their public float to increase share tradability
TRANSPORT
Nationwide air pax volumes rise 15% YoY in 4Q24, surpassing pre-Covid levels
  • Data from the airline regulator (Civil Aeronautics Board) showed that FY24 systemwide pax volumes rose 19% YoY to 59.9mn, a tad below pre-Covid levels
    • Domestic pax volumes rose 11% YoY to 32.1mn, exceeding pre-Covid levels by 9%
    • International pax volumes rose 11% YoY to 27.8mn, 9% below pre-Covid levels
  • Derived 4Q24 systemwide pax volumes rose 15% YoY to 15.8mn, exceeding pre-pandemic levels in a quarter for the first time
    • Industry growth in the quarter was largely led by Cebu Air (CEB PM); CEB’s domestic market share jumped to 57% in 4Q from 51% pre-Covid and 53% in 9M24
    • Domestic pax volumes grew 19% YoY to 8.5mn in 4Q24 while international pax volumes rose 10% YoY to 7.4mn

 

 

 

MACRO
Government debt inches up to P16.31tn in end-Jan
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 1.6% MoM (P261.5bn) to P16.31tn as of end-Jan
    • This was due to the net incurrence of new domestic and external debt as well as the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of November
  • Domestic debt rose by 1.4% (P153.7bn) MoM to P11.08tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P152.2bn)
  • External debt increased by 2.1% (P107.8bn) MoM to P5.23tn given net foreign loan additions (P59.3bn) and unfavorable currency movements
  • The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn (see here)
    • This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%
    • The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); By the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%
