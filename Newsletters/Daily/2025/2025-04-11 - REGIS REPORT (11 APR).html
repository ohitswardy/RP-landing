REGIS REPORT

11 April 2025

As had been expected, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) announced a 25bp reduction in its policy rate to 5.50% yesterday. With headline CPI inflation falling to 1.8% for March, the BSP had more than enough elbow room to further ease.  Recall as well that the BSP also reduced reserve requirement ratios by 2ppt effective March 28.

 

Beyond the widely expected rate cut, what’s also significant is the BSP revising its inflation forecast for this year. It now expects the “risk-adjusted” inflation for 2025 to be 2.3%, well below the 3.5% it previously forecast. This is a material change in outlook, driven not just by the lower prints so far this year (2.2% average for 1Q25) but also by expectations of softer food prices. We are tempted to add that a global recession resulting from the chaos of US trade policy could also lead to lower energy prices.

 

The BSP’s inflation forecasts are also an indicator of its leanings with regard to monetary policy. The BSP’s 2.3% estimate for FY25 is much closer to the low end of its official inflation target range (2-4%). We take this as a sign that there could be more easing moves in the coming months.

Loan growth for the month of Feb was 12.5%, slower than the previous month’s 13.5%. Meanwhile, the NPL (gross) ratio was practically unchanged at 3.58%.

Puregold (PGOLD PM) delivered FY24 results that are likely to have surprised the market positively. Revenues grew 10% to P219bn, while net profit jumped 21% to P10.4bn for the year. The results are likely to have come as a surprise given how the market reacted to 3Q figures. The 3Q results may have looked weak to the Street given a meaningful decline in margins. This time, the 4Q numbers showed a 12% rise in revenue and an outsized jump (+45%) in operating profit. We estimate same-store sales growth to be around 7.5% for Puregold stores and 13% for its membership shopping operation S&R.

CONSUMER
PGOLD 4Q/FY24 Results

 

Net profit P10.4bn (+21% YoY). This looks to be above consensus estimates of P9.7bn but broadly in-line with ours. For 4Q alone, net profit surged 77% as gross margins bounced back QoQ. PGOLD had cited aggressive matching in 3Q24, it’s unclear if there was any change in 4Q. Note 4Q margins could have also been boosted by supplier rebates. We hope to get clarity from the briefing.

 

Topline grew faster than peers. FY24 revenues were up 10% YoY to P219bn which is in-line with consensus and Regis expectations. Relative to peers, PGOLD delivered faster revenue growth compared to its most comparable peers. SM Retail's Food Group that grew 8% to P253bn and RRHI's Food Segment which was up 4.6% to P120bn. Note that PGOLD’s revenue growth for 4Q alone accelerated to 12%. The figures clearly imply that PGOLD made gains in market share.

 

SSSG for the FY24 accelerates as price reductions bear fruit. Puregold stores and S&R warehouse clubs posted strong SSSG FY24 of 4.5% and 6.4%, respectively – also higher than SM Retail and RRHI's Food Segments, which recorded more modest growth of 2.3% and 3.6%. For 4Q alone, we estimate SSSG for the Puregold branded stores was +7.5% while for S&R it was 13%. It’d be interesting to see what management says about how this was achieved.

 

Dividends. The company has declared regular dividends equating to P1.09/sh alongside special dividends of P0.72/sh with record dates of April 30 and September 2, respectively. The two dividend declarations amount to a total dividend of P1.81/sh or P5.21bn and reflects a dividend yield of 7.1%.

BANKS
Asset quality stable in Feb; loan growth slows to 12.5%

Asset quality metrics were broadly stable in February, despite the slight 0.1% MoM drop in loans. (As before, we have adjusted our numbers using loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP) to reduce “noise” from those items.) Total loans (ex-IBL and RRP) increased 12.5% YoY, slowing from the 13.5% YoY pace in January.

 

Gross NPL ratio was 3.58%, barely changed, up 0.9bps from the previous month, but 7.7bps lower than in Feb 2024. In PHP terms, NPLs were up just 0.1% MoM to P513bn (+10.1% YoY). Past Due (PD) ratio worsened somewhat, up 3.9bps MoM to 4.45% from 4.41% in January. However, this was a full 14bps better than the 4.59% clocked the previous year. Nominal PDs were up 0.7% MoM and 9.2% YoY to P638bn. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio was flat MoM at 2.17% (up 0.2bps), but down 12bps YoY. The Peso value of RLs was flat (marginally down) MoM, and up just 6.5% YoY. NPL Cover was 95.36% flat (+11bps) MoM, but nearly 5%age points lower than the 100.06% in Feb 2024.

 

By bank type, the Digibanks (DBs) saw huge improvements in asset quality, with NPL ratio at 9.64%, down 40bps MoM and down a full 18%age points from the 28% recorded in Feb 2024. More importantly, DBs’ PD ratio was at 16.92%, flat-ish MoM, but close to 20%age points better than the 36.6% rate the same time last year. Meanwhile. Thrift Banks (TBs) saw the worst moves, with PD ratios jumping 99bps MoM to 10.35%, and NPLs up 19bps MoM to 6.97%. Surprisingly, the Digibanks (DBs) saw big improvement in NPL ratio, down 40bs MoM.

 

We remain bullish on the banking sector for 2025. While benchmark interest rates are set to fall, banks could hold on to NIMs through a) sustained loan growth, b) improving loan mix in favor of higher-yielding consumer loans, and b) the cumulative 450bps in RRR cuts in October 2024 and March 2025. Keeping asset quality under control will be crucial for banks to maintain/improve their ROEs from the 2024 highs.
