REGIS REPORT

06 May 2025

CPI inflation for April came in at 1.4%, well below Street estimates (median of 1.8%). The decline in inflation was led, as we had been predicting (or hoping), by a drop in food prices (-1.7% MoM), specifically rice prices (-3.1% MoM). The easing of rice prices, admittedly, did not come as early as we expected but as the old adage (cliché) says, “Better late than never”.

 

With both the US$ and energy prices (e.g. oil) weakening, this latest print will almost certainly (1) prompt a reexamination of everyone’s estimates for this year, including estimates of the Bangko Sentral (BSP) and (2) give a further nudge for the BSP to ease monetary policy further.

 

+++

 

World crude oil prices dropped again overnight, after OPEC announced increased production despite global growth concerns. Brent, WTI, and Dubai (most relevant for the Phils) all hit lows not seen since the Covid-19 pandemic. Since the Philippines imports virtually all of its petroleum needs, and since there are no subsidies for petrol/diesel/etc, oil prices have a strong, and direct impact on inflation. Weak oil prices – and the PHP’s strength / USD weakness – should help keep inflation under control in coming months.

 

As we have recently (and repeatedly) written, this, together with announcements of rice subsidies and food stamps support our thesis of a recovery in consumer demand, particularly for lower income households. Direct beneficiaries include URC, MONDE, PGOLD, and CNPF.

 

Cebu Air (CEB PM) would also be a clear winner on both weak fuel prices, and a weak USD. Margins at GT Capital (GTCAP PM) subsidiary Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) would also be a clear beneficiary from USD weakness, while Semirara (SCC PM) will be hit by lower export prices.

Today’s results round-up features port operator ICTSI (ICT PM), hardware retailer Wilcon (WLCON PM) and property firm Robinsons Land (RLC PM).

 

  • ICTSI (ICT PM) (-) - ICT reported solid 1Q results, with recurring income jumping 28% to US$236mn, underpinned by a 12% improvement in TEU volumes and 4% increase in yield per TEU. The results are on trend to meet consensus expectations even if they are trending slightly behind our forecasts. During yesterday’s briefing, management dismissed speculations of any frontloading activity influencing its results and noted that it has not seen any glaring weakness in trade activity at their ports in 2Q so far. While uncertainties linger, management was again resolute that its operations will remain resilient amidst the trade war and even suggested that the reshuffling of supply chains out of China could benefit its ports in the medium-to long-term.

CONSUMER
WLCON 1Q25 net profit -28% YoY to P536mn—behind

1Q25 net profit -28% YoY to P536mn—behind

WLCON reported net profit of P536mn in 1Q25, falling 28% YoY. This is tracking behind expectations due to worse-than-expected margins and same store sales (SSS). Net sales rose 1% as SSS contracted 3.6%.  The silver lining here is SSS is down less than last year. The 1Q operating margin saw a 4.2 ppt drop YoY to 9.6% given higher opex from new store openings and an increase in lease liabilities interest expense.

1Q topline shows minimal improvement as demand remains tepid

Revenue was up slightly (+1% YoY) to P8.4bn in 1Q, better than the -1% YoY decline in FY24. SSSG in 1Q was -3.6%, an improvement from both the -7.3% in 1Q24 and the -7.0% in 4Q24. However, note that the timing of this year’s Holy Week (typically a slow period for sales) was likely a factor, which took place in 2Q this year vs. 1Q last year (Apr ’25 vs. Mar ’24). Transaction count turned positive, growing 1% YoY in 1Q after declining -2.8% in FY24, but average basket size continued to shrink (1Q: -4.5% YoY, FY24: -3.5% YoY).

Continued topline push and higher opex from new stores compress margins

Gross margin contracted 110 bps YoY due to the continued push of “best deals” promotions and “price refreshes.” Operating margin saw a significant YoY decline of 4.2 ppts to 9.6% given new store openings opex and temporary accounting adjustments that led to the frontloading of lease liabilities interest expense. WLCON opened 2 new stores in 1Q25 (1 Depot/1 Do-It-Wilcon) out of the 8 stores (of which 7 Depots) planned this year.

Management sees possible turnaround in 2H

Management elaborated that average daily sales was encouraging in March and after Holy Week in April compared to Jan and Feb which performed poorly. If this positive momentum continues, they are hopeful for a reversal in the net earnings decline that could be seen later in the year. However, any signs of a turnaround will only be visible in late 2Q and remains hinged on the effectiveness of strategies/promotions and market recovery.

 

 

PROPERTY
Robinsons Land’s residential segment disappoints in 1Q25; targets P25bn profit in 2030
  • Robinsons Land’s headline net profit fell 14% YoY to P3.5bn. Stripping off non-recurring gains in 1Q24 (from the dilution in GoTyme), recurring profit grew 4% to P3.5bn, broadly in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. Both the JV and standalone residential businesses saw weak revenue and presales. However, the unusually strong mall margins cushioned the impact on the bottom line.
  • Both the JV and standalone residential businesses saw weak revenue and presales. Revenue of the standalone residential business fell 16% QoQ and YoY (to P453mn) while equitized earnings from JV projects saw an even bigger drop (47% QoQ and 31% YoY). Meanwhile, presales of the standalone business fell 80% QoQ to P846mn while JV presales declined 99% QoQ and YoY to just P47mn. The weak revenue and presales figures could be due to cancellations.  
  • Mall EBIT margins jumps. Mall revenue grew 6% YoY (down 2% QoQ), as occupancy remained stable at 93%. Mall EBIT rose 11% YoY and 17% QoQ due to the substantial jump in the mall EBIT margin (+7ppt QoQ, +2ppt YoY) to 45%, an unusually high level. However, we suspect that this will decline in the coming quarters, similar to the FY24 experience.
  • Office occupancy stays flat QoQ. Office revenue grew 6% YoY thanks to the 7% expansion in floor area. On a QoQ basis, revenue remained flat, as did occupancy, at 86%. 
  • Industrial leasing to experience the fastest growth. Industrial leasing revenue climbed 40% YoY (flat QoQ) thanks to the 30% expansion in floor area. The company plans to grow this segment faster than the other businesses. It is targeting to expand floor area from 295k sqm in FY24 to 329k sqm this year (12% growth), and 635k sqm in 2030, representing 14% CAGR.
  • 2030 profit target set at P25bn. RLC announced a 2030 net profit target of P25bn, which represents 12% recurring profit CAGR from 2024. While the company intends to grow its rental portfolio, we would expect the profit target to require a substantial recovery of the residential businesses as well.   
  • RLC declared a P0.75/share cash dividend, representing 6.2% dividend yield. The stock is trading at 73% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY25E P/B, and 4.2X FY25E P/E.
  • The company will host a briefing this morning
