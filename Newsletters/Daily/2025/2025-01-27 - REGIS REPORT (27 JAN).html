REGIS REPORT

27 January 2025

The Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE PM) announced the changes to its main index, the PSEi (“PCOMP”), that will take effect at the start of business on Feb 3 (the first trading day of the month).

China Banking Corp (CBC PM) and AREIT (AREIT PM) will join the PSEi at the expense of Nickel Asia (NIKL PM) and Wilcon (WLCON PM). We expect the potential inflows into CBC and AREIT to be fairly chunky at over US$100m (combined). These PSEi inclusions and deletions are largely expected so many speculators would have already taken positions on those moves.

Given the relative size of the two new entrants (~4.4% weight, combined), the projected outflows from the other PSEi components due to will also be large and likely not quite anticipated. For example, we could see an outflow of US$14m from SM (SM PM), equivalent to more than 3x daily turnover.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) subsidiary Integrated Microelectronics (IMI PM) disclosed the closure of its Chengdu facility as part of its rationalization initiatives. We believe IMI is making a push to reduce costs and get firmly back into the black ahead of a likely divestment by AC.

+++

The Marcos administration is expending more effort to drive rice prices down, with an announced plan to lower the price ceiling of imported rice by 15% to P49/kg by next month. The Dept of Agriculture previously said it will begin selling rice through some of the country’s biggest retailers in a bid to push down prices. Retail rice prices have remained stubbornly (and some say artificially) high despite the reduction in rice import tariffs and the plunge in international export prices.

+++

The trade deficit for the full year of 2024 came in at US$54bn (approximately 12% of GDP), slightly higher (+3%) than the previous year. The drop in import prices was largely offset by the decline in exports.

Price cap on imported rice to go as low as P49 in March
  • The maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice, currently set at P58 per kilo, will be gradually reduced starting February 2025, with a potential drop to P49 by March if global prices remain stable, according to Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel
    • The MSRP will be lowered to P55 on February 5, then to P52 on February 15
    • This reduction is contingent on global prices staying within $530–$550 per metric ton for 5% broken rice
      • To note, recent prices from Asian exporters are notably lower, with Vietnam offering 5% broken rice at $413 per metric ton and 25% broken rice at $387 per metric ton
  • The gradual price reduction aims to give rice traders and importers time to adjust and to avoid destabilizing the industry
    • Japanese black rice, red rice, basmati, imported glutinous rice, and locally produced rice are exempt from the MSRP
  • The initiative is an additional measure to tackle persistently high domestic rice prices despite falling global prices alongside President Marcos' earlier rice tariff cut from 35% to 15% last June 2024
    • Recall the DA implemented the MSRP on January 20 to keep rice affordable for consumers while supporting the rice industry's profitability
  • The DA is closely monitoring compliance with the MSRP in public markets.
    • Strict enforcement will begin once official guidelines are issued in February as those caught with violations could result in penalties
MACRO
Trade deficit further narrows to $4.14bn in December
  • The December trade deficit narrowed down further to US$4.14bn, the smallest since Mar 2024, down 1.0% YoY and 14.8% MoM from $4.85bn last Nov
    • Driven by the decline in the value of imports which narrowed the deficit for the period
  • Imports for the month declined 2% YoY to US$9.79bn
    • The drop was led by fuel as it continued to decrease YoY by 3%, a six month streak of declines in fuel imports
  • Exports continue to fall at 2.2% YoY to US$5.66bn, marking the fourth consecutive month of contraction
    • Led by the decline in electronics exports which continued to drop by 17% YoY to US$2.80bn, electronics accounted for 50% of total exports during the period
  • Bringing the FY24 trade deficit to US$54.21bn, +3% YoY
    • Although wider than last year's deficit, this latest figure is lower than the largest trade gap since the US$57.65bn deficit in 2022

IMI to shut down Chengdu facility
  • Integrated Micro-Electronics, Inc. (IMI PM), the electronics manufacturing arm of the Ayala Group, is strategically closing its facility in Chengdu, China
  • This was done in an effort to streamline operations as IMI will continue serving its global client base from other manufacturing sites
CONGLOS
Tanduay eyes France, Italy, Latin America in global expansion push
  • Tanduay Distillers Inc., the alcoholic beverage arm of the LT Group (LTG PM), aims to sustain its growth trajectory by entering into both emerging and established markets
    • The company plans to introduce its products to France, Italy, Spain, Denmark, the Nordic region, and several Latin American markets, on top of its offerings in 26 countries.
  • As of late, Tanduay is the world's number one rum brand having sold 23.4mn case liters in 2023
    • Tanduay’s Bacolod-based plant produces up to 55,000 cases of rum daily, made with molasses sourced from the sugarcane farms of Negros Occidental
