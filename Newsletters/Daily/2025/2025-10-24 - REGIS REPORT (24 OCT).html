REGIS REPORT

24 October 2025

The budget deficit disclosure of the government showed a deficit of P248bn for the month of Sept, down 9%. One thing notable is the 7.5% decline in expenditures, driven by a 10% fall in primary (ex- debt service) spending. It indeed looks like the corruption scandal is taking a toll on spending, as public officials feel the glare of heightened scrutiny. 

 

+++

 

Auto industry association CAMPI reported sales volume for Sept of 38,029 units, which imply a decline of 3.8% YoY. Note the CAMPI data does not include BYD, which we reckon should have more than 5% market already. If we include BYD, it is likely that Sept unit sales would have just been flat YoY and YT-Sept sales would have been up close to 5%.

 

Sales of Toyota, the market leader, appear to have been flat YoY for the month, and +3.6% for the YT-Sept period.

 

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) is 51% owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM), while Ayala Corp (AC PM) has the distribution rights for BYD in the country.

 

+++

 

The government is drafting rules to extend the life of mineral processing permits to as long as 25 years. At the same time, it also plans to tighten enforcement of a “use it or lose it” policy on mining tenements. Soaring gold prices have led to a revival of interest in the industry over the past year.

MACRO
Government posts P248bn budget deficit in Sep; P1.17trn deficit as of 9M25
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P248bn in Sep, 9% narrower YoY on slower gov't spending
  • This brings the 9M25 deficit to P1.17trn, 15% wider YoY
    • The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "expected to remain within the P1.56trn full-year program"
    • The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues
  • Revenue collections in Sep declined by 6% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues
    • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 5% mainly due to higher revenues from corporate and personal income tax
    • Customs collections were higher by 5%, reversing from the 1% decline in Aug
    • Non-tax revenues dipped 66% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024
  • Expenditures fell 7% YoY in Sep due to the decrease in primary spending
    • Primary spending for the month was lower by 10%
      • Similar to Jul and Aug, the spending slowdown in Sep was due to the "lower spending performance of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH)"
    • Interest payments rose 11% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt
  • The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024

 

 

 

 

CONGLOS
Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 3.8% YoY in September; Toyota sales flat
  • CAMPI reported car sales fell 3.8% YoY (+5.1% MoM) to 38,029 units in September
    • Note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales
    • Brings YT-September vehicle sales to 343,410 units (-0.3% YoY)
    • Assuming BYD’s market share was at ~5%, we estimate industry car sales may have risen by ~5% YTD
  • EV sales (ex-BYD) reached 20,662 units in 9M25, accounting for 6% of total sales
    • Implies that for the month of September alone, sales of EVs stood at 2,223 units, down 1% MoM and accounting for 5.8% of overall sales
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 164,797 units in 9M25, up 3.6% YoY and lagging TMP’s and REGIS’ full-year sales targets
    • Derived TMP sales for September of 18,440 units indicate flat YoY trend, breaking the downward trend seen in the prior two months
    • To reach TMP’s sales target of 235k units (+8%), sales should average 23.4k units per month for the rest of the year
    • To reach REGIS’ sales forecast of 231k units (+6%), sales should average 22.2k units per month for the rest of the year
    • We’ve baked in narrower margins for TMP, projecting GP margins/net margins of 10.8%/2.5% in 2H25 from 17.1%/9.2% in 1H25 to reflect expectations of a weaker PHP (vs. USD) and higher promotional activities
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV (24% of gross asset value) and 35% of profit
  • GTCAP is trading at a 48% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,128/sh and 4x PER

 

 

MINING
Government drafting rules to extend mining processing permits to a fixed 25-year period
  • Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) Undersecretary Carlos Primo David said that they are drafting an executive order (EO) that will extend mineral processing permits to a fixed 25-year period
    • Currently, processing permits carry an initial validity of five (5) years and are renewable every five years, up to a maximum period of 25 years
    • Draft EO is meant to protect mining operations from abrupt policy changes including retroactive tax adjustments and new local ordinances that could restrict or halt activities
    • EO would also tighten enforcement of the “use it or lose it” policy for mining tenements, allowing the government to take back idle mineral assets and open them for redevelopment
  • Draft EO is still awaiting approval from the Executive Secretary and must also under stakeholder consultation before issuance, which the DENR is aiming to complete next year
  • DENR plans to launch the bidding of about 50 idle mining assets by November
