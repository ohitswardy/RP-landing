REGIS REPORT

22 December 2025

The implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for RA 12253 or the new Mining Act are out. The law increases taxes on the sector – the Department of Finance expects an additional P6.3bn in revenues for the government. More importantly, though, the Act streamlines the fiscal and regulatory framework around the mining industry, which has been under-invested over the last 30 years. We recently wrote a big piece (see report here) with a positive outlook on the potential to unlock billions of USD worth of investments in the space. This could help not only the fiscal deficit, but also provide new sources of foreign currency, and narrow the Current Account deficit (CAD).

Consumer loans in the banking sector grew 21.3% YoY in 3Q25, marking a slight acceleration from the 21.1% in 2Q25, and setting a new fastest quarterly YoY growth rate since early 2015. The unsecured segment – credit cards, salary loans, etc – again powered the growth, up 31.6% YoY. That said, credit cards also saw a blip up in NPL ratio, up 31bps QoQ and 47bps YoY. Interestingly, mortgages grew 10.9%, contradicting the narrative of weak residential demand. It probably suggests that the prices have come down enough that the secondary market is much more active than the primary / pre-sale market.  Auto loans were up 17.2%, which also paints a much healthier demand for cars than the industry sales data (flat, at best) suggests.

The Balance of Payments (BOP) logged a deficit of $225mn in November. However, Gross International Reserves (GIR) stood at $111bn, up about $1bn MoM, equivalent to about 7.4 months of imports.

MINING
Department of Finance issues mining IRR
  • The Department of Finance (DOF) issued the Implementing Rules and Regulations of RA 12253 or the Enhanced Fiscal Regime for Large-Scale Metallic Mining Act
  • The DOF expects P6.3bn in additional revenues annually
  • RA 12253 streamlines the taxation/royalties on mining (see below)
  • The filing and payment of royalties on locally produced or extracted metallic minerals or mineral products shall be made within 60 days after the end of the calendar quarter
  • The share of each LGU shall be released directly and immediately to the provincial, city, municipal, or barangay treasurer without the need for further action and without being subject to any lien or holdback that may be imposed by the national government
    • The DOF, Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall jointly issue the necessary rules and regulations for a streamlined disbursement scheme

 

 

BANKS
Consumer loans +21.3% in 3Q25; Credit card NPLs blip up

Consumer loans in the Philippine banking system grew 21.3% YoY in 3Q25 to P3,537bn, just slightly faster than the 21.1% in 2Q25, but still setting a new fastest YoY pace since 2015. Consumers now make up 22.7% of total loans, up from 22.3% in 2Q, to a new record high. Growth was driven by credit cards, up 29% YoY, and salary loans, up 37.3% YoY. As a group, unsecured loans (including “others”) were up 31.6% YoY, and now account for 47.5% of consumer loans. For perspective, that was just under 30% at end-2019 pre-Covid. Meanwhile, consumer loan NPL ratio stood at 5.38%, inching up from the 5.33% in 2Q, driven largely by a 31bps QoQ jump in credit card NPLs ratio.

 

  • Mortgages grew 10.9% YoY to P1,178bn, maintaining the pace set in 1Q/2Q, while NPL ratio was 6.38% improving 5bps QoQ and 40bps YoY. This runs slightly counter to the narrative of a slowing residential property sector. It does indicate that the secondary market is still active, compared to the primary / pre-sale market.
  • Motor Vehicle (MV) loans were up 17.3% YoY, slowing slightly from the 17.6% in 2Q, with autos up 17.2% and motorcycles up 20.6%. MV NPL ratio fell 9bps QoQ and 46bps YoY to 4.90%, as auto NPLs were down 8bps to 4.25% and two-wheel NPLs improved 34bps QoQ to 16.14%. The data here, too, seems inconsistent with the CAMPI car loan data that shows weak new car sales figures.
  • Credit card receivables continued to surge 29.5% YoY to P1,094bn, nearing the levels of mortgages. To the downside, credit card NPL ratio blipped 4.82% up 31bps QoQ and 47bps YoY.
  • Salary loans jumped 37.3% YoY, while NPLs improved to 4.57%, down 8bps QoQ and a massive114bps YoY.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
BOP posts US$225mn deficit in Nov; GIR settles at US$111.3bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Nov posted a deficit of US$225mn
    • A reversal from the US$706mn surplus in Oct but narrower than the $2.3bn deficit in Nov 2024
    • The narrower deficit YoY in Nov reflected the seasonal rise in remittances which offset higher imports and softer exports
  • This brings the YT-Nov deficit at US$4.8bn, reversing from the US$2.1bn surplus in the same period last year
    • The reversal to a YTD deficit stems from earlier front-loaded imports of capital goods and energy as well as the continued trade in goods gap
  • The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP)
    • The BOP projection for 2025 reflects the BSP's expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Nov stood at US$111.3bn, up from the US$110.2bn in end-Oct
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • GIR is expected to slightly decrease from $106.3bn in 2024 to $106.0bn in 2025
