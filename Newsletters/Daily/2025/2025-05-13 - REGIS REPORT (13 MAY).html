Well, that was a surprise. The Senatorial election results (80% of the vote counted so far) point to a sharp rebuke of the administration of President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr, whose candidates took just 5 out of the 12 seats up for grabs. In contrast, allies of former President Duterte also took 5 seats, including two that bolted from the Marcos camp (Camille Villar, and BBM’s sister Imee Marcos). The other two seats went to Paolo “Bam” Aquino (old time rivals of the Marcos family), and Francis Pangilinan, both of whom were outside the “Magic 12” in nearly every pre-election survey.

 

Last month, we wrote that President Marcos’s popularity cratered in March (link here), likely due to the arrest of the elder Duterte, but also due to disappointment over inflation and wages. With the midterms results, it looks highly unlikely that the impeachment trial against VP Sara Duterte will progress. (It wouldn’t be a surprise if the Supreme Court is “convinced” to rule it unconstitutional, to save face.) It would also seem that any move to amend the Constitution is dead in the water.

 

A more fractured and fractious Senate could mean more significant horse-trading to get any new legislation through. The Marcos, Jr administration will likely need to push even more populist policies beyond the ones recently announced?

Today’s results round-up is dominated by misses. Results from Globe (GLO PM), Jollibee (JFC PM) and Cebu Air (CEB PM) all appear to be trending well below Street and/or Regis estimates, for various reasons.  Meanwhile, the results of Okada Manila and City of Dreams Manila were so far below the industry average that they may point to a positive preview of Bloomberry’s (BLOOM PM) own 1Q25.

 

Have a look at our brief summaries.

 

  • Globe (GLO PM) (-) - 1Q25 results were surprisingly weak, with core income down 22% to P4.5bn as group-wide service revenues slid 3%. The weakness was visible across the major segments: mobile, broadband and corporate data.  Mynt’s (now 34% owned by Globe) profits offered some redemption, with profits there almost doubling to P5.1bn. Note Mynt’s profits in 1Q were higher than Globe’s. More below. The company will hold its briefing later today.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (-) – Revenue grew 15% to P70bn, with systemwide sales (SWS) expanding 19% in 1Q.  While operating income was up a robust 18%, headline income to shareholder actually dropped 8% to P2.4bn. The domestic business seems to be continuing its winning ways, with SSSG of +8.5%.  On the other hand, the two most problematic operations, China and Smashburger, saw 8% declines in SSS. We await more detail from its earnings call later.

 

 

 

  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (+) – Manila Water’s 1Q25 profit after adjusting for the share of participating prefs rose 14% YoY to P3.12bn, trending well ahead of Regis’ FY25E but broadly in line with consensus forecast.  Its core East zone unit saw profits rise 16% YoY, largely on the back of tariff hikes and more than offset the 2% decline in billed volumes. However, the more crucial metrics on cash opex and capex are lagging its regulatory commitments.

More below.

TELCOS
Globe Telecom 1Q25 results
  • Weak operating results all around.  Core income for 1Q dropped 22% as revenue was surprisingly soft across all operating segments, falling 3%.  Mobile revenue dropped 3% while broadband fell 5%.  Even corporate data, which had been growing better than the rest, saw a 1% drop YoY.

 

  • Mobile revenue declines 3%. Mobile service revenue fell 3% YoY in the quarter and the drop appears to have been from prepaid. The prepaid business is, by its nature, most vulnerable to  pricing changes in the market.

 

  • EBITDA down 3%.  It is a wonder that EBITDA  declined only 3% given the apparent weakness in operating revenues and the high operating leverage.  Cash opex declined 4% YoY in the quarter.

 

  • Wireline broadband net adds hit 132k. One unambiguous positive this quarter was the surprise strength in wireline broadband additions.  Globe added 132k subscribers for wireline broadband, likely to be almost entirely prepaid.

 

 

  • Mynt profit soars.  Mynt’s profit for 1Q25 rose to P5.09bn (+88% from P2.57bn) and Globe’s share of it in 1Q was P1.79bn, 39% of Globe’s core profit.  In case you missed it, Mynt’s 1Q25 income (P5.1bn) was also bigger than Globe’s.  Note Globe’s ownership of Mynt was diluted to 34% (36%) due to the entry of new investors in Mynt.

 

 

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air 1Q25 results: Strong topline negated by higher fleet costs
  • 1Q25 Profit falls 74% QoQ/YoY to P506mn. Headline profit fell 79% to P466mn. Adjusting for one-offs and FX adjustments, we estimate core profit dropped 74% QoQ/YoY to P506mn. The results are trending well below Regis’ FY25E but looks on track to meet consensus forecast of P6.3bn. We attribute the miss to (a) lower-than-expected fares, as well as (b) larger-than-expected maintenance, airport costs, and financing costs.
  • Revenue up a strong 20%; Sequential fare improvements encouraging. Revenues rose 20% to a new record level of P30.4bn, underpinned by a 26% increase in pax volumes. Average fares slipped 6% YoY to P3,045, which management blamed on base effects due to the timing of Easter break, pushing the seasonal peak to 2Q25. It is worth noting that fares have been creeping up sequentially since the low in 3Q24, alongside sequential improvements in seat load factors (see Fig 3 in page 2), indicating improving take-up of new routes introduced last September.
  • EBITDA flat while EBIT drops 26% YoY. Despite the drop in jet kero fuel prices and favorable PHP:USD exchange rate, operating costs surged 26% YoY due to higher fixed costs related to fleet maintenance and airport costs.  While airport costs have been rising since 4Q24, provisions for maintenance of leased aircraft surprisingly jumped 34% QoQ, which management reckons should not recur in succeeding periods. Given that opex rose more than revenues, EBITDA came in flat at P6.65bn and EBIT dropped 26% YoY to P2.0bn. 
  • Management lowers seat growth guidance to 15-20% on global supply issues. CEB continues to suffer from global supply chain challenges, resulting in persistent delays in Airbus’ aircraft delivery schedules, as well as in the engine maintenance schedules of Pratt & Whitney. Aircraft on ground (AOG) is currently at 13, higher than management’s initial expectations of 7-9 for the year. These issues have prompted management to lower its seat growth guidance to 15-20% from 20-25% previously. Note that our forecasts assume 9% seat growth.

 

GAMING
City of Dreams Manila’s 1Q25 GGR falls 28% QoQ
  • We estimate the gross gaming revenue (GGR) of City of Dreams Manila fell 28% QoQ and 2% YoY to P6.3bn in 1Q25
    • On a QoQ basis, VIP rolling chip and mass market table drop both fell
    • The QoQ decline was further exacerbated by a reduction in both the VIP and mass market table hold rates
  • PAGCOR reported that 1Q25 GGR of licensed casinos fell 5% QoQ and 1% YoY to P49.3bn
    • From the reported figures of Okada Manila and City of Dreams Manila, we estimate that the GGR of other licensed casinos (in aggregate) grew 3% QoQ and 2% YoY
    • We forecast BLOOM’s Philippine GGR to stay flat in FY25E (at P61bn); we project SEC’s GGR to fall 23% YoY to P40.9bn

 

Note: Melco Resorts (200 HK) discloses City of Dreams Manila’s figures in US$, we derive the peso figures

 

 

POLITICS
2025 Midterm Elections: Duterte camp outperforms while Marcos allies disappoints
  • As of May 13 (8:00 am), the partial and unofficial results from the Commission on Elections (COMELEC) transparency surveys have transmitted 80% of the votes thus far
  • The 2025 midterm elections have been heavily divisive given the feud between President Marcos and Vice President Sara Duterte as the outcome of the elections will dictate how the impeachment trial may go in July
    • Assuming current trends continue, five of the 12 available Senate seats are likely to be secured by Marcos allies and another five by Duterte allies, while Bam Aquino and Kiko Pangilinan are poised to claim two
      • Marcos allies which were seen to secure around more than half of the 12 available senate seats in previous surveys, have disappointed with previously projected frontrunners out of the top 12 (Tulfo [Ben], Revilla, Binay)
      • While for the Duterte camp, the results have surprised expectations relative to the pre-election surveys where only 2-3 Duterte allies were expected to win vs. the current five set to win
        • The two being Go and Dela Rosa, then the third expected winner was Camille Villar who recently sided with VP Sara Duterte
  • Considering the top 12 winners and the 12 current senators who will continue their term, the projected senate slate exhibits nine administration allies, seven Duterte allies, and eight independent candidates
    • At least 16 votes (two thirds majority) are needed to impeach VP Sara Duterte
  • Note that those independent are not explicitly aligned with Marcos or Duterte; while those with administration affiliation have been supportive of Marcos through being part of the majority bloc/forged alliances/is part of the admin slate
