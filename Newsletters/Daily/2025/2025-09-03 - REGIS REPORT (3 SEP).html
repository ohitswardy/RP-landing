03 September 2025

The widening scandal over “flood control projects” threatens to bring broader economic repercussion, even if temporarily.

 

The new head (Secretary) of the public works department (DPWH) has already asked all executives in the department to resign (though not all resignations will be accepted). Many other projects with the DPWH, even those not at the center of the scandal, are at risk of being stalled as the wheels of the bureaucracy grind to a halt.

 

One has to appreciate that the DPWH is responsible for a rather large chunk (18%) of the national government budget this year (and 13% of the proposed 2026 budget), so the impact of a slowdown could be significant. Those who have longer memories might recall the slowdown in growth as a consequence of anti-corruption efforts following the assumption of Benigno Aquino III as President (in June 2010).

 

 

Beyond just the first order impact, we might also see repercussions in other types of spending if this scandal does not subside and an honest crackdown follows. Watchers of other markets (e.g. China) would know that a possible slowdown in spending on luxury or sin products / services is a familiar unintended consequence.

 

+++

 

Metro Pacific is reportedly considering a bid for the water businesses of Prime Water Infrastructure Corp.  Prime Water is a holding firm of the Villar family, who control Vista Land (VLL PM), All Home (HOME PM), Golden MV (HVN PM), VREIT (VREIT PM), PREIT (PREIT PM), All Day (ALLDY PM) among others.

 

Prime Water operates or holds joint ventures that run the water distribution in many areas covering millions of consumers. It has been subject of criticism recently because of poor service.

 

We reckon Manila Water (MWC PM) is a natural buyer for many of Prime Water’s assets and the same can be said for Maynilad. We would not be surprised to see competition to arise for at least some of Prime Water’s assets. Note that Metro Pacific is the controlling shareholder of Maynilad, which itself is planning to launch an IPO next month.

MACRO
DPWH faces probe and revamp following corruption scandal
  • Newly appointed Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vince Dizon has asked for the resignations of all executives at the department in what appears to be a sweeping revamp
    • The call follows an explosive corruption scandal over a multitude of projects meant to address perennial flooding in many areas
  • Dizon noted that the flood control projects also need to be “corrected” and re-bid as soon as possible given that "the people [especially in flood-prone communities] need the projects"
  • Allocations for such projects rose from under P50 billion a year in 2010 to over P350 billion by 2025, amounting to a total of P1.4 trillion over the past 15 years
    • In the proposed 2026 budget, P275bn is being allotted for flood control
  • A number of contractors, even those not involved in the ongoing investigation into flagged flood control projects, were said to have stopped or partially halted their operations and progress in gov't contracts awarded by the DPWH
    • Some contractors noted that receivables from the DPWH have been steadily accumulating and are unsure on when they will be paid by the gov't
    • Certain banks have also been said to pause on new loan releases connected to flood control projects until the situation stabilizes
  • Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto commented that "maybe if the money had been spent better, we could have grown by 6%"
    • The country has failed to hit its growth targets in the recent years
      • 5.7% in 2024 (vs. 6.0-6.5% target)
      • 5.6% in 2023 (vs. 6.0-7.0% target)
  • Recto noted that the anomalous flood control projects by the DPWH have cost the economy an estimated P42bn-P118bn from 2023 to 2025 or around 95,000-266,000 jobs
  • 1H25 infra spend amounted to P720bn, flat YoY and broadly in line with the 1H government’s target (here)
    • Government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP)
