REGIS REPORT

08 September 2025

Banking sector Past Due (PD) ratios spiked in July, up 10bps MoM to 4.60% from 4.50% in June, marking the highest level since October 2024. While the NPL ratio increased a very modest 2bps MoM to 3.58%, the PD ratio is typically a more reliable measure of real-time levels of distress. The NPL numbers could also have been flattered by an 11bp MoM increase in restructured loans (RLs) equivalent to about P17.6bn. It is not clear what drove the jumps in PD and RL ratios. The PD data was probably due to the continued surge in unsecured loans, particularly credit cards, which are still up 29% YoY. The RL data is more likely commercial/corporate accounts, given the sheer size.

 

To the upside, loan growth continues to be robust, with system-wide loans still up 12.1% YoY in July, the same pace set in June. While larger universal/commercial bank (UKB) loans were up 11.8%, thrift banks (TBs) saw loans jump 26.5% YoY while Digibank loans surged 99.3% YoY.

 

The data points to likely continued rise in credit costs heading into 3Q25. The important question is this – are the asset quality data pointing simply to the change in loan mix? That is, given the higher loan yields for unsecured loans, is the risk-adjusted return still worth the increased risks?

The pick-up in reported CPI inflation for Aug (1.5% vs 0.9% in July), combined with Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Remolona’s earlier statements about interest rates being in the “sweet spot” is likely to cast some doubt on whether the BSP will cut rates again by year-end. The Street had been anticipating another cut in part due to BSP signaling, but confidence for that cut within the year is likely on the wane.

 

There is one more CPI inflation announcement before the next Monetary Board meeting on Oct 9 and that data point could set the tone for the immediate direction of policy rates.

BANKS
Asset quality deteriorates in July - PDs up 10bps MoM

Banking sector asset quality ratios worsened across the board in July. (Note that we use loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP) which tend to distort the monthly numbers.) The ratios were hurt slightly by the mild 0.3% MoM increase in total loans (es-IBL/RRP). That said, all bad loan data was up even more MoM in nominal terms.

 

Most notably, Past Due (PD) ratios blipped up 10bps MoM to 4.60% from 4.50% in June, marking the highest level since October 2024 (4.66%). In PHP terms, PD loans jumped 2.5% MoM to P687.6bn. NPL ratio was up a much milder 2bps MoM to 3.58% from 3.56% the previous month, with nominal NPLs up 1.0% MoM to P535.4bn. The drop in NPL ratio could partly be attributed to a jump in Restructured Loans (RLs) ratio, up 11bps MoM to 2.20% from 2.09% in June. The PHP value of RLs surged 5.6% MoM to P329.6bn, or a P17.6bn increase over the June level.

 

Notable, too, was that the MoM asset quality slip was driven by the larger Universal and Commercial banks (UKBs). While Thrift Banks (TBs) saw slightly higher MoM increases, the Digibanks actually saw a sharp decline in PD and NPL ratios.

 

The data do not bode well for credit costs heading into 3Q25. Recall that provision expense for all banks we cover – and for many that we don’t – surprised to the upside in 2Q25

 

On a positive note, loan growth stayed elevated, up 12.1% YoY (ex-IBL/RRP), sustaining the pace from June. This is despite the MoM drop, as July historically tends to be a soft month.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS
Commercial banks’ loan growth 11.8% in July; M3 growth at 6.2%
  • Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.8% YoY in July, moderating from the 12.1% rate in June
    • Loans to productive activities increased 10.8% YoY, slowing down from the 11.1% in June due to decreased lending in key industries such as
      • Manufacturing (-6.3% from -4.0%), trade (+8.5% from +9.7%), and information, communication (+8.5% from +9.6%)
    • Consumer loans grew 23.6%, slightly slower than the 24.0% pace in June
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up to 6.2% in July, accelerating from the 5.9% revised growth in June

 

 

 

MACRO
Inflation rate accelerates to 1.5% in August
  • Headline inflation picked up to 1.5% in August from 0.9% in the previous month of July
    • This is higher than the consensus forecast of 1.2% but is within the BSP's forecast range of 1.0-1.8% for August
    • Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past six months
  • Primary driver for the faster pace in inflation in Aug was food up 0.9% YoY, reversing from being down 0.2% YoY in July
    • Food was driven by sharp YoY price increases in vegetables and fish which were up 10% and 9.5%, respectively (vs -4.7% and 6.3% in July)
    • Notably, rice prices are on their fourteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024
      • According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Sep 4), prices for local and imported well-milled rice range from P37-52/kg and P40-50/kg, respectively vs. P42-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 2
  • Core inflation accelerated to 2.7% in August from 2.3% in Jul, driven by the surge in core food inflation to 3.9% from 2.8%; while core non-food inflation inched up to 2.1% from 2.0%
  • This brings the YT-Jun headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year
  • For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%
    • Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8%
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (5th out of 6 this year) will be on Oct 9
    • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%

 

 

 

 

 

PROPERTY
President Marcos signs law allowing foreigners to lease land up to 99 years
  • President Marcos signed a law that allows foreigners to lease land for up to 99 years
    • The maximum of 99 years is in line with Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia
    • Currently, the Foreign Investors’ Long Term Lease Act allows foreigners to lease land for up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years (total of 75 years)
  • Foreigners will be allowed to sublease properties unless their contracts prohibit them from doing so
  • The lease contract can be terminated if the foreign investor fails to commence the project within three years of signing
  • The law allows long-term land lease for “the establishment of industrial estates, factories, assembly or processing plants, agro-industrial enterprises, land development for industrial or commercial use, tourism, agriculture, agro-forestry, ecological conservation and other similar priority productive endeavors.”
UTILITIES
Manila Water divests entire stake in Eastern Water
  • Manila Water disclosed that it sold its entire 18.72% stake in Thai-based Eastern Water (EASTW TB)
    • Stake sold to Mr. Bhavin Kunanderekwong for a total consideration of THB623mn (~P1.1bn)
    • Divestment effective price of THB0.37/sh is a large discount to EASTW’s current price of THB2.46/sh
  • EASTW is one of MWC’s legacy assets (initial investment was THB5.3bn in 2018 or P8.8bn)
    • MWC wrote down its investment to zero in 2024, after a major regulatory setback removed the company’s exclusive rights to supply water to Thailand’s eastern region
    • We thus anticipate MWC will book a paper gain from this sale

MACRO
Government debt inches up to P17.56trn in end-July; exceeds year-end debt level projection
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 1.7% MoM to P17.56trn as of end-July
    • Domestic debt rose by 1.3% MoM to P12.11trn
    • External debt increased by 2.6% MoM to P5.46trn
    • The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.9% and 31.1% being external borrowings
  • The P17.56trn figure already exceeds the P17.36-trn full-year projection for 2025 by 1.15%
    • Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury said that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down"
    • The rising debt figure was driven by sustained borrowing needs given the 22% wider YoY fiscal deficit of P784bn as of 7M25
  • The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 1H25 stood at 63.1% of GDP up from 60.7% in 4Q24
  • The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025
    • The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028

 

 

 

MACRO
PH Dollar reserves at $105.9bn end-August
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Aug increased 0.5% MoM to US$105.9bn from US$105.4bn at end-Jul
    • The increase primarily reflected the surge in global gold prices and income from BSP investments
    • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.4x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$104bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn
