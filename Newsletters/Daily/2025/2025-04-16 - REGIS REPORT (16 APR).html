REGIS REPORT

16 April 2025
  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) saw passenger volume growth of 19% YoY in March, slower than the 30% in Jan/Feb, though this was due more to the timing of the Easter break (March 2024 vs April this year). Despite the “weak” March, passenger growth for 1Q25 was still a punchy +26% YoY. This also suggests that the April 2025 figure will likely bounce hard, benefiting from the relatively low 2024 base.

 

  • Okada Manila’s 1Q25 GGR fell 13% QoQ and 12% YoY to P7.8bn, the lowest level since 1Q22. The drop was led by a particularly sharp decline in VIP GGR, down 31% YoY. The POGO ban as well as the weak tourist arrivals from China and Korea likely contributed to the decline. The figures do not bode well for Bloomberry’s (BLOOM) Solaire property in Entertainment City. Thankfully, its Solaire North casino in Quezon City seems to be doing quite well, driven more by domestic clients.

 

+ + +

 

The Philippine government seems confident that the economy can grow north of 6% in 2025, though they do admit that the top end of the 6-8% range is rather ambitious. Like us, they cited slower inflation and election spending as potential drivers for domestic demand. Our forecast of 6.0% for 2025 GDP growth is a shade above consensus of 5.9%.

 

Supporting the higher-growth narrative would be that tax collections were up strongly +13% in 1Q25, according to the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). Notably, the BIR said that the surge was due to VAT collections, which would again be indicative of improved consumption spending.

 

Remittances, too, posted steady growth of 2.7% YoY to P2.72bn in February. Given the PHP depreciation, the growth in Peso terms was stronger at +6.9% YoY by our estimate.

 

We remain Overweight on the Consumer sector.

 

+ + +

 

The arrest and extradition of former President Rodrigo Duterte seems to have sparked a wave of sympathy for him. This is being reflected in the surveys leading up to the mid-term elections on 12 May.

 

  • On the Senate race, a recent PulseAsia survey showed Duterte allies gaining ground, with incumbent Senator Bong Go leading the pack, and former police chief Senator Bato dela Rosa climbing the ranks. Two other Duterte-linked candidates – actor Philip Salvador and Congressman Rodante Marcoleta are hanging around the bottom of the “Magic 12”.
  • Two members of the coalition ticket under President Bongbong Marcos have sought the endorsement of Vice President Sara Duterte, whose popularity is also recovering after her father’s arrest. Most ironically, one of them is Senator Imee Marcos, the sister of the President. The other is Congressman Camille Villar, daughter of businessman Manny Villar, and whose mother (Cynthia) and brother (Mark) are sitting Senators.

 

On paper, the Marcos coalition is still poised to take a super-majority in the Senate. However, political alliances in the Philippines are tenuous at best. Should the Duterte “brand” keep rising, we could see some interesting shifts post-election.

NEDA confident on at least 6% GDP growth for 2025
  • Secretary Arsenio Balisacan of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) stated that the upper end of the 6-8% gov't GDP growth target range may be unlikely especially given heightened global uncertainties
    • But he also noted that the Marcos administration is still "confident" on hitting the lower-end of the target band of 6%
  • Balisacan stated that the boost from the midterm elections will be positive for growth but may not be strong enough to offset the drag from significant uncertainties
  • The NEDA Secretary stated that he would like to see 6% growth in the first quarter, and he added that it is not too far-fetched considering easing inflation and support from domestic consumption
    • 1Q24 GDP growth was 5.9%
    • 1Q25 GDP data will be released on May 8
  • Consensus expects GDP growth to be 5.7% for 1Q25 and 5.9% for the full-year
    • We expect GDP growth to be 6.0% for the year
MACRO
OFW Remittances up 2.7% YoY in February
  • Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.7% YoY to US$2.72bn, decelerating from the 2.9% growth seen in Jan
    • Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth
    • Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 1.2% YoY from 0.9% in Jan
    • In PHP terms, remittances grew by 6.9%, slower relative to the previous three months given 4Q's seasonal strength

 

 

 

 

MACRO
BIR collections rise 13% YoY to P669bn in 1Q
  • The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) saw a 13% YoY increase in tax collections to P669bn for 1Q25, higher than the P592bn in 1Q24
    • This was a combination of higher net income taxes, excise, VAT, percentage taxes of the regional offices, and the large taxpayer service
  • BIR Commissioner Romeo Lumagui is confident on continuing the good collection performance in the following months
  • For 2025, the BIR has a full-year tax goal of P3.23trn; the 1Q tax collection of P669bn is ~20.6% of the full-year target
POLITICS
Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences sees 8 from the Marcos-admin slate
  • Based on the latest Pulse Asia survey, 16 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 seats in the upcoming May 12, 2025 midterm elections
    • Re-electionist Bong Go leads the race with a voter preference of 61.9%
    • He is followed by ACT-CIS Rep. Erwin Tulfo (51.1%) and Sen. Ronald “Bato” dela Rosa (48.7%), ranking 2nd to 3rd
    • Former Senate President Vicente Sotto III is in 4th with 44.2%
    • Sen. Pia Cayetano (37.5%), Sen. Bong Revilla (36.9%), former Sen. Panfilo Lacson (36.0%), Willie Revillame (35.7%), Ben Tulfo (35.4%), and Makati Mayor Abby Binay (35.3%) are in the 5th to 12th range
    • Sen. Lito Lapid (33.3%) follows at 6th to 13th, while former Sen. Manny Pacquiao (32.0%) places 8th to 16th
    • Actor Phillip Salvador (30.0%), Rep. Camille Villar (29.0%), former Sen. Bam Aquino (28.6%), and Rep. Rodante Marcoleta (28.3%) round out the group with rankings between 11th and 18th
  • Six of the probable 16 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go, Dela Rosa, Salvador, Marcoleta are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo, and Revillame are independent
    • Notably, Salvador and Marcoleta now have a probable chance of winning after trailing in the previous surveys

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air pax volume growth decelerates to 19% in March on timing of Easter break; 1Q25 pax volumes rise 26%
  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) reported its pax volumes stood at 2.2mn in March, up 18.9% YoY, as seat capacity grew 19.2% YoY
    • Note that pax volume growth in March of 19% sharply moderated from the 30% pace in 2M25 due to high base effects from the timing of Easter break, which fell in March last year
    • Domestic pax volumes rose +20% YoY while international pax volumes grew 15% in March
    • Seat load factors dipped 20bps YoY to 81.4% due to a 500bps YoY drop in international load factors to 74.3%; Domestic load factors inched up 170bps YoY to 84.1%
  • Brings 1Q25 pax volumes to 6.95mn, up 26% YoY and trending ahead of our FY25 pax volume forecast of 27.7mn (+13% YoY)

 

 

 

 

GAMING
Okada Manila GGR falls in 1Q25
  • Okada Manila’s 1Q25 GGR fell 13% QoQ and 12% YoY to P7.8bn, the lowest level since 1Q22
    • The decline was led by VIP, which saw a 44% QoQ and 31% YoY decline to P1.8bn, the lowest level since 2Q21
    • Gaming machine GGR also fell 8% QoQ and 7% YoY to P3.0bn, the lowest level since 2Q22
    • GGR from gaming machines and mass online also fell on a QoQ and YoY basis
  • Volumes from VIP, gaming machines, and mass online also fell on a YoY and QoQ basis
  • The POGO ban likely contributed to the decline in volumes
  • Furthermore, 1Q25 tourist arrivals from the two largest foreign gaming markets, China and Korea, fell 34% and 14% respectively, to 73k and 395k
