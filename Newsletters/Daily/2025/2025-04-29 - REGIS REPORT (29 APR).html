REGIS REPORT

29 April 2025

1Q profits for Meralco (MER PM) rose 9% to P10.5bn, with core income up 11% to P11.2bn, in line with Street expectations.  While distribution volumes were relatively soft (up only +2%), better contributions from the generation side continued to support earnings expansion. 

 

Management provided an update on the pending tariff review, and it looks like the tariff review for 5RP is unlikely to be completed by June. This should not surprise anyone anymore even if some may hold out hope that the regulator can actually issue a decision on schedule (already revised many times).

BDO (BDO PM) held its 1Q25 briefing yesterday, shedding more light on the seemingly weak results relative to consensus. On the loan growth slowdown, the bank noted that corporate loans decelerated to 8.7% from 12.6% in 4Q, as companies held off pulling the trigger due to uncertainties around the Trump tariffs, and how it might affect local/global growth. Particular attention was focused on opex, which popped 16% YoY, causing much of the PPOP drag. Management explained that they had front-loaded some expenses – business taxes, IT costs, etc – that should moderate in coming quarters. BDO is confident that they can deliver 15% ROE for FY2025E, despite the 13.8% showing in 1Q25. That said, the market may have been rattled after BDO management talked about an 18% ROE target at the FY24 briefing in February. We are holding on to our forecasts for now, and maintain our Buy call on the stock, which is trading on 1.3x P/bk on our FY2025E estimates.

We published a new report on Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM). While we lowered our EBITDA forecasts by c. 30% and our target price by 58% (to P4.40/share), we maintained our Buy rating, as we consider the sell-off to be excessive. BLOOM has fallen 66% YoY, underperforming the PCOMP by 60%. While we forecast the company’s first casino, Solaire Entertainment City, to see a decline in gross gaming revenue, we expect substantial growth for its second site, Solaire North, as well as its online business.

POWER
Meralco 1Q25 profit rises 11% YoY
  • Core profit up 11% to P11.2bn in 1Q25. Meralco reported 1Q25 net income of P10.5bn (+9% YoY) and core income of P11.2bn (+11% YoY). The results appear to be broadly in line with consensus forecasts. The sustained strength in the generation segment helped drive overall profitability.

 

  • Distribution sales vol up 2%.  Distribution volume growth saw a marked deceleration, rising by just 2% YoY in 1Q25 due partly to high base effects from last year’s warmer temperatures, as well as the adverse impact of POGO closures. Despite the slowdown, management is sticking with its full-year sales growth target of 4.5%, citing the recent uptick in demand this summer and expectations of a rebound in 2H. 

 

  • 5RP review will likely extend beyond July.  Public hearings were conducted last April 4 on Meralco’s 5RP application but management reckons that delays in the ERC’s procurement of consultants could prolong the review beyond July 1.  Recall that Meralco has applied for a ~25% increase in its tariff (P1.69/kwh vs P1.35/kwh) premised on a regulatory return (WACC) of 14.6% and a chunky P246bn (US$4.2bn) capex program over four years.  The regulatory return recently approved for the transmission operator was 11.3%.

 

  • Generation profits up 25% YoY to P3.4bn. The generation business grew 1Q25 profits by 25% YoY to P3.4bn, boosted by (a) better plant availability across all its fossil fuel-based plants, as well as (b) lower fuel costs and higher contracted prices at Pacific Light. “Chromite”, its newly-acquired LNG unit, contributed P660mn before financing expenses, which includes a little more than a month’s worth of profits from the two operating units of Excellent Energy (EERI). Once all three units of EERI are completed by end-May, management expects Chromite to add P5-6bn to MER’s bottomline after considering financing costs.

 

  • FY25 profit/dividend guidance. Management is guiding for FY25 profits to grow by ~10-12% this year, which could take full year profits to at least P50bn.  It plans to declare 50% of profits as dividend, with the first tranche in 3Q, and a “lookback” payout of 10% by early next year. Assuming it does achieve core income of ~P50bn, this would translate to over 4% dividend yield.

 

 

BANKS
BDO 1Q25 looks soft, mgt confident of 15% ROE; div raised

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported 1Q25 NPAT of P19.7bn, up 6% YoY, slightly behind REGIS and consensus (around P90bn for FY25). On the face of it, the results look on the soft side, with PPOP up just 1% YoY, and with loans slowing to 12% YoY. However, management seemed confident that revenues would pick up over the remainder of the year, and that opex growth would moderate (+17% in 1Q) as the bank front-loaded some cost items. The figures are not fully comparable to our forecasts on a line by line basis, as they are not yet updated to conform to the bank’s revised accounting presentation. Management expressed confidence that the bank could sustain its 15% ROE target for the full year, despite the 13.8% turnout in 1Q.

 

Some highlights:

  • Loan growth slowed to 11.7%, from 13.2% in 4Q24. This was driven by a sharp deceleration in corporate loans to just +8.7% YoY (vs 12.6% in 4Q), due to corporate clients holding off on new loans with the uncertainties around Trump tariffs. To the upside, consumer loan growth sped up to +17.3% (from 16.1%), with the segment now accounting for 25% of total loans (from 24% in 4Q). Deposit growth also slowed to 5.9%, as the bank took advantage of the RRR cuts to reduce reliance on high-cost TDs.
  • NIMs were flat QoQ at 4.31% by the company’s estimate. (Note again that the NIMs were distorted by the accounting change, which are difficult to back-check.) Asset yields were up 3bps QoQ, matched by a similar uptick in funding costs. NII was up just 6% YoY and were actually down 1% QoQ.
  • Fee incomes were strong, up 28% YoY, driven primarily by volume growth according to management. This offset weak trading/fx gains.
  • Opex growth surprised +16%, with manpower costs up 17% YoY. Cost-to-income ratio jumped to 60% from 56% in FY24. Management explained that they had front-loaded some business taxes and other expenses (IT, eg), and expected these to moderate sharply in coming quarters.
  • PPOP was flat, up just 1% YoY, due to the above-mentioned front-loading of expenses.
  • Provision expense dropped 11% YoY as asset quality was stable/improving. NPL ratio dopped to 1.77% (from 1.83% in 4Q), and NPL cover was at 143% (from 145%). Credit cost for the quarter was 37bps by our estimate. Management is guiding for c40bps of full-year credit costs.
  • ROE was 13.8% for the quarter, by management estimate. Management reiterated that, with improving revenues, and with moderating opex, they are maintaining their target of 15% ROE for the full-year.
  • CET-1 (solo) improved to 14.0% from 13.7% in 4Q24, partly due to the slowdown in loan growth, and high ROEs. The bank increased its dividend to P1.10 per quarter, up from P1.00/quarter. This would take FY25 to P4.30/sh (1Q25 already paid at old P1.00/sh rate), for a dividend yield of 2.8%

 

There could be some market weakness given the soft 1Q25 result. Most notably, the 13.8% ROE could be disappointing to some investors that expected ROEs to rise to the bank’s recently-announced “aspirational” goal of 18%.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
P20/kg rice program expands to additional LGUs
  • The Department of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Fransico P. Tiu Laurel Jr. announced that the P20/kg rice will be sold at KADIWA centers (DA backed stores) and participating LGUs starting May 2
    • This comes after the program was initially launched in Visayas but has now expanded to select locations such as San Juan City, San Jose del Monte in Bulacan, Camarines Sur, and Mati City in Davao Oriental
  • Sale of P20 rice at KADIWA centers will only be available to vulnerable consumers (indigents, senior citizens, solo parents, PWDs); while LGUs can make the cheaper rice available for all regardless of vulnerability status
  • Based on the current program budget of P4.5bn, the program is expected to last until Dec 2025
    • The P4.5bn project cost will be shouldered by the contingency fund of the Office of the President
  • The DA Secretary noted that a full-scale subsidy for all Filipinos is not feasible, but he is finalizing a recommendation to the President for the nationwide implementation of the P20 rice to be available to 50% of the population
