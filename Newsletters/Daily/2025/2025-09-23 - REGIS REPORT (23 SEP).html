REGIS REPORT

23 September 2025

The Lower House is aiming to submit by end-Sept its amendments to the FY26 national budget previously submitted by the Marcos administration. Allocations for the public works department (“DPWH”) are expected to be cut in favor of greater allocations to the national health insurance agency Philhealth, the Dept of Education, the Dept of Agriculture, and the Dept of Health.

 

As always, amendments made at the House committee level are subject to change until the budget is really “finalized” at the bicameral committee.

 

+++

 

The Secretary of the Dept of Information and Communication Technology (DICT) Henry Aguda says there may be as many as three foreign operators who may come into the telecom market following the passage of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA). We expect that it is more likely that new operators will enter the broadband market rather than the mobile market.

 

The DICT is aiming to release the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the law by early Oct. The IRR could give better clarity on how the law will change the competitive landscape for telecom services.

 

+++

 

Meralco (MER PM) is evaluating three possible Chinese contractors for its 1,200MW Atimonan coal-fired power plants. The Atimonan project is being revived by Meralco after being mothballed. This power generation project is likely to be used by Meralco to submit a bid to supply the needs of its own utility business.

 

+++

 

The inflation watchers at the Bangko Sentral (BSP) are of the view that inflation risks are tilted towards the downside. Assuming they are correct, another policy rate reduction could come soon (possibly as early as two weeks from now).

 

Short-term rates have already been responding to the BSP’s rate decisions even as longer-term yields stay at just around 6%. Another cut would be welcomed not just by the corporate sector and the financial markets, but also by the government’s financial stewards (i.e. Bureau of Treasury).

MACRO
House to finalize budget amendments; reallocates flood control budget
  • House Appropriations Committee Chairperson Rep. Mikaela Suansing stated that the House will finish plenary deliberations on the budget prior to passing the budget bill on second readings before the end of September
    • The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than this year's P6.33trn budget
  • The P255bn allocation for flood control projects in 2026 was said to have been reallocated to the health and education sectors as well as other social programs
  • The reallocation has led to the increase in funding for certain departments/programs:
    • Dept. of Education (DepEd) +3% (P27bn) to P955bn
    • Dept. of Health (DOH) +3% (P20bn) to P948bn
    • Dept. of Agriculture +30% (P41bn) to P176bn
    • Dept. of Social Welfare and Development's (DSWD) Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations (AICS) program budget increase 84% (P27bn) to P59bn
    • The gov't subsidy for PhilHealth was tweaked to P60bn
  • Recall that infrastructure spend for 2026 was initially projected to be P1.56trn (+3% YoY, 5.1% of GDP)
    • Accounting for the P255bn cut in spending for flood control projects, infra spend would amount to P1.3trn (-13% YoY, 4.3% of GDP)
    • For 2025, projected infra spend is P1.51tn (-3% YoY, 5.3% of GDP)
TELCOS
DICT says 3 foreign telcos may enter Philippine market
  • Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) Secretary Henry Aguda stated that three foreign firms as well as several local firms have expressed interest to participate in the telco industry after the Konektadong Pinoy Act recently lapsed into law
    • Aguda added that "more than a dozen" local companies seek to participate in the industry, with more expected to enter after the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) are completed
  • The Konektadong Pinoy Act has lapsed into law in Aug with the IRR targeted to be completed by the first week of Oct
    • Aguda noted ongoing consultations with other gov't agencies, telco companies, and internet service providers (ISPs)
    • He set Oct 2 as the self-imposed deadline to complete the IRR

MACRO
Inflation risks tilted to the downside—BSP
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees risks to inflation to be weighted towards the downside ahead of the next Monetary Board meeting on Oct 9 (5th out of 6 this year)
  • Director of BSP's Monetary Policy Research Group Lara Ganapin emphasized that inflation needs to stay within target for the BSP to consider easing further
    • Ganapin highlighted that "the risk will clearly be tilted to the downside" as expectations are "well-contained and well-anchored"
  • However, she notes of some risk from geopolitical, global trade policy uncertainty, and adverse weather driven supply shocks
  • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75% and inflation to average 1.8%

POLITICS
President Marcos willing to submit himself to ICI investigation
  • President Marcos Jr. said he is willing to face investigation by the Independent Commission for Infrastructure (ICI) over alleged campaign fund irregularities
  • A Philippine Center for Investigative Journalism (PCIJ) report alleged he received P20mn from contractor Rodulfo Hilot Jr. during the 2022 elections
    • Such donations are barred under the Omnibus Election Code (no contributions from government contractors)
  • Palace Press Officer Claire Castro said the Comelec will also investigate as part of its mandate
  • Marcos created the ICI in response to flood control project anomalies and pledged transparency
POLITICS
ICC prosecutors charge former President Duterte with 3 counts of crimes against humanity
  • International Criminal Court (ICC) prosecutors charged former President Rodrigo Duterte with three counts of crimes against humanity tied to at least 76 killings in the “war on drugs"
    • The charges cover murder and attempted murder during his time as Davao City mayor (2013-2016) and President (2016-2022)
  • The confirmation of the charges hearing was originally set for Sept. 23 but was postponed as Duterte’s defense requested an indefinite adjournment, citing he is “not fit to stand trial”
    • The ICC granted a "limited postponement" of the proceedings
