REGIS REPORT

05 December 2025

The statistics office reported that CPI inflation for the month of Nov was 1.5%, which is a tad lower than expectations and also a slight decline from Oct’s 1.7%. The Monetary Board is meeting next week, and we reckon it’s very likely that the MB will reduce policy rates by another 25bp then.

 

+++

 

The SEC, under new leadership, is proposing new minimum public float rules for IPOs which would generally reduce the public ownership thresholds. If approved, this would reduce hurdles for a number of planned IPOs.

 

+++

 

 

The Senate passed its version of the national budget on second reading, bringing the budget approval

 

The Senate’s latest version increases funding for Education and restores funding for Public Works (relative to the House version). Health, Social Welfare and Agriculture were trimmed. There is still a lot of room to change the budget as recent events involving the “flood control” corruption scandal have clearly demonstrated. At the end of the day, the reconciled bicameral version is what matters because that’s the one that gets sent to the President.

 

 

 

+++

 

Loans for commercial banks grew 10.2%, slightly lower than the prior month. Consumer lending, credit cards in particular, are still the fastest growing segment.

MARKET
SEC proposes tiered minimum public ownership requirement for IPOs
  • The SEC issued a draft memorandum proposing a tiering system for minimum public ownership requirement of IPOs
  • Tier 1 – market cap below P500mn – at least 33%
  • Tier 2 – market cap from P500mn to P1bn – at least 25% public float
  • Tier 3 – market cap from P1bn to P50bn – at least 20% public float
  • Tier 4 – market cap from P50bn to P150bn – at least 15% public float
  • Tier 5 – market cap above P150bn – at least 12% public float
  • After listing, companies will be required to maintain a minimum level of public ownership corresponding to their IPO tier
MACRO
Senate passes 2026 budget on 2nd reading
  • The Senate passed on second reading the proposed P6.79trn 2026 national budget
  • Some key amendments made relative to the House of Representatives' version of the budget were:
    • Increased funding for social services and a reversal of the P52.1bn reduction in the DPWH to avoid project delays
      • Dept. of Education (+P78.5bn)
      • 4Ps Program (+P11.2bn)
      • Dept. of Interior and Local Gov't (+P22.6bn)
      • Dept. of Public Works & Highways (+P52.1bn)
    • Budget cuts in certain line items and agencies
      • Unprogrammed appropriations (-P68.5bn to P174.5bn)
      • Dept. of Health., Dept. of Social Welfare, and Dept. of Agriculture budgets were reduced by ~P20-30bn each
  • Senator Gatchalian stated that more details including line by line amendments will be disclosed on Dec 6, prior to approval on third reading of the General Appropriation Bill (GAB)
  • The current schedule for remaining budget deliberations is as follows:
    • Third and final reading on Dec 9, then finalization of the bicameral report by Dec 16 and ratification of the budget by Dec 17
    • Tentative signing of the General Appropriations Act (GAA) by the President on Dec 29
  • The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than 2025's P6.33trn (+10% YoY) budget
  • Note that the budget breakdown table below is subject to change and that the "2026 Pending" budget is a rough estimation from snippets from various news sources

 

BANKS
Commercial banks’ loan growth inches down to 10.3% in Oct
  • Preliminary data showed that loans of commercial banks rose 10.3% in Oct, slightly moderating from the 10.5% pace in Sep
    • Loans to productive activities were unchanged relative to the previous month at 9.1%
    • Consumer loans still grew at a heady pace of 23.1%, though a tad slower than Sep’s 23.5% rate
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated further to 8.3% in Oct from 7.6% in Sep

 

 

 

INDUSTRIALS
D&L brought into arbitration by NTPI for claim of damages due to Batangas fire
  • D&L Industries, Inc (DNL PM) has received a Notice of Arbitration from NYK TDG Philippines Inc. (NTPI) over damage claims related to a fire on April 6 this year that happened within DNL and NTPI's adjacent properties in the First Industrial Township SEZ in Tanauan, Batangas
    • DNL denied liability as the company claims no fault or negligence on its part as the cause of the fire
    • The company also assured in the same disclosure that the arbitration has no material impact on its operations
  • The fire affected DNL's stockyard which reached the adjacent property that had more than 1,000 cars, with around 200 of them, MG-branded vehicles, (Morris Garages Motor Philippines) that were affected by the blaze
    • Apart from the warehouse stockyard, no other areas of DNL's Batangas facility were said to have been affected given its distance from the main processing and production buildings
  • In a separate news report, the provincial fire marshal stated that the stockyard included scrap with leftover chemicals that likely ignited from exposure to the hot weather at the time
