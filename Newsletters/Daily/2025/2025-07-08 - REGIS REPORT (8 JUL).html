08 July 2025

Yesterday’s statements from Malacañang about President Marcos’ views on restrictions on online gambling bolster our belief that restrictions are coming. There now appears to be a consensus that online gambling in its current form is having a deleterious impact on “society.” In response, many legislators have proposed bans or restrictions.

 

We believe the imposition of restrictions are now a matter of when and not if. Consequently, it is unrealistic to expect the online gaming industry not to decline going forward.

President Marcos will not oppose proposal to tax online gambling if it is well studied
  • Communications Undersecretary Claire Castro said that President Marcos will not oppose the proposal of the Department of Finance to tax online gambling as long as sufficient studies are made
    • He would also be open to placing restrictions on online gambling
    • “We want to limit this kind of gambling and reduce those who are addicted to it”
  • Separately, members of the Akbayan party-list filed a bill in the House of Representatives seeking to restrict online gambling 
    • Advertising would be prohibited
    • E-wallets and super apps would be banned from linking to online gambling
    • E-games operators will be required to assess a player’s source of funds and spending habits to determine whether they can afford to gamble without financial harm
    • PAGCOR would be required to set daily, weekly, and monthly betting and loss limits to promote responsible gaming practices
    • A 10% tax will be imposed on online gambling. This will fund addiction rehabilitation, education, enforcement and research
  • Online gaming firms currently pay 38% of their e-casino and e-bingo GGR to the government. They pay 24.25% of sports betting GGR to the government
  • PLUS is trading at 8.5X FY25E P/E (based on consensus forecast)

PH Dollar reserves at $105.3bn end-Jun
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Jun were relatively unchanged, up 0.1% MoM to US$105.3bn from US$105.2bn at end-May
    • The increase primarily reflected the income from BSP's investments and the foreign exchange deposits by the national gov't with the BSP

    • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.3x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments

  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$104bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn

 

 

UTILITIES
Key takeaways from MWC call on Wawa dam operator acquisition
  • Sale of water under Wawa supply contract to 3rd parties will fall under East Zone’s regulated return. Management confirmed that MWC may choose to sell any undrawn water under the 518MLD take-or-pay agreement to third parties. However, any revenue generated from the sale of the excess water will be covered by the East Zone’s regulated return framework and will be treated as a deduction to its Opening Cash Position (OCP), which is essentially its regulatory value. This means that MWC will not make money from the sale of excess/undrawn water from Wawa with regard to the 518MLD supply agreement.

 

Maynilad, the West Zone operator, is the only natural buyer for the excess water today, given that the East Zone and West Zone already have facilities for cross-border distribution.  Note that Maynilad’s payments for the cross-border purchases will be treated as an addition to its OCP. 

 

  • Existing conveyance/transmission facility can only accommodate up to 518MLD. While the dam’s capacity at full-build will be 712MLD, the existing transmission system is only built for a maximum capacity of 518MLD.  Thus, MWC and/or a third party will still need to spend to connect the dam to a water treatment plant.  

 

We reckon MWC may choose to spend for the additional conveyance system and treatment plant itself and include such spending as part of the East Zone’s capex in the next rate rebasing.  What is unclear, however, is if the regulator will allow such spending, given that the East Zone has more than enough water supply buffer today. 

 

It is more likely, in our view, that the regulator will include the spending for the transmission and water treatment plant into Maynilad’s spending, given that the latter has less water supply redundancies than MWC.

 

  • Deal will be largely debt-funded at onset.  Management plans to fund the P38bn purchase price through debt and internally generated cash.  It estimates its parent-level debt-to-equity ratio could rise to 1.65x, which is well below the limit of its covenant (up to 3x).  Management may later on choose to fund the acquisition with its treasury shares but it will find the appropriate time to do so. 

 

  • Transaction is earnings dilutive near-term.  The transmission/conveyance system’s capacity limitation currently clouds the deal’s long-term strategic value. Near-term, we believe the net impact of the deal to MWC’s bottomline will be negative, after considering the interest expenses on WawaJV’s project loans and MWC’s acquisition-related loans.

BANKS/DIGITAL BANKS
Digital payments make up 57% of PH retail transactions in 2024
  • Digital payments reached 57.4% of volume and 59.0% of value in 2024, exceeding the BSP’s targets of 52–54% and 55–57%, respectively
    • Merchant payments accounted for 66.4% of digital volume, followed by person-to-person transfers at 20.6% and business-to-business payments at 6.2%
    • QR PH merchant adoption rose by 148.7% YoY while InstaPay transactions grew by 67.8% in volume and 46.3% in value from 2023 to 2024
    • Government disbursements were 97.2% digital in both volume and value, while person-to-person and person-to-business payments also posted strong gains
  • Key initiatives underway include pilot testing of central bank digital currency and expansion of request-to-pay and direct debit services
