REGIS REPORT

20 May 2025

The vehicle sales reported by CAMPI/TMA amounted to 33,580 for the month of April, which is down 10% from a year earlier. The sales volume figure is a disappointment. There are, however, two important caveats to that figure.

 

First, the Holy Week timing effect was a factor in depressing sales during the month. Second, the fastest growing vehicle brand – BYD – is not part of CAMPI (yet).

 

YT-April, vehicle sales for CAMPI were up 2.5% to just over 150k. If one were to include BYD, we would estimate volume growth to be over 5%. This should put Toyota’s unit sales growth (+6%) for the period in proper context.

ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Mexico port saw volume growth of +14% in April, an acceleration from +8% 1Q25.

The country’s gross international reserves declined to US$105.3bn in April as the BOP deficit for the month hit US$2.6bn.

Foreign ownership levels for the stock market dipped marginally in April, down by 4bp to 19.0%. Foreign ownership for PSEi firms declined 8bp to 24%.

CONGLOS
Vehicle sales fall 10% YoY in April; Toyota sales drop 8%
  • CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales fell 10% YoY, 17% MoM to 33,580 units in April
    • Cited seasonal factors, economic conditions, and evolving consumer demands
    • Adjusting for Easter break, combined sales for the months of March and April rose 1% YoY, moderating from the 6% pace in 2M25
    • Note that CAMPI/TMA’s data does not include sales of BYD
    • Brings 4M25 industry (CAMPI) car sales up 2.5% YoY to 150,654 units; If we include BYD, we estimate industry car sales are up ~5% YoY in 4M25
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 16,414 units in April, down 8% YoY and 13% MoM
    • Brings TMP’s 4M25 industry car sales up 6% YoY to 71,927 units
    • TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year; To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 20.4k units per month
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses
  • GTCAP is trading at a 59% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,322/sh and 4.8x FY25E PER

 

 

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port volumes jump 14% YoY in April
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), grew volumes by 14% YoY to 122.5k TEUs in April
    • This is faster than the 8% YoY rate in 1Q25
    • Meanwhile, combined TEU volumes of ports across Mexico (excluding CMSA) grew by 4% YoY in April, also outpacing 1Q25 growth rate of 2%
  • Brings CMSA’s 4M25 TEU volumes to 509k TEUs, up 9% YoY and trending below our full-year forecast of 1.69mn TEUs (+16%)
    • Note that our forecasts assume the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) treaty will be cancelled only by July 2026
    • Every 5% decrease (increase) in CMSA’s FY25E volumes will shave off (increase) EBITDA by 1.6% and profit by 2.4%
    • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of group EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 8.7x Regis’ FY25E EV/EBITDA (consensus: 10x) vs. peer average of 11x

 

 

 

MACRO
BOP widens to US$2.6bn deficit in April; April GIR settles at US$106.7bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of $US2.6bn
    • Wider than both Feb 2025's US$2.0bn deficit and the US$639mn deficit in Apr 2024
    • The deficit reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits drawdowns for external debt obligations as well as its net foreign exchange operations
      • External debt obligations that matured in April includes a EUR$650mn 5-year bond
  • This brings the YT-Apr deficit at $5.5bn, much higher than the $401mn deficit for the same period last year
    • The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$4bn deficit (-0.8% of GDP)
    • The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Apr stood at US$105.3bn, down from the US$106.7bn in end-Mar
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025

 

MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in April
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 4bps MoM to 19.0% (in April) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 8bps MoM to 24.0%
  • Net foreign selling amounted to US$54mn, a reversal from net foreign buying of US$50mn in March
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 8 saw increases in foreign ownership while 22 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership fell 68bps MoM to 13.2%. Net foreign selling amounted to US$5.7mn, the fifth highest for the month.  
    • AGI – foreign ownership dropped 38bps MoM to 16.2%.  
    • ICT – foreign ownership declined 36bps MoM to 33.8%, as it saw $50.1mn of net foreign outflow, the highest in the market.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 58bps MoM to 21.2%.
    • BDO – foreign ownership increased 39bps MoM to 28.7%. Net foreign buying hit US$16.6mn, the second-highest for the month

CONSUMER
URC P10bn Malvar plant set to be operational before end of year
  • Universal Robina Corp.'s (URC PM) P10bn production plant in Malvar, Batangas is expected to be operational before the end of the year
    • Recall that the company broke ground for construction of this plant last Feb 2024
    • The plant's expansion will span 10-15 years and once completed, will total 30.7 hectares with ~3,000 workers directly and indirectly employed
    • Manufacturing lines to be installed in the plant were guided to streamline operations by reducing material resource inputs, product wastage, labor efforts, energy and water consumption
  • Lance Gokongwei in an interview stated that the company is focused on "reigniting growth" in the Philippine branded business especially as inflation continues to ease and consumer confidence is coming back
    • URC's Domestic BCF revenues saw 4% YoY growth to P20bn in 1Q25, accounting for 44% of the company's total revenues (P45bn +7% YoY) in the quarter
    • He also elaborated that the number one priority is to bring back growth to the domestic business, highlighting that they are taking some active measures on marketing, distribution, and innovation
