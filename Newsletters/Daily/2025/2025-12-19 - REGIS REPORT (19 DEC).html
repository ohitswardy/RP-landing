REGIS REPORT

19 December 2025

The bicam approved the 2026 budget, and the President is scheduled to sign it on 29 December after review. The General Appropriations Bill calls for P6.79trn (about $120bn) in spending, up 7% compared to 2025. Notably, the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) saw its budget slashed by 40%, following the massive corruption scandals over flood control projects. That money has been reallocated to “social spending” programs in healthcare, education, agriculture, etc. We hope that this move supports a recovery in consumer spending in 2026E.

The budget also has a provision for the CARS auto incentive program, which looks to us like Toyota Motor Phils (TMP) will get its P4bn tax credits in 2026.

+ + +

Speaking of Toyota, the brand sold 5.3% more cars YoY in November, bucking the industry which saw an 8.7% YoY contraction. That said, the CAMPI sector data does NOT yet include BYD, which we reckon could have brought industry numbers to flat YoY. TMP is owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM), while BYD is distributed by Ayala Corp (AC PM).

+ + +

7-11 (SEVN PM) is rapidly rolling out Visa payments in its stores, with an estimated 700 branches enrolled, up from just 11 last month. The company aims to have 1,000 connected by year-end, and 3,000 (out of >4,000) by 1Q26. SEVN also recently sorted out connectivity issues with GCash. These should see a meaningful bump to same-store-sales numbers in 2026.

+ + +

MREIT (MREIT PM) is acquiring 165,000sqm of office space Megaworld (MEG PM) in exchange for new MREIT shares. Oddly, MREIT will also receive P187.5mn in cash as part of the transaction. The MREIT shares will be priced at P16.27/sh, or a 15% premium to the 30-day VWAP.

MACRO
Bicam approves 2026 P6.79tn budget; Presidential sign off by Dec 29
  • The bicameral conference committee reconciled provisions of the P6.79tn (+7% YoY) General Appropriations Bill
    • Department of Public Works secured a budget of P530bn, 40% (~P350bn) lower than its initial proposal of P881bn and 49% YoY lower than its 2025 budget due to removal of locally funded flood control projects
    • Reduction in public works was re-allocated to education, agriculture, healthcare (including PhilHealth, which received an additional P130bn), and social spending
    • Unprogrammed funds, intended for use if excess revenues or new collections arise, now total P243bn, including increased incentives for vehicle manufacturing to P4.3bn from P333mn
    • Recall that GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) Toyota Motor Phils (TMP) still has P4.5bn worth of un-credited tax incentives under the CARS program, equivalent to ~30% of TMP’s FY26E profit
  • Bicam report is expected to be signed on Dec. 28, ratified by both chambers on Dec. 29, and signed into law by the President on the same day
MACRO
DPWH seeks charges vs Romualdez, 86 others in flood control scandal
  • The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) has recommended the filing of criminal charges against 87 people over irregularities in flood control projects
    • Recommending charges of plunder, malversation, graft, and bribery
    • List includes Leyte Rep. and former House Speaker Martin G. Romualdez and resigned Party-list Rep. Elizady S. Co
CONGLOS
Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 9% YoY in November; Toyota sales up 5%
  • CAMPI reported industry car sales fell 8.7% YoY (-6.7% MoM) to 37,352 units in November
    • CAMPI does not capture sales of BYD
    • Brings 11M25 sales to 420,776 units, down 1% YoY
    • Assuming BYD’s market share stood at 5%, we estimate vehicle sales are up ~4-5% YTD
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 20,351 units, up 5.3% YoY (flat MoM)
    • Brings TMP’s 11M25 sales up 3.9% YoY to 205,552 units and lagging our full-year forecast of 231k units (+6%)
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV and 35% of profit
  • GTCAP is trading at a 49% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,123/sh and 4x PER

PROPERTY/REITS
MREIT to acquire office assets from Megaworld
  • MREIT will acquire office assets from its sponsor, Megaworld, in a share-for-property swap
  • MREIT will issue 997mn new shares to Megaworld in exchange for 165,000sqm of offices in McKinley Hill, Fort Bonifacio and P187.5mn in cash
  • The shares will be issued at P16.27/share
    • This represents a 16% premium to the last closing price and a 15% premium to the 30-day VWAP
  • MREIT’s portfolio will grow by 34% 645,000sqm
  • MREIT’s shares outstanding will expand by 27%
  • Megaworld’s ownership in MREIT will increase from 56.6% to 65.8%
  • MEG currently offers 4.5% dividend yield and is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.1X FY26E P/E
  • MREIT currently offers 7.7% FY26E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The 10-year government bond yield is currently 6.0%
RETAIL
Visa eyes to expand to 1,000 7-11 convenience stores by year-end and to 3,000 stores by 1Q26
  • Visa Philippines said that it is now available as a payment option in 700 7-11 branches from only 11 branches in November
    • According to Country Manager Jeffrey Navarro
    • It aims to be available to 1,000 stores by year-end and to 3,000 stores by 1Q26
  • This is part of Philippine Seven’s (SEVN PM) initiative to expand non-cash payments. At the start of the year, only a handful of stores accepted credit cards
  • As of end-September, SEVN had 4,366 stores nationwide
GAMING
PAGCOR revokes provisional gaming license of PH Resorts in Cebu
  • PAGCOR revoked the provisional gaming license for the Cebu casino project of PH Resorts (PHR PM)
  • PHR previously intended to build and operate a US$300mn integrated resort project in Cebu
  • Earlier this year, China Bank (CBC PM) foreclosed the 12-hectare land parcel on which the casino was supposed to be built
  • PAGCOR also revoked the provisional gaming license for PHR’s Clark casino in 2024
