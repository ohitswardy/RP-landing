REGIS REPORT

19 September 2025

The board of Ayala Corp (AC PM) expanded its P20bn buyback program to cover not just AC’s own shares but also shares of its subsidiaries.

 

AC’s key listed subsidiaries are BPI (BPI PM), Ayala Land (ALI PM), Globe (GLO PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM). Among those named, we think AC is more likely to deploy its buyback funds for ALI and Globe.  AC has already earmarked funds for ACEN via the latter’s (“postponed”) rights issue but we shouldn’t discount AC stepping in to buy more.

 

+++

 

Digiplus (PLUS PM) is reportedly in talks to buy the City of Dreams Manila (CODM) operations held by Melco Resorts (MLCO US). Melco runs the operations of CODM through a partnership with Belle Corp (BEL PM) and BEL is controlled by SM Investments (SM PM).

 

The contemplated acquisition of CODM may be motivated not just by “diversification’ reasons but also by the possibility of lower fees to PAGCOR. Operators who run both physical and online casinos pay only 29% of gaming revenue as fees, while pure online operators pay 38%.

 

+++

 

Megaworld (MEG PM) sold 168.6m shares of subsidiary MREIT (MREIT PM), reducing the stake of the AGI (AGI PM) group to 57.7% (from 62.2%). This will create some room for share-for-asset injections into MREIT, which needs to maintain a public float of >33.3%.

 

+++

 

PLDT (TEL PM) fintech associate Maya signed a partnership with buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) credit provider Atome.

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp. expands P20bn buyback program to include listed subsidiaries
  • Ayala Corp.’s board approved the widening of coverage of the company’s share buyback program to include its purchase of shares in publicly listed subsidiaries
    • AC’s buyback program of P10bn was approved in Dec 2019 and was further expanded to P20bn in 2021
    • Since Dec 2019, we estimate AC has spent P7.9bn for its buyback program
    • AC has also spent P6.5bn since Dec 2019 buying shares of ALI, GLO, and BPI
  • AC trades at a 31% discount to its market-based NAV of P753/sh and 6.8x FY25E PER
  • ALI trades at 50% discount to NAV, 1.3X FY25E P/B, and 12.8X FY25E P/E. Ayala Land still has P4.5bn remaining in its own share buyback program, equivalent to 1% of market cap
  • BPI trades at 1.2x FY25E PBV
  • GLO trades at 5x FY25E EV/EBITDA
GAMING
DigiPlus reportedly in talks to acquire COD Manila operations
  • According to Bilyonaryo.com, DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is in talks to acquire Melco’s contract for City of Dream’s Manila  
  • Talks have reportedly been “complicated by PLUS’s share price slide…”
  • Bilyonaryo.com previously reported that Melco wanted US$1bn for its contract with Belle Corp (BEL PM) to operate COD Manila
    • The two have a revenue sharing agreement  
  • Integrated resort operators (that have spent minimum capex of US$200mn) are charged only 29.2% of online gross gaming revenue vs 38% of GGR for pure online firms
  • We estimate that if the lower fees/taxes were applied to 1H25, then the net profit of PLUS would have been c. 40% higher – c. P11.8bn vs the actual 1H25 net profit of P8.4bn
  • City of Dreams Manila generated US$58mn (c. P3.3bn) of EBITDA in 1H25
    • Melco’s share of this was US$27mn (c. P1.54bn)
  • PLUS is trading at 5.9X FY25E P/E (based on consensus forecast)
PROPERTY
Megaworld sells MREIT shares
  • Megaworld sold 168.6mn MREIT shares at P13.28/share
    • Total value of P2.24bn
    • At this price, MREIT offers 7.6% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
  • MREIT seeks shareholder approval for the issuance of 1.36bn primary shares
    • It will hold its stockholders’ meeting on September 29
  • This will likely be used for a share-for-property swap with its sponsor, Megaworld
  • MREIT is targeting to grow its leasable area to 600k sqm by end-2025 and to 1mn sqm by 2027
    • It currently has a leasable portfolio of 480k sqm
  • The share issuance will raise MREIT’s shares outstanding by 37%
  • We estimate that the sale of shares brought the AGI group’s ownership from 62.2% to 57.7% of MREIT
  • If all of the 1.36bn new shares will be issued to the AGI group, then we estimate it will need to sell another c. 150mn MREIT shares in order for the latter to meet minimum public ownership requirements
    • At the last closing price, this would be equivalent to c. P2.0bn
  • MEG is trading at 68% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY25E P/B, and 3.5X FY25E P/E
  • MREIT currently offers 7.5% FY25E dividend yield (based on consensus forecast)
    • The 10-year government bond yield is currently 5.9%
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Maya Bank and Atome sign P2.75bn loan channeling partnership
  • Maya Bank Inc. entered a P2.75-billion loan channeling partnership with Atome to expand credit access for Filipinos
  • Atome Philippines President Christian Quiros says the collaboration expands access to responsible and transparent flexible payment options for millions of Filipinos
    • Atome first launched in Singapore in 2019 then entered the Philippine market in Oct 2021, offering 'buy now, pay later' interest-free payment options through its app and the "Atome Card" which is a no fee and no minimum income required credit card
    • It has served over 4mn buy-now-pay-later customers in the Philippines and has partnered with the SM Store and other retailers
  • Maya Bank has disbursed P152bn in cumulative loans life-to-date as of 2Q25 including loan channeling to other partners
  • PLDT (TEL PM) owns 37.7% of Maya Innovations, which holds Maya Bank

 

 

MACRO
DEPDev expects holiday spending to boost GDP growth
  • The Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that faster growth in household consumption (>70% of the economy) will be the primary driver to bring GDP growth to the lower end of the 5.5%-6.5% growth target for 2025
    • Recall that 2Q25 GDP growth was 5.5%, slightly faster than 1Q's 5.4%
    • GDP growth in the 2H needs to average 5.6% in order to meet the minimum 5.5% growth target and 7.5% to hit the upper end
  • Continued benign inflation and the lagged effect of interest rate cuts were also noted to help prop up GDP growth in the 2H of this year
    • Balisacan added that the delays in the rollout of infra projects due to the ongoing probe in anomalous flood-control projects may be a potential risk
    • Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman, however, downplayed that infra spending as a growth driver will unlikely be affected by the probe
  • The gov't targets 5.5-6.5% growth for 2025 and 6.0-7.0% for 2026-2028
    • Consensus and Regis expect 2025 GDP growth to be 5.5%
      • 2026 GDP growth expectations are 5.7% and 5.8%, respectively
