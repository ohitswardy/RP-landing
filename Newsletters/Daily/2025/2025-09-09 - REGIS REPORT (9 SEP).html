REGIS REPORT

09 September 2025

A surprise leadership change shook the Senate yesterday, as Senator Vicente Sotto III replaced Senator Francis Escudero as leader of the chamber. Ten senators (out of 24) switched sides – definitely a surprise as it comes only two months after Escudero secured the top post.  Sotto was elected to the Senate presidency with the (implicit) support of 15 members, including himself.

 

What’s noteworthy about the change is that the bloc openly aligned with the Dutertes (former President Rodrigo and current Vice President Sara) has now been consigned to the minority and would thus have diminished roles. Changes in committee assignments (chairmanships) typically come with changes in the Senate and the Duterte allies will likely lose many of the committee chairmanships.

 

Having said the above, it is difficult for us to predict exactly how the changes will affect the wheels of legislation and politics, but we view this shift would make the Senate more favorable (less hostile) towards the Marcos administration. This could make passage of the Marcos priority bills more likely.

 

+++

 

The SEC approved the share-for-property swap between RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) and its sponsor, Robinsons Land (RLC PM). RCR will acquire 324,000sqm of malls from RLC is exchange for 3.83bn new RCR shares. This would expand RCR’s shares outstanding by 24% and raise RLC’s stake from 57% to 66%.  

 

We believe this transaction could be a catalyst for RLC shares. RLC’s current market cap of P72bn is only equivalent to the value of its stake in RCR, highlighting how cheap RLC shares are. However, after the share-for-property swap, the value of its stake in RCR would rise to P103bn (assuming the RCR share price stays the same), further emphasizing RLC’s attractive valuation.

 

+++

 

The Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE PM) is considering an easing of requirements for small firms listed/listing. The appointment of the new SEC chairman, Francis Lim who is himself a former CEO of the PSE, could be an impetus for positive changes that could help the domestic capital markets.

MARKET
SEC, PSE push for lighter disclosure rules, tax relief for SMEs to encourage more IPOs
  • PSE President Ramon Monzon said that the exchange is currently reviewing disclosure rules such as considering no penalties on the first and/or second instance of disclosure violations by SMEs (small to medium-sized enterprises)
    • Current penalties for non-compliance with disclosure requirements can amount to as much as P50,000 or P500,000 depending on the total assets of the listed company
  • SEC Chair Francis Ed. Lim stated his support for easing disclosure rules, especially for minor breaches, as the current "one size fits all" rules and penalties disincentivizes small companies from going public
  • To give smaller firms more time to prepare for an IPO, Monzon elaborated on a possible joint initiative with the SEC and the BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) to implement low priority audit for SMEs that have difficulties processing both accounting and tax requirements
