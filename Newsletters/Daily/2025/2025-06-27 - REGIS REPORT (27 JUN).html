REGIS REPORT

27 June 2025

The national government reported revenue collections of P433bn in May, up 13% YoY, largely thanks to non-tax revenues (which are mostly dividends from government owned and controlled corporations or GOCCs). Meanwhile, expenditures rose 4% YoY to P578bn, resulting in a deficit of P145bn.

 

YT-May, the budget deficit amounted to P524bn, +29% over the previous year. 

 

+++

 

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) discussed its revised economic forecasts in a press briefing yesterday. In the said briefing, the DBCC announced both lower GDP growth and higher budget deficit targets, due to lower revenues. It reduced the FY25 GDP growth target to 5.5%-6.5%, from 6%-8% previously, due to the weaker-than-expected 5.4% growth in 1Q25. As a result, the DBCC now expects the deficit-to-GDP ratio to hit 5.5% in 2025, from 5.3% previously. Furthermore, the DBCC reduced the GDP growth range to 6%-7% in 2026-2028 (from 6%-8% previously) and now expects the deficit-to-GDP ratio to fall at a much slower pace.

 

In the same briefing, the government proposed a 2026 budget of P6.793trn, equivalent to 22% of GDP and a 7.4% increase from 2025.

 

+++

 

The opening of the integrated resort (IR) of Suntrust Resort Holdings (SUN PM) will be delayed from 4Q25 to 3Q26. The IR is slated to add about 20% to table capacity and 15% to gaming machine capacity in PAGCOR Entertainment City, where Okada Manila and Bloomberry Resorts’ (BLOOM PM) Solaire Entertainment are located.

MACRO
Government posts P145bn budget deficit in May
  • The government recorded a budget deficit of P145.2bn in May, which is narrower by 17% YoY and a reversal from the P67bn surplus in April
    • The lower deficit was due to higher growth (+13% YoY) in revenue collections and slower expenditure growth (+4% YoY) given the election ban
  • Revenue collections in May increased 13% YoY to P433.1bn, driven by higher tax receipts from the BIR
    • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 11% YoY to P242.7bn mainly due to higher corporate income tax collections as well as increased personal income tax and VAT collections on the back of the enhancement of digital services
    • Customs collections fell 7% to P75.7bn given the impact of reduced tariffs (i.e., rice and EVs) and lower import volumes given global trade uncertainties
    • Non-tax revenues surged 41% to P110.2bn due to higher dividend remittances from Government Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs)
  • Expenditures grew a modest 4% YoY in May to P578.2bn driven by tepid growth in primary spending
    • Primary spending for the month inched up 2% YoY to P508.3bn
      • The slower primary spending growth can likely be attributed to the election ban on public spending which started late March till election day on May 12
    • Interest payments were up 14% to P70.0bn from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt
  • This brings the 5M25 deficit to P523.9bn, wider by 29% YoY
    • The 5M25 deficit appears on trend to exceed the full-year deficit target of P1.54tn due lower-than-expected tax revenue collections

 

 

MACRO
Government tempers GDP growth targets; raises fiscal deficit targets on lower projected revenues
  • The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) trimmed its economic growth target for 2025 to a range of 5.5-6.5% narrower than the previous 6-8% goal
    • Cited lower-than-expected 1Q25 GDP print of 5.4%, dampened global sentiment due to Trump's tariffs, and heightened risk from the Israel-Iran conflict
    • To meet the lower end of the GDP growth target range (5.5%), GDP growth needs to average ~5.5% for the next three quarters
    • To hit the mid-point (6.0%) and high end (6.5%) of the range, GDP growth needs to average ~6.2% and ~6.8% for the rest of the year, respectively
  • Inflation target band was also trimmed to 2-3% from 2-4%
    • This was hinged on the assumption that inflation will continue to be manageable and benign over the near term
    • Inflation has averaged 1.9% so far for the first five months of the year
  • It also raised its fiscal deficit-to-GDP targets due to lower revenue collection assumptions (see charts below)

Government proposes budget of P6.793tn for 2026
  • The government is proposing a national budget of P6.793tn for 2026, equivalent to 22% of GDP and a 7.4% increase from the 2025 GAA of P6.326tn
    • Details of the budget will be revealed once the Department of Budget and Management submits the proposal to the 20th Congress after July 28
    • National Budget prioritizes human capital development by prioritizing investments in quality education, healthcare, and workforce upskilling
MACRO
DOTr to privatize LRT-2 via PPP
  • President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. has approved the public private partnership mode of privatization for LRT-2
  • Bidding is set to start before year-end with the International Finance Corp. as the project advisor
  • Line expansions in the pipeline, rising ridership, as well as hours-long unplanned shutdowns underscore the need for PPP to improve reliability and overall quality of operations
    • Despite private operation, train fares will remain regulated by the Dept. of Transportation (DOTr), mitigating concerns over sudden fare hikes
GAMING
Suntrust integrated resort opening delayed to 3Q26
  • The opening of Suntrust’s integrated resort will be in 3Q26
    • It was previously scheduled for completion in 4Q25
  • The integrated resort will have 280 gaming tables and 1,260 gaming machines
    • These will add about 20% to table capacity and 15% to gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City
  • Suntrust will use the gaming license of Travellers under a revenue sharing agreement
