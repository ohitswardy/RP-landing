REGIS REPORT

25 September 2025

Ayala Corp (AC PM) disclosed that it signed an agreement with CP Axtra of Thailand to bring (back) Makro stores into the Philippines. AC will own 49.9% of the venture with CP Axtra (CPAXT TB).

 

CP Axtra operates both wholesale and retail stores under the Makro and Lotus brands in a number of countries across Asia. It is also a subsidiary of CP All (CPALL TB), the leading convenience store operator in Thailand.

 

The new partnership aims to open its first store next year and it is expected to be in a large format (>5,000sqm). It is worth noting that outside of the membership shopping businesses (e.g. S&R and Landers), large format stores have been struggling.  In recent years, growth in modern retail has been skewed towards small format stores.  It would be interesting to see if this venture can introduce something different and defy trends.

 

This move reflects AC’s efforts to enter (or re-enter) the retailing industry, which was made apparent with the investment in Anko Philippines earlier. AC used to have a minority stake in the local Makro business, which was a JV with the SM (SM PM) group and Dutch entity SHV Makro.

The national government reported a budget deficit of P85bn for Aug, +56% wider YoY even though primary spending for the month was 3.5% lower. Revenues were down 9% due to a fall in non-tax receipts and debt service costs jumped 20%.

 

YT-Aug, the deficit is now at P869bn, +25% wider YoY.

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) applied for online gaming licenses in South Africa. If approved, it would mark DigiPlus’ second international market, after Brazil. However, unlike the Philippines, South Africa’s online gaming regulations only allow sports betting. That’s why its online gaming market only registered US$1.6bn in GGR in 2024, lower than the Philippines’ US$2.3bn (which has risen to US$2.0bn in 1H25 alone).

DigiPlus applies for license in South Africa
  • DigiPlus Interactive applied for three licenses with the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB): National Manufacturer License, Bookmaker License, and Bookmaker Premises License
    • The process is expected to take at least six months
    • South Africa currently does not allow online casino games
  • Sports betting gross gaming revenue (GGR) in South Africa reached approximately US$1.9bn in 2024
    • Online sports betting contributed about US$1.6bn

  • South Africa’s total GGR hit US$3.3bn in 2024, a 25% jump YoY
    • The Philippine gaming industry GGR was US$6.4bn (P373bn) in 2024

MACRO
Government posts P85bn budget deficit in Aug; P869bn deficit as of 8M25
  • The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P85bn in Aug, 56% wider YoY
  • This brings the 8M25 deficit to P869bn, 25% wider YoY
    • The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "well-managed and on track with the P1.56trn full-year program"
    • The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues as customs collections continues to lag behind the full-year target
  • Revenue collections in Aug increased by a modest 3% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues and lackluster customs collections
    • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 5% YoY mainly due to higher revenues from corporate and personal income tax
    • Customs collections declined 1% as the BOC given lower global oil prices
    • Non-tax revenues dipped 68% as remittances from the treasury and other gov't offices fell
  • Expenditures inched down 1% YoY in Aug as primary spending decreased
    • Similar to July, the spending slowdown in Aug was likely driven by the delayed timing of big-ticket disbursements of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways, Dept. of Social Welfare and Development, and Dept. of National Defense
    • Primary spending for the month was lower by 3%
    • Interest payments jumped 20% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt
  • The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024
