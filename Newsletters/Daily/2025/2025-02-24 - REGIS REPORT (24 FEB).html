REGIS REPORT

24 February 2025

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) announced a reduction in bank reserve requirements effective March 28, surprising the market with its timing. The reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for commercial banks will go down by 200bp to 5.0% while the RRR for digital banks and thrift banks will be cut by 150b and 100bp, respectively.  Most of the deposits are held by commercial banks.

 

The RRR reduction, in theory, releases over P350bn (US$6bn) into the financial system which would lead to easier credit and lower rates. This, however, assumes the BSP will not be siphoning the liquidity back into its own figurative vaults.

 

If the banks keep the money with the BSP anyway, then the RRR could end being an unrequited gift to the banks. We estimate annual earnings of the banks under coverage could be enhanced ~3% by this cut.

BDO (BDO PM) issue a press release on its FY24 results ahead of its earnings call later today. Net income rose 12% to P82.0bn on loan growth of 13%.

 

NPL ratio was 1.83% with NPL cover at 145% (largely unchanged QoQ) under revised BSP guidelines. The bank noted its book value is now at P107.8/share, and ROE for the year was 15.1%.

 

 

+++

 

AREIT (AREIT PM) announced FY24 results on Friday, revealing that profits grew 49% to P7.4bn. While this appear ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, the outperformance came from accounting adjustments, which did not affect cash flow. Earnings would have been up 45% to P7.2bn if not for the adjustment, in line with expectations.

 

The company had also just disclosed a new asset injection from its controlling shareholder Ayala Land (ALI PM). AREIT said that its P21bn share-for-property swap transaction with ALI will be priced at a cap rate of around 6.5% and would raise AREIT’s DPS by 3%.

MACRO
BSP cuts reserve requirement ratio
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas will reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for commercial banks by 200 bps to 5% starting March 28
    • Reserve requirements for digital and thrift banks will also be cut by 150 bps to 2.5% and 100 bps to 0.0%, respectively
  • The cut is expected to inject at least P300bn worth of liquidity into the financial system
  • Recall that this comes after the BSP kept rates steady during its last monetary board meeting where Remolona also guided an RRR cut this year with the timing said to be within 1H25

 

BANKS
BDO FY24 net profit rises 12%, in line
  • BDO’s FY24 earnings rose 12% to P82bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts
    • 4Q24 net profit grew 10% to P21.4bn
  • FY24 net interest income rose 8%
  • FY24 non-interest income grew 8%
  • Gross customer loans grew 13% YoY (to P13.2trn), the same pace as 3Q24
    • The bank saw double-digit growth across all segments
    • This is in line with the 12.2% loan growth of the industry
  • Deposits rose 6% YoY to P3.8trn, slower than the 10% YoY growth in 3Q24
    • CASA ratio stood at 71%
  • The NPL ratio was flat QoQ at 1.83% (1.82% in 3Q24)
    • This is lower than the industry’s NPL ratio of 3.27%
  • NPL cover also was stable QoQ at 145% (144% in 3Q24)
  • The CET1 ratio was 14.1%
  • Return on common equity hit 15.1% for FY24
MACRO
Philippines removed from FATF's gray list
  • The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) removed the Philippines from its increased monitoring list for "dirty money"
    • Note that the PH was on the FATF's gray list for over three years since June 2021
  • This comes after BSP Governor Remolona attended meetings with the FATF Plenary last week
    • The FATF noted that the country has had positive progress in addressing anti-money laundering (AML), countering the financing of terrorism (CFT) and proliferation financing (CPF)
    • Recall that last July 2023, an executive order mandated all gov't offices to adopt the National AML, CTF, CPF strategy 2023-2027
    • 2027 will be the next assessment for the FATF to verify whether the Philippines has been able to sustain current measures in addressing AML/CFT
  • The exit from the gray list should result in faster and lower-cost cross-border transactions, reduce compliance barriers, as well as encourage more foreign banks/entities to do business
    • Finance Secretary Ralph Recto added that this supports the gov't's push to achieve an A-credit rating
MACRO
Tax collections rise 12% YoY in January
  • In a Facebook post, the Department of Finance said that preliminary data showed a combined 12% YoY increase in tax collections to P429.9bn for the month of January
    • Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections for January rose by 13.7% YoY to P350.6bn
    • Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P79.3bn, up 8.1% YoY
  • For 2025, the BIR and BOC are tasked to collect P4.33tn, equivalent to 15% of GDP
    • YT-November 2024 tax collections amounted to P3.55tn, up 11.5 YoY and broadly in line with the year-end target of P3.82bn (+12% YoY)
