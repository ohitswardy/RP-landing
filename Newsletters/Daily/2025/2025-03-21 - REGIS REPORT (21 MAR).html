21 March 2025

PLDT (TEL PM) is said to be launching (re-launching) Smart Money, an e-wallet service that eventually became what’s now Maya.

 

Smart Money was first launched as a money transfer service two decades ago, but it never quite caught on. It was subsequently named “Paymaya”, before suffering another renaming exercise to become “Maya”. A digital bank was also then attached to it. PLDT today has ~37% ownership in the company holding the bank and e-wallet business.

 

Maya has fallen far behind the leader in the e-wallet space, GCash/Mynt. The revival of Smart Money suggests the group wants another go at it, using a different strategy. We wonder how PLDT will resolve the possible conflict between Maya and the revived Smart Money.

Converge (CNVRG PM) held its FY24 briefing yesterday. Among what’s “new” is that the capex for FY25 could be as high as P25bn, partly because of delays in prior years’ projects (e.g. international cable systems).   More below.

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona suggested that a policy rate cut at the next Monetary Board (MB) meeting is high. We are expecting a 25bp rate cut to be announced that meeting, which will come a few days after the March CPI print is released.

 

+++

 

Ayala Corp (AC PM) is raising US$200m through a JPY-denominated loan that will be subsequently hedged to PHP. The company expects the cost of the Samurai bond to be lower than if it borrowed PHP directly.

DIGITAL FINANCE/TELCOS
PLDT's Smart Money to be launched this 2025
  • The PLDT Group (TEL PM) targets to restart the services of Smart Money in 2025 with potentially cheaper transaction fees as an alternative in the e-money market currently dominated by Ayala-led GCash, which currently has ~94mn users
    • For context, Smart Money formerly known as Smart Padala started two decades ago and was estimated to have hit 75% market share
  • Smart Communications (PLDT's wireless unit) Chief Artificial Intelligence Advisor, Dheeraj Kumar stated that they applied for an e-money issuer and remittance licenses with the BSP three months ago
    • He was optimistic on receiving approval soon as he touts the launch can be the "next big unicorn for the country"
  • Kumar guided that the app will be live by this year and would "disrupt" the market with "seamless pricing" (i.e., for cashing in and sending money) given that current players charge fees that may have been burdening users
    • This is on the back of PLDT Chairman Manuel Pangilinan's vision for "seamless frictionless payments in the Philippines that will revolutionize digital payments and digital adoption in the country"
  • The app is currently live in the Apple App Store and Google Play but is still under pilot testing by the Pangilinan group employees (Metro Pacific Investment Corp., Metro Pacific Tollways Corp., and Manila Electric Co., among others)
TELCOS
Converge FY24 results
  • Profit rises 19% to P10.8bn for FY24. Converge ICT reported profit for FY24 of P10.8bn (+19%), which appears in line with the Street.  Revenues grew a healthy 15%, led by the Enterprise segment (+22%). Residential revenues grew 14%.

 

  • G&A expenses up sharply in 4Q. Profit for 4Q alone, however, was P2.6bn -- down 11% QoQ and 5% YoY.  There was a large jump in G&A expenses (+76% or + P1.2bn). Management says it was due to a rise in the cost of managed services.

 

  • Net adds of 104k.  Converge added a net +104k of subscribers for the last quarter, roughly the same as 3Q.  The company is maintaining lead in wireline broadband sub base, with roughly half of all new subscribers.

 

  • Dividend of P0.43/share.  The company declared a dividend of P0.43/share, equivalent to ~30% of previous year’s earnings. This is CNVRG’s second dividend (after P0.18/sh in Sept 2024). While management did not rule out another dividend this year, we think it’s probably unlikely given the elevated capex this year.

 

  • FY25 guidance for revenue and capex. Management is aiming for net subscriber gains of 100k per quarter and offered guidance of revenue growth of 14-16%, . Capex for FY22 is expected to reach P20-25bn (vs P9bn in FY24), which P12bn are for special projects, including the investment in two international cable systems and two data centers (30MW capacity).

 

 

 

MACRO
BSP to cut its policy rate by 50-75 bps this year—hinged on inflation and growth
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Remolona stated the high likelihood that the Monetary Board (MB) will cut on April 10, especially if inflation beats expectations on April 4
    • Recall that Feb inflation posted at 2.1%, bringing the YTD average to 2.5%—within the BSP's target range of 2-4%
    • Consensus expects 75 bps worth of rate cuts to 5.00% for 2025
      • We expect 50 bps worth of cuts
  • Remolona guided a base case of up to 50 bps worth of rate cuts but also hinted on the possibility of up to 75 bps should the economy output turn out to be weaker than expected
    • This is as 2024 GDP disappointed at 5.6% below the 6-8% gov't target and with 2025-2026 GDP expected to settle within the lower-end of the range given elevated global macro uncertainties (i.e., commodity prices, trade outlooks, etc.)
    • 1Q25 GDP is set to be released on May 8 with consensus expecting 5.7% growth for the first quarter
    • While consensus and Regis expectations for FY25 GDP is 6.00%
  • The BSP Governor also commented that there will be negative spillover effects of the tariff war on the Philippines
    • As multiple countries raising tariffs will slow down growth and heighten inflation but he emphasized that the mere uncertainty of how much negative impact is the bigger factor in itself
    • However, he noted that the hit on the Philippine economy will only be "modest"
    • In another article, BSP MB member Romeo Bernardo cited that the country's information technology and business process management (IT-BPM) sector may be at risk should Trump push for more protectionist/onshoring policies given that 70% of its clients originate from the United States
CONGLOS
Ayala Corp. raises US$200mn Samurai debt
  • Ayala Corp. signed its first yen-denominated loan worth US$200mn (P11.5bn) with Mizuho Bank and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corp.
    • Proceeds will be used for general corporate purposes
  • As of end-2024, AC’s parent-level gross debt stood at P204bn (inclusive of P25bn prefs) while cash stood at P11.6bn
    • Parent level net debt-to equity (treating prefs as debt) stood at 1.45x
  • For 2025, AC has earmarked P31bn for capex, of which P15.9bn has been earmarked for ACEN’s capital raising plans
    • During its analyst briefing, management announced plans of issuing P10-20bn preferred shares and tapping into its P70.7bn credit line to fund its maturities and capex
  • AC trades at a 28% discount to its market-based NAV of P837/sh
