REGIS REPORT

05 February 2025

Headline CPI inflation for January came in at 2.9%, unchanged from the December figure, and just a shade higher than the 2.8% consensus estimate. This — in theory — gives the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sufficient reason to cut policy rates by 25bps at its next Monetary Board (MB) meeting next week (13 February). A rate cut would also ease the interest burden of the National Government, given that 2/3 of the national debt is in PHP. Interest payments were c14% of the total budget in 2024, up from just 9% in 2019.

 

However, given the country's twin fiscal/CA deficits, and given the Fed's recent pause, it could lead to further PHP weakness. We forecast 50bps worth of cuts for FY25E.

 

 

 

 

Rice is life! Speaking of inflation, Department of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Mr Francisco Tiu-Laurel declared a "food security emergency", allowing the government to release rice inventory from its "buffer stock" to bring down the retail price of rice. While regional rice export prices have collapsed, the domestic prices have stayed relatively sticky. The retail price in Metro Manila of imported well-milled rice ranged from PHP40-52/kg as of 01 February. In theory, this is similar to the price range in 1Q23, before the sharp spike in export prices due to El Nino. However, the import tariffs were cut last year from 35% to 15%, which suggests that domestic prices should be some 15% lower than they are currently.

MACRO
Food security emergency declared and ceiling set on imported rice
  • The Department of Agriculture (DA) will impose a P55 per kilogram maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice starting February 5.
    • The price will now be P3 lower than the level set on January 20.
    • With MSRP in place, the DA hopes to stabilize rice prices and ease inflationary pressures on basic commodities.
  • The move follows Monday’s declaration of a food security emergency that allows the government to release rice buffer stock held by the National Food Authority (NFA) to bring down prices that have remained high in markets despite a cut in tariffs on imported rice.
  • According to Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel, the MSRP could be further reduced in the coming weeks to below P50 per kilogram to reflect the lower global prices of rice and the reduced tariff.
    • “After this reduction, we plan to lower it further to P52 by mid-February and then P49 per kilo two weeks after.”
  • In the gov't plan, the NFA would release 150,000 metric tons (MT) of its rice stock at P33 per kilogram, leading to an estimated loss of P12 to P15 per kilogram, based on the agency’s average buying price in 2024. Ultimately adding up to a loss of P1.8 billion to P2.25 billion.
    • Government agencies, government-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs) and local government units (LGUs) will sell rice to the public at a suggested retail price of P25 per kilogram
    • The Agri Sec. also stated that this will speed up the release of rice stocks in their warehouses to make way for unmilled rice procurement as the harvest season starts this month.
MACRO
Government debt goes down to P16.05tn in end-December
  • The government's total debt stock declined by 0.2% MoM to P16.05tn as of end-December
    • Given the appreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of November
  • Domestic debt rose by 0.1% (P8.8bn) MoM to P10.9tn due to the impact of net borrowings
  • External debt decreased by 0.9% (P48.4bn) MoM as the peso appreciated and reduced the level of foreign denominated debt
  • The year-end figure is in-line with the P16.06 outstanding debt target (see here)
  • Relative to the gov't's debt-to-GDP target, it stood at 60.7% higher than end-2023's 60.1% and is slightly above the target debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.6%
    • Mainly due to the lower-than-expected FY24 GDP growth of 5.6%
    • Note that the gov't target level of debt for 2025 is 60.4% of GDP
    • The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); By the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%

 

TRANSPORT
Second tranche of tariff hike for Manila ports takes effect on Feb 6
  • The second tranche (+6%) of the cargo-handling tariff hike at Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) and South Harbor takes effect on Feb 6
    • This is part of the 16% tariff increase approved by the Philippine Ports Authority (first +10% tranche was implemented last Aug 6)
  • MICT is a wholly-owned unit of ICTSI (ICT PM), which we estimate accounts for 21% of EBITDA
    • We have incorporated this increase in our forecasts
  • ICT trades at 7.5x EV/EBITDA on Regis’ FY25E and 8.2x on consensus’ EBITDA (vs. peer average of 11x)

CONSUMER
7-Eleven PH eyes further expansion
  • Philippine Seven Corp. (SEVN PM), operator and exclusive licensee of the 7-Eleven convenience store chain in the country, plans to invest P5bn as it targets to open more than 400 stores this year
    • Note that 7-Eleven was able to open its 4,000th store last Oct 2024 during 7-Eleven's 40th anniversary in the country
  • The company's Taiwanese investor—Alex Lo, Chairman of Uni-President Enterprises Corp., stated that Philippine Seven would focus on taking more market share in Visayas and Mindanao as well as opening in areas where it has no presence in Luzon
    • The number of stores located in the VisMin regions are above 1,000 as of 9M24
    • He also comments that there is still plenty of space for the chain to grow given the 100mn population and seeks to utilize the company's scale and efficiently
  • SEVN's net profit as of 9M24 was 2.6bn (+14% YoY) with FY24 consensus earnings pegged at P3.95bn (+13% YoY)
