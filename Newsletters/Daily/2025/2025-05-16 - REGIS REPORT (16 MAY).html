REGIS REPORT

16 May 2025

We are nearing the tail-end of 1Q results season and today we have telcos PLDT (TEL PM) and Converge (CNVRG PM), who held their earnings briefings one after the other yesterday. We also have the initial numbers from Security (SECB PM) and GT Capital (GTCAP PM).

 

 

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (-) – We estimate core income (including Maya contributions) was down 4% to P8.6bn, on revenue growth of 2%.  Notable was the 2% drop in Mobile revenue, which suggests an industry-wide contraction for the period.  We also highlight the apparent growth in Fixed Line Voice (supposedly +14%) which is likely more an accounting issue rather a true increase in demand.

For Philippine-based investors>>PLDT - 1Q25: Mobile weakness … wireline strength?

 

  • Converge (CNVRG PM) (+) – Revenues rose 13% to P10.8bn while profits jumped 18% to P3.0bn for the quarter. The Enterprise business, 15% of revenue, remained the faster growing segment (+23%).  The company’s subscriber net adds for the period continue to lead the industry, but the competition is now close on its heels.  More below.

 

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) – 1Q25 NPAT disappointed, as a 57% YoY surge in provisions overshadowed remarkable growth in revenues (+23% YoY) and PPOP (+24%). Topline growth was even more impressive given 1Q24 numbers included a P1.4bn chunky fee from their bancassurance JV. But NPL ratio jumped to 3.1% from 2.85% in 4Q, and credit cost surged to 147bps, far higher than the 90-100bps FY25 guidance. It is unclear whether the asset quality deterioration came from the bank’s fast-growing consumer book – up 37% YoY, and now at 36% of SECB’s loans – or from the corporate/commercial book (as what hit RCBC in 1Q25). Management was mum, and said they would provide color during the earnings call on Tuesday, 20 May at 2pm.

 

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – Headline 1Q25 net income rose 29% YoY to P9.14bn. Stripping out one-off net gains at Metro Pacific, recurring profit stood at P8.7bn, up 27% YoY and trending ahead of consensus and Regis estimates. Its 51%-owned unit, Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), reported a 57% YoY increase in standalone profits to P6.33bn, on the back of (1) a +12% jump in retail sales volumes, (2) favorable FX movements, and (3) strong sales of higher-margin vehicles. Management is holding a briefing at 2:30pm.

TELCOS
Converge 1Q profits rise 18%
  • Profits up 18%, revenue up 13%.  Converge reported revenues of P10.8bn, up +13%, with net income rising 18% toP3.0bn.  Operating and financial leverage helped to magnify bottom line growth.

 

  • Revenue expansion led by Enterprise.  Residential revenue grew by a healthy 12% to P9.1bn as the company added another 138k residential subscribers.  Enterprise revenue grew faster at 23% to P1.7bn.  Enterprise is only 15% of revenue today but is gradually becoming more important.

 

  • Subscriber adds still lead industry.  The 138k net subscriber additions in 1Q25 remain better than the competition but the others appear to be catching up.  Globe had 132k wireline broadband net adds in the quarter while PLDT has 76k. 

 

 

We estimate broadband (wireline) penetration is now over 30% but we believe subscriber growth can still be maintained but ARPU will need to fall further.

 

  • Elevated capex this year; guidance maintained.  Management still expects capex to be P20-25bn this year (P9bn in FY24) after delays in the completion of a couple of large turnkey projects.  Guidance has remained unchanged at 14-16% revenue growth and 61-62% EBITDA margin.

 

 

BANKS
SECB 1Q25 - Provisions surge, wiping out strong revenue gains

Security Bank (SECB PM) booked P2.8bn in NPAT in 1Q25, tracking behind Regis (18% of FY25E P15.4bn) and consensus (21% of FY13.2bn). The result was doubly disappointing, because revenues were strong, up 23% YoY, which was impressive given the high base in 1Q24 which included P1.4bn worth of payments from their bancassurance JV. However, this was nearly wiped out by a 56% surge in provision expense, as asset quality deteriorated in 1Q25.

 

The bank will hold an earnings call on Tuesday, 20 March, at 2pm. Ahead of that, some highlights:

 

  • Loans grew 18.5% YoY, slower than the 25.9% clocked in 4Q24. This was due solely to corporate loans, which decelerated sharply to +11% YoY from 21% the previous quarter. Consumer/MSME loans still surged 37%, sustaining the pace from the previous quarter, and now make up 36% of total (from 32%), a new all-time high. Deposits grew 32%, as the bank swapped out of its wholesale funds
  • NIMs improved 5bps QoQ, by our estimate, as loan yields were up 7bps QoQ. Recall that the YoY figures are distorted by the cross-currency swap products, though the bank adjusted the accounting gradually in 2024. This is also the reason that the FX losses were so large in 124 and earlier. Loan yields were up 9bps QoQ and 49bps YoY presumably due to the improving loan mix in favor of consumer/MSMEs. Interest income on loans was up an impressive 30% YoY.
  • Fee incomes dropped 17% YoY, though the 1Q24 figure was bloated by a chunky P1.4bn progress payment at their bancassurance JV. Excluding this, we estimate that fees were up closer to 30% YoY.
  • Opex remained elevated, up 23% YoY due to continued investment in IT and manpower
  • PPOP was up 24% YoY, despite the high base in 1Q24.
  • However, provision expense surged 56% YoY, as NPL ratio jumped 25bps QoQ to 3.1%, and NPL cover dipped slightly to 79% from 81% at end-2024. Credit cost was exceptionally high at 147bps, significantly higher than the bank’s guidance of 90-100bps for FY25E. It is unclear which segment/s – corporate or consumer – drove the deterioration in asset quality. Bank management says they will ‘provide more color” on this during the earnings call next week.

 

ROE for the quarter was 7.9%. While the revenue performance was laudable, the deteriorating asset quality puts the bank’s goal of crossing 10% ROE sometime this year into some doubt.

 

More after the briefing next week. We expect much of the focus to be on the explanation / outlook for asset quality and credit costs.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

MACRO
BSP sees 2025 inflation below 3%, GDP at lower end of 6-8% target
  • The BSP published key highlights from its Monetary Board meeting held last April 10 where it cut rates by 25bp to 5.50%
  • Inflation expectations remain within target for 2025-2027
    • 2025 Inflation is projected to stay below the midpoint (3%) of the target range (2-4%) until the fourth quarter due to lower food and oil prices
    • Upside risks stem from higher transport costs, meat prices, and electricity rates but may be offset by lower rice prices
  • 2025-2027 GDP growth is seen to settle around the low end (~6%) of the gov't's growth target for 2025-2027 (6-8%)
    • Domestic demand will be bolstered by low inflation, improvements in real wages and the labor market, as well as the gradual easing of monetary policy
    • Ongoing global macro uncertainties and a challenging external environment continue to pose risks that may negatively impact domestic economic activity
  • The BSP noted that current monetary policy settings "still appear to be relatively tight."
    • The central bank also guided that the timing and magnitude of rate cuts will be decided on a meeting-by-meeting basis
  • Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be at 2.4% and projects 75bp total rate cuts this year to 5.00%
    • Regis expectations for full-year inflation are at 3.0% while our policy rate expectations are 50bp worth of cuts to 5.25%
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of 6 this year) is scheduled for June 19
MACRO
OFW Remittances up 2.6% YoY in March
  • Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.6% YoY to US$2.81bn, a slight deceleration from the 2.7% growth seen in Feb
    • Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth
    • Sea-based remittances growth slowed to 1.0% YoY from 1.2% in Feb
    • In PHP terms, remittances grew by 5.5%

 

 

 

 

TRANSPORT
Cebu Air pax volume growth slows to 18% in April; Domestic SLF drops to 85.6%
  • In its latest traffic report, Cebu Air (CEB PM) said that systemwide pax volumes rose 18% YoY to 2.3mn on seat growth of +20%
    • This is slower than the 26% print in 1Q25 due to a slowdown in domestic pax volume growth to +14% (from +28% in 1Q25)
    • International pax volume growth surged 32% YoY in April, faster than the 22% pace in 1Q25 and seat growth of +28% in April 2025
  • Management attributed the slowdown to a shift in demand from domestic to international due to more expensive domestic travel costs (e.g. hotels) vis-à-vis international
    • We highly suspect that the government’s move to remove operations of turboprops from the Manila airport last March 30 may have dampened demand
    • Note that domestic seat load factors (SLF) slid 2.6ppts YoY to 85.6% while international SLF improved 2.7ppts to 79% for the month of April
  • As of 4M25, pax volumes are up 24% YoY to 9.24mn while SLF is up 40bps YoY to 84.6%
