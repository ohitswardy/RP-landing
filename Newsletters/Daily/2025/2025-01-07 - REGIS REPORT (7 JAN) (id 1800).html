Jollibee (JFC PM) surprised us yesterday with an announcement that it plans to spin off and list its international operations by latter part of 2027. Based on the press release, the spin-off will be accomplished by giving existing JFC shareholders a proportionate ownership in the new spun-off entity (Jollibee Foods Corp International or JFCI).

 

Given that the market seemed to be assigning little (or even negative) value to JFC’s international operations, the spin-off (break-up?) will appear immediately value-accretive for shareholders. The market reaction yesterday seemed to agree.

Electricity volumes in Meralco’s (MER PM) core franchise area (excluding Clark, et al) declined 0.65% in FY25, according to management.  The FY25 number implies 4Q volume was still weak – likely flattish or slightly down YoY.

 

There are a couple of things to be said here. First, this is not an issue of base effects (i.e. the 4Q24 base was not unusually high). Second, this does not bode well for what 4Q GDP might look like.

POWER/UTILITIES
Energy sales in Meralco’s franchise area decline by 0.65% in 2025; Company sees modest 3% rebound in 2026
  • Indicative figures showed energy sales within Meralco’s franchise area fell 0.65% YoY to 53,257 GWH in 2025
    • Meralco has yet to consolidate figures from Clark Electric Distribution Corp. and other distribution utilities (DU)
    • Meralco’s franchise area accounts for 99% of total DU sales
    • According to Meralco Senior VP and Chief Revenue Officer Ferdinand O. Geluz
    • Recall that Meralco’s consolidated DU sales dipped by 0.4% YoY in 9M25
  • In 2025, Meralco’s residential and commercial sales dropped 2% and 0.5%, respectively, while the industrial segment grew by 1%
  • For 2026, Meralco is forecasting a 3% growth in energy sales, supported by higher customer connections and normalizing temperatures
  • Meralco’s distribution unit accounted for 55% of 9M25 profit while its unregulated generation/RES units accounted for the balance
MACRO
BSP guides easing cycle nearing its end
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona signaled that the end of the central bank's easing cycle is near, with one more potential rate cut to 4.25% in Feb "on the table"
  • Remolona stated that no cut or one more cut are the likely scenarios, as more than one cut would require a deterioration beyond the BSP's base outlook
    • He added that there may be grounds for more than one cut if 2026 growth goes below 5%
  • The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%
  • The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2025/26/27 are 4.6/5.4/6.0%, respectively
    • For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively
MACRO
Inflation inches up to 1.8% in December
  • Headline inflation picked up to 1.8% in Dec from Nov's 1.5%
    • This was higher than consensus expectations of 1.4% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.2-2.0% for Nov
    • Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past ten months
  • Inflation in Dec accelerated slightly given faster YoY increases in vegetable prices
    • Food prices overall rose 1.4% YoY, accelerating from the 0.1% pace in Nov—driven by the +11.6% YoY jump in vegetable prices vs +4.0% the previous month
    • Notably, rice prices were up MoM (+2.8%)—marking the second MoM price increase since Jun 2024
      • Rice prices are down 12.3% YoY in Dec, the twelfth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025
      • According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P43.6/kg and P44.9/kg as of Dec 2025 vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan 2025, respectively
  • Core inflation was unchanged at 2.4% in Dec relative to the previous month, core non-food inflation decelerated to 2.1% in Dec from 2.3% in Nov but was offset by the acceleration in core food inflation to 2.9% from 2.5%
  • This brings the FY25 headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target but in line with the BSP's 2025 expectation of 1.7%
    • The 1.7% FY inflation print number brought is lower than the 3.2% in 2024 and the lowest in nine years since 2016
    • Consensus and Regis 2025 inflation expectations were 1.7% and 1.8%, respectively
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.6% and 3.0%
  • The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19
    • For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%
