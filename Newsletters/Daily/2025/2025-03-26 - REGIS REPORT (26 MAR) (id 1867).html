REGIS REPORT

26 March 2025

President Marcos signed the law granting him the power to suspend excise taxes on petroleum products. The excise taxes are currently at P10/liter for gasoline and P6/liter for diesel, representing ~10% and ~5% of their respective pump prices, so eliminating them would cut pump prices by those proportions.

 

The government previously estimated that removing oil excise taxes would cut government revenue by over P200bn (US$3.5bn) annually. We are less alarmed by the impact on tax revenue, however, and believe that increased VAT collections (12%) from higher pump prices would just about compensate (based on the latest price data).

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) reported FY25 profit of P33.7bn (+17% YoY), boosted by a P3.5bn revaluation gain from the acquisition of an additional stake in Metrobank (MBT PM) in September, as well as its share of Toyota Motor Philippines’ (TMP) CARS tax incentive booked in 4Q25.  Removing these items, FY25 recurring profit rose 8% YoY to P30.47bn, below consensus’ P31.6bn forecast but above our P29bn estimate. 

 

Its 51%-owned auto unit, TMP, reported headline FY25 of P18.95bn, up 19% YoY.  Removing the tax incentive of P390mn in 4Q25, we estimate TMP’s recuring profit fell 59% QoQ and 53% YoY to P1.8bn in 4Q25 alone, as GP margins contracted 280bps QoQ/18bps YoY to 9.0%, offsetting the 21% QoQ/10% YoY jump in retail sales volumes. We hope to find out more later during management’s briefing at 2:30pm.

MACRO
Marcos signs oil excise tax suspension law
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed into law Republic Act (RA) 12316 which grants him the power to suspend or reduce the excise tax on petroleum products
    • After signing the law, Marcos emphasized that he would wait for the "best time" to implement it
  • Fuel excise taxes may now be adjusted if the average Dubai crude oil based on Mean of Platts Singapore benchmark reaches or exceeds US$80/bbl for a month
    • The adjusted excise tax will automatically revert to the original rate a week after the three-month limit or if the one-month average of Dubai crude is below US$80/bbl as certified by the Dept. of Energy
    • Implementation for a period cannot exceed three months and if extended, the aggregate period of the adjustment shall not exceed one calendar year
    • The President may only suspend or reduce excise tax collections on fuel products until Dec. 31, 2028
  • Government estimated the revenue losses could reach as much as P43bn if suspension lasts for three months and P106bn if extended to a total of six months
  • However, we believe tax revenue losses could actually be minimal if one considers the higher VAT collections from the increased pump prices
POWER
FGEN FY25 recurring profit rises 8% to US$264mn
  • In a very limited press release, FGEN reported its attributable recurring income rose 8% YoY to US$264mn in FY25, which appears below consensus estimate of US$306.5mn
    • Note that the 60% sale of its natgas assets to Prime Infrastructure was completed in November 2025, leaving FGEN with a 40% minority stake
    • As a result of the sale, the company recorded US$200mn in net income from discontinued operations, covering natgas profits from January to October 2025, as well as an US$11mn equitized profit beginning November 2025
  • Implied 4Q25 recurring profit of US$52.5mn was up 33% YoY but down by 13% QoQ
    • Derived 4Q profits from EDC (ex-hydro) declined by 7% YoY to US$14mn, taking FY25 contributions to US$52mn (-31%) due to lower sales volumes from Leyte and Mindanao, lower spot prices, and higher financing costs
    • Contributions from its hydro unit jumped 73% YoY in FY25, supported by higher output from higher water levels and higher power supply contract prices
  • The company noted that 2026 will be the year EDC’s investments in its drilling program bears significant fruit, while the recently announced partnership with Prime Infra for the 600MW Wawa and 1400 MW Pakil Pumped Storage Hydro Projects marks the company’s debut as greenfield hydro developers

 

 

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital transactions - decelerating, but still up 42% YoY in February

Digital transactions over PesoNet and Instapay in February stood at P2,343bn, up 42.3% YoY. This was slightly slower than the 43.1% growth clocked in January, and it was also a 9.2% decline MoM following the seasonal peaks of Dec/Jan. That said, the two-month total of P4,929bn was up 42.7% YoY, a shade faster than the 42.0% in FY2025. We reiterate that the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) only records transactions between platforms, and does not include transfers within the platform (ie GCash to GCash, Maya to Maya, BDO to BDO, etc), which greatly understates total digital universe.

 

For smaller-ticket (under P50,000, or about $833) real-time InstaPay transactions, February came in at P1,167bn, up 63.7% YoY compared to the 65.5% in January. The 2M26 total of P2,410bn grew 64.6%, significantly faster than the 57.3% clocked for FY25. By volume, the number of InstaPay transactions in February was still a blistering 298.6% YoY. This brought down the average ticket to P1,875 ($31.25), indicating to us that the technology is gaining ever-wider acceptance for everyday payments.

 

Larger, batched transactions over PesoNet reached P1,176bn, up 26.0% YoY, a slight deceleration from the 27.1% YoY the previous month. The 2M26 figure of P2,514bn was up 26.6%, also slower than the 30.9% pace in FY2025. It would seem that PesoNet is maturing faster, which makes sense given that adoption of digital transfers happened faster as it is a safer way to pay versus cash.

 

The adoption of digital payments has been crucial in the explosion of digibanks, neo-banks, and online lending apps, and is proving a crucial tool in financial inclusion.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

CONSUMER
RRHI announces closure of No Brand Stores in the Philippines
  • RRHI will gradually wind down operations of its 11 remaining No Brand stores by end-June 2026
    • Management cited "evolving consumer preferences" as the company simplifies its portfolio
    • No Brand standalone stores were first introduced in the Philippines back in 2019 through a master franchise agreement between RRHI and South Korea's Emart
  • RRHI states they expect no material impact on financial performance as No Brand accounts for 0.2% of annual net sales and a small portion of total assets
  • The overall food segment of RRHI had 799 stores (including 14 No Brand stores) out of the 2,763 total store network as of end-2025
    • The food segment in 2025 accounted for 60% of total net sales and 62% of total EBITDA
