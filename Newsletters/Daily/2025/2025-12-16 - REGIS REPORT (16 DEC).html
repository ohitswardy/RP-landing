REGIS REPORT

16 December 2025

Lots of bits and bobs this morning. Refreshingly all positive news!

 

It seems RRR cuts on banks’ PHP deposits are back on the table. In an interview over the weekend, BSP Gov Eli Remolona said he’d be “happy” to see RRR reduced from 5.0% today to 2.0% “in a year or so. He was quick to point out, though, that the RRR is NOT part of an economic support package like the policy rate cut. Instead, it’s part of the long-term structural goals set by the BSP way back in 2018, and accelerated in recent years. We estimate that a 300bps RRR cut could add 7-9bps to NIMs and about 4-7% to NPAT based on our 2026E forecasts.

+++

Property also saw some welcome positive news, with consultancy firm Leechiu Property Consultants estimates office take-up of both BPO firms and traditional tenants improved sequentially in 4Q25.

+++

Some news out of the two port operators. ICTSI (ICT PM)  is investing $175mn to increase capacity of its Rio port in Brazil by 70%. This is already in our forecasts, but is separate from the potential bidding of the Santos port, also in Brazil. Meanwhile, Asian Terminals (ATI PM) is heading for a voluntary delisting, with the Maharlika Fund launching a tender offer at P36/sh, a slight premium over the current share price, valuing the stock at 7x trailing EV/Ebitda, and 4% div yield.

+++

Manila Water (MWC PM) and Maynilad (MYNLD PM) will both receive tariff hikes in January, with MWC getting a chunkier 7.64% bump compared to MYNLD’s 2.85%

+++

Finally, OFW remittances were up 3.0% YoY in October to $3.2bn, taking 10M25 up 3.2% to $29.2bn. This should continue to support consumption, house purchases, etc.

BANKS
Potential RRR cut to 2.0% in 2026?

Bloomberg reported that BSP Governor Eli Remolona said he would be “happy” to see the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) on banks’ PHP deposits down to 2.0% in 2026. The quote was from a TV interview (watch it here, at around 7:00 in the clip) where Gov Remolona explained the recent policy rate cut. While we believe the impact of any further RRR cuts to the economy is questionable, they would definitely be positive for banks

There’s a bunch of things to unpack here:

  1. Gov Remolona has consistently advocated for a move to zero RRR, at least eventually, citing the inefficiency of RRR in fine-tuning monetary policy. The BSP, too, has long shared this view. Following its new charter in 2018 – which gave it a broader set of monetary tools – the BSP steadily reduced the RRR from as high as 21.0% in 2005, to 5.0% in its most recent cut in March 2025 (see chart below).
  2. However, the Governor also recently said that a RRR cut at this time “won’t do that much” to support economic growth. (Read it here.) The economic slowdown is the result of lost confidence over the ongoing corruption scandal, and a RRR cut is unlikely help in the confidence department.
  3. That said, the banking system’s liquidity is not as ample as it seems at first glance. While headline LDR stood at 74.4% in 2Q25, we estimate that the LDR of banks’ PHP books is much tighter, at 84% as of 2Q25. This is because the FX book’s LDR is a mere 26% (see chart below). While that suggests that there’s still 11% to go (to the theoretical 95% LDR limit), it’s much less than the headline numbers suggest.
  4. Positive for banks. As in the past, we simplistically and conservatively assume that banks deploy the freed-up PHP liquidity in 10-year PHP Treasuries (currently back above 6.0%). Under these conditions, we estimate a 300bps reduction to 2.0% could increase NIMs by 7-9bps, and NPAT by 4-6% on our 2026E forecasts. (See table below)

We will be watching this space very closely.

PROPERTY
LPC: 4Q25 net office take-up rises YoY and QoQ
  • Leechiu Property Consultants reported Metro Manila office vacancy was flat QoQ and YoY at 18%
  • Nationwide net office take-up increased to 169,000sqm in 4Q25, from net take-up of 21,000sqm in 3Q25 (net incremental vacancy of 100,000sqm in 4Q24)
    • BPO net office take-up expanded to 85,000sqm, from net take-up of 6,000sqm in 3Q25 (1,000sqm net take-up in 4Q24)
    • Net take-up from traditional tenants hit 84,000sqm, from 23,000sqm in 3Q25 (down from 98,000sqm in 4Q24)
  • ALI is trading at 59% discount to NAV, 1.0X FY26E P/B, and 10.5X FY26E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.2X FY26E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY26E P/B, and 5.5X FY26E P/E
  • SMPH is trading at 47% discount to NAV, 1.3X FY26E P/B, and 13.5X FY26E P/E

TRANSPORT
ICTSI investing US$175mn to boost Rio port’s capacity by 70%
  • ICTSI is investing a total of BRL948mn (US$175mn) to raise the capacity of its port in Rio, Brazil by 70% to 750k TEUs by 2029
  • Our estimates and valuation already assume this expansion
  • We estimate the port in Rio, Brazil accounts for 3% of EBITDA and 7% of NAV
  • ICTSI trades at 10.4x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 17.5x PER - vs peer average (12.6x and 18x) and historical average (11x and 20x)
UTILITIES
Metro Manila water utilities to implement fourth tranche of tariff hike in January
  • The water regulator, MWSS, approved the tariff increases of +7.64% for Manila Water (MWC PM) and +2.85% for Maynilad (MYNLD PM) beginning January 1, 2026
    • These adjustments cover the (a) fourth tranche of the approved “R” tariff, which assumes the construction of a new water source, Kaliwa dam, as well as (b) CPI adjustment (+0.68%)
  • MWSS also approved the increase in MWC’s environmental surcharge to 30% from 25%
  • Note that our valuation and profit estimates for Manila Water already assume these increases
MACRO
Remittances rise by 3% in October
  • Cash remittances amounted US$3.17bn in October, 3% higher YoY
  • Brings 10M25 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$29.2bn
    • Weak remittance growth from North America (e.g. USA: -0.2%) has been partially offset by stronger remittances growth from the rest of the world
