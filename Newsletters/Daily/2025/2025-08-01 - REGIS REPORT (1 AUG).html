01 August 2025

Chinabank’s (CBC PM) NPAT of P13.0bn (+14% YoY) for 1H25 seemed in line with consensus of P27bn for FY25E. However, the innards of the P&L were… odd. Provision expense exploded to P6.5bn, with 2Q alone at P6.22bn. The 2Q provision alone was worth about 0.7% of total loans, or about 260bps in credit cost. This was offset by an equally odd surge in non-interest income. The results were just a press release, so details were rather thin. We should hear more during the briefing at 10:30am this morning.

 

 

Metrobank (MBT PM) issued a press release showing 1H25 NPAT of P24.8bn, up 5% YoY in line with our P50.9bn forecast for FY25E (slightly higher than consensus P50.1bn). Loans +13.2%, with consumer +15.3% and corporate +12.7%. Non-interest income was boosted by trading/fx gains, though this was a bit illusory, as 1H24 still had interest rate swaps (which used to lower interest expense, but booking trading/FX losses). Provision expense was P5.8bn up sharply from the (unusually low base of) P1bn in 1H25, but were in line with our forecasts (P12bn FY25E), and in line with management guidance. NPL improved to 1.5% (was 1.7%). Overall the results were broadly a solid set of results. Briefing today at 4pm.

 

 

+++

 

The Dept of Agriculture says it expects record rice harvests this year, with total palay (unmilled rice) output to exceed 20.5mn MT (which would be the highest on record). Last year’s output was 19.1mn MT. The DA is citing the record harvest as a reason to raise rice tariffs from the current 15%.

 

The Philippines consumes ~16mn MT of rice annually and the government estimates the “conversion ratio” from palay to milled rice is 63%.

 

Low rice prices have been a major factor in the low inflation prints over the last few months. And the low inflation number has offered the Banko Sentral (BSP) elbow room to further ease monetary policy.

 

+++

 

Ayala Corp (AC PM) secured clearance from the competition authorities to proceed with the sale of 50% of AC Ventures to Mitsubishi Corp at a valuation for 100% of ~US$5bn. AC Ventures holds a 13+% stake in Mynt, the firm that houses e-wallet operator GCash. Recall AC Ventures raised its stake in Mynt to 13% (from ~5%) partly to facilitate the participation of Mitsubishi in Mynt.

BANKS
Chinabank - 2Q NPAT - massive provisions vs massive "other income"

Chinabank (CBC PM) reported in a press release that 1H25 NPAT came in at P13bn up 14% YoY, and in line with consensus forecast of P27bn for FY25E. However, the innards of the disclosure were… odd. Notably, provision expense exploded to P6.25bn in 2Q25 alone, equivalent to about 0.7% of total loans, or an annualized credit cost of 260bps. This contrasts with management’s guidance of 40bps (and 1Q25’s 12bp credit cost). This was offset by a massive swing in non-interest income to P4.8bn in 2Q25. No details were given re: the nature of the gains. In previous briefings and conversations, management cited prior periods’ low credit costs were due to write-backs / write-ups of NPLs that became current. Unsure whether a) these loans became NPL again, or b) if this is due to new accounts/s, or c) this is, as they said, “proactive” provisioning.

 

Elsewhere, operations seemed to chug along. Loan growth was 18%, sustaining the 18-19% level over the previous two quarters. Management estimates NIMs were up 13bps YoY, though we take this with a healthy helping of salt, considering the size of the interest rate swaps. In previous briefings, CBC’s CFO noted that the impact of the swaps would be to reduce NIMs by c100bps. Opex was steady QoQ, still up sharply YoY due to heavy IT and manpower investments.

MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture expects record rice output this year
  • The Department of Agriculture is "cautiously optimistic" on hitting a record-high harvest of palay (unmilled rice) this year despite the recent typhoons
  • Unmilled rice production in 1H25 is up 6% YoY to 9.1mn MT, currently on track to beat the 20.5mn MT full-year target, assuming 1H continues to account for over 40% of annual output, which has typically been the case in past years
  • If production at least hits the 20.5mn MT target this year, it will be 7% higher than the 19.1mn MT output last year and 2% higher than the record 20.06mn MT record output in 2023
CONGLOS
PCC clears Mitsubishi’s investment in AC Ventures
  • The Philippine Competition Commission has approved Ayala Corp’s sale of a 50% stake in AC Ventures (ACV) to Japan’s Mitsubishi Corp. for P18.4bn
    • ACV is the entity that holds 13.7% of Mynt
    • AC’s effective stake in Mynt will be reduced to ~6.8%
    • Secondary share sale implies a P36.8bn valuation for 13% of Mynt and thus ~P280bn (~US$5bn) for 100%
  • AC trades at a 24% discount to its market-based NAV of P778/sh and 7.6x FY25E PER
MACRO
BSP projects July inflation to settle within 0.5-1.3%
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) in its month-ahead inflation forecast expects July inflation to fall within 0.5-1.3%
    • Inflation has averaged 1.8% so far for the first six months of the year; inflation in June was 1.4%
  • Upward price pressures are higher prices of meat and vegetables, elevated fuel and electricity prices as well as the depreciation of the peso
    • These may have been partially offset by lower prices of rice
  • Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.0%
    • Regis expects the full year average to be 1.8%
  • July inflation data will be released by the PSA (Philippine Statistics Authority) on Aug 5 (Tuesday)
MACRO
Government debt inches up to P17.27trn in end-June
  • The government's total debt stock went up by 2.1% MoM to P17.27trn as of end-June
    • Domestic debt rose by 1.4% MoM to P11.95trn due to the impact of the additional issuance of gov't securities
    • External debt increased by 3.5% MoM to P5.32trn
    • The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.2% and 30.8% being external borrowings
  • The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 1Q25 stood at 62% of GDP up from 60.7% in 4Q24
  • The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 60.4% by end-2025
    • The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%
  • The Dept. of Finance stated that "the gov't is on track to reduce the national gov't debt-to-GDP ratio to within 60% by end of the President's term"
