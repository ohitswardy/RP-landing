We have a new report out on the telecom operators, refreshing our views on the sector and our stock coverage.

 

The industry has already been seeing challenges in recent periods, with mobile revenue ceasing to grow in 1H. The passage of the new law on the sector (aka Konektadong Pinoy Act or KPA) will likely add further pressure medium term, particularly for the broadband business.

 

Despite the rather lackluster outlook for the core businesses, we remain BUYers for the telco names under coverage. Both PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) offer what we believe are very attractive, sustainable yields of 8% and 7%, respectively. We peg our target price (TP) for PLDT at P1,300/share and for Globe at P2,100. Our TP for Converge (CNVRG PM) is also now revised to P18/share.

 

We also highlight that our assumed valuation for PLDT’s 37%-owned fintech associate Maya is US$1.5bn, which would amount to 15% of our target valuation for PLDT. For Globe, we have assumed a valuation of US$7bn for its own fintech Mynt (~34% owned). US$7bn translates to an attributable value of US$2.4bn to Globe. At the valuation, Mynt would account for 44% of Globe’s net value / TP.

 

For Converge, we think the recent battering the shares have received is overdone, notwithstanding concerns over the impact of KPA on the broadband business. The stock is now on only 7x PER for FY26E, with still respectable top line (>10%) and EPS growth (9% CAGR).

The raging corruption scandal over the “flood control” funds in the national budget claimed House Speaker Martin Romualdez as a casualty. Romualdez, a cousin of President Marcos, officially stepped down yesterday amidst controversy over the scandal.

 

Rep Faustino “Bojie” Dy took over as Speaker. Dy is from northern Luzon and is also a member of a powerful political dynasty. We don’t think the legislative priorities of the House will be much different with Dy as Speaker. What may change, however, is the aggressiveness with which the House may (or not) pursue impeachment against the Vice President.

 

Meanwhile, another effect of the scandal is unfolding. The Dept of Public Works and Highways (DPWH, the main agency involved in the scandal) will likely see a massive cut in its budget for FY26. Much of the funds previously earmarked for the DPWH will likely be diverted to other priorities, like education, agriculture and health.

A new law that is meant to accelerate the acquisition of private property for government projects was signed by President Marcos yesterday. There have been previous laws to address the main bottleneck for most infrastructure projects (i.e. acquisition of what’s called “rights of way” or ROW) but those have failed to be as effective as hoped.

 

+++

 

Senator Zubiri has called for the imposition of interest rate caps from online lending platforms, noting that some charge as much as 20% a month. While that level is definitely way too high, we are wary of any regulatory intervention on rates at this time. The formal banking sector loans to GDP is woefully low at just 11%. This does not include non-bank fincos (NBFCs). But the point is that access to formal credit is still quite low. We have only just seen the likes of Mynt/GCash, Home Credit, Maya Bank, etc. significantly growing the formal lending market. By our estimates, these platforms charge an annualized interest rate of between 35-70%. While those seem high, consider that their credit costs are also high at 15-30%. This is largely because the Philippines still does not have a fully functioning national credit bureau.

 

Any attempt to limit rates now could push borrowers back into the informal sector.

POLITICS
Romualdez steps down as House Speaker
  • Congressman Martin Romualdez has stepped down as speaker of the House of Representatives after he relinquished his post during a plenary session
    • Romualdez served as speaker in the 19th and briefly in the 20th Congress
  • This marks another leadership change after the recent installation of Senator Tito Sotto III as Senate President, ousting Senator Chiz Escudero
  • The changes in congressional leadership took place amid the ongoing corruption crackdown on flood control projects by the Marcos administration
    • The President emphasized that no one will be spared from an independent investigation—including his relatives and allies
  • Congressman Faustino 'Bojie' Dy III, a deputy speaker, was the only person nominated by lawmakers to replace Romualdez and was elected in a 253-28-0 vote with no votes against Dy
    • It was reportedly said that Romualdez recommended to party leaders to vote for Dy as his replacement
    • Dy ran under the same party as President Marcos and was elected as Isabela's 6th District Representative
MACRO
DPWH cuts P255bn worth of flood control projects in 2026 budget proposal, resumes bidding of infra projects with stricter requirements
  • Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vince Dizon recommended to the House of Representatives a revised budget of P626bn which is 30% (P255bn) lower than the initially proposed P881bn and 42% lower than this year's DPWH budget of P1.1trn
    • The main revision follows President Marcos' directive for a "sweeping review" of the DPWH's budget and the removal of the P255bn allocation for flood control projects for 2026
    • The P255bn was said to likely be reallocated to other priority sectors such as education, agri, health programs, etc.
  • Infrastructure spend for 2026 was initially projected to be P1.56trn (+3% YoY, 5.1% of GDP)
    • Accounting for the P255bn cut in spending for flood control projects, infra spend would amount to P1.3trn (-13% YoY, 4.3% of GDP)
    • For 2025, projected infra spend is P1.51tn (-3% YoY, 5.3% of GDP)
  • The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than this year's P6.33trn budget
  • The gov't has lifted the temporary suspension on infrastructure project bidding but prospective bidders for public works now face stricter requirements such as:
    • Live-streaming of all bidding activities
    • Geotagged photos that will be monitored by relevant offices
    • Net financial contracting capacity (NFCC) to be assessed based on tax filings through the BIR's electronic system with backed audited financial statements
    • Sworn disclosures of beneficial ownership, including individuals who ultimately own or control the entity

 

 

MACRO
President Marcos signs amended RoW Act
  • President Marcos signed into law the Accelerated and Reformed Right-of-Way (RoW) Act
  • The law’s key provisions are:
    • Expansion on the RoW law to include all national government infrastructure projects undertaken through Public-Private Partnerships (PPP)
    • Revision of rules on negotiated sale, which now allows the price offer to be based on the standardized property valuation system and schedule of market values established (vs. BIR zonal values previously)
    • Payment terms are now clearly laid out to be: 50% of land price and 70% of structures upon execution of a deed of sale; Remainder will be paid upon transfer of title or annotation of deed of sale
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Senator flags lack of law capping rates on online lending apps
  • Senate Majority Leader Miguel Zubiri pushed for tighter regulations on predatory online lending applications (OLAs) as the absence of any law that caps interest rates will continue to the detriment of a number of Filipinos, especially those addicted to online gambling
    • He said that some OLAs were charging interest rates as high as 20% a month
  • SEC lawyer Romarie Abrazaldo stated that the Usury Law has been suspended and that there is no regulation that currently limits interest rates on these platforms
  • The SEC highlighted that they are still reviewing possible actions but Zubiri emphasized that many Filipinos will continue to fall for predatory practices with the continued inaction of the regulatory body
  • Zubiri suggested for the BSP to step in to oversee these OLAs instead of the SEC citing the BSP's thorough due diligence
    • He noted that OLAs only require P1mn in capitalization and registration with the SEC while micro loaning institutions and cooperative banks go through exhaustive background checks which take years to get BSP approval
