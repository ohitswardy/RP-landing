REGIS REPORT

15 July 2025

ICTSI’s (ICT PM) CMSA port in Manzanillo, Mexico saw throughput drop 14% YoY. This was NOT because of tariff concerns, but instead due to a 4-day labor strike in mid-May that caused logistics disruptions that lasted into June. The strike affected all port operators in Manzanillo area. The 1H25 volume growth for CMSA was 6%, tracking well below our 16% YoY full-year forecast. It is unclear if the operations have already normalized in July.

 

+ + +

 

Banks’ asset quality ratios were steady MoM in May. Excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP), industry NPL ratio inched up 2bps MoM to 3.58%. Meanwhile Past Due (PD) and Restructured Loan (RL) ratios improved, with PD down 2bps MoM to 4.47%, and RL down 1bp to 2.13%.

 

Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) re-accelerated to 11.7% YoY, compared to the 11.2% recorded in April, reversing the three consecutive months of slowdown.

 

The continued strength in loan growth, and stable asset quality support our current overweight call on banks.

 

+ + +

 

In the continuing online gaming saga, GCash told InsiderPH that while it has not removed “clickable links” to e-gaming sites, it will be increasing “friction” starting tomorrow 16 July. It is unclear what this entails.

 

+ + +

 

Ms Monalisa Dimalanta, the Chair of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), has resigned. Just last week, two other ERC commissioners retired. Ms Dimalanta’s departure leaves the ERC with just two remaining commissioners, which means the ERC cannot promulgate any regulatory decisions (minimum 3) until at least one more commissioner is appointed. This could further slow down approvals of tariffs – for Meralco (MER PM), for example – and for new projects.

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Mexico port throughput drops in June, as aftermath of customs strike lingers
  • Data from Mexico’s port authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s port, Contecon Manzanillo (CMSA), plunged 14% YoY to 111k TEUs in June
    • Decline in June was consistent across all ports in Manzanillo, which saw volumes fall 11% YoY
    • A four-day customs labor strike ensued from May 12-15, disrupting logistics operations across Manzanillo for more than a month (see here, here)
    • Given that tariffs for import/export boxes are identical at CMSA, we suspect that the disruption may have also resulted in lower yield
  • This brings 2Q25 volumes at CMSA to 354k TEUs, up 4% YoY
    • This is slower than the 8% print in 1Q25
  • Cumulative 1H25 volumes at CMSA to 741k TEUs, up 6% YoY and trailing our forecast of 1.69mn TEUs (+16% YoY)
    • To reach our forecast, volumes for the rest of the year would have to average 157k TEUs per month
    • The port’s capacity today is 1.7mn TEUs (~142k TEUs per month) and this capacity will increase to “over 2mn TEUs” (~168-175k TEUs per month) by year-end (new berth will be completed by Aug/Sep 2025)
  • Note that our forecasts assume the US-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) treaty will be cancelled only by July 2026
    • Every 5% decrease (increase) in CMSA’s FY25E volumes will shave off (increase) EBITDA by 1.6% and profit by 2.4%
    • We estimate CMSA accounts for 12% of consolidated volumes, 21% of revenues, and 25% of group EBITDA
  • ICTSI trades at 9.1x on Regis’ FY25E EV/EBITDA (consensus: 10x), in line with peer median 9.1x but below peer average of 11.6x
    • Note that shares of most listed port operators, including ICTSI, have rebounded from lows, suggesting improvement in investor sentiment

 

 

 

 

 

BANKS
Asset quality steady in May

Philippine banks’ asset quality metrics in May were broadly steady MoM. As before, we are looking at loans excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP), as these create unnecessary noise in the data. The charts feature both the as-reported numbers, as well as the ex-IBL/RRP data for reference.

 

NPL ratio (ex-IBL/RRP) was up 2bps MoM (actually closer to 1.5bps) to 3.58% in May, compared to 3.56% in April. In nominal terms, NPLs were up 1.6% MoM to P527bn, slightly faster than the 1.2% MoM growth in loans. Past Due (PD) ratio went the other direction, down 2bps to 4.47% in May from 4.49% the previous month. In PHP terms, PD loans were up 0.9% MoM to P659bn. Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio inched down 1bp MoM to 2.13% from 2.14% in April. NPL cover, meanwhile, was 94.6% in May, down slightly from 95.1%.

 

Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) re-accelerated to 11.7% YoY, from 11.2% YoY in April. This was slightly slower than the reported 12.4%, due to the sharper increase in IBL/RRPs (+27% YoY). This is in line with our industry forecast of 12% loan growth for FY25.

 

By bank type, it would seem that the increase in NPLs came from the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs), whose NPLs were up 2bps MoM. The biggest improvement came from Digibanks, whose NPL ratio were down 38bps MoM to 9.79% and whose PD ratio fell 79bps MoM to 18.28%.

 

The stability of asset quality is comforting, despite the continued strong(ish) loan growth.
