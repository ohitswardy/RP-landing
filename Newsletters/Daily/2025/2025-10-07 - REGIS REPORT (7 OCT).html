REGIS REPORT

07 October 2025

The headline CPI inflation figure for Sept came in at 1.7%, higher than the prior month’s 1.5% but apparently lower than Street guestimates (=1.9%). This CPI print does not alter our view that the Bangko Sentral (BSP) is likely to keep rates unchanged at this week’s Monetary Board meeting. The MB will have two more CPI data points to consider before the last meeting this year and the current rice import ban could have influence over those data points.

 

 

+++

 

The government is extending the ban on rice imports to the end of April 2026 but with a one-month “window” for imports to come in. The import moratorium took effect at the start of Sept and retail prices seem to have been responding. Retail prices are already creeping up despite the harvest season and lower import prices (see chart).

 

We expect this import ban will lead to progressively higher prices in the coming months. This will have implications on CPI inflation (rice is 9% of CPI basket) and, consequently, monetary policy.

 

 

+++

 

Figures from the Leechiu Property Consultants 3Q25 briefing suggest that residential sales improved while office demand weakened.

 

Despite anecdotes of subdued high-end residential demand due to the flood control corruption scandal, residential sales grew 16% QoQ (+12% YoY) to 7,700 units, the highest level in seven quarters. This could mean that aggressive promotions on ready-for-occupancy units continued to entice buyers in the third quarter.

 

On the other hand, office demand declined sequentially in 3Q25. Among the various office markets, Makati and Fort Bonifacio are performing better, with vacancy rates of only 9-10%, much lower than the 18% figure for the entirety of Metro Manila.

 

+++

 

Last week, Security Bank (SECB PM) announced it had hired Mr Victor Lee as its new CEO effective Jan 2026. He joins new CFO Allen Reyes, who took the position in August. The new senior management team seem to be good fits for the jobs, to push SECB’s “customer centric” wealth mgt / consumer / MSME strategy. They will face some challenges and opportunities, though. The bank’s credit card business has been hit hard with delinquencies, with credit costs dragging ROEs in recent years. That said, the outgoing management seems convinced that main cleanup is done, and that credit costs will peak in 3Q25. This could give the new team a clean-ish slate from which to grow and improve ROEs. Meanwhile, the stock is trading at seemingly distressed levels of 0.34x P/bk and 3.7x PER on our 2026E forecasts. With the bar set quite low, any improvement in ROEs could see the stock re-rate. Buy maintained.

+ + +

 

Digital payments continue to surge, particularly in the lower-ticket InstaPay. Overall payments were up 45% YoY to P2.1trn in August. But InstaPay transactions exploded 65% YoY to P1.03trn. At this pace, InstaPay will likely overtake PesoNet – at P1.06bn, up 30% YoY – by transaction value in a month (or less?). In a call with the BSP last June, it was clear that the regulator is pushing for even broader digital adoption, through lowering fees, and improving inter-operability between platforms. The recent move to limit daily cash withdrawals to P500,000 could also accelerate the shift to digital payments.

MACRO
Gov't extends rice import ban till April 2026; lawmakers push for higher rice import tariffs
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has approved The Dept. of Agriculture's (DA) plan to extend the current three-month rice import ban to the end-April 2026 with a one-month rice import window in Jan 2026
    • Recall that a suspension of rice imports from Sep 1 to end-Nov was done to boost farmgate prices given the current peak harvest season of palay (unmilled rice)
  • DA Secretary Tiu Laurel said rice imports should have totaled only 2.7 MMT as of 9M25, but the actual volume has already exceeded the target by 800,000 tons, reaching 3.5 MMT by end-Sept
    • He emphasized that the DA continues to push for the reversion to a higher rice import tariff but notes that it is still under review
    • The original duty on imported rice was 35% but reduced to 15% until 2028 back in Jul 2024 to reduce the retail prices of rice
  • House Speaker Faustino Dy III stated that one million farmers will receive P7,000 in cash aid under the proposed 2026 budget to mitigate losses from low palay prices
  • Dy noted that long-term reforms are being sought with the RICE Act (Rice Industry and Consumer Empowerment), which is a priority bill
    • The bill seeks to restore the National Food Authority's (NFA) power to buy palay during harvest season, raise the rice import tariff to 35%, set a P25/kg palay floor price, and allocate 75% of rice tariff revenue to farmers

    • BPO take-up seems to have declined in 3Q25

  • Unsold condominium units in Metro Manila fell 4% QoQ to 79,400
    • We estimate that unsold inventory represents 38 months of take-up (vs 41 months in 2Q25)

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
Digital payments +45%% YoY in August; InstaPay crosses P1trn +65% YoY

Digital payments over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) clocked in at P2,086bn in August, up 44.8% YoY, but edging down slightly -2.3% MoM from July. This took the year-to-August figure up to P15,348bn, up 40.9% YoY, faster than the 35.4% YoY pace in FY2024. At the current monthly pace, the 9M25 figure will likely surpass the P17,423bn for the whole of 2024.

 

The growth was led by InstaPay – for smaller-ticket payments of up to P50,000 – which crossed the P1trn level for the first time to P1,027bn, up 64.6%, the fastest monthly YoY growth rate since November 2021. For 8M25, InstaPay transactions were up 54.6% YoY to P6,898bn, just a hair’s breadth away from the P7,347bn in FY2024. Meanwhile, the volume of transactions continued to explode, up 255% YoY, indicating continued broadening of adoption for everyday payments.

 

InstaPay transactions will likely get a mild boost in October, as the BSP required all “cash-in” transactions from banks to wallets to be done via InstaPay. Some banks and wallets were still operating on bilateral agreements. Most notable was between sister companies BPI and GCash, both of which announced the switch on 30 Sept.

 

Meanwhile, larger-ticket, batch-processed payments over PesoNet stood at P1,059bn, up 30% YoY, though this was a slowdown from the 33% YoY pace in July. It was also down 8% MoM. For the 8M25 period, PesoNet transactions were P8,360bn, up 31% YoY, slightly faster than the 29% growth in FY2024.

 

Beyond payments, digital adoption has enabled banks, digibanks, and fintechs, to expand financial inclusion, particularly  for online lending. As we reported in July, the BSP is working hard to further broaden digital adoption by lowering transaction fees, and by improving inter-operability between banks and fintechs.
