Our results round-up today:

 

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (+) – 9M core profit reached P36.6bn, practically flat YoY with 3Q core profit at P12.8b (+4% YoY). The improvement in the smaller and newer investments is visible, following the clean-up of the past few years. AC Mobility is gaining market share, principally via BYD sales. AC Health is at breakeven and even IMI (IMI PM) is in the black.

 

 

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) – Cebu Air posted a modest P199mn profit in 3Q25 due to a tax benefit. EBIT actually turned negative in 3Q on softer revenue growth of +5% (+23% in 1H), which management attributed to a longer lean season. 9M25 profit reached P4.4bn, up 117% YoY but looks a tad short of estimates. At yesterday’s briefing, management sounded upbeat that favorable market dynamics will allow them to (a) raise fares to offset the lower seat growth guidance and (b) resume paying dividends to common shareholders by 1Q26.

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp: 3Q25 core profit up 4% on turnaround of portfolio investments

9M25 core profit flat at P36.6bn, tracking mildly ahead of estimates

AC’s net income (before pref divs) rose 96% YoY to P22.9bn in 3Q25, boosted by a revaluation gain from AC Ventures. Stripping this out, core profit for 3Q rose 4% YoY to P12.8bn, supported by the profit turnaround at its portfolio investments, which outweighed the subdued performance of BPI (here), Ayala Land (here), and Globe (here). Even as ACEN (here) reported a 3Q profit improvement, ACEIC, its direct parent, appears to have recorded lower profits. 

 

Cumulative 9M25 core profit was flat at P36.6bn. Given that AC’s historical tendency to catch up on costs in 4Q, we’d view the results as just modestly ahead of Regis and Street estimates. 

 

AC Mobility’s losses narrowed to P104mn in 3Q25, as unit sales double

AC Mobility’s unit sales rose 111% YoY to 31,669 units, suggesting that in 3Q25 alone sales jumped 8% QoQ, 101% YoY to 11,649 units.  Its market share rose to 8.4% in 9M25 from 8.0% in 1H25, largely due to BYD, whose share we estimate grew further to 6% in 3Q from 5% in 2Q. Thanks to robust sales, AC Mobility’s net loss in 3Q was halved to P104mn, taking 9M25 profit to P18mn, a reversal of the P176mn net loss in the same period last year.

 

AC Health revenues/EBITDA up 30%/69% YoY in 3Q

Core losses from its healthcare arm narrowed by 82% YoY to P75mn in 9M25, implying that it broke even (+P25mn) in 3Q. Revenue growth was broadly sustained at 30% in 3Q25, resulting in a 69% YoY EBITDA improvement.

 

IMI sustains profit turnaround

Even though revenues fell 66%/51% YoY in 3Q25/9M25, IMI sustained its turnaround, posting a profit of US$7.2mn in 3Q25 and US$14.8mn in 9M25, as cost reduction initiatives bear fruit. The same can be said for AC Logistics, whose net losses narrowed 23% YoY to P771mn in 9M25.

 

CONGLOS
JG Summit writing off petrochem business
  • JG Summit plans to write off JGS Olefins Corp (aka JG Petrochem), after more than two decades of losses
  • The write-off will mean an estimated impairment loss of P114.bn (see below)
    • This is equivalent to 30% of JGS’ book value per share so the impairment will reduce JGS’ book value per share from ~P50/share to around P35/share
  • The positive take on this is that JG Summit will significantly reduce the cash losses from the business until it is able to sell or completely shut it down
  • We understand JGS will still need to spend P100-P150m/month just to keep the plants in such a condition for it to be eventually sold
UTILITIES
Maynilad’s 9M25 capex rises 33% but trails commitment; Interim OCP rises to P165bn
  • Maynilad reported its 9M25 net income rose 18% to P11.4bn
  • In 3Q25 alone, profits jumped 16% YoY to P3.95bn, on the back of tariff hikes
    • Billed volumes slipped 1% YoY while NRW improved 440bps YoY to end at 35.9%
  • We estimate overall cash spending (capex, cash opex, con fees) rose 6% YoY to P9.1bn in 3Q25 and 23% YoY to P27.3bn in 9M25
    • Spending remains short of its regulatory commitment of ~P43bn per year
    • Cash opex surged 27% in 3Q25, taking 9M25 cash opex up 3% YoY
    • Capex (incl. con fees) rose 6% to P9.1bn in 3Q25 and 33% YoY to P27.3bn in 9M25
  • Maynilad estimates its interim OCP has risen to P165bn as of end-September 2025 from P145bn in end-2024 and P100bn in end-2022
    • The OCP increase largely reflects its 12% regulatory return (or Appropriate Discount Rate, ADR), as free cash flow generation in 9M25 was only a very small negative P189mn.
  • The company is holding a briefing on Monday, November 17 at 1pm (register by email: investor.relations@mayniladwater.com.ph)

MACRO
Marcos seeks to ramp up gov't spending in 4Q
  • President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has stated that "public spending will now be increased" to ensure that spending levels are aligned with the original programmed P1.31trn disbursement allocated for 4Q
  • The President seeks to recover from the disappointing 3Q GDP growth of 4% through accelerating infrastructure and social sector disbursements in the final quarter of the year
    • Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vince Dizon stated that multiple projects will resume before year-end as construction will ramp up "but with safeguards"
    • P9.5bn was said to be allocated for conditional cash transfers
MACRO
Budget committee to revise debt-to-GDP and GDP growth projections; retains fiscal deficit target
  • The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC)—the gov't committee in charge of macro, revenue, budget projections—for 2025, has retained its fiscal deficit projection of 5.5% but revised the debt-to-GDP target of 61.3% to 63%
    • The current debt-to-GDP ratio is 63.1% as of 3Q25, unchanged from the previous quarter but higher than the 60.1% in 3Q25
  • Senator Sherwin Gatchalian, head of the Senate finance committee, notes that the figure is slightly better than ASEAN peers (~70% debt-to-GDP)
  • Gatchalian highlighted that the DBCC is targeting to restore the debt to GDP ratio to its pre-pandemic levels especially given the current trajectory which was exacerbated by the disappointing 3Q GDP print of 4.0%
  • The DBCC is also set to deliberate possible revisions on its current growth targets of 5.5-6.5% for 2025 and 6-7% for 2026-2028
    • GDP growth expectations for consensus are currently at 5.4% and 5.7% for 2025 and 2026, respectively
    • Regis expectations are at 4.8% and 5.5% for 2025 and 2026
