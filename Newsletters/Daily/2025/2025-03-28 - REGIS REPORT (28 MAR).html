28 March 2025

We cover two names for today’s FY24 results wrap, Monde (MONDE PM) and Cebu Air (CEB PM). For Monde, recall that it already provided a glimpse of FY24 outcomes when it released “guidance” a few weeks ago. The actual results disclosed yesterday don’t stray far from what was “guided”.

 

  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) (=) – “Core” income as reported by management rose 29% to P9.8bn, in line with expectations. Large impairment charges related to its Meat Alternative business Quorn wiped out nearly all of that though. We estimate that the “backstop” provided by members of the controlling family on the value of Quorn is now fully depleted based on current values.  Notwithstanding the challenges faced due to Quorn, the core APAC BFB business is doing well, with both the noodle and biscuit categories showing healthy growth.

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) – Removing P2.2bn worth of one-off (realized) gains from sale-and-leaseback deals and FX adj, CEB’s FY24 core profit fell 50% to P3.25bn, falling short of both consensus and Regis forecasts. While results disappointed, management’s decision last year to accelerate investments for “operational resiliency” amidst the ongoing global aircraft shortage already began to bear fruit in 4Q24, with revenue growth accelerating to 28% in 4Q24 (vs. 11% in 9M) and EBIT growing by 43% YoY (vs. -8% in 9M). Earnings prospects for 2025 also look very promising, given the sustained topline improvements and cost tailwinds from fuel and FX.

Following a number of confusing media reports, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) clarified that it is not changing the rule imposing a minimum 20% public float on new listings. However, it did not rule out granting exemptions to this rule (“exemptive relief”) on specific cases.  Among the ones expected to seek a lower minimum float is Mynt, the fintech firm partly owned by Globe (GLO PM). Mynt has been reported to be eyeing a listing within the year.

 

+++

 

Gaming agency PAGCOR is again cutting fees (inexplicably) it is collecting from online gaming operations. It is reducing fees on (a) e-casino games from the integrated resort operators (like Bloomberry (BLOOM PM)) as well as (b) bingo (and e-bingo) games run by the likes of Digiplus (PLUS PM).

 

Meanwhile, it looks like Thailand is moving forward with its plan to allow the entry of casino operators.

MARKET
SEC releases statement on minimum public float requirement for IPOs
  • In a press release, the Securities and Exchange Commission reiterates its firm stance on the 20% minimum public float requirement for companies seeking to list
    • The SEC highlighted the float requirement's importance in enhancing market liquidity, improving price discovery, reducing the likelihood of price manipulation, and reducing ownership concentration
  • It was also stated that after discussions with the PSE, "the SEC has allowed, by way of exemptive relief, an initial public float of 15%, subject to strict criteria."
    • This clarifies SEC Commissioner McJill Bryant Fernandez and PSE President Ramon Monzon's previous comments last week on allowing 15% public float over the 20% requirement for large IPOs
  • Companies may apply for this relief to IPO at 15% public float but are required to reach the 20% standard "within less than 24 months from the listing date and only as deemed necessary by the Commission."
  • The exemptive relief covers listing applications that have already been filed by the SEC and PSE
    • As of March 25, the Commission and the Exchange have not received any application for regulatory relief from any potential IPO applicant
GAMING
PAGCOR to lower fees on bingo operators and e-games from integrated resorts
  • PAGCOR will changing the fee structures of 1) traditional bingo, electronic bingo, and sports betting offered by online operators (also called PIGO firms), and 2) e-casino games offered by integrated resorts
  • PAGCOR will lower the fees it charges on Traditional Bingo, eBingo, and sports-betting beginning July 2025 (see table below)
    • The change will affect, at most, 20% of the revenue of Digiplus (PLUS PM).  About 80% of the revenue of PLUS is from e-casino games, which will still be charged 30% of GGR. The remaining 20% of revenue might be charged 2.5ppt to 10ppt less by PAGCOR
    • We estimate this could raise PLUS’ net profit by 5%-10%, holding all other things constant
  • PAGCOR will also lower the fees it charges on e-casino games offered by integrated resorts
    • For integrated resorts that have spent/committed at least US$200mn, they will only be charged 22% of e-casino GGR by PAGCOR (exclusive of 5% franchise tax and audit fees)
      • From 25% currently (exclusive of 5% franchise tax and audit fees)
      • This is lower than the 30% charged on PIGO firms (exclusive of 5% franchise tax audit fees), such as PLUS
    • Despite the 170% growth in FY24 GGR of e-games (to P155bn), Okada Manila’s e-games GGR fell 31% to P292mn
      • This implies that the 5ppt advantage in fee structure (PIGO firms) was not sufficient to gain market share
    • E-casino operations currently comprise only c. 5% of Bloomberry’s (BLOOM PM) GGR
    • BLOOM is targeting to launch an enhanced online platform in 2H25
  • BLOOM is trading at 4.6X FY25E EV/EBITDA while PLUS is trading at 9.5X FY25E P/E (based on consensus forecast)

 

GAMING
Thailand Cabinet approves draft law for casinos and entertainment complexes
  • Thailand’s Cabinet approved a draft law for casinos and entertainment complexes
  • The draft law is expected to be proposed to parliament in July
  • If passed, it will need approval from the Senate and the King
  • Based on the draft regulations:
    • Gaming space can only comprise a maximum of 10% of the entertainment complex
    • A Thai citizen who would like to enter a casino must pay a Baht5,000 entry fee and present a bank statement showing that he/she has Baht50mn (US$1.5mn)
    • Foreigners must show that they are at least 20 years old
TELCOS
Globe sets up JV with Singtel subsidiary in IT
  • Globe disclosed it was combining Yondu, its IT solutions subsidiary, with the local operations of NCS Pte Ltd
    • NCS Pte Ltd is a technology services firm that’s a subsidiary of Singtel
  • Globe will end up owning 49% of Yondu, which will in turn own 100% of NCSI Philippines
  • The press release says the expanded firm will have an Enterprise Value of P1,868m (US$32m)
  • Globe will likely de-consolidate Yondu which will result in a small decrease in revenue
