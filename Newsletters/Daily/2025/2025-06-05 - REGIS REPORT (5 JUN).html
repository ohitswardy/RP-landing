REGIS REPORT

05 June 2025

The Lower House approved on third reading the bill mandating a P200/day increase in minimum wages.  The Senate has a similar, counterpart bill calling for a smaller P100/day increase. 

 

The P200/day increase, if it does get implemented, would indeed be a very sizeable jump over current wages. For context, P200/day would be equivalent to ~30% of current minimum wage in NCR and up to >50% of that in other areas.

 

Both Houses now have eight calendar days (less session days) to agree to a reconciled bill and approve the measure before Congress adjourns.

 

+++

 

CPI inflation for May dipped further to 1.3% from the 1.4% print for April. The lower headline inflation figure is being led declining commodity prices. Rice prices were again down MoM (-2.4%) as were energy-linked costs (e.g., electricity, transportation). This CPI outcome within Street expectations.

 

This inflation print further strengthens the likelihood that the Bangko Sentral (BSP) Monetary Board will reduce policy rates further at its next meeting on June 19.

 

 

+++

 

Following the public release of the final version of the Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act (CMEPA), we now see only a couple of minor items were vetoed. The CMEPA, whose most relevant provision for those investing in the listed equity market in the Philippines is the cut in stock transaction tax (STT), is supposed to take effect on July 1.

 

+++

 

Ayala Corp (AC PM) subsidiary Integrated Microelectronics (IMI PM) is divesting its facility in the Czech Republic as part of its rationalization program.

We update our estimates and target price (TP) for convenience store operator Philippine Seven (SEVN PM), reducing our EPS estimates by 16-20% and our TP by 14% (to P60/share). The latest results were disappointing, which we believe were a result of the mandated POGO exodus and signal softness for this year. We nonetheless think the growth runway for the business remains long and retain our Buy rating.

MACRO
House approves P200 wage hike after third reading
  • House Bill No. 11376 or the Wage Hike For Minimum Wage Workers Act was approved on third reading last Tuesday
  • Under the bill, the daily rate of minimum wage workers in the private sector will be increased by P200 per day
    • This includes those in contractual and sub-contractual arrangements, whether agricultural or nonagricultural
    • Note that the minimum wage in NCR is P608-645 so the Lower House version could raise minimum wages by 31-33% 
  • The bill also warned that the wage hike must not lead to the reduction of existing allowances and benefits
    • Not adhering to the law will result in fines ranging from P100K to P500K and the imprisonment of responsible officers
  • The proposed P200 per day wage hike is higher than the Senate's version of the bill (SB 2534), which proposes a P100-daily minimum wage increase
  • Note that the bill has been passed by both houses of Congress, but will only become law after being ratified by both chambers after going through a bicameral conference committee before it is signed by the President
    • The Congress will adjourn for a final time on June 13
MACRO/MARKET
Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act sees vetoed changes; law to take effect July 1
  • President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. signed Republic Act No. 12214 or the Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act (CMEPA) last May 30
    • Some key features of the law would be the reduction of stock transaction tax 0.6% to 0.1%, documentary stamp tax (DST) on original share issues lowered to 0.75% from 1%, and DST exemption for mutual fund and UITF share deals (original issuance, redemption, or other disposition of shares)
  • President Marcos vetoed certain provisions prior to signing
    • Marcos vetoed the proposed removal of the tax exemption of foreign investors' income from their transactions with foreign currency deposit units (FCDUs)
      • The removal of such tax exemption would have implied higher costs for dollar-denominated borrowings
    • The President also vetoed the provision imposing DST liability on betters of the Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) games
      • This was done to avoid negative impacts on revenues from legal gaming activities which are a funding source for official gov't programs
  • According to the Dept. of Finance, CMEPA was projected to raise P41.9bn in gov't revenues by 2030 but after adjusting for the vetoed items, the law is expected to generate P25bn by 2030
  • The law will take effect on July 1, following publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one newspaper of general circulation
INDUSTRIALS
IMI exits Czech Republic
  • IMI’s wholly-owned unit, Cooperatief IMI Europe UA, has a greed to sell 100% of IMI Czech Republic for Eur10mn
    • Buyer is a subsidiary of China-listed Kebodo Technology
    • Sale is part of IMI’s restructuring and footprint rationalization program, initiated in 2H24
    • Share sale agreement is still subject to closing requirements, including regulatory approvals
  • Divestment would reduce overhead and administrative expenses
    • IMI is consolidating its Europe operations into its Bulgaria and Serbia facilities
  • IMI turned a profit in 1Q25, despite a drop in revenues due to its cost rationalization efforts
  • IMI is a 52%-owned unit of Ayala Corp. (AC PM)

TELCOS
DITO clarifies visa probe involving contractor staff; expects minor service impact
  • On May 29, Bureau of Immigration agents inspected DITO Telecommunity’s Taguig office and brought 39 foreign nationals in for visa and work-permit verification
  • DITO CME’s June 4 SEC 17-C disclosure clarifies that the detainees work for an independent 5G-support contractor and that DITO has no equity stake in, or employment relationship with, that firm
  • DITO says the workers were onsite under valid service contracts and expects their visas/work permits to be confirmed after review
  • Management says any service disruption should be minor although they acknowledged that the specialists have scarce skills that could slow some support tasks
  • Overall network operations are expected to remain stable as alternative resources are deployed
