REGIS REPORT

09 May 2025

 

The country’s GDP growth clocked in at 5.4% YoY, which was disappointing against consensus (5.7%) and tracking behind our 6.0% FY25E forecast, and well below the government’s own lofty 6-8% goal. Domestic demand was strong, with Private Consumption accelerating to 5.3% from 4.7% in 4Q24, in line with our thesis of a consumer recovery this year. Government spending surged 18.7% as agencies rushed to beat the 45-day ban on new spending approvals ahead of the 12 May elections. What was a bit tepid though was Investments, which grew just 4.0% YoY. The big drag came from Net Imports, which knocked 210bps off growth. On the supply side, the Services sector grew 6.3%, Industry was up 4.6%, while Agri was up 2.2%. The construction sector remained tepid, up 3.8%, which was expected given oversupply in both office and residential condos.

 

We expect GDP to accelerate in 2Q25 and through the rest of the year, given a) sharply lower inflation, b) election spending, and c) the recent and expected interest rate cuts. We remain bullish on the consumer and banking sectors.

CONSUMER
URC 1Q25: Modest Bounce for core Domestic BCF

1Q25 profit -2% YoY—ahead

URC’s recurring profit in 1Q25 was down 2% YoY to P4.1bn, which appears to be trending ahead of Regis and consensus expectations. Consolidated EBIT (+1% YoY to P5.5bn) also looks ahead of expectations. Revenues look in line with expectations at P45.3bn, up 7% YoY. The downbeat 2H24 results as well as the modest guidance from management appear to have kept expectations for this year rather subdued (and thus less challenging to meet).

 

Volume-based recovery for Domestic BCF

Domestic BCF saw a +4% YoY improvement in terms of revenue, driven by volume. The top line increase was led by double-digit volume growth in RTD beverages, snacks, and confectionary. Snacks, which were notably affected by downtrading during elevated inflation periods last year, saw recovery due to value-based pricing and promotional activities. Apart from snacks, the company prioritized A&P spend for coffee and noodles as those categories are still facing competitive challenges. Market shares across categories were generally stable, but 10-20bp decreases were observed in snacks, biscuits, and noodles (within the margin of error).

 

International BCF has upward momentum

Growth at the international BCF business continues to be faster than the domestic business, with revenues up 10% YoY to P9.6bn. Vietnam was the primary growth driver posting double digit growth with market share gains. Malaysia and Indonesia also performed well despite Ramadan season in March. These positive trends offset the headwinds in Thailand and Cambodia as they face ongoing retailer negotiations and border distribution issues, respectively.

 

EBIT margin for international expanded significantly (+120bp YoY to 14.1%). Management intends to reinvest gains in the international business as the priority will continue to be volume and top line growth

 

Higher output and sales boost Non-BCF sales

Non-BCF (Agro-Industrial and Commodities) sales were up 8% YoY during the quarter as both refined sugar and flour delivered higher volumes. The SURE (sugar and renewables) segment benefited from strong refining output and higher external sales. EBIT margins normalized (-3.4ppt YoY) from last year’s high-margin base and sugar price volatility.

 

Execution will be key

Management reiterated its previous guidance, which is for full-year revenue growth to be in the high single-digit range, while profit growth is expected to be in the mid single-digits. The lower profit growth vs revenue growth is a consequence of management’s plans to increase spending this year. We share management’s view that increased efforts (spending), whether in brand-building A&P or product development, are an imperative in order to lay the foundation for future gains. Whether the execution will live up to the plans, we should see in the next few quarters (or year).

 

POWER
ACEN: Extended repairs cause 16% YoY drop in 1Q25 profit
  • 1Q25 profit drops 16% YoY to P1.95bn – Behind Street estimates. ACEN reported its 1Q25 net income fell 28% YoY to P1.95bn. Adjusting for last year’s one-offs, profit would have declined by 16% YoY, owing to (a) the extended repair of its wind farms in north Luzon, (b) lower solar irradiance in the Philippines and Australia, as well as (c) lower spot prices. The company also began recognizing depreciation and interest expenses on over 1GW of new capacity, which had been just on commissioning phase in the same period last year.  The results are trending below Street forecasts.

 

  • Lower plant output, spot prices weigh on profitability of PH assets. Att. EBITDA of its PH assets dropped 15% YoY to P2.1bn, pulled down by lower output from its solar and wind plants, as well as lower spot prices.  ACEN missed the peak season for wind due to the prolonged repair of the Pagudpud and Capa wind plants (collectively represents ~6% of overall operating capacity), which were damaged from a typhoon in the previous quarter.  Management estimates revenue opportunity losses of P800-850mn from this incident in 1Q25 alone.     

 

  • International units improve, bar Australia. Its international units collectively performed better, thanks to a strong wind regime in Vietnam and new capacity additions in Vietnam, India, and Australia.  These factors more than offset lower output and spot prices in Australia.   

 

  • Better 2H outlook; Adding ~800 MW of capacity this year.  Given that the Philippine government deferred plant maintenance activities of thermal plants in the run-up to the national elections, management reckons spot prices today are artificially low but should rise in 2H. It also hopes to complete the repair of the wind farms in time for the peak season in 4Q. Lastly, ACEN expects to operationalize ~800MW of capacity this year in Australia, Vietnam, and India.

 

  • More on the stock rights offering (SRO). Despite anti-RE policy noise coming from the USA, management believes that its strong growth prospects and presence in markets where renewable energy remains a key priority should entice participation of minority investors in the upcoming SRO, citing its dominant market share of 30% in Australia’s RE pipeline.  Assuming it raises P30bn at P2.30/sh, we estimate it will need at least 82% of minority shareholders to participate in the SRO to maintain a public float above 20%. Management also emphasized that it does not intend to issue more equity in the foreseeable future, as it expects to more than double its operating capacity by 2027 from ~3GW in 2024, which should allow them to generate cash to re-invest in more projects. 

 

 

BANKS
Chinabank - 1Q25 up 10%, but…

Some additional detail from the briefing this morning. The key takeaways were a) provision expense was unusually low, with credit cost at just 12bps; and b) lower income tax accounted for nearly all of the 10% YoY increase in net profit. It is tempting to call the results “poor quality”. But it is difficult to gauge the bank’s “normalized” level of profitability with this kind of volatility in the provision / tax expense.

 

Some key takeaways from the call:

  • Loan growth of 19% was pretty even across consumer (+19%) and “business” (+18%) segments. The bank is guiding for “low-teens” – ie 11-12% – for 2025E. CFO Patrick Cheng said there was “no need to be stretching right now”, considering the headwinds (he specifically mentioned the real estate oversupply issues).
  • NIMs were down 8bps QoQ, with asset yields down 5bps, and funding costs up 5bps. Note that the NIMs still included cross-currency swaps, which the CFO estimated was steady at about 100bps addition to NIMs. They’re already seeing positive impact on (lower) deposit costs in 2Q from the lower policy rates, and the RRR cuts in March. The bank may also tap the market for bonds, taking advantage of lower interest rates. They could start winding down the cross-currency swaps, which would wreak havoc on the quarterly NIM data…
  • Opex was up 17% YoY, driven by a 25% jump in manpower costs. Management attributed this to the signing bonus on the recently-concluded CBA with the bank’s labor union. Without it, opex growth would have been a more moderate 14%. IT costs were up more than three-fold, and are now c7-8% of total opex.
  • Provision expense was down 6%, from an already low base in 1Q24. Credit cost for the quarter was just 12bps, far lower than the guidance of 40bps for the full year. Mr Cheng said there were no major write-backs in 1Q25, but NPL ratio was down 10bps QoQ and NPL coverage was up 400bps QoQ.

 

Given the noise of a) the cross-currency swaps, b) wild swings in provision expense, and c) very low tax expense in 1Q, it is difficult to determine what the normalized net profit / ROE of the bank is. Based on its 1Q25 book, the stock is trading on about 1.3x trailing P/bk, about the same as its (much) larger sister bank BDO, on similar ROEs.

MACRO
1Q25 GDP grows 5.4%; falls short of expectations
  • GDP grew at 5.4% in 1Q, lower than the 5.9% growth in 1Q24 but a tad higher than the 5.3% growth rate in 4Q24
    • The 5.4% GDP growth is below consensus expectations of 5.7%
    • The print fell short of the government’s 6–7% target range, implying that growth of at least 6.2% for the rest of the year is needed to meet the 6% full-year goal
  • On the demand side, the strong growth in private and gov't consumption was offset by the deterioration of net imports to -2.1% which contracted sharply from -0.1% the previous quarter.
    • Private consumption saw improvement to 5.3% in 1Q25, better than the 4.7%growth seen a year ago and the previous quarter
    • Gov't spending jumped 18.7%, much higher than the 2.6% in 1Q24 and 9.0% in 4Q24, likely from frontloaded infra spending ahead of the 45-day election ban on public works that started last Mar 28
    • Capital formation grew by 4% in the first quarter, faster than the 0.8% last year but slower than the 5.5% the previous quarter
    • Exports grew 6.2% for the quarter, while imports rose 9.9%
  • On the supply side, growth was led by the services sector at 6.3% but was slower than the 7.0% expansion in 1Q24
    • The agri sector exhibited a positive turnaround growing 2.2% in the quarter, after contracting for three consecutive quarters previously
    • The industry sector slowed to 4.5% from 5.2% a year ago but was unchanged from the fourth quarter
      • Manufacturing saw a slowdown to 4.1% growth but was slower than the 4.6% last year
  • Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated the gov't remains confident in hitting its 6-7% full-year growth target despite global and domestic headwinds
    • He also highlighted the resilience of the Philippine economy amidst heightened global uncertainty and noted that the presence of clear signals of accelerating demand should bolster growth
  • The Department Budget Coordinating Committee (DBCC) will meet next week to review the country's economic targets as a prerequisite for the 2026 national budget
  • The Bloomberg consensus median forecast for 2025 is 5.9%
    • Regis expects GDP to be 6.0% for the full-year
