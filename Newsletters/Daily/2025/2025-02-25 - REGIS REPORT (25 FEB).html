Check out our thoughts on the results of two of the country’s biggest utility firms, Meralco (MER PM) and Manila Water (MWC PM), both of whom announced their FY24 numbers yesterday. 

 

  • Meralco (MER PM) (+) – Core income rose 22% to P45bn for FY24, boosted by contributions from the generation segment. Electricity volumes for its regulated operations rose 6% for the year, as growth normalized to +4% in 4Q.  The medium-term growth looks promising, with the LNG assets (“Chromite”) and Terra Solar likely to add over P10bn in profits annually.  Short-term, the big event will be the rate reset for the 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) which may happen by June, but we wouldn’t advise holding one’s breath for that one.  More below.

 

 

  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (+) - Manila Water’s FY24 recurring profit to common of P12.3bn (+46% YoY) came in line with consensus estimates but was well above Regis’ forecast. The quality of the beat was subpar, as it largely came from under-spending at its main East zone unit.  We believe the under-spending could have long-term consequences for the East zone’s regulatory value.

+++

 

The Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE PM) has reportedly asked Emperador (EMI PM) to explain the discrepancy between its Public Ownership Report and the financial statements of its controlling shareholder Alliance Global (AGI PM).  Its Public Ownership Report claims AGI holds 79.5% of EMI while AGI’s financials claim its ownership is 81%.

 

If AGI indeed owns 81% of EMI, then EMI’s public float will have to be less than 20%. Twenty percent (20%) is the minimum public float for a company to be part of the 30-member PSEi (“PCOMP”) and EMI is currently one of the thirty.

 

If EMI’s public float is indeed below 20%, it would trigger EMI’s deletion from the PSEi. On the other hand, if AGI’s ownership is really only 79.5% and not 81% (thus public float is over 20%), then AGI’s auditors will have some explaining to do as the 81% figure has been there since 2022.

POWER
Meralco FY24 core profit up 22% to P45bn
  • Core income up 22% to P45bn.  Meralco reported FY24 of P45.1bn for FY24, up 22% YoY and ahead of Street estimates. The earnings were lifted by stronger contributions from its generation assets as well as healthy volumes from core distribution utility (DU) business.

 

  • DU unit sales grew 6%. Units sales were up 6.4% for the year with residential volumes leading the way (+9%). Note the pace of growth normalized to +4.4% in 4Q, after a blistering 1H24.  The industrial segment still managed to show positive YoY growth (+0.9%) in the last quarter.  The DU business delivered 62% of core income for the year.

 

The company is guiding for 4% to 4.5% DU volume growth this FY25.

 

  • Lift from generation. The unregulated business, the largest component of which is power generation, contributed P17bn, 38% of core income, and up ~28%. We note 4Q contributions saw a nice jump from GBP (now “MThermal”) and MGreen. The former began selling ancillary power services last year.  The other unregulated businesses, though, seems to have fared poorly.

 

For FY25, the acquisition of the LNG assets (“Chromite”) from San Miguel (SMC PM) is likely to power earnings even higher. Management expects contributions of P5-6bn from the Chromite acquisition on an annualized basis. Meralco’s economic ownership of Chromite is now 40.2% following the completion of the deal a few weeks ago.

 

  • Franchise extension almost done.  The extension of Meralco’s congressional franchise by another 25 years appears practically done. Meralco says a reconciled House-Senate bill is already set to be enrolled before it is sent to Malacañang for the President’s signature.

 

  • Rate reset imminent?  The ERC appears to be rushing to complete Meralco’s long-delayed reset for its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) by June. Meralco’s application seeks a ~25% increase in its tariff (P1.69/kwh vs P1.35/kwh) premised on a regulatory return (WACC) of 14.6% and a chunky P246bn (US$4.2bn) capex program over four years.  We reckon there’s a lot of room for that applied tariff to be cut.

 

  • Dividends of P13.736/sh.  Meralco declared dividends of P13.736/sh, equivalent to 2.8% yield.  This final dividend, combined with the one declared 3Q24 (=P10.295/sh), is equivalent to ~60% of prior year’s income.  Meralco typically declares dividends twice a year.
BANKS
BDO FY24 net profit rises 12%, in line
  • BDO’s FY24 earnings rose 12% to P82bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts
    • 4Q24 net profit grew 10% to P21.4bn
  • FY24 Pre-provisioning operating profit rose 4% to P117.7bn, in line with REGIS forecast
    • 4Q24 fell 6% YoY, up 3% QoQ
  • FY24 net interest income rose 8% to P187bn
    • 4Q24 net interest income grew 7% YoY (+3% QoQ)
  • FY24 net interest margin fell to 4.35%, from 4.37% in FY23
    • 4Q24 net interest margin rose to 4.31%, from 4.23% in 3Q24
  • Gross customer loans grew 13% YoY (to P13.2trn), the same pace as 3Q24
    • Consumer +16%, middle-market +12%, corporate +13%
    • This is slightly higher than the 12.2% loan growth of the industry
  • Deposits rose 6% YoY to P3.8trn, slower than the 10% YoY growth in 3Q24
    • CASA ratio stood at 71%
  • Strong asset quality allowed the bank to lower provisions – FY24 down 14%, 4Q24 fell 31% YoY
  • The NPL ratio was flat QoQ at 1.83% (1.82% in 3Q24)
    • This is lower than the industry’s NPL ratio of 3.27%
  • NPL cover was also stable QoQ at 145% (144% in 3Q24)
  • Restructured loans fell to 1.3% of total loans, from 1.9% in 2023
  • The CET1 ratio was 14.1%
    • Parent CET1 at 13.7%
  • Return on common equity hit 15.1% for FY24
  • In 2025, BDO is targeting:
  • Low-teens loan growth
    • Provincial areas and consumer loans will likely grow more quickly
    • REGIS FY25E: +12%
  • Stable NIM
    • The bank expects lower RRR and rate cuts to offset each other
    • The lending environment is competitive
    • REGIS expects a 10-basis point reduction in 2025 NIM
  • Mid-teens growth in fee income
    • REGIS FY25E +8%
  • Deceleration in opex growth
    • REGIS FY25E: opex +7%
  • Capital ratios to be maintained
MARKET
PSE flags Emperador's float noncompliance
  • The PSE has issued letters to both Alliance Global Inc. (AGI PM) and Emperador (EMI PM), the former's listed liqour-arm, requiring them to explain the discrepancy in EMI's current public float level
    • This comes after AGI reported an 81% ownership stake in EMI as of its latest quarterly report for the 9M24 (page 22)—implying 19% public float which is below the PSE's 20% minimum free float rule
    • While, in PSE EDGE (Electronic Disclosure Generation Technology), EMI's free float level is stated to be 20.1%
  • PSE President Ramon Monzon commented in an article stating that this issue was likely a typographical error but regardless as aforementioned required the two companies to explain the discrepancy
    • He also mentioned that both companies have replied stating that EMI's latest public ownership report which lists AGI's stake at 79.48% is the correct figure
    • Notably, there has been no released disclosure by AGI or EMI as of this morning explaining the discrepancy
