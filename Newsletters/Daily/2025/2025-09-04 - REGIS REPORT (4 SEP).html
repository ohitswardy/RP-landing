REGIS REPORT

04 September 2025

Government agency pronouncements dominate today’s news. And some of them leave us scratching our heads. These include 1) a proposal to lower the VAT from the current 12% to 10% at a time when the fiscal deficit and debt levels are stubbornly high, and 2) a Department of Energy (DOE) proposal to shut down all coal mining and coal-fired power plants, when more than 40% of the country’s electricity generation comes from coal.

 

The are even more odd, given conflicting government actions. For example the Department of Finance (DOF) is pushing for the urgent passage of NEW taxes, including a tax on single-use plastics, a tax amnesty program, and minimum global tax. Meanwhile, the DOE itself just recently approved Meralco’s (MER PM) huge 1,200 MW Atimonan coal-fired greenfield plant. A study may likely compel the government to revise its RE ambitions in line with practical realities. 

 

 

+ + +

 

To the upside, the Department of Transportation (DOTr) is pushing through with the awarding of the segments of the P489bn Metro Manila Subway project, and the P229bn North-South Commuter Railway project. The country desperately needs more infrastructure. Hopefully, there’s enough money to pay for all of it…

MACRO
Dept. of Finance pushes for Congress to prioritize key tax reforms
  • The Dept. of Finance (DOF) wants the 20th Congress to support the gov't's fiscal consolidation effort by prioritizing several tax measures
  • Finance Undersecretary Karlo Adriano stated that they want to tap new revenue streams to ease the limited fiscal space as the debt-to-GDP ratio currently stands at 63% as of 1H25
    • He emphasizes the need for a fiscal buffer amidst heightened global uncertainties and in case of a crisis
  • Some key tax measures being pushed by the DOF are as follows:
    • Single-use plastics excise tax
      • P150/kg on single-use plastics with an annual 4% increase
        • Estimated to generate ~P12bn annually for the first five years of implementation
    • Tax amnesty
      • Proposes a general tax amnesty on various taxes, at 2% of total assets (as of Dec 2024) or 5% of net worth, whichever is higher
      • Estate tax amnesty, which expired in June 2025, is sought to be extended to June 2028
      • The gov't collected P2.7bn in estate tax amnesty in 2024 and P4.2bn in 7M25
    • Global minimum tax (GMT)
      • 15% domestic top-up tax for multinational companies with a global revenue exceeding EUR€750mn (~P50bn) in two of the four preceding fiscal years
      • The Philippines, as part of the OECD’s Base Erosion and Profit Shifting program, risks losing revenues without a legislated 15% GMT
      • The absence of a GMT framework has already cost an estimated P163bn annually since 2021, as taxes may be paid in other jurisdictions

 

 

MACRO
Proposed lower 10% VAT under review by House panel
  • The "VAT Reduction Act of 2025" or House Bill 4302 proposes to reduce the current value-added tax (VAT) rate by two percentage points from 12% to 10%
    • The bill, if passed, is estimated to reduce annual VAT collections by ~P200bn, which is equivalent to ~P7,000 savings per household each year
  • Congressman Leandro Leviste said he authored and filed the bill to further address inflation and provide relief to millions of Filipino households
    • He highlighted that increased consumption and improved tax compliance could offset the revenue shortfall
  • The bill includes a clause that allows the President, upon recommendation of the Dept. of Finance (DOF) and Budget Committee, to temporarily restore the VAT rate to 12% in case the gov't's deficit exceeds target projections
  • House Committee on Ways and Means Chairman Romiro Quimbo stated that the panel will give it serious consideration as "any measure that eases the tax burden [or makes our tax system more progressive] is very welcome"
  • DOF Undersecretary Karlo Adriano, however, warns of several billions of lost revenue if VAT is reduced with no new taxes in place
    • Leviste added that he may push for a "wealth" tax to offset possible revenue losses
  • The Philippines imposes the highest VAT rate in Southeast Asia at 12%
    • Vietnam and Cambodia impose a 10% VAT; Indonesia, 11%; Singapore, 9%; Thailand, 7%; Malaysia, Laos, and Myanmar 5-7%
  • Recall that the VAT rate was raised to 12% from 10% back in 2005
    • VAT collections have grown from P157bn in 2005 to P1.2trn in 2024

 

 

 

POWER
Government mulls shutdown of coal mines, power plants
  • The Department of Energy (DOE) plans to conduct a study to evaluate the impact of a permanent closure of coal mines and coal-fired power plants in the country
    • According to DOE director Michael Sinocruz on the sidelines of the 2025 Philippine Energy Transition Dialogue
    • Study may start next year and will focus on the possible impact on workers and host communities
  • Coal-fired plants account for 45% of nationwide dependable capacity and 62% of generation
    • Renewables account for 31% of nationwide dependable capacity and 22% of generation
    • Government aims to raise the share of renewables to 35% of overall generation by 2030
POWER
Visayas, Mindanao power spot prices jump in August; Luzon stays muted
  • Nationwide WESM prices climbed ~15% MoM to P4.59/kwh in August
    • According to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP)
  • In Luzon, WESM prices slipped by 4% MoM to P3.76/kwh in August
    • Available supply increased by 0.7% MoM to 14,646 MW while demand grew by 1.8% to 9,882 MW
  • In Visayas, spot prices climbed 45.7% MoM to P6.40/kwh
    • Supply fell 5% MoM to 2,405 MW while demand increased by 1.5% MoM to 2,027 MW
    • Cited outages of several coal plants
  • In Mindanao, spot prices jumped 75% MoM to P6.66/kwh from P3.80/kwh
    • Supply fell 3% MoM to 3,561 MW while demand rose 1.9% MoM to 2,144 MW
    • Cited forced and planned outages of coal plants

Transport Secretary pushing through with major transport projects
  • Acting Transport Secretary Giovanni Lopez is aiming to award all segments of the P489bn Metro Manila Subway project by November
    • Remaining three segments (Kalayaan to Bonifacio, Lawton to Senate and the airport line) are currently being negotiated
    • Project is expected to be completed by 2032
  • He also expects to begin the bidding process for the P229bn O&M contract for the North-South Commuter Railway within the year
