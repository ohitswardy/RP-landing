+++

Jollibee (JFC PM) announced it was buying a Taiwanese restaurant chain specializing in soups. The acquisition is small (US$3m) but we have to wonder about the wisdom of buying another small brand, even if it is profitable.

+++

Meralco (MER PM) subsidiary Pacific Light secured the right to build a power plant (at least 600MW CCGT facility) in Singapore. The facility will be able to run on hydrogen and will be operational in 2029. The cost of the project will not need funding from Meralco.

Meanwhile, the power regulator says it will need to review the proposed acquisition by Meralco and Aboitiz Power (AP PM) of the LNG assets of San Miguel (SMC PM) (aka the Chromite deal).  The transaction has not been closed and was announced last year after Meralco awarded a power supply agreement (PSA) to one of the power plants (Excellent Energy or EERI) that’s subject of the transaction. We would not be surprised if the delayed completion of the deal results in a revision in the pricing / valuation.

We understand that one unit (440MW) of EERI finally began testing last month after some delays. EERI was scheduled to be operational in Dec under its contract with Meralco.

+++

December inflation came in at 2.9%, higher than market expectations (2.6%) after a jump in prices for food (but not rice). Food inflation rose to 3.5% after a surge in vegetable inflation (14.2%) even as rice inflation sank to 0.8% (vs 5.1%).

CONSUMER
Jollibee's Milksha to buy 70% stake in Taiwan's Moon Moon Food
  • Milkshop International Co., Ltd. (Milksha), a 51% owned subsidiary of Jollibee Foods Corporation (JFC PM), signed an agreement to buy a 70% stake in Tien Hsia Sheng Co., Ltd. (Moon Moon Food)
    • Yung-Cheng Lai, Moon Moon Food Founder and CEO, will retain ownership of the 30% minority interest
    • Once the transaction is completed, Moon Moon Food will be consolidated into Milksha's portfolio and financial reports. While JFC will take on 51% of any acquisition impact to Milksha
  • The deal will be made through a share sale and purchase agreement with Milksha acquiring 980,000 Moon Moon Food shares at NT$105.92 (P187.80) per share with the total price at NT$103.8mn
    • Equivalent to US$3.15mn or P183.9mn
    • The purchase price was based on a multiples-based valuation on the FY23 NPAT of Moon Moon Food
  • Moon Moon Food is currently operating a profitable business with 13 restaurants in Taiwan and has recently opened its 1st international location in Singapore in 2024
    • The restaurant is a popular restaurant brand in Taiwan specializing in Chinese wellness soups, having seven consecutive years of the Michelin Bib Gourmand award from 2018 to present
  • As of Sept 2024, JFC has 9,598 stores in its store network globally—3,340 in the Philippines and 6,258 international branches
POWER
Meralco’s PacificLight to build hydrogen-ready gas power plant in Singapore
  • PacificLight Power Pte Ltd. was awarded the right to build, own, and operate a hydrogen-ready Combined Cycle Gas Turbine (“CCGT”) facility in Jurong Island by the Energy Market Authority of Singapore
    • The project will be the largest single H-class CCGT plant in Singapore, with capacity of at least 600MW; Site can accommodate a second CCGT unit
    • The plant is scheduled for commercial operations in January 2029
  • Recall that MGen Gas Energy Hldgs. Inc. President and CEO Yari Miralao previously said that the company plans to invest US$900mn if it wins the project (see here)
  • PacificLight is a 58%-owned unit of Meralco’s (MER PM) PowerGen Corp. (MGen) while the balance is owned by First Pacific
    • It currently operates an 830MW LNG plant in Jurong Island
  • Other greenfield projects of PacificLight are the following:
    • 100MW hydrogen-ready gas turbine, with commercial operations slated to begin by 2Q25
    • 600MW solar farm in Bulan Island in Indonesia (granted conditional approval by the Singaporean government)
POWER
ERC to review Aboitiz-Meralco-San Miguel US$3.3bn LNG deal
  • The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) said it will review the joint acquisition of Meralco/Aboitiz (Chromite) of two gas-fired power plants and an LNG terminal of San Miguel Global Power (SMGP) to ensure compliance with market share limitations
    • According to ERC Chairperson and CEO Monalisa Dimalanta
    • Under the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) of 2001, no company or related group can own, operate, or control more than 30% of the installed generation capacity of a grid and 25% on a national scale
    • Recall that the Philippine Competition Commission approved the deal last month but subject to certain conditions, including PCC oversight of the Competitive Selection Process (CSP)
  • Under the US$3.3bn deal, Meralco and Aboitiz will jointly invest in SMGP’s 1,278-megawatt (MW) Ilijan power plant, its new 1,320-MW combined cycle power facility (Excellent Energy), and its LNG import and regasification terminal
    • MER will own a 40% stake in the gas-fired plants and LNG terminal; SMC’s stake will be diluted to 33% (from 100%) while AP will own 27%
    • Transaction will be funded through a leveraged buyout, wherein 50% of the funds will come from debt that will be injected into the operating companies
MACRO
Maharlika plans first investment in 1Q25
  • The Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) is expected to make its first investment this quarter, most likely in the energy sector
    • According to MIC President and CEO, Rafael D. Consing, Jr.
    • Its plan will be up for board approval on January 9
  • Mr. Consing said he believes that investments in the energy sector, particularly transmission lines, would be “most impactful.”
    • MIC would like to buy into the national grid, if it’s able to, or execute on its signed MOUs with SPUGS (small power utilities group) in Mindoro and Palawan
    • State-owned, National Power Corp. is currently doing an inventory of all its assets in Mindoro and MIC “will have to sit down and determine which part of those assets it would need as part of the expansion of these lines.”
  • MIC previously said that it will invest in energy, food security, healthcare, and resource development, particularly mining
    • If its due diligence on the healthcare sector works out well, it could invest in 1H
