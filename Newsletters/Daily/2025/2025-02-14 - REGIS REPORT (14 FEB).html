REGIS REPORT

14 February 2025

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) appeared to have surprised the market with its inaction yesterday when it left the policy rate unchanged at 5.75%. The Street had been expecting a rate reduction, but those expectations may have been altered by the hot CPI figures from the US the day (night) before.

 

The BSP Governor is still sticking to his “easing cycle” narrative, however, and continues to guide for 50bp of rate cuts this year as well as a likely 2ppt cut in reserve requirements (RRR) within 1H25.

 

We believe the upcoming inflation numbers are likely to be more subdued and would thus offer more elbow room for the BSP to cut.

In this volatile investing environment, dividend yield has become a key attraction for investors. And relatively stable dividends are arguably the main attraction of Philippine REITs, which has led to them being among the big outperformers last year (>20% return over 2024).

 

As they continue to grow with asset injections, it’s becoming apparent the REITs as a group will become even more prominent.

 

We now officially dive into the sub-sector with a coverage initiation of AREIT (AREIT PM), the largest Philippine REIT in terms of leasable area, assets under management, and market cap. Among Philippine REITs, we reckon it has superior portfolio occupancy and a track record of consistent DPS growth. On the other hand, its portfolio is heavily concentrated in the office segment, which has a lackluster outlook, and our forecast DPS CAGR of 5% in 2024-2026E represents a deceleration from historical trend. We thus are of the view that valuations are relatively fair and initiate coverage of AREIT with a Hold rating and a target price of P38.80/share.

MACRO
BSP keeps rates steady
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) held interest rates unchanged leaving the target reverse repurchase rate at 5.75% which was against consensus expectations of a 25 bps cut
    • This marks the first pause since having cut rates by 25 bps at each of the last three meetings since Aug 2024
  • BSP Governor Remolona stated that uncertainties on the outlook of inflation and growth was the main rationale for keeping rates steady
    • "Normally, we would have cut further…" but uncertainty over global economic policies and their impact on the domestic economy needs to be assessed in more detail
  • Remolona emphasized that the central bank remains in the easing cycle and that it is a matter of when to cut in the remaining five meetings
    • He also added that the central bank will likely continue reducing rates by 25 bps at a time and may cut by up to 50 bps in total for the year
    • A rate cut is still "on the table" for the next Monetary Board meeting on April 3
  • On the reserve requirement ratio (RRR), Remolona also gave guidance for an RRR cut from 7% to 5% with the timing likely around 1H25
  • Notably, the central bank slightly raised its risk-adjusted inflation forecast this year from 3.4% to 3.5% but kept its 2026 projection at 3.7%
  • Bloomberg median consensus currently expects 75 bps worth of rate cuts this year
    • We expect the BSP to cut its policy rate by 50 bps to 5.25% this year

    • He previously served as Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) President and CEO and Presidential Adviser for COVID-19 Response under the Duterte administration
  • Mr. Dizon will replace DoTr Secretary Jaime Bautista, who resigned due to health reasons
CONSUMER
PSE removes Jollibee foreign ownership limit
  • The PSE has approved the request of Jollibee to amend its foreign ownership limit from 40% to “no limit”
  • This is due to the amendments to Jollibee’s articles of incorporation, particularly the removal of the company’s ability to “own, acquire, mortgage, pledge, or encumber land and/or any interest therein
  • This means that JFC can now be 100% foreign-owned
  • In practice, the entire public float of JFC (45.5%) can be held by foreign investors

While this will not affect the weight in the PSE Index, it will have implications for JFC’s weights in other indexes, such as MSCI

  • As of December 2024, foreigners held 23.9% of JFC’s shares outstanding
BANKS
Banks’ asset quality improves further in December
  • Using preliminary figures from the BSP, the NPL ratio of Philippine banks fell to 3.27% in December from 3.54% in November. This was slightly higher than the 3.24% NPL ratio in Dec 2023.
    • In nominal terms, NPLs fell 3.9% MoM to P500.3bn but was higher by 11% YoY (vs. Dec 2023: P449.1bn)
    • Total gross loans rose 4.1% MoM to P15.3tn; On a YoY basis, gross loans accelerated slightly to 10.6% YoY in December from 10.4% YoY in November
  • NPL coverage ratio rose to 96.08% in December from 93.21% in November (vs. 101.74% in Dec 2023)
  • Past due loans fell by 4.8% MoM to P604.9bn, bringing the past due ratio down to 3.95% in December from 4.32% in November and 3.96% in Dec 2023
  • Restructured loans accounted for 2.03% of the industry’s loan portfolio, a tad higher than the 2.0% in November but lower than the 2.18% in Dec 2023
  • Loan loss reserves inched up by 0.9% to P481bn in December from P485bn in November
    • Brings loan reserve ratio to 3.14% in Dec 2024 from 3.30% in Nov and 3.3% in Dec 2023
