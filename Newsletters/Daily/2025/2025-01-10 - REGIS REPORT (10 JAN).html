REGIS REPORT

10 January 2025

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona has publicly expressed, again, his view that the BSP still has room to ease monetary policy. The BSP cut its policy rate by 75bp to 5.75% last year but given where CPI has been and is expected to be, real interest rates are still high relative to history. The next Monetary Board meeting to determine monetary policy (rates) will be next month (Feb), after the Jan CPI data is released.

The biggest uncertainty over the course of this year will be the outlook for US inflation, given the new administration, and the Federal Reserve’s response to it. The BSP actions on monetary policy will be unavoidably influenced by those factors.

+++

The BSP, according to Governor Remolona, is toying with the idea of adopting a subscription model for digital payments. Small-ticket digital payments (up to P50,000) between institutions are now generally routed through Instapay. It’s unclear if Remolona means the subscription model would apply to the institutions using Instapay or the end-users, but we are inclined to think it’s the former. Applying a subscription model for retail users would probably signal the death of the BSP’s aims for financial inclusion.

+++

The tax bureau has approved the tax exemption on asset-backed securities (ABS), removing one roadblock to the development of this market. It is hoped that the development of the ABS market would unlock more funding for asset classes that are long-term and illiquid in nature (e.g. mortgages) and spur growth in home ownership and the property market.

+++

The operators of the LRT-1 have asked the regulator to grant them a fare increase of over 30%. The fare hikes promised when the LRT-1 was privatized (in 2015) were mostly not implemented, with only one fare hike approved.

If approved unaltered, the fare for the country’s first light rail will cost P60 (~US$1) for the 26-km end-to-end journey. Ayala Corp (AC PM) owns 35% of the LRT-1 operator but has been considering a sale of this stake.

MACRO
BSP chief states the central bank still has room to ease
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona states that "we're still in restrictive territory compared to what we think the Goldilocks rate is. So there's still some room to ease"
    • Recall that the Monetary Board brought the target reverse repurchase rate to 5.75% as it delivered a total of 75 bps of rate cuts last year
  • Although the FY24 average inflation rate of 3.2% was within the gov't target band of 2-4%, the BSP Governor states the uncertainties looming from US President elect Donald Trump's trade policies poses risks to the upside
    • Key factors that could impact the rate cut expectations would be Trump's immigration policy as well as the proposed 10% universal tariff and 60% tariff on Chinese goods
    • Remolona however believes that the Philippines may be in better shape than other countries due to its significant reliance on services trade of BPO revenues and remittances
  • The BSP Governor also highlighted that the Fed has become more cautious than before as the market expects rate cuts of 25-50 bps this year vs. 100 bps previously
    • Despite this, Remolona emphasized data-driven monetary policy decision making that is not too reliant on the Fed's decisions
  • We expect the BSP to cut its policy rate by 75bps in 2025
    • Jefferies expects the Fed to cut rates by another 75bps for 2025
  • The BSP Monetary Board will have its first policy review on Feb. 20
MACRO
Trade deficit narrows to $4.76bn in November
  • The November trade deficit narrowed down to US$4.76bn, the smallest in three months, down 0.04% YoY and 17.5% MoM from $5.8bn last Oct
    • Driven by the decline in the value of imports which narrowed the deficit for the period
  • Imports declined 5% YoY to US$10.45bn
    • This decline is the first after four months of increases due to a big drop in fuel imports by 24% as well as decreases in capital and raw/intermediate goods imports at 4% and 2% respectively
  • Exports continue to fall at 9% to US$5.69bn, marking the third consecutive month of contraction
    • Led by the decline in electronics exports which continued to decrease down 21% YoY to US$2.78bn, electronics accounted for 49% of total exports during the period
  • Bringing the YT-November trade deficit to US$49.96bn, +3% YoY

INFRA
LRT-1 operator seeks 36% fare hike
  • The Department of Transportation held its first public hearing on the Light Rail Manila Corp.’s (LRMC) petition to hike fares for the LRT-1 rail line
    • LRMC is seeking to raise the boarding fee to P18.15 (+37% from current P13.29) and per kilometer fee to P1.65 (+36% from P1.21)
    • This would increase the single journey end-to-end trip of a passenger to P60 from P45 today
  • Government has a maximum of 30 days from the public hearing to evaluate the petition and new tariffs could be implemented by April
  • LRMC president and CEO Enrico Benipayo justified the concessionaire’s fare hike appeal by presenting several improvements in the LRT1
    • Added five new stations (+6.5km) last November and added more trains
    • Since LRMC took over the concession in 2015, there has only been one fare increase approved in 2023 and the deferral of previous tariff petitions has resulted in a P2.17bn deficit as of Nov 2024
  • LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (35.8%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19.2%), and Macquarie Infra (10%)
TRANSPORT
ICTSI secures tax incentives for Iloilo port expansion
  • The Board of Investments approved fiscal incentives for ICTSI’s P2.35bn Visayas Container Terminal (VCT) port expansion
    • Tax incentives include three-year income tax holiday, five years of enhanced deductions, and duty-free equipment importation
  • VCT is a brownfield port (capacity of ~200k TEUs) that ICT acquired last year
  • We estimate VCT accounts for <1% of ICT’s EBITDA
