REGIS REPORT

22 August 2025

PAGCOR chairman Alejandro Tengco says online gambling transactions fell by half in the first days following the de-linking of e-wallets with gambling sites and apps.  The Bangko Sentral (BSP) ordered e-wallets to stop re-directing traffic from their apps to gambling sites/apps effective Sunday (Aug 17).

 

Tengco claims illegal online gambling operators are still thriving and account for 60% of all such activities. We are in no position to dispute these claims but are not convinced that the illegal operators are still larger than the legal, PAGCOR-licensed operators as of recent months (quarters).

 

+++

 

In related news, digital finance adoption continues to grow rapidly, with payments coursed through the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) amounting to P2.13trn in July, an increase of +44% YoY and +7% MoM.  YT-Jul, the cumulative amount reached P13.3trn, +40% YoY.  Interestingly, the figures show that Instapay transactions (smaller, real-time transactions) are growing more rapidly (+61% in July) and are fast approaching the amounts for PesoNet.

 

+++

 

SM Prime (SMPH PM) CFO John Ong says the previously proposed REIT spin-off for the company may only take place after 2026.  SMPH has enough funds to pursue its projects without a REIT-related capital-raising.  The most high-profile such project is the Pasay reclamation (now called a “coastal redevelopment”).

 

+++

 

Foreign ownership in the overall listed Philippine market dipped 10bp to 18.8% in July.

  • There are 77 licensed online gaming operators

BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
July Digital payments jump 44% YoY, 7% MoM, a new record

Transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) hit P2,135bn ($37.2bn) in July, setting yet another record high, rising 44.4% YoY and 6.7% MoM. This put the 7-month total at P13,261bn, up 40% YoY, faster than the 35% growth pace set in FY2024.

 

Payments over PesoNet – larger, batched transactions – stood at P1,151bn, up 32.7% YoY.  While this was a deceleration from the 47% YoY clip in June, the July figure was still up 6% MoM. For 7M25, PesoNet payments of P7,300bn were up 31% YoY, compared to the 29% recorded in 2024.

 

Meanwhile, real-time, smaller-ticket transactions over InstaPay continued to surge, up 60.9% YoY and 7% MoM to P983bn, and seem to be fast closing in on the size of PesoNet. The July growth was also fastest YoY pace since late 2021, when adoption exploded during the Covid pandemic. Year-to-date, InstaPay payments were P5,961bn, up 53% YoY, still accelerating from the 46% growth in 2024.

 

The continued heady pace of growth is both surprising and encouraging, showing that adoption of digital payments, particularly at the retail consumer level, still has lots of headroom. As we pointed out in our report last month, the BSP is constantly pushing for innovations in the fintech space to further improve financial inclusion.

MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in July
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 10bps MoM to 18.8% (in July) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index rose 8bps MoM to 23.9%
    • High foreign-owned stocks such as MONDE, TEL, and ICT outperformed the market in July
  • Net foreign selling fell to US$29mn, from net selling of US$72mn in June
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 12 saw increases in foreign ownership while 17 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • GTCAP – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership. Foreign ownership rose 1.20 percentage points MoM to 23.0%, as net foreign buying hit US$23.6mn, the second highest for the month. 
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership climbed 1.03 percentage points MoM to 12.3%, as it experienced net foreign buying of US$6.7mn. It previously saw the biggest declines in foreign ownership for three consecutive months.   
    • AREIT – foreign ownership declined 3.72 percentage points MoM to 4.4%. We suspect that the 4.1% percentage point increase in June was an error. 
    • PGOLD – foreign ownership fell 66bps MoM to 17.6%.

MACRO
Infrastructure spending picks up in June, reversing slump during election ban
  • Overall infrastructure spending (incl. infra components of subsidy and equity to GOCCs and LGU transfers) rose 17% MoM and 3% YoY to P162.5bn in June
    • Due to a rebound in public works spending after declining in April/May or during the election ban
    • Also cited base effects from sizable releases for the Dept. of National Defense’s (DND) modernization program in June last year (bulk of DND’s big-ticket releases are expected in 2H25)
  • Brings 1H25 infra spend to P720bn, flat YoY and broadly in line with the 1H government’s target (here)
    • Government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP)

 

 

MACRO
BOP shifts to US$167mn deficit in July; GIR settles at US$105.4bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$167mn
    • A reversal from both the US$62bn surplus in July 2024 and June 2025's US$226mn surplus
  • The deficit reflected the national gov't's drawdowns on its foreign currency deposits with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) to service debt obligations
  • This brings the YT-July deficit at US$5.8bn, reversing from the US$1.5bn surplus in the same period last year
    • The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade in goods gap and external debt repayments
  • The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (-1.3% of GDP)
    • The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-July stood at US$105.4bn, down from the $106bn in end-June
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
    • GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025
