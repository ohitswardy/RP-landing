REGIS REPORT

01 October 2025

A bill has been filed to raise taxes on sweetened beverages over and above what were imposed by the law passed during the previous administration (2018). 

 

The bill proposes to not just (1) significantly increase levies on sweetened beverages already being taxed but also (2) expand the coverage of the taxes to include sweetened/flavored milk and coffee (including mixes).

 

The bill, if passed into law in its current form, would likely have a meaningful impact on the likes of Universal Robina (URC PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) and even Century Pacific (CNPF). We estimate as much as 15% of URC’s revenue is derived from branded categories directly affected. That figure still excludes the group’s sugar milling business, which would another ~15%. On the other hand, MONDE also sells yogurt/cultured milk drinks which is a subset of its “Others” category (21% of FY24 revenue), but this is unlikely to be a big segment.  CNPF produces milk/dairy products (<20% of revenue) but it’s also unclear how much of what it produces could be affected.

 

Among the companies not under coverage that could be affected would be the likes of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM), which has a minority (40%) interest in the Philippine Coca-Cola bottler.

 

Note that this bill isn’t one proposed or sponsored by the Marcos administration. That would suggest that the likelihood of it getting passed by the House (never mind the Senate) is not high.

Security Bank (SECB PM) announced the hiring of Mr Victor Lee to replace Mr Sanjiv Vohra as its next CEO. The change will be effective in January 2026, giving time for his “gardening leave”, BSP regulatory approvals, work visas, etc. Mr Lee was, until recently, CEO of CIMB Singapore, prior to which he was with CIMB in Malaysia. According to the SECB disclosure, Mr Lee presided over CIMB Singapore’s doubling of revenues, and taking ROE close to 20%. Mr Lee also has experience in the microfinance, retail, SME segment while he was with Fullerton Financial in Singapore.

 

SECB has made great strides in growing its consumer/MSME banking business, which now comprise 36% of its loan book. However, asset quality concerns have dragged on profitability in the last two years, keeping ROEs below 10%. The stock has reflected that – perhaps to an excessive degree – now trading at 0.35x P/book for FY2025E. Pretty low hurdles for Mr Lee, then…

CONSUMER
Tax hike on sugar-sweetened beverages filed by lawmakers
  • House Bill (HB) 5003 seeks to amend the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) law by significantly raising the excise tax on sugar sweetened beverage (SSBs) in an effort to protect the health of Filipinos given rising obesity rates
    • The excise tax on drinks (e.g., soda, sweetened juice) using caloric or noncaloric sweeteners will be raised to P20 per liter from the current P6 and to P40 per liter from P12 for beverages containing high fructose corn syrup
    • A P6 per liter tax on flavored milk and non-dairy beverages as well as all sweetened coffee products was also proposed
    • The Excise tax rates will be hiked 6% annually starting in 2026
    • White plain milk, infant formula, roasted ground coffee, beverages sweetened with coconut sap sugar or steviol glycosides remain exempt
  • The proposal was estimated to raise about P55bn annually with 40% of proceeds to be allocated to PhilHealth, 10% to the Dept. of Health, and 50% to the Dept. of Interior and Local Gov't
  • Rep. Leila de Lima, principal author alongside co-authors Rep. Cielo Lagman and Rep. Arlene Bag-Ao is pitching the bill as a public health initiative rather than an added levy on consumption
  • In URC (URC PM) RTD Tea and Coffee were 18% and 16% of total FY24 Domestic BCF sales, respectively
    • Domestic BCF sales was 46% of total revenue in 2024
  • For MONDE (MONDE PM), the others category (which includes yogurt drinks, cultured milk, oyster sauce) was 21% of total FY24 APAC BFB sales

    • Flavored milk likely represents only a very small share of total revenue, as it is a sub-segment within an already small portion of the business

    • APAC BFB sales was 84% of total revenue in 2024
  • CNPF’s (CNPF PM) 'Milk & Others' category was 29% of total revenue in 2024

CONGLOS
Ayala inks partnership with Spinneys for Philippine stores
  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) signed an agreement with Spinneys (SPINNEYS UH) for the operation of its premium fresh food supermarkets
    • AC will own 60% of the partnership while Spinneys will hold 40%
    • Spinneys will initially spearhead the operations prior to handing day-to-day management to the new entity
  • Spinneys is a UAE-based premium grocer which has 80 stores in UAE, Oman, and Saudi Arabia with this partnership marking its first venture outside the Gulf area
    • Sunil Kumar, CEO of Spinneys, highlighted the Philippines' long-term growth potential, strong economic fundamentals, and growing affluent population as key factors driving the company’s expansion beyond the Gulf region
  • The group intends to open its first store by 4Q next year (2026)
  • The deal is indicative of Ayala Corp's continued retail push as it follows the recent deal with CP Axtra to bring warehouse club Makro to the country
    • Another notable recent entry into retail for AC was Anko, where it holds a 50% stake
  • The group’s entry into retail could help speed up securing tenants for its malls; it would also put it in direct competition with other retailers like Puregold (PGOLD PM), Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) and SM’s (SM PM) SM Retail
MACRO
President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating improves in June SWS survey
  • President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating improved by 20points to +10 in June from the -10 rating in April
    • His gross satisfaction rating improved to 46% in June from 38%
    • Gross dissatisfaction declined to 36% from April’s 48%
    • Gross undecided rose to 19% from 14% in April
  • SWS noted the increase in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas:
    • Rating jumped by 35 points in Mindanao (from “bad” -44 to “neutral” -9)
    • +21 points in Balance Luzon (from “neutral” +7 to “good” +28)
    • +9 points in the Visayas (from “poor” -11 to “neutral” -2), and
    • +7 points in Metro Manila (from “neutral” -6 to “neutral” +1)
  • Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,200 adults from June 25-29, 2025

 

 

MARKET
RCR could be added to PSE Index during February 2026 rebalancing
  • Based on our preliminary estimates, RL Commercial REIT (RCR PM) could be added to the PSE Index during the February 2026 rebalancing
    • If this will be the case, Alliance Global (AGI PM) will likely be removed from the index
  • The PSE Index rebalancing in Feb 2026 will be based on the 12 months from Jan 2025 to Dec 2025
  • Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap
  • Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when
    • 1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or
    • 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 
  • Based on our estimates, RCR is ranked within the top 25
    • If this will remain the case by the end of 2025, then the lowest ranked PSE Index constituent (which is currently AGI) will be removed

MACRO
Chinese firm submits lowest bid for land segment of Bataan-Cavite bridge
  • China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC) offered to build the land segment (i.e. CP1) of the Bataan-Cavite Interlink bridge (BCIB) for P4.87bn
    • CHEC submitted the lowest bids among the eight (8) qualified bidders, including the construction arm of DMCI (DMC PM, P7.8bn) and EEI (EEI PM, P7.19bn)
  • CP1 involves the construction of the 5-km Bataan Land Approach, covering 6.89km of ramps
  • The DPWH said all financial proposals are still subject for further evaluation
  • The US$3.9bn BCIB project will be divided into seven contract packages, with the construction to begin in the two on-land packages
    • The BCIB project will span 32km and will provide a permanent road link between the provinces of Bataan and Cavite
    • Once the bridge is completed, travel time between the provinces of Bataan and Cavite will be reduced from 5 1⁄2 hours to about 45 minutes
    • In 2023, the Asian Development Bank (ADB), which is co-financing the project, approved a US$2.11-billion loan for the project
