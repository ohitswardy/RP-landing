REGIS REPORT

28 April 2025

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) results for 1Q25 seemed quite soft, with NPAT coming in at P19.7bn, up 6%, trailing behind REGIS and consensus (at around P90bn for FY25E). Some of the YoY numbers are distorted by the accounting presentation change that came into effect in 3Q24. Loans grew 12% YoY, slowing slightly from the 13.4% posted in 4Q24. By our initial estimates, NIMs were flat to slightly down QoQ. Fee incomes were quite strong, up 31% YoY. However, this was blunted by a 17% YoY surge in opex, led by manpower expenses. PPOP was up just 1% YoY as a result. NPAT was supported by an 11% YoY drop in provision expense, as NPL ratio improved to 1.77% from 1.83% the previous quarter.

 

Overall, not the best quality of earnings. The bank will host a briefing at noon today. More after then.

Digital payments re-surged in March, up 45% YoY from 34% in Feb, with total payments at P1,843bn, a new monthly record high. There could have been some base effect, as the Easter holidays fell in March last year, which could mean a commensurate slowdown in April. Both PesoNet and InstaPay posted all-time high volumes too with PesoNet up 38% YoY to P1,017bn and InstaPay up 52.9% YoY to P826bn. More impressively, the volume of transactions over InstaPay was up a scorching 97% YoY, indicating continued broadening out of adoption of digital payments on smaller ticket items.

 

Globe’s (GLO PM) Mynt/GCash remains the dominant wallet, though PLDT’s (TEL PM) Maya says it broke even in 1Q25. Among the larger banks, BPI (BPI PM) seems the most aggressive in attacking the payments space.

BANKS/DIGITAL BANKS
Digital payments re-surge in March, up 45% YoY, partly on low-base

Payments made digitally over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped sharply to P1,843bn in March, a new record-high month, up 44.5% YoY, compared to the 33.7% pace in February. We believe, though, that this could be partly due to the (relatively) low base in March 2024, owing to the timing of the Easter holidays, which landed in April this year. If so then, we could see a commensurate slowdown in the YoY figures next month. Year-to-date, the 1Q25 total stood at P5,292bn, up 38.8% YoY, faster than the 35.4% booked in FY2024.

 

For PesoNet – large-ticket, batched transactions – March transactions came in at P1,017bn, also a new record, up 38.3%, accelerating from the 26.9% the previous month. This brought the 3M24 figure up to P3,003bn, up 32.4%, compared to the 28.5% for FY24..

 

Meanwhile, InstaPay – real-time payments of up to P50,000 – saw payments explode +52.9% YoY to P825.7bn, also a new all-time high, and growing much faster than the 43.6% clocked in February. Year-to-date, P2,289bn went through InstaPay, up 48.3% YoY, accelerating from the 46.3% growth for the whole of 2024.

 

Perhaps more crucially, transaction volumes over InstaPay skyrocketed 96.7% YoY in March. That’s the fastest monthly YoY growth rate since mid-2021, at the height of the Covid-19 pandemic. As a result, the value per transaction dropped sharply to P4,223, again highlighting to us the continued broadening out of the use cases for digital payments for more and more daily small-ticket purchases.

 

As usual, it is important to highlight that the data only covers payments made BETWEEN platforms, and does not include transactions WITHIN the same platform (ie BDO-to-BDO or GCash-to-GCash). Thus we reckon the numbers here vastly understate the true value/volume of digital transactions system-wide.

 

Globe Telecom’s (GLO PM) GCash/Mynt remains the dominant player in this space. Meanwhile, PLDT (TEL PM) recently announced that its own wallet/Digibank Maya broke into profitability in 1Q25. At its 1Q25 earnings call this week, BPI (BPI PM) said they were competing aggressively for the payments pool.

NEDA: Inflation downtrend likely continued in April
  • The National Economic and Development Authority's (NEDA) projected inflation to continue easing in April
    • Inflation declined for two consecutive months from 2.9% in Jan to 2.1% in Feb, and to 1.8% in Mar
    • This brings the 1Q25 inflation at 2.3%, lower than 2.6% the previous quarter
      • The downtrend was primarily driven by slower increases in food and transportation costs
  • NEDA Chief Arsenio M. Balisacan stated that easing inflation should support household consumption, while recent interest rate cuts by the BSP can stimulate both consumption and investments
    • However, he cautioned about the lingering global macroeconomic uncertainties that may negatively impact the domestic economy
  • Consensus expects for 2025 inflation to be at 2.8%
    • While REGIS expectations for inflation this year are 3.0%
