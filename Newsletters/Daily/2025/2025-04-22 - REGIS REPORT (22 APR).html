REGIS REPORT

22 April 2025

BPI (BPI PM) kicked off the 1Q25 earnings season yesterday. In a press release, the bank reported 1Q25 NPAT of P16.6bn, up 9% YoY, in line with REGIS estimates (P70.5bn, but tracking ahead of consensus (P67.4bn). Loan growth of 13.2% YoY was steady vs the FY2024 pace of 13%, ex-Robinsons Bank (headline was +19% in 2024). The bank estimated that NIMs expanded 30bps YoY, likely due to a) better loan mix in favor of “non-institutional” consumer/MSME/micro loans, and b) the 250bps RRR cut in late October last year. Provision expense doubled to P3bn, with credit cost rising to 54bps (in line with guidance), as the bank completed the run-down of its NPL cover to 100% as of 1Q25. Overall, a solid set of results. We maintain our Buy rating on the stock. Management will host an earnings call tomorrow, Wednesday at 4pm.

Moody’s revised Security Bank’s (SECB PM) bond outlook down from Stable to Negative, while retaining its overall Baa (investment grade) rating. The report cited the rapid decline in SECB’s CET-1 ratio – which fell to 12.50% at end-2024, down 227bps YoY – due to its aggressive 26% loan growth. We note that the 12.50% does not yet reflect the potential impact from the acquisition of 25% of Home Credit (HCPH), which we estimate will knock another 100bps off CET-1 ratios. In its FY24 earnings call, management acknowledged these concerns, and guided for a sharp slowdown in loan growth to c10% for 2025E to manage their capital, adding that the bank has no intention of raising new equity.

 

+++

 

AC Mobility, the exclusive distributor of BYD vehicles in the country, says it aims to have 77 dealership locations by end of the year, from 27 end of 2024.  For perspective, there were 74 Toyota dealerships as of last year.  The size of Toyota dealerships is likely to be much larger but the target number of BYD locations should give one a sense of how much growth is being expected for the brand.

 

It was already previously reported AC Mobility intends to have over 700 charging stations by this year, from 215 last year.  AC Mobility is a subsidiary of Ayala Corp (AC PM).

 

+++

 

ICTSI (ICT PM) is looking undeterred by the ongoing global trade war and continues its expansion push.  It announced yesterday that it acquired a 47% stake in a 32-hectare inactive shipyard adjacent to its port in Rio, Brazil, which will support its expansion plans there.

 

+++

 

The updated March 2025 GIR shows a figure of US$106.7bn, slightly higher than the preliminary number (US$106.2bn). As noted, the MoM decline was largely due to the payment of government bond maturities.

MACRO
BOP swings to US$2.0bn deficit in March 2025; Final GIR settles at US$106.7bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of $US2.0bn
    • A reversal from both Feb 2025's US$3.1bn surplus and the US$1.2bn surplus in Mar 2024
    • The deficit reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits drawdowns for external debt obligations as well as its net foreign exchange operations
      • External debt obligations that matured in March includes a US$2.0bn 25-year bond
    • This brings the 1Q25 deficit at $3.0bn, reversing from the $238mn surplus for the same period last year
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Mar 2025 stood at US$106.7bn, down from the US$107.4bn in end-Feb 2025
    • The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
  • BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is -0.8% of GDP
    • GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025
