23 May 2025

With President Marcos, Jr asking his entire Cabinet to resign after the recent election setback (see our note on the midterm election results here), the question now is who will end up being replaced, and by whom.

 

A reset is not necessarily a bad thing. What’s important is what (and who) comes out of the revamp. Will the changes be cosmetic? Or substantive? Will it boost or even be detrimental to the administration’s popularity? How much will it affect the key policy direction/s of this government? The personnel changes that will be announced should give us an idea.

 

++++

 

News of the emerging threat from a sugarcane pest infestation hit the news over the past few days, and this apparently led to weakness in the shares of Universal Robina (URC PM), which derives as much as 20% of EBIT from the milling and sale of sugar to third parties.

 

The pest threat is now present in the country’s largest sugarcane producing province (Negros Occidental), which accounts for half of the country’s output. The situation, however, has not yet turned into a full-blown epidemic and the new planting season is just starting. Thus, the possibility of avoiding the worst effects is still there. 

 

A decline in the sugarcane crop could lead to a decline in sugarcane supply for the sugar millers and prices should also soar. The effect should, intuitively, be negative for the industry, including the millers.

 

However, things are never just this simple. Beyond just the possible first order impact, one also needs to consider how the government responds if such a supply disruption does happen. Note the Philippines is a closed, protected market for sugar, one cannot import sugar without an import license (which is why domestic sugar prices are >2x higher than international prices). Consequently, what government policy (e.g. on imports) will be if the epidemic does hit is likely to be a more important driver of the sugar millers’ profitability.

Emerging pest infestation threatens Philippine sugar output
  • Sugar farms in Negros Occidental, which accounts for about half (~54%) of the country's sugar production, have reported an infestation of red-striped soft scale insects (RSSI)
  • The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) reported that RSSI can potentially reduce sugar content in cane by roughly 50%
    • Pest infestation may have been brought to Negros (Western Visayas) from Pampanga and/or Batangas (Luzon), where RSSI infestations are still present in some farms
  • The pest was first observed in Bacolor, Pampanga in June 2022, then Balayan, Batangas in Aug 2023
    • Notably, two fields in Balayan, Batangas were observed, with one field was moderately damaged, while the other was 20-40% heavily infested
    • The quantified impact of the previous RSSI infestations on sugar output was not stated, but based on PSA data, contribution of Batangas to total sugar output fell to 3.6% in 2023 from 4.3-4.8% in 2020-2022
  • SRA Administrator Pablo Luis S. Azcona highlights the need for urgency given that some farmers have already started to plant cane for next crop year
    • Sugar production for crop year 2024-2025 could total 1.84 million metric tons (MMT), lower than the output of 1.92 MMT in the previous crop year
    • The PH imported ~200,000 MMT of sugar in 2024
  • The National Crop Protection Center has previously tested insecticides that counter RSSI infestations which have been deemed effective
    • However, official use outside of testing will require an emergency-use permit from the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority, as said insecticides have not been registered for sugarcane yet
