REGIS REPORT

08 April 2025

The continued slowdown in inflation to 1.8% in March – driven by the -7.7% rice CPI for the month – should be more than enough reason for the BSP to cut policy rates at its Monetary Board meeting on Thursday, 10 April. The weak USD and increasing expectations of aggressive US Fed rate cuts (was as high as 122bps intraday yesterday) makes a BSP cut a near certainty, in our view. We expect domestic inflation to stay benign over the next few quarters given the continued softness in rice prices. Oil prices have been weak, too, putting further downward pressure on Philippine CPI.

 

 

 

 

Meanwhile, the fears of slowing global GDP growth as a result of “Liberation Day” tariffs are seeing even more downside to commodity prices. As we opined in our report last week, the high tariffs into the US could see manufacturing countries look to sell more goods outside the US – at lower prices.

 

It is tempting to revise our 2025E forecast of 50bps in BSP rate cuts. However, given the volatile nature of recent US policy statements – and the unpredictability of global reactions to those volatile policies – we are inclined to hold off on any changes, at least for now

The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) dipped 1% MoM to US$106.2bn in March. The decline was primarily due to the payment of a US$2bn bond maturity last month. The government has another bond maturity (EUR 650mn) this month.

The Dept of Trade (DTI) is exploring options, including the lowering of tariffs on American goods, in response to the unilateral imposition by the US government of higher tariffs on all of its trading partners. Frankly, we find it hard to imagine what the Philippines can do, short of curtailing its rather modest export volumes, to cut its trade surplus with the States to near zero.

MACRO
PH Dollar reserves down 1.1% MoM at end-March
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Mar saw a 1.1% MoM decrease to $106.2bn from $107.4bn at end-Feb
    • This primarily reflected the national government's drawdowns on foreign currency deposits with the BSP to meet external debt obligations as well as the BSP's net foreign exchange operations
    • The level of dollar reserves by end-Mar was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • Reserve position in the IMF decreased 2.6% MoM to $653mn
  • Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $12.8bn, which jumped 5.9% MoM as gold continues hit new all-time highs
  • Foreign investments decreased by 1.7% MoM to $88.6bn
  • Foreign currency deposits sharply dropped 38.2% MoM to $498mn
  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025

 

 

MACRO
DTI open to lowering tariffs on US goods
  • The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) Secretary Cristina Roque stated that they are "definitely" looking at reducing tariffs on US goods but will discuss the extent and terms of the offer as they are set to meet [with the US] soon
    • Recall that US President Donald Trump announced sweeping reciprocal tariffs which will take effect on April 9, which includes a 17% tariff on Philippine goods
  • Roque also cited that the gov't is considering a collective response with other ASEAN member-countries to address the higher US tariffs
    • This comes after Malaysia Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's call for a united ASEAN response to the US tariffs, stating that the country, as ASEAN chair, will be ready to lead efforts to be heard on the int'l stage
    • Cambodia is facing a 49% tariff, followed by Laos (48%), Vietnam (46%), Myanmar (45%), Thailand (37%), Indonesia (32%), Malaysia (24%), Brunei (24%), Philippines (17%), and Singapore (10%)
  • In 2024, the Philippines exported $12.14 bn worth of goods to the US while importing $8.17 bn, resulting in a trade surplus of $3.97 bn
