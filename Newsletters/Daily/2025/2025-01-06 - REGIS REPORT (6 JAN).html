REGIS REPORT

06 January 2025

In his Asia Maxima quarterly report (attached), Chris Wood highlights the contradictory nature of the stated goals of the Trump 2.0 administration.

  • Fed rate cuts are needed to bring down debt funding costs. But tariffs and mass deportations are inflationary. The market still expects rate cuts in 2025E, but down to c43bps from 68bps as recently as 6 Dec. Meanwhile, 10-year bond yields are back to 4.5%, up >90bps since September.
  • Planned $2trn in budget cuts will be great for the US fiscal deficit, but will be massively deflationary / contractionary, and bad for the stock market.

 

His base case is the Fed will keep cutting rates this year. On tariffs, he thinks China will negotiate, and give Trump a “win”, fending off a full-scale tariff war. Also, he’s less worried about China, as China now exports more to ASEAN then it does to either the US or to the EU. In contrast, the EU’s trade surplus vs the US has ballooned in recent years. On spending cuts, Chris thinks that Trump will watch for market reactions, and may moderate his stance.

 

Chris is also worried about the AI / data center craze, and when the market will start to demand to see returns on the billions of dollars in recent investments.

 

He remains Overweight on Asia ex-Japan equities, considering the recent under-performance, and cheap valuations vs history. There are also lots of companies (270 within the MSCI Asia-ex Japan index) with dividend yields higher than the US 20yr Treasury bonds.

 

He also is Overweight the Philippines, on the potential for a consumer rebound, and on cheap valuations. The main risk, though, is a weak PHP given the twin fiscal/CA deficits.

 

+++

 

In its latest BOP outlook (link), the BSP made significant revisions to its projections, and now expects larger Current Account Deficits (CAD) for 2024E and 2025E due in part to the potential trade disruptions from the incoming Trump 2.0 administration, as well as challenges posed by AI adoption to the BPO sector. The wider CAD forecasts will complicate the BSP’s monetary stance and could limit its ability to cut policy rates for fear of a sharply weaker PHP.

 

For 2024E, the BSP sees the CAD at 2.2% of GDP,  wider than their target of 1.5% of GDP in their 3Q24 review, and more than double from their 2Q24 estimate of 1.0% of GDP. In USD terms, the BSP now forecasts a $10.4bn deficit, larger than their estimates of $6.8bn as of 3Q24 and $4.7bn in 2Q24. Compared to their 3Q24 numbers, the big drivers were lower goods and service exports, notably for tourist receipts, as well as larger service imports.

 

The BSP’s changes were much more severe for 2025E, with the CAD forecast to widen to 2.4% of GDP. That compares to their 3Q24 forecast of just 1.1% of GDP, and significantly larger than the 2Q24 target of just 0.4% of GDP. Note, too, that the BSP’s previous estimates had narrower CAD in 2025E relative to 2024E. In USD terms, they see the CAD at $12.1bn, versus the $5.5bn and $2.0bn targets in 3Q and 2Q, respectively. This was due to sharply lower forecasts for goods and services exports , with downgrades to both tourism and BPO revenues.

 

Furthermore, the BSP said, “The balance of risks… (is) tilted to the downside.”

 

Surprisingly, despite the worsening CAD, and lower FDIs, the BSP actually increased its BOP surplus forecasts, as well as for the Gross International Reserves (GIR). We assume that this means the BSP expects larger USD-denominated debt issuances.

 

That said, the CAD forecasts for 2024E and 2025E are lower than the 2.6% posted in 2023 and 4.5% in 2022. However, the persistent deficits still put pressure on the PHP, and limit the BSP’s ability to cut policy interest rates, especially if the US Fed goes an extended pause on its own rate-cut cycle.

FX loans out of banks’ Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDU) were broadly flat in 3Q24 at $15.75bn, up just 1.6% YoY and 0.7% QoQ. FX loans accounted for just 6.1% of total loans, a new low (was 6.6% in 2Q24). This was partly due to the PHP strength in 3Q; in PHP terms, FX loans were up just 0.6% YoY, and down 3.7% QoQ.

 

FX deposits grew 10.9% YoY and 4.2% QoQ to $57.5bn. The 3Q24 YoY growth was a further slowdown from the 12.6% pace in 2Q24 and 20.3% in 1Q24, suggesting that the “flight to USD” is easing somewhat. It also helped that the USD weakened against the PHP in 3Q (from P58.61 in June to P56.05 in Sept). Give the USD’s strength in 4Q, it will be interesting to see if FX depos re-accelerates. FX deposits accounted for 16.8% of total, down from a recent high of 17.5% in 1Q24.

 

This takes FX LDR down to 27.4%. This suggests that the PHP LDR is up to 82.8%, up from 79.4% in 2Q24, and much higher than the 73.6% of total loans. The high PHP LDR is likely – at least partly – what prompted the BSP to cut the RRR from 9.5% to 7.0% in late October 2024. Governor Remolona has often stated that his aim is to bring the RRR to 5.0%. (Philosophically, he thinks the RRR should be nil.)

 

The low level of USD loan exposure on banks’ balance sheets shields them from potential corporate stress from swings in the PHP:USD exchange rate. The low FX LDR meanwhile means the vast majority of banks’ FX deposits are invested in bonds, largely in ROPs (Philippine government-issued FX-denominated bonds), with some placed in regional sovereigns and corporate bonds.

Ferronoux Holdings (FERRO PM), a listed firm with barely any operations, will become a backdoor listing vehicle for an entity holding 9 hectares of land adjacent to the Okada Manila casino. We are a bit puzzled why one would bother to inject what appears to be just idle land assets into a listing. Among the reasons we can guess is that it will allow the landowners to reduce taxes in a subsequent sale.

 

+++

 

Dito Telecommunity (Dito Tel), the telecom subsidiary of Dito CME (DITO PM), will reduce capex beginning this year after it completed and passed the audit conducted by the telecom regulator. After reaching 7% market share (revenue), we expect Dito Tel to report a modestly positive FY24 EBITDA. Given the depreciation expenses and financing costs, however, earnings are still likely to be deeply negative. The recent announcement of a capital raise at Dito CME will likely buy it only a few months.

Ferronoux Holdings to become backdoor listing vehicle
  • Ferronoux Holdings (FERRO PM) will become the vehicle for the listing of 9 hectares of land adjacent to Okada Manila (in the Manila Bay area)
  • This will be done through a series of transactions
    • Michael Cosiquien’s ISOC Holdings sold 133.5mn FERRO shares to Thermis at P2.2242/share (total value of P297mn)
    • 80mn new FERRO shares will be issued to Thermis Group from the existing capital stock (at P1/share)
    • The authorized capital stock of the company will be increased from P550mn to P2.5bn – par value of P1/share
    • 240mn new FERRO shares will be issued to Thermis Group (at P1/share)
    • 918mn new FERRO shares will be issued to Eagle 1 Landholdings at P4.70/share (total of P4.3bn) in exchange for 94,144sqm of land adjacent to Okada Manila
    • 300mn new FERRO shares will be issued to investors in a private placement in order to comply with minimum public ownership requirements 
  • After all the transactions, FERRO’s shares outstanding will increase from 262mn to 1.8bn
    • We estimate that Thermis Group will own 69% of the company after the transactions are completed
    • The public float will drop from 37.5% to 23.8%
  • Mr. Michael Cosiquien resigned as President and Chairman
    • Mr. James Lorenzana, who is also President of Okada foundation, was elected as Chairman
    • Atty. Abel Almano was appointed as President
  • FERRO was 51%-owned by ISOC Holdings
    • We estimate that ISOC will own 7% of the company after all the transactions are completed
  • Some parcels of the land to be exchanged currently house support buildings of Okada Manila
    • These will be torn down in favor of new projects
  • The implied value of the land (based on the share swap) is P45,830/sqm, lower than the value we assign to existing Mall of Asia land (P400,000/sqm)

DITO slashes capex budget to focus on efficiency
  • Dito Telecommunity Corp. (Dito Tel) is reducing its capex budget for 2025, with a target spend of P15bn-P20bn which is lower than the 2024 allocation of P25bn to P30bn
    • This is given that the company has completed its 2024 commitment of over 86% population coverage
  • This year's capex budget will mainly be used for existing infrastructure optimization rather than expansive rollouts
    • i.e., addressing network gaps, enhancing capacity in high-demand locations, utilizing advanced technology
  • DITO Tel CEO Eric Alberto sets the company goal of financial stability given the shift of focus from network expansion to operational efficiency
    • Alberto expects FY24 revenue to have increased 50%, which is around P16 to P17bn, from P11bn in 2023. While it projected its subscriber base to be 16mn by year-end
      • DITO for 9M24 posted P11.9bn in revenues, up 47% YoY and had about 13mn subscribers
      • As of Dec 2024, Smart Communications Inc., wholly owned wireless communications and digital services subsidiary of PLDT Inc. (TEL PM), has 60.3 million subscribers while Globe Telecom Inc. (GLO PM) has 60.2 million users
  • The company targets to be profitable by 2028
  • DITO Telecommunity Corp. (Dito Tel) is the telecom subsidiary of Dito CME Holdings Corp. (DITO PM)
