REGIS REPORT

10 April 2025

Massive rebound in overnight markets as Donald Trump hit the pause button on his “reciprocal tariffs”, applying instead just the minimum 10% tariff for most countries, but increasing China’s tariff to an eye-watering 125%. Expect similar bounces in regional indices today, especially those pummeled in the last week. This is not likely to be the end of the tariff / trade story. If anything, the “pause” only highlights the volatile nature of Mr Trump and his policies. It also highlights how tightly tied the fates of many countries are to the US. And as we written last week, the Philippines is among the least affected in a potential global tariff war.

The Monetary Board of the BSP meets today, and the broad consensus is for a 25bps rate cut in policy rates from 5.75% to 5.50%. As we wrote earlier this week, the sharp slowdown in CPI to 1.8% for March – or indeed the 2.1% in February – was enough to warrant the cut. But the additional factors of a) expectations of more Fed rate cuts (now at least 3 cuts for 2025), and b) the weak USD, likely relieve many of the BSP’s concerns over a depreciating PHP feedback loop into inflation.

BANKS
Banks’ asset quality remains steady in February
  • Using preliminary figures from the BSP, the NPL ratio of Philippine banks remain unchanged at 3.38% in Feb relative to Jan. This was slightly lower than the 3.44% NPL ratio in Feb 2024
    • In nominal terms, NPLs were flat MoM at P513.3bn but was higher by 10% YoY (vs. Feb 2023: P466.1bn)
    • Total gross loans likewise were unchanged at P15.2tn; On a YoY basis, gross loans decelerated slightly to 12.1% YoY in Feb from 13.4% YoY in Jan
  • NPL coverage ratio was steady at 95.36% in Feb from 95.25% in Jan (vs. 100.06% in Feb 2024)
  • Past due loans inched up slightly 0.8% MoM to P637.8bn, bringing the past due ratio to 4.20% in Feb from 4.17% in Jan and 4.31% in Feb 2024
  • Restructured loans accounted for 2.05% of the industry’s loan portfolio, unchanged from Jan but lower than the 2.16% in Feb 2024
  • Loan loss reserves were stable MoM at P489.6bn in Feb
    • This brings the loan reserve ratio to 3.23% in Feb 2024 from 3.22% in Jan and 3.44% in Feb 2024
MACRO/MARKET
Trust AUMs jump 19% YoY to P7.4trn in 2024

Trust Assets Under Management (AUMs) closed 2024 at P7,372bn – just under $130bn – up 18.6% YoY and up 2.3% QoQ. The distribution of the growth, though, was somewhat complicated. AUMs directly within the banks were up just 6% to P4,451bn, while “non-bank” AUMs surged 44% YoY to P2,921bn. This was partly due to two banks – RCBC and Union Bank – that spun off their AUMs into “stand-alone trust” vehicles. Those two alone combined for about P300bn, or a third of the growth in non-bank AUMs. We also saw roughly P200bn growth in AUMs of SunLife, as the new rules allowed the assets of the insurance company to be managed by the Trust entity. (And to recall, BPI’s AUMs are also considered part of the “non-bank” trust group, having spun off its Trust department a while back.)

 

BDO (BDO PM) again topped the league tables, with consolidated AUM of P2,305bn, up 15.8% YoY. This was driven by a 19.4% increase in AUMs of the parent bank to P1,668bn, and a more modest 7.5% increase to P638bn from its subsidiary BDO Private Bank. BPI’s AUMs jumped another 25.2% YoY to P1,531bn, following the aggressive push of its BPI Wealth division. Metrobank (MBT PM) kept its third place ranking at P581bn, up 16.8% YoY.

 

Other big movers include Security Bank (SECB PM) which saw AUMs surge by 41.9% to P154bn, and RCBC (RCB PM), whose AUMs were up 25% YoY to P195bn.

 

Independent asset manager ATRAM saw its AUMs grow 15.8% YoY to P363bn. This does not yet include the recently-announced merger between ATRAM and Union Bank Trust. Based on the 2024 numbers, the combined entity would manage about P464bn, closing the distance to MBT’s size.

 

Meanwhile, average net fee incomes (as a % of AUM) came down again, most notably for SECB and for MBT. This to us is consistent with anecdotes that the fastest growing products in 2024 were either fixed income / money market, or foreign ETFs whose fees are shared with the white-labeled foreign asset manager.

 

We continue to maintain that, given the size of the domestic AUMs, the Philippine equity market could easily be supported by a shift in AUMs back into local equities. Every 100bps re-allocation into the stock market would be $1.2bn, in a market turning over barely $100mn a day.

 

 

 

 

 

PROPERTY
LPC: BPO office demand rebounds in 1Q25; residential unsold inventory rises
  • During its 1Q25 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy fell 1ppt QoQ to 17%
    • Metro Manila office vacancy fell 2ppt QoQ to 16%
    • We estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels      
  • Net take-up hit 78,000sqm nationwide in 1Q25 – from 100,000sqm of net closures in 4Q24
    • BPO net office take-up rebounded to 136,000sqm, from 1,000sqm in 4Q24 (66,000sqm in 1Q24)
    • Net office take-up from traditional tenants (including government agencies) fell 15% QoQ (flat YoY) to 83,000sqm
    • Meanwhile, POGO firms experienced net closures of 141,000sqm, from net closures of 200,000sqm in 4Q24
  • LPC expects office rental rates in Makati and BGC to rise this year but also projects rental rates in other parts of Metro Manila to fall
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 5.0X FY25E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 73% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 2.9X FY25E P/E
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 75% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY25E P/B, and 3.9X FY25E P/E
    • 75% of AREIT’s AUM – AREIT currently offers 6.3% FY25E dividend yield
  • Meanwhile, the residential unsold inventory level rose from 34 months of sales in 4Q24 to 38 months in 1Q25
  • Residential sales fell 6% YoY (up 49% QoQ) to 6,508 units
  • Unsold inventory rose 28% YoY and 9% QoQ to 81,400 units
    • ALI is trading at 58% discount to NAV, 1.1X FY25E P/B, and 10.7X FY25E P/E
    • SMPH is trading at 47% discount to NAV, 1.4X FY25E P/B, and 14.2X FY25E P/E
