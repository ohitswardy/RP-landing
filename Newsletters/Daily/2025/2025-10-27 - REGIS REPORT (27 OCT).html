27 October 2025

BDO (BDO PM) this morning reported 9M25 NPAT of P61.3bn, up 4% YoY, and broadly in line, representing c73% of our FY25E of P86.7bnn (consensus P86.6bn). Loans grew 14%, a similar pace to 2Q (13.8%), with NII up 8%, indicating some margin compression. Non-interest income jumped 14%, on strong fees (+15%). Opex was up 15.5% (same pace as 1H), slightly disappointing, as management had guided for slower growth in 2H25. Provisions were up 12% YoY , though credit cost for 3Q25 alone moderated to 45bps (from 49bps in 2Q), and in line with mgt guidance. NPL ratio was steady at 1.77% (from 1.75% in 2Q) while NPL cover slipped to 134% (from 140% in 2Q, and from 145% at end-2024)

 

More after the briefing (noon today). We would pay close attention to management communication around opex, and the falling NPL coverage.

Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) is the first consumer name to report 3Q earnings with 9M25 sales and EBIT up 5% and 4% to P149.3bn and P6.6bn, respectively—broadly in line with expectations.

 

Blended SSSG moderated in 3Q to 1.6% from 4.8% the previous quarter. The deceleration in consolidated SSSG reflected softer growth in the Food and Drugstore segments (80% of total revenues), alongside a sharp 15% contraction in Department Store SSS due to school calendar changes.

MREIT (MREIT PM) disclosed that it would acquire 200,000sqm of offices from its sponsor, Megaworld (MEG PM), in a share-for-property swap. Megaworld will subscribe to 1.36bn new MREIT shares at a minimum price of P14.39/share, expanding MREIT’s shares outstanding by 37%. Based on our estimates, the Alliance Global (AGI PM) Group would need to sell about 100mn MREIT shares in order for the latter to meet the minimum public ownership requirement.

First Gen (FGEN PM) received the all-clear from the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) on the sale of 60% of its natgas operations to Enrique Razon’s holding company, Prime Infrastructure (not listed). Recall that The sale brings P50bn of cash into FGEN, which it will use to pay down debt, and increase investments in other RE capacity – which sadly precludes potential large special dividends. That said, the sale underscores FGEN’s undervaluation, as its market capitalization is just under P55bn, despite its portfolio of hydro and geothermal assets, as well as its remaining 40% stake in the natgas assets. We estimate FGEN’s NAV at P40/sh based on the transaction, while its share price is at P15/sh.
