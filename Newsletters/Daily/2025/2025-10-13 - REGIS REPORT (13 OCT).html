REGIS REPORT

13 October 2025

ICTSI (ICT PM) received surprise positive news over the weekend as the Durban High Court of South Africa ruled decisively in its favor in the matter of its bid for Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2). The judge found no irregularities in the auction process and rejected the bid of logistics firm Maersk to block the awarding of the concession to ICTSI by state-owned Transnet.

 

Barring a possible but unlikely court appeal, the decision paves for ICTSI to take over a major port in the largest economy in Africa. ICTSI reportedly offered ZAR11.1bn (equivalent to ~US$630mn today) for 50% (less one share) of the DCT-2 concession, a bold bid that would be 20% higher than the Maersk’s. While this may appear expensive on our estimates of historical operations (17x EBITDA), we believe there is significant scope to extract efficiencies from the operation.

 

DCT-2 is supposed to have an annual capacity of 2.9mn TEUs, compared to ICTSI’s ~20mn. For now, we estimate this transaction can add P30/share (6%) to our equity valuation for ICTSI. However, one should not be surprised if this rises further after the takeover is consummated and operations progress.

Banking industry asset quality slipped in August, with NPL ratio – excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP) – up 8bps MoM to 3.66%, though this was still 15bps lower YoY. The August NPL figure is the highest level, YTD, and the highest since Nov 2024, as there was a large write-off of loans in Dec 2024. The Past Due (PD) ratio up 2bps MoM to 4.62%. The overall increase in problem loans is likely due to the rapid growth in unsecured consumer/MSME loans such as credit cards, microfinance etc. To illustrate the point – Digibanks make up just 0.3% of total loans, and yet the segment accounted for 3% of new NPLs and a whopping 25% of new PD loans in August. The headline figures seem to be cause for alarm. It remains to be seen though if the higher yields and fees from unsecured loans are enough to compensate for the higher loan losses.

The Philippine Ambassador the US, Jose Manuel Romualdez, says the government is working with “friends” in the US Congress to “exempt” the Philippines from legislation imposing penalties on firms outsourcing to foreign countries. The Philippines is likely the biggest offshore location for voice operations, so we’d be surprised if legislation is passed with such a glaring exemption.

 

The BPO industry in the Philippines generated an estimated US$38bn in revenue last year.

 

+++

 

The Dept of Agriculture (DA) is aiming for 84% rice self-sufficiency by next year, reducing need for rice imports. Rice production is up 6% so far in 1H and the DA is hoping to increase output further next year. The country imported 4.7mn MT of rice last year, and 84% self-sufficiency would imply annual imports of around 2.5mn MT.

TRANSPORT
Court clears ICTSI to run Durban container terminal
  • Durban High Court Judge Mahendra Chetty rejected APM Terminals’ petition seeking to block state-owned Transnet from awarding ICTSI the 25-year concession to operate Durban Container Terminal Pier 2 (DCT-2)
    • Judge Chetty found no irregularities in the bidding process, saying that ICTSI was transparent about its calculation of solvency ratio and that it has provided ample evidence of financial capacity
    • Judge also ruled that APM Terminals’ petition unnecessarily prolonged the proceedings on baseless grounds
    • Maersk said “We recognise the importance of operational improvements and infrastructure development in Durban proceeding without further delay”
  • This development should pave the way for the turnover of DCT-2’s operations to ICTSI
    • ICTSI reportedly offered an upfront payment of ZAR11.1bn (~US$630mn) to secure a 50% less one share in DCT-2’s concession
    • Assuming DCT-2’s estimated 2024 volumes of 1.8mn TEUs, yield per TEU of US$115/TEU, and EBITDA margin of 35%, we estimate ICT’s bid implies ~17x EV/EBITDA, above ICT’s current multiple of 10x
    • It also implies a valuation of US$600 per TEU on 2.1mn of TEU capacity annually or US$430 per annual TEU capacity (using Transnet's 2.9mn TEU annual capacity) – broadly in line with replacement cost
    • We estimate DCT-2 could contribute about US$210mn in revenues and US$73mn in EBITDA annually on a consolidated basis
    • While the deal could be earnings dilutive initially, we see meaningful long-term potential upside from tariff adjustments and efficiency improvements
    • We estimate DCT-2 could add ~P30/sh to ICTSI’s NAV
BANKS/DIGITAL FINANCE
August NPLs tick up 8bps MoM to 3.66%

Asset quality deteriorated MoM in August, with Philippine banks’ NPL ratio climbing 8bps to 3.66% from 3.58% in July. That said, the figure is 15bps lower YoY vs the 3.81% in August 2024.  (Note that we compute these ratios excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs) as these create unwanted “noise” in the monthly data. The reported BSP data was up 11bps MoM to 3.59% because the IBL+RRP total was down MoM.) In PHP terms, NPLs stood at P550bn, up 2.7% MoM and 7.3% YoY.

 

Past Due (PD) ratio edged up 2bps Mom to 4.62% in August from 4.60% the previous month, but down 7bps YoY from 4.69%. In nominal terms, PD loans were up 0.8% MoM and 9.8% YoY. Restructured Loans (RL) inched down 1bp MoM to 2.19%. Meanwhile, NPL Cover fell to 94.4% in August, from 95.6% in July.

 

Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) slowed slightly to 11.5% YoY, from 12.1% in July. On a MoM basis, loans were up just 0.3% MoM, which partly caused the worsening NPL and PD ratios MoM. The sharp drop in IBL+RRP saw reported loan growth slow sharply to just +9.9% YoY, from +11.0% in July.

 

By bank type, the MoM asset quality deterioration was most pronounced in the Digibank sector, which saw PD ratio jump 228bps MoM to 21.45% from 19.17% in July. To be fair, that is still 213bps better on a YoY basis. NPL ratios, too blipped up 60bps MoM to 10.5% YoY, though this was 717bps lower YoY, due in part to write-offs in September and December 2024.

 

We would attribute the increase in PD and NPL numbers to the increasing share of unsecured consumer, MSME and microfinance loans in the banking sector. The question remains whether the higher yields and fees more than compensate for higher credit costs. 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

PROPERTY
Philippines seeks exemption from “Keep Call Centers in America” Bill
  • Philippine ambassador to the US Jose Manuel Romualdez said that he is working “…with our friends in the US Congress to exempt the Philippines” from the “Keep Call Centers in America” Bill
  • He also asked US companies and industry associations to lobby for less stringent restrictions on BPO firms already established outside the US
  • In July 2025, US Senators Ruben Gallego and Jim Justice filed a bill that seeks to penalize firms that outsource call centers operations
  • The Secretary of Labor will be required to maintain a publicly available list of all employers that relocate call center or contract center work overseas
    • Such companies will not be eligible for Federal grants or guaranteed loans
    • The head of an agency, when awarding a Federal contract, shall give preference to a United States employer that does not appear on the list
  • Customer service agents must inform the customer of his/her physical location and the customer may request to be immediately transferred to a customer service agent who is physically located in the US
  • The IT-BPO Association previously estimated that outsourcing work to the Philippines (from the US) could result in cost savings of about 80%
  • About 70% of the Philippine BPO industry caters to North America
  • About 70% of BPO revenue is from voice operations
  • BPO firms account for 60%-80% of the occupied office space of the developers we cover
  • MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY26E P/B, and 3.1X FY26E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY26E P/B, and 5.2X FY26E P/E
MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture eyes 84% rice self-sufficiency by 2026
  • Dept. of Agriculture (DA) Assistant Secretary U-Nichols Manalo stated that 84% level rice sufficiency is achievable by end-2026 if its P216bn 2026 budget will be approved
    • The proposed budget includes funding for initiatives to boost rice production, support farmers, and improve farm-to-market logistics
  • Currently, rice self-sufficiency is estimated to be at 77%, with 3.5 MMT of rice imported as of 9M25
    • Rice self-sufficiency was approximately 72% in 2024, with 4.7 MMT of rice was imported last year
    • An 84% rice self-sufficiency target implies that rice imports are projected to amount to 2.5 MMT for 2026
    • The Marcos administration targets around 90% self-sufficiency by 2028
  • Rice (palay) production as of 1H25 hit 9.08 MMT, 6% higher YoY and 45% of the 20.35 MMT FY25 target
    • This is on track to reach the 20.3 MMT full-year target, assuming the 1H continues to account for ~45% of annual output, which has typically been the case in past years
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in September
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 31bps MoM to 18.4% (in September) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index declined 21bps MoM to 23.6%
  • Foreign activity reversed to net buying of US$47, net foreign selling of US$74mn in August
  • Among the PSEi constituents, 11 saw increases in foreign ownership while 19 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • AC – Foreign ownership fell 42bps MoM to 27.0%, as net foreign selling hit US$42.8mn, the second highest for the month. 
    • AREIT – foreign ownership declined 40bps MoM to 4.1%.   
    • PGOLD – foreign ownership increased 37bps MoM to 17.9%, as foreign buying reached US$13.2mn, the highest for the month. 
    • CNPF – foreign ownership climbed 21bps MoM to 23.3%. Net foreign buying hit US$5.6mn, the fifth highest for the month.
