REGIS REPORT

25 July 2025

Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM) disclosed it is finalizing a partnership with Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP), with the latter taking a 40% stake in Aboitiz InfraCapital Corp (AIC). AIC holds the infrastructure investments of the group, which include airports, water, economic estates, et al.

 

GIP is wholly owned by global investment firm BlackRock. It was a partner in the unsuccessful bid of the “super consortium” for the NAIA privatization. Notably, GIP is also part of the consortium that signed a deal to buy the port assets of Li Ka-Shing, which include those at/near the Panama Canal.

 

AEV did not disclose a valuation for the proposed transaction.

 

+++

 

The gaming revenue data from PAGCOR released yesterday point to two diverging trends.

 

First, gaming revenue (GGR) from brick-and-mortar casinos was quite weak, declining 11% QoQ to P44bn. This is not a good indicator for the 2Q numbers for the integrated resorts operators.

 

Second (and in contrast to the physical casinos), GGR from other sources (mostly online gambling), likely grew >20% QoQ to P63bn. This is a strong number and points to a re-acceleration of online gambling activity, prompted by very visible advertising and promotional campaigns by the gaming firms.

 

Note we believe that increased gaming activity is likely to have an unfavorable impact on spending on other things, including consumer products. 

 

+++

 

The government’s budget deficit hit P766bn for 1H25, +25% wider YoY. This is roughly half of this year’s target of P1.56trn, which is, in turn, supposed to be 5.5% of GDP. While the deficit was supposedly “relatively within target”, DOF Secretary Ralph Recto warned that fiscal consolidation will not proceed as quickly as hoped. In other words, deficit/GDP and debt/GDP are unlikely to go down as much as previously forecast.

CONGLOS
AEV partnering with Global Infrastructure Partners
  • AEV is in the process of finalizing a strategic partnership with Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP)
    • Discussions include GIP acquiring a 40% stake in AEV’s infrastructure unit, Aboitiz InfraCapital (AIC)
    • Finalization of the partnership is subject to entering into definitive documentation and the satisfaction of customary conditions including confirmatory due diligence and relevant proposals
  • AIC holds a diversified portfolio of infrastructure assets, with investments in airports, water infrastructure, digital infrastructure, and economic estates
    • Airports – Mactan-Cebu International Airport; Laguindingan International Airport; Bohol-Panglao International Airport
    • Water – Davao City bulk water supply; water and wastewater services for locators at LIMA Estate; and a minority investment in Balibago Waterworks, which has 75 water distribution franchises across the country
    • Digital infra – telecom towers and data enter
    • Economic estates – industrial, commercial, and mixed-use developments in Batangas, Cebu, and Tarlac
  • GIP has over US$183bn in AUM, focusing on energy, transport, digital infra, and water sectors
    • GIP is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Blackrock
GAMING
GGR of licensed casinos falls 11% QoQ in 2Q25, e-games likely grew over 20% QoQ
  • PAGCOR reported that GGR of licenses casinos hit P93.36bn in 1H25 (down 6% YoY)
  • This implies that 2Q25 GGR fell 11% QoQ and YoY to P44.1bn
    • 2Q25 GGR of Okada Manila fell 9% QoQ and 20% YoY to P7.1bn
    • 2Q25 gaming revenue share of Belle Corp (BEL PM) fell 22% QoQ and 37% YoY to P339mn
  • PAGCOR also said that total industry GGR in 1H25 stood at P215bn (up 25% YoY)
  • From this, we estimate that 2Q25 GGR of bingo and e-games likely grew about 25% QoQ and 75% YoY to c. P63bn
    • PLUS is the market leader in e-games, with market share of c. 45% as of 1Q25 

  

 

MACRO
Government posts P242bn budget deficit in June; P766bn deficit as of 1H25
  • The government recorded a budget deficit of P242bn in June, which is wider both by 16% YoY and 67% MoM
  • This brings the 6M25 deficit to P766bn, 25% wider YoY
    • The YTD budget deficit was stated to have "remained relatively within target" at P766bn as the gov't continues to ramp up public spending to support economic growth
    • The wider deficit results from the weaker than expected revenues especially as customs collections continue to lag behind the full-year target
  • Revenue collections in June increased by 4% YoY, with tax revenues up 12% but offset by a significant decline in non-tax revenues of 43%
    • BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections climbed 16% YoY mainly due to higher corporate income tax collections as well as increased personal income tax and VAT collections
    • Customs collections rose by a modest 3% given the impact of reduced tariffs (i.e., rice and EVs)
    • Non-tax revenues dipped by 43% given the high base effect of one-off remittances in 2024
  • Expenditures grew 9% YoY in June on higher primary spending and interest payments
    • Primary spending for the month was higher by 9%
      • The spending growth can be attributed to ramp up in expenses following the election ban on public spending which ended early May after the midterm elections
    • Interest payments inched up 3% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt
  • The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024

 

 

 

MACRO
Dept. of Agriculture: Food supply stable, tempered price upticks despite storm
  • The Department of Agriculture (DA) stated that the supply of crops will continue to be stable and price increases are not expected to exceed 10% despite the recent agricultural damages from the recent tropical storm and southwest monsoon
  • Total damage from the recent storm was reported to be P454mn in agricultural losses, with around 60% of total damage involving palay or unhusked rice, equivalent to 8,200 MT (~P275mn) in lost rice production
    • This represents >1% of total projected local rice production of 12.3mn MT this year
  • The DA has begun distributing P600mn worth of agricultural and fishery inputs (i.e., seeds, pesticides, etc.) to support affected farmers and fisherfolk
  • Consensus expectations for inflation are 2.0% for 2025, while REGIS expects inflation to settle at 1.8% this year
