REGIS REPORT

11 February 2025

One of the key takeaways from the BPI earnings call yesterday was that their digital push is really gaining traction. In 2024, BPI added 5mn new customers, increasing total customer count by nearly 50% YoY to 16mn, from just 11mn in 2023. Of the additional 5mn customers, roughly 52% were acquired digitally (a good portion of the non-digitally acquired customers likely were from the Robinsons Bank merger). Granted, this is a far cry from their target of 50mn customers. But this puts them close to BDO’s 18mn count, and far above MBT’s 5mn.

 

Some other bits from the call, on BPI’s digital / non-branch initiatives.

 

On their apps:

 

  • On BPI’s main customer app, there are 7.8mn enrolled customers, of which 5.1mn are active (up 17.7% YoY)
  • VYBE, BPI’s wallet, has 1.7mn sign-ups already
  • BanKo, their microfinance arm, has 587k users, of which 335k are active

 

On QRPH, which is a potential threat to GCash and other wallets/payments platforms:

 

  • BPI has signed up 1,286 merchants, with 16,000 “doors” (branches/outlets) use BPI OneQRPH payment solution
    • Presumably, a good number of these are Gokongwei/RRHI group merchants
    • 974k transactions, with a total value of P860mn
  • It’s still early days, but the combination of acquiring both customers and merchants could build enough of an ecosystem to really make a dent in usage of pure e-wallets for retail purchases/payments

 

On agency banking – where BPI partners with retail outlets to provide basic banking services (acct opening, deposit taking, loan applications, payments, etc)

 

  • BPI has 22 partners, with a total of 6,400 doors
    • Mostly Gokongwei / RRHI group
    • For 2025E the goal is to have 30 partners with 8,100 doors
  • In 2024, BPI acquired 679,000 new-to-bank customers through agency banking partners

Senate President Francis Escudero plans to begin the impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte-Carpio only in July (yes, five months from now). Around 80% of the House membership endorsed the impeachment complaint against Duterte-Carpio, setting the stage for the trial in the Senate.

 

Should the trial begin in July, it would mean the composition of the Senate would have already been affected by the elections in May.  Consequently, the results of the Senate elections could play a key role in the outcome of any impeachment process.

 

As it stands now, and considering the probable outcome of elections, it does not look certain that the pro-impeachment (pro-administration) bloc can muster the 16 (out of 24 votes) to convict Duterte-Carpio. However, neither does it appear that the pro-Duterte bloc will have the assured nine votes to block any conviction.  A handful of “swing” votes are likely to determine the outcome.

The Philippine Ports Authority announced that volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) port in Manila, MICT, grew by 5% to 2.95mn TEUs in FY24, which is in line with our expectations and suggests that volume growth may have accelerated sharply to 9% YoY in 4Q24 from just 4% in 9M24.

 

Given the robust 4Q24 volume data that came out for its port in Mexico a few weeks ago (here), ICT looks poised to report strong 4Q24 results. However, given the uncertainty arising from Trump’s tariff threats, investors are likely to stay on the sidelines.

Payments made over the National Retail Payments System – via InstaPay or PesoNet – totaled P1,799bn in December, up 38% YoY, slightly slower than the 41% pace in November. This took the full-year 2024 figure to P17,423bn, up 35% YoY, faster than the 29% recorded in 2023. Note that these datasets only capture payments made between platforms (e.g., BDO-to-BPI, GCash-to-MBT, Maya-to-GCash, etc.), and not those done within a platform, and thus severely under-estimates the size of total digital transfers.

 

Growth was again driven by InstaPay – real-time, smaller-ticket payments – which continued to surge 47% YoY in December to P810bn, though this was a sharper slowdown from the 54% YoY clip in November. For FY24, InstaPay recorded a total of P7,347bn, up 46% YoY. The use case for InstaPay continues to broaden, with the volume of transactions jumping 65% YoY in December, and 68% for FY24.

 

Meanwhile payments over PesoNet – larger, batched transactions – came in at P989bn, up 31% YoY in December, a shade slower than the 32% clocked in November. This brought the 2024 total to P10,077bn, up 29% YoY, faster than the 22% growth in 2024.

 

It is worthy of note that the InstaPay values are already nearing those of PesoNet. In December 2024, InstaPay was already c82% of PesoNet. That ratio was 72% in December 2023, and 58% in December 2022. This again reinforces our views that adoption of digital finance is broadening out and is increasingly used for everyday transactions by more individuals and/or more often.

 

Among banks we cover, BPI seems the most prepared to take advantage of increased digitalization, though its competitors BDO, MBT and SECB have made significant investments in their respective IT stacks. Outside of the banks, GCash / Mynt is by far the dominant player in digital payments, which it aims to parley into the high-growth high-margin digital lending business.

Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences see 10 from the Marcos-admin slate

TRANSPORT
ICTSI’s Manila port volumes up 5% YoY in FY24; 4Q24 volumes up 9%
  • In a PR, the Philippine Ports Authority revealed that container throughput at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila port, MICT, grew by 5% YoY to 2.95mn TEUs in FY24, bang in line with Regis FY24E
    • Competitor, South Harbor of Asian Terminal (ATI PM), rose by 6.9% to 1.29mn TEUs in FY24
  • Implies MICT’s volumes grew by 9% YoY to 816k TEUs in 4Q24 alone, accelerating from the 6% pace in 3Q24 and 4% in 9M24
    • South Harbor grew volumes by 8% YoY to 345k TEUs in 4Q24 (vs. +7% YoY in 9M24)
  • We estimate MICT accounts for 23% of ICT’s FY24E volumes, 22% of revenues, and 20% of EBITDA
  • ICT trades at 7.2x FY25E EV/EBITDA (consensus FY25E: 8.0x), below peer average (11x) and historical average (11x)
