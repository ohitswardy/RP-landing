President Marcos has asked for the courtesy resignations of the Cabinet in the wake of election results that were likely to have been a significant disappointment. The question is whether the reshuffle will be able to introduce positive changes or whether they will be cosmetic or worse, purely political.

We refresh our view on power firm ACEN (ACEN PM), cutting our target price to P3.20 and downgrading our rating to HOLD ahead of the planned capital raise. 

 

Our revised TP considers the projected impact of the P30bn rights issue and, most importantly, reflects a bottom-up evaluation rather than a multiples-based peer comparison. We believe there’s value here and the share price now is meaningfully lower than the sum of the value of its individual projects. However, there are a few events / developments that investors are likely to be wary about.  Among them: (1) the rights issue, (2) project deliveries of close to 900MW of new capacity and (3) repairs for the ~200MW of wind capacity knocked down last year.

 

The stock has performed poorly in the last six months following the weak operating results. We think there’s attractive fundamental value at current levels but reckon investors are likely better off waiting for better clarity or a resolution of some of the above issues. Have a look at our note:

The operation of the casino being developed by Suntrust (SUN PM) is now expected to be delayed beyond this year. The casino will have 280 gaming tables and 1,260 gaming machines, adding about 20% to table capacity and 15% to gaming machine capacity in Entertainment City.

 

Suntrust is now controlled by the LET group, which was previously the casino junket operator Suncity. Suntrust does not have a casino license, so the casino is being developed under what we’d describe as effectively a sub-licensee agreement with Travellers.

 

+++

 

The Dept of Agriculture is planning to continue its P20/kg rice program next year with a budget of P10bn (US$180m). Continuing and expanding the program, which was just recently introduced, should help improve consumption for the low-income segments with a trade-off on the fiscal position.

MACRO
DA proposes P10bn budget for continuation of P20/kg rice in 2026
  • The Department of Agriculture (DA) is seeking for a P10bn budget in order to sustain the P20/kg rice program next year
    • The P10bn will be needed to subsidize next year's operation of Food Terminal Inc. (FTI) which currently purchases the aging rice stocks of the National Food Authority (NFA) that are sold in the program
    • Current inventory of the NFA is equivalent to around 8mn 50/kg bags of milled rice or around 400,000 metric tons
  • This proposed P10bn budget for 2026 is double the P5bn currently allocated for the pilot run this year (which started early this month)
    • The P5bn was sourced from the Office of the President's contingency fund—with P4.5bn to be used as rice subsidy, and the remaining for logistics
  • Nationwide rollout of the subsidized rice program is set to continue throughout the year with a target to reach around 14mn Filipinos or 3.3mn households by Sept.
    • Initial target areas will be provinces with high poverty incidence and/or typhoon prone locations (i.e., Zamboanga del Norte, Maguindanao, Sorsogon, Catanduanes)
  • The pilot run of P20/kg rice set till December is only available to vulnerable sectors such as indigents, senior citizens, solo parents, and PWDs who can purchase up to 30kg a month
    • For local gov't units (LGUs) that will share the subsidy, the LGU can determine eligibility for the program
