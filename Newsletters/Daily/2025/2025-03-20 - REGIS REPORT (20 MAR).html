20 March 2025

Regulators have reportedly approved a “temporary” exemption for large IPOs with regard to the 20% public float the companies listing should have. This is likely to ease the listing of a few firms, notably Mynt (the holding company for GCash) as the ability of the domestic market to absorb an offering equivalent to 20% of Mynt (= possibly US$1.6bn) is doubtful.

PLDT (TEL PM) is reportedly acquiring property in Alabang, Muntinlupa at the southern end of Metro Manila with an eye to move its group headquarters there by 2028.  We expect PLDT to sell its properties to partly fund any such move. Among the big landowners in that area is the Filinvest group.

MARKET
SEC open to lower float requirement for large IPOs
  • The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) approved the PSE's proposal to grant an exemption to large IPOs of P5bn or more to initially offer 15% with a commitment to conduct a follow-on offering or private placement within two to three years to be compliant with the minimum 20% public float requirement
    • PSE President Ramon Monzon stated that GCash's planned listing given its size that is "too big for the market to absorb" may not be required to do a follow-on but may have to do so anyway given the index's strict 20% minimum public float
    • Monzon added that the policy is not permanent and will be in place while liquidity problems persist, as the policy will be reassessed for its effectiveness in the future
    • SEC Commissioner McJill Bryant Fernandez also commented that these 20% public float exemption requests are assessed on a case-to-case basis
  • This policy comes to light with GCash's upcoming IPO (tenatively by end-2025) as Globe Telecom's (GLO PM) President and CEO Ernest Cu cited that they are eyeing an $8bn valuation
    • Assuming they offer 15%, it implies an IPO of $1.2bn~ which in peso terms would be larger than the P48.6bn raised by Monde Nissin Corp. (MONDE PM) with their IPO back in 2021

MACRO
BOP surplus at US$3.1bn in February 2025; GIR rises to US$107.4bn
  • The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$3.1bn
    • A significant turnaround from both Jan 2025's $4.1bn deficit and the $196mn deficit in Feb 2024
    • The surplus reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits which includes ROP Global Bonds proceeds and foreign investments net income
    • This brings the YTD deficit at $992mn as of Feb 2025, slightly higher than the $936mn deficit a year ago
  • Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Feb 2025 stood at US$107.4bn, up from the US$103.3bn in end-Jan 2025
    • GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income

Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in February
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 7bps MoM to 19.3% (in February) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 44bps MoM to 24.4%
    • The reduction in the foreign ownership of the PSE Index was partly due to the index rebalancing – CBC and AREIT have lower ownership than NIKL and WLCON, which they replaced in the PSEi

    • MBT – foreign ownership fell 53bps MoM to 20.8%. It also saw the third highest net foreign selling for the month, at US$28.4mn.  
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership dropped 36bps MoM to 14.9%.
    • ALI – foreign ownership declined 31bps MoM to 26.0 %, as it experienced $38.5mn of net foreign outflow, the highest in the market.
    • AC – foreign ownership increased 33bps MoM to 27.1%.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership rose 23bps MoM to 20.6%.
    • BPI – foreign ownership increased 22bps MoM to 18.8%. It also saw the highest foreign buying for the month, at US$12.9mn.

 

UTILITIES
Maynilad IPO

Company name: Maynilad Water Services Inc.

 

  • Brief background: Maynilad is the exclusive holder of the concession that provides water and wastewater services to the West Zone of Metro Manila.  Valid until January 21, 2047, the concession grants Maynilad sole responsibility over its concession area, which spans 540 square km and covers a population of over 10.4mn. With leakage, or non-revenue water (NRW), still above 30%, the West Zone concession poses more challenges and offers more opportunities for growth than Manila Water’s (MWC PM) East Zone concession, in our view. 

 

Outside the West zone, Maynilad wholly owns Philhydro, a bulk water operator in various provinces, and Amayi Water, a water distributor in Boac, Marinduque.  It also owns a 10% stake in Subic Water. Its concession in the west zone accounts for almost all of revenues and profits (c.99%).

 

Maynilad is undertaking this IPO to allow it to comply with the requirement under its legislative franchise to offer 30% of its shares to Filipino citizens by Jan 21, 2027.  Currently, Maynilad is controlled by Metro Pacific, which holds an effective stake of 53.7%. Listed conglomerate, DMCI (DMC PM), also owns 25.7% while Japan-based Marubeni owns 20.3%. 

 

 

  • IPO offer summary: Based on the prospectus, the company plans to offer up to 2.46bn primary shares, inclusive of an optional 646mn shares. These shares may be offered at the maximum offer price of P20/sh, which would put Maynilad’s market capitalization at P161bn.

 

  1. A total of 1.81bn primary shares will be offered to the public, of which 36mn shares (“preferential shares”) will be offered to First Pacific (142 HK), the parent company of its controlling shareholder, Metro Pacific.  These shares will account for 24.4% shares in the company, of which 0.5% will be owned by First Pacific. 

 

  1. A total of 646mn shares (“option shares”) can be offered at the option of the issuer, which consists of 266mn primary “overallotment shares” and 379mn primary “upsize shares”.  Should existing shareholders of Maynilad opt to sell secondary shares during the IPO, the number of upsize shares will be reduced by the number of secondary shares sold. 

 

  • Deal size of up to P49bn; Overhang if option shares are not exercised. Assuming the maximum number of primary shares (2.46bn) is sold to the public, Maynilad’s public float will be 30.1% and it will meet the requirements of its legislative franchise.  This also suggests that the deal size could be as much as P49bn. If less than 30% is sold, Maynilad would not be able to fully comply with its franchise requirements, as its public float will only be 24%.  This scenario could create an overhang due to the expectation of future share sales.  

 

  • Foreign ownership. Given that Maynilad is considered a public utility, it is subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit. This suggests that foreigners can only own up to 2.05bn shares of Maynilad, or ~83% of the total offer shares of 2.46bn. 

 

 

  • Use of proceeds. Depending on the outcome of the IPO, Maynilad could raise as much as P47.6bn, after deducting for underwriting fees, etc. The company plans to use majority (~99%) for its 2025-2026 capex program for the West Zone, which could amount to P58.6bn. 

 

During its 2022 rate rebasing exercise, Maynilad committed to spend P163bn on capex from 2023-2027, of which 34% is for wastewater, 25% for new water sources, and 19% is for NRW management.  The company has spent P52bn of its regulatory commitments so far and will need to spend P111bn over the next three years.
