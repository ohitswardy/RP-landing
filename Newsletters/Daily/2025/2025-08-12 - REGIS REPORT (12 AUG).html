In the United States, Democratic Senator Ruben Gallego and Republican Senator Jim Justice jointly filed the “Keep Call Centers in America” Bill. The Bill seeks to punish firms that outsource call center operations. However, the penalties appear relatively minor, in our view. Companies that outsource call center operations outside the US will not be eligible for Federal grants/loans. Furthermore, when awarding Federal contracts, preference shall be given to firms that do not outsource overseas. Given the relatively minor penalties, we would expect only minor impact to the Philippine BPO sector, even if the Bill were to become law.

Ahead of what are likely to be highly publicized hearings on online gambling at the Senate, Frederick Go, the “economic czar” of President Marcos, is proposing restrictions and rule changes for the regulated online gambling sector. Another day, another proposal.

 

We believe online gambling is not likely to be banned outright but expect restrictions to be imposed in order to curb it. With so many “suggestions” from everyone, one needs to focus on the views of the people who matter.

PROPERTY
US Democratic and Republican senators file "Keep Call Centers" in America Bill
  • US Senators Ruben Gallego (Democrat) and Jim Justice (Republican) filed a bill that seeks to penalize firms that outsource call centers operations
  • The Secretary of Labor will be required to maintain a publicly available list of all employers that relocate call center or contract center work overseas
    • Such companies will not be eligible for Federal grants or guaranteed loans
    • The head of an agency, when awarding a Federal contract, shall give preference to a United States employer that does not appear on the list
  • Customer service agents must inform the customer of his/her physical location and the customer may request to be immediately transferred to a customer service agent who is physically located in the US
  • The IT-BPO Association previously estimated that outsourcing work to the Philippines (from the US) could result in cost savings of about 80%
  • About 70% of the Philippine BPO industry caters to North America
  • About 70% of BPO revenue is from voice operations
  • BPO firms account for 60%-80% of the occupied office space of the developers we cover
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV - FLI is trading at 61% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY25E P/B, and 5.6X FY25E P/E
    • 43% of Megaworld’s NAV - MEG is trading at 6% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY25E P/B, and 3.5X FY25E P/E
    • 28% of Robinsons Land’s NAV - RLC is trading at 67% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY25E P/B, and 5.2X FY25E P/E
GAMING
Higher minimum bets and entry fees proposed for online gambling
  • Frederick Go, Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs, proposed higher minimum bets and the imposition of an entry fee on online gambling
  • As an example, he said that online gambling firms could impose an entry fee of P1,000 and minimum bets of P100
    • The minimum bet on some games is currently P1  
  • BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. also said that the online gambling should be made more difficult
    • The BSP is finalizing rules that will require banks, e-wallets, and other financial service providers to adopt stronger safeguards against gambling-related harm
    • The upcoming measures will include:
    • Strict identity verification, including biometric checks, such as facial recognition, to ensure only eligible individuals can use their funds for online gambling
    • Daily limits on gambling-related transfers to reduce excessive financial losses
    • Time-based restrictions on gambling payments to help curb impulsive behavior
    • User tools for setting personal spending caps, taking voluntary breaks, or self-excluding from gambling transactions
  • DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is the market leader in online gambling, with market share of about 45%
  • PLUS is trading at 8.3X FY25E P/E (based on consensus forecast) 
TRANSPORT
Philippine ports’ foreign container volumes rise 10% YoY in 2Q25 and 12% YoY in 1H25
  • Data from the Philippine Ports Authority showed that nationwide foreign container volumes grew by 10% YoY to 1.53mn TEUs in 2Q25, moderating from the 15% pace in 1Q25
    • Growth decelerated due to the normalization of import volume growth to +6% in 2Q25 from +16% in 1Q25
    • Export boxes grew by 13% YoY in 2Q25, just mildly decelerating from the 14% pace in 1Q25
    • Data is consistent with 2Q25 GDP and merchandise trade  
    • Brings 1H25 nationwide container volumes up 12% to 3.0mn TEUs
  • ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) likewise saw volume growth decelerate to 6% (to 782k TEUs) in 2Q25 from 14% in 1Q25
    • MICT’s competitor in Manila, Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbor, saw volumes grow 21% YoY to 368k TEUs in 2Q25 from +25% in 1Q25, indicating market share loss for MICT
    • We estimate South Harbor’s capacity is nearly fully utilized and the port does not have space for expansions so its market share gains will unlikely be sustained
    • Brings 1H25 container volumes at MICT to 1.54mn TEUs, up 10% YoY and just slightly behind our full-year forecast of 3.17mn TEUs (+8%)
    • ICTSI trades at 9.6x FY25E EV/EBITDA (11x consensus) vs. peer median of 11.3x and peer average of 12.5x

 

 

MACRO
Philippines seeks exemption from US tariff on semiconductor exports
  • Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (SAPIEA) Frederick Go stated that the gov't will be in dialogue with the US Trade Representative for clarification on the tariff on semiconductors
    • Recall that US President Donald Trump announced last week a 100% tariff on imports of semiconductors to encourage onshoring of manufacturing
  • Go stated that they are lobbying for Philippines' semiconductor exports to be exempted, "if there is such a thing"
  • The Philippines exported about $39bn worth of electronics in 2024, about 53% of total exports. Of this, about $29bn is semiconductors
    • Total PH exports to the US was $12.1bn, of which about $6.4bn (or 53%) was electronics
    • There’s no sub-breakdown into semicons, but assuming the same ratio to the total exports, we get about $4.75bn, which is equivalent to about 6.5% of total PH exports
MACRO
BSP: August rate cut on the table, two more cuts possible this year
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that an August rate cut is "quite likely" and that two more cuts this year remain "more likely" than just one, depending on economic data
    • This implies 125bp total rate cuts for 2025, bringing the key policy rate to 4.75% by year-end from the current 5.25%
  • The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled for Aug 28 (fourth out of six this year)
  • For 2025, Bloomberg median consensus and REGIS expects 75bp total worth of cuts to 5.00%
