REGIS REPORT

19 February 2025

The implementing rules and regulations (IRR) covering the CREATE MORE legislation (enacted Nov 2024) have now been released. Among the more relevant items in the law (and IRR) are the provisions explicitly allowing greater work-from-home flexibility for firms granted fiscal incentives and the exemption from VAT of firms registered with the investment promotion agencies (IPA).

 

+++

 

Government data showed that nationwide container throughput grew 5% in FY24. The available figures also showed ICTSI’s (ICT PM) MICT saw a 6% increase in volumes for 4Q alone, which would be in line with our estimates.

 

+++

 

The Dept of Transportation is allowing a 21% increase in tariffs for the country’s oldest light rail line (LRT-1). Beginning April 2, the minimum fare will rise to P16.25 ($0.28) and the end-to-end fare will become P52 (US$0.90). These rates are still lower than what LRMC, the LRT-1 operator, sought and are roughly the same as what LRMC should have gotten in 2022 under the pre-agreed formula.

 

Ayala Corp (AC PM) has been reported to be trying to sell its 35% stake in LRMC.

 

+++

The government is contemplating a new incentive program for the “manufacture” of cars in the country. The current program (dubbed “CARS”), which gives subsidies to car manufacturers (assemblers, really) in the form of tax incentives, will expire next year.  Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), a subsidiary of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) is one of the two firms that qualified for CARS. The extension of the incentive program is likely to be positive for TMP, which is among the few car firms with assembly operations in the country.

 

+++

 

Foreign ownership levels for Philippine listed equities dropped another 29bp in January to 19.2%, following another month of net foreign selling (>US$100m).

MACRO
CREATE MORE Implementing rules and regulations signed and released
  • The implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the CREATE MORE Act were released
    • IT-BPO firms can continue to enjoy incentives despite implementing work-from-home policies
    • The IRR clarifies that purchases of goods and services made by firms that are registered with Investment Promotion Agencies (IPA) are not subject to value-added tax (VAT)
      • This applies to purchases that are "directly attributable" which allows more VAT zero-rating purchases not just for primary business operations as per previous version of CREATE—but also for financial, consultancy services, marketing, and administrative functions
    • Importation of raw materials and capital equipment by these Registered Business Enterprises (RBE) shall also be exempt from customs duties
    • All IPAs shall establish a one-stop shop to assist RBEs in obtaining relevant business permits/licenses 
  • BPO firms under PEZA can now implement work-from-home policies (up to 50% WFH) and still enjoy all the fiscal incentives
    • Prior to CREATE MORE, BPO firms were only allowed 30% WFH
    • Most PEZA-accredited BPO firms are likely instituting c. 30% WFH
  • BPO firms availing of BOI incentives can enjoy all the said incentives even if they implement 100% WFH
TRANSPORT
Philippine ports’ container throughput growth accelerates to 8% YoY in 4Q; FY24 volumes up 5%
  • Philippine container ports handled 1.52mn TEUs of foreign containers in 4Q24, up by 8% YoY and accelerating from the 7% pace in 3Q24
    • Brings nationwide foreign container volumes up 5% YoY to 5.62mn TEUs in FY24
  • There were slight rounding errors from the PPA’s FY24 PR for the two main ports in Manila
    • Official data showed that ICTSI’s MICT grew volumes by 5% YoY to 2.927mn TEUs in FY24 (in line) and in 4Q24 alone, MICT’s volumes grew by 6% YoY (similar pace in 3Q24: +6%)
    • MICT lost market share (again) in 4Q24, after Asian Terminal’s (ATI PM) South Harbor posted 9% YoY growth rate during the quarter, albeit a slower pace than in 3Q24 (+17% YoY)
    • We estimate MICT accounts for 23% of ICT’s FY24E volumes, 22% of revenues, and 20% of EBITDA
  • ICT trades at 7.3x FY25E EV/EBITDA (consensus FY25E: 8.1x), below peer average (11x) and historical average (11x)

 

CONGLOS
Government approves 21% fare hike for LRT-1
  • The Department of Transportation approved a 21% fare adjustment for LRT-1 operator, Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC), effective April 2
    • Boarding fee will rise to P16.25 from P13.29 while the increment per km fare will rise to P1.47 from P1.21 currently
    • Single end-to-end journey fares will rise to P52 from P45
  • The fare hike is lower than LRMC’s petition of 36%
    • LRMC said the newly approved fair scheme is only equal to its initial petition in 2022, as provided for in the concession agreement (CA)
    • Under its CA, LRMC is entitled to fare hikes of 10.25% every two years, which is aligned with an annual 5% rate adjustment that was initially due in 2016
    • Since LRMC took over the concession in 2015, there has only been one fare increase approved in 2023, after the government deferred fare adjustments in 2018, 2020, and 2022
  • LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (35.8%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19.2%), and Macquarie Infra (10%)
MACRO
Government eyes new car manufacturing program
  • The Department of Trade and Industry plans to roll out the Revitalizing the Automotive Industry for Competitiveness Enhancement (RACE) program
    • According to the Office of the Special Assistant to the President for Investments and Economic Affairs (OSAPIEA) Secretary Frederick Go
  • Program is a re-designed version of the Comprehensive Automotive Resurgence Strategy (CARS) program
    • RACE program aims to provide incentives to more participants than CARS, which only allotted three slots for participating models (only two slots were availed – Toyota’s Vios and Mitsubishi’s Mirage)
    • Unlike the CARS Program, RACE will be a department initiative and would not need an executive order for implementation since the new program has a budget allocation in the 2025 national budget (P250mn)
GAMING
BLOOM Acting COO reaches settlement with Australian Securities and Investments Commission
  • Greg Hawkins, the former Chief Casino Operator of Star Entertainment Group (AGR AU), agreed to settle with the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)
    • ASIC accused Mr. Hawkins of failing to properly manage money laundering risks associated with Suncity (in 2018 and 2019) and not informing the Board of the junket operator’s reported criminal links
  • Mr. Hawkins will pay A$180,000 (US$115,000) and will be prohibited from managing ASX-listed companies for 18 months
    • Bloomberry will not pay for this settlement
  • The case against Mr. Hawkins was first filed in 2022 and BLOOM management was aware of this when he was hired in November 2023
  • Mr. Hawkins was appointed Acting COO of BLOOM in December 2024
  • The settlement should have no impact on Bloomberry
MARKET
Foreign ownership of Philippine market falls in January
  • We estimate that foreign ownership of the overall market fell 29bps MoM to 19.2% (in January) while foreign ownership of the PSE Index dropped 5bps MoM to 24.8%
  • Net foreign selling increased to US$113mn, from US$104mn in December 
  • Of the 30 PSEi constituents, 9 saw increases in foreign ownership while 21 experienced declines
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • BLOOM – foreign ownership fell 74bps MoM to 15.3%. Net foreign selling was the eighth highest for the month, at US$8.1mn.  
    • CNVRG – foreign ownership dropped 64bps MoM to 16.7%.
    • MBT – foreign ownership declined 51bps MoM to 21.3%, as it experienced $20.6mn of net foreign selling, the second highest for the month.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership increased 39bps MoM to 20.4%.
