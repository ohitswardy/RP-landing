REGIS REPORT

14 August 2025

Here’s our summary of 2Q/1H results this morning, amidst peak results period.

 

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (=) – 1H profits were largely unchanged YoY (-1%) at P5.6bn, as revenues rose 15% and systemwide sales grew 19%. Operating profit was up 18% but non-operating items (e.g. financing) took net income/growth lower.  SSSG for the Philippine businesses slowed to a still respectable +6.4% while that for the international operations improved to +4.1% (vs +0.7%). More after the briefing tomorrow.

 

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (=) – Ayala Corp.’s 1H25 recurring profit of P23.7bn (-2%) was broadly in line with both consensus and Regis estimates. Core profit dipped 1% to P12.4bn in 2Q25, as softness at Globe and ACEN were partly made up for by better profits at BPI, ALI, and a marked recovery at its portfolio investments. The latter was buoyed by strong BYD demand at AC Mobility and continued double-digit revenue growth at AC Health. More below.

 

  • Puregold (PGOLD PM) (-) – Net income for 1H grew 7% to P5.30bn as revenues rose 12%. While the bottom line appears to be lagging estimates, we note PGOLD’s strong top line numbers and the probability of a profit bounce in 4Q/2H.  SSSG for 2Q alone was +6.3%, which is outpacing most of its direct competitors.

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (-) – Profits for 1H rose 7% to P3.9bn, which look a tad behind FY25 estimates. For 2Q alone, revenues were up only 1% as branded revenues rose +6% and the OEM business contracted 17%. Notably, management trimmed its full-year guidance to “high single to low double-digit” top line growth (vs. 10-15% growth previously).

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – Management provided more color on its 2Q25 results with its briefing yesterday. Apart from the +8% QoQ and 35% YoY profit expansion to P9.3bn in 2Q25, some of the key highlights were (a) management’s view that margin gains at Toyota may not hold, as promotional activities ramp up in line with seasonal patterns and (b) property reservation sales softened in 2Q and unsold inventory levels remain elevated at ~30months. Notwithstanding management’s cautious margin guidance, we see the strong 1H results and recent, relative stability of the PHP/USD to prompt further Street upgrades.

  • Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) (=) – Both revenue and income rose 12% in 1H to P22bn and P5.9bn, respectively. The headline figures appear in line with Street estimates, but management reduced its guidance by a meaningful amount.  Converge is now guiding for revenue growth of 10-12% (down from 14-16%).

 

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (-) - reported 1H25 NPAT of P5.9bn, up 8% YoY, behind our forecast (P15.5bn for FY25) and consensus (P13.3bn). Revenues (+23%) and POPP (+19%), were strong, both broadly in line with our expectations, but dragged by provision expense of P5.1bn (+53%) as the bank cleans up its credit card book. Loan growth slowed to 16% (from 19% in 1Q); consumer/MSME was still up 32% YoY, and account for 36% of total loans. Meanwhile, 2Q25 already included the 25% stake in Home Credit Phils (HCPH). It is unclear how much of the associate income P211mn in 2Q was attributable to HCPH. More after the briefing on Monday, 18 August at 3pm. For now, see more details below.

 

  • Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) (-) – During its 2Q25 briefing, the company revealed that the two junket operators that closed in May were replaced in July. Meanwhile, mass table volumes at Solare Entertainment City fell 8% QoQ due to lower premium mass activity from Chinese and Koreans. The company spent P460mn in marketing expenses for the soft launch of its new online platform, MegaFUNalo, in 2Q25 but will hold back spending until the full launch in 1Q26.

CONSUMER
JFC 1H25 net profit -1% to P5.6bn, top line up 15% to P148bn—in line
  • 1H Headline net profit -1% to P5.6bn. Revenue increased 15% to P147.9bn with systemwide sales (SWS) growing 18%
  • For 2Q25 alone, top line rose 15% YoY to P77.6bn with SWS growing 20% YoY
    • Operating income climbed 19% to P6.0bn, while headline net profit was up 6% to P3.2bn
  • For the domestic business 2Q SSSG was +6.4%, a mild deceleration from +8.5% in 1Q
  • The international business saw 2Q SSSG accelerate to +4.1% from +0.7% in 1Q25, with the strong results led by the NA Asian Brands [Jollibee, Red Ribbon, Chowking] (+7.8%)

 

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp - 2Q25: Positive momentum continues for portfolio investments
  • 1H25 core profit dips 2% to P23.7bn, within Street and REGIS forecasts. Headline profit of P23.4bn (+5%) included net one-off losses mostly from the impairment of ACEN’s Vietnam investments offset (ACEN) by gains from AC Ventures-MUFG transaction and others. Removing one-offs, recurring profit of P23.7bn (-2%) is broadly in line with both Regis and Street forecasts. Weakness at GLO and ACEN were partly mitigated by profit gains at ALI and BPI, as well as a profit turnaround at its portfolio investments. The latter was driven by the strong performance of AC Mobility, AC Health and others. 
  • AC Mobility unit sales surge >2x. Sales of NEV (largely of BYD vehicles) propelled the two-fold increase in AC Mobility’s 1H25 unit sales to 20k (vs. 9.1k units in 1H24). The 1H25 figure implies that sales volumes in 2Q25 jumped 17% QoQ, 122% YoY to 10.8k units. While 1H25 profit of P122mn (+408%) included P277mn in income from dividends, we are encouraged by the solid sales momentum. Its EV charging network has expanded to 239 as of end-June from 120 as of end-2024, with management aiming to end the year with 700 charging points.
  • AC Health: Solid topline momentum boosts EBITDA and trims losses. AC Health’s revenues jumped 35% to P6bn in 1H25, which implies that revenues rose 19% QoQ and 46% YoY to P3.3bn, driven by strong patient count and higher bed occupancy. EBITDA rose 144% YoY to P586mn in 1H25, further trimming losses to P100mn.
  • Management said that the planned equity infusion from a Temasek-backed fund, which will give the fund a 16% stake, values AC Health at a post-money EV of US$500mn, equivalent to “high-teens to low-20s” forward multiples. AC Health plans to use the funds to expand its portfolio to 10 hospitals (vs. 6), 300 clinics (vs. 270), and 1,150 pharmacies (vs. 879) by 2027.

 

 

BANKS
SECB 1H25 NPAT P5.9bn, strong PPOP but credit cost still high
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1H25 NPAT of P5.9bn, up 8% YoY, tracking behind our forecast (P15.5bn for FY25) and consensus (P13.3bn). Revenues (+23%) and POPP (+19%), both broadly in line with our expectations. As with the previous quarter, profits were dragged by provision expense of P5.1bn (+53%) as the bank cleans up its credit card book. Management had previously guided that credit costs (161bps in 2Q) would peak in 2/3Q25. Meanwhile, 2Q25 already included the 25% stake in Home Credit Phils (HCPH). It is unclear how much of the associate income P211mn in 2Q was attributable to HCPH, but it does look like it would be in line with our previous estimates of a PE of around 10x. More after the briefing on Monday , 18 August at 3pm.

 

Meanwhile, some highlights:

  • Loan growth slowed to 16% YoY (from 19% in 2Q and 26% in 4Q), in line with the bank’s guidance to end the year at 10-12% to conserve capital. Consumer/MSME loans were still up 32%, driven by credit cards +43%, auto +54%, and MSME +46%. Mortgages were up 17%. Consumer/MSME loans are 36% of total , steady QoQ
  • NIMs improved 16bps QoQ to 4.64%. Loan yields jumped 30bps QoQ and 79bps YoY, on the improving loan mix. Overall asset yields were up a more modest 17bps, while funding costs dipped 5bps QoQ.
  • Opex remained elevated, up 26% YoY on increased staff and IT costs. Despite that, PPOP was up 19% in 1H25
  • Provision expense surged again in 2Q25, up 51% to P2.7bn, or a credit cost of 161bps (was 147bps in 1Q25). This was flagged by management in 1Q25 as the bank continues to clean up its problematic credit card loans. The bank had guided that the cleanup would be complete by 2025, and that credit costs could peak in 2Q/3Q.

MACRO
Gov't revises debt-to-GDP targets
  • Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) Assistant Secretary Romeo Balanquit guided that the country's debt-to-GDP ratio would settle at 61.3% this year, increase to 61.8% in 2026, and improve to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028
    • This was revised from 60.4% in 2025, 60.2% in 2026, 58.4% in 2027, and 56.9% by 2028
  • The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio stands at 63.1% as of end-June
    • This is up from 62% the prior quarter and 60.7% at end-2024
  • Outstanding debt as of end-June stood at P17.27trn (+12% YoY, +2.1% MoM)
    • The total debt is already 99.5% of the total debt expectation this year (P17.36trn)
    • By end-2026, debt is seen to rise 10% YoY to P19.06trn
