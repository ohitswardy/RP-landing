REGIS REPORT

07 April 2025

The CPI print on Friday (=1.8% CPI inflation) conforms with our predictions of declining headline inflation, led by falling food prices. Notably, vegetable prices dropped 6% while rice prices declined 2%. With this latest data point showing inflation below the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) 2-4% target band, we believe it’s almost certain that the BSP will cut policy rates next week.

 

Over the near term, global macro developments are likely to help keep inflation subdued. Oil prices plunged and the USD weakened after the US government unilaterally raised import tariffs on all trading partners in a bid to rein in its trade deficit. The implication of these actions will of course be more far-reaching than just on Philippine CPI.

MACRO
Inflation rate eases further to 1.8% in March
  • Headline inflation eased to 1.8% in March from 2.1% in the previous month of Feb, marking the lowest print since May 2020
    • This is lower than the consensus forecast of 2.0% and stands at the lower-end of the BSP's forecast range of 1.7-2.5%
  • The downtrend in overall inflation for March was primarily driven by slower annual price increases in food & non-alcoholic beverages (2.2% YoY in Mar vs. 2.6% in Feb) and in restaurants/misc. goods & services (2.3% YoY in Mar vs. 2.8% in Feb)
    • The transport index also saw a faster YoY decrease at -1.1% YoY vs. -0.2% in Feb
  • On a MoM basis, vegetable and rice prices dropped 6.1% and 2.0%, respectively
    • Notably, rice prices are on their ninth consecutive MoM price decline; rice prices are now down 7.7% YoY
      • According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Apr 3), prices for local and imported well-milled rice ranged from P38-54/kg and P43-46/kg, respectively vs. P40-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 3
      • The current MSRP for imported rice is P49/kg
  • Core inflation decelerated to 2.2% in Mar from 2.4% in Feb. While core non-food inflation also dropped to 2.0% from 2.2% for the same periods
  • Inflation for the bottom 30% income group dipped to 1.1%, down from 1.5% in Feb and 4.6% in Mar 2024
  • This brings the YT-Mar inflation rate to 2.2%, within the 2-4% gov't target for this year
    • Consensus and Regis expectations for the full year average is 3.0%
  • The next Monetary Board meeting (2nd out of 6 this year) is at April 10, Thursday
    • For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps worth of cuts to 5.00%
    • We expect 50 bps worth of cuts to 5.25% but more could come if inflation stays here or if the US Fed cuts rates more than 75 bps
