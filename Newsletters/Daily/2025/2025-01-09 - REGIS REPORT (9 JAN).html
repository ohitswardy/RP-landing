REGIS REPORT

09 January 2025

Rice export prices out of Vietnam are in freefall. As of Wed night (early morning US time), the price of the grain collapsed 4% to $444.50/MT, compared to last week's $461.75. That's down 28% from the high of $619.50 in December 2023. This is the lowest level since March 2023. 

 

VN and TH rice prices started to fall sharply after India and Pakistan lifted export bans in October 2024, due to large oversupplies as a result of the ban. Since then, both countries have been competing aggressively to supply other countries, most recently with Indonesia. In December, both nations started negotiations with the Philippines for a combined 2mn MT of rice (1mn MT each). Last week, the Department of Agriculture (DA) said it expects to sign a deal with Pakistan by June. For perspective, the Philippines last year imported a record 4.68mn MT of rice. Securing 2mn MT would be huge. 

 

This opens the door for further downside for local rice prices. As of December, the price of well-milled rice fell to P40/kg, sharply lower than the P47/kg in September. We reckon that traders have not yet fully passed on the cut in rice tariffs.

 

This bodes well for lower-income consumers. In December, the headline CPI accelerated to 2.9%, from 2.5% in November. However, rice CPI dropped to just 0.8% YoY. This is crucial for low-income households, for whom rice is 18% of their CPI basket. In fact, for December, inflation for the "bottom 30%" of households dipped to 2.5%, slower than headline inflation.

 

According to the BSP, consumer confidence (outlook for next quarter) improved significantly in 4Q24. To be fair, the improvement was seen more in the middle- and higher-income brackets. But a continued fall in rice prices should help lift lower-income families' sentiment, too.

Rep. Joey Salceda, chairman of the House Ways and Means Committee, is in the news today for two different reasons.

 

First: he filed a new bill seeking to grant a congressional franchise to ABS-CBN (ABS PM) for the latter to operate a media business. Recall that the franchise of ABS-CBN was not renewed by Congress under the previous (Duterte) administration.

 

Second: Salceda called for a refund of “unauthorized” “excess revenues collected by the grid operator, National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP). Salceda claims the excess revenues amount to P204bn (US$3.5bn on current FX rates). Salceda is also calling for an investigation into the true ownership % of China’s State Grid in NGCP, which is supposed to be limited to 40%. Listed firm Synergy Grid (SGP PM) has 40% economic ownership of NGCP.

The government Commission on Audit (COA) is telling the water regulator MWSS to collect P5.1bn in payments from Maynilad Water.  Note the amount already forms part of Maynilad’s liabilities and committed payments (i.e., it is already on Maynilad’s books).  Maynilad is 25% owned by DMCI Holdings (DMC PM).

MEDIA
House receives new bill for ABS-CBN franchise
  • The bill (House Bill No. 11252) filed at the House of Representatives last Tuesday seeks to provide media company ABS-CBN Corp. (ABS PM) with a new 25-year franchise
    • This comes more than four years since lawmakers denied its initial franchise renewal application
  • Recall that ABS-CBN was forced to halt broadcast operations in May 2020 after former President Duterte's allies in Congress denied its franchise renewal application
    • They were denied on the alleged tax issues and violations of the original franchise's terms
  • Rep. Joey Salceda said the bill was filed as he states that it is beneficial for a country to have a free market of ideas in the reporting of events and regarding what is happening in the nation
    • He also stated that the SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) and BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) have cleared ABS-CBN of the allegations against them
  • Four bills seeking to provide ABS a new franchise have been filed since 2022, as the proposals remain pending at the House legislative franchises committee
  • For the 9M24, ABS-CBN saw a net loss of P2.59bn, on the back of lower cable TV and broadband revenues
POWER
Congressman seeks refund of NGCP’s alleged ‘excess revenues’ worth P204bn, calls probe on ownership
  • House Committee on Ways and Means chairperson and Albay 2nd District Rep. Joey Salceda is seeking a refund of alleged “unauthorized” excess revenues worth a “minimum” of P204bn collected by the National Grid Corp. of the Philippines (NGCP) from 2016-2020
    • Salceda said that based on the Energy Regulatory Commission’s preliminary rate review, only P183.5bn of NGCP’s P387.8bn revenue for the period was ERC-approved
  • Congressman Salceda also called for a probe into the ownership of NGCP to determine whether it violated the Anti-Dummy Law and foreign ownership limit of 40%
    • He claimed that two officials of the State Grid Corp. of China report to the Chinese Communist Party, and that State Grid Corp owns 47.8% of NGCP, above the maximum 40% threshold for public utilities
UTILITIES
Commission on Audit tells MWSS to collect P5.1bn concession fees from Maynilad
  • The Commission on Audit has required the Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) to provide proof of its efforts to collect concession fees worth P5.071bn from water concessionaire Maynilad
    • Claim covers unpaid concession fees in March 2001 due to Maynilad’s financial difficulties
    • MWSS signed a loan with BNP Paribas in 2004 to cover the unremitted concession fees
    • In Dec 2007, the Rehabilitation Court declared Maynilad had "successfully implemented the DCRA subject to fulfillment of certain conditions."
    • MWSS then requested payment from Maynilad of the prepayment premium on the BNP Paribas Notes, but the latter declined, citing the 2009 court order.
  • MWSS earlier requested approval to drop the penalty from its books
  • Note that this disputed claim is included in Maynilad’s service concession liabilities and committed spending plan for 2023-2047
  • Maynilad is a 25%-owned affiliate of DMCI (DMC PM) and accounts for 23% of its NAV; DMC trades at a 19% discount to its market-based NAV of P13.44/sh
