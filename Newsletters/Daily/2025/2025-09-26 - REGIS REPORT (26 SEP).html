REGIS REPORT

26 September 2025

It looks like the IPO of Maynilad may really happen now, even if there’s an announcement of a slight delay which pushed back the target listing to Oct 30.

 

The proposed price range looks attractive relative to its regulatory value (i.e. “opening cash position” or OCP), which we reckon is the more appropriate valuation benchmark vs PER and EV/EBITDA multiples. The estimated interim OCP now is P144bn and that figure will compound by 12% annually. This compares with the enterprise value (EV) at the top of the price range (P20/share) of ~P180bn.

 

+++

 

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona provided what we’d describe as noncommittal answer to questions about possible rate cuts. He says he’s inclined to keep rates on hold if current growth and inflation rates stay intact but did not rule out a cut.

 

The BSP’s Monetary Board meets on Oct 9, by which time there will be one additional CPI data point. GDP growth data for 3Q will not be available by then.

 

+++

 

Initial signs of infrastructure spending slowing down are filtering in. We are likely to see public works and infrastructure spending to slow down even more in the coming months as investigations into the corruption scandal ramp up.

UTILITIES
Maynilad pushes back IPO timetable
  • Maynilad has revised its IPO timetable to allow more time for investor education
    • Investor education timetable extended to Sep 22 to Oct 3 (from Sep 22-26)
    • Institutional roadshow/bookbuilding pushed back to Oct 6-17 (from Sep 29-Oct 10)
    • Listing date move to Nov 7 from Oct 30
  • Maynilad is offering 2.289bn (1.9bn primary and 355mn secondary) shares to the public at a maximum offer price of P20/sh
    • At the max. offer price and full subscription of offer shares, Maynilad’s market cap could be P151bn, and public float will be 30%
    • Under its franchise, Maynilad is required to offer at least 30% of its outstanding capital stock by Jan 21, 2027

 

 

MACRO
BSP open to Oct cut if growth slows
  • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that a fourth consecutive 25bp rate cut to 4.75% by Oct is a possibility if output slows down because of the lack of demand
    • Remolona also noted that two 25bp cuts in Oct and Dec are "possible but not likely"
    • He emphasized that the BSP is inclined to hold rates steady as long as inflation and growth forecasts remain intact
      • For 2025, the BSP expects GDP growth to settle at the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% goal, and inflation to be at 1.7%
        • Consensus and Regis expect GDP growth and inflation to be 5.5% and 1.8%, respectively
  • Recall that BSP Governor Remolona during the last Monetary Board meeting in Aug mentioned that the current rate of 5.00% is the "sweet spot" given recent data
  • The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively
    • For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%

 

MACRO
Infrastructure spending dips in July given slow DPWH disbursements
  • Overall infrastructure spending (incl. infra components of subsidy and equity to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell 29% MoM and 20% YoY to P114.7bn in July
    • Due to slow Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) disbursements given project schedules, delays in procurement, incomplete submission of progress billings and required documents by contractors
    • Also cited the new tax clearance requirement of the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) for the release of final payments
      • Clearance will only be given when a contractor has no outstanding tax liabilities
  • Brings 7M25 infra spend to P835bn, -3% YoY and behind the 1H government’s target (here)
    • Government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP)

 

MACRO
Budget secretary expects slower gov't spending given corruption crackdown
  • Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman states that gov’t spending may slow due to the ongoing investigations into the alleged corruption in flood control projects
    • The investigations by lawmakers and the independent commission for infrastructure (ICI) as well as suspensions on multiple DPWH projects were cited to temper infrastructure outlays
    • The probes were also cited to possibly impact other agencies’ budget utilizations
  • Pangandaman, however, noted that the spending slowdown is unlikely to significantly affect GDP, as gov’t spending is less than 20% of the nation's economic output
MACRO
ADB still willing to fund flood control projects
  • Asian Development Bank (ADB) Country Director for the Philippines Andrew Jeffries stated that the flood control corruption scandal will not deter it from financing work in the Philippines given continued "close oversight" of its investments
    • Jeffries emphasized that the recent corruption scandal "doesn't stop our work, but makes the [due diligence] processes we have in place all the more important"
  • The ADB has a US$13bn financing portfolio in the Philippines
    • One ADB-funded flood control project (covering two river basins in Mindanao and northern Luzon) was said to be underway, while another flood mitigation project was also noted to be in the pipeline
