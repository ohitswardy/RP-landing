Another raft of results today. Plus, comments on GDP, government debt, tariffs, and (never ending!) online gambling below.

 

  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (+) – Tailwinds on both revenues and costs fueled the 214% YoY improvement in CEB’s 2Q25 profit of P3.72bn. Management expressed cautious optimism entering the lean season, noting that (a) forward bookings are improving and (b) fares/yields are up vs. last year. The company also declared dividends of P9.12/sh to convertible preferred shareholders, which represents the cumulative unpaid dividends since 2021 and is equivalent to a yield of 19%.

Philippine Real GDP growth was rather lackluster at +5.5% YoY. Personal Consumption only received a mild bump (+5.5% YoY vs +5.3%) despite the very low inflation, and despite elections spending from the May midterm elections. As we wrote recently, we suspect that the tepid consumer growth was due at least in part to the surge in online gambling. According to the regulator, online gambling GGRs exploded +23% QoQ in 2Q25, far outstripping the single-digit QoQ growth seen by listed traditional consumer companies that have reported thus far. Capital Formation was also very weak up just 0.6% YoY, despite the banking sector posting healthy loan growth of 12%.

 

There is continued hope for stronger 2H25 growth, with a) soft inflation (+0.9% in July) boosting consumption, b) interest rate cuts encouraging capex spend, and c) the lifting of the election ban on construction. That said, we still have our FY2025E forecast at 5.5%.

 

The lukewarm GDP growth – particularly on a nominal basis, up just 7.4% YoY – has further negative impact on all xxx-to-GDP ratios. The Treasury announced that government’s Debt-to-GDP ratio popped to 63.1%, up from 62.0% in 1Q25 and 60.7% at end-2024. This is far higher than the government’s target of 60.4% by year-end. The budget deficit, too, was a bit higher at 5.7% of GDP in 1H25, versus the FY25 forecast of 5.5%.

 

The low inflation, and soft GDP prints likely give the BSP further rationale to reduce policy rates by 25bps at its Monetary Board meeting on 28August.

 

 

+ + +

 

US President Donald Trump announced 100% tariffs on semiconductor imports. While the world digests all the ways this can be short-circuited, here are some hard numbers.

 

  • The Philippines exported about $39bn worth of electronics in 2024, about 53% of total exports. Of this, about $29bn is semiconductors.
  • Total PH exports to the US was $12.1bn, of which about $6.4bn (or 53%) was electronics. There’s no sub-breakdown into semicons, but assuming the same ratio to the total exports, we get about $4.75bn. That’s equivalent to about 6.5% of total PH exports.

 

We await further details of Trump’s plans before assessing any potential impact to the Philippine economy.

A couple of news bits on online gambling.

 

  • The BSP is finalizing rules on banks/wallets and online gambling. These include tougher user ID verification, daily betting limits, and time-based restrictions on transferring funds to gaming sites.
  • President Marcos said he is creating a body to help the government formulate policy on online gaming. He said the panel will include Catholic bishops, who have been loudly opposed to the industry.

MACRO
2Q25 GDP grows 5.5%—in line with expectations
  • GDP grew at 5.5% in 1Q, a tad higher than the 5.4% growth in 1Q25 but much lower than the 6.5% growth rate back in 2Q24
    • The 5.5% GDP growth is in line with consensus expectations of 5.5%
    • The print is at the lower end of the government’s 5.5–6.5% target range (was previously 6-7%)
  • On the demand side, the slight acceleration in growth from private consumption (+5.5%) was offset by the slowdown in gov't consumption (+8.7% from +18.7%) and capital formation (+0.6% from +4.8%).
    • Private consumption saw improvement to 5.5% in 2Q, slightly better than the 5.3% growth seen in the previous quarter
      • Notably, the 5.5% growth was the highest since 1Q23 (+6.3%)
    • Private investments dipped to 0.6% in 2Q from 4.8% in 1Q, the sharp decline was led by the fall in construction to 0.6% from 7.0% for the same period
      • The significant contraction was likely due to the frontloading of construction in 1Q prior to the election ban leading up to the midterm elections last early May
      • Gov't construction declined -7.7% in 2Q, a reversal from 9.1% growth in 1Q and offset sustained double-digit growth in private construction (+16.7% from +14.6%)
    • Exports grew for the third straight quarter (+4.4%), while imports tempered to 2.9% from 10.3% in 1Q
      • Merchandise exports grew 13.6% from 8.1% seeing a boost from frontloading prior to the effectivity of higher US tariffs
  • On the supply side, growth was led by the services sector at 6.3% but was slower than the 7.0% expansion in 1Q24
    • The agri sector gained momentum accelerating to 7.0% from 2.2% in the quarter
    • The industry sector slowed to to 2.1% from 4.6% in 1Q as all industry subsectors saw decelerations in growth rates
    • Services sector saw faster growth at 6.9% from 6.2% in 1Q
  • Bringing 1H25 GDP growth to 5.4%, right at the lower end of the government's target of 5.5-6.5% and in line with consensus and REGIS expectations of 5.5% GDP growth for 2025
    • GDP growth for 2H25 needs to average 5.6% to hit the minimum full-year target

CONGLOS
DMCI: Weak 2Q25 results across segments

1H25 profit falls 18% to P9.0bn – Behind Regis and Street estimates

1H25 core profit (ex-FX adj) dropped 18% YoY to P9.0bn, trending below Regis and consensus forecasts. 2Q25 profit was particularly soft, falling 28% YoY to P4.0bn. after most units suffered from weak commodity prices and demand, as well as bad weather and project delays.

 

Property: 2Q25 reservation sales drop 35% QoQ, 52% YoY

Even though RFO sales grew 13% YoY to P2.0bn, reservation sales (net of backouts) still fell 35% QoQ, 52% YoY to P4.7bn. Cumulative 1H25 sales are down 44% YoY to P11.9bn, trending below management’s target of P33bn. We estimate unsold inventory levels have dropped 6% QoQ to P80bn, equivalent to over three (3) years of sales.

Order book drops to P36bn but awards of two (2) subway packages underway

The construction unit’s order book contracted 11% QoQ to P36.4bn, after booked revenues outpaced project wins. Even though it won two packages of the Metro Manila subway, these have not been added to its backlog, as it continues to negotiate technical matters with the government.

Water: 1H25 capex trails regulatory commitment on project delays

Maynilad’s capex in 2Q25 dropped 15% QoQ to P6bn, bringing 1H25 capex to P13bn (+59% YoY), trailing its P40bn target. It has adjusted its capex target for this year to P28bn (+11%) and will carry over the balance to next year’s spending.

Nickel mining profits contract QoQ; Off-grid energy sales up 4%

2Q25 profits from nickel mining were lower QoQ as shipments contracted due to the early onset of rains. Meanwhile, cooler temperatures dampened sales volumes growth for the off-grid power unit to 4% YoY in 2Q25. Lastly, while cost reduction initiatives continued at its cement unit, these were negated by limestone production constraints at its plant in Cebu.

 

 

GAMING
Government to craft policy on online gambling
  • President Marcos said the government will convene a conference in order to craft a comprehensive policy on online gambling
  • The conference will include religious groups, parents, etc.
  • The primary focus will be the effective regulation of online gambling
  • President Marcos believes that banning online gambling will push the industry underground, then the government will have no control over it
  • “The problem is not online gambling. The problem(s) are the social effects on our children and those who are addicted to gambling,”
  • The President said a comprehensive policy will address the social problems associated with online gambling, such as addiction, debt, and exposing young persons to gambling
GAMING
BSP finalizing rules related to online gambling
  • The BSP is finalizing rules that will require banks, e-wallets, and other financial service providers to adopt stronger safeguards against gambling-related harm
  • The upcoming measures will include:
  • Strict identity verification, including biometric checks, such as facial recognition, to ensure only eligible individuals can use their funds for online gambling
  • Daily limits on gambling-related transfers to reduce excessive financial losses
  • Time-based restrictions on gambling payments to help curb impulsive behavior
  • User tools for setting personal spending caps, taking voluntary breaks, or self-excluding from gambling transactions
  • DigiPlus Interactive is the market leader in online gambling, with market share of about 45%
  • PLUS is trading at 6.3X FY25E P/E (based on consensus forecast)

MACRO
PH Dollar reserves at $105.7bn end-July
  • The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Jul slightly decreased 0.3% MoM to US$105.7bn from US$106bn at end-Jun
    • The decline primarily reflected lower gold prices and drawdowns on foreign exchange deposits by the national gov't with the BSP
      • Notably, foreign exchange deposits were down 34% MoM
    • The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 3.4x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments
  • Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at US$104bn by end-2025, slightly down from the end-2024 figure of US$106bn
