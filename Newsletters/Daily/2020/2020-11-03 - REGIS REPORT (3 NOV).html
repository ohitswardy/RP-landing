03 November 2020

The strongest storm in the world this year just hit the Philippines over the weekend and property companies will likely have to brace for another one.

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) is effectively adjusting the tax on online gaming (POGO) operations to 5% of bets, from what was previously 5% of winnings (i.e. gross gaming revenue or GGR). This change is part of the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) released by the BIR covering the tax provisions of Bayanihan II. Frankly, we had been half-expecting the tax bureau to water down the Bayanihan II tax provisions and interpret it to mean 5% of GGR.

This tax change could have a massive impact on POGO operations, if properly enforced. Many online gambling activities have theoretical win rates (= winnings as a % of bets) below 5% and such a tax would render them unprofitable.

If it can be enforced, this new tax rule could mean the death knell for the POGO “industry” in the country. Filinvest Land (FLI PM) and Megaworld (MEG PM) are the most directly exposed to POGOs through their office leasing business. SM Prime (SMPH PM), while it has minimal direct exposure, could also be affected as many of its assets (present and future) are in the area where POGOs are most concentrated (and have thus led to the biggest jumps in recent years).

We have results from Metrobank (MBT PM) and Wilcon (WLCON) this morning. 

  • Metrobank (MBT PM) (-) - 9M results seemed well behind our expectations and those of consensus. However, the “miss” was because the bank chose to keep provision expense very high at P12.6bn in 3Q and P35.4bn in 9M (4-4.5x yoy). NPL ratio increased 69bps to 2.25% but NPL cover stayed at 174%, by far the most conservative of the Big Three. In a clear sign of extreme risk aversion, MBT’s loans contracted 13.4% yoy and 17.4% ytd (industry was +4.1yoy and -1.5% ytd in August).

To the upside, MBT’s deposit cost plunged to 0.46% in 3Q, even lower than BDO’s 0.49%. If the bank’s franchise hasn’t been damaged by its loan posture, then its low funding cost could serve them well once loan demand returns. MBT will hold an earnings call tomorrow, 4 Nov at 4pm Manila time.

Trade deficit at US$2.076bn in August, down 31% YoY
  • Balance of trade in goods was at a US$2.076bn deficit in August, a slower drop of 31% YoY than in previous months
    • Exports fell 19% YoY and 10% MoM, with electronic exports dropping 20% YoY
    • Imports fell 23% YoY and 4% MoM, dragged down by a contraction in oil imports (-48% YoY)
  • 8M20 trade deficit at US$14.61bn, down 46% YoY

BANKS
Banks tighten credit standards on real estate loans
  • Based on the BSP’s 3Q20 Senior Bank Loan Officers’ survey, about half of banks tightened their credit standards on both housing and commercial real estate loans
    • About half of respondents kept their lending standards for commercial real estate and housing loans unchanged
  • 47.7% of banks tightened their credit standards on loans to enterprises while 45.5% kept their standards unchanged

CEMENT
Holcim 3Q20 profit up 34% YoY, revenues down 11%
  • Holcim Philippines (HLCM PM) recorded a profit of P613mn in 3Q20, up 34% YoY and reversing the losses in 2Q20
    • Buoyed by lower costs, partly from (a) opex reductions and (b) lower fuel/power costs
  • 3Q20 revenues dropped 11% YoY, as ASPs fell 8% YoY and sales volumes declined by 3% YoY
    • While retail prices of cement have been flat to slightly up, ex-factory prices have actually dropped (retailers hiking margins)
    • It also appears that ASPs were mildly weaker QoQ
    • Recall that Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) reported 3% drops in ASPs and sales volumes in 3Q20
  • 9M20 profit is down 45% YoY to P1.03bn, following a 13% decline in sales volumes and 8% drop in ASPs
