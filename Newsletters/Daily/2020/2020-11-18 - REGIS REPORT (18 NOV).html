REGIS REPORT

18 November 2020

We begin our update today with a few more results.

The impact of the pandemic on construction activity and the income recognition that comes with it is apparent. Beyond Covid-19, however, one other thing that’s also become apparent is a dip in POGO tenants. Note this exodus was already happening before the most recent change in how they are supposed to be taxed. We might need to brace for this trickle becoming a deluge.

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (-) – The auto and property units had a reasonable QoQ bounce but earnings were weighed down by massive provisions at the bank.  Vehicle sales rebounded in 3Q but GPM contracted QoQ as some costs incurred in 2Q were recognized in 3Q.  10M20 vehicle sales are on track to beat our estimates but management noted that despite favorable FX, net margins may be flat QoQ as promotional activities intensify. The other noteworthy highlight from the results call was the firm’s disclosure that it sold 15 hectares of land (part of the 700 hectares acquired from Profriends) at what could be a significantly NAV-enhancing price.

For domestic investors >> GT Capital – Auto, property units recover but elevated provisions at the bank weigh on 3Q

  • Vista Land (VLL PM) (+) – 9M20 earnings fell 40% to P1.9bn, already very close to hitting our full-year estimate, thanks to strong residential and rental revenue. 3Q20 rental revenue only fell 12%, compared to the 60%-80% drops of the major mall operators. While the other developers gave rental concessions to all mall tenants, Vista Land only granted these to a few tenants. Meanwhile, 3Q20 reservation sales fell 33%, better than the performance of most other developers, which have a higher proportion of projects in Metro Manila.

For foreign investors >> Vista Land – 3Q20 rental revenue “only” falls 12% on limited mall tenant concessions

For domestic investors >> Vista Land – 3Q20 rental revenue “only” falls 12% on limited mall tenant concessions

  • Megawide (MWIDE PM) (+) – 9M20 net loss of P822mn is tracking smaller than our FY20E on higher construction revenue bookings, as well as lower non-cash and financing expenses. The company extended its losses in 3Q, following (1) a pre-emptive reversal of revenues at its landport unit, which has seen POGO tenants exiting and (2) continued softness in airport pax volumes.

For foreign investors >> Megawide – 3Q20: Key operating units remain under pressure

For domestic investors >> Megawide – 3Q20: Key operating units remain under pressure

  • Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (+) – 9M20 profit fell 40% to P2.6bn, on track to beat our full-year forecast. What caught our attention though was the 10% QoQ drop in office revenue brought about by POGO closures. The remaining POGOs still occupy about 15% of FLI’s office portfolio and pay nearly double the rental rate of non-POGOs. Meanwhile, residential reservation sales rebounded 83% QoQ (+4% YoY) on looser payment schemes and price discounts on selected units. Sales in Metro Manila fell in 9M20 but were cushioned by growth in adjacent provinces.

+++

Broadband services provider Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) suffered a major outage yesterday, with broadband service unavailable “nationwide” beginning around 1pm.  Service was restored in some areas by early evening with more areas back online a few hours later.  It’s unclear how much of its service has been restored as of this morning (Nov 18).

Senators were surprised to learn that the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) has P83bn (US$1.7bn) that remained unspent from the funds allocated by the national budget as well as the Bayanihan 1 and 2 laws.  The DSWD has apparently been unable to deploy the funds to assist those most affected by the pandemic and, more recently, the typhoons.
