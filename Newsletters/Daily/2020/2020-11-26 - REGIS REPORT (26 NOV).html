Airlines are the epitome of leverage: operating and financial leverage. Small movements in some of the key operating variables (capacity factor, fuel price, etc.) typically have a much magnified impact on the bottom line. When was the last time that net profit estimates for airlines by sell-side analysts came within 5% of actual results?

So it should come a no surprise that airlines are teetering on the brink of a precipice due to the coronavirus. The news that Philippine Airlines (PAL) is seeking court protection from creditors should thus not come as a shock. This move, announced yesterday, will hopefully allow for (1) better clarity on creditor exposure, (2) an orderly restructuring of PAL’s liabilities and (3) also a restructuring of its operations.

Some eyes will focus on PNB (PNB PM) and its 60% owner LT Group (LTG PM), both of whom are controlled by the same principals. PNB is likely to have at least some exposure, direct or indirect. The government’s budget deficit amounted to P61bn in Oct. While this is up 25% from last year, it remains well below trend to reach the full year program. YT-Oct, the deficit has reached P941bn, which is barely half of the programmed P1.82trn for FY20. Revenues are down but are trending about estimate. It is expenditures that are lagging. To reach target expenditures the national government will have to spend over P500bn a month for the last two months, which it has not ever been done before. 

ICTSI (ICT PM) disclosed that completed a block placement of 40m treasury shares last night at a price of P117/share (4% discount to market close), raising P4.68bn in gross proceeds. We don’t think ICT had immediate need for liquidity but the sale gives an additional equity buffer (almost US$100m) ahead of FY results that could include provisions for its Argentine investment as well as its unrecovered deposit in Sudan.

Carissa (carissa.mangubat@regis.ph) and Aaron (aaron.salvador@regis.ph) discuss the visible trends that emerged from 3Q results of the consumer firms in their note released yesterday.

Aggregate earnings for our consumer universe jumped over 2x QoQ in 3Q20 as restrictions were lifted post-ECQ lockdown in 2Q20. Underlying consumer spending, however, was weak and revenues were still down significantly YoY across most segments. Even essentials, such as grocery retail and consumer staples, saw a slowdown without the boost from pantry stocking and gov’t handouts. Downtrading was also seen across key segments, such as packaged food and alcoholic beverages.

While top line should continue to improve sequentially in 4Q, we expect it to still be down significantly YoY given more subdued holiday spending. Lower income consumers have borne much of the lockdown’s impact, and will likely continue to see continued pressure on incomes (and consumption) beyond 2020.

For the alcoholic beverage segment, there were very divergent trends resulting from the same underlying reason: damaged incomes due to the pandemic.  Volumes of cheap liquor jump as much as 44%YoY in 3Q, while beer volumes were still 20% off pre-Covid levels. There was down-trading within the broader alcohol segment (from beer to spirits) and also within distilled spirits (from expensive to cheap). Meanwhile, industry tobacco volumes in 3Q already matched pre-Covid levels as sales outlets reopened. The >20% price increase implemented by PMFCT at the start of 4Q will dampen volume trajectory again, but will likely be net-positive for profitability.

MACRO
Budget deficit widens in October
  • The government’s budget deficit swelled to P61.4bn in October 2020 from a P49.3bn deficit in the same period last year
  • Revenues contracted by 13% YoY, as BIR and BOC collections dropped 15% YoY and 12% YoY, respectively
    • MoM, BIR are up +8.2% but BOC collections were flattish (-0.3%)
  • Meanwhile, expenditures dropped by 7% YoY largely attributed to the base effect of the one-off pension differential releases for the military and uniformed personnel (MUP) and lower capital outlays
  • YT-October deficit at P940.6bn, +170% YoY (vs government’s FY20E deficit target of P1.8tn)
    • Revenues are down 8.4% YoY to P2.37tn, on track to beat the government’s (downgraded) revenue goal of P2.5tn
    • Expenditures are up 12.7% YoY to P3.3tn, accounting for 76% of the govt’s full-year target
