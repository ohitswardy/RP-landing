REGIS REPORT

17 November 2020

We’ve got a few more results, and a downgrade note on Jollibee (JFC PM), and car sales data. But before that, a quick word on the surprise OFW numbers.

Remittances jumped 9% YoY in September to $2.6bn, a strong surprise rebound from the -4% YoY in August. The recovery was most surprising for its breadth, with every major geography (including se-based workers), posting positive yoy numbers. In particular, the Middle East saw remittances surge 16.6% YoY. The 9M20 remittance figure is now just -1.4% YoY, much better than the BSP’s expectation of -5% for the full-year. The fly in the ointment is the strong PHP. In local currency terms, the 9M20 remittance is down 5.2% YoY. Continued outperformance by remittances could help consumption recover faster than we expect.

+ + +

We downgrade Jollibee Foods (JFC PM) to a Hold after recent share price outperformance (+40% vs PCOMP since March), which has been largely driven by optimism on the economy’s potential reopening amidst vaccine developments. While we have largely kept our operating margin assumptions, our 2020-21E revenue forecasts are reduced by 3-6%. We believe that while JFC could realize longer term cost savings from its business transformation initiatives, top line recovery will be an uphill climb, particularly in the Philippines, where mobility continues to be limited by the availability of public transportation. With current PE multiples of 52x 21E / 30x 22E on our revised estimates, we believe much of the near term positives have already been priced in. While we raise our target price by 9% to P185/sh to account for lower cost of debt, it seems the market may have gotten ahead of itself.

On to earnings. More misses than beats in this morning’s collection…

Alliance Global (AGI PM) – behind – Albeit slower than expected, business from the casino and restaurant segments have started to pick up. Casino GGR hit P3.3bn (-56%YoY) in 3Q and capacity restrictions are gradually being relaxed.  Meanwhile, McDonald’s SSSG was -46% for the quarter, with dine-in at just 16% sales contribution. Liquor was again the big outperformer, with the international whisky business surging 72% YoY and 42% QoQ.

GT Capital (GTCAP PM) – behind – 9M20 core profit before pref divs dropped 70% YoY to P3.74bn, with 3Q20 profit alone at P570mn (+70% QoQ).  The auto segment’s profit recovered QoQ to P1.2bn and 9M20 profit of P2.2bn is on track to reach our FY20E.  However, higher provisions at the bank weighed on the results. 

Megaworld (MEG PM) – behind – Residential reservation sales fell 26% QoQ (down 63% YoY) due to weak sales in Metro Manila. Meanwhile, a POGO tenant vacated a 29,000sqm office building. Fortunately, Megaworld has re-leased 60% of the vacated building with new tenants. In addition, POGOs generally pay 6 months of advanced rent and 3 months of security deposit so the company’s near-term earnings should not be affected by the closure.

BANKS
SECB Briefing notes

Asset quality

  • As we had surmised, CFO Edu Olbes said that the “lion’s share”, or roughly 80% of the new NPLs in 3Q20 came from the retail book. He qualified, though, that this was largely from autos, mortgages, and SME loans that were booked under the retail bank (clients for whom personal and business cash is fungible). On the wholesale banking side, the affected sectors included agriculture (rice tariffication), construction, and transportation (eg bus companies, shipping).
  • NPL outlook – no guidance on peak NPLs. However, they expect the pace of new NPL formation to decelerate from the 3Q, which was artificially bloated by delayed recognition as a result of Bayanihan 1. The number of borrowers opting in for Bayanihan 2 is far, far less than it was for Bayanihan 1 (c60% of retail loans).
  • On provisions – managements intends to continue to be proactive, in line with their ECL models. If NPLs continue to rise, then they will continue to provision.
  • On restructuring – For consumer loans, loan officers have been given authority to offer “pre-packaged” restructuring plans to customers. For commercial clients, these would be negotiated on a case to case basis.

Loan growth

  • SECB has significantly tightened up on credit standards, and expect continued deceleration (or even contraction) in the consumer loan book.
  • Recovery of loan demand will likely depend on a) government easing of movement restrictions, b) fiscal stimulus. Management expects a “K-shaped” recovery, which will be uneven, and inconsistent, ie very selective on sectors.

NIMs / trading gains / interest rates

  • NIMs will be a challenge, given the shrinking loan book (esp high yield consumers). Also, much of the NIM gains this year are from a collapse in deposit costs. Falling loan and asset yields will put downward pressure on NIMs.
  • Treasurer Raul Pedro noted that he expected the yield curve to stay flat for the next 2-3 years. As such, the trading gains they booked in 3Q20 was actual sell-down of securities (ie not just mark-to-market). With a flat curve, they believe the opportunities for trading gains from here could be limited. As a result, they aggressively shrank the bond portfolio -25% qoq and -55% yoy (see chart below). In particular, the HTM/HTC book was down to P25bn, from P213bn in 3Q19 (and from a peak of P286bn in 3Q17). This could be a relief to some investors who have been frustrated with the “noise” generated by the (previously) massive treasury book.
