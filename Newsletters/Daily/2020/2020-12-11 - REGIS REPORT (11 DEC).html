REGIS REPORT

11 December 2020

The government’s infrastructure spending remained in the doldrums, amounting to P57bn in Oct, which is practically unchanged MoM but still sharply lower (-30%) compared to a year ago. Infrastructure spending YT-Oct is down 18%.

Beyond this year, the government’s programmed infrastructure spending for the near-term is also not looking very growth-accretive. Cuts of up to (or more than) 20% vs. pre-pandemic are being programmed, which appears reflective of the inability to execute (“lack of absorptive capacity”) and the likely reduction/s in government revenue. Recent decisions taken by the administration’s economic team suggest predisposition towards “small” government (i.e. lower taxes, lower spending).

+++

Canned food firm Century Pacific (CNPF PM) is sounding quite optimistic about FY21, predicting revenue growth of 10-15% next despite already coming off a high base this year.

Media is reporting that new telco Dito (DITO PM) has been recruiting former military personnel to help run its operations. It can be recalled Dito appointed an ex-general to become its Chief Technology Officer. The firm does not appear to have been poaching talent (much) from the two incumbent telcos.

MACRO
Infra spend falls 30% YoY in October
  • Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell by 30% YoY to P57.1bn in October (flat MoM)
    • Cited lower spending from the Department of Public Works and Department of Transportation
  • This brings YT-October infra spend to P508.5bn, down 18% YoY
    • Due to delays in construction of some public works, as well as the discontinuance of some projects due to the pandemic
    • Gov’t expect infrastructure disbursements to further rise in the remaining months of the year and reach P660bn, higher than its downgraded FY20 target of P610bn

MACRO
September FDI drops
  • In September, Foreign Direct Investments (FDI) recorded a net inflow of US$523mn, down 12% YoY
    • Nonresidents’ net investments in debt instruments fell 14% to US$362mn
    • Reinvestments of earnings -20% to US$62mn
    • Net equity capital infusions +2% to US$99mn
    • Equity capital infusions largely came from Japan, US, and Singapore
  • Prior to September, FDI had been rising on a YoY basis for four consecutive months
  • In 9M20, FDI fell 9% to US$4.832bn
  • The BSP expects US$5.6bn in inflows this year
MACRO
Foreign trade decline continues in October
  • Preliminary data by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) showed merchandise exports in October contracted by 2.2% YoY to $6.202bn
    • This compares to a revised 2.9% growth in September and flat 0.5% growth in October 2019
    • Exports of electronic products was flat in October at $3.584bn
    • Exports of agro-based products declined by 21.7% to $358.417mn
    • Exports of minerals rose 48% to $448.951mn
  • Merchandise imports declined for the 18th straight month in October by 19.5% to $7.979bn
    • This was worse compared with the 15.3% and 7.6% contraction logged in September 2020 and October 2019, respectively
    • Purchases of raw materials and intermediate goods fell 9.6% to $3.223bn in October
    • Capital goods fell 19% to $2.736bn
    • Imports of consumer goods fell 21.8% to $1.355bn
    • Purchase of mineral fuels, lubricants and related materials fell 50% to $580.329mn
  • Trade deficit for the month stood at $1.77bn, lower than the $1.783bn in September 2020 and $3.573bn in October 2019
  • Total external trade in goods was $14.181bn in October, down 12.8% from $16.256bn last year
    • Total trade in 10M20 was $122.151bn, 20.2% lower than the same period last year
  • Year to date, the trade balance amounted to a $17.924bn deficit, narrower than the $34.052bn trade gap in the same period last year
CONSUMER
CNPF sees double digit growth in 2021
  • CNPF is looking to grow revenues by 10-15% in 2021 (Regis: +1%), despite coming from a high base in 2020
  • The company also expects margin uplift from reduced one-off Covid-related expenses
  • Top line growth is expected to be driven by:
    • Recovery of tuna OEM
    • Continued expansion of coconut OEM
    • New product innovations
  • For 2020, the company expects earnings growth of at least 20% (Regis: +28%)
TELCOS
Ex-military to lead Dito’s cybersecurity
  • At-least nine former military officers have jointed Dito Telecommunity Corp, amid concerns over its partnership with a Chinese state-owned telecom firm
  • Retired Col. Roleen del Prado leads the cybersecurity operations team
  • Dito Chief Technology Officer Rodolfo D. Santiago is also a retired military general
  • In September, Dito announced it would be investing P1bn in cybersecurity solutions this year to be supplied by 12 tech firms from the US
