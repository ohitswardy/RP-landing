REGIS REPORT

04 December 2020

The country’s unemployment rate dropped to 8.7% in Oct from 10% in July but the headline numbers mask what is actually a deterioration in the employment situation.

The decline in the unemployment rate for Oct is really a result of a drop in labor participation and not an increase in employed people. In layman’s terms, the drop in unemployment rate was because more people stopped looking for work and not because they found work. In fact, the number of people who had jobs in Oct was actually lower than the number who had jobs in July. Lower by 1.5m, in fact, if the government data is to be believed.

The gradual easing of Covid-19 restrictions, hopefully, will improve the jobs situation in subsequent months.

+++

The Oct money supply and loan growth data released by the Bangko Sentral (BSP) showed continued to show a wide disparity in the M3 and loan growth numbers. Loan growth continued to decline, dropping to 1.9% from 2.6% in Sept. M3 growth, on the other hand, was 11.8%, reflecting efforts by the BSP to expand its balance sheet (i.e. print money). Those efforts, however, have still to show an impact on credit availability as the loan data show.

+++

The government (DBCC) reduced its GDP expectations for this year to 8.5%-9.5%, following the release of 3Q GDP a few weeks back. We at Regis remain a tad less optimistic and believe the economy (GDP) will contract by 10% this year. Based on the DBCC’s projections, the budget deficit this year will be at 7.6% of GDP and will rise further to 8.9% next year.

The Finance Dept is against another stimulus package and is instead looking to extend the validity of the current (2020) budget as well as the Bayanihan 2 measures in order to boost spending in 2021. Recall that 2019 budget allocations were also carried over to this fiscal year but it is looking unlikely that the government will be able spend, in absolute amount, even just the 2020 allocations (not to mention the carryovers from last year).

MACRO
Unemployment rate drops to 8.7% in October
  • Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority showed that the unemployment rate dropped further to 8.7% in October 2020 from 10% in July and 17.6% in April but remained higher than the 4.6% unemployment rate in the same period last year. 
  • It is worth noting that while the absolute number of unemployed dropped by 758k QoQ, the number of employed dropped more by 1.47mn.  Thus, the QoQ drop in the unemployment rate appears to have been partly due to a lower labor force participation rate, which stood at only 58.7% in October (vs. 61.9% in July and 55.7% in April). 

  • Employment in the services sector rose slightly QoQ, buoyed by a 48% jump in communications (1% of total employment). However, this was more than offset by decreases in agri (-14%, 21% of total), manufacturing (-10%, 8% of total), and trade (-6%, 21% of total). Meanwhile, employment in the construction sector (10% of total) shrunk 4.9% YoY and 1.5% QoQ.  

MACRO
DBCC sees deeper economic slump
  • The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) once again cut its FY20 GDP estimate to -8.5% to -9.5%
  • This is a deeper contraction than the July 28 estimate of -4.4 to -6.6%
  • Cited the prolonged imposition of community quarantines in various regions in the country, as well as recent typhoons
  • The government kept its growth forecast for FY21 at 6.5% - 7.5% and raised FY22E to 8-10%

MACRO
Gov't looking to extend validity of Bayanihan 2, 2020 budget
  • In a Thursday briefing, Finance Secretary Carlos Domninguez III said that the unspent portions of the said fiscal measures, together with the proposed 2021 budget are vital to the economic rebound next year
  • Dominguez added that have another stimulus package may not be an option
  • In November, several lawmakers filed a version of Bayanihan 3 for additional assistance amid the Covid pandemic, with Bayanihan 2 set expire in less than a month
BANKS
Loan growth slows further to 1.9% in October
  • Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, eased to 1.9% in October from 2.6% in September (revised)
    • On a month-on-month seasonally-adjusted basis, outstanding universal and commercial bank loans, net of RRPs, declined by 0.4%
  • Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-3.6%), wholesale and retail trade and repair of motor vehicles and motorcycles  (-4.3%) continued to contract
    • Consumer loans (credit cards and auto) expanded at a slower pace of 8.0% in October from 9.8% in September
  • Meanwhile, money supply grew by 11.8% in October, mildly slower than the 12.2% growth in September (revised)

Manila Water consortium secures management contract in Saudi Arabia
  • The consortium of Saur SAS, Miahona Company, and Manila Water (MWC PM) has signed a Management, Operation and Maintenance Contract (“MOMC”) with the National Water Company (“NWC”), Kingdom of Saudi Arabia for the latter’s North West Cluster
  • The MOMC will comprise the management, operations and maintenance of the water and wastewater facilities of the North West Cluster (Madinah and Tabuk) over a 7-year contract period
    • This will entail the implementation of Enabling Projects and deployment of Key Personnel to manage the Cluster and achieve the Key Performance Indicators target set by the NWC
