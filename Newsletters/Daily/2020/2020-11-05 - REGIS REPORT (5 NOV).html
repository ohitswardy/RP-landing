REGIS REPORT

05 November 2020

We are at the peak of results season right now, with a slew of reports released over the past day.

The biggest name, SM Investments (SM PM), saw earnings recover in 3Q with BDO (BDO PM) and its retail operations bouncing back. ICTSI (ICT PM) is looking like its operations will beat our FY20 estimates but this could be marred by a one-off hit from its deposit with the Sudanese government. Metro Pacific (MPI PM) and Aboitiz Power (AP PM) are both on the weaker side as power demand has still not fully bounced back.

The just-released numbers for San Miguel (FB PM), meanwhile, appear on track to meet our FY20 numbers due to the outperformance of its gin business.

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (=) – 9M profits of P15.1bn (-54% YoY) are within expectations, with 3Q profits amounting to P8.1bn (-19%). The record numbers of BDO (BDO PM) certainly helped as did the partial recovery of the retail operations. During the earnings call, management also provided some insight on how the mall and retail businesses, especially the grocery operations, are shaping up so far.

  • ICTSI (ICT PM) (+) – ICTSI arrested the decline seen in the past two quarters, with 3Q profit up 14% YoY and 32% QoQ to US$53mn. Volumes rose 3% YoY in 3Q and cost savings drove EBITDA higher by 13% YoY. 9M20 profit reached US$138mn (-3% YoY), on track to beat Regis and consensus forecasts. Although risks loom on a possible provision for the remaining Euro189mn from the Sudanese government by year-end, we believe underlying operations are strong and should support a strong recovery in 2021.

MACRO
Exports up, trade deficit lower in September
  • Trade deficit in September stood at US$1.71bn, lower than the US$3.4bn trade gap in the same period last year
    • Exports rose 2% YoY, 13% MoM, snapping a six-month losing streak
    • Imports dropped 16% YoY but was up 8% MoM (slowest contraction since February)
  • 9M20 trade deficit at US$16.07bn, down 47% YoY

MACRO
Infrastructure spend falls 43% YoY in September
  • Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) plunged 40% YoY to P56.9bn in September
    • MoM, infra spend was higher by 28.6%
    • Cited discontinuance of some capital outlay projects which are unlikely to be completed nor implemented this year due to the pandemic
  • YT-September infra spend at P451.4bn, down 17% YoY
  • Last July, the government lowered its infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY
  • For FY21, the infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) is supposed to increase by 43% YoY to P1.12tn
    • This target is c. 7% higher than what was spent in 2019 for infrastructure (P1.05tn)

BANKS
MBT earnings call takeaways
  • Loan contraction (-13%yoy and -17%ytd) was due to pay-downs from large corporates that issued bonds. On the consumer side, loans were amortizing faster than new loans being written (especially in autos). (Rafa note: corp shift to bonds is still disintermediation, and still negative for loan growth and NIMs.)
  • NIMs likely to compress as loan / asset yields go down, but low funding / deposit costs should help.
  • NPL – expect NPL of <3% by end-2020 and 5% by end-2021. NPL stress could persist into 2022, due to uncertainties over vaccine, production, and distribution (ie when will it get to Phils?).
  • Restructuring – P3.5bn at 9M20, about 0.3% of loans (was P2.4bn at end-2019, and 9M19).
  • Provisions – full review of portfolio. Book aggressive provisions while NPAT is strong. Did not want to wait to act when NPLs already apparent. That way, when growth opportunities present themselves, they are ready, and “unburdened” by need to provision. Learned from 1997 experience. (Rafa note – It took MBT until 2011 to write off all the NPAs from 1997/98 and 2000/01 mergers.)
  • Grace period – Much fewer clients opted in for Bayanihan 2 vs Bayanihan 1. For commercial loans, only c10% opted in under B2. For consumer, only about 20% for B2 (compared to 50% for B1).
PROPERTY
SM Prime mall tenant sales reach 70-75% of pre-pandemic levels
  • SM Supermalls President Steven Tan said that although SM Prime’s mall foot traffic is only at 30-40% of pre-COVID levels (as of October), tenants sales reached 70-75% of pre-pandemic levels
    • From c. 60% of pre-pandemic sales as of September
    • SM Prime credits the strong sales to its “E-Commerce” initiatives as well as some shoppers spending two to three times more per mall visit
  • Due to the strong sales, the company believes that it can reduce mall rental concessions beginning January
  • Occupancy is currently at 75% and the company expects this to reach 90% by December
  • Very few tenants (“very low single digit”) did not renew their contracts
  • SMPH is currently trading at 28.3X FY21E P/E and 10% discount to NAV

GAMING
DoF says tax treatment is the same for all online gambling
  • Department of Finance Secretary Dominguez said that the tax treatment for online gambling, whether done onshore or offshore is the same
  • The clarification came after PAGCOR gave the greenlight to accept online bets
  • The BIR recently came out with an IRR that taxed POGOs 5% of gross bets or drop
