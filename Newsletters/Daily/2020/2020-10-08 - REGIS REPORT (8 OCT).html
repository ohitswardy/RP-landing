REGIS REPORT

08 October 2020

Dito Telecommunity has signed an agreement with an affiliate for the latter to undertake its network rollout, at least in terms of building towers and laying out fiber. The “third operator” claims to have over 800 towers now and aims to launch operations by March 2021. We reckon one big issue that could have significant implications on Dito emerging as a credible new operator is the proposed law lifting foreign ownership limits on telecom companies. Lifting that would allow China Telecom to raise its stake in Dito above 40%, allowing China Telecom to fund it more aggressively.

+++

First Gen’s (FGEN PM) LNG plans took another step forward with a finalization of its partnership with Tokyo Gas and the awarding of the EPC contract for its offshore terminal. The project could be in operation as soon as 3Q of 2022, which should give them some lead time before Malamapaya gas runs out.

+++

Cebu Air (CEB PM) disclosed it plans to raise up to USD500mn via a combination of (1) a USD250mn convertible preferred shares stock rights offering and (2) a USD250mn private placement of convertible bonds. With CEB’s current market capitalization at USD460mn, we estimate the share dilution from these exercises to be over 50%.However, the preferred share rights offer should minimize the dilution to existing shareholders. 

The pandemic is continuing to hurt the Philippine airline industry, with travel restrictions still largely in place, even for domestic routes. CEB’s operations remain low at only 15% of pre-COVID levels on domestic routes and as a result, its cash burn of an estimated P1.7bn per month has extended beyond the lockdown.

MACRO
Term deposit facility rates rise across the board
  • The average rates of all Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas’ (BSP) term deposit facility (TDF) tenors increased yesterday
    • The BSP auctioned off P200bn in 7-day TDF at 1.843%; P220bn in 14-day TDF at 1.849%; P60bn in 28-day TDF at 1.845%
    • Last week, yields for 7-, 14-, and 28-day TDFs were at 1.837%, 1.848%, 1.843%
  • All tenors were over- subscribed but bid coverage ratio of the shortest tenor facility declined to 1.1697 from last week’s 1.3974

TELCOS
Dito hires affiliate for tower rollout
  • Dito Telecommunity signed a P10bn deal with Udenna Infrastructure Corp to construct cell towers and roll out fiber optic cables
  • It would involve construction of 1,500 towers and the rollout of 1,400km of fiber
  • Dito is claiming 859 towers as of Sept, compared to its target of 1,300 initially aimed for July 2020 (now Jan 2021)
  • The company is still hoping to launch operations by March 2021
POWER
FGEN advances Tokyo Gas partnership, announces EPC contractor
  • First Gen disclosed that it signed a joint cooperation agreement with Tokyo Gas Co., Ltd representing the next phase of their joint development of FGEN LNG Corporation’s interim offshore LNG terminal project
    • FGEN and Tokyo Gas initially signed a joint development agreement in December 2018
    • Under the latest agreement, Tokyo Gas will have a 20% participating interest in the project and will provide support in development, construction, o&m to achieve a final investment decision
  • FGEN also announced the selection of McConnel Dowell Philippines as Preferred Tenderer for EPC of the interim offshore LNG terminal
    • McConnell Dowell is an Australian-based engineering and construction firm with over 60 years experience in marine design and construction projects
    • Recent LNG projects include Donggi Senoro LNG project in Sulawesi, Indonesia and marine works for Tangguh LNG project in West Papua, Indonesia
  • The project consists of modifying the existing jetty and building an adjunct onshore gas receiving facility
    • It will allow FGEN LNG to accelerate its ability to introduce LNG to the Philippines as early as 3Q22 to serve the natural gas requirements of existing and future gas-fired plants of third parties and FGEN LNG affiliates

BANKS
Chelsea Logistics – banks’ exposures
  • Chelsea Logistics (C PM) of Davao-based Dennis Uy announced in a press release and a disclosure that they are expecting a recovery in the business from 2H2021. In the meantime, they are:
    • deferring most of their capex (note – Chelsea owns 25% of DITO Telecom)
    • disposing of idle assets
    • laying off workers
    • extending loan payments, as per Bayanihan 1 and 2
    • increasing authorized capital from P2bn to P3.5bn. This will cover subscription by parent co Udenna of 375mn common shares at P3.26/sh (P1.22bn), and subscription by Global Kingdom Investments of 500,000 convertible preferred shares at P1,000/sh (P500mn).
  • It is unclear at this stage whether Chelsea has begun restructuring or rescheduling negotiations with their banks. As far as we can gather, Chelsea is still current on their loans, but has deferred interest payments and pushed back maturities (total 4.5 months) allowed by Bayanihan 1 and 2.
  • As of 2019, Chelsea had total interest-bearing debt of P16.3bn (cash was just P375mn), on an equity base of P12.4bn, or a net gearing level of 130%. Annual net finance cost was P1.2bn, while gross profit was P1.55bn and opex was P1bn. As of 1H20, net debt was roughly the same (P16bn), but equity shrank to P11.2bn, taking net gearing up to 143%. The company booked a net loss of P1.29bn in 1H20.
  • Of the total indebtedness, roughly P13bn was written by local banks. That’s equivalent to about 0.12% of total banking sector loans (P10.5trn as of 1H20).
  • We’ve summarized the exposure of different banks to Chelsea in the table below. The biggest lenders to Chelsea are Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP, fully government-owned), Chinabank (CBC PM), and Philippine Business Bank (PBB PM). Relative to its balance sheet, though, Chinabank’s exposure is not that large at 0.6% of loans and 3.5% of equity as of 1H20.  In contrast, PBB’s exposure is roughly 3.3% of total loans and 20% of equity as of 1H20.
  • Of the banks we cover, only BDO (BDO PM) has anything close to significant exposure at P691mn; but given the sheer size of its book (P2.3trn), the Chelsea loans are about 0.03% of total loans. BPI (BPI PM) had a paltry P1mn loan.
  • All the Term Loans are collateralized by ships/vessels. About a quarter of the Bank Loans are collateralized by ships, while the balance is unsecured. Term Loan maturities ranged from 5-15 years. Majority of the Bank Loan maturities were 60-180 days.

OIL & GAS
Petron considers shutdown of Bataan refinery
  • Petron says it may shut down its refinery supposedly because of an “uneven” playing field
  • CEO Ramon Ang says he will close down the 180,000b/d refinery if talks with government will not succeed
    • Ang is claiming that it is more viable to just import finished product, blaming it on the tax regime
    • Petron supposedly pays taxes upon arrival of its crude imports, while importers pay only when the finished goods leave their depots
  • The facility has not been operating since 1Q20, even before the pandemic, supposedly because of maintenance activity
  • Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) announced it was shutting down its refinery only two months ago
  • Petron spent US$2bn to upgrade its refinery and the project was completed in 2014
MINING
Sale of state-owned mining assets eyed
  • The Department of Finance has asked for the revival of the privatization of state-owned mining assets
    • These assets could include Nonoc Nickel Mines in Surigao City and copper mines in Basay, Negros Oriental, among others
    • According to Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III
  • The government is firm on reviving its mining assets to boost job opportunities and lift economies around the mine sites
