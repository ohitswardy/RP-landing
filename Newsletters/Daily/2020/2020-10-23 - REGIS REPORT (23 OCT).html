REGIS REPORT

23 October 2020

Universal Robina’s (URC PM) 9M20 performance came just a tad above our expectations on better-than-expected margins. 9M20 net profit rose 7% YoY to P7.5bn, white EBIT rose 8% YoY to P11.9bn.

3Q20 revenues were flattish across all its business segments - demand in the PH weakened in the absence of government dole outs and pantry stocking, while recovery trends on its int’l business were mixed. For its PH branded consumer food (BCF) segment specifically, “essential” categories (e.g. coffee, instant noodles, bakery) saw a sharp deceleration from 2Q20 levels, while “impulse” categories (e.g. RTD tea, snacks, candies) have yet to recover.  

Despite the challenging environment, URC managed to gain market share across all its key categories in the PH, which suggests consolidation within the sector and within the trade. Favorable input cost and sales mix helped boost margins, but management noted that some of the margin gains may be reinvested towards brand building and supply chain improvements to ensure availability to the trade.

+ + +

The URC results, as well as those of Belle (BEL PM) and Cebu Landmasters (CLI, not rated) echo sentiments of their respective peers (see the sector comments of our summary of last week’s conference).

  • BEL reported that its share of earnings of the City of Dreams Manila casino turned positive in 3Q from a loss in 2Q. Gaming regulator Pagcor has allowed integrated resort operators to open under a “trial period”, at a maximum of 30% capacity.
  • CLI reported reservation pre-sales of P3.1bn in 3Q. While this was still 22% down yoy, it is already approaching the monthly average pace of 2019. Its provincial properties are selling much better than those in Metro Manila, consistent with reports from other developers we cover.

+ + +

The economic re-opening continues, perhaps slower than we would like, but we seem to heading in the right direction. The regulator LTFRB has allowed 1,700 more jeeps to ply their routes in Metro Manila, taking them to over 50% of pre-Covid levels. Public transportation has been a crucial bottleneck in the ability of Filipinos to get around, go to work, and spend. Slowly, slowly, slowly may win the race, but maybe a little less slowly would be better.

TELCOS
PLDT drops Sun Prepaid
  • PLDT is now re-classifying Sun prepaid subscribers as Smart prepaid and dropping the Sun prepaid brand.  Henceforth, the prepaid subscribers under Sun will be consolidated under Smart prepaid.  From a consumer perspective, they can now subscribe to packages and promos applicable to the latter.
  • This exercise should streamline PLDT’s mobile brands, reducing them to two on prepaid (Smart and TNT, formerly Talk N Text) and two on postpaid (Smart and Sun).  This is likely to result in more rationalized marketing efforts (i.e. no need to produce ads or marketing material for Sun Prepaid, maintain separate apps or websites).  We don’t know yet if PLDT will plan to drop Sun on the postpaid side, though we think there could be a more distinct identity for the Sun postpaid business (vs Smart postpaid).
  • Sun Prepaid accounted for 5.7m of PLDT’s 70.2m subscribers  (67.7m of whom are prepaid).  PLDT acquired Digitel, which used to operate Sun, in 2011.
GAMING
BEL’s losses narrow to P46mn in 3Q
  • Belle Corp incurred a net loss of P46mn in 3Q, smaller than the P238mn loss in 2Q, as restrictions on gambling activities eased
  • This brings the 9M20 net income to P236mn, -90%yoy

  • Equipment rental and scratch ticket sales recovered from 15mn in 2Q20 to P44mn (-79%yoy)
