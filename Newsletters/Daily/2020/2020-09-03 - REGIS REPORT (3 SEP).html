REGIS REPORT

03 September 2020

Revenue collections from the Bureau of Customs are unsurprisingly down (-17% YoY).  What’s comforting, however, is that the revenues did not fall as much as feared.  Total collections came in at P45bn for the month, which was way better than the P34bn target.

Hopefully, that means deficit won’t be as bad as feared and the government would be more amenable to spending more to save business and the economy.

Broadband provider Converge ICT, owned and controlled by someone coincidentally named Dennis Uy (like the one from Davao), is set to do an IPO in a few weeks’ time.

Is this deal worth a look?  We offer a few highlights and our thoughts on the deal in the section below.

+++

Highlights

Fast-growing new operator in a growing market.  Broadband subscriber base has been rising over 20% CAGR in recent years and is likely to top 50% this year, propelled by the pandemic-induced lockdowns.  

Converge ICT has grown faster than market, lifting its residential subscriber base more than five-fold since 2017 (albeit from a low base) and now has 732k subscribers.  This is equivalent to 11% share of all broadband subscribers and 19% of wired broadband subs.

For 1H alone, it added over 200k subs, accounting for 13% of new broadband subscribers and 49%% of new wired subscribers.

Higher speed offering.  Converge ICT's (CICT) offers only fiber to the home (FTTH) or hybrid fiber co-axial (HFC) broadband, which is faster than DSL over copper.  Both Globe and PLDT have fiber and are gradually phasing out DSL services in favor of fiber. 

CICT’s product is cheaper but PLDT and Globe’s wired broadband offerings are typically bundled with fixed line voice.  Check out a comparison of their lower priced wired plans.

Fiber network across Luzon.  As of now, Converge ICT's (CICT) network only covers Luzon with a total of 4.1m homes passed.  Extensions to VisMin by 2021 are in the works.  For comparison, PLDT’s comparable number of homes passed a few months ago was 7m.

Residential broadband is bulk of business.  Residential broadband accounts for bulk of Converge ICT's revenue (70% as of 2019) with the rest classified as from the Enterprise segment (which includes corporate data, and other services). Residential broadband is the fast growing of the two segments, with a CAGR of 95% since 2016 (vs. 39% for Enterprise).

Bulk of IPO shares will be secondary.  Around 72% of the shares to be sold (including the overallotment) in the IPO will be secondary shares (see table below).  Warburg Pincus invested US$225m into Converge ICT last year in exchange for preferred shares convertible into 2.045b common shares.  This implies Warburg Pincus ("Coherent Cloud") acquired the shares for US$0.11 per share (approximately P5.5/share).

Our thoughts:

  • 5G is already being gradually rolled out by both PLDT and Globe. And Dito (assuming it actually pushes through).  Longer-term demand for wired broadband could be affected if 5G becomes sufficiently pervasive, fast, reliable and affordable.
  • The company has minimal debt (net debt of only P1.5bn) so an equity issue is not necessary to fund capex.  However, its franchise requires it to list. 
  • The company has definitely grown very fast but that is coming off a low base. Growth (%) in succeeding years will have to decelerate.  PE-G multiples are likely to be brought up.  However, any multiples (e.g. PER) for FY21 MUST be compared against growth AFTER FY21.  So 30x FY21 PER is NOT justified by 30% FY21 EPS growth.
  • PLDT has a market cap of US$6bn and enterprise value of US$10bn.  Its residential broadband and corporate data segments (most comparable to CICT's business) generated revenue of P53bn in FY19, equivalent to 33% of its consolidated revenue. CICT is probably going to do P14-15bn of revenue this year.  It is hard to imagine CICT being worth more than US$2bn especially since EBITDA margins are the same (~50%).

Globe’s broadband and enterprise business, on the other hand, have a combined revenue of P35bn.  Its enterprise value is US$9bn and the broadband and enterprise businesses contributed 23% of revenue.
