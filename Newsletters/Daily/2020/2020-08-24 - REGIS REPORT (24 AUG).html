REGIS REPORT

24 August 2020

The BSP issued Circular 1093 that significantly eases real estate lending limits of banks. The main features are:

  • Raising the limit of total real estate loans from 20% to 25% of a bank’s total loan portfolio
  • In computing a banks’ Real Estate Stress Test (REST), the following will be excluded:
    • Residential mortgages for primary homes (“for own occupancy”)
    • Foreclosed real estate assets

The relaxation is meant to promote continued lending to residential property buyers. Anecdotally, banks have been reluctant to lend for new mortgages. However, we believe the real estate limits are not a major reason for the hesitance. Rather, banks are likely worried about individuals’ ability to pay (ie employment, cashflow, etc).

It also signals that the BSP expects that, with higher NPLs and defaults, banks will likely take in large real estate assets in foreclosure / dacion proceedings.

On the other hand, to reduce the financial burden on consumers, the Central Bank is looking to cap the annualized effective interest rate on credit card debt to a maximum of 24%. For reference, most banks currently charge 3.2% per month or an effective annual rate of 46%.

The Bicameral Conference Committee has approved the Bayanihan II Bill. The Bill provides for a total P165bn in "fresh funds" for COVID-19 relief. This is as opposed to Bayanihan I, which realigned current budgets. Once passed into law, Bayanihan II will be effective until Dec 2020.

MACRO
Bicam approves Bayanihan II of up to P165bn
  • The Bicameral Conference Committee approved the Bayanihan II bill
    • Bill provides for P140bn in regular appropriations and P25bn in standby appropriations, if the funds become available
  • Bayanihan II retained the P5-8k cash assistance to low-income households, but it will now be limited to areas under “specific quarantine conditions” and to households will recently-returned OFWs
  • A 60-day grace period for the payment of all "existing, current, and outstanding loans," falling due on or before December 31, 2020, irrespective if there is community quarantine or not, will be implemented
  • Payments of residential and commercial rents, and utility bills falling due within the period of ECQ or Modified ECQ shall also be covered by a minimum of 30-day grace period
  • Once passed into law, Bayanihan II will be effective until Dec 19, 2020

  • The BSP raised the cap on real estate loans by universal and commercial banks from 20% of total loans (net of interbank loans) to 25%
  • In June, bank loans to the real estate sector grew 17% to P1.72 trillion 
    • This represents 18.5% of total loans
  • Additionally, the following will now be included when computing for a bank’s Real Estate Stress Test

    • Foreclosed real estate assets
  • The relaxation is meant to promote continued lending to residential property buyers
  • However, the real estate limits are not a major reason for banks’ hesitance to lend

CEMENT
2Q20 cement sector roundup
  • 2Q cement demand plunges on lockdown.  After gathering data from various cement manufacturers and the Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI), we estimate industry cement demand in 2Q20 collapsed by c. 38% YoY, after the lockdown halted construction activity for at least two months.  Cement plants in Luzon were shut while plants in Visayas and Mindanao remained open but at decreased utilization levels.  Eagle Cement lost market share in 2Q while independent importers seemed to have gained share a bit, with imports declining by c. just 28% YoY. 

After an estimated 24% drop in industry demand in the 1H, we estimate demand could fall by another 18% in 2H.   Note that the government’s latest infrastructure spend budget implied a drop of 25% YoY this year, after funds were re-allocated for the pandemic. Given that 1H20 infra spend is only down by 4% YoY, we expect infra spend numbers to further drop in 2H.

  • ASPs contract, competition intensifies. Given weak demand, ASPs dropped in 2Q by c. 2-6% although prices seem to have stabilized QoQ.  Cement manufacturers we spoke with indicated that competition was intense during the quarter.  Eagle Cement, in particular, talked about the possibility of dropping prices more to gain back market share. 
  • Challenges post-lockdown.  Construction activity has recovered since lockdown, albeit at varying degrees.  With support from the national government, big-ticket infrastructure projects are proceeding without hiccups while bite-sized infrastructure projects, largely in provinces and which comprise bulk of public construction, are facing bottlenecks in securing permits.  Private construction (i.e. residential, office, commercial) is estimated to be at 20-50% of pre-COVID levels due to permitting issues and strict social distancing requirements at construction sites. 
  • Import tariff review next month.  Given these challenges, local cement players are lobbying hard to hike the import duty in the next review (either September or October 2020).  Note that the import duty was set at P10 per 40-kg bag last September 2019 and based on the previous order, it is supposed to go down to P9-per bag on the second year, depending on market conditions.
