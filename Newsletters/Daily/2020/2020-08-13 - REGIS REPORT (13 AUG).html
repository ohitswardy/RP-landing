REGIS REPORT

13 August 2020

To mark our new Jefferies-REGIS co-brand partnership, we issued a Strategy report this morning "Pushing on a string". Not the most cheerful way to start a relationship, we know.

But the numbers are hard to ignore. 

The -16.5% GDP print in 2Q20 was likely the bottom, and we are already seeing promising signs of sequential improvement. However, the pace of recovery seems muted -- "stable, yet moderate" as DOF Secretary Dominguez put it. The re-imposition of MECQ due to a surge in new Covid-19 cases could take some momentum off. The sharp fall in OFW Remittances (-19% in May) could be a medium-term challenge to consumption. 

Meanwhile, Congress is unlikely to pass a massive stimulus bill like the P1.3tn ARISE bill. The government is already projecting the budget deficit to hit 9.6% of GDP this year, even before any stimulus. Instead, we are likely to get Bayanihan 2 (now in bicam) and CREATE (corporate tax cuts). While both are good, the combined stimulus -- including Bayanihan 1 -- would only be 3.6% of GDP, the lowest among our ASEAN peers.

The BSP has been the bright spot, unleashing aggressive monetary and regulatory intervention including 175bps in policy rate cuts and a 200bps RRR cut. Without the "pull" of fiscal stimulus though, we fear the BSP is "pushing on a string", diluting the efficacy of its moves.

Fundamentally, we maintain our defensive posture, preferring names like PLDT, PGOLD, CNPF, MER and ICT that continue to thrive under the current environment. For those looking for "reopening plays", we'd recommend large caps like BDO, ALI, URC, JFC, GTCAP and BLOOM. A list of "deep value" names for contrarians would include MBT, SECB, AGI, MPI, MEG and LTG.

On to results, with Bloomberry, Cebu Air, Shakey's and Shell reporting overnight. All booked 2Q losses, as expected.

Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) - in line - BLOOM incurred a loss of P4.7b in 2Q due to the suspension of casino operations. While the loss was expected, the likely extension of restrictions on leisure activities puts our 2H estimates at risk. The cash burn for the period also came in higher than expected.

Cebu Air (CEB PM) - in line -  1H20 core net loss reached P9.14bn after losses ballooned to P7.95bn in 2Q20 alone following the pandemic-induced lockdown.  Cash as at end-June dropped to P9.7bn from P17.5bn in end-March, suggesting a cash burn of c. P2.5bn per month (in line with estimates). 

Shakey's (PIZZA PM) - in line - Headline net loss in 1H20 was P290mn, but was P167mn excluding non-recurring items relating to store closures, among others. The 2Q20 net loss (P281mn) was expected as revenues plunged 54% YoY due to the lockdown. Notably, the company barely broke even at the EBITDA level (P44mn loss) owing to its cost saving initatives. Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) issued a disclosure this morning, with the most important item there an announcement that it was “permanently” shutting its 110,000bpd refinery in Batangas.  With refinery margins in the red, aggravated by the IMO mandate on fuel oil for ships and depressed demand due to Covid-19, the decision to pull the plug was finally made.  1H losses reached P6.7bn, implying a 2Q loss of P1.2bn.

By law, entities operating refineries are required to be listed.  The shutdown of the refinery means that requirement would no longer apply.  We would not be surprised if Shell the parent company decides to take it private eventually.

MACRO
Loan growth in June slows to 5.2%; PDs normalize 3.5%, NPLs inch up to 2.6%
  • The BSP just posted the preliminary data for all banks (ie not just UKBs) for June 2020. Lots of charts, after the text.
    • As we had expected loan growth slowed to 5.2% yoy in June, compared to 7.1% in May. On a qoq basis, the June loans were actually down 2.5% from the 1Q20 levels. This is notable, because 2Q is typically a strong quarter qoq.
    • Meanwhile, Past Due (PD) ratios normalized to 3.47%, down from 5.22% in May. Recall that some banks were reporting accounts as PD even during ECQ. This suggests the vast majority of borrowers updated their payments in June. The PD ratio of 3.47% in June was up from the 3.10% in March.
    • NPL ratios increased to 2.53% in June, vs 2.43% in May, and vs 2.25% at end-1Q.
    • Because of all the provisions put through in 2Q, NPL cover jumped to 110% in June, compared to 91% in March, and 97% in May.
  • Now that the "noise" of the errant banks is gone, the numbers are back in line with our forecasts for the sector. To refresh, our numbers assume 5% loan growth for FY2020, NPLs will rise to 4-5% by 2021, and NPL cover will not fall below 100%. 

 

AIRLINES
CEB 1H20 results – in line
  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) just released its 17-Q.  It posted a net core loss (i.e. excluding FX adj) of P8.65bn in 2Q20 alone, bringing 1H20 core loss to P9.51bn.  This appears in line with our expectations (P12.95bn net loss) but behind consensus (P6.14bn net loss).  1H EBITDAR stood at P1.39bn, down 91% YoY.  
  • In 2Q, fares and pax volumes collapsed, resulting in an EBITDAR loss of P2.15bn.  We reckon the losses will continue in 3Q, as the cost management initiatives (i.e. large staff reduction, AC parking, IT, and digitalization) only took effect this month.  
  • Cash and cash equivalents as at end June stood at P9.7bn, down substantially from P17.5bn in end-March.  The company burned through c. P2.5bn of cash per month, broadly in line with our estimates.   
  • The company is holding an earnings briefing today (Aug 13), 3PM. 

GAMING
BLOOM incurs P4.7bn loss in 2Q20
  • In a press release, Bloomberry said it incurred a net loss of P4.7bn in 2Q due to the suspension of casino operations. 1H20 net loss amounted to P3.3bn vs a profit of P4.7bn in 1H19. While the loss is expected, our FY20 forecast is premised on a gradual recovery in operations/profit in 3Q. However, the return to MECQ (until Aug 18) and likely extension of restrictions on leisure activities throughout August likely means similar losses in the succeeding quarter.
  • It booked 2.0bn in losses before interest, taxes dep & amortization in 2Q, dragging 1H EBITDA to P1.5bn (-84%yoy). This is 12% of our FY20 estimate.
  • Authorities have quietly allowed the dry-run of gaming operations (open to in-house guests and invited VIPs) under GCQ from June 15. This allowed BLOOM to generate GGR of P687mn in 2Q, down 95%yoy.
  • Cash balance stood at P31.2bn as of June from P39bn at the end of 1Q20.
