REGIS REPORT

27 August 2020

The government’s budget deficit for the month of July reached P140bn, as inflows continued to be weak (-11% YoY).  The cumulative seven-month deficit has now amounted to P700.6bn, up 6x from last year.

+++

Several media outlets reported that China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism established a “blacklist” of overseas tourist destinations. The move was apparently aimed at destinations that endanger “the personal property and safety” of its citizens, which has been interpreted to mean places that, among other things, promote gambling.  It is unclear for now which destinations are subject of the blacklist and what form the restrictions take.

Chinese gambling activity has fueled the rise in gaming revenue for the casinos in the Philippines and accounts for a third of Bloomberry’s (BLOOM PM) GGR.  Beyond just direct gambling, travel restrictions could also curtail other activities dependent on the entry of Chinese nationals, including the POGO sector activity and overall tourism.

+++

Meanwhile, the lower House committee on Ways and Means has approved a bill granting an exceptionally expansive blanket exemption from all taxes, direct and indirect, for San Miguel’s (SMC PM) Bulacan airport project.

While the bill proposes an already generous 10-year time frame for the tax exemptions, it also appears that the project will really only begin to pay taxes after it recovers its investment cost with an IRR of 12% (if the bill becomes law). Yes, you read that right: if the House gets its way, the project will not pay any direct or indirect taxes unless it generates a 12% return.

The Finance Department negotiated the terms of the airport project in order to minimize government exposure and subsidies but the House seems bent on undermining it. Given the DoF’s very public stance on these matters, we can expect it to recommend a presidential veto should this bill advance.

MACRO
Fiscal deficit swells to P140bn in July
  • The country recorded a budget deficit of P140bn in July, up 86% YoY
    • Revenues dropped 11% YoY to P235bn, as collections from the BIR and BOC dropped 12% and 9% respectively
    • Expenses rose 10.4% YoY due to the second tranche releases for the social amelioration program
  • This brings 7M20 fiscal deficit to P700.6bn (vs. govt’s FY20 target of P1.8tn deficit, 9.6% of GDP)
    • Revenues in 7M20 are down by 7% YoY to P1.69tn, accounting for 67% of the govt’s target (P2.5tn, -20% YoY)
    • 7M20 expenditures up by 24% YoY to P2.39tn, accounting for 55% of FY20 target (P4.34tn, +14% YoY)

INFRASTRUCTURE
House panel approves tax perks for San Miguel’s Bulacan airport project
  • The House ways and means committee approved a proposal to exempt San Miguel Corp’s. (SMC PM) Bulacan airport project from all taxes for at least 10 years
    • During the 10-year construction period, San Miguel Aerocity shall be exempt from any and all direct and indirect taxes and fees of any kind
    • At the end of the construction period, San Miguel Aerocity will remain exempt from income tax and taxes on real estate, buildings and property for the remainder of its franchise
    • The tax exemptions will expire “as soon as it is determined by a competent authority that the grantee has fully recovered its investment cost” (or project IRR of 12%) on the airport project
  • Tax exemptions were included in the substitute version of House Bill No. 7241 which seeks to grant San Miguel Aerocity, Inc. a 50-year franchise
  • Finance Assistant Secretary Maria Teresa Habitan said that the department is “not okay” with the proposed grant of tax incentives
    • Under the BoT (build-operate--transfer) law, the government must not provide subsidies or guarantees to proponents, including tax incentives
  • San Miguel Aerocity, a wholly-owned unit of SMC, is building a P740bn airport in Bulacan, with an annual passenger capacity of 100mn by 2025
    • Project also includes an 8.4-km tollroad, which will connect to the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX)
GAMING
China to black list overseas destinations that promote gambling
  • According to several news reports, China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism announced on Wednesday that it has established a blacklist of overseas tourist destinations that promote gambling for mainland Chinese customers
    • Cities that will be included in the blacklist are “endangering the personal and property safety of Chinese citizens”
    • The announcement did not mention any specifics – the countries included in the blacklist, how the travel restrictions will be implemented, etc
  • Other than potential travel restrictions, the renewed crackdown could mean tighter monitoring of cross-border money flow, proxy betting and online casinos
  • Philippine Integrated resorts like Bloomberry generate one third of gross gaming revenues from Chinese players
  • The sharp increase in Chinese arrivals to the Philippines in recent years have been linked to widespread operations of Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGO), online casinos that are typically run by and target Chinese individuals
UTILITIES
Manila Water closes bottled water business
  • Manila Water (MWC PM) is permanently closing Manila Water Total Solutions Corp. (MWTS) on Oct. 31
    • MWTS Healthy Family Business Division will close due to recurring losses and the inability to financially sustain its business operations
  • MWC launched the Healthy Family brand in 2015
    • MWTS booked EBITDA loss and net loss of P29mn and P169mn, respectively, in 2019
