REGIS REPORT

16 November 2020

The Philippines was one of the signatories to the blandly named Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) trade pact yesterday. The RCEP covers many of the Asian giants, including China and Japan, as well as all ten members of the ASEAN. It notably does not include India, who opted out earlier.

The trade deal will cut tariffs on many goods and pave the way for common “rules of origin”. For the Philippines specifically, the government expects RCEP to enhance market access for Philippine products like garments, auto parts and agricultural products.

+++

We have a whole slew of results again today and, surprisingly, more than half of them beat our expectations (albeit lowered).

The most notable results come from Security Bank (SECB PM) and LT Group (LTG PM). SECB’s reported 9M earnings already exceed our FY20 estimates even after P21bn in provisions. For perspective, BDO (BDO PM), which is many times bigger, set aside P24bn in provisions YTD.

For LTG, the tobacco business is clearly doing well despite contracting volumes. However, unlike Security Bank, its 60%-owned PNB (PNB PM) chose not to provision as conservatively (or aggressively, depending on how ones views it).

Have a look at our summaries and links to published comments below.

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (+) – Headline 9M20 NPAT of P6.7bn exceeded our FY20E of P6.5bn. This was despite massive P21bn in provisions, of which 10bn came in 3Q alone. Profit was boosted by another quarter of monster trading gains, as well as plunging deposit costs. The focus though will likely be on NPLs, which jumped to 4.03% from 1.58% in 2Q. It would appear that the vast majority of the NPL increase came from the consumer book, as virtually all of the 3Q provision was allocated to that segment. Consumer loans also contracted 9% qoq (+2% yoy). The bank will hold an earnings call at 430pm today.

  • Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (+) – Filinvest Land’s 3Q20 net profit fell 75% to P329mn. 9M20 net profit of P2.63bn (-40%) appears slightly ahead of our forecasts. However, what caught our attention was the 9% QoQ drop in rental revenue. We estimate that office revenue fell c. 10% QoQ, which might signal terminations of contracts or office rental concessions.   • Pilipinas Shell (SHLPH PM) (+) – Shell’s 9M headline net loss swelled to -P13.9bn, inclusive of P5.7bn inventory losses and a P7.5bn write-down relating to the decommissioning of its refinery. Excluding these items, we estimate core operations swung to a profit of P200mn in 3Q (vs -937mn loss in 2Q), as volumes continued to improve. In September, commercial volumes and retail volumes were down just 2% and 19% from pre-Covid levels

+++

Foreign portfolio investment in the Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE) continued to exit the country in Oct. We estimate foreign ownership of Philippine equities dipped another 16bp MoM to 22.6% even with the entry of recent IPO Converge (CNVRG PM). The drop for the PSEi components was 30bp.

The country’s FX reserves, however, continued to go up with more overseas borrowings. The gross international reserves rose US$3bn in a month to US$103.8bn as of end-Oct.  One normally associates a rise in international reserves with growing money supply (and credit) but things are clearly different during a pandemic. M3 growth is still in the double-digits (albeit slowing) but loan growth is now at only 3%.

MACRO
Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership signed
  • Fifteen Asia Pacific economies, including the Philippines, signed the world’s largest free trade deal
  • The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) is a trade pact that includes China, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea and all 10 ASEAN member countries
    • This is the first time that China, Japan and South Korea are all in a single free trade agreement
  • The prospective free trade area accounts for a third of global population and economy and builds on existing bilateral and multilateral agreements in the region
    • It also allows for a common “rules of origin” in the region, simplifying regulations identifying if products are made in a country
  • “The RCEP will further broaden the Philippines’ economic engagements with its trading partners should improve trade and investment, enhanced transparency, integrated regional supply chains and strengthened economic cooperation”, said Trade Sec. Ramon Lopez
    • RCEP is expected to enhance market access for key Philippine products like garments, auto parts, and agricultural products

GIR rises to US$103.81bn in October

  • GIR equivalent to 10 months’ worth of imports

  • FDI contracted by 5.6% YoY to US$4.4bn in 8M20
BANKS
Loan growth slows further to 2.8% in September
  • Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, grew 2.8% in September
    • Slowing down from the revised August loan growth figure of 4.7%
    • On a month-on-month seasonally-adjusted basis, loans decreased by 1.0%
  • Loans across most sectors decelerated during the month
    • Loans to manufacturing, as well as wholesale and retail trade and repair of motor vehicles and motorcycles contracted
    • Meanwhile, there was continued slowdown in credit card and motor vehicle loans during the month
  • Meanwhile, money supply grew by 12.3% in September, slower than the 13.7% growth (revised) in August

INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide 9M20 net loss at P822mn - Ahead of Regis but behind consensus
