REGIS REPORT

16 December 2020

Real estate brokers Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) released its quarterly update on the property market, confirming a further exodus of POGO office occupants. Based on its estimates, the office space occupied by POGOs dropped by another 131,000sqm in 4Q alone, bringing the net reduction to 180,000sqm for this year.

This will add downward pressure not just on office rents but also property values, particularly in the Manila Bay area which is disproportionately exposed to POGOs. We are already seeing this with LPC reporting substantial drops this year.

What’s a little surprising, however, about LPC’s latest report is that property values in the main CBDs of Makati and BGC are actually up. We cannot help but conclude the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) unprecedentedly loose monetary policy is a big reason for that. It is becoming apparent that one of the unintended consequences of the BSP’s money-printing is growing divergence in the situations of haves and have-nots as asset prices rise despite the pandemic.

More colour below.

+++

Broadband provider Converge ICT (CNVRG PM) announced that it has reached 1m subscribers and that it was increasing the speeds for its higher service plans to ‘celebrate’ this. Speeds for its higher packages are being raised by as much as 1/3rd, e.g., its P2,500/month plan will now get 100Mbps from 75Mbps, while its P3,500/month plan will get a speed increase to 200Mbps from 150Mbps.

The speed increase would make its service meaningfully cheaper than those from PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) on similar speeds. PLDT’s and Globe’s plans, however, come with voice lines.

One other important point to highlight is that CNVRG’s entry plan (P1,500/month for 25Mbps) remains unchanged. Most of its subscribers (57% as of 2019) are on this plan and that proportion is likely even higher today.

+++

Overseas remittances were up 3% in Oct, holding up surprisingly well despite global job losses. We suspect this may be supported by OFW sending more money to help tide their families over during the pandemic.

Megawide’s (MWIDE PM) original proponent status (OPS) for the US$2bn Manila airport rehab has been revoked by the government, apparently due to issues regarding its financial capability. While MWIDE will seek reconsideration for this decision, we think it unlikely that it can regain the project.

PROPERTY
POGO closures accelerate in 4Q20
  • Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) reported that POGO closures accelerated in 4Q20, leading to declines in Manila Bay area office rents/land prices
  • Meanwhile, office rents and land prices fared much better in Makati and Bonifacio
  • LPC estimates that Metro Manila office transactions reached 262,000sqm in 2020
    • BPO – 105,000sqm
    • POGO – 8,000sqm
    • Others – 149,000sqm
  • However, back-outs reached 442,000sqm, resulting in a net increase in vacancy of 180,000sqm from a net take-up of 1.4mn sqm in 2019
    • POGOs saw a net increase in vacancy of c. 250,000sqm while BPOs and other tenants had minor net take-up
    • LPC estimates that office rental rates in the Manila Bay area fell c. 40% YoY while rents in Makati and Bonifacio were down only mildly (c. 5%)
    • In 4Q20 alone, Metro Manila saw a net increase in vacancy of 131,000sqm (from only 49,000sqm in 9M20)
  • LPC also estimates that commercial land prices in Makati and Bonifacio rose 10-20% YoY while prices in the Manila Bay area fell c. 25%
  • While our channel checks suggest that Metro Manila condominium prices have fallen 5%-20%, LPC estimates that prices of high-end gated communities in Metro Manila have risen 10%-20%
  • We assumed that land prices in the Manila Bay area would fall 20% this year while prices in other parts of Metro Manila would fall 10%

TELCOS
Converge ICT raises speeds for mid- to upper-level service plans
  • Converge just announced it is increasing the speeds for many of its broadband plans (by around 1/3rd) after revealing it has reached 1m subscribers. 
    • So the 75Mbps plan goes up to 100Mbps and so on.
  • This should add some pressure for PLDT and Globe to do the same, but not as much as one might initially conclude. Why? The entry level plan (25Mbps for P1,500/month), which accounts for majority of its subscriber base, is actually unchanged.
  • Fifty-seven percent (57%) of its residential subscriber base was on the entry level plan (P1,500/month) as of end-2019. We believe this proportion should be higher now given the subscriber take-up over this year.
