REGIS REPORT

28 October 2020

The energy sector takes center stage in this morning’s REGIS Report, with strong results from Semirara (SCC PM), a temporary moratorium on new coal power plants, and Petron’s (PCOR PM) threat to shut its petroleum refinery plant.

First up, Semirara (SCC PM) reported headline 9M20 profit of P3.0bn (-64% YoY), which appears to be tracking ahead of Regis (P3.3bn) but behind consensus estimates (P4.6bn). We attribute the outperformance on better-than-expected coal mining ASP and sales volumes. Despite a rebound in coal and power sales volumes, 3Q20 profit fell by 28% QoQ to P750mn due to lower coal ASPs. Management is holding a briefing via Zoom at 1:30pm HKT tomorrow (October 29).

Speaking of coal, the Department of Energy (DOE) announced Renewable Energy (RE) initiatives, which included a) a moratorium on endorsements for greenfield coal power plants, and b) allowing foreigners to own 100% of geothermal power plants. On the face of it, the two pronouncements would be positive for listed RE players First Gen (FGEN PM) and AC Energy (ACEN PM). However, the grand irony is that the moves may actually benefit existing coal plant operators. While we laud the push towards renewables, these generally produce power at higher costs, and will likely push up WESM spot prices, making incumbent coal plants even more profitable. The DOE push also only speaks of greenfield coal plants. All the major players (AP, SCC, SMC, etc) have existing coal plants that can be expanded (ie brownfield). If indeed the ban is only on greenfield plants, then the DOE may have inadvertently (??) put up a huge barrier to entry, ensuring outsized profits for incumbent coal plant operators.

Petron (PCOR PM) chairman Ramon Ang threatened to shut down its oil refinery, as the imposition of 12% VAT on domestically produced fuel is not removed. Imported products are not subject to VAT, just to the oil excise tax. If the DOE, DOF and Congress don’t blink, then the shutdown could mean a huge write-down of its recently-completed $2bn upgrade.

+ + +

The Lower House has transmitted the 2021 General Appropriations Act (GAA) to the Senate. This will allow the Senate’s various sub-committees to review their respective sections of the voluminous document ahead of the resumption of the session on 16 November. The schedule is tight, though. After the budget hurdles the Senate, it will then go to the bicameral conference committee (bicam), where significant insertions and revisions can happen. All of this has to be one before the session is adjourns on 18 December. Tight, but doable. The last thing the country needs is a repeat of the 2019 budget, which was delayed until April that year.

+ + +

Grocery retailer Puregold's (PGOLD PM) 9M20 net profit (+11% YoY to P5.1bn) - not entirely surprising - came short of expectations. The 9M20 figures suggest a mild SSS decline in 3Q, while supplier support continued to be soft both QoQ and YoY. We can expect similar trends for the other listed retailers, who will be reporting over the next few days.

PGOLD will be hosting a briefing this afternoon at 3pm.

OIL & GAS
Petron refinery to close “very soon”
  • Petron President and CEO Ramon Ang said in an online briefing that Petron’s 180k barrel/day refinery in Bataan will be closed “very soon”, citing continued losses and uneven playing field between refiners and importers
  • Mr. Ang added that the only way to save the refinery is going to Congress and asking lawmakers to amend the tax regime for the downstream oil industry
  • The statement comes after rival Pilipinas Shell Petroleum announced the permanent shutdown of its Batangas refinery
