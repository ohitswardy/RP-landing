With Joseph Biden now the presumptive President of the United States, the inevitable question that gets asked is what does this mean for the Philippines. Frankly, it is hard to imagine that the Philippines will figure anywhere near the top of American foreign policy issues for the incoming administration.

Nonetheless, we thought we’d highlight some key themes that will likely see greater priority by a Biden administration and that might affect the country: (1) greater US multilateralism in both trade and defense, (2) climate change and green energy, and (3) human rights.

The US under Trump has largely pursued its trade and defense priorities unilaterally, neglecting engagement with allies in its “America First” policies, and notably abandoning Obama’s pivot to Asia. This arguably led to a further drift to China’s orbit of the Philippines, led by a President who already had anti-American pre-dispositions.

With climate change likely a top item on the Biden agenda, the decline of fossil fuels globally is likely to be accelerated. This could mean renewable energy becoming cheaper, sooner with profound, albeit not immediate, implications on Philippine energy policies as well.

The last item, human rights, could emerge to be a thorn in US-Philippine relations given the widespread international perception of the human rights situation in the country. Given the Philippine President’s particular sensitivities on this matter, it’s hard to imagine an improvement in PH-US relations if this matter is brought up.

On top of that, it is also worth mentioning that Duterte did openly endorse Trump, asking Filipino Americans to vote for him. While American diplomats can deftly gloss over this, the Philippine President’s personal inclinations are pretty clear.

Ayala Land (ALI PM) and Robinsons Land (RLC PM) showed some similar patterns in their 3Q20 results, with profits dropping by 3/4ths. Their mall businesses were hampered by transportation constraints while their residential earnings were curtailed by limited construction activities. On the bright side, mall foot traffic of both increased to c. 40% of pre-COVID levels from c. 20% in June. Ayala Land’s reservation sales fared better though, thanks to high-end provincial sales.

  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (+) – 3Q20 earnings fell 78% to P717mn. 9M20 profit dropped 40% to P4.4bn, slightly above our expectations. Earnings were boosted by an accounting change which would have otherwise brought earnings down 63% (to P2.7bn). 3Q20 Philippine presales fell 80%, worse than the 3%-40% drops of other developers. 9M20 presales fell 63% as the lack of Chinese bulk buyers reduced foreigners to 22% of total sales (from 43% in 2019).

  • Ayala Land (ALI PM) (–) – 3Q20 net profit fell 77% to P1.85bn, bringing the 9M20 figure down 73% to P6.4bn. Operating profit was broadly in line with our forecast but item such  as interest, taxes, etc. dragged the bottom line further. 3Q20 reservation sales recovered 65% QoQ (-38% YoY) to P22.5bn thanks to improving demand for suburban developments in provinces adjacent to Metro Manila. High-end lots by the Premier and Alveo brands seemed particularly strong.

D&L Industries (DNL PM) also announced results this morning, with 3Q earnings at P573m, which is 2x the level in 2Q20 but still slightly down YoY (-7%). The figure brings 9M earnings to P1.37bn, which is down 32%. Overall, the cumulative net income is trending below our FY20 estimate but looks like it will modestly exceed company guidance of P1.6bn to P1.8bn for the year.
