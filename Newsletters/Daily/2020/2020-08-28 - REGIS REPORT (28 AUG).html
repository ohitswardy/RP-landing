28 August 2020

The Dept of Finance (DOF) is anticipating revenue losses of around P100bn (US$2bn) annually just from the proposed cut in corporate tax rates alone embedded in the “CREATE” bill.  CREATE is the corporate tax reform package currently pending in Congress.

The proposed legislation aims to plug revenue leakage by rationalizing tax incentives even as it lowers headline tax rates. The DOF agreed to accelerate the reduction in tax rates as a “stimulus” measure in response to the pandemic.

We suspect the efficacy of the tax cut as stimulus would be much less than what’s been projected given that profits have plunged and are likely to still be under pressure next year. 5ppt of sharply lower profits would also naturally be sharply lower.

We are continuing to see hot money outflows in July with US$453m portfolio money leaving the country during the month.  YT-July, the net ouflows have reached US$7.76bn, with equities accounting for only a fraction of that.

Notwithstanding these hot money movements, the PHP has remained uncharacteristically strong. If you were wondering why, the reason is twofold.

Not only have (1) imports collapsed, including oil imports but (2) corporates and the national government itself have gone on an overseas borrowing spree, resulting in unprecedented dollar inflows.

Combined, private companies and the Bureau of Treasury have borrowed an aggregate US$11bn (at least) so far this year. No wonder the country’s international reserves have jumped US$10bn from the end of last year and are now, at (US$98bn) just shy of US$100bn.

  • The Department of Finance estimates that from 2020-2022, Tax Package 2 will lead to P249bn in lower tax collections, largely due to the immediate five percentage point reduction in the corporate income tax rate   
    • 2020 – P44.572bn
    • 2021 – P97.219bn

  • Tax Package 2, which is also called Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act or CREATE,  seeks to:
    • Lower the corporate income tax rate from 30% to 25%
    • Extend the applicability of net operating loss carry over (NOLCO) from 3 years to 5 years
    • Extend the sunset period for companies currently enjoying tax incentives
