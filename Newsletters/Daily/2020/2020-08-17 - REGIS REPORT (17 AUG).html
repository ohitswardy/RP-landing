REGIS REPORT

17 August 2020

The bicameral committee moved closer to approving the final/compromised version of the Bayanihan II bill.  Among the salient provisions highlighted were:

  • 60-day loan moratorium. The Finance Secretary and BSP Governor have strongly opposed the lengthy loan moratorium, suggesting a maximum of 45-day grace period instead.  Both have shared our view that the reinstatement and expansion of the loan grace period could only strain the banking sector.
  • 3-year suspension of permits for building of telco infrastructure. The telco industry will be relieved of the bureaucratic process of constructing infrastructure such as telco towers for a period of three years. The government is hoping to cut the time needed to secure permits to 16 days from, according to some parties, over 200 days. 
  • P160bn additional stimulus funding. The bill also appears to adopt the House version, which provides P162bn (=0.9% of GDP) of additional funding for COVID-19. The bicam version also reduced the allocation for the transportation sector to P10bn (vs. House: P30bn and Senate: P17bn). Discussions are also ongoing for a possible inclusion of other measures for the banking industry, including raising loan guarantees and the transfer of bad loans to asset management companies. 

We await the final version. 

+++

The auto industry reported vehicle sales data for July, with unit sales rebounding 32% MoM to 20,542 units.  In contrast, Toyota’s sales volumes recovered by 18% MoM to 8,833 units, bringing 7M20 sales volumes to 44,481 units.  Given the re-imposition of MECQ early this month, we reckon sales could suffer once again and put our FY20E sales volume target (90k units) at risk.

Max's Group (MAXS PM) - behind - Net loss widened in 2Q20 as the company had to temporarily shut operations in late-Mar/early-Apr, which led to a 50% drop in same-store sales. The company has resumed operations for 85% of its stores, though permanent closures are planned in order to cut costs.

MACRO
Shorter loan moratorium pushed; Other details of Bicam version of Bayanihan II
  • The country’s economic managers are recommending shorter loan moratorium than the 60-day loan moratorium given under the Bicameral version of the Bayanihan II
    • Finance Secretary Sonny Dominguez is recommending a maximum of 45-day grace period on loans
    • BSP Governor Diokno does not want to mandate a one-size-fits-all- moratorium.  He wants to leave the banks discretion to deal with the individual debtor, with the promise of some relief from the BSP
    • Bicam version rejects Senate’s proposed 30-day grace period and the 1-year extension proposed by the Lower House
    • The 60-day grace period approved by the joint panel on Friday “will cover everything, including credit card bills”
  • Moreover, the Bicam version also allowed the three-year suspension of permits to build cell phone towers
    • Senator Frank Drilon proposed the suspension of most of the 29-35 permits/documentary requirements, except for building permits
    • Bill also included a provision saying that only the Supreme Court could issue a TRO against building of telecom infrastructure, including cell sites and towers
  • Meanwhile, the Bicameral version of the Bayanihan II has followed the House version, which has a higher funding allocation of P162bn (vs. P140bn for Senate version)
  • Final version will determine if there is a need for the House to pass other measures for the banking industry
    • House version of Bayanihan II incorporated key features of the bills on Financial Institution Strategic Transfer (FIST) and Government Financial Institutions Unified Initiatives to Distressed Enterprises for Economic Recovery (GUIDE)
    • GUIDE bill proposed to increase the max. loan guarantee per borrower to benefit MSMEs
    • FIST bill, which allows banks to transfer bad loans to asset management companies, has already been approved by the House

MACRO
GIR level surges to US$98bn in July
  • The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by 2% (US$4.5bn) to US$98bn as of end-July 2020
    • MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the revaluation gains from the BSP’s gold holdings (+US$4.6bn MoM)
    • The end-July 2020 GIR level represents an ample external liquidity buffer which can cushion the domestic economy against external shocks
    • Equivalent to 8.9 months’ worth of imports

MACRO
Vehicle sales recover by 32% MoM in July (-35% YoY); Auto industry seeks help
  • CAMPI and TMA reported industry vehicle sales fell 35% YoY to 20,542 units (+32% MoM)
    • This brings YT-July vehicle sales to 105,583 units, down 49% YoY
    • CAMPI/TMA forecast industry car sales will reach 240k units (-41.5% YoY)
  • Volume reduction has serious operational and financial impact on the automotive industry
    • Submitted recommendations for industry support to the Dept. of Trade and Industry
    • Recommendations were not disclosed
  • Toyota remained the market leader in 7M20 at 42.1% market share, selling 44,481 units in 7M20 (-49.2% YoY)
    • Implies July 2020 sales stood at 8,833 units (-37% YoY, +18% MoM)
    • Our forecasts assume FY20 car sales will drop by 44% to 90,372 units
  • Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV
