REGIS REPORT

15 October 2020

Public opinion survey firm Social Weather Stations (SWS), in a poll released a few days ago, estimates adult joblessness at 40% in Sept, down from 45% three months prior. What’s remarkable about this is how different this data point is from the 10% unemployment figure reported by the government agency, Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) (for July).

What the numbers from both have in agreement is that there is an improvement since April, when the lockdowns were at their most severe. The big difference between them, obviously, is their approximation of the scale of the joblessness problem.

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) provides us a glimpse of what it is seeing on the ground from its operations in 3Q. Vehicular traffic at its toll roads continues to improve and is now down ~20% from pre-pandemic levels. What’s more interesting, though, is that electricity sales at Meralco (MER PM) for Sept are now practically flat YoY. More businesses are back, apparently, and residential usage is likely to be elevated due to work-from-home and study-from-home arrangements. Weather is likely to have played a part.

MACRO
SWS – Joblessness still at 40% in September
  • Last week, Social Weather Stations report (link) showed that adult joblessness remained extremely high at 39.5% in September, down from the 45.5% level in July.
  • This runs counter to the report of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) which said that unemployment improved sharply from 17.7% in April to 10.0% in July. Even if we include under-employment, the combined PSA figure dropped to 27.3% in July, from 36.6% in April.
  • Needless to say, there’s a world of difference between the two sets of data, and what they might mean for a recovery in GDP and indeed for corporate earnings.

  • The SWS also released a survey last week (link) that 77% of Filipinos still consider it risky to go to the grocery store or market (34% very risky, 43% somewhat risky). It also showed that, of those that have a job, 65% still consider it risky to go to work (24% very risky, 41% somewhat risky). 
  • This could have implications on the trajectory of the recovery, ie if people are scared to spend / go out. More so for the likes of mall operators, restaurants, etc.
MACRO
Imported vehicle sales drop 46% in 9 months
  • The Association of Vehicle Importers and Distributors (AVID) reported that its members sold 34,977 units in 9M20, down 45.6% YoY
  • Combining the data of AVID and CAMPI (adj. for brands present in both), 9M20 industry-wide vehicle sales fell by 46% YoY to 161,212 units
    • This implies that in September alone, industry-wide car sales dropped 24% YoY but was up +34% MoM to 26,595 units
    • Toyota’s market share in 9M20 stood at 39%, slightly lower than end-2019 (40%)
TRANSPORT
ICTSI to invest US$18mn in Ecuador
  • ICTSI is investing US$18mn to boost the port of Ecuador’s capacity
    • According to José Antonio Contreras, the general manager of ICTSI’s Contecon Guayaquil
  • Capacity expansion will be done in stages and will allow the port to accommodate larger vessels
    • First stage would raise the height of quay cranes five and six from 42 meters to 52 meters, and extend their boom lengths from 50 meters to 56 meters
    • The second stage involves works to reinforce the docks
    • Stage three will further deepen Pier 1’s draft to 13.5 meters
  • After this, ICTSI plans to increase the total investment to US$30mn
  • Last Dec 2019, Contecon Guayaquil signed the 19-year concession extension of the port
  • These investments are already in our estimates
  • We estimate the port of Ecuador accounts for 6% of NAV and 7% of revenues
  • ICTSI is trading at 9x FY21E EV/EBITDA
CONGLOS
MPI volume update

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) circulated an update on the volume recovery of its key subsidiaries. Below are the highlights for Meralco (MER PM) and the tollroad division, which respectively account for 66% and 25% of MPI’s gross profit (i.e. before parent company expenses):

  • Meralco electricity volumes down 6% YoY in 3Q20. Meralco’s electricity sales dropped 6% YoY in 3Q20, sharply recovering from the 18% YoY fall in 2Q20. In September alone, electricity volume sales were flat YoY and up 9% MoM at 4,007 GWH. This brings 9M20 electricity volumes to 32kGWH, down 7% YoY and on track to reach our full year forecast of 44k GWH (-5% YoY).

  • Domestic traffic volumes down just 20% YoY in 3Q20. Combined traffic volumes at the NLEX, Cavitex, and SCTEX are down by just 20% YoY in 3Q20, gradually recovering from the 62% YoY drop in 2Q20. While traffic volumes dropped marginally MoM in August after the 14-day re-imposition of tighter lockdowns (“MECQ”), they bounced 20% MoM in September and were at ~20% below pre-COVID levels.

We believe the volume recovery in 3Q20 should also translate to a large QoQ profit improvement for this division, as the operating leverage is high with over 60% of cash costs fixed. Recall that EBITDA and profit for this unit dropped by 40% YoY and 62% YoY in 1H20. 

  • Risks to Regis estimates. Cumulative 9M20 traffic volumes on its toll roads are down by 30% YoY to 360k. To reach our full-year average traffic forecast of 415k (-21% YoY), volumes need to rise by 3% YoY (to 580k/day) in 4Q, which is a tall order.

Nonetheless, we believe traffic volumes should recover further in October, after the government allowed more provincial buses to travel to Metro Manila and some LGUs in the north (e.g. La Union) also partially lifted restrictions for non-essential travel. The speed of recovery will depend largely on lifting travel restrictions further, in our view.

Note that every 5% reduction in our volume estimates reduces our profit forecast for the tollroad unit by 14% and for MPI by 5%. While this year’s traffic volumes could fall short of our forecast, we believe our 2021 average daily traffic assumption of 495k (+19% YoY) is reasonable, as this is still 5% below 2019.
