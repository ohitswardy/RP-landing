REGIS REPORT

21 September 2020

It seems households are bearing the brunt of the Covid-19 crisis. Consumer loan NPLs jumped 109bps qoq to 5.5% in 2Q20, compared to just 30bps qoq for total loans in the same period. Consumer loans accounted for c85% of new NPLs in 2Q. Surprisingly, the deterioration was more severe in mortgages (+200bps qoq) and autos (+87bps qoq) than it was for the unsecured book, ie credit cards (+42bps) or salary loans (-18bps). Granted, there could have been some distortion from the differences in the way banks counted NPLs during the 75-day grace period (16 March – 31 May). In any case, it’s clear that the accounts remained unpaid in June.

The NPL figures could have implications beyond the banks. Tighter credit standards – ie an increased reluctance to lend – could hurt residential sales, car sales, and overall consumption. Frustratingly, the BSP only releases consumer loan data quarterly, and with a 2+ month lag.

The state gaming regulator PAGCOR reported that there are now 55 registered POGOs, down from the 60 as of end 2019. Of that number, 29 have been allowed to resume operations, which means these firms have agreed to pay the requisite taxes (including the 5% franchise tax and the income taxes of its employees). While it is quite apparent that POGO operations have been disrupted this year, what is less clear is whether POGO operations can return (and to what degree) as the lockdown and travel restrictions are eased.

Filinvest Land (FLI PM) and Megaworld (MEG PM) are the most directly exposed to the POGO phenomenon as the latter occupy significant amounts of office spaces owned by them. The second order effects of a POGO exodus (or reduction) are more wide-ranging as Chinese workers have been responsible for driving up demand for residential property, most conspicuously in the Manila Bay area.

D&L Industries (DNL PM), in a press briefing, has lifted its earnings guidance for 2H, suggesting improved confidence from what it is seeing in its operations.

+++

Megawide (MWIDE PM) appears to be catching a break, or two, now. Not only has it been awarded the contract to build part of the rail from Manila to the Clark economic zone in Pampanga, it appears that the government will sweeten the terms of the NAIA rehabilitation project to make sure the project proceeds despite the pandemic. The Lower House is already pushing to grant exceptionally generous tax exemptions for the other airport project (by San Miguel (SMC PM).

Securing the NAIA project will make MWIDE the firm operating terminals at the country’s two most important airports: NAIA and Mactan-Cebu. It would have also built the Clark terminal. Whether the economics are favorable, though, is another matter altogether.
