REGIS REPORT

20 November 2020

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) surprised markets after today’s Monetary Board (MB) meeting, cutting its policy rate by 25bps to 2.0%, a new all-time low. Consensus was for another pause, with the next cut expected in early 2021.

This rate cut, like the last one in June, was in response to indicators of a much slower economic recovery than expected. While continued movement restrictions are keeping the spread of Covid-19 contained, they have definitely taken its toll on the economy. GDP contracted 11.5% YoY in 3Q20, prompting us to cut our FY20E to -10.0%. This was also reflected in 3Q20 earnings where more results missed our expectations than beat them. And the slow grind seems to be continuing into 4Q20. In the BSP press release for this rate cut, it noted “strong headwinds” including “muted business and household sentiment”, and the recent typhoons. The ban on Christmas parties won’t help, either.

As we wrote back in August, monetary stimulus, without accompanying fiscal stimulus, is effectively Pushing on a string. The Philippine economy is not highly dependent on (formal) financial intermediation. Household debt in the banking sector is less than 10% of GDP. And low interest rates are not enough to incentivize banks to lend. At end-September, bank loans were up just 1.5% YoY, but already -3.2% ytd.

Accelerated GDP growth, in our view, will require a) loosening of movement restrictions, including b) further opening of public transportation, and c) strong fiscal stimulus whether in the form of the 2021 budget, or additional “Bayanihan” spending bills.

That being said, loose monetary policy could be good for stock valuations. Apart from the rate cuts, the BSP has launched aggressive QE and DM to keep interest rates down across the curve. Some will note that the cut means negative real rates, with October inflation at 2.5%. However, even before this cut, market interest rates were already well below the overnight policy rate. The 1-month bill has been sub-1% for most of November. In fact, you’d have to go the 3-year BVAL to get above the (previous) overnight rate of 2.25%.

We’ve got the valuation support. Now to get earnings back up.
