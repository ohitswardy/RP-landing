REGIS REPORT

13 November 2020

Another batch of results today, a little more than usual, with work suspended yesterday because of another major typhoon. We had howling winds into yesterday morning (Thu) and widespread flooding across Metro Manila.

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (-) – Ayala Corp’s net income for 9M came in at P11.4bn, which is down 75% from the previous year, which itself was padded by large divestment gains. For 3Q alone, earnings of P3.4bn were down 59%, trending below estimates, but an improvement over 2Q. The group disclosed plans to restructure its listed power subsidiary AC Energy (ACEN PM), which will involve the sale of a minority stake to sovereign wealth fund GIC.

  • Shakey’s Pizza (PIZZA PM) (-) – 3Q20 recurring losses narrowed QoQ to P100mn, while recurring EBITDA turned positive as revenues improved 15% due to the resumption of dine-in services. Recovery appears to be outpacing the broader foodservice industry owing to its strong delivery business, but profitability is likely to recover faster given management’s tight rein on cost. Despite 3Q results being below expectations, we are raising our estimates for FY21 and FY22, and with them our target price (to P10/share).

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (=) – in line – We estimate that 9M20 net profit fell 41% to P7.6bn, broadly in line with our forecast but behind consensus. What caught our attention was the 7% QoQ increase in 3Q20 office revenue. While this could be a positive signal of better occupancy or higher rental rates, we suspect that this was merely due to the lapse of rent free periods of (relatively) recently-signed tenants. We hope to get more information during the company’s briefing on Monday.
  • First Gen (FGEN PM) (-) – First Gen’s recurring income for 9M fell 12% to $190mn. After preferred dividends, we estimate core income was $178mn (-13%), slightly behind our FY20 estimate of $250mn. Contribution from natural gas plants were weighed by planned outages and lower dispatch of San Gabriel and Avion.  EDC was also lower due to lower selling prices and higher tax payments.
  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) CEB (-) – 3Q20 core net loss remained elevated at P7.31bn, as travel restrictions persisted. 3Q capacity in operation was only a 6% of pre-Covid levels but this has improved to 15% in October and is expected to improve further to 23-25% by December.

Ayala Corp (AC PM) subsidiary AC Energy (ACEN PM) held a briefing yesterday to discuss its restructuring plans, which will involve (1) a rights issue, (2) a follow-on offering, (3) the injection of its international assets and (4) the entry of GIC as a financial investor (for 17.5%). This will be a lengthy process that will be completed only around a year from now, but management is already giving out the numbers so the pathway is clear rt and investors have an understanding of what they are getting into.  For those interested, we have more detail below.

The earnings call of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) was an opportunity to get an update on the reports the BLOOM is getting a license to operate online gaming sites. Frankly, allowing BLOOM to offer online gaming to local players, with lower fees/taxes it appears, seems directly contradictory to what other, senior administration officials previously said. Let’s see how these contradictions will be resolved or if they’ll just be quietly ignored.  More below.

Speaking of senior administration officials, media reports say that President Duterte has finally approved the draft revised concession agreements with the water concessionaires. The shape and form of the contracts will determine the value of the concessions and possibly affect the entry of tycoon Enrique Razon into Manila Water (MWC PM).

ACEN briefing takeaways
  • ACEN 9M20 income reached P2.9bn
    • ACEN reported a 9M20 net income of P2.935bn (from a loss in the same period last year).
    • The growth in attributable output was due to the asset infusion late last year, acquisition of operating plants and better thermal ability from SLTEC.
    • The increase in equity earnings was driven by higher contracted capacity

  • Board approves terms of the SRO
    • 2,267,580,434 shares at P2.37/share
    • Parent company AC Energy Inc. will not participate in offer to provide max availability of shares to minorities
    • Unsubscribed shares will be offered to institutions; ACEI may participate there
    • Every 1.11 shares held entitles minorities to subscribe to one share
    • Target receipt of regulatory approvals by Dec 2020 and SRO competition by 1Q2021
  • GIC investment
    • An affiliate of GIC Private limited will acquire 17.5% ownership in ACEN in a deal worth P20bn
    • Combination of
      • 4bn primary shares (via private placement) – 1Q21 - and
      • Purchase of 2.806bn secondary shares from ACEN’s parent, AC Energy Inc – 4Q21 - post infusion of international assets
    • Purchase price consideration of P2.97/share – valuation basis is after full competition of capital changes, SRO and infusion of international assets
    • 25% premium to board-approved SRO price of P2.37/share
    • At par with theoretical ex-rights price using 30-day vwap of P3.51/share and board approved SRO price
  • Timeline
    • ACEN just acquired the Philippine assets of its parent in exchange for 6.2bn shares, which increases its  share count to 13.7bn from 7.5bn.
    • Rights offer of 2.27bn shares (1Q21)
    • GIC buys 4bn new shares (1Q21)
    • Follow-on offering for an estimated 2.25bn new shares (2Q/3Q)
    • ACEN’s parent will swap its international assets into ACEN for estimated 16.7bn new shares (2Q/3Q)
    • AC will sell around 2.8bn shares to GIC (4Q)
    • By the end of this exercise, ACEN’s share count will rise to an estimated 39bn.

GAMING
Additional takeaways from BLOOM earnings call

Operational status –

  • VIP segment is operating at 25% of capacity, while mass/slots can go up to 40-50% during peak times.
  • They have been working closely with PAGCOR to allow increased operations particularly for slots where momentum is building. Even without the formal lifting of capacity restrictions, management expects the regulator will allow the gradual ramp-up above 30%.

Online gaming –

  • Management is working with PAGCOR on the amendment of the license and IRR.
  • They envision the first phase of its online platform to involve live streaming table games to local patrons (similar to what junkets do for video streaming/proxy betting). At the same time, they are also working on developing systems/applications for an online casino (program accessed thru website or app).
  • Tax structure has not been established.
  • They think they begin dry-run for live streaming within the month and maybe see revenue impact by 1H21
  • They will focus roll-out on domestic, but will explore international if the amendment to the gaming license allows
