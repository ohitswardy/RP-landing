REGIS REPORT

19 November 2020

We’ve got a roundup of the 3Q20 earnings below. But first, a quick word on Covid-19.

The world’s larger economies (in the northern hemisphere) seem to be struggling with a surge in coronavirus cases. In contrast, the Philippines seems to have things largely under control. This is probably due to continued tight restrictions on mobility within Metro Manila and between provinces. Confirmed Covid-19 cases have leveled off at 1,500-2,000/day. Perhaps more importantly, hospitals are NOT overwhelmed, as occupancy rates for beds allocated to Covid-19 treatment have come down significantly. Anecdotes are that doctors have resumed their “regular” practice as Covid cases dwindle.

This, together with hopes of several successful vaccines, could speed up the reopening of the Philippine economy in early 2021.

+ + +

And, that’s a wrap. The 3Q20 earnings reports are all in. And no one is calling for a recount. The results aren’t particularly good – yes, they’re much better than 2Q20, but they’re still predominantly disappointing. Of the 43 stocks under REGIS coverage, just over half (22) came in behind our expectations, with about a third (14) posting beats. And since REGIS analysts are natural bears, the misses against consensus were even more stark. Of the 30 stocks in the PSEi, almost two thirds (19) missed Street estimates.

To the upside, the 45% yoy decline in aggregate earnings for REGIS covered stocks (-40% for the PSEi) was significantly better than the -80% yoy collapse in 2Q20. In fact, earnings more than doubled,+137% qoq for REGIS coverage, and almost trebled +196% qoq for PSEi stocks. The biggest rebounds (like 3x qoq) came from banks (as BDO did a huge one-time provision in 2Q20), and consumers, particularly restaurant and non-essentials that were closed during the hard lockdown (or “ECQ”) during much of 2Q20.

Unsurprisingly, telcos continued to fare better, though more so for PLDT which continues to gain share. Other surprising sub-sectors included liquor (Emperador, LT Group), and non-Metro Manila residential property (Filinvest, Vista). Big disappointments came retailers Puregold and Robinsons Retail, both of which saw demand weaken, even for basic grocery necessities. Banks were all over the place, as banks took differing approaches to provisioning in 3Q20.

Anecdotes point to continued sequential improvement into 4Q20. However, the recovery could be blunted by a) a ban on Christmas parties, which drives much consumption on a typical 4Q, and b) the apparent exodus of POGOs. Will the hope of a vaccine and a further reopening of the local/global economy bring back “normalcy” in 2021? Or has Covid-19 done permanent damage to households and companies?

Meanwhile, here’s a rundown of 3Q results, by sector.

  • Banks – mixed – Sector earnings were all over the place. On one extreme, SECB and MBT still booked strong trading gains in 3Q, which both used to offset massive provisions. On the other, BDO sharply reduced 3Q provisions as the bank instead restructured 3% of its loans in 3Q alone, heading off an increase in NPLs. BPI was the happy middle ground. Operationally, loan growth slowed sharply, with MBT (-13% yoy) at one end, and BDO (+6%) at the other. NIMs were firm, driven by plunging deposits costs, though asset yields are likely to catch up. NIMs will also suffer from the rate cap on credit cards, which took effect in November. The focus remains on asset quality with industry NPLs rising to 3.50% in September.
  • Telcos – mixed - Overall wireless revenue has been recovering from the lows in 2Q but the more remarkable story is the growth in broadband demand. The pandemic has spurred work-from-home and study-from-home programs, resulting not just in an acceleration of growth but also shifts in the where’s and how’s of data consumption. The result is an industry seeing record additions in broadband subscribers.
  • Power – behind – Power demand is recovering, albeit gradually from its lows in 2Q but are still off from last year’s levels (-6% for Meralco in 3Q). Residential demand is up while commercial power use remains down as people stay at home. Spot prices have risen in 3Q but are also still relatively depressed.
  • Consumer/Retail – behind – The sector saw a sequential improvement in revenues as retail stores (outside of essentials) and restaurants reopened post-ECQ lockdown. The pace of recovery, however, was not as fast as initially expected given 1) the re-lockdown in mid-August and 2) limits on public transportation in Metro Manila. Consumer spending was noticeably weak, with staples and grocery retailers seeing softer growth vs 2Q. Pantry stocking had faded, and the absence of government handouts in 3Q put pressure on spending, especially for the lower income classes. With easing lockdown restrictions, the sector continues to see a sequential improvement in Oct-Nov, though 4Q may not receive the seasonal boost from holiday spending.
  • Gaming – behind – After the government quietly allowed casinos to operate within quarantine restrictions, pent-up demand appears to have driven industry GGR back to around 30% of pre-Covid levels. VIP has picked up in the same way that the mass segment has, even without foreign tourists, helped perhaps by video-streaming revenues. Despite limited operations, integrated resorts (IRs) have started to break even on a cash basis. Since then, PAGCOR has 1) softened its stance on the 30% capacity restriction and 2) approved Bloomberry’s use of an online platform to take bets (and considering the application of other IRs). These could result in a further improvement in GGR in the coming months. The online platform, in particular, could significantly expand the casino’s addressable market and would add value to the gaming license of the casinos.
  • Property – mixed – Though they were still below pre-COVID levels, mall revenue and residential reservation sales generally improved in 3Q20. However, cracks in the office segment became more apparent as multiple developers reported closures of POGO tenants. Residential presales were up 30% qoq on average, but still down some 40% yoy, with provincial demand far outpacing Metro Manila. Malls foot traffic is still only about 40% of pre-Covid levels, and mall operators are giving hefty rent discounts to tenants. And while office revenue showed the shallowest drop compared to pre-Covid levels, developers are reporting POGO closures given the continued crackdown from the Chinese government. The exodus could accelerate, as new legislation increasing POGO taxes took effect only in October.
