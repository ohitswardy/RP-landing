REGIS REPORT

11 September 2020

The banking sector’s NPL ratio increased to 2.75% in July, compared to 2.60% in June and 2.08% at end-2019. More strikingly though was that the past due (PD) ratio spiked to 5.28% from just 3.49% in June. The deterioration is hard to understate – July PD loans were up 89% yoy and 52% mom. In nominal terms, almost P200bn (c$4bn) worth of loans became delinquent in July alone. The surge came as the loan “grace period” from the Bayanihan 1 Act expired on 31 May. After a couple of false readings in Apr/May, we reckon the July PD print is only beginning to show the stress on borrowers – and the broad economy – brought about by Covid-19 and the lockdowns.

Given the lag, we expect NPL ratios to follow the PD trajectory into 2H20. Complicating matters, though, is the potential of a new grace period under Bayanihan 2, which is expected to be signed soon. While this could again push back NPL recognition into 2021, it seems that our forecast of 4-5% peak NPL ratio is increasingly at risk.

To the upside, banks aggressively provisioned in 1H20, taking NPL coverage to 111% and LLR up to 2.65% of total loans.

In a very short disclosure to the PSE (link) Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) said that its board had approved “a plan to realize the market value of the Bank’s prime properties”.  No further details were released. It is unclear whether the properties will be sold outright, developed internally, or developed in JV with a property developer. Another option could be a sale / lease-back / REIT of their branch assets.

Far as we can tell, the main assets would include

  • The 10-hectare head office property in the Manila Bay area
  • The former Allied Bank head office on Ayala Avenue in Makati
  • The former “RJ” property along Sen Gil Puyat Ave in Makati

The stock is trading at 0.23x its historic 2Q20 book value. Its market cap as of yesterday’s close was P34.9bn. The book value of its PPE and ROPA at 2Q20 was about P35.4bn. By our estimate, the PNB head office land alone has a market value of at least P20bn…

MACRO
External trade still down in July
  • Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority showed the trade deficit plunged by 50% YoY to US$1.8bn in July
    • Imports fell 24% YoY (vs -23% in June) while exports dropped 10% YoY (vs. -13% in June)
    • Slower decline in exports reflects improving demand abroad but continued contraction of imports reflects weak domestic demand
    • Imports of raw and intermediate goods dropped 14.8% YoY while imports of capital goods shrank faster by 29.8% YoY
  • Brings 7M20 trade deficit to US$12.5bn, down 48% YoY

MACRO
Dollar reserves seen to top US$100bn
  • BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said it was likely for the gross International reserves (GIR) to top the US$100bn mark before the end of the year
    • BSP plans to actively participate in the gold trading market largely because of the change in the price dynamics of gold
  • The GIR stood at a record high of US$98.6bn in July due to the national government’s foreign borrowings to finance its COVID-19 response
    • GIR is equivalent 8.9 months of imports
    • This was higher than the GIR level of US$87.8bn in end-2019
BANKS
Past due ratio spikes to 5.3% in July; NPL ratio at 2.75%
  • Past due (PD) ratios jumped to 5.28% in July, compared to 3.49% in June and to 2.71% at end-2019
  • In nominal terms, PD loans  surged 89% yoy to P573bn. The spike comes as the loan grace periods under Bayanihan Act 1 expired on 31 May 
  • Meanwhile, NPL ratios also rose to 2.75% in July, from 2.60% in June and 2.08% at end-2019
  • To the upside, banks used the massive trading gains to accelerate loan loss provsions in 1H20. As a result, NPL cover improved to 111% and loan loss reserves (LLR) to total loans rose to 2.96%
  • The PD and NPL ratios are exacerbated somewhat by the sharp slowdown in loan growth. As of July, loans of the Philippine Banking System (PBS) – including thrift and rural banks – decelerated further to 4.8% yoy, from 5.2% in June, and 10.8% in March
    • Loans were flat (-0.2%) mom, and down 2.55% year-to-date
POWER
First Gen’s San Gabriel plant trips
  • The 414MW San Gabriel plant tripped and First Gen’s contractor Siemens is investigating the issue
  • The plant is unavailable in the meantime
  • The facility is part of First Gen’s 2,000MW natural gas-fired capacity in Batangas
  • San Gabriel is fully contracted with Meralco; it is given 65 days downtime annually of which 32 are still available for the year
  • We estimate the plant’s effective selling price to be just over P3/kwh given the drop in fuel prices
