REGIS REPORT

15 March 2022

Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr extended his commanding lead in the latest opinion poll on voter preferences conducted by Pulse Asia. Marcos was the choice for 60% of those polled from Feb 18-23, up from 53% in the previous survey.  Vice President Leni Robredo was a distant second at only 15%.

Is it now all over but the shouting?  With the elections less than three months away, the likelihood of an upset is now looking remote.  One thing that can be said is that businesses and investors should now have time to prepare themselves for the new realities.

+++

Oil companies implemented possibly the biggest single day increase in pump prices today. Diesel prices were raised around P13/liter (~20%) while gasoline prices were lifted P7/liter (~10%). While the weekly (usually) price adjustments are something consumers are used to, the magnitude of the price hike this week will be among the early big hits to wallets that consumers will feel and, crucially, a significant psychological jolt.

The Philippines imports most of its oil as refined product, so refined product prices in the region drive pump prices, with a two week lag.  The correction in diesel prices last week could provide some welcome relief by next week.

+++

Speaking of price increases, the country’s biggest tobacco firm, PMFTC, is raising prices by as much as 21%.  The price increase comes much later than it usually does (by around five months) amidst rising incidence of illicit trade but the magnitude will help to lift average prices for the year close to our expectations.  We reckon LT Group (LTG PM), which owns 49.6% of PMFTC and derives most of its income from it, should be able to continue paying a dividend yield of ~10% given how the price adjustments will affect profitability. LTG is currently trading at <4x PER for FY22.

+++

Foreign ownership in the Philippine stock market dipped to 21.9% in Feb.  Check out the details below, including some of the issues affecting the consistency/accuracy of the data.

We also have an update on Cebu Air (CEB PM), which is seeing air travel slowly normalizing.  The big spike in oil prices, however, has thrown a monkey wrench into the company’s (and the industry’s) growth plans.

AIRLINES
Cebu Air update - Capacity + Oil
  • CEB reducing capacity? According to Jefferies’ airline capacity survey, CEB’s weekly seats (domestic + international) dipped back to 51% of pre-Covid levels in the week starting March 7 from 53% the previous week and 55% in end-Feb.  We reckon the decline could be reflecting (a) normalization after the Feb 25 holiday and/or (b) a reaction to the surge in crude oil prices in the first two weeks of March.  It is worth noting that the survey is based on estimated seat counts of flights for the upcoming week and can still change once actual data is captured and reflected after 1-2 weeks.
  • No capacity reduction, according to management. Our conversations with CEB management suggest capacity has not been reduced at all and they have been mounting flights in preparation for the upcoming summer peak season.  They are, however, closely monitoring seat load factors, fares, and fuel cost increases on a per route level, given the recent spike in oil prices.

Year-to-date, CEB’s capacity seems to have reverted to 46% of pre-Covid levels.  To reach our forecast for the year (70% of pre-Covid levels), CEB’s capacity would need to average c. 90% for the rest of the year.  Note that our forecast is broadly in line with management’s announced guidance.  A reduction in capacity due to surging oil prices raises downside risks to our forecasts.  Every 1% reduction in pax vols/capacity could reduce CEB’s EBITDA/profit by 4%/15%, holding other things constant.

  • Jet kero prices drop but still elevated. Since Russia invaded Ukraine on Feb 24, jet kero prices have swung wildly to as high as US$151/bl on March 9 but has since declined to US$118/bbl.  CEB has zero outstanding fuel hedges this year and we do not have visibility on the hedging strategy of Philippine Airlines and Air Asia Philippines.  Needless to say, the most hedged player (if there’s one) will be able to compete more aggressively in the market.

The fuel surcharge currently in place provides protection from rising crude prices and currency depreciation. It is, however, delayed by approximately 3 months. Ultimately, demand and supply will dictate the direction of CEB’s profitability.  Prior to the recent fuel spike, CEB has been able to raise fares over and above the fuel surcharge, with its competitors following its lead. 

However, the recent fast and steep increase in jet kero prices has made fare hikes more tricky, likely leading to margin compression, near term.  Every 10% increase in our jet kero forecast (average of US$80/bbl) could reduce CEB’s EBITDA/profit by 4%/12%, holding other things constant.

  • CEB inexpensive relative to peers but elevated fuel prices an overhang.  Trading at 5.2x FY22E EV/EBITDAR, CEB is inexpensive relative to LCC peers (Capital A – 20x; Spring Airlines – 18x, Vietjet – 40x) and regional airlines (36x).  However, given the volatility in oil prices, investors may not be in a rush to buy it.
CONSUMER
PMFTC raises prices by +17-21% for high and low-end brands effective March 21, retains pricing for mid-priced segment
  • LT Group said that its unit PMFTC raised cigarette prices by around P20/pack for its high (Marlboro) and low-end (Jackpot) brands effective March 21. However, it did not raise prices for mid-priced brand Fortune. The price hikes for the high/low-end are above our estimate for the year (+P10/pack) while the low-end prices the mid-segment below our estimates  – all coming in later in the year than anticipated. The higher price hike but later implementation means the average effective price for FY22 of about P109 will be slightly higher (+1%) than our estimate of average price/pack for the year.
  • Truck prices higher by as much as 21% per pack except for Fortune. The truck price of Marlboro has been raised to P138/pack (+17%) while the truck price of Jackpot has been raised to P109/pack (+21%). Meanwhile, it held off any increase for its mid-priced segment Fortune, which retained pricing at P109/pack. Our forecasts have the price increase at P10/pack across the board beginning Jan 1. On a per stick basis, the increases translate to a +14-20% for Marlboro and Jackpot, but none for Fortune.
  • Average price for FY22E of P109/pack very close to our estimate. The late implementation and different hikes across brands mean the effective price increase for the year will be very similar to our estimate. We estimate that PMFTC’s average price for FY22E will be around P109/pack vs our estimate of P108/pack. Assuming that volumes hold (c.35bn sticks), we think PMFTC’s annual profit can come in around P38bn. Of this amount, LTG’s share is about P19bn.
  • LTG can comfortably maintain dividend at >P1/share. LTG’s prospective share in PMFTC’s profits  is still well above the dividends LTG paid out last year (P1.08/share ~ P11.7bn total), which gives us confidence that the dividend yield can be sustained at >10%.
  • LTG is trading at an estimated 3.5x PE for FY22E and a discount of 71% to market-NAV.

OTHERS
Market foreign ownership declines in February
  • We estimate that market foreign ownership fell 38bps MoM to 21.9% while PSEi foreign ownership rose 13bps MoM to 26.2% (in February)
  • In addition to declines in foreign ownership of some index constituents, the reduction in market foreign ownership was exacerbated by the 17.4% MoM share price drop of Globe, which has among the highest levels of foreign ownership (at 57.7%)
  • The increase in the PSEi’s foreign ownership was largely due to the index rebalancing in mid-February
    • MONDE, which we estimate has foreign ownership of 64%, was added to the PSEi
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • AC – foreign ownership fell 2.2ppt to 26.4%. This is inconsistent with trading data, which show that AC did not even turn over 1% of shares outstanding in February.
    • ALI – foreign ownership dropped by 2.1ppt to 30.9%. This is inconsistent with trading data, which show that ALI experienced net foreign buying in February.
    • GTCAP – foreign ownership declined 1.6ppt to 19.6%.
    • SECB – saw the biggest increase in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents. Foreign ownership rose 26 basis points to 38.0%.
