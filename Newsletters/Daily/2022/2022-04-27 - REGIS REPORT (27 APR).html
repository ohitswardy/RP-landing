Rizal Commercial Bank (RCB PM) plans to remove the 40% foreign ownership limit on the bank, via amendments to its Articles of Incorporation during its AGM in June. Presumably this is to allow new equity from either of the two existing foreign shareholders – Cathay Life (22.2%) and Sumitomo (4.99%) – or from a new entity. (Total foreign ownership was 33.7% at end-March.) The potential infusion of equity will help the bank grow (solo CET-1 was 11.8% at end-2021). However, this could make it more difficult for the bank to foreclose on land assets (particularly for mortgages) given the Constitutional requirement of 60% Filipino ownership. At yesterday’s close of P20.10, the stock is trading at less than 0.4x P/book (end-2021 BV P54.50). The last purchase by Sumitomo in June 2021 was priced at around P44/sh. The million-dollar question is – will there be a tender offer this time around?

While other firms already held their 1Q22 results call, liquor firm Emperador (EMP PM) held its earnings call for its FY21 results. Beyond the headline numbers (headline income up 25% to P9.98bn, with 4Q profit of P2.7bn (+30% YoY)), the main takeaways  from the briefing were the strength of the brandy business and the weakness in whiskey operation, and the looming impact of rising costs and logistics issues.

The company was mum about its plan for a secondary listing in Singapore and did not even provide a broad statement on the motivation behind it (e.g. “to raise funds for expansion” or “to achieve better valuations”).

BANKS
RCBC to remove foreign ownership limit
  • RCBC (RCB PM) disclosed that its board of directors have approved the deletion of the provision limiting foreign ownership to 40% from its articles of incorporation. The change requires ratification by the shareholders at its AGM scheduled for 27 June. As of March 2022, RCBC’s foreign ownership was at 33.67%. Of this, Cathay Life was 22.19%. In June 2021, Japan’s Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corp (SMBC) acquired a 4.99% stake in the bank. It is unclear at this stage if RCBC is making room for either Cathay or SMBC (or both?), or indeed for yet another foreign partner.

  • In any case, a stronger, better capitalized RCBC could pose a new competitive threat in the industry. As of end-2021, RCBC was the country’s 6th largest non-government bank, with total assets of P959bn (roughly $18.5bn). However, that’s less half the size of the Big Three, at between P2.5-3.7trn. As of end-2021, RCBC’s CET-1 was 12.16% at the consolidated level, and 11.81% at the “solo” or parent bank level, not far from the regulatory minimum of 11.0%. (Though the BSP suspended the capital conservation buffer for Covid, it will probably reinstate it at some stage.)

  • To the downside, though, having foreigners own more than 40% of the bank will constrain it from owning land assets. The bank will likely sell/transfer these into different entities to comply with the Constitutional limit of 60% Filipino ownership of land. However, the inability to own land will hinder the bank’s ability to do mortgage lending, or indeed to lend while taking any land assets as collateral. In theory, the bank can set up an SPV that will take the asset at foreclosure. Apart from the requirement to sell within 5 years, the SPV will make foreclosing more complicated and/or expensive. Historically, foreign banks have tried to enter mortgage lending, but this requirement forced them to eventually retreat.
  • Beyond RCBC though, one has to wonder if either Cathay or Sumitomo (or both) might also be interested in the family’s insurance businesses. Malayan Insurance is the country’s largest non-life insurance company by premiums written, and second-largest by assets. The family also controls 51% of Sun Life Grepa Financial, with Sun Life of Canada taking the 49% stake.
  • At current prices (P20.10/sh), the bank’s market cap is just under P40bn (c$780mn), or about 0.35x Price-to-book, based on its end-2021 book value (P111bn, or P54.50/sh). SMBC’s purchase of 4.99% in June 2021 was priced at about P44/sh.
CONSUMER
EMP FY21 profit +25%yoy; profit jumps in 4Q on seasonal strength of brandy
  • EMP’s net FY21 net income increased 25%yoy to P9.98bn, helped by a sequential improvement in profits from brandy. Brandy profits jumped 49%qoq to P2.118bn on the back of a resurgence in revenues (+46%qoq) in the 4th quarter, offsetting a softer QoQ performance from whisky. The FY headline number included a one-time deferred tax expense at WMG. Excluding this, profits would have been 34% higher yoy at P10.6bn.
  • The brandy segment benefited from the seasonal jump in 4Q sales, with revenues and profits accounting for 33% and 28% of the segment’s FY contribution. GP margin also improved to 34.4% in 4Q from 29.6% in 3Q21. However, YoY sales were down 8%YoY. Management noted that this was largely caused by unavailable SKUs of Fundador due to logistics issues. The FY21 profit contribution was higher by 30%, driven by particularly high margins (>40%) in the early part of the year.
  • Profit from whisky dipped 21%QoQ to P594mn on lower GP margins (38% vs 40%) in the quarter, offsetting the 15%QoQ growth in sales. Throughout last year, GPMs have been under pressure as sales shifted to less expensive brands (largely off-trade) within the branded portfolio. The FY growth profit growth of whisky was still strong at +44% to P3.2bn. Whisky comprised about 32% of the group’s overall business.
  • Management spent a great deal talking about cost pressures and logistics issues that have affected its businesses since the latter part of 2021. They discussed having to increase to prices by up to 10% early this year to partly cover the rise in costs (dry goods, packaging etc). Logistics issues have also resulted in a number of SKUs not being available. For the year, management said that the focus will be balancing margins/pricing and volume targets.

POWER
ACEN, partners plan to enter US wind farm market
  • ACEN disclosed that its board had approved plans to seek opportunities in the renewable energy market of the United States
  • The US entry is thorough a partnership with UPC Solar & Wind Investments LLC and Pivot Power Management (PPM)
  • The partners intend to acquire operating wind projects in the US while exploring strategies to extend their useful life through preventive maintenance and re-powering
  • Transaction is subject to the usual conditions precedent ahead of closing
  • It has an attributable capacity of 3,800MW of which 87% are from renewables
  • ACEN is trading at 44.1x PE and 22.0x EV/EBITDA for FY22E
