REGIS REPORT

01 April 2022

Loans across the entire commercial banking industry  were up +8.8% in Feb. While the growth figure is exaggerated by the merger of BPI (BPI PM) and subsidiary BPI Family Bank, there is still an acceleration from January (+8.4%) to February. We are expecting a recovery in credit this year, with growth hitting double digits by year-end.

+++

Union Bank’s (UBP PM) will be proceeding soon with its shares going ex-rights on April 6. The final terms (price, rights ratio) should be known in the next few days. UBP previously said it would be raising around P40bn to fund its purchase of Citibank’s consumer banking business in the Philippines.

+++

Electricity spot prices continue to climb amidst recovering power demand and despite the price caps. Not only are fuel costs soaring, output from the Malampaya gas field, the single largest source of fuel for Luzon, has declined sharply. With the DOE not acting to extend its contract, the current operator of Malampaya has not been investing to maintain output.

The high power prices are a foretaste of what are likely to be a recurrent issues over the next few years: tight supply, higher prices and intermittent outages. We expect the gencos to benefit from it, particularly Aboitiz Power (AP PM) and Semirara (SCC PM). Other gencos like ACEN (ACEN PM) and First Gen (FGEN PM) will also benefit from this tightness longer term but could face short-term issues as they will need to buy power from the spot near-term.

+++

The government’s budget deficit declined 9% in Feb as both revenue and expenditures dropped. People counting on a government-driven bump in spending this election cycle might be disappointed. The spend last year was high due to pandemic-related expenditures.

Speaking of the pandemic, the government is keeping majority of areas in the country, including Metro Manila, under Alert Level 1 until at least April 15.

BSP chief: Inflation may breach March target
  • Headline inflation could accelerate beyond the central bank’s target of 3.3-4.1% (point inflation target at 3.7%) in March
    • February inflation rate stood at 3.0%
    • Street forecasts at +3.6% for March
    • Cited oil price hikes along with high electricity rates in Meralco (MER PM) service areas, higher meat prices, and the peso depreciation are the primary sources of inflationary pressures during the month

  • BSP Governor Diokno has said they are looking to start increasing interest rates by the second half of the year (to 2.5%), noting the key policy rate could reach 2.75% by 2023
MACRO
Budget deficit narrows in February
  • The National government’s budget deficit narrowed to P106bn in Feb, down 9% YoY
    • Tax revenues dropped 3% YoY due to lower collections from the BIR (-11% YoY)
    • Expenses also fell 5% YoY on high base effects from the stimulus under Bayanihan II
    • Excluding last year’s stimulus, expenses would have grown by 9.6% YoY due to higher national tax allotments to LGUs; health and education programs; and funds for the COMELEC
  • This brings YT-Feb budget deficit to P129bn, lower YoY

MACRO
National government debt at P12tn in end-Feb
  • The national government’s outstanding debt hit a record of P12.09tn in February
    • This is higher by P63.83bn (+0.5% MoM) from January 2022 due to currency fluctuations, and net financing from both local and foreign sources
    • 70% of debt stock are from domestic lenders while 30% are from external sources
  • In March 2022, the government raised US$2.25bn from its triple tranche USD-denominated bond offering, as well as P458bn of retail treasury bonds
  • The government’s debt stock reached a record of P11.7tn in end-2021, equivalent to 60.5% of GDP

BANKS
U/KBs loan growth accelerates to 8.8% in Feb
  • Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, grew at a faster rate of 8.8% YoY in February
    • Cited easing COVID-19 restrictions drive the improvement in mobility and market demand
    • Driven by faster credit growth for wholesale and retail trade, as well as credit card loans
    • Auto loans posted a smaller drop of 5.2% (vs 5.6% the previous month)
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth was steady at 8.5% YoY in February

POWER
Power prices increase as demand inches up
  • Average electricity spot market prices rose in March as margins thinned with power demand breaching pre-pandemic levels, according to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines
  • In a virtual briefing, the IEMOP said average wholesale electricity spot market prices increased from P6.19/kWh last February to P6.97/kWh in March
  • The average margin for March decreased by 4.04% due to increasing demand
  • Peak demand was recorded on March 23 at 13,749MW, surpassing the peak demand in February at 11,985MW
  • Luzon peak demand reached 11,617MW
