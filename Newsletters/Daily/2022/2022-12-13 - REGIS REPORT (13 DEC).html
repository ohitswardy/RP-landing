REGIS REPORT

13 December 2022

Banks’ NPL and consumer loan data continue to improve, underscoring our Overweight call on the sector. October asset quality improved across the board, with NPL ratio down to 3.49% (ex-interbank loans), the lowest level since September 2020, when the first Bayanihan iteration lapsed. More notably, sector Past Due (PD) ratio also improved to 4.03%, indicating that inflation and rising interest rates are not causing significant stress on borrowers… at least not yet. Granted, it’s early days, and not all loans have yet to reprice to fully reflect the 300bps policy rate hikes. Watch this space.

Meanwhile, consumer loans accelerated to 11.7% YoY in 3Q22, from 6.4% YoY in 2Q. NPL ratios for consumer loans also dropped 92bps QoQ to 7.19%. Consumer loan growth was driven by credit cards (+25%) and salary loans (+52%), reflecting the strong reopening data. We suspect part of the salary loan surge is due to the launch of fintechs and digibanks. The “dark side” might be that the resilience in consumption might be driven by increasing household debt. Mortgage and car loan data also continued to improve, reflecting banks’ relaxing of credit standards. Given the low credit penetration, we suspect that rising interest rates will have less of an impact on home/car demand as will the availability of credit.  

+++

The implementing rules (IRR) for the recently passed SIM Registration Act have been released. The law requires the registration of all SIMs six months after it takes effect on Dec 27 (extendable by another 120 days).

The holders of existing SIMs will be required to register at the risk of them being activated. New SIMs will have to be registered before activation. This piece of legislation was enacted to help contain crime / fraud relating to mobile phones, whose owners were able to stay anonymous

Among the more immediate effects of this law will be a substantial drop in the headline number of subscribers (SIMs), especially in the prepaid segment. Many will be either unable, unwilling or even unaware of the requirement to register. This is thus likely to result in a short-term dip in revenue when the wholesale deactivation of SIMs is implemented after the deadline lapses.

Longer term, we expect the registration requirement to reduce the practice of throwaway SIMs and rotational churn. It is also likely to reduce switching between carriers given the extra requirements.

The impact of SIM registration will, however, go beyond just the telcos. The ability to identify the holder of a SIM and associate a phone number with a specific individual will help accelerate the adoption of other mobile-based services like lending.

+++

Property consultancy Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) estimates that office vacancies stayed at 18% this 4Q22, unchanged from 3Q22. This implies net Incremental take-up in the quarter (177,000 sqm) roughly matched new supply. Despite rising demand from BPOs, we still expect office vacancies to go up further in the next two years.

On the other hand, capital values of residential property remained largely flat this year. The notable exception here was beach property, whose prices are up between 5%  and 35% this year.

+++

Meralco (MER PM) issued a claim against San Miguel (SMC PM), asking it to pay for the increased cost of purchasing power from the spot market. The latter stopped supplying power under its power supply agreement (PSA) on Dec 7, using the restraining order issued by the Court of Appeals as cover. 

SMC is trying to escape its contract to supply power to Meralco at a fixed price, as fuel prices soared and its gas supply contract from Malampaya ended without it being able to find alternatives immediately (its LNG supply was unable to come in on time).

How this matter is resolved by the courts, eventually, will determine if SMC pays the bill or if the consumers do.

BANKS
October asset quality improves, in both ratio and PHP terms; loan growth moderates a smidge
  • Asset quality (all banks) improved across all major ratios in October, showing even nominal PHP declines MoM. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) came in at 3.49%, down 3bps MoM; that’s the lowest since August 2020, when the first Bayanihan period expired. In PHP terms, NPLs fell to P411bn, from P414bn in September. Past Due (PD) loans improved to 4.03%, down 1bp MoM, with the nominal value at P487bn down from P489bn the month prior. Restructured Loans (RLs) resumed their downtrend, down 5bps MoM to 2.71%, after a small blip up in September. In PHP terms, RLs fell to P327bn, from P334bn in September. NPL cover inched up to 104% from 103% the previous month.
  • Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) unexpectedly slowed a tad to 10.9% YoY, from 11.2% in September. This was in stark contrast to the UKB (universal and commercial bank) data that showed loan growth accelerating to 13.9% in October from 13.4% in September. Even the MoM data was odd, as the All Banks data showed a MoM decline of 0.4%, while the UKB data was up 0.7% MoM.
  • The improving data – particularly the PD data – is encouraging, as it shows no deterioration in asset quality despite the sharply rising interest rates. Of course, it’s early days, and loan rates may not have repriced yet as of October. We will be watching this data closely over then coming months. The loan data is a bit of a head-scratcher; we will look into it closer, but would not be surprised to see some revisions (either to the All Banks, or to the UKB numbers) next month.

BANKS
Consumer loans jump 11.7% YoY in 3Q22 led by salary loans, credit cards, and mortgages; Asset quality improves across the board
  • Consumer loan growth in the banking sector accelerated to 11.7% YoY in 3Q22, up from the 6.4% YoY pace in 2Q22, and the fastest YoY growth since the pandemic. This is notable, as motor vehicle (MV) loans are still -8%, though the pace of decline is slowing sharply. Growth was led by salary loans (+52%), credit cards (+26%), and mortgages (+9%). Meanwhile, consumer NPLs dropped 92bps QoQ to 7.19% from 8.11% in 2Q22 (peaked at 9.86% in 2Q21). Asset quality improved across the board, with ratios lower across all major segments, and with even nominal PHP NPLs down QoQ for most segments.
  • The growth in higher-margin consumer loans bodes well for bank NIMs, and further supports our positive view on the banking sector as a beneficiary of the reopening / recovery story.
  • Mortgages grew 8.6% YoY, faster than the 8.1% clip in 2Q22. On a QoQ basis, mortgages were up 1.4%. Mortgage NPL ratio eased to 7.92%, down 71bps QoQ from the 8.63% in 2Q22 (peak was 9.85% in 3Q22). In PHP terms, mortgage NPLs fell 12% YoY and 7% QoQ. Despite rising interest rates, anecdotes from banks suggest continued acceleration in mortgage demand heading into 4Q22/2023.
  • Motor Vehicle (MV) loans contracted 8.2% YoY. Notably, though, this was a vast improvement over the -12% YoY in 2Q22 and -16% YoY at end-2021. On a QoQ basis, MV loans were already flat (-0.4%) QoQ in 3Q22. Anecdotally, we should start to see MV loans in positive QoQ territory in 4Q22, and positive YoY by 1Q23. MV NPL ratio improved to 9.31%, own 129bps QoQ from the 10.60% in 2Q22 (peak was 12.7% in 3Q21).
  • Credit card loans surged 25.5% YoY, accelerating from 18.9% in 2Q22. On a QoQ basis, credit card receivables were up 6.1% vs 2Q22. NPLs for credit cards improved to 5.02%, compared to 5.80% in 2Q22. Even on a nominal PHP basis, credit card NPLs fell 8% QoQ. The acceleration is likely supported by continued relaxation of international travel restrictions, which is a huge driver of credit card spend.
  • Salary loans spiked 52.4% YoY and 20.8% QoQ. We suspect this is due in part to the start of operations of the digibanks, for whom salary loans are the main product. While they alone won’t account for the P44bn QoQ increase, it is likely that the incumbent banks are starting to compete in what should be a relatively lucrative segment. NPL ratios here fell to 4.69%, from 5.17% in 2Q22. However, that makes sense given the sharp increase in the denominator. In absolute PHP terms, salary loan NPLs were up c10% QoQ.
  • It is also possible that the surge in credit card and salary loans is due to consumers / households feeling the pinch from inflation. If so, we could see some asset quality issues pop up in the coming quarters.

TELCOS
Implementing rules for SIM Registration Law released
  • The implementing rules and regulation (IRR) for the SIM Registration Act (RA 11934) were released by the NTC
  • The Law provides for the mandatory registration of all SIMs
    • Individuals will have to provide personal details and an ID
    • “Juridical entities” will have to provide business names and authorized signatories
    • Foreign tourists will have to provide passport details and addresses in the country
  • All new SIMs will have to be registered before activation
  • Existing SIMs will have 180 days to register or risk deactivation; deadline can be extended by another 120 days
  • SIMs registered to tourists will be automatically deactivated after 30 days, but this can extended
  • Penalties are imposed for registering false information or selling stolen SIMs
  • Telcos are required to deactivate SIMs used for fraudulent calls/text upon investigation
  • We expect the implementation of this to result in a large decrease in active SIMs / subscribers
    • Many SIMs likely to be deactivated by the deadline
    • Revenues are also likely to be temporarily affected but ARPUs likely to rise (subscriber count decreased more than revenue)
  • Longer term, the practice of throwaway or burner SIMs and thus, “rotational” churn, will be reduced
  • The other key impact will be on micro-finance / fintech firms as SIMs can now be identified with individuals
    • Allowing lending through mobile apps to be accomplished with greater security
PROPERTY
LPC: Metro Manila land prices flat in 2022; 4Q22 office vacancy flat QoQ at 18%
  • Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) estimates that prices of exclusive gated communities in Metro Manila are generally flat this year
    • Prices are generally 10%-30% above 2019 levels
  • Commercial land prices in Makati and Fort Bonifacio are also flat this year
    • They are 15%-25% above 2019 levels
  • Metro Manila condominiums in the primary market generally rose c. 5% in 2022
    • They are generally 5%-10% above 2019 levels
  • However, prices of high-end beach properties performed better, rising 5%-35% in 2022
    • Prices of such properties are about 3X the 2019 levels
  • Meanwhile, LPC also said that Metro Manila 4Q22 office vacancy stayed flat QoQ at 18%
  • Total nationwide net office take-up dropped to 177,000sqm, from 209,000sqm in 3Q22
    • BPO nationwide net office take-up dropped to 127,000sqm, from 146,000sqm in 3Q22 
  • On the positive side, the 2H22 BPO net office take-up (of 273,000sqm) is comparable to pre-COVID levels
    • We estimate that annual pre-COVID BPO net office take-up was 500,000sqm-650,000sqm
  • We forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 22% in 2023E
    • Due mostly to new office completions
    • Also, in September 2022, the government effectively allowed BPO firms to maintain WFH policies indefinitely
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 55% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
    • 41% of Megaworld’s NAV
    • 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV
  • FLI is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY22E P/B, and 6.0X FY23E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 4.7X FY23E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 58% discount to NAV, 0.6X FY22E P/B, and 8.1X FY23E P/E

POWER/UTILITIES
Meralco asks SPPC to pay for cost of TRO
  • Manila Electric Co (Meralco) has issued a notice of claim against San Miguel’s South Premiere Power Corp (SPPC) to cover the additional costs it has been incurring in relation to a 60-day stay order on their Ilijan power supply agreement (PSA)
  • Meralco said it had told SPPC to pay the difference between their contract price and the price at the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM), to which the utility is exposed amid the TRO on their contract
  • Aside from the additional cost of sourcing power from WESM, Meralco said it was also asking SPPC to pay fines, penalties and liquidated damages under the PSA
