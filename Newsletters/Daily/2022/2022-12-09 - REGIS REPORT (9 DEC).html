REGIS REPORT

09 December 2022

The saga of the proposed Maharlika sovereign wealth fund (SWF) continues. Legislators backed down, and have removed the state pension funds SSS and GSIS from the list of funders. The new proposal leaves just the two government banks – DBP and Land Bank – and dividends from the BSP (ie, not the GIR) as sources of funds. Of the original planned P275bn seed funds, more than half (P175bn) was originally meant to come from GSIS and SSS.

But even the DBP and Land Bank portions could be shaky. BSP Governor Felipe Medalla has said that any investment made by these banks need to go through regulatory approval, for risk assessment, etc. And even the appropriation of BSP dividends will mean a reduction in revenues for the National Government.

While the creation of a SWF could be good, it seems like the Congressmen need to go back to the drawing board, and rethink the legal and financial implications of sourcing funding it.

+ + +

Gross International Reserves (GIR) resumed its downtrend in November, though down by a marginal $73mn (0.1%) MoM to $93.95bn. Recall that the GIR increased by $1bn in October thanks to a $2bn new sovereign debt issuance. Year-to-date, the GIR is down by about $15bn, or 13.6% from end-2021 levels, as the BSP has aggressively defended the PHP. With the recent PHP strength / USD weakness, the pressure has come off. We expect the BSP to start buying back USD if the forex rate overshoots – there are already commentaries that the PHP is at or near “expensive” territory.

Despite the drop, the GIR is still extremely healthy, at roughly 7.5 months of imports. A potential drop in commodity import prices could improve the ratio near term.

We maintain that the PHP is likely to stay relatively weak in the near- to medium-term, due to the large Current Account deficit (CAD), which the BSP forecasts at 6% this year, and around 5% for 2023E.

+ + +

We refresh our forecasts for Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), update our TP to P13/sh (-7%) and maintain our Hold rating on the stock. While earnings could see a bounce in 2023E as margins receive much-needed relief from easing input prices, we believe both businesses – APAC BFB and Quorn – are likely to continue facing competitive pressures over the near term. The instant noodle segment, MONDE’s single largest and most profitable category, has been seeing market share declines due to challenger Universal Robina (URC PM) making inroads into the space. Meanwhile, Quorn continues to compete for a share of what now appears to be a shrinking pie. At 23x 2023E PE, we believe the risk-reward at these levels is not favorable given the multiple headwinds.

MACRO
Government debt up 0.9% MoM to P13.64tn in end-October
  • The National Government’s outstanding debt rose by 0.9% MoM (+P124bn) to P13.64tn in end-October
    • This is 16% higher than in end-Dec 2021 (P11.7tn)
    • Equivalent to 62.5% of GDP (Nominal GDP 2022E: P21.8tn)
  • Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% of total debt and was up +0.6% MoM and 15% higher than in end-Dec 2021
  • External debt accounted for 31.4% of total debt and was up +1.6% MoM and +20% higher than in end-Dec 2021
    • In USD terms, external loans stood at US$73.8bn (+2.7% MoM and +5.8% higher than in end-Dec 2021)
  • The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually go down to 51% by 2028

Proposed Philippine sovereign wealth fund will not receive capital from pension funds
  • Representative Stella Quimbo, senior vice chair of the House appropriations panel, said that the proposed Maharlika Wealth Fund will not receive capital from Social Security System (SSS) or Government Service Insurance System (GSIS)
  • The original proposal was to source the Maharlika Wealth Fund’s P275bn from:
    • GSIS – P125bn
    • SSS – P50bn
    • Land Bank of the Philippines – P50bn
    • Development Bank of the Philippines – P25bn
    • National Budget – P25bn
  • Instead, Representative Quimbo said that funds will be sourced from the profits of BSP
