REGIS REPORT

03 November 2022

The 3Q results from two of the names that reported yesterday demonstrate what’s going on in the power sector, which will become increasingly evident for consumers in the next few years.  Aboitiz Power (AP PM) recorded robust numbers, as did Semirara (SCC PM).  The results for the latter were strong not just for the most obvious reason (coal prices) but also for the favorable supply situation in the power markets.

  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (+) - Aboitiz Power’s core income of P9.2bn in 3Q22 (+65%YoY, +48%QoQ) exceeded its 1H22 total of P9.1bn, bouncing back sharply on the back of a growing contribution from new capacity (GNPower Dinginin). 9M22 is now tracking ahead of Regis (P23.6bn) and well above consensus (P19.3bn) estimates for FY22E, even after a weak start to the year. We expect the full operation of GNPD to allow for a much better 2H, while margins of its distribution business catch up as purchased power costs stabilize.
  • Semirara (SCC PM) (+) – Semirara’s 3Q profit (excluding FX gains) remained elevated at P9.38bn (+152% YoY, -7% QoQ), bringing 9M22 profit up 3x to P34.29bn. While coal ASPs mildly disappointed, profits are on track to exceed Regis and street estimates due to lower costs at the coal mining unit and higher power generation sales volumes. The company is generating more cash than we had anticipated, with 9M22 EBITDA of P40.2bn (+155% YoY) already accounting for 82% of our FY22E. Management expects to remain in a massive net cash position by year-end, even after paying a chunky special dividend this month, which should bode well for next year’s dividend announcement.

Union Bank’s (UBP PM) board approved a P20bn (US$345m) equity raising, to be done via a rights and/or private placement. The announcement seems odd, given the bank just completed a P40bn (US$690m) rights issue in May, to fund the acquisition of the Citi retail banking assets (completed in 3Q). While the bank hasn’t released capital ratio data yet for 3Q22, we parent CET-1 to be well above the 11.0% regulatory minimum (probably closer to 14% post-rights and post-Citi closing).

We suspect at least part of the reason is to improve free float, to maybe get the stock into the PSEi Index. Headline float is 34%, more than enough to meet the 20% minimum for index inclusion. However, the 34% includes the 17% stake of the Social Security System (SSS), which the PSE could exclude in its float computations. The rights/placement/top-up will have to happen by end-2022 if the stock is to make it to the PSEi rebalancing in February 2022.

+ + +

Bloomberg reports that Sumitomo Mitsui Bank (SMBC) will take an additional 15% stake in Rizal Commercial Bank (RCB PM), bringing its ownership up to 20%. RCB has been paving the way for this, earlier selling off its remaining land assets to free it from the 40% foreign ownership limit. Other reports said SMBC paid $460mn for the 15% stake. If correct, that would suggest a share price of P74/sh, or about 1.35x P/bk on the trailing 2Q22 book. That would be more than 3x the current market price of P22.30/sh.

Assuming these are new shares, they would help boost RCB’s capital ratios. As of 2Q22, its parent CET-1 ratio was 12.01%.

Barring any unusual events, it is looking very likely that DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) will re-enter the PSEi by early next year.  With its likely entry, one of the stocks of the Alliance Global (AGI PM) group will have to exit.  Check out more of our prognostications below.

DMC likely to enter PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing
  • DMC will likely enter the PSE Index during the February 2023 rebalancing
  • We expect this to lead to the removal of either EMI or MEG
  • If EMI does not increase its public float to 20% (from 16% as of end-September), then it will be removed from the PSEi
  • If EMI increases its public float to 20% by end-December, then MEG will be removed from the PSEi
  • The PSEi rebalancing in February will be based on the 12 months from January 2022 to December 2022
  • Stocks that meet the free-float and liquidity requirements will be ranked by 12M market cap
  • Changes to the constituents of the PSE will occur when
    • 1) a non-index constituent is ranked within the top 25 or
    • 2) a PSEi constituent falls outside the top 35 
  • Based on our estimates, DMC has reached the top 25 (as of 10M22)
  • In addition to DMC, we also expect relatively large inflow to SMC due to the increase in its public float
  • In addition to the PSEi constituent to be removed, we also expect relatively large outflow from ALI due to the reduction in its public float
  • Note: since SSS has a board seat in UBP, we expect the PSE to treat the SSS stake as non-public. Thus, we estimate that UBP will not meet the 20% public ownership requirement (to enter the PSEi)
