REGIS REPORT

21 March 2022

LT Group (LTG PM) reported net income of P20.3bn for FY21, down 4%.  This is below our estimate (P22.6bn) but ahead of the Street’s.  LTG’s 49.6% owned tobacco unit PMFTC did not raise prices in 4Q last year as it usually did in the past.  This is the primary reason for the shortfall.  LTG has since raised prices.

Meanwhile, its bank PNB (PNB PM) booked a P5.4bn profit in 4Q  due to the reversal of loan loss provisions in the amount of P7.6bn.  NPLs supposedly dropped to 4.8% from 6.9%.  We remain a little wary about how PNB books NPLs, provisions and thus earnings. While how it reports these numbers might pass the auditors, its standards appear to fall well below those of the bigger banks.

+++

Semirara (SCC PM) secured a Mineral Production Sharing Agreement (MPSA) with the government covering the limestone resources on the island.  The MPSA is a crucial and necessary step for its planned cement plant, which has been delayed a number of years due to the lack of government approvals.  A cement facility on Semirara island will likely be very competitive, cost-wise, as it has the resources for cement production (coal, limestone) in abundance on-site.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) announced it was selling the Muntinlupa-Cavite Expressway (MCX, previously called the Daang Hari Toll Road) to the Villar Group for P3.8bn.  The transaction price is equivalent to ~P240m per lane-km, which is 2x higher than other at-grade toll roads.  MCX is a small (4km) toll road project that AC won in 2011.  The project cost then was P2bn.

The toll road and thus the transaction are small relative to AC’s size.  However, it is another clear step towards its goal of raising US$1bn (~P52bn) from divestments under its Strategic Agenda. This deal would put AC at just under P30bn.

+++

The Bangko Sentral (BSP), in its monthly report, said the BOP was in a US$157m deficit in Feb.  The more significant item, however, is the revision in its outlook for the current account as it is now expecting a US$16bn deficit (equivalent to 3.8% of GDP).

MACRO
BOP posts US$157mn deficit in February; BSP revises 2022 BOP outlook
  • The country’s overall balance of payments position posted a deficit of US$157mn in February 2022
    • This is lower than the US$2.02bn deficit in Feb 2021 but larger than the US$102mn deficit in January 2022
    • Cited national government foreign currency debt payments
  • Brings YT-Feb BOP to a cumulative deficit of US$259mn, lower than the US$2.77bn deficit in the same period last year
  • The BSP revised its 2022 BOP outlook.  They are now looking at a BOP deficit of US$4.3bn (from a mild surplus in its previous forecast), from a US$1.3bn surplus in 2021
    • Due to a wider trade gap and current account deficit

MACRO
Size of labor force dips in January 2022
  • The country’s labor force participation rate dropped to 60.5% in January from 64% in December
    • In absolute terms, 2.7mn were removed from the workforce MoM due likely to the Omicron surge
  • The number of employed persons dropped by 2.5mn MoM, led by the agriculture sector

POWER
DENR grants MPSA to Semirara’s limestone project
  • The DENR granted the Mine Production Sharing Agreement (MPSA) to Semirara’s (SCC PM) Himalian limestone project last March 10
    • Semirara applied for the MPSA in 2015
    • The said MPSA allows SMPC the exclusive right to immediately undertake mining operations within the contract for a period of 25 years, renewable for like period
  • The MPSA brings the company closer to its plans to expand into cement
    • The company previously planned to build a 2.5mn MT greenfield cement plant in Semirara island at an estimated cost of US$600mn
    • Once the cement plant is built, we believe Semirara will likely have the lowest production cost in the country
    • We estimate the cement plant could add c. P4bn to Semirara’s FY23E EBITDA of P24.9bn
  • However, even with the MPSA, we believe Semirara will need clarity on the renewal of its coal operating contract, which is due to expire in 2027, before it can proceed with its cement expansion
  • We believe this development is unlikely to reduce our 2022 forecast dividend of at least P3/sh (9.6% yield)
  • SCC is trading at 5.6x/9.4x on Regis’ 22F/23F PER
INFRASTRUCTURE
Ayala Corp to sell Muntinlupa-Cavite Expressway Concession to Villar group
  • Ayala Corp will sell MCX Project Company Inc. (MCXPCI) to the Villar group’s privately held company, Prime Asset Ventures Inc., for P3.8bn
    • We estimate the acquisition was valued at P237mn EV per lane-km, higher than previous valuations of brownfield at-grade tollroads (P120mn) and also higher than AC’s bid valuation of P179mn in 2011
  • MCXCPI holds the concession assets, rights, and obligations under the concession agreement for the Muntinlupa-Cavite Expressway (MCX) Project
  • MCX is a 4km toll road which connects Muntinlupa and Cavite; Pre-pandemic, daily traffic volumes were over 30k
  • The Villar group has large tracts of land in Cavite, Muntinlupa, and Las Pinas, which is adjacent to Muninlupa
    • These are held privately as well as through Vista Land (VLL PM)
  • We estimate that c. 50% of VLL’s GAV is comprise of land in the said provinces/cities
  • VLL is trading at 63% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 4.5X FY22E P/E
CONGLOS
LTG 4Q21 rebounds on reversal of loan loss provisions; FY21 profit -4% - behind
  • LTG’s FY21 came in at P20.3bn, -3.7% YoY. This is behind Regis (P22.6bn) but ahead of consensus (P16.8bn). While the bank appears to have outperformed estimates for the year, PNB largely cause the group’s underperformance, netting out the impact of the P34bn reval gain in 2Q. Tobacco also underperformed, but its profits nevertheless grew 4%YoY despite the volume pressure.
  • Tobacco profits dip 3%QoQ on soft volumes, no price hike. Tobacco profits dipped 3%QoQ to P4.167bn in 4Q as volumes declined 3%QoQ to 8.7bn sticks. Unlike last year, there were no price hikes that helped margins in 4Q. The FY profit nevertheless increased 4%, despite volumes coming in 18% lower YoY.

  • PNB books P7.6bn reversal of loan loss provisions. Bank profits jumped to P5.4bn in 4Q, buoyed by a P7.6bn reversal in loan loss provisions. Management said a small portion of this amount had been transferred to “performing status”, while a large chunk remained in NPL but there was an improvement in the expected recovery from the account. Management added that there could be more reversals of NPL when the PAL debt-to-equity conversion is executed in 1H22.
    • Gross NPL ratio slightly improved to 10.07% at the end of 2021, from 10.8% in 9M21
    • NPL coverage was 60.3% for FY21, retreating from 64% in 9M21
    • Total loans grew 2%, led by large corporate (+4%) and consumer (+7%)
    • Deposits were flat at P895bn, dragged by 32% fall in time deposits
    • Trading gains for FY21 amounted to P732mn, implying losses of P270mn in 4Q21
  • Beverage units soft, margin pressure adds to woes. Liquor volumes ended the year down 1% to 23.7mn cases, despite 4Q showing a decent 11% growth QoQ. Profits from both spirits and beverage units contracted QoQ arising from pressure from higher production costs. Tanduay’s GP margin, in particular fell to mid single-digit in the quarter, from low-teens in 9M21.
  • At P9.30/share, LTG trades at <4x FY22 PE and a 70% discount to market NAV.
