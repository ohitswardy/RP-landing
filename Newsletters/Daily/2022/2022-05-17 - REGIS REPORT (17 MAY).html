REGIS REPORT

17 May 2022

Overseas remittances for the month of March amounted to US$2.59bn, just around +3% higher from the previous year.

+++

For today’s results tally, we have Security Bank (SECB PM) and Megaworld (MEG PM) as well as the just-released numbers from GT Capital (GTCAP PM).

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) – in line – 1Q22 NPAT of P2.7bn was up 66% YoY, in line with Regis (FY22E of P10.9bn) but well ahead of consensus (P8.7bn). However, this was due mostly to a sharp drop in provision expense to just P80mn in the quarter. While this can be partly explained by NPLs falling 29bps QoQ to 3.65%, we note that NPL cover fell to 90% from 93% at end-2021. Loans grew 8% YoY, driven by corporates (+11%) while consumer loans were still -4% YoY. The bank will host an earnings call tomorrow Wed, 18 May at 5pm, where we hope to get more particularly on the provisions.
  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (+) – Operating profit jumped 53% YoY as the higher proportion of revenue from lots raised residential margins. Horizontal projects now comprise 35% of reservation sales, from only 9% in 2019. Megaworld’s office occupancy also improved 1ppt QoQ to 91% and management is confident of further improvements later this year.

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – GTCAP 1Q22 core income to common jumped 19% YoY to P3.84bn, trending AHEAD of Regis and street forecasts. We attribute the outperformance to lower provisions at the bank and higher sales for its auto unit. Revenues for Toyota and Federal Land were strong but were offset by higher costs.  Management is holding a briefing at 2:30pm.

Broadband service provider Converge (CNVRG PM) continued to grow nicely with revenue rising 40% to P7.5bn and income up +27% to just under P2bn. While we don’t cover it, we suspect the numbers are likely to have disappointed the market. The impact of rising competition for subscribers as well as slowing net adds industry-wide is unmistakable. More below.

+++

Vista Land’s REIT received approval from the regulator to list. The REIT vehicle will house ten malls and two office buildings. Among the most important things investors have to be aware of is that VLL’s current rental revenue as recognized in its P&L is far above the actual rent it receives. This is because of the revenue recognition policies under PFRS 16 that apply average rents over the life of a long-term rental contracts.

MACRO
OFW remittances rise 3.2% in March
  • Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.2% YoY to US$2.59bn in March
    • In PHP terms, remittances jumped 11% YoY
    • 3-month moving average growth rate at 2.3% in March
    • Land-based remittances jumped 3.7% YoY while sea-based remittances rose 1.3% YoY
  • Brings 1Q22 remittances to US$7.77bn, up 2.3% YoY
    • Tracking behind the BSP’s FY22E target of +4% YoY

BANKS
SECB 1Q21 NPAT P2.7bn beats consensus, as provisions drop 80%
  • In a press release, Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 1Q22 net profit grew 66% to P2.7bn, in line with Regis (FY22E P10.9bn), but well ahead of consensus (P8.7bn). However, the outperformance was due nearly entirely to a plunge in provision expense to just P80mn. This was likely due to a 29bps QoQ drop in NPLs to 3.65%. Loans grew 8% YoY, driven by corporate loans (+11%). The bank will hold a briefing on Wednesday, 18 May at 5pm.
  • Ahead of the briefing, some highlights from the press release:
    • Loans grew 8% YoY, surpassing 2019 levels. Growth was driven by corporate loans (+11%). Consumer loans were still down 4% YoY; despite looser credit underwriting, new loans were unable to overcome maturing loans. In past calls, management expects consumer loan balances to show YoY growth from 2H22. Deposits were up 2% YoY, driven by a 20% surge in CASA, which now accounts for 61% of total deposits.
    • NII was up 5% YoY; management estimates NIMs inched down 2bps YoY, likely driven by loan mix in favor of corporates.
    • Fee incomes continued to jump 22% YoY, now consistently at or above pre-Covid levels. Based on the press release, it looks as though trading/fx gains broke even (small gain or small loss).
    • Opex was up 8% YoY, likely due to catch-up investments in IT spend
    • Provision expense plunged 80% to just P80mn. To be fair, NPL ratio fell 29bps to 3.65% from 3.94% at end-2021. However, the low provision meant that NPL Cover dipped again to 90% from 93% in 4Q. In previous calls, management said they expect NPL Cover to get back above 100% by 2H22.
  • While loan and fee data are encouraging, we are slightly disappointed with the low provision and low NPL Cover. We await further details from the earnings call.

TELCOS
Converge ICT reports P1.97bn (+27%) 1Q profit
  • 1Q22 income was P1.97bn, up +27% YoY and 20% of consensus estimate for FY22E
  • Revenues grew 40% to P7.75bn and EBITDA jumped 49% to P4.56bn.
  • EBITDA margins expanded to 58.8% in 1Q22 vs 55.0% in FY21
  • Net adds came in at 111k for the quarter.  This is the 3rd straight quarter that it’s lower than PLDT’s.  The respective 1Q market shares for wired broadband are 53% for PLDT, 30% for Converge and 17% for Globe.
  • ARPU for 1Q dropped to P1,243.  That figure is 11% down YoY and 5% down QoQ.
    • The company blamed refunds relating to Typhoon Odette
  • Churn for residential customers rose to 1.47% from 0.99%
    • Management said that it relaxed customer onboarding policies and no longer required upfront deposits, resulting in higher churn
  • G&A expenses were up a lot (+54%), with the biggest increases from depreciation (+163% YoY) and promotion (+190%).
    • CNVRG expects to spend up to P200m for the recently acquired professional basketball team
  • The company also recently raised the speed of its entry level plan to 100Mbps (from 50Mbps) applicable for new subscribers

PROPERTY
PSE approves Vista REIT IPO
  • Vista Land will spin off 10 malls and two PEZA-registered offices into a REIT
    • REIT GLA of 256,000sqm
  • 3.33bn secondary shares with an overallotment option of 333.75mn shares
  • Maximum offer price of P2.50/share
    • Maximum gross proceeds of P9.2bn
    • Implies a 2023E dividend yield of 6.4% (based on prospectus)
  • Implies a maximum market cap of P25bn
  • Offer period: May 30 to June 3
  • Listing date: June 15
  • The malls are located in: Las Pinas, Bacoor, General Trias, Imus, Tanza, Antipolo, San Jose Del Monte, San Fernando, and Talisay in Cebu
  • The office buildings are in Taguig and Bacoor
  • As of 3Q21, Vista Land had total debt of P167bn and net debt of P104bn
    • Net debt/equity of 93%
  • Vista’s entire rental portfolio has a GLA of about 1mn sqm
  • Vista’s 9M21 rental revenue jumped 25% YoY to P6.2bn, 20% higher than the 9M19 figure
    • This is a contrast to other mall operators, whose mall revenues are about 60% lower than 2019 levels
    • We estimate that Vista’s actual cash rents are only about 60% of reported rental revenue in 9M21
  • At the maximum offer price, Vista’s NAV could rise by 5%-10%
  • VLL is trading at 67% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, 4.0X FY22E P/E
