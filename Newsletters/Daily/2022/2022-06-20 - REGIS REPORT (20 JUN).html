REGIS REPORT

20 June 2022

We have a new note out refreshing our estimates and view on the telcos.  Our valuation and target prices for both PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) have been lifted -- PLDT to P2.300 (+35%) and Globe to P2.800 (+3%). Our rating on Globe has also been raised to BUY (from Hold).

Between the pandemic re-opening and the specter of inflation, we expect the two operators to continue seeing growth in revenue. Broadband revenue should decelerating expansion while mobile should see more modest but improving growth. Developments point to a diminished threat from the third mobile operator.

The big event is the sale of PLDT’s towers for a whopping P77bn, ~20x EBITDA. That transaction alone added P300/share to PLDT’s value. It also points to the previously hidden value in Globe’s own tower network (potentially over P450 per Globe share at the same valuation). The less “hidden” value for Globe has been Mynt/GCash which has now swung firmly into the black and contributed >6% of 1Q profit.

We reckon both names are good places to take shelter in, amidst the inflation-induced storm of rising rates. Valuation-wise, PLDT’s on 5.6x EBITDA, while Globe’s slightly higher at 6.1x. Note those multiples have yet to strip out the value of their fintech operations (Voyager and Mynt/GCash). Both also offer decent yields of over 4.5%.

In a bit of unfortunate timing for our note, Globe (GLO PM) disclosed that it intends to raise up to P32bn in a rights issue. While we had been predicting this possibility as far back as March, we were also half-expecting a capital raise might not be needed if the company can raise money from a tower sale.

All things considered, however, given the amount to be raised relative to its market cap (~10%), the actual dilution (to EPS and valuation) is unlikely to be big.

At the maximum amount to be raised (P32bn), we estimate Globe’s net gearing will fall to 2.0x, from 3.0x, on our FY22E estimates.

+++

President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr has selected someone from the Bangko Sentral (BSP) to take the crucial position of Commissioner of the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). Lillia Guillermo is the present head of the BSP Technology and Digital Innovation Office. She was apparently backed for the BIR position by incoming Finance Secretary (and current BSP Governor) Benjamin Diokno.

“Red alert” was raised for the Luzon grid as power outages hit yesterday. There was an apparent tripping of transmission lines which led to the shedding of 1,200MW of capacity (that’s less than 8% of headline capacity). That an outage resulted from this supply reduction illustrates the precariousness of the supply-demand balance in the grid.

+++

Further evidence of reopening/recovery. Mobility data is still improving (to 12 June) across all categories. The numbers for Retail/Recreation have leveled off, but are holding. Mobility at Transit Stations is now consistently above pre-Covid levels, eking out mild gains in the last week (the small blip in May was on the 8th, the day before the elections). Most importantly, though, is the Workplace data continues to rise. This is consistent with recent Unemployment data which fell to near 2019 lows at 5.8% in March.

Thus far, there seems to be no evidence (yet) of a slowdown due to inflation, transport costs, etc. Fingers crossed.
