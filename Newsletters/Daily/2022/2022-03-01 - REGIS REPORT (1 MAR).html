REGIS REPORT

01 March 2022

Total reported new Covid-19 cases dropped to 951 yesterday. Cases have already been on a marked downtrend for weeks but this latest number takes it below the threshold of 1,000 – a psychological confidence boost for further re-opening.

In another sign that things are slowly climbing back to “normal”, loan growth for the commercial banks jumped to +8.5% in January. This a 19-month high and is an acceleration from the +4.8% in Dec.

+++

We have FY21 results from two index heavyweights, SM Investments (SM PM)

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – Income for the year reached P38.5bn, +65% higher YoY and above Street expectations. The outperformance was driven by better numbers across all its key segments as Covid-19 cases dropped and mobility improved in 4Q. The retail operations saw a big QoQ bounce in earnings in the last quarter. The group’s “portfolio investments” were responsible for 7% of the year’s earnings with mining firm Atlas (AT PM) and the Neo portfolio of office buildings contributing the most.
  • Meralco (MER PM) (+) – Meralco had net income of P23.5bn (+44%) for FY21, and reported core income of P24.6bn (+13%) – ahead of our expectations and the Street’s, which we suspect is due to tax adjustments. Core operations appear to have otherwise been in line with volume growth of just under 6%. The company is preparing to file its tariff reset application for the next regulatory period (beginning July 2022) and the regulator should also render a decision on its retroactive tariffs for the lapsed period very soon.

More on both below.

+++

AREIT (AREIT PM), the REIT unit of Ayala Land (ALI PM), reported income of P2.3bn for 2021, an increase of +56%. While the likely dividend will rise this year, the yield would still only be ~4.2% based on the latest price of AREIT. We reckon that’s low relative to LT sovereign yields (>5%).

CONGLOS
SM Investments 4Q/FY21 results comment
  • Net income P38.5bn (+65%). SM Investments net income for FY21 amounted to P38.5bn, +65% YoY and above the Street (P34.8bn).  This implies income of P11.3bn for 4Q alone, up ~40% YoY and ~60% QoQ, The eased restrictions and improved personal mobility of the population helped boost 4Q profits.

The earnings split for FY21 was 51% from banking, 25% from property, 17% from retail. The company cited, for the first time, the contribution of “portfolio investments” -- 7% to income for the year. 

  • Retail operations see 4Q bounce.  Income from SM Retail amounted to P9.6bn for FY21 (+133% YoY) boosted by a rebound in 4Q21. Income for 4Q21 alone was 2.5x the previous year.  Revenue across the three main segments all saw sharp increases QoQ with the rebound more pronounced in the more discretionary formats (Department store and Specialty retail). Income from the Specialty store segment was unusually high in 4Q.

  • Food retail same store sales down 6.7% for FY21.  Overall, the same store sales (SSS) for SM Retail were supposedly 1.1% lower YoY.  It was +10.9% for the Department Stores and +7.3% for the Specialty segment. However, we reckon only the SSS number for Food retail segment is actually useful and meaningful since those stores were open for practically the entire period since the pandemic broke out. SSS for Food retail was down 6.7% in FY21 but that figure implies that 4Q SSS would have been down only around 1% -- a significant improvement from 9M21.
  • Update on portfolio investments.  The investments of SM not grouped under banking, property or retail are lumped under “portfolio investments”. These include its investments in mining, shipping/logistics, office property (ex- those under SM Prime), bakeries, dorms and gaming. The company increased its stakes in a number of these investee companies: 2GO (now 53%), Goldilocks (74%) and MyTown (71.3%).  Among these investments, the biggest contributors to profits this past year were Atlas (AT PM), a copper mining firm, and the Neo portfolio of office buildings.
UTILITIES
Meralco FY21 Results Comments
  • FY21 core income P24.6bn.  Meralco reported headline profit of P23.5bn (+44% YoY).  Core income, as reported by the company grew 13% to P24.6bn, which is better than our estimate (P22.4bn) and consensus (P23.2bn). The “outperformance” is largely a consequence of differences in the treatment of tax assets and, to some extent, contributions from generation, particularly Pacific Light. Core distribution operations were otherwise within expectations.
  • Volumes up 6% in FY21, with growth of 4% in early 1Q22. Electricity sales for MER were up 5.7% in FY21 and 5.8% in 4Q alone, which is just a tad below our assumption of 6%.  Sales for Jan were only up around 2% due to the Omicron-fueled surge but Feb numbers were up around 6%.  Management expects volume growth of 5-6% in FY22.
  • Dividends of P10.226/share declared, on track for 4% yield. Meralco declared dividends of P10.226/share today.  With a likely dividend of at least P5/share later this year, this calendar year’s dividend would be over P15/share.  This is higher than we expected and equivalent to 4.2% yield on recent price.  Meralco ended the  year with ~P110bn in cash equivalents (P55bn) and investments, putting net cash at ~P15bn, inclusive of debt at recently acquired GBP.
  • Tariff review update.  Meralco will file its tariff review application for its 5th Regulatory Period (5RP) by March 11 with the regulator expecting a Final Determination by July 2022.  Should there be delays (likely in our opinion), the decision/s will still be retroactively applied to July 2022.

Also important is a decision on Meralco’s tariffs for the “lapsed period” (i.e. from July 2015 to present), which would affect  the level of refunds it may give to customers.  Management expects a decision on the lapsed period to be released before one on 5RP can proceed. Meralco is seeking a decision from the ERC that not result in a retroactive application of tariffs which could result in a reversal of over P50bn in cumulative provisions.

  • Work continues for Atimonan but now aiming for 2026.  Meralco is still continuing site  development work on the 1200MW Atimonan project but is now hoping to secure a contract under Meralco’s power supply auction (“CSP”) process to supply the DU’s needs by 2026. Meralco is also targeting the development of 1,500MW of RE projects in the next 5 to 7 years.

PROPERTY
AREIT 2021 net profit rises 56%
  • AREIT’s 2021 net profit rose 56% to P2.3bn (excluding fair value gains)
    • 4Q21 earnings climbed 56% to P755mn
  • The rental portfolio grew 221% YoY to 550,000sqm
    • 98% occupancy
  • FY21 DPS rose 34% to P1.77/share, 12% higher than the forecast in the REIT plan (due to acquisitions)
    • 4Q21 DPS increased 7% to P.47/share
  • Management expects revenues to rise 35%-45% this year (before acquisitions)
    • This could lead to DPS of c. P1.96/share (before acquisitions)
    • The 2022 consensus DPS forecast is P2.05/share – implying dividend yield of 4.2%
  • AREIT plans to expand its portfolio by about 100,000sqm annually from 2022 to 2024
  • Management said the REIT’s AUM would increase by 13% (to P60bn) in 2022
PROPERTY
Ayala Land willing to redeem voting preferred shares
  • Ayala Land will redeem its voting preferred shares (at the option of the shareholder)
  • Price – P0.10/share (par) plus accrued dividends
  • Offer period – 24 March 2022 to 24 May 2022
  • Beginning 29 June 2022, the voting preferred shares are convertible to 1 ALI common share at a price which shall be the higher of
    • Average closing price of the preceding 30 trading days less the par value of P0.10/share
    • Closing price preceding conversion date less the par value of P0.10/share
  • Ayala Corp holds 93% of the voting preferred shares
    • We do not expect Ayala Corp to redeem or exercise the option to convert to common shares
  • There are 13bn voting preferred shares (vs 14.8bn ALI common shares)
  • The current dividend rate is 4.74786%
    • Beginning 29 June 2022, the new dividend rate will be 90% of the 10-year PHP BVAL (currently 5.3758%)
  • ALI is trading at 30% discount to NAV, 2.4X FY22E P/B, and 30.7X FY22E P/E
