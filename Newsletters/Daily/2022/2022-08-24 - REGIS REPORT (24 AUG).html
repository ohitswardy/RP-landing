REGIS REPORT

24 August 2022

The latest personal mobility numbers from Google show that people are still going out despite the rise in Covid-19 infections. This should be a good leading indicator economic activity in 3Q and should bode well for businesses.

+++

San Miguel Corp (SMC PM) is threatening actions that would cause even higher power prices for Luzon if the regulator doesn’t act favorably on its recent application. The application submitted by SMC and Meralco was to allow it to recover the higher fuel costs arising from surge in coal prices and the Malampaya derating.

As a background, SMC subsidiary San Miguel Global Power, through its own subsidiaries, signed contracts to supply power at fixed prices to Meralco (MER PM), but is now seeking to terminate its contracts unless the ERC agrees to allow it to recover losses (some but not all, according to the company) due to fuel price hikes. SMC is using a “change in circumstances” clause, because of the unprecedented surge in energy prices, to justify its move. Terminating the contracts (for ~1000MW) would mean Meralco would need to source power elsewhere (including the spot market), leading to even higher prices. SMC has been suffering huge losses in recent months from the hike in energy costs.

Frankly, this is a messy situation that also highlights the precariousness of Luzon power supply. San Miguel’s position in the market is large enough and the supply is tight enough for it to be able to cause such a market disruption.  However, allowing San Miguel to effectively amend its commitment to sell at a fixed price would, in our view, set a dangerous precedent.  It would create a moral hazard that may encourage companies (buyers and sellers) to renege on agreements (“change in circumstances” or “force majeure” notwithstanding) if they don’t turn out favorably.

+++

The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) is allowing up to 30% of employees at BPO companies to work from home (WFH) until March next year. That 30% cap was supposed to lapse next month and revert to 10%. Allowing more employees to work from home would reduce demand for office space. We expect this to have a negative, albeit small, impact on the recovery of demand for office space. 

A new consortium has secured the right to develop the Sangley Airport. The Sangley Airport if developed properly, could pose as an alternative to the existing NAIA and also to the Bulacan Airport currently under development.

Businessman Alejandro Tengco has been appointed as chairman of the state gaming agency PAGCOR, which runs and/or regulates the casinos in the country. Among the immediate issues he faces are the possible transfer of the casino license/s of the Udenna group to the Razon group, as well the treatment of online gaming activities.

MACRO
Mobility continues to improve into mid-August
  • Been a while since we sent an update. But yes, mobility continues to improve even into August. Nearly all indicators are now above pre-Covid levels, with Metro Manila workplace data just a smidge under (-1%). It’s important to note that mobility was rising despite the spike in fuel prices and jeepney fares. We expect the numbers to rise again given the start of face-to-face schooling this week (though maybe dampened by the typhoon, pun intended).
  • Seems no evidence (yet) of a slowdown in economic activity. That said, the low base effect will start to fade, particularly heading into 4Q22. (Though there were still lockdowns in August/September 2021).

RETAIL
Key takeaways from PGOLD 2Q22 briefing
  • Overall, PGOLD’s 2Q22 results are tracking in line with expectations, but management’s guidance implies a more subdued outlook over the next few quarters.
  • PGOLD currently trades at 11x 2022E PE on our 2022E NPAT forecast of P9.5bn. Management guidance implies 2022 NPAT of P8.5-9bn.

Puregold

  • Gross margin contracted both QoQ and YoY to 15.8% in 2Q22, which was partly attributed to seasonality as Puregold resumed its Tindahan ni Aling Puring (TNAP) Convention. The three-day promotional event allows TNAP members to purchase merchandise at higher discounted prices.

  • The TNAP Convention typically accounts for ~15% of the quarters’ sales and generates lower GPMs. Pre-pandemic, margins typically dip in 2Q because of this event. Margins would then recover going into the seasonally strong 4Q.

  • Management, however, is guiding for GPMs to settle at 16-16.3% for the year (Regis FY22E: 16%). This implies that 2H22 margins will be largely flat vs 2Q22 levels. Management is looking to adopt a more affordable pricing strategy as it opens more stores in the provincial areas, where consumers are more price sensitive. We believe local competition also plays a factor, especially in areas where larger retail chains have minimal presence.

S&R

  • S&R’s margins also dipped in 2Q22, led by a 70bps YoY contraction in GPM to 23.8%. This was attributed to the unfavorable forex, higher logistics cost, and price inflation. While S&R raised prices to pass on some of the higher cost, it was a deliberate strategy to absorb some of the margin to keep prices competitive.

  • S&R’s President Anthony Sy mentioned that margins will likely continue to taper as S&R adopts a more affordable pricing strategy (similar to Puregold). During the call, Mr Sy explicitly said they are “willing to sacrifice some margin for growth”.
  • We believe this could be a response to rising competitive pressure from Landers, which is accelerating store expansion. Landers opened its first store in 2016 and did not really expand much more than its first 5 stores in 2018. Based on its website, Landers will end the year with 10 stores, with 4 new stores opening in 2022 alone.

  • S&R management is guiding for FY22 margins to settle at 22-22.5% (Regis FY22E: 23.7%), which is at the lower end of the historical range of 22-24%. While the guidance implies further margin compression in 2H22, Mr Sy also mentioned that 2H margins are trending better than 2Q despite the cost pressures. We believe S&R’s margins are likely to exceed management guidance, unless cost and competitive pressures intensify.
POWER
SMC warns of power price surge starting October after issuing notices of PSA termination to Meralco
  • SMC Global Power Holdings warned on Tuesday that electricity prices in Metro Manila and nearby provinces could go up as much as 30% in October if the energy regulator fails to act on its petition for a temporary rate hike
  • The petition was jointly filed with the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) by San Miguel Corp and Meralco covering the companies’ two power supply agreements
  • If the ERC approved the petition for temporary relief (filed in May), SMC Global Power said electricity prices in Luzon would rise by 30 centavos per kWh over a period of six months
  • Without it and with a termination of the PSA, SMC Global Power said energy prices would rise by P0.80-1.30/kWh for the next three to four months  as “it will need to find alternative sources of energy.. including the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market”
  • San Miguel units SPPC and SMEC, administrators of the Ilijan and Sual plants, had already issued notices of termination of their PSAs to Meralco
    • They blamed the “change in circumstance” including global surge of fuel prices
    • They claim the termination is effective starting October 4 if no relief is given
  • SMC President and CEO Ramon Ang is urging the ERC for a “fair and objective” assessment of the joint petition
PROPERTY
PEZA allows 30% WFH for BPOs until March 2023
  • PEZA allowed 30% of BPO employees to work from home until March 2023
  • Previously, the 30% cap would lapse on September 12, after which only 10% of employees would be allowed to work from home
  • We expect only minor impact from this extension
  • For the most part, we believe BPO firms have decided if they will expand office footprint or if they will no longer avail of PEZA incentives
  • Most BPO firms are expanding via Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have a requirement regarding WFH policies
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) comprise:
    • 55% of FLI’s NAV
    • 41% of MEG’s NAV
    • 30% of RLC’s NAV
  • FLI is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY22E P/B, and 7.3X FY22E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 61% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY22E P/B, and 5.7X FY22E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 49% discount to NAV, 0.8X FY22E P/B, and 13.2X FY22E P/E
INFRASTRUCTURE
Consortium to sign contract for Sangley airport in Sept
  • The SPIA Development Consortium is set to be awarded the contract to develop the Sangley Point International Airport (SPIA) on September 14
    • According to Cavite Governor Juanito Victor “Jonvic” C. Remulla, Jr.
  • SPIA consortium is composed of Philippine, European and South Korean companies:
    • Virata-led Cavitex Holdings; Yuchengo-led House of Investments, Inc.; MacroAsia Corp.
    • Samsung C&T Corp., a South Korean construction and engineering company
    • Munich Airport International, a wholly owned subsidiary of Flughafen München GmbH
    • Arup, a UK-based design company
  • Cavite government did not receive any competing bids during the Swiss challenge
  • To recall, MacroAsia and its partner China Communications Construction Co. Ltd. had negotiated with Cavite for the project in 2020 but the province canceled the project in Jan 2021 due to lack of requirements
  • Project is estimated to cost US$11bn and will expand the airport’s annual capacity to 25mn passengers initially, with plans to create a second runway that could expand capacity further to 75mn
