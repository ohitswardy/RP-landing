REGIS REPORT

23 November 2022

The Bureau of Treasury’s bond issuance yesterday produced a yield of 8.012% for the 20-year bond.  While this may be up 20bp from the secondary market benchmark (7.81%), it is lower than the resulting yield at last week’s auction (8.168%).

On the shorter end of the yield curve, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) signaled that it is unlikely to implement a “jumbo” rate hike at the last Monetary Board policy meeting for the year.  The MB will meet on Dec 15, hours after the US Fed’s FOMC meeting (Dec 14 in the US), allowing it to take its cue from the US central bank. With the Fed likely to raise rates by less than the last one (75bp), the size of the BSP’s next rate move is also expected to be smaller.

+++

Another firm is up for listing before the end of the year.  Premiere Island Power REIT, owned by the Villar family behind Vista Land (VLL PM) and All Home (HOME PM), will begin its public offer next week on Nov 28.  The firm appears to be structured similarly to the Citicore REIT (CREIT PM), which derives its rental income from solar farms but employs a REIT structure in order to reduce (eliminate) its income tax payments.

Only secondary shares will be sold in the IPO of Premiere Island Power REIT, which means proceeds will accrue to the selling shareholders and not the listing firm.

BSP Governor rules out jumbo rate hikes
  • BSP Governor Felipe Medalla ruled out the possibility of another jumbo rate hike, as he expects the US Federal Reserve to slow the pace of tightening in December
    • If the Fed will hike by 50 bps, Mr. Medalla said the BSP cannot afford to keep rates unchanged
  • The Monetary Board will be meeting on December 15; Fed meeting on Dec 14
  • The US central bank raised rates by 375 bps since March while the BSP has raised rates by 300bps

MACRO
Government raises P93bn from sale of T-bonds
  • The Bureau of Treasury partially awarded P27.597bn worth of original issue of 20-year T-bonds on offer during the last auction for November
    • Effectively bringing total borrowings for T-bonds to P93.3bn, about 67% of the P140bn program for the month
  • In yesterday’s auction, the 20-year T-bond fetched an average rate of 8.012%, 20bps above the 7.81% BVAL reference rate
    • Yield was lower than the 8.168% last week
    • Yields went from a low of 7.73% and a high of 8.249%
    • The government targeted to issue P35bn but only awarded P27.6bn
    • The auction was oversubscribed by 2.01x
PROPERTY
PSE approves P3.2bn IPO of Villar-backed Premiere REIT
  • The Philippine Stock Exchange approved Premiere Island Power REIT Corp’s listing application
    • The offer period of its P3.2bn IPO is set on Nov 28-Dec 5
    • Pricing is set on Nov 23 (stock code: PREIT)
  • Premiere REIT appears to be a firm leasing land to a solar farm, effectively a solar farm disguised as leasing company
  • IPO involves the sale of up to 1.4bn common shares priced at P2/sh plus an option to sell another 210mn shares
    • Shares are entirely secondary
    • Proceeds would be used by sponsors to acquire and develop real estate that would be leased to affiliate PAVI Green Renewable Energy Inc. for its various projects
    • Initial portfolio will consist of land and power plant assets in Cebu and Siquijor, which have a total combined capacity of 21.2MW
  • The company is projecting a “base case” dividend payout of 6.9% in 2022 and 7.2% in 2023
  • This will be the 9th IPO for the year and the 7th publicly-listed firm of the Villar group

Manila Water (MWC PM) Rate Rebasing Results

Conclusion: Manila Water’s approved Business Plan for the 6th rebasing period will be incrementally positive for the value of the East Zone concession, given higher future capex spend.  We estimate MWC shares are worth P20/sh, after the regulator approved MWC’s committed expenditure proposal of P1.15tn for the remaining 25-year concession life. The substantially higher expenditure program more than offset the impact of (a) a lower Opening Cash Position (OCP) of P28bn and (b) higher billed volume targets. 

  • OCP at P28bn, lower than forecast.  The regulator approved an OCP of P28bn, lower than our forecast of P31bn but higher than the approved OCP of P13bn in the 2018 rebasing.
  • Committed expenditures substantially higher. Committed expenditures for 2023-2047 were raised substantially to P1.15tn due to a , much higher than our assumption of P656bn. 
  • Billed volume target higher.  Billed volumes are expected to reach c. 3,000 MLD by the end of the concession in 2047 from 1,340 MLD in 2021, implying a CAGR of +4%.  We only forecasted a demand CAGR of +1%.
