REGIS REPORT

07 December 2022

Headline inflation and core inflation rates accelerated to 8.0% YoY and 6.5% YoY, respectively, in November. While it appears that inflation has not peaked, the BSP’s moves on this month’s policy meeting could likely be influenced by the recent strength of the PHP:USD exchange rate, opening up the possibility of a smaller rate hike than the US Fed. 

+++

San Miguel (SMC PM) has issued a termination notice, effective today, covering its 670MW power supply agreement (PSA) with Meralco (MER PM).  SMC is invoking its right to terminate the PSA ostensibly because of a change in circumstances (e.g. higher fuel costs, lack of fuel from Malampaya, etc), thus breaking its commitment to supply power at a fixed price.  The regulator, however, has ruled that SMC cannot unilaterally do so, leading SMC to file a suit with the Court of Appeals, which issued a restraining order against the regulator.

Assuming the termination does take effect, the cost of buying from the spot market at high/er prices will fall to Meralco and thus, ultimately, to consumers.

In the meantime, as a stop gap measure, Meralco is supposedly considering the offer to operate the idle power capacity of Ilijan with the use of natural gas from Malampaya.  However, given the meager supply from Malampaya, the gas will have to come from the fuel allocated to First Gen’s (FGEN PM) plants. Under this scenario, FGEN’s plants will thus have to run on much more expensive liquid fuel (which they are technically able to do) and the higher costs will be passed on to Meralco customers.  Such a “solution” will allow more capacity to be available (as Ilijan can now operate) but the higher costs will be borne by the consumers, and not SMC.

+++

Newswires reported that Jollibee Foods (JFC PM) is in talks to sell a minority stake (10-15%) in Highlands Coffee, which is supposedly being valued at USD800mn. Highlands Coffee (through SuperFoods Group) is currently 60%-owned by JFC and contributes ~3% of JFC’s recurring profit. Assuming the reported valuation is accurate, this would imply a valuation for Highlands Coffee equivalent to around 10% of JFC’s current market cap.

+++

Aaron (aaron.salvador@regis.ph) just released a new report on ACEN Corp (ACEN PM), upgrading the stock to a BUY. The stock is down more than 40% from the peak, and the risks that he’s warned about have largely been discounted. There is still some volatility to be expected in the near term, but we think market expectations on ACEN and management have reset to more realistic levels.

We factored in contributions from new RE plants that are coming online over the medium term and forecast earnings to jump to P8.6bn next year (52%) and P12.6bn in FY24 (47%). Valuations are by no means cheap (at 40x PE and 18x EV/EBITDA at target), but are no longer daunting relative to growth (49% EPS CAGR up to FY24E).

Beyond the numbers, we think that the reversal in cost advantage between solar and fossil fuels is the bigger determinant in the success of RE projects and, as a result, ACEN’s investment proposition. Over the long term, ACEN’s share price will likely be a reflection of RE’s improving viability over fossil fuels.

MACRO
Inflation quickens to 8.0% in November
  • Headline inflation rose by 8.0% YoY, 0.9% MoM in November
    • Consensus: 7.8% YoY
    • BSP: 7.4%-8.2% YoY
  • Prices of food (i.e. rice, fish, fruits, vegetables, and sugar) picked up due to the severe tropical storm Paeng
  • The full-month effect of the transport fare hike, as well as higher LPG prices, contributed to the higher inflation print
  • Fuel prices (diesel and gasoline) also rose 0.8% MoM
    • in PHP terms, regional prices of gasoline/diesel dropped 2%/8% MoM but impact on pump prices may have lagged
  • Core inflation (ex- food and fuel) accelerated to 6.5% in November from 5.9% in October
  • YT-November inflation average at 5.6%
  • Assuming MoM trends slow to 0.5% in December, inflation for December 2022 will likely be slightly higher at +8.1%
  • Jefferies expects the Fed to raise policy rates by another 50bps this month (Dec 14)
    • Regis expects the same rate hike in the next BSP policy meeting (Dec 15)

MACRO
Full award of T-bond as rates drop
  • The Bureau of Treasury made a full award of P35bn worth of re-issued 25-year T-bonds, with a remaining life of 11 years and 11 months
    • T-bonds fetched an average rate of 7.189%, lower than the 8.168% yield when it was reissued in November and lower than the original coupon rate of 9.25% set in Nov 2009
    • Yield is slightly higher than BVAL reference rate of 7.179%
    • Cited expectations of inflation peaking and BSP’s slowing pace of rate hikes
  • Auction attracted total tenders of P79.4bn, more than 3x oversubscribed
  • National Treasurer Rosalia de Leon said the government may prefer longer tenors in the borrowing program in the future if the market pricing is good
AIRLINES
Cebu Air to expand NEO fleet in 2023; announces leadership changes
  • Cebu Air (CEB PM) will take delivery of 11 NEO aircraft in 2023 to replace 10-11 CEOs/ATR exits
    • Deliveries: 3 A320NEOs, 4 A321NEOs, 4 A330NEOs
    • NEOs will account for 56% of CEB’s fleet in 2023 from 43% in 2022
    • Fleet guidance flattish, with projected 73 aircraft in 2023
    • As of end-September 2022, CEB’s fleet size stood at 77
  • Committed capex of P53bn in 2023, up from P33bn in 2022
    • Net capex (i.e. less proceeds from leasebacks) will be c. P20bn, up from P4bn in 2022
    • We estimate net capex of P32bn in 2023 from P4bn in 2022
    • CEB’s net debt to equity stood at 5.02x in end-September 2022, assuming conversion of CBs
  • CEB has restored 92% of its pre-pandemic capacity to date, with > 100% of domestic capacity reinstated but only 68% of international capacity
    • It expects to break even in 4Q22 and to reach profitability in 2023
  • CEB also announced succession planning:
    • Lance Gokongwei will be Chairman (previously President & CEO)
    • Michael Szucs will be CEO (previously Chief Executive Adviser)
    • Alexander Lao will be President (from Chief Commercial Officer)
    • Appointments will become effective by Jan 1, 2023
POWER
SMC plants stop supplying Meralco
  • San Miguel Corp is walking away from its contract to supply electricity to Manila Electric Co (Meralco) under its Ilijan power supply agreement, citing mounting losses caused by the surge in fuel costs that it could not recover through power rates
  • SMC Global Power Holdings Corp notified Meralco that it would no longer supply Meralco the 670MW under the Ilijan PSA effective December 7, 2022
  • ERC meanwhile has not received any notice from Meralco yet
  • At the same time, there are reports that Meralco is attempting to woo First Gen to the divert the use of its natural gas allocation from Malampaya to the 1,200MW Ilijan gas-fired power plant of San Miguel Coro
  • Under the offer, Meralco will be the one to procure the fuel for the gas-fired power plant
  • Meralco first vice president and head of regulatory management Jose Valles said that Energy Sec. Lotilla wants the 1,200MW to remain operational in the grid “so the only way to do it is to source fuel for it”
  • The mechanics of the proposal would have to be agreed upon first by Meralco, SMC and First Gen, and approved by the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC)
