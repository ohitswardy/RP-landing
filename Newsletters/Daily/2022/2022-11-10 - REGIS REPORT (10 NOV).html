REGIS REPORT

10 November 2022

We have another day of 3Q results predominantly marked by a strong recovery from the pandemic lockdowns of the past two and a half years.  The strong consumer demand is spilling over to other sectors, including retail property (malls) and even to vehicular traffic, despite exceptionally high pump prices.

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – Reported profit for 9M22 reached P43bn, which is up +50% YoY. While the retroactive inclusion of Philippine Geothermal (PGPC) contributed to the relative outperformance (vs consensus expectations), all three main operating segments delivered better-than-expected results.  We note SM Retail’s revenue and income for 3Q have now both exceeded the comparable pre-pandemic levels (2019).  More below.
  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (=) – 3Q22 NPAT jumped 47% YoY due to operating leverage from the continued improvement in SSSG (+15%). The Philippines led much of the top line growth with SSSG of +49% in 3Q22 alone. On a QoQ basis, margins contracted partly because of seasonality, but also because of continued cost pressure. We understand that the company did another round of price increases during the quarter, but the magnitude of the adjustments has yet to be disclosed. The company’s results briefing will be on 16 Nov.
  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) (-) – 3Q performance was hit by a product recall in the EU, which indirectly affected its core Philippine instant noodle business. Consolidated sales were flat YoY as domestic instant noodle sales contracted 24% YoY. While sales have recovered in early-4Q, margin pressure from input cost inflation could persist as the company will need until 2Q23 to work through high-cost inventory (mainly wheat and palm oil). Meanwhile, Quorn announced a restructuring of its organization as it aims to rationalize cost amidst challenging industry dynamics.

  • Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM) (=) – Operating profit jumped 53% YoY in 3Q22, driven mostly by operating leverage as nearly all formats (ex-DIY and CVS) have recovered to pre-pandemic SSS levels. On the Supermarkets side, SSSG was +9% during the quarter, due mainly to higher foot traffic as baskets continued to be pressured by downtrading. The strong top line momentum is reportedly carrying over into early-4Q, fueled by the start of the Holiday season.

  • Metro Pacific (MPI PM) (+) – 9M22 core profit rose 26% YoY to P12.0bn, after 3Q22 profit jumped 27% YoY on the back of strong earnings from Meralco and its tollroad unit, which more than compensated for the disappointment in the rail unit. 9M22 profit already matched our FY22E and is also trending well ahead of street estimates. Given strong underlying operations and easing regulatory issues, the company is optimistic it can revert to pre-pandemic profit levels as early as next year.

CONGLOS
SM reports 9M profit of P43bn
  • Reported income of P43bn for 9M (+50%).  SM Investments reported net income of P42.9bn for 9M22, up +50% from P28.6bn the prior year.  Note that both periods were restated to retroactively include the merger with Philippine Geothermal (PGPC).  Even without it, however, earnings were strong, with all three main segments showing robust results.
  • Banking and property deliver.  Both BDO and SM Prime chipped in good numbers for 3Q/9M. BDO had net income of P40bn (+23%) in 9M while SM Prime reported 9M earnings of P22bn, +41%. Income from the former was lifted by trading gains and the rise in NIMs.  The performance for the latter was boosted by recovering consumer activity at its shopping malls as well as recovering demand for residential property.
  • Retail revenue and income exceeds pre-pandemic levels.  3Q marked the first quarter where both revenue and income for SM Retail exceeded the comparable period in 2019 (i.e. before the pandemic).  Revenue and income for 3Q was unusually high for what is a seasonally soft period, with both up QoQ.  We believe the uplift was partly due to pent-up demand or, as some would call it, revenge spending. It was the Department Stores (“SM Stores”) which showed the biggest bounce for the period. In fact, that segment delivered its highest quarterly profit we have on record.

In terms of SSSG, the non-Food segments (SM Stores and Specialty Stores) delivered very high numbers because of a depressed base of comparison in 2021.  More noteworthy, in our view, is the SSSG for the Food segment (i.e. groceries) which came in at over 9% in 3Q (+7.1% in 9M22).

  • Portfolio investments delivered 13% of earnings.  Portfolio investments, which used to not merit any mention in the press releases and presentations before this year, accounted for 13% of earnings in 9M22.  Of that figure, 8% was from PGPC which was acquired this year. Most of the rest was from mining firm Atlas (AT PM), which is now making money.

CONSUMER
JFC 9M22 NPAT to common surges to P6.4bn – in line with Regis, ahead of consensus
  • JFC’s NPAT to common (ex-perps and prefs) surged over 3x YoY to P6.4bn, tracking broadly in line with Regis FY22E but ahead of consensus. While revenues (+38% YoY to P150bn) are trending ahead, margins are slightly lagging our FY22 expectations.

  • 3Q22 NPAT jumped 47% YoY to P1.9bn, driven mostly by operating leverage. Operating profit surged 3x to P3bn as revenues grew 49% YoY. Consolidated SSSG during the quarter was 31%, led by the Philippines (+49%). Except for China and Superfoods (Vietnam), we estimate SSS levels for all geographies are well above pre-pandemic levels.

  • On a QoQ basis, consolidated operating profit was flattish due to gross margin contraction. GPM contracted 80bps YoY, driven mostly by the Philippines, which saw GPM contract >200bps QoQ. While this is partly due to seasonality, we believe there is also continued pressure from input cost inflation. JFC had raised prices cumulatively by +7-8% on average as of 1H22, and management previously signaled further pricing action in the succeeding quarters. The magnitude of the price adjustments in 3Q have yet to be disclosed. We expect to get more detail during the briefing on 16 Nov.

  • JFC currently trades at 2023E multiples of 24x PE and 12x EV/EBITDA.
PROPERTY
Megaworld 3Q22 net profit falls 20% YoY, likely due to forex losses
  • Megaworld’s 3Q22 net profit fell 20% YoY (11% QoQ) to P2.5bn
    • 9M22 net income rose 3% to P8.4bn, in line with REGIS forecast
  • Since operating revenue appears flattish, the drop in earnings was likely due to forex losses
    • We estimate that the company has about US$600mn in unhedged US$ debt
  • Residential revenue rose 3% QoQ (down 4% YoY)
    • Unlike developers that saw large cancellations in 2Q22, Megaworld did not see a bump in 3Q22 residential revenue
  • Office vacancy improved to 8% in 3Q22, from 9% in 2Q22
  • We estimate that mall revenue fell 2% QoQ, implying that the company has not yet fully removed rental concessions
  • Megaworld will hold a briefing at 2PM on November 14
  • MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY23E P/B, and 4.3X FY23E P/E
