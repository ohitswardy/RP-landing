REGIS REPORT

09 March 2022

Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) and DMCI (DMC PM) held their respective earnings briefings yesterday.

Bloomberry provided more detail on its 4Q results: on some accounting adjustments and, more importantly, its latest operating figures. A non-cash P820mn impairment charge was booked relating to its Jeju property, which muted the operational outperformance. Locally, things appear to be headed higher with capacity limits lifted for the first time in 2 years – foot traffic is back to 12k/day (60% of pre-Covid) and hotel occupancy is over 20%. Management appears to have shifted its focus back to its physical casinos (vs its online casino product last year), hoping to get a bigger share of the returning players as the economy reopens.

Meanwhile, DMCI’s 4Q21 core profit reached a high of P5bn, after posting record profits at its coal mining/power unit Semirara, and improvements in other investments. Cumulative FY21 profit reached P17.1bn, up 162% YoY. Prospects for 2022 look better given the surge in commodity prices. The shares are also inexpensive in our view, trading at an 18% discount to market NAV and offering a dividend yield of at least 10%.

+++

The government is examining measures to blunt the impact of the war in Ukraine. The proposals include increasing fuel subsidies, suspending the biofuels law and boosting food imports. All these are supposed to help restrain inflation in the near term.  It is looking increasingly likely that inflation will be breaching the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target (2% to 4%) in the next few months as skyrocketing commodity prices feed through to consumer prices.

An increase in fares for public utility vehicles (e.g. “jeepneys”) is already looking imminent with pump prices where they are.

+++

Robinsons Land (RLC PM) is injecting more assets into its REIT, RL Commercial (RCR PM), through a share-for-asset swap. This may lead to RLC selling some of its shares in RCR in order to maintain its minimum public float of 33%.

+++

Asset quality deteriorated slightly in January 2022, compared to end-2021, likely due to the sharp jump in Covid-19 cases – and the resulting tighter mobility restrictions – January. The ratios were also affected by the seasonal drop in loans, which were down 1.9% MoM in January. NPL ratio blipped up 15bps MoM to 4.27%, as gross NPLs increased slightly (+1.6%) MoM. Past Due (PD) ratio also edged up to 4..84%, up 18bps MoM, with the Peso value rising a similar 1.6% MoM. Restructured loans (RLs) were flat MoM in nominal terms at P356bn, but the drop in loans saw RL ratio tick up 7bps MoM to 3.20%. NPL cover was flat 87%.

Loan growth accelerated to 4.7% YoY from 4.2% in Dec, marking the 10th consecutive month of improving YoY growth. (The figure for the total banking industry is much lower than the previously reported 8.5% YoY growth for the commercial banking segment. This was due to the merger between BPI and its thrift bank subsidiary Family Savings Bank. The BSP does not do pro-forma historical data for these types of mergers.)

The investment case for the sector as our top reopening play in 2022 is based largely on a) recovering loan demand, and b) normalizing provision expense as the economy recovers from the Covid-19 pandemic. However, we are mindful of the sharp increases in global commodity prices, and will be watchful of how these might affect economic activity, inflation, the PHP, etc.

MACRO
GIR level at US$107.98bn in February
  • The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) edged slightly higher in end-February to US$107.98bn
    • Up from US$107.69bn in January and US$105.2bn in Feb 2021
    • Rise largely due to the increase in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings after the rise in gold prices
  • Reserves are equivalent to 10.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income
  • The BSP is projecting the GIR to rise to US$112bn by year-end

MACRO
Gov’t explores ways to cushion impact of Ukraine-Russia war
  • Economic managers approved the implementation of several measures to cushion the impact of higher energy and food prices to the economy
  • They have requested President Duterte to issue executive orders to implement the following:
    • Double fuel subsidy to the public transport sector to P5bn (US$96mn)
    • Suspend biofuels law
    • Boost coal supply and reduce prices by removing the most favored nation tariff rate of 7% under the ASEAN India Free Trade Agreement until December
    • Boosting rice imports if local production falters
    • Extending lower tariffs on rice and pork imports
    • Boosting imports of fish, given a supply gap of 200k MT
    • Increasing buffer stock of petroleum to 45 days from 30 days and liquefied petroleum gas to 15 days from 7 days
    • Allowing foreign ownership in micro-grid, solar, wind, and tidal energy
  • Some of these proposals will require certain changes in existing laws and a special session in Congress should the situation escalate
MACRO
Pressure to raise jeepney fares grows
  • The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) is facing pressure to restore the minimum jeepney fare of P10 vs. P9 currently
    • The LTFRB decided to “temporarily” reduce the minimum jeepney fare to P9 as diesel pump prices fell to P38 per liter in December 2018
    • The current price of diesel is at P63-63 per liter
  • There is also a proposal to raise the minimum fare to P14-15 but the LTFRB has deferred public hearings on this to March 22
  • Transport fares account for c. 4.5% of the CPI basket
CONGLOS
DMCI achieves record profits in 4Q21; Better 2022 prospects
  • 4Q21 core profit up 162% YoY to P5.03bn.  DMCI’s headline profit for FY21 stood at P18.4bn.  Excluding one-offs and FX adjustments, recurring profit jumped 166% YoY to P17.1bn, in line with street estimates. Core profit for 4Q21 alone surged YoY/QoQ to a record of P5.03bn, boosted by (a) higher thermal coal and power prices at Semirara (link to results comment), (b) higher margins and non-operating income at its property unit, (c) cost reductions at Maynilad, and (d) higher power volumes for its off-grid plants. These more than offset (1) the poor performance of the construction unit and (2) higher costs at its nickel mining unit. 
  • Property: Guiding +30% increase in FY22 reservation sales. Even though revenue bookings dipped YoY, 4Q21 profit for DMCI Homes jumped to P1.2bn on (a) margin improvements and (b) higher income from sales cancellations. Prospects for 2022 were unexciting, with management expecting revenues and EBITDA margins to be flat/slightly lower amid the decline in reservation sales and rising input costs.

Reservation sales remained weak in 4Q, declining by 26% YoY but better QoQ.  Management is targeting sales to improve by 30% this year as it plans to launch 8 projects from only 1-2 the previous two years. It has seen sales improve sequentially so far in 2022 as mobility restrictions were further eased. 

  • Construction unit hit by un-recovered claims in 4Q.  The construction incurred a loss in 4Q after it conservatively adjusted revenues downwards for claims that remained uncollected.  Management also gave a very somber outlook for 2022, as it expects construction activity to slow down markedly this year from both public and private sectors. Its order book is now even lower at P49n from P59bn the previous year.  The company is participating in 24 bids worth P128bn, of which biddings for ODA-funded rail projects are likely to push through in 2Q despite the election ban.
  • Higher input costs cut nickel profits in 4Q; Some mining permits secured.  4Q profits from its nickel unit dropped slightly YoY, after mining development and shipping costs surged, offsetting the 20% YoY increase in sales volumes. Even though LME nickel prices rose 24% YoY in 4Q, its ASPs were down slightly (-2%) as the company mostly sold low-grade nickel from its depleted mine.

The good news is that the company secured tree cutting permits for its mine in Zambales and is also starting exploration activities for its potentially new mines in Palawan. Management is guiding sales volumes of 1.5mn MT this year from 1.9mn MT last year but noted that there is potential for an increase should they secure more mining permits.  Even though ASPs are better so far this year at US$45/MT (from US$42/MT last year), the company is however managing expectations on the surge in nickel prices. Current conditions have forced traders to hold off buying at these elevated prices and management noted further that freight costs have doubled.

  • Outlook and valuations.  We believe DMCI’s 2022 profit will be much better than last year, considering elevated thermal coal prices.  Assuming our revised forecasts for Semirara, we estimate DMC’s 2022 earnings could rise to P21bn, equivalent to a PER of 5.6x, lower than its 5-year average PER of 10x.

Even though the stock has outperformed the PCOMP by 22% YTD, we believe it is inexpensive, trading at an 18% discount to its market NAV of P11/sh, wider than its historical discount of 11%.  Its dividend yield of at least 10% is also attractive. Buy.
