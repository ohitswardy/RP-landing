REGIS REPORT

07 November 2022

CPI inflation accelerated to 7.7% in Oct, surprising consensus expectations. The rise was driven by food prices, which jumped by 1.5% MoM, as vegetable prices soared. 

While this print is well above Street expectations, it is unlikely to figure much in the near-term rate decision of the Bangko Sentral (BSP) next week. The BSP leadership has pretty much indicated that plans to follow the Fed’s move, which was to raise rates by +75bp.

We note, however, that core inflation was 5.9% for the month, an even sharper acceleration from the 5.0% in Sept. Beyond immediate policy decisions, rising core inflation today could elevate inflation expectations and lead to a feedback loop affecting the longer-term CPI trajectory.

MACRO
Inflation quickens to near 14-year high in October at 7.7% on rising food prices, transport fare hikes
  • Headline inflation accelerated to 7.7% in October from +6.9% in September
    • This is the fastest pace since December 2008
    • Disappointed relative to consensus: +7.1%; Came in at the high-end of the BSP’s target range: 7.1%-7.9%
  • On a MoM basis, headline CPI jumped +0.9%, fastest sequential increase since June 2022
    • Food rose +1.5% MoM, driven by vegetables (+12%), corn (+3%), and sugar (+4%); Note that there was a big typhoon (“Karding”) in late-Sep
    • Transport services rose by 2% MoM due to nationwide transport fare hike
    • These items more than offset 3% MoM decline in fuel-related category of “operation of transport equipment” and the mild deceleration in electricity prices
  • Core inflation rate accelerated to +5.9% in October, vs. a revised +5% in September
  • YT-October average inflation rate now at 5.4%
    • To reach Regis’ FY22E ave. inflation rate forecast of +5.8%, CPI for Nov/Dec should rise by +0.9% MoM, which translates to an average of +8.1% YoY
    • To reach the BSP’s FY22E target of +5.7%, CPI for Nov/Dec should rise by 0.5% MoM, which translates to an average of +7.6% YoY

MACRO
Trade deficit shrinks MoM to US$4.8bn in September; Exports rebound +12% MoM
  • The country’s trade deficit narrowed to US$4.8bn in September from the peak of US$6.0bn in August
    • Based on preliminary data from the Philippine Statistics Authority
    • The trade deficit grew at a much slower pace of +27% YoY during the month
    • Due to a strong rebound in exports (+12% MoM, +7% YoY); Imports also grew at a slower pace of +14% YoY and were 4% lower MoM
  • Import growth decelerated due to slower growth in fuel imports and lower raw material imports
    • Consumer goods imports accelerated to +29% while capital goods imports grew at a steady pace of +11%
  • Exports rebounded after two consecutive months of YoY declines due to faster growth in electronic exports
    • By country, exports to HK jumped +16% YoY (vs. YTD: +1.3%)
    • This offset sustained softness in exports to China (-14% YoY in Sep vs -7% YTD) and USA (+2.5% YoY in Sep vs +3.7% YTD)
  • YT-September trade deficit now at US$46.65bn, +63% YoY

BANKS
MBT 9M22 NPAT P24.3bn well ahead; but asset quality concerns as NPLs blip up 20bps QoQ
  • Metrobank (MBT PM) reported NPAT of P24.3bn, up 45% and tracking well ahead of FY forecasts of Regis (P27.2bn) and the Street (P29.6bn). Loan growth accelerated to 13% (net), from 10% in 2Q22, and NIMs jumped 22bps QoQ. The beat, though, was largely due to flat Opex, and lower provision expenses. However, we have some concerns over a 20bps QoQ jump in NPL ratio to 2.11% (from 1.91% in 2Q22), and a drop in NPL coverage to 172% (from 196% in 2Q).
  • The bank will host a call on Monday at 4:30pm. Ahead of that, though, here are some highlights.
    • Loan growth (net) accelerated to +13.3% YoY from 10.2% in 2Q22. The bank disclosed that gross loans were up 12%, driven by corporates (+15%) and credit card receivables (+22%). Deposits were up 10.7% YoY, though dipped slightly -0.6% QoQ. CASA ratio also slipped a bit to 71.2%.
    • NIMs jumped 22bps QoQ to 3.44%, as loan yields surged 30bps QoQ while deposit costs increased 18bps QoQ. Much of the NIM gains also came from the Treasury book, with average yields on non-loan assets rising nearly 40bps QoQ and 70bps YoY, as the bank likely gapped out their fixed income portfolio.
    • Fee incomes were up 15% YoY in 9M, settling well above pre-Covid levels. The bank though, booked P965mn worth of trading/fx losses in 3Q22.
    • Opex was flat YoY, with both manpower and occupancy expenses down YoY. This was by far the biggest source of the outperformance, which saw PPOP jump 49%, and already accounting for 80% of our FY22E. Revenues were broadly in line.
    • Provision expense dropped 43% in 9M, and 61% YoY in 3Q22 alone.
    • Asset quality was a disappointment, with NPL ratio rising 20bps QoQ to 2.11% (from 1.91% in 2Q). This, together with the drop in provision expense saw NPL cover drop sharply to 172% (from 196% in 2Q).
  • As with other banks, the loan growth and NIM trajectory are extremely encouraging, and bode well for 4Q22 and 2023E forecasts. We are wary though over the asset quality deterioration.
