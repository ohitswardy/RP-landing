REGIS REPORT

03 January 2022

The country ushered in the New Year with another big jump in Covid-19 cases. The daily case count rose to 4,600, the highest in two months and roughly 20x what it was only two weeks prior.

The spike appears to be fueled by the Omicron variant, which has driven case counts to record levels globally and brought about resurgence of Covid-19 cases in places where it had appeared to be tamed (e.g. India).

The rise in cases was alarming enough to prompt the government to raise the Covid-19 Alert Level for Metro Manila to Level Three beginning today. This declaration would cut the allowable capacity for many activities to 30%, from 50% under Level Two. This development will be a setback for the re-opening story. How temporary this setback will be will be dependent on how Omicron and other future variants can be subdued.

+++

Loan with commercial banks rose 4% in November, a further acceleration from the +3.5% pace in October. Month-on-month, the absolute level of loans is 0.8% higher, with loans to the Trade segment rising fastest. Meanwhile, auto loans continue to contract, dipping 1.9% MoM.

+++

Ayala Corp’s (AC PM) investment in Myanmar’s Yoma group, disclosed amendments to its purchase of a stake in the latter following delays to its completion. The deal will be completed via the conversion of US$46.4m worth of loans (+ accrued interest) into shares of Yoma Strategic (YSH) at US$0.45/share following certain conditions.

+++

With the completion of the criteria/data in Dec, the likelihood of Monde (MONDE PM) and Emperador (EMP PM) being included in the PSEi has gone up. Barring any changes to the rules, MONDE and EMP should become part of the main PSE benchmark in February, at the expense of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) and Robinsons Retail (RRHI PM).

If EMP does indeed get included next month, it would mark its return only six months after its deletion from the PSEi. The frequency of changes should prompt one to ask if it’s time to reevaluate how the PSEi is constituted.

MACRO
NCR under Alert Level 3 from Jan 3 to Jan 15
  • The Inter-Agency Task Force for the Management of Emerging Infectious Diseases (IATF-EID) decided to shift to a more stringent alert level status (Level 3) from Jan 3-15 due to rising Covid cases in Metro Manila
    • Under Level 3, establishments or activities shall be allowed to operate or be undertaken at a maximum of 30% indoor venue capacity for fully vaccinated individuals only and 50% outdoor venue capacity
    • Those below 18 years of age can go out for essential activities only; Individual outdoor exercises allowed for all ages regardless of comorbidities or vaccination status
    • Train capacity to remain at 70% but the government will undertake random antigen tests
    • Intrazonal and interzonal movement are still allowed, however, reasonable restrictions may be imposed by the LGUs
  • Daily case counts in Metro Manila (7-dma) surged to 960 from <100 a week prior
    • Nationwide case counts (7-dma) surged to 2061 from c.300 a week prior
  • Nationwide positivity rate up to 11% (7-dma) from 1% a week prior; Latest positivity rate at 21%

BANKS
Bank lending accelerates to 4.0% YoY in November
  • Loans for universal/commercial banks (UKBs) grew 4.0% YoY in November, faster than the 3.5% pace in October
  • On a month-on-month basis, bank loans were up 0.8% sequentially over October
  • Sectors that accelerated most include Manufacturing, Trade, and Real Estate. Most notably, the Trade related loans turned positive (+1.5% YoY) compared to the -0.7% in October. Trade also saw the biggest MoM gain (+2%).
  • Consumer loans (ex-mortgages) were still down 7.1% YoY, though slightly better than the 7.4% contraction in October. Credit cards were up 2.6% YoY. However, auto loans were still down 17.5% YoY, and continued to contract on a monthly basis (-1.9% vs October).

CONGLOS
AC amends terms for investment in Myanmar group Yoma
  • Ayala Corporation will convert US$46m in loans to Yoma Strategic (YSH) to shares at a fixed US$0.45/share upon fulfillment of certain conditions
  • AC previously announced plans to acquire a 20% stake in Singapore-based Yoma Strategic Holdings Inc (YSH) and a 20% stake in First Myanmar Investment Public Co Ltd. (FMI)
    • The deal was not fully completed because certain conditions precedent were not fulfilled
  • Total investment in these firms amount to $237.5mn
    • $155mn in YSH, of which $108.6mn was completed on December 2, 2019
      • Balance will be completed once approved by the Singapore Exchange
    • Investment in FMI of $82.5m through a convertible loan disbursed on Jan. 23, 2020
  • YSH and FMI will serve as Ayala’s main platform for strategic investments in Myanmar

  • Meanwhile, we estimate the rebalancing will result in outflows for all the existing PSEi constituents, particularly for RRHI (US$25mn) and BLOOM (US$18mn)
