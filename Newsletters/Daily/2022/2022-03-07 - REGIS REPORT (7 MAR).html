REGIS REPORT

07 March 2022

The reported inflation figure for Feb came in at 3.0%, which was, frankly, a surprise – falling below what the Street was anticipating. The Feb number was flat vs. January.

We expect Feb to be the bottom for CPI inflation, with numbers heading up beginning next month. Not only are commodity prices already soaring, the benefit of the high base of food prices in 2021 (due to disease outbreaks then) should wear off as well.

+++

We have a few more results from the 4Q/FY21 reporting season today. Universal Robina’s (URC PM) came in a little short, while Security Bank’s (SECB PM) was in line with the Street. Aboitiz Power (AP PM) also reported this morning, though details are still scant ahead of its earnings call on Wed.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (-) – We estimate recurring profit for FY21 was P10bn (-7% YoY), behind expectations. Top line trends for the branded consumer food (BCF) segments saw a sequential improvement in 4Q21 (+5 % YoY / +7% QoQ) as key markets (PH / VN / TH) eased mobility restrictions. However, higher input cost pressured margins across the board, dragging 4Q EBIT -15% YoY. The cost environment remains challenged, with prices of key inputs (wheat/flour, palm oil, packaging) surging as much as 60% YTD, giving limited visibility on near term margins.

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (=) – SECB’s core business showed strong positive trends in 2021/4Q21. NPAT of P6.9bn (-7% YoY) was in line with consensus. Loan growth crept back into positive territory (+2.2% YoY) in 4Q21, and NIMs widened another 5bps QoQ in 4Q2021. Fee incomes were strong, up 25% YoY for FY21, already beating pre-Covid levels. Asset quality improved, with NPL ratio down 21bps QoQ to 3.94%. However, with NPL cover still below 100% (though improving to 93% in 4Q, vs 91% in 3Q), the bank may still need to rebuild loan loss reserves in the near term. SECB will hold their earnings call on Wednesday, 9 March, at 5pm, but before that we have a little more detail below.
  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (=) – AP’s 4Q core income dipped 5.4%QoQ to P5.4bn, with the increase in generation energy sales (+14%QoQ) offset by higher steam costs and lower water inflows. Electricity sales for its distribution utilities on the other hand, dipped 4%QoQ, presumably impacted by the December typhoon which cut off power lines in Visayas. The FY21 core income of P21.1bn is nevertheless in line with our FY forecast.

MACRO
Inflation steady in Feb as food prices ease
  • Headline inflation was steady at 3% in February, below street forecasts (3.2%)
    • Due to deceleration in food inflation to 1.2%, driven by slower inflation rates for meat and fish
    • Non-food inflation accelerated due largely to rising oil prices
  • The government will use available resources to provide targeted subsidies to affected sectors and to tame inflation in the next few months, given rising commodity prices amid the Ukraine-Russia conflict
    • It will continue efforts to increase food supply by helping farmers improve productivity and importing, when necessary, to fill supply gaps
  • BSP Governor Diokno said 2022 inflation rate could average 4.4%-4.7% in worst case
    • The BSP’s base case inflation forecast is 2-4%
    • Impact of the war in PHP:USD exchange rate is muted
    • BSP is prepared to deploy liquidity tools if needed

BANKS
SECB FY21 NPAT P6.9bn; loans +2.2% YoY, NPLs
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT of P6.9bn for FY21, down 7% YoY and in line with consensus estimates. The headline figure belies improving trends across the bank’s core business. Loans were up 2.2% YoY (+4% QoQ), NIMs are on the uptrend, fee incomes back above 2019 levels, and asset quality is improving. The bottomline drag came from the high base of trading gains in 2020, and the one-off tax expense in 1Q21. NPAT for 4Q21 alone was P2.1bn, up 171% YoY.
  • Some highlights:
    • Loans grew 2.2% YoY (4.2% QoQ), posting its first positive YoY print since 2Q20. Growth was driven by the corporate/middle-market segment (+5% YoY), while the consumer segment was still -9% YoY, though improving from the -12% clip in 3Q21. Consumer loans were 23% of total loans, down from 25% in 2020, and from 29% in 2019. We expect the bank to make a renewed push into the consumer segment in coming quarters. Deposit were up 19% YoY, with CASA now accounting for 61% of total deposits.
    • NIMs inched up another 5bps QoQ, thanks largely to strong deposit growth, which kept deposit costs near all-time lows of 0.53%.
    • Fee incomes were up 25% YoY in 2021 to P4.5bn, already surpassing the P4.1bn posted in 2019. Other non-interest income surged 55% YoY driven in part by profits from asset sales, including foreclosed assets.
    • Trading/fx gains were down 91%, due to the very high base in 2020. There is some concern over MTM impact on its large AFS portfolio as interest rates rise in 2022E.
    • Provision expense fell 80% in FY21, and 78% in 4Q21. The drop was attributable to improving NPLs, which fell 21bps QoQ to 3.94% (from 4.15% in 3Q). NPL cover also improved slightly to 93% from 91% in 3Q21. Management previously stated that they expected NPL cover to return above 100% in 1H22.
    • Tax expense was bloated by a P1.2bn write-down of Deferred Tax Assets in 1Q21 as part of the CREATE law, which lowered overall corporate tax rates.
  • As mentioned, the trends in the bank’s core business seem on the path to recovery. The bank will hold an earnings call on Wednesday, 9 March, at 5pm Manila/HK/SG time.

POWER
AP’s 4Q core income down 5.4%QoQ to P5.3bn; FY21 core income of P21.1bn in line with Regis, ahead of consensus
  • Aboitiz Power’s core income came in at P5.3bn (-5.4%QoQ, -12%YoY), with the company attributing the decline to higher steam costs and lower water inflows for the hydros
  • For FY21, AP’s core income was P21.1bn (+68%YoY), in line with Regis FY21E (P21.1bn) but ahead of consensus (P18.7bn)
  • Generation and RES EBITDA jumped to P43.4bn, +15%YoY, implying Gen/RES EBITDA dipped 6%QoQ to P11.1bn in 4Q21
    • Energy sold grew 14%to 26,031GWh for FY21
    • Implied 4Q was 7,589GWh (+14%QoQ)
  • The distribution segment’s EBITDA grew 5% to P7.5bn
    • Energy sales increased  4% for the FY
    • Implies 4Q energy sales dipped 4%QoQ
