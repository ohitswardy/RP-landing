REGIS REPORT

02 May 2022

The three names that reported results on Friday, Universal Robina (URC PM), Metrobank (MBT PM) and Cemex PH (CHP PM), all saw bottom line figures beating expectations.

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (+) – The company reported a strong set of results, with a solid rebound (+22%) in revenue lifting 1Q22 earnings to P3.5bn, up +16% YoY, and above expectations. The core branded business saw nearly all categories reporting >20% growth, further boosted by the recent purchase of Malaysian firm Munchy’s. Input cost pressure remains a major issue but it’s now well-flagged and the impact was tempered by price adjustments.  We adjust our estimates upwards slightly and raise our TP to P128/share (+4%).  Click the link for more.

  • Metrobank (MBT PM) (+) - 1Q22 NPAT came in at P8.0bn, well ahead of Regis (P27.2bn) and consensus (P27.6bn) full-year estimates. The beat came from much lower provision expenses than we expected, as NPL ratio dipped to 2.16% (was 2.20% at end-2021) and NPL cover edged up to 179% (from 175%). The bank also booked surprisingly strong trading/fx gains. Notably, MBT’s loan book grew 5.2% YoY, the first positive YoY print since the start of the pandemic in 1Q20. The bank also built up its Treasury book, funded by a sharp increase in deposits/bills. Some more detail below and even more after the earnings call today at 4pm.

 

  • Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) (+) – Cemex PH’s 1Q22 profit outperformed, jumping 27% YoY to P261mn, with EBITDA up 2% YoY to P1.0bn. However, revenues barely grew (+1%), as volumes dropped 6% YoY. CHP managed to raise ASPs by +7% YoY, thanks to a similar increase in the landed cost of imports (+6%).  We’d caution against annualizing 1Q22 due to (a) seasonal factors and (b) sustained weakness in demand, given likely soft government spending this election year.   Further detail below.

+++

Ayala Corp (AC PM) declared a property dividend covering 1.86bn of the ACEN (ACEN PM) shares it owns.  AC will distribute the ACEN shares to its shareholders at a ratio of three (3) ACEN shares for each AC share held.

The exercise will, to use the cliché, hit two birds with one stone.  First, it will raise ACEN’s public float to above 20%, allowing it to meet the minimum requirement to stay in the PSEi benchmark (PCOMP).  Second, it will provide value to AC shareholders who’ve seen the share price’s discount to NAV widen considerably.  At last prices, this incremental value would be worth around 3%.

+++

The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) will implement an 8% tariff increase beginning May 14 for the ports in Manila, including ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT).  This adjustment represents the balance of the 10% it previously approved and should lift revenues for MICT (and thus ICTSI) further.  MICT accounts for 28% of ICTSI’s revenue.

BANKS
MBT 1Q22 NPAT P8.0bn, well ahead on lower provisions; loans +5%, first +ve since 1Q20
  • Metrobank (MBT PM) reported NPAT of P8.0bn for 1Q22, up just 3% YoY but tracking well ahead of REGIS (P27.2bn) and the Street (P27.6bn). The beat was driven largely by much lower provisions than we were expecting, as NPLs and cover continue to improve. Loans were up 5% YoY, the first positive YoY growth print since 1Q2020. NIMs were slightly lower QoQ though, as the bank built up its treasury/placement book, funding it through TDs and borrowings. MBT also booked surprisingly strong trading/fx gains and “miscellaneous” income.
  • Some highlights:
    • Loans grew 5.2% YoY, the bank’s first positive print since 1Q2020, just before the pandemic. Growth was driven by corporate loans (+10%) and credit cards (+8%).
    • NIMs dipped 4bps QoQ, despite higher loans yields and flat deposit costs. This was due to a surge in time deposits (+28% QoQ) and bills payable (+40% QoQ), for an incremental P160bn QoQ increase. The additional liquidity went largely into placements with the BSP and other banks. We suspect the bank will either be paying down loans, or will be gapping out its treasury book soon as yields have risen considerably at longer tenors. LDR dropped below 70% in 1Q22.
    • Fee incomes were up 8% YoY to P3.6bn, normalizing well above pre-Covid levels. The 19% YoY jump in non-interest income was driven by trading/fx gains, as well as a 39% surge in “miscellaneous” income.
    • Opex was up just 1% YoY with compensation expenses actually down 1.6% YoY.
    • Provision expense fell 18% YoY to P2bn, as NPL ratio stood at 2.16% (was 2.2% at end-2021) and NPL cover at 179% (vs 175%).
  • Overall a pretty strong set of results. The bank will hold an earnings call today, 02 May at 4:00pm Manila/SG time.

 

 

CEMENT
Cemex Philippines’ (CHP PM) 1Q22 profit jumps 27% YoY, EBITDA up 2%
  • CHP reported 1Q21 profit of P261mn, up 27% YoY and tracking ahead of consensus (P931mn) and Regis forecasts
    • EBITDA stood at P1.05bn, up 2% YoY and was in line with consensus but above Regis forecasts
    • We attribute the beat to lower-than-expected power & distribution costs, non-cash, and financial expenses, which we believe are not sustainable
  • Revenues grew 1% (in line), after ASPs jumped +7% YoY (+3% QoQ) to offset input cost inflation
    • Based on data from the Bureau of Customs, we estimate that the landed cost of imports rose to US$67/MT in 1Q22, up 6% YoY and flat QoQ
  • Volumes however dropped 6% YoY due to lingering effects of Covid and typhoon Odette
    • Management noted that volumes of importers grew +10-12% YoY during the quarter, suggesting CHP lost share
    • The company is guiding volumes to grow by +7% YoY this year, with most of the growth pushed back to 2H22 on low base effects
  • Cash expenses were flat YoY and up 3% QoQ as input costs were contained, as we estimate power and distribution costs per MT were lower by 9% YoY and 18% YoY, respectively
    • Management noted that it has secured long-term contracts for power and made “arrangements” for fuel (thermal coal and diesel) requirements for the year, suggesting that EBITDA margins could be maintained
  • Non-cash expenses dropped 14% YoY due to timing of interest capitalization for its new clinker line
  • CHP is trading at 4.3x FY22E (consensus) EV/EBITDA, lower than historical average (7x)

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp to pay out part of ACEN stake as property dividend
  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) declared a property dividend covering part of its stake in ACEN (ACEN PM)
    • Property dividend will involve 1.86bn shares of ACEN, equivalent to 4.7% of ACEN’s share capital
  • Shareholders of AC will receive three (3) shares of ACEN for each share of AC held
  • The exercise is to achieve the aim of increasing ACEN’s float as well as improve the shareholder value for AC
    • The property dividend will raise the public float of ACEN to at 21%, allowing it meet the minimum float required to be a component of the PSEi (PCOMP)
    • It will also give AC shareholders a dividend equivalent to 3% yield, based on the last prices of AC and ACEN
  • AC’s stake in ACEN will go down to 57.8% from 62.5%
INFRASTRUCTURE
PPA to implement remaining 8% tariff hike in Manila ports on May 14
  • The Philippine Ports Authority approved the implementation of the 8% balance of the approved 10% increase in cargo handling charges at the Manila South Harbor and MICT
  • New cargo handling tariffs will be implemented on May 14
    • This is slightly earlier than our expectations (July)
  • We estimate MICT accounts for 28% of consolidated revenues and EBITDA
  • ICTSI is trading at 9x FY22E EV/EBITDA, a discount to peers and historical average (11x)

 

TRANSPORTATION
LTFRB to open over 7k ride-share slots
  • The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) has opened up the application process for 7,870 transport network vehicle services (TNVS) slots
    • Cited the need to expand public transportation capacity
  • Some 7,000 slots are up for grabs in the National Capital Region (NCR), 500 units in Region V (Bicol Region), 220 units in Region III (Central Luzon), and 150 units in Region VI (Western Visayas)
  • The number of driver-partners has "reduced to almost half of pre-pandemic levels" due to the previous imposition of hard lockdowns, forcing many driver-partners to look for other income streams
    • There are currently 25k units of TNVS today vs. an estimated 65k pre-pandemic
  • This development is positive for Toyota Motor Phils (TMP), a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital and accounts for c. 20% of the latter’s profit/NAV
    • GTCAP is trading at a 19% discount to its market-based NAV of P626/sh
