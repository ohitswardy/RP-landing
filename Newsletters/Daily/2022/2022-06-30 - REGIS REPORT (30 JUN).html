REGIS REPORT

30 June 2022

Ferdinand Marcos Jr, will take his oath of office and formally assume the Presidency at noon today.

Marcos, widely known by his nickname “Bongbong” or “BBM”, won the Presidency with a clear, unambiguous mandate. His share of the vote (59%) made him the first person to achieve an electoral majority since the 1980s. Marcos takes over for a six-year term from Rodrigo Duterte, whose own daughter also takes office as Vice President today. While his announced Cabinet line-up is yet to be complete, most of the key senior positions in the economic team have been named. And, so far, they appear to be credible, with enough gravitas in the public domain.

Among the notable seats that have yet to be filled are those of the Secretaries of Foreign Affairs, Energy and Health. We highlight those positions because of the importance of the country’s foreign policy tilt, the looming energy crisis and the still-unresolved issues of the Covid-19 pandemic.

+++

According to estimates by property consultancy Leechiu Property Consultants, there was net positive take-up of 85,000sqm of office property in 2Q22. While office take-up appears to have improved from 1Q22 (+20,000sqm), it is still far below pre-COVID levels. At the current pace of take-up and with the continued prevalence of work-from-home policies, office vacancy is unlikely to see any meaningful improvements in the near term and is, in fact, likely to worsen from the current 19%.

+++

Bloomberg reported that Globe Telecom (GLO PM) is in “advanced talks” to sell part of its telecom tower network. Among the parties Globe is reportedly negotiating with are consortia that count the Aboitiz Group (AEV PM) and, separately, Meralco (MER PM), most likely through subsidiary MIESCOR, as participants. Private equity firm KKR is supposedly also one of the potential bidders.

A sale involving half of Globe’s 12,000 towers on similar terms and at the same valuation as PLDT’s (TEL PM) would be very value-accretive for Globe. We estimate the value enhancement could be worth over P450/share (>15% of our TP and 20% of current price) on an after-tax basis. Such a deal would also significantly improve Globe’s debt ratios even without a capital raise.

+++

The transport regulator announced that the minimum jeepney fares will be raised to P11 (~US$0.20) per ride nationwide. The regulator previously raised it to P10 (from P9) for Metro Manila a few weeks ago but this order will cover the entirety of the Philippines. Pump prices for diesel have roughly doubled from where they were at the start of the year. With transport fares and minimum wages going up, the second order effects of soaring commodity prices on inflation are spreading.

Meanwhile, incoming Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla says he is not ruling out rate hikes of more than 25bp each in subsequent Monetary Board meetings. There will be four more such meetings for the rest of the year. YTD, the BSP has raised policy rates by 50bp, taking them to 2.50%, still below the 4.0% level immediately before the pandemic. Given how inflation prints are likely to turn out in the coming months, the markets are starting to expect more than just a further +50bp for the rest of the year.
