REGIS REPORT

12 October 2022

Lots of bits and bobs this morning. But they all seem consistent with our narrative – strong domestic demand, but poor external accounts.

Domestic demand remains robust, as seen in surprising vehicle sales in September (+64% YoY and +17% MoM) to 35,282 units, now well above pre-Covid levels. In fact, we haven’t seen monthly car sales this high since late 2017, when there was a rush to by cars ahead of the higher taxes from the TRAIN law. To be fair, a similar thing may be happening now, as Congress announced in August that they want to revise TRAIN to impose higher excises taxes on pick-up trucks. Regardless, it’s a good number, and we’ll take it.

However, this is overshadowed by a ballooning trade deficit, which hit another record high of $6bn in August. While the strong demand for (expensive) imports is easy to blame, we note that exports have also been weak, posting the second monthly YoY decline. This takes the 8M2022 total trade deficit to $41.8bn, up 69% YoY and nearly matching the full-year deficit of $42.2bn in 2022. Needless to say, the pressure on the PHP to depreciate against the USD should remain.

+ + +

Gaming was in the spotlight yesterday after Senator Miguel Zubiri said that the Philippines was on China’s “blacklist” due to the continued presence of POGOs (online gaming catering to Mainland Chinese players). Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) took a hit -7.4% yesterday on the news. However, this is not new – in fact, we wrote about this back in August 2020, as China was cracking down, not just on POGOs, but also on junket operators. It came to a head with high profile arrests in Macao in 2021. We remain bullish on BLOOM, largely on the recovery in the higher-margin domestic mass market. Our forecasts assume that VIP volumes only return to about half their peak levels.

Speaking of POGOs, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) estimates that office vacancy in Metro Manila stayed high at 18% in 3Q22. The good news is that BPO office demand is back to pre-Covid levels with net take-up of 146,000sqm nation-wide. The bad news is that there’s about 2.5mn sqm of vacant office space in Metro Manila alone, with more supply coming online in the next 12-18 months. Also, not all the BPO take-up was in Metro Manila. Including non-BPOs, nation-wide office take up of 209,000 sqm is a high in the Covid-era. But that still suggests office rents will likely stay soft for a few years to come.

MACRO
Trade deficit at US$6.0bn in August
  • The country’s trade deficit ballooned to US$6.003bn in August
    • This is wider by 81% YoY and broadly unchanged from July 2022 (US$5.989bn)
  • Import growth accelerated to 26% YoY in August from 22% YoY in July 2022
    • This is despite fuel imports decelerating to 76% YoY in August from 87% YoY in July 2022
    • Consumer goods imports soared by 46% YoY while capital goods import grew at a faster rate of 15% YoY
  • Exports dropped for the second straight month
  • YT-August trade deficit is 69% YoY wider at US$41.8bn, almost matching full-year 2021’s trade deficit of US$42bn

PROPERTY
LPC: Metro Manila 3Q22 office vacancy stays flat QoQ at 18%
  • Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) said that Metro Manila 3Q22 office vacancy stayed flat QoQ at 18%
  • On a positive note, nationwide BPO net office take-up rose to 146,000sqm in 3Q22
    • This is comparable to the pre-COVID annual take-up of 500,000sqm to 650,000sqm
  • However, nationwide net office take-up of 209,000sqm is still far from the 2018-2019 annual pace of 1.6mn to 1.8mn sqm
  • We forecast Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 19% in 2022E, 22% in 2023E
    • Due to new office completions
    • In September 2022, the government effectively allowed BPO firms to maintain WFH policies indefinitely
  • Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for:
    • 55% of Filinvest Land’s NAV
    • 41% of Megaworld’s NAV
    • 30% of Robinsons Land’s NAV
  • FLI is trading at 71% discount to NAV, 0.2X FY22E P/B, and 5.1X FY22E P/E
  • MEG is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 4.5X FY22E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 58% discount to NAV, 0.6X FY22E P/B, and 10.9X FY22E P/E

CONSUMER
Vehicle sales jump strongly in September
  • Vehicle sales jumped 17% MoM, 64% YoY to 35,282 units in September
    • According to CAMPI/TMA
    • Brings 9M22 sales up by 29% YoY to 246,815 units
  • Toyota grew sales by 10% MoM, 63% YoY to 16,138 units in September alone, despite the blanket price hike early in the month
    • Its market share slipped by 530bps MoM to 45.7%
    • Toyota’s 9M22 sales reached 124,884 units, up 35% YoY, well ahead of the company’s target (162k, 25% YoY) and Regis estimates (158k, +22% YoY)
  • Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP) and accounts for c. 20% of the latter’s NAV/profit
  • GTCAP is trading at a 55% discount to its market-based NAV of P873/sh

CONSUMER
Retail prices of sugar hit all-time high
  • Retail prices of refined sugar in Metro Manila have risen to as much P134/kg as of Oct 7, according to data from the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA)
  • The average retail price is now at P97.54/kg (+82% YoY)
  • The SRA previously said that it expects retail prices of sugar to start easing by November as imports arrive and domestic supply stabilizes
    • 150k MT of refined sugar imports are expected to arrive by 15th Nov
    • Ramping up of sugar milling capacity by Nov following the start of the milling season in Sep
  • The SRA is targeting to bring down the average retail price of refined sugar to P70-80/kg
GAMING
Philippines in China’s tourism blacklist over POGOs -Sen. Zubiri
  • Senate President Juan Manuel Zubiri said during a Senate inquiry that China has placed the Philippines on a tourism blacklist over the continued operations of offshore gaming operations in the country
  • He cited that the information came from Chinese Ambassador Huang Xilian
  • China’s culture and tourism ministry has referred to the existence of a tourism blacklist at least three times since it was first announced in August 2020
    • This was when it said it will impose travel restrictions on specific areas that were “endangering the personal and property safety of Chinese citizens”
  • There are no details as to what being in the blacklist entails
  • The Chinese crackdown on gaming operations abroad (particularly those targeting mainland Chinese) eventually led to the arrest of the heads of the two biggest junket operators from Macau and closure of their junket rooms in Manila
  • We expect revenue growth of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) to be driven by local mass market (67% of GGR in 22E, 61% in 23E)
    • Our assumptions assume VIP volumes hitting only 53% of peak level this year and already take into account the crackdowns on Chinese VIPs happening over the past two years
