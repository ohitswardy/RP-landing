REGIS REPORT

13 May 2022

The 1Q GDP growth print of +8.3% yesterday was within our expectations but a positive surprise for the Street. This comes from a 3.8% contraction the prior year and +7.8% expansion in 4Q. As it is, 1Q22 economic output is already just above the 1Q19’s.

The rebound in growth was broad-based across most sectors with agriculture being the exception. Notably, public construction was quite weak for an election year. Net exports remain a drag with rising imports and sputtering exports.

Arguably among the biggest takeaways is what the GDP number means for monetary policy: the BSP Governor has indicated that the BSP could raise rates next month if the GDP print showed recovery was underway.

More results today. The outcomes are a little varied, but leaning to disappointment. Ayala Land (ALI PM), Cebu Air (CEB PM) and Manila Water (MWC PM) are the notable underperformers while DMCI (DMC PM) was the outperformer.

  • Ayala Land (ALI PM) (-) – Headline profit rose 14% to P3.2bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts. Although the mall and office segments outperformed our expectations, they could not compensate for the weak residential revenue (which was brought about by cancellations). Meanwhile, reservation sales fell 15% YoY to P24.1bn, also trending behind our estimates.

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (=) – The net profit outperformance was largely due to a one-off gain and operating profit (+34% YoY) is trending mildly behind estimates. Margins were under pressure from input cost inflation, but this was cushioned by (1) price adjustments (+3-4% cumulatively since 1Q21), and (2) operating leverage from the improvement in SSS. Group SSSG was +17%, led by the Phils (+23%), which we estimate is now tracking ~90% of pre-pandemic SSS levels.   • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) – Cebu Air’s core and EBITDA losses widened QoQ due to the lockdown in January and abnormal acceleration of promotional activities that occurred after restrictions were lifted. While results are trending BEHIND estimates, we believe that 1Q22 was still distorted and operations should be better in succeeding periods, with its domestic capacity already exceeding pre-pandemic levels in 2Q.
  • DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) (+) – Its property and construction units are still facing near-term macro challenges induced by the pandemic. Nevertheless, there’s scope for earnings upgrades, on top of likely upgrades in Semirara. Management announced it will be using Semirara as the main vehicle for its plans to expand in the (1) non-coal mining sector, (2) cement sector, and (3) renewable energy sector.
  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (-) – 1Q22 profits are still down YoY, as billed volumes remain weak. More importantly, capital expenditures continue to accelerate, with 1Q22 capex of P6bn tracking ahead of 2021 capex and our 2022 forecast. We note that the faster MWC spends, the faster the concession accumulates value.

MACRO
GDP expands by 8.3% in 1Q22 on strong domestic demand recovery
  • GDP grew +8.3% YoY in 1Q22, reversing the 3.8% decline in 1Q21 and was faster than 4Q21 (+7.8%)
    • This was above consensus expectations (+6.8%) but broadly in line with Regis expectations (+8.5%)
    • It also surpassed pre-pandemic (1Q19) GDP level
    • Mobility improved after the government eased restrictions in February
  • Growth was broad-based as most sectors rebounded strongly, except for agriculture (due to swine flu)
  • On the demand side, consumption and capital formation grew robustly at +10%/ 20%, respectively, reversing last year’s decline
    • Gov’t spending slowed, with public construction contracting by 5% YoY due to the election ban and the Mandanas ruling
    • Negative net exports expanded +32%, reversing last year’s 4.8% drop
  • The government is maintaining its +7-9% GDP growth target for the year
    • Resumption of face-to-face classes is still missing
  • Regis is forecasting 2022 GDP growth of +6%

CONSUMER
Vehicle sales match pre-pandemic levels in April
  • Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, jumped 41% YoY to 25,149 units in April
    • April 2021 was still a low base, given c. 2-month lockdown
    • While this was down 15% MoM (due likely to Holy week), it nonetheless matched levels seen in April 2019
    • Brings 4M22 vehicle sales to 99,903 units (+13% YoY)
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 50,945 units in 4M22 (+22% YoY), implying that it sold 13,714 units in April alone (+59% YoY, -9% MoM)
    • Its market share improved further to 54.5% from 50.9% the previous month and 48.5% in end-December
    • To reach Toyota’s target of 162k (+25% YoY) units this year, Toyota needs to sell 13.900 units per month for the rest of the year
  • Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV
  • GTCAP is trading at a 20% discount to its market-based NAV of P623/sh and 7.8x FY22E consensus PER

CONGLOS
Semirara propels DMCI’s 1Q22 profit to record level
  • DMCI 1Q22 profit up 177% YoY – Ahead of estimates. Core profit surged to a record high of P11.2bn (ex-FX) in 1Q22, up 177% YoY and trending AHEAD of Regis and street estimates. Its coal mining/power unit, Semirara (SCC PM, report link), outperformed, along with other key segments. Group earnings will likely drop sequentially as operations normalize at Semirara. Despite a challenging macro environment, the positive medium- to long-term prospects for its other business units look intact. 

Management is also looking to acquire copper, nickel, and other mining assets, as well as enter new businesses such as cement and renewable energy.  To our surprise, management has decided to put these potential investments under Semirara. 

  • Strong reservation sales in 1Q22. The property unit’s 1Q22 income dropped, on higher sales cancellations and fewer qualifying accounts. Management guided “flattish to slightly down” earnings growth this year, which appears better than our expectations (-15%). Moreover, reservation sales rebounded strongly to P8bn, after launching a new project in March. This level of take-up is already c. 90% of its pre-pandemic sales and is on track to beat our forecast. The company has several projects that are ready to be launched but timing will depend on market conditions.
  • Construction outlook still bleak. Profits from its construction unit fell 7% YoY on high base effects. EBITDA improved slightly (+2% YoY) on the back of an mild improvement in revenues and the absence of vaccine expenses. Management noted that most of its infrastructure projects continue to face right-of-way delays, which could weigh on profits in subsequent periods. The order book dropped further to P46.7bn but it expects to secure a portion of a rail project, where its consortium submitted a lone bid. 
  • Nickel unit benefits from bull market.  Nickel mining’s profit jumped 20% YoY largely on the back of a +26% YoY increase in shipments. ASPs fell due to unfavorable grade mix. While global nickel prices surged by 48% YoY, it is worth noting that local nickel prices only rose 12% YoY. The company sold 600k MT of nickel in 1Q22, 40% of its year-end target (1.5mn MT).  After securing more tree cutting permits, the company remains optimistic it could increase shipments further this year.
  • Higher demand buoys off-grid power units.  Profits rose 19% YoY, on the back of a +18% YoY jump in energy sales.  The company plans to increase its capacity by 14% by mid-2023, after it completes the construction of a 4MW solar plant in Masbate and a 15 MW coal-fired plant in Palawan.
  • Attractive valuation. The stock has underperformed Semirara by 22% year-to-date and is trading at a 21% discount to its market-based NAV of P10/sh, wider than its historical discount (13%). It has minimal debt and a steady cash flow stream from almost all of its investments, which would also make it an attractive div yield play (at least 8% div yield).

AIRLINES
CEB posts core net loss of P5.1bn in 1Q22 - BEHIND estimates
  • CEB reported 1Q22 headline net loss of P7.6bn.  Excluding MTM losses on its convertibles and FX, we estimate its core net loss was wider QoQ at P5.1bn, trending BEHIND Regis and street forecasts.  EBITDA losses were also wider QoQ at P2bn, as pax fares dropped likely due to (1) the lockdown in January and (2) heavy promotional activities ahead of peak summer months. The surprise drop in fares more than offset (1) better-than-expected pax volumes and (2) lower-than-expected cash costs.
  • The company is holding a briefing on May 18 (3PM).  Call details are not available yet.
