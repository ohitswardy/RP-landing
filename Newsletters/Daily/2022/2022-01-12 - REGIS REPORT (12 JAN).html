REGIS REPORT

12 January 2022

NPL ratios (ex-interbank) improved again in November to 4.48% for the entire banking sector, down from 4.56% in October, and down from the 4.66% peak in August. Furthermore, the PHP value of NPLs also dropped 0.5% MoM to P481bn. The Past Due (PD) ratio also fell to 5.12% from 5.16% the previous month, while NPL coverage improved to 87% from 85%. The one ratio that deteriorated slightly was Restructured Loans (RLs), at 3.11% in November, from 3.08% in October. The ratios were helped by further acceleration in loan growth to 3.9% YoY from 3.2% in October.

While there is some risk of slippage with the ongoing spike in Covid cases and slump in mobility data. However, we note that asset quality did not materially deteriorate in the last Aug/Sept lockdown in Metro Manila.

The data bodes well for a sharp recovery in banks’ profits. Among the banks we cover, Metrobank (MBT PM) has the most potential for significantly lower provision expense and upside to earnings. As of 3Q21, MBT had the lowest NPL ratio at 2.1% and by far the highest NPL coverage at 191%.

+++

The country’s trade deficit for Nov reached US$4.7bn, the highest monthly deficit based on official records. The jump in commodity prices (oil) led to imports rising 37%. YT-Nov, the cumulative trade deficit has now reached US$38bn, on track to roughly match the country’s largest annual deficit. 

The wider trade gap, compounded by higher interest rates externally, is likely to exert pressure for the PHP to weaken near-term.

MACRO
PH total trade growth accelerates to 24% in November; Trade deficit expands to US$4.7bn
  • The country’s total external trade rose 24% YoY in November, fastest since August
    • Due to imports jumping +37% YoY (fastest since June) on sustained growth of fuel imports, raw & intermediate goods, and consumer goods
    • Exports also rose +7% YoY
    • Brings the country’s trade deficit to US$4.7bn for the month, up 120% YoY and +17% MoM
  • YT-November total trade is up 24% YoY to US$175bn and trade deficit at US$38bn – both above pre-pandemic levels

BANKS
Asset quality ratios better again in Nov, as loan growth accelerates further
  • Major asset quality ratios improved MoM in November, while absolute values were flat-ish. Only Restructured Loans (RLs) deteriorated, both in Peso and ratio terms. The ratios were helped by a continued acceleration in loans. The data suggests that the NPL cycle has peaked, which could provide significant upside for bank earnings in 2022.
    • NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dropped to 4.48% down 8bps MoM compared to 4.56% in October. In nominal terms, NPLs were down slightly (-0.4% MoM) to P481bn.
    • Past Due (PD) ratio improved to 5.12% from 5.16% the previous month. The PHP value edged up a bit, but less so than loans.
    • Restructured Loans (RL) ratio deteriorated slightly to 3.11% from 3.08% in October. In PHP terms, RLs jumped 2.1% MoM to P345bn.
    • NPL cover edged up to 87% from 84% the previous month. Loan loss reserve ratio (LLR) also improved by a smidge.
    • Loan growth accelerated to 3.9% YoY in November, from 3.2% in October, marking the 7th consecutive month of improvement. Loans (ex-interbank) were up 1.2% MoM. Loans typically jump 2.5-3.5% MoM in December (even in 2020), partly on trade stocking, and partly on window-dressing. If the trend holds, we could see the loan growth close the year at or about 5%.
  • We could conceivably see a deterioration in ratios in January from the Covid surge, and drop in mobility. However, we suspect that households and businesses have amply adapted to the sporadic opening and closing of the economy. We note that asset quality did not suffer materially during the Aug/Sept lockdowns. And if our current wave follows global patterns, this one should hopefully burn out relatively quickly. Fingers crossed.
