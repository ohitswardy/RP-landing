Filipinos are back on the streets with Google mobility data showing a sharp rebound by early Feb. With Covid-19 infections down, the data should see a further rise in the coming weeks especially as the government’s Alert Levels are expected to be lowered.

Among the other nuggets from Globe’s (GLO PM) 4Q/FY21 results we want to highlight ahead of the earnings briefing are the (1) surprising size of its capex and (2) the “distortions” in 4Q.

Globe’s actual FY21 capex reached P92.8bn (US$1.8bn), which is a whopping 61% of service revenue. The guidance for FY22 is only slightly lower at P89bn, suggesting intense efforts to regain/achieve network parity with PLDT (TEL PM) on wireless and broadband.

Among the “distortions” present in 4Q results were the impact of the typhoon, which management estimated to be P2.2bn financially, and a P3.3bn gain which we reckon is from the dilution of its equity in Mynt.

A few more highlights below.

Property consultancy firm Colliers says prices of condominiums in Metro Manila are likely to have bottomed, but office vacancies and rents are likely to still worsen. More below.

Jollibee Foods (JFC PM) reported FY21 headline profit of P5.9bn, well ahead of expectations. Operating profit (P6.3bn), however, was in line with estimates.

MACRO
Mobility recovers quickly, back to Nov'21 levels

Filipinos are back on the move. While the Covid-19 omicron surge drove people back into their homes in early January, the current sharp drop in Covid cases saw an equally sharp rebound in mobility. As of the last weekend, Google mobility numbers are back to where they were in November, right before the Christmas rush. Perhaps more importantly, the Jan/Feb mobility numbers are 10-20 percentage points higher than they were in Jan/Feb 2021.

This is consistent with our “Glass still half full” view, that the economic reopening/recovery momentum is picking up nicely. We expect mobility data to improve further as Metro Manila mayors push for a further easing of restrictions down to Alert Level 1. While no timetable was given, we expect this in the coming weeks as Covid-19 cases plunge in Metro Manila.

TELCOS
Globe 4Q/FY21 initial comment

Some initial highlights on Globe’s 4Q/FY21 results ahead of its earnings briefing this morning:

  • 4Q income distorted by one-off gains and typhoon costs. Globe said Typhoon Odette, which had inflicted serious damage on various parts of central Philippines in Dec, caused P2.2bn worth of impact on the firm’s operations in 4Q. This came in the form of rebates given to broadband customers as well as network-related damage.  On the flip side, Globe recorded a lumpy gain in 4Q, which we suspect is attributable to the investment of Warburg Pincus into Mynt / GCash.
  • Service revenue was practically flat QoQ (+2% YoY). While mobile service revenue was slightly up QoQ (+1%), home broadband revenue dropped a surprising 11% QoQ. The drop was blamed on the typhoon, which led to lower top-ups for its fixed wireless service as well as rebates for others.
  • Broadband disappoints. After a strong pick-up in 3Q, broadband revenue, surprisingly, dropped 11% QoQ. We thought the period of the period of broadband underperformance was over but it doesn’t appear so. Globe added 58k wired broadband subscribers in 4Q, taking it to 965k subs. The company added a net 77k subs in 3Q.
  • Capex surprise. Another surprise was capex --- Globe spent a surprising P92.8bn (US$1.82b) in capex over 2021, higher than previous guidance (P76bn) and likely higher than PLDT (whose guidance is P88bn – P92bn). That figure implies Globe spent P27bn in 4Q alone. For 2022, Globe is guiding for P89bn in capex, a slight dip from last year.
  • Leverage ratios higher than they appear. Globe issued US$600m worth of perpetuals which are treated as equity for accounting purposes. The fact that they  are treated as equity for accounting reasons does not change their effect on financial leverage from a common shareholders’ perspective. Treating them as debt, we estimate net debt to EBITDA is now at 2.7x and net gearing would now be 2.6x.
  • GCash’s GTV reaches P3.8trn for 2021. The value of transactions that went through GCash reached P3.8trn, which is more than 3x the level in 2020 (P1.2trn).

CONSUMER
JFC FY21 headline net profit ahead of estimates at P5.9bn; operating profit in line at P6.3bn
  • JFC reported FY21 headline net profit of P5.9bn, coming in ahead of Regis (P3.7bn) and consensus (P3.1bn) expectations
    • Excluding coupons to perps and divs to prefs, we estimate recurring net profit to common was P4.8bn
  • The profit outperformance was largely driven by non-operating income and a lower effective tax rate; FY21 operating profit of P6.3bn is in line with Regis expectations
  • For 4Q21 alone, recurring net profit to common was P3bn
  • Revenues in 4Q21 jumped 22% YoY
    • SSSG in the Philippines was +24%, implying that SSS was tracking 80% of pre-Covid levels
  • 4Q21 operating profit was P2.5bn, bringing margins to 5.6% (back to pre-Covid levels). The improvement was attributed to:
    • Cost savings from the 2020 Business Transformation Program, which allowed the Phils to generate similar operating profit vs 4Q19
    • A reduction in Smashburger’s losses (we estimate the 4Q21 loss was P100-120mn vs P250mn in 3Q21)
    • CBTL’s turn to profitability during the quarter

We hope to get further detail during the earnings briefing on 15 Feb.

PROPERTY
Colliers: residential prices have bottomed, office rents will fall further
  • During its 4Q21 briefing, Colliers said that prices of Metro Manila condominiums (in the secondary market) fell 7% YoY in 4Q21 (down 1% QoQ)
    • Metro Manila condominium prices are c. 15%-25% lower than 2019 levels
    • It forecasts prices to rise 2% in 2022
    • Colliers’ condominium price estimates are generally in line with our own forecasts 
  • Meanwhile, Metro Manila office rents fell 12% YoY (down 3% QoQ)
    • Metro Manila office rental rates are c. 20%-30% below 2019 levels
    • Colliers expects rental rates to fall another 10% in 2022 as office vacancy rises
    • In contrast, we forecast rental rates to stay approximately flat in 2022E as vacancy declines slightly
    • We expect BPO office take-up to return to pre-COVID levels but Colliers likely expects work-from-home policies to curtail office demand
BANKS
Tonik gets $131mn fresh equity for expansion
  • Digital bank operator Tonik Financial Pte Ltd. has raised $131mn from a new equity funding round led by Japanese banking giant Mizuho Bank
    • Other significant new investors in the “series B” funding round included Prosus Ventures (formerly Naspers Ventures), the cofounder of DST Partners Rahul Mehta, as well as Singapore investment fund Sixteenth Street Capital and Indonesian family office Nuri Group
  • The digibank’s previous venture capital investors – Sequoia India, Point72 Ventures, Insignia, iGlboe, Alpha JWC, Citius, Blauwpark and Kraft – also participated in the funding round
  • Launched in March 2021, Tonik has set new bank growth records in the Philippines by reaching $20m and $100mn in consumer deposits within the first and eight months of operations
