REGIS REPORT

21 January 2022

Ayala Corp (AC PM) and Ayala Land (ALI PM) announced this morning that ALI will be acquiring certain assets from AC valued at P17.3bn. ALI will be paying for the assets with new shares to be issued to AC at a price of P55.80/share --  premium of over 50% from ALI’s latest price. The most significant of these assets is a 50% stake in Ayala Hotels, which owns the super prime lot where the Peninsula Manila hotel stands and is also a part owner of the Park Central condominium development. AC’s controlling shareholder Mermac is also selling properties in a similar transaction but the values involved (P110.6m) are not that big.

The swap streamlines the ownership of the assets, placing them within ALI and simplifying the corporate structure. AC’s stake in ALI will rise to 47.2% (from 46.1%) as a result of this transaction.

Even based purely on the nominal value of P17.3bn for the assets it is divesting, we reckon AC is letting them go at a reasonable, if not cheap, valuation. Compounding this with the fact that the ALI stock it will receive is being valued at >50% premium to market, AC seems to be getting the short end of the stick. From a market perspective, however, we are doubtful many analysts or investors have included the proper valuation for these assets AC is selling anyway.

Conversely, we estimate the deal is likely to be mildly NAV-accretive for ALI and its outstanding shares will rise by 2%.

Renewable energy (RE) is still a key investor theme (meme?) that has attracted an inordinate amount of interest in recent years. This has led to an astronomical rise in the valuations of RE developers on the basis of project plans -- valuations that are many times the project costs even before these plans come close to completion.

Recent listing Solar Philippines Nueva Ecija (SPNEC PM) revealed it is eyeing the injection of “over 10,000MW” worth of planned projects in exchange for the issuance of ~24bn new shares. This would quadruple the outstanding shares of SPNEC from the current 8.1bn.

While SPNEC’s current valuation (US$800k per MW) is already well above replacement cost (~US$500k per MW), it is still only fraction of ACEN’s (ACEN PM) US$3.2m per MW. If (and it’s a big IF) one reckons ACEN’s valuation is rational and justified, it’s not a stretch to think SPNEC’s shares would follow ACEN’s trajectory.

Interest in RE has become fashionable that an upcoming IPO, Citicore Energy REIT, will be combining that theme with another trendy one, REITs, to fetch what its principal hopes will be even more princely valuations.
