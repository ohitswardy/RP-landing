REGIS REPORT

10 March 2022

We cover a whole slew of4Q/FY21 results today.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (=) – FY21 operating profit grew 23% to P15.6bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts. 4Q21 reservation sales jumped 44% YoY (50% QoQ) to P25.7bn, with commercial lots from new townships contributing P6bn. This brought FY21 reservation sales up 17% to P80bn. Horizontal projects have sold well and have risen to 32% of FY21 reservation sales, from only 9% in 2019. Meanwhile, office vacancy was flat QoQ at 10%, better than the industry average of 18%.

  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (-) – FY21 EBIT rose 14% to P9.7bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, as residential revenue fell 47%. 4Q21 residential revenue fell sharply due to cancellations and timing issues. However, we are not too concerned about this since despite the cancellations, 4Q21 net presales rebounded (+117% YoY and +23% QoQ) and FY21 reservation sales rose 48%. 4Q21 mall foot traffic improved to 65% of pre-COVID levels, from only 40% in the previous five quarters. Meanwhile, 2021 office revenue grew 8% to P6.5bn, already ahead of our 2022E forecast

  • Metro Pacific (MPI PM) (+) – MPI’s FY21 core income stood at P12.8bn, up 22% YoY and above our forecast but in line with consensus. 4Q21 profit rose 24% YoY due to a sharp volume/profit recovery of its tollroad and rail units, as well as lower costs for Maynilad. The company also shared its views on the impact of the oil price shock to its operations and the upcoming rate review. The company is actively looking for acquisitions in non-regulated sectors but remains committed to maintain its dividends and continue its ongoing buyback program. The shares are inexpensive, trading at a 51% discount to its market NAV of P7.80/sh.

  • AC Energy (ACEN PM) (-) – ACEN’s  FY21 income came in at P5.251bn (+22%), below Regis and consensus estimates. While it benefitted from new capacity coming on line, the sequential performance was partially impacted by Typhoon Odette. We believe though that profits were largely tempered by purchases from the WESM given its short position.
  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (+) – The company provided more color results on its FY21 numbers at its earnings call yesterday. 4Q came in softer QoQ on the impact of higher steam costs on the generation side and the typhoon impact to its distribution utilities. The decline was tempered, however, by P1bn in liquidated damages claimed in 4Q relating to the GNPD Unit 2 delay. Management stressed that amidst the surge in coal prices, AP is broadly unaffected given the pass-through nature of contracts and financial hedges.

  • San Miguel Food & Bev (FB PM) (+) – FY21 consolidated income reached P31.4bn, up 40%YoY, as 4Q grew 5%QoQ. The limited press release cited operating income for beer (+10%) and gin (+39%) both growing last year, presumably helped by increased mobility towards the latter part of the year. We hope to get more detail in the call today at 4PM.

Employees of BPO firms in PEZA areas will have to begin reporting for work in their respective offices beginning April if the firms are to continue enjoying the fiscal incentives attached to being located in PEZA areas. The return of employees to their workplaces should give a nudge to demand for office space as, hopefully, the pandemic winds down.

PROPERTY
BPO workers to return to offices beginning April 1
  • The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) rejected the request of the BPO industry to extend the current policy allowing 90% of workers (in PEZA zones) to work from home
  • Finance Secretary Dominguez said this was meant to be a temporary policy and would lapse on April 1
  • Thus, 90% of BPO employees (whose offices are in PEZA zones/buildings) must return to the office beginning April
  • This could soon lead to more BPO firms signing up for office space
  • Office leasing (including their REIT stakes) comprises:
    • 48% of MEG’s NAV
    • 32% of RLC’s NAV
  • MEG is trading at 62% discount to NAV, 0.5X FY22E P/B, and 7.3X FY22E P/E
  • RLC is trading at 50% discount to NAV, 0.8X FY22E P/B, and 13.9X FY22E P/E
POWER
ACEN FY21 profit P5.3bn (+22%YoY), behind Regis/consensus; 4Q21 down 38%QoQ
  • ACEN reported that FY21 net income increased 22% to P5.251bn – behind Regis (P6.0) and consensus (P5.8bn). The FY21 figure implies 4Q21 net income declined 38%QoQ to P981mn, but still represented a 26% growth over the same period last year.

  • For FY21, attributable output grew 21% to 4,633GWh on the back of 23% jump in generation from RE sources. Attributable capacity currently stands at 3,800MW of which 3,300MW (87%) is renewable.
  • Despite the negative short-term impact to its own operations, management thinks that elevated prices of power and fossil fuel have actually made the case for renewables more compelling.
    • Ended 2021 still short because of some delays in plants that are about to come online
    • Aim to close the gap by summer and hopefully end the year a net-seller
    • Where fossil fuel prices are today, even solar with battery won’t look expensive anymore
  • Coal update
    • Some 20-30MW of coal exposure in Philippines
    • Outside the Philippines: Vietnam, India, Indonesia – nothing linked to fuel; Australia – uncontracted
  • Management expressed their intention to comply with the 20% float requirement to stay in the PSE Index
    • After the UPC transaction is executed, the free float will be 18.5%
    • Less than 1bn shares needed to maintain free float – either use shares as currency for acquisitions or major shareholders could sell down
  • ACEN trades at an EV/EBITDA (attributable) of 21x FY22E and PE of 41.8x FY22E.
