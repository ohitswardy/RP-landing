REGIS REPORT

06 May 2022

CPI inflation climbed to 4.9% in April, above Street expectations. The April print is a three-year high and also higher the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target band and March’s 4.0%. We reckon the May is unlikely to be lower and should put pressure on the BSP to raise policy rates as soon as June.

+++

Today’s set of 1Q results were all “beats”, surprisingly. The scale of the positive surprise was larger for ICTSI (ICT PM) but the telcos also managed to exceed expectations.  See our brief summaries below.

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (+) – Headline profit for 1Q came in at P9.1bn, but that included two rather lumpy items. Stripping out the exceptional items, core income was P7.5bn (+5%), inclusive of losses at Voyager. Broadband continued to do well but it was a bad quarter for mobile. Losses at Voyager climbed further ahead of the launch of its digital bank.  Click on the link for more.

  • Globe (GLO PM) (+) -1Q net income was P13.8bn, but included a chunky P10.5bn gain from the partial divestment of its data center business. Core income for the quarter was P5.1bn, up slightly (3%) YoY and ahead of expectations.  The main driver of the outperformance appeared to be Mynt, which contributed over P300m in profit vs a loss the prior year. Operationally, the broadband business was a disappointment but wireless fared better.  A little more below and also after today’s earnings briefing.
  • ICTSI (ICT PM) (+) - 1Q22 core profit to common jumped 80% YoY while EBITDA rose 28% YoY, trending ahead of forecasts. Yields surprised to the upside, rising by 14% YoY to a record US$183, and more than made up for the mild shortfall in volumes. Despite the challenging global macro environment, management reiterated its optimism on the sustainability of its yields, as it plans to implement more tariff hikes across its portfolio in succeeding periods.

We also got more detail from the earnings calls of D&L Industries (DNL PM) and San Miguel F&B (FB PM) yesterday following the initial press releases.

The outperformance of DNL largely stemmed from its oleochemical segment, which saw profits jump 57% on improved volumes and margins, making up for the 19% drop in food segment profits. We suspect DNL’s pricing power on oleochemical (coconut oil based) exports stemmed from sourcing coconut oil locally while benefitting from generally elevated benchmarks abroad for edible oils and its derivatives.  Click for more.

FB’s beat, on the other hand, came from a bigger-than-expected rebound from food (1Q22 profits 82x QoQ), with management crediting favorable prices of poultry and flour. The omicron surge had a contrasting effects on its alcohol businesses, as gin volumes (+6%YoY) continued its uptrend while beer (-6%YoY) retreated back to c.70% of pre-pandemic levels.

MACRO
Inflation surges to 3-year high in April
  • Inflation surged to 4.9% in April, the highest level recorded since December 2018 and higher than in March 2022
    • Higher than the BSP’s target range for the year (2-4%) and near the upper bound of its 4.2%-5% forecast range for the month
    • Higher than street estimates (median: +4.6%)
  • Prices of food jumped by 3.8%, faster than the previous month
  • Electricity, gas, fuel inflation increased further by 20% from 17% in March
  • Transport inflation also picked up to 13% from 10% the previous month
  • Brings 4M22 average inflation rate to 3.7%
  • BSP Governor Diokno previously signaled the Monetary Board could raise the policy rates in June
    • Decision will depend on 1Q22 GDP growth data (if GDP grows by 6-7%, it may consider a rate hike in June)
    • The government will release 1Q22 GDP figures on May 12 (street estimate: +6%)
  • We are forecasting key policy rates to rise to 2.5% by year-end from 2% currently

MACRO
National debt soars to record P12.68tn in March
  • The outstanding debt of the Philippines soared to P12.68tn in March
    • Higher than in end-2021 (P11.7tn) and pre-pandemic (P7.7tn)
    • Assuming the government’s nominal GDP target for FY22 (P21.5tn), the national debt is equivalent to 58.8% of GDP
  • 69.9% of debt is domestic while 30% is foreign
  • By maturity, 5% is short term, 29% is medium term, and 66% is long term

TELCOS
Globe 1Q22 core net income ahead of expectations
  • Core income P5.1bn, ahead of expectations.  Globe’s headline profit in 1Q of P13.7bn (+86% YoY) included P10.5bn of gains from the partial sale of its data center business.  Stripping that and other exceptional items out, core income was P5.1bn (+3%), which is trending ahead of estimates.  Contributions from Mynt were the main factor for the positive variance, with costs also being another factor.  Cash costs were steady YoY, which helped magnify the 4% growth in revenue down the P&L.
  • Wireless business up, broadband business down. In an exact reversal of PLDT’s own 1Q, the wireless business did well. Revenue for the quarter was up 3% QoQ, 4% YoY.  We estimate GLO’s revenue share rose by 2ppt in the quarter but it may have been skewed a bit by how the impact of the typhoon was accounted for.  Broadband, meanwhile, surprised to the downside again.  Revenue for this segment was down -4% YoY with the loss of more fixed wireless subscribers.  Wired broadband subscribers crossed the 1m mark, rising 65k to 1.03m.
  • Mynt contributes P327m to profit.  Globe’s share of Mynt’s profit in 1Q was P327m, a sharp turnaround from the P282m loss the prior year.  It’s also further improvement from the P76m profit in 4Q. 

CONSUMER
FB's 1Q22 rebounds on jump in food, gin contributions
  • 1Q22 profit +63%QoQ, -5%YoY to P6.2bn – ahead. FB’s net income to equity holders was about P6.2bn for 1Q22 (+63%QoQ, -5%YoY)) based on the reported profits of its subs, tracking ahead of estimates (P21.9bn) for the full year. The poor performance from beer was offset by the continued growth of spirits as well as the aggressive turnaround in food from 4Q. After barely breaking even in 4Q21, food segment profits jumped to P3.036bn (82x QoQ, -10%YoY) on supposedly better poultry prices, feeds and flour.

  • Beer volumes down again, back to c.70% of pre-pandemic level. Profits of SMB fell to P4.9bn (-23%QoQ, -10%YoY). Domestic beer volumes fell to 43.5mn cases (-15%QoQ, -6%YoY), with management attributing the decline to the impact of the Omicron surge. EBIT margin also came in lower (22.8% vs 25.5% in 4Q21, 23.4% in 1Q21) despite a price hike implemented a few months ago. Compared to the improved trends in previous quarters, 1Q22 volumes were roughly 30% lower than pre-pandemic levels.
  • Gin volumes climbed 5%QoQ, 6%YoY to 12.2mn cases. Volumes continued to climb, posting another 5% QoQ growth to 12.2mn cases (and 6%YoY). The strength in volumes may have captured some trade-loading ahead of the price increase implemented in February, but overall trends were nevertheless upward vs beer. Profits jumped to P1.4bn (+39%QoQ, +34%YoY) as EBIT margin expanded 2.8ppts to 14.2%
  • Food posts substantial QoQ recovery. Reversing the commodity impact in 4Q, food segment profits jumped to P3.036bn from just P37mn in 4Q21. Management cited favorable prices of poultry as a main factor in the rebound, overshadowing rising prices of other inputs like wheat and soybean meal. The profit level was nevertheless 10% lower YoY, with EBIT margin down by 2.3ppt YoY to 10.2%.
