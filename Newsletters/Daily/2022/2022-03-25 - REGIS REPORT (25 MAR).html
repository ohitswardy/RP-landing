REGIS REPORT

25 March 2022

The BSP kept its policy rate at an all-time low of 2%. However, it raised its 2022 inflation forecast from 3.7% to 4.3%. With inflation on the rise, we forecast the BSP to raise policy rates by 50 basis points in 2H22.

Speaking of higher inflation…

Monde Nissin's (MONDE PM) FY21 core net profit (-5% YoY to P8.2bn) came in behind Regis and consensus expectations as margins across both key business segments (APAC BFB and Quorn) took a hit in 4Q21 amidst heightend input cost pressure. Prices of wheat and palm oil -- among the largest inputs for MONDE (~20% of COGS) -- jumped in 4Q, and continue to surge YTD. Price increases were not enough to mitigate the higher input cost, and management has signaled that further price action will have to be made in the coming quarters.

The stock currently trades at 26x 2022E PE on our recently-downgraded estimates (Philippine Consumer - Balancing act). We reiterate our Hold rating as margin volatility is likely to persist in the near term.

Meanwhile, consumer loans showed signs of early recovery in 4Q21, growing 2.7% QoQ. While auto loans were still lethargic, there was a noticeable uptick from mortgages. The consumer NPL ratio also fell 60 basis points QoQ to 9.31%.

These numbers should improve even further with the reopening of the economy as banks have more confidence in normalizing (loosening) their credit standards. This should bode well not only for consumer loans but also for auto sales.

The BLOOM vs GGAM saga continues. While dismissing the case by GGAM against Mr. Razon’s real estate and oil/gas companies in the US, a federal court in New York did not grant Bloomberry’s motion to the dismiss the case against subsidiaries Sureste Properties, BRHI and Mr. Razon himself. Nearly a decade after the dispute started, an end is not looking anywhere in sight.

BSP maintains key rates

  • BSP Governor Diokno said they are considering to cut the reserve requirement ratio for banks in 2H22

BANKS
Consumer loan growth recovering, NPLs falling - past the hump?
  • Consumer loans showed signs of early recovery in 4Q21. Overall, consumer loans were still down 0.6% YoY, but significantly better than the -2.6% in 3Q21. Furthermore, consumer loans were already up 2.7% QoQ. Asset quality also improved in 4Q21, with NPLs falling nearly 60bps QoQ to 9.31% from the 9.90% in the previous quarter. Granted, the level is still uncomfortably high.
  • The growth was most notable in Mortgages, where loans were up 7.9% YoY (was 5.6% in 3Q), and up 4.3% QoQ. NPL ratio also fell to 9.36%, down 22bps QoQ.
  • However, the trends don’t seem to have turned yet for Motor Vehicle (MV) loans, which were still down 16.5% YoY, and down another 4.6% QoQ. According to the banks we cover, new loan approvals are up sharply. However, with very little new lending in the last two years, and given the relatively short duration, the loans are maturing faster than the banks can write new loans. Offhand, we’d expect outstanding loan balances to flatten out in 2Q/3Q22 (on a QoQ basis). To the upside, MV NPLs fell sharply QoQ to 12.01% from 12.70% the previous quarter. More encouragingly, MV NPLs in absolute terms were down nearly 10% QoQ, though we suspect this is partly on more aggressive foreclosures.
  • For Credit Cards (CC), loan balances contracted 4.6% YoY and 0.9% QoQ. This seems counter-intuitive, given the sharp jump in mobility/spending in 4Q21. This is likely due to aggressive write-offs by banks. This is partly evidenced by a sharp 118bp QoQ drop in NPL ratio from 7.96% to 6.78% in 4Q. The nominal value of CC NPLs fell 15.6% QoQ.
  • Based on our recent conversations with bank CEOs, the consensus seems to be that the industry is ready to re-grow the consumer loan portfolio, as the economic reopening gives management confidence in loosening credit standards. This would be positive not only the banks (higher NIMs etc), but also for residential real estate, and especially for auto sales, where demand is boosted by availability of credit.

GAMING
BLOOM petition to dismiss GGAM complaint denied in NY
  • Bloomberry Resorts (BLOOM PM) disclosed that a New York Court did not grant its motion to dismiss the case file by GGAM against Sureste Properties (SPI), Bloomberry Resorts and Hotels Inc (BRHI) and Mr. Enrique K. Razon, Jr.
    • SPI and BRHI are subsidiaries of BLOOM
    • It did, however, dismissed the cases file against real estate and oil and gas companies in the U.S. associated with Mr. Razon
  • This means that GGAM, SP, BRHI and Mr. Razon will have to present their evidence to prove their respective claims/defense for the enforcement of an Arbitral Award in the New York Court
  • In March 2021, Global Gaming Asset Management LLC (GGAM) sued Enrique K. Razon, Jr and affiliated companies in a federal court in New York to confirm a $297mn arbitration award by a Singapore court
  • BLOOM maintains that the arbitration award can only be enforced by a Philippine court
    • The Final Award issued in September 2019 ordered Bloomberry to pay GGAM about US$100mn in damages due to the termination of the management services agreement
    • It also ordered BLOOM to buy GGAM’s 921mn shares in BLOOM for P10.17bn@P11/sh (or allow the sale of the shares and make up the difference between market sale and P10.17bn)
