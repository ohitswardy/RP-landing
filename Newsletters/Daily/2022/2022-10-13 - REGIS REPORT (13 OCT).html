REGIS REPORT

13 October 2022

The Chinese embassy in the Philippines denied that the Philippines was on the country’s (China) tourism blacklist, after the Senate President himself, Miguel Zubiri, declared receiving such information from the Chinese ambassador. The “blacklist” was supposed to be because of the presence of online gambling firms targeting Chinese nationals in the country, an “industry” which the Chinese government has been trying to stamp out.  Zubiri subsequently said that something might have been “lost in translation” in his conversation with Ambassador Huang Xilian.

Immediately before the pandemic, tourists from China accounted for 21% of overseas arrivals.  For the integrated casino resorts, Chinese gamblers were an even more dominant presence, contributing around half of gaming revenue.

The newly released implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Build Operate Transfer (BOT) Law are notable for what they did NOT revise, rather than for what they did.

The “new” IRR kept two provisions that the Duterte administration sought to revise following the brouhaha over the water concession contracts.  First, the Material Adverse Government Action (MAGA) clause will still cover all actions of the government.  The Duterte government sought to limit the clause to cover only actions of the Executive branch.  Second, the “new” IRR will also still allow the extension of contracts to compensate private parties for any shortfall, something the previous government wanted to change.

One change that was accomplished was in the venue of any arbitration proceedings, which must now be held within the Philippines.

The release of the “new” IRR should clear the way for the government’s infrastructure program, which will rely heavily on Public-Private Partnerships (PPP). The BOT Law (and the attached IRR) is an integral framework for most of the projects being considered.

The Dept of Energy (DOE) clarified that its approval of Prima Infrastructure’s purchase of 45% of the Malampaya gas field operations is conditional upon Prime Infra’s commitment to maximize the resources covered.  This sounds like an implicit approval of the contract extension of the operator Spex.  The contract of Spex over the Malampaya field expires  in 2024.

Chinese embassy denies PH has been put on tourism blacklist
  • The Chinese embassy in Manila said the Philippines is not in its country’s tourism blacklist, a clarification it made after Senate President Juan Manuel Zubiri’s statement earlier

  • The Chinese embassy issued another statement saying crimes associated with POGO not only harms China’s interests and China-Philippine relations but also the interests of the Philippines
INFRASTRUCTURE
New BOT Law IRR in effect
  • The revised implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) Law took effect yesterday
    • The amendments aim to promote transparency and accountability in delivering infrastructure projects
    • The amendments push for “appropriate risk sharing” between the government and the private sector
  • The approved revised IRR is more favorable to the private sector than the proposed revisions of the Duterte administration, as it essentially reinstated most of the provisions in the original BOT IRR
    • It reinstated the definition of MAGA as encompassing all actions of the government (vs. Duterte admin’s proposal to limit MAGA to executive actions and excluded regulatory actions)
    • Reinstated the original provision of allowing contract extensions as a mode of compensation to the private proponent
    • However, the new BOT IRR now only allows arbitrations to be done in the Philippines (i.e. no longer allows international arbitrations)
  • The PPP Center said there are currently 74 projects worth P2.25tn in the pipeline as of end-August. These include 53 national projects and 21 local projects

POWER
Gov’t consent to Razon’s Malampaya buy-in conditional
  • The Department of Energy clarified that the approval of Prime Infrastructure’s buy-in of the Malampaya gas project was premised on its commitment to maximize the development of Malampaya’s existing resources, with a view to developing the nearby fields as well
    • According to Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla
  • The DoE cleared the sale last month after Prime Infra was found to be technically, financially and legally qualified to take over as new operator of Malampaya
  • Prime Infra will assume full ownership and control of the project upon completion by Nov. 1, 2022 of the process for safe and seamless turnover of operations from Shell
  • Next on the agenda of the Malampaya consortium— composed of Prime Infra, UC38 LLC and PNOC Exploration Corp. (PNOC EC)—is the extension of the service contract that will expire in 2024
