05 September 2022

Megawide (MWIDE PM) and its partner GMR revealed that they were selling their entire stakes in the operator of the Mactan Cebu International Airport (MCIA) to a subsidiary of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM).

This divestment, P25bn for 100% equity, puts MWIDE in a much improved but still not-so-firm financial footing. At 22x pre-pandemic EBITDA, the sale would be very accretive to MWIDE, lifting NAV by almost 40% to over P7/share. On the other hand, it also means MWIDE practically returns to what it once was: a construction company with its attraction as an airport play now gone. This sale, however, doesn’t preclude MWIDE from pursuing future projects in this area.

From the AEV perspective, the acquisition further reduces its dependence on its power business. Management has been actively trimming the share of power in its portfolio.  The sale of part of its stake in Aboitiz Power (AP PM) to JERA, the increased investment in Union Bank (UBP PM) through the rights issue and now this purchase have all pushed power to below 50% of AEV’s gross assets.

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) is now preparing to act on the joint application by Meralco (MER PM) and San Miguel Global Power (SMGP) to amend their power supply agreement to raise power tariffs. The parties are asking the ERC to allow SMGP to raise what were fixed prices in order to recover losses from the surge in fuel costs, citing “change in circumstances”. SMGP is threatening to terminate its fixed price contracts if it is not allowed to raise selling prices, an event which would “force” Meralco to procure power at even higher prices.

The higher tariffs, if approved, will ultimately be passed on to end-users.

Related to this is the news that Shell has not started delivering natural gas to SMGP’s Ilijan plant, citing technical issues. SMGP supposedly purchased the banked gas of the government that Shell and Malampaya consortium are supposed to provide. The delivery of gas to Ilijan will affect the supply available to First Gen (FGEN PM). Consequently, any delay in the commencement of the banked gas delivery will be positive for FGEN. Both SMGP’s Ilijan and FGEN are awaiting the operation of their respective LNG terminals, which would provide fuel supply in the face of diminished Malampaya gas.

The government raised a massive P420bn in retail Treasury bonds (RTB) due March 2028. For perspective, P420bn is equivalent to 3% of all Peso (PHP) deposits in the banking system. Granted around 1/4th of that figure was from investors swapping from existing Treasuries, the figure is still quite large.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), 51% owned by GT Capital (GTCAP PM), raised prices on a number of vehicle models by 1-2% at the start of Sept. The company has seen margins eroded by the strength of the USD (vs PHP). Around 50% of TMP’s costs of sales is USD-linked and the PHP has depreciated by just over 10% vs the USD since the start of the year.

MACRO
Gov’t raises P420bn from local-currency retail bond
  • The Philippine government raised P420bn from local-currency retail bonds
  • Bonds are due in Mar 2028 and pay a coupon of 5.75%
    • Of the total sold, P108.5bn pesos were switch subscriptions -- a record high
    • Accounted for 17% of the government planned borrowings this year
  • As at end-July, the national government debt stood at P12.89tn, 0.8% up MoM and 9.9% higher than in end-Dec 2021
    • Domestic debt accounted for 68.5% while external debt accounted for 31.5%

CONSUMER
Toyota hikes prices anew
  • Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) has implemented its fourth price hike this year on its various models
    • The latest round of price hike of between 1-2% on selected models took effect last Sep 1
    • The latest price hikes resulted in higher prices for the Raize, Avanza, and Veloz models at an average of P15,000 to P20,000 or 1-2%
    • Other models affected are: Vios, Innova, HiAce Cargo, Coaster, Alphard, Commuter Deluxe
  • TMP implemented price hikes in January, June, and July
  • Over 50% of TMP’s COGS is USD-linked while around 10% is JPY-linked
    • As a result, every 5% depreciation/appreciation of PHP vs USD is 40% reduction/increase in profit, ceteris paribus
    • Every 5% depreciation of PHP vs JPY is 8% reduction/increase in profit, ceteris paribus
    • Our forecast assumes GPMs will drop to 8.7% for the rest of the year, after averaging 10.1% in 1H22
  • TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/gross asset value
  • GTCAP is trading at a 44% discount to its market-based NAV of P914/sh and 7.6x FY22E PER

POWER
ERC considers rate hike bid of Meralco, SMC power units
  • The temporary rate hike petitions that were jointly filed by Meralco and SMC Global Power units are now up for deliberation at the ERC
  • Meralco presented various scenarios – all of which would lead to higher electricity rates
  • The first scenario presented by Meralco is the one where the CIC claim by SMC Global Power is approved, which would result in an increase of P1.57/kWh vs the original rate over a six month period
    • If the CIC claims are approved, the rate impact to Meralco’s generation charge is P0.28-0.29/kWh against the generation charge of around P10/kWh
  • All other scenarios presented by Meralco (emergency PSAs, sourcing from WESM, etc) would result in higher rates than the CIC claim
  • “Meralco believes that it can continue supplying power to its customers at the least cost by preserving the PSAs while addressing the financial concern of power suppliers through recognition of the CIC claim”
POWER
Shell refuses to deliver banked Malampaya gas to San Miguel
  • The Malampaya consortium and its operator SPEx continue to drag their feet on the delivery of the banked natural gas to San Miguel (SMC PM)
  • Consortium claimed that delivery could affect the pressure of the gas reservoir at the gas field
  • PNOC earlier said SPEx had failed to comply with the directives of DOE requiring it to immediately resume supply to the Ilijan plant
  • In the near term, the refusal of SPEx to deliver banked gas to San Miguel is positive for First Gen (FGEN PM)
    • FGEN’s gas plants have been getting their full gas allocation from Malampaya since Ilijan’s contract expired in June
