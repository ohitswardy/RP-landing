28 December 2022

PLDT’s (TEL PM) “overspending” saga continues. The company says it is now in talks with its suppliers to negotiate down the P48bn worth of budget overruns, either through canceling portions of the orders, and/or getting the prices discounted. While the financial impact might be mitigated, the reputational hit from this episode could take much longer to overcome.

+ + +

Congress passed on third reading a bill that aims to harmonize land valuation for the purposes of tax computation. This was part of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Package (CTRP) of the previous administration (aka Tax Package 3). At the moment, the national government sets the “zonal value” of properties, which is the basis of the tax on a sale of a real property asset. However, it is currently the local government units (LGUs) that set the “assessed value” of properties, off which the annual real property tax (RPT) is based. In many cases, the assessed value does not get updated for years, even decades. As a result, the RPT can be a tiny fraction of the market value of the property, especially in areas where property values rise sharply.

We have highlighted this in the past – since the whole CTRP was unveiled in 2016/17 – as a risk to property developers. In particular, the law seems to penalize holding on to large tracts of idle land. Since RPTs are based on the value of land and improvements (ie buildings), the change in land values will likely have less of an impact on owners of rental properties (ie office, malls, hotels, etc).

It is, however, incredibly difficult to estimate the potential earnings impact to listcos. Initially, the fear was that RPTs could double, triple (or more), depending on the discrepancy between assessed value and market values. However, the law does allow the LGUs to set the percentage of the assessed value that is levied the RPT. For example, if the assessed value is doubled from, say 100 to 200, the LGU could halve the rate from say 1% to 0.5%. In the end, though, we suspect that LGUs will want to use the law to increase local tax revenues.

We estimate that in 2018/2019, RPT accounted for roughly 3-7% of pre-tax profit for real estate companies we cover. We will be watching the bill’s progress closely. The bill now gets transmitted up to the Senate. Given the legislative process, we reckon the soonest this could be implemented will be 2024.

+ + +

Speaking of taxes, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) has suggested a revamp of the country’s vehicle taxation scheme. At present, the tax is broadly based on a vehicle’s price (“luxury tax”), and/or its function (personal vs commercial). The IMF wants to include environmental components to the taxation, whether at the point of sale, and/or at the annual registration level.

This could have implications on the product mix of auto distributors, including GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) Toyota business

PROPERTY
House Bill reforming property tax assessment transmitted to Senate
  • The House of Representatives approved on third reading a bill “Instituting reforms in real property valuation and assessment…”
  • The bill has been transmitted to the Senate
  • The bill seeks to transfer the responsibility of determining market values (for purposes of real property taxes) from Local Government Units (LGU) to the national government (specifically, a division of the Department of Finance)
    • LGUs (such as city governments) have generally been reluctant to raise market values/property taxes because the elected officials might lose their reelections
    • Some LGUs have not updated their values in over 10 years
  • However, local government units will still determine the tax rate to be applied for real property taxes
  • Thus, local governments might lower the tax rate on real property in order to limit the impact on their constituents
  • Pre-COVID, real property taxes comprised 3%-7% of the pretax profits of the developers we cover
