REGIS REPORT

07 July 2022

Banks’ asset quality continued to improve in May, both in ratio and in absolute value terms, across all metrics. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) fell nearly 20bps MoM to 3.85% (vs 4.04% in April) the lowest NPL reading since January 2021. In Peso terms, NPLs shrank 4.1% MoM and 10.5% YoY. Past Due (PD) levels also improved to 4.44% (vs 4.65% in April), with the nominal level down 3.9% MoM and 14.3% YoY. Restructured Loans (RLs) edged down 5bps to 2.94% from 2.99% the previous month. NPL coverage jumped to 94.8% in May from 90.6% in April, given the sharp drop in NPLs and a (very) mild increase in loan loss reserves.

Loan growth (ex-interbank) also accelerated to 7.7% YoY in May, marking the 14th consecutive month of improving YoY growth. Loans were up 1.1% MoM. LDRs were steady at 70.7%.

The data remains consistent with positive outlook on the banks as an ideal play on the country’s economic recovery.

+++

The new administration has given us further glimpses of its economic plan with the press briefing of Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. Among the highlights were:

  • Reducing poverty incidence to 9%
  • Achieving “upper middle income” status for the Philippines (i.e. US$4,000 GDP per capita) by 2028
  • Trimming public sector debt to GDP to 60% by 2025
  • Keeping public sector infrastructure spending at 5-6% of GDP but encouraging public-private partnerships (PPP) to improve infrastructure

What’s also interesting it that the administration will aim to pursue the next legs of the Duterte government’s tax reform agenda. These will involve (1) a reform of the real property valuation and the taxes that come with that as well as (2) streamlining of taxes on passive income and the financial sector. This last one could be positive for the local equity market.

+++

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) ruling on Meralco’s (MER PM) tariffs for the lapsed period (July 2015 to June 2022) has now been released and the outcome is largely as we discussed yesterday. The regulator affixed a tariff of P1.3522/kwh for Meralco for the lapsed period, lower than the P1.38/kwh previously approved. As a result, the ERC directed Meralco to refund P21.8bn to consumers, inclusive of P3.8bn of  carrying costs (i.e. interest).

As we pointed out yesterday, the ERC decision will result in Meralco having ~P50bn in excess provisions that it would no longer need to “refund”.

Beyond that, it is also worth asking if Meralco will cease setting aside provisions for its tariff beginning this month (July 2022). Meralco will be charging P1.35/kwh but one can argue this is a “provisional” rate. A decision on its rate review for the 5th Regulatory Period (5RP, which is supposed to begin this month) is many months away. If it keeps rates at P1.35/kwh, annual pretax earnings could be as much as P12bn higher.

+++

Jollibee (JFC PM) and McDonald’s Philippines (of Alliance Global [AGI PM]) confirmed social media reports of a shortage of its chicken products in selected stores over the past few days. The shortage is being brought about by the sudden spurt in demand, which supply was unable to quickly address. Apart from the reduction in capacity during the pandemic (which is just ramping up now), the high cost of feeds (e.g. corn, soybean, wheat) is putting further cost pressure on growers. The impact of the foregone sales by the recent shortage won’t move the needle for JFC and McDonald’s, but it may take a few more months before the overall poultry supply-demand situation normalizes.

BANKS
Bad loan ratio falls to 16-month low in May
  • Gross NPL ratio for the banking sector stood at 3.75% in May
    • Lowest since 3.72% in January 2021 and down from 3.93% in April
  • Ex-interbank loans, the NPL ratio fell nearly 20bps MoM to 3.85% (vs 4.04% in April)
  • Bad loans dropped by 4% MoM and 11% YoY to P429bn in May
  • Gross loan portfolio expanded by 7% YoY and 0.4% MoM to P11.44tn
  • NPL coverage ratio improved to 94.76% from 79.96% a year ago
  • Past due loans fell by 14% YoY to P508.5bn, bringing the ratio down to 4.44% from 5.56% a year ago
  • Restructured loans climbed by 27.78% YoY to P337bn, accounting for 2.94% of banks’ loan portfolio in May
  • Loan loss reserves were higher at P407bn from P383bn a year ago, bringing the ratio down to 3.55% vs. 3.59% a year ago

MACRO
Marcos Jr. admin tempers 2022 economic growth target; unveils six-year fiscal framework
  • Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said they are now aiming for GDP to grow by 6.5%-7.5% this year
    • Slightly lower than the 7-8% target set by the Devt Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) in May
    • Will still be the highest GDP growth rate among ASEAN+3
    • DBCC will meet on Friday to firm up projections
  • Diokno said the economy likely grew at a double-digit rate pace (or above 10%) in 2Q, faster than the 8.3% print in 1Q22
    • 2Q GDP data is set to be released on August 9
  • Its 6-year fiscal framework is aimed to:
    • +6.5-8% GDP growth rates from 2023-2028 (faster than Duterte admin’s 6-7% goal)
    • Reduce poverty incidence to its lowest-ever of 9% by 2028 from 17-18% pre-pandemic
    • Revert budget deficit to pre-pandemic levels equivalent to 3% of GDP starting 2026
    • Ease public debt ratio to 60% of GDP by 2025 from 63% currently
    • Infrastructure spend-to-GDP to be limited at 5-6% in 2023-2028; Will welcome PPP projects for national and local levels
    • Achieve an “upper-middle income status” or US$4,046 per capita income by 2028
  • More details will be released during President Marcos Jr’s first SONA on July 25
  • The Marcos admin will also push for the passage of Packages 3 and 4 of the Duterte-era Comprehensive Tax Reform Program (CTRP)
    • Package 3 looks to build an “equitable and efficient” real property valuation system (currently at the Senate committee since end-2021)
    • Package 4 (PIFITA) seeks to make passive income and financial intermediary taxes “fairer and more competitive”
      • Will reduce the number of tax rates from 80 to 36 and harmonize the tax rates on interest, dividends and capital gains, and business taxes on financial intermediaries
    • These packages will be “revenue-neutral” but will simplify the tax structure
