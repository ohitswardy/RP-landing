REGIS REPORT

04 April 2022

SM Investments (SM PM) announced it will be acquiring 81% of a company that operates the Tiwi and Makban geothermal fields in exchange for 17.4m new shares (1.4% of capital). 

From an EPS perspective, the transaction is likely to be accretive, based on management’s statements. We expect earnings from PGPC (the operating company) to rise sharply this year since its tariffs are indexed to coal.

Apparently, the injection of PGPC was made for ESG reasons as it will provide carbon offsets for the listed company. While it will burnish SM’s green credentials, the transaction will not actually help in global warming and reducing emissions unless its operations can be expanded beyond the current ~300MW.

The taxman cometh, again. After trying to impose additional taxes on casino operators in 2013, the BIR wants a separate 5% franchise tax from licensees on top of the license fees they are already paying PAGCOR. The additional tax on operating income could dent profit estimates by up to 10%, but we expect Bloomberry (BLOOM PM)to take this all the way to the high court.

+++

Globe (GLO PM) announced that the deal to sell part of its data center operations has been completed. It may be recalled that Globe earlier disclosed that it will form a joint venture for its data center business with ST Telemedia and Ayala Corp. Globe will hold 50% in the venture while ST Telemedia and AC will hold 40% and 10% respectively.

The post-money valuation of US$350m and the US$100m of proceeds that Globe will receive post-deal implies that Globe’s data center business was being valued at US$275m. The press release issued today says Globe will book a pretax gain of P10.5bn (US$202m). This means Globe’s holding cost for the data centers is little more than US$70m.

+++

Metro Pacific (MPI PM) is taking full control of Landco again by converting advances to the real estate company that would allow it to have a 100% stake. Landco is a niche property developer doing projects in Batangas that MPI used to control.

Megaworld (MEG PM) is injecting 45,000 sqm worth of properties into MREIT (MREIT PM) that will expand the latter’s portfolio by 16%. In exchange MEG will receive 264m new MREIT shares in a transaction that will be mildly accretive for both company but not for the taxman.

Both Semirara (SCC PM) and DMCI (DMC PM) declared dividends last Friday. While the amounts look lower than we expected for this round, we reckon more could be forthcoming later this year.

CONGLOS
SM to acquire geothermal energy firm from principals
  • SM Investments will acquire 81% of Allfirst, holding company of Philippine Geothermal Production Company (PGPC)
    • SM will buy it from its controlling shareholders, the Sy family.  SM already owns 19% of Allfirst so its stake will rise to 100%
  • PGPC is the entity that operates the Tiwi and Makban geothermal fields
    • Its steam output is sold entirely to Aboitiz Power which converts it into ~300MW of electricity
  • The transaction will generate carbon offsets under the Task Force on Climate-Related Disclosures (TFCD) for the company
    • From a real-world perspective, it will not have a practical impact on reducing emissions since it is only being transferred
  • SM will issue 17.4m of its own shares (valued at P903.88/share) to acquire the 81% stake in Allfirst
    • Valuation was based on cash flow of the existing geothermal fields and does not include the other concession areas PGPC holds
  • Management expects the deal to be accretive on the basis of 2021 earnings
    • This implies that Allfirst made at least P700m in earnings in 2021
  • PGPC sells its geothermal output at tariffs which are based on coal prices so earnings are likely to be much higher this year
  • The shares SM are currently trading at par with NAV
CONGLOS
MPI revives real estate ambitions in Landco buyout
  • MPI announced it will be converting P429mn worth of shareholder advances in Landco into equity, allowing it to own 100% of Landco (from 38%)
    • The previous owner of Landco was Alfred Xerez-Burgos
    • Landco is an upscale developer of the 88-hectare Peninsula de Punta Fuego in Nasugbu, Batangas in 1995
    • Landco has a landbank of 15 hectares (largely in San Juan, Batangas)
  • Takeover revives MPI’s ambitions in the real estate/hospitality sectors, in line with its plans to diversify out of highly regulated businesses
    • MPI currently has c. 330 hectares of raw land in San Juan, Batangas, which we never accounted for in our NAV, as this was inherited from Metro Pacific Corp. and had titling issues
    • MPI estimates the market value of its land in Batangas at P5.6bn, or <P2,000 per sqm
  • MPI is trading at a 53% discount to its market-based NAV of P7.93/sh

GAMING
BIR says 5pct franchise tax due from PAGCOR licensees
  • The Philippines Bureau of Internal Revenue filed on Wednesday a clarification that it expects licensees and contractees of the country’s casino regulator, the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) to pay 5 percent franchise tax to the BIR on their income from operations
  • BIR said the clarification on the casino industry’s liabilities regarding franchise tax, income tax, and VAT was based on current laws and recent jurisprudence
  • The circular added that “the regulatory fees paid by contractees to PAGCOR are different and distinct from the 5 percent franchise tax payable to the BIR”
  • In 2014, Bloomberry petitioned the Supreme Court to nullify the provision of a 2013 BIR Memorandum that imposed corporate income tax on casinos
    • The High Court affirmed that Bloomberry, as a contracting party of PAGCOR was subject only to the 5% franchise tax on gross gaming revenue, in lieu of all taxes, as provided under the PAGCOR Charter
    • Bloomberry then said that the decision allowed PAGCOR and BRHI to revert to the original license fee structure of 15% for junket and 25% for mass licensee fee (inclusive of the 5% franchise tax)
