REGIS REPORT

16 August 2022

Security Bank (SECB) 1H22 NPAT doubled YoY to P6.25bn, well ahead of Regis and consensus forecasts. The beat was driven by a collapse in provision expense to just P408mn, down 83% YoY, and just a small fraction of our full-year estimate. While NPLs improved 37bps QoQ to 3.28%, the extremely low provisions kept NPL cover at 92%. Core operations were broadly in line, with loan growth accelerating to 15% (from 8% in 1Q), as “wholesale” loans jumped 18% and consumer loans returned to growth, up 6% YoY. NIMs were up 9bps QoQ. While we remain positive on the stock (at 0.5x P/bk), we find the low NPL coverage an unnecessary distraction. The bank will hold a briefing tomorrow at 5pm.

Meanwhile, July vehicle sales as reported by CAMPI/TMA rose 29% YoY to 27,813. This takes the YT-July to +18%. Toyota unit sales for the month were 13,900, which would imply market share for the month was practically unchanged at just below 52%.

BANKS
SECB NPAT doubles YoY, on provision plunge; PPOP slightly ahead

Security Bank (SECB PM) reported NPAT of P6.25bn, double YoY, and well ahead of Regis (8.24bn) and consensus (P9.08bn) for the full year. We note though that the beat was driven largely by a collapse in provision expense; while NPLs improved, NPL cover was still sub-100%. Otherwise, the results were broadly in line, and in keeping with our thesis of a) accelerating loan growth (15% YoY), improving NIMs (+9bps QoQ), and improving asset quality. Notably, consumer loans turned positive (+6% YoY). Some details, below.

  • Loan growth accelerated to 15% YoY from 8% in 1Q22. Corporate/mid-market loans led the way again, jumping 18% (was +11% in 1Q). Notably, though, consumer loans grew 6% YoY, turning from a 4% contraction in 1Q22. Consumer loans account for 23% of total loans, still among the highest in the sector. Deposits grew 9% YoY.
  • NIMs expanded 9bps QoQ and 17bps YoY in 2Q22. Loan yields widened 6bps QoQ, matched by a 6bps increase in deposit costs.
  • Fee incomes grew 22% YoY in both 2Q and 1H, staying well above pre-Covid levels. Trading/forex gains were down 51% YoY in 1H as expected, given the high base in 2021 and the rising interest rates in 2Q22.
  • PPOP was up 6% YoY in 1H22, trending slightly ahead of our expectations. Opex was up just 4% in 1H, but was up 8% in 2Q alone.
  • Provision expense collapsed -83% YoY to just P408mn. NPL ratio dropped 37bps QoQ to 3.28% from 3.65% in 1Q. However, with the drop in provisions, NPL cover just edged up to 92% from 90% in 1Q. Of the stocks we cover, only SECB is still under 100% NPL cover.

SECB’s core operations seem solid, trending slightly ahead of our forecasts. We are disappointed that the bank did not take the opportunity to significantly increase NPL cover this quarter.

Management will hold a briefing on Wednesday at 5pm.

CONSUMER
Vehicle sales jump 29% YoY in July
  • Vehicle sales jumped by 29% YoY in July to 27,813 units, though sales dipped by 3% MoM
    • According to data from CAMPI/TMA
    • Brings YT-July sales to 182,687 units (+18% YoY), trending behind the industry group’s target of +25%
  • Toyota sold 94,026 units in 7M22, up 26% YoY
    • Implies sales in July alone rose 29% YoY to 13,936 units (-3% MoM)
    • Toyota’s market share dipped to 51.5% from 51.7% in June but remained high relative to end-2019 (47%)
    • YT-July sales are tracking ahead of Regis FY22E (158k, +22%); Toyota needs to sell 12.8k units per month for the rest of the year to reach target
    • YT-July sales are tracking within Toyota’s year-end target of 162k units (+25% YoY); Toyota needs to sell 13.6k units per month for the rest of the year to reach its target
  • Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 24%/20% of the latter’s profit/gross asset value
  • GTCAP is trading at a 44% discount to its market-based NAV of P915/sh

OTHERS
Market foreign ownership falls in July
  • We estimate that market foreign ownership fell 36bps MoM to 20.3% (an all-time low) while PSEi foreign ownership dropped 6bps MoM to 24.8% (in July)
  • Market net foreign selling amounted to US$80mn in July (from US$207mn in June)
  • Although the PSEi foreign ownership declined, only 14 of the 30 PSEi constituents saw reductions in their foreign ownership levels
    • 15 PSEi constituents experienced increases in foreign ownership while 1 was unchanged
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • SECB – foreign ownership increased 55 basis points MoM to 38.1%. The stock saw net foreign buying of US$3mn, the fifth highest in July. 
    • PGOLD – foreign ownership fell 37 basis points MoM to 18.9% as net foreign selling reached US$4.2mn
    • WLCON – foreign ownership dropped 30 basis points MoM to 17.1%.
