REGIS REPORT

16 November 2022

Overseas remittances amounted to US$2.84bn in Sept, which is  3.8% higher YoY. YT-Sept, remittances are up 3.1%.

Meanwhile, vehicles sales for CAMPI/TMA for the month of Oct were just over 32k, up +42% YoY but actually slightly lower (-9%) MoM. We estimate that Toyota unit sales were 15.5k, +39% YoY and -4% MoM. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP), like much of the industry, has seen a sharp rebound in unit sales but is also seeing profitability hurt by the strength of the USD.

Check out today’s round-up of results:

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (+) - Ayala Corp held its earnings briefing yesterday, with 9M income up 23% to P24bn.  In aggregate, the main listed units did better than expected, particularly BPI (BPI PM). Among the highlights was that it took a P2.3bn provision on its investment in Yoma, the business group with operations in the Myanmar.  AC announced its investment there in 2019 before the turmoil.  Apart from that, one other item is that the non-listed operations are still losing money, most notably AC Industrials.
  • Vista Land (VLL PM) (-) –3Q22 residential revenue fell 43% YoY and 52% QoQ, likely due to sales cancellations. However, net profit grew 40% QoQ (+11% YoY), boosted by curiously large drops in interest expense and taxes. Meanwhile, 9M22 rental revenue grew 33% to P8.2bn, about 155% of the 9M19 figure. However, rental receivables of P16.7bn is equivalent to about 18 months of rental revenue, implying that revenue is overstated relative to cash rent.
  • Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (-) –9M22 headline profit of P2.0bn (-37%) is trending behind REGIS and consensus forecasts but the rental segment appears to be improving. Office revenue grew 11% QoQ, potentially signaling an improvement in occupancy rates (from c. 70% in 2Q22). Moreover, mall revenue climbed 10% QoQ (+88% YoY), suggesting that the company reduced rental concessions further.

President Marcos has appointed a new Commissioner for Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), replacing the one appointed less than five months ago. Romeo Lumagui is replacing Lilia Guillermo, who is more of an IT person associated with Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. Lumagui is a tax lawyer who was affiliated with the law firm of Liza Marcos, who is now First Lady.

Vehicle sales dip by 9% MoM in October

    • Toyota’s market share improved slightly to 48.3% in October from 45.7% in September
  • Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV
  • GTCAP is trading at a 55% discount to its market-based NAV of P908/sh

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp 9M Results
  • 9M earnings up 23% to P24bn.  Profits for the group rose 23% to P23.9bn but 3Q profit was down 15% to P7.6bn.  The 3Q decline was blamed on one-off losses, which we believe refer to provisions on its Myanmar investment. Without one offs in both 9M22 and 9M21, management claims income would have been up +44% in 3Q.  Overall, profits for 9M22 appear on track to exceed Street estimates for the year (P29.3bn).
  • Key listed subsidiaries perform.  In aggregate, the group’s key listed subsidiaries did better than expected.  The key standout was BPI, whose contributions rose 82% in 9M on strength of both interest and non-interest income.  ACEN showed improvement in 3Q but is still facing issues with its short market position that will only be reversed next year.
  • Unlisted subsidiaries still in the red.  AC Industrials continued to lose money (P1.2bn in 9M), notwithstanding an improvement in AC Motors.  AC Logistics had the biggest (negative) delta as losses rose more than 3x to P889m.  Meanwhile AC Health reported a profit of P88m but it would have been a loss if not for gains most likely attributable to the merger of its health app with that of Globe’s.
  • Provision for Myanmar investment.  AC set aside P2.3bn in provisions for its investment in the Yoma group, the conglomerate operating in Myanmar. AC committed to invest over US$200m in the Yoma group in 2019, before the troubles there reignited.
PROPERTY
Vista Land 3Q22 residential revenue falls 43% YoY (52% QoQ)
  • Vista Land’s 3Q22 residential revenue fell 43% YoY and 52% QoQ
    • This is the second consecutive QoQ and YoY drop
  • The reduction in residential revenue coincided with a drop in residential receivables and an increase in inventory, implying that there were cancellations
  • Rental revenue grew 28% YoY and 39% QoQ to P3.3bn
    • However, rental receivables of P16.7bn is equivalent to about 18 months of rental revenue, implying that revenue is overstated relative to cash rent
  • Interest expense fell 77% QoQ despite a flat debt level
    • This likely means that interest expense on new debt is being capitalized
  • Taxes also fell c. 90% YoY and QoQ as the tax rate was an unusually low 2%
  • 9M22 operating profit rose 10% to P7.7bn, behind REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • Reservation sales grew 14% YoY (+10% QoQ) to P16.6bn

PROPERTY
Filinvest Land mall and office revenue rise in 3Q22
  • Filinvest Land’s 9M22 operating profit rose 11% to P5.7bn, in line with REGIS forecast (behind consensus)
    • However, 9M22 headline profit of P2.0bn is trending behind both REGIS and consensus forecasts
  • Office revenue grew 11% QoQ (+6% YoY), potentially signaling an improvement in occupancy rates (c. 70% in 2Q22)
  • Mal revenue climbed 10% QoQ (+88% YoY) to P442mn, about 90% of the 3Q19 figure
  • The company will hold a briefing on November 17
