REGIS REPORT

10 August 2022

The 2Q22 GDP print left us scratching our heads. For the first time in a good long while, the top-down numbers did not match our bottom-up data. According to the PSA, GDP growth was 7.4% YoY, slower than the (revised) 8.2% recorded in 1Q22. Most bizarrely, the report showed that Personal Consumption not only slowed (to 8.6% YoY in 2Q from 10.0% in 1Q), but also that it contracted by 2.1% QoQ. This is directly at odds with industry data we follow – car sales, electricity sales, loan growth, etc – which showed strong YoY and sequential growth. It was also inconsistent with Google Mobility data, and with early 2Q22 corporate results. We expected a huge drag from Net Exports, which pulled GDP growth down by a full 400bps. However, what really stood out was the “Statistical Discrepancy” line, which dragged growth by a whopping 140bps. Needless to say, we’re taking the PSA data with a healthy dose of salt.

In any case, 1H22 GDP growth of 7.8% was still quite good, and is likely among the fastest in the world, especially in light of global recession fears. That said, we are sticking to our 6.0% FY22E GDP growth forecast for now, wary of inflation-driven slowdown in consumption in 2H22.

+++

Speaking of positive data, BSP showed the banking sector continued acceleration in loan growth to 9.1% YoY in June, faster than the 7.7% pace in May, and nearly matching the pre-Covid pace of growth. Asset quality, meanwhile, continued to improve sharply, both in ratio and nominal PHP terms. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dropped 16bps MoM to 3.69%, the lowest since Sept 2020. Past Due ratios fell even faster, down 25bps MoM to 4.19%, the lowest since April 2020.

The banks continue to be among our favored sectors to play the economic reopening story.

+++

For today’s results round-up, we have Megaworld (MEG PM) and DMCI (DMC PM), both of whom delivered better-than-expected operating numbers.

  • Megaworld (MEG PM) (+) – 1H22 operating profit jumped 44% to P10bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts. Contrary to our expectations, the residential GPM expanded 240 basis points, likely due to the higher proportion of sales from lot over the past two years. There were also no indications of meaningful sales cancellations, as 2Q22 residential revenue rose 17% YoY and 11% QoQ. Reservation sales were also strong, growing 68% YoY and 20% QoQ. Meanwhile, office vacancy was flat QoQ at 9%, lower than the industry average of 18%.
  • DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) (+) – Recurring income for 1H surged 142% YoY to P20bn, with 2Q22 profit alone more than doubling to P8.7bn, after almost all key segments posted strong recoveries. Even though profits are likely to be lower HoH, DMCI is still on track to report record earnings this year, which we believe will also translate to higher dividends. Management already hinted at declaring similar or higher dividends in Oct/Nov for both Semirara (SCC PM) and DMC. Last year’s special div alone (P0.48/sh) would translate to a yield of 5%, based on current prices.  More below.

Meanwhile, ACEN (ACEN PM) held a briefing to not just discuss its 2Q results but also its ambitious aim of reaching 20,000MW of renewable power capacity by 2030. The discussions revealed some interesting details, including about the divestment of its coal plant SLTEC, and where its growth pipeline will be focused on. More here in the links below.

The trade deficit for June came in at US$5.8bn (+75% YoY), bringing the cumulative 1H to just a shade under US$30bn (+66% YoY). The decline in commodity prices should help lower the deficits in the coming months but it appears the country is still on track to register a record US$60bn trade deficit this year.

+++

Globe (GLO PM) is firing back at Dito Tel, accusing it of failing to control the illegal bypassing of international termination fees on voice calls, and demanding US$11m in penalties.

We don’t think the financial penalties or even the operational remedies that might arise from these issues will move the needle much. What’s more important, in our view, is how these disputes will influence public perception.

MACRO
2Q22 GDP growth slows to 7.4%, led by an odd 2% QoQ drop in consumption
  • Philippine real GDP growth clocked in at 7.4% YoY in 2Q22, slowing from the (revised) 8.2% pace in 1Q22. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data, Personal Consumption Expenditure (PCE) slowed sharply to 8.6% YoY from 10.0% in 1Q22; more oddly, the PCE figure for 2Q22 was down 2.1% QoQ. This was despite relatively lower base effects in 2Q21 given the tight lockdowns in April/May 2021, and lighter lockdowns in January this year. The slowdown was at odds with bottom-up sector data, Google Mobility data, and early corporate results, which showed an acceleration in demand (see charts, below).
  • Government consumption grew 11.1% YoY and 34.3% QoQ likely in part due to spending related to the May 2022 elections. Capital Formation surged 20.5% YoY and 34.66% QoQ. The growth was driven largely by Changes in Stock, ie huge inventory buildup (vs drawdown in 2Q21), and by Construction, which was up 15.7% YoY and 67.9% QoQ. We estimate Domestic Demand grew 12.8% YoY, compared to 12.1% in 1Q22. Net Exports was a major drag, with the deficit ballooning 39.6% YoY in real terms, which we estimate pulled GDP down by roughly 400bps. Exports grew 4.3% YoY, while Imports were up 13.6%. Annoyingly, the “Statistical Discrepancy” line accounted for a 140bp reduction to the YoY growth in 2Q22.
  • On the Supply side, Industry slowed sharply to 6.3% YoY from 10.5% in 1Q22, with Manufacturing growing just 2.1% YoY vs 9.8% in 1Q22. This negated a surge in Construction, up 19% YoY. The Services sector accelerated to 9.1% YoY (and up 10.5% QoQ), driven by Transportation (+27.1% YoY) and Accommodation/Food Service (+29.9% YoY) as mobility restrictions were eased. Agriculture dragged, growing just 0.2% YoY, and contracting 4.1% QoQ.
  • GDP growth in 1H22 was 7.8% YoY, significantly faster than our 6.0% forecast for FY 2022E. While it is tempting to upgrade, we retain our numbers for now, wary of a) higher base effect from 2H21, b) higher inflation in 3Q, and c) potential normalization after the “revenge spending” in 1H22.

MACRO
Trade deficit widens further in June
  • Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit widened further to US$5.8bn
    • Higher than the revised US$5.6bn deficit (previously US$5.7bn) in May 2022 and the US$3.33bn deficit in June 2021
  • Imports rose 26% YoY, 5% MoM in June
    • Due to a sustained surge in fuel imports (+125% YoY) and a strong rebound in consumer goods imports (+30% YoY)
    • Capital goods imports rose just 3% YoY
  • Exports barely improved, up 1% YoY, as electronic imports dropped
  • 1H22 trade deficit now at US$29.8bn, up 66% YoY

BANKS
Asset quality, loan growth continue to improve in June
  • Asset quality continued to improve in June, not only in ratio terms, but also in absolute PHP amounts. NPL ratio (ex-interbank), dropped 16bps MoM to 3.69% (vs 3.85% in May), and is the lowest NPL print since September 2020. The PHP value fell to P421bn, down 1.8% MoM and down 12.8% YoY. The Past Due (PD) ratio dropped even faster, down 25bps MoM to 4.19% in June vs 4.44% in May, with PD loans down 3.5% MoM. Restructured Loans (RLs) edged down another 5bps to 2.89% (vs 2.94% in May), though the nominal amount crept up 0.7% MoM. NPL cover jumped to 97.1% from 94.8% in May, due largely to the drop in NPLs; loan loss reserves were up marginally MoM.
  • Loan growth (ex-interbank), meanwhile, accelerated again to 9.1% YoY in June, compared to 7.7% in May. This is now the 15th consecutive month of faster YoY growth rates. Loans were up 2.5% MoM. LDRs were slightly up to 71.0% from 70.7% in May.
  • The data remains consistent with our thesis of the banking sector as a play on the economic reopening.

CONGLOS
DMCI 2Q22 profit doubles YoY; Property and construction units rebound, along with SCC
  • Exceptionally strong 1H21 profit.  Headline 1H22 profit of P20.3bn, up 114% YoY. Stripping out one-offs and FX, we estimate core income jumped 142% YoY to P19.9bn, representing 59% of consensus FY22E. While 1H22 looks like a strong beat, management flagged group profits could be significantly lower HoH due to (a) lower coal and nickel mining sales volumes, as well as (b) input cost inflation affecting margins across its businesses. Nevertheless, the company will likely post its strongest full-year earnings, with all segments likely to exceed pre-pandemic levels.
  • Firing on all cylinders in 2Q22.  DMCI’s profit more than doubled YoY to P8.7bn in 2Q, with almost all segments posting stellar YoY growth rates, except for Maynilad. We emphasize, however, that Maynilad’s underperformance relative to the other segments is not a concern, as it is still in the midst of tariff negotiations, which could likely change the company’s fate for the better soon. Apart from Semirara, there were notable QoQ/YoY improvements in construction, property, nickel, and off-grid units.
    • Property unit’s 2Q profit boosted by higher margins, other income. DMCI Homes’ profit jumped 79% YoY largely on the back of (1) a 6ppt expansion in EBITDA margins to 20% and (2) an 88% YoY increase in “other income” related to sales cancellations and rental income. Management indicated it has likely seen the worst in 2021, as its default rate is now down to a more “normal” 2-3% from 6% last year. Reservation sales dropped by 30% QoQ to P5.7bn due to the absence of launches during the quarter. Given that it plans to launch 1-2 projects in 2H, management is optimistic it can achieve its sales target of P27bn this year (vs. pre-pandemic: P37bn).
    • Construction posts strong 2Q rebound on base effects. The construction unit’s profit jumped more than 3x to P516mn, after last year’s low base that was driven by heavy provisioning. However, revenues are still on a downward trajectory, with 2Q down 11% YoY, due to lower completions and absence of new projects. Its order book is now down to P44bn, but management is hopeful the government can already announce bid awards, as well as conduct more biddings, soon.
  • Special dividend looks in the bag.  Management indicated it is eyeing to acquire new businesses that are aligned with its strengths in construction, engineering, and material handling. Hence, the group is studying possible investments in mining (not related to coal) and agriculture. However, given that nothing is imminent at the moment, management hinted at declaring similar or higher dividends in Oct/Nov for both SCC and DMC. SCC declared P3/sh in dividends last year (P1.75/sh of which was “special”) while DMC declared P0.96/sh in dividends last year (of which P0.48/sh was “special”).
  • Valuation. DMCI is trading at a 24% discount to its market NAV and at 4x FY22E consensus PER.  Both look fair relative to more recent history.

TELCOS
Globe seeks penalties for Dito Tel on international call fees
  • Globe asked the regulator, NTC, to impose P622m (US$11m) in penalties on Dito
    • Relating to international voice calls that are being masked as local calls, and thus not paying the appropriate termination fees
  • International calls terminating with local carriers have to pay interconnection/termination fees
    • Domestic mobile calls don’t pay interconnection fees
  • Globe’s complaint comes after Dito Tel filed an anti-competition complaint with the Philippine Competition Commission
    • Dito Tel’s complaint arose from the difficulty in completing voice calls on the networks of Globe and PLDT
