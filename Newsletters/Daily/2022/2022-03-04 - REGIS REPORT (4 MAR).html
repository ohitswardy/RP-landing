Some banks will soon become proud (?) owners of Philippine Airlines (PAL). As part of the airline’s rehab plan, it will reportedly convert the loans of its unsecured lenders – including Philippine banks, and international leasing firms – into shares equivalent to a total 20.5% stake in PAL. The local banks include: Philippine National Bank (PNB PM) at $89mn, Asia United Bank (AUB PM) at $75mn, Chinabank (CHIB PM) at $65mn, and Union Bank (UBP PM) at $20mn. PNB is part of LT Group (LTG PM), which is a sister company of PAL. UBP management says that their exposure loan has already been fully provisioned, and any potential value they can recoup could be effectively booked as profit.

BDO (BDO PM) also has direct exposure, but their loans were collateralized. As such, they were not part of the deal.

The rehab will make PAL better able to compete against Cebu Air (CEB PM). However, given CEB’s stronger financial position, PAL will likely continue to have a tougher time accessing loans – or at least will likely make loans more expensive – than its competitor.

We have 4Q/FY21 results from telco PLDT (TEL PM) and port operator ICTSI (ICT PM) today.

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (-) – Core income (including losses at Voyager) came in at P28bn (+3%), with telco core income at P30.2bn (+8%).  EBITDA at P96bn was actually in line with expectations so we’d describe the results a mild miss due to higher losses at Voyager and higher depreciation charges.  Arguably the most notable item from yesterday was PLDT’s revelation that it was selling half of its telco tower network at valuation “north of US$1bn”. Given the economics of the sale-and-leaseback deal disclosed, the deal appears quite value-accretive for PLDT.
  • ICTSI (ICT PM) (+) - FY21 recurring profit to common rose 74% YoY to US$384mn, outperforming Regis and street forecasts. 4Q21 profit/EBITDA rose 43%/33% YoY, on volume growth of +5% and sustained improvements in yield (+17%) and margins (+4ppts). Early 2022 indications point to robust “re-opening” growth rates and management reiterated its continued positive yield and EBITDA margin guidance. We believe street forecasts will upgrade and valuations (9.6x FY22E EV/EBITDA) remain attractive relative to history (11x) and peers (11x).

MACRO
Mobility - upward grind (data as of 27 Feb)
  • Mobility data continues to grind upwards. The figures for the weekend 26-27 Feb are slightly wonky, though, as Friday, 25 Feb was a public holiday. To the upside, it showed a sharp jump in Transit Station data, as many took the long weekend to travel (mostly domestically, we presume). To the downside, the Retail numbers were a bit soft relative to the previous week. It also meant a big drop in workplace for that day, which skewed our 7-day avg figure.
  • Two things to note:
    • First, as we pointed out recently, the figures are now significantly higher by some 30-40ppts compared to the corresponding data in Feb 2021.
    • Second, we should see an additional bump this week, with Metro Manila being relaxed further to Alert Level 1 from 01 March.
  • This might be relevant for the office segment of the property sector. Workplace mobility as employers may opt to bring more staff back into the office either full-time, or on a hybrid basis. It might even allow for BPOs to resume talks with potential landlords, as there could be a clear path to bringing workers back to the office at close to full capacity.

TELCOS
PLDT 4Q/FY21 Highlights
  • Core earnings P28bn (+3%), ex- Voyager P30.2bn (+8%). Despite some mild disappointment at the revenue line, EBITDA of P96bn (+11%) came within expectations. While core income came in short due to non-cash charges as well as losses at Voyager, we would characterize FY21 results as only a mild miss. The top line fell a little short due to the impact of Typhoon Rai (Odette) across the Visayas region.
  • Wireless revenue market share largely unchanged. Wireless revenue in 4Q was practically unchanged QoQ but down YoY. Even broadband revenue was a mild disappointment. Both were blamed on the impact of the typhoon which affected telecom facilities across large parts of the country. We estimate PLDT’s wireless revenue market share vs. Globe was 48.6%, almost unchanged QoQ.

A key metric we also keep track of is mobile data yield (revenue/GB) and we note PLDT’s mobile data yield rose to P22/GB in 4Q, and is now ~10% higher than Globe’s. This suggests Globe has been more aggressive with its pricing.

  • Wired broadband net adds slow down slightly. PLDT added 198k subscribers for its wired broadband service in 4Q, a slight dip from the 230k added in 3Q. PLDT again blamed the typhoon, in part, for this slowdown. It ended the year with 2.97m subscribers. For perspective, Globe added 58k wired subscribers in 4Q and ended 2021 with 965k subscribers.
  • Telco tower sale will be value accretive. PLDT claims to have received offers from parties to buy 6,000 of its telco towers (half of its total) at a valuation “north of US$1bn”. This should be viewed in the context of the leaseback arrangement that PLDT is stipulating with the sale: i.e. that PLDT will pay a lease broadly equivalent to the current opex (rent, security, power, etc) of these towers. Under those terms, the apparent value accretion to PLDT would be the net, after tax proceeds of such a sale. This is likely to be significant compared to its current market cap of US$7.3bn.
  • Voyager losses rise in 4Q. PLDT’s share of Voyager’s losses was almost P800m in 4Q (P2.2bn for FY21), implying losses of ~P2.3bn at the Voyager level. We reckon Voyager has been spending aggressively in recent months to catch up to Gcash and in preparation for the launch of Mayabank early this year.
  • Dip in capex for FY22, with aim to be FCF positive; special dividend eyed. PLDT is guiding for capex of P76-80bn in FY22, and is aiming to be free cash flow (FCF) positive this year after 2021’s record capex of P89bn. The company is eyeing a special dividend from the proceeds of the telco tower sale.
  • Earnings guidance. Management is guiding for mid-single digit growth in revenue, with EBITDA of over P100bn and telco core income of P32-33bn for FY22.

UTILITIES
MWSS removes VAT on water bills
  • The MWSS announced it will be removing the 12% VAT beginning this month, which will result in lower water bills
    • The VAT will be replaced by a 2% national franchise tax and the actual rate of local franchise tax, which can range bet. 2-2.85%
    • This after Manila Water and Maynilad were granted new legislative franchises
  • Will take effect starting March 21
  • We reckon there will be a short term negative impact on the profitability of Manila Water and Maynilad since input VAT will no longer be offset against output VAT
    • The inability to benefit from input VAT will raise costs but these will be included in the calculation of OCP and should have no material impact on the value of their concessions
POWER
Duterte signs EO to revive nuclear power
  • President Duterte signed an executive order (EO) allowing the country to tap nuclear power as an alternative energy source
    • EO directed the Department of Energy to conduct a pre-feasibility study on the viability of introducing nuclear power
    • The Nuclear Energy Program Inter-Agency Committee (NEP-IAC) was also tasked to make recommendations on the use and viability of the mothballed Bataan Nuclear Power Plant
  • Energy Undersecretary Gerardo D. Erguiza, Jr. said the government needs to come up with a regulatory framework for the nuclear energy program, which will need legislation
    • nuclear power plants with small modular reactors, which are being eyed in off-grid areas, can be built as soon as the regulatory framework is already
  • In 2020, the Philippines’ power mix consisted of 57% coal, 21% renewable energy, 19% natural gas, and 2% oil-based
