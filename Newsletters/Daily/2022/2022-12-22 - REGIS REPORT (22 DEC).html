REGIS REPORT

22 December 2022

PLDT (TEL PM) management held a briefing yesterday to shed light on the capex overspending that it disclosed last Friday.

While the forensic audit is not fully complete, PLDT emphasized there was “no fraud”, “no anomaly” and “no overpricing”, just “over-ordering” of equipment / services. It reiterated that there will be no write-down of P48bn and that its financial statements are accurate. Ultimately, the current situation was the result of the failure of management and the board to see what should have been apparent: capex was overshooting the aggregate amount that the board had approved. The conditions of the pandemic and the overlapping multi-year timelines of many individual projects led to this major oversight.

The total capex spend (overrun and all) has yet to be fully reflected in the balance sheet as some of the assets (services) have yet to be delivered. This, however, does not mean there’s a P48bn hole in the balance sheet. All it means is that PLDT spent more than its board previously approved. We can compare this to a power plant being completed at a cost 13% more than the board authorized. While it is an embarrassment that the board didn’t figure it out sooner, it does not mean that the power plant was overpriced or that someone stole 13% of the funds.

Ultimately, the briefing yesterday affirmed our initial assessment and conclusions published here (For international investors >> PLDT – Budget blowout / For Philippine-based investors >> PLDT – Budget blowout) on Monday. The P48bn overspend will mean higher depreciation charges in the future (P4.8bn per year if we assume ten-year depreciation period), but management will try to cosmetically lower this by taking accelerated depreciation charges against the P55bn+ gain on its tower sales.

The >20% drop in PLDT’s share price, which wiped out more than US$1.2bn of market value is overdone. That steepness of the plunge was a reflection of the lack of comprehension that led to speculation of a P48bn hole in PLDT’s balance sheet (not helped by a rather hastily written press release). The eventual clearing of those misperceptions will help the share price recover a bit (though we expect some to insist there must be something anomalous going on), but there will be some lingering damage from the failings revealed in this episode.

Budget deficit narrows to P124bn in November
  • The government’s budget deficit shrunk by 4% YoY to P124bn in November
    • Revenues grew 17% YoY, outpacing growth in expenditures of 10% YoY
  • Tax revenues jumped 16% YoY, 19% MoM, well ahead of target due to higher collections from the Bureau of Customs
  • Expenses rose 10% YoY, 17% MoM as primary spending accelerated, rising by 12% YoY, 21% MoM
    • Interest expenses dropped 16% YoY and 21% MoM
  • YT-November budget deficit at P1.23tn, down by 7% YoY and tracking below the government’s target (P1.5tn deficit, 6.9% of GDP)
