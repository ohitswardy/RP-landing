REGIS REPORT

12 August 2022

A few significant transactions were announced over the last twenty-four hours, involving the two telecom incumbents.

The PLDT (TEL PM) group announced it had entered into a series of transaction that would effectively give it ownership of Sky Cable in exchange for half of free-to-air broadcaster TV5. PLDT’s Cignal (where the telco owns 64% of economic rights) will be acquiring, initially, 39% of Sky Cable with another 61% eventually through an Exchangeable instrument. The Lopez group, through ABS-CBN (ABS PM), will then take a 35% interest in TV5, which could eventually rise to just below 50%.

The transactions, if they go through without regulatory hitches, should be win-win for the two groups. For PLDT, it will add value to its pay TV business and increase broadband subscriber count. This is also likely a catalyst for TV5 to actually begin making money. ABS-CBN, on the other hand, will finally be able to air and monetize its content again, after being denied a franchise extension by the Duterte administration.

The other blockbuster deal (or deals) announced overnight relate to the sale of as many as 7,000 telecom towers by Globe (GLO PM). The initial 5,700 towers will be sold for P71bn, which would simplistically translate to ~P12.4m per tower, only slightly lower than PLDT’s tower deal (P13m per tower). Upon consummation of the sale of the entire 7,000 towers, we reckon Globe’s net debt/equity will fall to around 1.2x, well below the >2x today (treating the perps as debt). Frankly, we don’t see a need for the company to proceed with a rights issue but management might decide to go ahead anyway.

We begin our results round-up today with Jollibee (JFC PM), which was a big beat. We also have numbers from Cebu Air (CEB PM) and the just-released results from Globe (GLO PM).

  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – The strong 2Q performance blew Street estimates out of the park, with operating income doubling YoY during the quarter. Same-store sales grew across all geographies (ex-China), led by the Philippines, which saw a 52% jump in SSS as mobility (and dine-in restrictions) were lifted. We estimate SSS levels and margins in the Phils are already at (or even slightly above) pre-pandemic levels even as input costs remain elevated. The Philippines is JFC’s most profitable market, thus the operating leverage from the SSS recovery is more significant than in other markets.   • Cebu Air (CEB PM) (-) – Core net losses shrunk QoQ/YoY to P2.95bn in 2Q22, with the company reporting its first positive EBITDA of P453mn since the pandemic began. It also generated cash (i.e. cash from operations + debt service) for the first time of P5.5bn in 2Q, after domestic travel restrictions were removed and systemwide pax volumes reverted to 75% of pre-pandemic levels (vs. 36% in 1Q). Fares improved QoQ largely on favorable network mix (i.e. it flew more to higher yielding domestic tourist destinations) but management indicated fares are improving further sequentially, as it is seeing more rational domestic competition so far in 3Q. Despite higher fares and seat capacity, three-month forward bookings are still rising. While 1H results are tracking behind our forecasts, management conveyed optimism of a return to pre-pandemic utilization and net profit levels by 4Q.

  • Globe (GLO PM) (=) – Core income for 1H came in at P11bn, marginally higher YoY and within expectations.  The core operations look a little soft, with 2Q revenue only growing 6%.  On the broadband side, Globe added 29k wired subscribers in the quarter while its fixed wireless business continued to shrink. The revelation that it will be launching a prepaid fiber service (which Converge also disclosed) should shake the broadband market.  We should have more information after the briefing this morning.

Apart from the above, we also had more detail on the 2Q numbers from Emperador (EMI PM) and Megaworld (MEG PM).

  • Emperador (EMI PM) (=) – The 1H22 profit of P3.140bn (+5%YoY) was buoyed by some P1.0bn in other income in the quarter which the company claims arose from the sale of scraps, molasses, etc. The underlying brandy business, however, was under pressure from higher input costs, with GP margins falling to just 25.9% in 2Q22 from 45.0% last year. Whisky’s performance somewhat made up for this, posting a normalized 20% YoY growth in income in 2Q/1H on steady growth and stable margins.

TELCOS
Converge ICT 2Q highlights
  • Revenue and income growth slow down.  Revenue for 2Q reached P8.3bn (+33%) while income for the quarter was up +16% to P1.98bn.  1H revenue growth was +36% (to P16.1bn) while income growth was +21% (to P3.95bn).  The slowdown is evident in (and the result of) the steep drop in net adds to only 15k for 2Q.
  • Guidance reduced.  Management reduced guidance for revenue growth to 25-30%.  This was +50% at the start of the year, which consensus cut to ~40% after 1Q results.   Guidance for year-end subscribers is now only 2m (vs 2.4m), which implies net adds of 308k for the entire FY22 or 183k for the rest of the year.  This would mean +18% growth in subscriber base in FY22 over FY21.
  • Gross adds down, churn up.  Gross adds for 2Q were 150k, and churn was 135k (2.2% monthly).  Gross adds were the lowest in the last two years and churn the highest (ever). Management claims churn was high because it attracted sub-optimal subscribers after deciding to stop collecting upfront fees a couple of quarters ago.  It has since reinstated upfront fees (in June).  Imposition of upfront fees, however, will normally reduce gross adds.
  • ARPUs recover slightly.  While net adds were down, ARPUs are up slightly (+3% QoQ) to P1,278.  Management didn’t say the reason for this but it may have been lifted by the upfront fees (which are amortized and form part of monthly ARPU).  Management guidance for ARPU is for it to remain stable.
  • Soft launch of prepaid fiber.  Converge said it “soft launched” a prepaid service in July.  For this service to have a meaningful uptake and impact, we believe monthly revenue (ARPU) has to be significantly lower than current levels, which contradicts the guidance for stable ARPUs.  Management expects the prepaid service to be launched within the year.
  • Consensus profit estimate for this year is P9.76bn (before 2Q results were released) and we think Converge is likely to reach just slightly above P8bn. The Street will thus likely have to cut estimates by over 15%.  Assuming ~P8bn profit for FY22, CNVRG would be trading at 18x PER.

  • The decline in net adds has been attributed to higher churn (disconnections). This is reflective of lower quality subscribers who simply are unable to pay for wired broadband on a continuing basis at current prices (P1,500/month).
TELCOS/MEDIA
PLDT group’s Cignal investing in ABS-CBN’s Sky Cable; ABS-CBN to invest in TV5
  • Cignal Cable Corp is acquiring 38.8% of Sky Cable for P2.862bn
    • From the Lopez group through Sky Vision Corp, ABS-CBN, and Lopez Inc
    • It is also investing in an Exchangeable instrument (face value of P4.388bn) to allow it to acquire the remaining 61.1% of Sky Cable
    • A small (P250m) convertible instrument of Sky Cable is also being purchased
  • Sky Cable is the second largest pay TV operator behind Cignal and the 4th largest broadband operator
    • It has 300k cable TV subscribers and 350k broadband subscribers
  • Cignal is effectively controlled by PLDT through Mediaquest, a holding company under its employee pension fund
    • PLDT has depositary instruments giving it 64% economic rights in Cignal
  • PLDT and the Lopez group intend to close the deal within this month
    • In order to avoid having to secure approval from the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC)
    • The Bayanihan 2 Act lifted the requirement for PCC review for transactions below P50bn until Sept 2022
  • The PCC will, however, still have the right to review the transaction post-closing
    • We believe there is high risk that the PCC may impose post-closing conditions (or worse)
  • The deal will have competition implications not just on pay TV but also on broadband
  • In a separate transaction, ABS-CBN announced that it is acquiring 34.99% of TV5 for P2.16bn
    • TV5 is ultimately owned by the PLDT Beneficial Trust Fund through MediaQuest
  • ABS-CBN is also investing in a convertible note (face value of P1.84bn)
    • This will allow ABS-CBN to increase its stake in TV5 to a maximum of 49.92% within the next 8 years, pending regulatory approvals
  • The deal appears to be beneficial for both parties given ABS’ extensive content library and TV5’s free-to-air franchise
