REGIS REPORT

14 November 2022

We have more results today, across a broader range of sectors, with the most notable results from Globe (GLO PM), Security Bank (SECB PM) and LT Group (LTG PM).  As has been the case for much of this results period, the beats outnumber the misses.

  • Globe (GLO PM) (=) – Excluding exceptional items, Globe’s core income for 9M22 came in at P16.0bn, slightly higher (+3%) YoY.  The results at the EBITDA and core profit line put it on track to just meet our FY22 estimates.  Mobile revenue was largely expected, up +3% YoY, while broadband revenue fell 9% with the loss of the Fixed Wireless (FW) subscribers.  Interestingly, Globe added 71k subs (net) on wired broadband for the quarter, which is more than PLDT (TEL PM) and Converge (CNVRG PM) combined.  More below and after today’s briefing.

 

  • Security Bank (SECB PM) (+) - Security Bank’s big 9M22 beat seemed of poorer quality than those of the Big Three. 9M22 NPAT of P8.6bn (+77% YoY) already exceeded Regis FY22E (P8.2bn) and was well ahead of consensus (P9.8bn). However, loan growth slowed to 12% YoY vs 15% in 2Q, with loans actually down 1.3% QoQ. Meanwhile, NIMs fell 20bps QoQ partly on asset mix (lower loans). More crucially, funding costs surged 44bps QoQ, far outpacing the 16bps increase in asset yields. This underscores SECB’s reliance on bought funds (TDs and wholesale funding), given its lower CASA base. The loan and NIM trends ran counter to the experience of the Big Three banks which saw accelerating loan growth and widening margins. The NPAT beat came largely from a 60% YoY drop in provision expense, despite NPL cover of just 94% (though higher than the 92% in 2Q). SECB will hold a briefing on Wednesday, 16 Nov at 5pm.

 

  • LT Group (LTG PM) (+) - 9M22 profit of P20.4bn (+105%YoY) is tracking ahead of estimates, although 3Q22 (P5.0bn, -44%QoQ) is down sequentially due to a return to booking provisions  (vs net reversals in 1H22) for PNB. The more important tobacco business improved 21%QoQ (P4.3bn) on the full-quarter impact of the price hikes announced in March, which offset the volume decline (7.7bn sticks, -10%QoQ, -13%YoY).

  • Megawide (MWIDE PM) (=) - Operations worsened for MWIDE in 3Q22, as net losses widened by 89% QoQ and 24% YoY. Management attributed the poor turnout to a renegotiation of construction and office contracts, which came out worse for the company.  The airport unit improved QoQ/YoY but the improvement is slower than we had anticipated. Positive catalysts are limited and leverage remains high, which could mean the stock may continue to underperform in the near term.

 

  • Max’s Group (MAXS PM) (+) - 3Q22 EBITDA more than doubled YoY due to the continued improvement in the top line. While SSSG was not immediately disclosed, we estimate that it was likely in the ~50% range coming off a low base (lockdown in 3Q21). We expect to get further detail during the company's briefing later this week. 

 

Finally, Philippine Seven (SEVN PM), whom we don’t officially cover, saw 3Q22 net profit reaching P426mn, a turnaround from the loss in the same period last year. SSSG continued to be strong at 35%, albeit slower vs the 39% in 2Q due to seasonality. SSS levels, we estimate, are now well above pre-pandemic levels and should continue to improve going into the seasonally strong 4Q.

TELCOS
Globe 3Q/9M initial comments
  • 9M headline income of P26.5bn (+48% YoY), with core income (excluding exceptional items) at P16.0bn (+3%).  Service revenue was up 4% to P118bn and EBITDA was up 6% to P60.3bn for 9M. Overall, we would describe the results as being within expectations.
  • Service revenue was up 4% for 9M despite unexciting numbers for the mobile business and a contraction in home broadband (-9%).   What helped lift revenue was the corporate data segment, which was +21% in 9M, better than PLDT’s +11%.
  • Mobile revenue market share was practically unchanged QoQ vis-à-vis PLDT (53%).  We note that 3Q saw meaningful drop (-7% QoQ) in mobile data yields.
  • Home broadband revenue contracted  due to the continuing loss of fixed wireless (FW) subscribers, which is not a big surprise.  The one clear bright spot was Globe added almost 71k subscribers to its wired broadband business on a net basis.  This is slightly more than PLDT and Converge combined (~69k).  Looking at it another way, Globe accounted for more than half of all new wired broadband subs in 3Q.
  • The sale of 1,501 of the 7,059 telecom towers Globe agreed to sell has been completed.  Of that figure, 800 were sold within 3Q and the gains also booked in that period.
  • Globe declared a dividend of P25/share for 4Q22.  While this appears lower than the P27/share paid for the previous quarters in 2022, it is the first dividend after the completion of the rights issue which increased share count by 7.8% (and raised P17bn).

BANKS
SECB 9M22 - NPAT up 77% as provision drop; PPOP +9%; Loans slow, NIMs down QoQ
  • Security Bank (SECB PM) reported 9M22 NPAT of P8.56bn, up 77%, already eclipsing Regis FY22 of P8.24bn, and well ahead of consensus of P9.8bn. However, the headline profit figure is less impressive than it seems at first glance. PPOP for 9M was up just 9% YoY, with bottomline driven largely by a 60% YoY drop in provision expense. Loan growth slowed to 12% YoY (was 15% in 2Q22), and was actually down 1.3% QoQ, in contrast to the industry’s accelerating growth. NIMs were also down 20bps QoQ, again in contrast to its competitors which showed fast-widening NIMs. While yields on loans and other earning assets expanded QoQ, deposit and funding costs rose much more sharply. All told, we reckon the beat was of much poorer quality than those of the Big Three banks. Management will host a briefing on Wed, 16 Nov at 5pm.
  • Some highlights below:
    • Loan growth decelerated to 12.4% YoY in 3Q22, from +15.3% in 2Q and actually dipped 1.3% QoQ. This goes against the accelerating growth trend seen in the industry as a whole, and seen in the Big Three banks (BDO, BPI and MBT). The Big Three also saw larger loans QoQ. According to the press release, the drop was due to corporate loans (-3% QoQ), while consumer loans were up 6% QoQ. Deposit growth was 11.7% YoY, accelerating from the 9.1% pace in 2Q.
    • Net interest income was up 7% YoY in 9M22, but up just 1% QoQ in 3Q. We estimate NIMs contracted 20bps QoQ to 4.04%, also running counter to the expanding margins seen among the Big Three banks (+15-25bps QoQ). While loan yields were up 28bps QoQ, deposit costs were up 29bps QoQ. Overall asset yields were up 16bps QoQ while total funding costs jumped an eye-watering 44bps QoQ
    • Fee incomes jumped 22% YoY in 9M22. We note, though, that fees have flattened out on a quarterly basis since 4Q21. Meanwhile, trading/fx gains were down 37% YoY as interest rates resulted in trading losses, offset by strong FX gains.
    • Opex was up just 5% in 9M22, but was accelerating 10% QoQ in 3Q. Notably, manpower costs jumped 13% YoY in 9M22, and surged 22% YoY in 3Q22 alone.
    • Provision expense fell 60% YoY in 9M22 to P1.6bn, though the bank put through P1.2bn in 3Q22 alone. We suspect there were some write-offs and foreclosures in 3Q22, as NPL ratio inched down 4bps QoQ and NPL cover edged up to 94% (from 92% in 2Q). Meanwhile, investment assets were up close to P700mn QoQ to P2.6bn (and more than double YoY). The low NPL coverage is a worry, as concerns rise over potential asset quality deterioration given high inflation, rising interest rates, and a weak PHP. For comparison, the Big Three have NPL coverage ratios of 150-170%.
  • We would expect the slowing loan growth, high funding costs, and low NPL coverage to dominate the briefing on 16 Nov.

CONSUMER
Max’s Group (MAXS PM) 9M22 NPAT rebounds to P427mn
  • MAXS reported 9M22 NPAT of P427mn, tracking ahead of Regis FY22E (P457mn). Revenues of P7.8bn (+46% YoY) are broadly in line with expectations, but margins are trending ahead.
  • 3Q22 NPAT was P146mn, a turnaround from the P159mn loss in the same period last year
  • Revenues jumped 64% YoY in 3Q, but was flattish QoQ due to seasonality
  • Raw material cost as a % of revenues rose 100bps YoY but was stable QoQ
  • The company is hosting a briefing on 18 Nov

INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide posts wider net loss in 3Q22
  • Wider net losses QoQ/YoY. Megawide’s 3Q22 net loss widened by 89% QoQ, 24% YoY to P429mn. Cumulative 9M22 net losses reached P826mn, higher by 79% YoY.  Revenues across key segments disappointed and higher input costs squeezed the construction unit’s GP margins.  While liquidity risks are now more manageable, the company’s high leverage ratio even post-asset sale and longer recovery could continue to dampen investor sentiment. 
  • Construction unit’s GP margins shrink; Collection issues remain.  The construction unit’s gross profit fell 22% QoQ and GP margins dropped 330bps QoQ to 12% as input costs rose. Collection issues remain, which could further drag profitability in succeeding periods should the company accelerate provisions.
  • Landport unit incurs losses for 4th straight quarter.  The landport unit incurred losses even at the gross profit level in 3Q, as revenues dropped 78% QoQ/83% YoY to an all-time low of P26mn. We suspect this could be related to a worsening situation with its POGO tenants.
  • Airport unit rebounds in 3Q. The airport unit improved operations marginally in 3Q22.  Revenues rose 13% QoQ, as overall pax volumes reverted to 51% of pre-pandemic levels from only 48% in 2Q. International pax volumes rebounded strongly QoQ and reverted to 20% of 2019 average.
  • Leverage remains high.  We estimate parent net debt-to-equity worsened to 5.5x in end-September.  With the expected c. P14bn net proceeds from the sale of Mactan-Cebu airport to the Aboitiz group (AEV PM) next month, we estimate its leverage ratio will drop to 3.2x.  The company recently announced plans to raise another P3-3.6bn of preferred shares on or before 2Q23 to add to its acquisition/investment “war chest”.
