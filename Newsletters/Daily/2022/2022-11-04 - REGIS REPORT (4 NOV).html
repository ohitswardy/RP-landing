The overall macro situation (pandemic rebound + inflation pressure) is manifesting itself differently in the two results released yesterday. Telecom spend is looking anemic, based on PLDT’s (TEL PM) results, while Century Pacific (CNPF PM) is posting double-digit top line growth. What’s common, though, is that cost pressures are evident.

  • PLDT (TEL PM) (+) – Core income (inclusive of losses at Voyager) amounted to P23.4bn as of 9M22, which is +7% YoY and a tad ahead of estimates. The profit outperformance does, however, look a little cosmetic, padded by lower non-cash charges. Mobile revenue remains soft as consumers are diverting spend to other essentials amidst rising prices. Net adds for the wired broadband business was only +42k for the quarter – a further slowdown from 2Q and a clear sign that saturation at the current price points is near.

  • Century Pacific (CNPF PM) (=) – 3Q22 NPAT was flat YoY even as revenues grew 13% YoY. Margins continued to be pressured by input cost inflation despite another round of adjustments during the quarter. CNPF has raised prices cumulatively by ~9% YTD, but this has only been enough to cover 50-60% of the cost inflation. It may take a few more quarters for margins to fully recover with another round of pricing planned for 4Q. In the meantime, volume growth remains healthy (+mid-single digit in 3Q) despite inflation pressures to the consumer, illustrating the defensive nature of CNPF’s core branded marine and meat business.

We published a new report on ICTSI (ICT PM), upgrading our FY22E profit forecast by 9% and reaffirming our BUY rating with a 12-month TP of P250/sh.

While the global macroeconomic outlook appears challenging, we believe ICT is well-prepared to weather the current storm, given (a) the resilience of EM economies where ICT operates in, (b) the defensive characteristics of its Origin & Destinations (O&D) port model, and (c) the relatively benign risks from higher rates and a strong USD.  We forecast organic volume growth to moderate to +2% in FY23 from a projected +6% this year. Our forecasts assume FY23 earnings growth will moderate to +8% and re-accelerate to +18% in FY24 once global macro conditions settle. 

The shares have fallen 30% from its peak and underperformed the PCOMP by 20% over this period due to exaggerated fears about ICT’s prospects. The share price movement appears to be pricing in a significant earnings decline, which we believe is unlikely to happen. At 9x FY23E PER and 6x FY23E EV/EBITDA, the shares are trading at historic lows (average of 23x PER/11x EV/EBITDA) and are at a deep discount to its peers (13x/12x). We advise investors to take this period of share price volatility as an opportunity to accumulate shares.

MACRO
BSP to raise policy rate by 75bps on November 17
  • The Monetary Board will raise the policy rate of Bangko Sentral ng PIlipinas by 0.75 percentage points to bring it to 5% when they meet on November 17, BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said on Thursday
  • Medalla said this six hours after the US Fed announced that their target range for the federal funds rate was raised to 3.75-4% from 3-3.25% that was in place since September 21
  • “The BSP deems it necessary to maintain the interest rate differential prevailing before the most recent Fed rate hike, in line with its price stability mandate and the need to temper any impact on the country’s exchange rate of the most recent Fed rate hike”
  • Since May, the MB has raised the BSP’s overnight borrowing rate by a total of 2.25 percentage points from a record low of 2%
MACRO
Government’s debt stock rises to P13.517tn in September
  • The National Government’s outstanding debt rose further to P13.517tn in September
    • This is P496bn (+3.8%) higher MoM and P1.79tn (+15.2%) higher than in end-Dec 2021
    • Fastest MoM increase this year due to the peso’s depreciation against the USD and the net issuance of govt securities to support the budget
  • Domestic debt accounted for 68.8% of total at P9.3tn (+4% MoM, +13.8% higher than in end-Dec 2021)
    • Cited net issuance of govt securities (+P352.09bn MoM) and the impact of the local currency depreciation against the USD (+5.18bn)
  • External debt stood at P4.216tn (+3.4% MoM, +18.5% higher than in end-Dec 2021)
    • Due to the P180bn impact of the local currency depreciation against the USD, which was partially offset by the P31bn effect of third-currency depreciation against the USD and net repayment of P10.8bn
    • In USD terms, external loans stood at US$71.896bn, lower by 1% MoM)
  • The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually fall to 51% by 2028
BANKS
Sumitomo to infuse P27bn of equity into RCBC
  • Sumitomo Mitsui will purchase 382mn shares of Rizal Commercial Banking Corp (RCB PM) at P71/share
    • 213mn treasury shares
    • 168mn new shares
  • RCB’s last closing price was P23.95/share
  • The equity infusion will increase Sumitomo’s ownership in RCB from 4.99% to 20%
  • The parent bank expects its CET-1 ratio to increase from 11.9% to 15.9%
  • We estimate that BVPS will increase from P47.9/share to P51.50/share
  • RCB will seek value-add from Sumitomo’s expertise in:
    • Corporate banking, sustainable finance, and project finance
    • Leasing
    • Consumer lending
    • Branch banking (digital technology and data-based sales strategies)
    • Wealth management and trust
    • Digital
