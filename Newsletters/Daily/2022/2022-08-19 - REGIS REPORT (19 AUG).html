REGIS REPORT

19 August 2022

The BSP delivered a 50bp increase in its policy rate to 3.75%, in line with consensus estimates. As mentioned yesterday, though, the 10-year BVAL is already down >100bps from the June high, indicating lower inflation expectations. The Monetary Board is scheduled to meet three more times this year (Sept, Nov, and Dec). Looking at the longer-term data though, the BSP has always been (dare we say it?) behind the curve, hiking rates well after the initial inflation spike, at times hiking even after inflation started moderating. Unsurprisingly, the market-based rates have been much quicker to react/predict inflation moves.

+ + + +

Turns out the 2Q22 results were more positive than we originally thought. Aggregate earnings of the companies we cover amounted to P181bn in 2Q22, already surpassing the peak levels in 2Q19 pre-Covid. With reopening momentum, and with possible margin expansion as commodity prices ease, we could see new record highs in coming quarters. The clear winners in 2Q22 were the consumer, banking, and retail sectors, all of whom are at or well above 2019 peak quarterly profits. The clear laggard is property with aggregate earnings still down 30-40% from their highs.

Have a look further below for some compelling charts and insights.

+ + + +

Some updates on corporate action:

Ayala Corp (AC PM) and Globe (GLO) formally announced the merger of three healthcare-related apps – KonsultaMD, HealthNow, and AIDE – to be completed by 1Q23. The three have a combined base of around 2mn customers. Globe will end up owning 45%, while AC Health will have 30%, with the 25% balance going to their partner Salud.

The SEC approved the share-for-asset swap between Robinsons Land (RLC PM) and its subsidiary RCR Commercial REIT (RCR PM). RCR will acquire 44,797sqm of office space from RLC, resulting in RLC increasing its stake in RCR from 63.5% to 66.1%. The deal was announced in April 2022.

MACRO
BSP hikes key policy rates by 50bps
  • The BSP delivered another 50bps rate hike in yesterday’s Monetary Board meeting
    • Rates on the overnight reverse repurchase facility is now at 3.75%
    • BSP has so far raised policy rates by 175bps so far this year
    • Pre-covid, the policy rate stood at 4%
    • The Fed has raised its policy rates by 225bps so far this year
  • The BSP also raised its 2022 inflation forecast to 5.4% (from 5% previously)
    • It lowered inflation projections for 2023/2024 to 4%/3.2% from 4.2%/3.3%
    • Inflation averaged 4.7% in 7M22, staying above the BSP’s 2-4% target range, after quickening to 6.4% in July from 6.1% in June

MACRO
2Q22 even more bullish than we thought
  • Not only were aggregate earnings up strongly, but also NPAT already exceeded the 2019 peak. Total profit for Regis-covered stocks for 2Q22 was P181bn, compared to the previous peak of P174bn in 2Q19. The sector pictures are more telling, with one or two surprises.
    • Power + Utilities – Profit was P31bn, more than 50% higher than the 2Q19 high. However, this includes Semirara, which is having a bumper year due to its coal operations. Excluding SCC, though, sector NPAT was still 21% higher than the 2Q19 levels.
    • Consumer/Retail – This was the big surprise. Total profit was already above pre-Covid levels even in 1Q22. (We did not include MONDE, as we only have quarterly data to 2020.) This is noteworthy, considering the margin squeeze the companies are currently under. 2H22/2023 could be massive, if demand doesn’t falter due to inflation.
    • Banks – Total profits are just about back to 2019 highs. That said, for some (MBT and SECB), the low 2Q provisions may not be sustainable. To the upside, 2H22 will likely see better loan growth and wider NIMS.
    • Telcos – Profits never really dropped. If anything, telco services were more crucial when mobility was severely restricted. Recent asset sales, though, significantly improve cashflows and debt positions.
    • Property – unsurprisingly is the real laggard. Aggregate NPAT of P20bn in 2Q22 was nearly 40% lower than the pre-Covid peak in 4Q19. To the upside, mall operators are seeing a) rising foot traffic, and b) a wind-down of rent concessions, which should see mall profits rise sharply in 2H22. The resi and office segments, though will likely take longer to recover.
    • Gaming – could this be THE reopening sector (ok, fine, it’s just BLOOM). But check out the company’s profits in 2Q22. And that’s without huge junket business from North Asia.
