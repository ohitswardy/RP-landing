REGIS REPORT

07 June 2022

The CPI inflation print for May came in at 5.4%, an acceleration from the 4.9% in April but within consensus expectations. Unsurprisingly, food and transport costs were the main contributors to the higher inflation print.

+++

Consumer loans turned the corner in 1Q22, growing 0.7% YoY, the first positive print since 4Q20. Overall consumer NPLs also improved, falling 32bps QoQ to 8.99%. Growth was driven by credit cards, with balances jumping 11.5% YoY, likely due to the reopening of domestic and international travel. Oddly, mortgage growth slowed to 5.3% YoY (vs 7.9% in 4Q21), running counter to banks’ commentaries during their 1Q22 briefings. Motor Vehicle loans were still down 15.8% YoY, as maturities/paydowns continue to outpace new loans; we expect balances to turn positive by 3Q22.

The recovery is positive, not only for bank margins, but also for overall demand for residential property, autos, and broad consumption.

+++

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) implemented retail price increases of 1–2% a few days ago., the third such increase over the past seven months. The price adjustment is meant to address margin pressure from rising vehicles prices, aggravated by the PHP’s weakness.  TMP’s gross margin in 1Q was 10%, lower than it was historically (closer to 12%).

TMP accounts for ~20% of GT Capital’s (GTCAP PM) profit and NAV.

+++

There were three important contracts relating to the power sector that simultaneously expired over the weekend.

First was the fuel supply contract for the 1,200MW Ilijan power plant.  With Malampaya no longer supplying Ilijan, the plants of First Gen (FGEN PM) will now get their full allocation, and their output will no longer be curtailed because of the lack of supply from Malampaya. It also means Ilijan will have to run on diesel until such time LNG supply is available.  We expect LNG supply to become available half a year from now, barring delays,

The two other contracts relate to the actual ownership and operation of the Ilijan power plant itself.  The BOT arrangement of KEPCO-TEAM Energy expired and the plant was supposed to have been turned over to the government via the power agency PSALM.  Also expiring at the same time was the IPP Administrator (IPPA) agreement over the facility, which is supposed to trigger a transfer of the power plant to San Miguel (SMC PM).

However, with the payment dispute between PSALM and SMC unresolved, it’s unclear who will operate the facility for now. 

+++

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor and incoming Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno says he does not plan to reduce expenditures to address the budget deficit.  Instead, he intends to rely on improved tax collections, even though he plans no new taxes.

BANKS
PH Banks - Consumer loans - 1Q22 back in +ve growth territory; NPLs improving
  • Consumer loans continue to recover in 1Q22, posting 0.7% YoY growth, back in positive territory for the first time since end-2020.
    • This was despite a surprise deceleration in mortgages, which grew 5.3% YoY, down from 7.9% in 4Q21. In fact, mortgages were down 1.5% QoQ, the first QoQ decline since mid-2015. We suspect there may have been a bank that missed the deadline for submission.
    • Consumer NPL ratio also improved o 8.99%, down 32bps QoQ from 9.31% in 4Q21, and down from the peak of 9.90% in 3Q21.
  • Growth in 1Q22 was led by credit card (CC) receivables, which jumped 11.5% YoY, following four quarters of decline. In absolute PHP terms, the P446bn balance was just a shade below the all-time high posted in 1Q20 just before the pandemic hit. Credit card NPLs also improved to 6.54%, down 24bps from 6.78% in 4Q21 (the peak was 8.85% in 4Q20).
    • The positive spin would be that CC usage is up sharply with the reopening of international and domestic tourism, as well as overall consumer confidence as the economy reopens. The negative spin would be that consumers are under some stress given high inflation. We will be watching the growth and NPL figures closely in coming quarters.
  • As mentioned, mortgages grew 5.3% YoY, though balances were down 1.5% QoQ. NPL ratios here were flat at 9.37%, from 9.36% the quarter prior.
  • Motor vehicle (MV) loans were still down 15.8% YoY and down 2.0% QoQ.
    • While banks are anecdotally loosening credit standards, the balances are still shrinking due to the faster roll-off of existing loans. Banks also noted the lack of supply of vehicles, owing to the global supply bottlenecks.
    • We expect MV loan balances to stabilize/grow sequentially in 2Q/3Q22.
    • To the upside, NPL ratios improved to 11.42% from 12.01% in 4Q21.
  • Based on banks’ earnings calls, the industry seems to be ready to re-grow its consumer loan portfolio. This could boost NIMs, given the higher yields on consumer loans compared to corporate / commercial / mid-market loans. Higher willingness to lend could also be positive for residential sales, auto sales, and even broad consumption.

 

 

POWER
Malampaya supply contract for Ilijan’s 1,200MW expires
  • The 1,200MW Ilijan plant operated by KEPCO is no longer getting gas from Malampaya beginning June 6 after the gas-offtake agreement expired
  • There was reportedly no keen interest on the part of PSALM or San Miguel to renew the gas contract with Malampaya
  • The plant’s build-operate-transfer (BOT) contract is also supposed to lapse this month, and turnover talks are reportedly being sorted with SMC Global Power’s South Premier Power Corporation which is its IPPA
    • Under PSALM’s privatization terms for supply contracts, the ownership of the plant has to be turned over to the IPPA at zero value upon expiration of the BOT provided all outstanding obligations are mutually resolved
    • On the part of San Miguel group, it is already looking at a timeline of bringing in imported LNG by August this year, which should fuel the gas-fired facilities including Ilijan
  • The expiration of Ilijan’s contract means First Gen get full allocation of gas again, even with the declining output from Malampaya
    • Its San Gabriel power plant has largely been derated (210MW from 420MW) over the last few months because of gas restrictions, weighing on FGEN’s earnings over the last two quarters
  • Ilijan’s lack of gas supply over the next few months could reduce the grid’s capacity by 800MW or put pressure on prices as it sources replacement power
