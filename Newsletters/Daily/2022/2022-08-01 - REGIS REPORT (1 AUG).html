The 2Q earnings season is now fully underway. Among those whose results we cover today is Universal Robina (URC PM), which delivered operating results better than Street expectations (albeit in line with ours).

  • Universal Robina (URC PM) (=) – 2Q22 EBIT was flattish (-3% YoY) as URC bore the brunt of peak input cost (e.g. wheat, palm oil). While URC was able to raise prices by ~12% cumulatively since 3Q21, these adjustments were not enough to fully mitigate the impact of input cost inflation. Volumes remained surprisingly healthy despite the sizeable price adjustments, but management expects growth to moderate in the coming quarters. We believe margins are likely to stabilize at these levels as input prices come off the peak, but margin recovery could be protracted given (1) the depreciation of the PHP and other currencies vs the USD and (2) supply chain/logistics costs, which remain elevated.

  • Aboitiz Power (AP PM) (=) – AP’s 2Q22 core income rebounded to P6.23bn (+111%QoQ, 59%YoY), boosted by P2.5bn in income contributions from both units of GNPower Dinginin (2x668MW). Overall plant availability improved, with GMEC, TVI and Pagbilao plants largely back online in 2Q. We expect 2H earnings to be much better on account of rising contributions from GNPD and a  recovery in margins for its distribution segment.

  • Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) (-) - Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) swung to a net loss of P528mn in 2Q22.  EBITDA fell 17% YoY P1.077bn in 2Q, after volumes dropped 11% YoY.  This brought 1H22 EBITDA to P2.1bn, down 9% YoY and short of street estimates.  Risks to earnings remain skewed to the downside in the near-term, with (a) higher input costs pressures and (b) the potential removal of the safeguard duty in 4Q.

JG Summit (JGS PM) disclosed that it sold 36m shares of Meralco (MER PM) in an overnight placement, trimming its stake in the latter to 26.4% (from 29.6%).  The sale was done at a 6% discount to Thursday’s close and raised P12.4bn for the firm.

Given the size of the transaction in this soft market,  it was not a surprise that the main buyer of this deal was Metro Pacific (MPI PM).  MPI bought 22.5m shares of MER, lifting its stake to 47.5% from 45.5%.  From a fundamental perspective, the deal is likely to deliver value enhancement for MPI, especially in the wake of the recent regulatory rulings on MER’s tariffs.  However, it also simultaneously bolsters the view that MPI has become even more of a Meralco proxy as the share of Meralco in MPI NAV is now 65%.

From a technical perspective, however, the deal will also likely a create (or reinforce) the perception of an overhang on MER’s share price.  JGS still owns 26.4%. and this transaction will remind market investors that it may sell that stake (in part or full) anytime.

As we expected, the Exchange announced that Semirara (SCC PM) will replace Security Bank (SECB PM) following the semi-annual review of the benchmark 30-member PSEi (aka “PCOMP”). Union Bank (UBP PM), which others predicted will enter the same index, will not do so.  The changes will take effect at the close of business on Friday (Aug 5) so one can expect some activity in the run-up to this.  SCC has risen 19% since we first predicted it would become part of the PSEi so one might have to be wary that it might not see the usual pop from PSEi inclusion on rebalancing day.

Beyond the entry of SCC and exit of SECB from the PSEi, there are also likely to be increased weightings for Ayala Corp (AC PM), ACEN (ACEN PM), Converge (CNVRG PM) and Monde (MONDE PM).  Most of the rest will see modest reductions in their weightings as a consequence.

INFRASTRUCTURE
Cemex PH: 2Q volumes drop 11% YoY; FY22 volume guidance cut
  • 2Q22 EBITDA drops YoY, posts net loss.  Cemex (CHP PM) swung to a net loss of P528mn in 2Q22, after sales volumes dropped 11% YoY. This brings CHP’s 1H22 cumulative net loss to P266mn. 1H22 EBITDA stood at P2.12bn, down 9% YoY and trending BEHIND street expectations (FY22E: P4.6bn) but in line with ours (P3.6bn).  The near-term outlook remains uninspiring, with management signaling upward cost pressures on fuel, power, and distribution costs, as well as the possible non-extension of the safeguard duty.
  • Volumes drop 11% in 2Q, 8% in 1H; FY22 guidance revised down to “flat to low single-digit percentage decrease”.  CHP’s sales volumes fell 11% YoY and were up just slightly (+1%) QoQ.  Management blamed the election ban and bad weather, and consequently downgraded its FY22 volume growth guidance to “flat to low single-digit percentage decrease” from “mid-single digit percentage increase”.  With volumes in 1H down by 8% YoY, management expects volumes to recover strongly in 2H.
  • 2Q ASPs up 9% YoY, 3% QoQ, broadly in line with FX movements and the slight uptick in landed costs.  We estimate the landed cost of bagged cement were flat YoY and were up 2% QoQ to US$68/MT while import volumes of third party cement traders dropped 31% YoY.  We believe competitive dynamics neither improved nor deteriorated during the quarter. Management still awaits the government’s decision on the extension of the safeguard duty, which accounts for c. 5% of import prices.
  • EBITDA margin likely to fall further in 2H.  EBITDA fell 17% YoY to P1.08bn in 2Q22, with EBITDA margin dropping 3ppts to 20%. Even though fuel costs per MT jumped 89% YoY, CHP’s power costs and distribution costs were broadly unchanged, thanks to favorable contracts. Management however noted that its power suppliers are negotiating to change its prices upwards, while the impact of higher diesel prices will start kicking in 2H. Costs will also increase in 2H, in line with its planned maintenance activities for its two plants.
