REGIS REPORT

07 October 2022

The government raised US$2bn through a three-tranche global bond offering overnight.  The yields on these new issues are not only higher (around +200bp) than the previous bond issue/s, but the spreads over US Treasuries have also widened between 30bp and 40bp.  More below.

+++

San Miguel (SMC PM) says it will pay cash for the acquisition of Eagle Cement (EAGLE PM).  Assuming it does intend to pay cash for all 5.0bn shares of Eagle Cement, it would mean SMC will need to fork out P110bn, or close to US$2bn.   Raising that amount to fund the purchase will be challenging, in our view in this environment.  Beyond just securing government approvals, we would not be surprised to see delays in the completion of (or even revisions to) this transaction.  The apparently wide merger arbitrage opportunity offered by the gap between the traded price (PhP19.30/share) and the expected tender offer price (PhP22.02/share) does not appear irrational to us.

On an unrelated matter, SMC also says it will continue to provide power to Meralco (MER PM) for 60 days until the termination of its fixed price power supply agreement (PSA) takes effect.  SMC is terminating these PSAs (which have seven years more to run) because it’s losing money from higher fuel prices.  In theory, the unilateral termination could trigger penalties amounting to over P250bn (>US$4bn).

Will Meralco even chose to hold SMC to these PSAs and contest their termination? Or will it acquiesce to SMC and try to pass the higher costs to consumers? If they do try to collect from SMC, SMC will surely contest any penalties and drag this all the way to the Supreme Court.

+++

Most asset quality indicators – NPLs, NPL cover, Restructured Loans (RLs) – improved again in August. However, Past Due (PD) loan ratios ticked up a bit… not a lot, but PDs are more of a leading indicator of stress, and do bear close watch. PD ratio crept up 2bps MoM to 4.19% from 4.17% in July. In absolute terms, PDs grew 1.0% MoM or close to P5bn over July. Meanwhile, NPL ratio (ex-interbank) improved 6bps MoM to 3.62%, with the nominal amount down 0.5% MoM. RL ratio dropped much more significantly, down 20bps MoM to 2.70%, with more than P22bn leaving the RL classification, presumably moving back to Stage 1 (performing) category. NPL cover improved a smidge to 100.0% from 99.2% in July.

Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) improved again to 9.5% YoY in August, compared to 9.2% in July. This is now the 17th consecutive month of accelerating loan growth. Loans were up 1.1% MoM.

While we cheer the continued asset quality gains, we will be watching the PD rates closely for early signs of borrower stress given the inflation / weak PHP environment.

MACRO
Gov’t raises US$2bn in fixed rate global offering
  • The Republic of the Philippines raised US$2bn in a three-tranche fixed rate global bond offering
    • Follows previous fund raising exercises: JPY70bn Samurai bond offering in April 2022 and US$2.25bn bond offering in March 2022
  • US$500mn, 5-year tranche was priced at US Treasury plus 120bps with a coupon of 5.17%, 194bps higher than March offering
    • Yield stood at 5.119%, 105bps spread over US treasury (vs. spread of 74.37bps over US treasuries for March auction)
  • US$750mn, 10.5 year tranche was priced at US Treasury plus 185bps with a coupon of 5.609%, 205bps higher than March
    • Yield stood at 5.546%, 172bps spread over US treasury (vs. spread of 135bps over US treasuries for March auction)
  • US$750mn, 25-year Sustainability tranche was priced at 6.1% with a coupon of 5.95%, 190bps higher than March
    • The 25-year Global Bonds marks the country’s third ESG G3 bond offering
  • The government’s debt as of end-August stood at P13.02tn, of which 69% was domestic and 31% was external
    • The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% this year and gradually decline to 51% by 2028

CONGLOS
SMC to acquire Eagle Cement in cash transaction, to launch tender offer for minorities
  • SMC told the stock exchange that as a result of the planned acquisition of 88.5% of Eagle Cement, the buyer is required to conduct a tender offer of the 11.5% shares held by the target company’s minority shareholders
  • SMC said the acquisition of the Eagle Cement shares will be paid in cash
  • It added that the closing of the transaction is subject to approval by the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC)
  • It intends to file a request for exemption with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) to allow the tender offer to start after the PCC approval
POWER
SMC will keep supplying Meralco power under current PSA for next 60 days
  • San Miguel confirmed that it had received the ERC order dated September 29 denying its petition for a temporary rate increase
  • It said that its subsidiaries will keep supplying power to Meralco for next 60 days “at a cost higher than the contracted price under the PSAs”
  • Once the termination of the PSAs take effect, SPPC and SMEC will have to sell its power to WESM and enter into bilateral contracts with off-takers
  • SPPC and SMEC will continue to explore other legal remedies relating to the ERC orders
