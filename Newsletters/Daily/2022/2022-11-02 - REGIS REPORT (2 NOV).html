REGIS REPORT

02 November 2022

Banks still seem to be the clear “reopening theme” winner.

Loan growth for the commercial banking system accelerated to +13.4% YoY in September, from +12.2% in August. As before, we caution that the data is bloated by the BPI Family Bank merger in Jan. However, this does suggest that the All Banks loan growth will likely come in at above 10.5%, compared to 9.6% in August, which would put it at the fastest clip since 1H2019.

Margins are also expanding, and at a faster clip than we were expecting. Both BPI (BPI PM) and BDO (BDO PM) reported 3Q/9M results that blew REGIS and consensus numbers out of the water. On the surface, the NIM increases were already impressive enough, at +15bps QoQ for BPI and +24bps QoQ for BDO. But we reckon the “exit NIMs” (i.e. vs the average for the quarter) were even higher, considering a) the BSP only started aggressively raising policy rates in July, and b) there is typically a 3-6 month lag for loan repricing.

On the back of these, we upgraded BPI earnings by 14-16% across our 2022-24E forecast range, and increased our target price to P126/sh (for 34% potential total return). Given similar story at BDO (and potentially the whole sector), it wouldn’t be unreasonable to expect the same trends. Click the link for Rafa’s (rafael.garchitorena@regis.ph) piece on BPI.

Results season is now proceeding in earnest. We cover the interim numbers from BDO (BDO PM), Meralco (MER PM) and Cemex PH (CHP PM) today:

  • BDO Unibank (BDO PM) (+) – NPAT for 9M22 was up +23% to P40bn, with P16b for 3Q alone (+46% YoY). This is clearly trending ahead of both Regis and Street estimates. While fee income was the primary driver of the beat, the positive momentum for net interest income is what is likely to drive upgrades. The bank will hold an earnings call tomorrow. In the meantime, check out the detail provided so far below.
  • Meralco (MER PM) (+) – The core distribution business looks quite healthy with YT-Sept volume growth of +6.3%, with 3Q alone at +7%. The key source of outperformance, however, was the generation segment, particularly Pacific Light, which brought 9M core income to P19.6bn (+9% YoY). The Singapore-based genco delivered what appeared to be another record quarter. We note as well that Meralco has (a) neither reversed provisions relating to the lapsed period nor (b) even ceased provisions for tariffs on current operations, demonstrating conservatism with regards to its financial reports.

BANKS
Bank lending growth picks up to 13.4% YoY in September
  • Outstanding loans by U/KBs, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, rose by 13.4% YoY in September
    • Faster than August’s 12.2% and fastest pace in 27 months
    • Fueled by loans to the following sectors: real estate (+16%), manufacturing (+16%), trade (+11%)
    • Consumer loans sustained momentum, rising by 21% YoY, with both credit card (+26%) and auto loans (+4%) accelerating
  • Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector jumped by 9.6% in August
    • U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022
    • We are forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E
  • Meanwhile, domestic liquidity growth (M3) decelerated 5.0% in September (from 6.7% in August)

BDO
BDO 9M22 P40bn +23% well AHEAD on NIMs, fees
  • BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported 9M22 NPAT of P40bn, up 23% YoY. For 3Q22 alone, NPAT was P16bn, surging 46% YoY. The results are WELL AHEAD of Regis (P48bn) and Street (P49bn) forecasts for FY22. On the surface, the key beats came from fee incomes, and from FX gains which overwhelmed the trading losses. While NII looked in line, the momentum is clearly building on a QoQ basis, and will likely also surpass our FY estimates. Asset quality also improved with better NPLs and NPL cover. Most remarkable, in our view, is that the Restructured Loans (RLs) have dropped to about 3.7%, down from 4.9% in 2Q22, and from a peak of 5.75% in 2Q21.
  • Some other highlights:
    • “Customer loans” grew 10% YoY, up from the 9.2% recorded in June. The press release did not say which segments led the growth. Deposits grew 11%, but was due largely to TDs, which were up 27% YoY and 23% QoQ as wealthier customers demanded higher rates. CASA ratio dipped to 82% from 85% in 2Q.
    • NIMs expanded 24bps QoQ to 4.23%, which saw NII surge 17% YoY in 3Q22 alone (+11% for 9M). Loan yields jumped 38bps QoQ to 5.67%, far outpacing the 18bps increase in average deposit costs to 0.42%, which is still an industry low. As benchmark interest rates continue to rise, we expect the NIM expansion trend to continue into 4Q22 and into 2023.
    • Fee incomes grew 22% YoY in 3Q22, and 18% in 9M. This was driven primarily by service charges, which surged 37% YoY in 3Q and 30% in 9M. Trust fees were up 7% in 9M, while insurance premiums grew 6.7% in the same period. BDO had a monster P3bn in FX gains in 3Q alone, dwarfing the P1.1bn trading loss.
    • Opex accelerated to 12.2% in 3Q22, driven by a 20% YoY surge in manpower costs for the quarter.
    • Provision expense stood at P12.2bn in 9M22, up 16.5%, equivalent to a credit cost of about 64bps. Asset quality improved across the board. NPL ratio eased to 2.23%, from 2.39% in 2Q22, and NPL cover improved to 152% from 138% in 2Q. Most impressively, RL ratio dropped sharply to 3.7% from around 4.9% in 2Q. In nominal terms, RLs dropped to P92bn from P118bn in 2Q22, and from a high of P143bn in 2Q21.
  • Overall a solid set of results. Management will hold a briefing on Thursday, 03 November at 2pm

INFRASTRUCTURE
Cemex Philippines’ 3Q22 EBITDA plunges 33% YoY, 38% QoQ
  • EBITDA collapses in 3Q.  Cemex Philippines (CHP PM) reported a wider net loss of P552mn in 3Q22, taking 9M22 net losses to P819mn.  3Q22 EBIT plunged 63% YoY, 66% QoQ to P208mn while EBITDA collapsed 33% YoY, 38% QoQ to P671mn.  The results are trending well below estimates due to lower than expected sales volumes and higher cash costs.  
  • Sales volumes drop 16% YoY in 3Q.  CHP’s sales volumes dropped by another 16% YoY in 3Q22, which management attributed to both weak demand and intense competition. The company revised its FY22 guidance yet again to “mid-single digit percentage decrease” from “flat to low single digit decrease” in July and from “low single digit increase” at the start of the year. Management estimates industry volumes rose slightly in 3Q, as third party importers gained share.
  • 3Q22 ASP rises 12% YoY; Cement/clinker export tax in Vietnam by Jan 2023.  ASPs in 3Q22 alone rose by 12% YoY, after CHP raised prices by another 4% QoQ. We estimate this hike follows FX movements and the slight increase in the landed cost of imported cement.  Domestic ASPs, however, could find it more difficult to raise prices in 4Q, as the safeguard duty, which we estimate accounts for 5% of landed cost, is removed. To the upside, Vietnam is reportedly implementing a 5-10% tax on clinker and cement exports beginning January 2023, which could give domestic manufacturers room to raise prices. 
  • EBITDA margin plunges to 13%.  EBITDA margin dropped by 6ppts to 13% in 3Q22, as cash costs per MT rose faster than ASP.  Fuel and power costs surged, as fuel hedges expired and power contracts were revised upwards. Management also flagged it intends to shut down both its plants in 4Q for maintenance.
  • Trading below replacement cost but headwinds aplenty.  Cemex trades at US$55 EV per MT, well below replacement cost.  At 5x FY22E EV/EBITDA, the shares are also inexpensive relative to peers in ASEAN.  However, we believe risks to earnings remain skewed to the downside, given higher input costs pressures, soft demand, and intense competition.
