REGIS REPORT

15 August 2022

We have a whole slew of 1H/2Q results this morning, and, similar to what we’ve seen for much of the 2Q season, the beats outnumber the misses.

  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) (+) – Ayala Corp reported 1H profit of P16.3bn, up 56% YoY with 2Q earnings alone at P8.5bn (+68%).  While there is evident improvement for many key units in 2Q, the outperformance vs Street expectations (P29.6bn for FY22E) is more a result of the large divestments, one at BPI and one at Globe.  The company also disclosed that it spent close to P12bn adding another 3.2% stake in subsidiary Ayala Land (ALI PM).  More below.
  • Globe (GLO PM) (=) – The core telco business of Globe wasn’t as sprightly as we had been expecting for 2Q, but revenues (+6% in 2Q) were lifted by non-telco sources.  Overall, core income of P11bn was up a modest 3%.  The company shed more light on the announced sale of ~7,000 of its ~12,000 towers, which is likely to raise close to P90bn (before tax) and be very value-enhancing. Operationally, Globe also revealed that it launched a prepaid fiber WiFi as one way of tapping lower-income users.

  • LT Group (LTG PM) (+) – While 1H profit of P15.4bn (+313%YoY) is tracking ahead of estimates, we consider the beat to be low in quality. Profits from tobacco fell 16%QoQ despite the price hikes announced in March, which suggest the actual hikes could have been implemented later in the quarter. PNB’s profits jumped nearly 3x from 1Q22 as it reversed P3.7bn in loan loss provisions and booked a gain of P5.2bn on the sale of ROPA.

  • Alliance Global (AGI PM) (+) – Profits in 2Q22 jumped 11%QoQ to P4.3bn, bringing 1H slightly ahead of estimates. The outperformance came on the back of 1) an acceleration in casino GGR (25%QoQ), particularly from the mass segment (75%QoQ) and 2) Golden Arches keeping margins steady despite higher raw material costs.

  • Manila Water (MWC PM) (=) – 2Q22 profit jumped 27% YoY to P1.82bn, buoyed by a tariff adjustment for accumulated FX losses in prior periods (FCDA) after it took the position to revert to the old concession agreement. The Department of Finance failed to submit the Letter of Undertaking by June 30, which was necessary for the implementation of the revised concession agreement. The reversion to the old agreement, however, is by no means certain given that President Marcos Jr. has yet to appoint the MWSS Board, which will ultimately decide on this issue.

  • First Gen (FGEN PM) (=) – FGEN saw a marked improvement in 2Q earnings (+19%QoQ) on improved fuel availability for its natural gas plants. Although gas restrictions have been resolved beginning June, the government’s sale of “banked” gas to San Miguel (SMC PM) raise the possibility that its allocation from Malampaya might again be reduced.

  • Megawide (MWIDE PM) (-) – MWIDE incurred a larger net loss of P227mn in 2Q22, bring 1H22 losses to P397mn.  We attribute the shortfall to slower revenue bookings for its construction and land port units. Revenues for the airport unit rebounded to 46% of pre-Covid levels in 2Q22 from only 24% in 1Q, but are also trending behind forecasts. We hope to get further details if and when management holds its 2Q briefing (no schedule yet).
  • Max’s Group (MAXS PM) (+) – MAXS swung to profitability in 2Q22 from practically breakeven levels in 2Q21 and 1Q22. Much like the rest of the sector, revenues saw a sharp jump (+58%) in 2Q22 as dine-in and mobility restrictions were lifted. Operating leverage drove EBITDA margins back up to above pre-pandemic levels despite elevated input costs. Further detail is expected after the company’s briefing on 17th Aug.

Meanwhile, 7-Eleven operator Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) saw SSSG jump to 39% in 2Q22, bring sales nearly back to pre-pandemic levels. Stores located within the vicinities of schools and offices (25-30% of total stores) are still underperforming. Sales levels, however, should improve further in the coming months as schools resume classes onsite. There was a margin windfall in 2Q from inventory loading ahead of price increases by suppliers.

CONGLOS
Ayala Corp 1H/2Q results
  • Ayala Corp (AC PM) reported net income of P16.3bn for 1H22, up +56% YoY. Earnings for 2Q alone were P8.5bn, +68% YoY and also a further improvement from 1Q22 (+8%). There’s a recovery evident across operating units in 2Q (vs 1Q22) for banking, property and even power. However, we would describe AC’s results as being above estimates principally because of divestments at two of its key operating companies. BPI booked a large gain from the sale of property where one of its branches used to be located (2Q22), while Globe sold half of its data center business (in 1Q22)
  • Among the big segments, BPI’s numbers were the most unambiguously positive, with loan growth rising, interest margins widening and asset quality improving. The bottom line was further boosted by a P3.7bn post-tax gain on a property sale. Ayala Land’s results were not as clear cut despite the improvement in the leasing business (especially malls). The residential business is seeing a rise in back-outs (cancellations), casting doubt on the health of residential property market which accounts for >50% of ALI’s earnings
  • ACEN saw a marked profit improvement in 2Q due to improved plant availability, but not quite enough to offset the disappointing 2Q. The firm unveiled an ambitious plant to have 20,000MW of renewable capacity by 2030, 6x the current level. Globe’s results, on the other hand, were also mixed, with service revenue for its telco business falling short. The divestment of half of its data center business boosted 1Q/1H profit, and the just-announced sale of a large part of telecom tower portfolio should lift 2H.
  • Not a lot of detail on the unlisted business was provided. The company, however, did say that AC Health is now above breakeven (in addition to being EBITDA positive since last year) and is expected report its first full-year profit this year. AC Motors, which had been posting losses consistently over recent years, was also in the black in 1H.
  • Ayala Corp also disclosed that it acquired another 468.7m shares (3.2%) of Ayala Land last week, at a price of P25.2/share (P11.82bn or ~US$210m). Together with the impact of the impending property-for-share swap with ALI, we estimate AC’s stake in ALI will rise from 46.3% to 50.5%.
RETAIL
Philippine Seven (SEVN PM) 2Q22 NPAT jumps 3.5x QoQ as SSSG accelerates
  • SEVN reported 2Q22 NPAT of P691mn, up 3.5x QoQ, and a turnaround from the P103mn net loss in the previous year. This brings 1H22 NPAT to P890mn from a P403mn net loss in 1H21
  • Revenues jumped 48% YoY during the quarter as SSSG accelerated to +39% vs +16% in 1Q22
  • We estimate SSS levels are just slightly below pre-pandemic levels, but is likely to improve further in the coming quarters as schools resume onsite classes
    • ~10% of SEVN’s stores are located near schools and universities
  • Operating margin expanded to 7%, tracking above pre-pandemic levels
    • Margins were partly boosted by inventory loading ahead of price increases by suppliers
  • SEVN ended the period with 3,241 stores, a net increase of 168 stores since end-2021, on track to meet (or even exceed) its 2022 target of at least 300 new stores
  • The company has also rolled out over 1,800 cash-recycling ATMs (2022 target: 2,800 ATMs)

CONSUMER
Max’s Group (MAXS PM) 1H22 NPAT jumps to P281mn, ahead of expectations
  • MAXS posted 2Q22 NPAT of P239mn, a turnaround from the near-breakeven levels in 2Q21 and 1Q22. This brought the 1H22 cumulative profit to P281mn, tracking ahead of our FY22 expectations
  • Revenues jumped 58% YoY in 2Q22 as mobility and dine-in restrictions were lifted. SSSG was not disclosed
  • EBITDA margin was 22.2% during the quarter, tracking well above pre-pandemic levels (19.4% in 2Q19)
  • We hope to get further detail during the company’s briefing on 17 Aug -- Registration link

INFRASTRUCTURE
Megawide 2Q22 net loss widens QoQ/YoY
  • Net loss to common widened by 246% to P397mn 1H22, trending behind Regis FY22E (+P85mn) and street forecast (+P181mn)
    • We attribute the underperformance to slower revenue bookings for the construction, airport, and landport units
  • Net loss in 2Q alone also widened QoQ/YoY to P227mn, as construction and landport revenues dropped QoQ/YoY
    • Airport unit’s revenues rebounded to 46% of pre-Covid levels in 2Q22 from 24% in 1Q
    • Pax volumes reverted to 48% of pre-Covid levels (vs. 21% in 1Q) driven largely by domestic (65% of pre-Covid in 2Q)
  • Net debt to equity stood at 5.5x (treating prefs as debt), up from 4.77x in end-2021 due to higher working capital loans
  • Our NAV-based TP is P7.20/sh, of which the airport unit accounts for P3.87/sh
