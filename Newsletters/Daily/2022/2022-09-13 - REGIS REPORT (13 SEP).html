REGIS REPORT

13 September 2022

It’s not always nice to be proven right.

Various parts of the country were hit with power outages yesterday, as the country’s main Luzon electricity grid was placed under “Red Alert” by the National Grid Corp. The immediate reason for the electricity shortage was un-scheduled shutdowns of power plants. However, reserve margins were already thin, with available capacity of 10,727MW against a peak demand of 10,585MW.

There are, however, underlying structural concerns. Remember that electricity demand is seasonally low now, as we are in the rainy/cool season. And electricity demand is growing around 6% so far this year.

We have been warning of a tightening situation in the country’s power supply situation for a while now (relevant notes here, here, and here). While the country’s headline capacity is more than 18,000MW, on average just 13,000MW is available. Peak demand in 2Q22 (our summer) was close to 12,500MW. And with the ban on new coal plants still in place, we will not see new baseload capacity until 2024/25. And while the country is building out lots of solar farms, without batteries, the actual output is a fraction of the rated capacity.

With spot electricity prices already at P9/kwh, the situation clearly favors generation companies who have significant un-contracted capacity. The big winner is Semirara (SCC PM) with about 60% of its capacity exposed to the WESM prices, followed by Aboitiz Power (AP PM) at 15% and First Gen (FGEN PM) at 8%. Near-term, ACEN (ACEN PM) will be hurt given their short position. However, elevated prices would make the whole RE sector much more viable longer-term.

The power shortage, though, could be an unwelcome risk to the country’s economic growth. The most expensive power is no power.

The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) rejected all bids for the 91-day and 364-day T-Bills, as bidders put in high rates in anticipation of large policy rate hikes by the US Fed and the BSP next week (21 and 22 Sept, respectively). Had the BTr fully awarded these tenors, the rates would have risen by more than 100bps for the 91-day, and more than 60bps for the 364-day T-Bills.

+ + +

Mr Fernando Zobel de Ayala formally resigned all his positions at the Ayala Group to focus on his health. He has been on medical leave for an undisclosed condition since 15 August, with Mr Cezar (Bong) Consing acting as President and CEO of Ayala Corp (AC PM). It is unclear at this stage if Mr Consing’s appointment will be made permanent, and if so, whether he will pursue the strategic changes that Mr Zobel de Ayala was pursuing, including a major strategic rebalancing of AC’s portfolio.

POWER
Brownouts hit Luzon as 7 power plants shut down
  • Power interruptions were experienced in Nueva Ecija, Aurora, Pampanga and Zambales after the National Grid of the Philippines (NGCP) placed the Luzon grid under red alert on Monday afternoon
    • Heightening the yellow alert status declared in the morning
    • Yellow alert is issued when the excess power is insufficient to meet the transmission grid’s regulating and contingency requirements
    • Red alert is placed under a grid area when the electricity supply is insufficient to meet demand
  • As of 10am Monday, the Luzon grid’s available capacity stood at 10,727MW while peak demand was 10,585MW
  • At least 3,627MW were lost from the grid after seven power plants went on forced outage
    • These include units of GN Power, Calaca, Masinloc, Sual, Quezon Power and Avion
  • WESM has been averaging >P9/kWh in recent days
