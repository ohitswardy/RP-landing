REGIS REPORT

19 September 2022

The government Land Transportation and Franchising Regulatory Board (LTFRB) approved fare increases for various modes of transport effective Oct 4. The fare increases range from a low of 9% to a high of 23% from current fares. Apart from jeepney fares, the LTFRB has held back on raising fares for nearly all other modes of public transport (including buses, taxes and ride-shares) until this action. This is despite the surge in fuel price since Feb.

There should be inevitable upward pressure on CPI inflation next month even with transport services representing only 4.5% of the CPI basket. Headline inflation may have dipped marginally in Aug, but the period of elevated price pressures is not over.

+++

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) revised its estimates for the country’s BOP accounts for this year and next, raising BOP deficit expectations for 2022 while leaving 2023 forecasts little changed.

The BSP now expects the BOP deficit to be US$8.4bn (from US$6.3bn) this year, equivalent to 2.0% of GDP (1.5%). The projected current account (CA) deficit is now US$20.6bn (vs US$19.6bn), 5% of GDP. The BSP’s BOP and CA deficit projections have progressively widened over the course of the year.

Beyond just the impact of the interest rate differentials, the BOP and CA deficits are among the fundamental macro factors contributing to the weakness of the PHP. The PHP has weakened over 10% since the start of the year.

MACRO
BOP/Current account deficits balloon in 1H22; BSP raises deficit forecasts
  • The government’s balance of payments position stood at a deficit of US$3.1bn in 1H22, wider than the US$1.9bn deficit in 1H21
    • The current account deficit was almost 9x larger YoY at US$12.03bn in 1H22 vs US$1.35bn in 1H21 due to a higher trade deficit
    • The financial account reversed to a net inflow of US$7.2bn in 1H22 from US$1.4bn net outflows in 1H21 due to lower net outflows of portfolio investments and higher net inflows of other investments
  • The government raised its BOP deficit projections for 2022/2023 to US$8.4bn/US$2.5bn from US$6.3bn/US$2.6bn previously
    • It now expects larger current account deficits of US$20.6bn/20.1bn in 2022/2023 from US$19.1bn/US$20.5bn previously
