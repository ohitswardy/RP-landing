REGIS REPORT

14 January 2022

The country’s international reserves (GIR) rose by over US$1bn in Dec to US$108.9bn, equivalent to roughly ten (10) months of imports. The MoM rise is almost certainly due to the inflows from JERA’s purchase of a 25% stake in Aboitiz Power (AP PM), completed during the month.

+++

The purchase of a strategic stake in Aboitiz Power by JERA was also the biggest reason why foreign ownership of the domestic equity market went up by 59bp to 21.7%. JERA acquired the stake in AP shares last month and Singapore’s GIC also increased its stake in ACEN (ACEN PM) with the purchase of shares owned by Ayala Corp (AC PM).

Without those deals, foreign ownership of the market would have been roughly flat MoM. Consumer name Universal Robina (URC PM) saw the biggest outflow for the period.

+++

Recent listing Solar Philippine Nueva Ecija Corp (SPNEC PM) appears to be taking a page from ACEN’s playbook. Its application to increase its capital base signals an intention to undertake injections of assets from its parent company. This could significantly enlarge its still small portfolio.

Even with the recent decline in its share price, Ayala Corp (AC PM) subsidiary ACEN managed to turn investments costing less than US$1m per MW of renewable capacity into a stock market valuation of over US$3m per MW. SPNEC’s current size (market cap of US$220m) and portfolio (50MW by end-2022, rising to 225MW by 2023) are pretty modest today but its has big ambitions and is aiming to do something similar to ACEN.

MACRO
Gross international reserves rise in December
  • The country’s GIR rose US$1.17bn MoM to US$108.89bn in December 2021
    • This is lower than the US$111bn target of the government
    • It is the highest level since the US$110.12bn recorded in December 2020
  • The MoM increase reflected the national government’s net foreign currency deposits
  • The value of the BSP’s gold holdings also rose 3.6% to US$9.33bn in December
  • The GIR is equivalent to 10.3 months of imports of good and payments of services

PROPERTY
Cebu Landmasters 2021 reservation sales climb 16%
  • Cebu Landmasters’ reservation sales rose 16% to P16.5bn
    • 2019: P12.7bn, 2020: P14.2bn
  • Growth was led by the high-end and mid-market segments as well as geographic diversification to more areas within Visayas
  • Economic housing – 41%, high-end – 31%, mid-market – 27%
  • Cebu – 48%, Iloilo – 17%, Cagayan de Oro 13%
  • 4Q21 reservation sales climbed 26% YoY and 42% QoQ to P4.7bn
  • Most developers experienced stronger sales in areas outside Metro Manila

SPNEC parent to inject other assets into listco

  • SPNEC is constructing a 500MW solar project in Nueva Ecija
    • Start of construction before the end of 2021
    • Total project capacity of 500MW
    • Looking at completing 50MW by end 2022 (Phase 1A) and another 175MW by early 2023 (Phase 1B)
    • Phase 2 consists of an additional 275MW

  • The parent company is also consolidating as much as 20k hectares for of land (6k over the next three years) to supply solar developers under various commercial agreements
    • Typical arrangements would be to sell or lease land to developer where Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings is also a JV partner
  • We estimate SPNEC trades at around $750k/MW

OTHERS
December 2021 market foreign ownership rises due to strategic shareholders
  • We estimate market foreign ownership rose 59bps MoM to 21.7% while PSEi foreign ownership increased 95bps MoM to 26.6% (in December)
  • The increases were primarily due to the purchase of Aboitiz Power (AP PM) shares by JERA and the acquisition of additional AC Energy Corp. (ACEN PM) shares by Arran Investment
    • If not for these, we estimate that foreign ownership of both the PSEi and the market would have been flat MoM
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • AP – foreign ownership increased by 27 percentage points to 29.1%. The entire increase was due to the acquisition of AP shares by JERA.
    • ACEN – we estimate that foreign ownership rose 7.4ppt MoM to 23.9%. About 7ppt of this was due to shares purchased by Arran Investment.
    • URC – saw the largest decline in foreign ownership among PSEi constituents (-67bps to 27.1%). It had the highest net foreign selling for the month (US$23.7mn).
