We examine the growth of renewable energy and solar farms in the Philippines and its potential implications on power supply in a report released over the weekend.

Despite the seeming deluge of capacity (over 24,000MW of capacity until 2030, mostly solar), we don’t think the hype has led to a prospective oversupply yet. The lower availability of solar means the current pipeline is really equivalent to only ~5,000MW of baseload capacity (coal or gas) – way short of the expected 9,000MW in absolute demand growth for the entire Philippines over the same period.

We also highlight that the constraints specific to solar could result in a situation where there is an oversupply among solar operators, even when there isn’t enough power for consumers over the entire day. A case of “Water, water everywhere but not a drop to drink.”

This abrupt transition away from thermal, and apparent lack of planning, will actually lead to potential shortages and could be a multi-year positive for gencos with existing capacity. We reiterate our BUY ratings on Aboitiz Power (AP PM) and First Gen Corp (FGEN PM), but have a SELL on AC Energy (ACEN PM) on valuation/execution risk.

Credit rating agency Fitch affirmed the investment grade rating of the Philippines (BBB) with a negative outlook. This follows the deterioration of the country’s fiscal ratios due to the effects of the pandemic. Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez will be proposing a new set of taxes to the incoming administration (whoever they might end  to be). Among the new revenue measures are a carbon tax, a tax on cryptocurrencies, a removal of all VAT exemptions, and an increase in sin taxes. It is unlikely, however, that any new administration will choose to begin its term with the imposition of new taxes.

Fitch affirms PH investment grade rating but outlook still negative
  • Fitch Ratings affirmed the Philippines' investment grade rating of ‘BBB’ with a negative outlook
    • Negative outlook could mean current rating could be downgraded
  • Cited weak government revenue mobilization compared with peers and government debt-to-GDP ratio is forecast to stay close to the ‘BBB’ median over the next few years
    • According to the government’s own data, its debt-to-GDP stood at 60.5% in 2021 from 39.6% in 2019; Forecasts for 2022/2023 are 60.8%/60.7%
    • Fitch forecasting general debt-to-GDP ratio to reach 54.5% in 2022 and 53.1% in 2023 from an estimated 54% in 2021 and 48% in 2020
  • Fitch forecasting Philippine GDP to grow by 6.9% in 2022 and 7% in 2023

    • Removal of all exemptions from 12% VAT

Balance of payments reverts to deficit in January
  • Based on preliminary data, the country’s balance of payments (BOP) position stood at a US$102mn deficit in January 2022

  • For 2022, the BSP expects a smaller BOP surplus of US$700mn from a US$1.35bn surplus in 2021
