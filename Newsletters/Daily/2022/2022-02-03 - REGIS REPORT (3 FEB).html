REGIS REPORT

03 February 2022

Congress approved a whole slew of bills yesterday, with the bill amending the Public Service Act the most notable one.

As reported by media, under the bill, a number of industries will no longer be considered “public utilities”. These include telecoms, tollways, domestic shipping, airlines and airports. This means foreigners would be allowed to own up to 100% of firms that operate in these industries. There appears to be an important caveat, though. Foreign entities will not be allowed to own more than 50% of companies that operate “critical infrastructure” unless there is reciprocity from the foreign entity’s home country. If applied strictly, this last provision would be an effective constraint on China Telecom taking over the new operator Dito Telecommunity. Taking this forward, it would mean Dito’s ability to roll out a competing service will be limited by the capital constraints of its current majority owner, Dito CME (DITO PM) and its controlling shareholder Udenna.

We have yet to see a copy of the “final” version of this bill so we withhold a more definitive view on its impact. However, based on reports by media, it appears the bill’s overall impact will be net positive for the incumbent telecom firms, PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM).

Will President Duterte will choose to veto this bill before it becomes law? We think that is unlikely but not impossible.

+++

Among the other bills also approved by Congress was the one requiring registration of SIM cards.

The bill will require registration of the identity of all those who purchase SIM cards and the maintenance of this database for at least ten years. This is a requirement that would put the Philippines in line with most countries that require such in order to deter and combat crime and fraud.

We believe the financial impact on the telcos of this new regulation will be minimal but it should have an effect on (1) headline subscribers numbers and (2) prepaid churn rates, with both expected to go down. The buy-and-throw-away culture with regard to SIM cards should cease. This new regulation, however, could help speed up the adoption of many fintech services, including the provision of micro-credit.

Interestingly, one section included in this bill is the requirement that individuals also have to provide their real names and phone numbers when creating social media accounts. This was apparently included in order to deter the alleged  troll armies being deployed to influence  public opinion and the upcoming elections. Whether this can be implemented and implemented effectively is another question. Filipinos are among the heaviest social media users globally. The country has over 70m Facebook accounts compared to a population of 110m (including babies and people over 80). That ratio is among the highest, if not the highest, in the world.

+++

AC Energy (ACEN PM) announced it is buying a 49% stake in the Vietnam solar platform of Thai-listed Super Energy Corp Plc for US$165mn. The deal will bump up ACEN’s total attributable capacity to a little over 4,700MW, and is on track to comfortably exceed its 5,000MW target ahead of 2025. Vietnam will now account for ACEN’s largest exposure outside the Philippines at 21% of capacity.

The purchase price for the Vietnam solar plants implies a valuation of roughly US$800-900k/MW. Measured against where ACEN is currently trading (>US$3mn/MW), the recent acquisition looks like a steal.

+++

The arrest of another major Macau junket boss over the weekend highlights the continued threats on the future of VIP and junket gaming in the region. Tak Chun and Macau Legend chairman Levo Chan has been jailed in Macau on suspicion of his involvement in a triad, money laundering and illegal gambling operations.

Tak Chun is widely considered to be the 2nd largest junket operator in Macau after Suncity and operated rooms in Manila’s Entertainment City casinos.

Pre-pandemic, we estimate that as much as 40% of GGR of casino operators came from VIPs brought in by junkets. The intensifying crackdown in China will likely mean the reopening prospects of Bloomberry (BLOOM PM) and Alliance Global (AGI PM) will likely be limited to local patrons.

Congress approves amendments to Public Service Act, with limits on foreign state-owned enterprises’
  • Congress ratified a reconciled version of the Public Services Act, effectively lifting the 40% foreign ownership limits on a number of industries
    • Among the industries that are no longer considered “public utilities”: telecoms, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports 
    • These are thus no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit
    • Those still considered public utilities are: distribution and transmission of electricity; petroleum pipeline transmission systems; water distribution systems; seaports and public utility vehicles
  • Foreign state-owned entities will not be allowed to own more than 50% of companies that own “critical infrastructure” unless there is reciprocity
    • This will be a constraint on China Telecom taking over Dito Telecommunity fully
