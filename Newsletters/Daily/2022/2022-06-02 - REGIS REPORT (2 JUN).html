REGIS REPORT

02 June 2022

PLDT (TEL PM) announced that it has completed the sale of just around half the towers it agreed to sell to two buyers a few weeks ago. A total of 3,012 towers were transferred and cash of P39bn was received. The sale of the remainder of the towers will be completed by 4Q.

PLDT management earlier said it expects a pretax gain of P55bn from the tower sales, of which P38bn will be taken up front. From the proceeds, up to P9bn will be set aside for a special dividend, which would be equivalent to P42/share.

Looking beyond just PLDT, we reckon there’s no reason why Globe (GLO PM) cannot do a similar sale with its own tower network, which is comparable in size to PLDT’s. If Globe were able to achieve comparable net proceeds (after tax), we reckon the incremental value to Globe would be north of P450/share.

+++

Converge (CNVRG PM) announced that it was increasing speeds for all subscribers of its entry level plan (FiberX 1500) from 50mbps to 100mbps. It previously had a promotion that offered the higher speeds only for new subscribers. The new speeds make Converge’s entry level twice as fast as the closest entry level packages of Globe (GLO PM) and PLDT (TEL PM). We can expect the other broadband providers to follow suit sooner rather than later.

It is becoming apparent that the “land grab” / hypergrowth phase of the wired broadband sector is slowly giving way to an industry with greater competition on service standards (speeds) and eventually price.

+++

Commercial banks’ loans accelerated yet again to 10.1% YoY in April, from 8.9% in March. As before, the numbers here are bloated by the merger of BPI (BPI PM) with BPI Family Bank in January. Still, the equivalent growth for the total banking sector was 5.8% YoY in March. We’d estimate that this accelerated to around 7% for all banks. That would put be a) the 13th consecutive month of accelerating growth, and b) the fastest YoY pace since just before the pandemic (7.5% in March 2020).

+++

The last company under our coverage to report 1Q results, Vista Land (VLL PM), finally released its numbers yesterday and the results were disappointing. While 1Q22 net profit of P2.2bn (+11% YoY) looks ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, we would argue that this is due to overstated rental revenue (relative to cash rent). Due to an accounting technicality, rental revenue reached P2.6bn (+29% YoY), already 150% of the 2019 quarterly average. However, we estimate that on a same-mall basis, VLL’s cash rent is c. 60% of 2019 levels, similar to the major mall operators. Meanwhile, after three consecutive quarters of declines, residential revenue rose 64% QoQ (-23% YoY) to P4.8bn.

    • Acceleration driven by consumer loans and loans to manufacturing sector
  • Recall that loan growth for Philippine banks jumped by +5.8% in March

TELCOS
PLDT completes sale of initial batch of telecom towers
  • PLDT completed the sale of 3,012 telecom towers of the 5,907 towers subject of the sale agreement earlier
  • PLDT received P39bn in cash for the tower portfolio, out of the total P77bn consideration

TELCOS
Converge raises speeds for entry level plan again
  • Converge increased speeds for subscribers (existing and new) for its entry level plan (FiberX 1500) from 50mbps to 100mbps beginning yesterday
  • It previously increased speeds only for new subscribers
  • The entry level plan costs P1,500 / month
  • Installation fees (up to P2,500) have also been waived
  • The speed for its entry level plan is now 2x those of its close competitors (see below)
  • The move is a reflection that industry is now moving to a new phase, from being a “land grab” to a situation of rising competition on price and service
  • We can expect the other providers to follow suit, even if they don’t match the new speed/s exactly

  • We estimate that cash rent is only c. 50% of the rental revenue figure
  • Net debt stood at P107bn
    • Gross debt of P173bn
    • Net debt/equity of 93%
  • The company will hold a briefing at 2PM today
  • VLL is trading at 66% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, and 4.1X FY22E P/E

GAMING
Okada group forcibly takes control of casino-hotel
  • The group of Kazuo Okada regained physical possession of the eponymous Okada Manila casino-hotel after a forced takeover on Tuesday led by Filipino partner Antonio Cojuangco
  • The ouster was part of a wider cross-border legal battle in Japan, Hong Kong and Philippines to regain control of his business empire after being pushed out of Universal in 2017
  • Okada’s camp said the Philippine Supreme Court last April issued a “status quo ante order” that recognized Okada as the sole representative of HK-based Tiger Resort Asia Ltd, owner of Tiger Resort Leisure and Entertainment Inc. (TRLEI) and subsidiary of Universal Entertainment (UE)
    • The group claims that this paves the way for Okada’s return as shareholder, director, chairman and CEO of TRLEI and recognized by PAGCOR
  • Meanwhile, the TRLEI board that was removed from Okada Manila accused the other group of illegally and violently taking over the casino without furnishing a copy of the order they were supposedly implementing
    • The removed board claims there was no change in leadership in management of UE, TRLEI and Tiger Resort Asia Ltd.
    • It filed an urgent appeal asking the SC to reconsider or revoke the status quo ante order
  • The recent development could affect operations which are ramping up to accommodate the increased mobility in Manila
  • Other plans that could be put on hold include -
    • Potential backdoor listing of TRLEI through Asiabest Group International
    • Listing on Nasdaq by merging with Jason Ader’s SPAC (deal valuation of $2.6bn) expected to close by June 2022
GAMING
CTA rejects P897mn tax refund bid of City of Dreams Manila
  • According to a local law news website, the Court of Tax Appeals (CTA) junked the petition for review filed by Melco Resorts Leisure Corp, operator of City of Dreams Manila, concerning P897.4mn in refund or tax certificate of its excess and unutilized input VAT from 4Q2014 to 2Q2015
  • Melco pointed out that it had zero rated sales and incurred input taxes coming zero-rated sales during the period covered
  • It stressed that as a licensee of PAGCOR it is exempt from VAT and thus entitled to the refund or tax credit of input VAT
  • The CTA Third Division denied the petition for lack of merit, ruling that “those engaged in zero-rated sales can apply for input VAT refund”  but “VAT zero rating is different from VAT exemption”
