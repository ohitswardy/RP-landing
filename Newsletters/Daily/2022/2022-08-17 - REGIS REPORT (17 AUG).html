REGIS REPORT

17 August 2022

We have a handful more results this morning, among the last to report this season.

Ahead of a full recap, it’s noteworthy that the earnings misses are disproportionately in the real estate sector including the two names that reported 1H results yesterday, Vista Land (VLL PM) and Filinvest Land (FLI PM).

  • GT Capital (GTCAP PM) (+) – 2Q22 core profit to common surprised to the upside, surging by 72% YoY on the back of robust results from the bank and infrastructure units. Despite the strong volume rebound, income at the auto unit did not do too well in 2Q, weighed down by (a) the PHP’s depreciation against the USD and (b) inventory challenges in higher margin CBUs from Japan. Meanwhile, the property unit’s high-end residential projects, which are equity-accounted, drove profits higher by 41% YoY in 2Q, and offset lingering problems at its mid-income segment.

  • Vista Land (VLL PM) (-) – Despite an 18% YoY drop in residential revenue, 2Q22 EBIT rose 24% due to an unusually high residential GPM as well a drop in opex. However, we believe both are unlikely to be sustained in the coming quarters. Furthermore, after 1H22 residential revenue dropped 21% YoY, management said it would likely fall 40% HoH in 2H22. Thus, the company will likely see lower revenue and margins in 2H22.

  • Filinvest Land (FLI PM) (-) – 2Q22 EBIT dropped 11% QoQ to P1.2bn. While the company did not seem to book substantial residential cancellations, we estimate that 27% of residential receivables are over 90 days past due. Thus, we might still see revenue reversals in the coming quarters.
  • Cosco Capital (COSCO PM) (=) – Liquor distribution arm Keepers Holdings (KEEPR PM) was the outperformer in 2Q, with profits (before minorities) jumping 42% YoY. The reopening and lifting of dine-in restrictions were key drivers of volumes during the quarter (+27% YoY / +53% QoQ), while margins were supported by price increases and a favorable sales mix despite the depreciating PHP. Meanwhile, plans for the recently announced JV with Siam Global, marking COSCO’s entry to the home improvement retail segment, appear to be moving along. However, the first few stores are not likely to be opened until late-2023 / mid-2024.

With sugar prices dominating the headlines the past few days, we thought it was time to revisit this topic after we highlighted it a few weeks back.

Since our last update, the situation for domestic sugar supply has become more acute, with even the big carbonated soft drink firms releasing a statement about the difficulty in sourcing sugar.  Domestic sugar prices have continued to soar and have now exceeded the equivalent of US$0.50 per pound.  This would put it at 3x global price, from the more “usual” 2x.

The big losers are, obviously, those who consume sugar.  Among the big listed firms, that would be Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) and Universal Robina (URC PM). URC, however, also produces its own sugar and is, in fact, a net seller.

Frankly, addressing the near-term supply and price issue for sugar is very simple.  All the government needs to do is allow more imports into a highly protected and cartelized industry.  The question really is whether that can happen in an environment where vested interests and the sugar lobby are strong, and with a President who has shown a protectionist bent apparently bequeathed by his father.

The Bureau of Treasury’s auction for 10-year government bonds netted a yield of 5.813% -- a decline of over 100bp from the last auction a month ago. This drop appears to reflect a similar, if smaller, decline in US Treasury yields, which are themselves a reflection of expectations that short-term rates are close to peaking.

CONSUMER
Domestic sugar prices jump to 5-year high
  • Domestic sugar prices continue to climb, with wholesale prices breaching P3,000/50kg bag since the end of July. This is 75% higher vs year-ago and end-2021 levels. In USD terms, it is nearly 3x global sugar prices.
  • The surge in prices has been attributed to poor supply resulting from Typhoon Odette last year, coupled with the recovery in demand.

  • Consumer companies that are exposed to beverages, bakery, and confectionery will be the most affected by an increase in sugar prices.
    • MONDE - Biscuits account for ~30% of APAC BFB’s revenues. Sugar is a major raw material for its APAC BFB business, accounting to ~10% of the segment’s raw material cost.  
    • URC - Beverages (coffee, tea), confectionery, and baked goods account for c70% of the domestic BCF segment. Sugar accounts for 8-10% of the segment’s total raw material cost. Note, however, that URC is a net seller of sugar through its Sugar and Renewables (SURE) segment.
