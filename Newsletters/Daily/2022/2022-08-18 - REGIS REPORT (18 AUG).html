REGIS REPORT

18 August 2022

We recap the 2Q/1H earnings season below. Spoiler alert – it was pretty durned good, with 55-60% of companies beating expectations. Market now trades at 15x PE for 2022E, with upside risk to earnings.

The BSP’s Monetary Board meets today, and the Street is expecting a 50bps hike. We note that the current 3.25% policy rate is still 75bps away  from the 4.0% just before the pandemic hit. Meanwhile, the 10-year BVAL rate has already be reflected the “peak inflation” narrative, falling some 130bps to  5.84% from the June peak of 7.15%.

Meanwhile, the House Ways and Means Committee approved (on 1st reading) the bill seeking to impose 12% VAT on digital transactions. This will cover things like app purchases, subscriptions to streaming services, digital advertising, etc. This will NOT cover online shopping / food delivery services (Shoppee, Lazada, GrabFood, etc) as those goods and services are already subject to VAT. The government will need additional revenues to help bring the fiscal deficit down from the 7-8% of GDP currently.

+ + +

That must have been the most (positively) surprising quarter in a good long while. Aggregate NPAT for Regis-covered stocks in 2Q22 grew a whopping 49% YoY, taking 1H22 to a nice round +40% YoY. Fully 56% (24/43) of companies beat our expectations, while only 26% came in behind. The tally was even more lopsided for the PSEi, with close to 60% exceeding consensus estimates, though NPAT was up a milder 36% YoY in 2Q22. The main earnings drag came from San Miguel (SMC PM), which posted an eye-watering P9.3bn loss (after prefs and perps) in 2Q22. Excluding SMC, we estimate PSEi 2Q profit would have been +46% YoY.

The results underscore the power of the economic reopening (and actually belie the soft 2Q GDP print… but we digress). Revenues were strong nearly across the board, as companies were surprised by the resilience of demand despite high inflation. Mall traffic, car sales, loan growth, electricity sales all showed continuous improvement. The earnings beat came despite margin pressure, particularly on consumer companies, whose price hikes didn’t fully cover rising input costs. The disappointment came largely from residential developers, as cancellations of previous sales dragged revenues.

The outlook for 2H22 is looking pretty good, too. Anecdotally, momentum has held in July/August. While every company professes to be wary of a slowdown – was 1H just “revenge spending”? That said, none seem to see evidence of weaking demand… at least not yet.

Meanwhile, we estimate the PSEi is now trading on about 15x PER for 2022E, based on consensus earnings. And with consensus growth of c20% in 2023E, PE drops to 13x. Looks pretty compelling from where we sit.

Seems like the glass is looking a bit more than half full…

A quick summary by sector:

  • Banks – ahead – Loan growth accelerated, NIMs expanded, and NPLs fell. All according to script. Sector earnings jumped 38% YoY in 2Q. Profits doubled for MBT and SECB as provision expenses plunged. Rising interest rates should bode well for NIMs heading into 2H22 and 2023. However, banks were wary that high inflation, rising rates, and a weak PHP could see another round of new NPL formation, particularly at the mid-sized corporate level. We remain positive on the sector.
  • Consumer/Retail – ahead – The recovery of consumption eclipsed cost pressures from high commodity input prices. Despite hefty price increases (we estimate c8-10% from end-2021 levels), volumes across nearly all categories – snack foods, noodles, alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages, canned food – were resilient. On a same-store level, we estimate restaurant and retail sales (ex-groceries) have recovered to pre-pandemic levels. The recovery, though, seems “K-shaped”, with retailers and food manufacturers noting down-trading, particularly at the lower-income segments hardest hit by inflation. We expect top-line momentum be sustained into the seasonally strong 4Q. Falling commodity prices could represent upside risks to margins and earnings heading into 2023E.
  • Power – ahead – Electricity sales extended its growth trajectory, up 3-6% for distribution utilities. Meralco (MER PM) saw a 17% jump in commercial electricity consumption in 2Q, and a 1% drop in residential sales, reflecting the back-to-work shift. Power generators benefitted from tight power supply, which saw spot prices jump to P9.01/kwh (+24% YoY) in June. Aboitiz Power (AP PM) was a big winner with its new coal plant coming on line.
  • Telcos – mixed – In contrast to many other sectors, telcos did not see a bump from the reopening. Mobile revenues grew, but not as much as we had hoped. Meanwhile, fiber broadband saw a  sharp slowdown, suggesting an end to the land grab, and a likely grind for new, lower-ARPU subs. Divestments (data centers, cell tower) in 1H and into 3Q will not only boost profit, but also will bolster balance sheets, coming off a period of unprecedented capex.
  • Property – mixed - Two main headlines for the sector in 2Q22: 1) mall operations rebounded, and 2) cancellations hounded the residential business. Thanks to the economic reopening, mall revenues grew 139% YoY and 44% QoQ and now represent c. 80% of pre-COVID levels. On the other hand, aggregate residential revenue dropped 10% YoY and 4% QoQ as cancellations led to revenue reversals. Meanwhile, Metro Manila office vacancy was approximately flat in 2Q22 at 18% (from 19% in 1Q22, 18% in 4Q21). The trends are likely to continue into 2H22, with malls leading the way, while resi and office go through a longer bottoming-out period.
