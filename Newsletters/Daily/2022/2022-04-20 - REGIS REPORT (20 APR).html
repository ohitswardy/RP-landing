REGIS REPORT

20 April 2022

PLDT (TEL PM) announced yesterday that it had signed definitive agreements to sell almost 6,000 of its telecom towers to two separate groups at a handsome, P77bn (US$1.5bn), valuation. The deals will come with a leaseback arrangements allowing PLDT to continue using those assets. While these transactions are something that’ve been signaled, the confirmation at that valuation should be welcome for the market, as the share price has barely moved in reaction to this development previously.

We estimate value accretion (after tax) of as much as P300 per share for PLDT from this deal, equivalent to ~16% of the share price. Financially, this will be visible through a reduction in debt (US$1bn) and a likely P9bn (US$180m) special dividend.  From a continuing perspective, PLDT’s EBIT will be broadly unchanged (excluding recognition of gains from the deal, deferred or immediate) the sale of half its tower network. This is a key highlight of the sale-leaseback and why it’s so value-accretive.

Taking a step further, these transaction could also have positive implications for Globe (GLO PM) as well even if it is not a party to them.  There’s no reason why Globe cannot do the same, even if the scale might differ.  If it is able to achieve the same value for a similar proportion of its towers, the value increment to Globe could be as much as P470 per share (19% of share price).  This would be even greater than the attributable value of its 36% stake in Mynt, based on the last funding round.

TELCOS
PLDT signs deal to sell half of its telecom towers for P77bn
  • Deal raises P77bn (US$1.5bn). PLDT signed a deal to sell 5,907 telecom towers and related passive infrastructure for P77bn (US$1.5bn) yesterday. The transactions would see the transfer of half of its towers to the winning bidders, subsidiaries of Edgepoint and edotco Group and PLDT subsequently leasing the towers back for the next ten years.

The company will post a gain of ~P41bn (US$800m) from these deals, of which P28bn will be recognized when they close and the rest over the life of the leaseback arrangement.

  • EBIT neutral, EPS positive.  The transaction price is estimated to be at a whopping 20x EBITDA and the ~P7bn in annual lease payments that PLDT expects to pay will approximate the opex (cash and non-cash) it currently has for the towers.  As a result, the deal will be EBIT neutral (excluding the impact of the recognition of the transaction gains) even if it will reduce EBITDA by a fraction. After accounting for the impact of savings on financing costs, we estimate earnings enhancement of over P2bn annually (+6% to 7%), excluding the recognition of deferred gains on the tower sale.
  • Favorable lease terms.  PLDT will pay around P100,000 (US$2,000) per month to lease each tower, which appears high (half that in Indonesia) but the payments will cover capex for power (batteries, generators, etc) and PLDT will benefit from co-location discounts should the towercos secure other tenants.  PLDT is also protected from future inflation with annual lease escalation effectively capped at less than 1%.
  • Proceeds for debt reduction and special dividend.  Management has earmarked P52bn (US$1bn) to reduce PLDT’s debt (partly outright debt prepayment) and up to P9bn to pay a special dividend.  The P9bn would be approximately P42 per PLDT share.  We expect any special dividend to be paid only in early FY23 given that the transactions will be closed on a staggered basis up to 4Q22.
  • Value accretion of ~P300 per share. With PLDT trading at 6x EBITDA, a sale of its passive tower assets at 20x EBITDA would add value. We estimate the transaction will allow PLDT to realize/capture incremental value of close to P300/share (16% of the share price) given the terms of the deal (sale price and leaseback arrangements) and the fact that minimal value was likely to have been attached to the towers in the first place. 
  • Possible positive for Globe too. There should be anticipation that Globe can conclude a similar deal with regard to its own tower assets.  Such a deal would not only enhance value for Globe, it would also obviate the need to raise new capital (something which we were raising).  Even without such a sale, there would also be benefits to Globe as ~6,000 towers would be become available for it to lease too. This could reduce future capex needs.
