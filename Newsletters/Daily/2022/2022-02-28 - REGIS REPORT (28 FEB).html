28 February 2022

The government lowered the pandemic Alert classification for Metro Manila and 38 others areas to Level One (from Level Two), effective March 1 (tomorrow). Alert Level One removes most restrictions and allows nearly all businesses to operate at full capacity.

The 4Q/FY21 results season is beginning in earnest with a slew of results late last week and many more scheduled for today.

The two big banks, BDO (BDO PM) and Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY21 earnings last Friday – and both will have earnings calls this afternoon. Trends were quite similar – accelerating loan growth, flat NIMs, strong fees, and falling provisions. These were consistent, too, with BPI’s results which came out earlier this month.

BDO’s NPAT came in at P42.8bn, up 51% YoY, slightly ahead of consensus. Loans accelerated to 6.5% (from 5.5% in 3Q), and faster than the industry’s 4.2% average. NIMs were flat QoQ, despite slightly lower loan yields. Fees were strong, up 20% YoY and comfortably above pre-Covid levels. NPAT also got a boost from lower tax expenses. Asset quality improved markedly, with NPL ratio falling to 2.8% (from 3.1% in 3Q) and with NPL cover rising to 111% (from 101% in 3Q). By our calculations, though, restructured loans (RL) stayed elevated at c5.7% of loans.

  • Semirara (SCC PM) (+) Semirara reported headline 4Q21 profit up 20x YoY and 1.6x QoQ to P5.9bn, taking FY21 profit to P16.2bn, well above Regis (P14.7bn) and consensus forecasts (P14.2bn). +++

Globe (GLO PM) is increasing its authorized capital (i.e. the maximum amount of capital it can legally issue), specifically its authorized common shares. The firm will increase its authorized common shares by 20m from 148.9m to 168.9m. We reckon this is a likely prelude to an equity issuance.

Globe’s nominal debt levels are already on the high side (gross debt/equity of 1.84x, net debt/equity of 1.62x and gross debt/EBITDA of 2.65x) even if they remain within debt covenants (gross debt/equity of 3.0x, gross debt/EBITDA of 3.5x). However, these ratios understate the true amount of financial leverage because Globe’s recent US$600m perpetual is being treated as equity for the purposes of calculating these ratios.

With another year of near-record capex coming (P89bn capex guidance in FY22), an equity raising exercise appears likely, in our view.

BANKS
BDO FY21 P42.8bn, driven by fees; NPLs, cover improve

BDO Unibank (BDO PM) reported FY21 NPAT of P42.8bn up 51% YoY, slightly ahead of consensus (P42.1bn). Revenues were driven by a sustained 20% surge in fee incomes, which are now well above 2019 levels. Loan growth accelerated to 6.5% YoY (was 5.5% in 3Q), faster than the 4.2% pace for the total banking industry. FY21 NIMs were down c20bps YoY, but were flat for most of the year. Headline NPL ratio improved markedly in 4Q, falling to 2.8% (vs 3.1% in 3Q), and NPL cover rising to 111% (from 101% in 3Q), as the bank used strong revenues to increase provision expense in 4Q21. Restructured loans, though, were still high; we estimate RLs were steady at c5.7% of total loans at end-2021. Overall a solid set of results.

Some highlights:

  • Loans expanded 6.5% YoY, up from 5.5% in 3Q21, and beating the industry average of 4.2%. Deposits grew 8.1%, with CASA ratio staying high at 85% (flat QoQ).
  • NIMs were steady QoQ at 3.96%, though still down c20bps YoY. Loan yields dipped 12bps QoQ, while deposit costs edged lower to 0.23%.
  • Trading/fx gains halved to P3.8bn, as expected, given the high base in 2020, and the rising bond yields in 2021.
  • Fee incomes (incl trust and insurance) continued their surge, up 20% YoY, and are now comfortably higher than FY2019 levels.
  • Provision expense fell 44% in FY21 to P17bn, as NPL ratio improved to 2.8% from 3.1% in 3Q21. Provisions jumped to P6.6bn though in 4Q, as the bank used strong revenues to begin rebuilding its NPL cover, which improved to 111% at end-2021 from 101% in 3Q21. Restructured loans, though stayed high at c5.7% of total loans by our estimate.
  • NPAT also got a boost from sharply lower tax expense (-27% YoY) thanks to the lower corporate tax rate thanks to the CREATE law.

Overall, the FY21 results were pretty solid. The bank will hold an earnings call at 2pm today (Manila/HK/SG time).

BANKS
MBT FY/4Q profits surge as provision normalize

Metrobank (MBT PM) reported FY21 NPAT of P22.16bn, up 60% YoY and slightly ahead of consensus (Bberg P21.5bn). For 4Q21 alone, profits more than doubled to P6.0bn. Both the FY and 4Q bottomline were boosted largely by a sharp drop in provision expense. The bank seems to be on its way to normalizing its provisioning, allowing its NPL cover to fall from 191% in 3Q21 to 175% in 4Q21 despite a slight uptick in NPL ratio to 2.2% (vs 2.1% in 3Q). We expect provision expense to continue to drive profit growth in 2022E.

The bank announced an increase in their regular dividend policy from P1.00/sh per year to P1.60/sh (in two tranches of P0.80). It also will pay out a special dividend of P1.40/sh, bringing total cash dividends this year to P3.00/sh, or roughly a 5% dividend yield. The bank’s capital ratios remain high, with CAR at 20.1%, and CET-1 of 19.3% at end-2021.

Some highlights from the financials (lots of charts, below):

  • Loans ended FY21 at -1.3% YoY, though improving from the -4.2% in 3Q21. On a sequential basis, loans were up 5.3% QoQ, driven by corporate loans and by credit cards. Its end-2021 loan book was roughly 17% lower than pre-Covid levels.
    • Big resurgence in “trade loans” which presumably will be the SME traders. However, auto loans were still down 23% YoY, and down 35% from the 2019 levels.

  • NIMs were 3.17% by our estimate, holding steady at these sort of levels since 2Q21. As loan growth recovers, and as interest rates rise, NIMs should see improving trends into 2022E.
  • Fees (incl trust) were up 15% YoY in FY21, and roughly +20% YoY in 4Q21 alone, closing in on pre-Covid levels.
  • There was a surge in “miscellaneous income” to P3.0bn in FY21 from just P669mn in 2020. This was driven by ROPA foreclosures – ie excess of assessed value vs loan value –  worth P813mn vs P127mn in 2020; and by “recovery on charged-off assets” worth P1.4bn vs P692mn in 2020.
  • Trading/fx income fell 72% YoY, as expected, given the extremely high base in 2020.
  • Opex was flat, -1% YoY. Compensation expense was up just 1.5% YoY while occupancy expense was down 6%. Taxes and licenses were down 20% YoY, in line with the drop in revenues.
  • PPOP was down 32% YoY for FY21, but was rising nicely in 4Q, up 12% YoY and 13% QoQ.
  • Provision expense fell 71% in FY21 to P11.8bn, given the extremely high base in 2020. In 4Q alone, provision expense dropped to P1.8bn, down 66% YoY and down 39% QoQ. This was despite a slight uptick in headline NPL ratio to 2.2%, up from 2.1% in 3Q21. NPL cover also fell to 174.7% at end-2021, from 190.7% in 3Q. It would seem that MBT management is beginning to normalize its provisioning policy, and is willing to run down its excess allowances. This should continue to be a significant earnings driver in 2022E

Management will hold an earnings call on Monday, 28 February at 4pm (Manila/HK/SG time). The relatively slow recovery in loan growth will likely be a key focus. Another will be the bank’s large portfolio of Treasury assets, the vast majority of which is in the AFS/FVOCI classification. Will these be hit with mark-to-market losses as interest rates rise? Or will higher rates present opportunities to gap out their book to higher-yielding, longer-dated bond (or, indeed, into loans).

TELCOS
Globe increasing authorized capital; equity issue looking likely
  • Globe is increasing its authorized capital, raising its authorized common shares from 148.9m to 168.9m (a +20m increase). Only the authorized common shares is being increased. There is no change for the preferred shares being planned.
  • This move suggests that Globe is planning to do an equity issue, possibly a rights offer. Given where leverage ratios are, an equity raise is a prudent course of action.
  • Globe’s gross debt to equity was 1.84x and gross debt to EBITDA was 2.65x as of end-FY21. These are technically within debt covenants (3x and 3.5x). These ratios, however, would treat the recently issued perps (US$600m) as equity and not debt.
  • Its reported net debt to equity was 1.6x (63:37 debt-equity) and net debt/EBITDA was 2.35x as of Dec 2021. Treating the perps as debt would push net gearing to 2.6x (that’s 72:28 debt-equity) and net debt/EBITDA to 2.7x respectively. And this is before the planned capex spend this year of US$1.7bn (P89bn).
  • We believe an equity issue of close to US$1bn is looking probable.

  • Solar Philippines Board of Directors approved an asset for share swap involving the acquisition of 100% of the outstanding shares of Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings, Inc. and affiliates in various entities including
    • Solar Philippines Calatagan – 63MW, 38% owned by Kepco
    • Solar Philippines Tarlac Corp – 200MW (100MW operating), 50% owned by Prime Infra
    • Solar Philippines Tanauan 140MW under construction, 50% owned by Prime Infra
    • Including other entities, collectively hold 33 solar energy service contracts with c.14,395MW of capacity under development according to company
  • SPNEC parent would subscribe to 24,373,050,000 shares of SPNEC at P2.50/share
    • We estimate that the price translates to a valuation of $1.1m/MW of the existing (Nueva Ecija) holdings of SPNEC
  • SPNEC’s total outstanding shares to jump to 32.497bn, with Solar Philippines Power Project Holding’s stake rising from 67% to 92%
