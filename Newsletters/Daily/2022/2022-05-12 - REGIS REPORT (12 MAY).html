REGIS REPORT

12 May 2022

For today’s results round-up, we have a number of the bigger index names, SM (SM PM), Monde (MONDE PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM) as well as Robinsons Land (RLC PM) and DMCI (DMC PM). Jollibee (JFC PM) also announced its results early this morning.

  • Monde Nissin (MONDE PM) (-) - Cost inflation continued to weigh on margins despite price increases implemented across the two key business segments. APAC BFB sales were boosted by the ~5% cumulative price increase and volume growth was driven by the easing of mobility restrictions. Quorn sales were flattish YoY as the surge in foodservice (+124% YoY) helped cushion the decline in retail sales. MONDE is implementing another round of price increase in 2Q but given the volatility in commodity prices, we reckon the price hikes implemented may not be the last.

  • SM Investments (SM PM) (+) – Income for 1Q22 reached P12bn, +27% more than the previous year and already higher than the pre-pandemic level (1Q19).  While earnings from its main subsidiaries have yet to match pre-pandemic numbers, income was padded by its “portfolio investments” (which includes its smaller operations).  SM Retail’s Food group saw revenues practically unchanged YoY, implying a slight decline in same-store sales (SSS).

  • AC Energy (ACEN PM) (-) – Prolonged outages at its coal plant widened ACEN’s short position in 1Q22, with the purchase of expensive replacement power from WESM being the largest contributor to the 68% decline in earnings. The P450mn hit from buying out a fixed-price customer contract suggests ACEN believes fuel costs will continue to be volatile moving forward. Although ACEN is a currently a net seller to the spot, management expects to be short again in 3Q when they shut off some plants for maintenance. More below.
  • Robinsons Land (RLC PM) (-) – Operating profit underperformed due to weak Philippine residential revenue (which was brought about by sales cancellations). However, malls are recovering well and the office segment is performing better than our expectations. Furthermore, despite the sales cancellations in 1Q22, management is guiding for a 40% jump in net presales this year.

  • DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) (+) – DMCI’s 1Q22 profit came in better than expected at P11.2bn (+177% YoY), following Semirara’s outperformance (see report link), as well as surprisingly strong recoveries at its property, nickel, and off-grid power segments. The lone underperformer was the construction unit, whose margins and order book continued to face downward pressures. The stock trades at a 21% discount to its market-based NAV, wider than its historical discount (13%).  The company is holding its briefing later but in the meantime there’s some detail below.
  • Jollibee (JFC PM) (+) – 1Q22 net profit was P2bn, a turnaround from the loss in the same period last year. Revenues jumped 24% YoY on the back of +16.5% SSSG (consolidated) driven mostly by the Philippine operations (+22.9%) and CBTL (+23.3%). Operating profit jumped 34% YoY despite higher input and freight cost, which we believe could be attributed to (1) operating leverage and (2) cost-cutting initiatives implemented over the past two years. The stock currently trades at 2022E multiples 36x PE and 12x EV/EBITDA. We hope to get more detail after the briefing early next week.

CONGLOS
DMCI profit surges to all-time high in 1Q22
  • DMCI (DMC PM) reported a core profit of P11.2bn in 1Q22, up 177% YoY
    • This accounts for 58% of street forecasts and about 66% of its full-year 2021 profit
    • Apart from Semirara’s outperformance, results were also better than expected for its property, nickel mining, and off-grid power units
  • The property unit’s profit dropped 7% YoY, as revenues dropped on lower downpayment recognition and higher sales cancellations
    • The good news is that reservation sales bounced back strongly (+86% QoQ) to P8.1bn (vs. c. P9bn per quarter pre-pandemic)
  • Its construction unit also recorded a 7% YoY drop in profit due to high base effects
    • While revenues rose, gross margins are still dropping but this was offset by the absence of vaccine-related costs
    • The order book dropped further to P46.7bn on the absence of new contracts
  • Its nickel mining unit’s profit rose 20% YoY on higher shipments and higher ASPs
  • Its off-grid power unit benefitted from higher electricity demand
  • The company is holding a briefing at 3:30pm today; Registration link

POWER
ACEN’s 1Q22 profit falls 68% to P405mn - behind
  • ACEN’s 1Q22 profit fell 68% YoY to P405mn, tracking behind Regis (P7.416bn) and consensus (P8.255bn) estimates for FY22E. Management attributed the decline to a) higher cost of purchased power from spot during SLTEC outage, b) a one-time buyout expense related to a customer contract and c) curtailment in Visayas due to a transmission line damage caused by Typhoon Odette.
    • Management estimates that these items collectively depressed profit by around P1.6bn. Excluding these, profit would have been 23% higher YoY.
  • Extended outages at SLTEC resulted in much lower availability for the period (40%), which prompted expensive replacement power from WESM to supply its contracts. The company estimates a negative impact of about P550mn to net income as result. It also bought out a fixed-price customer contract that had an upsizing option to reduce risk moving forward, which resulted in a one-time expense of P450mn. The balance of c.P150mn (of P1.6bn) relates to profits that could have been booked without the curtailment in Visayas.
  • Largely shielded from coal spikes. Management reiterated that they have “physical” fixed price, long-term agreements with a coal supplier that shields them from spikes, save for some 20MW worth that sources coal from a third party supplier (partly hedged, not long-term). According to management, the bigger impact/risk would be from when plants are not running and they have to buy replacement power.
  • Back to net-buyer by 3Q. As it stands, ACEN is currently a net seller to the spot market, with a baseload supply of about 450MW matching about the same in peak demand from customers (Meralco and RES customers). It has an estimated 266MW (attributable) of solar selling to the spot market plus 250MW of diesel elected for ancillary. However, planned outages in 3Q22 would return ACEN to a net buyer status for at least a month.
  • AC’s property dividend of ACEN shares would reduce AC’s stake in ACEN to 57.8% from 62.5% and improve public float to at least 21%. This takes into account the shares (1.332bn) still to be issued to UPC for their stakes in Australia, etc which will increase total outstanding shares to 39,647.84mn. The exercise will allow ACEN to meet the minimum float required to be a component of the PSEi.
  • ACEN trades at an EV/EBITDA (attributable) of 19.4x and a PE of 38.1x for FY22E

CONSUMER
JFC 1Q22 net profit at P2bn – ahead of Regis and consensus
  • JFC reported 1Q22 headline net profit of P2.3bn. We estimate net profit to common was P2bn, a turnaround from the P159mn loss in the same period last year. This is tracking ahead of Regis and consensus expectations
  • The outperformance was driven by a one-off gain of P1.8bn relating to the conveyance of its land properties to CentralHub, a subsidiary of DoubleDragon (DD PM)
  • Revenues (in line) jumped 24% YoY on the back of +16.5% SSSG (consolidated) driven mostly by the Phils (+22.9%) and CBTL (+23.3%)
  • Operating margin (behind) jumped 34% YoY despite higher input and freight cost, which we believe could be attributed to (1) operating leverage and (2) cost-cutting initiatives implemented over the past two years
