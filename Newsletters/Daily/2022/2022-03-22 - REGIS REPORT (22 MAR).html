REGIS REPORT

22 March 2022

President Duterte signed into law the bill amending the Public Service Act.  The new law would effectively lift foreign ownership limits of range of industries including telecoms, airlines, airports, shipping and even toll roads. This law is one of a series of measures that the administration has been pushing in order to liberalize the economy.

For the incumbent telcos, the lifting of foreign ownership restrictions will have minimal practical impact.  Foreign entities can already own way more than the 40% of the common shares of PLDT (TEL PM) and Globe (GLO PM) even before the new law.  This is because of the loopholes of existing regulations on preferred shares which allow them to do so.

Notwithstanding the lifting of the foreign ownership limit, the law has provisions disallowing foreign state-owned entities from acquiring any additional capital in companies involved in “critical infrastructure”, which includes telecoms.  If the law is implemented to the spirit and letter, we reckon it would forbid Dito Telecommunity from receiving any more capital from its 40% shareholder China Telecom. We believe this could have implications on the future viability of the company.

With airlines and airports no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit, Cebu Air (CEB PM), Philippines Airlines (PAL) and even Megawide (MWIDE PM) would no longer face the cap as long as they dispose of all their land holdings.

First Gen’s (FGEN PM) FY21 recurring profit of $252mn fell short of estimates, as 4Q fell 37%QoQ. Management attributed the decline to buying expensive replacement power for San Gabriel and certain plants of EDC during the period. As output from Malampaya declines, the potential de-rating of San Gabriel (420MW) could mean FGEN will be short on power over the next few months.  More below.

+++

Pump prices were cut by a significant amount effective this morning across many of the fuel retailers.  The reduction reverses most, but not all, of the unprecedented price increases implemented two weeks ago.

This will provide some welcome relief for consumers and eases immediate pressure on the CPI.

MACRO
Duterte signs law amending Public Service Act, raising foreign ownership limits on various sectors
  • Duterte signed the bill amending the Public Services Act into law (Republic Act# 11647), paving the way for the lifting of the 40% foreign ownership limits on a number of industries
    • Among the industries that are no longer considered “public utilities” : telecoms, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports 
    • These are thus no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit
    • Those still considered public utilities are: distribution and transmission of electricity; petroleum pipeline transmission systems; water distribution systems; seaports and public utility vehicles
  • Foreign state-owned entities will not be allowed to own shares in public utilities or companies that own/operate “critical infrastructure”
    • Those who already own the shares are allowed to keep their shares but will not be allowed to add more
    • This will be a constraint on China Telecom taking over Dito Telecommunity fully
MACRO
Fuel retailers cut pump prices
  • Fuel retailers implemented a rollback of prices of gasoline, diesel, and kerosene by P5.45 (c. -7%), P11.45 (c. -15%), and P8.55 (c. -10%) per liter, respectively
    • This ends 11 straight weeks of price hikes, which has totaled P20.35 (+30%), P30.65 (+60%), and P24.90 (+45%) per liter, respectively, thus far
  • Energy Secretary Cusi cited the drop in the average price of gasoline to US$120/bbl last week, down from US$138/bbl the previous week
    • Average price of diesel slipped to US$125/bbl from US$157/bbl the previous week
  • Despite the decline in fuel prices, the government will still double fuel subsidies to P6.1bn from P3bn

MACRO
Largest labor group seeks P430 wage hike in Central Visayas
  • The Trade Union Congress of the Philippines (TUCP) petitioned to increase the minimum wage in Central Visayas (Region 7) by P430 from P404 currently
    • Central Visayas region comprises provinces of Cebu, Negros Occidental, Bohol, and Siquijor
  • The Regional Wage Board raised the minimum wage in Central Visayas by 4.5% or P18 in January 2020
  • TUCP also called to increase the minimum wage in NCR to P1007 from P537 last week
    • Wages in NCR have not changed since Nov. 2018
  • President Rodrigo R. Duterte is set to announce his decision on the proposals soon

POWER
FGEN recurring profit of $252, flat - behind Regis/consensus; 4Q21 37% lower QOQ
  • FGEN’s FY21 recurring income hit $252mn, flat YoY and behind Regis ($279mn) and consensus ($287mn) estimates for the full-year.
    • The full year figure implies 4Q fell to $40mn, down 37%QoQ from $64mn in 3Q
    • After pref dividends, we estimate FY21 core income was $240mn, lower than our FY21E of $259mn
    • Decline in 4Q was largely caused by need to buy replacement power for plant outages that exceeded outage allowance. Without citing details, management attributed the contraction to buying expensive replacement power for San Gabriel and certain plants of EDC.
    • EDC also booked a lot of expenses in 4Q relating to steamfield maintenance, etc. 2021 was generally a catch-up year for a lot of maintenance related opex,and picked up towards the latter part of the year as restrictions lifted.
  • Nat gas earnings grew 6% to $198mn, largely driven by higher electricity sales from Avion in the early part of the year
    • Implied 4Q of nat gas was $40mn, down 38%QoQ
    • Lower contribution from 420MW San Gabriel due to outages and replacement power
    • Impacted by outages of Avion unit 2 (until October) and Unit 2 (beginning December)
  • EDC’s attributable income for FY21 of $88mn was lower by 18%YoY
    • Implied 4Q of $18m declined 25%QoQ
    • Press release cited higher plant and steamfield maintenance expenses
  • Earnings from hydro almost tripled to $4mn for the year
  • FGEN is trading at 7.1x FY22E and a discount to our TP of P34/share
