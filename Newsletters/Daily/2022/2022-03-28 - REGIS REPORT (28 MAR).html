REGIS REPORT

28 March 2022

Property firm Vista Land (VLL PM) is pursuing its own REIT listing, with a plan to spin of ten malls and two office buildings.  These properties have a leasable area of 256,000 sqm, close to 25% of VLL’s rental portfolio.

The REIT offering/listing will raise up to P9.2bn for the group and should reduce its relatively high gearing level (93% net gearing).  We reckon one thing to watch out for in this REIT offering is how reported rental revenue matches actual cash rents given its policy for straight-line accounting of rents under long-term contracts. For VLL's rental portfolio (not necessarily for the planned REIT), we estimate EBIT would be 75% lower if straight-line accounting were not adopted.

The government’s infrastructure spending rose +1.5% in Dec to P135bn.  This took full year infra spend to P1.124trn, +28% YoY and equivalent to 5.8% of GDP.

+++

Meanwhile, the country looks like it might have to contend with another potential natural calamity again on top of the pandemic, the seasonal ones (e.g. typhoons) as well as the man-made ones. The Taal volcano is rumbling again and the government has raise its status to Alert Level 3.  If it erupts, it could affect again the areas around the volcano, which is only 60km south of Metro Manila.

Among the major property names, Ayala Land (ALI PM) has the biggest proportion of landbank close to Taal, with as much as 40% of its NAV in the Cavite, Laguna and Batangas area.

We hosted a call with Bob Herrera-Lim of Teneo Intelligence on Friday to discuss his views on the upcoming Presidential elections in May.

Ferdinand Marcos, Jr (aka “Bongbong”, or “BBM”) continues to lead the race, with surveys from PulseAsia having him at a commanding 60%. The current Vice President, Leni Robredo, is in distant second place at 15%. Granted, that survey was conducted in mid-February, which in political speak is ancient history. Since then, Ms Robredo’s rallies have drawn huge crowds in cities in Metro Manila and around the country. Some have pointed to Google data showing that the tide may have shifted significantly in her favor. Ms Robredo needs to have gotten herself up to at least the 20-30% range by mid-March to have a chance at winning. Prior survey results as of late March / early April have so far correctly predicted the winner in May.

Mr Herrera-Lim reckons a Marcos, Jr win would come with great uncertainty, if only because he has not attended any of the debates, or given many interviews. His campaign has largely been based on rehabilitating the Marcos family name, and the purported legacy of his father, who ran the country for 20 years, mostly under Martial Law. The role of government during the period of Marcos, Sr, was definitely much larger than it is today. Among other things, Marcos Sr instituted the Oil Price Stabilization Fund (OPSF) which managed the retail price of petrol. There was also significant state presence in the agriculture industry, including directed lending, and participation in the retail market. Many industries (including coconuts, sugar etc.) were effectively nationalized and/or controlled by select “champions”.

Bongbong Marcos has publicly spoken about the benefits of an oil subsidy, and on intervention in the rice industry. Either could have significant negative implications on the fiscal deficit, which is still suffering from heavy Covid-related spending. Whether this is just populist election rhetoric in a period of high inflation, or a true policy statement is unclear.

Beyond just these specific pronouncements, which could be interpreted as just rhetoric about reviving a mythical past, the greater uncertainty is the lack of clarity about who his economic advisers are. Should Marcos win the Presidency, he will need to quickly announce his economic team, who will hopefully be credible, and broadly orthodox in policy. Fingers crossed.

PROPERTY
Vista Land to raise up to P9.2bn from REIT listing
  • Vista Land will spin off 10 malls and two PEZA-registered offices into a REIT
    • REIT GLA of 256,000sqm
  • 3.33bn secondary shares with an overallotment option of 333.75mn shares
  • Maximum offer price of P2.50/share
  • Implies a maximum market cap of P25bn
  • The malls are located in: Las Pinas, Bacoor, General Trias, Imus, Tanza, Antipolo, San Jose Del Monte, San Fernando, and Talisay in Cebu
  • The office buildings are in Taguig and Bacoor
  • As of 3Q21, Vista Land had total debt of P167bn and net debt of P104bn
    • Net debt/equity of 93%
  • Vista’s entire rental portfolio has a GLA of about 1mn sqm
  • Vista’s 9M21 rental revenue jumped 25% YoY to P6.2bn, 20% higher than the 9M19 figure
    • This is a contrast to other mall operators, whose mall revenues are about 60% lower than 2019 levels
    • We estimate that Vista’s actual cash rents are only about 60% of reported rental revenue in 9M21
  • At the maximum offer price, Vista’s NAV could rise by 5%-10%
  • VLL is trading at 65% discount to NAV, 0.3X FY22E P/B, 4.3X FY22E P/E
