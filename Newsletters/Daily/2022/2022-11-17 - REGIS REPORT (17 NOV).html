We got more details from results briefings of a few more companies yesterday.

Jollibee Foods (JFC PM) continued to see robust top line growth in 3Q, led by the Philippine business (+49% SSSG). Margin pressure from higher input cost was apparent, though the company took a deliberate strategy to price below inflation in order to squeeze out competitors. Despite this, the company continues to reap the benefits of the business transformation it undertook during the pandemic, with operating leverage eclipsing raw material cost inflation. JFC is on track to meet (or even beat) our FY22 operating profit estimates with the strong top line momentum persisting into early-4Q.

Meanwhile, Alliance Global (AGI PM) and Emperador (EMI PM) held a group briefing held yesterday.

Higher raw material and selling costs at Emperador (EMI) and lower win rates at Travellers weighed down Alliance Global’s 3Q. Cost pressures also caught up to Golden Arches (GADC), with EBIT margins falling 3ppts QoQ, even after raising prices four times early this year. Both EMI and GADC have already raised prices at the start of 4Q to recover some ground on the margin front.

For EMI specifically, brandy came under pressure from both weaker sales and higher opex. The resulting dip in contribution has propelled whisky to 55% of total profits.

BANKS
BSP keeps credit card rate cap; next review in Jan 2023
  • The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) reported that the Monetary Board decided to keep the existing rate/fee caps on credit card transactions. The “reasonableness” of the caps will be reviewed again in January.
  • Recall that the rate caps were set for the first time in 4Q2020. The most important of the caps was the rate on unpaid balances, set at 2.0% a month, or 24% per annum. Before the cap, banks were charging 3.0-3.5% a month on revolvers. Prior to 2020, the BSP did not (explicitly) regulate ANY rates/fees/charges that banks levied on its customers
  • That the BSP is reviewing the caps is potentially positive for banks. However, as we remarked back in 2020, the fact that the BSP imposed the cap at all was a slippery slope. Note that the BSP is reviewing the cap, but is not giving up its authority to regulate the caps at all. We remain wary that the BSP will use the precedent to set rate/fee caps on other bank products/services in the future.
TELCOS
SIM Registration ACT effective on December 27
  • The SIM Registration is expected to be made effective on December 27
  • According to Senator Grace Poe during a hearing, the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) is expected to issue the implementing rules and regulations for the SIM Registration Act on December 12
  • By then, new SIM cards will have to be registered before they are activated
  • Existing SIM users will have to register within 180 days (with possible 120 days extension)
PROPERTY
Filinvest REIT to acquire Boracay land
  • Filinvest REIT (FILRT PM) will acquire 29,000sqm of land in Boracay from Filinvest Development Corp
    • Filinvest Development Corp is the parent company of Filinvest Land (the sponsor of Filinvest REIT)
  • The land will be acquired for P1.05bn (exclusive of VAT)
    • FILRT estimates that this will yield 6%
    • The acquisition price will be paid in installments
  • The land will be to affiliate Boracay Seascapes Inc for 40 years
    • BSI is the owner of Crimson Resort & Spa Boracay
    • The lease is based on a percentage of revenue (with a minimum guaranteed rent)
  • FILRT currently offers a 2023E dividend yield of 7.3% (based on consensus forecast)
OTHERS
Foreign ownership inches up in October
  • We estimate that market foreign ownership rose 8bps MoM to 21.0% while PSEi foreign ownership also increased 8bps MoM to 25.2% (in October)
  • Market net foreign selling dropped to US$12mn in October (from US$216mn in September)
  • The increase in market foreign ownership appears to be due to the outperformance of some high foreign-owned stocks such as: GLO (+14.4% MoM), ICT (+10.5% MoM), TEL (+8.4% MoM), SM (+13.1% MoM)
  • 21 PSEi constituents saw declines in foreign ownership while 7 experienced increases
  • The notable movements among PSEi constituents were:
    • SMC –saw the highest increase in foreign ownership. Its foreign ownership rose 1.16 percentage points MoM to 5.6%. It also experienced the highest net foreign buying for the second consecutive month.
    • AC – saw the largest drop in foreign ownership, which fell 1.02 percentage points MoM to 25.2%. It also experienced the second highest net foreign selling for the month (at US$7.9mn)
    • PGOLD – foreign ownership declined 56 basis points to 18.9%. Foreign selling amounted to US$3.7mn
