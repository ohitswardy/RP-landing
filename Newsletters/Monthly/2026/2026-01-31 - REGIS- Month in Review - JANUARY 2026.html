REGIS REPORT

31 January 2026
 

Philippine equities kicked off 2026 on strong footing, with the PCOMP advancing 4.6% or 276.05 pts MoM in January to close at 6,328.97. The rally was underpinned by strong foreign inflows and improving risk appetite, which allowed the index to break above its 200-day moving average early in the month and briefly challenge the 6,500 level. However, momentum faded toward month-end amid macro disappointments as 4Q25 GDP hugely missed estimates while the central bank struck a less dovish tone in their latest comments around monetary policy.

 

Foreign participation was a key driver, with net foreign buying totaling a robust $226m for the month although largely concentrated in large-cap names. The peso’s stabilization toward month-end also helped temper risk aversion.

 

ICT (+13.2% MoM) was the standout performer, hitting fresh all-time highs and delivering the largest positive contribution to the index on strong foreign accumulation. This continued outperformance lifted ICT's index weight to 17.9% from 16.5% at end-2025. In contrast, property stocks were the biggest drags on the PCOMP, with SMPH (-5.1% MoM) and ALI (-5.3% MoM) pulling back as weak GDP data and soft demand indicators revived concerns over a slow recovery in the residential segment.

 

Elsewhere, mining stocks surged, taking the sector index up 25.8% MoM which tracked the strong upswing in global gold prices for most of January. However, a sharp reversal in metal prices on the last trading day -- after Asia’s close -- erased roughly half of those gains, pointing to potential near-term choppiness in February.

 

Macro developments were mixed though more skewed negatively. Inflation surprised with a higher-than-expected print in December but remained benign overall, while BSP guidance pointed to an easing cycle nearing its end, with only one more rate cut expected. Sentiment took a hit late in the month following a sharp miss in 4Q25 GDP, marked by weak capital formation and below-trend consumption as business and consumer sentiment softened in the wake of the flood control corruption scandal that unfolded over the latter half of last year. Against this cautious backdrop, government cut growth targets for 2026-27, from formerly 6-7% to 5-6% this year and 5.5-6.5% next year.

 

After hitting a fresh low of 59.470:$1 at mid-month, the peso rebounded to close just modestly lower for January, -0.1% MoM to 58.880:$1.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Index breadth remained positive in January with 18 advancers outnumbering 12 decliners.

 

ICT (+13.2% MoM) remained a standout as it continued to hit fresh all-time highs and accounted for the largest positive contribution to index performance. Gains were driven by strong foreign accumulation and positive structural developments, reinforcing its status as the market’s primary growth and liquidity anchor. With this outperformance, ICT finished the month with an index weight of 17.9%, up from 16.5% at end-2025.

 

As the rally broadened out in early January, conglos were among the most heavily bought up, with JGS (+17.5% MoM), AEV (+13.6% MoM), GTCAP (+12.6% MoM), and AC (+8.1% MoM) sustaining gains into month-end despite general market weakness.

 

JFC (+13.9% MoM) was another large outperformer as it announced plans to spin off and pursue a US listing of its international business by 2027, a move welcomed by investors who have long viewed its overseas operations as a drag on returns.

 

Elsewhere, mining stocks enjoyed outsized gains, with the sector index finishing the month up 25.8% MoM which mirrored the sharp advance in global gold prices for most of January. Note though that metal prices pared roughly half of their monthly gains on the final trading day after Asian markets had already closed, suggesting some near-term volatility could spill into early February trading.

 

Banks were mixed in January with market proxy BDO (+0.3% MoM) ending flat, reflecting increased cautiousness over domestic macro. BPI (+6.8% MoM) would have finished in the red for the month had it not been for outsized MOC buys on the last trading day of the month, coinciding with the PCOMP rebalancing. MBT (+6.6% MoM) outperformed within the sector as flows rotated into laggards.

 

Meanwhile, losses from the two largest developers were the biggest offset to the index's upward move. ALI (-5.3% MoM) and SMPH (-5.1% MoM) bore the brunt of selling from the deteriorating macro outlook, after 4Q25 GDP hugely missed market expectations and the BSP turned less dovish in recent comments around monetary policy.

 

Freshly-added RCR (-10.0% MoM) was the second largest decliner on the index, despite substantial passive inflows ($33.5m est.) expected for its entry into the PCOMP at the month-end rebalancing. Shares were sold down on rebalancing day as speculators unwound positions bought at a P7-bn ($119-m) placement of RCR shares done just a few days before.

 

Biggest decliner on the index was PLUS (-19.0% MoM) which continued to sell off on market chatter of insiders selling out amid current regulatory headwinds. In contrast, BLOOM (+12.6% MoM) rebounded from lows following news that the Philippines has allowed visa-free entry for Chinese nationals.

 
MACRO NEWS

4Q25 GDP grows 3.0%, missing 2025 target

GDP grew at 3.0% in 4Q24, decelerating sharply from the already lackluster (revised) 3.9% growth rate in 3Q. This is well below consensus and REGIS expectations of 4.2%. The 3.0% print is the lowest (ex-pandemic) since hitting 3.0% back in 3Q11. On the demand side, weakness was evident all around. The largest factor in the poor GDP print was the even steeper decline in capital formation (-10.9% from -2.8%). Capital formation has declined for two consecutive quarters, mainly due to the significant decline in construction. Consumption, both private (3.8%) and government (3.7%), was also below historical trend. Private consumption was 3.8%, the lowest since 3Q10's 2.6% (ex-pandemic), slowing further from 3Q25's 4.1%. Post-pandemic (2022 onwards), private consumption was at least 4.7% or higher. Net exports, however, saw significant improvement to 1.9% from 0.9% given robust growth in exports at +13.2% accelerating further from +7.4% the previous quarter. On the supply side, growth in all three major sectors (services, industry, and agri) deteriorated relative to the previous quarter. Growth in the service sector slowed a tad to 5.2% from 5.4% in 3Q. Industry sector growth turned negative at -0.9% for the first time (ex-pandemic) since 3Q11's -1.6%. Construction activity fell sharply to -7.1% from -0.4%, as growth remained in the negative given the impact of the flood control scandal on gov't infra spending. Government construction was negative for the third consecutive quarter at -41.9% much worse than the -26.2% growth in 3Q. Household construction contributed to the continued decline in capital formation as it posted a modest 2.2%, moderating from four consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. Bringing FY25 GDP growth to 4.4%, below the government's target of 4.8-5.0% (5.5-6.5% initial target) and the consensus forecast of 4.9%. The full year figure was also below REGIS' FY25E forecast of 4.8% and significantly slower than FY24's 5.7% GDP growth.

 

PH gov't revises GDP growth targets

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) has reduced the gov't's GDP growth targets for 2026-2027, revising the 2026-2028 6-7% target to 5-6% in 2026 and 5.5-6.5% in 2027, while keeping the 2028 target unchanged. The previously revised growth target in 2025 is still expected to be 4.8-5.0% (vs 5.5-6.5% initially). Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development Secretary Arsenio Baliscan cited "short-term fluctuations" from recent governance challenges and still present global uncertainties but notes that the economy's overall trajectory remains firm. He stated that the revision of growth targets will not derail fiscal planning as improving the quality of growth will remain a top priority. Balisacan expects growth to pick up in the latter half of this year as 1H is expected to "still not quite be as rosy as we would want it to be". Consumption is still expected to drive growth which will be supported by employment, growth and remittances as well as a rebound of consumer confidence. For 2026/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 5.3/5.8% and 5.5/5.7%, resp.

 

Trade deficit narrows to $3.5bn in Dec

The Dec trade deficit narrowed to US$3.5bn, contracting 15% YoY, reflecting the faster growth of exports vs imports. This brings the FY25 trade deficit to US$49.2bn, 10% narrower YoY. Imports for the month grew by 7% YoY to US$10.5bn, a reversal from the 2% decline in Nov. The rebound to growth was primarily driven by the surge in capital goods (+20%) and fuel imports (+6%). Notably, consumer goods fell 1%, the fourth consecutive month of a YoY decline—the longest streak since the pandemic in 2020. Exports climbed by 23% YoY to US$7.0bn, the twelfth consecutive month of YoY growth and accelerating from the 21% increase in Nov. Electronics exports jumped by 44% YoY to $4.0bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 58% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 3% to US$3.0bn.

 

 

Inflation inches up to 1.8% in December

Headline inflation picked up to 1.8% in Dec from Nov's 1.5%. This was higher than consensus expectations of 1.4% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.2-2.0% for Nov. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past ten months. Inflation in Dec accelerated slightly given faster YoY increases in vegetable prices. Food prices overall rose 1.4% YoY, accelerating from the 0.1% pace in Nov—driven by the +11.6% YoY jump in vegetable prices vs +4.0% the previous month. Notably, rice prices were up MoM (+2.8%)—marking the second MoM price increase since Jun 2024. Rice prices are down 12.3% YoY in Dec, the twelfth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P43.6/kg and P44.9/kg as of Dec 2025 vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan 2025, respectively. Core inflation was unchanged at 2.4% in Dec relative to the previous month, core non-food inflation decelerated to 2.1% in Dec from 2.3% in Nov but was offset by the acceleration in core food inflation to 2.9% from 2.5%. This brings the FY25 headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target but in line with the BSP's 2025 expectation of 1.7%. The 1.7% FY inflation print number brought is lower than the 3.2% in 2024 and the lowest in nine years since 2016. Consensus and Regis 2025 inflation expectations were 1.7% and 1.8%, respectively. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.6% and 3.0%. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

 

BSP uncertain on another rate cut this year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona guided that another rate cut this year is uncertain given current economic conditions. Recall that Remolona previously said that a rate cut in Feb was "on the table". He stated that a cut during the next Monetary Board meeting "… is still a maybe" and not certain. Remolona highlighted that if inflation and 4Q growth are benign and weaker-than-expected, respectively, it may prompt another cut. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%. The BSP's GDP growth forecasts for 2025/26/27 are 4.6/5.4/6.0%, respectively. For 2025/26/27, consensus and Regis expectations for GDP growth are at 4.9/5.3/5.8% and 4.8/5.5/5.7%, respectively. Consensus and Regis Inflation expectations for 2026 are 2.6% and 3.0%, respectively.

 

Government debt inches up to P17.65trn in end-Nov

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.5% MoM to P17.65trn as of end-Nov. The increase was driven the net issuance of domestic and external debt. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.7% and 31.3% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 0.6% MoM to P12.12trn. External debt rose by 0.3% MoM to P5.53trn. The P17.65trn figure exceeds the P17.36-trn end-2025 projection by 1.7%. Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury previously stated that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as fundraising activities wind down. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of 3Q25 stood at 63.1% of GDP unchanged vs 2Q25 but up from 60.7% in 4Q24. The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 61.3% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to increase in 2026 to 61.8%, then decrease to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% by 2028.

 

PH dollar reserves at US$110.9bn end-Dec

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Dec decreased 0.3% MoM to US$110.9bn. The slight increase primarily reflected the drop in foreign exchange holdings due to the BSP's intervention in the forex market but was offset by the continued surge in gold prices. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 4.0x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.4x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The end-2025 GIR figure of US$110.9bn is 4% higher YoY and ahead BSP's expectations of US$109bn by year-end. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

 

President Marcos vetoes P93bn in unprogrammed funds, including CARS incentive

President Marcos vetoed over P93bn of unprogrammed appropriations in the recently signed 2026 General Appropriations Act, including the following. P35.8bn of Philippine government counterpart funding for foreign-assisted projects. P4.3bn of fiscal support arrearages under the Comprehensive Automotive Resurgence Strategy (CARS) program. P50bn for the revised Armed Forces of the Philippines modernization program. Exec. Secretary Ralph Recto said that they will work on identifying a mechanism to ensure payment of CARS arrearages. Note that our forecasts do not assume Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) will receive any tax incentives under the CARS program. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 35% of profit. The veto cut unprogrammed funds to P150bn, the lowest level of unprogrammed appropriations since 2019.

 

Infra spending target set at 4.3% of GDP for 2026

The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) revised the 2026 infrastructure spending target to 4.3% of GDP (~P1.30trn) from 5.1% (~P1.56trn) previously. The cut in infra spending follows the flood control corruption scandal which weighed on gov't spending and economic growth last year. The 4.3% of GDP allocation for infra is the lowest since 2017. Acting Budget Secretary Rolando Toledo stated that the gov't sees little risk of delays in infra projects for 2026 given the "clean" budget process. Toledo said that the absence of "ghost projects" and fully specified allocations across programs should help streamline infra construction progress.

Remittances rise by 3.6% in November

Cash remittances amounted US$2.9bn in Nov, 3.6% higher YoY—accelerating from the 3.0% growth in Oct. This brings 11M25 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$32.1bn. Weak remittance growth from North America (e.g. USA: 0.5%) has been partially offset by stronger contributions from the rest of the world.

 

Commercial banks' loan growth unchanged at 10.3% in Nov

Preliminary data showed that loans of commercial banks was 10.3% in Nov, unchanged from the previous month of Oct. Loans to productive activities posted steady growth at 9.0% vs 9.1% in Oct. Consumer loans still grew at a heady pace of 22.9%, though a tad slower than Oct’s 23.1% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) decelerated to 7.6% in Nov from 8.3% in Oct.

 

SEC revises REIT regulations

The SEC issued Memorandum Circular No. 1, which revises the regulations regarding the REIT law. The SEC expanded the definition of “income generating real estate”. Income-generating real estate includes properties with steady cash flow, such as rentals, toll fees, user fees, ticket sales, parking fees and storage fees. They include toll roads, railways, airports and air navigation facilities, ports, information and communications technology infrastructure, energy infrastructure assets and data centers. REITs are permitted to directly or indirectly own real estate, including special purpose vehicles. The SEC also extended the period to reinvest in Philippine real estate to two years (from one year previously). Reinvestment may take the form of investments in equity, the extension of loans or purchase of debt instruments or the repayment of debt in relation to any real estate or infrastructure projects in the Philippines. We do not expect the revised regulations to materially affect existing REITs. However, they may lead to more listings of REITs from sectors such as telecommunications, power, infrastructure, etc.

 

IT-BPO Association “cautiously optimistic” about growth in 2026

Jonathan Madrid, President of the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines, said that the association was “cautiously optimistic” about growth in 2026. He said that two factors influencing the growth were: ease of doing business and supply of employable talent. The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) expects the industry to achieve revenue of US$42bn and employ 1.97mn Filipinos by the end of 2026. These represent 4%-5% growth from the 2025 revenue (US$40bn) and employee (1.9mn) figures. Mr. Madrid previously said that the industry’s growth will still be driven by core segments – financial services and healthcare. He also said that he expects Global Capability Centers (GCC) to grow faster. These are offshore units established by multinational corporations to provide specialized services such as finance, IT and customer support to their global operations. IBPAP previously reported that American and Australian companies had plans to expand in the Philippines.

 

Chinese nationals allowed visa-free entry to PH starting Jan 16

The Dept. of Foreign Affairs (DFA) announced that Chinese nationals will be allowed to enter the Philippines with no visa requirement for a stay of up to 14 days. The visa-free 14-day stay is non-extendable and non-convertible to any other type of PH visa category. Requirements for Chinese nationals are a passport valid for at least six months, confirmed accommodation/booking, and a return ticket to next country of destination. The visa-free privilege is only valid through Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA) in Metro Manila and Mactan-Cebu International Airport (MCIA) in Cebu. The visa-free entry will be in effect for one year and will be reviewed prior the expiry of the arrangement. As of 11M25, Chinese tourists account for 5% of total tourist arrivals, much lower than the 18% contribution to total back in 2019.

 

CAMPI says vehicle sales (ex-BYD) up 2% YoY in December; Toyota sales up 18% YoY

CAMPI data showed that car sales rose 2% YoY to 42,870 units in December. Brings FY25 unit sales of CAMPI members down by 0.8% YoY to 463,646 units. Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI and BYD previously reported its FY25 rose more than five-fold to 26,122 units (~5% market share). Including other industry data, CAMPI said that vehicle sales stood at 491,395 units in FY25, up 3.7%. Sales of electric vehicles (including other available industry data) reached 58,905 units in FY25, accounting for 12% of demand. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 229,447 units in FY25, up 5.2% YoY but fell a tad short of our forecast of 231k units (+6%). Derived Dec 2025 volumes stood at 23,895 units, up 17.9% YoY. Toyota’s market share (incl. BYD) may have reached 47% in FY25, up from ~46% in FY24. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 23% of FY26E profit.

Lower House repasses Department of Water Resources bill

The Lower House of the 20th Congress approved House Bill 6789, which is a measure seeking to create the Department of Water Resources. Recall that the Lower House approved a similar proposal on third reading in the 19th Congress last December 2023 but the bill was not tackled by the Senate. The bill’s salient provisions are: Creation of a “Department for Water Resources”, which will solely supervise the country’s water resources (except fisheries and aquaculture), sanitation, flood risk, and drought risk management. Creation of a “Water Regulatory Commission”, which will be an independent, quasi-judicial regulatory body with overall authority and powers on all public and private providers. Reconstitution of the National Water Resources Board as the “National Water Resource Allocation Board”, which would serve as the approval body for using water resources, including dam construction. We would view the passage of this bill positively, as a more streamlined policies could help draw investments in the water sector.

 

Impeachment complaint filed against President Marcos

An impeachment complaint has been filed against President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. over his alleged involvement in a corruption scheme concerning unprogrammed appropriations. The complaint also cited the alleged kidnapping and surrender of former President Rodrigo Duterte to the International Criminal Court, alleged failure to veto unprogrammed appropriations and other unconstitutional provisions in past budgets, alleged kickbacks from budget insertions, among others. This is the first impeachment complaint against Marcos since he assumed presidency in 2022. The Committee on Justice will determine if the complaint has probable cause to proceed; otherwise, it may recommend dismissal of the complaint. The House plenary may override the dismissal recommendation if at least one-third of all House members vote to proceed with the impeachment trial. If the impeachment complaint is dismissed before a Senate trial, another cannot be initiated against him for one year, as prohibited in the Constitution.

 

Supreme Court rejects impeachment case against Vice President with finality

The Supreme Court (SC) dismissed the motions for reconsideration on its ruling that declared last year’s impeachment case against the Vice President unconstitutional. Last year, the SC dismissed the impeachment complaint delivered by the House of Representatives. The SC cited constitutional infirmities, including the apparent lack of due process, et al. The dismissal was not really in doubt since the SC decision to stop the impeachment proceedings last year was unanimous. The SC decision, however, does not stop new impeachment proceedings from being initiated beginning Jan 14.
