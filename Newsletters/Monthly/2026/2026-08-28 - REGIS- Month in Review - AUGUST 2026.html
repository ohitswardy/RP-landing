REGIS REPORT

28 August 2026
 

Philippine equities erased its July rally in August, with persistent foreign selling, profit-taking in large caps, and a weaker macro outlook weighing on sentiment. The PCOMP fell 4.5% or 280.11 pts MoM to 5,956.33, turning negative YTD by 1.6%. The index broke below the 6,000 level toward month-end as investors digested a weak 2Q GDP print, underwhelming 2Q corporate earnings, higher inflation expectations, and another BSP rate hike, while the peso weakened to a record low.

 

The month's biggest macro concern was the combination of slowing growth and renewed inflation pressures. 2Q GDP growth slowed sharply to 2.3% YoY from 2.8% in 1Q, well below expectations, as weaker consumption and a deeper investment contraction weighed on activity. While July inflation eased for a third straight month to 6.2% from 6.4% mostly from lower transport prices, the BSP still raised its 2027 inflation forecast sharply to 5.4% from 4.5%, as it remained cautious over elevated oil prices, El Niño, and wage pressures.

 

The BSP consequently delivered another 25-bp rate hike to 5.0%, its third consecutive increase since the tightening cycle resumed in April. While the move was widely expected and the BSP signaled that further hikes may not be necessary, the combination of higher inflation expectations and weak growth reinforced concerns around the medium-term macro outlook. The peso weakened 1.6% MoM to a record-low of 62.260:$1, while the PH10Y yield eased by 17.2 bps MoM to 7.2577% as global yields moderated late in the month.

 

Corporate activity provided some of the month's biggest stock-specific catalysts. KKR's proposal to acquire an additional stake in FGEN (+29.5% MoM) and launch a tender offer for its public float sent shares sharply higher. At its peak, FGEN was up 57% MTD before much of the gains reversed following parent's FPH (+5.3% MoM) rejection of the P35/share offer. Meanwhile, DHI (+99.0% MoM) shares had doubled prior to the suspension of trading of its shares, after weeks of speculation around the consolidation of the Sy family's mining assets -- including the massive Tampakan copper-gold project -- was validated later in the month with the M&A announcement.

 

Not to be left behind, other miners rallied in August following the rebound in metal prices, led by gold and copper miners PX (+24.9% MoM) and APX (+13.9% MoM). A standout laggard was SCC (-15.9% MoM) -- also the largest decliner on the PCOMP -- which remained under pressure as the government released revised draft rules for the rebidding of the Semirara coal contract, increasing uncertainty over the concession's economics and viability.

 

Toward month-end, large-cap index names corrected amid heavy foreign selling and profit-taking. ICT (-8.1% MoM) was the biggest drag on the index as investors locked in gains following its strong run, with concerns on operations and prospects in Brazil adding to the pressure. Maersk's shift of cargo away from ICT's Suape terminal and potentially greater competition for the Santos bid weighed on sentiment. Meanwhile, market proxies BDO (-6.8% MoM) and SM (-6.6% MoM) pulled back amid lingering macro concerns.

 

Foreign investors were net sellers throughout the month, with cumulative outflows of roughly $223m. August selling was compounded by MSCI rebalancing flows, particularly with the deletion of ALI (-8.0% MoM) from the Standard Index, while an overnight placement for SMPH (-2.5% MoM) shares also contributed to foreign outflows.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Large banks underperformed amid the broader market sell-off and continued foreign outflows. BDO (-6.8% MoM) was the biggest drag among the large banks as investors took profits amid weaker macro expectations, while BPI (-2.4% MoM) also declined.

 

Property was also weak, pressured by macro concerns as well as soft 2Q results with persistent underlying weakness in the residential segment. ALI (-8.0% MoM) was the biggest laggard, dragged by earnings disappointment as well as its deletion from the MSCI Standard Index. SMPH (-2.5% MoM) saw an overnight share placement mid-month while shares were generally weighed down by foreign selling.

 

Conglomerates broadly declined, with market proxies SM (-6.6% MoM) and AC (-3.2% MoM) among the laggards. Dividend payers DMC (+6.0% MoM), AEV (+4.5% MoM), and LTG (+3.8% MoM) managed to buck the downtrend.

 

ICT (-8.1% MoM) was the biggest drag on the index as foreign investors locked in gains, with concerns around its Brazil operations adding to the pressure. Maersk's planned shift of cargo away from ICT's Suape terminal and potentially greater competition for the Santos terminal bid weighed on sentiment.

 

Consumer names were largely resilient despite concerns over the weaker macro outlook. JFC (+0.8% MoM) was supported by record 2Q earnings that bore a significant sequential recovery in margins, although domestic SSSG moderated and management lowered its FY26 guidance. CNPF (+3.3% MoM)and URC (+3.1% MoM) also gained.

 

Power and utilities were mostly weaker amid regulatory uncertainty and stock-specific developments. MER (-4.2% MoM) remained weak as investors weighed the potential earnings implications of proposed EPIRA amendments and uncertainty around its rate rebasing. Broad downbeat sentiment on regulated businesses also hit MWC (-2.0% MoM) and MYNLD (-0.5% MoM). ACEN (-1.7% MoM) declined following a weaker-than-expected 2Q result. FGEN (+29.5% MoM) was the standout, surging after KKR proposed to acquire FPH's 8.4% stake and launch a tender offer for FGEN's public float, although much of its gains were pared after FPH rejected the P35/share offer. SCC (-15.9% MoM) sold off as the DOE revised draft rules for the rebidding of its coal contract, raising uncertainty over the concession's economics and viability.

 

Telcos broadly sold off mostly on soft 2Q results. CNVRG (-15.6% MoM) led the decline dragged by disappointing 2Q results that showed a decline in subscribers. GLO (-9.9% MoM) and TEL (-7.1% MoM) also declined amid broad foreign selling.

 

Gaming bucked the broader market weakness lifted by bottom-fishing. BLOOM (+7.5% MoM) bounced following months of significant underperformance, while PLUS (+1.9% MoM) was relatively resilient despite lingering concerns on online gaming. Both names, however, remained large underperformers YTD.

 

Mining continued to outperform as gold prices rebounded. PX (+24.9% MoM) led gains, followed by APX (+13.9% MoM). DHI (+99.0% MoM) was the month's biggest stock mover, surging on speculation around the consolidation of the Sy family's mining assets.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP raises key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%; revises inflation expectations

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised the key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%. The rate hike was in line with expectations. This is the third 25bp rate hike in a row since the rate hike cycle resumed back in April. Despite easing headline inflation, the "preemptive move" was done due to the still volatile oil prices as well as the looming El Niño and wage hike. BSP Governor Eli Remolona said the central bank is hopeful that no further rate hikes will be necessary, although it remains open to further tightening if needed to bring inflation back toward its ±3% (2-4%) long-term target. The central bank revised its inflation estimates for 2026/27E from 6.4%/4.5% to 6.1%/5.4%. July CPI was 6.2%, bringing the 7M26 average inflation rate to 5.0%. This implies that inflation could average 7.6% for the rest of the year. The BSP expects headline inflation to peak in 4Q26 and ease closer to the ±3% target by 4Q27. The BSP also stated that economic growth is expected to rebound in 4Q26, as fundamentals for growth "are still in place". The fifth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled this year will be held on Oct 22.

 

Philippine 2Q26 GDP growth slows to 2.3%, falls short of expectations

Philippine GDP grew 2.3% YoY in 2Q26, slowing from 2.8% in 1Q26 and missing consensus expectations of 2.9%. This marked the weakest pace of economic growth since 4Q09, excluding the pandemic period. Growth was weighed down by weaker domestic consumption and investment amid the prolonged Middle East conflict and elevated energy prices, with household consumption slowing to 2.8% from 3.0% and investment contracting 9.2% versus a 3.1% decline in the previous quarter. Government spending accelerated to 8.3% from 4.8%, while exports strengthened to 12.2% from 7.8% and import growth moderated to 5.5% from 6.8%. The government had recently lowered its 2026 GDP growth target to 3.5-4.5% from 5%-6%, which implies the economy will need to grow by at least 4.4% in 2H26 to meet the lower end of the target range. Note too that the government has announced additional cash aid, fuel subsidies, and tax relief measures to support households and small businesses.

 

Inflation moderates further to 6.2% in July

Headline inflation moderated to 6.2% in Jul from Jun's 6.4%. This was within the BSP's forecast range of 5.6-6.6% for Jul. The softer inflation print is the third consecutive month of deceleration following five consecutive months of accelerating inflation. This brings 7M26 inflation to 5.0%, above the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) but still below their forecast of 6.4% for the year. Inflation in Jul decelerated given primarily driven by the slower YoY increase in fuel prices vs Jun. Food prices overall rose 5.2% YoY, unchanged relative to May, as lower prices of meat were offset by higher rice prices. Rice prices climbed 17.0% YoY in Jul, picking up from the 15.0% YoY increase in Jun. Rice prices are currently at P48.5/kg and P47.9/kg as of end-Jul for local and imported well-milled rice. Transport prices rose 11.9% YoY, slower than +12.8% the previous month. The increase in diesel prices was relatively steady at +38.5% YoY from +39.0% YoY, while the gasoline price increase moderated to +34.1% YoY from +39.2% YoY in Jun. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, eased to 4.2% in Jul from 4.4% in Jun, as the moderation in core non-food (4.4% from 4.6% in Jun) offset the acceleration in core food (3.6% from 3.3%). The BSP in a statement highlighted that the acceleration in core inflation indicates persistent underlying inflationary pressures despite moderation in headline inflation. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.4%. The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Aug 27. The current policy rate is 4.75%.

Government posts P106bn budget deficit in July

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P106bn in July, wider by 462% YoY as spending outpaced revenues. This brings the 7M26 deficit to P893bn, 14% wider vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in July rose a modest 2% YoY given the sharp decline in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections picked up to +7% from +5% the previous month. Customs collections moderated to +7% growth from +12% the previous month. Non-tax revenues fell 40% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances. Recall that dividends from GOCCs (Gov't-Owned and Controlled Corporations) were remitted early this year in Feb. Gov't spending was up 20% YoY, picking up from the 4% growth in June. Primary spending for the month accelerated to +17% vs +5% the month prior. The acceleration in spending was attributed to direct payments for various rail transport projects as well as the continued implementation of the UPLIFT framework projects to address rising oil prices. Recall that primary spending declined YoY for six consecutive months from July 2025 to Jan 2026, following President Marcos' disclosure of the flood control scandal during his SONA. Interest payments rose 9% YoY. The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66trn (-5.4% of GDP), 5% wider YoY from P1.58trn (-5.6% of GDP) in 2025.

 

BOP posts US$1.5bn deficit in July; GIR settles at US$103.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of July posted a deficit of US$1.5bn. A reversal from the US$3.4bn surplus in the previous month of June but wider than the US$167mn deficit in July 2025. Recall that the gov't raised US$2.5bn through a US dollar bond issuance in June. This brings the 7M26 deficit to US$5.4bn, narrower than the US$5.8bn deficit in the same period last year. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$10.7bn (2.1% of GDP), much wider than the US$5.7bn deficit (1.2% of GDP) in 2025. Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-July stood at US$103.3bn, -1.4% MoM. The BSP attributed the decline to the central bank's net foreign exchange operations and the national gov't's drawdowns on foreign currency deposits for external debt payments. The peso weakened to P61.4/USD at end-July from P61.2/USD at end-June. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.7 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at US$104.0bn, down from US$110.8bn in 2025.

 

Moody's affirms "Baa2 stable" investment rating for the Philippines

Moody's Ratings retained its "Baa2 stable" investment-grade rating for the Philippines on expectations of a gradual economic recovery alongside continued fiscal consolidation efforts by the gov't. Recall that in April earlier this year, Moody’s also retained its Baa2 stable rating for the Philippines. The "stable" outlook was maintained given the country's medium-term growth potential and expectations of stabilizing fiscal metrics over the next two years despite headwinds. Moody's also cited ample access to financing markets and sufficient international reserves. Concerns include slower private investment, infrastructure execution delay, debt affordability, physical climate risk, and political uncertainty. A ratings upgrade would require sustained fiscal consolidation, a clear decline in gov't debt, and stronger medium-term growth.

The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) provided further details on the P7.2trn (+6% YoY) 2027 National Expenditure Program (NEP), with education and infrastructure identified as the government’s top priorities, accounting for 16% and 14% of the total budget, respectively. The proposed budget will be discussed in and revised by Congress before it is enacted into law. Education (DepEd and CHED) was allocated the biggest portion of the budget at P1.15trn (-5% YoY, 3.5% of GDP). DepEd alone is set to receive P976bn, the largest allocation among gov't departments. The allocation for education includes P61bn for universal access to tertiary education, P38bn for gov't assistance, P31bn for facilities, among others.

 

Dept. of Finance open to reduce some taxes, wealth tax proposal

Dept. of Finance (DoF) Secretary Frederick Go stated that the gov't is open to proposals to reduce or repeal some taxes, such as the VAT, granted that alternative sources will offset the resulting revenue losses. The gov't expects VAT and related sales tax collections to amount to P762bn in 2026, 12% higher YoY from P680bn (+6%) in 2025. This proposal is separate from the PROGRESS bill which includes higher tax rates on sin products with annual increases as well as a 75% (vs. 50% previously) automobile excise tax for vehicles worth more than P8mn.

DOE publishes draft bidding framework for Semirara coal contract, other natural resources

The Department of Energy posted the draft framework that will likely govern the bidding of the Semirara coal contract. Point 6.3 introduces a “minimum economic threshold” based on the government’s own valuation of confirmed reserves, although the document did not specify the valuation methodology. This threshold amount will not be disclosed to the bidders, but financial offers must meet or exceed this threshold. Under Point 6.9, the financial offer, essentially the royalty payment to the government, will now be the sole determinant of the winning bidder. The basis for determining royalty payments remains unclear, including whether these will be based on gross or net revenues and whether they will be fixed or subject to minimum annual payments. Based on the draft, the terms of the royalty appear to be left to the bidder’s discretion. Recall that the previous auction gave preference to the most technically and financially competent operator. Point 7.4 requires bidders to commit to supplying a defined portion of production to domestic end-users, with the government seeking to dictate both volume and pricing. Energy Secretary Sharon Garin also announced that the bidding for the Semirara coal block will be delayed by at least two (2) months from the original September target.

 

President Marcos orders creation of critical minerals policy framework

President Marcos signed Executive Order No. 122, which establishes a national policy framework for the critical minerals industry and reorganizes the Mining Industry Coordinating Council (MICC). The MICC will be jointly chaired by the secretaries of Department of Environment and Natural Resources and Department of Finance. Creation of a comprehensive industry work plan by the MICC within 90 days, in which international sourcing standards, environmental safeguards, and valuation frameworks are outlined. The DENR was directed to issue a Philippine Critical Minerals List within 30 days of the order taking effect. The DENR and the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) will create a streamlined permit processing within six months from the issuance of EO 122, which will entail a fully digital virtual one-stop shop platform to be implemented within a one-year period. The Department of Trade and Industry is tasked to provide incentives for downstream refining, battery production, and side-stream industries. A “use it or lose it” policy will be implemented for critical minerals mining tenements, immediately cancelling and reallocating freed-up mineral areas into declared reservations.

Legislator seeks two-year suspension of property valuation law

House Ways and Means Committee Chairman Romero Quimbo filed House Bill No. 10808 (HB 10808), which seeks to suspend the adoption of new or updated Schedules of Market Values (SMVs) and general property assessments, which are key components of the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA). President Marcos signed the RPVARA into law in 2024. Under HB 10808, current SMVs, zonal values, assessed values and other valuation bases will remain in effect during the two-year suspension for purposes of national and local taxation, assessments, fees, charges and government transactions. Mr. Quimbo said the suspension would allow government agencies to continue preparatory work for the implementation of RPVARA. The RPVARA transfers the responsibility of updating the “Schedule of Market Values” (from which real property taxes are based) from Local Government Units (such as city governments) to the national government. While raising real property taxes would expand the coffers of a city government, it could also lead to the mayor (and other officials) losing the next election. As a result, local governments in many places are hesitant to regularly update their Schedule of Market Values. As a result, there are cities/municipalities that might not have updated it for over 10 years. RPVARA is slated to take full effect in 2028.

Senator files Bill to ban online gambling advertisements

Senator Francis Escudero filed Senate Bill No. 2347 (SB 2347) or the proposed Gambling Advertising Prohibition Act. Senator Escudero compared the Bill to the Tobacco Regulation Act of 2003. SB 2347 would prohibit gambling advertisements across television, radio, newspapers, magazines, billboards, websites, online platforms, and social media. Gambling advertisements are currently prohibited on print media but are allowed on television and social media. It would also ban celebrity and influencer endorsements, product placements, promotional giveaways, branded merchandise appealing to people under 21 and gambling sponsorships involving sports, concerts, and cultural events. The proposal provides a one-year transition period after the law takes effect, allowing operators, advertisers, broadcasters, and platforms to wind down existing agreements. No new advertising or sponsorship agreement could be entered into during that period except where necessary to conclude existing obligations.

 

SEC looking at more capital market reforms

SEC Chairperson Atty. Francis Lim is studying potential capital market reforms. The SEC is considering raising the P100mn minimum capital requirement for stockbrokers, which was instituted in 2001. It is also working with the PSE and the Philippine Dealing and Exchange (PDEx) on market-making rules for publicly listed securities (in order to enhance liquidity). It is consulting with the World Bank to review the framework for public offerings, including requirements for debt and equity securities. The review seeks to make capital raising more accessible by making disclosure requirements more proportionate to the type and risks of securities being offered. It is collaborating with the ADB to develop a Philippine Capital Market Master Plan. The goal is to improve the competitiveness of the domestic market.
