REGIS REPORT

29 May 2026
 

Philippine stocks clocked a third consecutive monthly decline in May, taking the PCOMP down by 1.1% or 64.88 pts MoM to 5,768.76 which is its lowest close in six months, as investors contended with a downbeat macroeconomic backdrop, weak corporate earnings, rising interest rate expectations, geopolitical uncertainty, and significant index-related outflows. This took the PCOMP's YTD losses to 4.7%, the second worst performer in Asean after JCI's -28.3%, and lagging well behind largely positive global equity markets for the year thus far.

 

Early in the month, sentiment was weighed down by April inflation accelerating to 7.2% from 4.1% in March, also blowing past consensus estimate of 5.5% and the BSP's 5.6-6.4% forecast range, while 1Q26 GDP growth slowed to 2.8%, hugely missing an already very weak 3.3% consensus forecast. Oil prices remaining persistently high has reinforced concerns over a stagflationary environment and prompted expectations of further BSP policy tightening.

 

Of course, geopolitical developments in the Middle East also remained a major overhang during the month. Markets swung between optimism over a potential US-Iran peace agreement and renewed risk aversion following the re-escalation of military tensions later in the month, which drove volatility in oil prices and further clouded the outlook for inflation and economic growth.

 

Against this backdrop, investors were cautious throughout the 1Q26 reporting season. While aggregate earnings still posted mid-single-digit growth and consumer demand appeared relatively resilient, results generally pointed to mounting pressure on corporate profitability from higher input costs, margin compression, and elevated financing expenses. Growing concerns over weakening consumption and business activity due to persistently high oil prices further limited risk appetite.

 

Technical and flow-related factors weighed heavily as the month progressed, as seen in the four biggest index decliners for May. MSCI rebalancing outflows dragged JFC (-20.2% MoM) which was deleted from the Standard Index and migrated into Small Cap, as well as MER (-12.5% MoM) which was down-weighted on its lower foreign inclusion factor. Speculation surrounding the upcoming PSE index review for the July 2025 to June 2026 period pressured stocks perceived to be at risk of exclusion when MYNLD (+1.1% MoM) is inevitably added, leading to sharp sell-downs in PLUS (-22.7% MoM) and CNVRG (-13.4% MoM).

 

Meanwhile, ACEN (+16.2% MoM) was by far the top gainer on the index, bought up on the resurgence of the renewable energy theme as global markets grapple with the energy crisis triggered by the US-Iran war. Another outperformer was ICT (+6.0% MoM) which notched fresh highs anew in May amid strong earnings and a resilient outlook despite global and domestic macro headwinds.

 

Rising domestic bond yields, which reached their highest levels since 2018, compounded pressure on equities, particularly interest rate-sensitive sectors. At month-end, the PH10Y yield was at 7.5208% -- modestly off the recent peak at 7.8094%. The peso also hit a historic low at 61.745:$1 by mid-May, and closed the month just slightly off the lows at 61.596, down 0.2% MoM.

 

With $100m of net foreign outflow at the MSCI rebalancing at month-end, the May tally reached $150m, taking total YTD net outflow to $226m.

Key data
 
 

Foreign net selling reached $149m in May, extending the market's outflow streak to three months. MSCI held its semi-annual rebalancing in May, where the expeced net outflows for the Philippines were over $200 million, larger than the usual outflows seen in past rebalancing events. But despite the liquidity event, market activity only saw marginal improvement MoM at $119m vs $115m with foreigners more active at 58% market participation.

  • Banks and Properties saw heavy outflows this April following 1Q26 results from BDO, BPI, ALI, and SMPH that trailed market expectations. All four stocks hit new 52-week lows this month. Notably, SMPH marked its 15th straight month of net foreign selling, while ALI extended its outflow streak to 8 months.
  • Consumer names also saw significant outflows due to inflation concerns following March’s (much) higher-than-expected CPI print, and the BSP’s consequent sharp upward revision of its average inflation forecast for 2026. Outflows for both JFC and CNPF accelerated vs the previous month, dragging both shares prices to 52-week lows. Meanwhile, PGOLD and MONDE eked out gains, with the former being the top foreign-bought name this April.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Index breadth was still negative in May amid broad risk-off mood. Mining was the only broadly positive space while the resurgence of the renewables theme across global markets lifted ACEN (+16.2% MoM), which was the top gainer in the PCOMP for the month.

Banks were mostly lower as investors weighed the deteriorating macro backdrop against rising interest rate expectations. BPI (+4.9% MoM) was the sector's lone outperformer, supported by bargain-hunting following its sharp correction in prior months, while BDO (-0.1% MoM) was relatively resilient despite broader sector weakness. CBC (-8.5% MoM) and MBT (-5.2% MoM) were the bigger drags on the sector.

Property names remained under pressure as elevated bond yields and a more hawkish BSP outlook weighed on sentiment. ALI (-3.4% MoM) and SMPH (-3.2% MoM) continued to decline as investors remained cautious on residential demand and earnings prospects. The large REITs traded mostly lower for the month, although RCR (+3.6% MoM) stood out following strong foreign inflows and continued demand for defensive yield plays.

Conglomerates were mixed, reflecting the divergence in their underlying businesses. AEV (+9.3% MoM) was among the best-performing index names after strong earnings from its power unit. In contrast, AC (-6.6% MoM) underperformed after reporting weaker-than-expected 1Q results dragged by misses from key subsidiaries including banks and property, while SM (-5.3% MoM) also dropped amid subdued consumer and property sentiment.

Consumer stocks were highly polarized during the month. Early optimism following stronger-than-expected earnings from staples names faded as investors increasingly focused on margin pressures and weakening purchasing power. CNPF (-8.1% MoM) and JFC (-20.2% MoM) were among the largest decliners on the index, with JFC further pressured by earnings disappointment and its deletion from the MSCI Standard Index. On the other hand, PGOLD (+4.9% MoM) outperformed after delivering solid 1Q results marked by robust revenue growth, and MONDE (+4.1% MoM) was resilient largely thanks to upbeat dividends.

Power and utilities were mostly lower despite potential windfall gains from higher energy prices. ACEN (+16.2% MoM) was the standout amid renewed global interest in renewables, while MYNLD (+1.1% MoM) continued to benefit from its defensive earnings profile.  However, MER (-12.5% MoM) was a notable laggard after being down-weighted during the MSCI rebalance, triggering significant passive outflows.

ICT (+6.0% MoM) remained a standout throughout the month, notching fresh highs supported by strong earnings, persistent foreign inflows, and optimism surrounding higher allowable fund allocations after the BSP's circular increasing single-entity exposure limits for UITFs. That said, the stock saw a sharp correction toward the month-end MSCI rebalancing, which slashed gains for the month that reached 16.3% at its peak.

Telcos were also mixed. GLO (+2.6% MoM) advanced following reports that the IPO of fintech subsidiary Mynt (GCash) could take place as early as July, while PLDT (-8.1% MoM) weakened alongside the broadly weak market. CNVRG (-13.4% MoM) was among the biggest index decliners following its earnings miss and as speculation intensified over its potential deletion from the PSE Index in the upcoming August rebalancing.

Gaming remained the weakest-performing sector largely due to weak 1Q results that were well behind market expectations. PLUS (-22.7% MoM) was the largest decliner on the index as investors increasingly speculated on its possible removal from the PSE Index at the upcoming August review, while sentiment toward the broader gaming space remained subdued. BLOOM (-12.9% MoM) likewise underperformed amid lingering concerns over weak sector growth and as the company continued to print a net quarterly loss in 1Q.

Mining stocks generally outperformed, with gold miners OGP (+4.6% MoM), PX (+1.1% MoM), and APX (+1.0% MoM) posting gains as elevated precious metal prices supported sentiment.

 
MACRO NEWS

1Q26 GDP slows to +2.8% on subdued capital formation, food consumption

The Philippine economy grew at its slowest pace since Covid, at just +2.8% in 1Q26. Growth likewise fell short of consensus’ +3.3% forecast for the quarter. On the demand side, capital formation remained subdued, posting a -3.3% decline on top of the previous quarter’s 9.4% drop. Private consumption grew by a mere +3%, weighed down by food and non-alcoholic consumption, which barely grew (+0.3%). Construction declined by 4.5%, extending the 9.2% drop in 4Q25, dragged down by weak government infra spending. On the supply side, both agriculture and industry declined by -0.2% and -0.1%, respectively, while services grew +4.5%.

 

Inflation surges to 7.2% in April

Headline inflation accelerated to 7.2% in Apr from Mar's 4.1%. This was higher than consensus expectations of 5.5% and above the BSP's forecast range of 5.6-6.4% for Apr. The 7.2% print is the highest since Mar 2023 (7.6%) and marks the fifth consecutive month of acceleration in inflation. The BSP in a statement stated that it will take "necessary actions" to ensure that inflation returns to its 3% (± 1ppt) target within a reasonable time. The third out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Jun 18. For 2026, consensus expects one more rate hike to 4.75% by year-end. This brings 4M26 inflation to 3.9%, within the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) and still below their forecast of 6.3% for the year. Inflation in Apr accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Mar in food, utilities (electricity, gas, fuel), and transport prices. Food prices overall rose 6.0% YoY, accelerating from the 3.0% pace in Mar, driven by higher prices of rice, fish, and vegetables as post-harvest and transportation costs rose. Rice prices are jumped 13.7% YoY in Apr, accelerating from the 3.6% YoY increase in Mar. Rice prices are currently at P50.6/kg and P48/kg as of end-Apr for local and imported well-milled rice, rising from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25. Utilities and transport prices climbed to 16.9% YoY and 21.4% YoY faster than +7.2% and +9.9%, the previous month. Diesel and gasoline prices spiked 122.7% YoY and 59.6% YoY in Apr. Core inflation picked up to 3.9% in Apr from 3.2% in Mar, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 4.0% in Apr from 3.2% in Mar. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.3% (previously revised from 5.1% and 3.6%).

 

BSP considering off-cycle rate hike

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona in an interview stated that the Monetary Board is considering a second straight hike prior to its June 18 meeting (3rd out of 6 this year). Remolona elaborated that while the likelihood of an off-cycle hike is not likely, "we are considering it". He also added that the board may wait until after May inflation data is released on June 5 before making the next monetary policy decision. The BSP Governor also stated that the central bank can likely tolerate the peso hitting P63.50 to the dollar given that it moves in a "measured" way which would not stoke inflation. The USD-PHP rate has depreciated from P58.8 at the start of the year to P61.7 as of May 21. The BSP and consensus expects inflation to average 6.3% for 2026. Consensus expectations for the policy rate is for two more 25bp hikes to 5.00% by year-end.

Government debt inches up to P18.49trn in end-Mar; debt-to-GDP ratio highest since 2005

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.8% MoM (P328.4bn) to P18.49trn as of end-Mar. This was due to the net incurrence of new domestic as well as the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of February. The peso weakened to P60.74 as of end-Mar from P57.55 end-Feb. Domestic debt rose by 0.44% MoM (P55.4bn) to P12.53trn due to the impact of net borrowings. External debt increased by 4.8% MoM (P273.0bn) to P5.95trn primarily driven by unfavorable currency movements. The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2% as of 1Q26 from 63.2% end-2025. The 65.2% ratio is the highest since hitting 65.7% back in 2005. The Dept. of Finance's (DoF) forecasts in Aug 2025 projected the debt-to-GDP ratio to hit 61.8% in 2026, before gradually declining to 61.3% in 2027 and 60.3% in 2028. By 2029, the DoF expects the ratio to fall below 60% to 59.5%.

 

Trade deficit remains steady YoY at $4.5bn in March

The Mar trade deficit remained steady at US$4.5bn, unchanged YoY. This brings the 3M26 trade deficit to US$12.8bn, 3% wider YoY. Imports for the month grew by 12% YoY to US$12.7bn, a tad slower from the 13% increase in Feb. The growth in imports was primarily led by the 35% jump in fuel which offset the decline in consumer goods. Exports climbed 20% YoY to US$8.2bn, the fourteenth consecutive month of YoY growth. Electronics exports jumped 33% YoY to $4.8bn, accelerating from the 20% growth in Feb. These made up the bulk of exports at 59% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 6% to US$3.3bn.

 

BOP posts US$2.1bn deficit in April; GIR settles at US$104.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of April posted a deficit of US$2.1bn. Narrower than both the US$2.6bn deficit in the previous month of March and the US$2.6bn deficit in April 2025. This brings the 4M26 deficit to US$7.4bn, wider than the US$5.5bn deficit for the same period last year. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn. Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Apr stood at US$104.3bn, down 1% YoY and 2% lower MoM. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.9 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

 

Government posts P31bn budget surplus in April

The Philippine government recorded a budget surplus of P31bn in April, 53% lower YoY but a reversal from the P350bn deficit in Mar. This brings the 4M26 deficit to P324bn, 14% narrower vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in April rose 3% YoY, decelerating from +6% YoY in March, primarily as collections from the BIR were flat (+0.4% YoY). Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections slowed to +0.4% YoY from +7% the previous month. The disappointing BIR figure was partly attributed to the extension of the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return from April to 15 to May 15 following the declaration of the State of National Energy Emergency. Customs collections picked up to 16%, accelerating from the 6% growth the previous month on continued digitalization efforts. Non-tax revenues rose 7% slowing from +46% in March. Gov't spending continued to recover as expenses were up 11% YoY in April accelerating from the 5% growth in March. Primary spending for the month increased by 8% YoY. This marks the third straight month of YoY growth after six consecutive months of YoY declines in gov't spending. Interest payments rose 37% YoY.

 

Marcos sets P50/kg price cap on imported rice

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. set the price ceiling for imported rice at P50/kg, he highlighted the need to protect consumers by containing prices given the national energy emergency and by deterring market manipulation (e.g., profiteering, unjustified price increases). Executive Order (EO) 118 puts a cap on the price of 5% broken imported rice for an initial period of 30 days and takes effect immediately. The National Price Coordinating Council is tasked to review the cap every 15 days for possible adjustment or discontinuation. Rice prices are currently at P50.3/kg and P48/kg as of end-May for local and imported well-milled rice, rising from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25.

 

More countries join Luzon Economic Corridor

In a joint statement, the governments of the Philippines, United States, and Japan said that seven more countries would support the development of the Luzon Economic Corridor.

The United Kingdom will provide technical assistance and P411bn (US$6.8bn) in export finance for infrastructure and energy projects under the LEC. Australia committed P1.9bn (US$32.6mn) for inclusive economic growth initiatives, while South Korea pledged support for transport and digital connectivity, including a P1.5bn (US$25.6mn) grant for the National Cyber Security Center and NAIA modernization PPP project. Denmark will support shipbuilding and green maritime innovation, while France will finance 100 bridges and aeronautics-related industrial projects. Italy will support infrastructure and investments in transport, semiconductors, and manufacturing sectors. Sweden will provide a P74mn (US$1.2mn) grant for a feasibility study on the Subic-Clark-Manila-Batangas freight railway. The Luzon Economic Corridor is part of Pax Silica initiative of the United States. There is a plan to establish a 1,600-hectare AI Industrial hub in New Clark City.

 

Government infrastructure spending declines by 20% YoY in March; YTD infra spend down 26% YoY

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 26% YoY to P236bn in 3M26. Derived infrastructure spending in March stood at P108bn, rebounding 68% MoM but still down 20% YoY. The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that the YoY decline was due to the "ongoing completion of carry-over projects" as well as the stricter validation process for billing. The DBM expects infra spending to recover in 2Q26 as spending ramps up and construction picks up during summer. The government is targeting infra spend of ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) this year, broadly flat from last year’s P1.32tn (~4.7% of GDP).

Remittances rise by 2.3% in March

Cash remittances amounted US$2.9bn in Mar, 2.3% higher YoY—decelerating from the 2.6% growth in Feb. This marks the lowest YoY growth since June 2023 (+2.1%). Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+3.1%), Saudi Arabia (+2.5%), and the United Arab Emirates (+2.8%).

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth picks up to 10.7% in March

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth accelerated to 10.7% in Mar from 9.6% in Feb. Loans to productive activities picked up to 9.7% vs 8.6% the previous month. Consumer loans grew at a still robust pace of 20.5%, a tad slower than Feb's 20.8% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated to 12.0% in Mar from 10.3% in Feb, the fastest since Sep 2020 (12.2%).

 

Banks' asset quality stable in March as loan growth accelerates

Philippine banks' asset quality remained broadly stable in March despite macroeconomic pressures, while loan growth accelerated to 11.6% YoY. The non-performing loan (NPL) ratio excluding interbank loans and reverse repos was unchanged month-on-month at 3.51% and improved by 5bps YoY, although nominal NPLs rose 2.7% MoM and 10.2% YoY to P568bn. Past due (PD) ratios increased only marginally to 4.54%, with the value of PD loans rising 2.9% MoM and 13.9% YoY to P736bn, while restructured loan ratios improved to 2.09%. Sector data showed the most pronounced deterioration in construction, where NPL and PD ratios reached 13.15% and 16.08%, respectively, while manufacturing and trade also showed early signs of stress. In contrast, real estate asset quality remained stable and credit card delinquencies increased only gradually.

 

Department of Energy eyeing revisions to auction framework of Semirara coal contract

At the sidelines of an event, Energy Secretary Sharon Garin said that the agency is refining the auction framework for the Semirara mine coal block before re-starting the bidding by mid-year. Secretary Garin is eyeing to include a provision that would require the winning bidder to supply an undetermined minimum percentage of coal output for domestic sales. It is also ironing out some concerns raised by other bidders, including contract duration, the status of Semirara Mining & Power Corp.’s (SCC PM) existing equipment, as well as the expected production volumes. The bidding was postponed indefinitely from the initial bid submission date of April 28.

 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 19% YoY in April

CAMPI reported that vehicle sales slumped 18.9% YoY to 27,225 units in April. Brings 4M26 car sales to 133k units, down 12% YoY. Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI. Cited oil price shock discouraging vehicle purchases. Sales of electric vehicles (including hybrids) jumped 288% YoY to 5,855 units.

 

Philippine gambling revenue falls 16% YoY

PAGCOR reported that Philippine GGR fell 16% YoY (+1% QoQ) to P87.6bn. The decline was led by e-games – GGR fell 22% YoY (down 1% QoQ) to P39.9bn. We believe the reduction in e-games GGR is largely due to the prohibition of e-wallets from redirecting to online gambling platforms, which took place in mid-3Q25. We estimate that the e-games market share of PLUS rose to 42.8% in 1Q26, from 42.6% in 4Q25. GGR of licensed (land-based) casinos fell 10% YoY (up 2% QoQ) to P44.5bn.

 

House impeaches Vice President Sara Duterte

The House of Representatives has voted to impeach Vice President Sara Duterte on vote with 257 lawmakers in favor, 25 against, and nine abstaining. The 257 votes were more than enough for the minimum one-third or 106 House members vote requirement for the articles of impeachment to be transmitted to the Senate for trial. This marks the second impeachment of VP Duterte after the Supreme Court voided the first case initiated last year over procedural flaws, with the current complaint garnering support from more lawmakers than the previous impeachment backed by 215 legislators. The articles of impeachment allege culpable violation of the Constitution and betrayal of public trust by VP Duterte from misused confidential funds, bribery of officials, amassed unexplained wealth, and an assassination plot against the President.

 

Senate convenes as impeachment court for the trial of VP Duterte

The Senate has convened as an impeachment court for the trial of Vice President Sara Duterte following the House of Representatives' transmittal of the articles of impeachment. Note that this does not indicate the start of the trial as the initial period will be for the defense and the prosecution to submit their pleadings, with a pre-trial to be held for submission of evidence. The VP's defense team stated that they will comply with the Senate's request for a response to the articles of impeachment within the prescribed ten-calendar day window. Recall that the Senate had a change in leadership last May 11, where Duterte-allied senators were put under the majority bloc with Senator Alan Peter Cayetano as the new Senate President.

President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating hits record low in March SWS survey

President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating deteriorated from "neutral" at -3 in Nov 2025 to "poor" -15 in Mar 2026. Recall that Marcos’ net satisfaction rating first became negative back in Mar 2025 (-12) following Former President Rodrigo Duterte's arrest by the ICC and turned negative again in Sep 2025 (-5) after his flood control scandal expose during his State of the Nation Address last Jul 2025. Marcos' gross satisfaction rating declined from 33% in Nov to 40% in Mar. Gross dissatisfaction rose from 43% to 49% in Mar. SWS cited the following movements in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas. -14 points in Metro Manila (from “poor” -17 to "bad" -31). -11 points in Balance Luzon (from “moderate” +13 to "neutral" +2). -17 points in the Visayas (from “neutral” +2 to "poor" -15). -11 points in Mindanao (from “poor” -29 to "bad" -40). Marcos' average net satisfaction was zero in 2025, down from +25 in 2024 and +51 in 2023. Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,500 adults from Mar 24-30, 2026.

 

BSP amends single-entity exposure limits for UITFs

The BSP issued Circular 1234 amending single-entity exposure limits for unit investment trust funds (UITFs). Under the new rules, the combined exposure of a UITF to any single entity remains capped at 15% of the fund’s market value, but UITFs invested partially or substantially in exchange-traded equity securities may increase exposure to a single issuer up to 20%. The circular specified that any exposure above 15%, up to the 20% limit, must be solely in the form of listed equity securities of the same issuer. Meanwhile, equity index-tracking UITFs may exceed the 20% cap and mirror the actual benchmark weighting of index component issuers. The amendments imply that bond-only UITFs remain subject to the 15% issuer cap, while balanced and equity-focused UITFs may increase exposure to listed equities up to 20% per issuer.

 

SEC imposes term limits on broker directors of exchanges

The SEC will impose a term limit for broker directors of exchanges. This should include the Philippine Stock Exchange. The maximum cumulative term limit is ten years. After serving a cumulative five years, a broker director would need to observe a one-year cooling off period. The SEC has provided a two-year transition period for incumbent broker directors, who may complete their current term and remain eligible for election in the next two annual elections. After this transition period, four of the current five broker directors would no longer be eligible. Ma. Vivian Yuchengco has been broker director for 28 years. Eddie Gobing has been broker director for 25 years. Wilson Sy has been broker director for 12 years. Diosdado Arroyo has served for 6 consecutive years. Anthony Te would be the only current broker director who would be eligible for another term. At least 51 percent of the 15-member board must be non-brokers-- five independent directors and four directors representing the interests of issuers, investors and other market participants, with each sector having at least one representative.
