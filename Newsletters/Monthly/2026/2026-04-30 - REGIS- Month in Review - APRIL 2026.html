REGIS REPORT

30 April 2026
 

Philippine equities extended their decline in April, reversing early-month gains as the prolonged Iran conflict and its impact on global oil prices continued to dominate sentiment. While a temporary ceasefire early in the month briefly lifted the PCOMP back above the 6,000 level, the lack of a lasting resolution and renewed escalation with the intensified US blockade of the Strait of Hormuz kept oil prices elevated and ultimately drove sustained risk-off moves across the market. For the month, the PCOMP was down -1.9% or -115.30 pts MoM to 5,833.64.

 

The Philippines’ vulnerability as net energy importer remained top of mind, with the oil shock feeding through to inflation, interest rates, and the currency. March inflation surprised to the upside at 4.1%, breaching the BSP’s target range which set the stage for a more hawkish policy response. In its April meeting, the BSP delivered a 25-bp rate hike -- its first since October 2023 -- and significantly raised its inflation forecasts for 2026 further to 6.3%.

 

The peso was under pressure throughout the month, steadily weakening alongside persistent foreign outflows and deteriorating macro conditions. After briefly strengthening to sub-60:$1 levels early in April, the currency reversed course and depreciated sharply, ultimately breaching the 61:$1 mark to hit fresh record lows. The currency weakness further exacerbated inflation concerns and contributed to tighter domestic financial conditions, with the PH10Y yield climbing beyond the end-March highs past the 6.9% level.

 

On the corporate front, 1Q26 earnings began to reflect early signs of strain from the energy-driven macro shock. Property developers were among the most impacted, with ALI (-6.4% MoM) and SMPH (-7.1% MoM) both reporting weaker-than-expected results as residential revenues declined double digits QoQ and YoY. Project delays and sales cancellations highlight growing cost pressures and softer demand conditions.

 

Banks leaned even more cautious, with BDO (+1.2% MoM) and BPI (-10.4% MoM) both increasing provisions to account for potential asset quality deterioration amid a slowing economic backdrop. Consumer names remained under pressure throughout the month as rising input costs and weakening purchasing power weighed on sentiment, with key index heavyweights like CNPF (-18.6% MoM) underperforming.

 

In contrast, defensives and yield plays consistently outperformed amid the volatile environment. Names in power, utilities, telcos, and staples -- such as MER (+5.6% MoM), MBT (+5.5% MoM), PGOLD (+4.6% MoM), RCR (+4.2% MoM), and CNVRG (+4.1% MoM) -- benefited from rotation into more resilient earnings profiles.

 

Against this backdrop, the PCOMP failed to sustain its early rebound and trended lower through the rest of the month, ultimately breaking back below the 6,000 level as external headwinds outweighed any improvement in global risk sentiment. Foreign selling persisted throughout April with total net outflows of $211m, fully reversing accumulated inflows prior to the war. Philippine equities have now seen YTD net outflows amount to $73m.

Key data
 
 

The market saw continued foreign selling this April, erasing all year-to-date inflows, as traders remained wary of the inflationary risks brought about by rising oil prices as well as a weak set of 1Q results. Monthly outflows remain elevated at $209m, concentrated in banks and property as sentiment toward both sectors remains poor following 1Q earnings misses.   • Consumer names also saw significant outflows due to inflation concerns following March’s CPI print, and the BSP’s upward revision of its average inflation forecast for 2026. Meanwhile, PGOLD and MONDE eked out gains, with the former being the top foreign-bought name this April.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Index breadth remained negative in April as risk sentiment deteriorated through the month, with close to a third of index constituents finishing lower MoM amid sustained foreign outflows, rising rates, and a sharply weaker peso.

 

Banks were among the key laggards as the macro backdrop worsened. BPI (-10.4% MoM) was one of the biggest underperformers in the index for the month following 1Q26 results that showed pre-emptive provisioning for potential asset quality deterioration, reflecting growing caution over the economic outlook. BDO's (+1.2% MoM) share price fared better though, despite also raising provisions.

 

Property names also came under renewed pressure, reversing early-month gains as weakening earnings expectations took hold especially given the BSP's fresh hawkish tilt. ALI (-6.4% MoM) was again among the biggest decliners after reporting a huge 1Q26 earnings miss, alongside news that it is pausing a luxury residential project and canceling another in the middle-income segment due to rising cost pressures. SMPH (-7.1% MoM) similarly disappointed on softer residential revenues, dragged further by cancellations.

 

Conglomerates remained big underperformers for the month, with GTCAP (-9.0% MoM) worst off due to the peso weakness and its expected impact on the profits of its auto business. For the proxies, AC (-7.1% MoM) continued to correct amid weakness in the underlying listed assets while SM (-2.3% MoM) also finished lower amid underwhelming results.

 

Consumer names remained weak throughout April as inflation concerns intensified following the March CPI report and persistently rising global oil prices. CNPF (-18.6% MoM), JFC (-10.5% MoM), and URC (-6.6% MoM) were among the consistent underperformers, weighed down by expectations of margin compression from higher input costs and declining purchasing power. Staples proved more resilient, with PGOLD (+4.6% MoM) vastly outperforming while MONDE (+1.9% MoM) also managed to buck sector weakness.

 

In contrast, defensives and yield plays emerged as clear outperformers in the volatile environment. Power and utilities names such as MYNLD (+10.7% MoM) and MER (+5.6% MoM) led gains for much of the month, alongside telco CNVRG (+4.1% MoM), REIT RCR (+4.2% MoM) and port operator ICT (+3.2% MoM), as investors rotated into more stable earnings and dividend profiles. However, SCC (-9.2% MoM) reversed gains after the postponement of the Semirara coal contract bidding raised concerns over its chances of a renewal, with outside parties effectively given more time to come up with more competitive bids.

 

Gaming names were also in the red, although PLUS (-15.0% MoM) was among the bigger laggards on the index as the outlook on the sector remained broadly negative.

 

Mining bucked the market weakness with NIKL (+19.5% MoM), PX (+10.5% MoM), and APX (+9.8% MoM) leading gainers amid elevated global metal prices.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP delivers 25bp rate hike, raises inflation forecasts above 4% ceiling

The Monetary Board raised its policy rate by 25bps to 4.5%, in line with Street expectations. This marked the first rate hike since October 2023. BSP Governor Remolona said the decision was not unanimous, as the board considered a 50bp hike but opted for a more reasonable smaller hike. Move was done to anchor inflation expectations amid the Middle East conflict, with higher oil and fertilizer prices beginning to feed through domestic fuel and food prices. Core inflation has continued to rise, pointing to a broadening of underlying price pressures. BSP raised its average headline inflation rate forecast to 6.3% for 2026 (vs. 5.1% previously) and 4.3% for 2027 (vs. 3.8% in 2027), breaching its 4% ceiling.

 

Inflation accelerates to 4.1% in March

Headline inflation picked up to 4.1% in Mar from Feb's 2.4%. This was above the BSP's forecast range of 3.1-3.9% for Mar. The 4.1% print is the highest since Jul 2024 (4.4%) and marks the fourth consecutive month of acceleration in inflation. This brings 3M26 inflation to 2.8%, within the BSP's long-term target range of 2-4% and below their forecast of 5.1% for the year. Inflation in Mar accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Feb in food, utilities (electricity, gas, fuel), and transport prices. Food prices overall rose 3.0% YoY, accelerating from the 1.8% pace in Feb, driven by higher prices of rice and vegetables. Rice prices are up 3.6% YoY in Mar, the first YoY price increase after fourteen consecutive months of YoY price declines. Rice prices have notably been on the rise to P50/kg and P48/kg as of Apr-26 for local and imported well-milled rice from the low of around P42/kg each back in Oct-25. Core inflation inched up slightly to 3.2% in Mar from 2.9% in Feb, driven by core non-food inflation which likewise rose by 3.2% in Mar from 2.9% in Feb.

BSP revises CAD forecasts for 2026E from 3.0% to 4.0% of GDP

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised its 2026E CAD forecast to $20.3bn (4.0% of GDP) from $15.3bn (3.0% of GDP), with the increase driven mainly by a wider goods deficit due to a higher import bill of $137.9bn versus $130.2bn previously. It also introduced a 2027E CAD forecast of 4.0% of GDP. Note that the 2025 CAD came in at $16.3bn (3.3% of GDP) versus a prior estimate of 3.2%, with travel receipts of $8.7bn (-6.4% YoY).

 

 

Government budget deficit mildly up in March on energy crisis support; 1Q26 deficit down 20% to P356bn

The government’s budget deficit increased by 2% YoY to P350bn in March. Revenues rose 9% YoY to P305bn, supported by the +46% YoY increase in non-tax revenues due to the early remittance of GOCC dividends. BIR collections rose 6.5% YoY in March, up from the 3% pace in 2M26, but still below the full-year growth target of +10%. Customs collections grew 5.5% YoY in March, accelerating from the 2% pace in 2M26, but also below target (+7.5%). Expenditures rose 5.2% YoY in March, as primary spending rose 4.5% YoY, reversing the mild 1% contraction in 2M26. Primary spending in March was partly supported by the P20bn Emergency Energy program (3% of expenditures), aimed at augmenting the country’s fuel supply. Brings 1Q26 budget deficit to P355.5bn, down 20% YoY and broadly in line with target as weaker revenues were offset by subdued spending.

 

BOP posts US$2.6bn deficit in March; GIR settles at US$106.6bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of March posted a deficit of US$2.6bn. Wider than both the US$2.3bn deficit in Feb and the US$2.0bn deficit in March 2025. This brings the 3M26 deficit to US$5.3bn. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP). Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Mar stood at US$106.6bn, flat YoY and down from the US$113.3bn in the previous month of Feb. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.0 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

 

Government debt rises to P18.16trn in end-Feb

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.1% MoM to P18.16trn as of end-Feb. The increase in domestic debt was offset by the decline in external issuances as the USD-PHP rate strengthened to P58.3 end-Feb from P59.2 end-Jan. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.7% and 31.3% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 1.2% MoM to P12.48trn. External debt declined by 2.2% MoM to P5.68trn. The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) stated that the debt level is within the P19.06trn FY26 borrowing program.

 

Gov’t infrastructure spending drops 23% YoY in December; FY25 spending drops 14% YoY

Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 14% YoY to P1.32tn in FY25. Derived infrastructure spending in December stood at P126bn, down 23% YoY (+83% MoM). In 4Q25 alone, infrastructure spending fell 30% YoY and 11% QoQ to P283bn. Apart from the delays in payment settlements for public work projects amid the flood control corruption scandal, adverse weather conditions in 4Q were also cited as a reason. Note that the government recently reduced its infra spend target for 2026 to ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) from P1.56tn previously (~5.1% of GDP).

 

Marcos suspends excise tax on kerosene and LPG; gas and diesel unchanged

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. approved the suspension of excise taxes on kerosene and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) but did not remove the levies on gasoline and diesel as it was still up for discussion. The reduction for kerosene and LPG would be equivalent to P5.60/liter and P3.36/kg (P37 per tank), respectively. Kerosene and LPG prices are currently around P154-P177.2/liter and P1,000-P1,600/tank. Marcos stated that the Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport (UPLIFT) Committee will meet to discuss and decide on the possible reduction or suspension of gasoline and diesel excise taxes. Excise taxes are at P10/liter for gas and P6/liter for diesel. The President stated that he was not supportive of calls to cut or suspend the VAT on oil as revenues are needed to fund aid programs for the public. However, Marcos mentioned that he remains open for a future review if necessary.

 

Dept. of Agriculture expands P20/kg rice program

The Dept. of Agriculture (DA) expanded its P20/kg rice program for this year as it targets to open 900 additional selling sites nationwide for vulnerable communities in need of affordable rice. The expansion would add to the 932 sites already currently selling P20 rice across the country. Food Terminal Inc. (FTI) has signed an agreement with the National Food Authority (NFA) to secure funding and supply commitments for 2026. P3bn will be allocated for the deal to procure 1.8mn 50kg bags of well-milled rice. Funding will be sourced from the DA's P10bn Rice-for-All program budget. FTI President and CEO Joseph Lo emphasized on the "significant increase" in buyers of P20/kg rice given the unstable macroeconomic environment.

Government to contract PUVs, provide P10/liter fuel subsidy

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in a video statement has announced a P10/liter fuel subsidy for drivers and operators of public utility vehicles (PUVs). The P10/liter fuel subsidy would be implemented for three months starting next week with a 150-liter cap per week, this implies that PUVs can avail of weekly savings of P1,500 and a total savings of P18,000 for the program period. The subsidy is separate from the Dept. of Transportation's (DOTr) one-time fuel subsidy of P1,500-P10,000 as well as the Dept. of Social Welfare and Development's (DSWD) P5,000 cash relief for transport workers. The President has also announced that the DOTr will implement a nationwide service contracting program for PUVs starting April 15, which will cover around 50,000 PUVs and 1,000 operators, and benefit up to 15mn passengers. Contracted PUVs will be paid P40 to P100/km on top of their regular income, and will cover off-peak hours. The gov't will also provide 20% fare discount for commuters. The Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM) stated that P238bn, sourced from the 2026 budget, in available funding will be used to support fuel subsidies, cash relief assistance, and other targeted social protection programs.

 

Manufacturers agree with DTI to extend price freeze till May 10

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that the ongoing price freeze of basic and prime commodities will be extended to May 10 from April 30 previously following commitment from manufacturers. This is the second extension after the price halt was initially extended from April 16 to end-April. Goods covered by the price freeze include canned sardines and meat, coffee, processed milk, instant noodles, bread, among others. Trade Secretary Ma. Cristina Roque stated that the decline in fuel prices over the past week enabled the DTI to secure another extension.

 

Remittances rise by 2.6% in February

Cash remittances amounted US$2.8bn in Feb, 2.6% higher YoY—decelerating from the 3.5% growth in Jan. Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+3.8%), Saudi Arabia (+5.1%), and the UAE (+5.2%).

 

Fitch downgrades PH outlook to "negative" from "stable" but affirms credit rating

Credit ratings agency Fitch downgraded its outlook for the Philippines to "negative" from "stable" but affirmed the country's "BBB" investment-grade rating. Fitch cited the country's high exposure to the global energy shock as well as disrupted public investment due to the corruption scandal. Political friction was also noted as a risk to the country's macro outlook. The credit rating agency highlighted that these risks currently outweigh favorable medium-term growth prospects. Note that a "negative" outlook implies the possibility of a future credit rating downgrade if underlying risks continue or worsen in one to two years.

 

S&P downgrades PH outlook to "stable" from "positive" but affirms credit rating

S&P Global Ratings downgraded its outlook for the Philippines to "stable" from "positive" but affirmed the country's "BBB+" investment-grade rating. The S&P cited that the Middle East conflict has further complicated the Philippines' economic outlook following the fallout from the flood control corruption scandal. S&P also highlighted the potential need to absorb a higher deficit as the gov't ramps up support measures to mitigate the impact of elevated fuel prices. The agency added that a future credit rating upgrade remains anchored on a lower gov't deficit, stable debt and accelerated fiscal consolidation. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona noted that the central bank will continue to monitor upcoming data to implement policies aimed to safeguard price and financial stability given heightened uncertainties.

 

Moody's retains "Baa2 stable" investment rating for the Philippines

Moody's retained its "Baa2 stable" investment-grade rating for the Philippines. The ratings agency noted key strengths such as a stable banking system, ample foreign-currency reserves, pipeline of PPP projects, and resilient OFW remittances. Credit challenges include low capita per income, "weak rule of law and control of corruption", high climate and natural disaster risk. A ratings upgrade could be considered if there is rapid improvement in fiscal and gov't debt metrics but a downgrade may occur if such metrics deteriorate.

U.S. and the Philippines plan to launch 1,600-hectare economic security zone

United States Undersecretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg announced plans to establish a 4,000-acre (~1,600-hectare) industrial hub in the Luzon Economic Corridor of the Philippines in order "to secure vital inputs to American and global supply chain". The U.S. cited the Philippines' significant reserves of nickel, copper, chromite, and cobalt. The US will occupy the rent-free and will administer it as a special economic zone. The hub will have diplomatic-style immunity similar to a U.S. embassy and operate under U.S. common law with a two-year lease renewable for up to 99 years. The agreement is part of the U.S.' Pax Silica initiative, with the Philippines joining as the 13th signatory, as the U.S. aims to reduce dependence on China-linked materials used for semiconductors, AI technology, and critical minerals.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth picks up to 9.5% in Feb

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.5% in Feb from 9.3% in Jan, the third month of single-digit growth after a previous 19-month streak of double-digit growth. Loans to productive activities picked up to 8.6% vs 8.2% in Jan. Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 20.8%, a tad slower than Jan's 21.3% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) significantly accelerated to 10.3% in Feb from 8.6% in Jan, this marks the highest M3 growth print since Nov 2020.

 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 10% YoY in March; Toyota sales down 6.8% YoY

Data from CAMPI showed that vehicle sales fell 10.4% YoY (+0.7% MoM) to 36,104 units in March. Brings 1Q26 sales to 105,642 units, down 9.8% YoY. Sales of electric vehicles (ex-BYD) surged to 6,148 units in March (~17% of total car sales), up >3x YoY and 2x MoM due to the rise in fuel prices. Brings 1Q26 EV sales up 36% YoY to 11,800 units (HEVs: +10% YoY to 8261 units / BEVs: +123% YoY to 2,289 units / PHEVs: +925% YoY to 1,250 units). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,622 units in March, down 6.8% YoY and 1.85% MoM. Brings TMP’s 1Q26 unit sales to 51,922 units, down 6.5% YoY TMP is targeting unit sales to grow “up to 5%” (~229k-240k units) this year.
