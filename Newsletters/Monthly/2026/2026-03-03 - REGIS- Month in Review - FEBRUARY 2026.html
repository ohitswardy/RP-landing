REGIS REPORT

03 March 2026
 

The PCOMP extended its momentum from the start of the year, rising by another 4.5% to 6611 in February and marking new 52-weeks high during the month. Notwithstanding the worse-than-expected GDP slowdown on the ground, Philippine shares were once again primarily boosted by continued foreign inflows which netted $144m, inline with the broader regional theme of funds flowing into EM. This brought net inflow to almost $370m in just 2 months so far into 2026.

The central bank trimmed the benchmark rate for the 6th time in a row by 25bps to 4.25%, inline with consensus forecast, after recent data showed that CPI remained muted at 2% in January (albeit slightly above the 1.8% consensus) and the  worse-than-expected slowdown in GDP growth in 4Q (to 3% vs 3.7% forecast). Note the policy rate now sits at its lowest since 2022. Despite this the PHP likewise sharply rallied alongside equities, gaining as much as 2.07%, its best MoM gain since April 2025, to 57.66. This marked its highest levels in 5 months.

Another key event was the MSCI rebalancing on month-end which saw the inclusion of MYNLD and APX into the small cap index. MYNLD, which only made its debut in the market in November, gained +21.5% in February and is now +46.7% from its IPO price. Gold miner APX meanwhile likewise advanced sharply, +15.1% MoM, and is now +40.7% YTD, boosted not just by its inclusion into MSCI but also by the sharp surge in gold prices this year. 

In company-specific events, the government announced the rebidding of SCC's coal operating contract after the Department of Justice ruled that the current contract cannot be extended beyond its July 2027 expiry (as opposed to the company's request to extend the current contract by 13 years). This drove a sharp selloff on SCC -12% MoM and its parent DMC -10.7%.

In terms of index movers, we saw gains broaden out to other sectors like conglomerates and consumers with AC +18.6% MoM, PGOLD, +17.3% being among the top gainers. But that said, index heavyweight ICT continued to be an important driver of the PCOMP as it rose by another +11.8% during the month, supported by continued foreign demand. 

Investors also digested the start of 4Q/FY2025 earnings season in late February. Healthy numbers by RRHI helped support sentiment on the broader consumer sector. But in contrast continued weakness in ALI’s residential business reinforced aversion for the property space. In utilities, MER showed better than expected numbers, while telcos showed positive trends, particularly for their mobile businesses.

Key data
 
 

Foreigners remained net buyers in the market (+$143mn) this February, helping propel the index to revisit key 6,500 levels for the first time since July 2025. Strong foreign demand, alongside MSCI’s quarterly rebalancing, boosted average daily turnover to $132 million (up from $125 million the prior month). The foreign and local split remains even at 50:50.

  • ICT continued to be the top foreign bought name in the Philippines, extending its net foreign inflow streak to its 10th straight month. Sustained foreign inflows fueled by positive outlook have propelled its share price to new record levels, which helped push the index to revisit the key 6500 level.
  • Foreigners were generally net buyers of Holdcos, with AC seeing the strongest foreign demand, rallying +18.6%. Meanwhile, SM continued underperform due to persistent foreign outflows. The stock has now been net foreign sold for the 10th straight month since its placement last July.
  • Banks finished the month two-way. BPI saw foreign outflows even though its FY25 results were in line with estimates; meanwhile, despite MBT’s FY25 NPAT figures being slightly behind forecast, the stock still saw decent foreign inflows given dividend support. 

Property stocks ALI and SMPH continued to come under pressure of foreing selling, as weak presales weighed on sector sentiment.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Breadth of the index was again positive in February with 20 advancers vs 9 decliners (1 unchanged).

Leading the way this time were consumer stocks with PGOLD +17.3% MoM, MONDE +15.6% and URC +12.7% as investors anticipate positive 4Q/FY25 results.

Index heavyweight ICT +11.8% was again an important driver of the market, continuing to mark fresh highs as it was supported by heavy net foreign buying.

Top gainer in the benchmark meanwhile was PLUS +22.7% amid increasing expectations that lower fees paid to PAGCOR could drive earnings growth, notwithstanding the threat of lower GGR amid the delinking of online gambling platforms from e-wallets.

Next to PLUS was AC +18.6% which was driven by substantial foreign buying. Note that despite its sharp rise during the month sharp rise, valuations remain fairly low at only ~6x FY2026E.

On the losing end, among the worst performers stocks in the benchmark during the month were SCC -12% and its parent DMC -10.7% after the government announced the rebidding of the latter’s coal operating contract which is set to expire in 2027 – as opposed to the company’s request to extend their existing contract by 13 years. Note however that shares have bounced from lows during the month as investors price in a favorable outcome for SCC in the rebidding.

Property trailed behind the market rally as well with ALI -1.7% and SMPH -0.5% as residential pre-sales remained muted in 4Q following the corruption scandal last year. Instead, investors flocked into REITs such as RCR +4.7% and MREIT +5% amid the BSP cut.

Banks were mixed with BPI -6.5%, BDO +1.7%, MBT +5.5% as investors turn wary of potentially slowing loan growth. Although gains on MBT indicate preference towards high-yield stocks, again amid a backdrop of easing interest rates.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP cuts rates further to 4.25%; lowers GDP growth forecasts for 2026 and 2027. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.25%, the lowest since Aug 2022. This is the sixth consecutive 25bp cut (150bp total) since the decision to resume easing back in Apr 2025. The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 225bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024. In a press briefing, BSP Governor Eli Remolona emphasized that rationale for the cut was because softer than expected growth alongside a slowdown in investments. Recall that 4Q25 GDP growth was 3.0%, bringing FY25 growth to 4.4%, much lower than the BSP's expectations of 3.8% growth in 4Q and 4.6% in FY25.

Inflation inches up to 2.0% in January. Headline inflation picked up to 2.0% in Jan from Dec's 1.8%. This was a tad higher than consensus expectations of 1.8% but still within the BSP's forecast range of 1.4-2.2% for Jan. This is the first time inflation has hit 2.0% since Feb 2025 (2.1%) as the CPI has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the previous ten months. Inflation in Jan accelerated slightly given faster YoY increases vs Dec in electricity, gas, fuel as well as restaurants and accommodation services prices. Food prices overall rose 1.1% YoY, decelerating from the 1.4% pace in Dec—as vegetable prices normalize following the impact of strong rains during 2H25. Notably, rice prices were up MoM by 3.4%—marking the third consecutive MoM price increase. Rice prices are down 8.5% YoY in Jan, the thirteenth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. Core inflation accelerated to 2.8% in Jan from 2.4% in Dec, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 2.7% in Dec from 2.1% in Jan. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.6% and 3.0%. The first out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Feb 19. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

BOP posts US$373mn deficit in Jan; GIR settles at US$112.6bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Jan posted a deficit of US$373mn. Narrower than both the US$827mn deficit in Dec and the US$4.08bn deficit in Jan 2025. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP). Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Jan stood at US$112.6bn, 4% higher YoY and up from the US$110.8bn in end-Dec. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. This is higher than the BSP's forecast of $109bn for 2025. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

Remittances rise by 3.5% in December. Cash remittances amounted US$3.5bn in Dec, 4.2% higher YoY—accelerating from the 3.6% growth in Nov. Bringing FY25 remittances up 3.3% YoY to US$35.6bn. Remittance growth was primarily driven by high-single-digit YoY growth from countries in the Middle East.

 

Finance Secretary expects new 10% Trump tariff to have minimal impact on Philippine goods. Dept. of Finance Secretary Frederick Go stated that US President Donald Trump's recent declaration to impose a 150-day 10% global duty (effective Feb 24) will have minimal impact on Philippine goods. Go highlighted that majority of Philippine exports were already exempted (i.e., semiconductor and key agricultural exports such as coconut and banana). The new global tariff declaration by Trump follows the US Supreme Court's decision to void the earlier imposed Trump tariffs. Recall that the prior US-imposed tariff on Philippine goods was 19%. In 2025, Philippine exports to the US amounted to US$13.3bn (+11% YoY) and comprised 16% of total exports.

PH dollar reserves at $112.5bn end-Jan. The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Dec increased 1.5% MoM to US$112.5bn from US$110.9bn the previous month. The increase primarily reflected the surge in gold prices alongside a significant increase in foreign exchange holdings. The level of dollar reserves was enough to cover about 4.1x the country's short-term external debt and equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. For 2026, the BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$110bn.

Government debt rises to P17.71trn in end-Dec. The government's total debt stock went up by 0.3% MoM and 10% YoY to P17.71trn as of end-Dec. The increase was driven by the net issuance of debt and the depreciation of the peso. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.4% and 31.6% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 0.3% MoM to P12.12trn. External debt rose by 1.1% MoM to P5.59trn. The P17.71trn year-end figure exceeded the P17.36-trn end-2025 projection by 2%. The gov't debt-to-GDP ratio as of end-2025 rose slightly to 63.2% from 63.1% as of 3Q25 and is also higher vs 60.7% back in end-2024. The 63.2% debt-to-GDP figure is above the 61.3% projected 2025 target by the gov't and is the highest since the 65.7% recorded back in 2005.

Government raises an additional P128bm from T-bond offer, bringing total to P235bn. The Bureau of Treasury raised another P128bn from the sale of 10-year T-bond through the TAP facility. This follows the initial P107bn raised on Wednesday from a rate-setting auction. Offer period was cut short due to overwhelming demand, driven by expectations of a BSP rate cut on Thursday afternoon. T-bonds carried a coupon rate of 5.925% and yielded 5.893%. The Bureau of Treasury plans to borrow a total of P2.68tn, of which P2.05tn will come from domestic creditors.

 

Government infrastructure spending plunges by 35% YoY in November; YTD infra spend down 13% YoY. Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 13% YoY to P1.2tn in 11M25. Derived infrastructure spending in November stood at P69bn, down 22% MoM, 35% YoY. Cited delays in settlement of progress billings for public work’s projects amid the flood control corruption scandal. Note that the government recently reduced its infra spend target for 2026 to ~P1.3tn (~4.3% of GDP) from P1.56tn previously (~5.1% of GDP).

Department of Energy set to launch auction for coal areas, including Semirara sites, on Feb 27. The Department of Energy is set to launch the auction for 18 coal areas with verified reserves on Feb 27; Sites include the following. 10 blocks in Semirara island in Caluya, Antique. Five blocks across the municipalities Benito Soliven, Naguilian, Cauayan in Isabela. Three blocks across the municipalities of Amulung and Iguig in Cagayan province. From the launch date of Feb 27, a pre-submission conference will be held 20 days after the launch (~March 19). interested bidders will be given sixty (60) calendar days to submit application documents (or on April 29 at 11am). Opening of applications will be held on the same day as the deadline (April 29). Note that the blocks in Semirara island are covered by the existing Coal Operating Contract (COC) of Semirara Mining & Power Corp. (SCC PM). We await the bidding’s terms of reference on Feb 27.

Commercial banks' loan growth slows to 9.2% in Dec. Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.2% in Dec from 10.3% in Nov, breaking a 19-month streak of double-digit growth and marking the slowest pace since Feb 2024 (8.6%). Loans to productive activities decelerated to 8.0% vs 9.0% in Nov. Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 21.4%, though slower than Nov's 22.9% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) moderated to 7.0% in Dec from 7.6% in Nov.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 10% YoY in January. CAMPI data showed that vehicle sales fell 10% YoY to 33,696 units in January. Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI. Including non-CAMPI members, CAMPI estimates car sales stood at 35,053 units, which would be ~9% YoY lower. Sales of electric vehicles (ex-BYD) totaled 2,610 units, with hybrids accounting for 79% of total EV sales, PHEVs at 11% and BEVs at 10%.

Colliers: Residential net take-up worsens in 4Q25. Residential vacancy in Metro Manila was relatively flat QoQ at 25%. Net take-up of Metro Manila condominium units fell 80% QoQ (but quintupled YoY) to 1,200 units, due to higher cancellations QoQ. Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy declined slightly to 19.4% in 4Q25, from 19.8% in 3Q25. Makati CBD vacancy inched up c. 1% QoQ to 9%. BGC vacancy fell c. 2ppt QoQ to 10%. Colliers said that the number of unsold condominium units in Metro Manila was equivalent to about 8 years’ worth of sales, down from 12 years in 2Q25. We estimate that in 3Q25, unsold condominium units represented c. 9 years of sales. Net office take-up fell 21% QoQ to 91,000sqm. This was a reversal from net incremental closures of 185,000sqm in 4Q24. Office rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ. They inched up modestly in Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio. Colliers estimates that land values in Makati CBD, Fort Bonifacio, and Ortigas Center rose 3%-5% QoQ. Office and residential vacancies in these districts were lower than the Metro Manila averages.

 Marcos establishes new economic affairs body. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., through the signing of Executive Order (EO) No. 108, transferred the functions of the Office of the Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (OSAPIEA) to the new established Office of Economic Affairs (OEA) in order to streamline the administration's investment and economic initiatives. OEA, the new investment and economic policy body, will be headed by Executive Secretary Ralph Recto. Recall that the OSAPIEA was previously under Finance Secretary Frederick Go prior to his appointment to the Dept. of Finance (DOF).

PAGCOR tightens regulations on online gambling. In a Senate hearing, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the regulator has tightened know-your-customer (KYC) protocols and advertising regulations on online gambling. PAGCOR Electronic Gaming Licensing Department officer in charge Jessa Mariz Fernandez said the updated KYC process requires a valid government ID and a real-time selfie before the initial deposit can be made. TV and radio ads during primetime are no longer allowed. PAGCOR might expand the ban to the entire day. Other implemented measures include online self-exclusion tools on gaming platforms, accreditation of treatment and rehabilitation centers for problem gamblers, stricter KYC enforcement, and the removal of gambling-related billboards and other outdoor advertisements.

PAGCOR license fees from land-based casinos fall 5% in 2025. PAGCOR reported that the fees it collected from licensed (land-based) casinos fell 5% to P31.4bn in 2025. PAGCOR did not disclose the figures for 2Q25 and 3Q25. 9M25 GGR of licensed (land-based) casinos fell 7% to P139bn. From these figures, we estimate that GGR of licensed (land-based) casinos might have been flattish YoY (c. P52bn) in 4Q25. If this is the case, then 4Q25 GGR likely rose 10%-15% QoQ. Okada Manila reported that 4Q25 GGR fell 15% QoQ and 34% YoY to P5.9bn.

 

 

 

President Marcos' net satisfaction rating remains neutral in November SWS survey. President Marcos’ net satisfaction rating remained "neutral" at -3 in Nov 2025 from "neutral" -5 in Sep 2025. Note that Marcos’ net satisfaction rating declined to -5 in Sep from "moderate" +10 in Jun, with the drop occurring after his flood control scandal expose during his SONA last July. Marcos' gross satisfaction rating inched up 1% to 40% from Sep to Nov. Gross dissatisfaction dropped a tad to 43% from Sep’s 44%. Gross undecided was stable at 17%. SWS cited the following movements in Marcos’ net satisfaction rating in all four major areas: -15 points in Metro Manila (from “neutral” -2 to “poor” -17), +5 points in Balance Luzon (from “neutral” +8 to “moderate” +13), +10 points in the Visayas (from “neutral” -8 to “neutral” +2), and +1 point in Mindanao (from “poor” -28 to “poor” -29). Marcos' average net satisfaction was zero in 2025, down from +25 in 2024 and +51 in 2023. Survey was conducted via face-to-face interviews with 1,200 adults from Nov 24-30, 2025.

House panel dismisses Marcos impeachment complaints. The House Committee on Justice dismissed the two impeach complaints filed against President Marcos, which accused him of corruption, violating the constitution, and betraying public trust, as they were deemed "insufficient in substance". The House is expected to convene for a plenary vote to either uphold or override the committee’s findings. If upheld, the junking of the complaints will trigger the one-year ban rule given the Constitutional rule that an official can only undergo one impeachment proceeding per year.

VP Sara Duterte announces 2028 Presidential run, amid fresh impeachment charges. Vice President Sara Duterte announced in a press briefing that she will run for president in the 2028 election. Duterte also stated an apology for helping elect President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in 2022 given that she was his running mate. There are four impeachment petitions against VP Duterte, which have yet to be referred to the Lower House’s justice committee by Speaker Faustino “Bojie” Dy. The House has until March 2 to include these charges in the Order of Business for plenary consideration.
