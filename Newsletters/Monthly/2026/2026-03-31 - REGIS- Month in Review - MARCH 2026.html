REGIS REPORT

31 March 2026
 

Philippine equities experienced a sharp risk-off month of March as the escalation of the US-Israel conflict with Iran dominated sentiment. The Philippines’ status as a net energy importer saw the surge in oil prices to above $100/bbl trigger broad-based selling across equities, the peso, and bonds.

 

The index ultimately broke down below the 6,000-level, reflecting deteriorating investor confidence as external shocks outweighed domestic fundamentals and earnings strength. For the month, the PCOMP slumped by 10.0% or 662.30 pts MoM to 5,948.94, just slightly off the lows at the 5,800-level as quarter-end window dressing helped pare some of the monthly losses. The March losses has totally reversed gains from strong inflows in the previous months, taking the PCOMP's YTD performance to -1.7%.

 

Inflation quickly re-emerged as the central concern, with rising oil prices raising the risk of second-round effects. The BSP shifted to a more cautious stance, signaling potential rate hikes if inflationary pressures intensify, while revising inflation forecasts higher for 2026-27. The government's responses thus far, include fuel subsidies, potential excise tax adjustments, and interventions in transport, mall hours, and even the electricity spot market.

 

Currency weakness was another major theme, with the peso breaking past the 60:$1 level for the first time and ending the month at historic lows amid sustained foreign outflows and rising import costs. For the month, the peso fell 5.1% to 60.745. This depreciation amplified inflation concerns and contributed to tighter financial conditions, as government bond yields climbed to multi-year highs. The PH10Y yield breached 7% for the first time since 2Q24.

 

Outside of macro turbulence, corporate earnings remained relatively resilient, with aggregate 4Q25 results delivering mid-teens growth and even stronger 1Q26 indications especially for consumer names. However, market reactions were muted to negative as investors focused on macro risks that are seen impeding improving fundamentals.

 

We saw broad-based sectoral declines led by cyclicals, with the Big Three banks BDO (-17.8% MoM), BPI (-13.9% MoM), and MBT (-17.5% MoM), residential developer ALI (-22.8% MoM), and consumer heavyweights URC (-18.4% MoM) and JFC (-17.0% MoM) all posting steep losses, reflecting sensitivity to rates and inflation. Conglos with larger exposure to banks, property, and consumer likewise underperformed, with GTCAP (-22.4% MoM), AC (-16.1% MoM), and SM (-12.1% MoM) down the most.

 

In contrast, pockets of outperformance emerged in energy-linked names such as SMC (+5.6% MoM), ACEN (+5.0% MoM), DMC (+3.4% MoM) and SCC (+2.3% MoM).

 

Foreign flows were consistently negative throughout the month, marking a clear reversal from earlier inflows. Net foreign outflow amounted to $231m in March, which has slashed YTD net inflows to $138m.

Key data
 
 

Foreign investors turned heavy net sellers in March, especially in the latter half of the month, as the sudden conflict in the Middle East drove widespread aversion on risk assets, particularly in the energy-dependent EM. Net outflows for the month stood at a hefty $231m, which put an end to a 2-month inflow streak and marked the largest monthly outflow since May 2025. This brought down the YTD inflow to less than $140m from a peak of as much as $380m near the beginning of the month. The sharp selloff in the market was also backed by heavier market activity with a daily average of $143m +8% MoM. Split in foreign-local market participation however remained virtually neutral at 49-51 (compared to 50-50 in February).

  • ICT extended its net foreign inflow streak for the 11th straight month despite the broad risk-off mood. It remained the top foreign-bought stock in the market for the month of March.
  • Foreigners turned heavy sellers of banks while outflows persisted for property names, reflecting poor overall sentiment on cyclicals. The Big Three banks and the two large property developers bore the brunt of selling.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Index breadth was heavily negative in March with 23 decliners against 7 advancers.

 

Banks were among the worst hit as rising oil prices, peso weakness, and surging bond yields drove foreign outflows and pressured cyclicals, with big three banks BDO (-17.8% MoM), BPI (-13.9% MoM), and MBT (-17.5% MoM) seeing steep losses.

 

ALI (-22.8% MoM) was the biggest decliner in the index for the month amid higher-for-longer rate expectations, weak reservation sales, and elevated inventory levels. Overall though, the property sector fared relatively better than the PCOMP with SMPH (-4.9% MoM), RLC (-5.9% MoM), and MEG (-7.1% MoM) all clocking narrower losses.

 

Conglos tracked the broader market sell-off, particularly those with larger exposure to consumption, property, and banks, with GTCAP (-22.4% MoM), AC (-16.1% MoM), and SM (-12.1% MoM) leading declines. Notably, energy-linked names like SMC (+5.6% MoM) and DMC (+3.4% MoM) outperformed -- these were two of the top 5 largest gainers in March.

 

Two more of these biggest index outperformers were still energy producers ACEN (+5.0% MoM) and SCC (+2.3% MoM), with the former also benefitting from market chatter around a potential tender offer and consequent delisting. Other non-index utilities like AP (+8.4% MoM) and MWC (+5.1% MoM) also finished higher for the month.

 

Meanwhile, consumer names broadly sold off as inflation fears intensified and demand outlook weakened, with URC (-18.4% MoM), JFC (-17.0% MoM), and CNPF (-12.4% MoM) among the laggards. Likely margin pressures from higher input costs in the coming quarters due to rising oil prices reinforced negative sentiment over the sector. Staples fared better with PGOLD (-2.4% MoM) registering a much smaller monthly decline. RRHI (+21.1% MoM) was the runaway winner after its share price surged on its announcement of a tender offer which could subject the company to voluntarily delist from the PSE.

 

Transport names also declined on the back of surging fuel costs and margin pressures, with CEB (-24.7% MoM) particularly hard hit. ICT (-4.2% MoM) proved more resilient, supported by strong earnings and structural demand, outperforming the index though it still ended the month lower.

 

Similarly, telcos though broadly lower managed to fare better than the PCOMP, at least for TEL (-7.5% MoM) and GLO (-6.6% MoM). CNVRG (-15.3% MoM) lagged as 4Q results disappointed, baring slower subscriber growth in the quarter as competitive pressures mounted.

 

Another name that fell on a 4Q miss was BLOOM (-25.9% MoM), which saw continued operating challenges and widening losses further dampen sentiment.

 

Mining names declined as metal prices -- particularly gold -- corrected, with PX (-27.9% MoM), NIKL (-22.9% MoM), OGP (-18.4% MoM), and APX (-15.0% MoM) all posting steep losses.

 

REITs weakened alongside rising yields, reducing their relative attractiveness, with RCR (-12.3% MoM), worst hit.

 
MACRO NEWS

President Marcos declares state of national energy emergency

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. through Executive Order (EO) 110 has declared a "state of national energy emergency" amidst significant concern on the price and supply of fuel/energy. The President has established the "Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food and Transport (UPLIFT)" committee in order to ensure a coordinated, whole-of-government response framework approach to the crisis. The committee is chaired by the President with the various gov't agencies as members in order to oversee and coordinate energy crisis mitigation measures and intiatives. Measures include fuel and energy optimization plans, action against hoarding, profiteering, and supply manipulation, as well as ensuring the stability and adequacy of domestic fuel and energy supply through the Philippine National Oil Company (PNOC). The EO also directs the departments/agencies to monitor key inputs and implement necessary response measures under their scope (i.e., cash relief, food supply, essential goods price monitoring, etc.). One of the ongoing discussions mentioned was the adjustment of the 12% VAT on fuel with Energy Undersecretary Felix Fuentebella citing that they are leaning towards an adjustment of the tax rate rather than an outright suspension. Senator Bam Aquino stated that the upper chamber is willing to work overtime to pass urgent tax reforms if economic managers give the green light. Dept. of Economy, Planning and Development (DepDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan, however, warned of a possible economic slowdown if both excise tax and VAT on fuel are removed.

 

Marcos signs oil excise tax suspension law

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed into law Republic Act (RA) 12316 which grants him the power to suspend or reduce the excise tax on petroleum products. After signing the law, Marcos emphasized that he would wait for the "best time" to implement it. Fuel excise taxes may now be adjusted if the average Dubai crude oil based on Mean of Platts Singapore benchmark reaches or exceeds US$80/bbl for a month. The adjusted excise tax will automatically revert to the original rate a week after the three-month limit or if the one-month average of Dubai crude is below US$80/bbl as certified by the Dept. of Energy. Implementation for a period cannot exceed three months and if extended, the aggregate period of the adjustment shall not exceed one calendar year. The President may only suspend or reduce excise tax collections on fuel products until Dec. 31, 2028. Estimated revenue losses could reach as much as P43bn if suspension lasts for three months and P106bn if extended to a total of six months.

 

Trade deficit widens to $3.7bn in Feb

The Feb trade deficit widened to US$3.7bn, expanding 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth of imports vs exports. This brings the 2M26 trade deficit to US$36.9bn, 6% wider YoY. Imports for the month grew by 13% YoY to US$11.0bn, a reversal from the 3% decline in Jan. The rebound to growth was primarily driven by the surge in capital goods (+55%) and the reversal to growth in consumer goods imports (+10%). Consumer goods in Feb broke a previous five-month streak of YoY declines. Exports rose 8% YoY to US$7.0bn, the thirteenth consecutive month of YoY growth. Electronics exports climbed 20% YoY to $4.2bn, continuing the momentum from the 19% growth in Jan. These made up the bulk of exports at 58% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 5% to US$3.1bn.

 

 

BSP keeps rates unchanged at off-cycle meeting; revises GDP and inflation forecast

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) held its policy rate at 4.25% during an off-cycle meeting. This marks the first time the BSP held rates steady since Feb 2025, after six consecutive 25bp cuts. The central bank noted that raising the policy rate at this time would delay the economic recovery. BSP Governor Eli Remolona highlighted that the monetary policy would have limited effectiveness against supply-driven inflationary risks. However, he also stated that the BSP may hike rates if it can address demand-driven inflation from second-round effects of oil shocks. Remolona added that the central bank is planning regulatory relief measures for the local banking sector, particularly for lenders from the informal sector and low-income businesses. He elaborated the plan is similar to what was done during the pandemic (Bayanihan 2, to Recover as One Act)—to have a standardized restructuring framework on default loans, postponing some payments depending on the sector. The second out of six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Apr 23. The central bank's GDP forecast for 2026 was trimmed to 4.4% from 4.6% but left its forecast for 2027 unchanged at 5.9%. The BSP raised its inflation forecast for 26/27E to 5.1% from 3.6% and 3.8% from 3.2%, respectively.

 

Inflation accelerates to 2.4% in February

Headline inflation picked up to 2.4% in Feb from Jan's 2.0%. This was in line with consensus expectations of 2.4% and within the lower-end of the BSP's forecast range of 2.3-3.1% for Feb. The 2.4% print is the highest since Jan 2025 (2.9%) and marks the third consecutive increase in inflation as favorable base effects begin to wane. This brings 2M25 inflation to 2.2%, within the BSP's long-term target range of 2-4% and below their forecast of 3.6% for the year. National Statistician Claire Dennis Mapa guided that inflation may further pick up in the coming months as higher fuel prices impact other basket items. Inflation in Feb accelerated given faster YoY increases vs Jan in food and utilities (electricity, gas, fuel). Food prices overall rose 1.8% YoY, accelerating from the 1.1% pace in Jan, driven by higher prices of fish and vegetables. Rice prices are down 3.3% YoY in Feb, the fourteenth consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. Core inflation inched up slightly to 2.9% in Feb from 2.8% in Jan, driven by core non-food inflation which rose by 2.9% in Feb from 2.7% in Jan. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 3.6%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to be 2.7% and 3.0%. The second out of six Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Apr 23. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.25%.

 

Current account deficit drops by 50% YoY in 4Q25; YTD CA deficit lower by 12% YoY to US$16.3bn

The current account deficit plunged 50% YoY, 39% QoQ to US$3.2bn (equivalent to 3.3% of GDP). Driven by a narrower trade-in-goods deficit, higher net receipts from trade in services and the continued modest increase in remittances. Trade deficit declined by 14% YoY to US$17.5bn due to strength in electronic exports (+87%), machinery exports (+52%), and gold exports (+54%). Total estimated BPO export revenues, including computer and other business services, reached US$9.1bn in 3Q25, a 7.8% increase from US$8.4bn in 3Q24. This brings FY25 current account to a narrower (-12% YoY) deficit of US$16.3bn, equivalent to 3.3% of GDP. The narrower YoY deficit is due to higher electronic exports (+20%) as well as gold (+76%) and other mineral exports (+27%). The FY25 CA deficit figure is wider than expected and is behind the BSP’s year-end CA deficit target of US$15.5bn (3.2% of GDP).

 

Government posts P165bn budget surplus in Jan

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P165bn in Jan, 142% wider YoY as gov't spending continued to decline. Revenue collections in Jan were flat (+0.4% YoY) given lackluster growth in tax collections and the decline in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were relatively flat, +1%. Customs collections rose a modest 2%. Non-tax revenues fell 12% given a moderation in Bureau of Treasury (BTr) income. Expenditures dipped 24% YoY in Jan due to the steep decline in primary spending. Primary spending for the month fell 40%. Since July 2025, January 2026 marks the sixth consecutive month of a YoY decline in gov't spending amid the slowdown in infrastructure projects following the flood control scandal. Interest payments rose 22% from additional debt and coupon payment timing changes following refinancing.

 

Government debt rises to P18.13trn in end-Jan

The government's total debt stock went up by 2.4% MoM and 11% YoY to P18.13trn as of end-Jan. The increase was driven by "front-loading domestic and external issuances" ahead of global market uncertainties that may raise interest costs. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 68.0% and 32.0% being external borrowings. Domestic debt increased by 1.7% MoM to P12.32trn. External debt rose by 3.9% MoM to P5.81trn. The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) stated that the debt level is within the P19.06trn FY26 borrowing program.

 

BOP posts US$2.3bn deficit in Feb; GIR settles at US$113.3bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Feb posted a deficit of US$2.3bn. Wider than both the US$373mn deficit in Jan and a reversal from the US$3.09bn surplus in Feb 2025. This brings the 2M26 deficit to US$2.7bn. For 2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn (1.2% of GDP). Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-Feb stood at US$113.3bn, 5% higher YoY and up from the US$112.6bn in the previous month of Jan. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

 

Remittances rise by 3.5% in January

Cash remittances amounted US$3.0bn in Jan, 3.5% higher YoY—decelerating from the 4.2% growth in Dec. Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+5.1%), Saudi Arabia (+4.6%), and the UAE (+9.9%).

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth slows to 9.3% in Jan

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth slowed to 9.3% in Jan from 9.6% in Dec, the second month of single-digit growth after a previous 19-month streak of double-digit growth. Loans to productive activities moderated to 8.2% vs 8.4% in Dec. Consumer loans still grew at a robust pace of 21.3%, a tad slower than Dec's 21.5% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated to 8.6% in Jan from 7.2% in Dec.

 

Manufacturers, retailers agree with DTI to halt price increases for two weeks

The Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI) stated that there will be a price freeze for basic necessities and prime commodities from April 1 to 16 as several manufacturers and retailers agreed defer any price adjustments to absorb rising logistics and inventory costs. Participating manufacturers include Century Pacific Food Inc. (CNPF PM), Monde Nissin (MONDE PM), Universal Robina Corporation (URC PM), San Miguel Foods (FB PM), Nestle Philippines, Proctor & Gamble Philippines, Unilever Philippines, among others. Retailers that also participated include Robinsons Retail Holdings Inc. (RRHI PM), SM Markets—food retail arm of SM Investments (SM PM), Metro Retail Stores Group Inc. (MRSGI PM), etc. The DTI stated that it will monitor compliance and ensure that prices remain stable during the period.

 

Department of Energy says oil crisis won’t lead to power outages

The Department of Energy assured a Senate panel that the worsening global oil supply situation will not lead to power outages. Energy Undersecretary Alessandro Sales said that the power sector may be affected, but to a limited degree. Note that natural gas accounts for 25% of the Luzon grid’s energy mix.

 

Energy regulator suspends electricity spot market

The Energy Regulatory Commission ordered the suspension of the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM), following the declaration of a state of national energy emergency. WESM trading was officially halted at 12:05 am on March 26. During the suspension period, the regulator will implement an administered price (AP) and will oversee dispatch, which will prioritize renewables. The ERC is consulting with stakeholders to finalize a Modified Administered Pricing Mechanism by April 1. The proposed framework introduces technology-specific pricing to reflect fuel costs. Coal plants may receive a fixed rate of P6/kwh, while natural gas plants will be paid based on contracted prices. The suspension of the spot market trading will create uncertainty on how buyers and sellers of power can transact and agree on pricing until the administered pricing mechanism is finalized. For some sellers of spot electricity, the suspension will remove the opportunity to make windfall profits.

 

Malls to implement shorter operating hours

Mall operators announced that they would reduce their operating period by 1-2 hours per day beginning March 30. Malls are typically open from 10AM to 10PM. New operating hours - 11AM to 9PM from Monday to Friday; 10AM to 9PM on Saturday and Sunday.

 

Energy regulator pushes for staggered increases in electricity bills

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) urged power utilities to implement staggered billing with suppliers to soften the impact of rising electricity rates on consumers. The Department of Energy estimates that electricity prices could rise 16% in April.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 8.5% YoY in February; Toyota unit sales drop 3%

CAMPI data showed car sales fell 8.5% YoY to 35,842 units in February (+6.4% MoM). Note that BYD is not a member of CAMPI; BYD’s market share in 2025 stood at 5%. Brings 2M26 unit sales down 9.4% YoY to 69,538 units. Sales of electric vehicles (ex-BYD) surged 71% YoY to 3,098 units, accounting for 9% of car sales. Brings 2M26 EV sales up 67% YoY to 5,708 units, accounting for 8% of YTD sales. Hybrids accounted for 80% of sales while PHEVs and BEVs each accounted for 10%. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,954 units, down 3% YoY but up 10% MoM. Brings TMP’s 2M26 unit sales down 6.3% YoY to 34,300 units. TMP is targeting unit sales to grow “up to 5%” (~229k-240k units) this year.

Government moves to insulate airlines amidst oil price shock

The Department of Transportation, together with airline regulators, ordered to reduce passenger service fees across all airports and shorten the evaluation and implementation of fuel surcharge to 15 days (from 1 month). Moves aim to alleviate the burden on both airlines and passengers affected by the spike in jet fuel costs. Fuel accounts for 36% of CEB’s cash costs and 30% of operating costs. We are forecasting jet kero prices will average US$81/bbl vs. spot at US$198. Every 10% increase in jet kero prices will reduce EBITDA by 10% and profit by 50%, holding other things constant.

 

House rules complaints against VP Sara Duterte sufficient in substance; Duterte asked to submit response

The House Committee on Justice has ruled that the third and fourth impeachment complaints filed against Vice President Sara Duterte were "sufficient in substance, with 54 voting in favor of the motion and only one voting against. The committee will then issue the respondent (Duterte) with a copy and a notice to respond to the complaints within 10 calendar days. Both sides must submit affidavits/counter-affidavits and documentary evidence along with their pleadings. The committee will conduct hearings and submit a recommendation whether to approve or dismiss the complaints. Note that the committee has 60 session days since referral of the verified complaint to submit a recommendation to the House. The House would then need a one-third vote to approve and elevate the impeachment complaint against Duterte to the Senate for trial.

 

Marcos' approval rating rises 2ppt to 36% in March

In the latest Pulse Asia Research Survey, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s approval rating increased 2ppt to 36% from 34% in Dec. Notably, this is lower than his rating prior to the arrest of Former President Duterte (Feb-25, 42%), pre-flood control scandal exposé (Jun-25, 42%), and much lower than the first survey in the start of his term (Nov-25, 82%). Marcos' approval rating in Luzon stands at 54% in March from 51% in Dec, Visayas at 18% from 25%, Mindanao 12% from 7%. Vice President Sara Duterte's approval rating was 1ppt lower at 55% in March from 56% in Dec. VP Duterte's approval rating in Luzon was at 31% from 45%, Visayas at 72% from 68%, Mindanao stable at 95%.
