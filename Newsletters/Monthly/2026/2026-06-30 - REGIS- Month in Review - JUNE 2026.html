REGIS REPORT

30 June 2026
 

Philippine stocks rebounded in June, recovering steep losses in the last two months lifted by a positive surprise in inflation, lower oil prices from easing Middle East tensions, and slowing foreign outflows. The PCOMP reclaimed the 6,000-handle with a 4.7% or 268.41-pt MoM gain in June to 6,037.17.

 

The June rally erased the PCOMP's losses in the last two months, taking 2Q gains to 1.5% and tempering the YTD decline to -0.3%, which still largely lags most global peer markets.

 

May inflation unexpectedly slowed to 6.8% YoY from 7.2% in April, well below consensus forecast of 7.8% and the BSP's 7.1-7.9% forecast range. Still, the BSP proceeded with a widely expected 25-bp rate hike to 4.75% and maintained a hawkish stance. Globally, optimism over potential US-Iran peace talks drove crude oil prices sharply lower for much of the month, easing concerns over prolonged elevated inflation and supporting risk appetite despite choppy progress in negotiations toward month-end.

 

ICT (+16.4% MoM) remained the market's standout performer, extending its record-breaking rally on sustained foreign buying and a resilient earnings outlook, along with the BSP's decision to raise the single-stock exposure limit for UITFs from 15% to 20%. Banks also outperformed with the sector index up 3.5% MoM, as higher interest rates and resilient loan growth supported earnings expectations. The larger consumer names CNPF (+18.3% MoM) and JFC (+6.0% MoM) likewise benefited from softer-than-expected inflation and easing oil prices.

 

Outside of these themes, GLO (+12.3% MoM) was among top gainers for the month after its fintech unit Mynt announced it has finally filed for its long-awaited IPO. Based on the initial prospectus, the offering could raise up to P92.3bn (US$1.5bn), making it the largest IPO in Philippine history. The listing is seen unlocking value for GLO which owns a third of Mynt and derives a quarter of its profits from the business.

 

On the other hand, mining stocks were big underperformers as weaker global metal prices triggered heavy selling across the sector, while SCC (-11.5% MoM) and DMC (-17.6% MoM) remained under pressure amid uncertainty over the rebidding of the Semirara coal mining contract.

 

Flow-related factors continued to drive market moves, with speculation surrounding the upcoming August MSCI review pressuring stocks perceived to be at risk of exclusion, particularly ALI (+1.5% MoM), AC (-4.3% MoM) and TEL (-1.7% MoM). Meanwhile, MYNLD (-11.4% MoM) was added to the FTSE Small Cap Index. The consensus expectation is that it will join the PCOMP at the upcoming August rebalancing, replacing CNVRG (-8.0% MoM).

 

Domestic financial conditions improved over the course of June. The PH10Y yield fell to 6.9% from its recent peak of 7.8% in late-May, while the peso strengthened to 61.356:$1 from the 61.745 May low, as lower oil prices eased pressure on the country's external accounts.

 

Foreign flows remained skewed to sell for the month, but total outflows narrowed to $22m in June from $150m in May. This took 1H26 net foreign outflows to $245m.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Index breadth turned more neutral in June with 16 gainers against 14 losers.

 

Transport remained the clear standout, with ICT (+18.3% MoM) extending its record-breaking rally on sustained foreign inflows, strong operating data, and flow-driven buying following the BSP's decision to raise single-stock exposure limits for UITFs.

 

Banks recovered as expectations of higher-for-longer interest rates and resilient loan growth supported earnings outlooks. SECB (+5.6% MoM) and CBC (+4.0% MoM) saw sharp gains while the big three banks BDO (+3.9% MoM), MBT (+2.4% MoM), and BPI (+2.2% MoM) also finished strong despite Fitch's late-month downgrade of the sector outlook on slowing growth and rising credit costs.

 

Property names remained mixed as elevated interest rates continued to weigh on sentiment. ALI (+1.5% MoM) recovered from 16-year lows, partly lifted by optimism after parent AC (-4.3% WoW) disclosed significant share purchases early in the month, while SMPH (-1.6% MoM) remained under pressure.

 

Conglomerates underperformed the broader market. DMC (-17.6% MoM) fell sharply following delays in the rebidding of its subsidiary SCC's (-11.5% MoM) coal operating contract. AC (-4.3% MoM) was under pressure by MSCI deletion speculations and general weakness in ALI. AEV (+3.5% MoM) and SM (+2.8% MoM) notably bucked the sector weakness.

 

The larger consumer proxies rebounded on the softer May inflation print and lower oil prices. CNPF (+18.3% MoM) led gainers, while JFC (+6.0% MoM) recovered from May's MSCI-driven selloff. PGOLD (-9.8% MoM) was a notable laggard as investors took profits given its general outperformance.

 

Power and utilities were broadly weaker. ACEN (-8.5% MoM) gave back part of May's gains as the renewable energy theme cooled, while MYNLD (-11.4% MoM) and MWC (-10.4% MoM) sold off on concerns over potential water shortages amid El Niño.

 

Telcos were mixed. GLO (+12.3% MoM) outperformed after its fintech unit Mynt finally filed for its long-awaited IPO. TEL (-1.7% MoM) ended the month lower amid speculation of its deletion from the MSCI Index, while CNVRG (-8.0% MoM) also remained pressured by further selling ahead of a widely-expected deletion from the PCOMP at the August rebalancing.

 

Gaming rebounded from heavily oversold levels, with PLUS (+11.4% MoM) recovering strongly after May's steep decline, while BLOOM (+0.6% MoM) was little changed.

 

Mining was the weakest-performing sector as the correction in global metal prices triggered broad-based selling. APX (-26.7% MoM), NIKL (-27.0% MoM), PX (-27.7% MoM), and OGP (-13.8% MoM) all posted sharp declines during the month.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP raises key policy rate by 25bp to 4.75%; revises inflation forecasts

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.75%. This is the second 25bp rate hike in a row and in line with consensus expectations. The move was done to help anchor inflation expectations as well as to mitigate the risk of second-round effects. The BSP noted that there may be potential further hikes to bring inflation back to its 3% point long-term target. BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that global oil and fertilizer prices remain elevated and continue to put pressure on domestic fuel and food prices. He added that the upcoming El Niño could be "unusually big" and that it poses a "big risk" to inflation. However, it was noted that El Niño's potential impact has not yet been factored into the BSP's revised inflation forecast. The central bank slightly revised upwards its inflation forecasts for 2026/27 from 6.3%/4.3% to 6.4%/4.5%. 

 

Philippine inflation eases in May amid fuel price rollbacks

Philippine inflation slowed to 6.8% YoY in May from 7.2% in April, coming in below the 7.8% median Street estimate and also below the BSP's forecast range of 7.1-7.9%. This cooled from 7.2% in April and brings YTD-May inflation to 4.5%, vs the FY26 Street/BSP forecast of 6.3%. The deceleration was attributed in part to lower fuel prices following progress in ceasefire talks between the US and Iran, which helped ease global energy prices. The development benefited the Philippines, which imports more than 90% of its oil requirements from the Middle East. Fuel price rollbacks took Transport inflation down to 16.2% (from 21.4%); Food also slid to 5.7% (from 6.0%) as did Housing & Utilities to 7.8% (from 8.2%). BSP Governor Eli Remolona said policymakers would monitor the inflation data as they assess the need for a policy rate adjustment ahead of the central bank’s June 18 meeting.

 

Gov't cuts 2026 GDP growth target to 3.5-4.5%; expects recovery in 2H26

Dept. of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan stated that the GDP growth target for 2026 has been reduced to 3.5-4.5% from 5.0-6.0% previously given the impact of the Middle East conflict and gov't corruption crackdown. Recall that 1Q26 GDP growth was 2.8%, the slowest since 2009 excluding the pandemic. To reach the lower and upper ends of the FY26 growth target band, GDP growth needs to average 3.7% and 5.1%, respectively, for the rest of the year. Balisacan added that he expects government spending to rebound in 2H26, noting that the gov't is seeing an improving fiscal outlook in the second half of this year. He remains optimistic that the Middle East crisis did not erode the country's growth narrative with household consumption and services expected to continue to anchor growth. He also added that the return to high growth can be done through mitigating external risks and inflationary pressures, prioritizing high-impact infra projects, as well as restoring public trust.

Trade deficit widens 50% YoY to $6.0bn in April

The April trade deficit widened to US$6.0bn (+50% YoY) as imports picked up while exports growth slowed—this is the highest deficit recorded since Aug 2022. This brings the 4M26 trade deficit to US$19.3bn, 17% wider YoY. Imports for the month climbed 22% YoY to US$13.2bn, accelerating from the 12% increase in Mar. The growth in imports was primarily led by the 106% surge in fuel imports as oil prices soared, which offset the 17% decline in consumer goods. Exports growth decelerated to 6% YoY (US$7.2bn) from 20% YoY the previous month, marking the sixteenth consecutive month of YoY growth in exports. Growth in electronics exports significantly slowed to 1% YoY to US$3.4bn from 33% YoY in Mar, while non-electronics exports rose 11% to US$3.8bn, up from 6% in the previous month.

 

Government posts P198bn budget deficit in May

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P198bn in May, 37% wider YoY but a reversal from the P31bn surplus in April, which brings the 5M26 deficit to P522bn, flat (-0.3%) vs the same period last year. Revenue collections in May declined 7% YoY, a reversal from +3% YoY growth in April as the 12% growth in tax collections were offset by the 64% dip in non-tax revenues. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections accelerated to +15% from +0.4% the previous month but this huge swing can be attributed to the extension of the filing/payment deadline of the Annual Income Tax Return from April to 15 to May 15 following the declaration of the State of National Energy Emergency. Customs collections moderated to 6% growth from +16% the previous month amid the the temporary suspension of excise taxes on LPG and kerosene. Non-tax revenues plunged 64% due to the difference in timing of dividend remittances as well as one-off receipts booked from the Bases and Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) (recall that dividends from GOCCs were remitted early this year in Feb.) Meanwhile gov't spending was up 4% YoY, moderating from the 11% growth in April, as primary spending for the month slowed to 1% from 8% the month before, while interest payments rose 21% YoY.

 

President Marcos mulls extra budget to address oil shock impact

President Ferdinand Marcos stated that the gov't is considering a supplemental budget and legislative amendments to existing laws in order to mitigate the impact of the Middle East conflict. A supplemental budget is an additional spending plan approved through legislation in order to fund programs not covered by the national budget. The amount was not specified, but recall that the Dept. of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev) back in April stated that the gov't may require P429bn to fund ongoing response measures if the war were to prolong until December. The budget deficit as of 4M26 was P324bn (-14% YoY) and equivalent to ~20% of the P1.65tn deficit projected for this year.

 

Government debt inches down to P18.49tn in end-Apr

The government's total debt stock went down by 0.1% MoM (P17.6bn) to P18.49tn as of end-Apr due to the repayment of domestic securities, which offset the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to Mar. As of end-Apr, the USD-PHP depreciated to P60.3 from P59.4 at end-Mar. The debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2% as of 1Q26 from 63.2% at end-2025, the highest since hitting 65.7% in 2005. The Dept. of Finance's forecasts as of Aug 2025 projected the debt-to-GDP ratio to hit 61.8% in 2026, before gradually declining to 61.3% in 2027, 60.3% in 2028, and 59.5% by 2029.

 

BOP posts US$131mn surplus in May; GIR settles at US$104.0bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of May posted a surplus of US$131mn, marking a reversal from both the US$2.1bn deficit in the previous month of April and the US$298mn deficit in May 2025. This brings the 5M26 deficit to US$7.3bn, wider than the US$5.8bn deficit for the same period last year. For FY2026, the BSP projects a BOP deficit of US$5.9bn. Meanwhile, the revised GIR level for end-May stood at US$104.0bn, down 1% YoY and flat (-0.3%) MoM. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 6.7 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. For 2026, the GIR is expected to settle at $110.0bn.

 

Remittances rise by 2.0% in April

Cash remittances amounted US$2.7bn in Apr, 2.0% higher YoY—decelerating from the 2.3% growth in Mar. This marks the lowest YoY growth figure since May 2022 (+1.8%). The BSP highlighted that the continued YoY growth reflects the resilience of remittances from Overseas Filipinos despite "prevailing global economic conditions". Remittance growth was primarily driven by Singapore (+5.5%), Saudi Arabia (+2.9%), and the UAE (+2.8%).

 

BIR collections for May increase by 14%

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) guided that gross revenue in May amounted to P279bn, up 14% YoY—an acceleration from the flat growth (+0.4% YoY) in April. BIR Commissioner Charlito Mendoza noted that the rebound in collections was due to the extension of the annual income tax return filing deadline by one-month from the original date of April 15. As of 5M26, the BIR collected P1.43tn (+5.5% YoY), this exceeds the bureau's P1.42tn five-month collection target by P9.7bn. The BIR targets to collect P3.43tn (+10% YoY) for 2026.

 

Government infrastructure spending drops 28% YoY in 4M26

Infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 28% YoY to P300bn in 4M26. This trails the government’s full-year target of P1.29tn, down 3% YoY and equivalent to 4.2% of GDP. Derived infra spend for the month of April alone fell 37% YoY and 40% MoM to P64bn. Officials cited slow completion and billings in the Department of Public Work’s projects.

 

More than 50 companies interested in Pax Silica hub

Joshua Bingcang, President of the Bases Conversion Development Authority (BCDA), said that more than 50 companies, including trillion-dollar tech firms, are interested in participating in the Pax Silica Initiative. One such firm is in advanced stages of due diligence. The Pax Silica hub will be situated in 1,600-hectare plot in Clark. Mr. Bingcang said the BCDA and the US Department of State are working on a framework agreement that would deal with the legal, commercial and other issues related to developing the hub. He previously said that the Pax Silica AI industrial hub will not be operational until at least two years from now. The Pax Silica coalition includes 14 countries.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth picks up to 11.4% in April

Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loan growth accelerated to 11.4% in Apr from 10.7% in Mar. The 11.4% loan growth rate is the fastest since July 2025 (11.8%). Loans to productive activities picked up to 10.7% vs 9.7% the previous month. Consumer loans grew at a still robust pace of 19.6%, but moderating from Mar's 20.5% rate. This is the first time consumer loan growth was below 20% since Aug 2022 (18.3%). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth inched up slightly to 12.2% in Apr from 12.1% in Mar.

 

BSP moves to lower fees on digital fund transfers

The BSP amended rules on pricing mechanisms for fees imposed on person-to-person electronic fund transfers. It lifted the moratorium, which previously disallowed increases in InstaPay and PESONet transaction fees. However, the new rules mandate that all fees be based on actual processing costs and that fees remain lower than over-the-counter banking fees. New rules are grounded on the implementation of zero fees for small merchant payments and reduce person-to-person transfer fees.

 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 16% YoY in May; Toyota unit sales down 13%

CAMPI reported vehicle sales dropped 16% YoY to 33,532 units in May (+24% MoM). This brings YT-May industry unit sales down 12% YoY to 167k units. Note though that BYD’s sales are not captured by CAMPI’s data. Various news outlets reported that industry sales reached 35,200 units in May (+6% MoM) if estimates of BYD and Denza sales are included in the tally which suggests that BYD and Denza brands combined accounted for 5% of the market in May. It further suggests that on a month-on-month basis, derived sales of the BYD brand fell to 1.7k units in May from almost 6k units in April. BYD/Denza is the third best-selling car brand after Toyota (48.5%) and Mitsubishi (15.4%), besting other brands, such as Suzuki (4.1%), Ford (3.6%), and Isuzu (2.6%).

 

Power spot prices spike in May

Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) prices surged in May, after the ERC lifted the WESM’s suspension on May 1. Recall that on March 26, the ERC suspended the WESM to address supply risks and price volatility triggered by the Middle East conflict. Spot prices in Luzon jumped to P7.02/kwh in May, up 66% YoY and 11% MoM.

 

PAGCOR expects gaming revenue to decline in 2026

PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that industry GGR is likely to be between P320bn and P350bn in 2026. This range reflects a 12%-19% decline from the 2025 figure of P396bn and implies GGR could decline further from 1Q26 levels. He cited the delinking of e-wallets from online gambling platforms as well as the Middle East Crisis as the reasons for the forecasted decline. In 1Q26, industry GGR fell 16% YoY (up 1% QoQ) to P87.6bn. GGR of bingo + e-games fell 22% YoY (down 1% QoQ) to P39.9bn, while GGR of (land-based) licensed casinos declined 10% YoY (up 2% QoQ) to P44.5bn. GGR of PAGCOR-operated casinos decreased 8% YoY (up 10% QoQ) to P3.2bn.

 

Senator Gatchalian formally elected as Senate President

Senator Sherwin Gatchalian was elected as Senate President as the majority bloc was able to garner 13 votes following the addition of Senator Joel Villanueva to the majority bloc. Gatchalian replaced Senator Alan Peter Cayetano who was removed as leader on June 3. Senator Tito Sotto III was elected as Senate pro tempore while Senator Migz Zubiri took oath as the Senate majority leader. Senator Cayetano, despite being absent, formally assumed the role as the minority leader following Senator Loren Legarda's move to nominate him. Senator Cayetano alongside his allies in the minority bloc, however, petitioned the Supreme Court to nullify the election of the Gatchalian leadership, arguing that the 12-person quorum was illegitimate. House Deputy Speaker Jay Khonghun stated that the petition does not automatically suspend or delay the impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte.
