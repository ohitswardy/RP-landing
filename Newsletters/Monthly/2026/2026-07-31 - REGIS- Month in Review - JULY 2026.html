REGIS REPORT

31 July 2026
 

Philippine equities extended their recovery in July, supported by easing inflation, returning foreign inflow, and continued bargain hunting in beaten-down large caps. However, gains were pared late in the month mostly as geopolitical tensions resurfaced and oil prices rebounded. The PCOMP still finished July higher by 3.3% or 199.27 pts MoM at 6,236.44, turning positive YTD by 3.0%. Amid the broad rotation out of AI plays, the Philippines along with most of Asean managed to broadly outperform global benchmarks for the month.

June inflation slowed further to 6.4% YoY from 6.8% in May, below consensus expectations and comfortably within the BSP's forecast range. While BSP Gov. Eli Remolona maintained that the economy could withstand another rate hike, he later clarified there was only a "small chance" of a larger 50-bp move at the August policy meeting, reinforcing expectations that monetary tightening would remain measured. However, renewed conflict between the US and Iran later in the month pushed Brent crude back above $100/bbl, reversing some of the optimism from easing oil prices and weighing on the peso.

The market's biggest macro surprise came from Pres. Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA), where he called for amendments to the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA), including the removal of system loss charges passed on to consumers. MER (-15.8% MoM) was among the biggest index decliners for July as investors priced in the potential hit to earnings and the risk of delays to its ongoing rate rebasing process.

Property emerged as one of the strongest-performing sectors as investors rotated into oversold names. ALI (+14.2% MoM) led gains following sustained bargain hunting and continued support from parent AC (+22.4% MoM) that has been acquiring ALI shares in the market. Banks also outperformed, with BPI (+6.8% MoM) and BDO (+5.1% MoM) advancing after reporting broadly in-line 2Q results, supported by resilient loan growth despite higher provisioning costs. ICT (+8.2% MoM) remained an outperformer amid sustained foreign buying and upbeat earnings expectations.

Several corporate stories also drove stock-specific performance. FGEN (+22.6% MoM) surged following reports that Indonesia's Barito Renewables Energy is in talks to acquire its subsidiary Energy Development Corp (EDC) in a transaction reportedly valuing the geothermal business at more than $5bn. On the other hand, uncertainty surrounding the rebidding of the Semirara coal mine continued to pressure sentiment on SCC (-11.1% MoM) and parent DMC (-2.5% MoM), with the government delaying the bidding process further after proposing a selling volume cap for the domestic market.

Positioning around index events was also a key driver of individual stock moves. At the August PSEi rebalancing executed by month-end, MYNLD (-5.7% MoM) formally replaced CNVRG  (+6.5% MoM), although investors largely sold into the inclusion and bought into the deletion. Separately, the PSE announced sweeping changes to its index methodology effective at the February 2027 rebalancing, including lower free float requirements for large-cap stocks and new liquidity screens based on tradability relative to float. The revisions drove speculation over future index changes, benefiting potential inclusions such as AP (+4.5% MoM) while weighing on the likes of CBC (-7.3% MoM), which is perceived to be at risk by the stricter liquidity criteria, as well as PLUS (-16.2% MoM), which is seen to be dropped should there be need to make room for predicted additions.

Domestic financial conditions softened over the month as higher oil prices pressured the currency, although the peso still managed to finish July stronger by 0.2% MoM at 61.246:$1. The PH10Y yield meanwhile rose to 7.43% from 6.90% at end-June. Foreign flows reverted to net buying by $67m for July, the first monthly inflow since before the Iran war broke out in March, as outflows from AI themes rotated into global underperformers.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Banks broadly outperformed amid resilient loan growth and largely in-line 2Q earnings despite lingering macro headwinds. BPI (+6.8% MoM) led the Big Three after kicking off reporting season with solid operating results, while BDO (+5.1% MoM) and MBT (+1.1% MoM) also reported similarly upbeat 2Q numbers. CBC (-7.3% MoM) sold off on speculation of its potential removal from the PSEi based on stricter liquidity thresholds from the revised index methodology.

 

Property emerged as one of the strongest sectors as investors rotated into oversold large caps. ALI (+14.2% MoM) led gains on sustained bargain-hunting and continued support from parent AC (+22.4% MoM), which disclosed additional budget for share purchases early in the month. RLC (+3.8% MoM) and MEG (+6.7% MoM) also participated in the sector's recovery although SMPH (+1.1% MoM) lagged.

 

Conglomerates were led by AC (+22.4% MoM), benefiting from the rebound in ALI as well as bottom-fishing after previous MSCI deletion fears had left the stock oversold. On the other end, JGS (-16.0% MoM) remained weak, weighed down by weakness in its subsidiary URC (-4.7% MoM) along with the deep correction in its core investment MER (-15.8% MoM).


Transport remained among the market's best-performing sectors, with ICT (+8.2% MoM) extending its rally on continued foreign buying and resilient earnings expectations despite some profit-taking late in the month. CEB (-2.0% MoM) underperformed as higher oil prices renewed concerns over fuel costs.

 

Consumer names were mixed as investors weighed softer inflation, the Metro Manila minimum wage hike, and renewed oil price concerns later in the month. JFC (+10.6% MoM) continued its rebound from a rather weak 1H performance. RRHI (-19.5% MoM) fell sharply following the completion of its tender offer and impending voluntary delisting, while food manufacturers URC (-4.7% MoM) and MONDE (-4.0% MoM) lagged.

 

Power and utilities fell after Pres. Marcos called for amendments to the EPIRA during his SONA, most notably on the removal of system loss charges passed on to consumers. MER (-15.8% MoM) led decliners as investors priced in the potential earnings impact and uncertainty over its rate rebasing process. Sentiment also weighed on ACEN (-3.6% MoM), while FGEN (+22.6% MoM) bucked the sector following reports that Indonesia's Barito Renewables Energy is in talks to acquire its subsidiary EDC. Also, PSE index-related positioning dragged MYNLD (-5.7% MoM) as investors sold into its August inclusion, and lifted AP (+4.5% MoM) as the market reacted to its likely February inclusion.

 

Telcos were mixed. TEL (+7.6% MoM) also rebounded following previous speculation of its MSCI deletion, while GLO (-4.6% MoM) gave back some of June's gains following the IPO filing of its fintech arm Mynt. CNVRG (+6.5% MoM) recovered after heavy underperformance in recent months, as investors bought into its deletion from the PSEi during the August rebalancing.

 

Gaming underperformed as PLUS (-16.2% MoM) reversed part of June's sharp rebound amid speculation it could eventually lose its place at the next PSE rebalancing, while BLOOM (+21.0% MoM) rallied on bottom-fishing following months of large underperformance.

 

Miners broadly rebounded alongside firmer metal prices, led by PX (+31.7% MoM) and APX (+17.7% MoM).

PH gov't revises GDP growth, inflation, and fiscal program targets

The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) has adjusted the gov't's various macroeconomic assumptions following the impact of domestic and geopolitical developments. The gov't's GDP growth targets for 26/27/28E were reduced to 3.5-4.5%/5.0-6.0%/5.0-6.0% from 5.0-6.0%/5.5%-6.5%/6.0-7.0%. 1Q26 GDP growth was 2.8%, the slowest since 2009 excluding the pandemic. To reach the lower and upper ends of the FY26 growth target band, GDP growth needs to average 3.7% and 5.1%, respectively, for the rest of the year. Inflation expectations for 26/27E were revised upwards to average 6-7%/4-5%, respectively, before returning to target to 2-4% from 2028-20230. The DBCC for 2026 expects the fiscal deficit to hit P1.66tn (-5.4% of GDP) from P1.61tn (-5.3% of GDP) previously. For 2027, the deficit is expected to widen further to P1.69tn (-5.1% of GDP) from P1.59tn (-4.8% of GDP). 2028-2030 fiscal deficits are expected to be -4.8%, -4.2%, and -3.5% of GDP. Revenue projections for 26/27/28E were adjusted to P4.81tn/5.21tn/P5.52tn from P4.82/P5.12tn/5.57tn. The disbursements for 26/27/28E were revised higher to P6.47tn/P6.90tn/P7.25tn from P6.43tn/P6.71tn/P7.11tn. The 2027 proposed budget is P7.2tn or 21.7% of GDP, +6% YoY vs 2026's P6.79tn budget.

 

Inflation moderates further to 6.4% in June

Headline inflation moderated to 6.4% in Jun from May's 6.8%. This was in line with consensus expectations of 6.5% and within the BSP's forecast range of 6.0-7.0% for Jun. The softer inflation print is the second consecutive month of deceleration following five consecutive months of accelerating inflation. This brings 6M26 inflation to 4.8%, above the upper limit of the BSP's long-term target of 3% (± 1ppt) but still below their forecast of 6.4% for the year. Inflation in Jun decelerated given slower YoY increases vs May in food and transport prices. Food prices overall rose 5.2% YoY, decelerating from the 5.7% pace in May, driven by lower prices of meat and lower YoY increases in fish and rice which offset higher vegetable prices. Rice prices jumped 14.9% YoY in Jun, moderating slightly from the 15.6% YoY increase in May. Rice prices are currently at P49.5/kg and P48.0/kg as of end-Jun for local and imported well-milled rice, with prices stable relative to the previous month. Transport prices rose 12.8% YoY, slower than +16.2% the previous month. The increase in diesel prices slowed to +39.0% YoY from +58.5% YoY, while the gasoline price increase likewise moderated to +39.2% YoY from +51.6% YoY in May. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, picked up to 4.4% in Jun from 4.1% in May, driven by both core food (3.3% from 2.9% in May) and core non-food (4.6% from 4.4%). The 4.4% core inflation print is the highest since Dec 2023. The BSP in a statement highlighted that the acceleration in core inflation indicates persistent underlying inflationary pressures despite moderation in headline inflation. For 2026, the BSP forecasts inflation to settle at 6.4% (previously revised from 6.3%). The fourth out of the six Monetary Board meetings scheduled for the year will be held on Aug 27. The current policy rate is 4.75%.

Highlights from Marcos' fifth State of the Nation Address

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. delivered his fifth State of the Nation Address (SONA), outlining the administration's priorities for the remaining two years of his term, including governance, economic reforms, energy, infrastructure, social welfare, education, and tax relief. Marcos said cases had been filed against contractors, DPWH officials, and lawmakers over alleged irregularities in flood control projects, with charges also to be filed against former House Speaker Martin Romualdez, and said recovered funds would be returned to the public treasury. He urged Congress to amend the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) to remove system loss charges, expand renewable and nuclear energy, and reiterated a target for 50% of all cars to be EVs by 2040. On the economy, Marcos called for tax relief measures, including raising the income tax exemption threshold to P350,000 annually and exempting small businesses from the minimum corporate tax, while highlighting the US-backed Pax Silica Industrial Hub. He said MRT-7 is scheduled to open 12 stations next year, while the North-South Commuter Railway (Valenzuela-Malolos) is expected to partially begin operations next year, alongside continued efforts to accelerate digitalization and financial inclusion. Marcos also reported that 12mn people have benefited from the P20/kg rice program, that over P60bn has been allocated for the UPLIFT program, and announced education initiatives including a school feeding program for 4.5mn students, 30,000 additional teaching positions, and AI training programs for 1.8mn individuals.

P85 NCR minimum wage hike suspended by court following legal challenge

The National Capital Region (NCR) Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved a P85 (+12%) minimum wage increase, raising the NCR minimum wage to P780 to be implemented in two tranches: +P60 effective July 19, 2026, and the remaining +P25 effective January 20, 2027. The Dept. of Labor and Employment (DOLE) said the wage hike is estimated to directly benefit 1.1mn minimum wage workers and indirectly benefit 1.9mn workers through the correction of wage distortions. However, DOLE confirmed that the implementation of the first tranche was suspended following the issuance of a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) after Readycon Trading and Construction Corp. and R-II Builders Inc. challenged the wage order in court. The petition questioned whether the Regional Wage Board adequately considered the factors under Article 124 of the Labor Code, particularly the "fair return of the capital invested and capacity to pay of employers", and argued that the increase was issued "without adequate regard to compounding economic conditions". Labor Secretary Francis Tolentino said the agency respects the judicial process following the court's decision. Under the suspended wage order, the NCR minimum wage was set to increase to P780 through two tranches of +P60 and +P25 effective July 25, 2026 and January 20, 2027, respectively.

The June trade deficit widened to US$4.9bn (+12% YoY). This brings the 6M26 trade deficit to US$30.8bn, 26% wider YoY. Imports of electronics was the primary growth driver, surging 83% YoY vs non-electronics which was relatively flat (+1%) for the month. Exports growth accelerated to 24% YoY (US$8.8bn) from 8% the previous month. This marks the eighteenth consecutive month of YoY growth in exports. Growth in electronics exports picked up to +35% from +12% in May. These accounted for 60% of total exports in the month. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up +11%, accelerating from +3% the previous month.

Government expects US$40bn to US$70bn in investments from Pax Silica hub

The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) expects the Pax Silica hub to generate between US$40bn and US$70bn in investments at full development. The Philippines recorded US$7.8bn in FDI in 2025. It is also projected to generate up to 130,000 to 190,000 direct jobs and 500,000 to 800,000 indirect jobs. Exports could hit up to US$200bn. The 1,600-hectare site in New Clark City has an initial investment target of US$10bn. Timetable. 2026 - Negotiations with prospective partners. 2027 - Planning. 2028 - Start of construction. Participation in the project has expanded to more than 30 countries from fewer than 15 in April. Pax Silica will integrate semiconductor design, fabrication and advanced packaging, artificial intelligence computing, critical mineral processing, energy and data infrastructure, research and development (R&D), logistics, offices, housing, and commercial facilities as part of the Luzon Economic Corridor. The project seeks to move the Philippines up the manufacturing value chain by processing critical minerals domestically instead of exporting raw materials. The industrial hub is expected to require about three gigawatts of power at full development. A 500MW solar project with Saudi Arabia’s ACWA Power is under development. BCDA is also evaluating a proposal from an American company to develop a dedicated LNG plant and pipeline linking Subic and Clark. For water supply, the project will rely on surface water harvesting, storage, treatment, and recycling instead of groundwater extraction. The initial system is designed to supply 120 million liters per day, with capacity expandable to 300 million liters per day.

Consumer sentiment and outlook in 2Q26 declines amid rising inflation — BSP survey

The latest Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) results exhibited that current sentiment for the overall consumer confidence index (CI) was more pessimistic deteriorating to -42.0% in 2Q26 from -15.8% the previous quarter. A negative CI implies more respondents are pessimistic than optimistic about the country's economic condition, family financial situation and income. The survey was conducted from April 6-18 with 5,503 households surveyed nationwide. The more pessimistic sentiment in 2Q stemmed from concerns over higher inflation, corruption in the gov't, and insufficient gov't policies and programs to mitigate rising prices. -42.0% is the lowest since 4Q20 (-47.9%). Households indicated an inclination to prioritize essential goods while cutting back on discretionary spending. For the next quarter, consumer sentiment deteriorated to -16.3% from 1.8% the previous quarter. For the upcoming 12 months, consumers were less optimistic on economic prospects improving, with a neutral CI at 0.2%, down from 9.6% the previous quarter. Consumers expect higher unemployment and a weaker Philippine peso. The CES results are taken into account in the BSP's decision-making for monetary policy. Inflation has averaged 4.5% so far as of 5M26. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Aug 27 (fourth out of six scheduled this year).

 

BSP amends e-payments framework

The BSP issued Circular No. 1238, which states that fees for person-to-person e-payments across banks, e-wallet operators, and other payment service providers (PSPs) should not be materially different from fees within the same institution. Since transfers within one bank or e-wallet are often free, any difference in pricing should mainly reflect fees paid to the network switch operator. These institutions must also have an analysis of their costs for delivering electronic payment products and services. The BSP may require this in the conduct of its oversight activities.

 

Loan growth accelerates in May; NPLs up, but PDs improve

Bank lending growth accelerated to 12.5% YoY in May, a 16-month high, based on data excluding interbank loans (IBLs) and reverse repos (RRPs). Growth was driven by corporate or "loans for production," which increased 11.4% YoY, with Mining (+48%), Utilities (+30-33%), and Transport (+23%) posting the strongest gains, while consumer loans (excluding mortgages) slowed to 19% YoY. The banking system's NPL ratio rose 8bps MoM to 3.63%, up 5bps YoY. In contrast, the Past Due (PD) ratio declined 8bps MoM to 4.60%, though it remained 12bps higher YoY. Restructured loans were flat at 2.10%, while NPL coverage declined to 89%, falling below 90% for the first time since March 2022. Construction continued to record the weakest asset quality, with an NPL ratio of 14.55% and a PD ratio of 16.47%. Higher delinquencies were also recorded in the Trade, Real Estate, and Hotels/Restaurants sectors. Within consumer lending, credit card NPLs rose to 5.32% (+17bps MoM, +55bps YoY), while PDs increased to 7.85% (+14bps MoM, +54bps YoY).

Marcos signs order on Data Sovereignty

President Marcos signed Executive Order (EO) 119 that establishes a “data residency framework” for all government data. The EO mandates that some of the government’s data will need to be stored within the Philippines. Such data includes those classified as “Secret” or “Top Secret”. “Confidential” data should also be stored domestically, with exceptions. Other data can be stored in the cloud. Total government data currently housed in data centers/cloud is estimated to be equivalent to 200-300MW worth of IT load. Domestic data center capacity is currently only around ~130MW. Government agencies (and thus third-party providers) will have three years to transition and comply. This EO will ultimately mean greater demand for local data centers.

VP Sara Duterte impeachment trial starts

The proceedings for the impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte began Monday afternoon with the outcome determining whether she will be acquitted or perpetually be disqualified from holding public office and ending her 2028 presidential bid. Conviction requires the votes of at least two-thirds (16) of the Senate. The first day of trial focused on opening statements and procedural matters with no witnesses or presentation of evidence yet. The proceedings will start with allegations of Duterte's threats against President Marcos then charges involving confidential funds, bribery and unexplained wealth. The prosecution team stated that they are expecting and preparing for the defense's possible bid to dismiss the case. Spokesperson of the prosecution panel, Robert Ace Barbers, said that the team would also seek Duterte's perpetual disqualification from public office if she resigns before the end of the trial.

 

Sara Duterte leads in Pulse Asia's 2028 presidential preference survey

The Pulse Asia survey on voter preferences for the May 2028 presidential election showed Vice President Sara Duterte leading with 49% support, followed by former Vice President Leni Robredo at 26% and Senator Raffy Tulfo at 14%. The remaining potential candidates each garnered 2% or less. Note that the survey was conducted June 28-July 3 & 6, while the impeachment trial of VP Sara Duterte started on July 6. Duterte posted commanding leads across all major island groups, with her strongest support in Mindanao (87%), followed by the Visayas (53%), while she also led in Balance Luzon (31%) and Metro Manila (39%). Robredo ranked second nationally, drawing her strongest support from Metro Manila (38%) and Balance Luzon (31%), while posting more modest support in the Visayas (27%) and limited backing in Mindanao (8%). Tulfo placed third with relatively broad but lower support across regions, performing best in Balance Luzon (20%) and Metro Manila (16%), followed by the Visayas (10%) and Mindanao (2%). Across socioeconomic classes, Duterte led all segments, topping Class E with 59% support, while Robredo's strongest showing was among Class ABC (34%) and Tulfo's support was relatively consistent across income groups at 10-15%. For the senatorial preferences survey, the results found that the five strongest contenders are all incumbent senators—Robin Padilla (Rank 1–2), Tito Sotto (Rank 1–3), Raffy Tulfo (Rank 2–5), Alan Peter Cayetano (Rank 3–5), and Loren Legarda (Rank 3–5). Other incumbent senators that are within the top 12 are DepEd Secretary Sonny Angara (Rank 8–9), Senator Mark Villar (Rank 8–9), Senator Jinggoy Estrada (Rank 10–17), and Senator Chiz Escudero (Rank 11–19) all remained within or around the projected winning circle. Several candidates remained within striking distance of the Top 12, led by Philip Salvador (Rank 13–25), Jimmy Bondoc (Rank 13–25), and JV Ejercito (Rank 14–26).
