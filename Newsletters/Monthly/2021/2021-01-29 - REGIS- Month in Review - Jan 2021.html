REGIS REPORT

29 January 2021
 

The Philippines faltered in January – dropping 7.4% to close the month at 6612.62. The  PSEi posted its worst month since March last year – when the country was placed under strict lockdown. With large scale vaccinations expected to start only in 3Q, concerns of a delayed recovery weighed on Phils stocks. A lack of any near-term catalysts kept markets weak even as funds steadily flowed out. 

Philippine GDP contracted by 8.3% YoY in 4Q20, behind consensus estimate of -7.9%. FY20 GDP was at -9.5%, the country’s worst full-year contraction on record (since 1981). It will also likely be the worst 4Q/FY20 GDP print among our Asian peers. Domestic demand was weak, with personal consumption still -7.2% YoY in 4Q20, and capital formation collapsing -29.0% YoY. 

Metro Manila remains under the current General Community Quarantine status at least until end-February.  But while transportation restrictions have seen gradual easing, mobility numbers are still not particularly promising. Based on Google mobility data, movement has declined steadily in the first three weeks of the year with latest reading (Jan 22) back to around late 3Q and early 4Q levels. 

With mobility curtailed, much of the recovery is contingent on vaccination. The government has signed agreements for some 100m doses with various manufacturers. The vaccines will be arriving in batches starting February but will only really pick up in 2H21. 

The delayed recovery is being priced in, if foreign outflows were to be the gauge. With a decline of 7.5% (USD terms) YTD, the Philippines lags its ASEAN peers.  Malaysia shed 4.2% in January while Indonesia at a much lower pace of -1.95%.  Singapore is up 1.5% YTD.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreign outflows continued on to the new year with US$273mn exiting in January. 

Foreign participation was light at 25% of turnover (vs 47% in 2020). Local retail activity spiked, with speculative small-cap names accounting for > 40% of market volume.

Foreign selling was broad-based, with index heavyweights SM/SMPH/JGS bearing the brunt. Big cap conglos AC/GTCAP and consumers JFC/URC were also among top sold for the month. There was a mix of active and passive selling, with almost 60% of the month’s outflows coming through in the last week of January. 

Some inflows were seen in banks BDO and BPI. For BDO, long-only and HF buying was apparent in early January. 

Other marginal inflows were in ICT (reversal from US$10m outflow in December), and CNVRG. Budget airline operator CEB also saw a bit of foreign buying.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Large net foreign outflows were seen in Ayala Corp (-6.9% MoM) and GT Capital (-8.7% MoM) at US$26.1m, and US$18.8m respectively. SM Investments (-6.1% MoM) was also foreign sold (US$12.9m). 

Index heavyweights Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-15.8% MoM) and JG Summit (-15.1% MoM) dropped significantly on passive selling.

GT Capital dropped on plans by the Dept of Trade and Industry (DTI) to impose safeguard duties on large classes of imported automobiles in a supposed bid to protect “local manufacturers”. The DTI will slap a duty of P75k and P110k on imported CBUs of light passenger cars (PCs) and light commercial vehicles (CVs), respectively.  The safeguard duty will affect mostly low-to mid-end vehicles from most countries, except China and India. Also, the safeguard duty will not cover  the more expensive “luxury” vehicles (“luxury” threshold of US$25k FOB price for PCs and US$28k FOB price for CVs).  

Regis estimates models comprising half Toyota’s unit sales will likely be affected by the safeguard duty and the impact on retail prices is likely to be between 7% and 12% for those affected.  Assuming overall volumes for Toyota decline by 5%, this would translate to a 6% drop in TMP’s profit and 2% decline for GT Capital.

Sales of Toyota reached 13,710 in December, up +20% MoM. For the full year, Toyota vehicles sales reached 100,013 units, which is 38% lower vs 2019 and in line with management guidance (Regis FY20e is at 90k). Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV.

Metro Pacific (-5.6% MoM) signed a US$130m 5-year term loan from Mizuho Bank Ltd. The proceeds will be used to partially finance investments. As at end-September, MPI’s parent company gross debt (including off-balance sheet debt) stood at P88bn and cash stood at P25bn while net debt-to-equity at 45%.

San Miguel Aerocity was granted the franchise for the P740bn Bulacan airport after the legislation (RA 11506) lapsed into law last month. RA 11506 provides tax exemptions during a decade-long maximum construction period. San Miguel(-4.7% MoM) can continue to enjoy tax exemptions after construction or until the Bureau of Internal Revenue decides that it has fully recovered its investment costs. The 50-year franchise also entitles the company to a 12% return until investment recovery has been determined.

Banks were down across the board, reflective of weak market sentiment. 

BPI (+2.3% MoM) posted FY20 NPAT of P21.4bn down 26% YoY, in line with Regis forecast. Loans contracted 4.6% YoY, the bank’s first negative print since 2002. BPI reported a surprise improvement in asset quality, with NPL ratio falling 30bps QoQ from 2.98% in 3Q20 to 2.68% at end-2020. Management attributes the drop to some accounts turning current as well as write-offs of NPLs that were already fully provisioned. BPI restructured loans equivalent to about 1% of total loans, up from near-nil (0.07%) at 3Q20. Provisions stayed high at P7bn in 4Q20, which took NPL cover up to 115% from 100% in 3Q.

Meanwhile, the bank is merging its wholly-owned thrift bank BPI Family Savings Bank (BFSB) into the parent bank within the year. The move will likely have minimal impact on profits, but could boost parent CAR/CET-1 by 50-100bps. The merger should remove extra administrative and regulatory layers that come with having separate legal entities.

The rest of the sector did worse as concerns on asset quality and loan growth were aggravated by longer-than-expected economic recovery. 

Metrobank (-8.3% MoM) fell markedly in January, with US$19.8m in net outflows.  BDO (-5.7% MoM), and Security Bank (-3.1% MoM) also declined.

Philippine National Bank (-13.1% MoM) fell.  The BSP has given PNB the green light to infuse additional equity investment its wholly owned holding firm as part of a plan to dispose of low-earning assets. PNB acquired an additional 466.77mn shares of PNB Holdings amounting to P46.67 in exchange for certain real estate properties of the listed bank. Meanwhile, PNB’s CEO Jose Arnulfo Veloso earlier said that the transaction would allow the bank to beef up its lending portfolio by P80bn from the sale of low-earnings assets. PNB’s inventory includes the 10-ha PNB Financial Center in Pasay, office buildings in Makati CBD and the foreclosed 8-ha property of Ramon Jacinto.

 

The property sector was also soft.   

SM Prime (-8.3%MoM) says foot traffic at its malls reached 50-60% of pre-pandemic levels in December. This would be an improvement from the 35-40% in September.  However, with mobility data declining in the first three weeks of the year, overall mall foot traffic for the industry is not expected to make significant strides until a vaccine is more widely available.

Voting 13-1, the Supreme Court granted a restraining order against the implementation of the 5% tax on POGO’s gross bets imposed by the Bayanihan 2 Act.  If enforced, the 5% tax on gross bets was seen to make many online gaming operations unprofitable.

Filinvest Land (-0.9% MoM), Megaworld (-6.4% MoM), and SM Prime are most exposed to the POGO sector. POGOs occupy about 15% of FLI’s office space and 10% of MEG’s office portfolio. The Manila Bay area also comprised c. 25% of MEG’s 2019 residential launches. Meanwhile, although POGOs have little direct impact on SMPH’s earnings, c. 45% of NAV is comprised of land in the Manila Bay area, the POGOs’ preferred location.

AREIT (+9.0% MoM) signed an agreement to acquire The 30th office and mall complex from Ayala Land  (-7.8% MoM) for P5.1bn.  The complex has 75,000 sqm of leasable area. The deal does not involve the land on which the complex sits. The transfer is being done at P5.1bn, which equates to 6.1% EBITDA yield.

Filinvest Land announced it was planning to turn one of its subsidiaries into a REIT company. Given its asset portfolio, the expected NAV enhancement from the move to inject these office assets into a REIT should be modest (~5%).

The consumer sector was laden with double digit declines as investors priced in the likelihood of stalled recovery as vaccines have yet to be widely administered.

The Philippine Retailers Association (PRA) sees retail sales in 2021 to still be 20-30% below 2019 pre-pandemic levels, but 10% higher vs 2020. The group cited that malls continued to see weak foot traffic, cannibalization from e-commerce, land imits on public transportation.

Restaurants Jollibee (-9.0% MoM) Shakey’s Pizza (-11.0% MoM) and Max’s (-9.3% MoM) did not fare well. 

Retailers were also weak with Puregold (-12.12% MoM) dropping markedly.  Robinsons Retail (-5.9% MoM) and SSI Group (-13.4% MoM) also declined.   However,  continued easing in construction benefitted Wilcon (+4.1% MoM).

Food and beverage companies were mixed – with large cap Universal Robina (-11.5% MoM) dropping on net foreign selling (US$16.9m). San Miguel Food and Beverage (+1.4% MoM) and  Century Pacific (unch) fared better, however

 

Increasing competition kept the telco sector muted. 

Broadband service provider Converge ICT (+4.3% MoM) raised the speeds of its entry level package by 40% from 25Mbps to 35Mbps, widening the gap between its offering and those of its competitors. This latest action from CNVRG would have a more significant impact than its previous move to raise speeds of its higher-price packages, because a large majority of its subscribers are on its entry-level P1,500 plan. Over 90% of its fiber broadband subscribers are on the entry level package and should thus see a corresponding improvement in broadband speeds. This development is likely to prompt the other telcos, PLDT (-2.0% MoM) and Globe (-4.4% MoM), to either do the same or to reduce prices.

Converge is hopeful to reach its objective of 55% household coverage by 2025 with our fiber network. After passing 1mn subscribers before the end of 2020, the company is hoping to serve around 4-5mn customers in five years.

Meanwhile, the technical audit for new operator Dito Telecommunity, 53% owned by the listed Dito CME (+10.1% MoM) is underway. Dito Telecommunity received spectrum and license in exchange for a commitment to deliver service meeting certain standards. The first goalpost under those commitments is 37% population coverage and 27Mbps wireless internet speed by July 7, 2020. The regulators subsequently agreed to delay the date to Jan 7, 2021.

First Gen (+5.9% MoM) ended higher  as the green energy theme continued to play out.

First Gen is set to start construction of its LNG Terminal in March.  FGEN earlier said the facility would allow it to introduce LNG to the country as early as 3Q22. The project includes a floating storage regasification unit (FSRU), an LNG carrier than can store LNG.

Semirara (-10.9% MoM) and Meralco (-10.6% MoM) dropped.

The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) directed Meralco to refund P1.4bn in over-recoveries relating to transmission charges, lifeline subsidies and senior citizen subsidies. The refunds are to be done over three months. However, Meralco will also be allowed to recover P2.38bn in under-recoveries relating to generation charges and these higher charges will be collected over 24 months. Both items have already been accounted for (provisioned for) in Meralco’s income statement (P&L) and would have no direct impact on its reported income. The impact will only be on cash flow.

Meralco said at least three interested bidders increased their offered capacities for the 1,800-MW CSP of greenfield baseload capacity for 2024-2025. The aggregate offered capacity now totals 7,000MW, from the original offered 6,050MW when invitation to bid was first published. The opening of price offers of qualified bidders will be on Feb 10.

Aboitiz Power (-9.6% MoM) says it remains on track to more than double capacity by 2030. It will continue to pursue a major shift in energy mix – to an almost even thermal to clean energy ratio. Coal comprises two thirds of AP’s capacity, followed by hydro (13%), oil (11%), geothermal (9%) and solar (1%). In April, the company announced its 10-year plan to double capacity from 4,432MW to 9,300MW.

The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) said it approved the acquisition by Scatec Solar ASA, a Norweigan renewable energy company, of 100% shares in SN Power AS in a decision issued last January 14. The overall value was placed at $1.166bn. After the  acquisition Scatec will assume SN Power’s role as Aboitiz Power’s partner in several hydro projects including Magat, Ambuklao-Binga and Maris.

AC Energy (-20.6% MoM) announced that it will take a majority ownership in Solar Philippines Central Luzon Corp to develop and construct solar projects. SPCLC is a subsidiary of Solar Philippines Power Project Holdings of Leandro L. Leviste. ACEN said it signed a subscription agreement for P619k worth of common shares in SPCLC. Leviste’s Solar Para Sa Bayan Corporation was granted a congressional franchise to construct, install, establish, operate and maintain distributed energy resources and microgrids in the remote and unviable or unserved areas in selected provinces in the Philippines.

 

Cebu Air (-5.9% MoM) plans to issue US$250m worth of convertible preferred shares to existing shareholders through a stock rights offer at an offer price of USD 0.74/sh to USD 0.84/sh. Existing shareholders will be entitled to subscribe to the convertible pref shares before ex-date (February 5). Holders of the convertible prefs will be able to convert these shares into common shares at the indicative price range of P38/sh to P45/sh.

The conversion price range is below Regis’ assumption (P53/sh) and the current share price of P47.60/sh (as of end-Jan). This suggests that CEB’s share count could expand to 1.14bn -1.24bn (vs. base case of 1.06bn) from 601mn shares currently. Assuming a fully diluted share count of as much as 1.24bn would put the equity value of CEB at P65/sh.

CEB plans to raise another US$250mn via a private placement of convertible bonds/convertible warrants/convertible bonds + detachable warrants.

ICTSI (-4.0% MoM) completed the berth expansion at the Manila International Container Terminal (MICT). The 150-meter extension of Berth 7 and the container yard extension have increased MICT’s capacity by 10% to 3.3mn TEUs. MICT accounts for 25% of ICT’s group-wide volumes, 35% of revenues, and 50% of EBITDA.

Megawide (-10.3% MoM) closed in the red.  The Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) board has junked the appeal filed by the consortium of Megawide  and India-based GMR. Megawide had filed a motion for reconsideration after the MIAA announced the revocation of the consortium’s original proponent status it was originally awarded for its P109bn unsolicited proposal for the NAIA rehab. 

ABS-CBN (ABSP +1.0% MoM) was up slightly.  ABS-CBN could be granted a new franchise.  Deputy Speaker Vilma Santos-Recto said she will file a new bill that would grant ABS-CBN a fresh 25-year legislative franchise. Additionally, Senate President Vicente Sotto III has filed Senate Bill 1967, which also seeks to grant ABS-CBN a new franchise to construct, install, operate, and maintain television and radio broadcasting stations for 25 years.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation rises to 3.5% in December. Inflation hit a 22-month high of 3.5% in December. This is above street expectations (3.2%) but within the BSP’s forecast range of 2.9%-3.7%. Food inflation (vegetables, fruits, meats) accelerated in December (vs Nov ‘20) due to supply constraints from typhoons and the African Swine Fever. Meanwhile, rice inflation turned positive for the first time since March 2019 (rice tariffication). Additionally, transport (8.3%) and restaurants (2.5%) inflation also spiked. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno is not concerned about the accelerating inflation, saying the uptick in December is transitory, only brought about by the impact of typhoons on food supply and prices. There’s still more room to reduce banks’ reserve requirement ratio.

Budget deficit reaches P1.36tn in FY20. Based on preliminary data, the budget deficit reached P1.36tn in FY20 which is equivalent to 7.5% of GDP. This is higher than FY19 deficit of P660bn (3.4% of GDP) according to Dept. of Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez. This also implies Dec 2020 budget deficit stood at P294bn (+17% YoY). FY20 revenues stood at P2.842tn, down 10% YoY and short of the P2.853tn target, which means Dec 2020 revenues dropped 8% YoY, 9% MoM to P225bn. Meanwhile, expenditures reached P4.205tn in FY20, up 11% YoY but a tad short of target (P4.233tn) and implies Dec 2020 expenditures rose 5% YoY and 39% MoM to P519bn. To plug the deficit, the government borrowed P2.6tn from local and foreign lenders, excluding P540bn borrowed from the BSP. For 2021, the government is targeting a wider budget deficit of P1.78tn (8.9% of GDP). The debt-to-GDP ratio expected to settle at 57% this year (vs. 51% in end-Nov 20).

BSP may keep rates on hold in 1H21. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that interest rates will likely remain on hold at least through 1H21. The current monetary stance will persist for the next two quarters or even longer. There may be “some play” to further cut banks’ reserve requirement ratio from 12%, but not likely soon.

OFW remittances rise by 0.3% in November. OFW cash remittances grew by 0.3% YoY to US$2.38bn in November (-13.4% MoM). In PHP terms, remittances fell 4.6% YoY and 14% MoM and brings the 3MMA growth rate to +4.1%. Land-based remittances rose by +0.5% YoY while sea-based remittances dropped by -0.2% YoY. Additioanly, remittances from Asia, Europe, and Saudi Arabia dropped by 5.7%, 16%, and 7%, respectively. This brings YT-November remittances down 0.8% YoY to US$27bn.

PH Dollar reserves hit new record of US$109.8bn. Gross international reserves rose to US$109.8bn in end-December 2020, up by US$4.98bn (+4.8% MoM). This was helped by the National Government’s (NG) issuance of US$2.75bn ROP Global Bonds last December 2. The GIR is now equivalent to 11.7 months’ worth of imports.

 

Infrastructure spending drops 50% YoY, 29% MoM in November. Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell by 50% YoY, 29% MoM in November to P40.3bn. The govt cited high base effects from completed building projects of the LTO and LTFRB last year. This brings YT-November infra spend to P549bn, down 22% YoY. Some of this was due to delays in construction of some public works, as well as the discontinuance of some projects. For 2021, the government expects to increase infra spend by over 60% to P997bn.

BSP lends another P540bn to gov’t for pandemic response. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) approved another zero-interest bridge loan worth P540bn loan to the national government last Dec 28. This is the third time the BSP threw a credit line to the national government. The BSP loaned P300bn in March (repaid in September) and another P540bn in October 2020 (repaid in December). Under the law, the central bank can lend the government a maximum of about P850bn this year. The 10-year BVAL is at 2.956%, lower than in March (5%).

BSP Governor on medical leave. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno went on medical leave after undergoing a successful procedure to address a blood clot caused by a minor head accident. He has assigned BSP Deputy Governor Francisco Dakila, Jr. as officer-in-charge of the BSP. Additionally, BSP Governor Diokno was expected to be discharged from the hospital in four to five days and was anticipated to return to work subsequently.

Exports rise in November; Trade deficit narrows to US$1.7bn. Exports rose by 3% in November (vs. -1.2% YoY in October), as electronic exports rose 5%. Imports fell 19% YoY to US$7.5bn (vs. -18.8% YoY in October), extending its 19-month decline. Electronic products were flat YoY; Imports of non-electronics continued to drop. The trade deficit was 53% YoY lower at US$1.73bn, bringing 11M20 deficit to US$19.66bn, down 48% YoY.

Fitch keeps BBB rating for Philippines. Fitch Ratings affirmed the 'BBB' rating and Stable Outlook for the Philippines. Fitch forecasts GDP to fall by -8.5% in 2020 and to recover by +6.9% in 2021 and +8% in 2022. Meanwhile, the S&P affirmed its BBB+ rating in May 2020; Moody’s kept its Baa2 rating outlook last July.

 

Mobility deteriorates into January. The data has deteriorated over the succeeding 1.5 weeks (latest data as of 22 Jan). We are back to early 4Q20 levels of movement. The baseline data for Google is the 5-week period from 3 Jan to 6 Feb 2020.

Metro Manila mayors recommend extension of GCQ status. The mayors of the cities in Metro Manila recommended the extension of General Community Quarantine (GCQ) until the end of February. The Interagency Task Force (IATF) has yet to make an official announcement regarding Metro Manila’s quarantine status in February.

Supreme Court issues TRO on higher POGO taxes. The Supreme Court (SC) issued a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) on the collection of franchise taxes from POGOs. The Supreme Court Justices voted 13-1. The Bayanihan II Act, which became law in September 2020, had a provision which imposed franchise taxes on POGOs equivalent to 5% of bets/turnover/drop. In their petition, 14 foreign-based POGOs argued that the provision on higher POGO taxes is illegal because it imposes higher taxes even if Bayanihan II does not create or refer to the imposition of any new tax. Also, the imposition of taxes on offshore income is not constitutional. Bayanihan II unfairly uses bets as the “tax base” even though that amount includes money that will eventually be paid out as winnings to gamblers. The POGOs vacated c. 250,000sqm of Metro Manila office space in 2020 and we estimate they continue to occupy about 1.05m sqm (9% of office stock) in Metro Manila. The POGOs occupy 15% of FLI’s office portfolio and 10% of MEG’s office space. Land bank in the Manila Bay area, the preferred location for POGOs, accounts for c. 45% of SM Prime’s NAV.

Congress prioritizes economic charter change. House Speaker Lord Allan Velasco said that the Committee on Constitutional Amendments will open deliberations on the proposed Resolution of Both Houses (RBH) No. 2, which seeks to change certain economic provisions of the Philippine Constitution. The RBH No. 2 seeks to insert the phrase “unless otherwise provided by law” to certain sections:

-National patrimony and economy

-Education, science, technology, arts, culture, and sports

-General provisions on media and advertising

This could eventually pave the way for greater foreign ownership in certain industries, including utilities, media, and real estate. Changes to the constitution would require a national referendum.

DOF urges Congress to pass CREATE ahead of tax season. The Department of Finance has called on Congress to immediately pass the CREATE bill before month-end. This will provide taxpayers ample time to comply with adjustments to their returns due to the lowering of income taxes effective July 2020 before the tax filing season ends in April 2021. A bicameral conference committee is expected to harmonize the conflicting provisions of the Senate’s and House of Representatives’ versions. The congress resumes session on Jan. 18. Under the Senate version, domestic corporations with total assets (ex-land) <P100mn and a net taxable income of <P5mnand below will enjoy an outright 10% reduction in their income tax to 20% from 30%; The rest will have an immediate tax rate cut of 5ppts. Under the House version, CREATE proposes a uniform reduction of 5ppts. Meanwhile, foregone revenues from CREATE are expected to reach P133.2bn in 2021 and P117.6bn in 2022 if implemented retroactively to July 2020. Additionally, the Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer (FIST) is now just awaiting the signature of President Duterte after being ratified by Congress last month.

 

Mobile number portability to take effect end-Sept. Mobile number portability (MNP) will be implemented by Sept 30, according to the National Telecommunications Commission. The statement was made at a Senate hearing by NTC Deputy Commission Edgar Cabarios. The law mandating MNP was passed two years ago. This will allow mobile subscribers to keep their numbers even when they switch carriers. We expect the impact to be impact to be felt principally on the postpaid subscriber base. Postpaid subscribers account for 3% of Globe’s (GLO PM) mobile subscriber base and 28% of mobile revenue. For PLDT (TEL PM), the proportions are 3% and 20%. Mobile accounts for 71% of Globe’s revenue and 57% of PLDT’s

DOE sets scope of ban on new coal plants. The Department of Energy (DoE) excluded existing and operational coal-fired power generation facilities from its moratorium. Last November, the DoE declared a moratorium on new coal-fired power plants. Also not covered are: Committed coal power projects and existing power plant complexes that already have firm expansion plans and existing land site provisions. It also exempts projects with substantial accomplishments, particularly those that have signed lease agreement for the projects, and approved permits or resolutions from local government units and the Regional Development Council where the facilities would be locate.

More on vehicle safeguard duty. Low-to mid-end cars most affected. The DTI is imposing a safeguard duty of P75k and P110k on imported CBUs of light passenger cars (PCs) and light commercial vehicles (CVs), respectively. The safeguard duty will affect mostly low-to mid-end vehicles from most countries (e.g. Toyota WIGO, Toyota Hilux, Nissan Navara). But it will not cover the more expensive (“luxury”) vehicles (e.g. Toyota Land Cruiser, BMW), as well as CVs from China (e.g. MG RX5) and PCs/CVs from India (e.g. Hyundai Accent). It is uncertain at this stage if vehicles that are at or close to the “luxury” threshold of US$25k FOB price for PCs and US$28k FOB price for CVs. (e.g. Toyota Fortuner) will be covered.  

Banks’ NPL ratio hits 3.81% in November.  Gross non-performing loan (NPL) ratio accelerated to 3.81% in November from 3.72% in Oct 2020 and 2.19% in Nov 2019. This is the highest NPL ratio since January 2013 (3.48%). The BSP expects NPL ratio to reach 4.6% by end-2020. Meanwhile, gross NPL reached P405bn, up 2.43% MoM and 69% YoY.

Loan growth slows to 0.3% in November; Domestic liquidity growth eases to 10.5%. Based on preliminary data, outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, slowed to 0.3% in November from 1.8% (revised from 1.9%) in October. On a month-on-month seasonally-adjusted basis, loans contracted by 0.3%. Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-4.2%), trade (-6.0%) contracted. Meanwhile, consumer loans (credit cards and auto) expanded at a slower pace of 7.1% from 7.9% in October. Additionally, auto loans contracted by -3.3% (from -1.3% in October). Meanwhile, money supply grew by 10.5% in November, slower than the 11.6% growth (revised) in October.
