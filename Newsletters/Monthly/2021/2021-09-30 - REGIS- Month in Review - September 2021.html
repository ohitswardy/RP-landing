REGIS REPORT

30 September 2021
 

The Philippines gained +1.4% in September, building on its 9.3% gain in August.  The market traded around the at 6,900-6,950 level and attempted to break the 7,000 level a few times but failed due to local-led selling.  The sideways action occurred even as the the sought-after reopening continued to remain elusive on the ground, while global markets grappled with troubles at China Evergrande and concerns over Fed tightening.

Vaccination rates have taken somewhat of a disappointing turn, with September so far clocking in at around 395k jabs per day. This is behind the August daily average of 427k daily. With daily doses reaching as high as over 700k in early August, the deceleration is disconcerting.  Vaccine supply is however not an issue, with the country now having 26m COVID-19 vaccine doses in inventory. This is equivalent to over two months of vaccinations at the most recent pace. 

Hopefully, the government’s decision to allow vaccination to the general public as well as adolescents by October – vs limiting them for specific categories of individuals currently -- will help bolster vaccination rates.  Around 52% of the Metro Manila population is now fully vaccinated. The nation-wide number stands at 19%. The government aims to fully vaccinate 58m Filipinos by end-2021

New Covid-19 infections continue be on the downtrend, with the latest 7-day moving average settling at ~16.5k daily new cases vs recent peak of 21k.

Starting September 16, the National Capital Region was placed on Alert Level 4 under a new “granular lockdown” system with five “Alert Levels”.   Level 4 allows limited indoor restaurant dining and operation of personal care services.  Lower Alert Levels allow gradual opening of retail, restaurants, travel, and schools. Trade Secretary Ramon Lopez said about P30bn worth of wages could be restored per week if Metro Manila’s restrictions are eased to Alert Level 3

Meanwhile, the government is gearing to allow schools to restart face-to-face learning for the first time since March 2020.  The absence of face-to-face schooling has been cited as a reason for the rise in unemployment across the country. Philippines is one of only two countries in the world that have not reopened schools.

Election fever has hit, ahead of the May polls next year. The latest poll shows Presidential daughter Sara Duterte still in the lead among preferred Presidential candidates. However, her lead in the poll narrowed significantly with an 8ppt drop to 20% in the survey. Coming in second was was Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr who polled 15%. Statistically tied with Marcos were Manila Mayor Isko Moreno (13%) and boxer Manny Pacquiao (12%). Moreno and Pacquiao have declared their intention to run for the Presidency.  Including Senator Ping Lacson,  a total of three candidates have so far declared their intention to run in next year’s May elections.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners turned sellers for US$47.7m in September as a stricter lockdown and record COVID cases prompted investors to take profit after August’s 9% rally. September included the IPO of Robinsons Land REIT which registered US$74.4m in inflows, a US$100m Monde Nissin overnight placement which clocked in just US$3.7m in outflows, and FTSE rebalancing where Converge (+29% MoM) was added while AC Energy (+17.6%) saw a significant upweight. If we exclude these, foreign selling would have been much higher at US$102m.

Foreign participation remained steady at 38% (from 37% last month).

Biggest outflows were seen in consumer names. Monde Nissin which has gained 39% since listing in May 31 saw continued foreign profit taking. The stock has seen  brewing speculation of possible index inclusions in the coming months. Meanwhile, foreigners turned sellers in Universal Robina after the stock jumped 19% in August. The stock has seen very little news flow after announcing the sale of its Australia-NZ business last July.

Foreigners were seen accumulating Ayala Corp. The stock has been relatively flat since June while its subsidiaries Globe and AC Energy have gained 62% and 37% respectively.

Meanwhile, AC Energy continues to see foreign accumulation as a renewable energy play. The stock also saw a sizable upweight in FTSE rebalancing, which added to the inflows as well.

Semirara saw late foreign buying this month, pushing the stock up 27% on optimism that higher coal prices will boost earnings. Newcastle coal continues  to surge – already at US$210/MT. Semirara’s coal business accounts for roughly 80% of its profits.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

Converge (+29.0% MoM) was the top index gainer in September.  The stock surged as investors played its FTSE Index inclusion during the month. Gains were extended further in the latter half of the month, on speculation that the stock would also make it in on the next MSCI rebalancing (in November).   

The company likewise indicated a very strong a very strong 3Q, with subs likely to hit 1.7m subs by year-end from 1.4m as of end-June. The company also provided  guidance of 65% YoY growth in revenues and P6.5-7bn in profits this year (1H profit at P2.25bn).

While Converge was the top index gainer, PLDT (+14.7% MoM) was the top index mover – accounting for 35% of the market’s overall gain in September. The stock played catch up to Globe which advanced a massive 46% in August. Helping to boost buying interest was news that its 38.5%-owned fintech holding company Voyager was granted a digital banking license by the central bank. It will be one of only seven entities receiving a digital bank license in this round.  Voyager’s e-wallet has over 40m registered users. 

Globe (+9.7% MoM) meanwhile extended its gains. GCash, the e-wallet operator partly owned by Globe released details on the inroads it is making on financial services. Funds in its savings product (GSave) have grown to P9bn (US$180) from P5bn (US$100m) in six months; its micro-finance operation (GCredit) now disburses P1bn (US$20m) in loans a month (cumulative P15bn). Its platform now also accounts for a third of all insurance policies issued in the country. The fintech firm also revealed that it plans to go into the Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) service later this year.

The power companies also made a move.  First Gen (+20.0% MoM) rose sharply as KKR-controlled Philippines Clean Energy Holdings submitted a tender offer for between 3.0% to 5.7% of the shares of First Gen at P33.0/share. The offer price represents a 17% premium over last close as of Aug 26 and over the one month VWAP. Philippines Clean Energy Holdings is controlled by KKR unit Valorous Asia Holdings which already holds 12.59% of First Gen.

AC Energy (+17.6% MoM) also surged, seeing a sizable upweight in September’s FTSE rebal.

Aboitiz Power (+10.7% MoM) climbed significantly, building on its 20.7% gain in August.  Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+12.9% MoM) is selling a 25% stake in Aboitiz Power to Japanese power firm JERA for US$1.463m. 

Semirara (+23.8% MoM) was also up sharply as Newcastle thermal coal prices continued to surge, presenting significant upside to SCC’s earnings.

 

 

Conglos were mixed.  LT Group (+18.1 MoM) led, after many weeks of large selling activity. 

San Miguel (+8.2% MoM) gained, as did Ayala Corp (+3.1% MoM). Ayala Corp is issuing US$400m of new perpetuals, whose proceeds will be used to buy back previously issued perps. 

On the other hand, GT Capital (-5.6% MoM) and MPI  (-5.2% MoM) fell, as did large cap SM Investments (-1.9% MoM).

Meanwhile, Universal Robina (+9.9% MoM) was the top index decliner, correcting from its 19.0% gain in the previous month. However, Puregold (+6.6% MoM) , Jollibee (+1.6% MoM), and Robinsons Retail (+1.6% MoM) gained in September. 

 

Banks and property also underperformed, with six of the 10 index decliners in September belonging to the two sectors. Security Bank (-6.4% MoM) fell, followed  by Metrobank (-3.3% MoM), and BPI (-2.1% MoM). BDO (+0.9% MoM) however managed to gain. 

SM Prime (-3.7% MoM), Robinsons Land (-3.0% MoM), and Ayala Land (-0.7% MoM) also dropped.

Cebu Air (-11.4% MoM) declined even as competitor Philippine Airlines (PAL) filed a Chapter 11 bankruptcy petition in the Southern District of New York. 

 

 

 

 

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation jumps to 4.9% in August on higher food inflation. The country’s inflation rate accelerated to 4.9% in August from 4.0% the previous month. This is the fastest inflation recorded since January 2019 and higher than consensus forecast (+4.4%). This print fell at the high end of the BSP’s monthly forecast range of 4.1%-4.9% due to higher price increases of food (+6.5%), specifically of meat (+16%), fish (+12%), and vegetables (+16%) on weather-related supply disruptions. For non-food items, inflation of fuel (+8%) and operation of transport equipment (+14%) also accelerated due to higher power generation/transmission prices. Meanwhile, core inflation (excluding selected food and energy items) picked up to 3.3% in August from 2.9% in July. The YT-August average inflation rate at 4.4%, above the BSP’s target of 4.1% for FY21E. The latest CPI outturn is consistent with the BSP’s assessment that inflation could settle close to the high end of the target range (2-4%) in the near term before decelerating back to within the target by year end. In 2022-2023, inflation is expected to decline towards the midpoint of the target.

BSP holds key rate to boost recovery. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) left the key rate at 2%, in line with street expectations. By keeping a steady hand on the BSP’s policy levers will allow the momentum of economic recovery to gain more traction by helping boost domestic demand and market confidence.

Remittances jump to US$2.85bn in July. Cash remittances rose 2.5% YoY, 8.2% MoM to US$2.85bn in July. In PHP terms, remittances rose 3.5% YoY. This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate at +7.1%. The July remittance is close to the peak remittance of US$2.9bn in December 2019. Sea-based remittances continue to rebound and was very near pre-pandemic levels. Land-based remittances rose 1.6% YoY, 8.0% MoM; Growth appears to have stabilized. For YT-July remittances are at US$17.8bn, up 5.8% YoY (+1.8% YoY in PHP terms) and is on track to reach the BSP year-end target of US$31bn (+4% YoY).

 

GIR rises to US$108bn in end-August. Preliminary gross international reserves (GIR) stood at US$108.05bn in end-August, US$895mn (+0.8% MoM) higher than in end-July (US$107bn). This represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 12.3 months’ worth of imports. The government cited additional allocation of special drawing rights to the Philippines given the IMF’s efforts to increase global liquidity.

Trade deficit balloons to US$3.3bn in July. Preliminary trade data showed the trade deficit at US$3.291bn in July. This is slightly smaller than the revised June 2021 trade deficit of US$3.397bn (previously US$2.8bn deficit). Imports continue to improve (+24% YoY), with higher imports of fuel, consumer goods, and capital goods. Meanwhile, exports rose 13% YoY. The YT-July trade deficit now at US$21.3bn, wider than the US$13.5bn deficit in the same period last year.

Budget deficit expands to P121bn in August. The National Government’s budget deficit rose threefold to P120.9bn in August, as spending accelerated (+34% YoY). Revenues in August just slightly below (-1%) pre-pandemic monthly average; Tax revenues higher by 2% vs. pre-pandemic levels. Primary spending rose 37% YoY, 12% MoM due to release of cash aid in Metro Manila during ECQ (last year’s cash aid was mostly released in September, a month later than implementation of ECQ in August). The YT-August deficit at P958bn, up 29% YoY.

Infrastructure spending rises 39% YoY in July. The national government spent P72.8bn on infrastructure in July, up 39% YoY but down 23% MoM due to DPWH projects, higher spending for defense/PNP projects. This brings YT-July infra spending (excluding subsidies and equity to GOCCs and transfers to LGUs) up 42.6% YoY to P499.4bn. Total infrastructure spending in YT-July (i.e. including GOCC subsidies and LGU transfers) rose 35.6% YoY to P625.2bn. This accounts for 61% of the government’s year-end target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY), equivalent to 5.1% of GDP. For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn (+25% YoY), equivalent to 5.8% of GDP.

 

 

Labor force participation drops in July. Preliminary data from the Philippine Statistics Office showed the country’s total labor force dropped by 4mn MoM in July. The labor force participation rate dropped to 59.8% in July, lowest since October 2020 (58.7%) and below pre-pandemic level of 61.7%. The number of employed dropped by 3.4mn MoM, largely coming from agri and services sectors. Those who are looking for work (i.e. unemployed) also fell by 690k MoM, bringing the unemployment rate lower at 6.9%.

Bank lending drops 0.7% in July. Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, declined by 0.7% YoY in July. This is a smaller drop than in previous months; Loans up 0.2% MoM. Loans for production activities expanded by 0.8% YoY, reversing contractions since December 2020. Consumer loans dropped 8.2% YoY with auto loans contracting at a faster rate of 15.4% than in June. Domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 5.9% YoY in July, slower than the 6.5% (revised) growth recorded in June.

Evergrande debt crisis unlikely to affect Philippine financial system. The Philippines is insulated from shocks that may come from the possible collapse of Evergrande according to Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas Governor Benjamin Diokno. The Philippine banks are largely domestic-oriented with cross border exposures or claims from counterparties in other countries at 9.4% of total banking system assets. In terms of exposures to China, claims from counterparties based in China and its Special Administrative Regions is minimal at 0.86% of total banking system assets. 

Gov’t raises US$866mn from maiden retail dollar bond offer. The Philippine government raised an initial US$866.2mn from its maiden offering of retail dollar bonds (RDBs). The Bureau of the Treasury (BTr) awarded US$551.8mn worth of five-year RDBs and another US$314.4mn via the 10-year dollar-denominated notes. The five-year bonds, which mature in October 2026, had a coupon rate of 1.375% (vs. 5-year ROP due 2026 indicative offer of 1.086%). The 10-year bonds due in October 2031 fetched a 2.25% coupon rate (vs. 10-year ROP due 2031 indicative offer of 1.82%). The government aims to raise P3tn this year from local and foreign sources to plug its budget deficit seen to hit 9.3% of overall economic output.

Gov’t lifts moratorium on new casinos. Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp chair Andrea Domingo said the agency was ready to execute the President’s new directive to lift the moratorium on new casino applications to help raise government revenues. This comes three years after Duterte imposed the moratorium on new licenses. Projects that were seeking a casino licenses before the moratorium include the LRWC-Galaxy project in Boracay and Landing International’s proposed Entertainment City project. Despite the lifting, Domingo said no new casinos would be allowed in Pagcor Entertainment City in Paranaque since there was no more space for new locators.

PH lifts travel ban on 10 countries. The IATF lifted the travel ban imposed on 10 countries beginning today (September 6). Travel bans lifted for international travelers coming from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, UAE, Oman, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. The travel bans have been in place in early May. IATF also approved to shorten facility quarantine to 7 days (from 10 days previously) for arrivals from “green list” countries. IATF also introduced a “yellow list” of countries; quarantine requirement of 14 days (10 in a quarantine facility + 4 at home).

Philippines to begin vaccinating minors in October. President Duterte approved the vaccination of the general population starting October.  Priority will still be given to medical frontliners, senior citizens, people with comorbidities, , rontliners in essential sectors, and indigent population. Vaccination of children aged 12 to 17 years will also begin in October. The population above 15yrs old and above is 77mn (out of a total population of 109mn); Population above 10yrs old is 87m. The Philippines is slated to receive a total of 175mn doses from Feb 2021 to Jan 2022, including 70mn that already arrived. The Philippines has received a total of 70mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 44mn doses (as of September 26).  The government aims to fully vaccinate 58mn Filipinos by end-2021.

 

 

Government pushing for reforms on real property taxes. The Department of Finance seeks to standardize the methodology in computing for real property valuation (for the purpose of computing real property taxes). This is the third package of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Program. The House of Representatives already passed its version of the bill but the Senate has yet to act on this proposal. The assessed values used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. For reference, pre-COVID real property taxes accounted for c. 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover.  

Duterte approves pilot testing of face-to-face classes in low-risk areas. President Duterte has approved the pilot testing of limited face-to-face classes in areas with low COVID-19 cases. The Depart of Education Secretary Leonor Briones said a maximum of 100 public schools which have passed its “readiness assessment” will participate in the pilot test. Around 20 private schools are also expected to join subject to DepEd and DOH validation. The DepEd added that it will gradually increase face-to-face classes if the pilot is safe and effective. Classes in the basic education level opened on September under an online learning setup. The United Nations Children’s Fund Philippines has called for the phased reopening of schools, adding that Philippines and Venezuela are the only remaining countries that have not reopened schools. 

Senate ratifies Bicam committee report on retail trade. The Senate has ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee report, which seeks to reduce the minimum paid-up capital requirement for foreign retailers. The minimum paid-up capital requirement for foreign retailers was set at P25mn (cUSD500k) with a per store requirement of P10mn. The existing law requires minimum paid-up capital of P125mn (cUSD2.5mn). The amendments are unlikely to materially impact operations for larger retail groups, but could allow small foreign retail shops (e.g. independent specialty retailers) to operate without a local partner.

President Duterte signs POGO tax law. President Duterte signed RA 11590 into law. This will require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue. PAGCOR may also impose a regulatory fee on POGOs, which shall not exceed 2% of GGR. POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P145bn in tax revenue over five years. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new law will lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. Thus, the new law could slow down POGO closures.

Boxer Manny Pacquiao to run as President in 2022. Pacquiao accepted the nomination of his political allies during the national assembly of the faction he leads in the ruling PDP-Laban party. PDP-Laban has been the ruling party since 2016 under the administration of President Rodrigo Duterte. Pacquiao’s faction in the party has not expressed support for Duterte’s vice-presidential bid. In July, Pacquiao was voted out as PDP-Laban leader, weeks after challenging Duterte over his position on China and record on fighting corruption, but his ouster was rejected by his faction. Pacquiao trails the front-runners in opinion polls that have been topped consistently by Duterte's daughter, Sara Duterte-Carpio.

Manila Mayor “Isko Moreno” to run for President. Manila Mayor Francisco “Isko Moreno” Domagoso will run for president in the 2022 elections. In a June Pulse Asia survey, Mayor Domagoso trailed the front-runner, Sara Duterte-Carpio, by a wide margin (14 vs 28). Mayor Domagoso’s running mate for vice president is Dr. Willie Ong. Dr. Ong ran for the Senate in 2019 but landed on the 18th spot (out of only 12 incoming senators).

Sara Duterte remains top presidential candidate in polls. In the September 2021 Pulse Asia survey, Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte (daughter of President Rodrigo Duterte) remains the number 1 choice to be President in the May 2022 elections. However, she dropped from 28 points in June to 20 points in September. Her numbers fell in every category of geography and social class. Sara Duterte said she would not run for national office. The following are statistically tied at second place: Former Senator “Bongbong” Marcos with 15, Manila Mayor Francisco “Isko Moreno” Domagoso with 13 and Senator Emmanuel “Manny” Pacquiao with 12.
