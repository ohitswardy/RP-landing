REGIS REPORT

01 September 2021
 

The Philippines climbed 9.3% in August, making up for its 9.2% decline in the previous month.  The steep drop in August prompted renewed interest in the market, even as the covid situation on the ground turned for the worse.

Gains were broad based, with 26 of the 30 index members ending the months with gains.  Index heavy weight SM Investments alone chipped in close to a fifth of the PSEI’s 585-index point gain.  ICTSI contributed another 12% to the rise.

The biggest moves however were delivered by the telcos, with Globe up a blistering 48% and PLDT up 24% for the month as investors bought into the excitement surrounding the telcos’ fintech units.

The 2Q21 earnings season concluded in August and results were decidedly mixed.  Against consensus numbers, 37% of the PSEi beat expectations but a similar 37% fell short.  Coming off 2Q20 lows, collective earnings grew 271% YoY –- led by banks, consumer, and property.  However, profitability actually deteriorated 9% QoQ.  Earnings grew 69% YoY for 1H21. 

The government reported that 2Q GDP growth was 11.8%, after a 17% plunge in 2Q last year at the height of the lockdowns. Seasonally adjusted GDP for 2Q21 actually contracted 1.3% QoQ, however. With economic output in 2Q worse than 1Q, concerns turned to 3Q economic activity given the hard lockdown in place in August and September. 

The PSE announced the changes to the composition of the PSEi effective mid-August. AC Energy was added to the index and surprisingly, Converge as well. The inclusion of the latter came as a surprise due to rules requiring a stock to be listed for at least a year before it qualified for inclusion. CNVRG was only listed 4Q20.  PSE apparently changed the inclusion rule, requiring listing of just six months instead of one year. DMCI and Emperador were taken off the PSEi, meanwhile. 

The government re-implemented another round of its strictest form of lockdown (ECQ or enhanced community quarantine) beginning August 6, as threats of the Delta variant loomed.  The capital (as well as  other areas) has since been moved to a lower notch to MECQ (modified enhanced community quarantine) from 20 August to 7 September but dine-in, personal care services and religious activities are still not allowed and curfew hours remain. 

New Covid-19 infections continued to hit new highs with the new daily infection rates hitting above 22,000 for the first time late August.  Hospitals around the country are getting filled, with occupancy already matching the previous peak in April. As of August 29, some 18% of the population has received at least one dose; 13% are fully vaccinated. For the capital, an estimated 32% of the ~14m population has now been fully vaccinated, while 54% have had at least one dose (As of Aug 22).

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners turned buyers for US$32m in August – the first month of inflows since October 2019. Foreign inflow was seen consistently towards the last two weeks of the month despite tighter lockdowns and higher COVID cases.

August included the Filinvest REIT IPO which registered US$12.8m of inflows. Excluding the Filinvest REIT, foreigners would be still be net buyers for US$19.

Foreign participation remained light at 37%.

Foreigners flocked into Globe Telecom, pushing the stock up close to 50% in the span of a month amid the euphoria surrounding its fintech unit Mynt (parent of GCash). E-payment transactions over the past year (partly due to the pandemic) even as there is also growing speculation of a potential Mynt IPO.

Foreigners were two way in banks and conglomerates.  

Foreigners turned buyers in BDO while continued to be net sellers of BPI. Meanwhile, JGS (likely passive) and SM were top foreign bought while LTG and GTCAP saw biggest outflows.

 

 

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

The telcos had a blistering month, with Globe (+46.2% MoM) and PLDT (+19.6% MoM) gaining massively in August. Excitement on the telco incumbent’s fintech units fueled gains.  Globe’s e-wallet GCash (~40% owned) is now seeing P300bn worth of transactions in a month on its e-wallet operation. This means it is likely to comfortably exceed P3trn in transactions this year.

Meanwhile, Converge’s  (+36.0% MoM) unexpected inclusion in the PSEi during the latest rebal propelled the stock in during the month.

For the telcos, 2Q profits came in line with consensus estimates. Demand for broadband services continued.  Conversely, mobile revenue has been weak. 

ICTSI (+19.7% MoM) was not far behind the telcos. Results in 2Q overtook consensus expectations as volumes and yields rose.  Indications of healthy “double-digits” volume growth in 3Q also helped boost sentiment. 

 

In the meantime, Cebu Air (-0.3% MoM) was slightly down. Its net loss in 2Q21 came in wider-than-expected as passenger volume recovery fell short.  

Banks did well in August.  Large cap BDO (+7.7% MoM) led the sector, gaining markedly during the month, and reporting 1H21 provision-led earnings outperformance.  Provision expense was down 70% YoY.  

Metrobank (+5.1% MoM) meanwhile, kicked up provision expense by 80% QoQ despite NPL ratio improving to 2.3% from 2.4% in 1Q21. 

BPI (+3.35% MoM) likewise rose as did Security Bank (+2.9% MoM). Security Bank’s provision expense was up sharply QoQ in 2Q.  NPL ratio jumped 50bps QoQ to 3.93%.

 

 

Macro proxies staged a recovery post large declines in July. Leading the charge in August were index heavy weights SM Investments (+10.8% MoM), Aboitiz Equity (+14.5% MoM) and JG Summit(+15.9% MoM). 

Lower provisions at BDO led to SM’s profit outperformance in 2Q.  Reported profit for 1H21 reached P20.1bn, almost 3x the previous year’s level. SM Retail’s 2Q revenue was down slightly QoQ 1Q but well above the depressed levels a year ago. SM Prime’s (+8.1% MoM) mall revenues in the Phils jumped 55% YoY despite continued lockdowns. 2Q21 net profit jumped 145% YoY. 

Meanwhile, Ayala Land’s (+3.2% MoM) 2Q21 profit jumped but was dragged by lower residential margins and soft mall operations.  Parent Ayala Corp’s (+8.6% MoM) reported 2Q earnings were double of last year’s depressed levels. However, earnings were surprisingly down QoQ wtih drops in contributions from, Globe, power and AC Industrials. 

Power unit AC Energy (+19.6% MoM) reported that EBITDA dipped 3% to P5.8bn due to higher cost of purchases from WESM in 2Q. One unit of SLTEC was down the month of May.

AC Energy surged as it was added to the PSEi in the August rebal. 

Meanwhile, DMCI (+5.2% MoM) was taken off the PSEi index during the same PSEi rebal. DMCI reported  exceptionally strong numbers in 2Q (core profit up more than threefold) thanks to its coal mining operations of Semirara(+3.9% MoM).  

Emperador (+18.7% MoM) was also taken off the PSEi index. However, the stock surged following approval from its board of its SGX listing.

 

EMP’s 2Q21 jumped to P3.0bn (+44%QoQ, +61%YoY), as GPM of the brandy business expanded by 17pptQoQ and international revenues increased 30%QoQ. Meanwhile, parent Alliance Global (+1.4% MoM) gained slightly.  Along with the jump in EMP’s profits, a one-time gain at gaming unit Travellers helped boost 1H21 profits.  Meanwhile,  property unit Megaworld (+1.8% MoM) came in behind forecasts for 2Q on back of a drop in office revenues. Meanwhile, provisions at PNB (unch) led to a 2Q loss for LT Group (-12.5% MoM) even as tobacco’s contribution came broadly in-line.

GT Capital (-0.6% MoM) reported a QoQ drop in profits.  Earnings were pulled down sequentially by higher provisions at MBT and lower sales volumes and margins at Toyota. Sales volumes and margins were adversely affected by the imposition of the safeguard duty.

Metro Pacific (-10.0% MoM) rebounded from its 10% decline in the previous month.  2Q21 core profit jumped 84% YoY as volumes recovered from last year’s hard lockdown. Sequentially, however, operations for its rail and tollroad units faced challenges after tighter lockdowns were re-imposed in the country’s capital.

Cosco’s (+2.0% MoM) liquor distribution, whose volumess jumped 74% in 2Q, is trending well ahead of FY21 estimates and already back to pre-pandemic levels.

Retai unit Puregold’s (+3.6% MoM ) sales meanwhile showed continued pressure for key customers of the core Puregold brand (lower-income consumers) while S&R’s SSSG was healthy despite the high base in the previous year.  The core Puregold brand saw a 12% decline in sales – which is largely expected given the high base from pantry stocking last year.

 

For the most part, the consumer sector saw a sharp YoY bounce in top line and earnings due to an extremely low base in the previous year.

Restaurants and non-essential retail saw a turnaround in 2Q21 vs the hard lockdown (EQC) in 2Q20. Restaurants Jollibee (+6.2% MoM) and Shakey’s (+2.3% MoM) are still tracking around 25-50% below pre-pandemic sales given the lockdowns in Mar-April in the Phils, however.  For Max’s (+10.5% MoM) Regis estimates sales are even lower (30-35%) relative to pre-covid levels given renewed dine-in restrictions.

Spending continued to be focused on staples (instant noodles, canned food) and less so on discretionary items.

Monde (+13.9% MoM) continued to see a resilient topline in 2Q, despite coming off a high base from pantry stocking last year. 

Meanwhile, a one off gain lifted Universal Robina’s (+19.0% MoM) 1H earnings but EBIT was in line with expectations.  Revenues  and operating profit  were broadly in line.

The company announced that it was selling its Australia-New Zealand business.  URC expects cash proceeds of US$476m from the sale of its operations (60%) in Uni Snack. According to its disclosure, URC is selling an initial 9% at an Enterprise Value (EV) of US$1bn and the remaining 51% at US$1.45bn. URC initially acquired the businesses for close to ~US$1bn (EV) a few years ago.

 

San Miguel FB (-6.6% MoM) and Century Pacific (+2.0% MoM) meanwhile managed to deliver beats. Despite an exceptionally high base last year,  CNPF’s branded revenue growth was notably resilient while tight cost control led to EBITDA margin expansion. Meanwhile, although beer (-11%QoQ) and gin (-25%QoQ) volumes of SMFB declined, they were much higher than volumes during the original ECQ last year. High chicken prices also helped profitability.

Robinsons Retail (-5.0% MoM) was dragged by revenue shortfalls across its different segments in 1H.

Meanwhile, the rest of the property sector managed to rise despite challenges in 2Q. Filinvest Land  (+1.8% MoM) saw office occupancy drop to 70%, from 91% a year ago, mostly due to POGO closures. Meanwhile, despite generally looser restrictions compared to a year ago, Vista Land’s (+4.7% MoM) 2Q21 residential revenue fell 13% YoY. Robinsons Land’s (+1.8% MoM) 2Q profit surged but this was due to lot sales to JV.

 

 

 

KKR-controlled Philippines Clean Energy Holding, Inc  submitted a tender offer for between 3.0% to 5.7% of the shares of First Gen (-2.4% MoM) at P33.0/share. The offer price represents a 17% premium over last close as of Aug 26 and over the one month VWAP.  Philippines Clean Energy Holdings Ic is controlled by KKR unit Valorous Asia Holdings which already holds 12.59% of First Gen.

The company saw a 1H21 earnings uplift on the back of higher electricity sales and prices and lower interest expenses and tax rate. 

Aboitiz Power’s (+20.7% MoM) recent results show that underlying demand for power appears on the uptrend, as exhibited by the 11%YoY increase in energy sales and 20%YoY growth in distribution volumes in 2Q21.

In the meantime, underlying operations remained soft for most of Manila Water’s (+12.0% MoM) PH units, with 2Q21 revenues and EBITDA dropping YoY.

 

Cement demand was buoyant in 2Q21 but competition remained intense. For Cemex (+0.8% MoM) sales volumes improved 7% QoQ, aided by favorable weather and higher infra spending. 

Meanwhile, a slower pace of construction work and a drop in landport revenues led to a wider-than-anticipated loss for Megawide (+0.3% MoM) in 2Q. 

The stricter lockdown also weighed on Bloomberry (+4.2% MoM) -- its net loss widened sequentially to -P1.159bn in 2Q21 on reduced operating days due to stricter quarantine restrictions in April-May.

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Q21 GDP +11.8% YoY, but still soft QoQ; lowering FY21 GDP est to +4.5%. Philippine GDP in 2Q21 jumped +11.8% YoY. According to Bloomberg, consensus estimates had 2Q21 at +10.9%. That took 1H21 GDP growth to +3.7%. Before we break out the bubbly, don’t forget that we were operating off an extremely low base (was -17.0% in 2Q20). Compared to pre-pandemic levels, the 2Q21 print was still 7.3% lower than 2Q19. In contrast, 1Q21 was only 4.6% below 1Q19. In fact, according to the PSA, 2Q21 was -1.3% QoQ compared to 1Q21 on a seasonally adjusted basis. Given the harder lockdown in August (compared to April), it is probably safe to assume that the quarterly weakness could persist into 3Q21. We lower our FY GDP forecast to +4.5%, compared to Bloomberg consensus of about 5.0%.

Inflation eases to 4% in July. Inflation slowed to 4.0% in July from 4.1% the previous month. Due to decelerating transport inflation (fares dropped as capacity limits eased). However, food inflation accelerated, as prices of fish and vegetables surged. Utilities inflation also accelerated due to elevated global crude prices. The year-to-date inflation is averaging at 4.4%, above BSP’s target range of 2-4%.

BSP keeps policy rates unchanged. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) left its key policy rate at a record low for a sixth consecutive meeting. Overnight reverse repurchase facility was left unchanged at 2%, within street expectations. The BSP also retained the overnight deposit and lending rates at 1.5% and 2.5%, respectively. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that the BSP is ready to adjust policy settings as needed, adding that the risks to inflation appeared balanced. But the reimposition of quarantine measures could pose a risk to the ongoing economic recovery. The Monetary Board has three more policy meetings this year. The next one is set on Sept. 23.

Remittances jump to US$2.64bn in June. Remittances jumped to US$2.64bn in June, up 7% YoY, 11% MoM. Land-based remittances surged across various locations; notably for Europe, Americas, Asia, and the Middle East. Additionally, sea-based remittances also rebounded but amount still c. 8% below pre-Covid levels. The 3-month moving average growth rate accelerated further to +10.7% YoY in June.  In PHP terms, remittances rose +4% YoY in June. In 1H21, remittances rose 6.4% YoY to US$14.9bn. To reach the BSP’s target of +4% growth in remittances this year, remittances in succeeding months only need to average US$2.3bn.

Government cuts GDP growth target. The government slashed its economic growth target to 4-5% for this year from 6-7% previously. The government cited mobility restrictions in Metro Manila and other parts of the country continue to crimp economic activity. It nonetheless kept its 2022 GDP target of +7% to +9% for 2022.

Budget deficit narrows to P121bn in July.  The National Gov’t recorded a P121bn deficit in July, down YoY and MoM. Revenues rose with both BIR and Customs collections improving. Meanwhile, expenditures modestly rose YoY due to base effects from disbursements of the second tranche of cash aid and timing of subsidy releases for Philheath and National Housing Authority. Expenditures of personnel services and infra outlays rose >6% YoY in July. This brings YT-July deficit to P873bn, up 20% YoY.

 

Trade deficit balloons to US$2.8bn in June.  The country’s trade deficit jumped 98% YoY to US$2.8bn in June (vs. +141.4% YoY in May 2021). Exports rose +18% YoY in June (vs. 31% YoY in May) due to higher exports of electronics. Imports rose 34% YoY in June (vs. 55.6% YoY in May) due to higher imports of mineral fuels. In 1H21, trade deficit stood at US$15.4bn, up 39% YoY.

BOP surplus widens to US$642mn in July.  The country’s balance of payments reversed to a surplus of US$642mn in July from a deficit of US$312mn in June. This was due to proceeds from the government’s US$3bn global bond sale less deposit withdrawals of US$1.68bn, payment of debt obligations of US$465mn, and US$398mn net FX operations. This brings YT-July BOP deficit to US$1.3bn, a reversal from the US$4.117bn surplus a year ago due to a wider merchandise trade deficit.

GIR level rises to US$106.55bn in end-July.  The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by US$0.79bn (+0.7% MoM) to US$106.55bn as of end-July 2021. The government cited net inflows from issuance of ROP global bonds. This represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 12.1 months’ worth of imports.

More hot money flows out in July.  Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. “hot money”) reverted to a net outflow of US$340mn in July from a net inflow of US$335mn inflow in June, ending two straight months of net inflow. This was due to a 65% MoM decrease in gross inflows to US$730mn (of which 65% are PSE-listed securities) and a 40% MoM drop in gross outflows to US$1.1bn. The gov’t also cited rising Delta-related COVID infections, re-imposition of hard lockdowns, and vaccine supply constraints. This brings YT-July hot money net outflow to US$446mn, down from a large US$3.76bn net outflow in the same period last year.

Bank lending shrinks 2% in June; Domestic liquidity expands by 6.4% YoY.  Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks amounted to P9.09tn in June, down 2% YoY. This is a milder drop than the previous month (-4% YoY). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 6.4% YoY to P14.4tn in June, faster than the +4.7% growth rate in May.

 

Car sales rise 5% YoY in July. Data from CAMPI/TMA showed the industry sold 21,499 units in July, up 5% YoY (-5% MoM). In mid-July, the Tariff Commission rejected the imposition of safeguard duty on imported vehicles. The DTI ordered the removal of the duty on Aug 11. The re-imposition of lockdown measures in NCR could slow sales in August. This number also brings YT-July industry sales up 46% YoY to 154,266 units. Toyota sold 10,763 units in July, up 22% YoY but was down 4% MoM (Market share steady at 50%). YT-July sales up 68% YoY to 74,541 units, tracking ahead of FY21E (117k). 

DTI stops safeguard duty on imported cars, LCVs. Trade Secretary Ramon Lopez signed an order to stop the imposition of safeguard duty on imported CBU passenger cars and light commercial vehicles last August 6. The lifting of the provisional safeguard duty will take effect upon issuance by the Bureau of Customs of an order to do so. The process may take 1-2 weeks.

Gov’t submits 2022 budget to Congress; trims 2022 infrastructure budget to P1.18tn. The Department of Budget and Management submitted a 2022 budget of P5.024tn to Congress. This is 11.5% higher than the 2021 approved budget of P4.506tn equivalent to 22.8% of GDP. Under the proposed budget, P1.18tn will go to infrastructure, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP and is 15% YoY higher than the P1.019tn for 2021. However, infra budget is lower than the initial program of P1.29tn due to the implementation of the Mandanas ruling next year. The budget for public works is slightly down YoY while the transportation budget is 72% YoY higher due to ongoing rail transport projects (P110.9bn for NSCR railway and Metro Manila subway).

Metro Manila, 15 other areas under MECQ until September 7. Metro Manila will remain under modified enhanced community quarantine (MECQ) until September 7. Other areas under MECQ are: Apayao, Ilocos Norte, Bulacan, Bataan, Cavite, Lucena City, Rizal, Laguna, Aklan, Iloilo province, Iloilo City, Lapu-Lapu City, Cebu City, Mandaue City, and Cagayan de Oro City.  Metro Manila, Bataan and Laguna shall have added restrictions on dining, personal care services and religious activities.  Curfew hours in Metro Manila under the prevailing MECQ is from 8 pm to 4 am.
