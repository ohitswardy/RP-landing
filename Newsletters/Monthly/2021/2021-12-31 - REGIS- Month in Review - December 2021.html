REGIS REPORT

31 December 2021
 

The Philippines bid 2021 adieu with a subdued 1.1% decline in December as Omicron and associated lockdown fears dampened sentiment. The market likewise took its cue from global markets weighing Fed tightening and the highly transmissible variant.

With Omicron hitting the country's shores in December as well as traditional gatherings during the holiday season, case counts have started picking up. New COVID cases jumped to almost 3,000 by the end of December, well above the ~300 cases before Christmas. While well below the peaks in September, cases are expected to surge in the coming weeks.

As cases started to come back up, the government has placed the National Capital Region under tightened restrictions as it returns to Alert Level 3 (from Alert Level 2) from 3-15 January. Under the tighter lockdown level, establishments will operate at diminished (30% indoor and 50% outdoor) capacity.

Low case counts and loosened mobility restrictions prior to Christmas had led to Covid-era highs in terms of mobility (based on Google data). This lent optimism for 4Q21 economic growth and corporate earnings. However, Typhoon Odette struck central and southern portions of the country, dampening optimism. Regis estimates these affected regions account for over a fifth of GDP.

Nonetheless, vaccination continued to progress. Some 46% of the country is now fully vaccinated. Hospital occupancy has also stayed low. The government also lowered the time frame for booster shots to 3 months from vaccination, leaving more than 20m Filipinos eligible for the booster. The Pfizer Covid-19 vaccine for children aged 5 to 11 was also approved by the Philippine FDA.

The BSP, as expected, kept policy rates unchanged at record lows during its Monetary Board meeting. The BSP appears inclined to keep rates low notwithstanding the current inflation environment and rising rates in the developed world.

2021- A QUICK RECAP

For the year, the Philippines registered a performance of -0.24% to end 2021 at 7122.63.

The market had been down as much as 13.65% YTD sometime May as prospects of economic recovery were impinged by a two-week Enhanced Community Quarantine (the tightest form of lockdown) in April, followed by a Modified ECQ that lasted until Mid-May.

An improved COVID situation on the ground thereafter fueled reopening optimism, paring losses to -1.45% YTD as of early July. The market quickly unraveled however, with a steep 9.2% plunge for the month as Delta swept the globe. The government re-implemented another round of ECQ beginning August 6. The reopening theme played out however, leading to a quick recovery, bouncing +9.3% in August, and rising in subsequent months.

Starting September 16, the National Capital Region was placed on Alert Level 4 under a new “granular lockdown” system with five “Alert Levels”, allowing limited indoor restaurant dining and operation of personal care services. 

Metro Manila’s lockdown classification was eased to  Alert Level 3 in the last two weeks of October, allowing most business and services to operate at increased capacities.  Universities were allowed in-person schooling for the first time since March 2020 and “pilot tests” for primary/secondary started subsequently.   

Alert level 2 was announced early in November for the capital, increasing indoor capacity of establishments and allowing children outside their homes. Mobility restrictions continued to be eased, made possible by the continued decline in case counts.  The market rose to its YTD peak as of early November at 4.2% YTD (Nov 9 at 7441.67). Omicron however doused optimism, wiping out YTD gains entirely. 

The year’s index performers were the telcos.  Secular growth stock Converge rose a massive 114.1% in 2021.  Globe and PLDT also surged – 71.8% and 44.2% respectively -- as investors eagerly bought into their fintech businesses.  ICTSI, with its stellar profit performance, climbed 69.5% for the year.  Banks Metrobank, BDO, and BPI likewise outperformed – up 22.2%, 16.7%, and 16.0% respectively for the year. 

Other big gainers in 2022 were Emperador (+25.7% YTD), and RE play AC Energy (+17.0% YTD).  Aboitiz Power (+15.5% YTD), Union Bank (+73.0% YTD), and parent Aboitiz Equity (+19.2% YTD) also outperformed.

 
MORE ON FLOWS...
 

Foreigners were net buyers for US$1.724bn in December as multiple corporate transactions skewed net foreign flows. These transactions included the closing of the acquisition by JERA of a stake in Aboitiz Power which clocked in US$1.59bn inflows, Arran Investment’s acquisition of an additional stake in AC Energy which brought in US$185m, and SM Prime blocks which registered US$29m in inflows. Excluding these, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$80m.

For the year, headline net foreign outflows stood at US$4.5m. This amount however was influenced by corporate transactions. Excluding one off blocks --  JERA/AP acquisition, Arran/ACEN, multiple REIT IPOs, Converge and Monde Nissin IPOs (among others), and SM Prime blocks to name a few – funds flowing out were at a larger US$1.8bn.

Foreign participation in December was 45%.  

December Top Foreign Flows
Foreigners were seen selling consumer food groups Universal Robina, Jollibee, and Monde Nissin while buying banks BDO, BPI, and Metrobank. Monde Nissin was top foreign sold as the lack of near-term catalysts prompted investors to take some profit after being added to the MSCI last month. Meanwhile, foreigners showed some renewed interest in the banking space following the acquisition of Citibank’s Philippine retail business by Aboitiz-owned UnionBank.

Foreigners were two-way telcos as they were seen taking profit in Globe (which has gained for 7 consecutive months) while loading up on PLDT which has been a relative laggard.

2021 Top Foreign Flows

Foreigners flocked into AC Energy –the stock saw the biggest foreign inflows this year as it continues to expand at a rapid pace (locally, and regionally) and as it establishes itself as the Philippines’ only investible pure renewable-energy play.

Foreigners were also buyers of mining stocks Semirara (and parent DMCI) and Nickel Asia on the back of soaring global coal and nickel prices. Meanwhile, ICTSI managed to attract more foreign buying, pushing the stock to new all-time highs as the company had another banner year on the back of sustained yield improvements, volume growth, and higher EBITDA margins.

Biggest outflows were in property names Ayala Land and Megaworld. Meanwhile, Converge surged 114% despite being top foreign sold.  Consumer food groups Universal Robina and Monde Nissin were also top foreign sold. 

 

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
DECEMBER LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Within the banks space in December, the spotlight was on Union Bank (+16.2% MoM), which surged on its acquisition of Citi’s consumer business in the Philippines.

UBP’s purchase will include Citi’s credit card and personal loans, personal deposits, and wealth management businesses. It will also include real estate assets (including  the Citibank Square in Eastwood, 3 branches, 5 wealth centers, and 2 “branch lite” locations).

The total consideration is estimated at P55bn -- a P45.3bn premium to the P9.7bn “equity required” based on the risk weighting of the assets bought. Management puts the deal at 9.3x PE, lower than UBP’s 14x and thus suggesting a potentially highly accretive transaction.

The acquisition is transformative as it would propel UBP into one of the major players in the consumer lending and wealth management markets. Regis estimates the deal could take UBP to the fourth largest in credit card receivables (Citi is currently the #3 player in credit cards in the Phils) and the sixth largest in wealth management AUM.   

The bank’s Board also approved a P40bn rights issue to fund the acquisition. The rights issue will be fully underwritten by UBP’s major shareholders Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+10.9% MoM), Insular Life, and the Social Security System.

Other news in the sector was the hack of BDO (-2.4% MoM) depositors’ online accounts.  The breach was attributed to its “ten-year old web service” which is in the process of being decommissioned and replaced.  In a call hosted by Regis, management said the total amount involved was “not material”. The  bank has promised to reimburse all affected depositors though the bank is insured against cybercrimes, and will substantially recover the amount lost. Fines or sanctions by regulators, if any, have yet to be known at this juncture.

The overall sector fared well in December, with laggard interest in Metrobank (+11.6% MoM) lifting the stock.  BPI (+1.3% MoM) and Security Bank (+3.5% MoM) ended higher as well.

Meanwhile, property was more of a mixed bag, with SM Prime (-9.4% MoM) registering a decline in December while Megaworld (+0.3% MoM), Robinsons Land (+5.4% MoM), and Ayala Land (+6.4% MoM) all closing higher.

 

 

 

The acquisition theme dominated the consumer sector. Universal Robina (+3.8% MoM) announced its acquisition of leading Malaysian biscuit firm, Munchy’s, in a ~US$450m deal. Based on 2020 financial statements, Regis estimates the valuation for Munchy’s is 4x EV/sales and 17-18x EV/EBITDA – a premium to current multiples for URC, adjusted for the sale of the Oceania business (2x EV/sales, 10-11x EV/EBITDA). URC outlined several revenue and cost synergies they are targeting including  cross selling products into MY and around the ASEAN region and scale advantages on manufacturing and distribution. 

Elsewhere, Century Pacific Group announced two acquisitions by Century Pacific (+8.3% MoM) and Shakey’s Pizza (+16.2% MoM).

CNPF is acquiring leading sardine brand Ligo. The acquisition would solidify CNPF’s #2 position in the category with a total market share of close to 30%. The leader in the space (Mega sardines) accounts for around 30% of the market. Regis estimates that Ligo could add c3-5% to CNPF’s consolidated top line.  Meanwhile, Pizza is acquiring one of the largest food kiosk chains in the Philippines, Potato Corner. Potato Corner has a network of over 1,000 company-owned and franchised kiosks, of which c20% are overseas. Regis estimates Potato Corner could add as much as 20% to PIZZA’s systemwide sales.

Most consumer names were soft however, with large foreign outflows seen across different names – with lingering concerns from input cost inflation and interrupted economic recovery.

Among the top net sold stocks in December were URC and Jollibee (-7.9% MoM) for US$24 and US$22m, respectively.  Monde (-4.8% MoM) also saw outflows of US$14m as foreign LO’s used its addition in the FTSE rebal as a chance to trim.

Net outflows were also recorded for retailers Puregold (-4.3% MoM) and Robinsons Retail (-1.65% MoM) at around US$3-4m each.

 

 

 

 

 

Telcos were strong in December though PLDT (+7.9% MoM) outperformed, playing catch up to Globe (+1.2% MoM). The latter amassed a 63.65% gain YTD vs PLDT’s 35.2% gain for the year.  The two were among the top performing stocks in 2021, though Converge (-0.3%MoM) surged even higher, with its 114% gain in 2021.

The December moves within the Telco space were triggered by the Senate’s approval of the amendments to the Commonwealth-era Public Service Act. These amendments would remove the classification of telecoms, airlines and domestic shipping from the list of “public utilities”, thus enabling them to be 100% foreign owned. A previous version of the Senate bill contained provisions which would still restrict foreign state-owned entities to no more than 40% ownership of critical infrastructure. If present in the final law, this could limit the avenues for the full takeover of third player DITO by China Telecom.

Meralco (+2.5% MoM) led the utilities, with a decent gain in December.  Aboitiz Power (-0.8% MoM) delivered a milder gain.  The energy regulator issued a certificate of compliance covering the first unit of GN Power Dinginin (majority owned by AP), effectively allowing the commercial operations of the 668MW Unit. This comes more than eight years after the plant was first announced and more than two years after it initial target operation date. The plant is expected to contribute up to 20% of AP's earnings when fully operational. 

Meanwhile, AC Energy (-2.5% MoM) fell.

Parent Ayala Corp (-0.2% MoM) finalized its agreement to sell another 2.69bn more shares of AC Energy to Singapore sovereign wealth fund Government Investment Corp (GIC), completing the agreement to sell the shares signed last year. Following this deal and the upcoming share issuances relating to ACEN’s purchase of increased stakes in JV projects with its partner, Ayala Corp will own 62.5% of ACEN while GIC, through “Arran”, will own 16.9%.

Other macro proxies dropped as well.  SM Investments (-3.8% MoM) slid in December, as did MPI (-3.9% MoM), GT Capital (-5.3% MoM), and JG Summit (-6.3% MoM).   

Gaming play Bloomberry (-19.1% MoM) was also weak, dampened by Omicron fears and the arrest of Alvin Chau, the head of Macau’s biggest casino junket operator Suncity, which continued to be an overhang for the sector.

Suncity Holdings (HK listed) clarified that the VIP business are under a different entity owned by Mr. Chau on a personal capacity. Bloomberry’s Solaire casino confirmed that Sun City-operated rooms have been closed since November 30. At its peak, Regis estimates Suncity accounted for as much as 13% of total industry GGR. VIP, was about 50% of total industry GGR. The VIP segment was predominantly driven by junket operators (90% of VIP). 

Transport stocks were mixed. Omicron and lockdown fears hit Cebu Air (-8.8% MoM) in December. Nonetheless, the airline expects to operate 84% of its pre-pandemic domestic capacity in 2022.  The company expects a slower recovery on the international side, targeting restoring 39% of pre-pandemic seat capacity in 2022. 

ICTSI (+1.1% MoM) is allocating US$230m to expand its port operations in Mexico by over 20% to 1.7M TEUs annually. The port in Manzanilla is one of the largest in ICTSI’s portfolio, accounting for 11% of group revenues and 14% of NAV by Regis estimates. ICT also secured a 15-year extension for its Madagascar port concession which was supposed to end in 2025. The port accounts for 5% of group EBITDA and the  extension should add to its share of group NAV (currently at  2%).

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

BSP keeps key rate at record low of 2%. BSP kept overnight reverse repurchase rate at 2% for the eighth straight policy meeting in line with street expectations. BSP chief Benjamin Diokno emphasized the need to preserve the ongoing monetary policy support to help sustain the economy’s momentum over the next few quarters.

Inflation eases further to 4.2% in November. The country’s Inflation rate decelerated for the third straight month to 4.2% in November. This is slightly higher than the BSP’s forecast range for the month of 3.3%-4.1% and also higher than consensus (4.0%). The lower CPI is due to moderating food inflation (e.g. vegetable, fish, and meat). Meanwhile, non-food inflation rose to 4.1% in November from 3.8% the previous month due to higher crude oil prices (transport and electricity). The YT-November average inflation is at 4.5%. The slowdown in November inflation supports the BSP’s assessment that inflation would decelerate to within the target range of 2-4% over the policy horizon.

BOP posts US$123mn deficit in November. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$123mn in November 2021 which is a reversal from the US$1.14bn surplus in October. This brings YT-November BOP surplus to US$353m, down from US$11.79bn surplus in the same period last year. The BSP is targeting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn by end-2021.

End-November 2021 GIR Level Settles at US$107.67bn. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, settled at US$107.67bn as of end-November 2021. This is slightly lower than the end-October GIR level of US$107.89bn and equivalent to 10.2 months’ worth of imports of goods. The BSP is targeting an end-2021 GIR level of US$111bn, lower than the previous forecast of US$114b.

Easing exports temper foreign trade growth in October. External trade rose 15% YoY to US$16.8bn in October, slower than the 17% YoY growth in September. Meanwhile, export growth slowed to 2% from +6% the previous month while imports grew at a sustained rate of 25% YoY due to surging fuel imports and sustained improvement in consumer goods imports. With the import bill growing at a faster pace than export sales, the Philippines’ trade deficit stood at US$4.02bn in October, slightly bigger than US$4bn gap in September. This brings YT-October total trade and trade deficit to US$157bn and US$33bn, respectively.

Remittances hit 3-month high in October. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.4% YoY to US$2.812bn in October. This is the highest amount since July. In PHP terms, October remittances rose 7.1% YoY and brings the 3-month moving average growth rate steady at 4.2%. Land-based remittances up 4% MoM but this was offset by a 3% contraction in sea-based remittances. The YT-October remittances reached US$25.9bn, up 5.3% YoY. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +6% YoY in 2021 and +4% in 2022.

Budget deficit widens in November. The National Government’s budget deficit widened to P128.7bn in November, doubling from the previous month due to higher primary spending (i.e. public works, health, education, national defense, and social welfare agencies). Meanwhile, tax collections improved MoM and YoY. This brings YT-November fiscal deficit to P1.33tn, c. 83% of the government’s downgraded budget deficit target of P1.6tn (c. 8.2% of GDP).

Duterte signs P5tn 2022 budget into law. President Duterte signed into law the P5.024tn national budget for 2022. The 2022 budget is 21.8% of GDP and c. 11% higher than 2021. The health sector’s budget saw the largest increase, with an allocation of P268bn (+28% YoY). Around P80bn of this will be used to subsidize the health insurance premiums of 13mn indigent families and 7mn senior citizens while P56bn will be used to improve health facilities. The DPWH and the DOTr will receive P786.6bn and P75.8bn, respectively, to accelerate the Build, Build, Build program. The program includes Network Development Program (P127bn), Flood Management Program (P129bn), Rail Transport Program (P23bn) and the Land Public Transportation Program (P16bn).

Gov’t revises macroeconomic targets. The interagency Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) raised its 2021 GDP growth target to 5-5.5% which is higher than previous target of 4-5% given in August. The medium-term growth rates kept at 7-9% for 2022 and 6-7% for 2023/2024. The 2021 inflation rate target was raised to 4.3%-4.5% from 2-4% previously; For 2022-2024, inflation forecast kept at 2-4%.

BSP revises external sector targets. The BSP revised its end-2021 BOP surplus target to US$1.6bn (0.4% of GDP) from US$4.1bn (1.1% of GDP) previously. This reflects current account deficit of US$4bn (-1% of GDP) from a surplus of US$3.5bn (0.9% of GDP) due to widening of trade-in-goods deficit. OFW remittances growth target retained at +6% for FY21.

 

Implementation of the Mandanas ruling seen to dampen economic growth. The implementation of the Mandanas ruling expanding the local govt’s share in national taxes next year would lead to a 3% lower economic growth according to Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III. The higher tax allocation for local government units (LGUs) would dampen spending efficiency because the National Government usually spends at double the pace. The government is looking at GDP to grow by 7-9% in 2022. To minimize the impact, the Finance department is reviewing a possible fiscal consolidation plan, or policies aimed at reducing the country’s deficit. Based on the 2022 proposed budget, LGUs will be tasked to spend 22% of the budget, up from 19% in previous years.

MB approves rate cap on lending firms. Last December 16, the Monetary Board issued Resolution 1717 approving the cap on interest rates and other fees charged by lending firms, financing companies and online lending platforms pursuant to the provisions of the Lending Company Regulation Act of 2007 or the Financing Company Act of 1998. Diokno said these laws empower the Monetary Board the prescribe maximum interest rates that could be charged by lending and financing companies in consultation with the SEC if warranted by economic and social conditions. The BSP has set a nominal interest rate ceiling of 6% per month for covered loans. Effective interest rate was pegged at a maximum of 15% per month – including applicable charges such as processing, handling and other fees. The regulator also set a 5% per month ceiling on penalties for late payment. The BSP chief pointed out that the ceilings on interest rates and fees is subject to periodic review by the BSP, SEC and the industry.

DENR lifts ban on open pit mining. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) issued an administrative order, lifting the nationwide ban on the open pit method of mining metals. The order was published in a newspaper on Dec. 25 and will take effect after 15 days. The government imposed the ban in 2017. Last April, President Duterte lifted the moratorium on new mineral agreements imposed in 2012. This latest development should allow the government to issue mining permits. 

Semirara (SCC PM) is waiting for its limestone mining permit to be able to proceed with its plans to enter the cement business. Other big-ticket mining projects that are currently on hold are: Tampakan of Sagittarius Mines ($5.9bn project), King-king copper-gold - St. Augustine ($2bn), Silangan project - Philex ($750mn). SM (SM PM) was reportedly interested in taking a stake in Sagittarius Mines.

Duterte signs P5tn 2022 budget into law. President Duterte signed into law the P5.024tn national budget for 2022. The 2022 budget is 21.8% of GDP and c. 11% higher than 2021. The health sector’s budget saw the largest increase, with an allocation of P268bn (+28% YoY). Around P80bn of this will be used to subsidize the health insurance premiums of 13mn indigent families and 7mn senior citizens while P56bn will be used to improve health facilities. The DPWH and the DOTr will receive P786.6bn and P75.8bn, respectively, to accelerate the Build, Build, Build program. The program includes Network Development Program (P127bn), Flood Management Program (P129bn), Rail Transport Program (P23bn) and the Land Public Transportation Program (P16bn).

BIR to start collecting taxes from POGOs. The BIR issued implementing rules on RA 11590 (POGO taxes). This will require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue. Meanwhile. non-gaming revenue will be subject to 25% corporate income tax. POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P145bn in tax revenue over five years.

Progress on the Public Service Act in the Senate. The Senate approved on second reading the bill amending the Commonwealth era Public Service Act.  These amendments are supposed to remove foreign ownership limits on several industries, most significantly in Telecoms, Airlines and Domestic Shipping.

The Senate version of the bill (or at least one of them) has several crucial limitations. First is the foreign ownership limit stays at 40% for foreign state-owned entities.  Second, it requires reciprocity for the country of the foreign entity/entities.  Regis reckons these clauses, if present in the final law, could still prevent an entity like China Telecom from taking over Dito completely.

The industries that are still considered public utilities are: distribution of electricity, the transmission of electricity, and water pipeline distribution and sewerage pipeline systems, airports, seaports, and public utility vehicles.  This means foreign ownership limits will still apply to Megawide (and its airport) and its hope that this bill will change that are likely to be dashed.

Without foreign ownership limits, the telcos could be 100% foreign-owned if they spin off their property assets. From a technical point of view, it would have implications on MSCI weightings et al.

 

 

Bank lending expands 3.5% YoY in October; M3 growth slightly decelerates. Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, rose 3.5% YoY in October. This is faster than the 2.7% YoY increase in September 2021. Meanwhile, loans from production activities rose 4.9% YoY, with sustained recoveries in manufacturing, real estate, transpo/storage, and information/communication sectors.

Consumer loans dropped 7.2%, milder than the 7.8% drop in September due to the slight YoY increase in credit card loans and the slower contraction in salary-based general purpose loan. Auto loans still contracting, down by 16% YoY and 1% MoM. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded 8.2% YoY to P14.6tn in October, slightly slower than the 8.3% (revised) growth in September.

Asset quality continues to improve in October. NPL ratio edged lower to 4.56% from 4.59% in September, and from the peak 4.66% in august. The improvement was seen in nominal PHP terms too. More encouraging is that Past Due (PD) ratio fell 5bps MoM to 5.16%, with the absolute values falling a full 1% MoM. This points to further easing NPLs in the coming months. Even Restructured Loans (RLs) eked out some improvement, down 1bp MoM to 3.08%. NPL cover crept up to 85% from 84% in September, taking Loan Loss Reserves (LLR) to 3.77%, up 3bps MoM. Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated again, up 3.2% YoY, better than the 2.4% pace in September. 

While some of the gains were admittedly small, all the numbers are moving in the right direction. Barring a major lockdown from Omicron, it would seem that bank balance sheets are truly on the mend.

Vehicle sales rebound strongly in November. Industry car sales jumped +14% YoY, +17% MoM to 26,456 units in November according to data from the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines Inc. (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA). This is the highest monthly unit sales this year, as COVID restrictions eased and brings YT-November industry sales to 240,643 units (+23% YoY). This is 28% below 2019 and tracking below CAMPI’s year-end target of 295k units (+32% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 12,690 units in November, up 11% YoY and 14% MoM. Their market share slightly dropped to 48% from 49% the previous month. This brings YT-November sales to 116k units (+35% YoY), trending slightly behind TMP’s target of 130k units (+30% YoY). Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit. GTCAP is trading at an 8% discount to its market-based NAV of P625/sh.

Duterte declares state of calamity in 6 regions. President Duterte placed regions 4B, 6, 7, 8, 10, and 13 under a state of calamity to hasten relief and rehab efforts. The much needed calamity funds will be released on or before Friday. This will also be an effective mechanism to control the prices of goods and commodities in the areas. The latest tally showed 156 deaths, 275 injured, and 37 missing. 

Typhoon ‘Odette’ may dampen PHL recovery. Agriculture and productivity losses caused by Typhoon Odette (Rai) could significantly impact fourth-quarter economic expansion according to Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua. The gov’t is awaiting a full impact/damage report and reconstruction efforts could offset the economic damage. A total of 11,859 families or 41,434 individuals were affected by the typhoon in 249 barangays in the regions of Bicol, Western Visayas, Eastern Visayas, Northern Mindanao, and Caraga.

FDA approves Pfizer vaccine for 5 to 11 year-old children. The Philippine FDA issued Emergency Use Authorization for the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID vaccine to be used for 5 to 11 year-old children. Vaccines to be administered on this age group will have lower dosage and concentration than those administered on adults. The current vaccines in the Philippines cannot be administered on this age group. Around 42% of the Philippine population (45.3mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. The Philippines has received a total of 192mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 104mn doses (as of December 20).

Government reduces interval for booster dose. Filipinos can now receive a COVID-19 booster dose 3 months after receiving their second dose from 6 months previously. As of end-September, 21.4mn Filipinos were fully vaccinated. Additionally, 1.15mn booster doses have been administered (as of December 20). Around 41% of the Philippine population (44.2mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. The Philippines has received a total of 190mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 102mn doses (as of December 20).

Marcos/Sara Duterte lead President/VP survey. The tandem of former senator Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. and Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte-Carpio topped the December 2021 survey of Pulse Asia. For President, 53% of respondents picked Marcos, who also led in terms of geographical areas and majority of classes. Meanwhile, VP Leni Robredo took 2nd place with 20%, up from just 8% in the previous survey while Isko Moreno and Pacquiao dropped to 8% each. For VP, Sara Duterte got 45% while Senate President Vicente Sotto III had 31%. A total of 2,400 respondents participated in the survey, which was conducted from December 1-6 using face-to-face interviews.
