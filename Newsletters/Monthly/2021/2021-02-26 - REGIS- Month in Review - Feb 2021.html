REGIS REPORT

26 February 2021
 

The PSEi rose 2.8% MoM in February, recovering somewhat from a 7.4% drop in January. Some respite in foreign selling early in the month helped stock prices climb. The gain in February came amid concerns on the slow pace of economic recovery for the country.

President Duterte rejected proposals to loosen the country’s lockdown status to “Modified General Community Quarantine” (MGCQ), supposedly saying he wanted vaccinations to begin before approving any further easing. Vaccines are expected to be widely avaible only in 3Q21. The PSEi had been up over 7% MTD (as of Feb 10) but gains were pared as the market discounted a longer recovery period for the economy.

The CPI jumped to 4.2% YoY in January – the highest level since Jan 2019. Inflation was driven by food (+6.2%) and transportation (+8.6%). The BSP  said that despite CPI breaching its 2-4% target, it would not change its monetary stance, as inflation is “transitory” and is led by cost-push, and not by increased demand.

The inflation surprise in January has caused the 10-year benchmark (BVAL)  to move up.  As of the latest print, the 10-year was at 3.893, 96bp higher for the month of February and 90bp higher YTD. The local currency has come under pressure as well.  The PHP deteriorated 1% in February to settle at PHP48.585/US$. YTD, the PHP is down 1.2%.

Already, the Philippines is underperforming its ASEAN peers. The PSEi is already down 4.8% YTD in PHP and 6.0% in USD terms, underperfoming Indonesia (+2.1%), Malaysia (-3.7%), Singapore (+2.8%), and Thailand (+2.3%). The threat of inflation has worsened the already somber outlook on consumption spending.  Higher input prices are also weighing on sentiment for company margins. Meanwhile, the NEDA  has warned of a negative GDP print in 1Q21 due to the continued Community Quarantine restrictions.

Congress has ratified the reconciled version of the corporate tax reform package dubbed CREATE.  The bill would reduce corporate tax rates by 5ppt to 25%. This should increase the after-tax profits for most of covered stocks by between 5-7%. The tax cut would be implemented retroactively to July 2020.

MORE ON FLOWS...

February saw continued foreign outflows of US$190m. Outflows remained consistent throughout the month with inflows only seen in two trading days in February. 

There was an MSCI rebalancing at the end of February where an estimated US$40m of passive outflows was expected to exit; the market  saw US$20m outflow that day on what is likely due to pre-positioning flows. Biggest outflows were expected in SMPH ALI SM AC BDO JGS, while just marginal inflows in ICT.

Foreign participation remained light at only 26% in February (25% in January) despite a slowdown in local retail activity. Small-cap names dominated by retail activity accounted for roughly 35% of total market volume, lower than the > 40% seen in January.



Foreigners were two-way banks, buying BPI, while selling MBT and BDO. MBT was top foreign sold as foreign investors were seen taking profit amid its 12% surge in February.  Telco operator CNVRG saw heavy foreign selling as investors took the rally as an opportunity to sell after the spike fueled by retail punters following speculation by a local business tabloid over a possible partnership with Elon Musk’s Space X.

Decent outflows were also seen in other big caps SMPH AC JFC in part due to MSCI rebalancing where the entire Phils. saw another slight down-weight.  

Meanwhile, there was increased foreign interest into Nickel Asia (NIKL) -- the only pure nickel play in the Philippines.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The banking sector led the gains in February.

After declining over 8% in the previous month, Metrobank (+12.7% MoM) recovered in February. The stock was the top index gainer, propelled by a surprise special dividend (P3/sh) announced by the bank (translating to 8% yield).  The dividend came despite the bank expensing a massive P40.8bn in provisions in 2020 which pushed FY20 NPAT down to P13.8bn and below estimates. Nonetheless NPL coverage reached 163%, despite NPLs increasing to 2.41% in 4Q (from 2.25% in 3Q). Net loans fell 15.5% YoY. 

Outside of Metrobank, other banks were buoyant. Sentiment was helped by the FIST Law which was signed by President Duterte during the month. The Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer Law grants banks tax breaks relating to the disposition of their non-performing assets. The law was rushed in response to the expected bad debt problems arising from the pandemic and should allow banks to recover from asset quality issues more quickly.  Nonetheless, investors remain wary of asset quality.  The headline NPL ratio for Phils banks came in at 3.7% in December and is expected to rise in 1Q21 as the new grace periods under Bayanihan 2 Act expire.

BPI (+12.0% MoM) was not far behind.  The bank’s sanguine tone, expecting NPLS to peak at 4% or lower in 2H21, helped sentiment. 

BDO (+4.8% MoM)  reported headline FY2020 NPAT of P28.2bn, slightly ahead of Regis (P26.9bn) and consensus (P27.3bn). BDO bumped up provisions to P6.4bn in 4Q, taking the full year to P30.2bn. NPLs jumped to 2.65% from 1.95% in 3Q20, and NPL cover fell to 105%. Loan growth was 3% YoY, down from 6% in 3Q20. BDO was the only Big Three bank to post FY20 growth in loans.

Conglomerates also helped push up the market. Alliance Global (+8.5% MoM), LT Group (+7.7% MoM), JG Summit (+6.0% MoM), and Aboitiz Equity (+5.9% MoM) all gained markedly.

Meanwhile, GT Capital's (+4.8% MoM) Toyota Motor Philippines will address the issue of the safeguard duty by asking for a refundable deposit from buyers beginning March 1. The deposits (P78k or P123K per vehicle) will have to be paid upfront and will be refunded, in theory, should the ruling be reversed.

Meralco (+3.8% MoM)  closed up for the month.  The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) approved the application of Meralco to refund to its customers P13.886bn, representing the amount it has collected in excess of the previously approved Interim Average Rate (IAR) of P1.381/kWh since Jul 2015. As the company has  more than fully provided for the amount, there will be no direct impact on the P&L. A potential affirmation of the finality of the IAR could be positive for Meralco as the company has been setting aside annual provisions of least P10bn should rates be cut retroactively. This could translate to a potential reversal of massive provisions (could be >P60bn) as well as higher prospective earnings.

 

Big news for the property sector was the approval (by the House of Representatives) of a bill that would lower Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) taxes from 5% of bets/turnover to 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR).  The bill will be transmitted to the Senate, and if approved there, would possibly slow down the exodus of POGOs. POGOs occupy c. 15% of Filinvest Land’s (+3.6% MoM) office portfolio and 10% of Megaworld’s (-5.2% MoM) office space.  For SM Prime (+2.4%), around 45% of NAV is comprised of land in the Manila Bay area – an area preferred by POGOs.

The pandemic and lockdowns continued to wreak havoc on property earnings.  SM Prime’s 4Q20 net profit fell 65% YoY to P3.6bn. FY20 earnings were down 53% to P18.0bn, slightly below REGIS forecast (and well below consensus estimates). Revenue of the major segments improved QoQ and appear in line or above our forecasts for the full year, suggesting that the underperformance was driven by expenses. Meanwhile, reservation sales surged in 4Q20.

Ayala Land’s (+3.6% MoM) FY20 profit of P8.7bn (-74%) was behind REGIS and consensus forecasts, but operating profit was in line as faster construction boosted 4Q20 residential revenue.

 

Among the top index decliners for the month were consumer stocks Robinsons Retail (-7.5% MoM) and Universal Robina (-5.4% MoM).  Both names saw net outflows of around US$11-12m each.  

Jollibee (+0.7% MoM) fared a bit better. JFC reported a headline net loss of P11.5bn for FY20, better than Regis and consensus expectations. Operating profit, in fact, returned to the black in 4Q20, helped by the restructuring costs which management believes will yield P2.6bn in annual cost savings. However, the Philippines business has yet to recover, dragging revenues in 4Q20 down 30%.

PLDT (-1.9% MoM) inched down. 

The telecom regulator has given the new operator DITO (+34.5% MoM) a passing grade in its first technical audit. This is in line with commitments made by DITO to secure its license and spectrum. New telco DITO will begin offering services in 17 cities beginning March 8 and will slow roll out nationwide from there. These cities will be in Mindanao and Visayas, likely tier two or tier three cities. The firm’s initial strategy and pricing could give us a glimpse of how competition will shape up in the coming months or year.

Meantime, Converge ICT Solutions (+12.6% MoM) saw buying interest. The company grew its fiber network by 107% to 55,000km in 2020. Residential subscribers reached over 1mn, from 529,000 in 2019. The company also recorded 6.1mn homes passed, accounting for 25% of total households. Its 2025 target is to reach 15mn households, 55% of total Philippine households.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation spikes to 4.2% in January. Headline inflation surged to 4.2% in January, the fastest since January 2019 (4.4%). It surpassed the government’s target range of 2-4% and street estimates (3.5%). The spikes in food prices, particularly meat, vegetables, and fruits, were partly due to the lingering effects of typhoons, as well as the African Swine Flu. In NCR alone, price of fresh pork with bones and pure meat rose by 68% and 77% YoY, respectively. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) said the breach of its inflation target still does not warrant a quick action in monetary policy. The average inflation would still fall within its target range this year as the projected upturn in the first half is seen as temporary. The BSP chief pointed out supply-side shocks are best addressed by non-monetary interventions.

Remittances dip in December. OFW remittances dipped 0.4% YoY to US$2.89bn in December (+21.5% MoM). That means that in PHP terms, remittances fell 5.7% YoY but rose 21% MoM. Remittances from Europe (-31%) and Asia (-9%) fell, offsetting the 14% jump in remittances from the USA. This brings the 3MMA growth in remittances was at +0.9%. FY20 remittances fell slightly (-0.8%) to US$29.9bn (in PHP terms, remittances fell 5% YoY). The BSP expects remittances to grow by 4% in 2021.

NEDA to review GDP goal. The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) will have to reassess its GDP target for this year, amid another delay in fully reopening the economy. The government is currently targeting the economy to recover by +6.5% to +7.5% this year. This is premised on: shift to a modified general community quarantine (MGCQ), implementation of the recovery program, and timely COVID vaccination. Regis FY21 GDP forecast: +7%; Consensus: +7.5%.

Gov’t debt-to-GDP ratio jumps to 14-year high in 2020. The end-2020 national government (NG) debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 54.5% in December 2020. This is the highest since 2006 (58.8%). The government accelerated borrowings last year amid the pandemic-induced recession. The total outstanding NG debt amounted to P9.79tn in December. While this was 3.3% lower than the outstanding debt in November due to repayments, this was 26.7% YoY higher than the end-2019 debt level of P7.73tn. Domestic loans accounted for 68.4% of total, up from 66.3% in 2019. For 2021, the programmed P3.03tn in gross borrowings would further hike the debt-to-GDP ratio to 57%.

 

BOP posts US$752mn deficit in January. The country’s balance of payments swung to a deficit of US$752m in January. This is the first deficit after 11 months of surplus but was 44.7% YoY lower than the US$1.355bn deficit in January 2020. This also reflected the 1.19% MoM decrease in gross international reserves (GIR) to US$108.67bn in end-January (equivalent to 11.6 months’ worth of imports). The BSP sees the BOP surplus narrowing to US$3.3bn this year (equivalent to 0.8% of GDP). In 2020, the BOP posted a surplus of US$16.02bn.

GIR dips slightly to US$109bn in January. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, settled at US$108.8bn as of end-January 2021. This is lower than the end-December 2020 GIR level of US$110.12bn. The drop came from outflows mainly from the foreign currency withdrawals of the National Government (NG) from its deposits in the BSP and revaluation of gold holdings. This level is equivalent to around 11.6 months’ worth of imports.

US$4.2bn in ‘hot money’ exits the Philippines in 2020 amid pandemic. Foreign portfolio investments posted a net outflow of US$4.2bn in 2020, more than double the US$1.9bn net outflow in 2019. This is the highest net outflow since 2012. The net outflows may be broken down to net outflows in the following instruments: Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE)-listed shares (US$3.3bn); Peso government securities (GS) (US$931mn); Other portfolio instruments (US$22mn). In December alone, hot money posted net outflow of US$523.86mn, up 63.2% YoY. This is the biggest net outflow since the US$1.006bn outflow recorded in May.

Gov’t eyes P1tn in foreign loans for 2021. The Philippine government is planning to borrow $23.71bn (P1.14tn) from foreign lenders this year to plug the deficit. This will be higher than the US$17.06bn (P820bn) borrowings from external sources in 2020. US$7.67bn (P369bn) will be sourced from multilateral lenders, US$10.54bn (P507bn) from bilateral partners; US$5.5bn (P264bn) from commercial markets. Around 66% or US$15.65bn of the loans will be in the form of project financing, while 34% or US$8.06bn will support the national budget. In 2020, 85% of US$14.52bn was used for budget support while the rest were in project loans. The government expects a budget deficit of P1.78tn this year, around 8.9% of GDP. In 2020, the budget deficit reached P1.36tn, around 7.6% of GDP.

Bicam approves CREATE Act. The bicameral conference committee approved the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act. Among the provisions reportedly written into the bill by the panel that were not in the versions previously passed in the Senate and the House was on the fair treatment for petroleum refiners. This exempts crude oil intended for refining from duties and excise, and taxes the product upon removal once refined, to avoid stranded value-added tax credits, consistent with international practice. CREATE also lowers CIT from 30% to 20% for small and medium corporations and to 25% for all other corporations. Other fiscal incentives give; up to 17 years of incentives for exporters and for “critical” domestic market enterprises and up to 12 years of incentives for other domestic market enterprises. The future law will also give additional 3 years of income tax holiday for registered enterprises that will fully relocate outside of NCR and additional 2 years of income tax holiday for registered enterprises in areas recovering from disasters or conflict. Other tax relief: Higher VAT exemption threshold from P1.5mn to P2.5mn for socialized and low-cost housing, and from P2.5mn to P4.2mn for house and lot and other residential dwellings.

Duterte signs FIST into law. President Duterte signed Republic Act No. 11523 (a.k.a. Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer or FIST). FIST will allow financial institutions to offload bad loans to asset management companies. This should be generally positive for banks as this relieves them of the burden of managing bad assets. The key provisions are “At least 2-year tax exemption on asset sales from a bank to the FIST Corporation (FIST-C)” and “At least 5-year tax exemption on asset sales from a FIST-C to a third party”. FIST-Cs will be exempt from income tax on its NII, and DST on new loans. Meanwhile, losses arising from a bank’s sale of NPAs to a FIST-C will be considered an “ordinary loss”. Banks will be allowed to invest up to 10% in the FIST-Cs that will buy their NPAs.

House of Representatives approves bill on POGO taxes. The House of Representatives approved on third and final reading a bill on POGO taxes which would be 5% of gross revenue or receipts. Additionally, POGO employees who earn an annual salary of at least P600,000 will pay 25% in income taxes. The bill will be transmitted to the Senate and Joey Salceda, Chairman of the House ways and means committee, estimates that the government can earn P144bn in revenue from the proposed taxes. The Bayanihan 2 Act, passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. We think the new bill could slow down POGO closures.

 

Duterte defers nationwide shift to MGCQ until vaccinations begin. President Rodrigo Duterte has rejected a proposal to put the entire country to the lowest quarantine status, Modified General Community Quarantine (MGCQ). President wants the vaccine to be rolled out first before easing restrictions. Duterte also rejected the renewed proposal to allow limited in-person classes in low-risk areas. The coronavirus task force had recommended that NCR and 13 other provinces join the rest of the Philippines under MGCQ starting March.

Supreme Court dismisses Marcos electoral protest vs Robredo. The Supreme Court, sitting as the Presidential Electoral Tribunal (PET), dismissed the electoral protest filed by former Senator Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr., for lack of merit. This affirms the election victory of Vice President Ma. Leonor “Leni” Robredo. However, lawyers of Marcos said that the PET only resolved the second cause of action (the manual recount and judicial revision) and not the entire petition. The Marcos camp said that the PET has yet to decide on the third cause of action, which is the annulment of votes in parts of Mindanao due to alleged discrepancies.

Bank lending contracts in December.Preliminary data on outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase placements, showed a -0.7% contraction in December vs a 0.5% growth in November. This is the first time that bank lending contracted in 14 years (September 2006: -1.6%). The issues cited were lower appetite or demand from firms or businesses, specifically from large enterprises, and to some extent the relatively stricter bank loan standards. Loans to businesses fell by 0.4%, with the sustained drop in manufacturing (-5.2%) and trade (-6.8%). Loan growth to the real estate sector (mortgages + corporates), however, accelerated slightly to 5.3% from 5.2% the previous month. Meanwhile, consumer loan growth decelerated to 4.4% in December from 7.1% in November. Domestic liquidity (M3 growth) expanded by 9.5% in December to P14.2tn but moderated from November (+10.5%).

Vehicle sales slightly down in January. Members of CAMPI/TMA sold 23,380 units in January, down 1% YoY and 15% MoM. The MoM drop looks typical for a January performance. Last year’s sales were also affected by the Taal volcano eruption. Toyota’s market share stood at 46.28%, implying that it sold 10,820 units in January, up 22% YoY but down 21% MoM. However, sales from February onwards will likely already reflect the impact of the provisional safeguard duty on imported vehicles. The Tariff Commission started its 200-day review of the duty last Feb 4. Toyota estimates the import duty will likely reduce its FY21 sales volume estimate to 104k-108k units (+4-8% YoY) from 130k units (+30% YoY). Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 15% discount to its market-based NAV of P647/sh.
