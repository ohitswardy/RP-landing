REGIS REPORT

02 May 2021
 

The PSEi gave up 1.1% in April, the staying below the 6500 level.

The market dropped to 6370.87 at the end of the month, reeling from new highs in daily COVID infections, crowded hospitals, and the renewed tight lockdowns.

Concerns of economic recovery pushed back even further sent stock prices lower as the pace of vaccinations remained sluggish.  Since starting in early March, 1.8m doses have been administered. The Philippines has received a total of 4m COVID vaccine doses so far, with the government expecting 7-8m doses to arrive in May. The government had previously said that 140m doses would arrive by the end of the year to inoculate 70m Filipinos. 

The month of April went by with NCR Plus (NCR and neighboring provinces) in lockdown – going  from the tightest form (ECQ or Enhanced Community Quarantine) and to a slightly eased MECQ (Modified ECQ) from April 12 onwards.  While previously suspended, the government has allowed limited dine-in for restaurants in NCP Plus at 10% of seating capacity. Other  specific sectors and establishments have also been allowed to re-open. Curfew hours have been shortened to 10pm-4am (from 8pm to 5am).  Nonetheless, they daily tally of new infections remained high despite coming off peak levels. 

After growing its portfolio of Treasury holdings almost 6x the level before the COVID-19 pandemic as of September (to P1.3trn), the BSP sems to have slowed down its own version of Quantitative easing.  Increasing its Treasury holdings by just P75bn QoQ in 4Q, the BSP also appears to be on the sidelines so far this year. As a result, 10-year yields rose sharply in Febreuary and March and hit as high as 4.492% from 2.996% at end-Dec.  The 10-year yield is now at 4.129%

CPI inflation in March came in at 4.5%, lower than Street expectations and a tad lower than the February print as food prices (ex meat) softened. Energy prices are likely to keep inflation elevated over the near term, however.

Key data
 
FLOW UPDATE

April registered another US$279m of foreign selling (net), bringing YTD net foreign outflows to US$1.225bn. It was a month of consistent selling again, with foreigners net sellers in every trading day of April.  

Foreign participation jumped to 39% (from 30% in March), but mainly due to a slowdown in local retail activity.

 

 

Foreigners were clearly on one side of the market this month, with not a single name seeing decent foreign inflows. Foreign outflows were again seen across the board, with property and banks capturing the bulk of it.  Ayala Land was top foreign sold for a 2nd consecutive month, and accounted for nearly a third of outflows. 

Banks continue to be sold, with Metrobank, Bank of the Philippine islands, and Security Bank all seeing heavy outflows as there remains some concern that the prolonged lockdown could trigger a fresh wave of loan defaults into the second half of the year.

COVID “defensive” names were not spared, with telcos PLDT and Globe also seeing aggressive foreign selling.

Meanwhile, budget airline operator Cebu Air is starting to gain attention from some foreign investors. The company successfully raised P28.5bn through its convertible preferred share offering and should tide them over the next 18 months.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The PSEi dropped 72 index points in April.  Bulk of the losses were contributed by index heavyweight JG Summit (-11.7% MoM), which pulled down the market by 58 index points. Ayala Land (-6.3% MoM) also pushed the market lower, chipping in 48 index points to the market’s decline in April.

Property was weak all around with Megaworld (-12.0% MoM) the top index decliner in April.  Robinsons Land (-8.4% MoM) was not far behind. Spillover selling in Megaworld was apparent after reporting 4Q20 profits that fell behind forecasts.  The company’s flat reservation sales guidance in 2021 and its 40% QoQ drop in office continued to weigh. Office leasing accounts for 52% of Megaworld’s NAV. 

Office vacancy in Metro Manila is expected to rise (Regis: from 9% in 2020 to 12 % by end 2021E) due largely to POGO closures.  Rental rates are expected to drop further, as a consequence. 

BPI (+1.6% MoM) and BDO (+1.1% MoM) showed similar trends in their 1Q21 results. Benign NPLs and lower provisions boosted profits. These made up for contracting loans and NIM squeeze. PPOP for both were broadly in line. Benign asset quality could provide upside to forecasts though NPLs could be an issue again given the renewed lockdowns.

Speaking of banks, Philippine National Bank (+37.5% MoM) surged. PNB is distributing 51% of PNB Holdings as property dividend to its shareholders. There is also an intention to list PHC via introduction. PHC is the holding company for three of the bank’s most valuable pieces of real estate which Regis estimates at a sizeable value of P41bn. This compares to PNB’s market cap of ~P48bn. A PHC listing would be a tax efficient way of monetizing these assets (stocks listed the PSE are only levied a 0.6% tax when selling vs capital gains tax of 6% of value).

PNB parent LT Group (-0.6% MoM) would receive its proportionate share of PHC (~29%), which is likely to be worth ~P12bn based on PHC’s underlying property value

The pandemic continued to factor in other 1Q profits.

Universal Robina’s (+2.9% MoM) Phils BCF business was down 4% YoY, coming from a high base due to last year’s pantry stocking.  Total revenues came in up 3% YoY. Robinsons Retail’s (-8.3% MoM) 1Q also saw SSS for the Supermarket segment (59% of sales) declining 15% YoY largely due to the high base in 1Q20.  Meanwhile,  Dept Stores and Specialty Stores (collectively 12% of sales), underperformed due to lockdown restrictions in 1Q.

Meantime, Wilcon’s (-1.1% MoM) numbers showed signs of recovery from the strict ECQ in the last two weeks of March last year.   Net sales grew 19% on the back of 9.8% SSSG.  

Meralco’s (+0.3% MoM) results showed that demand (at -4% YoY for 1Q) has yet to fully recover.  Aboitiz Power (-2.1% MoM) also reported an 8% decline in electricity demand for its distribution utilities during the quarter.

The government finally signed a revised agreement with Manila Water (-8.3% MoM) covering its East Zone concession. The nominal return is now 12% (it was last set at an inflation-adjusted 7% in 2018). Combined with a lowered tax rate (now 25%), the unlevered after-tax return would be a pretty decent 9%. The only large difference from a financial perspective is that MWC will no longer be granted a free FX hedge on its debt.

There are also new rules that would make it easier for the regulator to seek information, audit and impose sanctions. New provisions in the contract also call for the turnover of fully recovered and depreciated assets owned by MWC to the government (MWSS) even before the concession expires. The new rules also provide changes in government protections and limiting arbitration to local arbitrators. 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases to 4.5% in March. Headline inflation eased to 4.5% in March from 4.7% in February. This is lower than consensus forecast (4.9%) but came withing the BSP’s target range (4.2%-5%). Core inflation stood at 3.5%, unchanged MoM. Food inflation, particularly for fish, fruits and vegetables, decelerated due to the lifting of the closed fishing season and improving weather conditions. Inflation of utilities moderated but this was partly offset by accelerating transport inflation due to higher fuel prices. 1Q21 average inflation rate stood at 4.5%, still above the government’s target range of 2-4% for the year.

Trade gap widens in February. The country’s trade-in-goods deficit widened to US$2.3bn in February. Imports rose 3% YoY, the first time in 22 months; However, imports were lower by 12% MoM while exports dropped 2% YoY, 4% MoM.

OFW remittances rise 5% in February. OFW remittances rose 5% to US$2.47bn in February. In PHP terms, remittances fell slightly (-0.2% YoY). This brings the 3-month moving average at +0.8% YoY and brings YT-Feb remittances to US$5.08bn (+1.5%). The BSP expects remittances to rise by 4% this year, after dipping -0.8% in FY20.

BoP deficit reaches US$73mn in March. The country’s balance of payments (BoP) stood at a deficit for the third straight month in March, at US$73mn, reversing the US$448mn surplus a year earlier. The deficit in March, however, was smaller than in February 2021 (US$2.019bn). The deficit due to payments of National Government’s foreign debt obligations. This brings 1Q21 BOP deficit to US$2.844bn (vs US$68mn deficit in the same period last year).

Budget deficit widens to P191bn in March. The National Government reported a wider budget deficit of P191bn in March. Revenues fell 17% YoY due to base effects from last year’s dividend remittances to fund Bayanihan I. Tax revenues were up 7% YoY, driven by higher collections from the Bureau of Customs. The BIR collections remained soft, down 13.5% MoM. Expenditures rose 22% YoY due to infrastructure disbursements. The 1Q21 budget deficit at P321.5bn (vs full-year target of P1.78tn = 8.9% of GDP). Tax collections up by 0.8% YoY to P626bn in 1Q21, tracking behind the government’s FY21E: P2.7bn (+8% YoY). Expenditures rose by 20% YoY to P1.018tn, 22% of the government’s FY21E. 

Infra spending picks up in 2M21. Government spending on infrastructure rose +9% YoY to P51bn and +23% YoY to P56bn in January and February, respectively. The increases were attributed to DPWH payments for completed projects and mobilization costs for new projects this year. The figures exclude LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies. This brings YT-Feb infra spend up 14% YoY to P107bn. The government expects to infra spend to further pick up in March, given the trend of increasing expenditures before the validity of cash allocations end.

Dollar reserves slip in March after gov’t pays maturing foreign debt. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, settled at US$104.82bn as of end-March 2021 from the end-Feb GIR level of US$105.16bn. This is equivalent to 12 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The MoM dip reflected outflows mainly from the net withdrawal in the National Government’s foreign currency deposits with the BSP, which were largely used for debt servicing, and a downward adjustment in the value of BSP’s gold holdings due to the decrease in the price of gold in the international market. These outflows were partly offset, however, by the BSP’s income from its investments abroad.

BSP bond buying slowed in Dec. The BSP finally updated its balance sheet data to end-2020 (previously was only to Nov). The numbers clearly show what we had suspected – that the BSP had taken its foot off the monetary pedal in 4Q20. In 2Q/3Q20, the BSP engaged in its own version of Quantitative Easing (QE), with its open market operations (OMO) aggressively buying Treasury paper (mostly long-dated, we gather), peaking at over P400bn bought in June alone. By September, the BSP’s portfolio of Treasury holdings stood at P1.3trn, almost 6x the level before the Covid-19 pandemic. In 4Q20, however, the BSP hung back, increasing its Treasury holdings by just P75bn from the 3Q level. It would appear that the BSP continues to be on the sidelines so far into 2021. This is evidenced by their allowing the 10-year yields to rise sharply in Feb/Mar following the jump in inflation. For contrast, the BSP was the “not-so-invisible hand” in April and June when market rates started moving up. The BSP has publicly admitted the limits of monetary easing, and is likely wary, given that the monetary expansion has not (yet) been complemented with fiscal spending. And despite all the additional liquidity, loan growth has not re-started and has in fact contracted. What does this mean for equities? Without continued liquidity infusions from the BSP, the stock market’s performance will have to be driven by external flows. And with the lockdowns and slow rollout of the vaccine, we could likely be “range-bound” until the macro/earnings recovery are more apparent… hopefully in 3/4Q.

 

January FDI rises 42%. Net FDI jumped 41.5% YoY to US$961mn while debt instruments rose by +116% to US$535mn. Equity other than reinvestment of earnings grew +0.5% to US$351mn while reinvestment of earnings dropped -9.2% to US$74mn. Investments mostly came from Singapore, Japan, and Netherlands. Investments were mostly made to financial, manufacturing, and scientific and technical industries.

US$541mn hot money exits in March. Foreign portfolio investments posted a net outflow of US$540.97mn in March. This was 44% smaller YoY but was up MoM (vs. US$40.41mn in February 2021). The March outflow was the biggest in 10 months.

February NPL ratio highest in 11 years. The nonperforming loan (NPL) ratio rose to 4.08% in February, highest since October 2009 (4.09%). Gross NPLs surged 80% to P431bn (+9.05% MoM). Meanwhile, NPL coverage ratio dropped to 86.64% from 91.2% last year. The past due loans in February reached P551.472bn (+71% YoY), bringing the past due loan ratio to 5.21% from 2.95% a year ago. Restructured loans also surged by more than six times (346% YoY) to P200.986bn. With this, its ratio reached 1.9%, up from the 0.41% in February last year.

Bank lending slows further in February. Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, fell by 2.7% YoY, 0.1% MoM in February. Credit activity eased further as demand for loans remained soft. The consumer loans fell 8.3% in February due to the continued decline in credit card and motor vehicle loans as well as the slowdown in salary-based consumption loans. Outstanding loans to major industries continued to decline, particularly to wholesale and retail trade. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose by 9.4% YoY to P14.0tn in February, faster than the 8.9% (revised) growth in January.

Vehicle sales fall 21% MoM in March. Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, fell 21% MoM to 20,702 units (+88% YoY). MoM drop due to the imposition of provisional safeguard duties on imports and renewed lockdown restrictions. This brings 1Q21 industry car sales up 9% YoY to 70,312 units. CAMPI expects industry sales to grow by +20-25% YoY in 2021, slower than its initial projection of +30-35% YoY due to the provisional safeguard duty. Toyota sold 9,201 units, down 30% MoM but up 103% YoY. Its market share dropped 6ppts MoM to 44%. Meanwhile, Toyota’s 1Q21 retail sales are up 29% YoY to 33,095. Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year. To reach our forecast, Toyota will need to sell c. 9,400 units per month. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales should average 7,900 units. Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 13% discount to its market-based NAV of P604/sh.

President Duterte lifts moratorium on mining projects. President Duterte lifted the nine-year moratorium on new mineral agreements, in a bid to boost revenues as the country recovers from the pandemic. The Executive Order (EO) No. 130 allows the government to enter into new mineral agreements and review existing mining deals for possible renegotiation. The Finance department and DENR were ordered to implement measures to “rationalize existing revenue-sharing schemes and mechanisms. Mining accounts for c. 1% of GDP or approximately 5% of exports.

Duterte certifies three economic bills as urgent.  President Rodrigo R. Duterte certified as urgent three bills aimed at easing foreign investment restrictions in the country. The three bills are the Public Service Act (PSA), Foreign Investments Act (FIA) and the Retail Trade Liberalization (RTL) Act. The Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) expects the bills to be passed before Congress adjourns in June. Congress is currently in recess and will resume session on May 17 until June 4. An urgent certification from the President means these bills can be passed on the third reading immediately after the second reading. President Duterte will not certify as urgent the proposed third stimulus law and will instead work to identify sources of stimulus funds that will not bust the 8.9% budget deficit cap.

Duterte signs EO lowering tariffs on pork imports for one year. President Duterte signed EO No. 128 on Wednesday, temporarily lowering the Most Favored Nation tariff rates on imported pork meat for one year to address the currently supply shortage due to African Swine Fever. This comes after the President asked Congress last month to increase the minimum access volume (MAV) for pork imports this year by 350k MT on top of the 54k MT under the current limit. The EO temporarily lowers the import duties on fresh, chilled or frozen pork to 5% from 30% under the MAV quota for three months after the order takes effect. The rate will be increased to 10% for the succeeding nine months. The tariff rate for imported meat outside the existing quota on the other hand is reduced to 15% from 40% for the first three months. It will then be raised to 20% for the next nine months. Meanwhile, Agriculture Secretary William Dar said that price caps for selected pork and chicken products will expire after April 8. DA will instead implement suggested retail prices for imported pork products.

Gov’t expects US$4bn in mining investments. The government expects over US$4bn in capex from projects of Sagittarius Mines, KingKing Mining Corp., and Silangan Mindanao according to Presidential Spokesperson Herminio “Harry” L. Roque. The new mining projects are also expected to generate P40bn in local taxes and P20bn worth of social development projects. Meanwhile, the indigenous groups are also expected to benefit with around P15bn in royalties expected to be collected from the major mining projects. The President lifted the moratorium on new mining projects as Republic Act No. 10963, or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Act, has already doubled the excise tax on minerals from 2% to 4%.

 

Philippines to receive 7mn to 8 mn COVID vaccine doses in May. The country’s National Task Force against COVID-19 chief implementer and “vaccine czar” Carlito Galvez Jr. said that the Philippines might receive 7 to 8mn COVID vaccine doses in May due largely to more deliveries from the COVAX facility. This is higher than the previously announced 3.5mn and this would imply that by end-May the Philippines would have received a total of 11.7mn to 12.7mn COVID vaccine doses vs the previous announcement was 8.2mn. The government previously said 140mn doses would arrive by end-2021 to inoculate 70mn Filipinos. The country has received 3mn doses and has administered 1.62mn doses.

Another 500,000 Sinovac doses arrive. The Philippines received another 500,000 doses of Sinovac COVID vaccine. The country has received a total 4mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 1.8mn doses. The government expect 7mn to 8mn doses to arrive in May. It previously said 140mn doses would arrive by end-2021 to inoculate 70mn Filipinos.

Duterte extends ECQ in Metro Manila, nearby provinces. President Duterte extended the stringent lockdown enforced in the National Capital Region and the provinces of Bulacan, Rizal, Laguna, and Cavite until April 11. ECQ was supposed to end on April 4. The gov’t will decide in a week if another extension is needed. The economic managers will meet on April 8 to review growth targets.

MECQ in NCR Plus extended until May 14. President Duterte extended the Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine (MECQ) in Metro Manila and neighboring provinces until May 14 to ease pressure on hospitals that were at near breaking point. The quarantine status is subject to the appeals of the local government units. Meanwhile, Metro Manila mayors have agreed to shorten curfew hours to 10pm to 4am on May 1 (previously 8pm to 5am). To give a “breathing spell” for businesses in Metro Manila, adding more time for malls and other establishments to operate.

NEDA: P83bn wages lost due to stricter lockdowns.  The National Economic and Development Authority estimates P83.3bn in income was lost during the nearly five weeks of stricter lockdown in Manila. Around P19.6bn in workers’ income is sacrificed for every week of enhanced community quarantine (ECQ), while P14.7bn is lost for every week of modified ECQ (MECQ). Last Dec, the gov’t estimated the 2021 nominal GDP to settle at P19.985tn. In February, around 9.3mn jobs were generated, although the unemployment rate rose to 8.8% vs 8.7% the month prior. The gov’t is targeting to bring down the unemployment rate to 4-5% next year. Meanwhile, Labor Secretary Silvestre Bello said the number of OFWs who lost their jobs due to the pandemic reached 647,827 (19% of the 3.5mn documented OFWs).

IATF allows indoor dine-in, personal care services in areas under MECQ. Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) has cleared indoor dine-in services at 10% capacity. Additionally, personal care services like barbershops and salons can reopen at 30% capacity. Establishments offering dine-in services and personal care services might operate beyond the said limitations provided they comply with the Safety Seal Certification Program. This will apply to areas under MECQ: Metro Manila, Rizal, Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, Santiago City in Quirino province and Abra province.
