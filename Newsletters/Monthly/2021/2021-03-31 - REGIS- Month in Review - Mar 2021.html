REGIS REPORT

31 March 2021
 

The Philippines lost 5.2% MoM in March as record COVID cases sent the market reeling. Hopes of reopening were dashed by the spike in COVID cases in the last weeks of March to some 10k new COVID cases a day (coming from around 2000/day in Jan/Feb). For 1Q, the Philippines is down 9.8%.

The spike elicited a tightening response that took the capital and four neighboring provinces (NCR-Plus) back to the strictest lockdown (Enhanced Community Quarantine) until April 4 --  with expectations of a possible extension not far-fetched. The lockdown restricts movement into and out of NCR-Plus, imposes a 6pm-5am curfew, limits transportation, closes retail establishments (excluding essentials), and limits restaurant operations to delivery and take-away. Meanwhile, President Duterte approved P23bn US$480m worth of aid for lower-income families in the NCR-Plus bubble. 

A total of 2.5m COVID vaccine doses have arrived in the country.  Inoculation has been slow, however, with only 656,000 doses (as of 27 March) administered since the start of the month.  While this pace has quickened, the government’s target of 500k-1m per week (and inoculating 70% of the population) would take longer than hoped.

Inflation rose to 4.7% in February, up from 4.2% in January. This was the highest monthly CPI print in over two years. However, as expected, the BSP held policy rates steady at 2.0% during its latest Monetary Board meeting. While inflation is indeed driven by supply-shocks, CPI may remain elevated in the coming months as commodity prices continue to rebound and may compel the BSP to act.

Nonetheless, the 10-year BVAL rate now stands at 4.408%, up from 3.893% in end-Feb and 2.93% in end-Jan. The government’s latest P30bn 10-yr bond issuance was at a rate of 4.614%, well above the 3.066% rate they paid last month. 

Needless to say, the new round of tightening does not augur well for economic activity. With mobility data already trending down post the holidays, a prolonged ECQ would likely push back the recovery story will into next year. Recent bottom-up data --loan growth -2.7%, toll road traffic -13%, electricity sales -4% --  suggest another negative GDP print in 1Q21.

The CREATE (Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises) Act was signed into law on 26 March, cutting corporate income tax rates from 30% to 25%. Apart from the lower tax rate, the Act simplifies tax administration, and rationalizes tax incentives given to different industries and companies. Also, the negative impact on exporters and BPOs seem manageable. Simplistically, this would lift NPAT forecasts 7% (though the tax cut is retroactive to 2H20). Deferred tax assets/liabilities and the retroactive (2H20) feature could create a lot of “noise” for 2021E profits.

FLOW UPDATE

Foreign outflows continued on in March, with US$483m exiting this month – bringing 1Q net foreign outflows to US$946m (already close to 40% of last year’s outflow). Foreign selling was consistent throughout the month, with only two trading days seeing marginal inflows. 

March saw some outsized blocks that skewed foreign flows. A PLDT block transfer registered US$205m outflows. Meanwhile, the listing of Double Dragon Meridien Park (DDMP) REIT registered some US$19.7m inflows as some foreign funds participated in the deal (selling shareholders were local).  

Foreign participation remained light at 35% (excluding a US$$500m JFC local transfer). 

ICTSI (+2.4% MoM)  and Metrobank (-5.1% MoM) were the only two large-caps that saw decent foreign inflows. ICT saw foreign buying after 4Q numbers showed a strong rebound in volumes across its portfolio. 

Meanwhile, Metrobank saw follow through foreign buying even after the stock went ex-cash for Php 4/sh (8% yield). Metrobank’s position after nearly a year of the pandemic has sparked buying interest. As of end-2020, it had the lowest NPL coverage, and the highest capital ratios amongst the Big 3 even post a chunky dividend declaration.

Large outflows were seen across the most liquid, large conglos (SM AC JGS MPI) and banks (BPI BDO SECB) as foreigners continue to lighten exposure in the Philippines.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Index decliners outnumber gainers 21:9 in March as the spike in COVID cases once again threatened to put economic activity to a halt. Meanwhile, 4Q results continued, and for the most part, COVID and resultant lockdowns continued to take their toll on the numbers.

Market proxies large index names declined - with Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-18.1% MoM), JG Summit (-7.3% MoM), Metro Pacific (-8.1% MoM), GT Capital (-6.7% MoM) and SM Investments (-4.9% MoM) all ending the month lower. Cyclicals were also notably down with Ayala Land (-12.0% MoM) , Robinsons Land (-8.7% MoM) , BPI (-8.4% MoM), Security Bank (-7.6% MoM) and Metrobank (-12.4% MoM) among the big decliners.

With added restrictions and limited mobility, casino stock Bloomberry (-13.0% MoM) declined markedly while restaurants such as Shakey's Pizza (-1.8% MoM), Max’s (-16.0% MoM), and Jollibee (-1.2% MoM) corrected.

Meanwhile, investors flocked to  defensive names,  with Puregold (+6.2% MoM) topping the index leaders list in March. PGOLD delivered a beat in 4Q20, with S&R driving topline growth with its SSSG of c. 13%. 

Consumer name Universal Robina (+4.2% MoM) claimed second spot. Other consumer names Century Pacific (+2.7% MoM), Emperador (+2.5% MoM) and retailer Robinsons Retail (+1.2% MoM) likewise advanced. Utilities Meralco (+0.4% MoM) and  First Gen (+1.0% MoM) also occupied the index gainers list.

Semirara (+6.4% MoM) gained as soaring coal (Newcastle ) prices present significant upside risk to Semirara’s earnings (and dividends).  The company also declared a dividend of P1.25/sh translating to 10 % yield.  

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation climbs to 4.7% in February. Inflation accelerated further to 4.7% in February from 4.2% the previous month within the government’s monthly forecast range of 4.3%-5.1%. This is the highest inflation posted since January 2019. Food inflation continued to creep up (+6.7%), driven by meat (+20.7%) and rice (+0.5%); the latter was attributed to the end of the harvest season. Higher fuel prices also drove inflation higher, with transport inflation accelerating +10.4% YoY and +0.6% MoM. The MoM, inflation decelerated to +0.2% from +1.3% in January which was due to the deceleration in meat inflation (attributed to price ceilings imposed on pork and chicken products in the National Capital Region). This brings YT-Feb average inflation rate to 4.5%, above the government’s target range of 2-4%.

BSP maintains policy rates. The Monetary Board kept all monetary policy levers unchanged, with the overnight reverse repurchase facility at 2.0%. The decision was made despite their newest inflation forecast, which was raised to 4.2% for 2021 (from 4% previously). The new inflation forecast is above its target of 2-4%. BSP officials say they are able to keep the monetary policy accommodative because inflation drivers are largely supply side in nature and transitory. There is currently no evidence of any spillovers to other commodities apart from those that are subject to the supply shocks.

GIR reserves rise to US$109.1bn in end-February. The country’s dollar reserves rose to US$109.082bn in end-February (+0.38% MoM) after dipping slightly in January (US$108.7bn). This was higher by 23.7% YoY. The MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad (+2.4% MoM). End-February GIR is enough to cover 11.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to end the year at US$102bn, down from last year’s record level US$110.1bn.

Budget swings to P14bn deficit in January. The National Government posted a P14.1bn budget deficit for January 2021 which was a reversal of the P23bn surplus last year. This is partly due to a 12% YoY contraction in revenues. Tax revenues dropped 8% YoY, with BIR and BOC collections lower by 7% and 15%, respectively. Non-tax revenues dropped 29% YoY due to high base effects of dividend remittances from GOCCs last year. Meanwhile, expenditures rose 1.2% YoY to P275bn, tracking behind the government’s full-year target.

Spending picks up in February, Budget deficit balloons to P116bn. The government’s budget deficit ballooned to P116bn in February largely due to a rise in spending. Revenues were up 6% YoY, of which tax revenues grew 7%; Non-tax revenues dropped due to lower collections from PAGCOR and investment income. Meanwhile, expenditures rose YoY and MoM due to a P45bn release of equity to government financial institutions for various COVID-19 support programs under Bayanihan II. This brings 2M21 budget deficit to P130bn.

President Duterte vetoes some provisions of CREATE. President Duterte signed the Tax Package Two (also called CREATE), though he vetoed certain provisions. The corporate income tax rate will drop. For small business – from 30% to 20% (retroactively from July 2020). For other companies – from 30% to 25% (retroactively from July 2020). It will also streamline the government’s fiscal incentives system to make it time-bound. New export projects (including BPOs) – can avail of incentives for 14-17 years (from perpetuity currently). The existing export projects (including existing BPO operations) – can continue to avail of existing incentives for another 10 years.

 

 

BSP raises BOP forecast. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas raised its Balance of Payments surplus target this year to US$6.2bn (=1.6% of GDP) and cited the improvement in imports and exports as a reason for the upgrade. The BSP maintained OFW remittance growth forecast at +4% while tourism receipts are expected to rise by +15% this year. The current account surplus was upgraded to US$9.1bn (2.3% of GDP). In 2020, the BOP surplus stood at US$16bn (4.4% of GDP), with the current account surplus at US$13bn (3.6% of GDP). The BOP surplus projected to stabilize to US$3.8bn (0.9% of GDP) in 2022. The GIR is also expected to reach US$114bn this year and US$117bn in 2022. The GIR stood at US$110bn in end-December 2020 and US$109bn in end-Feb 2021.

BOP posts US$2.02bn deficit in February; end-Feb GIR revised down to US$105bn. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$2.02bn in February 2021. This is a reversal from the US$839mn surplus recorded in Feb 2020 and a larger BOP deficit than in January 2021 (US$752mn). This brings YT-Feb BOP to a deficit of US$2.771bn (vs. deficit of US$516mn in the same period last year). The final gross international reserves level for end-February stood at US$105.16bn, lower than the preliminary figure of US$109.1bn. This is lower than in end-January 2021 of US$108.67bn. The buffer is equivalent to 12 months’ worth of imports.

GIR reserves rise to US$109.1bn in end-February. The country’s dollar reserves rose to US$109.082bn in end-February (+0.38% MoM) after dipping slightly in January (US$108.7bn). This was higher by 23.7% YoY. The MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad (+2.4% MoM). End-February GIR is enough to cover 11.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to end the year at US$102bn, down from last year’s record level US$110.1bn.

Remittances drop 1.7% to US$2.6bn in January. OFW remittances fell 1.7% YoY and 10% MoM to US$2.6bn in January which means in PHP terms, remittances dropped 7% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate was at -0.6%. Remittances from the USA grew 12% YoY but this was more than offset by declines from other regions. The BSP expects remittances to rise by 4% this year, after dipping -0.8% in FY20.

Treasury sells P30bn in 10-year reissued bonds. Bureau of the Treasury raised P30 bn from reissued 10-year bonds; bond fetched a rate of 4.614%. Bid rates ranged between a 4.35% and a high of 4.7% (BVAL: 4.4789%). This was higher than the coupon fetched last month, 3.066%.

National Government debt rises to P10.4tn in February.  The National Government’s total outstanding debt rose to a new record of P10.405tn in February. Around 71% are domestic borrowings while 29% was sourced externally. This is equivalent to 52% of GDP, and still below the internationally acceptable threshold of 60% of GDP. The government expects the ratio of debt-to-GDP to rise to 57% by end-2021, up from 54.5% in 2020.

Imports flat MoM, exports shrink in January. Preliminary trade data showed imports unchanged MoM to US$2.34bn in January 2020. Non-electronic imports dropped slightly while imports of electronics rose 2%. Meanwhile, Fuel imports rose +6% MoM, Capital goods (-5% MoM), Raw material imports (+6% MoM), Consumer goods (-3% MoM). Meanwhile, exports dipped slightly by 4% MoM, led by a 10% drop in electronic exports. The trade deficit stood at US$2.42bn in January.

Infrastructure spending surges MoM in December, exceeds FY20 target. The government spent P132.3bn in December 2020 (-25.6% YoY), excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies. This brought FY20 infra spend to P681bn (-28% YoY), surpassing the government’s P609.3bn target. The govt cited better-than-expected accomplishments by the Dept. of Public Works. This took the government’s overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in 2020 to P869.5bn (-17% YoY), surpassing the target of P825bn. For FY21, the government is targeting to spend P1.17tn on infrastructure (+35% YoY).

 

 

Metro Manila and adjacent provinces on stricter lockdown. Metro Manila, Bulacan, Rizal, Cavite, and Laguna have been placed on Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ), the tightest lockdown from March 29 until April 4. There will also be a curfew from 6PM to 5AM. Only one member per household is allowed to leave the home to procure essential goods. Only essential industries (such as medical services) are allowed to operate at full capacity. Most businesses are only allowed to have a skeleton workforce on site (or no workforce at all). Meanwhile, groceries, pharmacies, and hardware stores are allowed to operate in malls while restaurants can only serve delivery and take-out. Presidential Spokesperson Harry Roque said that the government will provide financial aid. Public transport allowed to operate at 50% of capacity 24 hours a day, following one-seat-apart rule.

Gov’t to distribute cash aid for areas under ECQ. Budget Secretary Wendel Avisado said 22.9mn poor Filipinos in Metro Manila, Bulacan, Cavite, Rizal and Laguna are set to get the aid. The group represents 80% of low-income population in the area. The assistance will be lower than what the government gave under Bayanihan I. Assistance will be P1,000 worth of in-kind aid per person, but no more than P4,000 for each family (previously, households were given P5k-8k). The funds will be sourced from the P23bn unreleased funds under Bayanihan II.

Philippines receives another 1mn doses of COVID vaccine. The Philippines received another 1mn doses of COVID-19 vaccine of Sinovac. The country has now received a total of 2.5mn doses. As of March 27, 656,000 doses have been administered.

Bank lending declines 2.4% in January; Domestic liquidity rises 9%. Loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, decreased by 2.4% YoY in January. This is slower than the 0.7% drop in December. Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-7.4%), trade (-6.9%) dropped further. Meanwhile, consumer loans (credit cards and auto) collapsed by 6.9% YoY (vs. +4.1% in December). Credit cards fell 10% while auto loans dropped 5.8%. Money supply grew by 9% in January, slower than the 9.5% growth (revised) in December.

Vehicle sales rise 12% MoM in February. Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI and TMA), jumped 12% MoM in February to 26,230 units. The MoM recovery was attributed to improvements in sales of Asian utility vehicles and light trucks. This was however 12% lower YoY due to disruptions caused by the Taal volcano eruption last year. This brings YT-Feb industry vehicle sales  down by 7% YoY to 49,610 units. Toyota sold 13,074 units in February, up 6% YoY and 21% MoM and brings YT-Feb unit sales to 23,894 units (+13% YoY). The dealerships began implementing the safeguard duty in March so we expect sales to plunge. Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively. GT Capital is trading at a 14% discount to its market-based NAV of P636/sh.

Gov’t to sign a new agreement with Manila Water this month. The Department of Justice is finalizing the government’s new concession agreement with Manila Water according to Justice Secretary Menardo Guevarra.

Manila Goldcoast Development secures approvals to reclaim in Manila Bay. The Philippine Reclamation Authority (PRA), with the approval of the Office of the President, has issued a Notice to Mobilize and a Notice to Commence Actual Reclamation Works for the 148 hectare Solar City Manila. Solar City Manila is a project of the City of Manila and Manila Goldcoast Development Corp (MGDC). The project will be beside the Cultural Center of the Philippines and behind Manila Yacht Club and Philippine Navy Headquarters. MGDC is part of the Solar Group of the Tieng family, which has business interests in entertainment, communications, and real estate. SM Prime has already received the same approvals for its 360 hectare reclamation project in Pasay.
