REGIS REPORT

29 October 2021
 

The Philippines closed up 1.5% in October, fueled by re-opening optimism.

The drop in infection rates boosted sentiment, as daily new case counts plunged to 4660 (seven-day moving average as of 28 Oct) vs the peak of ~21k sometime September. Latest case counts of below 4000 on a single day is the first time new daily cases plunged below 4,000 in over 3 months. 

Vaccination rates also moved higher, with  a quarter of the country’s total population now fully vaccinated, up from 19% in September. The proportion in Metro Manila is 62% (from 52% in Sep).  Average vaccines administered per day also improved to around 450k/day (as of Oct 28) from the 396k/day in the previous month. The Department of Health hopes to increase the pace of vaccinations to 800k daily in November. The government is soon expanding the vaccination of minors nationwide, after allowing vaccination of the general adult population and minors in specific areas.

From Alert Level 4,  Metro Manila’s lockdown classification was eased to  Alert Level 3 in the last two weeks of October, allowing most business and services to operate at increased capacities.  Universities can now have in-person schooling for the first time since March 2020 and “pilot tests” for primary/secondary levels are set to start in November. The government is signaling further easing for Metro Manila to Alert Level 2 if COVID cases continue to come down.  

Travel restrictions across the country have been slowly eased as well. The government  allowed vaccinated individuals to travel on point-to-point basis, regardless of age. The IATF also eased quarantine restrictions for Filipinos and foreigners arriving from the “green lane” countries. Meanwhile, over ten of the top domestic destinations no longer require swab tests to enter.  

Already,  mobility data (Google) is already showing that mobility is on the rise even as further easing measures are expected. Mobility is nearing the highest since the initial outbreak and lockdowns. The Dept of Transportation (DoTr) is also proposing to increase the allowed maximum passenger capacity of public transport vehicles. If implemented, this would further reduce constraints on local movement.

The 3Q results season has kicked off.  Banks are seeing loan demand slowly returning and asset quality remaining manageable. Electricity volumes are still somewhat soft. The hard lockdown in August is expected to lead to softer toplines for 3Q though continued easing sohuld lend more optimisim for 4Q. 

Inflation decelerated to 4.8% for September, as softening food prices helped offset rising energy prices. Over the near-term, skyrocketing energy prices are likely to keep CPI inflation elevated.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Headline foreign inflows in October stood at US$8m. This month though saw the Megaworld REIT IPO which clocked in US$60.5m in inflows and a Union Bank block which registered US$47.5m in inflows. Excluding these, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$100m. Foreign participation fell to 34% (from 38% last month).


Biggest outflows were in recent winners and index inclusion bets Monde Nissin and Converge. The two were among last month’s best performers amid speculation of inclusion into the MSCI in November, but it appears those bets have reversed.

Wilcon also saw foreign profit taking after gaining up to 27% MTD in the wake of being added into the PSEi.

Foreigners were two-way banks as they were seen selling BPI while buying BDO and Metrobank. 

Inflows were focused in reopening plays such as Jollibee and cyclicals Ayala Land and SM Prime. Meanwhile, decent inflows into Semirara and parent DMCI contineud as coal prices remain near all time high levels.

 

 

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

The market’s rally was led by the cyclicals – with BDO (+12.2% MoM) and Ayala Land (+5.2% MoM) chipping in 51 and 26 index points respectively to the market’s overall 102-index point rise in October.

Other banks Security Bank (+14.1% MoM), Metrobank (+9.15% MoM), and BPI (+6.75% MoM ) were also up sharply as well. 

Banks were among the first to release 3Q results and provisions were the main feature of performance. BPI’s 3Q21 came in behind forecasts, with the big jump in provisions (+31% QoQ) the main drag.  NPLs however fell to  2.76% (-21bs QoQ) and NPL cover rose to 131% (from120% in 2Q).  On the flipside, BDO beat estimates and this came nearly entirely from lower provision expense. Credit cost for 9M came in at roughly 60bps -- lower than BPI’s (~100bps) and MBT’s (~120bps as of 1H).  NPLs were flat QoQ at 3.1% while NPL cover crept up to 101% (from 100% in 2Q). Both banks reported loan growth – 5% for BDO and 1% for BPI.

Property names were likewise up despite office vacancies rising to 17% in 3Q (from 13% in 2Q21).  The property space was largely uneventful, though there was news news on Ayala Land and Megaworld (+7.7% MoM) announcing plans for additional asset infusions into their respective REITs. Robinsons Land (+6.25% MoM) and SM Prime (+1.1% MoM) closed the month up as well.  

Select consumer names also surged.  Prospects of continued easing pushed Jollibee (+14.7% MoM) and Robinsons Retail (+20.2% MoM) significantly higher.

Century Pacific Foods (+5.6% MoM) rose steadily. The company announced its foray into the pet food category and is also introducing a new item (powdered chocolate malt drink) to its dairy product line.

Rotation from recent strong outperformer Wilcon (-10.7% MoM) was apparent this month, despite the company reporting a solid set of 3Q results.  While the hard lockdown in August caused sales revenue to dip 2% in 3Q, margins were nonetheless robust, helping drive net profits up 17%.

Wilcon leaped earlier in the month on speculation and eventual inclusion in the PSEi.  Wilcon was added into the index to replace First Gen (-11.3% MoM) which was removed due to its public float falling below the 15% minimum for PSEi constituents.  It’s public float fell to 11.4% after KKR- controlled Philippines Clean Energy Holdings  completed its tender offer for FGEN shares.

Monde Nissin (-14.4% MoM) saw profit taking after a surge fueled by index inclusion speculation. 

 

Reopening plays Bloomberry (+14.5% MoM) and Cebu Air (+13.8% MoM) surged. The airline announced that over 10 domestic destinations no longer require expensive RT-PCR or Antigen swab tests for fully vaccinated passengers. 

ICTSI (-7.7% MoM) gave up some of its previous strong gains, succumbing to profit taking. The coup in Sudan raised uncertainty, as ICTSI has yet to recover EUR180m out of the total deposits it made to take over a port in the country. However, provisions here would have a non-cash impact; Regis’ valuation already assumes that the balance will not be recovered.

Meanwhile, the Department of Justice (DOJ) plans to indict Megawide’s (-1.5% MoM) senior officers, alleging violation of the Anti-Dummy Law. The charges claim that MWIDE allowed foreign nationals to intervene in the “management, operations, administration of control” of the Mactan Airport. It is unclear what the consequences of the DOJ case might be, and whether it could mean the forfeiture of MWIDE’s contract. 

Last month’s top index gainer Converge (-20.5% MoM) was this month’s top decliner.  The stock corrected from its surge last month on the back of its inclusion in the FTSE index.  The stock also corrected after the company disclosed that the lockup of a significant shareholder expired during the month. PLDT (-2.3% MoM) and Globe (+0.4% MoM) consolidated this month after large gains driven by their respective fintech units.

 

Utilities were mostly up.

Manila water (+42.5% MoM) climbed markedly as positive news flows on the water franchise bills and two acquisitions helped lift investor sentiment. The former could potentially change the regulation of the East zone concession. Meanwhile, the impact of the acquisitions in Pangasinan and Saudi Arabia are estimated to be minimal but would give  MWC a point of entry in these locations.

AC Energy (+8.7% MoM) also rose.  The company is buying out its partner in its Australian solar project,  and is also raising its interests in three wind farms by acquiring its partners’ stakes.  

In the meantime, Meralco (-3.7% MoM) announced fairly good 3Q results despite electricity volumes still soft (up +3% YoY). Core income for the quarter came in at P6.66bn, up 30%, with rising contributions from its non-regulated subsidiaries. YT-Sept core income was P18.1bn, up 15%.

Semirara (+23.8% MoM) shot up this month, as coal prices remained elevated.  The stock is now up 89%% YTD.

Parent DMC (+15.3% MoM) surged as well. 

Among the other holdcos,  GT Capital (+10.8% MoM) also outperformed. With its Japan-based partners Mitsui and Premium Group, GT Capital launched Premium Warranty Services Phils (PWSPI).  GT Capital, through its wholly-owned unit GTCAD, will effectively own 40% of PWSPI.

Toyota Motor Phils sold 9,929 units in September (-7% YoY, +27% MoM). This brings YT-September sales to 92,318 units (+46% YoY). Toyota is targeting 130k units (+30% YoY) this year. 

SM Investments (-2.5% MoM) consolidated while Ayala Corp (+5.6% MoM) closed the month with gains.   

 

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

September inflation slows to 4.8%.  Inflation slowed slightly to 4.8% in September from 4.9% in August due to decelerating inflation of food (particularly meat, fish) and transport. Consensus estimate at 5.1%; Came in at the low-end of the BSP’s target range of 4.8%-5.6% for the month. September inflation is higher than the 10-year treasury yield of 4.69%. YT-September inflation averaging at 4.5%, still above the BSP’s 2-4% target this year.

OFW remittances rises 5.1% YoY in August.  Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 5.1% YoY in August but dropped 8.6% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances are up 8.1% YoY and the 3-month moving average growth rate is now at +4.8%. Additionally, YTD, remittances are up 5.7% YoY to US$20.38bn. The BSP looking at remittances reaching US$31.7bn (+6% YoY).

BOP position reverts to deficit in September. The country’s Balance of Payments stood at a deficit of US$412mn reversing the surplus worth US$2.104bn a year earlier and US$1.044bn in August due to outflows from the National Government’s debt service payments and a higher trade deficit. The BOP data could improve in October/November following proceeds from the US$1.6bn retail dollar bond issuance and other fund-raising activities. This brings 9M21 BOP deficit to US$665mn (vs last year’s surplus of US$6.878bn). The BSP is targeting a BOP surplus of US$4.1bn in 2021. 

Trade deficit expands further in August.  Preliminary data showed that total external trade rose 25% YoY to US$16.51bn in August, faster than the 23% growth rate in July and a turnaround from last year’s contraction. Exports rose 18% YoY, marking its sixth straight month of growth. Meanwhile imports rose 31% YoY, slightly faster than the previous month (+30%). Trade deficit expanded to US$3.58bn. The 8M21 trade deficit rises 64% YoY to US$26bn.

 

“Hot money” posts net outflow in September.  Foreign portfolio investments (or “hot money”) posted a net outflow of US$24.16mn in September reversing the US$11.51mn net inflow in August 2021 but lower than the US$494mn net outflow posted in September 2020. Majority of investments (or 74%) registered were in PSE-listed securities while the remainder went to investments in Peso government securities. This brings YT-September net outflows to US$459mn, lower than the US$4.4bn net outflows in the same period last year.

Infrastructure spending rises 60% YoY in August. Gov’t infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 60% YoY to P70.9bn. On a MoM basis, infra spend slipped by 3%. The govt cited spending for public works and airport projects, as well as defense and education. This brings YT-August infra spend (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P570bn, up 44.6% YoY. This accounts for 75% of the government’s FY21E target of P761bn (+12% YoY). Meanwhile, the YT-August overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 38% YoY to P718bn. This accounts for 70% of the government’s FY21E target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY). For FY22, the government has programmed to spend P1.27tn on infra (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), up 25% YoY.

Dept. of Transportation eyes fuel subsidies to stop fare hike. The Department of Transportation (DOTr) on Monday said it was looking at fuel subsidies and direct aid to both public utility drivers and passengers. Looking at possible uniform discounts on public utility vehicles (PUVs) across all gas stations nationwide. PUVs are allowed to carry half of its maximum passenger capacity. PUV drivers have sought fare hikes but Transport Secretary Tugade has dismissed the petition. The last time the LTFRB approved a fare hike was in 2018, when diesel prices soared to P53 a liter. Transport costs account for 8.1% of the CPI basket. Transport CPI is up 5.2% YoY in September 2021 and up 13.9% vs September 2019.

 

Budget gap widens to P181bn in September. The National Government recorded a budget deficit of P181bn in September, up 31% YoY and 50% MoM. Tax revenues in September were 1% above September 2019 levels, bringing YT-September tax collections just 3% below 2019. Expenses sustained YoY, MoM improvement, partially boosted by a P10bn Coconut Farmers and Industry Trust Fund transfer. The YT-September budget deficit reached P1.1tn, tracking below the government’s FY21E due to lower-than-programmed interest payments and underspending.

PH raises US$1.6bn via retail dollar bonds. The government raised US$1.593bn (P80.9bn) from its first-ever onshore retail dollar bond (RDB) sale. The two-week offering ended last Friday with the government awarding nearly four times as much as the initial US$400mn target. The five-year bonds, which will mature in October 2026, had a coupon rate of 1.375%, while the 10-year bonds due in October 2031 fetched a 2.25% coupon.

Vehicle sales drop 12% YoY in September.  Industry car sales fell -12% YoY to 21,493 units in September according to CAMPI/TMA. On a MoM basis, car sales recovered +36% after quarantine restrictions in Metro Manila were lifted. YT-September industry car sales are up 29% YoY to 191,606 units (vs CAMPI’s FY21E of 295k units, +32% YoY). To reach CAMPI’s target, car sales should average 35k per month for the rest of the year. Toyota sold 9,929 units in September (-7% YoY, +27% MoM). This brings YT-September sales to 92,318 units (+46% YoY). To reach Toyota’s target of 130k units (+30% YoY), Toyota needs to sell 12.6k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 117k units (+17% YoY), Toyota needs to sell 8k units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit.

Pilot run of limited face-to-face classes to start Nov. 15. Pilot implementation of limited face-to-face classes will start on November 15 according to the Department of Education. The initial run will last until December 22, after which an assessment of the conduct of the in-person classes will be made. The pilot study will end on January 31, 2022. Once approved, the DepEd is planning to start the expanded pilot run of face-to-face classes on March 7, 2022. Duterte approved in September limited face-to-face classes in areas with minimal risk of Covid-19 infection.

Pace of vaccinations picks up. As of October 24, the 7-day average vaccinations rose to 487,398/day from 375,773/day as of October 17. The Department of Health hopes to increase the pace of vaccinations to 800,000/day in November. Around 24% of the Philippine population (25.7mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID and around 62% of the Metro Manila population (8.3mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 97.7mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 55.7mn doses (as of October 24). The country might administer booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities in November or December 2021.

DOTr backs proposal for 100% PUV capacity. The Department of Transportation (DOTr) is pushing its proposal to raise the passenger capacity in public transport back to 100%. The passenger capacity limit is currently at 50%. The increase will be implemented in Metro Manila. DOTr also requested the Department of Energy to grant P1bn in fuel subsidies to public transport drivers.

 

 

Vaccinations of minors to expand nationwide on November 3. The vaccination of children aged 12 to 17 will expand nationwide on November 3 around 18,666 children with comorbidities have been vaccinated in Metro Manila. The Department of Health estimates that there are 12.7mn children aged 12 to 17 in the Philippines or 12% of the total population. As of October 26, 24% of the Philippine population (26.2mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of October 25, 62% of the Metro Manila population (8.4mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 97.7mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 56.8mn doses (as of October 26). The government might administer booster shots/third doses for healthcare workers, senior citizens, and people with comorbidities in November or December 2021.

Government targeting to fully vaccinate 50% of population by February. The government hopes to fully vaccinate 50% of the total population by February. Around 22% of the Philippine population (24.5mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. The country is currently administering only 375,000/day. At the current pace, 50% of the population will be vaccinated by April. As of October 18, 59% of the Metro Manila population (7.9mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 91.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 52.8mn doses (as of October 18).

Gov’t eases domestic, international travel requirements.  The government is allowing fully vaccinated individuals regardless of age from Metro Manila to travel on a point-to-point (P2P) basis. The Department of Tourism will be releasing guidelines for P2P travel. The RT-PCR test requirements would depend on the protocols imposed by the destination. Meanwhile, the government reduced the quarantine days for fully vaccinated incoming travelers from “green” and “yellow” countries.

 

 

Metro Manila eases to Alert Level 3 until end-October. Metro Manila’s quarantine classification will be eased from Alert Level 4 to Alert Level 3 from October 16 to 31. Most business establishments, facilities, and services are allowed to open at varying degrees. Indoor venues allowed to operate at a max capacity of 30% for fully vaccinated; 50% outdoor. Additionally, cinemas are now allowed to open. Pre-COVID, cinemas comprised c. 10% of mall revenue. The government also released quarantine classifications for the rest of the Philippines, with restrictions for most cities/provinces eased.

Gov’t drops quarantine for fully vaccinated travelers from “green” countries. Fully vaccinated Filipinos and foreign nationals traveling from ‘green’ countries or territories tagged as low risk for COVID-19 will no longer be quarantined upon entry to the Philippines beginning Oct. 14. A negative RT-PCR test taken 72 hours before arrival is the only requirement; Filipinos can also choose to take the test upon arrival and quarantine until results are out. Unvaccinated or partially vaccinated individuals shall undergo a facility-based quarantine until the release of a negative RT-PCR test taken on the fifth day. However, inbound tourism still not allowed. Green countries as of October 8 include Mainland China and New Zealand, among others. Other key countries are not included in the list: South Korea, UK, US, Japan.

 

VP Robredo to run for President in 2022.  Vice President Leni Robredo announced her decision to run as President in the 2022 national elections. Robredo filed her certificate of candidacy yesterday as an independent. Senator Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan is Robredo’s vice presidential pick. The following have also filed certificates of candidacies for President are: Senator Panfilo Lacson, Former Senator Bongbong Marcos, Mayor Isko Moreno, Senator Manny Pacquiao, Leody de Guzman, and Norberto Gonzales. Friday, Oct. 8 is the last day of the filing of certificates of candidacy. The Comelec will release the tentative list of candidates by Oct 29. November 8 is the last day of corrections on the list of candidates to appear on official ballots.
