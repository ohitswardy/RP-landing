REGIS REPORT

01 June 2021
 

The Philippines gained 4.0% in May, arresting two straight months of declines. The PSEI had been down over 3% MTD intra-month going below the 6200 level but a two-day spike towards the end of the month reversed earlier losses.  

The market continued to weigh the pandemic situation impact on economic recovery. Things are, nevertheless, going in the right direction.   Cases are improving, with the 7-day average case count hovering at around 6,000 from a peak of over 11,000.  Hospital occupancy rates for Covid beds are also sharply down from their highs.  The government has moved the lockdown classification in NCR Plus to a looser “general community quarantine (GCQ) with heightened restrictions”. 

Meanwhile, the rate of vaccinations is now at around 145k/day – up manyfold from just around 35k/day at end-April.  Around 5.2m doses of vaccines have so far been administered.   However, the daily vaccination rate will have to rise much more dramatically for government targets to be reached by year-end (lowered to 50-60% of population).  After receiving 4m doses in May, the government expects to receive 12.5m doses in June and 15-20m per month after that.  Outside of vaccines arriving, the challenge of course is vaccine resistance.  According to a recent survey by the Social Weather Stations,  only a third of Filipinos are willing to be vaccinated.

In 1Q, GDP contracted 4.2%  YoY as the pandemic and lockdowns continued to have an impact.  The drop was steeper than Street predictions. Investment (i.e. capital formation) was the drag. 

The 1Q corporate earnings concluded in May and results were decidedly mixed.  Against Regis estimates, 46% came in ahead but 46% also came in behind. The trend is not much different  against consensus expectations. Going through the sectors, all banks under Regis coverage beat expectations on lower provisions. NPLs were surprisingly benign though margin compression was not uncommon. On the flipside, all property counters under coverage turned in a disappointing 1Q.  Weak residential revenue weighed on profitability while the mall side also underperformed as foot traffic remained weak. The consumer sector was a mixed bag. Restaurants and mall-based retailers continued to be impacted by quarantine restrictions (eg dine-in, public transport). Meanwhile, grocery retailers and food and beverage manufacturers showed resilience despite the high base given pantry stocking in March 2020.

The Monetary Board of the Bangko Sentral (BSP) kept policy rates unchanged at 2%, as expected. Local inflation is likely to accelerate over the near term however and coupled with increasing pressure for US rates to rise, calls to lift rates may also go up.

Key data
 
 
 

Foreign outflows continued on into May, with US$240m exiting – bringing year-to-date net foreign outflow to US$1.465bn. Foreign flows in May included US$41m inflows from SM Prime (-10.3% MoM) blocks as well as US$54m inflows from the AC Energy (+6.9% MoM) secondary offering. It also included US$121m outflows from an Ayala Corp block.

The last week of May saw the market spiking 7.7% heading into MSCI rebalancing despite an expected US$200m in outflows. MSCI rebalancing day itself saw the market jump 5.5% and only registered net outflows of US$18.5m as it appeared that pre-positioning unwinds more than countered most of the MSCI outflows.

Ayala Land (+6.8% MoM) was the month's top foreign sold name. The name has seen multiple foreign sellers for a couple of months now, which has been bought mostly by local funds as well as the company’s buy back program.

Foreign selling (though exacerbated by an MSCI down-weight) was spread across the Philippines’ major sectors with conglomerates -- SM Investments (+1.4% MoM), Ayala Corp (+7.15% MoM), and Metro Pacific (-1.7% MoM). Telcos Globe (unch), PLDT (+3.1% MoM) and banks (BPI +2.5% MoM), Security Bank (+5.6% MoM) all saw heavy outflows as foreigners continued to generally lighten their exposure in the country – a trend stretching for more than a year now. 

MSCI deletions Aboitiz Power (-1.3% MoM) and Megaworld (-3.5% MoM) were also among this month’s top foreign sold. They were also among the top laggards in May. 

ICTSI (+10.2% MoM) was the lone PSEi member that saw decent inflows in May. Foreigners flocked back into the name after the company reported strong 1Q21 results. 1Q volume growth accelerated 8% and yields jumped 7% while EBITDA margins hit an all time higher of 61%, with EBITDA expanding 25%.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation steady at 4.5% in April. Headline inflation settled at 4.5% in April, broadly the same as in March (4.5%) but faster than the inflation print a year ago (2.2%). This is lower than street forecast (4.7%) and within the 4.2%-5.0% target range of the BSP. Food inflation decelerated, despite another increase in meat inflation. Even with the reduction in pork tariffs, prices of pork still rose, as well as prices of chicken, and beef also increased. These were offset by decelerating inflation for rice, fruits, and vegetables. Additionally, transport costs continued to surge, as tricycle fares surged by +48% YoY, followed by fuel costs (+32% YoY) and jeepney fares (+6% YoY). This brings YT-April average inflation to 4.5%, above the government’s target range of 2-4% for the year..

Monetary Board keeps policy settings steady. The BSP’s Monetary Board decided to maintain the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight reverse repurchase facility at 2.0%. This is in line street expectations.

OFW remittances rise 4.9% YoY, 1.5% MoM in March. Remittances in March stood at US$2.51bn, up 4.9% YoY and 1.5% MoM. The 3-month moving average growth rate at +2.6% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances were up slightly (+0.1% YoY). This is a reversal from the 4.7% contraction in March 2020. Remittances from land-based OFWs sent US$1.9bn (+5% YoY) while sea-based remitted US$566mn (+4.5% YoY). This brings 1Q21 remittances up +2.6% YoY to US$7.6bn. The BSP expects remittances to rise by 4% this year, after dipping -0.8% in FY20. To reach the target, remittances would have to average US$2.6bn monthly for the balance of the year.

Budget deficit narrows to P44bn in April as spending slows. The National Government posted a narrower budget deficit of P44bn in April. The base effects distorted YoY figures due to last year’s hard lockdown and the social amelioration program.MoM, however, the deficit in April was substantially below deficits recorded in Feb and March due to (a) timing of income tax payments and (b) moderation of expenditures. This brings 4M21 deficit to P366bn, accounting for 26% of the government’s year-end deficit target (~9.4% of GDP).

Trade deficit narrows in March. 3.5mn COVID vaccine doses arrived from May 7 to 9. The breakdown is 1.5mn Sinovac doses and 2mn AstraZeneca doses (through the COVAX facility). The country has received a total of 7.5mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 2.3mn doses. The government expects at least 3.5mn more vaccine doses to arrive in May.

GIR rises to US$107.25bn in end-April. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by US$2.77bn to US$107.25bn as of end-April 2021. This reflected inflows that were mainly from the proceeds of the National Government’s (NG) ROP Global and Samurai Bond issuances. The buffer is equivalent to 12.3 months’ worth of imports.

Infrastructure spending jumps 57% MoM, 41% YoY in March. The government spent P87.7bn on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in March, up 57% MoM and 41% YoY. The government cited payment for completed and partially completed project of the Dept. of Public Works. This brings 1Q21 infra spend to P195.2bn, up 25% YoY. Bulk of the funds were spent on the construction of roads and foreign-assisted projects such as the Metro Manila Subway Project and the Davao City By-Pass Construction Project. The increase in infrastructure disbursements in 1Q21 of P39.1bn was enough to offset the P22.1bn reduction recorded in 1Q20. For FY21, the government is targeting to spend 35% more to P1.17tn on infrastructure (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies).

Bank lending contracts by 4.5% in March; M3 growth moderates to 8.3%. Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, further contracted to 4.5% in March from -2.7% in February. Consumer loans fell 10% YoY and 1.8% MoM while loans to retail and manufacturing sectors still soft. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) rose 8.3% to P14.2tn in March, moderating from the 9.4% expansion in February.

Industry car sales fall 14% MoM in April; Toyota sales drop 6% MoM. Vehicle sales reported by CAMPI/TMA dropped 14% MoM to 17,843 units in April. They cited the reimposition of tighter quarantine measures, as well as tighter bank lending and the safeguard duty as the reason for the weakness. This brings 4M21 industry car sales to 88,155 units (+36% YoY). CAMPI/TMA is forecasting industry sales to grow by +20-25% (with provisional duty) this year. Toyota sold 8,642 units in April, down 6% MoM and brings 4M21 retail sales to 41,737 units (+62% YoY). Our forecasts assume vehicle sales to rise by 17% YoY to 117k units this year. To reach our forecast, Toyota will need to sell c. 9,400 units per month. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). Meanwhile, to reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales should average 7,800 units. Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively.

Lower House passes Bayanihan III. The House of Representatives on Tuesday approved on second reading the proposed Bayanihan III, which provides funds amounting to P401bn. The approved version on the plenary floor provided for a slightly lower amount than the initial P405.6bn Bayanihan 3 proposal. Around P216bn will be allocated for the provision of cash aid of P2,000 per Filipino regardless of social status, which will be given in two installments. It will also include a one-time cash subsidy of P5k-10k for pandemic-affected households in areas under Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ). The House Committee on Appropriations earlier approved the measure despite the absence of a certification of availability of funds from the Bureau of the Treasury. A certification is a requirement for special appropriations bills under the Constitution. =

House approves economic charter change on second reading. The House of Representatives approved on second reading Resolution of Both Houses (RBH) No. 2. RBH No. 2 seeks to loosen constitutional foreign ownership restrictions on natural resources, public utilities, educational institutions, mass media companies, and advertising companies. The House of Representatives can approve RBH No. 2 on third reading next week. However, the Senate has not yet begun its deliberations on the matter. Should both houses of Congress approve RBH No. 2, a plebiscite would still be necessary.

Gov’t eases quarantine status in NCR+; shifts to “GCQ with heightened restrictions”. Metro Manila and 4 adjacent provinces will be under GCQ 'with heightened restrictions' from May 15 until the end of May. This means 18 to 65-year-old persons can leave their homes. Only essential travel in and out of NCR Plus allowed. 20% capacity for indoor dine-in in restaurants, 50% capacity for outdoor or al fresco dine-in dine-in, 30% capacity for outdoor tourist sites, 10% capacity for religious gatherings, gatherings for necrological services, wakes, inurnment, funerals. Meanwhile, outdoor non-contact sports, games, scrimmages outdoors will be allowed. Also, 30% capacity for personal care services which do not require the removal of masks, like salons, parlors, and beauty clinics.

COVID vaccine target may need to be adjusted. “Vaccine Czar” Carlito Galvez Jr. said that the government’s target to inoculate 70mn Filipinos by the end of the year would have to be tweaked. He now recommends prioritizing areas “that have economic and social importance”. He is now targeting 70% herd immunity in those areas (mostly urban centers). Now targeting 50mn to 70mn vaccine recipients - focused on those areas. The country has received 4.04mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 2.07mn doses.

Filipinos evenly split between being willing / uncertain / unwilling to be vaccinated. Last week, the Social Weather Stations (SWS) released a survery that showed only 32% of Filipinos are “willing” to get vaccinated against Covid-19. The balance was roughly evenly split between those who were “uncertain” (35%) and those who were “unwilling” (33%) to be inoculated. Of the 33% unwilling, the vast majority (26%) replied “surely not”. The main reason for the reluctance was fear of side effects (39% among “uncertain” and 30% among “unwilling”). Other reasons included doubts over the safety/effectivity, and (false) reports of fatalities as a result of taking the vaccine. The same survey showed that only half of Filipinos are “confident” in the thoroughness of the government’s evaluation of the vaccines. The figures are worrisome, as we believe a successful Covid-19 vaccination program is critical to the economy’s recovery. A stronger information / PR campaign will be needed to increase the population’s confidence in getting inoculated.
