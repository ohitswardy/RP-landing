REGIS REPORT

30 June 2021
 

The Philippines advanced 4.1% in June on reopening optimism.  YTD losses had gone to as wide as 13.65% (as of May 24) but lighter foreign outflows pared losses to 3.3% for 1H21.  

The turn in flow was in conjunction with the domestic COVID situation. From 5.2m at end May, the country has administered 10.1m doses of the vaccine.  Menawhile almost 20% of  residents of key economic center Metro Manila has been vaccinated.

The government is aiming to administer another 8-10m doses in July, translating to roughly 300k doses per day.  This is a marked improvement from 35k/day at end April and roughly 145k/day in end May.  At the latest pace, the country would hit around 65m doses by year-end (both 1st and 2nd), compared to a total population of ~110m.

The prospect of inoculation rates improving further is boosted by improved availability of vaccines, with the government expecting to receive 13.3m doses in July and 13.6m doses in August.   A signed agreement for 40m Pfizer doses would raise to around 157m the number of vaccine doses the Philippines is set to receive  -- enough to inoculate 70% of the population.  

Inflation for the month of May came in at 4.5%, still above the government’s band (2-4%). High prices of meat, especially pork, remain the biggest contributing factor. With measures to increase meat imports and increase supply, meat prices are likely to gradually come down and with them, headline CPI inflation.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) opted to continue maintaining record-low policy rates. The BSP does not seem to be leaning on lifting rates anytime soon.

FLOW UPDATE

Foreigners were net sellers in June by another US$80m, bringing YTD net foreign outflow to US$1.545bn. Though foreigners remained net sellers, the market was able to absorb it quite well as reflected by the 4.1% gain in June.

Foreign selling tapered off significantly month-on-month, with foreigners even starting the month as buyers – registering two consecutive weeks of inflow up until June 11. This was the first time the market saw consecutive weeks of foreign inflow since October 2020.

June included some oversized blocks that skewed net foreign flow. Blocks in SM Prime (+US$77m) and 2GO (+US$41m) registered inflows, while Manila Water (-US$11m) and Ayala Land (-US$134m) registered decent outflows. If these blocks were exlcuded, the broader picture on foreign flows would have remained largely the same wherein foreigners were sellers – but not nearly as much as the previous months.

 Foreign participation in June was at 44%.

SMPH saw headline net foreign inflow but was skewed by blocks that registered US$77m in net foreign buying. Adjuting for these, SMPH would have been top foreign sold alongside another property player Megaworld.

Meanwhile, telcos PLDT and Globe also saw heavy foreign outflows as foreigners were seen switching out of defensives into reopening plays Jollibee, GT Capital, and Wilcon.

Newly listed Monde Nissin was also among the top foreign sold names as some hedge funds and other foreign deal participants were seen selling post listing, only to be gobbled up by the stabilization fund (local) which disclosed to have bought 274 million shares.  

 

 

BDO and Metrobank finally saw foreign inflow after consecutive months of outflows, fueled by reopening optimism. 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Reopening plays dominated in June.

Jollibee (+10.7% MoM) surged, as its dine in operations are seen to be one of the best beneficiaries of a reopening economy. Restaurant Max’s (+7.9% MoM) likewise gained markedly during the month. Big box home improvement retailer Wilcon (+9.5% MoM) also gained markedly.

Puregold (+8.7% MoM), Robinsons Retail (+4.5% MoM ), and Universal Robina (+4.0% MoM) closed the month up as well.

Other consumer names moved despite a dearth developments. Emperador (+30.7% MoM) led the sector and the index in June. Sentiment was underpinned by the strong 43% rise in 1Q profits, boosted by strong whisky performance. Strong gin volumes likewise sustained sentiment for San Miguel FB (+17.8% MoM).   Ginebra’s sales volumes grew by almost a third (YoY) in 1Q to its highest quarterly volume on record.  The company is also among the few beneficiaries of current food inflation, deriving about a quarter of sales form poultry and hogs in 1Q. 

Banks benefitted from the turn in market sentiment and buying interest was captured by liquid large cap BDO (+9.2% MoM). BPI (+5.6% MoM) outperformed as well while Metrobank (+1.5% MoM) also ended up.

Rizal Commercial Bank (+30.6% MoM) surged significantly on news that Japan’s second largest bank Sumitumo Mitsui is taking a 5% stake in it, acquiring the stake for ~P44/share (book value). Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group (SMFG) will invest JPY10bn (roughly PHP4.4bn ) for the stake. 

PNB (+3.7% MoM) turned in a steady performance.  The bank is set to unlock P34bn in capital through extraordinary gains from the spinoff of its interest of its three most valuable property assets into a real estate unit. PNB disclosed that the transaction would raise P10bn in fresh capital after the issuance of property dividends to stockholders.

Property stocks gained. REIT news was at the forefront. Ayala Land (+4.8% MoM) infused more properties into AREIT (+1.1% MoM) through a share-for-property swap while Filinvest Land’s (+1.9% MoM) REIT is slated to be listed in late July. Meanwhile, Megaworld (+6.3%) said that it could raise up to P27bn from its planned REIT IPO and separately announced an improvement in residential sales. 

June was a big month for transport stocks. ICTSI (+14.4% MoM)  advanced further in June  after announcing it acquired a 100% stake in Manila Harbour Centre (MHC) from a related party. MHC is the sole international breakbulk and bulk operator in Manila. The acquisition price of P2.45bn looks reasonable at an implied valuation of 8.3x EV/EBITDA.  The immediate earnings accretion will be minimal (<1% EBITDA uplift) but the value to the group is certainly going to be greater (i.e. greater flexibility to maximize point of handling across its three ports in Manila and reduce the cost base).

Cebu Air (+18.7% MoM)  gained on news reports that rival PAL is finalizing its restructuring plan that will be submitted to authorities as a Chapter 11 filing.  It reportedly plans to raise US$630m (mostly from a “major stockholder”).  The restructuring is expected to result in a reduction in its fleet size, which will consequently lead to a reduction in industry capacity, which should bode well for Cebu Air.  

Macro proxies mostly closed with gains in June.  DMCI (+13.1% MoM) rallied, as did index names  JG Summit (+9.0% MoM) and  Aboitiz Equity (+4.5% MoM). Index heavyweight SM Investments (+3.0% MoM) ended the month higher. SM Investments purchased a 22.36% stake in 2GO (from the Udenna Group) at P8.50/share (P4.7bn), taking its stake in 2GO to 52.85%. 

GT Capital’s  (+2.9% MoM)  auto unit Toyota saw a nice 25% MoM (+344% YoY) rebound in unit sales in May. Sales for the first five months are up 87% YoY.  Meanwhile, LT Group (-6.5% MoM) dropped. MPI (-2.3% MoM) also ended lower. MPI has expressed interest in the re-bid of the US$10bn Sangley airport project.

Utilities were mixed.  Manila Water (+29.1% MoM) delivered a massive rise in June.    Enrique Razon has taken over Manila Water and is now the Chairman, President and CEO of the water utility.  Mr. Razon now has a 34% economic stake and 51% voting stake in Manila Water. 

Semirara (+12.2% MoM) rose.  SCC is seen to benefit the most from the near-term spike in spot prices, selling about 20% of capacity to WESM. 

Aboitiz Power (+7.7% MoM), meanwhile, declined.  A halt order for 3 hydro plants of AP has been issued though the affected plants are less than 1% of AP’s NAV, capacity and earnings.

The telcos underperformed with the threat of the third telco still weighing on performance. Dito Telecommunity (-6.4% MoM) has committed to cover 51% of the population and offer minimum after internet speeds of 55mbps for its next annual review in July. 

Meanwhile,  Globe’s (+1.9% MoM)  917Ventures said it is considering listing GCash.  GCash is operated by Globe Fintech innovations (Mynt), a venture led by Globe and Ant Financial. The mobile wallet is one of the largest in the country with over 40 million users and targeting to handle P2trillion transactions this year. Globe also said that it is on track to install an additional 1 million fiber-to-the-home lines by end of 2021 as part of its P70bn capex program this year.

PLDT (-1.7% MoM) Chairman Manual V. Pangilinan has officially relinquished his position as PLDT president and chief executive officer to Alfredo Panlilio. Panlilio was Chief Revenue Officer and CEO of PLDT subsidiary Smart and no major changes are expect for the company’s strategy.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

May inflation steady at 4.5%. Headline inflation remained at 4.5% for the third consecutive month, within consensus estimates and the BSP’s forecast range of 4-4.8%. Food inflation eased slightly in May as rice prices , fruits, and vegetables declined, offsetting still elevated meat inflation. Non-food inflation (e.g. utilities) accelerated slightly due to higher oil prices. The 5M21 average inflation stood at 4.4%, above the government’s target range of 2-4%.

BSP keeps policy rate at record low. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) kept its key interest rate at a record low for a fifth straight meeting. The BSP maintained the rate on the overnight reverse repurchase facility at 2%. The Monetary Board observed that economic activity has improved in recent weeks, but the overall momentum of the economic recovery remains tentative. The BSP raised its inflation outlook for this year to 4%, from the previous forecast of 3.9% (vs. YT-May average inflation at 4.4%).

Trade in April moderates MoM. Preliminary trade data showed a deficit of US$2.7bn in April, broadly unchanged MoM. The deficit was larger than the US$187mn recorded in the same period last year during the hard lockdown. MoM, exports and imports dropped 16% and 11%, respectively, following the renewed strict lockdown in Metro Manila. The total exports amounted to US$5.7bn in April vs. monthly average of US$5.9bn in 2019 while imports amounted to US$8.5bn vs. monthly average of US$9.2bn in 2019. The 4M21 trade deficit at US$11.1bn vs. US$8.6bn in 4M20 and US$14.1bn in 4M19.

Remittances at US$2.3bn in April. OFW remittances grew 12.7% YoY to US$2.3bn in April (-8% MoM). This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate at +7.3% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances rose 7.6% YoY. Remittances from land-based workers rose by 15.2% YoY to US$1.779bn and sea-based workers by 4.9% to US$526mn. To date, remittances have risen by 4.8% to US$9.9bn from a year earlier. The BSP expects FY21 remittances to rise by 4% YoY to US$31bn.

 

Budget deficit expands MoM to P200bn in May. The national government’s budget deficit stood at P200bn in May, as expenses accelerated. Tax revenues fell MoM to P234bn due to timing of tax filings in April but the figure for May is broadly the same as the monthly average in 2019. Expenses surged YoY and MoM to P457bn despite the high base from last year’s social amelioration program and cited capital outlay disbursements of the DPWH, education and health programs, Philhealth releases for health insurance premiums. This brings the government’s 5M21 budget deficit to P566bn. Last May, the government hiked its budget deficit target to P1.86tn (~9.4% of GDP) from P1.78tn previously (~8.9% of GDP) due to higher programmed expenditures. The government, however, lowered its FY21E infrastructure outlay target to P1.019tn (5.1% of GDP) from P1.69tn (5.9% of GDP) previously.

Dollar reserves dip in May. The country’s gross international reserves, based on preliminary data, settled at US$106.98bn in end-May from US$107.71bn in end-April (-US$727mn). The decline was due to the foreign currency withdrawals of the national government from its deposits with the BSP to pay its foreign currency debt obligations and various expenditures. The GIR level represented a “more-than-adequate external liquidity buffer” equivalent to 12.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The BSP projects the GIR to reach US$114bn this year from US$110.117bn in end-Dec ’20.

Balance of payments back to deficit in May. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$1.4bn in May 2021. This is a reversal from the US$2.43bn BOP surplus recorded in April 2021 (due to National Govt’s ROP Global and Samurai Bond issuances). The deficit was due to payments of the National Government’s foreign debt obligations. This reflects a slight decrease in GIR level to US$107.25bn in end-May from US$107.71bn in end-April and US$110.12bn in end-2020. The 5M21 BOP deficit stood at US$1.63bn (vs. US$4.03bn surplus in the same period last year) due to a wider merchandise trade deficit and net outflows of foreign portfolio investments. The BSP projects the BOP to be at a US$7.1bn surplus by end-2021 (equivalent to 1.8% of GDP) vs. 2020 BOP surplus of US$16bn (4.4% of GDP).

 

Infra spend drops MoM in April. Gov’t infrastructure and other capital outlays (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose to P58.2bn in April (+45% YoY, -34% MoM). Metro Manila and its adjacent provinces were placed under a stricter lockdown in April, although construction work both in public and private sectors were allowed to continue. This brings 4M21 infra spend stood at P253bn, up 29% YoY. The government is targeting to spend P1.02tn on infrastructure (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) this year, up 17% YoY.

Duterte orders nat'l gov't, LGUs: Prepare for full devolution of basic services. President Rodrigo Duterte issued an executive order (no. 138) telling national government agencies to fully transfer the task of delivering basic services to local governments by the year 2024. The EO will provide local governments with more funds (at least 50% more) starting 2022. To prepare for the “phased” devolution, all national government agencies and local governments will prepare “Devolution Transition Plans” within 120 days from the effectivity of the EO. A Growth Equity Fund will be included in the 2022 Budget and proposed by the Committee on Devolution to Congress.

Unemployment rate inches up in April.  Preliminary results of the PSA’s April 2021 round of the Labor Force Survey showed an unemployment rate of 8.7%, inching up from 7.1% in March and cited “temporary reversal” of gains made in previous months due to the lockdown, according to a joint statement of economic managers. The total jobs in April remained above pre-pandemic levels. While the economy lost 3.9mn jobs last April 2020, total jobs generated since was 6.3mn, suggesting net addition of +2.5mn jobs. It could also be lower paying jobs vs pre-pandemic.

 

 

Vehicle sales in May jump +24% MoM; Toyota sales up +25% MoM. Data from CAMPI/TMA showed that industry sales rose +24% MoM, +361% YoY to 22,062 units in May. This brings 5M21 industry car sales up 59% YoY to 110,217 units. Toyota sold 10,799 units, up 25% MoM and 344% YoY and brings 5M21 sales to 52,5236 units (+87% YoY). We’ve modeled a +17% YoY jump in Toyota’s unit sales to 117k units and to reach our forecast, Toyota will need to sell c. 9,200 units per month. With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales should average 7,400 units. Assuming Toyota’s forecast, we estimate our forecast for Toyota and GTCAP could fall by 10-14% and 4-5%, respectively.

Bank lending shrinks 5% in April. Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks contracted by 5% YoY in April (vs. -4.5% YoY in March). This is the steepest decline seen since May 2004. Meanwhile, money supply growth slowed to 5.1% in April from 8.3% in March.

Consumer loan NPLs up to 9.75% in 1Q21, but decelerating. Consumer loan asset quality deteriorated again QoQ in 1Q21, though the pace has decelerated nicely. Overall consumer loan NPL ratio was 9.75%, up 42bps from the 9.33% at end-2020. That is the slowest QoQ increase since the Covid-19 pandemic started. That’s also slower than the 59bps QoQ increase in total NPLs in 1Q21. Mortgage NPLs inched up 21bps QoQ to 9.44%. The biggest jump was seen in Motor Vehicle NPLs, which surged 142bps QoQ to 12.10% from 10.68% at end-2020. The MV NPLs were driven by a spike in Motorcycle NPLs to 9.60% from 6.20%. Auto loan (4-wheels) NPLs were still higher at 12.26%, up 129bps QoQ. Credit card NPLs improved to 8.48%, down 37bps QoQ, while Salary loan NPLs were flat at 6.60%.

 

 

 

POGO tax bill certified as urgent. President Duterte certified Senate Bill (SB) 2232 as urgent. SB 2232 would require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue. This would be in addition to the 2% of GGR paid to PAGCOR. POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P120bn in tax revenue over four years. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover and could slow down POGO closures. 

Senate approves POGO tax bill.  The Senate approved on third reading a bill on POGO taxes. SB 2232 would require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue in lieu of all other taxes. Additionally, POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P120bn in tax revenue over four years. The House Ways and Means Chairman Joey Salceda said that the House of Representatives might adopt the Senate version rather than proceed to Bicameral Conference. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. Thus, the new bill could slow down POGO closures.

Lifting of ban on open-pit mining seen. The ban on open-pit mining is expected to be lifted soon as the draft implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of Executive Order No. 130 have been finalized according to Wilfredo Moncano, director of the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB). MGB submitted the proposed IRR to Environment Secretary Roy Cimatu. A repealing clause in the draft IRR will lift the four-year ban on the use of open-pit mines to extract minerals. Billions of dollars are expected to flow from the mining industry once the ban is lifted as this will allow two mines to resume operations. US$5.9bn Tampakan copper project of Sagittarius Mines; US$2bn King-king copper-gold project of St. Augustine Gold and Copper Ltd; US$750mn Silangan project of Philex Mining.

Finance chief firm on keeping stimulus at P173bn. Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III said the government can only provide P173bn in stimulus funds this year. Dept. of Finance (DoF) will remain open to Bayanihan III if lawmakers can identify other potential revenue-generating measures. He believes it is unsustainable to have the fiscal deficit exceed 9.3% of GDP. The DoF is not proposing any tax hikes to boost revenues, but instead looked for savings from the 2020 budget and asked state-run firms to increase their dividend remittances to P47bn this year. The Bayanihan III bill aims to provide more than P400bn in stimulus funds. The Bill already passed the Lower House and the Senate will tackle the bill when it resumes session on July 26. Bayanihan I and II provided funds of P275bn and P160bn, respectively.

Philippine government signs supply agreement with Pfizer. The Philippines signed a supply agreement for 40mn doses of the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID vaccine. We estimate that the Philippines has signed agreements for 157mn COVID vaccine doses which includes the 44mn doses from COVAX. The Philippines has now received a total of 14.2mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 8.1mn doses. The country is scheduled to receive 10.2mn doses in June including 5.9mn doses that already arrived. The Philippines expects to receive 11.7mn doses in July and 17mn doses in August. The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

'NCR Plus' under GCQ with restrictions until end-June.  President Rodrigo Duterte extended the quarantine status (i.e. GCQ with restrictions) of Metro Manila and four nearby provinces until end-June. Metro Manila and Bulacan will be under GCQ with some restrictions. Calabarzon provinces of Rizal, Laguna and Cavite will be under the same status but with heightened restrictions. Metro Manila mayors have shortened the curfew to 12mn-4am from 10pm-4am previously.

Gov't allows leisure travel from 'NCR Plus' to MGCQ areas. The government is allowing non-essential domestic travel from NCR Plus to areas under general community quarantine (GCQ) and modified GCQ regardless of age from June 1 to 15. Local government units (LGUs) still have the final word if they would allow tourism in their areas. The eased requirements will only apply to point-to-point travel and will only accept travelers with negative RT-PCR tests.
