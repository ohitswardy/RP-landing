REGIS REPORT

29 November 2021
 

The PSEi gained 2.1% in November, lifted by prospects of economic reopening spurred by a large loosening of mobility restrictions. Reopening optimism had taken the market to as high as +5.5% intra-month, firmly above the 7400 level, but gains were shaved off late in the month as Omicron caused a global market rout.  News of the highly mutated COVID variant prompted fears of a return to lockdowns, even as border closing and travel restrictions immediately emerged.

A lower lockdown level (Alert level 2) was announced early in November for the capital, increasing indoor capacity of establishments and allowing children outside their homes. Mobility restrictions continued to be eased, made possible by the continued decline in case counts.  The latest 7-day moving average of new daily cases has fallen to below 1,000, well below the September peak of close to 21,000 cases in September.

Meanwhile, the government is accelerating its vaccination efforts. On November 29, the government began a three-day day push to administer 9m vaccines (3m/day).  It has earmarked another three days in December for another big push.  The government is targeting to inoculate 54m Filipinos by end-2021, 77m by end-1Q22, and 90m by end-2Q22 (vs total population of ~110m).

Meanwhile, latest data from the government showed 33% of the country is now fully vaccinated with the proportion for Metro Manila now at 69%. The push from the government, as well as declining COVID vaccine hesitancy (only 18% did not want to be vaccinated as of September based on the latest opinion poll from 33% in May and 21% in June) increases optimism for increased vaccination rates.  

GDP in 3Q came in at +7.1%, beating Regis (4.0%) and Street estimates (4.9%). On the demand side, investments led the rebound, with capital formation up 22%, led by construction activity. Last year's 3Q20 GDP contracted 11.6%, placing this year’s 3Q output still ~5% below what it was in 2019.  

In the meantime, all 3Q21 corporate earnings are in. Aggregate NPAT was up 14-15% YoY, with strict lockdowns and input cost pressures apparent during the quarter. Aggregate earnings for 9M21 were up 44% for PSEi constituents, still 30-35% below their pre-Covid (9M2019) levels. However, mobility restrictions continue to be eased from 3Q, suggesting better prospects for the next earnings round.  

Data from Google show personal mobility rising to another pandemic period high.  Retail/recreation mobility is practically where it was immediately before the pandemic. The data for workplaces, shows mobility still just 5% below what it was in the Jan-Feb 2020.  Domestic tourism could also provide an uplift to the economy, as LGUs loosen domestic travel restrictions.

MORE ON FLOWS...

The market saw marginal foreign inflows for a second consecutive month, with US$5m flowing through in November. Multiple block events during the month skewed foreign flows. Among them were the follow-on offering of Synergy Grid which registered US$24.2m inflows, SMPH blocks with US$10m inflows, and a Converge follow-on offering that saw US$27.2m in net foreign selling.  Excluding these blocks would have reversed to net outflows of US$2m. Year-to-date net foreign outflows stand at P1.730bn.

MSCI rebalancing at month-end held expectations of US$113m of passive inflows, driven by the addition of AC Energy and Monde Nissin into the main index. The actual rebalancing though led to only US$13m in inflow, as foreign active funds took the liquidity event as an opportunity to take some profit.  

Foreign participation was higher at 49% (from 41% in October).

AC Energy and Monde Nissin were top foreign bought after being included in the MSCI main index. Certain cyclicals/reopening plays  -- BPI,  SM Prime, and Bloomberry -- continued to see inflows. Globe saw renewed foreign buying and was one of this month’s best performers.

Biggest outflows were seen in Converge and Synergy Grid, with foreigners selling post deal.  Outflows in Universal Robina continued while conglomerates SM and JG Summit were also top sold.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

Financials were mixed in November.  BPI (+4.7% MoM) and Metrobank (+4.6% MoM) outperformed. The latter’s 3Q numbers tracked behind consensus due largely to weak trading/fx gains.  Asset quality improved with NPL ratio falling to 2.1% (from 2.3% in 2Q) and coverage at 191%.

Union Bank (+18.2% MoM) turned in a noteworthy performance on excitement surrounding its digital bank license and prospects of combining this with a strong credit card/consumer lending franchise.  There was some market talk/peculation on the bank’s prospects with regard to the acquisition of the Citi retail assets. 

3Q21 earnings of most real estate companies disappointed as mall revenue remained muted (due to the lockdown) and residential revenue of some of the developers plunged (due to the sales cancellations). Most property stocks fell during the month but two of the largest mall operators, Robinsons Land (+7.2% MoM) and SM Prime (+13.0% MoM), bucked the trend. The easing of Metro Manila's lockdown level to Alert Level 2 and the clearance for minors to enter malls have caused a noticeable surge in mall visitation.

Market proxies were likewise mixed though small/mid caps overtook their larger counterparts this month.

LT Group (+5.4% MoM) declared a higher-than-expected special dividend of P0.60/share, which brought the total DPS for the year to P1.08/share (~11% annual yield). Alliance Global Group (+8.8% MoM), meanwhile, also gained despite 3Q profit in declining 41%QoQ. Looser restrictions in Metro Manila raised expectations of a recovery particularly from its casino and quick service restaurant units. MPI (+8.3% MoM) jumped posting better-than-expected 3Q21 profits, buoyed by higher earnings from Meralco’s non-distribution units and lower-than-expected interest expenses at the parent. The re-opening of travel since mid-September should also help its tollroad and rail units’ performance from 4Q onwards.

For the large caps, SM Investments(+1.6% MoM) closed higher, with lower provisions at BDO taking it on track to beating FY21 profit expectations.  Ayala Corp (-3.1% MoM) however declined.  For 3Q alone, net income was P9.0bn, up 2.6x YoY as the base was low the prior year and 3Q this year was lifted by P3.5bn in gains from the partial sale of its coal-fired power plant.

 

Robinsons Retail (+6.8% MoM) and Shakey’s Pizza (+8.5%) – largely considered reopening plays – were among the key outperformers within the consumer sector.during the month.

3Q results across the sector were unsurprisingly weak due to the Aug/Sep lockdown. Restaurants’ SSSG deteriorated during the quarter amidst dine-in restrictions, while higher raw material cost pressured margins. For Jollibee (-0.1%) margins were further pressured by higher labor cost in the US, leading to wider QoQ losses for Smashburger.

Food companies were likewise impacted by higher commodity prices, which led to YoY contraction in margins for Universal Robina (-3.6% MoM) and Monde Nissin (+5.7% MoM). Monde outperformed the market during the month largely due to its inclusion in the MSCI. Results came in below expectations as Quorn sales lagged. Meanwhile, URC announced plans to acquire Malaysian biscuit company Munchy Food after completing the sale of its Oceania business.

San Miguel Food  & Beverage (-0.7% MoM) reported a 21%QoQ decline in 3Q profits. While beer and gin performed better sequentially, profits from its food segment contracted 50% due to volatility of commodity prices.

Emperador’s (-2.7%MoM) 3Q profit fell 27%QoQ on substantially lower margins for its brandy segment.

On the contrary, D&L Industries (+3.1% MoM) grew 3Q profits by 14% on improved margins from oleochemicals and plastics.

Results across the retail sector were mixed, illustrating the K-shaped recovery of the sector. RRHI, Wilcon (+2.2% MoM) and Puregold’s (-2.8% MoM) S&R saw better QoQ SSS trends, while the core Puregold brand had yet to see a more meaningful recovery.

Globe (+9.5% MoM) led the telcos in November on continued excitement surrounding its e-wallet.  Mynt,  the holding company that owns 100% of e-wallet GCash,  achieved positive EBITDA and net profit for 3Q21.  Globe’s share of Mynt’s profit in the quarter was P115m. This implies a net profit of ~P287m for the quarter for the entire company – over P1bn annualized.

The registered user base of GCash is now over 51m and it is on track to achieve P3.5trn GTV for FY21 (previous guidance was P3trn). Interestingly, GCash claims it has 4x more active users than its direct competitor, assumed to mean PLDT’s (+2.3% MoM) Paymaya.

Mynt received over US$300m in new investment that valued it at “over US$2bn”.  Globe’s stake in Mynt is estimated to dip close to 35% post-transaction, which would imply a minimum valuation of US$700m. Regis estimates that at a share price of around ~P3,000 for Globe already implies a valuation between US$4-5bn for Mynt.   

Converge (+0.6% MoM) meanwhile underperformed.  Warburg Pincus, sold 420m shares of the company in an overnight placement. The sale reduced its stake in CNVRG to 10.2% from 15.8%. The deal was done at P30/share which was at the bottom of the indicative price range (P30-P31/share) and was a 7.2% discount to the prior day’s close. The deal was apparently upsized by 18% despite being priced at the bottom of the range.

 

 

 

ICTSI (+9.9% MoM) jumped 10% following stronger-than-expected 3Q21 profits and a successful refinancing exercise. Both are seen to  lead to more street upgrades.  Meanwhile, the re-opening of travel also pushed Cebu Air's  (+4.4% MoM) shares higher MoM, with the company now operating at c. 40% of its pre-Covid capacity, up from just c. 20% in 9M21.

Utils generally retreated as the market focused on reopening names. AC Energy (-8.0% MoM) disclosed its tie-up with CleanTech Global Renewables Inch for a 200MWdc project in Cagayan. It also reported that 3Q profit rose 11%QoQ on the back of a recovery in electricity demand and incremental capacity from recent acquisitions and completed projects.

Semirara (-12.1% MoM) corrected from previous month’s gains following a sharp correction in coal prices (-35% in the monthly average).  After hitting a record high in October, coal prices collapsed following China’s signal that it would help stabilize thermal coal prices.

 

Megawide (-14.8% MoM) dipped after a local court issued arrest warrants for various executives of its 60-40 JV GMR Megawide Cebu Airport Corp. over an alleged violation of the Anti-Dummy Law. The company is seeking the dismissal of the case for the lack of probable cause and due process.

Reopening play Bloomberry (+14.6% MoM) extended its climb in November, helped by strong GGR reported for 3Q and further loosening of capacity restrictions on casinos. Its gains were tempered late in the month, however, following reports that Suncity Group CEO Alvin Chau was arrested by Macau authorities over illegal gambling and money laundering allegations. Suncity is the biggest junket operator in the Philippines, accounting for as much as 25% of VIP revenue.

  

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

3Q GDP up 7.1%, beating forecasts. GDP grew +7.1% YoY in 3Q21, stronger than street (4.9%) and Regis (4.0%) forecasts. On a seasonally adjusted basis, 3Q GDP expanded by 3.8%. The 3Q21 GDP was 9% below 2019 average. The 9M21 GDP growth at 4.9% is tracking above Regis forecast (+4.5%). Domestic demand was strong, up 11% YoY in 3Q. Consumption was resilient despite renewed lockdowns, up 7.1% YoY (broadly unchanged QoQ).

Meanwhile, investments also grew double-digits, with construction unhampered; Public and private construction rose 55%/12% YoY respectively. Net exports were higher, on strong import growth. On the supply side, industry and service sectors expanded but agri sector lagged (cited typhoons and African Swine Flu).

Inflation eases to 4.6% in October.  Inflation slowed to 4.6% in October from 4.8% in September due to moderating prices of meat, vegetables, and sin products which offset acceleration of fuel-related products. The YT-October average inflation at 4.5%.

Infra spend flat MoM in SeptemberThe Monetary Board has kept the overnight borrowing rate at its historic low of 2%. BSP cited latest baseline forecasts are “broadly unchanged” from the previous assessment round. BSP is also looking at an average inflation of 4.3% in FY21, 3.3% in FY22, and 3.2% in FY23. Inflation averaged at 4.5% in YT-October, with October inflation at 4.6%. BSP Governor Diokno however said that the risks to the inflation outlook is to the upside for 2022, considering typhoons and fare increases. The Monetary Board will hold its final policy rate review this year on Dec. 16.

 

OFW remittances rise 5.2% YoY in September. OFW remittances rose 5.2% YoY, 4.9% MoM in September to US$2.74bn. In PHP terms, remittances grew 9% YoY. This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate to +4.2%. Land-based remittances rose +6.2% YoY while sea-based remittances rose +1.9% YoY. Remittances from the Middle East and Asia moderated while remittances from USA and Europe posted double-digit growth rates. The 9M21 remittances are up 5.6% YoY to US$23bn. To reach the BSP’s FY21E target of US$31.7bn (+6% YoY), remittances for Oct/Nov/Dec should average US$2.86bn per month.

End-October GIR level rises to US$107.95bn. Based on preliminary data, the country’s gross international reserve rose by US$1.35bn MoM to US$107.95bn as of end-October. This reflects the rise of gold prices and  is equivalent to 10.8 months’ worth of imports. 

BOP posts US$1.14bn surplus in October. The country’s overall balance of payments position posted a surplus of US$1.14bn in October 2021. This is a reversal from the US$412mn BOP deficit In September 2021 but lower than the US$3.44bn surplus in the same period a year ago. The National Government deposited US$1.59bn in the BSP from its Retail Onshore Dollar Bonds during the period. This brings YT-October BOP surplus to US$476mn, lower than the US$10.3bn surplus in the same period last year. The BSP is looking at a BOP surplus position of US$4.1bn in FY21 and US$1.7bn in FY22.

 

 

 

Budget deficit narrows to 6-month low in October. The government ran a P64.3bn budget deficit in October, down MoM and lowest level since April 2021. On a YoY basis, the budget deficit in October was up 5% YoY. Meanwhile, tax revenues improved 3% MoM and 7.5% YoY; Tax revenues for October were 7% below pre-Covid levels. Expenditures in October dropped 23% MoM and was the lowest since January 2021, with primary spending also lowest this year. This brings YT-October budget deficit to P1.2tn, up 28% YoY.

Trade deficit widens further in September. Total trade in September improved further, up 17% YoY. The trade deficit was larger at US$4bn, with imports up 25% YoY. Fuel imports driving the recovery; Consumer and capital goods imports very close to pre-Covid levels. Exports also sustained rebound and is now above pre-Covid levels. The YT-September trade deficit at US$29bn.

Infra spend flat MoM in September. Government infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) stood at P71.2bn in September (+25% YoY, flat MoM). This brings YT-September overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P807.5bn (+36% YoY). Additionally, this is 79% of the government’s FY21E target of P1.019tn (+17% YoY). For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn on infrastructure (+25% YoY).

Bank lending rises by 2.7% YoY in September. Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, rose by 2.7% YoY, 0.6% MoM in September, faster than the 1.3% expansion in August.  Loans to manufacturing, utilities, and finance/insurance accelerated and there was a smaller drop in consumer loans/credit card loans. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew at a faster rate of 8.2% in September (from 6.9% in August). 

 

Vehicle sales drop 10% YoY in October, TMP sales down 4% YoY. Car sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA dropped 10% YoY in October to 22,581 units. On a MoM basis, car sales recovered +5%. The 10M21 industry car sales rose 24% YoY to 214k units (29% below 2019).

To reach CAMPI’s FY21E of 295k(+32% YoY), car sales should average 41k per month for Nov/Dec. Toyota sold 11,157 units in October (-4% YoY, +12% MoM) and this brings 10M21 sales to 104kunits (+38% YoY). To reach Toyota’s target of 130k units (+30% YoY), Toyota needs to sell 13k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 117k units (+17% YoY), Toyota needs to sell 7k units per month for the rest of the year. For FY22E, Regis is  looking at sales of 130k units (11k units per month), 20% below 2019.

Unemployment rate slightly rises in September; Labor force drops MoM. More people lost jobs in September, with the unemployment rate rising to 8.9% from 8.1% the previous month and remains above pre-pandemic level (5.3%).  MECQ was extended in NCR to September 15; weather disturbances also affected agri sector.  The country’s active workforce also dropped slightly MoM, with the labor force participation rate at 63.3%.  The higher unemployment rate, combined with the lower labor force participation rate, led to a net employment loss of 642,000. Employment increased by 414,000 in the services sector, mostly driven by wholesale and retail trade, tempering the net employment loss in September. 

 

 

Metro Manila eases to Alert Level 2.Metro Manila was placed under a more relaxed Alert Level 2 starting Nov. 5.  Under Alert Level 2, indoor activities will be allowed to operate at 50% capacity, even if unvaccinated.  

Government to require on-site workers to be fully vaccinated by Dec 1. Beginning December 1, on-site workers for both the private and public sectors must be fully vaccinated against COVID-19. This rule will only apply to areas with “stable” supply of vaccines. Eligible employees who remain unvaccinated may not be terminated but they shall be required to undergo RT-PCR or antigen tests at their own expense. The Philippines is targeting to administer 5mn doses/day from November 29 to December 1. These days might be declared holidays. As of November 12, the 7-day average vaccinations was 802,025/day. Around 29% of the Philippine population (31.45mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of November 10, 66% of the Metro Manila population (8.9mn people) has been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 122mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 69mn doses (as of November 12).

Mobility at new highs (20-21 Nov). Mobility data keeps rising, with nation-wide retail already slightly above the Jan/Feb 2020 baseline. All indicators posted new Covid-era highs. Interestingly, though, the nation-wise numbers seem better than Metro Manila. This despite the significantly higher vaccination rates within the capital (c70%) compared to the national average (c30%). One possible explanation is that Metro Manila has adapted more to a) e-commerce, and b) remote work, given much better internet connectivity. Either way, the data is hugely positive, and points to an even better December. 

 

Vaccine willingness improves further in Sep. Willingness to get the Covid-19 vaccine improved again in September, according to a Social Weather Stations (SWS) survey published earlier this month. Nation-wide, 64% of adults were willing to get the jab (including the 35% that had already received at least one dose). This is up from the 55% in June, and 32% in May. Vaccine reluctance dropped to just 18% of adults, from 21% in June and 33% in May. Adults who were “uncertain” also dropped to 19% in September, down from 24% in June and 35% in May.

Metro Manila residents were by far more willing to be vaccinated, with 87% willing (including 71% already jabbed), up from 75% in June and 41% in May. Anti-vaxxers were down to just 7%, from 8% in June and 30% in May. Those on the fence fell to just 6% in September, from 16% in June and 30% in May.

However, reluctance is still quite high, particularly in areas in the Visayas and in Mindanao, with both at 24-25% (combining the “surely not” and “probably not” responses). For those in Luzon (ex-Metro Manila), about 15% are still anti-vaxxers.

Sara Duterte runs for VP with Marcos; Bong Go runs for President instead. Davao City Mayor. Sara Duterte filed her candidacy as the bet of Lakas-Christian Muslim Democrats or Lakas-CMD. She was adopted as the running mate of presidential aspirant Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr. Meantime, longtime Duterte aid, Senator Bong Go, will be running for President. Go withdrew from the Vice Presidential race. Its is said that President Duterte will be filing his candidacy for VP again according to Communications Secretary Martin Andanar. President Duterte will be the running mate of Bong Go.
