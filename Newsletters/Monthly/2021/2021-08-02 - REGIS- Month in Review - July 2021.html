REGIS REPORT

02 August 2021
 

The PSEi took a beating in July – dropping a massive 9.2% to close at 6270.23.   After hitting the 7000 index level to open the month and taking the market to just down 1.45% YTD (on  July 5), gains quickly unraveled with losses week in and week out. The Philippines is now down 12.2% YTD, and down 15.6% in dollar terms.

As the Delta variant swept through countries around the region, particularly Indonesia, the country braced for a Delta variant fueled surge in cases.  Investors sold the market and buyers stayed sidelined.   Market turnover was thin, averaging only US$117m daily during the month.  

The Peso’s weakness also weighed on the market.  The PHP continued to deteriorate in July, down 2.4% MoM after depreciating a similar 2.3% in the June. The PHP breached P50/US$ to as weak as 50.77 intra month before settling at PHP50.006/US$ at the end of the month.

Nevertheless, on the vaccination front, things moved in the right direction.  From around 170k daily vaccinations in June, the 7-day averagey daily adminstered doeses (as of Aug 1) hit 523k. Vaccine reluctance in the country has also dropped significantly – from 33% in May to 21% in June, according to a Social Weather Stations survey.  For Metro Manila, this number is down to just 8%. Some 55% of 55% of Filipino adults were willing to get the vaccine  (75% in NCR).

The country received 16.4m doses in July and expects to receive 19.8m in August.  Against a population of around 109m, The country has now received a total of 33.9mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 20.8m doses (as of 1 Aug). The government aims to vaccinate 50%-60% of the population by end-2021.

The government has imposed the strictest lockdown level (ECQ or Enhanced Community Quarantine) on the capital effective 6-20 August in an effort to quell the spread of the Delta variant.  Metro Manila will be under GCQ (General Community Quarantine) with heightened restrictions from July 30 to August 5.

Credit rating agency Fitch maintained the country’s rating at BBB but revised the outlook to negative. Fitch cited the impact of the pandemic on the economy and the government’s fiscal position.  Among the other risks highlighted by Fitch was the implementation of the Supreme Court ruling ordering the higher revenue transfers to local governments.

Key data
 
FLOW UPDATE

Foreigners were sellers again in July for US$183m, with lockdown fears causing accelerated selling late in the month. 


July included blocks which skewed foreign flows such as an RCBC US$89.3m block (net foreign inflow) which represented Sumitomo’s 5% purchase of the former, and a MONDE block which registered US$76m outflows (transfer of the shares purchased for the stabilization fund). Adjusting for these, outflows were still chunky at US$170m. 



Foreign participating remained relatively light at only 36% of turnover. 

Biggest outflows were spread across major sectors conglos/property/banks. LT Group saw the biggest outflows. Meanwhile Ayala Land, Ayala Corp, BDO, Metrobank, and SM Investments – reflecting broad-based Philippine selling.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

 Reflecting weak market sentiment, losses were across the board for macro proxies.  Largest conglos SM Investments (-8.9% MoM) and Ayala Corp (-8.8% MoM) were sold down.  

Large selling activity also took LT Group (-24.3% MoM) drastically lower.  Aboitiz Equity (-10.8% MoM), JG Summit(-9.9% MoM), and Metro Pacific (-10.0% MoM) ended down.  

Cosco (-8.2% MoM) also fell.  The liquor distribution business of Cosco, in the process of being listed via the shell company Da Vinci (DAVIN PM), plans to conduct a follow-on offering to raise as much as P7.5bn. This business will be renamed “The Keepers Holdings” (KEEPR PM). Collectively, the Group is the largest distributor of imported liquor in the Philippines with market shares of 74% of volumes and 67% of retail sales value in 2020 (IWSR).

There was potential good news for GT Capital’s (-10.5% MoM) auto unit Toyota as the Tariff Commission came out against the safeguard duty on imported vehicles, citing the lack of evidence that vehicle imports affected local vehicle assembly. The Commission’s stance could be a precursor to a final decision by the Dept of Trade (DTI) removing the duty. The safeguard duty amounted to a chunky 5-12% of the retail price of a majority of vehicles. 

Banks declined markedly in July, reflecting risk aversion.  BDO (-9.9% MoM) and Metrobank (-11.7% MoM) saw net foreign outflows of around US$13-14m each, while net outflows in BPI (-9.2% MoM) reached US$5m. 

Banks were weak despite upside potential to earnings – as benign asset quality could potentially leading to cuts in provision expectations.  Helped by lower provisions,  BPI’s 2Q21 net income hit P6.8bn (+29%YoY, +36%QoQ).  

Security Bank (-6.8% MoM) declined as well, as did East West Bank (-9.1% MoM) and RCBC (-11.7% MoM).

Union Bank (+0.2% MoM) turned in a flat performance. The bank obtained approval from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to establish a wholly owned digital banking subsidiary to be known as Union Digital Bank. UnionBank is expected to fold EON, a mobile-only platform launched in 2016, into this new subsidiary.

 

The property sector was not spared from the selldown.   

Large outflows were seen in Ayala Land (-9.3% MoM) , with US$21m flowing out (net) during the month.  Megaworld(-14.2% MoM), also down significantly was net sold during the month (US$9m). 

SM Prime (-13.8% MoM), Robinsons Land (-6.8% MoM) and Vista Land (-10.3% MoM) also fell. 

Filinvest Land (+0.9% MoM) is pushing through with its REIT Filinvest REIT will be priced at P7/share and FLI could raise up to P12.6bn from the IPO. FILRT would offer 2022E dividend yield of 6.6% (based on prospectus). The REIT will initially have 300,000sqm of office space (mostly in Alabang) which is 90% occupied with only 2% leased to POGOs. FILRT does not own land. Filinvest Land previously said that it would eventually infuse malls into FILRT.

The consumer sector was a mixed bag.  Wilcon (+9.2% MoM) and Emperador (+9.2% MoM) led, helped by 2Q results beats. 

Wilcon’s nearly four-fold jump in 2H21 profit came in ahead of forecasts.  For 2Q21 alone, net profit was P643mn, recovering from the break-even level in 2Q20. Margin expansion helped boost the outperformance.  Margins also boosted EMP’s 2Q profit, which rose 60% YoY.  Revenues rose 21% YoY.  

Universal Robina (-12.3% MoM) also reported results.   Revenues for the Phils branded consumer food (BCF) segment declined 7% YoY in 2Q given the high base from pantry stocking in the same period last year. For 2Q21 alone, operating profit was down 9% YoY to P3.9bn.

Threats of stricter lockdown rules took restaurants lower – with Jollibee (-11.0% MoM), Max’s (-12.7% MoM) and Shakey’s Pizza (-11.8% MoM) all dropping significantly. 

More defensive names fared better, led by Century Pacific (+6.8% MoM),  Robinsons Retail, (unch) and Puregold(-0.5% WoW) relatively outperforming.

Cebu Air (-18.7% MoM) once again succumbed as heightened threats to travel, both domestic and international, loomed all month. 

The Philippine Ports Authority approved a ten percent (+10%) tariff increase on stevedoring and cargo handling charges for both Asian Terminals Inc. and ICTSI (-5.0% MoM) at South Harbor and MICT, respectively. Regis  estimates the adjustment will apply to approx.. 70% of MICT’s revenues and will lift ICT’s consolidated FY22E EBITDA and profit by 3% and 7%, respectively. 

Sentiment for construction was soft as well, with Megawide (-9.7% MoM) also ending down in July. Megawide expects to secure one contract worth P20bn by December and another by early next year, bringing its order book to P100bn according to Megawide Chairman and Chief Executive Officer Edgar B. Saavedra. MWIDE’s order book stood at P66.1bn in end-March 2021.

Prospects of reclosing also hurt Solaire operator Bloomberry (-12.7% MoM).

Voyager, the holding company for a number of investments of PLDT (-5.3% MoM) in the digital/fintech space, raised US$167m in new funding from existing shareholders as well as a new investor (a fund managed by IFC Asset Management). Voyager holds Paymaya, which is the e-wallet and payment solutions platform of the group.

In the meantime, Globe’s (+1.1% MoM) unit  Mynt will aim to achieve a US$2bn valuation at its next fund-raising. This would be more than double the valuation at its last one 6-7 months ago. Fintech investor Bow Wave bought into Mynt at an estimated valuation of ~US$900m (post-money). Mynt is ~40%-owned each by Globe and Ant Financial.

Defensive telcos relatively outperformed the market.  

The utilities sector was down save for Semirara (+10.7% MoM) as the continued strength in regional coal prices provided upside risk to earnings.   Coal accounts for around 80% of SCC’s profits.

Meralco (-4.4 MoM) reported a nice 19% bounce in electricity sales for 2Q, taking 1H21 to +7%.  2Q21 electricity sales were almost at 2Q19 levels (pre-pandemic).  The company expects volumes to be up 7% for the year.  Implied core income for 2Q was P6.29bn (+23% YoY).

Aboitiz Power’s (-5.1% MoM) 2Q core income jumped 175%YoY to P3.9bn, helped by commissioning revenue from GNPD Unit 1, as well as higher water inflow, higher WESM dispatch and higher demand. For 1H21, AP’s core net income was P10.1bn (+188%YoY).

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Headline inflation eases to 6-month low in June. Headline inflation slowed down to 4.1% in June from 4.5% in May. Below consensus which was at 4.3%; and also within the BSP’s monthly forecast range of 3.9-4.7% for the month. The deceleration was due to non-food items, particularly transport inflation (base effects). Food inflation was steady; Utilities (+2.3% from +1.9% in May). This brings 6M21 average inflation rate to 4.4%, above the government’s target of 2-4%.

Remittances up 3% MoM, 13% YoY in May. Cash remittances from overseas Filipinos reached US$2.38bn in May, up 13% YoY and 3% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances rose 7% YoY; 3-month moving average growth rate at +10% YoY. May 2020 was still a low base due to lockdown. There were sustained recoveries seen from USA, Asia, and Middle East; Europe and sea-based remittances were still soft. This brings YT-May remittances up 6.3% YoY to US$12.3bn (in PHP terms, up +1% YoY). The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 4% YoY in FY21 (US$31.1bn), implying that remittances should average US$2.68bn per month for the balance of the year.

Budget deficit swells to P150bn in June. The country’s fiscal deficit stood at P150bn in June. Revenues dropped MoM and YoY on soft BIR collections while expenditures dropped 13% MoM (due to timing of subsidies for PhilHealth). The 1H21 budget deficit of P716bn is tracking below the government’s FY21E due to underspending while 1H21 revenues surpassed goal by 5%. The underspending was due to timing of subsidies and maintenance spending (awaiting submission of documents/progress billings), interest savings, release of funds under GUIDE bill (pending). The infra spend is tracking slightly ahead of target. 

Trade deficit widens in May. The country’s trade deficit doubled YoY to US$2.75bn in May. However, this was smaller than the revised US$3.08bn gap in April 2021. This brings YT-May trade deficit to US$12.6bn, up 30% YoY. Imports rose by 48% in May, as fuel imports surged and capital/consumer goods recovered while exports jumped 30% YoY, as electronics exports rose 25%.

 

BOP position at US$312mn deficit in June. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$312mn in June 2021. This is a reversal from the US$80mn surplus a year ago and a narrow deficit than in May 2021 (US$1.4bn). The data reflected mainly the outflows arising from the govt’s settlement of foreign currency debt obligations and various expenditures, and the BSP’s net foreign exchange operations. The 1H21 BOP stood at a US$1.94bn deficit, a reversal from last year’s US$4.1bn surplus due to a higher trade deficit.

GIR settles at US$106bn in June.The country’s gross international reserves dropped by US$1.1bn (-1.1% MoM) to US$106bn in end-June and cited falling gold prices, debt payment of foreign currency obligations, and BSP’s forex operations. This number covers 12.1 months of imports. However, this was 13.5% YoY higher than the end-June ’20 level of US$93.46bn. The BSP expects GIR to rise to US$115bn by year-end.

Infra spend up in June. Infrastructure disbursements (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) amounted to P94.4bn in June, up 50% YoY and 20% MoM. This brings 1H21 infra spend to P427bn, +43% YoY and accounted for 56% of the government’s P761bn full-year target. Overall 1H21 infrastructure spending, which included subsidy and equity injected into state-run corporations as well as transfers to LGUs totaled P535.9bn (+40% YoY). This is broadly on track to reach the government’s year-end target of P1.02tn (equiv. to 5.1% of GDP).

 

 

DBCC keeps GDP targets; Approves 2022 budget proposal of P5.02tn (+11.5% YoY).The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) retained its GDP growth targets of +6-7% for FY21 and +7-9% for FY22. To reach its target, the economy would have to grow an average of +10% in the remaining three quarters of 2021. It also approved its 2022 budget proposal of P5.02tn (+11.5% YoY). With revenues expected to recover by +14% YoY in 2022, the fiscal deficit ceiling is projected to narrow to P1.67tn (c. 7.5% of GDP) from a target of P1.86tn in FY21 (9.3% of GDP). It also cut its 2023/2024 fiscal deficit-to-GDP targets to 5.9%/4.9%, respectively from 6.3%/5.3% previously and cited spending cuts following reductions in projected revenue shares of local government units from local taxes. The DBCC did not discuss the need for Bayanihan 3, as there’s no directive from the Office of the President. President Rodrigo Duterte ordered agencies in May to declare savings from last year's allotments to fund its COVID-19 response instead.

Gov’t debt climbs to new high of P11.2tn. The National Govt’s outstanding debt inched up to a new high of P11.166tn as of end-June. This is equivalent to c. 56% of GDP (Gov’t FY21E Nominal GDP at P19.8tn). The gov’t targeting NG debt-to-GDP ratio to reach 59.1% by year-end from 54.6% in end-2020. Domestic accounts for 71% of total while external is at 29% of total.

Tariff Commission thumbs down safeguard duty on imported vehicles. The Tariff Commission recommended that there should be no safeguard duty imposed on imported CBU passenger and light commercial vehicles. They cited the lack of evidence of increased import quantities of CBUs both in absolute terms and relative terms to domestic production in 2014-2020. The recommendation will be submitted to the Dept. of Trade and Industry (DTI); DTI has 15 calendar days to issue a decision. The cash bond should be returned 10 days after issuance of decision. The DTI will not be able to accept another petition from the same industry within one year after date of decision. 

 

Vehicle sales rise 2% MoM in June; Toyota sales up 4% MoM.CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales jumped 45% YoY and 2% MoM to 22,550 units in June. This brings 1H21 industry car sales to 132,767 units (+56% YoY). Toyota sold 11,242 units, up 50% YoY and 4% MoM and this brings 1H21 sales 63,778 units (+79% YoY). With the safeguard duty, Toyota expects unit sales to reach on 104k-108k units this year (+4% to +8%). To reach Toyota’s target of 104k units, monthly sales only need to average 6.7k units for the rest of the year. Our forecast assumes Toyota’s sales will reach 117k units, implying that monthly sales for the balance of 2021 should average 8,900 units per month. GTCAP is trading at a 5% discount to its market-based NAV of P634/sh.

Asset quality deteriorates in May. The Philippine banking system’s NPL ratio (ex-interbank loans) of rose to 4.63%, up 15bps MoM and 90bps ytd. In absolute terms, NPLs increased 3.4% MoM to P479bn. Every other major asset quality indicator also deteriorated in the month. Past Due (PD) ratio was up 17bps MoM to 5.56%, Restructured loans (RL) ratio increased 20bps MoM to 2.47%, and NPL coverage fell to 80.0%, down 153bps MoM. The May NPL numbers should already fully incorporate the Bayanihan 2 loan grace periods which officially ended in February. However, they would not yet include asset quality issues from the April lockdowns in Metro Manila and surrounding provinces. The one silver lining is that the loan contraction seems to be moderating. Loans (ex-interbank) in May were down 1.9% YoY, better than the -2.6% in April and -4.0% in March. Loans were flat MoM at P10.34bn. It remains to be seen if the deterioration was sector-wide, or if it again was concentrated on a few banks. In 1Q21, the banking industry’s NPLs were up 59bps QoQ. But for the banks we cover – BDO, MBT, BPI and SECB – NPLs were broadly flat (marginally up/down) QoQ.

 

House of Representatives to adopt Senate version of POGO tax bill. House Ways and Means Committee chair “Joey” Salceda said that the House of Representatives would adopt the Senate version of the POGO tax bill. The SB 2232 would require POGOs to pay 5% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) as tax to the Bureau of Internal Revenue in lieu of all other taxes. Meanwhile, POGO employees will also pay 25% income tax (with an assumed minimum annual salary of 600,000). The government expects the passage of this law to raise P177bn in tax revenue over five years. The Bayanihan 2 Act, which was passed in September 2020, imposed taxes based on 5% of gross bets or turnover on POGOs. This new bill would lower POGO taxes since gross revenue is just a fraction of gross bets/turnover. Thus, the new bill could slow down POGO closures. 

Fitch Ratings revises Philippines’ outlook to negative. Fitch Ratings maintained its investment grade “BBB” credit rating for the Philippines but revised its outlook to “negative” from “stable”. Revision of outlook reflects increasing risks to the credit profile from the impact of the pandemic and its aftermath on policy-making as well as on economic and fiscal out-turns. General government debt-to-GDP ratio is expected to reach 52.7% and 54.5% in 2021 and 2022. These are much higher than the 34.1% in 2019 and will exceed the median increase for BBB-rated peers. Fitch described the government’s target to vaccinate up to 70% of the eligible population by end-2021 as “ambitious,” given only less than 3% of the population have received two jabs as of end-June. It also warned that the impact of the Supreme Court decision commonly known as the Mandanas ruling – which will increase revenue transfers to local government units from the national government – also poses uncertainty on the fiscal front. The poor execution could lead to underspending by local governments, which the authorities are seeking to address through capacity building. Fitch expects gross domestic product (GDP) to grow by 5% and 6.6% in 2021 and 2022. This is lower than previous than April estimates which were at 6.3% and 8.3%, respectively. Both projections are also below the 6-7% and 7-9% targets set by the government for the 2021 and 2022, respectively.

Dutertes top Pulse Asia Presidential/Vice-Presidential survey. In polls conducted by Pulse Asia from June 7 to 16, Sara Duterte topped the presidential survey at 28%, followed by Manila Mayor Isko Moreno at 14%, Ferdinand Bongbong Marcos at 13%, Grace Poe at 10% and Emmanuel Pacquiao at 8%. For the vice presidential survey, sitting President Rodrigo Duterte placed first at 18%, followed by Isko Moreno at 14%, Vicente Sotto at 10%, Ferdinand Bongbong Marcos at 10% and Emmanuel Pacquiao at 9%.

Duterte 2021 SONA: Key highlights. Accomplishments. Given that this was his last SONA (at least as of now), President Duterte highlighted his accomplishments during his six-year term, including raising salaries for cops and soldiers, as well as the passage of laws providing free tertiary education, universal healthcare, free irrigation, rice tariffication, and the tax reform programs. Unsurprisingly, he spoke at length about his fight against drugs/criminality and corruption, as well as his usual rants about the case against him at the ICC and his stance on having a cordial relationship with China over the country’s rights on the West Philippine Sea.  He noted that he knew he had limited time to accomplish things because the constitution constrained him to a single six-year term.

For the remainder of his term, he urged Congress to pass priority bills: Foreign investments Act, Public Service Act, Retail & Trade Liberalization Act. He also reiterated his call for Congress to establish a single agency that will focus on OFWs.  

Build! Build! Build! He flaunted his administration’s infrastructure program, raising infrastructure spend-to-GDP ratio to a record high of over 5%. He claimed that his administration was able to solve problems on public transportation, with much shorter waiting times for the MRT-3 (from 10 trains at 30km per hour to 23 trains at 60 km per hour) and reductions in travel times with the completion of the LRT-2 East extension project, Skyway 3, BGC-Ortigas Link bridge.  He noted that he deliberately asked his economic managers to prioritize projects outside of Davao (eg. Davao Coastal road project will only be completed in 2023).  He directed line agencies to be on full speed to ensure flagship projects will be completed within his term, especially those outside Metro Manila. 

Attacks on water companies/ABS-CBN anew.  He renewed his attacks against the Metro Manila water concessionaires and lauded that the new water contracts will be able to protect consumers at least until 2037.  He admitted that he has not thoroughly digested the new water contracts yet so he has not signed it.  He also accused ABS-CBN again for not paying right taxes on imported equipment and real estate. He threatened to reassign ABS-CBN’s radio frequencies.  

Pandemic.  President Duterte thanked the private sector for supporting the government during the pandemic. He committed to “assess” the private sector to regain the commercial vibrance of the country prior to the pandemic.  He indicated that the country cannot afford more lockdowns, but if the Delta variant causes another surge, he will not hesitate to lock down the country again.  He also urged local governments to hasten inoculations and urged Congress to enact a law to create a center for disease control and a Phil. Virology Institute so Philippines can make its own vaccines in the future.  He highlighted the recently signed Executive Order for an employment recovery strategy, which aims to create over 1mn jobs, re-tool workers for better job opportunities by providing online training.
