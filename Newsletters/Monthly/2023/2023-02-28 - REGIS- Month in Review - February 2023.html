REGIS REPORT

28 February 2023
 

The Philippines dropped 3.5% in February, reversing the 3.5% gain it delivered in the preceding month.  The market sustained losses, as foreigners resumed selling the market, and as global markets digested the probably of further rate increases by the Fed. Trading activity lacked conviction amidst a lack of domestic catalysts.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised policy rates by 50bp to 6.0%. This is the highest level policy rates have been in almost in almost 15 years. The spread over the US Fed funds rate of 125-150bp remains low compared historically, however. The rate hike did not come as a surprise, with half the Street expecting a 50-bp hike (and another half expecting a smaller 25bp move).

While BSP Governor Felipe Medalla previously hinted that BSP need not match the Fed, the inflation print in January likely prompted a larger move. Inflation for the month of January accelerated to 8.7% (vs. 8.1% in Dec), well above expectations. Food inflation continued to be the key driver for the higher CPI print, rising 11.2% (vs 10.6% in Dec). The vegetable sub-category alone was at +37.8%.

Short-term interest rates and loan pricing are expected to move parallel to the rate decision. Banks should continue to benefit the most, while interest rate sensitive sectors such as real estate should see less favorable sentiment. Meanwhile the central bank also hiked its inflation forecast in 2023 to 6.1% from 4.5% suggesting a longer period for monthly inflation to fall back within the BSP’s 2-4% target band.

The strength in remittances has helped prop up domestic demand. Overseas remittances in December surpassed expectations, coming in at a robust US$3.16bn, rising 5.8% YoY. This was the first time remittances breached US$3bn in a single month. In PHP terms, remittances rose 17.2% YoY during the month.  Remittances for FY22 reached US$ US$32.54bn, up 3.6% YoY, broadly in line with the BSP’s target (+4%).  Remittances for the year are equivalent to 8.0% of GDP.  For FY22, the BSP is forecasting remittances to grow by 4%.

Key data
 

Foreigners turned sellers in February, with US$142.3m exiting this month. Bulk of this month’s outflows came through on MSCI rebalancing where US$65.8m flowed through. Outside of MSCI rebalancing, foreigners were net sellers for most of the month with the market seeing net outflows for eight consecutive sessions to close out the month. Year to date, foreigners now net sellers for US$20.3m. Foreign participation stood at 48%

Biggest outflows were seen in MSCI deletions Globe and AC Energy which saw US$70.5m and US$20.2m of net foreign selling respectively. Property names also saw large outflows as rising rates seen to weigh on the sector, with Ayala Land, Robinsons Land, Megaworld, and SM Prime all among top sold.

Foreigners continue to flock to banks with BDO, Metrobank, and Security Bank all top bought. Meanwhile, Meralco was a top gainer and among top foreign bought as it saw an upweight in the MSCI.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The market was weighed down by macro proxy heavy weights SM Investments (-7.4% MoM) and Ayala Corp (-11.60% MoM) as well as SM Prime (-7.7% MoM).  The three collectively dragged the index down by 173 index points, representing 73% of the market’s total drop for the month (-237.05 index points MoM).

Other macro proxies came down as well, with JG Summit (-7.9% MoM), Aboitiz Equity (-5.0% MoM), and DMCI (-7.7% MoM) also pushing the index lower. 

Metro Pacific (+0.99% MoM) however delivered a small gain, after rising by 18% MoM in January after it confirmed that it is considering a plan to voluntary delist its shares from the Philippine Stock Exchange as part of ongoing discussions with third-party investors.

Meantime, MPI acquired a 34.76% stake in agricultural (coconut-based) products company Axelum (-3.7% MoM) for P5.32bn (US$100m).  This follows its acquisition of an interest in dairy firm The Laguna Creamery Inc., in line with its interest to get into the agricultural sector. 

Apart from this, MPI unit Metro Pacific Agro Ventures Inc. and Israel-based LR Group will be investing around P800 million to P1 billion in a vegetable greenhouse facility, Metro Pacific Fresh Farms (MPFF), in San Rafael, Bulacan.

LT Group (+4.2% MoM) also gained, building on its 9.6% gain in the previous month. LTG’s tobacco affiliate PMFTC announced chunky price increases across some of its key products effective 27 Feb.

The wholesale price of its flagship brand Marlboro was raised by P20 (+14%) to P155/pack while Fortune and Jackpot prices were also raised by P20 (+17%) to P126/pack. The price increases are much larger than the P5/pack tax increase that took effect in January which would suggest the impact to its earnings will be net positive despite the expected drop in volumes.

 

Among the steepest declines this month came from Globe (-11.0% MoM) and ACEN (-9.4% MoM). Both were deleted from the MSCI index during the rebal at month end.

On the flipside, Meralco (+13.3% MoM), which saw an upweight during the rebal, gained sharply.  The company delivered net profit that came in slightly ahead of estimates.  While the core distribution business came in just short of expectations, the generation business compensated.

Also managing a gain was Aboitiz Power (+4.6%), with sentiment boosted by the tight power supply situation of the sector.

Semirara (-6.25% MoM) continued its downtrend as coal prices trended down.  Newcastle coal stood at US$192.85/MT, down 23% MoM in June after falling 38% in January. Semirara reported FY22 core profit reached P40bn (+145% YoY), representing 86% of consensus estimates and 104% of REGIS forecast.

Apart from its MSCI deletion, a disappointing FY 22 result also weighed on sentiment for Globe.   Service revenue was a mild disappointment.  contributions from associates (chiefly Mynt) also came in lower than expected.

PLDT (-4.25% MoM) also dropped. A class action lawsuit has been filed against PLDT and its executives relating to the P48bn capex overrun disclosed in December. There are 14.5m PLDT shares lodged as ADRs, approximately 6.7% of its common shares.

Also causing the stock to fall further, the local government of Makati issued a closure order on the head office of PLDT’s mobile subsidiary Smart Communications. This was due to a supposed tax deficiency on the part of Smart, in the amount of P3.2bn. Operations of PLDT are not expected to be materially affected, however.

 

ICTSI (-3.7% MoM) corrected from its gain in January (+3.7%).  ICTSI is proposing to invest as much as AUD500m (US$343m) into its terminal in Melbourne in order to further improve efficiencies. The Melbourne terminal accounts for an estimated 4% of the gross asset value (GAV) of ICTSI and 9% of its NAV.

Banks outperformed as prospects of higher interest rates ahead are seen to further prop up banks’ margins. Banks have also begun imposing higher interest rates on credit card transactions after the BSP approved the increase in the interest rate cap for credit card receivables from 2.0% to 3.0% per month, or from 24% to 36% pa.

Higher margins have  helped banks’ profits in the latest round yet again. The big three closed up for the month led by BPI (+4.6% MoM).  The bank reported sustained loan growth at 15% YoY. NIMs continued to expand in 4Q22 (+9bp QoQ).

Metrobank (+1.7% MoM) likewise gained, with loans likewise accelerating 15% in 4Q.  NIMs were up though to a lesser degree than the two bigger banks.  BDO’s (+0.5% MoM) results however came in well ahead of consensus forecasts.  NIMs expanded 13bps QoQ. 

UBP (-6.3% MoM) meanwhile corrected, following three months of strong gains (+12.25% in Jan) as investors anticipated its inclusion in the Feb PSEi rebalancing.

Most consumer names in the index decline in February save for Jollibee (+0.8 MoM) which registered a modest gain. Monde (-8.6% MoM) meanwhile declined the most within the sector, correcting from its 19% rise in January and after new of a Quorn product recall in the UK.

Puregold (-6.0% MoM), Wilcon (-1.1% MoM), and URC (-0.9% MoM) all declined. 

Robinsons Retail (-6.45% MoM) declined, despite FY22 NPAT surpassing expectations. EBIT continued to show recovery, with consolidated sales growing 17% YoY for the quarter on the back of 11.8% SSSG.  

Property stocks were mostly down as the higher-than-expected January inflation raised concerns that interest rates would remain high for a longer period. Ayala Land (-2.7% MoM) declined for the month. SM Prime (-7.7% MoM) likewise fell, reporting a 26% QoQ drop in reservation sales.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation zooms to 8.7% in January. Headline CPI rose by 8.7% YoY in January, accelerating further from 8.1% YoY the previous month. It also exceeded consensus expectations (+7.6%) and the BSP’s forecast range (+7.5%-8.3%). On a MoM basis, CPI rose by 1.7%, of which 1.0% was due to food (ie. fish, vegetables, and dairy) and 0.3% was from higher utilities (water, electricity). Core inflation (ex-fuel, food) jumped to 7.4% in January from 6.9% in December. To reach the BSP’s and consensus’ FY23E average inflation target of 4.5%, CPI would have to drop by -0.2% MoM in succeeding months (or average 4.1% for the rest of the year). To reach Regis’ FY23E average inflation target of 5.5%, CPI should be flat MoM (or average 5.4% for the rest of the year). The Monetary Board will be meeting on Feb 16.

BSP raises policy rates by 50 basis points. The BSP raised its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to 6.0%. Overnight borrowing rate of 6.0% is the highest level since November 2008. The BSP has raised its benchmark rate by 400 basis points since May 2022. BSP Governor Medalla said that more increases are possible and that a pause during the March meeting was unlikely. The BSP also raised its 2023 inflation forecast to 6.1%, from 4.5% previously. Inflation accelerated to a 14-year high of 8.7% in January. The Central Bank expects inflation to return to the 2-4% band in 2024. It increased its 2024 inflation forecast to 3.1%, from 2.8% previously. The BSP noted that upside CPI pressures emanate from global food market uncertainties, shortages in key food items, transport fare hikes, elevated oil prices and higher-than expected wage adjustments.

 

Remittances rise 5.8% YoY to record high of US$3.16bn in December.  Cash remittances coursed through banks reached a record US$3.16bn in Dec 2022. This is up 5.8% YoY and 14.1% MoM. The 3-month average growth rate accelerated to 5.0% in Dec from 4.3% in November. In PHP terms, remittances rose 17.2% YoY during the month. Most sources of remittances saw YoY, MoM improvements. This brought FY22 remittances to US$32.54bn, up 3.6% YoY, broadly in line with the BSP’s target (+4%) and equivalent to 8.0% of GDP. For 2023, the BSP is forecasting remittances to grow by 4%.

GIR rises by 3.7% MoM to US$99.724bn in January. Preliminary data showed that the country’s gross international reserves rose to US$99.7bn in end-January. This is up by 3.7% (US$3.574bn) MoM. Reflecting issuance of US$3bn ROP Global Bonds in early January. The GIR level represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports.

 

Government debt reaches P13.4tn in end-2022. The National Government’s total outstanding debt settled at P13.4tn as of end-2022. This is up by 14.4% YoY (+P1.69tn) but down by 1.7% MoM (-P225bn). Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% (vs. 69.7%) of total while external debt accounted for 31.4% (vs 30.3% in 2021). The debt stock is equivalent to 60.9% of GDP, below the government’s target of 61.8%. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.4% in 2023. It aims to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and further down to 51% by 2028.

FDI drops by 44% YoY, 14% MoM to US$793mn in November. Foreign direct investments (FDI) recorded US$793mn net inflows in November 2022, down 44% YoY and 14% MoM. This is the lowest monthly figure since Sep 2022. The MoM drop was due to declines in (a) non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments, (b) reinvestment of earnings, and (c) equity other than reinvestment earnings. This brings YT-November net inflows down by 13% YoY to US$8.43bn. The BSP expects FDI net inflows at US$8.5bn by end-2022 and at US$11bn by end-2023.

Commercial banks lending growth slows to 13.4% YoY in December. U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) moderated to 13.4% YoY in December from a revised 13.9% in November. Loans to the manufacturing, ICT, and finance sectors grew at more moderate paces vs. previous months. Loan growth to utilities, trade, real estate, and consumer loans accelerated. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking system rose by 11.5% in November. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Banks, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth accelerated to 6.4% in December from a revised 5.5% in November.

Banks’ NPL ratio improves to 3.17% in December. Preliminary data showed asset quality (all banks) improved across all major ratios in December. NPL ratio further declined to 3.17% in Dec from 3.35% in Nov 2022 and 3.97% in Dec 2021. Past Due (PD) loan ratio improved to 3.80%, down 24bps MoM (Nominal PD loans also declined by 2.7% MoM to P479bn. Restructured Loans (RLs) ratio declined to 2.62%, down 7bps MoM. NPL cover dropped to 106.84% in Dec from 105.73% in Nov 2022. Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) moderated slightly to 11.4% in Dec from 11.5% in November.  

 

PH, Japan ink 7 key deals in Marcos visit. The governments of the Philippines and Japan have inked seven key bilateral agreements spanning a range of areas, including agriculture, infrastructure, and disaster and humanitarian aid.

The agreements are as follows:

  • Exchange of Notes on Japanese Official Development Project: North-South Commuter Railway Extension (NSCR) Project (II)
  • Exchange of Notes on Japanese Official Development Project: NSCR - Malolos to Tutuban Project (II)
  • Loan Agreement for the NSCR Extension Project (II)
  • Loan Agreement for the NSCR- Malolos to Tutuban Project (II)
  • Umbrella Term of Reference (TOR) on Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR) Cooperation
  • Memorandum of Cooperation (MOC) between the Department of Agriculture (DA) and Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries on Agriculture Cooperation
  • MOC in the Field of Information and Communications Technology

Vehicle sales jump 42% YoY in January. CAMPI/TMA reported auto sales rose by 42% YoY to 29,499 units in January but MoM, vehicle sales fell by 21%. CAMPI has yet to give its sales target for 2023. Toyota sold 13,428 units in January, up 36% YoY (-22% MoM). Market share dipped to 45.5% from 46.2% in December. 

Marcos approves Philippine tax refund plan for foreign tourists. President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. has approved a value-added tax refund program for foreign tourists by 2024. An executive order will be issued in order to implement the tax refund program

If enacted, foreign travelers will be refunded the 12% VAT imposed on goods and services, if they spend above a minimum amount. The measure is among the proposals a private sector advisory council presented to Marcos recently to boost the tourism industry. Other initiatives include expanding the e-visa program to Chinese, Indian, South Korean and Japanese nationals. The government is targeting 4.8mn foreign tourists this year that will generate P2.58tn in revenues. In 2019, the country received 8.3mn tourists.

Higher power spot market prices loom. Average wholesale electricity spot market (WESM) prices rose to P7.43/kwh in the first few days of the February billing period. This is up from the P5.76/kwh recorded in January due to a lower supply margin according to the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP). For the rest of February, covering also the remaining days of the Malampaya shutdown, IEMOP projects an average market price of P7.55/kwh. Citing a lower overall supply margin equivalent to 3,882 megawatts compared to January’s 4,979 MW.
