REGIS REPORT

01 February 2023
 

The Philippine kicked off the year with 3.5% MoM in January, to close at 6793.25. Foreign inflows had helped to put the market close to 7100 levels, rising as high as 8.1% MTD (to 7094.86 close).  The market went into consolidation mode however post the run up paring MTD gains. Foreign selling at the end of the month also reversed a large portion of the rise.

Inflation for December came in at 8.1%, accelerating from the previous month’s print of 8.0%.  Food and Utilities (Electricity, Gas, Fuel) drove the headline inflation figure higher. While  inflation pressure has not completely dissipated,  the BSP says there is less pressure to keep up with the Federal Reserve’s tightening due to the weakening of the US$.  BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that the BSP will likely raise interest rates by 25 or 50 basis points during its next meeting on Feb 16. The PHP appreciated 2% in January.

The trade deficit came in at US$4.6bn in December, taking the full-year trade deficit to US$58bn.  This is not only the highest in absolute terms, but at 14%, is the widest relative to GDP. Nonetheless, while the trade deficit persists, falling commodity prices is helping this narrow and this should helping ease pressure on the current account portion of the BOP.

The FY22 BOP deficit reached US$7.3bn, a reversal of the US$1.3bn surplus in 2021 due to a wider trade deficit. The BOP deficit is equivalent to 1.8% of GDP, assuming the BSP’s GDP forecast. For 2023, the BSP expects the BOP to remain at a deficit of US$5.4bn, equivalent to 1.3% of GDP.

The 4Q GDP print came in at +7.2%, higher than Street and Regis expectations. Personal consumption remained strong, rising 7.0% in the latest quarter. it was net imports that lifted GDP growth in 4Q, as the drop here +1.5ppts to GDP. Full-year 2022 GDP growth stood at +7.6%. A slowdown from reopening or “revenge spending” should be cushioned by  the decline in global commodity prices, as well as domestic minimum wage hikes and personal income tax cuts.

Key data
 

Foreigners were net buyers for US$122m in January. Foreign buying was consistent throughout the month with the market seeing net inflows in all but four trading days in January.

There were some oversized blocks that skewed net foreign flows and turnover. Among them was an AC Energy placement which helped boost inflows by US$13m after Mermac, a local entity, sold shares to a mix of both local and foreign investors.  Meanwhile, Robinsons Retail’s purchase of a 4% stake in BPI for US$275m helped increase average turnover this month.

Average daily volume was at US$145m, with foreign participation at 37%.

                               

Biggest inflows were seen in banks BDO and Metrobank, and conglomerates Ayala Corp., GT Capital, and JG Summit as foreign investors flocked to these sectors given their scope and liquidity.  Meanwhile, biggest outlfows were seen in telcos Globe, PLDT, and Converge.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The Philippine rose 3.45% MoM in January to close at 6793.25. Foreign inflows had helped to put the market close to 7100 levels, rising as high as 8.05% MTD (to 7094.86 close).  The market went into consolidation mode however post the run up paring MTD gains. Foreign selling at the end of the month also reversed a large portion of the gains.

Nonetheless, index gainers outnumbered losers 11 to 4 in January. Unsurprisingly, the top index mover was BDO (+16.2% MoM), with its liquidity and market cap helping to capture buy interest.

Apart from BDO, banks in general continued to be in favor given the current interest rate environment. Further boosting sentiment for the banks, the BSP raised the cap on credit card interest rates from 2% to 3% a month (from 24% to 36% annually) for unpaid balances. Regis estimates a full-year implementation of the new rate cap could increase NPAT forecasts by 8-12%, with Metrobank (+6.0% MoM)  being potentially the biggest winner. Security Bank (+5.8% MoM) meanwhile also gained.

 

Union Bank (+12.25% MoM), which completed its P12bn rights issue in January,  gained sharply as investors anticipated its inclusion in the PSEi in the Feb rebalancing. 

BPI (+2.9% MoM) rose as well. Robinsons Retail (+8.0% MoM) acquired an additional 4.4% interest in BPI. Following this transaction and the previously-announced merger between BPI and Robinsons Bank, RRHI will have ~6.5% equity interest in BPI. The partnership is expected to result in synergies and cross selling opportunities for BPI.

A slew of other consumer names also registered significant increases in January, led by San Miguel Food and Bev (+19.4% MoM), recovering from a 45.8% drop in 2022. Monde (+19.1% MoM) likewise surged.  Monde’s market share has already begun to recover post the product recall in July last year. Also, investors are beginning to price in margin recovery this year.  Monde was also one of the worst performers last year, dropping 31.6%. 

 

 

Monde announced its acquisition of 15% of restaurant operator Figaro Coffee Group for P820m through the purchase of 820m new shares at P1.00/share --  a modest 12% premium to prevailing market price on announcement. FCG has a network of 124 stores, with 90% of its revenue is derived from its pizza restaurant brand, Angel’s Pizza.

Other strong gainers were Shakey’s (+9.8% MoM) and Wilcon (+8.3% MoM). Large caps Jollibee (+3.5% MoM) and URC (+1.3% MoM) also managed to end with gains.

Likewise capturing buying interest was large cap SM Prime (+3.9% MoM), also one of the top movers in January. Sentiment for the sector however continued to be weighed by the high interest rate environment.

 

Ayala Land (-4.9% MoM) was the top index decliner, with selling accelerating after the PSEi rebal announcement which showed large expected outflows in Ayala Land owing to a large reduction in its free float. 

Megaworld (+6.0% MoM) and Robinsons Land (+2.3% MoM) meanwhile managed a gain despite their announced deletion from the PSE Index in the February rebal. Both stocks rebounded from weakness in the previous months as investors had sold down the stocks in anticipation of their removal. 

Macro proxies likewise gained, buoying the overall market. SM (+2.2% MoM) pushed the market higher as did JG Summit (+6.5% MoM).  GT Capital (+17.2% MoM) also surged. MPI (+18.1% MoM) delivered the largest gain among the conglos. MPI confirmed it is considering a plan to voluntary delist its shares from the Philippine Stock Exchange as part of its ongoing discussions with third-party investors. 

 

 

Meanwhile, Cemex Philippines (+90.2% MoM) almost doubled in January after its major shareholder, Cemex Asia South East Corp launched a tender offer to buy up to 12% of the company. This should bring the public float to just above 10% should it be fully transacted. The tender offer price is set at P1.30/sh, which is a generous 51% premium over its closing price on Jan 23 and a 65% premium to its 1-year VWAP. 

Meanwhile, ACEN (-8.1% MoM) was the top index decliner, giving up its +7.2% rise in the previous month.   A placement in ACEN by Mermac (335m shares at Php6.70) weighed on sentiment. The placed shares represent 0.8% of ACEN’s outstanding shares.  Meantime, ACEN purchased 500m shares (equivalent to a 5% stake) in SP New Energy (+11.7% MoM). The acquisition will be treated as a prepayment of a P1bn loan facility previously extended to the latter.

Other utils was weak, with Semirara (-7.25% MoM) reacting to the correction in coal prices.   Meralco (-6.1% MoM) was likewise down. Aboitiz Power (+6.3% MoM) however continued to benefit from the tight power supply situation given a portion of their portfolio is uncontracted.  

 

Notable moves were seen in Bloomberry  (+20.5% MoM) and Cebu Air (+11.7% MoM), both rising markedly on the back of the China reopening theme.

Telcos were a mixed bag.  Converge (+10.1% MoM) had a strong month, while Globe (-7.3% MoM) lagged.  PLDT (+1.9% MoM) inched up. PLDT is guiding for capex to reach P95-97bn in 2022, higher than earlier guidance of P85bn and reflects the overruns that have yet to be fully booked. The budget overrun was estimated to be around P48bn. Management expects capex to remain  elevated in 2022 and 2023, possibly into 2024.

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

4Q GDP growth slightly moderates to 7.2%. The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that the economy grew by 7.2% in 4Q22, better than consensus (6.8%) and Regis (6.9%) forecasts. Domestic demand slowed to +7% YoY, broadly in line with historical trend (+6.8% from 2009-2019). Consumption remained buoyant, up 7% YoY and contributed 5.3ppts to GDP while government spending accelerated to +3.3% YoY from 0.8% YoY in 3Q. Private investments slowed sharply to 5.9%, with inventory buildup moderating. Net imports dropped and contributed +1.5ppts to GDP. On the supply side, the services sector was the outperformer, offsetting the decline in agricultural production. The services sector grew at a faster rate of +9.8% on “revenge” and holiday spending. Industry sector normalized and grew by 4.8%, below historical trend of +6%. This brings full-year 2022 GDP growth at +7.6%. 

Headline inflation jumps to 8.1% YoY in December. Headline CPI rose by 8.1% YoY and 0.3% MoM in December 2022 vs consensus +8.4% and BSP expectation of +7.8%-8.6%. Core inflation (ex-fuel, food) accelerated further to +6.9% in Dec from +6.5% in Nov. By category, headline inflation was driven by an acceleration in food, utilities, and other service-related categories; tempered by decelerating pump prices. Food prices rose at a faster rate due to higher prices of rice, fruits, vegetables, sugar; but offset by deceleration in other items (e.g. meat, fish) due to favorable base effects. Utilities grew at a slightly faster rate of +14.2% from 13.8% due to higher electricity prices and gas. 2022 average headline inflation rate was at +5.8% (in line with BSP latest upgrade). Assuming the headline CPI rises at a similar +0.3% MoM in the next 12 months, inflation for 2023 could average +5.9%. This implies two things: (1) inflation will unlikely revert to the BSP’s 2-4% target band by 2H23 and (2) it will also unlikely meet the BSP’s 2023 average inflation target of 4.5%. Monetary board meeting on Feb 16; Fed meeting on Feb 2.

GDP grew by at least 7.5% in 2022. The Philippine economy likely expanded by at least 7.5% in 2022 according to Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. This implies 4Q22 GDP growth at +6.9%, moderating from +7.7% in 9M22. The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release 4Q22 GDP figures on Jan 26. The government expects GDP growth to slow down to 6.5% in 2023 due to the expected slow down of the global economy.

 

Cash remittances up 5.7% YoY in November. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 5.7% YoY to US$2.64bn in November. The amount is the lowest in six months due to seasonality; Note that remittances grew by +5.1% YoY in November 2021. In PHP terms, remittances were up +21% YoY and the 3-month moving average growth rate accelerates to +4.3% YoY. This is faster YoY growth rates across regions for land-based and sea-based remittances. YT-November remittances are up 3.3% to US$29.38bn, trending behind the BSP’s 2022 target (+4%).

Trade deficit at US$4.6bn in December; Full-year 2022 trade deficit at US$58bn. Based on preliminary data, the country posted a trade deficit of US$4.59bn in December. While the deficit is lower by 10% YoY, it is nonetheless 24% larger than the US$3.7bn deficit recorded in November. MoM drop can be attributed to (a) exports collapsing to its lowest absolute level since Feb 2021 and (b) imports dropping by 5% MoM. Imports fell 10% YoY, biggest monthly drop since January 2021. This is despite a re-acceleration of fuel import growth to +13% YoY from +8% YoY in November. Capital goods imports fell further, dropping by 11% YoY. Exports dropped 10% YoY, with exports to the US, Japan, HK, Singapore contributing negative 8.7ppts to the drop. This brings full-year 2022 trade deficit to US$58.3bn, equivalent to 14% of GDP.

BOP posts US$612m surplus in December 2022; FY22 BOP deficit at US$7.3bn. The country’s balance of payments posted a surplus of US$612mn in December 2022, down 38% YoY. This is a reversal of the US$756mn deficit in November 2022. The surplus reflected inflows arising mainly from the BSP’s net foreign exchange operations and net income from its investments abroad. GIR increased to US$96.1bn in end-Dec 2022 from US$95.1bn in end-Nov 2022; Latest GIR level represents 7.3 months of imports. The FY22 BOP deficit reached US$7.3bn, a reversal of the US$1.3bn surplus in 2021 due to a wider trade deficit. This is lower than the BSP’s latest estimate of US$11.2bn deficit but larger than its initial forecast of a mild US$0.7bn surplus at the start of the year. The BOP deficit is equivalent to 1.8% of GDP, assuming the BSP’s GDP forecast. For 2023, the BSP expects the BOP to remain at a deficit of US$5.4bn, equivalent to 1.3% of GDP.

 

 

BSP sees 25-50bps rate in next policy meeting. The BSP is likely to raise benchmark interest rates by 25 or 50bps at its meeting on Feb 16 according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla who cited the need to anchor inflation expectations. BSP expects inflation to average 4.5% in 2023 from 5.8% in 2022. The pressure to match the US Fed’s policy tightening is waning and the need for large policy adjustment is “no longer there” and cited a weaker USD. The BSP has raised policy rates by 350bps to 5.5%; the Fed has raised rates by 425bps to 4.25%

BSP: Cut in RRR before end-June very likely. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla said the likelihood of the banks’ reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cut happening before end of June is quite high, especially if inflation starts to slow down. The RRR for big banks is currently at 12%, one of the highest in the region. Reserve requirements for thrift and rural banks are at 3% and 2%, respectively. Medalla reiterated that they did not want to slash the RRR while the BSP is raising the interest rates so as not to confuse the market with policy signals. He added that the BSP will buy dollars to beef up its gross international reserves when the peso hits a certain exchange rate.

Government debt rises to P13.6tn in end-November. The National Government’s total outstanding debt stood at P13.64tn as of end-November 2022. This is up 0.02% (P3.15bn) MoM due to the PHP’s appreciation against the USD. Compared with end-Dec 2021, the government’s debt level is higher by 16.3% (P1.92tn). This is equivalent to 62.5% of GDP (2023E nominal GDP: P21.82tn). Domestic debt rose by 0.78% (P72.7bn) MoM to P9.43tn. External debt declined by 1.62% (P69.58bn) MoM to P4.22tn. The PHP appreciation resulted in a P106.98bn MoM decrease in debt levels, tempered by the impact of third-currency fluctuations against the USD (P50.78bn).

 

 

Foreign borrowings down to US$10.3bn in 2022.  Foreign borrowings approved by the BSP declined for the second straight year to US$10.32bn in 2022. This is down by 21.4% from US$13.14bn in 2021 due to lower bond issuances, as well as the significant drop in program loans.

DOE warns of thin power reserves this year, expects 12 yellow alerts. In a virtual briefing, Department of Energy Undersecretary Rowena Guevara warned that electricity end-users in Luzon may encounter supply woes throughout the year, especially during the summer months, due to thin power reserves. She added that yellow alerts are projected to be issued once in March, twice in April, the entire month of May, twice in June, one in September one in October and one in November

The DoE noted the Luzon grid is expected to experience peak demand of 13,125MW in May (+8.3%). The estimates made by the DOE factored in the scheduled outage of power plants, and unscheduled outages could trigger red alerts. A yellow alert means insufficient power reserves or ancillary services while a red alert means a supply deficiency exists which may result in power interruptions.

Infrastructure spending rises 14% YoY to P1.1tn in 11M22. Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 14.1% YoY to P1.10tn in 11M22. This implies that infra spend in November 2022 alone rose 16% YoY and 3.7% MoM to P96.3bn. The government is targeting to spend P1.23tn (+9.5% YoY) in 2022. To reach the target, infra spend for Dec 2022 should be P130bn (down 17% YoY vs. Dec 2021: P157bn). For 2023, the government is targeting to spend P1.25tn (+1.4% YoY).

 

 

NPL ratio drops further in November. Bad debts held by Philippine banks continued to drop in November, with NPL ratio at 3.35%. This is down from 3.41% in October 2022 and 4.35% in Nov 2021. NPLs fell by 0.9% MoM to P408.097bn in November from P411.6bn in October. Loans of the entire banking system (ex-interbank) grew 11.5% YoY in November, slightly faster than the +11.4% in October. Past due loans fell by 13.2% to P492.5bn from P567.512 bn a year earlier. These borrowings are equivalent to 4.04% of the industry’s total loan portfolio, down from 5.12% a year earlier. Restructured loans fell by 4.9% year on year to P327.760 bn in November, from P344.897bn in the same month of 2021. This brought the ratio to 2.69% in November, from 3.11% in the year prior. Banks increased loan loss reserves by 2.7% to P431.501bn in November, from P419.863bn in the year prior. With this, the ratio went down to 3.54% from 3.79% in November a year ago. NPL coverage ratio — which shows the allowance for potential losses due to bad loans — increased to 105.73% from 87.13% a year earlier.

Tourist arrivals hit 2.65m in 2022. The Philippines received 2.65mn international tourist arrivals in 2022 of which, 2.02mn were foreigners while 628k were returning Filipinos (or Philippine passport holders who are permanently residing abroad). Top markets by volume were US (19%), Korea (16%), Australia (5%), Canada (5%). For 2023, the government is targeting 4.8mn international tourist arrivals. In 2019, tourist arrivals hit 8.26mn. Top markets by volume were Korea (24%), China (21%), USA (13%) and Japan (8%).

Vehicle sales up 6% MoM in December; Toyota sales up 5% MoM (+28% YoY). Vehicle sales from CAMPI/TMA rose 6% MoM in December to 37,259 units (+34% YoY). Note that CAMPI/TMA began including Chinese-brand Cherry in Sep 2022. This brought FY2022 car sales to 351k units, +37% YoY. Toyota sold 17,232 units in December, up 28% YoY and 5% MoM and brought FY2022 sales to 174,106 units, +34% YoY, exceeding Toyota’s target of 162k units and Regis 157k units.

 

Government sees 3,600 infra projects worth US$372bn. The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) have started compiling potential infrastructure projects from various agencies that could be implemented through 2028. So far, NEDA has identified 3,600 projects amounting to US$372bn in total. Of which, 180 projects will cost over US$45mn. 206 projects worth US$159bn will be implemented in the short term, of which 87 projects worth US$54bn have been identified for Public-Private partnerships. NEDA chief, Balisacan noted that the government will release the Public Investment Program (PIP) for 2023-2028 this quarter. This will include the three-year rolling infrastructure program for 2024-2026, which aims to inform the public on the budgeting process for key priority projects. The government plans to spend 5-6% of GDP on infrastructure through 2028. 

Marcos approves Philippine tax refund plan for foreign tourists. President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. has approved a value-added tax refund program for foreign tourists by 2024. If enacted, foreign travelers will be refunded the 12% VAT imposed on goods and services, if they spend above a minimum amount. The measure is among the proposals a private sector advisory council presented to Marcos recently to boost the tourism industry. Other initiatives include expanding the e-visa program to Chinese, Indian, South Korean and Japanese nationals.

The government is targeting 4.8m foreign tourists this year that will generate P2.58tn in revenues. In 2019, the country received 8.3m tourists, which spent P600bn (3.1% of GDP). Total tourism spend in 2019 amounted to P3.74tn (19% of GDP), of which P3.14tn (16.1% of GDP) came from domestic tourism.
