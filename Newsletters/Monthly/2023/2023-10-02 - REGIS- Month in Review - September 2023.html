REGIS REPORT

02 October 2023
 

The PSEi climbed 2.4% in September, as it attempted to recover from a 6.3% plunge in August.  The market drifted to as low as -2.2% MTD (Sep 20 close) with the PSEI breaking the 6,100 support level. A strong final week where in the index gained 2.9% (WoW) however, reversed earlier losses.

Still reeling from the weaker-than-expected 2Q GDP print, investors grappled with the worse-than-forecast inflation print. August CPI inflation came in at 5.3%. This was a surprisingly sharp acceleration from the 4.7% in July and well ahead of Street expectations of also 4.7%. Rice prices were the key driver of inflation, with rice price inflation hitting 8.7% for the month. 

It was indeed a month filled with headlines on the rice issue.  The President approved the imposition of price controls on rice (capping rice prices at P41-P45/kg, equivalent to US$730 to $800/MT), with many in government convinced that rising rice prices are no more than a result of price manipulation. Since the signing of the order, the price cap has been beset by complaints including ineffective enforcement, retailer losses and reduced supply, what with wholesale prices already above the price cap. This led to the administration saying it would be providing subsidies to rice retailers to ensure compliance with the rice cap. Meanwhile, various officials already have already hinted at the lifting of price controls even as recommendations to lower rice tariffs have come about. The President, however, subsequently, proclaimed that rice tariffs will not be reduced, contrary to recommendations of the Department of Finance and the National Economic Development Authority (NEDA).

The issue on rice – which is 8.9% of the CPI basket – has made it difficult for BSP to ease monetary policy.   As expected, the BSP kept policy rates unchanged at 6.25% at the September 21 monetary policy meeting. Instead, BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. signaled a rate hike on November 16, with the magnitude of the increase contingent on the inflation print. He also signaled that there will be no rate cut for the first two quarters of 2024.

Meanwhile, a total of five regions – CALABARZON, Central Visayas, Cagayan Valley, Central Luzon and SOCCSKSARGEN – are implementing wage hikes, mostly in the high single digits (%).  These wage hikes should be a boon to consumer spending. On the flipside however, it should translate to higher labor costs and risk raising core inflation.

The BSP reported that the current account deficit narrowed to US$3.6bn in 2Q23, largely on the back of a lower trade deficit, which itself was due to lower commodity prices. The lower CA deficit prompted the BSP to reduce its CA deficit forecast to US$11bn, equivalent to 2.5% of GDP. The external accounts still remain a concern however, and continues to weigh on the local currency.  The PHP held up in September however (+0.04% MoM), helped by the government’s retail dollar bond launch.

Key data
 

It was a pretty eventful month for the Phils, as the market saw 3 rebalancing events which helped stimulate trading activity. For September, average daily turnover expanded to US$130m vs US$97m the month prior. Excluding blocks and rebal days, ADV was at US$92m. Net Foreign outflow grew to US$472m this month, with the market seeing 19 straight days of net selling only to be broken on the last two trading days of the month.

  • There were three sets of PSEi rebalancings this month: two of them off-cycle as MPI and AP got booted out and were replaced by CNPF and BLOOM. Phils saw overall outflow at the quarterly FTSE rebalancing as AP got booted out from the Large Mid Cap index while RCR and RRHI got deleted from the small cap index.  
  • AP saw the biggest outflow due to its deletion from the FTSE. The stock fell 6.2% this month and saw $47.7m worth of net outflow. The company took advantage of the liquidity event by buying back its own stock, which in turn prompted the stock’s public float to fall below the minimum requirement, triggering its deletion from the PSEi.
  • MPI  also saw huge outflows (-$25.5m), with foreigners tendering their shares in anticipation of a delisting from the exchange.
  • Other notable outflows; CNPF -$13.4m as foreigners took advantage of the liquidity event to take profit on its inclusion in the PSE index. TEL -$15.9m continue to see foreign selling post soft 2Q numbers but still managed to end the month green despite selling pressure.
  • Foreign inflows were seen in GTCAP. The stock was the  top foreign bought and only saw $5.4m worth of inflow. Foreigners have been net buying in the name for the 5th straight month on positive outlook on Toyota.
  • Other notable foreign buying; MONDE +$2.6m and EW +1.7m but both names saw a surge in share price on bargain hunting.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

It was a month for this year’s laggards. The sharpest move in September came from Monde (+25.4% MoM), which prior to September had been down 35% YTD (Jan – Aug).  Converge (+23.6% MoM) also climbed sharply, after falling a whopping 51.1% in the Jan- Aug period.

Apart from Monde, other consumer names that pared YT-August losses were Universal Robina (+6.4% MoM) and Puregold (+3.6% MoM). 

The property sector, which itself has lagged the market, also helped to move push the index higher.  Ayala Land (+8.5% MoM) which contributed 20.2% of the PSEi’s overall 145.99-index point rise for the month, was the top mover in September.  The stock had been down close to 12% YT-August. Ayala Land disclosed that its CEO Bernard Dy will be retiring after nine years at the helm of ALI. Dy will be replaced by Anna Margarita Bautista-Dy who is currently COO, and also heads the residential and mall business of the group.

 

SM Prime (+3.8% MoM) was also among the top index movers, chipping in 21 index points (or 14.4%) to the PSEi’s rise. SM Prime gained after a 12.6% drop in the previous month which came after the national government suspected all Manila Bay reclamation projects. The company’s Pasay and Paranaque reclamation projects account for a combined 37% of Regis’s SMPH NAV estimate.

Meanwhile, SM Prime says mall revenue for 3Q is likely to exceed 2Q. This is a pleasant surprise as 3Q is typically a leaner period compared to 2Q for malls. Meanwhile, SM Prime’s residential reservation sales are likely to reach record levels in 2023, though note that presales are gross of backouts or cancellations.

Other property names managed gains, with Robinsons Land (+3.8%), Vista Land (+3.1%), and Megaworld (+1.5% MoM) ending the month higher.

 

 

Banks had a good month, as the BSP’s hawkish tone point to rates staying supportive of strong NIMs.  Large caps BDO (+1.9% MoM), and BPI (+1.8% MoM) led the sector.

Union Bank (-6.5% MoM) moved lower as the PSE announced that it will be removed from the main stock market benchmark, the PSEi (PCOMP) at the close of business on October 3. The PSEI decided that UBP’s float was actually lower than the 20% required threshold.  Replacing UBP will be Nickel Asia (+18.8% MoM), which surged on the back of the inclusion news.

The PSE revised the composition of the PSEi in September.  Aboitiz Power (-6.2% MoM) was removed from the PSEi and Bloomberry Resorts (-8.7% MoM) was added at the close of September 25. Bloomberry corrected for the month however, after a top-up placement in the name was announced on  priced at P10/sh (7.1% discount to Sep 28 close).  

Following the disclosures that AP and one of its directors bought AP shares in the market, AP’s public float fell below 20% the minimum threshold for any stock to be part of the PSEi. 

On the second tranche of the rebalancing, at the close of September 26, Metro Pacific (+2.6% MT-26Sep) was deleted from the PSEi. The consortium that proposed to take MPI private secured enough shares to delist it after conducting a tender offer. Replacing MPI was Century Pacific (+3.5% MoM). 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation accelerates to 5.3% in August. Headline inflation for August accelerated to 5.3% from 4.7% in July. This is above street expectations (4.7%) and at the high-end of BSP’s forecast range: 4.8%-5.6%. On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose at its fastest rate in six months at +1.1%. This was due largely to faster price increases in food items, particularly rice (+8.7% YoY in Aug from 4.2% in July), vegetables, and fish due to weather disturbances. Higher fuel prices also pushed transport CPI up 7.9% MoM and tempered the YoY reduction in fuel prices to just 9.1% from 23% in July. Meanwhile, core inflation decelerated to 6.1% from 6.7%. This brings average YT-August inflation rate at 6.6%, still above the government’s target range of 2-4%. The BSP retained its view that inflation will decelerate back to its 2%-4% target band in 4Q. To achieve this, MoM CPI changes for the rest of the year should be flat. The next Monetary Board meeting is on Sep 21; Fed’s next policy meeting is on Sep 20.

BSP keeps rates but signals rate hike in November. The BSP maintained its key policy rate at 6.25% for the fourth consecutive month. It raised its 2023/2024 average inflation rate forecasts to 5.8%/3.5% (from 5.6%/3.3% previously). The gov’t cited spillovers from weather disturbances, rising global crude oil prices, and the PHP’s depreciation against the USD. In YT-Aug, inflation has averaged 6.6% and the BSP thinks inflation will remain above its 2-4% target range until October. BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. also signaled a hike on November 16. The magnitude of the increase will depend on how bad inflation will be. He also signaled that there will be no rate cut for the first two quarters of 2024.

Hawkish monetary policy stance stays until inflation reverts to BSP’s 2-4% target. The BSP would like to see inflation go back within the 2-4% target band before any policy easing is considered due to the need to anchor inflation expectations. According to BSP Deputy Governor Francisco Dakila, Jr during the Philippine Economic Briefing in Dubai. The BSP sees inflation falling within the target range in 4Q23, barring unprecedented supply shocks. 

Remittances rise 2.6% YoY in July. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.6% YoY to US$2.99bn in July. In PHP terms, remittances were up just 0.8% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate for July was at 2.5% YoY. This brought the 7M23 remittances to US$18.79bn, up 2.9% YoY. This is tracking within the BSP’s FY23 growth target of +3% YoY (US$33.5bn).

BSP sees within-target inflation by 1Q24. BSP Governor Eli Remolona now sees inflation landing within its target band of 2-4% by 1Q24, not 4Q23. The BSP will likely revise its inflation forecast of 5.6% for 2023 at its next policy meeting on Sep 21. Average inflation in YT-August stood at 6.6%, with inflation for August alone at 5.3%. Mr. Remolona said the central bank still has space for further tightening. 

Marcos rejects proposal to lower rice tariffs; Price cap to remain. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has rejected a proposal to reduce tariffs on imported rice, saying the projection of world rice prices was to go down. The decision was made in consultation with the economic managers. Dept. of Finance, Dept of Budget and Management and National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) had previously sought to suspend rice tariffs. During the sectoral meeting, NEDA Chief Balisacan and other agriculture officials agreed with the President’s decision. Marcos said the price cap would continue to be in effect since it has to be studied carefully.

Gov’t to provide subsidies to rice retailers affected by price caps. Pres. Marcos said that the gov’t will provide subsidies to rice retailers amid the retail price caps set. The gov’t imposed nationwide price ceiling on rice of P41/kg of regular milled rice and P45/kg for well-milled rice. The price controls are a “temporary measure”, and the gov’t expects the prices of rice to ease when new shipments arrive in mid-September. House Speaker Martin Romualdez is looking to allocate P2bn for the subsidy program.

DTI urges manufacturers to hold off on price hikes.The Dept of Trade and Industry (DTI) will meet with manufacturers of basic necessities and prime commodities to urge them to postpone price increases. The DTI confirmed 14 pending price increase requests from manufacturers covering 43 SKUs. These products include canned sardines, evaporated milk, powdered milk, coffee, instant noodles, bottled water, and canned meat. The planned price increases range from 1-5% for food items and 6-10% for non-food items. Manufacturers are opposing the postponement, citing increasing raw material and fuel cost.

BOP deficit narrows to US$57mn in August. The country posted a balance of payments (BOP) deficit of US$57m in August. This is lower by 90% YoY (vs. Aug 2022 deficit of US$572m) but higher by 7% MoM (July 2023: US$53m). This brings YT-August BOP to a surplus of US$2.1bn, a reversal of the US$5.5bn deficit recorded in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$100mn by year-end.

Budget deficit widens to P133bn in Aug. The country’s budget deficit widened to P133bn in Aug from a P72bn deficit in the same period last year. On a MoM basis, the deficit is nearly 3x vs July. The government’s revenues contracted 6.6% YoY. The lower collections were attributed to work suspensions brought about by the heavy rains. According to DoF Sec. Diokno, collections should improve in the following months. Meanwhile, expenditures rose 10% YoY, driven mainly by interest payments (+39% YoY). The YT-August deficit stood at P732.5bn (+12% YoY), tracking behind the gov’t target of P1.5tn.

Current account deficit narrows in 2Q23, 1H23; BSP lowers BOP deficit targets. The BSP reported the current account stood at a deficit of US$3.6bn in 2Q23. This is lower than the US$4.6bn deficit in 1Q23 and US$4.2bn in 2Q22. The government cited (a) narrowing trade deficit of US$16bn (vs. US$17bn in 1Q23 and US$19bn in 2Q22) and (b) higher receipts from trade services (e.g travel) and secondary income accounts. This brings 1H23 current account deficit to US$8.2bn, lower than the US$12.1bn deficit in the same period last year. Following its quarterly review, the BSP lowered its FY23E BOP deficit target to US$100m due to a lower current account deficit forecast. The lower current account was due to a smaller trade deficit forecast and higher forecasts for travel receipts. This was offset by lower FDI and foreign portfolio investments forecasts. 

Trade deficit narrows to US$4.2bn in July. Preliminary data showed the trade deficit shrank by 30% YoY to US$4.2bn in July. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit in July was higher by 7% (vs. June 2023: US$3.94bn). Imports fell 15% YoY in July, showing similar trends as in June. Due to base effects from last year’s high commodity prices, with fuel and raw material imports falling 34% YoY and 22% YoY, respectively. Consumer goods imports rose 6% while capital goods imports dipped 4% YoY. Exports fell 1% YoY, reversing positive YoY growth rates in May and June. Decline due to “other manufactured goods” (-50% YoY), mineral products (-79% YoY), chemicals (-34% YoY), and coconut oil (-86% YoY). Electronic exports actually rose 8% YoY. Brings YT-July trade deficit to US$32.18bn, down 10% YoY. The government is forecasting exports and imports to grow by 1%/2% YoY, respectively, suggesting a trade deficit of US$60bn (~14% of GDP) in FY23. The trade deficit in 2022 stood at US$58.24bn (14% of GDP). 

Government plans another US$1bn bond sale this year. The Philippine government is looking to return to the international bond market before year end or in 1Q24 with a US$1bn Islamic/sukuk bond offering, according to National Treasurer Rosalia de Leon. It also plans to issue US$2bn through retail bonds this month. This is in line with its programmed US$5bn overseas bond sales for the year.

Study on reclamation policy could take one year. Environment Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo-Loyzaga said that her department was in talks with scientists from the University of the Philippines Marine Science Institute and the Manila Observatory to conduct a study on reclamation policy. She said that the study could take up to one year. SM Prime’s Manila Bay reclamation projects comprise 37% of our (P48.27/share) NAV estimate. Pasay reclamation – 20% - P9.6/share. Paranaque reclamation – 17% - P8.2/share.

 

Two senators reportedly withdraw support for POGO ban. In its Biz Buzz section, the Philippine Daily Inquirer reported that two senators withdrew their support for a ban on the POGO sector. The Senate Committee on Ways and Means recommends a three-month phaseout of POGO firms. Earlier 10 of the 18 members signed the Bill. The two senators reportedly prefer a longer phaseout period of two to three years. The POGO sector occupies about 800,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. This is lower than 1.4m sqm as of end-2019. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 17% as of 2Q23 and POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

NAIA privatization attracts three bidders so far. The Department of Transportation (DOTr) received interest from three (3) potential bidders so far, according to Secretary Jaime Bautista. Manila consortium consisting of six Philippine conglomerates – AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners, San Miguel Corp (SMC PM) and India-based GMR.

DOTr will be inviting more foreigners to participate in the auction in the next few weeks. The government prefers an operator with a track record and “very good” financial background. Based on the instructions to bidders document, the bidders will be competing on the government’s percentage share of gross revenues, which will exclude passenger service charge. Financial terms that are fixed are (1) P30bn upfront payment and (b) P2bn annuity payment. The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62m annually from 32m today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years). Submission of bids set on Dec. 27.
