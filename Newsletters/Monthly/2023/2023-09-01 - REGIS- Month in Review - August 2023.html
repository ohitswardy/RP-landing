REGIS REPORT

01 September 2023
 

The Philippines plunged 6.3% in August to close at 6175.25. Foreigners sold down the market amidst illiquid conditions, causing deeper drops in share prices.  Speculation on the Fed’s next moves continued to influence direction. Meanwhile, a disappointing 2Q GDP print served as impetus for accelerated selling.

Sentiment went further downhill in the wake of the weak Phils GDP print. GDP growth in 2Q settled at 4.3%, well below the Street’s 6.0% forecast, and markedly slower than the 1Q GDP growth of 6.4%. Personal Consumption Expenditure, which settled at 5.5% YoY (from 6.4% in 1Q23) was broadly in line. The disappointment stemmed from the sharp drop in Government expenditures (-7.1% YoY), and the flat (0.0%) performance of Investments.  

Headline inflation came in at 4.7% for the month of July, slowing further from June (+5.4%) due to base effects from lower energy prices. Inflation came in a tad lower than Street expectations, providing room for BSP to keep rates unchanged despite the Fed’s recent rate hike.

Indeed, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) held policy rates steady at 6.25% in its August meeting, with statements from the BSP appearing mildly hawkish. In earlier briefings, BSP Governor Remolona has spoken of possible tightening to head off a potential uptick in inflation. 

The threat of higher inflation, combined with weak GDP complicates matters for policy rate direction.  With expectations of delayed rate cuts, despite the GDP print showing the 425bp increases in policy rates so far has stifled private investments, the PHP has been under pressure. The PHP fell 3.01% in August to US$56.602/US$.

This is not at all surprising, given the recent spikes in fuel prices and rice. Rice has been in the headlines recently as predictions of reduced harvests has led to fears of supply issues. Retail rice prices in the country are up ~17% YTD and rice is 9% of the CPI basket. The landed cost of imports is a crucial determinant of local prices of food items, given the country’s dependence on food imports.  The economic team is apparently pushing to retain lower tariffs on a number of import commodities in order to help curb inflation.

The government has issued the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) governing the Maharlika Investment Fund. The Maharlika Investment Fund is the sovereign wealth fund recently created by an act of Congress. Among the key provisions outlined by the Finance Secretary include the infusion of P125bn from the National Government and two government banks to provide initial capital. It also bears close watching who will be appointed to run the fund, and eventually, what kind of investments it will make.

The government officially launched the bidding for the P170.6bn takeover and rehabilitation of Manila’s airport NAIA.  Submission of bids is set on Dec 27 this year.  Meanwhile, the Department of Transportation (DOTr) is trying to court Japanese firms to participate in the PPP program, which will include the privatization of operations for the Metro Manila Subway, the North-South Commuter Rail (NSCR) and the NAIA.

Key data
 

Foreigners turned net sellers in August, with large flows coming out of SM Prime and parent SM Investments. The quarterly rebalancing of the MSCI this month saw a decrease in the weighting of the country, spelling outflows for the Phils.

Meanwhile, average daily turnover meanwhile saw some improvement.  August ADV stood at $97m vs $87m in July.

Foreign participation meanwhile fell to 44% vs 49% last month

SM Prime saw the largest outflow of US$31m after the government placed all reclamation projects in Manila Bay under review.  The impact of the suspension could be very material, as SMPH’s reclamation projects accounts for 37% of Regis's NAV estimate. Parent SM, also saw heavy foreign selling of US$22.0m and fell -8.7%, the highest single monthly drop since Feb this year. On the flipside, foreigners accelerated their buying on ALI and the stock was the top foreign bought ion August with an inflow of US$38m. 

Foreign flows were seen in MPI (US$17.2m), with  with foreigners exiting ahead of the tender offer deadline, while AC also saw an outflow of $11.3m mostly due to a decrease in its MSCI weighting.

Telcos saw net outflows except for GLO, but all three names underperformed this month on the back of soft 2Q numbers. Converege fell the most, trading at an all time low.

GTCAP’s Strong 1H23 earnings prompted some interest from foreigners, propelling the stock to break its 52-week range.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Banks were down across the board, in conjunction with the market sell off.  Among the Big Three, Metrobank (-8.0% MoM) dropped the most, despite its 2Q profits boosted by a surge in net interest income.

BDO (-4.0% MoM) fell, even as profit hit an all-time high of P18.7bn (+13% QoQ), underpinned by NIM expansion.  BPI (-3.9% MoM) likewise retreated at the same clip.

Similar to most other sectors, property stocks fell after the weak GDP print.  Despite reporting respectable earnings and reservation sales, shares of Ayala Land (-0.9% MoM), Filinvest Land (-5.8% MoM), Megaworld (-2.9% MoM), and Robinsons Land (-2.5% MoM) also ended the month lower.

SM Prime (-12.6% MoM) was the major underperformer, dropping sharply after the national government suspended all Manila Bay reclamation projects. This puts the company’s Pasay and Paranaque reclamation projects, which account for a combined 37% of Regis’s SMPH NAV estimate, at risk.

 

The weakness at unit SM Prime dragged parent SM Investments (-8.7% MoM) lower. The widely owned large cap also bore the brunt of the weak sentiment for the Philippines in August.

JG Summit (-15.1% MoM) and Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-12.6% MoM) also declined. AEV disclosed it had submitted a bid, together with Coca-Cola Europacific Partners PLC (CCEP), for the bottling operations of Coke Philippines (Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines, Inc or CCBP). The bid was for an enterprise value of USD1.8bn on a “debt-free and cash-free basis” and would be split 60:40 between CCEP and AEV. The foray into the consumer space follows a series of acquisitions triggered by the sale of a significant stake in AP to JERA (USD1.6bn).

Pressure on its dividend outlook weighed on LT Group (-5.0% MoM). PMFTC’s contribution, which largely funds its dividend payments, fell 21%YoY, dragged by declining cigarette sales. Downtrading and illicit trade continue to pressure its outlook in 2H.

 

 

 

GT Capital (+5.0% MoM) rose after the company reported another consensus-beating set of results in 2Q23. Vehicle sales for July also remained strong, with Toyota’s unit sales up 19% YoY in the month. 

MPI (+3.7% MoM) gained, bringing the share price closer to the tender offer price of P5.20/sh. The company also declared a small interim dividend of P0.05/sh, which Regis estimates should cover most if not all friction costs that could arise from participating in the tender offer.

Ayala Corp (+0.2% MoM)  was flat. Its unit AC Industrials announced a welcome divestmentof a loss-making unit. AC Industrials is selling its 92.45% stake in MT Technologies GmbH (MT) to a private equity firm. The sale by AC Industrials of its stake in the German subsidiary was for a nominal one Euro, and will result in a EUR26m loss. AC already made provisions for this loss in its 2Q results.

 

Ayala Corp and Globe (-6.1% MoM) also disclosed more details on their partnership with Gogoro Inc, Gogoro PH.  Gogoro PH will be capitalized at US$8.5m and will engage in the importation and sale of electric scooters apart from developing a battery-swapping network for these e-scooters. Globe will own 49% of the joint venture, AC will own 21% and Gogoro Inc will own 30% of the company

Aside from Globe, the rest of the telco sector was down, as revenue growth challenges ahead weighed on sentiment. PLDT (-12.2% MoM) fell markedly, as 2Q revenue remained sluggish. Converge (-23.1% MoM) fell even more sharply.

Consumer names underperformed mainly due to soft 2Q results and concerns on rising rice prices driving inflation higher and clouding consumer sentiment heading into 2H23. Monde(-12.1% MoM) and URC (-8.9% MoM) succumbed to weak sentiment. Jollibee (-7.1% MoM) declined as well, despite strong operating results in 2Q, with international operations improving.

 

 

Transport stocks also dropped.  ICTSI (-4.5% MoM) ended lower for the month. The company reported better-than-expected 2Q23 results --  2Q23 revenues and EBITDA were better sequentially, as yield per TEU continued to climb and more than compensated for the very marginal improvement in volumes.

Cebu Air’s (-3.7% MoM) 2Q23 earnings failed to impress, after a slew of cancellations in 2Q resulted in a -1% QoQ drop in average fares. Higher jet kero prices during the month also tempered share price performance. 

Bloomberry (-6.7% MoM) corrected, even as 2Q23 profit comfortably beat expectations. The excitement surrounding the potential inclusion of BLOOM in the index peaked after shareholders of Metro Pacific shareholders voted in favor of the voluntary delisting of MPI shares.

 

Semirara (+10.9% MoM) gained, alongside the rebound in global thermal coal prices. The strength in coal prices, as well as the company’s healthy cash levels of P6.50/sh in end-June, support Regis’s view of another special dividend payment of P3/sh (=11% dividend yield) in October/November. 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

2Q23 GDP slows to 4.3% on soft government spending, private investments. 2Q23 GDP came in at 4.3%, slowing from 6.4% in 1Q23 and falling short of consensus estimates (+6%).

On the demand side, both government spending and private investments dragged. Government spending declined by 7.1% and  due to the implementation of the Mandanas ruling. Private investments barely grew, dragged down by a sharp deceleration in construction activity (+2.1%) and inventory withdrawals. The slowdown in construction was due to government (+0.8%) and private households (-3.9%). Consumption normalized to +5.5%, lifted by transport (+30%), restaurants/hotels (+23%), and housing/utilities (+6%) but offset by clothing (-27%), alcoholic bevs and tobacco (-9%) and household furnishings/equipment (-2.7%). Net exports lifted GDP, contributing +0.9ppts to growth. 

On the supply side, the service sector was the sole driver of GDP growth, thanks to strong motor vehicle sales, transport, accommodation/food services. Fisheries pulled down overall agri sector to just +0.2%. The Industry sector moderated to 2.1% due to sharp deceleration of construction.

This brings 1H23 GDP growth to +5.3% YoY. Regis forecast FY23 GDP of 5.5%; To reach this, GDP growth for the rest of the year should be +5.6%. The government believes their target (+6-7% ) is still attainable, as they “formulate catch-up plans, accelerate, and even frontload the implementation of programs and projects”.

Inflation eases further to 4.7% in July. Headline CPI rose 4.7% YoY and 0.1% MoM in July, easing from June (+5.4% YoY, +0.2% MoM). This came in below Bloomberg consensus estimate of 4.9% but at the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.1%-4.9%. Energy-related base effects continued to be the main driver for the YoY deceleration, with transport CPI down 4.6% YoY. Rice prices continued to creep up MoM, up +0.9% in July from just +0.5% in June, pushing overall food CPI up 0.5% MoM in July (vs. +0.3% in June).

Meanwhile, core inflation rose at a slower rate of 6.7% in July from 7.4% in June. Non-food core CPI was up just +0.3% MoM and eased to +5.4% YoY. Note that the minimum wage increase for NCR (+7%) was implemented on July 16. This brings 7M23 average inflation rate to 6.8%. The BSP retained its view that inflation will decelerate back to its 2%-4% target band next quarter. 

BSP keeps policy rates steady. The Monetary Board decided to keep the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight reverse repurchase facility at 6.25%, in line with Street expectations.

However, the signaling for policy settings remained hawkish. BSP Governor Remolona said that the BSP is “ready to tighten”. It is also unlikely to reduce the RRR. The BSP cited risks to inflation remain to the upside, with new threats emerging including a weak peso and higher rice prices. BSP raised its average inflation forecasts for 2023/2024 to 5.6%/3.3% (from 5.4%/2.9% previously). 

Remittances grow 2.1% YoY in June. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.1% YoY to US$2.81bn in June. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 3.7% YoY, slowing from previous months. This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate also slowed to +2.8% YoY in June. Land-based remittances from Japan, UAE, and Qatar lifted overall remittances while sea-based remittances grew 1.9% YoY. This brings 1H23 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$15.793bn. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +3% YoY this year. To reach this, remittances for the rest of the year should average US$2.95bn per month.

Balance of payments narrows to US$53mn in July; End-July GIR revised up to US$100bn. The country’s balance of payments stood at a deficit of US$53mn in July. Narrower than the deficit of US$606mn in June and US$1.8bn deficit in July 2022. This brings YT-July BOP to a surplus of US$2.2bn, a reversal of the US$4.9bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP expects a BOP deficit of US$1.2bn by year end (0.3% of GDP), smaller than the US$7.26bn (1.8% of GDP) in 2022. Meanwhile, the BSP revised the GIR level for end-July to US$100bn (from US$99.7bn). This is up from US$99.4bn in end-June and equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports.

 

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.92bn in June. Preliminary data showed that the trade deficit in June shrunk by 33% YoY and 12% MoM to US$3.92bn. Imports dropped 15% YoY on base effects from high commodity prices last year. Fuel and raw material imports fell 43% YoY / 15% YoY. Consumer goods and capital goods imports were flattish YoY. Exports grew 0.8% YoY in June after rising +2.4% YoY in May.

Exports to China posted positive growth rates for two consecutive months. Exports to HK also rebounded (+16% YoY), reversing declines in the past six months. This brings 1H23 trade deficit to US$27.96bn, down 6% YoY. The government is forecasting exports to grow by 1% YoY and imports to grow by 2% YoY in 2023, suggesting a trade deficit of c. US$60bn (~14% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 stood at US$58.244bn (14% of GDP).

Economic team prefers extension of reduced tariffs on key commodities. National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan said that there is a need to address elevated inflation to achieve its 6-7% GDP growth target. Mr. Balisacan said there is a need to extend the executive order that reduced tariffs on key commodities, which is set to expire in December. He expressed support for extending the reduced tariffs for pork, rice, corn, and coal to keep inflation contained. Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said that the economic team will have another meeting next month to review if there is a need to extend tariffs across all commodities.

Government debt rises to P14.15tn in end-June. The government’s outstanding debt rose by P51.3bn (+0.4%) MoM to P14.15tn in end-June. his is up by P729bn (+5.4%) from end-2022. Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% of total at P9.7tn (+1.2% MoM). External debt accounted for 31.4% of total at P4.4tn (-1.4% MoM). In USD terms, external debt rose by 0.2% MoM to US$80.3mn. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of c. 61% this year, unchanged YoY. Under its medium-term fiscal program, the government expects the ratio to decline to 51% by 2028.

 

Budget deficit narrows in July; YT-July deficit down 21% YoY to P600bn. The government reported a budget deficit of P47.8bn in July, down 45% YoY. Revenues jumped 33% YoY, largely due to timing changes in VAT payments. The customs collections fell 13% YoY likely due to base effects from high commodity prices and weather disruptions. Expenditures grew 16% YoY, accelerating from anemic growth (+0.4% YoY) in 1H23. Interest payments rose at a faster rate of +22% YoY (vs. 10% YoY in 1H). Primary expenditures rebounded by 15% YoY after dipping 1% YoY in 1H23. The YT-July deficit was at P600bn, down 21% YoY and tracking slightly below the government’s target due to higher revenues.

Domestic liquidity growth slows to 5.9% in June. Preliminary data showed that domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 5.9% YoY to about P16.4tn in June 2023. This is slower than the +6.6% YoY growth rate in May and nominal GDP growth of 11.3% in 1H23.

DBM submits P5.77tn national budget to Congress. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) submitted its proposed P5.768tn national budget for 2024. This is 9.5% YoY higher than the 2023 approved budget and is equivalent to 21.7% of GDP. Combined budgets for public works/transportation are up 3.6% YoY to P1.04tn (c. 18% of total budget). Defense budget hiked by 14% YoY but budget for health cut by 2.7% YoY. Under the proposal, the Department of Transportation (DoTr) is seeking P164bn in funding for the construction and maintenance of its seven railways. North-South Commuter Rail (NSCR) will receive P76.3bn, of which P53bn will be used to fund right-of-way (ROW) acquisition. Metro Manila Subway project will get an allocation of P68bn, of which P21bn will be for ROW and tax expenses. MRT-3 will obtain P10.3bn for its maintenance and rehabilitiation. It will also allocate funding for its portion of the LRT-1 Cavite extension and pre-construction works for MRT 4 and PNR South Long Haul.

 

 

Infrastructure spending up 2% YoY in 1H23. Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 2% YoY to P607bn in 1H23. This implies spending in June 2023 alone fell 15% YoY on high base effects from the lifting of last year’s election ban. The 2Q23 infra spend rose 8% YoY to P363bn. The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn (+1% YoY) in FY23, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP.

Government opens bidding for NAIA privatization. The Department of Transportation (DOTr) has officially launched the bidding for the P170.6bn contract to operate and maintain the NAIA. The operator will need to raise passenger capacity to 62mn per year from 32mn today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years). Based on the bidding schedule, the DOTr will issue the draft concession agreement on Sept. 8 and will host site visits for interested parties between Aug. 28 and Nov. 14.

BSP Governor says 5% reserves ratio possible. BSP Governor Eli Remolona said that “eventually” the RRR will be reduced to 5% or 0%

RRR is currently at 9.5%. The timing of when to cut RRR is crucial and not while the BSP is still in a hawkish policy stance amid a still elevated inflation expectations. The BSP may reconsider the cut by end-2023. Regis estimates every 100bps RRR cut could increase NPAT for banks under our coverage by 1.2%-1.5%.

July tourist data - big jumps in Korean, Chinese, Japanese visitors. Tourist arrivals jumped in July, up 75% YoY and up 15% MoM to 516,304, a new post-Covid high. This is roughly 75% of the 2019 monthly average (72% of July 2019). For 7M23, the Philippines took in 3.21mn tourists, up 189% YoY and coming in at about 66% of 7M2019 levels. July was notable in that Regis saw big jumps in arrivals from Korea, China and Japan, all of which are at post-Covid highs. Korean tourists are already at 84% of the 2019 monthly average, while Japanese visitors are about halfway there. Chinese tourists, meanwhile, are just under a quarter of where they were pre-Covid. It is difficult to estimate, though, how much of the Chinese visitors were POGO employees. The improving tourism picture bodes well for the Current Account, and the economy in general. In terms of stocks, BLOOM and CEB are the likely main beneficiaries.

 

Vehicle sales jump 33% YoY in July.Vehicle sales rose 33% YoY, 2% MoM to 37,086 units in July. This brings 7M23 sales up 31% YoY to 239,501 units. Toyota sold 110,158 units in 7M23, up 17% YoY. This implies unit sales for July rose 19% YoY, 1% MoM to 16,583 units. To reach Regis FY23E (19,200 units, +8% YoY), monthly sales for the rest of the year only need to average 16.4k units. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM), accounting for c. 20% of profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 52% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,120/sh and 5x FY23E PER.

Bureau of Treasury issues IRR for Maharlika Investment Fund.The Bureau of Treasury issued the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) Act of 2023 on Aug 28. The IRR shall take effect on Sep 12. The Maharlika Investment Corporation (MIC) will have an authorized capital stock of P500bn, of which P125bn will be subscribed at the time of incorporation. The National government will contribute P50bn to the initial capitalization and this will be sourced from (a) 100% of dividends of the BSP for the first and second fiscal years, (b) 10% share from the income of PAGCOR for a period of five years, (c) 10% of revenues from gaming operations of other state-owned gaming operators/regulators, privatization proceeds, and other sources.

Other GFIs and government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs) may invest into the MIF as well, subject to their respective investment and risk management strategies. Those providing social security and public health insurance services, however, are absolutely prohibited from investing in the MIF. The MIC’s board of directors will be composed of Secretary of Finance as ex officio chairperson, President and chief executive officer (PCEO) of the MIC as vice chairperson, President and CEO of the LBP, President and CEO of the DBP and two regular directors with three independent directors from the private sector.
