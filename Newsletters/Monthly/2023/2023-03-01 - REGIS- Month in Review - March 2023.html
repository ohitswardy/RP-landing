REGIS REPORT

01 March 2023
 

The Philippines ended 0.9% lower in March, wrapping up a volatile month of trading.   The market was up 1.5% MTD in early March but dropped to as much as -3.2% MTD mid-month before finally settling at 56.5 index points lower to close just below 6500. Market sentiment was cloudy for the most part, marred by the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank and then Credit Suisse, as the market discounted the direction of interest rates moving ahead.

CPI inflation for February stood at 8.6%, inching slightly lower than the 8.7% print in January and also falling below Street expectations of 8.9%. Headline CPI was flat on a MoM basis, as prices of food and petrol dropped, offsetting the uptick in utilities.  With food prices and fuel prices normalizing, CPI inflation is expected to trend lower, barring any significant new supply shocks. 

The Monetary Board of the Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised policy rates by 25bps, taking it to 6.25%. The gap between US and Philippine policy rates remained at 1.0% to 1.25% - still narrower than what it has been historically. The BSP also reduced its inflation forecast to 6.0% (from 6.1%) and 2.9% (3.1%) for 2023 and 2024.

The BSP has raised rates by 425bps since last year. According to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla, however, it may be too early for the BSP to pause, unless prices are seen falling. Expectations surrounding the Fed's next moves will also be key.  The next monetary policy meeting is on May 18. 

Slightly positive notes on the country’s twin deficit emerged during the month.  The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas has tweaked its FY23 expectations for the country’s current accounts deficit, lowering it to US$17.1bn (from US$19.9bn), equivalent to 4.0% of GDP. The BSP is also looking at BOP deficit of US$1.6bn in 2023 (-0.4% of GDP) from US$7.3bn in 2022.  This is narrower than its previous projection of a US$5.4bn, equivalent to 1.3% of GDP. The better outlook has been attributed to improved expectations on BPO revenues, tourism/travel-related accounts, FDI as well as correcting global fuel prices.

Key data
 

March outflows accelerated to US$498m, bringing YTD net foreign selling to US$518m. Multiple block trades skewed foreign flows in March. An ICTSI and Monde Nissin block registered US$375m and US$88m in net foreign outflows, respectively. Meanwhile, an Ayala Land REIT placement and a Cemex tender offer clocked in $23.8m and $29.4m of net inflows each. Excluding these,  foreigners would have been net sellers in the market for US$88m.

Foreign participation remained steady at 47% (48% last month). 

Biggest outflows were seen in telcos and Globe and PLDT. Globe saw follow through foreign selling after being deleted from MSCI in February while PLDT continued to see foreign selling pressure after reporting weak FY22 results and amid the P48bn budget overrun issue.

Property names also saw large outflows, with Ayala Land, Megaworld, and Robinsons Land all top foreign sold.

Monde Nissin saw late foreign selling on the back of weak FY22 results. The company disclosed a capital restructuring after a P20bn writedown in its Quorn business impacted retained earnings.  

Meanwhile, large inflows were only seen in ICTSI and BDO this month.

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Converge (-21.3% MoM) was the top index decliner in March, dropping sharply on the back of disappointing FY22 earnings. Profit for 4Q alone was down 32% YoY as margins fell.

PLDT (+10.5% MoM) also recorded a soft 4Q.  Mobile revenue contracted by 4% in FY22 and 3% in 4Q. The stock gained in March however, posting some recovery this month, after dropping a cumulative 26.7% since the start of December last year.

PLDT announced sale of another 1,012 towers to Frontier Tower for P12.1bn. Frontier Tower is a subsidiary of Pinnacle Towers, which is backed by KKR. This latest transaction would bring total proceeds of PLDT’s tower sales to P98bn. PLDT also announced it was buying Sky Cable, the pay TV cum broadband operator held by ABS-CBN. The acquisition price for 100% is P6.75bn. Sky Cable has a reasonably sized broadband subscriber base (~350,000 subscribers) on top of a pay TV operation (~300,000 subscribers).  The deal is subject to approval by the Philippine Competition Commission. 

Globe (+5.0% MoM) also ended the month with gains, after four consecutive months of declines.

Monde (-18.7% MoM) was among the large index drags in March. The stock was sold down after FY22 profits disappointed, as high cost inventories took their toll on margins. Monde also recorded a massive impairment charge for its alternative meat business and announced a capital restructuring as a result.

The consumer sector was mostly down in March, despite fairly consistent healthy topline growth a main feature of the 4Q22 reporting season.  Jollibee’s (-6.7% MoM) reported inline operating profits during the quarter.  Group revenues though were robust, rising 37% YoY in 4Q22 led by the Philippines (63% of total), where SSS rose 39% YoY. Wilcon (-3.75% MoM) delivered continued margin expansion and likewise saw strong sales momentum, with SSSG of +10% in 4Q, a healthy number given the relatively higher YoY base. Puregold (-0.6% MoM) was relatively flat. 4Q22 net sales grew 16% YoY on the back of +12% consolidated SSSG (+12%), accelerating from 3Q.

Meanwhile, Universal Robina (+6.6% MoM) was the outlier within the sector, ending the month with gains. Strong top line momentum continued in 4Q22 with consolidated revenues jumping 35% YoY as volumes were pushed by reopening and holiday spending.

 

 

The big three banks closed up for the month.  BDO (+4.1% MoM) led, followed by BPI (+1.7% MoM). BPI disclosed that it would distribute 406.18m treasury shares (a result of the BPI Family Bank merger in Jan 2022) via a property dividend, with existing shareholders receiving 0.08964 shares for every 1 share held. The stock went  ex-div on 24 March with record date 29 March.

Metrobank (+0.5% MoM) also managed a gain, reporting profits slightly ahead of street expectations and loan growth of 14.7% and slight NIM expansion QoQ.

The smaller banks did not fare as well.  Union Bank (-3.3% MoM) fell as did Security Bank (-10.5% MoM) with the latter reporting NIM contraction in 4Q22.

The  property sector remained out of favor given the interest rate environment.   Large cap property names SM Prime (-3.7% MoM) and Ayala Land (-7.0% MoM) weighed on the index in March. Ayala Land sold 205m shares of AREIT (-9.3% MoM), in order to keep it in compliance with the 33% minimum public float rule. The shares sold are equivalent to 12% of AREIT’s outstanding capital before the AREIT’s purchase of assets from ALI via a share-for-asset swap. AREIT’s float would have fallen below 33% after the swap.

 

Megaworld (-1% MoM) also dropped, falling almost in line with the market, despite  a strong EBIT jump in FY22 (+32%, ahead) and 4Q22 reservation sales rising 30%. 

Robinsons Land (-2.4% MoM) likewise registered a drop, even as 4Q22 reservation sales surged (+124% YoY) to record levels.  Robinsons Land expanded its share buyback program to P9bn from P6bn. The original P3bn buyback program was announced in November 2021. It was then expanded to P6bn in November 2022. The company has spent P4.1bn to purchase 238mn RLC shares.

Vista Land (-5.0% MoM) also underperformed, while Filinvest Land (-7.4% MoM) registered a steeper drop. FLI’s office vacancy was flat QoQ at 32%, much higher than industry average of 18%. This is expected to deteriorate, with the company expecting its office vacancy to rise to 43% this year.

 

Utilities were mostly down.

First Gen (-3.8% MoM), Aboitiz Power (-1.7% MoM) and Meralco (-2.1% MoM) dropped despite all delivering profit beats in 4Q22.  FGEN’s beat was driven by much better performances from nat gas units and EDC. Higher contributions from the GNPD plants and overall higher plant availability meanwhile helped boost AP’s profits.  Meralco’s core profit meanwhile also came in ahead with robust contributions from generation.  Power sales for its core distribution business were up 6% in 4Q and FY22.

ACEN (-3.5% MoM) however reported disappointing results for FY22, dragged by ACEN’s net buyer position as well as the mismatch between fuel cost and selling price (particularly for SLTEC).

Manila Water (+3.7% MoM) closed higher, reporting profits well ahead of expectations. Headline results were lifted by one-offs and adjustments though billed volume growth accelerated (6% YoY in 4Q22) in line with re-opening.

 

Semirara (+7.8% MoM) was the best performer in the sector. FY22 headline profit more than doubled to P39.87bn. SCC’s strong cash position suggests the company can pay out a chunky dividend this year.  SCC already announced dividends of P3.5/sh, equivalent to 11% of the share price. 

This took SCC parent DMCI (+8.9% MoM) higher. 

Large cap SM (+4.3% MoM) likewise advanced. SM reported a strong rebound in FY22 profits, with record earnings from BDO and SM Retail, earnings recovery from SM Prime, as well as the retroactive inclusion of the geothermal production operation.

Meanwhile, Ayala Corp (+4.0% MoM), also closed up, despite reporting disappointing underlying operations, compounded by write-downs.  BPI and Ayala Land delivered as expected while ACEN saw earning squeezed by higher power costs and selling price commitments. Losses at AC Logistics and AC Industrial continued. 

 

 

The rest of the holdcos closed lower in March. Declines were led by Aboitiz Equity Ventures  (-10.4% MoM). Metro Pacific (-9.8% MoM) also fell sharply, after surging 18% in January.   MPI is reportedly still in talks with Mitsui on the possible entry of the latter as a strategic investor as well as a potential delisting. MPI’s FY22 adjusted profits exceeded expectations as its power and tollroad segments outperformed.

The company meanwhile announced that it has entered into an agreement to acquire 1.6bn shares (16%) of SP New Energy Corp. (SPNEC PM) from Solar Philippines Power Projects (Solar PH), with an option to increase the stake to 43% once SPNEC increases its authorized capital stock to P50bn from P10bn through an asset-for-share swap.

LT Group’s (-5.7% MoM) FY22 income came in slightly below estimates, dragged by the underperformance from tobacco and a depressed contribution from PNB.  However, PMFTC has raised prices for its products effective 27 Feb.  Prices were raised for Marlboro (+14% to P155/pack), Fortune (+17% to P125.7/pack) and Jackpot (+17% to P125.7/pack). The Price hikes are higher than the tax increase that took effect in January of P5/pack.

 

 

GT Capital (-0.6% MoM) was relatively flat.  GTCAP’s 4Q22 core profit improved 38% QoQ and 56% YoY, bolstered by higher NIMs and faster loan growth at Metrobank, profit recovery of the hotel segment,  and margin improvements at Toyota. Favorable currency movements and price hikes led to a 50% QoQ jump in profit for the auto segment.

Bloomberry (+7.2% MoM) surged as GGR in 4Q climbed another 10% QoQ to P14.7bn, surpassing the same period 2019 revenue of P14.6bn. Slots is now well above pre covid, making up for the trailing VIP and mass table segments whose volumes are still down by 30% and 20% vs 2019. 

ICTSI (+6.8% MoM) closed higher. FY22 profits came in well ahead of forecasts (FY22 recurring profit +57% YoY to US$601m), boosted by higher-than-expected yield per TEU and a lower tax rate.

Cebu Air (-1.2% MoM) meanwhile inched down.  The airline reported disappointing 4Q earnings on slower than-expected revenue recovery and higher than expected costs.  EBITDA in 4Q improved sharply however on the re-opening of North Asian markets as well as favorable fuel movements.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases to 8.6% in February. Headline inflation eased to 8.6% YoY in February from 8.7% in the previous month. This was below street expectations (8.9%) and within the BSP’s forecast range 8.5-9.3%. On a MoM basis, headline CPI was flat, as prices of food (i.e. vegetables) and petrol (i.e. operation of transport equipment) dropped, offsetting the uptick in utilities. The latest print takes 2M23 average inflation to 8.6% (vs. BSP’s 2023 target of +6.1%).

However, core inflation (excluding fuel and food) rose further to 7.8% in February from 7.4% in January. On a MoM basis, core CPI rose by 0.5%, slower than +1.0% in January. Assuming core CPI is flat MoM for March, the YoY rate will already moderate to 7.5%.

The BSP’s Monetary Board will be having its next policy meeting on March 23; Fed meeting also on March 23.

Monetary Board raises policy rates by 25bps. The Monetary Board decided to raise the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight repurchase facility by 25bps to 6.25%. Decision was based on new information and its assessment of the effects of past policy actions. They also cited the steady increase of core inflation in Feb, despite the modest decline in headline inflation. The BSP tempered its 2023 average inflation forecast slightly to 6.0% from 6.1% previously. It also lowered its inflation forecast for 2024 to 2.9% from 3.1% previously.

BSP chief says too early for rate hike pause. It may be too soon for the Philippine central bank to pause from raising interest rates at its next policy meeting in May according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla. May is “too early” to pause, “ unless we actually see a price fall. The BSP has raised rates by 425bps since last year, among the most aggressive moves in the region.

Government posts P46bn budget surplus in January. The government recorded a budget surplus of P46bn in January. This is a reversal of deficits in January 2022 (P23bn) and in December 2022 (P378bn) due to faster YoY, MoM increases in revenues amidst a slack in expenditures. Tax revenues rose 19.5% YoY and 20.3% MoM, led by sustained improvements in BIR collections. Expenditures barely grew to P302bn due to a 28% YoY drop in interest payments (base effects: FX interest payment due in Feb 2022 was paid in Jan 2022). Primary spending rose 8.3% YoY, outperforming the government’s full-year projection (-1.3%). Net of interest payments, the primary surplus rose two-fold YoY to P92.7bn from last year’s surplus of P42.2bn.

BOP deficit widens to US$895m in February; GIR falls to US$98.2bn. Preliminary data showed  the Balance of Payments widened to an US$895m deficit in February, up 4.7x YoY (vs Feb 2022: -US$157m). This is a reversal of the US$3bn BOP surplus in January 2023. The BSP cited government debt payments. This brings YT-Feb BOP to a US$2.2bn surplus, a reversal of the US$259mn deficit in the same period a year ago. The BSP also revised the end-February GIR to US$98.2bn (previously US$99.3bn). This is down from US$100.7bn in January 2023 and is equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports.

GIR level settles at US$99.3bn in end-February. Preliminary data showed the country’s gross international reserves settled at US$99.3bn in end-February. This is down from a revised US$100.7bn GIR level in January.  Payment of the government’s debt obligations and downward adjustments in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings were cited for the latest print. The GIR level represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.5 months’ worth of imports.

 

Government debt at P13.75tn in end-February. The National Government’s outstanding debt rose by P54.26bn (+0.4% MoM) to P13.75tn in end-Feb due to net issuance of domestic securities. Out of the total debt stock, 68.7% was sourced from domestic borrowings while 31.3% was sourced externally. The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually go down to 51% by 2028.

FDI posts US$634m net inflows in Dec 2022; FY22 FDI reaches US$9.2bn. Foreign direct investments (FDI) amounted to US$634mn in Dec 2022, lower by 76.2% YoY and 22.4% MoM. This brings FY22 FDI to US$9.2bn, down 23.2% YoY. Non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments and equity capital dropped by 49.5% YoY and 15.6% YoY, respectively. Reinvestment of earnings increased by 5.9% YoY.

PH posts biggest trade deficit in 5 months. Data released by the PSA showed the trade deficit widened by 27.2% YoY to US$5.74bn in January 2023. Exports plunged -10% MoM and -13% YoY to US$5.23bn in January, the lowest since March 2020, due to softening demand from China and electronics. Imports grew 6% MoM and 4% YoY. Fuel, which constitutes 18.8% of imports, saw a 71% increase YoY.  This is in line with the increase in fuel prices, and Fitch Solutions’ projected increase in oil product imports amid the continued growth in fuel demand in 2023. This is the first monthly rise in imports in three months. January 2023’s trade deficit is the biggest since August 2022’s deficit of US$6.02bn.

 

Remittances up 3.5% YoY to US$2.76bn in January. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.5% YoY to US$2.76bn in January 2023. YoY growth a tad slower than 2022’s average growth rate of +3.6% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances rose 11.1% YoY. On a MoM basis, remittances dropped 12.6% largely due to seasonality. Remittances grew across regions/countries, with USA jumping by 5.7% YoY. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 4% YoY to US$33.8bn. This suggests remittances will need to average US$2.83bn per month for the rest of the year to reach the BSP’s target.

Marcos admin bares P9tn flagship infrastructure projects. Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) announced that it has approved a new list of Infrastructure Flagship Projects (IFP), which includes 194 projects worth P9tn. Of these, 45 projects will be financed through public-private partnerships (PPPs). Majority will prioritize physical connectivity and water resources. As of today, NEDA has not uploaded the list in its website. 

Recall that close to the end of President Duterte’s term, its IFP still included 112 projects worth P4.69tn. Only12 projects were funded under “solicited” PPPs (P355bn) while 20 projects were “unsolicited” PPPs (1.50tn).

Commercial bank lending grows 10.4% YoY in January. U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) moderated to 10.4% in January. While this appears to have moderated sharply from 13.7% in Dec 2022, data on U/KB loans from Jan 2022-Dec 2022 were distorted by the merger of BPI/BPI Family Savings. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking system rose by 11.4% YoY in Dec 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow by 12-15% for banks under Regis coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth slowed to 5.5% in January from 6.7% in December.

 

 

Vehicle sales rise 27% YoY, 5% MoM in February. CAMPI/TMA reported unit sales of 30,905 units in February. This is up 27% YoY on low base effects. On a MoM basis, car sales rose by 5%. This brings YT-Feb sales up 34% YoY to 60,404 units. CAMPI is targeting vehicle sales to rise by +10-15% YoY this year. Toyota sold 14,871 units, up 21% YoY and 11% MoM. Their market share improved to 48.1% in Feb from 45.5% in January 2023. This brings YT-Feb sales up 28% YoY to 28,299 units (YTD market share at 47%, down from 50% in end-2022 but higher than pre-Covid: 40%). 

Amended Public Service Act to take effect in April. NEDA released the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for R.A. 11647, which amends the Public Service Act. The IRR will take effect on April 4. The law effectively allows full foreign ownership in telecommunications, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports. Under Rule 8, Section 32 of the IRR, telecommunication is the only public service considered as a “critical infrastructure,”. The rules included a reciprocity requirement for foreign investments in critical infrastructure. Foreign nationals cannot own more than 50% of a firm engaged in services classified as critical infrastructure. Sovereign wealth funds and independent pension funds of each state may also collectively own up to 30% of the capital of a public service classified as critical infrastructure.

PAGCOR eyes US$1.47bn from sale of 41 casinos. PAGCOR is considering selling all 41 casinos it operates to raise P80bn (US$1.47bn) in government revenue and in order to focus on its regulatory functions according to Pagcor Chairman Alejandro Tengco. The total GGR will likely hit the P256bn pesos pre-pandemic level by 2024 versus P214bn last year.

DOE chief to assess taxes on energy sector. DOE Secretary Raphael Lotilla said his agency is looking to assess the taxes imposed in the energy sector to determine how these contributed to the high power cost in the country. Lotilla said the government also needs to look into the possible impacts on power cost if it will impose a carbon tax. Additionally, Lotilla said DOE would like to study the proposal on carbon tax “more deliberately and more systematically” to determine the possible effects of its imposition to the energy sector.

President Marcos looking to reform military pension program. Finance Secretary Diokno said that President Marcos was willing to use his political capital to reform the military pension system. Secretary Diokno warned of a fiscal collapse if this does not happen. The government has set aside P120bn-P130bn for the pension program this year. Pensions of retired military personnel are indexed to salaries of active members of the military. Secretary Diokno said that Defense Secretary Galvez and Interior Secretary Abalos agreed with all the options. The retirement age will rise from 56 to 57, active members of the military will contribute to their pensions and pensions of retired personnel will no longer be indexed to salaries of active members of the military.
