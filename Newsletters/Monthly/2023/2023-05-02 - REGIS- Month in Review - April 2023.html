REGIS REPORT

02 May 2023
 

The Philippines climbed 1.9% MoM in April, breaking a two-month decline.  The index closed at 6625.08 index points, helped higher by foreign inflows.

The 4Q22 earnings season concluded in April, even as 1Q results started to trickle in.  FY22 earnings growth for stocks in the PSEi reached +28% YoY.  In 4Q22, PSEi earnings growth slowed to +13% YoY from +34% YoY in 9M22, on expected normalization post re-opening. Against consensus expectations, some 37% managed to beat, though those that fell short was at a slightly higher  40%.  The rest came in line with forecasts. Banks and utils were among those that delivered beats, though telcos and consumers were a drag.  Banks posted the highest YoY growth for the year, with earnings up 46% thanks to higher NIMs and lower provisions. 

As initial 1Q results come in, it bears watching if a broad consumption slowdown results from the continued inflation pressures on consumers. Already, electricity sales for Meralco’s core distribution operation, a leading indicator of economic activity, were up a mere 2% YoY in 1Q23.

The Phils saw its first monthly CPI decline since September 2021, with March CPI cooling to 7.6% YoY, down a full ppt from 8.6% in February.  CPI declined 0.2% MoM as prices of vegetables, meat, and gasoline corrected. These more than offset upticks in rice, flour, and electricity prices.

After the sharp deceleration in the March CPI print, the BSP has revised down its inflation target for 2023E to an average of 6.0%, from 6.1% previously. For 2024E, the BSP forecasts average inflation at 2.9%, from their previous 3.1% estimate, well within the target band of 2-4%.

With the decline in inflation pressure, BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said the BSP is likely to pause its tightening cycle as early as next month, if the inflation downtrend continues. According to Medalla, the BSP is not likely to imitate monetary policy action of the Federal Reserve in particular. This would suggest that the gap between Fed funds rate and BSP policy rate of 150bps -- narrower than historical average of 350bps – would get even tighter. The next Monetary Board meeting is set for 18 May.  

The BSP’s pronouncements could result in some near-term weakness in the PHP, with the market expecting the Federal Reserve to raise rates next month.  Already, these expectations seem to be reflected in the PHP. The PHP fell 1.8% in April (to PHP55.384/US$), despite the USD Index dropping 0.8% MoM during the month. 

The trade balance is another source of pressure for the PHP.  While the trade deficit narrowed slightly in Feb (to US$3.88bn) -- as easing global commodity prices caused imports to contract -- the collapse in exports raises concerns.  For the first two months of the year, the trade deficit was still up 16% to $9.61bn, with imports down 4%, but exports plunging 16%. . Notably, both electronics and non-electronics exports were down 21% and 9% respectively. The Current Account deficit  remains a concern for the Phils in the longer term.

Moving ahead, the inflation respite might be short lived, as the Department of Agriculture is warning of a potential 10%-15% increase in rice prices further down the road. Rice accounts for a significant 8.9% of the CPI basket.

 

 
PSEi +1.9% | 6,625.08
 
Key data
 

Foreigners turned buyers for US$34m in April, bringing year-to-date net foreign outflows to US$484m. Turnover was generally thin at US$87m per day as  multiple trading holidays kept most traders sideline. Foreign participation stood at 50% (vs 48% last month).

The largest  inflows seen in banks BDO and BPI amid continued strong first quarter earnings driven by lower provisions and higher loan yields.  Meanwhile, port operator ICTSI was among the top foreign bought for a second straight month ahead of an expected strong 1Q report. Biggest outflows were seen across property names Ayala Land, SM Prime, and Robinsons Land as rising rates environment seen to weigh on the sector. Telco operators Globe and PLDT also saw heavy foreign outflows amid benign growth in the sector and as SIM card registration seen to weigh on revenues in the short term.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

Banks were the main drivers of the index’s gain, as foreigners continued to put money in large, liquid names in the sector. Banks continued to be in favor, as the interest rate environment remains supportive of NIMs. BDO (+12.1% MoM) surged, contributing almost half of the PSEi’s 125- index point gain. BPI was the next top mover, helping push up the index by 30 index points. Between the two banks, 1Q23 results trends were pretty similar, from widening NIMs, moderating loan growth, and lower provisions.  While the two big banks came in line/ahead, Union Bank’s (-2.5% MoM) 1Q fell short of expectations, as similar to other big sized banks in the previous quarter,  UBP’s profitability was dragged by deposit cost pressure.  

Topping the list of index gainers was Metro Pacific (+20.4% MoM) which rose in relation to a tender offer for its entire public float. A consortium of First Pacific (142 HK), GT Capital (-7.4% MoM), and a firm jointly owned by Japan-based Mitsui and Japan Overseas Infrastructure Investment (Mit-Pacific), and MPI’s management, led by Mr. Manuel Pangilinan, disclosed that it is undertaking a tender offer for MPI’s entire public float, equivalent to 10.5bn shares and representing 36.5% of its outstanding stock. The consortium is offering a price of P4.63/sh, which is a 22% premium to its one-year VWAP. The price represents an 8% premium to the last closing price before announcement.  Should the tender offer be successful, it will pave the way for MPI’s shares to be de-listed from the exchange.

Other conglos populated the top gainers list, including Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+13.1% MoM), Alliance Global (+11.7% MoM), JG Summit (+5.8% MoM). Along with Ayala Corp (-1.7% MoM),  LT Group (+2.6% MoM), Filinvest Development Corp.(-0.9% MoM). Through a consortium, these six groups submitted a new P100bn (US$1.8bn) unsolicited proposal to rehabilitate and expand the Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA). 

LT Group (+2.6% MoM) rose as well despite a larger-than-expected 31% YoY (-13% QOQ) drop in cigarette volumes in 1Q for its unit PMFTC after it selectively raised prices in February.  

Other conglos did not fare as well though. DMCI (-14.7% MoM) fell, weighed down by unit Semirara (-16.5% MoM) which fell on expectations of coal prices correcting further. Thermal coal prices (XW1) have fallen from  US$404/MT in end Dec 2022 to US$190/MT as of end April  2023.

 

Meralco (+9.0% MoM) gained after reporting surprisingly strong profits in 1Q (core income +40% YoY to P9bn) on the back of record performance from the generation business. Electricity sales for Meralco’s core distribution operation, was only up 2% YoY.

Aboitiz Power (+2.2% MoM) also gained, as 1Q23 profits beating street expectations, with growth coming from contributions from GNPD and higher availability and higher water inflow.

Other utils ended lower, with First Gen (-4.7% MoM), Manila Water (-2.4% MoM) and ACEN (-1.8% MoM) dropping for the month.

Bloomberry (+7.1% MoM) made a move, as it is a potential substitute for MPI in the PSEi should a successful tender offer lead to its delisting.  Sentiment for Bloomberry was also bolstered by regulator PAGCOR’s report of a 50% YoY surge in 1Q23 revenues to P16.8bn, which suggests a good showing from Bloomberry, as PAGCOR derives a portion of its revenues from fees from licensed operators.

Aside from Bloomberry, Nickel Asia (-1.9% MoM) and Century Pacific (unch) are also PSEi inclusion candidates.

While CNPF, which continued to see cost pressures in 4Q22, the rest of the consumer sector was a mixed bag. Monde (-4.7% MoM) declined, dragged lower by disappointing FY22 earnings wherein core profit dropped 20%.  Investors also continued to digest Monde’s capital restructuring which came as a result of a massive impairment charge for its alternative meat business.

Peer Universal Robina (+2.1% MoM) meanwhile managed a gain. Emperador (unch) was flat in April. Its brandy segment jumped in 4Q22 on seasonally strong sales while sales of whisky continued to climb on strong single malt sales in Asia and Europe.

Shakey’s (+4.8% MoM) likewise gained, with its FY22 beating expectations and already above pre-pandemic levels. SSSG in was expectedly strong at >40% given holiday spending.

 

 

Among the retailers, Wilcon (-3.5% MoM) declined.  1Q23 net profit of P962m (+13% YoY) tracked mildly behind expectations mainly due to a weaker-than-expected SSSG (+3%). This was attributable partly to normalization of sales post pandemic.

Puregold (+4.2% MoM) meanwhile gained, snapping four straight months of declines. FY22 profits came in ahead of consensus, with 4Q supported by holiday spending. Robinsons Retail (+2.1% MoM) also closed higher for the month, with core retail operations in 1Q23 broadly in line with expectations. Net sales rose 13% YoY on the back of 9% SSSG, driven by the continued recovery of non-essential formats, namely department stores (+26%) and convenience stores (+22%).

The property sector managed to close mostly flat to up, despite interest in the sector remaining tepid as high interest rates prevail, office vacancies high and still rising and overall presales yet to reach pre-pandemic levels.  Large cap SM Prime (+3.5% MoM) recouped its 3.7% losses in the previous month. Ayala Land (+0.6% MoM) was flat, consolidating after its 7% decline in the prior month. Robinsons Land (-1.9% MoM) slid, despite 1Q23 profit rising 90% and coming in ahead of expectations multiple segments surprised positively.

 

Telcos were out of favor in April, with losses here led by PLDT (-15.2% MoM).  PLDT announced “voluntary separation(s) from service” of key posts which are likely related to the company’s ongoing investigations into the “over-spending” issue that has continued to be an overhang for the stock. CFO Ms Anabelle Chua has opted for early retirement, while two other Senior Vice Presidents (SVPs) have left, with its Chief Procurement Officer resigning, and Network Head also taking early retirement.

Globe (-8.8% MoM) and Converge (-7.8% MoM) also dropped. Sentiment for the sector continued to be weighed down by the apparent industry wide slowdown in broadband and mobile revenue. Meanwhile, the government extended the  period to register mobile SIMs to July 25, or for another 90 days. The extension should mitigate the short-term disruption to what should otherwise be a broadly revenue-neutral event. 

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Headline inflation cools to 7.6% in March. Headline CPI rose 7.6% YoY in March, moderating from 8.6% in Feb. On a MoM basis, headline CPI declined by -0.2%, the first time of negative growth since September 2021. This was due to a decline in prices of food (i.e. vegetables, meat, and oil) and petrol. Sugar prices also rose by just 0.1% MoM, slowest MoM rate in 24 months. These more than offset upticks in rice, flour, and electricity prices. Meanwhile, core inflation accelerated to 8.0% in March from 7.8% in Feb. On a MoM basis, core CPI rose by +0.4%, slightly decelerating from +0.5% in Feb

1Q23 average inflation rate at 8.3%. To reach the BSP’s 6.0% target by year-end, CPI should increase by +0.2% MoM on average for the rest of the year. 

BSP pause likely in April inflation eases. BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that a pause in interest rate increases was possible if the April CPI (CPI) is not higher than the March CPI. “A zero or negative month-on-month inflation” may also support the case for a rate hike pause. Assuming flat MoM, headline inflation rate could slow down to 6.8% in April from 7.6% in March. Headline inflation could only remain at or above 7.5% in April, if MoM rate increase is >+0.8%. Since May 2022, the Monetary Board raised key interest rates by 425bps. Monetary Board’s next meeting is scheduled on May 18; Fed meeting on May 3.

Cash remittances grow 2.4% to US$2.57bn in February. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew by 2.4% to US$2.57bn in February. This is a slower pace than in January (+3.5%). The 3-month average growth rate stood at +4% YoY in February from +5% YoY in the previous month.

In PHP terms, remittances grew by 9.4%. Land-based remittances rose by 2.6% YoY, lifted by higher remittances from Asia and the Middle East. Sea-based remittances grew at a steady rate of +1.6%. 2M23 remittances reached US$5.33bn, up 3% YoY. This is broadly in line with the BSP’s FY23 target of +3% YoY

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.88bn in February. Preliminary data showed the trade deficit narrowed to US$3.88bn in February. This is down by 3% YoY and 32% MoM. Imports declined by 12% YoY and 19% MoM, led by lower fuel, capital goods, and raw mat’l goods imports. The drop in imports can likely be attributed to lower commodity prices. Exports fell by 18% YoY and 3% MoM. Exports fell across trading partners, likely reflecting soft global demand. This bought 2M23 trade deficit to US$9.6bn, up 13% YoY.

 

 

BOP surplus hits US$1.27bn in March. The overall balance of payments position posted a surplus of US$1.3bn in March, a reversal of the US$895mn BOP deficit in Feb 2023 but is 68% YoY higher. The position was attributed to inflows arising mainly from the government’s net foreign currency loans and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. The government received US$1.6bn loans from the World Bank, ADB, and IBRC.

In 1Q23, BOP is at a surplus of US$3.5bn, up from US$495m surplus in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final end-March GIR level stood at US$101.5bn (up from a prelim figure of US$100.2bn). This is higher than the US$98.2bn figure in February and is equivalent to 7.6 months’ worth of imports.

BSP adjusts inflation forecast to below 6%. BSP is further revising downward its inflation forecast to below 6% this year after it cooled to a six-month low of 7.6% in March. BSP would likely step on the brakes of its tightening cycle as early as next month if the downtrend in inflation continues in the coming months, according to BSP Governor Medalla.

 

Government posts P106bn budget deficit in February. The government posted a budget deficit of P106bn in February, up slightly (+1.5%) YoY. This was a reversal of the P45.7bn surplus in January 2023. Revenue growth decelerated to -0.3% YoY in February, after rising 25.2% YoY in January. To reach the government’s target, revenues should average P315bn per month for the rest of the year. Expenditures were flattish (0.3%) due to the decline of the National Tax Allocation (NTA) shares of local government units resulting from the lower tax collections in 2020. Primary expenditures fell 1.7% YoY but interest payments jumped 21% YoY due to base effects. The 2M23 budget deficit narrower by 53% YoY at P60.6bn.

FDI posts US$448mn net inflows in January 2023. Foreign direct investment (FDI) posted US$448mn net inflows in January 2023, a 45.7% YoY decline. The gov’t cited global economic uncertainties and high inflation, which continued to weigh on investor decisions. Non-residents’ net investments in debt instruments and equity capital dropped by 57% YoY and 13% YoY, respectively. Meanwhile, reinvestment of earnings increased by 4% YoY.

 

 

Gov’t maintains GDP growth targets; Sees 1Q23 GDP growth “closer” to 7.1%. The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) maintained its medium-term economic growth targets. 2023: +6-7%, 2024-2028: 6.5%-8%. NEDA Secretary Arsenio Balisacan projects 1Q23 GDP to be “closer” to the 7.1% print in 4Q22. Note that GDP is scheduled to be released on May 11 (consensus: +7.2%). 1Q22 GDP grew by 8.2% YoY. DBCC raised its inflation target to 5-7%, higher than its previous assumption of 2.5%-4.5%. DBCC kept its budget deficit-to-GDP ratio target of 6.1% this year, but raised 2024-2028 revenue assumptions slightly to take into account new taxes. The 2024 revenue is projected to rise to P6.62tn by 2028 (previously P6.58tn) from P3.73tn in 2023. PIFITA (Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act) will generate P8.5bn by 2024 / VAT on digital service providers is expected to yield P13.7bn in revenues by 2024.  The two bills are still at the Senate.

Economic managers are still pushing for the passage of three tax reform measures to boost revenues, such as the excise tax on sweetened beverages, motor vehicle road user’s tax, and mining fiscal regime. Excise tax on sweetened beverages is expected to generate P53.7bn. Modern vehicle road user’s tax will reach P15.8bn in the first year and up to P48.6bn by the third year. The proposed fiscal regime for the mining sector is expected to yield P12.4bn in 2025, P12.9bn in 2026, P13.4bn in 2027, and P13.9bn in 2028. The Lower House approved the mining fiscal regime, which would bring the country’s effective tax rate on mining (considering all taxes) to 51%, up from 38% under the current system.

NPL/PD ratios inch up in Feb; Loan growth accelerates, back over 11%. Asset quality data was mixed in Feb 2023, with NPL and PD ratios inching up, but with RL ratios down a bit. Banking system NPL ratio (ex-interbank) was up 4bps MoM to 3.42%, while Past Due (PD) ratio was up 3bps MoM to 4.04%. In nominal terms, NPLs and PDs were up 1.5% and 1.4% MoM respectively. Recall that January NPL and PD ratios jumped 13bps and 22bps MoM respectively in January, though Regis noted that this was in keeping with seasonal trends.

The February milder MoM uptick was also in line with pre-Covid trends. NPL cover dipped to 105.0% compared to 106.3% in January. Meanwhile, Restructured Loans (RL) ratio inched down 3bps MoM to 2.58%. Loan growth in February accelerated a tad to 11.1% YoY, compared to the 10.8% pace recorded in January. LDRs inched up to 71.7% from 71.5% the previous month.

Car sales jump 19% MoM, 24% YoY in March. CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales jumped 19% MoM and 24% YoY to 36,880 units in March. This brings 1Q23 sales to 97,284 units, up 30% YoY. Toyota sold 16,906 units in March, up 14% MoM and 12% YoY. The March figure is nearing Dec 2017 peak of 17,312 units (prior to excise tax hike). The 1Q23 sales at 45,205 units is up 21% YoY. To reach the company’s FY23 target of 188k units (+8% YoY), monthly sales should only average 15,900 units for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV.

 

 

NAIA pax volumes revert to 93% of pre-Covid in 1Q23. The Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) said passenger throughput at NAIA was 10.86mn in 1Q23. This is up 158% YoY and 13% QoQ. The 1Q23 is about 93% of 1Q19 levels. Domestic pax volumes reached 6.16mn in 1Q23, up 94% YoY and +7% QoQ. Domestic pax volumes are now 12% above 1Q19 due to slow recovery of international routes. International pax volumes reverted to 77% of 1Q19. Pre-Covid, NAIA accounted for 80% of the country’s pax volumes. NAIA accounted for 78% of domestic pax volumes and 82% of international pax volumes.

1Q23 tourist arrivals at 67% of pre-Covid levels. Tourist arrivals in March slipped by 2% MoM to 67% of pre-Covid (from 68% in Feb), due to the 18%/10% MoM declines in Korean/American travelers. The slack could be partly explained by seasonality, as arrivals from these countries typically peak in December and diminish in succeeding months.

The recovery of Chinese tourist arrivals remains slow, with March arrivals back to just 13% of pre-Covid, up from 10% in Feb. The good news is overseas Filipinos are still more than 6x of pre-Covid levels, up 10% MoM, Although, this is down 20% YoY. This brings 1Q23 tourist arrivals to 67% of pre-Covid levels, or up 34% QoQ and more than 8x YoY.

Rice prices to rise by up to P5 on reduced imports. The retail price of rice is expected to rise by up to P5 this year due to lower import volume according to Department of Agriculture Deputy Spokesperson, Rex Estoperez. Prices could be kept in check if imports of rice are fast tracked. Domestically-produced regular milled rice is currently priced at P34-40 per kilo in Manila while imported regular-milled rice is selling at P37-44 per kilo. As of March 30, the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI) estimated that the volume of imported rice landed in the country fell 16.20% YoY to 360,196.175 metric tons (MT). Rice inventory declined by 5.1% YoY to 1.77mn MT in end-Dec 2022, after importing 3.8mn MT in full-year 2022.

U/KB loan growth moderates slightly to 10% in February. Preliminary data show that outstanding loans from universal and commercial banks (U/KBs) grew by 10% YoY in February, slightly slower than 10.4% in January. Loans to manufacturing, utilities, and retail trade grew at slower rates, offsetting faster growth in real estate and consumer loans. Meanwhile, M3 growth accelerated to 6.0% in February from 5.6% in January.

VAT collections up 20% YoY in 2022. The Bureau of Internal Revenue announced that it collected P463bn in VAT in 2022, up 20% YoY. This is also 14% higher than 2019 VAT collections of P406bn. Moving forward, the shift to quarterly filing of VAT returns will afford taxpayers relief from the former system of monthly filing. VAT filing deadline for 1Q is April 25. The BIR is targeting to collect P2.6tn in revenue this year, up 14% YoY. As of 2M23, BIR collections are up 9.6% YoY to P364bn.

 

Consortium submits unsolicited proposal for NAIA upgrade. The Manila International Airport Consortium (MIAC) submitted an unsolicited proposal to the government for the upgrading of the country’s largest airport, NAIA. The consortium includes six Filipino conglomerates + US-based Global Infrastructure Partners:

Aboitiz InfraCapital, AC Infrastructure, Asia’s Emerging Dragon Corp., Alliance Global – Infracorp Dev’t., Filinvest Development Corp., JG Summit.

Proposal is valued at over P100bn, which includes a significant upfront fee to government and committed investments in new facilities and technology. The proposal will increase NAIA’s pax capacity to 62.5m by 2028 from only 31m.

Electricity spot prices rise in April as demand peaks. The Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) said the average electricity spot market price in early April rose by P1.11 per kilowatt-hour (kWh) to P7.68 as Luzon’s peak demand came in at 12,221 megawatts (MW) in April, the high for the year so far, and exceeding the 2022 peak of 12,103 MW recorded in May. Peak demand in the Visayas was 2,380 MW in April, exceeding the 2022 high of 2,297 MW recorded in September. Mindanao’s peak demand in April was 2,363 MW, exceeding the region’s 2022 peak of 2,167 MW recorded in June. As of April 23, IEMOP said average supply was 14,612 MW against average demand of 11,033 MW. In March, average supply was 14,226 MW and average demand 10,244 MW. While demand for electricity increased due to rising temperatures brought about by the summer season, the market remained stable, with ample supply levels across all grids. Given the current trends, especially with El Niño, the probability of prices remaining at the P7 per kWh level in May is high – IEMOP.
