REGIS REPORT

18 October 2023
 

The Philippines plunged 5. 5% in October, breaching the 6,000 Index level late in the month and finally closing at 5973.78. The market sell-off was triggered by a disappointing September inflation print, exacerbated by the Bangko Sentral’s announcement of an off-cycle policy rate hike. These added to the weight on investor sentiment already fraught with  concerns on the Israel Hamas war.

A few days after floating the possibility of an “off-cycle” hike, the BSP indeed announced a 25-bp hike in policy rates to 6.50% effective October 27. The move, coming weeks before the next monetary board meeting on November 16, came as surprise as the recent re-acceleration of CPI inflation was driven almost entirely by supply factors. The BSP cited the need for “urgent monetary action” to fend off second-round effects of supply side pressures .

The BSP now expects inflation to stay above its target band of 2-4% until July of next year.  Also,  BSP Governor Remolona did not rule out another hike at the November 16 Monetary Board meeting.   All these point to rates staying elevated for an extended period, despite its expectations on economic growth.  During its off cycle announcement, the BSP also gave out a 3Q GDP growth estimate of 4.5%, just barely above the soft 2Q GDP number which in itself has weighed on investor sentiment on the country.

The price of rice was the main driver for the uptick in September inflation.  The CPI inflation for September hit 6.1%, much higher than consensus expectations and an acceleration from the previous month’s figure, both at 5.3%. The culprit was rice – at 8.9% weigh in the CPI – with prices rising 17.9% YoY in September, up sharply by +9.2pt from the 8.7% in August.

Early in the month, President Marcos removed price controls on rice, after imposing a cap on September 5 in an effort to quell rising rice prices which many in government deem to be a result of price manipulation.  However, the administration has kept rice tariffs untouched, despite calls to reduce them by the Department of Finance and the National Economic Development Authority (NEDA). National Statistician Dennis Mapa also highlighted that compliance with the price cap was low, with only 20% of retailers surveyed complied with the price ceiling.   The lifting of the price cap is thus likely to have only a marginal effect on consumer prices. 

Meanwhile, the government has reportedly signed a deal with India to import 295,000 MT of rice.  For perspective, the full-year rice shortfall in the Philippines is expected to be close to 4m MT (roughly 25% of consumption). With the Philippines a net importer of this much rice,  local prices would continue to be linked to international prices, which remain elevated.  Vietnam FOB export prices  are still over US$600/MT.

The implementation of the law creating the Maharlika Investment Fund was suspended by President Marcos, pending “further study” of its implementing rules and regulations (IRR).Among the issues cited was the contribution of P25bn by the Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP), which could impair the latter’s capital adequacy ratios. This setback appears to be a temporary one, with the Maharlika Fund Law backed by Marcos himself.

President Marcos’ approval rating dropped by 15pt to +65pt based on the latest survey of Pulse Asia (Sept vs June). The fall in support from the lower income demographic (C-D-E income classes) was largely the driver of the drop. This is very likely a consequence of accelerating inflation of basic commodities, including rice. While Marcos’ approval ratings remain high, the sharpness of the decline is likely to raise some concern within the administration.

Key data
 
 

Volumes dried up in October, with average daily turnover dropping to US$80.1m vs US$130m in September. Foreigners remained net sellers in the market though much lower at US$171m, -63% MoM. The market barely saw days with inflows, numbering only four out of the 21 trading days this month. Foreigners were more active in October, cornering 52% of activity vs 43% the prior month. 

  • Foreigners remain net sellers in the market; only a few of the listed names saw decent inflow. Property names saw modest demand, with SMPH recording +US$9.5m in inflows. It was the top foreign bought stock for the month.
  • ACEN also saw decent foreign buying at US$3.8m (net) and the stock rallied 7.9% this month.   
  • Outflows were seen across the board. Consumer names URC (-US$13.3m)  and Jollibee (-US$10.3m) saw the biggest foreign outflows. Both names were laggards, dropping -8.1% and 10.6% respectively.
  • UBP was also top sold (-US$12.4m) as the stock got booted out of the PSEi in an off-cycle rebalancing in October due to minimum float requirement. NIKL (-US$9.8m) replaced UBP in the PSEi  and foreigners took advantage of its inclusion and sold the stock on rebalancing day.  

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The market fell across the board in October, with hardly any sectors left unscathed.


Despite what appears be to an extended period of higher interest rates and hence elevated NIMs looming, banks succumbed in October on widespread risk aversion. Widely-held, large, liquid banks BDO (-10.1% MoM) and BPI (-10.3% MoM) fell the most and dragged the index lower. Metrobank (-3.1% MoM) likewise dropped.  All the Big Three Banks have reported profits of that were in line/slightly ahead of expectations but this failed to keep share prices afloat.  Results in 3Q so far share the same features: decelerating loan growth, improving asset yields and NIMs and benign NPLs. 

Mid-sized banks Security Bank (-3.7% MoM) and Union Bank (-7.8% MoM) also ended the month lower.  UBP was removed from the PSEi in an off-cycle rebalancing earlier in the month. It was replaced by Nickel (-10.9% MoM).


Property stocks were likewise lower against the backdrop of elevated interest rates.  Declines were led by Robinsons Land (-8.2% MoM) and Ayala Land (-5.4% MoM). SM Prime (1.0% MoM) continued to consolidate as the market continued to await resolution on its Pasay coastal redevelopment after the national government earlier suspended all Manila Bay reclamation projects.


Macro proxy conglos were similarly down across the board.  Large-caps SM Investments (-5.0% MoM) and Ayala Corp (-3.0% MoM) retreated. Other index names JG Summit (-3.5% MoM) and Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-8.7% MoM) also dropped.    

GT Capital (-3.7% MoM) moved lower. Toyota sales for the month were roughly in line with industry, with unit sales of 17k, also up 8% YoY and 5% MoM.

Steeper declines were seen in Alliance Global (-14.0% MoM) and DMCI (-12.0% MoM), whose power and coal unit Semirara declined (-16.9% MoM) steeply. Semirara declared a special dividend of P3.50/sh, equivalent to a yield of 10% based on Oct 10 close. The stock went ex-div on October 20.

LT Group (-1.7% MoM) managed a relatively flat performance despite poor 3Q volumes reported by Philip Morris (PM US) for its Philippine subsidiary PMFTC. Cigarette volumes collapsed 27% to 5.6bn sticks on the back of the re-emerging problem of illicit tobacco. 

The consumer sector was weak, as widely-owned Universal Robina (-8.1% MoM) and Jollibee (-10.6% MoM) were sold off along with the market. Disappointing and/or soft  3Q earnings from Puregold (-6.2% MoM), Robinsons Retail (-12.2% MoM), and Wilcon (-10.0% MoM) served as impetus for marked corrections.  RRHI’s 9M profits tumbled 41% due to losses from non-core investments.  EBIT for the same period was up a modest 4%, with revenue up 9%.

Puregold also fell behind Regis forecasts, with 3Q underperformance driven by soft results for its membership shopping operation S&R. SSS for the brand contracted 5% YoY. Though this could be , attributable partly to a high YoY base, it is likely competition may have also been a factor.  Weaker-than- expected SSS was also the culprit for Wilcon’s 3Q (-18% YoY).  In 3Q23 alone, SSS contracted by 5.2%. Operating profit (-5% YoY in 9M / -14% YoY in 3Q) was dragged lower by higher opex.

Monde (-9.2% MoM) gave up some of its 25.4% advance in September. 

Shakey’s Pizza (-0.5% MoM) was flat. Singapore sovereign wealth fund GIC sold its 17% stake in Shakey’s to the Century Pacific Group and JE Holdings, holding companies controlled by the Po and Gokongwei families. The transaction price was P9.50/share, putting the value of the transaction at P2.7bn. 


In the telcos space, the big move was seen in Converge (-12.8% MoM), correcting in the wake of its 23.6% MoM gain in the previous month. Converge said it has reached 2m residential broadband subscribers as of Sept. The announcement implies the firm added at least 30k subs in 3Q. CNVRG added over 30k subs in each of the three prior quarters.

Globe (-1.8% MoM) ended slightly lower. Globe is transferring its 77% ownership of ECPay, an operator of a payments platform, to Mynt for P2.31bn. Full integration of ECPay into Mynt should enable ECPay to benefit from GCash’s digital capabilities. GLO acquired its stake in ECPay in 2019 for P1.54bn.

PLDT (+2.9% MoM) closed higher. 


Power generators Aboitiz Power (+7.3% MoM) and ACEN (+7.9% MoM) outperformed given the outlook on rates and power generator’s earnings.  Aboitiz Power disclosed that a family member, Sandro Aboitiz, will take over the CFO position by next month. He will replace Liza Montelibano, who will move to a different position within the group

ACEN also came up on laggard interest, with the stock being down 35% in 9M23.  ACEN subsidiary Yindjibarndi Energy Corporation (YEC) signed a memorandum of understanding with Rio Tinto for opportunities to collaborate on renewable energy projects on Yindjibarndi country in the Pilbara region of Western Australia.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

nflation quickens further to 6.1% in September. Headline inflation accelerated to 6.1% YoY in September from 5.3% YoY in August. Higher than consensus forecast (5.3%) and at the high-end of the BSP’s forecast range for the month (5.3-6.1%). On a MoM basis, headline CPI grew by another 1.1% in September. The biggest driver was rice, whose price rose by 17.9% YoY and 8.4% MoM (vs. August: 8.7% YoY and +4.1% MoM). Prices of vegetables remained elevated, rising by 2.5% MoM and 29.6% YoY (vs. Aug: +11.2% MoM, 31.9% YoY). There were offsets by favorable YoY base effects for electricity and petrol prices (base effect for petrol prices should start fading by Nov). Core inflation slowed to 5.9% YoY in September from 6.1% YoY in August.

This brings 9M23 average inflation rate to 6.6%, trending above the BSP’s average inflation rate forecast of 5.8% (BBG consensus median: 5.6% / high: 6.4%). To reach the BSP’s target, headline CPI should decline by -0.5ppts MoM for the rest of the year. If recent MoM trends persist (i.e. +1.1%), headline CPI could average 6.6% YoY this year. Vietnam rice spot price at US$642/MT (vs Sep average: US$631/MT).

BSP raises policy rates by 25bps in off-cycle hike. The Monetary Board surprised again, announcing a 25bps off-cycle rate hike. The overnight borrowing rate was adjusted to 6.5% from 6.25%, effective 27 Oct. The BSP cited need to prevent supply-side pressures from inducing second-round effects and further dislodging inflation expectations. The latest baseline projection is headline inflation will remain above 4% until July 2024 before coming down to c. 3% or within its 2-4% target band. It raised its 2024E average inflation forecast to 4.7% from 4.3%. There as no mention of changes to 2023E inflation of 5.8% (YT-Sep ave. inflation rate at 6.6%). The recent demand indicators point to dissipating pent-up demand in the near-term. The 3Q GDP estimate was said to be 4.5% (mild recovery from 2Q’s 4.3%). The monetary board is set to meet on Nov 16; BSP Governor Remolona Jr. did not rule out the possibility of further action during this meeting. The decision will depend on October inflation data (out on Nov 7) and 3Q GDP data (Nov 9).

Cash remittances rise 2.7% YoY in August. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 2.7% YoY to US$2.796bn in August, in PHP terms, remittances rose 3.5% YoY. This brought the 3-month average growth rate steady at +2.5% YoY. Similar to previous months, land-based remittances continued to drive remittances, rising by +3.2% YoY while sea-based remittances were up just 1% YoY. The YT-August remittances rose to US$21.58bn, up 2.8% YoY, broadly in line with the BSP’s full-year target of +3% YoY (or US$33.5bn).

Budget deficit widens in September/3Q23 as spending accelerates. The government recorded a budget deficit of P251bn in September 2023, up 40% YoY and 89% MoM. Revenues contracted by 12% YoY due to timing difference in VAT payments affecting BIR collections and lower commodity prices hitting the Bureau of Customs. Expenses rose 8% YoY, driven by higher interest payments and primary spending. In 3Q23 alone, the deficit was wider at P432bn, as expenses jumped 11% YoY while revenues grew at a slower rate of +5% YoY. Primary spending accelerated, up 9.4% YoY in 3Q23 (vs. -1% YoY in 1H23). This brings 9M23 deficit to P984bn, down 2.9% YoY. Based on the government’s FY23E deficit target of P1.5tn (6.1% of GDP), the deficit will shrink to P516bn in 4Q23E from P601bn in 4Q22. This suggests that overall expenditures will drop by 6% YoY and primary spending will contract by 9% YoY in 4Q23E.

End-September GIR drops to US$98.69bn. Preliminary data showed the GIR level dropped by 0.9% MoM (US$878m) to US$98.69bn. GIR figure is lowest in 7 months due to a US$607m MoM drop in foreign investments. The level is equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The BSP expects the GIR to settle at US$99.5bn by year-end.

Balance of payments posts US$414m deficit in September; End-Sep GIR level revised down to US$98.1bn.The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) posted a deficit of US$414m in September. This is lower than the US$2.3bn deficit in Sep 2022 but higher than the US$57m deficit recorded in Aug 2023. The government cited net outflows from payment of the government’s foreign-currency obligations. This brings 9M23 BOP to a surplus of US$1.7bn, a reversal of the US$7.8bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a US$100m BOP deficit by year-end. The BSP also revised the end-Sep GIR level to US$98.1bn from the previously reported figure of US$98.69bn. This suggests that the GIR dropped by US$1.5bn MoM (vs. end-Aug: US$99.568bn) and is equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports. The GIR could rise in October due to the government’s US$1.9bn retail Treasury bond sale.

 

Government debt stock rises 0.7% MoM to P14.35tn in August. The national government’s outstanding debt rose to P14.35tn in end-August. This is up by P105bn (0.7%) MoM primarily. The government cited depreciation of PHP against the USD (from P54.83 in July to P56.65 in August). Domestic debt dipped by 0.2% (P253bn) MoM to P9.79tn while external debt rose 2.9% (P127bn) MoM to P4.56tn. They cited upward revaluation of USD-denominated debt of P147bn, partially offset by downward revaluation of third-currency debt (P22bn). The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61% this year. As of end-June 2023, the debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 61%.

Infrastructure spending picks up in Aug. Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 12% YoY to P877bn in 8M23. This implies spending in August rose 49% YoY, 3% MoM to P136bn. Recall that 1H23 infra spend rose by a mere 3% YoY. The govt cited accelerated project implementation for roads and bridges and higher direct payments by foreign creditors for ODA-funded rail projects. The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn (+1% YoY) in FY23, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP. To reach this target, the government only needs to spend P104bn per month for the rest of the year.

NAIA airport bidding attracts seven potential bidders.Seven groups have so far bought bidding documents for the auction of the NAIA PPP:

  • Incheon International Airport
  • San Miguel Holdings Corp.
  • Manila International Airport Consortium (AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners)
  • Cengiz Insaat Sanayi ve Ticaret AS
  • GMR Airports International
  • Spark 888 Management
  • Asia Airport Consortium.

It is unclear at the stage if all seven bidders will submit bids on Dec 27. The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62m annually from 32m today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years).

Marcos removes price cap on rice. President Marcos Jr. lifted the cap on rice prices. Marcos admitted the government still needs to address the staple grain’s high prices in Metro Manila. Marcos said he had instructed House Speaker Ferdinand Martin G. Romualdez to create a program for the capital region, where rice prices are “most volatile. The government implemented price caps of P41/kg (US$730/MT) on regular milled rice and P45/kg (US$800/MT) on well-milled rice on Sep 5.

LTFRB approves nationwide P1 jeepney fare hike. The Land Transportation and Franchising Regulatory Board (LTFRB) approved a P1 provisional jeepney fare increase nationwide. The minimum fare will be adjusted to P13 from P12. The fare hike will be effective by October 8. A provisional increase means the additional fare is temporary until the main petition for an increase is resolved. There’s no increase in fares for succeeding kilometers (currently at P1.80). Road transport fares have a weight of 3.5% in the CPI basket.

Gov’t raises US$1.26bn from 2nd tranche of retail dollar bonds. The Marcos government raised US$1.26bn in the second tranche of its retail dollar bond offering. The offer was upsized from the initial offer of US$1bn (bonds will mature on April 11, 2029). This was much higher than the minimum issue size of US$200m but below the US$1.593bn raised at the maiden retail dollar bond auction in Oct 2019. Offering targeted individuals, mostly OFWs according to Dept. of Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. The five-and-a-half-year bonds fetched a coupon rate of 5.75% and were awarded at rates ranging from 5% to 5.75%, bringing the average to 5.509%.

The coupon was 437.5 basis points (bps) higher than the 1.375% rate of the five-year retail dollar bonds and 350 bps higher than the 2.25% set for the 10-year bonds offered in 2021. The demand for the bonds reached US$1.28bn but the Treasury did not make a full award as it did not accept tenders that went beyond the coupon rate. During the initial retail bond offer last Sep 27, the government raised US$611m; Yield for the 5.5-year bond was also at 5.509% (vs. 5.32% in secondary market benchmark rate). The government is targeting around US$1bn from issuing Sukuk bonds by end-November. The government initially programmed US$5bn overseas bond sales for the year.

 

August NPLs steady, but slight uptick in Past Due; Loan growth steady at 9.4%. NPL ratio (net of interbank loans) was steady at 3.51% in August, with the nominal value up 0.6% to P442.6bn. Slightly worrying, though, was the slight uptick in Past Due (PD) rates to 4.15% in August, up 3bps MoM from 4.12% in July, and 4.09% in June. In PHP terms, PDs were up 1.8% MoM to P538.2bn. On the other hand, Restructured Loan (RL) ratio inched down 2bps MoM to 2.36%, from 2.38% in July. This is the lowest RL ratio since May 2021. NPL cover improved to 103.0% from 102.3% the previous month.

Loan growth came in at 9.4% YoY August, slightly slower than the 9.6% (revised) in July. Interestingly, loan growth for “All Banks” is materially higher what was reported for the Universal and Commercial Banks (UKBs), which was +7.2% in August.

Tourist arrivals reach 4m as of Sep 29. International arrivals have breached the 4m mark as of Sep 29. This is 35% below 2019 levels. The government is targeting 4.8m tourist arrivals this year or about 58% of 2019 arrivals (8.26m). The government expects tourist arrivals to reach pre-pandemic levels in 2025. This suggests that tourist arrivals dropped MoM to 344k from Sep 1-29 (~50% of 2019 levels). September is seasonally a lean month.

Vehicle sales rise 9% YoY, 5% MoM in September. CAMPI reported that sales of new vehicles rose 9% YoY, 5% MoM to 38,628 units in September 2023. This brings 9M23 sales to 314,843 units, up 27% YoY. CAMPI targeting to reach 423k unit sales by year-end, up 20% YoY. Toyota sold 144,232 units in 9M23, up 15.5% YoY. This implies September unit sales at 17,437 units, up 8% YoY and 5% MoM. To reach Regis FY23E sales forecast of 192k units (+10% YoY), sales for the rest of the year should only average 15,923 units per month. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 28% of net profit and gross asset value (36% of NAV). GTCAP is trading at a 50% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,085/sh and 5.4x/4.9x FY23E/FY24E PER.

 

Pesonet, Instapay transactions hit P9.2tn in 9M23.Electronic fund transfers through PESONet and InstaPay jumped 30% YoY to P9.24tn from Jan-Sep 2023. By volume, transactions surged by 40.4% to 638.9m. Recall that in 2022, the value of transactions rose 36% YoY to P9.94tn. PESONet transactions rose 24% YoY to P5.69tn while InstaPay transactions jumped 42% YoY to P3.55tn. The number of banked Filipino adults almost doubled to 56% in 2021 from 29% in 2019.

Dept of Finance: No sponsor yet for proposed tax on junk food. According to the DOF, no one in either the Senate or Congress has sponsored the proposed junk food tax and that no bill has been filed. In June, Sec. Diokno announced the govt’s plan to impose a tax on pre-packaged foods “lacking nutritional value” (P10 per 100g or P10 per 100ml). It also called for the expansion of the current tax on sweetened beverages. Sec. Diokno said the economic team may review its revenue outlook as many of the tax measures may not be passed this year and he may thus propose expenditure cuts.

Free jeepney and bus rides in Metro Manila from Nov 1 to Dec 31. The government will relaunch its free ride program for public utility jeepneys and buses in Metro Manila from November 1 to December 31. The LTFRB will give priority to modern jeepneys and cooperatives previously enrolled in the service contracting scheme. The Department of Budget and Management has approved funding of P1.3bn for the free rides.

 

Philippines to lose at least P60bn this year from illicit tobacco. House of Representatives Ways and Means Chairman Joey Salceda said excise tax collections from cigarettes fell by 7.8% to P160.4bn in 2022 from P173.9bn in 2021. “The biggest decline in excise tax collection since the enactment of the sin tax reform law in 2012”. The decline came despite excise taxes on cigarettes rising to P55/pack last year. Excise tax rose another P5 to P60/pack at the start of 2023 and are scheduled to increase 5% annually beginning 2024. Mr. Salceda estimates the government revenue losses due to illicit trade in cigarettes at P60.6bn this year if the trend persists. Separately, BIR Commissioner Romeo Lumagui, Jr. said that the government stands to lose P30.57bn in revenue this year, rising to P42.54bn in 2027. LTG paid out virtually its entire share in PMFTC’s earnings last year as dividends (P15.1bn, P1.40/share, 14% yield) to LTG holders. Assuming the same payout, dividends this year may be closer to P12-13bn (P1.10-1.20/share) for an 11-12% div yield.

Marcos approval rating drops in latest Pulse Asia survey. The approval rating of President Ferdinand Marcos plunged by 15ppts to 65% in September. This came from an 80% score in June 2023. The biggest drop in approval rating (down 29ppts) came from the poorest, Class E. Meanwhile, the approval rating of VP Sara Duterte also dropped by 11ppts to 73% from 84% in June. The survey was conducted from September 10 to 14 among 1,200 respondents. It has a margin of error of ± 2.8%. The key issues that took place during the survey were the rice crisis, inflation, among others.

Implementation of short selling delayed to November 6. The PSE has moved the implementation date of short selling from October 23 to November 6. To give market participants more time to prepare. Only constituents of the PSE Index, the MidCap Index, and the Dividend Yield Index will be eligible for short selling. An eligible security must maintain short interest to outstanding shares at less than or equal to 10%. Naked shorting will not be allowed. Short selling of a stock can only be done on a price uptick.

Marcos suspends Maharlika fund implementation. President Marcos, Jr. signed a memorandum on Oct 12 directing relevant departments to suspend the implementation of the IRR of the Maharlika Fund Law. According to Exec. Secretary Bersamin, the suspension stands “pending the further study” of the IRR. The Maharlika Fund will source its funds from the initial P125bn capitalization of the Maharlika Investment Corp., which in turn will be sourced from state-run banks (P75bn) and the national government. State-run banks remitted the funds to the Treasury on Sep 14 but soon after sought relief from compliance with the BSP’s rules on capital requirements. The request for relief was preemptive, considering that the contribution—if not considered part of DBP’s capital—would undermine DBP’s ability to comply with BSP regulations.
