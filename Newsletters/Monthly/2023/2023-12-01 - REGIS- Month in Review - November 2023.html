REGIS REPORT

01 December 2023
 

The Philippines climbed 4.2% in November, arising from the 5.5% loss it sustained  the previous month.  The sub-6000-index point close in October helped to spur buy interest, even as global investors continued to make bets on the next Fed moves and direction of interest rates.  With one month to go for 2023, the index is down 5.2% YTD.

CPI inflation surprised the market with a drop in October. CPI came in at 4.9%, below consensus expectations of 5.6%.  Food inflation moderated to 7.3% from 10% in September.  Rice inflation settled at 13.2% from 17.9% in September while vegetable price inflation slowed down to 11.9%.

The better-than-expected inflation print allowed the BSP to keep its policy rates unchanged in its November 16 meeting. The move was in step with similar inaction from the Fed at its latest FOMC meeting.  The BSP had also surprised the market with a 25bp policy rate hike in an off-cycle announcement a few weeks prior to the MB meeting. One more MB meeting is left before year-end. Unless CPI disappoints in November, it seems unlikely the BSP will announce further hikes then.

Nearer term, rice prices continue to be of concern, given where Vietnam rice prices are. Vietnam is the source for over 80% of rice imports. The BSP in its previous announcement said that it expects inflation to stay above its target band of 2-4% until July next year.  However, its possible inflation falls below 4% in the next couple of months on the back of the elevated CPI base last year. 

GDP in 3Q came in at 5.9%, well above consensus forecast of 4.7%. This was also a sharp acceleration from the 4.3% in 2Q which had weighed heavily on market sentiment. The main reason for the surprise was government spending, which rebounded to +6.7% growth from a 7.1% contraction. Private consumption growth slowed slightly to 5.0% in 3Q from 5.5% in 2Q.  The 3Q GDP print brought 9M23 GDP growth to +5.9% YoY. To reach the low-end of the government’s target range of +6-7%, 4Q GDP must rise by 7.2% YoY.

The 3Q earnings season concluded in November.  Just as elevated food inflation caused private consumption to slow, topline growth reflected the impact of inflation. Tighter purse strings were apparent  in company earnings, as consumer traded down. On a 9M tally, and against consensus forecasts, a third (10) of PSEi companies managed to come in ahead, roughly the same as companies that missed (9).  The rest were in line.  Earnings for 9M23 are up 23%, with large banks and property names contributing most to these gains.

Remittances for September were up 2.6% YoY to US$2.91bn. The figure translated into PHP, however, is up just 1.4%. Like the GDP figures, remittances will be lapping an elevated base of comparison for the Oct-Dec period. Nonetheless, the PHP appreciated 2.25% in November to end at PHP55.487/US$. This coincides with the DXY dropping 3.0% in the same period.

Key data
 
 

Another dry month here in Phils with turnover dropping to $71m vs $80m in October. Market appetite remains lackluster despite improving macro numbers and is mostly skewed in Property, Banks and some Consumer names given its sensitivity to inflation plus the interest rates. Foreigners finally turned net buyers for $18m, with the help of inflows in ALI and ICT. But overall sentiment still mixed. Foreigners pretty active this month taking 53% of the split vs 52% the month prior.

  

  • Property names saw good foreign demand in November. ALI consistently saw foreign demand (+US$21.2m) throughout the month on the back of BSP’s move, while SMPH (+US$3.2m) saw interest late in the month after President Marcos exempted the company from the ban on Manila Bay reclamation. 
  • Foreigners were pretty mixed on consumer names despite the better-than-expected October CPI inflation number. Demand was mostly in JFC (+US$7.8m) after good 3Q results and bargain hunters stepping in after the stock hit its 52-week low end October. URC (-US$6.4m) meanwhile continued to see outflows. Foreigner have been net sellers of the name for the 8th straight month.
  • MBT underperformed vs its peers this month. Though foreigners were two way in BDO and BPI, MBT alongside parent GTCAP consistently saw foreign outflows. Foreigners were also sellers in SECB, driving the share price lower. 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Property names lifted the market as sector heavyweights gained substantially. SMPH (+7.7% MoM) soared after the government exempted its coastal development project from the ban on Manila Bay reclamation, which was imposed last August. These projects account for 23% of Regis’ Php40 NAV. Outside the news, earnings were likewise strong. SM Prime reported that 9M23 net profit rose 37% to P30.1bn while operating profit grew 29% to P44.5bn. Mall revenue remained resilient despite pressure on consumer wallets.

Ayala Land (+12.0% MoM) likewise advanced. Ayala Land’s 9M23 net profit grew 38% to P18.4bn. Moreover, new CEO Anna Ma. Margarita is targeting to double earnings by 2028 (relative to 2023). 

Most of the sector closed higher, despite a bill approved by the House Committee on Ways and Means with one of the provisions relaxing regulations on work-from-home (WFH) for BPO firms.  This proposed relaxation would allow BPO firms to implement WFH for most employees and continue to avail of tax incentives.  Roughly half of all office space in Metro Manila occupied by BPOs, a change in WFH arrangements could have a drastic impact on demand for office space.  Currently, BPO companies are allowed a regulatory maximum of 30% WFH.

AREIT (-9.0% MoM) has also announced a 199m share issuance to ACEN (-9.4% MoM) to acquire 276 hectares of industrial land, as well as another asset injection into ALI. AREIT will issue 642m new shares to Ayala Land in a property-for-share swap. The properties are valued at Php21.8bn. Based on Regis estimates, ALI will have to sell 280m AREIT shares to the public in order for AREIT to meet the public float requirements.  Both names dropped markedly in November. 

Outside of the sale-and-leaseback deal between ACEN and AREIT, ACEN reported earnings during the month.  A low wind regime and another outage at SLTEC dragged core operations of ACEN much lower QoQ, with management estimating that “core operating earnings” fell a massive 92% QoQ to just P176m in 3Q23. 

First Gen (-8.2% MoM) likewise fell, despite 9M23 earnings tracking ahead of estimates on solid contributions from its natural gas units. On the contrary, stronger-than-expected profits propped the share prices of Manila Water (+6.4% MoM) and Aboitiz Power (+4.3% MoM). 

Meralco (+1.4% MoM) rose slightly, with 3Q core income rising 90% YoY.  The primary source of outperformance was the generation business. Semirara (-0.2% MoM), meanwhile, ended slightly lower.  Earnings for 9M lagged estimates as heavy rains affected coal sales volumes in 3Q.

BDO (+4.7% MoM) and the BPI (+2.8% MoM) were the sole gainers withing the banks sector in November, with the largest most liquid names cornering buy interest. Metrobank (-4.5% MoM) dropped on foreign outflows. Meantime, smaller banks Union Bank (-5.9% MoM) and Security Bank (-8.4% MoM) retreated, with 3Q numbers looking below street expectations.

There was an apparent rotation into the consumer sector.  Consumer names outperformed led by Jollibee (10.5% MoM). Despite inflation, strong SSSG in the Phils drove core operations to come in well ahead of expectations.  

Monde Nissin (+2.4% MoM) rebounded as well, reporting a 41% jump in 3Q23 core net income. 3Q EBITDA surged 72% YoY to P3.6bn as the core instant noodle business rebounded. Apart from the 3Q results, principal shareholders extended a backstop for Quorn as it continues to operate in a challenged environment, with sales down 4% YoY. The family shareholders are effectively covering further losses at Quorn with a maximum guarantee of 2.156bn MONDE shares until Dec 2032. This should limit the impact of any future potential losses or impairment charges from Quorn on MONDE’s balance sheet. 

Trends for 3Q were somewhat mixed, however. For the rest of the sector, the effects of inflation on consumer wallets was more apparent on topline performance. URC (+3.4% MoM) total BCF revenues were down 4% YoY. Net profit for 9M23 rose 4% YoY to P9.7bn, trending behind Regis and consensus expectations. 

URC saw downtrading across categories with its confectionery business the most negatively impacted. Similarly, Philippine Seven's (-1.3% MoM) SSSG grew 12% but average baskets were flattish for 3Q23, suggesting downtrading. 

CNPF’s (+8.6% MoM) dairy business benefited from downtrading, however. CNPF's branded segment revenues (82% of 3Q revenues) grew 14% YoY mostly on volumes. CNPF gained another 2ppts of market share QoQ (28% as of end Sept) as its value-for-money proposition captured much of its downtrading within the category.

Telcos were mixed. Globe (-2.8% MoM) slipped, with 9M core income falling below expectations. However, the results showed positive signs including improved mobile revenue (+2% QoQ), positive broadband net adds, and  continued improvement in the profitability of Mynt.

Bloomberry (+5.9% MoM) gained in November, bouncing from its 9.8% decline in the prior month. BLOOM’s 3Q23 profits came in 46% lower QoQ. Management has nevertheless pointed to some recovery after the very weak July, and they expect to receive an uplift from the holiday traffic in the coming months.

Conglomerates went in different directions for the month. Ayala Corp  (+9.7% MoM) outperformed.  Its banking and property units, BPI and ALI reported results well ahead of expectations. SM (+2.2% MoM) saw a similar trend, with key subsidiaries BDO and SMPH contributing the most profits. Positive developments on SM Prime's coastal redevelopment also boosted sentiment for  SM. 

GT Capital (-1.2% MoM) reported a stellar quarter, as all business segments sustained strong revenues. Although Toyota’s 3Q23 profit dipped 8% QoQ due to the depreciation of the PHP and a jump in promotional and launch expenses, overall 9M23 profit is still trending well above Regis forecasts. 

On the flipside, DMC (+0.3% MoM) underperformed after SCC saw disappointing numbers. 

Cebu Air (-4.5% MoM) finally recovered from its pandemic woes, with the 9M23 profits finally reversing from last year’s Php10.9bn loss. The results are trending ahead of Regis FY23E and consensus estimates due to better-than-expected fares. Management guided though for tempered capacity guidance for 2024. 

ICT (+7.3% MoM) advanced. 9M23 core profit rose 8% YoY to US$473m, ahead of estimates. Volumes at the port of Manila have recovered, with September and October volumes hitting the highest levels for the year. It also secured a major client in Melbourne, which will start calling at VICT in 1Q24. Lastly, they expect to implement a chunky tariff hike at the port of Manila by July 2024. 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

3Q23 GDP rebounds to 5.9% as government spending picks up. 3Q23 GDP came in at 5.9%, accelerating from +4.3% in 2Q23 and beating consensus forecast of +4.7%. The key swing factor was higher government spending, which in 3Q23 rose 6.7% YoY, rebounding from the 7.1% drop in 2Q as government agencies caught up from the 1H shortfall. Private consumption growth modestly slowed to 5.0% in 3Q from 5.5% in 2Q, as consumers felt the brunt of elevated food inflation. Inventory withdrawals continued to drag private investments, with its contribution to GDP growth accelerating to -2.1ppt in 3Q from -1.0ppt in 2Q. Inventory withdrawals also more than offset the recovery in public construction (+26.9% YoY in 3Q vs +0.7% in 2Q) and private construction (+7.8% YoY in 3Q vs -3.2% YoY). Net exports also lifted GDP growth and contributed 1.4ppts to GDP, as imports fell 1.3% YoY in the quarter.

On the supply side, growth in key sectors accelerated

  • Agriculture up +0.9% from +0.2% in 2Q
  • Industry rose 5.5%, better than +2.1% in 2Q
  • Services grew +6.8% from +6.1% in 2Q

This brings 9M23 GDP growth up +5.9% YoY. To reach the low-end of the government’s target range of +6-7%, 4Q GDP must rise by 7.2% YoY. To downside risk is primary government spending could turn negative (-9% YoY) in 4Q, as this year’s budget (+5% YoY) is constrained by the implementation of the Mandanas ruling. According to the BSP’s business expectations survey, businesses are more upbeat for 4Q23 and the next 12 months

BSP maintains key policy rates. The BSP maintained policy rate at 6.5% at yesterday’s Monetary Board meeting. This was adjusted last Oct 27.

The BSP expects full year inflation at 6.1%, slightly better than previous forecast of 6.2% (YT-Oct avg 6.4%). The forecast for 2024 is 4.4% (from 4.7% previously), while that of 2025 is 3.4%. According to BSP Deputy Governor Francisco Dakila Jr., supply-side pressures continue to ease due to government non-monetary interventions as well as seasonal factors. The next Monetary Board meeting is set for 14 Dec.

Inflation decelerates to 4.9% in October. Headline CPI rose by 4.9% YoY in October, moderating from 6.1% in September. This came in below consensus median forecast (5.6%) and the BSP’s target range for the month (5.1%-5.9%). On a MoM basis, headline CPI declined by 0.2%, first negative growth rate since April due to lower food prices (rice, vegetables, meat, fish, sugar, oils) and petrol prices (-2.3% MoM vs +4.2%). Rice CPI dropped for the first time in eight months at -4% MoM due to onset of peak harvest and import arrivals (rice CPI rose +13% YoY in Oct from +18% in Sep). The slower food inflation was offset by upticks in (a) housing rental prices (+4.0% YoY in Oct vs +3.9% Sep), (b) gas prices (+6.8% vs +0.2%), and (c) clothing/footwear (+4.8% vs. 4.7%). Core inflation moderated further to 5.3% in October from 5.9% in September.

The MoM uptick for core inflation was steady at +0.3%, given higher nationwide jeepney fares (+8%) in Oct 8 and more regions implementing minimum wage hikes (+7-10%). This brings YT-October average inflation rate to 6.4%. The BSP is projecting headline inflation will remain above 4% until July 2024 before coming down to c. 3% or within its 2-4% target band.

Trade deficit narrows further in September. Preliminary data showed the trade deficit shrank by 27% YoY to US$3.51bn in September 2023. This is the lowest trade deficit figure since October 2022. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit also narrowed by 15% from US$4.13bn in August. Imports fell by 15% YoY in September, a faster YoY decline than in August 2023 (-13% YoY). By commodity group, electronic imports fell 26% YoY while non-electronic imports dropped 11% YoY. By major type of goods, raw material and fuel imports declined by 18% YoY and 27% YoY, respectively, due to base effects from last year’s surge in commodity prices. Consumer goods imports lower by 2% YoY while capital goods imports were down by 11% YoY.

Exports fell by 6% YoY in September after rising by 4% YoY in August due to lower exports to Japan (-12% YoY) and HK (-21% YoY), offset by a +16% YoY rebound in exports to China. Meanwhile, electronic exports fell 9% YoY in September. This brings 9M23 trade deficit to US$39.8bn, lower by 15% YoY. The government is forecasting exports and imports to fall by -4%/-3% YoY, respectively, suggesting a FY23E trade deficit of US$57.29bn (-1.6% YoY, ~13% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 peaked at US$58.24bn (14% of GDP).

 

BOP posts US$1.5bn surplus in October. The country’s balance of payments posted a surplus of US$1.5bn surplus in October. This is a reversal of the US$414m deficit in September and was due to the government’s issuance of US$1.24bn global bonds. This brought YT-October BOP to a surplus of US$3.246bn, a reversal of last year’s US$7.1bn BOP deficit for the same period. The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$100m by year-end.

Remittances rise 2.6% YoY to US$2.91bn in September. Remittances coursed through banks rose 2.6% YoY to US$2.91bn in September. In PHP terms, remittances rose 1.4% YoY. Land-based remittances continued to drive the growth in Sep/9M23, as sea-based remittances remained sluggish. Remittances from Saudi Arabia (+12%), UAE (+8%), and Singapore (+5%) were they key drivers. This brings 9M23 remittances up 2.8% YoY to US$24.49bn. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 3% this year to US$33.5bn.

House Bill seeks to allow BPO firms to enjoy tax incentives despite full implementation of WFH. House Ways and Means Chair Joey Salceda said that the House Bill “CREATE MORE” seeks to allow BPO companies to enjoy tax incentives despite full implementation of work-from-home policies. Currently, BPO firms registered with PEZA can only implement 30% WFH. Regis estimates that 80%-90% of BPO occupied office space is registered with PEZA. Most BPO firms appear to be implementing WFH for 1-2 days per week. Regis estimates that BPO companies comprise 70%-90% of the occupied office space of the developers Regis covers.

Government hikes 2023 budget program by P261bn to P5.53tn. The Department of Budget and Management hiked the national budget by P261bn to P5.53tn from P5.23tn. The entire P261bn hike went to unprogrammed appropriations. Funding for unprogrammed appropriations come from the excess of collections, new revenues, and approved loans.

The additional budget must be released before year end but implementation can be varied. Personnel services can be implemented until year-end but maintenance and capital outlay can be spend until end-2024. Assuming the entire additional P261bn will be spent on primary spending this year, then primary spending in 4Q23 could jump by 12% YoY instead of declining by 9% YoY.

EV subsidy pilot program set in 3Q24. The Department of Trade and Industry plans to begin the pilot phase of the subsidy program for buyers of electric vehicles by 3Q24. Subsidies are initially set at P500k per unit for public utility vehicles, P20k per unit for three-wheelers and 10k for e-bikes. The subsidy program will be funded by the United Nations Industrial Development Organization and the Global Environmental Facility. To date, there are more than 16k EV units in the country (data includes hybrids). Sales of EVs grew to 2,536 units in 1Q23, up 495% YoY. The EV Association of the Philippines (EVAP) targets to grow EV sales to a cumulative 6.61m units by 2030, majority of which will be two-wheeled vehicles such as e-motorcycles. 

Commercial banks loan growth slows to 6.5% in September; M3 growth picks up to 7.9%. Preliminary data showed loans of commercial banks rose by 6.5% in September, slowing from 7.2% in August.  Loans to productive activities slowed to 4.9% from 5.5%. Consumer loans mildly picked up, rising by 23.5% in September from 23.1% in August. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 7.9% in September, accelerating from 6.8% in August.

September NPL ratio improve, but PD rates tick up. The NPL ratio (ex-interbank) improved 3bps MoM to 3.48% from 3.51% in August. Also, Restructured Loans (RLs) dipped a smidge to 2.35% from 2.36% the previous month. Meanwhile, NPL cover improved a tad to 103.65% from 103.02% in August.

Past Due (PD) ratio blipped up 6bps MoM to 4.21% in September from 4.15% in August. This is the third consecutive month that PD ratios increased, now a cumulative 12bps up from the June level (4.09%), and fully 42bps up from the end-2022 low of 3.79%. This is slightly worrisome as PDs tend to be leading indicators for NPLs (90 days past due).

Vehicle sales rise 19% YoY in October. CAMPI reported that vehicle sales rose 19% YoY to 38,128 units in October. On a MoM basis, vehicle sales fell 1%. This brought YT-October industry sales to 352,971 units, up 26% YoY. Meanwhile, Toyota sold 17,997 units, up 16% YoY and 3% MoM and bought cumulative 10M23 sales up 16% YoY to 162,229 units. This is trending well above our FY23E of 192k units (+10% YoY).

 

International air travel market reverts to 73% of pre-Covid levels in 3Q. Data from the Civil Aeronautics Board showed that international passenger traffic reached 14.53m in 9M23. This implies that in 3Q23 alone, international pax volumes stood at 5.6m, or about 73% of 2019 average. This is sequentially better than the 5.1m pax volumes in 2Q23 (67% of pre-Covid). Data for 3Q is consistent with tourist arrivals data, which showed 1.34m tourists in 3Q23 vs 1.29m in 2Q23, with the month of July posting highest level (516k) since Covid. Domestic passenger volumes has grown to 21.85m in 9M23, near 22.14m in 9M19. In 3Q23 alone, domestic pax volumes reached 7.29m, better than the 6.98m pax volumes in 3Q19 but slightly lower than the 7.5m domestic pax vols in 2Q23. Change in school calendar shifted from April/May to June-Aug, which also lifted international market (i.e. OFWs shifted travel plans to coincide with graduation and school breaks). The systemwide pax volumes reverted to 86% of pre-Covid levels in 3Q.

EO to expedite flagship projects out by yearend. A proposed executive order (EO) aimed at expediting the processing of requirements for infrastructure flagship projects will be ready before yearend according to NEDA Undersecretary Joseph Capuno. The EO will aim to “enable the expeditious processing of licenses, clearances, permits, certifications and authorizations”. It is unclear at this stage how the EO will address right-of-way delays, which is the most significant bottleneck to execution of infra projects.

House committee OK’s amendments to CREATE law. The House Committee on Ways and Means approved the CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy) bill. The Bills seeks to amend Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act.

Proposed amendments include:

  • Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%
  • Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports
  • Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board
  • Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises
  • IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements

Committee Chairman and Albay Rep. Jose Ma. Clemente Salceda said that they aim to approve the bill by the end of the month.

ADB: Potential NAIA bidders seek extension. The Asian Development Bank (ADB) is requesting the Department of Transportation (DOTr) to extend the deadline for the submission of bids for the NAIA PPP by a month. ADB is the transaction adviser of the government. It cited that four prospective bidders have requested for an extension. ADB warned that if deadline is not moved, only two prospective bidders would be able to submit bids. ADB is proposing to move the bid submission date to Jan 29, 2024 from the original Dec 27, 2023 deadline.

There are currently six “active” prospective bidders which have been participating in pre-bid activities.

Last month, the DoTr disclosed that there were eight companies/groups who bought bid documents:

  • Incheon International Airport
  • San Miguel Holdings Corp.
  • Manila International Airport Consortium (AEV, AC, LTG, AGI, FDC, JGS – and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners)
  • Turkish firm Cengiz Insaat Sanayi ve Ticaret AS
  • Turkish firm Limak Group
  • GMR Airports International
  • Spark 888 Management
  • Asia Airport Consortium

The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62m annually from 32m today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years).

Government finalizes Maharlika fund IRR. The Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) have been finalized according to President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. The Law will become effective upon the government’s approval; They are committed to having the MIF operational by year-end. Last October 12, President Marcos suspended the implementation of the MIF pending the study of its IRR. The government wanted to explore methods to enhance and improve the MIF.

Rafael Consing Jr. named Maharlika PCEO. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. appointed Rafael Consing Jr. as the President and CEO (PCEO) of the Maharlika Investment Corporation (MIC). Consing, who is the executive director of the executive director of the Office of the Presidential Adviser for Investment & Economic Affairs, will now supervise the operations and internal administration of the MIC.

In the revised IRR, the president has the power to accept or reject nominees for director positions and PCEO shortlisted by the advisory body. The revised IRR also stripped the requirement of the PCEO of having additional educational requirements.
