REGIS REPORT

03 January 2023
 

The Philippines ended 2022 with 3.2% drop for the month in December. The market failed to sustain the 18% gain it had accumulated in October and November, as rising global interest rates dampened sentiment. Renewed risk off behavior was reflected in fund flow, which resumed their outward trend.

The PSEi dropped for third straight year in 2022, failing to overcome levels just prior to the pandemic.  As the Philippines started to turn its back on protracted lockdowns, and COVID restrictions at the start of the year, The market reached pandemic highs (7,500 level) early in Feb. This was to be the index-high for the year.  The market continued to fade as the year progressed, with losses set off by Russia’s invasion of Ukraine shaved off gain.  Soaring commodity prices sparked sell offs in subsequent months.  Persistent inflation and aggressive tightening by the Fed, and recession fears weighed on sentiment.  The Phils found its trough as 3Q concluded as low market valuations had begun to attract buyers. Foreigners started to buy the market, even as A\robust corporate earnings, driven by strong demand, reflected the economic recovery story.

While declines in December were widespread, spotlight was on PLDT. The stock accounted for a third?? of the market’s overall decline in December.  PLDT acknowledged that it had overspent  as much as P48bn (~US$900m)on its capex budget over the last four years. While PLDT asserts there is no fraud involved, the admission is a blow to the credibility of the company’s financial controls.  The cumulative (“maximum”) overspending  that occurred over the last four years at P48bn is equivalent to 13% of total capex over the period.

Headline inflation accelerated to 8.0% YoY in November, ahead of consensus estimate of 7.8% YoY.  Prices of food rose due to the a server tropical storm, and the full-month effect of the transport fare hike, as well as higher LPG prices, contributed to the higher inflation print. YT-November average inflation stood at 5.6%.

The Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised its policy rate by 50bp, matching the rate hike announced by US Federal Reserve. The BSP policy rate is now 5.5%, the highest in 14 years and ~125bp higher than the Fed’s. While the market expects the Fed to raise rates by another 50-75bp next year, BSP Governor Medalla pointed out he sees less “urgency” to match the Fed. This is likely influenced by the PHP which has strengthened against the USD.

Notwithstanding the recent strength of the PHP, it is likely to depreciate medium term, as flattening remittances remain insufficient to cushion the ballooning trade deficit. The cumulative trade deficit as of October now stands at US$50bn. While it is looking unlikely that it will reach US$60bn or~15% of GDP as commodity import prices have fallen, The BSP continues to expect the current account deficit to settle at US$20bn, or roughly 5% of GDP for 2022 and 2023.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The PSEi slipped 3.2%  in December, failing to build on the 7.2% and 10.2% gains it had recorded in October, and November, respectively. Rising global interest rates and recession fears dampened sentiment, even as foreigners resumed selling the market.

Index decliners reached 20m, overtaking the 10 that managed a gain in December.

By far recording the largest decline was PLDT (-24.8% MoM), which dropped dramatically post statements that it uncovered budget overruns of as much as P48bn in relation to capex over the last four years.  The P48bn figure is 13% of PLDT’s capex over the last four years.  While PLDT asserts there is no fraud and there are no missing assets or hidden liabilities, the admission is a blow to the company’s credibility.  PLDT is now undertaking a reconciliation of accounts and is in talks with vendors to negotiate down the P48bn worth of budget overruns.

Meanwhile, Converge (+2.7% MoM) rose while Globe (-5.2% MoM) ended December down. The derating of tech/tech related sectors in 2022, saw Converge’s share price come off 50.2% for the period, after rising 114.1% in 2021.  Globe was also among the big underperformers this year, falling 33.3% (vs +63.65% in 2021).

Consumer names were mostly down.  Monde (-12.3% MoM) fell markedly on lingering concerns for its alternative protein business and competitive pressure for APAC BFB.  The stock ended 2022 down 31.6%. 

Wilcon (-8.2% MoM) likewise fell, giving up the previous month’s gain of 9.5%.  Jollibee (-6.1% MoM) declined also, correcting from recent strength.  JFC (+6.3% YTD) is among the top performing consumer names for 2022, with its share price butressed by robust recovery in 2022. URC rose by the same amount, with reopening also fueling topline growth momentum.

For the rest of the sector, most names ended the year down, despite the recovery story clearly apparent in their numbers.  Century Pacific (+2.4% MoM), San Miguel Food and Beverage (+3.5% MoM) and DNL (-0.3% MoM) fell 11.8%, 45.8%, and 16.4%  respectively for 2022. Meanwhile, retailers Puregold (-1.7% MoM), Robinsons Retail (-8.0% MoM), and Phils Seven (-0.7% MoM) recorded declines of 11.2%, 8.0%, and 17.6%. 

Restaurants Max’s (-0.4% MoM) and Shakey’s Pizza (+2.0% MoM) also ended the year lower (by 24.7% and 24.3%).

 

Property was weak in December, with the threat of higher interest rates continuing to weigh on the sector. Robinsons Land (-6.8% MoM), Ayala Land (-2.2% MoM), SM Prime (-2.5% MoM) , and Megaworld (-10.7% MoM) all declined  in December. As the PSEi constituents with the lowest market capitalizations, Megaworld and Robinsons Land d RLC are candidates for deletion during the February 2023 rebalancing, putting further pressure on their share prices.

The higher interest rate theme was a recurring one throughout the year, with property companies almost down across the board, save for SM Prime (+4.7% YTD) which managed a gain on the back of the strong recovery of malls, which account for about 60% of EBIT

Declines for the year were led by Vista Land which retreated 53.3%. Other names fell significantly too, led by Megaworld, down 36.5%. Robinsons Land (-6.8% YTD), Filinvest Land (-18.2% YTD) and Ayala Land (-16.1% YTD) also ended in the red.

Banks saw some selling in December. Coming out unscathed however was Union Bank (+6.4% MoM), which rose on investors betting that it would it would be an inclusion in the next PSEi rebal in February.  Union Bank said it aims to raise P12bn from its stock rights offering, down from the initial P20bn. This is equivalent to 6.7% of market cap. An offering would help the bank rectify its low free float, which has been an obstacle to inclusion in local and foreign indices.

Meanwhile, BDO (-2.5% MoM)  received SEC approval to increase its authorized capital stock from 6.5bn shares (5.5bn common and 1bn prefs) to 9.5bn (8.5bn common and 1bn prefs). The bank will issue a 20% stock dividend to cover part of the increase. BDO’s CET-1 ratio of 12.82% (3Q22) is well above regulatory minimums though a capital raise could help the bank prepare for potentially robust (beyond mid-teens) loan growth.

Unsurprisingly, banks were one of the best performing sectors for the year, as higher interest rates have been a boon to NIMs, even as reopening/recovery showed up in loan growth acceleration and improving asset quality. Large caps BDO and BPI closed the year with gains of 5.1% and 10.7%, while MBT relatively outperformed the overall market with its -3.1% performance.

 

Holdcos were mixed in December. DMCI (+25.0% MoM) surged during the month, as the stock is seen as one of the primary candidates for inclusion in the next PSEi rebal. Large gains were seen in other smaller conlgos as well, with Alliance Global (+21.8% MoM), and Cosco (+16.8% MoM) leading.  Larger holdcos SM Investments (-4.2% MoM), and Ayala Corp (-0.1% MoM) were flat to down. 

For the year, macro proxies declined almost across the board. SM closed 4.6% lower while AC registered a 13.7% decline.  GT Capital (-19.4% YTD) and MPI (-12.3% YTD) were down as well. DMC emerged as the clear winner, with a 55.6% advance for year, pulled higher by unit Semirara’s 61.6% rise.

Most news for the utilities sector centered around the Meralco (+6.7% MoM) and San Miguel (-4.2% MoM) saga. Meralco has filed a second motion with the Court of Appeals (CA) to deny SMC’s bid to suspend the power supply agreement with Meralco. SMC is trying to suspend power subsidiaries’ contracts with Meralco (a total of 1,000 MW of electricity to Meralco at a fixed price) as these have become  hugely unprofitable given the spike in global energy prices.

For the year, the recurring theme within the space was the tightness of electricity supply in the Luzon grid. Near-term, this is clearly positive for power generators like Aboitiz Power 14.6% in 2022, with a portion of their portfolio with un-contracted capacity . Semirara was the clear winner, amassing a gain of 61.6% for the year as soaring coal prices boosted profitability.

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation quickens to 8.0% in November. Headline inflation rose by 8.0% YoY, 0.9% MoM in November. Consensus was expecting 7.8% YoY and the BSP had a range of 7.4%-8.2% YoY. Prices of food (i.e. rice, fish, fruits, vegetables, and sugar) picked up due to the severe tropical storm Paeng. The full-month effect of the transport fare hike, as well as higher LPG prices, contributed to the higher inflation print. Fuel prices (diesel and gasoline) also rose 0.8% MoM. In PHP terms, regional prices of gasoline/diesel dropped 2%/8% MoM but impact on pump prices may have lagged. Core inflation (ex- food and fuel) accelerated to 6.5% in November from 5.9% in October. The YT-November inflation average at 5.6%. Assuming MoM trends slow to 0.5% in December, inflation for December 2022 will likely be slightly higher at +8.1%. Jefferies expects the Fed to raise policy rates by another 50bps this month (Dec 14). Regis expects the same rate hike in the next BSP policy meeting (Dec 15).

BSP raises rates by 50 bps, signals more tightening. The BSP raised its benchmark interest rate by 50bps to 5.5% within consensus expectations. The move followed the 50bp rate hike of the Fed. Overnight borrowing rate of 5.5% at the highest since November 2008. The BSP cited the further uptick in headline and the sharp rise in core inflation in November amid pent-up demand. Recall headline and core inflation accelerated to 8% and 6.5%, respectively, in November. The BSP raised its 2023 average inflation target to 4.5% from 4.3%, up from 5.8% in 2022 (unch). This is due to faster-than-expected Nov 2022 inflation and the looming impact of approved water rate hikes beginning Jan 2023. The latest forecast point to inflation peaking in December. The BSP expects inflation to return to the 2-4% target band by 2H23. Additionally, it lowered its 2024 average inflation target to 2.8% from 3.1% previously. The BSP Governor Medalla expects the pace of rate increases to slow down next year, depending on the data. He sees less urgency to match the US Federal Reserve’s monetary tightening next year as bringing inflation back to the 2-4% target remains their top priority.

Budget deficit narrows to P124bn in November. The government’s budget deficit shrunk by 4% YoY to P124bn in November. Revenues grew 17% YoY, outpacing growth in expenditures of 10% YoY. Tax revenues jumped 16% YoY, 19% MoM, well ahead of target due to higher collections from the Bureau of Customs. Expenses rose 10% YoY, 17% MoM as primary spending accelerated, rising by 12% YoY, 21% MoM. Interest expenses dropped 16% YoY and 21% MoM. The YT-November budget deficit at P1.23tn, down by 7% YoY and tracking below the government’s target (P1.5tn deficit, 6.9% of GDP).

Cash remittances rise 3.5% YoY in October. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.5% YoY to US$2.9bn in October. In PHP terms, remittances are up 20% YoY and the 3-month average growth was rate at +3.9% YoY. This was driven by higher inflows from Europe, Middle East, HK, Singapore. MoM, Oct remittances were higher by 2.5%. This brought YT-October remittances to US$26.7bn, up 3.1% YoY and was driven by inflows from USA, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Qatar. To reach the BSP’s 22E of +4% YoY, remittances for the balance of the year should average US$2.97bn per month.

GDP growth to exceed 7.5% in 2022. Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno said that the Philippines will exceed the government’s full-year GDP growth target of 6.5%-7.5%. This implies that 4Q22 GDP will grow over 6.9%.

Government trims 2023 growth target. The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) downgraded its 2023 GDP growth target to 6-7% from 6.5%-8% previously. The gov’t cited headwinds such as slowdown in major advanced economies, amidst sustained strength in domestic demand. GDP is expected to moderate from this year’s target growth rates of 6.5%-7.5% (9M22A: +7.7%). Regis expects 2022/2023 GDP of 6%/6% while Bloomberg consensus expects GDP to grow by 6.9% in 2022 and 5.5% in 2023. For 2024-2028, DBCC kept its growth targets of 6.5%-8% to reflect impact of government strategies and the Philippine Dev’t Plan.

GIR revised up to US$95.1bn in end-Nov; BOP posts US$756mn deficit in November. The BSP revised the end-November 2022 GIR level to US$95.1bn from a previously reported figure of US$93.95bn. GIR rose by 1.1% MoM (c. +US$1.1bn) but down from end-2021 GIR level of US$108.79bn. This represents adequate external liquidity buffer of 7.2 months’ worth of imports and 5.8x short-term external debt. The balance of payments recorded a deficit of US$756mn in November, reversing the BOP surplus of US$711mn in October 2022. On a YoY basis, deficit was higher than the US$123mn deficit recorded in Nov 2021. This brings YT-November BOP deficit to US$7.9bn (BSP FY22E target at US$11.2bn, equivalent to 2.8% of GDP).

Trade deficit shrinks to US$3.3bn in October, as exports rebound. The country’s trade balance recorded a deficit of US$3.31bn in October. This is 32% lower than the trade deficit recorded in September 2022 (US$4.8bn) and 13% lower than the trade deficit in Oct 2021 (US$3.82bn). This is the lowest trade deficit in 13 months. Imports growth slowed sharply to 7% YoY in October due to moderating growth in fuel & raw material imports, and a 3% YoY drop in capital goods imports. Exports rebounded by 20% YoY on stronger electronic exports. The YT-October trade deficit at US$49.98bn, up 54% YoY.

 

 

BSP to cut reserve ratio at end June 2023.The BSP plans to further reduce banks’ reserve requirement ratio before the end of the first half. The cut will coincide with monetary authority’s plan to wind down from tightening. It has pledged to cut the reserve requirement ratio to below 10% by 2023 from 12% currently. The BSP will keep raising interest rates in the next two meetings according to BSP Governor Felipe Medalla. The BSP has raised key policy rates by 350bps so far this year to catch up with the Fed’s 425bps rate hike.

Bank lending rises 13.9% YoY in October. U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) accelerated to 13.9% YoY in October from 13.4% in September. This is the 15th straight month of loan growth. On a MoM basis, loans rose +0.6%, buoyed by consumer loans (+2.2%). Recall that loan growth for the entire banking system rose by +10.7% in September. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) accelerated slightly to +5.4% in October from +5.2% in September.

IT-BPM sector on track to meet 2022 targets. The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it was on track to reach 1.56mn employees (+8% from 2021) and US$32bn in revenue (+8% from 2021). These are in line with the association’s 2022 targets. The BPO sector is one of the major drivers of office demand. However, Regis expects continued implementation of work-from-home policies to keep office demand below pre-COVID levels.

Vehicle sales rise 9% MoM, 32% YoY in November. Vehicle sales jumped 9% MoM, 32% YoY in November to 35,037 units according to data from CAMPI/TMA. Note that CAMPI/TMA began including Chinese-brand Cherry in September 2022. This brings YT-November sales to 314k units, up 30% YoY. Toyota unit sales were undisclosed. CAMPI/TMA targeting sales of 336k units (+25% YoY) in 2022.

 

Government debt up 0.9% MoM to P13.64tn in end-October. The National Government’s outstanding debt rose by 0.9% MoM (+P124bn) to P13.64tn in end-October. This is 16% higher than in end-Dec 2021 (P11.7tn) and equivalent to 62.5% of GDP (Nominal GDP 2022E: P21.8tn). Domestic debt accounted for 68.6% of total debt and was up +0.6% MoM and 15% higher than in end-Dec 2021. Meanwhile, external debt accounted for 31.4% of total debt and was up +1.6% MoM and +20% higher than in end-Dec 2021. In USD terms, external loans stood at US$73.8bn (+2.7% MoM and +5.8% higher than in end-Dec 2021). The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually go down to 51% by 2028.

Congress ratifies 2023 budget. The Bicameral Conference Committee ratified the proposed P5.268tn national budget for 2023. Under the consolidated version, the Bicam restored the P150mn confidential and intelligence funds of the Department of Education, among other things. 

Key changes were:

Education sector budget raised to P900.9bn, up from the Senate’s proposal of P884.6bn

Infrastructure budgets for the Department of Justice and Department of Interior and Local Government  were also raised (but were not disclosed)

Budget for the Energy Regulatory Commission was increased by P453mn to support its capital outlay

An additional P10bn was given to the Department of Public Works and Highways to address delays in project implementation and to repair school buildings and bridges

Budget could be signed into law before Congress goes on Christmas break on Dec 17

 

House Bill reforming property tax assessment transmitted to Senate. The House of Representatives approved on third reading a bill “Instituting reforms in real property valuation and assessment…”. The bill has been transmitted to the Senate. The bill seeks to transfer the responsibility of determining market values (for purposes of real property taxes) from Local Government Units (LGU) to the national government (specifically, a division of the Department of Finance). LGUs (such as city governments) have generally been reluctant to raise market values/property taxes because the elected officials might lose their reelections. Some LGUs have not updated their values in over 10 years. However, local government units will still determine the tax rate to be applied for real property taxes. Thus, local governments might lower the tax rate on real property in order to limit the impact on their constituents. Pre-COVID, real property taxes comprised 3%-7% of the pretax profits of the developers we cover.

Lower House approves Maharlika Investment Fund bill. The House of Representatives approved on third and final reading the bill creating the controversial Maharlika Investment Fund. Voting 279-6, the House of Representatives overwhelmingly approved House Bill (HB) No. 6608, which was just filed two weeks ago. President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. on Wednesday certified the MIF bill as urgent. A mix of public funds and dividends from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) will be used as seed money for the MIF, based on the current iteration. Initial funding will come from the Land Bank of the Philippines (P50bn), Development Bank of the Philippines (P25bn) and BSP. The BSP will initially put in the MIF 100% of its dividends to be declared as income. Subsequent annual contributions will come from BSP dividends, and part of the gaming revenues of the PAGCOR. and other state-owned gaming operators, among others. According to a BSP official, the BSP’s projected dividends for 2022 is P30-35bn, which is equivalent to 50% of its income. Recall that the original bill involved public funds of P275bn, which included contributions from SSS (P50bn) and GSIS (P125bn).

Proposed Philippine sovereign wealth fund will not receive capital from pension funds.  Representative Stella Quimbo, senior vice chair of the House appropriations panel, said that the proposed Maharlika Wealth Fund will not receive capital from Social Security System (SSS) or Government Service Insurance System (GSIS). The original proposal was for GSIS and SSS to contribute a combined P175bn out of the Maharlika Wealth Fund’s P275bn. Instead, Representative Quimbo said that funds will be sourced from the profits of BSP.

Implementing rules for SIM Registration Law released. The implementing rules and regulation (IRR) for the SIM Registration Act (RA 11934) were released by the NTC. The Law provides for the mandatory registration of all SIMs. Individuals will have to provide personal details and an ID.  “Juridical entities” will have to be provide business names and authorized signatories. Foreign tourists will have to provide passport details and addresses in the country. All new SIMs will have to be registered before activation. Existing SIMs will have 180 days to register or risk deactivation; deadline can be extended by another 120 days.

SIMs registered to tourists will be automatically deactivated after 30 days, but this can extended. Penalties are imposed for registering false information or selling stolen SIMs. Telcos are required to deactivate SIMs used for fraudulent calls/text upon investigation. Regis expects the implementation of this to result in a large decrease in active SIMs / subscribers. Many SIMs likely to be deactivated by the deadline. Revenues are also likely to be temporarily affected but ARPUs likely to rise (subscriber count decreased more than revenue). Longer term, the practice of throwaway or burner SIMs will be reduced. The other key impact will be on micro-finance / fintech firms as SIMs can now be identified with individuals. This will also allow lending through mobile apps to be accomplished with greater security.
