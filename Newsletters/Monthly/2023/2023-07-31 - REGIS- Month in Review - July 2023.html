REGIS REPORT

31 July 2023
 

The PSEi fought its way back to the 6,600 level in July, closing just short of it with a 1.9% MoM ascent for the month. After two straight months of declines, and after a drop of as much as 1.4% MTD, the slowing US inflation print provided impetus for the market’s rise. In conjunction, foreign inflows helped to lift the PSEi.

Domestically, inflation also slowed. CPI inflation for the month of June came in at 5.4%, coming in slightly lower than Street expectations (5.5%). The inflation figure represents a deceleration from the prior month’s 6.1%, which itself was down from 6.6% in April. This is also near the lower end of the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) expectation range (5.3% to 6.1%). Core inflation was at 7.4%, dropping from 7.7%.  

While the decline in headline CPI provides room for the Bangko Sentral ng PIlipinas (BSP) to cut rates later in the year, the US Fed’s latest 25-bp hike should also influence the BSP’s next move. New BSP Governor Eli Remolona gave the market a hint on monetary policy direction.  Amidst policy rate speculation, Remolona says “we’re contemplating whether to hike or not to hike…not thinking about whether to cut or not to cut.” Meanwhile, Romolona also said that he would like to lower the reserve requirement ratio down further from 9.5%.

President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr made his second State of the Nation Address (SONA).  Among the most notable legislative priorities he mentioned were taxes on single use plastics, and VAT on digital services. President Marcos also outlined the creation of a Department  of Water Resources, reform of the pension system for uniformed personnel, reform of the immigration system and the rationalization of taxes on mining. Apart from the legislative priorities, Marcos discussed plans to expand road and bridge infrastructure to connect the country as well the push for renewable energy. Separately, House Speaker Martin Romualdez separately mentioned other legislative proposals including amendments to the BOT Law, reform of financial markets taxes, valuation reform for real property, amendments to the legal framework of the power reform and changes to the Bank Secrecy Law.

President Marcos signed the bill creating the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) into law. The MIF will have initial seed capital of P125bn from government financial institutions. The MIF is supposed to be used for infrastructure investments outside of the amounts directly appropriated under the national budget.

The PHP appreciated for a second straight month, appreciating by 0.6% to PHP54.898/US$. This coincides with USD weakness globally, with the DXY falling 1.0% last month.

Key data
 

For the month of July,  foreigners remained net buyers for $332m largely due to RCBC’s share sale to Sumitomo 15%. Average daily turnover fell sharply to US$87m, lower by 37% MoM. Excluding the RCB sale, foreigners were still net buyers in the market for US$56m, while ADV for the month stood at a mere US$73m.

Foreigners were overall net buyers in July,  with JFC seeing the largest net inflow of US$21.2m. Foreign interest in the name strengthened after the company guided that its international operations grew. ADV of the stock jumped 1.8x MoM.

The consumer space was volatile this month after proposed taxes on junk food, sugar sweetened bevrages and single-use plastic. The government is also looking to stabilize domestic sugar prices by allowing local sugar earmarked for exports to be sold domestically. URC fell 11% this month and is one of the top foreign sold name.

Banks continued to see foreign interest. Foreigners have been buying BDO and BPI for the past seven months. Foreigners have been sellers in MBT but July shows a much slower pace (-US$2.3m) vs that of June (US$12.7m). 

Foreign inflow was seen in property names this month. ALI was most notable, with foreigners turning buyers after selling heavily the previous month. The stock rallied 12.8%.

TEL meanwhile was the top foreign sold name in July, with US$8.5m in net outflows. But despite the selling, the stock managed to end the month flat.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Ayala Land (+12.8% MoM) was sharply higher, taking the top index gainer spot. Laggard interest finally pulled the stock higher, after a long string of underperformance.  Ayala Land was also the top index mover for the month, pushing the index higher by 40 index points (vs the PSEi’s overall 123-point gain).  Large cap SM Prime () also managed to close higher.  The broader property sector was also higher, with Robinsons Land (+5.0% MoM), Megaworld (+3.0% MoM) and SM Prime (+1.7% MoM) also advancing. 

Meanwhile, the smaller cap property companies bore the brunt of lingering concerns on the sector.  Vista Land (-7.6% MOM) fell, with consecutive quarters (14) of residential revenue declines. Filinvest Land (-1.4% MoM) widened its YTD decline to 23.3%, as continued concerns on office vacancies prevailed.  FLI’s vacancy rate of 39% is the highest among developers under Regis developers by a wide margin; non-REIT office vacancy stands at 66%.

Banks continued to outperform, with BDO (+5.4% MoM) and BPI (+5.2% MoM) contributing 31 and 25-index points (25% and 20%)  to the PSEi’s overall gain in July.  Both banks reported a solid set of 2Q results which pointed to room for consensus forecast upgrades. BPI’s 1H23 P25.1bn NPAT was up 23% and came in well ahead of consensus forecasts. BDO meanwhile reported a record -high P18.7bn profit for 2Q23, taking 1H NPAT to 35.2bn – ahead of consensus. Meanwhile, playing catch up was Metrobank (+7.7% MoM), also pushing the index higher. 

Conglomerates were mixed.  Large cap SM Investments (-1.4% MoM) and Ayala Corp (-1.6% MoM) had a muted performance in July.  Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-1.1% M0M) also inched lower.  AEV denied a news article saying that the group acquired controlling interest in Air Asia Philippines.  The purchase would have made the group the partner of the parent company of Air Asia (CAPITALA MK) for the latter’s Philippine operations.

Meanwhile, smaller cap Cosco Capital (+9.0% MoM), which is reportedly contemplating a REIT listing that would be spun off from its leasing operations (real estate and fuel terminal facility leasing), rose. DMCI (+6.0% MoM) and GT Capital  (+5.8% MOM) also ended higher. Unit sales of GTCAP’s auto unit Toyota were 14% YoY higher in June, with 1H unit sales up 17% and looking on track to meet full year assumptions.

LT Group (+0.9% MoM) managed a slight gain despite its tobacco operations seeing a 20% YoY drop in tobacco volumes for 2Q23

Metro Pacific (+2.1% MoM) disclosed that the terms of the proposed tender offer have been adjusted to reflect a higher buyout price of P5.20/share. This represents a 12% increase from the previous tender offer price of P4.63/share. A consortium comprising First Pacific (142 HK), GT Capital (GTCAP PM), Mit-Pacific and MPI management previously offered P4.63/share to buy the 36.5% stake held by the public and thus take MPI private.

Meanwhile, MPI and San Miguel (+3.3% MoM) are teaming up to build a new 96-km toll road traversing the provinces of Cavite and Batangas and connecting the STAR and CALAX toll roads.

 

URC (-10.8% MoM) was the top index decliner for the month, marking its third month of declines. A myriad of issues has been hurting sentiment, starting with the junk food and expanded sugar tax as well as the  single-use plastic tax that was floated earlier this month.

Retailers were soft, as 2Q results from Wilcon (-5.4% MoM) and Robinsons Retail (-0.3% MoM) were disappointing despite expected normalization, mainly due to a high YoY base.  Wilcon’s revenue for 2Q was just 4% higher YoY as spending on home improvement slowed down. Meantime, RRHI’s revenue growth decelerated to +8% in 2Q while EBIT margins stayed low at 4.4%. Puregold () also declined.  For 2Q23, net sales rose 7% YoY, with SSSG estimated to have slowed to low single digits, driven by base effects.

On the other hand, Jollibee (+6.6% MoM) was among the top index gainers.  CFO Richard Shin says that the company is aiming for system-wide sales growth of 15-20% for the year as conditions normalize across its markets.  This is based on expectations of same-store sales growth of 7-10% across its portfolio, with international operations seeing an acceleration.

Telcos were mixed, with laggard interest taking Globe (+10.9% MoM) higher. Globe’s potential annual prepaid fiber subscribers could reach 200,000 by end of the year, according to a news report.  If indeed 200k is added annually, the incremental revenue for this (assuming ARPU of P500/month) is P1.2 billion or 0.7% of Globe’s revenue, the prepaid segment could be a growth driver for the broadband segment given the saturated postpaid fiber market.

ICTSI (+6.5% MoM) won another port contract to run a terminal in the largest port in Africa. ICT was selected as the equity partner of South Africa’s state logistics firm Transnet SOC and thus the operator of Pier 2 in the Port of Durban. The Durban port handled 2.4m TEUs of throughput in 2021, of which Pier 2 accounted for the lion’s share. For perspective, ICTSI’s groupwide throughput last year was 12m TEUs.

Meanwhile, the Manila North Harbour terminal of ICTSI secured a ~10% tariff increase. The tariff adjustment is estimated to raise profits by 1%, enough to offset the recent loss of ICTSI’s Pakistani operations.

 

Aboitiz Power (-6.2% MoM) fell on speculation of FTSE deletion in the next rebal.  AboitizPower Corp., through its subsidiary AboitizPower Distributed Renewables, Inc. (APX), has partnered with Upgrade Energy Philippines, Inc. (UGEP) to pursue solar rooftop projects in the Philippines. The group is committed to developing 500 MW of utility-scale and 100 MW of commercial-scale renewable energy by 2030.

Meanwhile, ACEN (-3.2% MoM) continued to decline in July.  ACEN is issuing up to P12.5bn worth of preferred shares under two series. The first series will have a step-up on the fifth year and will be callable from the third anniversary; so, it is properly benchmarked against the 5-year Philippine Treasury yields. The other series is a true perp with no step-up and is callable only at the option of the issue on the 7th year. This latter series is thus more properly benchmarked against the longest dated Treasury yields. ACEN also announced a joint venture agreement with Yindjibarndi Aboriginal Corp, an Australian aboriginal group in Western Australia, to develop as much as 3GW of power generation capacity. The initial project plans involve 750MW of RE capacity. This deal provides another leg up for ACEN in relation to its goal of reach 20GW by 2030. The company also plans to enter a joint venture with Norway's investment fund Norfund to provide solar power to telecommunications towers in the Philippines.  

First Gen (+8.8% MoM) surged. First Gen signed an agreement for the delivery of its first LNG cargo. The delivery and subsequent use of that LNG would mark the initial operation of FGEN’s LNG terminal. The terminal would allow FGEN to run ~2,000MW of its generation assets even in the absence of gas from Malampaya.

 

Meralco (+6.3% MoM) reported core income of P19.2bn for 1H23, up +47%, which would put it well on track to beat full-year estimates of both Regis and the Street. The source of the outperformance, again, was the generation business, with Singapore genco PacificLight contributing the lion’s share of profit.

The unplanned shutdown relating to one unit of Semirara’s (+3.0% MoM) Calaca plant will take until end of August. This will take out 135MW of SCC’s generation capacity, around 20% of its total. Power prices have been high, however, boding well for SCC’s 2Q profitability.

Bloomberry (+6.8% MoM) advanced. Apart from earlier speculation of a possible PSEi inclusion down the road, sentiment for BLOOM has been bolstered by gaming agency PAGCOR’s hike in its 2023 gaming revenue target (P273bn), implying 27% growth vs 2022. This is also 11% higher than the target set in March and suggests improved trends on the ground, including integrated resorts.

The parent company of Holcim Philippines (suspended) has initiated a tender offer for the remaining 5% public float of HLCM. The float of HLCM is down to just slightly above 5% following Holderfin’s acquisition of 9% of HLCM from Sumitomo. The tender offer price is P5.33/share, equivalent to an EV/MT of just around US$90.

Megawide (-3.1% MoM) is paying P5.2bn to acquire a set of property assets from its controlling shareholders. The purchase price is equivalent to ~80% of MWIDE’s market cap of P6.3bn at the time of the announcement. With net debt at P22bn and equity of P10bn (treating prefs as debt), gearing will become even higher post-transaction.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases to 5.4% YoY but MoM trend accelerates. Headline CPI rose 5.4% YoY in June, moderating further from 6.1% the previous month. This came in slightly below consensus estimates (+5.5%) and at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range (+5.3%-6.1%). The drivers of easing were similar to what we have seen in previous months: base effects of food and fuel-related items such as transport and utilities.

On a MoM basis, however, headline CPI rose 0.2%, accelerating further from 0.0% in May. This was due to higher prices of food items (+0.3% MoM vs 0.2% in May), mainly rice (+0.6% MoM, rising for four consecutive months) and vegetables. Prices of alcohol were up +0.5% MoM in June, though decelerating from a +2.0% MoM increase in March 2023. Non-food items were muted (+0.0%), with electricity prices and fuel falling 1.3% MoM and -0.5% MoM, respectively. These offset rising prices for passenger transport services (air and sea).

Core CPI remained stubbornly high, rising by 0.2% MoM, 7.4% YoY. This was on the back of higher prices of fish and vegetables; Non-food items: passenger transport (toll fees, air, and sea fares) and housing rental.

1H23 inflation averaged at 7.2%, still above the BSP’s 2-4% target range. Assuming CPI rises +0.2% MoM for the rest of the year, Dec 2023 inflation should already be c. 2%.

Newly appointed BSP Governor Eli Remolona said it will be “dangerous” to cut rates ahead of the Fed.

The next BSP policy meeting is on Aug 17 while the Fed’s next rate decision will be on July 27 (consensus: +25bps).

Cash remittances rise 2.8% YoY in May. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 2.8% YoY to US$2.49bn in May. In PHP terms, remittances in May are up 9.8% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate accelerated slightly to +3.2% in May. For the month, land-based remittances grew 2.9% YoY, lifted by remittances from Singapore, Saudi Arabia, and UAE. Meanwhile, Sea-based remittances were also higher by 2.4% YoY. This brings YT-May remittances to US$12.98bn, up 3.1% YoY and in line with the BSP’s 2013 target (+3% YoY, US$33.5bn).

GIR dips to US$99.8bn in June. The country’s dollar reserves (GIR) dipped to US$99.8bn in end-June from US$100.6bn the previous month (revised from US$100.3bn). This is also the first time in four months that the GIR level went below US$100bn. The government cited national government’s debt payments and a drop in the value of its gold holdings. The end-June level is sufficient to cover 7.6months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects dollar reserves to hit U$100bn by year-end.

Trade deficit narrows to US$4.4bn in May. The country’s trade deficit shrunk to US$4.4bn in May, down 21% YoY and 9% MoM due to a rebound in exports (+2% YoY) and a sustained drop in imports. Imports fell 9% YoY due largely to a drop in the value of fuel imports (-32% YoY) and raw materials (-12%). Regional gasoline and diesel prices were down by 39% YoY in May. Consumer goods imports surged by 25% YoY buoyed by passenger cars, home appliances, and food. Capital goods imports declined by 6% YoY on lower telecom equipment and electrical machinery. Exports rose 1.9% YoY in May, snapping a consecutive five-month decline due to a recovery of exports to China (+23% YoY). For 5M23, the trade deficit is unchanged YoY at US$23.99bn. The government is forecasting a trade deficit of c. US$60bn for 2023 (~14% of GDP). The trade deficit in 2022 stood at US$58.244bn (14% of GDP).

Gov’t debt service rises 98% YoY to P819bn in 5 months. The Philippines' debt service nearly doubled in the January to May 2023 period, reaching P819.5bn, a 97.92% YoY increase. Amortization of debt payments in the first five months of 2023 amounted to P589.952bn, up 204.72% YoY. Debt service payments was highest in February 2023 at P375.714bn, followed by P204.763bn in April 2023. In May 2023, the debt service contracted by 14.62% YoY to P49.047bn. Interest payments accounted for the bulk of the debt service payments in May, reaching P41.34bn, up 22.21% YoY.

Domestic interest payments in May were P29.529bn, while payments for foreign loans reached P11.815bn. Amortization payments in May 2023 reached P7.71bn, lower by 67.38% YoY. Meanwhile, domestic amortization payments in May were P2.656bn and external payments amounted to P5.047bn. The government’s debt stock reached P14.096tn in end-May, up 5.1% from end-2022. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.2% for 2023. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51.1% by 2028.

 

PH ‘hot money’ records US$1mn net inflow in June; 1H23 net outflow reaches US$803m. Foreign investment transactions (short-term) registered with the BSP (i.e., “hot money”) saw a net inflow of US$1mn, a turnaround from four consecutive months of net outflows. Improvement attributed to the easing inflation trend in both the Philippines and  the United States, as well as the pause in the Fed’s rate hikes. This brings 1H23 net outflow to US$803mn, a reversal of the US$778.3mn net inflow in the same period last year. Philippine economy was showing rapid recovery amid continued reopening, but there was marked volatility in both domestic and international markets, particularly in the United States due to high inflation.

Govt. revenues hit P1.9 trillion in the first half.  The National Government's revenues grew by 7.7% YoY to P1.9 trillion in the first six months of the year, driven by higher economic activity and digital transformation in revenue agencies. Tax collections in the January-to-June period rose by 7.5%, while nontax revenues grew by 9.1%. The improved revenue collection attributed to the digitization efforts of the BIR and Bureau of Customs. The government is targeting to raise P3.729 trillion in revenues this year, equivalent to 15.2% of GDP. The BIR Commissioner expressed hope in meeting the P2.6-trillion collection target for this year, emphasizing improved services for taxpayers. The proposed P5.768-trillion 2024 national budget will be submitted to the House of Representatives on August 2, with priorities on infrastructure, food security, digital transformation, and human capital development.

Government debt rises to P14.1tn in May. The National Government’s total outstanding debt rose by 1.4% MoM (+P185.4bn) to P14.096tn in May. This is also higher by 5.1% (P678bn) from end-2022. The MoM increase due to net issuances of domestic and external debt and the depreciation of the PHP against the USD. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 61.2% for 2023. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51.1% by 2028.

Domestic borrowings were 1.2% higher from the previous month at P9.59tn, accounting for 68% of total debt. External debt rose to P4.51tn, up 1.2% MoM due to net availment of external loans and the impact of the PHP depreciation against the USD.

Marcos signs Maharlika bill. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. will sign the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) Act into law. The MIF is a sovereign fund that the government will use to invest in major projects as part of the country's economic transformation. Fund has faced criticism over concerns of potential misappropriation of public funds. The MIF will have a total of P125 billion, with contributions from the Land Bank of the Philippines, the Development Bank of the Philippines, and the national government. President Marcos Jr. assures the public that the fund will be independent from the government and managed professionally to ensure the safety of people's pension funds. Representative Luis Raymund Villafuerte Jr. expresses confidence that the MIF will provide the economy with a new growth stimulant, enabling increased spending on public infrastructure and other major programs. The establishment of the MIF comes amid global economic challenges, but the Philippines has secured a higher investment-grade status, providing a bright spot for domestic growth with the MIF attracting private investments.

Banks’ asset quality ratios deteriorate further in May. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) rose to 3.55% in May from 3.51% in April and 3.26% in end-Dec 2022. In nominal terms, NPLs rose 1.9% MoM, slower than +3.1% in April. Past due ratio rose to 4.17% from 4.13% in April and 3.8% in end-Dec 2022. Past due loans rose 1.3% MoM to P525bn in May vs. end-Dec 2022: P479bn. Meanwhile, restructured loan ratio improved to 2.46% in May from 2.58% in April. NPL cover dipped to 101.8% in May from 103.3% in April. The NPL data seems at odds with the commentary from management of banks Regis covers.

During their respective 1Q23 briefings, all four banks suggested that asset quality ratios were steady into 2Q23, and all claimed that there was no meaningful stress among borrowers. This was likely due to consumer loans, higher yielding/higher NPLs, are growing faster than total loans. As of 1Q23, consumer loans grew 17% YoY (vs. +10.5% for overall banking sector). Consumer loan NPLs dipped 15bps QoQ to 6.39% from 6.54% in end-2022.

Vehicle sales rise 27% YoY in June. Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI, jumped 27% YoY in June (-5% MoM) to 36,311 units. This brings 1H23 sales to 202,415 units, up 31% YoY. Toyota sold 16,381 units, up 14% YoY but down 8% MoM. Regis estimates Toyota’s market share dipped further to 45.1% in June from 46.8% in May. This brings 1H23 sales to 93,575 units, up 17% YoY.

 

 

Registered SIMs hit 63% of total before deadline. The number of registered SIM cards reached 105.92 million, meeting the target before the July 25 deadline, according to the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC). The registration represents 63.04% of the total 168.02 million subscribers across the three public telecommunication entities. The NTC stated that there will be no further extension in the deadline for registering active SIM cards. Smart Communications, Inc. registered 49.99 million subscribers, representing 75.39% of its total subscribers. Globe Telecom, Inc. recorded 48.38 million registrants, accounting for 55.77% of its total subscribers.

Manufacturing growth weakest since July '22. Philippine manufacturing growth slowed to a one-year low in June, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence. The manufacturing purchasing managers’ index remained above the 50.0 mark but dropped to 50.9 in June from 52.2 in May, indicating the weakest improvement in business conditions since July 2022.

Output and factory orders supported overall growth, but the rates of increase eased, and manufacturers reported weaker underlying demand trends.

Employment in the manufacturing sector fell, with some firms reducing their payroll numbers and not replacing voluntary leavers. On the other hand, no adverse impact on the overall level of unfinished worked, which remained unchanged in June.

Manufacturers remain optimistic about production in the next 12 months, although the level of positive sentiment was weaker than the series trend.

Global headwinds could potentially dampen the outlook for manufacturers in the Philippines. The growth potential for manufacturing is affected by elevated rates and China's faltering recovery.

PH set to hit digital payments target. The Philippine payments system aims to convert 50% of retail payments to digital form by 2023. Digital platforms accounted for 42% of transactions in 2022, up from 30% the previous year.

In terms of value, digital payments represented 40% of all transactions, down from 44% in 2021. Total transactions were valued at $195 billion, with $78.17 billion conducted digitally. Government payments were predominantly digital, making up 96% of all transactions. Individuals and businesses made digital payments 55% and 10% of the time, respectively. Merchant payments, person-to-person transfers, and salaries contributed to the growth of digital payments.

Ownership of transaction accounts, especially e-money accounts, has significantly  increased, indicating increased usage of payments, according to the BSP Financial Inclusion Survey Report.

Additional P100bn revenues seen from digital transaction tax. The Department of Finance (DOF) expects to generate nearly P100bn in revenues over the next five years from the proposed value-added tax (VAT) on digital transactions. The imposition of a 12% VAT on digital service providers aims to level the playing field between traditional and digital businesses. Digital advertising, subscription-based services, and other services using information and communication technology-enabled infrastructure are included in the plan.

The DOF's estimate assumes a 50% collection efficiency in the first year, improving to 70% by 2025 until 2028. Revenues are projected to reach P72.69bn with 50% collection efficiency over the five-year period and up to P145.37bn with 100% collection efficiency.

The tax system currently covers only traditional businesses, missing substantial revenues from the digital economy. Taxing non-resident digital service providers is seen as fair and equal treatment in taxation, ensuring competition and preventing erosion of the VAT base.

The government aims to collect taxes from the growing digital economy to fund public services, infrastructure, and socioeconomic programs. Neighboring Asian countries have also updated their tax frameworks to capture revenue from digital transactions, with VAT rates ranging from 5% to 11% on digital transactions.

Department of Transportation prefers 15-year concession life for NAIA, with option for 10-year extension.  The Department of Transportation stands by its decision to lock the concession period for the NAIA privatization at 15 years. However, they have incorporated an option to extend it by another 10 years in the event that circumstances warrant (or if demand/passenger volumes breach the theoretical limit of 62mn per annum).

They want a total of four gateways to choose from for the Greater Capital Region: NAIA, Clark, San Miguel’s New Manila International Airport, and Sangley, according to Transportation Undersecretary Roberto Lim.

NAIA’s pax volumes reached 47.8mn in 2019; Pax volumes grew at a 6.5% CAGR from 2013-2019. A separate unsolicited proposal from a consortium is seeking for a 25-year concession. The proposal involves an investment of P267bn. NEDA is currently reviewing the two proposals. 

 

NEDA Board approves P170.6bn solicited NAIA PPP project, 2 other infrastructure projects.  The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) approved the P170.6bn solicited proposal to privatize the operations of the Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA). The project was proposed by the Department of Transportation and the Manila International Airport Authority. The winning bidder will be able to operate NAIA for a period of 15 years, with an option to extend by another 10 years in the event that construction for the Bulacan airport and Sangley airports are delayed. The project aims to increase the pax volume capacity of the airport to 62mn pax (from 35mn today) and boost air traffic movements to 48 per hour (from 40 today).

The NEDA Board also rejected the P267bn unsolicited proposal of the Manila International Airport Consortium (MIAC). MIAC proposed to pay an upfront payment of P57bn and capex of P210bn over a 25-year concession period. Capex proposal aimed to increase the capacity of the airport to 62.5mn by 2028 and 70mn by 2048. The government believes project implementation under the solicited route will be faster. It aims to publish the bid documents by August and auction the project before year-end. The NEDA Board also approved two other infrastructure projects. The P7.48bn Samar Pacific Coastal Road 2 Project and the P12.75bn unsolicited proposal for the upgrade, expansion, and O&M of the Laguindingan airport. (Proponent: Aboitiz InfraCapital, a unit of Aboitiz Equity Ventures, AEV PM). 

Sugar for export may now be diverted for local use. The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) allows the reallocation of unused sugar originally intended for the global market for domestic use to meet the growing local demand.

SRA issued Sugar Order (SO) No. 8 and SO No. 9 to reinstate homeless "A" (sugar for export to the US) and "D" quedans (for the world market) and reclassify them for the local market, subject to conditions. The move aims to stabilize the supply and selling prices in the local sugar market and alleviate the perceived tightness in domestic sugar balance for crop year 2022-2023

Requests for the conversion of unshipped "A" and "D" sugar quedans have been received, with around 11,432 MT for "A" sugar and 5,478 MT for "D" sugar remaining outstanding. Traders filing requests for the reinstatement of homeless "A" and "D" sugar quedans need to pay a fee of P15 per 50-kilogram bag, and the deadline for accepting applications is August 4. In a separate order, the SRA allows the reclassification or conversion of verified unshipped "A" and "D" sugar quedans from crop year 2020-2021 and previous crop years to "B" sugar on a voluntary basis for crop year 2023-2024. Holders of verified unshipped "A" and "D" sugar quedans can apply for conversion to "B" sugar until August 15, and late applications will not be accepted.

P138bn in projects get green lane certificates. Four projects worth P183bn have received green lane certificates from the BoI, indicating significant investments in sustainable projects, including floating solar, data centers, and common towers. SunAsia Energy's floating solar panel project on Laguna Lake tops the list with a P66 billion investment to produce 1,300 megawatts (MW) of power. Nayon Kontrol System Solar One Inc. will establish a P15 billion, 250-MW floating solar project in Caliraya, Laguna. Philtower Consortium Inc. plans to build 7,907 built-to-suit common towers valued at P52 billion for telecommunication infrastructure.

Narra Technology Development Park's P50-billion hyperscale data center in New Clark City, Tarlac, aims to support robust, scalable applications and boost digitalization in the Philippines. Eight more projects worth P78.5 billion are under evaluation, including floating solar projects, a micro-hydroelectric plant, a reinforcing steel bars producer, and a section mill project. Projects expected to generate at least 19,250 jobs in total, according to Trade and Industry Secretary Alfredo Pascual. The green lane certification is part of the government's efforts to improve ease of doing business and streamline government processes for strategic investments through Executive Order 18.
