REGIS REPORT

03 July 2023
 

The PCOMP was barely changed in June, slipping 0.1% amidst a lack of domestic catalysts. Trading was lackluster, with volumes settling at an anemic US$88m daily (ave) excluding one-off blocks.

CPI inflation for May came in at 6.1%, in line with market expectations and below the 6.6% number in April. The latest print provided the BSP room to maintain the policy rate, which it left unchanged at 6.25% during its Monetary Board meeting on 22 June. BSP Governor Felipe Medalla, whose term just expired, has said that he would consider cutting rates if CPI inflations falls within the BSP’s target band (2%-4%) for two consecutive months.

President Marcos appointed Eli Remolona as the next Governor of the BSP. He takes office on July 3. Remolona’s monetary policy views have yet to be made known. Remolona has a PhD from Stanford University and was previously with the US Federal Reserve and Bank for International Settlements (BIS). Nonetheless, it is not unreasonable to assume the BSP under Remolona would lean towards rate cuts sooner rather than later. Needless to say, the US Federal Reserve, which is signaling more rate hikes this year, will have an influence on the BSP’s next moves.  As for inflation, even the BSP is acknowledging the upside risk to inflation. While headline inflation has been dropping largely because international commodity prices, core inflation (ex- food and energy) is still at 7.7% (vs 7.9% in Apr) and may pose a challenge to tame.

The Department of Finance unveiled its first major tax reform proposal, with the proposed imposition of new taxes on what it considers “junk food” and an expansion of the existing sweetened beverage tax.  Under its proposal, the measure will raise excise taxes on beverages sweetened by sugar by a further P6/liter and remove most exemptions (including the one on coffee mixes). The DOF measure also plans to impose a tax on snacks like potato chips equivalent to 20-40% of current retail prices.  Diokno is also floating the idea of combining these tax proposals with the liberalization of the highly protected sugar industry.  

The administration is opting to pursue private sector funding to support infrastructure projects, given national government finances have been relatively weaker vs pre-pandemic years.  Supportive of this cause, the government has been taking steps to allow overdue tariff increase to come through, in order to honor commitments with the previous PPP concession agreements.  Among the tariff increase granted by the administration so far include water rate increase for the water concessionaries, toll fee hikes for various toll roads, as well as tariff increase for two of the rail lines in Metro Manila (LRT-1 and LRT-2).

Already, there is movement in terms of PPP (public-private partnerships) initiatives. The Department of Transportation (DOTr) has submitted its proposal to privatize the operation of Manila’s airport NAIA, in what could be the first major PPP project under the current administration.  A consortium of the country’s biggest business groups, the Manila International Airport Consortium, also submitted its revised unsolicited proposal to rehabilitate and operate NAIA.  The revised proposal involves capex of P267bn (US$4.8bn), more than doubling NAIA’s capacity to 70m per annum and payments to government of as much as P280bn (US$5bn). The NEDA has also cleared the proposal to extend the  Tarlac-Pangasinan-La Union Expressway (TPLEX).

Key data
 

For the month of June, foreigners turned net buyers for $97m. Average daily turnover meanwhile slightly improved to $140m, largely due to the cross related to BPI's property dividend. Excluding that cross, foreigners are actually net sellers in the market for $57.8m. Average daily turnover was anemic -- at just  $88m.

Foreign participation meanwhile dipped to 38% vs 53% last month.

Foreign interest for the month was mostly in banks, with BPI and BDO seeing the largest inflows.  Both have seen consistent foreign buying since start of the year even as peer Metrobank has underperformed in the past two months as  foreign selling has weighed on the stock.

Ayala Land was the top underperformer and top sold for the month with net outflows of $23.8m.  Foreign selling accelerated after stock broke the P26 support level early in June, but losses were cut in half with bargain hunters buying the stock near its 52-week low.

Consumer names also saw some volatility after DoF/DoH’s proposal to increase tax in junk food and sweetened beverages. Foreigners ended net sellers in the sector with URC seeing the most outflow for $8.3m.

Locals were pretty active in MPI on speculation of a higher tender offer price.

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The PSEi (-0.1% MoM) consolidated in June, after declining over 2% in May.

Banks were mixed in June, after dragging the index lower in May. The BSP is unwinding the temporary relief measure relating to alternative RRR compliance.  The BSP allowed bank loans to micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and large corporations as an alternative compliance with reserve requirements (RR) during the pandemic. The BSP is cutting RRR for different types of banks however and the RRR reduction should offset the expiration of the relief measure. The BSP is implementing a 250bps reduction in the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for universal and commercial banks. 

After shedding over 7% in the previous month, BPI (+8.9% MoM) rebounded. BPI’s property dividend declared last March (equivalent to an 8.96% stock dividend) became payable during the month.  The property dividend involved 406.18m shares.  

BDO (+1.3% MoM), meanwhile, arrested last month’s 5.6% drop, with a slight gain in June.

Most of the sector declined, despite scope for interest rates to be taken higher by the US Fed, thus giving room for sector margins to rise further. Metrobank (-3.3% MoM ) and  UnionBank (-4.5% MoM) declined.

Security Bank (-1.8% MoM also shed.

Much of the action within the consumer sector sprung from the Department Finance’s proposal to impose new taxes on “junk food” and expand the existing beverage tax.  Under its proposal, the measure will raise excise taxes on beverages sweetened by sugar by a further P6/liter and remove most exemptions, including the one on coffee mixes. The DOF measure also plans to impose a tax on snacks like potato chips equivalent to 20-40% of current retail prices The measure is also aimed at discouraging consumption of food with little nutritional value.  

Both URC (-2.2% MoM) and Monde (-1.1% MoM) dropped significantly on news of the proposal though the steep declines attracted buying interest thereafter.  DOF Secretary Diokno’s clarification that instant noodles would not be covered by the tax also helped supported the bounce.  Instant noodles are 7-8% of revenue for URC and ~40% for Monde. Excluding instant noodles, an estimated 40% of URC’s revenue and 25% of Monde’s revenue could be covered by the new tax proposals. Diokno also floated the idea of combining these tax proposals with the liberalization of the highly protected sugar industry.

Century Pacific (+9.6% MoM), DNL (+5.0% MoM) and Robinsons Retail (+6.4% MoM) recovered,  after falling 9.7%, 17.2%, and 5.5% in the previous month.

 

Puregold (-3.0% MoM), which announced a relatively small acquisition – 14 locations of Divimart Supermarket (a local chain with stores mostly outside Metro Manila) –ended the month lower.  Puregold currently has 531 stores/outlets as of 1Q23.

Wilcon (-15.0% MoM) was the sector underperformer, as its President and CEO Lorraine Belo-Cincochan guided for muted growth for the company in 2023, partly due to high base effects last year driven by the post-lockdown surge in demand.

Within the conglomerates space, it was Metro Pacific (+9.7% MoM) that made a marked move. MPI gained, with market speculation that MPI’s tender offer price would be higher than the original P4.63/sh.  The consortium proposing a tender offer and potential de-listing had asked for a deferral of the shareholder vote. The consortium said that the required “fairness opinion” from a third-party has “not been finalized”, and will not be ready in time for MPI’s AGM (6 June). The company will have to hold a special shareholders’ meeting at a later date, which has yet to be determined.

MPI meanwhile gained approval from the regulator to implement the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX).  The tariff hikes cover tariff petitions for years 2012/2014 (last tranches) and half of the 2018/2020 increases. NLEX is a 76%-owned unit of Metro Pacific, accounting for 23% of MPI’s gross asset value. Regis estimates the toll hikes will increase the tollroad unit’s FY23E/FY24E profit by 9%/19%, which will consequently increase MPI’s FY23E/FY24E profits by 3%/6%.

Other fare adjustments were approved, involving two of the rail lines in Metro Manila.  Fares for the LRT-1 and LRT-2 were increased 21%, effective Aug 2.  The minimum fare will now be P13.29 (24 US cents) from P11.0 (20 US cents). LRT-1 is operated by a consortium which includes Metro Pacific and Ayala Corp (-5.3% MoM) under a concession agreement. 

The Manila international Airport Consortium (MIAC) unveiled its P267bn unsolicited proposal to rehab and develop NAIA over a 25-year concession period. Along with US-based Global Infrastructure Partners,  MIAC includes six large conglos including Aboitiz InfraCapital –(AEV -0.25 MOM), AC Infrastructure (AC), Asia’s Emerging Dragon Corp.(LTG -0.7% MoM), Alliance Global – Infracorp Dev’t – (AGI +1.2% MoM), Filinvest Development Corp. (FDC -5.45% MoM ), and JG Summit (JGS ).  The proposal involves a total capex of P267bn over three phases and total concession fee payments of P280bn (covers revenue sharing and taxes) over the life of the concession.

 

Apart from prospects of higher interest rates, lingering concerns on the quality of pre-sales and a muted outlook for the office segment weighed on the property sector.   Property stocks were mostly lower, led by Ayala Land (-7.6% MoM) which continued to succumb to selling pressure.  Ayala Land is raising up to P19bn from fixed rate bonds with tenors of five (5) and ten (10) years. The funds will partly be for refinancing and partly for capex.

Megaworld (-2.5% MoM), Filinvest Land (-1.4% MoM) and Vista Land (-0.6% MoM) also ended lower for the month.

Robinsons Land (+2.7% MoM) rose, while SM Prime (+0.5% MoM) was up marginally. 

Strong buy interest in PLDT (+9.3% MoM) lifted the share price higher, after dropping to YTD lows in the prior month. Its  fintech arm Voyager Innovations is aiming to raise up to US$150m within this year.  Voyager is the holding company for e-wallet Maya and digital bank Maya Bank.

Globe (+1.8% MoM) closed up for the month as well.  Globe has introduced its prepaid fiber broadband product, GFiber Prepaid.  The prepaid fiber service comes over six months after Converge (+0.4% MoM) introduced its prepaid service, Surf2Sawa. The introduction of prepaid fiber is expected to help restart growth of the broadband.

Cebu Air (-10.2% MoM) was sold down in June, amidst a Senate probe related to passenger complaints against  the airline on issues including overbooking, flight delays, etc.  Also, Cagayan de Oro City 2nd District Rep. Rufus Rodriguez filed a resolution seeking the suspension of Cebu Air’s legislative franchise.  The resolution aims to serve as punishment for the airline’s “terrible” service. 

ICTSI (+4.5% MoM) recovered, in the wake of a 10% decline in the May.  The company disclosed that the port authority of Pakistan has allowed  ICT’s contract Pakistan International Container Terminal to expire (June 17, 2023).  Regis estimates PICT accounts for 4% of FY23E volumes, 2% of FY23E revenues, 1% of FY23E profit. It is unclear at this stage if ICT will need to impair the US$13mn of goodwill related to PICT.

 

Utilities were mixed. ACEN (-8.0% MoM) dropped sharply in June, extending its YTD decline to close to 30%.  The decline comes amidst speculation that AC holders entitled to receive ACEN shares as part of a property div announced last year have been adding to selling pressure.  ACEN completed the first phase of its transaction to acquire 49% of a portfolio of solar farms in Vietnam.  The entire deal, covering 837MW, was first announced last year.  Once fully closed, the purchase would add 10% to ACEN’s claimed capacity of 4,177MW.

Meanwhile, First Gen (-5.3% MoM) signed a MOU with Prime Infra for the latter to eventually lease and operate its LNG terminal. Prime Infra is the holding company of Enrique Razon for his infrastructure relate assets.

Holcim Philippines (+0.5% MoM) announced it will take steps to delist itself after its major shareholder Holderfin increased its stake by 9ppt to 27%. 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases to 6.1% in May. Headline CPI rose by 6.1% in May, further moderating from 6.6% in April. This is in line with Bloomberg consensus forecast (6.1%) and came at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 5.8%-6.6%. High base effects led to slower YoY increase in food prices of 7.4% YoY from 7.9% in April. It is worth noting that food prices actually rose +0.2% MoM in May, accelerating from negative MoM prints since February due to higher prices of rice and vegetables. Lower global fuel prices pushed transport and gas inflation to moderate to -0.5% YoY and -9.4% YoY, respectively. Electricity inflation also eased to 13.6% YoY in May from 14.6% in April, with flattish MoM growth rate.

Core inflation also eased to 7.7% in May from 7.9% in April, as core CPI rose just +0.2% MoM. YT-May inflation rate averaged 7.5% YoY, still above the BSP’s target band of 2-4% for the year.

Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno, who is part of the Monetary Board, predicts inflation will move closer to the target band by Sep. The next Monetary Board policy meeting is scheduled on June 22.

BSP extends policy rate hike pause. Monetary Board kept benchmark interest rates unchanged for a second straight meeting. The overnight repurchase rate maintained at 6.25%. 

BSP has raised its policy rate by 425bps since May 2022. The gap between the Federal Funds rate (5%) and the BSP’s policy rate maintained at 125bps (narrower than the average gap of c. 260bps since 2013). It trimmed its 2023 average inflation rate forecast to 5.4% (vs. 5.5% previously). The BSP expects inflation to average at 2.9% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025.

BSP Governor Felipe Medalla said that he will consider cutting rates if inflation rate falls below 4% for two consecutive months.  The latest inflation print in May stood at 6.1%, with 5M23 average inflation rate at 7.5%. Assuming CPI MoM is unchanged, headline inflation rate could fall below 4% by Sep 2023.

Remittances up 3.7% YoY in April. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.7% YoY in April.  In PHP terms, remittances are up 10.4%. The 3-month average growth rate steady at +3%.

Land-based remittances grew by 4% YoY, slightly accelerating from c.2.9% in Feb/Mar.  Growth was due to faster growth in remittances from the USA (+5.8% YoY in April vs. +3.1% in 1Q23). Sea-based remittances rose 2.7% YoY, fastest monthly increase since Dec 2022.

This took 4M23 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$10.49bn, in line with the government’s target of +3%.

 

Budget deficit narrows in May. The government recorded a budget deficit of P122bn in May, 17% lower YoY, due to higher revenues (+9.4% YoY) and muted expenditures (+0.9% YoY).

Revenues were boosted by higher tax collections (+2.9% YoY) and higher dividend and PAGCOR remittances, investment income and interest of deposits. The BIR collected P213bn, down -1.5% YoY but is broadly in line with the 4M23 monthly average collection of P210bn. Customs collections jumped 18% YoY (vs. +10% YoY in 4M23).

Government spending marginally increased (0.9% YoY), with primary spending (i.e. excluding interest) lower by 0.85% YoY. Excluding the lower National Tax Allotment (NTA) shares of local government units and net lending assistance to GOCCs, spending grew by 5.7% YoY. Interest payment rose sharply (+22% YoY).

The May print brings 5M23 deficit to P326bn, down 29% YoY and on track to come in lower than the government’s year-end target due to stronger revenues.

BOP posts US$439mn deficit in May. The country’s balance of payments recorded a US$439mn deficit in May. This is lower than the US$1.6bn deficit in May 2022 but higher than the US$148mn deficit in April 2023.  The government cited the government’s foreign currency debt obligations for the latest print.

This brings 5M23 BOP to a surplus of US$2.9bn, a reversal of the US$1.5bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a BOP deficit of US$1.2bn this year (0.3% of GDP), lower than last year’s deficit of US$7.3bn (1.8% of GDP).

Trade deficit shrinks to US$4.5bn in April. The country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.53bn in April, down by 15% YoY and 11% MoM. Imports slowed down sharply, falling by 18% YoY due likely to lower commodity prices. Exports also experienced its steepest monthly drop in April, falling by 20% YoY.

The cumulative 4M23 trade deficit is 5% YoY higher at US$19.28bn. The government is forecasting imports to rise by 4% YoY and exports to rise by 3% YoY in FY23. This implies a wider trade deficit of US$61.3bn for FY23E from US$58.2bn in FY22.

 

 

Dollar reserves slip to US$101.3bn in May. Gross international reserves (GIR) as of end-May fell by US$464mn (-0.5% MoM) to US$101.3bn, due to debt servicing, lower value of gold holdings, and exchange rate market operations. This represents a more-than-adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.6 months’ worth of imports.

BSP expects the GIR level to end at US$100bn by Dec 2023, up from US$82bn in Dec 2022 but lower than the peak of US$110bn in end-Dec 2020.

Wage board approves P40 hike in minimum wage in Metro Manilla. The regional wage board approved a P40 increase (+7%) in NCR’s minimum wage, which will take effect of July 16. The order will raise the minimum wage to P610 a day for non-agriculture workers. The increase is less than half of the P150 propose in the Senate. Minimum wage in the capital is now P140 below the P750 daily national minimum wage advocated by labour groups.

In June 2022, the government hiked the minimum wage in NCR by P33 (+6.1%). Prior to this, the minimum wage increase was last implemented in 2017 (+4.3%).

It is unclear at this stage if wages in other regions will also be hiked. In 2022, the government implemented wage hikes of +12%-14% throughout the country.

BSP creates overnight reference rate. BSP will peg overnight rate (ON) as the reference rate to replace the LIBOR expiring 30th June. This will serve as a reference rate for overnight borrowing and lending transactions between banks. The BSP is aiming to enhance transparency and efficiency in the financial market. According to the BSP, there is an urgency in having a credible yield curve with macro-financial decisions being based on these benchmarks.

The ON rate will be generated by converting the 28-day BSP bill rate to its ON equivalent, and a 56-day tenor will also be introduced.  Banks were required to submit quarterly reports of their remaining LIBOR-related exposures until the end of 2023 as part of the BSP transition plan.

Gov’t could boost infra spending to 12% of GDP. The country could boost infrastructure spending to as much as 12% of GDP as early as next year if the Maharlika Investment Fund (MIF) and PPP go as planned, according to Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno. Of this, 5-6% will come from the national budget and the rest will come from PPP and MIF.

The government is targeting to spend P1.29tn on infrastructure this year (+1% YoY), equivalent to 5.3% of GDP. From 2022-2028, infrastructure spending is expected to rise at a +10% CAGR, slower than 10-year historical CAGR of +18%.

 

Current account deficit mildly higher in 1Q23 due to wider trade deficit. The current account deficit was slightly higher at US$4.3bn in 1Q23 vs. US$4.0bn deficit in 1Q22. This is due to wider trade in goods deficit and lower net receipts in primary income account, offset by higher net receipts in services.

Trade in goods posted a wider deficit of US$17bn in 1Q23, up 8.2% YoY, due  to faster decline in exports (-10% YoY) vis-à-vis import drop (-0.5% YoY). Net receipts of trade in services rose by 40.9% YoY to US$4.5bn due to upsurge in receipts on travel services (US$2.2bn, up 617% YoY) and higher BPO export revenues of US$6.7bn (+8.3% YoY).

Primary income posted net receipts of US$815mn in 1Q23, down by 29% YoY. Secondary income account posted slightly higher (+0.05% YoY) net receipts of US$7.3bn in 1Q23, driven by the 3.2% YoY increase in remittances.

The BSP expects the current account deficit to narrow to US$15.1bn (3.4% of GDP) in 2023 from US$17.8bn in 2022.

BSP trims BOP forecasts. The Monetary Board lowered its BOP, current account deficit projections. This was after taking into account 1Q23 developments.

For 2023, the BOP deficit is expected to narrow to US$1.2bn (-0.3% of GDP), lower than the previous forecast of US$1.6bn (-0.4% of GDP) given a narrower trade gap.   Import growth is likely to slow down sharply following the pullback of commodity prices while export growth is likely to be weaker.

Tourist VAT refund seen adding up to P13bn to GDP. The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) expressed support to implement a VAT refund for tourists to attract more visitors and increase tourism receipts, according to Undersecretary Rosemarie Edillon during the Senate Committee on Ways and Means hearing.

Based on its study, the additional spending per annum is estimated to be from P6bn to P9bn starting 2024. In comparison, the status quo only yields revenues of P3.4bn to P5.1bn. Considering multiplier effect on other sectors, additional gross value added could be P8.6bn to P12.8bn per year.

Tax revenue loss is estimated at P4bn but the proposal only covers a refund of 85% of the VAT.

 

 

President Marcos, Jr. appoints Eli Remolona as next BSP Governor. President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. appointed Monetary Board Member (MBM) Eli M. Remolona as the next Governor of the BSP. He will replace incumbent BSP Governor Felipe Medalla, who was appointed last 30 June 2022. Mr. Remolona will be the 7th BSP Governor since the enactment of the New Central Bank Act in 1993.

Mr. Remolona is 70 years old and will serve a 6-year term starting July 3, 2023. He joined the central bank’s policymaking Monetary Board in August 2022. He has extensive international experience in central banking, having worked for 14 years at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and for 19 years at the Bank for International Settlements in Switzerland and Hong Kong. Before moving to the BSP, Remolona served as an independent director of the Bank of the Philippine Islands and chairman of its risk management committee.

Economic team to propose P5.768tn budget for 2024. The proposed 2024 national budget of P5.768tn is larger by 9.5% compared to this year’s budget (P5.268tn), according to the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC). Under the Constitution, the executive branch must submit the proposed national budget to Congress within 30 days after the President’s State of the Nation Address. Based on its plan, the government expects to trim the fiscal deficit to 4% of GDP by 2025 and 3% by 2028 from a peak of 8.6% in 2021.

‘Hot money’ exit continues in May. Foreign portfolio investments in the Philippines posted a net outflow of US$124.49mn in May, a 54% drop from the previous year. Gross inflows of hot money in May amounted to US$844.72mn (down 12.5% compared to US$965.62mn last year). Gross outflows decreased by 21.6% to US$969.21mn.

The decrease attributed to an improved economic landscape, the resolution of the US debt ceiling impasse, expectations of the Fed pausing its hiking cycle (at that time), and the slightly deprecating peso. Majority of investment went into PSE listed securities, particularly banks, food, beverage and tobacco, holding firms, property and transportation services.

Top investor countries were the UK, USA, Singapore, Luxembourg, and Hong Kong.

For 5M23 net inflow amounted to US$805mn, reversing the US$1.1bn net inflow recorded in the same period last year. The central bank expects foreign portfolio investments to end the year with a US$2.5bn net inflow.

 

DoF: Instant noodles to be exempted from "junk food" tax; seeks sugar liberalization in exchange for expanded SSS tax. Finance Sec. Diokno stated that instant noodles will not be subject to the proposed "junk food" tax despite high sodium content. A pack of noodles typically contains 1500mg to over 2000mg of sodium; recommended amount per day for a person is less than 2300mg. Emphasis was placed on instant noodles being for the poor and will not be covered by higher taxes, despite the higher sodium content of 60%.

DOF consulting with the National Nutrition Council and the DOH to determine the list of foods subject to the tax.  

Meanwhile, Sec. Diokno is also seeking to liberalize sugar imports to cushion the impact of the expanded sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) tax. This would not require the enactment of a measure into law; a directive from the President through the issuance of a Sugar Order would suffice.

Finance Secretary wants to ban POGO sector. Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno reiterated his view that the Philippines should “get rid of POGOs” . While the government would lose some revenue, he believes it would be better for the country in the long run.He opined that the presence of POGO firms might be the reason why the country remained on the Financial Action Task Force’s “gray list”.

At the end of 2019, POGOs occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-2Q23. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock.

House eyes tax hike on vehicle owners. The House of Representatives tax panel will consider proposed increases in the motor vehicle user’s charge (MVUC) before moving forward and increasing excise taxes on junk food and sweetened drinks.

MVUC, also known as road user’s tax, is paid upon registration and/annual renewal of registration of vehicles. The new rate could increase MVUC for passenger cars to P10,400 from P2,080, depending on the gross vehicle weight.

For utility vehicles, SUVs, buses, trucks, and trailers with a gross vehicle weight of 4,500 kg or more will be levied an MVUC of P1.0 or more per kg of gross vehicle weight. Tax hike will exempt motorcycles, which means that the proposal will only cover 39% of registered vehicles (i.e. cars, jeepneys, van, trucks).

Albay Rep. Jose Ma. Salceda, chair of the House committee on ways and means, said that he still has questions on the proposed taxes on junk food and sweetened drinks. The government estimates the proposed taxed on junk food and sweetened drinks could generate P76bn in addl revenues on its first year of implementation. Note that the government collected P17.8bn from MVUCs in 2022.

 

BAP launches USD-PHP cross currency swap market. The Bankers Association of the Philippines (BAP) launched a dollar-peso cross currency swap market to allow its members to manage foreign exchange and interest rate risks.

The USD-PHP cross currency swap involves a peso fixed rate and US$ floating swap with standard tenors of 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, and 10 years and will use the USD Secured Overnight Financing Rate as the floating rate benchmark. The swap market is supported by Bloomberg and will be traded in Bloomberg’s FIQ.

BSP unwinds relief measure for banks. Starting July 1st, big banks will no longer be allowed to use loans to micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and large corporations as an alternative compliance with reserve requirements (RR). The relief measure for smaller banks, however, is extended until the loans are fully paid, but not later than the end of December 2025. Past due or nonperforming loan, as well as new loans granted after the relief measures expires, will no longer be eligible as alternative compliance with the RR.

The BSP will begin unwinding the temporary relief measure to coincide with the reduction in reserve requirement ratios (RRR) by June 30, 2023. This aims to restore the use of the RR as a liquidity management tool and simplify banks’ compliance with reserve requirements.

BSP plans to cut the RRR for different types of banks, with reductions ranging from 100 to 250 basis points:

  • 250 bps to 9.5% for Universal and commercial banks and nonbank financial institutions with quasi-banking functions
  • 200 bps to 6% for digital banks
  • 100 bps for thrift banks, and rural and cooperative banks to 2% and 1% respectively

The central bank will conduct auctions of 56-day and 28-day securities on June 30 to manage excess liquidity in the financial system.

NPLs up 9bps MoM in April to 3.51%. Asset quality ratios deteriorated slightly in April. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) stood at 3.51%, blipping up 9bps MoM, and is now up 26bps ytd compared to 3.25% at end-2022. In PHP terms, NPLs jumped 3.1% MoM, the biggest monthly increase since May 2021. The Past Due (PD) ratio was up 5bps MoM to 4.12% (was 3.79% at end-2022), with the nominal amount of PD loans up 1.9% MoM. On the other hand, Restructured Loan (RL) ratio improved slightly to 2.59% from 2.60% in March and from 2.61% at end-2022. NPL cover dipped slightly to 103%, from 105% in March. Meanwhile, system loan growth slowed to 9.9% YoY in April, compared to 10.6% YoY in March, dipping back below 10% for the first time since August 2022. On a MoM basis, loan portfolios expanded 0.3%.

Digital payments surge 36% in May to new record highs. Online transactions done over the BSP’s National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped 35.9% YoY in May to P1,075bn (roughly $19bn), a new monthly record. For payments done via InstaPay – P50,000 or less – the value of transactions surged 48.0% YoY to P414bn. For larger transactions done over PesoNet, the total value was up 29.2% YoY to P616bn. Both were all-time highs. For year-to-May, total NRPS transactions hit P4,879bn, up 31.6% YoY, with InstaPay up 41.0% and PesoNet up 26.3%.

The InstaPay data is particularly interesting, in our view. Given the relatively low limit per transaction, it is likely more indicative of peer-to-peer or of retail transactions. Based on the BSP data, the average transaction value over InstaPay was P6,550 (under $120) in 5M22, with May alone at an even lower P6,286 (under $115). In terms of number of transactions, InstaPay was up an astounding 51.4% YoY in May.

This suggests continued rapid adoption of digital payments among Filipino individuals. Note, too, that this data only captures payments made between financial institutions. It does not take into account transactions done by parties within the same platform, ie BDO-to-BDO, or BPI-to-BPI, or GCash-to-GCash, etc. Thus, the BSP’s NRPS data likely vastly understates the actual usage of digital payment services.

Vehicle sales rebound strongly in May. CAMPI reported vehicle sales jumped by 45% YoY and 25% MoM to 38,177 units in May. Recall that April sales volumes were affected by public holidays. This brings YT-May sales to 166,104 units, up 32% YoY and on track to beat CAMPI’s target of 395k (+12%).

Toyota’s sales volumes rose 21% YoY, 27% MoM to 17,866 units, taking 5M23 sales to 77,194 units, up 18% YoY. To reach Toyota’s 188k FY23 target (+8% YoY), monthly sales should reach 15,800 units for the rest of the year.

Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV.

 

Consortium unveils proposal for multi-phased NAIA masterplan. The Manila international Airport Consortium (MIAC) unveiled its P267bn unsolicited proposal to rehab and develop NAIA over a 25-year concession period. The proposal involves a total capex of P267bn and total concession fee payments of P280bn (covers revenue sharing and taxes) over the life of the concession.

Capex of P267bn will be earmarked for a three-phase expansion:  

  • Upfront payment to the government of P57bn
  • Capex of P57bn for Phases 1&2: Increase terminal capacity to 54mn passengers per annum (MPPA) by 2025 and 62.5 MPPA by 2028 (expansion of terminal floor area, addition of airfield facilities, and cross-terminal transport)
  • Capex of P154bn for Phase 3: Increase capacity to 70 MPPA by 2048 (long-term expansion and development projects to further expand terminal space and airfield capacity)

MIAC includes six Filipino conglomerates + US-based Global Infrastructure Partners:

  • Aboitiz InfraCapital –AEV PM
  • AC Infrastructure – AC PM
  • Asia’s Emerging Dragon Corp. – LTG PM
  • Alliance Global – Infracorp Dev’t – AGI PM
  • Filinvest Development Corp. - FDC PM
  • JG Summit – JGS PM

Based on its 2019 annual report, NAIA recorded a net income of P5.0bn, operating profit of P6.8bn, and revenues of P15.2bn. Revenue breakdown is 36% Passenger service charge, 34% landing/parking fees, and 30%  rent income and others.

NAIA privatization doable by early 2024. The privatization of NAIA could kick off by 1Q24, According to Undersecretary for Aviation and Airports Roberto Lim. The winning bidder must commit to invest P141bn for the expansion and improvement of NAIA within a 15-year concession period. In addition, the private concessionaire will be asked to pay an upfront cost of P30bn and an annuity payment of P2bn. The government will also have a share in revenues from commercial and non-commercial operations.

Government approves new tariffs for LRT-1, LRT-2. The Department of Transportation (DoTr) announced fare hikes for LRT-1, LRT-2 beginning August 2. The minimum boarding fare will increase by 21% to P13.29 from P11. Fare per km will increase to P1.21 from P1.00. The approved increases are lower than the cumulative un-implemented tariff hike of +50% stipulated under the concession agreement.

Last April, President Marcos ordered the deferral of the fare hike implementation due to its economic impact on commuters. The last fare increase for LRT-1, LRT-2, MRT-3 occurred in Jan 2015, before LRMC took over operations on Sep 2015.

Light Rail Manila Corp (LRMC) holds the P65bn 32-year concession agreement to operate and extend the LRT-1 railroad. LRMC is owned by Metro Pacific (36%), Ayala Corp (35%), Sumitomo (19%), Macquarie Infra (10%).

Regis  valuation for 100% of LRMC’s equity value is negative P19.16bn (equivalent to an attributable value of -P0.24 per MPI share).

NEDA Board approves P23.4bn TPLEX extension project.  The board of the National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) has accepted San Miguel’s unsolicited proposal to extend the Tarlac-Pangasinan-La Union Expressway project. The P23.4bn project is the fastest unsolicited proposal approved by the body, from its submission last March 17. The project will extend the existing 89-km tollroad by another 59-km to connect Rosario, La Union to San Juan, La Union. The extension is expected to be fully completed by 2028. The next steps are to have negotiations with the proponent, a Swiss challenge, and implementation of the project.
