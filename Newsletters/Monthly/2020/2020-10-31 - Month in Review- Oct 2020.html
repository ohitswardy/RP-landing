REGIS REPORT

31 October 2020
 

The PSEI climbed 7.8% in October, snapping a three-month losing streak. The market closed at 6324, carried higher by reopening optimism and a turn in foreign flow during the month. The market climbed to as high as 10.7% MTD (to Oct 26). However,  rising global COVID infections and stricter lockdowns in Europe led to weak global markets, tempering the market’s gain towards the close of the month.

While Metro Manila remained in the same lockdown level (General Community Quarantine or GCQ) in October, easing measures have been constantly introduced. Age restrictions have been loosened, curfew hours shortened, and  transportation restrictions eased.  The ban on non-essential foreign travel is being lifted. Hotels are allowed to operate at 100% capacity and certain industries such as construction and restaurants have been allowed the same. The mayors of Metro Manila are recommending the extension of GCQ until at least the end of the year. Notwithstanding this, the introduction of more easing measures are expected moving ahead even if the lockdown level remains in place.

Gains were broad-based as value and cyclical plays moved up. LT Group surged, gaining close to 30% of its value in October. Other value plays – holdcos Metro Pacific, Alliance Global, GT Capital, and DMCI also advanced. Cyclicals led by BPI, SM Prime, Metrobank and Ayala Land also climbed. Prospects of reopening fueled Jollibee’s almost 17% rise.  Rotation out of defensives was evident, with retailers Puregold and Robinsons Retail ending with declines. Telcos Globe and PLDT were also among the handful of decliners. 

The Phils is about halfway through the 3Q earnings season but some trends have already become apparent. Revenue trends are already improving from 2Q to 3Q.  The absence of pantry stocking and government doleouts, however, have resulted in retailers seeing a sequential slowdown with flattish SSSG in 3Q. They now face the seasonally strong 4Q amidst the pandemic.  Mall foot traffic is still nowhere near normal, with SM Prime saying that it is still below 40% of pre-COVID level.  Residential revenue sales are beginning to bounce but focus now is on POGOs which have been under pressure and already contributing to office vacancies. For the banks, provisions (or lack thereof) continue to heavily influence profits. While results have yet to come out, school closures and the prevalence of work from home suggest continued strong growth in broadband for the telcos.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreign outflows persisted through October although at a much slower pace thanks to strong foreign demand in the Converge IPO and a short string of foreign buying late in the month.

October saw net foreign selling of US$156m, bringing YTD net outflows to US$2.173bn. Foreign outflows for the month though would have been larger at US$240m had it not been for US$83mn foreign inflow brought in by the Converge IPO.

The market posted its best month since March 2016 despite continued outflows. October also saw a short string of foreign buying late in the month, with net inflows registered for five consecutive days (a streak not seen since October 2019) heading into the fourth week.

Staples URC and PGOLD were net sold consistently throughout October. For PGOLD in particular, net outflows accelerated in mid-October following muted guidance on FY20 sales that was later revised upwards. A general shift into reopening/value/laggard plays out of outperformers  staples/telcos/defensives PGOLD TEL GLO is apparent though.  

Meanwhile, foreigners were seen taking advantage of the good liquidity in MPI to take profit as their buy-back program was seen aggressively participating in the market throughout the month. Foreign investors were also seen rotating out of telcos  ahead of the onverge IPO (listing October 26). Post listing, CNVRG only saw US$3.5m in  net outflows despite falling as much as 20% as the stabilization program was seen offsetting foreign agency selling (likely hedge funds flipping the deal).

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

With asset quality the main focus heading into the results season, banks'results were decidedly mixed.

With asset quality the main thing to watch – banks reported various  levels of deterioration.

BPI’s (+16.4% MoM) asset quality deteriorated sharply, with NPLs reaching 2.98% up 115bps from the previous quarter.  As a result, provisions stayed elevated in 3Q (P6.05bn), taking earnings for the quarter down 34% YoY. 

On the other end, BDO’s (+3.1% MoM) NPLs were at 1.97%, flat QoQ.  Provisions in 3Q came in at just P1.4bn after the P20bn provision expense in 2Q.  Net profit in 3Q reached a quarterly record of P12.3bn.  Deposit costs fell to 0.49% as well. 

Meanwhile,  Metrobank’s  (+6.7% MoM) NPL ratio climbed 69bps QoQ to 2.25%.  Provision expense for the quarter was P12.6bn, still up 4x YoY.  Metrobank’s NPL coverage was at 174% -- the highest among peers with BDO at 138% and BPI at 100%.  Net profit plunged 78% YoY for the quarter. Another data point suggesting the bank’s conservatism was the 13.2% YoY loan contraction. In contrast, BDO’s loan book grew 6% YoY while BPI’s was flat at 0.9% YoY. 

Jollibee (+17.2% MoM) led the consumer sector and was one of the top index gainers for October as the reopening theme played out. The company is increasing its investment in Titan Dining LP by another 25%, raising its stake to 85%. The total cash consideration was around SGD36m for the additional 25%. Titan is the holding company of the Tim Ho Wan brand of restaurants. JFC is currently the master franchise holder for the Tim Ho Wan brand in Shanghai and opened its first store in Sep 2020.

However, some rotation from the relatively more defensive names was apparent, with underperformance coming particularly from the retailers whose profits also came in short of expectations. 

Not entirely surprising, the absence of pantry stocking and the absence of government hand outs in 3Q resulted in softer topline growth. For 3Q20, Puregold’s (-15.8% MoM) net profit declined 4% YoY to P1.7bn. The core Puregold brand posted SSS decline of -4.5% in 3Q20. S&R also posted SSS decline in 3Q20 (-2.9%) mainly due to the high base in 3Q19 and the absence of its Member’s Treat sale this year. SSSG would have been positive otherwise. Management is guiding for FY20 top line growth of +4-5%. 

Robinsons Retail (-1.5% MoM) also fell behind expectations. For 3Q20 alone, net profit of P749m was down 30% YoY. Overall weak consumer spending and consumer behavior shifts led to the Supermarkets segment’s SSSG slowing to just +2% from the +16% clip in the first half.  All other formats continued to see SSS contraction in 3Q20, albeit at a slower rate of decline vs the hard lockdown period in 2Q20. Blended SSS was -12% in 3Q20. 

Meanwhile, the recent acquisition of Visayan drug store chain Rose Pharmacy (100% for P4.5bn) is likely to be margin dilutive in the near term, but provides a foothold in the VisMin regions.

Wilcon’s (-10.6% MoM) 3Q net profit was down 3% YoY on the back of lower interest income and higher effective tax rate.  Revenues were up 8% YoY, however. SSSG in 3Q20 was +0.4%, rebounding from the -48% clip in 2Q20 as store operations resumed after quarantine restrictions were lifted.

Universal Robina (+2.8% MoM) meanwhile managed to beat expectations, with its net profit in 3Q up 6% as EBIT margin expansion made up for the softness in topline across key categories.  Revenues for the Philippines branded segment declined 2% YoY.  

Meanwhile, continued strength of its international whisky segment boosted Emperador’s (+1.3% MoM) 3Q net profit.  Profit for the quarter jumped 26% YoY, beating expectations. 

 

Previously sold down conglomerates surged in October as the value theme played out. 

LT Group (+28.2% MoM) was the top index gainer during the month. LT Group’s 49.6%-owned tobacco affiliate, PMFTC, raised prices across the board by close to P20/pack. The price hike represents an increase of between 20% to 29% from the previous wholesale (truck) price. The magnitude of the price increase is well above Regis estimates (around +P8/pack) and also many times higher than the tax increase scheduled to take effect in Jan 2021 (+P5/pack + 12% VAT). 

Meanwhile, Philip Morris International (PM US) disclosed that cigarette volumes in the Philippines (PMFTC) came in at 11.7bn in 3Q20, down 6% YoY but higher by 21% from the low in 2Q. Volumes for the 9M20 were lower by 14% to 32.1bn sticks vs Regis FY20E of 45.7bn sticks (-8%). Tobacco accounts for over 70% of LTG’s earnings. 

MPI (+14.9% MoM) also gained. The company announced a P5bn share buyback program early in the month. 

Alliance Global (+13.4% MoM) also rose, as did JG Summit (+12.1% MoM) and GT Capital (+11.3% MoM).  GT Capital’s Toyota Motor Philippines modestly increased its FY20 sales forecast to 100k units (from 94-95k units previously).  Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV.

DCMI (+9.8% MoM) also climbed; large caps Ayala Corp (+10.7% MoM) and SM Investments (+8.0% MoM) also gained markedly.

 

Cyclical stocks made a move in October.   SM Prime (+14.6% MoM) rose significantly despite reporting a 52% YoY drop in 3Q20 net profit (to P4.0bn), dragged by a 72% YoY plunge in mall revenue. Mall foot traffic remained below 40% of pre-COVID levels. Nonetheless, residential reservation sales were resilient while China mall operations continued to recover.

Ayala Land (+11.1% MoM) likewise gained. AREIT is expanding its asset portfolio with the announced acquisition of The 30th, a mall-cum-office complex owned by ALI. AREIT (-1.0% MoM) will buy the asset for P5.1bn. Regis the purchase will likely result in a mild accretion to AREIT earnings and dividends.

Telcos generally underperformed along with with other defensive stocks. Fund raising in preparation for fixed broadband service provider Converge’s  (-12.0% MTD) listing during the month likely contributed to the incumbents’ underperformance. 

Both PLDT (-0.8% MoM) and Globe (-2.4% MoM) ended the month with declines. 

 

 

First Gen (+22.1% MoM) was one of the top index gainers in October. The stock surged after the Department of Energy announced Renewable Energy initiatives, which included a moratorium on endorsements for greenfield coal power plants.

First Gen disclosed that it signed a joint cooperation agreement with Tokyo Gas Co., Ltd representing the next phase of their joint development of FGEN LNG Corporation’s interim offshore LNG terminal project. FGEN and Tokyo Gas initially signed a joint development agreement in December 2018 and under the latest agreement, Tokyo Gas will have a 20% participating interest in the project.

The DOE’s moratorium on greenfield coal plants also muted Semirara’s (+6.2% MoM) gains earlier in the month as 80% of its profits is derived from supplying coal.  Meanwhile, SCC’s 3Q20 core profit dropped 39% QoQ and 55% YoY after coal mining exports fell on the back of China’s import quota. The power unit’s recovery in 3Q was slower than forecast after a surprise reduction in power supply contracts and unexpected plant outages.

Meralco’s (+10.7% MoM) 3Q income was down 16% YoY to P5.1bn as electricity volumes remained weak (-6% YoY). 9M core income came in at P15.7bn, down 15%.

Cement companies also reported results. Cemex PH (-4.2% MoM) announced a surprising profit rebound in 3Q. The outperformance largely came from lower costs, offsetting soft topline and higher-than- expected financial expenses. Cement sales volumes rebounded after lockdown and was only 3% lower YoY in 3Q20. Net sales for the quarter declined 6% YoY. 

Also buoyed by lower costs, Holcim Philippines (+1.4% MoM) recorded a profit of P613m in 3Q20, up 34% YoY and reversing the losses in 2Q20.  3Q20 revenues dropped 11% YoY, as ASPs fell 8% YoY and sales volumes declined by 3% YoY.  

 

Transport stocks closed up in October. 

Cebu Air (+2.8% MoM) gained for a second month after falling 60% in the first eight months of the year. 

Cebu Air disclosed its plans to raise up to US$500m through a combination of a rights issue of US$250m worth of convertible preferred shares and a private placement of US$250m worth of convertible bonds. The conversion price is expected to be the same for the two instruments and will be set within the range of P38-45/sh, which represents a premium of 2% to 21% over CEB’s 30-day VWAP (August 26, 2020 to October 7, 2020).  The US$500m amount to be raised will likely sustain operations for more than 12 months, after considering its monthly cash burn of US$35-40m. 

ICTSI (+4.8% MoM) also rose, buoyed by the recovery in global trade. 

The Philippine Ports Authority is proposing to impose tariffs on yard services (a.k.a. arrastre) for empty containers and all services for OOG (Out of Guage) shipments at the ports of Manila and Batangas. The proposed rates for empty containers will be similar for the full/laden boxes while OOG shipments will be charged three times more than the basic rate of containers. However, ICTSI management believes these proposed rates may still be negotiated downwards over the next couple of months. Regis estimates around 25-30% of MICT’s container volumes are empties. Assuming the rates are only at a 20% discount to full containers, charging for the empty boxes alone could potentially increase MICT’s revenues by 6% annually and c. 2% to ICT’s group-wide revenues (and an estimated maximum of 10% to profits). No timetable had been given for the tariff imposition. 

 

 
 
 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases to 2.3% in September.  Headline inflation further eased to 2.3% in September from 2.4% in August. This is within BSP’s expected range of 1.8-2.6%. This was due to a deceleration in food (i.e. meat, milk, cheese, eggs, and vegetables) and alcoholic & tobacco products and offset by higher inflation for transport services and education. Core inflation rose to 3.2% in September from 3.1% in August which brings YT-Sep inflation to 2.5%, within government’s target range of 2-4%.

BSP keeps interest rates steady.  The Monetary Board kept the overnight reverse repurchase rate steady at a record low of 2.25%, in line with street expectattions. BSP lowered ave inflation rate forecasts to 2.3% for FY20E (vs 2.6% previously), 2.8% for FY21E (vs 3%), and 3% for FY22E (vs. 3.1%).

Budget deficit narrows to P138.5bn in September on lower expenditures. The National Government recorded a budget deficit of P138.5bn in September, 22% lower than the same period last year. Revenues contracted by 10% YoY, with BIR collections dropping 6.6% YoY and BOC collections dropping 13.7% YoY. Meanwhile, expenditures declined by 15% YoY due to the timing of subsidy releases and high base effects from higher infra spend last year. The full impact of Bayanihan II (P166bn), which was approved last Sept 11, is expected to be felt in succeeding periods. This brings YT-September budget deficit to P879.2bn, up 194% YoY and 32% below the revised program of P1.298tn for the same period. The gov’t is targeting a budget deficit of P1.815tn in FY20. So far, revenue collections for 9M20 are down 7.9% YoY to P2.14tn, tracking ahead of FY20 target. Meanwhile, expenditures for 9M20 reached P3.0tn, up 15% YoY and accounting for 70% of FY20 target.

Finance chief sees worse GDP slump. GDP is expected to shrink by 6% this year according to Department of Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III. This compares with the government’s July projection of 5.5%. The govt cited the reimposition of the modified enhanced community quarantine (MECQ) in Metro Manila and some areas of Calabarzon in August.

OFW remittances down 4.1% YoY, 10.8% MoM to US$2.48bn in August. Remittances in August stood at US$2.48bn, down 4.1% YoY and 10.8% MoM. The remittances from Asia and the Middle East dropped by 5% and 28%, respectively. Meanwhile, sea-based remittances continued to drop, albeit at a slower rate of -2%. In PHP terms, remittances dropped 10% YoY and 12% MoM in August. The 3-month moving average growth rate at +3.6% in August. This brings the cumulative 8M20 remittances to US$19.3bn, down 2.6% YoY (in PHP terms, down 6% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 5% in FY20 (FY19A: US$30bn).

Infra spend drops further in August. Infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) dropped further by 25% YoY, 15% MoM in August to P44.3bn. This brings YT-August infra spend down 11.5% to P394.5bn. Last July, the government lowered its infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. For FY21, the infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) is supposed to increase by 43% YoY to P1.12tn. This target is c. 7% higher than what was spent in 2019 for infrastructure (P1.05tn).

BOP surplus swells to US$2.1bn in September. Balance of Payments (BoP) surplus stood at US$2.104bn in September, ballooning from the US$38mn surplus in Sep 2019. This is also higher than the US$657mn surplus in August 2020 and the highest since May 2020’s US$2.431bn. The govt cited inflows from the BSP’s forex operations and income from investments abroad and the National Government’s (NG) foreign currency deposits. The GIR level stood at US$100bn in September, equivalent to 10 months’ worth of imports. This brings YT-September BoP surplus to US$6.88bn, up 24% YoY, supported mainly by higher net foreign borrowings by the NG and lower merchandise trade deficit. The NG foreign borrowings surged 85% YoY to P509.69bn in YT-August. Earlier this month, the BSP revised its FY20 BoP projection to US$8.1bn (vs previous US$600mn).

GIR climbs to US$100bn in September. Based on preliminary data, gross international reserves (GIR) level rose by US$1.54bn (+1.5% MoM) to US$100.49bn in end-September 2020. The GIR equivalent to 10 months’ worth of imports.

 

 

 

FDI climbs to 7-month high in July. Foreign direct investments net inflows jumped by 35% YoY, 66% MoM to US$797mn. This brings 7M20 FDI net inflows to US$3.795bn, down 11% YoY. The BSP expects FDI inflows to reach US$4.1bn this year, less than half of the US$8.8bn initial target before the pandemic.

Gov’t seeks P540B advance from BSP. The National Government is asking the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) for P540bn in additional short-term financial assistance. The funds will be settled on or before Dec 29 at zero interest. The debt plan comes immediately after the Bureau of the Treasury repaid the P300bn it borrowed from the BSP in March through a repurchase agreement of government securities.

BSP’s liquidity support to economy hits US$39.2bn. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has provided about 1.9tn pesos (US$39.2bn), equivalent to 9.6% of GDP, in liquidity to the financial system according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. Amount includes P540bn in advances by national government targeted for release in first week of Oct. The BSP working with government to ensure the pandemic “will leave little permanent scar on the Philippine economy and its people”.

House transmits P4.5-trillion 2021 budget bill to Senate. The House of Representatives transmitted to the Senate its final version of the P4.5tn 2021 General Appropriations Bill (GAB). The chamber was originally set to transmit the budget bill to the Senate on Oct. 28. The early transmittal of the will allow Congress to work on its passage, in time for its signing before Dec. 25.

SEC wants cap on salary loan interest. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) wants to put a limit on interest and penalty charges that lending companies charge for salary and payday loans. The SEC has been lobbying for the payday loan limit since October last year in a bid to stop “predatory” lending practices. “We are hopeful that the Monetary Board will likewise consider soon the Commission’s proposal for similar interest rates, fees and other charges imposed by lending and financing companies on consumer and payday loans”, said SEC Chairperson Emilio Aquino.  He cited the recent cap on credit card charges as timely and much-needed to promote responsible lending.

Bank reserve rules eased anew. Under BSP Circular 1100, banks and financial institutions could use loans extended to business enterprises hardest hit by the pandemic as part of their compliance with the reserve requirements. The loans extended by banks will be to large enterprises that record a 25% drop in gross receipts for one calendar quarter. Critically impacted business shall include the transport industry, tourism industry and export-import industry. Under the previous guidelines, loans to enterprises that experienced at least a 50 percent decline in gross receipts could be used as alternative mode of compliance to the required reserves. Banks may continue to utilize past due or non-performing loans to MSMEs and large enterprises as alternative compliance with the reserve requirements for an additional 30 days from the date the obligations become past due or non-performing, whichever comes first.

Loan growth eases to 4.7% in August; M3 growth mildly slower at 14.2% in August. Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, eased to 4.7% in August from 6.7% in July. The loans to manufacturing and trade declined further in August and this is the 5th straight month of weak loan demand. Meanwhile, money supply grew by 14.2% in August, mildly slower than the 14.7% growth in July.

 

SWS – Joblessness still at 40% in September. A Social Weather Stations report showed that adult joblessness remained extremely high at 39.5% in September, down from the 45.5% level in July. This runs counter to the report of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) which said that unemployment improved sharply from 17.7% in April to 10.0% in July. Even if we include under-employment, the combined PSA figure dropped to 27.3% in July, from 36.6% in April. There’s a world of difference between the two sets of data, and what they might mean for a recovery in GDP and indeed for corporate earnings.\

Car sales rise 37% MoM in September. Industry car sales reached 24,523 units in September, down 23% YoY but up 37% MoM. According to the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines (CAMPI) and the Truck Manufacturers Assoc. This brings 9M20 industry car sales down 45% YoY to 148,012 units. Meanwhile, Toyota sold 10,657 units, -21% YoY but +32% MoM and brings thier 9M20 sales to 63,182 units, down 45% YoY. Our forecasts assume FY20 car sales will drop by 44% to 90,372 units. Toyota accounts for 23% of GTCAP’s FY20 profit estimate and 30% of NAV.

Imported vehicle sales drop 46% in 9 months. The Association of Vehicle Importers and Distributors (AVID) reported that its members sold 34,977 units in 9M20, down 45.6% YoY. Combining the data of AVID and CAMPI (adj. for brands present in both), 9M20 industry-wide vehicle sales fell by 46% YoY to 161,212 units. This implies that in September alone, industry-wide car sales dropped 24% YoY but was up +34% MoM to 26,595 units. Toyota’s market share in 9M20 stood at 39%, slightly lower than end-2019 (40%).

GCQ in Metro Manila eyed until year-end. The mayors of Metro Manila want to remain under general community quarantine up to the end of the year according to Presidential spokesman Harry Roque. Protocols on economic activity can be relaxed without downgrading the quarantine classification. It is assumed that President Duterte is expected to make a decision by month-end. Metro Manila mayors have recommended the following to the Inter-Agency Task Force: (1) Broaden the age group allowed to go out of their houses to 15-18 to 65 years old (vs. 21-59 years old currently) and (2) Shorten curfew hours. The Department of Trade and Industry has also asked companies to adopt staggered work hours to decongest the still-limited public transportation. The gov’t has also allowed mall-wide sales in GCQ and MGCQ areas.

Transport rules further eased: Riders allowed one seat apart. The government has approved a “one-seat-apart” rule on mass transport during a Cabinet meeting. The cabinet also approved to gradually increase the capacity to allow “sitting together” but with plastic barriers, or ultraviolet light disinfection. The gov’t had previously planned to reduce the distance to 0.75m from 1m today but objections from some Cabinet officials and health experts rolled back the plan. Meanwhile, the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board will release a memorandum circular to define the rule ′′ one-seat apart ′′ and steps that can be taken to increase capacity.

 

 

 

IATF backs shorter curfew hours for Metro Manila; Oks easing outbound travel limits. The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) for the Management of Emerging Infectious Diseases (IATF) is in favor of easing curfew hours in Metro Manila according to IATF vice chairman and Cabinet secretary Karlo Nograles. It is also eyeing to implement staggered work shifts to prevent crowding in offices and establishments. But staggered work shifts could not be implemented if curfew hours start early. The malls and groceries should be allowed to operate for longer hours. The IATF agreed in principle to relax the outbound travel restrictions on Filipinos who want to be with their foreigner partners and are currently finalizing the procedure. The IATF also must coordinate with accepting countries, airline companies and various agencies to ensure that the relaxed travel protocols are implemented smoothly.

Gov’t to lift ban on non-essential foreign travel on Oct 21; All rail lines hike capacity. The government is lifting the ban on non-essential travel of Filipinos by Oct 21, subject to strict requirements. This means that Filipino travelers must have a confirmed round trip ticket and travel health insurance. They also must have negative antigen test result taken within 24 hours before departure and a swab test and quarantine upon return. The government is also allowing travel agencies and tour operators to reopen even in Metro Manila and other areas under general community quarantine effective October 16. The IATF lifted a standing ban for people traveling from a GCQ to an MGCQ area even if not for official or work purposes. Meanwhile, all of Metro Manila’s rail lines will increase capacity up to 30% with capacity gradually expanded to 50%. Rail lines have been operating at 13-18% of capacity since June.

Hotels allowed to operate at 100%. Tourism Secretary Bernadette Puyat announced that all hotels in areas classified under general community quarantine (GCQ) and modified GCQ may now be allowed to operate at full capacity. The decision to open at 100% operational capacity would be subject to hotel management’s compliance with safety guidelines.
