REGIS REPORT

08 July 2020
Philippines: The month in review
 

The PSEi clambered up 7. 1% in April, after plummeting over 20% in March. The steep 31.9% market plunge as of 1Q20 attracted bargain hunting. Despite the rebound in April, the market continued to be fraught with uncertainty, even as the world dealt with the unprecedented impact of the pandemic. Oil prices plunged, with WTI dropping below zero during the month.

On the ground, the ECQ (enhanced community quarantine) was extended to April 30 and then again to May 15, in a number of areas including Metro Manila, with mass testing yet to commence. The lockdown will ease in other lower-risk areas. Official COVID-19 cases reached 8,488 as of month end (+276 vs previous day).

Congress is getting ready to pass the Philippine Economic Recovery Act (PERA). PERA proposes a P613bn stimulus package, partly in the form of wage subsidies (P110bn) but mostly in interest-free loans. Also within the PERA bill is a proposal to create National Emergency Investment Corp. to inject money into companies that will fall into distress in this environment.

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas issued a surprise “offcycle” 50bps cut in the policy rate, from 3.25% to 2.75% in order to stimulate the economy amidst the outbreak. Policy rates have now been cut a total of 125bps so far this year. The Monetary Board has also given the BSP authority to bring the RRR down by another 200bps to 10%, after the 200bp cut to 12% effective end-March.

Indeed, the BSP has been busy providing banks relief and encouraging lending, particularly to the MSMES. The BSP reduced the risk weight of MSME loans to 50% from 75% and let new MSME loans qualify as part of reserve requirements. In March, the BSP issued a Memorandum allowing banks to exclude COVID-related loans from its computation of past due. Among the many other regulatory easing measures, it also allowed banks to stagger credit losses over five years and allowed them to reclassify their treasury assets as HTC, retroactively pricing the assets to any date between 1 March and 30 April.

Banks just might need all the encouragement, with asset quality deterioration a major concern amidst the pandemic. The latest string of 1Q results reveal (most) banks pre-empting the likely impact of COVID-19 on asset quality, in the form of massive increases in provisioning. Concern has been evidenced in foreign flows as well — with the top three largest net outflows in April seen in the big three banks. Net outflows in the three names reached a total of US$94m.

The market overall saw net outflows, nonetheless. Net outflows in April reached US$325m — marking the fifth straight month of outflows for the Philippines. YTD, some US$971m has already left the country. Excluding SM group transfers for the period, net outflows are already at US$1.186bn.

On the contrary, Jollibee saw the top net inflows, with US$11m flowing into the stock. Jollibee was also the top index gainer for the month, jumping 35%. Other consumer names, viewed as relatively more defensive in the current environment, outperformed. Gains were led by Puregold, Universal Robina, and Robinsons Retail. Rebounds were seen in Ayala Corp, and ICTSI. While other defensive names First Gen and the telcos Globe and PLDT led as well.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Asset quality deterioration as an impact of COVID-19 has become the major theme for the banks, causing them to underperform in April.

In particular, NPL risk coming from SMEs is of chief concern.To incentivize banks to lend to SMEs, the central bank has reduced the risk weights for loans to them to 50% from 75%. The BSP also recently allowed new MSME loans to qualifyas reserve requirement (RRR). Depending on the length and severity of the economic disruption, Bank of the Philippine Islands (-5.3% MoM) estimates its NPL ratio could jump to4% (from 1.66% at end 2019).

Already, BPI has taken some provisions upfront. In its 1Q earnings release, provisions rose 135% YoY to P4.23bn.This is the highest quarterly provision expense the bankhas booked, more than double the previous high of P2bnat end-2018.

Loan growth slowed to 7.3% from 8.9% in 4Q19 and 11.5%at 1Q19. NPLs were up slightly from 1.66% to 1.82%.

NPAT for 1Q20 was P6.39bn down 5% YoY.

Security Bank (-3.4% MoM) took the opportunity given by strong revenues to put through a massive provision of P5.7bn, as a preemptive measure anticipating potential impact of COVID-19 on asset quality. The provision for the quarter is

higher than what SECB provided in FY19 (P4.2bn) and much larger than what the big three banks provided in 1Q.

Despite the massive provision, 1Q20 NPAT expanded an impressive 21% to P2.8bn. Loans grew 14% YoY, with consumer loans up 44%, and now account for 29% of total loans. NIMs expanded 129bps YoY, driven both by the loanmix and a drop in deposit costs. Trading gains were massiveat P3.5bn more than 5x that of 1Q19.

Metrobank (-2.4% MoM) reported NPAT in 1Q20 of P6.1bn, down 9% YoY. Operations were strong, with PPOP up 18% YoY driven by net interest income. However, the bank doubled provision expense to P5bn, pre-empting the likely increase in borrower stress from COVID. The provision was done despite the bank not having massive treasury gains. Loan growth slowed to 5.5% YoY, the slowest pace since 1Q10.

Unlike the others banks, BDO (-2.4% MoM) did not seem to provide aggressively yet for COVID-19 related losses in 1Q.

Provision expense was up 80% YoY to P2.3bn, with estimated annualized credit cost at c40bps. NPAT in 1Q20 was P8.8bn, down 10% YoY and 27% QoQ. The result was behind Regis and consensus forecasts. The main drag was large (unspecified) mark-tomarket losses at BDO Life's equity investment portfolio. Loans grew 11% YoY.

 
CONSUMER STOCKS

Consumer stocks populated the outperfomer list in April, given the defensive nature of their businesses as well as their ability to post a relatively quicker recovery post the crisis.

Jollibee (+34.5% MoM) was the top index gainer of the month, staging a rebound after halving in the first three months of the year (-50.7% YT-end March). Jollibee saw strong foreign buying interest, with US$11m in net inflows -- the highest level of the month.

While Jollibee’s delivery sales in the Philippines are growing 50% vs the early part of 2020, these will not be enough to make up for lost dine-in revenue. The company also announced that it has cut its semiannual dividend by 50% to P0.62/sh (Regis FY20E: P2.22/sh). The 2020 capex has also been reduced to P5bn from P14bn given operational constraints and uncertainty of demand.

Meanwhile, Shakey's Pizza (+37.5% MoM) also rebounded. The company initially closed all Metro Manila stores immediately after the ECQ was imposed in mid-March, but has since started to reopen stores in phases, with service limited to delivery and carry-out. Sales at the store level have been encouraging thus far, with a surge in delivery sales offsetting the foregone dine-in sales (typically 75% of store sales).

Retailers also led. Puregold (+27.0% MoM) saw strong sales in early March as a result of pantry-stocking by consumers ahead of the ECQ. Sales, however, have moderated since given the restrictions imposed under the ECQ, but remain higher by 8-10% YoY. For S&R, SSSG for the first quarter tracked mid-single digit.

Robinsons Retail’s (+17.5% MoM) 1Q20 net profit jumped 45% YoY to P923m, while core earnings rose 33% YoY to P784m.

EBIT grew 17% YoY to P1.7bn, tracking broadly in line with our FY20E.

As of the end of 1Q20, only RRHI’s “essential” retail formats (77% of 1Q20 sales) were operational. The Supermarket segment (61% of sales) SSSG was +19%, driven by a 26% growth in basket size amidst panic buying and pantry stocking ahead of the lockdown in mid-March. The rest of RRHI’s retail formats (Department Stores, DIY Stores, and Specialty Stores) posted negative SSS given the temporary closures due to the lockdown.

Given the challenges, management scaled down its guidance for consolidated SSSG to 0-3% for FY20 from +3-5% previously (Regis estimate: +5.6%).

Headline 1Q profit for Universal Robina (+21.1% MoM) was down 5% to P2bn, tracking behind Regis' FY20E as the FX losses dragged. Underlying EBIT was otherwise flat YoY, despite the international business suffering from COVID-19 and the associated lockdowns.

Philippines BCF sales grew 3% YoY in 1Q20. While pantry stocking boosted “essential” categories, such as instant noodles (+28%) and biscuits, the growth was less apparent for instant coffee (down mildly) given base effects from the relaunch of Great Taste in 1Q19. The PH BCF segment saw healthy EBIT margin expansion (+125bps YoY) on favorable raw material cost.

Management noted that top line growth trends have held up in April, though overall profit performance is likely to be mixed given the supply chain constraints during the lockdown.

 
TELCOS

Telcos continued to be strong as the sector is expected to show more resilience than most under the current environment. The lockdown is seen to spur accelerated adoption of digital use leading to higher data usage. PLDT (+15.1% MoM) gained, taking its YTD rise to 31.5%. PLDT says 1Q20 numbers were looking strong, with momentum carried from 4Q19.

Prepaid top-ups, however, began to “moderate” by the third week of the ECQ (end of March), as retailers’ credit balances dropped to nil and daily wage earners lost their incomes. Over 70% of PLDT’s mobile revenue is still from prepaid subscribers. Globe (+13.8% MoM) rose as well.

 
CONGLOMERATES

Market proxies helped drive the market higher.

Laggard Ayala Corp (+24.5% MoM) was among the top gainers. The stock had been down 40.5% as of 1Q20.

Ayala Corp disclosed it will consolidate its energy, water, and infrastructure businesses under AC Energy Inc and rename the new entity to “AC Energy and Infrastructure Corporation” or ACEIC. The integrated holding company will house AC’s holdings in AC Energy Philippines Inc. (ACEPH PM), Manila Water (MWC PM), and unlisted AC Infrastructure Holdings Corp.

GT Capital (+11.3% MoM) also gained on bargain hunting. Unit sales for GT Capital’s Toyota were down 65% in March, and down 23% in 1Q20.

Toyota Motor Philippines’ 71-dealer network has been shut since the start of the lockdown in mid-March but management expects 17

dealerships (largely in Visayas and Mindanao) to re-open by May 2 under more relaxed measures.

Other conglos posted gains, with Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+10.1% MoM) and DMCI Holdings (+9.3% MoM) rising markedly.

Metro Pacific (+6.2% MoM) and SM Investments (+3.3% MoM) also gained. SM Retail profit is seeing strong sales driven by a jump in basket sizes.

LT Group (-6.6% MoM) declined however. Cigarette volumes for LT Group associate PMFTC dropped 9% in 1Q20. The contraction comes as no surprise given the hefty price increases implemented in the latter of 2019. Earnings for 1Q20 are still likely to be robust given that PMTFC is still working off low-tax inventory.

Alliance Global (-7.4% MoM) was also weak.

 
UTILITIES

The defensive power sector also gained.

First Gen (+15.2% MoM) led the gainers.

Meralco (+13.1% MoM) also posted a significant rise. 1Q electricity volumes grew 4.8% YoY despite the lockdown implemented beginning March 17. This was driven by residential (+11.8% YoY) and commercial (+5.3% YoY) demand. Industrial demand fell 2.4% YoY due to disruptions caused by the Taal volcano eruption in January as well as the ECQ.

Upon implementation of the ECQ, company management cited volumes fell as much as 30% YoY as commercial and industrial customers remain closed.

Aboitiz Power (+4.2% MoM) meanwhile reported a 1Q20 core income (excluding non-recurring gains/losses) decline of 49% YoY to P2.1bn. Headline profit fell 43% YoY to P2.1bn trending behind expectations. EBITDA for the generation and retail electricity supply (RES) business was dragged by outages at Therma South and GN Power Mariveles as well as lower selling prices.

 
GAMING

Bloomberry (-6.7% MoM) dropped, as its Solaire casino remains shut under the lockdown. Bloomberry Resorts announced that it intends to make additional future investments in the interest of enhancing shareholder value.

It clarified, however, that it does not have any imminent investment/acquisition considerations.

 
PROPERTY

The property sector saw some rebounds in varying degrees despite the sector seeing close to no revenues during the lockdown.

SM Prime (+9.15% MoM) led the sector. SM Prime has waived rent for all mall tenants nationwide during the enhanced community quarantine, including tenants that are currently operating.

SMPH expects a gradual reopening of mall tenants after the quarantine is lifted, similar to its experience in China.

Robinsons Land (+1.3% MoM) announced that malls in areas under general community quarantine (a lower level than enhanced community quarantine) will open by May 1. Only non-leisure tenants will be allowed to operate in these areas.

Robinsons Land recently received the sales permit to launch succeeding phases of its Chengdu residential project. Within a few days of launch, RLC sold 76% of 564 condominium units and 73% of 64 duplex villas. Regis estimates that the total sales value of the two new phases would be close to P10bn, implying that the company sold over P7bn worth of projects in a few days. In 2019, RLC’s total Philippine reservation sales amounted to P20bn Ayala Land (+5.8% MoM) likewise rose. The company will not launch new residential projects this year since it had 18 months’ worth of unsold inventory as of end-2019.

Ayala Land estimates that it has foregone rental revenue of P2.6bn from mall tenants which are not operating (for 1.5 months) which is equivalent to 12% of 2019 mall revenue.

 
TRANSPORT

ICTSI (+19.95% MoM) surged after declining 41.9% in 1Q20.

Volume growth has decelerated since March and will likely weaken further in succeeding months. While shipments related to discretionary spending have fallen, those related to staples have been steady.

Despite the negative carry, management has pre-emptively drawn down some US$400m worth of short-term bank loans (1-2 years maturity) even before lockdowns were executed.

Meanwhile, Cebu Air (+6.7% MoM) reported a net loss of P1.18bn in 1Q20. Revenues dropped 25% YoY, on a 16.5% drop in pax vols to 4.4m.

EBITDAR fell 53% YoY to P3.54bn. Despite a 23% YoY drop in jet kero prices and lower fuel consumption (due to fewer flights), cash costs dropped byjust 6% YoY as repairs/maintenance costs rose 14%.

 

Key Macro and Political Developments

Inflation mildly dips to 2.5% in March. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported CPI inflation was at 2.5% in March, slightly lower than 2.6% in Feb. This brings 1Q20 average inflation to 2.7%. Prices for food & non-alcoholic beverages picked up 2.6% in Mar (vs 2.1% in Feb) largely due to fish (+10.5% in Mar vs +8.6% in Feb) and fruit (+9.9% vs 8.5%). Inflation was tempered with lower prices for transport (-1.8% vs +1.8%) and electricity, gas and other fuels (-3.0% vs -1.1%) as global oil prices fell. Meanwhile, Core inflation mildly dipped to 3.0% in Mar from 3.2% in Feb. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said Apr inflation will likely be even lower due to the collapse of oil prices, price freeze on basic goods and near zero increase in utilities.

BSP cuts policy rate by 50bps, approves package for MSME support. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board decided to cut policy rates by 50bps to 2.75% effective today (Apr 17) due to the COVID-19 pandemic’s effects on the economy. This decision substitutes for the scheduled monetary policy meeting on May 21. According to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno, “the off-cycle cut is meant to strongly encourage lending to various sectors, especially the most vulnerable, amid the COVID-19 pandemic”. In addition, Monetary Board approved measures to reduce the financial burden on loans to MSMEs such as counting such loans as part of the banks’ reserve requirements.

BSP eyes more cuts to stimulate economy. In a message to reporters, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Benjamin Diokno said the BSP is pursuing deeper cuts in policy rates and reserve requirements for a “soft landing” for the economy amidst the outbreak. According to Diokno, the Monetary Board has authorized him to cut the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) by an additional 200bps after the 200bps cut recently to 12%. Recall, Diokno said they were also looking at deeper cuts in the policy rate after cutting it by 150bps to 3.25% since last year. Reverting to where the country was in 2018 (policy rate at 3.0%) is no longer an appropriate policy goal according to Diokno. On top of this, the BSP has allowed P300bn worth of term deposits to mature from Mar 11 till last week.

BSP cuts OFW remittance forecast to 2%. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut its OFW remittances growth forecast in FY20 to 2% from 3% previously due to the COVID-19 outbreak. In 2019, OFW remittances rose 4.1% YoY to US$30.13bn which exceeded BSP’s 3% growth forecast.

Trade deficit falls 39% YoY to US$1.66bn in Feb. Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported trade deficit fell to US$1.66bn (-39% YoY) as imports fell 12% while exports were up 3% YoY. Imports fell 12% YoY to US$7.06bn as all categories fell YoY such as capital goods (-16%), raw materials (-9%), fuel (-12%) consumer (-9%). Meanwhile, Exports picked up 3% YoY on both electronics (+3%) and non-electronics (+2%) export growth.

 

Overall BOP surplus at US$839m in February. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) reported overall balance of payments (BOP) position at US$839m surplus in February. The BSP attributed the surplus to “inflows arising from the National Government’s foreign currency deposits with the BSP, and BSP’s foreign exchange operations as well as income from its investments abroad”. This brings YTD BOP position to US$516m deficit. The BSP attributed the deficit “partly to merchandise trade deficit and net outflows of foreign portfolio investments”. The Gross int’l reserves (GIR) rose by $1.3bn MoM to US$88.2bn in Feb.

Budget deficit narrows to P37.6bn in February. Budget deficit narrowed to P37.6bn in Feb (-51% YoY) as expenditures fell 12% YoY to P244.4bn while revenues rose only 2% YoY to P206.8bn. The gov’t described Feb 2019 as a high base due to the adjustment of the Internal Revenue Allotment (IRA) for local gov’t following a Supreme Court ruling. Excluding this, disbursements would have been up 6% in Feb. This brings 2M19 budget deficit to P14.6bn (-54% YoY).

BSP sees further room to cut rates, reports 2019 CAD at US$464m. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Benjamin Diokno said there is “a lot of room for cutting interest rates and also a lot of room in cutting the reserve requirement”. He added that these decisions will be dependent on what the actual inflation is as well as their outlook for the next 3 months. BSP has cut the policy rate by 75bps to 3.25% and cut the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) of the banks by 200bps to 12% this year. The BSP also reported the FY19 current account deficit (CAD) was at US$464m. In 4Q alone, the current account surplus was at US$748m, a reversal from US$2.70bn deficit the prior year. This was largely driven by a smaller goods trade deficit at US$11.37bn in 4Q19 vs US$14.16bn in 4Q18. “There’s a real risk there will be a technical recession… I think Q2 and Q3 we might have negative growth.”

Domestic liquidity growth eases in February. Domestic liquidity (M3) reached P12.76trn in February (+10.9% YoY, -0.3% MoM). M3 growth slowed from 12.0% YoY (revised from 11.9% YoY) growth in January.

Bank lending picks up to 12.2% in February. Loans outstanding of universal and commercial banks rose 12.2% YoY to P9.20trn (ex-RRPs) in Feb, mildly accelerating from the YoY growth in Jan (+11.7% YoY). Loan growth picked up for electricity, gas, steam (+9.7% in Feb vs +8.2% in Jan), trade (+1.5% vs +0.8%) and finance (+19.0% vs +16.3%). Based on total banking industry figures as of 4Q19, credit cards were up 28% YoY and motor vehicles +12% (cars +11%, motorcycles +34%). The discrepancies vs universal and commercial banks loans data due to the merger of Metrobank Cards into Metrobank parent (for cards) and merger of RCBC Savings into RCBC parent (for motor vehicles).

Finance Secretary eyes wage subsidies for MSMEs. In an interview, Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III said the gov’t is looking at loan guarantees for MSMEs while their workers could get wage subsidies. Additionally, it is unlikely for the middle class to receive a similar cash aid given to poor families. Dominguez clarified that measures such as the 30-day grace period for payment of bank loans and credit card bills are meant to assist the middle class. It is estimated that about 3.5m people will get cash support for MSMEs and their workforce amounting to about P35-50bn. The gov’t has set aside P1.17trn spending plan (5.7% of GDP) for its fight against the pandemic, P305bn emergency support to vulnerable sectors such as low-income families, P830.5bn to support the economy and P35.7bn for medical expenses. Of the P830.5bn includes P310bn additional funds from multilaterals (ADB, World Bank, AIIB), P300bn in BSP’s purchase of gov’t securities and P220bn expected from RRR and policy cuts.

 

Gov't to restart infrastructure work amid extended quarantine. The Public rail infrastructures have secured approval to restart “limited works” during the Luzon-wide community quarantine next week. Around 13 rail projects “may begin at different times next week: Metro Manila Subway; LRT1 extension, MRT7, Mindanao railway. The government admitted that while infra projects will resume, these will be done under conditions of "limited mobilisation" and skeletal workforce. Contractors must provide accommodation for workers on site while regular disinfection of work areas and health monitoring should be undertaken.

Government to boost funds against COVID-19 to P1.45trn. In a presentation to the House of Rep, Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III unveiled a P1.45trn strategy (P1.17trn previously) against the COVID-19 outbreak. The gov’t will allocate P120bn for the provision of credit guarantee for supporting small businesses. The gov’t also allocated P51bn as a wage subsidy program for employees of small business to benefit 3.4m workers. The Finance Dept is now forecasting 2020 GDP growth at - 0.8-0.0%, budget deficit at 5.3% of GDP and Debt-to-GDP ratio of 46.7%. In 2019, GDP grew 5.9%, budget deficit was at 3.55% and debt-to-GDP at 41.5%. Forecasts assume the pandemic will last until mid-2020.

BSP Governor Diokno predicts recession, followed by recovery. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Benjamin Diokno said he expects the country to enter a recession in the next 6 months before recovering in 4Q20 and upward trajectory next year. “The Philippines will likely follow a Ushaped economic recovery in 2021” Diokno said. GDP is expected to shrink 0.2% in 2020 (+1% previously) and bounce back to about 7.7% in 2021 as the impact of the gov’t policy support gains traction, assuming COVID-19 pandemic is contained in 2H20. Inflation

is now expected to settle to 2.0% in 2020 (2.2% previously) and 2.45% in 2021 (2.4% previously) due to low oil prices, decline in non-oil prices and weak domestic and global economic growth. BSP Asst. Governor Iluminada Sicat said the BSP expects OFW cash remittances to fall 0.2-0.8% in 2020 (+3% previously) due to the repatriation of OFWs.

National ID registration to start in October with 10m sign-up target. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Deputy National Statistician and Asst. Sec Lourdines dela Cruz, the national ID is expected to begin in Oct with 10m registration target. Oct sign-up period represents a few months’ delay from the original June-July scheduled rollout. Full-scale registration will prioritize heads of household and single parents in order to capture more info about potential beneficiaries for the gov’t’s cash subsidy programs. PSA hopes to register “most” Filipinos before President Duterte steps down in 2022. Dela Cruz said the internal target to launch the online preregistration initiative is by Aug-Sept.

Local government can now aid poor, DSWD to verify later. The Dept of Social Welfare and Dev’t (DSWD) said local governments can proceed to give cash aid to impoverished residents of their jurisdictions without waiting for the agency to validate the list of beneficiaries. Previously, the DSWD required potential beneficiaries to submit forms to local gov’t which will forward these to DSWD for verification. The lists got trimmed down in verification and local gov’t had to wait for truncated lists before they can start distributing aid. In order to expedite the distribution of cash aid, the DSWD said local gov’t could proceed to hand out cash aid within 24 hours of receiving funds from the dept, which would verify the eligibility of the beneficiaries within 15 days from receipt of documents from local gov’t confirming completion of the aid distribution.

 

House panel proposes P613bn economic stimulus package. House panel proposed P613bn economic stimulus package called Philippine Economic Recovery Bill (PERA) which consolidated proposals of Rep. Stella Quimbo and Rep. Joey Salceda. Under the bill the P613bn include the following programs:

  • P383bn zero interest loan program for MSMEs through Land Bank and DBP
  • P110bn wage subsidy for workers of private companies (25-75% of wages)
  • For sectoral assistance, P25bn in low-interest loans for MSMEs, P10bn for agri-fishery enterprises
  • P43bn in loans, incentives, grants to companies in the tourism sector

Metro Manila, other ‘high risk’ areas to remain on ECQ. Malacanang announced Metro Manila and several “high risk” areas will remain under Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ) till May 15. The areas to remain under ECQ include the following:

  • For Luzon: Metro Manila, Benguet, Pangasinan, Bataan, Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, Pampanga, Tarlac, Zambales, Batangas, Laguna, Cavite, Rizal, Quezon, Albay, Island of Mindoro, Catanduanes.
  • For Visayas: Cebu, Iloilo, Antique

The areas identified for selective ECQ are places that continue to report a high number of COVID-19 cases. Other areas will now be under a “general community quarantine” wherein workers in low-risk industries resume work, “non-leisure stores” in malls partially reopen, colleges finish academic year, public transport partially resume, etc.

Vehicle sales slump in March due to ECQ. Industry vehicle sales (CAMPI/TMA/AVID) dropped 67% YoY, 63% MoM to 12,104 units, bringing 1Q20 industry car sales down 27% YoY to 70,730 units. Toyota's sales in March fell 65% YoY, 63% MoM to 4,523 units. Dealerships nationwide are shut since the start of ECQ in mid- March. Toyota's 1Q20 vehicle sales are down 23% YoY to 25,696 units. Toyota accounts for c. 45% of GTC.

Tourist arrivals drop 40% in 1Q20. Tourism Secretary Bernadette Romulo-Puyat said foreign tourist arrivals likely dropped 40% YoY in 1Q20 with tourists in March falling by as much as 70% YoY. Though no official figures were given, a 40% YoY drop would imply tourist arrivals hit around 1.23m in 1Q20 vs 2.05m in 1Q19. The negative impact became more pronounced upon the commencement of the enhanced community quarantine (ECQ) beginning Mar 17. Puyat added that revising the initial targets was “not a priority”. In 2019, tourist arrivals hit 8.26m (+15% YoY) with the initial 2020 target at 9.2m (+11% YoY).
