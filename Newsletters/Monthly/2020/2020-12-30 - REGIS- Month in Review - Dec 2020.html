REGIS REPORT

30 December 2020
 

The PSEi advanced 5.1% in December as continued vaccine optimism lifted sentiment. Strong domestic liquidity kept markets higher despite foreigners continuing to sell the market.  Macro proxies and cyclicals drove gains as defensives took a back seat.

The gain in December shaved the PSEi’s 2020 decline to -8.6%. At its trough this year, the market had been down 40.8% YTD (as of March 19). The PSEi plummeted over 20% in March as COVID left markets in disarray. Prior to this, market tension had already been elevated as uncertainty surrounding the water concessionaires’ contracts cast doubt on the overall business climate in the country. The market fell 31.9% in 1Q. Reopening optimism however allowed the market some recovery in subsequent months. For the year, the PSEi is down 3.3% in USD terms, underperforming the MSCI Asia Ex Japan which is up 18.0% YTD but outperforming MSCI ASEAN which is down 9.3% YTD.

President Duterte placed the island of Luzon under a hard lockdown (“enhanced community quarantine” or ECQ) effective March 17. The lockdown placed all households under home quarantine, suspended mass transportation, and school activities, and allowed for only "essential" work. Land, sea, and air travel were also restricted. Lockdowns have gradually eased since then, with the National Capital Region switching to GCQ or general community quarantine – a looser type of lockdown  in June. While the lockdown status remained to the end of the year, easing measures have continuously been introduced. As a result, mobility has improved from the lows, though still not at pre-pandemic levels.  

Two months of hard lockdown and quarantine restrictions in various forms for the balance of the year wreaked havoc on the economy. GDP bottomed in 2Q with a 16.9% YoY contraction.  Recovery has been hard to come by, with the latest GDP print still down 11.5% YoY in 3Q.  A ban on large gatherings for the holiday season should likely reflect in muted 4Q consumer spending.  Meanwhile, the total amount of approved stimulus packages (P275bn Bayanihan 1 and the P140-165bn Bayanihan 2) is still the lowest among ASEAN peers as a share of GDP: a mere 3.6% (including CREATE).

Nonetheless an aggressive monetary and regulatory response to the COVID crisis in the form of policy (200bp) and RRR (200bps) cuts plus quantitative easing and direct monetization could carry the PSEi higher. Interest rates are expected to stay low – this may blunt the impact of earnings uncertainty and cause valuations to move ahead.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners were sellers every single month this year, with December seeing US$171m in outflows despite the market gaining another 5.1%. For 2020, foreigners were net sellers for US$2.5bn, the biggest yearly outflow recorded to date.

COVID played a big part, though foreigners were net sellers in the Philippines well before that – stretching back to August 2019 as both regulatory and political concerns worried investors.

Banks were top foreign sold again in December, with BPI, MBT, and BDO all seeing heavy outflows on what appeared to be continued profit taking given their recent outperformance. The big three banks had all gained more than 10-20% since the start of  November, and outperformed the PSEi which advanced around 13%.

Retail name PGOLD was also top foreign sold and among this month’s biggest laggards on the back of expected soft holiday spending this year.

Foreigners were two-way property, selling SMPH while buying ALI. Meanwhile, Philippines’ biggest nickel miner NIKL (+23.1% MoM) was top foreign bought on increased interest from investors as global commodity prices continue to climb.

+++

Heavy foreign selling was seen across the board this year, with liquid big-caps banks/property/conglomerates seeing the biggest outflows. Banks were clearly out of favor, as they are expected to bear the brunt of the pandemic induced economic fallout.

Telcos GLO/TEL were also top sold despite being pandemic “winners” as the entrance of a third player -- China-backed DITO Telecommunity proved to be an overhang throughout the year.  

Meanwhile, SM and SMPH were the only names that saw decent inflow, but these capture arranged crosses that went through during the year.  Excluding these, SM and SMPH would have seen net foreign outflows of US$244m and US$195m, respectively.

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Macro proxies were up across the board as the index gained.

Alliance Global (+8.2% MoM) continued to perform, though big index names SM (+8.1% MoM), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+12.0% MoM) and JG Summit (+9.1% MoM) also gained significantly.

MPI (+4.4% MoM) closed the month with gains. MPI has sold its 56% stake in Global Business Power (GBP) to Meralco at a valuation of P22.4bn. The implied equity value (100%) of P37.9bn is higher than Regis' valuation of P30.6bn. The annualized FY20 PER of 17x is also high considering the multiples of listed gencos (<10x PER) as well as the fact that bulk of its assets are coal-fired.

GBP is a holding company for a number of power generation assets located almost entirely in the Visayas region, with a net attributable capacity of 617MW. 

The deal is positive for MPI as it is able to raise  a tidy sum of cash from an asset that underperformed since it was acquired from GT Capital. Regis estimates the sale could improve MPI’s NAV by 2% and also place it in a better financial position for its infrastructure investments (toll roads, LRT-1). 

MPI’s NLEX toll booths in Valenzuela were suspended in December. The suspension was evenutally lifted soon after, however. The suspension came in the wake of massive traffic jams emerged with the full implementation of wholly cashless (RFID only) toll collection system.  The duration of the suspension implies a profit hit of up to P20m for Metro Pacific (vs FY20e P13bn). The toll plazas affected by the suspension account for c. 10% of NLEX’s revenues while NLEX accounts for 20% of MPI’s NAV and profit.

First Philippine Holdings (+16.3% MoM) will make a tender offer for 20% to 45.6% of its parent, Lopez Holdings (+20.8% MoM) at P3.85/share. This is a 25% premium to LPZ’s last traded price (on announcement) and a 27% discount to its estimated underlying market-price NAV (~P5.3/share), which is composed mostly of shares in FPH and its media subsidiary ABS-CBN. If the tender offer is successful and LPZ is delisted, FPH could end up owning as much as 45% of an unlisted company that itself owns 50% of FPH. The deal should be significantly enhancing to FPH’s NAV. Regis estimates this transaction could raise FPH’s underlying NAV/share by as much as 22% from the current P169/share to P206/share. 

The Ayala group announced a shuffling of senior management at parent Ayala Corp ( +0.4% MoM) as well as BPI and Globe. The CFO at AC, TG Limcaoco, is taking over as CEO of BPI by April next year. Limcaoco will replace Cezar Consing, who will remain a director at BPI and also a member of the executive committee. Immediately prior to becoming CFO at AC, Limcaoco was the president of the consumer banking subsidiary of BPI.

Replacing Limcaoco at AC will be Alberto de Larrazabal, who is currently Chief Commercial Officer at Globe. Larrazabal was previously CFO at Globe.

Ayala Corp is also splitting the chairman and CEO positions. The current chairman Jaime Augusto Zobel (JAZA) will stay on but the CEO position will be taken on by his brother Fernando.

Meanwhile, AC is acquiring 70% of Mercado General Hospital.  Mercado General Hospital runs four hospitals and a number of other health facilities some of which operate under the Qualimed brand. Some of the 70% stake will be purchased from subsidiary Ayala Land.

GT Capital's (+0.9% MoM) Toyota sold 11,455 units in November, down 27% YoY and 2% MoM.  This brings 11M20 sales to 86303 units on track to beat Regis FY20 estimate of 90k units. Management is targeting 100k units this year.

However, the impact of COVID on operations however pushed the stock down -30.9% for the year. Other big decliners were SMC, down 21.9% for the year, as well as Cosco which fell 17.4% 

Meanwhile, MPI and LT Group (-0.8% MoM) delivered gains of +23.0% and 9.3% respectively in 2020. Large caps AC and SM managed to end with gains for the year with their gains of 5.3% and 0.5%, respectively.

 

Banks mostly gained in December as cyclicals saw laggard interest.

Security Bank (+7.6% MoM) led the gainers within the sector.  Metrobank (+2.3% MoM) and BDO (+3.4% MoM) also gained. 

BPI (-2.0% MoM) lagged after having outperformed previously.  Nonetheless, BPI outperformed its peers in 2020 as the bank’s traditionally conservative stance attracted interest amidst the pandemic.

Large declines were seen across the sector, however, as COVID brought about concerns on asset quality deterioration. 

BDO declining a marked 32.4% for the year. Security Bank dropped 31.3%, and Metrobank shed 26% in 2020. BPI fell a less steep 7.5%. 

Laggard interest sent Robinsons Land (+27.6% MOM) higher in December. 

Newsflow was scarce during the month though continued rotation into cyclicals resulted in gains for Ayala Land (+7.6% MoM) and SM Prime (+6.9% MoM). Ayala Land was the top net foreign bought stock during the month, seeing US$11.3m in net inflows.

The continued acceleration of POGO closures weighed on the sector, despite the sequential improvement of mall and residential segments post the strictest lockdowns. Office vacancy increased while rental rates fell as POGO closures accelerated; revisions to POGO tax regulations in October further accelerated the closures.

SM Prime and Ayala Land ended the year in the red dropping by 8.6% and 10.1%, respectively.  Smaller cap names Vista Land, Filinvest Land, and Robinsons Land were worse hit with declines of 39.5%, 25.3%, and 23%. Megaworld managed a modest gain of 1.7% in 2020.

 

Consumer names were mixed in December amidst a dearth of news. Gains in December were seen in large caps Universal Robina (+7.4% MoM) and Jollibee (+3.3% MoM). 

Rotation from defensives saw Puregold (-2.7% MoM) and Century Pacific (-0.2% MoM) underperform during the month. Retailers RRHI (-4.4% MoM), Wilcon (-6.1% MoM) and SSI Group (-13.4% MoM) dropped as well.

Emperador (+0.2% MoM) was flat. In a press release, Emperador said that that volumes of Don Pedro and Presidente brandy products in the US have been growing at “double digits” amid the pandemic. Both brands are part of the Case Pedro Domecq portfolio in Mexico owned by Grupo Emperador Spain. They also saw sales of Fundador in the US grow by “around 23%” from January to September.

With a 40% gain, Emperador was the top performer within the sector in 2020. Strong international operations and resilience in its brandy segment bolstered sentiment for the stock. 

Restaurants and non-essential retail operations were badly hit by temporary store closures (due to the lockdowns). This saw Jollibee declining 9.6% for the year. Smaller cap restauranats were worse off, with Shakey's Pizza down 22.2% adn Max's Group down 44.4%. 

Meanwhile, SSI group and RRHI dropped significatly (down 46.4% and 18.8%, respectively) for the year as well. 

Consumer staples and grocery retailers did better – benefitting from pantry stocking at the height of the strict lockdowns. CNPF rose 15.3% for the year.  Puregold managed to end the year with a gain of 3.1%.  Gains were pared however by muted holiday spending given the ban on large Christams gatherings and as mobility remained sub. 

URC closed up 5.2% for the year.

 

Telcos  closed up in December.

PLDT (+2.0% MoM) and Globe (+4.2% MoM) are aiming to build as many as 2,000 telecom towers each next year, adding to some 10,000 they each now have.  PLDT has already upped its capex guidance to P88bn – P92bn for next year, up from P70bn this year.  This comes ahead of the impending launch of new operator Dito which says it has 1900 towers.

Dito, the telecom firm jointly owned by the Davao Dennis Uy and China Telecom, is beginning ‘friendly user tests’ ahead of its technical audit. Dito’s technical audit is supposed to be done in a month’s time (Jan 7). The company is committed to deliver 37% population coverage and 27mbps average wireless data speed by that time.

 

In the meantime, PLDT sold Smart Tower, a Grade A office building along Ayala avenue in Makati, for P6.14bn with a GFA of 38,000sqm. The building was purchased by Consunji-owned DMC Urban Property Developers (not part of the listed DMCI Holdings). Under the agreement, PLDT will continue to occupy the building for the next five years through a leaseback agreement. If 80%-90% of GFA is leasable, the transaction values the building at P180,000/sqm-P202,000/sqm of leasable area. Regis estimates the transaction values Smart Tower at 4-6% cap rate, lower than the 8% currently assumed for office properties.

PLDT amassed a 35.6% gain in 2020 as investors flocked to defensive earnings.  PLDT was also rewared for succesfully reducing its market share gap against Globe ( now 49% to 51%).  Globe was flat (+0.5%) in 2020.

Broadband gained markedly for the telcos  as more as more people worked or studied from home.   

 

AC Energy (+44.2% MoM) released details of its upcoming stock rights offering. ACEN will be offering 2.268bn new shares to shareholders at a ratio of 1 rights share for every 1.11 shares held. ACEN’s parent (AC Energy, Inc (ACEI) will not participate in the rights offer and will have itself diluted. The price of the new shares will be set at P2.37/share; the shares will go ex-rights on Jan 8.

This exercise is part of a series of transactions that will also involve the purchase of shares by Singapore sovereign wealth fund GIC, the injection of ACEI’s international assets and an eventual follow-on offering.

Meralco (+3.7% MoM) is buying a combined 86% of Global Business Power (GBP) for P34.5bn. The GBP stake/s are being acquired from Meralco’s two biggest shareholders, Metro Pacific (56% stake) and JG Summit (30%). The transaction would take Meralco’s ownership in GBP to 100%. The transaction is expected to be NAV-dilutive by close to 2% for Meralco.

Meanwhile, Meralco is extending its “no-disconnection” policy to end of January for small users. Customers with monthly consumption below 200kwh will not be disconnected even if their payments are overdue. These customers comprise 47% of total customers and account for <10% by kwh usage. The grace period extended by Meralco since the pandemic has resulted in a rise in receivables by as much as P25bn as of September.

Semirara (+11.1% MoM) surged on laggard interest. Semirara has voluntarily deferred mining operations in North Block 7 (NB7), one of four mining blocks in Molave Mine. The deferral is due to water build-up in the sump of NB7, which was caused by water seepages and aggravated by heavy rainfall from the recent typhoons. Coal shipments will not be affected, however, given that it has 3.4m MT of coal inventory as of Dec 1.  However, the company also flagged that it may not be able to meet the coal quality specifications of a “few” customers.

Meanwhile, the consortium of Saur SAS, Miahona Company, and Manila Water (+8.0% MoM) has signed a Management, Operation and Maintenance Contract with the National Water Company, Kingdom of Saudi Arabia for the latter’s North West Cluster. The MOMC will comprise the management, operations and maintenance of the water and wastewater facilities of the North West Cluster (Madinah and Tabuk) over a 7-year contract period.

Aboitiz Power (+3.1% MoM) is looking to raise P30bn by issuing retail bonds which it plans to sell in tranches. Its board approved the issuance of the first tranche of retail bonds worth P4bn scheduled to be rolled out in 1Q21. Proceeds from the first tranche will be used for refinancing of corporate debts and other general corporate purposes.

 

 

Transport stocks were up in December. 

Reopening optimism took Cebu Pacific (+7.4% MoM) higher. The stock contracted substantially in 2020 – down 43.6%.  CEB expects to lose as much as P25bn in 2020 and as of 9M20, CEB’s net loss stood at P14.7bn. CEB is currently operating at 16% of its pre-Covid capacity, up from only 10% from June to November.

ICTSI (+5.7% MoM) also gained.  The Philippine Ports Authority is allowing port operators to impose tariffs for the handling of empty containers beginning next year. Empties account for ~30% of containers at the Manila port and Regis expects the tariffs for handling such to be around 30% (of usual full boxes). These imply incremental revenue of around 2% for ICTSI. Regis estimates that this could add 3% to EBITDA and as much as 10% to earnings.

ICTSI ended the year down a mere 4.0% as the normalization of global trade volumes led to a share price recovery.

 

Megawide (-16.2% MoM) dropped in December.  The Manila International Airport Authority (MIAA) announced the revocation of the partnership of Megawide and GMR Infrastructure’s original proponent status it was originally awarded for its P109bn unsolicited proposal for the NAIA rehab.  Megawide has filed a motion for reconsideration.

The decline in December took its YTD drop to 52.5% as pandemic related disruptions to its construction and airport units weighed on operations even as its landport tower dealt with a POGO tenant prematurely ending its lease.

Holcim (+5.1% MoM) also registerd a big decline of 46.7% for the year, with the drop triggered by the collapse of the deal for San Miguel’s acquisition of Holcim Phils after failure of the Philippine Compeititon Commission to grant approval for the deal to proceed.

Cemex Phils (-11.6% MoM) fell 20.3% in 2020. 

 

Bloomberry (-8.9% MoM) consolidated in December, after surging over 25% in the previous month. Prospects of continued reopening pared the stock's losses from as much as 62% YTD (in March) to -28.2% for 2020.

Bloomberry creditors agreed to waive the financial covenant  testing relating to its debts until Jun 2023. This seems to suggest the view that there would be some risk of the firm breaching its covenant ratios as its tries to get back to normal amidst the pandemic and as it prepares to invest in its Quezon City property.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation jumps to 3.3% in November. Headline inflation rose to 3.3% in November from 2.5% the previous month. This was slightly higher than BSP’s expectations (2.4-3.3%). The uptick in inflation was due to higher food inflation (e.g. fish, fruits, and vegetables) due to weather-related supply disruptions while the African Swine Fever (ASF) continued to adversely affect pork supply. Inflation for alcoholic beverages and tobacco also accelerated. Meanwhile, Core inflation rose to 3.2% from 3.0% in October. This brings YT-November inflation to 2.6%, within the government’s target range of 2-4%.

BOP surplus at US$1.47bn in November. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$1.47bn in November. This is up 172% YoY but lower than the US$3.4bn surplus in October 2020. This brings YT-November BOP surplus to US$11.79bn, higher than the US$6.27bn in the same period last year. 

GIR climbs to US$104.5bn in November. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by US$706mn MoM (+0.7%) to US$104.5bn as of end-November 2020. This reflects inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad and was partly offset, by the National Government’s debt payments and revaluation losses from the BSP’s gold holdings. The GIR is equivalent to 11.2 months’ worth of imports.

 

 

Budget deficit widens to P128bn in November. The National Government’s budget deficit climbed to P128bn in November. This is higher than the P61bn deficit in the same period last year. Revenues dropped 19% YoY in November, as tax collections from the BIR and BOC dropped 17% and 13%, respectively. Month-on-month, tax revenues from BIR jumped 26%; BOC revenues dropped 13.6% MoM. Expenditures rose 2.3% YoY on subsidy releases for the Unconditional Cash Transfer Program, National Health Insurance Program, as well as equity to GFIs for COVID-19 lending programs.

YT-November budget deficit is at P1.069tn, up 161% YoY. The government lowered its FY20 deficit target to P1.38tn (7.6% of GDP) from P1.815tn (9.6% of GDP) previously. For FY21, the government is targeting a budget deficit of 1.78tn (8.9% of GDP)

DBCC sees deeper economic slump. The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) once again cut its FY20 GDP estimate to -8.5% to -9.5%. This is a deeper contraction than the July 28 estimate of -4.4 to -6.6%. They cited the prolonged imposition of community quarantines in various regions in the country, as well as recent typhoons. The government kept its growth forecast for FY21 at 6.5% - 7.5% and raised FY22E to 8-10%.

 

 

OFW Remittances up 2.9% YoY in October.The BSP reported OFW remittances rose 2.9% YoY, 5.6% MoM to US$2.75bn in October. In PHP terms, remittances dropped 3.2% YoY but was higher by 6% MoM. This brings the 3-month moving average growth rate at +2.5%. Land-based remittances rose due to a 16% YoY jump in remittances from the USA. This brings the cumulative 10M20 remittances to US$24.6bn, down 0.9% YoY (in PHP terms, down 5.0% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 2% in FY20 and 4% in FY21 (FY19A: US$30bn).

President signs 2021 budget into law. President Duterte signed the P4.5tn 2021 General Appropriations Act (GAA). The budget includes a P72.5bn vaccination program, which is targeted to inoculate 24.7mn Filipinos.

BSP holds key interest rates steady. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas kept the overnight reverse repurchase, lending and deposit facilities at all-time lows of 2%, 2.5%, and 1.5%, respectively which was broadly in line with market expectations. The BSP has cut rates by a cumulative 200bps this year. The BSP upgraded its inflation forecast to 2.6% (from 2.5%) in 2020 and 3.2% (from 2.7%) in 2021 and cited the increase in global oil prices.


BSP to keep rates low until end-2022. The BSP is committed to keeping interest rates low until the end of 2022, according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno.  Low rates will be kept until the economy recovers to its previous 6.5-7.5% growth and unemployment is down to 5% range. The BSP still has room for further monetary easing amid a benign inflation environment (2-4%) until 2024. The BSP’s Monetary Board is set to meet again on Feb 11. 

Foreign portfolio investments yield net inflow in November. Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. “hot money”) registered a net inflow of US$227mn in November. This is lower than the US$439mn net inflow recorded in October 2020. YT-November net outflow at US$3.72bn, up 135% YoY.

Philippines launches second USD bond issue. The Philippine gov’t launched another dollar bond offering. The offering of 10.5-year and 25-year US dollar-denominated bonds would carry a yield of around 100bps above the 10-year US Treasury benchmark. The proceeds will be used to support the budget. In April, the government raised US$2.35 bn from its dollar-denominated bond sale. The 2021 budget of P4.5tn (+10% YoY) is currently pending bicam approval. The gov’t is targeting a budget deficit of P1.7tn (8.5% of GDP) in 2021, lower than the budget deficit target of P1.8tn in 2020 (9.6% of GDP).

 

Treasury to borrow P140bn from domestic mart in January. The Bureau of Treasury has set a P140bn borrowing program for January. The Treasury is planning to borrow P80bn via T-bills and P60bn via T-bonds. This is higher than the P120bn program for December. 

Gov’t borrowings have reached P3.224tn in 10M20, exceeding the full-year program. This is due to a new round of cash advances from the BSP in October worth P540bn. 

The government has a  budget deficit target at 7.6% of GDP in 2020.  For FY21, the government plans to borrow another P3tn to plug a deficit of 8.9% of GDP. 

Unemployment rate drops to 8.7% in October.Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority showed that the unemployment rate dropped further to 8.7% in October 2020 from 10% in July and 17.6% in April but remained higher than the 4.6% unemployment rate in the same period last year. It is worth noting that while the absolute number of unemployed dropped by 758k QoQ, the number of employed dropped more by 1.47mn. Thus, the QoQ drop in the unemployment rate appears to have been partly due to a lower labor force participation rate, which stood at only 58.7% in October (vs. 61.9% in July and 55.7% in April). Employment in the services sector rose slightly QoQ, buoyed by a 48% jump in communications (1% of total employment). However, this was more than offset by decreases in agri (-14%, 21% of total), manufacturing (-10%, 8% of total), and trade (-6%, 21% of total). Meanwhile, employment in the construction sector (10% of total) shrunk 4.9% YoY and 1.5% QoQ. 

 

 

Metro Manila, Davao City among areas under GCQ in January. President Duterte extended GCQ status in Metro Manila and other areas throughout January 2021. Areas under GCQ include: Davao City , Tacloban City, Santiago City, Iligan City, Iloilo City, Isabela, Batangas, Lanao Del Sur, Davao del Norte. The rest of the country will be under the more lenient modified GCQ.

Duterte orders speedy vaccine procurement. President Duterte has signed Executive Order 121, granting the emergency approval (called “emergency use authorization” or EUA) on COVID-19 medicines and vaccines. The EUA will be issued and remain valid if there is evidence that the drug or vaccine is effective in diagnosing, treating, or preventing the coronavirus. The Gov’t is targeting the arrival of the vaccine by 1Q21 for clinical trials, according to National Task Force against COVID-19 chief implementer Carlito Galvez Jr. The 1Q21 target will involve vaccines from China’s Sinovac Biotech Ltd. and Russia’s Gamaleya. Negotiations are currently ongoing with a base case target of May 2021 and the worst scenario is that the vaccine might be available by 2022. The government is prioritizing its COVID-19 vaccine purchase from four firms: Sinovac, Gamaleya, AstraZeneca plc, and Pfizer, Inc. The government is also holding talks with other foreign firms, including four US vaccine developers and targeting to have at least 10 to 15 or even 20mn vaccine (doses from) each of these vaccine makers.

 

Loan growth slows further to 1.9% in October. Outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, eased to 1.9% in October from 2.6% in September (revised). On a month-on-month seasonally-adjusted basis, outstanding universal and commercial bank loans, net of RRPs, declined by 0.4%. Loans to key sectors including manufacturing (-3.6%), wholesale and retail trade and repair of motor vehicles and motorcycles  (-4.3%) continued to contract. Consumer loans (credit cards and auto) expanded at a slower pace of 8.0% in October from 9.8% in September. Meanwhile, money supply grew by 11.8% in October, mildly slower than the 12.2% growth in September (revised).

Banks' bad loans hit record-high in October. The banking sector’s gross NPL ratio (ex-interbank) rose to 3.80% in October. This is the highest on record since 2013 and higher than the 3.58% in September 2020. The gross non-performing loans rose 69.9% YoY and 5.56% MoM to P391.4bn. The allowance for credit losses have been trending below NPL over the past 2 months at P347.7bn as of October. Meanwhile, NPL coverage ratio sank to their record-low of 88.83% and non-performing assets surged 48.8% YoY to P504.83bn. Including restructured loans, total distressed assets now account for 5.72% of banks’ real estate properties.

Credit card delinquency rate rises to 11.5% in September. The Credit Card Association of the Philippines (CCAP) said that the credit card delinquency rate rose to 11.5% in September. This comes from 10.5% in August and 4.36% in December 2019. CCAP said the increase was primarily due to loss of income and unforeseen expenses. 

Bicameral panel approves FIST bill. The Bicameral Conference Committee approved the Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer (FIST) bill. FIST bill will allow financial institutions to offload bad loans to asset management companies. The Bill will likely be ratified by Congress ahead of the Christmas break (Dec 19).

House of Representatives approves bill granting fee discounts on OFW remittances. The House of Representatives approved (on third reading) a bill which seeks to reduce fees on OFW remittances by up to 50%. The bill would also prohibit financial and non-financial intermediaries from raising their current remittance fees without prior consultation with the DOF, BSP, or Philippine Overseas Employment Administration (POEA). However, the measure would also grant tax discounts to intermediaries for the cost of services to OFWs.

Congress OKs extension of Bayanihan II validity. The House of Representatives approved the bill extending the validity of the Bayanihan to Recover as One Act (Bayanihan II) by six months or until June 30, 2021. The Bayanihan II was supposed to expire on Dec 19. The Senate also approved the bills extending the validity of the 2020 GAA and Bayanihan II.

Bayanihan III bill worth P485bn filed in Senate. Senator Ralph Recto filed Senate Bill 1953 or the Bayanihan III stimulus package. The Bill has a counterpart measure in the Lower House (House Bill 8031). Additionally, the Bill allocates P55bn for COVID-19 vaccines and another P20bn for vaccine storage. Another P35bn is allocated for hiring of contact tracers, fund tests and treatments. Meanwhile, P100bn is allocated for worker subsidies; P70bn in cash assistance to households; P20bn for typhoon-affected households. The Bill also has P30bn for displaced workers; P50bn for the rehab of typhoon-hit areas; P5bn internet allowance for teachers and students. Bayanihan I and II’s stimulus packages amounted to P375bn and P166bn, respectively. Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III previously indicated that another stimulus package may not be an option. He cited instances where this kind of intervention was not able to push other economies back up, especially when not implemented properly.
