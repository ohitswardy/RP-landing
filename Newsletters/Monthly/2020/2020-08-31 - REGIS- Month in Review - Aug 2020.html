REGIS REPORT

31 August 2020
 

The Philippines closed down 0.75% in August, hovering around the 6000 level all month as the fate of economic recovery remained uncertain.  YTD, the Philippines is down 24.7%.   

The reimposition of stricter lockdown measures (MECQ or Modified Enhanced Community Quarantine) for two weeks in August set off renewed concerns of a further setback for an economy already struggling to recover. While the economy is now operating under looser restrictions (GCQ or General Community Quarantine), continued restrictions on public transport and movement suggests an uphill climb to normalcy. OFW remittances, meanwhile, have been gravely hurt, falling 7% in PHP terms in 1H20. The destruction of household incomes and balance sheets continued to undermine sentiment.

Indeed strict lockdowns and limited mobility have resulted in severe economic impact.  The  country’s 2Q GDP shrank 16.5% in 2Q, falling below consensus forecasts of a 9.4% contraction, with nearly all segments posting sharp declines.

Earnings were no different.  Corporate earnings in 2Q for PSEi components plunged 81% YoY.  Cumulative 1H earnings collapsed 54% YoY.  Across Regis’ coverage universe, 51% of 2Q results fell below estimates, with only 26% ahead. Nine of the 43 companies under coverage posted “core” net losses in the quarter. Every single sector saw aggregate 2Q earnings fall YoY. Only the telcos managed to buck the trend, gaining mildly thanks to a large jump from PLDT.

Along with PLDT, investors continued to favor the pandemic defensives in August such as Puregold and ICTSI.  Other consumer names URC, CNPF,  also led, as did names offering deep value such as LT Group, Metro Pacific and Alliance Global.

Foreign net selling continued on for the 10th month in a row, reaching US$ 261m in August.  Excluding SM group transfers (+US$147m), net outflows were larger at US$408m. Headline net outflows YTD now stand at US$1.7bn.

The outflows are more pronounced against paltry market volumes. Average daily turnover of US$168m was boosted SM group crosses without which daily volumes would have just been at US$115m.

The Bayahinhan II Bill has been approved by the Bicameral conference Committee. The Bill provides for a total P165bn in fresh funds for COVID-19 relief, or around 1% of GDP. The DOF has also tweaked Tax Package 2 –  the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act (CREATE) – as its main stimulus bill. The new iteration cuts corporate income tax rates by 500bps immediately from 30% to 25%, then a more gradual fall to 20%. The bill also allows for 5 years of net operating loss carry-over (NOLCO).  The combined impact of Bayanihan 1 and 2, and the tax cuts of CREATE amount to roughly 3.6% of GDP, the lowest among our Asean peers.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 

August was a month filled with 2Q corporate results. And there was certainly a lot of carnage. Aggregate core profits for the quarter came in ~80% below the previous year (-81% for the PSEi components and -79% for Regis coverage). For the PSEi companies, cumulative 1H earnings are 54% below last year and amount to 37% of Regis FY estimates. Across the Regis coverage universe, 51% of 2Q results fell below estimates, with only 26% ahead. Nine of the 43 companies under coverage posted “core” net losses in the quarter. 

 

After BDO (-2.3% MoM) reported a 2Q net loss of P4.5bn last month due to a surge in provisions, Metrobank (-2.2% MoM) reported a large jump in provisions as well.  Metrobank accelerated its provisions to P18bn in 2Q alone.  Profits for 1H fell 30% YoY. Loans also contracted 5% YoY. 

Security Bank (+5.4% MoM) was of the few notable exceptions during the reporting season. The bank managed to book hefty trading gains, allowing it to post strong profits.  1H profits of P5.7bn are already 90% of REGIS FY20E even after the bank set aside P11bn in provisions.  Trading gains were up 6-fold YoY to P7.1bn during the quarter. Loan growth slowed to 7% YoY, compared to 14% in 1Q.

 

 

The consumer sector saw earnings plunge an aggregate 52% in 2Q. However, the divergence in earnings was stark between subsectors. The restaurant operators all fell into the red while consumer staples and grocery retailers saw sales spike.

Jollibee (+0.1% MoM) announced a headline loss of P10bn in 2Q alone, including P7bn in provisions relating to its transformation initiatives. The company had to temporarily shut 50% of its store network globally at the beginning of 2Q given the lockdowns in key markets. 

Store closures also resulted in losses for Shakey’s Pizza (-4.7% MoM), with a headline net loss of P282m in 2Q.  Max’s Group (+3.7% MoM) restaurant reported a net loss of P431m during the quarter. 

Meanwhile, the closure of restaurants and hotels caused poultry demand and prices to collapse, resulting in a loss for San Miguel FB’s (+6.2% MoM) food business. FB’s profit in 2Q20 fell 86%YoY.  Beer volumes plunged more than 60%. 

Consumer staples however, saw a surge in sales. 

Century Pacific’s (+12.0% MoM) net profit in 1H20 jumped 31% YoY to P2.2bn as consolidated revenues grew 28%. Branded revenues (82% of total) grew 35% YoY.  For 2Q20 alone, net profit grew 31% YoY to P1.2bn, with branded revenue growth accelerating 39% during the quarter, on heightened demand for shelf-stable products during the lockdown period. Nearly all of CNPF’s key categories – Marine, Meat, and Milk – posted growth of >30% during the quarter.

Universal Robina (+12.6% MoM) also saw earnings jump. EBIT in 2Q20 jumped climbed 16% YoY as margins improved across all business segments. Branded consumer food in the Phils was steady, flattish during the quarter.  

Puregold (+12.5% MoM) also saw an expansion in profits.  Net profit in 2Q jumped 24% YoY,on sales growth of 13% YoY. the core Puregold brand (75% of consolidated sales) saw a moderation in SSSG QoQ (+4.4% in 2Q20) due to the lack of mobility for its target market in the absence of public transportation. On the flipside, S&R’s SSSG (+19.7% in 2Q20) accelerated QoQ, boosted by pantry stocking for its target market (upper-middle to upper income households).

Meanwhile, Robinsons Retail (+13.5% MoM) reported a 33% net profit decline in 2Q. Consolidated same-store sales contracted 13.9% in 2Q20 alone as majority of RRHI’s formats had to be temporarily closed during the lockdown. As expected, supermarkets (69% of sales) was the outperformer, posting SSSG of +14.2%.

Emperador (+9.9% MoM) meanwhile surged.  2Q20 income increased 24%yoy to P1.869bn, lifted by a 103% jump in profit from its whisky segment (P694m). Brandy, consisting of Emperador and Fundador, was also resilient despite the liquor restrictions in April-May. The stock was also an addition in the August 2020 rebal.

 

The property sector was severely affected by the hard lockdown until end-May, particularly the mall and residential segments. 

SM Prime’s (-2.7% MoM) 2Q20 net profit fell 80% to P2.1bn due to the hard lockdown until the end of May. 2Q20 total mall revenue fell 73% (to P4.3bn) as Philippine mall revenue fell c. 80% (to P3.1bn). During the hard lockdown, only essential services, which occupy 20% of mall space, could operate.  2Q20 residential revenue was flat YoY (up 8% QoQ).  

 

 

Ayala Land’s (-13.5% MoM) 2Q20 net profit plunged 97% to P197m as the hard lockdown until end-May caused weak residential revenue and losses in the mall business (in 2Q20). 

Net profit at Robinsons Land (-4.9% MoM) also collapsed, ending 76% YoY lower.  

Filinvest Land’s (+8.0% MoM) 2Q20 net profit fell 27% to P952m.  Weak residential revenue was flattered by an unusually low tax rate, unusually high equitized earnings, and a sharp drop in opex.

 

 

Conglos reported massive declines in earnings as well in 2Q, dragged by blighted profitability at the subs.  

SM Investments (-4.5% MoM) incurred a net loss of ~P1.9bn for 2Q as both BDO and SM Retail dipped into the red for the quarter.  Philippine mall revenue dropped around 80% for SM Prime during the quarter, meanwhile. 

GT Capital’s (-8.9% MoM) implied 2Q profit of P183m represented a 95% plunge YoY.  Toyota Motor Philippines incurred a net loss of P436m in 2Q as vehicle sales plunged 81% as a result of dealership and plant closures.  Income at Metrobank fell 53% YoY in 2Q20. 

Meanwhile, the closure of  casinos led to P2.757bn in losses for Travellers, dragging Alliance Global’s (+8.4% MoM)  2Q net income down 78% YoY to P838m.  The lockdown also led to  an P840m loss for Golden Arches. Megaworld’s 2Q20 profit fell 58% to P1.898bn on curtailed mall operations and residential construction. The bright spot was Emperador. 2Q20 income increased 24% YoY to P1.869bn, lifted by a 103% jump in profit from its whisky segment (P694m). Brandy, consisting of Emperador and Fundador, was also resilient despite the liquor restrictions in April-May.

 

Metro Pacific’s (+8.8% MoM)  2Q core profit fell 63% YoY to P1.9bn, as demand dropped across its operating units during the lockdown. Contributions from the power segment dropped 31% YoY after electricity volumes at Meralco and GBP fell.  The tollways arm incurred a mild net loss while rail and hospital units reported losses as well during the quarter.  The water unit saw a net profit drop of 29%. 

LT Group (+15.4% MoM) reported a net income of P3.814bn in 2Q20, a decrease of 21%YoY, on depressed contributions from its bank and beverage units. Its share in the tobacco segment in 2Q20, meanwhile, registered a growth of 6% from last year’s levels despite lower cigarette volumes.

DMCI (+12.7% MoM)  reported 1H20 profit of P2.03bn, down 69% YoY. Excluding one-offs and forex adjustments, 1H20 core profit fell short at P2.61bn, down 60% YoY. Apart from halting construction activity, the lockdown also resulted in lower than expected power and coal prices, as well as coal sales volumes. 2Q20 core profit was down 57% YoY to P1.59bn   

For 2Q20 Cosco’s (-2.2% MoM), net profit dropped 19% YoY to P1.07bn. Excluding PGOLD, which saw profit growth of 24% YoY, all other key segments saw profits decline in 2Q20.

 

 

While every other sector saw aggregate 2Q earnings fall YoY, telcos managed to produce a small gain (+1%), thanks to PLDT’s (+10.4% MoM) strong quarter. 

PLDT’s core profit in 2Q rose 15%.  The better-than-expected bottom line was driven by robust top-line growth. 

 

Meanwhile, Globe’s (+1.2% MoM) core income for 2Q fell 15% YoY, as mobile revenue dropped a result of the lockdown. Broadband take-up surged however. Regis estimates PLDT gained 1ppt of market share in wireless revenue over the quarter. Nonetheless, both telcos beat expectations.  

For 2Q alone, core income was P6.6bn (+15% YoY). The better-than-expected bottom line was driven by robust top-line growth. Service revenues were up +7% in 1H and +5% in 2Q. 



 

The power/utilities sector was less hit than most, with aggregate profits down 42%.

First Gen (-9.6% MoM) was the least affected in the sector due to its offtake agreements. First Gen’s 2Q recurring income was US$68m, down 14% YoY.  

Aboitiz Power (+3.9% MoM) saw a larger 67% YoY decline in 2Q20 profits as the lockdown affected energy sales and selling prices. Core income for the generation and retail electricity supply (RES) segment fell to P1.648bn (-62% YoY. On top of plant outages that spilled over into the quarter, energy volumes declined to 5,089 GWh (-17%YoY) as quarantine restrictions dampened demand from commercial and industrial customers.

 

 

Semirara’s (-5.3% MoM) 2Q20 profit dropped 68% YoY to P1.1bn.  The coal mining unit saw 2Q20 profits plunge 65% YoY, 38% QoQ to P990m as the lockdown affected sales volumes.  Coals ASPs also dropped. Nonetheless, the power unit reversed its losses in 2Q, booking P77m in profits during the quarter.  The stock was removed from the PSEi during its August rebal. 

Manila Water’s (+12.3% MoM) 2Q profit to common fell 24% to P1.14bn, bringing 1H20 profit down 17% to P2.66bn.  Earnings for MWC’s local and regional investments were weighed down by the deferral of tariff hike at the East zone early this year and the lockdown. 

 

Also falling victim was Cebu Air (-5.2% MoM) which saw losses balloon to P7.95bn in 2Q due to travel restrictions. CEB’s flights dropped  95% YoY in 2Q, with pax volumes down 99% YoY to a mere 62k.  

Faring much better was ICTSI (+8.8% MoM).  ICT’s 2Q20 profit remained flat at US$40m, as higher ancillary revenues and lower costs compensated for the 11% drop in volumes.  

The suspension of casino operations due to lockdowns caused losses for Bloomberry (-2.9% MoM) in 2Q. 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL DEVELOPMENTS

GDP in 2Q comes in at. -16.5% YoY.GDP shrank by 16.5% yoy in 2Q20, the worst quarterly YoY contraction on record (previous worst was -10.5% in 3Q 1984).

The strict Enhanced Community Quarantine (ECQ) lockdown in April/May saw massive contractions across nearly every sector.

  • By Expenditure – Personal spending dropped 15% YoY; as the lockdown hurt not only demand (incomes) but also supply (shop closures). Capital formation plunged 53.5%. Government spend jumped 22% thanks to the Bayanihan Act handouts. Net exports helped too; the halving of the trade deficit provided a 500bps lift, by our estimates.
  • By Industry – Every major sector saw double-digit declines. Expectedly, the Accommodations and Food Service segment collapsing -68% YoY, followed by Construction (-34%), Manufacturing -21%, and Real Estate (- 20%). Slight gains were posted by Agriculture (+2%) and Finance (+7%).

External trade plunges in June. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported the trade deficit stood at US$1.3bn in June, down 51% YoY. Imports fell 24% YoY (vs -41% in May) while exports dropped 13% YoY (vs. -27% in May). The slower decline in trade performance reflects resumption of economic activities. This brings 1H20 trade deficit to US$10.6bn, down 48% YoY.

Hot money outflow hits US$453.17mn in July. Foreign portfolio investments hit a net outflow of US$453.17mn in July, a reversal of last year’s net inflow of US$15.02mn. From January to July, the Philippines recorded a net outflow of US$7.76bn vs a net outflow of US$706m vs 28mn in the same period last year. The BSP said attributed the larger outflows to uncertainties due to the pandemic, geopolitical and trade tensions and corporate governance issues involving the water concessionaires. The BSP lowered its foreign portfolio investments projection to a net inflow of US$2.4bn, from an original target of US$8.2bn. 

Inflation creeps up to 2.7% in July. Headline inflation increased to 2.7% in July, up from 2.5% in June. This is within BSP’s expected range of 2.2-3.0%. Inflation driven by transport (due to limited public transportation), alcohol and tobacco. Food inflation (fish, fruits, vegetables), however, eased further, more than offsetting meat inflation. Core inflation was higher at 3.3% YoY in July (vs. 3.0% in June 2020).

BSP keeps benchmark rates steady; Inflation forecast raised. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas maintained the overnight reverse repurchase rate at an all-time low of 2.25%. They cited manageable inflation and improvement in domestic economic activity post-lockdown backed a “prudent pause”.  The BSP also upgraded its 2020 inflation forecast to 2.6% from 2.3% in June. The  2021 and 2022 forecasts already raised to 3% (vs. 2.6% previously) and 3.1% (3.0%), respectively.

BOP surplus at US$80mn in June. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$80mn in June. This is a reversal from the US$404mn BOP deficit in the same period last year. However, this was smaller than the US$2.4bn surplus in May 2020. This brings 1H20 BOP surplus to US$4.11bn, lower than the US$4.79bn surplus a year ago.

GIR level surges to US$98bn in July. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by 2% (US$4.5bn) to US$98bn as of end-July 2020.The MoM increase reflected inflows mainly from the revaluation gains from the BSP’s gold holdings (+US$4.6bn MoM). The end-July 2020 GIR level represents an ample external liquidity buffer which can cushion the domestic economy against external shocks. This is equivalent to 8.9 months’ worth of imports.

 

Remittances rebound strongly in June. Remittances jumped by 7.7% YoY to US$2.47bn in June, reversing three months of negative growth. This is close to the 2019 average monthly remittances of US$2.5bn. In PHP terms, remittances in June rose 4.1% YoY. There was a rebound seen in remittances from the USA (+25%), Asia (+25%), Middle East (+5%) while Sea-based remittances continued to decline (-13%). This brings 1H20 remittances to US$14bn, down 4.2% YoY (in PHP terms, -7% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 5% in FY20.

Fiscal deficit swells to P140bn in July. The country recorded a budget deficit of P140bn in July, up 86% YoY. Revenues dropped 11% YoY to P235bn, as collections from the BIR and BOC dropped 12% and 9% respectively. Meanwhile, expenses rose 10.4% YoY due to the second tranche releases for the social amelioration program. This brings 7M20 fiscal deficit to P700.6bn (vs. govt’s FY20 target of P1.8tn deficit, 9.6% of GDP). Revenues in 7M20 are down by 7% YoY to P1.69tn, accounting for 67% of the govt’s target (P2.5tn, -20% YoY). 7M20 expenditures up by 24% YoY to P2.39tn, accounting for 55% of FY20 target (P4.34tn, +14% YoY).

Gov’t expands 2021 budget proposal to P4.506tn. The proposed 2021 budget was increased to P4.506 tn from the initial P4.357 tn estimate according to Finance Undersecretary Gil Beltran. The new budget proposal accounts for 21.8% of GDP. It is 9.9% higher than the P4.1 trillion budget for 2020 and is also 6.6% higher than the 2020 expenditure target of P4.225 tn. The increased budget reflects expectations of a vaccine by end-2020 (vs previous expectation of Aug/Sep 2020). The spending plan will “sustain” government initiatives for COVID-19 pandemic response. 

Government revises fiscal targets. The government revised its fiscal targets, targeting larger budget deficits from 2020-2022. For FY20, the government is targeting a deficit of P1.8 tn, equivalent to 9.6% of GDP (vs. previous deficit-to-GDP target of 8.4%). <eanwhile, for FY21 and FY22, the gov’t is targeting deficit-to-GDP of 8.5% and 7.2%, respectively (vs. 6.6% and 5.0%, previously). Additionally, the government tweaked its infrastructure outlay targets (i.e. includes NG infrastructure, infra subsidies to GOCCs, LGU transfers). 

Infra spend jumps 44% YoY, 61% MoM in June.  Infra spend (excluding subsidies to GOCCs/transfers to LGUs) rose to P62.8bn in June, up 44% YoY and 61% MoM. They cited various nationwide projects of the Dept. of Public Works, the military, and Dept. of Education. This brings 1H20 infra spend to P297.9bn, down 4.3% YoY. The Gov’t lowered its FY20 infra spend target (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. This may suggest that comparable infra spend target for FY20 (ex. subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) may have been lowered to P660bn (vs. P943bn, +6% YoY target, pre-COVID).

DOF expects P249bn revenue loss over three years due to Tax Package 2. The Department of Finance estimates that from 2020-2022, Tax Package 2 will lead to P249bn in lower tax collections, largely due to the immediate five percentage point reduction in the corporate income tax rate. Tax Package 2, which is also called Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act or CREATE, seeks to lower the corporate income tax rate from 30% to 25% and extend the applicability of net operating loss carry over (NOLCO) from 3 years to 5 years. Additionally, it also plans to extend the sunset period for companies currently enjoying tax incentives.

 

Bicam approves Bayanihan II of up to P165bn. The Bicameral Conference Committee approved the Bayanihan II bill. The Bill will provide P140bn in regular appropriations and P25bn in standby appropriations, if the funds become available. Bayanihan II retained the P5-8k cash assistance to low-income households, but it will now be limited to areas under “specific quarantine conditions” and to households will recently-returned OFWs. A 60-day grace period for the payment of all "existing, current, and outstanding loans," falling due on or before December 31, 2020, irrespective if there is community quarantine or not, will be implemented. Meanwhile, payments of residential and commercial rents, and utility bills falling due within the period of ECQ or Modified ECQ shall also be covered by a minimum of 30-day grace period. Once passed into law, Bayanihan II will be effective until Dec 19, 2020.

Domestic liquidity growth eases to 14.9% in June. Domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 14.9% in June to P13.6 tn (-1.1% MoM). This was slower than the 16.7% expansion in May. 

Metro Manila, 4 provinces back to less restrictive GCQ. President Duterte announced last night that Metro Manila and the provinces of Laguna, Cavite, Rizal, and Bulacan, will revert to the less restrictive lockdown (“GCQ”) by August 19. The areas are estimated to account for two thirds of GDP. Other areas placed under GCQ are Nueva Ecija, Batangas, Quezon in Luzon; Iloilo City, Cebu City, Lapu Lapu City, Mandaue City, Talisay City, the municipalities of Minglanilla and Consolacion in Cebu province in the Visayas.  The rest of the country were placed under the most lenient quarantine (“MGCQ”).

June UKB loan growth slows to 9.6%; total loans probably closer to 5%. The BSP reported that loans of Universal and Commercial Banks (UKBs) grew 9.6% yoy, slower than the 11.3% recorded in May. However, as we have noted in the past, the UKB data is bloated by the mergers of RCBC Savings into parent RCBC in July 2019, and of PNB Savings into PNB in March 2020. The BSP's UKB data only covers the parent company, and not its thrift/rural bank subsidiaries. In April and May, the BSP reported UKB loan growth rates of 12.7% and 11.3% respectively. However, the data for all banks (Philippine Banking System, or PBS) showed loan growth of just 7.4% and 7.3% respectively. This is why we tend to look at the (PBS) data. While it is slightly delayed, it is not subject to this kind of "noise".

 

Vehicle sales recover by 32% MoM in July (-35% YoY); Auto industry seeks help. CAMPI and TMA reported industry vehicle sales fell 35% YoY to 20,542 units (+32% MoM). This brings YT-July vehicle sales to 105,583 units, down 49% YoY. The CAMPI/TMA forecasts industry car sales will reach 240k units (-41.5% YoY). The volume reduction has serious operational and financial impact on the automotive industry. There have been submitted recommendations for industry support to the Dept. of Trade and Industry however, recommendations were not disclosed. Toyota remained the market leader in 7M20 at 42.1% market share, selling 44,481 units in 7M20 (-49.2% YoY). This implies July 2020 sales stood at 8,833 units (-37% YoY, +18% MoM).

PSE raises minimum public ownership requirement for small IPOs. The PSE now requires the following minimum public ownership (MPO) for IPOs.

  • Market cap below P500m – 33%
  • Market cap from P500m to P1bn – 25%
  • Market cap above P1bn – 20% 

The company must maintain the MPO of 20% even after listing. The 20% requirement also applies to companies that list by way of introduction or backdoor listing. Since 2017, the PSE has required all IPOs to have a minimum public float of 20%.
