REGIS REPORT

01 October 2020
 

The Philippines drifted down 0.34% in September, in a month of uninspired trading. Index losers outnumbered gainers 16:13 amidst a lack of market catalysts. 

Domestically, investors awaited a possible shift to even loser lockdown restrictions, but the President announced that Metro Manila and a few other areas would remain under the current lockdown levels (general community quarantine or GCQ) for the entire month of October. Nonetheless, the total number of active cases has been declining, with active cases at the end of the month at 53,000 from 59,700 at the end of August. This, along with improving hospital occupancy numbers, suggest that the spread of COVID-19 is being contained.

Prospects of re-opening helped propel companies that would benefit from a resumption in mobility (and economic activity) such as casino operator Bloomberry and QSR Jollibee. Rotation from lockdown outperformers PLDT and Puregold also ensued with both leading the drops during the month. Deep value bombed out names Metrobank, Alliance Global and LT Group also populated the leader board in September. 

Financials were in focus during the month. 

For the property sector, POGOs were of chief concern as signs of stress have been emerging given a China clampdown, recently increased POGO  fees as well as travel bans. Of the 60 POGO licenses, five have already been canceled while five have been suspended. And of the remaining 50, only 32 are currently operating. POGOs account for roughly 11% of Metro Manila office space and mass closures of POGOs would have huge negative consequences beyond just office rents.

Nevertheless, prospects of a return of economic activity helped property cos stay buoyant. Developments on the residential segment was encouraging, with Ayala Land revealing that July and August reservation sales have reached 60-70% of pre-COVID (2019 monthly ave) levels.

Concern also turned to the banks.  The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has set an interest rate cap of 24% on unpaid credit card balances, compared to the current rate of around 42%. It has also capped rates on installment loans, and fees on cash advances. Regis estimates the new limits could cut revenues by 3-5%, and profit by 6-10% for banks under coverage. Of the banks under Regis estimates, credit card receivables account for 3.4% to 5.8% of total loans.

Beyond the near-term profit impact, the latest move sets a major precedent for more regulation of rates and or fees for the banks. This is the first time the BSP has explicitly set rate limits.  Before this amendment, the Manual of Regulations for Bank (MORB) read, “The rate of interest, including commissions, premiums, fees and other charges... shall not be subject to any regulatory ceiling”.

Key data
 
FLOW UPDATE

Foreigners remained one-sided this month, with foreign outflows seen in 20 out of 21 trading days in September.

Net foreign selling accelerated further to US$306m in September – the largest monthly outflow since April (early stages of the pandemic/lockdown) despite average daily turnover falling 33% MoM to US$109m and foreigners only 45% of volume (vs YTD of 50%).

Headline net foreign outflows were already heavy but net selling would have even been larger had it not been for more block crosses in SM and SMPH which collectively brought in US$128m in net inflows. Excluding these, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$438m.

 
 
 

Year to date foreign outflow stood at US$2.017bn – already the largest annual outflow ever recorded with one entire quarter remaining.

 
 
 

Outflows were seen across the board with big-cap, liquid banks, conglomerates, and property bearing the brunt.

 
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Rotation from defensives to reopening plays was apparent in September.   Recent outperformers gave way to bombed out names with perceived deep value.

 

PLDT (-9.5% MoM), along with Puregold (-9.3%) took a backseat to recovery playsBloomberry (+19.4% MoM) and Jolllibee (+7.3% MoM).   
“Deep value”  names Metrobank (+14.0% MoM), Alliance Global (+13.3% MoM) and LT Group (+6.9% MoM) also outperformed this month.

 

Much of the focus in September was in the financials.

Banks grappled with deteriorating asset quality. NPLs climbed to 2.75% and PD ratios jumping to 5.28% in July reflecting the stress that the pandemic and lockdowns have had on borrowers.

Meanwhile, consumer loan NPLs jumped 109bps QoQ to 5.5% in 2Q20. Consumer loans accounted for c85% of new NPLs in 2Q, with the deterioration even more severe in mortgages (+200bps QoQ) and autos (+87bps QoQ).

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has set an interest rate cap of 24% on unpaid credit card balances (compared to the current rate of around 42%). It has also capped rates on installment loans, and fees on cash advances. Regis estimates the new limits could cut revenues by 3-5%, and profit by 6-10% for banks under coverage. Of the banks under Regis coverage, credit card receivables account for 3.4% to 5.8% of total loans.  East West’s (+1.3% MoM) credit card receivables are at 13.5% of total loans (2019A).

Beyond the near-term profit impact, this latest move sets a major precedent for more regulation of rates and or fees for the banks. This is the first time the BSP has explicitly set rate/fee limits.  Before this amendment, the Manual of Regulations for Bank (MORB) read, “The rate of interest, including commissions, premiums, fees and other charges... shall not be subject to any regulatory ceiling”.  

Banks were mixed though, with previously sold down Metrobank (+14.0% MoM) gaining significantly.  BDO (+0.3% MoM) was flat, while Security Bank (-5.0% MoM) and BPI (-4.5% MoM ) declined.

Philippine National Bank (+19.0% MoM) surged, however.  The bank’s board approved “a plan to realize the market value of the its prime properties”.  No further details were released and it is unclear whether the properties will be sold outright, developed internally/in JV, etc.  Regis estimates the PNB head office land alone has a market value of at least P20bn, accounting for a large chunk of market cap (~P36.5bn)

For the property sector, POGOs were of chief concern as signs of stress have been emerging given a China clampdown, recently increased POGO  fees as well as travel bans. Of the 60 POGO licenses, five have already been canceled while five have been suspended. And of the remaining 50, only 32 are currently operating In the meantime, according to regulator PAGCOR, POGO fees are down by 50%. If not for a minimum guaranteed fee, POGO fees would have been even lower.

Megaworld (+1.7% MoM) and Filinvest Land  (-3.2% MoM ) are the most directly exposed to POGOs.  POGOs account for roughly 11% of Metro Manila office space and mass closures of POGOs would have huge negative consequences beyond just office rents.

Nevertheless, prospects of a return of economic activity helped property share prices stay buoyant. Developments on the residential segment was encouraging, with Ayala Land(+3.1% MoM)  revealing that July and August reservation sales have reached 60-70% of pre-COVID (2019 monthly ave) levels.

Sold down Vista Land (+12.9% MoM) rose sharplys.  Vista Land said that its reservation sales reached 70% of pre-COVID levels in June and improved further in July and August. The company cited demand for residential projects outside Metro Manila.

SM Prime (+0.7% MoM) was steady in September.

 

Recovery optimism propelled Bloomberry (+19.4% MoM) to the top index gainer spot in September. Bloomberry confirmed that it has been allowed to go up to 30% of capacity, but will only serve invited guests and patrons during this trial run. Under previous IATF guidelines, gambling activities are allowed to run at 50% under MCGQ.

ICTSI (+4.5% MoM) continued to outperform, with the prospects of normalizing trade boding well for a recovery in its volumes.

 

Cebu Air (+5.9% MoM) also climbed, after collapsing 60% in the first eight months of the year.  The financial assistance coming from the government however seems to fall short of the requirements of the airline sector. The Air Carriers Association of the Philippines (ACAP) said about P700m had been allocated for local airlines under Republic Act 11494 (i.e. Bayanihan II). Each airline is expected to receive around P140m loan assistance under the economic stimulus package. For Cebu Air, its cash burn per month alone is c. P2bn per month.

 

The consumer sector was mixed. Reopening optimism fueled Jollibee (+7.3% MoM) while lockdown outperformer Puregold (-9.3% MoM) corrected.  Puregold raised P12bn in fixed rate corporate notes (P7bn 7-year notes at 4%, P5bn 10-year notes at 4.513%). Proceeds will be used for business expansion though looks like an opportunistic exercise; Puregold had P17bn in cash as of 1H20.

Universal Robina (-2.8% MoM) declined after the rising 12.6% in the previous month. The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) has approved URC’s acquisition of ROX’s sugar milling, bioethanol plant, and other assets of Roxas Holdings' facility in La Carlota City, Negros Occidental. The acquisition price was not disclosed but the facilities will add c.42% to URC’s total rated milling capacity. URC's sugar and renewables business (SURE) accounted for 12% of sales and 23% of EBIT in 1H20.

Power stocks also consolidated. Aboitiz Power (-4.1% MoM) dropped.  First Gen (-4.1% MoM) likewise fell.  The 414MW San Gabriel plant tripped, leaving it unavailable in the meantime.  San Gabriel is fully contracted with Meralco and has around 11 of 65 days downtime allowed annually. San Gabriel is part of First Gen’s 2,000MW natural gas-fired capacity.

Meralco (+0.7% MoM) was flat. The distribution utility has been ordered not to charge lifeline customers for distribution, supply and metering for one month and also imposed a P19m fine. The order was a result of complaints regarding the billing issues of Meralco during the lockdown. Meralco estimates that order could cost it over P200m. The company says it will comply with the order but seek reconsideration from the ERC.

Telcos corrected as flows rotated out of the sector. PLDT (-9.5% MoM) fell significantly in the wake of its significant market outperformance. According to reports, the company was making a bid to acquire Sky Cable, the pay TV operation owned by ABS-CBN (ABSP, -2.5% MoM). It was reported shortly after that PLDT had decided to drop any such plans. Sky is known primarily as a pay TV business but it also has a broadband operation with over 300,000 subscribers. It’s not clear if the mulled transaction would have involved the broadband business as well. The media report/s suggested a price of P15bn to P18bn.

 

Conglos were mixed. 

JG Summit (-6.4% MoM) dropped. A fire broke out at JG Summit’s petrochemical facility but the damage does not appear to be serious. JG Summit Petrochem accounts for about 13% of market-based NAV.

Ayala Corp (-6.1% MoM) also fell.

UAC Energy Holdings, owned by AC Energy Group and UPC Renewable, divested its 20% stake in Infigen Energy to Spain’s Iberdrola Group at AUD0.92 per share. UAC acquired its stake in Infigen over several months from April to July 2020 at an average price of AUD$0.794 per share. Regis estimates UAC made a pretax gain of around AUD24m on the trade. 

Meanwhile, the listing of VIA Optronics, the IMI (+0.9% MoM) subsidiary which makes display panels, is a welcome bit of good news for the latter despite the precipitous fall in the former’s share price on listing day. IMI now has paper gains of over US$60m from its investment in VIAO, which has been losing money since IMI purchased it in 2016.

GT Capital’s (-3.4% MoM) Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) announced it return to two-shift production, signaling normalization of operations. In August, Toyota sold 8,044 units, down 39% YoY and 9% MoM, bringing  their YT-August sales to 52,525 units, down 48% YoY. Regis forecasts FY20 car sales will drop by 44% to 90,372 units.

The House of Representatives approved on final reading a bill that will grant San Miguel Aerocity, Inc. a 50-year franchise over the Bulacan airport project. The franchise will be in effect for 50 years from the effectivity of the act, inclusive of a 10-year maximum period for the design, planning, and construction of the airport and the airport city. On August 27, the House ways and means committee approved a separate bill (House Bill 7241) that will exempt the grantee from taxes during the 10-year construction period. 

 

In related news, Megawide (+14.2% MoM) appears to be catching a break,  as it seems the  government will sweeten the terms of the NAIA rehabilitation project to make sure the project proceeds despite the pandemic. The government is open to extend the contract validity of the NAIA rehab project to 25-30 years from the previously approved 15 years, among other things. The government is currently in discussions with the consortium of Megawide and India-based GMR for the project.

Udenna, the holding company of the Davao Dennis Uy, is signaling that it could exercise its preferential right to acquire the 45% stake in the Malampaya service contract that oil major Shell (-8.8% MoM)  is planning to sell. This comes after a few parties, including San Miguel  and the group of Manuel Pangilinan, expressed interest in acquiring the 45% from Shell.

Uy’s UC Malampaya Philippines already holds 45% of the project while 10% belongs to PNOC.

 

 
 
 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation moderates to 2.4% in August. Inflation slowed down to 2.4% in August from 2.7% in July. This was below the BSP’s expected range of 2.5-3.3% for the month. The government cited slower inflation from food items (meat, fruits, milk, cheese, eggs, and fish), of which rice inflation (9.59% weight) still at negative 1.1%. Meanwhile, core inflation also eased to 3.1% in August from 3.3% in July. This means YT-August inflation rate stood at 2.5%, within government’s target range of 2-4%.

OFW remittances up 7.8% YoY, 12.9% MoM to US$2.78bn in July. Remittances rose for the second consecutive month, increasing by 7.8% YoY and 12.9% MoM to US$2.783bn in July. Land-based remittances rose 12.6% YoY and offset the 9.2% drop in sea-based remittances. Meanwhile, remittances from USA and Asia rose 25% and 23%, respectively. In PHP terms, remittances rose 4.3% YoY and 11% MoM in July. This brings 7M20 remittances down 2.4% YoY to US$16.8bn (in PHP terms, down 5.4% YoY). The BSP is forecasting remittances to drop by 5% in FY20.

PH records P40.1bn budget deficit in August. The National Government’s budget deficit swelled to P40.1bn in August. Revenues dropped 13% YoY to P243bn, as collections from the BIR and BOC dropped 9% and 17%, respectively. Expenditures were slightly higher (+0.4%) due to a 15% jump in interest expenses and the release of subsidies to PhilHealth. This brings YT-August budget deficit to P740.7bn, accounting for 41% of the government’s FY20E deficit target of P1.815tn (9.4% of GDP). The revenues in 8M20 reached P1.93tn, down 8% YoY and represents 77% of FY20E while the expenditures in 8M20 are up 21% YoY to P2.67tn and represents 62% of FY20E.

President Duterte signs P165.5bn pandemic stimulus package into law.President Duterte signed the new Bayanihan to Recover as One Act (a.k.a. Bayanihan II) into law, 18 days after Congress ratified the bill. The law provides a P165.5bn COVID-19 relief package, of which P140bn will be sourced from general appropriations while P25.5bn will serve as a standby authority should additional resources become available. The law also grants a 30-day grace period for payments of residential rents and utility bills, falling within the enhanced community quarantine (ECQ) or modified ECQ without incurring interests, penalties and other charges. It also grants a 60-day grace period on all loans, regardless of community quarantine status. Additionally, it will also raise P17.5bn in additional revenues from Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGO), after imposing a 5% franchise tax. The Bayanihan II will be effective until December 19, 2020.

 

BIR, BOC exceed collection targets in August. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) generated P172.06bn in August, down 16.3% YoY but 45.6% higher than the government’s revised target of P118.2bn for the month. This brings 8M20 collections to P1.29tn, down 11% YoY and accounting for 74% of the government’s FY20 target (P1.74tn, -20% YoY). Meanwhile, the Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P44.65bn in August, down 16.7% YoY but 32.6% higher than target of P33.68bn. This brings 8M20 collections to P348bn, down 16% YoY and accounting for 64% of FY20 target (P542bn, -14% YoY).

GIR climbs to US$98.95bn in August. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) level, based on preliminary data, rose by US$350mn MoM to US$98.95bn as of end-August 2020. This reflects inflows mainly from the BSP’s foreign exchange operations and income from its investments abroad and was partly offset, by the National Government’s debt payments and revaluation losses from the BSP’s gold holdings. The GIR now is equivalent to 9 months’ worth of imports.

Dollar reserves seen to top US$100bn. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said it was likely for the gross International reserves (GIR) to top the US$100bn mark before the end of the year. The BSP plans to actively participate in the gold trading market largely because of the change in the price dynamics of gold. The GIR stood at a record high of US$98.6bn in July due to the national government’s foreign borrowings to finance its COVID-19 response. The GIR is equivalent 8.9 months of imports which was higher than the GIR level of US$87.8bn in end-2019.

Foreign direct investments continue to rise in June. Foreign direct investment (FDI) net inflows sustained its growth momentum, rising by 7.1% to US$481mn in June 2020. Net equity capital investments expanded to US$173 million on the back of a 138% YoY increase in equity capital placements to US$185mn and the decline in withdrawals. Bulk of equity capital placements originated from Japan, the UK, and the USA. By economic activity, these equity placements were invested in 1) manufacturing, 2) human health and social work, 3) financial and insurance, and (4) real estate. The net investments in debt instruments in June 2020 dropped by 28.8% to US$229mn. Meanwhile, reinvestment of earnings was also lower by 19.4% at US$80mn. The cumulative net FDI inflows stood at US$3bn, down 18.3% YoY.

 

BSP may remain accommodative for at least two years. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) may maintain the low interest rate environment in the next two years to provide support to the economy according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. The BSP has taken a deliberate pause in order to gauge how the market took in the estimated P1.4tn liquidity support (~7.3% of GDP). The BSP kept policy rates unchanged last Aug 20 and the BSP’s next policy meeting is set on Oct 1.

Gov’t sells P30bn 3-year bond at a coupon of 2.375%. The Bureau of Treasury raised P30bn in 3-year T-bonds. The offer was more than 3x oversubscribed with total tenders reaching P87.963bn. The bond fetched a coupon rate of 2.375% and an average yield of 2.279%. The coupon was lower than the 4.75% in the July 2019 auction. For comparison BVAL was around 2.380%.

BSP issuing its own securities. The Bangko Sentral announced it will be issuing its own securities on Sept 18. The plan is to issue P20bn worth of 28-day BSP bills with the amount to be gradually increased over time. The new BSP charter finally allowed the BSP to issue its own securities, in line with shift to more market-based monetary policy.

Infrastructure spending drops in July. National government infrastructure spending (excluding subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) dropped 31% YoY and 17% MoM to P52bn in July. The government cited lower spending from the DPWH due to the gradual implementation of public works and healthy protocols in place. This brings YT-July infrastructure spend to P350bn, down 9.4% YoY. Last July, the government lowered its infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) to P785bn, down 25% YoY. For FY21, the infrastructure budget (including subsidies to GOCCs and LGU transfers) is supposed to increase by 43% YoY to P1.12tn. This target is c. 7% higher than what was spent in 2019 for infrastructure (P1.05tn).

Gov’t willing to ease NAIA rehab terms. The government is open to extend the contract validity of the NAIA rehab project to 25-30 years from the previously approved 15 years according to presidential adviser for flagship programs and projects Vivencio B. Dizon. The government is also open to deferring its revenue share while the air sector tries to get back on its feet. The government is currently in discussions with the consortium of Megawide (MWIDE PM) and India-based GMR for the project. The NAIA rehab project is estimated to cost P100bn.

 

 

oan growth slows to 6.7% in July; domestic liquidity (M3) posts mildly slower growth of 14.5% in July. Bank lending of universal and commercial banks, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements grew by 6.7% in July. This is slower than the 9.6% loan growth in June. The reason cited for this was weaker corporate sector performance and declining loan demand. Additionally, the loan growth to most sectors slowed. Credit card loans grew by 27%  YoY, broadly steady vs. 28% in June while auto loan growth sharply slowed to 9.6% YoY from 29% in June. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 14.5% to P13.6tn in July. This is broadly steady from 14.9% in June 2020.

Past due ratio spikes to 5.3% in July; NPL ratio at 2.75%. The past due (PD) ratios jumped to 5.28% in July, compared to 3.49% in June and to 2.71% at end-2019. In nominal terms, PD loans surged 89% YoY to P573bn. The spike comes as the loan grace periods under Bayanihan Act 1 expired on 31 May. Meanwhile, NPL ratios also rose to 2.75% in July, from 2.60% in June and 2.08% at end-2019. To the upside, banks used the massive trading gains to accelerate loan loss provisions in 1H20. As a result, NPL cover improved to 111% and loan loss reserves (LLR) to total loans rose to 2.96%. The PD and NPL ratios are exacerbated somewhat by the sharp slowdown in loan growth. As of July, loans of the Philippine Banking System (PBS) – including thrift and rural banks – decelerated further to 4.8% YoY, from 5.2% in June, and 10.8% in March. Loans were flat (-0.2%) mom, and down 2.55% year-to-date.

Senate warned of slow recovery if bad loan relief bill is delayed. Economic managers pressed the Senate on the urgency of passing the Financial Institutions Strategic Transfer (FIST) bill. The bill aims to create asset management companies that will buy out unpaid loans from lenders at a discount. The also cited that delays in passing the 2002 Special Purpose Vehicle Law hampered the economy’s recovery from the Asian Financial Crisis of 1997. The FIST bill has passed the Lower House but the Senate version is still being evaluated by the chamber’s Committee on Banks and Financial Intermediaries. Senator Ralph Recto highlighted exposing the government from potential risks on purchasing bad loans. Meanwhile, Senate Minority Leader Franklin Drilon raised funding concerns for a bad-loan rescue scheme.

 

BSP sets cap on credit card charges. The BSP approved an annual interest rate cap of 24% on credit card transactions to ease the financial burden brought about by the COVID pandemic. The implementation will start on November 3 but will be reviewed by the BSP every six months. The BSP also set the limit for monthly add-on rates for credit card installment loans at 1%. Additionally, credit card issuers also cannot impose other charges on credit card cash advances except for a maximum processing fee of P200/transaction. In 1H20, the annualized interest rates on credit card receivables ranged from 18% to 58% with an average of 26%.

More POGOs closing. Finance Secretary Dominguez said that more POGOs were shutting down due to weak demand and the Chinese government’s clampdown on money transfers. We believe that travel bans preventing mainland Chinese employees from entering the Philippines are also a major reason for lower POGO operations. On September 22, a building owner in Makati informed Secretary Dominguez that POGOs and their service providers have started canceling their lease contracts. PAGCOR reported that there were 55 registered POGOs as of September 8, from 60 as of end-2019. 29 of the POGOs are authorized to resume operations while 99 out of 218 service providers are permitted to resume operations. In 2019, we estimate that the POGO industry (including service providers) occupied about 1.3m sqm of office space in Metro Manila and likely employed 300,000-400,000 mainland Chinese.

PAGCOR online gaming revenues cut in half. Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) AVP for Offshore Gaming Licensing Jose Tria said that monthly income from POGOs is down by almost half from cP600mn/month pre-Covid. He added that the fees should have been lower if not for the minimum guaranteed fee. Meanwhile, according to Tria, only 32 out of 60 POGOs were allowed to resume operations at 30% capacity. Around 111 out 218 accredited POGO service providers were also given clearance to operate. Five POGO licenses were already canceled while another five were suspended. Additionally, 42 service providers have requested the cancelation of their accreditation.

 

Gov’t allows use of rapid antigen tests for domestic travel. The government allowed the use of rapid antigen test as a substitute for RT-PCR test as a requirement for domestic travel. The Inter-Agency Task Force (IATF) for the management of emerging infectious diseases issued Resolution No. 69 last September 7.

Public transport improving (slowly). The government seems to be slowly opening mass transit in Metro Manila. By Monday, 14 September, the LFRB will be allowing 17,372 jeepneys  (c32% of pre-Covid levels) to ply their routes again. Also the IATF has allowed a gradual easing of distancing rules inside public transportation vehicles. The first phase runs for 2 weeks, also starting 14 September. We understand that the new IATF rules would bring train/LRT/MRT capacity initially up to 20%, then eventually to 50% by mid-October. It is unclear how the new rules will affect jeepneys and buses currently already running at 50% passenger capacity. Improving public transportation access – gradual as it may be – is critical to reopening the economy. We expect malls, retailers and restaurants – particularly those catering to the mass and middle-income segments – to benefit.

 

Metro Manila provincial bus operations may resume within the month. The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) is preparing for the reopening of some provincial bus routes originating from and ending in the National Capital Region (NCR). The government plans to start with provinces near Metro Manila, such as regions III and IV. The LTFRB is currently in talks with local governments to come up with a uniform set of requirements for passengers to prove they can cross provincial borders (i.e. travel clearance, police clearance, gov’t-issued ID). Meanwhile, some provincial governments have agreed to reopen their borders to accommodate cross-province public transportation. Out of 81 provinces nationwide, at least three have initially agreed to accommodate cross-border travel from Metro Manila, including Antique, Quirino and Bataan.
