REGIS REPORT

30 June 2025
 

Philippine equities traded sideways through June as investors navigated a mix of positive inflation data, supportive monetary policy moves, and rising external risks, along with flows related to index rebalancing changes and speculation. The PCOMP finished the month higher by 0.4% MoM to 6,364.94.

 

On macro, the biggest driver was the sustained decline in inflation, down to 1.3% in May. This took YTD-May inflation to 1.9%, which is already below the BSP’s 2-4% target range and well under full-year Street forecasts. Along with currency stability earlier in the month, this helped pave the way for the BSP’s widely-expected 25-bp policy rate cut at its mid-June meeting.

 

However, global volatility also partly weighed on the monetary easing narrative, particularly with the sharp spike in global oil prices mid-month triggered by renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Given the Philippines' vulnerability as net energy importer, the peso sharply weakened late in the month, ending 1.0% lower MoM to 56.322:$1.

 

The property sector was a clear outperformer in June, lifted mostly by ALI (+17.4% MoM) which was the top positive mover for the PCOMP and the second biggest advancer for the month. Beyond the BSP's policy easing, more aggressive Fed rate cut expectations toward month-end helped support shares of the big developers. MEG (+10.1% MoM) followed suit as did RLC (+4.1% MoM) and SMPH (+4.0% MoM).

 

In contrast, the rates view saw rotation out of the banking sector which was the largest underperformer this month. While the decline in BPI (-6.5% MoM) and BDO (-5.1% MoM) weighed down the index the most, CBC (-10.1% MoM) was the biggest loser in the PCOMP as it is likely to be deleted from the benchmark should the PSE adopt a freshly proposed index methodology in August.

 

Elsewhere, gaming names saw sharp gains in early June reverse in the latter half of the month following news of a potential Senate investigation into the proliferation of online gambling. BLOOM (+22.2% MoM) which had more than doubled from its April low shed its monthly advance by half, while PLUS (-1.6% MoM) completely erased its gains for the month after hitting fresh ATHs.

 

Foreign outflows extended to a third straight month in June, hitting $72m due in part to FTSE rebalancing outflows with estimates ranging between $80-100m (though net outflow stood at $15m on rebal day). ADTO for the month moderated to $122m.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

18 Advancers outnumbered 11 decliners (1 unch.) in the PCOMP in June.

 

Among sector indices, Property (+8.2% MoM) outperformed while Banks (-4.9% MoM) lagged as investors acted on rate expectations. The BSP's 25-bp rate cut in June along with Fed easing forecasts turning more aggressive late in the month led to the thematic rotation. ALI (+17.4% MoM) and SMPH (+4.0% MoM) were two of the three top positive movers for the PCOMP while BPI (-6.5% MoM) and BDO (-5.1% MoM) were the biggest drags.

 

Outside of the rates theme, CBC (-10.1% MoM) also dragged Financials as a newly proposed index methodology by the PSE is seen to result in its deletion from the benchmark, should the changes be adopted in the next rebalancing in August. Speculation on potential index inclusions from these changes also led to the sharp advances in AP (+16.9% MoM) and RCR (+12.8% MoM).

 

Meanwhile, gaming stocks were actively traded in the month. BLOOM (+22.2% MoM) saw sharp gains in early June following the much-awaited launch of its online gaming platform, and PLUS (-1.6% MoM) rode on the online gaming frenzy to hit fresh ATHs. However, BLOOM's monthly gains were halved and PLUS' wiped out after Sen. Cayetano, one of the co-authors of the recently-passed Anti-POGO Act, called for a Senate investigation into the proliferation of online gambling in the country.

 

Consumer names saw mixed performance thanks to mixed macro and company-specific drivers. Falling inflation benefited staples retailer PGOLD (+16.1% MoM) while the approval of a P50/day (c.8%) minimum wage hike in the National Capital Region (NCR) at month-end weighed down JFC (-3.1% MoM). Despite news of a worsening pest infestation in a key sugar-producing province, URC (+6.0% MoM) managed to end higher for the month. A clear stand-out was WLCON (+46.1% MoM) that rebounded strongly following its deletion at the MSCI May rebalancing and continued to rally amid the monetary easing view.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP trims policy rate by 25bp, signals one more cut and easing inflation ahead. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board cut key policy rates by 25bp to 5.25%. BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that they will probably cut one more time this year if things "stay on track" and depending the data. The BSP policy rate has been reduced by a total of 125bp from 6.50% since the easing cycle started back in Aug 2024. 

Adjustments were also made to the BSP's inflation forecasts, with 2025 inflation projected to average 1.6% from 2.4% previously. Upward revisions (+10bp) were made for 2026 and 2027 inflation projections to 3.4% and 3.3%, respectively. The revisions were made considering heightened risks to inflation from geopolitical tensions and US trade policy uncertainty.

Remolona elaborated that the BSP may need to intervene more seriously if the peso depreciation continues further given the possible impact on inflation. The USD-PHP exchange rate has slumped to P57.4 since P55.7 at end-May.

The next Monetary Board meeting (4th out of 6 this year) will be on Aug 28. For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps total worth of cuts to 5.00%.

May inflation down further to 1.3%. CPI inflation for May dipped further to 1.3% from the 1.4% print for April. The lower headline inflation figure is being led declining commodity prices. Rice prices were again down MoM (-2.4%) as were energy-linked costs (e.g., electricity, transportation). This CPI outcome was within Street expectations, and takes YTD-May inflation down to 1.9%, already below the BSP's 2-4% target range.

OFW Remittances up 4.0% YoY in April. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 4.0% YoY to US$2.66bn, accelerating from the 2.6% growth seen in March. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 3.8%.

Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth accelerated to 3.8% YoY from 1.0% in Mar.

BOP posts US$298mn deficit in May; May GIR settles at US$105.2bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$298mn. A reversal from the US$2.0bn surplus in May 2024 but narrower than Apr 2025's US$2.6bn deficit.

The deficit reflected the national gov't's drawdowns on the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits for external debt obligations. This brings the YT-May deficit at US$5.8bn, reversing from the US$1.6bn surplus for the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade gap, higher net outflows in the financial account, and front-loaded external debt repayments.

The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$4bn deficit (-0.8% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments.

Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-May stood at US$105.2bn, down slightly from the US$105.3bn in end-Apr. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.1 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025.

PH current account deficit balloons to $4.2bn in 1Q25. The widening deficit was driven by the Philippines' wider merchandise trade gap and slower services trade. The trade-in-goods deficit was wider 15% YoY to US$16.8bn, given the faster growth in imports (+8%) vs. exports (+1.2%). Net receipts from services trade fell 9% YoY to US$3.3bn, as imports grew 1.7% while exports slowed 1.5%.

The decline in services receipts was primarily due to lower earnings from transport services (from US$1.1bn to US$755mn) and technical, trade-related, other business services (from US$5.5bn to US$5.4bn). This was offset by the growth in BPO export revenues which grew 1.3% to US$7.2bn from US$7.1bn last year.

For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to hit US$19.8bn or -3.9% of GDP. This was revised earlier in the year from the previous US$12.1bn CA deficit (2.4% of GDP) projection back in 4Q24. Recall that in 2024, the country posted its second largest CA deficit at US$17.5bn (3.8% of GDP)—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of US$12.4bn (2.8% of GDP).

 

Government posts P145bn budget deficit in May. The government recorded a budget deficit of P145.2bn in May, which is narrower by 17% YoY and a reversal from the P67bn surplus in April. The lower deficit was due to higher growth (+13% YoY) in revenue collections and slower expenditure growth (+4% YoY) given the election ban.

Revenue collections in May increased 13% YoY to P433.1bn, driven by higher tax receipts from the BIR. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 11% YoY to P242.7bn mainly due to higher corporate income tax collections as well as increased personal income tax and VAT collections on the back of the enhancement of digital services.Customs collections fell 7% to P75.7bn given the impact of reduced tariffs (i.e., rice and EVs) and lower import volumes given global trade uncertainties. Non-tax revenues surged 41% to P110.2bn due to higher dividend remittances from Government Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs). 

Expenditures grew a modest 4% YoY in May to P578.2bn driven by tepid growth in primary spending. Primary spending for the month inched up 2% YoY to P508.3bn.

The slower primary spending growth can likely be attributed to the election ban on public spending which started late March till election day on May 12. Interest payments were up 14% to P70.0bn from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt.

This brings the 5M25 deficit to P523.9bn, wider by 29% YoY. The 5M25 deficit appears on trend to exceed the full-year deficit target of P1.54tn due lower-than-expected tax revenue collections. 

Government debt inches up to P16.75trn in end-April.The government's total debt stock went up by 0.41% MoM to P16.75tn as of end-April. This was due to additional borrowings which was offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P57.38 end-Mar to P56.25 end-Apr). Domestic debt rose by 1.8% (P211bn) MoM to P11.59trn due to the impact of net borrowings. External debt decreased by 2.7% (P142.3bn) MoM to P5.16trn given a decrease in the peso equivalent of USD debt (P124.7bn) due to local currency appreciation, and net repayments (P58.3bn). The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.2% and 30.8% being external borrowings. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35trn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn (see here). This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%.

DOTr to privatize LRT-2 via PPP. President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. has approved the public private partnership mode of privatization for LRT-2. The bidding is set to start before year-end with the International Finance Corp. as the project advisor. Line expansions in the pipeline, rising ridership, as well as hours-long unplanned shutdowns underscore the need for PPP to improve reliability and overall quality of operations.  Despite private operation, train fares will remain regulated by the Dept. of Transportation (DOTr), mitigating concerns over sudden fare hikes.

 

Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act sees vetoed changes; law to take effect July 1. President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. signed Republic Act No. 12214 or the Capital Markets Efficiency and Promotion Act (CMEPA) last May 30. Some key features of the law would be the reduction of stock transaction tax 0.6% to 0.1%, documentary stamp tax (DST) on original share issues lowered to 0.75% from 1%, and DST exemption for mutual fund and UITF share deals (original issuance, redemption, or other disposition of shares). It is to note that President Marcos vetoed certain provisions prior to signing. Marcos vetoed the proposed removal of the tax exemption of foreign investors' income from their transactions with foreign currency deposit units (FDCUs). The removal of such tax exemption would have implied higher costs for dollar-denominated borrowings. The President also vetoed the provision imposing DST liability on betters of the Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) games. This was done to avoid negative impacts on revenues from legal gaming activities which are a funding source for official gov't programs. According to the Dept. of Finance, CMEPA was projected to raise P41.9bn in gov't revenues by 2030 but after adjusting for the vetoed items, the law is expected to generate P25bn by 2030. The law will take effect on July 1, following publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one newspaper of general circulation.

Government tempers GDP growth targets; raises fiscal deficit targets on lower projected revenues. The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) trimmed its economic growth target for 2025 to a range of 5.5-6.5% narrower than the previous 6-8% goal.  The DBCC cited lower-than-expected 1Q25 GDP print of 5.4%, dampened global sentiment due to Trump's tariffs, and heightened risk from the Israel-Iran conflict. To meet the lower end of the GDP growth target range (5.5%), GDP growth needs to average ~5.5% for the next three quarters. To hit the mid-point (6.0%) and high end (6.5%) of the range, GDP growth needs to average ~6.2% and ~6.8% for the rest of the year, respectively.

We anticipate a 20% excise tax rate will apply to most pickup trucks. News reports indicate that single-cab chassis would still be exempted. PCTO warns against the Konektadong Pinoy Act. The Philippine Chamber of Telecommunication Operations (PCTO) called on President Marcos to think twice before signing the Konektadong Pinoy Act into law. The law allegedly favors the technology of satellite providers, which are mostly foreign companies, given the cost of deploying and managing space-based assets.

The country’s biggest telcos, who’ve largely invested in terrestrial assets, warned that the Philippines would be the only country that permits satellite providers to come in without regulation. New foreign operators, under the bill, will not be required to build infrastructure. They will also not be required to service or subsidize underserved rural areas, nor support government in times of national emergency.

The Konektadong Pinoy bill eliminates barriers to entry in the telecommunications sector, removing the need for legislative franchises for connectivity services in the hopes of attracting more foreign investment in digital infrastructure.

 

 

 

Multiple groups call for signing of Konektadong Pinoy bill. Thirty-five organizations called for President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. to sign the "Konektadong Pinoy" bill despite aggressive lobbying for it to be vetoed. The multiple groups in favor of the bill include various ICT industry groups, tech organizations, foreign chambers of commerce, among others.

The Open Access in Data Transmission measure or Konektadong Pinoy bill will remove the Congressional franchise requirement for data providers. The multiple organizations in a statement cited that the issues raised against the bill are "unfounded". Endangerment of national security was said to not be an issue given that the bill asks network providers to comply with internationally recognized standards on cybersecurity. The bill mandates a cybersecurity performance audit as a requirement for continuing operation and license renewal. It also disallows foreign gov't-controlled and state-owned enterprises from operating data transmission networks.

The thirty-five groups emphasized that the bill would slash internet costs and improve accessibility to internet connectivity. 19,000 barangays (45.5% of all barangays nationwide) were cited to still lack internet access.

Some members of Congress want online gambling to be inaccessible on e-wallets. Some members of the House of Representatives are drafting a bill that seeks to remove gambling apps from e-wallet platforms. Representative Jonathan Keith Flores said that e-wallets have become “…channels of addiction”. Meanwhile, Representative Rolando Valeriano believes that the government should prohibit all online gambling.

Dept. of Agriculture drafting bill to restore NFA powers. The Department of Agriculture (DA) is drafting a bill to restore some of the powers of the National Food Authority (NFA) such as selling rice in the commercial market and the implementation of a floor price on palay (unmilled rice). DA Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that the proposed bill will also amend other key provisions of the Rice Tariffication Law (RTL)—i.e., removing the restriction on the NFA's ability to import rice for better response to supply shortages and price volatility. Other proposed key measures would be restoring the NFA's authority to sell non-aging stocks of rice commercially and doubling their palay procurement budget to P18bn in 2026. Currently, the NFA can only sell two months and one-day old rice stocks (one month before the aging process begins).

House Speaker Martin Romualdez sought the help of the DA to draft a bill for next Congress to help the gov't's food security agenda, specifically President Ferdinand Marcos' Jr.'s P20/kg rice program for vulnerable sectors through 2028. The prices for local and imported well-milled rice as of June 19 both range from P38-48/kg, compared to P40-55/kg [local] and P44-50/kg [imported] back in Jan 19.

 

DTI aiming to roll out electric vehicle incentive program soon. The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) has completed the EV incentive framework called “Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy” (EVIS). The Fiscal Incentives Review Board will deliberate and finalize EVIS next month. Once the review is over, the DTI is expected to start the rollout of fiscal incentives.

The DTI aims to implement tax breaks, duty exemptions & investment support under EVIS:

  • 0% excise tax on fully built-up EVs imported as CBUs (Completely Built Unit) through 2028 
  • 15% excise tax on hybrids versus 50% under the TRAIN law
  • Duty-free importation of CKD EV units and EV parts upon BOI approval and Zero-tariff rates on batteries, motors, and charging-station equipment
  • Up to seven-year Income Tax Holiday for new EV manufacturing/assembly Post-ITH option to pay a 5% special corporate income tax on gross income in lieu of all national and local taxes
  • Zero-rating of VAT on EVs, parts, and batteries sold to end-users with and exemption from motor vehicle user’s charge and other local levies during the incentive period
  • One-stop-shop BOI processing for permits, environmental clearances, and import licenses 
  • Priority LTO registration and issuance of special EV license plates 

The DTI expects EVIS to create 680,000 jobs and spur P120bn in investments. Program is expected to produce P11.4 T in output and P400bn in additional tax revenues. The DTI aims to produce 9mn EVs from 2028-2040 under this program and mandates the installation of 400k charging stations.

 

19th Congress adjourns without passing minimum wage hike bill. Both the House and Senate adjourned on Wednesday, ending the third regular session without either chamber adopting the other's bill or presenting a bicameral conference committee report after not being able to agree on the amount of increase.

Rizal Rep. Juan Fidel Nograles, chair of the House committee on labor and employment, pushed for a bicameral conference committee for transparent discussions on the minimum wage hike. However, Sen. Joel Villanueva urged the House to adopt the Senate's P100 wage increase version, which would bypass the need for a bicameral conference, so it can be signed by the President. Villanueva expressed "strong reservations" against the House's proposed P200 hike, citing potential "adverse economic and social impacts" like increased unemployment and higher consumer prices. Admin’s economic managers also warned against the bill stating that a P200 increase in wages could raise inflation by about 2%, while a P100 may add 0.7%.

Under the Congress’ rules, when there are different provisions in similar bills approved by the Senate and the House, a bicameral conference committee shall be convened, which will come up with a final version of the proposed measure. The final version, in a bicameral conference committee report, must be separately ratified by the House and Senate before being sent to the President for approval.

Bills last only until the end of the congress they are filed in, but the bills that are not passed into law can be refiled after the next congress convenes.

Senate sends VP Duterte impeachment case back to House. The Senate voted 18-5 with no abstentions, to remand the articles of impeachment against Vice President Sara Duterte back to the House of Representatives. This followed a failed proposal to outright dismiss the articles of impeachment, which was proposed by Senator Dela Rosa and backed by other Duterte allied senators. The only five who voted against the motion to remand were Senators Pimentel, Hontiveros, Binay, Poe, and Gatchalian.

Senator Alan Peter Cayetano moved to amend Senator Dela Rosa's motion—to instead return the articles of impeachment to the House, without dismissing or terminating the case until a) the House can certify its non-violation of the Constitution; b) the House of the 20th Congress communicates to the Senate that it is willing and ready to pursue the impeachment complaint against the VP. The 20th Congress will convene in July. At least 16 votes (two thirds majority) are needed to impeach VP Sara Duterte, should the trial still proceed.

SEC chief eyes GOCCs to list in the PSE. Francisco Ed. Lim, new chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), hopes to encourage Philippine gov't-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs) to list on the stock exchange to spur investor activity. Currently there are no GOCCs listed on the PSE. 

Lim, who was PSE president from 2004-2010, stated that he will check the implementation of laws that require the public listing of companies that are availing of gov't incentives.  Republic Act 11534 or the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act mandates that in order for certain incentives to be granted, companies must either list on the PSE or have committed to a specified timeframe to do so. Sugar pest outbreak hits 842-hecatres in Negros; threatening 65% of PH sugar output.  According to the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA), the red-striped soft scale insects (RSSI) infestation in Negros reached 842 ha as of June 6, nearly a tenfold rise from 87 ha on May 22, threatening plantations that supply 65% of national sugar output.

The Department of Agriculture has released P10 billion for pesticide procurement to support containment efforts. The SRA will bar unapproved movement of planting materials to or from Negros requiring farmers and research facilities to obtain clearance via the Bureau of Plant Industry. Researchers have identified a natural parasite for long-term control, but the SRA is prioritizing chemical spraying as the immediate response. RSSI can slash crop yields by up to 50%.


Congress approves Bill allowing 99-year land leases to foreigners.

The Senate and House of Representatives approved a bicameral conference committee report extending the land lease term to foreigners to a maximum of 99 years. The maximum of 99 years is in line with Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia. Currently, the Foreign Investors’ Long Term Lease Act allows foreigners to lease land for up to 50 years, renewable for another 25 years (total of 75 years).

Foreigners will be allowed to sublease properties unless their contracts prohibit them from doing so. The Bill should now be transmitted to the Office of the President for signing (into law).
