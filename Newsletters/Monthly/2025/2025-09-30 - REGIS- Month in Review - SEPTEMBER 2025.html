REGIS REPORT

30 September 2025
 

Philippine equities endured a volatile and ultimately negative September, with the PCOMP sliding back below the 6,000 level, erasing mid-month gains and leaving YTD returns firmly in the red. Earlier in the month, optimism over a widely anticipated US Fed rate cut briefly lifted share prices. This was quickly followed by a sharp pullback that stretched into month-end as the corruption scandal at the national public works department continued to unfold, leading to weak investor confidence in the market.

 

Notably, the peso depreciated by 1.8% MoM to P58.205:$1, the weakest among Asian currencies for the month.

 

The Philippines vastly underperformed vs the region for both the month of September and the quarter, sliding 3.3% or 202.11 pts MoM and falling 6.5% or 411.48 pts QoQ to 5,953.46. This brings the PCOMP's YTD losses to 8.8%, the second worst performer in Asean following Thailand's SET.

 

Political risk and governance concerns emerged as the dominant local theme. Revelations of widespread corruption in the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) linked to agency officials as well as lawmakers have sapped investor confidence and raised expectations of a slowdown in public infrastructure spending. This dented near-term growth prospects, as broader government disbursements are likewise seen tightening as agencies adopt a more cautious stance. It has also added selling pressure to property names and especially high-end developers where pre-sales are now expected to cool.

 

Sector performance reflected these anxieties, with Property (6.8% MoM) bearing the brunt as ALI (-13% MoM) in particular suffered outsized losses with the market pricing in slower demand along with a less dovish BSP. Recent comments from central bank Gov. Remolona that rates are already at a “sweet spot” dampened hopes of further easing despite benign inflation.

 

Banks led the mid-month rally after the Fed’s 25-bp cut, recovering from oversold levels, though widely-held BDO (-1.7% MoM) was among the big laggards in the recent market rout, down 11.9% against the PCOMP's 5.0% decline in the last seven-day slump. ICT (-2.4% MoM) also powered to a fresh ATH mid-month on resilient global port volumes and easing tariff worries, but likewise underperformed with a 7.4%slide in this last market sell-off.

 

By the end of September, a weak currency, heightened political risk, and limited space for monetary easing set a cautious tone for at least the beginning of the final quarter of 2025.

Key data
 
 

Average daily turnover for the month fell by 8% to $126m from $137m the month prior, as traders continue to thread the market in caution given the lackluster 1H25 results and the ongoing corruption scandal. The market meanwhile snapped its 5 month net outflow streak partly due to the inflows seen during the RCR placement but outside the block, foreign outflow stood at $88m.

 

 

Foreign-Local split even this month.

 

 

• Foreigners continue to reduce their exposure to the country and market proxies SM and AC topped the net foreign sold names this September at -$44.1m and $42.8m respectively. The barrage of selling sent both names to a new 52 week low this September with investors in a risk off stance, as the ongoing corruption scandal is hampering the overall outlook towards the market.

 

• Banks net foreign sold for the 4th straight month due to concerns on asset quality and 2Q earnings miss.  Property names also saw significant outflows and ALI saw $18m worth of net foreign selling partly due to BSP less dovish comments  that the interest rates have hit a sweet spot.

 

• Meanwhile, foreign interest continues to flow into PGOLD  buoyed by its robust 1H25 revenues  and management’s upward revision of its revenue guidance.  ICT also top foreign bought for the 5th straight month and printed a new all-time high this September, after its port in Mexico saw a 12% growth in August.

 

• Notable names this month: AP saw an inflow of $12.8m as the stock was added to FTSE’s mid cap index. PX net foreign bought for $5.7m and surged +23% on the back of a rally in gold.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

19 Decliners outnumbered 11 advancers in the PCOMP in September.

 

Among sector indices, Property (-6.8% MoM) was the big underperformer following i) increased expectations that high-end demand will falter with increased scrutiny in the luxury sector due to the corruption scandal, and ii) recent comments from the BSP that rates are now at a “sweet spot,” which is seen limiting the scope for further monetary easing. Poor sentiment on the residential segment hit ALI (-13.0% MoM) the hardest, while RLC (+1.5%) was a bright spot within the sector.

 

Of course, Mining (+28.6% MoM) finished the month in the green led unsurprisingly by gold miners.

 

Another positive space was gaming which saw a strong bounce in BLOOM (+33.5% MoM) and PLUS (+9.8%) likewise rebounded while investors mulled over the regulatory impact from ongoing public scrutiny on the sector.

 

The September Fed rate cut temporarily lifted Banks (-1.5% MoM) which ultimately succumbed to the recent market rout by month-end, with BDO (-1.7% MoM) and MBT (-2.9% MoM) dragging while BPI (+1.8% MoM) managed to outperform the sector.

 

Holdings (-4.0% MoM) saw mixed performance, though were generally weak. Between the large proxies, AC (-11.4% MoM) was sold off more than SM (-2.6% MoM), following the slump in its property arm ALI. GTCAP (-16.6% MoM) was the biggest underperformer, falling after announcing its purchase of MBT shares from a suspected related party.

 

Telcos all closed lower, CNVRG (-10.6% MoM) standing out with double-digit losses as investors awaited the implementing rules of the recently passed Konektadong Pinoy Act which is aimed at lowering barriers to entry into the telecoms sector.

 

Consumer likewise finished broadly lower amid fragile market sentiment, with SEVN (-15.0% MoM) and URC (-12.5% MoM) falling the most.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP open to Oct cut if growth slows

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that a fourth consecutive 25bp rate cut to 4.75% by Oct is a possibility if output slows down because of the lack of demand. Remolona also noted that two 25bp cuts in Oct and Dec are "possible but not likely". He emphasized that the BSP is inclined to hold rates steady as long as inflation and growth forecasts remain intact. For 2025, the BSP expects GDP growth to settle at the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% goal, and inflation to be at 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect GDP growth and inflation to be 5.5% and 1.8%, respectively. Recall that BSP Governor Remolona during the last Monetary Board meeting in Aug mentioned that the current rate of 5.00% is the "sweet spot" given recent data. The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%.

 

Inflation risks tilted to the downside—BSP

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees risks to inflation to be weighted towards the downside ahead of the next Monetary Board meeting on Oct 9 (5th out of 6 this year). Director of BSP's Monetary Policy Research Group Lara Ganapin emphasized that inflation needs to stay within target for the BSP to consider easing further. Ganapin highlighted that "the risk will clearly be tilted to the downside" as expecations are "well-contained and well-anchored". However, she notes of some risk from geopolitical, global trade policy uncertainty, and adverse weather driven supply shocks.

 

Inflation rate accelerates to 1.5% in August

Headline inflation picked up to 1.5% in August from 0.9% in the previous month of July, higher than the consensus forecast of 1.2% but within the BSP's forecast range of 1.0-1.8% for August. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past six months. The primary driver for the faster pace in inflation in Aug was food up 0.9% YoY, reversing from being down 0.2% YoY in July. This brings the YT-Aug headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%.

 

Dept. of Finance expects to hit lower end of GDP goal

Finance Undersecretary and Chief Economist Domini Velasquez stated that they expect GDP growth to be faster in 2H25 to be able to meet the lower end of the 5.5%-6.5% growth target for 2025. Recall that 2Q GDP growth was 5.5%, slightly faster than 1Q's 5.4%. GDP growth in the 2H needs to average 5.6% in order to meet the minimum 5.5% growth target. Velasquez expects that household consumption (>70% of the economy) will continue to be the main growth driver alongside benign inflation and lower interest rates. She also noted a lower base for 2H25 given that GDP growth was 5.3% in 2H24 vs. 6.2% in 1H24.

 

Current account deficit narrows to $5.0bn in 2Q25

The country's CA deficit narrowed to US$5.0bn (4.0% of GDP) in 2Q25 from the US$5.9bn (5.2% of GDP) deficit in 2Q24. The decline in the deficit was driven by the Philippines' narrower trade in goods gap - the trade-in-goods deficit was narrower by 5% YoY to US$12.6bn, given the faster growth in exports (+7%) vs. imports (+4%). This brings the 1H25 CA deficit to 3.9% of GDP, wider than last year's 3.6%. For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to hit US$16.3bn or 3.3% of GDP. Recall that in 2024, the country posted its second largest CA deficit at US$17.5bn (3.8% of GDP)—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of US$12.4bn (2.8% of GDP).

 

BOP shifts to US$359mn surplus in Aug; GIR settles at US$107.1bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$359mn, wider than the US$88mn surplus in Aug 2024 but a turnaround from July 2025's US$167mn deficit. The August surplus reflected the BSP's net income from its investments abroad. This brings the YT-Aug deficit at US$5.4bn, reversing from the US$1.6bn surplus in the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (-1.3% of GDP), which reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Aug stood at US$107.1bn, down from the $105.4bn in end-Jul. This is equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025.

 

 

Government posts P85bn budget deficit in July; P869bn deficit as of 8M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P85bn in Aug, which is wider both by 56% YoY and 348% MoM. This brings the 8M25 deficit to P869bn, 25% wider YoY. The wider YTD deficit results from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues as customs collections continues to lag behind the full-year target. Revenue collections in Aug increased by a modest 3% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues and lackluster customs collections. Expenditures inched down 1% YoY in Aug as primary spending decreased. Similar to July, the spending slowdown in Aug was likely driven by the delayed timing of big-ticket disbursements of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways, Dept. of Social Welfare and Development, and Dept. of National Defense. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

 

Government debt inches up to P17.56trn in end-July; exceeds year-end debt level projection

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.7% MoM to P17.56trn as of end-July. Domestic debt rose by 1.3% MoM to P12.11trn, while external debt increased by 2.6% MoM to P5.46trn. The P17.56trn figure already exceeds the P17.36-trn full-year projection for 2025 by 1.15%. Despite this, the Bureau of Treasury said that the debt stock is seen to ease by year-end with the gov't set to repay P814bn in domestic bonds by Dec and as "fundraising activities wind down."

 

Infrastructure spending dips in July given slow DPWH disbursements

Overall infrastructure spending (incl. infra components of subsidy and equity to GOCCs and LGU transfers) fell 29% MoM and 20% YoY to P114.7bn in July due to slow Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) disbursements given project schedules, delays in procurement, incomplete submission of progress billings and required documents by contractors. Also cited the new tax clearance requirement of the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) for the release of final payments. Clearance will only be given when a contractor has no outstanding tax liabilities. Brings 7M25 infra spend to P835bn, -3% YoY and behind the 1H government’s target (here). Government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP).

Proposed lower 10% VAT under review by House panel

The "VAT Reduction Act of 2025" or House Bill 4302 proposes to reduce the current value-added tax (VAT) rate by two percentage points from 12% to 10%. The bill, if passed, is estimated to reduce annual VAT collections by ~P200bn, which is equivalent to ~P7,000 savings per household each year. The Philippines imposes the highest VAT rate in Southeast Asia at 12%. For context, Vietnam and Cambodia impose a 10% VAT; Indonesia, 11%; Singapore, 9%; Thailand, 7%; Malaysia, Laos, and Myanmar 5-7%. Recall that the VAT rate was raised to 12% from 10% back in 2005. VAT collections have grown from P157bn in 2005 to P1.2trn in 2024.

 

House committee approves 2026 national budget bill; starts plenary deliberations

The House Committee on Appropriations approved House Bill No. 4058 or the General Appropriations bill on a vote of 54 for, 6 against, 4 abstaining—moving the budget bill to plenary level deliberations. This comes after 37 days of committee-level vetting and the recent finalization of budget amendments after the reallocation of the P255bn flood control budget from the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) to the education, health, and agricultural sectors. After passing the committee level, the budget bill will be deliberated through the House plenary level, then to the Senate, the bicam, and finally to President Marcos Jr. for approval. The House targets to approve the budget bill on second reading before Oct 10, and by final reading after sessions resume a month after in Nov. The proposed 2026 national budget amounts to P6.79trn, 7% higher YoY than this year's P6.33trn budget.

 

OFW Remittances up 3.0% YoY in July

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.0% YoY to US$3.2bn in July, decelerating from the 3.7% growth seen in June. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, and Saudi Arabia were cited to be the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total. Sea-based remittances growth moderated to 3.1% YoY from 3.5% the previous month. In PHP terms, remittances were unchanged YoY.

 

BPO Association expects industry to grow in 2026

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) expects the industry to achieve revenue of US$42bn and employ 1.97mn Filipinos by the end of 2026. These represent 4%-5% growth from its 2025 targets of US$40bn in revenue and 1.9mn employees. IBPAP President Jonathan Madrid said that the industry’s growth will still be driven by core segments – financial services and healthcare. He also said that Global Capability Centers (GCC) are growing faster. These are are offshore units established by multinational corporations to provide specialized services such as finance, IT and customer support to their global operations.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth 11.8% in July; M3 growth at 6.2%

Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.8% YoY in July, moderating from the 12.1% rate in June. Loans to productive activities increased 10.8% YoY, slowing down from the 11.1% in June due to decreased lending in key industries such as Manufacturing (-6.3% from -4.0%), trade (+8.5% from +9.7%), and information, communication (+8.5% from +9.6%). Consumer loans grew 23.6%, slightly slower than the 24.0% pace in June. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up to 6.2% in July, accelerating from the 5.9% revised growth in June.


August tourist arrivals -3% YoY, 8M25 -2% YoY to 3.96mn

Data from the Department of Tourism (DOT) exhibited that tourist arrivals for August dropped 3% YoY to 457,438—bringing the 8M25 down by 2% YoY to 3.96mn. The 3.96mn figure is ~67% of the FY24 arrivals. 2024 saw 5.9mn tourist arrivals for the year, short of the gov't target of 7.7mn. The DOT has yet to provide its tourist arrivals target for 2025. Tourism's share to GDP in 2024 was 8.9% (P2.35trn, +10.3% YoY) (Tourism Direct Gross Value Added [TDGVA]) – this was the highest in five years but still lower than 2019's 12.9%. The DOT expects TDGVA to amount to P2.7trn (+15% YoY) this year.

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) slump again in August

CAMPI reported vehicle sales fell 7.6% YoY, 5.5% MoM to 36,174 units in August, marking the second month of weakness (note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales). This bring YT-August vehicle sales to 305,381 units, +0.2% YoY. EV sales totaled 2,244 units in August, down 17% MoM and accounting for 6.2% of overall sales. 8M25 EV sales reached 18,439 units, accounting for 6.04% of sales. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,023 units, down 5% YoY, 5.7% MoM. This brings TMP’s 8M25 unit sales to 146,357, up 4.1% YoY. To reach TMP’s sales target of 235k units (+8%), sales should average 22k units per month for the rest of the year.

 

Marcos vows accountability amid anti-corruption protests, says he is confident of finishing his term

Palace Press Officer Claire Castro stated that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. is not concerned about any destabilization attempts as the anti-corruption protests are not targeted towards him. Castro emphasized the President's promise that "no one will be spared; those who must be held accountable will face the consequences ". The number of participants in the anti-corruption rallies in Luneta Park, Manila and EDSA People Power Monument in Quezon City were estimated to be around 50,000-80,000 people, marking one of the biggest protests in years. Protests were also held in other regions nationwide such as Bulacan, Davao City, and Baguio City.

 

ICC prosecutors charge Former President Duterte with 3 counts of crimes against humanity

International Criminal Court (ICC) prosecutors charged former President Rodrigo Duterte with three counts of crimes against humanity tied to at least 76 killings in the “war on drugs". The charges cover murder and attempted murder during his time as Davao City mayor (2013-2016) and President (2016-2022). The confirmation of the charges hearing was originally set for Sept. 23 but was postponed as Duterte’s defense requested an indefinite adjournment, citing he is “not fit to stand trial”. The ICC granted a "limited postponement" of the proceedings.
