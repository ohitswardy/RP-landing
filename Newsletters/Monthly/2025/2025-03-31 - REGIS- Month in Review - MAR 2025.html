REGIS REPORT

31 March 2025
 

Philippine shares finished decently higher in March, taking the PCOMP through a second straight monthly advance to close +3.0% or +182.75 pts to 6,180.72. Strong optimism in early March was driven by falling domestic inflation and the weaker US dollar, which lifted the PCOMP to its high of 6,360.77 for the month, or up 6.0% MTD at its peak. However, these gains were halved through the better half of March with jitters over global trade gripping regional equity markets.

 

The PCOMP still managed to outperform regional benchmarks for the month, supported by foreign inflows that reached $50m. Notably, Bloomberg data shows that the Philippines was the only Asian market along with India that saw positive equity inflows for the month.

 

The March gains have now trimmed the PCOMP's YTD losses to 5.3%, from 10.2% at its lowest point in end-January.

 

Investors kept close watch of Trump's pronouncements on trade tariffs, with a deteriorating global macro outlook keeping the market on a risk-off mood through the latter half of the month. Unsurprisingly, ICT (+1.5% MoM) struggled to recover and underperformed the index after a choppy month amid concerns over its port operations in Mexico and Canada. On the other hand, the rally in safe haven assets resulted in a surge in local gold miners' shares, with non-index PX (+58.4% MoM) among the big winners.

 

As for local drivers, 4Q earnings releases also dictated the performance of some of the outliers for the month. We saw encouraging 4Q subscriber additions and a decent dividend payout for CNVRG (+19.9% MoM) which was the top gainer in the PCOMP, while there were big disappointments for BLOOM's (-8.5% MoM) core gaming operations and JFC's (-6.7% MoM) overseas ventures, which dragged the names to be the second and third largest decliners in the index.

 

Quarter-end window-dressing also resulted in last-minute, outsized gains for some of the stocks that were otherwise underperformers for the month -- 50-70% of the MoM gains of SMPH (+7.6% MoM), AC (+7.1% MoM), and ALI (+6.0% MoM) were clocked on March 31st.

 

On domestic macro, the central bank is guiding 50-75bps of policy rate cuts this year, likely starting with the April 10th meeting if March inflation beats expectations on April 4th, according to BSP Gov. Remolona. Recall the Monetary Board left the key rate unchanged at 5.75% in the February meeting, citing continued global macro uncertainty. Meanwhile, inflation fell to 2.1% in February from 2.9% in January and against market's median forecast of 2.6%. This was as food inflation dropped to 2.6% from 3.8%, largely on falling rice prices, while CPI for housing/utilities and transport also came off. This took YTD headline inflation to 2.5% which is firmly within the BSP's 2-4% target range.

 

The local politics scene also heated up with former Pres. Duterte's widely-covered arrest and extradition to The Hague by the International Criminal Court (ICC) over the "war on drugs" conducted during his term, though equities seemed little impacted by the event. The market is closely watching the implications, if any, on the upcoming mid-term elections in May, although recent surveys have shown two-thirds of the top senatorial candidates are still part of the Marcos administration's slate.

 

The peso appreciated by 1.3% MoM to 57.23:$1 -- the second best-performing currency in Asia in March after the Indian Rupee.

Key data
 
 

Average daily turnover in March stood at $113.3m, -2% MoM, almost at pace with the 5-month high in February. Activity during the month was helped by another rebalancing (FTSE, which took place on the 21st), although it was a much lighter affair compared to end-February’s MSCI rebal ($205m vs $356m), as it involved smaller changes, particularly the inclusion of PLUS and RCR into the FTSE all-cap index.

 

Volumes post-rebal also visibly eased with an average daily turnover of only $80m over the last 6 sessions of the month as heightened concerns over trade wars evidently sparked risk aversion across global risk assets, amid the nearing April 2 deadline when the US is expected to announce and immediately effect another round of tariffs.

 

 

Foreign participation was identical to last month at 53%, which matches the highest since November 2023.

 

 

  • Net foreign flow on the ground finally turned skewed to buy by a moderate +$49m following a 4-month streak of heavy foreign selling which had netted over $700m in outflows (half of which in November alone).

 

  • Top foreign bought stocks were big cap lenders BDO and BPI as investors continued to flock into banks after another quarter of good results and intact prospects, vis-à-vis the growing uncertainty over the global macro outlook.

 

  • Another bank, CBC, also made it into the top inflows amid speculation over potential inclusion into other regional indices following its recent entry into the PCOMP earlier this year. It remains by far the best performing stock in the benchmark YTD, +46% vs PCOMP’s -5%, although bulk of its gains came prior to its inclusion.

 

  • Inflows into MER and CNVRG helped drive their big gains in March, +12% and +20% MoM, respectively. Sentiment on the former appears to be driven by growing confidence that its franchise renewal will be approved by lawmakers, while the latter’s latest results showed that it continued to lead against other telcos in terms of subscriber additions.

 

  • Big YTD laggards ACEN and BLOOM continued to be weighed down by outflows in March. ACEN’s share price continues to be weighed down by expectations of a fund-raising amid its massive project pipeline. While BLOOM was dragged down by news of progress in Thailand’s casino legalization. BLOOM is the worst performing stock in the benchmark, -36% YTD, while ACEN -24%.

 

  • PX was yet again among the top foreign bought names as gold prices hit a record high in March amid evident flocking into haven due to global macro uncertainty due to tariffs. It extended its sharp uptrend and is now +162% YTD.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

PCOMP breadth was skewed to positive in March with 19 advancers against 11 decliners.

 

Among sector indices, Mining (+22.3% MoM) vastly outperformed thanks to the gold rally that lifted local gold and copper miners PX (+58.4% MoM), APX (+34.7% MoM), and AT (+14.0% MoM). Property (+4.7% MoM) managed to outperform thanks to quarter-end window-dressing pushing up SMPH (+7.6% MoM) and ALI (+6.0% MoM). Banks (+4.4% MoM) still proved resilient in a relatively risk-off market.

 

While all sector indices managed to close higher MoM, Industrials (+1.2% MoM) and Services (+1.3% MoM) underperformed notably dragged by the underperformance of consumer names, largely on earnings disappointment. Third biggest PCOMP decliner JFC (-6.7% MoM) missed expectations, with a mild disappointment in operations exacerbated by a one-time loss related to its acquisition of Titan, and deteriorating performance of Smashburger and its China business in 4Q. MONDE (-4.2% MoM) though in-line saw another round of large impairment charges on Quorn. BLOOM (-8.5% MoM) which was the second largest decliner in the index missed forecasts, as 4Q losses widened from a one-time tax charge related to debt refinancing, while core operations remained soft with 4Q volumes down QoQ and YoY. The biggest PCOMP underperformer was actually CNPF (-9.5% MoM) which has yet to release 4Q numbers, but pulled back likely due to the sharp increase in skipjack tuna prices in February.

 

Meanwhile, CNVRG (+19.9% MoM) was the top gainer in the PCOMP after results came in-line with forecasts, with market-leading net adds in 4Q and a decent dividend payout equivalent to 4% yield at the time of the announcement.

 

In all, releases for the month saw more misses than beats, among the more notable ones were: AC (+7.1% MoM) missed, dragged by misses in telcos and power along with continuing losses in its smaller, non-listed subsidiaries; ACEN (-1.0% MoM) missed, dragged by typhoon disruptions, higher pre-development costs, and depreciation of newly operational plants; SCC (-5.6% MoM) missed, as mining and power ASPs disappointed, as did higher non-cash costs; DMC (+2.5% MoM) missed, dragged by mining and power as well as construction delays and property reversals; GTCAP (+0.2% MoM) missed, dragged by lower contributions from infra and property that offset decent banks and auto numbers; RLC (-1.8% MoM) missed, dragged by the residential segment underperforming. WLCON (-11.3% MoM) missed, with negative SSSG even worsening in 4Q and margins suffering from "price refreshes" done in the quarter.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP to cut its policy rate by 50-75 bps this year, hinged on inflation and growth. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Remolona stated the high likelihood that the Monetary Board (MB) will cut on April 10, especially if inflation beats expectations on April 4. Recall that February inflation posted at 2.1%, bringing the YTD average to 2.5%—within the BSP's target range of 2-4%. Remolona guided a base case of up to 50 bps worth of rate cuts but also hinted on the possibility of up to 75 bps should the economy output turn out to be weaker than expected. The BSP Governor also commented that there will be negative spillover effects of the tariff war on the Philippines. However, he noted that the hit on the Philippine economy will only be "modest."

 

Inflation rate eases to 2.1% in February. Headline inflation eased to 2.1% in February from 2.9% in the previous month, marking the lowest print since Sept 2024. This is lower than consensus forecast of 2.6% and came in below the BSP's forecast range of 2.2-3.0%. On a MoM basis, CPI dropped 0.2%, primarily driven by lower food and diesel prices. Food & non-alcoholic beverages saw a drop in prices 0.9% MoM for the first time since Sept 2024. This is as vegetables dipped 12.4% MoM with prices starting to normalize after the onslaught of typhoons last 4Q24. Rice also saw its eighth consecutive month of price decline (-1.6% MoM), taking the cumulative decline to 6.2%. Core inflation dipped slightly to 2.4% in Feb from 2.6% in Jan, with core non-food inflation falling to 2.2% from 2.5% for the same periods. 

 

PH current account deficit widens to $17.5bn in 2024, missing BSP target. The country posted its second-largest CA deficit at $17.5bn, equivalent to 3.8% of GDP—41.4% bigger than the 2023 deficit of $12.4bn or 2.8% of GDP. For context, 2022's CA deficit was the record largest at $18.3 or 4.5% of GDP. The 2024 deficit breached the BSP's forecast of a $10.4bn (2.2% of GDP) CA deficit for the year. The larger-than-expected deficit was driven by the Philippines' heavy import bill and slower services trade. Recall that the country imported $68.7bn more goods than what was exported in 2024, primarily due to the FY24 seeing higher consumer goods imports, which were up 6% YoY to $25.8bn, and lower electronics exports, down 7% to $38.1bn. Also, service exports were only up 7.5% to $52bn relative to the 24% jump in service imports to $37bn. These led to more dollar outflows, which added pressure on peso volatility. For 2025, the BSP expects the country's CA deficit to be $12.1bn or 2.4% of GDP.

Trade deficit narrows to $3.15bn in Feb. The February trade deficit narrowed to US$3.16bn, down 11% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports and growth recovery in exports. This is the lowest deficit in four years. Imports for the month inched down 2% YoY to US$9.41bn, a significant slowdown from the 11% increase in Jan. Primarily driven by the decline in fuel imports, which was down 23% YoY to US$1.3bn, negating the increases in other major goods categories. Exports grew 4% YoY to US$6.25bn, the second month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction. Electronics exports grew by 3% YoY to $3.52bn, a turnaround from the 3% YoY decline in Jan. These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 6% YoY to US$2.73bn.

 

BOP surplus at US$3.1bn in February 2025; GIR rises to US$107.4bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$3.1bn, a significant turnaround from both January 2025's $4.1bn deficit and the $196mn deficit in February 2024. The surplus reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits, which include ROP Global Bonds proceeds and foreign investments' net income. This brings the YTD deficit to $992mn as of February 2025, slightly higher than the $936mn deficit a year ago. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-February 2025 stood at US$107.4bn, up from the US$103.3bn in end-January 2025. GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income.

 

January gov't budget surplus down 22.3% YoY to P68bn, -22.3% YoY. The government recorded a budget surplus of P68bn, which is down 22.3% from January 2024's surplus of P88bn but a reversal from the deficit of P330bn in December. Revenue collections in January grew 10.8% YoY to P467bn, primarily driven by growth in tax revenues, which is up 13.6% YoY. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 15.1% to P355bn, mainly due to an increase in VAT and income taxes as well as stronger collection efforts, crackdown on illicit trade, and digital transformation projects. Customs saw 8% growth to P79bn in collections from higher VAT and excise collections. Non-tax revenues dipped 26% to P30bn, given the one-time gain recognized in January 2024. Expenditures jumped 19.5% YoY in January to P399bn as both primary expenses and interest payments were up 13.4% and 40.7%, respectively. Primary spending for the month rose 13.4% YoY to P294bn.

 

Government debt inches up to P16.31tn in end-Jan. The government's total debt stock went up by 1.6% MoM (P261.5bn) to P16.31tn as of end-Jan. This was due to the net incurrence of new domestic and external debt as well as the impact of depreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of November. Domestic debt rose by 1.4% (P153.7bn) MoM to P11.08tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P152.2bn). External debt increased by 2.1% (P107.8bn) MoM to P5.23tn given net foreign loan additions (P59.3bn) and unfavorable currency movements. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn. This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. 

 

PH Dollar reserves up 3.3% at end-February. The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-January saw a 3.3% MoM drop to $106.7bn from $103.3bn at the end-January. Primarily reflecting the BSP's net foreign exchange operations and proceeds from its recent global bond sale worth $3.3bn. The level of dollar reserves by end-Feb was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and is equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. 

 

OFW Remittances up 2.9% YoY in January. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.9% YoY to US$2.92bn, but down 13.7% MoM given December's seasonal strength. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances, posting significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 0.9% YoY from 0.6% in December. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 7.9%, slower relative to the months of November and December but faster than the growth seen in September and October. This year's BSP's remittances target growth rate has yet to be disclosed as of late. Recall that last year's growth of 3.0% was in-line with the BSP target of 3.0%.

Commercial banks' loan growth accelerates to 12.8% in January. Commercial bank loans rose by 12.8% YoY in January, mildly accelerating from 12.2% in December. Loans to productive activities rose by 11.8% (from 10.8%), led by utilities (+24%), trade (+14%), real estate (+10%), and transport/storage (+21%). Consumer loans rose by 24% YoY, mildly decelerating from +25%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 6.8% YoY, decelerating from the 7.7% pace in December.

 

Banking sector NPL rises MoM in January. Preliminary data from the BSP showed that the banking sector’s NPL ratio rose to 3.38% in January, from 3.27% in December 2024. However, this is lower than the 3.44% figure in January 2024. The value of NPL rose 2.5% MoM (+11% YoY) to P512.8bn. NPL coverage fell to 95.3%, from 96.0% in December 2024. Total loans of the banking sector fell 1% MoM but grew 13% YoY to P13.4trn. The past due ratio also increased to 4.17% in January, from 3.95% in December 2024. However, this is lower than the 4.27% figure in January 2024. The value of past due loans rose 5% MoM and 11% YoY to P571.6bn. The ratio of restructured loans also increased to 2.05%, from 2.03% in December 2024. It was 2.26% in January 2024.

 

Digital payments up 34% in Feb; transaction volumes surge. The value of payments over InstaPay (real-time, small-tickets) and PesoNet (larger, batched payments) grew 33.7% YoY in February to P1,646bn. The figure is down 8.7% MoM, though that’s rather typical following the holiday season surge. The headline growth is also slower than the 38.2% growth in January, and slower than the 40.6% posted in February 2024. That said, it is important to note that 2024 was a leap year (adding 3.3ppts), which likely skewed the growth figures. For 2M25, digital payments were up 36.0% YoY to P3,449bn, still slightly faster than the 35.4% in FY2024 despite the lost day.

 

Vehicle sales rise 2.9% YoY in February; Toyota sales up 9%. CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales of 39,164 units, up 2.9% YoY and 4.1% MoM. This brings 2M25 vehicle sales to 76,768 units, up 6.4% YoY. Sales of electric vehicles reached 3,416 units in 2M25, with hybrid EVs at 3,034 units, followed by battery EV at 335 units and plug-in hybrid EV at 47 units. Note that CAMPI/TMA data does not include BYD. CAMPI is targeting sales of 500k units for 2025 (+7% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 18,528 units in February, higher by 9.1% YoY and 2.5% MoM. This brings 2M25 retail sales up 10.7% YoY to 36,606 units.

 

Department of Energy launches GEA-4, seeks bids for 10.5GW of RE capacity. The Department of Energy released the terms of reference for the fourth Green Energy Auction (GEA-4), which will involve 10,478 MW of solar, onshore wind, and integrated energy storage technologies nationwide. 8,088 MW of will come from ground-mounted solar, roof-mounted solar, and floating solar, of which Luzon will account for 5,553 MW. 2,390 MW will come from onshore wind projects, of which 1,670 MW will be in Luzon. Delivery of projects will be between 2026-2029, with a supply contract of 20 years from commercial operations. Recall that GEA-3, covering 4,650 MW of mostly hydro capacity, is currently ongoing with results likely out by June.

 

PAGCOR says that new BSP guidelines could curtail growth of e-games. PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that the BSP draft guidelines could curtail the growth of the e-casino sector. The BSP’s draft “Guidelines on the Adoption of a Digital Marketplace Model” seeks to prohibit online marketplaces (banks and electronic money issuers) from offering or presenting gambling. In addition to access, advertising of gaming platforms will likely be prohibited in online marketplaces. About 80% of the players of DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) access its platform through Globe's (GLO PM) GCash. The draft guidelines would suggest that players will no longer be able to access the PLUS platform through GCash but will still be able to transfer funds via GCash or banks. Existing players would likely continue to play on the PLUS platform. However, PLUS and other online gaming operators might experience more difficulty growing their current user bases.

Bicam approves bill on capital markets reform. The Bicameral Conference Committee ratified the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotions bill. A copy of the bicameral conference committee report of the measure was not immediately available. Key features of the bill include: lowering the stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1% to encourage stock market activity; the documentary stamp tax (DST) on the original issue of shares of stock will be lowered from 1% to 0.75% to reduce transaction costs for investors; the DST on unit investment trust funds (UITFs) will also be removed; a 50% tax deduction will be introduced for employers' contributions to their employees’ personal equity and retirement accounts to encourage businesses to help employees save for retirement.

 

Philippines sends ex-President Duterte to ICC over 'drugs war' killings. Former Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte was arrested in the Manila airport after arriving from Hong Kong, and was flown to the Netherlands (RP-C5219) to face crimes against humanity charges at the International Criminal Court (ICC). The ICC issued an Interpol arrest warrant accusing Duterte of crimes linked to deadly anti-drug crackdowns during his presidency.

 

SWS survey for senatorial preferences see 9 from the Marcos-admin slate. Based on the Social Weather Stations (SWS) conducted in March 15-20, 13 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 available seats in the upcoming midterm elections on May 12, 2025. This is the first survey conducted and published after the March 11 arrest of Former President Rodrigo Duterte and turnover to the International Criminal Court (ICC) in Hague, Netherlands. Bong Go (42%) and Erwin Tulfo (42%) secured the top spots, ranking 1st-2nd. Only four of the probable 13 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go and Dela Rosa are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo and Revillame are independent. Notably, comparing the March survey vs. the one conducted in February (before Duterte’s arrest), the composition of the top 13 remains unchanged but exhibited some changes in rankings such as Bong Go moving to the top spot and Camille Villar moving to 11-13th from 13th previously.
