REGIS REPORT

31 May 2025
 

Philippine equities snapped a three-month advance with the PCOMP declining by 0.2% or 13.46 pts MoM to 6,341.53, as gains from the earlier half of the month were erased by foreign selling pressure towards month-end. Tariff and trade headlines took to the backburner with investors instead zeroed in on soft 1Q25 corporate earnings, improving macro cues, and the evolving local politics scene following the May mid-term elections.

 

Thus the PCOMP's YTD losses widened to 2.9% from 2.7% at YTD-April, though still much narrower vs the 10.2% decline at its lowest point in end-January. The Philippines is so far middle-of-the-pack among Asean-5, tracking behind modest gains in Singapore (+2.8% YTD) and Indonesia (+1.4% YTD) but faring better than Malaysia (-8.2% YTD) and Thailand (-17.9% YTD).

 

Aggregate PCOMP earnings are so far trailing market expectations of low double-digit growth for FY25, after clocking mere low- to mid-single-digit growth in 1Q25. Around half of the corporates on our watchlist reported numbers that were in-line with forecasts, though earnings misses vastly outnumbered the few beats, making a rather weak 1Q25 showing overall.

 

On domestic macro, 1Q25 GDP also disappointed at 5.4% vs the median estimate of 5.7%, with solid growth in private and public consumption offset by the deterioration in net imports and soft investment growth. However, beyond 1Q, inflation continued to decelerate to 1.4% in April -- a strong beat vs Street's 1.8% estimate and a large fall from the 1.8% print in March. YTD-April inflation is now at 2.0%, at the lower end of the central bank's 2-4% target range and tracking well below the FY25 consensus forecast of 2.8%. The BSP has thus signaled two more possible rate cuts for 2025, following the 25-bp rate cut in April, which would take the key rate to 5.00% by year-end.

 

With global trade tensions de-escalating, ICT (+20.5% MoM) staged an impressive recovery and was the biggest positive mover for the PCOMP in May. Meanwhile, oversold BLOOM (+22.6% MoM) was the second largest gainer in the index as traders grew more optimistic over the nearing re-launch of its online gaming platform. Interest also rotated into unloved conglos, lifting JGS (+17.8% MoM), AGI (+36.3% MoM), and GTCAP (+11.1% MoM), as well as AEV (+6.2% MoM) that was added to the MSCI Small Cap Index at the May 2025 rebalancing at month-end.

 

On the other hand, the biggest drag on the index was CBC (-16.9% MoM), which dropped after predictions of a potential MSCI inclusion fell through. SMPH (-7.4% MoM) and ALI (-8.2% MoM) were also large drags as indications from 1Q25 earnings showed residential property demand remained weak. Also taking cues from 1Q25 earnings, the sector disappointment in telcos dragged GLO (-8.8% MoM) and TEL (-6.5% MoM), rounding up the list of five largest index decliners for the month.

 

On politics, the midterm Senatorial election held May 12th resulted in a lackluster showing for Marcos-backed candidates, with administration allies only taking 5 out of the 12 seats. The Duterte side also critically took 5 seats, which could spell furthering of the Marcos-Duterte feud ahead of the 2028 Presidential race. A week after the elections upset, Pres. Marcos called for courtesy resignations from his Cabinet, but thereafter decided to retain officials handling critical posts in his economic management team (i.e. Finance, Budget, Trade, Socioeconomic Planning, and Investment).

 

Foreign outflow accelerated to $259 for the month, pulled down by a $283-m block sale of shares of RRHI (-3.7% MoM) held by DFI Retail Group, which were reacquired by the company.

 

The peso continued to advance albeit at a slower pace of 0.2% MoM to 55.772:$1.

Key data
 
 

Foreigners continue to be heavy sellers in the market and registered $259m worth of outflows in May, the highest since November of last year. Global trade uncertainty continue to weigh on sentiment alongside mixed 1Q25 results, and outflows further accelerated at month-end due to the MSCI semi-annual rebalancing where traders took advantage of the liquidity event. Big blocks seen in BDO and RRHI.

 

 

Foreign local split even this month 50/50.

 

 

• Foreigners turned buyers in ICT for $50m and is the top foreign bought name this month, reversing April’s outflow of $50m. Improving US-China trade tensions brought interest back to the port operator and lifted its share price +20% this month. URC was also among top foreign bought names this May, extending its inflow streak to the 3rd straight month after its deletion from the MSCI last February.

• Banks generally saw inflows and continue to be the favored sector (foreign flow-wise) post 1Q25 results where numbers were pretty much in-line. All the big 3 banks topped the foreign bought stocks this month but performance was mixed and only BDO generated gains this May.  Property names on the other hand saw significant outflows despite BSP’s view of at least 2 more rate cuts this year with inflation improving. The sector still lags due to soft pre-sales and higher inventory levels and both ALI and SMPH were top foreign sold names with -$37.3m and -$27m worth of outflows, respectively.

 

• Notable names this month: AEV finished the month net foreign bought as the stock was added to MSCI's Small Cap index. JFC on the other hand still finished the month net foreign sold despite its upweight in the MSCI index. Weak international business continues to weigh on sentiment.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The PCOMP saw 16 decliners only slightly outnumber 14 advancers in May.

 

Among sector indices, Services (+10.0% MoM) vastly outperformed thanks to sharp gains in gaming stocks PLUS (+32.1% MoM) and BLOOM (+22.6% MoM) as optimism over online gaming remains strong as ever. ICT (+20.5% MoM) also saw a strong comeback in May as US-China trade tensions de-escalated. These more than offset the slump in telcos GLO (-8.8% MoM) and TEL (-6.5% MoM) that fell on 1Q25 earnings disappointment.

 

Although Holdings (+0.1% MoM) likewise relatively outperformed, gains in second-liner laggards AGI (+36.3% MoM), JGS (+17.8% MoM), GTCAP (+11.1% MoM), and AEV (+6.2% MoM) were offset by the decline in index heavyweights SM (-4.0% MoM) and AC (-3.3% MoM).

 

Property was the big underperformer as ALI (-8.2% MoM) and SMPH (-7.4% MoM) dropped on weak underlying numbers in residential. Despite the sequential recovery in pre-sales and bookings in 1Q25, mid-income sales remain challenged which has led developers to offer steep discounts and rent-to-own schemes for ready-for-occupancy units. Developers have also guided for reduced launched plans for the year. RLC (+11.8% MoM) was a bright spot thanks to bargain hunters rotating into laggards.

 

Financials (-2.8% MoM) also underperformed dragged mostly by CBC (-16.9% MoM) which sharply corrected after predictions for a potential MSCI inclusion did not pan out. We saw divergent performance across the Big 3 Banks BDO (+1.3% MoM), BPI (-0.6% MoM), and MBT (-4.4% MoM), but overall strength from the start of the year looks to have already stalled amid a relatively pedestrian 1Q25 showing.

 

Despite positive cues from continued falling inflation, Consumer names were rather mixed, taking most of their cues from 1Q25 results. MONDE (-2.3% MoM) bucked modest gains in manufacturers that saw CNPF (+4.7% MoM) and URC (+2.1% MoM) end higher for the month, as investors weighed a curious volume drop in its core noodles business. JFC (-2.5% MoM) also underperformed despite its MSCI upweight as earnings showed certain international segments continued to lag. Retailers RRHI (-3.7% MoM) and PGOLD (-3.0% MoM) also slipped despite results coming in-line, while SEVN (-17.0% MoM) sharply fell after the earnings miss.

 

Utilities was a broadly negative sector due largely to 1Q25 earnings disappointment dragging AP (-6.2% MoM), ACEN (-4.5% MoM), SCC (-4.4% MoM), and MWC (-2.3% MoM).

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP expects at least two more rate cuts this year, RRR cut next year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) sees to cut rates at least twice more in 2025 after the first 25 bps cut last April. This would bring the current benchmark rate from 5.5% to 5.0% by year-end. BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. elaborated that there is more leeway for easing and that a rate cut is on the table for the next Monetary Board policy meeting given the slowdown in inflation. Inflation cooled to 1.4% in April from 1.8% in March, YT-Apr inflation currently stands at 2.0%. Remolona also stated that the BSP is looking to shift to a point target for inflation (2%), from the current 2–4% target band. Expectations for reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cuts were said to be unlikely for the rest of the year but more likely to be done next year. Last March, the RRR for universal and commercial banks was reduced by 200bp to 5%, while the ratio for digital banks was cut by 150bp to 2.5%, and the RRR for thrift lenders was cut 100bp to 0%. The next Monetary Board meeting (third out of six this year) is on June 19.

 

BSP sees 2025 inflation below 3%, GDP at lower end of 6-8% target

The BSP published key highlights from its Monetary Board meeting held last April 10 where it cut rates by 25bp to 5.50%. Inflation expectations remain within target for 2025-2027. 2025 inflation is projected to stay below the midpoint (3%) of the target range (2-4%) until the fourth quarter due to lower food and oil prices. Upside risks stem from higher transport costs, meat prices, and electricity rates but may be offset by lower rice prices. 2025-2027 GDP growth is seen to settle around the low end (~6%) of the government’s growth target for 2025-2027 (6-8%). Domestic demand will be bolstered by low inflation, improvements in real wages and labor market conditions, as well as the gradual easing of monetary policy. Ongoing global macro uncertainties and a challenging external environment continue to pose risks that may negatively impact domestic economic activity. The BSP noted that current monetary policy settings "still appear to be relatively tight." The central bank also guided that the timing and magnitude of rate cuts will be decided on a meeting-by-meeting basis.

 

Inflation rate decelerates further to 1.4% in April

Headline inflation eased to 1.4% in April from 1.8% in the previous month of Mar, marking the lowest print since Nov 2019 (1.2%). This is lower than the consensus forecast of 1.8% and stands at the lower-end of the BSP's forecast range of 1.3-2.1%. Inflation has continued to be on a downtrend since the start of the year, falling from 2.9% in Jan to 1.4% as of Apr. Primary drivers for easing inflation were MoM price declines in key food staples—rice -10.9%, meat -0.3%, and vegetables -5.4%. Notably, rice prices are on their tenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. Core inflation was unchanged at 2.2% in Apr vs. Mar. While core non-food inflation in Apr inched up slightly to 2.1% from 2.0% in Mar. This brings the YT-Apr inflation rate to 2.0%, at the lower-end of the 2-4% gov't target for this year. Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting (3rd out of 6 this year) is at Jun 19, Thursday. For 2025, consensus expects 75 bps total worth of cuts to 5.00%.

1Q25 GDP grows 5.4%; falls short of expectations

GDP grew at 5.4% in 1Q, lower than the 5.9% growth in 1Q24 but a tad higher than the 5.3% growth rate in 4Q24. The 5.4% GDP growth is below consensus expectations of 5.7%. The print fell short of the government’s 6-8% target range, implying that growth of at least 6.2% for the rest of the year is needed to meet the 6% full-year goal. Main drags stem from slower capital formation and the higher differential between exports and imports. On the demand side, the strong growth in private and gov't consumption was offset by the deterioration of net imports to -2.1% which contracted sharply from -0.1% the previous quarter. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated the gov't remains confident in hitting its 6-8% full-year growth target despite global and domestic headwinds. He also highlighted the resilience of the Philippine economy amidst heightened global uncertainty and noted that the presence of clear signals of accelerating demand should bolster growth. The Department Budget Coordinating Committee (DBCC) will meet next week to review the country's economic targets as a prerequisite for the 2026 national budget. The Bloomberg consensus median forecast for 2025 is 5.9%.

 

Gov't expects higher domestic rice output; mulls floor price for rice

The Department of Agriculture (DA) stated that they expect the domestic output of palay (unmilled rice) to hit their target of 20.4 MMT (million metric tons), 7% higher than last year, barring any harsher than usual weather disturbances. This would yield approximately 12.65 MMT of rice. Note that the Philippines imported a record 4.68 MMT of rice in 2024, this filled the shortfall between domestic production and the estimated total consumption of 16.5 MMT that year. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. in a dialogue with farmers said that the gov't is studying the implementation of a floor price for rice. A floor price is being considered in order to protect farmers from losses and ensure stable supply. Marcos also eyes to return the role of the National Food Authority (NFA) as a rice buyer and price stabilizer. Recall that the Rice Tariffication Law in 2019 rescinded the NFA's ability to import rice and sell it directly to the public—effectively liberalizing the importation of rice. Currently the NFA can directly buy rice from local farmers but is limited to maintaining a buffer stock of around 555,000 MT or 15 days' worth of rice. The NFA at present, buys fresh palay (unhusked rice) for P19/kg and P24/kg for dried. Marcos states that they are trying to determine whether the current P19 and P24 prices are enough.

 

Trade deficit widens to $4.1bn in Mar

The March trade deficit widened to US$4.1bn, up 23% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in imports than exports. This brings the 1Q25 trade deficit to P12.7bn, 13% up YoY. Imports for the month grew 12% YoY to US$10.7bn, a reversal from the 2% decline in Feb. Primarily driven by the growth in imports across all categories except fuel. Exports grew 6% YoY to US$6.59bn, the third month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction. Electronics exports were flat at $3.64bn, a turnaround from the 3% YoY increase in Feb. These made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 13% YoY to US$2.95bn.

 

Gov't posts P67bn budget surplus in April

The government recorded a budget surplus of P67.3bn in April which is higher 57% YoY and a reversal from the P376bn deficit in March. This brings the 4M25 deficit to P411.5bn, wider 79% YoY. The Bureau of Treasury stated the higher cumulative deficit was due to "faster expansion in public spending to fuel economic activity and support priority programs of the Marcos administration". Revenue collections in Apr inched down 2.8% YoY to P522.1bn, as non-tax revenues dipped 69% YoY due to the earlier timing of remittances from Government Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs) last year. Expenditures slowed 8% YoY in Apr to P454.8bn mainly driven by lower primary spending and interest payments. The gov't targets for the 2025 budget deficit to narrow to 5.3% (1.5trn) of GDP from 5.7% in 2024, and to further reduce this to 3.7% in 2028.

 

Government debt inches up to P16.68tn in end-Mar

The government's total debt stock went up by 0.31% MoM to P16.68tn as of end-Mar. This was due to the net issuance of new domestic and external debt which was offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P57.897 end-Feb to P57.382 end-Mar) as well as net repayments. Domestic debt rose by 1.4% while external debt decreased by 1.9%, taking the current debt mix of the country's total obligations to 68%/32% domestic/external. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn, up 8% YoY and equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%.

 

OFW Remittances up 2.6% YoY in March

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.6% YoY to US$2.81bn, a slight deceleration from the 2.7% growth seen in February. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their percentage contribution to the total and significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth slowed to 1.0% YoY from 1.2% in February. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 5.5%.

 

 

PH Dollar reserves down 1.9% MoM at end-April

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Apr saw a 1.9% MoM decrease to $104.6bn from $106.7bn at end-Feb. The decrease primarily reflected the national government's drawdowns on foreign currency deposits with the BSP to meet external debt obligations as well as the BSP's net foreign exchange operations. The level of dollar reserves by end-Apr was enough to cover about 3.6x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.2x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Reserve position in the IMF jumped 13.6% MoM to $742mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $13.3bn, which rose 4.5% MoM as gold continues hit new all-time highs in April. Foreign investments decreased by 3.2% MoM to $86.1bn. Foreign currency deposits surged 23.5% MoM to $649mn. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025.

 

Infrastructure spending rises by 19% YoY in the first two months of 2025

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 19% YoY to P182.9bn in 2M25. For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP. Recall that the gov't spent 1.55tn (+9% YoY) or 5.8% of GDP back in 2024. The DBM (Department of Budget and Management) noted that while disbursements likely accelerated before the March 28 election ban on public funds, spending may temporarily slow in April but is expected to pick up again from late May to June once the ban is lifted.

 

 

BPO sector to hit US$40bn in revenue this year

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it would hit its US$40bn revenue target this year. This represents c. 5% growth from US$38bn in 2024. It is also projecting the sector to employ 2mn Filipinos in 12 to 18 months. The sector had 1.82mn employees at the end of 2024.

 

Commercial banks' loan growth moderates to 11.8% in March; M3 growth slows to 6.1%

Loans of commercial banks rose by 11.8% YoY in March, a tad slower than the 12.2% rate in February. Loans to productive activities grew 10.9%, moderating from 11.2% in February due to slower growth in retail (+11.6% from 13.7%) and real estate (+9.6% from 9.9%). Consumer loans grew 23.6%, slightly slower than the 24.1% pace in February. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 6.1% in March, slightly slower than the 6.3% growth rate in February.

 

Vehicle sales fall 10% YoY in April; Toyota sales drop 8%

CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales fell 10% YoY, 17% MoM to 33,580 units in April. Cited seasonal factors, economic conditions, and evolving consumer demands. Adjusting for Easter break, combined sales for the months of March and April rose 1% YoY, moderating from the 6% pace in 2M25. Note that CAMPI/TMA’s data does not include sales of BYD. Brings 4M25 industry car sales up 2.5% YoY to 150,654 units. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 16,414 units in April, down 8% YoY and 13% MoM. Brings TMP’s 4M25 industry car sales up 6% YoY to 71,927 units. TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year; To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 20.4k units per month.

 

DENR to tighten ECC rules following Manila Bay assessment

The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said it would tighten its guidelines on the issuance of Environmental Compliance Certificates (ECC) after its Manila Bay Cumulative Impact Assessment revealed severe environmental threats. This could include ECCs that have been issued. The assessment revealed that the 21 proposed and ongoing reclamation projects in Greater Metro Manila could threaten marine ecosystems and fisherman’s livelihoods, and worsen flooding in urban areas. Environment Secretary Maria Antonia Yulo Loyzaga said that Manila Bay will be part of a continuing review. “There is in the ECC a section that will allow us to modify any approval that has been granted, and we take that section very seriously in light of the results of the cumulative impact assessment phase one.” Part of the review process will be a consultation with the Philippine Reclamation Authority, proponent local government units, and stakeholders to determine whether the ECCs need to be revised or amended.

Philippine gross gaming revenue rises 27% YoY in 1Q25

PAGCOR reported that the industry’s GGR rose 27% YoY (-2% QoQ) to P104bn. GGR from Bingo and electronic games grew 88% YoY (flat QoQ) to P51.4bn. For the most part, the fees charged by PAGCOR on e-games fell 5ppt QoQ to 30%. GGR from licensed casinos fell 1% YoY (down 5% QoQ) to P49.3bn. Solaire North was only opened in late 2Q25. Okada Manila’s 1Q25 GGR fell 13% QoQ and 12% YoY to P7.8bn.

 

Marcos retains economic team but makes changes in other departments

Following President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s call for voluntary resignations to his cabinet, fifty-two (52) Executive Branch officials submitted their courtesy resignation. Those who will remain in the Cabinet are Trade Secretary Cristina Roque, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, DepDev (Department of Economy, Planning, and Dev't) Secretary Arsenio Baliscan, Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman, and SAPIEA (Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs) Frederick Go. The five economic team members were retained given their importance in keeping the country's "economic health" amidst current domestic and international headwinds. Meanwhile, Energy Secretary Raphael Lotilla was reassigned to be the new head of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), replacing Maria Antonia Yulo-Loyzaga. DOE undersecretary Sharon Garin will serve as the Officer-in-Charge for the DOE. The ongoing performance review for the rest of the fifty-two officials will be completed in the coming days. Results of said review and any further "calibrations" to the cabinet will be announced in the following days as well.

 

Marcos' trust rating inches up 3ppt to 32% in May

In the latest Pulse Asia Research Survey, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s trust rating improved slightly to 32% from 29% in April. Notably, this is still much lower than the 82% trust rating at the start of his term as well as his 42% trust rating in Feb prior to dropping to 25% in March following the arrest of Former President Rodrigo Duterte. Marcos' trust rating in Luzon stands at 41% in May from 34% in April, Visayas at 39% from 38%, Mindanao at 3% from 10%. Vice President Sara Duterte's trust rating saw a 7ppt dip to 50% in May from 57% in April. Duterte's trust rating in Luzon was at 29% from 37%, Visayas at 46% from 70%, Mindanao at 97% from 95%.
