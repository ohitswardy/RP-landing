REGIS REPORT

22 October 2025
Conference Wrap: Key Themes and Company Highlights
 

The conference attracted 128 investor sign-ups from 52 institutions, reflecting healthy interest from both domestic and foreign funds. We facilitated 186 one-on-one and group meetings — 88 on Day 1 and 98 on Day 2 — with 33 participating corporates and 112 company representatives, complemented by three on-site tours across three themes: auto and EVs, consumer and retail, and fintech. In our view, this level of participation indicates that investors continue to monitor Philippine opportunities closely, weighing valuation appeal against near-term macro risks.

That said, even as investor interactions remained upbeat, the prevailing tone was more neutral and restrained toward the market. Foreigners continue to point to poor market liquidity as a large deterrent, while for many fund managers, cheap valuations alone (PCOMP at 9x!) are not enough reason to pull the trigger. Investors sought comments from corporates on the fallout from the flood control corruption scandal and implications on the macro outlook. Outside of these concerns, lacklustre expectations for 3Q corporate earnings (save for banks and select names) added to the broad somber tone throughout the conference.

Across sectors, Banks may prove resilient once more with steady loan growth and supported margins even amid monetary easing, while valuations remain compelling. Property players are grappling with weaker residential demand and are shifting focus toward recurring income streams, while valuations hover near historical troughs. Conglomerates are trading at record NAV discounts, with autos a space to watch but property exposure still a drag. Ports and logistics stand out as ICT progresses in Durban and benefits from Mexico growth, while Power and Utilities continue to expand renewables capacity though face near-term demand and spot price headwinds. Telcos will navigate regulatory shifts under the KPA but find upside in fintech arms, and Consumers and Retailers show mixed demand trends with pressures from down-trading and on discretionary spending. Finally, Gaming operators continue to see persistent VIP softness and are challenged by regulatory overhang on online gaming.

Overall, discussions struck a more pragmatic tone, with investors focusing on operational resilience, earnings visibility, and yield support, as valuations across key sectors remain undemanding amid lingering domestic and global headwinds.

 
 
 
 
BANKS

The outlook for Banks remains resilient, with loans still steadily growing and NIMs still largely supported despite the easing cycle. It's largely status quo for the Big Three, and valuations are attractive at just around 1x PBR for BPI and BDO while MBT is only at 0.7x. Mid-size CBC is trading nearly at par with BPI and BDO with comparable ROE, while SECB is only at 0.3x as it aims to get ROE up to the 10%-level.

 

BDO (BDO PM)

14 October 2025 | 11:00 HKT

Speakers: Andrew Contreras – AVP, IR & Industry Research; Jerome Guevarra – Chief of Staff; Luis Reyes – Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • Loans. Growth is trending similarly to clips seen in June at 13+%. On full year basis, could see a bit of tempering due to base effects, with a 5% QoQ growth in the loan portfolio in 4Q last year. Management is confident however that loans could still deliver QoQ incremental growth.
  • Asset quality. Saw slight increase in absolute amounts but still trending lower as a proportion to the loan book.  Management considers the slight increase still manageable and expected due to changes in loan mix. Share of consumer was 25% of loan book in 1H25. 
  • Credit cost. Management is comfortable with the same guidance they had at the start of the year - 40-45bp in credit costs.
  • Margins. Margin outlook is stable for the year, with management still looking at NIMs settling at 4.3%.  Management continues to estimate 4bp sensitivity to every quarter cut in BSP policy rate though loan/asset mix is helping asset yields.
  • Cost to income. Hoping to see cost to income trend down over time from high 50s currently. Opex side to remain elevated in near term due to continuing IT spend but management hopes to see this gradually coming down in the next few years.  IT spend is about 17-18% of total opex currently. Coupled with revenue gains from IT investments, management hopes to for cost to income to hit mid 50s in the mid term.

Actionable idea: The stock is trading at FY26E P/BV of 1x with prospective ROE of 14%.  Loan growth remains steady while asset quality remains manageable. Regis rates BDO a BUY with TP of 200. 

 

BPI (BPI PM)

14 October 2025 | 14:00 HKT

Speakers: Haj Narvaez – Head of Corporate Planning & IR; Eliza May Taco – Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • Consumer lending. Orientation continues to be growing the consumer book. In terms of mix, the share of consumer looks to still be pointing up, with BPI already reaching its goal of non-institutional loans accounting for 30% of the loan portfolio – a goal initially set for 2026.
  • Loan growth. Corporate loans seeing some acceleration from last year, slightly better-than-expected. However, the flood control issue may give pause to corporates in the meantime. The consumer segment however is expected to continue to drive growth.  Overall, BPI is looking to sustain 11-13% clip in terms over loan growth.
  • Margins. Management believes NIMs can be supported at the 4.6% level, as the impact of rate cuts is expected to be mitigate by the shift in loan mix towards non institutional (now 30% of total loans).
  • Credit cost. Pick up in NPL ratio came from smaller unsecured businesses from microfinance and business bank which saw aggressive growth. Credit costs are expected at 65-70bp for FY25. Management is fairly comfortable at keeping NPL cover at 95-100% in the near term.

Actionable idea: Our banks analyst Rafa Garchitorena rates BPI a BUY with a TP of 160/sh, representing ~45% upside from current levels. The stock has been sold off aggressively and is looking attractive at 1.1x P/BV with a prospective ROE of 14.6% FY26E.

 

China Banking Corp (CBC PM)

14 October 2025 | 14:00 HKT

Speakers: Patrick Cheng – CFO; Gerald Florencio - Investor and Corporate Relations Group Head

Key Takeaways:

  • Consumer loans – nearly doubled from P123bn in 2001 to P238bn in 1H25, while NPLs have come down from 4.9% to 2.5% in the same time frame. Consumer now 25% of total loans (from 20% in 2021), targeting high-20s. Strategy is really to broaden customer base. The idea of just catering to wholesale customers and financial markets “has run its course”. Also help defend NIMs in lower interest rate environment. Deposit costs also improving since CASA ratio is low compared to Big 3.
  • Credit cards – Pushing aggressively, but still “measured”. CC receivables were just P1.4bn in 2021, at P7.7bn in 1H25. Cards-in-force from 79,000  in 2021 to 414,000 in 1H25 (with target of 550k by end-2025E, and 1mn in 2027E). Offering “Ultra Rewards”, 50% discounts at restaurants, etc. Growing outside of existing customer base through it s”30-Minute Instant Card Issuance” program kiosks at malls (mostly SM). Show existing credit card, and CBC will check customer’s standing with TransUnion (credit bureau) and, if rating is good, can get approved in 30 mins. (But low credit limit to begin with.)
  • Provisions – did a “pre-emptive provision” of P6bn in 2Q25, bringing NPL cover from 108% to 125%, offset against a land revaluation gain. Protect against “bad sentiment” and potential impact on credit quality.
  • IT vs branches – slowly ramping up IT spend. But board needs to decide which route to go – Digital or Branches. Can’t spend on both, lack scale. Pre-pandemic, digital was 10% of transactions, now >60%. “Its expensive!”, and management will need to justify the payback to the board.
  • ROE and growth (organic vs M&A) – Board asking what it will take to catch up to “Big Three” by assets (BPI and MBT double CBC). CFO said need to grow 30% pa; but in his experience, pushing 30% growth over many years “always ends in tears”. Might also require capital, which board/shareholders may not be willing to fund. Prefers to keep ROEs in the 15% range – any higher will be quite difficult to sustain. Always open to M&A (or foreign equity partner), but needs to be “transformative” in terms of scale / new business (more consumer).

Actionable idea: (CBC stock not rated) Stock is trading at 0.94x trailing book value; not far off from BPI/BDO with similar ROEs. Stock is down 36% from recent high, on speculation that the PSE may revise index inclusion/weight rules based on stock liquidity vs declared free float. CBC currently claim float of 58%. Ironically easier to bring declared float down than to increase liquidity.

 

Metrobank (MBT PM)

15 October 2025 | 11:00 HKT

Speakers:  Minda Olonan – FVP, Head of IR; Jose Lim – Investor Relations; Dominique Ravelas – Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • Provisions. Credit cost was 63bp in 1H24. Management noted that the bank’s expansion in the consumer segment has been contained and is therefore comfortable with current provisioning levels.
  • Cost to income. The bank would like to keep this at low 50s on a year basis.  The bank is still ramping up on IT spend but managing all other expense items.

Actionable idea: BUY MBT with PBR of 0.7x and 7.2% yield support.

 

Security Bank Corp (SECB PM)

14 October 2025 | 16:00 HKT

Speakers: Allen Reyes – CFO; Ropi Dangaso - Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • New management team – Mr Allen Reyes joined as CFO in August, and the bank recently announced a new CEO – Mr Victor Lee – who will take over in Jan 2026. Mr Lee was until recently head of CIMB in Singapore, where he reportedly doubled revenues and took ROEs to c20%. His focus seems to be on consumer and wealth management, two key pillars of SECB’s growth strategy.
  • Core business doing well – HCPH accretive – SECB’s 1H25 revenues surged 23%, with strong growth across business lines. Consumer/MSME loans grew 32%, now accounting for 36% of total loans. This helped loan yields jump to 8.71% up 30bps QoQ and 42bps YoY. NIMs expanded 18bps QoQ as funding costs also improved with lower policy interest rates. Meanwhile, the bank closed the acquisition of 25% of Home Credit Phils (HCPH) – a leader in white-goods / appliance / mobile phones finance – in 2Q25, which should be accretive on Year 1. While there is little synergy for SECB to tap HCPH’s customers, the bank hopes to cross sell into HCPH’s network of retailer partners.
  • Provisions – The major pain point has been credit quality, particularly with their credit card business, with large write-offs weighing on profits in 2024/25E. Management are in active clean-up mode, and have guided for credit costs to peak in 3Q25, moderating in 4Q (FY25E guidance 150bps). No guidance yet for 2026E. Provisions should fall as the bank re-focuses its target customer base to high net worth, and “mass-affluent” customers, and improves underwriting / collection policies.
  • Capital constrained – sustainable profitable growth – The rapid loan growth in recent years, plus the HCPH acquisition have put a strain on capital, with CET-1 (solo) down to 12.15% as of 1H25 (minimum is c10%). The bank has guided for loan growth to moderate to c10%, focusing on quality, sustainable, profitable growth. Maintain committed to P3/sh annual dividend, will reconsider increasing once ROE improves. Equity raising not being discussed; prefer to conserve capital / grow capital via higher profit.
  • ROE target 10% near term – SECB maintains its near-term target ROE of 10% (from c8% currently). Focus on continuing to grow revenues, and control provisions. Opex growth should also begin to moderate with completion of IT / app rollout in 2H26, creating some “positive jaws”. (REGIS forecast ROE 9.7% in 2025E and 10.5% in 2026E.)

Actionable idea: (BUY – TP P150) Stock has been severely punished for recurring credit cost / low ROE. But current valuations seem way overdone – at 0.32x P/bk and 3.5x PER on our FY2026E (8% above consensus). Also pays a P3/sh dividend, translating to a 4.3% yield. However, market may need to be convinced that the credit issues are behind them before buying interest returns in earnest.

 

 
PROPERTY

In the Property sector, the residential segment is seeing weakness from the fallout of the corruption scandal at the public works department -- ALI is cutting launches and targets further on account of slower resi, while RLC already noted weak JV sales from its affluent target market. All the more now that focus is shifting towards recurring income streams. Mall renovations are ongoing for both ALI and SMPH while for office, a major soundbite is the "Keep Call Centers in America" Bill which developers don't seem too concerned about. Investors are still holding their breath for further updates on the SMPH reclamation. The sector is trading near historical trough valuations.

 

Ayala Land (ALI PM)

15 October 2025 | 15:00 HKT

Speakers: Joahnna Soriano – Head of Investor Relations; Michael Aquilizan – IR manager

Key Takeaways:

  • The company lowered some of its targets. 1) It reduced its 2025E residential launch target from P80bn to P60bn. 2) It lowered its 2025E capex budget from P95bn to P80bn. 3) It also lowered its net income growth target from 10%-12% to 6%-8%.

  • Ayala Land wants to infuse assets into AREIT at a faster pace. While its official target is to infuse P15bn-20bn worth of rental assets annually to AREIT, it hopes to spin off a larger amount than this. (It infused P21bn worth of assets into AREIT in 2025)
  • The company has been utilizing 800 hectares annually, in line with its target to reduce its land bank in order to improve returns. 

Actionable Ideas: Buy. Ayala Land is trading at 58% discount to NAV, 1.0X FY26E P/B, and 10.8X FY26E P/E, all of which are close to record lows. We forecast 7% net profit CAGR from 2024-2027E, in line with management’s lower guidance.

 

Robinsons Land (RLC PM)

15 October 2025 | 09:00 HKT

Speakers: Kerwin Max Tan – CFO; Cialeit Francisco – Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • RLC is targeting to grow its recurring net profit to P25bn by 2030. This would represent 12% CAGR from 2024. This will be achieved by growing its mall (7% CAGR), office (7% CAGR), and warehouse portfolios (14% CAGR) over the medium term.

Actionable Ideas: Buy. RLC offers 5% dividend yield and is trading at 69% discount to NAV, 0.4X FY26E P/B, and 5.1X FY26E P/E, all of which are close to record lows. Furthermore, the value of its stake in RCR is about 20% higher than its own market cap.

CONGLOMERATES

Conglo valauations are also near record lows with widening discounts to NAV. Auto is a hot space with BYD -- distributed under AC's auto unit AC Mobility -- eating up share from ICE vehicles, while GTCAP's Toyota is maintaining market leadership while also launching in the HEV space to remain competitive and relevant amid changing consumer preferences. Ayala Corp (AC PM)

14 October 2025 | 09:00 HKT

Speakers: Alberto de Larrazabal – CFO; Juan Carlos Syquia – Deputy CFO; Johann-David Eduardo Quiazon – Sr. Manager - Investor Relations; Jose Martin Lopez – Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • Mobility strategy – dual focus on ICE and EV. AC is committed to being present in both internal combustion engine (ICE) and electric vehicle (EV) markets. In the ICE segment, its remaining brands Isuzu and Kia service their own niches — Isuzu with ICE pick-ups, while Kia will remain a “bridge”, operating in both ICE and EV spaces. Under its distribution agreement with BYD, AC Mobility is focused on its dealership rollout where it is on track to overtake Toyota by YE-25. It aims to have 700 charging stations in 100 locations. AC Mobility sold 20,000 vehicles in 1H25, with 50% from new energy vehicles (NEVs), and has achieved its YE target market share of 7–8% as of 1H25.
  • More spending on mobility, consumer ventures. AC has earmarked P230bn for group capex. Beyond mobility, another area of focus will be the store rollout for its growing consumer segment. Australian home improvement retailer Anko will have 5 stores by YE-25 which will double next year. For its recently-announced partnership with big box retailer Makro from Thailand, the first Makro store should open by 2026.  It also recently announced a joint venture with high-end supermarket chain Spinney’s from the UAE. AC’s approach to its consumer ventures is its ability to scale within Ayala Land’s malls and estates. 
  • Property outlook remains soft. AC reiterated ALI’s renewed focus on premium real estate, where it is better-positioned than competition. AC management believes the industry will probably take 3 years to cycle out.
  • Long-term planning, investment strategy. AC has largely shifted its mindset from looking three generations down to forming more focused, nearer term strategies, prioritizing clarity and profitability within defined timeframes. A key driver for its investment decisions is getting to profitability in no longer than 5 years, with strong potential for dividends shortly after. AC noted the timeframe for BYD could exceed this measure, tracking profitability by 3 years.

Actionable idea: AC trades near historical trough valuations, but as with most of the market. For 85% of its NAV, investors can go directly into the listed subs. Key for mid- to long-term re-rating will be the growth of its auto business but this alone is not a compelling enough reason to buy the stock now. Performance could continue to lag as long as the property segment remains a drag.

 

DMCI Holdings (DMC PM) / Semirara Mining & Power Corp (SCC PM)

15 October 2025 | 10:00 HKT

Speaker(s):  Isidro Consunji – Chairman; Joseph Legasto – Deputy CFO; Cristina Gotianun – President & COO Semirara; Brian Lim – VP CFO & Treasurer, Concreat; Hannah Chan – Investor Relations

Key Takeaways:

  • Coal contract extension remains unresolved. Semirara is still awaiting government approval for its coal operating contract extension. Leadership changes at the Departments of Justice and Energy have delayed discussions, prompting management to postpone major capital spending until the issue is resolved. Management remains optimistic about securing the extension, noting that even in the event of a re-bid, the group would retain a strong competitive edge given its existing mining equipment, power and port facilities, as well as mining capabilities.
  • Power unit faces tough spot market.  The on-grid power unit under Semirara continues to face pressure from low spot prices, with only 54% of its capacity currently contracted. The surge in solar capacity has weighed on peak-hour prices, while the unexpected PPA extension of Sta. Rita gas plant and Meralco has further limited opportunities for spot price improvements.  Its off-grid unit, however, continues to explore opportunities for renewable technology in their areas, with the successful completion of a 4MW wind facility in Semirara.
  • Sees lower housing inventory levels next year.  Management believes residential RFO inventory of over P20bn today will fall next year, as long as sales are maintained at over P30bn. 
  • Nickel production is expected to more than double in two years’ time.  Under existing permits, the nickel mining unit is positioned to raise production to over 5mn MT by 2027, up from roughly 1.8 mn MT at present. Faster permitting approvals could support additional upside, as management estimates its nickel resources at over 500mn MT. 

Actionable idea: We view Semirara’s risk-reward profile as tilted to the downside, with the current share price seemingly reflecting expectations of a coal operating contract extension. Our estimates suggest fair value could decline to P21/sh in a non-extension scenario, though the stock continues to offer an attractive 10% dividend yield.  Meanwhile, given that Semirara contributes roughly 60% of DMCI’s profits and NAV, the stock appears fairly valued, trading at only a 13% discount to NAV based on market prices. Nonetheless, its 8% dividend yield remains a potential draw.

On store expansions, SEVN lost some sites to Dali on bids and Alfamart has been opening stores closer to residents which has backfired as they’re closing some stores due to cannibalization.
  • Aggressive expansion into provincial areas where there is virtually no competitors present, store performance here is 1.1-1.15x better than stores located in metro manila. Competitors are not present in these areas due to the lack of supply lines.

Actionable Idea: BUY, but downside risk to numbers remains with Alfamart’s aggressive expansion plan. Believe SEVN has a clear strategy here: 1. Prioritise store expansion within provincial areas given the lack of competition given its extensive supply chain. 2. CVS+ strategy shaping up well as we’re seeing sales growth coming through. Stock has fallen >40% off its recent peak.

 

Puregold (PGOLD PM)

Speakers: John Hao – Investor Relations; Anthony Sy – President of S&R

  • 3Q so far was good with July and August tracking within mid-single-digit in terms of SSSG. Recent earthquakes in Cebu and Davao have not affected stores materially, as stores remain operational with no significant or zero damages.

  • Ramp-up in fresh (vegetables/greens and frozen meats) offerings to increase frequency of store visits to overall boost sales. Actionable idea: BUY as the company has consistently put out strong results with double-digit topline growth, the highest vs other retail peers, despite continued expansion of smaller formatted peers. Even if the stock is already up ~30% YTD there is still room for upside—the stock currently trades at FY25E/FY26E PE multiples of 10.5x/9.6x, still cheap relative to other consumer names.

Bloomberry (BLOOM PM)

14 October 2025 | 13:00 HKT

Speakers: Estella Tuason-Occeña – CFO; Jonas Ramos – Executive Director for Investor Relations; Fritz Lacap – Vice President; Robin Venturina – Investor Relations Senior Manager

Key Takeaways:

  • Shift toward mass and premium mass segments. BLOOM has reduced VIP tables at Solaire Entertainment City (SEC) by 100 as it pivots toward mass and premium mass gaming. Current run rates show VIP now contributes only ~20% of GGR. 
  • Growing online presence. BLOOM’s online business is generating P300m in monthly GGR. It has two products – (i) Solaire Online which is geared towards high-end/premium, and positioned as a complement to on-premise gaming which remains at 90% of the player wallet; and (ii) Mega FUNalo for mainstream online, targeting the lower B, C, and D markets (and competing with the likes of PLUS), which was soft-launched in June. Management aims for online gaming to contribute positively to topline and EBITDA by 2026.
  • Solaire North driving market share gains. The opening of Solaire North (SN) has boosted BLOOM’s overall market share from 30-35% to 40%, though SN’s GGR remains about half that of SEC. SN foot traffic is comparable to SEC at 25,000 daily visits, but customer spending is significantly lower (at P10k vs. P100k at SEC), with a predominantly local clientele. Management aims to reach breakeven at SN by 2026.
  • Philippines gaming market still under pressure. While total Philippine GGR grew 25%, Metro Manila’s physical casino GGR declined 5% YoY, impacted by the POGO sector’s exit. That said, without SN’s contribution, the drop would have been more pronounced – i.e., SN was still able to capture players from previously untapped areas of the metro.
  • New supply coming to EC. The new property opening in EC will be operated by Newport World, which has shifted its strategy aggressively from looking equally at mass and VIP to really focusing on VIP, offering comps that BLOOM cannot match as BLOOM is focused on profitability and not just on “buying” revenue. Nonetheless, management believes existing EC properties can benefit from a new player coming in, as cluster effect increases with 5 properties to visit in the gaming complex.

Actionable idea: Market will likely remain wait-and-see across several fronts – the regulatory overhang on online gaming, the pick-up in SN, and the impact of new supply in EC. At 7.0x fwd EV/EBITDA, BLOOM appears fairly valued considering the earnings risk from the aforementioned issues.

 

DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM)

14 October 2025 | 11:00 HKT

Speakers: Celeste Jovenir – VP; PJ Lee Hok – IR; Andy Tsui – President

Key Takeaways:

  • Regulatory Landscape & Market Dynamics. DigiPlus has actively supported Pagcor in Senate hearings to educate lawmakers on the risks of illegal gambling, which is estimated makes up 60% of the online gambling market.  Licensed operators like  DigiPlus are advocating for tighter regulations, not a ban, to protect legitimate businesses. Digiplus thinks normalization might be achieved Q4 2025 or Q1 2026. 3QF might see earnings slippage on a qoq basis as while GGR has fallen, there are still costs to cover like promo and mkting.
  • Land-Based Business Strategic Role & Long-Term Vision. DigiPlus sees land-based operations as complementary to its online business. Land-based venues help acquire and retain VIP players, who are crucial for profitability. While land-based business also benefit from lower tax rates vs online operators. Over the next 5 years, it expects the business mix to shift to 60% online / 40% land-based, from the current 70–80% online. No numbers on potential spend as yet (could also be M&A), but not expected to equity raise to fund it.
  • Online gaming industry still a growing market. 2025 growth expected at 20% YoY.

Actionable idea: 3Q will be a weak moment for PLUS due to the de-linking of e-wallets from online gambling platforms, however normalization is expected by 1Q26. Will remain watchful of the regulatory environment for now. Company is currently trading at fwd 5x PE, -1sd level from its 2yr mean.
