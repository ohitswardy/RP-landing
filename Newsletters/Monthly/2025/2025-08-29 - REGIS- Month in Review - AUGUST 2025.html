REGIS REPORT

29 August 2025
 

The PCOMP fell 1.6% to 6155.57, settling near its lowest levels since April. Shares continued to be dragged down by outflows despite the BSP’s third consecutive rate cut and continued expectations of at least two cuts by the FOMC before the year ends. Meanwhile, the 2Q corporate earnings season on the ground failed to materially inspire conviction on local shares.  Excluding extraordinaries, 2Q earnings for the market rose a mere 3% YoY. 

Inflation in July further decelerated to just 0.9% (vs 1.1% forecast and 1.4% prior), marking the lowest print since October 2019. This brought the 7M2025 inflation average to 1.7%, below the BSP’s target range of 2%-4%. Meanwhile, 2Q GDP growth looked generally inline with forecast at 5.5% (vs 5.4% forecast and 5.4% prior), albeit at the low-end of the government’s (already lowered) 5.5%-6.5% target range.

The continued easing in inflation, along with the recovery in the Peso, which gained +2% in August, paved the way for the BSP to cut rates for the third meeting in a row. The latest action brought down the benchmark rate to 5%. However, in his statement after the decision,  BSP Governor Remolona struck a less dovish tone and commented that the current setting is at a “sweet spot,” suggesting that he may be less inclined to trim rates further in the remaining policy meetings this year, as opposed to market expectations of at least one more cut.

The much-anticipated PCOMP review took place in mid-August. The rebal saw the addition of online gambling play PLUS in the main benchmark and the removal of integrated resort BLOOM -23.7%, which has also recently entered the contentious online gambling space. Despite its inclusion however, PLUS capped the month -8.7% as restrictions on online gambling soured sentiment. Online gambling  has now been officially delinked from e-wallets after an official directive from the BSP.

Another key event was the MSCI rebal during the final week of the month. The Philippines was initially expected to see inflows but ended up getting heavily weighed down by substantial foreign outflows, potentially as sellers sought to take advantage of the liquidity event.  The benchmark dropped by -2.2% during the session as foreign selling surged to over P2b, the largest daily outflow since the sizable placements in SM and SMPH that took place back in mid-July.

Key data
 
 

The market extended its net foreign outflow streak to five straight months. Outflow accelerated to US$74m from last month’s US$29m as investors continued to be in a risk off stance. Turnover meanwhile was flattish at US$137m buoyed by a couple of rebalancing events (PSEi and MSCi). Foreign participation stood at 46%, lower than last month’s 47%. 

  • Banks continued to be the top foreign sold names in the market amidst expectations of lower interest rates exerting downward pressure on NIMs, coupled with concerns on rising credit costs.  BDO was top foreign sold this month with US$39m in net outflows alongside BPI at US$16m.  Both names printed new 52-wk lows in August.
  • Telcos also saw sizeable foreign selling, as investors priced in new legislation seen to result in more competition. Some US$10.6m in net outflows were seen for CNVRG. The company reduced its guidance for FY25 growth to 10-12% (from 14-16%). PLDT also saw significant outflows.  SM and SMPH likewise saw continued foreign selling pressure after its recent private placement.
  • ICT meanwhile continued to be the most favored Philippine stock and briefly touched the P1 trillion mark in terms of market capitalization. ALI also led the top foreign bought ahead of an expected September Fed rate cut, alongside GTCAP after 1H25 core profit came in ahead of forecast, fueled by robust numbers from Toyota Motor Philippines.
  • Notable name this month: BLOOM saw a US$4.1m worth of outflow and the stock fell 24% after its deletion from the PSEi and the ongoing hearing on online gambling at the senate.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

In terms of sectors, among the worst performers during the month were telcos with CNVRG -21.7% being the worst in the benchmark.  TEL -12% and GLO -9.8% likewise suffered hefty losses. In focus on the ground during the last few weeks was the Konektadong Pinoy Act which has now officially lapsed into law after neither being signed nor vetoed by President Marcos. The new law essentially removes the barriers to entry in the telecom space as new entrants no longer need to seek a legislative franchise in order to provide telecom services in the country, among other measures.

Another weak space was Banks with the Big 3 all turning lower: MBT -5.7%, BDO -5.3% and BPI -4.4%. BDO and BPI were consistently among the top foreign sold names during the month, and both even marked new 52-week lows (during MSCI rebal day) – as they are also among the most widely-held names in the Philippines by foreign investors. The broad selloff instead appears to be driven by the outlook of lower interest rates as markets continue to price in at least 2 rate cuts by the Fed in the remainder of the year.

In contrast, Property names ALI +12% and RLC +1.4% benefitted from the lower rate theme – although SMPH -0.4%, which had a sizable private placement in July, continues to trail behind peers. 2Q results generally showed signs of sequential improvement in residential sales across the industry, helped by aggressive discounts and rent-to-own schemes.  In the same theme of dovish rate outlook, property REITs were broadly higher during the month: AREIT +6.4%, RCR +3.1%, MREIT +4.7%, AREIT and FILRT +6.7%.

Other notable winners in the benchmark were ICT +7.8% and GTCAP +15.1% as both were among the few stand outs in a generally lackluster 2Q results season. The latter was the top performing stock in the benchmark in August, helped also by the recovery in the PHP +2%.

Meanwhile, the consumer space was mixed with JFC +6.9% and PGOLD +3.1% outperforming peers after results showed that the former finally saw signs of recovery in its international business, while the latter maintained market-leading topline growth (2Q SSSG of over 6%). In contrast, manufacturers broadly trailed behind with URC -8.5%, MONDE -5.8% and CNPF -0.85%.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP cuts rate by 25bp to 5.00%; forecasts inflation to settle at 1.7% for 2025. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 5.00%. This is the third consecutive 25bp cut this year since previously cutting rates in April and June. BSP Governor Eli Remolona stated that the current policy rate level is the "sweet spot" or 'Goldilocks' rate given the latest data. However, he added that another cut this year is still a possibility depending on how the data develops for the rest of the year (e.g., weakness in demand).

The inflation outlook was noted to be very manageable and is seen to settle at 1.7% for 2025, 3.3% for 2026, and 3.4% for 2027. Primary risks were said to be potential electricity rate adjustments and increased rice tariffs. Average inflation for 7M25 is 1.7%.

The last two Monetary Board meetings for the year will be held on Oct 9 and Dec 11, respectively.

2Q25 GDP grows 5.5%—in line with expectations. GDP grew at 5.5% in 1Q, a tad higher than the 5.4% growth in 1Q25 but much lower than the 6.5% growth rate back in 2Q24. The 5.5% GDP growth is in line with consensus expectations of 5.5%. The print is at the lower end of the government’s 5.5–6.5% target range (was previously 6-7%).

On the demand side, the slight acceleration in growth from private consumption (+5.5%) was offset by the slowdown in gov't consumption (+8.7% from +18.7%) and capital formation (+0.6% from +4.8%).

Private investments dipped to 0.6% in 2Q from 4.8% in 1Q, the sharp decline was led by the fall in construction to 0.6% from 7.0% for the same period.  Gov't construction declined -7.7% in 2Q, a reversal from 9.1% growth in 1Q and offset sustained double-digit growth in private construction (+16.7% from +14.6%)

Exports grew for the third straight quarter (+4.4%), while imports tempered to 2.9% from 10.3% in 1Q. Merchandise exports grew 13.6% from 8.1% seeing a boost from frontloading prior to the effectivity of higher US tariffs

On the supply side, growth was led by the services sector at 6.3% but was slower than the 7.0% expansion in 1Q24. 

The reading brings 1H25 GDP growth to 5.4%, right at the lower end of the government's target of 5.5-6.5% and in line with consensus and REGIS expectations of 5.5% GDP growth for 2025. GDP growth for 2H25 needs to average 5.6% to hit the minimum full-year target.

Inflation rate dips to 0.9% in July. Headline inflation fell to 0.9% in July from 1.4% in the previous month of June. This is slightly ahead of the consensus forecast of 1.1% and stands well within the BSP's forecast range of 0.5-1.3%. This is also the lowest inflation print since Oct 2019 (0.6%). Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past five months.

Primary drivers for the continued benign inflation were YoY price declines in rice (-15.9%) and transport (-2.0%) as well as the slower price acceleration in electricity, gas, fuel (+1.5% from +5.5% in June), which offset rising prices of meat (+8.8%) and fish (+6.3%). Notably, rice prices are on their thirteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Aug 2), prices for local and imported well-milled rice range from P37-52/kg and P38-50/kg, respectively vs. P42-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 2.

This brings the YT-Jun headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. Consensus expects 2025 inflation to be 2.0%, while Regis expects the full year average to be 1.8%.

BOP shifts to US$167mn deficit in July; GIR settles at US$105.4bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$167mn, marking a reversal from both the US$62bn surplus in July 2024 and June 2025's US$226mn surplus. The deficit reflected the national gov't's drawdowns on its foreign currency deposits with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) to service debt obligations. This brings the YT-July deficit at US$5.8bn, reversing from the US$1.5bn surplus in the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade in goods gap and external debt repayments.

The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (-1.3% of GDP). This reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments.

Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-July stood at US$105.4bn, down from the $106bn in end-June. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025.

OFW Remittances up 3.7% YoY in June. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.7% YoY to US$3.0bn, accelerating from the 2.9% growth seen in May. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, and Saudi Arabia were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 3.5% YoY from 3.1% in May. In PHP terms, remittances declined by 0.5% YoY given the appreciation of the peso.

 

Government posts P19bn budget deficit in July; P784bn deficit as of 7M25. The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P19bn in July, which is narrower both by 34% YoY and 92% MoM. This brings the 7M25 deficit to P784bn, 22% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "well within target and on track with the P1.56trn full-year program" as the gov't continues to ramp up public spending to support economic growth. The wider YTD deficit results from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues as customs collections continues to lag the full-year target.

Revenue collections in July increased by a modest 3% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues. Expenditures inched up 1% YoY in July as primary spending was weighed down by delayed disbursements. Primary spending for the month was lower by 5% - this slowdown was said to stem from timing of big-ticket disbursements of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways, Dept. of Social Welfare and Development, and Dept. of National Defense. Interest payments jumped 34% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

Debt-to-GDP ratio grows to 63.1% as of end-June. Debt-to-GDP ratio as of end-June increased to 63.1% of GDP, up from 62% the prior quarter and 60.7% at end-2024. Outstanding debt as of end-June stood at P17.27trn (+12% YoY, +2.1% MoM). The gov't targets the debt-to-GDP ratio to decline to 60.4% by end-2025. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%

 

Infrastructure spending picks up in June, reversing slump during election ban. Overall infrastructure spending (incl. infra components of subsidy and equity to GOCCs and LGU transfers) rose 17% MoM and 3% YoY to P162.5bn in June. This was driven by a rebound in public works spending after declining in April/May or during the election ban. Officials also cited base effects from sizable releases for the Dept. of National Defense’s (DND) modernization program in June last year (bulk of DND’s big-ticket releases are expected in 2H25). This brings 1H25 infra spend to P720bn, flat YoY and broadly in line with the 1H government’s target. The government is targeting to spend P1.51tn on infrastructure in 2025 (-2.1% YoY, equivalent to 5.3% of GDP).

Commercial banks’ loan growth 12.1% in June; M3 growth at 6.3%. Loans of commercial banks rose by 12.1% YoY in June, an acceleration from the 11.3% rate in May. Loans to productive activities increased 11.1% YoY, growing faster than the 10.2% in May due to increased lending in key industries such as: Electricity, gas, steam, air conditioning supply (+29.2% from +25.6%), transpo, storage (+15.9% from +14.0%), finance, insurance (+12.0% from +9.6%), and real estate (+9.9% from +8.7%). Consumer loans grew 24.0%, slightly faster than the 23.7% pace in May. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up to 6.3% in June, accelerating from the 5.5% growth in May.

 

Heavy rains, tax on pickup trucks dampen vehicle sales (ex-BYD) in July. CAMPI reported vehicle sales fell 2.6% YoY, 5.4% MoM to 38,295 units. CAMPI cited weather-related disruptions, but we suspect the imposition of excise taxes on pickup trucks last July 1 may have also contributed to the weak sales (Toyota raised prices for the Hilux by 10-20%). ote that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales. The latest data brings YT-July car sales to 269,207 units, up 1.4% YoY.

BSP orders supervised institutions to remove links to online gambling. On August 15, the BSP issued a directive to all BSP-supervised Institutions, including e-wallets, to remove links providing in-app gambling access from their payment apps and websites within 48 hours. Suspended in-app gambling access includes any product/service/feature that redirects an account holder to a gaming/gambling site. While links that redirect to online gambling platforms in the e-wallets will be removed, e-wallets can likely still be used to fund online gambling.

Konektadong Pinoy Act becomes law. The bill further liberalizing the telecom sector (“Konektadong Pinoy Act”) has become law, thirty days after the approved bill was submitted to President. The Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) will allow operators to engage in “data transmission” services without needing to secure a franchise from Congress.  The new law also calls for the establishment of a “spectrum management policy framework” (SMPF) to govern the allocation and assignment of spectrum. The SMPF will also allow for the recall of spectrum in line with the SMPF.  The government will publish a list of infrastructure assets (“access list”) that it deems necessary to open up in order to increase competition and improve services. 

The entities that own these assets (“access providers”) are required to allow new operators to use such on a non-discriminatory basis. The new law provides more powers to the DICT and NTC. Among these powers are the assignment and recall of spectrum, the issuance of licenses to new operators and also the inclusion or exclusion of assets that will existing operators will be required to share (and also the terms for such sharing).

US Democratic and Republican senators file "Keep Call Centers" in America Bill. US Senators Ruben Gallego (Democrat) and Jim Justice (Republican) filed a bill that seeks to penalize firms that outsource call centers operations. The Secretary of Labor will be required to maintain a publicly available list of all employers that relocate call center or contract center work overseas. Such companies will not be eligible for Federal grants or guaranteed loans. The head of an agency, when awarding a Federal contract, shall give preference to a United States employer that does not appear on the list. Customer service agents must inform the customer of his/her physical location and the customer may request to be immediately transferred to a customer service agent who is physically located in the US

The IT-BPO Association previously estimated that outsourcing work to the Philippines (from the US) could result in cost savings of about 80%. About 70% of the Philippine BPO industry caters to North America and about 70% of BPO revenue is from voice operations. BPO firms account for 60%-80% of the occupied office space of the developers we cover.
