REGIS REPORT

31 December 2025
 

Philippine shares extended gains into the last month of the year, with the PCOMP sustaining above the 6,000-mark and ultimately closing out December higher by 0.5% or 30.68 pts MoM to 6,052.92. On macro drivers, softer-than-expected November inflation allowed the BSP to deliver a widely-anticipated rate cut and signal that the easing cycle is nearing its end. While this improved the medium-term policy backdrop, concerns around slowing domestic growth continued to cap risk appetite. Foreign investors remained consistent sellers throughout the month, particularly in banks, property, and consumer bellwethers, while local buying supported the market.

 

Cyclicals notably outperformed in December with Property (+2.6% MoM) and Financials (+2.2% MoM) leading gains among sector indices -- that is, only after Mining (+9.8% MoM) which saw gold miners rally in-step with record metal prices. ALI (+10.3% MoM) was the second largest positive mover for the index after ICT (+4.5% MoM), which hit fresh all-time-highs after officially securing the Durban port concession, not to mention positive developments in its domestic business as well.

 

For the year, the PCOMP clocked a 7.3% YoY decline, vastly underperforming vs Asian equity benchmarks that mostly finished 2025 with double digit gains. Philippine equities saw a weak start to the year amid the higher-for-longer narrative, PCOMP rebalancing outflows, and Trump tariff pronouncements, before softer inflation and a shift in the monetary policy outlook ushered in a significant rebound back to the 6,500-mark by mid-2Q. However, this was quickly reversed as disappointing 1Q earnings and the huge miss in 1Q GDP dampened sentiment. Towards end-3Q, the market was rattled by the P1-tn ($17-bn) 'flood control' corruption scandal at the national public works department, which is seen undermining domestic growth, consumer and business confidence, and investor sentiment. Soft 3Q corporate results and another round of macro disappointment further weighed down shares, taking the PCOMP to multi-year lows in November, before bargain-hunting helped trim the YTD-loss by half from -14.8% at the trough.

 

Gaming names were big losers for the year, with BLOOM (-44.5% YoY) and PLUS (-40.3% YoY) falling due to regulatory headwinds. Heavyweight consumer stocks JFC (-33.1% YoY) and MONDE (-32.6% YoY) also dragged, amid growing domestic growth concerns despite low inflation. Proxies SM (-22.2% YoY) and AC (-21.9% YoY) lagged the performance of their underlying banks and property subsidiaries. ALI (-14.3% YoY) was worse off vs SMPH (-9.5% YoY), as the outlook on the residential segment remained weak. BDO (-5.7% YoY) and BPI (-4.8% YoY) managed to outperform the benchmark despite their YoY decline.

 

Net foreign outflow accelerated to $202m in December, bringing FY 2025 net outflows to $873m which marks the eighth consecutive year that foreigners were net sellers of Philippine shares. ADTO was at a softer $127m for the month ($103m if we exclude rebalancing flows and special blocks).

 

The currency depreciated by 0.3% MoM to 58.822 in December, taking the FY 2025 depreciation of the peso to 1.7%. Meanwhile, the PH10Y yield climbed 13.6bps to 6.0722% in December (-10.4bps YoY).

Foreigners turned heavy sellers in December, taking advantage of liquidity from local bargain hunters. Despite renewed foreign selling pressure, the PSEi nonetheless managed to hold 6000 levels.  Average turnover meanwhile dipped in December (US$126.8m vs US$139m in Nov). Excluding the FTSE rebalancing and sizeable blocks in RCB, ATI and SECB – ADV for the month stood at a mere $103m.  Foreigners were more active, and accounted for 54% of trading activity.


TABLES/CHARTS

  • Foreigners remained sellers in Banks, with BDO the top sold name in December, alongside SECB. Concerns on NIMs have been fueled by recent  and expected rate cuts.
  • Property names, too, continued to be sold by foreigners on the back of soft 3Q numbers and concerns over high inventory levels. Notably, SMPH was net foreign sold for the 11th straight month, while ALI registered net foreign selling for the 4th straight month.
  • ICT, meanwhile, saw the biggest net inflows, in the wake of securing the concession to operate the Durban Container Terminal in South Africa. The news propelled the stock to print a new all-time high and extended its net foreign inflow streak to the eighth straight month. Outside the name, marginal inflows were seen in GTCAP RLC and AP.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Market breadth was positive in December with 17 advancers outnumbering 13 decliners. ICT (+4.5% MoM) was the biggest positive mover for the index, hitting fresh all-time-highs after officially securing the Durban port concession, followed by ALI (+10.3% MoM) which was lifted by local bargain-hunting. Meanwhile, shares of PLUS (-33.9% MoM) slumped with market chatter of insiders trimming positions due to mounting regulatory headwinds. The consumer space was also broadly negative, seeing PGOLD (-9.5% MoM), MONDE (-5.1% MoM), JFC (-4.8% MoM), and CNPF (-3.5% MoM) among the bigger losers for the month -- along with consumer proxy SM (-5.5% MoM) -- amid mounting domestic growth concerns.

 

For FY 2025, only six PCOMP members finished in the green. ICT (+46.9% YoY) was the best performer in the index despite a shaky start to the year due to Trump tariffs, as consistently strong earnings and a positive final outcome to the Durban port concession ultimately lifted sentiment. Yield plays and defensives LTG (+40.8% YoY), MER (+17.6% YoY), and AREIT (+14.6% YoY) also stood. A bright spot among a generally weak consumer sector, PGOLD (+23.2% YoY) was a big outperformer due to resilient earnings and decent dividends. JGS (+15.1% YoY) also bucked general market weakness, staging a strong rebound in December not long after it announced the write-off of its Petrochem business after more than two decades of losses.

 

For the year, the biggest laggard was PLUS (-40.3% YoY), which was at one point the top gainer in the index, up 139.4% at its peak for the year, before a sharp turn in regulations for online gaming completely reversed its fortunes. The big underperformance in consumer heavyweights JFC (-33.1% YoY) and MONDE (-32.6% YoY) reflected growing concerns over domestic consumption, as well as some dissatisfaction over their respective earnings performance.

 

Market proxies SM (-22.2% YoY) and AC (-21.9% YoY) were also big laggards. Their underlying property developers ALI (-14.3% YoY) and SMPH (-9.5% YoY) fared better, though still underperformed as the outlook on residential and office remained weak. The big banks BDO (-5.7% YoY), BPI (-4.8% YoY), and MBT (-4.9% YoY), on the other hand, finished the year lower but managed to outperform the index. Shifting monetary policy expectations influenced moves throughout the year, though share prices saw a modest lift by year-end after the BSP signaled its intent to further cut the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) by another 300bps down to 2.0%.

 

Of course, gold miners were the big winners off-index for the year -- APX (+260.6% YoY), PX (+254.8% YoY), and OGP (+129.7% YoY) -- as gold notched fresh all-time-highs.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP cuts rates further to 4.50%; PH economy expected to recover in 2H26. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.50%, the lowest since Sep 2022. This is the fifth consecutive 25bp cut this year (125bp total) since the decision to resume easing back in Apr 2025. The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 200bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024. For 2026, consensus expectations are one 25bp cut to 4.25% and inflation at 2.6%, while Regis expects two 25bp cuts to 4.00% and inflation to settle at 3.0%.

Inflation inches down to 1.5% in November. The headline inflation rate in Nov eased to 1.5% from Oct's 1.7%. This was a tad below with consensus expectations of 1.7% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.1-1.9% for Nov. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past nine months. Inflation in Nov decelerated slightly given slower YoY increases in meat and vegetable prices. Food overall was only up 0.1% YoY, moderating from the 0.5% pace in Oct. Notably, rice prices were up MoM (+0.4%)—the first MoM price increase since Jun 2024. Rice prices are down 15.4% YoY in Nov, marking the eleventh consecutive YoY price decline since Jan 2025. According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P42.7/kg and P43.5/kg as of Nov vs. P46.6kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan, respectively. Core inflation inched down to 2.4% in Nov from 2.5% in Oct, core non-food inflation was unchanged at 2.3% relative to the previous month. This brings the YT-Nov headline inflation rate to 1.6%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target. For 2025, the BSP expects inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.7% and 1.8%, respectively. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 125bp total worth of cuts to 4.50%. For 2026, consensus expects one 25bp cut, while Regis expects 50bp cuts total.

 

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.5bn in Nov. The Nov trade deficit narrowed to US$3.5bn, contracting 29% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports and the robust growth of exports. This brought the 11M25 trade deficit to US$45.2bn, 14% narrower YoY. Imports for the month fell by 2% YoY to US$10.4bn, a slower decline vs the 7% decrease in Oct. The decline was primarily driven by the decrease in consumer goods (-6%) and fuel imports (-19%). Exports climbed by 21% YoY to US$6.9bn, the eleventh consecutive month of YoY growth and accelerating from the 19% increase in Oct. Electronics exports surged by 51% YoY to $4.2bn. These made up the bulk of exports at 61% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were down 7% to US$2.7bn.

BOP posts US$225mn deficit in Nov; GIR settles at US$111.3bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month of Nov posted a deficit of US$225mn, a reversal from the US$706mn surplus in Oct but narrower than the $2.3bn deficit in Nov 2024. The narrower deficit YoY in Nov reflected the seasonal rise in remittances which offset higher imports and softer exports. This brings the YT-Nov deficit at US$4.8bn, reversing from the US$2.1bn surplus in the same period last year. The reversal to a YTD deficit stems from earlier front-loaded imports of capital goods and energy as well as the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects the BSP's expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Nov stood at US$111.3bn, up from the US$110.2bn in end-Oct. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to slightly decrease from $106.3bn in 2024 to $106.0bn in 2025.

 

 

Government posts P158bn budget deficit in Nov; P1.26trn deficit as of 11M25. The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P158bn in Nov, 26% narrower YoY as gov't spending continued to decline. This brings the 11M25 deficit to P1.26trn, 7% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was said to be in line with the P1.56trn full-year program and the gov't's "commit to prudent fiscal management and consolidation efforts." The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues. Revenue collections in Nov were flattish, only up 0.7% YoY, given the modest 3% growth tax revenues. Expenditures continued to decline by 10% YoY in Nov due to the decrease in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 13%. Similar to the previous four months, the continued gov't spending decline YoY in Nov was primarily caused by the slowdown in infra spending due to the flood control scandal. Interest payments rose 16% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

Remittances rise by 3% in October. Cash remittances amounted US$3.17bn in October, 3% higher YoY. This brings 10M25 remittances up 3.2% YoY to US$29.2bn. Weak remittance growth from North America (e.g. USA: -0.2%) has been partially offset by stronger contributions from the rest of the world.

Government infrastructure spending extends decline in October. Overall infrastructure spending (i.e. disbursements from national government, LGUs, and GOCCs) fell 11.5% YoY to P1.129tn in 10M25. Derived infra spend for October alone stood at P88.7bn, down 33% YoY and 11% MoM. Delays in settlement of progress billings for public work’s projects amid the flood control corruption scandal were cited for the decline. According to the DBM, subdued infrastructure spending should persist as a drag on total disbursements in 4Q25. The government has targeted to spend P1.5tn (+2% YoY, 5.3% of GDP) in 2025 and P1.56tn in 2026 (+3% YoY, 5.1% of GDP). However, the bicam-approved General Appropriations Bill cut the Department of Public Works’ budget by P350bn, implying 2026 infrastructure spending could fall to P1.2tn (-20% YoY) from the proposed P1.56tn.

2026 budget bicam report finalized; budget signing by President eyed by Jan 5. The bicameral conference committee has signed the report on the P6.79tn (+7% YoY) General Appropriations Bill (GAB) for 2026, in-line with expectations to ratify the report by Dec 29, Monday. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. was initially expected to sign the budget on the same day of ratification but disagreements on the Dept. of Public Works and Highways' allocated budget have prompted the President to need "at least one week review to carefully study and review the provisions". The new target date to sign the 2026 GAA is now Jan 5, implying the need for a reenacted budget in the first few days of Jan.

Department of Finance issues mining IRR. The Department of Finance (DOF) issued the Implementing Rules and Regulations of RA 12253 or the Enhanced Fiscal Regime for Large-Scale Metallic Mining Act. The DOF expects P6.3bn in additional revenues annually. RA 12253 streamlines the taxation/royalties on mining (see below). The filing and payment of royalties on locally produced or extracted metallic minerals or mineral products shall be made within 60 days after the end of the calendar quarter. The share of each LGU shall be released directly and immediately to the provincial, city, municipal, or barangay treasurer without the need for further action and without being subject to any lien or holdback that may be imposed by the national government. The DOF, Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall jointly issue the necessary rules and regulations for a streamlined disbursement scheme.

 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) fall 9% YoY in November; Toyota sales up 5%. CAMPI reported industry car sales fell 8.7% YoY (-6.7% MoM) to 37,352 units in November. CAMPI does not capture sales of BYD. This brings 11M25 sales to 420,776 units, down 1% YoY. Assuming BYD’s market share stood at 5%, we estimate vehicle sales are up ~4-5% YTD. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 20,351 units, up 5.3% YoY (flat MoM). This brings TMP’s 11M25 sales up 3.9% YoY to 205,552 units and lagging our full-year forecast of 231k units (+6%).

SEC sets monthly interest rate cap on loans up to P10,000. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), through a memorandum circular, has issued an order limiting the interest rate that financing and lending companies can charge on loans of up to P10,000 and loan tenor of up to four months. The circular will take effect on April 1, 2026. Banks, investment houses, savings and loans associations, insurance companies, cooperatives, and other credit institutions already regulated by law are not subject to the new circular. The prescribed ceilings are as follows: The nominal interest rate is capped at 6% per month or 0.2% per day. The effective interest rate is limited to 12% per month or 0.4% per day. Effective interest rate is the total nominal interest paid plus other fees and charges but excluding penalty and late payment fees. Penalties for late or non-payment is capped at 5% per month on the outstanding scheduled amount due. A total cost cap of 100% of the total amount borrowed (applying to all interest, other fees and charges, and penalties), regardless of the time the loan has been outstanding. Non-compliance with the prescribed ceilings will be subject to a fine of P50,000 on first offense, a larger monetary penalty plus suspension of financing and lending activities for 60 days for the second offense, and a revocation of Certificate of Authority and Certificate of Incorporation by the third offense. Any circumvention or attempt to circumvent the ceilings shall also constitute a violation.

DPWH seeks charges vs Romualdez, 86 others in flood control scandal. The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) has recommended the filing of criminal charges against 87 people over irregularities in flood control projects. The recommendations include charges of plunder, malversation, graft, and bribery. The list includes Leyte Rep. and former House Speaker Martin G. Romualdez and resigned Party-list Rep. Elizady S. Co.

Marcos' net trust rating turns negative to -3% in Nov. In the November Social Weather Stations Survey, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s net trust rating deteriorated back into negative territory—falling to -3% from +7% back in Aug.  Net figures are computed by % "much trust" rating minus % "little trust" rating. The movement to -3% is the same level as his net trust rating in Mar 2025 following the arrest of Former President Rodrigo Duterte.  The president’s net trust rating has been declining since Jun, following Marcos’ disclosure of the flood-control scandal during his end-Jul SONA.  Meanwhile, Vice President Sara Duterte's net trust rating has improved 7ppt to 31% in Nov from 25% in Aug. The 38% net trust rating of the VP is much lower than her 73% net trust rating at the start of her term, but reflects an improvement for her 23% rating when she was impeached by the Lower House in Feb 2025.
