REGIS REPORT

30 April 2025
 

The PCOMP closed higher for a third straight month in April, up by 2.8% or 174.27 pts MoM to 6,354.99 despite the volatile start to the month from Trump's 'Liberation Day' tariff pronouncements. The index hit a fresh 52-week low at the 5,800 level after foreigners dumped shares amid tariff woes in early April, before climbing 9.1% to close at the month's high point as trade tensions softened and bargain hunters emerged.

 

The index continued to pare its YTD losses, now just at 2.7% from 10.2% at its lowest point in end-January. This is better than most of the Asean region, only behind Singapore's 1.2% gain.

 

Investors weighed global uncertainty against mostly positive domestic developments, both on the macro and corporate fronts. For one, the BSP resumed its easing cycle with a 25-bp cut in the April Monetary Board meeting, after March inflation continued to fall to a lower-than-expected 1.8% print. Meanwhile, early 1Q25 earnings releases showed resilient loan growth from the Big 3 banks BDO (+4.6% MoM), BPI (+6.1% MoM), and MBT (+5.3% MoM), as well as sequentially improved residential pre-sales for SMPH (+1.5% MoM).

 

For the month, top-weighted SM (+10.4% MoM) was the biggest positive mover for the index accounting for over 40% of the PCOMP's advance, amid solid local macro indicators. Large cap property and banks were lifted too with ALI (+8.9% MoM), SMPH (+1.5% MoM), BDO (+4.6% MoM), and BPI (+6.1% MoM) collectively making up over half of the index's monthly gain. On the other hand, the slump of heavyweight ICT (-4.2% MoM) on global trade jitters dragged the index by a quarter.

 

Inflation expectations continue to fall with Trump's tariff war driving down prices of oil and key commodities. Moreover, the Department of Agriculture unveiled a new program to start selling P20/kg rice in the Visayas region and select provinces, fulfilling Pres. Marcos' campaign promise at the 2022 presidential race. Another potential driver for consumption comes from the National Economic and Development Authority’s (NEDA) enhanced food stamp program, dubbed the ‘Reducing Food Insecurity and Undernutrition with Electronic Vouchers’ or ‘REFUEL’ Project.

 

The rosier outlook on domestic consumption also helped lift consumer names, with URC (+18.2% MoM) and PGOLD (+13.7% MoM) two of the top index gainers for the month.

 

Elsewhere onshore, the local politics scene continued to heat up ahead of the May 12 mid-term elections. The latest Pulse Asia survey has revealed Senatorial candidates that are allied with former Pres. Duterte have moved up the polls, though the top 8 continue to be from the Marcos administration slate. Note the survey was taken after Duterte's arrest and extradition to The Hague by the ICC. Duterte's arrest also seemed to have influenced the latest approval ratings -- positively for his daughter VP Sara Duterte and very broadly negatively for Pres. Marcos.

 

The peso appreciated by 2.5% MoM to 55.86:$1 as Trump tariffs continued to add downward pressure on the US$.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

PCOMP breadth was largely positive with 20 out of 30 names ending higher.

 

Among sector indices, Holdings (+4.7% MoM) outperformed lifted by SM (+10.4% MoM). Financials (+3.9% MoM) outperformed with Big 3 banks BPI (+6.1% MoM), MBT (+5.3% MoM), and BDO (+4.6% MoM) all decently higher on upbeat 1Q25 results. Property (+3.2% MoM) also outpaced the PCOMP's gains mostly boosted by ALI (+8.9% MoM) that strengthened after the BSP's April rate cut and amid mounting expectations for further easing thanks to the falling inflation outlook. On the other hand, Services (-1.9% MoM) underperformed dragged by GLO (-13.5% MoM) and ICT (-4.2% MoM).

 

Consumer names led gainers in the index, URC (+18.2% MoM) and PGOLD (+13.7% MoM) being two of the three top advancers, lifted by the improving outlook on domestic consumption especially amid falling inflation. PGOLD specifically also rode on strong 4Q24 results coupled with an upbeat dividend declaration offering 7.0% yield. BLOOM (+16.8% MoM) rebounded after its slump the previous month as traders bought up the stock following news of an earlier-expected launch for its enhanced online gaming platform.

 

GLO (-13.5% MoM) was the largest decliner in the PCOMP dragged by heavy foreign outflow, followed by ACEN (-12.2% MoM) that saw selling pressure following its official disclosure of a P30-bn SRO priced at a maximum discount of 18% to the pre-announcement share price.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP resumes easing cycle and guides for more cuts this year

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas cut the policy rate by 25 bps to 5.50%. The move can be attributed to the improved inflation outlook and heightened external risks which enabled the shift to a more accommodative monetary policy. Notably, the inflation forecasts have been revised from the previous Feb policy meeting. The risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2025 is down to 2.3% from 3.5%, while the 2026 forecast is now 3.3% from 3.7%. While the 2027 risk-adjusted inflation forecast remains at 3.2%. The BSP notes that risks to the inflation outlook have eased and is broadly balanced from 2025 to 2027. Upside pressures stem from possible higher transport costs, meat prices, utility prices while downside risks are from easing rice prices and the impact from weaker global demand. The Monetary Board however cautioned a more challenging external environment, which may hamper global GDP growth and can negatively impact domestic economic activity. BSP Governor Remolona guided for more rate cuts this year which will be done 25 bps at a time. Although he added that it's unlikely that all four remaining Monetary Board meetings will result in a cut. He also noted that the easing cycle may likely conclude this year but remains cautious for the possible risk of a hard landing. 

 

March CPI slows to 1.8% YoY from 2.1% in Feb

Philippines March CPI rose 1.8% YoY, below the 2% consensus forecast. This was also near the lower-end of the BSP's 1.7-2.5% forecast range, and brings 1Q25 average CPI to 2.2%. March CPI fell 0.2% MoM vs the estimated +0.1%. Core inflation for March at +2.2% YoY. Overall inflation eased, primarily due to the slower annual increment in the index of food and nonalcoholic beverages in March. Note that rice prices fell 7.7% YoY in March.

 

DA to sell rice at P20/kg starting in Visayas

Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. stated that the Department of Agriculture (DA) will start to sell rice at P20/kg in Visayas as a pilot run before implementing the program nationwide. Details of the launch and points of sale have not been disclosed as of late. The DA estimates that it will spend around P3.5bn-P4.5bn for the pilot program. Rationale behind the launching of the program was easing global rice prices as well as its currently abundant rice buffer stock due to ongoing harvest season. The initial program length was slated until Dec 2025, but President Marcos requested an extension until Feb 2026. Laurel also added that Marcos directed the DA to draft a roadmap to sustain the program until 2028. As of April 23, prices of local well-milled rice ranges from P37-54/kg while imported is P42-46/kg.

NEDA approves enhanced P74.6bn e-voucher food stamp program

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), chaired by President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., has approved the Reducing Food Insecurity and Undernutrition with Electronic Vouchers (REFUEL) Project. The program will be implemented from July 2025 to July 2028 and costs an estimated P74.6bn, to be funded by the Asian Development Bank, Agence Française de Développement, and the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries Fund for International Development. The REFUEL program is an enhancement of the pilot Philippine Food Strategic Transfer and Alternative Measures Program (STAMP) of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), rolled out late last year. REFUEL targets to benefit around 750,000 food-insecure households, which is higher than STAMP's 300,000 household beneficiaries last year. These food-insecure households will be given an electronic benefit transfer (EBT) card with monthly food credits worth P3,000 that can be used to buy high-nutrition food items from DSWD's partner retailers.

 

PH targets beneficial tariff deal with the US

Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (SAPIEA) Secretary Frederick Go will lead a delegation to Washington to discuss the tariffs on Philippine goods. The PH gov't stated that it is confident on securing a "mutually advantageous" arrangement with the US ahead of scheduled trade talks with US counterparts in the first week of May. United Portusers Confederation of the Philippines President Nelson Mendoza elaborated on the current situation as a lot of orders for exports, although have not been canceled, were put on hold. He added that orders are being held due to the tariff announcements given the uncertainty of what will happen after the 90-day pause. 

 

NEDA confident on at least 6% GDP growth for 2025

Secretary Arsenio Balisacan of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) stated that the upper end of the 6-8% gov't GDP target range may be unlikely especially given heightened global uncertainties. But he also noted that the Marcos administration is still "confident" on hitting the lower end of the target band of 6%. Balisacan stated that the boost from the midterm elections will be positive for growth but may not be strong enough to offset the drag from significant uncertainties. The NEDA Secretary stated that he would like to see 6% growth in the first quarter, and he added that it is not too far-fetched considering easing inflation and support from domestic consumption. 1Q24 GDP was 5.9%. 1Q25 GDP data will be released on May 8. Consensus expects GDP to be 5.7% for 1Q25 and 5.9% for the full-year. We expect GDP to be 6.0% for the year.

 

Trade deficit narrows to $3.15bn in Feb

The February trade deficit narrowed to US$3.16bn, down 11% YoY, reflecting the decline in imports and growth recovery in exports. This is the lowest deficit in four years. Imports for the month inched down 2% YoY to US$9.41bn, a significant slowdown from the 11% increase in Jan. Primarily driven by the decline in fuel imports, which was down 23% YoY to US$1.3bn, negating the increases in other major goods categories. Exports grew 4% YoY to US$6.25bn, the second month of growth after four consecutive months of contraction. Electronics exports grew by 3% YoY to $3.52bn, a turnaround from the 3% YoY decline in Jan. These made up the bulk of exports at 56% of the total. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports were up 6% YoY to US$2.73bn.

 

March budget deficit at P376bn; 1Q budget deficit widens 76% YoY to P479bn

The government recorded a budget deficit of P374.6bn which is wider both 91.8% YoY and 119.2% MoM. This brings the 1Q25 deficit to P478.8bn, 75.6% wider YoY. Revenue collections in Mar inched down 3.0% YoY to P279.3bn, as non-tax revenues dipped 71.0% YoY. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections jumped 20.9% to P175.7bn mainly due to ongoing revenue enhancement measures such as the crackdown on fake receipts, illicit trade, as well as improvements in digitalization and tax payment facilities.

 

BOP swings to US$2.0bn deficit in March 2025; Final GIR settles at US$106.7bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$2.0bn. This is a reversal from both Feb 2025's US$3.1bn surplus and the US$1.2bn surplus in Mar 2024. The deficit reflected the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) net foreign currency deposits drawdowns for external debt obligations as well as its net foreign exchange operations. External debt obligations that matured in March includes a US$2.0bn 25-year bond. This brings the 1Q25 deficit at $3.0bn, reversing from the $238mn surplus for the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Mar 2025 stood at US$106.7bn, down from the US$107.4bn in end-Feb 2025. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is -0.8% of GDP. GIR is expected to decrease slightly from $106bn in 2024 to $105bn in 2025.

Government debt inches up to P16.63tn in end-Feb

The government's total debt stock went up by 1.96% MoM (+P319.3bn) to P16.63tn as of end-Feb. This was due to the net issuance of new domestic and external debt, which was slightly offset by the appreciation of the PHP against the USD (from P58.375 end-Jan to P57.990 end-Feb). Domestic debt rose by 1.26% (+P139.6bn) MoM to P11.22tn due to the impact of net borrowings (P140.7bn). External debt increased by 3.44% (+P179.64bn) MoM to P5.41tn, given net foreign debt additions (P193.7bn). This includes the previous global bond offering of $2.25bn USD for the 10- and 25- USD bonds as well as the $1.08bn USD for the 25-year EUR bonds.The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn, which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn. This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%.

 

PH Dollar reserves down 1.1% at end-March

The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-Mar saw a 1.1% MoM decrease to $106.2bn from $107.4bn at end-Feb. This primarily reflected the national gov'ts drawdowns on foreign currency deposits with the BSP to meet external debt obligations as well as the BSP's net foreign exchange operations. The level of dollar reserves by end-Mar was enough to cover about 3.7x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.3x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. Reserve position in the IMF decreased 2.6% MoM to $653mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $12.8bn, which jumped 5.9% MoM. Foreign investments decreased by 1.7% MoM to $88.6bn. Foreign currency deposits sharply dropped 38.2% MoM to $498mn. Note that the BSP expects the country's GIR to be at $110bn by end-2025.

 

OFW Remittances up 2.7% YoY in February

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.7% YoY to US$2.72bn, decelerating from the 2.9% growth seen in Jan. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE were the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total and significant YoY growth. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 1.2% YoY from 0.9% in Jan. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 6.9%, slower relative to the previous three months given 4Q's seasonal strength.

 

BPO sector to hit US$40bn in revenue this year

The IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that it would hit its US$40bn revenue target this year. This represents c. 5% growth from US$38bn in 2024. It is also projecting the sector to employ 2mn Filipinos in 12 to 18 months. The sector had 1.82mn employees at the end of 2024.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth decelerates to 12.2% YoY in February; Domestic liquidity (M3) slows to 6.3%

Loans of commercial banks expanded by 12.2% YoY in February, moderating from 12.8% pace in January. Loans to productive activities grew 11.2%, slower than the 11.8% rate in January due to moderating growth for manufacturing (+0.9% from +4.6%), utilities (+21.5% from +23.6%), and retail trade sectors (+13.7% from +13.9%). Consumer loans grew by 24.1%, broadly steady from 24.4% in January. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 6.3% in February, the slowest rate since Oct 2024.

 

Banks' asset quality remains steady in February

Using preliminary figures from the BSP, the NPL ratio of Philippine banks remain unchanged at 3.38% in Feb relative to Jan. This was slightly lower than the 3.44% NPL ratio in Feb 2024. In nominal terms, NPLs were flat MoM at P513.3bn but was higher by 10% YoY (vs. Feb 2023: P466.1bn). Total gross loans likewise were unchanged at P15.2tn; On a YoY basis, gross loans decelerated slightly to 12.1% YoY in Feb from 13.4% YoY in Jan. NPL coverage ratio was steady at 95.36% in Feb from 95.25% in Jan (vs. 100.06% in Feb 2024). 

 

AMLC assesses risks related to online gambling

BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr said that the Anti money laundering council (AMLC) is assessing potential risks and vulnerabilities related to online gambling that criminals could exploit to facilitate illegal flow of funds. DigiPlus Interactive (PLUS PM) is the market leader in online gambling, with about 50% market share. The BSP previously published draft guidelines that would prohibit access of online gambling via digital marketplaces (which would include GCash).

Vehicle sales rise 8% YoY, 3% MoM in March; Toyota sales up 14% YoY, 2% MoM

CAMPI/TMA reported industry car sales of 40,306 units, up 7.6% YoY and 2.9% MoM. Brings 1Q25 vehicle sales up 6.8% YoY to 117,074 units. Note that CAMPI/TMA data does not include BYD. CAMPI is targeting sales of 500k units for 2025 (+7% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 18,907 units in March, up by 13.9% YoY and 2.1% MoM. Brings 1Q25 retail sales up 11.8% YoY to 55,513 units. TMP is targeting to sell 235k units (+8% YoY) this year; To reach this, sales for the rest of the year should average 19.9k units per month. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses. GTCAP is trading at a 59% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,279/sh and 4.1x FY25E PER.

 

Pulse Asia survey for senatorial preferences sees 8 from the Marcos-admin slate

Based on the latest Pulse Asia survey, 16 out of 64 senatorial candidates have a statistical chance of securing one of the 12 seats in the upcoming May 12, 2025 midterm elections. Re-electionist Bong Go leads the race with a voter preference of 61.9%. He is followed by ACT-CIS Rep. Erwin Tulfo (51.1%) and Sen. Ronald “Bato” dela Rosa (48.7%), ranking 2nd to 3rd. Former Senate President Vicente Sotto III is in 4th with 44.2%. Sen. Pia Cayetano (37.5%), Sen. Bong Revilla (36.9%), former Sen. Panfilo Lacson (36.0%), Willie Revillame (35.7%), Ben Tulfo (35.4%), and Makati Mayor Abby Binay (35.3%) are in the 5th to 12th range. Sen. Lito Lapid (33.3%) follows at 6th to 13th, while former Sen. Manny Pacquiao (32.0%) places 8th to 16th. Actor Phillip Salvador (30.0%), Rep. Camille Villar (29.0%), former Sen. Bam Aquino (28.6%), and Rep. Rodante Marcoleta (28.3%) round out the group with rankings between 11th and 18th. Six of the probable 16 candidates are not part of the Marcos administration’s slate—Go, Dela Rosa, Salvador, Marcoleta are known Duterte allies, while Ben Tulfo, and Revillame are independent. Notably, Salvador and Marcoleta now have a probable chance of winning after trailing in the previous surveys.
