REGIS REPORT

31 October 2025
 

Philippine equities extended their slide in October, logging a fourth straight monthly loss to further below 6,000 as foreign selling persisted and sentiment remained fragile amid political noise and weakening growth prospects.

 

The PCOMP ended the month down 0.4% or 23.78 pts MoM to 5,929.68, its lowest close since April, and widened its YTD losses to 9.2%, which is the worst performance in Asean. Trading activity stayed muted, reflecting cautious investor mood despite supportive inflation data and monetary easing.

 

Macro developments set the tone as the BSP surprised markets with a 25-bp rate cut to 4.75% at its early October meeting, citing slower domestic growth and benign inflation. With investors already focused on the downside risks to growth, optimism faded further late in the month when the peso plunged to a record low of 59:$1, raising concerns on inflation and complicating expectations for further policy easing. Though the peso managed to recover from its lows, it still closed down 1.2% MoM to 58.885:$1.

 

The market stayed broadly risk-off through most of the month as the government’s ongoing probe into the flood control corruption scandal continued to dampen investor confidence, with concerns mounting that stalled infrastructure disbursements and weaker investments could weigh on near-term growth.

 

Sector performance was mixed, with Property (-4.9% MoM) and Financials (-4.5% MoM) dragging the most. ALI (-18.3% MoM) was the biggest decliner on the index as foreign investors continue to cut exposure while management guided for slower residential launches and trimmed its mid-term earnings growth target. Banking names were volatile, with BPI (-8.3% MoM) and BDO (+0.5% MoM) facing persistent selling pressure despite delivering in-line 3Q results.

 

Meanwhile, ICT (+12.4% MoM) led gains on strong foreign inflows, while defensives such as MER (+10.4% MoM) also outperformed, with investors rotating into yield and stable earners amid heightened volatility.

 

Going into early November, investors are eyeing the 3Q GDP report and Maynilad’s (ticker: MYNLD) P34.3-bn ($582-m) IPO, as well as the bulk of 3Q earnings releases. The market remains caught between easing inflation and accommodative monetary policy against a deteriorating growth outlook, with the peso’s weakness and sustained foreign outflows likely to keep sentiment cautious near term.

Key data
 
 

Average daily turnover in October was virtually same as last month at $138m. However, the figure was primarily bloated this time by substantial blocks on several low-trafficked names: more than $300m each in SPNEC and SMC preferred shares, and about $200m on HOUSE. The latter 2 were mostly related to tender offerings, while SPNEC’s blocks were mostly related to Leandro Leviste’s sale of shares to Meralco’s MGreen Renewable.

As such, liquidity on main index names felt lighter compared to the past month, evidently affected by the downcast sentiment among investors amid the ongoing corruption scandal on the ground and concurrent concerns over its impact on the country’s growth and corporate earnings outlook over the short term.

In terms of net flow, foreign investors turned heavy net sellers this month by $100m which is more than twice the $47m inflow in September. This brings the YTD net outflow to a hefty $750, already much higher than FY2024’s $410m.

 

 

  • Top sold stock during the month was ALI, which was also the worst performing name in the PCOMP, weighed by concerns over property demand amid ongoing corruption issue.
  • Banks (BDO, BPI, MBT) and Conglos (AC, GTCAP, SM) were also among the top sold as well amid a generally downbeat mood on the market.
  • On the opposite end, top bought stock and also the top performing name in the benchmark was ICT as investors digested the positive outcome of the Durban court case from earlier in the month.
  • Outside of ICT however, foreign buying in the market was quite minimal – next top inflow names were PX and PLUS but both by under $10m inflow each. Outside of the evident flocking into index heavyweight ICT and some light inflows into a few mining names like PX and NIKL, no clear trend in terms of foreign buying this month.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

19 Decliners outnumbered 11 advancers in the PCOMP in October.

 

Most sectors closed lower as risk appetite waned amid persistent foreign selling and a record-low peso. Defensive counters were among the few bright spots, while property, gaming, and consumer faced the brunt of selling pressure.

 

Property (-4.9% MoM) remained the month’s worst performer, down sharply led by ALI (-18.3% MoM) as foreign investors cut exposure following lowered guidance on launches and profit growth. The continued fallout from the public works corruption scandal and a consequently cooling luxury residential market weighed further on sentiment.

 

Defensives MER (+10.4% MoM) and ICT (+12.4% MoM) offered some respite, both hitting their fresh all-time-highs some time in the month. MER drew investor interest as a yield play, while ICT remained the top positive mover for the index on solid global port operations and a positive court ruling in South Africa for its planned Durban port takeover.

 

Banks were mixed, reflecting some caution over the impact of BSP rate cuts on net interest margins and slower domestic growth. BPI (-8.3% MoM) declined despite in-line 3Q earnings, while BDO (+0.5% MoM) managed to recover from a 52W low. Among conglomerates, most issues were dragged by exposure to cyclicals like property and banks, with AC (-3.1% MoM) and SM (-0.8% MoM) extending YTD losses, now down 21.9% MoM and 18.9% MoM, respectively.

 

Consumer was also a mixed space. Profit-taking hit PGOLD (-9.3% MoM) and CNPF (-7.0% MoM), while manufacturers URC (+3.0% MoM) and MONDE (+1.0% MoM) managed to buck the trend despite soft 3Q indications.

 

Gaming stocks were notable laggards, with BLOOM (-22.7% MoM) and PLUS (-17.0% MoM) among the bigger losers in our watchlist for the month amid continued regulatory headwinds. Meanwhile, the mining sector was up 18.1% at its peak in mid-October, before the sharp pullback in gold erased gains for the month.

 

Overall, October’s trading reflected a clear rotation toward defensives and exporters amid domestic macro uncertainty and the weakening currency. Cyclical stocks bore the weight of the slowing growth outlook, political noise, and fragile sentiment heading into year-end.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP cuts rate by 25bp to 4.75%; expects GDP growth to slow

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) cut the key policy rate by 25bp to 4.75%, the lowest since Sep 2022. This is the fourth consecutive 25bp cut this year (100bp total) since previously cutting rates in Apr, Jun, and Aug. The BSP has lowered rates by a total of 175bp since the start of the easing cycle in Aug 2024. The BSP stated that the outlook on GDP growth has softened in the near term, highlighting dampened business sentiment from "governance concerns" on public infra spending and moderating demand due to external uncertainties. The BSP kept its GDP growth forecast for 2025 and 2026 at 5.5% and 6.0%, respectively. These are at the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% and 6-7% growth targets for the same periods. 3Q25 GDP data will be released on Nov 7. BSP Governor Eli Remolona noted that the "sweet spot" has moved closer to 4% now vs the initial assessment of 5% especially with the favorable inflation outlook and moderating domestic demand. Another cut this year was said to be a possibility as "the Monetary Board sees scope for a more accommodative monetary policy stance." The inflation outlook was noted to be benign and well within the target range with expectations well-anchored. BSP still expects inflation to settle at 1.7% for 2025 but revised its inflation forecasts for 2026 and 2027 to 3.1% and 2.8% from 3.3% and 3.4%, respectively. Primary risks were said to be potential electricity rate adjustments and increased rice tariffs. Remolona also guided that peso defense will be done if depreciation is sharp and large enough to be inflationary. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus expects 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75% and inflation to average 1.8%.

 

Monetary Board member expects one more 25bp cut in December

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Monetary Board member Benjamin Diokno stated in an interview that he expects another 25 bps cut [to 4.50%] by the next meeting in Dec with more rate cuts possible "maybe sometime next year." Diokno highlighted that the economy may "slow down a bit" given the corruption controversy. The gov't's GDP growth target this year is 5.5-6.5%. 1H25 GDP growth was at 5.4% with 3Q GDP to be released on Nov 7. Regis expects GDP growth to be 5.5% this year. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.5%.

 

BSP releases statement on recent peso depreciation

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) stated that they will "allow the exchange rate to be determined by market forces" as the USD-PHP rate closed at a record low, P59.13 by end of day, breaching the P59 level for the first time. The central bank also emphasized that intervention will be done "to dampen inflationary swings" in the exchange rate over time and not to address day-to-day volatility. Recent peso depreciation was said to reflect market concerns over dampened economic growth prospects due to the infra spending scandal as well as expectations of possible further easing by the BSP. Recall that the BSP against expectations cut earlier than expected in Oct and guided for another cut 25bp in Dec, with further cuts possible in 2026.

Inflation rate inches up to 1.7% in September

Headline inflation picked up to 1.7% in Sept from 1.5% in the previous month of Aug. This is lower than the consensus forecast of 1.9% but is within the BSP's forecast range of 1.5-2.3% for Sept. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past seven months. Primary drivers for the faster pace in inflation in Sept was food up 1.0% YoY, a tad higher from 0.9% YoY in Aug as well as transport which was up 1.0% YoY reversing from a 0.3% decline the previous month. Food was driven by a sharp 19.4% YoY increase in vegetable prices given adverse weather while transport saw higher diesel prices. Notably, rice prices are on their fifteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture (as of Oct 4), prices for local and imported well-milled rice range from P38-52/kg and P38-46/kg, respectively vs. P42-52/kg and P40-54/kg back in Jan 2. Core inflation inched down to 2.6% in Sept from 2.7% in Aug, while core non-food inflation climbed to 2.4% from 2.1% the previous month. This brings the YT-Sep headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. For 2025, the BSP expects 2025 inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting (5th out of 6 this year) will be on Oct 9. For 2025, consensus expects 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%.

 

Finance Secretary says corruption scandal may slow GDP growth until 1Q26; hopeful for another rate cut

Dept. of Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that the Philippine economy may experience a slowdown in growth until early 2026 given the flood control corruption scandal. Recall that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. flagged irregularities in flood control projects with investigations initiated by the Senate, the House, and the recently formed Independent Commission for Infrastructure. Recto guided that there will be a slowdown on public spending but highlighted that there will also be upside from reforms moving forward. He said that if the budget was utilized properly, GDP growth may have grown 6.0-6.2% as well as tax and customs collections would have been higher. Recto is optimistic that the economy will hit the lower end of the gov't's 5.5-6.5% GDP growth target this year. 1H25 GDP growth was at 5.4%. The Finance Secretary said that there will "hopefully, [be] another rate cut" but it will be hinged on inflation staying within target (2-4%). Inflation averaged 1.7% for 9M25. 

 

Government posts P248bn budget deficit in Sep; P1.17trn deficit as of 9M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget deficit of P248bn in Sep, 9% narrower YoY on slower government spending. This brings the 9M25 deficit to P1.17trn, 15% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was stated to be "expected to remain within the P1.56trn full-year program." The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues. Revenue collections in Sep declined by 6% YoY, dragged by lower non-tax revenues. BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) collections rose 5% mainly due to higher revenues from corporate and personal income tax. Customs collections were higher by 5%, reversing from the 1% decline in Aug. Non-tax revenues dipped 66% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024. Expenditures fell 7% YoY in Sep due to the decrease in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 10%. Similar to Jul and Aug, the spending slowdown in Sep was due to the "lower spending performance of the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH)." Interest payments rose 11% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The government eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

 

BOP posts US$82mn surplus in Sep; GIR settles at US$109.1bn

The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$82mn, narrower than both the US$3.5bn surplus in Sep 2024 and Aug 2025's US$359mn surplus. The smaller surplus in Sep reflected lower forex inflows and a still-wide trade gap. This brings the YT-Sep deficit at US$5.3bn, reversing from the US$5.1bn surplus in the same period last year. The reversal to a deficit stems from the timing of government debt issuances and the continued trade in goods gap. The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.9bn deficit (-1.4% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-Sep stood at US$109.1bn, up from the $107.1bn in end-Aug. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106.3bn in 2024 to $105.0bn in 2025.

 

Government infrastructure spending extends decline in August

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) fell 16% YoY, 8% MoM to P105bn in August, citing suspension of payments for public works amid ongoing validation and audit related to corruption concerns. This brings YT-August infra spend to P940bn, down 5% YoY and lagging the government’s infra spend target of P1.51tn (-2% YoY).

 

House approves P6.79trn budget on final reading

The House of Representatives passed the proposed P6.79trn national budget for 2026 on final reading, with 287 voted for, twelve voted against, and two opted to abstain. The 2026 national budget is 7.4% higher YoY. The House deliberated on the bill for 62 days with numerous revisions made given the recent corruption scandal involving flood control projects. P255bn was initially allocated for flood control infrastructure for 2026. P201bn was redirected to the agricultural, health, and other social sectors. After the budget bill has finished deliberations through the House plenary level, it will move to the Senate, the bicam, and finally to President Marcos Jr. for approval.

DOF priority tax reforms expected to generate P170bn in the next five years

The Department of Finance (DOF) expects to raise P170.9bn in the next five years from its priority tax reforms, according to the DBCC’s 2025 Mid-Year Report. About P3.8bn is expected to be collected this year. The reforms include: VAT on Digital Services (RA 12023) – estimated to generate P126.8bn from 2025–2030; requires both local and foreign platforms to remit VAT on local transactions. CREATE MORE Act (RA 12066) – projected to reduce revenues by P13.1bn from 2025–2030 due to lower corporate income tax (25% from 30%) and expanded deductions on power and trade fair expenses. Capital Market Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA, RA 2214) – expected to raise P35bn from 2025–2030 by rationalizing capital market taxes and adding an excise tax on pickup trucks. Mining Fiscal Regime (RA 12253) – will take effect January 2026, projected to generate P32.1bn in incremental revenues. The revenue-to-GDP ratio is projected to rise from 15.9% in 2025 to 16.8% by 2030.

 

BSP eyes tighter rules on digital/cash transfers to curb fund misuse, protect banks

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is deliberating on new thresholds not just for large cash withdrawals but also for digital and other fund transfers in a continued effort to prevent the misuse of public funds and strengthen detection of suspicious transactions. For now, the proposed thresholds will likely apply to government transactions, especially transfers from the state to private individuals or contractors. BSP Governor Eli Remolona noted that banks were uneasy about releasing funds from gov't agencies that are suspicious but could not refuse since the money came from the Treasury. The central bank plans to clearly define when banks can refuse transactions suspected of corruption. Uniform rules are sought to be applied across institutions to prevent depositors from simply moving funds elsewhere. Recall that the BSP required financial institutions to cap large-value cash transactions at P500,000 per banking day unless the amount is processed through traceable channels.

 

August NPLs tick up 8bps MoM to 3.66%

Asset quality deteriorated MoM in August, with Philippine banks’ NPL ratio climbing 8bps to 3.66% from 3.58% in July. That said, the figure is 15bps lower YoY vs the 3.81% in August 2024. (Note that we compute these ratios excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRPs). The reported BSP data was up 11bps MoM to 3.59% because the IBL+RRP total was down MoM.) In PHP terms, NPLs stood at P550bn, up 2.7% MoM and 7.3% YoY.

 

Colliers: Office and residential net take-up improve in 3Q25

Colliers reported that Metro Manila office vacancy remained flat QoQ at 20% in 3Q25. Makati CBD vacancy was flat QoQ at 8.2%. BGC vacancy fell c. 2ppt QoQ to 12.4%. Net office take-up rose 75% QoQ to 105,000sqm. This was largely due to higher take-up from traditional tenants as well as fewer POGO closures. Office rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila were generally flat QoQ. Residential vacancy in Metro Manila was also relatively flat QoQ at 25%. Net take-up of Metro Manila condominium units doubled QoQ and YoY to 5,800 units, the highest level in eight quarters. The improvement in net take-up was driven by stronger take-up of mid-income units as well as lower cancellations. Colliers said that aggressive promotions (such rent-to-own schemes of up to 10 years) and cash discounts of up to 60% on select ready-for-occupancy units continued to entice buyers. On the other hand, Colliers Head of Research Joey Bondoc said that “…the flood control mess has had some impact on the more expensive condo segments – luxury and ultra luxury…”

 

Draft Konektadong Pinoy IRR seeks stronger cybersecurity and competitive safeguards

The Dept. of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) eyes to tighten the cybersecurity requirements of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) ahead of the potential entry of new players. The draft implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of the KPA exhibit a tighter due diligence process to reinforce cybersecurity protocols and to encourage fair competition. Some of the draft rules and regulations are as follows: The DICT will formulate guidelines which will detail the risk profile per data transmission industry participant (DTIP) segment with considerations for market share, network coverage, among others. DTIPs must follow DICT cybersecurity rules under the National Cybersecurity Plan, including a response team, secure SDLC, and zero-trust framework. DTIPs need to adopt global standards for internet security, submit risk plans, vulnerability tests, and incident reports. DTIPs are required to obtain a third-party issued cybersecurity certification and certificate of DICT compliance within two years from registration. Non-compliance will lead to suspension, license revocation, and permanent ban from PH operations.

 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) drop 3.8% YoY in September; Toyota sales flat

CAMPI reported car sales fell 3.8% YoY (+5.1% MoM) to 38,029 units in September. Note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales. This brings YT-September vehicle sales to 343,410 units (-0.3% YoY). Assuming BYD’s market share was at ~5%, we estimate industry car sales may have risen by ~5% YTD. EV sales (ex-BYD) reached 20,662 units in 9M25, accounting for 6% of total sales. This implies that for the month of September alone, sales of EVs stood at 2,223 units, down 1% MoM and accounting for 5.8% of overall sales. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 164,797 units in 9M25, up 3.6% YoY and lagging TMP’s full-year sales target. Derived TMP sales for September of 18,440 units indicate a flat YoY trend, breaking the downward trend seen in the prior two months.

 

Big-ticket gold/copper mining projects to commence 2026-2028

Philex Mining said that its Silangan project is on course to produce copper cathode and gold doré by 1Q26, with or without new investors. According to Philex Mining president and CEO Eulalio Austin Jr., it is targeting to produce 2k MT per day initially, but volume will be gradually ramped up over time to the mine’s full capacity of 12k MT/day by the 12th year. He said that Philex was in discussions with several local and foreign firms to invest in the project, although no agreement has been reached. Meanwhile, Sagitarius Mines is seeking a strategic partner to advance the Tampakan copper-gold project in 2028. The production target was pushed back from the original target of late-2026. The Chamber of Mines of the Philippines (COMP) expects the local mining industry to become a major driver of the national economy once again. COMP Chairman Michael Toledo cited the lifting of the moratorium on new projects, the removal of the open-pit ban, and the ongoing efforts to simplify the permitting process. He is confident that mining can once again reach the heights it once reached during the 1980s, when the industry accounted for over 20% of the country’s export earnings (from only <10% last year).

 

Marcos' approval rating dips 9ppt to 33% in Sep

In the latest Pulse Asia Research Survey, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s trust rating deteriorated 9ppt to 33% from 42% in Jun. The drop in approval rating follows Marcos' order to investigate alleged corruption in flood control projects during his 2025 State of the Nation Address (SONA) on July 28 as well as the nationwide protests against flood control corruption that took place on Sep 21. Notably, this is still much lower than the 82% approval rating at the start of his term and a reversal from the 10ppt gain to 42% in Jun from 32% in May. Marcos' approval rating in Luzon stands at 50% in Jun unchanged from May, Visayas at 23% falling sharply from 41%, while his approval rating in Mindanao nosedived to 4% from 25%. Vice President Sara Duterte's approval rating saw a 4ppt decrease to 55% in Sep from 59% in Jun. VP Duterte's approval rating in Luzon was at 36% from 41%, Visayas at 56% from 58%, Mindanao at 97% from 96%.

 

PSE to change index methodology in 2026

PSE President and CEO Ramon Monzon said that the Exchange plans to revise its index policy. The effectivity will likely be in June 2026 [for the August rebalancing]. The PSE received comments on some of its proposed revisions. Among the proposed changes is a lower public float requirement (of 15%) for companies with market capitalizations above P120bn. The minimum public float requirement for PSE Index constituents is currently 20%. Mr. Monzon wants the PSE’s index policy to be “…benchmarked against the best: MSCI, FTSE, S&P Global…”
