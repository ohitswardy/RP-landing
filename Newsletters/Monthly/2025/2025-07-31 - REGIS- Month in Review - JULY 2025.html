REGIS REPORT

31 July 2025
 

The PCOMP dropped by -1.8% to 6265 in July, posting the worst monthly performance since January. Evolving Trump tariff negotiations influenced the market in different directions. On the ground, however, the PSEi was met with some notable headwinds.

Sizable private placements on SM and SMPH sapped the liquidity in the broader market just when the benchmark appeared to be on the verge of testing key resistance levels past 6500 and the 200-day moving average. Meanwhile, the start of the 2Q earnings season has failed to inspire investors thus far. A notable earnings miss from heavy weight BDO set a somber tone. Elevated opex and a jump in provisions (+36% YoY) weighed on profits. BPI on the other hand managed to meet street net profit expectations though a jump in credit costs also dampened sentiment.

The PHP suffered a notable loss of 3.4%, back to levels not seen since January. The PHP closed 58.327/US$,while the DXY showed strength and gaining 3.2% in July. Bulk of the PHP’s decline came at the tail end of the month when expectations of Fed cuts this year were broadly trimmed after the latest US PCE reading remained above target and 2Q GDP growth stayed robust at 3% QoQ. Investors are now assessing the potential impact of a weak PHP on the BSP’s next move.

A cut by the central bank at its next Monetary Board (MB) meeting on Aug 28, had previously looked virtually certain given the soft, sub-2% inflation reading for the past 4 months. As expected, June inflation came in at 1.4%, led by the continuing softness in food prices, specifically rice prices. 

Market activity was heavily concentrated on non-PCOMP member PLUS, which for the past weeks has consistently been the most actively traded name on the board (20% to 30% of daily volumes). The stock was highly volatile, bleeding -51.5% in July after previously breaking out to new highs in June, as several lawmakers filled bills seeking to either impose tighter restrictions or completely ban online gaming in the country amid the growth public scrutiny on the sector. Additionally, investors are also anticipating the upcoming PCOMP rebalancing in August where PLUS remains a top candidate for a potential inclusion.

Key data
 
 

Foreigners continued to be net sellers in the market for the 4th straight month and registered a net outflow of US$29m. Cautious sentiment persisted, even as the market waited for more trade deals to materialize and 2Q earnings to pile in.  At US$140m average daily (US$129m ex SM and SMPH placement), turnover continued to be patchy albeit higher than last month's US$122m. Foreign participation was lower in July at 47% vs 50% in June. 

  • Banks saw the biggest outflow this month following the release of BPI and BDO’s 1H numbers. BPI was the top foreign sold name among the big 3 and fell -9.1% after a  +142% spike in provisions and a decline in NPL cover to 97% in 2Q, suggesting a jump in write-offs. BDO dropped 6.6% after reporting weaker-than-expected 1H numbers on the back of higher opex.
  • A large foreign holder meanwhile sold SM and SMPH via a private placement worth $140m at 830/sh and US$77m at 23.18/sh respectively.
  • Conglos generally saw foreign demand in July, with GTCAP the top foreign bought stock in the sector for US$23.6m. AC likewise saw net outflows, reaching US$12.4m. Both stocks have been trading at deep discounts to their underlying valuations. 
  • Gaming names also saw decent inflows despite wild price swings after more senators called for outright bans on online gambling. PLUS finished the month 51.5% lower on heavy retail activity, while BLOOM fell -20.6%.
  • Consumer names were two-way despite cooling inflation. Foreigners continued to buy into URC. The stock has seen 5 straight months of net foreign inflows, after its deletion from the MSCi in February. JFC, on the other hand, was net sold for the 5th straight month as uncertainty surrounding its international businesses continued to weigh on sentiment.
  • ICT remained the top foreign bought name in July for US$33.4m, hitting a new all time high.

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The top index name in July was PGOLD +11.1%, adding to its strong surge in June and extending YTD gain to almost +30%, now the best performing in PCOMP. Shares appear to be supported by anticipation for 2Q results, which in the past tends to be boosted by the election period, after an already strong showing in 1Q. However, gains in PGOLD stood out as the rest of the consumer names looked broadly mixed with MONDE +1.1%, JFC +0.5%, URC -1.2% and CNPF -8.1%. Another big winner was outside of the PCOMP, SEVN +16.5%.

Likewise a notable outperformer in the benchmark was ICT +9.0% as investors assessed several trade agreements between the US and other countries, including the Philippines, which potentially lessens the uncertainty over the port operator’s outlook moving forward. There seems to be some optimism on results as well as its Mexico port managed to show strong growth through April-May (prior to the customs strike which affected June).

On the opposite end, banks were mostly weak with BPI -9.1% and BDO -6.6% extending their declines in June after reporting results during the month. Both lenders showed strong growth in loans but this was accompanied by higher provisions/credit costs. Overall, BPI’s numbers looked broadly inline with consensus, but BDO’s came in short of expectations due to a jump in its operating expenses. Meanwhile MBT managed to gain +2.3% during the month. 

Property names were generally weak as well with ALI -7.4% and SMPH -1.1% partially reversing gains from June; although outside the benchmark RLC +6.6% gained for the third month in a row.  As such, it’s the REIT counterparts that fared relatively better this month with RCR +5.8% and AREIT +2.4%.

 
MACRO NEWS

Inflation rate inches up to 1.4% in June. Headline inflation inched up to 1.4% in June from 1.3% in May, in line with the consensus forecast of 1.5% and within the BSP’s target range of 1.1% to 1.9%. Inflation has declined notably from 2.9% in January and has remained below 1.5% for three straight months. The continued deceleration was mainly driven by year-on-year price declines in rice (-14.3%) and transport (-1.6%), which offset rising costs of meat (+9.1%) and housing/utilities (+3.2%). Rice prices, notably, have now dropped for twelve consecutive months. As of July 2, the Department of Agriculture reported that local well-milled rice costs between ₱37–48/kg, while imported well-milled rice is priced at ₱38–48/kg—down from ₱42–52/kg and ₱40–54/kg, respectively, in early January.

Core inflation was steady at 2.2% in June, with non-food core inflation unchanged at 2.0%. This brings the year-to-date (YT-June) headline inflation rate to 1.8%, which is below the BSP’s 2–4% target. Consensus forecasts peg 2025 inflation at 2.1%, while Regis expects full-year inflation to average 1.8%. The next Monetary Board meeting, the fourth of six scheduled for the year, will take place on August 28. Both consensus and Regis forecast a total of 75 basis points in policy rate cuts this year, bringing the policy rate to 5.00%.

BSP on track for two more rate cuts this year. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona Jr. stated that "we're still on that same easing cycle" (to cut rates two more times this year) and highlighted that the central bank is on track. This implies 100bp total rate cuts for 2025, bringing the key policy rate to 4.75% by year-end from the current 5.25%. Remolona stated that it would be a good time for another cut if the data shows worsening growth and still benign inflation. He also mentioned that the US Federal Reserve's outlook will carry some weight but not as much as before given the peso's relative strength despite not closely matching the Fed's rate path. The BSP Governor emphasized that the domestic economy is in good shape given solid fundamentals, ample reserves, stable remittances, and slowing inflation. The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled for Aug 28 (third out of six this year). For 2025, Bloomberg median consensus and REGIS expects 75bp total worth of cuts to 5.00%.

Trump cuts tariffs on PH imports to 19%; PH removes tariffs on US-made cars. US President Donald Trump, Jr. reduced tariffs on imports from the Philippines to 19% from 20%. In exchange, President Marcos, Jr. will remove tariffs on certain imports sourced from the US, such as automobiles. Currently, imports of US-made automobiles are charged a tariff of 30% (up to 10-seater) and 20% (buses and trucks). However, PH imports of US car brands (e.g., Ford) are already sourced from ASEAN nations, which already enjoy zero duty. Imports of electric vehicles from the US (BEV and HEV) also enjoy zero tariffs today. President Marcos also mentioned that the Philippines agreed to buy more products (e.g., soy, wheat, and medicine) from the US, but details will still need to be worked out.

 

Government posts P242bn budget deficit in June; P766bn deficit as of 1H25. The government recorded a budget deficit of P242bn in June, which is wider both by 16% YoY and 67% MoM. This brings the 6M25 deficit to P766bn, 25% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was stated to have "remained relatively within target" at P766bn as the gov't continues to ramp up public spending to support economic growth. The wider deficit results from the weaker than expected revenues especially as customs collections continue to lag behind the full-year target.

The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

Trade deficit narrows to $4bn in June. The June trade deficit narrowed to US$4bn, contracting 9% YoY, reflecting the faster growth in exports over imports. This brings the 6M25 trade deficit to US$24bn, 4% narrower YoY. Imports for the month increased by 11% YoY to US$11.0bn, a reversal from the 4% decrease in May. This was primarily driven by the increase in capital and consumer goods imports.

Exports surged by 26% YoY to US$7.0bn, the sixth consecutive month of growth and the fastest since posting 28% growth back in April 2024. These made up the bulk of exports at 55% of the total. Growth in non-electronics exports was led by mineral products as well as machinery and transport equipment. Electronics exports climbed by 30% YoY to $3.8bn, an acceleration from the 8% increase in May. Meanwhile, non-electronics exports jumped by 22% YoY to US$3.1bn, a tad slower than the 24% growth in May. 

BOP shifts to US$226mn surplus in June; GIR settles at US$106.0bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a surplus of US$226mn, which marks a turnaround from both the US$155bn deficit in June 2024 and US$298mn deficit in May 2025. The surplus reflected the national gov't's foreign currency deposits with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) and income from BSP investments. This brings the YT-June deficit at US$5.6bn, reversing from the US$1.4bn surplus in the same period last year. The ballooning deficit stems from the wider trade gap, higher net outflows in the financial account, and frontloaded external debt repayments

The BSP projection for the overall BOP position for 2025 is a US$6.3bn deficit (or -1.3% of GDP). The BOP projection for 2025 reflects expectations of slower global trade and subdued investor confidence given uncertainties from trade policy and geopolitical developments.

Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-June stood at US$106.0bn, up from the $105.2bn in end-May. The GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. GIR is expected to decrease from $106bn in 2024 to $104bn in 2025.

 

 

BSP Revises BOP and Current Account Deficit Projections. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has revised its 2025 balance of payments (BOP) deficit forecast to -1.3% of GDP, wider than its previous projection of -0.8%. For 2026, however, the BOP deficit is now expected to narrow to -0.5% of GDP from -0.8%. The wider 2025 projection reflects continued current account (CA) shortfalls and weaker financial flows, despite an improving trade deficit.

Although the CA deficit is expected to narrow—to -3.3% of GDP in 2025 and -2.5% in 2026 from -3.9% in 2024—this is offset by a softer financial account, driven by global trade uncertainty, geopolitical risks, and subdued investor sentiment. Export growth is expected to weaken in 2025, with goods exports seen contracting by 1% (from +1%) and services export growth slowing to 6% (from 8%). Nonetheless, business process outsourcing (BPO) revenues and overseas Filipino worker (OFW) remittances remain resilient, providing a cushion to the external position. As of Q1 2025, the CA deficit stands at -3.7% of GDP, compared to -3.8% for full-year 2024.

Marcos approves P6.8tn 2026 budget. President Ferdinand Marcos. Jr. approved the proposed P6.8tn national budget for 2026 which will be submitted to Congress later this month. The P6.8tn budget is equivalent to 22% of GDP and 7% higher than 2025's P6.33 budget. Priority allocations are the education, agriculture, and transportation sectors, which will see increases in their respective budgets for next year.

NCR Wage Board Approves P50 Minimum Wage Hike. The National Capital Region (NCR) Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) has approved a P50 increase in the daily minimum wage, raising it to P695—an 8% hike. This adjustment follows the 19th Congress's adjournment in June without passing a broader P100–200 across-the-board wage hike bill. The P50 increase represents the highest percentage hike approved by the NCR wage board in the past decade.

The wage adjustment is expected to directly benefit 1.2 million minimum wage earners, while an additional 1.7 million full-time wage workers may benefit indirectly through the correction of wage distortions. The new rate will take effect on July 18, the day after the anniversary of the previous P35 wage hike granted on July 17, 2024.

Government debt inches up to P16.92tn in end-May. The government's total debt stock went up by 1% MoM to P16.92tn as of end-May. The current debt mix of the country's total obligations has domestic debt accounting for 69.6% and 30.4% being external borrowings. The year-end debt target for 2025 is pegged at P17.35tn which is 8% higher YoY than end-Dec's P16.1tn. This 2025 target is equivalent to 60.4% of GDP vs. 2024's debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.7%. The ratio is expected to further decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); by the end of the Marcos admin's term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.9%.

SEC mulls tiered public float for IPOs. The Securities and Exchange Commission is considering using a tiered approach for the minimum public float requirement of companies that eye to list on the PSE. SEC Chairperson Francisco Ed. Lim stated that the current 20% minimum public float requirement is a "one-size-fits-all" approach and does not take into account differing market capitalizations. Lim suggests using tiers depending on the company's market capitalization to decide the minimum float requirement, "the higher the market cap, the lower the percentage of free float." Previously, the SEC has allowed a lower initial public float of 15% through exemptive relief with conditions. Lim however stated he is not in favor of exemptive relief given that it is granted on a case-to-case basis while the tiered approach is more flexible and applicable to all. The PSE targets six IPOs this year - only one has listed so far, Top Line Business Development Corp. (TOP PM). Key future listings slated to take place in 2025 include GCash, Maynilad Water Services, Inc., and Hann Holdings, Inc.

Lower Stock Transaction Tax Effective. Effective July 1, Republic Act No. 12214—or the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act (CMEPA)—has reduced the stock transaction tax (STT) from 0.6% to 0.1%. This aligns the Philippines with other ASEAN markets such as Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, and Malaysia, where transaction taxes also average around 0.1%. This reform is expected to boost trading activity and encourage more participation in the local stock market by lowering trading costs for investors.

 

Digital payments rise 52% YoY in June. Payments made digitally over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) rose by 52% YoY to P2trn in June, accelerating from the 38% pace in May and bringing the 6M25 transaction value to P11.1trn, up 39% YoY.

Commercial banks’ loan growth 11.3% in May; M3 growth at 5.5%. Loan growth of Philippine commercial banks remained stable in May 2025, with an 11.3% year-on-year increase—slightly higher than April’s 11.2%. Loans to productive activities grew 10.2%, marginally slower than April’s 10.3%, weighed down by a 3.0% decline in manufacturing and slower agricultural growth at 6.7% from 9.5%. Consumer loans continued to show strength, growing 23.7%, just slightly below April’s 24.0%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) expanded by 5.5% in May, easing from 5.8% in April.

Asset quality steady in May. Philippine banks maintained broadly stable asset quality in May. When excluding interbank loans (IBL) and reverse repos (RRP)—which often distort the data—the non-performing loan (NPL) ratio edged up slightly by around 2 basis points (closer to 1.5bps) to 3.58% from 3.56% in April. In nominal terms, NPLs rose 1.6% month-on-month to ₱527 billion, outpacing the 1.2% growth in total loans. Meanwhile, the Past Due (PD) ratio fell by 2bps to 4.47%, despite PD loans increasing 0.9% MoM to ₱659 billion. Restructured Loans (RL) ratio ticked down by 1bp to 2.13%. The NPL coverage ratio dipped slightly to 94.6% in May from 95.1% in April. Loan growth (ex-IBL/RRP) re-accelerated to 11.7% YoY, up from 11.2% in April, though slightly behind the reported 12.4% due to a sharper rise in IBL/RRPs (+27% YoY). This growth aligns with the industry’s full-year forecast of 12%. 

Marcos delivers fourth State of the Nation Address. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. delivered his fourth State of the Nation Address (SONA), discussing the administration's accomplishments and progress for ongoing initiatives in key areas but controversial topics such as online gambling, the 19% US tariffs on PH goods, and any mention of the Vice President were not brought up. Marcos also talked about additional social welfare programs such as expanding PhilHealth coverage, higher financial assistance limits in public hospitals, more funding for free tertiary education programs, free bus transportation in certain cities, among others. These were likely in response to public dissatisfaction that showed in the administration slate's poor performance in the recent midterm elections last May. 

Marcos' net trust rating improves by 19ppt to 18% in June. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s net trust rating improved significantly in June, climbing to 18% from -1% in May, according to the latest Social Weather Stations Survey. Net trust ratings are derived by subtracting the percentage of respondents who express "little trust" from those who say they have "much trust." Marcos' "much trust" rating rose to 48% in June from 38% in May, while the "little trust" rating dropped to 30% from 39%. Despite this improvement, the current net trust rating remains considerably lower than the 63% he enjoyed at the start of his term. The recent uptick follows a decline to -3% in March 2025 after the arrest of former President Rodrigo Duterte.

Meanwhile, Vice President Sara Duterte’s net trust rating inched up by 1 percentage point to 38% in June from 37% in May. Her "much trust" rating slightly increased to 61%, while the "little trust" figure held steady at 23%. Her current rating, though improved from the 23% seen during her February 2025 impeachment by the Lower House, still pales in comparison to the 73% net trust rating she held at the start of her term.

Supreme Court rules VP Sara Duterte impeachment as unconstitutional; Lower House to appeal SC ruling. The Supreme Court (SC) ruled that the impeachment of Vice President Sara Duterte was unconstitutional, halting the trial that was supposed to take place early July. 

Four impeachment complaints were filed against Duterte, with the final one that was lodged back in Feb 2025 being the only one approved by the Lower House. The SC argued that failing to properly process the first three filings effectively dismissed these prior complaints. The high court argues that the dismissal of these cases, triggered the Constitutional rule of the one-year ban on filing new cases. This implies that another complaint has to be refiled by Feb 2026, should the Lower House pursue the impeachment of VP Duterte. Senate impeachment court spokesperson Regie Tongol said the SC ruling affirms the Senate's decision to act in line with the Constitution and uphold the rule of law, emphasizing their duty to respect the finality of high court decisions. 

Princess Abante, the Lower House spokesperson, stated that they will file a motion of reconsideration on the impeachment after it was declared unconstitutional by the SC.

 

BSP issues draft regulations on online gambling payment services. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has issued draft regulations governing online gambling payment services. Under the proposed rules, individuals who wish to participate in online gambling will be required to create a designated account called an Online Gambling Transaction Account (OGTA). Transfers to this account will be capped daily at 20% of the average daily balance of the user’s main transaction account. Additionally, the OGTA will only be operational during a six-hour window each day. Once created, all lending services offered on the same digital platform will be disabled, and users will have the option to further limit daily transfers or to disable the OGTA altogether. Separately, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto stated that a new law to tax online gambling could be passed within the year. At present, online gaming operators pay 38% of their gross gaming revenue (GGR) from e-casinos and e-bingo to the government, and 24.25% from sports betting GGR.

Gambling ads to be regulated and pre-screened. PAGCOR signed an MOU with the Ads Standard Council (ASC) to pre-screen and regulate all gambling-related advertisements across media platforms. Gambling ads are now part of the ASC’s must-screen categories, which include alcoholic beverages, over-the-counter medicines, food supplements, and breast milk substitutes. Earlier, PAGCOR ordered the removal of billboard and other out-of-home advertisements related to gambling activities by August 15.

Senate committee to tackle online gambling next week. The Senate committee on games and amusement will probe the online gambling sector next week. Panel Head Senator Erwin Tulfo said that the committee will invite all stakeholders: PAGCOR, Department of Finance, youth organizations, parent organizations, etc. He said that the panel intends to form a strong position before asking the President to issue an Executive Order banning online gambling.
