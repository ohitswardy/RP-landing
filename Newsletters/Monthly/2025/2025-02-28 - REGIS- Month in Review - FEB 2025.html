REGIS REPORT

28 February 2025
 

The Philippine market slightly recovered in February following the previous month's sharp sell-off, taking the PCOMP higher by 2.3% or 135.38 pts MoM to 5,997.97. The index traded in a narrow range throughout the month as investors weighed domestic cues from steady January inflation, the BSP rate pause and subsequent RRR cut, and largely pedestrian local corporate 4Q earnings, along with fluctuating US rate forecasts and the Trump tariff war. MSCI rebalancing changes that took effect on the close of February 28th prompted a surge in foreign selling which sent the PCOMP down below the 6,000-handle to close the month.

 

January inflation was steady MoM at 2.9% and clocked just slightly ahead of consensus forecast of 2.8%. Food inflation accelerated to 3.8% from 3.4% which was attributed to typhoons hitting late last year that impacted meat and vegetable supplies. With BSP Gov. Remolona citing uncertainties on the inflation and growth outlook, the central bank held the key policy rate steady at 5.75% in February, defying market's expectation for a 25-bp cut at the Monetary Board's first meeting for the year. Nonetheless, the BSP proceeded with its planned 200-bp RRR cut earlier than expected. This is now set to take effect on March 28th and estimated to inject at least P300-bn worth of liquidity into the financial system.

 

Meanwhile, 4Q earnings reports thus far have mostly come in-line with market forecasts. The Big 3 banks managed to grow their loan books by 13-18%, although NIMs struggled to expand save for BPI (+7.0% MoM) that pushed more into the consumer segment. Earnings of the Big 2 property developers bore divergent residential performance in 4Q, with SMPH (-3.7% MoM) seeing pre-sales rebound while ALI's (-2.7% MoM) continued to fall. Moreover, indications point to a weak outlook for the sector with developers offering more aggressive discounts and paring down launches in a move to continue reducing inventory levels. Still, there are some bright spots such as consumer names seeing a rebound in 4Q, with SSSG recovering to 3-5% for the food/staples segments of SM (-1.9% MoM) and RRHI (+2.3% MoM). Telcos saw TEL (-0.7% MoM) gain in mobile while GLO (+2.9% MoM) edged out in broadband; on e-wallets/digibanks, GLO's GCash/Mynt earnings disappointed due to a one-off clean-up of assets whereas TEL's Maya losses were narrower than forecasted. Still in utilities, MER (+9.4% MoM) stood out with a strong earnings beat thanks to higher contribution from its generation business.

 

MSCI rebalancing names were actively traded in the month. MONDE (+13.5% MoM) which was added to the Small Cap Index was among the top gainers in the PCOMP. URC (+8.9% MoM) and JGS (-1.9% MoM) which were demoted from Large Cap into Small Cap eventually saw some support from bargain hunters, more so for the former than the latter. Also, investors bought up JFC (+15.9% MoM) on speculation of a potential MSCI upweight after the removal of its foreign ownership limit, which should increase its foreign inclusion factor.

 

And as the market mulled over the potential impact of a widening tariff war from Trump's flurry of pronouncements throughout the month, ICT (-0.1% MoM) though little changed for February remained one of the top foreign sold names in the index.

 

Foreign outflows accelerated to $145m in February (from $114m in January), while ADTO picked up to $116m (from $93m).

 

The currency appreciated by 0.7% MoM to 57.99:$1, while the PH10Y yield eased by 10.4bps to 6.1245%.

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Consumer names largely rebounded with JFC (+15.9% MoM), MONDE (+13.5% MoM), and PGOLD (+12.6% MoM) among the PCOMP's biggest gainers in February, after being among the largest decliners in the previous month. JFC benefitted from speculation of a potential MSCI upweight from the removal of its foreign ownership limit, which should increase its foreign inclusion factor, while MONDE also saw inflows following its addition into the MSCI Small Cap Index. URC (+8.9% MoM) bounced after bargain hunters took advantage of the recent MSCI-related weakness to pick up shares for cheap.

 

On the other hand, EMI (-23.3% MOM) underperformed due to speculation of a potential deletion from the PCOMP after the PSE flagged a discrepancy in its reported free float level vs parent AGI's (+8.3% MoM) latest financial reports. The AGI report would imply free float of sub-20% for EMI which would fail the minimum level required for PCOMP constituents. WLCON (-6.0% MoM) remained weak following its sharp decline in the previous month due to the PCOMP deletion.

 

Banks continued to outperform as a sector, seeing BDO (+9.0% MoM), BPI (+7.0% MoM), and MBT (+3.3% MoM) post modest gains as broad macro uncertainty kept investors coming back to the space. However, CBC (-7.0% MoM) corrected following outsized gains from the PCOMP rebalancing at the close of January.

 

Unsurprisingly, property names failed to recover from the previous month’s slump after SMPH (-3.7% MoM), ALI (-2.7% MoM), and MEG (-1.7% MoM) 4Q results provided an uninspiring outlook for the residential segment going into 2025. This also dragged RLC (-5.1% MoM) ahead of its actual earnings release.

 

Defensives visibly outperformed as investors searched for yield, taking top dividend payers MWC, MER, AP, LTG, and SCC higher by 8-11% MoM.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP cuts reserve requirement ratio. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) will reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) for commercial banks by 200 bps to 5% starting March 28. Reserve requirements for digital and thrift banks will also be cut by 150 bps to 2.5% and 100 bps to 0.0%, respectively. The cut is expected to inject at least P300bn worth of liquidity into the financial system.

 

BSP keeps rates steady. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) held interest rates unchanged, leaving the target reverse repurchase rate at 5.75%, which was against consensus expectations of a 25 bps cut. BSP Governor Remolona stated that uncertainties on the outlook of inflation and growth were the main rationale for keeping rates steady, though he emphasized that the central bank remains in the easing cycle and that may cut by up to 50 bps in total for the year.

 

Inflation rate unchanged at 2.9% in January. Headline inflation was unchanged at 2.9% in January vs. the previous month. This is slightly higher than consensus forecast of 2.8% and came in at the midpoint of BSP's forecast range of 2.5-3.3%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose 0.5%, primarily driven by higher food prices which increased by 1.1% MoM. The increase was mainly due to price upticks in vegetables and meat due to the lingering effects of the several typhoons that hit the country in October and November.

Prices of rice and vegetables on the decline. According to the Dept of Agriculture's (DA) price monitoring data, the prices of local well-milled rice remain sticky but prices of imported well-milled rice saw significant decline, with the price range for local well-milled rice narrowing slightly from P40-54/kg (last Dec 18) to P40-52/kg (as of Feb 15). While prices for imported well-milled rice saw a bigger decline from P52-60/kg (last Dec 18) to P44-46/kg (as of Feb 15).

 

OFW Remittances up 3.0% YoY in December. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.0% YoY to US$3.38bn, a 20.3% MoM jump as Nov was the lowest monthly inflow in the past six months. This brings FY24 remittances up 3.0% YoY to US$34.5bn, in-line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year.

 

BOP deficit at US$4.1bn in January 2025; GIR drops to US$103bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$4.1bn. This is higher than Dec 2024's $1.51bn deficit and the $740mn deficit in Jan 2024. It was also the widest monthly BOP deficit in 11 years, primarily reflected the BSP's net foreign exchange operations and drawdown on the national government's deposits. The final GIR level for end-Jan 2025 stood at US$103.3bn, down from the US$106.3bn in end-Dec 2024. GIR level is reflective of an external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income.

 

Gov't records budget deficit of P330bn in December (-17.8% YoY); P1.51tn for FY24 (-0.4% YoY). The government recorded a budget deficit of P330bn in Dec, narrower than Dec 2023's deficit of P401bn, bringing the FY24 deficit to 1.51tn, which narrowed by P5.7bn (0.4% YoY). The FY deficit is equivalent to 5.7% of GDP—much lower than the 6.2% seen in 2023 but short of the 5.6% of GDP target set by the gov't.

 

Tax collections rise 12% YoY in January. The Department of Finance reported that preliminary data showed a 12% YoY increase in tax collections to P429.9bn for January. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections rose by 13.7% YoY to P350.6bn, while the Bureau of Customs (BOC) collected P79.3bn, up 8.1% YoY.

 

Government debt goes down to P16.05tn in end-December. The government's total debt stock declined by 0.2% MoM to P16.05tn as of end-December, given the appreciation of the PHP against the USD relative to the previous month of November. Domestic debt rose by 0.1% (P8.8bn) MoM to P10.9tn due to the impact of net borrowings. External debt decreased by 0.9% (P48.4bn) MoM as the peso appreciated and reduced the level of foreign denominated debt. The year-end figure is in-line with the P16.06 outstanding debt target. The debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 60.7%, higher than end-2023's 60.1% and is slightly above the target of 60.6%.

Commercial banks' loan growth picks up to 12.2% YoY in December. Bank loans rose by 12.2% YoY in December, faster than November's 11.1% rate. Loans to productive activities rose 10.8% in Dec from 9.8% the previous month, due to faster loan growth in retail, motor vehicle repair, electricity/gas/steam supply industries, and construction. Consumer loans growth accelerated to 25.0% in Dec from 23.3% as growth momentum was driven by credit card, salary loans, and motor vehicle loans.

 

Banks' asset quality improves further in December. NPL ratio of Phil banks fell to 3.27% in December from 3.54% in November. In nominal terms, NPLs fell 3.9% MoM to P500.3bn. NPL coverage rose to 96.08% in December from 93.21% in November. Restructured loans accounted for 2.03% of the industry’s loan portfolio, a tad higher than the 2% in November. Loan loss reserves inched up by 0.9% to P481bn in December from P485bn in November, bringing the loan reserve ratio to 3.14%.

 

January digital payments jump 38% to P1.8tn. Digital transactions over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) jumped 38.2% YoY in January, keeping pace from the 38% growth seen in Dec, and 41% in Nov. Combined PesoNet and InstaPay payments totaled to P1.8tn (roughly $30.8bn), much higher than P1.3tn last Jan 2024 but unchanged relative to the previous month of Dec.

 

PAGCOR says Philippine GGR grew 24% in 2024. PAGCOR said that the country’s gross gaming revenue (GGR) rose 24% to P410bn in 2024. 49% of GGR came from licensed casinos, implying FY24 GGR of P200bn (-3% YoY) and 4Q24 GGR of P51bn (flattish to mildly up QoQ and YoY). 38% of GGR came from e-games, implying FY24 GGR of P155bn (+170% YoY) and 4Q24 GGR of P52bn (+30% QoQ).

 

Vehicle sales rise 10.4% YoY in January. Industry vehicle sales grew by 10.4% YoY to 37,604 units (-10.6% MoM), according to the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Assoc. (TMA). CAMPI is targeting sales of 500k units for 2025 (+7% YoY). A total of 1,600 EVs were sold in January, of which 1,445 units were hybrid EVs, 146 were battery EVs, and nine were plug-in hybrid EVs.

 

Philippine ports' container throughput growth accelerates to 8% YoY in 4Q; FY24 volumes up 5%. Philippine container ports handled 1.52mn TEUs of foreign containers in 4Q24, up by 8% YoY and accelerating from the 7% pace in 3Q24. This brings nationwide foreign container volumes up 5% YoY to 5.62mn TEUs in FY24..

 

 

Bicam approves capital markets reform bill. Congress ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee report, which reconciled the Senate and Lower House’s versions of the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotions bill. Key measures include reduction in the tax on stock transactions to 0.1% from 0.6% and a cut in the documentary stamp tax (DST) on original issue of shares of stock to 0.75% from 1% of the par value of the shares of stock.

 

BPO firms can continue to enjoy incentives with 50% WFH. The implementing rules and regulations of the CREATE MORE Act was released. PEZA-accredited BPO firms can now implement work-from-home policies (up to 50% WFH) and still enjoy all the fiscal incentives. Prior to CREATE MORE, BPO firms were only allowed 30% WFH. Most PEZA-accredited BPO firms are likely instituting c. 30% WFH. BPO firms availing of BOI incentives can enjoy all the said incentives even if they implement 100% WFH.

 

Senate passes mining tax regime measure. The Senate approved on the third reading the bill setting up a margin-based royalty and windfall profit system for the mining industry with expectations that it will raise the gov't's share of mining profits. The Lower House approved its version of the bill last Sep 2024 so a reconciled bicameral version will be needed.
