REGIS REPORT

28 November 2025
 

Philippine equities delivered a volatile but ultimately positive performance in November, swinging from multi-year lows to a strong late-month recovery as investors navigated a mix of weak macro data, soft 3Q corporate earnings, heightened political risk, and shifting policy expectations. Ultimately, the PCOMP finished 1.6% or 92.56 pts higher MoM to 6,022.24, snapping four months of decline and trimming the YTD loss to 7.8%, though still remaining among the bigger underperformers in the region.

 

Sentiment was initially dominated by the sharp disappointment in 3Q GDP growth, which slowed to 4.0% and significantly undershot estimates. This fueled broad risk aversion early in the month, with consumer and property stocks falling the most.

 

3Q earnings releases also further weighed on the market, as results were generally mixed to soft across key sectors. A number of consumer names posted weaker-than-expected numbers, reflecting slowed household spending, while property developers faced pressure from weak residential sales and cautious launches. Financials largely met forecasts, but their results did little to offset fragile sentiment given the broader macro backdrop.

 

Political instability added another layer of uncertainty mid-month as allegations tying senior government officials to the flood control corruption scandal triggered aggressive de-risking, particularly among local institutions. This pushed the PCOMP below the 5,600 handle -- its weakest close since May 2020. But the tone shifted sharply after valuations hit even lower than crisis-level troughs, with the PCOMP’s forward PER dropping to just 8.2x. This triggered broad-based local bottom-fishing and fueled the market's strongest weekly rally since 2021, led by high-beta banks and property stocks.

 

Rate-cut expectations also influenced shifting sentiment throughout the month, amid failed bets of an off-cycle BSP rate cut and consistent messaging from the BSP about further monetary easing. Telcos and utilities outperformed during risk-off periods but lagged once the rebound took hold, while holdings played catch-up toward month-end as market breadth broadened.

 

While foreign flows remain choppy and political developments continue to warrant attention, the market enters December on firmer footing, supported by local buying amid more attractive valuations, although lingering growth concerns continue to limit upside.

  • Market turned net foreign bought this month, buoyed by inflows in PLUS $151.4mn and MYNLD $79.5mn. Outside the two, ICT continue to see foreign interest, the stock is now net foreign bought for the 7th straight month while RCR saw decent inflows due to its MSCi upweight.
  • Outflows in banks and property names meanwhile accelerated this month, as the corruption scandal continued to weigh on the overall outlook. But despite the strong outflows, both sectors erased losses for the month as local demand provided support given cheap valuations.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

19 Decliners outnumbered 11 advancers in the PCOMP in November.

 

Most sector indices closed higher as the market rebounded in the latter half of the month. Mining (+7.5% MoM) unsurprisingly bested other sectors, followed by Services (+4.9% MoM) that was largely lifted by telcos. CNVRG (+22.3% MoM) was the top index gainer in the month thanks to bottom-fishing shortly following the release of the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA) implementing rules and regulations (IRR). TEL (+15.4% MoM) and GLO (+8.5% MoM) were also among the outperformers amid the continued search for yield.

 

Property (+2.4% MoM) only came alive in the second half, ending higher for the month following a rebound from a deep slump that took ALI (+2.3% MoM) and SMPH (+2.7% MoM) to their lowest close since the 2010s. A soft 3Q showing for the sector -- along with downgraded guidance for ALI -- dampened sentiment, before bargain huters took over.

 

Banks (+2.2% MoM) was a more mixed space with BPI (+10.5% MoM) pulling away from its Big Three peers BDO (-1.9% MoM) and MBT (-4.3% MoM), despite comparably positive 3Q results that saw all three banks report numbers that were in-line with forecasts.

 

Consumers were mostly lower in November due to weak 3Q private consumption and mixed corporate earnings results. URC (-13.7% MoM), JFC (-12.7% MoM), and MONDE (-9.5% MoM) were three of the four biggest laggards in the PCOMP while CNPF (+15.4% MoM) and PGOLD (+10.2% MoM) managed to buck the sector weakness as sentiment held up better for stocks that were more exposed to staples.

 

Power & Utilities generally outperformed in November, though heavyweight MER (+1.1% MoM) was a notable laggard after 3Q results bore soft volumes due to weak economic activity, and as flows generally rotated away from outperformers. MYNLD (+5.7% MTD) at its lowest point was down 6% from the early-November IPO, before bouncing to a peak at +10% vs IPO price, and eventually settling with a respectable mid-single-digit gain to close out its debut month despite the market volatility.

 

Gaming stocks were divergent with PLUS (+16.9% MoM) rebounding from the deep sell-off the previous month and BLOOM (-10.9% MoM) down sharply after disappointing 3Q earnings.

 

Meanwhile, despite relatively tempered gains, ICT (+2.4% MoM) remained an outperformer thanks to solid 3Q earnings and continued strong foreign inflow. The stock is still the best-performing PCOMP name with its 41.8% YTD advance.

 
MACRO NEWS

3Q25 GDP grows 4.0% -- behind expectations

GDP grew at 4.0% in 3Q, much slower than the 5.5% growth in 2Q25 and the 5.2% growth rate back in the same period last year. The 4.0% GDP growth is significantly behind consensus expectations of 5.2%. The print also missed the government’s 5.5–6.5% target range. It was the lowest growth rate since 3Q11's +3.0%. On the demand side, the improvement in net exports (+1.0% in 3Q from -0.1% in 2Q) was offset by deteriorating metrics across the expenditure accounts—private consumption (+4.1% from 5.3%), gov't consumption (+5.8% from +8.7%), and capital formation (-2.8% from +1.2%). If we exclude the pandemic YoY declines from 2020-2021, private consumption growth at +4.1% was the lowest since 3Q10's +2.6%. Private investments were negative for the first time at -2.8% since 3Q19's -0.7%. The decline was likely caused by the slump in construction (-0.5%) given the corruption scandal involving infrastructure projects. Gov't construction declined -26.2% in 3Q, slowing further from the 8.2% growth in 1Q and offset sustained double-digit growth in private construction (+16.7% from +14.6%). Exports grew for the fourth straight quarter (+7.0%), while imports tempered to 2.6% from 3.5% in 2Q. This brings 9M25 GDP growth to 5.0%, below the lower end of the government's target of 5.5-6.5% and consensus and Regis expectations of 5.5% GDP growth for 2025. GDP for 4Q25 needs to grow by at least 6.9% to hit the minimum full-year target. Economic Secretary Arsenio Balisacan commented that current and next year macroeconomic forecasts are under review.

 

Finance Secretary expects gov't revenue target revisions and budget adjustments

Dept. of Finance Secretary Frederick Go said revenue target revisions and budget adjustments need to be made, as the ongoing corruption probe has weighed on economic activity and put collection goals at risk. Recall that the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and Bureau of Customs (BOC) have been guided to likely to miss their targets due to lower remittances from the Dept. of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and slower import growth. Go emphasized that corrective actions need to be made (i.e., optimized spending, identifying areas where savings can be achieved) to address current gov't fiscal challenges. Executive Secretary Ralph Recto stated that the Budget Committee will review its macroeconomic targets and assumptions in Dec. As of 9M25, revenue collections are up 2% to P3.37trn (74.5% of the gov't's P4.52 trn goal for 2025).

 

BSP considering 25bp cut in Dec; RRR cut not urgent

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona stated that the central bank will consider another 25bp rate cut in Dec given that "demand is affected, just like investor sentiment." He noted that a jumbo 50bp cut is unlikely. Remolona also elaborated that a reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cut is "on the table… but there's no urgency in adjusting it." The RRR is currently at 5.0%. The ratio was 9.5% at the start of Remolona's term in 2023. On forex intervention, the BSP Governor stated that the central bank lets the market determine the exchange rate as it does not have a specific target peso level for it to intervene. Intervention was guided to only be done for "large swings in the peso that could lead to inflation." The peso depreciated from P58.15/USD on Oct 1 to P58.98/USD as of Nov 24. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. 

Inflation holds steady at 1.7% in October

Headline inflation remained unchanged at 1.7% in Oct relative to the previous month of Sep. This is in line with the consensus forecast of 1.8% and within the BSP's forecast range of 1.4-2.2% for Oct. Inflation has fallen from 2.8% in Jan and has been hovering below BSP's 2-4% target for the past eight months. Inflation held steady in Oct as price upticks in fish and utilities (electricity, gas, and fuel) were balanced by slower increases in meat and vegetable prices. Food overall was up 0.5% YoY, slower than the 1.0% pace in Oct. Food saw faster price upticks in fish but slower price increases in meat and vegetables. Notably, rice prices are on their sixteenth consecutive MoM price decline since Jul 2024. According to the Dept. of Agriculture, average prices for local and imported well-milled rice are around P42.6/kg and P42.9/kg as of Oct vs. P46.6/kg and P46.7/kg back in Jan, respectively. Electricity, gas, and fuel saw an acceleration to 2.5% YoY from the 0.9% increase the previous month likely driven by the recent depreciation in the peso. The peso depreciated to P59 per US dollar by end-Oct from P58.15 in the beginning of the month. Core inflation inched down to 2.5% in Oct from 2.6% in Sep, core non-food inflation likewise went down to 2.3% from 2.4% the previous month. This brings the YT-Oct headline inflation rate to 1.7%, below the lower end of the 2-4% BSP target for this year. For 2025, the BSP expects inflation to be 1.7%. Consensus and Regis expect inflation to average 1.8% in 2025. The last Monetary Board meeting for the year will be held on Dec 11. For 2025, consensus and Regis expect 100bp total worth of cuts to 4.75%. For 2026, consensus and Regis expect one 25bp cut to 4.5%.

 

S&P reaffirms Philippines' investment grade credit rating

S&P Global Ratings affirmed the Philippines’ investment grade credit rating of BBB+ with a “positive” outlook as growth prospects are noted to remain strong despite the corruption scandal weighing on the economy this year. BBB+ is a notch below “A”-level grade and the positive outlook implies the Philippines’ credit rating can be raised within 24 months if improvements are sustained.

 

Government posts P11bn budget surplus in Oct; P1.11trn deficit as of 10M25

The Philippine government recorded a budget surplus of P11bn in Oct, 76% wider YoY on sluggish revenue growth and slower gov't spending. This brings the 10M25 deficit to P1.11trn, 15% wider YoY. The YTD budget deficit was said to be in line with the P1.56trn full-year program. The wider YTD deficit resulted from the faster growth in expenses vs revenues. Revenue collections in Oct declined 7% YoY, dragged by poor tax and customs collections. Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) collections were only up by 1%. Recall that former BIR commissioner Romeo Lumagui previously stated that there was a slowdown in revenue collection given the ongoing probe on the Dept. of Public Works and Highways' (DPWH) flood control issues. Customs collections fell 5%, reversing from the 2% increase in Sep due to lower import volumes and fewer working days in the month. Non-tax revenues dipped 53% given the recognition of one-off remittances in 2024. Expenditures decreased by 8% YoY in Oct due to the decline in primary spending. Primary spending for the month was lower by 9%. Similar to the previous three months, the gov't spending decline in Oct was primarily caused by the ongoing flood control probe. Interest payments rose 4% from higher coupon payments for domestic and external debt. The gov't eyes to narrow the deficit to 5.5% of GDP this year from 5.7% in 2024.

 

Infrastructure spending slump persists in September

Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) fell 9% YoY to P1.04tn in 9M25. This implies that infrastructure spending contracted by 35% YoY and 5% MoM to P100bn for the month of September alone. Cited suspension of payments for public works amid ongoing validation and audit related to corruption concerns. DPWH Secretary Vince Dizon lifted the suspension of procurement last September 16. Cumulative 9M25 spending of P1.04tn lags the full-year target of P1.5tn (-2%).

 

Marcos implements flexible rice tariff starting next year

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. Signed Executive Order (EO 105), modifying the import duty on rice depending on global rice prices. Starting Jan 1, 2026, Most Favored Nation (MFN) rice tariffs are to be adjusted based on global rice prices. The duty will increase by 5ppt for every 5ppt drop in the int'l rice price or decrease 5ppt for every 5ppt rise—the MFN tariff cannot fall below 15% or exceed 35%. The current 15% base tariff (which has been lowered from 35% originally back in June 2024) will be maintained when imports resume in Jan, with a newly established Inter-Agency Group on Rice Tariff Adjustment to set guidelines on future tariff changes. Agriculture groups commented that they were not supportive of the new EO as they argue that the tariff rate should be based on local not int'l rice prices, they also highlighted the possibility that tariff adjustments could encourage more speculative behavior and smuggling.

OFW Remittances up 3.7% YoY in September

Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.7% YoY to US$3.1bn in Sep, accelerating from the 3.2% growth seen in Aug. Land-based remittances from the USA, Singapore, and Saudi Arabia were cited to be the primary drivers in the increase in remittances given their % contribution to the total. Sea-based remittances growth inched down to 3.7% YoY from 3.8% the previous month. In PHP terms, remittances climbed +7.9% YoY as the peso depreciated sharply from P57.08/USD end-Aug to P58.04/USD end-Sep.

 

Government issues IRR for Konektadong Pinoy Act

The Dept. of Information & Communications Technology (DICT) has released the final implementing rules and regulations (IRR) for the Konektadong Pinoy Act (KPA). The publication of the IRR paves the way for the implementation of the KPA. The IRR takes effect 15 days from publication (Nov 5). Among the highlights of the KPA: new operators no longer need to secure a franchise (license) from Congress to offer services; carriers will be required to share their infrastructure with other operators at non-discriminatory rates; government will establish a “Spectrum Management Policy Framework” to govern management, issuance and recall of spectrum; and it provides for consumer rights and expectations on performance standards. We expect implementation of the KPA to usher in greater competition, particularly for the wireline broadband sector. DICT claims that “three” foreign operators are planning to enter the market because of the KPA.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth slows to 10.5% in September

Preliminary data showed that loans of commercial banks rose 10.5% in September, moderating further from the 11.2% pace in August. Loans to productive activities decelerated to 9.1% from 9.9% the previous month, driven by a steeper 10% contraction in manufacturing loans (vs. -5.5% YoY in Aug) and a sustained slowdown in real estate loan growth (+9.2% in Sep from +11% in Aug). Consumer loans still grew at a heady pace of 23.5%, though a tad slower than August’s 23.9% rate. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) picked up further to 7.3% in September from 6.6% in August.

 

Vehicle sales (ex-BYD) flat in October; Toyota sales up 6% YoY

CAMPI reported vehicle sales for October was flat (+5.2% MoM) at 40,014 units. Note that CAMPI’s data does not capture BYD sales. Brings 10M25 vehicle sales to 383,424 units, down by 0.2% YoY. Assuming BYD’s market share of ~6%, we estimate industry car sales may be up 6% in 10M25. EV sales (ex-BYD) jumped 62% MoM to 3,603 units and accounted for 9% of the market. Brings 10M25 EV sales (ex-BYD) to 24,265 units (~6% of car sales). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 20,404 units, up 5.5% YoY, 10.7% MoM. Brings TMP’s 10M25 sales to 185k units, up 3.8% YoY. To reach TMP’s sales target of 235k units (+8%), sales should average 24.9k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach REGIS’ sales forecast of 231k units (+6%), sales should average 23k units per month for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 31% of NAV (24% of gross asset value) and 35% of profit. 

SEC issues draft revisions on REIT regulations

The SEC issued a draft memorandum circular proposing revisions to REIT regulations. The draft circular is open for public comments until December 3. Among the proposals is to broaden the definition of “income-generating real estate”. The SEC proposes to allow power, infrastructure, and telecom assets to qualify as REIT-able assets. Reinvestment of REIT proceeds must be completed within two years (instead of one year). The proposed amendments would also require detailed disclosures on leases, related-party transactions, and fees. We do not expect the proposed revisions to significantly affect the existing REITs.

 

Budget, executive secretaries resign as President Marcos initiates Cabinet reshuffle

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has appointed and reassigned new Cabinet Secretaries following the recent resignations of Budget Secretary Amenah Pangandaman and Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin. Pangandaman and Bersamin resigned after their departments were cited to be allegedly involved in the flood control corruption scandal. This marks the second Cabinet reshuffle after the 'bold reset' back in May after the administration slate only won 5 out of 12 seats in the previous midterm elections. The new appointments/reassignments are as follows: Finance Secretary Ralph Recto is the new executive secretary. Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (SAPIEA) or economic czar Frederick Go is the new finance secretary. Budget Undersecretary Rolando Toledo is OIC at the Dept. of Budget and Management (DBM). Recto as the newly appointed Executive Secretary emphasized focus on good governance and transparency—adding that "the president has guaranteed that cases will be filed, masterminds [be] named and accountability [will be] delivered".
