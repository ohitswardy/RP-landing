REGIS REPORT

31 January 2025
 

Philippine equities started 2025 on a sour note with the PCOMP plunging into bear market territory, down 10.2% MoM to 5,862.59, as the higher-for-longer narrative took hold once more while PCPOMP rebalancing outflows at month-end further dragged shares. On the last trading day of the month alone, the benchmark slid 4.0% as outsized inflows for the inclusion of CBC (+46.5% MoM) and AREIT (+10.7% MoM) amounting to over $100m was funded by the same amount of selling out of the rest of the index constituents in order to accommodate the additions. Naturally, deleted names WLCON (-41.3% MoM) and NIKL (-37.8% MoM) were the month's biggest decliners. 

Outside of these, deletion predicitons for the MSCI February rebalancing have also resulted in some drastic action for URC (-23.0% MoM) and JGS (-21.4% MoM). Meanwhile, ICT (-9.3% MoM) was among the bigger drags on the index for the month as investors grew concerned over the impact of Trump tariffs on Mexico. Given the broadly risk-off mood, investors hid in high yield plays TEL (+3.5% MoM), LTG (+1.5% MoM), GLO (+1.2% MoM), and DMC (+0.7% MoM) -- only 4 out of the 30 PCOMP names closed in the green this month.

Not helping sentiment, macro releases in January largely disappointed. December 2024 inflation of 2.9% accelerated from 2.5% in the previous month and was worse than Street's 2.6% estimate, amid faster rise in prices of housing utilities and transport. 4Q24 GDP of 5.2% was flat QoQ and also missed Street's 5.5% forecast, with consumption being the main drag as growth unusually slowed to 4.7% (from 5.2%) despite the expected holiday spending boost. 

On monetary policy, BSP Gov. Remolona has telegraphed more rate cuts of as much as 50bps this year, after 75bps in rate cuts last year that brought the key rate to 5.75%. Remolona also said banks' RRR could be slashed by another 200bps down to 5.0% possibly this year, which would follow the 250-bp RRR cut that took effect in Oct 2024. The Monetary Board is set to hold its first meeting of the year on Feb 13th. 

Foreign outflows continue for the Philippine market, amounting to $114m in January. ADTO remained sluggish at $93m for the month. 

The currency depreciated by 0.9% MoM to 58.372:$1, while the PH10Y yield rose by 5.3bps MoM at 6.2289%.

Key data
 
 

Philippines starting off slow this 2025, as appetite towards the stock market is dry, as investors struggle to find a catalyst, given the uncertainty. Although January’s average daily turnover stood at $93m, a big chunk of it was actually volumes that rotated during the PSEi rebalancing held on the last day of the month, where CBC and AREIT got included in the main index, replacing NIKL and WLCON. Excluding the liquidity event, average daily  turnover was only at $80m, a steep drop from December’s ADV of $105m (-23% MoM). Foreigners also are net sellers in the market, and registered a $104m outflow this month.



Foreign-Local split close to even at 49/51.

  • Market extends its net foreign outflow streak for the 3rd straight month and  foreigners continue to be sellers across the board as Trump’s return to the oval office poses uncertainty to emerging markets.  Outflows were skewed heavily on Banks and all the Big 3 banks top the list this month. CBC though bucked the trend and saw $25.1m worth of inflows with its inclusion to the PSEi.  Consistent foreign demand was seen the whole January,  and further accelerated after the exchange announced its inclusion to the main index.
  • Foreigners also continue to sell consumer names despite the government’s effort to curb inflation by capping rice prices. URC is the top foreign sold among its peers with an outflow of $16.4m, trading back to 2012 levels.
  • Property names meanwhile ended the month mixed. ALI remained the top foreign sold stock and saw $21.2m worth of outflows, while foreigners turned buyers in SMPH for $1.1m after 3 months of straight outflows taking advantage of the stock trading near pandemic lows. Property names though were generally weak as outlook towards the sector continues to be negative. Selling pressure further accelerated during the last trading day of the month and both ALI and SMPH printed a new 52wk lows, on the back of PSEi rebalancing outflows. Index inclusion AREIT meanwhile saw $28.7m of inflow and rose +11% in January.
  • Notable name: ICT outflow of $12.3m and fell -9.3% on the back of Trump’s plan to impose higher tariffs on Mexico, which could potentially hurt port operations in the country.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

PCOMP breadth was negative in January, with only 4 advancers vs 26 decliners. The eventful PCOMP February rebalancing saw deleted names WLCON (-41.3% MoM) and NIKL (-37.8% MoM) clock the worst monthly performance for January; on the flipside, added constituents CBC (+46.5% MoM) and AREIT (+10.7% MoM) were the biggest outperfromers. 

Defensive names TEL (+3.5% MoM), LTG (+1.5% MoM), GLO (+1.2% MoM), and DMC (+0.7% MoM) were the four index names that managed to finish higher MoM as investors bought up high yield plays. On this note, REITs also bucked the market slump with FILRT (+6.1% MoM), RCR (+1.5% MoM), and MREIT (+1.2% MoM) closing higher MoM.

Conglomerates visibly underperformed during the month with AGI (-33.3% MoM), GTCAP (-24.8% MoM), and SMC (-24.2% MoM) down the most and closing out the month among the index's worst performers, as poor liquidity paired with heavy PCOMP rebalancing outflows dampened shares.

Consumer was another broadly negative space with several stocks down double-digits. Beyond rebalancing names, MONDE (-22.7% MoM), PGOLD (-19.9% MoM), and JFC (-17.5% MoM) were down the most as higher-than-expected December inflation and weaker-than-expected 4Q consumption weighed on sentiment.

With the narrative for higher-for-longer rates once again gaining ground, Property names likewise pulled back, though to a much less extent vs Conglomerates and Consumer. Residential-heavy ALI (-14.9% MoM) was again the worst of the bunch, followed by MEG (-13.7% MoM), whereas names with a larger recurring income base SMPH (-8.0% MoM) and RLC (-5.3% MoM) fared a bit better.

While they also declined for the month, big banks technically outperformed vs the benchmark's 10.2% decline -- BPI (-4.8% MoM), BDO (-4.4% MoM), and MBT (-3.8% MoM) remained relatively well-supported amid the view that higher-for-longer rates will keep NIMs afloat while RRR cuts in the horizon should support credit growth.

 
MACRO NEWS

BSP chief sees 50bps in rate cuts, 200bps in RRR cuts in 2025 . BSP Gov. Remolona said the key policy rate may be slashed by 50bps in total this year, with the first 25-bp possible in 1H (and as early as in the February meeting), and the next 25-bp in 2H. The BSP is also discussing a possible 200bps cut in banks' reserve requirement ratio (RRR) which may happen in the middle of the year. This comes amid the central bank's benign inflation expectations for the year. 

4Q24 GDP grows 5.2%, misses 2024 target given weak consumption. GDP grew at 5.2% in 4Q24, flat vs. the 5.2% growth rate in 3Q. This is below consensus expectations of 5.5%. On the demand side, the lackluster growth stems from the slowdown in private consumption (4.7% from 5.2% in 3Q) and a slowdown in private investments (4.1% from 13.7%). Private consumption may have been dampened by the six typhoons that hit the country from Oct to Nov as well as the lingering effects of elevated inflation. The slowdown in private investments was mainly due to durable equipment growth coming off from a high base due to significant aircraft orders in 2023. Net imports narrowed to -0.3% from -3.0%, but still weighs down on overall GDP. On the supply side, the agri sector exhibited a decline for the third month in a row, declining by -1.8%, but is still an improvement from -2.7% the previous quarter. Growth in the service sector also improved and was faster at 6.7% from 6.3% but the industry sector slowed to 4.4% from 5.1%. Bringing FY24 GDP growth to 5.6%, below the government's target of 6-6.5% and the Bloomberg consensus median forecast of 5.8%. Recall that the gov't revised its GDP growth expectations for this year last Dec 2024. The 2025 GDP growth target is set at 6.0-8.0% a wider band than the previous 6.5-7.5% range given "evolving domestic and global uncertainties."

Inflation rate up to 2.9% in December. Headline inflation rose to 2.9% in December from 2.5% in November. This is slightly higher than consensus forecast of 2.6% and came in at the higher end of BSP's forecast range of 2.3-3.1%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose 0.6%, primarily driven by higher food prices which increased by 0.8% MoM. The increase was mainly due to price upticks in vegetables and meat due to the several typhoons that hit the country in October and November. Rice CPI sequentially dipped for the sixth consecutive month by 0.7% bringing the cumulative decline to 3.7%. Outside of food, increases in the price of rentals and LPG contributed the higher inflation print, rising 2.4% and 7.8% respectively. Core inflation increased slightly to 2.8% in December from 2.5% in November. While core non-food inflation inched up to 2.8% from 2.7% for the same periods. With this, the country's average inflation rate in 2024 is at 3.2% in-line with BSP expectations and lower than the 6% average for 2023.

Price cap on imported rice to go as low as P49 in March. The maximum suggested retail price (MSRP) for imported rice, currently set at P58 per kilo, will be gradually reduced starting February 2025, with a potential drop to P49 by March if global prices remain stable, according to Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel. The MSRP will be lowered to P55 on February 5, then to P52 on February 15. This reduction is contingent on global prices staying within $530–$550 per metric ton for 5% broken rice.

Remittances rise 3.3% YoY in November. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.3% YoY to US$2.81bn but dropped 8.8% MoM—marking the lowest monthly inflow in the past six months. The acceleration in land-based remittances from Saudi Arabia, which was up 26.2% YoY and 2.0% MoM, was offset by the slower remittances growth from the USA, only up 0.6% YoY and down 18.6% MoM. Sea-based remittances growth picked up to 1.0% from 0.6% in October. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 8.5%, marking the second-fastest increase of the year and surpassing the 3.6% growth recorded in October. Bringing YT-November remittances up 3.0% YoY to US$31.1bn, broadly in line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year.

Trade deficit further narrows to $4.14bn in December. The December trade deficit narrowed down further to US$4.14bn, the smallest since Mar 2024, down 1.0% YoY and 14.8% MoM from $4.85bn last Nov. Driven by the decline in the value of imports which narrowed the deficit for the period. Imports for the month declined 2% YoY to US$9.79bn. The drop was led by fuel as it continued to decrease YoY by 3%, a six month streak of declines in fuel imports. Exports continue to fall at 2.2% YoY to US$5.66bn, marking the fourth consecutive month of contraction. Led by the decline in electronics exports which continued to drop by 17% YoY to US$2.80bn, electronics accounted for 50% of total exports during the period. Bringing the FY24 trade deficit to US$54.21bn, +3% YoY. Although wider than last year's deficit, this latest figure is lower than the recently largest trade gap of US$57.65bn in 2022.

BOP posts US$1.5bn deficit in December 2024. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$1.5bn in December 2024, according to preliminary data. This is a reversal of the US$642mn BOP surplus recorded in Dec 2023 but lower than the US$2.3bn deficit in November 2024. The BOP deficit in December 2024 reflected the Bangko Sentral nf Pilipinas’ (BSP) net foreign exchange operations and drawdown on the National Government's (NG) deposits. This brings the cumulative BOP position to a surplus of US$609mn in 2024. This is lower than the US$3.7bn surplus recorded in 2023 and the US$3.5bn BOP surplus 2024 target of the BSP. Preliminary data illustrates the decline in cumulative BOP surplus was due to higher trade in goods deficit, and lower net receipts from trade in services and net foreign borrowings by the National Government.

 

Dollar reserves drop 1.5% at end-December. The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) at end-December saw a 1.5% MoM decrease to $106.8bn from $108.5bn at the end-November. Primarily due to the BSP's net foreign exchange operations, payments for foreign debt obligations, downward valuation adjustments on gold holdings. The level of dollar reserves by end-Dec was enough to cover about 3.8x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.5x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The BSP expects the country's GIR to settle at $106bn by end-2024. Reserve position in the IMF increased 1.1% MoM to $676.2mn. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $11bn, slightly down 0.2% MoM. Foreign investments declined by 1.3% MoM to $90bn. The end-December figure is in-line with the BSP's end-2024 target of $106bn. While the BSP expects $110bn by end-2025.

Government debt inches up to P16.09tn in end-November. The government’s total debt stock rose by 0.4% (P71bn) MoM to P16.09tn as of end-November. Cited depreciation of the PHP against the USD. Domestic debt rose by 0.3% (P31.8bn) MoM to P10.9tn due to the net issuance of domestic securities and the impact of the depreciation PHP on retail dollar bonds. External debt rose by 0.8% (P39bn) MoM to P5.17tn on the back of the PHP’s depreciation and net foreign loan additions. The government is targeting its outstanding debt to end at P16.06tn in 2024 (60.6% of GDP) and P17.35tn (60.4% of GDP) in 2025 (see here). As of end-September 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 61.3%. The ratio is expected to decline below 60% by 2027 (58.4%); By the end of the Marcos admin’s term in 2028, it expects the debt-to-GDP ratio to fall to 56.3%.

Government plans to raise US$3.5bn from global bond sales this year. According to Finance Secretary Ralph Recto, the government is eyeing the issuance of global bonds totaling US$3.5bn this year, with its first issuance likely in 1H. These issuances will mostly be in USD but there could also be euros. The government plans to focus more on domestic borrowing, with a financing mix of 80% domestic and 20% foreign this year, to minimize FX exposure. Recall that the government raised over US$5bn from global bond sales in 2024. The national government’s debt stock amounts to P14.51tn in end-November, of which 69% is domestic debt and 31% is external debt.

 

Senate approves bill for lowering tax on stock transactions. Senate Bill No. 2865 or the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act was approved on final reading. Rationale for the bill was to make the country's capital market more competitive relative to its regional peers as well as to encourage more Filipinos to invest in the stock market. A forecast by the PSE, states that a lowered 0.1% stock transaction tax would boost stock trading to P4.9tn by 2029. Key measures in the bill are as follows: Reduction of stock transaction tax; Tax on stock transactions from 0.1% to 0.6%; Taxes on dividends; Tax on dividends for non-resident investors to be lowered from 25% currently to 10%, unified with the current 10% tax rate on dividends for residents; Minimum corporate income tax for foreign entities; Requires resident foreign corporations and their regional operating headquarters to pay a minimum corporate income tax of 10% on taxable income from 2% previously. While tax rates on capital gains and interest income remain unchanged at 15% and 20%, respectively. The bill shall proceed to a bicameral conference committee with the lower house' version before the President signs the final version into law. The effectivity date of the bill was stated to be on or after January 1, 2025, as of the latest version of the reading but may likely be adjusted until after it is signed into law.

House Committee approves P200 minimum wage hike. The House Committee on Labor and Employment approved a substitute bill, proposing for a P200 across-the-board daily wage increase. The approval follows Speaker Martin Romualdez's announced support for the passage of the wage hike. Recall that the Senate approved a bill increasing the daily minimum wage in the private sector by P100. Note that the 19th Congress will adjourn on Feb 8 for the mid-term elections and will resume on June 2 to June 13. A new set of lawmakers (20th Congress) will be appointed by July 28. Minimum wages in NCR today are between P608-645 while the region with the lowest min. wages (P316-361) is BARMM.

Lower House approves bill to lower tax rates on cigarettes, sought to improve collection. The House of Representatives approved the bill which will lower the excise tax increases for cigarettes, tobacco, vapor (e-cig/e-vape) products as to mitigate the decline in tax collection from the goods. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) was P52bn short of its P185bn tobacco excise tax target last year. The new bill seeks to lower the increase to 2% every even-numbered year effective Jan 2026 and maintain 4% every odd-numbered year effective Jan 2027 till Dec 2035. Current law has the annual increase on excise tax for tobacco products at 4%. A projection from the House ways and means committee says the measure can net the gov't P66bn from 2026 to 2030. The bill also gives the President the option to increase the tax rate up to 5% if the national gov't deficit of the previous year exceeds the program deficit by an equivalent of 2% of GDP of the previous year.

 

Commercial banks' loan growth picks up to 11.1% YoY in November. Preliminary data showed the outstanding commercial bank loans rose by 11.1% YoY in November, a tad faster than October's 10.6% rate. Loans to productive activities rose 9.8% in Nov from 9.1% the previous month due to faster loan growth in retail, motor vehicle repair, and electricity/gas/steam supply industries. Consumer loans growth decelerated slightly to 23.3% in Nov from 23.6% as growth momentum continued by credit card and vehicle loans. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth remained steady at 7.7% in November.

Banking sector NPL ratio falls in November. The NPL ratio of the banking sector fell to 3.54% in November, from 3.6% in October. It stood at 3.41% in November 2023. Nonperforming loans fell 0.7% MoM to P520.55bn in November (from 524.31bn in October), and NPL rose 14.6% YoY (from P454.28bn). The total loan portfolio rose 1.2% MoM to P14.72trn (from P14.55trn in October), growing 10.4% YoY (from P13.34trn in November 2023). Past due loans declined 0.8% YoY to P635.6bn in November (from P640.9bn in October), though it increased 12.8% YoY (from P563.4bn in November 2023). As a result, the past due ratio fell to 4.32% in November, from 4.4% in October. It stood at 4.22% in November 2023. Restructured loans rose 0.3% MoM to P293.7bn, but this represents a 4% decline YoY. NPL coverage rose to 93.2% in November, from 92.3% in October. However, this is lower than the 101.5% figure in November 2023.

Vehicle sales rise 7% YoY in December; FY24 sales up 8.7%. Industry car sales grew 7.4% YoY, 2.8% MoM to 42,044 units in December, according to the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Assoc. (TMA). This brings FY24 vehicle sales to 467,252 units, up 8.7% YoY and broadly in line with CAMPI’s target of 468,300 units. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 20,263 units in December, up by 3.6% YoY and 4.8% MoM. This brings FY24 retail sales higher by 9% YoY to 218,019 units, above management’s target of 215k units (+7%) and Regis’ forecast of 204k units (+3%). TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 32% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of profit before parent expenses.

 

LPC: Office vacancy rises in 4Q24; residential unsold inventory days rises.In its 4Q24 presentation, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy rose 2ppt QoQ to 18%. Metro Manila office vacancy rose 2ppt QoQ to 18%, largely due to POGO closures. Net closures hit 100,000sqm nationwide in 4Q24 – from 68,000sqm of net take-up in 3Q24. BPO net office take-up fell 98% QoQ and YoY to 1,000sqm. Net office take-up from traditional tenants (including government agencies) rose 40% QoQ (up 133% YoY) to 98,000sqm. POGO experienced net closures of 199,000sqm, from net closures of 53,000sqm in 3Q24. Meanwhile, the residential unsold inventory level rose from 29 months of sales in 3Q24 to 34 months in 4Q24. Colliers estimates that as of 3Q24, the unsold inventory level in Metro Manila stood at about 5.5 years. Residential sales fell 55% YoY and 36% QoQ to 4,375 units.

PAGCOR revenue grows 41% in 2024. Revenue of the gaming regulator, PAGCOR, grew 41% to P112bn in 2024. Revenue from e-games (including bingo) climbed 125% to P48.8bn. Revenue from licensed casinos was flat at P33.1bn.

Maharlika takes 20% stake in Synergy Grid. Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) signed a binding agreement with Synergy Grid & Development Phils., Inc. (SGP PM) for an undisclosed amount. MIC will subscribe to preferred shares offered by SGP and will be entitled to two (2) seats in SGP’s expanded nine-member board (currently 7 board seats). At the NGCP level, MIC will also have two board seats, once total seats are increased to 15 from 10. MIC President and CEO Rafael D. Consing said that this flagship investment will “safeguard the nation’s power supply from external threats and disruptions, citing the need for the government to have a say in NGCP decisions.” A news outlet wrote that the MIC has initially purchased 1.3bn preferred shares of SGP at P15/sh (P19.5bn). MIC has the option to convert the preferred shares to common shares at an additional P7.50/sh (P9.75bn). SGP last traded at P10.98/sh (total authorized and issued common shares at 5.266bn). SGP indirectly controls 60% of National Grid Corp’s (NGCP) voting capital stock and has an effective equity interest of 45.726%.
