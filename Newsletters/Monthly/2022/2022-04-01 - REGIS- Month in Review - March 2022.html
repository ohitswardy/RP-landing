REGIS REPORT

01 April 2022
 

The Philippines closed down 1.5% in March, as the Russia-Ukraine conflict took its toll on markets across the globe. As soaring commodity prices sparked selldowns during the month, the PSEi dropped below the 7000 index level. The PSEI saw its worst day since June 2020 in early March, with a 4.3% drop. The market had been down as much as 6.8% MTD to 6816.95 but losses narrowed, allowing a close above the 7200 level.  

The latest inflation print came in at 3.0% in February, flat vs January and falling below street forecasts.  With commodity prices soaring, and with the benefit of high base of food prices in 2021 wearing off, inflation is seen to head north beginning next month. Food makes up 37.8% of the CPI while fuel accounts for 6.7%.

Even before the dramatic spikes in commodity prices, the country’s trade deficit has been widening sharply, exerting pressure on the current account and the PHP.  The January figure has already ballooned to US$4.7bn, up sharply from US$2.9bn the previous year. The trade deficit swelled to $43.2bn in 2021, nearly double YoY, and equivalent to around 11% of GDP. Regis estimates the current account deficit can jump all the way to 5% of GDP or higher (vs 1% last year), if commodities stay where they are for the balance of the year.

Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Benjamin Diokno has said that an increase in policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% is  “reasonable” as part of an exit from pandemic era accommodative policy. The BSP cut policy rates from 4.0% to 2.0% following the outbreak of the pandemic.  Diokno says the normalization process will begin in 2H22 and 2.75% “might be reached by next year”. However, the increase in rates is likely to be less than what is expected in the developed world, placing further pressure on the PHP.

Despite challenges, the reopening theme remained. Effective March 1, the pandemic Alert classification for Metro Manila and 38 other areas dropped to Level One – the lowest restriction level in which nearly all businesses are allowed to operate at full capacity. Google Mobility has jumped sharply and most indicators are at or above pre-Covid levels.

President Duterte signed an Executive Order aimed at accelerating the economy’s recovery from the pandemic. Among other things, the EO opens up domestic and international tourism. Mobility should see a further bump from a separate government order to the BPOs to bring 100% of worker back to the office, or risk losing their tax incentives.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners turned sellers for US$205m in March, bringing YTD net foreign outflow to US$120m. Geopolitical tensions in Ukraine, inflation risks, and a FTSE rebalancing which saw the deletion of SM Investments added to the foreign selling pressure. Foreigners were more active than usual, accounting for 48%  (vs 35%-40% average).

SM Investments saw the largest outflows after being deleted from the FTSE index. Expected outflows were a lot larger (US$215m) than the actual outflows seen.

Other large cap names Ayala Corp., and Globe Telecom were also among top foreign sold in March.

Foreigners were two-way banks and property – as they were seen selling Ayala Land and Metrobank while buying SM Prime, BPI, and BDO.



Meanwhile, foreigners were buyers of ICTSI, with an earnings beat in FY21 and a positive outlook for 2022. Foreigners were also buyers of Semirara on the back of surging coal prices. Ayala Land REIT and Converge were also foreign bought after seeing being added to the FTSE small-cap and FTSE mid-cap indexes respectively.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The consumer saw some of the largest index declines in March as the threat of commodity price spikes on margins weighed, blunting the topline recovery story. Even before these dramatic spikes, margin vulnerability was already evident in 4Q21 results of consumer companies.

The surge in wheat and palm oil prices hit Monde’s (-17.1% MoM) margins in 4Q21, blunting the healthy topline on APAC BFB and leading to profit disappointment.  Meanwhile, Quorn reported a significant reduction in EBITDA on higher raw materials cost.  Universal Robina (+0.8% MoM) likewise saw margin contraction due to input cost inflation. Recurring income was likewise behind forecast despite a rebound in BCF sales (+5% YoY). San Miguel FB’s (-7.8% MoM) 4Q21 was hurt by the food segment as margins here were crimped by rising prices of commodities like wheat and soybean meal. The FY21 results D&L Industries (-0.2% MoM) told a similar story. Margins also fell on fast rising palm oil prices.  

Retailers also fell, despite a relatively more sanguine margin outlook.  Robinsons Retail (-10.0% MoM) corrected from its 9.4% gain in the previous month. Wilcon (-7.7% MoM) also dropped. Meanwhile, Puregold (+1.7% MoM), which had clocked declines for five consecutive months, saw some respite in March.

Emperador (-27.4% MoM) led the index decliners for the month. 

Property stocks dragged the index lower.  Large caps Ayala Land (-10.1% MoM) and SM Prime (-4.9% MoM) weighed on the market.  Vista Land (-14.7% MoM), Megaworld (-5.9% MoM), and Filinvest Land (-1.8% MoM) likewise fell, likely on concerns that higher steel prices would dent residential margins. Meanwhile, mall operator Robinsons Land (+6.7% MoM) jumped as Metro Manila was placed on quarantine Alert Level One. On the other hand, SM Prime, which had already risen 17% YTD as of end-Feb, succumbed to profit taking.

Most of the banks declined save for large cap BDO (+2.7% MoM).  BDO and Metrobank (-0.2% MoM) both slightly beat FY21 forecasts. Both saw profits boosted by a drop in provisions though BDO’s loans accelerated to 6.5% while MBT’s loans were still down 1.3% YoY.

BPI (-0.4% MoM) was down slightly.

Security Bank (-6.6% MoM) gave up some of its 8.9%  gain in the previous month.  The bank reported inline FY21 profits, with provisions down significantly and loan growth of 2.2% the first positive YoY print since 2Q20.

 

Macro proxies were a mixed bag in March.  Large cap SM Investments (+1.9% MoM) made up some of its -6.0% drop in March when its deletion from the FTSE Index was announced and was subsequently sold down. FY21 profits came in ahead of expectations as eased restrictions and improved personal mobility helped boost 4Q profits.

Ayala Corp (-3.1% MoM) meanwhile ended March with a decline as foreign outflows weighed. Ayala Corp made progress towards its goal of raising US$1bn (~P52bn) from divestments under its Strategic Agenda, with the company announcing that it was selling the Muntinlupa-Cavite Expressway to the Villar Group for P3.8bn. This deal would put AC at just under P30bn. AC is also looking to sell its 35% stake in Light Rail Manila Corp. (LRMC) as part of the realignment of its asset portfolio. 

AC’s FY21 income showed mixed performances from its units.  Ayala Land and BPI did better than expected while Globe and ACEN fell short of expectations. 

LT Group (-8.1% MoM) declined. LTG reported net income came in behind Regis estimates as 49.6% owned tobacco unit PMFTC did not raise prices in 4Q last year as it typically did in the past.  LTG has since raised prices (by around P20/pack for its high (Marlboro) and low-end (Jackpot) brands effective March 21). Meanwhile, PNB (-3.0% MoM) booked a P5.4bn profit in 4Q due to the reversal of loan loss provisions in the amount of P7.6bn. 

GT Capital (-4.5% MoM) retreated, given uninspiring YT-Feb auto sales numbers (-7% YoY) and a poor 4Q profit turnout for its auto business.  

Metro Pacific (-1.5% MoM) fell, despite a marked improvement in 4Q21 profit. Investors are likely spooked by the impact of elevated oil prices to the recovery of its tollroad and rail units.

Meanwhile, commodity price spikes have pushed DMCI’s (+6.5%) share price. The company posted record profits in 4Q, which would have much scope to go even higher this year, given its large exposure to thermal coal and nickel.  

Transport stocks outperformed. ICTSI (+6.7% MoM) jumped after reporting above-consensus FY21 results. The reopening story should continue to play out for ICT, with most of its ports located in areas heavily hit by the pandemic.

Meanwhile, investors seemed to have shrugged off Cebu Air’s (+4.7% MoM) poor 4Q results and the impact of elevated global oil prices, with the stock ending higher during the month. The airline’s recovery is well underway, with the company signaling its domestic capacity could already exceed pre-pandemic levels in the immediate term.

Converge (+16.2% MoM) was the top index gainer in March, surging on the back of a 114% jump in FY21 net profits as revenues grew 69% while EBITDA expanded 80%. The company is guiding for 50% growth in revenue this year.

PLDT (+2.8% MoM) also ended higher, with FY21 EBITDA coming in in line with expectations. PLDT revealed that it was selling half of its telco tower network at valuation “north of US$1bn” in a deal that appears value-accretive.

Globe (-1.1% MoM) did not fare as well.  Globe is increasing its authorized capital (common shares by 20m from 148.9m to 168.9m), signaling a likely equity issuance given where debt levels are and its near-record capex guidance (P89bn) for FY22. 

 

 

Sentiment for the power sector continued to be bolstered by the tight supply situation, leading to sustained elevated power prices.

Aboitiz Power (unch) was steady in March, with 4Q21 results coming in ahead of consensus.  The stock had already climbed 21.2% YTD as of February.  

Meralco (+1.5% MoM) ended higher, taking its YTD performance to +26.6%. Investors have been anticipating a decision on  Meralco’s tariffs for the “lapsed period” (i.e. from July 2015 to present), which would affect the level of refunds it may give to customers.  Meralco is seeking a decision from the ERC that does not result in a retroactive application of tariffs. This could result in a reversal of over P50bn in cumulative provisions. 

Core distribution operations of Meralco were within expectations, with volumes up 6% in FY21.

AC Energy (+2.7% MoM) likewise climbed, rebounding somewhat from a steep correction (-10.9%) in the previous month. 

First Gen (-5.4% MoM), meanwhile, declined on disappointing 4Q21 results as it had to resort to to buying expensive replacement power for San Gabriel and certain plants of EDC during the quarter.

The share price of the country’s largest coal miner, Semirara (+10.8% MoM) leapt, alongside strong moves in the prices of seaborne thermal coal. It also finally received its limestone permit, which will pave the way for their cement expansion, which in turn could add c. 15% to EBITDA on its first full year of operations.

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation steady in Feb as food prices ease. Headline inflation was steady at 3% in February, below street forecasts (3.2%) due to deceleration in food inflation to 1.2%, driven by slower inflation rates for meat and fish. Meanwhile, non-food inflation accelerated due largely to rising oil prices. The government will use available resources to provide targeted subsidies to affected sectors and to tame inflation in the next few months, given rising commodity prices amid the Ukraine-Russia conflict. It will continue efforts to increase food supply by helping farmers improve productivity and importing, when necessary, to fill supply gaps. BSP Governor Diokno said 2022 inflation rate could average 4.4%-4.7% in worst case. The BSP’s base case inflation forecast is 2-4% said it was prepared to deploy liquidity tools if needed.

BSP maintains key rates. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) maintained the interest rate for the overnight reverse repurchase facility at an all-time low of 2%. The BSP revised its 2022 inflation rate forecast to 4.3% from 3.7% previously. It also raised its 2023 inflation rate forecast to 3.6% from 3.3% previously. The BSP cited uncertainties in the crude market brought about by Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. Oil price forecast at US$102/bbl in 2022 (from US$83 previously) and US$88/bbl in 2023 (from US$76/bbl previously). BSP Governor Diokno said they are considering to cut the reserve requirement ratio for banks in 2H22. The next Monetary Board meeting is scheduled on May 19.

BSP Governor sees rise in policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% as “reasonable”.  Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Benjamin Diokno sees an increase of policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% to be “reasonable and consistent” with growth and inflation targets, as part of the exit from the pandemic era accommodation. The current policy rate is 2.0% and was cut from 4.0% before the pandemic. Diokno says the normalization process will begin in the second half of the year and 2.75% “might be reached by next year”. This is consistent with Regis expectations and with the Street’s. The rate hikes being considered by the BSP will almost certainly lag those of the Fed’s.

 

Remittances climb by 2.5% in January. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.5% YoY to US$2.67bn in January. Remittances were slightly above (+0.8%) January 2020 remittances of US$2.65bn. In PHP terms, remittances were up 9.3% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate broadly steady at +3.6% YoY. Meanwhile, land-based remittances jumped +2.9% YoY, on the back of strong remittances from Asia, USA, Saudi Arabia, and Europe. For 2022, the BSP is looking at remittances growing by +4% YoY to US$36bn.

Budget deficit widens to P23bn in January. The National Government’s budget deficit widened to P23bn in January on faster disbursements. Revenues rose 7%, driven by an 11% YoY jump in tax revenues but was tempered by a 23% contraction in non-tax revenues due to high base effects from dividend remittances from Transco and PAGCOR. Expenditures rose 9.7% due to higher national tax allocation releases and higher interest payments. The government is targeting a budget deficit of P1.65tn for 2022, equivalent to 7.7% of GDP. In 2021, the budget deficit stood at a record P1.67tn (8.6% of GDP).

Trade deficit widens YoY but dips MoM in January.The country’s trade deficit widened to US$4.7bn in January from US$2.9bn in the same period last year. However, it dipped from US$5.3bn in December 2021. Imports jumped 27% YoY, on the back of increases in fuel imports (+97%) and raw/intermediate goods imports (+30%). Petroleum crude oil imports rose 129% YoY while imports of wheat/corn/unmilled cereals rose 70%/879%/1022% YoY. Imports of consumer goods rose 11% YoY and were 1% above pre-pandemic levels. Exports rose 9% YoY, faster than in December 2021 (+7%) and January 2021 (-4%); It was also above pre-pandemic levels by c. 2%.

 

 

BOP posts US$157mn deficit in February; BSP revises 2022 BOP outlook. The country’s overall balance of payments position posted a deficit of US$157mn in February 2022. This is lower than the US$2.02bn deficit in Feb 2021 but larger than the US$102mn deficit in January 2022 and the govt cited national government foreign currency debt payments. This brings YT-Feb BOP to a cumulative deficit of US$259mn, lower than the US$2.77bn deficit in the same period last year. The BSP revised its 2022 BOP outlook.  They are now looking at a BOP deficit of US$4.3bn (from a mild surplus in its previous forecast), from a US$1.3bn surplus in 2021 due to a wider trade gap and current account deficit.

GIR level at US$107.98bn in February. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR) edged slightly higher in end-February to US$107.98bn. This is up from US$107.69bn in January and US$105.2bn in Feb 2021. The rise was largely due to the increase in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings after the rise in gold prices. The reserves are equivalent to 10.2 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and primary income. The BSP is projecting the GIR to rise to US$112bn by year-end.

Infra spend rises by 1.5% YoY in December. The national government spent P134.7bn for infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in December 2021, up by P2bn/1.5% YoY. The gov’t cited releases for the AFP Modernization Program and the construction of smart campuses in Marawi. The govt also attributed to high base effects from the timing of payments for DOTr ODA-funded rail projects in Dec 2020, which amounted to P12.5bn (vs. only P2.5bn in Dec 2021). For FY21, overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) jumped by 29% YoY to P1.124tn, equivalent to 5.8% of GDP. For FY22, the government is aiming to spend P1.27tn (+13% YoY) on infrastructure, or 5.9% of GDP.

 

Gov’t raises US$2.25bn via offshore bonds. The Philippine government raised US$2.25bn from its triple tranche US dollar-denominated bond offering. The US$1bn was from a 25-year green bond offer, priced at 4.2% or 50bps tighter than the initial guidance of 4.7%. Additionally, US$500mn was from 5-year bonds, priced at 90bps over treasuries or a coupon of 3.229% while US$750mn was from 10.5-year bonds, priced at 125bps over treasuries or a coupon of 3.556%. In 2021, the government raised (a) US$3bn from a dual tranche global bond offering in June; (b) EUR2.1bn triple tranche Global Bonds in April and (c) JPY 55bn Samurai bond offering in March. The government plans to raise a total of P2.2tn (US$42bn) this year, of which 75% will be sourced from the domestic market. As at end-January 2022, the government’s debt stock stood at P12.029tn, of which 70% is domestic and 30% is external.

Duterte signs law amending Public Service Act, raising foreign ownership limits on various sectors. Duterte signed the bill amending the Public Services Act into law (Republic Act# 11647), paving the way for the lifting of the 40% foreign ownership limits on a number of industries. Among the industries that are no longer considered “public utilities” : telecoms, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports. These are thus no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit. Those still considered public utilities are: distribution and transmission of electricity; petroleum pipeline transmission systems; water distribution systems; seaports and public utility vehicles. Foreign state-owned entities will not be allowed to own shares in public utilities or companies that own/operate “critical infrastructure”. Those who already own the shares are allowed to keep their shares but will not be allowed to add more. This will be a constraint on China Telecom taking over Dito Telecommunity fully.

 

 

 

House panel approves bill amending oil deregulation law. The House committee on energy approved the bill amending the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act. The bill seeks to require a minimum inventory level (of 30 days) and unbundle the cost of petroleum. Marikina City Representative Stella Luz Quimbo said that the retail price should not be based on the weekly changes in the Mean of Platts Singapore. Rep. Quimbo also proposed to add a provision in the bill giving the President the power to suspend or lower the excise tax rates on fuel products when the price of Dubai crude oil reaches US$80/barrel.

Duterte signs EO on economic recovery.President Rodrigo Duterte signed an executive order aimed at accelerating the economy’s recovery from the pandemic. Restrictions on the use of COVID-19 vaccines by the private sector will be reduced. Expansion of public transport capacity, and resumption of face-to-face learning. Streamline requirements for domestic travel, limiting these to vaccination cards or negative RT-PCR test results for unvaccinated individuals, and a QR code that can be scanned by various contact-tracing apps. Further loosen the requirements for international travel, such as providing quarantine exemptions for vaccinated foreign tourists. Passage of laws to accelerate digital transformation. 

Duterte nixes suspension of fuel excise tax; Looking at other options. President Duterte thumbed down a proposal to suspend excise taxes of fuel products. The government expects to collect P147bn in taxes from fuel products this year, of which P131bn is from excise taxes. The government is instead looking at direct subsidies equivalent to P200 per month to the bottom 50% of Filipino households or an estimated 74.7m Filipinos. The subsidy will amount to P33bn and will be sourced from VAT collections on oil imports. Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Karl Kendrick Chua is proposing a four-day workweek to cut the costs of businesses and workers. A similar four-day workweek was implemented in 2008 when fuel prices were also high. Workers will still work 40 hours per week, which will involve working 10 hours per day (vs. 8 hours) for 4 days. Labor Assistant Secretary Tutay also proposed a three-month wage subsidy worth P24bn, benefitting 1 million workers. If approved, the subsidy will be distributed from April to June. These proposals are in lieu of calls to increase minimum wage amid the rising costs of petroleum products. NEDA chief Chua estimates a P39 increase in the daily minimum wage will add 1ppt to inflation. Raising jeepney fares by P1.25 will add 0.4ppts to inflation. Labor groups are calling to raise the minimum wage to P1007 from P537 currently; There are also calls to raise jeepney fares by P6-7 from P9 currently. Ukraine-Russia war could shave off 0.4% to the government’s 2022 GDP target of +7-9%. 

 

NCR to remain under Level 1, more areas ease restrictions. The National Capital Region will remain under Alert Level 1 while 47 areas have also shifted to the lowest COVID-19 alert level until the end of March, Malacanang announced Tuesday. Areas not under Alert Level 1 shall be under Alert Level 2 beginning March 16 until March 31. Under Alert Level 1, most health restrictions have been removed. This will allow 100% capacity in government offices and public transport. Nationwide cases averaged 590/day from March 7 to 13, 35% lower than previous week.

Marcos Jr, Duterte keep leads in February 2022 Pulse Asia survey. The latest Pulse Asia survey showed Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos, Jr and running mate, Sara Duterte maintaining their leads. The tandem saw huge jumps in Metro Manila, with Marcos rising to 66% preference from 57% in January. They dominated in all subnational divisions (NCR, Balance Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao) and were also the top choice across all socio-economic classes. VP Leni Robredo was a distant second for the Presidential race, with a score of 15%, down from 16% in January. Survey conducted from Feb 18-23 and involved 2,400 registered voters.

BPO workers to return to offices beginning April 1. The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) rejected the request of the BPO industry to extend the current policy allowing 90% of workers (in PEZA zones) to work from home. Finance Secretary Dominguez said this was meant to be a temporary policy and would lapse on April 1. Thus, 90% of BPO employees (whose offices are in PEZA zones/buildings) must return to the office beginning April. This could soon lead to more BPO firms signing up for office space 

Phivolcs declares Alert Level 3 on Taal Volcano. The Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (Phivolcs) raised the alert status of Taal Volcano from Alert Level 2 (increasing unrest) to Alert Level 3 (magmatic unrest). Some nearby communities have been evacuated. Taal Volcano is about 60km south of Metro Manila.
