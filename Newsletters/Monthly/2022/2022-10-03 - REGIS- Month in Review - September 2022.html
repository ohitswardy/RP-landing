REGIS REPORT

03 October 2022
 

The Phils plunged 12.8%  in September, as Fed tightening and prospects of further hikes moved global and regional indices. The large drop in September took the PCOMP’s YTD decline to 19.4%. Losses were across the board, as funds flowed out.

Adding to the negative sentiment was the weakness of the PHP. The PHP dropped 4.25% in September, to close at PHP58.65/US$, with pressure coming not only from the spread between the Fed rate and the BSP’s policy rate but also the state of the country’s external accounts.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas raised policy rates by +50bp to 4.25%, taking it higher than it was right before the pandemic. However, the +50bp increase still lagged the +75bp hike done by the Fed at its FOMC meeting the day/hours before. The gap between the BSP policy rate (4.25%) and the Fed Funds Rate (3.00% to 3.25%) narrowed even further following the separate rate moves. The current interest rate differential is still much narrower than it has been historically. For perspective, the gap has averaged ~300bp over the past decade (with the BSP being higher than the Fed Fund Rate).

Apart from this pressure, the country’s trade deficit hit a new all-time high in July, ballooning 75% YoY to $5.9bn. The trade deficit for the first seven months has expanded 67% YoY to US$35.7bn. The worrying aspect was 4% YoY and 7% MoM contraction in exports for the month. The bright spot was that the July import growth slowed to +22% YoY, and was actually down 3% MoM. This was likely due to the drop in commodity prices, particularly oil/fuel.

Brent crude oil prices dropped to as low as US$84 a barrel, plunging highs reached in January. Regional prices of refined gasoline are have returned to December 2021 levels and regional diesel prices are near  pre-Ukraine war levels. Fuel (mostly refined oil) now accounts for c. 18% of the country’s import bill, and the oil price correction should ease pressure on the ballooning trade deficit. It should also temper food and transport prices, giving consumers and consumer companies alike some much-needed relief. 

Nonetheless, the trade data is expected to continue to put pressure on the PHP to depreciate against the USD.  Regis expects the Current Account deficit (CAD) for 2022E to hit 5% of GDP, levels not seen since the 1990s.

Aug CPI inflation came in at 6.3%, slightly dipping from the July figure of 6.4%.  The August print takes the average monthly inflation to 4.9% for the first eight months of this year. The slight dip was due to a moderation in YoY price increases of fuel and food. Despite the decline in headline CPI, core inflation actually rose to 4.6% (vs 3.9% in July), a reflection of demand side pressures.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreign selling continued on for a seventh consecutive month, with another US$216m exiting in September. This took YTD foreign outflows to US$1.224bn. September saw several blocks that skewed foreign flows such a cross in 8990 Holdings that clocked in US$198m in outflows and multiple San Miguel blocks that brought in US$100m. Foreigners would have been net sellers for US$118m excluding these. 

Biggest outflows were seen across big-cap banks, property, and conglos. Property names Ayala Land and SM Prime saw a combined US$57m in outflows. Meanwhile, BDO and Ayala Corp saw outflows of US$11m each. Meanwhile San Miguel saw inflows, mostly through crosses done at close. Consumer names Jollibee saw foreign buying for a 2nd consecutive month alongside Emperador.  

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Declines were all around in September, with all but 2 of 30 index members closing lower for the month.

The broad-based drop was led by well held large caps property names SM Prime (-20.5% MoM) and Ayala Land (-20.2% MoM). Both names accounted for 18% and 12% of the market’s total 843-index point drop for the month. Other property names fell as well, with Megaworld (-18.4% MoM) and Robinsons Land (-12.3% MoM) down substantially for the month. Apart from the inevitable rise in interest rates, the pressures on office demand depressed sentiment on the sector.

The recent spate of crimes linked to offshore gambling has led  several lawmakers and administration members to call for a reassessment on POGOs in the country. Among the proposals are an  outright ban on POGOs or transferring them to specific zones (likely meaning provincial areas).  Coupled with the government decision that effectively allows BPO firms to implement WFH policies indefinitely, a POGO exodus would likely cause Metro Manila office vacancy to rise even further.  Office vacancy as of 2Q22 was at 18%. Meanwhile, negative rental reversion will look increasingly likely; rental rates are largely 20-40% below 2019 levels.

Filinvest Land (-14.6% MoM) and Megaworld are the most exposed to office leasing, as this business comprises 40%-55% of their NAVs.

 

Among the top index decliners was Monde (-26.2% MoM), correcting from a 15.9% gain in the previous month due to an upweight during the PSEi rebal.  Other consumer names fell as well, including Puregold (-17.5% MoM), Century Pacific (-9.5% MoM), Robinsons Retail (-8.8% MoM), Universal Robina (-6.6% MoM), and Jollibee (-4.4% MoM).  Wilcon (+6.7% MoM) however managed a gain, with a strong surge at the close of the month.

ACEN (-25.3% MoM) reported a steep decline as well. ACEN added to its 10.4% MoM decline last month after UPC trimmed its stake in ACEN through an overnight secondary share placement. Investors are likely anticipating a weak 2H as planned outages should result in ACEN having to purchase expensive replacement power. 

Power generator Aboitiz Power (-1.6% MoM) fared relatively better, as sentiment for  generators has been kept buoyant by the tight supply situation and thus high power prices. Around 15% of AP’s capacity is exposed to spot.   Semirara (-5.1% MoM) also outperformed the market albeit correcting from a strong YTD surge of +92.5% as of July.

 

First Gen (-12.0% MoM) however continued to be weak, with investors discounting risk to its gas supply and a delay in its LNG terminal weighing on sentiment. Meralco (-12.7% MoM) also dropped, despite reported energy sales were up between 6-8% per month in 3Q, trending slightly higher than 1H with commercial volumes leading the way (+15-19%).

The temporary rate hike petitions that were jointly filed by Meralco and SMC Global Power units are now up for deliberation at the ERC. The petitions aim to amend their power supply agreement to raise power tariffs, allowing SMGP to adjust what were fixed prices in order to recover losses from the surge in fuel costs. SMC has issued notices of termination of their PSAs effective Oct 4 – if ERC denies its appeal for temporary relief. Meralco has apparently signed emergency supply agreements to replace the supply SMC is threatening to withhold. Either would mean an increase in power prices of at least 3% or more to the residential consumers.

Also pushing the index lower were macro proxy conglos.  SM (-12.8% MoM) pushed the index lower by 114 index points (14%).  JG Summit (-17.9% MoM) also dropped.

Ayala Corp (-12.3% MoM), which announced the official appointment of Cezar “Bong” Consing as its president and chief executive officer, fell alongside the rest of the conglos. Consing is officially taking over the post vacated by Fernando Zobel de Ayala. Consing was first appointed as interim chief on August 15 after Zobel de Ayala took a medical leave of absence.

The company also announced the completion of the transaction to acquire 60% of logistics company Air21 after it was announced a few months ago.  AC bought the stake for P6bn, which represents ~1% of NAV.

GT Capital (-13.8% MoM), Alliance Global (-17.8% MoM), MPI (-10.7% MoM) and LT Group (-6.6% MoM) also ended lower.

Aboitiz Equity’s (-4.5% MoM) EV’s infrastructure arm Aboitiz InfraCapital (AIC), Inc entered into an agreement with Megawide (-20.2% MoM) and GAIBV Airports International for AIC to acquire shares in the developer and operator of the Mactan Cebu International Airport (GMCAC).   AIC is purchasing 100% of GMCAC for an equity value of P25bn, to be paid in cash and in exchangeable notes.

 

Despite rising interest rates expected to benefit banks’ NIMs, risk aversion also took banks lower. BDO (-13.4% MoM) dropped as did Metrobank (-7.8% MoM) and BPI (-5.7% MoM).  BPI and Robinsons Bank (RBC), a bank currently controlled by the Gokongwei group, are in discussions on a potential collaboration. According to news reports, the merger will be done through a share swap, wherein BPI would emerge as the surviving entity and the Gokongwei Group would end up with a collective 6% stake in BPI. 

BPI is the third largest private bank in the country in terms of assets, with P2.35tn.  Robinsons Bank is roughly 7% the size of BPI and acquiring it would lift BPI slightly above Metrobank in terms of asset size. RBC is 60% owned by JG Summit, and 40% by Robinsons Retail.

Telcos fell though Globe (-1.6% MoM) fared relatively better.  Globe signed an agreement to sell its third tranche of towers numbering 1,350.  This batch is being sold to Phil-Towers, a consortium led by an Australian investment bank, for P20bn. In total, Globe will receive P91bn for all three tranches (total of 7,059 towers). This translates roughly to a compelling price of  P12.9m per tower, similar to PLDT’s own tower sale. The transaction will mean large cash inflows for Globe amounting to over P80bn, after tax, or  over P550/share.

Globe released details for its rights issue. From the initial plan of raising up to a maximum of P32bn, Globe will now be raising P17bn (US$300m) instead with a ratio of 1 share for every 13.2366 at an offer price of 1680/sh. Given the terms, Globe will be issuing a maximum of approximately 10.1m new shares, an increase in its share count by ~7.7%.

Globe’s e-wallet GCash is launching a stock trading feature on its app by next month, adding to its features and functionalities. GCash said that its gross transaction value surpassed P3trn in 1H22, nearly matching the P3.8trn it posted in FY21. The mobile wallet is expected to end 2022 with a P6trn gross transaction value. GCash said its user base has expanded to 66m.

Meanwhile, Converge (-24.7% MoM) dropped steeply, with selling exacerbated by talks of possible deletion from the MSCI in its next rebal.

PLDT (-10.2% MoM) dropped as well. The deal PLDT and ABS-CBN (-20.9 MoM) announced recently was called off, causing ABS to drop significantly.  The deal involved ABS-CBN selling its cable TV/broadband business Sky in exchange for 49.9% of TV5. No reason was provided officially but politicians and government officials involved in blocking the renewal of ABS-CBN’s franchise (license) opposed the deal. The deal was seen to make strategic sense for both parties.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation at 6.3% in August. Headline inflation was recorded at 6.3% in August. The median forecast: +6.4%, BSP: 5.9%-6.7%. The print is mildly moderating from 6.4% in July due to slower growth in food and transport prices. Food inflation slowed to 6.2% from 6.9% (due to meat, fish, vegetables). Operation of transport equipment grew by 35% YoY in August from 48% YoY in July (regional gasoline/diesel prices rose 28%/71% YoY in August from 57%/95% YoY in July). MoM, CPI rose by 0.4%, slowest since May due to drops in fuel and meat prices. The regional gasoline/diesel prices dropped by 17%/12% MoM in August. Meanwhile, core inflation (i.e. ex-energy, food) climbed to 4.6% in August from 3.9% in July, depicting underlying demand-side pressures. The YT-August inflation at 4.9%, above the BSP’s target band of 2-4%. The next monetary board meeting is on Sep 22; policy rate currently at 3.75% (+175bps from end-2021). The Fed meeting is set on Sep 21; US Fed funds rate at 2.25%-2.5% (+225bps from end-2021).

BSP hikes policy rate by 50bps. The Monetary Board increased the overnight borrowing rate by 50bps to 4.25% within street expectations (50bps). “Price pressures continue to broaden. Second-round effects continue to manifest, with inflation expectations remaining elevated in Sep due min wage hikes and transport fare hikes”. The BSP is targeting inflation to average 5.6% this year, up from previous target of 5.4%. The YT-August ave inflation rate at 4.9%, with latest print in August at +6.3%. Inflation may start to decelerate in 4Q22 and ease to within the BSP’s 2-4% target range by the second half of next year. It raised its inflation forecast for 2023 slightly to 4.1% (from 4%) but expects inflation to ease to 3% in 2024. The BSP has raised its policy rates by 225bps year-to-date (vs. Fed: +300bps).

Remittances rise to 7-month high in July. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.3% YoY, 5.9% MoM to US$2.92bn in July which is the highest level so far this year. The three-month average growth in remittances stood at +2.8% in July, slightly slower than in June (+3.4%). In PHP terms, remittances rose by 14% YoY in July. Land-based remittances rose by 6% MoM, driven by USA (+8%), Singapore (+27%), Japan (+8%), Saudi Arabia (+31%), and UAE (+15%). Remittances from Europe sustained softness, with UK down -30% MoM. Sea-based remittances also improved by 7% MoM. The YT-July remittances are up by 2.8% YoY to US$18.3bn. To reach the government’s FY22E target of +4% YoY, remittances would have to average US$2.88bn per month for the rest of the year.

 

Budget deficit narrows in July. The government recorded a budget deficit of P86.8bn in July, lower YoY/MoM due to faster growth in tax collections vis-à-vis expenses. Tax collections jumped 23% YoY/13% MoM to P281bn, exceeding 2019 monthly average by 20%. The government cited re-opening and digitized/modernized systems. The primary expenses rose by 8% YoY due to the release of PhilHealth subsidy and higher National Tax Allocation transfers. Interest payments declined YoY due to the timing of coupon payments for Global Bonds last year. The YT-July budget deficit at P761bn, trending slightly lower than the government’s full-year target due to higher revenues and lower expenses.

August BOP deficit at US$572m. The country’s balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$572mn in August, based on preliminary data. This is a reversal of the US$1bn BOP surplus in August 2021. However, August’s BOP deficit was the lowest since April 2022. The government cited outflows from the national government’s foreign currency debt payments and wider trade deficit. This brings 8M22 BOP deficit to US$5.5bn, higher than the US$253mn deficit in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the GIR dropped to a revised figure of US$97.4bn (vs. US$98.97bn based on preliminary data) in August. This is down from US$99.8bn in July 2022 and US$108.8bn in end-2021. The August GIR represents a more than adequate external liquidity buffer equivalent to 7.8 months’ worth of imports and is also is also about 7x the country’s short-term external debt.

Trade deficit wider in July, as exports slump.The country’s trade deficit widened to US$5.93bn in July. Higher than the revised trade deficit of US$5.87bn in June 2022 and US$3.51bn deficit in July 2021. Imports rose at a slower rate of 22% YoY in July (vs. 26% in June), likely largely reflecting sequential drops in commodity prices (e.g. oil). Imports of consumer goods remained strong, up 26% YoY. Meanwhile, capital goods imports still posting lackluster recovery, up by just 7% YoY while exports dropped by 4% YoY, with electronic exports down 8% YoY. The YT-July trade deficit is now at US$35.75bn, up 67% YoY. Additionally, the FY21 trade deficit revised down slightly to US$42.2bn (vs. US$43.1bn previously), equivalent to 11% of GDP.

 

GIR settles at US$98.98bn in August. The country’s gross international reserves stood at US$98.98bn in August. US$863mn (-0.9%) lower MoM and is down from US$109bn in end-2021. However, this is still above end-2019 GIR level of US$87.8bn (~7.7 import cover). The gov’t cited payments of the government’s foreign currency debt obligations and downward adjustments in BSP’s gold holdings due to lower gold prices. August GIR level represents 8.3 months’ worth of imports and 7.1x the country’s short-term external debt.

Gov’t raises P420bn from local-currency retail bond. The Philippine government raised P420bn from local-currency retail bonds. The bonds are due in Mar 2028 and pay a coupon of 5.75%. Of the total sold, P108.5bn pesos were switch subscriptions which were a record high. This also accounted for 17% of the government planned borrowings this year. As at end-July, the national government debt stood at P12.89tn, 0.8% up MoM and 9.9% higher than in end-Dec 2021. Domestic debt accounted for 68.5% while external debt accounted for 31.5%.

FDI net inflows slip to 13-month low in June. FDI net inflows dropped 52% YoY to US$471mn in June. This is the lowest monthly FDI net inflow since the US$455mn seen in May 2021. MoM, FDI net inflows dropped 36.5% from US$742mn in May and cited higher payments of debt instruments, offsetting net investments in equity capital and reinvestment earnings. The 1H22 net FDI inflows are up by 3.1% YoY to US$4.641bn. The BSP is targeting net FDI inflows to reach US$11bn this year, lower than the US$12.41bn in 2021. The government is projecting the balance of payments to be in a US$6.3bn deficit by year-end (1.5% of GDP). Due to a projected wider current account deficit of US$19.1bn (4.6% of GDP) vs. net financial account inflow of only US$11.8bn.

Loan growth steady at 12% YoY in July. U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) stood at 12% YoY in July, unchanged from June’s +12%. MoM loans rose by 0.2%. Loan growth to manufacturing and real estate sectors slowed but was offset by faster growth in consumer loans. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector jumped by 9.0% in June. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under Regis coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity growth (M3) decelerated slightly to 7.0% in July (from 7.2% in June).

Bicam approves SIM card registration bill. The bicameral conference committee gave its nod to the subscriber identity module (SIM) registration bill. The bicameral’s approval happened on the same day the Senate passed its own bill on its third and final reading. The Bill seeks to eradicate crimes aided by SIM, such as scam text messages, by requiring end-users to submit their personal information. The Lower House’s version approved fines ranging from P5,000 to P1mn on telcos, their authorized SIM card sellers, and even government workers who fail to comply with the law. Both the Senate and Lower House’s versions removed the social media provision. Recall that former President Duterte vetoed the bill in April citing the inclusion of a provision that required social media accounts to be registered. The Bill will likely make headline subscriber count lower and ARPUs higher; It could also pave the way for accelerated adoption of credit processes via mobile.

 

Brownouts hit Luzon as 7 power plants shut down. Power interruptions were experienced in Nueva Ecija, Aurora, Pampanga and Zambales after the National Grid of the Philippines (NGCP) placed the Luzon grid under red alert on Monday afternoon. Heightening the yellow alert status declared in the morning. The yellow alert is issued when the excess power is insufficient to meet the transmission grid’s regulating and contingency requirements. Meanwhile, red alert is placed under a grid area when the electricity supply is insufficient to meet demand. As of 10am Monday, the Luzon grid’s available capacity stood at 10,727MW while peak demand was 10,585MW. At least 3,627MW were lost from the grid after seven power plants went on forced outage. These include units of GN Power, Calaca, Masinloc, Sual, Quezon Power and Avion. WESM has been averaging >P9/kWh in recent days.

POGO shut down after rescue of 43 Chinese nationals. Authorities rescued 43 Chinese nationals who claim they were abducted and forced to work for Lucky South 99 Outsourcing, a licensed POGO company. Some lawmakers and members of the Marcos administration have recently called for the abolition of POGOs due to their relation to crime. 

Senator files bill to ban online gambling. In light of the spate of news linking kidnapping cases to online gambling firms, Senate Majority Leader Joel Villanueva filed Senate Bill 1281 or the anti-online gambling act. The bill seeks to prohibit online gambling and the placing of wagers or bets online. PAGCOR said that there are only 26 active POGO firms in the country. At the end of 2019, there were 60 licensed POGO firms and occupied about 1.4m sqm of Metro Manila office space but they have vacated about 650,000sqm. POGO firms took up about 20,000sqm of office space in 2Q22. Metro Manila office vacancy stood at 18% as of end-2Q22. POGO firms still occupy c. 5% of Metro Manila office stock. The Entertainment City casinos were able to secure online casino permits from PAGCOR during the pandemic to help augment lost revenue from mobility restrictions.  

 

Congressman proposes to limit POGO operations within specific zones. Albay Representative Joey Salceda, who is the chairman of the House Committee on Appropriations, has opposed the call to ban POGOs, saying that it will only create an underground sector. Instead, he proposes to limit POGO operations within POGO-specific zones. The new licensees will be restricted to these zones while existing licensees will be allowed to finish their current leases. Regis believes that these POGO-specific zones will likely be in provincial areas. Thus, this proposal would still lead to higher office (and residential) vacancy in Metro Manila. 

DOH wants additional taxes on junk food, sweetened beverages. The Department of Health (DOH) is pushing for additional taxes on junk food and sweetened beverages to address obesity and boost revenues for the universal healthcare program. The DOH has yet to provide a specific proposal on taxes but Vergeire said the additional revenue will be used to finance the Universal Healthcare which enrolls all Filipino citizens under the National Health Insurance Program. The TRAIN Law mandates a P6/liter excise tax on beverages using caloric and non-caloric sweeteners and a P12/liter tax on beverages using high fructose corn syrup.

LTFRB approves fare hikes across public transportation. The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) has approved fare hikes for several modes of transportation, effective by October 4. This is the first blanket fare increase implemented this year. Prior to this, the government only allowed fare increases for jeepneys (implemented in June). The approved fare increases were lower than petitions. Transport services account for 4.5% of the CPI basket.
