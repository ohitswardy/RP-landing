REGIS REPORT

01 June 2022
 

The Philippines managed a +0.65% MoM advance in May, breaking a three-month losing streak. The market moved with global sentiment influenced by a slew of factors including global inflation, Fed tightening, geopolitical risk, COVID lockdowns in China, and China-US tensions. On the ground, the main preoccupation was the presidential elections held May 9.

Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. has been proclaimed president-elect, having won a landslide victory as the first outright majority President under the 1987 Constitution. Sara Duterte has been proclaimed vice president-elect. Both will assume office on June 30.

Subsequently, investor attention was focused on the appointments to key cabinet positions, as a means to help lift the uncertainty surrounding the new government’s economic program. Economic policy is key, especially at a time when the country’s fiscal position has been impacted by the pandemic and amidst global macro headwinds. Perhaps eliciting sighs of relief from investors, President-elect Marcos has dialed back on his campaign promises of reducing rice prices to P20/kg and suspending fuel excise taxes.

While the list of Cabinet appointments remains incomplete, the nominations thus far are largely credible and qualified. The incoming Finance Secretary will be BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. Felipe Medalla, former NEDA Director General under the Estrada administration, will be appointed Governor of the BSP to take over Diokno’s current post. Arsenio Balisacan will serve as the head of NEDA, a post he also held under the Aquino government. All three are career economists and have extensive government experience and suggest continuity of current fiscal and monetary policy. Alfredo Pascual, currently the head of the Management Association of the Philippines, will be appointed Trade secretary. Rounding up the economic cluster is Bangko Sentral (BSP) Assistant Governor Amenah Pangandaman, who will be Budget Secretary.

Meanwhile, the recovery story is intact. The 1Q GDP growth print came in at +8.3%. This comes from a 3.8% contraction the prior year and +7.8% expansion in 4Q. 1Q22 economic output has already surpassed 1Q19’s (pre-pandemic). Strong recovery was evident across all major sectors such as consumption and capital formation which grew 10% and 20%, respectively. Recovery is evident as well in 1Q22 corporate results and bottom-up indicators including car sales, electricity demand, domestic air travel, and mall foot traffic.

On the back of strong GDP numbers, it came as no surprise that the BSP hiked policy rates by 25bps to 2.25%, though the hike came a little earlier than anticipated. Diokno has practically guided for another hike in policy rates in June.

Higher inflation continues to loom. CPI inflation climbed to 4.9% in April, above Street expectations. The April print is a three-year high and is also higher than the BSP’s target band and the 4.0% in March. Wages are also headed north, providing further upward pressure. Eleven out of the 17 regions in the country have approved wage hikes, with increase so far in the double digits.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners were sellers for a third consecutive month, with US$350m exiting in May. Foreign outflows were bloated by multiple Converge deals after they did an overnight placement of 288.7m shares and sold another 260mn shares to Converge itself (buy-back program) – resulting in US$164mn in outflows. If we exclude the Converge deals, foreigners would have been net sellers for US$186m. 

MSCI rebalancing was also expected to skew foreign flows with US$42m net outflows anticipated – but pre-positioning trades countered the expected outflows, resulting in only US$4.4m in outflows. But despite the marginal net foreign flows on rebalancing day itself,  MSCI related trades dictated the direction of the flows for most of the month.

Year to date, net foreign outflow stands at US$569m. Foreign participation increased to 52.3% from 45% in April.

Biggest outflows were spread across big-caps Ayala Land, Ayala Corp. which saw down-weights on MSCI. Converge was surprisingly top foreign sold despite being added into the MSCI where an expected US$54mn was expected to flow through.

Usually illiquid index heavyweights JG Summit and Aboitiz Equity Ventures were also top foreign sold as passive funds were seen selling.   

Meanwhile, biggest inflows were seen in Monde Nissin and SM Prime after seeing an upweight in MSCI.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The PSEi inched up 0.65% MoM in may with the index constituents decidedly mixed. Index losers outnumbered the gainers 16 to 13.

Monde (+14.0% MoM) was the top index gainer May, with moves related to index changes. Monde rose on the back of  increased weightings in the PSEi effective mid-month and the MSCI at month end.

Universal Robina (+5.4% MoM) likewise gained in May, partially making up for some of it is 15.3% MoM drop in March.

The recovery story was reflected in the consumer companies results. Food manufacturers Monde, URC, and Century Pacific (-2.2% MoM) saw healthy revenue growth albeit results were impacted by input cost inflation.  URC’s outperformance was largely driven by strong revenue growth across the board (+22% YoY), while revenues from CNPF’s branded segments (+17% YoY) drove the top line. Monde’s volumes for key categories (e.g. instant noodles, biscuits) were healthy, particularly for biscuits, which has recovered to pre-pandemic levels.

Restaurants Jollibee (-2.6% MoM) and Shakey’s Pizza (-6.4% MoM) likewise saw strong revenue jumps in 1Q. Jollibee’s revenues jumped 24% YoY.  SSSG (consolidated) of +16.5% was driven mostly by the Philippine operations (+22.9%) and CBTL (+23.3%). PIZZA’s 1Q22 net profit surged 2.7x to P76m from the near-breakeven level in 1Q21. Revenues rose 26% YoY to P1.6bn (mid teens ex consolidation of Potato Corner).

Meanwhile, Robinsons Retail  (-0.6% MoM) saw most of its formats recover to pre-pandemic levels.  Puregold’s (unch MoM) net sales were flattish (+2% YoY) during the quarter as SSS levels continued to normalize from the high base.

 

 

Sector wise, banks led the index with banks seen to have more cushion amidst a rising interest rate environment (ie higher NIMs). Loans growth is also picking up.

Metrobank (+7.6% MoM),  BPI (+5.3% MoM),  BDO (+3.4% MoM) carried the index higher.

Metrobank delivered a beat in 1Q as provisions were lower than expected.  Metrobank’s loan book grew 5.2% YoY, the first positive YoY print since the start of the pandemic in 1Q20. 

Security Bank (-10.4% MoM), however, was the PSEI’s biggest loser in May.  Speculation that the stock would be removed from the PSE Index in the next rebal impacted the stock’s performance.  Meanwhile, its 1Q earnings beat came on the back of a sharp drop in provisions (to just P80m in the quarter), though NPL cover fell to 90%.

Also falling markedly was Converge (-8.0% MoM).  Private equity firm Warburg Pincus placed 3.8% (289m shares) of its remaining 10.2% stake in Converge to public investors.  It also simultaneously sold 3.4% (260m shares) to the company itself. The residual 3.0% will be “distributed” to “certain parties as part of existing arrangements” within 30 days. The series of transactions allows Converge to comply with the 30% minimum public float mandated by June 2022 under its franchise. 

Meanwhile, telcos PLDT (+1.8% MoM) and Globe (+7.0% MoM) beat expectations in 1Q22. Globe’s surge in May reflects renewed excited for its fintech unit. Mynt drove Globe’s 1Q22 outperformance, swinging to profitability from a loss the prior year.  On operations, broadband fell short but wireless performed better.  For PLDT, broadband did well but mobile was soft. Losses at Voyager climbed further ahead of the launch of its digital bank.  

 

 

Property meanwhile was mixed.  Sector heavyweights SM Prime (+5.4% MoM) and Ayala Land (-7.6% MoM) moved in opposite directions on the back of MSCI weight adjustments ( upweight for SMPH and down weight for Ayala Land). 

Economic reopening was also apparent for the property sector.  SM Prime’s Philippine mall revenue recovered 40%, and residential sales take-up is trending well above 2019 levels. The residential segment however was dampened by cancellations in 1Q.  Earnings disappointments came from Ayala Land as cancellations dampened residential revenue. Robinsons Land (+1.5% MoM) likewise saw sales cancellations muting residential revenue.

There was some confusion surrounding Megaworld (-3.5% MoM) in May in what appeared to be a closure threat  from the tax bureau.  The Secretary of Finance however has since directed the BIR to suspend until further notice all audit-related activities of three task forces, including the one for real estate developers.  Megaworld insists that it has no outstanding tax liabilities. 

Semirara (+20.0% MoM) surged. The company reported an exceptional P15bn in profits in 1Q22, up 552% YoY, almost approximating its full year profit last year, as the rise in global thermal coal prices lifted profits. Speculation of It’s inclusion in the PSEi on the next rebal also spurred interest in the stock.

Meanwhile, both AC Energy (-1.0% MoM) and First Gen’s (-17.6% MoM) 1Q numbers were negatively impacted by the need to purchase expensive replacement power during the period.  Meralco (+5.9% MoM) meanwhile outperformed.

 

Bloomberry’s (+4.5% MoM ) 1Q22 profit marked  a return to profitability after seven quarters of pandemic-related losses.

Bloomberry disclosed a deal with subsidiaries of PH Resorts Group (PHR PM) covering a proposed investment into Emerald Bay Resort Casino in Cebu and The Base Resort Casino in Clark, Pampanga. Bloomberry also disclosed that it was acquiring roughly 280 hectares of beachfront land about 60km from Manila for P7.55bn ($144m) to develop into an integrated resort with mixed-use development. The recent announcements would push BLOOM’s portfolio to five properties in the Philippines. 

Most of the holdcos ended in the red in May. SM Investments’ (+1.5% MoM) managed a gain however. Net income for 1Q expanded 27%, already surpassing pre-pandemic level (1Q19). 

Alliance Global (-9.5% MoM) dropped markedly, with an earnings miss in 1Q.  Metro Pacific (-3.2% MoM) also declined. In line with the recovery theme, among the key highlights from MPI’s results were volumes for water and tollroads already matched pre-pandemic levels, despite another lockdown in January.

Meantime, the government regulator approved a chunky (+22%) tariff increase for the Subic-Clark-Tarlac Expressway (SCTEX) effective June 1. Toll fees for the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX) and Cavitex were also approved during the month.

Ayala Corp (-2.4% MoM) declined, with a down weight in the MSCI rebal putting pressure on the share price.  The company reported a P7.8bn profit (+45% YoY) for 1Q looks which were within expectations, note it was lifted by exceptional gains at Globe. Without those extraordinary items, income would have been P5.8bn (below Street expectations) as contributions from property and power fell short.

 
 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

GDP expands by 8.3% in 1Q22 on strong domestic demand recovery. GDP grew +8.3% YoY in 1Q22, reversing the 3.8% decline in 1Q21 and was faster than 4Q21 (+7.8%). This was above consensus expectations (+6.8%) but broadly in line with Regis expectations (+8.5%). It also surpassed pre-pandemic GDP level. Mobility improved after the government eased restrictions in February. Growth was broad-based as most sectors rebounded strongly, except for agriculture (due to swine flu). On the demand side, consumption and capital formation grew robustly at +10%/ 20%, respectively, reversing last year’s decline. Gov’t spending slowed, with public construction contracting by 5% YoY due to the election ban and the Mandanas ruling. Negative net exports expanded +32%, reversing last year’s 4.8% drop. The government is maintaining its +7-9% GDP growth target for the year. Resumption of face-to-face classes is still missing. Regis is forecast 2022 GDP growth of +6%.

Inflation surges to 3-year high in April. Inflation surged to 4.9% in April, the highest level recorded since December 2018 and higher than in March 2022. This is higher than the BSP’s target range for the year (2-4%) and near the upper bound of its 4.2%-5% forecast range for the month and also higher than street estimates (median: +4.6%). Prices of food jumped by 3.8%, faster than the previous month while electricity, gas, fuel inflation increased further by 20% from 17% in March. Transport inflation also picked up to 13% from 10% the previous month. This brings 4M22 average inflation rate to 3.7%. BSP Governor Diokno previously signaled the Monetary Board could raise the policy rates in June. The decision will depend on 1Q22 GDP growth data (if GDP grows by 6-7%, it may consider a rate hike in June). The government will release 1Q22 GDP figures on May 12 (street estimate: +6%). Regis is forecasting key policy rates to rise to 2.5% by year-end from 2% currently.

BSP hikes policy rates by 25bps. The Monetary Board raised the benchmark rate by 25bps to 2.25%, within street expectations. The BSP cited elevated inflation pressures could persist over the horizon. The BSP is projecting inflation to average 4.6% this year and settle down to 3.9% in 2023. Inflation averaged 3.7% in 4M22, with the latest print in April at 4.9%. The balance of risks to the inflation outlook now leans toward the upside for both 2022 and 2023. The BSP will have its next policy review on June 23.

 

BSP chief hints at another rate hike. The Monetary Board is likely to pursue a gradual tightening of the country’s monetary policy stance next month according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. The BSP raised policy rates by 25bps to 2.25% last May 19 and the next meeting is set on June 23.

National debt soars to record P12.68tn in March. The outstanding debt of the Philippines soared to P12.68tn in March. This is higher than in end-2021 (P11.7tn) and pre-pandemic (P7.7tn). Assuming the government’s nominal GDP target for FY22 (P21.5tn), the national debt is equivalent to 58.8% of GDP. Around 69.9% of debt is domestic while 30% is foreign. By maturity, 5% is short term, 29% is medium term, and 66% is long term.

OFW remittances rise 3.2% in March. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.2% YoY to US$2.59bn in March. In PHP terms, remittances jumped 11% YoY. The 3-month moving average growth rate was at 2.3% in March. Land-based remittances jumped 3.7% YoY while sea-based remittances rose 1.3% YoY. This brings 1Q22 remittances to US$7.77bn, up 2.3% YoY. This is tracking behind the BSP’s FY22E target of +4% YoY.

End-April 2022 GIR settles at US$106.76bn. The country’s gross international reserves (GIR), based on preliminary data, settled at US$106.76bn in end-April. This is down from end-March level of US$107.31bn. This also reflects the government’s payments of foreign currency debt and the decrease in the price of gold. This is equivalent to 9.4 months’ worth of imports. The BSP projects the GIR to reach US$108bn by the end of 2022.

Trade deficit expands further in March. Preliminary data showed the trade deficit widened further to US$5bn in March. This is up from a revised US$4.2bn deficit in Feb 2022 and US$2.8bn deficit in March 2021. The trade deficit was due to a steady growth in imports (+28% YoY) and a deceleration of export growth (+6% YoY from +16% YoY in Feb 2022). This brought 1Q22 trade deficit to US$13.89bn.

 

 

Balance of payments reverses to a deficit in April. The country recorded a US$415mn balance of payments (BOP) deficit in April. This is reversing from the US$754mn surplus in March 2022 and US$2.6bn surplus in April 2021 and was due to the national government’s payments of foreign currency debt. Additionally, this brings 4M22 BOP surplus to US$79mn, a reversal from the US$231mn deficit in the same period last year.

Rates for 10-yr bonds rise to 6.89%. Treasury rates for 10-year bonds jumped to almost 7% yesterday after the BoT issued all of the P35bn in reissued 10-year Treasury bonds with a remaining term of 9 years and 8 months. The auction marked the first time the Treasury made a full award since the May 9 elections. The 10-year T-bonds fetched a rate of 6.894%, up by 58.1bps from the last time they were offered in April. Bids for the securities ranged from a low of 6.625% to 6.95%. The yield also exceeded by 48.7bps the market pricing of 6.407% for the tenor. Last week, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas delivered its first rate hike, pushing the benchmark rate to 2.25% from a record low of 2.0%.

Infra spend drops 3% in 1Q22. Infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 14% YoY to P100bn in March. This reverses the 16% YoY drop in 2M22. The government cited payments of completed projects of Dept. of Public Works, Dept. of National Defense, and Education. This brings 1Q22 infra spend down 3% YoY to P190bn. Overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in 1Q22 amounted to P253bn, up 4% YoY. The government is targeting to spend P1.27tn on infra this year (+13% YoY).

DoF urges next government to impose new taxes. Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III said in a press conference that the government could cover existing debt by borrowing more ore reducing spending by P249bn each year

Instead, he said the government has to raise revenues, improve tax administration and cut unnecessary spending. The DoF proposed three tax reform packages for 2023-2025.

Some of the items were:

  • Thee-year deferment of reduction of personal income tax under TRAIN, which will have an estimated average revenue impact of P97.7bn.
  • Expansion of VAT base and possible reduction in VAT rate.
  • Intensified implementation of income tax on social media influencers.
  • Mandatory casino admission charge at P3,500 and 5% tax on gross gaming revenues of electronic betting machines, which is expected to contribute P13.1bn annually.
  • Health tax reform that involves increasing taxes on cigarettes, e-cigarettes, alcopops and sweetened beverages estimated to bring in P91.4bn a year.
  • Increasing petroleum tax by P1/liter for a minimum of three years.
  • Excise tax on domestic coal and imported coal.
  • Tax on cryptocurrencies.
  • Tax on carbon emissions.
  • In total, the projected revenue impact of all packages combined is seen to be an average of P349bn a year between 2023 and 2027.

DBCC lowers growth target for 2022; Proposed 2023 budget at P5.268tn (+5% YoY). The Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC) revised its macroeconomic/fiscal program to account for domestic and external developments. It lowered the upper-end of its 2022 GDP growth target to +8% from +9% previously. It raised its 2022E inflation rate target range but kept 2023E/2024E. The fiscal deficit targets for 2023/2024 were also tweaked. Changes reflect 1Q22 GDP (+8.3%) and heightened external risks. The revised estimates assume 2022E Dubai crude oil prices of US$90-110 per barrel (from US$68-70 per barrel previously) and 2023E Dubai oil price of US$80-100 per barrel. The DBCC said next year’s proposed national budget is now at P5.268tn, up 5% from last year’s approved budget of P5.024tn. The 2023 budget accounts for 23% of GDP.

 

Wage hikes approved in Metro Manila, Western Visayas.The wage boards of Metro Manila and Western Visayas have approved an increase in minimum wage for workers in various sectors in the regions. For Metro Manila, workers are granted a P33 (+6%) wage hike to P570. Wages were previously hiked in November 2018 (+5%). For Western Visayas (Region 6), the minimum wage has been increased by P55-110 (14-35%) to P420-450. Wages were previously hiked in Jan 2020 (+13%).

Government approves wage hikes in 3 more regions. The wage boards of Region I, II, XIII issued orders to raise minimum wages in their areas: Region I will increase wages by 18-32%, Region II will increase wages by 14-16%, and Region XIII will increase wages by 15%.

Meanwhile, recently approved wage hikes in NCR and Western Visayas shall take effect on June 4 and 5, respectively. There were 10 wage hike petitions filed with six regional wage boards involving the NCR, Regions 3, 4A, 6, 7, and 8.

Duterte stops e-sabong operations. President Duterte said Monday he will order the termination of online cockfighting operations in the Philippines, citing the impact on families and values. Duterte gave Interior Secretary Ano until May 3 to finalize the order to terminate e-sabong operations. PAGCOR earlier estimated revenues from online cockfighting averaged P400m monthly last year and P640mn monthly since January 2022.
