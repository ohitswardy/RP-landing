REGIS REPORT

01 August 2022
 

The Philippines climbed 2.6% in July, recovering somewhat from a bloody 9.1% plunge in June. The market moved despite a lack of domestic catalysts, as investors across the globe digested rising interest rates, recession worries, and softening commodities.

Volumes thinned to anemic levels, averaging only US$90m daily in July. Excluding a placement in Meralco, volumes reached only US$79.5m daily average, with some days  in the month logging the lowest levels (~US$54m) of the year.

The BSP’s off-cycle 75-bp policy rate hike was surprising in both timing and magnitude.  The hike was higher than the latest hints of new BSP Governor Felipe Medalla and came in between policy meetings.  The move was in line with aggressive policy rate hikes seen in other countries such as Canada, South Korea, and Singapore and came in the wake of repeated criticisms of the BSP being behind the curve. The BSP cited “spillovers from other countries” that could have second-round effects on inflation, which may refer to local prices of imported commodities priced in USD. The policy rate at 3.25 is still below the 4.0% level in end-2019, immediately before the pandemic. There was no mention of PHP weakness connected to the rate hike though some believe it was partly aimed at the local currency’s rapid slide.

The CPI print rose to 6.1% in June, with food prices the primary reason for the increase.  The CPI figure does not seem to have peaked yet, with the impact of transport fare increases only expected to be evident in the coming months.  Nonetheless, with global commodity prices rolling over, domestic inflation may likely peak in late 3Q22, barring another spike in commodity prices.  The 10-year sovereign bond yield is already reflecting this, coming off its peak in June. 

Recovery in domestic demand seems to be sustained, amidst rising inflation.  Latest industry data trends and early corporate results show demand closing in on or surpassing pre-pandemic levels. Data from the CAMPI/TMA showed vehicle sales for the month of June  came in up +27% YoY and +9% MoM. Metro Pacific says traffic at its toll road network  in June was running at 5-6% above the average levels in 2019. Meralco’s electricity volumes rose 5.8% YoY for 2Q and 1H. Meanwhile, BPI’s loans surged 14.4% YoY as of 2Q.

President Marcos delivered his first State of the Nation Address (SONA). Among the specific policy issues taken up in his speech were  his support for the continuation of tax reform measures of the previous government,  emphasis on agriculture, the promotion of natural gas and examination of nuclear power to address the country’s energy needs, and amendments to two landmark pieces of legislation, the Power Reform Act (“EPIRA”) and the BOT Law. Marcos also promised not to impose any more lockdowns.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners were net sellers for a fifth straight month, with US$80m exiting in July. Foreign selling remained heavy despite average turnover falling by another 22% MoM to US$76.4m.   

Outflows actually would have been higher had it not been for a Meralco placement where JG Summit sold 3.2% of their stake in an overnight placement – the deal alone brought in US$47mn in net inflows. If the Meralco deal were excluded, foreigners would have been sellers for US$ 127m.
 
Foreign participation was at 41% from 44% in June.

Biggest inflows were again seen in coal miner Semirara amid elevated coal prices. In addition, there has been long speculation  of a re-entry into the PSEi.

Inflows were also seen in MONDE after foreign bargain hunters stepped in earlier this month after the stock fell more than 10% in two days on the back of food safety concerns – which the company was quick to address.

Heavy outflows were seen in most big-cap property Ayala Land and SM Prime, and banks Metrobank, BDO, and BPI as large shareholders continue to lighten their Philippine exposure.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Index gainers overtook the losers 17:2 in July, as the market labored to make back some it’s the previous month’s losses (-9.1%  in June).

Macro proxy conglos helped to push the index higher, with Aboitiz Equity (+20.0% WoW) leading the pack. Recent laggard Ayala Corp (+3.9% MoM) also helped prop up the index. Alliance Global (+12.4% MoM), LT Group (+8.6% MoM), and Metro Pacific (+4.5% MoM) and JG Summit (+5.75% MoM) likewise gained.

JG Summit sold 36m shares of Meralco (-5.6% MoM) in an overnight placement at P344/sh (6% discount to Jul 28 close). Of the total shares sold, Metro Pacific took 22.54m shares (~2% of Meralco), increasing its indirect and direct stake in Meralco to 47.5%. JG Summit still holds around 297m shares in Meralco post the transaction.

Meanwhile, SM Investments (-0.8% MoM) was flat, GT Capital (-7.3% MoM) lagged despite car unit Toyota’s 1H22 sales tracking ahead of management’s 2022 target (162k, +25% YoY).

 

Banks helped prop up the index with Bank of the Philippine Islands (+9.7% MoM) and BDO (+8.1% MoM) both pitching in around 32-33 index points each to the PSEi’s overall 160-index point gain for the month. BPI surged, after a strong 2Q showing in which loans surged 14.4%, NIMs widened 15bps YoY and NPLs dropped below 2%.

The sector was rattled on a news report that a consortium led by BDO initiated foreclosure proceedings against a subsidiary of Udenna Corp, the holding company of Davao-based businessman Dennis Uy. The news report claimed that the subsidiary, Global Gateway Development Corp (GGDC), was behind in about $4m lease payments to Clark International Airport Corp (CIAC). Udenna has since made the P274m lease payment, allowing it to avoid to avoid a default. The same news report claimed estimates of between P70bn and 110bn in loans for the group. This estimate of total loans (ex-DITO), is that’s just about 1% of total banking sector loans (P11.1trn ex-interbank as of May 2022).

 

 

Some names in the consumer space surged this month. Wilcon (+16.2% MoM) surged after reporting a 49% jump in 1H22 profits, underpinned by strong margin expansion. Margins have been expanding sequentially since 1Q21 despite supply chain pressures. Monde (+9.4% MoM) likewise gained, as commodities softened and as bargain hunters started to pick up the stock after a weak June when it fell 12.2%.  The stock quickly bounced back after news of Taiwan, along with several other EU countries ordering a recall of several batches of MONDE’s flagship brand Lucky Me! instant noodles. The recall came after traces of ethylene oxide (a commonly used pesticide) were detected in the products. MONDE maintained that its products are safe to consume and comply with local and US FDA standards for ethylene oxide. Branded exports account for ~35% (50% of APAC BFB sales).

The rest of the sector, such as Universal Robina (unch) and Jollibee (-1.2% MoM) saw consolidation in July, after earlier rising on softening commodities.

 

The interest rate sensitive property sector mostly underperformed, with the rising rate environment adding to existing concerns on a slower than expected recovery in the residential segment as well as the rising office vacancies. Ayala Land (-1.0% MoM) and Robinsons Land (-1.4% MoM) ended the month down. SM Prime (+0.1% MoM) was flat, though Megaworld (+4.6% MoM) managed a gain.

Telcos, however, were the biggest drag to the index. Converge (-10.2% MoM) emerged as the month’s  top index decliner. Not far behind was Globe (-9.1% MoM) which declined almost just as much. The two stocks have posted monthly declines six of the seven months this year, with investors pricing in the normalization of demand post pandemic as well as intensifying competition as shown in lower priced entry levels plans and higher speed plans coming up as of late. PLDT (-1.4% MoM) fared better though ended the month down as well.

 

 

Utilities were mixed. Meralco received a long awaited decision relating to its distribution tariffs for the “lapsed period” (July 2015 to June 2022) where no proper tariff review was conducted. The regulator affixed a tariff of P1.3522/kwh for Meralco for the lapsed period, lower than the P1.38/kwh previously approved. The regulator issued an order directing Meralco to refund P21.8bn to consumers.

The refund order is much smaller than what Meralco has provisioned for. Regis estimates Meralco would have P50bn in “excess” provisions that it can reverse. The after-tax impact of a P50bn provision reversal would be ~P38bn, equivalent to 10% of Meralco’s market capitalization. Meanwhile, a decision on its rate review for the 5th Regulatory Period points to significant earnings uplift should rates be kept at P1.35/kwh.

The distribution utility delivered expectation-beating 2Q/1H results (core income +15% to P13.1bn). Electricity volumes rose 5.8% YoY for the quarter and for the half year. The profit outperformance was largely due to its 58%-owned Singapore subsidiary Pacific Light, which reported exceptionally good numbers.

 

Meanwhile, AC Energy (+4.1% MoM) is selling out of its last coal-fired plant, the 244MW facility held under SLTEC, with a low valuation of P3.7bn. Despite the value-dilutive deal (with SLTEC estimate to have cost ACEN close to P17bn for 100% equity), ACEN’s exit of SLTEC should open it up to a section of ESG mandates that have avoided investing in coal assets.

Aboitiz Power (+10.7% MoM) surged after it reported a strong bounce for its 2Q profits (59% YoY, +111% QoQ), post disappointing 1Q results, thanks to fresh contributions from the newly available capacity of GN Power Dinginin (GNPD). Semirara (+18.6% MoM) surged on speculation that it would be in added in the next PSEi rebalancing exercise.

ICTSI (+5.5% MoM) was another big gainer for the month, after dropping 15.2% in the prior month. ICTSI is acquiring another port in Indonesia, lifting its portfolio to 36 across the globe. The transaction involves a price of US$46.5m,with a size of 5m MT annually (bulk).

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation soars to four-year high in June at 6.1%. Headline inflation rate accelerated to 6.1% in June from 5.4% in May. This is broadly within street expectations (6%) and the BSP’s forecast range (5.7%-6.5%) and  the highest recorded inflation data since October 2018. The MoM, inflation jumped 0.9% biggest jump since January 2022. The inflation was driven by food, particularly rice, meat, fish, fruits, vegetables, and sugar. Prices of electricity and fuel used for transport services also rose by 18% and 54%, respectively. The average inflation for 1H22 stood at 4.4%. The gov’t plans to continue and to expand the fuel subsidy for the transport sector to include tricycles.

Remittances rise by 2% YoY in May, +11% in PHP terms. The BSP reported that cash remittances were up 1.8% YoY, 1.2% MoM in May to US$2.43bn. In PHP terms, remittances rose 11% YoY, 2% MoM. The 3-month moving average growth rate accelerates slightly to +3% from +2.8% in April. Land-based remittances drove remittances higher YoY, MoM, particularly from USA and Saudi Arabia. The growth of sea-based remittances was steady at 1.3% YoY. Cumulative 5M22 remittances up 2.5% YoY to US$12.6bn. Tracking behind the government’s year-end target of +4% (c. US$32.7bn).

Budget deficit widens in June. The government’s budget deficit widened YoY, MoM to P216bn in June. Tax revenues jumped 17% YoY but dropped 12% MoM, owing to strong tax collections from the Bureau of Customs; BIR collections lagged. Non-tax revenues were lifted by higher dividend remittance and interest income from NG deposits. Expenditures rose 28% YoY, 12% MoM due to higher capital outlay disbursements for public works and transport projects, as well as the release of P10bn coco levy fund. This brings 1H22 deficit to P674bn, 6% YoY lower 6. While revenue collections are in line with the government’s target, expenditures are tracking behind due to slower-than-expected capex during the election ban and the timing of release of LGU funds to July. The government is targeting deficit of P1.65tn in FY22, equivalent to 7.6% of GDP (vs. 2021 deficit of P1.67tn, 8.6% of GDP).

GIR drops to US$102bn in June. The country’s gross international reserves dropped by US$1.7bn (1.6%) MoM to US$102bn. This is down from the peak of US$110bn in December 2020. The gov’t cited national government’s foreign currency debt payments and decrease in value of gold holdings. The GIR is equivalent to 8.5 months’ worth of imports of goods.

Trade deficit widens to US$5.7bn in May. The country’s trade deficit widened to US$5.68bn in May, based on preliminary data. This is higher than in US$5.35bn in April 2022 (revised up from prelim figure of US$4.77bn) and also higher than in May 2021 (US$3.18bn). This is the largest monthly trade deficit figure. Imports jumped 31% YoY to US$12bn, led by fuel (+129% YoY). Note that regional prices of refined oil products were up sharply in May, with gasoline up 74% YoY and diesel up 122% YoY. Imports of raw materials and consumer goods rose 24% YoY and 7% YoY. The capital goods imports also jumped by 22% YoY. Exports rose 6% YoY, 3% MoM to US$6.3bn. The cumulative 5M22 trade deficit now at US$24.9bn, up 70% YoY.

Balance of payments records US$1.57bn deficit in June. The country’s balance of payments position stood at a US$1.57bn deficit in June. This is slightly lower than the US$1.6bn deficit in May 2022 but wider than the US$312mn deficit in June 2022. The Govt cited payments of government’s foreign debt and wider trade deficit. This brings 1H22 BOP deficit to US$3.1bn, wider than the US$1.9bn deficit in 1H2. The BSP expects a BOP deficit of US$6.3bn in 2022 (1.5% of GDP).

 

Bad loan ratio falls to 16-month low in May. Gross NPL ratio for the banking sector stood at 3.75% in May. This is the lowest since 3.72% in January 2021 and down from 3.93% in April. Bad loans dropped by 4% MoM and 11% YoY to P429bn in May. The gross loan portfolio expanded by 7% YoY and 0.4% MoM to P11.44tn. NPL coverage ratio improved to 94.76% from 79.96% a year ago. The past due loans fell by 14% YoY to P508.5bn, bringing the ratio down to 4.44% from 5.56% a year ago. Meanwhile, restructured loans rose by 27.78% YoY to P337bn, accounting for 2.94% of banks’ loan portfolio in May. Loan loss reserves were higher at P407bn from P383bn a year ago, bringing the ratio down to 3.55% vs. 3.59% a year ago.

Gov’t eyes 100% resumption of face-to-face classes in November. President Marcos Jr. announced that his government is targeting to fully implement in-person classes nationwide by November. Gov’t will start phased schooling by September.

Infrastructure spending up by 2% YoY, 26% MoM in May.The government spent P80.5bn on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in May, up 2% YoY, 26% MoM and cited payments for railway projects (Malolos-Clark Railway project and Metro Manila Subway project). This brings YT-May spending up 0.6% YoY to P334bn. Overall infrastructure spending (incl. LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 2% YoY to P103bn in May. This brings YT-May overall infra spend to P435bn, up 3% YoY. This is tracking behind the government’s target of P1.2tn (+7% YoY) this year.

Government debt dips in May. The government’s debt stock decreased by P267bn (-2%) MoM to P12.5tn in May and cited settlement of P300bn in provisional advances from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas. Compared with end-2021, the national government’s debt was P768bn (+6%) higher. Of the P12.5tn debt stock, 69% were domestic borrowings while 31% were sourced externally. The government expects its debt-to-GDP ratio will peak in 2023 at 67% before falling to 66% in 2026. Former Finance Secretary Dominguez previously said that the country’s debt-to-GDP ratio may not return to pre-pandemic levels (40%) for at least 10 years. To reach this ratio by 2031, a median annual primary balance (revenues minus non-interest expenditures) increase of 2.42% of GDP would be needed. Based on the most optimistic scenario that assumes a GDP growth rate of 7% and a real interest rate of 2%. 

Lotilla nominated DOE Secretary. President Bongbong Marcos has nominated Raphael Lotilla as next energy secretary according to Press Sec Trixie Cruz Angeles. Angeles however stressed that is right now a nomination pending clarification of his employment status. Lotilla is currently an independent director of Aboitiz Power and Enexor. Lotilla was energy secretary of GMA from 2005-2007. He was also previously president of PSALM and deputy director general of NEDA.

Marcos Jr. nominates Toni Yulo-Loyzaga as environment chief.  President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has nominated Ma. Antonia “Toni” Yulo-Loyzaga as secretary of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR). Ms. Loyzaga is a former executive director of Manila Observatory & current president of National Resilience Council. Her work largely focuses on disaster science and risk management. She also served as technical adviser of the Philippine Disaster Resilience Foundation and was a part of the Senior Advisory Board of the Armed Forces of the Philippines’ Command and General Staff College. President Marcos has yet to announce his selections for Dept. of Health, Science and Tech, and Human Settlements and Urban Dev’t.

 

Marcos Jr's first SONA - key highlights. President Marcos Jr. began his speech discussing his solutions for the country’s macroeconomic challenges

    • Reiterated medium-term fiscal objectives of his economic team:
      • GDP growth rate of 6.5%-7.5% in 2022 and 6.5%-8.0% from 2023-2028
      • Bring down poverty rate by 2028
      • Lower debt-to-GDP ratio to below 60% by 2025
      • Attain upper-middle income status by 2024
      • Lower deficit-to-GDP ratio to 3% by 2028
    • Implement sound fiscal management while supporting economic recovery through CREATE law, Public Service Act, the Foreign Investments Act
    • Impose VAT on digital services. This is estimated to generate an initial revenue of P11.7bn in 2023 alone
    • Promote ICT in tax collection to streamline processes
  • On the agriculture sector, which he currently heads, he vowed to raise production by providing financial and technical assistance to those in agriculture sector (i.e. farmers, fisherfolks)
    • Continue agrarian reform program
    • He plans to issue an executive order that will impose a 1-year moratorium for the payment of land amortization and interest payments
    • He urged Congress to pass a law (amendment to section 26 of RA6657) that will emancipate program beneficiaries from the agrarian reform debt burden. This will amount to P58bn, benefiting 654k agrarian reform beneficiaries and involving 1.18mn hectares of awarded land
    • Infuse new blood in the agriculture sector; Provide technical skills to farmers
  • On the education sector, he pushed for the return of full face-to-face classes in November
    • Wants to review the medium of instruction in schools and maintain the advantage of Filipinos as an “English-speaking people”
    • Provide education materials and supplies to schools that are necessary for this day and age, such as internet connectivity, devices and educational tools
  • On the tourism sector, he vowed to provide basic developments such as road improvements
    • He wants to upgrade airports and build more international airports to held decongest the bottleneck in the Manila airport

 

  • On infrastructure, he wants to keep the momentum and expand planned infrastructure projects by encouraging PPPs
    • His administration is committed to finishing ongoing rail projects: North-South Commuter railway, Metro Manila Subway, LRT-1 Cavite railway extension, MRT-7
    • Improve roads and transport systems in key cities through Davao High Priority Bus system, Ilocos Norte Transpo Hub, El Nido Transport Terminal
  • On energy, President Marcos acknowledged that demand for energy is outstripping supply; expansion of existing power sources limited
    • Emphasized need to build more supply, particularly from renewable energy sources long term
    • In the interim, his government will provide investment incentives by clarifying the uncertain policy in upstream gas, particularly the area close to Malampaya
    • They will also re-examine strategy towards building nuclear power plants, specifically small scale modular nuclear plants
    • PPPs will play a part to support funding for power projects
  • On Covid, he vowed to no longer impose lockdowns.  Instead, his administration will review the current alert level system to adapt to the current environment
  • Pushed Congress to pass 19 legislations, including the following:
    • Budget Modernization Bill – institutionalize cash-based budgeting system to ensure budgets will actually lead to delivery of programs/projects
    • Tax package 3 or valuation reform bill – establishment of real property valuation standards and database of real property transactions
    • Gov’t Financial Institutions Unified Initiatives to Distressed Enterprises for Economic Recovery (GUIDE) – provide financial assistance to distressed enterprises/MSMEs
    • Passive income and financial intermediary taxation act (PIFITA) – Will reform taxation of capital income and financial services
    • Enactment of an enabling law for the natural gas industry – development of mid-stream natural gas for energy security; promote the role of natural gas as a complimentary fuel to variable renewable energy
    • Establishment of Department of Water Resources – To have a national water management, policymaking and planning
    • Amendments to EPIRA – improve implementation of law’s provisions and address effectiveness to address high cost of electricity; restructure ERC to foster accountability
    • Amendments to BOT Law - implementation of PPP and direct desired outputs with targets; address ambiguity in existing law, bottlenecks/challenges, foster more competitive environment for PPPs
