REGIS REPORT

02 February 2022
 

The Philippines had a good start to the year, ending an otherwise choppy first month of 2022 up 3.4%. The market had a volatile month, against a backdrop of Omicron, lockdown threats, and as the world anticipated Fed tapering. Volumes were on the thin side (averaging US$119m daily) with market conviction lacking. Index gainers and losers were split practically down the middle.

In the wake of an exponential rise in COVID cases post the December holidays, the government tightened restrictions – raising the lockdown level to Alert Level 3 at the capital (and to other areas subsequently) from Jan 3. This effectively limited capacity for establishments to 30% (from 50% under Level Two). As cases peaked (to over 35025 daily 7 day ma) then subsided to (15,856 new daily cases 7 day ma) at month end, the Alert level for NCR and seven other provinces was lowered back to Level 2 effective 1 February. 

Rules for inbound travelers have also been relaxed, with fully vaccinated individuals allowed entry into the country without them having to undergo facility-based quarantine, therefore paving the way for the gradual normalization of travel.

The pace of vaccinations though have much room to gather pace, with the latest stat at just 443k jabs a day.  The country has fully vaccinated 54% of the population and the beginning of vaccination for children ages 5-11 in February should allow this proportion to jump in the following weeks.

The country’s 4Q21 GDP grew 7.7% as private consumption accelerated. FY21 GDP growth stood at 5.6%, beating consensus (5.1%) and government (5.0%-5.5%) forecasts. The government remains optimistic GDP growth could range +7 to +9%, higher than Regis (and consensus) expectations of +6.5%.

The country’s trade deficit ballooned, with FY2021 at an all-time high $43bn, almost double YoY. This came as imports surged 30% YoY on economic recovery and higher import prices. If the deficit holds even at the 4Q21 average, the 2022 full year trade deficit could reach US$55bn. The deteriorating trade deficit is the principal reason for Regis’ forecast of a 4% depreciation in the peso this year (to PHP53.0:US$).

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners started the year off as sellers for US$ 56.3m, as the surge in COVID cases earlier this month coupled with concerns over a US Fed lift-off prompted investors to continue to take profit on select names. January proved to be a quiet month in terms of block events, but saw busier than usual corporate buy-back programs active in the market.

Biggest outflows were seen in Universal Robina and Ayala Land. Coincidentally, Universal Robina and Ayala Land were the most aggressive in buying back shares this month. Universal Robina disclosed they bought US$5m worth of stock in January while Ayala Land bought US$10.5m. Corporates are required by the exchange to disclose buy-back transactions on the same day – this may have prompted some foreign investors to make use of this liquidity to lighten their positions.

Big outflows were also seen in last year’s best performers ICTSI and Globe as investors too profit amidst shaky market conditions.

 

Meanwhile, foreigners were seen buyers in banks BDO and Metrobank. Monde Nissin also saw decent inflows ahead of an expected inclusion into the PSEi this February.  Foreign participation fell to 35% (from 45% in December) --- primarily due to increased local retail participation amid multiple small/mid-cap listings this month.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
DECEMBER LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The PSEi’s 30 members were split almost evenly in January, with 15 gainers against 14 losers. Power names and financials outperformed, while consumer and property underperformed.

Aboitiz Power (+18.9% MoM) was the top index gainer for the month. Buying interest in the stock has been sustained by expectations of elevated power prices in the coming years, owing to tight power supply. No significant baseload plant has broken ground in Luzon since the 2x668MW GN Power Dinginin (GNPD). AP is in best position to benefit from elevated power prices among the generators; as much as 40% of AP’s power sales are expected to be repriced over the next two years.  

Meralco (+13.5% MoM) surged, on expectations of a decision on tariffs for the lapsed regulatory period (from July 2015) coming imminently, thus removing an overhang on the stock. A tariff reduction retroactively applied to July 2015 is not likely to impact Meralco negatively as it has already provisioned as if a tariff cut was implemented in 2015. Should there be no retroactive calculation for its rates, Meralco would keep the P1.38/kwh it has been charging and could see a large windfall (US$1bn, pretax) from a reversal of provisions.

On the other hand, green energy play AC Energy (-13.3% MoM) dropped, as the increasing interest rate outlook dampened sentiment for high growth names globally. 

Meanwhile, Semirara (+17.3% MoM) rose sharply, lifted by Indonesian government’s halting of  coal exports for the entire month of January – a move seen to bolster demand and support coal prices regionally.

Holdcos gained, led by index name JG Summit (+17.9% MoM). Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+154.7% MoM) likewise surged,  pulled up by power unit AP’s strong outperformance as well as its banking arm Union Bank (+1.1% MoM) which rose 73% in 2021. Alliance Global (+8.5% MoM) and GT Capital (+5.6% MoM) also gained.  Its 51%-owned auto unit Toyota Motor Phils sold 129,667 units in 2021 – hitting its 30% YoY growth target this year (130k unit).  TMP raised prices for a select number of vehicles in January, after selective price increases implemented in November as well. This should help ease the margin pressure arising from the weakness of the PHP.

Incidentally, GTCAP’s property arm Federal Land also revealed a joint venture with Nomura Real Estate. The latter will invest US$324m for a 34% stake in the joint venture, while GTCAP will infuse an undisclosed amount of cash and property assets. The venture will initially develop ~250 hectares of land in Metro Manila, Cavite and Cebu.

Large caps Ayala Corp (+4.7% MoM) and SM Investments (+0.6% MoM) rose.

 

Banks outperformed in January, as the sector is seems to have seen the worst in terms of asset quality, pointing to normalization in credit costs. Loan growth has also  started to recover. BDO (+11.9% MoM) led the gains, followed by BPI (+6.5% MoM) and Metrobank (+5.75% MoM).  Security Bank (-9.9% MoM) however declined.

The consumer sector was weak, with retailers bearing the brunt in January. Robinsons Retail (-13.8% MoM) declined, with concerns on discretionary retail amidst the Omicron surge.  Sentiment was weighed down further by speculation of a PSEi removal in the upcoming February rebal. RRHI disclosed its purchase of  the 40% stake of Robinson Convenience Stores held by Ministop (Japan) after the latter announced it is exiting the Philippine market.   RRHI intends to rebrand its convenience store business.  Meanwhile, RRHI's e-commerce arm Growsari raised US$45m from KKR.  Growsari operates an online B2B platform that allows  mom-and-pop shops to purchase goods from it at lower prices.

Retailers Puregold (-4.4% MoM) and Wilcon (-1.6% MoM) ended the month down as well.

URC (-0.8% MoM) was soft. 

Meanwhile, Monde (+1.1% MoM) managed a gain. The announcement by Beyond Meat that it will supply its meatless chicken alternative to KFC in the US dampened sentiment for  Monde’s alternative meat business Quorn earlier in the month, as investors have been hopeful for the latter to secure a similar deal.

Century Pacific (+1.1% MoM) was up slightly despite concerns on rising input costs. 

 

Property was mostly down. Robinsons Land (-4.7% MoM) dropped, as did Megaworld (-1.9% MoM).

Ayala Land (-2.2% MoM) announced that it will be acquiring certain assets from AC valued at P17.3bn. ALI will be paying for the assets with new shares to be issued to AC at a price of P55.80/share – a premium of over 50% from ALI’s latest price.  The swap streamlines the ownership of the assets, placing them within ALI and simplifying the corporate structure. AC’s stake in ALI will rise to 47.2% (from 46.1%) as a result of this transaction.

SM Prime (+3.5%) however managed a bounce from its 9.4% decline in the previous month.

Recent land parcel sales by DM Wenceslao (+1.0% MoM) in the  Manila Bay area at roughly P425,000/sqm-P450,000 suggest that upside risk to SMPH’s NAV estimate, with assigned prices in the area (including reclamation projects) lower than recent transaction values.  Some 31% of SM Prime's NAV is comprised of reclamation projects while the total Manila Bay area account for 35%. 

Telcos were mixed. Globe (-6.4% MoM) corrected after seven straight months of gains. The stock was up 64% in 2021. Converge (-3.9% MoM) likewise corrected after more than doubling last year.   PLDT (+1.2% MoM) was steady.

DITO (+0.6% MoM), the controlling shareholder (54%) of third player Dito Telecommunity (Dito Tel), announced it was “deferring” its P8bn rights issue days after the offer period already lapsed. Management blamed the “less than ideal market conditions” for the deferment. This is despite the fact the deal supposedly had a “firm” underwriting commitment from its sole underwriter China Bank (-2.1% MoM) and an undertaking from its main shareholder to buy all shares left unsubscribed in the rights issue.  While China Telecom could easily fund more equity at the operating company level,  they are hampered by the current law that limits them to 40% max.  The current challenges faced by DITO in raising capital are positive for incumbents TEL and GLO as the threat of a new player has served as overhang to the sector.

Reopening play Cebu Air (+6.8% MoM) gained as the Omciron threat tapered during the month. Loosened travel restrictions the government has placed are also seen to boost air travel.  

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

GDP expands 7.7% in 4Q21 / 5.6% in FY21. The country’s GDP expanded by +7.7% in 4Q21 and +3.1% QoQ on a seasonally adjusted basis beating analyst expectations of +6.3%. By expenditure, private consumption accelerated to +7.5%, as the government shifted to an Alert Level system with granular lockdowns. Both industry and services sector sustained improvements. For FY21, GDP expanded by +5.6%, beating consensus estimates (+5.1%) and came in above the government’s target range of +5.0-5.5% a reversal from the 9.6% contraction in 2020. In nominal terms, 2021 GDP stood at P19.39tn, just a shade below 2019 (P19.52tn). For FY22, the government remains optimistic GDP growth could range +7 to +9%; Consensus/Regis at +6.5%.

Inflation eases to 3.6% in December. The country’s inflation rate eased further to 3.6% in December from 4.2% in November, due to decelerating food prices (3.1% from 3.9%), specifically rice, vegetables, and fish.  Transport inflation also slowed to 6.1% from 8.8% on lower crude oil prices. This brings 2021 average inflation to 4.5%, within the government’s recently upgraded target range of 4.3%-4.5%. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno, however, expects inflation to accelerate over the near-term due to supply disruptions and agricultural damages from Typhoon Odette. The BSP expects inflation to ease close to its midpoint target range of 2-4% in 2022 and 2023.

Remittances up 5% in November. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 5.1% YoY to US$2.5bn in November. By region, remittances from the Middle East, Asia, USA remained robust while sea-based remittances were up 1% YoY while in PHP terms, November remittances were up +9.7% YoY. This bring the 3-month moving average growth rate steady at +4.2%. The YT-November remittances are up 5.3% YoY to US$28.4bn, below the BSP’s forecast (+6% YoY). For 2022, the BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +4% YoY (vs. 2010-2020 CAGR of +5%).

 

BOP posts US$991mn surplus in December.The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$991mn in December 2021. This is a reversal from the US$123mn BOP deficit in November 2021. This is likely due to inflows from JERA’s purchase of a 25% stake in Aboitiz Power (AP PM). Additionally, this brings 2021 BOP surplus at US$1.345bn, lower than the US$16bn surplus is 2020.

Trade deficit balloons in 2021; worrying monthly trend. The country’s trade deficit ballooned, with FY2021 at an all-time high $43.22bn, almost double YoY. This came as imports surged 30.4% YoY, far outpacing export growth of 15%. The import explosion was due to a combination of a) economic recovery, ie demand for imported goods, and b) higher import prices. The monthly picture looks even more worrisome, with the deficit worsening almost every month last year. 

Timing of unwinding of monetary policy remains uncertain. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said that the timing of the BSP’s exit from the current loose monetary policy will depend on economic outcomes rather than calendar dates. He said that COVID-19 continues to pose downside risk to both growth and inflation. The BSP’s strategy includes: returning to normal operations, unwinding liquidity provision, reducing monetary accommodation, and preparing for the next crisis.

 

 

Customs 2021 collection exceeds pre-pandemic levels. Based on preliminary data, the Bureau of Customs’ (BOC) revenue collection monitoring group showed end-2021 collections reached P645.8bn (+20% YoY). This is exceeding the agency’s year-end target of P616.8bn and was c. 2% better than 2019 collections of P630bn. The government cited 13 of 17 ports overshot its 2021 targets, including ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal. In December alone, the BOC collected P62.5bn, up 38% YoY and the highest level of collections for the year. This is also a significant improvement over its 2019 monthly average collection of P53bn. For 2022, the agency is confident to meet its collection target of P671bn (+4% YoY), as it expects imports to rise by +10% YoY.

Moody’s downgrades PH GDP growth rate to 5.6% due to Omicron. Moody’s Analytics expects the Philippine economy to grow by 5.6% this year, below Regis and consensus median forecast of +6.5%. This is also lower than government’s target range of +7-9%.  This is lower than its previous forecast of +6.4% released in October 2021. Moody’s cited the Omicron-driven surge in COVID cases and the return of movement restrictions. 

Unemployment rate falls in November, but job quality worsens. The unemployment rate fell to 6.5% in November from 7.4% the previous month. This is also the lowest jobless rate since the pandemic started and was due to more relaxed mobility restrictions. However, the quality of available jobs (ie. underemployment rate) remained high at 16.7%, highest in four months. Around 7.6mn employed are looking for additional work or longer working hours.


DoE solicits green energy bids for 2,000MW. The Department of Energy (DoE) said it is inviting companies to supply 2,000MW of renewable energy (RE) in its first round of its Green Energy Auction Program (GEAP). In the Notice of Auction, the Doe Said the power will be supplied to Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao at the following volumes: 1,400MW, 400MW and 200MW, respectively. For Luzon bidders, the total capacity for auction is 900MW of solar, 360MW of wind, 80MW hydro and 60MW biomass. For Visayas, the breakdown is 120MW biomass, 260MW solar and 20MW wind. For Mindanao, the targets are 100MW solar, 50MW biomass and 50MW hydro. The GEAP aims to boost RE as a source of energy, with RE generators participating in an auction for their output. The GEAP features the Green Energy Tariff, which influences the commercial value of power generated from qualified RE facilities and sets a benchmark price for DUs under an opt-in mechanism. DU can procure power from the GEAP pool of winning bidders to reduce Feed-in Tariff-All charges to the end-users.

COVID vaccination of children ages 5 to 11 to begin on Feb 4. The country is scheduled to receive 2.6mn doses of the pediatric formulation of Pfizer’s COVID-19 vaccine next week. Vaccination in Metro Manila will begin on February 4. The program will be rolled out nationwide in the middle of February. The DOH estimates that there are 13.5mn Filipinos aged 5 to 11. Around 53% of the Philippine population (58.1mn Filipinos) has been fully vaccinated against COVID. As of January 8, 78% of the Metro Manila population (10.5mn people) had been fully vaccinated. The Philippines has received a total of 216mn COVID vaccine doses and has administered 125mn doses (as of January 26). The government is targeting to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22 and 90mn Filipinos by end-2Q22.

 

Metro Manila, 7 other provinces to be under Alert Level 2 starting Feb. 1. Metro Manila and 7 other provinces will be placed under Alert Level 2 from Feb 1 to 15. The provinces include Batanes, Bulacan, Cavite, Rizal, Biliran, Southern Leyte, and Basilan. 

Gov’t scraps facility quarantine for fully vaccinated inbound international travelers. The Philippines is suspending the COVID-19 risk classification for incoming international travelers beginning Feb 1. The removal of the red, yellow, green list classification means there will be no distinction regardless if a traveler arrived from a country that is high, moderate, or low risk for COVID-19 transmission. 

There will instead be different requirements for arriving passengers based on their vaccination status; All travelers are required to present a negative RT-PCR test done within 48 hours before departure. Fully vaccinated travelers are no longer required to undergo facility-based quarantine. They only have to monitor themselves for symptoms at home for 7 days.  Unvaccinated and partially vaccinated travelers have to undergo facility-based quarantine until they get a negative test result taken on the 5th day, then complete their 14-day quarantine at home. Outbound travelers, regardless of vaccination status, must present a negative RT-PCR test taken within 48 hours before their departure

The country will once again accept fully vaccinated foreign tourists from visa-free countries starting Feb 10.
