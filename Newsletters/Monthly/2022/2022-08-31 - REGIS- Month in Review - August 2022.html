REGIS REPORT

31 August 2022
 

The PSEi climbed 4.2% MoM in August to close at 6583.65.  The gain in August whittled down the PSEI’s YTD losses to 7.6%, from as much as down 14.85% YTD as of June 23.  A generally strong 2Q earnings season boosted market sentiment, while a streak of foreign inflow helped boost share prices.

Overall 2Q results for the market reflected the strength of economic reopening. Aggregate profits for 2Q22 surged 53% YoY for Regis-covered stocks and total NPAT already surpassed 2Q19 levels. Some 60% of companies exceeded Regis and Street expectations. The beats were delivered by power, consumer,  banking sectors, as economic reopening boosted the topline.  Profit and/or revenues shot to or ahead of pre-COVID levels, even in the face of inflation. Meanwhile, property lagged, as cancellations of previous sales weighed on revenues.

GDP expanded 7.4% YoY in 2Q22, down from the 8.2% pace in 1Q22. Personal consumption was at +8.6% in 2Q, decelerating from 10.0% in 1Q and down 2.1% QoQ. The deceleration seemed to be at odds with the 2Q22 corporate results story as well as high frequency industry data such as loan growth, and electricity sales.  As expected, Net Exports dragged while coming in as a surprise was the “Statistical Discrepancy” line which also weighed on growth. 

While revenge spending could fade, early indicators point to continued momentum in 3Q, undoubtedly to be helped by schools finally reopening to face-to-face classes in August.  Easing global commodity prices should also bode well for a boost in consumer demand. 

The BSP hiked its policy rate by 50bp during its Aug 18 meeting, coming as no surprise following the solid GDP performance  and CPI (6.4% in July) prints. The hike was in line with consensus estimates.  Nonetheless, the 10-year BVAL is already down over 100bps from the June high, indicating lower inflation expectations.  The current 3.25% policy rate is still 75bps away from the 4.0% just before the pandemic hit.

Top-down concerns over the twin fiscal and current account deficits remain.  The PHP depreciated another 1.8% MoM in August to close at 56.155 – inching closer to its all-time low of PHP56.46/US$ back in 2004.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Headline foreign outflows continued on for a 6th month, with US$152m exiting in August. This brought YTD foreign outflows to US$1bn. While the headline outflows look heavy, the Phils saw the longest streak of foreign buying since February – with five straight sessions of inflows adding up to US$57m.  Had it not been for a US$210m Ayala Land block in which a large foreign holder sold to Ayala Corp., the month of August would have actually registered US$58m in inflows.

Foreign participation increased to 45% from 41% last month.

Biggest inflows were seen in consumer food groups Jollibee, Monde Nissin, and Universal Robina on continued optimism that commodity prices coming off would translate to margin pressure relief.

Decent inflows were also seen in Ayala Land and Ayala Corp. after the latter increased its stake in the former after purchasing US$210mn of stock from a large foreign holder who is believed to have cleaned out his position.

Security Bank saw heavy foreign selling after being removed from the PSE Index in the August rebal. AC Energy also saw outflows and was among this month’s worst performers after UPC trimmed its stake to a set of primarily foreign investors.

Meanwhile, Meralco saw follow through selling after JG Summit disclosed they sold a portion of their stake in the distribution utility in late July. This caused some speculation that other placements may be in the pipeline.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The PSEi gained 4.2% in August with gains across the board. Of the 30 index members, 21 climbed during the month.

The consumer sector populated the leaderboard, as sentiment was boosted by commodity prices coming off as well as strong 2Q earnings –- thanks to the consumption recovery which helped ease cost pressures.  Volumes across categories - snack foods, noodles, alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages, canned food were resilient despite high inflation. Also boosting sentiment were expectations of a boost in demand owing to the resumption of face-to-face classes across the country, after a prolonged, pandemic-induced pause.

Jollibee (+19.1% MoM) was the top gainer, after a strong 2Q performance, in which operating income doubled YoY.  Same-store sales grew across all geographies (ex-China), led by the Philippines, which saw a 52% jump in SSS as mobility and dine-in restrictions were lifted.

Monde (+15.9% MoM) likewise gained, helped by an upweight during the PSEi rebal in early August. Monde’s sales grew 13% in 2Q, with APC BFB sales expanding 18% YoY. 

The rest of the consumer sector that reported results in August reflected economic recovery as well.  Robinsons Retail’s (+8.2% MoM) SSSG accelerated to +15% in 2Q. while  Puregold’s (+13.4% MoM) net sales grew 14% YoY as SSSG during the quarter accelerated for both its brands. SM’s (+7.2% MoM) SM Retail is now almost at pre-pandemic levels (2Q19). Meanwhile, volumes for Universal Robina(+9.9% MoM) remained healthy despite upward  price adjustments (~12% cumulatively since 3Q21). Century Pacific (+5.5% MoM) likewise had healthy volume growth of >10% despite the mid-single digit price increase. 

Other consumer names were also strong: PIZZA (+11.9% MoM) gained despite its 2Q falling behind expectations.  Emperador (+4.9% MoM), which came in line in 2Q, also rose while DNL (+5.9% MoM) came in ahead.  

Also pushing up the index were large cap conglos SM Investments (+7.2% MoM) and Ayala Corp (+13.2% MoM).

SM Investments reported another strong quarterly result as the rebound in consumer spending lifted revenues for SM Retail.  SM Prime (+3.4% MoM) beat forecasts, with a 51%% QoQ jump in Philippine mall revenue, already 85% of 2Q19 revenue. BDO (+7.8% MoM) also jumped in August. The bank saw loan growth accelerating to 9%, NIIMs expanding 8bps QoQ, and NPLs coming down. 

The rest of the banks that reported in August mimicked the same trends with NIM expansion, loan growth acceleration, and improving asset quality recurring themes.  Security Bank (-0.6% MoM) continued the trend of positive surprises from the banks, with its 1H22 NPAT doubling and coming in well ahead of estimates.  The stock performance however was muted by its deletion from the PSEi in the August rebal. 

Metrobank (+8.2% MoM) likewise surged during the month.  The bank reported loan growth acceleration to 10.2% and NIMs expanding by 9bps QoQ. Provisions dropped sharply. Profits here helped to boost parent GT Capital’s (+6.1% MoM) which surged ahead of estimates (core profit up 72% YoY).  Auto unit TMP reflected strong recovery, with a 40% increase in retail sales volumes.

 

Meanwhile, Ayala Corp 2Q profit rose 68% YoY. The outperformance in 1H is mainly due to divestments at two of its key operating companies. BPI (+2.1% MoM) booked a large gain from the sale of property (2Q22), while Globe(+1.7% MoM) sold half of its data center business (in 1Q22).  Nonetheless, AC’s operating units reflected recovery, including for BPI, Ayala Land, and ACEN.  Ayala Land (+13.5% MoM) finally saw some respite in August, after five straight months of declines.  2Q22 net profit jumped 51% YoY (+55% QoQ) as the economic reopening boosted rental revenue and expanded margins. However,  2Q22 residential revenue fell 12% YoY as cancellations hit the middle-income and affordable brands.

Trends for the rest of the property sector were similar, with mall operations rebounding but cancellations hounding the residential business.  SMPH’s residential revenue fell 50% QoQ due to sales cancellations. Vista Land’s (+2.5% MoM)  residential revenue fell 18% YoY and QoQ, with cancellations partly to blame.  Meanwhile, Filinvest Land’s (+1.1% MoM) cancellation rates inched up to 3.4% (from 1.9%) in 2019; office vacancy remained high at 30%.

Nonetheless, Robinsons Land (+10.6% MoM) surged. RLC reported lower residential cancellations in the last round of results, while China revenue and a bounce in mall revenue helped profits. 

 

Cancellations were also less of a concern for Megaworld  (+10.6% MoM), as residential revenue for the quarter rose YoY and QoQ.

Parent AGI (+1.5% MoM) also reported a bigger-than-expected jump in GGR from Travellers and stable margins from Golden Arches, while Emperador’s (+4.9% MoM)  weak brandy margins were offset by a 20% growth in whisky and other income booked in the period.

LT Group’s (+0.45% MoM)  Tanduay also reflected topline recovery as volumes grew 17% YoY (22% QoQ).  Cigarette sales fell to 7.1bn sticks though (-27%QoQ, -18%YoY) following a price hike implemented in March.

Economic recovery was also evidenced by Meralco (-11.4% MoM), as it reported a 17% jump In commercial electricity consumption. Meralco’s performance however seemed to reflect the overhang created by JG Summit’s sale of 36m shares of the distribution utility at the end of July.  JG Summit still holds around 297m shares in Meralco post the transaction.

 

 

Nonetheless, Meralco’s performance helped profits of MPI (+1.1% MoM) which were likewise boosted by stronger-than-expected revenues from its  tollroad units.  Organic daily traffic volumes for the tollroad unit rebounded by 26% YoY, already exceeding pre-pandemic levels.

Other utilities were weak as well in August, with losses led by ACEN (-10.4% MoM) following an overnight secondary share placement on the stock. 

Meantime, power generators benefitted from tight power supply, which saw spot prices jump to P9.01/kwh (+24% YoY) in June. Aboitiz Power (-4.1% MoM) benefitted, with another unit of its GN Power Dingingin plant coming on line.   First Gen (+7.4% MoM) saw a marked improvement in 2Q earnings ($67.1m, 19%QoQ) on the back of higher fuel availability for its natural gas plants.

Meanwhile, Semirara (-1.0% MoM)  delivered a big beat, with 2Q22 core profit at P10.07bn. This propelled DMCI’s (unch) profits, with 2Q22 profit alone more than doubling.  Aside from robust numbers from SCC,  almost all key segments posted strong recoveries. 

Telcos were mixed. In contrast to many other sectors, telcos did not see a bump from the reopening. Mobile revenues grew, though not as much as the market had hoped. Meanwhile, fiber broadband saw a  sharp slowdown. 

PLDT’s (+1.4% MoM)  1H core income came in ahead of estimates despite softness on the revenue side as the wireless business contracted. Globe’s core telco businesswas not as strong as hoped, with revenues (+6% in 2Q) lifted by non-telco sources. 

 

Globe announced the sale of its 7000 towers (over half of its 12k total towers). The initial 5,700 towers will be sold for P71bn, which would imply ~P12.4m per tower, only slightly lower than PLDT’s tower deal (P13m per tower). The sale is estimated to reduce net gearing to 1.2x from the >2x today.

Converge (-8.4%  MoM) fell, with its 2Q performance falling behind street forecasts despite strong 42% EBITDA growth (36% revenue growth).  Management  guided for revenue growth of 25-30% for the year, down from 50% previously. Net adds guidance for the year was also tempered to ~300k from 700k previously.

For transport stocks, numbers buoyant.  ICTSI’s (-6.7% MoM) 2Q trended ahead of estimates as operations remained robust.  Yield per TEU remained high at US$179 in 2Q and throughput volumes rose +6% YoY, slightly accelerating from 1Q (+5%). 

In the meantime, Cebu Air’s (-4.0% MoM) 2Q EBITDA turned positive for the first time since the start of the pandemic on a strong rebound in revenues, tempered by high fuel costs. 

The same can be said for gaming.  BLOOM posted a net income of P1.806bn, boosted by the strong performance of its slots and VIP segments. GGR jumped to nearly 90% of pre-covid levels in the quarter on the back of strong recovery in volumes as well as a high win rate. The healthy numbers came in despite the absence of fly-in Chinese VIPs. 

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

2Q22 GDP growth slows to 7.4%, led by an odd 2% QoQ drop in consumption. Philippine real GDP growth clocked in at 7.4% YoY in 2Q22, slowing from the (revised) 8.2% pace in 1Q22. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data, Personal Consumption Expenditure (PCE) slowed sharply to 8.6% YoY from 10.0% in 1Q22; more oddly, the PCE figure for 2Q22 was down 2.1% QoQ. This was despite relatively lower base effects in 2Q21 given the tight lockdowns in April/May 2021, and lighter lockdowns in January this year. The slowdown was at odds with bottom-up sector data, Google Mobility data, and early corporate results, which showed an acceleration in demand.

Government consumption grew 11.1% YoY and 34.3% QoQ likely in part due to spending related to the May 2022 elections. Capital Formation surged 20.5% YoY and 34.66% QoQ. The growth was driven largely by Changes in Stock, ie huge inventory buildup (vs drawdown in 2Q21), and by Construction, which was up 15.7% YoY and 67.9% QoQ. Regis estimates Domestic Demand grew 12.8% YoY, compared to 12.1% in 1Q22. Net Exports was a major drag, with the deficit ballooning 39.6% YoY in real terms, which Regis estimates pulled GDP down by roughly 400bps. Exports grew 4.3% YoY, while Imports were up 13.6%. Annoyingly, the “Statistical Discrepancy” line accounted for a 140bp reduction to the YoY growth in 2Q22.

On the Supply side, Industry slowed sharply to 6.3% YoY from 10.5% in 1Q22, with Manufacturing growing just 2.1% YoY vs 9.8% in 1Q22. This negated a surge in Construction, up 19% YoY. The Services sector accelerated to 9.1% YoY (and up 10.5% QoQ), driven by Transportation (+27.1% YoY) and Accommodation/Food Service (+29.9% YoY) as mobility restrictions were eased.

Agriculture dragged, growing just 0.2% YoY, and contracting 4.1% QoQ. GDP growth in 1H22 was 7.8% YoY, significantly faster than Regis’ 6.0% forecast for FY 2022E. While it is tempting to upgrade, Regis retain their numbers for now, wary of a) higher base effect from 2H21, b) higher inflation in 3Q, and c) potential normalization after the “revenge spending” in 1H22.

 

Inflation accelerates further to 6.4% in July.Headline inflation continued its uptrend, rising to 6.4% in July, from 6.1% in June. This is the fastest recorded inflation rate since October 2018. This came at the upper-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 5.6-6.4% and above consensus (6.1%). Almost all categories saw an uptick in inflation, except for moderation in utilities, operation of transport equipment, and health. Food inflation driven by fish, meat, sugar; offset by lower inflation of vegetables. Prices of transport services jumped by 7%, faster than June’s 3%, due to nationwide increase in jeepney fares. The YT-July inflation now at 4.7%, above the government’s average inflation target of 2-4%. Monetary Board meeting is set for Aug 18.

BSP hikes key policy rates by 50bps. The BSP delivered another 50bps rate hike in yesterday’s Monetary Board meeting. Rates on the overnight reverse repurchase facility is now at 3.75%. The BSP has so far raised policy rates by 175bps so far this year. Pre-covid, the policy rate stood at 4%. The Fed has raised its policy rates by 225bps so far this year. The BSP also raised its 2022 inflation forecast to 5.4% (from 5% previously). It lowered inflation projections for 2023/2024 to 4%/3.2% from 4.2%/3.3%. Inflation averaged 4.7% in 7M22, staying above the BSP’s 2-4% target range, after quickening to 6.4% in July from 6.1% in June.

10-year government bond yield drops. The Philippine treasury sold P35bn worth of 10-year bond at a yield 5.813%. There were P128.8bn worth of bids. The yield at the previous auction (on July 19) was 6.865%. Bloomberg 10-year BVAL - 5.8423%.

 

 

Trade deficit widens further in June. Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit widened further to US$5.8bn. This is higher than the revised US$5.6bn deficit (previously US$5.7bn) in May 2022 and the US$3.33bn deficit in June 2021. Imports rose 26% YoY, 5% MoM in June due to a sustained surge in fuel imports (+125% YoY) and a strong rebound in consumer goods imports (+30% YoY). Capital goods imports rose just 3% YoY. Exports barely improved, up 1% YoY, as electronic imports dropped. The 1H22 trade deficit now at US$29.8bn, up 66% YoY.

End-July GIR level drops 2% MoM to US$98.8bn. The country’s GIR level, based on preliminary data, dropped by US$2.0bn (-2%) MoM to US$98.8bn in July. This is down from US$108.8bn in end- 2021 and US$110.1bn in end-2020. However, this is still above end-2019 GIR of US$87.8bn (~7.7 import cover). July level represents 8.3 months’ worth of imports.

Remittances rise 4% YoY, 14% MoM in June. Cash remittances coursed through banks reached US$2.76bn in June, up 4.4% YoY and 13.6% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances rose 18% YoY in June. The 3-month average growth rate rises slightly to +3.4% in June from +3.0% in May. Land-based remittances jumped 5% YoY, led by USA, Asia, and Middle East, offsetting decline in Europe. Sea-based remittances jumped 2.6% YoY but were still below pre-Covid levels. The YT-June remittances are higher by 2.9% YoY to US$15.3bn, trending behind the government’s target (+4%).

Budget department submits P5.268tn budget for 2023 to Congress. The Department of Budget and Management submitted the proposed P5.268tn budget for 2023 to Congress. The proposed 2023 budget is a 4.9% increase from the 2022 budget. The 2023 budget is estimated to account for 22% of GDP. Education, infrastructure, food security, healthcare and clean energy got top priority in the 2023 budget. On infrastructure, the government expects to spend P1.180tn on infrastructure in 2023 (5.0% of GDP), 2% lower than its 2022 target of P1.1995tn (5.5% of GDP). Congress is expected to approve the budget by October and Marcos is expected to sign it into law before year’s end. The government is targeting a lower fiscal deficit of P1.45tn (6.1% of GDP) in 2023. The 2021A deficit stood at P1.67tn (8.6% of GDP) and the 2022E deficit projected at P1.65tn (7.6% of GDP). The budget deficit is expected to return to the pre-pandemic levels of 3.2% of GDP beginning 2027, before closing at 3% of GDP by 2028.

 

Hot money exit continues in July. Data released by the BSP showed that total foreign investments registered through authorized agent banks logged a net outflow amounting to US$103.14mn in July. However, this is lower than the US$342mn outflow in June 2022 and US$340mn outflow in July 2021. Gross inflows amounted to US$681mn in July, lower than the US$1.0bn recorded in June 2022. Majority of investments (or 64.8%) registered were in PSE-listed securities. The gross outflows amounted to US$784mn in July, lower than the US$1.4bn outflow in June 2022. The YT-July hot money remains at a net inflow of US$625mn, a reversal from the US$446mn net outflow in the same period last year.

NG debt rises to P12.79tn in end-June. The national government’s outstanding debt reached P12.79tn in end-June. This is P296bn (2.4%) higher MoM and P1.063tn (9.1%) higher than in end-December 2021. Of the total debt stock, 31.5% was sourced externally while 68.5% were domestic borrowings. The government is targeting to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to below 60% only by 2025.

Dept. of Finance proposes new fiscal regime for mining. Department of Finance (DOF) bared its proposal to reform the mining fiscal regime to achieve simplification, fair share, value-adding and good governance. DOF wants to impose a royalty rate of 5% for all large-scale mining operations and provide incremental revenues of at least P5bn yearly. This would effectively change the current set-up where only those located inside a mineral reservation are subject to royalty payment. A royalty payment for non-metallic minerals extracted within or outside mineral reservations is also being proposed. The DOF is also pushing for a rationalized and single fiscal regime applicable to all large-scale metallic mines, regardless of location. In terms of value-adding, the DOF is proposing a 10% export tax on the gross value of mineral ore to encourage downstream and proper valuation of minerals. Currently, there is no duty on unprocessed export of minerals. The current mining tax policies in the country include a 25% corporate tax, 4% excise tax of gross value, and 5% mineral royalty inside mineral reservation areas, as well as 50:50 mode of sharing between government and mining proponents under an FTAA. The proposal is estimated to result in total revenue of P37.52bn per year for the government.

 

 

Asset quality, loan growth continue to improve in June. Asset quality continued to improve in June, not only in ratio terms, but also in absolute PHP amounts. NPL ratio (ex-interbank), dropped 16bps MoM to 3.69% (vs 3.85% in May), and is the lowest NPL print since September 2020. The PHP value fell to P421bn, down 1.8% MoM and down 12.8% YoY. The Past Due (PD) ratio dropped even faster, down 25bps MoM to 4.19% in June vs 4.44% in May, with PD loans down 3.5% MoM. Restructured Loans (RLs) edged down another 5bps to 2.89% (vs 2.94% in May), though the nominal amount crept up 0.7% MoM. NPL cover jumped to 97.1% from 94.8% in May, due largely to the drop in NPLs; loan loss reserves were up marginally MoM. Loan growth (ex-interbank), meanwhile, accelerated again to 9.1% YoY in June, compared to 7.7% in May. This is now the 15th consecutive month of faster YoY growth rates. Loans were up 2.5% MoM. LDRs were slightly up to 71.0% from 70.7% in May.

Vehicle sales jump 29% YoY in July.Vehicle sales jumped by 29% YoY in July to 27,813 units, though sales dipped by 3% MoM according to data from CAMPI/TMA. This brings YT-July sales to 182,687 units (+18% YoY), trending behind the industry group’s target of +25%. 

Lower House committee approves removal of tax exemption of pick-up trucks. The Ways and Means Committee of the House of Representatives approved the Dept. of Finance’s proposal to remove the excise tax exemption on pick-up trucks. The removal of tax exemption was included in the bill formerly called “Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act (Pifita)”. Previously, Pifita was pitched as a revenue-neutral measure however, with the removal of tax exemption, the bill is now expected to generate incremental revenues of P18bn in 2023, P7.9bn in 2024 before collections taper off from 2025 onwards. Pick-up trucks (i.e. Hilux) accounted for 15% of Toyota’s sales volumes in 2021. Regis forecasts assume Toyota’s sales volumes will reach 158k units in 2022 (+22% YoY) and 166k units in 2023 (+5% YoY). Toyota accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit and gross asset value. GTCAP trades at a 46% discount to its market-based NAV of P914/sh.

Domestic sugar prices jump to 5-year high.Domestic sugar prices continue to climb, breaching P3,000/50kg bag since the end of July. This is 75% higher vs year-ago and end-2021 levels. In USD terms, it is nearly 3x global sugar prices.The recent surge in prices has been attributed to poor supply resulting from Typhoon Odette last year, coupled with the recovery in demand. Consumer companies that are exposed to beverages, bakery, and confectionery will be the most affected by an increase in sugar prices. MONDE’s biscuits account for ~30% of APAC BFB’s revenues. Sugar is a major raw material for its APAC BFB business, accounting to ~10% of the segment’s raw material cost.  URC’s beverages (coffee, tea), confectionery, and baked goods account for c70% of the domestic BCF segment. Sugar accounts for 8-10% of the segment’s total raw material cost. Note, however, that URC is a net seller of sugar through its Sugar and Renewables (SURE) segment.

PSE Index August 2022 rebalancing: SCC (+), SECB (-). The PSE has announced that SCC will be added to the PSE Index while SECB will be removed. 

PH to open SY22-23 with face-to-face classes. After two years without face-to-face classes, the Department of Energy formally opens the School Year 2022-2023 on Monday, August 22. Public schools nationwide will be opening their doors to all students for face-to-face classes. As of August 20, DepEd said there are 27,691,191 enrolled students for this year school year.

PEZA allows 30% WFH for BPOs until March 2023. PEZA allowed 30% of BPO employees to work from home until March 2023. Previously, the 30% cap would lapse on September 12, after which only 10% of employees would be allowed to work from home.
