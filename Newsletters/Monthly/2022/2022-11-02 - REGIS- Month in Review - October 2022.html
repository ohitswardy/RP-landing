REGIS REPORT

02 November 2022
 

The Philippines gained back some ground in October, rising 7.2% MoM, after the 12.8% plunge in the previous month sparked buying interest.  Foreign selling abated during the month, even as investors continued to come to terms with rising global interest rates. The PSEi closed firmly above 6000 index points, at 6153.43. 

On the ground, higher interest rates are an inevitability, as Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla said the BSP is inclined to match the Fed’s rate moves. With the Fed widely expected to raise rates by 75bp as its next meeting, the BSP should do the same at 17 November when the Monetary Board meets. Governor Medalla has said that domestic policy rates should be at least 100bp higher than US Fed rates in order to “ensure stability’ in the exchange rate. The gap today between the BSP policy rate (4.25%) and the US Fed’s target rate (3.00% to 3.25%) is around that, though the gap over the past two decades has been closer to 300bp. In any case, markets are clearly anticipating more policy rate increases.  T-bill yields have risen markedly, while yields on 10-year government paper have climbed again.

The Peso ended the month at PHP58.111/US$, improving 0.9% MoM. The BSP has admitted to intervening in the FX market to help moderate PHP weakness. The BSP’s defense of the PHP is evidenced by the country’s GIR which declined US$4.4bn (-5% MoM) in September. The latest print marks the largest single month drop in the GIR in at least the last decade. The GIR now stands at US$93bn. According to news reports, Secretary of Finance Benjamin Diokno “vows” to keep the Peso from breaching PHP60 (to the USD).

Putting further pressure on the PHP is a ballooning trade deficit, which hit another record high of US$6bn in August. The deficit has been driven by strong demand for imports, though exports have also been weak.  The 8M2022 total trade deficit now stands at $41.8bn, up 69% YoY and nearly matching the full-year deficit of $42.2bn in 2022.  Meanwhile, inflation jumped to 6.9% in September, accelerating sharply from the 6.3% in August.

Nonetheless, domestic demand remains robust, as seen in surprising vehicle sales in September (+64% YoY and +17% MoM), now well above pre-Covid levels.  Initial results for 3Q have also trickled in – supporting the healthy domestic demand story. Wilcon’s SSSG accelerated to +23.5% during the quarter, albeit coming from a low base given the hard lockdown in Aug 2021. BPI reported loan growth of 15% and BDO also reported acceleration. And as the few beneficiaries of rising rates, banks’ margins are also expanding, faster than anticipated.

And the clear winner in this environment would be the banks as NIM expansion is expected to drive pofits.  This has been the case for BPO who saw NII rising 29% in 3Q. Loans also accelerated, growing 15.4% Yoy (from 14.4% YoY in 2Q).

President Marcos issued an Executive Order (EO) making the indoor use of face masks voluntary, in a bid to further ease pandemic-related regulations.  Masks will still be required in public transportation and medical facilities. The easing of rules should pave the way for the further normalization of activity and thus the economy.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners were slight net sellers for US$12m in October, bringing year-to-date net foreign outflows to US$1.236bn.

October saw numerous blocks and liquidity events that skewed the headline net foreign flow figure, but in the end they practically canceled each other out.  Among them were San Miguel and SM Prime blocks that brought in US$16m and US$7m in inflows respectively, and an ETF rebalancing that saw net outflows of US$21.5m. Excluding these, net foreign outflows for the month would have been $14m.

 

Biggest inflows were seen in property names Ayala Land and SM Prime as foreign bargain ensued after each dropped 20% each the month prior. Foreigners were also buyers of BDO, with most of the inflows coming through at the last week of the month, in step with its 3Q results release. Meanwhile, consumer names Jollibee and Emperador were top foreign bought for a 3rd straight month.

Biggest outflows were seen in ICTSI as large foreign holders continued to reduce their holdings given the stock’s multi-year outperformance. Large outflows were also seen in conglomerates Ayala Corp., GT Capital, and Aboitiz Equity Ventures.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The Philippines mustered a 7.2% MoM gain, recovering from a 12.8% plunge in the previous month.  Trading was uninspired however, with trading volumes falling to as low as only US$39m on one day, with a lack of market catalysts on the ground.

The market’s rise was broad based, with index gainers outnumbering losers 22 to 8.

Topping these index gains was Meralco (+14.6% MoM), recovering from a 12% tumble in the previous month.  The Supreme Court (SC) upheld the validity of the P27.5bn settlement between Meralco  and National Power Corp  over disputes about their power purchase agreement and other related matters. From a Meralco perspective, the cost of the settlement has been provided for. Thus this latest development could be neutral to positive; should the regulator allow for some cost recovery, the company would be able to reverse provisions and book another chunky one-off gain.

ACEN (+11.6% MoM) likewise gained in the wake of a 25.4% drop in September.  ACEN said it signed an agreement to buy 43MW of power from Aboitiz Power’s (+2.8% MoM)  GN Power Dinginin coal plant. This should help cap ACEN’s short position in the spot market. The fresh earnings contribution from the new coal plant will also help AP’s bottom line beginning this year.

Semirara (-10.4% MoM) underperformed the market, after the shares went ex-dividend on October 26. The company announced a hefty special cash dividend of P3.50/sh, taking full-year cash dividends to a record P5/sh, equivalent to a dividend yield of 12%.   

Meanwhile, Manila Water (+19.8% MoM) surged after the regulator revealed the company is applying for a hefty tariff hike of P20.37 per cubic meter, which is roughly equivalent to 76% of today’s basic water tariffs. This development provides a glimpse of the final determination, which is likely to be released on or before December 15. 

Globe (+14.4% MoM) was also among the top gainers in October.  Globe announced the completion of the sale of 701 towers to MIESCOR Infrastructure Development Corp for a cash consideration of P8.4bn. To date, Globe has closed the sale of 1,501 towers for a total P18.4bn. Globe has agreed to sell 7,059 towers for a total P91bn. The tower sale and leaseback terms will be very value-accretive for Globe. Regis estimates it could add value of over P500/share.

Meantime, Globe  completed its P17bn rights offering, with 10.1m new shares issued (taking outstanding shares to 144m).  Combined with its recent tower sale, the capital raise would improve Globe’s financial position. Regis estimates gearing to fall just below 1x. 

PLDT (+8.4% MoM) also gained. The telco said it was contemplating the sale of another 2,000 telecom towers for “about US$300m”. This simplistically implies a price of US$150k per tower, around 30% lower than the previous tower sales.

Converge (-6.0% MoM) however continued to decline, despite already falling 59% YTD as of 9M22, as the market has been pricing in too-high growth expectations.  Converge disclosed that it was the Uy couple, the founders, that acquired 3% of the company the other day. The price of the purchases was P12.5/share, which is half of the price at the last placement by Pincus. At the time of the placement (in May), it was said that the last bit of Pincus’ stake would also be “distributed” at P25/share.

 

 

 

Banks fared well, with the sector seen to be of the few beneficiaries of rising interest rates as it translates to NIM expansion.  BDO (+14.2% MoM) climbed sharply as it reported a 3Q beat.  For 3Q22 alone, NPAT was P16bn, surging 46% YoY. NIMs expanded 24bps QoQ, which saw NII surge 17% YoY in 3Q22 alone (+11% for 9M). Customer loans grew 10% YoY, up from the 9.2% recorded in June.

The same theme was seen in BPI (+7.3% MoM), with its 3Q profit doubling to P10bn. Loans as of 3Q were 15% higher YoY and the NPL ratio was at 1.94%. NIMs rose  23bp to 3.53%. 

Metrobank (+6.6% MoM) and Security Bank (+6.7% MoM) moved higher as well.

Consumers gained during the month, led by Universal Robina (+7.0% MoM) and Puregold (6.2% MoM), both of which bounced from the previous month’s lows. Newsflow on the sector was thin throughout the month, though 3Q earnings expectations for the sector are positive as top line momentum from 2Q has likely continued. Wilcon (-8.1% MoM) lagged however, even after reporting strong results, with 3Q NPAT surging 77% on continued margin expansion.

After being the top foreign sold stock in September (and dropping 20% MoM), Ayala Land (+11.6% MoM) helped lead the market higher in October. Megaworld (+2.0% MoM) and SM Prime (+4.7% MoM) also rallied after dropping 18%-20% in September. Robinsons Land (-10.0% MoM) dropped, however. 

 

 

SM (+13.1% MoM) also gained markedly, and contributed almost a quarter of the PSEi’s total gain for the month (102 index points of the PSEi’s total 412-index point rise). Other macro proxies also fared well, with Metro Pacific (+9.9% MoM), Alliance Global (+8.8% MoM), Ayala Corp (+8.3% MoM), Aboitiz Equity (+5.7% MoM) all ending with gains.

Some conglos fell however.  The ERC junked the joint petition of San Miguel (-1.8% MoM) and Meralco for a temporary price adjustment to allow for a partial recovery of the increase in fuel prices this year. Amidst this development, Meralco said that it had already secured emerging power supply deals in case San Miguel terminates its supply contract.

Meawnhile,  Eagle Cement (+39.3% MoM) surged after San Miguel announced it was buying 88.5% of the target company from its principal shareholder, who is concurrently SMC’s President & COO, Ramon Ang, as well as, buying out the remaining 11.5% shares held by minority shareholders through a tender offer. Eagle Cement is being bought for P22.02/sh, a 43% premium from the closing price prior to the announcement and also equivalent to a premium EV/MT valuation of US$277 (vs. replacement cost of c. US$150/MT). SMC said that the acquisition will be paid in cash. The transaction will not be subject to the review of the Philippine Competition Commission. 

DMCI  (-4.0% MoM) also underperformed the broader market, after the shares went ex-dividend on October 27. GT Capital (-0.7% MoM) closed lower despite the announcement of strong vehicle sales numbers for September. Its auto unit, Toyota, saw sales jump 10% MoM, 63% YoY to over 16k units in September alone, bringing 9M22 sales up 35% YoY to 125k units, trending well ahead of its full-year target (162k units, +25% YoY).

LT Group’s (-2.3% MoM) PMFTC reported cigarette sales of 7.7bn sticks (a slight recovery from 2Q but down 13%YoY). Despite the soft volume, revenue for the period is likely to have increased, taking into account the impact of the price hikes in March.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Headline inflation jumps to 6.9% in September. Headline CPI for the month of September rose by 6.9% YoY, faster than in August 2022 (6.3% YoY) which came within street expectations (6.9%) and the BSP’s target range of 6.6%-7.4%. Food was the biggest driver, after last year’s low base for prices of meat, fish, vegetables, sugar, and oils/fats. Electricity prices also grew at a faster rate of 16.7% from 15.7% in August. Transport decelerated slightly to 14.5% from 14.6% in August, as the YoY growth in regional prices of diesel/gasoline (in PHP terms) decelerated to 78%/31% from 100%/47% in August.

On a MoM basis, headline CPI increased by 0.4%, broadly unchanged from August (+0.4%). This was due largely to higher prices of food (i.e. flour, vegetables, and sugar) and electricity prices (+1.7% MoM) and semi offset by lower fuel prices; regional prices of diesel/gasoline (in PHP terms) dropped by 6%/9% MoM.

The Core CPI (ex-food and fuel) rose by 4.5% YoY in September, slightly moderating from 4.6% in August. On a MoM basis, core CPI was flat for the first time since December 2021. This brings 9M22 average inflation rate to 5.1% (vs BSP’s target of +5.6% for 2022). The next monetary board meeting is on November 17; policy rate currently at 4.25% (+225bps since end-2021). The Fed meeting on Nov 3; US Fed funds rate at 3%-3.25% (+300bps since end-2021).

Trade deficit at US$6.0bn in August. The country’s trade deficit ballooned to US$6.003bn in August. This is wider by 81% YoY and broadly unchanged from July 2022 (US$5.989bn). Import growth accelerated to 26% YoY in August from 22% YoY in July 2022. This is despite fuel imports decelerating to 76% YoY in August from 87% YoY in July 2022. Consumer goods imports soared by 46% YoY while capital goods import grew at a faster rate of 15% YoY. Meanwhile, eports dropped for the second straight month. The YT-August trade deficit is 69% YoY wider at US$41.8bn, almost matching full-year 2021’s trade deficit of US$42bn.

GIR drops to US$93bn in September; BOP deficit balloons to US$2.3bn. The BSP revised the end-September GIR to a lower figure of US$93.0bn (from a preliminary data of US$95.0). The GIR dropped by US$4.4bn (-5%) MoM, the largest sequential drop in the past 10 years. This is equivalent to 7.4 months’ worth of imports and is also 6.6x the country’s short-term external debt. The balance of payments (BOP) posted a deficit of US$2.3bn in September, larger than the US$572mn deficit in Aug 2022 and the US$412mn in Sep 2021. This brings YT-September BOP deficit to US$7.8bn, higher than the US$665mn deficit in the same period a year ago. The BSP is targeting a BOP deficit of US$8.4bn by year-end, equivalent to 2% of GDP.

 

Cash remittances rise 4.3% YoY in August. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew by 4.3% YoY in August to US$2.72bn. MoM, remittances were lower by 6.7% but the absolute amount was still higher than the average in 1H. The 3-month moving average growth rate accelerated to +3.6% YoY in August from +2.8% in July 2022. Land-based remittances from Asia and Middle East were the main growth drivers, jumping by 9.5% and 5.2%, respectively; Europe remained a drag. Meanwhile, Sea-based remittances sustained MoM improvements, growing by 3% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances jumped 15.8% YoY. On a year-to-date basis, cash remittances amounted to US$20.99bn, up by 3.0% YoY and tracking slightly below the government’s target (+4%).

Budget deficit slightly lower YoY. The government’s fiscal deficit stood at P179.8bn in September, 0.6% lower YoY. On a MoM basis, the budget deficit was wider than the P72bn deficit in August. Revenues dropped MoM amidst higher expenses, specifically on interest payments. 

Tax revenues rose by 18.6% YoY but dropped 17.9% MoM; MoM drop is consistent with pre-pandemic trends. Non-tax revenues surged by 83% YoY, 48% MoM due to higher PAGCOR profit, BTr managed funds and interest on NG deposits

Primary spending gradually rose by 12% YoY and 9% MoM, indicating a gradual catch-up of agency disbursements. Interest payments surged 25% YoY and 95% MoM. 

YT-September budget deficit stood at P1.013tn, down 11% YoY and trending slightly lower than the government’s target.

Gov’t partially awards T-bills, yields rise. The government raised P6.6bn out of its P15bn goal from T-bills.  The auction was more than 1.4 times oversubscribed, attracting P20.9bn in total tenders.  The government made a partial award amid a pickup in demand for the T-bills due to banks needing to place their funds in debt instruments, according to National Treasurer Rosalia V. de Leon. 

The yield on the 91-day T-bill rose by 41bps to 4.22% (BVAL: 3.7036%)

The yield on the 182-day T-bill rose by 23.5bps to 4.65% (BVAL: 4.4823%)

The yield on the 364-day T-bill went up by 109.3bps to 4.875% (BVAL: 4.8598%)

 

 

BSP may match US Fed rate hikes. According to BSP Gov. Felipe Medalla, policy rates could be hiked by as much as 100bps before the year ends, in line with the large hikes expected from the US Federal Reserve. Gov. Medalla says that if the Fed hikes rates by 75bps in Nov, the BSP will “have to match it”. The BSP has raised policy rates by 225bps so far this year, among the steepest moves in the region. The next Monetary Board meeting is set for 17 November. 

BSP Governor says policy rate should be 100bps higher than US. BSP Governor Felipe M. Medalla said the BSP policy rate should at least be more than 100 basis points (bps) higher than US Federal Reserve rates to ensure stability in the peso-US dollar exchange rate. The BSP’s policy rate is currently at 4.25%, 125bps higher than the lower bound of the Fed funds rate of 3%. The historical average gap has been c. 300bps. The next BSP Monetary Board meeting is scheduled on Nov 17 (Fed meeting on Nov 3).

Rates on 10-year government T-bond rises to 7.344%. The Bureau of Treasury (BTr) said that the average yield on its 10-year bond at auction rose to 7.344%. The yield was higher by 153bps than the auction in August. The 10-year BVAL was at 7.2019%. The auction was oversubscribed (P58bn bids vs. P35bn offered).

 

Finance chief vows to keep peso from breaching P60. The Philippine government will act aggressively to prevent the peso from weakening to P60 per dollar. It is prepared to deploy billions of dollars more in reserves, according to the Secretary of Finance and concurrently member of the BSP Monetary Board, Benjamin Diokno. He has assured President Marcos that the peso slump would have eased by year-end to “where we want it to be” at P55, citing another US$15.8bn of inflows from overseas Filipinos and BPOs from now until year-end, of which US$10bn will be used to defend the peso. The government also plans to sell USD-denominated bonds targeting overseas Filipinos in December, to capture at least 10% of remittances or at least US$3bn.

The BSP Monetary Board is also considering an additional 100bps of policy rate hikes in its last two meetings for 2022, taking the policy rate to 5.25% by year-end. The BSP has raised policy rates by 225bps so far this year, among the steepest moves in the region. 

Infrastructure spending still lackluster in August. The government spent P73.7bn on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in August (+4% YoY, -4% MoM). In terms of overall infra spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), the government spent P91.1bn (-1% YoY, -7% MoM).  

Overall infra spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) YT-August amounted to P789bn (+10% YoY). To reach the government’s full-year infra spend target of P1.2tn (+7% YoY), infra spend should average P103bn per month for the rest of the year.

 

 

Hot money outflows widen to US$367m in September. Gross inflows amounted to US$892mn in September, +12.7% MoM. Gross outflows were higher by 43.4% MoM to US$1.3bn. The latest print brings YT-September foreign portfolio investments to a net inflow of US$221.6mn, a reversal from the US$494m net outflow in the same period last year.

Gov’t raises US$2bn in fixed rate global offering. The Republic of the Philippines raised US$2bn in a three-tranche fixed rate global bond offering. This follows previous fund raising exercises of JPY70bn Samurai bond offered in April 2022 and the US$2.25bn bond offering in March 2022. The US$500m, 5-year tranche was priced at US Treasury plus 120bps with a coupon of 5.17%, 194bps higher than March offering. The US$750mn, 10.5 year tranche was priced at US Treasury plus 185bps with a coupon of 5.609%, 205bps higher than March and the last US$750m, 25-year Sustainability tranche was priced at 6.1% with a coupon of 5.95%, 190bps higher than March. The 25-year Global Bonds marks the country’s third ESG G3 bond offering. The government’s debt as of end-August stood at P13.02tn, of which 69% was domestic and 31% was external. The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% this year and gradually decline to 51% by 2028.

Philippines to make indoor face mask use voluntary. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.  issued an executive order making the wearing of face masks indoor areas voluntary, according to Tourism Secretary Christina Frasco. Masks will still be required in public transportation, medical transportation and medical facilities. 

 

 

Vehicle sales jump strongly in September. Vehicle sales jumped 17% MoM, 64% YoY to 35,282 units in September according to CAMPI/TMA. This brings 9M22 sales up by 29% YoY to 246,815 units. Toyota grew sales by 10% MoM, 63% YoY to 16,138 units in September alone, despite the blanket price hike early in the month. Its market share slipped by 530bps MoM to 45.7%. Toyota’s 9M22 sales reached 124,884 units, up 35% YoY, well ahead of the company’s target (162k, 25% YoY) and Regis estimates (158k, +22% YoY). 

Loan growth accelerates in August. U/KB loan growth (net of RRP) accelerated slightly to 12.2% YoY in August from 12.0% in July. MoM, loans rose by 1.1% (faster than the +0.2% MoM improvement in July). The notable improvements were seen in manufacturing, trade, utilities, and consumer loans. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector rose by 9.2% in July. Note, the U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity growth (M3) decelerated further to 6.8% in August (from 7.0% in July).
