REGIS REPORT

01 March 2022
 

The Philippines dropped 0.7% in February to end just above the 7300 level. Reopening optimism fueled by the rapid drop in new COVID cases and easing lockdown restrictions had taken the market to pandemic highs (7500 level) earlier in the month, even as investors anticipated moves of the Fed. However, Russia’s invasion of Ukraine shaved off gains.

The reopening story played out during the month, supported by a steep drop in case counts to below 1000 new daily cases at the end of February from 9,500 at the start of the month (and as high as 39,000 in mid-January). Hospital utilization rates are also low in NCR (23-24%) as well as other areas. As case counts softened, expectations of continued easing kept the market buoyant.

The government lowered the pandemic Alert classification for Metro Manila and 38 other areas to Level One (from Level Two), effective March 1. Alert Level One is the most relaxed quarantine level, allowing nearly all businesses to operate at full capacity. This should continue to drive mobility data (Google) which already showed  a sharp rebound early in the month.

The 4Q21 results season is underway and the recovery story is apparent. Mall revenues are surprising to the upside while banks are seeing accelerating credit growth and falling provisions.  Container volumes at the Manila International Container Terminal were up markedly in 4Q as well. Consumer companies, particularly restaurants are seeing momentum sequentially.

The Bangko Sentral, as expected, kept policy rates unchanged at its Monetary Board meeting. BSP has maintained the record low 2% policy rate after cutting during the early part of the pandemic. There should be pressure however for higher rates towards the latter half of the year as the markets anticipate an increasing number of rate hikes from the Fed and as local CPI numbers creep up in the coming months.

Credit rating agency Fitch affirmed the investment grade rating of the Philippines (BBB) with a negative outlook. This follows the deterioration of the country’s fiscal ratios due to the effects of the pandemic.

Bongbong Marcos widened his lead in Presidential surveys. According to Pulse Asia, 60% of its respondents chose Marcos as their candidate in the survey conducted 19-24 January. This figure is up from the 53% in Pulse Asia’s December 2021 survey. In second place is current VP Leni Robredo, whose  numbers fell to 16% in January, from 20% in December.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreigners turned buyers in February for US$141m as reopening optimism amid a big drop in COVID cases brought buyers back into the market. Inflows were consistent throughout the month, with fifteen out of eighteen trading days seeing net foreign buying. Foreign participation stood at 39% (from 35% in January).

Foreign investors flocked back into cyclicals with property names SM Prime and Ayala Land seeing biggest inflows. Foreign inflows were also seen in BLOOM despite being removed from the PSEI as it is expected to be a main beneficiary of looser mobility restrictions. 

Nickel Asia also saw decent inflows amid a broad rally in metal prices.

Meanwhile, biggest outflows were seen in recent outperformers Globe and Converge. Converse saw increased selling pressure ahead of the lock-up expiry of a major shareholder as investors speculate that they will go right into market. Large inflows were also seen in consumer retail names Puregold and Robinsons retail as well as in Universal Robina.

 

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
DECEMBER LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Telcos led the laggards in February. Globe (-17.4% MoM) was the top index decliner for the month after reporting a 4Q21 earnings miss.  Lower valuations ascribed to tech stocks globally have also tarnished valuations for the local telcos’ e-wallets. 

Globe is increasing its authorized common shares by 20m from 148.9m to 168.9m, suggesting an equity issuance. Globe’s nominal debt levels are already on the high side (gross debt/equity of 1.84x, net debt/equity of 1.62x and gross debt/EBITDA of 2.65x), excluding its recent  recent US$600m perpetual.  Capex guidance in FY22 is also near-record levels.

Converge (-15.2% MoM) dropped almost just as sharply in February, after more than doubling (+114%) in 2021.   

PLDT (-1.9% MoM) fell as well though to a lesser extent.

Macro proxies were mostly down. Pushing the index down was SM Investments (-6.0% MoM) which declined after an announcement that it would be removed from the FTSE GEIS Asia Pacific Index. SM failed one of the liquidity tests, which requires that a certain percentage of shares trade in a month. Thus, the higher the market cap of a company, the higher the required daily turnover (in US$ terms). 

Index stocks Aboitiz Equity (-4.9% MoM) and JG Summit (-3.5% MoM) likewise took the index lower.  Conglos Ayala Corp (-2.3% MoM) fell as well. 

Meanwhile, MPI (+0.3% MoM) gained, announcing a P5bn share buyback, equivalent to around 4.6% of market cap. GT Capital (+1.4% MoM) also rose.

 

The consumer sector was soft, with concerns on cost inflation weighing on the sector.

Emperador (-18.7% MoM) fell sharply, despite its inclusion in the PSEI rebal during the month. Also an addition, Monde (+0.9% MoM) gained slightly.   

Weakness was widespread, seen in URC (-5.5% MoM), CNPF (-5.5% MoM), and D&L (-5.8% MoM).

Jollibee (+0.4% MoM) managed to gain slightly, carried by a 4Q21 earnings beat.  Revenues in 4Q21 jumped 22% YoY. SSSG in the Philippines was +24%, implying that SSS was tracking 80% of pre-Covid levels.  Operating leverage as a result of its Business Transformation initiative helped boost operating profit in 4Q. The company is looking to achieve 2018 levels in terms of operating profit (P9.3bn) and net profit (P8.2bn) in 2022, largely driven by a double-digit growth (vs 2021) in revenues, and a further improvement in margins.

However, smaller restaurants Shakey’s Pizza (-13.0% MoM), and Max’s Group (-4.0% MoM) ended lower during the month. 

Retailers were a mixed bag.  RRHI (+9.4% MoM) had a strong month while Puregold (-5.3% MoM) did not fare as well. Wilcon (-2.5% MoM) ended lower.  Results were in line with expectations as SSSG and margins stabilized in 4Q21. SSSG should continue to improve in 2022 as restrictions are lifted though supply chain issues could lead to near term margin pressure.

 

 

Property outperformed in February.  Gains were led by SM Prime (+13.4% MoM) which surged on prospects of mall revenue recovery. FY21 earnings came in ahead of forecasts, with 4Q21 net profit surging 71% on back of a 57% jump in Philippine mall revenue.  

Large cap property name Ayala Land (+8.6% MoM) also closed markedly higher. FY21 profits came in ahead.  Reservation sales grew 13% for the year.  Mall revenue doubled YoY and QoQ to P3bn, 43% of the 4Q19 figure.

Robinsons Land (+5.0% MoM) also rose, as did Megaworld (+3.6% MoM).  Megaworld’s share buyback program will be extended by 12 months to end-February 2023. Regis estimates that P3.8bn (out of the original P5bn allotted) is still unused and is equivalent to 3.7% of market cap. 

The Big Three banks have reported FY21 earnings and results were in line to slightly ahead.  Earnings for BPI (+1.9% MoM), BDO (-4.3% MoM), and Metrobank (-3.1% MoM) showed similar trends, with provisions falling significantly.  Loan growth for BDO and BPI accelerated to 6.5% and 4.9% respectively.  MBT’s loans however are still down 1.3% though this improved from a 4.2% contraction in 3Q.

Meanwhile, laggard interest led Security Bank (+8.9% MoM) higher.

 

Transport names had a solid month on prospects of recovery. Cebu Pacific (+2.4% MoM) Chief Executive Adviser Michael Szucs indicated the company is on track to reach 60-65% of pre-pandemic domestic capacity in February. He expects this to climb to 100% by April as restrictions are further rolled back. Regis estimates assume domestic capacity to reach 90% of pre-pandemic levels and for overall capacity to reach c. 70% of pre-pandemic levels for the year.

ICTSI (+5.3% MoM) turned in a solid performance. Data from the Philippine Ports Authority showed that container volumes at ICTSI’s MICT port rose 14% YoY and 4% QoQ in 4Q21. This brings FY21 volumes up 20% YoY to 2.1m TEUs, exceeding 2019 by 2%.

The utilities sector saw big moves. 

Meralco (+9.9% MoM) continued to rise on speculation that a decision on tariffs for the lapsed regulatory period (from July 2015) is forthcoming. A decision here would remove an overhang on the stock.

Semirara (+10.6% MoM) also gained. Semirara reported headline 4Q21 profit was up 20x YoY and 1.6x QoQ (P5.9bn), taking FY21 profit to P16.2bn, well above expectations. 4Q21 profit was boosted by lower-than-expected coal mining costs and  higher power ASPs. Of note, the company’s disclosure also included 2022 guidance on Newcastle coal prices (average: US$197/MT) and power spot prices (average: +20% YoY or c. P5.7/kwh), both above Regis assumptions. Assuming management’s guidance, SCC could be trading closer to 3.5x PER and 2.5x EV/EBITDA. 

Manila Water (-20.0% MoM) fell on profit taking post its 55% ascent in 2021. Meanwhile, AC Energy (-10.9% MoM) fell for the fourth consecutive month.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Metro Manila, 38 other areas under Alert Level 1 starting March 1. Metro Manila and 38 other areas will be under Alert Level 1 from March 1 to March 15. The rest of the country is under Alert Level 2. 

Alert Level 1 is the most relaxed quarantine setting, allowing business establishments to operate at full capacity. Travel between and within areas with different alert settings, regardless of age and comorbidities, will also be permitted. 

Nationwide daily cases (7dma) are down to <1400 (from 2200 the previous week), with NCR at <300 (from 425 the previous week). Hospital utilization rates are also low in NCR (23-24%) and other areas

Inflation slows to 3.0% in January. Headline inflation slowed down to 3.0% in January 2022 from 3.2% in December 2021 and 3.7% in January 2021. The Philippine Statistics Authority re-based the CPI series to 2018. Inflation settled at the midpoint of the government’s target range of 2-4%. The slowdown was due to lower electricity prices and LPG prices, as well as easing inflation for alcoholic beverages and tobacco. Restaurants and accommodation services inflation also moderated. Using the re-based series, the annual headline inflation for 2021 stood at 3.9% (vs 4.5% for the 2012-based CPI).

Monetary Board maintains policy rates. For a 10th straight meeting, the BSP maintained its overnight reverse repurchase rate at a historic low of 2% which was within market expectations. The BSP cited a manageable inflation environment and emerging uncertainty surrounding domestic and global growth prospects. However, BSP Governor Diokno hinted at an “eventual normalization” of policy once recovery is sustained or inflation risks rise. They see the economy reaching pre-pandemic GDP levels by 3Q22. The risks to the inflation outlook continue to lean slightly towards the upside for 2022 but remain broadly balanced for 2023. The upside risks are linked mainly to the continued shortage in domestic pork and fish supply and the possible impact of higher oil prices on transport fares. The BSP raised its average inflation forecast for 2022 to 3.7% (from 3.4%) and for 2023 to 3.3% (from 3.2%). Inflation is seen to decelerate in early 2022 due to base effects. But inflation could accelerate towards the upper end of the target band (2-4%) in the second quarter on elevated oil and nonoil prices. By 2H22, inflation is expected to move back to within target range.

 

GIR falls slightly in January. The level of gross international reserves fell to US$108.5bn in January (from US$108.8bn in December). This is equivalent to 10.3 months’ worth of imports of goods and payments of services and 8.6X short-term debt based on original maturity and 5.9X based on residual maturity.

The MoM reduction was due to payments of the national government’s foreign currency debt obligations and the lower price of gold.

Remittances rises to a record US$2.99bn in December. Remittances rose to the highest monthly level of US$2.99bn in December (+3% YoY, +19% MoM). Land-based remittances rose +3%, pushed higher by remittances from Asia (+6%) and USA (+5%) but dragged lower by the Middle East (-2%). In PHP terms, remittances rose +8% YoY and the 3mma growth rate slightly moderated to +3.5% (from +4.2%). This brings FY21 remittances to US$31.42bn, up +5% YoY but slightly short of the BSP’s +6% YoY target. For FY22, the BSP projects remittances to grow by +4%. 

Balance of payments reverts to deficit in January. Based on preliminary data, the country’s balance of payments (BOP) position stood at a US$102mn deficit in January 2022. This is a reversal of the surpluses in the past three months but lower than the US$752mn deficit in January 2021. The government cited payment of maturing national government foreign debt. For 2022, the BSP expects a smaller BOP surplus of US$700mn from a US$1.35bn surplus in 2021. 

Gov’t infrastructure spending rises in November. Government infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) stood at P60bn in November. This is up 49% YoY but was slightly down (-1%) MoM. The YT-November overall infra spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) to P967bn, up 33% YoY. This implies November infra spend at P83bn, up 52% YoY and 8% MoM. The YT-November infra spend is ahead of the government’s FY21 target of P1.095tn (+26% YoY). For 2022, the government is targeting to spend P1.27tn (+16% YoY).

 

 

Fitch affirms PH investment grade rating but outlook still negative. Fitch Ratings affirmed the Philippines' investment grade rating of ‘BBB’ with a negative outlook. The negative outlook could mean current rating could be downgraded. Fitch cited weak government revenue mobilization compared with peers and government debt-to-GDP ratio is forecast to stay close to the ‘BBB’ median over the next few years. According to the government’s own data, its debt-to-GDP stood at 60.5% in 2021 from 39.6% in 2019; Forecasts for 2022/2023 are 60.8%/60.7%. Fitch is forecasting general debt-to-GDP ratio to reach 54.5% in 2022 and 53.1% in 2023 from an estimated 54% in 2021 and 48% in 2020. Fitch forecasting Philippine GDP to grow by 6.9% in 2022 and 7% in 2023.

BSP has room to keep policy rates low. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said there is room to maneuver on monetary policy amid manageable inflation and the improving economy. He wants to see 4-6 quarters of economic growth before looking into a possible rate hike. The economy has improved in the last three quarters. While the BSP has maintained policy rates low, it has started unwinding support for the National Government. The BSP’s participation in government securities market is now down to 0.25% of total volumes, from 4% in 2021 and 22% in 2020. Governor Diokno is also not worried about the PHP’s weakness against the USD and is looking at the PHP settling between P48-53 until 2024.

‘Hot money’ continued to exit in 2021. Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. hot money) saw a net outflow of US$574.46mn in 2021. This is the third straight year of net outflows but the 2021 outflow was significantly lower than the US$4.24bn outflow in 2020. In December 2021 alone, hot money posted a US$4.38mn net outflow. This is lower than the US$524mn outflow in December 2020 but was a reversal from the US$110mn net inflow in November 2021.

NPLs drop sharply to 4.12% in December, PDs fall 50bps MoM. Asset quality improved significantly in December 2021. Total banking sector NPL ratio (ex-interbank) fell to 4.12%, down 36bps MoM, and the lowest since Feb’21. In nominal terms, NPLs fell to P454bn, down 5.7% MoM (about P27bn MoM) . Regis believes  there were chunky write-offs done in December, as loan loss reserves (LLR) also fell by P22bn MoM. Perhaps more encouraging was the even sharper drop in Past Due (PD) ratio to 4.66%, down 46bps MoM, with the peso value down 6.4% MoM to P531bn (-P36bn). Restructured Loan (RL) ratio was up slightly to 3.13% (vs 3.11% in Nov), with RLs up 3.3% MoM in peso terms.

NPL cover was flat MoM at 87.4% (vs 87.1% in Nov), and LLR ratio fell to 3.49%, down 30bps MoM, both due to our presumed write-offs in the month.

Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated again to end the year at +4.2% YoY, marking the 9th consecutive month of improving growth prints. Loans were up 2.7% MoM, as December is typically a strong month for corporate borrowing.

 

U/KB loans jump 4.6% YoY, 2.6% MoM in December; Domestic liquidity expands 7.7%. Preliminary data show that outstanding loans of universal and commercial banks (U/KBs), net of reverse repurchase placements with the BSP, grew at a faster rate of 4.6% YoY in December. Growth accelerating from 4.0% in November and improving by 2.6% MoM. Lending to real estate activities (+9%), ICT (+27%), manufacturing (+9%), finance (+10%), and transport (+9%) accelerated. Consumer loans declined at a softer rate of -6% (from -7% in November), with credit card loans up +4.7% (+2.6% previously) and auto loans flattening. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 7.7% in December to P15.3tn, slowing from the +8.3% expansion in November.

Unemployment rate inches up to 6.6% in Dec. The unemployment rate inched up to 6.6% in Dec from 6.5% in the previous month. Labor force participation rate also improved to 65.1%. According to NEDA Sec. Karl Kendrick Chua, some 3.7m new jobs were created, already above pre-pandemic levels. He also expects unemployment figures to slightly deteriorate in Jan 2022 due to the Omicron surge. 

Vehicle sales slump in January 2022 due to Omicron surge. CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales dropped 11% YoY/25% MoM to 20,765 units in January 2022. The group cited tighter restrictions imposed in January 2022 due to the surge in COVID cases, as well as seasonality. Toyota’s market share stood at 47.57%, implying it sold 9,878 units (-9% YoY, -27% MoM). Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 16% discount to its market-based NAV (P684/sh).

Over 7,000 visitor arrivals logged since Feb. 10 border reopening. 7,051 international visitors landed in the Philippines since the borders reopened on Feb. 10 to fully vaccinated travelers according to Tourism Secretary Bernadette Romulo-Puyat. Many visitors are returning Filipinos, though many foreigners are on business or are reuniting with spouses. On Feb 10, the government allowed quarantine-free entry of fully vaccinated foreign nationals who do not require a visa to enter the Philippines. Other requirements include a negative RT-PCR test 48 hours before departure, outbound ticket, and travel insurance with coverage payout of a minimum of US$35k. The country has also been admitting returning Filipinos quarantine-free since Feb 1. Pre-pandemic, tourist arrivals stood at 8.3mn annually (or c. 23k monthly); In 2020, tourist arrivals plunged to 1.5mn (or c. 4k daily).

 

Law on registration of SIM cards and social media ratified. Congress approved a new law requiring the registration of all SIM cards. All public telecommunications entities must require registration prior to activation and sale of SIM cards. Additionally, existing SIM card subscribers will have 180 days to register with the telcos. According to a media report, the law also requires an individual’s real name and phone number upon the creation of a social media account. Troll accounts spreading fake news have been employed effectively by a number politicians in order to influence elections and public opinion.

Congress approves amendments to Public Service Act, with limits on foreign state-owned enterprises’.  Congress ratified a reconciled version of the Public Services Act, effectively lifting the 40% foreign ownership limits on a number of industries. Among the industries that are no longer considered “public utilities” : telecoms, domestic shipping, railways and subways, airlines, expressways and tollways, and airports. These are thus no longer subject to the 40% foreign ownership limit. Those still considered public utilities are: distribution and transmission of electricity; petroleum pipeline transmission systems; water distribution systems; seaports and public utility vehicles. Additionally, foreign state-owned entities will not be allowed to own more than 50% of companies that own “:critical infrastructure” unless there is reciprocity. This will be a constraint on China Telecom taking over Dito Telecommunity fully.

Finance Secretary to recommend new taxes to service debt. Finance Secretary Carlos Dominguez III is ready to brief Presidential candidates and their economic teams a fiscal consolidation package. Among the new taxes being eyed are: Carbon tax, Levy on cryptocurrencies, Removal of all exemptions from 12% VAT, Further hikes in excise taxes on cigarettes, e-cigarettes, alcoholic drinks, and sugary beverages. 

Mobility continues to improve (updated for 13 Feb data). Mobility across key segments continues to rebound. The country’s Covid case count drops to under 3,000/day, with Metro Manila cases creeping down to the P500/day mark. Alert Levels across the country are being relaxed. Retail/recreation destinations jumped back up to pre-pandemic levels, or close to it (though one wonders how much of that was the Valentine’s weekend). Transit stations stats are also rising quickly as restrictions fall off. Workplace data is basically back to December levels (Covid-era highs). While the numbers seem to be so much lower than December, they are significantly better – as in 20-30 percentage points higher – than they were in Jan/Feb 2021. And that’s considering that there was no Covid surge in Jan/Feb 2021. The data supports our continued broad reopening theme, but also highlights our recent bullish report on Philippine Property. 

 

Vaccine hesitancy falls sharply in December. Filipinos are increasingly willing to get vaccinated against Covid-19. According to a December 2021 survey by the Social Weather Stations (SWS), only 8% of Filipino adults refuse to be inoculated (7% “surely not” and 1% “probably not”), down from 18% in September. Meanwhile, 87% are either willing to be jabbed, including 51% who have reported already receiving at least one dose. Vaccine hesitancy is now extremely low in Metro Manila (or NCR) at just 4% of adults, down from 7% in September. The figures are also very low in Luzon (ex-Metro Manila) and Mindanao, both at 8%. The anti-vaccine sentiment seems stubbornly high in the Visayas, with 16% unwilling to get the jab. That said, vaccine hesitancy fell in every geographic area between September and December. With so many Filipinos open to being vaccinated, the slow progress in the nation-wide vaccine rollout lies squarely on the government’s inability to deliver the doses outside the major city centers. As of last count, Regis estimates that only 55% of Filipinos have been jabbed, compared to 80% in Metro Manila, and roughly 70% in Cebu and Davao.

Comelec’s First Division dismisses Marcos DQ cases for lack of merit. The Commission on Elections’ (Comelec) First Division has dismissed the consolidated disqualification cases against presidential candidate Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr for lack of merit.

The resolution covered the cases filed by martial law survivors through Bonifacio Ilagan, Akbayan party-list group, and the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos Commissioner Abubakar Mangelen. The groups sought to disqualify Marcos because of his conviction for failing to file income tax returns from 1982-85. The First Division’s resolution read that perpetual disqualification by reason of failure to file income tax returns was not provided for under the original 1977 NIRC. 

Another disqualification case against Marcos is pending before the Comelec’s Second Division.

Marcos-Duterte tandem sustains lead in January 2022 Pulse Asia poll. Ferdinand Marcos Jr.’s lead rose in the latest Pulse Asia survey, conducted from January 19-24. Marcos was the president of choice of 60% of respondents (up from 53% in Dec 2021 survey). In second place was VP Leni Robredo, with only 16%.  This was down from 20% in Dec.  Manila mayor Isko Moreno and Senator Pacquiao were tied at 8%.

In the VP race, Sara Duterte widened her lead, with 50% from 45% previously. Senator Sotto came in second with 29%, followed by Senator Kiko Pangilinan at 11%.

Pulse Asia’s survey is based on a sample of 2,400 representative adults 18 years old and above, with a ± 2 percent margin of error at the 95 percent confidence level.
