REGIS REPORT

01 July 2022
 

The Philippines plunged in June, giving up 9.1% MoM  as recession fears shook global markets. Persistent inflation fears and aggressive tightening by the Fed clouded sentiment even as economic reopening continued to play out on the ground.  The PSEi closed 1H at 6199.55, lower by 13.6% YTD.

The PSEi’s YTD performance of -19.7% (in USD terms) lags that of its neighbors Indonesia and Singapore which are at  +0.4% and -3.6% YTD, respectively (in USD terms).  It also trails Thailand (-10.7% YTD) and Malaysia (-13.0% YTD) which are down to a lesser degree.

The CPI inflation print for May came in at 5.4%, an acceleration from the 4.9% in April. Food and transport costs were the main contributors to the higher inflation print. It seems inflation has yet to peak as the transmission of the high commodity prices does not seem to be fully reflected to the retail level as yet. The recently announced minimum wage hikes can only contribute to further upward pressure on prices. All 17 regions in the country approved minimum wage increases, in response to rising inflation. The regions have increased wages by an average of about 12-15%. Likewise, the recently announced hikes on minimum jeepney fares (~+22% hike nationwide) should push inflation higher.

The BSP raised its policy rate (overnight RRP) by 25bp to 2.50% at  its  Monetary Board meeting in June. YTD, the policy rate in the country has been raised by +50bp, at a pace slower than the rate hikes of +150bp done by the Fed.  Consequently, the PHP  has fallen 7.25% YTD, dropping 4.7% in June alone.

Outside of the interest rate differential, the trade and current account deficits are providing enough pressure on the PHP to depreciate. YT-April, the trade deficit was stood at US$18.7bn (~US$4.7bn/month average), widening by +64% YoY. The trade deficit is on pace to exceed last year’s record deficit of US$43bn last year, and looks to be headed to around ~15% of GDP. Meanwhile,  The BSP expects a current account deficit of US$19.1bn (4.6% of GDP) for the year.

Incoming Bangko Sentral (BSP) Governor Felipe Medalla says he is not ruling out rate hikes of more than 25bp each in subsequent Monetary Board meetings, of which there are four more for the rest of the year.

Nonetheless, the economic reopening story on the ground is intact, The numbers from Google show that mobility data are now firmly above pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, loan growth accelerated again in May, up 10.7% YoY. 

Ferdinand Marcos Jr, took his oath of office and formally assumed the Presidency on June 30.  His announced Cabinet is yet to be complete but most key senior positions appear to be credible and non-controversial. Among his key priorities include agriculture and food security, with President Marcos himself serving as agriculture secretary.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreign outflows remained elevated, with US$207m exiting in June amidst inflation and growth slowdown fears.  This took first half outflows to US$774m. Selling was heavy despite deteriorating volumes, with average daily turnover in June falling 42% MoM to US$108m. Foreign participation stood at 45%, down from 52% the month prior.

Biggest inflows were seen in SM Prime after it saw an upweight in FTSE rebalancing where an estimated US$70m of passive inflows were expected. Semirara also saw decent inflows as it continues to report record earnings amid surging coal prices.

Meanwhile, biggest outflows were spread across major large-caps. ICTSI and Ayala Land saw its buy-back program (local flag) active in June  -- buying back against foreign funds trimming their positions. Banks also saw large outflow, with BDO and BPI among the top sold.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The Philippines sank 9.1% in June.  The index was mostly red – with 26 stocks closing down, overwhelming the four index names that managed to advance. Sectors were mostly down across the board though large cap consumer posted some outperformance.

Large caps pushed the market lower as risk off sentiment took hold. SM Investments (-8.9% MoM) and BDO (-17.2% MoM) pushed the index down over 80 index points each, cumulatively accounting for over a quarter of the PSEi’s 619-index point decline in June.

ICTSI (-15.2% MoM) also pushed the market lower.  Ayala Land (-13.9% MoM), BPI (-14.2% MoM), and parent Ayala Corp (-13.9% MoM) also dragged the market lower.

Converge (-20.2% MoM) declined sharply in June, leading the laggards for the month. The stock has fallen for a third straight month, with YTD losses now at 34%.

Heralding increased competition within the sector, Converge announced that it was increasing speeds for all subscribers of its entry level plan from 50mbps to 100mbps. The new speeds are twice as fast as the closest packages for PLDT (-11.6% MoM) and Globe (-6.6% MoM). Globe meanwhile lowered the entry level price points for its broadband service plans (P899/month as well as a P1,299/month fiber package) – a move that is likely to usher in lower pricing from competitors as well.

Globe is planning to raise up to P32bn in a rights issue. At the maximum amount to be raised (P32bn), Globe’s net gearing is estimated to drop to 2.0x, from 3.0x (on Regis FY22E estimates). Given the amount to be raised, actual dilution to EPS and valuation is not likely to be large. Meanwhile, according to a news report, Globe is in “advanced talks” to sell part of its telecom tower network. Among the parties Globe is reportedly negotiating with are Aboitiz Group (AEV, -10.3% MoM), Meralco (-3.5% MoM), and private equity firm KKR.  A sale involving half of Globe’s 12,000 towers on similar terms and at the same valuation as PLDT’s  would be very value-accretive for Globe. Regis  estimates the value enhancement could be worth over P450/share on an after-tax basis. These transactions would both help de-lever Globe’s balance sheet.

 

Megaworld (-21.7%) was the top index decliner in June, with parent Alliance Global (-17.8%  MoM) not far behind in terms of decline. The rest of the property sector was mostly weak, with Robinsons Land (-11.1% MoM), Vista Land (-15.7% MoM), Filinvest Land (-5.4% MoM) also dropping. SM Prime (-0.9% MoM) was the notable outperformer.

According to property consultancy Leechiu Property Consultants,  office take-up reached 85,000sqm in 2Q22.  While this is an improvement from the previous quarter, take-up is far below pre-COVID levels. Meanwhile, developer margins are under pressure;  wholesale prices of building materials in Metro Manila have been on the rise and in May (+8.3%) grew at the fastest pace since No ember in 2011. 

Universal Robina (+2.8% MoM) was among the handful of gainers this month, bouncing off lows after recent selldowns. Investors took interest in the stock, in conjunction with key soft commodities coming off sharply from recent peaks.  Meantime, soaring domestic  sugar prices  may help provide some margin cushion from costs pressures for the branded business. The recent downtrend for soft commodities also allowed Jollibee (-2.8% MoM) to relatively outperform the market as well in June.  

Emperador (+6.6% MoM) likewise gained.  The company received an update on its application (eligibility-to-list letter) and is taking steps to comply with the requirements in order to have a secondary listing in Singapore.

AC Energy (+12.5% MoM) was the top index gainer for June, bouncing from recent lows. The stock fell markedly in April (-17.3%) on AC’s announcement that it would distribute a total of 1.86bn ACEN shares (equivalent to 4.9% of outstanding shares) as property dividend.

First Gen (-12.0% MoM) disclosed that the operation of its LNG terminal will be delayed by around half a year.  The terminal was supposed to be operating by early 2023. However, the direct impact of the delay will not be large, as FGEN’s  gas-fired plants are now receiving enough fuel from Malampaya after the end of the fuel supply contract between Malampaya and the Ilijan facility.

Meanwhile, the government has signed the paperwork for the turnover of the 1,200MW Ilijan power facility to San Miguel (+6.7% MoM).

Laggard mid-cap conglos relatively outperformed  LT Group (-1.3% MoM), and GT Capital (+2.7% MoM) recorded smaller losses than their larger peers. 

MPI (-4.1% MoM) also fared relatively well. Metro Pacific is acquiring 51% of dairy producer, Carmen’s Best Group, in a deal worth P198m (less than $4m). The Carmen’s Best Group is an integrated dairy firm producing and selling premium ice cream and milk products. The transaction is part of MPI’s strategy of diversifying out of highly regulated industries. 

Meanwhile, Cosco Capital (-8.5% MoM) announced a joint venture with Thai firm Siam Global (GLOBAL TB), a leading retailer of home improvement and

building materials in Thailand. The JV will begin with 3-5 stores. This venture could pose a longer-term threat to market leader Wilcon (-10.2% MoM).

The anti-dummy case against Megawide (+5.1% MoM) officers was dismissed by a local court. The court cited the recent passage of the Amended Public Service Act. The newly-enacted law lifted foreign ownership limits on, among other industries, airport operations. MWIDE and its partner GMR were being accused of circumventing these restrictions in the anti-dummy case.

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Monetary Board hikes policy rates by 25bps; Raises inflation forecasts. The Monetary Board decided to raise the interest rate on the BSP’s overnight reverse repurchase facility by 25bps to 2.5%. This is within street expectations. The policy rate has risen by 50bps so far this year (vs. YTD for Fed: +150bps). The policy rate is still 150bps lower than it was prior to the pandemic (4%). BSP Governor Diokno said that inflation expectations continue to rise. It raised its 2023 inflation rate forecast to 4.2% from 3.9% previously. The 2022 inflation forecast kept at 5%; BSP forecasting inflation to average 5.6% in 2H22. Incoming BSP Governor and current Monetary Board member Felipe Medalla previously signaled another 25bps hike in August. Further rate hikes beyond this will be data-dependent.

Inflation jumps to 5.4% in May. Headline inflation jumped to 5.4% in May, up from 4.9% the previous month, broadly within consensus expectations (5.4%) and the BSP’s target band of 5-5.8%. This is the fastest pace since November 2018 (+6.1%).

Food inflation accelerated (+4.9% vs 3.8% the previous month) due to faster price increases in flour, meat, vegetables, fish, and oils/fat. Rice inflation dipped slightly to 1.5%; government yesterday announced an extension of lower rice tariffs to end-2022. The government also cut tariffs for corn (5% from 15% previously) and pork (15%-25% from 30-40% previously).

Transport inflation inched up further (+15% from +13%) due to higher gasoline and diesel prices. Inflation of electricity and LPG slowed to 19% from 20% the previous month. Prices of sin products (cigarettes, spirits, liquor, and beer) also jumped.

The latest print brings 5M22 inflation average to 4.1%, above the BSP’s 2-4% target band. The Monetary Board will meet on June 23.

Remittances rise 3.9% YoY in April. Remittances coursed through banks rose 3.9% YoY in April to US$2.4bn. However, this was lower by 8% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances in April rose 1.9% YoY to P125bn, which is lower than the monthly averages in 3M22 (P133bn) and FY21 (130bn). The 3-month average growth rate at +2.8%. Land-based remittances jumped 4.7% YoY, driven by USA (+9%) and Middle East (+7%). This brings YT-April remittances to US$10.2bn, up 2.7% YoY. To reach the BSP’s FY22 target (+4% YoY), remittances would have to average US$2.8bn per month for the rest of the year.

Budget deficit narrows in May, on soft primary spending. The national government posted a budget deficit of P147bn in May, narrower than the deficit in May 2021. The MoM, budget reversed from April’s surplus, which was due to timing of annual tax collections. Revenues in May rose 19% YoY, with tax collections up 22% YoY and already over 20% of pre-pandemic levels. Expenditures, however, dipped due to lower primary spending. This brings YT-May budget deficit to P459bn, down by 19% YoY. This is tracking below target, as revenues are trending ahead of the government’s target while primary spending trails.

 

 

 

Peso breaches P55 vs dollar.The Philippine peso breached the P55-level against the US dollar on Monday but managed to temper losses at the end of the trading. The local currency finished at P54.78 against the USD from an intra-day low of P55.15. The last time the peso sank below P55 was in October 2005. The more aggressive tightening by the US Federal Reserve was a stark contrast to the dovish strategy taken by central banks in the region including the BSP. The peso is down 7.4% ytd, the worst performer among ASEAN currencies. THB is down 5.7% ytd, IDR is down 3.9% ytd, VND is down 1.9% ytd and MYR is down 6.3% ytd.

BOP posts US$1.6bn deficit in May 2022. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$1.61bn in May 2022. This is larger than the deficits recorded in April 2022 (US$415mn) and in May 2021 (US$1.4bn). The government cited outflows from NG debt payments. This brings YT-May BOP deficit to US$1.53bn, lower than the US$1.63bn deficit in the same period last year.

GIR level dips to US$103.5bn in May. The country’s GIR level, based on preliminary data, dipped by 1.8% (US$1.87bn) MoM to US$103.5bn in end-May, due to the national government’s foreign currency debt payments and various expenditures, as well as lower gold prices. The GIR is equivalent to 9.1 months’ worth of imports.

New BSP head signals at least two more rate hikes; may cut reserve ratio.  Incoming BSP Governor and current Monetary Board member Felipe Medalla signaled to raise policy rates to 2.75% from 2.25%. Around 25bps rate hike each will be implemented on June 23  and August 18.

Increases beyond this will be data-dependent. The BSP says it need not move in lockstep with the Federal Reserve. The Fed has hiked policy rates by 75bps so far this year and analysts expect 100bps hike in June and July meetings.

Mr. Medalla also plans to lower banks’ reserve ratio to 10% from 12% currently to counter the impact of stimulus withdrawal. A cut will likely be done a month or two before a rule that allowed lending to small businesses be counted as compliance to the requirement expires in December.

New BSP Governor open to big hikes to aid peso. Incoming BSP Governor Felipe Medalla will not shy away from larger interest rate increases if the exchange rate overshoots and selling foreign exchange wont resolve the problem. He will not rule out rate hikes of more than 25bps. The aggressive hikes will depend on the exchange rate “overshooting” and stoking imported inflation. As it stands, there was no need to match the Fed’s hikes like-for-like. Policy makers will balance managing inflation and helping the nation’s “nascent” economic recovery.

Trade deficit stays elevated in April. The country’s trade deficit stayed elevated at US$4.8bn in April vs US$5.0bn in March. This brough YT-April trade deficit to US$18.7bn, up +63% YoY. 

Imports grew at its slowest pace in 13 months. Imports rose 23% YoY in April from 28% YoY in March. This was due to slower growth in capital goods imports (+3% YoY) from the previous month (+10% YoY). Consumer goods import growth also slowed to +7% YoY from +9% due lower imports of non-durable goods (i.e. rice and fruits). Imports of fuel and raw material sustained strong increases. Exports rose +6% YoY, broadly unchanged MoM. 

 

Infra spend drops MoM in April.Infrastructure spending (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) reached P63.8bn in April, down 36% MoM but up 9.6% YoY. Infra spend in April largely towards Department of Agriculture’s farm equipment and machineries and the Dept. of Education’s basic education facilities. The MoM drop was due to the election ban. This brings 4M22 infra spend relatively unchanged YoY at P254bn. Overall infra spend (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) in 4M22 amounted to P332bn, up 3% YoY. This implies infra spend in April only rose by 1% YoY to P79bn. The government is targeting to spend P1.27tn on infrastructure this year (+13% YoY).

Rates for 10-year T-bonds hit 7.1%. The Bureau of Treasury raised P34.9bn through 10-year bonds and fetched a coupon rate of 7.25% and a rate of 7.145% (vs. BVAL: 6.944%). Demand reached P67.3bn, oversubscribing the offer by almost 2x.

Loan growth accelerates to 10.1% YoY in April. U/KBs bank lending grew 10.1% YoY in April, faster than in March (+8.9%) according to preliminary data from the BSP. The acceleration was driven by consumer loans and loans to manufacturing sector. Recall that loan growth for Philippine banks jumped by +5.8% in March. U/KBs loans figure distorted by the merger of BPI with Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) slowed to 7.3% in April from 7.7% in March.

PH Banks - Consumer loans - 1Q22 back in +ve growth territory; NPLs improving. Consumer loans continue to recover in 1Q22, posting 0.7% YoY growth, back in positive territory for the first time since end-2020. This was despite a surprise deceleration in mortgages, which grew 5.3% YoY, down from 7.9% in 4Q21. In fact, mortgages were down 1.5% QoQ, the first QoQ decline since mid-2015. Regis suspects there may have been a bank that missed the deadline for submission.

Consumer NPL ratio also improved o 8.99%, down 32bps QoQ from 9.31% in 4Q21, and down from the peak of 9.90% in 3Q21.

Growth in 1Q22 was led by credit card (CC) receivables, which jumped 11.5% YoY, following four quarters of decline. In absolute PHP terms, the P446bn balance was just a shade below the all-time high posted in 1Q20 just before the pandemic hit. Credit card NPLs also improved to 6.54%, down 24bps from 6.78% in 4Q21 (the peak was 8.85% in 4Q20).

As mentioned, mortgages grew 5.3% YoY, though balances were down 1.5% QoQ. NPL ratios here were flat at 9.37%, from 9.36% the quarter prior.

Motor vehicle (MV) loans were still down 15.8% YoY and down 2.0% QoQ. While banks are anecdotally loosening credit standards, the balances are still shrinking due to the faster roll-off of existing loans. Banks also noted the lack of supply of vehicles, owing to the global supply bottlenecks. To the upside, NPL ratios improved to 11.42% from 12.01% in 4Q21.

Based on banks’ earnings calls, the industry seems to be ready to re-grow its consumer loan portfolio. This could boost NIMs, given the higher yields on consumer loans compared to corporate / commercial / mid-market loans. 

Vehicle sales improve further in May. Vehicle sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, rose 20% YoY, 5% MoM to 26,370 units in May. This is about 85% of pre-pandemic sales (vs. 2019 average) and brings 5M22 vehicle sales to 126,273 units (+15% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 14,723 units in May (+36% YoY, +7% MoM). Toyota’s market share improved to 55.8% from 48% in end-December. Sales were strong due to pull-forward sales ahead of blanket price hike in June. This brings 5M22 sales to 65,668 units, up 25% YoY. To reach Toyota’s target of 162k (+25% YoY) units this year, Toyota needs to sell 13,800 units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 22% discount to its market-based NAV of P617/sh and 7.2x FY22E consensus PER.

Sugar prices jump. Based on data from the Sugar Regulatory Admin, wholesale prices of raw sugar have jumped (+48% YTD) to ~P2700/50kg bag as of the week ending June 17, surpassing previous highs of P2100-2200/50kg bag in mid-2018. Consumer companies that are exposed to beverages, bakery, and confectionery will be the most affected by an increase in sugar prices. For MONDE, biscuits account for ~30% of APAC BFB’s revenues. Sugar is a major raw material for its APAC BFB business, accounting to ~10% of the segment’s raw material cost. Meanwhile for URC, beverages (coffee, tea), confectionery, and baked goods account for c70% of the domestic BCF segment. Sugar accounts for 8-10% of the segment’s total raw material cost. Note, however, that URC is a net seller of sugar through its Sugar and Renewables (SURE) segment. Historically, the SURE segment outperforms when sugar prices are high. This supports consolidated margins by cushioning compression at the BCF segment. This segment accounted for 16% of URC’s consolidated revenue and 36% of EBIT in 1Q22.

Wholesale prices of building materials in Metro Manila rise by 8.3%, fastest since 2011. Wholesale prices of building materials in Metro Manila rose by 8.3% in May vs +6.9% in April and +2% in May 2021. This is the fastest pace of growth since the 8.6% figure in November 2011. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) said that supply chain issues also delayed the delivery of materials from abroad, which pushed up costs onshore. The minimum wages have risen 6% in Metro Manila and c. 15% in most parts of the Philippines.

LTFRB approves P2 hike in jeepney fares nationwide. The LTFRB approved a provisional P2 fare (+22%) increase in the minimum fare of public utility jeepneys (PUJs) nationwide beginning July 1. The minimum fares for traditional PUJs will now be P11 for the first four kilometers. In early June, the LTFRB approved a provisional fare hike to P10 from P9 in select regions such as Metro Manila, Central Luzon, Calabarzon, and Mimaropa. The LTFRB did not increase fares for succeeding kilometers. Transport fares have a weight of 4.54% in the CPI basket.

Government cuts tariffs on food, coal. The government issued an executive order modifying the tariff schedule for imported rice, pork, corn, and coal from countries outside Southeast Asia.

Tariffs on rice imports were reduced to 35% (from 50%) until Dec 31, 2022. Tariff rates on pork imports were reduced to 10-15% from 30-40% until Dec 31, 2022. Meanwhile, Corn tariffs were decreased to 5-15% from 35-50% until Dec 31, 2022. Tariffs for coal (7%) were removed until Dec 31, 2022.

In early May, the government also extended reduced tariffs on the same commodities from within southeast asia to end-2022. 

BSP targets 1-digit bank reserve ratio. The BSP will pursue the reduction of the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) of big banks to within single digit in the fourth quarter according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. Governor Diokno previously said that the BSP was targeting to bring the RRR to single digits only by 2023. In 2020, the BSP lowered the RRR by 200 basis points to 12%.

 

Metro Manila, most areas stay under Alert Level 1 until July 15. Alert Level 1 will remain in Metro Manila from July 1-15. Around 37 areas from across the Philippines “shall be placed” under the alert system’s level 2. The Gov’t also redefined alert level classifications to be based “on the revised cross tabulation of total beds utilization rate and average daily attack rate”. The new classification removes the two-week growth rate in determining case-risk classification. Recently, the nationwide cases (7-day average) are now at 711 (up from 479 the previous week), with NCR at 342 (vs 238 the previous week). The nationwide weekly covid bed occupancy rates are now at 14.9% (ICU) from 14.6% the previous week.

Mobility well above pre-Covid, focus on Workplace data. Mobility data is firmly above pre-pandemic levels, at least for the national figures, whether it be for retail/recreation, for grocery/pharmacy, for transit stations, or for the workplace. As before, Regis believes Metro Manila remains below pre-Covid levels because a) more companies are still on WFH or hybrid work mode, particularly for BPOs, b) adoption of e-commerce (online shopping or food delivery)  is higher in the capital, and c) there is still greater residual wariness over Covid in Metro Manila compared to the provinces.

Workplace data likely tells us that employment is pretty much back to 2019 levels. This would also explain why the consumption figures are so strong despite the inflationary environment. Filipinos are used to periods of high inflation – 2011 and 2018 are the most recent episodes (inflation ran up to 6.7% in 2018). 

Diokno sees no need for spending cuts. Incoming Secretary of Finance and current BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno will not reduce spending to address the ballooning deficit. He will focus instead on raising enough taxes; He is confident of the tax system that the Duterte admin will leave. He believes recent tax reforms would provide the next administration with enough room to generate enough revenues.

The Bureau of the Treasury earlier estimated the government needs to raise P249bn every year in incremental revenues to avoid new borrowings. Finance Secretary Carlos G. Dominguez III has proposed a fiscal consolidation plan that involves imposing new tax measures, repealing some tax exemptions, and deferring the personal income tax reductions.

Marcos names himself agriculture chief. President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr. appointed himself agriculture secretary “at least for now”. He wants to review or restructure the Department of Agriculture; many sub-agencies have changed their function over the years. The incoming leader has pledged to increase farm output through loans and modern machinery. He also said that he wants to invest more for farmers by using funds collected from the rice tariffication law. Other presidents that assumed concurrent roles in the cabinet were Gloria Arroyo (Defense), Joseph Estrada (Interior), Ferdinand Marcos (Defense). Marcos Jr. also indicated that the Philippines must export processed metals instead of raw ore. He reiterated that mining should be “environmentally neutral”. Additionally, he favors targeted subsidies to sectors affected by fuel price hikes, instead of suspending oil excise tax. Marcos is yet to name his health, foreign affairs, transportation, energy, and environment secretaries.

SolGen Calida is COA chair under Marcos admin; PNB exec appointed to GSIS. President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has appointed outgoing Solicitor General Jose Calida as chairperson of the Commission on Audit under his administration, incoming press secretary Trixie Angeles announced Wednesday. Marcos’ camp also confirmed the appointment of Jose Arnulfo "Wick" Veloso as president and general manager of the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS). Veloso is the president and CEO of the Philippine National Bank, and also served as president of the Bankers Association of the Philippines. He was president of HSBC Philippines from 2012 to 2018.

 

Marcos appoints ex-PAL president Jaime Bautista as Secretary of Transportation. President-elect Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. picked former Philippine Airlines (PAL) president Jaime Bautista to lead the Department of Transportation (DoTr). Mr. Bautista spent four decades in various executive capacity in the Lucio Tan group. He spent 26 years in PAL; He retired in 2019. Other appointments under the DoTr include: Philippine Ports Authority General Manager: Christopher Pastrana (businessman, owner of Archipelago Philippine Ferries Corp). Undersecretary of railways: Cesar Chavez (career has been mostly in DoTr rail unit). LTFRB head: Cheloy Garavil (lawyer, currently serving as service director at the House committee on rules).

Marcos names new BIR chief. President-elect Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has tapped BSP Assistant Governor and former Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Deputy Commissioner Lillia Guillermo to head BIR. Ms. Guillermo heads the BSP Technology and Digital Innovation Office and is in charge of managing the BSP’s IT modernization roadmap. She was also credited with implementing the Philippines tax computerization project at the BIR and BOC.

Marcos eyes more PPP projects. The incoming admin will work to entice investors in the government’s infrastructure program through PPP according to the next head of the Dept of Public Works (DPWH) and currently SMC Tollways President/CEO Manuel Bonoan. In contrast to outgoing President Duterte, who steered clear of PPPs. DPWH will focus on food production and tourism programs and will reprioritize some projects in the pipeline to favor high-impact infra projects that will contribute immediately to the economic recovery.

More regions approve wage hikes. The government approved wage hikes for five (5) more regions over the past week. MIMAROPA (IV-B) wages hiked by 10-12% (to be implemented in two tranches). Bicol (V) wages hiked by 18% (to be implemented in two tranches). Central Visayas (VII) hiked by 8-9%. Northern Mindanao (X) hiked by 12-13% (to be implemented in two tranches). SOCCKSARGEN (XII) hiked by 10% (to be implemented in two tranches). These will add to recently approved wage hikes in NCR, Ilocos, Cagayan valley, and Caraga.

President-elect Marcos wants his priority programs in 2023 budget. President-elect Marcos instructed the Department of Budget and Management to ensure that his priority programs are in the 2023 budget. The proposed budget for 2023 is P5.268trn, equivalent to 22.1% of GDP. The priority programs inclue: 1. Agriculture and food security, 2. Climate change adaptation, 3. Economic recovery, 4. Improved health care and education, 5. Enhanced infrastructure projects, including digital infrastructure, 6. Utilization of renewable energy sources, 7. Strengthened tourism and jobs creation, 8. Sustainable development.
