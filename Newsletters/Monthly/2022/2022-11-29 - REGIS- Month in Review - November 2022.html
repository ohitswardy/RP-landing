REGIS REPORT

29 November 2022
 

The Philippines surged 10.2% in November, cementing further its 7.2% ascent in the previous month. The 4Q has been good to the Phils so far, allowing it to pare its its YTD decline to 4.8% from as much as 19.4% YTD at the close of 3Q.

As of month end, the Phils had risen for six straight weeks, propelled chiefly by foreign inflows. Net foreign flows finally turned inward as it has for EM, after an 8-month stretch of continued outflows.

A robust 3Q earnings season also helped lift risk appetite. PSEi earnings growth accelerated slightly to +42% YoY in 3Q22 from +40% in 2Q22.   For 9M22, PSEi earnings are up 34% YoY, well on track to exceed Regis estimates of +33% earnings growth for FY22.

Top line momentum continued to be strong for the consumer sector, albeit margin pressure persisted.  In contrast to the consumer names, banks saw higher margins, even as loans continued to reflect economic reopening.  Malls benefitted vastly from the removal of rental concessions set in place during the pandemic, while residential cancellations were less of an issue. However, mobile spend seemed to have felt the budget cuts brought on by high inflation.

Strong underlying demand was reflected in the country’s GDP, which expanded  7.6% in 3Q. That figure came in much higher than Street expectations (6.2%) and also accelerated from the +7.5% revised growth in 2Q. Private consumption was the main engine for growth.

Demand has been buoyant, notwithstanding the inflationary environment.  CPI inflation accelerated to 7.7% in October, overtaking consensus expectations. The rise was driven by food prices, which jumped by 1.5% MoM.

In a well-telegraphed move, the Bangko Sentral (BSP) raised policy rates by 75bp in November to 5.0%.  The hike was in step with the latest move by the Fed.  Policy rates have been raised by 300bp thus far so far, and are now higher than they were immediately before the pandemic (4.0%).

The BSP signaled that it is unlikely to implement a “jumbo” rate hike at its final Monetary Board policy meeting for the year. The meeting takes place on Dec 15, just after the  Fed’s FOMC meeting (Dec 14 in the US). The PHP appreciation gives the BSP less reason to match the magnitude of the Fed’s moves.  The PHP appreciated 2.7% in November, closing at PHP56.57/US$ in November.

Key data
 

Foreigners turned net buyers for the first time since February, with US$99m (net) coming in this month. Foreign buying was consistent throughout the month with the market registering net inflows in all but three sessions in November.

This month saw a $34m block of BPI go through where a large foreign holder was seen selling to a local.  Meanwhile, MSCI rebalancing at the end of the month registered US$38m in outflows – much lower than passive outflow estimates as foreign active funds were seen buying names marked down at the close. Year to date, net foreign outflow stood at US$1.137bn.

Foreign participation stood at 50%.

Index heavyweights BDO, Ayala Land, and SM Investments saw the biggest inflows on a mix of both passive and active buying.  Meanwhile, consumer large-caps Jollibee and Universal Robina were also top foreign bought.

Biggest outflows were seen in MSCI deletions Metro Pacific, GT Capital, and Converge – with almost all of the month’s outflows coming through on rebalancing day.

Meanwhile, ICTSI, this month’s third best performer, saw foreign profit taking late in the month.

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The 10.2% surge of the Philippines in November was propelled by gains across the board, with all but 3 index members ending the month with gains.

Large cap macro proxy SM (+14.5% MoM) was the top index mover, chipping in 128 index points (or 20.4%) to the market’s 627-point ascent in November. Large cap property names SM Prime (+15.6% MoM) and Ayala Land (+24.15% MoM) also helped pushed the index higher, by +96 and +88 index points, respectively.

Ayala’s strong surge came in the wake of a 12% climb in October. The company’s 3Q22 net profit doubled YoY, beating consensus expectations. Mall operators SM Prime (+16% MoM) and Robinsons Land (+8% MoM) also performed, with profits benefitting from the full removal of rental concessions on mall tenants in 3Q22. Megaworld (+7.7% MoM) also gained. 3Q22 reservation sales doubled as international buyers comprised 20% of 9M22 sales, from only 14% in 1H22. 

ICTSI (+18.5% MoM)  jumped after reporting another stellar quarter and contributed 61 index points or 9.7% to the PSEi’s total gain.  The market also appears to be gaining confidence on management’s positive yield and volume outlook.

The top index gainer was Converge (+25.5% MoM), recovering  after the stock hit lows earlier in the month.  The sector saw lackluster overall telco spend in 3Q, though PLDT (+7.2% MoM), whose profits came in slightly ahead in 3Q  managed to outperform Globe (+0.2% MoM) which reported in line results.

 

 

Banks closed up in November, as profits largely beat estimates behind loan growth acceleration and marked NIM expansion.  Prospects of further NIM improvement in line with higher interest rates boosted sentiment.  BPI (+11.5% MoM) advanced, as did Metrobank (+10.8% MoM). BDO (+2.2% MoM) also ended higher though at a more tempered pace after outperforming in the previous month with a 14.2% gain. Security Bank (+11.0% MoM) rose, with 9M profits trending well ahead of forecasts largely due to a large drop in provisioning.  Loan growth slowed during the  quarter and NIMs fell sequentially.

Meanwhile, Union Bank’s (+5.1% MoM) board approved a P20bn (US$345m) equity raising, to be done via a rights and/or private placement, after completing a P40bn (US$690m) rights

Sumitomo Mitsui Bank (SMBC) will take an additional 15% stake in Rizal Commercial Bank (+6.4% MoM).  SMCB will purchase 382m shares at P71/sh, taking their stake from 4.99% to 20%. This will take the bank’s CET-1 ratio from 11.9% to 15.9%.

 

Top line growth momentum across the consumer sector was strong during the quarter, fueled by the reopening. However, input cost inflation also continued to dampen margins and results across the sector came in mostly in line with expectations. Most names traded broadly in line with the index – Wilcon (+9.5% MoM), Universal Robina(+9.0% MoM), and Monde (+8.2% MoM). Meanwhile, Puregold (+18.3% MoM) outperformed after bouncing off the previous months’ lows. Results also came in above consensus expectations as operating leverage eclipsed cost pressure at S&R. 

Other macro proxies also gained, as risk appetite improved.  YTD laggards LT Group (+16.5% MoM), JG Summit (+13.8% MoM), and Alliance Global (+10.2% MoM) rose.  Ayala Corp (+4.4% MoM) also advanced.

GT capital (+6.35% MoM) likewise rose, following the recent strength of the PHP against the USD, spelling some margin relief.  Vehicle sales for the month of October jumped 34% YoY, with Toyota gaining market share and rising by 39% YoY.  

 

 

Meanwhile, Metro Pacific  (-5.5% MoM) was of the few index losers in November, moving in line with its largest asset, Meralco.

Meralco (-7.1% MoM) was the top index loser in November. Profit trends remained healthy up to the third quarter, buoyed by robust volumes (+7%) and a sharp turnaround at its genco in Singapore. MER was in and out of the news, however, due to its involvement in San Miguel Corp’s (+0.8% MoM)  petition for a temporary rate hike to cover losses. ERC rejected the joint petition, but San Miguel later on secured a TRO against the implementation. 

Meanwhile, ACEN (+13.8% MoM) gained. Its 3Q recovered 9% QoQ despite a shutdown at one of its main plants. It also finalized the technical divestment from its 244-MW coal plant, which management hopes would attract more ESG investors.

 

Semirara (-3.7% MoM) retreated further, even after correcting 10% in October. Regional thermal coal prices were volatile during the month, swinging to a low of US$320 per MT from a high of US$390 per MT. 

Manila Water (-32.2% MoM) surged after securing a +70% tariff hike from the water regulator, after several years of deferment.  The tariff increase will be implemented over a five-year period and will have huge implications on next year’s earnings.  

 

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

3Q22 GDP growth surprises, accelerating to 7.6%. 3Q22 GDP rose 7.6% YoY, slightly faster than the revised +7.5% (7.4% initially) in 2Q22. This beat Bloomberg consensus median estimate of +6.1%. To achieve the government’s FY22E GDP target of +6.5 to +7.5%, 4Q22 GDP should grow by +3.3% to +6.9%. The demand-side drivers were private consumption, investments, and moderating net imports due to further easing of mobility and the (partial) resumption of in-person classes. On capital formation, inventory build-up (i.e. change in stocks) remained evident, as it grew at a faster pace in 3Q22 vs. 2Q22. The net import growth slowed to 32% and partly lifted overall GDP. On the supply side, the service sector sustained momentum, +9.1% YoY.

Inflation quickens to near 14-year high in October at 7.7% on rising food prices, transport fare hikes. Headline inflation accelerated to 7.7% in October from +6.9% in September. This is the fastest pace since December 2008 and disappointed relative to consensus: +7.1%; Came in at the high-end of the BSP’s target range: 7.1%-7.9%. On a MoM basis, headline CPI jumped +0.9%, fastest sequential increase since June 2022. Food rose +1.5% MoM, driven by vegetables (+12%), corn (+3%), and sugar (+4%); Note that there was a big typhoon (“Karding”) in late-Sep. Transport services rose by 2% MoM due to nationwide transport fare hike. These items more than offset 3% MoM decline in fuel-related category of “operation of transport equipment” and the mild deceleration in electricity prices.

Meanwhile, core inflation rate accelerated to +5.9% in October, vs. a revised +5% in September. The YT-October average inflation rate now at 5.4%. To reach Regis’ FY22E ave. inflation rate forecast of +5.8%, CPI for Nov/Dec should rise by +0.9% MoM, which translates to an average of +8.1% YoY. To reach the BSP’s FY22E target of +5.7%, CPI for Nov/Dec should rise by 0.5% MoM, which translates to an average of +7.6% YoY.

 

Monetary Board raises key rates by 75bps.The BSP raised the interest rate on the overnight reverse repurchase facility by 75bps to 5.0%. This brings total YTD rate hike to 300bps and within street expectations. The move followed the 75-bp hike of the Federal Reserve at its meeting early this month, which took its policy rate to 3.75%. The BSP tweaked its average inflation target for 2022 to 5.8% from 5.7% previously. The average inflation rate YT-October is at 5.4%. This implies that inflation for Nov/Dec will rise by an average of +8.1% YoY. It also raised its average inflation targets for 2023/2024 to 4.3%/3.1% from 4.1%/3%.

BSP Governor rules out jumbo rate hikes. BSP Governor Felipe Medalla ruled out the possibility of another jumbo rate hike, as he expects the US Federal Reserve to slow the pace of tightening in December. If the Fed will hike by 50 bps, Mr. Medalla said the BSP cannot afford to keep rates unchanged. The Monetary Board will be meeting on December 15; Fed meeting on Dec 14. The US central bank raised rates by 375 bps since March while the BSP has raised rates by 300bps.

Remittances rise 3.8% YoY, 4.4% MoM in September. Remittances coursed through banks in September stood at US$2.84bn, up 3.8% YoY, 4.4% MoM. In PHP terms, remittances grew 18.9% YoY and the 3-month ave growth rate was at +3.4%. The main drivers were remittances from Asia (+5%), Middle East (+9%), and USA (+1%). This brings 9M22 remittances to US$23.8bn, up 3.1% YoY. To reach the BSP’s target of US$32.7bn (+4% YoY), remittances should average US$2.95bn per month for the rest of the year.

 

 

Dollar reserves rise by 1.2% MoM to US$94bn in October. Preliminary data from the BSP showed gross international reserves (GIR) reached US$94.1bn in October, a US$1.07bn (+1.2%) increase MoM. The government cited proceeds from the issuance of ROP Global Bonds, and upward valuation adjustments in foreign currency-denominated reserves (or non-gold reserves). This represents 7.5 months’ worth of imports and 6.7x the country’s short-term external debt. The BSP is targeting an end-2022 GIR level of US$99bn.

Trade deficit shrinks MoM to US$4.8bn in September; Exports rebound +12% MoM. The country’s trade deficit narrowed to US$4.8bn in September from the peak of US$6.0bn in August based on preliminary data from the Philippine Statistics Authority. The trade deficit grew at a much slower pace of +27% YoY during the month due to a strong rebound in exports (+12% MoM, +7% YoY); Imports also grew at a slower pace of +14% YoY and were 4% lower MoM. Import growth decelerated due to slower growth in fuel imports and lower raw material imports. Meanwhile, consumer goods imports accelerated to +29% while capital goods imports grew at a steady pace of +11%. Exports rebounded after two consecutive months of YoY declines due to faster growth in electronic exports. By country, exports to HK jumped +16% YoY (vs. YTD: +1.3%). This offset sustained softness in exports to China (-14% YoY in Sep vs -7% YTD) and USA (+2.5% YoY in Sep vs +3.7% YTD). The YT-September trade deficit now at US$46.65bn, +63% YoY.

Government’s debt stock rises to P13.517tn in September.The National Government’s outstanding debt rose further to P13.517tn in September. This is P496bn (+3.8%) higher MoM and P1.79tn (+15.2%) higher than in end-Dec 2021. This is also the fastest MoM increase this year due to the peso’s depreciation against the USD and the net issuance of govt securities to support the budget. Domestic debt accounted for 68.8% of total at P9.3tn (+4% MoM, +13.8% higher than in end-Dec 2021). The government cited net issuance of govt securities (+P352.09bn MoM) and the impact of the local currency depreciation against the USD (+5.18bn). The external debt stood at P4.216tn (+3.4% MoM, +18.5% higher than in end-Dec 2021) due to the P180bn impact of the local currency depreciation against the USD, which was partially offset by the P31bn effect of third-currency depreciation against the USD and net repayment of P10.8bn. In USD terms, external loans stood at US$71.896bn, lower by 1% MoM). The government is targeting the debt-to-GDP ratio to peak at 62% in 2022 and gradually fall to 51% by 2028.

 

Gov’t raises P93bn from sale of T-bonds. The Bureau of Treasury partially awarded P27.597bn worth of original issue of 20-year T-bonds on offer during the last auction for November. Effectively bringing total borrowings for T-bonds to P93.3bn, about 67% of the P140bn program for the month. In the last auction, the 20-year T-bond fetched an average rate of 8.012%, 20bps above the 7.81% BVAL reference rate. The yield was lower than the 8.168% last week. The demand for the securities attracted P70.36bn, oversubscribing the auction by 2.01x.

Senate approves 2023 budget bill. The Senate voted unanimously to approve the 2023 proposed budget of P5.27tn (+5% higher than the 2022 budget). Senators realigned P170mn worth of agencies’ intelligence funds. Outside of this, it appears that the bills passed in Senate and the Lower House are not too different from the Department of Budget and Management’s proposal. The ratified version of the reconciled bill be up for the President’s approval by mid-December or “before Christmas”.

Bank lending growth picks up to 13.4% YoY in September.Outstanding loans by U/KBs, net of reverse repurchase (RRP) placements with the BSP, rose by 13.4% YoY in September. This is faster than August’s 12.2% and fastest pace in 27 months. The growth was fueled by loans to the following sectors: real estate (+16%), manufacturing (+16%), trade (+11%). Consumer loans sustained momentum, rising by 21% YoY, with both credit card (+26%) and auto loans (+4%) accelerating. Recall that loan growth for the entire banking sector jumped by 9.6% in August. U/KBs loans figure is distorted by the merger of BPI with BPI Family Savings Bank, whose loans were recorded in the thrift bank before Jan 2022. Regis is forecasting loans to grow 12-15% for banks under our coverage in 2022E-24E. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity growth (M3) decelerated 5.0% in September (from 6.7% in August).

Budget deficit widens in October. The National Government’s budget deficit ballooned to P99.1bn in October from P64bn in October 2021. On a MoM basis, the budget deficit was lower by 45% due to a sharp drop in expenditures. Tax revenues jumped 19% YoY, bringing 10M22 tax revenues up 18% YoY (trending ahead of the government’s target of +13.5% YoY). Expenses in October rose 22% YoY, as primary expenditures (i.e. excluding interest payments) jumped 24% YoY. 10M22 budget deficit at P1.11tn, down by 7.6% YoY and trending lower than the government’s target. For 2023, the government expects the deficit to GDP ratio to be lower at 6.1% from 7.6% in FY22 and 8.6% in 2021

 

 

 

Vehicle sales dip by 9% MoM in October. CAMPI/TMA reported vehicle sales stood at 32,146 units in October, 9% lower than in September 2022. This is higher by 42% YoY. Note that CAMPI/TMA began including Chinese-brand Cherry in September 2022.

This brings YT-October sales to 278,961 units, up 30% YoY. Toyota sold 140,425 units (+36% YoY) in YT-October, trending above Toyota’s FY22E of 162k (+25% YoY) and Regis’ FY22E of 158k (+22%). This also implies October retail sales were at 15,541 units, up 39% YoY but down 4% MoM.

Toyota’s market share improved slightly to 48.3% in October from 45.7% in September. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 55% discount to its market-based NAV of P908/sh.

SIM Registration ACT effective on December 27. The SIM Registration is expected to be made effective on December 27. According to Senator Grace Po during a hearing, the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) is expected to issue the implementing rules and regulations for the SIM Registration Act on December 12. By then, new SIM cards will have to be registered before they are activated. Existing SIM users will have to register within 180 days (with possible 120 days extension).

 

163 BPO firms endorsed by PEZA to BOI. PEZA has endorsed 163 BPO firms for registration to the Board of Investments (BOI). The Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) allowed PEZA-registered firms to transfer their registration to the Board of Investments (BOI), which does not have restrictions on work-from-home (WFH). There are about 1,300 BPO firms registered with PEZA. PEZA-registered firms have until 31 December 2022 to transfer their registration from PEZA to BOI. So far, it seems that only a minority of BPO firms will be able to transfer their registration to BOI.
