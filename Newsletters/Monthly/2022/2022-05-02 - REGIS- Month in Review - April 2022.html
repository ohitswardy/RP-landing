REGIS REPORT

02 May 2022
 

The Philippines plunged 6.6% in April to 6,731.25, marking a third straight month of declines. Anticipation of the Fed’s tapering plans, as well as continued geopolitical tensions, elevated commodity prices, and the impact of the continued COVID lockdown in China weighed on sentiment. 

Investors were reluctant to participate as well, opting to stay largely sidelined with the national elections just around the corner.  Volumes dried up in April, with average daily turnover a mere US$94m.  The lack of bids in the market led to steep moves. Declines were across the board – with only three of 30 index members managing to end the month with gains. 

YTD, the Philippines lags the region (ASEAN) as its 7.9% decline (USD adjusted) is well below the 8.0% gain of Jakarta and the 4.8% advance of Singapore.  It also lags Malaysia (-2.0% YTD) and Thailand (-2.2% YTD). 

Inflation overshot expectations in March, coming in at 4.0% and  higher than the 3% print in February.  Inflation has yet to peak, as the spike in prices of wheat-based products has yet to be seen in the CPI even as transport fares have not been raised either despite record retail pump prices. 

The central bank itself is expecting inflation to breach its 2% to 4% band in 2H. Governor Diokno has previously signaled an increase in policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% as being “reasonable”. He has also recently said that the BSP could raise policy rates in June if GDP grows by 6-7% in 1Q, suggesting increasing likelihood of policy rate hikes ahead of 2H.

Meanwhile, the trade deficit for the month of February came in at US$3.5bn, higher by 30% YoY. The cumulative trade deficit for the first two months is now US$8.2bn, 48% higher YoY. The risk from the trade deficit and hence the current account is clearly rising and is likely to deteriorate in the near term. This will have consequences on the currency, among other things. 

The interest rate, currency, and inflation headwinds are muting the recovery story which has played out in various data points and the ongoing 1Q corporate earnings season – at least in the near-term. Banks are seeing a return in loan growth (~5-7% YoY for the Big Three), domestic passenger volumes are close to 80% of pre-pandemic levels, and foot traffic and tenant sales at malls is already at 80-85% of pre-pandemic levels for SM Prime. Consumer companies are also seeing robust topline recovery.

MORE ON FLOWS...

Foreign outflows remained elevated in April despite anemic volumes, with another US$99m exiting in April. This brought YTD foreign outflows to US$219m. Foreign selling would have been larger this month at US$154.8m had it not been for an Ayala Land REIT overnight placement which clocked in US$55.8m inflows.  

Biggest outflows were spread across index heavyweights, indicating that the selling was not stock-specific and was rather general Philippine de-risking. Conglomerates SM Investments and Ayala Corp. were among top sold, alongside property names Ayala Land and SM Prime. Consumer food group Universal Robina was also top foreign sold for a third straight month on concerns over rising input costs and weakening consumer spending power amid rising inflation. Banks also saw heavy outflows this despite strong 1Q earnings from BPI and Metrobank.

Biggest inflows was seen in Converge, which we think is partly due to speculation of a possible addition into the MSCI. ICTSI saw decent inflows for a second consecutive month after a strong FY21 beat and as management indicated strong trends in early 2022.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

The PSEi’s drop in April was widespread, with 27 of the 30 index members recording declining for the month.  Large caps weighed heavily on the index, with conglos, property, consumer and banks all pushing the index lower.

SM Investments (-6.3% MoM) and property unit SM Prime (-7.5% MoM) dragged the index down.  SM Prime declined despite reporting an upward trajectory for its malls which are seeing foot traffic of more than 80% of pre-pandemic levels.  Management reckons revenue should be 80-90% of pre-pandemic by the end of this year.

During the month, SM Investments announced its acquisition of 81% of geothermal company Philippine Geothermal Production Company  that operates the Tiwi and Makban geothermal fields in exchange for 17.4m new shares (1.4% of capital). The notional value of the deal is  P15.72 bn (US$ 300m) is likely to be EPS accretive and should provide some carbon offsets for the listed company.

Also weighing heavily on the index were conglos Aboitiz Equity Ventures(-15.0% MoM) and Ayala Corp (-10.5% MoM) and property unit Ayala Land (-8.6% MoM).  Ayala Corp is distributing part of its stake in power arm AC Energy (-17.3% MoM) as a property dividend. Holders of each AC share will receive 3 shares of ACEN.  A total of 1.86bn ACEN shares (equivalent to 4.9% of outstanding shares) will be distributed. The exercise should provide a modest value boost to AC, whose shares are trading at considerable discount to NAV. It will also raise ACEN’s public float to above 20%, allowing it to meet PSEi requirements.  At current levels, the distribution is equivalent to 3% dividend yield.

Ayala Land (-8.6% MoM) meanwhile raised P3.5bn (US$70m) after selling another 87m shares of AREIT (-13.3% MoM) at P39.70/share.  Ayala Land will receive 252m new AREIT shares in exchange for six Cebu office buildings. RL Commercial REIT (-0.7% MoM) also completed its own transaction with Robinsons Land (-6.7% MoM), acquiring 55,000 sqm of offices from the latter.

Sentiment for the office segment continued to be weak. The country’s largest BPO firm is set to continue implementing work-from-home policies, thus foregoing the tax incentives associated with PEZA. The persistence of WFH arrangements would have implications on the property sector as it would delay the resumption of demand for office space. Megaworld (-5.0% MoM) and Filinvest Land (-2.8% MoM) fell as well.

Banks were sold down amidst elevated risk aversion. Among the big three banks, Metrobank (-10.4% MoM) saw the steepest decline. However, both BPI (-4.7% MoM) and BDO (-2.0% MoM) also fell.  The declines came amidst signs of economic recovery for the banks as evidenced by their 1Q earnings. BDO and BPI’s 1Q  revealed similar trends in terms of loan growth.  BDO saw credit growth accelerate slightly to 7.5% while BPI’s expanded 7.1%.  Both saw asset quality improve as well. Margins trends were slightly mixed, however. Metrobank likewise saw loan growth, at a clip of 5% in 1Q.

Union Bank (-16.7% MoM) declined as well, as did Security Bank (-5.5% MoM). RCBC (-2.4% MoM) also closed lower. The bank is planning to remove its 40% foreign ownership limit, presumably to allow new equity from foreign entities.  The bank has two existing foreign shareholders – Cathay Life (22.2%) and Sumitomo (4.99%). 

 

The consumer sector was not spared from the sell-off, with cost input inflation marring topline recovery.  Strong revenue growth (+22% YoY) was evident in Universal Robina’s (-15.3% MoM) 1Q22 result.  This helped mute the impact of EBIT margin compression on the back of the surge in input costs. Higher input costs meanwhile led to EBITDA margin contraction (c100bps) for Century Pacific (-4.3% MoM) in 4Q21.  Revenue growth was healthy however, at +19% YoY.

Emperador (+39.1% MoM) surged, after declining 18.7% in Feb and 27.4% in March. Emperador disclosed that it obtained a conditional eligibility-to-list for its proposed secondary listing on the Singapore Exchange from SGX-ST.  EMP’s net FY21 net income increased 25% YoY helped by a sequential improvement in profits from brandy which saw a resurgence in revenues (+46% QoQ) in 4Q. 

Retailers’ topline also reflected economic reopening. For Puregold (-7.7% MoM), 4Q21 SSSG improved sequentially across its brands as mobility restrictions eased.  Margins on a YoY basis appeared weak, though 4Q20 was a high base due to proportionately higher supplier support. Wilcon’s (+3.7% MoM) SSSG (+8.6%) for 1Q was healthy despite the Omicron surge in January. Strong margins boosted Wilcon’s profits for the quarter. Meanwhile, revenues for Philippine Seven (+3.6% MoM) grew strongly in 4Q21 (+33% QoQ / +14% YoY). SSSG accelerated to 8.5% as mobility restrictions eased. 

Shakey's Pizza (-1.9% MoM) rose after EBITDA margins in 4Q surprised to the upside as efforts to rationalize costs have begun to bear fruit. PIZZA reported 4Q21 net profit of P156mn, a turnaround from breakeven levels in the same period last year. Revenues rose 17% YoY; SSSG was at 5%.

Telcos were mixed. PLDT (+0.9% MoM) was among the few index gainers this month after announcing the sale of its  towers in a value accretive deal.  The telco is selling close to 6,000 of its telecom towers to two separate groups for P77bn (US$1.5bn). The deal will come with leaseback arrangements allowing PLDT to continue using those assets. Regis estimates value accretion (after tax) of as much as P300 per share for PLDT from this deal.

Meanwhile, PLDT’s  fintech arm Voyager raised US$210m in new funding. The latest funding round was completed at a valuation of US$1.4bn. Voyager is the holding company for e-wallet operator PayMaya and digital bank Maya Bank. Leading the new round of investors is SIG Venture Capital, with two other investors EDBI and First Pacific (142 HK) also coming in.

Converge (-4.6% MoM) and Globe (-10.6% MoM) did not fare as well. Globe announced the completion of its deal to sell its data center operations for which it will reportedly book a pretax gain of P10.5bn (US$202m). The post-money valuation of US$350m and the US$100m of proceeds that Globe will receive post-deal implies that Globe’s data center business was being valued at US$275m.

 

 

 

Utilities declined across the board after recent outperformance.  Aboitiz Power (-10.7% MoM) fell, giving up some of its earlier gains. Earnings for 1Q22 came in lower at P2.9bn (-53%YoY) but the declines looked worse than the actual underlying operations because of insurance claims booked last year.

Meralco (-5.7% MoM) likewise fell, despite 1Q results coming in within expectations and electricity sales rising 5.7%.  Meralco is seeking a distribution tariff of P1.57/kwh for its next regulatory period (Fifth Regulatory Period or 5RP).  While the regulator is likely to slash this number, a final tariff close to the current one (P1.38/kwh) could provide significant upside to current earnings (>25%). Despite the decline, the stock is still up 19.4% YTD.

Another stock that is up even more YTD despite an April decline is Semirara (-10.4% MoM), amassing a gain of 28.8% YTD as coal prices have soared.  Profit taking took the stock lower in April, however.

 

AC Energy meanwhile is venturing across the Pacific with its plan to enter the U.S. wind power market.  This venture will be with its frequent partner UPC and will add to its pipeline of projects. It has partnered with German solar developer ib vogt to build at least 1,000MW of renewable energy projects in Asia Pacific.

Transport stocks declined. ICTSI (-4.1% MoM) corrected, as did Cebu Air (-8.6% MoM), as the headwinds of elevated oil prices overshaded the strong bounce in passenger traffic and CEB’s airline capacity.

Other conglos declined as well.  LT Group (-4.3% MoM) dropped despite cigarette volumes in 1Q22 rising 18% to 9.6bn sticks.  AGI (-5.6% MoM) also dropped, despite FY21 profits coming in ahead of estimates, helped by tax benefits booked by Megaworld and the outperformance of Emperador’s brandy unit in 4Q.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation accelerates to 4.0% in March. Headline inflation rate rose to 4.0% in March from 3.0% the previous month. The street forecast ranged between +3.3% to +4.4% (median at +3.7%). BSP forecast ranged between +3.3% to +4.1%. Food inflation rate accelerated to +2.6%, with notable increases in corn (+31.3%, unch MoM), flour/bread (+3.5% from +3.1%), meat (+2.9% from +1.4%), and fish (+4.3% from +2.9%). Non-food inflation rate jumped to 5.0%, largely due to higher oil prices. Prices for utilities jumped +17% from +13% in Feb. The 1Q22 average inflation rate at 3.4%, within the BSP’s 2-4% target range. Policy interventions to manage supply and prices include: extension of lower tariffs on pork until Dec 2022, accelerating release of imported port and cold storages, fuel subsidies of P3k each to 158k corn farmers and fisherfolk / P6.5k fuel subsidy for 115k public utility drivers, cash transfers (P500 per month) to poorest 50% of households.

Remittances hit 3-month low in February. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 1.3% YoY in February to US$2.5bn. This is the lowest level of absolute remittances since November. The 3month moving average growth rate went down to +2.4% from 3.6% the previous month. In Peso terms, remittances in Feb grew +2.8% YoY. The government cited reimposition of restrictions in host countries and the Philippines amid a resurgence in COVID cases across the globe. Most land-based remittances sources posted declines, except for the USA which grew +4% YoY. This brings YT-Feb remittances to US$5.2bn, up 1.9% YoY. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by +4% YoY, suggesting that monthly remittances should average US$2.75bn for the rest of the year.

Trade deficit balloons to US$3.5bn in Feb. The country’s trade in goods balance stood at a US$3.5bn deficit in February, up 30% YoY but down from US$4.7bn in January. The YoY increase was due to higher imports of fuel (+132%) and sustained recoveries in consumer goods, capital goods, and raw/intermediate goods imports. Total trade (exports + imports) rose +18% YoY, slightly slower than in January (+20% YoY). This brings YT-Feb trade deficit to US$8.25bn, up 48% YoY. For 2022, the BSP is forecasting trade in goods imports to rise by +15% YoY and exports to rise by +7% YoY.

 

Gov’t revises 2020/2021 GDP growth numbers. The government revised annual 2021/2020 GDP estimates by industry and expenditure. 4Q21 GDP growth rate was revised upwards to 7.8% from 7.7% previously. Consequently, 2021 annual GDP growth rate was revised upwards to 5.7% from 5.6% previously.

BSP may consider rate hike in June. BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno said the monetary board may consider raising its key interest rate in June. The decision will depend on 1Q22 GDP growth data. If GDP grows 6-7%, may consider raising rates in June meeting. The BSP has maintained that rate increase isn’t the appropriate tool to address supply-driven price pressures. The Philippines can afford to wait; there is no evidence of second round effect on the demand side. Regis is forecasting key policy rates to rise to 2.5% by year-end from 2% currently.

Hot money inflows surge to US$274mn in February. Foreign portfolio investments (i.e. hot money) recorded a net inflow of US$274mn in February, up from a US$15mn in net inflow in Jan 2022 and a reversal of the US$40mn net outflow in Feb 2021. The US$945mn registered investments for February 2022 reflected an increase of 29% MoM (or by US$213mn) compared to the US$731mn recorded in January 2022, largely in PSE-listed securities. This brings YT-Feb foreign portfolio investments yielded net inflows of US$289m, larger than the US$57mn net inflows in the same period last year.

BOP reverses to a surplus in March. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$754mn in March. This reversed the deficits in Jan/Feb 2022 (c. US$130mn per month) and in March 2021 (US$73mn). The BSP cited inflows from the government’s US$2.25bn triple tranche US dollar-denominated bond offering. This brings 1Q22 BOP to a surplus of US$495mn, a turnaround from the US$2.84mn deficit in 1Q21. The BSP is targeting a BOP deficit of US$4.3bn by year-end, reversing from a US$1.345bn surplus in 2021.

 

GIR rises in March. Based on preliminary data, the country’s gross international reserves (GIR) rose to US$108.54bn in end-March. This is up by US$740mn (+0.7%) MoM from a revised end-Feb GIR level of US$107.8bn (previously US$107.9bn). This is equivalent to 9.6 months’ worth of imports. The increase reflected proceeds of ROP Global bonds.

BSP sees inflation breaching target in 2H. Inflation could breach the BSP’s 2-4% target in the 2H due to the surge in global oil prices but does not see this breaching 5%.  It expects inflation to gradually decelerate back to within the target range in the 1H23, according to BSP Governor Benjamin Diokno. 

Near-term, BSP Governor Diokno prefers to address elevated inflation in the coming months thru “non-monetary” measures. The BSP has not seen clear signs of second-round effects. The economy is expected to operate below capacity near term even as the recovery gains traction.

Given the potential broadening of price pressures over the near term, the BSP stands ready to deploy timely and appropriate monetary policy measures, in line with its price and financial stability mandate. Governor Diokno previously signaled that an increase in policy rates to 2.5% - 2.75% as being “reasonable”. BSP’s next policy meeting is on May 19.

Budget deficit narrows in March, as revenues pick up pace. Data from the Bureau of Treasury showed the budget deficit shrink by 2% YoY to P187.7bn in March. Revenues picked up pace, with tax revenues up 29% YoY while non-tax revenues surged 88% YoY due to higher dividends from state-run firms. Primary spending also reversed the slight decline in 2M22, rising by 18% YoY in March. The government cited release of funds for scholarship programs and capital outlay projects of the Dept. of Public Works and Dept. of National Defense. This brings 1Q22 budget deficit to P317bn, down 1.4% YoY.

 

Infrastructure spending drops in 2M22. Government disbursements on infrastructure (excluding LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) declined by 16% YoY to P90bn in 2M22. The drop was attributed to timing of payables for completed projects of the following agencies: agriculture, public works, transportation, and defense. The government did not provide similar figures for overall infrastructure spending (i.e. including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies). The Department of Budget and Management expects overall expenditures to improve in March but expects a slowdown in 2Q22 due to election ban. For FY22, the government is aiming to spend P1.27tn (+13% YoY) on overall infrastructure (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies), or 5.9% of GDP.

NPL ratio hits 3-month high in February. The NPL ratio of the banking industry rose to 4.24% in February coming from 4.14% in January 2022 and 4.08% in February 2021. Bad loans grew 2.4% MoM (9.6% YoY) to P472.664bn. The gross loan portfolio was flat MoM (+5.4% YoY) at P11.15trn. Past due loans rose 1.2% YoY to P558.0bn. The past due ratio was 5% of total loans (from 5.2% a year ago). 

Vehicle sales post strong rebound in March. Industry car sales, as reported by CAMPI/TMA, jumped +43% YoY, +22% MoM to 29,685 units. This brings 1Q22 car sales to 74,754 units, up 6% YoY. March performance has reached pre-pandemic level of sales; it is the highest monthly sales recorded since the start of the pandemic. Toyota sold 15,110 units (+64% YoY, +23% MoM) and their market share gained to 50.9% from 48% in Dec. Additionally, this brings 1Q22 sales to 37,231 units, up 12% YoY. To reach Toyota’s year-end target of 162k units (+25% YoY), Toyota would need to sell 13,900 units per month for the rest of the year. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for c. 20% of GTCAP’s profit/NAV. GTCAP is trading at a 21% discount to its market-based NAV of P650/sh and 7.4x FY22E consensus PER.

 

 

Domestic pax volumes in NAIA reverts to 78% of pre-pandemic levels in March.Domestic passenger traffic at NAIA jumped sharply to 1.5mn in March, reverting to 78% of pre-pandemic levels from 49% in Feb 2022 and 20% in the same period last year. This brings 1Q22 domestic pax volumes up 215% YoY to 3.17mn or 55% of pre-Covid pax vols. Meanwhile, international pax volumes rose 55% MoM and 186% YoY to 489k, or about 23% of pre-pandemic levels. 1Q22  international pax volumes doubled YoY to 1.03mn pax or 16% of pre-pandemic levels.

BIR says 5pct franchise tax due from PAGCOR licensees. The Philippines Bureau of Internal Revenue filed on Wednesday a clarification that it expects licensees and contractees of the country’s casino regulator, the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) to pay 5 percent franchise tax to the BIR on their income from operations. BIR said the clarification on the casino industry’s liabilities regarding franchise tax, income tax, and VAT was based on current laws and recent jurisprudence. The circular added that “the regulatory fees paid by contractees to PAGCOR are different and distinct from the 5 percent franchise tax payable to the BIR”. In 2014, Bloomberry petitioned the Supreme Court to nullify the provision of a 2013 BIR Memorandum that imposed corporate income tax on casinos. The High Court affirmed that Bloomberry, as a contracting party of PAGCOR was subject only to the 5% franchise tax on gross gaming revenue, in lieu of all taxes, as provided under the PAGCOR Charter. Bloomberry then said that the decision allowed PAGCOR and BRHI to revert to the original license fee structure of 15% for junket and 25% for mass licensee fee (inclusive of the 5% franchise tax).

 

Government lowers vaccination targets. The government lowered its end-June vaccination target to 77mn-80mn Filipinos from previous target of 90mn. The government also previously intended to fully inoculate 77mn Filipinos by end-1Q22. 

As of April 19, 67.1mn Filipinos (61% of the population) have been fully vaccinated against COVID while 12.7mn have received booster doses. The Philippines only administered 6.7mn doses in March (8.7mn doses were administered in February, 18.2mn jabs in January). 

NCR, over 60 areas under Alert Level 1 until April 30. NCR will remain under Alert Level 1 from April 16 to April 30. Over 60 areas will also be under Alert Level 1. Under Alert Level 1, all establishments, persons, activities are allowed to operate at full capacity. Meanwhile, 44 areas are under Alert Level 2 until April 30. Under Alert Level 2, all establishments, persons, activities are allowed to operate at 50% capacity indoors, 70% outdoors.

Marcos keeps lead, Robredo gains in March 2022 survey. Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. kept his lead in the 2022 Presidential race while closest rival, Leni Robredo, gained 9points in the latest Pulse Asia survey. The survey had a sample of 2,400 respondents and was held from March 17-21. Robredo gained points in all locations, except NCR, where she lost 1pt; Gain attributed to ground campaigns. Marcos dropped 4pts from the Feb 2022 poll; While he kept his lead in Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao, Marcos dropped points in NCR (-2), Luzon ex-NCR (-4), Visayas (-5) and Mindanao (-6). Moreno (-2pts) and Pacquiao (-2pts) also dropped points. Robredo gained points in all locations, except NCR, where she lost 1pt; Gain attributed to ground campaigns. In the VP race, Sara Duterte strengthened her lead, gaining +3pts.
