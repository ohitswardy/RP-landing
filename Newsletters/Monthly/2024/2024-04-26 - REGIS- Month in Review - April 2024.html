REGIS REPORT

26 April 2024
 

The PSEi turned lower for the month as risk assets across the globe faced headwinds from the Fed’s hawkish tilt. Amid signs of strength in the US economy, an above-estimate CPI print, and subsequent hawkish rhetoric by the FOMC, Fed funds are now only pricing in a single projected rate cut for the year, which marks a stark shift from the initial optimism by several analysts of up to three or more rate cuts at the start of the year. On the ground, the BSP Governor also struck a hawkish tone and indicated that the base case for them is to start cutting rates as far back as 4Q to 1Q2025.

The latest CPI reading at home showed that inflation picked up to 3.7% in March from 3.4% in February, driven primarily by food inflation which hit a 4-month high of 5.6%. The headline CPI reading however was still slightly below the 3.8% consensus forecast and well within the BSP’s 3.4%-4.2% forecast range. This brought the 1Q24 inflation rate to 3.3% vs BSP’s FY24 estimate of 3.6% and Regis' 4.6%.

Another key area of concern for policymakers and potentially a significant factor that may delay monetary policy easing is the sharp weakness in the PHP which touched 57.8 vs the USD during the month, its weakest level since 2022, and ultimately fell by -2.72% MoM, marking its biggest monthly swing since August. 

Early on, the index started the month on a positive note – it set a new 52-week high of 6979 on the first day of April, then tested the psychological 7000-mark intraday during the next session. That proved to be the peak however with shares tumbling through the next three weeks. At its bottom, the index hit as low as the 6400-level within the latter half of the month, dragged down by a barrage of outflows which had extended for 14 consecutive days and nearly wiped out our market’s YTD inflow. Shares however managed to find some signs of relief from there, supported first by evident bargain hunting after the benchmark hit oversold levels, particularly into value plays such as ACEN which capped the month +7.79 MoM, and then tracking the widespread rebound across broader Asian equities.

Investors are also looking at the start of 1Q earnings season to provide a fresh set of market catalysts. Early reporters so far include BPI (+7.96% MoM) which led the few gainers in the benchmark this month. The PSEi ultimately closed the month of April at 6700.49 -2.94% MoM, substantially above its lowest point for the month of 6443 -6.67% on April 19. This monthly decline trimmed the index's YTD gain down to +3.88%.

Key data
 
 

Headline turnover for April came in lighter at $116m vs $124m the month prior. Excluding big blocks, average turnover was lethargic at $93m. Nevertheless, it was a volatile month for the PSEi, with the absence of liquidity resulting in sharp movements. The index swung  from 7,070 to a low of  6,400, to settle at 6,700 (-2.9% MoM) come month end. Foreigners were on a selling spree this April and net outflow stood at -$414m, the highest since September of last year. A big portion of the outflow was due to a block in BLOOM (-$291m) but overall, foreigners were a better seller of the market.

Foreign participation fell this month to 46% vs 50% the month prior.

  • Foreign selling accelerated this month as sentiment turned risk-off on the back of the hawkish outlook by the FED and the geopolitical tension in the middle east. Foreigners were generally sellers  this April pushing the index to revisit 6,400 key support levels, but turned better buyers towards month end, opportunistically buying the battered names.
  • Property names saw heavy outflows, and both ALI and SMPH were are the top foreign sold stocks this April. Sentiment towards the sector continue to be negative, on the possibility of fewer rate cuts this year and last year’s slow down in pre-sales numbers. Consumer names also saw sizeable outflows with inflation not improving. URC is now net foreign sold for the 13th straight month and is back trading near pandemic lows.
  • Banks continued to see foreign demand and finished strong with the high interest rate environment favorable to the sector. MBT tops this month’s net foreign bought name with an inflow of $25.5m,  BDO and BPI meanwhile both printed new all time highs this month.
  • Conglos finish the month two-way and foreign demand stayed with market proxy SM, switching out from AC. And despite the strong performance of MBT, parent GTCAP fell -9.2% on the back of foreign selling as a depreciating PHP/appreciating USD poses risks to Toyota’s earning.
  • Notable names for the month: Retail favorite SSI surged +13.4% and saw some foreign interest. Continued foreign buying was seen in ICT ($+16m) now for the 3rd straight month.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Index laggards outnumbered winners this month, 21-9. Among the worst performing stocks in the PSEi were rate sensitive developers SMPH -14.53% MoM and ALI -10.85%, as investors priced in the hawkish monetary policy. Investors also digested the latest 1Q2024 industry data from the Leechiu Property Consultants’ briefing held earlier in the month which showed that office vacancy stayed flat at 18% during the quarter and that rental rates still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Other key highlights of the briefing: BPOs still led the demand, accounting for the 66k sqm of the 184k sqm total take-up in 1Q. While in residential, they cited a -29% QoQ and -42% YoY drop in sales take-up for Metro Manila condos while launches also fell -40% QoQ. However, demand outside Metro Manila remained strong with Cavite and Laguna particularly cited, benefiting from infra projects.

Amid the backdrop of higher-for-longer interest rates, on the opposite end were lenders BPI +7.96% MoM and MBT +7.44%. Investors also assessed the busy round of 1Q earnings reports from banks who typically are first to kick-off the reporting season. BPI reported NPAT of P15.3b in 1Q24, +26% YoY and beating Regis forecasts (27% of FY24E) which are near the top of the Street. The figures incorporate the merger with Robinsons Bank (RBC) which took effect January 1. Loans grew +18.7% YoY, with BPI estimating that even ex-RBC, loans grew +11.9% YoY, faster than the +10.5% pace in 4Q23.

MBT also came out with a press release of 1Q results after the closing bell of the month-end session. Its headline 1Q24 NPAT stood at P12b +14.5% YoY, which on the surface looks ahead of Regis (P44.8b) and Street (P46.7b) forecasts. The "beat" however appears to be mostly due to provisions falling by -77% YoY. Loan growth accelerated to +12.1% vs +7.5% in 4Q, with corporate loans jumping +11.2% on capex lending. Expecting more details in the company’s briefing on Thursday.

Notably moving against the sector’s trend this month however was BDO which fell -4.08% MoM. Note that the company was actually the first to report results among the “Big 3.” Its headline NPAT stood at P18.5b in 1Q, +12% YoY and broadly in line with Regis (P79.6b for FY24E) and consensus (P80.6b). Loan growth accelerated to +13% YoY, from the +9% pace in 4Q23. However, NIMs fell 12bps QoQ due to rising funding costs. Asset quality remained benign, with NPLs at 1.88% and NPL cover at 181%.

On a YTD basis, MBT leads with a hefty +36.45% gain (and +44.45% total return) and is also the top gainer in the PSEi, while BPI +22.83% (same total return) and BDO +13.56% (+14.11%).

Another bright spot this month was ACEN which climbed +7.79% MoM to P4.15/share. Its chart practically shows a V-shaped move for the month as the stock was battered through the first half of April, hitting a 52-week low of P3.1 mid-way through, then rising back up through the latter. The stock has come into focus recently amid the frequency of power shortage alerts all over the country. Our sense is that ACEN is possibly one of the best ways to play the theme of tight power supply in the country given the company's positive exposure to the spot market.

In contrast, one area of weakness this month was the consumer sector after the latest CPI data showed that food inflation remained elevated: PGOLD -11.57% MoM, JFC -6.81%, CNPF -5%, MONDE -2.2% and URC -1.99%. Furthermore, current weather conditions with record high temperatures also threaten to keep food inflation elevated for the month. A notable outperformer in the sector however is non-PSE index member SEVN which climbed +6.1% MoM. Note that our research team released an initiation on the stock near the start of April with a target price of P135 (~55% upside), banking on the strong growth potential of the convenience store segment (see below). 

Another non-PSEi stock in focus this month was CHP which plummeted by a hefty -31.34% MoM to P1.38, after the official announcement that DMC -2.46%, SCC -5.59% and the Consunji group’s privately-held Dacon Corp will acquire an 89.86% stake in the company at price of roughly ~P1.42/share. The transaction will effectively give DMC a 51% stake in CHP while Dacon Corp. and SCC will each own 28.9% and 10%, respectively. As such, our sense is that DMC may be less likely to pay another sizable dividend in 2H, while SCC on the other hand may be more compelled to do so in order to help the former fund the deal.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Monetary Board keeps policy settings unchanged; Governor sees RRR cut unlikely by 3Q. The Monetary Board kept its key policy rate unchanged at 6.5%. This was widely expected by the market. The BSP raised its inflation forecast for 2024 to 3.8% from 3.6% previously. Meanwhile, BSP Governor Remolona laid out two conditions for a potential rate cut in 3Q: softer inflation and a weak economic growth. The BSP is trying to figure out the right timing to reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) but Governor Remolona said that it is unlikely that the BSP will reduce the reserve requirement by 3Q24.

Inflation accelerates to 3.7% in March.  Headline CPI rose 3.7% YoY and 0.1% MoM in March. This is mildly better than consensus expectations (3.8%) and within the BSP’s forecast range of 3.4%-4.2% but faster than 3.4% in February. Food inflation rose to 5.6% in March, its fastest print in four months. Rice prices sustained uptick (+24% YoY, +1% MoM) and contributed 1.8ppts to headline inflation. Meat inflation accelerated to 2% in March from 0.7% in Feb. Higher fuel prices and tricycle fares pushed transport inflation to rise at a faster rate of 2.1% from 1.2% in Feb. Meanwhile, core inflation further eased to 3.4% in March from 3.6% in February and 8% a year ago. The 1Q24 average inflation rate stood at 3.3%. The BSP expects inflation to average 3.6% this year while REGIS expects 4.6%.

BSP says rate cut more likely in 2025. Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. said the window for reducing policy rates in 2H24 is narrowing, citing that upside risks to inflation are becoming worse. Monetary easing will more likely begin in 1Q25 and the cuts will likely just take the policy rate to 6% from 6.5% currently.

Budget deficit narrows in March. The government recorded a budget deficit of P196bn in March, down 6.8% YoY due to higher non-tax revenues and muted spending. Revenues rose 11% YoY to P288bn, even though tax revenues dipped 0.5% YoY. Meanwhile, BIR collections rose 3% YoY to P145bn while customs collections fell 7% YoY. Non-tax revenues rose 81% YoY due to higher dividend remittances, interest on advances from the National Irrigation Administration and share of PAGCOR income. Expenses rose 3.2% on the back of a 17% jump in interest payments. Additionally, primary spending grew by a mere 1% YoY, bringing the primary deficit to P125bn, down 16% YoY. Primary spending was weighed down by the lower subsidies to government corporations and transfers to local government units (LGUs). This brings 1Q24 budget deficit to P273bn, up 0.6% YoY. Primary deficit in 1Q24 stood at P79.7bn, down 38% YoY. The government has set a budget deficit ceiling of P1.48tn this year, down 1.8% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. It also aims to reduce the deficit-to-GDP ratio to 3.7% by 2028.

GIR rises to US$104bn in March. Preliminary data showed the country’s gross international reserves (GIR) rose by 2% MoM to US$104.03bn in March from US$101.99bn in end-February. The gov’t cited national government’s net foreign currency deposits with the BSP, upward valuation adjustments in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings from higher gold prices, and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. This is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. Additionally, the BSP projects the GIR level to settle at US$103bn.

Remittances rise 3% in February. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3% YoY to US$2.65bn in February. In PHP terms, remittances rose by 5.4% YoY. Top growth contributors for the month were Saudi Arabia (+4%), UAE (+3.5%), and Singapore (+3.8% YoY). This brings YT-Feb remittances up 2.8% YoY to US$5.48bn. The BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 3% this year.

 

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.6bn in February. Preliminary data showed the trade deficit narrowed further to US$3.65bn in February.This is down from US$4.39bn in January 2024 and US$3.88bn in Feb 2023 underpinned by a sustained recovery in exports. Imports rose 6.3% YoY in February from -6% in January due to higher imports of (a) fuel (+8%), (b) raw materials (+12%), and (c) consumer goods (+9%). Meanwhile, the decline in capital goods imports persisted (-3% YoY). Exports grew at a faster rate of +16% in February as Electronic exports rose 27% YoY. Exports to top trading partners jumped: USA (+23%), HK (+46%), and China (+14%). This brought YT-Feb trade deficit to US$8.0bn, down 15% YoY.

Government debt rises to P15.2tn in February. The national government’s debt stock reached P15.2tn in end-February, up 2.6% MoM and 3.8% from end-2023 due to net issuances of domestic government securities, which was partially tempered by the effect of a stronger peso on foreign debt valuation. By end-2024, the government is targeting debt levels to reach P15.84tn, equivalent to c. 60% of GDP. Under its medium-term fiscal framework, the government plans to bring down the debt-to-GDP ratio to less than 60% by 2025 and 51% by 2028.

FDI net inflows rise 90% YoY in January. Preliminary BSP data showed that FDI net inflows surged by 89.9% YoY to US$907m in January. This is also higher by 9.8% from US$826m recorded in December. The gov’t said that this is due to nonresidents’ net investments in debt instruments surging 173.2% YoY to US$820m. The BSP expects to record FDI net inflows of US$9bn at end-2024, slightly higher than US$8.9bn FDI net inflows in 2023.

Balance of payments post US$1.2bn surplus in March. Preliminary data showed the country posted a US$1.17bn BOP surplus in March. This was slightly lower than the US$1.27bn surplus recorded in March 2023 but reversed the US$196m BOP deficit in February 2024 due to inflows from the National Government’s net foreign currency deposits and net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. This brings 1Q24 BOP level to US$238m surplus, lower than the US$3.5bn surplus recorded in 1Q23. The BOP reflects an increase in the GIR level to US$104.1bn in March from US$102bn in end-Feb, equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports.

Government lowers 2024 growth target to 6-7%; Raises budget deficit ceiling targets. The government downgraded its 2024 GDP target range to 6-7% from 6.5-7.5% previously according to National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) chief Arsenio Balisacan. It also lowered its 2025 target to 6.5% to 7.5%, down from the initial range of 6.5% to 8.0%. The Philippine Statistics Authority also revised 2023 GDP to 5.5% from the 5.6% it initially reported on Jan 31. 4Q23 GDP growth was also revised down to 5.5%  from 5.6% previously. Meanwhile, the government maintained its inflation targets at 2-4% but raised the budget deficit ceiling for the next five years. The 2024 budget deficit was raised to P1.48tn (5.6% of GDP) from P1.39tn (5.2% previously). Budget deficit to GDP ratio targets are higher due to (a) Mildly higher disbursements (+2-3%) and (b) lower nominal GDP targets (-3% to -4%). The deficit program is expected to gradually decline from 6.2% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2028.

Infrastructure spending rises by 5.3% YoY in 2M24. Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 5.3% YoY to P153.4bn in 2M24. The gov’t cited disbursements for road projects and defense modernization program. The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth accelerates to 8.6% in February; M3 growth slows to 5.0%. Preliminary data showed commercial bank loans expanded by 8.6% YoY in February from 7.8% in January. This is consistent with all banks’ loan growth of 10% in February, up from 8.8% in January. This was due to faster lending to real estate activities (11.6%), electricity, gas, steam, and air conditioning supply (11.2%); transportation and storage (21.1%); and manufacturing (5.9%). Meanwhile, consumer loan growth was steady at 25% on sustained increases in credit card and auto loans. Lastly, domestic liquidity (M3) grew by 5.0% YoY in February from 6.0% in January.

Tourist arrivals up 6% in March; +18% in 1Q24. Tourist arrivals rose 6% YoY to 506k in March or 73% of pre-Covid levels. This is however down from 582k arrivals (85% of pre-Covid) in February. This brings 1Q24 tourist arrivals to 1.66m, up 18% YoY. The government is targeting 7.7m tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY or reverting to 93% of 2019 levels (vs 2023A: 5.5m or 66% of pre-Covid). To reach this target, tourist arrivals for the rest of the year should average 671k per month.

Auto sales rise 23% in February. Vehicle sales jumped 23% YoY, 12% MoM to 38,072 units in February. This brings YT-Feb sales to 72,132 units (+19% YoY). Toyota sold 33,070 units in YT-Feb (+17% YoY), with Feb sales alone at 16,977 units (+14% YoY, +5% MoM). 1Q is typically the time when other car brands ramp up promotions to catch up on fiscal year-end targets. To reach Toyota’s target of 215k unit sales (+7%), monthly sales for the rest of the year should average  ~18k units. To reach REGIS’ forecast of 206k units (+3%), sales should average ~17k units per month. Toyota is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 28% of profit. GTCAP trades at a 42% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,222/sh and 6x PER.

President Marcos issues order easing agri import process. President Marcos signed Administrative Order 20 to further streamline administrative procedures and policies on the importation of agricultural products and remove non-tariff barriers. Nontariff barriers include quotas, import licensing systems, regulations and red tape that restrict or delay the entry of imports. Processing of Sanitary and Phytosanitary Import Clearance (SPSIC) should be done within 15 days; otherwise applications with completed documents would be deemed approved. The Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) was also ordered to streamline and standardize existing guidelines within 30 days from the order’s effectivity to allow more traders and SRA-registered industrial users to participate in the sugar import program. DA, DTI, Philippine Competition Commission, and law enforcement agencies are ordered to create a surveillance team tasked to ensure the implementation of the AO.

Panel to address right-of-way issues hounding railway projects. President Marcos Jr. created the Inter-Agency Committee for Right-of-Way (RoW) Activities for National Railway Projects to streamline land acquisitions for railway projects. The Panel will ensure “effective completion” of programs related to land acquisition and other right-of-way activities such as livelihood programs, income restoration, and resettlement. The panel will be chaired by the secretary of the Department of Transportation (DoTr) and co-chaired by the secretary of the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development. The government’s infrastructure projects have been hampered by RoW issues that have delayed their completion. Around 45% of the P488bn Metro Manila Subway Phase 1 project’s RoW has not yet been secured, which threatens the project’s 2029 completion.

Philippines, US, Japan, to invest in “Luzon corridor”.The Philippines, US, and Japan have agreed to accelerate high-impact projects in Luzon. The Luzon corridor will be part of the United States’ Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment and will connect Subic Bay, Clark, Manila, and Batangas. It will include investments in ports, rail, clean energy, semiconductors, supply chains, and other forms of connectivity. Philippine Ambassador to the United States Jose Manuel Romualdez said that Tokyo and Washington were expected to invest at least US$100bn in the Philippines over the next 5 to 10 years.
