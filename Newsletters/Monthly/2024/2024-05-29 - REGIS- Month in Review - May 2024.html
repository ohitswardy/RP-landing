REGIS REPORT

29 May 2024
 

The PSEi plummeted by -3.99% MoM in May, extending its monthly losing streak to three and erasing all gains so far this year (-0.26% YTD) as sentiment turned decidedly risk-off in the latter part of the month due to rising US Treasury yields and growing uncertainty about the monetary policy outlook at home amid a weakening PHP. The benchmark fluctuated in the 6500-6700 range through majority of the month as investors parsed through a generally positive 1Q results season and assessed the chances of rate cuts in the second half of the year amid a dovish set of CPI and GDP readings. However, the index ultimately succumbed to selling pressure through the latter part of the month, breaking below key support levels and marking a fresh YTD low, as risk assets across the globe mostly turned lower amid a renewed rise in US Treasury yields.

On the macro front, data released in the earlier part of the month showed CPI in April at 3.8%, a tick higher than the prior month’s 3.7% but below the 4.1% consensus forecast; while 1Q GDP growth stood at 5.7%, similarly a tick faster than 4Q2023’s 5.6% but below the 5.9% consensus forecast. These data points, along with BSP Governor Remolona’s subsequent dovish comments where he signaled up to two rate cuts through 2H, helped drive optimism for dovish policy settings in the latter half of the year.

Another key catalyst for the market in the earlier part of May was the 1Q results season, which was generally positive in our view. Regis’ tally of 1Q corporate earnings of PSEi constituents show that there were more beats than misses, 40% vs 28%. The nominal growth 1Q earnings growth for index members stood at 6%, which on the surface looks low compared to our 9% FY24 estimate, but note that this was primarily weighed down by SMC – excluding which, we estimate that 1Q earnings growth of PSEi members would be at a healthy level of 12%.

Later in the month however, the sharp drop in the PHP to near the crucial 59-handle, which was the key support level in late 2022, along with some mixed signals on policy from members of the monetary board, cast some uncertainty over a dovish scenario in 2H, and put into question the BSP’s ability to cut rates ahead of the Fed. Along with this, the PSEi crumbled below the technical 200-day moving average and subsequently the more important 6400 line, which was the key support level during the mid-April selloff, erasing its YTD gains and setting a YTD low of 6371 just before the final trading session of the month. The benchmark then managed to ultimately finish May back above the crucial support line at 6,433, -3.99% MoM, primarily helped by an MOC-driven spike at the closing auction of the month-end session (+1.04% swing at the close), which was also MSCI rebalancing.

Key data
 
 
 

Turnover improved in May to US$130m vs US$124m in April due to the semi-annual rebalancing of MSCi, where Philippines saw an outflow of roughly $100m. Bargain hunters and speculators were pretty active and took advantage of the liquidity event, as AEV got deleted from the main index, while AC and SM both saw down-weights.

A couple of blocks also went through this month, which grew the headline turnover. AC sold its remaining stake in Manila Water, worth US$220m, to Trident Water, while ALI did a US$55m placement of AREIT shares. Oceana Gold (OGP) also listed this month, with local retail traders quite active in the stock.

Foreign Participation fell to 46% vs 50% the month prior.

 

  • Foreigners continued to be heavy sellers in the market with AEV the top sold name due to its deletion from the MSCI. The stock saw an estimated outflow of  $100m, about 100 times ADV, sending the stock to a new 52-week low on rebalancing day.  SM also saw significant outflows (-US$32m) due to a decrease in its weighting in the MSCi, as did AC (-US$7m). MSCI small inclusion ACEN meanwhile rallied +15.7% in May, boosted by net foreign buying of US$14m.
  • Property names likewise were the top foreign sold names, after BSP’s statement that they are not in a hurry to cut rates.  Both ALI  (-US$49m) and SMPH (-US$23m) saw significant outflows, skewed mostly towards the latter part of the month. Banks meanwhile saw some cracks despite the favorable high interest rate environment. Profit takers weighed on the sector after months of outperformance as traders took a risk-off stance.
  • Telcos were bought by foreigners this month after the sector reported strong 1Q numbers. Both GLO and TEL surged more than 10% this month with  GLO the top foreign bough stock for US$22m.
  • Consumer names were meanwhile mixed. URC snapped its 13-month net outflow streak, with foreigners turning net buyers of the name after the stock briefly broke key support levels. JFC (-US$31m) meanwhile saw heavy foreign selling despite 1Q24 numbers coming in line with expectations.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Unlike last month when index laggards outnumbered winners at a ratio of roughly 2:1, breadth of the index this May was virtually even, notwithstanding the index’s sharp -4% slump, with 14 gainers and 16 losers. Top gainers this time were lighter weighted members WLCON +19.76% MoM, ACEN +15.66% and CNVRG +14.47%.

WLCON +19.76% advanced sharply this month despite announcing downbeat 1Q results earlier. Its 1Q24 earnings reported near the beginning of May showed that net profit fell -23% YoY to P740m, trailing behind both Regis and consensus forecasts, as same store sales fell by over 7%. Management indicated that SSS has recovered in April, but remains down by -3% YT-April. The shares appeared to have benefited from some bargain hunting however as we note that WLCON was the worst performing stock in the index before May, down by -20.1% YT-April.

Among the index gainers for the second month in a row was ACEN +15.66% after reporting strong 1Q results, which look on trend to beat both Regis and consensus FY2024 forecasts. Furthermore, our sense is that the company may post even better results in 2Q given its newly onboarded additional capacity and its net seller exposure to the electricity spot market amid the frequency of power alerts in the country, which has notably extended even into night time recently. Another tailwind for the stock this May was its inclusion to the MSCI small cap index (vs deletions CEB -8.74% MoM and SPNEC -14.15%).

Another big gainer was CNVRG +14.47% after similarly reporting positive 1Q results. The key highlight of its earnings report in our view is the +125k subscriber additions. This is the highest figure since 3Q2021. For comparison, TEL saw a -14k net subscriber reduction for its wired business. Note however that later in the month, TEL introduced its own lower priced broadband offerings which are available in both postpaid and prepaid formats. TEL’s new postpaid plan is priced at P899/month, compared to CNVRG’s P888/month, but TEL requires an upfront payment of P3,500. Meanwhile its new prepaid plans are similarly priced to GLO’s GFiber prepaid at P299 for a seven-day duration, P599 for 15, and P999 for 30.

In general, the broader telco space was quite strong this month with GLO +11.05% MoM and TEL +10.45% both sharply rising as well, supported by widely positive 1Q results. Another key area of focus in the sector is the looming IPO of Mynt (which holds GCash) after Globe CEO Ernest Cu said that his best estimate for the timing of Mynt IPO is next year, and that management prefers to list in the Philippines, but that a dual listing with a second IPO in the US is another option that they are looking into. Note that Mynt, which is 36% owned by Globe, contributed almost P1b to its P6.8b reported net income in 1Q24 and is set to make over P10b (almost US$200m) this year if 1Q24 profits were annualized. Regis reckons a valuation of at least US$5b is not unreasonable on that level of earnings.

On the opposite end, biggest laggard this week was AEV -16.55% MoM to its lowest level since mid-2021, dragged by heavy foreign outflows amid its removal from the MSCI standard cap index, which took effect after the close of the month-end session. Turnover in AEV during the rebalancing hit $93m – note that outflow estimates leading into the event ranged from $100m-$120m.

Other notable laggards were lenders BDO -12.28% MoM and MBT -11.36% as focus started to move away from banks after the BSP’s initial dovish comments during the month. Despite the turn in policy outlook in the latter month however, lenders continued to submit to selling pressure, possibly driven by unwinding of winners after the index breached key support levels the mood on the ground turned decidedly risk-off in the latter part of the month. Note that before May, MBT had been the top index gainer, +36.45% YT-April.

That being said, the property sector was a generally weak spot as well with large caps ALI -8.7% MoM and SMPH -3.58% both in the red, as the monetary policy outlook remains largely uncertain at the moment. ALI is now the second worst performing stock in the benchmark, -23.8% YTD, next to NIKL -26.09% YTD, which eked out a modest +0.75% MoM gain this month.

The consumer sector meanwhile was mixed with gains in PGOLD +6.33% MoM, MONDE +4.87% and URC +3.68% countered by losses in JFC -8.07%, EMI -1.58% and CNPF -1.32%. Note that gainers PGOLD and URC are coming from lows and are hence potentially seeing some support from bargain hunting. Our sense is that the consumer sector, particularly those trading near lows, potentially offer good value as CPI is expected to ease in the next two months and may help boost demand, with weather conditions on the ground starting to improve after being affected by El Nino in 1Q. That said, the BSP recently reiterated that CPI risk remains tilted to the upside. Furthermore, they have also acknowledged the potential impact of a weak peso on inflation. The central bank expects inflation in May to be within 3.7%-4.5%, while the consensus forecast is currently at 4%, which would mark an uptick from the 3.8% print in April. The data will be released on Wednesday (June 5) morning.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

1Q24 GDP accelerates to 5.7% on export rebound; Domestic demand slows. GDP grew 5.7% in 1Q24, below consensus forecast of 5.9%. On the demand side, domestic demand growth moderated to 4.0% but was offset by a rebound in net exports to 9.5%. Private consumption grew 4.6%, the slowest pace since the lows of the pandemic as inflation limited spending. Meanwhile, government spending was up by just 1.7%, which the government attributed to the timing of large expenditures to April this year from March last year. Capital formation was still sluggish due to high interest rates, rising by just 1.3%, with construction moderating to 6.8% and investments in durable equipment contracting for the first time since 1Q21. On the supply side, the service sector remained robust, rising by 6.9% and contributed 4.2ppts to GDP. Industry sector grew at a faster rate of 5.1% due to faster growth in manufacturing and higher output of food, electronics, and chemical products. Agri sector grew by 0.4%. 

Inflation mildly accelerates to 3.8% in April. Headline inflation for April was mildly up at 3.8% from 3.7% the previous month. This is below the consensus forecast of 4.1% and at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.5%-4.3%. The mild uptick driven by food inflation (+6% from +5.6%) and higher fuel prices (+3.4% from +0.8%). Rice CPI rose by 0.4% MoM, the slowest pace since Oct 2023. Note that international rice prices have fallen c. 10% YTD But curiously, electricity CPI (4.6% of CPI basket) dropped 11% YoY and 3% MoM, despite tightness in the electricity market – as such, Regis’ sense that this could re-accelerate in the succeeding month. Core inflation slowed further to 3.2% from 3.4%. Headline inflation rate this year has averaged 3.4%, trending below the BSP’s target of 3.8%.

BSP maintains policy rate, signals possible rate cut in August. The BSP kept its policy rate unchanged at 6.5%, as expected. BSP Governor Remolona, Jr. however said “We are actually somewhat less hawkish than before, which means we could ease (or) cut rates by the third or fourth quarter this year.” They are looking at one or two rate cuts in 2H, possibly a 25bps cut at its Aug 15 meeting ahead of the Fed, but are not too worried about the potential impact on the peso. BSP also lowered its inflation forecast to 3.5% from 3.8% for this year, citing better-than-expected inflation in April of 3.8% (YT-April average inflation rate at 3.4%.) Furthermore, the BSP may also consider cutting banks’ reserve requirement ratios once the Monetary Board starts easing interest rates this year.

BSP to maintain "restrictive policy"; Finance Department sees 150bps rate cut over two years.  The BSP will keep monetary policy settings “restrictive” as upside risks to inflation remain, according to BSP Senior Assistant Governor Iluminada T. Sicat. The BSP expects inflation to accelerate from May to July, but expects it to return within the 2-4% target band after. Recall that inflation accelerated to 3.8% in April, bringing YT-April average inflation rate to 3.4% (BSP FY24E baseline forecast at 3.5%). On the peso, BSP reiterated that it does not target any specific exchange rate level. Mr. Sicat thinks that the recent weakness of the PHP is temporary, given the Fed’s hawkish stance.

Meanwhile, Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said the Monetary Board could reduce the policy rate by as much as 150bps in the next two years. Mr. Recto, who sits on the Monetary Board, said that BSP has room to reduce rates by 3Q.

Trade deficit narrows to US$3.2bn in March. Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit shrunk 37% YoY, 13% MoM to US$3.18bn in March due to a 20% drop in imports to US$9.3bn, which is the sharpest drop since July 2020. Raw material imports dropped 25% and capital goods imports dropped 15%. Consumer goods imports fell 19% and fuel imports dropped 18%.

Meanwhile exports fell 7% to US$6.13bn due to decline in mineral products (electronics exports up +1%). Exports to the US and HK rose 9% and 60%, respectively. This brings 1Q24 trade deficit down by 22% to US$11.25bn.

GIR dips to US$103bn in end-April. Preliminary data showed the GIR level fell to US$103.4bn in end-April from US$104.1bn in end-March, reflecting mainly the National Government’s (NG) payments of foreign currency debt obligations and various expenditures. This is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports.

BOP deficit widens to US$639m in April. The country’s overall balance of payments posted a deficit of US$639m in April, higher than the US$148m deficit posted in April 2023 and a reversal of the US$1.17bn BOP surplus in March 2024. Officials cited outflows arising from the national government’s net foreign currency debt service. This brings YT-April BOP deficit to US$401m, a reversal of the US$3.3bn surplus in the same period a year ago. The BOP position reflects the slight decrease in GIR level to US$102.6bn in end-April from US$104.1bn in end-March 2024.

Peso hits P58:USD1; BSP to intervene. The BSP warned it will intervene in the foreign exchange market as the PHP closed at its lowest level of P58.27 for the first time in over 18 months. The peso’s weakness against the US dollar was in line with other currencies in the region. Beyond just inflation, the weakness/strength of the PHP should also be a key factor in any BSP decision on interest rates. Thus, weakness could postpone any decision to cut policy rates.

Government posts lower budget surplus of P42.7bn in April. The national government recorded a budget surplus of P42.7bn in April, down 36% YoY. The decline is due to the 32% YoY jump in expenditures, which outpaced the 22% YoY increase in revenues. Revenues rose 22% YoY to P537bn, with tax collections up 14% and non-tax revenues more than doubling to P77.9bn. BIR collections rose 13% on double-digit growth in income tax and VAT collections during the annual income tax filing. Customs collections went up by 20%. Non-tax revenues doubled, boosted by the higher dividend payout of GOCCs to 75% from 50% previously. Meanwhile expenditures rose on the back of a 30% YoY increase primary spending and 46% increase in interest expenses. The expansion in primary spending was due to higher releases of national tax allotment and subsidies to GOCCs, including releases to PSALM and the 4th tranche of capitalization of Coconut Farmers Industry Trust Fund (CFITF). This brings YT-April budget deficit to P230bn, up 13% YoY.

 

 

Government raises target borrowings to P2.57tn this year. The government raised its 2024 borrowing program by 4% to P2.57tn this year. This will be 17% higher than the P2.19tn total borrowings in 2023. They cited recent adjustments in the budget deficit targets, which resulted in slightly lower tax revenue targets due to a lower nominal GDP forecast (-3% lower vs previous target). As of 1Q24, the government has borrowed P830bn, up 32% YoY.

NG debt rises to P15.02tn in April. The national government’s outstanding debt reached P15.02tn in end-April 2024. This was up 0.61% MoM due to net financing and the PHP depreciation against the USD. Domestic debt rose to P10.31tn (+0.3% MoM) due to net issuance of government securities, while external debt rose 1.3% MoM to P4.71tn due to PHP depreciation. This resulted in a P109bn MoM upward adjustment in translation of USD-denominated debt (82% of external debt are USD loans). Note that the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio target this year is set at 60.3% and it seeks to bring this down further to 55.9% by 2028. 

Remittances rise 2.5% to US$2.74bn in March. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.5% YoY to US$2.74bn in March. In PHP terms, remittances rose 4.5% YoY. Land-based remittances rose 3%, driven by UAE (+4.6%), Qatar (+6.2%), Singapore (+3%), and USA (+2.8%), while growth in sea-based remittances slowed to 0.9%. This brings 1Q24 remittances to US$8.22bn, up 2.7%. The BSP projects remittances to rise by 3% YoY, implying FY24E of US$34.5bn. To reach this, remittances for the rest of the year should average US$2.9bn per month.

FDI net inflows rise to US$1.36bn in February. FDI net inflows rose 29% YoY to US$1.36bn in February, driven by a 927% YoY expansion in net investments in equity capital (other than reinvestment of earnings) to US$764m but tempered by 42% YoY contraction in net investments in debt instruments and 3.8% drop in reinvestment of earnings. Bulk of the equity capital placements during the reference month came from the Netherlands with investments directed mostly to the financial and insurance industry. This brings YT-Feb FDI inflows up 48% YoY to US$2.3bn.

Marcos calls for review of minimum wage rates. President Marcos Jr. called on the Regional Tripartite Wage Productivity Boards to review the minimum wage rates in their respective regions and to consider the impact of inflation, among others. The review will be done within 60 days prior to the anniversary of their latest wage order. Since 2022 and 2023, the government has implemented cumulative wage hikes of +20-22% across regions.

Universal/commercial banks loan growth accelerates to 9.4% in March. Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 9.4% in March from 8.6% in February. Note that Regis is forecasting system-wide loan growth of 10-12% for 2024E. Breakdown showed that loans to productive activities rose 7.7%, up from 6.8% the previous month, while consumer loan growth was steady at 25%, with credit card loans up 30% and auto loans up 20%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) grew 5.7%, accelerating from 5.1% the previous month.

NPLs improve, -5bps MoM in March; big banks perform much better. Asset quality (all banks) saw slight improvement across all metrics in March, while loan growth inched up. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dropped 5bps MoM to 3.47% from 3.52% in February. More importantly, in our view, the absolute PHP value of NPLs fell 0.3% MoM. Past Due (PD) rates, meanwhile, edged down 1bp MoM to 4.30%, as did Restructured Loans (RL), to 2.15%. NPL Cover, also improved a hair, up to 100.7% from 100.1% in February. Meanwhile, loan growth (ex-interbank) inched up to 10.2% YoY in March, compared to 10.0% in February. On a MoM basis, loans were up 1.1%.

The asset quality data was much better for the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs), which saw PD ratios down 7bps, and NPL ratios down 8bps MoM. For Thrift Banks (TBs), PD rates jumped 77bps and NPLs increased 38bps MoM. The biggest jump by far was for Digibanks, whose PDs spiked 588bps MoM while NPLs jumped 369bps MoM.

Infrastructure spending rises 9% YoY in 1Q24. Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 9.3% YoY to P266bn in 1Q24. This implies that infra spend in March 2024 alone rose 36% YoY to P133bn. The Department of Public Works and Highways posted “strong spending performance” for payment of progress billings for projects and also made advance payments to contractors. Recall that in 1Q24 GDP, the construction sector grew 6.8%, driven primarily by government (+12.4% YoY). The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. To reach this target, infra spend should average P134bn per month for the rest of the year.

Vehicle sales rise 22% in April. CAMPI reported that car sales rose 22% to 37,314 units in April (but down 0.4% on a MoM basis). Balancing out the timing of Holy Week, industry car sales for the months of March and April rose 11%. This brings YT-April vehicle sales up 14.8%.

Toyota Philippines’ sales in 4M24 reached 67,580 units (+14% YoY), implying that in April alone, Toyota sold 17,913 units (+27% YoY, 7.9% MoM). To reach Toyota’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.4k units for the rest of the year. 

 

DA sees lower rice prices starting June. Department of Agriculture (DA) Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said rice prices will likely decline starting next month, citing the decline in international rice prices for the past weeks. The Secretary is also optimistic that palay production will go up in the next harvest season after being affected by El Niño in 1Q, when it declined 2% YoY to 4.68m MT.

Finance Secretary backs lower rice tariffs. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said he is open to cutting the import tariff on rice to 17.5%-20% from 35% today. The DOF is looking at the power of the President to modify the tariff rates when Congress is not in session. Note that Congress adjourned its session on May 24 and the next session opens on July 21. The Tariff Commission (TC) is expected to begin reviewing the proposed rice tariff cut soon.

Government expects rice prices possibility falling 20% by September. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto expects the retail price of rice to drop by as much as 20% by September as effects of El Niño fade and all restrictions on exports by major producing countries are expected to have been lifted by then.

ERC to suspend WESM trading when Luzon and Visayas are under red alert. The ERC ordered the suspension of trading in the WESM during red alerts to prevent a spike in electricity prices. During the suspension, the administrative price, or the price imposed by the market operator to the trading participants during market suspension or market intervention, will be applied. If a dispatch interval is subject to both a price mitigation and the administered price, “the lower of the two prices shall apply in the settlement of transactions for such interval. Suspensions will be lifted “only if the regional available capacity, less actual regional demand, reaches above zero for 24 consecutive hours.”.

The ERC said the average spot prices per day increased by 11% in Luzon and by 53% in the Visayas when alert notices were issued due to the high heat index. The ERC’s move will reduce the supernormal profits that could accrue to generators during periods of tight supply (e.g. now). It would also have implications on the willingness of gencos to invest, especially in the absence of contracts. Among those who have significant power capacity available for sale to the spot market are: Aboitiz Power (AP PM), Semirara (SCC PM) and ACEN (ACEN PM).

DENR to shorten permit process for mining. The DENR plans to implement “parallel” processing of mining permits this year to shorten bureaucratic approval of prospective mining operations to just 1-3 years from 6 years. Mining permits that are not prerequisites for securing another government permit will be simultaneously processed by concerned government agencies.

No excise tax rate hike amid projected revenue dip–DOF. The Department of Finance (DOF) will not impose a rate hike in excise taxes, particularly on sin taxes. The BIR collected P21.975bn in excise taxes on tobacco in 1Q24, down 20% YoY. Officials cited illicit trade and consumers’ shift to e-cigarettes sold in e-marketplace. Note that the excise tax rate on cigarettes rose to P63 per pack in 2024 and will increase by 5 percent in the succeeding years.

 

Senate OKs VAT on digital services. The Senate approved on third and final reading a bill that seeks to impose a 12% VAT on digital services provided by companies with no physical presence in the Philippines. Measure may cover e-commerce firms such as Amazon, Shein, Rakuten, Taobao, AliExpress and Temu. BIR commissioner can order the blocking or suspension of digital providers if they fail to withhold and remit the 12% VAT. Bill exempts online education platforms, services of banks/nonbank financial intermediaries. Department of Finance expects the bill to bring in P83.8bn in revenues from 2024-2028.

Digital payments surge in April, up 51% YoY, a new all-time high. Digital transactions over InstaPay and PesoNet surged to P1,413bn, up 51% YoY in April, accelerating from the 23% posted in March. As we reported last month, we suspected that the March slowdown – and thus the sharp rebound in April – were due to the timing of the Easter holidays, which landed in March this year vs April last year. The variance was much more pronounced for PesoNet, since these are batched transactions that are only processed during business days. PesoNet payments came in at P855bn, jumping 54% YoY in April, up from just 13% in March. Meanwhile, smaller-ticket, real-time transfers over InstaPay also rebounded, growing 47.8% YoY to P558bn in April, faster than the 39% clip in March. Both PesoNet and InstaPay figures for April were record-highs.

For the first four months of 2024, combined PesoNet and InstaPay transactions stood at P5,225bn, growing a healthy 37%, faster than the 29% posted in FY23. For PesoNet, 4M24 payments were up 33% YoY, compared to +22% in FY23, while InstaPay grew 45% YoY, up from 42% in FY23.

Rules tightened for Chinese tourist visas. The Department of Foreign Affairs will implement stricter rules for the issuance of tourist visas to Chinese nationals, citing cases of fraudulently acquired visas and crimes related to POGO firms. New visa requirement includes social security documents, on top of usual government IDs, proof of financial capacity, employment certificate, and bank statements. The DFA will also increase the minimum number for group applicants from three to ten. Unlike group applicants, group applicants don’t have to personally appear before a Philippine consular office in China. Note that Chinese tourists rose 105% YoY to 141k as of April 2024, which is about 24% of pre-Covid levels.

Senator files Bill seeking to ban POGO operations. Senator Sherwin Gatchalian filed a Bill seeking to ban offshore gaming operations in the Philippines. Recall that earlier this year, the House Games and Amusements panel approved a bill banning Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators due to crimes linked to the sector. Note however that PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco previously said that he is in favor of allowing them to continue operating if they are properly monitored and regulated. Regis estimates that offshore gaming firms, now called Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL), occupy about 900,000sqm of Metro Manila office space (about 6% of Metro Manila office stock).

PAGCOR issues more offshore gaming licenses. As of May 15, PAGCOR has now issued licenses to 40 Internet Gaming Licensees (IGL) and 9 provisional licenses have also been issued. Recall that as of January 15, there were only 7 IGL firms and 44 provisional licensees. In 2H23, PAGCOR required all POGO firms to reapply for licenses under the new nomenclature of IGL. From 2020 to 1Q23, offshore gaming firms vacated about 600,000sqm of Metro Manila office space. Since then, they have taken-up an incremental c. 200,000sqm of office space.
